Energy Propagation in Scattering Convolution Networks Can Be Arbitrarily Slow
Hartmut Führ, Max Getter

Abstract
. We analyze energy decay for deep convolutional neural networks employed as feature extractors, such as Mallat’s wavelet scattering transform. For time-frequency scattering transforms based on Gabor filters, it has been established that energy decay is exponential, for arbitrary square-integrable input signals. Our main results allow to prove that this is wrong for wavelet scattering in arbitrary dimensions. In this setting, the energy decay of the scattering transform acting on a generic square-integrable signal turns out to be arbitrarily slow. The fact that this behavior holds for dense subsets of L 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) emphasizes that fast energy decay is generally not a stable property of signals. We complement these findings with positive results allowing to conclude fast (up to exponential) energy decay for generalized Sobolev spaces that are tailored to the frequency localization of the underlying filter bank. Both negative and positive results highlight that energy decay in scattering networks critically depends on the interplay of the respective frequency localizations of the signal on the one hand, and of the employed filters on the other.



Keywords:
Deep convolutional neural networks, scattering transform, energy propagation, filter banks, wavelets

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and related work

Up to this day, the tremendous success of
convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks
[
22
]
like image classification is only partially understood. This outstanding performance of CNNs is generally believed to be due to their ability to capture information at multiple scales through the use of convolutions and subsequently aggregating the information observed at these scales by means of further operations such as pooling. Consequently, CNNs are seemingly able to extract semantic content from images while discarding irrelevant information. However, the available rigorous analytical explanations for their success are still incomplete.

In his pioneering work
[
11
]
, Stéphane Mallat introduced the
scattering transform, a nonlinear operator that cascades convolutions followed by the modulus non-linearity. The scattering transform can be viewed as an infinitely deep and infinitely wide CNN with the modulus as non-linearity, predetermined filters, and no pooling
[
19
]
. Due to the use of hand-crafted filters based on a wavelet construction, there is no need to train the network. The windowed scattering transform was extended in
[
19
]
to allow for the use of other (optionally learned) filters that are not wavelet-generated, with potentially different filters for different layers, other non-linearities than the modulus, and pooling operations between layers. The present work studies the scattering transform in a more classical fashion, with, for simplicity of exposition, identical (not necessarily wavelet-generated) filters across network layers, the modulus as non-linearity, and no pooling.

Despite the absence of a training phase and pooling, Mallat-type scattering networks are still performing comparably well to other state-of-the-art models, even after more than a decade of progress in machine learning since their invention (e.g.,
[
15
,
23
]
).
They are used in various applications such as vision and audio tasks, quantum chemistry, medicine, astronomy, and manifold learning
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
14
,
16
,
17
]
. In many of these, the scattering transform (or a variant thereof) is employed in the preprocessing phase of the input data in that it acts as a feature extractor, which is supposed to improve the overall performance of the machine learning model.

Such scattering networks satisfy many of the nice properties that are generally believed to be essential in a feature extractor
[
11
,
12
,
19
,
13
]
.
One of these properties, which is of particular importance both from a practical as well as a theoretical point of view, is the question of fast energy propagation across the network layers: Under mild assumptions on the filters, the energy of any input signal
decomposes, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, into the aggregated energy that is contained in the first
N
layers of the scattering network
and some energy remainder (see Proposition
1.3
for details). Thus, the scattering transform preserves the energy of an input signal if and only if the corresponding energy remainder converges to zero as
N\to\infty
. In this sense, all of the original information about the input signal is then still contained in the features generated by the scattering network. In practice, however, only finitely many features of the infinite scattering network can be computed. Therefore, a lower bound for the loss of information when restricting the network to finitely many features of the first
N
layers is given by the corresponding energy remainder. Hence, fast decay of this quantity is desirable.

Energy conservation in the above sense was first established in
[
11
, Theorem 2.6]
for wavelet-generated filter banks under a rather restrictive and technical admissibility assumption. Under less restrictive assumptions on the wavelets, but only in dimension
d=1
, the decay of the energy remainder was shown
[
18
, Theorem 3.1]
to be at least of exponential order
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{N})
for Sobolev functions with some
unspecified
, signal-independent
\alpha\in(0,1)
. In recent years, quantitative results in a similar fashion were also derived for other types of filter banks. Energy decay was proven (in arbitrary dimension) to be at least of exponential order
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{N})
for all finite-energy input signals, with some unspecified, signal-independent
\alpha\in(0,1)
, if the filters of the underlying scattering network form a so-called uniform covering frame
[
3
, Proposition 3.1]
. In particular, this class of filter banks includes certain Weyl-Heisenberg (Gabor) frames
[
3
, Proposition 2.3]
. However, the uniform covering property requires the Fourier supports of the filters to be uniformly bounded, so that a broad range of commonly used filter banks in signal processing (e.g., wavelet-generated filter banks) is not adressed by this result.

With refined estimates, but based on an idea similar to that of
[
18
, Theorem 3.1]
, energy decay was proven to be at least of exponential order
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{N})
for Sobolev functions if the filter bank is generated by a
bandlimited
wavelet in dimension
d=1
, with a concretely specified decay factor
\alpha
that depends on the bandwidth of the generator
[
20
, Theorem 2]
,
[
21
, Theorem 3.1]
. If the filters do not necessarily have an underlying structure as in the case of wavelet-generated filters, but instead satisfy certain mild analyticity assumptions in arbitrary dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}
, energy decay was shown to be at least of polynomial order
\mathcal{O}(N^{-m_{d}})
for Sobolev functions, where
m_{d}\in(0,1]
and
m_{d}\to 0
as
d\to\infty

[
20
, Theorem 1]
.

So far, an important question has remained unanswered:

Does exponential energy decay hold for the wavelet scattering transform, globally on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
?

1.2.
Contributions

Our first main result, Theorem
2.2
, provides a negative answer to the question of global exponential energy decay for the wavelet scattering transform. In fact, we show that energy decay can even become arbitrarily slow over
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
when emplyoing filter banks that have an underlying structure similar to wavelet-generated filter banks (including the latter). It turns out that, for given decay rate, the set of signals that do not obey this rate in terms of scattering energy decay is dense in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Informally, energy decay can thus be considered instable over
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for these types of scattering networks.

We complement these negative findings with our second main result, Theorem
3.9
, which is of positive nature. By exploiting the interplay between Fourier decay of the input signal and frequency concentration of the underlying filters of the scattering network, we provide explicit upper bounds on the convergence rates of the energy remainder for large filter-dependent subclasses of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, thereby generalizing, unifying, and partially improving the findings of the previous works
[
3
,
18
,
20
,
21
]
.

In view of selecting wavelet-generated filters for the scattering network (as is the case in many applications), these results have two main implications:

•

Fast energy propagation in wavelet scattering networks can only be guaranteed if a priori knowledge about the global Fourier decay of the input data is available, with sufficient conditions specified by Corollary
4.7
.

•

The set of those signals in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, for which the mixed
(\ell^{1},L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
scattering norm considered in
[
11
, Section 2.5]
is infinite, is dense in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Results in the spirit of
[
11
, Theorem 2.12]
, which guarantee stability of the windowed scattering transform to the action of small diffeomorphisms for signals with finite mixed
(\ell^{1},L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
scattering norm, therefore do not apply to such (in this sense) ill-behaved input signals. On the other hand, finiteness of this mixed scattering norm can be guaranteed by means of our positive results, e.g., for all
f\in H^{s}_{\log}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
s>1
, which generalizes
[
13
, Proposition 2.4]
to arbitrary dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}
if the generating wavelet is bandlimited.

1.3.
Structure of the paper

We begin our exposition in Section
1.5
by reviewing the windowed scattering transform and clarifying our base assumptions on the underlying filters of the scattering network.

Our results in Section
2
concern filter banks with an inherent structure similar to those generated by wavelets. The starting point of our analysis is Lemma
2.1
, which states an approximate version of super-additivity of the energy remainder for signals that are nearly separated in freqeuncy domain. We build on this result by constructing an adversarial signal by means of an infinite series of appropriately chosen, nearly pairwise separated (in frequency domain) signals.

In Section
3
, we derive upper bounds on the decay rate of the energy remainder, which are based on the interplay between Fourier decay of the input signal and the frequency concentration of the filters. Our results apply to a broad range of filter banks and their corresponding signal classes. The filters must satisfy a mild analyticity assumption, which can be seen as a refinement of the setting considered in
[
20
, Assumption 1]
.

Finally, we illustrate our results in Section
4
by outlining the consequences for a certain class of filters with uniformly bounded bandwidth, and for wavelet-generated filters. In doing so, we recover some existing results from the literature, and we improve and generalize others. These results are of considerable independent interest, which is further enhanced by the contrast to the negative findings from Section
2
.

1.5.
A brief review of the windowed scattering transform.

A scattering network computes cascades of convolutions followed by the application of the modulus as non-linearity, with no pooling between the layers. Given any
\psi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we denote by

U[\psi]:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\quad f\mapsto|f*\psi|

the
single step scattering propagator
associated to filtering with the function
\psi
(which in this context is thus commonly called a
filter
). Moreover, we refer to an ordered sequence
p=(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{N})\in(L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))^{N}

as a
path
of length
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Iterating the single step scattering propagators along this path yields the
scattering propagator

U[p]:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\quad f\mapsto U[\psi_{N}]\ldots U[\psi_{1}]f=|\ldots|f*\psi_{1}|\ldots*\psi_{N}|.

For notational convenience, we define the scattering propagator along the empty path
e
(the unique path of length zero) to be the identity operator
U[e]:=\id_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
.

A designated function
\chi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
induces a
windowed scattering propagator
along a path
p

of length
N\in\mathbb{N}
and window
\chi
(referred to as output-generating filter), given by

S[p;\chi]:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\quad f\mapsto(U[p]f)*\chi=|\ldots|f*\psi_{1}|\ldots*\psi_{N}|*\chi.

By Young’s convolution inequality, the operators
U[p]
and
S[p;\chi]
are well-defined.

Our main objective is to study the properties of the operators (see Figure
1
)

\displaystyle U[P]:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to\ell^{2}(P;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})),\quad f\mapsto\left(U[p]f\right)_{p\in P}

and

\displaystyle S[P;\chi]:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to\ell^{2}(P;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})),\quad f\mapsto\left(S[p;\chi]f\right)_{p\in P}

for certain path sets
P
.
In particular, this requires the operators to be well-defined in the sense that, for any
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, the series
\sum_{p\in P}\left\lVert\text{}U[p]f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
converges unconditionally. In this respect, let us now state our main requirement on the filters, which are the basic building blocks of the windowed scattering transform.

Throughout this paper, we assume that
\mathfrak{F}:=\{\chi\}\cup\Psi\subset L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a countably infinite collection of functions so that the corresponding filter transform

(1)

\displaystyle L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to\ell^{2}(\mathfrak{F};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})),\ f\mapsto(f*\chi)\cup\left(f*\psi\right)_{\psi\in\Psi}

is an isometry. This is precisely the case if
\mathfrak{F}
forms a semi-discrete Parseval frame
[

(2)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\text{}f*\chi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\left\lVert\text{}f*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

We will frequently use the fact (which follows directly from Parseval’s theorem and the convolution theorem) that

forms a semi-discrete Parseval frame if and only if

satisfies the Littlewood-Paley condition

(3)

\displaystyle|\widehat{\chi}(\xi)|^{2}+\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}=1\quad\text{a.e. }\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

If any of the latter equivalent conditions holds, then every

admits a decomposition

(4)

\displaystyle f=f*\chi*\chi^{*}+\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}f*\psi*\psi^{*}.

Remark 1.1
.

To simplify the notation, we do not assign the filters
\Psi
an explicit (countably infinite) index set, unless we work in a more concrete setting. This precludes the use of multiple occurrences of the same filter, which is often allowed in the definition of the scattering transform. In principle, this can be accommodated, primarily at notational cost, by extending the subsequent discussion to multisets. To us the benefit of such an extension seems quite limited, which is why we have refrained from making the necessary adjustments.

We will mainly consider the operators

and

on nonempty subsets

of the set of all finite length scattering paths
\mathcal{P}_{\Psi}:=\bigcup_{N=0}^{\infty}\Psi^{N}
, where we set
\Psi^{0}:=\{e\}
. The

is the operator

(5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}[\mathfrak{F}]:=S[\mathcal{P}_{\Psi};\chi]:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to\ell^{2}(\mathcal{P}_{\Psi};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})),\quad f\mapsto\left(S[p;\chi]f\right)_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{\Psi}}.

In the interest of generality, we do not make further assumptions on the Parseval frame

for now. Even in this very general setting, the windowed scattering transform

(see Figure

Proposition 1.3
.

Every

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left\lVert\text{}S[\Psi^{n};\chi]f\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\Psi^{n};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2}+\left\lVert\text{}U[\Psi^{N}]f\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\Psi^{N};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2}=\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

\left\lVert\text{}\mathcal{S}[\mathfrak{F}]f\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{P}_{\Psi};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2},

Moreover,

(7)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\lVert\text{}U[\Psi^{N}]f\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\Psi^{N};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2}=0

holds if and only if

(8)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\text{}\mathcal{S}[\mathfrak{F}]f\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{P}_{\Psi};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2}=\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

As already pointed out in Section

\displaystyle W_{N}[\Psi](f)\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot K_{N}(\xi)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

•

•

By Dini’s theorem
[
4
, Theorem 7.3]
, this upper integral bound entails, for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, the convergence (
7
) (cf.
[
20
, Proposition 2]
).
In particular, the scattering transform
\mathcal{S}[\mathfrak{F}]
is norm-preserving in these settings by Proposition
1.3
. Specifically the assumptions of
[
20
, Theorem 1]
allow for some flexibility in the choice of the filters while guaranteeing norm-preservation. Clearly, this implies that the single signal that produces zero-features in every layer by means of the scattering transform is the trivial null-signal
f=0
.

In order to produce many informative (in the sense of nontrivial) features by means of the scattering transform, one typically chooses an output-generating filter
\chi
that is of low-pass type. By the Littlewood-Paley condition (
3
), the remaining filters
\Psi
are automatically of high-pass nature. Let us briefly illustrate how informativeness of
\mathcal{S}[\mathfrak{F}]
can be achieved by imposing a low-pass condition on
\chi
: Let
0\neq g,\chi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and suppose that
\widehat{\chi}(0)\neq 0
. By continuity of
\widehat{\chi}
and
\mathcal{F}(|g|)
, and since
\mathcal{F}(|g|)(0)=\left\lVert\text{}g\text{}\right\rVert_{1}>0
, there exist
\varepsilon,\delta>0
such that
|\widehat{\chi}|\cdot|\mathcal{F}(|g|)|\geq\varepsilon
on
B_{\delta}(0)
. Thus, we have

\left\lVert\text{}|g|*\chi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\left\lVert\text{}\mathcal{F}(|g|)\cdot\widehat{\chi}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq\int_{B_{\delta}(0)}|\mathcal{F}(|g|)(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\chi}(\xi)|^{2}\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>\geq\varepsilon^{2}\cdot\vol(B_{\delta}(0))>0.

Hence, any feature of this type is nontrivial. The assumption that
\widehat{\chi}(0)\neq 0
is of vital importance for the argument, as is shown in
[
20
, Appendix A]
by the example of a scattering network (feature extractor) with nontrivial kernel.

For the remainder of this section, we will collect some basic auxiliary statements about the windowed scattering transform that will prove useful later. We include their proofs for self-containment.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
U[\mathfrak{F}^{N}]:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to\ell^{2}(\mathfrak{F};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
is well-defined and norm-preserving, i.e., for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

W_{N}[\mathfrak{F}](f)=\sum_{p\in\mathfrak{F}^{N}}\left\lVert\text{}U[p]f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

This is easily shown by induction: The base case
N=1
is exactly one of the characterizations of a semi-discrete Parseval frame, hence true for
\mathfrak{F}
. The induction step is established as follows. Suppose that the statement is true for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we have

\displaystyle W_{N+1}[\mathfrak{F}](f)=\sum_{\psi\in\mathfrak{F}}W_{N}[\mathfrak{F}](|f*\psi|)=\sum_{\psi\in\mathfrak{F}}\left\lVert\text{}|f*\psi|\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{\psi\in\mathfrak{F}}\left\lVert\text{}f*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

∎

Despite its simplicity and brevity, the argument illustrates how the underlying inductive structure of the scattering network can be exploited in proofs. The following Proposition (cf.
[
11
, Proposition 2.5]
) states the non-expansiveness of the operators
U
and
S
, which is also due to their inductive definition. Considering the desirable properties of the scattering transform as a feature extractor, this property can be considered as stability against additive noise.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
\emptyset\neq P\subseteq\Psi^{N}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then, for all
f,g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\text{}S[P;\chi]f-S[P;\chi]g\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(P;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq\left\lVert\text{}U[P]f-U[P]g\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(P;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq\left\lVert\text{}f-g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{S}[\mathfrak{F}]=S[\mathcal{P}_{\Psi};\chi]
is norm-preserving, then it is also nonexpansive.

Proof.

By the Littlewood-Paley condition (
3
), we have
\left\lVert\text{}\widehat{\chi}\text{}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1
. Thus, for all
p\in P
, and all
f,g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\left\lVert\text{}S[p;\chi]f-S[p;\chi]g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert\text{}(U[p]f-U[p]g)*\chi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert\text{}U[p]f-U[p]g\text{}\right\rVert_{2},

which proves the first inequality.

Observe that it suffices to prove the second inequality for the case
P=\Psi^{N}
, which we do by induction on
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The base case
N=0
is trivial. For the purpose of the induction step, suppose that the hypothesis is true for some
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
By the reverse triangle inequality, we have, for all
f,g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\lvert|U[\Psi^{N+1}]f-

\displaystyle U[\Psi^{N+1}]g\rvert|_{\ell^{2}(\Psi^{N+1};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\Psi^{N}}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\left\lVert\text{}|(U[p]f)*\psi|-|(U[p]g)*\psi|\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p\in\Psi^{N}}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\left\lVert\text{}|(U[p]f-U[p]g)*\psi|\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\Psi^{N}}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\left\lVert\text{}(U[p]f-U[p]g)*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\Psi^{N}}(\left\lVert\text{}U[p]f-U[p]g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-\left\lVert\text{}S[p;\chi]f-S[p;\chi]g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\text{}U[\Psi^{N}]f-U[\Psi^{N}]g\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\Psi^{N};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2}-\left\lVert\text{}S[\Psi^{N};\chi]f-S[\Psi^{N};\chi]g\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\Psi^{N};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2},

where the second last step is due to
\mathfrak{F}
being a frame. Applying the induction hypothesis to the right-hand side of the latter inequality concludes this part of the proof.

Finally, if
\mathcal{S}[\mathfrak{F}]
is norm-preserving, the non-expansiveness of
\mathcal{S}[\mathfrak{F}]
follows directly from the above estimates (which were derived independent of the induction hypothesis) using a telescoping series argument. In fact, by Proposition
1.3
, we have
\lim_{N\to\infty}W_{N}[\Psi](h)=0
for all
h\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, which entails that, for all
f,g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\lVert\text{}U[\Psi^{N}]f-U[\Psi^{N}]g\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\Psi^{N};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2}=0.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\lvert|\mathcal{S}[\mathfrak{F}]f-

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}[\mathfrak{F}]g\rvert|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{P}_{\Psi};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\left\lVert\text{}S[\Psi^{N};\chi]f-S[\Psi^{N};\chi]g\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\Psi^{N};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\left(\left\lVert\text{}U[\Psi^{N}]f-U[\Psi^{N}]g\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\Psi^{N};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2}-\left\lVert\text{}U[\Psi^{N+1}]f-U[\Psi^{N+1}]g\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\Psi^{N+1};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\text{}f-g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

∎

We exploit the non-expansiveness of
U
to derive some useful estimates concerning the energy remainder.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
\emptyset\neq\Phi\subseteq\Psi
, and let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, we have, for all
f,g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

a)

|W_{N}[\Phi](f)-W_{N}[\Phi](g)|\leq\sqrt{2}\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f-g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\cdot\sqrt{W_{N}[\Phi](f)+W_{N}[\Phi](g)}
,

b)

W_{N}[\Phi](f)\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot W_{N}[\Phi](g)-\left\lVert\text{}f-g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
f,g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Applying the reverse triangle inequality yields, for any
p\in\Phi^{N}
,

\displaystyle|\left\lVert\text{}U[p]f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-\left\lVert\text{}U[p]g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}|

\displaystyle=|\left\lVert\text{}U[p]f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}-\left\lVert\text{}U[p]g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}|\cdot(\left\lVert\text{}U[p]f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert\text{}U[p]g\text{}\right\rVert_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\text{}U[p]f-U[p]g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\cdot(\left\lVert\text{}U[p]f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert\text{}U[p]g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}).

Together with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the non-expansiveness of
U[\Phi^{N}]
, we obtain

\displaystyle|W_{N}[\Phi](f)-W_{N}[\Phi](g)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{p\in\Phi^{N}}\left\lVert\text{}U[p]f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-\left\lVert\text{}U[p]g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{p\in\Phi^{N}}\left\lVert\text{}U[p]f-U[p]g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\cdot\left(\left\lVert\text{}U[p]f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert\text{}U[p]g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{p\in\Phi^{N}}\left\lVert\text{}U[p]f-U[p]g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{p\in\Phi^{N}}\left(\left\lVert\text{}U[p]f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert\text{}U[p]g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\text{}f-g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\cdot\sum_{p\in\Phi^{N}}\left(2\left\lVert\text{}U[p]f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+2\left\lVert\text{}U[p]g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2\left\lVert\text{}f-g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\cdot(W_{N}[\Phi](f)+W_{N}[\Phi](g)).

Taking square roots on both sides of the latter inequality proves a).

We again use the non-expansiveness of
U[\Phi]
and find that

\displaystyle W_{N}[\Phi](g)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\text{}U[\Phi^{N}](g)-U[\Phi^{N}](f)+U[\Phi^{N}](f)\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\Phi^{N};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\lVert\text{}U[\Phi^{N}](g)-U[\Phi^{N}](f)\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\Phi^{N};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}+\left\lVert\text{}U[\Phi^{N}](f)\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\Phi^{N};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\lVert\text{}g-f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert\text{}U[\Phi^{N}](f)\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\Phi^{N};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert\text{}f-g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+2\left\lVert\text{}U[\Phi^{N}](f)\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\Phi^{N};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left\lVert\text{}f-g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+2W_{N}[\Phi](f).

By rearranging, we conclude the proof of b).
∎

It is now easy to derive the continuity of
W_{N}[\Phi]
, which we will use to prove the density result, Proposition
2.3
, in Section 2.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
\emptyset\neq\Phi\subseteq\Psi
, and let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,
W_{N}[\Phi]:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous.

Proof.

Continuity at
f=0
is obvious, since we have, for all
g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
W_{N}[\Phi](g)\leq\left\lVert\text{}g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. Now, fix
\varepsilon>0
and
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
. Define

\delta=\delta(f,\varepsilon):=\min\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{10}\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}},\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\right\}>0.

Applying Lemma
1.6
a) to
g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\left\lVert\text{}f-g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}<\delta
gives

\displaystyle|W_{N}[\Phi](f)-W_{N}[\Phi](g)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert\text{}f-g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\cdot(W_{N}[\Phi](f)+W_{N}[\Phi](g))

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta^{2}\cdot(\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert\text{}g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta^{2}\cdot(\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+(\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}+\delta)^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 10\delta^{2}\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2}.

∎

We conclude this section with a lemma describing the interplay of the scattering propagator with dilations, which is similar to covariance (cf.
[
11
, Equation (20)]
). Despite its elementary proof, this property forms the basis for our main result in Section
2
.

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
p\in(L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))^{N}
, and let
A\in\GL_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Then, for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle U[p]D_{A}^{1}f=D_{A}^{1}U[D_{A^{-1}}^{1}p]f,

where
D_{A^{-1}}^{1}p:=(D_{A^{-1}}^{1}p_{1},\ldots,D_{A^{-1}}^{1}p_{N})
.
Moreover, if
\emptyset\neq\Phi\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
, then we have, for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

W_{N}[\Phi](D_{A}^{2}f)=W_{N}[D_{A^{-1}}^{1}\Phi](f).

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and let
g\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. A direct computation shows that

(D_{A}^{1}f)*g=D_{A}^{1}(f*(D_{A^{-1}}^{1}g)).

The modulus operator

M:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\quad h\mapsto|h|

commutes with
D_{A}^{1}
, which proves
U[g]D_{A}^{1}f=D_{A}^{1}U[D_{A^{-1}}^{1}g]f
. Iterating this relation along the path
p
concludes the first identity.

Concerning the second, we note that
D_{A}^{1}=|\det(A)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot D_{A}^{2}
, which gives
U[p]D_{A}^{2}=D_{A}^{2}U[D_{A^{-1}}^{1}p]
. Since
D_{A}^{2}
is unitary on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, summing over all paths in
\Phi^{N}
entails the second identity.
∎

2.
Slow Scattering Propagation

In this section, we show that energy propagation can be arbitrarily slow in scattering networks that employ filters
\mathfrak{F}=\{\chi\}\cup\Psi
with an underlying structure similar to those generated by wavelets.

As some of the details of this section are quite technical, we begin by illustrating the conceptual idea behind them. In light of this, let us consider the situation from Example
1.2
again, i.e., suppose that the high-pass filters
\Psi=\{\psi_{j}\penalty\ |\penalty\ j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\}
are generated by dilations of
\psi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
,
\supp(\widehat{\psi})\subseteq[\frac{1}{2},2]
, according to

\displaystyle\psi_{j}:=\begin{cases}D^{1}_{2^{j}}\psi=2^{j}\psi(2^{j}\>\cdot)&\text{for }j\in\mathbb{N}\\
D^{1}_{2^{|j|}}\psi(-\>\cdot)=2^{|j|}\psi(-2^{|j|}\>\cdot)&\text{for }j\in\mathbb{Z}_{<0}\end{cases}.

We want to show that for any nonincreasing null-sequence
E\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
, there exists a function
f_{E}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
that satisfies the following conditions:

a)

We have
\left\lVert\text{}f_{E}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=E_{1}
.

b)

For all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
W_{N}[\Psi](f)\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot E_{N}
.

Proof sketch.

Define

(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}:=\left(\sqrt{E_{k}-E_{k+1}}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}},

and note that a telescoping argument gives

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}^{2}=\lim_{N\to\infty}E_{1}-E_{N+1}=E_{1}.

Fix any
f_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
that satisfies
\left\lVert\text{}f_{0}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}=1
and
\supp(\widehat{f_{0}})\subseteq[1,2]
. We will later (see Corollary
2.8
and Corollary
4.7
) show that, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lim_{m\to\infty}W_{N}\left(f_{m}\right)=1,

where
f_{m}:=2^{m/2}f_{0}(2^{m}\,\cdot\,)
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. This is a consequence of Lemma
1.8
.

Therefore, we can find a strictly increasing sequence
(m_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
m_{k+1}\geq 2+m_{k}
and
W_{k}(f_{m_{k}})\geq\frac{1}{2}
. We claim that

f_{E}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}f_{m_{k}}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})

does the job. To this end, first note that, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\supp(\widehat{f_{m}})\subseteq[2^{m},2^{m+1}]
. In particular, the
(f_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
are orthonormal, hence
\left\lVert\text{}f_{E}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}=E_{1}
. Moreover, since

\supp(\widehat{\psi_{j}})\subseteq[\sgn(j)\cdot 2^{|j|-1},\sgn(j)\cdot 2^{|j|+1}],

we have

\displaystyle f_{E}*\psi_{j}=\begin{cases}a_{k}f_{m_{k}}*\psi_{m_{k}}&\text{if }j=m_{k}\text{ for some }k\in\mathbb{N}\\
a_{k}f_{m_{k}}*\psi_{m_{k}+1}&\text{if }j=m_{k}+1\text{ for some }k\in\mathbb{N}\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases},\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}.

Altogether, we obtain, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle W_{N}(f_{E})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}W_{N-1}(|f_{E}*\psi_{j}|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(W_{N-1}(|a_{k}f_{m_{k}}*\psi_{m_{k}}|)+W_{N-1}(|a_{k}f_{m_{k}}*\psi_{m_{k}+1}|)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}\cdot\left(W_{N-1}(|f_{m_{k}}*\psi_{m_{k}}|)+W_{N-1}(|f_{m_{k}}*\psi_{m_{k}+1}|)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}\cdot W_{N}(f_{m_{k}})\geq\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}\cdot W_{k}(f_{m_{k}})\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot E_{N}.

\square

To summarize, the central idea of the proof is as follows: Given
\delta>0
, construct a sequence of signals
(f_{m_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
such that (i) the energy remainder
W_{N}
behaves (super-)additively due to separation in the frequency domain and (ii) the energy decays slowly for
f_{m_{k}}
across the first
k
layers of the scattering network, according to
W_{k}(f_{m_{k}})\geq\delta
. Weighting the signals with an
\ell^{2}
-sequence ensures square-integrability of
f_{E}
. The energy that is contained in the scattering layers of
f_{E}
of depth greater than
N-1
(i.e.,
W_{N}(f_{E})
) is controlled by the weighted energy remainders of the signals
(f_{m_{k}})_{k\geq N}
. This results in a slow propagation of energy across all network layers. The speed of convergence is bounded from below by the speed of convergence of the
\ell^{2}
-series of the chosen weights.

In order to generalize this idea to larger classes of filter banks and higher dimensions, we need to establish a much more general version of super-additivity for the family of nonlinear operators
(W_{N}:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to{\mathbb{R}})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
. In the proof sketch above, we have implicitly used their additivity on separated signals (cf.
[
13
, Lemma 2.3]
). However, if the generator
\psi
fails to be bandlimited, we cannot generally expect exact additivity on separated signals. Moreover, we would like to allow for more flexibility in the choice of our filter bank. The following lemma establishes an
approximate
version of super-additivity for the operators
(W_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
if the signals are nearly separated in Fourier domain. We use the high-pass filters
\Psi
to measure the degree of separation.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
F=(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subset L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, let
(\eta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, and let
a=(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0};{\mathbb{C}})
. Assume that there exist finite sets
\Psi(F,k)\subseteq\Psi
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, such that the following hold:

(i)

The series
\sum_{k,j=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}a_{j}\left<\text{}\,f_{k},f_{j}\,\text{}\right>|
converges.

(ii)

The series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}
converges, where
\varepsilon_{k}:=(k+1)\cdot\eta_{k}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\eta_{j}
.

(iii)

The sets
\Psi(F,k)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, are pairwise disjoint.

(iv)

We have, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in\Psi\setminus\Psi(F,k)}\left\lVert\text{}f_{k}*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\eta_{k}.

(v)

We have, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
0\leq j<k
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}\left\lVert\text{}f_{j}*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\eta_{k}.

Then, the series

f:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}f_{k}

converges in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{k,j=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}a_{j}\left<\text{}\,f_{k},f_{j}\,\text{}\right>|
, and we have, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

(10)

\displaystyle W_{N}\left(f\right)\geq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|a_{k}|^{2}}{2}\cdot W_{N}(f_{k})-2\left\lVert\text{}a\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})}^{2}\cdot\varepsilon_{k}\right).

Moreover, suppose that in addition to our standing assumptions (i)-(v) there exists
\delta>0
such that

(vi)

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

W_{k}(f_{k})\geq 4\delta.

(vii)

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\varepsilon_{k}\leq\frac{\delta\cdot|a_{k}|^{2}}{2\left\lVert\text{}a\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})}^{2}}.

Then we even have, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

(11)

\displaystyle W_{N}(f)\geq\delta\cdot\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}.

Proof.

The series defining
f
converges if

\left\lVert\text{}\sum_{k=n}^{m}a_{k}f_{k}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\xrightarrow{n,m\to\infty}0.

Expanding the latter norm squared yields

(12)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\text{}\sum_{k=n}^{m}a_{k}f_{k}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{k,j=n}^{m}a_{k}\overline{a_{j}}\cdot\left<\text{}\,f_{k},f_{j}\,\text{}\right>\leq\sum_{k,j=n}^{\infty}|a_{k}a_{j}\cdot\left<\text{}\,f_{k},f_{j}\,\text{}\right>|.

Being the remainder of a convergent series by assumption (i), the right-hand side of (
12
) converges to
0
, so that
f
is in fact a well-defined element of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Setting
n=0
in (
12
), we also obtain the claimed bound for the (squared) norm of
f
as
m\to\infty
.

Let us now turn to the proof of (
10
). Here, we first note that the recursive nature of
W_{N}
and Lemma
1.6
b) imply

\displaystyle W_{N}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}W_{N-1}(|f*\psi|)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}W_{N-1}(|f*\psi|)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}\left(\frac{1}{2}\cdot W_{N-1}(|(a_{k}f_{k})*\psi|)-\left\lVert\text{}|f*\psi|-|(a_{k}f_{k})*\psi|\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}\left(\frac{|a_{k}|^{2}}{2}\cdot W_{N-1}(|f_{k}*\psi|)-\left\lVert\text{}(f-a_{k}f_{k})*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

(13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|a_{k}|^{2}}{2}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}W_{N-1}(|f_{k}*\psi|)-\text{Rest}_{k}\right).

As a consequence of our assumptions (iii)-(v), the remainder is bounded by

\displaystyle\text{Rest}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}\left\lVert\text{}\left(\sum_{j=0,j\neq k}^{\infty}a_{j}f_{j}\right)*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}\left(\sum_{j=0,j\neq k}^{\infty}|a_{j}|\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f_{j}*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}\left[\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}|a_{j}|\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f_{j}*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}|a_{j}|\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f_{j}*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert\text{}a\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})}^{2}\cdot\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left\lVert\text{}f_{j}*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\left\lVert\text{}f_{j}*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert\text{}a\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})}^{2}\cdot\left(k\cdot\eta_{k}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\eta_{j}\right).

Moreover, for every
k\geq n
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}W_{N-1}(|f_{k}*\psi|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}W_{N-1}(|f_{k}*\psi|)-\sum_{\psi\in\Psi\setminus\Psi(F,k)}W_{N-1}(|f_{k}*\psi|)

\displaystyle=W_{N}(f_{k})-\sum_{\psi\in\Psi\setminus\Psi(F,k)}W_{N-1}(|f_{k}*\psi|)

\displaystyle\geq W_{N}(f_{k})-\eta_{k}.

Substituting this and the bound for
\text{Rest}_{k}
into (
2
) yields

\displaystyle W_{N}(f)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|a_{k}|^{2}}{2}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}W_{N-1}(|f_{k}*\psi|)-\text{Rest}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\left[\frac{|a_{k}|^{2}}{2}\cdot\left(W_{N}(f_{k})-\eta_{k}\right)-2\left\lVert\text{}a\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})}^{2}\cdot\left(k\cdot\eta_{k}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\eta_{j}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\left[\frac{|a_{k}|^{2}}{2}\cdot W_{N}(f_{k})-2\left\lVert\text{}a\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})}^{2}\cdot\left((k+1)\cdot\eta_{k}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\eta_{j}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|a_{k}|^{2}}{2}\cdot W_{N}(f_{k})-2\left\lVert\text{}a\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})}^{2}\cdot\varepsilon_{k}\right).

Finally, let us derive (
11
) from (
10
) under the additional assumptions (vi) and (vii). Choosing
n=N
in (
10
) and exploiting the upper bound for
\varepsilon_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we find that

(14)

\displaystyle W_{N}(f)\geq\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|a_{k}|^{2}}{2}\cdot W_{N}(f_{k})-2\left\lVert\text{}a\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})}^{2}\cdot\varepsilon_{k}\right)\geq\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|a_{k}|^{2}}{2}\cdot W_{N}(f_{k})-\delta\cdot|a_{k}|^{2}\right).

Since
W_{N}(f)
is nonincreasing in
N
for fixed
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we have, for all
k\geq N
,

W_{N}(f_{k})\geq W_{k}(f_{k})\geq 4\delta.

Substituting this into (
14
) concludes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to state and prove a first theorem about arbitrarily slow energy propagation in scattering networks. This is a generalization of the idea outlined at the beginning of this section.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
F=(f_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subset L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Suppose that for all tolerances
(\eta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
there exist finite sets
\Psi(F,k)\subseteq\Psi
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and a subsequence
(f_{m_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of
F
with
m_{0}=0
and such that the following hold:

(i)

The expression
C_{F}:=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left\lVert\text{}f_{m}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
is finite, i.e.,
F
is a bounded sequence.

(ii)

We have, for all
j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
j<k
,

\displaystyle|\left<\text{}\,f_{m_{k}},f_{m_{j}}\,\text{}\right>|\leq\eta_{k}.

(iii)

The sets
\Psi(F,k)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, are pairwise disjoint.

(iv)

We have, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in\Psi\setminus\Psi(F,k)}\left\lVert\text{}f_{m_{k}}*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\eta_{k}.

(v)

We have, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
0\leq j<k
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}\left\lVert\text{}f_{m_{j}}*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\eta_{k}.

(vi)

There exists
\delta>0
such that, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
W_{k}(f_{m_{k}})\geq 4\delta
.

If
E=(E_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
is a nonincreasing null-sequence, then there exists
f_{E}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
that satisfies

a)

\left\lVert\text{}f_{E}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq 2C_{F}\cdot(1+E_{1})
,

b)

\left\lVert\text{}f_{E}-f_{0}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq 2C_{F}\cdot E_{1}
, and

c)

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
W_{N}(f_{E})\geq\delta\cdot E_{N}
.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}:=\left(\sqrt{E_{k}-E_{k+1}}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}},

and set
a_{0}:=1
. A telescoping argument gives, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}=\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}(E_{k}-E_{k+1})=E_{N}.

In particular,
a=(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0};{\mathbb{C}})
with
\left\lVert\text{}a\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0};{\mathbb{C}})}^{2}=1+E_{1}
.

We want to apply Lemma
2.1
to a subsequence
(f_{m_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of
F
, which implies that
f_{E}
can be chosen of the type

f_{E}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}f_{m_{k}}.

Here,
(f_{m_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
will be the subsequence of
F
from the prerequisites of this theorem that depends on tolerance levels
(\eta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, which we will now specify.

Recall from Lemma
2.1
that
f_{E}
converges in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with an upper bound for its squared norm given by
\left\lVert\text{}f_{E}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{k,j=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}a_{j}\cdot\left<\text{}\,f_{m_{k}},f_{m_{j}}\,\text{}\right>|
. Likewise,

\left\lVert\text{}f_{E}-f_{0}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\left\lVert\text{}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}f_{m_{k}}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{k,j=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}a_{j}\cdot\left<\text{}\,f_{m_{k}},f_{m_{j}}\,\text{}\right>|.

We will bound the latter series using the approximate orthogonality relation (ii). Repeatedly applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k,j=n}^{\infty}

\displaystyle|a_{k}a_{j}\cdot\left<\text{}\,f_{m_{k}},f_{m_{j}}\,\text{}\right>|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}|a_{k}|\cdot\left(\sum_{j=n}^{k-1}|a_{j}|\cdot\underbrace{|\left<\text{}\,f_{m_{k}},f_{m_{j}}\,\text{}\right>|}_{\leq\eta_{k}}+|a_{k}|\cdot\underbrace{|\left<\text{}\,f_{m_{k}},f_{m_{k}}\,\text{}\right>|}_{\leq C_{F}}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}|a_{j}|\cdot\underbrace{|\left<\text{}\,f_{m_{k}},f_{m_{j}}\,\text{}\right>|}_{\leq\eta_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{F}\cdot\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}+\left(\sum_{j=n,j\neq k}^{\infty}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}|a_{k}|\cdot\left[(k-n)\cdot\eta_{k}^{2}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\eta_{j}^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{F}\cdot\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}+\left(\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\left[(k-n)\cdot\eta_{k}^{2}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\eta_{j}^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus, if we choose the tolerances
(\eta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
small enough such that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[k\cdot\eta_{k}^{2}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\eta_{j}^{2}\right]\leq C_{F}^{2},

then we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k,j=n}^{\infty}|a_{k}a_{j}\cdot\left<\text{}\,f_{m_{k}},f_{m_{j}}\,\text{}\right>|\leq 2C_{F}\cdot\left(\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)=\begin{cases}2C_{F}\cdot(1+E_{1})&\text{ if }n=0\\
2C_{F}\cdot E_{1}&\text{ if }n=1\end{cases}.

This proves the norm bounds in a) and b).

As in Lemma
2.1
, define
\varepsilon_{k}:=(k+1)\cdot\eta_{k}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\eta_{j}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Since the tolerances
(\eta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
are free to choose, we can additionally assume
that the series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}
converges and that it holds, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\varepsilon_{k}\leq\frac{\delta\cdot|a_{k}|^{2}}{2\left\lVert\text{}a\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{C}})}^{2}}.

By applying Lemma
2.1
, we conclude that, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

W_{N}(f_{E})\geq\delta\cdot\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}=\delta\cdot E_{N}.

∎

Under certain assumptions on the high-pass filters
\Psi
, the latter theorem guarantees that for any nonincreasing null-sequence
E=(E_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
there exists at least one input signal
f_{E}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
whose network energy propagates slower across the network layers than
E
. Specifically, this implies that the set

\left\{f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ W_{N}(f)\notin\mathcal{O}(E_{N})\right\}

is nonempty. Our next result provides a sufficient criterion under which this set is even dense in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
E=(E_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be a nonincreasing null-sequence. Assume that for all
g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
there are
C_{g},\delta_{g}>0
such that the following holds:
For every nonincreasing null-sequence
E^{\prime}=(E_{N}^{\prime})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
with
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\nicefrac{{E_{N}}}{{E_{N}^{\prime}}}=0
there exists
f_{g,E^{\prime}}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that

(i)

\left\lVert\text{}f_{g,E^{\prime}}-g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq C_{g}\cdot E_{1}^{\prime}
, and

(ii)

W_{N}(f_{g,E^{\prime}})\geq\delta_{g}\cdot E_{N}^{\prime}
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Then,

Y_{E}:=\left\{f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ W_{N}(f)\in\mathcal{O}(E_{N})\right\}

is a countable union of nowhere dense sets in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. In particular,
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\setminus Y_{E}
is dense in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proof.

Define, for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
,

M_{p}:=\left\{f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \forall N\in\mathbb{N}:\penalty\ W_{N}(f)\leq p\cdot E_{N}\right\},

and observe that
Y_{E}
can be written as countable union of these sets,

Y_{E}=\bigcup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}M_{p}.

Our goal is to show that
M_{p}
is nowhere dense in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for every
p\in\mathbb{N}
. To this end, let us first recall from Corollary
1.7
that the nonlinear operator
W_{N}:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous. Thus,

M_{p}=\bigcap_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ W_{N}(f)\leq p\cdot E_{N}\right\}

is closed and it only remains to show that the interior of
M_{p}
is empty:
Fix
g\in M_{p}
,
\varepsilon>0
, and define

\displaystyle E_{N}^{\prime}:=\begin{cases}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2C_{g}}&\text{if }N=1\\
E_{1}^{\prime}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{E_{N}}{E_{1}}}&\text{if }N\geq 2\end{cases},\quad\text{for }N\in\mathbb{N}.

Clearly,
E^{\prime}=(E_{N}^{\prime})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
is a nonincreasing null-sequence that satisfies
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\nicefrac{{E_{N}}}{{E_{N}^{\prime}}}=0
. Hence, there is
f_{g,E^{\prime}}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
that satisfies (i) and (ii) from the assumptions of this lemma.
Thus, there is
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

W_{N_{0}}(f_{g,E^{\prime}})\geq\delta_{g}\cdot E_{N_{0}}^{\prime}>p\cdot E_{N_{0}},

which means

f_{g,E^{\prime}}\in B_{\varepsilon}(g)\setminus M_{p},

where
B_{\varepsilon}(g)
denotes the open ball with radius
\varepsilon
and center
g
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with respect to
\left\lVert\text{}\cdot\text{}\right\rVert_{2}
.
Since this entails that the interior of
M_{p}
is empty, we have shown in total that
Y_{E}
is a countable union of nowhere dense sets in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Therefore, its complement
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\setminus Y_{E}

is dense in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by the Baire category theorem.
∎

For the remainder of this section, we will be examining the assumptions of Theorem
2.2
and Proposition
2.3
. While the number of conditions on the family
F
and the high-pass filters
\Psi
that must be met simultaneously for our results to apply is considerable, we will demonstrate that these conditions can in fact be easily satisfied. To provide a concrete reference model, let us next establish that
F
can be obtained by
L^{2}
-normalized dilations of a single function.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. If
(A_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\in\GL_{d}({\mathbb{R}})^{\mathbb{N}}

satisfies
\lim_{m\to\infty}\sigma_{\text{min}}(A_{m})=\infty
, then

\lim_{m\to\infty}\left|\left<\text{}\,f,D_{A_{m}}^{2}f\,\text{}\right>\right|=0.

Proof.

The statement is obvious if
f=0
. For the other case, fix
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
and
\varepsilon>0
. By Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem there is
R>0
so that
f_{R}:=f\cdot\mathds{1}_{B_{R}(0)}
satisfies

\left\lVert\text{}f-f_{R}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}}.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, splitting the inner product gives

(15)

\displaystyle\left|\left<\text{}\,f,D_{A_{m}}^{2}f\,\text{}\right>\right|\leq\left|\left<\text{}\,f,D_{A_{m}}^{2}f_{R}\,\text{}\right>\right|+\left|\left<\text{}\,f,D_{A_{m}}^{2}(f-f_{R})\,\text{}\right>\right|

We obtain an upper bound for the second term by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

(16)

\displaystyle\left|\left<\text{}\,f,D_{A_{m}}^{2}(f-f_{R})\,\text{}\right>\right|\leq\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\cdot\left\lVert\text{}D_{A_{m}}^{2}(f-f_{R})\text{}\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f-f_{R}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

We now turn our attention to the first term in (
15
). As can be seen from a singular value decomposition of
A_{m}
, we have, for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\text{}A_{m}x\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\geq\sigma_{\text{min}}(A_{m})\cdot\left\lVert\text{}x\text{}\right\rVert_{2}.

Since by assumption
\lim_{m\to\infty}\sigma_{\text{min}}(A_{m})=\infty
, we find that, for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{B_{R}(0)}(A_{m}x)\leq\mathds{1}_{B_{\frac{R}{\sigma_{\text{min}}(A_{m})}}(0)}(x)\xrightarrow{m\to\infty}0.

Hence, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem there is
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that, for all
m\geq m_{0}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\text{}f\cdot\mathds{1}_{B_{\frac{R}{\sigma_{\text{min}}(A_{m})}}(0)}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}}.

Thus, for all
m\geq m_{0}
,

\displaystyle\left|\left<\text{}\,f,D_{A_{m}}^{2}f_{R}\,\text{}\right>\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f(x)|\cdot|D_{A_{m}}^{2}f(x)|\cdot\mathds{1}_{B_{R}(0)}(A_{m}x)\>\mathrm{d}\,x\>

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f(x)|\cdot|D_{A_{m}}^{2}f(x)|\cdot\mathds{1}_{B_{\frac{R}{\sigma_{\text{min}}(A_{m})}}(0)}(x)\>\mathrm{d}\,x\>

(17)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\text{}f\cdot\mathds{1}_{B_{\frac{R}{\sigma_{\text{min}}(A_{m})}}(0)}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\cdot\left\lVert\text{}D_{A_{m}}^{2}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Plugging (
16
) and (
2
) into (
15
) concludes the proof.
∎

The following lemma shows that
F
satisfies assumptions (i)–(v) from Theorem
2.2
if it is generated by
L^{2}
-normalized matrix-dilations of a fixed function for matrices that asymptotically become increasingly expansive.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(A_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\in\GL_{d}({\mathbb{R}})^{\mathbb{N}}
be so that
\lim_{m\to\infty}\sigma_{\text{min}}(A_{m})=\infty
. Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and define
f_{m}:=D_{A_{m}}^{2}f
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Finally, choose
f_{0}\in\{0,f\}
.

Then,
F=(f_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
satisfies assumptions (i)–(v) from Theorem
2.2
.

Proof.

The statement is trivial if
f=0
. Hence, suppose
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
in the following. Furthermore, assumptions (i)-(v) of Theorem
2.2
are clearly easier to meet if we are in the case that
f_{0}=0
(instead of
f_{0}=f
). Therefore, we will only spell out the proof for
f_{0}=f
. For notational convenience, we set
A_{0}:=I_{d}
, so we can write
f_{0}=D_{A_{0}}^{2}f
.

By the normalization of the dilations,
C_{F}:=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left\lVert\text{}f_{m}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
is finite, i.e., assumption (i) of Theorem
2.2
is satisfied. We now have to show that for all tolerances
(\eta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
there exist finite sets
\Psi(F,k)\subseteq\Psi
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and a subsequence
(f_{m_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of
F
with
m_{0}=0
and such that assumptions (ii)-(v) of Theorem
2.2
hold.
We proceed by choosing the sets
\Psi(F,k)\subseteq\Psi
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
({m_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
inductively.

Starting with
k=0
and
m_{0}=0
, we only need to show that condition (iv) can be satisfied. Since

\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\left\lVert\text{}f_{m_{0}}*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}<\infty,

there is a finite subset
\Psi(F,0)\subset\Psi
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in\Psi\setminus\Psi(F,0)}\left\lVert\text{}f_{m_{0}}*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\eta_{0}.

For the induction step, let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and assume that
\Psi(F,0),\ldots,\Psi(F,k-1)
and
m_{0},\ldots,m_{k-1}
are such that (ii)-(v) hold. Our goal is now to obtain
\Psi(F,k)
and
m_{k}
with the desired properties.

It is easy to meet condition (ii): For all
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
0\leq j<k
, we have

\displaystyle|\left<\text{}\,f_{m},f_{m_{j}}\,\text{}\right>|=\left|\left<\text{}\,D_{A_{m_{j}}^{-1}}^{2}D_{A_{m}}^{2}f,f\,\text{}\right>\right|=\left|\left<\text{}\,D_{A_{m}A_{m_{j}}^{-1}}^{2}f,f\,\text{}\right>\right|.

Moreover,

\sigma_{\text{min}}\left(A_{m}A_{m_{j}}^{-1}\right)\geq\sigma_{\text{min}}\left(A_{m}\right)\cdot\sigma_{\text{min}}\left(A_{m_{j}}^{-1}\right)=\frac{\sigma_{\text{min}}\left(A_{m}\right)}{\sigma_{\text{max}}\left(A_{m_{j}}\right)}\geq\frac{\sigma_{\text{min}}\left(A_{m}\right)}{\max_{0\leq l<k}\sigma_{\text{max}}\left(A_{m_{l}}\right)}.

Since the latter bound is uniformly in
j
for
0\leq j<k
, and
\lim_{m\to\infty}\sigma_{\text{min}}\left(A_{m}\right)=\infty
by assumption, Lemma
2.4
implies that there exists
m_{k}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
|\left<\text{}\,f_{m_{k}},f_{m_{j}}\,\text{}\right>|<\eta_{k}
holds for all
j\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}
whenever we choose
m_{k}
larger than
m_{k}^{\prime}
.

The harder part is to satisfy the remaining conditions (iii)-(v) simultaneously.
We begin with a simple observation about the impact of matrix-dilations on the frequency content of
f
.
For
R>r>0
, let

P_{r,R}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}(f)\cdot\mathds{1}_{S_{r,R}})

be the projection of
f
on the subspace of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
whose Fourier transform is supported in
S_{r,R}
. We introduce
\varepsilon\in(0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})
, which will be chosen later in the proof (small enough). By Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem,
\widehat{f}
is concentrated in a spherical shell
S_{r(\varepsilon),R(\varepsilon)}
up to
\varepsilon
-error for some
R(\varepsilon)>r(\varepsilon)>0
, meaning that

\left\lVert\text{}P_{r(\varepsilon),R(\varepsilon)}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Now, if
A\in\GL_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
, then
\widehat{D_{A}^{2}f}
is concentrated in
S_{\sigma_{\text{min}}(A)\cdot r(\varepsilon),\sigma_{\text{max}}(A)\cdot R(\varepsilon)}
up to
\varepsilon
-error, since

\displaystyle\left\lVert\text{}P_{\sigma_{\text{min}}(A)\cdot r(\varepsilon),\sigma_{\text{max}}(A)\cdot R(\varepsilon)}D_{A}^{2}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\text{}\widehat{D_{A}^{2}f}\cdot\mathds{1}_{S_{\sigma_{\text{min}}(A)\cdot r(\varepsilon),\sigma_{\text{max}}(A)\cdot R(\varepsilon)}}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\det(A)|^{-1}\cdot|\widehat{f}(A^{-T}\xi)|^{2}\cdot\mathds{1}_{S_{\sigma_{\text{min}}(A)\cdot r(\varepsilon),\sigma_{\text{max}}(A)\cdot R(\varepsilon)}}(\xi)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(z)|^{2}\cdot\mathds{1}_{S_{\sigma_{\text{min}}(A)\cdot r(\varepsilon),\sigma_{\text{max}}(A)\cdot R(\varepsilon)}}(A^{T}z)\>\mathrm{d}\,z\>

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(z)|^{2}\cdot\mathds{1}_{S_{r(\varepsilon),R(\varepsilon)}}(z)\>\mathrm{d}\,z\>

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\text{}\mathcal{F}P_{r(\varepsilon),R(\varepsilon)}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Specifically, setting

\displaystyle r_{<k}:=\min_{0\leq j<k}\sigma_{\text{min}}(A_{m_{j}})\cdot r(\varepsilon)\quad\text{and}\quad R_{<k}:=\max_{0\leq j<k}\sigma_{\text{max}}(A_{m_{j}})\cdot R(\varepsilon),

the above frequency concentration estimate entails

\displaystyle\left\lVert\text{}(\id_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}-P_{r_{<k},R_{<k}})f_{m_{j}}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\quad\text{for }0\leq j<k.

By the Littlewood-Paley condition (
3
) and the continuity of the Fourier transform of the filters, there is a finite subset
\widetilde{\Psi}\subseteq\Psi
containing
\bigcup_{j=0}^{k-1}\Psi(F,j)
, and such that

(18)

\displaystyle|\widehat{\chi}(\xi)|^{2}+\sum_{\psi\in\widetilde{\Psi}}|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}>1-\varepsilon\quad\text{ for all }\xi\in S_{r_{<k},R_{<k}}.

By the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma, there exists

such that

(19)

\displaystyle|\widehat{\chi}(\xi)|^{2}+\sum_{\psi\in\widetilde{\Psi}}|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}<\varepsilon\quad\text{ whenever }|\xi|\geq T.

Since
\lim_{m\to\infty}\sigma_{\text{min}}\left(A_{m}\right)=\infty
by assumption, there is
m_{k}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
with
\sigma_{\text{min}}(A_{m})\cdot r(\varepsilon)\geq T
for all

.
Define

,

, and

. Thus,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\text{}(\id_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}-P_{r_{k},R_{k}})f_{m_{k}}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Moreover, because of (

), the Littlewood-Paley condition (
3
), and the continuity of the Fourier transform of the filters, there exists a finite subset

so that

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k,\varepsilon)}|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}>1-\varepsilon\quad\text{ for all }\xi\in S_{r_{k},R_{k}},

which itself entails

(20)

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in\Psi\setminus\Psi(F,k,\varepsilon)}|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}<\varepsilon\quad\text{ for all }\xi\in S_{r_{k},R_{k}}.

At the same time, (

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k,\varepsilon)}|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}<\varepsilon\quad\text{ for all }\xi\in S_{r_{<k},R_{<k}}.

We claim that
\Psi(F,k):=\Psi(F,k,\varepsilon)
with
\varepsilon:=\frac{\eta_{k}}{2\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}
does the job.

In fact, first note that conditions (i)-(iii) are immediately satisfied by the above construction. Concerning condition (iv), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in\Psi\setminus\Psi(F,k)}\left\lVert\text{}f_{m_{k}}*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\psi\in\Psi\setminus\Psi(F,k)}\left\lVert\text{}(P_{r_{k},R_{k}}f_{m_{k}})*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\sum_{\psi\in\Psi\setminus\Psi(F,k)}\left\lVert\text{}((\id_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}-P_{r_{k},R_{k}})f_{m_{k}})*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f_{m_{k}}}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\sum_{\psi\in\Psi\setminus\Psi(F,k)}|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\mathds{1}_{S_{r_{k},R_{k}}}(\xi)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>+\left\lVert\text{}(\id_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}-P_{r_{k},R_{k}})f_{m_{k}}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\cdot\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f_{m_{k}}}(\xi)|^{2}\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>+\varepsilon\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f_{m_{k}}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\eta_{k}.

Finally, we obtain condition (v) by proceeding analogously to the previous step. Indeed, for
0\leq j<k
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}\left\lVert\text{}f_{m_{j}}*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}\left\lVert\text{}(P_{r_{<k},R_{<k}}f_{m_{j}})*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}\left\lVert\text{}((\id_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}-P_{r_{<k},R_{<k}})f_{m_{j}})*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f_{m_{j}}}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\sum_{\psi\in\Psi(F,k)}|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\mathds{1}_{S_{r_{<k},R_{<k}}}(\xi)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>+\left\lVert\text{}(\id_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}-P_{r_{<k},R_{<k}})f_{m_{j}}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\cdot\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f_{m_{j}}}(\xi)|^{2}\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>+\varepsilon\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f_{m_{j}}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\eta_{k}.

∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let

be so that
\lim_{m\to\infty}\sigma_{\text{min}}(A_{m})=\infty

\delta:=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\\
\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}=1\end{subarray}}\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\limsup_{m\to\infty}W_{k}(D_{A_{m}}^{2}f)>0.

Then, for every

there exists a universal constant

with the following property:

For any nonincreasing null-sequence
E=(E_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
there is

such that

a)

\left\lVert\text{}f_{g,E}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq C_{g}\cdot(1+E_{1})
,

b)

\left\lVert\text{}f_{g,E}-g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq C_{g}\cdot E_{1}
, and

for all

.

Furthermore,

Y_{E}:=\left\{f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ W_{N}(f)\in\mathcal{O}(E_{N})\right\}

is a countable union of nowhere dense sets in

is dense in

.

Proof.

Let us first assume that

. Define

for

, where

. From the assumption that

, we conclude that there exists a strictly increasing sequence

For

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
(A_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\in\GL_{d}({\mathbb{R}})^{\mathbb{N}}
be such that the following are true:

(i)

We have

(ii)

Proof.

By the Littlewood-Paley condition (

On the other hand, as a consequence of

\displaystyle W_{k}[\Psi](D_{A_{m}}^{2}f)=W_{k}[D_{A_{m}^{-1}}^{1}\Psi](f)=W_{k}[\Psi_{m}](f).

3.
Convergence Rates for Scattering Propagation

In this section, we show that under mild analyticity assumptions on the scattering filters
\Psi
, fast energy propagation can still be achieved for large signal classes in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. These signal classes depend on the required speed of energy propagation, i.e., as one would expect, the faster the energy propagation, the smaller the signal class we can
guarantee
this speed for. This is reflected by an interplay between the frequency localization of the filters in terms of size and shape of their Fourier supports, and the decay behavior of the Fourier transform of the input signal relative to the filters.

Notably, our results in this section are very general,

•

since they hold in any dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}
;

•

because our assumptions can be easily satisfied;

•

as the setup provides several parameters that can be used for fine-tuning.

In particular, we will see in Section
4.2
that these results apply to filter banks generated by bandlimited wavelets, thereby complementing the negative results from the previous section about arbitrarily slow energy propagation in such scattering networks.

Our analysis begins with a similar approach to that of
[
18
, Theorem 3.1]
and
[
20
, Theorem 1, Theorem 2]
, establishing an upper bound on
W_{N}[\Psi](f)
, for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
N\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
, which is of the type

(22)

\displaystyle W_{N}[\Psi](f)\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot K_{N}(\xi)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

for a family
(K_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}}
of integral kernels (independent of
f
) that satisfy additional properties. In fact, we will partially combine the techniques from
[
18
,
20
]
in the proof of Theorem
3.5
, which allows for more flexibility in our assumptions on the filters. On this basis, we will then exploit the inductive structure of the scattering network to involve the filters
\Psi
in right-hand side of (
22
).

To clarify our assumptions for this section, we need to introduce some additional notation. Let
\nu^{*}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
be the vector with entries
\nu_{k}^{*}:=\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{d}}}
for each
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. For
\rho\in[0,1)
, we denote by

H^{\rho}:=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{d}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \left<\text{}\,x,\nu^{*}\,\text{}\right>\geq(1-\rho)\cdot\left\lVert\text{}x\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\right\}

the closed cone in the first canonical orthant with its tip at the origin and opening angle relative to
\nu^{*}
parameterized by
\rho
. Note that
H^{\rho}
is precisely the first canonical orthant
{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{d}
if
\rho\geq 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}
. In particular, in dimension
d=1
, we have, for all
\rho\in[0,1)
,
H^{\rho}=[0,\infty)
. The following assumptions are motivated by
[
20
, Assumption 1]
.

Assumption 3.1
.

Let
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
. Let
(r_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be a strictly increasing sequence of scales and suppose that
\gamma:=\gamma(\kappa):=\inf_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{r_{j}}{r_{j+\kappa}}>0
. In addition to the Littlewood-Paley condition (
3
), we assume that there exists a partition
\Psi=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\Psi_{j}
such that for every
\psi\in\Psi_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there is an orthogonal matrix
A_{\psi}\in O_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
such that

\displaystyle\supp(\widehat{\psi})\subseteq H_{A_{\psi}}^{\rho}\cap\mathcal{S}_{r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}},

where
H_{A_{\psi}}^{\rho}:=\{A_{\psi}x\penalty\ |\penalty\ x\in H^{\rho}\}
.

Figure 3.
Illustration of the allowed regions for the frequency supports of two different filters from (possibly) two different families of high-pass filters.
The parameter
\kappa
(left
\kappa=2
, right
\kappa=3
) reflects the number of scales the filter may interfere with, and
\rho
parameterizes the opening angle of the colored segment.

The present setting may be regarded as a refinement of
[
20
, Assumption 1]
, in that it incorporates additional information pertaining to the frequency localization of the filters, which ultimately affects the decay behavior of the energy remainder. We think of this as follows: By the Littlewood-Paley condition (
3
), the filters
\Psi
induce a decomposition of the frequency space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
according to

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}=B_{r_{1}}(0)\cup\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{\psi\in\Psi_{j}}(H_{A_{\psi}}^{\rho}\cap\mathcal{S}_{r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}}).

The filters
\Psi
are of high-pass nature in the sense that they leave a frequency gap around zero,

(23)

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}=0\quad\text{ for all }\xi\in B_{r_{1}}(0).

By the Littlewood-Paley condition (
3
), this gap is filled by
\widehat{\chi}
. Moreover, any high-pass filter is frequency-localized in a region that is the intersection of a spherical shell, and a (rotated) cone with tip at the origin and angular spread parameterized by
\rho
. The maximum number of scale-interferences is specified by
\kappa
. The parameter
\gamma
reflects the (inverse of the) maximal relative spread between the inner and outer radius of any such shell. Clearly, we have
\gamma\in(0,1)
, since the scales are strictly increasing. The closer
\gamma
is to
1
and the closer
\rho
is to
0
, the more localized the high-pass filters are in frequency space.

These additional parameters will be reflected in the decay rates that we can guarantee for the energy remainder of certain signals, offering new insight into the interplay between the localization of the filters (in terms of size and shape of their Fourier supports) and the frequency decay behavior of the signals. We note however that our assumptions implicitly require the filters to be bandlimited, which is slightly more restrictive than the setting considered in
[
20
, Assumption 1]
.

We begin our analyis with two auxiliary results about the geometry of the allowed domain for the Fourier supports of the filters. The first one provides an upper bound on the maximum distance from
\nu^{*}
to any other point in the set
H^{\rho}\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
t>0
, then
\max_{x\in H^{\rho}\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\left\lVert\text{}x-t\nu^{*}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq t^{2}-2t\cdot(1-\rho)+1.

Proof.

A direct computation shows that, for all
x\in H^{\rho}\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\text{}x-t\nu^{*}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\left\lVert\text{}x\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-2t\cdot\left<\text{}\,x,\nu^{*}\,\text{}\right>+t^{2}\cdot\left\lVert\text{}\nu^{*}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq t^{2}-2t\cdot(1-\rho)\cdot\left\lVert\text{}x\text{}\right\rVert_{2}+1=t^{2}-2t\cdot(1-\rho)+1.

∎

The next lemma will be crucial for the induction step in the proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Lemma 3.3
.

There is a family
(\nu_{\psi})_{\psi\in\Psi}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
so that, for all
\psi\in\Psi
, and all
\xi\in\supp(\widehat{\psi})
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\text{}\xi-\nu_{\psi}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\alpha\cdot\left\lVert\text{}\xi\text{}\right\rVert_{2},

where
\alpha=\alpha(\gamma,\rho):=\sqrt{1-\frac{4\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}\cdot(1-\rho)^{2}}\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Since the function
(0,1)\to{\mathbb{R}},\ \gamma\mapsto\frac{4\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}
is strictly increasing, we have

0<\frac{4\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}\cdot(1-\rho)^{2}\leq\frac{4\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}<\lim_{\gamma\uparrow 1}\frac{4\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}=1.

Thus,
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Now let, for every
\psi\in\Psi_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\nu_{\psi}:=\frac{2r_{j}}{1+\gamma}\cdot(1-\rho)\cdot A_{\psi}\nu^{*}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

By Assumption
3.1
, any
\xi\in\supp(\widehat{\psi})
can be written as
A_{\psi}\omega=\xi
for some
\omega\in H^{\rho}\cap\mathcal{S}_{r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}}
. Since
A_{\psi}
is an orthogonal matrix, it preserves the euclidean distances
\left\lVert\text{}\xi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert\text{}\omega\text{}\right\rVert_{2}
and

\|\xi-\nu_{\psi}\|_{2}=\left\lVert\text{}\omega-\frac{2r_{j}\cdot(1-\rho)}{1+\gamma}\cdot\nu^{*}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}.

It therefore suffices to show that the latter term is bounded by
\alpha\cdot\left\lVert\text{}\omega\text{}\right\rVert_{2}
. By Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert\text{}\omega-\frac{2r_{j}\cdot(1-\rho)}{(1+\gamma)}\cdot\nu^{*}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}}{\left\lVert\text{}\omega\text{}\right\rVert_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{s\in[r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}]}\left\lVert\text{}\frac{\omega}{\left\lVert\text{}\omega\text{}\right\rVert_{2}}-\frac{2r_{j}\cdot(1-\rho)}{s\cdot(1+\gamma)}\cdot\nu^{*}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{s\in[r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}]}\max_{x\in H^{\rho}\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\Bigg\|x-\underbrace{\frac{2r_{j}\cdot(1-\rho)}{s\cdot(1+\gamma)}}_{=:t(s)}\cdot\nu^{*}\Bigg\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{s\in[r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}]}\sqrt{t(s)^{2}-2t(s)\cdot(1-\rho)+1}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{s\in[r_{j},\gamma^{-1}r_{j}]}\sqrt{t(s)^{2}-2t(s)\cdot(1-\rho)+1}

(24)

\displaystyle=\max_{t\in\left[\frac{2\gamma\cdot(1-\rho)}{1+\gamma},\frac{2(1-\rho)}{1+\gamma}\right]}\sqrt{t^{2}-2t\cdot(1-\rho)+1}.

Since the polynomial

P:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad t\mapsto t^{2}-2t\cdot(1-\rho)+1

is convex, the maximum in (
24
) is attained at one of the boundary points of the interval.
A direct computation yields

\displaystyle\max_{r\in\left[\frac{2\gamma\cdot(1-\rho)}{1+\gamma},\frac{2(1-\rho)}{1+\gamma}\right]}\sqrt{r^{2}-2r\cdot(1-\rho)+1}=\sqrt{1-\frac{4\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}\cdot(1-\rho)^{2}}.

∎

The following lemma is due to Mallat (cf.
[
11
, Lemma 2.7]
) and will turn out to be essential for proving the main result of this section. For the sake of completeness, we reproduce its (elementary) proof.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
g\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and
\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. If
g\geq 0
, then

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|\text{}\mathcal{F}(|f|)(\xi)\text{}\right|^{2}\cdot\left(1-\left|\text{}\widehat{g}(\xi)\text{}\right|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|\text{}\widehat{f}(\xi)\text{}\right|^{2}\cdot\left(1-\left|\text{}\widehat{g}(\xi-\nu)\text{}\right|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>.

Proof.

Clearly, since
g\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\text{}|f|*g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\left\lVert\text{}|f|*|M_{\nu}g|\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq\left\lVert\text{}f*M_{\nu}g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

By the convolution theorem and Parseval’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|\text{}\mathcal{F}(|f|)(\xi)\text{}\right|^{2}\cdot\left(1-\left|\text{}\widehat{g}(\xi)\text{}\right|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\text{}|f|\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-\left\lVert\text{}|f|*g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-\left\lVert\text{}f*M_{\nu}g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|\text{}\widehat{f}(\xi)\text{}\right|^{2}\cdot\left(1-\left|\text{}\widehat{g}(\xi-\nu)\text{}\right|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>.

∎

We are now ready to establish an upper bound on the energy remainder that is of the type (
22
). Previous work in this direction
[
18
,
20
,
21
]
showed that, in dimension
d=1
, for certain wavelet-generated filter banks
\Psi_{\text{wav}}
, there exist constants
C=C(\Psi_{\text{wav}})>0
and
\alpha=\alpha(\Psi_{\text{wav}})\in(0,1)
such that, for all (real-valued, cf.
[
18
]
) signals
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, and for all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
,

W_{N}[\Psi_{\text{wav}}](f)\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\left(1-\left|\widehat{\vartheta}\left(C\cdot\alpha^{N-1}\cdot\xi\right)\right|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>.

Here,
\widehat{\vartheta}
denotes a concretely specified positive definite and even function that ensures asymptotic decay of the right-hand side (a Gaussian in
[
18
]
, and a truncated power function in
[
20
]
). Our result is an extension of this previous work, since it applies to the fairly large class of filter banks satisfying Assumption
3.1
, including filter banks that are not necessarily structured as in the case of wavelet-generated filter banks.
At the same time, our result holds in arbitrary dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, for filters that satisfy our Assumption
3.1
, which is slightly more restrictive than
[
20
, Assumption 1]
, we improve the bound given in
[
20
, Theorem 1]
from polynomial decay of order
m_{d}
(where
m_{d}\in(0,1]
and
m_{d}\to 0
as
d\to\infty
) in the argument of
\widehat{\vartheta}
to exponential decay.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\vartheta\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
\vartheta\geq 0
, and suppose that there exists a nonincreasing function
\eta:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
so that
\eta(0)=1
and
|\widehat{\vartheta}|=\eta(\left\lVert\text{}\cdot\text{}\right\rVert_{2})
.
Then, there exists a universal constant
C_{\chi,\vartheta}>0
such that, for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
,

(25)

\displaystyle W_{N}(f)\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\left(1-\left|\widehat{\vartheta}\left(C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha^{N-1}\cdot\xi\right)\right|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>,

where
\alpha=\alpha(\gamma,\rho):=\sqrt{1-\frac{4\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}\cdot(1-\rho)^{2}}
.

Moreover, if there is
\widetilde{C}>0
so that
|\widehat{\vartheta}(\widetilde{C}\,\cdot)|\leq|\widehat{\chi}|
, then (
25
) holds for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
with
C_{\chi,\vartheta}=\widetilde{C}
.

Proof.

We perform induction on
N
.

First, let us assume that there is
\widetilde{C}>0
so that
|\widehat{\vartheta}(\widetilde{C}\;\cdot)|\leq|\widehat{\chi}|
. In this scenario, it is easy to prove the base case, which works analogously to the proof of the base case in
[
20
, Theorem 1]
. In fact, by the Littlewood-Paley condition (
3
), for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle W_{1}(f)=\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\left\lVert\text{}f*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot(1-|\widehat{\chi}(\xi)|^{2})\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot(1-|\widehat{\vartheta}(\widetilde{C}\cdot\xi)|^{2})\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>.

The proof of the base case is harder if there is no
\widetilde{C}>0
such that
|\widehat{\vartheta}(\widetilde{C}\,\cdot)|\leq|\widehat{\chi}|
holds true. Here we proceed in three steps, motivated by
[
18
, Section 6.3]
. In the first two steps we construct certain auxiliary functions, whose properties will be exploited to eventually prove the base case in the last step.

Step 1:
Let
r_{1}>0
be as in Assumption
3.1
, corresponding to the radius of the frequency gap of the high-pass filters
\Psi
. Choose an even function
h\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
that is supported in
\overline{B_{\frac{r_{1}}{2}}(0)}
and that satisfies
h(\xi)>0
for all
\xi\in B_{\frac{r_{1}}{2}}(0)
. Set
g:=\mathcal{F}(h*h)
. Then,
g\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
g=|\widehat{h}|^{2}\geq 0
,
\supp(\widehat{g})\subseteq\overline{B_{r_{1}}(0)}
, and
\widehat{g}(\xi)>0
for all
\xi\in\overline{B_{\frac{r_{1}}{2}}(0)}
. In particular,

m_{g}:=\min_{\xi\in\overline{B_{\frac{r_{1}}{2}}(0)}}|\widehat{g}(\xi)|^{2}>0.

Finally, we may assume that
\|\widehat{g}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
by imposing
\left\lVert\text{}h\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\leq 1
if necessary.

Step 2:
Let
\tilde{c}:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be nonincreasing and such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\tilde{c}(\left\lVert\text{}\nu\text{}\right\rVert_{2})\>\mathrm{d}\,\nu\>=1.

Defining the functions
c:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,\infty),\ \nu\mapsto\tilde{c}(\left\lVert\text{}\nu\text{}\right\rVert_{2})
and
F:=|\widehat{g}|^{2}*c
, we find that, for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle F(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{g}(\nu)|^{2}\cdot c(\xi-\nu)\>\mathrm{d}\,\nu\>\geq m_{g}\int_{B_{\frac{r_{1}}{2}}(0)}c(\xi-\nu)\>\mathrm{d}\,\nu\>\geq m_{g}\cdot\vol\left(B_{\frac{r_{1}}{2}}(0)\right)\cdot\tilde{c}\left(\left\lVert\text{}\xi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}+\frac{r_{1}}{2}\right).

Thus, if we choose
c=\tilde{c}(\left\lVert\text{}\cdot\text{}\right\rVert_{2})
so that

\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{\eta^{2}(s)}{\tilde{c}\left(s+\frac{r_{1}}{2}\right)}=0,

then there is
M>0
such that, for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{M}(0)
, it holds
F(\xi)\geq|\widehat{\vartheta}(\xi)|^{2}
. Furthermore,
F
is strictly positive and continuous. Hence,

m_{F}:=\min_{\xi\in\overline{B_{M}(0)}}F(\xi)>0.

Since
\lim_{s\to\infty}\eta(s)=0
, there exists
C_{\chi,\vartheta}>\alpha^{-1}
such that, for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{r_{1}}(0)
,

m_{F}\geq|\widehat{\vartheta}(C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha\cdot\xi)|^{2}.

Finally, we deduce
\left\lVert\text{}F\text{}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1
from
\|\widehat{g}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and
\left\lVert\text{}c\text{}\right\rVert_{1}=1
. Altogether, for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{r_{1}}(0)
,

(26)

\displaystyle 0\leq 1-F(\xi)\leq 1-|\widehat{\vartheta}(C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha\cdot\xi)|^{2}.

Step 3:

Recall from (
23
) that
\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}|\widehat{\psi}|^{2}
vanishes on
B_{r_{1}}(0)
. Since
\widehat{g}
is supported in
\overline{B_{r_{1}}(0)}
and since
\|\widehat{g}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, we have

\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\leq 1-|\widehat{g}(\xi)|^{2}\quad\text{ a.e. }\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Hence, for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle W_{2}(f)=\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\sum_{\psi^{\prime}\in\Psi}\left\lVert\text{}||f*\psi|*\psi^{\prime}|\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\sum_{\psi^{\prime}\in\Psi}\left\lVert\text{}|f*\psi|*\psi^{\prime}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|\text{}\mathcal{F}(|f*\psi|)(\xi)\text{}\right|^{2}\cdot\left(1-|\widehat{g}(\xi)|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>.

Using (
23
) and Lemma
3.4
, we conclude that, for all
\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle W_{2}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\left(1-|\widehat{g}(\xi-\nu)|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{r_{1}}(0)}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\left(1-|\widehat{g}(\xi-\nu)|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>.

Finally, we average this inequality with the function
c
and insert the upper bound from (
26
) to obtain

\displaystyle W_{2}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}c(\nu)\cdot\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{r_{1}}(0)}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\left(1-|\widehat{g}(\xi-\nu)|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>\>\mathrm{d}\,\nu\>

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{r_{1}}(0)}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}c(\nu)-c(\nu)\cdot|\widehat{g}(\xi-\nu)|^{2}\>\mathrm{d}\,\nu\>\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{r_{1}}(0)}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\left(1-F(\xi)\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\left(1-|\vartheta(C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha\cdot\xi)|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>.

This concludes the proof of the base case.

The induction step goes along the lines of
[
20
, Theorem 1]
: Suppose that (
25
) holds for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Our goal is then to establish the analogous upper bound for
W_{N+1}(f)
.
For
\psi\in\Psi
, let
\nu_{\psi}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be as in Lemma
3.3
. Note that the inverse Fourier transform of
\widehat{\vartheta}\left(C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha^{N-1}\,\cdot\right)
is nonnegative by the non-negativity of
\vartheta
. Thus, applying the induction hypothesis to
|f*\psi|
, as well as employing Lemma
3.4
, we find that

\displaystyle W_{N}(|f*\psi|)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}(|f*\psi|)(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\left(1-\left|\widehat{\vartheta}\left(C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha^{N-1}\cdot\xi\right)\right|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\left(1-\left|\widehat{\vartheta}\left(C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha^{N-1}\cdot(\xi-\nu_{\psi})\right)\right|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>.

Summing over
\psi\in\Psi
on both sides of the above inequality yields

(27)

\displaystyle W_{N+1}(f)=\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}W_{N}(|f*\psi|)\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot h_{N-1}(\xi)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>,

where

\displaystyle h_{N-1}(\xi):=\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\left(1-\left|\widehat{\vartheta}\left(C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha^{N-1}\cdot(\xi-\nu_{\psi})\right)\right|^{2}\right).

Now, Lemma
3.3
comes into play to establish a pointwise upper bound for
h_{N-1}
that suffices to conclude the proof. Since
\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\leq 1
holds for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, it is enough to show that we have, for all
\psi\in\Psi
, and all
\xi\in\supp(\widehat{\psi})
,

\displaystyle 1-\left|\widehat{\vartheta}\left(C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha^{N-1}\cdot(\xi-\nu_{\psi})\right)\right|^{2}\leq 1-\left|\widehat{\vartheta}\left(C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha^{N}\cdot\xi\right)\right|^{2}.

In fact,
1-|\widehat{\vartheta}|^{2}=1-\eta^{2}(\left\lVert\text{}\cdot\text{}\right\rVert_{2})
, where
1-\eta^{2}
is nondecreasing, and Lemma
3.3
implies that

\left\lVert\text{}C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha^{N-1}\cdot(\xi-\nu_{\psi})\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert\text{}C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha^{N}\cdot\xi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}.

Consequently, we have, for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

h_{N-1}(\xi)\leq 1-\left|\widehat{\vartheta}\left(C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha^{N}\cdot\xi\right)\right|^{2}.

Inserting this into (
27
) concludes the induction step and thus the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Smaller values of
\alpha
lead to faster convergence to
0
of the integral on the right side of (
25
) as
N\to\infty
. Hence, let us briefly comment on the impact of the parameters on the value of
\alpha
. To this end, we note that the function

\alpha:(0,1)\times[0,1)\to(0,1),\quad(\gamma,\rho)\mapsto\sqrt{1-\frac{4\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}\cdot(1-\rho)^{2}}

is strictly decreasing in
\gamma
, while it is strictly increasing in
\rho
. Moreover, we have the asymptotic behavior

\lim_{(\gamma,\rho)\to(1,0)}\alpha(\gamma,\rho)=0.

We interpret this as follows:
\alpha
is small if the size of the Fourier supports of the filters is approximately constant across all scales and if the filters are well localized in terms of their maximum angular frequency spread. This result appears to be consistent with the earlier findings stated in
[
20
, Theorem 1]
, which indicate a faster decay in lower dimensions: A reduction of the underlying dimension implicitly forces a higher concentration of angular frequency, with a maximum concentration achieved in dimension
d=1
.

\alpha
as a function of the parameters
\gamma
and
\rho
.
Smaller values of
\alpha
guarantee faster decay.

Next, we show how Theorem
3.5
entails fast (up to exponential) energy decay for signals belonging to generalized Sobolev spaces that are tailored to the frequency localization of the high-pass filters
\Psi
. We introduce certain weights to define these spaces.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
k>0
. We say that a function
\omega:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is a
weakly
t^{k}
-dominated weight
if
\omega
is nondecreasing and if there exists
T>0
so that the auxiliary function

h_{k,\omega}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty),\ t\mapsto t^{k}\cdot\omega^{-2}(t)

is bounded on
(0,T)
, nondecreasing on
[T,\infty)
, and satisfies
\lim_{t\to\infty}h_{k,\omega}(t)=\infty
.

If, in addition to the above,
h_{k,\omega}
is nondecreasing on the entire interval
(0,\infty)
, then we say that
\omega
is a
strongly
t^{k}
-dominated weight
.

Remark 3.8
.

If
\omega
is differentiable, we can easily establish a sufficient criterion for the (simultaneous) monotonicity of
\omega
and
h_{k,\omega}
, relying on the non-negativity of their derivatives. In fact, for all
t>0
,

\displaystyle h_{k,\omega}^{\prime}(t)=k\cdot t^{k-1}\cdot\omega^{-2}(t)-2t^{k}\cdot\omega^{-3}(t)\cdot\omega^{\prime}(t)=\omega^{-2}(t)\cdot t^{k-1}\cdot\left(k-2t\cdot\omega^{-1}(t)\cdot\omega^{\prime}(t)\right).

Thus,
h_{k,\omega}^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
is equivalent to
k\cdot\omega(t)\geq 2t\cdot\omega^{\prime}(t)
. Altogether, if there is
T\geq 0
so that for all
t>T
,

k\cdot\omega(t)\geq 2t\cdot\omega^{\prime}(t)\geq 0,

then both
\omega
and
h_{k,\omega}
are nondecreasing on
(T,\infty)
.

We define, for all
\psi\in\Psi
,

d_{\psi}:=\inf_{\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sup_{\xi\in\supp(\widehat{\psi})}\left\lVert\text{}\xi-\xi^{\prime}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}=\min_{\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\max_{\xi\in\supp(\widehat{\psi})}\left\lVert\text{}\xi-\xi^{\prime}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}.

For a weakly
t^{k}
-dominated weight, we consider the generalized Sobolev space

\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(\Psi;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})):=\left\{\text{}f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\omega^{2}(d_{\psi})\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}<\infty\text{}\right\}.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\vartheta\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be as in Theorem
3.5
, and assume that there are
k=k_{\vartheta}>0
,
C=C_{\vartheta}>0
such that, for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

(28)

\displaystyle 1-|\widehat{\vartheta}(\xi)|^{2}\leq C\cdot\left\lVert\text{}\xi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{k}.

If
\omega:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is a weakly
t^{k}
-dominated weight, then
we have, for all
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(\Psi;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
,

W_{N}(f)\in\mathcal{O}\left(\omega^{-2}\left(\alpha^{-N}\right)\right),

where
\alpha=\alpha(\gamma,\rho):=\sqrt{1-\frac{4\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}\cdot(1-\rho)^{2}}\in(0,1)
.

Moreover, if there is
\widetilde{C}>0
so that
|\widehat{\vartheta}(\widetilde{C}\,\cdot)|\leq|\widehat{\chi}|
, and if
\omega
is a strongly
t^{k}
-dominated weight, then the following explicit upper bound holds: For all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
,

\displaystyle W_{N}(f)\leq\max\left\{1,C\cdot\widetilde{C}^{k}\cdot\alpha^{-k}\right\}\cdot\left(\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\omega^{2}(d_{\psi})\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)\cdot\omega^{-2}(\alpha^{-N}).

Remark 3.10
.

By the non-negativity of
\vartheta
, the condition (
28
) can only be satisfied if
k=k_{\vartheta}\leq 2
. Let us briefly sketch the reason for this. By definition of the
L^{1}
-Fourier transform, we have, for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle 1-|\widehat{\vartheta}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\re\left(|\widehat{\vartheta}(0)|^{2}-|\widehat{\vartheta}(\xi)|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\re\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\vartheta(x)\cdot\vartheta(y)\cdot\left(1-e^{-2\pi\cdot i\cdot\left<\text{}\,\xi,x-y\,\text{}\right>}\right)\>\mathrm{d\lambda}\,(x,y)\>\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\vartheta(x)\cdot\vartheta(y)\cdot\left(1-\cos(2\pi\cdot\left<\text{}\,\xi,x-y\,\text{}\right>)\right)\>\mathrm{d\lambda}\,(x,y)\>.

For all
z\in[-1,1]
,

1-\cos(z)\geq\frac{z^{2}}{3}.

Hence, if
\xi=s\cdot e_{1}
, where

denotes the first standard unit vector in

, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1-|\widehat{\vartheta}(\xi)|^{2}}{\left\lVert\text{}\xi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\left\{\text{}(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\penalty\ :\penalty\ |2\pi\cdot s\cdot(x_{1}-y_{1})|\leq 1\text{}\right\}}\vartheta(x)\cdot\vartheta(y)\cdot(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}\>\mathrm{d\lambda}\,(x,y)\>

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{s\to 0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\vartheta(x)\cdot\vartheta(y)\cdot\left(x_{1}-y_{1}\right)^{2}\>\mathrm{d\lambda}\,(x,y)\>>0.

We conclude that

.

Now, observe that larger values for

in (

) allow more flexibility in the choice of

, as the assumptions regarding

are easier to fulfill. However, as justified above, the largest possible value for any
\vartheta\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is

. Finally, note that, while we can show an analogous result to Theorem
3.5
for
\vartheta\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we would not gain a qualitative improvement to Theorem
3.9
, since the same threshold order of convergence
k_{\vartheta}=2
in (

) would still hold.

Proof.

Fix
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(\Psi;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
. Let, for every
\psi\in\Psi
,

\xi_{\psi}\in\argmin_{\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\max_{\xi\in\supp(\widehat{\psi})}\left\lVert\text{}\xi-\xi^{\prime}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}.

We start in a similar fashion to the induction step of the proof of Theorem
3.5
. By the same theorem, by the path structure of the scattering network, and by Lemma
3.4
, we find that, for all sufficiently large

,

\displaystyle W_{N}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}W_{N-1}(|f*\psi|)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|\text{}\mathcal{F}(|f*\psi|)(\xi)\text{}\right|^{2}\cdot\left(1-\left|\text{}\widehat{\vartheta}\left(C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha^{N-1}\cdot\xi\right)\text{}\right|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

(29)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\left(1-\left|\text{}\widehat{\vartheta}\left(C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha^{N-1}\cdot(\xi-\xi_{\psi})\right)\text{}\right|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>.

Our strategy is to split the integral into small scales and large scales compared with
\alpha^{-N}

guarantee that, for all sufficiently large

(30)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,\alpha^{-N}]}h_{k,\omega}(t)=h_{k,\omega}(\alpha^{-N})=\alpha^{-kN}\cdot\omega^{-2}(\alpha^{-N}).

For the small scales, we use (

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\alpha^{-N}}(\xi_{\psi})}

\displaystyle|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\left(1-\left|\text{}\widehat{\vartheta}\left(C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha^{N-1}\cdot(\xi-\xi_{\psi})\right)\text{}\right|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{\alpha^{-N}}(\xi_{\psi})}

\displaystyle|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\left(1-\left|\text{}\widehat{\vartheta}\left(C_{\chi,\vartheta}\cdot\alpha^{N-1}\cdot(\xi-\xi_{\psi})\right)\text{}\right|^{2}\right)\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle W_{N}(f)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{1,C\cdot C_{\chi,\vartheta}^{k}\cdot\alpha^{-k}\right\}\cdot\left(\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\omega^{2}(d_{\psi})\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)\cdot\omega^{-2}(\alpha^{-N}).

Now, if there is

.
This concludes the proof of the theorem.

The following lemma shows that fast decay (with respect to the weight
\omega
) of the Fourier transform suffices, independent of
\Psi
, for a signal to belong to the generalized Sobolev space
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(\Psi;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
k>0
. If
\omega:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is a weakly
t^{k}
-dominated weight, then

\mathcal{F}L_{\omega}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(\Psi;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})).

Proof.

Recall from Assumption
3.1
that
\Psi
can be decomposed into
\Psi=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\Psi_{j}
such that
\supp(\widehat{\psi})\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}}
whenever
\psi\in\Psi_{j}
. In particular,
d_{\psi}\leq r_{j+\kappa}\leq\gamma^{-1}\cdot r_{j}
.
By assumption on
\omega
, there exists
J\in\mathbb{N}
such that
h_{k,\omega}

is nondecreasing on
(r_{J},\infty)
. Hence, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>J}
,

(31)

\displaystyle\omega^{2}(d_{\psi})\leq\omega^{2}(\gamma^{-1}\cdot r_{j})\leq\gamma^{-k}\cdot\omega^{2}(r_{j}).

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}L_{\omega}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. We aim to show that the sum
\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\omega^{2}(d_{\psi})\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
converges. To do so, we split the sum into small scales and large scales compared with
J
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi_{j}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\omega^{2}(d_{\psi})\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>+\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi_{j}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\omega^{2}(d_{\psi})\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>,

so that it suffices to bound these expressions individually. We can easily control the small scales by the monotonicity of
\omega
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi_{j}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}}}

\displaystyle|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\omega^{2}(d_{\psi})\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle\leq\omega^{2}(r_{J+\kappa})\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi_{j}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega^{2}(r_{J+\kappa})}{\omega^{2}(r_{1})}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi_{j}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\omega^{2}(\left\lVert\text{}\xi\text{}\right\rVert_{2})\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>.

For the large scales, in addition to the monotonicity of
\omega
, we also use (
31
) to find that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi_{j}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}}}

\displaystyle|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\omega^{2}(d_{\psi})\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi_{j}}\omega^{2}(\gamma^{-1}\cdot r_{j})\cdot\int_{\mathcal{S}_{r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi_{j}}\gamma^{-k}\cdot\omega^{2}(r_{j})\cdot\int_{\mathcal{S}_{r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi_{j}}\gamma^{-k}\cdot\int_{\mathcal{S}_{r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\omega^{2}(\left\lVert\text{}\xi\text{}\right\rVert_{2})\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>.

Altogether, letting
C=\max\left\{\text{}\frac{\omega^{2}(r_{J+\kappa})}{\omega^{2}(r_{1})},\gamma^{-k}\text{}\right\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\omega^{2}(d_{\psi})\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi_{j}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{r_{j},r_{j+\kappa}}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\omega^{2}(\left\lVert\text{}\xi\text{}\right\rVert_{2})\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\kappa\cdot\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\omega^{2}(\left\lVert\text{}\xi\text{}\right\rVert_{2})\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\>

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\kappa\cdot\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\cdot\omega^{2}(\left\lVert\text{}\xi\text{}\right\rVert_{2})\>\mathrm{d}\,\xi\><\infty.

∎

There are choices of the weight
\omega
and the filter bank

, for which the inclusion
\mathcal{F}L_{\omega}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(\Psi;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
is strict, i.e., the spaces
\mathcal{F}L_{\omega}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(\Psi;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))

4.3
.

As a consequence of Theorem
3.9
and Lemma
3.11
, we obtain asymptotic convergence rates for signals of (logarithmic) Sobolev regularity.

Corollary 3.12
.

Suppose that Assumption
3.1
holds for
\Psi
with parameters
\gamma
and
\rho
.
As before, let

\alpha:=\sqrt{1-\frac{4\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}\cdot(1-\rho)^{2}}\>\in(0,1).

Then, the following hold:

a)

If

for some
s>0
, then

W_{N}(f)\in\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2\min\left\{\text{}s,1\text{}\right\}\cdot N}).

b)

If
f\in H^{s}_{\log}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for some
s>0
, then

W_{N}(f)\in\mathcal{O}(N^{-2s}).

Proof.

Let

\vartheta:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad x\mapsto e^{-\pi\cdot\left\lVert\text{}x\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}.

Then,
\vartheta
satisfies the prerequisites of Theorem
3.5
. From the series expansion of the one-dimensional Gaussian at

we can see that, for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

1-|\widehat{\vartheta}(\xi)|^{2}\leq 2\pi\cdot\left\lVert\text{}\xi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Now, part a) follows immediately from Theorem
3.9
and Lemma
3.11
if we take

\omega:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty),\quad t\mapsto(1+t^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}.

In fact, using the criterion from Remark
3.8
, it is straightforward to see that
\omega
is a strongly
t^{2}
-dominated weight if
s\in(0,1]
, since for all
t>0
,

2\omega(t)\geq 2s\cdot\frac{t^{2}}{1+t^{2}}\cdot(1+t^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}=2t\cdot\omega^{\prime}(t)\geq 0.

We draw the conclusion by noting that
\mathcal{F}L_{\omega}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})=H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and that, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\omega^{-2}(\alpha^{-N})\leq\alpha^{2s\cdot N}.

For

.

In order to establish part b),

\omega:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty),\quad t\mapsto\ln^{s}(e+t).

We verify the condition from Remark

again. In general,
\omega
is not a strongly

-dominated weight (if

is too large). However,

\omega
is a weakly

-dominated weight. Indeed, for all

2\omega(t)=2\ln(e+t)\cdot\ln^{s-1}(e+t)\geq 2s\cdot\frac{t}{e+t}\cdot\ln^{s-1}(e+t)=2t\cdot\omega^{\prime}(t)\geq 0.

Finally, by definition,

\omega^{-2}(\alpha^{-N})\leq\ln^{-2s}(\alpha^{-N})=\ln^{-2s}(\alpha^{-1})\cdot N^{-2s}.

3.9
.

4.
Applications

4.1.
Scattering with UFC filters

In this section, we apply our results from Section
3
to a large class of filter banks that is closely related to the class of uniform covering frames introduced in
[
3
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that the filters
\Psi
have
uniform frequency concentration
(UFC) if they fulfill Assumption
3.1
, and if

D_{\Psi}:=\sup_{\psi\in\Psi}d_{\psi}=\sup_{\psi\in\Psi}\inf_{\xi_{\psi}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sup_{\xi\in\supp(\widehat{\psi})}\left\lVert\text{}\xi-\xi_{\psi}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}<\infty.

It turns out that for such scattering networks, energy decay is exponential, globally on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Thereby, we complement the earlier result
[
3
, Proposition 3.3]
, which states global exponential energy decay for scattering networks based on uniform covering frames. We have several comments concerning the similarities and differences between our following result and the result in
[
3
]
:

•

In
[
3
]
, the output-generating low-pass filter is assumed to be bandlimited. Our result also applies if that is not the case.

•

In
[
3
]
, the frequency support of each filter
\psi\in\Psi
is assumed to be connected. Our result does not require connectedness of the frequency supports. However, as is pointed out in
[
3
, Remark 2.2]
, the motivation to require connectedness of the frequency supports is to preclude certain pathological behavior such as
\supp(\widehat{\psi})
having two connected components, where one component is near the origin and the other is far from the origin. In our setting, we implicitly preclude such pathological behavior by means of Assumption
3.1
, which imposes frequency localization of the filters.

•

If
D_{\Psi}<\infty
, then the frequency support of each filter
\psi\in\Psi
is contained in a closed ball of radius
D_{\Psi}
. In particular,
\Psi
satisfies the uniform covering property introduced in
[
3
]
, i.e., for any
R>0
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each
\psi\in\Psi
, the set
\supp(\widehat{\psi})
can be covered by
N
cubes of side length
2R
. In turn, if
\Psi
satisfies the uniform covering property, and if the frequency supports of the filters are connected, then
D_{\Psi}<\infty
.

•

The findings in
[
3
, Proposition 3.3]
guarantee exponential decay of the energy remainder: For all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

W_{N}(f)\leq\alpha^{N}\cdot\left(\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-\left\lVert\text{}f*\chi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right),

for an
unspecified
constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
depending only on the uniform covering frame. The main advantage of our result is that we can explicitly specify the values for all occurring quantities in the upper bound on the energy remainder, including a precise value for
\alpha
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Suppose that the filters
\Psi
have uniform frequency concentration with parameters
\gamma
,
\rho
, and
r_{1}
from Assumption
3.1
.

Then we have, for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

W_{N}(f)\leq\max\left\{\text{}1,\frac{2\left(\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right)}{\alpha\cdot r_{1}}\text{}\right\}\cdot D_{\Psi}\cdot\left(\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-\left\lVert\text{}f*\chi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)\cdot\alpha^{N},

where, as before,
\alpha:=\sqrt{1-\frac{4\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}\cdot(1-\rho)^{2}}
. Asymptotically, we even have

W_{N}(f)\in\mathcal{O}\left(\alpha^{2N}\right).

Proof.

Both statements (the specific bound and the asymptotic behavior) are immediate consequences of Theorem
3.9
. To derive the specific upper bound, we choose (in the notation of the theorem)

\omega:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty),\quad t\mapsto\sqrt{t}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widehat{\vartheta}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad\xi\mapsto\left(1-\left\lVert\text{}\xi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}\right)_{+}^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor+1}.

Clearly,
\omega
is a strongly
t
-dominated weight. Further,
\vartheta
satisfies the prerequisites of Theorem
3.5
with
|\widehat{\vartheta}(r_{1}^{-1}\,\cdot)|\leq|\widehat{\chi}|
, i.e.,
C_{\chi,\vartheta}=r_{1}^{-1}
. Moreover, by Bernoulli’s inequality we have, for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

1-|\widehat{\vartheta}(\xi)|^{2}\leq 2\left(\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right)\cdot\left\lVert\text{}\xi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}.

Finally, note that

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\omega^{2}(d_{\psi})\cdot\left\lVert\text{}f*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\omega^{2}(D_{\Psi})\cdot\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}\left\lVert\text{}f*\psi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=D_{\Psi}\cdot\left(\left\lVert\text{}f\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-\left\lVert\text{}f*\chi\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right),

which concludes the first part of the corollary.
The asymptotic rate also follows directly from Theorem
3.9
, if we take

\omega:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty),\quad t\mapsto t\qquad\text{and}\qquad\vartheta:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad x\mapsto e^{-\pi\cdot\left\lVert\text{}x\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}.

∎

Remark 4.3
.

In the context of the above corollary, it holds that

\mathcal{F}L^{2}_{\omega}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subsetneq L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})=\mathcal{D}_{\omega}(\Psi;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})).

This again shows the strength of Theorem
3.9
, and stresses the role of the filter bank specificity in energy propagation in scattering networks.

4.2.
Scattering with Wavelets

In this section, we apply our results from Section
2
and Section
3
to filter banks that are wavelet-generated.

We begin by briefly reviewing scattering with directional wavelets as introduced in
[
11
]
. Let
a>1
, and let
G<O_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
be a finite subgroup of rotations in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
that comprises the reflection operator
-I\in G
.

Definition 4.4
.

We say that a pair of functions
(\psi,\phi)\in\left(L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\right)^{2}
is
admissible for
(a,G)
-wavelet scattering
if

(33)

\displaystyle|\widehat{\phi}(\xi)|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{M\in G}|\widehat{\psi}(a^{-j}\cdot M^{-1}\xi)|^{2}=1\quad\text{a.e. }\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Note that (
33
) entails that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{M\in G}|\widehat{\psi}(a^{-j}\cdot M^{-1}\xi)|^{2}=1\quad\text{a.e. }\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

which also implicitly requires
\psi
to have at least one vanishing moment, i.e.,
\widehat{\psi}(0)=0
.
Fix
J\in\mathbb{Z}
, and set

\displaystyle\Lambda(a,G,J):=\{a^{j}\cdot M\penalty\ |\penalty\ j\in\mathbb{Z}_{>-J},M\in G\}\subseteq\GL_{d}({\mathbb{R}}).

Let, for each
\lambda=a^{j}\cdot M\in\Lambda(a,G,J)
,

\psi_{\lambda}:=D_{\lambda^{T}}^{1}\psi=a^{dj}\psi(a^{j}\cdot M^{-1}\,\cdot\,).

Moreover, set

\phi_{J}:=D_{a^{-J}\cdot I}^{1}\phi=a^{-dJ}\phi(a^{-J}\,\cdot\,).

Finally, define

\Wav(\psi,a,G,J):=\left\{\text{}\psi_{\lambda}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \lambda\in\Lambda(a,G,J)\text{}\right\}.

By construction, the filters
\Wav(\psi,a,G,J)
together with the corresponding low-pass filter
\chi:=\phi_{J}
form a semi-discrete Parseval frame.
In fact, we obtain the Littlewood-Paley condition (
3
) from the admissibility condition (
33
) by an index shift,

\displaystyle|\widehat{\phi}_{J}(\xi)|^{2}+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(a,G,J)}|\widehat{\psi}(\lambda^{-1}\cdot\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\widehat{\phi}(a^{J}\cdot\xi)|^{2}+\sum_{j=1-J}^{\infty}\sum_{M\in G}|\widehat{\psi}(a^{-j}\cdot M^{-1}\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\widehat{\phi}(a^{J}\cdot\xi)|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{M\in G}|\widehat{\psi}(a^{-j}\cdot M^{-1}(a^{J}\cdot\xi))|^{2}=1\quad\text{a.e. }\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Definition 4.5
.

We say that the filters
\Psi
are
wavelet-generated
if there exist a finite subgroup
G<O_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
with
-I\in G
,
a>1
,
J\in\mathbb{Z}
, and a pair
(\psi,\phi)\in\left(L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\right)^{2}
that is admissible for
(a,G)
-wavelet scattering such that
\Psi=\Wav(\psi,a,G,J)
.

Our main application of the negative findings from Section
2
is that energy propagation can be arbitrarily slow in wavelet scattering networks.

Corollary 4.6
.

If the scattering filters
\Psi
are wavelet-generated, then for any nonincreasing null-sequence
E=(E_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
there exists
f_{g,E}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that

a)

\left\lVert\text{}f_{g,E}\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq C_{g}\cdot(1+E_{1})
,

b)

\left\lVert\text{}f_{g,E}-g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq C_{g}\cdot E_{1}
, and

c)

W_{N}(f_{g,E})\geq\frac{\left\lVert\text{}g\text{}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{8}\cdot E_{N}
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Furthermore,

Y_{E}:=\left\{f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ W_{N}(f)\in\mathcal{O}(E_{N})\right\}

is a countable union of nowhere dense sets in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. In particular,
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\setminus Y_{E}
is dense in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward application of Corollary
2.8
. Suppose that
\Psi=\Wav(\psi,a,G,J)
. Setting
A:=a\cdot I_{d}
gives
\sigma_{\min}(A)=a>1
. Moreover, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, applying
D_{A^{-m}}^{1}
to the filters
\Psi
results in an index shift, which leads to the required inclusion relation

\displaystyle\Psi_{m}:=D_{A^{-m}}^{1}\Psi

\displaystyle=\left\{\text{}D_{A^{-m}}^{1}\psi_{\lambda}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \lambda\in\Lambda(a,G,J)\text{}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\text{}D_{\lambda^{T}\cdot A^{-m}}^{1}\psi\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \lambda\in\Lambda(a,G,J)\text{}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\text{}\psi_{\lambda}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \lambda\in\Lambda(a,G,J+m)\text{}\right\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\text{}\psi_{\lambda}\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \lambda\in\Lambda(a,G,J+m+1)\text{}\right\}=\Psi_{m+1}.

∎

The fact that for any nonincreasing null-sequence
E\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
the set
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\setminus Y_{E}
, which contains those signals whose associated energy propagates
not
at the order of
E
through the scattering network, is dense in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
indicates that wavelet scattering energy propagation is not a stable property of signals. This is further emphasized by the contrast that fast energy propagation provably also holds for dense signal classes of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
if the generating wavelet is bandlimited.

.

Suppose that the scattering filters are wavelet-generated according to
\Psi=\Wav(\psi,a,G,J)

,

If

We only need to show that Assumption

is satisfied in this setting. To this end, we note that we have, for all

\displaystyle\supp(\widehat{\psi_{\lambda}})\subseteq\lambda\cdot\supp(\widehat{\psi})\subseteq\lambda\cdot\left(H_{M_{\psi}}^{\rho}\cap\mathcal{S}_{a^{-1},a^{\kappa-1}}\right)=H_{M\cdot M_{\psi}}^{\rho}\cap\mathcal{S}_{a^{j-1},a^{\kappa+j-1}}.

Remark 4.9
.

The semantic content of a generic signal occurring in classification tasks is typically stable to the action of small diffeomorphisms that deform signals
[
11
,
12
]
. The windowed scattering transform was introduced as a model for feature extraction, building translation invariant representations of signals in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
that take this form of stability into account.

One approach
[
7
,
19
,
9
]
to prove stability of the scattering transform to the action of small diffeomorphisms relies on the non-expansiveness of
\mathcal{S}[\mathfrak{F}]
. In this case, the stability ultimately results from the deformation sensitivity of the class of signals being considered, and thus naturally only applies to strict subclasses of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

A different approach
[
11
,
13
]
explicitly takes into account the network architecture of scattering networks. Here, stability of the wavelet scattering transform to the action of small diffeomorphisms can be guaranteed for signals that have a finite mixed
(\ell^{1},L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
scattering norm, i.e., for signals
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
that satisfy

(34)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\text{}U[\mathcal{P}_{\Psi}](f)\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\Psi};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\left\lVert\text{}U[\Psi^{N}]f\text{}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\Psi^{N};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\left(W_{N}[\Psi](f)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty.

In general, it is not easy to decide whether a signal satisfies this condition.
In light of Corollary
4.6
, there exist dense subsets of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for which the mixed
(\ell^{1},L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
scattering norm (
34
) does not converge. On the other hand, if the generating wavelet of the filter bank is bandlimited, then Corollary
4.7
implies that the mixed
(\ell^{1},L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
scattering norm is finite at least for all signals belonging to
H^{s}_{\log}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for any
s>1
. This generalizes
[
13
, Proposition 2.4]
to arbitrary dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

5.
Concluding remarks

The successful application of wavelet based scattering transforms in pattern recognition or pattern classification tasks is attributed at least in part to the ability of the cascade of filter banks to correlate signal structures across different scales. It can be argued that our constructions of counterexamples to fast energy decay defy this intuition, by using signals consisting of components that are so dispersed in frequency that the scattering transform essentially processes them in separate channels. Hence for this class of signals, across-scale correlation of different frequency components is intentionally (approximately) disabled. That these signals exist in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
emphasizes the possibility that the intuitions underlying the interpretation of scattering transforms are generally applicable only to more limited signal classes, i.e., they might reflect a type of implicit bias on the modeling side. Understanding how this bias is related to the choice of filter bank has been one of the initial impulses of this work.

Preface

Unlike the classical theory of Fourier series which deals with decomposition of a function into sinusoidal waves the Vilenkin (Walsh) functions are rectangular waves. The development of the theory of Vilenkin-Fourier series has been strongly influenced by the classical theory of trigonometric series but there are a lot of differences also. The aim of my master thesis is to discuss, develop and apply the newest developments of this fascinating theory connected to modern harmonic analysis. In particular, we investigate Nörlund means but only in the case when their coefficients are monotone and prove convergence in Lebesgue and Vilenkin-Lebesgue points. Since almost everywhere points are Lebesgue and Vilenkin-Lebesgue points for any integrable functions we obtain almost everywhere convergence of such summability methods.

Key words

Key words:
Vilenkin systems, Nörlund means, almost everywhere convergence, Lebesgue points, Vilenkin-Lebesgue points, convergence, approximation.

Nörlund Means of Vilenkin-Fourier series in Lebesgue Spaces

2.1
Introduction

In the literature, a point
x\in G_{m}
is called a Lebesgue point of
f\in L^{1}\left(G_{m}\right),
if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}M_{n}\int_{I_{n}(x)}f\left(t\right)dt=f\left(x\right)\ \ \ \ \ a.e.\ x\in G_{m}.

It is well-known (for details see
[
88
]
and
[
61
]
) that if
f\in L^{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
then almost every point is a Lebesgue point and the following important result holds true:

Proposition 2.1.1

Let
f\in L^{1}(G_{m})
. Then, for all Lebesgue points
x
,

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}S_{M_{n}}f(x)=f(x)

In the literature there are notations of
n
-th Nörlund
L_{n}
and Riesz
R_{n}
logarithmic means
defined by

\displaystyle L_{n}f

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{S_{k}f}{n-k},

\displaystyle R_{n}f

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{S_{k}f}{k},

respectively, where

l_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}.

It is known that the Nörlund logarithmic mean has better approximation properties then the partial sums and that the Riesz logarithmic means is better than Fejér means in the same sense, but they have much more similar properties with them. In
[
23
]
Gát and Goginava proved some
convergence and divergence properties of the Nörlund logarithmic means of
functions in the class of continuous functions and in the Lebesgue space
L^{1}.
Moreover, Gát and Goginava
[
24
]
proved that for each measurable function satisfying

\phi\left(u\right)=o\left(u\log^{1/2}u\right),\text{ \ as \ }u\rightarrow\infty,

there exists an integrable function
f
such that

\int_{G_{m}}\phi\left(\left|f\left(x\right)\right|\right)d\mu\,\left(x\right)<\infty

and that there exists a set with positive measure such that the Nörlund logarithmic means of the function diverges on this set. It follows that weak-(1,1) type inequality does not hold for the maximal operator of Nörlund logarithmic means:

\displaystyle L^{\ast}f

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|L_{n}f\right|

but there exists an absolute constant
C_{p}
such that

\left\|L^{\ast}f\right\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{p},\text{ \ when \ }f\in L^{p},\text{ \ }p>1.

Moreover (for details see
[
3
]
), if we consider the following restricted maximal operator
\widetilde{L}_{\#}^{\ast}f,
defined by

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}_{\#}^{\ast}f

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|L_{M_{n}}f\right|,\ \ \ (M_{k}:=m_{0}...m_{k-1},\ \ \ k=0,1...),

The properties established in Lemma

ensure that kernel of the Fejér means
\{K_{N}\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
form what is called an approximation identity.

Definition 2.1.4

The family
\{\Phi_{n}\}^{\infty}_{n=1}\subset L^{\infty}(G_{m})
forms an approximate identity provided that

\displaystyle(A1)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \int_{G_{m}}\Phi_{n}(x)d(x)=1

\displaystyle(A2)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{G_{m}}\left|\Phi_{n}(x)\right|d\mu(x)<\infty

\displaystyle(A3)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{G_{m}\backslash I_{N}}\left|\Phi_{n}(x)\right|d\mu(x)\rightarrow 0,\ \ \text{as}\ \ n\rightarrow\infty,\ \ \text{for any}\ \ N\in\mathbb{N_{+}}.

The term ”approximate identity” is used because of the fact that

\Phi_{n}\ast f\to f\ \ \ \text{ as }\ \ \ n\to\infty

in any reasonable sense. In particular, the following results holds true (for details see the books
[
21
]
and
[
38
]
):

Proposition 2.1.5

Let
f\in L^{p}(G_{m}),
where
1\leq p\leq\infty
and the family

\{\Phi_{n}\}^{\infty}_{n=1}\subset L^{\infty}(G_{m})

forms an approximate identity. Then

\|\Phi_{n}\ast f-f\|_{p}\to 0\ \ \text{ as }\ \ n\to\infty.

It is well-known that the
n
-th Nörlund mean
t_{n}
and
T
means
T_{n}
for the Fourier series of
f
are, respectively, defined by

t_{n}f:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}q_{n-k}S_{k}f

(2.1.1)

and

T_{n}f:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}q_{k}S_{k}f,

(2.1.2)

where
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a sequence of nonnegative numbers and

Q_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q_{k}.

Let
\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
be a sequence of nonnegative numbers where
q_{0}>0.

Then the summability method (
2.1.2
) generated by
\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
is regular if and only if (see
[
36
]
)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}=\infty.

The representations

t_{n}f\left(x\right)=\underset{G_{m}}{\int}f\left(t\right)F_{n}\left(x-t\right)d\mu\left(t\right)

and

T_{n}f\left(x\right)=\underset{G_{m}}{\int}f\left(t\right)F^{-1}_{n}\left(x-t\right)d\mu\left(t\right)

play central roles in the sequel, where

F_{n}:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}q_{n-k}D_{k}

(2.1.3)

and

F^{-1}_{n}:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}q_{k}D_{k}

(2.1.4)

are called the kernels of Nörlund and
T
means, respectively.

Nörlund are generalizations of Fejér,
(C,\alpha)
and Nörlund logarithmic means. According to all these facts it is of prior interest to study the behavior of operators related to Nörlund means of
Fourier series with respect to orthonormal systems. The Nörlund
summation are general summability methods, which satisfy the conditions (A1)-(A3). This means that all Nörlund and
T
means are approximation identity. According to all these facts it is of prior interest to study the behavior of operators related to Nörlund means of
Fourier series with respect to orthonormal systems. Móricz and Siddiqi
[
37
]
investigated the approximation properties of
some special Nörlund means of Walsh-Fourier series of
L^{p}
functions in
norm. In particular, they proved that if
f\in L^{p}(G_{m}),

1\leq p\leq\infty,

n=M_{j}+k,

1\leq k\leq M_{j}\ (n\in\mathbb{N}_{+})
and
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\}
is sequence of non-negative numbers, such that

\frac{n^{\alpha-1}}{Q_{n}^{\alpha}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q^{\alpha}_{k}=O(1),\ \ \text{for some}\ \ 1<\alpha\leq 2,

then

\|t_{n}f-f\|_{p}\leq\frac{C_{p}}{Q_{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}M_{i}q_{n-M_{i}}\omega_{p}\left(\frac{1}{M_{i}},f\right)+C_{p}\omega_{p}\left(\frac{1}{M_{j}},f\right),

when
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is non-decreasing, while

\displaystyle\|t_{n}f-f\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{p}}{Q_{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(Q_{n-M_{j}+1}-Q_{n-M_{j+1}+1}\right)\omega_{p}\left(\frac{1}{M_{i}},f\right)+C_{p}\omega_{p}\left(\frac{1}{M_{j}},f\right),

when
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is non-increasing.

Let us define maximal operator of Nörlund means by

\displaystyle t^{\ast}f:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|t_{n}f\right|.

If
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is non-increasing and satisfying the condition

\frac{1}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),\text{ \ \ as \ \ }n\rightarrow\infty,

(2.1.5)

or if
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is non-decreasing, satisfying the condition

\frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),\text{ \ \ as \ \ }n\rightarrow\infty,

(2.1.6)

then

\displaystyle y\mu\left\{t^{\ast}f>y\right\}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{1},\ \ \ f\in L^{1}(G_{m}),\ \ y>0.

The boundedness of the such maximal operator of Nörlund means does not hold from
L^{1}(G_{m})
to the space
L^{1}(G_{m}).
However

\left\|t_{n}f-f\right\|_{p}\rightarrow 0,\text{ \ \ \ as \ \ \ \ }n\rightarrow\infty,\text{ \ }(f\in L^{p}(G_{m}),\text{ \ }1\leq p\leq\infty).

2.2
Well-known and New examples of Nörlund Means

We define
B_{n}
means as the class of Nörlund means, with monotone and bounded sequence
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
, such that

0<q<\infty\ \ \ \text{where}\ \ \ q_{\infty}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}q_{n}.

If the sequence
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is non-decreasing, then we have
that

nq_{0}\leq Q_{n}\leq nq_{\infty}.

In the case when the sequence
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is non-increasing,
then

nq_{\infty}\leq Q_{n}\leq nq_{0}.

In both cases we can conclude that conditions (
2.1.6
) and (
2.1.5
) are fulfilled.

Well-known examples of Nörlund means with monotone and bounded sequence
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is Fejér means

\sigma_{n}f:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}f

It is evident that in this case conditions (
2.1.6
) and (
2.1.5
) are fulfilled.

The Cesàro means
\sigma_{n}^{\alpha}
(sometimes also denoted
\left(C,\alpha\right)
) are defined by

\sigma_{n}^{\alpha}f:=\frac{1}{A_{n}^{\alpha}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}A_{n-k}^{\alpha-1}S_{k}f

where

A_{0}^{\alpha}:=0,\qquad A_{n}^{\alpha}:=\frac{\left(\alpha+1\right)...\left(\alpha+n\right)}{n!},\qquad\alpha\neq-1,-2,...

It is well-known that

A_{n}^{\alpha}=\overset{n}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}A_{n-k}^{\alpha-1},

(2.2.1)

A_{n}^{\alpha}-A_{n-1}^{\alpha}=A_{n}^{\alpha-1}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ A_{n}^{\alpha}\backsim n^{\alpha}.

(2.2.2)

It is obvious that

\frac{\left|q_{n}-q_{n+1}\right|}{n^{\alpha-2}}=O\left(1\right),\qquad\text{as}\qquad n\rightarrow\infty,

(2.2.3)

\frac{q_{0}}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\right),\qquad\ \ \ \ \ \text{as}\qquad n\rightarrow\infty,

(2.2.4)

and

\frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),\qquad\ \ \ \ \ \text{as}\qquad n\rightarrow\infty.

(2.2.5)

Let
V_{n}^{\alpha}
denote the Nörlund mean, where

\left\{q_{k}=\left(k+1\right)^{\alpha-1}:\quad k\in\mathbb{N},\quad 0<\alpha<1\right\},

that is

V_{n}^{\alpha}f:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\left(n-k-1\right)^{\alpha-1}S_{k}f.

It is obvious that

\frac{\left|q_{n}-q_{n+1}\right|}{n^{\alpha-2}}=O\left(1\right),\qquad\text{as}\qquad n\rightarrow\infty,

(2.2.6)

\frac{q_{0}}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\right),\qquad\ \ \ \ \ \text{as}\qquad n\rightarrow\infty,

(2.2.7)

and

\frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),\qquad\ \ \ \ \ \text{as}\qquad n\rightarrow\infty.

(2.2.8)

We just remind again that
n
-th Nörlund
L_{n}
and Riesz
R_{n}
logarithmic means are
defined by the sequence

\left\{q_{k}=1/k,\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right\}:

\displaystyle L_{n}f:=\frac{1}{l_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{S_{k}f}{n-k},\ \ \ R_{n}f:=\frac{1}{l_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{S_{k}f}{k},

respectively, where

l_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}.

It is evident that

\frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),\text{ \ \ as \ \ }n\rightarrow\infty

(2.2.9)

and

\frac{q_{0}}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{\ln n}\right),\text{ \ as \ }n\rightarrow\infty.

(2.2.10)

Let
U^{\alpha}_{n}
denote the Nörlund mean, where

\left\{q_{k}=\frac{1}{(k+3){\ln^{\alpha}(k+3)}}\ :\ k\in\mathbb{N},\ 0<\alpha\leq 1\right\},

that is

U_{n}^{\alpha}f:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{S_{k}f}{\left(n-k-3\right)\ln^{\alpha}\left(n-k-3\right)}.

It is obvious that

\frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),\qquad\text{as}\qquad n\rightarrow\infty

(2.2.11)

and

\frac{q_{0}}{Q_{n}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}O\left(1/\ln(\ln n\right)),\text{ \ \ \ if \ \ \ }\alpha=1,\\
O\left(1/\ln^{1-\alpha}n\right),\text{ \ \ \ \ \ if }0<\alpha<1.\end{array}\right.

(2.2.12)

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
If we define the sequence
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
by

\left\{q_{k}=\log^{\alpha}(k+1)\ :\ k\in\mathbb{N}\ :\ \alpha>0\right\},

then we get the class of Nörlund means with non-decreasing coefficients:

\beta_{n}^{\alpha}f:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log^{\alpha}\left(n-k-1\right)S_{k}f.

It is obvious that

\frac{n}{2}\log^{\alpha}\left(n/2\right)\leq Q_{n}\leq n\log^{\alpha}n.

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\leq\frac{c}{n\log^{\alpha}n}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\rightarrow 0,\text{ \ as \ }n\rightarrow\infty.

(2.2.13)

and

\displaystyle\frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}\leq\frac{c\log^{\alpha}\left(n-1\right)}{n\log^{\alpha}n}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\rightarrow 0,\text{ \ as \ }n\rightarrow\infty.

(2.2.14)

2.3

If we invoke Abel transformations for
a_{j}=A_{j}-A_{j-1},\ j=1,...,n,

\displaystyle\overset{n}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}a_{j}b_{n-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{n}b_{0}+\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}A_{j}(b_{j}-b_{j+1}),

(2.3.1)

\displaystyle\overset{n}{\underset{j=M_{N}}{\sum}}a_{j}b_{n-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{n}b_{0}-A_{M_{N}-1}b_{n-M_{N}}+\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=M_{N}}{\sum}}A_{j}(b_{j}-b_{j+1}),

(2.3.2)

when
b_{j}=q_{j}
,
a_{j}=1
and
A_{j}=j
for
j=0,1,...,n,
then (
2.3.1
) and (
2.3.2
) give the following identities:

\displaystyle Q_{n}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=0}{\sum}}q_{j}=\overset{n}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}q_{n-j}\cdot 1=\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j+q_{0}n,

(2.3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=M_{N}}{\sum}}q_{n-j}=\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=M_{N}}{\sum}}q_{n-j}\cdot 1

(2.3.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=M_{N}}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j+q_{0}n-(M_{N}-1)q_{n-M_{N}}.

Moreover, if we instead use the Abel transformations (
2.3.1
) and (
2.3.2
) for
b_{j}=q_{n-j}
,
a_{j}=D_{j}
and
A_{j}=jK_{j}
for any
j=0,1,...,n-1
we get the identities:

\displaystyle F_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\left(\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)jK_{j}+q_{0}nK_{n}\right),

(2.3.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{j=M_{N}}{\sum}}q_{n-j}D_{j}

(2.3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\left(\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=M_{N}}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)jK_{j}+q_{0}nK_{n}-q_{n-M_{N}}(M_{N}-1)K_{M_{N}-1}\right).

Analogously, if we use the Abel transformations (
2.3.1
) and (
2.3.2
) for
b_{j}=q_{j}
,
a_{j}=S_{j}
and
A_{j}=j\sigma_{j}
for any
j=0,1,...,n-1
we get the identities:

\displaystyle t_{n}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\left(\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j\sigma_{j}f+q_{0}n\sigma_{n}f\right)

(2.3.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{j=M_{N}}{\sum}}q_{n-j}S_{j}f

(2.3.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\left(\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=M_{N}}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j\sigma_{j}f+q_{0}nK_{n}-q_{n-M_{N}}(M_{N}-1)\sigma_{M_{N}-1}f\right).

First we consider Nörlund kernels with respect to Vilenkin systems, which are generated by non-decreasing sequences:

Lemma 2.3.1

Let
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of non-decreasing numbers, satisfying the condition (
2.1.6
).
Then

\left|F_{n}\right|\leq\frac{c}{n}\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{\left|n\right|}M_{j}\left|K_{M_{j}}\right|\right\},

where
c
is an absolute constant.

Proof.

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=N_{0}+1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=N_{0}+1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=0}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j<\varepsilon.

so a) is proved.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-2}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j-1}-q_{n-j}\right)j\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{N_{0}}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j-1}-q_{n-j}\right)j\alpha_{j}+\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-2}{\underset{j=N_{0}+1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j-1}-q_{n-j}\right)j\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle III_{1}+III_{2}.

Since sequence is non-increasing, we can conclude that

|q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}|<2q_{0}.

Hence,

\displaystyle III_{1}\leq\frac{2q_{0}N_{0}}{Q_{n}}\to 0,\ \ \ \text{as}\ \ \ n\to\infty

and

\displaystyle III_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-2}{\underset{j=N_{0}+1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j-1}-q_{n-j}\right)j\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon(n-1)}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-2}{\underset{j=N_{0}+1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon(n-1)}{Q_{n}}\left(q_{0}-q_{n-N_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2q_{0}\varepsilon(n-1)}{Q_{n}}<C\varepsilon.

Hence, also
III\to 0
so the proof of part b) is also complete.

Theorem 2.5.3

Let
p\geq 1
and
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of non-increasing numbers. Then

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}t_{M_{n}}f(x)=f(x)

for all Lebesgue points of
f\in L^{p}(G_{m})
.

Proof.
By using Lemma
2.3.6
we get that

\displaystyle t_{M_{n}}f\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{G_{m}}{\int}f\left(t\right)F_{n}\left(x-t\right)d\mu\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{G_{m}}{\int}f\left(t\right)D_{M_{n}}\left(x-t\right)d\mu\left(t\right)-\underset{G_{m}}{\int}f\left(t\right)\psi_{M_{n}-1}(x-t)\overline{F^{-1}}_{M_{n}}(x-t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I-II.

By applying Proposition
2.1.1
we get that
I=S_{M_{n}}f(x)\to f(x)

for all Lebesgue points of
f\in L^{p}(G_{m})
, where
p\geq 1.

Moreover, according to Proposition
1.1.1
we find that

\psi_{M_{n}-1}(x-t)=\psi_{M_{n}-1}(x)\overline{\psi}_{M_{n}-1}(t)

and

II=\psi_{M_{n}-1}(x)\underset{G_{m}}{\int}f\left(t\right)\overline{F^{-1}}_{M_{n}}(x-t)\overline{\psi}_{M_{n}-1}(t)d(t).

By combining Theorem
1.2.2
and Corollary
1.3.12
we find that the function

f\left(t\right)\overline{F^{-1}}_{M_{n}}(x-t)\in L^{p}(G_{m})\ \ \text{ where}\ \ p\geq 1\ \ \text{for any }\ \ x\in G_{m},

and
II
are Fourier coefficients of an integrable function. Hence, according to the Riemann-Lebesgue Lemma we get that

II\to 0,\text{ \ as \ }n\rightarrow\infty,\ \text{for any }\ x\in G_{m}.

The proof is complete.

Hochschild homology for log schemes
Martin Olsson

Abstract
. We extend the notions of Hochschild and cyclic homology to morphisms from algebraic spaces to algebraic stacks. Using this, we obtain generalizations to log schemes in the sense of Fontaine and Illusie of these homology theories.



1.
Introduction

The purpose of this article is to define Hochschild and cyclic homology for log schemes in the sense of Fontaine and Illusie. As in other work on logarithmic geometry of the author, the basic idea here is to view logarithmic Hochschild homology as a special case of the classical theory without log structures using the stacks
\mathbf{\mathcal{L}}og
introduced in
[
10
]
.

The approach discussed here was originally presented in a lecture in 2018
[
14
]
. Subsequently several other authors have defined Hochschild homology for log schemes including
[
4
,
5
]
. The theory developed here agrees with the one in
[
5
]
, as noted in that article. The difference between our approach and that in
[
4
]
should be akin to the comparison between the two approaches to the cotangent complex in
[
11
]
, though we do not study this here.

1.1
.

Let
k
be a ring and
M_{k}
a fine log structure on
\text{\rm Spec}(k)
. For a log flat morphism of fine log schemes

f:(X,M_{X})\rightarrow(\text{\rm Spec}(k),M_{k}),

with underlying morphism of schemes quasi-compact and quasi-separated, we define certain
k
-modules

HH_{n}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k})),

which we refer to as the
Hochschild homology groups of
(X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k})
, as well as
k
-modules

HC_{n}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k})),

which we refer to as the
cyclic homology groups of
(X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k})
.

In the case when the log structures are trivial our definitions agree with the definitions for schemes given in
[
19
,
21
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

Much of the discussion carries over without the flatness assumption. However, in the non-flat case it is better to consider derived versions of the theory. We do not consider that here and instead restrict to the flat case.

1.3
.

The Hochschild homology is obtained by taking cohomology of a complex
\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}_{(X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k})}
whose associated cohomology sheaves (with the usual reindexing) we will denote by

\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}_{*}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k})).

To define cyclic homology we need to use more structure on
\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}_{*}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k}))
as in the classical case of schemes. We will show the following properties of Hochschild and cyclic homology:

(i) (Multiplicative structure) The Hochschild homology sheaves
\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}_{*}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k}))
form a commutative graded algebra with
\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}_{0}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k})\simeq\mathscr{O}_{X}
(as sheaves of
k
-algebras).

(ii) (Connes operator) There is a generalization of the Connes operator

B:\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}_{m}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k}))\rightarrow\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}_{m+1}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k}))

giving
\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}_{*}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k}))
the structure of a commutative differential graded algebra. Moreover, the induced map

\textstyle{\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}_{0}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{B}
\textstyle{\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}_{1}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k}))}

has a natural extension to a log derivation (in the sense of
[
9
, 1.1.2]
) defining an isomorphism

\epsilon_{1}:\Omega^{1}_{(X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k})}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}_{1}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k})).

(iii) (HKR isomorphism) By the universal property of the log de Rham complex the map
\epsilon_{1}
extends to a morphism of commutative differential graded algebras

(\Omega^{\bullet}_{(X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k})},d)\rightarrow(\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}_{*}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k})),B).

If
(X,M_{X})\rightarrow(\text{\rm Spec}(k),M_{k})
is log smooth then this map is an isomorphism.

(iv) (SBI sequence) Cyclic and Hochschild homology are related by a long exact sequence

\cdots HH_{m}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k}))\rightarrow HC_{m}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k}))\rightarrow HC_{m-2}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k}))\rightarrow\cdots

generalizing the SBI sequence for schemes
[
8
, 2.2.1]
.

(v) If
X=\text{\rm Spec}(A)
is affine and
(X,M_{X})
is log smooth over
(k,M_{k})
then there is a spectral sequence abutting to
HC_{*}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k}))
with
E^{1}_{pq}
-term given by
\Omega^{q-p}_{(X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k})}
for
q\geq p\geq 0
and
0
otherwise.
If
k
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra then this spectral sequence degenerates at the
E^{2}
-page and there is an isomorphism

HC_{m}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k}))\simeq\Omega^{m}_{(X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k})}\oplus\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}H^{m-2i}_{\text{dR}}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k})).

Remark 1.4
.

(i) The decomposition in (v) above can be described more canonically using Adams operations, which we discuss in section
10
.

(ii) In (iv) above we write the SBI sequence in the form most commonly found in the literature. However, from the viewpoint of spaces with
S^{1}
-action the sequence is more canonically written with
HC_{m-2}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k}))
replaced by
HC_{m-2}((X,M_{X})/(k,M_{k}))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}H_{1}(S^{1},\mathbb{Z})
, the choice of an orientation for
S^{1}
giving an isomorphism (see for example
[
8
, D.6]
). Written this way the sequence in characterstic
0
also becomes compatible with Adams operations as in
[
7
, 4.8]
.

1.5
.

The work in this article is based on our earlier results
[
10
]
providing a dictionary between certain notions in logarithmic geometry with corresponding notions in the theory of stacks. We summarize some of the basic aspects of this dictionary in section
2
. Using this, the definitions and results about Hochschild and cyclic homology for log schemes are obtained from more general definitions and results about Hochschild and cyclic homology for morphisms
X\rightarrow\mathscr{S}
, from a scheme (or algebraic space) to an algebraic stack. We do not write out these more general statements here in the introduction, but it is in this generality that we work for most of the article.

There is one point in this regard, however, that we wish to highlight. In general, for a morphism
X\rightarrow\mathscr{S}
from an algebraic space to an algebraic stack, the universal property of
\Omega^{1}_{X/\mathscr{S}}
is not captured by derivations alone like in the case of schemes. In the logarithmic setting this manifests itself in the fact that a log derivation contains more information than an ordinary derivation. In the case of schemes, the de Rham complex
\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/\mathscr{S}}
is the universal commutative differential graded algebra receiving an algebra map from
\mathscr{O}_{X}
. In general, however, the universal property of
\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/\mathscr{S}}
is not expressed as simply. The solution to this problem used in this article involves the introduction of a site
\mathscr{C}_{X/\mathscr{S}}
which we discuss in section
6
.

Remark 1.6
.

Similar techniques to the ones employed here should enable a study of logarithmic topological Hochschild homology. A theory of logarithmic topological Hochschild homology, as well as logarithmic Hochschild homology, has been developed by Rognes, Sagave, and Schlichtkrull
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
using a different approach.

1.8.
Conventions

All schemes and stacks considered in this article will be assumed to be locally finite dimensional and locally noetherian.

2.
Log schemes and stacks

2.1
.

Recall from
[
10
]
that if
(S,M_{S})
is a fine log scheme then there is an associated algebraic stack, denoted
\mathbf{\mathcal{L}}og_{(S,M_{S})}
, over the category of
S
-schemes. The fiber of
\mathbf{\mathcal{L}}og_{(S,M_{S})}
over an
S
-scheme
f:X\rightarrow S
is the groupoid of pairs
(M_{X},f^{b})
, where
M_{X}
is a fine log structure on
X
and
f^{b}:f^{*}M_{S}\rightarrow M_{X}
is a morphism of log structures on
X
. By
[
10
, 1.1]
the stack
\mathbf{\mathcal{L}}og_{(S,M_{S})}
is an algebraic stack with finitely presented diagonal. Giving a morphism of log schemes
(X,M_{X})\rightarrow(S,M_{S})
is therefore equivalent to giving an ordinary morphism

X\rightarrow\mathbf{\mathcal{L}}og_{(S,M_{S})}.

The stack
\mathbf{\mathcal{L}}og_{(S,M_{S})}
is not of finite type in general, but we will primarily be interested in the case when
X
is quasi-compact. In this case a morphism
X\rightarrow\mathbf{\mathcal{L}}og_{(S,M_{S})}
factors through a quasi-compact open substack
\mathscr{U}\subset\mathbf{\mathcal{L}}og_{(S,M_{S})}
.

2.2
.

Properties of morphisms of log schemes can be described in terms of the corresponding morphisms of stacks. For example, a morphism of log schemes
(X,M_{X})\rightarrow(S,M_{S})
is log smooth (resp. log flat) if and only if the corresponding morphism
X\rightarrow\mathbf{\mathcal{L}}og_{(S,M_{S})}
is smooth (resp. flat) in the ordinary sense (see
[
10
, Theorem 4.6]
). Furthermore, the sheaf of logarithmic differentials
\Omega^{1}_{(X,M_{X})/(S,M_{S})}
is canonically isomorphic to the sheaf
\Omega^{1}_{X/\mathbf{\mathcal{L}}og_{(S,M_{S})}}
, and this canonical isomorphism is compatible with the universal derivations.

2.3
.

The stack
\mathbf{\mathcal{L}}og_{(S,M_{S})}
is somewhat unwieldy but there is a simpler description in terms of toric stacks.

For a fine monoid
P
the affine scheme

\text{\rm Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[P])

has a natural action of the torus
D(P^{\text{gp}}):=\text{\rm Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[P^{\text{gp}}])
, and we can consider the stack quotient

\mathcal{S}_{P}:=[\text{\rm Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[P])/D(P^{\text{gp}})].

By
[
10
, 5.20]
this stack can be viewed as the stack whose fiber over a scheme
T
is the groupoid of pairs
(M_{T},\beta)
, where
M_{T}
is a fine log structure on
T
and
\beta:P\rightarrow\overline{M}_{T}
is a morphism of sheaves of monoids which fppf locally on
T
lifts to a chart. The universal pair is obtained by descent from the natural log structure on
\text{\rm Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[P])
induced by the map
P\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[P]
.

2.4
.

More generally there is a relative version. Let
(S,M_{S})
be a fine log scheme and let
\beta_{S}:Q\rightarrow M_{S}
be a chart, with

a fine monoid. Fix also a morphism of fine monoids

is injective. Let

over

\mathcal{S}:=[\text{\rm Spec}_{S}(\mathscr{O}_{S}[P]\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{S}[Q]}\mathscr{O}_{S})/D(G)].

By
[
10
, 5.20]
the stack
\mathcal{S}
can be viewed as the stack whose fiber over an
S
-scheme

is the groupoid of triples
(M_{T},f^{b},\gamma)
, where
M_{T}
is a fine log structure on
T
,

is a morphism of log structures on
T
, and
\gamma:P\rightarrow\overline{M}_{T}
is a morphism of sheaves of monoids which fppf locally on
T
lifts to a chart and such that the diagram

\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\gamma}
\textstyle{\overline{M}_{T}}
\textstyle{Q\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\theta}
\scriptstyle{\beta}
\textstyle{f^{-1}M_{k}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces,}

2.5
.

There is a natural map

3.
Definition of Hochschild homology

3.1
.

Let
k

\mathscr{S}/k

4.
Relation with the diagonal

We continue with the notation of

4.1

6.
Interlude on differentials

6.1
.

If
X\rightarrow S
is a morphism of schemes and
E
is a quasi-coherent sheaf on
X
then the universal derivation
d:\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{X/S}
establishes a bijection between the
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module of derivations
\partial:\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow E
and
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-linear maps
\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow E
.

For a morphism
X\rightarrow\mathscr{S}
from a scheme to an algebraic stack, however, the
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module of maps
\Omega^{1}_{X/\mathscr{S}}\rightarrow E
can no longer be described easily using maps
\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow E
(at least if we only consider the small Zariski or étale topology).

Example 6.2
.

Let
k
be a field and consider the map
\text{\rm Spec}(k)\rightarrow B\mathbb{G}_{m,k}
corresponding to the trivial torsor. In this case the
k
-space
\Omega^{1}_{\text{\rm Spec}(k)/B\mathbb{G}_{m}}
is
1
-dimensional and the derivation
d:k\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{\text{\rm Spec}(k)/B\mathbb{G}_{m}}
is the zero map. For a
k
-vector space
E
the space
\text{Hom}_{k}(\Omega^{1}_{\text{\rm Spec}(k)/B\mathbb{G}_{m}},E)
is therefore isomorphic to
E
, whereas
the space of
k
-linear derivations
k\rightarrow E
is
0
.

6.3
.

We can address this issue by considering a bigger topology, which is intermediate between the big and small étale site, as follows. We call this topology the
relative étale topology
.

Let

be a morphism from an algebraic space to an algebraic stack. Let
\mathscr{C}_{X/\mathscr{S}}
be the category of
2
-commutative diagrams

(6.3.1)

\textstyle{U\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{S\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathscr{S},}

where

is a smooth morphism from a scheme and the induced map

is étale. We usually abbreviate the data of such an object by
(S,U\rightarrow X_{S})
. A morphism

(S^{\prime},U^{\prime}\rightarrow X_{S^{\prime}})\rightarrow(S,U\rightarrow X_{S})

in

is given by a

\textstyle{U\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{U^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Lemma 6.4

The category

[

Proof.

The axioms (1) and (2) in

are immediate from the corresponding properties of the étale site of an algebraic space. To verify axiom (3), let

be a covering, and let

v:\mathscr{C}_{V/\mathscr{S}}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}_{X/\mathscr{S}},\ \ (S,U\rightarrow V_{S})\mapsto(S,U\rightarrow V_{S}\rightarrow X_{S}).

There is also a functor

Definition 6.10

A

-algebras

\mathscr{O}_{X}
-algebras compatible with the differentials

Note that the differential

7.1

The sheaves on

7.2

To this end, first recall some basic results about the usual Hochschild homology of a map of rings

(i) The graded

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A^{\prime}}
\textstyle{k\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

(HKR isomorphism)

Reducible Suspensions of Anosov Representations
Max Lahn

Abstract
. We study through the lens of Anosov representations the dynamical properties of reducible suspensions of linear representations of non-elementary hyperbolic groups, which are linear representations preserving and acting weakly unipotently on a proper non-zero subspace. We characterize when reducible suspensions are discrete and (almost) faithful, quasi-isometrically embedded, and Anosov. Anosov reducible suspensions correspond to points in bounded convex domains in a finite-dimensional real vector space. Stronger characterizations of such domains for symmetric Anosov representations allow us to find deformations of Borel Anosov representations which retain some but not all of the Anosov conditions and to compute examples of non-Anosov limits of Anosov representations.



1.
Introduction

Hitchin components consist of deformations of irreducibly embedded Fuchsian representations of closed hyperbolic surface groups. These Hitchin representations are Borel Anosov
[
Lab06
, Theorem 4.1, Theorem 4.2]
and therefore discrete, faithful
[
Lab06
, Theorem 1.5]
, and quasi-isometric embeddings
[
GW12
, Theorem 5.3]
. On the other hand, large deformations of
reducibly
embedded Fuchsian representations of hyperbolic surface groups need not be Anosov, discrete, faithful, or quasi-isometrically embedded; the most relevant examples in
\mathrm{SL}_{3}\left\lparen\mathbb{R}\right\rparen
are due to Barbot
[
Bar01
,
Bar10
]
.

In this article, we generalize these examples of Barbot to arbitrary word hyperbolic groups and specific matrix groups of higher rank, and we investigate the dynamical properties of certain deformations within the space of reducible representations. Specifically, fix a non-elementary word hyperbolic group
\Gamma
, a field
\mathbb{K}
, either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, and an integer
d\geq 3
. We aim to study the dynamical properties of what we call
reducible suspensions
of linear representations of
\Gamma
, which are those representations
\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{d}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
which preserve and act up to projectivization by weakly unipotent transformations of a proper non-zero subspace of
\mathbb{K}^{d}
. Here,
\mathrm{SL}_{d}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
is the matrix group

\mathrm{SL}_{d}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen=\left\{\mathbf{A}\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen:\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\right\rvert=1\right\},

which is a smooth fiber bundle over
\mathrm{PGL}_{d}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
with fiber
\mathbb{S}^{0}=\left\{\pm 1\right\}
if
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{S}^{1}
if
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
.

Concretely, a reducible suspension of a representation
\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
is conjugate to a representation of the form
\rho=\rho_{p,q}\left\lparen\varphi,\xi,\zeta,\kappa\right\rparen\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{d}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
defined by the block upper-triangular formula

\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{1}{p}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\xi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen&\kappa\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\\
\mathbf{0}&e^{-\frac{1}{q}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\zeta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\end{pmatrix}

for some decomposition
d=p+q
with
p\geq 1
and
q\geq 2
, homomorphism
\varphi\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
, representation
\xi\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}U_{p}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
, and map
\kappa\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{K}^{p\times q}
, whose images are considered as
p\times q
matrices with entries in
\mathbb{K}
. Here,
U_{p}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
is the set

U_{p}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen=\left\{\mathbf{A}\in\mathrm{SL}_{p}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen:\lambda_{1}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen=\dotsb=\lambda_{p}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen=1\right\}

of
weakly unipotent
matrices, those whose complex eigenvalues are all of unit modulus; this is not a group, and so we clarify that by a representation
\Gamma\xrightarrow{}U_{p}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
we mean a representation
\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{p}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
whose image is contained in
U_{p}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
. In order for the above formula for
\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen
to define a homomorphism, we require that
\kappa\left\lparen 1\right\rparen=\mathbf{0}
and that

\kappa\left\lparen\gamma\delta\right\rparen=e^{\frac{1}{p}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\xi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\kappa\left\lparen\delta\right\rparen+e^{-\frac{1}{q}\varphi\left\lparen\delta\right\rparen}\kappa\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\zeta\left\lparen\delta\right\rparen

for all
\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma
. We will describe a map
\kappa\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{K}^{p\times q}
which satisfies these conditions as being
compatible
with
\varphi
,
\xi
, and
\zeta
.

When
\Gamma
is a closed surface group,
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
,
d=3
,
p=1
,
q=2
, and
\zeta
is discrete and faithful, these representations are precisely the
radial representations
as described by Barbot
[
Bar01
,
Bar10
]
, and whether or not the resulting representation is Anosov is determined entirely by
\varphi
; specifically, those
\varphi
which correspond to Anosov radial representations comprise a metric open ball in the stable norm on
\mathrm{H}^{1}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathbb{R}\right\rparen

[
Bar10
, Theorem 4.2]
. Our main results are to extend and refine this result for reducible suspensions of Anosov representations of arbitrary hyperbolic groups; even in this more general setting, whether or not a reducible suspension is Anosov is still determined entirely by the homomorphism
\varphi\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
. Specifically, for integers
p\geq 1
and
1\leq k\leq\frac{q}{2}
and a representation
\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
, we will denote

A^{p}_{k}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen=\left\{\varphi\in\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathbb{R}\right\rparen:\begin{matrix}\textrm{$\rho_{p,q}\left\lparen\varphi,\xi,\zeta,\kappa\right\rparen$ is $P_{k}$-Anosov for some (every) $\xi\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}U_{p}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen$}\\
\textrm{and some (every) compatible $\kappa\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{K}^{p\times q}$}\end{matrix}\right\}.

That
A^{p}_{k}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen
is well-defined is a consequence of
4.1
. In fact,
A^{p}_{k}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen
is a bounded convex domain in the finite-dimensional vector space
\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathbb{R}\right\rparen
.

Theorem 1
.

A^{p}_{k}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen
is a bounded, convex, and open in
\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathbb{R}\right\rparen
for any integers
p\geq 1
,
q\geq 2
, and
1\leq k\leq\frac{q}{2}
and representation
\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
.

We note as an immediate consequence of
4.2
that
A^{p}_{k}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen
is empty when
\zeta
is not
P_{k}
-Anosov, and thus
Theorem 1
is vacuously true in this case. On the other hand, we note that
A^{p}_{k}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen
may be empty even if
\zeta
is
P_{k}
-Anosov.

Drawing motivation from both Hitchin representations and Barbot’s radial representations, our main examples of interest will be reducible suspensions of Anosov
q
-Fuchsian/Kleinian representations, those representations for which
\zeta=\iota_{q}\circ\eta
factors as the composition of an Anosov representation
\eta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{2}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
with the irreducible representation
\iota_{q}\colon\mathrm{SL}_{2}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen\xhookrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
. Along with Anosov representations with images contained in the indefinite orthogonal/unitary group or the symplectic group, these are examples of
symmetric Anosov
representations, for which we are able to precisely determine which reducible suspensions are Anosov.

Theorem 2
.

For any integers
p\geq 1
,
q\geq 2
, and
1\leq k\leq\frac{q}{2}
and any symmetric
P_{k}
-Anosov representation
\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
,

A^{p}_{k}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen=\left\{\varphi\in\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathbb{R}\right\rparen:\inf_{\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\neq 0}{\frac{\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\zeta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rparen}{\left\lvert\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rvert}}>\frac{p+q}{pq}\right\}.

We are also able to gain more easily computable bounds on the sizes of these convex domains in terms of dynamical data associated to the symmetric Anosov representation
\zeta
. Fix a finite generating set
\Sigma
for
\Gamma
, and note that such a choice defines a uniform norm
\left\lVert\>\cdot\>\right\rVert_{\infty}
on
\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathbb{R}\right\rparen
. We denote the metric open ball of radius
r
centered at
0
by
B\left\lparen 0,r\right\rparen
.

Corollary 3
.

For any integers
p\geq 1
,
q\geq 2
, and
1\leq k\leq\frac{q}{2}
and any symmetric
P_{k}
-Anosov representation
\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
,

\mathrm{B}\left\lparen 0,\frac{pq}{p+q}\cdot s_{k}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen\right\rparen\subseteq A^{p}_{k}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen\subseteq\mathrm{B}\left\lparen 0,\frac{pq}{p+q}\cdot\max_{\sigma\in\Sigma}{\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\zeta\left\lparen\sigma\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen.

In the above statement,
s_{k}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen
is the optimal slope for the linear growth of
\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\zeta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rparen
in terms of the translation length
\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}
; this is expounded upon in
Section 2
.

If a representation
\eta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{2}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
is Anosov, then the corresponding
q
-Fuchsian/Kleinian representation
\iota_{q}\circ\eta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
is symmetric Borel Anosov and therefore satisfies the hypotheses of
Theorem 2
for all integers
1\leq k\leq\frac{q}{2}
simultaneously. In this case, a formula for the eigenvalues of
\iota_{q}\circ\eta
(see
2.4
) allows us to compare the convex domains established in
Theorem 1
and
Theorem 2
for different parabolic subgroups.

Corollary 4
.

For any Anosov representation
\eta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{2}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
,

A^{p}_{k}\left\lparen\iota_{q}\circ\eta\right\rparen=\left\{\varphi\in\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathbb{R}\right\rparen:\inf_{\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\neq 0}{\frac{\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\lambda_{1}\left\lparen\eta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rparen}{\left\lvert\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rvert}}>\frac{p+q}{pq\left\lparen q-2k+1\right\rparen}\right\}

for all integers
p\geq 1
,
q\geq 2
, and
1\leq k\leq\frac{q}{2}
. Moreover,
A^{p}_{k}\left\lparen\iota_{q}\circ\eta\right\rparen
is strictly increasing in
p
and
q
and strictly decreasing in
k
; that is,

\displaystyle A^{p}_{k}\left\lparen\iota_{q}\circ\eta\right\rparen

\displaystyle\subsetneq A^{p+1}_{k}\left\lparen\iota_{q}\circ\eta\right\rparen

\displaystyle A^{p}_{k}\left\lparen\iota_{q}\circ\eta\right\rparen

\displaystyle\subsetneq A^{p}_{k}\left\lparen\iota_{q+1}\circ\eta\right\rparen

\displaystyle A^{p}_{k}\left\lparen\iota_{q}\circ\eta\right\rparen

\displaystyle\supsetneq A^{p}_{k+1}\left\lparen\iota_{q}\circ\eta\right\rparen

Some of the reducible suspensions of these Anosov
q
-Fuchsian/Kleinian representations are themselves Anosov, but the boundedness conclusions of
Theorem 1
and
3
each imply that unlike in the case of Hitchin representations, large deformations of these reducible suspensions need not remain so. In particular, the aforementioned convex domains corresponding to Anosov reducible suspensions are strictly nested, and so we can find deformations of Borel Anosov representations which retain some but not all of the Anosov properties.

Corollary 5
.

If
\Gamma
admits an Anosov representation in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
and
d\geq 3
is odd, then for all integers
1\leq j\leq\frac{d}{2}
, there are continuous deformations of a Borel Anosov representation in
\mathrm{SL}_{d}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
which are
P_{k}
-Anosov for all integers
1\leq k\leq j
but not
P_{k}
-Anosov for any integer
j<k\leq\frac{d}{2}
.

We will pay particular attention to the reducible suspensions in
\mathrm{SL}_{3}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
(that is, when
p=1
and
q=2
), since this setting severely restricts the complexities in the general case. For an example of this, one may note that any reducible representation of
\Gamma
in
\mathrm{SL}_{3}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
is a reducible suspension or its contragredient, and that the nilpotence hypothesis of
3.1
are automatically satisfied in this case. From an analysis of these representations, we are able to make conclusions of independent interest.

Corollary 6
.

Any reducible, discrete, and faithful representation of a closed hyperbolic surface group in
\mathrm{SL}_{3}^{\pm}\left\lparen\mathbb{R}\right\rparen
or a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold group in
\mathrm{SL}_{3}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{C}\right\rparen
is a quasi-isometric embedding.

We expect that the conclusions of
6
do not hold for irreducible representations.

2.
Background and Notation

Originally introduced by Labourie
[
Lab06
, Section 3.1.2]
, Anosov representations provide a general framework through which many of the desirable properties of convex-cocompact discrete subgroups of rank one Lie groups may be recovered in the more general setting of discrete subgroups of Lie groups of arbitrary rank. Anosov representations encompass the monodromy representations of convex-cocompact real and complex hyperbolic manifolds, Hitchin representations of closed hyperbolic surface groups, maximal representations, and the monodromy representations of closed strictly convex projective manifolds, to name a few examples.

We recall that throughout this article,
\Gamma
is assumed to be a non-elementary word hyperbolic group, and we begin by making the following preliminary definitions. To each finite generating set
\Sigma
for
\Gamma
is associated a
word length
function
\left\lvert\>\cdot\>\right\rvert_{\Sigma}\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{N}
defined by the formula

\left\lvert\gamma\right\rvert_{\Sigma}=\inf\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\gamma=\sigma_{1}\dotsm\sigma_{n}\textrm{ for some }\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\sigma_{n}\in\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{-1}\right\}

and a
translation length
function
\left\lVert\>\cdot\>\right\rVert_{\Sigma}\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{N}
defined by the formula
\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}=\inf_{\delta\in\Gamma}{\left\lvert\delta\gamma\delta^{-1}\right\rvert_{\Sigma}}
. Moreover, to each matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathrm{SL}_{d}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
we associate
d

eigenvalue magnitudes

\lambda_{1}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\geq\lambda_{2}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen\geq\dotsb\geq\lambda_{d}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen.

These are the magnitudes of the complex eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}
, counted with algebraic multiplicity and listed in non-increasing order. Finally, given two functions
f,g\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
, we will say that
f\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen

grows at least linearly
in
g\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen
if there are constants
a>0
and
b\geq 0
so that

f\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\geq ag\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen-b

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. With this terminology and the above notation, we can define Anosov representations as follows:

Definition 2.1
.

For an integer
1\leq k\leq\frac{d}{2}
, a representation
\rho\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}_{d}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
is called
P_{k}
-Anosov
if the
k
th logarithmic eigenvalue gap
\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\frac{\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}{\lambda_{k+1}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen
grows at least linearly in the translation length
\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}
; that is,
\rho
is
P_{k}
-Anosov if there are constants
a>0
and
b\geq 0
so that

\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\frac{\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}{\lambda_{k+1}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen\geq a\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}-b

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. A representation
\rho\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}_{d}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
is called
Anosov
if it is
P_{k}
-Anosov for some integer
1\leq k\leq\frac{d}{2}
, and
\rho
is called
Borel Anosov
if it is
P_{k}
-Anosov for all integers
1\leq k\leq\frac{d}{2}
.

We note that a different choice
\Sigma^{\prime}
of finite generating set induces different translation lengths
\left\lVert\>\cdot\>\right\rVert_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
but not a different notion of
P_{k}
-Anosov, since each of
\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}
and
\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
grows at least linearly in the other.

Remark 2.2
.

In the setting of representations of
\Gamma
in the reductive Lie group
\mathrm{GL}_{d}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
, the above characterization of Anosov representations is due to Kassel–Potrie
[
KP22
, Corollary 4.6]
, and it is equivalent both to the original definition given by Labourie
[
Lab06
, Section 3.1.2]
in the case of closed hyperbolic surface groups and to several subsequent characterizations by Guichard–Wienhard
[
GW12
, Definition 2.10, Proposition 3.16]
, Kapovich–Leeb–Porti
[
KLP17
, Equivalence Theorem 1.1]

[
KLP18
, Theorem 1.5, Corollary 1.6]
, Guéritaud–Guichard–Kassel–Wienhard
[
Gué+17
, Theorem 1.3, Theorem 1.7]
, Bochi–Potrie–Sambarino
[
BPS19
, Proposition 4.5, Proposition 4.9]
, and Tsouvalas
[
Tso22
, Theorem 1.1]
.

A
P_{k}
-Anosov representation
\rho\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}_{d}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
comes equipped with an
Anosov limit map

\xi_{\rho}^{k}\colon\partial\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}^{d}\right\rparen
on the Gromov boundary
[
GW12
, Definition 2.10]
. This continuous and
\rho
-equivariant map is
dynamics-preserving
; that is, for any infinite order
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
\xi_{\rho}^{k}\left\lparen\gamma^{+}\right\rparen
is the unique attracting fixed point
\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen^{+}_{k}
for the action of
\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen
on the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}^{d}\right\rparen

[
GW12
, Lemma 3.1]
.

The
P_{k}
-Anosov condition is also well-known for being
stable
; that is, any small deformation of a
P_{k}
-Anosov representation is also
P_{k}
-Anosov.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
GW12
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

The set of
P_{k}
-Anosov representations
\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}_{d}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
is open in the representation variety
\mathrm{hom}\left\lparen\Gamma,\mathrm{GL}_{d}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen\right\rparen
.

We will call a representation
\rho\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}_{d}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen

symmetric
P_{k}
-Anosov
if it is
P_{k}
-Anosov and
\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen=\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma^{-1}\right\rparen\right\rparen
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Note that this automatically implies also that

\lambda_{d-k+1}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma^{-1}\right\rparen\right\rparen}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}=\lambda_{d-k+1}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma^{-1}\right\rparen\right\rparen

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Examples 2.4
.

The following are examples of symmetric Anosov representations:

•

Let
\eta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{2}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
be a Fuchsian/Kleinian representation. The composition
\iota_{q}\circ\eta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
with an irreducible representation
\iota_{q}\colon\mathrm{SL}_{2}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen\xhookrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
is called
q
-Fuchsian
or
q
-Kleinian
(depending on whether
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
). If
\eta
is Anosov, then
\iota_{q}\circ\eta
is symmetric Borel Anosov, since

\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\iota_{q}\left\lparen\eta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rparen=\lambda_{1}\left\lparen\eta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen^{q-2k+1}

for all integers
1\leq k\leq q
.

•

Fix a matrix
\Omega\in\mathrm{GL}_{q}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
, and consider the matrix group

G_{\Omega}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen=\left\{\mathbf{A}\in\mathrm{GL}_{q}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen:\Omega\mathbf{A}\Omega^{-1}={\mathbf{A}}^{-\mathrm{T}}\right\}.

Then any
P_{k}
-Anosov representation
\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}_{q}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
with image contained in
G_{\Omega}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
is symmetric
P_{k}
-Anosov, since

\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen=\lambda_{k}\left\lparen{\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\rparen=\frac{1}{\lambda_{q-k}\left\lparen{\mathbf{A}}^{-\mathrm{T}}\right\rparen}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{q-k}\left\lparen\Omega\mathbf{A}\Omega^{-1}\right\rparen}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{q-k}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}\right\rparen}=\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\mathbf{A}^{-1}\right\rparen

for all
\mathbf{A}\in G_{\Omega}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
. Particular choices of
\Omega
imply that an Anosov representation with image contained in the indefinite orthogonal group
\mathrm{O}\left\lparen r,s\right\rparen
, the indefinite unitary group
\mathrm{U}\left\lparen r,s\right\rparen
, or the symplectic group
\mathrm{Sp}_{q}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
is symmetric Anosov.

We also note that this class of examples subsumes the previous, since by work of McGarraghy
[
McG05
]
,
q
-Fuchsian/Kleinian representations have image contained in
\mathrm{Sp}_{q}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
when
q
is even and
\mathrm{SU}\left\lparen\frac{q-1}{2},\frac{q+1}{2}\right\rparen
when
q
is odd.

Lemma 2.5
.

If a representation
\rho\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}_{d}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
is symmetric
P_{k}
-Anosov, then
\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rparen
grows at least linearly in
\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}
.

Proof.

Note that

\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rparen=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen^{2}\right\rparen=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\frac{\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}{\lambda_{d-k+1}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\frac{\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}{\lambda_{k+1}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen}\right\rparen

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Since
\rho
is
P_{k}
-Anosov, the last of these expressions grows at least linearly in
\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}
. ∎

For such a symmetric
P_{k}
-Anosov representation
\rho\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}_{d}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
, we will denote the optimal slope for the linear growth of
\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rparen
in terms of
\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}
by
s_{k}\left\lparen\rho\right\rparen
; that is,

s_{k}\left\lparen\rho\right\rparen=\sup\left\{a\in\left\lparen{0},{\infty}\right\rparen:\textrm{There is some }b\geq 0\textrm{ so that }\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\lambda_{k}\left\lparen\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rparen\geq a\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\Sigma}-b\textrm{ for all }\gamma\in\Gamma\right\}.

Finally, we end this section with a useful lemma about commutators in non-elementary hyperbolic groups.

Lemma 2.6
.

Fix a sequence
\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen_{n=0}^{\infty}
in a non-elementary hyperbolic group
\Gamma
. If
\gamma_{n}
is not eventually central, then there is some
\delta\in\Gamma
so that
\left[{\gamma_{n}},{\delta}\right]
is not eventually
1
.

Proof.

Note that we may without loss of generality pass to subsequences. Since
\Gamma
acts on its Gromov boundary
\partial\Gamma
as a convergence group, we may pass to a subsequence so that either
\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen_{n=0}^{\infty}
is eventually constant or there are ideal points
a,b\in\partial\Gamma
so that
\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen_{n=0}^{\infty}
converges uniformly to
a
on compact subsets of
\partial\Gamma\setminus\left\{b\right\}
.

First, suppose that
\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen_{n=0}^{\infty}
is eventually constant; that is, there is some
\gamma\in\Gamma
so that
\gamma_{n}=\gamma
for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen_{n=0}^{\infty}
is not eventually central,
\gamma
is not central, and thus
\left[{\gamma},{\delta}\right]\neq 1
for some
\delta\in\Gamma
. In particular,
\left[{\gamma_{n}},{\delta}\right]
is eventually not
1
.

On the other hand, suppose that there are ideal points
a,b\in\partial\Gamma
so that
\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen_{n=0}^{\infty}
converges uniformly to
a
on compact subsets of
\partial\Gamma\setminus\left\{b\right\}
. Since
\Gamma
is non-elementary, there is an infinite order
\delta\in\Gamma
so that
\left\{a,b\right\}\cap\left\{\delta^{+},\delta^{-}\right\}=\emptyset
. Suppose for a contradiction that
\left[{\gamma_{n}},{\delta}\right]=1
for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we may pass to yet another subsequence so that
\left[{\gamma_{n}},{\delta}\right]=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which implies that

a=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\gamma_{n}\cdot\delta^{+}}=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\delta\gamma_{n}^{-1}\right\rparen^{+}}=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\delta^{+}}=\delta^{+}\neq a.

This contradiction implies that
\left[{\gamma_{n}},{\delta}\right]
is eventually not
1
. ∎

3.
Discreteness and Quasi-Isometric Embeddings

In this section, we will investigate when a reducible suspension is discrete and faithful and when it is a quasi-isometric embedding. To that end, fix for this section a homomorphism
\varphi\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
, representations
\xi\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}U_{p}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
and
\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
, and a compatible map
\kappa\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{K}^{p\times q}
, and consider the reducible suspension
\rho=\rho_{p,q}\left\lparen\varphi,\xi,\zeta,\kappa\right\rparen
. We first aim to characterize when
\rho
is discrete and faithful in terms of when
\zeta
is.

Proposition 3.1
.

1.

If
\zeta
is discrete and faithful (resp. has finite kernel), then
\rho
is discrete and faithful (resp. has finite kernel);

2.

If
\Gamma
is centerless,
\xi\left\lparen\Gamma\right\rparen
is nilpotent, and
\rho
is discrete and faithful, then
\zeta
is discrete and faithful; and

3.

If
\xi\left\lparen\Gamma\right\rparen
is nilpotent and
\rho
is discrete and has finite kernel, then
\zeta
is discrete and has finite kernel.

Proof.

1.

Consider a sequence
\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen_{n=0}^{\infty}
in
\Gamma
so that
\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\rho\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen}=\mathbf{I}_{p+q}
. The above formula for
\rho
implies that
\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{e^{-\frac{1}{q}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen}\zeta\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen}=\mathbf{I}_{q}
, so that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{e^{\frac{1}{q}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen}}

\displaystyle=\left\lparen\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{e^{-\varphi\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen}}\right\rparen^{-\frac{1}{q}}=\left\lparen\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen e^{-\frac{1}{q}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen}\zeta\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rvert}\right\rparen^{-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{e^{-\frac{1}{q}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen}\zeta\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen}\right\rparen\right\rvert^{-\frac{1}{q}}=\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\mathbf{I}_{q}\right\rparen\right\rvert^{-\frac{1}{q}}=1.

In particular,

\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\zeta\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen}=\left\lparen\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{e^{\frac{1}{q}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen}}\right\rparen\left\lparen\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{e^{-\frac{1}{q}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen}\zeta\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen}\right\rparen=1\cdot\mathbf{I}_{q}=\mathbf{I}_{q}.

If
\zeta
is discrete, this implies that
\gamma_{n}\in\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen
for all but finitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen
is finite, this further implies that
\rho
is discrete and
\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\rho\right\rparen\subseteq\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen
is finite. The analogous result for discrete and faithful representations follows by taking
\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen=\left\{1\right\}
.

2.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{A}_{n}=e^{-\frac{n}{p}}\mathbf{I}_{p}\oplus e^{\frac{n}{q}}\mathbf{I}_{q}
, and consider the representation
\rho_{n}\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{p+q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
defined by the formula

\rho_{n}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\mathbf{A}_{n}\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\mathbf{A}_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{1}{p}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\xi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen&e^{-\left\lparen\frac{p+q}{pq}\right\rparen n}\kappa\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\\
\mathbf{0}&e^{-\frac{1}{q}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\zeta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\end{pmatrix}.

Then the sequence
\left\lparen\rho_{n}\right\rparen_{n=0}^{\infty}
converges pointwise to the representation
\rho^{\prime}\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{p+q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
defined by the formula

\rho^{\prime}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=e^{\frac{1}{p}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\xi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\oplus e^{-\frac{1}{q}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\zeta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{1}{p}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\xi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&e^{-\frac{1}{q}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\zeta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
\rho^{\prime}=\rho_{p,q}\left\lparen\varphi,\xi,\zeta,\mathbf{0}\right\rparen
; that is,
\rho^{\prime}=\rho
in the case that
\kappa\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\mathbf{0}
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Furthermore, since each
\rho_{n}
is conjugate to
\rho=\rho_{0}
, it is discrete and faithful, and given that
\Gamma
is a non-elementary word hyperbolic group, the limit
\rho^{\prime}
is discrete and faithful by a well-known consequence
[
Kap10
, Theorem 8.4]
of the Margulis–Zassenhaus lemma
[
Zas37
]

[
KM68
, Theorem 1]
.

In the following computation, for any group
G
and integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we will denote by
c_{k}^{G}\colon G^{k+1}\xrightarrow{}G
the
k
th nested commutator map, which is defined recursively by the formulas
c_{0}^{G}\left\lparen a_{0}\right\rparen=a_{0}
and

c_{k+1}^{G}\left\lparen a_{0},\dotsc,a_{k+1}\right\rparen=\left[{c_{k}^{G}\left\lparen a_{0},\dotsc,a_{k}\right\rparen},{a_{k+1}}\right]=c_{k}^{G}\left\lparen a_{0},\dotsc,a_{k}\right\rparen a_{k+1}c_{k}^{G}\left\lparen a_{0},\dotsc,a_{k}\right\rparen^{-1}a_{k+1}^{-1}.

Let
\ell
denote the length of the lower central series for
\xi\left\lparen\Gamma\right\rparen
, so that
c_{\ell}^{\xi\left\lparen\Gamma\right\rparen}\left\lparen\xi\left\lparen\Gamma\right\rparen^{\ell+1}\right\rparen=\left\{\mathbf{I}_{p}\right\}
is trivial, and suppose for a contradiction that
\zeta
is not discrete and faithful. Then

\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\zeta\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen}=\mathbf{I}_{q}

for some sequence
\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen_{n=0}^{\infty}
in
\Gamma
which is not eventually
1
. Applying
2.6

\ell
times and passing to a subsequence (which we also denote
\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen_{n=0}^{\infty}
), we obtain
\delta_{1},\dotsc,\delta_{\ell}\in\Gamma
so that
c^{\Gamma}_{\ell}\left\lparen\gamma_{n},\delta_{1},\dotsc,\delta_{\ell}\right\rparen
is not eventually
1
. However, note that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\zeta\left\lparen c_{\ell}^{\Gamma}\left\lparen\gamma_{n},\delta_{1},\dotsc,\delta_{\ell}\right\rparen\right\rparen}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{c^{\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen}_{\ell}\left\lparen\zeta\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen,\zeta\left\lparen\delta_{1}\right\rparen,\dotsc,\zeta\left\lparen\delta_{\ell}\right\rparen\right\rparen}

\displaystyle=c^{\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen}_{\ell}\left\lparen\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\zeta\left\lparen\gamma_{n}\right\rparen},\zeta\left\lparen\delta_{1}\right\rparen,\dotsc,\zeta\left\lparen\delta_{\ell}\right\rparen\right\rparen

\displaystyle=c_{\ell}^{\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen}\left\lparen\mathbf{I}_{q},\zeta\left\lparen\delta_{1}\right\rparen,\dotsc,\zeta\left\lparen\delta_{\ell}\right\rparen\right\rparen=\mathbf{I}_{q},

and so

\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\rho^{\prime}\left\lparen c^{\Gamma}_{\ell}\left\lparen\gamma_{n},\delta_{1},\dotsc,\delta_{\ell}\right\rparen\right\rparen}=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}{\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{I}_{p}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\zeta\left\lparen c^{\Gamma}_{\ell}\left\lparen\gamma_{n},\delta_{1},\dotsc,\delta_{\ell}\right\rparen\right\rparen\end{pmatrix}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{I}_{p}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{I}_{q}\end{pmatrix}=\mathbf{I}_{p+q}.

This contradiction implies that
\zeta
is discrete and faithful.

3.

We will first show that
\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\rho\right\rparen\subseteq\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\varphi\right\rparen\cap\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\xi\right\rparen\cap\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\zeta\right\rparen\cap\kappa^{-1}\left\lparen\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}\right\rparen
. To that end, let
\gamma\in\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\rho\right\rparen
, and note that

\displaystyle\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen

\displaystyle=\mathrm{log}\left\lparen e^{\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\right\rparen=\mathrm{log}\left\lparen e^{\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\xi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rvert\right\rparen=\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen e^{\frac{1}{p}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\xi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\right\rparen\right\rvert\right\rparen=\mathrm{log}\left\lparen\left\lvert\mathrm{det}\left\lparen\mathbf{I}_{p}\right\rparen\right\rvert\right\rparen=0,

so that

\begin{pmatrix}\xi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen&\kappa\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\\
\mathbf{0}&\zeta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{1}{p}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\xi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen&\kappa\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\\
\mathbf{0}&e^{-\frac{1}{q}\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}\zeta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen\end{pmatrix}=\mathbf{I}_{p+q}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{I}_{p}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{I}_{q}\end{pmatrix}.

In summary, we have shown that if
\rho\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\mathbf{I}_{p+q}
, then
\varphi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=0
,
\xi\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\mathbf{I}_{p}
,
\zeta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\mathbf{I}_{q}
, and
\kappa\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen=\mathbf{0}
. Thus these maps descend to a homomorphism
\overline{\varphi}\colon\overline{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}
, representations
\overline{\rho}\colon\overline{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{p+q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
,
\overline{\xi}\colon\overline{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{}U_{p}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
, and
\overline{\zeta}\colon\overline{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
, and a compatible
\overline{\kappa}\colon\overline{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{K}^{p\times q}
, where
\overline{\Gamma}
is the non-elementary hyperbolic group
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\Gamma}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\rho\right\rparen}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\Gamma}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\rho\right\rparen}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\Gamma}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\rho\right\rparen}$}}{\raisebox{1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\Gamma}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\rho\right\rparen}$}}}
. Note that
\overline{\rho}
is discrete and faithful and itself a reducible suspension (of
\overline{\zeta}
).

As a finitely generated linear group,
\overline{\Gamma}
contains a finite index torsion-free subgroup
\overline{\Delta}
by the Selberg lemma
[
Sel62
, Lemma 8]
. Note that
\overline{\Delta}
is itself a torsion-free non-elementary hyperbolic group and therefore centerless. Applying part (2) to
\left.\overline{\rho}\right|_{\overline{\Delta}}=\rho_{p,q}\left\lparen\left.\overline{\varphi}\right|_{\overline{\Delta}},\left.\overline{\xi}\right|_{\overline{\Delta}},\left.\overline{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta}},\left.\overline{\kappa}\right|_{\overline{\Delta}}\right\rparen
, we find that
\left.\overline{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta}}
is discrete and faithful. Let
\Delta
be the preimage of
\overline{\Delta}
under the quotient map
\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow\overline{\Gamma}
, and note that
\left.\zeta\right|_{\Delta}
is discrete and has finite kernel, since
\mathrm{ker}\left\lparen\rho\right\rparen
is finite. Since
\Delta
has finite index
\left[{\Gamma}:{\Delta}\right]=\left[{\overline{\Gamma}}:{\overline{\Delta}}\right]
in
\Gamma
, this implies that
\zeta
is discrete and has finite kernel. ∎

Remark 3.2
.

The hypothesis that
\Gamma
is centerless in (2) of the above result is necessary; for example, extend a discrete and faithful representation
\zeta\colon\Gamma\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
to a representation
\widehat{\zeta}\colon\Gamma\times{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{2\mathbb{Z}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{2\mathbb{Z}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{2\mathbb{Z}}$}}{\raisebox{1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{2\mathbb{Z}}$}}}\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
with the formula
\widehat{\zeta}\left\lparen\gamma,k+2\mathbb{Z}\right\rparen=\zeta\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen
. Then
\widehat{\zeta}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen
is not faithful, but the reducible suspension
\rho\colon\Gamma\times{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{2\mathbb{Z}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{2\mathbb{Z}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{2\mathbb{Z}}$}}{\raisebox{1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{2\mathbb{Z}}$}}}\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{SL}_{d}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
defined by the formula
\rho\left\lparen\gamma,k+2\mathbb{Z}\right\rparen=\left\lparen-1\right\rparen^{k}\mathbf{I}_{p}\oplus\widehat{\zeta}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen
is discrete and faithful.

As a consequence of
3.1
, we obtain hypotheses under which a reducible suspension is discrete and faithful (resp. has finite kernel) if and only if the original representation is.

Corollary 3.3
.

Suppose that
\xi
has nilpotent image.

1.

\rho
is discrete and has finite kernel if and only if
\zeta
is discrete and has finite kernel; and

2.

If
\Gamma
is centerless, then
\rho
is discrete and faithful if and only if
\zeta
is discrete and faithful.

Proof.

(1) follows from (1) and (3) of
3.1
, and (2) follows from (1) and (2) of
3.1
. ∎

We now consider when
\rho
is a quasi-isometric embedding in terms of when
\zeta
is a quasi-isometric embedding. We recall that work of Delzant–Guichard–Labourie–Mozes
[
Del+11
]
implies that these conditions are equivalent to inducing displacing actions on the appropriate symmetric spaces.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
\zeta
is a quasi-isometric embedding, then
\rho
is a quasi-isometric embedding.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathrm{len}_{\zeta}
and
\mathrm{len}_{\rho}
the translation lengths of the respective actions of
\Gamma
on the symmetric spaces of
\mathrm{SL}_{q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
and
\mathrm{SL}_{p+q}^{*}\left\lparen\mathbb{K}\right\rparen
, respectively. By
[
Del+11
, Corollary 4.0.6]
, the first of these actions is
displacing
; that is,
\mathrm{len}_{\zeta}
grows at least linearly in the translation length
\left\lVert\>\cdot\>\right\rVert_{\Sigma}

Distribution of the Hessian values of Gaussian hypergeometric functions
Ken Ono, Sudhir Pujahari, Hasan Saad and Neelam Saikia

Abstract
. We consider a special family of Gaussian hypergeometric functions whose entries are cubic and trivial characters over finite fields. The special values of these functions are known to give the Frobenius traces of families of Hessian elliptic curves. Using the theory of harmonic Maass forms and mock modular forms, we prove that the limiting distribution of these values is semi-circular (i.e. S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) ), confirming the usual Sato-Tate distribution in this setting.

(-q)^{m}\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{{}_{2}F_{1}}(\lambda)_{q}^{m}=1-a_{q}^{\text{{\rm{Hes}}}}(0)^{m}+R(q)A(q)q^{m/2}+12\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\nmid s\\
s\equiv q+1\pmod{9}\end{subarray}}H^{*}\left(\frac{4q-s^{2}}{9}\right)s^{m},

where

and

are same as defined in Proposition

.

Proof.

By transforming

and using Theorem

we can rewrite the sum in terms of

as follows:

q^{m}\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{{}_{2}F_{1}}(\lambda)_{q}^{m}=q^{m}\cdot{{}_{2}F_{1}}(1)_{q}^{m}-(-1)^{m}a_{q}^{\text{{\rm{Hes}}}}(0)^{m}+(-1)^{m}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\\
\lambda^{3}\neq 27\end{subarray}}a_{q}^{\text{{\rm{Hes}}}}(\lambda)^{m}.

Now, by making use of the value

3.
Harmonic Maass forms and Mock modular forms

In this section we obtain the asymptotic formulas for the weighted sums of class numbers that we have obtained in the last section. To do this we need the theory of harmonic Maass forms (for a detailed study of harmonic Maass forms, see
[
10
]
). Let
\Gamma(\alpha;x):=\int_{\alpha}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{x-1}dt
be the incomplete Gamma function. We begin with the following celebrated theorem of Zagier.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
45
]
)
.

The function

\mathcal{H}(\tau)=-\frac{1}{12}+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}H^{\ast}(n)q_{\tau}^{n}+\frac{1}{8\pi\sqrt{y}}+\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\pi}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}n\Gamma(-\frac{1}{2};4\pi n^{2}y)q_{\tau}^{-n^{2}},

where
\tau=x+iy\in\mathbb{H}
and
q_{\tau}:=e^{2\pi i\tau},
is a weight
3/2
harmonic Maass form with manageable growth on
\Gamma_{0}(4).

In general, note that every weight
k\neq 1
harmonic weak Maass form
f(\tau)
has a Fourier expansion of the form

(3.1)

f(\tau)=f^{+}(\tau)+\frac{(4\pi y)^{1-k}}{k-1}\overline{c_{f}^{-}(0)}+f^{-}(\tau),

where

(3.2)

f^{+}(\tau)=\sum\limits_{n=m_{0}}^{\infty}c_{f}^{+}(n)q_{\tau}^{n}\ \ \ {\text{\rm and}}\ \ \ f^{-}(\tau)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{0}\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\overline{c_{f}^{-}(n)}n^{k-1}\Gamma(1-k;4\pi|n|y)q_{\tau}^{-n}.

The function
f^{+}(\tau)
is called the
holomorphic part
of
f,
or its corresponding
mock modular form.
In this work we consider
\mathcal{H}(9\tau),
which is a weight
3/2
harmonic Maass form with manageable growth on
\Gamma_{0}(36).

To obtain the weighted class number sums in Proposition
2.9
as Fourier coefficients of harmonic Maass forms, we need combinatorial operators involving derivatives of modular forms. To this end, we recall the Rankin-Cohen bracket operators.
Let
f
and
g
be smooth functions defined on the upper-half complex plane
\mathbb{H}
, and let
k,l\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.
Then the
\nu
th Rankin-Cohen bracket of
f
and
g
is defined by

(3.3)

[f,g]_{\nu}:=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{\nu}}\sum\limits_{r+s=\nu}(-1)^{r}\binom{k+\nu-1}{s}\binom{l+\nu-1}{r}\frac{d^{r}}{d\tau^{r}}f\cdot\frac{d^{s}}{d\tau^{s}}g.

Note that these operators preserve modularity.

Proposition 3.2

(Theorem 7.1 of
[
14
]
)
.

Let
f
and
g
be (not necessarily holomorphic) modular forms of weights
k
and
l,
respectively on a congruence subgroup
\Gamma.
Then the following are true.

(1) We have that
[f,g]_{\nu}
is modular of weight
k+l+2\nu
on
\Gamma.

(2) If
\gamma\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{R}),
then
under the usual modular slash operator we have

[f|_{k}\gamma,g|_{l}\gamma]_{\nu}=([f,g]_{\nu})|_{k+l+2\nu}\gamma.

The weighted sums of class numbers that appear in the moment formulas of the previous section can be constructed from Fourier coefficients of certain nonholomorphic modular forms by implementing the Rankin–Cohen bracket operators on the Zagier’s function
\mathcal{H}(9\tau)
and certain univariate theta functions. This method was already treated in
[
11
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
36
]
and
[
40
]
. In order to apply facts from the theory of holomorphic modular forms to these brackets, we recall a standard method known as holomorphic projection and some facts about it.

Let
f:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a (not necessarily holomorphic) modular form of weight
k\geq 2
on a congruence subgroup
\Gamma
with Fourier expansion

f(\tau)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{f}(n,y)q_{\tau}^{n},

where
\tau=x+iy.
Let
\{\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{M}\}
be the cusps of
\Gamma,

\kappa_{1}:=i\infty.
Moreover, for each

\gamma_{j}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

\gamma_{j}\kappa_{j}=i\infty.
Then suppose the following are true.

(1) There is an
\varepsilon>0
and a constant

for which

for all

(2) For all

we have that

Then the

(3.4)

(\pi_{\text{hol}}f)(\tau):=c_{0}+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}c(n)q_{\tau}^{n},

where
c_{0}=c_{0}^{(1)}
and for
n\geq 1

c(n)=\frac{(4\pi n)^{k-1}}{(k-2)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}c_{f}(n,y)e^{-4\pi ny}y^{k-2}dy.

Proposition 3.3

(Proposition 10.2 of
[
10
]
)
.

If the above hypotheses are true, then for
k>2

is a weight
k
holomorphic modular form on
\Gamma.

If
f
is a harmonic Maass form of weight
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
with manageable growth at the cusps and
g
is a holomorphic modular form of weight

on
\Gamma_{0}(N),
then the holomorphic modular form obtained by Proposition

has the following decomposition form

(3.5)

\pi_{\text{hol}}([f,g]_{\nu})=[f^{+},g]_{\nu}+\frac{(4\pi)^{1-k}}{k-1}\overline{c_{f}^{-}(0)}\pi_{\text{hol}}([y^{1-k},g]_{\nu})+\pi_{\text{hol}}([f^{-},g]_{\nu}).

The two following computations of Mertens
[

]
provide closed formulas for the Fourier expansions of the second and third terms of the above decomposition.

Lemma 3.4

(Lemma V.1.4 of
[
36
]
)
.

If the hypotheses above is true and

has Fourier expansion

g(\tau)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{g}(n)q_{\tau}^{n},

then we have

\frac{(4\pi)^{1-k}}{k-1}\pi_{\text{hol}}([y^{1-k},g]_{\nu})=\kappa(k,l,\nu)\cdot\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}n^{k+\nu-1}a_{g}(n)q_{\tau}^{n},

where

\kappa(k,l,\nu):=\frac{1}{(k+l+2\nu-2)!(k-1)}\sum\limits_{\mu=0}^{\nu}\left(\frac{\Gamma(2-k)\Gamma(l+2\nu-\mu)}{\Gamma(2-k-\mu)}\binom{k+\nu-1}{\nu-\mu}\binom{l+\nu-1}{\mu}\right).

To state the explicit Fourier expansion of the third term, let
P_{a,b}(X,Y)
be the homogeneous polynomial of degree

defined by

(3.6)

\displaystyle P_{a,b}(X,Y):=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{a-2}\binom{j+b-2}{j}X^{j}(X+Y)^{a-j-2},

Theorem 3.5

(Theorem V.1.5 of
[

]
)
.

If

and

\pi_{\text{hol}}([f^{-},g]_{\nu})=\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}b(r)q_{\tau}^{r},

where

\begin{split}b(r)=-\Gamma(1-k)&\sum\limits_{m-n=r}a_{g}(m)\overline{c^{-}_{f}(n)}\sum\limits_{\mu=0}^{\nu}\binom{k+\nu-1}{\nu-\mu}\binom{l+\nu-1}{\mu}m^{\nu-\mu}\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \times\left(m^{\mu-2\nu-l+1}P_{k+l+2\nu,2-k-\mu}(r,n)-n^{k+\mu-1}\right),\end{split}

where the sum runs over positive integers

3.1.
Combinatorial Identities

In this section we derive certain combinatorial formulas that are used in the proof of the main results. To this end, we recall some basic combinatorial definitions.

If
\alpha\in\mathbb{C},
and
j\geq 0,
define the Pochhammer symbol

(\alpha)_{j}:=\begin{cases}1&\ \ \text{ if }j=0\\
\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+j-1)&\ \ \text{ if }j>0.\end{cases}

Furthermore, we recall that Euler’s
\Gamma
function satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z).

(2)

\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})=\sqrt{\pi}.

(3)

\Gamma(z)\Gamma(z+1/2)=2^{1-2z}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(2z).
(Legendre’s duplication formula, see
[
4
, Th. 1.5.1]
)

In this notation, we have the following identities.

Lemma 3.6
.

If
k\leq\nu
are nonnegative integers, then we have

\sum\limits_{\mu=0}^{\nu-k}{2\nu+1\choose 2\nu-2\mu+1}{\nu-\mu\choose k}=4^{\nu-k}\frac{(2\nu-k)!}{k!(2\nu-2k)!}.

Proof.

Let
C_{\mu}:={2\nu+1\choose 2\nu-2\mu+1}{\nu-\mu\choose k}=\frac{(2\nu+1)!}{(2\nu-2\mu+1)!(2\mu)!}\frac{(\nu-\mu)!}{k!(\nu-\mu-k)!}.
Then we have

\frac{C_{\mu+1}}{C_{\mu}}=\frac{(\mu-\nu-\frac{1}{2})(\mu-\nu+k)}{(\mu+1)(\mu+\frac{1}{2})}.

As we can see, the quotient
\frac{C_{\mu+1}}{C_{\mu}}
is a monic rational function in
\mu
, hence
\sum C_{\mu}
a multiple of a hypergeometric series

{}_{2}F_{1}^{\text{class}}\left(\begin{matrix}a,&b\\
~&c\end{matrix}\mid z\right):=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{j}(b)_{j}}{(c)_{j}}~\frac{z^{j}}{j!}.

Namely we have

\sum\limits_{\mu=0}^{\nu-k}C_{\mu}={\nu\choose k}\cdot{{}_{2}F_{1}^{\text{class}}}\left(\begin{matrix}-\nu-\frac{1}{2},&-\nu+k\\
~&\frac{1}{2}\end{matrix}\mid 1\right).

By Gauss’s identity (see (1.3) of
[
6
]
and
[
21
]
), we have

{{}_{2}F_{1}^{\text{class}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
&c\end{array}|\ 1\right)=\frac{\Gamma(c)\Gamma(c-a-b)}{\Gamma(c-a)\Gamma(c-b)},

whenever
\mathrm{Re}(c-a-b)>0,
and therefore using this and Legendre’s duplication formula we have

\sum\limits_{\mu=0}^{\nu-k}C_{\mu}=4^{\nu-k}\frac{(2\nu-k)!}{k!(2\nu-2k)!}.

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\kappa(k,l,\nu)
be defined as in Lemma
3.4
. Then, for all

\nu\geq 0

\kappa\left(\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2},\nu\right)=\sqrt{\pi}\cdot 2^{-2\nu-1}{{2\nu+2}\choose{\nu+1}}.

Proof.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\kappa\left(\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2},\nu\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(2\nu+1)!}\sum\limits_{\mu=0}^{\nu}\left[\frac{\Gamma(1/2)\Gamma(2\nu-\mu+3/2)}{\Gamma(1/2-\mu)}{{\nu+1/2}\choose{\nu-\mu}}{{\nu+1/2}\choose{\mu}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sqrt{\pi}}{(2\nu+1)!}\sum\limits_{\mu=0}^{\nu}c_{\mu},

where

c_{\mu}=\frac{\Gamma(2\nu-\mu+3/2)}{\Gamma(1/2-\mu)}{{\nu+1/2}\choose{\nu-\mu}}{{\nu+1/2}\choose{\mu}}.
It is easy to compute that

\frac{c_{\mu+1}}{c_{\mu}}=\frac{(\mu+1/2)(\mu-\nu)(\mu-\nu-1/2)}{(\mu-2\nu-1/2)(\mu+3/2)(\mu+1)}.

Therefore, we have

\kappa\left(\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2},\nu\right)=\frac{2\Gamma(2\nu+3/2)}{(2\nu+1)!}{{\nu+1/2}\choose{\nu}}\cdot{{}_{3}F_{2}^{\text{class}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1/2,&-\nu,&-\nu-1/2\\
&-2\nu-1/2,&3/2\end{array}\mid 1\right).

Now, by using some standard hypergeometric identities e.g. Pfaff-Schulz identity (see Theorem 2.2.6 of
[
4
]
) and Legendre’s Duplication Formula, we conclude the result.
∎

To this end, we need results from the previous section, certain standard bounds of class numbers and bounds on Fourier coefficients of cusp forms. We first recall the celebrated theorem of Deligne, which bounds Fourier coefficients of integer weight cusp forms.

Theorem 4.1

Let

Lemma 4.2

Let

\Omega_{p^{r}}:=\{s\in[-2\sqrt{p^{r}},2\sqrt{p^{r}}]\cap\mathbb{Z}:p\mid s\ \text{and}\ s\equiv p^{r}+1\pmod{9}\}

It is easy to see that there are at most

4.1.

A One-Dimensional Symmetric Force-Based Blending Method for Atomistic-to-Continuum Coupling
Elaine Gorom-Alexander, Xingjie Helen Li

Abstract
Inspired by the blending method developed by [P. Seleson, S. Beneddine, and S. Prudhome, A Force-Based Coupling Scheme for Peridynamics and Classical Elasticity , (2013)] for the nonlocal-to-local coupling, we create a symmetric and consistent blended force-based Atomistic-to-Continuum (a/c) scheme for the atomistic chain in one-dimensional space. The conditions for the well-posedness of the underlying model are established by analyzing an optimal blending size and blending type to ensure the H 1 H^{1} semi-norm stability for the blended force-based operator. We present several numerical experiments to test and confirm the theoretical findings.



Keywords:

atomistic-to-continuum coupling, symmetric force-based blending, stability and blending size analysis

We will arrange the paper as follows. In section 2, we introduce the force-based symmetric blending method for a 1D atomistic chain. Also in section 4, we test a sine and Gaussian external force to the system to model the displacement with our force-based blending method.
The displacements produced by the blending methods with sufficient blending size agree with those of fully atomistic models. In addition, we compare the impact of interaction range of the atomistic model, and observe that an interaction range potential greater than
2

2
Derivation of the Symmetric and Consistent Force-Based Scheme

In this section, we will introduce notations, introduce the reference atomistic model, and then derive the continuum approximation.
After this, we introduce a blending equation to symmetrically combine these two models. Utilizing this blending equation, a coupling scheme for the blended atomistic and continuum forces is created.

2.1
Notations

We consider a 1D atomistic chain with finite interaction range up to the
N
-th nearest neighbor and a total number of
2M
atoms within the domain
\Omega
. We denote the scaled reference lattice
x_{\ell}=a\ell
for
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
with fixed reference lattice spacing constant
a\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{M}
such that we can select a reference domain which is fixed to be
\Omega=(-1,1]
. Throughout, the interaction range
N
will be fixed.
The chain is deformed to a current configuration
y_{\ell}=x_{\ell}+u_{\ell}
.

The displacement field
u=(u_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is assumed to be
2M
periodic discrete function and
\mathcal{U}
denotes the space of all
2M
periodic displacement functions

\mathcal{U}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{u\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,u_{\ell+2M}=u_{\ell}|\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Accordingly, we set the deformation space by

\mathcal{Y}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,y_{\ell}=x_{\ell}+u_{\ell}|u\in\mathcal{U},\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

We also define the discrete differentiation operator for simplicity,
u^{{}^{\prime}}
, on periodic displacements by

u^{{}^{\prime}}_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{u_{\ell+1}-u_{\ell}}{a}.

Then we may define the higher-order discrete differentiation
u^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
,
u^{(3)}
, and
u^{(4)}
for
\ell
by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{u^{\prime}_{\ell}-u^{\prime}_{\ell-1}}{a},\\
u^{(3)}_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell+1}-u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}}{a},\\
u^{(4)}_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{u^{(3)}_{\ell}-u^{(3)}_{\ell-1}}{a}.\end{cases}

(2.1)

For a displacement
u\in\mathcal{U}
and its discrete derivatives, we employ the discrete
\ell^{2}
and
\ell^{\infty}
norms by

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{\ell^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}}}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u_{\ell}\right\rvert}^{2}a,\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{\ell^{\infty}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max_{-M+1\leq\ell\leq M}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u_{\ell}\right\rvert}.

(2.2)

In particular, the associated inner product for
\ell_{2}
is

\langle u,w\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}u_{\ell}w_{\ell}\,a.

We also employ the discrete
H^{1}
semi norm,
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u\right\rvert}_{H^{1}}^{2}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{\ell_{2}}^{2}
in the stability analysis.

Meanwhile, we proceed with
\widetilde{u}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as a quintic spline interpolation of
u
such that

\begin{split}\widetilde{u}(a\ell)&=u_{\ell},\\
\widetilde{u}(-Ma)&=\widetilde{u}(Ma),\\
\lim_{t\rightarrow(a\ell)^{-}}\frac{d^{\omega}\widetilde{u}}{dx^{\omega}}(t)&=\lim_{t\rightarrow(a\ell)^{+}}\frac{d^{\omega}\widetilde{u}}{dx^{\omega}}(t),\quad\omega=1,\dots,4\end{split}

(2.3)

As
\widetilde{u}
is a continuous function, we can introduce notations for its derivatives, for instance,
\widetilde{u}_{x}
as its first derivative at
(a\ell)
, and
\widetilde{u}_{xx}
as its second derivative at
(a\ell)
, etc.

We can compare the derivatives of
\widetilde{u}(x)
with the differencing of
u_{\ell}
. Clearly, we have

\begin{split}u^{\prime}_{\ell}=&\widetilde{u}_{x}(a\ell)+\frac{a}{2}\widetilde{u}_{xx}(\xi)\\
u^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{\ell}=&\widetilde{u}_{xx}(a\ell)+\frac{a^{2}}{12}\widetilde{u}_{xxxx}(\tilde{\xi})\end{split}

(2.4)

Note that throughout, subscript
\ell
is used to denote when discrete differentiation is employed; whereas subscript
x
is used to denote when considering the true derivatives.

As in
[
9
]
, we will frequently use the following discrete summation by parts identity:

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
\{u_{\ell}\}^{b}_{\ell=a+1}
and
\{v_{\ell}\}^{b}_{\ell=a+1}
are two sequences, then we have

\sum_{\ell=a+1}^{b}u_{\ell}(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell-1})=[u_{b}v_{b}-u_{a}v_{a}]-\sum_{\ell=a+1}^{b}(u_{\ell}-u_{\ell-1})v_{\ell-1}.

Furthermore, when both
\{u_{\ell}\}^{b}_{\ell=a+1}
and
\{v_{\ell}\}^{b}_{\ell=a+1}
are periodic sequences with

u(a)=u(b)
and
v(a)=v(b)
, we have

\sum_{\ell=a+1}^{b}u_{\ell}(v_{\ell}-v_{\ell-1})=-\sum_{\ell=a+1}^{b}(u_{\ell}-u_{\ell-1})v_{\ell-1}.

We use this lemma to find conditions on coupling to ensure the positive-definiteness of the bilinear form of the symmetric, blended force-based operator.

2.2
1D atomisitic and continuum models

Energy formulations and consistency analysis.

We now consider a one-dimensional atomistic periodic chain deformed into configuration
y\in\mathcal{Y}
. Recall that the atomistic periodicity is fixed to
2M
, and the interaction range is fixed to
N
-th neighbors. The total atomistic energy for this periodic chain is given by

E^{a,tot}(y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\phi\big(\frac{y_{\ell+k}-y_{\ell}}{a}\big),

(2.5)

where
\phi(\cdot)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Lennard-Jones type potential. It can also be viewed as the energy density per unit volume for pairwise interactions. We assume that
\phi(\cdot)
has the following properties:

•

\phi(r)=\phi(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert r\right\rvert})
;

•

\phi
is at least four times differentiable; and

•

\phi_{xx}(1)>0
and
\phi_{xx}(k)\leq 0
for
k\geq 2
.

In the numerical simulation, we employ the Morse potential, and a graphical illustration can be found in Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
Graphical illustration of the Lennard-Jones type interaction potential (Morse potential) used in numerical experiments. Notice that the local minimum is achieved at the nearest neighbour distance
r=r_{e}
.

Next, we derive the continuum model by only using the first neighbor distance, that is we only use the differencing
u_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}
as we approximate the argument for
\phi
.
For
k=2,\dots,N
, we have

\begin{split}\frac{y_{\ell+k}-y_{\ell}}{a}&=\frac{x_{\ell+k}-x_{\ell}+u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a}\\
&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=}k+u^{\prime}_{\ell}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}u^{\prime}_{\ell+j},\end{split}

Using the error estimates listed in (
2.4
), we can replace
u^{\prime}_{\ell+j}
by
u^{\prime}_{\ell}
and estimate the discrepancy

\begin{split}\frac{y_{\ell+k}-y_{\ell}}{a}&=k+u^{\prime}_{\ell}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\widetilde{u}_{x}\left(a(\ell+j)\right)+c_{1}a\\
&=k+u^{\prime}_{\ell}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\widetilde{u}_{x}\left((a\ell)\right)+c_{2}a\\
&=k+ku^{\prime}_{\ell}+c_{3}a,\end{split}

(2.6)

where
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
are constants depending on
k
and regularity of
\widetilde{u}
.
For
k=-N,\dots,-2
, we can obtain similar consistency estimates.

The atomistic energy equation is rooted in discrete, nonlocal energy descriptions.
Assuming the finest mesh with each atom regarded as a node and substituting the previous approximation into the atomistic energy (
2.5
), we thus defined the continuum energy as

E^{c}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\phi(k+ku_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}).

(2.7)

So far, both the atomistic energy and the continuum energy are non-linearly dependent on the displacement field
\{u_{\ell}\}_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}
, and we would like to apply further simplifications to obtain linear models.

To linearize the total atomistic energy, we follow a similar argument as deriving the continuum energy,

\begin{split}\frac{y_{\ell+k}-y_{\ell}}{a}&=\frac{x_{\ell+k}-x_{\ell}+u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a}\\
&=k+\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a}.\end{split}

Therefore, the total atomistic energy can be written as

\begin{split}E^{a,tot}(y)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\phi\big(\frac{y_{\ell+k}-y_{\ell}}{a}\big)\\
&=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\phi\big(k+\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a}\big)\end{split}

(2.8)

Next, we utilize Taylor expansion to
\phi\big(k+\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a}\big)
at the reference configuration to linearize the expression.

\phi(k+\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a})=\phi(k)+\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a}\phi_{x}(k)+\frac{1}{2}(\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a})^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)+O\left((\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a})^{3}\right).

Inserting the Taylor approximation into the atomistic energy equation (
2.8
), we obtain

\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\left[\phi(k)+\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a}\phi_{x}(k)+\frac{1}{2}(\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a})^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)+O\left((\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a})^{3}\right)\right].

(2.9)

Then, without loss of generality, we assume
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\phi(k)=0
since this term will not contribute to force. Also, since the reference configuration is a local minimizer and the potential is symmetric, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a}\phi_{x}(k)=0.

Thus, the linearized atomistic energy is

\begin{split}E^{a,lin}(u)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a})^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right).\end{split}

(2.10)

Following the linearization of the atomistic energy, for the continuum energy, we utilize Taylor expansion to
\phi(k+ku_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}})
at the reference configuration to linearize the expression

\phi(k+ku_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=}\phi(k)+ku_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}\phi_{x}(k)+\frac{1}{2}(ku_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}\phi_{xx}(k){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}+O\left((ku^{\prime}_{\ell})^{3}\right)}.

Inserting the Taylor approximation into the continuum energy equation (
2.7
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\left(\phi(k)+ku_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}\phi_{x}(k)+\frac{1}{2}\left(ku_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\phi_{xx}(k){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}+O\left((ku^{\prime}_{\ell})^{3}\right)}\right).\end{split}

(2.11)

Following the same justification as above regarding the terms of the Taylor expansion, the linearized continuum energy associated with the finest mesh is defined as

\begin{split}E^{c,lin}(u)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{2}(u_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right)\\
&=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{k^{2}}{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right)(u_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}a.\end{split}

(2.12)

Remark 2.1
.

Notice that the classical continuum mechanics for interaction range up to the
N
-th nearest neighbour has the following form:

\widetilde{E}^{c,lin}(\widetilde{u})=\int W\left(\frac{d\widetilde{u}}{dx}\right)^{2}dx

(2.13)

where
\frac{d\widetilde{u}}{dx}
is the deformation field and
W
represents the strain energy density

W\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{k^{2}}{2}\phi_{xx}(k).

(2.14)

Notice that if we use a fine mesh and applying a Riemann sum to approximate the integral of (
2.13
), we can convert (
2.13
) into (
2.12
).
Therefore, the linearized continuum energy for a discrete lattice system is consistent with the theory of classical continuum mechanics.
For the consistency between the classical continuum mechanics and the linearized continuum energy, we refer to Section
7
as it follows closely with the consistency for the linearized continuum energy equation and the atomistic energy description.

Since we obtain the atomistic and continuum energies, in the next proposition, we will summarize the truncation errors.

Proposition 2.1

(Consistency Analysis of Linearized Energy Formulations)
.

Given a fully refined continuum mesh on the 1D atomistic chain, we derive the linearized continuum energy equation (
2.12
),

E^{c,lin}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{2}(u_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right).

from the atomistic energy description, (
2.5
),

E^{a,lin}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a})^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right).

with the deformed configuration and the displacement field linked by
y_{\ell}=x_{\ell}+u_{\ell}
.
Then, the consistency between the linearized continuum energy equation and the atomistic energy equation is
O(a^{2})
.

Proof.

Comparing
E^{c,lin}
and
E^{a,lin}
, we have

E^{c,lin}-E^{a,lin}=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{2}(u_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right)-\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a})^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right).

For any
k=2,...,N
, we compare
u_{\ell+k}
around
\ell
. Recall the quintic spline interpolation
\widetilde{u}
defined in (
2.3
), we have

u_{\ell+k}=u_{\ell}+ka\widetilde{u}_{x}+O(a^{2}).

Thus, for all
k
,

u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}=ka\widetilde{u}_{x}+O(a^{2}).

Then, the consistency analysis of energies yields:

\begin{split}E^{c,lin}&-E^{a,lin}\\
&=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{2}(u_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right)-\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a})^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right)\\
&=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{2}(\frac{u_{\ell+1}-u_{\ell}}{a})^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right)-\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a})^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right)\\
&=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{2}(\frac{a\widetilde{u}_{x}+O(a^{2})}{a})^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right)-\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{ka\widetilde{u}_{x}+O(a^{2})}{a})^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right)\\
&=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\frac{k^{2}}{2}\phi_{xx}(k)+O(a^{2})-\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{a}{2}\frac{k^{2}}{2}\phi_{xx}(k)+O(a^{2})\\
&=O(a^{2}).\end{split}

∎

Next, we derive the formulae of forces for both linear atomistic and continuum models. Because the mesh is fully refined, the linearized continuum force of atom
\ell
can be obtained from taking the first order variation of the linearized continuum energy (
2.12
) with respect to
u_{\ell}
, we thus get:

\begin{split}{F}_{\ell{\color[rgb]{0,0,0},}}^{c,lin}(u)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{a}\frac{\delta E^{c,lin}(u)}{\delta u_{\ell}}=\frac{\delta\left[\sum_{j=-M+1}^{M}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{k^{2}}{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right)\left(u_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right]}{\delta u_{\ell}}\\
&=-\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{k^{2}}{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right)\frac{2(u_{\ell+1}-u_{\ell})}{a^{2}}-\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{k^{2}}{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right)\frac{2(u_{\ell}-u_{\ell-1})}{a^{2}}\right)\\
&=-\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}k^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right)u_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime\prime}},\end{split}

(2.15)

where we recall the shorthand notation
u_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
as

u_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{u_{\ell+1}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-1}}{a^{2}}.

For the atomisitic forces, we recall
y_{\ell}=x_{\ell}+u_{\ell}
,
take the first order variation of the total atomistic energy (
2.5
) at atom
\ell
and notice we employ the forward finite-differencing, hence, we obtain

\begin{split}F_{\ell{\color[rgb]{0,0,0},}}^{a}(u)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\delta E^{a,tot}}{\delta u_{\ell}}=\frac{\delta}{\delta u_{\ell}}\sum_{j=-M+1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq{0}\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{2}\phi(\frac{y_{j+k}-y_{j}}{a})\\
&=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq{0}\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{2a}\left(\phi_{x}(k+\frac{u_{\ell+k}-u_{\ell}}{a})-\phi_{x}(k+\frac{u_{\ell}-u_{\ell-k}}{a})\right).\end{split}

(2.16)

Linearizing the forces around the reference configuration by applying Taylor expansion to
\phi_{x}(\cdot)
, we obtain the linearized atomistic forces

F_{\ell{\color[rgb]{0,0,0},}}^{a,lin}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq{0}\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\left(\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a^{2}}\right)=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi_{xx}(k)\left(\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a^{2}}\right).

(2.17)

In the next proposition, we summarize the consistency errors between the linearized atomisitic and linearized continuum forces.

Proposition 2.2

(Consistency analysis of force)
.

Given a fully refined continuum mesh on the 1D atomistic chain, the linearized atomistic force equation (
2.17
) for atom
\ell
is

F_{\ell{\color[rgb]{0,0,0},}}^{a,lin}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi_{xx}(k)\left(\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a^{2}}\right)

and the linearized continuum force equation (
2.15
) for node
\ell
is

F_{\ell{\color[rgb]{0,0,0},}}^{c,lin}(u)=-\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}k^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\right)u_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}.

Thus, the consistency error between (
2.17
) and (
2.15
) is
O(a^{2})
.

Proof.

Comparing
F^{c,lin}
and
F^{a,lin}
, we have

F_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\ell,}}^{c,lin}(u)-_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\ell,}}F^{a,lin}(u)=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi_{xx}(k)\left(k^{2}u_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi_{xx}(k)\left(\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a^{2}}\right).

For any
k=2,\dots,N
, we compare
u_{\ell+k}
and
u_{\ell-k}
around
\ell

Recall the quintic spline interpolation

defined in (

), we have

\begin{split}u_{\ell+k}&=\widetilde{u}\left(a(\ell+k)\right)-\widetilde{u}\left(a\ell\right)+\widetilde{u}\left(a\ell\right)=u_{\ell}+ka\widetilde{u}_{x}+\frac{1}{2}(ka)^{2}\widetilde{u}_{xx}+\frac{1}{6}(ka)^{3}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\widetilde{u}_{xxx}}+O(a^{4}),\\
u_{\ell-k}&=\widetilde{u}\left(a(\ell-k)\right)-\widetilde{u}\left(a\ell\right)+\widetilde{u}\left(a\ell\right)=u_{\ell}-ka\widetilde{u}_{x}+\frac{1}{2}(ka)^{2}\widetilde{u}_{xx}-\frac{1}{6}(ka)^{3}\widetilde{u}_{xxx}+O(a^{4}).\end{split}

Utilizing this Taylor expansion for the atomistic and continuous linear force equations, the consistency analysis yields

\begin{split}F_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\ell,}}^{c,lin}&(u)-F_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\ell,}}^{a,lin}(u)\\
&=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi_{xx}(k)\left(k^{2}u_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi_{xx}(k)(\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a^{2}})\\
&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi_{xx}(k)\big(k^{2}\frac{u_{\ell+1}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-1}}{a^{2}}\big)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi_{xx}(k)\big(k^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\widetilde{u}_{xx}}+O(a^{2})\big)\\
&={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi_{xx}(k)\big(k^{2}\widetilde{u}_{xx}+O(a^{2})\big)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi_{xx}(k)\big(k^{2}\widetilde{u}_{xx}+O(a^{2})\big)}\\
&=O(a^{2}).\end{split}

A more thorough proof can be seen in Subsection
7.1
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Notice that

is chosen to be
\frac{1}{M}
with
M
being large, hence, the consistency error becomes small when the number of atoms within
\Omega
is sufficiently large.

2.3
Derivation of a Symmetric Blending Model for the AtC Coupling

In this section we will derive a symmetric and consistent force-based atomistic-to-continuum scheme for the 1D atomistic chain.

We first divide the domain of interest into three distinct sub-domains:
\Omega^{a}
: the domain described by the atomistic force;
\Omega^{c}
: the domain described by the continuum force; and
\Omega^{b}
: the blending region where the atomistic and continuum force models are both used.

We now introduce a smooth blending function
\beta
that can be defined as such:

Definition 2.1

(Definition of blending function)
.

We may define a smooth blending function
\beta_{\ell}
such that:

\beta_{\ell}=\begin{cases}1,\quad&\ell\in\Omega^{a}\\
0,\quad&\ell\in\Omega^{c}\\
\in(0,1),\quad&\ell\in\Omega^{b}.\end{cases}

(2.18)

This blending function can take many forms and we will employ linear spline, cubic spline, and quintic spline blending functions in the numerical experiments in Section 4.

Notice that creating a linearized force equation will give way to easier analysis in studying the stability of the scheme and providing insights on the coupling conditions of more general cases, so we focus on blending the linearized atomistic and continuum forces. In order for consistent symmetry, we start from
k=-N,\dots,N,
and
k\neq 0
. Consequently, we start from the linearized, atomistic force equation in (
2.17
) and incorporate the blending function
\beta_{\ell}
as follows:

\displaystyle F_{\ell,}^{a,lin}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi_{xx}(k)\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a^{2}}=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq{0}\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a^{2}}

\displaystyle\,=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq{0}\end{subarray}}^{N}\left(\frac{\beta_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell+k}}{2}\right)\frac{1}{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a^{2}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-N,\\
k\neq{0}\end{subarray}}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\beta_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell+k}}{2}\right)\frac{1}{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a^{2}},

such that the term
\frac{u_{\ell+|k|}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-|k|}}{a^{2}}
is multiplied by the pair
\left(\frac{\beta_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell+|k|}}{2}\right)
and
\left(\frac{\beta_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell-|k|}}{2}\right)
, respectively.
Next, we further simplify and get

\displaystyle F_{\ell,}^{a,lin}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\beta_{\ell-k}+2\beta_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell+k}}{4}\right)\phi_{xx}(k)\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\beta_{\ell-k}+2\beta_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell+k}}{4}\right)\phi_{xx}(k)\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a^{2}}.

(2.19)

Then, we approximate the second term of the equation by using the linearized continuum portion. Therefore, we get the blended force function which is defined as:

\displaystyle F_{\ell,}^{bqcf,lin}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\beta_{\ell-k}+2\beta_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell+k}}{4}\right)\phi_{xx}(k)\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\beta_{\ell-k}+2\beta_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell+k}}{4}\right)\phi_{xx}(k)k^{2}\frac{u_{\ell+1}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-1}}{a^{2}}

(2.20)

\displaystyle\;=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\beta_{\ell-k}+2\beta_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell+k}}{4}\right)\phi_{xx}(k)\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a^{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\beta_{\ell-k}+2\beta_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell+k}}{4}\right)\phi_{xx}(k)k^{2}u_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}.

Remark 2.3
.

As
\ell-k
and
\ell+k
are both employed in the term of the blending function, this blending operator is symmetric as in
[
13
]
.

We also see that the first term recovers (
2.17
) for
\beta\equiv 0
, and the second term recovers (
2.15
) for
\beta\equiv 1
. Thus, the consistency error between the linearized

3
Stability Analysis for the Linearized Blending Model

3.1
Bilinear Form of Linearized Blending Model

In this section we study the size of the blending region with respect to the
H^{1}
stability of the blending operator. This is achieved by obtaining the optimal conditions in which the linearized coupling operator is positive definite under the discrete
H^{1}
semi-norm.

From (
2.3
), we consider the bilinear form

\langle F_{,}^{bqcf,lin}(u),v\rangle=\langle F_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0},}1}^{bqcf,lin}(u),v\rangle+\sum_{k=2}^{N}\langle F_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0},}k}^{bqcf,lin}(u),v\rangle,\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{U},

(3.1)

where the first neighbor interaction—the first term—is set apart due to its simplicity in analysis. The second term accounts for the next-nearest neighbor to the
N
-th nearest neighbor.

To observe the stability of the operator, we first look at the nearest and next-nearest neighbor interaction, for simplicity as well as for the coercivity assumption on
\phi_{xx}(1)>0
and
\phi_{xx}(k)\leq 0
with
k\geq 2
, to find the constraints on the size of blending region. Then, we discuss how the next-nearest neighbor analysis can be extended to the general
N
-th neighbor interaction.

The discrete stability analysis is inspired by and similar to the analogous continuous analysis for the force-based operator that can be seen in the Appendix
7
. We proceed with the analysis for the discrete case.

Lemma 3.1

S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\frac{\phi_{xx}(2)}{2}(u_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}})a^{2}\left(\beta^{\prime}_{\ell}u_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}-\beta_{\ell-2}^{{}^{\prime}}u_{\ell-2}^{{}^{\prime}}\right).

Utilizing similar inequalities, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S\right\rvert}&\leq\frac{-\phi_{xx}(2)}{2}\|\beta^{\prime}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\cdot 2\|u^{\prime}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}a\leq\frac{-\phi_{xx}(2)}{2}c_{1}(La)^{-1}\cdot 2\|u^{\prime}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}a=\big(-\phi_{xx}(2)\big)c_{1}(L)^{-1}\|u^{\prime}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

Hence, we prove the lemma.
∎

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that the number of atoms
M
within the chain model is sufficiently large, or equivalently the lattice spacing
a=\frac{1}{M}
is sufficiently small; also suppose that the fully atomistic model with next-nearest-neighbor interaction
N=2
, is stable so that
[\phi_{xx}(1)+4\phi_{xx}(2)]>0
. Let
L
denote the number of atoms within the blending region and let the blending function
\beta
be sufficiently smooth such that
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\beta^{(j)}\right\rVert}_{\infty}\leq(La)^{-j}
. Then there exists a positive constant
\widetilde{C}
such that

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sum_{k=1}^{2}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\langle F^{bqcf,lin}_{\ell,k}}u,u\rangle}\geq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\widetilde{C}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{\ell_{2}}^{2}

(3.7)

\widetilde{C}
is strictly bounded above zero as
a=\frac{1}{M}\rightarrow 0
.

Proof.

For
N=2
and from Lemma
3.1
, the blended force-based operator satisfies

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}<F_{\ell,2}^{bqcf,lin}(u),u>}=

\displaystyle\left(\phi_{xx}(1)+4\phi_{xx}(2)\right)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{\ell_{2}}^{2}-{\phi_{xx}(2)}a^{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\sqrt{\beta}u^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\right\rVert}_{\ell_{2}}^{2}+{\phi_{xx}(2)}a^{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\sqrt{\beta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}u^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{\ell_{2}}^{2}

(3.8)

\displaystyle+2R+2S.

From Lemma
3.2
, we have

\begin{split}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 2R+2S\right\rvert}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\leq}-\frac{\phi_{xx}(2)}{2}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2c_{3}}L^{-\frac{5}{2}}a^{-\frac{1}{2}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}4c_{2}}L^{-2}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}c_{1}}L^{-1}\right)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\\
&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\leq}-\frac{\phi_{xx}(2)}{2}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}c_{4}}L^{-\frac{5}{2}}a^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{\ell_{2}}^{2},\end{split}

(3.9)

hence, we have

2R+2S\geq\frac{\phi_{xx}(2)}{2}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}c_{4}}L^{-\frac{5}{2}}a^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{\ell_{2}}^{2}

with the latter inequality following from
L^{-2}\leq L^{-1}\leq L^{-\frac{5}{2}}a^{-\frac{1}{2}}
as
L^{-\frac{5}{2}}a^{-\frac{1}{2}}
dominates the latter two terms when
a
is sufficiently small.

From (
3.8
), we want to observe the terms that do not favor the coercivity in the terms of the
H^{1}
semi-norm.
For the second term, since
\phi_{xx}(2)\leq 0
, we have

-{\phi_{xx}(2)}a^{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\sqrt{\beta}u^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\right\rVert}_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\geq 0,

and thus it does not negatively contribute.
For the third term we observe that

{\phi_{xx}(2)}a^{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\sqrt{\beta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}u^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{\ell_{2}}^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\geq}{\phi_{xx}(2)}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}c_{5}}L^{-2}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{\ell_{2}}^{2}

because of
\phi_{xx}(2)\leq 0
. For up to
N=2
neighbour interaction, we have from the coercivity of atomistic model that
\phi^{\prime\prime}(1)+4\phi^{\prime\prime}(2)>0
, hence, for the linearized force-blended model, we have

\langle F_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0},}1}^{bqcf,lin}+F_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0},}2}^{bqcf,lin},u\rangle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\geq}\left[\phi_{xx}(1)+{\phi_{xx}(2)}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(4+c_{4}L^{-\frac{5}{2}}a^{-\frac{1}{2}}+c_{5}L^{-2}\right)}\right]\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{\ell_{2}}^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\geq\widetilde{C}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert}^{2}_{\ell_{2}}

for
\widetilde{C}=\left(\phi_{xx}(1)+2\phi_{xx}(2)\right)>0
strictly positive, and independent of
a\rightarrow 0
.

Thus, we can conclude that the necessary and optimal blending size for coercivity is
L^{-\frac{5}{2}}a^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim 1/c_{4}
that is
L=\widehat{C}a^{-\frac{1}{5}}=\widehat{C}M^{\frac{1}{5}}
for some
\widehat{C}>0
. ∎

Stability Analysis for general
N
-th Nearest Neighbor Interaction Range

To observe the general form of the
N
-th neighbor interaction range, we notice that for
k=3,\dots,N

\displaystyle\langle F^{bqcf,lin}_{,k}

\displaystyle(u),u\rangle

(3.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\phi_{xx}(k)\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\bigg\{\left(\frac{\beta_{\ell+k}+2\beta_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell-k}}{4}\right)\left(\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{\beta_{\ell+k}+2\beta_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell-k}}{4}\right)k^{2}\left(\frac{u_{\ell+1}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-1}}{a^{2}}\right)\bigg\}u_{\ell}a.

The
k

\beta_{\ell\pm k}
terms which are treated similarly to
\beta_{\ell}
terms.
\phi_{xx}(k)
is a non-positive constant term for all

Similarly to the previous subsection, we fix an interaction range

and at the moment only consider all terms that contribute to
\beta_{\ell}
, thus we get

\displaystyle T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\phi_{xx}(k)}{2}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\left(k^{2}\frac{u_{\ell+1}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-1}}{a}\right)(u_{\ell})

\displaystyle-\frac{\phi_{xx}(k)}{2}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\left[\bigg(\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a}-k^{2}\frac{u_{\ell+1}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-1}}{a}\bigg)\big(\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell}\big)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{1}+T_{2}.

For
T_{1}
, we similarly have

\displaystyle T_{1}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\phi_{xx}(k)}{2}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\left(k^{2}\frac{u_{\ell+1}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-1}}{a}\right)(u_{\ell})

(3.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\phi_{xx}(k)}{2}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}k^{2}\left(u^{\prime}_{\ell}-u^{\prime}_{\ell-1}\right)u_{\ell}=\frac{k^{2}}{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{\ell_{2}}.

For
T_{2}
, the simplification is more difficult, we have

\displaystyle T_{2}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\phi_{xx}(k)}{2}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\left[\bigg(\frac{u_{\ell+k}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-k}}{a}-k^{2}\frac{u_{\ell+1}-2u_{\ell}+u_{\ell-1}}{a}\bigg)\big(\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell}\big)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\phi_{xx}(k)}{2}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\left[\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{s=1}^{k}u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell-j+s}\bigg)-k^{2}u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}\right]\big(a\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\phi_{xx}(k)}{2}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\bigg(u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell-j+s}-u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}\bigg)\right]\big(a\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell}\big).

Due to the exact symmetry of

and

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{s=1}^{k}u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell-j+s}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{s=1}^{k}u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell-s+j}.

Hence,
T_{2}
can be converted into a symmetrical form

\displaystyle T_{2}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\phi_{xx}(k)}{2}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\bigg(u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell-j+s}-u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}\bigg)\right]\big(a\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell}\big)

(3.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\phi_{xx}(k)}{4}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\bigg(u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell-j+s}-2u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}+u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell+j-s}\bigg)\right]\big(a\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\phi_{xx}(k)}{4}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\big(u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell-j+s}-u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}\big)-\big(u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}-u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell+j-s}\big)\right]\big(a\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell}\big).

Therefore by the discrete summation by parts formula, we have

\displaystyle T_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{\phi_{xx}(k)}{4}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\big(u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell-j+s}-u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}\big)\right]a\big(\beta_{\ell-j+s}u_{\ell-j+s}-\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell}\big)

(3.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-\phi_{xx}(k)}{4}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{s=1}^{k}u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}a\right]\bigg(\big(\beta_{\ell-j+s}u_{\ell-j+s}-\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell}\big)-\big(\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell}-\beta_{\ell+j-s}u_{\ell+j-s}\big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-\phi_{xx}(k)}{4}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{s=1}^{k}u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}a\right]\bigg(\beta_{\ell-j+s}u_{\ell-j+s}-2\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell+j-s}u_{\ell+j-s}\bigg).

We can carefully work on the symmetrical term

\displaystyle\bigg(\beta_{\ell-j+s}u_{\ell-j+s}-

\displaystyle 2\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell+j-s}u_{\ell+j-s}\bigg)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=j-s}}{{=}}\bigg(\beta_{\ell-r}u_{\ell-r}-2\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell+r}u_{\ell+r}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\beta_{\ell-r}u_{\ell-r}-\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell+1}+\big(\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell+1}-2\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell-1}\big)-\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell-1}+\beta_{\ell+r}u_{\ell+r}

\displaystyle=\beta_{\ell}u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}a^{2}+\beta_{\ell-r}u_{\ell-r}-\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell+1}+\beta_{\ell+r}u_{\ell+r}-\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell-1}.

Without loss of generality, we assume

\displaystyle\bigg(

\displaystyle\beta_{\ell-r}u_{\ell-r}-2\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell+r}u_{\ell+r}\bigg)=\beta_{\ell}u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}a^{2}+\beta_{\ell-r}u_{\ell-r}-\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell+1}+\beta_{\ell+r}u_{\ell+r}-\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell-1}

\displaystyle=\beta_{\ell}u^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}a^{2}+\big(\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell-r}-\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell-1}\big)+\big(\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell+r}-\beta_{\ell}u_{\ell+1}\big)+u_{\ell+r}\sum_{t=0}^{r-1}\beta^{\prime}_{\ell+t}a-u_{\ell-r}\sum_{t=0}^{r-1}\beta^{\prime}_{\ell-r+t}a.

|T_{2}|\leq(-\phi_{xx}(k))\big(|\widetilde{c}_{4}|L^{-\frac{5}{2}}a^{-\frac{1}{2}}+|\widetilde{c}_{5}|L^{-2}\big)\|u^{\prime}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that the number of atoms
M
is sufficiently large, which is equivalent to
a=\frac{1}{M}
being sufficiently small, and the blending function
\beta
is sufficiently smooth. Also, we assume that the fully atomistic model is stable so that

\big[\phi_{xx}(1)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}k^{2}\phi_{xx}(k)\big]>0.

If the blending size
L
satisfies
L=\widetilde{C}a^{-\frac{1}{5}}
, then the linear B-QCF operator
F_{,}^{bqcf,lin}
is positive-definite in terms of the
H^{1}
semi-norm

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{\ell=-M+1}^{M}\langle F^{bqcf,lin}_{\ell,k}}u,u\rangle}\geq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\widetilde{C}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{\ell_{2}}^{2},

(3.16)

where
\widetilde{C}
is strictly bounded above zero as
a=\frac{1}{M}\rightarrow 0
.

Meanwhile, we can see that the bounds are dependent on the smoothness of
\beta
. Therefore, we aim to find the optimal types of blending function to preserve the bounds in the coming numerical session.

Remark 3.2
.

Throughout, the term ‘optimal’ is used to describe the conclusion that
L=\widetilde{C}a^{-\frac{1}{5}}
is the optimal blending size for keeping coercivity of the force operator when
a=\frac{1}{M}\rightarrow 0
. Optimality in these instances describes the smallest asymptotic order that could be expected to ensure stability. However, the analysis of consistency conditions is beyond the purview of this paper. Note that
\widehat{C}
is dependent on the blending function,
\beta
, and the choice for potential energy,
\phi
; and it is not on the lattice spacing constant,
a
.

Also notice from the inequality (
3.15
) that

\widehat{C}_{k}L^{-\frac{5}{2}}a^{-\frac{1}{2}}
dominates the other terms only when the lattice spacing
a=\frac{1}{M}
is very small. As a result, the asymptotically rate
L\sim a^{-\frac{1}{5}}=M^{\frac{1}{5}}
might be not observed when the size of atomistic chain is only moderately large. In this case, we suggest to take
L\sim a^{-\frac{1}{3}}=M^{\frac{1}{3}}
, which is observed in the numerical test.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, inspired by the force-based coupling of the peridynamics model of
[
13
]
, we have formulated a similar symmetric and consistent blended force-based atomistic-to-continuum coupling scheme in one-dimensional space. We were able to identify the optimal asymptotic conditions on the width of the blending region,
L\approx M^{\frac{1}{5}}
to ensure the
H^{1}
stability of the linearized force-blending operator when chain size is huge.

We have verified the theoretical findings with numerical experiments on the blending function and the blending region. From these numerical experiments, we find that the cubic blending provides the best results compared to the critical stretch of the fully atomistic model. We also find that the optimal blending width from these numerical experiments is
L\approx M^{\frac{1}{3}}
due to non-negligible terms when

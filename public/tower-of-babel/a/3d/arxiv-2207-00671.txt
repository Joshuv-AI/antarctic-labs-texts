Extinction of the TeV Gamma-Ray Background by Sunlight
Abraham Loeb

Abstract
I show that pair production on sunlight introduces a sizable anisotropy in the cosmic background of TeV gamma-rays. The anisotropy amplitude in the direction of the Sun exceeds the cosmic dipole anisotropy from the motion of the Sun relative to the cosmic rest-frame.



I
Introduction

Given its effective surface temperature of
T_{\rm eff}=5.8\times 10^{3}~{\rm K}
, the Sun acts as a source of photons with a
characteristic energy,
E_{{\gamma}_{\odot}}\approx k_{B}T_{\rm eff}=0.5~{\rm eV}
. When these photons collide with the cosmic background
of TeV photons
(
Inoue & Tanaka 2016
;
Yan-kun & Hou-dun 2022
)
, they
produce electron-positron pairs through the process:
\gamma_{\odot}+\gamma\rightarrow e^{+}+e^{-}
. For pair creation to occur, the energy
of the background photon,
E_{\gamma}
, must exceed the threshold value,

E_{\rm th}

(
Gould & Schréder 1967a
)
,

E_{\gamma}>E_{\rm th}=\frac{2m_{e}^{2}c^{4}}{E_{{\gamma}_{\odot}}\mu}\approx{0.5~{\rm TeV}\over(\mu/2)},

(1)

where
m_{e}=5.11\times 10^{5}~{\rm eV}
is the electron rest-mass,
c
is
the speed of light and
\mu=(1-\cos\theta)
with
\theta
being the
angle between the momenta of the two photons in the observer’s frame.
The value of
E_{\rm th}
is minimized for a head-on collision
(
\mu=2
) with a background photon of energy
E_{\gamma}\gtrsim 0.5~{\rm TeV}
.

Above this threshold, the cross-section for pair production is given by,

\sigma_{\gamma\gamma}=3.7\times 10^{-25}~{\rm cm^{2}}\times(1-\beta^{2})\left[(1-{1\over 3}\beta^{4})\ln\left({{1+\beta}\over{1-\beta}}\right)-{2\over 3}\beta(2-\beta^{2})\right],

(2)

where
\beta=(v/c)
, with
v
being the speed of the electron or
positron in the center-of-mass system. The value of
\beta
depends on

E_{\gamma}
and
\theta
through the relation,

\beta=\sqrt{1-{E_{\rm th}\over E_{\gamma}}}.

(3)

The same pair-production process introduces a cosmic opacity to the
TeV radiation background as a result of photon-photon collisions with
the extragalactic background light

(
Gould & Schréder 1967b
;
Singh et al. 2021
;
Franceschini 2021
)
. Here
we focus on the imprint of Solar photons on the cosmic TeV background
observable on Earth.

II
Results

The number density of Solar photons at a distance
R
from the Sun is
given by,

n_{{\gamma}_{\odot}}={L_{\odot}\over\left(4\pi cR^{2}E_{{\gamma}_{\odot}}\right)}\approx 2.7\times 10^{12}~{\rm cm^{-3}}\left({R\over R_{\odot}}\right)^{-2},

(4)

where
L_{\odot}=4\times 10^{33}~{\rm erg~s^{-1}}
is the Solar
luminosity. The Earth-Sun separation at
R=1.5\times 10^{13}~{\rm cm}

is
2.14\times 10^{2}
times larger than the Solar radius

R_{\odot}=7\times 10^{10}~{\rm cm}
. This photon density yields a local
optical-depth for pair-production at an energy
E_{\gamma}\sim 1~{\rm TeV}
, of magnitude,

\tau_{\gamma\gamma}(E_{\gamma}\sim 1~{\rm TeV})=n_{{\gamma}_{\odot}}\sigma_{\gamma\gamma}R\sim 7\times 10^{-2}\left({R\over R_{\odot}}\right)^{-1}.

(5)

The resulting absorption of background photons produces a deficit in
the gamma-ray background at the energy
\sim 1~{\rm TeV}
with an
anisotropy amplitude
\sim 7\times 10^{-2}
in the vicinity of the
Sun and
\sim 3.3\times 10^{-4}
at the Earth’s orbital radius, and an
anisotropy angular dependence that reflects the position of the Sun in
the sky at the observing time combined with the
\mu
dependence of

\sigma_{\gamma\gamma}
in equations (
2
-
3
).

III
Implications

Interestingly, the expected anisotropy amplitude is significant
relative to the dipole associated with the motion of the Solar system
relative to the cosmic rest-frame,
\sim 1.2\times 10^{-3}
, as
measured from the cosmic microwave background

(
Kogut et al. 1993
)
.

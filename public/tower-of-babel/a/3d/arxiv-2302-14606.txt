Horizontal decompositions, II
Paolo Lisca and Andrea Parma

Abstract
. We complete the classification of the smooth, closed, oriented 4 4 -manifolds having Euler characteristic less than four and a horizontal handlebody decomposition of genus one. We use the classification result to find a large family of rational homology ball smoothings of cyclic quotient singularities which can be smoothly embedded into the complex projective plane. Our family contains all such rational balls previously known to embed into ℂ ​ ℙ 2 \mathbb{CP}^{2} and infinitely many more. We also show that a rational ball of our family admits an almost-complex embedding in ℂ ​ ℙ 2 \mathbb{CP}^{2} if and only if it admits a symplectic embedding.

Introduction

Let
B_{p,q}
, where
p>q\geq 0
are coprime integers, be the rational homology ball smoothing of the cyclic quotient singularity
\frac{1}{p^{2}}(pq-1,1)
.
The rational balls
B_{p,q}
were used in the smooth rational blow-down construction
[
5
]
and in its symplectic counterpart
[
17
]
.
They played an important role in the construction of exotic
4
-manifolds, starting with the papers
[
15
,
4
]
.
The problem of embedding the
B_{p,q}
’s, smoothly or symplectically, in a
4
-manifold was considered in
[
8
,
10
,
9
]
.
As pointed out in
[
3
, Section 2]
, the existence of holomorphic embeddings
B_{p,q}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{2}
for certain
(p,q)
’s follows from results in algebraic geometry
[
7
]
, and explicit symplectic embeddings can be constructed.
Moreover, Evans and Smith
[
3
]
showed that no further symplectic embeddings exist beyond those which are already known.
Owens
[
14
]
showed that infinitely many
B_{p,q}
’s admit smooth embeddings in

\mathbb{CP}^{2}
but by
[
3
]
they cannot be symplectically embedded.
The authors extended Owens’ family
[
11
]
and proved the non-existence of almost
complex embeddings
[
13
]
without relying on
[
3
]
. The present paper is a natural
continuation of
[
12
]
, where we introduced certain handlebody decompositions that
we call horizontal, classified the closed
4
-manifolds with the simplest horizontal
decompositions and in doing so we recovered infinitely many of the known smooth embeddings
B_{p,q}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.

This paper consists of two parts. In the first part we complete the classification of the
smooth, closed, oriented
4
-manifolds
X
with Euler characteristic
\chi(X)\leq 3
and a
horizontal decomposition of genus
1
. In the second part we exploit the classification
result to determine the rational balls
B_{p,q}
which admit smooth embeddings
in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
induced by a genus-
1
horizontal decomposition. It turns out that in this
way we are able to find the largest family so far of
B_{p,q}
’s smoothly
embedded into the complex projective plane. The family contains all such rational balls
previously known to embed into
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
and many more. We show that each rational ball of the family
admits a symplectic embedding in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
if and only if it admits an almost-complex
embedding.

In order to state our results we need to recall the definition of a horizontal decomposition
[
12
]
.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a handlebody decomposition of a smooth, oriented 4-dimensional cobordism
X\thinspace\colon\partial_{-}X\to\partial_{+}X
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
has
h_{i}
handles of index
i
and let
\partial_{\pm}^{h_{i}}X=\partial_{\pm}X\#^{h_{i}}S^{1}\times S^{2}
for
i\in\{1,3\}
.
Let
X_{L}\thinspace\colon\partial_{-}^{h_{1}}X\to\partial_{+}^{h_{3}}X
be the cobordism obtained by attaching the
2
-handles along a framed link
L=\bigcup_{i=1}^{h_{2}}\gamma_{i}\subset\partial_{-}^{h_{1}}X
.
Acccording to
[
12
]
, the link
L
and the decomposition
\mathcal{H}
are called
horizontal

if, for some Heegaard decomposition
H_{g}\cup\Sigma_{g}\times[0,1]\cup H^{\prime}_{g}
of
\partial_{-}^{h_{1}}X

we have:

•

\gamma_{i}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{i}:=\Sigma_{g}\times\{t_{i}\}
for some
0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{h_{2}}<1
;

•

the
i
-th
2
-handle of
\mathcal{H}
is attached
to
\gamma_{i}
with framing
\fr(\gamma_{i})=\fr_{\mathfrak{S}_{i}}(\gamma_{i})\pm 1
.

Note that the
3
-manifold

\partial_{+}^{h_{3}}X
is obtained from
\partial_{-}^{h_{1}}X
by
3
-dimensional
surgery on each
\gamma_{i}
with the same framing
\fr(\gamma_{i})
. We say
that
\mathcal{H}
is a horizontal decomposition of type
(g,h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})
, or a

(g,h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})
-decomposition. As observed in
[
12
, Section 1]
,
the inequality
g\geq\max\{h_{1},h_{3}\}
always holds. We say that a smooth,
closed
4
-manifold
\hat{X}
has a horizontal decomposition
if
X:=\hat{X}\setminus\{B^{4}\cup B^{4}\}
has a such a decomposition
when viewed as a cobordism
S^{3}\to S^{3}
.

Together with
[
12
, Theorem 1.7]
, the following result yields a complete
classification of the smooth, closed, oriented
4
-manifolds
\hat{X}
having Euler characteristic

\chi(\hat{X})\leq 3
and a horizontal decomposition of genus
1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\hat{X}
be a smooth, closed
4
-manifold admitting a
(1,h_{1},h_{2},1)
-decomposition.
Then,
\hat{X}
is diffeomorphic, possibly after reversing orientation, to:

S^{4},\quad S^{1}\times S^{3},\quad\pm\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#S^{1}\times S^{3},\quad\pm\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#\pm\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#S^{1}\times S^{3}\quad\text{or}\quad B_{p,q}\cup-B_{p,q}\quad\text{if $\chi(\hat{X})\leq 2$,}

\mathbb{CP}^{2},\quad\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#B_{p,q}\cup-B_{p,q},\quad\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#2\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{2}\#S^{1}\times S^{3},\quad\text{or}\quad 3\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#S^{1}\times S^{3}\quad\text{if $\chi(\hat{X})=3$,}

for some
p>q\geq 0
.

From now on we concentrate on
(1,h_{1},h_{2},1)
-decompositions of a
4
-manifold
\hat{X}

-decomposition of a

3

4
-manifolds, each consisting of a
1
-handle and a

-handle attached to

Using this fact, in the following Theorem

we describe essentially all the smooth embeddings of

’s induced by
(1,1,3,1)
-decompositions of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.

We introduce some notation in order to give a more concise statement of Theorem

.
When we write

we shall always assume that

When

, where

.
Note that when

. When

Let
\{x_{n}(a,b)\}_{n\geq 0}
be the integer sequence defined by

x_{0}(a,b)=a,\quad x_{1}(a,b)=b,\quad x_{n+2}(a,b)=x_{n+1}(a,b)+x_{n}(a,b),\quad n\geq 2,

Theorem 1.2
.

A disjoint union
\pm B_{p_{1},q_{1}}\cup\pm B_{p_{2},q_{2}}\cup\pm B_{p_{3},q_{3}}
with

\max\{|p_{i}|\}>1
is induced by a
(1,1,3,1)
-decomposition of

\mathbb{CP}^{2}
if and only it coincides with one of the following:

(1)

B_{p_{1},q_{1}}\cup B_{p_{2},q_{2}}\cup B_{p_{3},q_{3}}
, where
(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
is a Markov triple and

p_{k}q_{i}\equiv\pm 3p_{j}\bmod p_{i}
,
where
(i,j,k)
is a permutation of
(1,2,3)
;

(2)

B_{x_{1},q_{1}}\cup-B_{x_{2},q_{2}}\cup-B_{x_{3},q_{3}}
, where
x_{1}^{2}+x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}=x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}
,

x_{2}q_{1}\equiv\pm x_{3}\bmod x_{1},\quad x_{3}q_{2}\equiv\pm x_{1}\bmod(x_{1}x_{3}+x_{2}),\quad\text{and}\quad x_{2}q_{3}\equiv\pm x_{1}\bmod x_{3};

(3)

B_{F_{a},q_{1}}\cup-B_{F_{b-\varepsilon a},q_{2}}\cup B_{F_{b},q_{3}}

where
a
and
b
are odd, coprime integers,
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
,
b\neq\varepsilon a
,

q_{1}\equiv\pm L_{b}/F_{b-\varepsilon a}\bmod F_{a},\quad q_{2}\equiv\pm L_{b}/F_{a}\bmod F_{b-\varepsilon a}\quad\text{and}\quad q_{3}\equiv\pm L_{a}/F_{b-\varepsilon a}\bmod F_{b}.

Remark 1.3
.

The fact that the disjoint unions of Theorem
1.2
(1) embed
in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is already known
[
7
]
.

Denote by
\mathcal{F}_{i}
, for

, the family of rational homology balls

B_{p,q}
which admit an orientation-preserving embedding in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
in view of
Theorem
1.2
(i).

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
|p|>1
and the rational ball
B_{p,q}
admits an
orientation-preserving embedding in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
induced by
a
(1,h_{1},h_{2},1)
-decomposition. Then,

(1)

B_{p,q}\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
if and only if
q^{2}\equiv-9\bmod p
;

(2)

B_{p,q}\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
if and only if
q^{2}\equiv-1\bmod p
;

(3)

let

S_{p}=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\ |\ x\equiv q^{2}\bmod p\ \text{for some $B_{p,q}\in\mathcal{F}_{3}$}\}.

Then, the sequence
\{|S_{p}|\}_{p}
is unbounded.

Remark 1.5
.

In
[
11
]
we constructed infinitely many embeddings
B_{p,q}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, extending a family of embeddings constructed by Owens
[
14
]
.
It is not hard to check that our constructions implicitly used horizontal decompositions of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
– in fact, those constructions led us to the discovery of horizontal decompositions.
At the end of
[
11
, Section 3]
we showed that all the balls shown to embed in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
satisfy

.
In view of Remark

, Theorem
1.4
implies that all
B_{p,q}
’s known so far to embed in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
belong to
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}
and that
(1,h_{1},h_{2},1)
-decompositions of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
induce infinitely many more embeddings of
B_{p,q}
’s.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
B_{p,q}
be a rational ball which admits an orientation-preserving embedding in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
induced by a
(1,h_{1},h_{2},1)
-decomposition.
Then,
B_{p,q}
admits an almost-complex embedding in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
if and only if it embeds symplectically in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
and belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{1}
.

Proof.

By
[
13
, Corollary 1.3]
, if
B_{p,q}
admits an almost-complex embedding in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
then
q\equiv-9\bmod p

1.4

B_{p,q}\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
and by Remark
1.3
it embeds symplectically in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. The converse
follows from the well-known fact that symplectic embeddings are almost-complex.
∎

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
when
\chi(\hat{X})\leq 2

Let
\hat{X}
be a smooth, oriented cobordism
admitting a
(1,h_{1},h_{2},1)
-decomposition. In this section we prove the first
part of Theorem
1.1
, that is we show that if

\chi(\hat{X})\leq 2
then
\hat{X}
is diffeomorphic, possibly after
reversing orientation, to:

S^{4},\quad S^{1}\times S^{3},\quad\pm\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#S^{1}\times S^{3},\quad\pm\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#\pm\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#S^{1}\times S^{3}\quad\text{or}\quad B_{p,q}\cup-B_{p,q}

for some
p>q\geq 0
.

We start recalling that to an
n
-component link
L\subset S^{1}\times S^{2}
, horizontal
with respect to the standard genus-
1
Heegaard decomposition of
S^{1}\times S^{2}
, one can associate
an element of mapping class group
\Map(\Sigma_{1})
of the genus-
1
surface, factorized as a product
of Dehn twists
[
12
, §1]
. This is done as follows.
Let
T\subset S^{1}\times S^{2}
be the standard Heegaard torus and
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}

the components of
L
, viewed as sitting on
T
. Then, to
\gamma_{i}
we associate
the Dehn twist
\tau_{i}:=\tau_{\gamma_{i}}\in\Map(T)
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
By definition, the
factorization
associated to
L
is

F_{L}=(\tau_{n}^{\delta_{n}},\ldots,\tau_{1}^{\delta_{1}}),

where
\delta_{i}=\fr_{\mathfrak{S}_{i}}(\gamma_{i})-\fr(\gamma_{i})\in\{\pm 1\}
.
The product
m_{L}=\tau_{n}^{\delta_{n}}\cdots\tau_{1}^{\delta_{1}}
is called the
monodromy
of
L
.

Now we prove an auxiliary result, which will be also used later on.
Recall (see e.g.
[
16
, Section 1.2]
) that each handlebody decomposition
\mathcal{H}

on a smooth, oriented cobordism
X\thinspace\colon\partial_{-}X\to\partial_{+}X
can be viewed as coming
from a Morse function
f\thinspace\colon X\to[0,1]
. The handlebody decomposition
\overline{\mathcal{H}}

associated with
1-f\thinspace\colon X\to[0,1]
is sometimes called the
dual
of
\mathcal{H}
.
When
X
has dimension
4
each
i
-handle of
\mathcal{H}
corresponds to a
4-i
-handle of

\overline{\mathcal{H}}
. In particular, the co-cores of the
2
-handles of
\mathcal{H}
are the
cores of the
2
-handles of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
, and vice-versa.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X\thinspace\colon\partial_{-}X\to\partial_{+}X
be a smooth, oriented
4
-dimensional cobordism
with a horizontal handlebody decomposition
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
be the dual
handlebody decomposition on
X
, viewed as an oriented cobordism

\overline{X}\thinspace\colon-\partial_{+}X\to-\partial_{-}X
. Then,
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
can be made horizontal
via a framed isotopy of the attaching circles of its
2
-handles. Moreover, if
\mathcal{H}

is of type
(g,h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})
then
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
is of type
(g,h_{3},h_{2},h_{1})
.

Proof.

We assume that we are in the setup of Section
1
.
Recall that the
i
-th
2
-handle of
\mathcal{H}
is attached to
\gamma_{i}

with framing
\fr(\gamma_{i})=\fr_{\Sigma_{i}}(\gamma_{i})\pm 1
and as a result the
3
-manifold

\partial_{+}^{h_{3}}X
is obtained from
\partial_{-}^{h_{1}}X
by
3
-dimensional
surgery on each
\gamma_{i}
with framing
\fr(\gamma_{i})
. Consider, for each
i=1,\ldots,h_{2}
,
a parallel copy
\gamma^{\prime}_{i}
of
\gamma_{i}
inside
\Sigma_{i}
. We may assume that the surgeries
are performed
using tubular neighborhoods of the
\gamma_{i}
’s disjoint from the
\gamma^{\prime}_{i}
’s, and therefore
that
\gamma^{\prime}_{i}\subset\partial_{+}^{h_{3}}X
. It is easy to check that, once endowed with the
framing
\fr(\gamma^{\prime}_{i}):=\fr_{\Sigma_{i}}(\gamma^{\prime}_{i})\mp 1
, the framed knot
\gamma^{\prime}_{i}
is framed isotopic
to a
0
-framed meridian of
\gamma_{i}
– also viewed inside
\partial_{+}^{h_{3}}X
. In other
words, the pair
(\gamma^{\prime}_{i},\fr(\gamma^{\prime}_{i}))
prescribes, up to framed isotopy,
how to attach the
i
-th
2
-handle of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
. Clearly the link

L^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{h_{2}}\gamma^{\prime}_{i}\subset\partial_{+}^{h_{3}}X
is horizontal with respect to
a Heegaard decomposition of
-\partial_{+}^{h_{3}}X
and the statement follows easily.
∎

.
Let
X
be the complement of two disjoint balls in

, viewed as a cobordism

Clearly

Assume first

.
The case

is not possible because a single
3
-handle cannot give a cobordism from
S^{3}
to
S^{3}
.
If
(h_{1},h_{2})=(1,0)

is the double of

.
If

the
2
-handle defines a cobordism

, where
\gamma\subset S^{1}\times S^{2}

and
\fr_{T}(\gamma)\pm 1
.

If

(1,2)

In the first case, by Lemma

.

Now suppose

2
-handles define a cobordism
X_{L}\thinspace\colon S^{1}\times S^{2}\to S^{1}\times S^{2}

(2.1)

and the monodromy of

m_{L}=\tau_{2}^{\delta_{2}}\tau_{1}^{\delta_{1}}

\tau_{i}:=\tau_{\gamma_{i}}

.

Let
\lambda\subset T

.
Then,

\partial_{+}X_{L}=S^{1}\times S^{2}

m_{L}(\lambda)\cdot\lambda=0

\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
and we abuse notation,
denoting with
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}

the corresponding homology classes in

. Define

x:=\gamma_{1}\cdot\lambda,\quad y:=\gamma_{2}\cdot\lambda\quad\text{and}\quad n:=\gamma_{2}\cdot\gamma_{1}.

If
x=y=0
we have
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}=\pm\lambda
and
X
has a handlebody decomposition
with a
1
-handle, two
2
-handles attached along
\pm 1
-framed unlinked trivial knots in

S^{1}\times S^{2}
and a
3
-handle. It is easy to check that

is orientation-preserving diffeomorphic
to
-\delta_{1}\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#-\delta_{2}\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#S^{1}\times S^{3}
. Hence, from
now on we assume
(x,y)\neq(0,0)
. Moreover, we choose the orientations of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}

so that

. Since

m_{L}(\lambda)=\tau_{2}^{\delta_{2}}(\tau_{1}^{\delta_{1}}(\lambda))=\tau_{2}^{\delta_{2}}(\lambda+\delta_{1}x\gamma_{1})=\lambda+\delta_{1}x\gamma_{1}+\delta_{2}y\gamma_{2}+\delta_{1}\delta_{2}nx\gamma_{2},

taking the intersection product with
\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\lambda

(2.2)

\delta_{2}x^{2}+\delta_{1}y^{2}+nxy=0.

Equation (

) implies that

and

\delta_{2}+\delta_{1}\pm n=0.

We conclude

. If

3.1.
A trace formula

Let
(V,\cdot)
be a 2-dimensional, real symplectic vector space. Given
v\in V
, denote
by
\tau_{v}:V\to V
the transvection defined by

\tau_{v}(w)=w+(v\cdot w)v,\quad w\in V.

Note that
\tau^{n}_{v}(w)=w+n(v\cdot w)v
for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given
u,v,w\in V
and
l,n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\Phi=\tau^{l}_{u}\tau^{n}_{v}\tau^{m}_{w}
. Then,

nm(v\cdot w)^{2}+lm(u\cdot w)^{2}+ln(u\cdot v)^{2}=lmn(w\cdot u)(w\cdot v)(v\cdot u)+2-tr(\Phi).

Proof.

Once we fix
l,n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
, the two sides of the equality are continuous functions
of
u
,
v
and
w
, therefore it is enough to prove that they agree when
v
and
w

are linearly independent. Using
\{v,w\}
as a basis of
V
, we easily see that for
any endomorphism
f

tr(f)=\dfrac{f(v)\cdot w+v\cdot f(w)}{v\cdot w}

If we write
\tau_{v}^{n}\tau_{w}^{m}(v)=av+bw
and
\tau_{v}^{n}\tau_{w}^{m}(w)=cv+dw
, we have

\begin{split}\tau_{u}^{l}\tau_{v}^{n}\tau_{w}^{m}(v)=\tau_{u}^{l}(av+bw)=a(v+l(u\cdot v)u)+b(w+l(u\cdot w)u)\\
\tau_{u}^{l}\tau_{v}^{n}\tau_{w}^{m}(w)=\tau_{u}^{l}(cv+dw)=c(v+l(u\cdot v)u)+d(w+l(u\cdot w)u)\end{split}

so that

\dfrac{\tau_{u}^{l}\tau_{v}^{n}\tau_{w}^{m}(v)\cdot w+v\cdot\tau_{u}^{l}\tau_{v}^{n}\tau_{w}^{m}(w)}{v\cdot w}=a+d+l\left(\dfrac{b}{v\cdot w}(u\cdot w)^{2}+\dfrac{a-d}{v\cdot w}(u\cdot v)(u\cdot w)-\dfrac{c}{v\cdot w}(u\cdot v)^{2}\right)

Finally, we compute
a=1-mn(v\cdot w)^{2}
,
b=-m(v\cdot w)
,
c=n(v\cdot w)
and
d=1
.
Plugging in theses values we get the desired equality.
∎

3.2.
Diophantine equations

As explained in
[
12
, §1]
, a horizontal link

determines a factorization of the form

The 2-handles produce a cobordism from

Let

From now on we fix arbitrary orientations of the
\gamma_{i}

We have

Let us introduce the variables

(3.1)

Then, the trace formula of Lemma

reads

(3.2)

\delta_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\delta_{2}x_{2}^{2}+\delta_{3}x_{3}^{2}-x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}=\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\delta_{3}(2-tr(m_{L})).

Moreover, observe that

=\lambda+\delta_{1}p_{1}\gamma_{1}+(\delta_{2}p_{2}-\delta_{1}\delta_{2}p_{1}x_{3})\gamma_{2}+\delta_{3}(p_{3}-\delta_{1}p_{1}x_{2}-\delta_{2}p_{2}x_{1}+\delta_{1}\delta_{2}p_{1}x_{1}x_{3})\gamma_{3}.

S_{a}^{\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3}}:=\left\{(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\{\pm 1\}^{3}\times\mathbb{Z}^{3}\ |\ \delta_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\delta_{2}x_{2}^{2}+\delta_{2}x_{3}^{2}-x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}=\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\delta_{3}a\right\}

3.3.

3.4.
Minimal solutions when

In this section we determine the weakly minimal solution of the equation

or

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(x,y,z)
be a solution of Equation (
3.4
) with
|x|
,
|y|
,
|z|
pairwise distinct.
Then,
(x,y,z)
is not weakly minimal. Indeed, mutation at the greatest element in absolute
value makes the quantity
|x|+|y|+|z|
strictly smaller. Moreover, if
\min(|x|,|y|,|z|)>1
then mutation at each of the other two elements makes the quantity
|x|+|y|+|z|
strictly bigger.

Proof.

Up to permuting the variables we may assume:

|x|>|y|>|z|.

Note that, by (
3.4
), this implies
|z|>0
(otherwise
x^{2}+y^{2}=a\in\{0,4\}
, which would imply
y=0=z
), therefore we have
xyz\neq 0
.
We claim that
|\hat{x}|<|x|
. After possibly applying sign changes we may assume that

x,y>0
, and since
xyz=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-a>0
, we have
z>0
as well. Also,
x\hat{x}=y^{2}+z^{2}-a>0
,
therefore
\hat{x}>0
. Replacing
x
with
y
in the equation gives

2y^{2}+z^{2}-y^{2}z-a<y^{2}(3-z),

therefore if

then

is between

and
\hat{x}
, and since
y<x
we must have
\hat{x}<x
,
as claimed. On the other hand,

is impossible because it implies

(x-y)^{2}=a-4,

which has no solutions if
a=0
and forces
x=y
if
a=4
. Finally,
z=1
gives:

x^{2}-xy+y^{2}=a-1\leq 3

but since
x>y>z>0
we have
x>2
, so that

x^{2}-xy+y^{2}=\dfrac{3}{4}x^{2}+\left(\dfrac{x}{2}-y\right)^{2}\geq\dfrac{3}{4}x^{2}>3

This proves the first part of the statement. To prove the second part it suffices to show that if
|x|>|y|>|z|\geq 2
then
|y|<|\hat{y}|
and
|z|<|\hat{z}|
. We have

2|y|<|xy|\leq|xy-z|+|z|<|\hat{z}|+|y|,

therefore
|z|<|y|<|\hat{z}|
. Similarly,

2|x|\leq|xz|\leq|xz-y|+|y|<|\hat{y}|+|x|,

therefore
|y|<|x|<|\hat{y}|
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

A weakly minimal solution
(x,y,z)
of Equation (

•

(0,0,0)
or
(3,3,3)
if

;

•

(2,0,0)
,
(-1,1,1)
or
(2,t,t)
for some

if

.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.4
,
|x|,|y|,|z|
are not pairwise distinct. Therefore we can assume, possibly after permuting the variables and changing two signs, that
y=z\geq 0
. Then, if
a=0
we have

If
y=0
we get the solution
(0,0,0)
, otherwise we must have
x=cy
, with
c^{2}=x-2
for some

. Therefore we have
c^{2}-cy+2=0
, which implies
c\ |\ 2
. Moreover,
y>0
implies

,
so that

. These two values of

lead to the solutions
(3,3,3)
and
(6,3,3)
,
and the conclusion when

which implies either

or

. It is easy to check that the solution

, which holds for all

; on the other hand, a necessary condition for
(y^{2}-2,y,y)
to be weakly minimal is

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(x,y,z)
be a solution of Equation (
3.5
) with
|x|
,
|y|
,
|z|
pairwise distinct.
Then,
(x,y,z)
is not weakly minimal. Indeed, mutation at the greatest element in absolute value makes the quantity
|x|+|y|+|z|
strictly smaller. Moreover, if
\min(|x|,|y|,|z|)>1
then mutation at the other
two elements makes the quantity
|x|+|y|+|z|
strictly bigger.

Proof.

Note that if
(x,y,z)
is a solution then so is
(x,z,y)
. Therefore,
up to swapping
y
and
z
we may assume
|y|>|z|
. Moreover, it is easy to check that
xyz\neq 0
,
because otherwise at least two among
x,y,z
would be zero. We split the proof into the three cases

|x|>|y|>|z|
,
|y|>|x|>|z|
and
|y|>|z|>|x|
.

First case:

|x|>|y|>|z|
. We claim that
|\hat{x}|<|x|
. Up to sign changes it suffices to assume
x,y>0
. This implies
z<0
, because
z>0
gives the contradiction

y^{2}+z^{2}=x^{2}+xyz-a>y^{2}+2xz-a\geq y^{2}+2(z+2)z-a>y^{2}+z^{2}.

We have
x(yz+x)=y^{2}+z^{2}+a>0
, therefore
\hat{x}=-yz-x<0
, which implies, since
-yz>0
and
-x<0
,
|\hat{x}|<|x|
.
This shows that mutation at the greatest element in absolute value makes the quantity
|x|+|y|+|z|
strictly smaller, in particular that
(x,y,z)
is not weakly minimal.
To prove the last part of the statement suppose
|z|>1
.
Then,
y>2
and

(3.6)

2|z|<xy<xy-z+|z|=\hat{z}+|z|,

therefore
|z|<\hat{z}
.

Similarly, we have

2x<|xz|\leq|xz-y|+|y|<|\hat{y}|+x,

therefore
y<x<|\hat{y}|
. This concludes the proof of the statement in the first case.

Before tackling the other cases we observe that up to sign changes we may
assume
y>0
. Moreover, if
xz<0
we have

y^{2}-xyz+z^{2}>x^{2}-xyz+z^{2}>x^{2}\geq x^{2}-a,

which is a contradiction. Hence, after a further sign change we may assume

x>0
and
z>0
as well. Therefore, from now on we assume
x,y,z>0
.

Second case:

|y|>|x|>|z|
.
Note that
y\hat{y}=z^{2}-x^{2}+a\geq 0
forces
x=2
,
z=1
and
a=4
, in which case

y\hat{y}=1
, which is impossible because
y>z\geq 1
. Therefore we have

y\hat{y}<0
, which implies
\hat{y}=xz-y<0
.
It follows that
0<xz<y
, hence
|\hat{y}|<|y|
.
To prove the last part of the statement suppose
|z|>1
. Then,

|z|<|\hat{z}|
follows as before from (
3.6
).
Finally, we have

2|y|\leq|-yz-x|+|x|<|\hat{x}|+|y|,

hence
|x|<|y|<|\hat{x}|
. This proves the statement in the second case.

Third case:

|y|>|z|>|x|
.
Since
y\hat{y}=z^{2}-x^{2}+a>0
we have
\hat{y}=xz-y>0
. If
\hat{y}\geq y
then
xz\geq 2y

and, using that
z\geq x+1\geq 2
, we get the contradictory inequalities

y^{2}+z^{2}=x^{2}+xyz-a>2y^{2}-a\geq y^{2}+(|z|+1)^{2}-a>y^{2}+z^{2}.

Therefore
|y|=y>\hat{y}=|\hat{y}|
.
To prove the last part of the statement observe that

2|x|<|yz|\leq|-yz-x|+|x|=|\hat{x}|+|x|,

hence
|x|<|\hat{x}|
. Using the extra assumption
|x|\geq 2
we have

2|y|\leq|xy-z|+|z|<|\hat{z}|+|y|,

hence
|z|<|y|<|\hat{z}|
. This proves the statement in the third case and concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

belong to
\mathcal{F}_{2}

because they arise, respectively, from the triples
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=(1,1,0)
and
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=(2,1,3)
. If
B_{F_{a},q}\in\mathcal{F}_{3}
,
as before we may assume
a>3
.
We claim that
q^{2}\equiv-1\bmod F_{a}
implies
B_{F_{a},q}=B_{F_{a},F_{a-2}}
.
This time we choose
b=1
and
\varepsilon=-1
.
Then, by (I9)
q\equiv\pm L_{1}/F_{1+a}\equiv\mp F_{a-1}\equiv\pm F_{a-2}\bmod F_{a}
and

F_{a-2}^{2}=F_{a}F_{a-4}-F_{2}^{2}\equiv-1\bmod F_{a}.

Therefore
q^{2}\equiv-1\bmod F_{a}
implies
q^{2}\equiv F_{a-2}^{2}\bmod F_{a}
.
As before, in view of Lemma
5.3
this proves the claim.
Since by (I9) the triple

F_{a+1}F_{a-1}\equiv-1\bmod F_{a}\quad\text{and}\quad F_{a-2}\equiv-F_{a-1}\bmod F_{a},

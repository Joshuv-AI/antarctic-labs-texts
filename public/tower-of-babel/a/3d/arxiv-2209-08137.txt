1.
Notation and Acknowledgement

We first recall some definitions from general topology. A Polish space
X
, is a topological space that is separable and where the topology is metrisable with a complete metric. In this paper we will always use
X
for a fixed Polish space. No extra assumptions will be put on
X
. There are no local compactness assumptions as we believe that the most interesting applications are when
X
is for instance an infinite dimensional Banach space. The space of real valued bounded continuous functions defined on
X
is denoted by
C_{b}(X)
.

Definition 1
.

A function
u\colon C_{b}(X)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is called a concave monetary utility function if it satisfies the following properties

(1)

u
is a concave function

(2)

if
f\in C_{b}(X)
is nonnegative then
u(f)\geq 0

(3)

for
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have
u(f+a)=u(f)+a
for all
f\in C_{b}(X)

(4)

we say that
u
is coherent if moreover
u
is positively homogeneous

(5)

we say that
u
satisfies the Fatou property if for every sequence
(f_{n})_{n}
in
C_{b}(X)
so that
f_{n}\downarrow f\in C_{b}(X)
pointwise,
u(f_{n})\downarrow u(f)

A concave monetary utility function is completely characterised by the acceptability set
{\mathcal{A}}=\{f\mid u(f)\geq 0\}
. For
f\in C_{b}(X)
we have
u(f)=\max\{a\in{\mathbb{R}}\mid f-a\in{\mathcal{A}}\}
.

Remark 1
.

The usual definition of the Fatou property is different. Usually one would require that
u(f)\geq\limsup u(f_{n})
for every uniformly bounded sequence
(f_{n})_{n}
pointwise converging to
f
. This is equivalent to the statement that for a uniformly bounded sequence
(f_{n})_{n}
in
{\mathcal{A}}
and pointwise converging to
f\in C_{b}(X)
, one also has
f\in{\mathcal{A}}
. We only use downward converging sequences and hence we get a more general concept. But once the main result is proved, one easily shows, using the Fatou lemma from measure theory, that
u(f)\geq\limsup u(f_{n})
holds for uniformly bounded pointwise converging sequences.

From now on we will suppose – if not otherwise mentioned – that
u
is a coherent utility function with the Fatou property. The following proposition is easily proved. We skip the details.

Proposition 1
.

{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

{\mathcal{A}}
is a convex cone containing the cone of nonnegative functions,
C^{+}_{b}(X)
. In other words
{\mathcal{A}}+C^{+}_{b}(X)={\mathcal{A}}
. If
f\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
f\leq g\in C_{b}(X)
then also
g\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

(2)

|u(f)|\leq\|f\|=\sup\{|f(x)|\mid x\in X\}
.

(3)

If
f_{n}
is a sequence in
{\mathcal{A}}
and
f_{n}\downarrow f\in C_{b}(X)
pointwise, then also
f\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

(4)

is closed for the topology of uniform convergence on

.

(5)

The constant function

{\mathcal{A}}
.

The main result of the paper is the following

Theorem 1
.

With the notation and properties introduced above there is a weak

{\mathcal{A}}=\{f\mid\mu(f)\geq 0\text{ for all }\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\}\quad\quad\quad u(f)=\inf\{\mu(f)\mid\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\}.

The difficulty of the proof lies in the fact that in general the dual space of

We will show how to extend the theorem so that it contains a result for concave monetary utility functions. The case of coherent utility functions is notationally simpler, so we preferred to prove this case first and then extend it.

When

is defined on the space of all continuous functions on

the Fatou property will be a consequence of the concavity and the monotonicity. In that case we have

Theorem 2
.

Suppose

(1)

(2)

(3)

During the 2014–visit to Shandong University in Jinan, Professor Xinpeng Li asked the author whether he could prove the main result of this paper. We looked at it but could not find an immediate breakthrough. The problem remained as a cold case on our desk. We thank Xinpeng Li for the discussions we had on this question.

As possible applications we think of
X
being a space of trajectories of prices or portfolios. Then a continuous function could be the value of a financial instrument, where the value depends on the trajectory. Classical examples are when
X
is the space of continuous functions on a compact interval, endowed with the sup norm. Another example could be the space of càdlàg trajectories endowed with the Skorohod topology. But of course these are just examples of possible applications. The development of such applications is beyond the limited scope of this paper.

The paper is organised as follows. In the next section we recall some well known results from general topology. These topological results will then be used in the third section to prove the main theorem. We also give a more constructive proof using some approximation result that allows continuous functions to be seen as limits of uniformly continuous functions. In section 5 we extend it to the case of concave functions. We show how to extend monetary utility functions to the space of bounded Borel functions on
X
in section 6. The extension is in most cases not unique. Finally we show that for concave monetary functions defined on the set of all continuous functions the algebraic properties imply the Fatou property.

We emphasize that we only use continuity with respect to downward converging sequences. The case of continuity with respect to increasing pointwise converging sequences is less general since continuity for increasing sequences implies continuity for decreasing sequences. That case was already studied and analysed in the literature, see for instance the paper by Cheridito, Kupper and Tangpi,
[
1
]
, where general theorems are proved and where a good overview is given. Roughly speaking the stronger convergence implies some kind of compactness. The precise meaning of this and its relation to some other concepts from functional analysis is outside the scope of this paper, see
[
3
]
.

2.
Some topological results

In our setting
X
will be a Polish space. This means that it is a separable metrisable space for which there exist a metric for which it becomes complete, i.e. every Cauchy sequence converges. It is a well known theorem from general topology that says that
X
can be embedded as a dense
G_{\delta}
set in a compact metric space. Also the converse holds, if the Polish space
X
is a dense subspace of a compact topological space
Y
, then
X
is a
G_{\delta}
set in
Y
. A
G_{\delta}
set is a set that is the countable intersection of open sets. The complement of a
G_{\delta}
set is the countable union of closed sets and such a set is called an
F_{\sigma}
set. These results were proved by Alexandroff, Hausdorff and Sierpinsky, see the book by Kelley,
[
5
]
, p. 208, Problem K. A more general treatment was developed by Čech,
[
4
]
, p. 196. This book also contains a history of this result.

The space

Proposition 2

The mapping

\beta X\setminus X
onto

\beta X\setminus X

Proof
The first line of the proposition can be proved as follows. Suppose

. Then of course

. There are neighbourhoods

The set

Corollary 1
.

For each set

Proof
We only have to give a construction of the functions

. On

and use the function

.∎

Remark 2
.

That the sets

can be chosen as

Remark 3
.

The above reasoning can be generalised to yield the result that when

The dual space of

3.
Coherent monetary utility functions

We use the notation introduced in the preceding sections. We first note that
{\mathcal{A}}
is a cone in
C(\beta X)
that is also closed for the uniform or norm topology. As in the classical case of the theory of risk measures it is easily seen that there is a weak
∗
closed convex subset
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathcal{P}}(\beta X)
such that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\beta X)\mid\mu(f)\geq 0\text{ for all }f\in{\mathcal{A}}\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f\in C(\beta X)\mid\mu(f)\geq 0\text{ for all }\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}\}.

The proof is a simple application of the Hahn-Banach theorem or the bipolar theorem, see
[
7

is constructed as a base for the polar cone

.
The problem we face is that some elements of

might charge the set

For each

. The set

is clearly convex and so is its closure

. Suppose now that
{\mathcal{W}}\neq{\mathcal{S}}
. Then by the separation theorem

In case

Proof of the main theorem
The set

4.
Another – more constructive – proof of the main theorem

In this section we will give a more constructive proof of the main theorem. We will not use the axiom of choice.
3
3

3

That we give such a proof should not be seen as an indication that the author has converted to constructivism
We will make use of the separation theorem and the compactness of the dual ball of a Banach space in its weak
∗
topology but since the spaces we will use are separable, the general version of the axiom of choice can be replaced by a countable equivalent. The interested reader can look up such developments in the specialised literature. We do not give details, not even references. The proof we will give also uses some, maybe useful result on the approximation of continuous functions by uniformly continuous functions.

We again use a compactification of
X
and this time we suppose that the Polish space
X
is a dense
G_{\delta}
of a compact metric space
K
. The metric on
K
will be denoted by

. Bounded continuous functions on

cannot necessarily be extended to continuous functions on

. However we know that a bounded continuous function on

can be extended to a continuous function on

Lemma 2
.

For each element
f\in C_{b}(X)
there is a sequence of functions
h_{n}\in C(K)
such that
h_{n}\downarrow f
on
X
.

Proof
We will construct a sequence of elements
g_{n}\in C(K)
such that
g_{n}\uparrow f
. By applying the result to
-f
we then get the statement of the lemma, in fact we then will have proved that there are two sequences in
C(K)
one approaching
f
from above and the other approaching
f
from below. The proof uses a convolution technique. For each
n
we define

g_{n}(x)=\inf\{f(y)+n\,d(x,y)\mid y\in X\}.

Clearly
g_{n}(x)\leq f(x)
. For two points
x_{1},x_{2}
in
X
we have

\displaystyle g_{n}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{f(y)+n\,d(x_{1},y)\mid y\in X\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf\{f(y)+n\,d(x_{2},y)+n\,d(x_{1},x_{2})\mid y\in X\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{n}(x_{2})+n\,d(x_{1},x_{2}).

By symmetry we then get
|g_{n}(x_{1})-g_{n}(x_{2})|\leq n\,d(x_{1},x_{2})
, proving that
g_{n}
can be extended to an element of
C(K)
. It is also obvious that
g_{n}\leq g_{n+1}
. We now show that for each
x\in X
,
g_{n}(x)\uparrow f(x)
. To estimate
g_{n}(x)
we distinguish between
d(x,y)\leq\frac{2\|f\|}{n}
and
d(x,y)\geq\frac{2\|f\|}{n}
. In the latter case we have
f(y)+n\,d(x,y)\geq f(y)+2\|f\|\geq\|f\|\geq f(x)
. In the former case we have
f(y)+n\,d(x,y)\geq\inf_{d(x,z)\leq\frac{2\|f\|}{n}}f(z)
. We get
g_{n}(x)\geq\inf_{d(x,y)\leq\frac{2\|f\|}{n}}f(y)
. As
n\rightarrow\infty
the continuity of

implies that
g_{n}(x)\rightarrow f(x)
.∎

Let now
{\mathcal{A}}_{u}
be the set of elements of
{\mathcal{A}}
that are uniformly continuous, identified with their extensions to

. With a slight abuse of notation
{\mathcal{A}}_{u}={\mathcal{A}}\cap C(K)
. The set

is a cone, closed for the uniform convergence on

. There exists a convex, weak

The cone

satisfies the same sequential closedness property as

. This means that if

is a sequence in

that converges on

, then also

. We can now copy the proof of the preceding section and get that

is weak

. This gives us

{\mathcal{A}}_{u}=\{h\in C(K)\mid\mu(h)\geq 0\text{ for all }\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\}.

But the approximation lemma then shows that also

{\mathcal{A}}=\{f\in C_{b}(X)\mid\mu(f)\geq 0\text{ for all }\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\}.

∎

5.
Extension to concave monetary utility functions

For concave monetary utility functions
u
, we will use a metrisable compactification of

. The probability measures on

c(\mu)=\sup\{\mu(-f)\mid f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{u}\}.

The function

{\mathcal{C}}=\{(\mu,t)\mid t\geq c(\mu),t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(K)\}

{\mathcal{P}}(K)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. Observe the trivial fact that

implies that

.
Then the duality result (proved using the Hahn-Banach theorem) says that

\displaystyle u(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{\mu(f)+c(\mu)\mid\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(K)\}=\min\{\mu(f)+c(\mu)\mid\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(K)\}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f\in C(K)\mid\mu(f)+c(\mu)\geq 0\text{ for all }\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(K)\}.

Suppose that

{\mathcal{P}}(K)\times{\mathbb{R}}
and suppose that
{\mathcal{W}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}
. We first suppose that
{\mathcal{V}}
is not empty and will handle this trivial case afterwards. Let us fix an element
(\mu_{0},t_{0})\in{\mathcal{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{W}}
. Then the separation theorem gives us a couple
(f,\alpha)
(
f\in C(K),\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
) such that

\mu_{0}(f)+\alpha t_{0}<0\leq\inf\{\mu(f)+\alpha t\mid(\mu,t)\in{\mathcal{W}}\}.

Because
{\mathcal{W}}\neq\emptyset
we must have that
\alpha\geq 0
, otherwise the right hand side is
-\infty
. (That
0
can be used as a separating value can be achieved by adding a constant function to
f
.) If
\alpha=0
we can increase
\alpha
by an arbitrary small positive number so that the inequality remains valid. So we may assume that
\alpha>0
and we can divide both sides of the equation by
\alpha
. In either case and using
t_{0}\geq c(\mu_{0})
, we get

\mu_{0}(f)+c(\mu_{0})<0\leq\inf\{\mu(f)+c(\mu)\mid(\mu,t)\in{\mathcal{W}}\}.

In case
{\mathcal{V}}
is empty we take
f=-1
and proceed as in the case
\alpha=0
above.
Elements
(\mu,t)\in{\mathcal{C}}
that are not in
{\mathcal{W}}
satisfy
\mu(M_{n})\geq\epsilon
. Now take
k
big enough to make sure that
-\|f\|+k\epsilon\geq 0
. Then the functions
f_{N}=f+k\phi_{n}^{N}
satisfy
\mu(f_{N})+c(\mu)\geq\mu(f_{N})\geq 0
for all
\mu
with
\mu(M_{n})\geq\epsilon
and
\mu(f_{N})+c(\mu)\geq\mu(f)+c(\mu)\geq 0
if
\mu(M_{n})<\epsilon
because
(\mu,c(\mu))\in{\mathcal{V}}
in that case. This shows that

f_{N}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{u}
. As in section 3 we have
f_{N}\downarrow f
on
X
and hence
f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{u}
which is a contradiction. This ends the proof that
{\mathcal{V}}
is dense in
{\mathcal{C}}
. We must still show that the set
{\mathcal{V}}
is a
G_{\delta}
in
{\mathcal{C}}
. This is obvious since
{\mathcal{V}}={\mathcal{C}}\cap\left(\{\mu\mid\mu(M_{n})<\epsilon\}\times{\mathbb{R}}\right)
and hence is an open set in
{\mathcal{C}}
. The set
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
is the countable intersection of dense open sets of
{\mathcal{C}}
and since
{\mathcal{C}}
is a Polish space, it has the Baire property. Therefore
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}
is also a dense set in
{\mathcal{C}}
.∎

6.
Extension to bounded Borel functions

A Fatou concave monetary utility function
u\colon C_{b}(X)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
can be written using probability measures on the Polish space
X
. As proved above there is a weak
∗
lower semi continuous function
c:{\mathcal{P}}(X)\rightarrow\overline{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
such that for
f\in C_{b}(X)
we have
u(f)=\inf\{\mu(f)+c(\mu)\mid\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(X)\}
. This formula can be used for bounded Borel functions and hence we get an extension
\tilde{u}
of
u
. If the space of bounded Borel functions is denoted by
{\mathcal{B}}_{b}(X)
we get

(1)

\tilde{u}\colon{\mathcal{B}}_{b}(X)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\quad\tilde{u}(f)=\inf\{\mu(f)+c(\mu)\mid\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(X)\}

(2)

\tilde{u}
is concave, monetary and
f\leq g
implies
\tilde{u}(f)\leq\tilde{u}(g)

(3)

it has the Fatou property: if
f_{n}
is a sequence in
{\mathcal{B}}_{b}(X)
tending downward pointwise to
f\in{\mathcal{B}}_{b}(X)
then
\tilde{u}(f_{n})\downarrow\tilde{u}(f)
.

However there is no guarantee that the extension is uniquely defined, not even among the Fatou, monetary concave utility functions. To see this we will define two Fatou, coherent utility functions
u_{1},u_{2}
which agree on
C_{b}(X)
but are different on
{\mathcal{B}}_{b}(X)
. This example even works on perfect compact metrisable spaces. Let us recall that a set is of first category if it is contained in the countable union of closed sets having empty interior. In other words the complement contains a dense
G_{\delta}
. We now define the two functions as follows:

\displaystyle u_{1}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{f(x)\mid x\in X\}

\displaystyle u_{2}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{a\mid\{x\mid f(x)\leq a\}\text{ is of first category }\}.

For the interval
[0,1]
let
f
be the indicator function
{\rm\bf 1}_{J}
, of the irrational numbers
J
. Clearly
u_{1}(f)=0
whereas
u_{2}(f)=1
. Both functions satisfy the Fatou property, see
[
2
]
, example 4.8. For continuous functions we have that both functions agree. The defining set for
u_{1}
is the set of all probability measures, whereas for
u_{2}
we can only use finitely additive probability measures.

7.
The case
C(X)

In this section we assume that
X
is a non compact Polish space. The space
C(X)
of all continuous real valued functions defined on
X
, is usually endowed with the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets. The dual space is then the space of sigma-additive measures on
X
, having compact support. This suggest that monetary concave utility functions on
C(X)
are representable by a set of probabilities concentrated on the same compact set. Since the space
C(X)
is in general not metrisable, we might think that sequential continuity as in the Fatou property, is not sufficient. But if the utility function is defined on such a big space and has some algebraic properties, more is true.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose
u\colon C(X)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies

(1)

u
is a concave function that is positively homogeneous

(2)

u
is monetary: for each
f\in C(X)
and each
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have
u(f+a)=u(f)+a

(3)

u
is monotone:
f\leq g
implies
u(f)\leq u(g)
.

Then
u
also satisfies: for decreasing sequences
f_{n}\downarrow f
converging pointwise to a continuous function
f
we have
u(f_{n})\downarrow u(f)
.

Proof
We will show that if
f_{n}\downarrow f
,
f_{n},f\in C(X)
, are such that for some
\delta>0
and for all
n
,
u(f_{n})>u(f)+2\delta
then
u
cannot take finite values on the whole of
C(X)
. The first step is to replace the sequence
f_{n}
by a sequence that tends to
f
in a stationary way. For this we look at
g_{n}=\max(f,f_{n}-\delta)
. Clearly
u(g_{n})\geq u(f_{n})-\delta\geq u(f)+\delta
. Since
f_{n}\downarrow f
, we have that also
g_{n}\downarrow f
but there is more. The open sets
G_{n}=\{x\mid g_{n}(x)>f(x)\}
are decreasing. If
x\in G_{n}
then
g_{n}(x)>f(x)
but this implies that
f_{n}(x)>f(x)+\delta
. For the closure this implies that on
\overline{G_{n}}
,
f_{n}(x)\geq f(x)+\delta
. Hence if
x\in\cap_{n}\overline{G_{n}}
we would have
\lim_{n}f_{n}(x)\geq f(x)+\delta
, a contradiction. This implies
\cap_{n}\overline{G_{n}}=\emptyset
. For every
x
this means that it can only be an element of
G_{n}
for a finite number of natural numbers. So for every
x
there is
n_{0}
(depending on
x
) such that for all
n>n_{0}
, we have
g_{n}(x)=f(x)
, i.e.
g_{n}(x)\downarrow f(x)
in a stationary way. For each
n
we will use the function
(n+1)f-ng_{n}=f-n(g_{n}-f)\leq f
. From the previous line we deduce that this sequence tends to
f
in a stationary way. Using that

f=\frac{1}{n+1}\left((n+1)f-ng_{n}\right)+\frac{n}{n+1}g_{n}

and applying concavity we get

u(g_{n})-\delta\geq u(f)\geq\frac{1}{n+1}u\left((n+1)f-ng_{n}\right)+\frac{n}{n+1}u(g_{n}).

This implies

\frac{1}{n+1}u(g_{n})-\delta\geq\frac{1}{n+1}u\left((n+1)f-ng_{n}\right)

yielding
u\left((n+1)f-ng_{n}\right)\leq-(n+1)\delta+u(g_{n})\leq-(n+1)\delta+u(g_{1})
. We now use the properties of the sequence
G_{n}
to prove that

F=\inf\{(n+1)f-ng_{n}\mid n\geq 1\}

is a continuous function. The stationary convergence implies that
F\colon X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
. Let now
x_{m}\rightarrow x
be a convergent sequence in
X
.
We look at the compact set
C=\{x_{m}\mid m\geq 1\}\cup\{x\}
and observe that

C\cap\cap_{n}\overline{G_{n}}=\emptyset
. The compactness then says that there is
n_{0}
with
C\cap\overline{G_{n_{0}}}=\emptyset
.
For the function
F
this means:

\displaystyle F(x_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{((n+1)f-ng_{n})(x_{m})\mid n\leq n_{0}-1\}

\displaystyle F(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{((n+1)f-ng_{n})(x)\mid n\leq n_{0}-1\}.

Hence
F(x_{m})\rightarrow F(x)
. The definition of
F
and the monotonicity of
u
give that for each
n
,
u(F)\leq-(n+1)\delta+u(g_{1})
, contradicting that
u(F)>-\infty
.
∎

Theorem 5
.

Suppose
u\colon C(X)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies

(1)

u
is a concave function that is positively homogeneous

(2)

u
is monetary: for each
f\in C(X)
and each
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have
u(f+a)=u(f)+a

(3)

u
is monotone:
f\leq g
implies
u(f)\leq u(g)
.

Then there is a compact set
L\subset X
and a convex set,
{\mathcal{S}}
, of probability measures having support contained in
L
, i.e.
\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}
implies
\mu(L)=1
, such that for all
f\in C(X)
,
u(f)=\inf\{\mu(f)\mid\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}\}
.

Proof
The previous theorem shows that
u
has the Fatou property and hence its restriction to
C_{b}(X)
can be represented by a convex set
{\mathcal{S}}
of probability measures on
X
. So for each
f\in C_{b}(X)
we have
u(f)=\inf\{\mu(f)\mid\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}\}
. But the downward sequential continuity then shows that for
f\in C(X)
,
f
bounded above,
u(f)=\inf\{\mu(f)\mid\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}\}
. We will now show that the elements of
{\mathcal{S}}
are concentrated on a compact set. For each
\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}
we denote by
F_{\mu}
the support of
\mu
, this is the smallest closed set that has measure equal to one. We now show that
\cup_{\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}}F_{\mu}
is relatively compact. We argue by contradiction and suppose that there is a sequence
\mu_{n}\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
x_{n}\in F_{\mu_{n}}
so that
(x_{n})_{n}
has no convergent subsequence and are all different. The proof of the following lemma will ge given later.

Lemma 3
.

There is a sequence of functions
\psi_{n}
with
0\leq\psi_{n}\leq 1
,
\psi_{n}(x_{n})=1
and such that for each sequence of real numbers
(a_{n})_{n}
the sum
\sum_{n}a_{n}\psi_{n}
converges pointwise to a continuous function.

Take now
a_{n}=\frac{n}{u(-\psi_{n})}
. This is well defined since
u(-\psi_{n})<0
,
x_{n}
being in the support of
\mu_{n}
. The function
g=\sum_{n}a_{n}\psi_{n}
is in
C(X)
and
u(g)\leq-a_{n}u(-\psi_{n})=-n
by monotonicity. Since this must hold for each
n
, we get a contradiction with
u(g)>-\infty
. Let
L=\overline{\cup_{\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}}F_{\mu}}
which is a compact set. For each
f\in C(X)
, bounded above, we now have

u(f)=\inf\{\mu(f)\mid\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}\}=\inf\{\mu(f{\rm\bf 1}_{L})\mid\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}\}.

For
f
not necessarily bounded above we again use concavity to get the same expression. Let
\sup\{f(x)\mid x\in L\}=n_{0}
and observe that for each
n\geq n_{0}
,
u(f\wedge n)=u(f\wedge n_{0})=\alpha
. Suppose that
u(f)\geq\alpha+\delta
, where
\delta>0
. The function
f-2(f-f\wedge n)=2(f\wedge n)-f
is bounded above and on
L
it is equal to
f
. Hence
u(2(f\wedge n)-f)=\alpha
. Now use concavity of
u
and
f\wedge n={1\over 2}f+{1\over 2}(2(f\wedge n)-f)
to get

\alpha=u(f\wedge n)\geq{1\over 2}u(f)+{1\over 2}u(2(f\wedge n)-f)\geq{1\over 2}(\alpha+\delta)+{1\over 2}\alpha=\alpha+{1\over 2}\delta,

a contradiction. This shows that also
u(f)=\alpha
and hence

u(f)=\inf\{\mu(f)\mid\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}\}=\inf\{\mu(f{\rm\bf 1}_{L})\mid\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}\},

for all
f\ C(X)
.∎

Proof of the lemma
Let
d
be a metric that defines the topology of
X
. We may suppose that
d\leq 1
. For each
J\subset{\mathbb{N}}
, the set
\{x_{n}\mid n\in J\}
is closed. Obviously
x_{1}\notin\{x_{n}\mid n\geq 2\}
, which is a closed set. Let
0<\eta_{1}<{1\over 4}\operatorname{dist}(x_{1},\{x_{n}\mid n\geq 2\})
. Inductively we now define
\eta_{k},k\geq 2
so that

0<\eta_{k}\leq\min\left(\frac{\eta_{k-1}}{2},{1\over 4}\operatorname{dist}(x_{k},\{x_{n}\mid n\geq{k+1}\}\right).

For each
n
let
O_{n}
be the open ball around
x_{n}
with radius
\eta_{n}
. It is easy to see that for
n\neq m
,
\overline{O_{n}}\cap\overline{O_{m}}=\emptyset
. The sequence of closed sets
\overline{O_{n}};n\geq 1
is locally finite, i.e. for each
y\in X
there is
\epsilon>0
so that
B(y,\epsilon)
(the open ball of radius
\epsilon
around
y
) only intersects a finite number of sets of the form
\overline{O_{n}}
. Indeed if this would not be true, then for some
y\in X
and all
\epsilon>0
, the set
\{n\mid B(y,\epsilon)\cap\overline{O_{n}}\neq\emptyset\}
would be infinite. This allows to construct a sequence of points
z_{k},k\geq 1
such that
d(y,z_{k})\leq 2^{-k}
and
d(z_{k},x_{n_{k}})\leq\eta_{n_{k}}
where
n_{k}\rightarrow\infty
. Consequently
d(y,x_{n_{k}})\leq 2^{-k}+\eta_{n_{k}}\rightarrow 0
implying that
x_{n_{k}}\rightarrow y
, a contradiction to the choice of the sequence
x_{n},n\geq 1
. We know – and this is easily proved – that for a locally finite family of closed sets the union is also closed. For each
n
, the function
\psi_{n}
can be taken as

\psi_{n}(x)=\frac{\operatorname{dist}(x,O_{n}^{c})\wedge\operatorname{dist}(x_{n},O_{n}^{c})}{\operatorname{dist}(x_{n},O_{n}^{c})}.

For a sequence of real numbers
(a_{n})_{n}
the sum
h=\sum_{n}a_{n}\psi_{n}
converges pointwise since the supports of the functions
\psi_{n}
are contained in the pairwise disjoint sets
\overline{O_{n}}
. To see that
h
is a continuous function we take a convergent sequence
y_{m}\rightarrow y
in
X
and we will show that

. If the point
y
is not in the closed set
\cup_{n}\overline{O_{n}}
, then for

and hence

. If

then let

. But in this case

and since this is a closed set we again have for

. This means that for all

and this converges to
a_{k}\psi_{k}(y)=h(y)
.∎

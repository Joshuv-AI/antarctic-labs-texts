Dynamic Spectral Clustering with Provable Approximation Guarantee
Steinar Laenen, He Sun

Abstract
This paper studies clustering algorithms for dynamically evolving graphs { G t } \{G_{t}\} , in which new edges (and potential new vertices) are added into a graph, and the underlying cluster structure of the graph can gradually change. The paper proves that, under some mild condition on the cluster-structure, the clusters of the final graph G T G_{T} of n T n_{T} vertices at time T T can be well approximated by a dynamic variant of the spectral clustering algorithm. The algorithm runs in amortised update time O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) and query time o ⁡ ( n T ) o(n_{T}) . Experimental studies on both synthetic and real-world datasets further confirm the practicality of our designed algorithm.

Introduction

For any graph
G=(V,E)
and parameter
k\in\mathbb{N}
as input, the objective of graph clustering is to partition the vertex set of
G
into
k
clusters such that vertices within each cluster are better connected than to the rest of the graph. Since large-scale graphs are commonly used to model practical datasets, designing efficient graph clustering algorithms is an important problem in machine learning and related fields.

In practice, these large-scale graphs usually evolve over time: not only are new vertices and edges added into a graph, but the graph’s clusters could also change gradually, resulting in a new cluster-structure in the long term. Instead of periodically running a clustering algorithm from scratch, it is important to design algorithms that can quickly identify and return the new clusters in dynamically evolving graphs.

In this paper we study clustering for dynamically evolving graphs, and obtain the following results. As the first and conceptual contribution, we propose a model for dynamic graph clustering. In contrast to the classical model for dynamic graph algorithms
(
Thorup, 2007
;
Beimel et al., 2022
)
, our proposed model considers not only edge insertions but also vertex insertions; as such the underlying graph can gradually form a new cluster-structure with a different number of clusters from the initial graph.

As the second and algorithmic result, we design a randomised graph clustering algorithm that works in the above-mentioned model,
and our result is as follows:

1.1
Overview of the Algorithm

For any input graph
G_{1}
with a well-defined cluster structure, we first construct a
cluster-preserving sparsifier

H_{1}
of
G_{1}
, which is a sparse subgraph of
G_{1}
that maintains its cluster-structure, and employ spectral clustering on
H_{1}
to obtain the initial
k
clusters of
G_{1}
. After this, with a new edge arriving at every time
t
, our designed algorithm applies two components to track the cluster-structure of
G_{t}
.

The first component is a dynamic algorithm that maintains a cluster-preserving sparsifier
H_{t}
for
G_{t}
. Our designed algorithm is based on sampling edges with probability proportional to the degrees of their endpoints, and these edges get resampled if their degrees have significantly changed. We show that
H_{t}
always
preserves the cluster-structure of
G_{t}
, and the algorithm’s amortised update time complexity is
O(1)
.

Our second component is an algorithm that dynamically maintains a
contracted graph

\widetilde{G}_{t}
of
G_{t}
, and this contracted graph is used to sketch the cluster-structure of
G_{t}
. For the first input graph
G_{1}
and the output of spectral clustering on
H_{1}
, our initial contracted graph
\widetilde{G}_{1}
consists of
k
super vertices with self-loops: these super vertices correspond to the
k
clusters in
G_{1}
, and are connected by edges with weight equal to the cut values of the corresponding clusters in
H_{1}
. After that, when new edges (and potentially new vertices) arrive over time, our algorithm updates
\widetilde{G}_{t}
such that (new) clusters are represented by either the same super vertices, newly added vertices, or a combination of both. The algorithm further updates the edge weights between the super vertices.
With slight increase in the number of vertices of
\widetilde{G}_{t}
over time, we prove that the cluster-structure in
G_{t}
is approximately preserved in
\widetilde{G}_{t}
. In particular, when new clusters are formed in
G_{t}
, this new cluster-structure of
G_{t}
can be identified by the eigen-gap of
\widetilde{G}_{t}
’s Laplacian matrix. See Figure
1
for the illustration of our approach.

1.2
Related work

Our work directly relates to a number of works on incremental spectral clustering algorithms (e.g.,
(
Dhanjal et al., 2014
;
Martin et al., 2018
;
Ning et al., 2007
)
). These works usually rely on analysing the change of approximate eigenvectors and don’t show the approximation guarantee of the returned clusters. Many works along this direction further employ matrix perturbation theory in their analysis, requiring that the total number of vertices in a graph is fixed.

Our work is also linked to related dynamic graph algorithm problems (e.g.,
(
Bernstein et al., 2022
;
Saranurak & Wang, 2019
)
). However, most works in dynamic graph algorithms focus on the design of dynamic algorithms in a
general
graph, while for dynamic clustering one needs to assume the presence of cluster-structures in the initial and final graphs,
such that the algorithm’s performance can be rigorously analysed. Nevertheless, some of our presented techniques, like the adaptive sampling, are inspired by the dynamic graph algorithms literature.

2.3
Model for Dynamic Graph Clustering

We assume that the initial graph
G_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1})
with
n_{1}
vertices satisfies
\lambda_{k+1}(\mathcal{L}_{G_{1}})=\Omega(1)

and
\rho_{G_{1}}(k)=O(k^{-8}\log^{-2\gamma}(n_{1}))
for some constant
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. This condition is similar to lower bounding

\Upsilon_{G_{1}}(k)
, and
ensures that the initial input graph
G_{1}
has
k
well-defined clusters. After this, the underlying graph is updated through an edge insertion at each time, and let
G_{t}=(V_{t},E_{t})
be the graph constructed at time
t
.
We assume that every edge insertion introduces at most one new vertex; as such the underlying graph is always connected, and the number of vertices
n_{t}\triangleq|V_{t}|
could increase over time. We further assume that, after every
\Theta(\log^{\gamma}(n_{t}))
steps, there is time
t^{\prime}
such that

G_{t^{\prime}}=(V_{t^{\prime}},E_{t^{\prime}})
presents a well-defined structure of
k^{\prime}
clusters, which is characterised by

\lambda_{k^{\prime}+1}(\mathcal{L}_{G_{t^{\prime}}})=\Omega(1)
and
\rho_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(k^{\prime})=O(k^{\prime-8}\cdot\log^{-2\gamma}(n_{t^{\prime}}))
for some
k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
.

Notice that, since both the number of vertices
n_{t}
in time
t
and the number of clusters could change, our above-defined
dynamic gap assumption
allows the underlying graph to gradually form a new cluster structure, e.g.,
O(\log^{\gamma}(n_{1}))
newly added vertices and their adjacent edges could initially form a small new cluster which gradually “grows” into a large one. On the other side, our assumption prevents the disappearance of the underlying graph’s cluster-structure throughout the edge updates, which would make the objective function of a clustering algorithm ill-defined.

Dynamic Cluster-Preserving Sparsifiers

A graph sparsifier is a sparse representation of an input graph that inherits certain properties of the
original dense graph, and their efficient construction plays a key role in designing a
number of nearly-linear time graph algorithms. However, typical constructions of graph sparsifiers are based on fast Laplacian solvers, making them difficult to implement in practice. To overcome this,
Sun & Zanetti (2019)

studied a variant of graph sparsifiers for graph clustering, and introduced the notion of a cluster-preserving sparsifier:

Definition 3.1

(Cluster-preserving sparsifier)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be any graph with
k
clusters, and
\{S_{i}\}^{k}_{i=1}
a
k
-way partition of
G
corresponding to
\rho_{G}(k)
. We call a re-weighted subgraph
H=(V,F\subset E,w_{H})
a cluster-preserving sparsifier of
G
if (i)
\Phi_{H}(S_{i})=O(k\cdot\Phi_{G}(S_{i}))
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
, and (ii)
\lambda_{k+1}(\mathcal{L}_{H})=\Omega(\lambda_{k+1}(\mathcal{L}_{G}))
.

To examine the two conditions of Definition
3.1
, notice that
graph
G=(V,E)
has exactly
k
clusters if (i)
G
has
k
disjoint subsets
S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}
of low conductance, and (ii) any
(k+1)
-way partition of
G
would include some

A\subset V
of high conductance,
which would be implied by a lower bound on
\lambda_{k+1}(\mathcal{L}_{G})
due to (
1
).
With the well-known eigen-gap heuristic and theoretical analysis on spectral clustering
(
Peng et al., 2017
)
, these two conditions ensure that the
k
optimal clusters in
G
have low conductance in
H
as well.

3.1
The
SZ
Algorithm

We first present the algorithm in
(
Sun & Zanetti, 2019
)
for constructing a cluster-preserving sparsifier; we call it the

SZ

G=(V,E)

p_{u}(v)\triangleq\min\left\{C\cdot\frac{1}{\lambda_{k+1}(\mathcal{L}_{G})}\cdot\frac{\log n}{\mathrm{deg}_{G}(u)},1\right\}

p_{v}(u)\triangleq\min\left\{C\cdot\frac{1}{\lambda_{k+1}(\mathcal{L}_{G})}\cdot\frac{\log n}{\mathrm{deg}_{G}(v)},1\right\},

for every
e=\{u,v\}

C\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
is some constant.

Then, the algorithm samples

with probability

p_{e}\triangleq p_{u}(v)+p_{v}(u)-p_{u}(v)\cdot p_{v}(u)
,
and sets the weight of every sampled

By setting

Sun & Zanetti (2019)

On the other side, while Definition

4

This section presents our main dynamic spectral clustering algorithm, and is organised as follows: In Section
4.1

4.2
Properties of the Contracted Graph

Now we analyse the properties of the contracted graph. Since the amortised time complexity for every edge update (Theorem
3.3
and Lemma
4.2
) remains valid when we consider a sequence of edge updates at every time, without loss of generality let
G_{t^{\prime}}=(V_{t}\cup V_{\mathrm{new}},E_{t}\cup E_{\mathrm{new}})
be the graph after a sequence of edge updates from
G_{t}=(V_{t},E_{t})
with
n_{t}
vertices, and
\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}
be the contracted graph of
G_{t^{\prime}}
constructed by sequentially running
\mathsf{UpdateContractedGraph}
for each

.
We assume that
|E_{\mathrm{new}}|\leqslant\log^{\gamma}(n_{t})
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

We first prove that the clusters returned by spectral clustering on
H_{t}
also have low conductance in
G_{t}
. Notice that, as the underlying graph
G_{t}
could be dense over time, running a clustering algorithm on its sparsifier
H_{t}
with
\widetilde{O}(n_{t})
edges is crucial to achieve the algorithm’s quick update time.

Lemma 4.3
.

It holds with high probability that

\Phi_{H_{t}}(P_{i})=O\left(k^{2}\cdot\rho_{G_{t}}(k)\right)

and

\Phi_{G_{t}}(P_{i})=O\left(k^{2}\cdot\rho_{G_{t}}(k)\right)
for all
P_{i}\in\mathcal{P}
.

Next, we define the event
\mathcal{E}_{1}
that

\Phi_{H_{t}}(P_{i})=O\left(k^{-6}\cdot\log^{-2\gamma}(n_{t})\right)

and

\Phi_{G_{t}}(P_{i})=O\left(k^{-6}\cdot\log^{-2\gamma}(n_{t})\right)

hold for all
P_{i}\in\mathcal{P}
.
By the fact that
\lambda_{k+1}(G_{t})=\Omega(1)
,
\rho_{G_{t}}(k)=O(k^{-8}\cdot\log^{-2\gamma}(n_{t}))
and Lemma
4.3
,
\mathcal{E}_{1}
holds with high probability. We further define the event
\mathcal{E}_{2}
that

(1/2)\cdot\mathrm{deg}_{G_{t}}(u)\leqslant\mathrm{deg}_{H_{t}}(u)\leqslant(3/2)\cdot\mathrm{deg}_{G_{t}}(u)

hold for all
u\in V_{t}
,
and know from the proof of Theorem
3.3
that
\mathcal{E}_{2}
holds with high probability. In the following we assume that both of
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
happen.

Next, we study the relationship between the cluster-structure in
G_{t^{\prime}}
and the one in
\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}
.
Recall that the number of vertices in
\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}
is much smaller than the one in
G_{t^{\prime}}
. We first prove that there are
\ell
disjoint vertex sets of low conductance in

if and only if there are
\ell
such vertex sets in
\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

The following statements hold:

•

If
\rho_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(\ell)\leqslant\log^{-\alpha}(n_{t^{\prime}})
holds for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha>0
, then
\rho_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\ell)=\max\left\{O\left(\log^{-0.9\alpha}(n_{t^{\prime}})\right),O\left(k^{-6}\cdot\log^{-\gamma}(n_{t^{\prime}})\right)\right\}
.

•

If
\rho_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\ell)\leqslant\log^{-\delta}(n_{t^{\prime}})
holds for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta>0
, then
\rho_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(\ell)=\max\left\{O\left(\log^{-\delta}(n_{t^{\prime}})\right),O\left(k^{-6}\cdot\log^{-\gamma}(n_{t^{\prime}})\right)\right\}
.

Secondly, we show that there is a close connection between
\lambda_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{L}_{G_{t^{\prime}}})
and
\lambda_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}})
for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

The following statements hold:

•

If
\lambda_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}})=\Omega(1)
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\lambda_{\ell+1}\left(\mathcal{L}_{G_{t^{\prime}}}\right)=\Omega\left(\log^{-\alpha}(n_{t^{\prime}})/{\ell^{6}}\right)
for constant
\alpha>0
.

•

If

\lambda_{\ell+1}\left(\mathcal{L}_{G_{t^{\prime}}}\right)=\Omega(1)

, then
\lambda_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}})=\Omega(1)

Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
imply that the cluster-structures in

and
\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}

Update Stage.

We start by calculating the first moment of

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{C\cdot\log{n_{t+1}}},

where the second inequality follows by the min-max theorem of eigenvalues, and (

) holds by (

It only remains to show that

\frac{x^{\intercal}\mathcal{L}_{H_{t+1}}x}{x^{\intercal}x}=\frac{y^{\intercal}\mathcal{L}_{H_{t+1}}^{{}^{\prime}}y}{x^{\intercal}x}=\Omega\left(\frac{y^{\intercal}\mathcal{L}_{H_{t+1}}^{{}^{\prime}}y}{y^{\intercal}y}\right),

where the final guarantee follows from the fact that the degrees in
H_{t+1}
are preserved up to a constant factor.
The conclusion of the theorem follows from the Courant-Fischer Theorem.

Finally, it remains to analyse the amortised update time of the algorithm. Notice that, if one only needs to sample the incoming edge at time
t+1
, then the update time is
O(1)
. Otherwise, all the edges adjacent to some vertex

vertex additions, and in the worst case we only have to resample all the edges in present in
G_{t}
every

edge updates, which again leads to the amortised update time of

∎

\displaystyle<\frac{\log^{\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})}{4\cdot c_{1}}\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(A^{(t)})+2\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(A^{(t)}),

where the first inequality holds by statement (C1). Hence, we reach a contradiction with (
19

Now we are ready to prove statement (C2). We have that

\displaystyle\Phi_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\widetilde{A})

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\widetilde{A},\widetilde{V}_{t^{\prime}}\setminus\widetilde{A})}{\mathrm{vol}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\widetilde{A})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{w_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(A,V_{t}\setminus A)+w_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(A\setminus\widetilde{A},\widetilde{A})}{\mathrm{vol}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\widetilde{A})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\log^{-\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(A)+\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(A\setminus\widetilde{A})}{\mathrm{vol}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\widetilde{A})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(A)}{\log^{\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\widetilde{A})}+\frac{8\cdot c_{1}\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(A^{(t)})}{\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(A^{(t)})\cdot\log^{\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})}

(20)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(A^{(t)})+\mathrm{vol}_{E_{\mathrm{new}}}(A)}{\log^{\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\widetilde{A})}+\frac{8\cdot c_{1}}{\log^{\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{3\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(A^{(t)})+\mathrm{vol}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\widetilde{A})}{\log^{\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\widetilde{A})}+\frac{8\cdot c_{1}}{\log^{\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})}

(21)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{3\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(A^{(t)})\cdot c_{1}\cdot 4}{\log^{2\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(A^{(t)})}+\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(A)}{\log^{\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\widetilde{A})}+\frac{8\cdot c_{1}}{\log^{\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})}

(22)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{12\cdot c_{1}}{\log^{2\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})}+\frac{1}{\log^{\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})}+\frac{8\cdot c_{1}}{\log^{\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1+20\cdot c_{1}}{\log^{\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})}\leqslant\frac{21\cdot c_{1}}{\log^{\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})},

where (

by statement (C1).
(
21

, and
(

B.3.2
.

(23)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Phi_{\widetilde{G}_{t}}(p_{i})\cdot\min\{\mathrm{vol}_{\widetilde{G}_{t}}(p_{i}),\mathrm{vol}_{\widetilde{G}_{t}}(\widetilde{V}\setminus p_{i})\}+\log^{\gamma}(n_{t})+2\cdot\log^{\gamma}(n_{t})

27

Before analysing the spectral gap in the contracted graph
\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}
with respect to the spectral gap in the full graph
G_{t^{\prime}}
, we show that for any small subset of vertices
A\subset V
with a low value of

, the conductance of its corresponding set in the contracted graph
\Phi_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\widetilde{A})
is low as well.

Lemma B.5
.

Let
C\subset V_{t^{\prime}}
be a subset of vertices such that
\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(C)\leqslant k^{6}\cdot\log^{2\gamma}(n_{t})
and
\Phi_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(C)\leqslant\log^{-\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})
for some constant
\varepsilon>0
. Then, it holds that

\Phi_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\widetilde{C})=O(\log^{-0.9\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})).

Proof.

We prove this by contradiction. Assume by contradiction that

\Phi_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\widetilde{C})>\frac{21}{4}\cdot\log^{0.1\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})\cdot\log^{-\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})=\frac{21}{4}\cdot\log^{-0.9\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}}).

Setting
c_{1}\triangleq(1/4)\cdot\log^{0.1\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})
, it holds by Corollary
B.4
that

\Phi_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)})<4\cdot\log^{-0.1\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}}).

(30)

We will show that
C^{(t)}
can be used to create a
(k+1)
-partition in
G_{t}
with low outer conductance, contradicting with the fact that
\lambda_{k+1}(G_{t})=\Omega(1)
.

Let
S_{1},\ldots S_{k}
be the optimal clusters in
G_{t}
corresponding to
\rho_{G_{t}}(k)
. Given that
G_{t}
is a connected graph and
\rho_{G_{t}}(k)=O\left(k^{-8}\cdot\log^{-2\gamma}(n_{t})\right)
, it holds that
\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(S_{i})=\Omega\left(k^{8}\cdot\log^{2\gamma}(n_{t})\right)
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
. We then create the following
(k+1)
-partition:

\mathcal{A}\triangleq C^{(t)}\cup\left\{S_{1}\setminus C^{(t)},\ldots,S_{k}\setminus C^{(t)}\right\},

which is a valid partition as we know that
\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)})\leqslant\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(C)\leqslant k^{6}\cdot\log^{2\gamma}(n_{t})
by the conditions of the lemma.
Now we will compute the conductance of each cluster in
\mathcal{A}
.

First of all, we have from (
30
) that

\Phi_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)})<4\cdot\log^{-0.1\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}}).

(31)

Second, for any cluster
S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)}
we have that

\Phi_{G_{t}}(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)})=\frac{w_{G_{t}}(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)},V_{t}\setminus(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)}))}{\min\{\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)}),\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(V_{t}\setminus(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)}))\}}.

Our proof is by the following case distinction:

Case 1:
\min\{\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)}),\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(V_{t}\setminus(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)}))\}=\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(V_{t}\setminus(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)}))
.

\displaystyle\Phi_{G_{t}}(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)})

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{G_{t}}(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)},V_{t}\setminus(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)}))}{\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(V_{t}\setminus(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)}))}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{w_{G_{t}}(S_{j},V_{t}\setminus S_{j})+w_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)},V_{t}\setminus C^{(t)})}{\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(V_{t}\setminus S_{j})+\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)}\cap S_{j})}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\cdot\max\left\{\frac{w_{G_{t}}(S_{j},V_{t}\setminus S_{j})}{\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(V_{t}\setminus S_{j})},\frac{w_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)},V_{t}\setminus C^{(t)})}{\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(V_{t}\setminus S_{j})}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\cdot\max\left\{\Phi_{G_{t}}(S_{j}),\Phi_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)})\right\}

(32)

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\{O\left(k^{-8}\cdot\log^{-2\gamma}(n_{t})\right),4\cdot\log^{-0.1\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})\},

where for (
32
) it holds that
\min\{\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(S_{j}),\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(V_{t}\setminus S_{j})\}=\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(V_{t}\setminus S_{j})
because we know that
\mathrm{vol}(G_{t})/2\geqslant\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(V_{t}\setminus(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)}))\geqslant\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(V_{t}\setminus S_{j})
, and we also know that
\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(V_{t}\setminus S_{j})\geqslant\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)})
.

Case 2:
\min\{\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)}),\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(V_{t}\setminus(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)}))\}=\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)})
.

\displaystyle\Phi_{G_{t}}(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)})

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{G_{t}}(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)},V_{t}\setminus(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)}))}{\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{w_{G_{t}}(S_{j},V_{t}\setminus S_{j})+w_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)},V_{t}\setminus C^{(t)})}{\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(S_{j})-\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{w_{G_{t}}(S_{j},V_{t}\setminus S_{j})+w_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)},V_{t}\setminus C^{(t)})}{\Omega\left(\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(S_{j})\right)}

(33)

\displaystyle=O\left(\Phi_{G_{t}}(S_{j})\right)+O\left(\Phi_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)})\right)

(34)

\displaystyle=2\cdot\max\left\{O\left(k^{-8}\cdot\log^{-2\gamma}(n_{t})\right),O\left(\log^{-0.1\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})\right)\right\}

(35)

where (
33
) holds because
\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(S_{i})=\Omega\left(k^{8}\cdot\log^{2\gamma}(n_{t})\right)
and
\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)})=O\left(k^{6}\cdot\log^{2\gamma}(n_{t})\right)
,
(
34
) holds because
w_{G_{t}}(S_{j},V_{t}\setminus S_{j})\leqslant\Phi_{G_{t}}(S_{j})\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(S_{j})
and
w_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)},V_{t}\setminus C^{(t)})\leqslant\Phi_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)})\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t}}(C^{(t)})
.

Combining both cases, we have for every
1\leqslant j\leqslant k
that

\Phi_{G_{t}}(S_{j}\setminus C^{(t)})=2\cdot\max\left\{O\left(k^{-8}\cdot\log^{-2\gamma}(n_{t})\right),O\left(\log^{-0.1\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})\right)\right\}.

(36)

Therefore, by combining (
31
) and (
36
), we have shown that

\rho_{G_{t}}(k+1)\leqslant\max_{A_{j}\in\mathcal{A}}\Phi_{G_{t}}(A_{j})=2\cdot\max\left\{O\left(k^{-8}\cdot\log^{-2\gamma}(n_{t})\right),O\left(\log^{-0.1\varepsilon}(n_{t^{\prime}})\right)\right\},

which contradicts the fact that
\rho_{G_{t}}(k+1)\geqslant\frac{\lambda_{k+1}(\mathcal{L}_{G_{t}})}{2}=\Omega(1)
. Hence, the statement of the lemma follows.
∎

A_{1},\ldots A_{\ell}
be the partition such that
\Phi_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(A_{i})=O\left(\log^{-\delta}(n_{t^{\prime}})\right)
for every
1\leqslant i\leqslant\ell
. Recall that
\hat{A_{i}}
is the representation of the set
A_{i}
in the full graph
G_{t^{\prime}}
, i.e.,

\hat{A_{i}}\triangleq A_{i}^{\mathrm{nc}}\bigcup\left(\bigcup_{p_{j}\in A_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}}P^{(t^{\prime})}_{j}\right),

where
P^{(t^{\prime})}_{j}=P_{j}\setminus(P_{j}\cap\widetilde{V}^{\mathrm{nc}}_{t^{\prime}})
,
A_{i}^{\mathrm{nc}}\triangleq A_{i}\cap V^{\mathrm{nc}}_{t^{\prime}}
, and
A_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}\triangleq A_{i}\cap V^{\mathrm{c}}_{t^{\prime}}
. One can see
P^{(t^{\prime})}_{j}
as the vertices in
P_{j}
that have not been pulled out into the contracted graph yet.

Notice that, when
A_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}=\emptyset
, it holds by construction that
\Phi_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(A_{i})=\Phi_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(A_{i})\leqslant\log^{-\delta}(n_{t^{\prime}})
.
So we only look at the case where
A^{\mathrm{c}}_{i}\neq\emptyset
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
A^{c}_{i}
does not contain all the contracted nodes
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
. If it did, then

\Phi_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(\hat{A}_{i})=\Phi_{G_{t^{\prime}}}\left(\bigcup_{A_{j}^{c}=\emptyset}A_{j}\right)\leqslant\log^{-\delta}(n_{t^{\prime}}).

Therefore, given that for any
1\leqslant i\leqslant\ell
it holds that
\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(A_{i}^{\mathrm{nc}})\leqslant 2\cdot|E_{\mathrm{new}}|\leqslant 2\cdot\log^{\gamma}(n_{t})
, and for any
1\leqslant j\leqslant k
it holds that
\mathrm{vol}_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(P_{i}^{(t^{\prime})})=\Omega(k^{6}\cdot\log^{2\gamma}(n_{t}))
, we get that

\Phi_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(\hat{A}_{i})=O\left(\Phi_{G_{t^{\prime}}}\left(\bigcup_{p_{j}\in A_{i}^{\mathrm{c}}}P^{(t^{\prime})}_{j}\right)\right)=O\left(k^{-6}\cdot\log^{-\gamma}(n_{t^{\prime}})\right),

where the last line holds because of property (C3) of Lemma
B.3
. This proves the second statement.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

We first prove the first statement, and we will prove this by contradiction. Assume by contradiction that
\lambda_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{L}_{G_{t^{\prime}}})<C\cdot\frac{\log^{-\alpha}(n_{t^{\prime}})}{(\ell+1)^{6}}
for some constant
C
. Then, by the higher-order Cheeger inequality, there exists an optimal
(\ell+1)
-way partition
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},\ldots S_{\ell+1}
} such that for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant\ell+1

\Phi_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(S_{i})\leqslant\rho_{G_{t^{\prime}}}(\ell+1)\leqslant C_{\ref{lem:Higher Cheeger}}\cdot(\ell+1)^{3}\cdot\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell+1}(G_{t^{\prime}})}=O\left(\log^{-0.5\alpha}(n_{t^{\prime}})\right).

By Lemma
4.4
, it then holds that
\rho_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\ell+1)=\max\left\{O\left(\log^{-0.45\alpha}(n_{t^{\prime}})\right),O\left(k^{-6}\cdot\log^{-\gamma}(n_{t^{\prime}})\right)\right\}
, which contradicts the fact that

\rho_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}(\ell+1)\geqslant\frac{\lambda_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}})}{2}=\Omega(1),

from which the first statement of the lemma follows.

Next we prove the second statement.
We prove this by analysing the spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}
with respect to
\mathcal{L}_{G_{t^{\prime}}}
through
\mathcal{L}_{H^{\prime}_{t^{\prime}}}
. As proven in Lemma
B.2
,
H^{\prime}_{t^{\prime}}
is a cluster preserving sparsifier of
G_{t^{\prime}}
, and therefore we know that

\lambda_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{L}_{H^{\prime}_{t^{\prime}}})=\Omega(\lambda_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{L}_{G_{t^{\prime}}})).

(43)

Our next analysis is inspired by the work on meta graphs of Macgregor and Sun
(
Macgregor & Sun, 2022
)
. We will analyse the spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{H^{\prime}_{t^{\prime}}}
with respect to the spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}}
, and for simplicity we denote
H^{\prime}_{t^{\prime}}\triangleq H
,
\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}\triangleq\widetilde{G}
, and
n_{t^{\prime}}\triangleq n
.
For every vertex
u_{j}\in V(\widetilde{G})
in the contracted graph, we associate it with a non-empty group of vertices
A_{j}\subset V(H)
as follows: for all
u_{j}\in\widetilde{V}_{t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{nc}}
, we associate
u_{j}
with its unique corresponding single vertex
v\in V(H)
, and for every
u_{j}=p_{r}\in\widetilde{V}_{t^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{c}}
for some
r
, we associate it with its corresponding vertices in the cluster
P^{(t^{\prime})}_{r}\subset V(H)
. Then, let
\chi_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the indicator vector for the vertices
A_{j}\subset V(H)
corresponding to the vertex
u_{j}\in V(\widetilde{G})
.

We define
\widetilde{n}=|V(\widetilde{G})|
, and let the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}}
be
\gamma_{1}\leqslant\gamma_{2}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant\gamma_{\widetilde{n}}
with corresponding eigenvectors
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{\widetilde{n}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\widetilde{n}}
. We further define vectors
\bar{g}_{1},\ldots\bar{g}_{\widetilde{n}}
which will represent the eigenvectors
g_{1},\ldots g_{\widetilde{n}}
of the normalised Laplacian
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}}
, but blown up to size
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Formally, we define

\bar{g}_{i}\triangleq\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{n}}\frac{D_{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{j}}{\|D_{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{j}\|}g_{i}(j).

We can readily check that these vectors form an orthonormal basis. First,

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{i}\bar{g}_{i}^{\intercal}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{n}}\sum_{u\in A_{j}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{d_{H}(u)}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}_{H}(A_{j})}}g_{i}(j)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{n}}g_{i}(j)^{2}\sum_{u\in A_{j}}\frac{d_{H}(u)}{\mathrm{vol}_{H}(A_{j})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{n}}g(j)^{2}=1.

And similarly for any
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
,

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{i_{1}}\bar{g}_{i_{2}}^{\intercal}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{n}}\sum_{u\in A_{j}}\frac{d_{H}(u)}{\mathrm{vol}_{H}(A_{j})}g_{i_{1}}(j)g_{i_{2}}(j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{n}}g_{i_{1}}(j)g_{i_{2}}(j)=0.

We also get the useful property that for the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
of
\mathcal{L}_{H}
and
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{\widetilde{n}}
of the contracted Laplacian
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}}
, it holds that
\lambda_{i}\leqslant 2\cdot\nu_{i}
. In particular,

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{i}\mathcal{L}_{H}\bar{g}_{i}^{\intercal}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{\widetilde{n}}\sum_{y=x}^{\widetilde{n}}\sum_{u\in A_{x}}\sum_{v\in A_{y}}w_{H}(u,v)\left(\frac{\bar{g}_{i}(u)}{\sqrt{d_{H}(u)}}-\frac{\bar{g}_{i}(v)}{\sqrt{d_{H}(v)}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{\widetilde{n}}\sum_{y=x}^{\widetilde{n}}w_{H}(A_{x},A_{y})\left(\frac{g_{i}(x)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}_{H}(A_{x}})}-\frac{g_{i}(y)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}_{H}(A_{y}})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=2\cdot g_{i}\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}}g_{i}^{\intercal}.

Therefore we have an
i
-dimensional subspace
X_{i}
such that

\max_{x\in X_{i}}\frac{x^{\intercal}\mathcal{L}_{H}x}{x^{\intercal}x}=2\cdot\gamma_{i},

from which it follows by the Courant-Fischer theorem that
\lambda_{i}\leqslant 2\cdot\gamma_{i}
. Combining this with (
43
), we get that

\lambda_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{G}_{t^{\prime}}})\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\cdot\lambda_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{L}_{H^{\prime}_{t^{\prime}}})=\Omega\left(\lambda_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{L}_{G_{t^{\prime}}})\right)=\Omega(1),

which proves the lemma.
∎

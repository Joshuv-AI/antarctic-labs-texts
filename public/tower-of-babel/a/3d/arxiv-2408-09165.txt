Boundedness of fractional integrals and fractional derivatives on Laguerre Lipschitz spaces
He Wang Jizheng Huang Yu Liu

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we study the boundedness of a class of fractional integrals and derivatives associated with
Laguerre polynomial expansions on Laguerre Lipschitz spaces.
The consideration of such operators is motivated by the study of corresponding results on Gaussian Lipschitz spaces.
The key idea used here is to develop the Poisson integral theory in the Laguerre setting.

Keywords:
fractional integration, fractional
differentiation, Lipschitz spaces, Laguerre measure.

AMS Mathematics Subject Classification:
26A16, 46E35, 33C45.

1
Introduction

As we know, Lipschitz space is an class of important function spaces which have been used in the fields of harmonic analysis and PDEs and
it has been
intensively studied. As its generalization,
the Gaussian Lipschitz space was defined by Gatto and Urbina
[
6
]
in
terms of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Poisson kernel. In 2016, Liu and
Sjögren gave a characterization of this space via a combination of
ordinary Lipschitz continuity conditions (see
[
9
]
). In a
subsequent paper
[
10
]
, they similarly investigated a Lipschitz
space in the same setting and as applications, they also
characterized that spaces by means of a Lipschitz-type continuity
condition. Furthermore, the Lipschitz space associated with other
operators has also been investigated by some scholars, see

[
7
,
16
]
and the references therein. Our investigation is
devoted to Laguerre Lipschitz spaces and their associated issues
that expand beyond those of Gaussian Lipschitz spaces (see

[
6
]
). In order to provide a foundation for our main
findings, we introduce some fundamental concepts for the Laguerre
operator (see
[
4
]
).

Consider the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}=(0,\infty)^{d}
endowed with the Laguerre measure
\mu_{\alpha}
, which is defined as

\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{{x_{i}}^{\alpha_{i}}e^{-x_{i}}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)}\mathrm{d}x

for a multiindex
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})
.

The Laguerre differential operator is defined by

\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigg[x_{i}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+(\alpha_{i}+1-x_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\bigg],

which is positive and symmetric in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
has a closure which
is self-adjoint in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha})

and it also will be denoted by
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
.

Given
\alpha>-1
, the one-dimensional Laguerre polynomials of type

\alpha
are denoted by

L_{k}^{\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{k!}e^{x}x^{-\alpha}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{k}}{\mathrm{d}x^{k}}(e^{-x}x^{k+\alpha}),\ k\in\mathbb{N},\ x>0.

Note that each
L_{k}^{\alpha}
is a polynomial of degree
k
. Given a multiindex

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})
with
\alpha\in(-1,\infty)^{d}
, the
d
-dimensional Laguerre
polynomials of type
\alpha
are tensor products of the one-dimensional Laguerre
polynomials, that is,

L_{k}^{\alpha}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}L_{k_{i}}^{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i}),\ k\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},

then it is well known that the Laguerre polynomials are eigenfunctions of

\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
, that is,

\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}L_{k}^{\alpha}(x)=|k|L_{k}^{\alpha}(x).

(1)

By the orthogonality of the Laguerre polynomials with respect to
\mu_{\alpha}
, it is easy to see that the system
\{L_{k}^{\alpha}:k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\}
constitutes
an orthogonal basis in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha})
. Thus we have an
orthogonal decomposition:

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha})=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{n},

where
\mathcal{H}_{n}=\mathrm{lin}\{L_{k}^{\alpha}:|k|=n\}
.

The heat semigroup generated by
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
and defined in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha})
is called the Laguerre semigroup

T_{t}^{\alpha}=e^{-t\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}},\ t\geq 0,

which can be defined as

T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}G_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)f(y)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha}(y),\ f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha}).

(2)

Via the Hille-Hardy formula
[
14
, Chapter 1, Section 1.11]
, we
know that the kernel
G_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)
may be computed explicitly,
that is,

G_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{j}+1)}{1-e^{-t}}e^{-\frac{e^{-t}}{1-e^{-t}}(x_{j}+y_{j})}\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}^{-\alpha_{j}}I_{\alpha_{j}}\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}}{1-e^{-t}}\bigg),

(3)

where
I_{\nu}
denotes the modified Bessel function of the first kind and order
\nu
(see
[
8
]
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce
the Poisson-Laguerre semigroup, which is the semigroup subordinated to the
Laguerre semigroup. By obtaining some properties of the modified Bessel function
(
4
) and (
5
), we deduce the representation of
T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)
:

T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)\approx\frac{2^{|\alpha|}}{(1-e^{-t})^{d+|\alpha|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}{y_{j}}^{\alpha_{j}}e^{-\frac{e^{-t}x_{j}+y_{j}}{1-e^{-t}}}f(y)\mathrm{d}y,\quad\mathrm{for}\ \frac{\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}}{1-e^{-t}}\leq 1,\ j=1,2,\ldots,d,

and

\begin{split}T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)&=\frac{e^{(|\alpha|+\frac{d}{2})\frac{t}{2}}}{(2\sqrt{\pi})^{d}(1-e^{-t})^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{y_{j}^{\frac{\alpha_{j}}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}}{x_{j}^{\frac{\alpha_{j}}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}}e^{-\frac{(\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}}-\sqrt{y_{j}})^{2}}{1-e^{-t}}}\bigg[1-\frac{[\alpha_{j},1]}{2}\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{e^{-t}xy}}{1-e^{-t}}\bigg)^{-1}+\frac{[\alpha_{j},2]}{4}\\
&\quad\times\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{e^{-t}xy}}{1-e^{-t}}\bigg)^{-2}+O\bigg(\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{e^{-t}xy}}{1-e^{-t}}\bigg)^{-3}\bigg)\bigg]f(y)\mathrm{d}y,\quad\mathrm{for}\ \frac{\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}}{1-e^{-t}}>1,\ j=1,2,\ldots,d,\end{split}

and
\mathrm{for}\ \frac{\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}}{1-e^{-t}}\leq 1\ (j=1,2,\ldots,k),\frac{\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}}{1-e^{-t}}>1\ (j=k+1,k+2,\ldots,d),\mathrm{where}\ k\in\mathbb{N},0\leq k\leq d
,

\begin{split}T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)&=\frac{2^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}}e^{(\alpha_{k+1}+\alpha_{k+2}+\cdots+\alpha_{d}+\frac{d-k}{2})\frac{t}{2}}}{(2\sqrt{\pi})^{d-k}(1-e^{-t})^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}+\frac{k+d}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}{y_{j}}^{\alpha_{j}}e^{-\frac{e^{-t}x_{j}+y_{j}}{1-e^{-t}}}\prod_{j=k+1}^{d}\frac{y_{j}^{\frac{\alpha_{j}}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}}{x_{j}^{\frac{\alpha_{j}}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}}e^{-\frac{(\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}}-\sqrt{y_{j}})^{2}}{1-e^{-t}}}\\
&\quad\times\bigg[1-\frac{[\alpha_{j},1]}{2}\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{e^{-t}xy}}{1-e^{-t}}\bigg)^{-1}+\frac{[\alpha_{j},2]}{4}\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{e^{-t}xy}}{1-e^{-t}}\bigg)^{-2}+O\bigg(\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{e^{-t}xy}}{1-e^{-t}}\bigg)^{-3}\bigg)\bigg]f(y)\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

Using the relation between the Poisson-Laguerre semigroup and the
Laguerre semigroup in (
6
), we obtain the kernel estimation of the Poisson-Laguerre semigroup. In Lemma

,
we derive the

with

In Section

, we obtain the equivalence between conditions (

) and (

.
In Definition

, we introduce Laguerre Lipschitz spaces denoted by

and prove

2
Some results for Laguerre semigroups

Firstly, we start by recalling some basic notations and facts from the Laguerre semigroup theory. For more details, see
[
1
,
13
]
and the references therein.

We are going to quote two properties of the modified Bessel function
I_{\nu}
that we will need in the subsequent proofs (see
[
8
, Chap. 5]
),

I_{\nu}(z)\approx z^{\nu}\ \mathrm{as}\ z\to 0^{+},

(4)

for
n=0,1,2,\ldots
, and

\sqrt{z}I_{\nu}(z)=\frac{e^{z}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\bigg(\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-1)^{r}[\nu,r](2z)^{-r}+O(z^{-n-1})\bigg),

(5)

where
[\nu,0]=1
and
[\nu,r]=\frac{(4\nu^{2}-1)(4\nu^{2}-3^{2})\cdots(4\nu^{2}-(2r-1)^{2})}{2^{2r}\Gamma(r+1)}
,
r=1,2,\ldots
.

It follows from
[
4
]
that
\{T_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a symmetric diffusion semigroup. In particular
T_{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}
and
T_{0}^{\alpha}f(x)=f(x)
a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
.
The Poisson-Laguerre semigroup
P_{t}^{\alpha}=e^{-t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}}}
,
t\geq 0
, can be defined from
\{T_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t\geq 0}
by subordination as

P_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}}{\sqrt{u}}T_{\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}^{\alpha}f(x)\mathrm{d}u.

(6)

Furthermore, by a change of variables, we obtain the subsequent representation

P_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}T_{s}^{\alpha}f(x)\mu_{t}^{(\frac{1}{2})}\mathrm{d}s,

(7)

where the one-side stable measure on
(0,\infty)
of order
\frac{1}{2}
is defined by

\mu_{t}^{(\frac{1}{2})}\mathrm{d}s:=\frac{t}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4s}}}{s^{\frac{3}{2}}}\mathrm{d}s=g(t,s)\mathrm{d}s.

Using (
2
) and the expression of the Laguerre heat kernel (
3
),
we obtain

\begin{split}T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{j}+1)}{1-e^{-t}}e^{-\frac{e^{-t}}{1-e^{-t}}(x_{j}+y_{j})}\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}^{-\alpha_{j}}I_{\alpha_{j}}\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}}{1-e^{-t}}\bigg)f(y)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha}(y)\\
&=\frac{1}{(1-e^{-t})^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}e^{-\frac{e^{-t}}{1-e^{-t}}(x_{j}+y_{j})-y_{j}}\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}^{-\alpha_{j}}{y_{j}}^{\alpha_{j}}I_{\alpha_{j}}\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}}{1-e^{-t}}\bigg)f(y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\frac{1}{(1-e^{-t})^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\bigg(\frac{y_{j}}{x_{j}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha_{j}}{2}}e^{-\frac{e^{-t}x_{j}+y_{j}}{1-e^{-t}}+\frac{\alpha_{j}t}{2}}I_{\alpha_{j}}\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}}{1-e^{-t}}\bigg)f(y)\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

(8)

From (
6
), (
8
) and after the change of the variable

r=e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}
and exchanging the order of integration, we obtain

\begin{split}P_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}}{\sqrt{u}(1-e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}})^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\bigg(\frac{y_{j}}{x_{j}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha_{j}}{2}}e^{-\frac{e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}x_{j}+y_{j}}{1-e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}}+\frac{\alpha_{j}t^{2}}{8u}}I_{\alpha_{j}}\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}x_{j}y_{j}}}{1-e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}}\bigg)f(y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\\
&=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{te^{\frac{t^{2}}{4\log r}}}{(1-r)^{d}(-\log r)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\bigg(\frac{y_{j}}{x_{j}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha_{j}}{2}}e^{-\frac{rx_{j}+y_{j}}{1-r}-\frac{\alpha_{j}\log r}{2}}I_{\alpha_{j}}\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{rx_{j}y_{j}}}{1-r}\bigg)\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}f(y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)f(y)\mathrm{d}y,\end{split}

where

p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y):=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{te^{\frac{t^{2}}{4\log r}}}{(1-r)^{d}(-\log r)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\bigg(\frac{y_{j}}{x_{j}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha_{j}}{2}}e^{-\frac{rx_{j}+y_{j}}{1-r}-\frac{\alpha_{j}\log r}{2}}I_{\alpha_{j}}\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{rx_{j}y_{j}}}{1-r}\bigg)\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}.

(9)

The family
\{P_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t\geq 0}
is also a symmetric diffusion semigroup equipped with the infinitesimal generator
-(-\mathcal{L}^{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. In particular,

P_{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}
due to (
7
) and
T_{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}
.

By (
1
), we obtain

T_{t}^{\alpha}L_{k}^{\alpha}=e^{-t|k|}L_{k}^{\alpha}

and

P_{t}^{\alpha}L_{k}^{\alpha}=e^{-t\sqrt{|k|}}L_{k}^{\alpha},

which imply that
L_{k}^{\alpha}
are eigenfunctions of
\{T_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{P_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t\geq 0}
, respectively.
Since the maximal operator
(T^{\alpha})^{*}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}|T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)|
satisfies the weak type
(1,1)
inequality with respect to the measure

\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha}
(see
[
2
]
), therefore,

T_{0}^{\alpha}f(x)=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)=f(x)\ \mathrm{a.e.}\ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}

and

T_{\infty}^{\alpha}f(x)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}f(y)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha}(y)\ \mathrm{a.e.}\ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}

hold for all
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha})
, and hence hold for all

f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha})
with
1\leq p\leq\infty
due
to
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha})\subset L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha})
for

p\leq q
. Please refer to
[
15
]
for more results about Laguerre expansions.

Secondly, we will need the following lemma for the
L^{1}
-norm
of the derivatives of the kernel
p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)
is the Poisson-Laguerre kernel.

(i)
If
\frac{\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}}{1-e^{-t}}\leq 1
,
j=1,2,\ldots,d
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\bigg|\frac{\partial p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)}{\partial t}\bigg|\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{C}{t},

(10)

where
C
is a constant independent of
x
and
t
.

(ii)
If
\frac{\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}}{1-e^{-t}}>1
,
j=1,2,\ldots,d
, and
\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},\infty)^{d}
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\bigg|\frac{\partial p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)}{\partial t}\bigg|\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{C}{t},

where
C
is a constant independent of
x
and
t
.

(iii)
If
\frac{\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}}{1-e^{-t}}\leq 1
,
j=1,2,\ldots,k
,
and
\frac{\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}}{1-e^{-t}}>1
with
\alpha_{j}>-\frac{1}{2}
for
j=k+1,k+2,\ldots,d
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq k\leq d
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\bigg|\frac{\partial p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)}{\partial t}\bigg|\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{C}{t}.

Additionally, for any positive integer
k
and
\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},\infty)^{d}
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\bigg|\frac{\partial^{k}p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)}{\partial t^{k}}\bigg|\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{C}{t^{k}}.

(11)

Proof.

(i) We first prove (
10
). By the expression of the Poisson-Laguerre kernel (
9
) we have

\frac{\partial p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1+\frac{t^{2}}{2\log r})e^{\frac{t^{2}}{4\log r}}}{(1-r)^{d}(-\log r)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\bigg(\frac{y_{j}}{x_{j}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha_{j}}{2}}e^{-\frac{rx_{j}+y_{j}}{1-r}-\frac{\alpha_{j}\log r}{2}}I_{\alpha_{j}}\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{rx_{j}y_{j}}}{1-r}\bigg)\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}.

(12)

From (
4
) and by exchanging the order of integration, in case

\frac{\sqrt{e^{-t}x_{j}y_{j}}}{1-e^{-t}}\leq 1
,
j=1,2,\ldots,d
, we
obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\bigg|\frac{\partial p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)}{\partial t}\bigg|\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|1+\frac{t^{2}}{2\log r}|e^{\frac{t^{2}}{4\log r}}}{(1-r)^{d}(-\log r)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\bigg(\frac{y_{j}}{x_{j}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha_{j}}{2}}e^{-\frac{rx_{j}+y_{j}}{1-r}-\frac{\alpha_{j}\log r}{2}}\bigg|I_{\alpha_{j}}\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{rx_{j}y_{j}}}{1-r}\bigg)\bigg|\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|1+\frac{t^{2}}{2\log r}|e^{\frac{t^{2}}{4\log r}}}{(1-r)^{d}(-\log r)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\bigg(\frac{y_{j}}{x_{j}}\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha_{j}}{2}}e^{-\frac{rx_{j}+y_{j}}{1-r}-\frac{\alpha_{j}\log r}{2}}\bigg(\frac{2\sqrt{rx_{j}y_{j}}}{1-r}\bigg)^{\alpha_{j}}\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{|\alpha|-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{\frac{t^{2}}{4\log r}}}{(-\log r)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\bigg|1+\frac{t^{2}}{2\log r}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{e^{-\frac{rx_{j}+y_{j}}{1-r}}}{(1-r)^{\alpha_{j}+1}}y_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\mathrm{d}y\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{|\alpha|-1}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\Gamma(\alpha_{j}+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{\frac{t^{2}}{4\log r}}}{(-\log r)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\bigg|1+\frac{t^{2}}{2\log r}\bigg|e^{-\frac{r|x|}{1-r}}\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{\frac{t^{2}}{4\log r}}}{(-\log r)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\bigg|1+\frac{t^{2}}{2\log r}\bigg|\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r},

where in the second inequality we have used the fact that

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{e^{-\frac{rx_{j}+y_{j}}{1-r}}}{(1-r)^{\alpha_{j}+1}}y_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\mathrm{d}y=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\Gamma(\alpha_{j}+1)e^{-\frac{rx_{j}}{1-r}}.

(13)

Therefore, to show (
10
) it suffices to prove

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{\frac{t^{2}}{4\log r}}}{(-\log r)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\bigg|1+\frac{t^{2}}{2\log r}\bigg|\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}\leq\frac{C}{t}.

(14)

Taking
s=-\log r
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{\frac{t^{2}}{4\log r}}}{(-\log r)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\bigg|1+\frac{t^{2}}{2\log r}\bigg|\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4s}}}{s^{\frac{3}{2}}}\bigg|1-\frac{t^{2}}{2s}\bigg|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4s}}}{s^{\frac{3}{2}}}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4s}}}{s^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{t^{2}}{2s}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=:I+II.

For
I
, via the change of the variable
v=\frac{t^{2}}{4s}
, we have

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-v}\bigg(\frac{t^{2}}{4v}\bigg)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\frac{t^{2}}{4v^{2}}\mathrm{d}v=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-v}\frac{(4v)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{t^{3}}\frac{t^{2}}{4v^{2}}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-v}v^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}v=\frac{C\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}{t}=\frac{C^{\prime}}{t}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-v}\bigg(\frac{t^{2}}{4v}\bigg)^{-\frac{3}{2}}v\frac{t^{2}}{4v^{2}}\mathrm{d}v=2\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-v}\frac{(4v)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{t^{3}}v\frac{t^{2}}{4v^{2}}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-v}v^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}v=\frac{C\Gamma(\frac{3}{2})}{t}=\frac{C^{\prime}}{t}.

(ii) Similarly, using (
5
) and (
12
), we obtain the following estimate

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)}{\partial t}\\

with

(iii) If

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k+1}p_{u}^{\alpha}(x,y)}{\partial u^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial t^{k}}p_{t+s}^{\alpha}(x,y)

3
Laguerre Lipschitz spaces

It is well known that the Poisson semigroup provides an alternative characterization of
the Lipschitz spaces (see
[
12
]
). Following this approach and using the Poisson-Hermite semigroup,
Gatto and Urbina
[
6
]
introduced the Gaussian Lipschitz space (see also
[
5
]
and
[
11
]
). Similarly, we
can define the Laguerre Lipschitz space as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

For
\beta>0
, let
n
be the smallest integer greater than
\beta
.
A
L^{\infty}
function defined in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
belongs to the Laguerre Lipschitz space

Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
associated with
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}
if there exists a constant
A_{\beta}(f)
such that

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{n}P_{t}^{\alpha}f}{\partial t^{n}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq A_{\beta}(f)t^{-n+\beta}.

(21)

The norm of
f\in Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
is defined as

\|f\|_{Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})}:=\|f\|_{\infty}+A_{\beta}(f),

where
A_{\beta}(f)
is the smallest constant appearing in (
21
).

Remark 3.1
.

We only focus on the condition (
21
) when
t
approaches zero, since

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{n}P_{t}^{\alpha}f}{\partial t^{n}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq At^{-n}

(22)

implies (
21
) when
t
away from zero. For the proof of (
22
), we conclude that for
f\in L^{\infty}
and

\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},\infty)^{d}
,

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{n}P_{t}^{\alpha}f}{\partial t^{n}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\bigg|\frac{\partial^{n}p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)}{\partial t^{n}}\bigg||f(y)|\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{C}{t^{n}}\|f\|_{\infty},

where we have used (
11
) in the last step.

Now, we need to establish the following equivalent relations:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}
,
\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},\infty)^{d}
and
\beta>0
. Then, for any two integers
k
and
l
that both greater than
\beta
,
we conclude that

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{k}P_{t}^{\alpha}f}{\partial t^{k}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq A_{\beta,k}t^{-k+\beta}

(23)

and

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{l}P_{t}^{\alpha}f}{\partial t^{l}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq A_{\beta,l}t^{-l+\beta}

(24)

are equivalent. Furthermore, the smallest constants
A_{\beta,k}
and
A_{\beta,l}
that satisfy the aforementioned inequalities are comparable.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove that for
k>\beta
,

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{k}P_{t}^{\alpha}f}{\partial t^{k}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq A_{\beta,k}t^{-k+\beta}

(25)

and

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{k+1}P_{t}^{\alpha}f}{\partial t^{k+1}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq A_{\beta,k+1}t^{-(k+1)+\beta}

(26)

are equivalent.

We first assume that (
25
) holds. Then by the semigroup property of

\{P_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t\geq 0}
and Lemma
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{k+1}P_{t}^{\alpha}f}{\partial t^{k+1}}\bigg\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\bigg\|\frac{\partial P_{t_{1}}^{\alpha}}{\partial t_{1}}\bigg(\frac{\partial^{k}P_{t_{2}}^{\alpha}f}{\partial t_{2}^{k}}\bigg)\bigg\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{k}P_{t_{2}}^{\alpha}f}{\partial t_{2}^{k}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\bigg|\frac{\partial p_{t_{1}}^{\alpha}(\cdot,y)}{\partial t_{1}}\bigg|\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq A_{\beta,k}t_{2}^{-k+\beta}Ct_{1}^{-1},

where
t=t_{1}+t_{2}
. Taking
t_{1}=t_{2}=t/2
, we conclude that (
26
) is valid.

Next, we assume that (
26
) holds. Applying Lemma
2.1
again, we
derive

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{k}P_{t}^{\alpha}f}{\partial t^{k}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\bigg|\frac{\partial^{k}p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)}{\partial t^{k}}\bigg|\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{C}{t^{k}}\|f\|_{\infty}.

Therefore,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\partial^{k}P_{t}^{\alpha}f}{\partial t^{k}}=0

uniformly, and then

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{k}P_{t}^{\alpha}f}{\partial t^{k}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq\int_{t}^{\infty}\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{k+1}P_{s}^{\alpha}f}{\partial s^{k+1}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s=Ct^{-k+\beta},

which implies that (
25
) holds and the proof of Proposition
3.1
is completed.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Proposition
3.1
implies that the definition of
Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
doesn’t depend on the index
k
for
k>\beta
and the resulting
norms are equivalent provided that
\alpha\in(-1/2,\infty)^{d}
.

At the same time, he Laguerre Lipschitz spaces have the following monotonicity property, which can be similarly proved by adopting the method of Proposition 2.2 in
[
6
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},\infty)^{d}
.
If
0<\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}
, then
Lip_{\beta_{2}}(\mu_{\alpha})\subset Lip_{\beta_{1}}(\mu_{\alpha})
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
f\in Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
and
0<\beta<1
, we have

\|P_{t}^{\alpha}f-f\|_{\infty}\leq A_{\beta}(f)t^{\beta}.

(27)

Proof.

Using the fundamental theorem of Calculus, we get

\displaystyle\|P_{t}^{\alpha}f-f\|_{\infty}=\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial P_{s}^{\alpha}f}{\partial s}\mathrm{d}s\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\bigg\|\frac{\partial P_{s}^{\alpha}f}{\partial s}\bigg\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\leq A_{\beta}(f)\int_{0}^{t}s^{-1+\beta}\mathrm{d}s=A_{\beta}(f)t^{\beta}.

∎

4
Boundedness of fractional integrals and fractional derivatives associated with Laguerre expansions on
Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})

The Bessel Laguerre potential of order
\lambda>0
denoted by
\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
,
associated to the Laguerre measure, is defined formally as

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}=(I+\mathcal{L}^{\alpha})^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}.

By (
1
) we have

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}L_{k}^{\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{(1+|k|)^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}}L_{k}^{\alpha}(x)

for any Laguerre polynomial
L_{k}^{\alpha}(x)
.

Through the principle of linearity, this definition can be broadened to encompass any polynomial, and
P.A. Meyer’s theorem enables us to extend Bessel Laguerre potentials to form a continuous operator
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},d\mu_{\alpha})
for
1<p<\infty
(see
[
4
, Lemma 6.1]
).
Bessel Laguerre potentials can be defined in another form as an alternative
integral representation:

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\lambda)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\lambda-1}e^{-s}P_{s}^{\alpha}f(x)\mathrm{d}s,\ f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha}).

(28)

Please refer to
[
4
]
for more details.

In what follows, we give the action of the Bessel Laguerre potential on Laguerre Lipschitz spaces.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},\infty)^{d}
,
\beta>0
and
\lambda>0
. Then
\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
is bounded from

Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
to
Lip_{\beta+\lambda}(\mu_{\alpha})
.

Proof.

Assume that
f\in Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
. Then for any
n>\beta+\lambda
,
by Proposition
3.1
we have

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{n}P_{t}^{\alpha}f}{\partial t^{n}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq A_{\beta}(f)t^{-n+\beta},\ t>0.

Since
\|P_{s}^{\alpha}f\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
, we have
P_{s}^{\alpha}f\in L^{\infty}
due to
f\in L^{\infty}
. Moreover, by (
28
) we get

P_{t}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f)(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\lambda)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\lambda-1}e^{-s}P_{t+s}^{\alpha}f(x)\mathrm{d}s.

(29)

Therefore

\|P_{t}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f)\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty},

(30)

which implies that
P_{t}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f)\in L^{\infty}
.

By (
29
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{n}P_{t}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f)(x)}{\partial t^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\lambda)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\lambda-1}e^{-s}\frac{\partial^{n}P_{t+s}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial t^{n}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\lambda)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\lambda-1}e^{-s}\frac{\partial^{n}P_{t+s}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial(t+s)^{n}}\mathrm{d}s.

Then

\displaystyle\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{n}P_{t}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f)(x)}{\partial t^{n}}\bigg\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\lambda)}\int_{0}^{t}s^{\lambda-1}e^{-s}\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{n}P_{t+s}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial(t+s)^{n}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\lambda)}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{\lambda-1}e^{-s}\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{n}P_{t+s}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial(t+s)^{n}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=:I+II.

For
I
, we can get

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A_{\beta}(f)}{\Gamma(\lambda)}\int_{0}^{t}s^{\lambda-1}(t+s)^{-n+\beta}e^{-s}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A_{\beta}(f)}{\Gamma(\lambda)}t^{-n+\beta}\int_{0}^{t}s^{\lambda-1}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-n+\beta+\lambda}A_{\beta}(f).

Since
n>\beta+\lambda
, we have

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A_{\beta}(f)}{\Gamma(\lambda)}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{\lambda-1}e^{-s}(t+s)^{-n+\beta}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A_{\beta}(f)}{\Gamma(\lambda)}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{\lambda-1}e^{-s}s^{-n+\beta}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-n+\beta+\lambda-1}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=CA_{\beta}(f)t^{-n+\beta+\lambda}.

Hence,

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{n}P_{t}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f)(x)}{\partial t^{n}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq CA_{\beta}(f)t^{-n+\beta+\lambda}.

Therefore,
\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f\in Lip_{\beta+\lambda}(\mu_{\alpha})
and
via (
30
) we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f\|_{Lip_{\beta+\lambda}(\mu_{\alpha})}\leq C\|f\|_{Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})}.

∎

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha})
and
\lambda>0
, we define the fractional integral of order
\lambda

associated with Laguerre expansions of type
\alpha
by:

I_{\lambda}^{\alpha}=(\mathcal{L}^{\alpha})^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}\Pi_{0},

where

\Pi_{0}f=f-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}f(y)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha}.

This definition is correct for all polynomials due to the fact
that

I_{\lambda}^{\alpha}L_{k}^{\alpha}=|k|^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}L_{k}^{\alpha},\ |k|>0,

for all Laguerre
polynomials.
If
f
is a polynomial with
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}f(y)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha}(y)=0
,
then

I_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\lambda)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\lambda-1}P_{s}^{\alpha}f(x)\mathrm{d}s.

(31)

By P.A. Meyer’s multiplier theorem,
I_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
satisfies a continuous extension to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha})
for
1<p<\infty
, and (
31
)
can be extended for
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{\alpha})
(see
[
4
]
).

The Laguerre fractional derivative
D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
of order
\lambda>0

is defined formally by:

D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}=(\mathcal{L}^{\alpha})^{\frac{\lambda}{2}},

therefore, for all Laguerre polynomials we have

D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}L_{k}^{\alpha}=|k|^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}L_{k}^{\alpha}.

For
0<\lambda<\beta<1
and
f\in Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
, we have an alternative
representation of
D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
as follows:

D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f=\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}(P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)f\mathrm{d}s,

(32)

where
c_{\lambda}=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{-\lambda-1}(e^{-u}-1)\mathrm{d}u<\infty
when
0<\lambda<1
.

Now we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},\infty)^{d}
and
0<\lambda<\beta<1
,
the Laguerre fractional derivative of order
\lambda
, denoted as
D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
,
is bounded from
Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
to
Lip_{\beta-\lambda}(\mu_{\alpha})
.

Proof.

Assume that
f\in Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
, then
f\in L^{\infty}
and
\|\frac{\partial P_{t}^{\alpha}f}{\partial t}\|_{\infty}\leq A_{\beta}(f)t^{-1+\beta}
.
By (
32
) and Proposition
3.3
we have

\displaystyle|D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{-\lambda-1}|P_{s}^{\alpha}f(x)-f(x)|\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}}\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}|P_{s}^{\alpha}f(x)-f(x)|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{-\lambda-1}\|P_{s}^{\alpha}f-f\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s+\frac{2\|f\|_{\infty}}{c_{\lambda}}\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A_{\beta}(f)}{c_{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{\beta-\lambda-1}\mathrm{d}s+\frac{2\|f\|_{\infty}}{c_{\lambda}}\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{\beta}(f)}{c_{\lambda}(\beta-\lambda)}+\frac{2\|f\|_{\infty}}{\lambda c_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta,\lambda}\|f\|_{Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})},

which shows that
D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f\in L^{\infty}
.

To prove the Lipschitz condition, fixing
t

and using again (
32
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(P_{t}^{\alpha}D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}(P_{t+s}^{\alpha}f(x)-P_{t}^{\alpha}f(x))\mathrm{d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}\bigg[\frac{\partial P_{s+t}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial P_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial t}\bigg]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\lambda-1}\bigg[\frac{\partial P_{s+t}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial P_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial t}\bigg]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}\bigg[\frac{\partial P_{s+t}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial P_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial t}\bigg]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=:I+II.

We use Proposition
3.1
to get

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{2}P_{r}^{\alpha}f}{\partial r^{2}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq Ar^{\beta-2},

and by the fundamental theorem of calculus, we write

\begin{split}\bigg|\frac{\partial P_{t+s}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial P_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial t}\bigg|\leq\int_{t}^{t+s}\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{2}P_{r}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial r^{2}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}r\leq At^{\beta-2}s\end{split}

(33)

for
s>0
.

Hence,

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\lambda-1}\bigg|\frac{\partial P_{t+s}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial P_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial t}\bigg|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq A\frac{t^{\beta-2}}{c_{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\lambda}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=C_{\beta,\lambda}t^{-1+\beta-\lambda}.

Next, we deal with
II
. Since
f\in Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
,
therefore

\displaystyle|II|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}\bigg[\bigg|\frac{\partial P_{t+s}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial t}\bigg|+\bigg|\frac{\partial P_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial t}\bigg|\bigg]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{c_{\lambda}}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}[(t+s)^{-1+\beta}+t^{-1+\beta}]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-1+\beta}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=C_{\beta,\lambda}t^{-1+\beta-\lambda}.

Then we have

\bigg\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(P_{t}^{\alpha}D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f)\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\beta,\lambda}t^{\beta-\lambda-1},

This finishes the proof.
∎

The Bessel fractional derivative with respect to the Laguerre measure denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
(the Bessel Laguerre fractional derivative in short) is defined formally as

\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}=(I+\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}})^{\lambda},

which means that

\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}L_{k}^{\alpha}(x)=(1+\sqrt{|k|})^{\lambda}L_{k}^{\alpha}(x)

for all Laguerre polynomials.
If
0<\lambda<1
,
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f
has the following integral representation:

\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f=\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\lambda-1}(e^{-t}P_{t}^{\alpha}-I)f\mathrm{d}t,

where
c_{\lambda}=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{-\lambda-1}(e^{-u}-1)\mathrm{d}u<\infty
.

Similarly, we can investigate the action of the Bessel Laguerre fractional derivative
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
on the Laguerre Lipschitz space.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},\infty)^{d}
and
0<\lambda<\beta<1
. Then the Bessel Laguerre fractional derivative
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
is bounded from
Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
into

Lip_{\beta-\lambda}(\mu_{\alpha})
.

Proof.

This theorem can be proved by a method similar to the one used in the proof of Theorem
4.2
. We leave the details to the reader.

∎

Furthermore, if
\lambda\geq 1
, let
k
be the smallest integer such that
\lambda<k
, it can be proved that the Laguerre fractional derivative
D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
can be represented as

D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f=\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}(P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f\mathrm{d}s,

(34)

and the Bessel Laguerre fractional derivative
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
can be represented as

\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f=\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}(e^{-s}P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f\mathrm{d}s,

(35)

where
c_{\lambda}^{k}=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{-\lambda-1}(e^{-u}-1)^{k}\mathrm{d}u<\infty
.

Since it is obvious to verify that for all Laguerre polynomials
L_{k}^{\alpha}
,

D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}L_{k}^{\alpha}=|k|^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}L_{k}^{\alpha}\ \mathrm{and}\ \mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}L_{k}^{\alpha}=(1+\sqrt{|k|})^{\lambda}L_{k}^{\alpha},

then we conclude that (
34
) and (
35
) are valid.

In what follows, we will study the boundedness of
D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
on the Laguerre Lipschitz space when
\lambda\geq 1
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},\infty)^{d}
. For
1\leq\lambda<\beta
, then

(i)
The Laguerre fractional derivative of order
\lambda
, denoted as
D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
, is bounded from
Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
into
Lip_{\beta-\lambda}(\mu_{\alpha})
.

(ii)
The Bessel Laguerre fractional derivative of order
\lambda
, denoted as
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
is bounded from
Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
into
Lip_{\beta-\lambda}(\mu_{\alpha})
.

Before giving the proof of Theorem
4.4
, we need some preparations.
By the binomial theorem and the semigroup property, we get

\begin{split}(P_{t}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)&=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(P_{t}^{\alpha})^{k-j}(-I)^{j}f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}P_{(k-j)t}^{\alpha}f(x)\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}u(x,(k-j)t)=\Delta_{t}^{k}(u(x,\cdot),0),\end{split}

(36)

where
u(x,t)=P_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)
, and we call

\Delta_{s}^{k}(f,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}f(t+(k-j)s)

(37)

the
k
-th order forward difference of
f
starting at
t
with the increment
s
.
Next, we will need certain technical outcomes regarding
forward differences that will be employed later. For more established results in the theory of forward differences, refer to
[
3
]
, while the proofs can be found in
[
6
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any positive integer
k
and
j
, then

(i)

\Delta_{s}^{k}(f,t)=\Delta_{s}^{k-1}(\Delta_{s}(f,\cdot),t)=\Delta_{s}(\Delta_{s}^{k-1}(f,\cdot),t).

(ii)

\Delta_{s}^{k}(f,t)=\int_{t}^{t+s}\int_{v_{1}}^{v_{1}+s}\cdots\int_{v_{k-2}}^{v_{k-2}+s}\int_{v_{k-1}}^{v_{k-1}+s}f^{(k)}(v_{k})\mathrm{d}v_{k}\mathrm{d}v_{k-1}\cdots\mathrm{d}v_{2}\mathrm{d}v_{1}.

(iii)

\frac{\partial}{\partial s}(\Delta_{s}^{k}(f,t))=k\Delta_{s}^{k-1}({f}^{\prime},t+s),

\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial t^{j}}(\Delta_{s}^{k}(f,t))=\Delta_{s}^{k}(f^{(j)},t).

(38)

The following proposition provides estimates similar to (
27
) and (
33
), which are crucial for proving Theorem
4.4
.

Proposition 4.5
.

(see
[
6
]
)
Let
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that
\delta<k
. For some integer
k
, if a function
f
satisfies

|f^{(k)}(r)|\leq Cr^{-k+\delta},

(39)

then

|\Delta_{s}^{k}(f,t)|\leq Cs^{k}t^{-k+\delta}.

(40)

In the following statement, Proposition
3.3
is
generalized to the multidimensional case.

Proposition 4.6
.

(see
[
6
]
)

(i)
If
f\in L^{\infty}
, for any positive integer

k
, then

\|(P_{t}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{k}\|f\|_{\infty}.

(41)

(ii)
If
f\in Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
with
\beta>1
and let
n
be the smallest integer bigger than
\beta
, then

\|(P_{t}^{\alpha}-I)^{n}f\|_{\infty}\leq A_{\beta}(f)t^{\beta}.

(42)

Based on the above results, we are now in a position to prove Theorem
4.4
.

Proof.

(i) Taking
f\in Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
and by
Definition
3.1
, we get
f\in L^{\infty}

and

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{n}u(\cdot,t)}{\partial t^{n}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq A_{\beta}(f)t^{-n+\beta}.

Since
1\leq\lambda<\beta
, let
k
be the smallest integer bigger than
\lambda
and let
n
be the smallest integer bigger than
\beta
so that
k\leq n
. Using (
34
), we obtain

\displaystyle|D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}|(P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{-\lambda-1}|(P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)|\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}|(P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=:I+II.

When
k=n
, the argument is obvious, thus we will assume
k<n
.
Choose

\epsilon>0
such that
\lambda+\epsilon<k
. By Proposition
3.2

we know
Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})\subset Lip_{\lambda+\epsilon}(\mu_{\alpha})
.
Then
(
42
) gives

I\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{-\lambda-1}\|(P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s=\frac{A_{\lambda+\epsilon}(f)}{c_{\lambda}^{k}\epsilon}.

On the other hand, by (
41
) we have

II\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}\|(P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{2^{k}\|f\|_{\infty}}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}\mathrm{d}s=C_{\lambda}\|f\|_{\infty}.

It follows that
D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f\in L^{\infty}
.

Finally, we need to prove the Lipschitz condition. Noting that by (
36
),
(
37
) and taking
u(x,t)=P_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)
, we have the following semigroup
property:

\displaystyle P_{t}^{\alpha}[(P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)]

\displaystyle=P_{t}^{\alpha}(\Delta_{s}^{k}(u(x,\cdot),0))=P_{t}^{\alpha}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}P_{(k-j)s}^{\alpha}f(x)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}P_{t+(k-j)s}^{\alpha}f(x)=\Delta_{s}^{k}(u(x,\cdot),t).

For fixed
t>0
, using again (
34
) and (
38
), we
get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{n}(P_{t}^{\alpha}D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f(x))}{\partial t^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}P_{t}^{\alpha}[(P_{s}-I)^{k}f(x)]\mathrm{d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}[\Delta_{s}^{k}(u(x,\cdot),t)]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\lambda-1}[\Delta_{s}^{k}(u^{(n)}(x,\cdot),t)]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}[\Delta_{s}^{k}(u^{(n)}(x,\cdot),t)]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=:III+IV.

Via (
21
) and Proposition
3.1
, we obtain

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial t^{k}}(u^{(n)}(\cdot,t))\bigg\|_{\infty}=\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{n+k}(u(\cdot,t))}{\partial t^{n+k}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq A_{\beta}(f)t^{-(n+k)+\beta}=A_{\beta}(f)t^{-k+(\beta-n)},

that is, (
39
) is satisfied for

\delta=\beta-n
. Then by (
40
), we obtain

\displaystyle|III|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\lambda-1}|\Delta_{s}^{k}(u^{(n)}(x,\cdot),t)|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{At^{-k+(\beta-n)}}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\lambda+k-1}\mathrm{d}s=C_{\beta,\lambda}t^{-n+\beta-\lambda}.

On the other hand, using (
37
) and Definition
3.1

\displaystyle|\Delta_{s}^{k}(u^{(n)}(x,\cdot),t)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}|u^{(n)}(x,t+(k-j)s)|

\displaystyle\leq A_{\beta}(f)\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}|(t+(k-j)s)^{-n+\beta}|\leq Ct^{-n+\beta},

where
u(x,t)=P_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)
.
Thus, we further have

\displaystyle|IV|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}|\Delta_{s}^{k}(u^{(n)}(x,\cdot),t)|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Ct^{-n+\beta}}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}\mathrm{d}s=C_{\beta,\lambda}t^{-n+\beta-\lambda}.

Hence,

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{n}(P_{t}^{\alpha}D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f)}{\partial t^{n}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq Ct^{-n+\beta-\lambda}.

Observing that
\beta-\lambda<n
, we conclude from Proposition
3.1
that

D_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f\in Lip_{\beta-\lambda}(\mu_{\alpha})
.

(ii) Take
f\in Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})
, that is,
f\in L^{\infty}
and

\|\frac{\partial^{n}u(\cdot,t)}{\partial t^{n}}\|_{\infty}\leq A_{\beta}(f)t^{-n+\beta}
. Since
\lambda<\beta
, let
k
be the smallest integer bigger than

\lambda
and let
n
be the smallest integer bigger than

\beta
. Using (
35
), we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}|(e^{-s}P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{-\lambda-1}|(e^{-s}P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)|\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}|(e^{-s}P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=:I+II.

When
k=n
, the argument is obvious, thus we will assume
k<n
.
Choose
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}
such that

\lambda+\epsilon<k
. Via Proposition
3.2
we know that

Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})\subset Lip_{\lambda+\epsilon}(\mu_{\alpha})\subset Lip_{j-\epsilon}(\mu_{\alpha})

for
j=1,2,\ldots,k-1
. Then by the binomial theorem, we have

(e^{-s}P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}e^{-js}(P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{j}(e^{-s}-1)^{k-j}f(x).

By combining the aforementioned equality with (
42
), it follows that

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{-\lambda-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}e^{-js}|(e^{-s}-1)^{k-j}||(P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{j}f(x)|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\int_{0}^{1}s^{-\lambda-1}e^{-js}|(e^{-s}-1)^{k-j}|\|(P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{j}f\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\int_{0}^{1}s^{-\lambda-1}s^{k-j}\|(P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{j}f\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{k-\lambda-1}\mathrm{d}s\|f\|_{\infty}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\binom{k}{j}\frac{A_{j-\epsilon}(f)}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{-\lambda-1}s^{k-j}s^{j-\epsilon}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{A_{\lambda+\epsilon}(f)}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{\lambda+\epsilon-\lambda-1}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{c_{\lambda}^{k}(k-\lambda)}\|f\|_{\infty}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\binom{k}{j}\frac{A_{j-\epsilon}(f)}{c_{\lambda}^{k}(k-\lambda-\epsilon)}+\frac{A_{\lambda+\epsilon}(f)}{c_{\lambda}^{k}\epsilon}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda}\|f\|_{Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})}.

We use the following binomial theorem

(e^{-s}P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(e^{-s}P_{s}^{\alpha})^{k-j}(-I)^{j}f(x)

(43)

to obtain

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}e^{-(k-j)s}\|P_{(k-j)s}^{\alpha}f\|_{\infty}\bigg]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}(1+e^{-s})^{k}\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{2^{k}\|f\|_{\infty}}{\lambda c_{\lambda}^{k}}\leq C_{\lambda}\|f\|_{Lip_{\beta}(\mu_{\alpha})},

which deduces
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f\in L^{\infty}
.
Finally, it is necessary to validate the Lipschitz condition. By
Remark
3.1
, we only need to consider the case
0<t<1
. Then, using (
43
) and the semigroup property

\displaystyle P_{t}^{\alpha}[(e^{-s}P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)]

\displaystyle=P_{t}^{\alpha}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}e^{-(k-j)s}P_{(k-j)s}^{\alpha}f(x)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}e^{-(k-j)s}P_{t+(k-j)s}^{\alpha}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}e^{-(k-j)s}u(x,t+(k-j)s),

which implies that

\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}P_{t}^{\alpha}[(e^{-s}P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)]=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}e^{-(k-j)s}u^{(n)}(x,t+(k-j)s).

Similarly, using again (
35
), we divided the estimation of
\frac{\partial^{n}(P_{t}^{\alpha}\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f(x))}{\partial t^{n}}
into two parts:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{n}(P_{t}^{\alpha}\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f(x))}{\partial t^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}P_{t}^{\alpha}[(e^{-s}P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)]\mathrm{d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\lambda-1}P_{t}^{\alpha}[(e^{-s}P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)]\mathrm{d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}\bigg[\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}P_{t}^{\alpha}[(e^{-s}P_{s}^{\alpha}-I)^{k}f(x)]\mathrm{d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\lambda-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}e^{-(k-j)s}u^{(n)}(x,t+(k-j)s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}e^{-(k-j)s}u^{(n)}(x,t+(k-j)s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=:III+IV.

For
III
, using (
37
), we can write

\displaystyle|III|

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{t}}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\lambda-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}e^{-t-(k-j)s}u^{(n)}(x,t+(k-j)s)\mathrm{d}s\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{t}}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\lambda-1}|\Delta_{s}^{k}(e^{-\cdot}u^{(n)}(x,\cdot),t)|\mathrm{d}s.

For the sake of convenience, let us take
f(t)=e^{-t}u^{(n)}(x,t)
, by (
21
) and Proposition
3.1
we deduce that for any
k>0

\bigg|\frac{\partial^{k}(u^{(n)}(x,t))}{\partial t^{k}}\bigg|\leq Ct^{-(n+k)+\beta}=Ct^{-n+(\beta-k)}.

Combining this fact with the Leibnitz formula, we get

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\partial^{k}[e^{-t}u^{(n)}(x,t)]}{\partial t^{k}}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|e^{-t}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}u^{(n+(k-j))}(x,t)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-t}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}|u^{(n+(k-j))}(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-t}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}t^{-(n+(k-j))+\beta}

\displaystyle=Ce^{-t}t^{-n+\beta}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}t^{-(k-j)}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-t}t^{-n+\beta}2^{k}t^{-k}=Ce^{-t}t^{-(n+k)+\beta},

with
t\in(0,1)
.
Then using an idea similar to the one contained in Proposition

4.5
, we obtain

|\Delta_{s}^{k}(e^{-\cdot}u^{(n)}(x,\cdot),t)|\leq Ct^{-(n+k)+\beta}e^{-t}s^{k},

and consequently,

|III|\leq\frac{Ct^{-(n+k)+\beta}}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-\lambda+k-1}\mathrm{d}s=C_{\beta,\lambda}t^{-n+\beta-\lambda}.

Finally, for
IV
, using (
21
), we have

\displaystyle|IV|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}e^{-(k-j)s}|u^{(n)}(x,t+(k-j)s)|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{\lambda}^{k}}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}2^{k}(t+(k-j)s)^{-n+\beta}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-n+\beta}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-\lambda-1}\mathrm{d}s=C_{\beta,\lambda}t^{-n+\beta-\lambda}.

Putting together all the above estimates, we conclude that

\bigg\|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}(P_{t}^{\alpha}\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f)\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\beta,\lambda}t^{-n+\beta-\lambda}.

Since
\beta-\lambda<n
, we use Proposition
3.1
to
conclude that
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\alpha}f\in Lip_{\beta-\lambda}(\mu_{\alpha})
.
∎

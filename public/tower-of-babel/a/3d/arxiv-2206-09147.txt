ℛ ⁡ ( p , q ) \mathcal{R}(p,q) -multivariate discrete probability distributions


Abstract
We construct the multivariate probability distributions (Pólya, inverse Pólya, hypergeometric and negative hypergeometric) from the generalized quantum algebra. Moreover, we derive the bivariate probability distributions and determine their properties( ℛ ⁡ ( p , q ) \mathcal{R}(p,q) - factorial moments and covariance). Besides, we deduce particular cases of probability distributions from the quantum algebras known in the literature.

3.3
Multivariate
\mathcal{R}(p,q)-
hypergeometric distribution

In this section, the multivariate
\mathcal{R}(p,q)
- hypergeometric and the negative multivariate
\mathcal{R}(p,q)
- hypergeometric distribution are determined.

We
consider a sequence of independent Bernoulli trials and suppose that the probability of success at the
i
th trial is given as follows:

p_{i}=\frac{\theta\tau_{2}^{i-1}}{\tau^{i-1}_{1}+\theta\tau_{2}^{i-1}},\ \ i\in\mathbb{N},\,\,\ 0<\theta<1.

We denote by
H_{j}
the number of successes after the
(s_{j-1})
th trial and until the
(s_{j})
th trial, with
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,k+1\},

s_{0}=0
,
s_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}r_{i}
,
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,k+1\}
, and
s_{k+1}=r
. Thus, the
\mathcal{R}(p,q)
- random variables
H_{j}

are independent and the probability distribution of the
\mathcal{R}(p,q)
- binomial probability distribution of the first kind is given by:

P(H_{j}=h_{j})=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{r_j}{h_j}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)}\frac{(\theta\tau_{2}^{s_{j-1}})^{h_{j}}\tau^{\binom{r_{j}-h_{j}}{2}}_{1}\tau_{2}^{\binom{h_{j}}{2}}}{\big(\tau_{1}^{s_{j-1}}\oplus\theta\tau_{2}^{s_{j-1}}\big)^{r_{j}}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)}},\ \ h_{j}\in\{0,1,\ldots,r_{j}\}.

(3.16)

Theorem 3.18

The conditional probability function of the
\mathcal{R}(p,q)
- random vector
(H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{k})
, given that
H_{1}+H_{2}+\ldots+H_{k+1}=n
, is the multivariate
\mathcal{R}(p,q)
-hypergeometric distribution. Its probability distribution is given by:

P(H_{1}=h_{1},\ldots,H_{k}=h_{k})=\mathcal{H}_{k}(p,q)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{h_1,\cdots,h_k}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)}{\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\beta_j}{h_j}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)}\over\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\beta}{n}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)}},

(3.17)

where
\mathcal{H}_{k}(p,q)=\tau^{\sum_{j=1}^{k}h_{j}(\beta_{j+1}-h_{j+1})}_{1}\tau_{2}^{\sum_{j=1}^{k}(n-y_{j})(\beta_{j}-h_{j})}

for
h_{j}\in\{0,1,\cdots,n\}
,
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,k\},
with
\sum_{j=1}^{k}h_{j}\leq n
,
h_{k+1}=n-\sum_{j=1}^{k}h_{j}
,
\beta_{k+1}=\beta-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}
, and
y_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}h_{i}
, for
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,k\}.

Proof 3.19

From the relation (
3.16
), the probability distribution of the sum
Y=H_{1}+H_{2}+\cdots+H_{k+1}
, which is the number of successes in
r
trials, is

P(Y=n)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{r}{n}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)}\frac{\theta^{n}\tau_{1}^{\binom{r-n}{2}}\tau_{2}^{\binom{n}{2}}}{\big(1\oplus\theta\big)^{r}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)}},\ \ n\in\{0,1,\ldots,r\}.

Then, the joint conditional probability distribution of the
\mathcal{R}(p,q)
- random vector
(H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{k})
, given that
Y=n,

P(H_{1}=h_{1},\ldots,H_{k}=h_{k}|Y=n)=\frac{P(H_{1}=h_{1})\ldots P(H_{k}=h_{k})P(H_{k+1}=n-y_{k})}{P(Y=n)},

on using these expressions, is obtained as:

P(H_{1}=h_{1},H_{2}=h_{2},\ldots,H_{k}=h_{k}|Y=n)=\big(\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\big)^{c_{k}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{r_j}{x_j}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)}}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{r}{n}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)}},

where

c_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}s_{i-1}+(n-y_{k})s_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{x_{j}}{2}+\binom{n-y_{k}}{2}-\binom{n}{2}.

Thus, after some algebraic manipulations, it reduces to

\displaystyle c_{k}

\displaystyle=n\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}(s_{k}-s_{i-1})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{x_{j}}{2}+\binom{y_{k}+1}{2}-ny_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{j}(n-y_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}(n-y_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(n-y_{j})(y_{j}-x_{j}),

and the proof is completed.
\hfill{\square}

3.4
Multivariate negative
\mathcal{R}(p,q)
-hypergeometric distribution

We consider a sequence of independent Bernoulli trials and suppose that the conditional probability of success at a trial, given that
j-1
successes occur in the previous trials, is determined by:

p_{j}=1-\theta\tau_{1}^{1-j}\tau_{2}^{j-1},\ \ j\in\mathbb{N},\,\,\ 0<\theta<1.

We denotes by
V_{j}
the number of failures after the
(s_{j-1})
th success and until the occurrence of the
(s_{j})
th success, for
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,k+1\}
, with
s_{0}=0
,
s_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}r_{i}
,
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,k+1\}.

Thus, the
\mathcal{R}(p,q)
- random variables

are independent and the
\mathcal{R}(p,q)
- binomial probability distribution of the second kind is presented by:

P(V_{j}=v_{j})=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{r_j+v_j-1}{v_j}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)}(\theta\tau_{2}^{s_{j-1}})^{v_{j}}\big(\tau_{1}^{s_{j-1}}\ominus\theta\tau_{2}^{s_{j-1}}\big)^{r_{j}}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)},\,v_{j}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

(3.23)

Theorem 3.24

The conditional probability function of the
\mathcal{R}(p,q)
- random vector
(V_{1},V_{2},\cdots,V_{k})

\mathcal{R}(p,q)
-hypergeometric distribution with probability function:

(3.24)

Equivalently,

\displaystyle P(V_{1}=v_{1},\cdots,V_{k}=v_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{v_1,v_2,\cdots, v_k}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)}\big(\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\big)^{\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{j}(n-y_{j})}

(3.25)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}[r_{j}+v_{j}-1]_{v_{j},\mathcal{R}(p,q)}}{[r+n-1]_{n,\mathcal{R}(p,q)}},

for
x_{j}\in\{0,1,\cdots,n\},

j\in\{1,2,\cdots,k\},
with
\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}\leq n
, and, where
x_{k+1}=n-\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}
,

,

.

Proof 3.25

According to the relation (
3.23
), the probability function of the sum
U=V_{1}+V_{2}+\cdots+V_{k+1}
, which is the number of failures until the occurrence of the
r
th success, is

P(U=n)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{r+n-1}{n}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)}\theta^{n}\big(1\ominus\theta\big)^{r}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

Then, the joint conditional probability function of the random vector
(W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{k})
, given that
V=n
,

P(W_{1}=w_{1},\ldots,W_{k}=w_{k}|V=n)=\frac{P(W_{1}=w_{1})\cdots P(W_{k}=w_{k})P(W_{k+1}=n-v_{k})}{P(V=n)},

on using these expressions, is obtained as

P(W_{1}=w_{1},\ldots,W_{k}=w_{k}|V=n)=\big(\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\big)^{c_{k}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{r_j+w_j-1}{w_j}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)}}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{r+n-1}{n}_{\mathcal{R}(p,q)}},

where

c_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}s_{i-1}+(n-v_{k})s_{k},\ \ v_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}w_{i},\ \ j\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}.

Thus, after some algebra, it reduces to

c_{k}=n\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}(s_{k}-s_{i-1})=n\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{j}v_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{j}(n-v_{j})

and the proof of (
3.24
) is completed.

Abstract
. We prove a result describing the structure of the formal neighborhoods of certain arcs in the arc space of an algebraic variety which are completely contained in the singular locus. In particular, we provide a precise formulation of the intuitive statement that constant arcs centered in the singular locus are the most singular points of the arc space.

Let
X
be a scheme of finite type over a field
k
. The arc space
X_{\infty}
of
X
is the
k
-scheme whose points parametrize formal arcs on
X
. In particular, for each field extension
K\supset k
, the
K
-points of
X_{\infty}
correspond to morphisms

\Spec K[[t]]\to X.

Since the work of Nash, the topological structure of
X_{\infty}
has been known to encode deep properties about the singularities of
X
. More recently, the singularities of
X_{\infty}
itself have been an object of study. One of the first main results in this direction is the theorem of Drinfeld, Grinberg and Kazhdan. It roughly says that, even though the arc space
X_{\infty}
is infinite-dimensional, its singularities at arcs not contained in the non-smooth locus
X\setminus X_{\mathrm{sm}}
are encoded in a finite-dimensional model.

Theorem

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
X
be a scheme of finite type over a field
k
. Let
\alpha\in X_{\infty}(k)
be such that
\alpha\notin(X\setminus X_{\mathrm{sm}})_{\infty}
. Then there exists a scheme
Y
of finite type over
k
, a point
y\in Y(k)
and an isomorphism of formal neighborhoods

\widehat{(X_{\infty})}_{\alpha}\simeq\widehat{Y}_{y}\times\Spf k[[z_{i}\mid i\in I]].

Here
\widehat{X}_{x}=\Spf\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}
denotes the formal neighborhood of the scheme
X
at
x\in X
. For further development see
[
4
,
2
,
9
,
12
]
.

In this article we are interested in the case where the main assumption of the Drinfeld–Grinberg–Kazhdan theorem is not fulfilled, that is, when the arc
\alpha
is contained in
X\setminus X_{\mathrm{sm}}
. Such arcs are sometimes called
degenerate
. In
[
3
]
it is proven that the existence of a finite-dimensional model fails for the constant arc centered at
0
on the variety
X=\Spec k[x,y]/(x^{2}+y^{2})
over a non-algebraically closed field
k
. This failure was verified for all degenerate arcs on an arbitrary scheme of finite type over a perfect field in
[
5
]
, showing that the assumptions in the Drinfeld–Grinberg–Kazhdan theorem are optimal. The approach in
[
5
]
was to characterize nondegenerate arcs by means of the finiteness of embedding codimension, an invariant of singularities.

Our main result gives a description of the formal neighborhood of constant arcs in the case of characteristic
0
. We say that a formal
k
-scheme
\mathcal{X}
admits a smooth factor if
\mathcal{X}\simeq\mathcal{Z}\times\Spf k[[z]]
, where the product is taken in the category of formal
k
-schemes.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a scheme over a field
k
of characteristic
0
. Let
x\in X(k)
and for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
let
\alpha_{x}\in X_{n}
be the constant
n
-jet resp. arc centered at
x
(see
Section

1
). Then
\widehat{X}_{x}
admits a smooth factor if and only if
\widehat{(X_{n})}_{\alpha_{x}}
admits a smooth factor.

In particular, this partially recovers the aforementioned result of
[
5
]
for arcs fully contained in the respective isosingular locus, see
Proposition

3.8
. We should emphasize that the statement of
A
holds only for constant arcs. In fact, the formal neighborhood of any nonconstant arc admits a smooth factor. This suggests that, in some sense, the worst singularities of the arc space are located at its constant arcs. A precise statement is mentioned in
Theorem

3.11
.

1.
Jet and arc spaces

In this section we briefly review some of the basic properties of jet and arc spaces we will use in this paper. For a more comprehensive treatment we refer the reader to some of the excellent surveys such as
[
13
,
10
]
.

Throughout this section we will fix a field
k
. Let
X
be a scheme over
k
. The
n
-th jet space
X_{n}
of
X
is the
k
-scheme representing the
n
-th jet functor of
X

Y\mapsto\Hom_{k}(Y\times_{k}\Spec k[t]/(t^{n+1}),X).

In particular, for each field extension
K\supset k
, the
K
-points of
X_{n}
are in bijection with
n
-jets on
X
; that is, morphisms of the form

\alpha_{n}\colon\Spec K[t]/(t^{n+1})\to X.

Note that
X_{0}=X
and the first jet space
X_{1}
is just the total space of the Zariski tangent bundle of
X
.

For each
m\geq n
we have induced morphisms
\pi_{m,n}\colon X_{m}\to X_{n}
forming an inverse system. As all
\pi_{m,n}
’s are affine, the inverse limit exists in the category of
k
-schemes and is called the arc space
X_{\infty}
of
X
. By definition,
X_{\infty}
represents the functor

Y\mapsto\Hom_{k}(\varinjlim_{n}Y\times\Spec k[t]/(t^{n+1}),X).

In particular, the
K
-points of
X_{\infty}
are in bijection with arcs on
X

\alpha\colon\Spec K[[t]]\to X.

The arc space comes attached with induced projections
\pi_{n}\colon X_{\infty}\to X_{n}
. Write
\eta
for the generic point and
0
for the unique closed point of
\Spec K[[t]]
. Then
\pi_{0}\colon X_{\infty}\to X
is given by
\alpha\mapsto\alpha(0)
, where we identify a
K
-point of
X_{\infty}
with its corresponding arc. We say that
\alpha
is centered at
\alpha(0)
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
. We define a section
\iota_{n}\colon X\to X_{n}
of
\pi_{n,0}\colon X_{n}\to X
as follows. For each
k
-scheme
S
and
S
-point
x
, the image of
x
is given as the
S
-point of
X_{n}
corresponding to the composition

\textstyle{S\times_{k}\Spec k[t]/(t^{n+1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pr_{1}}
\textstyle{S\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{X,}

if
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
; and for
n=\infty

\textstyle{\varinjlim_{n}S\times_{k}\Spec k[t]/(t^{n+1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pr_{1}}
\textstyle{S\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{X.}

If
x\in X
with residue field
k_{x}
, then
\alpha_{x}:=\iota_{n}(x)\in X_{n}(k_{x})
is called the
constant jet
or
constant arc
centered at
x
. Note that the constant arc centered at
x
satisfies
\alpha_{x}(0)=\alpha_{x}(\eta)=x
. If
x\in X(k)
is a closed point, then
\alpha_{x}
is the unique point of
X_{\infty}
with this property; in general,
\alpha_{x}
is only maximal with respect to specialization.

Example 1.1
.

Consider the arc
\alpha
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
given by

k[x,y]\to k((x))[[t]],\>x\mapsto x+t,y\mapsto 0.

Then
\alpha
is a nonconstant arc with
\alpha(0)=\alpha(\eta)
.

Jet and arc spaces are functorial with respect to their base scheme. That is, if
f\colon X\to Y
is a morphism of
k
-schemes and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
, then there exists an induced morphism
f_{n}\colon X_{n}\to Y_{n}
. One of the fundamental properties of jet and arc spaces (which follows essentially from the definitions) is that, for étale morphisms
f
, the induced morphism
f_{n}
is given by base change and is hence again étale
[
13
, Proposition 2.5]
. We will mention here a variant of this statement at the level of formal neighborhoods of closed points. As we are dealing with completions of non-Noetherian schemes which differ in subtle ways from the Noetherian case, we include a short alternative argument for the reader’s convenience.

For any
k
-scheme
X
and
x\in X(k)
the formal neighborhood of
X
at
x
is defined as

\widehat{X}_{x}:=\Spf\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}},

where
\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}
denotes the completion of
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
with respect to its maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
, endowed with the inverse limit topology. If
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
is not finitely generated, then this topology no longer agrees with the
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
-adic topology. Moreover, the functor of points of the formal scheme
\widehat{X}_{x}
is not determined by its values over Artinian rings. Instead, one has to allow general
test rings
, that is, local
k
-algebras
(A,\mathfrak{m}_{A})
with
A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}=k
and
\mathfrak{m}_{A}^{d}=0
for some
d
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
and
\alpha\in X_{n}
with
x:=\alpha(0)
. For any test-ring
(A,\mathfrak{m}_{A})
consider the set
\widehat{(X_{n})}_{\alpha}(A)
of
A
-deformations of
\alpha
. This set is in bijection to

\{\widetilde{\alpha}\colon\Spec A[[t]]\to X\mid\widetilde{\alpha}\equiv\alpha\mod\mathfrak{m}_{A}\}.

Each such morphism
\widetilde{\alpha}
factors through
\Spec\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
; consider the induced ring map

\widetilde{\alpha}^{\sharp}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\to A[[t]].

As
A
is discrete we have that
A[[t]]
is a complete local ring and thus
\widetilde{\alpha}^{\sharp}
factors through the completion
\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}
. To summarize, the formal neighborhood of
\alpha\in X_{n}
depends only on the formal neighborhood of
x=\alpha(0)\in X
.

Lemma 1.2

(
[
13
, Proposition 2.5]
)
.

Let
X
be a scheme over
k
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
. Let
\alpha\in X_{n}(k)
and
x=\alpha(0)
. Assume that there exists a
k
-scheme
Y
,
y\in Y(k)
, and an isomorphism of formal neighborhoods
\widehat{X}_{x}\simeq\widehat{Y}_{y}
. Then there exists an induced isomorphism

\widehat{(X_{n})}_{\alpha}\simeq\widehat{(Y_{n})}_{\beta},

where
\beta\in Y_{\infty}
is the image of
\alpha
.

As an immediate consequence, we obtain the easy implication in
A
.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
X
be a scheme over a field
k
and
x\in X(k)
. Assume that
\widehat{X}_{x}\simeq\widehat{Y}_{y}\times\Spf k[[z]]
, with
Y
some
k
-scheme and
y\in Y
. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
denote by
\alpha_{x}\in X_{n}
and
\beta_{y}\in Y_{n}
the constant
n
-jets resp. arcs centered at
x
and
y
. Then

\widehat{(X_{n})}_{\alpha_{x}}\simeq\widehat{(Y_{n})}_{\beta_{y}}\times\Spf k[[z_{0},\ldots,z_{n}]].

Proof.

Follows from
Lemma

1.2
and the fact that, for
X
smooth of dimension
d
, the jet space
X_{n}
is smooth of dimension
d(n+1)
.
∎

2.
Derivations and cancellation

In this section we collect some results on derivations on the rings underlying formal neighborhoods of non-Noetherian schemes over
k
. For convenience we assign these rings a name.

Definition 2.1
.

A
quasi-adic ring
is a local
k
-algebra
(R,\mathfrak{m})
which is the completion of a local ring
(A,\mathfrak{m}_{A})
with
A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}=k
, where we require that if
R
is Noetherian then so is
A
. We consider
R
as a topological ring endowed with its inverse limit topology.

The prefix “quasi” here points to the fact that, in case
A
is not Noetherian, the topology on
R
can be approximated by the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic one. More precisely, a basis for the open subsets is given by the topological closure of powers
\mathfrak{m}^{n}
. By definition, any Noetherian quasi-adic ring
R
is adic.

Example 2.2
.

The requirement for
A
to be Noetherian in case
R
is Noetherian is motivated by
[
5
, Example 5.4]
. Consider the following example by Whitney: take
g(t)=\sum_{n\geq 1}g_{n}t^{n}
to be any transcendental series in
{\mathbb{C}}[[t]]
and set

f:=xy(y-x)(y-(3+t)x)(y-(4+g(t))x)\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x,y,t]].

In
[
15
]
it is proven that the Noetherian ring
R:={\mathbb{C}}[[x,y,t]]/(f)
is not isomorphic to the completion of a
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra essentially of finite type over
{\mathbb{C}}
. However, in
[
5
, Example 5.4]
it is shown that
R
is the completion of the non-Noetherian ring
{\mathbb{C}}[x,y,t,z_{n}\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}]/\mathfrak{a}
, where

\mathfrak{a}:=(h,z_{n-1}-z_{n}t-g_{n}t\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0})

and

h:=xy(y-x)(y-(3+t)x)(y-(4+z_{0})x).

Translating the previous definition into the algebraic language, we say that a quasi-adic ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
admits a smooth factor if
\Spf R\simeq\mathcal{Z}\times\Spf k[[z]]
for some formal scheme
\mathcal{Z}
. Note that
\mathcal{Z}
is necessarily affine, so
R
has a smooth factor if there exists an admissible ring
S
(see
[
11
, 0, (7.1.2)]
) and an isomorphism of topological rings
R\simeq S\>\widehat{\otimes}\>_{k}k[[z]]
, the completed tensor product of the topological rings
S
and
k[[z]]
(see
[
11
, 0, (7.7.6)]
).

The main tool in the proof of
A
are derivations. Even though we will restrict our attention to the characteristic
0
case in the next section, here we give the characteristic-free version of the results we make use of. For a more detailed account we refer the reader to
[
1
]
.

Let
R
be any
k
-algebra. A
higher derivation
of
R
is a ring map
d=\sum_{i\geq 0}d_{i}\colon R\to R[[t]]
such that
d_{0}=\id_{R}
. The set of all higher derivations of
R
will be denoted by
\Der_{k}^{\infty}(R)
. If
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is quasi-adic, then
d\in\Der_{k}^{\infty}(R)
is called
regular
if it is continuous (with respect to the induced topology on
R[[t]]\simeq R\>\widehat{\otimes}\>_{k}k[[t]]
) and there exists
x\in\mathfrak{m}
with
d_{1}(x)\in R
invertible. If
k
is of characteristic
0
, then any usual derivation
d\in\Der_{k}(R)
extends uniquely to a higher derivation.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be the completion of
(A,\mathfrak{m}_{A})
. Assume that there exists
d\in\Der_{k}^{\infty}(R)
regular. Then there exists
d^{\prime}\in\Der_{k}^{\infty}(R)
and
x\in\mathfrak{m}_{A}
with
d^{\prime}(x)=x+t
.

Proof.

By assumption there exists
x\in\mathfrak{m}
such that
d_{1}(x)
is invertible. Then there exists an index set
J
and a surjection of local rings
k[X_{j}\mid j\in J]_{(X_{j}\mid j\in J)}\to A
, mapping
X_{j}
to
x_{j}\in\mathfrak{m}_{A}
. Completing gives rise to a surjection
k[[X_{j}\mid j\in J]]\to R
, hence there exists
f\in k[[X_{j}\mid j\in J]]
such that

x=f(x_{j}\mid j\in J).

By continuity of
d
we have that
d_{1}(x_{j})
is invertible for some
j\in J
. Then we proceed as in
[
1
, Lemma 2.1]
.
∎

The following is the main result of this section and, in case
R
is Noetherian, is sometimes known as the Zariski–Lipman–Nagata criterion.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a quasi-adic ring. The following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a regular
d\in\Der_{k}^{\infty}(R)
.

(2)

R
admits a smooth factor.

(3)

There exists
(S,\mathfrak{n})
quasi-adic with
R\simeq S\>\widehat{\otimes}\>_{k}k[[z]]
.

Proof.

For the complete proof we refer the reader to
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
; here we just give a brief remark arguing that (
1
) implies (
3
) (and not just (
2
)). Given
d\in\Der_{k}^{\infty}
continuous and
x\in\mathfrak{m}
, it is proven that there exists a subring
S
of
R
and a continuous isomorphism

\theta\colon S[[Y]]\to R,\>\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{i}Y^{i}\to\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{i}x^{i}.

Assume that
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is the completion of
(A,\mathfrak{m}_{A})
. By
Lemma

2.3
, we may choose
x\in\mathfrak{m}_{A}
, and then
\theta
induces an isomorphism of
S
with the completion of
A/(x)
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

If
k
has characteristic
0
, then in (
1
) it is enough to assume the existence of a (usual) derivation
d\in\Der_{k}(R)
which is regular, i.e.  continuous and such that there exists
x\in\mathfrak{m}
with

Remark 2.6
.

In
[

]
, the fact that in (

) of

. Note that the proof given there has a gap: in the last sentence it is claimed that

with

.

A consequence of

.

(
[

)
.

Let

3.
Applications to formal neighborhoods of the arc space

Let us move towards the proof of the remaining implication in
A
. For that, we assume that
k
is a field of characteristic
0
. Let
X
be a scheme over
k
and
x\in X(k)
. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
let
\alpha_{x}\in X_{n}
be the constant
n
-jet resp. arc. By
Theorems

2.4
and
2.5
it is enough to show that, given a regular derivation
d\in\Der_{k}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X_{n},\alpha_{x}}})
, there exists a regular derivation
d_{0}\in\Der_{k}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}})
.

We may assume that
X=\Spec A
is affine. Then
X_{n}=\Spec A_{n}
, where
A_{n}
denotes the
n
-th Hasse–Schmidt algebra of
A

[
14
]
. If
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then the universal
n
-jet
\gamma_{n}\colon A_{n}\to A_{n}[t]/(t^{n+1})
is given as

\gamma_{n}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}x^{(i)}t^{i},

and similarly for the universal arc
\gamma\colon A_{\infty}\to A_{\infty}[[t]]
/As described in
Section

1
, the natural inclusion
p\colon A\to A_{n}
corresponding to
\pi_{n,0}
has a retraction
q\colon A_{n}\to A
corresponding to
\iota_{n}
. If
\mathfrak{p}\subset A
denotes the prime corresponding to
x
and
\mathfrak{p}_{n}\subset A_{n}
the prime corresponding to
\alpha_{x}
, we get a factorization of the identity on
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
into

\textstyle{A_{\mathfrak{p}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{0}}
\textstyle{(A_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}_{n}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q_{0}}
\textstyle{A_{\mathfrak{p}}.}

Here we use that
\alpha_{x}
is the constant arc centererd at
x
. Set
R:=\widehat{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}
,
R_{n}:=\widehat{(A_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}_{n}}}
and let
\widehat{p}
,
\widehat{q}
be the completions of
p_{0}
and
q_{0}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
d\in\Der_{k}(R_{n})
regular. Then there exists
d\in\Der_{k}(R)
regular.

Proof.

For simplicity we assume
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
; the case
n=\infty
is done similarly. Let
M
be an
A_{n}
-module. Set
B_{n}:=A_{n}[t]/(t^{n+1})
and consider the map
\gamma_{n}\colon A\to B_{n}
induced by the universal
n
-jet. Then we have isomorphisms (see
[
7
, Lemma 5.1]
)

\displaystyle\Der_{k}(A_{n},M)

\displaystyle\simeq\{\phi\in\Hom_{k}(A_{n},A_{n}\oplus\varepsilon M)\mid\phi\equiv\id_{A_{n}}\mod\varepsilon\}

\displaystyle\simeq\{\phi\in\Hom_{k}(A,B_{n}\oplus\varepsilon(M\otimes_{A_{n}}B_{n}))\mid\phi\equiv\gamma_{n}\mod\varepsilon\}

\displaystyle\simeq\Der_{k}(A,M\otimes_{A_{n}}B_{n}).

Note that any continuous derivation
d\in\Der_{k}(R_{n})
is uniquely determined by its restriction in
\Der_{k}(A_{n},R_{n})
. Note that
R_{n}\otimes_{A_{n}}B_{n}\simeq(R_{n})^{n+1}
as
A_{n}
-modules. Composing with
\widehat{q}\colon R_{n}\to R
and by the above
d
corresponds to a continuous derivation
d_{0}\in\Der_{k}(R,R^{n+1})
. Note that
d
regular implies that
d_{0}(x)\neq 0
for some
x
in the maximal ideal of
R
. It is straightforward to verify that

(3a)

\Der_{k}(R,R^{n+1})\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}\Der_{k}(R).

and
d_{0}(x)\neq 0
implies that one of the components of the image of
d_{0}
is a regular derivation in
\Der_{k}(R)
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

It is not clear what the equivalent of the identity (
3a
) should be for higher derivations. In fact, this is the main reason to restrict to the characteristic
0
case.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
X
be a scheme of characteristic
0
and
x\in X(k)
. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
let
\alpha_{x}
be the constant
n
-jet resp. arc. If
\widehat{(X_{n})}_{\alpha_{x}}
admits a smooth factor, then so does
\widehat{X}_{x}
.

The situation is completely different for nonconstant arcs, as they always admit a smooth factor coming from the universal arc, independent of the characteristic.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X
be a scheme over
k
and
\alpha\in X_{\infty}(k)
nonconstant, that is, not in the image of
\iota_{\infty}\colon X\to X_{\infty}
. Then
\widehat{(X_{\infty})}_{\alpha}
admits a smooth factor.

Proof.

We may assume
X=\Spec A
affine; then
X_{\infty}=\Spec A_{\infty}
. The universal arc on

We now move to several consequences of

If

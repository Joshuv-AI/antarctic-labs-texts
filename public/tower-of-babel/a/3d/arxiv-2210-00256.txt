Remarks on extremals of sharp Sobolev trace inequalities on the unit balls
Cheikh Birahim NDIAYE, Liming Sun

Abstract
. We show explicit forms for extremals of some fourth order sharp trace inequalities on the unit balls recently proved by Ache-Chang. We also give a classification result of the bi-harmonic equation on ℝ + 4 \mathbb{R}^{4}_{+} with some conformally covariant boundary conditions.

Introduction

Let

-

If

(1.1)

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}e^{f}d\sigma\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}fd\sigma.

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
\Delta v=0

,

If

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}|\mathbb{S}^{n}|^{1/n}\left(\oint_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|f|^{\frac{2n}{n-1}}d\sigma\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n+1}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+\frac{n-1}{2}\oint_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|f|^{2}d\sigma.

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
\Delta v=0
and
f=c|1-\langle z_{0},\xi\rangle|^{-(n-1)/2}

A natural question is what the extremal

\displaystyle v(\xi)=-\log\left|\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}-{|\xi|}\omega_{0}\right|^{2}+\log(1+|\omega_{0}|^{2}),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{B}^{2}

and the harmonic extension of

\displaystyle v(\xi)=(1+|\omega_{0}|^{2})^{n-1}\left|\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}-{|\xi|}\omega_{0}\right|^{1-n},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{B}^{n+1}

where

\displaystyle\log|\omega_{0}-\xi|^{2}=\log(1-2\omega_{0}\cdot\xi+|\omega_{0}|^{2})=\log(1-\langle z_{0},\xi\rangle)+\log(1+|\omega_{0}|^{2})

2.
Sobolev trace inequality of order four

Recall that the following Möbius transformation which maps the upper half space to the unit ball.

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{S}:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}=\{X=(x,t)\}&\mapsto\mathbb{B}^{n+1}=\{\xi=(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n+1})\}\\
X&\to\frac{2(X+e_{n+1})}{|X+e_{n+1}|^{2}}-e_{n+1}\end{split}

where
e_{n+1}=(0,\cdots,0,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Conversely

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{-1}(\xi)=\frac{2(\xi+e_{n+1})}{|\xi+e_{n+1}|^{2}}-e_{n+1}.

It is well-known that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{*}|d\xi|^{2}=\left(\frac{2}{|X+e_{n+1}|^{2}}\right)^{2}|dX|^{2}.

(2.4)

\displaystyle|x|^{2}+t^{2}=\frac{-4\xi_{n+1}}{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+(\xi_{n+1}+1)^{2}}+1,\quad|\xi|^{2}=\frac{-4t}{|x|^{2}+(t+1)^{2}}+1.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
(a,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
, the following identity holds for
\omega=\mathcal{S}(a,\lambda)\in\mathbb{B}^{n+1}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{|x-a|^{2}+|t+\lambda|^{2}}=\frac{(1-|\omega|^{2})}{4}\ \frac{|\xi+e_{n+1}|^{2}}{F(\xi,\omega)^{2}}.

Proof.

Denote
A=|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+|\xi_{n+1}+1|^{2}
for short. We plug in (
2.2
) to the LHS and achieve

\displaystyle\ |\lambda+t|^{2}+|x-a|^{2}=A^{-2}[(2(\xi_{n+1}+1)+(\lambda-1)A)^{2}+|2\xi^{\prime}-aA|^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{A^{2}}[((\lambda-1)^{2}+|a|^{2})A^{2}+4(\xi_{n+1}+1)(\lambda-1)A-4Aa\cdot\xi^{\prime}+4A]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{A}[((\lambda-1)^{2}+|a|^{2})|\xi|^{2}+2(\lambda^{2}+|a|^{2}-1)\xi_{n+1}-4a\cdot\xi^{\prime}+(\lambda+1)^{2}+|a|^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(\lambda+1)^{2}+|a|^{2}}{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+|\xi_{n+1}+1|^{2}}[|\omega|^{2}|\xi|^{2}-2\omega\cdot\xi+1]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(\lambda+1)^{2}+|a|^{2}}{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+|\xi_{n+1}+1|^{2}}\left|\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}-|\xi|\omega\right|^{2}

where
\omega=\mathcal{S}(a,\lambda)
with

(2.6)

\displaystyle\omega^{\prime}=\frac{2a}{(\lambda+1)^{2}+|a|^{2}},\quad\omega_{n+1}=\frac{2(\lambda+1)}{(\lambda+1)^{2}+|a|^{2}}-1.

We also used the following fact

(2.7)

\displaystyle|\omega|^{2}=\frac{-4\lambda}{(\lambda+1)^{2}+|a|^{2}}+1.

Consequently

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{|\lambda+t|^{2}+|x-a|^{2}}=\frac{1-|\omega|^{2}}{4}\frac{|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+|\xi_{n+1}+1|^{2}}{F(\xi,\omega)^{2}}.

∎

3.
Classification of the solution to a bi-harmonic equation

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.2
. The strategy is to separate the nonlinear effect, by subtracting a function constructed from nonlinear boundary conditions. Such trick has been used by
[
8
,
15
]
. The resulting linear fourth order equation can be classified under the finite volume condition. The proof here is greatly inspired by
Lin 1998
, who initiated the classification of some conformal bi-harmonic equation on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

Given any
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we define
v
for
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}

(3.1)

\displaystyle v(x,t)=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{3}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(y)\log\frac{|y|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}+t^{2}}dy.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
f(y)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, one has

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}v=0&\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+},\\
\partial_{t}\Delta v=4f&\text{on}\quad\partial\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+},\\
\partial_{t}v=0&\text{on}\quad\partial\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Using the Lebesgue dominating theorem, it is easy to see
\partial_{t}v(x,0)=0
and for any
t>0

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Delta v(x,t)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{-4}{|\mathbb{S}^{3}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{2}+t^{2}}dy

(3.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Delta v(x,t)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{8}{|\mathbb{S}^{3}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{tf(y)}{(|x-y|^{2}+t^{2})^{2}}dy

(3.5)

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}v=

\displaystyle\ 0.

Note that
\frac{2}{|\mathbb{S}^{3}|}\frac{t}{(|x-y|^{2}+t^{2})^{2}}
is the Possion kernel of
\Delta
on
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}
. Then one has

(3.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\partial_{t}\Delta v(x,t)=4f(y).

∎

Now suppose that
u
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
. In the following, we will denote

(3.7)

\displaystyle v(x,t)=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{3}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{3u(y,0)}\log\frac{|y|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}+t^{2}}dy.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
v(x,t)
defined in (
3.7
), there exists some constant
C>0
such that

(3.8)

\displaystyle v(X)\geq-\alpha\log|X|-C

where

(3.9)

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{2}{|\mathbb{S}^{3}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{3u(y,0)}dy.

Proof.

The proof is essentially contained in
[
10
]
. For readers’ convenience, we present it here.

For
|x|\geq 4
, we decompose
\mathbb{R}^{3}=A_{1}\cup A_{2}
, where
A_{1}=\{y||(y,0)-X|\leq|X|/2\}
and
A_{2}=\{y||(y,0)-X|\geq|X|/2\}
. For
y\in A_{1}
, one has
|y|\geq|X|-|X-(y,0)|\geq|X|/2\geq|X-(y,0)|
. Consequently, we have
\log|y|/|X-(y,0)|\geq 0
and

(3.10)

\displaystyle\int_{A_{1}}e^{3u(y,0)}\log\frac{|y|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}+t^{2}}dy\geq 0.

For
y\in A_{2}
, one has
|X-(y,0)|\leq|X||y|
if
|y|\geq 2
and
\log|X-(y,0)|\leq\log|X|+C
if
|X|\geq 4
and
|y|\leq 2
. Thus

(3.11)

\displaystyle v(x)\geq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{3}|}\int_{A_{2}}e^{3u(y,0)}\log\frac{|y|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}+t^{2}}dy

(3.12)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ -\frac{2}{|\mathbb{S}^{3}|}\log|X|\int_{A_{2}}e^{3u(y,0)}dy+\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{3}|}\int_{|y|\leq 2}e^{3u(y,0)}\log\frac{|y|^{2}}{|X-(y,0)|^{2}}dy

(3.13)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ -\alpha\log|X|-C.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
u
satisfies (
1.14
) and (
1.15
). Then there exists a constant
C_{1}\geq 0
such that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\Delta u(x,t)=-\frac{4}{|\mathbb{S}^{3}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{e^{3u(y,0)}}{|x-y|^{2}+t^{2}}dy-C_{1}.

Moreover, there exist constants
c_{*}\leq 0
,
a_{i}\leq 0
,
i=1,2,3
such that

(3.15)

\displaystyle u-v=c_{*}t^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}(x_{i}-x_{i}^{0})^{2}+c_{0}.

Proof.

Denote
w=u-v
where
v
is defined in (
3.7
). Then
w
satisfies

(3.16)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}w=0&\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+},\\
\partial_{t}\Delta w=0&\text{on}\quad\partial\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+},\\
\partial_{t}w=0&\text{on}\quad\partial\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}.\end{cases}

We extend
w
by
w(x,t)=w(x,-t)
for

. We denote
\hat{w}
this new function on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. It follows from the mean value property of harmonic functions that
\Delta\hat{w}
is smooth is
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Consequently,
\hat{w}
is also smooth in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

It follows from Lemma
3.2
that for

(3.17)

\displaystyle w(x,t)=u(x,t)-v(x,t)\leq u(x,t)+\alpha\log|(x,t)|+C.

Thus
\hat{w}(X)\leq\hat{u}(X)+\alpha\log|X|+C
, where
\hat{u}
is the even extension of
u
to
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. By Pizzetti’s formula (see
[
12
]
), we have

(3.18)

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}}{8}\Delta\hat{w}(X_{0})=\fint_{\partial B_{r}(X_{0})}\hat{w}d\sigma-\hat{w}(X_{0}).

By Jensen’s inequality

(3.19)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{r^{2}}{2}\Delta\hat{w}(X_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-4\hat{w}(X_{0})}\exp\left(4\fint_{\partial B_{r}(X_{0})}\hat{w}d\sigma\right)

(3.20)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-4\hat{w}(X_{0})}\fint_{\partial B_{r}(X_{0})}e^{4\hat{w}}d\sigma.

Since
\hat{w}(X)\leq\hat{u}(X)+\alpha\log|X|+C
and (
1.16
), then
r^{3-4\alpha}\exp(\frac{r^{2}}{2}\Delta\hat{w}(X_{0}))\in L^{1}[1,\infty)
. Thus
\hat{w}(X_{0})\leq 0
for all

. By Liouville’s Theorem,
\Delta\hat{w}(X)\equiv-C_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
for some constant

.

For bi-harmonic functions, one has the following fact that (for instance, see
[
12
]
)

(3.21)

\displaystyle|D^{3}\hat{w}|(X_{0})\leq\frac{C}{r^{3}}\fint_{B_{r}(X_{0})}\hat{w}d\sigma

holds for some universal constant

.
However, (

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{r}(X_{0})}\hat{w}d\sigma=O(r^{2})

Thus

. Therefore
\hat{w}
is a polynomial of degree at most 2. By the boundary condition of

, one has

Since

), then

. Moreover, after an orthorgonal transformation, we can assume

\displaystyle p(x)=\sum_{i}a_{i}(x-x_{i}^{0})^{2}+c_{0}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Fei Pu

1
Introduction

Consider the parabolic Anderson model with narrow wedge initial condition

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t\,,x)=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}u(t\,,x)+u(t\,,x)\,\xi(t\,,x),\\
u(0)=\delta_{0},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\xi
denotes the space-time white noise. Conus et al
[
10
]
studied the growth of the tallest peaks of solution to stochastic heat equation. For the parabolic Anderson model with flat initial data, i.e.,
u(0)\equiv 1
, they showed that there exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that almost surely

\displaystyle c_{1}\leq\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac{\max_{|x|\leq R}\log u(t\,,x)}{(\log R)^{2/3}}\leq\limsup_{R\to\infty}\frac{\max_{|x|\leq R}\log u(t\,,x)}{(\log R)^{2/3}}\leq c_{2},

(1.2)

(see
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
). Later on, Chen
[
9
]
obtained the precise asymptotic form, that is,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\max_{|x|\leq R}\log u(t\,,x)}{(\log R)^{2/3}}=\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{2t}{3}\right)^{1/3},\quad\text{a.s.}

(see
[
9
, Corollary 1.5]
).

Huang and Lê
[
17
]
have investigated the spatial asymptotic of the stochastic heat equation with compactly supported initial data. In the case of narrow wedge initial data, i.e.,
u(0)=\delta_{0}
, we see from
[
17
, Theorem 1.4]
that

\displaystyle\limsup_{R\to\infty}\frac{\max_{|x|\leq R}\left(\log u(t\,,x)+\frac{x^{2}}{2t}\right)}{(\log R)^{2/3}}\leq\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{2t}{3}\right)^{1/3},\quad\text{a.s.}

A lower bound in
[
17
, Theorem 1.4]
is missing and it is conjectured in Huang and Lê
[
17
]
(see
[
17
, Conjecture 1.2 and (1.18)]
) that, in the case of narrow wedge initial data,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\max_{|x|\leq R}\left(\log u(t\,,x)+\frac{x^{2}}{2t}\right)}{(\log R)^{2/3}}=\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{2t}{3}\right)^{1/3},\quad\text{a.s.}

For the flat initial data, Conus et al
[
10
]
established the lower bound in (
1.2
) using a localization argument. In the situation of the narrow wedge initial data, this localization procedure does not apply because of some technical difficulty. We refer to
[
17
, Section 6.2]
for detailed explanation on this. As an attempt to obtain the exact spatial asymptotics for narrow wedge initial data, Huang and Lê
[
17
]
consider a sequence of approximating solution and use the Gaussian nature of the noise to give some estimate on the spatial asymptotic of this approximating solution; see
[
17
, Theorem 1.5]
.

The goal of this paper is to establish a lower bound on the spatial asymptotic of the parabolic Anderson model with narrow wedge initial data. We have the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\{u(t\,,x):(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}\}
be the solution to parabolic Anderson model (
1.1
). For fixed
t>0
, we have almost surely,

\displaystyle\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac{\max_{|x|\leq R}\left(\log u(t\,,x)+\frac{x^{2}}{2t}\right)}{(\log R)^{2/3}}\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{1/3}.

(1.3)

Conus et al
[
10
]
introduced a coupled process to approximate the solution to stochastic heat equation. This localization argument implies that the random variables
u(t\,,x)
and
u(t\,,y)
are approximately independent when the
spatial variables
x
and
y
are far away, where
u
denotes the solution to parabolic Anderson model with flat initial condition. Combined with the asymptotic behavior of the tail probability of the solution and Borel-Cantelli’s lemma, it leads
to the lower bound in (
1.2
). Our approach to the independence structure of the solution to parabolic Anderson model (
1.1
) relies on the association property of the solution, which has been proved in
[
8
]
. The association property and boundedness of the density of parabolic Anderson model (see Proposition
3.1
and
Corollary
3.3
below) together lead to a crucial probability inequality (
3.4
), which implies that the independence structure of the solution can be characterized by the asymptotic behavior of the covariance of the solution, and hence enables us to study the lower bound on spatial asymptotic of parabolic Anderson model with narrow wedge initial condition.

We will introduce some basic facts on Malliavin calculus and association in Section
2
. The boundedness of density is proved in Section
3
. Finally, we prove Theorem
1.1
in Section
4
.

Let us close the Introduction with a brief description of the notation of this paper.
For every
Z\in L^{k}(\Omega)
, we write
\|Z\|_{k}
instead of

(\mathrm{E}[|Z|^{k}])^{1/k}
. Throughout we write “
g_{1}(x)\lesssim g_{2}(x)
for all
x\in X
” when
there exists a real number
L
such that
g_{1}(x)\leq Lg_{2}(x)
for all
x\in X
.
Alternatively, we might write “
g_{2}(x)\gtrsim g_{1}(x)
for all
x\in X
.” By
“
g_{1}(x)\asymp g_{2}(x)
for all
x\in X
” we mean that
g_{1}(x)\lesssim g_{2}(x)

for all
x\in X
and
g_{2}(x)\lesssim g_{1}(x)
for all
x\in X
.

2
Preliminaries

Following Walsh
[
22
]
, we interpret the stochastic PDE in
the following mild form:

\displaystyle u(t\,,x)=\bm{p}_{t}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}_{t-s}(x-y)u(s\,,y)\,\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y),

(2.1)

where

\bm{p}_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/(2t)}\qquad\text{for all }t>0\text{ and }x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Let

U(t\,,x):=\frac{u(t\,,x)}{\bm{p}_{t}(x)}\qquad\text{for all $t>0$ and $x\in{\mathbb{R}}$}.

(2.2)

By
[
7
, Lemma A.4]
,
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}U(t\,,x)=1
in
L^{k}(\Omega)

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
k\geq 2
.
Therefore, we also define

U(0\,,x):=1\qquad\text{for all $x\in{\mathbb{R}}$},

throughout.
Amir et al
[
1
, Proposition 1.4]
showed
that the process
U(t):=\{U(t\,,x)\}_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}
is stationary for every
t>0
.
The formulation (
2.1
) of the stochastic PDE (
1.1
)
can be recast equivalently in terms of
U
as follows:

U(t\,,x)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\bm{p}_{t-s}(x-y)\bm{p}_{s}(y)}{\bm{p}_{t}(x)}\,U(s\,,y)\,\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y).

Because

\frac{\bm{p}_{t-s}(a)\bm{p}_{s}(b)}{\bm{p}_{t}(a+b)}=\bm{p}_{s(t-s)/t}\left(b-\frac{s}{t}(a+b)\right)\quad\text{for all $0<s<t$ and $a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}$},

(2.3)

equation (
2.1
) can be recast as the following random evolution equation for
U
:

\displaystyle U(t\,,x)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}_{s(t-s)/t}\left(y-\frac{s}{t}x\right)U(s\,,y)\,\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y).

(2.4)

Chen and Dalang
[
3
, Theorem 2.4]
found
that there exists a real number
c_{T,k}>0
such that for all
(t\,,x)\in(0\,,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}
and
k\geq 2
,

\|u(t\,,x)\|_{k}\leq c_{T,k}\,\bm{p}_{t}(x),

which implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}}\|U(t\,,x)\|_{k}\leq c_{T,k}.

(2.5)

2.1
Malliavin calculus

Let
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}})
.
The Gaussian family
\{W(h)\}_{h\in\mathcal{H}}
formed by the Wiener integrals

W(h)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(s\,,y)\,\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y)

defines an
isonormal Gaussian process
on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
In this framework we can develop the Malliavin calculus (see Nualart
[
20
]
).
We denote by
D
the derivative operator. Using Clark-Ocone formula (see Chen et al
[
6
, Proposition 6.3]
), we can derive the following Poincaré-type inequality

|\Cov(F\,,G)|\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\ \left\|D_{s,y}F\right\|_{2}\left\|D_{s,y}G\right\|_{2}\qquad\text{for all $F,G\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}$.}

(2.6)

(see
[
7
, (2.1]
).

The divergence operator
\delta
is defined as the adjoint of the derivative operator
D
as an unbounded operator from
L^{1}(\Omega)
to
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
. Denote by
{\rm Dom}\,\delta

the domain of
\delta
.
For an
\mathcal{H}
-valued random variable
v:\Omega\to\mathcal{H}
and
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,1}
, we write

\displaystyle D_{v}F=\langle DF\,,v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

The following criterion on the boundedness of the density of random variables was proved in
[
2
, Proposition 1]
;
see also
[
18
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,1}
and
v\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
be such that
D_{v}F\neq 0
a.s. Assume that
v/D_{v}F\in{\rm Dom}\,\delta
. Then the
law of
F
has a continuous and bounded density given by

\displaystyle f_{F}(a)=\mathrm{E}\left[\bm{1}_{\{F>a\}}\delta\left(\frac{v}{D_{v}F}\right)\right],\quad a\in{\mathbb{R}}

Remark 2.2
.

We have the following sufficient conditions for
v/D_{v}F\in{\rm Dom}\,\delta
in Proposition
2.1
:

(i)

F\in\mathbb{D}^{2,p}
and
(D_{v}F)^{-1}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
,

(ii)

v\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p}(\mathcal{H})
,

for some
p\geq 6
and
q\geq 4
; see the discussions below Proposition 3.1 of
[
18
]
.

According to Chen et al
[
4
, Proposition 5.1]
,
u(t\,,x)\in\bigcap_{k\geq 2}\mathbb{D}^{1,k}

for all
t>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Moreover, by
[
7
, Lemma 2.1]
, for every
T>0
and
k\geq 2
, there exists a real number
C_{T,k}>0

such that for
t\in(0\,,T]
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and for every
(s\,,y)\in(0\,,t)\times{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\|D_{s,y}u(t\,,x)\|_{k}\leq C_{T,k}\,\bm{p}_{t-s}(x-y)\bm{p}_{s}(y),

which implies that

\displaystyle\|D_{s,y}U(t\,,x)\|_{k}\leq C_{T,k}\,\bm{p}_{s(t-s)/t}\left(y-\frac{s}{t}x\right)

(2.7)

by (
2.2
) and (
2.3
).

2.2
Association

We recall the association property of solution to parabolic Anderson model. We refer to
[
19
]
for more details on
associated random variables. First, let us recall the definition of association; see Esary et al
[
12
]
.
A random vector
X:=(X_{1}\,,\ldots,X_{m})
is said to be
associated
if

\Cov[h_{1}(X)\,,h_{2}(X)]\geq 0,

(2.8)

for every pair of functions
h_{1},h_{2}:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}

that are nondecreasing in every coordinate and satisfy
h_{1}(X),h_{2}(X)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. A random field
\Phi=\{\Phi(x)\}_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
is

associated
if
(\Phi(x_{1})\,,\ldots,\Phi(x_{m}))
is associated
for every
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Chen et al
[
8
]
established the association property of solution to stochastic heat equation; see
[
8
, Theorem A.4]
. In particular, it implies that the solution
\{u(t\,,x):(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}\}
to (
1.1
) is associated. From the definition of association, we deduce the following.

Lemma 2.3
.

The process
\{\log U(t\,,x):(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}\}
is associated.

3
Boundedness of density

The solution
u(t\,,x)
to equation (
1.1
) is strictly positive (see
[
16
]
and
[
15
]
) and has a smooth probability density function on
(0,\infty)
(see
[
4
]
). In this section,
we show that this probability density function is bounded.

Proposition 3.1
.

For fixed
t>0
, the random variable
U(t\,,0)
has a continuous and bounded probability density function given by

\displaystyle f_{U(t\,,0)}(a)=\mathrm{E}\left[\bm{1}_{\{U(t\,,0)>a\}}\delta\left(\frac{v}{D_{v}U(t\,,0)}\right)\right],\quad a\in(0,\infty)

where
v
is chosen as

\displaystyle v(r\,,z)=\bm{1}_{\{0<r<t\}}\bm{p}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)U(r\,,z),\quad\text{for $(r\,,z)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}$}.

Proof.

We will apply Proposition
2.1
to prove this result.
It is clear that the random variable
U(t\,,0)
belongs to
\mathbb{D}^{1,1}
.
From the choice of
v
, we have
v\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
. Moreover, since the Malliavin derivative
D_{r,z}u(t\,,x)
is nonnegative almost surely (see
[
8
, (A.4)]
) and

v(r\,,z)
is strictly positive,
we see that

\displaystyle D_{v}U(t\,,0)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(r\,,z)D_{r,z}U(t\,,0)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r

is strictly
positive almost surely. According to Proposition
2.1
, we need show that
v/D_{v}F\in{\rm Dom}\,\delta
, which reduces to show that the conditions (i) and (ii)
in Remark
2.2
are satisfied. In order to check condition (ii) of Remark
2.2
, we use Minkowski’s inequality to see that for all
p\geq 6
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{E}[\|Dv\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}}]\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}r\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}z\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{d}r_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\bm{p}^{2}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)(D_{r_{1},z_{1}}U(r\,,z))^{2}\right\|_{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}r\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}z\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{d}r_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\bm{p}^{2}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)\left\|(D_{r_{1},z_{1}}U(r\,,z))^{2}\right\|_{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}r\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}z\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{d}r_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\bm{p}^{2}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)\left\|D_{r_{1},z_{1}}U(r\,,z)\right\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}r\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}z\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{d}r_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\bm{p}^{2}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)\bm{p}^{2}_{r_{1}(r-r_{1})/r}\left(z_{1}-\frac{r_{1}}{r}z\right),

where the last inequality holds by (
2.7
). The semigroup property of the heat kernel yields that

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{E}[\|Dv\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}}]\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}r\,\bm{p}_{2r(t-r)/t}\left(0\right)\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{d}r_{1}\,\bm{p}_{2r_{1}(r-r_{1})/r_{1}}\left(0\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}r\,\bm{p}_{2r(t-r)/t}\left(0\right)r\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}r_{1}\,\bm{p}_{2r(1-r_{1})}\left(0\right)

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{r}\,\bm{p}_{2r(t-r)/t}\left(0\right)\mathrm{d}r<\infty.

Hence, we have
v\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p}(\mathcal{H})
for all
p\geq 6
. It is clear that
U(t\,,0)\in\mathbb{D}^{2,p}
for all
p\geq 2
. Now it remains to verify that

\displaystyle(D_{v}U(t\,,0))^{-1}\in L^{q}(\Omega),\quad\text{for some $q\geq 4$}.

(3.1)

We will adopt the method in
[
18
, Proposition 5.1]
to prove (
3.1
).
Choose
\alpha\in(\frac{4}{3},2)
.
Then we have

\displaystyle t-\epsilon^{\alpha}\geq\frac{t}{2},\quad\text{for all $\epsilon\in(0,1\wedge\frac{t}{2})$}.

(3.2)

For
\epsilon\in(0,1\wedge\frac{t}{2})
, since the Malliavin derivative
D_{r,z}U(t\,,0)
is nonnegative almost surely.
We have

\displaystyle D_{v}U(t\,,0)\geq\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(r\,,z)D_{r,z}U(t\,,0)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r.

Because the Malliavin derivative of
U(t\,,0)
satisfies

\displaystyle D_{r,z}U(t\,,0)=\bm{p}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)U(r\,,z)+\int_{r}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}_{s(t-s)/t}\left(y\right)D_{r,z}U(s\,,y)\,\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y)

for
0<r<t
and
D_{r,z}U(t\,,0)=0
for
r\geq t
, we write

\displaystyle D_{v}U(t\,,0)\geq I_{1}+I_{2},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}^{2}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)U^{2}(r\,,z)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\bm{p}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)U(r\,,z)\int_{r}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}_{s(t-s)/t}\left(y\right)D_{r,z}U(s\,,y)\,\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y)\bigg)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r.

Hence, we have for
\epsilon\in(0,1\wedge\frac{t}{2})

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{D_{v}U(t\,,0)<\epsilon\}\leq\mathrm{P}\{I_{1}<2\epsilon\}+\mathrm{P}\{I_{2}|>\epsilon\}.

(3.3)

We first estimate
\mathrm{P}\{I_{1}<2\epsilon\}
. By Chebyshev’s inequality, for any
q\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{I_{1}<2\epsilon\}=\mathrm{P}\{I_{1}^{-1}>(2\epsilon)^{-1}\}\leq(2\epsilon)^{q}\mathrm{E}\left[\left(\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}^{2}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)U^{2}(r\,,z)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r\right)^{-q}\right].

Set

\displaystyle m(\epsilon)

\displaystyle:=\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}^{2}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\bm{p}_{2r(t-r)/t}\left(0\right)\mathrm{d}r\asymp\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{\sqrt{t-r}}\qquad\text{because of \eqref{eq:int}}

\displaystyle\asymp\epsilon^{\alpha/2}.

(3.4)

Since the function
x\to x^{-q}
is convex, we apply Jensen’s inequality with respect to the probability measure

\frac{1}{m(\epsilon)}\bm{p}^{2}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r
to write

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{m(\epsilon)}\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}^{2}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)U^{2}(r\,,z)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r\right)^{-q}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{1}{m(\epsilon)}\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}^{2}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)\mathrm{E}[U^{-2q}(r\,,z)]\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim\frac{1}{m(\epsilon)}\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}^{2}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r=1,

where the second inequality is due to
[
18
, (72)]
; see also
[
5
, Theorem 1.4]
. Hence, we obtain that
for any
q\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{I_{1}<2\epsilon\}\lesssim\epsilon^{q}m(\epsilon)^{-q}\asymp\epsilon^{(1-\alpha/2)q},

(3.5)

thanks to (
3
).

Next, we estimate
\mathrm{P}\{I_{2}|>\epsilon\}
. Using stochastic Fubini’s theorem, we write

\displaystyle I_{2}=\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\bm{p}_{s(t-s)/t}\left(y\right)\int^{s}_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)U(r\,,z)D_{r,z}U(s\,,y)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r\right]\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y).

By Burkholder’s inequality and Minkowski’s inequality, for any
q\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|_{q}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}^{2}_{s(t-s)/t}\left(y\right)\left[\int^{s}_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)U(r\,,z)D_{r,z}U(s\,,y)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r\right]^{2}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}^{2}_{s(t-s)/t}\left(y\right)\left\|\int^{s}_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)U(r\,,z)D_{r,z}U(s\,,y)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r\right\|_{q}^{2}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}^{2}_{s(t-s)/t}\left(y\right)\left[\int^{s}_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)\|U(r\,,z)D_{r,z}U(s\,,y)\|_{q}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r\right]^{2}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

Using Hölder’s inequality, (
2.5
) and (
2.7
), we see that

\displaystyle\|U(r\,,z)D_{r,z}U(s\,,y)\|_{q}\leq\|U(r\,,z)\|_{2q}\|D_{r,z}U(s\,,y)\|_{2q}\lesssim\bm{p}_{r(s-r)/s}\left(z-\frac{r}{s}y\right).

Thus, the preceding implies that

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|_{q}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}^{2}_{s(t-s)/t}\left(y\right)\left[\int^{s}_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}_{r(t-r)/t}\left(z\right)\bm{p}_{r(s-r)/s}\left(z-\frac{r}{s}y\right)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r\right]^{2}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}^{2}_{s(t-s)/t}\left(y\right)\left[\int^{s}_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}\bm{p}_{[r(t-r)/t]+[r(s-r)/s]}\left(\frac{r}{s}y\right)\mathrm{d}r\right]^{2}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}^{2}_{s(t-s)/t}\left(y\right)\left[\int^{s}_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}\frac{s}{r}\,\bm{p}_{(s^{2}[r(t-r)/t]+s^{2}[r(s-r)/s])/r^{2}}\left(y\right)\mathrm{d}r\right]^{2}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

(3.6)

where the first equality holds by the semigroup property of heat kernel and the second equality follows from the
following identity

\displaystyle\bm{p}_{t}(\alpha x)=\alpha^{-1}\bm{p}_{t/\alpha^{2}}(x)\qquad[t,\alpha>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}].

(3.7)

Denote

\displaystyle\lambda(r)=(s^{2}[r(t-r)/t]+s^{2}[r(s-r)/s])/r^{2}.

We use the formula

\bm{p}_{\sigma}(x)\bm{p}_{\tau}(x)=\bm{p}_{\sigma+\tau}(0)\bm{p}_{\sigma\tau/(\sigma+\tau)}(x)\qquad[\sigma,\tau>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}],

(3.8)

to derive that

\displaystyle\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bm{p}^{2}_{s(t-s)/t}\left(y\right)\left[\int^{s}_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}\frac{s}{r}\,\bm{p}_{\lambda(r)}\left(y\right)\mathrm{d}r\right]^{2}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\mathrm{d}s\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\,\bm{p}_{2s(t-s)/t}\left(0\right)\bm{p}_{s(t-s)/(2t)}\left(y\right)\int_{[t-\epsilon^{\alpha},s]^{2}}\frac{s\mathrm{d}r_{1}}{r_{1}}\frac{s\mathrm{d}r_{2}}{r_{2}}\,\bm{p}_{\lambda(r_{1})+\lambda(r_{2})}\left(0\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\bm{p}_{\lambda(r_{1})\lambda(r_{2})/(\lambda(r_{1})+\lambda(r_{2}))}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\mathrm{d}s\,\bm{p}_{2s(t-s)/t}\left(0\right)\int_{[t-\epsilon^{\alpha},s]^{2}}\frac{s\mathrm{d}r_{1}}{r_{1}}\frac{s\mathrm{d}r_{2}}{r_{2}}\bm{p}_{\lambda(r_{1})+\lambda(r_{2})}\left(0\right)\bm{p}_{[s(t-s)/(2t)]+[\lambda(r_{1})\lambda(r_{2})/(\lambda(r_{1})+\lambda(r_{2}))]}\left(0\right),

(3.9)

where we used semigroup property of heat kernel in the second equality.
Notice that

\displaystyle\bm{p}_{[s(t-s)/(2t)]+[\lambda(r_{1})\lambda(r_{2})/(\lambda(r_{1})+\lambda(r_{2}))]}\left(0\right)\geq\bm{p}_{\lambda(r_{1})\lambda(r_{2})/(\lambda(r_{1})+\lambda(r_{2}))}\left(0\right).

Thus, in view of (
3
) and (
3
),
it follows that

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|_{q}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\mathrm{d}s\,\bm{p}_{2s(t-s)/t}\left(0\right)\int_{[t-\epsilon^{\alpha},s]^{2}}\frac{s\mathrm{d}r_{1}}{r_{1}}\frac{s\mathrm{d}r_{2}}{r_{2}}\bm{p}_{\lambda(r_{1})+\lambda(r_{2})}\left(0\right)\bm{p}_{\lambda(r_{1})\lambda(r_{2})/(\lambda(r_{1})+\lambda(r_{2}))}\left(0\right)

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\mathrm{d}s\,\bm{p}_{2s(t-s)/t}\left(0\right)\left[\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{s}\bm{p}_{\lambda(r)}(0)\frac{s\mathrm{d}r}{r}\right]^{2}.

Now for all

and
r\in[t-\epsilon^{\alpha},s]
,
we have
s\in[\frac{t}{2},t]
and

; see (

). Moreover, for

, we have

\displaystyle\lambda(r)\geq s^{2}r(t-r)/(tr^{2})=\frac{s(t-r)}{tr}\gtrsim t-r.

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|_{q}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\sqrt{t-s}}\left[\int_{t-\epsilon^{\alpha}}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{\sqrt{t-r}}\right]^{2}\asymp\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha}.

Hence, by Chebyshev’s inequality, for any

,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{I_{2}|>\epsilon\}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{-q}\mathrm{E}[|I_{2}|^{q}]\lesssim\epsilon^{(\frac{3}{4}\alpha-1)q}.

(3.10)

Letting

, we deduce from (

(3.11)

for

, where the implicit constant only depends on

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Fix
t>0
. Let
\beta
be a positive number that is strictly less than
\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{\frac{t}{2}}
. Choose and fix

a\in(0,\frac{1}{6})
,
\epsilon\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\beta<\frac{a}{\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{\frac{2}{t}}+\epsilon}.

(4.8)

For this fixed
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, by (
4.3
), there exists a positive constant
\tilde{C}

depending on
\epsilon
and the fixed
t>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(\log U(t\,,0)\geq\theta\right)\geq\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{\frac{2}{t}}+\epsilon)\theta^{3/2}},\quad\text{for all $\theta\geq\tilde{C}$}.

(4.9)

Define
x_{j}=2jR/\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor

for
j=1,\ldots,\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor
. Here the notation
\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor
denotes the largest integer which is
less than or equal to
x
.
Assume that
R
is sufficiently large so that
\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor\geq R^{a}/2
and
(\beta\log R)^{2/3}\geq\tilde{C}
, where

\tilde{C}
is the constant in (
4.9
).

By stationarity of
\{U(t\,,x):x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq 2R}\log U(t\,,x)\leq(\beta\log R)^{2/3}\right\}\leq\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor}\log U(t\,,x_{j})\leq(\beta\log R)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor}\log U(t\,,x_{j})\leq(\beta\log R)^{2/3}\right\}-\prod_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor}\mathrm{P}\left\{\log U(t\,,x_{j})\leq(\beta\log R)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left(1-\mathrm{P}\left\{\log U(t\,,0)>(\beta\log R)^{2/3}\right\}\right)^{\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor}

(4.10)

Since the process
\{\log U(t\,,x):x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
is associated (see Lemma
2.3
), by
[
21
, (2.6)]
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor}\log U(t\,,x_{j})\leq(\beta\log R)^{2/3}\right\}-\prod_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor}\mathrm{P}\left\{\log U(t\,,x_{j})\leq(\beta\log R)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor}\bigg(\mathrm{P}\left\{\log U(t\,,x_{j})\leq(\beta\log R)^{2/3},\log U(t\,,x_{k})\leq(\beta\log R)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\mathrm{P}\left\{\log U(t\,,x_{j})\leq(\beta\log R)^{2/3}\right\}\mathrm{P}\left\{\log U(t\,,x_{k})\leq(\beta\log 2R)^{2/3}\right\}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor}\left[\Cov(\log U(t\,,x_{j})\,,\log U(t\,,x_{k}))\right]^{1/3}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor}\frac{1}{|x_{k}-x_{j}|^{1/3}}\asymp R^{(a-1)/3}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor}\frac{1}{|k-j|^{1/3}},

where the second inequality holds by Corollary
3.4
and stationarity, and the third inequality holds by
Lemma
4.1
. Evidently, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor}\frac{1}{|k-j|^{1/3}}\lesssim R^{\frac{5a}{3}}.

Hence, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor}\log U(t\,,x_{j})\leq(\beta\log R)^{2/3}\right\}-\prod_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor}\mathrm{P}\left\{\log U(t\,,x_{j})\leq(\beta\log R)^{2/3}\right\}\lesssim R^{\frac{1}{3}-2a},

which together with (
4

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq 2R}\log U(t\,,x)\leq(\beta\log R)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{\frac{1}{3}-2a}+\left(1-\mathrm{P}\left\{\log U(t\,,0)>(\beta\log R)^{2/3}\right\}\right)^{\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor}

\displaystyle\leq R^{\frac{1}{3}-2a}+\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{\frac{2}{t}}+\epsilon)\beta\log R}\right)^{\left\lfloor R^{a}\right\rfloor}

\displaystyle\leq R^{\frac{1}{3}-2a}+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}R^{a-\frac{4}{3}(\sqrt{\frac{2}{t}}+\epsilon)\beta}},

where the second inequality is due to (
4.9
) and the third inequality follows from the fact
that
1-x\leq\mathrm{e}^{-x}
for all
x\geq 0
.
Since
\frac{1}{3}-2a<0
and

by (
4.8

letting

, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{0\leq x\leq 2^{n+1}}\log U(t\,,x)\leq(\beta\log 2^{n})^{2/3}\right\}<\infty.

Hence, by Borel-Cantelli’s lemma, we have almost surely,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq x\leq 2^{n+1}}\log U(t\,,x)}{(\log 2^{n})^{2/3}}\geq\beta^{2/3}.

Since the quantity

\displaystyle\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq x\leq 2R}\log U(t\,,x)}{(\log R)^{2/3}}\geq\beta^{2/3}.

Letting

\displaystyle\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\leq x\leq 2R}\log U(t\,,x)}{(\log R)^{2/3}}\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{1/3},

which is equivalent to (

The proof is complete.

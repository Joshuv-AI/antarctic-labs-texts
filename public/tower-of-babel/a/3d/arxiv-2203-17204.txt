Dynamics of mean-field bosons at positive temperature
Marco Caporaletti, Andreas Deuchert, Benjamin Schlein

Abstract
We study the time evolution of an initially trapped weakly interacting Bose gas at positive temperature, after the trapping potential has been switched off. It has been recently shown in [ 24 ] that the one-particle density matrix of Gibbs states of the interacting trapped gas is given, to leading order in N N , as N → ∞ N\to\infty , by the one of the ideal gas, with the condensate wave function replaced by the minimizer of the Hartree energy functional. We show that this structure is stable with respect to the many-body evolution in the following sense: the dynamics can be approximated in terms of the time-dependent Hartree equation for the condensate wave function and in terms of the free evolution for the thermally excited particles. The main technical novelty of our work is the use of the Hartree–Fock–Bogoliubov equations to define a fluctuation dynamics.

Introduction and main results

1.1
Background and summary

The time evolution of bosonic many-particle systems in the mean-field limit has received a considerable amount of attention in recent years. The main theme in most works is the study of the dynamics of Bose gases that are initially prepared in a ground state (equilibrium state at zero temperature) of a trapped Hamiltonian after the trap has been switched off. It is well-known, see e.g.
[
51
,
66
]
and references therein, that under appropriate conditions on the interaction potential, ground states of interacting mean-field systems display complete Bose–Einstein condensation (BEC), that is, the largest eigenvalue
\lambda_{N}
of the one-particle density matrix (1-pdm) of the ground state wave function satisfies
\lambda_{N}/N\to 1
as
N\to\infty
. It turns out that this structure is stable w.r.t. the dynamics generated by the time-dependent many-particle Schrödinger equation. More precisely, it can be shown that the system displays complete BEC also at time
t>0
, where the condensate wave function (the eigenfunction related to the largest eigenvalue of the 1-pdm) is given by the solution to the time-dependent Hartree equation.

The first results in this direction were obtained in
[
44
]
and then in
[

,
38
,

]
. The case of Coulomb interactions has been studied more recently in
[

30
]
and in
[
25
]
with a relativistic dispersion relation. In
[
31
,
36
]
the convergence towards the Hartree equation has been interpreted as an Egorov type theorem,
[

[

49

,

This, in particular, allows one to obtain a norm approximation for the wave function, and not only for the 1-pdm. A systematic expansion for correlation functions in terms of the solution of the Hartree equation and a Bogoliubov two-point function that approximates the original dynamics to arbitrary precision has been obtained in
[
12
]
.

Another interesting scaling limit for the Bose gas is the Gross–Pitaevskii (GP) limit, which can be used to describe modern experiments with cold alkali gases. In contrast to the mean-field limit, where particle collisions are frequent but weak, particles interact rarely but strongly in the GP limit. These strong collisions induce microscopic correlations between the particles that are the main reason why the GP limit is more challenging from a mathematical point of view than the mean-field limit. It has been shown in
[

Here we are interested in the dynamics of mean-field bosons, initially prepared in an equilibrium state at positive temperature (Gibbs state). Just above the critical temperature for BEC, correlation functions of the many-body Gibbs state have been shown to converge, in the mean-field limit, to correlation functions of the invariant measure associated with the nonlinear Hartree equation, see
[
52
,
32
,
53

[

[
24

Our goal here is to investigate the dynamics of such positive temperature states, after the trapping potential has been switched off. In our main theorem we show that the 1-pdm of the evolved state is given, to leading order, by the one of the ideal gas, propagated with the non-interacting time evolution, with the condensate wave function replaced by the solution of the time-dependent Hartree equation. In particular, the number of particles inside the condensate and outside the condensate is preserved by the time evolution, to leading order.

1.3
The model

In this article we consider the time evolution of an initially trapped Bose gas, prepared in a positive temperature state (an approximate Gibbs state), after the trapping potential has been switched off. We start by introducing our set-up.

We will be interested in quantum states with a fluctuating particle number. These states are naturally defined on the bosonic Fock space

\mathscr{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}L^{2}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathbb{R}^{3n})\,,

(1.1)

where
L^{2}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathbb{R}^{3n})
denotes the closed linear subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3n})
consisting of those functions
\Psi(x_{1},...,x_{n})
that are invariant under any permutation of the coordinates
x_{1},...,x_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. As usual, we have
L^{2}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathbb{R}^{0})=\mathbb{C}
. By
a_{x}^{*}
and
a_{x}
we denote the creation and annihilation operators (actually operator-valued distributions) on
\mathscr{F}
that create and annihilate a particle at point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, respectively. They satisfy the canonical commutation relations (CCR)

[a_{x},a_{y}^{*}]=\delta(x-y),\quad\quad[a_{x},a_{y}]=0=[a_{x}^{*},a_{y}^{*}]\,,

where
\delta(x)
denotes the delta distribution with unit mass at
x=0
. Quantum states on the bosonic Fock space with an expected number of particles equal to
N
are elements of the set

\mathcal{S}_{N}=\{\Gamma\in\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{F})\ |\ \Gamma\geq 0,\tr\Gamma=1,\tr[\mathcal{N}\Gamma]=N\}\,,

where
\mathcal{N}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}a_{x}^{*}a_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x
denotes the particle number operator on
\mathscr{F}
. The one-particle reduced density matrix (1-pdm)
\gamma
of a state
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
is defined via its integral kernel by

\gamma(x,y)=\tr[a_{y}^{*}a_{x}\Gamma]

and defines a nonnegative operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\tr\gamma=N
.

The time evolution we are interested in is governed by the Heisenberg equation

\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}\Gamma_{t}=[\mathcal{H}_{N},\Gamma_{t}]\,,

(1.2)

where

\mathcal{H}_{N}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla a_{x}^{*}\nabla a_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}v(x-y)a^{*}_{x}a^{*}_{y}a_{y}a_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

(1.3)

and
[A,B]=AB-BA
denotes the commutator of two linear operators
A
and
B
. The prefactor
N^{-1}
in front of
v
guarantees that, for particles in the condensate, the kinetic and the potential energy are comparable. Any solution to (
1.2
) with initial condition
\Gamma_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
can be written in terms of the strongly continuous unitary group
e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}_{N}t}
as

\Gamma_{t}=e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}_{N}t}\Gamma_{0}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}_{N}t}\,.

(1.4)

The fact that
\mathcal{H}_{N}
commutes with
\mathcal{N}
guarantees
\tr[\mathcal{N}\Gamma_{t}]=\tr[\mathcal{N}\Gamma_{0}]
. As explained in Section
1.1
, we are interested in the solution (
1.4
) for initial data describing equilibrium states of trapped gases at positive temperature. Let us now recall some known properties of such states.

1.4
Equilibrium states of trapped Bose gases

Equilibrium states of trapped Bose gases can be defined as minimizers, in
\mathcal{S}_{N}
, of the Gibbs free energy functional

\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)=\tr[\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\mathrm{trap}}\Gamma]-TS(\Gamma)\,,\quad\text{ with the von Neumann entropy }\quad S(\Gamma)=-\tr[\Gamma\ln(\Gamma)]\,.

(1.5)

Here

\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\mathrm{trap}}=\mathcal{H}_{N}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w(x)a_{x}^{*}a_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}a_{x}^{*}\left(-\Delta+w(x)\right)a_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}v(x-y)a^{*}_{x}a^{*}_{y}a_{y}a_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

(1.6)

denotes the Hamiltonian of the trapped system and
T>0
is the temperature. The trapping potential
w(x)
satisfies
w(x)\to\infty
for
|x|\to\infty
. The minimum of (
1.5
) is attained for the Gibbs state

\Gamma_{\mathrm{G}}=\frac{\exp(-(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\mathrm{trap}}-\mu\mathcal{N})/T)}{\tr[\exp(-(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\mathrm{trap}}-\mu\mathcal{N})/T)]}\,,

where the chemical potential
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
is chosen (depending on
N
) so that
\tr[\mathcal{N}\Gamma]=N
. This leads to the (grand canonical) free energy

F(T,N)=\min_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)=-T\ln\tr[\exp(-(\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\mathrm{trap}}-\mu\mathcal{N})/T)]+\mu N\,.

(1.7)

Before we discuss what is known about approximate minimizers of the Gibbs free energy functional
\mathcal{F}
, let us briefly recall some well-known facts about the ideal (non-interacting) trapped Bose gas, that is, the system described by the Hamiltonian in (
1.6
) with
v=0
. In the following we assume that the trapping potential
w
satisfies

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{w(x)}{L|x|^{s}}=1

(1.8)

for two constants
s>0
and
L>0
. The one-particle density matrix
\gamma^{\mathrm{id}}
of the Gibbs state
\Gamma_{\mathrm{G}}
of the ideal gas equals

\gamma^{\mathrm{id}}=\frac{1}{\exp((-\Delta+w-\mu_{N})/T)-1}\,.

(1.9)

The chemical potential
\mu_{N}
satisfies
\mu_{N}<e
, where
e
denotes the lowest eigenvalue of
-\Delta+w
, and is chosen s.t.
\tr\gamma^{\mathrm{id}}=N
holds. By definition, the system displays BEC if and only if the largest eigenvalue
N_{0}(T,N)
of
\gamma^{\mathrm{id}}
is of order
N
in the limit
N\to\ \infty
, that is, if and only if

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{N_{0}(T,N)}{N}=g>0\,,\quad\text{ where }\quad N_{0}(T,N)=\frac{1}{\exp((e-\mu_{N})/T)-1}\,.

(1.10)

The trapped ideal Bose gas is well-known to exhibit a BEC phase transition with critical temperature given by

T_{\mathrm{c}}(s)=\frac{N^{1/\alpha}}{(\kappa\alpha\Gamma(\alpha)\zeta(\alpha))^{1/{\alpha}}}\,,\quad\text{ where }\quad\kappa=\frac{2L^{-3/s}}{3\pi}\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)^{3/2}x^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{ and }\quad\alpha=\frac{6+3s}{2s}\,.

(1.11)

Here
\Gamma(\alpha)
denotes the Gamma function and
\zeta(\alpha)
is the Riemann zeta function. More precisely, let us define

, that is,
t_{\mathrm{c}}(s)=N^{-1/\alpha}T_{\mathrm{c}}(s)
. If

with

1.5
Construction of the initial data

In this section we define our class of initial states. Our definition is motivated by the discussion in Section
1.4
.

Let
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and let
\gamma\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
be a positive trace class operator. We think of
\phi
as a condensate wave function and of
\gamma
as a 1-pdm describing excitations of the condensate. The particle number
n(\phi,\gamma)
of the pair
(\phi,\gamma)
is defined by

n(\phi,\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\phi(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\tr\gamma\,.

(1.16)

Let
G
be the unique quasi-free state on
\mathscr{F}
that satisfies
[G,\mathcal{N}]=0
and has
\gamma
as 1-pdm. We also define the Weyl transformation

W(\phi)=\exp(a^{*}(\phi)-a(\phi))\,.

(1.17)

The above definitions allow us to associate to each pair
(\phi,\gamma)
the state

\Gamma(\phi,\gamma)=W(\phi)GW(\phi)^{*}

(1.18)

on the bosonic Fock space. The kernel of its 1-pdm is given by

\tr[a_{y}^{*}a_{x}\Gamma(\phi,\gamma)]=\phi(x)\overline{\phi(y)}+\gamma(x,y)\,,

(1.19)

which, in particular, implies
\tr[\mathcal{N}\Gamma(\phi,\gamma)]=n(\phi,\gamma)
. The state
\Gamma(\phi,\gamma)
is still a quasi-free state, in the sense that higher order correlations can be computed through the Wick theorem, summing over all possible partitions in groups of one or two creation and annihilation operators. Choosing
\phi=\sqrt{N_{0}(T,N)}\phi^{\mathrm{H}}
and
\gamma=\gamma^{\mathrm{id}}Q
, it is simple to check that
\Gamma(\phi,\gamma)
provides a good approximation for the free energy of the system in the sense of (
1.14
). In our main theorem, we will describe the evolution in (
1.4
) for initial data given by suitable perturbations of a quasi-free state of the form
\Gamma(\phi,\gamma)
, under appropriate assumptions on
(\phi,\gamma)
.

To this end, we first use the Araki–Woods representation, see
[
3
,
23
]
, to recast a mixed state
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
as a vector
\Psi
on the doubled Fock space
\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathscr{F}
. This will allow us to apply the formalism of Bogoliubov transformations and fluctuation dynamics. Note that a similar strategy was used in
[
7
]
to study the mean-field evolution of fermionic mixed states. The vector
\Psi
is conveniently defined in terms of the spectral decomposition
\Gamma=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{\alpha}|\psi_{\alpha}\rangle\langle\psi_{\alpha}|
as

\Psi=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{\alpha}^{1/2}\psi_{\alpha}\otimes\overline{\psi_{\alpha}},

(1.20)

where we define the complex conjugate of
\psi_{\alpha}
in the position-space representation. It satisfies

\tr[A\Gamma]=\langle\Psi,\left(A\otimes\mathds{1}\right)\Psi\rangle

(1.21)

for
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{F})
. It should be noted that this representation is far from being unique. When we replace the vectors
\psi_{\alpha}
in the second tensor factor in (
1.20
) by elements of another orthonormal basis of
\mathscr{F}
, we obtain an equivalent representation of
\Gamma(\phi,\gamma)
on
\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathscr{F}
in the sense that (
1.21
) continues to hold.

For the next step in our construction we need the unitary equivalence

\mathcal{U}\mathscr{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\otimes\mathscr{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))=\mathscr{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\oplus L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))

defined by the relations
\mathcal{U}\Omega\otimes\Omega=\widetilde{\Omega}
, where
\widetilde{\Omega}
denotes the vacuum vector of
\mathscr{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\oplus L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
,

\mathcal{U}\big(a(f)\otimes\mathds{1}\big)\mathcal{U}^{*}=a(f\oplus 0)\eqqcolon a_{\mathrm{\ell}}(f),\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{U}\big(\mathds{1}\otimes a(f)\big)\mathcal{U}^{*}=a(0\oplus f)\eqqcolon a_{r}(f).

(1.22)

Thus, every state
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
can be represented by a normalized vector
\widetilde{\Psi}=\mathcal{U}\Psi\in\mathscr{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\oplus L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
satisfying

\tr[A\Gamma]=\langle\widetilde{\Psi}\mathcal{U}(A\otimes\mathds{1})\mathcal{U}^{*}\widetilde{\Psi}\rangle

If
A
is expressed in terms of creation and annihilation operators
a^{*},a
, then
\mathcal{U}(A\otimes 1)\mathcal{U}^{*}
is obtained from
A
by replacing all operators
a^{*}
with
a^{*}_{\ell}
and all operators
a
with
a_{\ell}
. Let us now consider, in particular, the state (
1.18
). An important observation is that the quasi-free state
G
can be described, on
\mathcal{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\oplus L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, by the vector
\widetilde{\Psi}_{G}=\mathcal{T}(\gamma)\widetilde{\Omega}
, where

\mathcal{T}(\gamma)=\exp\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}k_{\gamma}(x,y)a^{*}_{\mathrm{\ell},x}a^{*}_{r,y}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y-\text{h.c.}\right)

(1.23)

and
k_{\gamma}(x,y)
denotes the integral kernel of the operator
k_{\gamma}=\mathrm{arcsinh}(\sqrt{\gamma})
. We observe that
\mathcal{T}(\gamma)
is a Bogoliubov transformation and satisfies the relations

\mathcal{T}^{*}(\gamma)a_{\ell}(f)\mathcal{T}(\gamma)=a_{\ell}(uf)+a_{r}^{*}(\overline{vf})\,,\hskip 42.67912pt\mathcal{T}^{*}(\gamma)a_{r}(f)\mathcal{T}(\gamma)=a_{r}(uf)+a_{\ell}^{*}(\overline{vf})\,,

(1.24)

where
u=\sqrt{1+\gamma}
and
v=\sqrt{\gamma}
. With (
1.24
), it is clear that
\widetilde{\Psi}_{G}
is quasi-free (it satisfies the Wick theorem) and that it has the same 1-pdm as
G
, in the sense that

\langle\widetilde{\Psi}_{G},a_{\ell,y}^{*}a_{\ell,x}\widetilde{\Psi}_{G}\rangle=\gamma(x,y)\,.

Recalling (
1.18
), we find that the quasi-free state
\Gamma(\phi,\gamma)
, now with a condensate described by the wave function
\phi
, can be described, on
\mathscr{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\oplus L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
by the vector
\widetilde{\Psi}(\phi,\gamma)=\mathcal{W}(\phi)\mathcal{T}(\gamma)\widetilde{\Omega}
(sometimes called the purification of
\Gamma(\phi,\gamma)
), in the sense that

\tr[A\Gamma(\phi,\gamma)]=\langle\widetilde{\Omega},\mathcal{T}^{*}(\gamma)\mathcal{W}^{*}(\phi)\mathcal{U}\left(A\otimes\mathds{1}\right)\mathcal{U}^{*}\mathcal{W}(\phi)\mathcal{T}(\gamma)\widetilde{\Omega}\rangle\,.

(1.25)

Here, we introduced, on
\mathscr{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\oplus L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, the Weyl operator

We conclude this section with a brief discussion of the relevant scales related to our time evolution. In the whole discussion we assume that

with

1.7
Proof strategy and organization of the article

For the convenience of the reader we give in this section a short summary of the main steps leading to a proof of Theorem
1.2
.

In Section
2
we show that the initial data in Remark
1.3
.5, consisting of the minimizer of the Hartree energy functional in (
1.12
) and the 1-pdm of the ideal gas trapped by the potential

with

, satisfies Assumption
1.1
.

Our many-body analysis is based on the definition of fluctuation dynamics around the dynamics generated by the Hartree–Fock–Bogoliubov (HFB) equations, see e.g.
[
4
,
10
]
. We do not give a derivation of these equations from quantum mechanics but rather use them as a technical tool. In Section
3
we introduce the HFB equations, we recall their well-posedness, as established in
[
4
]
, and we show how they can be approximated by the much simpler effective dynamics appearing in Theorem
1.2
, if the initial data satisfies Assumption
1.1
. We also prove a bound for solutions of the HFB equations that guarantees the diluteness of the thermal cloud during the time evolution and is a crucial ingredient for our many-body analysis.

Section
4
is devoted to the construction of the fluctation dynamics around the HFB equations and to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. We start by recalling some basic facts about Weyl and Bogoliubov transformations in Section
4.1
, and afterwards define the fluctuation dynamics in Section
4.2
. In Section
4.3
we prove a bound for the trace-norm difference of
\gamma_{\xi,t}
and the 1-pdm related to the solution to the HFB equations in terms of the expected number of excitations in the time-dependent fluctuation vector. A bound for this quantity is stated without proof in Proposition
4.3
in Section
4.4
. We end Section
4
by showing how Proposition
4.3
implies Theorem
1.2
.

In Section
5
we give a proof of Proposition
4.3
that is based on a Grönwall argument for the expected number of particles in the fluctuation dynamics, and we start our analysis in Section
5.1
with the computation of its generator. In Section
5.2
we introduce a fluctuation dynamics with a cut-off in the particle number and prove the equivalent of Proposition
4.3
for this dynamics. Section
5.3
is devoted to the proof of weak a-priori bounds for the original fluctation dynamics without a cut-off, that are later used in Section
5.4
to show that the two fluctuation dynamics are close in a suitable sense. Finally, in Section

we use the results of the previous sections to give a proof of Proposition

\tr\,(1+h)^{3}\gamma=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{e^{\,\beta(e_{j}-\mu)}-1}(1+e_{j})^{3}\lesssim\tr e^{-ch}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-cw(x)/2}dx<\infty

for some constant

The bound

, which implies that

\|\gamma\|\leq\frac{1}{\exp(\beta\Delta e)-1}\leq\frac{1}{\beta\Delta e}\lesssim T_{\mathrm{c}}(s)\,.

Next, we show (
2.2
). We use here the notation
\psi_{j}
for the eigenfunction of

corresponding to the eigenvalue

, for

\gamma=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\infty}\left(\exp(-\beta(h-\mu)\alpha)-\exp(-\beta(e_{0}-\mu)\alpha)|\psi_{0}\rangle\langle\psi_{0}|\right)\,.

Using the above representation of
\gamma

\displaystyle|\hat{\gamma}(p,q)|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}\exp(\beta\mu\alpha)k_{\beta\alpha}(p,q)+\sum_{\alpha=M+1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\exp(-\beta\alpha(e_{i}-\mu))|\hat{\psi}_{i}(p)|\ |\hat{\psi}_{i}(q)|

(2.16)

The norm on the r.h.s. is bounded by the

\psi_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\beta(M+1)(e_{i}-\mu)}}{1-e^{-\beta(e_{i}-\mu)}}|\hat{\psi}_{i}(p)|\ |\hat{\psi}_{i}(q)|\leq\frac{1}{\beta\Delta e}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-\beta M(e_{i}-\mu)}|\hat{\psi}_{i}(p)|\ |\hat{\psi}_{i}(q)|\,.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta\Delta e}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-\beta M(e_{i}-\mu)}\left(\int|\hat{\psi}_{i}(p)|\,\mathrm{d}p\right)^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{\beta\Delta e}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-\beta M(e_{i}-\mu)}\,\langle\psi_{i},(1-\Delta)^{2}\psi_{i}\rangle\lesssim\frac{1}{\beta\Delta e}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-\beta M(e_{i}-\mu)}(1+e_{i})^{2}\,.

Choosing

), and (
2.16
).

Finally, let us show the bound on

3
The Hartree–Fock–Bogoliubov equations

As explained in Section
1.7
, we use the HFB equations to define our fluctuation dynamics in Section
4.2
. Here we introduce the equations and collect some of their properties that we need for the many-body analysis.

For the triple
(\phi_{t},\gamma_{t},\alpha_{t})
consisting of a condensate wave function
\phi_{t}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, a positive trace class operator
\gamma_{t}
(a 1-pdm), and a pairing function
\alpha_{t}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
, the HFB equations take the form

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}\phi_{t}=

\displaystyle h(\gamma_{t})\phi_{t}+k(\alpha_{t}^{\phi_{t}})\overline{\phi_{t}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}\gamma_{t}=

\displaystyle\;[h(\gamma_{t}^{\phi_{t}}),\gamma_{t}]+k(\alpha_{t}^{\phi_{t}})\alpha_{t}^{*}-\alpha_{t}k(\alpha_{t}^{\phi_{t}})^{*}

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}\alpha_{t}=

\displaystyle\;[h(\gamma_{t}^{\phi_{t}}),\alpha_{t}]_{+}+[k(\alpha_{t}^{\phi_{t}}),\gamma_{t}]_{+}+k(\alpha_{t}^{\phi_{t}}),

(3.1)

where
[A,B]_{+}=A\overline{B}^{*}+B\overline{A}^{*}
,
\gamma^{\phi}=\gamma+|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|
, and
\alpha^{\phi}=\alpha+|\phi\rangle\langle\overline{\phi}|
. By
\overline{A}
we denote the operator with the complex conjugate integral kernel in position space. Moreover, we use the notations

h(\gamma)=-\Delta+b(\gamma),\qquad k(\alpha)=\frac{1}{N}v\sharp\alpha\,,

(3.2)

where

b(\gamma)=\frac{1}{N}v\ast\varrho_{\gamma}+\frac{1}{N}v\sharp\gamma\,,

v\sharp\sigma
denotes the operator with kernel
v(x-y)\sigma(x,y)
, and the density associated with the 1-pdm
\gamma
is given by
\varrho_{\gamma}(x)=\gamma(x,x)
. Here
v
is an interaction potential satisfying suitable assumptions to be specified later. The mean-field scaling we are interested in is reflected in the factor
N^{-1}
multiplying
v
in the equations.

The HFB equations arise naturally as a quasi-free approximation of the many-body dynamics (which does not preserve quasi-freeness), for quasi-free initial data. For more details concerning this argument and more information about the HFB equations we refer the reader to
[
4
,
10
]
.

3.1
Well-posedness

In this section we state conditions that guarantee the well-posedness of the HFB equations. We define
M=(1-\Delta)^{1/2}
, and for
j\geq 0
we introduce the linear spaces

\mathcal{H}^{j,1}=M^{-j}\mathcal{L}^{1}M^{-j}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{H}^{j,2}=\left\{\alpha\in\mathcal{L}^{2}\,|\,M^{j}\alpha,\alpha M^{j}\in\mathcal{L}^{2}\right\}

with
\mathcal{L}^{j}
defined in Section
1.2
, which are Banach spaces when endowed with their natural norms

\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{H}^{j,1}}=\|M^{j}\gamma M^{j}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{H}^{j,2}}=\|M^{j}\alpha\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+\|\alpha M^{j}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\,,

respectively.
We also define the Banach space
(X^{j},\|\cdot\|_{X^{j}})
, where

X^{j}=H^{j}\times\mathcal{H}^{j,1}\times\mathcal{H}^{j,2}\,\quad\text{ and }\quad\|(\phi,\gamma,\alpha)\|_{X^{j}}=\|\phi\|_{H^{j}}+\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{H}^{j,1}}+\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{H}^{j,2}}\,.

The following holds true, see
[
4
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\phi_{0},\gamma_{0},\alpha_{0})\in X^{3}
and suppose that

\Gamma_{0}^{(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{0}&\alpha_{0}\\
\overline{\alpha}_{0}&1+\overline{\gamma}_{0}\end{pmatrix}

is positive, i.e.
\Gamma_{0}^{(1)}\geq 0
as an operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(this is always the case if
\gamma_{0}
,
\alpha_{0}
are the reduced 1-pdm and pairing density of a state
\Gamma
on
\mathscr{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
). Assume also that the interaction potential
v
satisfies
v(x)=v(-x)
and
v\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for some
p>3
. Then there exists a unique triple
(\phi_{t},\gamma_{t},\alpha_{t})\in C^{1}([0,\infty),X^{1})\cap C^{0}([0,\infty),X^{3})
satisfying (
3.1
) with initial datum
(\phi_{0},\gamma_{0},\alpha_{0})
in the classical sense in
X^{1}
. Moreover, the number of particles

n(\phi_{t},\gamma_{t})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\phi_{t}(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\tr\gamma_{t}

(3.3)

and the energy

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\phi_{t},\gamma_{t},\alpha_{t})=

\displaystyle\;\tr[h(\gamma_{t}^{\phi_{t}})\gamma_{t}]+\frac{1}{N}\tr[(v\ast|\phi_{t}|^{2}+v\sharp|\phi_{t}\rangle\langle\phi_{t}|)\gamma_{t}]

\displaystyle\;+\frac{1}{2N}\tr[(v\ast\varrho_{\gamma_{t}}+v\sharp\gamma_{t})\gamma_{t}]+\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}v(x-y)|\alpha_{t}(x,y)+\phi_{t}(x)\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

(3.4)

are conserved along the evolution, i.e.
n(\phi_{t},\gamma_{t})=n(\phi_{0},\gamma_{0})
,
\mathcal{E}(\phi_{t},\gamma_{t},\alpha_{t})=\mathcal{E}(\phi_{0},\gamma_{0},\alpha_{0})
, for every
t>0
. Finally, the dynamics is positivity-preserving, that is,

\Gamma_{t}^{(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{t}&\alpha_{t}\\
\overline{\alpha}_{t}&1+\overline{\gamma}_{t}\end{pmatrix}\geq 0

(3.5)

for every
t>0
.

3.2
Approximation of the HFB dynamics

Although we use the HFB equations to define our fluctuation dynamics in Section
4.2
, we ultimately want to show convergence of the full many-body dynamics to a simpler effective dynamics, with
\phi_{t}
solving the time-dependent Hartree equation (
1.32
),
\gamma_{t}=e^{\mathrm{i}\Delta t}\gamma_{0}e^{-\mathrm{i}\Delta t}
evolving freely, and
\alpha_{t}=0
. To reach this goal, we will use the next proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let the pair
(\phi_{0},\gamma_{0})
satisfy Assumption
1.1
with
s\in(0,\infty)
, and let the interaction potential
v
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
. Moreover, let
(\phi_{t},\gamma_{t},\alpha_{t})
denote the solution to the HFB equations (
3.1
) with initial datum
(\phi_{0},\gamma_{0},0)
. Then there exists a constant
c>0
independent of
N
and

such that

\displaystyle\left\|\gamma_{t}-\gamma_{t}^{\mathrm{F}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\lesssim N^{1/2}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/4}(s)t\exp(ct)\,,

\displaystyle\left\|\phi_{t}-\phi_{t}^{\mathrm{H}}\right\|\lesssim T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/4}(s)t\exp(ct)\,,

(3.6)

where
\phi_{t}^{\mathrm{H}}
is the solution to the time-dependent Hartree equation in (
1.32
) with initial condition
\phi_{0}
and
\gamma_{t}^{\mathrm{F}}=e^{\mathrm{i}\Delta t}\gamma_{0}e^{-\mathrm{i}\Delta t}
.

Remark 3.3
.

1.

Our assumptions on
(\phi_{0},\gamma_{0})
and
v
guarantee that the HFB equations are well-posed, see Theorem
3.1
. It is well-known that they also guarantee the global well-posedness of the time-dependent Hartree equation in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and that the
L^{2}
-mass of the solution is preserved along the Hartree flow.

2.

As explained in Section
1.4
, the scaling of the critical temperature with the number of particles is given by
T_{c}(s)\sim N^{1/\alpha}=N^{\frac{2s}{6+3s}}
. In particular
T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/4}(s)\ll N^{1/2}
for every

, showing that the bounds given in (
3.6
) are non-trivial.

3.

It is possible to get better rates than those in (
3.6
) if one compares to a slightly more complicated evolution for the 1-pdm. Indeed, let
\omega_{0}=|\phi_{0}\rangle\langle\phi_{0}|+\gamma_{0}
, and define
\omega_{t}
to be the solution of the Hartree equation (
1.33
) with initial datum
\omega_{t=0}=\omega_{0}
. Then we have the improved bound

\left\|\ |\phi_{t}\rangle\langle\phi_{t}|+\gamma_{t}-\omega_{t}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\lesssim N^{1/2}T_{c}^{1/2}(s)t\exp(ct)\,,

(3.7)

for some constant
c>0
independent of
t
and
N
. As a consequence, one gets a better (indeed, optimal) rate of convergence in (
1.31
) by replacing
|\phi_{t}^{\mathrm{H}}\rangle\langle\phi_{t}^{\mathrm{H}}|+e^{i\Delta t}\gamma_{0}e^{-i\Delta t}
with
\omega_{t}
in the statement, see Remark
1.3
.3.
The estimate (
3.7
) is proven by a suitable adaptation of the Grönwall-type argument given below, but since we do not need it for our main result, we omit the details. We only highlight that the gain from using (
1.33
) is that it allows one to replace the direct term
[v\ast|\phi_{t}|^{2},\gamma_{t}]
appearing on the r.h.s. of (
3.11
), which we can only bound by
N^{1/2}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/4}(s)
(see (
3.21
)), with some simpler terms that can be shown to be of order
N^{1/2}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{1/2}(s)
.

Proof.

We introduce the notation
\theta_{t}=(\phi_{t},\gamma_{t},\alpha_{t})
, and we write the HFB equations as

\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}\theta_{t}=A\theta_{t}+f(\theta_{t})\,,

(3.8)

where

A\theta=\left(-\Delta\phi,\penalty\ [-\Delta,\gamma],\penalty\ [-\Delta,\alpha]_{+}\right)

(3.9)

is a linear operator on
X^{0}
with domain
\mathcal{D}(A)=X^{2}
, and
f\in C(X^{0})
is defined by

\begin{split}f(\theta)=\Big(b(\gamma)\phi+k(\alpha^{\phi})\overline{\phi},\penalty\ [b(\gamma^{\phi}),\gamma]+(k(\alpha^{\phi})\alpha^{*}-\alpha k(\alpha^{\phi})^{*})\gamma,\penalty\ [b(\gamma^{\phi}),\alpha]_{+}+[k(\alpha^{\phi}),\alpha]_{+}+k(\alpha^{\phi})\Big)\,.\end{split}

Combining (
3.8
) and (
1.32
) we get

\displaystyle\phi_{t}-\phi_{t}^{\mathrm{H}}=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mathrm{i}(t-s)\Delta}\left(\left(\frac{1}{N}v\ast|\phi_{s}|^{2}\phi_{s}-\frac{1}{N}v\ast|\phi_{s}^{\mathrm{H}}|^{2}\phi_{s}^{\mathrm{H}}\right)+b(\gamma_{s})\phi_{s}+k(\alpha_{s})\overline{\phi_{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\gamma_{t}-\gamma_{t}^{\mathrm{F}}=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mathrm{i}(t-s)\Delta}\Big([b(\gamma_{s}^{\phi_{s}}),\gamma_{s}]+k(\alpha_{s}^{\phi_{s}})\alpha_{s}^{*}-\alpha_{s}k(\alpha_{s}^{\phi_{s}})^{*}\Big)e^{-\mathrm{i}(t-s)\Delta}\,\mathrm{d}s\,,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mathrm{i}(t-s)(\Delta_{x}+\Delta_{y})}\Big([b(\gamma_{s}^{\phi_{s}}),\alpha_{s}]_{+}+[k(\alpha_{s}^{\phi_{s}}),\gamma_{s}]_{+}+k(\alpha_{s}^{\phi_{s}})\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

(3.12)

Here, we identify
\alpha_{t}
with its kernel
\alpha_{t}(x,y)
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
and we denote by

\Delta_{x}+\Delta_{y}
the Laplacian acting on this space.

Let us start by deriving a bound for the r.h.s. of (
3.10
). We use the identity

\frac{1}{N}v\ast|\phi_{s}|^{2}\phi_{s}-\frac{1}{N}v\ast|\phi_{s}^{\mathrm{H}}|^{2}\phi_{s}^{\mathrm{H}}=\left(\frac{1}{N}v\ast(|\phi_{s}|^{2}-|\phi_{s}^{\mathrm{H}}|^{2})\right)\phi_{s}^{\mathrm{H}}+\left(\frac{1}{N}v\ast|\phi_{s}|^{2}\right)(\phi_{s}-\phi_{s}^{\mathrm{H}})\,,

(3.13)

Young’s inequality, and the conservation of the particle number for the Hartree and HFB equations, to see that

\begin{split}\left\|\frac{1}{N}v\ast|\phi_{s}|^{2}\phi_{s}-\frac{1}{N}v\ast|\phi_{s}^{\mathrm{H}}|^{2}\phi_{s}^{\mathrm{H}}\right\|\lesssim&N^{-1}\|v\|_{\infty}\left(\|\phi_{s}\|^{2}+\|\phi_{s}^{\mathrm{H}}\|^{2}\right)\|\phi_{s}-\phi_{s}^{\mathrm{H}}\|\\
\lesssim&N^{-1}\|v\|_{\infty}\left(\|\phi_{0}\|^{2}+\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\right)\|\phi_{s}-\phi_{s}^{\mathrm{H}}\|\lesssim\|\phi_{s}-\phi_{s}^{\mathrm{H}}\|\,.\end{split}

In the second term on the r.h.s. of (
3.10
) we write

b(\gamma_{s})\phi_{s}=b(\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}})\phi_{s}+b(\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}})\phi_{s}\,,

and we estimate the two terms separately. First, we bound the operator norm of
b(\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}})=N^{-1}v\ast\varrho_{\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}}+N^{-1}v\sharp\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}
. Using Assumption
1.1
(
B
), we see that

\|N^{-1}v\ast\varrho_{\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}}\|=N^{-1}\|v\ast\varrho_{\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}}\|_{\infty}\leq N^{-1}\|v\|_{1}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}(x,x)|\lesssim N^{-1}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/2}(s)\,.

(3.14)

Moreover, for any
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
we can estimate

\begin{split}N^{-1}|(v\sharp\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}})\psi(x)|\leq&N^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v(x-y)|\ |\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}(x,y)|\ |\psi(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\\
\leq&N^{-1}\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}(x,y)|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v(x-y)|\ |\psi(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim N^{-1}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/2}(s)(|v|\ast|\psi|)(x)\,,\end{split}

which implies

N^{-1}\|(v\sharp\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}})\psi\|\lesssim N^{-1}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/2}(s)\|\ |v|\ast|\psi|\ \|\lesssim N^{-1}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/2}(s)\|\psi\|\,.

(3.15)

For the operator norm of

we have the bound

\displaystyle N^{-1}\|v\ast(\varrho_{\gamma_{s}}-\varrho_{\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}})\|=

\displaystyle N^{-1}\|v\ast(\varrho_{\gamma_{s}}-\varrho_{\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}})\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N^{-1}\|v\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\gamma_{s}(x,x)-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}(x,x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim N^{-1}\|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\,,

(3.16)

and

\displaystyle N^{-1}\|v\sharp(\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}})\|\leq

\displaystyle N^{-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}v(x-y)^{2}|\gamma_{s}(x,y)-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}(x,y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N^{-1}\|v\|_{\infty}\|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\lesssim N^{-1}\|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\,,

(3.17)

where in the last step we used the fact that the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of a trace-class operator is bounded by its trace norm.
Collecting (
3.14
)–(
3.17
) we deduce

\displaystyle\|b(\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}})\|\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{-1}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/2}(s)

\displaystyle\|b(\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}})\|\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{-1}\|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\,.

(3.18)

The above bounds and the conservation of the particle number imply

\begin{split}\|b(\gamma_{s})\phi_{s}\|\lesssim&N^{-1/2}\left(\|\phi_{0}\|^{2}+\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\right)^{1/2}\left(N^{-1/2}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/2}(s)\|v\|_{1}+N^{-1/2}\|v\|_{\infty}\|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\right)\\
\lesssim&N^{-1/2}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/2}(s)\|v\|_{1}+N^{-1/2}\|v\|_{\infty}\|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\,.\end{split}

Similarly, we estimate

\|k(\alpha_{s})\phi_{s}\|\leq N^{-1}\|v\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{s}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\|\phi_{s}\|\lesssim N^{-1/2}\|v\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{s}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\,.

We have thus shown that

\|\phi_{t}-\phi_{t}^{\mathrm{H}}\|\lesssim N^{-1/2}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/2}(s)t+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\phi_{s}-\phi_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|+N^{-1/2}\|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}+N^{-1/2}\|\alpha_{s}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

(3.19)

We now turn to the r.h.s. of (
3.11
). Using again the bounds in (
3.18
), we find

\displaystyle\|[b(\gamma_{s}^{\phi_{s}}),\gamma_{s}]\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq

\displaystyle\|b(\gamma_{s}^{\phi_{s}})\gamma_{s}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}+\|\gamma_{s}b(\gamma_{s}^{\phi_{s}})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\Big(\|\gamma_{s}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\left(\|b(\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}})\|+\|b(\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}})\|\right)+\|b(|\phi_{s}\rangle\langle\phi_{s}|)\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}+\|b(|\phi_{s}\rangle\langle\phi_{s}|)\|\ \|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\infty}\|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}+T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/2}(s)\|v\|_{1}

\displaystyle+N^{-1}\|(v\ast|\phi_{s}|^{2})\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}+N^{-1}\|(v\sharp|\phi_{s}\rangle\langle\phi_{s}|)\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}+\|b(|\phi_{s}\rangle\langle\phi_{s}|)\|\ \|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\,.

(3.20)

The first term in the last line is bounded by

\displaystyle N^{-1}\|(v\ast|\phi_{s}|^{2})\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq

\displaystyle N^{-1}\|\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}^{1/2}\|(v\ast|\phi_{s}|^{2})(\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}})^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{-1/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|(v\ast|\phi_{s}|^{2})(x)|^{2}|\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}(x,x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{-1/2}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/4}(s)\|v\ast|\phi_{s}|^{2}\|\lesssim N^{1/2}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/4}(s)\|v\|\lesssim N^{1/2}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/4}(s)\,.

(3.21)

The exchange term can be estimated by

\|v\sharp|\phi_{s}\rangle\langle\phi_{s}|\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\leq\int\hat{v}(p)\|\ |e^{ip\cdot.}\phi_{s}\rangle\langle e^{ip\cdot.}\phi_{s}|\ \|_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}p\leq\|\hat{v}\|_{1}\|\phi_{s}\|^{2},

\begin{split}\|b(|\phi_{s}\rangle\langle\phi_{s}|)\|\ \|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq N^{-1}\left(\|v\ast|\phi_{s}|^{2}\|_{\infty}+\|v\sharp|\phi_{s}\rangle\langle\phi_{s}|\ \|\right)\|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\lesssim\|v\|_{\infty}\|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\,.\end{split}

, which follows from (

\|\gamma_{t}-\gamma_{t}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\lesssim\left(N^{1/2}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/4}(s)+T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/2}(s)+T_{\mathrm{c}}(s)\right)t+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}+\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

(3.23)

\displaystyle\|b(\gamma_{s}^{\phi_{s}})\|\leq

\displaystyle N^{-1}\|v\ast(\varrho_{\gamma_{s}}+|\phi_{s}|^{2})\|_{\infty}+N^{-1}\|v\sharp(\gamma_{s}+|\phi_{s}\rangle\langle\phi_{s}|)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\|[b(\gamma_{s}^{\phi_{s}}),\gamma_{s}]\|\leq 2\|b(\gamma_{s}^{\phi_{s}})\|\ \|\gamma_{s}\|\lesssim\left(\|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|+T_{\mathrm{c}}(s)\right)\,.

\|k(\alpha_{s}^{\phi_{s}})\alpha_{s}^{*}\|\lesssim\|k(\alpha_{s}^{\phi_{s}})\alpha_{s}^{*}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\lesssim\|\alpha_{s}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{t}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\|[b(\gamma_{s}^{\phi_{s}}),\alpha_{s}]_{+}+[k(\alpha_{s}^{\phi_{s}}),\gamma_{s}]_{+}+k(\alpha_{s}^{\phi_{s}})\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{c}}(s)t+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{s}^{\mathrm{F}}\|+\|\alpha_{s}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s

holds.

Let us define the norm

\|\theta\|_{*}=T_{\mathrm{c}}^{-3/4}(s)\|\phi\|+N^{-1/2}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{-3/4}(s)\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}+T_{\mathrm{c}}^{-1}(s)(\|\gamma\|+\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}})\,.

Lemma 3.5

Proof.

We use Duhamel’s formula to write

When take the

\begin{split}\|\widehat{\phi}_{t}\|_{1}\leq&\|\widehat{\phi}_{0}\|_{1}+N^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\|\hat{v}\ \widehat{|\phi_{s}|^{2}}\|_{1}\|\widehat{\phi}_{s}\|_{1}\,\mathrm{d}s\\

which proves the claim.

\displaystyle\exp(c\exp(ct))\,,

(3.29)

3.5

\begin{split}\left\|k(\alpha^{\phi_{s}})\alpha_{s}^{*}-\alpha_{s}k(\alpha^{\phi_{s}})^{*}\right\|_{\mathrm{d}}\leq&N^{-1}\|v\|_{1}\left(\|\alpha_{s}\|_{\mathrm{d}}+\|\phi_{s}\|_{\mathrm{d}}^{2}\right)\|\alpha_{s}\|_{\mathrm{d}}\\

3.29

\displaystyle\|\gamma_{t}\|_{\mathrm{d}}\leq

\displaystyle\|\gamma_{t}^{\mathrm{F}}\|_{\mathrm{d}}+C\exp(ct)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\gamma_{s}\|_{\mathrm{d}}+\|\alpha_{s}\|_{\mathrm{d}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s

which implies

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{t}\|_{\mathrm{d}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\exp(ct)+\exp(ct)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\gamma_{s}\|_{\mathrm{d}}+\|\alpha_{s}\|_{\mathrm{d}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s

(at

5.3
Weak bounds on the original dynamics

In order to prove that
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}^{\textrm{fluct}}
remains close to the full fluctuation dynamics, we need some rough a-priori bounds on the growth of the number of particles operator when conjugated with
\mathcal{U}^{\textrm{fluct}}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
v
be an interaction potential satisfying the same assumptions as in Theorem
1.2
, and let
\phi\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
\gamma\in\mathcal{H}^{3,1}
with
n(\phi,\gamma)=N
. For
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
t>s>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(t,s)^{*}\mathcal{N}^{2j}\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(t,s)\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(\|U_{t}\|^{2}+\|V_{t}\|^{2}\big)^{2j}\big(\|U_{s}\|^{2}+\|V_{s}\|^{2}\big)^{2j}\ \mathcal{N}^{2j}

\displaystyle+\left(1+\big(\|U_{t}\|^{2}+\|V_{t}\|^{2}\big)^{2j}\right)N^{2j}\,.

(5.33)

Proof.

We claim that the bounds

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{t}^{*}\mathcal{N}^{2j}\mathcal{W}_{t}\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{2j}+N^{2j}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{t}\mathcal{N}^{2j}\mathcal{W}_{t}^{*}\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{2j}+N^{2j}

(5.34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{t}^{*}\mathcal{N}^{2j}\mathcal{T}_{t}\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(\|U_{t}\|^{2}+\|V_{t}\|^{2}\big)^{2j}\ \mathcal{N}^{2j}+N^{2j}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{t}\mathcal{N}^{2j}\mathcal{T}_{t}^{*}\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(\|U_{t}\|^{2}+\|V_{t}\|^{2}\big)^{2j}\ \mathcal{N}^{2j}+N^{2j}

(5.35)

hold for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
t>0
. Assuming these bounds for the moment, (
5.33
) is easily proved by a repeated application of (
5.34
), (
5.35
). Indeed, by the definition of the fluctuation dynamics we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(t,s)^{*}\mathcal{N}^{2j}\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(t,s)=&\mathcal{T}_{s}^{*}\mathcal{W}_{s}^{*}e^{it\mathcal{L}_{N}(t-s)}\mathcal{W}_{t}\mathcal{T}_{t}\mathcal{N}^{2j}\mathcal{T}_{t}^{*}\mathcal{W}_{t}^{*}e^{-it\mathcal{L}_{N}(t-s)}\mathcal{W}_{s}\mathcal{T}_{s}\\
\lesssim&\big(\|U_{t}\|^{2}+\|V_{t}\|^{2}\big)^{2j}\ \mathcal{T}_{s}^{*}\mathcal{W}_{s}^{*}e^{it\mathcal{L}_{N}(t-s)}\mathcal{W}_{t}\mathcal{N}^{2j}\mathcal{W}_{t}^{*}e^{-it\mathcal{L}_{N}(t-s)}\mathcal{W}_{s}\mathcal{T}_{s}+N^{2j}\\
\lesssim&\big(\|U_{t}\|^{2}+\|V_{t}\|^{2}\big)^{2j}\ \mathcal{T}_{s}^{*}\mathcal{N}^{2j}\mathcal{T}_{s}+\big(1+\|U_{t}\|^{2}+\|V_{t}\|^{2}\big)^{2j}N^{2j}\\
\lesssim&\big(\|U_{t}\|^{2}+\|V_{t}\|^{2}\big)^{2j}\big(\|U_{s}\|^{2}+\|V_{s}\|^{2}\big)^{2j}\mathcal{N}^{2j}+(1+\big(\|U_{t}\|^{2}+\|V_{t}\|^{2}\big)^{2j})N^{2j}\,,\end{split}

where in the second estimate we also used the fact that the Liouvillian
\mathcal{L}_{N}
defined in (
1.30
) commutes with the number of particles operator. Thus we just need to prove (
5.34
), (
5.35
). The bounds in (
5.34
) follow by a straightforward adaptation of the arguments in
[
65
, Lemma 3.6]
.

The proof of (
5.35
) is also inspired by the proof of the same lemma, but since the adaptation is less straightforward in this case, we provide the details. We start with the first bound in (
5.35
), and we proceed by induction on
j\in\mathbb{N}
. For the base case, we use (
4.12
) to compute
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{*}\mathcal{N}\mathcal{T}_{t}
, bring all terms to normal order, and find

\mathcal{T}_{t}^{*}\mathcal{N}\mathcal{T}_{t}=\int\left[a^{*}(U_{t,\mathbf{x}})a(U_{t,\mathbf{x}})+a^{*}(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})a(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})+a^{*}(U_{t,\mathbf{x}})a^{*}(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})+a(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})a(U_{t,\mathbf{x}})+(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}},\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})\right]\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+5\,,

(5.36)

where
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the inner product defined in (
5.15
). To obtain a bound for
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{*}\mathcal{N}^{2}\mathcal{T}_{t}
, we square (
5.36
) and apply Cauchy-Schwartz. This yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{t}^{*}\mathcal{N}^{2}\mathcal{T}_{t}\lesssim

\displaystyle 1+\int\Big[a^{*}(U_{t,\mathbf{x}})a(U_{t,\mathbf{x}})a^{*}(U_{t,\mathbf{y}})a(U_{t,\mathbf{y}})+a^{*}(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})a(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})a^{*}(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{y}}})a(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{y}}})+(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}},\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{y}}},\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{y}}})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+a^{*}(U_{t,\mathbf{x}})a^{*}(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})a(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{y}}})a(U_{t,\mathbf{y}})+a(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{y}}})a(U_{t,\mathbf{y}})a^{*}(U_{t,\mathbf{x}})a^{*}(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\,.

(5.37)

The first two terms in the integral are bounded by
(\|U_{t}\|^{4}+\|V_{t}\|^{4})\mathcal{N}^{2}
, and we use

\int(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}},\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[\widetilde{\gamma}_{t}((x,\ell),(x,\ell))+\widetilde{\gamma}_{t}((x,r),(x,r))\right]\,\mathrm{d}x=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\gamma_{t}(x,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2N

(5.38)

to see that the third term in the integral is bounded by
4N^{2}
. To bound the first term in the second line of (
5.37
) we write it as

\int a^{*}(U_{t,\mathbf{x}})a(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{y}}})a^{*}(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})a(U_{t,\mathbf{y}})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}+\int a^{*}(U_{t,\mathbf{x}})a(U_{t,\mathbf{y}})(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{y}}},\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\,.

Using Cauchy-Schwartz and (
5.38
), we check that the first term is bounded by
\|U_{t}\|^{2}\|V_{t}\|^{2}\mathcal{N}^{2}+\|U_{t}\|^{2}N
. The second term equals the second quantization of the operator
U_{t}V_{t}^{*}V_{t}U_{t}^{*}
. Accordingly, it is bounded by
\|U_{t}\|^{2}\|V_{t}\|^{2}\mathcal{N}
. To obtain a bound for the last term on the r.h.s. of (
5.37
), we write it as

\int\Big[a(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})a^{*}(U_{t,\mathbf{y}})a(U_{t,\mathbf{y}})a^{*}(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})+a^{*}(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})a(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{y}}})(U_{t,\mathbf{y}},U_{t,\mathbf{x}})+(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{y}}},\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})\ (U_{t,\mathbf{y}},U_{t,\mathbf{x}})\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\,.

(5.39)

An application of Cauchy-Schwartz shows that the first term is bounded by
2\|U_{t}\|^{2}\|V_{t}\|^{2}\mathcal{N}^{2}+\|V_{t}\|^{2}N
. The second operator is the second quantization of
V_{t}\overline{U_{t}^{*}U}_{t}\overline{V_{t}^{*}}
, and is therefore also bounded by
\|U_{t}\|^{2}\|V_{t}\|^{2}\mathcal{N}
. A computation that uses (
4.5
) and (
5.16
) shows that the last term in (
5.38
) can be written as

\tr[V_{t}^{*}V_{t}U_{t}^{*}U_{t}]=\tr[\widetilde{\gamma}_{t}(1+\widetilde{\gamma}_{t})]\leq 2N+\left(\tr\widetilde{\gamma}_{t}\right)^{2}\leq 2N+4N^{2}.

To obtain the second bound we used
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}
. Collecting the above estimates we find

\mathcal{T}_{t}^{*}\mathcal{N}^{2}\mathcal{T}_{t}\lesssim\left(\|U_{t}\|^{2}+\|V_{t}\|^{2}\right)^{2}\mathcal{N}^{2}+N^{2}\,.

(5.40)

It remains to consider the induction step. Let us denote
\mathrm{ad}_{A}(B)=[B,A]
and
X_{t}=\mathcal{T}_{t}^{*}\mathcal{N}\mathcal{T}_{t}
. From (
5.36
) we have

\mathrm{ad}^{n}_{\mathcal{N}}(X_{t})=(-2)^{n}\int a^{*}(U_{t,\mathbf{x}})a^{*}(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+2^{n}\int a(\overline{V_{t,\mathbf{x}}})a(U_{t,\mathbf{x}})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,.

Therefore, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
we can bound

|\mathrm{ad}^{n}_{\mathcal{N}}(X_{t})|^{2}\lesssim\left(\|U_{t}\|^{4}+\|U_{t}\|^{4}\right)\mathcal{N}^{2}+N^{2}\,.

(5.41)

From the induction assumption, we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{t}^{*}\mathcal{N}^{2j+2}\mathcal{T}_{t}=X_{t}\mathcal{T}_{t}^{*}\mathcal{N}^{2j}\mathcal{T}_{t}X_{t}\lesssim

\displaystyle X_{t}\left(C_{t}^{2j}\ \mathcal{N}^{2j}+N^{2j}\right)X_{t}\lesssim C_{t}^{2j}X_{t}\mathcal{N}^{2j}X_{t}+C_{t}^{2}\mathcal{N}^{2}N^{2j}+N^{2j+2}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{t}^{2j}X_{t}\mathcal{N}^{2j}X_{t}+C_{t}^{2j+2}\mathcal{N}^{2j+2}+N^{2j+2}\,,

(5.42)

where we defined
C_{t}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}}}=\|U_{t}\|^{2}+\|V_{t}\|^{2}
.
With Cauchy-Schwartz, (
5.40
), (
5.41
) and the commutator expansion

[A^{n},B]=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{m}A^{m}\mathrm{ad}_{A}^{n-m}(B)\,,

we obtain

\displaystyle X_{t}\mathcal{N}^{2j}X_{t}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathcal{N}^{j}X_{t}^{2}\mathcal{N}^{j}+2\left|[\mathcal{N}^{j},X_{t}]\right|^{2}\leq 2\mathcal{N}^{j}X_{t}^{2}\mathcal{N}^{j}+4^{j+1}\sum_{n=0}^{j-1}\mathcal{N}^{n}\left|\mathrm{ad}^{j-n}_{\mathcal{N}}(X_{t})\right|^{2}\mathcal{N}^{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{t}^{2}\mathcal{N}^{2j+2}+N^{2}\mathcal{N}^{2j}\,.

(5.43)

Inserting (
5.43
) into (
5.42
) we get

\mathcal{T}_{t}^{*}\mathcal{N}^{2j+2}\mathcal{T}_{t}\leq C_{t}^{2j+2}\mathcal{N}^{2j+2}+\mathcal{N}^{2j+2}\,,

which proves the induction step.

Finally, the second bound in (
5.35
) follows from the first bound and the observation that
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{*}
is also a Bogoliubov tranformation, implementing the symplectomorphism

T_{t}^{-1}=\mathcal{S}T_{t}^{*}\mathcal{S}=\begin{pmatrix}U_{t}^{*}&-V_{t}^{*}\\
-\overline{V_{t}^{*}}&\overline{U_{t}^{*}}\end{pmatrix}\,.

This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

shows that

\displaystyle\int|v(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|\ \big\langle\xi_{t},\mathcal{N}\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(t,s)a^{*}(U_{s,\mathbf{x}})a^{*}(U_{s,\mathbf{y}})a(U_{s,\mathbf{y}})a(U_{s,\mathbf{x}})\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(t,s)^{*}\mathcal{N}\xi_{t}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\|v\|_{\infty}\|U_{s}\|^{4}\big\langle\xi_{t},\mathcal{N}\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(t,s)\mathcal{N}^{2}\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(t,s)^{*}\mathcal{N}\xi_{t}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\lesssim\|v\|_{\infty}C_{s}^{2}\left[C_{s}^{2}C_{t}^{2}\langle\xi_{t},\mathcal{N}^{4}\xi_{t}\rangle+C_{t}^{2}N^{2}\langle\xi_{t},\mathcal{N}^{2}\xi_{t}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\lesssim\|v\|_{\infty}C_{s}^{2}\left[\left(C_{s}^{2}C_{t}^{6}C_{0}^{4}+C_{t}^{6}C_{0}^{4}\right)\langle\xi,\mathcal{N}^{4}\xi\rangle+\left(C_{s}^{2}C_{t}^{6}+C_{t}^{4}\right)N^{4}\right]\,.

(5.47)

To bound the second factor, we use

\mathds{1}(\mathcal{N}>N^{\eta})\leq(\mathcal{N}/N^{\eta})^{k}

(5.48)

with
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Using (
5.48
) and Lemma
5.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int|v(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|\ \big\langle\xi_{\eta,s},\mathds{1}(\mathcal{N}>N^{\eta})a^{*}(U_{s,\mathbf{x}})a^{*}(U_{s,\mathbf{y}})a(U_{s,\mathbf{y}})a(U_{s,\mathbf{x}})\mathds{1}(\mathcal{N}>N^{\eta})\xi_{\eta,s}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt\leq\|v\|_{\infty}\|U_{s}\|^{4}N^{-\eta k}\big\langle\xi_{\eta,s},\mathcal{N}^{k+2}\xi_{\eta,s}\big\rangle\leq\|v\|_{\infty}\|U_{s}\|^{4}N^{-\eta k}\exp(c\exp(c\exp(cs)))\langle\xi,\mathcal{N}^{k+2}\xi\rangle\,.

(5.49)

We note that Lemma
4.1
implies
C_{t}\lesssim T_{\mathrm{c}}\exp(ct)
. When combined with (
5.47
) and (
5.49
), this gives

\eqref{eq:comparison_1a}\lesssim\frac{\|v\|_{\infty}T_{\mathrm{c}}}{N^{1+\eta k/2}}\exp(c\exp(c\exp(ct)))\langle\xi,\mathcal{N}^{k+2}\xi\rangle^{1/2}\left[T_{\mathrm{c}}^{7}\langle\xi,\mathcal{N}^{4}\xi\rangle^{1/2}+T_{\mathrm{c}}^{5}N^{2}\right]\,.

(5.50)

When inserted into (
5.45
), the other terms in
\mathcal{I}_{N,t}^{(1,\eta),>}
can be estimated in the same way by the r.h.s. of (
5.50
).

Next, we consider the terms arising from
\mathcal{I}_{N,t}^{(2,\eta),>}
. We start by considering the term with four creation operators. We bound its contribution to (
5.45
) by

\displaystyle\frac{2}{N}\int_{0}^{t}\int\Big|v(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\big\langle\xi_{t},\mathcal{N}\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(t,s)a^{*}(U_{s,\mathbf{x}})a^{*}(U_{s,\mathbf{y}})a^{*}(\overline{V_{s,\mathbf{y}}})a^{*}(\overline{V_{s,\mathbf{x}}})\mathds{1}(\mathcal{N}>N^{\eta})\xi_{\eta,s}\big\rangle\Big|\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\frac{2}{N}\int_{0}^{t}\Bigg(\int|v(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|\ \big\langle\xi_{t},\mathcal{N}\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(t,s)a^{*}(U_{s,\mathbf{x}})a^{*}(U_{s,\mathbf{y}})a(U_{s,\mathbf{y}})a(U_{s,\mathbf{x}})\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(t,s)^{*}\mathcal{N}\xi_{t}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\Bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\Bigg(\int|v(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|\ \big\langle\xi_{\eta,s},\mathds{1}(\mathcal{N}>N^{\eta})a(\overline{V_{s,\mathbf{x}}})a(\overline{V_{s,\mathbf{y}}})a^{*}(\overline{V_{s,\mathbf{y}}})a^{*}(\overline{V_{s,\mathbf{x}}})\mathds{1}(\mathcal{N}>N^{\eta})\xi_{\eta,s}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\Bigg)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

(5.51)

A bound for the first factor has been obtained in (
5.47
). To estimate the other factor, we use the identity (
5.26
). The normal-ordered term has already been considered in (
5.49
). We use Proposition
3.4
and estimates similar as the ones above to bound the remaining contributions by

\|v\|_{1}\exp(c\exp(c\exp(cs)))\left[T_{\mathrm{c}}^{5/2}N^{-\eta(k+1)}+T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/2}N^{1-\eta(k+2)}\right]\langle\xi,\mathcal{N}^{k+2}\xi\rangle\,.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:comparison_2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\|v\|_{1}+\|v\|_{\infty}}{N^{1+\eta k/2}}\exp(c\exp(ct\exp(ct)))\left[T_{\mathrm{c}}^{7}\langle\xi,\mathcal{N}^{4}\xi\rangle^{1/2}+T_{\mathrm{c}}^{5}N^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\times\Big[T_{\mathrm{c}}+T_{\mathrm{c}}^{5/4}N^{-\eta/2}+N^{-\eta}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/4}N^{1/2}\Big]\langle\xi,\mathcal{N}^{k+2}\xi\rangle^{1/2}\,.

The remaining terms in
\mathcal{I}_{N,t}^{(2,\eta),>}
can be estimated analogously.

We are left to consider the contributions from
\mathcal{I}_{N,t}^{(3,\eta),>}
. We insert the term with three creation operators into (
5.51
) and find

\displaystyle\Bigg|\frac{3}{N}\int_{0}^{t}\int v(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\boldsymbol{\phi}_{s}(\mathbf{x})\ \big\langle\xi_{t},\mathcal{N}\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(t,s)a^{*}(U_{s,\mathbf{x}})a^{*}(U_{s,\mathbf{y}})a^{*}(\overline{V_{s,\mathbf{y}}})\mathds{1}(\mathcal{N}>N^{\eta})\xi_{\eta,s}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigg|

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\leq\frac{3}{N}\int_{0}^{t}\Bigg(\int|v(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|\ \big\|a(U_{s,\mathbf{y}})a(U_{s,\mathbf{x}})\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{fluct}}(t,s)^{*}\mathcal{N}\xi_{t}\big\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\Bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\times\Bigg(\int|v(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})|\ |\boldsymbol{\phi}_{s}(\mathbf{x})|^{2}\big\|a^{*}(\overline{V_{s,\mathbf{y}}})\mathds{1}(\mathcal{N}>N^{\eta})\xi_{\eta,s}\big\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\Bigg)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\lesssim\frac{(\|v\|_{1}+\|v\|_{\infty})}{N^{1/2+\eta k/2}}\exp(c\exp(c\exp(ct)))\left[T_{\mathrm{c}}^{7}\langle\xi,\mathcal{N}^{7}\xi\rangle^{1/2}+T_{\mathrm{c}}^{5}N^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\left[T_{\mathrm{c}}^{1/2}N^{-\eta/2}+T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/4}N^{-\eta}\right]\langle\xi,\mathcal{N}^{k+2}\xi\rangle^{1/2}\,.

The remaining terms in
\mathcal{I}_{N,t}^{(3,\eta),>}
can be estimated in a similar way by the r.h.s. of the above equation.

Collecting the above bounds, and using
N^{-1/2}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/4}=N^{-1/2}T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/4}(s)\lesssim 1
(which follows from the condition
s\leq 3/2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\big|\big\langle\xi_{t},\mathcal{N}\big(\xi_{t}-\xi_{\eta,t}\big)\big\rangle\big|\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{-1/2-\eta k/2}\exp(c\exp(c\exp(ct)))\left(T_{\mathrm{c}}^{7}\langle\xi,\mathcal{N}^{4}\xi\rangle^{1/2}+T_{\mathrm{c}}^{5}N^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(T_{\mathrm{c}}N^{-1/2}+T_{\mathrm{c}}^{1/2}N^{-\eta/2}+T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/4}N^{-\eta}\right)\big\langle\xi,\mathcal{N}^{k+2}\xi\big\rangle^{1/2}\,.

∎

5.5
Proof of Proposition
4.3

We are now ready to prove our final bound for the growth of the number of excitations in the fluctuation vector. For
\eta\in(0,1/7]
we have, by Proposition
5.3
and Lemma
5.5
,

\displaystyle\big\langle\xi_{t},\mathcal{N}\xi_{t}\big\rangle\leq

\displaystyle\big\langle\xi_{\eta,t},\mathcal{N}\xi_{\eta,t}\big\rangle+\Big|\big\langle\xi_{\eta,t},\mathcal{N}(\xi_{t}-\xi_{\eta,t})\big\rangle\Big|+\Big|\big\langle(\xi_{t}-\xi_{\eta,t}),\mathcal{N}\xi_{t}\big\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\exp(c\exp(c\exp(ct)))\big\langle\xi,\mathcal{N}\xi\big\rangle+N^{-1/2-\eta k/2}\exp(c\exp(c\exp(ct)))

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(T_{\mathrm{c}}^{7}\langle\xi,\mathcal{N}^{4}\xi\rangle^{1/2}+T_{\mathrm{c}}^{5}N^{2}\right)\left(T_{\mathrm{c}}N^{-1/2}+T_{\mathrm{c}}^{1/2}N^{-\eta/2}+T_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/4}N^{-\eta}\right)\big\langle\xi,\mathcal{N}^{k+2}\xi\big\rangle^{1/2}\,.

Choosing
\eta=1/7
and using
T_{\mathrm{c}}=T_{\mathrm{c}}(s)\leq T_{\mathrm{c}}(3/2)\lesssim N^{2/7}
we arrive at (
4.43
).

Quantum Wasserstein distance based on an optimization over separable states
Géza Tóth, József Pitrik

Abstract
We define the quantum Wasserstein distance such that the optimization of the coupling is carried out over bipartite separable states rather than bipartite quantum states in general, and examine its properties. Surprisingly, we find that the self-distance is related to the quantum Fisher information. We present a transport map corresponding to an optimal bipartite separable state. We discuss how the quantum Wasserstein distance introduced is connected to criteria detecting quantum entanglement. We define variance-like quantities that can be obtained from the quantum Wasserstein distance by replacing the minimization over quantum states by a maximization. We extend our results to a family of generalized quantum Fisher information quantities. Dedicated to the memory of Dénes Petz on the occasion of his 70 th birthday.

Entanglement theory

Next, we review entanglement theory
[
55
,
56
,
57
]

[

\sum_{k}p_{k}|\Psi_{k}\rangle\langle\Psi_{k}|\otimes|\Phi_{k}\rangle\langle\Phi_{k}|,

are for the probabilities,

The mixture of two separable states is also separable, thus the set

A relevant subset of separable states are the symmetric separable states, which can be given as

\sum_{k}p_{k}|\Psi_{k}\rangle\langle\Psi_{k}|\otimes|\Psi_{k}\rangle\langle\Psi_{k}|.

The mixture of two symmetric separable states is also symmetric and separable, thus the set
S^{\prime}
is also convex. Clearly

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{S}.

(20)

For such states for the expectation value

\langle{P_{s}}\rangle=1

holds, where

is the projector to the symmetric subspace defined by the basis vectors

Equivalently, any state in

where

is the flip operator for which

(23)

for all

The set of quantum states with a positive partial transpose (PPT)

consists of the states for which

\varrho^{T_{k}}\geq 0

holds for

where

is a partial transposition according to the

\mathcal{P}
is clearly a convex set. Moreover, all separable states given in Eq. (

Finally, we will define the set of symmetric PPT states

States in this set are symmetric, thus Eqs. (

Convex roofs and concave roofs play also a central role in entanglement theory. The entanglement of formation is defined as a convex roof over pure components of the von Neumann entropy of the reduced state

Optimization over the two-copy space

In this section, we first review the formalism that maps the optimization over the decompositions of the density matrix to an optimization of an operator expectation value over bipartite symmetric separable quantum states with given marginals
[
100
]

2

For a reference in the literature, see Eq. (67) in Ref.
[
62
]
and Eqs. (S23)-(S25) in Ref.
[
100
]
. In Eqs. (S23)-(S25) in Ref.
[
100
]
, there are some additional constraints for the expectation values
\langle{O_{i}}\rangle_{\varrho},
where
O_{i}
are some operators. For our discussion, they are not needed.
. Then, we show that the same result is obtained if the optimization is carried out over general separable quantum states rather than symmetric separable states.

We start from writing the variance of a pure state
|\Psi\rangle
as an operator expectation value acting on two copies as

(\Delta H)^{2}_{\Psi}={\rm Tr}(\Omega|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|\otimes|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|),

(60)

where we define the operator

\Omega=H^{2}\otimes\mathbbm{1}-H\otimes H.

(61)

Based on Eq. (
60
), the expression of the quantum Fisher information given in Eq. (
13
) can be rewritten as
[
62
,
100
]

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}[\varrho,H]=

\displaystyle 4\min_{\{p_{k},|\Psi_{k}\rangle\}}\;

\displaystyle\sum_{k}p_{k}{\rm Tr}[\Omega(|\Psi_{k}\rangle\langle\Psi_{k}|)^{\otimes 2}],

s. t.

\displaystyle\varrho=\sum_{k}p_{k}|\Psi_{k}\rangle\langle\Psi_{k}|.

(62)

The sum can be moved into the trace and we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}[\varrho,H]=

\displaystyle 4\min_{\{p_{k},|\Psi_{k}\rangle\}}\;

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left\{\Omega\left[\sum_{k}p_{k}(|\Psi_{k}\rangle\langle\Psi_{k}|)^{\otimes 2}\right]\right\},

s. t.

\displaystyle\varrho=\sum_{k}p_{k}|\Psi_{k}\rangle\langle\Psi_{k}|.

(63)

On the right-hand side in Eq. (
63
) in the square bracket, we can recognize a mixed state living in the two-copy space

\varrho_{12}=\sum_{k}p_{k}|\Psi_{k}\rangle\langle\Psi_{k}|\otimes|\Psi_{k}\rangle\langle\Psi_{k}|,

(64)

States given in Eq. (
64
) are symmetric separable states, thus
\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}.
We can rewrite the optimization in Eq. (
63
) as an optmization over symmetric separable states given in Eq. (
64
) as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}[\varrho,H]=\min_{\varrho_{12}}\;

\displaystyle 4{\rm Tr}(\Omega\varrho_{12}),

s. t.

\displaystyle\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime},

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{2}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho.

(65)

Due to the optimization over symmetric separable states,
{\rm Tr}_{1}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho
is fulfilled without adding it as an explicit constraint.

Equation (
65
) contains an optimization over symmetric separable states, which cannot be computed directly. However, we can consider an optimization over a larger set, the set of symmetric PPT states, which leads to an expression that can be obtained numerically using semidefinite programming
[
101
]

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{({\rm PPT})}[\varrho,H]=\min_{\varrho_{12}}\;

\displaystyle 4{\rm Tr}(\Omega\varrho_{12}),

s. t.

\displaystyle\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime},

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{2}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho.

(66)

In general, the relation

{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{({\rm PPT})}[\varrho,H]\leq{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}[\varrho,H]

(67)

holds, while for two qubits we have an equality, since for that system size
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\mathcal{P}^{\prime},
as we discussed in Sec.
3
.

Let us now change the operator to be optimized, making it permutationally symmetric. Equation (
65
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}[\varrho,H]=\min_{\varrho_{12}}\;

\displaystyle 2{\rm Tr}[(H\otimes\mathbbm{1}-\mathbbm{1}\otimes H)^{2}\varrho_{12}],

s. t.

\displaystyle\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime},

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{2}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho.

(68)

We will now show that the expression in Eq. (
4
) remains true if we change the set over which we have to optimize to the set of separable states.

Observation 1.
The quantum Fisher information can be obtained as an optimization over separable states as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}[\varrho,H]=\min_{\varrho_{12}}\;

\displaystyle 2{\rm Tr}[(H\otimes\mathbbm{1}-\mathbbm{1}\otimes H)^{2}\varrho_{12}],

s. t.

\displaystyle\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{S},

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{2}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho,

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{1}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho.

(69)

Proof.
Using

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}[(H\otimes\mathbbm{1}-\mathbbm{1}\otimes H)^{2}\varrho_{12}]

\displaystyle\quad\quad=2{\rm Tr}(H^{2}\varrho)-2{\rm Tr}[(H\otimes H)\varrho_{12}],

(70)

the right-hand side of Eq. (
69
) can be reformulated as

\displaystyle 4{\rm Tr}(H^{2}\varrho)-4\max_{\varrho_{12}}\;

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}[(H\otimes H)\varrho_{12}],

s. t.

\displaystyle\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{S},

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{2}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho,

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{1}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho.

(71)

Due to the permutational invariance of
H\otimes H
and that both marginals must be equal to
\varrho,
if a separable state given in Eq. (
18
) maximizes the correlation
\langle{H\otimes H}\rangle
, then the separable state

\sum_{k}p_{k}|\Phi_{k}\rangle\langle\Phi_{k}|\otimes|\Psi_{k}\rangle\langle\Psi_{k}|.

(72)

also maximizes the correlation. Then, the mixture of the above two separable states also maximize the correlation with the given marginals, which can always be written as

\varrho_{12}=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\tilde{p}_{k}|\tilde{\Psi}_{k}\rangle\langle\tilde{\Psi}_{k}|\otimes|\tilde{\Psi}_{\pi_{k}}\rangle\langle\tilde{\Psi}_{\pi_{k}}|,

(73)

where
\pi_{k}
for
k=1,2,..,M
is a permutation of
1,2,...,M
for some
M.
Based on our arguments, it is sufficient to look for the separable state that maximizes
\langle{H\otimes H}\rangle
in the form Eq. (
73
), and
\tilde{p}_{k}\geq 0,

\sum_{k}\tilde{p}_{k}=1.
Then, the correlation can be given as

{\rm Tr}[(H\otimes H)\varrho_{12}]=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\tilde{p}_{k}h_{k}h_{\pi_{k}},

(74)

where we define the expectation values of
H
on
|\tilde{\Psi}_{k}\rangle
as

h_{k}={\rm Tr}(H|\tilde{\Psi}_{k}\rangle\langle\tilde{\Psi}_{k}|).

(75)

Based on the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\tilde{p}_{k}h_{k}h_{\pi_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{k}\tilde{p}_{k}h_{k}^{2}\sum_{k}\tilde{p}_{k}h_{\pi_{k}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left(\sum_{k}\tilde{p}_{k}h_{k}^{2},\sum_{k}\tilde{p}_{k}h_{\pi_{k}}^{2}\right).

(76)

Thus, when we maximize
{\rm Tr}[(H\otimes H)\varrho_{12}]
over separable states with the constraints for the marginals, the maximum is taken by a symmetric separable state given in Eq. (
64
). In this case both inequalities are saturated in Eq. (
76
).
\hfill\blacksquare

Based on Eq. (
15
), we can obtain the variance also as a result of an optimization over a two-copy space.

Observation 2.
The variance can also be obtained as an optimization over symmetric separable states as

\displaystyle(\Delta H)^{2}=\max_{\varrho_{12}}\;

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}[(H\otimes\mathbbm{1}-\mathbbm{1}\otimes H)^{2}\varrho_{12}],

s. t.

\displaystyle\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime},

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{2}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho,

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{1}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho.

(77)

Example 1.
Note that if we replace the set of symmetric separable states by the set of separable states in Eq. (
77
), then we get a different quantity, which can be larger in some cases than
(\Delta H)^{2}.
Let us see a concrete example. For instance, if
\varrho=\mathbbm{1}/2
then among symmetric separable states, the maximum is
1
and it is attained by the state

\varrho_{12}=\frac{1}{4}\left(|00\rangle\langle 00|+|11\rangle\langle 11|+2|\Psi^{+}\rangle\langle\Psi^{+}|\right),

(78)

where the Bell state
|\Psi^{+}\rangle
is given as

|\Psi^{+}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|01\rangle+|10\rangle).

(79)

The state given in Eq. (
78
) can be decomposed into the mixture of symmetric product states as

\frac{1}{4}(|\alpha_{+1}\rangle\langle\alpha_{+1}|+|\alpha_{-1}\rangle\langle\alpha_{-1}|+|\alpha_{+i}\rangle\langle\alpha_{+i}|+|\alpha_{-i}\rangle\langle\alpha_{-i}|),

(80)

where the symmetric product state is defined as

|\alpha_{q}\rangle=(|0\rangle+q|1\rangle)\otimes(|0\rangle+q|1\rangle).

(81)

Among separable states, the maximum is
2
and it is attained by
the state

\varrho_{12}=\frac{1}{2}\left(|01\rangle\langle 01|+|10\rangle\langle 10|\right).

(82)

Note that
\varrho_{12}
given in Eq. (
82
) is not symmetric.

5
Quantum Wasserstein distance based on a separable coupling

Next, we will connect our result in Observation
4
to results available in the literature mentioned in the introduction. We will consider the quantum Wasserstein distance such that the optimization takes place over separable states rather than over general bipartite quantum states. We will consider such modifications of
D_{\rm GMPC}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2}
and
D_{\rm DPT}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2},
and examine their properties.

Our first finding concerning the GMPC distance is the following.

Definition 3.
Modifying the definition in Eq. (
1

\displaystyle D_{\rm GMPC,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2}\quad

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\min_{\varrho_{12}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\;

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}[(H_{n}\otimes\mathbbm{1}-\mathbbm{1}\otimes H_{n})^{2}\varrho_{12}],

s. t.

\displaystyle\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{S},

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{1}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho,

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{2}(\varrho_{12})=\sigma.

(83)

We can see immediately two relevant properties of the newly defined distance. Observation
4
showed that for
N=1
for a given
H_{1}

D_{\rm GMPC,sep}(\varrho,\varrho)^{2}=\frac{1}{4}{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}[\varrho,H_{1}]

(84)

holds. Moreover, based on Eqs. (
1
) and (
5

D_{\rm GMPC}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2}\leq D_{\rm GMPC,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2},

(85)

since on the left-hand side of Eq. (

84
) and (
85

for the self-distance for
N=1
for a given
H_{1}
we obtain

D_{\rm GMPC}(\varrho,\varrho)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}[\varrho,H_{1}].

(86)

Now, our goal is to give an optimal transport map (plan) corresponding to an optimal coupling of
D_{GMPC,\rm sep}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2}.
Note that there can be several optimal couplings. We look for a CPTP map
\Phi
corresponding to an optimal separable coupling
\varrho_{12}.
Let us assume that an optimal separable coupling equals the separable state given in Eq. (
18
). Based on Definition
5
, it has the following marginals. We obtain
\varrho
as in Eq. (
14

\sigma

\sigma=\sum_{k}p_{k}|\Phi_{k}\rangle\langle\Phi_{k}|,

(87)

holds. Let us consider the map
[

\Phi(X)=\sum_{k}B_{k}XB_{k}^{\dagger}.

(88)

B_{k}=\sqrt{p_{k}}A_{k}\varrho^{-1/2},

(89)

and
\varrho^{-1}
is the inverse of
\varrho
on its support.

For our particular transport problem, let us choose

A_{k}=|\Phi_{k}\rangle\langle\Psi_{k}|.

(90)

It is clear that
\Phi

\displaystyle\sum_{k}B_{k}^{\dagger}B_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}p_{k}\varrho^{-1/2}|\Psi_{k}\rangle\langle\Phi_{k}|\Phi_{k}\rangle\langle\Psi_{k}|\varrho^{-1/2}

\varrho

\displaystyle\Phi(\varrho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\sqrt{p_{k}}|\Phi_{k}\rangle\langle\Psi_{k}|\varrho^{-1/2}\varrho\varrho^{-1/2}|\Psi_{k}\rangle\langle\Phi_{k}|\sqrt{p_{k}}

the CPTP map

Let us see some properties of the map we have just found. If

B_{k}=A_{k}=|\Phi_{k}\rangle\langle\Psi_{k}|

(93)

holds. In this case, the map can be realized by a von Neumann measurement in the basis given by

It is instructive to look at the action of the map on the state

Observation 3.
The two quantum Wasserstein distance measures are equal to each other

D_{\rm DPT,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2}=D_{\rm GMPC,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2}.

(101)

Proof.
We need to know that for two matrices
X
and
Y

{\rm Tr}(XY)={\rm Tr}(X^{T_{k}}Y^{T_{k}})

(102)

holds for
k=1,2.
Then, from Eq. (
5

\displaystyle D_{\rm GMPC,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2}\quad\quad\quad

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\min_{\varrho_{12}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\;

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}[(H_{n}^{T}\otimes\mathbbm{1}-\mathbbm{1}\otimes H_{n})^{2}\varrho_{12}^{T_{1}}],

s. t.

\displaystyle\varrho_{12}^{T1}\in\mathcal{S},

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{2}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho^{T},

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{1}(\varrho_{12})=\sigma.

(103)

Then, we arrive at Eq. (
5
) by noticing that

\varrho_{12}^{T_{1}}\in\mathcal{S}

(104)

holds if and only if

\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{S}

(105)

holds.
\hfill\blacksquare

It is instructive to obtain a quantum state that maximizes

and

for

6
Variance-like quantities

When we compare the expression in Eq. (
4
) defining the quantum Fisher information and the other expression in Eq. (
77
) defining the variance, we can see that the main difference is that the minimization is replaced by a maximization. We also showed a similar relation between the entanglement of formation given in Eq. (
27
), and the entanglement of assistance given in Eq. (
29
). Based on this observation, we can define variance-like quantities from the various forms of quantum Wasserstein distance by replacing the minimization by maximization.

Such a variance-like quantity can be interpreted in the framework of transport problems as follows. The quantum Wasserstein distance determines the smallest cost possible for the transport problem by a minimization. The variance-like quantities presented in this section determine the largest cost possible for the transport problem. Knowing the largest possible cost is useful when judging how close the cost of a given transport plan is to the optimal cost.

Let us now define the first variance-like quantity.

Definition 5.
From the GMPC distance with an optimization restricted over separable states given in Eq. (
5
), we obtain the following variance like quantity

\displaystyle V_{\rm GMPC,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)\quad\quad\quad

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\max_{\varrho_{12}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\;

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}[(H_{n}\otimes\mathbbm{1}-\mathbbm{1}\otimes H_{n})^{2}\varrho_{12}],

s. t.

\displaystyle\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{S},

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{2}(\varrho_{12})=\varrho,

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{1}(\varrho_{12})=\sigma.

(142)

Clearly, the inequality

V_{\rm GMPC,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)\geq D_{\rm GMPC,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2}

(143)

holds, since since on the left-hand side of Eq. (
143
) we maximize over a set of quantum states while on the right-hand side we minimize over the same set.

We can define analogously

V_{\rm DPT,sep}(\varrho,\sigma),

V_{\rm GMPC}(\varrho,\sigma),
and

V_{\rm DPT}(\varrho,\sigma),

modifying the definition of
D_{\rm DPT,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2},

D_{\rm GMPC}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2},
and
D_{\rm DPT}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2},
respectively. In all these cases, a relation analogous to the one in Eq. (
143
) can be obtained.

The value of
V_{\rm GMPC,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)
is related to the variance.

Observation 6.
The GMPC variance defined in Eq. (
6
) is bounded from below as

V_{\rm GMPC,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(\Delta H_{n})^{2}_{\varrho}+(\Delta H_{n})^{2}_{\sigma}.

(144)

Proof.
We can rewrite the expression to be computed for
V_{\rm GMPC,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)
as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\max_{\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{S}}\;\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\rm Tr}[(H_{n}\otimes\mathbbm{1}-\mathbbm{1}\otimes H_{n})^{2}\varrho_{12}]

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(\Delta H_{n})^{2}_{\varrho}+(\Delta H_{n})^{2}_{\sigma}+(\langle{H_{n}}\rangle_{\varrho}-\langle{H_{n}}\rangle_{\sigma})^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\min_{\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{S}}\;C_{\varrho_{12}},

(145)

where
\varrho_{12}
has marginals
\varrho
and
\sigma,
as given in Eq. (
128
), and we define

C_{\varrho_{12}}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle{H_{n}\otimes H_{n}}\rangle_{\varrho_{12}}-\langle{H_{n}}\rangle_{\varrho}\langle{H_{n}}\rangle_{\sigma}.

(146)

Since the product state
\varrho\otimes\sigma
is separable and fulfills the conditions on the marginals, it is clear that

\min_{\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{S}}C_{\varrho_{12}}\leq C_{\varrho\otimes\sigma}=0.

(147)

Substituting Eq. (
147
) into Eq. (
145
), using the fact
(\langle{H_{n}}\rangle_{\varrho}-\langle{H_{n}}\rangle_{\sigma})^{2}\geq 0,
we can prove the observation.
\hfill\blacksquare

We can even obtain an upper bound.

Observation 7.
The GMPC variance defined in Eq. (
6
) is bounded from above as

\displaystyle V_{\rm GMPC,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}(\Delta H_{n})^{2}_{\varrho}+(\Delta H_{n})^{2}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle+\langle{H_{n}}\rangle_{\varrho}^{2}+\langle{H_{n}}\rangle_{\sigma}^{2}.

(148)

Proof.
We can find an upper bound for the expression to be computed for
V_{\rm GMPC,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)
as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\max_{\varrho_{12}\in\mathcal{S}}\;\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\rm Tr}[(H_{n}\otimes\mathbbm{1}-\mathbbm{1}\otimes H_{n})^{2}\varrho_{12}]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle{H_{n}^{2}}\rangle_{\varrho}+\langle{H_{n}^{2}}\rangle_{\sigma},

(149)

where
\varrho_{12}
has marginals
\varrho
and
\sigma,
as given in Eq. (
128
), and we used that

H_{n}^{2}\otimes\mathbbm{1}+\mathbbm{1}\otimes H_{n}^{2}\geq 2H_{n}\otimes H_{n}.

(150)

\hfill\blacksquare

Example
5
highlighted that in certain cases the minimum for separable states is larger than the minimum for general states. In this case, entanglement can help to decrease the quantum Wasserstein distance. In this section, we analyze the relation of the quantum Wasserstein distance and entanglement conditions on the optimal
\varrho_{12}
couplings.

First, we make the following simple observation.

Observation 9.
If

D_{\rm DPT,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2}>D_{\rm DPT}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2}

(164)

holds then all
\varrho_{12}
states that minimize the cost for a given
\varrho
and
\sigma,
when computing

are entangled. In short, all optimal
\varrho_{12}
states are entangled. The situation is analogous if

D_{\rm GMPC,sep}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2}>D_{\rm GMPC}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2}.

(165)

Then, all optimal
\varrho_{12}
states for a given
\varrho
and
\sigma,
when computing
D_{\rm GMPC}(\varrho,\sigma)^{2},
are entangled. Thus, we can even use the quantum Wasserstein distance as an entanglement criterion detecting entanglement in the optimal
\varrho_{12}

We can even consider conditions with the self-distance. Based on Eqs. (
3

84

\frac{F_{Q}[\varrho,H_{1}]}{4}>I_{\varrho}(H_{1}).

(167)

Thus, the condition in Eq. (

\varrho_{12}

are all entangled.

Let us now use entanglement criteria to construct relations for the quantum Wasserstein distance, that can verify that the coupling
\varrho_{12}
is entangled. If the inequality given in Eq. (
35

\sum_{n=1}^{d^{2}-1}\langle{(G_{n}^{T}\otimes\mathbbm{1}-\mathbbm{1}\otimes G_{n})^{2}}\rangle\geq 4(d-1),

(169)

for the second moments

\sum_{n=1}^{d^{2}-1}\langle{(G_{n}^{T}\otimes\mathbbm{1}-\mathbbm{1}\otimes G_{n})^{2}}\rangle=4(d-1)

(170)

holds since for this state

\langle{G_{n}^{T}\otimes\mathbbm{1}-\mathbbm{1}\otimes G_{n}}\rangle=0

(171)

Next, we will define a quantum Wasserstein distance related to the entanglement condition in Eq. (

Observation 10.
Let us consider

H_{n}=G_{n}

(172)

Thus, independently from what
\varrho
and
\sigma
are, their distance

cannot be smaller than a bound. This is true even if

Let us now use another entanglement condition to construct relations for the quantum Wasserstein distance that can verify that the coupling is entangled. We know that the inequality given in Eq. (
50

10
Alternative definition of the Wasserstein distance such that the self-distance equals various generalized quantum Fisher information quantities

In this section, we will modify the definition of the Wasserstein distance such that the self-distance equals a quantity different from the ones we considered so far. In particular, we would like that it equals various generalized quantum Fisher information quantities. The Wigner-Yanase skew information and the quantum Fisher information are two members of this family.

The basic idea of Refs.
[
111
,
112
]
is that for each standard matrix monotone function
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+},
a generalized variance and a corresponding quantum Fisher information are defined. The notion standard means that
f
must satisfy

\displaystyle f(1)

\displaystyle=1,

(211a)

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle=tf(t^{-1}).

(211b)

For a review on generalized variances, generalized quantum Fisher information quantities and covariances see Ref.
[
113
]
. Moreover, it is also useful to define the mean based on
f
as

m_{f}(a,b)=af\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)

(212)

and use it instead of
f.
The normalization condition given in Eq. (
211a
) corresponds to the condition

m_{f}(a,a)=a

(213)

for the means. The requirement given in Eq. (
211b
) corresponds to

m_{f}(a,b)=m_{f}(b,a).

(214)

A list of generalized quantum Fisher information quantities generated by various well-known means
m_{f}(a,b)
can be found in Refs.
[
111
,
112
]
.

Using the normalization suggested by Ref.
[
62
]
, we arrive at a family of generalized quantum Fisher information quantities
F^{f}_{Q}[\varrho,H]
such that (i) for pure states, we have

\mathcal{F}^{f}_{Q}[|\Psi\rangle,H]=4(\Delta H)^{2}_{\Psi}.

(215)

(ii) For mixed states,
F^{f}_{Q}[\varrho,H]
is convex in the state.

The generalized variance
{\rm var}^{f}_{\varrho}(H)
fulfills the following two requirements. (i) For pure states, the generalized variance equals the usual variance

{\rm var}^{f}_{\Psi}(H)=(\Delta H)^{2}_{\Psi}.

(216)

(ii) For mixed states,
{\rm var}^{f}_{\varrho}(H)
is concave in the state.

A family of generalized quantum Fisher information and generalized variance fulfilling the above requirements are
[
62
]

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{f}[\varrho,H]

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k,l}\frac{m_{f}(1,0)}{m_{f}(\lambda_{k},\lambda_{l})}{(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l})^{2}}|H_{kl}|^{2},

(217a)

\displaystyle{\rm var}_{\varrho}^{f}(H)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,l}\frac{m_{f}(\lambda_{k},\lambda_{l})}{m_{f}(1,0)}|H_{kl}|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2m_{f}(1,0)}\left|\sum\lambda_{k}H_{kk}\right|^{2},

(217b)

where the matrix elements of
H
in the eigenbasis of
\varrho
are denoted as

H_{kl}=\langle k|H|l\rangle.

(218)

{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{f}[\varrho,H]/4
has been called metric-adjusted skew information
[
114
]
.

Note that the definition of the variance in Eq. (
217b
) requires that
m_{f}(1,0)\equiv f(0)
is nonzero. In such cases
f
is called regular
[
115
]
.

The usual quantum Fisher information and the usual variance corresponds to

f_{\max}(x)=\frac{1+x}{2},

(219)

and the arithmetic mean

m_{f_{\max}}(a,b)=\frac{a+b}{2}.

(220)

Note that
f_{\max}(x)
is the largest among standard matrix monotone functions.
Due to this,
{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{f_{\max}}[\varrho,H]
is the largest among
{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{f}[\varrho,H]

and
{\rm var}_{\varrho}^{f_{\max}}(H)
is the smallest among
{\rm var}_{\varrho}^{f}(H).

The Wigner-Yanase skew information corresponds to

f_{\rm WY}(x)=\frac{(\sqrt{x}+1)^{2}}{4},

(221)

and the mean

m_{f_{\rm WY}}(a,b)=\frac{(\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b})^{2}}{4}.

(222)

Note that we get 4 times the usual Wigner-Yanase skew information due to the chosen normalization

\mathcal{F}^{f_{\rm WY}}_{Q}[\varrho,H]=4I_{\varrho}(H).

(223)

We now show a method to express the various generalized quantum Fisher information quantities with each other.

Observation 16.
Let us define for given
f
the following matrix in the eigenbasis of
\varrho

(X_{f})_{kl}=\langle k|X|l\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{m_{f}(1,0)}{m_{f}(\lambda_{k},\lambda_{l})}}.

(224)

Then, any generalized quantum Fisher information can be expressed as

{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}^{f_{1}}[\varrho,H]={\mathcal{F}}^{f_{2}}_{Q}[\varrho,Q_{f_{1},f_{2}}\circ H],

(225)

where ”
\circ
” denotes element-wise or Hadamard product defined as

A\circ B=\sum_{k,l}\langle k|A|l\rangle\langle k|B|l\rangle|k\rangle\langle l|,

(226)

where
|k\rangle
and
|l\rangle
are the eigenvectors of the density matrix, and the coefficient for converting one type of quantum Fisher information into another ons is given as

(Q_{f_{1},f_{2}})_{kl}=\begin{cases}\frac{(X_{f_{1}})_{kl}}{(X_{f_{2}})_{kl}},&\text{if }\lambda_{k}\neq\lambda_{l},\\
0,&\text{if }\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{l}.\end{cases}

(227)

Proof.
The statement can be verified by direct comparison of the definition of the quantum Fisher information given in Eqs. (
217a
) and (
225
).
\hfill\blacksquare

It is interesting to compute the matrix needed for

and

We obtain, in the basis of the eigenvectors of
\varrho,

(Q_{f_{\max},f_{\rm WY}})_{kl}=\begin{cases}\frac{(X_{f_{\max}})_{kl}}{(X_{f_{\rm WY}})_{kl}}=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}+\sqrt{\lambda_{l}}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{l}}},&\text{if }\lambda_{k}\neq\lambda_{l},\\
0,&\text{if }\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{l}.\end{cases}

(228)

Here note that in Eq. (

\lambda_{k}
and
\lambda_{l}
is positive if
\lambda_{k}\neq\lambda_{l}.
With the matrix in Eq. (

\mathcal{F}_{Q}[\varrho,H]=4I_{\varrho}(Q_{f_{\max},f_{\rm WY}}\circ H).

(229)

Next, we compute the matrix needed for
f_{1}=f_{\rm WY}
and
f_{2}=f_{\max}.
We obtain, in the basis of the eigenvectors of
\varrho,

(Q_{f_{\rm WY},f_{\max}})_{kl}=\begin{cases}\frac{(X_{f_{\rm WY}})_{kl}}{(X_{f_{\max}})_{kl}}=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{l}}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}+\sqrt{\lambda_{l}}},&\text{if }\lambda_{k}\neq\lambda_{l},\\
0,&\text{if }\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{l}.\end{cases}

(230)

Then, we can also obtain the Wigner-Yanase skew information with the quantum Fisher information as

I_{\varrho}(H)=\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{F}_{Q}[\varrho,Q_{f_{\rm WY},f_{\max}}\circ H].

(231)

Conclusions

We discussed how to define the quantum Wasserstein distance as an optimization over bipartite separable states rather than an optimization over general quantum states. With such a definition, the self-distance becomes related to the quantum Fisher information. We introduced also variance-like quantities in which we replaced the minimization used in the definition of the quantum Wasserstein distance by a maximization, and examined their properties. We discussed the relation of our findings to entanglement criteria. We examined also the quantity obtained after we considered optimizing the variance rather than the second moment in the usual expression of the quantum Wasserstein distance. Finally, we extended our results to the various generalized quantum Fisher information quantities. The details of the numerical calculations are discussed in Appendix
A
.

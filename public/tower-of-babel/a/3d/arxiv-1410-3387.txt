Derived sections

The purpose of this paper is to lay down a basic formalism which would answer the question posed in the last paragraph. In this paper we define

As a consequence, for example, it is difficult to understand the behaviour of
{\sf DSect}({\cal C},{\cal E})
under the base change. Namely, given a functor

the pull-back of the opfibration

admits a homotopy adjoint, or is homotopically full and faithful when restricted to derived sections. And unfortunately, answering these questions without any additional structure on

To deal with this problem, we propose the approach of homotopical
\Delta
-categories, which axiomatises the notion of geometric realisation of simplicial objects. Admittedly, this is a fairly restrictive technical tool specifically adapted to our setting, and the notion of derived section does not depend on
\Delta
-categories and can be studied using other means. Nonetheless,
\Delta
-categories permit a variety of constructions involving realisations of simplicial objects, which, together with homotopy coproducts, are known to produce all homotopy colimits in other homotopical settings
[
19
]
. Examples of
\Delta
-categories include
{\rm\bf DVect}_{k}
, simplicial vector spaces
\Delta^{\sf op}\mathbf{Vect}_{k}
and some other categories which admit a sufficiently reasonable action of simplicial sets. For
{\rm\bf DVect}_{k}
and
\Delta^{\sf op}\mathbf{Vect}_{k}

\Delta
-category structure interacts with the tensor product, giving rise to what we call homotopical
\Delta
-opfibrations.

With the use of this technology, we are able to construct, for each
F:{\cal D}\to{\cal C}
and a homotopical
\Delta
-opfibration

, a weak equivalence preserving functor

\mathbb{F}_{!}:{\sf PSect}({\cal D},F^{*}{\cal E})\to{\sf PSect}({\cal C},{\cal E}),

defined as an elaborate version of the bar construction
[
24
]
. This functor is not adjoint to
\mathbb{F}^{*}
and does not, in general, preserve derived sections. However,
\mathbb{F}_{!}
comes together with additional structure, for example, with a natural transformation
\epsilon:\mathbb{F}_{!}\mathbb{F}^{*}\to id
on the level of localisation

, which behaves like the counit of an adjunction data, see Proposition

under some additional assumptions on

While our construction of the functor

uses
\Delta

Thus if one finds another way to compute homotopy colimits in the setting of fibrations (say, with the use of model or higher categories), our construction can then be largely carried over. We would also like to note that our approach is similar in spirit to that of Costello
[
7

We then restrict our attention to one specific class of examples which are motivated by algebraic geometry

\mathbb{F}^{*}:{\sf DSect}({\cal C},{\cal E})\to{\sf DSect}({\cal D},F^{*}{\cal E})
, is homotopically full and faithful.

In some specific cases we may also characterise (Theorem
5.4
) the essential homotopical image of
\mathbb{F}^{*}
. It consists of those derived sections which are locally constant (in the weak sense) when restricted to the simplicial replacements
{\mathbb{D}}(c)
of fibres
{\cal D}(c)
. In the case when our opfibration
{\cal E}\to{\cal C}
is the constant opfibration
{\rm\bf DVect}_{k}\times{\cal C}\to{\cal C}
, we reproduce the result for derived categories discussed above. In general, resolutions serve as a testing case, indicating that
{\sf DSect}
is a reasonable thing to consider. For the example of
{\rm\bf DVect}_{k}^{\otimes}\to{\rm\bf Fin}_{*}
, resolutions can be used to provide the proof of Deligne conjecture without operadic considerations; the details were investigated by the author in his thesis and will be published in the near future.

Organisation of the paper

In the first section, we introduce the formalism of homotopical
\Delta
-categories which we need in order to treat the issues arising from the breakdown of model-categorical formalism. The content of this section is not genuinely new and is somehow present in the folklore. For instance, the geometric realisation functor for homotopical categories, in the setting which goes beyond simplicial model categories, has been considered in
[
4
, Appendix]
.

In the second section, we recall some of the basic notions and constructions related to the theory of Grothendieck (op)fibrations, including the less known constructions of transpose and power (op)fibrations. Since Grothendieck opfibrations are natural tools for encoding the notion of families of categories, we introduce a class of suitably structured opfibrations, called homotopical
\Delta
-opfibrations, which formalise the notion of a covariant family of
\Delta
-categories.

In the third section, we introduce simplicial replacements and then use them to define derived sections. The fourth section deals with the construction of the pushforward functor
\mathbb{F}_{!}
and the map
\epsilon:\mathbb{F}_{!}\mathbb{F}^{*}\to id
, the data which one can use to verify if the ‘right adjoint’
\mathbb{F}^{*}
is full and faithful.

Finally, the fifth section consists of the analysis of the case of a resolution, stating the Theorems
5.3
and
5.4
and outlining their proof. It is proven that in this case, the inverse image on the derived sections is full and faithful on the homotopy level. In addition, under mild assumptions we can characterise the essential image of the inverse image functor
\mathbb{F}^{*}
.

2
Fibrations, opfibrations, sections

The main aim of this section is to provide background material for the language of Grothendieck fibrations (contravariant families of categories) and opfibrations (covariant families). Along with the basic notions, we also explain some operations such as taking powers and transpose fibrations. Some parts of this material are largely folklore with no well-known reference available, so we supply proofs. We finish by considering families of homotopical
\Delta
-categories as described by homotopical
\Delta
-opfibrations.

2.1
Basic notions

Definition 2.1

3
Derived sections

This section is devoted to our definition of derived sections. Starting from the introduction of simplicial replacements of categories, we assemble together a few homotopy-theoretic facts to be used later. We also outline how one prolongs an opfibration
{\cal E}\to{\cal C}
to the simplicial replacement
{\mathbb{C}}
of
{\cal C}
. We finish by defining presections and derived sections.

As shown in
[
5
]
and later in
[
24
]
, simplicial replacements of categories allow calculating homotopy colimits and may be viewed as certain cofibrant replacements in homotopy-algebraic sense. We will make this perspective on simplicial replacements concrete in our setting through the constructions of the next section.

3.1
Simplicial replacements

Definition 3.1

Given a small category
{\cal C}

by the rule

commutes with the projections from

and

to

Our choice of terminology is inspired by the simplicial replacement of diagrams in
[
5

. Note that

is an ordinary category and not a simplicial category. The assignment

defines a functor from

to the full subcategory of

when its underlying

-object is not important. Given two objects

Lemma 3.3

There are functors

and

\square

Definition 3.4

Proposition 3.5

(Factorisation and localisation properties)

For a small category
{\cal C}
,

1.

Every map
\mathbf{c}\to\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
can be uniquely factored as an anchor map
\mathbf{c}\to\mathbf{c}^{\prime\prime}
followed by a structural map
\mathbf{c}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathbf{c}
.

2.

Any functor
F:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\cal N}
which sends anchor maps of
{\mathbb{C}}
to isomorphisms factors essentially uniquely as
F=\tilde{F}\circ h_{\cal C}
for
\tilde{F}:{\cal C}\to{\cal N}
. In other words,
{\cal C}
is a localisation of
{\mathbb{C}}
with respect to anchor maps.

Proof.

The factorisation property is clear and is inherited from
\Delta
. For the second statement, we first note that the functor
h_{\cal C}^{*}:{\cal N}^{\cal C}\to{\cal N}^{\mathbb{C}}
is full and faithful (see e.g.
[
24
, Section 4.4]
). Let
F:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\cal N}
be a functor which sends

to isomorphisms. Define a new functor
\bar{F}:{\cal C}\to{\cal N}
. On objects,
\bar{F}(c)=F(c)
where
c
is viewed as an object of
{\mathbb{C}}
of zero length. Take a span

c\longleftarrow(c\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}c^{\prime})\longrightarrow c^{\prime},

the action of
F
on it gives a span
F(c)\leftarrow F(c\to c^{\prime})\to F(c^{\prime})
. Inverting the left arrow we get a map
\bar{F}(f):\bar{F}(c)\to\bar{F}(c^{\prime})
. The action of
F
on objects of higher length,
c\to c^{\prime}\to c^{\prime\prime}
, and on degenerate objects,

is indeed a functor and

.
\square

Remark 3.6

To stress, the class of anchor maps is not saturated in the sense one applies when one speaks of localisation
[
8
]
. Indeed, not every map which becomes an isomorphism under
h_{\cal C}
is an anchor map.

The proposition permits to justify the idea that, given a homotopical category
{\cal M}
with weak equivalences
{\cal W}
, a functor
F:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\cal M}
sending

to
{\cal W}
is a suitable weakening of the concept of a functor from
{\cal C}
to
{\cal M}
. The action of
F
on spans in
{\mathbb{C}}
like

where the left arrow is an anchor map, gives a span

, where the left map is a weak equivalence. On the level of
{\rm Ho}\,{\cal M}
, this span gives a map
F(c)\to F(c^{\prime})
, which one can denote
F(f)
. Applying
F
to higher-length objects then ensures higher coherences for the ‘weak functor’

.

The spans of the form

have appeared before many times in the context of localisation (for example, they are known under the name ’cocycles’ in
[
13
]
). For an arbitrary homotopical category

, such spans may not constitute a good presentation of morphisms in

. In practice one may need to make additional assumptions about

. For our purposes, homotopical

We conclude this subsection by summing up a few results concerning the functors from a simplicial replacement. Let
I
be a small category and denote by

Definition 3.7

Let

Lemma 3.8

Let

Proof.

Consider an ’augmented’ functor

(here

). It is then easy to see that there is a canonical equivalence

coming from the maps

defined by the formula

\tilde{X}^{aug}_{n}=\Pi X^{aug}_{n-1},\,\,\,n>0,

in particular, one augmentation map

comes from the image

of the degeneracy map

and the other map

\square

Lemma 3.9

Let
I
be a category with contractible nerve and
{\cal M}
be a homotopical
\Delta
-category. If a functor
X:\mathbb{I}\to{\cal M}
takes all morphisms of

is an equivalence for any

Proof.

Fix

. Proposition
3.5
implies that the functor

can be factored as

with

.
X
moreover factors through the fundamental groupoid of

\Pi X_{n}=\coprod_{\mathbf{i}_{[n]}}X(\mathbf{i}_{[n]})\cong\coprod_{\mathbf{i}_{[n]}}(\overline{X}\circ h_{I})(\mathbf{i}_{[n]})\cong\coprod_{\mathbf{i}_{[n]}}X(i_{0})\cong\coprod_{\mathbf{i}_{[n]}}X(i),

and so

, which is equivalent to

, and the map

in question is a homotopy inverse of the projection

\square

4
The pushforward functor

Given a functor
F:{\cal D}\to{\cal C}
, there is an induced pull-back morphism

\mathbb{F}^{*}:{\sf PSect}({\cal C},{\cal E})={\sf Sect}({\mathbb{C}},\mathbf{E})\to{\sf Sect}({\mathbb{D}},F^{*}\mathbf{E})={\sf PSect}({\cal D},F^{*}{\cal E})

which restricts well to

\mathbb{F}^{*}:{\sf DSect}({\cal C},{\cal E})\to{\sf DSect}({\cal D},F^{*}{\cal E})

and is moreover homotopical. It is natural to ask if such a functor may admit a homotopy adjoint.

In this section, we provide a partial answer to this question, by constructing

\mathbb{F}_{!}:{\sf PSect}({\cal D},F^{*}{\cal E})\to{\sf PSect}({\cal C},{\cal E}),

a homotopical functor in the other direction, together with natural spans relating
\mathbb{F}_{!}\mathbb{F}^{*}
and
\mathbb{F}^{*}\mathbb{F}_{!}
with identity functors (Proposition
4.11
). The functor
\mathbb{F}_{!}
may be viewed as an almost left adjoint to the pull-back functor
\mathbb{F}^{*}
. If
\mathbb{F}_{!}
preserves derived sections, the spans mentioned above indeed give well-defined maps
\mathbb{F}_{!}\mathbb{F}^{*}\to id
and
id\to\mathbb{F}^{*}\mathbb{F}_{!}
on the level of homotopy categories of derived sections, satisfying a triangle identity. Even under a weaker assumption that the span relating
\mathbb{F}_{!}\mathbb{F}^{*}
to the identity functor consists of weak equivalences, Corollary
4.12
implies that
\mathbb{F}^{*}
is full and faithful on homotopy level.

Lemma 5.21

For
F:{\cal D}\to{\cal C}
a resolution, the functor
\mathbb{F}^{*}
reflects the condition of being a derived section. That is, if
\mathbb{F}^{*}A
is a derived section, then
A
is one as well.

Proof.

If
\mathbb{F}^{*}A
is a derived section for
A\in{\sf PSect}({\cal C},{\cal E})
, then take any anchor map
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{c}
and find an anchor map
\mathbf{d}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{d}
such that
\mathbb{F}(\mathbf{d}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{d})=\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{c}
(this is possible due to
F
being an opfibration with contractible, and hence non-empty, fibres). Then since
\mathbb{F}^{*}A(\mathbf{d}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{d})=A(\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{c})
, we get that
A
is a derived section.
\square

Corollary 5.22

(proof of Theorem
5.4
)

\mathbb{F}_{!}
sends locally constant sections to derived sections, and

\mathbb{F}_{!}:{\rm Ho}\,{\sf DSect}_{lc}({\cal D},{\cal E})\rightleftarrows{\rm Ho}\,{\sf DSect}({\cal C},{\cal E}):\mathbb{F}^{*}

is an equivalence of categories for a special homotopical
\Delta
-fibration
{\cal E}\to{\cal C}
.

Proof.

We proved that the unit correspondence gives an isomorphism

of functors on
{\rm Ho}\,{\sf PSect}_{lc}({\cal D},{\cal E})
. Using Lemma

, we see that then

to the derived sections.

A classification of first order differential equations
Partha Kumbhakar, Ursashi Roy, Varadharaj R. Srinivasan

Abstract
. Let k k be a differential field of characteristic zero with an algebraically closed field of constants. In this article, we provide a classification of first order differential equations over k k and study the algebraic dependence of solutions of a given first order differential equation. Our results generalize parts of the work of Noordman et al. [ 20 ] and complements the work of Freitag et al. [ 4 ] .



1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a differential field of characteristic zero having an algebraically closed field of constants
C
. Let
f\in k[Y,Z]
be an irreducible polynomial involving the variable
Z.
By a
solution
of the differential equation
f(y,y^{\prime})=0,
we mean an element
u
in a differential field extension of
k
having
C
as its field of constants such that
f(u,u^{\prime})=0.
If
f
is defined over the field of constants
C
then
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
is called an
autonomous first order differential equation
. The field of fractions
k(f)
of the ring
k[Y,Z]/\langle f\rangle
has a natural derivation induced by
f
that maps
y
to
z
where
y,z
are the images of
Y,Z
in
k[Y,Z]/\langle f\rangle
under the canonical projection. It then follows that
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
has a nonalgebraic solution if and only if the differential field
k(f)
has
C
as its field of constants.

In this article, we study transcendence degree
1
differential subfields of iterated strongly normal extensions. As a consequence of our study, we obtain a classification of first order differential equations
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
over
k
into the following types:

(I)

(
algebraic type
) All solutions of
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
are algebraic over
k
or equivalently,
k(f)
with the induced derivation has a nonalgebraic constant.

(II)

(
Riccati type
) There are a nonalgebraic solution of
f(y,y^{\prime})=0,
a finite algebraic extension
\tilde{k}
of
k
and an element
t\in\tilde{k}(f)
such that
\tilde{k}(f)
is a finite algebraic extension of
\tilde{k}(t)
and that
t
is a solution of a Riccati differential equation:

(1.0.1)

t^{\prime}=a_{2}t^{2}+a_{1}t+a_{0},\ \text{with}\ \ a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}\in\tilde{k},\ \text{not all zero}.

(III)

(
Weierstrass type
) There are a nonalgebraic solution of
f(y,y^{\prime})=0,
a finite algebraic extension
\tilde{k}
of
k
and an element
t\in\tilde{k}(f)
such that
\tilde{k}(f)
is a finite algebraic extension of
\tilde{k}(t,t^{\prime})
and that
t
is a solution of a Weierstrass differential equation:

(1.0.2)

(t^{\prime})^{2}=\alpha^{2}(4t^{3}-g_{2}t-g_{3}),\ g_{2},g_{3}\in C,\alpha\in\tilde{k}\ \text{and}\ 27g^{2}_{3}-g^{3}_{2}\neq 0.

(IV)

(
general type
) The differential equation
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
is not of the above types.

When
k=C,
we obtain the classification of autonomous differential equation due to Noordman et al.
[
20
, page 1655]
. If a differential equation is of Riccati or Weierstrass type then it is easy to see that
k(f)
can be embedded in an iterated strongly normal extension. The following theorem contains the main results (Theorem
5.2
, Theorem
6.3
and Corollary
6.4
) of this article.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k
be a differential field with an algebraically closed field of constants
C
and
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
be a differential equation over
k.
Suppose that
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
has a nonalgebraic solution in an iterated strongly normal extension
E
of
k
or equivalently, the differential field
k(f)
can be embedded in an iterated strongly normal extension
E
of
k.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

The differential equation
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
is of either Riccati or Weierstrass type.

(ii)

If
k=C
and
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
is of Riccati type then there is an element
t\in C(f)
such that

(1.0.3)

\text{either}\ t^{\prime}=1\ \ \text{or}\ \ t^{\prime}=ct\ \ \ \text{for some nonzero}\ c\in C.

(iii)

If
E
is a strongly normal extension of
k
and
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
is of Riccati type (respectively, Weierstrass type) then the finite algebraic extension
\tilde{k}
and the element
t\in\tilde{k}(f),
as in the definition of a Riccati type (respectively, Weierstrass type), can be chosen so that
\tilde{k}(f)=\tilde{k}(t)
(respectively,
\tilde{k}(f)=\tilde{k}(t,t^{\prime})
).

We use the above theorem to produce a family of geometrically irreducible plane algebraic curves
f
over
k
such that
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
is of general type (see Theorem
7.1
). This family of examples of general type equations includes Abel differential equations (see Example
7.4
) of the form

y^{\prime}=a_{n}y^{n}+\cdots+a_{2}y^{2},

where
n\geq 3,

a_{i}\in k
and both
a_{2}
and
a_{3}
have no antiderivatives in
k.
Note that the differential equation
y^{\prime}=y^{3}-y^{2},
that appears in
[
15
]
and
[
20
]
, belongs to the above family of Abel differential equations and therefore is of general type. We also prove the following results concerning algebraic dependence of nonalgebraic solutions (see Theorem
7.6
):

(i)

If a first order differential equation over
k
has a nonalgebraic solution in an iterated strongly normal extension of
k
then any four nonalgebraic solutions from a differential field extension of
k
having
C
as its field of constants are
k-
algebraically dependent.

(ii)

If an autonomous differential equation has a nonalgebraic solution in an iterated strongly normal extension of
C
then any two nonalgebraic solutions from a differential field having
C
as its field of constants are
C-
algebraically dependent.

In a recent article
[
4
]
, Freitag et al. study the algebraic dependence of solutions of differential equations of any order using model theoretic methods. They show that if a first order differential equation (respectively, an autonomous differential equation) has four (respectively, two) algebraically independent solutions then any
m
distinct nonalgebraic solutions are algebraically independent. Thus, it is natural to make the following

Conjecture 1.2
.

A first order differential equation over
k
(respectively, An autonomous differential equation over
C
) is not of general type if and only if it has at most three (respectively, at most one) algebraically independent solutions in any given differential field extension of
k
(respectively,
C
) having
C
as its field of constants.

We provide some evidence for this conjecture in Section

. We will not prove any results concerning algebraic type differential equations. The articles
[

]
,
[

]
and
[

1.2.
Painlevé property and differential equations of Ricatti or Weierstrass type
[
12
]
&
[
14
]
:

Let
k
be a finite algebraic extension of the ordinary differential field
\mathbb{C}(x)
of rational functions over complex numbers with
x^{\prime}=1.
A differential equation
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
over
k
is said to have the
Painlevé property
if the set of all branch points and the set of all essential singularities of the solutions form a discrete set. A differential equation over
k
of algebraic type has the Painlevé property if and only if all of its solutions lie in a fixed finite algebraic extension of
k
(see
[
14
, Theorem 2.7 and Example 2.9]
).

Let
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
have a nonalgebraic solution
u.
Then the function field
k(f)
with the induced derivation is isomorphic to
k(u,u^{\prime})
as differential fields. Since
k(u,u^{\prime})
has
\mathbb{C}
as its field of constants, from
[
12
, Theorem 4.5]
, we deduce that
f
has the Painlevé property if and only if there exists a finite algebraic extension
\tilde{k}
of
k
and an element
t\in\tilde{k}(u,u^{\prime})
such that
\tilde{k}(u,u^{\prime})=\tilde{k}(t,t^{\prime})
and that
t
is a solution of either a Riccati or a Weierstrass differential equation over
\tilde{k}.
In the former case,
\tilde{k}(t,t^{\prime})
can be embedded in a Picard-Vessiot extension of
\tilde{k}
whose Galois group is isomorphic to a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and in the latter case,
\tilde{k}(t,t^{\prime})
is an abelian extension of
\tilde{k}
whose Galois group is isomorphic to the
\mathbb{C}-
points of a nonsingular elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{C}
(see
7.2
for details).

Now, from our Theorem
1.1
(
iii
), we can make the following observation:

Let
k
be a finite algebraic extension of the ordinary differential field
\mathbb{C}(x)
of rational functions in one variable
x
with the derivation
d/dx.
Let
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
be a first order differential equation with a nonalgebraic solution. Then,
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
has a nonalgebraic solution in a strongly normal extension of a finite algebraic extension of
k
if and only if
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
has the Painlevé property.

There are differential equations of Ricatti type that do not have the Painlevé property. For example, the differential equation

y^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{2x}y^{3}

has
u_{c}=1/\sqrt{\ln(x)+c}
as a one-parameter family of solutions, where the parameter
c\in\mathbb{C}
. Note that
u_{c}\in\mathbb{C}(x)(\ln(x))\left(\sqrt{\ln(x)+c}\right)
and that

\mathbb{C}(x)\subset\mathbb{C}(x)(\ln(x))\subset\mathbb{C}(x)(\ln(x))\left(\sqrt{\ln(x)+c}\right).

Clearly,
\mathbb{C}(x)(\ln(x))(\sqrt{\ln(x)+c})
is an iterated (Picard-Vessiot) strongly normal extension of
\mathbb{C}(x).
Since
\ln(x)\in\mathbb{C}(x)(u_{c})
and
(\ln(x))^{\prime}=1/x\in\mathbb{C}(x),
we see that
y^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{2x}y^{3}
is of Ricatti type. However, the set of all branched points of these solutions, namely the set
\mathbb{C},
is not discrete.

A differential field extension

Picard-Vessiot theory

A
Picard-Vessiot ring

R
for a matrix differential equation
Y^{\prime}=AY,
where
A\in M_{n}(k),
is a differential ring containing
k
and having the following properties:

(a)

R
is a simple differential ring, that is,
R
has no nontrivial differential ideals.

(b)

There is a
fundamental matrix

F
for
Y^{\prime}=AY
with entries in
R,
that is, a matrix
F\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)
such that
F^{\prime}=AF.

(c)

R
is minimal with respect to these properties, that is,
R
is generated as a ring by the elements of
k,
the entries of
F
and inverse of the determinant of
F.

By a
differential
k-
module

(M,\partial),
we mean a finite dimensional
k-
module
M
and an additive map
\partial:M\to M
such that
\partial(\alpha m)=\alpha^{\prime}m+\alpha\partial(m)
for all
\alpha\in k
and
m\in M.
Let
M
be a differential
k-
module. By fixing a
k-

e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
of

we obtain a matrix
A=(a_{ij})\in M_{n}(k)
such that
\partial(e_{i})=-\sum_{j}a_{ji}e_{j}
and a corresponding matrix differential equation
Y^{\prime}=AY.
Choosing any other basis will amount to obtaining a differential equation of the form
Y^{\prime}=\tilde{A}Y,
where
\tilde{A}=B^{\prime}B^{-1}+BAB^{-1}
for some
B\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k).
Furthermore, if
R
is a Picard-Vessiot ring for
Y^{\prime}=AY
with fundamental matrix
F
then
(BF)^{\prime}=\tilde{A}BF
and thus

is also the Picard-Vessiot ring for
Y^{\prime}=\tilde{A}Y.
This observation allows one to define a
Picard-Vessiot ring
for a differential module

to be a Picard-Vessiot ring for a corresponding matrix differential equation

of

Picard-Vessiot rings are integral domains and their field of fractions are called

. Let
E
be a Picard-Vessiot extension of

Thus, if

then

and thus

Then for

Therefore, if

Outline of the proof of the main theorem

(
i

4.
Differential Subfields of Liouvillian Picard-Vessiot Extensions

In
[
1
]
, a Galois correspondence between certain differential subfields, called the solution fields, and the observable subgroups of the differential Galois group has been established. We shall now explain relevant definitions and results from
[
1
]
that are needed to prove a structure theorem for the intermediate differential fields of liouvillian Picard-Vessiot extensions.

Solution algebras and solution fields

Let
k[\partial]
be the usual ring of differential operators. Let
M
be a differential
k-
module of dimension
n
. A differential field extension
E
of
k
having the same field of constants as
k
is called a
solution field
for
M
if there is a morphism
\psi:M\to E
of
k[\partial]-
modules such that
\psi(M)
generates
E;
in which case,
E
is said to be generated by a solution
\psi.
Let
S
be a differential
k-
algebra and an integral domain such that its field of fractions has field of constants
C
. Then
S
is called a
solution algebra
for
M
if there is a morphism
\psi:M\to S
of
k[\partial]-
modules such that
\psi(M)
generates
S.
Let
E
be a differential field extension of
k
having the same field of constants as
k.
For
i=1,\dots,n
, let
y_{i}\in E
and
\mathscr{L}_{i}\in k[\partial]
such that
\mathscr{L}_{i}(y_{i})=0.
Let
R
be the differential
k-
subalgebra of
E
generated by
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}.
Then
R
is a solution algebra. To see this, consider the differential modules
k[\partial]/k[\partial]\mathscr{L}_{i},
where
\mathscr{L}_{i}(y_{i})=0.
Then the images of the
k[\partial]-
morphisms
\oplus^{n}_{i=1}\psi_{i}:\bigoplus^{n}_{i=1}k[\partial]/k[\partial]\mathscr{L}_{i}\to E
defined by
\psi_{i}(1)=y_{i}
generates
R.
Similarly, the differential field generated by
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
is a solution field. Conversely, let
S
be a solution algebra generated by a solution
\psi.
Then we have a map
\psi:M\to S
of
k[\partial]-
modules such that
\psi(M)
generates
S
as a
k-
algebra. Note that
\psi(M)
is a (finite dimensional) differential
k-
module. Let
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
be a
k-
basis of
\psi(M).
Then each
y_{i}
must satisfy a linear homogeneous differential equation over
k.
We shall summarize this observation in the following

Proposition 4.1
.

Solution fields (respectively, Solution algebras) over
k
are generated as a field (respectively, as a
k-
algebra) by solutions of linear homogeneous differential equations over
k.

Let
M^{\vee}
be the dual of
M.
A differential field
E
is called a
Picard-Vessiot field
if it has the same field of constants as
k
and the
C-
modules Sol
(M,E):=
Hom
{}_{k[\partial]}(M,E)
and Sol
(M^{\vee},E):=

{}_{k[\partial]}(M^{\vee},E)
are of dimension
n
over
C
and
E
is minimal with respect to these properties. As noted in Section
2
,
M
and
M^{\vee}
have the same Picard-Vessiot extension and thus it is readily seen that the notions of a Picard-Vessiot field for
M
and a Picard-Vessiot extension for
M
are one and the same. Observe that if
E
is a Picard-Vessiot field of a differential
k-
module

of dimension

then

is a solution field of

Theorem 4.2
.

(
[
1
, Lemma 4.2.2, Theorem 1.2.2]
) Let

be the tannakian category over

generated by the differential

(i)

The quotient field of a solution algebra

.

(ii)

Conversely, any solution field

is the quotient field of (non unique) solution algebra

.

(iii)

Any solution field for

(iv)

Let

is a solution field for

if and only if the corresponding subgroup

is observable (i.e.

In view of the above theorem, it is natural to ask for a characterization theorem of those Picard-Vessiot extensions having only solution fields as intermediate differential fields. Equivalently, characterize those linear algebraic groups whose closed subgroups are observable. We shall provide such a characterization theorem in the next proposition. But first, a few definitions are in order.

A differential field extension
E
of
k
is called a

of

if there is a tower of differential fields

or

is called a

It is a standard fact that liouvillian Picard-Vessiot extensions are precisely those Picard-Vessiot extensions whose differential Galois groups have solvable identity components.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let

be a differential field extension of

Proof.

Let

From Lüroth’s theorem, we know that there are elements

We compute the derivative of the above equation and obtain

y^{\prime}(ct+d)^{2}=\ ca^{\prime}t^{2}+(a^{\prime}d+b^{\prime}c)t+db^{\prime}-\left(c^{\prime}at^{2}+(bc^{\prime}+ad^{\prime})t+bd^{\prime}\right)+(ad-bc)t^{\prime}.

If

is a polynomial of degree
\leq 2

is a polynomial of degree
\leq 2.
Since

We recall the following facts about a strongly normal extension

of

is a finite Galois extension of

with Galois group

\tilde{k}K=\tilde{k}(t,t^{\prime})

Decompose

into a tower of fields as in Equation (

\overline{E}
of

and extend the derivation of

Now we apply Theorem

and obtain

where

This, in particular, shows that the (geometric) genus of
\Gamma
is

Since
\Gamma
has
k-
point, we obtain that

(see

).

Thus

From Proposition

, we then have
t
is a solution of a Riccati equation over
E^{\mathscr{U}}.
Say,

where

is a polynomial over

of degree
\leq 2.
Since

is a differential field, we have

where

and

are relatively prime polynomials in

with

being a monic polynomial. Then

and

must remain relatively prime over any field extension of

. Now since

, we must have

and we obtain that the coefficients of

are in
k
.

Suppose that
E^{\mathscr{U}}K=E^{\mathscr{U}},
that is
K\subseteq E^{\mathscr{U}}.
Since
\mathscr{T}
is a commutative group,

must be a Picard-Vessiot extension of

and since

is connected,

is algebraically closed in

and therefore in

(see

is a genus zero curve. Therefore

and we obtain

Now a similar argument, as in the previous paragraph, shows that

This completes the proof of the theorem in this case.

Case (ii). Suppose that

Then since

is an abelian extension of

In particular,

is strongly normal over
E^{0}
and

Now we apply

and obtain that either

or

with

or

or
\mathrm{G_{m}}(C)
and in any event, we must then have a surjective morphism from the abelian variety

where

Since

of

∎

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
E
be an iterated strongly normal extension of
k
and
K
be an intermediate differential field having field transcendence degree

Then, there is a finite algebraic extension

of

(i)

\tilde{k}K
is a finite algebraic extension of
\tilde{k}(t)
and

is a solution of a Riccati differential equation over
\tilde{k}.

(ii)

\tilde{k}K
is a finite algebraic extension of
\tilde{k}(t,t^{\prime})
and
t
is a solution of a Weierstrass differential equation over
\tilde{k}.

Proof.

Let
k=:E_{0}\subseteq E_{1}\subseteq E_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq E_{n+1}=E
be a tower of strongly normal extensions, where we may assume that
E_{n+1}
is an algebraic extension of

and that
E_{n}
is a transcendental extension of

Let
\overline{E}
be an algebraic closure of

Consider the following tower of algebraically closed differential fields

Since the differential field
\overline{E}_{n-1}K
is contained in

by Proposition
6.1
, we obtain an element

For the moment, let us assume that

(C1)

for all

\overline{E}_{i}K
is a weakly normal extension of
\overline{E}_{i-1}K
and that the group
\mathscr{G}(\overline{E}_{i}K|\overline{E}_{i-1}K)

\overline{E}_{i}.

The differential field
K
, being a subfield of an iterated strongly normal extension, is finitely generated over
k.
Therefore,
\overline{E}_{i}K
is finitely generated over
\overline{E}_{i}.

will produce a solution

\overline{E}_{i-1}E_{i}
is strongly normal over

Therefore the

7.1.

In the next theorem, we device a mechanism to generate differential equations of general type.

. Here we have

and if

Thus, we obtain

.

Now we suppose that there is a transcendental element
w\in\bar{k}(y,y^{\prime})
satisfying a Weierstrass differential equation:
w^{\prime 2}=\alpha^{2}(4w^{3}-g_{2}w-g_{3})
for
g_{2},g_{3}\in C
and
\alpha\in\bar{k}.
Then from the
y-
adic expansion of
w
and from Equation (
7.1.1
), we have
w^{\prime 2}=a^{\prime 2}_{r}y^{2r}+\cdots
and thus

2\ \text{ord}_{p}(w)\leq\text{ord}_{p}(w^{\prime 2})=\text{ord}_{p}(\alpha^{2}(4w^{3}-g_{2}w-g_{3})).

If ord
{}_{p}(w)<0
then ord
{}_{p}(\alpha^{2}(4w^{3}-g_{2}w-g_{3}))=3\ \text{ord}_{p}(w)
and we obtain
2
ord
{}_{p}(w)\leq 3
ord
{}_{p}(w),
a contradiction. If ord
{}_{p}(w)>0
then ord
{}_{p}(\alpha^{2}(4w^{3}-g_{2}w-g_{3}))\leq
ord
{}_{p}(w),
and we obtain
2\ \text{ord}_{p}(w)\leq
ord
{}_{p}(w),
again a contradiction. Finally, let ord
{}_{p}(w)=0
and in the
y-
adic expansion of
w,
let
m
be the least positive integer such that
a_{m}\neq 0.
Then from Equation (
7.1.1
), we have the following equations

\displaystyle w^{\prime 2}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}(4w^{3}-g_{2}w-g_{3})

\displaystyle a^{\prime 2}_{0}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}(4a^{3}_{0}-g_{2}a_{0}-g_{3})

(7.1.2)

\displaystyle 2a^{\prime}_{0}a_{m}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\left(12a^{2}_{0}a_{m}-g_{2}a_{m}\right).

We assume for the moment that
a_{0}
is a constant. Then
a_{0}
must be one of the distinct roots of the polynomial
4Y^{3}-g_{2}Y-g_{3}
and in particular,
a_{0}
is not a root of
12Y^{2}-g_{2}.
Now Equation (
7.1.2
) becomes

\alpha^{2}(12a^{2}_{0}-g_{2})a_{m}=0,

which is absurd. Thus there is no element in
\bar{k}(t,t^{\prime})\setminus\bar{k}
satisfying a Weierstrass differential equation over
\bar{k}.

Now we shall show that
a_{0}
is indeed a constant. Assume otherwise and consider the nonsingular projective curve

(7.1.3)

X^{2}_{2}X_{0}-4X^{3}_{1}+g_{2}X^{2}_{0}X_{1}+g_{3}X^{3}_{0}.

We have two nonconstant points of this curve, namely,
(1:w:w^{\prime}/\alpha)
and
(1:a_{0}:a^{\prime}_{0}/\alpha).
If
(1:\eta:\xi):=(1:w:w^{\prime}/\alpha)(1:a_{0}:a^{\prime}_{0}/\alpha)
then

\eta=-w-a_{0}+\frac{1}{4\alpha^{2}}\left(\frac{w^{\prime}-a^{\prime}_{0}}{w-a_{0}}\right)^{2}

and thus
\eta\in\bar{k}(y,y^{\prime})\setminus\bar{k}.
Now we apply
[
7
, Lemma 2]
for the Weierstrass equations

w^{\prime 2}=\alpha^{2}(4w^{3}-g_{2}w-g_{3})
and
a^{\prime 2}_{0}=(-\alpha)^{2}(4a^{3}_{0}-g_{2}a_{0}-g_{3})
and obtain that
\eta^{\prime}=0.
This contradicts the fact that field of constants of
\bar{k}(y,y^{\prime})
is the same as the field of constants of
\bar{k}.

∎

Remark 7.2
.

If we have an irreducible affine curve of the form
Z-F_{2}-F_{3}-\cdots-F_{n},
where
F_{i}
are forms of degree
i,
then one can quickly find the coefficients
\lambda_{2}
and
\lambda_{3}
of the
Y-
adic expansion of
Z
as follows: Let
F_{2}=x_{20}Y^{2}+x_{11}ZY+x_{02}Z^{2}
and
F_{3}=x_{30}Y^{3}+R_{3}.
Then, on the curve, the value of
Z
equals

x_{20}Y^{2}+x_{11}ZY+x_{02}Z^{2}+x_{30}Y^{3}+R_{3}+F_{4}+\cdots+F_{n}.\\

In the above expression we shall substitute back for
Z
and obtain

x_{20}Y^{2}+x_{11}\left(x_{20}Y^{2}+x_{11}ZY+\cdots\right)Y+x_{02}\left(x_{20}Y^{2}+x_{11}ZY+\cdots\right)^{2}+x_{30}Y^{3}+\cdots.\\

Continuing this process one actually obtains the

Z=x_{20}Y^{2}+\left(x_{11}x_{20}+x_{30}\right)Y^{3}+\cdots.

.

Let
\mathbb{C}(x)
be the ordinary differential field of rational functions with the derivation

Then differential equation

y^{\prime}-\frac{1}{x}y^{2}-xyy^{\prime}-\frac{1}{x+1}y^{3}+y(y^{\prime})^{2}=0

is of general type as
1/x
and
x(1/x)+1/(x+1)=1+1/(x+1)
have no antiderivatives in
\mathbb{C}(x).

Example 7.4
.

The differential equation

(7.1.4)

y^{\prime}=a_{n}y^{n}+\cdots+a_{3}y^{3}+a_{2}y^{2},

where both
a_{2}
and
a_{3}
having no antiderivatives in
k,
is readily seen to be of general type.
Thus, the differential equation
y^{\prime}=y^{3}-y^{2}
is of general type over
\mathbb{C}.
In fact, any autonomous equation of the kind (
7.1.4
) with
a_{2}

nonzero is of general type.

Remark 7.5
.

Let
C
be an algebraically closed field with the zero derivation. From Theorem
1.1
and from
[
18
, Proposition 3.1]
, we have the following equivalent statements:

(i)

An autonomous differential equation over
C
of the form
y^{\prime}=f(y);\ f\neq 0
is not of general type, that is, the equation has a nonalgebraic solution in an iterated strongly normal extension of
C.

(ii)

The differential field
C(y)
has an element
z
such that either
z^{\prime}=1
or
z^{\prime}=cz
for some nonzero constant
c.

(iii)

There is an element
z\in C(y)
and a nonzero constant
c\in C
such that

\frac{1}{f(y)}=\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\quad\text{or}\quad\frac{1}{f(y)}=\frac{1}{cz}\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}.

Thus the autonomous equation
y^{\prime}=f(y)
is not of general type if and only if either
1/f(y)
has no residues at any element of
C
, that is, the partial fraction expansion of
1/f(y)
is of the form

(7.1.5)

h(y)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=2}^{n_{i}}\frac{d_{ij}}{(y-c_{i})^{j}},

or the partial fraction expansion of
1/f(y)
is of the form

(7.1.6)

c\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{m_{i}}{(y-c_{i})},

where
m_{i}
are nonzero integers and
c
is a nonzero constant.

The equations
y^{\prime}=y^{3}-y^{2}
and
y^{\prime}=\dfrac{y}{y+1}
are now easily seen to be of general type.

7.2.
Differential equations of nongeneral type.

In this section we shall study the algebraic dependence of first order differential equations of nongeneral type. Let
L
be a differential field extension of
k
having
C
as its field of constants. It is easily seen that any two nonzero solutions in
L
of a homogeneous first order linear differential equation
y^{\prime}=by,
where
0\neq b\in k,
are
C-
linearly dependent. Suppose that
L
has nonalgebraic solutions of the nonhomogeneous differential equation
y^{\prime}=by+c
for
b,c\in k
and
c\neq 0.
Consider a Picard-Vessiot extension
E
of
L
for
M=k[\partial]/k[\partial]\mathscr{L},
where
\mathscr{L}=\partial^{2}-(b+(c^{\prime}/c))\partial+b(c^{\prime}/c)-b^{\prime}
is obtained by homogenizing
y^{\prime}=by+c.
Let
V\subset E
be the set of all solutions of
\mathscr{L}(y)=0.
Then dim
{}_{C}V=2.
The differential field
\mathcal{E}:=k\langle V\rangle,
generated by elements of
V
and all its derivatives, is a Picard-Vessiot extension of
k
(for
M
). For any
u\in L
such that
u^{\prime}=bu+c,
we see that
\mathscr{L}(u)=0
and therefore
u\in V\subset\mathcal{E}.
Moreover, for any automorphism
\sigma\in\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{E}|k),
we have
\mathscr{L}(\sigma(u))=\sigma\left(\mathscr{L}(u)\right)=0
and that
(\sigma(u)-u)^{\prime}=b(\sigma(u)-u).
Thus, if we choose an automorphism
\sigma\in\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{E}|k)
such that
\sigma(u)\neq u
then since
\mathscr{L}(\sigma(u)-u)=\mathscr{L}(\sigma(u))-\mathscr{L}(u)=0,
we have
\{\sigma(u)-u,u\}
to be a
C-
basis of
V.
We therefore have
\mathcal{E}=k(\sigma(u),u),
where the fields
k(\sigma(u)-u)
and
k(u)
are differential fields. This implies
\mathrm{tr.deg}(\mathcal{E}|k)\leq 2
and thus any three solutions in
L
of
y^{\prime}=by+c,
where
b,c\in k
and
c\neq 0,
must be
k-
algebraically dependent.

Suppose that

(7.2.1)

y^{\prime}=ay^{2}+by+c\ \text{for}\ a,b,c\in k\ \text{with}\ a\neq 0

has a nonalgebraic solution in
L.
Let

\mathscr{L}=\partial^{2}-\left(\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}+b\right)\partial+ac

and
E
be a Picard-Vessiot extension of
L
for
M=k[\partial]/k[\partial]\mathscr{L}.
Let
V\subset E
be the set of all solutions of
\mathscr{L}(y)=0.
Then
v\in V\setminus\{0\}
if and only if
-v^{\prime}/av
is a solution of the Riccati equation (
7.2.1
). Note that
\mathcal{E}:=k\langle V\rangle
is a Picard-Vessiot extension of
k
for
M.
We first claim that every solution in
L
of (
7.2.1
) belongs to
\mathcal{E}.

Let
u\in L
be a solution of the Riccati equation (
7.2.1
). Let
L^{*}
be a Picard-Vessiot extension of
E
for the differential equation
y^{\prime}=-auy.
Then
L^{*}=E(x)
for some nonzero
x
such that
x^{\prime}=-aux.
Note that
V\subset\mathcal{E}\subseteq L^{*}
and that
V
is a two dimensional vector space over
C
. Since
C
is the field of constants of
L^{*}
and
\mathscr{L}(x)=0,
we must have
x\in V\subset\mathcal{E}.
Consequently,
u=-x^{\prime}/(ax)\in\mathcal{E}.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathcal{E}
be set of all nonalgebraic solutions of Equation (
7.2.1
). Now we shall show that any four distinct elements of
\Omega
must be
k-
algebraically dependent. That is,
\mathrm{tr.deg}(k(\Omega)|k)\leq 3.

The differential Galois group
\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{E}|k)
is a closed subgroup of the algebraic group
\mathrm{GL}(V).
Since dim
\mathrm{GL}(V)=4,
if
\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{E}|k)
is a proper closed subgroup then dim
\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{E}|k)\leq 3
and we obtain
\mathrm{tr.deg}(k(\Omega)|k)\leq

\mathrm{tr.deg}(\mathcal{E}|k)=
dim
\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{E}|k)\leq 3.
Thus we only need to deal with the case when
\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{E}|k)=\mathrm{GL}(V).
In this case, we first choose a
C-
basis
\{y_{1},y_{2}\}
of
V
and identify
\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{E}|k)
with
\mathrm{GL}(2,C).
Next, we consider the differential field
K=\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{Z}},
where
\mathcal{Z}
is the center of
\mathrm{GL}(2,C).
Then for any nonzero solution
v=c_{1}y_{1}+c_{2}y_{2}\in V
and any automorphism
\tau\in\mathcal{Z},
we have
\tau(v)=c_{1}c_{\tau}y_{1}+c_{2}c_{\tau}y_{2}.
Thus
\tau(v)=c_{\tau}v
and we obtain
v^{\prime}/v\in K.
This implies
\Omega\subset K.
Since
\mathcal{Z}
is normal,
K
is a Picard-Vessiot extension of
k
with Galois group

\mathscr{G}(K|k)\cong\mathscr{G}(\mathcal{E}|k)/\mathcal{Z}\cong\ \mathrm{PGL}(2,C).

Now
\mathrm{tr.deg}(K|k)=
dim
\mathrm{PGL}(2,C)=3
implies any four distinct elements in
\Omega
must be algebraically dependent over
k
. We would like to remark that the Riccati equation
y^{\prime}=-y^{2}+x
over the differential field
\mathbb{C}(x)
with the derivation
d/dx
has exactly three
\mathbb{C}(x)-
algebraically independent solutions in any Picard-Vessiot extension of
\mathbb{C}(x)
(see
[
11
, Example 4.29]
or
[
13
]
).

Suppose that
L
has a nonalgebraic solution
u
satisfying a Weierstrass differential equation;
u^{\prime 2}=\alpha^{2}(4u^{3}-g_{2}u-g_{3}).
Then since
L
has
C
as its field of constants, from
[
7
, Lemma 2]
, any other nonalgebraic solution of the Weierstrass equation must belong to the field
k(u,u^{\prime}).
In fact, the points
(1:z:z^{\prime}/\alpha),

(1:y:y^{\prime}/\alpha)
of the elliptic curve (
7.1.3
) has the following relation;
(1:z:z^{\prime}/\alpha)=(1:y:y^{\prime}/\alpha)(1:c_{1}:c_{2}),
where
(1:c_{1}:c_{2})
is a
C-
point of the curve. With all these facts, we shall now move on to prove the following

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
L
be a differential field extension of
k
having
C
as its field of constants.

(i)

If an autonomous differential equation over
C
is not of general type then there is at most one
C-
algebraically independent solution of the equation in
L.

(ii)

If a first order differential equation over
k
is not of general type then there are at most three
k-
algebraically independent solutions of the equation in
L.

Proof.

Let
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
be a differential equation of nongeneral type. We only need to consider the case when the differential equation has a nonalgebraic solution
u
in an iterated strongly normal extension of
k.
Let
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}
be distinct nonalgebraic solutions from
L.
Replace
k,
if necessary, by a finite algebraic extension of
k
so that
k(u,u^{\prime})
contains a nonalgebraic solution of a Riccati or a Weierstrass differential equation over
k.
Now for
i=1,2,3,4,
consider the natural differential embeddings

\psi_{i}:k(u,u^{\prime})\to L;\ \ \psi_{i}(u)=u_{i},\psi_{i}(u^{\prime})=u^{\prime}_{i}.

If there is a nonalgebraic solution
t\in k(u,u^{\prime})\setminus k
of a Riccati differential equation over
k,
say
t^{\prime}=a_{2}t^{2}+a_{1}t+a_{0},
then
\psi_{i}(t)^{\prime}=a_{2}\psi_{i}(t)^{2}+a_{1}\psi_{i}(t)+a_{0}
for each
i=1,2,3,4.
Then as noted earlier,
\mathrm{tr.deg}\left(k(\psi_{1}(t),\dots,\psi_{4}(t))|k\right)\leq 3.
Since
\psi_{i}(t)\in k(u_{i}),
each
u_{i}
is algebraic over
k(\psi_{i}(t))\subseteq k(\psi_{1}(t),\dots,\psi_{4}(t)).
This implies
u_{1},\dots,u_{4}
are
k-
algebraically dependent.

Similarly, if
t\in k(u,u^{\prime})\setminus k
is a nonalgebraic solution of a Weierstrass differential equation then we have
t^{\prime 2}=\alpha^{2}(4t^{3}-g_{2}t^{2}-g_{3})
and for
i=1,2,
we have
(\psi_{i}(t))^{\prime 2}=\alpha^{2}(4(\psi_{i}(t))^{3}-g_{2}\psi_{i}(t)-g_{3}).
As noted earlier, we then have

(1:\psi_{1}(t):\psi_{1}(t)^{\prime}/\alpha)=(1:\psi_{2}(t):\psi_{2}(t)^{\prime}/\alpha)(1:c_{1}:c_{2}),\ \ \text{where}\ c_{1},c_{2}\in C.

That is,
\psi_{1}(k(t,t^{\prime}))=\psi_{2}(k(t,t^{\prime}))
and we obtain that
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are algebraically dependent over
k.
Thus, in this case, any two nonalgebraic solutions of
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
are
k-
algebraically dependent.

Now we shall consider the special case when
f(y,y^{\prime})=0
is an autonomous differential equation having a nonalgebraic solution
u
in an iterated strongly normal extension of
C.
In this case, we know from Corollary
6.4
that there is an element
t\in C(u,u^{\prime})
such that
t^{\prime}=1
or
t^{\prime}=ct
for some nonzero constant
c.
Then,
\psi_{i}(t)^{\prime}=1
or
\psi_{i}(t)^{\prime}=c\psi_{i}(t).
Thus
\psi_{1}(t)=\psi_{2}(t)+e
for some
e\in C
or
\psi_{1}(t)=e\psi_{2}(t)
for some nonzero
e\in C.
This shows that
\psi_{1}(C(t))=\psi_{2}(C(t))
and we obtain that
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are algebraically dependent over
C.
∎

7.3.
Rational autonomous differential equations

We shall now verify Conjecture
1.2

for the class of rational autonomous differential equations
y^{\prime}=h(y),
where
h
is a nonzero rational function over
C.
Let
f(y,y^{\prime})=y^{\prime}-h(y).
We first make the following observations.

(a)

The differential field
C(f)=C(y)
with
y^{\prime}=h(y),
has
C
as its field of constants. This is easily seen by noting that if
u^{\prime}=0
for
u\in C(y)\setminus C
then
y
is algebraic over the differential field
C(u),
which contains only constants. An easy calculation using the monic irreducible polynomial of
y
over
C(u)
shows that
y^{\prime}=0.
This implies
h=0
, a contradiction. Thus every rational autonomous differential equation admits a nonalgebraic solution.

(b)

If
h
has no zero in
C
then
1/h
is a polynomial over
C
and has the form
(
7.1.5
) described in Remark
7.5
. Thus if
C(y)
is a transcendental extension of
C
with
y^{\prime}=h(y)
then
C(y)
has a nonzero element
z
such that
z^{\prime}=1.
This shows that rational autonomous equations;
y^{\prime}=h(y)
with
h
having no zeros in
C
, are not of general type.

Suppose that
\alpha\in C
is a zero of
h.
Consider the rational function
g\in C(y)
defined by
h(y):=g(y-\alpha).
Then, we have

(y-\alpha)^{\prime}=y^{\prime}=h(y)=g(y-\alpha).

Thus, given a nonzero rational autonomous differential equation
y^{\prime}=h(y)
with
h
having a zero in
C,
it does no harm to assume that
h
has a zero at
y=0.
In view of Theorem
7.6
the conjecture is verified once we prove the following: If an autonomous differential equation

y^{\prime}=h(y),\quad h\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad h(0)=0

has at most one nonalgebraic solution in any given differential field extension
L
of
C
having
C
as its field of constants then the equation is not of general type.

Consider the purely transcendental differential field extension
C(t,y)
of
C,
where
t^{\prime}=h(t)
and
y^{\prime}=h(y).
From our hypothesis, there is an element
u\in C(t,y)\setminus C(t)
such that
u^{\prime}=0
. Then we have following equations

y^{\prime}=h(y)=\sum^{\infty}_{i=m}c_{i}y^{i},\qquad u=\sum^{\infty}_{i=p}b_{i}y^{i},

where
m\geq 1,

c_{i}\in C
for all
i\geq m
and
c_{m}\neq 0,

p
is an integer and
b_{i}\in\overline{C(t)}
for all
i\geq p
and
b_{p}\neq 0.

Taking derivatives, we obtain

0=u^{\prime}=\sum^{\infty}_{i=p}b^{\prime}_{i}y^{i}+\left(\sum^{\infty}_{i=m}c_{i}y^{i}\right)\left(\sum^{\infty}_{i=p}ib_{i}y^{i-1}\right).

We observe that

(i)

If
p=0
and
m\geq 2
then letting
l
be the least positive integer such that
b_{l}\neq 0,
we get
b_{l}^{\prime}=0
and that
b^{\prime}_{l+m-1}=-lc_{m}b_{l}\in C\setminus\{0\}
.

(ii)

If
p\neq 0
and
m\geq 2
then
b^{\prime}_{p}=0
and
b^{\prime}_{p+m-1}=-pb_{p}c_{m}\in C\setminus\{0\}.

(iii)

If
p=0
and
m=1
then letting
l
be the least positive integer such that
b_{l}\neq 0,
we get
b^{\prime}_{l}/b_{l}=-lc_{1}\in C\setminus\left\{0\right\}
.

(iv)

If
p\neq 0
and
m=1
then
b^{\prime}_{p}/b_{p}=-pc_{1}\in C\setminus\{0\}.

Thus, in the event that (
i
) or (
ii
) holds, we obtain an element
z\in C(t)
such that
z^{\prime}=1
and in the event that (
iii
) or (
iv
) holds, we obtain an element
z\in C(t)\setminus\{0\}
such that
z^{\prime}=cz
for some
c\in C\setminus\{0\}.
Now from Remark
7.5
, the equation
y^{\prime}=h(y)
is not of general type. Thus, we have verified the conjecture for the class of rational autonomous differential equations.

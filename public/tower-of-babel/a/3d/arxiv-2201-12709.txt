Low-Rank Tensor Completion Based on Bivariate Equivalent Minimax-Concave Penalty
Hongbing Zhang, Xinyi Liu, Hongtao Fan, Yajing Li, Yinlin Ye

Abstract
Low-rank tensor completion (LRTC) is an important problem in computer vision and machine learning. The minimax-concave penalty (MCP) function as a non-convex relaxation has achieved good results in the LRTC problem. To makes all the constant parameters of the MCP function as variables so that futherly improving the adaptability to the change of singular values in the LRTC problem, we propose the bivariate equivalent minimax-concave penalty (BEMCP) theorem. Applying the BEMCP theorem to tensor singular values leads to the bivariate equivalent weighted tensor Γ \Gamma -norm (BEWTGN) theorem, and we analyze and discuss its corresponding properties. Besides, to facilitate the solution of the LRTC problem, we give the proximal operators of the BEMCP theorem and BEWTGN. Meanwhile, we propose a BEMCP model for the LRTC problem, which is optimally solved based on alternating direction multiplier (ADMM). Finally, the proposed method is applied to the data restorations of multispectral image (MSI), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and color video (CV) in real-world, and the experimental results demonstrate that it outperforms the state-of-arts methods.

BIVARIATE EQUIVALENT MINIMAX-CONCAVE PENALTY

In this section, we will construct and obtain the BEMCP theorem, which turns

\gamma
into variables at the same time. Then the BEWTGN theorem is applied to tensor singular values to deduce the BEWTGN theorem, and its corresponding properties are analyzed and established.

Theorem 3
(Bivariate Equivalent Minimax-Concave Penalty (BEMCP))

Let
\lambda,\upsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0},\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{>1}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
. The MCP
h_{\gamma,\lambda}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
is the solution of the following optimization problem:

\displaystyle h_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)=\min_{\upsilon\geqslant 0}\dfrac{2\upsilon\rvert y\rvert+(\upsilon-\lambda\gamma)^{2}}{2\gamma}.

(10)

Proof:

Consider the following function

\displaystyle h(y,\upsilon)=\dfrac{2\upsilon\rvert y\rvert+(\upsilon-\lambda\gamma)^{2}}{2\gamma}.

(11)

Let
\upsilon^{\star}
denote the first-order critical point of
h(y,\upsilon)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\upsilon^{\star}=\arg\min_{\upsilon}h(y,\upsilon).

(12)

Since
h(y,\upsilon)
is differentiable with respect to
\upsilon
, setting

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial h(y,\upsilon)}{\partial\upsilon}\rvert_{\upsilon=\upsilon^{\star}}=0

(13)

gives

\displaystyle\upsilon^{\star}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\lambda\gamma-\rvert y\rvert,\quad\rvert y\rvert\leqslant\gamma\lambda,\\
0,\qquad\quad\,\,\rvert y\rvert\geqslant\gamma\lambda.\end{array}\right.

The BEMCP is given by
h_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)=h(y,\upsilon^{\star})
. Substituting for
\upsilon^{\star}
in

, we get

\displaystyle h_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\lambda\rvert y\rvert-\frac{y^{2}}{2\gamma},\qquad\,\rvert y\rvert\leqslant\gamma\lambda,\\
\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\gamma},\qquad\qquad\quad\rvert y\rvert\geqslant\gamma\lambda.\end{array}\right.

∎

Remark 2

Proposition 1

The BEWTGN defined in (
21
) satisfies the following properties:

(a) Non-negativity
: The BEWTGN is non-negative, i.e.,
\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}\geqslant 0
. The equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{Y}
is the null tensor.

(b) Concavity
:

\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}
is concave in the modulus of the singular values of
\mathcal{Y}
.

(c) Boundedness
: The BEWTGN is upper-bounded by the weighted nuclear norm, i.e.,
\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}\leqslant\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\Lambda,\ast}.

(d) Asymptotic nuclear norm property
: The BEWTGN approaches the weighted nuclear norm asymptotically, i.e.,
\lim\limits_{\Gamma\to\infty}\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}=\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\Lambda,\ast}.

(e) Unitary invariance
: The BEWTGN is unitary invariant, i.e.,
\|\mathcal{U}\ast\mathcal{Y}\ast\mathcal{V}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}=\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}
, for unitary tensor
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
.

Proof:

Let

\displaystyle p(\mathcal{Y})=\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\frac{\upsilon}{\Gamma},\ast}+\frac{1}{2}\|\frac{\upsilon-\Lambda\star\Gamma}{\Gamma\star\Gamma}\|_{F}^{2}.

(a) Since
p(\mathcal{Y})
is the sum of two non-negative functions,
\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}\geqslant 0
. The equality holds if
\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\frac{\upsilon}{\Gamma},\ast}=0
, i.e.,
\mathcal{Y}=\textbf{0}
or
\upsilon=\textbf{0}
, the latter being the trivial solution.

(b) The function
p(\mathcal{Y})
is separable of
\mathcal{Y}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle p(\mathcal{Y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{3}=1}^{\mathit{I}_{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{R}\frac{\upsilon_{(i_{3},j)}}{\Gamma_{(i_{3},j)}}\sigma_{j}(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i_{3})})+\frac{(\upsilon_{(i_{3},j)}-\Lambda_{(i_{3},j)}\Gamma_{(i_{3},j)})^{2}}{2\Gamma_{(i_{3},j)}},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{3}=1}^{\mathit{I}_{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{R}p(\sigma_{j}(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i_{3})}))

Since
p
is an affine function in
\sigma_{j}(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i_{3})})
, we can write, for
0\leqslant\alpha\leqslant 1
, that

\displaystyle\|(\alpha\mathcal{Y}_{1}+(1-\alpha)\mathcal{Y}_{2})\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\min_{\upsilon}p(\alpha\mathcal{Y}_{1}+(1-\alpha)\mathcal{Y}_{2})

\displaystyle\geqslant\min_{\upsilon}\alpha p(\mathcal{Y}_{1})+(1-\alpha)p(\mathcal{Y}_{2})

\displaystyle\geqslant\min_{\upsilon}\alpha p(\mathcal{Y}_{1})+\min_{\upsilon}(1-\alpha)p(\mathcal{Y}_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha\|\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}+(1-\alpha)\|\mathcal{Y}_{2}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}.

Hence,
\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}
is concave in the modulus of the singular values of
\mathcal{Y}
.

(c) For
\gamma>1
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}\leqslant\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\frac{\upsilon}{\Gamma},\ast}+\frac{1}{2}\|\frac{\upsilon-\Lambda\star\Gamma}{\Gamma\star\Gamma}\|_{F}^{2},\quad\forall\,\upsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathit{I}_{3}\times R}_{\geqslant 0}.

Therefore, the above inequality holds true even for
\upsilon=\Lambda\star\Gamma
, which leads to
\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}\leqslant\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\frac{\upsilon}{\Gamma},\ast}
.

(d) As
\Gamma\to\infty
, the optimal
\upsilon
in (
21
) is
\Lambda\star\Gamma
. Therefore,
\lim\limits_{\Gamma\to\infty}\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}=\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\frac{\upsilon}{\Gamma},\ast}
.

(e) Consider the definition of the tensor weighted nuclear norm

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\frac{\upsilon}{\Gamma},\ast}:=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{I}_{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{R}\frac{\upsilon_{(i,j)}}{\Gamma_{(i,j)}}\sigma_{j}(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i_{3})}),

(22)

where
\sigma(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)})
denote the singular values of
i
th slice of
\bar{\mathcal{Y}}
. Let
w=\frac{\upsilon}{\Gamma}
, we can experss

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{w,\ast}=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{I}_{3}}Tr(diag(w(i,:))\sqrt{\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)T}\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)}}),

(23)

where
diag(w(i,:))
denotes a diagonal matrix with diagonal entries coming from the elements of
i
th column vector of
w
, and
Tr(\cdot)
denotes the Trace operator.

Next, consider
\|\mathcal{U}\ast\mathcal{Y}\ast\mathcal{V}\|_{w,\ast}
, where
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
are unitary tensors:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{U}\ast\mathcal{Y}\ast\mathcal{V}\|_{w,\ast}:=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{I}_{3}}\|\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{(i)}\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)}\bar{\mathcal{V}}^{(i)}\|_{w(i,:),\ast}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{I}_{3}}Tr(diag(w(i,:))\sqrt{(\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{(i)}\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)}\bar{\mathcal{V}}^{(i)})^{T}(\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{(i)}\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)}\bar{\mathcal{V}}^{(i)})}.

(24)

Properties of the tensor generated by performing discrete Fourier transformation (DFT) show that
\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{(i)}
and
\bar{\mathcal{V}}^{(i)}
are unitary matrices. Then, we get the following formula:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{U}\ast\mathcal{Y}\ast\mathcal{V}\|_{w,\ast}=\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{w,\ast}.

(25)

Based on (
25
), we can express

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{U}\ast\mathcal{Y}\ast\mathcal{V}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\min_{\upsilon}\left\{\|\mathcal{U}\ast\mathcal{Y}\ast\mathcal{V}\|_{\frac{\upsilon}{\Gamma},\ast}+\frac{1}{2}\|\frac{\upsilon-\Lambda\star\Gamma}{\Gamma\star\Gamma}\|_{F}^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\min_{\upsilon}\left\{\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\frac{\upsilon}{\Gamma},\ast}+\frac{1}{2}\|\frac{\upsilon-\Lambda\star\Gamma}{\Gamma\star\Gamma}\|_{F}^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda},

which is the BEWTGN. This establishes the unitary invariance of the BEWTGN.
∎

IV
PROXIMAL OPERATORS FOR THE BEMCP THEOREM and BEWTGN

In this section, to solve the model established based on the BEMCP theorem, we present the proximal operators for the BEMCP theorem and BEWTGN.

Theorem 5
(Proximal operator for the BEMCP)

Consider the BEMCP given in (
10
). Its proximal operator denoted by
\mathit{P}_{\gamma,\lambda}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{>1}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
and defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathit{P}_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)=\arg\min_{g}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(g-y)^{2}+h_{\gamma,\lambda}(g)\right\},

(26)

is given by

\displaystyle\mathit{P}_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)=\min\left(\rvert y\rvert,\max\left(\frac{\gamma(\rvert y\rvert-{\lambda})}{\gamma-1},0\right)\right)sign(y).

(27)

Proof:

Let

\displaystyle\mathit{P}_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{g}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(g-y)^{2}+\min_{\upsilon\geqslant 0}\left\{\frac{2\upsilon\rvert g\rvert+(\upsilon-\lambda\gamma)^{2}}{2\gamma}\right\}\right\},

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{g}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(g-y)^{2}+h(g,\upsilon^{\ast})\right\},

where

\displaystyle\upsilon^{\ast}=\arg\min_{\upsilon\geqslant 0}h(g,\upsilon)=max(\lambda\gamma-|g|,0).

We must now determine
\mathit{P}_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)
for a given
\upsilon^{\ast}
. This is derived considering various values that
\upsilon^{\ast}
can take:

(1) Case1:
\upsilon^{\ast}=0
. Correspondingly,
h(g,\upsilon^{\ast})=\frac{\lambda^{2}\gamma}{2}
, and then

\displaystyle\mathit{P}_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)=\arg\min_{g}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(g-y)^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{2}\gamma}{2}\right\}=y.

The condition
\upsilon^{\ast}=0
translates to
|g|\geqslant\lambda\gamma
, therefore,

\displaystyle\mathit{P}_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)=y,\quad\text{for}\quad|g|\geqslant\lambda\gamma.

(28)

(2) Case2:
0<\upsilon^{\ast}<\lambda\gamma
. By definition of
\upsilon^{\ast}
, we have that

\displaystyle\upsilon^{\ast}=\lambda\gamma-|g|,\quad|g|\leqslant\lambda\gamma.

(29)

Further, considering
0<g\leqslant\lambda\gamma
gives
\upsilon^{\ast}=\lambda\gamma-g
, which result in

\displaystyle\mathit{P}_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\min_{g}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(g-y)^{2}+\frac{2\upsilon^{\ast}g+(\upsilon^{\ast}-\lambda\gamma)^{2}}{2\gamma}\right\},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}(y-\lambda)\quad\text{for}\quad\lambda<y<\lambda\gamma.

Similarly, considering
-\lambda\gamma\leqslant g<0
gives
\upsilon^{\ast}=\lambda\gamma+g
, which result in

\displaystyle\mathit{P}_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\min_{g}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(g-y)^{2}+\frac{2\upsilon^{\ast}(-g)+(\upsilon^{\ast}-\lambda\gamma)^{2}}{2\gamma}\right\},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}(-y-\lambda)\quad\text{for}\quad-\lambda\gamma<y<-\lambda.

Combining the two expressions brings

\displaystyle\mathit{P}_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}(|y|-\lambda)sign(y),\quad\text{for}\quad\lambda<|y|<\lambda\gamma.

(30)

(3) Case3:
\upsilon^{\ast}=\lambda\gamma
. This induces
g=0
, which implies
\mathit{P}_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)=0
. Substituting
\upsilon^{\ast}=\lambda\gamma
in the definition of
\mathit{P}_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)
gives

\displaystyle 0=\arg\min_{g}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(g-y)^{2}+\lambda|g|\right\}.

Considering the subdifferential, we get
0\in-y+\lambda\partial|g|
, or equivalently,
|y|\leqslant\lambda
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathit{P}_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)=0,\quad\text{for}\quad|y|\leqslant\lambda.

(31)

Combining equations (
28
), (
30
), and (
31
) yields

\displaystyle\mathit{P}_{\gamma,\lambda}(y)=\min\left(\rvert y\rvert,\max\left(\frac{\gamma(\rvert y\rvert-{\lambda})}{\gamma-1},0\right)\right)sign(y).

(32)

∎

Theorem 6
(Proximal operator for the BEWTGN)

Consider the BEWTGN given in (
21
). Its proximal operator denoted by
\mathit{S}_{\Gamma,\Lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{\mathit{I}_{1}\times\mathit{I}_{2}\times\mathit{I}_{3}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathit{I}_{1}\times\mathit{I}_{2}\times\mathit{I}_{3}},\Gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{R\times\mathit{I}_{3}}_{>1}
,
\bar{\Lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{R\times\mathit{I}_{3}}_{\geqslant 0}
,
R=\min\{\mathit{I}_{1},\mathit{I}_{2}\}
and defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathit{S}_{\Gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{Y})=\arg\min_{\mathcal{L}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{L}-\mathcal{Y}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\mathcal{L}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}\right\},

(33)

is given by

\displaystyle\mathit{S}_{\Gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{Y})=\mathcal{U}\ast\mathcal{S}_{1}\ast\mathcal{V}^{H}.

(34)

where
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
are derived from the t-SVD of
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{U}\ast\mathcal{S}_{2}\ast\mathcal{V}^{H}
. More importantly, the
i
th front slice of DFT of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
, i.e.,
\bar{\mathcal{S}}^{(i)}_{1}=\sigma(\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(i)})
and
\bar{\mathcal{S}}^{(i)}_{2}=\sigma(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)})
, has the following relationship:

\displaystyle\sigma(\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(i)})=\min\left\{\sigma(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)}),\max\left\{\dfrac{\Gamma_{(i,:)}(\sigma(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)})-\Lambda_{(i,:)})}{\Gamma_{(i,:)}-1},0\right\}\right\}.

Proof:

Let
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{U}\ast\mathcal{S}_{2}\ast\mathcal{V}^{H}
and
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{W}\ast\mathcal{S}_{1}\ast\mathcal{R}^{H}
be the t-SVD of
\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{L}
, respectively. Consider

\displaystyle\mathit{S}_{\Gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{Y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\mathcal{L}}\frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{L}-\mathcal{Y}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\mathcal{L}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\mathcal{L}}\frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{W}\ast\mathcal{S}_{1}\ast\mathcal{R}^{H}-\mathcal{U}\ast\mathcal{S}_{2}\ast\mathcal{V}^{H}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\mathcal{L}\|_{\Gamma,\Lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\bar{\mathcal{L}}}\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{I}_{3}}\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{\mathcal{W}}^{(i)}\ast\bar{\mathcal{S}_{1}}^{(i)}\ast\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{(i)H}-\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{(i)}

\displaystyle\ast\bar{\mathcal{S}_{2}}^{(i)}\ast\bar{\mathcal{V}}^{(i)H}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(i)}\|_{\Gamma_{(i,:)},\Lambda_{(i,:)}}.

It can be found that (
35
) is separable and can be divided into
\mathit{I}_{3}
sub-problems. For the
i
th sub-problem:

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(i)}}\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{\mathcal{W}}^{(i)}\ast\bar{\mathcal{S}_{1}}^{(i)}\ast\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{(i)H}-\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{(i)}\ast\bar{\mathcal{S}_{2}}^{(i)}\ast\bar{\mathcal{V}}^{(i)H}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(i)}\|_{\Gamma_{(i,:)},\Lambda_{(i,:)}}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(i)}}\frac{1}{2}Tr(\bar{\mathcal{S}_{1}}^{(i)}\bar{\mathcal{S}_{1}}^{(i)H})+\frac{1}{2}Tr(\bar{\mathcal{S}_{2}}^{(i)}\bar{\mathcal{S}_{2}}^{(i)H})

\displaystyle+Tr(\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(i)H}\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)})+\|\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(i)}\|_{\Gamma_{(i,:)},\Lambda_{(i,:)}}.

(36)

Invoking von Neumann’s trace inequality
[
38
]
, we can write

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(i)}}\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{\mathcal{W}}^{(i)}\ast\bar{\mathcal{S}_{1}}^{(i)}\ast\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{(i)H}-\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{(i)}\ast\bar{\mathcal{S}_{2}}^{(i)}\ast\bar{\mathcal{V}}^{(i)H}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(i)}\|_{\Gamma_{(i,:)},\Lambda_{(i,:)}}

\displaystyle\geqslant\arg\min_{\bar{\mathcal{S}_{1}}^{(i)}}\frac{1}{2}Tr(\bar{\mathcal{S}_{1}}^{(i)}\bar{\mathcal{S}_{1}}^{(i)H})+\frac{1}{2}Tr(\bar{\mathcal{S}_{2}}^{(i)}\bar{\mathcal{S}_{2}}^{(i)H})

\displaystyle+Tr(\bar{\mathcal{S}_{2}}^{(i)}\bar{\mathcal{S}_{1}}^{(i)H})+\|\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(i)}\|_{\Gamma_{(i,:)},\Lambda_{(i,:)}}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\sigma(\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(i)})}\frac{1}{2}\|\sigma(\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(i)})-\sigma(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)})\|_{F}^{2}+\|\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(i)}\|_{\Gamma_{(i,:)},\Lambda_{(i,:)}}.

The equality holds when
\bar{\mathcal{W}}^{(i)}=\bar{\mathcal{U}}^{(i)}
and
\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{(i)}=\bar{\mathcal{V}}^{(i)}
. Therefore, the optimal solution to (
35
) is obtained by solving the
problem given below:

\displaystyle\sigma(\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{(i)})=\min\left\{\sigma(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)}),\max\left\{\dfrac{\Gamma_{(i,:)}(\sigma(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)})-\Lambda_{(i,:)})}{\Gamma_{(i,:)}-1},0\right\}\right\}.

∎

V-C

The BEMCP model

Using the BEMCP Theorem, we get the following optimization model:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathcal{X}}\sum_{1\leqslant l_{1}<l_{2}\leqslant N}^{N}\alpha_{l_{1}l_{2}}\|\mathcal{X}_{(l_{1}l_{2})}\|_{\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}},\Lambda_{l_{1}l_{2}}}\,s.t.\,\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{Z})=\mathbf{0}.

(50)

Under the framework of the ADMM, the easy-to-implement optimization strategy could be provided to solve (
50
). We introduce a set of tensors
\{\mathcal{Y}_{l_{1}l_{2}}=\mathcal{X}\}_{1\leqslant l_{1}<l_{2}\leqslant N}^{N}
and transform optimization problem (
50
), in its augmented Lagrangian form, as follows:

\displaystyle J(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\Lambda,\Gamma,\upsilon,\mathcal{Q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leqslant l_{1}<l_{2}\leqslant N}^{N}\alpha_{l_{1}l_{2}}\|\mathcal{Y}_{l_{1}l_{2}(l_{1}l_{2})}\|_{\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}},\Lambda_{l_{1}l_{2}}}

(51)

\displaystyle+\frac{\rho_{l_{1}l_{2}}}{2}\|\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{Y}_{l_{1}l_{2}}+\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{l_{1}l_{2}}}{\rho_{l_{1}l_{2}}}\|_{F}^{2}\quad s.t.\quad\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{Z})=\mathbf{0},

where
\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are tensor sets;
\Lambda,\Gamma,\upsilon
are matrix sets;
\{\mathcal{Y}_{l_{1}l_{2}}=\mathcal{X}\}_{1\leqslant l_{1}<l_{2}\leqslant N}^{N}
;
\{\mathcal{Q}_{l_{1}l_{2}}\}_{1\leqslant l_{1}<l_{2}\leqslant N}^{N}
are Lagrangian multipliers;
\{\Lambda,\Gamma,\upsilon\}_{1\leqslant l_{1}<l_{2}\leqslant N}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathit{I}_{3}\times R}
are MCP variable sets;
\{\rho_{l_{1}l_{2}}\}_{1\leqslant l_{1}<l_{2}\leqslant N}^{N}>0
are the augmented Lagrangian parameters;
\alpha_{l_{1}l_{2}}\geqslant 0
are weights and
\sum_{1\leqslant l_{1}<l_{2}\leqslant N}^{N}\alpha_{l_{1}l_{2}}=1
.

Besides, variables
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\Lambda,\Gamma,\upsilon,\mathcal{Q}
are updated alternately in the order of
\mathcal{Y}\to\upsilon\to\Lambda\to\Gamma\to\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Q}
. The update equations are derived in the following.

Update
\mathcal{Y}
: Fix other variables, and the corresponding optimization are as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{l_{1}l_{2}}^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\mathcal{Y}_{l_{1}l_{2}}}\alpha_{l_{1}l_{2}}\|\mathcal{Y}_{l_{1}l_{2}(l_{1}l_{2})}\|_{\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}},\Lambda_{l_{1}l_{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\rho_{l_{1}l_{2}}}{2}\|\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{Y}_{l_{1}l_{2}}+\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{l_{1}l_{2}}}{\rho_{l_{1}l_{2}}}\|_{F}^{2}.

Calling Theorem
6
, the solution to the above optimization is given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{l_{1}l_{2}}^{+}=\mathit{S}_{\frac{\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}\rho_{l_{1}l_{2}}}{\alpha_{l_{1}l_{2}}},\frac{\Lambda_{l_{1}l_{2}}\rho_{l_{1}l_{2}}}{\alpha_{l_{1}l_{2}}}}(\mathcal{X}+\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{l_{1}l_{2}}}{\rho_{l_{1}l_{2}}}),

(52)

where
\mathit{S}
denotes the proximal operator defined in (
34
).

Update
\upsilon
: Retaining only those components in
\mathcal{Y}_{l_{1}l_{2}}
in (
51
) that depend on
\upsilon_{l_{1}l_{2}}
, we write

\displaystyle\upsilon_{l_{1}l_{2}}^{+}=\arg\min_{\upsilon_{l_{1}l_{2}}}\|\mathcal{Y}_{l_{1}l_{2}(l_{1}l_{2})}\|_{\frac{\upsilon_{l_{1}l_{2}}}{\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}},\ast}+\frac{1}{2}\|\frac{\upsilon_{l_{1}l_{2}}-\Lambda_{l_{1}l_{2}}\star\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}}{\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}\star\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}}\|_{F}^{2},

which has the following closed-form solution:

\displaystyle\upsilon_{l_{1}l_{2}(i_{3},:)}^{+}=\max(\Lambda_{l_{1}l_{2}(i_{3},:)}\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}(i_{3},:)}-\sigma(\mathcal{Y}_{l_{1}l_{2}(l_{1}l_{2})}^{+(i_{3})}),\bar{\epsilon}),

(53)

where the element values of vector
\bar{\epsilon}
are all small values close to 0, which will avoid the situation where
\Lambda
becomes 0 and
\Gamma
cannot be solved.

Update
\Lambda
: The update for
\Lambda_{l_{1}l_{2}}
has the following closed-form solution:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{l_{1}l_{2}}^{+}=\arg\min_{\Lambda_{l_{1}l_{2}}}\|\frac{\upsilon_{l_{1}l_{2}}-\Lambda_{l_{1}l_{2}}\star\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}}{\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}\star\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}}\|_{F}^{2}=\frac{\upsilon_{l_{1}l_{2}}^{+}}{\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}}.

(54)

Update
\Gamma
: The update for
\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}
has the following closed-form solution:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}^{+}=\arg\min_{\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}}\|\mathcal{Y}^{+}_{l_{1}l_{2}}\|_{\frac{\upsilon^{+}_{l_{1}l_{2}}}{\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}},\ast}+\frac{1}{2}\|\frac{\upsilon^{+}_{l_{1}l_{2}}-\Lambda^{+}_{l_{1}l_{2}}\star\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}}{\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}\star\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}}}\|_{F}^{2}.

(55)

Problem (
55
) is element-wise separable, and by proposition
1
we have the following results:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\Gamma_{l_{1}l_{2}},\Lambda_{l_{1}l_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{I}_{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{R}\frac{2\upsilon_{(i,j)}\sigma_{j}(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)})+(\upsilon_{(i,j)}-\Lambda_{(i,j)}\Gamma_{(i,j)})^{2}}{2\Gamma_{(i,j)}}.

Then, we consider the case of one of the elements individually:

\displaystyle\frac{2\upsilon_{(i,j)}\sigma_{j}(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)})+(\upsilon_{(i,j)}-\Lambda_{(i,j)}\Gamma_{(i,j)})^{2}}{2\Gamma_{(i,j)}}.

(56)

The closed form of
\Gamma_{(i,j)}
can be derived by setting the derivative of (
56
) to zero:

\displaystyle\frac{(\Lambda_{(i,j)}\Gamma_{(i,j)})^{2}-2\upsilon_{(i,j)}\sigma_{j}(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{(i)})-\upsilon_{(i,j)}^{2}}{2\Gamma_{(i,j)}^{2}}=0.

So,
\Gamma_{(i,j)}
is updated by the following:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{+}_{(i,j)}=\sqrt{\frac{2\upsilon^{+}_{(i,j)}\sigma_{j}(\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{+(i)})+\upsilon_{(i,j)}^{+2}}{\Lambda_{(i,j)}^{+2}}}.

(57)

Update
\mathcal{X}
: The closed form of
\mathcal{X}
can be derived by setting the derivative of (
51
) to zero. We can now update
\mathcal{X}
by the following equation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{+}=\mathcal{P}_{\Omega^{c}}(\frac{\sum_{1\leqslant l_{1}<l_{2}\leqslant N}\rho_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\mathcal{Y}_{l_{1}l_{2}}-\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{l_{1}l_{2}}}{\rho_{l_{1}l_{2}}})}{\sum_{1\leqslant l_{1}<l_{2}\leqslant N}\rho_{l_{1}l_{2}}})+\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(\mathcal{Z}).

(58)

Update
\mathcal{Q}
: Finally, multipliers
\mathcal{Q}_{l_{1}l_{2}}
are updated as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{l_{1}l_{2}}^{+}=\mathcal{Q}_{l_{1}l_{2}}+\rho_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{Y}_{l_{1}l_{2}}).

(59)

The optimization steps of BEMCP formulation are listed in Algorithm
3
. The main per-iteration cost lies in the update of
\mathcal{Y}_{l_{1}l_{2}}
, which requires computing t-SVD. The per-iteration complexity is
O(LE(\sum_{1\leqslant l_{1}<l_{2}\leqslant N}[log(le_{l_{1}l_{2}})+\min(\mathit{I}_{l_{1}},\mathit{I}_{l_{2}})]))
, where
LE=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathit{I}_{i}
and
le_{l_{1}l_{2}}=LE/(\mathit{I}_{l_{1}}\mathit{I}_{l_{2}})
.

Model Analysis

The two parameters
\alpha
and
\rho
are included in our proposed BEMCP model. In addition, the initial values of the variables
\upsilon
,
\Lambda
, and
\Gamma
also strongly influence on the efficient solution of the BEMCP model. The parameter values of
\alpha
and
\rho
follow the settings in the N-tubal rank
[
25
]
. The initial value of the variable
\upsilon_{0}
is

VII
CONCLUSION

This paper proposes a new structural equivalence theorem called the BEMCP theorem, which converts two constant parameters in the MCP function into variables so that it can be adaptively updated with the iterative update of tensor singular values in the algorithm. Compared with other existing methods, the effect is remarkable, and it is slightly better than the same type of the NMCP and EMCP methods. On this basis, we give the BEMCP model for solving the LRTC problem. Extensive experiments show that our method can achieve better visual and numerical quantitative results than the comparison methods.

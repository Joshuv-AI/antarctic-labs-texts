The Inverse of the Incidence Matrix of a Unicyclic Graph
Ryan Hessert, Sudipta Mallik

Abstract
The vertex-edge incidence matrix of a (connected) unicyclic graph G G is a square matrix which is invertible if and only if the cycle of G G is an odd cycle. A combinatorial formula of the inverse of the incidence matrix of an odd unicyclic graph was known. A combinatorial formula of the Moore-Penrose inverse of the incidence matrix of an even unicyclic graph is presented solving an open problem.



1
Introduction

The
Moore–Penrose inverse
of an
m\times n
real matrix
A
, denoted by
A^{+}
, is the
n\times m
real matrix that satisfies the following equations
[
4
]
:

AA^{+}A=A,A^{+}AA^{+}=A^{+},(AA^{+})^{T}=AA^{+},(A^{+}A)^{T}=A^{+}A.

When
A
is invertible,
A^{+}=A^{-1}
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on
n
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
with
m
edges
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{m}
. The vertex-edge
incidence matrix
of
G
, denoted by
M
, is the
n\times m
matrix whose
(i,j)
-entry is
1
if vertex
i
is incident with edge
e_{j}
and
0
otherwise. When
G
is connected, the distance between its vertices
i
and
j
, denoted by
d(i,j)
, is the minimum number of edges in a path between
i
and
j
.

Observation 1.1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph on
n
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
with
m
edges and the incidence matrix
M
. If
G
has no odd cycles (i.e.,
G
is bipartite), then

[(-1)^{d(i,j)}]M=O_{n,m}.

Proof.

The
(i,j)
-entry of
[(-1)^{d(i,j)}]M
is

(-1)^{d(i,r)}+(-1)^{d(i,s)}

where edge
e_{j}=\{r,s\}
. If
G
has no odd cycles, then
d(i,r)=d(i,s)\pm 1
which implies
(-1)^{d(i,r)}+(-1)^{d(i,s)}=0
.
∎

In 1965, Ijira first studied the Moore-Penrose inverse of the oriented incidence matrix of a graph in
[
8
]
. Bapat did the same for the Laplacian and the edge-Laplacian of trees
[
2
]
. Further research studied the same topic for different graphs such as distance regular graphs
[
1
,
3
]
. Meanwhile the signless Laplacian of graphs started being an active area of research
[
5
,
6
]
. Hessert and Mallik studied the Moore-Penrose inverses of the incidence matrix and the signless Laplacian of a tree and an odd unicyclic graph in
[
7
]
. In particular, they provided the following theorem about the Moore-Penrose inverse of the incidence matrix of a connected graph:

Theorem 1.2
.

[
7
, Theorem 2.15]

Let
G
be a connected graph on
n
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
with the incidence matrix
M
.

(a)

If
G
has an odd cycle, then
MM^{+}=I_{n}
.

(b)

If
G
has no odd cycles (i.e.,
G
is bipartite), then

MM^{+}=I_{n}-\frac{1}{n}[(-1)^{d(i,j)}].

A
unicyclic graph
on
n
vertices is a simple connected graph that has a unique cycle as a subgraph. A unicyclic graph on
n
vertices has
n
edges. From the preceding theorem, we have the following observation.

Observation 1.3
.

Let
G
be a unicyclic graph with the incidence matrix
M
. Then
M
is invertible if and only if
G
is an odd unicyclic graph.

A combinatorial formula of the inverse of the incidence matrix of an odd unicyclic graph is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
7
, Theorem 3.1]

Let
G
be an odd unicyclic graph on
n
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
and edges
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}
with the cycle
C
and the incidence matrix
M
. Then
M
is invertible and its inverse
M^{-1}=[a_{i,j}]
is given by

a_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\frac{(-1)^{d(e_{i},j)}}{2}&\text{ if }e_{i}\in C\\
0&\text{ if }e_{i}\not\in C\text{ and }j\in G\setminus e_{i}[C]\\
(-1)^{d(e_{i},j)}&\text{ if }e_{i}\not\in C\text{ and }j\not\in G\setminus e_{i}[C].\end{cases}

The notation
G\setminus e_{i}[C]
and
G\setminus e_{i}(C)
are described in Section 2. The open problem of finding a combinatorial formula of the Moore-Penrose inverse of the incidence matrix of an even unicyclic graph was posed in
[
7
, Open Problem 1(a)]
. We solve this open problem in this article.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be an even unicyclic graph on
n
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
with
n

e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}
. Suppose
C
is the cycle of

. Let
e_{i}=\{r_{i},s_{i}\}\in C

be the vertex on

(i)

d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j^{*})

(ii)

d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j)

d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(s_{i},j)
have opposite parities.

Proof.

(i)

Since

is even,

is odd. Therefore
d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j^{*})
and
d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(s_{i},j^{*})
have opposite parities.

(ii)

Note that
d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j)=d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j^{*})+d(j,j^{*})
and
d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(s_{i},j)=d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(s_{i},j^{*})+d(j,j^{*})
. Then

d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j)
and
d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(s_{i},j)
have opposite parities by (i).

∎

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Without loss of generality, suppose that

(-1)^{d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j)}\left(-nd_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j^{*})+\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},t)\right)=-nd_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j^{*})+\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},t)

(-1)^{d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(s_{i},j)}\left(-nd_{G\setminus e_{i}}(s_{i},j^{*})+\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(s_{i},t)\right)=nd_{G\setminus e_{i}}(s_{i},j^{*})-\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(s_{i},t).

Since
d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(s_{i},j^{*})=|C|-1-d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j^{*})
and

, we have

\displaystyle\;nd_{G\setminus e_{i}}(s_{i},j^{*})-\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(s_{i},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;n(|C|-1-d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j^{*}))-\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}(|C|-1-d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;n(|C|-1)-nd_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j^{*})-(|C|-1)\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}+\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},t)

\displaystyle\;n(|C|-1)-nd_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j^{*})-n(|C|-1)+\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-nd_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j^{*})+\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},t).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be an even unicyclic graph on
n
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
with the cycle
C
. Let
e_{i}
be an edge and
j
be a vertex of
G
.

(a)

Let
e_{i}\notin C
and
j\in G\setminus e_{i}(C)
. If
k\in G\setminus e_{i}(C)
, then
(-1)^{d(e_{i},k)+d(k,j)}=(-1)^{d(e_{i},j)}
.
If

, then
(-1)^{d(e_{i},k)+d(k,j)}=-(-1)^{d(e_{i},j)}
.

(b)

Let
e_{i}\notin C
and
j\in G\setminus e_{i}[C]
. If
k\in G\setminus e_{i}(C)
, then
(-1)^{d(e_{i},k)+d(k,j)}=-(-1)^{d(e_{i},j)}
.
If
k\in G\setminus e_{i}[C]
, then
(-1)^{d(e_{i},k)+d(k,j)}=(-1)^{d(e_{i},j)}
.

(c)

Let
e_{i}=\{r_{i},s_{i}\}\in C
and
j\in G
. Then for any vertex

,

(-1)^{d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},k)+d(k,j)}=(-1)^{d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j)}.

(d)

Let
e_{i}=\{r_{i},s_{i}\}\in C
. Then

\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},t)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},k^{*}).

Proof.

(a)

First let
k\in G\setminus e_{i}[C]
. Then
d(e_{i},k)+d(k,j)=d(e_{i},j)+2d(e_{i},k)+1
. So
d(e_{i},k)+d(k,j)
and

have opposite parities which implies

(-1)^{d(e_{i},k)+d(k,j)}=-(-1)^{d(e_{i},j)}.

Now let

. It suffices to show that
d(e_{i},k)+d(k,j)
and

have the same parity. If
k\in P_{e_{i}-j}
, then
d(e_{i},k)+d(k,j)=d(e_{i},j)
. Now suppose
k\notin P_{e_{i}-j}
. Define
k^{\prime}
as the vertex on
P_{e_{i}-k}\cap P_{e_{i}-j}
that is closest to
k
. For example,
k^{\prime}=j
when

. In all possible cases for

,
d(e_{i},k)+d(k,j)=d(e_{i},j)+2d(k,k^{\prime})
.

(b)

The proof is similar to that of (a).

(c)

Let
k
be a vertex of
G
. First note that
d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(k,j)
is either

(when there is a shortest path, not necessarily unique, between

|C|+2d(j,j^{*})+2d(k,k^{*})-d(k,j)

is even and
-d(k,j)

d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(k,j)

d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j)

d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},k)+d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(k,j)
have the same parity. The unique shortest path between vertices

and

If
k\in P^{\prime}_{r_{i}-j}

d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j)=d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},k)+d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(k,j)

k\notin P^{\prime}_{r_{i}-j}

Case 1.

In this case,
d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},j)=d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},k)-d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(k,j)

(d)

For each vertex

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d(e_{p_{1}},i)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}[C]|}{n}+\sum_{t=2}^{k}(-1)^{d(e_{p_{t}},i)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{t}}(C)|}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left(|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}[C]|+|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}(C)|-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{n}.

Case 2.
i\in C

Let
e_{p_{1}}
and
e_{p_{2}}
be the edges on
C
that are incident with vertex

.
Then

\displaystyle\;\;\sum_{t=1}^{k}h_{p_{t},i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{p_{1},i}+h_{p_{2},i}+\sum_{t=3}^{k}h_{p_{t},i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{d_{G\setminus e_{p_{1}}}(i,i)}}{n|C|}\left(-nd_{G\setminus e_{p_{1}}}(i,i)+\sum_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{p_{1}}}(i,t)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{(-1)^{d_{G\setminus e_{p_{2}}}(i,i)}}{n|C|}\left(-nd_{G\setminus e_{p_{2}}}(i,i)+\sum_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{p_{2}}}(i,t)\right)+\sum_{t=3}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{d(e_{p_{t}},i)}|C||G\setminus e_{p_{t}}(C)|}{n|C|}.

Since
d_{G\setminus e_{p_{1}}}(i,i)=d_{G\setminus e_{p_{2}}}(i,i)=d(e_{p_{t}},i)=0
for
t=3,4,\ldots,k
, the above becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n|C|}\sum_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{p_{1}}}(i,t)+\frac{1}{n|C|}\sum_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{p_{2}}}(i,t)+\sum_{t=3}^{k}\frac{|C||G\setminus e_{p_{t}}(C)|}{n|C|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n|C|}\left(\sum_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{p_{1}}}(i,t)+\sum_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{p_{2}}}(i,t)+|C|\sum_{t=3}^{k}|G\setminus e_{p_{t}}(C)|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n|C|}\left(\sum_{t\in C,t\neq i}n_{t}(d_{G\setminus e_{p_{1}}}(i,t)+d_{G\setminus e_{p_{2}}}(i,t))+|C|(n_{i}-1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n|C|}\left(\sum_{t\in C,t\neq i}n_{t}|C|+|C|(n_{i}-1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{t\in C,t\neq i}n_{t}+(n_{i}-1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left((n-n_{i})+(n_{i}-1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n-1}{n}.

Now suppose
i\neq j
. Without loss of generality, let
e_{p_{1}}
be on a shortest

path.

Case 1.

and
j\notin C

Subcase (i)

,

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k}h_{p_{t},j}

\displaystyle=h_{p_{1},j}+\sum_{t=2}^{k}h_{p_{t},j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d(e_{p_{1}},j)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}(C)|}{n}+\sum_{t=2}^{k}(-1)^{d(e_{p_{t}},j)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{t}}(C)|}{n}.

Since

and

for

, the above becomes

\displaystyle-(-1)^{d(i,j)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}(C)|}{n}+\sum_{t=2}^{k}(-1)^{d(i,j)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{t}}(C)|}{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n}\left(-|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}(C)|+\sum_{t=2}^{k}|G\setminus e_{p_{t}}(C)|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n}\left(-|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}(C)|+|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}(C)|-1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n}.

Subcase (ii)
i\in P_{j-C}

Let
e_{p_{2}}\in P_{i-C}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k}h_{p_{t},j}

\displaystyle=h_{p_{1},j}+h_{p_{2},j}+\sum_{t=3}^{k}h_{p_{t},j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d(e_{p_{1}},j)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}[C]|}{n}+(-1)^{d(e_{p_{2}},j)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{2}}[C]|}{n}+\sum_{t=3}^{k}(-1)^{d(e_{p_{t}},j)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{t}}(C)|}{n}.

Since
d(e_{p_{1},j})=d(i,j)-1
and
d(e_{p_{t},j})=d(i,j)
for
t=2,3,\ldots,k
, the above becomes

\displaystyle\;\;-(-1)^{d(i,j)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}[C]|}{n}+(-1)^{d(i,j)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{2}}[C]|}{n}+\sum_{t=3}^{k}(-1)^{d(i,j)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{t}}(C)|}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n}\left(-|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}[C]|+|G\setminus e_{p_{2}}[C]|+\sum_{t=3}^{k}|G\setminus e_{p_{t}}(C)|\right).

Since
|G\setminus e_{p_{2}}(C)|=1+|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}(C)|+\sum_{t=3}^{k}|G\setminus e_{p_{t}}(C)|
, the above becomes

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\frac{(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n}\left(-|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}[C]|+|G\setminus e_{p_{2}}[C]|+\left(|G\setminus e_{p_{2}}(C)|-|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}(C)|-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n}\left(\left(|G\setminus e_{p_{2}}[C]|+|G\setminus e_{p_{2}}(C)|\right)-\left(|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}[C]|+|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}(C)|\right)-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n}(n-n-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{-(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n}.

Subcase (iii)
j\in P_{i-C}
.

The proof is similar to that of Subcase (ii).

Case 2.
i\in C
and
j\in C

Let
e_{p_{2}}\in C
.

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k}h_{p_{t},j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{p_{1},j}+h_{p_{2},j}+\sum_{t=3}^{k}h_{p_{t},j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{d_{G\setminus e_{p_{1}}}(i,j)}}{n|C|}\left(-nd_{G\setminus e_{p_{1}}}(i,j)+\sum_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{p_{1}}}(i,t)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{(-1)^{d_{G\setminus e_{p_{2}}}(i,j)}}{n|C|}\left(-nd_{G\setminus e_{p_{2}}}(i,j)+\sum_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{p_{2}}}(i,t)\right)+\sum_{t=3}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n|C|}|C||G\setminus e_{t}(C)|

Since
d_{G\setminus e_{p_{1}}}(i,j)=|C|-d(i,j)
which has the same parity as
d(i,j)
, the above becomes

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n|C|}\left[\left(-n(|C|-d(i,j))+\sum_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{p_{1}}}(i,t)\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\left(-nd(i,j)+\sum_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{p_{2}}}(i,t)\right)+\left.\sum_{t=3}^{k}|C||G\setminus e_{t}(C)|\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n|C|}\left[-n|C|+\sum_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{p_{1}}}(i,t)+\sum_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{p_{2}}}(i,t)+\sum_{t=3}^{k}|C||G\setminus e_{t}(C)|\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n|C|}\left[-n|C|+\sum_{t\in C,t\neq i}n_{t}(d_{G\setminus e_{p_{1}}}(i,t)+d_{G\setminus e_{p_{2}}}(i,t))+\sum_{t=3}^{k}|C||G\setminus e_{t}(C)|\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n|C|}\left[-n|C|+\sum_{t\in C,t\neq i}n_{t}|C|+|C|\sum_{t=3}^{k}|G\setminus e_{t}(C)|\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n}\left[-n+\sum_{t\in C,t\neq i}n_{t}+\sum_{t=3}^{k}|G\setminus e_{t}(C)|\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n}\left[-n+(n-n_{i})+(n_{i}-1)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n}.

and

Then

\sum_{t=1}^{k}h_{p_{t},j}=h_{p_{1},j}+\sum_{t=2}^{k}h_{p_{t},j}=(-1)^{d(e_{p_{1}},j)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}(C)|}{n}+\sum_{t=2}^{k}(-1)^{d(e_{p_{t}},j)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{t}}(C)|}{n}.

and

for

, the above becomes

\displaystyle-(-1)^{d(i,j)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}(C)|}{n}+\sum_{t=2}^{k}(-1)^{d(i,j)}\frac{|G\setminus e_{p_{t}}(C)|}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n}\left(-|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}(C)|+\sum_{t=2}^{k}|G\setminus e_{p_{t}}(C)|\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n}\left(-|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}(C)|+|G\setminus e_{p_{1}}(C)|-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{-(-1)^{d(i,j)}}{n}.

Case 4.
i\in C
and

The proof in this case is similar to that of Case 3.

∎

The preceding theorem gave
MH
. The following result gives a combinatorial formula for
HM
. In a connected graph, the distance between two edges
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
, denoted by
d(e_{i},e_{j})
, is the number of edges on a shortest path between a vertex on
e_{i}
and a vertex on
e_{j}
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
G
be an even unicyclic graph on
n
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
with
n
edges
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}
. Suppose
C
is the cycle of
G
and
M
is the incidence matrix of
G
. For the matrix
H
defined in (
2.1
),
HM
is given by

(HM)_{i,j}=\frac{(-1)^{d(e_{i},e_{j})}}{|C|}\begin{cases}|C|&\text{ if }e_{i}=e_{j}\notin C\\
|C|-1&\text{ if }e_{i}=e_{j}\in C\\
1&\text{ if }e_{i}\in C\text{ and }e_{j}\in C,\;i\neq j\\
0&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

(2.2)

Proof.

Let
e_{i}=\{r_{i},s_{i}\}
and
e_{j}=\{r_{j},s_{j}\}
.

Case 1.
i=j

Note that
(HM)_{i,i}=h_{i,r_{i}}+h_{i,s_{i}}
.

Subcase (a)
e_{i}\notin C

Since
d(e_{i},r_{i})=d(e_{i},s_{i})=0
and
(r_{i},s_{i})
is in
V(G\setminus e_{i}(C))\times V(G\setminus e_{i}[C])
or in
V(G\setminus e_{i}[C])\times V(G\setminus e_{i}(C))
, we have

(HM)_{i,i}=\frac{1}{n}|G\setminus e_{i}(C)|+\frac{1}{n}|G\setminus e_{i}[C]|=\frac{1}{n}n=1.

Subcase (b)
e_{i}\in C

\displaystyle(HM)_{i,i}=

\displaystyle h_{i,r_{i}}+h_{i,s_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n|C|}\left[(-1)^{d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},r_{i})}\left(-nd_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},r_{i}^{*})+\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},t)\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.(-1)^{d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},s_{i})}\left(-nd_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},s_{i}^{*})+\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},t)\right)\right].

Since
d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},r_{i})=0
and
d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},s_{i})=|C|-1
is odd, the above becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n|C|}\left[\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},t)-\left(-n(|C|-1)+\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},t)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n|C|}\left[n(|C|-1)+\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},t)-\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},t)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n|C|}\left[n(|C|-1)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|C|-1}{|C|}.

Case 2.
i\neq j

Subcase (a)
e_{i}\notin C
and
e_{j}\notin C

Note that
r_{j}
and
s_{j}
both are either in
G\setminus e_{i}(C)
or in
G\setminus e_{i}[C]
and
d(e_{i},r_{j})=d(e_{i},s_{j})\pm 1
. Then

(HM)_{i,j}=h_{i,r_{j}}+h_{i,s_{j}}=\frac{(-1)^{d(e_{i},r_{j})}G\setminus e_{i}[C]}{n}+\frac{(-1)^{d(e_{i},s_{j})}G\setminus e_{i}[C]}{n}=0

or

(HM)_{i,j}=h_{i,r_{j}}+h_{i,s_{j}}=\frac{(-1)^{d(e_{i},r_{j})}G\setminus e_{i}(C)}{n}+\frac{(-1)^{d(e_{i},s_{j})}G\setminus e_{i}(C)}{n}=0.

Subcase (b)
e_{i}\in C
and
e_{j}\in C

Without loss of generality, let a shortest path between
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
be the shortest path between
r_{i}
and
s_{j}
. Then
d(e_{i},e_{j})=d(r_{i},s_{j})=d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},s_{j})
.

\displaystyle(HM)_{i,j}

\displaystyle=h_{i,r_{j}}+h_{i,s_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n|C|}(-1)^{d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},r_{j})}\left(-nd_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},r_{j}^{*})+\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{j}}(r_{i},t)\right)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\frac{1}{n|C|}(-1)^{d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},s_{j})}\left(-nd_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},s_{j}^{*})+\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{j}}(r_{i},t)\right)

Since
r_{i}^{*}=r_{i}
,
s_{i}^{*}=s_{i}
, and
d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},r_{j})=d_{G\setminus e_{i}}(r_{i},s_{j})+1=d(e_{i},e_{j})+1
, the above becomes

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\frac{1}{n|C|}(-1)^{d(e_{i},e_{j})+1}\left(-n(d(e_{i},e_{j})+1)+\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{j}}(r_{i},t)\right)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\frac{1}{n|C|}(-1)^{d(e_{i},e_{j})}\left(-nd(e_{i},e_{j})+\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{j}}(r_{i},t)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n|C|}(-1)^{d(e_{i},e_{j})}\left(nd(e_{i},e_{j})+n-\cancel{\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{j}}(r_{i},t)}-nd(e_{i},e_{j})+\cancel{\sum\limits_{t\in C}n_{t}d_{G\setminus e_{j}}(r_{i},t)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n|C|}(-1)^{d(e_{i},e_{j})}n

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|C|}(-1)^{d(e_{i},e_{j})}.

Subcase (c)
e_{i}\notin C
and
e_{j}\in C

In this case,
r_{j}
and
s_{j}
are in
G\setminus e_{i}[C]
and
d(e_{i},r_{j})=d(e_{i},s_{j})\pm 1
. Then

(HM)_{i,j}=h_{i,r_{j}}+h_{i,s_{j}}=\frac{(-1)^{d(e_{i},r_{j})}G\setminus e_{i}(C)}{n}+\frac{(-1)^{d(e_{i},s_{j})}G\setminus e_{i}(C)}{n}=0.

Subcase (d)
e_{i}\in C
and
e_{j}\notin C

The proof is similar to that of Subcase (c).
∎

Now we are ready to state and prove our main result.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
G
be an even unicyclic graph on
n
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
and
n
edges
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}
with the cycle
C
and the incidence matrix
M
. Then the matrix
H
defined in (
2.1
) is the Moore-Penrose inverse of
M
.

Proof.

Since
G
is an even unicyclic graph,
G
is bipartite. Then by Theorem
2.5
,

MH=I_{n}-\frac{1}{n}[(-1)^{d(i,j)}]\text{ and }H[(-1)^{d(i,j)}]=O.

Then
HMH=H
and
(MH)^{T}=MH
. To prove
H=M^{+}
, it suffices to show that
HM
is symmetric and
MHM=M
. Since
[(-1)^{d(i,j)}]M=O
by Observation
1.1
,

MHM=M-\frac{1}{n}[(-1)^{d(i,j)}]M=M.

It remains to show that
HM
is symmetric which is evident from (
2.2
) in Theorem
2.6
.
∎

By (
2.1
) and Theorem
2.6
, we have the following corollary:

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
G
be an even unicyclic graph on
n
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
and
n
edges
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}
with the cycle
C
. Suppose
M
is the incidence matrix of
G
with its Moore-Penrose inverse
M^{+}=[m_{ij}^{+}]
. Then the following hold:

(a)

m_{ij}^{+}=\frac{n-1}{n}
if and only if edge
e_{i}
is a pendant edge incident with pendant vertex
j
.

(b)

The
(i,i)
-entry of
M^{+}M
is
\frac{|C|-1}{|C|}
if and only if edge
e_{i}
is on
C
.

3
Open Problems

We found a combinatorial formula for the Moore-Penrose inverse
M^{+}
of the incidence matrix
M
of an even unicyclic graph. Using
M^{+}
, we can find the Moore-Penrose inverses of the signless Laplacian
Q=MM^{+}
and signless edge-Laplacian
S=M^{+}M
as follows:

Q^{+}=(MM^{T})^{+}=(M^{T})^{+}M^{+}=(M^{+})^{T}M^{+},

S^{+}=(M^{T}M)^{+}=M^{+}(M^{T})^{+}=M^{+}(M^{+})^{T}.

But it still remains an open problem to find simple and compact combinatorial formulas for
Q^{+}
and
S^{+}
for even unicyclic graphs (like that in Theorem 3.5 and Theorem 3.9 in
[
7
]
). It is just a small part of the bigger problem of finding the same for bipartite graphs.

Another open problem is to extend Bapat’s work on trees
[
2
]
to unicyclic graphs: Find combinatorial formulas for the Moore-Penrose inverse of an oriented incidence matrix
N
and the Laplacian matrix
L=NN^{T}
of a unicyclic graph.

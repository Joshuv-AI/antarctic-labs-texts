Probing the Weinberg Operator at Colliders
Benjamin Fuks, Jonas Neundorf, Krisztian Peters, Richard Ruiz, Matthias Saimpert

Abstract

Introduction
– Among the most pressing mysteries shared in cosmology, nuclear, and high-energy physics is whether neutrinos are their own antiparticles
Ellis et al. 2019
;
Eur 2020
. This importance follows from Majorana neutrinos being necessary ingredients for standard leptogenesis, grand unification, as well as new gauge symmetries. Discovering that neutrinos are Majorana particles would indicate that lepton number (LN) symmetries are not conserved below the electroweak (EW) scale, and demonstrate the existence of a mass-generating mechanism beyond those responsible for chiral and EW symmetry breaking (EWSB).

Motivated by this, broad, complementary approaches are taken to explore the nature of neutrinos
Atre et al. 2009
;
Rodejohann 2011
;
Bilenky and Giunti 2015
;
Deppisch et al. 2015
;
Cai et al. 2017
;
Cai et al. 2018
;
Cirigliano et al. 2018
;
Dolinski et al. 2019
. A foremost probe is the search for the neutrinoless
\beta\beta
process
({0\nu\beta\beta})
in decays of nuclei. This is characterized by the transition
(A,Z)\to(A,Z+2)
and the appearance of two same-sign electrons but an absence of neutrinos in the final state. While no discovery has been confirmed, and assuming that the decay is mediated solely by the light neutrinos observed in nature, searches place upper limits of
79-180
meV
at 90% confidence level (CL)
Agostini et al. 2020
on the so-called effective
\beta\beta
Majorana mass, given by
Klapdor-Kleingrothaus et al. 2001

|m_{ee}|=\left|\sum_{k=1}^{3}U_{ek}m_{\nu_{k}}U_{ek}\right|.

(1)

In this definition,
m_{\nu_{k}}
are the mass eigenvalues of the three light neutrinos and
U_{\ell k}
are the Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata (PMNS) mixing matrix elements.

From the perspective that the Standard Model (SM) of particle physics is a low-energy effective field theory (EFT), Majorana neutrino masses, and hence
|m_{ee}|
, can be generated most minimally
Ma 1998
;
Bonnet et al. 2012
at dimension
d=5
from the LN-violating Weinberg operator
Weinberg 1979
:

{\cal L}_{5}=\ \frac{C_{5}^{\ell\ell^{\prime}}}{\Lambda}\big[\Phi\!\cdot\!\overline{L}^{c}_{\ell}\big]\big[L_{\ell^{\prime}}\!\!\cdot\!\Phi\big]+\text{H.c.}

(2)

Here,
\Lambda
is the scale at which the particles responsible for LN violation become relevant degrees of freedom;
C_{5}^{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
is a flavor-dependent Wilson coefficient;
L_{\ell}^{T}=(\nu_{\ell},\ell)
is the left-handed (LH) lepton doublet; and
\Phi
is the SM Higgs doublet, whose vacuum expectation value (vev)
v=\sqrt{2}\langle\Phi\rangle\approx 246{\rm~GeV}
generates the quantity

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=C_{5}^{\ell\ell^{\prime}}v^{2}/\Lambda}.

(3)

As the Weinberg operator can be realized by tree- and loop-level Seesaw models
Ma 1998
;
Bonnet et al. 2012
;
Cai et al. 2017
, limits on
|m_{ee}|
translate into lower bounds on the Seesaw scale of about
(\Lambda/C_{5}^{ee})\gtrsim(3.3-7.6)\cdot 10^{14}{\rm~GeV}
. While stringent, a caveat of this constraint is its flavor dependence. For instance,
C_{5}^{ee}
can be zero due to a flavor symmetry
Jenkins 2009
, or be immeasurably small due to accidental cancellations
Asaka et al. 2021a
;
Asaka et al. 2020
;
Asaka et al. 2021b
. More generally, the production of same-sign leptons involving muons or taus in
(A,Z)\to(A,Z+2)
decays is kinematically forbidden. Their production requires higher energies, implying a lack of sensitivity for non-electron flavors of
C_{5}^{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
at
{0\nu\beta\beta}
decay experiments.

The Standard Model at Dimension Five
–
To describe Majorana neutrino masses and the
{0\nu\beta\beta}
process from
d=5
interactions, we work in the SM effective field theory
Weinberg 1980
and extend the SM Lagrangian
(\mathcal{L}_{\rm SM})
by
gauge-invariant operators of
d>4
. In the canonical representation
Grzadkowski et al. 2010
, the Lagrangian is given by
Weinberg 1979

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm SM~EFT}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm SM}+\mathcal{L}_{5}+\mathcal{O}\left(\Lambda^{-2}\right),

(4)

where
\mathcal{L}_{5}
is defined in Eq. (
2
). By the power counting of Ref.
Grzadkowski et al. 2010
, the Weinberg operator is the only gauge-invariant operator at
d=5
in the SM
Weinberg 1979
;
Kobach 2016
.

After EWSB, the Higgs field can be expanded about its vev, which in the unitary gauge reads
\sqrt{2}\Phi\approx v+h
, where
h
is the Higgs boson. The resulting Lagrangian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{5}=

\displaystyle-\frac{C_{5}^{\ell\ell^{\prime}}}{2\Lambda}hh\overline{\nu^{c}_{\ell}}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}}-\frac{C_{5}^{\ell\ell^{\prime}}v}{\Lambda}h\overline{\nu^{c}_{\ell}}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}}

\displaystyle-\frac{C_{5}^{\ell\ell^{\prime}}v^{2}}{2\Lambda}\overline{\nu^{c}_{\ell}}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}}+\text{H.c.}

(5)

Here,
C^{\ell\ell^{\prime}}_{5}
is defined in the flavor basis. The minus signs above originate from the SU
(2)_{L}
-invariant product
\Phi\cdot\overline{L^{c}}=\Phi^{i}\varepsilon_{ij}\overline{L^{cj}}
, with
\varepsilon_{12}=1
. While the first two terms in Eq. (
5
) signify double- and single-Higgs couplings to neutrinos of flavors
\ell\ell^{\prime}
, the third term generates the
3\times 3
LH Majorana mass matrix

m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
, as defined in Eq. (
3
).
After rotating
m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
into the mass basis, the resulting eigenvalues parametrize the three neutrino mass eigenstates
m_{\nu_{k}}
that describe neutrino oscillation data.

We make no assumption on the structure of
C_{5}^{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
. It is therefore possible under this framework that one neutrino is massless, as allowed by data
Wyler and Wolfenstein 1983
; that all masses scale as
m_{\nu_{k}}\sim\mathcal{O}(m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}})
, indicating minor fine tuning; or that
m_{\nu_{k}}\ll m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
, indicating strong cancellations among the
m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
elements. As nuclear searches are only sensitive to
|m_{ee}|
, the latter possibilities remain under-explored.

The
{0\nu\beta\beta}
Process at Dimension Five
– A goal of this work is to estimate the sensitivity of the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) to the
{0\nu\beta\beta}
process, and hence the Weinberg operator. When simulating the Weinberg operator at the LHC, difficulties arise if working in the neutrinos’ mass eigenbasis. There,
d=5
vertices are proportional to
m_{\nu_{k}}
, which are unknown and small on LHC scales, and to
U_{\ell k}
, which carry unknown phases. So while the transition in Fig.
1
may proceed through a non-trivial incoherent sum of intermediate states, individual contributions may be too small for practical computations.

We propose a solution to this complication by working in the neutrino flavor basis and treating the mass term in Eq. (
5
) as a “two-point vertex”. From this perspective, the Weinberg operator in Fig.
1
couples one massless, LH neutrino of momentum
p
and flavor
\ell
with the conjugate of a second neutrino of momentum
p
and flavor
\ell^{\prime}
. After contracting Dirac matrices, the LN-violating
(\nu_{\ell}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}}^{c})
current in Fig.
1
reduces to the ratio of
m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
and the squared virtuality
p^{2}
. Explicitly, its graph simplifies to:

Up to corrections of
\mathcal{O}(|m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{2}/p^{2}|)
, which are assumed small, one can identify the rightmost ratio as the right-handed (RH) helicity state of an intermediate fermion with mass
m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
and momentum
p
. That is, one can write

\displaystyle\gamma^{\alpha}P_{L}\frac{i\left(\not\!p+m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\right)}{p^{2}-m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{2}}\gamma^{\beta}P_{R}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\alpha}P_{L}\frac{im_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}}{p^{2}-m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{2}}P_{L}\gamma^{\beta}

(6)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\alpha}P_{L}\frac{im_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}}{p^{2}}P_{L}\gamma^{\beta}

\displaystyle\times\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\left|\frac{m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{2}}{p^{2}}\right|\right)\right],

(7)

where
P_{R/L}=\frac{1}{2}(1\pm\gamma^{5})
are the usual chiral projection operators in four-component notation, and recover the same ratio at leading power of the expansion. Intuitively, this identification follows from the inversion of helicity in LN-violating currents as discussed in Refs.
Kayser 1982
;
Mohapatra and Pal 1998
;
Denner et al. 1992a
;
Denner et al. 1992b
;
Han et al. 2013
;
Ruiz 2021
;
Fuks et al. 2020
.

As a result, up to corrections of
\mathcal{O}(|m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{2}/p^{2}|)
, the
(\nu_{\ell}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}}^{c})
current itself can be modeled as an unphysical Majorana neutrino
N
with mass
m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
that couples to the
W
boson and all charged leptons
\ell
via the Lagrangian

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{L}=-\frac{g_{W}}{\sqrt{2}}W^{+}_{\mu}\sum_{\ell=e}^{\tau}\overline{N}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\ell^{-}+{\rm H.c.}

(8)

Here,
g_{W}\approx 0.65
is the SU
(2)_{L}
weak coupling constant. Up to factors of active-sterile mixing, Eq. (
8
) is identical to the interaction Lagrangian in the Phenomenological Type I Seesaw model
del Aguila and Aguilar-Saavedra 2009
;
Atre et al. 2009
, and therefore can also be employed in LN-violating decays of hadrons and leptons.

The
d=5
,
{0\nu\beta\beta}
Process at the LHC
– In LHC collisions the LN-violating
{0\nu\beta\beta}
process occurs through the scattering of two same-sign
W
bosons that are sourced from quarks and antiquarks, and exit as two high-
p_{T}
jets. At the hadronic level, the collider signature is given by

p~p~\to~j~j~\ell^{\pm}~\ell^{{}^{\prime}\pm}+X,

(10)

where
X
represents the additional hadronic and electromagnetic activity that may exist in the inclusive process.

To identify the dependence of Eq. (
10
) on the Weinberg operator, we consider the Effective
W
Approximation
Dawson 1985
;
Kane et al. 1984
;
Kunszt and Soper 1988
and treat the incoming
W^{\pm}W^{\pm}
pair as perturbative constituents of the proton. In this limit, we find that the
W^{\pm}W^{\pm}\to\ell^{\pm}\ell^{{}^{\prime}\pm}
sub-process is dominated by the scattering of longitudinal
W
bosons. After summing over all external helicities, the spin-averaged, parton-level cross section for the
2\to 2
process is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}(W^{+}W^{+}

\displaystyle\to\ell^{+}\ell^{{}^{\prime}+})

\displaystyle=\frac{(2-\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}})}{2\pi~3^{2}}\left|\frac{C_{5}^{\ell\ell^{\prime}}}{\Lambda}\right|^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{m_{W}^{2}}{M_{WW}^{2}}\right).

(11)

This shows that like in nuclear experiments the
{0\nu\beta\beta}
rate at the LHC scales as
\sigma\sim|m_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}|^{2}\propto|C^{\ell\ell^{\prime}}_{5}/\Lambda|^{2}
.

Using this scaling behavior, we have checked that setting
\Lambda\ll 200{\rm~TeV}
in simulations with the
SMWeinberg
UFO will generate unphysical cross sections. This is due to a breakdown of the expansion in Eq. (
7
),
which requires
v^{2}/\Lambda
to be small compared to the virtuality of
the internal
(\nu_{\ell}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}}^{c})
current. For the LHC and beyond, physical rates can be obtained by choosing, for example,
\Lambda=200{\rm~TeV}
and using the relationship

\sigma(\Lambda)=\sigma(\Lambda=200{\rm~TeV})\times\left(\frac{200{\rm~TeV}}{\Lambda}\right)^{2}.

(12)

Sensitivity to the Weinberg Operator
–
To quantify any excess of events, we apply a Poisson-counting likelihood with a background rate uncertainty that is constrained by an auxiliary Poisson measurement
ATLAS 2020
;
Cousins et al. 2008
. Assuming a
\delta_{b}=20\%
systematic uncertainty in the background, we estimate the sensitivity at 95% CL to
|C_{5}^{\mu\mu}|/\Lambda\propto m_{\mu\mu}
by fixing our signal significance to
Z\approx 2
and the number of signal events
n_{s}
to

n_{s}=n_{s}^{0}\times|C_{5}^{\mu\mu}|^{2}\left(\frac{200{\rm~TeV}}{\Lambda}\right)^{2},

(13)

where
n_{s}^{0}
is the number of signal events for our benchmark inputs. We then solve this equality for
|C_{5}^{\mu\mu}|
. With
\mathcal{L}=300{\rm~fb^{-1}}~(3{\rm~ab^{-1}})
, we report that the LHC (HL-LHC) is sensitive at 95% CL to scales below

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Lambda/|C_{5}^{\mu\mu}|\lesssim 8.3~(11){\rm~TeV}.}

(14)

These translate into effective
\mu\mu
Majorana masses of

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|m_{\mu\mu}|\gtrsim 7.3~(5.4){\rm~GeV}.}

(15)

Conclusions
– If the Weinberg operator is present in nature and is accessible at collider energies, then a key prediction are processes that violate LN, such as the
{0\nu\beta\beta}
transition, and possibly charged lepton flavor. Motivated by the flavor limitations of nuclear decay experiments, we have investigated the LHC’s sensitivity to the Weinberg operator using the
W^{\pm}W^{\pm}\to\ell^{\pm}_{i}\ell^{\pm}_{j}
process, which permits muon- and tau-flavored final states. We find sensitivity that exceeds representative searches at
B
- and
K
-meson factories, and establishes a complementarity across accelerator facilities in the search for the Weinberg operator.

In a generic gauge, the Higgs field in terms of the EW Goldstone bosons
G^{\pm,0}

\sqrt{2}\Phi=(-i\sqrt{2}G^{+},v+h+iG^{0})^{T}

\displaystyle L_{\ell}\!\!\cdot\!\Phi

\displaystyle=L_{\ell}^{i}\varepsilon_{ij}\Phi^{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\nu_{\ell}(v+h+iG^{0})+i\ell G^{+},

(16)

\displaystyle\Phi\!\cdot\!\overline{L}^{c}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=-iG^{+}\overline{\ell^{c}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(v+h+iG^{0})\overline{\nu^{c}_{\ell}}.

(17)

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{5}=

\displaystyle-\frac{C_{5}^{\ell\ell^{\prime}}}{2\Lambda}\left(v^{2}+2vh+hh\right)\overline{\nu^{c}_{\ell}}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}}

(18)

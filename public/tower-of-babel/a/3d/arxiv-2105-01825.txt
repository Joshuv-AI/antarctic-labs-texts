Inconsquential results on the Merino-Welsh conjecture for Tutte polynomials
Joseph P.S. Kung

Abstract
. The Merino-Welsh conjectures say that subject to conditions, there is an inequality among the Tutte-polynomial evaluations T ⁡ ( M , 2 , 0 ) T(M;2,0) , T ⁡ ( M , 0 , 2 ) T(M;0,2) , and T ⁡ ( M , 1 , 1 ) T(M;1,1) . We present three results on a Merino-Welsh conjecture. These results are “inconsequential” in the sense that although they imply a version of the conjecture for many matroids, they seem to be dead ends.



1.
Merino-Welsh inequalities

There are many variations on the Merino-Welsh conjecture. They are of the form “subject to conditions, there is an inequality involving the evaluations

T(M;2,0)
,
T(M;0,2)
, and
T(M;1,1)
of the Tutte polynomial of the matroid
M
”. We shall consider the following version.

Conjecture 1.1
.

If a matroid
M
has no loops or isthmuses, then

[formula]

T(M;2,0)+T(M;0,2)\geq 2T(M;1,1),

with equality if and only if
M
is a direct sum of
U_{1,2}
, the rank-
1
matroid consisting of two parallel elements.

The focus of this paper
is on finding sufficient conditions on a matroid so that inequality (1.1) holds.

As background, we note that
T(M;2,0)
is the number of no-broken-circuit or nbc sets and
T(M;1,1)
the number of bases in
M
. Hence Conjecture
1.1
asserts that the average number of nbc sets in
M
and its dual exceeds the average number of bases in
M
and its dual. A counting or algebraic-geometry proof of Conjecture
1.1
is quite possible. From the counting interpretation,
T(M;2,0)
and
T(M;0,2)
are positive integers. We shall tacitly use this fact, as well as the fact due to Tutte that the coefficients of his polynomial are non-negative.

2.
A density condition

The first result is that a sufficiently dense matroid satisfies Conjecture
1.1
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
M
be an isthmus-free matroid having size
n
and rank
r
. Then if
r\geq 4
and

n\geq\lceil r(\log r+\log\log r+\log\log\log r)\rceil,

with the logarithms in base
2
, then
T(M;0,2)\geq 2T(M;1,1)
. In particular, inequality (1.1) holds for
M
.

The theorem is true for the simple reason that exponential growth beats polynomial growth in the long run.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
r\geq 4
and
n\geq\lceil r(\log r+\log\log r+\log\log\log r)\rceil,
then
2^{n-r}\geq 2\binom{n}{r}
.

Consider the function

f(n,r)=2^{n-r}-2\binom{n}{r}=2^{n-r}-\frac{2n(n-1)\cdots(n-r+1)}{r!},

where, in spite of the notation,
n
could be a real number, although
r
is always a positive integer.
Suppose
n>2r
. Then its forward difference in the variable
n
satisfies

\displaystyle f(n+1,r)-f(n,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[2^{n+1-r}-2^{n-r}]-2\left[\binom{n+1}{r}-\binom{n}{r}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n-r}-2\binom{n}{r-1}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 2^{n-r}-2\binom{n}{r}=f(n,r).

This yields the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
n>2r
, then

f(n+1,r)>2f(n,r).

In particular, if
f(n,r)\geq 0,
then
f(n+1,r)>0
.

By the lemma, to prove Proposition
2.2
, it suffices to find a number
n_{r}
such that
n_{r}\leq\lceil r(\log r+\log\log r+\log\log\log r)\rceil
and
2^{n_{r}-r}\geq 2\binom{n_{r}}{r}

Returning to the proof of Theorem
2.1
, observe that if a matroid
M
has no isthmuses, then
T(M;x,y)=y^{n-r}+\cdots,
where the terms of lower degree in
y
have non-negative coefficients. In addition,
T(M;1,1)
is the number of bases and is bounded above by
\binom{n}{r}
. Hence, by Proposition
2.2
, under the hypothesis of the theorem,

T(M;0,2)\geq 2^{n-r}\geq 2\binom{n}{r}\geq 2T(M;1,1).

We remark that Theorem
2.1
says that for a fixed rank
r
, there are only finitely many matroids to check to show that Conjecture
1.1
holds at that rank.

3.
Cocircuits

A
cocircuit
is the complement of a copoint or flat of rank
r-1
. A matroid is isthmus-free if and only if it has no cocircuits of size
1
; thus, one can regard the condition that there are no isthmuses as a condition on the minimum size of a cocircuit. The following theorem says that Conjecture
1.1
holds if one assumes that all cocircuits are sufficiently large.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that every cocircuit in a rank-
r
size-
n
matroid
M
has at least
r+1
elements. Then
M
satisfies inequality (1.1).

We begin with an easy binomial identity.

Lemma 3.2
.

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\binom{r-j-1}{j}y^{r-1-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{2r-2}{r-1}+\binom{2r-3}{r-2}y+\cdots+\binom{r}{1}y^{r-2}+y^{r-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{2r-2}.

For example,

\binom{6}{3}2^{0}+\binom{5}{2}2^{1}+\binom{4}{1}2^{2}+\binom{3}{0}2^{3}=20+10\cdot 2+4\cdot 2^{2}+2^{3}=64=2^{6}.

Now observe that if every cocircuit in the matroid
M
has size at least
m
, then

T(M;x,y)=y^{n-r}+\binom{r}{1}y^{n-r-1}+\cdots+\binom{r+m-3}{m-2}y^{n-r-m+2}+p(x,y),

where
p(x,y)
is a polynomial with non-negative coefficients of degree
n-r-m+1
in
y
. In particular, if
m=r+1,
then

T(M;0,2)\geq 2^{n-2r+1}\left(\binom{2r-2}{r-1}+\cdots+\binom{r}{1}2^{r-2}+2^{r-1}\right)=2^{n-1}.

The hypothesis on cocircuits implies that
n\geq 2r
and under this condition,
2^{n-1}\geq 2\binom{n}{r}
. We conclude that inequality (1.1) holds.

Theorem
3.1
is not the sharpest possible. It is possible to replace the upper bound of
r+1
by a bound of the form
r-c\sqrt{r}
(with
c
a constant) for sufficiently large
r
using the normal approximation to the binomial distribution. It is also possible to combine the arguments to obtain sufficient conditions involving density and size of cocircuits for inequality (1.1) to hold, but neither refinement would prove the full conjecture.
We end with one further result of this type.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
M
be a rank-
r
size-
n
isthmus-free matroid with at least
r-1
loops. Then inequality (1.1) holds for
M
.

Proof.

Loops are never in a basis. Hence,

T(M;0,2)\geq 2^{n-r}\geq 2\binom{n-r+1}{r}\geq 2T(M;1,1)

and inequality (1.1) follows.
∎

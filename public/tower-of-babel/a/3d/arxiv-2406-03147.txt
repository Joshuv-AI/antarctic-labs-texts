A dynamical implementation of canonical second quantization on a quantum computer
Juan José Gálvez-Viruet and Felipe J. Llanes-Estrada Depto. de Física Teórica & IPARCOS, Fac. CC. Físicas, Plaza de las Ciencias 1 28040 Universidad Complutense de Madrid, Spain

Abstract
We develop theoretical methods for the implementation of creation and destruction operators in separate registers of a quantum computer, allowing for a transparent and dynamical creation and destruction of particle modes in second quantization in problems with variable particle number. We establish theorems for the commutation (anticommutation) relations on a finite memory bank and provide the needed symmetrizing and antisymmetrizing operators. Finally, we provide formulae in terms of these operators for unitary evolution under conventional two- and four-body Hamiltonian terms, as well as terms varying the particle number. In this formalism, the number of qubits needed to codify n n particles with N p N_{p} modes each is of order n ​ log 2 ​ N p n\log_{2}N_{p} . Such scaling is more efficient than the Jordan-Wigner transformation which requires O ⁡ ( N p ) O(N_{p}) qubits, whenever there are a modest number of particles with a large number of states available to each (and less advantageous for a large number of particles with few states available to each). And although less efficient, it is also less cumbersome than compact encoding.

II
Implementation of single-particle operators

The abstraction that we pursue is to assign a “register” to each particle active in a process.
Such registers are sets of qubits encoding the quantum numbers
q
of the represented particle, with one additional qubit that we dub the “presence/absence” qubit denoted as
P/A
, which is a handle allowing to decide whether one particle is represented by that register, or whether it should be considered empty and available.
If an additional particle is needed, an empty register has to be activated and its presence qubit set to 1.

For a particle with momentum, spin, and internal quantum numbers such as a color, the vacuum state (corresponding to the absence of the particle) is conveniently taken as

\left|\Omega\right\rangle\equiv\left|0\right\rangle_{\text{P/A}}\otimes\left|0\right\rangle_{\text{spin}}\otimes\left|00\right\rangle_{\text{color}}\otimes\left|0...0\right\rangle_{\text{momentum}}

(1)

the number of possible values of a quantum number dictates how many qubits are necessary for its representation: codification of spin requires one qubit, codification of color
[
39
]
two qubits and codification of momenta, which is a continuous variable, requires discretization on a variable number of qubits to reach the wished precision. From here on we consider
N_{p}
different values of momenta codified in
n_{p}=\lceil\log_{2}N_{p}\rceil
qubits (or in three dimensions, remark which we will usually omit in the rest of the article,
(\lceil\log_{2}N_{p}\rceil)^{3}
qubits).
In the vacuum state, each register is in a reference state, here taken to be the null ket, that does not represent any valid quantum number.

Our first goal is to represent
the action of the basic creation operator in Fock space, a staple of many-body theory,
a_{s,c,p}^{\dagger}
.

To implement it, we write it in terms of
set
operators
\mathfrak{s}
acting on each of the product Hilbert spaces describing each degree of freedom, and control operators
\mathfrak{C}_{{i}{j}}

a_{s,c,p}^{\dagger}\equiv{\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{s}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{c}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{p},

(2)

so that

\displaystyle a_{s,c,p}^{\dagger}\left|\Omega\right\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv{\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\left|0\right\rangle_{\text{P/A}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{s}\left|0\right\rangle\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{c}\left|00\right\rangle\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{p}\left|0...0\right\rangle

(3)

\displaystyle=\left|1\right\rangle_{\text{P/A}}\otimes\left|s\right\rangle\otimes\left|c\right\rangle\otimes\left|p\right\rangle\equiv\left|1scp\right\rangle\ .

(4)

The first qubit, that indicated the presence or absence of a particle in the register, has now being set to
|1\rangle_{P/A}
. The control operator
\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}
also prevents the attempt of creating a quantum if the register is already encoding another one (“occupied register”). Control operators acting over the presence/absence qubits fulfill

{\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{i}{j}}$}}\left|k\right\rangle=\delta_{jk}\left|i\right\rangle,\,\,\,{\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{i}{k}}$}}{\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{l}{m}}$}}=\delta_{kl}{\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{i}{m}}$}}.

(5)

The Hermitian conjugate of the abstract
a^{\dagger}
operators in many-body theory are the annihilation operators; they can be implemented as in

a_{s,c,q}=(a_{s,c,q}^{\dagger})^{\dagger}={\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{s}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{c}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q},

(6)

where, in intuitive notation,
\mathfrak{s}
are
scrap
operators which can clear the quantum numbers:
\mathfrak{s}_{s}
returns states of spin
\left|s\right\rangle
(spin up or down) to the reference spin state
\left|s_{0}\right\rangle
(spin down, for example), with analogous behaviour for
\mathfrak{s}_{c}
and
\mathfrak{s}_{q}
.
(Because the quantum computer will encode the unitary operators arising from exponentiation of combinations of
a^{\dagger}
and
a
, the fact that,
e.g.

{\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\left|1\right\rangle=0
yields an unrepresentable null vector will pose no problem, as it exponentiates to the identity.)

In summary, the operations that this implementation of the Fock algebra can be used for are:

•

To occupy an unassigned register, encoding a particle in it,

a_{s,c,p}^{\dagger}\left|\Omega\right\rangle\ =\ {\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\left|0\right\rangle_{\text{P/A}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{s}\left|0\right\rangle\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{c}\left|00\right\rangle\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{p}\left|0...0\right\rangle\ =\ \left|1scp\right\rangle\ .

(7)

•

To vacate an occupied register if its quantum numbers match those of the destruction operator, resetting them to vacuum default,

a_{s^{\prime},c^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\left|1scp\right\rangle\ =\ \left|0\right\rangle\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{s^{\prime}}\left|s\right\rangle\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{c^{\prime}}\left|c\right\rangle\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q^{\prime}}\left|q\right\rangle=\delta_{s^{\prime}s}\delta_{c^{\prime}c}\delta_{q^{\prime}q}\left|\Omega\right\rangle

(8)

•

To replace the quantum numbers of an existing particle occupying a register, employing combinations that conserve particle number (or, as a specific case, to pass on as with the identity) such as

\displaystyle(a^{\dagger}_{s_{2},c_{2},q_{2}}a_{s^{\prime}_{2},c^{\prime}_{2},q^{\prime}_{2}})\cdot(a^{\dagger}_{s_{1},c_{1},q_{1}}a_{s^{\prime}_{1},c^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{1}})\left|1s_{0}c_{0}q_{0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta_{s_{0},s^{\prime}_{1}}\delta_{c_{0},c^{\prime}_{1}}\delta_{q_{0},q^{\prime}_{1}}a^{\dagger}_{s_{2},c_{2},q_{2}}a_{s^{\prime}_{2},c^{\prime}_{2},q^{\prime}_{2}}\left|1s_{1}c_{1}q_{1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\delta_{s_{0},s^{\prime}_{1}}\delta_{c_{0},c^{\prime}_{1}}\delta_{q_{0},q^{\prime}_{1}}\delta_{s_{1},s^{\prime}_{2}}\delta_{c_{1},c^{\prime}_{2}}\delta_{q_{1},q^{\prime}_{2}}\left|1s_{2}c_{2}q_{2}\right\rangle

(9)

or, acting on an empty register,

\displaystyle(a_{s^{\prime}_{2},c^{\prime}_{2},q^{\prime}_{2}}a^{\dagger}_{s_{2},c_{2},q_{2}})\cdot(a_{s^{\prime}_{1},c^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{1}}a^{\dagger}_{s_{1},c_{1},q_{1}})\left|\Omega\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta_{s_{1},s^{\prime}_{1}}\delta_{c_{1},c^{\prime}_{1}}\delta_{q_{1},q^{\prime}_{1}}a_{s^{\prime}_{2},c^{\prime}_{2},q^{\prime}_{2}}a^{\dagger}_{s_{2},c_{2},q_{2}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta_{s_{2},s^{\prime}_{2}}\delta_{c_{2},c^{\prime}_{2}}\delta_{q_{2},q^{\prime}_{2}}\delta_{s_{1},s^{\prime}_{1}}\delta_{c_{1},c^{\prime}_{1}}\delta_{q_{1},q^{\prime}_{1}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle\ .

(10)

III
Implementation of two-particle boson operators

III.1
Basic necessary properties

In this section we address the setting and handling of two bosons in the memory.
The new complication, respect to dealing with only one particle, is the
need to represent the abstract symmetry under boson exchange independently of the underlying hardware.

Let us start by the vacuum states, omitting quantum numbers other than momentum to lighten the expressions.

\left|\Omega\right\rangle=\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{2}\otimes\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{1}\equiv\left|0\right\rangle_{\text{P/A}}\left|0...0\right\rangle_{\text{momentum}}\otimes\left|0\right\rangle_{\text{P/A}}\left|0...0\right\rangle_{\text{momentum}},

(11)

We have highlighted the two product states by the presence/absence and a “momentum” subscripts, that we now drop, with the ordering of the two states specifying which is which.

The action of any creation operators
a^{(2)\dagger}_{i}
over this pair of registers is defined to emulate their corresponding action on Fock space. (The superindex
(2)
indicates that it acts over a two-register memory, which will be useful to keep track of to distinguish the various operators discussed).

If applied over the vacuum, it creates a first particle, which we assign to the rightmost register labelled (1),

a_{q_{1}}^{(2)\dagger}\left|\Omega\right\rangle=\left|q_{1}\right\rangle\equiv\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{2}\otimes\left(\left|1\right\rangle\left|q_{1}\right\rangle\right)_{1}=\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{2}\otimes\left|1q_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\ .

(12)

If that register is already occupied by one boson, the creation operator needs to assign the second (note Bose’s symmetry):

a_{q_{1}}^{(2)\dagger}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{2}\otimes\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\left|1q_{1}\right\rangle_{2}\otimes\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}+\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{2}\otimes\left|1q_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)\ .

(13)

Finally, if both registers already contain a boson, no further particle can be encoded in this computer with only two registers.

The creation operator can then do nothing and we naturally choose to set its action to null,

a_{q_{1}}^{(2)\dagger}\left|1p_{2}\right\rangle_{2}\otimes\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}=0.

(14)

This is a condition at the boundary of the computer memory: its size must truncate the Fock space, and as usual, introduce a discretization error in the second quantized problem being treated. Adding further memory will alleviate it, so we will return to this below in section
IV.1
.

In the case in which
only one object sits in the two–register memory, we need to resolve the ambiguity of choosing one of them to carry the active quantum numbers.

We select to stack particles from right to left, entailing
the exclusion of states such as
\left|1p\right\rangle_{2}\otimes\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{1}
, i.e., with the first register empty and the second occupied.
Note that no symmetry is here necessary, since the registers are just sections of memory and do not represent physical particles: here there is just one, and nothing to symmetrize.

The action of the corresponding annihilation operators is also defined to mimic their behaviour in Fock space,

\begin{array}[]{ccc}a^{(2)}_{q_{1}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle=0;&&a_{q_{1}}^{(2)}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{2}\otimes\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\ =\ \delta_{q_{1}p_{1}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{2}\otimes\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{1}\ =\ \delta_{q_{1}p_{1}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle,\end{array}

(15)

and

a^{(2)}_{q_{1}}\left(\left|1p_{2}\right\rangle_{2}\otimes\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{S}=\delta_{q_{1}p_{2}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{2}\otimes\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}+\delta_{q_{1}p_{1}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{2}\otimes\left|1p_{2}\right\rangle_{1},

(16)

where
\left(\left|1p_{2}\right\rangle_{2}\otimes\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{S}
is a two-particle symmetric state with momenta
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
.

With these requirements we can now try to write down the higher level creation/annihilation operators in terms of the
lower level set/scrap operators from section
II
.

III.2
Implementation

We find convenient to split the Fock operator acting on the two-register memory into two auxiliary suboperators, one acting on each of the two registers when that register is the last occupied one (which regrettably requires an additional subindex),

\displaystyle a_{q_{1},1}^{(2)\dagger}

\displaystyle=\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}^{\dagger}\right)_{1},

\displaystyle a_{q_{1},2}^{(2)\dagger}

\displaystyle=\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}^{\dagger}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{1}.

(17)

(Parentheses isolate operators acting on the first or second register; the control terms
\mathfrak{C}_{{i}{j}}
act on presence qubits and the identity
\mathfrak{i}
and set
\mathfrak{s}
operators act on momentum qubits.)
The construction of these auxiliary operators is straightforward: the first one sets the quantum numbers of the first, empty, register to the particle being added and flips its presence qubit from absence to presence, while leaving the second register untouched.

The second operator leaves the first register, assumed occupied, untouched, and adds the particle to the second. Before summing it with the first to form the total creation operator, we need to note that its action produces a two-particle state that does need to be symmetric under exchange. Hence, we multiply it by a symmetrizer

a_{q_{1}}^{(2)\dagger}=a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{1},1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}+\mathcal{P}_{21}\right)a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{1},2}\ .

(18)

(Note that symmetrization cannot be trivially implemented in runtime after the initial state preparation, because it projects out the antisymmetric parts of the Hilbert space, which is not a unitary operation germane to a quantum computer. We can circumvent the problem as shown in Eq. (
74
) below.)

The
\mathcal{P}_{21}
permutation operation swaps the quantum numbers encoded in each of the two registers:

\mathcal{P}_{21}\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle\left|1p_{2}\right\rangle\equiv\left|1p_{2}\right\rangle\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle\ ,

(19)

whereas
\mathbb{I}
is the “register-level identity” which leaves the quantum numbers in the corresponding register unchanged.

If we substitute the auxiliary operators of Eq. (
17
) into Eq. (
18
) we can give a direct expression for the creation operator over the two-register memory

\displaystyle a_{q_{1}}^{(2)\dagger}

\displaystyle=\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}^{\dagger}\right)_{1}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}+\mathcal{P}_{21}\right)\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}^{\dagger}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{1}.

(20)

This equation fulfills the quantum field theory requirements of Eq. (
12
)
and (
13
) in the two-body sector.

The annihilation operators are then found by Hermitian conjugation

\displaystyle a_{q_{1}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\right)_{1}

\displaystyle+\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}+\mathcal{P}_{21}\right).

(21)

The first piece acts when only the first register is occupied, annihilating that one particle; but if both are filled, its action is null, and it is the second piece that is active and erases the particle in the second register.
We now turn to showing that this construction satisfies the canonical commutation relations in the Fock space restricted to two memory registers.

III.3
Canonical commutation relations among bosons

boson operators must fulfill, at the operator level,

\left[a_{q_{1}}a^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\right]=a_{q_{1}}a^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}-a^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}a_{q_{1}}=\delta_{q_{1}q_{2}}\ .

(22)

In this subsection we show that, in a two–register memory, this is satisfied for occupation numbers zero and one, but that the two-particle case takes a boundary term (which can only be avoided by adding further registers).

The first of the two terms of the commutator
\left[a_{q_{1}},a^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\right]
is, from Eq. (
20
) and Eq. (
21
),

\displaystyle a_{q_{1}}^{(2)}a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left[\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}^{\phantom{\dagger}\!}\right)_{1}\right]\left[\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{2}}^{\dagger}\right)_{1}\right]

\displaystyle+\left[\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}^{\phantom{\dagger}\!}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{1}\right]\left({\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}+\mathcal{P}_{21}\right)\left[\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{2}}^{\dagger}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{1}\right]

(23)

(the crossed terms cancel because of Eq. (
5
)).
Associating together the operators over each subspace allows this to be written as

a_{q_{1}}^{(2)}a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{2}}=\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\right)_{1}+\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\right)\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)+\mathcal{P}_{21}\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\right)\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\right)

(24)

The reversed-order product is

\displaystyle a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{2}}a_{q_{1}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\left[\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{2}}^{\dagger}\right)_{1}\right]\left[\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}^{\phantom{\dagger}\!}\right)_{1}\right]

(25)

\displaystyle+\left({\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}+\mathcal{P}_{21}\right)\left[\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{2}}^{\dagger}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{1}\right]\left[\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}^{\phantom{\dagger}\!}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{1}\right],

(26)

or, upon expanding the product and employing again the simplification of the factor
\mathcal{P}_{12}
because of the preprogrammed symmetrization of two-particle states,

\displaystyle a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{2}}a^{(2)}_{q_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\right)_{1}+\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{1}+\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\right)_{1},

(27)

Note the action of the diagonal operator with
q_{1}=q_{2}
as a number operator:
if only the rightmost register is occupied, it returns the same state multiplied by 1 due to the leftmost term. If both are occupied with bosons of equal
q_{1}
, it will return twice the state due to the middle and right pieces.
With these expressions in Eq. (
27
) we can now act the commutator over each of the three sectors of the Fock space abstracted from two particle registers.

Zero-particle state.

The zero-particle state is the vacuum, and the action thereon of the commutator reads

\displaystyle\left[a^{(2)}_{q_{1}},a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{2}}\right]\left|\Omega\right\rangle

\displaystyle=a^{(2)}_{q_{1}}a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{2}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{2}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{1}-a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{2}}a^{(2)}_{q_{1}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{2}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{1}

\displaystyle=\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{2}\otimes\left(\left|0\right\rangle\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\left|0...0\right\rangle\right)_{1}

\displaystyle=\delta_{q_{1}q_{2}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle,

(28)

where in the last line we used Eqs. (
4
) and (
6
).

One-particle states.

In this case we also have a faithful representation of what is expected in second quantization,

\displaystyle\left[a^{(2)}_{q_{1}},a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{2}}\right]\left|p\right\rangle

\displaystyle=a^{(2)}_{q_{1}}a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{2}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{2}\left|1p\right\rangle_{1}-a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{2}}a^{(2)}_{q_{1}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{2}\left|1p\right\rangle_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\left|0\right\rangle\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\left|0...0\right\rangle\right)_{2}\otimes\left|1p\right\rangle_{1}+\left(\left|0\right\rangle\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\left|p\right\rangle\right)_{2}\otimes\left(\left|1\right\rangle\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\left|0...0\right\rangle\right)_{1}-\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{2}\left(\left|1\right\rangle\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\left|p\right\rangle\right)_{1}

\displaystyle=\delta_{q_{1}q_{2}}\left|p\right\rangle+\delta_{q_{1}p}\left|q_{2}\right\rangle-\delta_{q_{1}p}\left|q_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta_{q_{1}q_{2}}\left|p\right\rangle\ ;

(29)

so far, these operators act as the standard commutation relations do.

Two-particle states.

Finally we show the action of the commutator on the doubly occupied and symmetrized (as denoted by the subindex
S
) two-particle register,

\displaystyle\left[a^{(2)}_{q_{1}},a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{2}}\right]\left(\left|1q\right\rangle\left|1p\right\rangle\right)_{S}

\displaystyle=\left(a^{(2)}_{q_{1}}a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{2}}-a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{2}}a^{(2)}_{q_{1}}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\left|1q\right\rangle_{2}\left|1p\right\rangle_{1}+\left|1p\right\rangle_{2}\left|1q\right\rangle_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=-a^{(2)\dagger}_{q_{2}}a^{(2)}_{q_{1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\left|1q\right\rangle_{2}\left|1p\right\rangle_{1}+\left|1p\right\rangle_{2}\left|1q\right\rangle_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\left(\left|1\right\rangle\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\left|q\right\rangle\right)_{2}\left|1p\right\rangle_{1}+\left(\left|1\right\rangle\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\left|p\right\rangle\right)_{2}\left|1q\right\rangle_{1}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left|1q\right\rangle_{2}\left(\left|1\right\rangle\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\left|p\right\rangle\right)_{1}+\left|1p\right\rangle_{2}\left(\left|1\right\rangle\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{2}}\mathfrak{s}_{q_{1}}\left|q\right\rangle\right)_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\delta_{q_{1}q}\left|q_{2}p\right\rangle+\delta_{q_{1}p}\left|q_{2}q\right\rangle+\delta_{q_{1}p}\left|qq_{2}\right\rangle+\delta_{q_{1}q}\left|pq_{2}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=-\delta_{q_{1}q}\left|q_{2}p\right\rangle_{S}-\delta_{q_{1}p}\left|q_{2}q\right\rangle_{S}.

(30)

As expected due to the boundary term in Eq. (
12
), in which the creation operator acting over two occupied registers is by necessity defined to be 0,
a boundary mismatch appears in this
calculation, that in textbook field-theory should yield
\delta_{q_{1}q_{2}}\left(\left|1q\right\rangle\left|1p\right\rangle\right)_{S}

instead of the outcome of Eq. (
30
).

If a third, unoccupied register is added to the memory, the commutator acting over two-particles states can then be faithfully reproduced. This leads us to generalizing to
n
particles in the next section
IV.1
.

IV.1
Commuting operators

We are now ready to explain the general case for a large memory bank containing an unspecified, variable number of particles. In this section we present the generalization of the preceding codifications in section

n
-register case.

Consider scalar particles with only their momentum as quantum number, with such momenta codified in
n_{P}

The null ket is taken as a reference and does not represent any valid momentum

\left|0...0\right\rangle\equiv\underbrace{\left|0\right\rangle_{P}...\left|0\right\rangle_{P}}_{n_{p}}.

(31)

Consider also presence/absence qubits initially in the state
\left|0\right\rangle_{P/A}
:
the
n-register vacuum
is then written as

\left|\Omega\right\rangle\equiv\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{n}\otimes...\otimes\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{1}=\underbrace{\left(\left|0\right\rangle_{P/A}\left|0...0\right\rangle\right)_{n}\otimes...\otimes\left(\left|0\right\rangle_{P/A}\left|0...0\right\rangle\right)_{1}}_{n},

(32)

Generally, operators acting over
n
-registers consist of projection and control operators
{\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{i}{j}}$}}=\left|i\right\rangle\left\langle j\right|
defined in Eq.(

) over the presence/absence qubits and set/scrap operators
\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}
,
\mathfrak{s}
over qubits representing quantum numbers as
defined in Eq. (

n
-register quantum computer is

a^{(n)\dagger}_{p,1}\equiv\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{n}...\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{2}\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{p}\right)_{1},

(33)

indeed

a^{(n)\dagger}_{p,1}\left|\Omega\right\rangle=\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\left|0\right\rangle_{P/A}{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\left|0...0\right\rangle\right)_{n}...\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\left|0\right\rangle_{P/A}\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{p}\left|0...0\right\rangle\right)_{1}=\left(\left|0\right\rangle_{P/A}\left|0...0\right\rangle\right)_{n}...\left(\left|1\right\rangle_{P/A}\left|p\right\rangle\right)_{1}\ .

(34)

We next define the generalization of creation and annihilation operators for arbitrary number of particles. Note that in Eq. (
12
) the two-register operator

\mathcal{S}_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}+\mathcal{P}_{21}\right)

(35)

is a two-particle
symmetrizer
. Thus it is tempting (but inefficient) to generalize the expression in Eq.(
14
) adding operators that create particles in each of the registers and higher-order symmetrizers, for example, in the case of three registers, changing
\mathcal{S}_{2}\rightarrow{\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}\otimes\mathcal{S}_{2}
and introducing a three-particles symmetrizer
\mathcal{S}_{3}
:

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}_{q}|_{3}\equiv

\displaystyle\,{\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}^{\otimes 3}\cdot\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{3}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\right)_{1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,{\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}\otimes S_{2}\cdot\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{3}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,S_{3}\cdot\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\right)_{3}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{2}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{1},

(36)

this generalization would be correct if applied to unsymmetrized two-particle states, but in the case of symmetric states it produces extra contributions:

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}_{q}\left|00\right\rangle_{3}\left(\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{2}\left|1p_{2}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{S}}=

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{S}_{3}\left|1q\right\rangle_{3}\left(\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{2}\left|1p_{2}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left({\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}^{\otimes 3}+\mathcal{P}_{21}+\mathcal{P}_{31}+\mathcal{P}_{32}+\mathcal{P}_{231}+\mathcal{P}_{312}\right)\left|1q\right\rangle\left(\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle\left|1p_{2}\right\rangle\right)_{\mathcal{S}}

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,2\left(\left|1q\right\rangle\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle\left|1p_{2}\right\rangle\right)_{\mathcal{S}},

(38)

since we are symmetrizing again the already symmetrized part. Additionally, even if designing adequate normalizations to overcome this, numerous redundant operations would be wasted resymmetrizing already symmetric parts of the wavefunction.

Thus, in the case of three or more registers, symmetrization or antisymmetrization of states will be implemented in a step-wise fashion, assuming that earlier creation operations already symmetrized a state with
n-1
particles, and only the newly added one requires symmetrization with the others. Imposing symmetric states, the three-particle symmetrizer should be changed to a “step-symmetrizer”

\mathcal{S}_{3}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{3\leftarrow 2}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left({\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}^{\otimes 3}+\mathcal{P}_{32}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}_{1}+\mathcal{P}_{31}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}_{2}\right)

(39)

for the general case, and omitting the identities for conciseness, we have
\mathcal{S}_{n\leftarrow n-1}
, which promotes an
(n-1)
-particle symmetric state to an
n
-particle symmetric one:

\mathcal{S}_{n\leftarrow n-1}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left({\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}^{\otimes n}+\mathcal{P}_{n(n-1))}+...+\mathcal{P}_{n2}+\mathcal{P}_{n1}\right).

(40)

Note that with this choice, the symmetrizer is not idempotent, but instead
S^{2}_{n\leftarrow n-1}=\sqrt{n}\ S_{n\leftarrow n-1}
.

Implementation of creation operators.

The auxiliary operator that attempts to create a particle on the
j

n
-register memory segment now reads

\displaystyle a^{(n)\dagger}_{q,j}

\displaystyle={\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}^{\otimes n-j}\otimes\mathcal{S}_{j\leftarrow(j-1)}\cdot\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{n}\otimes...\otimes\underbrace{\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\right)_{j}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{j-1}\otimes...\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{j\leftarrow(j-1)}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\underbrace{\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\right)_{j}}\otimes\,{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}},

where the identity multiplying the
j
-register step symmetrizer is omitted to alleviate notation and
\mathbb{P}^{({n})}_{{j}}
is the projector onto states with exactly
j
particles on an
n
-register quantum computer:

{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n})}_{{j}}$}}={\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j})}_{{j}}$}}=\left[\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{n}\otimes...\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{j+1}\right]\otimes\left[\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{j}\otimes...\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{1}\right]\ ,

(42)

which fullfil
{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n})}_{{j}}$}}{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n})}_{{i}}$}}=\delta_{ij}
(because of the unequal number of particle presence/absence control operator
\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}
and
\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}
for different values of
i
and
j
).
The action of this operator over a register that already contains
i-1
particles is automatically restricted to filling
j=i
(last particle on the stack) by its very definition, so that

\displaystyle a^{(n)\dagger}_{q,j}\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i+1}\otimes\left(\left|1p_{i-1}\right\rangle_{i-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle=a^{(n)\dagger}_{q,j}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-i+1})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({i-1})}_{{i-1}}$}}\cdot\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i+1}\otimes\left(\left|1p_{i-1}\right\rangle_{i-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{j\leftarrow(j-1)}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\underbrace{\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\right)_{j}}\otimes\,{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-i+1})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({i-1})}_{{i-1}}$}}\cdot\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i+1}\otimes\left(\left|1p_{i-1}\right\rangle_{i-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i,j}\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i+1}\otimes\left[\mathcal{S}_{i\leftarrow i-1}\left|1q\right\rangle\left(\left|1p_{i-1}\right\rangle_{i-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{S}}\right]

\displaystyle=\delta_{i,j}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{n}...\left(\left|1q\right\rangle_{i}\left|1p_{i-1}\right\rangle_{i-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{S}},

(43)

where in the third line we used the orthogonality of projectors to produce a

.

The total creation operator that implements the field-theory requirements is the addition of auxiliary creation operators over each register:

a^{(n)\dagger}_{q}=\sum^{n}_{i=1}a^{(n)\dagger}_{q,i}=\sum^{n}_{i=1}\mathcal{S}_{i\leftarrow(i-1)}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-i})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\right)_{i}\otimes\,{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({i-1})}_{{i-1}}$}}

(44)

note that
S_{2\leftarrow 1}=S_{2}
, so for the case
n=2
we recover the two-particle case studied earlier in section
III
. The action of the full
a_{q}^{(n)\dagger}
over an
i-particle
state
\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{n}...\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{S}}
is then

\displaystyle a^{(n)\dagger}_{q}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{n}...\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{S}}=\sum^{n}_{j=1}a^{\dagger(n)}_{q,j}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{n}...\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{j=1}\delta_{j,i+1}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{n}...\left(\left|q\right\rangle_{i+1}\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle=\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{n}...\left(\left|q\right\rangle_{i+1}\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{S}}

(45)

At last, its action over an
n

(46)

\displaystyle\hat{N}^{(n)}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{n}...\left(\left|1p_{j^{\prime}}\right\rangle_{j^{\prime}}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j^{\prime}}\sqrt{j^{\prime}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{n}...\mathcal{S}_{j^{\prime}\leftarrow j^{\prime}-1}\left(\left|1p_{j^{\prime}}\right\rangle_{j^{\prime}}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{S}

\displaystyle=j^{\prime}\ \left|\Omega\right\rangle_{n}...\left(\left|1p_{j^{\prime}}\right\rangle_{j^{\prime}}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{S}.

(54)

Having established the validity of the number operator, we can next check that
a^{(n)\dagger}
and
a^{(n)}
for
n
particles form a canonically conjugate pair.

Commutation relations.

We now adopt Eq.(
44
) and Eq.(
47
) as the definition of the creation and annihilation operators for
n
registers.

These operators satisfy the following commutation relations in the Hilbert subspace of symmetric states.

\left[a^{(n)}_{q_{1}},a^{(n)\dagger}_{q_{2}}\right]=\delta_{q_{1},q_{2}}\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{n}\otimes\sum^{n-1}_{j=0}{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-1})}_{{j}}$}}\ -\ \,S_{n\leftarrow n-1}\cdot\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{1}}\mathfrak{s}_{q_{2}}\right)_{n}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-1})}_{{n-1}}$}}\cdot S_{n\leftarrow n-1}\ ,

(55)

the operator
\sum^{n-1}_{j=0}{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-1})}_{{j}}$}}
projects over properly filled memories, in which registers are filled from right to left. Thus
\mathbb{I}^{(n-1)}-\sum^{n-1}_{j=0}{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-1})}_{{j}}$}}
, with
\mathbb{I}^{(n-1)}
the identity over
(n-1)
registers, is zero over properly filled memories, and we can write

\left[a^{(n)}_{q_{1}},a^{(n)\dagger}_{q_{2}}\right]_{\text{\rm properly\ filled}}=\delta_{q_{1},q_{2}}\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{n}\otimes\mathbb{I}^{(n-1)}\ -\ \,S_{n\leftarrow n-1}\cdot\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{1}}\mathfrak{s}_{q_{2}}\right)_{n}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-1})}_{{n-1}}$}}\cdot S_{n\leftarrow n-1}\ ,

(56)

The first term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
55
) is, in this notation, what one would expect of canonical commutation relations, the identity in a Fock space of up to
n-1
particles (the
\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}
control guarantees that the
n
th register is empty).

The second deviates from second quantization and emerges due to the quantum computer’s finite memory: it vanishes if less than
n
particles occupy the memory. As a corollary thereof, the canonical commutation relations are exactly satisfied for symmetric states of up to
n-1
particles (in an
n
-register quantum computer) when only the first term contributes.

The demonstration of Eq. (
55
), that requires a few bookkeeping steps, is presented in appendix
B
.

IV.2
Anticommuting operators

With the experience of the boson operators in the proceeding section
IV.1
, it is now easy to implement anticommuting ones and direct (if tedious) to derive their properties. The most notable difference is that the recursive step-symmetrizers of Eq. (
40
) should be changed to step-antisymetrizers, which (unsurprisingly) read

{\text{$\mathcal{A}_{{n}\leftarrow{n}-1}$}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left({\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}^{\otimes n}-\mathcal{P}_{n(n-1)}-\mathcal{P}_{n(n-2)}-...-\mathcal{P}_{n2}-\mathcal{P}_{n1}\right)

(57)

that antisymmetrize any dynamically added particle with all the previously filled ones.

Totally antisymmetric states can be written as

\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}=\sum_{\sigma}(-1)^{\eta_{\sigma}}\sigma\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right),

(58)

where
\sigma
runs over the permutations of
i
-elements and
\eta_{\sigma}
is the parity of the permutation
\sigma
:
(-1)^{\eta_{\sigma}}=1
if the permutations is even, and
(-1)
if odd. The action of the step-antisymmetrizer over this state is

\mathcal{A}_{{i}\leftarrow{i}-1}

\displaystyle\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}\left({\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}^{\otimes n}-\mathcal{P}_{i(i-1)}-...-\mathcal{P}_{i1}\right)\sum_{\sigma}(-1)^{\eta_{\sigma}}\sigma\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}\left[\sum_{\sigma}(-1)^{\eta_{\sigma}}\sigma-\sum_{\sigma}(-1)^{\eta_{\sigma}}\mathcal{P}_{i(i-1)}\sigma-...-\sum_{\sigma}(-1)^{\eta_{\sigma}}\mathcal{P}_{i1}\sigma\right]\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\ .

(59)

By the rearrangement lemma, no two
\mathcal{P}_{ij}\sigma
permutations are equal, so the sums
\sum_{\sigma}\mathcal{P}_{ij}\sigma
cover all possible permutations. Also, since
\mathcal{P}_{ij}
is a simple permutation, the parity of
\mathcal{P}_{ij}\sigma
is the negative of the parity of
\sigma
. Using the same index,
\sigma
, for all the new permutation we have that all the terms in Eq. (
59
) are identical, so

\displaystyle{\text{$\mathcal{A}_{{i}\leftarrow{i}-1}$}}\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}\left[\sum_{\sigma}(-1)^{\eta_{\sigma}}\sigma+\sum_{\sigma}(-1)^{\eta_{\sigma}}\sigma+...+\sum_{\sigma}(-1)^{\eta_{\sigma}}\sigma\right]\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{i}\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{A}.

(60)

Implementation of the Creation Operators.

We start again by an auxiliary operator that creates a particle precisely at the
j
th register in an
n
-register quantum computer, which now reads

\displaystyle b^{(n)\dagger}_{q,j}

\displaystyle={\text{$\mathbb{I}$}}^{\otimes n-j}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow j-1}\cdot\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{n}\otimes...\otimes\underbrace{\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{{p}}$}}\right)_{j}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{j-1}\otimes...\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{1}

\displaystyle={\text{$\mathcal{A}_{{j}\leftarrow{j}-1}$}}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\underbrace{\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\right)_{j}}\otimes\,{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}\ .

(61)

Its action over a register that contains
i
-particles is similar to the commuting case, but symmetric states should be changed to antysimmetric ones:

\displaystyle b^{(n)\dagger}_{q,j}\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i+1}\left(\left|1p_{i-1}\right\rangle_{i-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}=\delta_{i,j}\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i+1}\otimes\left[\mathcal{A}_{i\leftarrow i-1}\left|1q\right\rangle\left(\left|1p_{i-1}\right\rangle_{i-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}\right]

\displaystyle=\delta_{i,j}\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i}\left(\left|1q\right\rangle_{i}\left|1p_{i-1}\right\rangle_{i-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}},

(62)

where the Kronecker delta appears due to the same reasons it was generated among commuting operators. The field-theoretic creation operator is implemented by the addition of the creation operators over each register

b^{(n)\dagger}_{q}=\sum^{n}_{j=1}b^{(n)\dagger}_{q,j}=\sum^{n}_{j=1}{\text{$\mathcal{A}_{{j}\leftarrow{j}-1}$}}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\underbrace{\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\right)_{j}}\otimes\,{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}

(63)

its action over an
i
-particle antisymmetric state
\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{n}...\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}
is

\displaystyle b^{(n)\dagger}_{q}

\displaystyle\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i+1}\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}=\sum^{n}_{j=1}b^{\dagger(n)}_{q,j}\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i+1}\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{j=1}\delta_{j,i+1}\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i}\left(\left|q\right\rangle_{i+1}\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}=\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i}\left(\left|q\right\rangle_{i+1}\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}\ .

(64)

Finally, we need to adopt a boundary condition upon reaching a full memory, and we again choose

b^{(n)\dagger}_{q}\left(\left|1p_{n}\right\rangle_{n}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}=0.

(65)

Implementation of the Annihilation Operators.

By Hermitian conjugation of the creation operators in Eq. (
61
), we directly obtain

\displaystyle b^{(n)}_{p,j}

\displaystyle=\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{n}\otimes...\otimes\underbrace{\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{p}\right)_{j}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{j-1}\otimes...\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{1}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow(j-1)}

\displaystyle={\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\underbrace{\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{s}_{{p}}$}}\right)_{j}}\otimes\,{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow(j-1)},

(66)

whose action over an
i
-particle state with
j>i
is null by the same reasons argued in the commuting case, c.f. Eq. (
48
). The case with
j\leq
is similar, but some care is due for minus signs coming from antisymmetry

\displaystyle b^{(n)}_{p,j}

\displaystyle\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i}\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{j}\,{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{s}_{{p}}$}}\right)_{j}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i}\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{j}\,{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{s}_{{p}}$}}\right)_{j}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i}\frac{\sqrt{(i-1)!}}{\sqrt{i!}}\left[\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}\left(\left|1p_{i-1}\right\rangle_{i-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\sum^{i-1}_{l=1}\left|1p_{l}\right\rangle_{i}\left(\left|1p_{i-1}\right\rangle_{i-1}...\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{l}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}\right]

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}\,\sum^{i}_{l=1}\left(-1\right)^{i-l}\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i}\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\left|1\right\rangle\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{s}_{{p}}$}}\left|p_{l}\right\rangle\right)_{i}\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i-1}...\left|1p_{l+1}\right\rangle_{l}\left|1p_{l-1}\right\rangle_{l-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}\sum^{i}_{l=1}\left(-1\right)^{i-l}\delta_{pp_{l}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i+1}\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i-1}...\left|1p_{l+1}\right\rangle_{l}\left|1p_{l-1}\right\rangle_{l-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}

(67)

As in the commuting case, the action of
\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{p}\right)_{i}
“extracts” all the different momenta from
\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}
. To complete the discussion we can give the annihilation operator in register-operator notation as

b^{(n)}_{q}=\sum^{n}_{j=1}b^{(n)}_{q,j}=\sum^{n}_{j=1}{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{s}_{{p}}$}}\right)_{j}\otimes\,{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow j-1}

(68)

which is the same expression as for a boson operator upon change of step-symmetrizers for step-antisymmetrizers. We note again that
a^{(n)}_{q}
is defined to act over symmetric states, whereas
b^{(n)}_{q}
is the appropriate one on antisymmetric states. The action of the annihilation operator over an
i
-particle antisymmetric state is

\displaystyle b^{(n)}_{q}

\displaystyle\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{n}...\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{i+1}\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}=\sum^{n}_{j=1}b^{(n)}_{q,j}\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{n}...\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{i+1}\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\delta_{ij}\sum^{i}_{l=1}\left(-1\right)^{i-l}\delta_{pp_{l}}\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i+1}\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i-1}...\left|1p_{l+1}\right\rangle_{l}\left|1p_{l-1}\right\rangle_{l-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{pp_{l}}\sum^{i}_{l=1}\left(-1\right)^{i-l}\left|\Omega\right\rangle^{\otimes n-i+1}\left(\left|1p_{i}\right\rangle_{i-1}...\left|1p_{l+1}\right\rangle_{l}\left|1p_{l-1}\right\rangle_{l-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}\ .

(69)

Anticommutation relations.

Creation and annihilation operators then fulfill the anticommutation relations

\left\{b^{(n)}_{q_{1}},b^{(n)\dagger}_{q_{2}}\right\}\ =\ \ \delta_{q_{1},q_{2}}\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{i}$}}\right)_{n}\otimes\sum^{n-1}_{j=0}{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-1})}_{{j}}$}}\ \ +\ \ \mathcal{A}_{n\leftarrow n-1}\cdot\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q_{1}}\mathfrak{s}_{q_{2}}\right)_{n}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-1})}_{{n-1}}$}}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{n\leftarrow n-1},

(70)

where
\mathbb{P}^{({n})}_{{j}}
is the projector over states with
j
-particles on an
n
-register section of memory and
\mathbb{I}^{(n-1)}
is the identity over
(n-1)
-registers.
The demonstration is somewhat cumbersome, so as in the case of bosons it is relegated to appendix
B
.

Again, we see that the first term reproduces the canonical second quantization relation, and the second one is a boundary term appearing only when the memory is full.

V
Exponentiation of fixed particle-number operators

In this section we are going to exemplify the encoding developed with simple few-body Hamiltonian evolution.
The dynamics is implemented in terms of unitary operators, which are Lie exponentials of Hermitian operators written in terms of creation and annihilation operators in momentum space. The two basic cases that we examine are the free-evolution and the momentum-exchange kernel
without particle–number variation.

We shall concentrate on fermion operators
b
,
b^{\dagger}
and only on occasion quote analogous results for bosons.

V.1
Free-evolution operator

The free-evolution operator for fermionic particles on a
N
-register quantum computer is

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{f}_{11}\left(\Delta t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(-i\Delta t\sum_{q}E_{q}b^{(n)\dagger}_{q}b^{(n)}_{q}\right)

(71)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(-i\Delta t\sum_{q}E_{q}\sum_{j^{\prime}j}b^{(n)\dagger}_{q,j^{\prime}}b^{(n)}_{q,j}\right),

while the corresponding operator for bosons,
\mathcal{U}^{b}_{11}\left(\Delta t\right)
, is obtained by replacing fermion for boson operators.
(The
11
subindex indicates that both ket and brac contain exactly one particle.)
To implement the exponential we should first write it in terms of set/scrap operators, by Eq. (
68
)

\displaystyle b^{(n)\dagger}_{q,j^{\prime}}b^{(n)}_{q,j}=

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{A}_{j^{\prime}\leftarrow j^{\prime}-1}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j^{\prime}})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\right)_{j^{\prime}}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j^{\prime}-1})}_{{j^{\prime}-1}}$}}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}_{q}\right)_{j}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow j-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\delta_{j^{\prime}j}\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow j-1}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\mathfrak{s}_{q}\right)_{j}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow j-1}

(72)

where the tensor product of identities multiplying the step-antisymmetrizers is omitted. For boson operators

a^{(n)\dagger}_{q,j^{\prime}}a^{(n)}_{q,j}=\delta_{j^{\prime},j}\mathcal{S}_{j\leftarrow j-1}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\mathfrak{s}_{q}\right)_{j}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}\cdot\mathcal{S}_{j\leftarrow j-1}.

(73)

The operator preserves the number of particles (it has neither
\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}
nor
\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}
terms), and the set-scrap pair selects configurations with a momentum
q
in the
j
th register and discards the rest. The subsequent antisymmetrizer (symmetrizer) to its left then ensures a superposition over all registers from
1
to
j
so that each may have that momentum
q
. The same effect can be achieved by applying the operator
\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\mathfrak{s}_{q}\right)
to each register, assuming the memory is already antisymmetrized (symmetrized):

\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow j-1}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\mathfrak{s}_{q}\right)_{j}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow j-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j+k})}_{{k}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\mathfrak{s}_{q}\right)_{j-k}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1-k})}_{{j-1-k}}$}}\cdot\frac{\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow j-1}}{\sqrt{j}}\ ,

(74)

and correspondingly

\mathcal{S}_{j\leftarrow j-1}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\mathfrak{s}_{q}\right)_{j}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}\cdot\mathcal{S}_{j\leftarrow j-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j+k})}_{{k}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\mathfrak{s}_{q}\right)_{j-k}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1-k})}_{{j-1-k}}$}}\cdot\frac{\mathcal{S}_{j\leftarrow j-1}}{\sqrt{j}}\ ,

(75)

note that both expressions are valid because
\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-i+k})}_{{k}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\mathfrak{s}_{q}\right)_{i-k}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({i-1-k})}_{{i-1-k}}$}}
is a symmetry-preserving operator.

Because number-preserving operators over different registers commute, the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula becomes trivial,
e^{A+B}=e^{A}e^{B}
and the free-evolution operator from Eq. (
71
) can then be factorized

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{f}_{11}(\Delta t)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-i\Delta t\sum_{q}E_{q}\sum_{j^{\prime}j}\delta_{j^{\prime}j}\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow j-1}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\mathfrak{s}_{q}\right)_{j}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow j-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j}\exp\left(\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j+k})}_{{k}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\mathfrak{s}_{q}\right)_{j-k}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1-k})}_{{j-1-k}}$}}\cdot\frac{\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow j-1}}{\sqrt{j}}\right)\ .

(76)

Using the symmetry-preserving property of the exponent

\mathcal{U}^{f}_{11}(\Delta t)=\prod_{j}\left\{\mathbb{I}^{(n)}+\sum^{\infty}_{m=1}\left[\sum_{k}{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j+k})}_{{k}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes(-i\Delta t)\sum_{q}E_{q}{\text{$\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{{q}}$}}{\text{$\mathfrak{s}_{{q}}$}}\right)_{j-k}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1-k})}_{{j-1-k}}$}}\right]^{m}\cdot\frac{\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow j-1}}{\sqrt{j}}\right\},

(77)

where we have separated the
m=0
term of the sum. Because of the step antisymmetrizer
\mathcal{A}
, this operator reduces to the identity when applied to symmetric states. But this is never the case, since by construction the fermion states are antisymmetrized upon creation of each particle. Thus,
with all input states being antisymmetric,
\mathcal{A}
becomes redundant, and the operator may be written down as a double product,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{f}_{11}(\Delta t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j}\left\{\mathbb{I}^{(n)}+\sum^{\infty}_{m=1}\left[\sum_{k}{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j+k})}_{{k}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes(-i\Delta t)-\sum_{q}E_{q}{\text{$\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{{q}}$}}{\text{$\mathfrak{s}_{{q}}$}}\right)_{j-k}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1-k})}_{{j-1-k}}$}}\right]^{m}\right\}

\displaystyle=\prod_{jk}\exp\left[{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j+k})}_{{k}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{q}\mathfrak{s}_{q}\right)_{j-k}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1-k})}_{{j-1-k}}$}}\right],

(78)

where in the last step we used commutativity of terms with different index
k
.

The exponential can be further simplified using the idempotence of projector operators,
\left({\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({m})}_{{l}}$}}\right)^{n}={\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({m})}_{{l}}$}}
and of diagonal control operators
\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\right)^{n}={\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}
:

\mathcal{U}^{f}_{11}(\Delta t)=\prod_{ik}\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({l})}_{{n}}$}}+{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-i+k})}_{{k}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{1}}$}}\otimes\mathfrak{U}\left(\Delta t\right)\right)_{i-k}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({i-1-k})}_{{i-1-k}}$}}\right\},

(79)

where we have defined an auxiliary register-level exponentiation (we can think of it as a composite gate) leading to an operator in terms of set and scrap operators which formally looks like an evolution operator itself,

\mathfrak{U}_{11}(\Delta t)\equiv\exp\left[-i\Delta t\sum_{q}E_{q}{\text{$\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{{q}}$}}{\text{$\mathfrak{s}_{{q}}$}}\right].

(80)

The products over
j
and
k
can be transformed in a sum over
j
using the orthogonality properties of projectors, finally obtaining

Momentum-exchange operator

We now consider one of the simplest unitary operators whose action changes the values stored in registers.
4-body terms are a staple in various fields with instantaneous potentials or contact terms exchanging momentum among a conserved number of particles, and allow for contacting with nonrelativistic, potential quantum mechanics at fixed particle number. VI
Exponentiation of particle-number changing operators

The formalism that we have here developed would not be particularly advantageous with evolution operators such as those of subsections
V.1
and
V.2
, that do not really require a dynamical memory but can instead be statically implemented in other ways at fixed particle number; but it comes into its own when confronted with particle-number changing operators. The difficulty in encoding these comes from the lack of commutativity in the exponentials and the implementation of symmetrizers and antisymmetrizers. We here propose a way of encoding them which is consistent with all the earlier discussion and definitions, without prejudice that more efficient organizations can be thought of in future work. We have chosen to exemplify with one-body operators (“tadpoles”) and three-body operators (“splitting”) that occupy the rest of this section.

VI.1
One-body operators (tadpoles)

One-body operators do not typically appear in field-theoretical Hamiltonian densities formulated on a stable vacuum, as they are linear in the fields, but they can be useful in dynamical situations such as a system rolling down a classical potential.
In any case, our purpose here is to use the simplest possible operator which can expose the changing number of particles, and these are obviously one-body terms.
We first address fermion operators which, though not appearing in physical processes (the superselection rule associated with
(2\pi)
rotations forbids the creation of an odd number of fermions) provide a useful stepping stone for other algorithms. Then we will address the boson tadpole in Subsec.
VI.1.3
.

The resulting unitary operator is then

\mathcal{U}^{f}_{10}(\Delta t)=\exp\left[-i\Delta t\sum_{p}\lambda_{p}\left(b^{(n)\dagger}_{p}+b^{(n)}_{p}\right)\right]=\exp\left[-i\Delta t\sum_{p}\lambda_{p}\sum_{j}\left(b^{(n)\dagger}_{p,j}+b^{(n)}_{p,j}\right)\right].

(103)

The first step is to decompose the operator in products of terms separately acting over each register, which amounts to transforming the exponential of the sum over
j
into a product of exponentials. We iterate to factor each of the
j
-indexed pieces in turn.
Because of the noncommutativity and the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff rule for noncommuting exponents,
e^{A}e^{B}=e^{A+B+\frac{1}{2}[A,B]+\dots}
, we expect errors of order
(\Delta t)^{2}
.
To factor out the first,
\sum_{p}\lambda_{p}(b^{(n)\dagger}_{p,1}+b^{(n)}_{p,1})
we check its commutation with the rest:

\left[\left(b^{(n)\dagger}_{p,1}+b^{(n)}_{p,1}\right),\sum^{n}_{j=2}\left(b^{(n)\dagger}_{q,j}+b^{(n)}_{q,j}\right)\right]=b^{(n)\dagger}_{q,2}b^{(n)\dagger}_{p,1}-b^{(n)}_{q,2}b^{(n)}_{p,1},

(104)

as expected (for any operator changing the number of particles), the factorization is not exact, and some error depending on
\lambda_{l}
and
\Delta t
is introduced if particles are created/annihilated one at a time. Such uncertainty is controlled by the Trotter step with small parameter
\Delta t
, whose asymptotically small values would allow the use of the (approximate at finite
\Delta t
) product formula

\mathcal{U}^{f}_{10}=\exp\left[-i\Delta t\sum_{j}\sum_{p}\lambda_{p}(b^{(n)\dagger}_{p,j}+b^{(n)}_{p,j})\right]=\prod_{j}\exp\left[-i\Delta t\sum_{p}\lambda_{p}(b^{(n)\dagger}_{p,j}+b^{(n)}_{p,j})\right]+O((\Delta t)^{2})

(105)

up to
O((\Delta t)^{2})
in the exponents (thus, the calculation is systematically improvable for
\Delta t\to 0
independently of
\lambda
, even for strong coupling). A more rigorous error bound should also consider the norm of the first non-vanishing commutators; here there are
n
of those, each with a norm
\mathcal{O}\left({N_{p}\bar{\lambda^{2}}=\sqrt{\sum_{p}\sum_{q}\lambda^{2}_{p}\lambda^{2}_{q}}}\right)
, so controlling the error suggests operating with
\Delta t<<\hbar/\sqrt{nN_{p}\bar{\lambda^{2}}}
. In the rest of the manuscript the errors are not written explicitly and in particular its dependence on first commutators is not taken into account.

To continue, creation and annihilation operators are written in terms of their set and scrap counterparts,

b^{(n)\dagger}_{p,j}+b^{(n)}_{p,j}=\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow j-1}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{{p}}$}}\right)_{j}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}+{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{0}{1}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{s}_{{p}}$}}\right)_{j}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow j-1},

(106)

but it is not clear how such a term can be exponentiated, as Eq. (
74
) is not applicable because of the change in particle number. The issue can be circumvented if we manage to rewrite that expression in terms of some new unitary operators (note the circumflex hat), the ordered step antisymmetrizer and its conjugate,
\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger}_{j\leftarrow j-1}
and
\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{j\leftarrow j-1}
satisfying

\displaystyle b^{(n)\dagger}_{p,j}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{j\leftarrow j-1}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{{p}}$}}\right)_{j}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}

\displaystyle\equiv\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger}_{j\leftarrow j-1}\cdot{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({n-j})}_{{0}}$}}\otimes\left(\left({\text{$\mathfrak{C}_{{1}{0}}$}}\otimes{\text{$\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}_{{p}}$}}\right)_{j}\otimes{\text{$\mathbb{P}^{({j-1})}_{{j-1}}$}}\right)_{f,ord}\cdot\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{j\leftarrow j-1},

(107)

where the subscripts
f,ord
are explained in detail in the next paragraphs.
It is obvious that the naïve antisymmetrizers
\mathcal{A}
are not invertible (and thus, not possibly unitary) because their application projects out part of the Hilbert space. To construct a unitary operator which is implementable on a quantum computer, we need an invertible rule. This is possible if we force an initial ordering in the registers, so that the antisymmetrizer takes the ordered set producing an antisymmetrized state, whereas its inverse takes the antisymmetrized state and returns it to the unique, properly ordered state in a bijective manner. Such a procedure was already outlined by Abrams and Lloyd 20 years ago
[
6
]
, see
[
18
]
for a more up to date discussion. Thus we need to distinguish between the
\mathcal{A}
-antisymmetrizer, closer to that of field theory, and the
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
antisymmetrizer, specific for computer registers which have a well defined order.

To achieve unitarity, the new operator
\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger}_{j\leftarrow j-1}
is defined to act as an antisymmetrizer over the ordered subset of the
j
-particle states, and as the identity if the
j
th register is empty,

\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger}_{j\leftarrow j-1}\begin{cases}\left[\left|1p\right\rangle_{j}\left(\left|1p_{j-1}\right\rangle_{j-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}\right]_{ord}&=\left(\left|1p\right\rangle_{j}\left|1p_{j-1}\right\rangle_{j-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}\\
\ \ \left|\Omega\right\rangle_{j}\left(\left|1p_{j-1}\right\rangle_{j-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}&=\left|\Omega\right\rangle_{j}\left(\left|1p_{j-1}\right\rangle_{j-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}\ .\end{cases}

(108)

Here, a state
\left|1p\right\rangle_{j}\left(\left|1p_{j-1}\right\rangle_{j-1}...\left|1p_{1}\right\rangle_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}
is said to be ordered and subscripted so, if the momentum mode on the
j
th-register is the largest, in the sense defined by some appropriate ordering criteria. (Note that, as the antisymmetrization is performed one register at a time, the procedure is different to the one outlined in
[
6
]
.) We here make the following obvious and natural choices, first on the discretized momenta, subsequently on the ordering of states ensured
ad hoc
by the corresponding creation criterion.

Order criterion on momenta.

A mode
\left|p_{i}\right\rangle
is larger or equal than
\left|p_{j}\right\rangle
if and only if
i\geq j
. Checked on runtime by an order oracle

\displaystyle O(\left|x\right\rangle\left|p_{i}\right\rangle\left|p_{j}\right\rangle)=\begin{cases}\left|x\oplus 1\right\rangle\left|p_{i}\right\rangle\left|p_{j}\right\rangle&\text{if }i\geq j\\
\left|x\right\rangle\left|p_{i}\right\rangle\left|p_{j}\right\rangle&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(109)

Order criterion on creation operators.

If a new particle is to be created, the resulting state should have its largest mode, in the sense defined by the order criterion, on the recently occupied register.

(This is to facilitate the invertible antisymmetrization).

From here on, these criteria are enforced and remarked by the subscript
ord
, as in Eq. (
107
). The other subscript there,
f
, is a reminder that no mode is to be created if it is already on memory, and that
(-)
signs should occur each time two particle registers are permuted. We now discuss the detailed implementation of each piece, starting from the unitary operator
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
.

We now face the implementation of the order criterion on creation operators. The “fermion” and “order” subscripts impose two conditions over the creation/annihilation term of Eq. (

We will also need a piece of code which allows the agile exchange of entire particle registers, to which we now devote our attention.

Fermion-

\text{\tt fS}_{{j}}\left({p},{q}\right)

VI.2
Splitting operators

We have now all the elements to work out a splitting operator or vertex interaction. Let us focus, as a master template, on the emission (or absorption) of a boson by a fermion, a process key in QCD gluon emission, nuclear pion emission and more.

\mathcal{U}_{21}(\Delta t,\lambda)=\exp\left[-i\Delta t\sum_{p,k}\lambda_{k-p}\left(a^{\dagger}_{k-p}b^{\dagger}_{p}b_{k}+h.c.\right)\right].

Boson and fermion states are separately encoded because

VI.2.1

Several of the techniques developed earlier are used for exponentiation. First, Eq. (

VII.3
Comparison with JW transformation

A word of comparison with a benchmark method is now in order, and when dealing with second quantization, the Jordan-Wigner transformation comes to the fore.
An encoding of a physical problem in a digital quantum computer (by either technique) consists of a mapping between a given finite Hilbert space (a truncation of the full original one as needed) and the quantum computer’s memory:

J:H\rightarrow(B^{1/2})^{\otimes n},

(160)

with
B^{1/2}
the Hilbert space of a qubit and
n
some number which scales with the dimension of
H
. The states in
H
should be wisely chosen to represent the different configurations of the system to be simulated. For example, quantum chemistry studies commonly use the STO-nG atomic orbital basis
[
41
]
, nuclear shell-model simulations are based on the Slater determinant basis
[
4
]
, whereas for a quark model using the Cornell potential, generalized Laguerre polynomials are enough
[
42
]
, at least for heavy quarks. The common feature of these simulations is that a small number of basis functions or modes are chosen to represent the system, each associated with creation and annihilation operators. Single-particle states are then

\left|\psi_{i}\right\rangle=b^{\dagger}_{i}\left|0\right\rangle,

(161)

while two-particle states are

\displaystyle\left|\psi_{ij}\right\rangle=\pm\left|\psi_{ji}\right\rangle=b^{\dagger}_{i}b^{\dagger}_{j}\left|0\right\rangle,

(162)

etc. The fermionic or bosonic nature of the states is expressed as the relative sign between
\left|\psi_{ij}\right\rangle
and
\left|\psi_{ji}\right\rangle
. Fermions carry a negative sign, which is a way of considering the anticonmutation relations
\left\{b^{\dagger}_{i},b^{\dagger}_{j}\right\}=\left\{b_{i},b_{j}\right\}=0,

and

\left\{b_{i},b^{\dagger}_{j}\right\}=\delta_{ij}
.
Since
\left|\psi_{ij}\right\rangle=-\left|\psi_{ji}\right\rangle
, states of the form
\left|\psi_{ii}\right\rangle
are excluded, which means that
each mode is either occupied or empty
. A general fermionic state can therefore be written as

\left|f_{n},...,f_{1}\right\rangle=b^{\dagger f_{n}}_{n}...b^{\dagger f_{2}}_{2}b^{\dagger f_{1}}_{1}\left|0\right\rangle,

(163)

with
f_{i}\in{0,1}
,
b^{\dagger 0}_{n}=1
and
b^{\dagger 1}_{n}=b^{\dagger}_{n}
. For the annihilation operators

b_{i}|f_{n},...,\underset{i}{\underbrace{1}},...,f_{1}\rangle=(-1)^{\sum^{n}_{j=i+1}f_{j}}|f_{n},...,\underset{i}{\underbrace{0}},...,f_{1}\rangle,

(164)

the action of
b_{i}
is therefore non-local in this basis since it requires a sign that depends on the occupation state of modes with
j>i
.
The Jordan-Wigner mapping identifies the occupation-number basis and that of the direct product of
n
qubits:

\left|f_{n},...,f_{1}\right\rangle\in H\rightarrow J(\left|f_{n},...,f_{1}\right\rangle)=\left|f_{n}\right\rangle\otimes...\otimes\left|f_{1}\right\rangle\in(H^{1/2})^{\otimes n},

(165)

so that the creation and annihilation operators are written as

\displaystyle b^{\dagger}_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{i}^{\otimes n-j-1}\otimes\sigma^{-j}\otimes Z^{\otimes j-1}

(166)

\displaystyle b_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{i}^{\otimes n-j-1}\otimes\sigma^{+j}\otimes Z^{\otimes j-1}

(167)

which are also non-local. Some improvements are possible in basis such as the one introduced by Bravyi and Kitaev
[
8
]
, with even indexed qubits codifying occupation numbers and odd indexed ones encoding some partial sums
y_{i}=\sum^{n}_{j=i+1}f_{j}
, for more detailse see
[
41
,
8
]
.

The codification therefore uses
N
qubits to represent
N
possible single-particle states: the entire Fock space is encoded, from the vacuum to the state obtained from application of all creation operators, i.e, from the
0
-particle state to the
N
-particle state. In contrast, the particle-register codification uses
\mathcal{O}(n\log N)
qubits, with
n
the maximum number of particles to be codified and
N
the number of single-particle states.

It falls off that an implementation based on registers encoding particles is not economical when the number of such particles is high compared to single-particle modes
[
34
]
. The necessity of ensuring either Bose-Einstein or Fermi-Dirac statistics imposes tight constraints, and renders unphysical most combinations of single-particle states which are encodable, thus increasing the unused fraction of the Hilbert space with the number of particles. For example, using two qubits per particle-register to encode momenta allows for up to four different values; composing these registers to generate up to three-particle states will therefore involve six qubits in total. Of the
2^{6}
possible orthogonal states, only the antisymmetric combinations are physical so we have:
\binom{4}{1}=4
single-particle states,
\binom{4}{2}=6
two-particle states and
\binom{4}{3}=4
three-particle states. A direct Jordan-Wigner transformation or akin may thus be preferable on the grounds of memory alone when the number of momenta is very small, so that the logarithmic advantage of the register encoding is not yet manifest: with the same qubits they are able to represent
\binom{6}{1}=6
single-particle states,
\binom{6}{2}=15
two-particle states,
\binom{6}{3}
= 20 three-particle states, etc.
For larger
N
but moderate
n
, the JW encoding is inferior to the register encoding.

VII.4
Final comments

We have proposed a transparent encoding of canonical second quantization of quantum theory for digital quantum computers featuring standard gates.

In traditional field theory much of the emphasis concentrates on the normal modes
a
,
a^{\dagger}
, and we have striven to provide a straightforward construction thereof in terms of set/scrap
\mathfrak{s}^{\dagger}
,
\mathfrak{s}
quantum-number operators as well as memory-control operators
\mathfrak{C}_{ij}

that allow us to select read-write memory registers as needed.

Still, in quantum computation all operations are unitary, so that it is the exponentiatioon of the creation-destruction algebra which takes the central role, so we have provided detailed representations of all needed operations.

Much of the work has been devoted to describing an adequate implementation of the (anti)symmetrization of identical quanta and the bookkeeping necessary to carry out the usual operations (free evolution, particle splitting or annihilation, momentum exchange, etc). Note that explicit symmetrization is essential for our implementation; as seen explicitly on Eqs. (
44
) and Eq.(
63
), the creation and annihilation operators are written as sums of terms changing only the last register, and for this to work all active modes must “be” somehow on the last register. Of course, this has his drawbacks, and we have struggle on section
VI
to give an implementation of particle-changing operators while keeping “local” transformations, for the Pauli exclusion principle and the creation criterion
VI.1
are conditions over the entire memory.

All standard field theory requirements such as commutation relations are satisfied up to boundary terms which arise due to the finite computer memory. In future work we would like to address the necessary (Wilsonian-like) renormalization that arises from varying the memory size.
This implies that certain symmetries will only be recovered in the limit of an infinite memory (saliently continuous spacetime symmetries, for which the situation is identical to that in established lattice gauge theory).
Internal flavor and color symmetries are exactly implemented in our encoding.

Modern physics circumstances in which having this encoding at hand are many, among them the many-body problem in nuclear physics
[
43
]
, in hadron physics
[
44
,
45
]
, in condensed-matter physics and others
[
46
]
.

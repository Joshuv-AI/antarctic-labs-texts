Cavity Induced Topology in Graphene
Ceren B. Dag, Vasil Rokaj

Abstract
Strongly coupling materials to cavity fields can affect their electronic properties altering the phases of matter. We study the monolayer graphene whose electrons are coupled to both left and right circularly polarized photons, and time-reversal symmetry is broken due to a phase shift between the two polarizations. We develop a many-body perturbative theory, and derive cavity mediated electronic interactions. This theory leads to a gap equation which predicts a sizable topological band gap at Dirac nodes in vacuum and when the cavity is prepared in an excited Fock state. Remarkably, band gaps also open in light-matter hybridization points away from the Dirac nodes giving rise to topological photo-electron bands with high Chern numbers. We reveal that the physical mechanism behind this phenomenon lies on the exchange of chiral photons with electronic matter at the hybridization points, and the number and polarization of exchanged photons determine the Chern number. This is a generic microscopic mechanism for the photo-electron band topology. Our theory shows that graphene-based materials, with no need of Floquet engineering and hence protected from the heating effects, host high Chern insulator phases when coupled to chiral cavity fields.

Here we theoretically study a model where a graphene monolayer is coupled to a chiral cavity field with single or two circular polarizations. For the latter, the time-reversal symmetry (TRS) can be broken as a result of an imperfect phase shift with a Faraday mirror, so that one of the polarizations is not eliminated, but only suppressed. In such a setup, what breaks the TRS is the unequal light-matter couplings induced by the two polarizations of the same cavity mode. We formulate a many-body perturbative theory for the continuum Dirac Hamiltonian coupled to light, based on the Schrieffer–Wolff (SW) transformation
Luttinger and Kohn 1955
;
Schrieffer and Wolff 1966
, and obtain the cavity mediated electronic interactions. Then, we apply Hartree-Fock mean-field theory (MFT) and show that the cavity mediated interactions break TRS, and hence open a topological gap. Further, we derive the gap equations at finite temperature for a cavity either in vacuum or in a Fock state with low photon number. The perturbative treatment captures the numerically predicted enhancement of the gap with the number of chiral photons when the cavity is prepared in a Fock state
Rivera et al. 2023
. By also deriving a minimally coupled tight-binding (TB) Hamiltonian for this setup and examining the band structure, we show that our results remain valid within the microscopic theory. Hence we find that the single-polarization model

;
Wang et al. 2019
;
Masuki and Ashida 2023
overestimates the Dirac gap in vacuum when Faraday rotation cannot eliminate one of the polarizations.

A central finding of our work is that TRS breaking also opens topological gaps between the higher energy bands in photo-electron band structure, and away from the Dirac nodes. These topological gaps, being a signature of avoided crossings between strongly coupled electron and photons, contribute nonzero Berry phase to the band wave functions giving rise to higher Chern bands. We unveil the mechanism behind this phenomenon based on the chiral photon exchange processes with matter, and find that the number and polarization of the exchanged photons determine the topology of the photo-electron bands. Our work provides a physically intuitive and generic framework to engineer photo-electron bands with arbitrary Chern numbers, as well as a possible microscopic origin of Floquet topological insulators

Therefore, Chern insulator phases with higher Chern numbers can be engineered by utilizing chiral photonic fields. This mechanism seems very general, and not restricted to graphene. For instance, high Chern numbers were reported in transition metal dichalcogenides coupled to single-polarization cavity field
Nguyen et al. 2023
. Furthermore, the topological bands of the bulk suggests chiral edge modes with electron-photon localized states
Karzig et al. 2015
. Our observation of high Chern numbers might also suggest larger photo-electron currents at the edges, or the domain walls, of the sample which could lend itself to device applications.

Discussion and Outlook.
—We studied graphene subject to a chiral cavity field where TRS is broken through unequal coupling of left- and right-circularly polarized photons to the electrons. Hence, we find a sizable Dirac node splitting even in vacuum. The band gap increases when the cavity is populated with photons, facilitating its experimental measurement. Our analytical theory reveals chiral cavity-mediated electronic interactions in graphene. Understanding the competition of the cavity-mediated interactions with Coulomb interactions is an exciting future direction. This theory can also be applied to moiré materials
Andrei et al. 2021
coupled to cavities which can guide the exploration on how enhanced vacuum fluctuations affect strongly correlated electron systems.
Most importantly, the light-matter entanglement in the vicinity of the avoided crossings induces a nonzero Berry phase to the photo-electron wave function, leading to a rich topology based on the exchange processes of chiral photons with electronic matter. Our theory provides insights and intuition on the nature of topological photo-electron bands suggesting a microscopic mechanism underlying high Chern numbers in periodically-driven systems, and establishes a connection between Floquet and cavity engineering of materials.

\mathcal{H}=\frac{1}{2m}\left(\textrm{i}\hbar\nabla+e\mathbf{A}\right)^{2}+V_{\textrm{crys}}(\mathbf{r})+\sum_{\lambda=x,y}\hbar\omega_{c}\left(b^{\dagger}_{\lambda}b_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\right).

(S1)

In the above Hamiltonian for simplicity we assumed a single-mode photon field with frequency
\omega_{c}=c|\kappa_{z}|
where
\kappa_{z}
is photon momentum chosen along the
z

(x,y)
plane. Moreover, the operators
b^{\dagger}_{\lambda},b_{\lambda}
are the creation and annihilation operators of the photon field satisfying bosonic commutation relations
[b_{\lambda},b^{\dagger}_{\lambda^{\prime}}]=\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}
. The effective mode volume is
\mathcal{V}
,
\epsilon_{0}
is the vacuum permittivity and
m
is the effective electron mass. For two-dimensional materials whose thickness is at the order of a single or a few atoms

;
Andrei et al. 2021
, the variation of the photon field in the

direction can be ignored. This is the well-known long-wavelength (or dipole) approximation, and the photon field takes the simple form
Rokaj et al. 2018

\mathbf{A}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{\epsilon_{0}\mathcal{V}2\omega_{c}}}\sum_{\lambda=x,y}\mathbf{e}_{\lambda}\left(b^{\dagger}_{\lambda}+b_{\lambda}\right)

(S2)

In the above expression we have chosen linearly polarized light with polarization vectors
\mathbf{e}_{x}
and
\mathbf{e}_{y}
as a starting point. Later we discuss the case of circularly polarized photons.

I.1
TRS for Linearly Polarized Photons

In classical physics, the momentum
\mathbf{p}
of a particle and the classical vector potential
\mathbf{A}_{\rm{cl}}(t)
responsible for a classical electric field
\mathbf{E}_{\rm{cl}}(t)
transform under time-reversal
\mathcal{T}
transform as
Luo et al. 2020

\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{p})=-\mathbf{p}\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{A}_{\rm{cl}})=-\mathbf{A}_{\rm{cl}}.

(S3)

Eq. (
S3
) guarantees that the kinetic energy of the particle and the electric field
\mathbf{E}_{\rm{cl}}
are invariant under TRS. The momentum operator in quantum mechanics transforms under TRS in the same way as the classical momentum
\mathcal{T}(-\textrm{i}\hbar\nabla)=\textrm{i}\hbar\nabla
. Again in classical physics, a linearly polarized electric field preserves TRS. These transformation rules must be preserved under quantization. Thus, for the minimal coupling Hamiltonian to be invariant under TRS, the quantized vector potential for linearly polarized photons must transform as,
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{A})=-\mathbf{A}
. For this transformation to hold, the annihilation and creation photon operators must transform under
\mathcal{T}
as follows

\mathcal{T}(b_{\lambda})=-b_{\lambda}\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;\mathcal{T}(b^{\dagger}_{\lambda})=-b^{\dagger}_{\lambda}.

(S4)

With the use of the above relations we find that the energy of the photon number operator
b^{\dagger}_{\lambda}b_{\lambda}
is invariant under TRS
\mathcal{T}(b^{\dagger}_{\lambda}b_{\lambda})=b^{\dagger}_{\lambda}b_{\lambda}
. By combining all the transformation rules, we find that the minimal coupling Hamiltonian for linearly polarized light is invariant under TRS
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{H}
. We numerically confirmed this fact by considering graphene coupled to linearly polarized photons, and we found that no gap opens at Dirac nodes. This holds both with one, Fig.
S1
(c), and two linear polarizations, Fig.
S2
(f).

I.2
TRS for Circularly Polarized Photons

The linearly polarized photon field including both polarizations
\mathbf{e}_{x}
and
\mathbf{e}_{y}
can be equivalently written in terms of left
\mathbf{e}_{L}=(1,-\textrm{i})/\sqrt{2}
and right
\mathbf{e}_{R}=(1,\textrm{i})/\sqrt{2}
circular polarizations through the expressions
\mathbf{e}_{x}=(\mathbf{e}_{R}+\mathbf{e}_{L})/\sqrt{2}
and
\mathbf{e}_{y}=-\textrm{i}(\mathbf{e}_{R}-\mathbf{e}_{L})/\sqrt{2}

Baym 1973
. Then the photon field takes the form

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{\epsilon_{0}\mathcal{V}2\omega_{c}}}\left[\mathbf{e}_{R}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(b_{x}-\textrm{i}b_{y}\right)+\mathbf{e}_{R}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(b^{\dagger}_{x}-\textrm{i}b^{\dagger}_{y}\right)+\mathbf{e}_{L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(b_{x}+\textrm{i}b_{y}\right)+\mathbf{e}_{L}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(b^{\dagger}_{x}+\textrm{i}b^{\dagger}_{y}\right)\right].

(S5)

We can define the following set of annihilation and creation photon operators for left and right circularly polarized photons

\displaystyle b_{L}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(b_{x}-\textrm{i}b_{y}\right)\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;b^{\dagger}_{L}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(b^{\dagger}_{x}+\textrm{i}b^{\dagger}_{y}\right)

(S6)

\displaystyle b_{R}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(b_{x}+\textrm{i}b_{y}\right)\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;b^{\dagger}_{R}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(b^{\dagger}_{x}-\textrm{i}b^{\dagger}_{y}\right).

We note that the left and right handed photon operators satisfy standard bosonic commutation relations
[b_{L},b^{\dagger}_{L}]=[b_{R},b^{\dagger}_{R}]=1
and are independent
[b_{L},b^{\dagger}_{R}]=0
. Thus, the photon field in terms of the left and right handed photons takes the form

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{\epsilon_{0}\mathcal{V}2\omega_{c}}}\left[\mathbf{e}_{R}b_{L}+\mathbf{e}_{R}b^{\dagger}_{R}+\mathbf{e}_{L}b_{R}+\mathbf{e}_{L}b^{\dagger}_{L}\right].

(S7)

Using the definition of the left and right polarized photon operators we find their transformation under time-reversal,

\mathcal{T}(b_{L,R})=-b_{R,L}\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;\mathcal{T}(b^{\dagger}_{L,R})=-b^{\dagger}_{R,L}.

(S8)

Thus, we see that time-reversal exchanges left and right photon operators (up to a minus). The same also holds for left and right polarization vectors due to the imaginary unit,
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{e}_{R})=\mathbf{e}_{L}
and
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{e}_{L})=\mathbf{e}_{R}
. Using all the transformation rules we find that photon field written in terms of the left and right circular polarization, transforms in the same way as the photon field written in terms of the linear polarizations, i.e.,
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{A})=-\mathbf{A}
. The energy of the photon field in terms of left and right circularly polarized operators takes the standard form

\sum_{\lambda=x,y}\hbar\omega_{c}\left(b^{\dagger}_{\lambda}b_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\right)=\sum_{\lambda=L,R}\hbar\omega_{c}\left(b^{\dagger}_{\lambda}b_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\right).

Hence it is evident that the energy of the photon field, including both polarizations, is invariant under time-reversal. Thus, as long as we keep both polarizations for the mode the minimal coupling Hamiltonian preserves TRS, as expected. This is also numerically confirmed below in Fig.
S2
(f) by considering the graphene coupled to both left and right polarizations if their couplings and frequencies are exactly the same.

I.3
TRS Breaking

However, if we eliminate either the left or the right circularly polarized photons, the TRS is broken. To show this we consider the case where we have only the left polarized photons and the right ones are completely eliminated. In this case the photon field has the form

\mathbf{A}_{L}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{\epsilon_{0}\mathcal{V}2\omega_{c}}}\left[\mathbf{e}_{R}b_{L}+\mathbf{e}_{L}b^{\dagger}_{L}\right].

(S9)

We apply the TRS operator on the left circularly polarized photon field, and find that

\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{A}_{L})=-\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{\epsilon_{0}\mathcal{V}2\omega_{c}}}\left[\mathbf{e}_{L}b_{R}+\mathbf{e}_{R}b^{\dagger}_{R}\right]=-\mathbf{A}_{R}.

(S10)

This means that the left circularly polarized photon field is mapped to
-\mathbf{A}_{R}
, and thus TRS is broken. This is an extreme case where TRS is broken because the right circularly polarized photons are completely eliminated. However, TRS breaking also occurs if we have a field where both
\mathbf{A}_{L}
and
\mathbf{A}_{R}
are taken into account with different field strengths,
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}=\alpha_{L}\mathbf{A}_{L}+\alpha_{R}\mathbf{A}_{R}
where
\alpha_{R}\neq\alpha_{L}
. Then one sees that this photon field does not satisfy the necessary condition for the TRS to be preserved, i.e.,
\mathcal{T}\left(\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\right)\neq-\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
. This is the scenario which we investigate in the main text, and find that for graphene coupled to such a photon field a topological gap occurs at the Dirac node signaling the TRS breaking.

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\rm{pm}}_{\mathbf{k}}=\alpha\frac{e}{m}\mathbf{A}\cdot\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ c c }0&$\mathbf{e}_{x}t_{x}f_{x}(\mathbf{k})+\mathbf{e}_{y}t_{y}f_{y}(\mathbf{k})$\\
$\mathbf{e}_{x}t_{x}f^{*}_{x}(\mathbf{k})+\mathbf{e}_{y}t_{y}f^{*}_{y}(\mathbf{k})$&$0$\end{tabular}\right).

It is important to note that we have not set a specific choice for the photon field polarization to obtain the expression above, and thus the result is general within the dipole approximation. We write Eq. (
II
) in the spinor basis,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\rm{pm}}_{\mathbf{k}}=\alpha\frac{e}{m}\mathbf{A}\cdot\left[\mathbf{P}_{1}(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{1}+\mathbf{P}_{2}(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{2}\right],

(S43)

where the
\mathbf{k}
-dependent functions are now vectors,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{1}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathbf{e}_{x}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t_{x}\left(\sin(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_1)-\sin(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_2)\right)-\mathbf{e}_{y}\frac{1}{2}t_{y}\left(\sin(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_1)+\sin(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_2)\right),

(S44)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{2}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{x}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t_{x}\left(\cos(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_1)-\cos(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_2)\right)+\mathbf{e}_{y}t_{y}\left(-1+\frac{1}{2}\cos(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_1)+\frac{1}{2}\cos(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_2)\right).

We note that
t_{x}=t_{y}
is assumed for the calculations which should hold for an unstrained graphene. By adding all the terms —
\mathcal{H}^{\rm{m}}_{\mathbf{k}},\mathcal{H}^{\rm{pm}}_{\mathbf{k}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{p}
—, we find the expression for the tight-binding model of graphene coupled to a cavity photon field,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{k}}=d_{1}(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{1}+d_{2}(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{2}+\alpha\frac{e}{m}\mathbf{A}\cdot\left[\mathbf{P}_{1}(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{1}+\mathbf{P}_{2}(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{2}\right]+\sum_{\lambda=x,y}\hbar\omega\left(a^{\dagger}_{\lambda}a_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\right)

(S45)

Finally using the expression for the photon field in terms of the left and right handed photons

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}=\underbrace{\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{\epsilon_{0}\mathcal{V}2\omega}}}_{A_{0}}\left[\mathbf{e}_{R}a_{L}+\mathbf{e}_{R}a^{\dagger}_{R}+\mathbf{e}_{L}a_{R}+\mathbf{e}_{L}a^{\dagger}_{L}\right].

(S46)

we obtain the corresponding expression for the two-polarization model

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{k}}=d_{1}(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{1}+d_{2}(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{2}+\alpha\frac{e}{m}A_{0}\left[\mathbf{e}_{R}a_{L}+\mathbf{e}_{R}a^{\dagger}_{R}+\mathbf{e}_{L}a_{R}+\mathbf{e}_{L}a^{\dagger}_{L}\right]\cdot\left[\mathbf{P}_{1}(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{1}+\mathbf{P}_{2}(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{2}\right]+\sum_{\lambda=R,L}\hbar\omega\left(a^{\dagger}_{\lambda}a_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\right).

III
Derivation of effective Schrieffer–Wolff Hamiltonians

We will apply Schrieffer–Wolff (SW) transformation
Schrieffer and Wolff 1966
to integrate out the photonic degrees of freedom in the lowest order appearing in the Hamiltonian. This transformation provides an effective Hamiltonian
H
in the form of,

\displaystyle H=e^{S}\mathcal{H}e^{-S},

where
S
is the transformation operator that satisfies the condition
[S,\mathcal{H}_{0}]=-\mathcal{H}_{\text{int}}
in the decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{0}+\mathcal{H}_{\text{int}}
where
\mathcal{H}_{\text{int}}
is the light-matter interaction Hamiltonian. With the condition satisfied, we obtain

\displaystyle H=\mathcal{H}_{0}+\frac{1}{2}[S,\mathcal{H}_{\text{int}}]+\mathcal{O}(g_{R}^{3}).

In the following we apply the SW transformation to the continuum model of graphene.

III.1
Single-polarization model

We assume
g_{L}=0
for the single-polarization model as set in the main text. The following parts of the Hamiltonian include the Dirac model at
\mathbf{K}
point, the cavity energy and the light-matter interaction,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar v_{F}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}(k_{x}+ik_{y})c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+\text{h.c.}+\hbar\Omega_{R}\left(a^{\dagger}_{R}a_{R}+\frac{1}{2}\right),

(S48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\hbar v_{F}\sqrt{2}A_{0}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}a^{\dagger}_{R}c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+\text{h.c.}

(S49)

Here we define
g_{R}\equiv\hbar\sqrt{2}A_{0}
,
\Omega_{R}\equiv\omega_{R}/v_{F}
for convenience and assume
\hbar=1
, which recasts the equations to

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}(k_{x}+ik_{y})c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+\text{h.c.}+\Omega_{R}\left(a^{\dagger}_{R}a_{R}+\frac{1}{2}\right),

(S50)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g_{R}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}a^{\dagger}_{R}c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+\text{h.c.}

(S51)

We utilize the following commutators in the SW transformation,

\displaystyle\left[c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}},c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}\left(n_{B\mathbf{k}}-n_{A\mathbf{k}}\right),

(S52)

\displaystyle\left[c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}},c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}},

\displaystyle\left[c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}},c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\delta_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}},

\displaystyle\left[a_{R},a^{\dagger}_{R}a_{R}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{R},\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;\left[a^{\dagger}_{R},a^{\dagger}_{R}a_{R}\right]=-a^{\dagger}_{R}.

The following transformation operator gives us the result up to the second order in perturbation theory,

\displaystyle S=\tau_{1}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left(a^{\dagger}_{R}c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}-a_{R}c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}\right)+\tau_{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left[-a^{\dagger}_{R}(k_{x}-ik_{y})+a_{R}(k_{x}+ik_{y})\right](n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}}),

(S53)

with
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
, such that
[S,\mathcal{H}_{0}]=-\mathcal{H}_{\text{int}}
. Both of these operators are expectantly anti-Hermitian. Let us note separately the commutator results,

\displaystyle[S_{1},\mathcal{H}_{0}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{1}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg[a^{\dagger}_{R}(k_{x}-ik_{y})(n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}})-a_{R}(k_{x}+ik_{y})(-n_{A\mathbf{k}}+n_{B\mathbf{k}})+\Omega_{R}\left(-a^{\dagger}_{R}c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}-a_{R}c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}\right)\bigg],

\displaystyle\left[S_{2},\mathcal{H}_{0}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left(-a^{\dagger}_{R}(k_{x}-\textrm{i}k_{y})+a_{R}(k_{x}+\textrm{i}k_{y})\right)\left(2(k_{x}+\textrm{i}k_{y})c^{\dagger}_{Ak}c_{Bk}-2(k_{x}-\textrm{i}k_{y})c^{\dagger}_{Bk}c_{Ak}\right)

(S54)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\tau_{2}\Omega_{R}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left[a^{\dagger}_{R}(k_{x}-ik_{y})+a_{R}(k_{x}+ik_{y})\right](n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}}).

We find
\tau_{1}=-g_{R}/\Omega_{R}
and
\tau_{2}=g_{R}/\Omega_{R}^{2}
revealing that the small parameter in this perturbation theory is
1/\Omega_{R}
. Now we derive the effective Hamiltonian up to the second order. Let us start with,

\displaystyle[S,\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{int}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg[-\frac{g_{R}}{\Omega_{R}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left(a^{\dagger}_{R}c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}-a_{R}c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}\right)+\frac{g_{R}}{\Omega_{R}^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left[-a^{\dagger}_{R}(k_{x}-ik_{y})+a_{R}(k_{x}+ik_{y})\right](n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}}),

(S55)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle g_{R}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}a^{\dagger}_{R}c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+\text{h.c.}\bigg].

To calculate this, we will make use of the following commutators

\displaystyle\left[a^{\dagger}_{R}c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}},a_{R}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+a^{\dagger}_{R}a_{R}\hskip 2.84526pt\delta_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}\left(-n_{B\mathbf{k}}+n_{A\mathbf{k}}\right),

(S56)

\displaystyle\left[a_{R}c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}},a^{\dagger}_{R}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+a^{\dagger}_{R}a_{R}\hskip 2.84526pt\delta_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}\left(n_{B\mathbf{k}}-n_{A\mathbf{k}}\right).

This results in the first commutator,

\displaystyle[S_{1},\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{int}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{\Omega_{R}}\sum_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}-2\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{\Omega_{R}}a^{\dagger}_{R}a_{R}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left(n_{B\mathbf{k}}-n_{A\mathbf{k}}\right)+\text{h.c.}

(S57)

For the second commutator calculation, we need the following commutators,

\displaystyle\left[a^{\dagger}_{R}(n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}}),a_{R}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}(n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}})-2a^{\dagger}_{R}a_{R}\hskip 2.84526pt\delta_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}

(S58)

\displaystyle\left[a_{R}(n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}}),a^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}})c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+2a^{\dagger}_{R}a_{R}\hskip 2.84526pt\delta_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}

Then the second commutator becomes,

\displaystyle\left[S_{2},\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{int}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{\Omega_{R}^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}\bigg((k_{x}-ik_{y})\left[-a^{\dagger}_{R}(n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}}),a^{\dagger}_{R}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right]+(k_{x}-ik_{y})\left[-a^{\dagger}_{R}(n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}}),a_{R}\hskip 2.84526ptc_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right]

(S59)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(k_{x}+ik_{y})\left[a_{R}(n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}}),a^{\dagger}_{R}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right]+(k_{x}+ik_{y})\left[a_{R}\hskip 2.84526pt(n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}}),a_{R}\hskip 2.84526ptc_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right]\bigg),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{\Omega_{R}^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}\bigg(-2\left(a^{\dagger}_{R}\right)^{2}(k_{x}-ik_{y})\delta_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+(k_{x}-ik_{y})\left(c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}(n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}})+2a^{\dagger}_{R}a_{R}\hskip 2.84526pt\delta_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(k_{x}+ik_{y})\left((n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}})c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+2a^{\dagger}_{R}a_{R}\hskip 2.84526pt\delta_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right)-2a_{R}^{2}(k_{x}+ik_{y})\delta_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\bigg),

(S60)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{\Omega_{R}^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}(k_{x}+ik_{y})(n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}})c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+\frac{2g^{2}}{\Omega_{R}^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left((k_{x}-ik_{y})\left(a^{\dagger}_{R}\right)^{2}-(k_{x}+ik_{y})a^{\dagger}_{R}a_{R}\right)c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+\text{h.c.}

Therefore, the entire effective Hamiltonian reads,

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg[k_{x}+ik_{y}+\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{\Omega_{R}^{2}}\left((k_{x}-ik_{y})\left(a_{R}^{\dagger}\right)^{2}-(k_{x}+ik_{y})a^{\dagger}_{R}a_{R}\right)+\mathcal{O}(g_{R}/\Omega_{R}^{2},k^{2},a_{R},a_{R}^{\dagger})\bigg]c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+\Omega_{R}\left(a^{\dagger}_{R}a_{R}+\frac{1}{2}\right)

(S61)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{2\Omega_{R}}\sum_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}\bigg(c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+\frac{k_{x}+ik_{y}}{\Omega_{R}}(n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}})c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\bigg)-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{\Omega_{R}}a^{\dagger}_{R}a_{R}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left(n_{B\mathbf{k}}-n_{A\mathbf{k}}\right)+\text{h.c.}

Note that Eq. (
S61
) reduces to the Eq. (5) in the main text when it is projected to a cavity Fock state. The expression for the omitted term in the approximated SW transformation is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(g_{R}/\Omega_{R}^{2},k^{2},a_{R},a^{\dagger}_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2g_{R}}{\Omega_{R}^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg[-a^{\dagger}_{R}\left(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}\right)+a_{R}\left(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2}+i2k_{x}k_{y}\right)\bigg].

(S62)

Since the Dirac model is valid only for infinitesimal
\mathbf{k}
around
\mathbf{K}
and
\mathbf{K^{\prime}}
points, it is valid to omit this term although its strength is
g_{R}/\Omega_{R}^{2}
. Nevertheless, as long as the cavity is in vacuum or in a Fock state with
\Braket{a_{R}^{\dagger}a_{R}}\in\mathbb{N}
, this term drops, and the effective SW Hamiltonian becomes exact. Let us conclude by making the remark that when the cavity is in vacuum state,
\Braket{a_{R}^{\dagger}a_{R}}=0
, the effective Hamiltonian simplifies to,

\displaystyle H_{\rm{vac}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}(k_{x}+ik_{y})c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+\frac{\Omega_{R}}{2}-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{2\Omega_{R}}\sum_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}\bigg(c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+\frac{k_{x}+ik_{y}}{\Omega_{R}}(n_{A\mathbf{k}}-n_{B\mathbf{k}})c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\bigg)+\text{h.c.}

(S63)

Hamiltonian at
\mathbf{K}^{\prime}
valley can be similarly derived.

III.2
Two-polarization model

Focusing at
\mathbf{K}
point with two polarization model, the non-interacting and light-matter interaction Hamiltonians follow as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}(k_{x}+ik_{y})c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+\text{h.c.}+\Omega_{R}\left(a_{R}^{\dagger}a_{R}+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\Omega_{L}\left(a_{L}^{\dagger}a_{L}+\frac{1}{2}\right),

(S64)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg(g_{R}a_{R}^{\dagger}+g_{L}a_{L}\bigg)c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+\text{h.c.}

(S65)

We choose the following SW transformation to find the effective Hamiltonian up to the first order,

\displaystyle S=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left(\bigg[\alpha_{1}a_{R}^{\dagger}-\alpha_{2}a_{L}\bigg]c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}-\bigg[\alpha_{1}a_{R}-\alpha_{2}a_{L}^{\dagger}\bigg]c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}\right),

(S66)

with
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
, such that
[S,\mathcal{H}_{0}]=-\mathcal{H}_{\text{int}}+\mathcal{O}(g_{L(R)}/\Omega_{L(R)}^{2})
. Note that we will truncate the SW for this calculation at the order of
\mathcal{O}(g_{L(R)}/\Omega_{L(R)}^{2})
. The reason is the following: We already calculated the effective Hamiltonian for the single polarization model up to the second order in perturbation theory, and found out that the second order does not change the gap at the Dirac nodes, rather it perturbatively flattens the bands around the Dirac nodes, see Sec.
IV.4
. We find
\alpha_{1}=-g_{R}/\Omega_{R}
and
\alpha_{2}=-g_{L}/\Omega_{L}
. We have

\displaystyle[S,\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{int}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{\Omega_{R}}\sum_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+\text{h.c.}-2\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{\Omega_{R}}a_{R}^{\dagger}a_{R}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left(n_{B\mathbf{k}}-n_{A\mathbf{k}}\right)

(S67)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{g_{L}^{2}}{\Omega_{L}}\sum_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+\text{h.c.}+2\frac{g_{L}^{2}}{\Omega_{L}}a_{L}^{\dagger}a_{L}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left(n_{B\mathbf{k}}-n_{A\mathbf{k}}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle g_{R}g_{L}\left(\frac{1}{\Omega_{L}}-\frac{1}{\Omega_{R}}\right)\left(a_{R}a_{L}+\textrm{h.c.}\right)\left(n_{B\mathbf{k}}-n_{A\mathbf{k}}\right).

Then the effective Hamiltonian reads,

\displaystyle H_{\mathbf{K}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg[k_{x}+ik_{y}+\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{g_{L(R)}^{2}}{\Omega_{L(R)}^{2}},k\bigg)+\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{g_{L(R)}}{\Omega_{L(R)}^{2}},k^{2}\bigg)\bigg]c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+\Omega_{R}\left(a_{R}^{\dagger}a_{R}+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\Omega_{L}\left(a_{L}^{\dagger}a_{L}+\frac{1}{2}\right)

(S68)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}\bigg(\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{2\Omega_{R}}c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+\frac{g_{L}^{2}}{2\Omega_{L}}c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+\text{h.c.}\bigg)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{g_{L}^{2}}{\Omega_{L}}a_{L}^{\dagger}a_{L}-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{\Omega_{R}}a_{R}^{\dagger}a_{R}+\frac{g_{R}g_{L}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\Omega_{L}}-\frac{1}{\Omega_{R}}\right)\left(a_{R}a_{L}+\textrm{h.c.}\right)\bigg)\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left(n_{B\mathbf{k}}-n_{A\mathbf{k}}\right)

When the cavity is in a Fock state,
\Braket{a_{R}^{\dagger}a_{R}}\in\mathbb{N},\Braket{a_{L}^{\dagger}a_{L}}\in\mathbb{N}
, Eq. (
S68
) simplifies to,

\displaystyle H_{\mathbf{K}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{F}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg[(k_{x}+ik_{y})c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+\textrm{h.c.}+\bigg(\frac{g_{L}^{2}}{\Omega_{L}}a_{L}^{\dagger}a_{L}-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{\Omega_{R}}a_{R}^{\dagger}a_{R}\bigg)\left(n_{B\mathbf{k}}-n_{A\mathbf{k}}\right)\bigg]

(S69)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}\bigg(\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}}{2\Omega_{R}}c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+\frac{g_{L}^{2}v_{F}}{2\Omega_{L}}c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+\text{h.c.}\bigg)+\Omega_{R}\left(a_{R}^{\dagger}a_{R}+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\Omega_{L}\left(a_{L}^{\dagger}a_{L}+\frac{1}{2}\right)

When the cavity is in vacuum state,
\Braket{a_{R}^{\dagger}a_{R}}=\Braket{a_{L}^{\dagger}a_{L}}=0
, Eq. (
S68
) simplifies to Eq. (3) in the main text.

IV
Hartree-Fock mean-field theory

IV.1
Treating the first order interaction

Let us focus on the single-polarization model where
g_{L}=0
, and write Hartree and Fock terms by performing the Wick contractions,

\displaystyle H_{1,\textrm{MFT}}^{\textrm{e-e}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{\Omega_{R}}\sum_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}\bigg(\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}-\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}}\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}

(S70)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}c_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}+c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}}-\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}}\bigg).

Note that strictly speaking, this is not an equality. Since we take two channels into account, we rescale Eq. (
S70
) by a factor of
2
,

\displaystyle H_{1,\textrm{MFT}}^{\textrm{e-e}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{2\Omega_{R}}\sum_{\mathbf{kk^{\prime}}}\bigg(\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}-\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}}\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}c_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}+c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}}-\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}}\bigg).

Working with a thermal ensemble of electrons, we need to make sure that the Wick contractions are done with respect to an appropriate thermal ensemble which is the Fermi-Dirac distribution. This gives rise to the following
nonzero
Wick contractions (see Sec.
IV.2
for the intermediate steps),

\displaystyle\Braket{c^{\dagger}_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{E_{\mathbf{k}}+\Delta(\mathbf{k})}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}}\frac{1}{e^{\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}}+1}+\frac{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}(E_{\mathbf{k}}+\Delta(\mathbf{k}))}\frac{1}{e^{-\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}}+1}=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(1-\frac{\Delta(\mathbf{k})\tanh(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}/2)}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}\bigg),

(S71)

\displaystyle\Braket{c^{\dagger}_{B\mathbf{k}}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}(E_{\mathbf{k}}+\Delta(\mathbf{k}))}\frac{1}{e^{\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}}+1}+\frac{E_{\mathbf{k}}+\Delta(\mathbf{k})}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}}\frac{1}{e^{-\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}}+1}=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(1+\frac{\Delta(\mathbf{k})\tanh(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}/2)}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}\bigg),

(S72)

\displaystyle\Braket{c^{\dagger}_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k_{x}-ik_{y}}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{e^{\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}}+1}-\frac{1}{e^{-\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}}+1}\bigg)=-\frac{k_{x}-ik_{y}}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}}\tanh\bigg(\frac{\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}}{2} \bigg).

(S73)

For now, let us only consider the channel regarding the Eqs. (
S71
) and (
S72
), because these channels are responsible for the gap opening. The remaining channel’s effect is shown to be zero in Sec.
IV.3
. Then, we obtain an MFT Hamiltonian in the first order

\displaystyle H^{(1)}_{\textrm{MFT}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg[(k_{x}+ik_{y})c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+\text{h.c.}-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{2\Omega_{R}}\bigg(\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}(-c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}+c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}})-\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}^{2}+\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}\bigg)\bigg].

(S74)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg[v_{F}(k_{x}\sigma_{1}+k_{y}\sigma_{2})-\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{R}}\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}\sigma_{3}\bigg]+E_{0}.

Here we make a definition for the gap,

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{R}}\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}},

(S75)

as this is the amplitude of
\sigma_{3}
term.
E_{0}
in Eq. (
S74
) is the many-body ground state energy,

\displaystyle E_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{R}}{2}+\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{R}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg(\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}^{2}-\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}\bigg)=\frac{\omega_{R}}{2}+\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg(\frac{2\omega_{R}}{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\Delta(\mathbf{k})^{2}-\Delta(\mathbf{k})\bigg).

(S76)

By using the Wick rotations, we find the gap equation to be

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{R}}\frac{1}{2}\bigg(1+\frac{\Delta(\mathbf{k})\tanh(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}/2)}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}\bigg).

(S77)

Let us note that at zero temperature and exactly at
\mathbf{K}
point, we obtain
\Delta_{0}(\mathbf{0})=g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}/2\omega_{R}
as already discussed in the Letter. Here the notation
\Delta_{T}(\mathbf{k})
denotes the vacuum gap at temperature
T
and momentum
\mathbf{k}
. In the opposite limit where the inverse temperature
\beta\rightarrow 0
, we can expand
\tanh
around
0
, and obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{\infty}(\mathbf{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{4\omega_{R}}+\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{4\omega_{R}}\frac{\Delta}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}\bigg(\frac{\beta}{2}E_{\mathbf{k}}-\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{\beta}{2}E_{\mathbf{k}}\right)^{3}+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{5})\bigg)

(S78)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{4\omega_{R}}+\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2^{3}\omega_{R}}\Delta\beta-\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{3\times 2^{5}\omega_{R}}\Delta E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}\beta^{3}+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{5})=\frac{\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{4\omega_{R}}}{1-\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{8\omega_{R}}\beta}.

If
\beta\ll\frac{8\omega_{R}}{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{\infty}(\mathbf{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{4\omega_{R}}.

(S79)

Hence we find, the gap based on the first order perturbation, changes from
\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{R}}
to
\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{4\omega_{R}}
as temperature increases. Except these temperature points, we cannot solve the equation analytically. Hence we solve Eq. (
S77
) numerically to find the dependence on the temperature. Fig.

(a-c) depicts the numerical results to the gap equation in the first order perturbation theory in the single-polarization limit (blue-solid). In (a) we observe that as we move away from the Dirac nodes, the gap decreases. Fig.

S5

IV.2
Bogoliubov diagonalization for the MFT Hamiltonian in the single-polarization limit

Here we give the exact diagonalization of the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg(k_{x}\sigma_{1}+k_{y}\sigma_{2}-\Delta(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{3}+E_{0}(\mathbf{k})\bigg).

(S80)

This form appears after the Wick contractions. Since this is a fermionic system, the Bogoliubov transformation follows as

\displaystyle\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}=uc_{A\mathbf{k}}+vc_{B\mathbf{k}},\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}=-v^{*}c_{A\mathbf{k}}+u^{*}c_{B\mathbf{k}},

(S81)

while the inverse transformation is

\displaystyle c_{A\mathbf{k}}=u^{*}\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}-v\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}},\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;c_{B\mathbf{k}}=v^{*}\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}+u\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}.

(S82)

To write
H
in terms of the new basis, we need to find

\displaystyle c^{\dagger}_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|u|^{2}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}+|v|^{2}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\xi\mathbf{k}}\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}-uv\gamma^{\dagger}_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}-u^{*}v^{*}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\xi\mathbf{k}}\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}},

(S83)

\displaystyle c^{\dagger}_{B\mathbf{k}}c_{B\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|v|^{2}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}+|u|^{2}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\xi\mathbf{k}}\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}+uv\gamma^{\dagger}_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}+u^{*}v^{*}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\xi\mathbf{k}}\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}},

\displaystyle c^{\dagger}_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{B\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle uv^{*}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}-uv^{*}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\xi\mathbf{k}}\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}+u^{2}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}-(v^{*})^{2}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\xi\mathbf{k}}\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}.

Substituting these into the Hamiltonian results in the following three terms,

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}E_{0}(\mathbf{k}).

(S84)

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg[(k_{x}+ik_{y})uv^{*}+\text{h.c.}-\Delta(\mathbf{k})\bigg(|v|^{2}-|u|^{2}\bigg)\bigg]\bigg(\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}-\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}\bigg)

(S85)

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg[(k_{x}+ik_{y})u^{2}-(k_{x}-ik_{y})v^{2}-2uv\Delta(\mathbf{k})\bigg]\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}+\text{h.c.}

(S86)

For diagonalization to happen,
H_{2}=0
which leads to the expressions for
u
and
v
. By also using the fact that
|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}=1
,

\displaystyle(k_{x}+ik_{y})u^{2}-(k_{x}-ik_{y})v^{2}-2uv\Delta(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow u=\sqrt{\frac{E_{\mathbf{k}}+\Delta(\mathbf{k})}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}}},\hskip 5.69054ptv=\frac{k_{x}+ik_{y}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf{k}}(E_{\mathbf{k}}+\Delta(\mathbf{k}))}},

(S87)

where
E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}=k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}+\Delta(\mathbf{k})^{2}
is the excitation energy. Substituting these into
H_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}E_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg(\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}-\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}\bigg).

(S88)

Hence we see that the lower energy band is denoted by
\beta
with energy
-E_{\mathbf{k}}
. Note that the Bogoliubov quasi-particles (Bogoliubons) follow the Fermi-Dirac distribution when Wick contraction is applied in Sec.
IV.1

\displaystyle\Braket{\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}}=\frac{1}{e^{\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}}+1},\hskip 14.22636pt\Braket{\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}}=\frac{1}{e^{-\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}}+1},

(S89)

where
\beta
is the inverse temperature. The tunneling terms are

\displaystyle\Braket{\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\bigg[\rho\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}\bigg]=\text{Tr}\bigg[\sum_{\sigma\mathbf{k}}\frac{\gamma_{\sigma\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\Ket{\mathbf{0}}\Bra{\mathbf{0}}\gamma_{\sigma\mathbf{k}}}{e^{\beta E_{\sigma}(\mathbf{k})}-1}\gamma_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\xi\mathbf{k}}\bigg]=0.

(S90)

IV.3
Considering the second channel

Here let us discuss the fate of the channel
\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\Braket{c^{\dagger}_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}
. We immediately observe the following equation

\displaystyle v_{F}\kappa_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{\omega_{R}}\sum_{\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}\Braket{c^{\dagger}_{A\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}c_{B\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}=\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{\omega_{R}}\sum_{\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}\frac{v_{F}(k^{\prime}_{x}-ik^{\prime}_{y}+\kappa_{c})}{2E_{\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}}\tanh\bigg(\frac{\beta E_{\mathbf{k'}}}{2}\bigg)\in\mathbb{C}.

First, at infinite temperature
\beta\rightarrow 0
,
\kappa_{c}=0
. For the other temperatures, this gives an integral equation over the Fermi surface

\displaystyle\kappa_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{\omega_{R}}\int_{-\kappa_{y}}^{\kappa_{y}}dk^{\prime}_{y}\int_{-\kappa_{x}}^{\kappa_{x}}dk^{\prime}_{x}\frac{k^{\prime}_{x}-ik^{\prime}_{y}+\kappa_{c}}{2E_{\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}}\tanh\bigg(\frac{\beta E_{\mathbf{k'}}}{2}\bigg)

Note that
\kappa_{c}
is momentum independent, by definition. Also since
\kappa_{c}
can be written as a mere shift on the momenta,

\displaystyle\kappa_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{\omega_{R}}\int_{-\kappa_{y}}^{\kappa_{y}}dk^{\prime}_{y}\int_{-\kappa_{x}}^{\kappa_{x}}dk^{\prime}_{x}\frac{(k^{\prime}_{x}+\kappa_{c}^{x})-i(k^{\prime}_{y}+\kappa_{c}^{y})}{2E_{\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}}\tanh\bigg(\frac{\beta E_{\mathbf{k'}}}{2}\bigg)

We do a change of variables,
k_{x}=k_{x}^{\prime}+\kappa_{c}^{x}
and
k_{y}=k_{y}^{\prime}+\kappa_{c}^{y}
,

\displaystyle\kappa_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{\omega_{R}}\int_{-\kappa_{y}+\kappa_{c}^{y}}^{\kappa_{y}+\kappa_{c}^{y}}dk_{y}\int_{-\kappa_{x}+\kappa_{c}^{x}}^{\kappa_{x}+\kappa_{c}^{x}}dk_{x}\frac{k_{x}-ik_{y}}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}}\tanh\bigg(\frac{\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}}{2}\bigg).

Now let us change to polar coordinates,
k=\sqrt{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}}
and
\tan\phi=k_{y}/k_{x}
,

\displaystyle\kappa_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{\omega_{R}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\phi\int_{|\kappa_{c}|}^{|\kappa_{c}|+\kappa_{\textrm{F}}}kdk\frac{e^{-i\phi}}{2E_{k}}\tanh\bigg(\frac{\beta E_{k}}{2}\bigg)=0.

where
|\kappa_{c}|=|\kappa_{c}^{x}-i\kappa_{c}^{y}|=\sqrt{(\kappa_{c}^{x})^{2}+(\kappa_{c}^{y})^{2}}
, and
\kappa_{\textrm{F}}=\sqrt{\kappa_{x}^{2}+\kappa_{y}^{2}}
is the Fermi momentum. Hence
we find
\kappa_{c}=0
, and
\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\Braket{c^{\dagger}_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}=0
at any temperature. Hence the first line of Eq. (
S70
) vanishes.

IV.4
MFT on the second order interaction term

In this section, we perform the MFT to the second order interaction term in Eq. (
S63
). By using the following expressions,

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{2\Omega_{R}^{2}}(k_{x}+ik_{y})\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{4\Omega_{R}^{2}}e^{i\phi}k\bigg(1+\frac{\Delta(\mathbf{k})\tanh(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}/2)}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}\bigg),

(S91)

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{2\Omega_{R}^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}(k_{x}+ik_{y})\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{2\Omega_{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{4\pi\kappa_{\textrm{F}}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{i\phi}d\phi\int_{0}^{\kappa_{\textrm{F}}}k^{2}dk\bigg(1-\frac{\Delta(\mathbf{k})\tanh(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}/2)}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}\bigg)=0,

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{2\Omega_{R}^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}(k_{x}+ik_{y})\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

we find,

\displaystyle H_{2,\textrm{MFT}}^{\textrm{e-e}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{4\Omega_{R}^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}(k_{x}+ik_{y})\bigg(-\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}+c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}-\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}

(S92)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}-\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}+\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}\bigg)+\text{h.c.}

See the Sec.
IV.3
for the definition of
\phi
and
k
appeared in Eqs. (
S91
). Hartree-Fock expansion leads to the following MFT Hamiltonian in the second order perturbation theory,

\displaystyle H_{\textrm{MFT}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg[\bigg(1-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{2\Omega_{R}^{2}}\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}\bigg)(k_{x}+ik_{y})c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+\text{h.c.}

(S93)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{2\Omega_{R}}\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}+(k_{x}+ik_{y})\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{4\Omega_{R}^{2}}\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}+\text{h.c.}\bigg)(-c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}+c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(k_{x}+ik_{y})\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{4\Omega_{R}^{2}}\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}\bigg(2\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}-1\bigg)+\text{h.c.}+\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{2\Omega_{R}}\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}^{2}-\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{2\Omega_{R}}\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}\bigg].

Hence we have a new gap equation (with Fermi velocity
v_{F}
substituted back) and an equation for the Fermi velocity renormalization,

\displaystyle\Delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{R}}\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}+v_{F}(k_{x}+ik_{y})\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{4\omega_{R}^{2}}\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}+\text{h.c.}

(S94)

\displaystyle v_{F}^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(1-\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{R}^{2}}\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}\bigg)v_{F}.

(S95)

Let us substitute Eqs. (
S94
) and (
S95
) into Eq. (
S93
),

\displaystyle H_{\textrm{MFT}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg[v_{F}^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})(k_{x}+ik_{y})c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}+\text{h.c.}-\Delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})(-c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}+c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}})\bigg]+E_{0}^{(2)},

(S96)

where
E_{0}^{(2)}
is the many-body ground state energy in the second order perturbation theory,

\displaystyle E_{0}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{R}}{2}+\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\bigg[-\Delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})+\frac{4\omega_{R}^{2}}{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\Delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})\left(1-\frac{v_{F}^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})}{v_{F}}\right)-\frac{2\omega_{R}^{3}}{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\bigg(1-\frac{v_{F}^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})}{v_{F}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg].

(S97)

The energy expression in the second order reads
E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}=(v_{F}^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})k)^{2}+(\Delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{k}))^{2}
noting
v_{F}^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})\in\mathbb{R}
. The Wick contractions should be updated as
v_{F}k_{x}\rightarrow v_{F}^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})k_{x}
,
v_{F}k_{y}\rightarrow v_{F}^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})k_{y}
and
\Delta(\mathbf{k})\rightarrow\Delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})
below.

\displaystyle\Braket{c^{\dagger}_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{A\mathbf{k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bigg(1-\frac{\Delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})\tanh(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}/2)}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}\bigg),

(S98)

\displaystyle\Braket{c^{\dagger}_{B\mathbf{k}}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bigg(1+\frac{\Delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})\tanh(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}/2)}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}\bigg),

\displaystyle\Braket{c^{\dagger}_{A\mathbf{k}}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-v_{F}^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})\frac{k_{x}-ik_{y}}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}}\tanh\bigg(\frac{\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}}{2} \bigg).

The coupled MFT equations for the second order perturbation theory follow after Eqs. (
S98
) are substituted into Eqs. (
S94
) and (
S95

\displaystyle\Delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{4\omega_{R}}\bigg(1+\frac{\Delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})\tanh(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}/2)}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}\bigg)-v_{F}^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})v_{F}k^{2}\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{R}^{2}}\frac{\tanh(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}/2)}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}}

(S99)

\displaystyle v_{F}^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{F}-\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{3}}{4\omega_{R}^{2}}\bigg(1+\frac{\Delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{k})\tanh(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}/2)}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}\bigg).

Let us state these coupled MFT equations at
\mathbf{K}
point,

\displaystyle\Delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{4\omega_{R}}\bigg(1+\tanh(\beta\Delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{0})/2)\bigg)

(S100)

\displaystyle v_{F}^{(2)}(\mathbf{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{F}\bigg(1-\frac{\Delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{0})}{2\omega_{R}}\bigg).

The gap equation turns out to be the same with the gap equation in the first order perturbation theory, Eq. (
S77
). However, the second order perturbation introduces a renormalization to the Fermi velocity. At zero temperature,
\beta\rightarrow\infty
, equations can be analytically solved

\displaystyle\Delta^{(2)}_{0}(\mathbf{0})=\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{R}}\;\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;\;v_{F,0}^{(2)}(\mathbf{0})=v_{F}\bigg(1-\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{R}^{2}}\bigg).

(S101)

We solve the equations numerically around
\mathbf{K}
point in terms of Fermi momentum
\kappa_{\textrm{F}}
.

Fig.
S5
(a-c) summarizes the numerical solutions to the gap equation in both first and second order perturbation theories in the single-polarization limit. In (a), we observe that Fermi velocity renormalization is negligible at the Dirac nodes and away from them. This is consistent within the perturbation theory, see Eq. (
S101
). At the Dirac nodes, the effect is the largest and is practically independent of the temperature, Fig.
S5

This has an important effect in the match with the numerical band structure as shown in the main text, Fig. 1. The gap in both orders is the same at the Dirac nodes independently from the temperature, Fig.
S5
(b), and it expectantly decreases with the temperature. We observe that the gap at the Fermi momentum increases with temperature in the second order, eventually converges to the same value with the first order gap at large temperatures, Fig.
S5

IV.5
MFT on two-polarization model

There are two interaction terms in the effective SW Hamiltonian of two-polarization model, Eq. (
S69
), and they have to be treated separately giving rise to two independent gap equations. These interactions give rise to the following effective MFT Hamiltonians,

\displaystyle H^{(R)}_{\textrm{MFT}}=-\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{R}}\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}\sigma_{3}\;\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;\;H^{(L)}_{\textrm{MFT}}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{g_{L}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{L}}\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}}\sigma_{3},

(S102)

where
H_{0}=v_{F}(k_{x}\sigma_{1}+k_{y}\sigma_{2})
in both cases. Then defining

\displaystyle\Delta_{R}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{R}}\Braket{c_{B\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{B\mathbf{k}}}\;\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;\;\Delta_{L}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{g_{L}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{L}}\Braket{c_{A\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{A\mathbf{k}}},

we can write

\displaystyle H_{\mathbf{K}}^{\textrm{MFT}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\bigg(k_{x}\sigma_{1}+k_{y}\sigma_{2}-\bigg[\Delta_{R}(\mathbf{k})-\Delta_{L}(\mathbf{k})\bigg]\sigma_{3}+E_{0}(\mathbf{k})\bigg).

(S103)

By using Eqs. (
S71
) and (
S72
), we find the gap equations stated in the Letter, Eq. (4). Total gap is,
\Delta(\mathbf{k})=\Delta_{R}(\mathbf{k})-\Delta_{L}(\mathbf{k})
. Then one could see,
\Delta(\mathbf{k})=0
always holds if the time reversal symmetry is preserved. The many-body ground state energy will follow closely to the ones found for single polarization model in this case. Let us work this out explicitly. We have
E_{0}(\mathbf{k})=E_{0,R}(\mathbf{k})+E_{0,L}(\mathbf{k})
with,

\displaystyle E_{0,R}=\frac{\omega_{R}}{2}+\sum_{k}\bigg(\frac{2\omega_{R}}{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\Delta_{R}(\mathbf{k})^{2}-\Delta_{R}(\mathbf{k})\bigg)\;\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;\;E_{0,L}=\frac{\omega_{L}}{2}+\sum_{k}\bigg(\frac{2\omega_{L}}{g_{L}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\Delta_{L}(\mathbf{k})^{2}-\Delta_{L}(\mathbf{k})\bigg).

For higher energy gaps at the Dirac nodes, the result follows very similar to the single polarization case too:

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}(\mathbf{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+2n_{R})\frac{g_{R}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{R}}-(1+2n_{L})\frac{g_{L}^{2}v_{F}^{2}}{2\omega_{L}}.

(S104)

Figs.
S5
(d-e) show the numerically extracted gap for the two-polarization model where
g_{R}=\sqrt{2}g_{L}
is set due to the phase shift anticipated with the Faraday effect. Both with respect to momentum and temperature, we observe very similar behaviors to the single-polarization limit. Let us note that the calculated gaps are rescaled with
\Delta_{0}(\mathbf{0})
, the gap at zero temperature and Dirac nodes in the two-polarization model considered.

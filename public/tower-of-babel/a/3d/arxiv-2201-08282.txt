One-Dimensional Quantum Systems with Ground-State of Jastrow Form Are Integrable
Jing Yang, Adolfo del Campo

Abstract
The exchange operator formalism (EOF) describes many-body integrable systems using phase-space variables involving an exchange operator that acts on any pair of particles. We establish an equivalence between models described by EOF and the complete infinite family of parent Hamiltonians (PHJ) describing quantum many-body models with ground-states of Jastrow form. This makes it possible to identify the invariants of motion for any model in the PHJ family and establish its integrability, even in the presence of an external potential. Using this construction we establish the integrability of the long-range Lieb-Liniger model, describing bosons in a harmonic trap and subject to contact and Coulomb interactions in one dimension. We give a variety of examples exemplifying the integrability of Hamiltonians in this family.

Integrability in both classical and quantum many-body systems is associated with the existence of conserved quantities.
At the quantum level, the latter correspond to operators that commute with the system Hamiltonian and govern the nonequilibrium dynamics and thermalization of a system in isolation
Dziarmaga 2010
;
Mori et al. 2018
.
Several integrable models have been realized in the laboratory,
prompting their use as a test-bed for quantum many-body physics, statistical
mechanics, and nonequilibrium phenomena
Cazalilla et al. 2011
;
Guan et al. 2013
.

The integrability of a system may be proven by finding the set of conserved
quantities. In one spatial dimension, this is possible in systems that are exactly solved using Bethe ansatz, which posits that the wavefunction of any quantum eigenstate admits an expansion in terms of plane waves with suitable coefficients and quasimomenta. The latter set the integrals of motion, are also known as the Bethe roots or rapidities, and serve as “good” quantum numbers
(
Takahashi 1999
;
Gaudin 2014
)
.
An alternative framework is the exchange operator formalism (EOF)
(
Polychronakos 1992
;
Polychronakos 2006
)
, in which the Hamiltonian of the quantum system admits a decoupled form in terms of generalized momenta, which readily allows for the identification of integrals of motion. This approach can be applied to the study of excited states, as demonstrated in systems with inverse-square interactions
Lapointe and Vinet 1996
;
Ujino and Wadati 1996
.
An encompassing notion of quantum integrability relies on scattering without diffraction, encoded in the
Yang-Baxter equation
(
Yang 1967
;
Baxter 1978
;
Korepin et al. 1997
)
,
when collisions between particles can be described exclusively as
a sequence of two-body scattering events.
The system is then solvable by algebraic Bethe ansatz, i.e., using the quantum inverse scattering method.
Integrals of motion can be derived from the transfer matrix
Korepin et al. 1997
or invoking the
asymptotic Bethe ansatz
(
Sutherland 2004
;
Sutherland 1995
)
. While a definite notion of quantum integrability remains under debate, many of
these approaches are closely interrelated
(
Caux and Mossel 2011
;
Gaudin 2014
)
. In particular, EOF is related to the Yang-Baxter equation and asymptotic Bethe ansatz
(
Ujino and Wadati 1995
;
Polychronakos 2020
)
.

An important class of quantum systems is characterized by a ground-state of (Bijl-Dingle-) Jastrow form, in which
the wave function is simply the pairwise product of a pair function
(
Bijl 1940
;
Dingle 1949
;
Jastrow 1955
)
.
This facilitates the computation of correlation functions in these
systems
(
Sutherland 2004
)
. The family of parent Hamiltonians with
Jastrow wave functions (PHJ, for short) can be determined by solving an inverse problem:
by acting with the kinetic energy operator in the ground-state wave
function, one can recast the resulting terms in the form of a many-body
Schrödinger equation, thus identifying the parent hamiltonian.
This approach has its roots in the early works by Calogero
and Sutherland
(
Calogero 1971
;
Sutherland 1971a
;
Sutherland 1971b
)
. It
has been extended in a number of ways
(
Calogero and Marchioro 1973
;
Gambardella 1975
)

and by now, for identical particles without internal degrees of freedom,
the complete family of PHJ is known both in one and
higher spatial dimensions, provided that the ground-state wave function
includes at most the product of one-particle and two-particle functions

(
del Campo 2020
;
Beau and del
Campo 2021
)
. The corresponding Hamiltonians generally
contain two-body and three-body interactions. It was shown by Kane
et al.
(
Kane et al. 1991
)
that the three-body contribution does not affect
the low-energy physics. Further, the conditions for the three-body
term to vanish or reduce to a constant have been long-established
in the homogeneous case, in the absence of an external potential
(
Calogero 1975
;
Sutherland 2004
;
Šamaj and Bajnok 2013
)
.

Paradigmatic instances of PHJ are integrable.
Hard-core bosons in the Tonks-Girardeau regime, realized in the laboratory
with ultracold gases
(
Kinoshita et al. 2004
;
Paredes et al. 2004
)
, have ground
state of Jastrow form
(
Girardeau 1960
;
Girardeau et al. 2001
;
Girardeau and Minguzzi 2007
)

and are integrable, being related to noninteracting fermions via the
Bose-Fermi duality
(
Girardeau 1960
;
Girardeau et al. 2004
;
Cazalilla et al. 2011
)
. The
Calogero-Sutherland model with a Jastrow ground state has a harmonic
spectrum, it can be mapped to a set of independent harmonic oscillators

(
Kawakami 1993
;
Vacek et al. 1994
;
Gurappa and Panigrahi 1999
)
, and satisfies the asymptotic Bethe ansatz
(
Sutherland 1995
;
Sutherland 2004
)
.
Similarly, the attractive Lieb-Liniger (LL) model of bosons subject to contact interactions, used to describe ultracold gases in tight waveguides
Olshanii 1998
;
Lieb et al. 2003
, has a bright quantum soliton
as Jastrow ground-state
(
McGuire 1964
)
. This system is solvable
by coordinate Bethe ansatz, which yields the Bethe roots as integrals of motion
(
Lieb and Liniger 1963
;
Lieb 1963
;
Takahashi 1999
;
Gaudin 2014
)
.

One may thus wonder the extent to which the ground-state correlations can determine the complete integrability of the system, and what are the required conditions for this to be the case.
In this Letter, we show that the complete family of one-dimensional many-body quantum models with ground-state of Jastrow form is integrable.
To this end, we first establish the equivalence between this family and models described by EOF. In doing so, we identify explicitly the integrals of motion. Our construction holds in the presence of an external potential, which allows us to show the integrability of the long-range Lieb-Liniger model, describing bosons confined in a harmonic trap and subject to both contact and Coulomb interactions in one spatial dimension
(
del Campo 2020
;
Beau et al. 2020
)
.

Systems described by EOF—

Consider the family of one-dimensional systems of identical particles without internal degrees of freedom.
It will prove useful to consider those models subject to pair-wise interactions that are possibly supplemented with three-body interactions.
In this context,
EOF is a powerful framework due to Polychronakos that explicitly exhibits the integrability of a many-body quantum system in one spatial dimension
Polychronakos 1992
;
Polychronakos 2006
. Its application has been particularly fruitful in Calogero-Sutherland-Moser systems involving two-body inverse-square interactions
Calogero 1971
;
Sutherland 1971a
;
Sutherland 1971c
;
Moser 1975
;
Calogero and Marchioro 1973
;
Sutherland 2004
, as discussed in
Polychronakos 1992
;
Polychronakos 2006
.

Let
M_{ij}
denote the
exchange operator, which exchanges the positions
of two particles labelled by
i
and
j
, respectively. This operator is Hermitian,
idempotent
M_{ij}^{2}=\mathbb{I}
and symmetric with respect to
the indices, i.e.,
M_{ij}=M_{ji}
. For any
1
-body operator
A_{j}\equiv A(x_{j})
,
it obeys the relations
M_{ij}A_{j}=A_{i}M_{ij}

and
M_{ij}A_{k}=A_{k}M_{ij}
for distinct
i
,

j
,
k

(
Polychronakos 1992
;
Polychronakos 2006
;
Yang and del Campo 2022
)
. Note that when for spinless identical particles,

M_{ij}
can be identified with the permutation of two particles.
In terms of the canonical position and momentum coordinates,
x_{i}

and
p_{j}=-\text{i}\hbar\partial/\partial x_{j}
, one can introduce the generalized
momenta

\pi_{i}=p_{i}+\text{i}\sum_{j\neq i}V_{ij}M_{ij},

(1)

for particles
j=1,\cdots,N
. The so-called prepotential function

V_{ij}=V(x_{i}-x_{j})
should be antisymmetric (i.e.,
V_{ij}=-V_{ji}
)
to guarantee the Hermiticity of the generalized momenta. Using the
latter, one can construct a permutation-invariant quantities
I_{n}\equiv\sum_{i}\pi_{i}^{n}
.
In particular,
I_{2}
is quadratic in
p_{i}
’s, and resembles
the Hamiltonian of many-body systems. To describe states of
N
particles, consider the tensor product of the single-particle Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
,
i.e.,
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}
. For indistinguishable
particles, states are restricted to the bosonic
or fermionic subspaces of
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}
, denoted as
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta}

with
\zeta=+1
for spinless bosons and
\zeta=-1
for spinless
fermions. We define the projector
\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}

onto
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta}
as
(
Negele and Orland 1998
)

\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}\psi(x_{1},\,x_{2},\,\cdots,\,x_{N})=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\sigma}\zeta^{\sigma}\psi(x_{\sigma_{1}},\,x_{\sigma_{2}},\,\cdots,\,x_{\sigma_{N}})
,
where
\sigma
denotes a permutation of the tuple
(1,\,2,\,\dots,\,N)
. Projecting
I_{2}
onto the subspace
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta}

and using

M_{ij}\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}=\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}M_{ij}=\zeta\mathcal{P}_{\zeta},

(2)

we obtain
\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}I_{2}\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}/(2m)=\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}H_{0}\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}
,
where
H_{0}
is the translation-invariant quantum many-body Hamiltonian
defined as follows

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{p_{i}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{1}{m}\left[\sum_{i<j}(\zeta\hbar V^{\prime}_{ij}+V_{ij}^{2})-\sum_{i<j<k}V_{ijk}\right],

(3)

where
V_{ijk}=V_{ij}V_{jk}+V_{jk}V_{ki}+V_{ki}V_{ij}
is fully symmetric
and a prime denotes the spatial derivative. The form of
H_{0}

will play an important role in proving the integrability of the family
of Hamiltonians generated by EOF and PHJ. Specific choices of the
prepotential function
V(x)
gives rise to well-known models. For

V(x)=\lambda/x
,
V_{ijk}
vanishes by permutation symmetry and
one recovers the Hamiltonian of identical particles with inverse-square
interactions
Calogero 1971
;
Sutherland 2004
. For
V(x)=\lambda\cot(ax)
,
V_{ijk}
is constant
and
H_{0}
involves the inverse sine square potentials. The case

V(x)=c\text{sgn}(x)
, corresponding
V_{ijk}
being a negative
constant, gives rise to the celebrated Lieb-Liniger (LL) model
(
Lieb and Liniger 1963
;
Lieb 1963
)

describing ultracold gases in tight-waveguides
(
Olshanii 1998
;
Cazalilla et al. 2011
)
.
For all these cases where
V_{ijk}
vanishes or is constant,
I_{n}

commute with each other. As the system Hamiltonian coincides with

I_{2}
on the bosonic or fermionic sector, the set of
I_{n}
can
be identified as invariants of motion, i.e.,
[I_{n},I_{m}]=0
. We
note that all the models that have been shown to be integrable by
the EOF in Ref.
(
Polychronakos 1992
)
happen to have a ground-state
wave function of Jastrow form, which we discuss next.

Parent Hamiltonians with Jastrow ground-state.—
Consider
a homogenous one-dimensional many-body quantum system described by
a ground state of Jastrow form
(
Bijl 1940
;
Dingle 1949
;
Jastrow 1955
)
,

\Phi_{0}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=\prod_{i<j}f_{ij},

(4)

this is, the pairwise product of the pair function
f_{ij}=f(x_{i}-x_{j})

(
Sutherland 2004
)
.
In the “beautiful models”
(
Sutherland 2004
)
that concern us here, quantum statistics
is encoded in the symmetry of
f(x)
which is an even function for
bosons and odd for fermions, i.e., without resorting to the use of
permanents or determinants. The case of one dimensional anyons can
similarly be taken into account by including a phase factor
\theta
, i.e.,

f(x)=e^{-i\theta}f(-x)

(
Kundu 1999
;
Girardeau 2006
;
Batchelor et al. 2006
)
.
The complete family of PHJ of ground-state of the Jastrow form (
4
)
has been identified in one spatial dimension
(
del Campo 2020
)

and includes paradigmatic models such as the LL gas with contact interactions

(
Lieb and Liniger 1963
;
Lieb 1963
)
and the rational Calogero-Sutherland model with
inverse-square interactions
(
Calogero 1971
;
Sutherland 1971a
)
, as well
as the recently introduced long-range LL model
(
Beau et al. 2020
)
.
For a given choice of
f
, the parent Hamiltonian
H_{0}
takes
the form

H_{0}=\sum_{i}\frac{p_{i}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{m}\left[\sum_{i<j}\frac{f_{ij}^{\prime\prime}}{f_{ij}}+\sum_{i<j<k}\left(\frac{f_{ij}^{\prime}f_{ik}^{\prime}}{f_{ij}f_{ik}}-\frac{f_{ij}^{\prime}f_{jk}^{\prime}}{f_{ij}f_{jk}}+\frac{f_{ik}^{\prime}f_{jk}^{\prime}}{f_{ik}f_{jk}}\right)\right].

(5)

Here,
f^{\prime}
and
f^{\prime\prime}
denote the first and second spatial derivatives
of
f
, respectively. The explicit expressions for this Hamiltonian
directly follow from evaluating the Laplacian on the Jastrow wave
function (
4
) and recasting all resulting terms in
the form of a Schrödinger equation.

Equivalence of EOF and PHJ for spinless indistinguishable
particles.—
We now establish the correspondence between EOF and
PHJ for spinless identical particles. Comparing the
EOF Hamiltonian and the PHJ in Eqs. (
3
) and (
5
),
the two-body terms are equal if

\hbar^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(x_{ij})/f(x_{ij})=\zeta\hbar V^{\prime}_{ij}+V_{ij}^{2}.
Thus,
the prepotential reads

V_{ij}=\zeta\hbar\frac{d}{dx_{ij}}\log(f_{ij})=\zeta\hbar\frac{f_{ij}^{\prime}}{f_{ij}}.

(6)

Independently of whether the pair function is symmetric or antisymmetric,
its logarithmic derivative is guaranteed to be odd
f_{ij}^{\prime}/f_{ij}=-f_{ji}^{\prime}/f_{ji}
.
Thus, this property holds for spinless bosons and fermions.
The antisymmetry of the prepotential in Eq. (
6
) guarantees the Hermiticity
condition of the associated generalized momenta in the EOF,

\pi_{i}=p_{i}+\text{i}\zeta\hbar\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{f_{ij}^{\prime}}{f_{ij}}M_{ij}.

(7)

The prepotential in Eq. (
6
) further ensures the equivalence of the three-body interaction in the
EOF and the PHJ. Thus,
any spinless system described by EOF, as in Eq. (
3
), has a ground-state
of Jastrow form with a pair function
f_{ij}=\exp[\int^{x_{ij}}dyV(y)/(\zeta\hbar)]
.
Conversely, the complete infinite family of PHJ can be recast in the
EOF provided (
6
) is satisfied. This makes it possible to
identify the class of PHJ that is integrable as we shall see later.

Embedding in an external potential
.
—

The embedding of a system described by EOF in an external potential is known in
the case of a harmonic trap
(
Polychronakos 1992
)
. For the embedding of a homogenous system in an arbitrary
trapping potential, we draw inspiration from
supersymmetric quantum mechanics
(
Cooper et al. 1995
)
and introduce
the one-body superpotential
W_{i}\equiv W(x_{i})
in terms of which
the external trapping potential
U_{i}
will be identified. We
define the operators

a_{i}=\frac{\pi_{i}}{\sqrt{2m}}-\text{i}W_{i},\quad a_{i}^{\dagger}=\frac{\pi_{i}}{\sqrt{2m}}+\text{i}W_{i},

(8)

and the permutation-invariant quantities
\tilde{I}_{n}\equiv\sum_{i}h_{i}^{n}
,
where
h_{i}\equiv a_{i}^{\dagger}a_{i}
. Projecting
\tilde{I}_{1}

onto
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta}
, we find
\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}\tilde{I}_{1}\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}=\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}H\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}
,
where

is the Hamiltonian of the system in the presence of the
trap, i.e.,

H=H_{0}+\sum_{i}U_{i}-\zeta\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\sum_{i<j}V_{ij}(W_{i}-W_{j}),

(9)

with the external potential
U_{i}
being determined by the Riccati equation

U_{i}=W_{i}^{2}-\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m}}W_{i}^{\prime}.

(10)

As a familiar example, when
H_{0}
is the homogeneous Calogero model with inverse-square interactions
Calogero 1971
and
W_{i}=\sqrt{m/2}\omega x_{i}
,
Eq. (
9
) reduces to the rational Calogero-Sutherland model
Sutherland 1971a
;
Sutherland 1971c
including a harmonic trap.

In PHJ, the ground-state wave functions is not limited to the homogeneous
form (
4
), but also includes more general ground-states

\Psi_{0}=\prod_{i<j}f_{ij}\prod_{i}\exp(v_{i})=\Phi_{0}\prod_{i}\exp(v_{i}),

(11)

where the one-body function
v_{i}=v(x_{i})
accounts for the role
of the external potential
U_{i}=U(x_{i})
that breaks translational
invariance
(
del Campo 2020
)
. Specifically, if
H_{0}
is the parent
hamiltonian of
\Phi_{0}
in Eq. (
4

\Psi_{0}

has the parent Hamiltonian

H=H_{0}+\sum_{i}U_{i}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{m}\sum_{i<j}(v_{i}^{\prime}-v_{j}^{\prime})\frac{f^{\prime}_{ij}}{f_{ij}},

(12)

with the one-body local external potential
U_{i}
given in terms
of the function
v_{i}

U_{i}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\left[(v_{i}^{\prime})^{2}+v_{i}^{\prime\prime}\right].

(13)

As a result, the Hamiltonian
H
includes the external potential

and an additional pairwise (two-body) potential which is
generally of long-range character.

The equivalence between EOF and PHJ require that the one-body and potential and the additional long-range term
are equal in both representations.
Comparing Eq. (
10
)
and Eq. (
13

W_{i}
and the function

v_{i}
entering the one-body function of the Jastrow form are related

W_{i}=-\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m}}v_{i}^{\prime}.

(14)

Upon substituting Eq. (
14
) into Eqs. (
9

12

we find that the additional long-range potentials coincide, given the correspondence
Eq. (
6

with the external potential in terms of the prepotential and the superpotential
is

\Psi_{0}=\exp\left(-\frac{\sqrt{2m}}{\hbar}\sum_{i}\int^{x_{i}}W(y)dy\right)\prod_{i<j}\exp\left[\frac{\int^{x_{ij}}dyV(y)}{\zeta\hbar}\right].

Integrability via projection formalism.—

For quantum systems with classical analog, as the PHJ, one can define quantum integrability by promoting the Poisson bracket into commutators in the
definition of classical integrability. Polychronakos
(
Polychronakos 1992
)

pursued along this line and showed that
I_{n}\equiv\sum_{i}\pi_{i}^{n}

become integrals of motion, i.e.,
[I_{n},\,I_{m}]=0,\,\forall n,\,m
,
in the restricted case in which
V_{ijk}
vanishes or is constant.
Having shown that
any spinless model described by EOF is a PHJ with a Jastrow ground state, we next establish the
integrability of the complete family of PHJ models, i.e., without restrictions on the three-body potential
V_{ijk}
or the external potential
U_{i}
.

Note that any physical observable
\mathcal{O}
for spinless

\mathcal{O}(x_{1},\,x_{2},\,\cdots,\,x_{N})=\mathcal{O}(x_{\sigma_{1}},\,x_{\sigma_{2}},\cdots,\,x_{\sigma_{N}})
,
\,\forall

[\mathcal{P}_{\zeta},\,\mathcal{O}]=0,

which can be easily checked by acting on any wave function in

(
Yang and del Campo 2022
)
. Eq. (
16
)
implies the a permutation-invariant observable is block diagonal on

\mathcal{H}_{\zeta}
and its orthogonal complement. We define an observable is local if it only involves derivatives with respect to the coordinates up to a finite order. Then permutation invariance
and locality implies that if a permutation-invariant and local observable
\mathcal{O}

vanishes on
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta}

Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}
. That is

H_{0}
by projecting
I_{2}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta}
.
According to Theorem
1
,
K_{n}
’s and
\tilde{K}_{n}
’s
must commute on the whole Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}
,
respectively. Transitioning to the classical model, where the commutator
is demoted to Poisson brackets, the Poisson brackets of
K_{n}
’s
and
\tilde{K}_{n}
’s must vanish, respectively. Thus, we see that

K_{n}
’s and
\tilde{K}_{n}
’s are also the integrals of motions
for the classical model with Hamiltonians (
3
,
5
)
and (
9
,
12
), respectively.

Discussion.—
It is worth noting that the Jastrow wave
functions
\Phi_{0}
,
\Psi_{0}
may not be the true ground state
of the corresponding PHJ if they cannot be properly normalized.
Nevertheless, the family of models generated
by EOF and PHJ is always integrable, regardless of the normalization
of the Jastrow wave function.

For example, if
f_{ij}=\exp(g|x_{ij}|)
,
H_{0}
becomes the well-known
LL model
(
del Campo 2020
)
. However, the Jastrow wave function
is normalizable only when
g<0
, which corresponds to the McGuire
bright soliton
(
McGuire 1964
)
. Therefore
\Phi_{0}
is no longer
the ground state wave function of the repulsive LL model. However,
as we have discussed previously, the integrability of the Hamiltonian
is not affected, so our result reproduces the integrability of the
LL model with the integral of motion being
I_{n}
or
K_{n}
. More
interestingly, upon introducing the external harmonic potential, according
to Eq. (
11
),
\Psi_{0}
becomes normalizable even
if
\Phi_{0}
is not and Eq. (
5
) corresponds to the Lieb-Liniger-Coulomb
model introduced in Refs.
(
Beau et al. 2020
)
, i.e.,

H=\sum_{i}\left[\frac{p_{i}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}x_{i}^{2}\right]+g\sum_{i<j}\left[\frac{2\hbar^{2}}{m}\delta(x_{ij})-\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}|x_{ij}|\right],

(19)

with ground state
E_{0}=\frac{N\hbar\omega}{2}-\frac{g^{2}\hbar^{2}}{m}\frac{N(N^{2}-1)}{6}
. This system describes harmonically confined bosons subject to contact and Coulomb interactions or gravitational attraction in one spatial dimension.
Ref.
(
Beau et al. 2020
)
characterized its EOF representation and ground state properties. Using Theorem
2
, we conclude that this system is integrable, with the integrals of motion being
\tilde{I}_{n}\equiv\sum_{i}h_{i}^{n}
.

Further physical examples of integrable PHJ systems are provided in the Supplemental Material
Yang and del Campo 2022
, which includes Refs.
Calogero 1975
;
Beau 2017
. The proof leading to the integrability of PHJ essentially takes advantage of the permutation invariance and EOF. As a result it can be applied to models defined on the real line as well as those embedded in an external potential. Likewise, it holds for systems with hard-wall confinement or a ring geometry, provided the pair function
f_{ij}
and the one body potential
v_{i}
or
W_{i}
fulfill the corresponding boundary conditions.

Conclusion.—
We have established the equivalence
between the families of one-dimensional many-body quantum systems
generated by the exchange operator formalism and parent Hamiltonians with a ground-state wavefunction of Jastrow form, describing indistinguishable particles with
no internal degrees of freedom.
Making use of
the projection operator onto the spinless bosonic or fermionic subspace,
we have proved the integrability of all these systems
by constructing explicitly
the corresponding integrals of motion.
Embedding these translation-invariant models in an external
potential preserves the integrability, in the presence of long-range interactions,
as we have illustrated in the long-range Lieb-Liniger model and related systems.

These findings advance the study of many-body physics by uncovering the implications of ground-state correlations on integrability. They
should lead to manifold applications in
the study of quantum solitons, quantum quenches and the thermalization of isolated integrable systems (governed by integrals of motion), and strongly-correlated regimes, generalizing the super-Tonks-Girardeau gas
Astrakharchik et al. 2005
, among others.
Our results bear also implications on numerical methods for strongly-correlated systems such as variational methods and quantum Monte Carlo algorithms, in which the ubiquitous use of Jastrow trial wavefunctions may impose integrability on systems lacking it.
It may be possible to extend our results to higher spatial dimensions
(
Beau and del
Campo 2021
)
, higher-order correlations
Carleo et al. 2017
,
the inclusion of spin degrees of freedom
(
Polychronakos 2006
)
,
mixtures of different species
(
Girardeau and Minguzzi 2007
)
, and distinguishable
particles
(
Jain and Khare 1999
;
Pittman et al. 2017
)
.

I.1
Properties of the two-particle position exchange operator
M_{ij}

Consider the
N

\mathcal{H}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}
,
where we do not assume a particular exchange statistics for the particles.
This means that the particles may be distinguishable or
not. The position exchange operator
M_{ij}
acting on the spatial coordinates
x_{i}

and
x_{j}
on a many-particle state
\psi_{s}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}

M_{ij}\psi_{s}(x_{1},\cdots x_{i},\,\cdots,\,x_{j},\,\cdots x_{N})=\psi_{s}(x_{1},\cdots x_{j},\,\cdots,\,x_{i},\,\cdots x_{N}),\,i\neq j,

(S1)

\psi_{s}(x_{1},\cdots x_{i},\,\cdots,\,x_{j},\,\cdots x_{N})

\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}
and
s

M_{ji}=M_{ij}.

(S2)

M_{ij}^{2}\psi_{s}(x_{1},\cdots x_{i},\,\cdots,\,x_{j},\,\cdots x_{N})=\psi_{s}(x_{1},\cdots x_{i},\,\cdots,\,x_{j},\,\cdots x_{N}),

(S3)

which implies that
M_{ij}
is idempotent, i.e.,

M_{ij}^{2}=\mathbb{I}.

(S4)

The inner product on

Explicitly expanding the inner product according to Eq (

\displaystyle\sum_{s}\int\prod_{k=1}^{N}dx_{k}[M_{ij}^{\dagger}\phi_{s}(x_{1},\cdots,\,x_{i},\,\cdots x_{j},\,\cdots x_{N})]^{*}\psi_{s}(x_{1},\cdots x_{i},\,\cdots,\,x_{j},\,\cdots x_{N})

\displaystyle\sum_{s}\int\prod_{k=1}^{N}dx_{k}\phi_{s}^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,\,x_{i},\,\cdots x_{j},\,\cdots x_{N})\psi_{s}(x_{1},\cdots x_{j},\,\cdots,\,x_{i},\,\cdots x_{N})

\displaystyle\sum_{s}\int\prod_{k=1}^{N}dx_{k}\phi_{s}^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,\,x_{j},\,\cdots x_{i},\,\cdots x_{N})M_{ij}\psi_{s}(x_{1},\cdots x_{i},\,\cdots,\,x_{j},\,\cdots x_{N}),

(S6)

which indicates that

M_{ij}^{\dagger}\phi_{s}(x_{1},\cdots x_{i},\,\cdots,\,x_{j},\,\cdots x_{N})=\phi_{s}(x_{1},\cdots x_{j},\,\cdots,\,x_{i},\,\cdots x_{N}).

(S7)

Comparing above equation with Eq. (
S1
), we conclude that
M_{ij}
is a Hermitian operator,

M_{ij}=M_{ij}^{\dagger}.

(S8)

We define operators or observables

\displaystyle A_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle A(x_{i}),

(S9)

\displaystyle A_{ij}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle A(x_{i},\,x_{j}),

(S10)

\displaystyle A_{ijk}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle A(x_{i},\,x_{j},\,x_{k}),

\displaystyle\sum_{s}\int\prod_{l=1}^{N}dx_{l}\phi_{s}^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,\,x_{j},\,\cdots x_{i},\,\cdots x_{N})\mathcal{O}(x_{k})\psi_{s}(x_{1},\cdots x_{i},\,\cdots,\,x_{j},\,\cdots x_{N})

\displaystyle(\phi,\,\mathcal{O}_{k}M_{ji})

(S13)

We conclude that

M_{ij}A_{k}=A_{k}M_{ji}=A_{k}M_{ij},\quad\text{for }\,i,\,j,\,k,\,\text{distinct.}

(S14)

If

\displaystyle(\phi,\,M_{ij}A_{i}\psi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\int\prod_{l=1}^{N}dx_{l}\phi_{s}^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,\,x_{i},\,\cdots x_{j},\,\cdots x_{N})A(x_{j})M_{ij}\psi_{s}(x_{1},\cdots x_{i},\,\cdots,\,x_{j},\,\cdots x_{N})

where we have used change of the dummy indices

(S17)

\displaystyle M_{ij}A_{ji}

II
Projection onto the bosonic and fermionic

In EOF, one may restrict the construction to the bosonic or fermionic subspace to simplify

(
Polychronakos 1992

Polychronakos 2006

that if the prepotential is

\frac{1}{2}I_{2}=\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{i}\pi_{i}^{2}=\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{m}\sum_{i>j}\frac{l(l-M_{ij})}{(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}}.

(S29)

This Hamiltonian is still defined on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}
. Recall
that the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta}
of identical particles
is a subspace of
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}

operator on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}
to
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta}

\displaystyle M_{ij}\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}\psi(x_{1},\,x_{2},\,\cdots,\,x_{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\sigma}(\pm 1)^{\sigma}M_{ij}\psi(x_{\sigma_{1}},\dots\,x_{\sigma_{i}},\cdots x_{\sigma_{j}}\,\cdots,\,x_{\sigma_{N}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\sigma}(\pm 1)^{\sigma+1}\psi(x_{\sigma_{1}},\dots\,x_{\sigma_{i}},\cdots x_{\sigma_{j}}\,\cdots,\,x_{\sigma_{N}})

\displaystyle=\pm\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}\psi(x_{1},\,x_{2},\,\cdots,\,x_{N}),

(S30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}M_{ij}\psi(x_{1},\,x_{2},\,\cdots,\,x_{N})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}\psi(x_{1},\,\cdots x_{j},\,\cdots x_{i},\cdots\,x_{N})

\displaystyle=(\pm 1)\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}\psi(x_{1},\,\cdots x_{i},\,\cdots x_{j},\cdots\,x_{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\sigma}(\pm 1)^{\sigma+1}\psi(x_{\sigma_{1}},\dots\,x_{\sigma_{i}},\cdots x_{\sigma_{j}}\,\cdots,\,x_{\sigma_{N}})

\displaystyle=\pm\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}\psi(x_{1},\,x_{2},\,\cdots,\,x_{N}).

(S31)

Thus, we conclude

(S32)

\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}M_{ij}=M_{ij}\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}=\zeta\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}.

(S33)

(S35)

However, we warn the audience that when focusing on spinless bosons

For example, when calculating

(S36)

On the other hand, a rigorous calculation following Eqs. (

\displaystyle[p_{i},\,\sum_{k\neq j}V_{jk}M_{jk}]

\displaystyle=[p_{j},\,V_{ji}M_{ji}]=[p_{i},\,V_{ji}]M_{ji}+V_{ji}[p_{i},\,M_{ji}]

\displaystyle=\text{i}\hbar V_{ij}^{\prime}M_{ij}-V_{ij}(p_{i}-p_{j})M_{ij},\,(i\neq j)

Upon projecting this relation onto
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}

which yields

(S38)

This example clearly shows that

The caveat we would like to give to the audience is that whenever

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m}I_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{i}\left[p_{i}^{2}+\sum_{j\neq i}(\hbar V_{ij}^{\prime}M_{ij}+V_{ij}^{2})-\sum_{i\neq j\neq k\neq i}V_{ij}V_{ki}M_{ijk}\right],

(S43)

where in the last step we have swapped the indices

and

and

is antisymmetric in its indices, and that

is the three-particle exchange operator defined in Eq. (

,

\sum_{i\neq j\neq k\neq i}V_{ij}V_{ki}M_{ijk}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{j\neq k\neq i}(V_{ij}V_{jk}+V_{jk}V_{ki}+V_{ki}V_{ij})M_{ijk}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{j\neq k\neq i}V_{ijk}M_{ijk},

IV.1

For any permutation-invariant observable

[\mathcal{P}_{\zeta},\,\mathcal{O}]=0.

IV.2

Proposition
.

A permutation-invariant observable is block diagonal on
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta}

and
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta}^{\perp}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta}^{\perp}

is the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{H}_{\zeta}

The operator

-body operators, even if it involves

\displaystyle M_{ij}W_{j}

\displaystyle=\text{i}\sum_{k\neq j}M_{ij}V_{jk}M_{jk}=\text{i}\sum_{k\neq i,\,j}M_{ij}V_{jk}M_{jk}+\text{i}M_{ij}V_{ji}M_{ji}

\displaystyle M_{ij}W_{k}

\displaystyle=\text{i}\sum_{l\neq k}V_{kl}M_{kl}M_{ij}=W_{k}M_{ij}.

Sutherland model in a trigonometric trap

To the best of our knowledge, the possibility of embedding a periodic potential for the Sutherland
model has not been yet discussed in the literature. We take this step
with the PHJ-EOF approach. We shall employ Eq. (
S63
)
and take
f(x)
to be Eq. (
S90

v(x)=-\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\frac{L}{\pi}\right)^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right),

(S116)

which yields

U(x)=-\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi x}{L}\right)-\frac{m\omega^{2}L^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\cos\left(\frac{4\pi x}{L}\right)+\frac{m\omega^{2}L^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}.

(S117)

We have computed before
f^{\prime}(x)/f(x)
in Eq. (
S93
).
According to Eq. (
S63
), we find

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{p_{i}^{2}}{2m}-\sum_{i}\left[\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi x_{i}}{L}\right)+\frac{m\omega^{2}L^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\cos\left(\frac{4\pi x_{i}}{L}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{m}\sum_{i<j}\left[\left(\frac{\pi}{L}\right)^{2}\frac{\lambda(\lambda-1)}{\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi x_{ij}}{L}\right)}-\frac{\lambda m\omega}{\hbar}\cos\left(\frac{\pi x_{ij}}{L}\right)\cos\left(\frac{2\pi\bar{x}_{ij}}{L}\right)\right]-E_{0},

(S118)

where

E_{0}=\left(\frac{\pi}{L}\right)^{2}\frac{\lambda^{2}\hbar^{2}N(N^{2}-1)}{6m}-\frac{Nm\omega^{2}L^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}.

(S119)

In the thermodynamic limit
L,\,N\to\infty
with

kept fixed,
so that one can readily check Eq. (
S118

reduces to the Calogero model in a harmonic trap. Thus Eq. (

V.3
Fixing interactions first: Toda-like interactions in the continuum

The above examples have been found in the PHJ-EOF family by choosing the pair function
f(x)
.
As an alternative, one can twist the construction around, by fixing the interactions first.
For the sake of illustration, let us consider a two-body potential that decays exponentially with the interparticle distance over a length scale
\ell
, e.g.,

\displaystyle V_{2}=\frac{\hbar^{2}g}{m}\sum_{i<j}e^{-|x_{ij}|/\ell}.

(S120)

This potential is a generalization to the continuum of the Toda interactions and can also be considered as a low-density approximation to the hyperbolic potential, e.g., as discussed in
Sutherland 2004
;
del Campo 2020
.
Thus, we expect

\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(x)}{f(x)}=ge^{-|x|/\ell}+\text{singular terms}.

(S121)

Ignoring the singular terms for the moment, this differential equation for
x>0
admits as a specific solution

f(x)=I_{0}\left(2\sqrt{g}le^{-x/\ell}\right),

(S122)

where
I_{\alpha}(x)
is the modified Bessel functions of first kind and order
\alpha
. This solution motivates the choice

f(x)=I_{0}\left(2\sqrt{g}le^{-|x|/\ell}\right)

(S123)

for all
x
.
This pair function decays smoothly as function of
x
to unit value. To compute
f^{\prime\prime}(x)/f(x)
, care must be taken for the absolute value. Using Eq. (
S81
), we can readily calculate

\displaystyle\frac{dI_{0}\left(2\sqrt{g}le^{-|x|/\ell}\right)}{dx}

\displaystyle=\frac{dI_{0}\left(2\sqrt{g}le^{-|x|/\ell}\right)}{d|x|}\text{sgn}(x)

\displaystyle=-2e^{-|x|/\ell}\sqrt{g}I_{1}\left(2\sqrt{g}le^{-|x|/l}\right)\text{sgn}(x),

(S124)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}I_{0}\left(2\sqrt{g}le^{-|x|/\ell}\right)}{dx^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{2}I_{0}\left(2\sqrt{g}le^{-|x|/\ell}\right)}{d^{2}|x|}+\frac{dI_{0}\left(2\sqrt{g}le^{-|x|/\ell}\right)}{d|x|}\delta(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{2}I_{0}\left(2\sqrt{g}le^{-|x|/\ell}\right)}{d^{2}|x|}-2e^{-|x|/l}\sqrt{g}I_{1}\left(2\sqrt{g}le^{-|x|/\ell}\right)\delta(x).

(S125)

Thus we find

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}

\displaystyle=-2\sqrt{g}e^{-|x|/\ell}\frac{I_{1}\left(2\sqrt{g}le^{-|x|/\ell}\right)}{I_{0}\left(2\sqrt{g}le^{-|x|/\ell}\right)}\text{sgn}(x),

(S126)

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(x)}{f(x)}

\displaystyle=ge^{-|x|/\ell}-c\delta(x),

(S127)

where

c=\frac{2\sqrt{g}I_{1}\left(2\sqrt{g}\ell\right)}{I_{0}\left(2\sqrt{g}\ell\right)}.

(S129)

The corresponding three-body potential is nonzero, as expected, and takes the form given in Eq. (
S63
) with
f^{\prime}(x)/f(x)
given by Eq. (
S126
).
The expression of
f^{\prime}(x)/f(x)
sets the generalized momenta
\pi_{i}
through Eq. (
7
) in the main text and the corresponding integrals of motion
I_{n}
.
The resulting Hamiltonian thus takes the form

\displaystyle H=\sum_{i}\frac{p_{i}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{m}\sum_{i<j}\left[ge^{-|x_{ij}|/\ell}-c\delta(x_{ij})\right]+V_{3}.

(S130)

Given
I(0)=1
, i.e., the wave function does not decay when particles are far apart, the Jastrow wave function is not normalizable in the in absence of an external trap. We thus consider the case where system is trapped. The ground state of the trapped system is then

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}=e^{-\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{2}}\prod_{i<j}I_{0}[2\ell\sqrt{g}\exp(-|x_{ij}|/\ell)].

(S131)

The long-range two-body potential due to the embedding of the external harmonic trap in Eq. (
S63
) is similarly given in terms of the modified Bessel functions.

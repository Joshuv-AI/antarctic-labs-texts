Complete ( 2 + 1 ) (2+1) -dimensional Ricci flow spacetimes
Luke Thomas Peachey

Abstract
Ricci flow spacetimes were introduced by Kleiner & Lott as a way to describe Ricci flow through singularities, and have since been used elsewhere in the literature, prompting the question of their rigidity. In ( 2 + 1 ) (2+1) -dimensions, we show that every complete and sufficiently regular spacetime must be a cylindrical spacetime. That is, if the metric is complete on each spatial slice, after imposing a necessary continuity condition, we can conclude that every spatial slice must be diffeomorphic to a fixed surface, and the Ricci flow spacetime is isometric to a classical Ricci flow on this surface.

1.3
Outline of the paper

In Section
2
, we use a Harnack estimate to show that spacetimes with connected spatial slices are expanding. We then decompose any connected spacetime into the union of such spacetimes to show Theorem
1.11
.

In Section
3
, we show that expanding spacetimes can be embedded into a larger ambient space. We then use the ambient space to define a global conformal structure on our spacetime, allowing us to significantly simplify Theorem
1.16
.

In Section
4
, we further reduce Theorem
1.16
to complete and conformal Ricci flows on spacetimes within the unit disk. We then formulate a geometric condition equivalent to continuity for such spacetimes, which we use to then prove a comparison principle.

In Section
5
, we introduce the idea of an initial time blow-up. Taking any non-atomic Radon measure on a Riemann surface, we can start the Ricci flow from this measure
[
TY21
]
. By looking at larger and larger parabolic rescalings of this flow away from the support of the initial measure, in the limit at time zero, we have a hyperbolic metric. We combine this with the comparison principle from Section

to show Theorem

(3)

Recall that in dimension

, Ricci flow preserves the conformal class of the metric. Within a Ricci flow spacetime
(\mathcal{M}^{2+1},\mathfrak{t},\partial_{t},g)
, consider cylindrical coordinates

\Psi_{*}(g)=u(dx^{2}+dy^{2})\quad\textnormal{on }\Psi(U(I)),

(4)

where

is a smooth function satisfying the logarithmic fast diffusion equation (LFDE)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta\log u.

(5)

Working locally, we can combine the lower scalar curvature bound with the LFDE to give the following simple Harnack estimate.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a smooth manifold
M
, a subset
\Gamma\subseteq M
is a

in

\gamma:J\rightarrow M
, for some interval

.

\Gamma=\{\gamma(s):s\in J\}.

If we can take

is a

.

Lemma 2.3

(Simple Harnack estimate)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{2+1},\mathfrak{t},\partial_{t},g)
be a complete Ricci flow spacetime with
I=(0,T)
. For any
x_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{t_{0}}
, choose
r>0
sufficiently small such that the ball
B:=B(x_{0},r)\Subset\mathcal{M}_{t_{0}}
admits isothermal coordinates. Let
\Gamma
be a smooth arc in
B
. Then

\ell_{g}(\Gamma(t))\leq\sqrt{\frac{t-\mathfrak{h}(x_{0})}{t_{0}-\mathfrak{h}(x_{0})}}\cdot\ell_{g}(\Gamma),\quad\forall t\in[t_{0},T),

where
\ell_{g}(\Gamma(t))
denotes the length of
\Gamma(t)\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{t}
with respect to the metric
g(t)
. If
\mathcal{M}
is initially determined, this simplifies to

\ell_{g}(\Gamma(t))\leq\sqrt{\frac{t}{t_{0}}}\cdot\ell_{g}(\Gamma),\quad\forall t\in[t_{0},T).

Proof.

As we did above, there exist cylindrical coordinates
\Psi:B(I)\subseteq\mathcal{M}\rightarrow B\times I
, so that
\Psi_{*}(g)
satisfies (
4
) for some
u:\Psi(B(I))\rightarrow(0,\infty)
solving the LFDE (

).
Since

\Delta\log u=-u\cdot R_{\Psi_{*}g}=-u\cdot\Psi_{*}(R_{g}),

we can use Corollary
2.1
to bound the time derivative of our conformal factor

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(z,t)=-u(z,t)\cdot R_{g}(\Psi^{-1}(z,t))\leq\frac{u(z,t)}{t-\mathfrak{h}\circ\Psi^{-1}(z,t)}=\frac{u(z,t)}{t-\mathfrak{h}(x_{0})},\quad\forall(z,t)\in\Psi(B(I)).

Note that
(z,t)\in\Psi(B(I))
iff
[t_{0},t]\subseteq I_{z}
. So we can integrate the above to get

u(z,t)\leq\left(\frac{t-\mathfrak{h}(x_{0})}{t_{0}-\mathfrak{h}(x_{0})}\right)u(z,t_{0}),\quad\forall(z,t)\in\Psi(B(I)).

Let
\Gamma
be the image of the smooth map
\gamma:J\rightarrow B
. Recall, the set
\Gamma(t)
denotes the collection of points
\{x(t)\in\mathcal{M}_{t}:x\in\Gamma,t\in I_{x}\}
(see Definition
1.9
). We shall consider those points in
\Gamma
which persist until time

,

[\Gamma(t)](s)=\{x\in\Gamma:t\in I_{x}\}\subseteq\Gamma,

and let

As
[\Gamma(t)](s)
is open in
\Gamma
,
J_{t}
is open in
J
. Since
\Psi(\Gamma(t))=[\Gamma(t)](s)\times\{t\}
, we have

\ell_{g}(\Gamma(t))=\ell_{\Psi_{*}(g)}([\Gamma(t)](s)\times\{t\})=\int_{J_{t}}u(\gamma(s),t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lvert\gamma^{\prime}(s)\rvert\ ds,

where
\lvert\cdot\rvert
is the size of a vector with respect to our local isothermal coordinates. In particular

\displaystyle\ell_{g}(\Gamma(t))

\displaystyle=\int_{J_{t}}u(\gamma(s),t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lvert\gamma^{\prime}(s)\rvert\ ds

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{t-\mathfrak{h}(x_{0})}{t_{0}-\mathfrak{h}(x_{0})}}\cdot\int_{J_{t}}u(\gamma(s),t_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lvert\gamma^{\prime}(s)\rvert\ ds

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{t-\mathfrak{h}(x_{0})}{t_{0}-\mathfrak{h}(x_{0})}}\cdot\int_{J}u(\gamma(s),t_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lvert\gamma^{\prime}(s)\rvert\ ds

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{t-\mathfrak{h}(x_{0})}{t_{0}-\mathfrak{h}(x_{0})}}\cdot\ell_{g}(\Gamma).\qed

Remark

In higher dimensions, the same reasoning applied to the evolution equation for the volume form

gives an analogous inequality, and hence a local upper bound on volume growth.

By piecing together the above lemma locally, we have the same result for any smooth arc within a spatial slice.

Lemma 2.4

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{2+1},\mathfrak{t},\partial_{t},g)

. Suppose
\Gamma

\ell_{g}(\Gamma(t))\leq\sqrt{\frac{t-\mathfrak{h}(x_{0})}{t_{0}-\mathfrak{h}(x_{0})}}\cdot\ell_{g}(\Gamma),\quad\forall t\in[t_{0},T).

\Gamma(t)

Proof.

We aim to apply the Harnack estimate to small balls covering

. Let
\Gamma

2.2
Vanishing times are locally constant

Let
\Gamma\Subset\mathcal{M}_{s}
be a compact smooth arc with the spatial slice at time
s\in I
. Recall, in Definition

, we defined the interval
I_{\Gamma}=\cap_{x\in\Gamma}I_{x}
. Since
\Gamma
is compactly contained in

by Lemma

, be the vanishing time of the arc. The following lemma shows that along a compact smooth arc

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{2+1},\mathfrak{t},\partial_{t},g)

. Fix

. Let
\Gamma
be the image of the smooth map
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{s}

\Gamma
which persist past the vanishing time of
\Gamma

Let

In particular, by the Harnack estimate (

2.3
Spatially-connected spacetimes are expanding

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{t},\partial_{t},g)
be a connected Ricci flow spacetime. We say that
\mathcal{M}
is spatially-connected if the time slices
\mathcal{M}_{t}
are connected, for all
t\in I
.

Under the additional assumption that
\mathcal{M}
is spatially-connected, we have a well-defined vanishing time for each spatial slice. If our spacetime was not expanding, then
\mathcal{M}_{s}(t)=\emptyset
for some
s<t\in I
. We can now use this to contradict our assumption that our spacetime is connected.

Theorem 2.8

(Complete, spatially-connected spacetimes are expanding)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{2+1},\mathfrak{t},\partial_{t},g)
be a complete, spatially-connected Ricci flow spacetime with
I=(0,T)
. Then it is expanding. That is, the vanishing times

T_{x}=T,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{M}.

Proof.

By Corollary
2.6
, each spatial slice
\mathcal{M}_{s}
has extinction time
T_{s}\in(s,T]
for each
s\in(0,T)
. That is,
T_{x}=T_{s}
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{s}
. If
T_{s}<T
for some
s\in(0,T)
, then
T_{t}=T_{s}
for all
t\in(s,T_{s})
. Choosing any point
x\in\mathcal{M}_{T_{s}}
, we can pick
\delta>0
sufficiently small so that
T_{s}\pm\delta\in I_{x}
. This leads to the obvious contradiction

T_{s}+\delta\leq T_{x}=T_{x(T_{s}-\delta)}=T_{s}.\qed

2.4
Decomposing a spacetime

Finally, we show that any complete Ricci flow spacetime can be decomposed into a collection of complete and spatially-connected Ricci flow spacetimes.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{n+1},\mathfrak{t},\partial_{t},g)
be a Ricci flow spacetime and let
\eta:J\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
be a time-preserving path. Define
\mathcal{M}^{\eta}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
by setting
\mathcal{M}^{\eta}_{t}
to be the connected component of
\mathcal{M}_{t}
containing
\eta(t)
, for each
t\in J
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{n+1},\mathfrak{t},\partial_{t},g)
be a complete Ricci flow spacetime with
I=(0,T)
. Fix
(t_{0},t_{1})\subseteq(0,T)
. If

\partial_{t}
and

to
\mathcal{M}^{\eta}
is a complete and spatially-connected Ricci flow spacetime.

Proof.

In order to show that
(\mathcal{M}^{\eta},\mathfrak{t},\partial_{t},g)
is a Ricci flow spacetime, it suffices to prove that
\mathcal{M}^{\eta}
is an open subset of
\mathcal{M}
. Fix
s\in(t_{0},t_{1})
and
x\in\mathcal{M}^{\eta}_{s}
. Since
g(s)
is complete,
B:=B(x,1)\Subset\mathcal{M}^{\eta}_{s}
. Since
\mathcal{M}^{\eta}_{s}
is path-connected, there exists a continuous path
\rho:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\eta}_{s}
from

to
\eta(s)=\rho(1)
. Using the continuity of
\eta
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

for any

By compactness, after possibly shrinking

\mathcal{M}^{\eta}

\mathcal{M}

\mathcal{M}^{\eta}_{t}

3
Embedding spacetimes within an ambient space

As we shall see later, an ambient space for a Ricci flow spacetime can be a useful tool. In the case that our Ricci flow spacetime is expanding, we can define a map which looks like a global cylindrical coordinate chart. This will give an embedding (up to some non-final time) of our spacetime into an ambient space.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{n+1},\mathfrak{t},\partial_{t},g)
be an expanding Ricci flow spacetime with
I=(0,T)
. For each
\tau\in(0,T)
, there exists a smooth map

\Phi:\mathcal{M}_{(0,\tau)}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\tau}\times(0,\tau),

such that

(i)

\Phi
is a diffeomorphism onto its image.

(ii)

For each

is a diffeomorphism onto its image.

(iii)

If

denotes the standard projection, then
\mathfrak{t}=t\circ\Phi
and
\Phi_{*}(\partial_{t})=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
.

(iv)

\Phi_{*}(g)
is a solution to the usual Ricci flow equation (
1
) on
\Phi(\mathcal{M}_{(0,\tau)})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is expanding, for any

\mathcal{M}_{s}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\tau},\quad x\mapsto x(\tau).

Define the map

\Phi(x):=(x(\tau),\mathfrak{t}(x)).

\Phi

(p,t)\mapsto p(t)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Phi_{*}(g)=\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{*}(\partial_{t})}\Phi_{*}(g)=\Phi_{*}(\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{t}}g)=\Phi_{*}(-2\Ric(g))=-2\Ric(\Phi_{*}(g)).\qed

3.3
Lifting a spacetime

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{2+1},g)

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{2+1},g)

4.1
A comparison principle

From now on, we may assume that
(\mathcal{M}^{2+1},g)
is a complete, connected, continuous and initially determined spacetime in

with

, and that the Ricci flow is conformal with respect to the standard conformal structure on

in the form

Using the conformal structure on each spatial slice, we see that each slice admits a unique complete hyperbolic metric

(by Lemma

I=(0,T)
. A function
f:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
parabolically proper
if

\mathcal{M}_{(\epsilon,T-\epsilon)}\cap f^{-1}(I)\Subset\mathcal{M},\quad\forall I\Subset\mathbb{R},\ \forall\epsilon>0.

The following lemma shows that, for a complete spacetime in the disk, the conformal factors
H
being a parabolically proper map is an equivalent way to characterise our spacetime being continuous. In particular, due to equation (
10
),
H
is an appropriate lower bound to take.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{2+1},g)
be a complete Ricci flow spacetime in
D\times(0,T)
, and let
H:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be the conformal factors of the complete hyperbolic metrics
h(t)
on
\mathcal{M}
. Then
\mathcal{M}
is continuous iff
H:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is parabolically proper (see Definition
4.3
).

Proof.

If
\mathcal{M}
is not continuous, there exists
s\in(0,T)
and a point
p\in\left(\bigcap_{t>s}{\mathcal{M}_{s}}\right)^{\circ}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{s}
. In particular, with respect to the background metric
|dz|^{2}
, there exists
r>0
such that the ball
B(p,r)\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{t}
, for every
t>s
. By the Schwarz lemma
C.2
,
H(p,t)\leq\frac{4}{r^{2}}
, for every
t>s
. That is, for any sequence of times
t_{n}\searrow s
, and for any
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, we have the sequence of points
(p,t_{n})\in\mathcal{M}_{(\epsilon,T-\epsilon)}
such that
(p,t_{n})\rightarrow(p,s)\in\partial\mathcal{M}
, but
H(p,t_{n})
is uniformly bounded for all
n
. That is,
H
isn’t parabolically proper.

Conversely, suppose
\mathcal{M}
is continuous and fix
\epsilon>0
. Let
(z_{n},t_{n})\in\mathcal{M}_{(\epsilon,T-\epsilon)}
be any sequence such that
(z_{n},t_{n})\rightarrow(z,s)\in\partial\mathcal{M}
. If we can show that the sequence
H(z_{n},t_{n})
diverges then we are done. Passing to a subsequence, we may assume that the sequence of times
t_{n}
is monotone. Suppose
t_{n}\nearrow s
. As
\mathcal{M}
is expanding,
z_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{s}
with
H(z_{n},s)\leq H(z_{n},t_{n})
for every
n
. In particular,
z_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{s}
with
z_{n}\rightarrow z\in\partial\mathcal{M}_{s}
. As
H(\cdot,s):\mathcal{M}_{s}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is a proper map,
H(z_{n},s)
is unbounded, and hence so is
H(z_{n},t_{n})
. Instead we assume that
t_{n}\searrow s
. For any fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
, using that
\mathcal{M}
is expanding, we have that
z_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{t_{m}}
with
H(z_{n},t_{m})\leq H(z_{n},t_{n})
for any
n\geq m
. So, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have a sequence
z_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{t_{m}}
with
z_{n}\rightarrow z
. If
z\in\partial\mathcal{M}_{t_{m}}
, then using again that
H(\cdot,t_{m}):\mathcal{M}_{t_{m}}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is a proper map, we would deduce that
H(z_{n},t_{m})
diverges, so
H(z_{n},t_{n})
diverges. This leads us to assume that
z\in\mathcal{M}_{t_{m}}
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By the continuity of each
H(\cdot,t_{m}):\mathcal{M}_{t_{m}}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
, we have

H(z,t_{m})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}H(z_{n},t_{m})\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}H(z_{n},t_{n}),

and hence

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}H(z,t_{n})\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}H(z_{n},t_{n}).

From the above equation, it suffices to show that
H(z,t_{n})\nearrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Since
\mathcal{M}
is continuous,
z\in\left(\bigcap_{t>s}{\mathcal{M}_{t}}\right)\setminus\mathcal{M}_{s}=\left(\bigcap_{t>s}{\mathcal{M}_{t}}\right)\setminus\left(\bigcap_{t>s}{\mathcal{M}_{t}}\right)^{\circ}
. So there exists
\epsilon_{n}\searrow 0
such that the ball of radius
\epsilon_{n}
centred at
z
is contained in our spatial slice,
B(z,\epsilon_{n})\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{t_{n}}
, but some point in the boundary of the ball
b_{n}\in\partial B(z,\epsilon_{n})
isn’t in our spatial slice
b_{n}\notin\mathcal{M}_{t_{n}}
. Note that all of the spatial slices are contained within the disk and hence at each time
t_{n}
, each spatial slice is contained within the punctured ball of radius
2
centred at
b_{n}

\mathcal{M}_{t_{n}}\subseteq B^{\times}(b_{n},2),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Consider the complete hyperbolic metric on this punctured ball
B^{\times}(b_{n},2)

h_{n}^{\times}(z):=\frac{1}{|z-b_{n}|^{2}(-\log(2|z-b_{n}|))^{2}}|dz|^{2}.

By the Schwarz lemma
C.2
,
h(t_{n})\geq h_{n}^{\times}
on
\mathcal{M}_{t_{n}}
. In particular, we have that

H(z,t_{n})\geq\frac{1}{\epsilon_{n}^{2}(-\log(2\epsilon_{n}))^{2}},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

and thus
H(z,t_{n})\nearrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, concluding the proof.
∎

Using Lemma
4.4
, we now have a comparison principle for solutions to the LFDE on spacetimes in the disk.

Lemma 4.5

(Comparison principle)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{2+1},g)
be a complete, continuous and initially determined spacetime in
D\times(0,T)
. Let
u\in C^{2,1}(\mathcal{M})
be a bounded solution to the LFDE with
u>0
, and
v\in C^{2,1}(\mathcal{M})
be the solution to the LFDE such that
g(t)=v(z,t)\cdot|dz|^{2}
. If for some
s\in(0,T)
we have
v>u
on
\mathcal{M}_{(0,s)}
, then
v\geq u
on
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

Let
C:=\sup_{\mathcal{M}}u<\infty
and
\epsilon\in(0,s)
. Consider the region

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}:=\{(x,t)\in\mathcal{M}:t\in(\epsilon,T-\epsilon),H(x,t)<\frac{C+1}{2\epsilon}\}.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is continuous, by Lemma
4.4

H
is parabolically proper, and hence
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\Subset\mathcal{M}
. We note that
u,v\in C^{2,1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}})
. On the region
\mathcal{M}_{(\epsilon,T-\epsilon)}\setminus\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
, by the Schwarz lemma
C.2
, we have

v(z,t)\geq 2t\cdot H(z,t)\geq 2\epsilon\cdot H(z,t)\geq C+1>u(z,t).

Since
\epsilon\in(0,s)
, we conclude that
v>u
on the parabolic boundary
\partial_{P}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. Applying the usual comparison principle for the LFDE to
u
and
v
on the region
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
(Lemma

), we deduce that
v\geq u
on
\mathcal{M}_{(0,T-\epsilon)}
. To finish the proof take
\epsilon\searrow 0
.
∎

5
Initial time blow-ups

We now aim to find suitable lower barriers for our Ricci flow. In order to do so, we must make a small diversion into Ricci flows on Riemann surfaces starting from measures.

Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in(0,T)}
be a Riemann surface equipped with a complete and conformal Ricci flow. Since the flow is complete, we have the lower scalar curvature bound
R_{g(t)}\geq-\frac{1}{t}
, and hence
t\mapsto\frac{g(t)}{t}
is decreasing. It is then immediate that we have a well-defined (potentially infinite) limiting metric as time approaches zero.

Definition 5.1

(Initial time blow-up)
.

Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in(0,T)}
be a Riemann surface equipped with a complete and conformal Ricci flow. The initial time blow-up of
g(t)
is the (possibly infinite) conformal metric
\widehat{g}
on
M
, defined by

\widehat{g}:=\lim_{t\searrow 0}\frac{g(t)}{2t}.

Remark
.

Suppose
\phi:N\rightarrow M
is a local biholomorphism. Then if
g(t)
is a complete and conformal Ricci flow on
M
with initial time blow-up
\widehat{g}
, we have that
\phi^{*}(g(t))
is a complete and conformal Ricci flow on
N
, and its initial time blow-up is
\widehat{(\phi^{*}g)}=\phi^{*}(\widehat{g})
.

Given a connected Riemann surface
M
, denote the collection of all non-atomic, non-zero Radon measures on
M
by
\mathcal{R}(M)
. Due to the work of Topping & Yin in
[
TY21
]
, these are precisely those measures which can be smoothed out using Ricci flow. To be more precise, given any
\mu\in\mathcal{R}(M)
, there exists a complete and conformal Ricci flow
g(t)
starting weakly from
\mu
(see Theorem
B.1
for the full statement). The following lemma shows that, away from the support of the initial measure, the initial time blow-up is a hyperbolic metric.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
g(t)
be a complete and conformal Ricci flow on
M\times(0,T)
starting weakly from some
\mu\in\mathcal{R}(M)
. Then the initial time blow-up
\widehat{g}
of
g(t)
is a well-defined smooth metric on
M\setminus\supp(\mu)
with constant Gaussian curvature
K_{\widehat{g}}\equiv-1
.

Proof.

Consider the parabolically rescaled Ricci flows
(M,g_{m}(t)_{t\in(0,mT)})
given by

g_{m}(t):=mg(tm^{-1}),\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N},

so that
g_{1}(t)\equiv g(t)
. We note that
g_{m}(t)
is a complete and conformal Ricci flow starting weakly from the measure
m\cdot\mu\in\mathcal{R}(M)
. Given a local complex coordinate
z
on
M
, our metrics are given locally by
g_{m}(t)=u_{m}(z,t)|dz|^{2}
with the conformal factors
u_{m}
satisfying the relation

u_{m}(z,t):=mu_{1}(z,tm^{-1}).

By the monotonicity of
t\mapsto t^{-1}u_{1}(\cdot,t)
, we have that
u_{m}(z,t)
is an increasing sequence in
m
, for any fixed
(z,t)
. In particular, we have the uniform lower bound
u_{1}(z,t)
on our sequence of conformal factors. Moreover, we have local uniform upper bounds on our sequence
u_{m}
in
M\setminus\supp(\mu)
by Lemma
B.3
. Combining the lower and upper bounds with standard parabolic theory, we deduce local
C^{k}
-bounds on our sequence of conformal factors, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Finally, by Arzela-Ascoli, our sequence of Ricci flows
g_{m}(t)
converges locally smoothly to some limiting eternal Ricci flow
(M\setminus\supp{\mu},g_{\infty}(t)_{t\in(0,\infty)})
. For any fixed
t>0
, we note that

g_{\infty}(t):=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}g_{m}(t)=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}2t\cdot\frac{g(tm^{-1})}{2tm^{-1}}=2t\cdot\widehat{g},

and so
g_{\infty}(t)=2t\cdot\widehat{g}
. Substituting this into the Ricci flow equation

2\cdot\widehat{g}=\frac{\partial g_{\infty}}{\partial t}=-2\Ric(g_{\infty})=-2K_{\widehat{g}}\cdot\widehat{g},

and hence
K_{\widehat{g}}\equiv-1
.
∎

Appendix A
Topology of spacetimes

Let
(\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{t},\partial_{t},g)
be a Ricci flow spacetime. Given any point
x\in\mathcal{M}
, we define the set
U_{x}^{-}
to be the collection of all points at earlier times which can be connected to
x
by a time-preserving path:

U_{x}^{-}:=\{y\in\mathcal{M}:\mathfrak{t}(y)<\mathfrak{t}(x),\exists\ \eta:[\mathfrak{t}(y),\mathfrak{t}(x)]\rightarrow\mathcal{M}\textnormal{ time-preserving, with }\eta\circ\mathfrak{t}(y)=y,\eta\circ\mathfrak{t}(x)=x\}.

Similarily, we define
U_{x}^{+}
to be the points at later times connected to
x
by time preserving paths:

U_{x}^{+}:=\{y\in\mathcal{M}:\mathfrak{t}(x)<\mathfrak{t}(y),\exists\ \eta:[\mathfrak{t}(x),\mathfrak{t}(y)]\rightarrow\mathcal{M}\textnormal{ time-preserving, with }\eta\circ\mathfrak{t}(x)=x,\eta\circ\mathfrak{t}(y)=y\}.

Since every point in our spacetime admits a small unscathed parabolic cylinder around it, we deduce that the sets
U_{x}^{\pm}
are both non-empty and open in
\mathcal{M}
.

Fixing
x\in\mathcal{M}
, we can now union open sets of the form defined above in an iterative way to see which points in
\mathcal{M}
can be joined to
x
by a string of time-preserving paths. Define
U_{1}^{+}:=U_{x}^{+}
and
U_{1}^{-}:=\bigcup_{y\in U_{1}^{+}}U^{-}_{y}
. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we recursively set

U_{m}^{+}:=\bigcup\limits_{y\in U^{-}_{m-1}}U^{+}_{y},\quad U_{m}^{-}:=\bigcup_{y\in U_{m}^{+}}U^{-}_{y}.

Finally, let
U^{-}:=\cup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}U_{m}^{-}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
. The following Lemma shows that spacetimes are path-connected by strings of time-preserving paths.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{t},\partial_{t},g)
be a Ricci flow spacetime. Fix
x\in\mathcal{M}
and let
U^{-}
be the open subset of
\mathcal{M}
defined above. Then
\mathcal{M}=U^{-}
.

Proof.

Since
U^{-}
is open and non-empty, it suffices to show that
U^{-}
is closed in
\mathcal{M}
. As such, consider a sequence
x_{n}\in U^{-}
such that
x_{n}\rightarrow x_{\infty}\in\mathcal{M}
. For
r>0
sufficiently small, the parabolic cylinder
C(x_{\infty},r)
is unscathed in
\mathcal{M}
. For
n
sufficiently large,
x_{n}
lies inside this parabolic ball. Then, for this large value of
n
we have that either

\displaystyle\mathfrak{t}(x_{n})>\mathfrak{t}(x_{\infty})

\displaystyle\implies x_{\infty}\in U^{-}_{x_{n}},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{t}(x_{n})\leq\mathfrak{t}(x_{\infty})

\displaystyle\implies\exists\ y\in U^{+}_{x_{n}}\textnormal{ with }x_{\infty}\in U^{-}_{y}.

So, as
x_{n}\in U^{-}
, there is some
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x_{n}\in U_{m}^{-}
, and hence by the above,

as required.

\square u\geq-\frac{2}{\phi}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u+\frac{1}{(Ar_{0})^{2}}\left(\frac{2(\phi^{\prime})^{2}}{\phi}-\phi^{\prime\prime}\right)R+\frac{2}{n}\phi R^{2},

(15)

holds in the barrier sense. With our choice of
\phi
, we can ensure that we have the bound
\lvert\frac{2(\phi^{\prime})^{2}}{\phi}-\phi^{\prime\prime}\rvert\leq C^{\prime}\phi^{\frac{1}{2}}
, for some
C^{\prime}>0
depending only on
\phi
. Using Peter-Paul, we also have the inequality

\frac{C^{\prime}R\phi^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(Ar_{0})^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{\delta}\left(\frac{C^{\prime}}{(Ar_{0})^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\phi R^{2}=\frac{\delta}{2}\left(\left(\frac{C}{(Ar_{0})^{2}}\right)^{2}+\phi R^{2}\right),

where
C
now depends on
\delta
.
Therefore, we can simplify equation (
15
) to the differential inequality

\square u\geq-\frac{2}{\phi}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u+\left(\frac{2}{n}-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\phi R^{2}-\frac{\delta}{2}\left(\frac{C}{(Ar_{0})^{2}}\right)^{2}.

(16)

Applying the maximum principle, we conclude that

\displaystyle\liminf_{h\searrow 0}\frac{u_{0}(s+h)-u_{0}(s)}{h}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{2}{n}-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\phi R^{2}-\frac{\delta}{2}\left(\frac{C}{(Ar_{0})^{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{2}{n}-\delta\right)u_{0}^{2}(s)+\frac{\delta}{2}\left(u_{0}(s)^{2}-\left(\frac{C}{(Ar_{0})^{2}}\right)^{2}\right).

Integrating this up, we have the desired inequality.
∎

The following is the

smoothing result for solutions to Ricci flow
[

Theorem B.2

(

smoothing)
.

Suppose
g(t)=u(z,t)|dz|^{2}
is a smooth conformal Ricci flow on the ball
B_{3r}\times(0,T)
, for some

. Suppose there exists a non-atomic, non-zero Radon measure
\mu\in\mathcal{R}(B_{3r})
such that

starts weakly from

. If

satisfies

then

\sup_{B_{r}}u(t)\leq C_{0}r^{-2}t,

where
C_{0}<\infty
is universal.

Proof.

Since
\mu
is Radon, for some
\delta>0
,
\frac{\mu(B_{2r+\delta})}{2\pi}<t
. Choose a cut off function
f
such that
f\equiv 1
on
\overline{B_{2r}}
and supp
(f)\subseteq B_{2r+\delta}
. For
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small

\frac{\textnormal{Vol}_{g(\epsilon)}(B_{2r})}{2\pi}\leq\frac{\int fd\mu_{g(\epsilon)}}{2\pi}\leq\frac{\int fd\mu}{2\pi}+\left(t-\frac{\mu(B_{2r+\delta})}{2\pi}\right)\leq t.

Therefore applying the estimate in the smooth case
[
TY21
, Theorem 2.1]
to the Ricci flow on
B_{2r}\times(\epsilon,T)
gives

\sup_{B_{r}}u(\epsilon+t)\leq C_{0}r^{-2}t.

Since
u
is smooth away from zero, taking
\epsilon
to zero gives the result.

Lemma B.3
.

Let

of

For any point

on a neighbourhood of this point and choose
r>0
sufficiently small such that
B_{3r}\subseteq M\setminus\supp(\mu)
, where
B_{3r}
denotes the ball centred at

radius

with respect to the metric

The following is another result from Topping & Yin
[
TY21
, Lemma 3.1]
, which stops complete Ricci flows from losing volume too quickly within a conformal ball.

Lemma B.4

(Control on volume decay
[
TY21
]
)
.

Let
g(t)
be a smooth instantaneously complete conformal Ricci flow on
\mathbb{C}\times(0,T)
starting weakly from
\mu\in\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C})
. Fix
0<r<R<\infty
and suppose
B_{r}\subseteq B_{R}
are concentric balls in the plane. Then there exists an explicit constant
C:=4\pi\cdot\frac{1+(r/R)^{2}}{1-(r/R)^{2}}
, such that

\mu(B_{r})\leq\textnormal{Vol}_{g(t)}(B_{R})+Ct,\quad\forall t\in\left(0,T\wedge\frac{\mu(B_{r})}{4\pi}\right).

Proof.

We first deal with the case
g(t)
is smooth up to time zero. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
choose
r_{n}\in(r,R)
such that
r_{n}\searrow r
and define a new smooth metric
g_{n}
on
\mathbb{C}
such that

•

g_{n}\equiv g(0)
on
B_{r}
,

•

g_{n}\leq g(0)
on
\mathbb{C}
,

•

g_{n}\leq\frac{1}{n}H_{r_{n}}
on
\mathbb{C}\setminus B_{r_{n}}
,

where
H_{r}
denotes the complete hyperbolic metric on
\mathbb{C}\setminus B_{r}
. Let
g_{n}(t)
be the instantaneously complete Ricci flow on
\mathbb{C}
starting from
g_{n}
. Since
\textnormal{Vol}_{g_{n}}(\mathbb{C})\geq\textnormal{Vol}_{g(0)}(B_{r})=:v_{0}
, by the existence theorem for Ricci flows
B.1
, we have that each of the
g_{n}(t)
exist for
t\in(0,\frac{v_{0}}{4\pi})
. Using
[
GT11
, Corollary 3.3]
, we can deduce that
g_{n}(t)\leq g(t)
on
\mathbb{C}\times(0,T\wedge\frac{v_{0}}{4\pi})
. In particular, we have that for each
t\in(0,T\wedge\frac{v_{0}}{4\pi})
,

\displaystyle\textnormal{Vol}_{g(t)}(B_{R})\geq\textnormal{Vol}_{g_{n}(t)}(B_{R})

\displaystyle=\textnormal{Vol}_{g_{n}(t)}(\mathbb{C})-\textnormal{Vol}_{g_{n}(t)}(\mathbb{C}\setminus B_{R})

\displaystyle\geq v_{0}-4\pi t-(\frac{1}{n}+2t)\textnormal{Vol}_{H_{r_{n}}}(\mathbb{C}\setminus B_{R}).

By a direct calculation

\textnormal{Vol}_{H_{r}}(\mathbb{C}\setminus B_{R})=\frac{4\pi r^{2}}{R^{2}-r^{2}},

and so taking
n\rightarrow\infty
we have that, for each
t\in(0,T\wedge\frac{v_{0}}{4\pi})

\textnormal{Vol}_{g(t)}(B_{R})\geq\textnormal{Vol}_{g(0)}(B_{r})-Ct.

(18)

This deals with the case
g
is initially smooth. For the general case of weak initial data, fix
\epsilon>0
. Since
\mu
is Radon, there exists
K\Subset B_{r}
such that
\mu(K)\geq\mu(B_{r})-\epsilon
. Choosing a test function
f
with support in
B_{r}
and equal to
1
on
K
, we have for
\delta
sufficiently small

\textnormal{Vol}_{g(\delta)}(B_{r})\geq\int fd\mu_{g(\delta)}\geq\int fd\mu-\epsilon\geq\mu(K)-\epsilon\geq\mu(B_{r})-2\epsilon.

(19)

For any fixed
t\in(0,T\wedge\frac{\mu(B_{r})}{4\pi})
, we can choose
\delta\in(0,T)
sufficiently small such that equation (
19
) holds as well as
t\in(\delta,T\wedge\frac{\textnormal{Vol}_{g(\delta)}(B_{r})}{4\pi})
. Applying (
18
) to
g(t)
on
\mathbb{C}\times[\delta,T)
gives

\mu(B_{r})\leq\textnormal{Vol}_{g(\delta)}(B_{r})+2\epsilon\leq\textnormal{Vol}_{g(t)}(B_{R})+Ct+2\epsilon.

Taking
\epsilon\searrow 0
finishes the proof.
∎

Recall that the locally defined conformal factors of a
2
-dimensional Ricci flow solve the LFDE (
5
). In Section
4
we proved a stronger comparison principle for spacetimes by using the usual comparison principle for the LFDE stated below.

Lemma B.5

(Direct comparison principle)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a compactly contained, open subset of
D\times(0,T)
, and let
u,v\in C^{2,1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}})
be solutions to the LFDE (
5
) with
u,v>0
. If
v>u
on the parabolic boundary
\partial_{P}\mathcal{M}
, then
v\geq u
on
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

We modify the argument used by Giesen in
[
Gie12
]
.

By compactness, we can choose
\delta>0
such that
v\geq u+\delta
on
\partial_{P}\mathcal{M}
. For any
\epsilon>0
, consider

\mathcal{M}(\epsilon):=\{(z,t)\in D\times(0,T):(z,\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon t))\in\mathcal{M}\},

and the modified function

v_{\epsilon}(z,t):=(1+\epsilon t)\cdot v(z,\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon t)),\quad\forall(z,t)\in\overline{\mathcal{M}(\epsilon)}.

This modification makes
v_{\epsilon}
a strict supersolution to the LFDE on
\mathcal{M}(\epsilon)
:

(\partial_{t}v_{\epsilon}-\Delta\log(v_{\epsilon}))(z,t)=\epsilon\cdot v(z,\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon t))>0,\quad\forall(z,t)\in\mathcal{M}(\epsilon).

Since
u
and
v
are continuous on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
compact, they are uniformly continuous. In particular, from the inequality

0<t-\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon t)\leq T-\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon T),\quad\forall t\in(0,T),

we see that
\lvert t-\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon t)\rvert
converges to zero uniformly in
t\in(0,T)
as
\epsilon\searrow 0
. Thus, for
\epsilon
sufficiently small,
\lvert v(z,t)-v(z,\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon t))\rvert<\delta/2
, and
\lvert u(z,t)-u(z,\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon t))\rvert<\delta/2
, whenever
(z,t),(z,\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon t))\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}
.

We now consider those points lying in both
\mathcal{M}
and the shifted spacetime
\mathcal{M}(\epsilon)
.

Claim
.

Define
M(\epsilon)=\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{M}(\epsilon)
. For
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small,
v_{\epsilon}\geq u
on
\partial_{P}\left(M(\epsilon)\right)
.

Proof of Claim.

Fix
(z,t)\in\partial_{P}\left(M(\epsilon)\right)
. Note that
\partial_{P}\left(M(\epsilon)\right)\subseteq\left(\partial_{P}\mathcal{M}\right)\cup\left(\partial_{P}\left(\mathcal{M}(\epsilon)\right)\right)
. This splits our analysis into two cases:

(I)

(z,t)\in\partial_{P}\mathcal{M}
. In this case we have the inequality

\displaystyle v_{\epsilon}(z,t)

\displaystyle\geq v(z,t)-\lvert v_{\epsilon}(z,t)-v(z,t)\rvert

\displaystyle\geq u(z,t)+\delta-\lvert\epsilon t\rvert\cdot\lvert v(z,\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon t))\rvert-\lvert v(z,\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon t))-v(z,t)\rvert

\displaystyle\geq u(z,t)+\delta-\epsilon\cdot T\cdot\lVert v\rVert_{\infty}-\frac{\delta}{2}\geq u(z,t),

for any
\epsilon
sufficiently small.

(II)

(z,t)\in\partial_{P}\left(\mathcal{M}(\epsilon)\right)
. Note that, this implies
(z,\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon t))\in\partial_{P}\mathcal{M}
, and therefore we have the inequality

\displaystyle v_{\epsilon}(z,t)

\displaystyle\geq v(z,\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon t))

\displaystyle\geq u(z,\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon t))+\delta

\displaystyle\geq u(z,t)+\delta-\lvert u(z,\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon t))-u(z,t)\rvert

\displaystyle\geq u(z,t)+\delta-\frac{\delta}{2}\geq u(z,t),

for any
\epsilon
sufficiently small. ∎

Claim
.

v_{\epsilon}\geq u
on
M(\epsilon)
.

Proof of claim.

Let
M_{t}
denote
M(\epsilon)\cap\left(D\times\{t\}\right)
for each
t\in(0,T)
. If the claim doesn’t hold, then for
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large, the times

t_{n}:=\inf\{t\in(0,T):\min_{\overline{M_{t}}}(v_{\epsilon}-u)(z,t)\leq-1/n\}\in(0,T),

are well-defined. As
\overline{M_{t_{n}}}
is compact, there exists
z_{n}\in\overline{M_{t_{n}}}
such that
(v_{\epsilon}-u)(z_{n},t_{n})=-1/n
. By the previous claim,
(v_{\epsilon}-u)\geq 0
on the parabolic boundary
\partial_{P}\left(M(\epsilon)\right)
, and hence
z_{n}\in M_{t_{n}}
. Since the point
(z_{n},t_{n})
is in the interior, we have the inequalities

(v_{\epsilon}-u)=-1/n,\quad\Delta(v_{\epsilon}-u)\geq 0,\quad\partial_{t}(v_{\epsilon}-u)\leq 0,

and therefore

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\epsilon v(z_{n},\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+t_{n}\epsilon))

\displaystyle\leq(\partial_{t}v_{\epsilon}-\Delta\log(v_{\epsilon}))(z_{n},t_{n})-(\partial_{t}u-\Delta\log(u))(z_{n},t_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\Delta\log(u)-\Delta\log(v_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\Delta u}{u}-\frac{\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}}{u^{2}}\right)-\left(\frac{\Delta v_{\epsilon}}{v_{\epsilon}}-\frac{\lvert\nabla v_{\epsilon}\rvert^{2}}{v_{\epsilon}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{v_{\epsilon}}\Delta(u-v_{\epsilon})+(\frac{1}{u}-\frac{1}{v_{\epsilon}})\Delta u+\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\left(\frac{1}{v_{\epsilon}^{2}}-\frac{1}{u^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{\lvert\Delta u\rvert}{uv_{\epsilon}}+\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\left(\frac{(u+v_{\epsilon})}{u^{2}v_{\epsilon}^{2}}\right)\right).

Since
u
and
v
are uniformly bounded away from zero, and
u,v\in C^{2,1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}})
, there exists
C<\infty
, independent of
n
, such that

0<\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{v(z_{n},\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+t_{n}\epsilon))}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{\lvert\Delta u\rvert}{uv_{\epsilon}}+\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\left(\frac{(u+v_{\epsilon})}{u^{2}v_{\epsilon}^{2}}\right)\right)\right)\leq\frac{C}{n}.

Taking
n\rightarrow\infty
, this is gives a contradiction.
∎

Fix
(z,t)\in\mathcal{M}
. Recall that
\lvert t-\epsilon^{-1}\log(1+\epsilon t)\rvert
converges to zero uniformly in
t\in(0,T)
as
\epsilon\searrow 0
. Therefore, as
\mathcal{M}
is open, for any
\epsilon
sufficiently small,
(z,t)\in M(\epsilon)
. Moreover, by continuity,
v_{\epsilon}(z,t)
converges to
v(z,t)
as
\epsilon\searrow 0
, and we can conclude that
v\geq u
on all of
\mathcal{M}
.
∎

On The Evans Chain Complex
S. Joseph Lippert

Abstract
We elaborate on the construction of the Evans chain complex for higher-rank graph C ∗ C^{*} -algebras. Specifically, we introduce a block matrix presentation of the differential maps. These block matrices are then used to identify a wide family of higher-rank graph C ∗ C^{*} -algebras with trivial K-theory. Additionally, in the specialized case where the higher-rank graph consists of one vertex, we are able to use the Künneth theorem to explicitly compute the homology groups of the Evans chain complex.

E_{p,q}^{2}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{g}^{\binom{k-1}{p}},&2|q\\
0,&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

Proof.

The goal is to use Theorem
3.7
which yields
\mathcal{D}\cong\bigotimes^{k}\mathcal{C}^{j}
. Then, the Künneth Theorem with some classical finite group theory results to obtain the necessary homology groups. As before, we may without loss of generality suppose that
B_{1}>0
.

We proceed by induction on
k
. Consider the base case
H_{p}(\mathcal{C}^{1})
. Since
B_{1}\neq 0
, we observe that
E_{1,2q}=H_{1}(\mathcal{C}^{1})=\ker(B_{1})=0
and
E_{0,2q}=H_{0}(\mathcal{C}^{1})=\mathbb{Z}_{B_{1}}
as desired. Fix some
j\geq 1
and define
g_{j}:=\gcd(B_{1},\cdots,B_{j})
. Then suppose:

H_{p}(\mathcal{C}^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{C}^{j})\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{g_{j}})^{\binom{j-1}{p}}.

Consider
(\mathcal{C}^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{C}^{j})\otimes\mathcal{C}^{j+1}
. Since each module in our complex is free which implies projective
[
17
, cf
\sim\lx@sectionsign 2
]
, we may use the Künneth Theorem and the inductive hypothesis to obtain

\displaystyle H_{p}((\mathcal{C}^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{C}^{j})\otimes\mathcal{C}^{j+1})\cong

\displaystyle(\mathbb{Z}_{g_{j}})^{\binom{j-1}{p-1}}\otimes H_{1}(\mathcal{C}^{j+1})\oplus(\mathbb{Z}_{g_{j}})^{\binom{j-1}{p}}\otimes H_{0}(\mathcal{C}^{j+1})\oplus

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}\left((\mathbb{Z}_{g_{j}})^{\binom{j-1}{p-2}},H_{1}(\mathcal{C}^{j+1})\right)\oplus\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}\left((\mathbb{Z}_{g_{j}})^{\binom{j-1}{p-1}},H_{0}(\mathcal{C}^{j+1})\right).

There are two cases determined by
B_{j+1}
. First, suppose that
B_{j+1}=0
, and thus
g_{j+1}=g_{j}
. In this case,
H_{*}(\mathcal{C}^{j+1})\cong\mathbb{Z}
. This would make the torsion groups
0
, and

H_{p}((\mathcal{C}^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{C}^{j})\otimes\mathcal{C}^{j+1})\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{g_{j}})^{\binom{j-1}{p}}\oplus(\mathbb{Z}_{g_{j}})^{\binom{j-1}{p-1}}\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{g_{j+1}})^{\binom{j}{p}}.

Suppose that
B_{j+1}\neq 0
. This would mean that
H_{1}(\mathcal{C}^{j+1})=0
and
H_{0}(\mathcal{C}^{j+1})=\mathbb{Z}_{B_{j+1}}
. In particular, this means that the terms containing
H_{1}(\mathcal{C}^{j+1})
vanish leaving

(\mathbb{Z}_{g_{j}})^{\binom{j-1}{p}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{B_{j+1}}\oplus\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}\left((\mathbb{Z}_{g_{j}})^{\binom{j-1}{p-1}},\mathbb{Z}_{B_{j+1}}\right).

It is a well known fact
[
11
, cf
\sim\lx@sectionsign 6
]
that

\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}\left((\mathbb{Z}_{g_{j}})^{\binom{j-1}{p-1}},\mathbb{Z}_{B_{j+1}}\right)\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{g_{j}})^{\binom{j-1}{p-1}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{B_{j+1}}\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{g_{j}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{B_{j+1}})^{\binom{j-1}{p-1}}\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{g_{j+1}})^{\binom{j-1}{p-1}}

giving the desired form.

We conclude that
H_{p}(\mathcal{D})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{g}^{\binom{k-1}{p}}
which allows us to fill in the
E^{2}
page of the Kasparov-Schochet spectral sequence with the desired result. ∎

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
\Lambda
be a monoid
3
-graph with
|\Lambda^{e_{i}}|>1
for some
1\leq i\leq 3
and define
g=\gcd(B_{1},B_{2},B_{3})
. Then
K_{1}(C^{*}(\Lambda))=\mathbb{Z}_{g}^{2}
and there exists a short exact sequence

\mathbb{Z}_{g}\to K_{0}(C^{*}(\Lambda))\to\mathbb{Z}_{g}.

Proof.

Consider the map
d_{p,q}^{2}:E_{p,q}^{2}\to E_{p-2,q+1}^{2}
. Observe that the parity of
q
meaning that
\operatorname{im}(d_{p,q}^{2})=0
and
\ker(d_{p,q})=E_{p,q}^{2}
. We conclude that
E^{2}\cong E^{3}
, this is indeed true for any
k
-graph. Theorem
3.9
gives that
E^{3}_{p,2q}\cong E^{2}_{p,2q}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{g}^{\binom{2}{p}}
. In particular, this means that
d^{3}_{p,q}:E_{p,q}^{3}\to E_{p-3,q}
must be the zero map. We conclude as before that
E^{3}\cong E^{4}
, and thus the sequence has stabilized.

First we examine the
K_{1}
group. We look at the family
\cdots,E_{-1,2},E_{1,0},E_{3,-2},\cdots
. By Theorem
3.9

E_{3,-2}\cong 0
so all but
E_{1,0}
are isomorphic to
0
. Moreover, there exists a filtration of
K_{1}(C^{*}(\Lambda))
,
0\leq\cdots\leq F_{-1}\leq F_{0}\leq F_{1}\leq F_{2}\leq\cdots\leq K_{1}(C^{*}(\Lambda))
such that
F_{p}/F_{p-1}\cong E_{p,1-p}
. The zeros in all positions aside from
E_{1,0}
allows us to conclude that
F_{1}\cong K_{1}(C^{*}(\Lambda))
and
F_{0}\cong 0
. That is,
K_{1}(C^{*}(\Lambda))\cong F_{1}/F_{0}\cong E_{1,0}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{g}^{2}
.

To determine
K_{0}
, we must look at the family with even total degree (
2|(p+q)
). This family is

\cdots,0,0,\mathbb{Z}_{g},0,\mathbb{Z}_{g},0,0,\cdots

We now consider the filtration
0\cong F_{-1}\leq F_{0}\leq F_{1}\leq F_{2}\cong K_{0}(C^{*}(\Lambda))
. Since
2\nmid 1
, Theorem
1
, ensures that
E_{1,-1}=0
. So,
F_{1}\cong F_{0}
, allowing us to refine to the filtration
0\leq F_{0}\leq F_{2}
. This ensures that
F_{2}/F_{0}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{g}
providing the short exact sequence

0\to\mathbb{Z}_{g}\to K_{0}(C^{*}(\Lambda))\to\mathbb{Z}_{g}\to 0.\qed

Lemma A.1
.

Let

Bounds related to the edge-list chromatic and total chromatic numbers of a simple graph
M. Henderson, A.J.W. Hilton, R. Mary Jeya Jothi

Abstract
We show that for a simple graph G G , c ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ Δ ⁡ ( G ) + 2 c^{\prime}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+2 where c ′ ​ ( G ) c^{\prime}(G) is the choice index (or edge-list chromatic number) of G G , and Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) is the maximum degree of G G . As a simple corollary of this result, we show that the total chromatic number χ T ​ ( G ) \chi_{T}(G) of a simple graph satisfies the inequality χ T ​ ( G ) ≤ Δ ⁡ ( G ) + 4 \chi_{T}(G)\leq\ \Delta(G)+4 and the total choice number c T ​ ( G ) c_{T}(G) also satisfies this inequality. We also relate these bounds to the Hall index and the Hall condition index of a simple graph, and to the total Hall number and the total Hall condition number of a simple graph.

Introduction

We prove a new bound for the choice index
c^{\prime}(G)
of a simple graph in the first part of this paper. In sections 5 and 6, we show how our bound for the choice index implies new bound for the total chromatic number of a graph and for the total list chromatic number (or the total choice index) of a simple graph. Although the bounds themselves are new, the argument to get the bounds from the choice index is well-known.

In Section 7 we discuss the Hall index and the Hall condition index of a simple graph, and in Section 8 we discuss the total Hall number, and the total Hall condition number of a simple graph.

It may be that our proof could be improved upon so as to prove Conjecture 4. We give some thoughts about this possibility in Section 4.

5
Concluding Remarks about the choice index of a simple graph.

The obvious question raised by our theorem and its proof is whether Theorem 10 can be improved to “
c^{\prime}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+1
whenever
G
is simple graph”. We use the extra freedom, which arises when we assume
c^{\prime}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+2
in several places, but it is not obvious that the same kind of proof could not be found if we assume
c^{\prime}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+1
. But the difficulties which seem to arise in attempting this, also raise the question of whether there are simple graphs for which
c^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)+2
.

It might be that our proof could be shortened, but one reason for sticking to the present proof is that it shows up quite well where the difficulties lie in trying to improve it so as to show that
c^{\prime}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+1
, and we hope that the present proof will be helpful in obtaining this improved bound.

To prove
c^{\prime}(G)=\chi^{\prime}(G)
, a different method of proof would have to be found.

6
The total chromatic number of a simple graph.

The total chromatic number
\chi_{T}(G)
of a simple graph
G
is the least number
j
of colours needed to colour the edges and vertices of a graph
G
so that

(i)

no colour is used on two edges which have a common vertex;

(ii)

no colour is used on two adjacent vertices;

(iii)

no colour is used on a vertex
v
and an edge incident with
v
.

Clearly
\Delta(G)+1\leq\chi_{T}(G)
. In 1965 a conjecture, due independently to Behzad
[
1
]
and to Vizing
[
21
]
was made that, if G is a simple graph, then
\chi_{T}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+2
. For a full discussion of the attribution of this conjecture, see [
[
19
]
, section 16.2]. In 1993, Hilton and Hind
[
13
]
showed that
\chi_{T}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+2
, if
\Delta(G)\geq\frac{3}{4}|V(G)|
. Some related results were given in 1991 by Chetwynd, Hilton and Zhao Cheng in
[
5
]
. It has been known since 1998 that
\chi_{T}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+c
, where
c
is a constant: Molloy and Reed
[
18
]
showed that for large enough
\Delta(G),\chi_{T}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+10^{26}
. Here we prove the following similar result which is slightly weaker than the conjectured result.

Theorem 11. Let

be a simple graph, then
\chi_{T}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+4

It has been known ever since the choice index was thought of, that a bound for the choice index yields a bound for the total chromatic number. We use this short argument to prove Theorem 11.

. Let

Of course, there is really no known reason why this should not hold for multigraphs also.

We prove here:

Theorem 13. For a simple graph
G

\Delta(G)+1\leq\chi_{T}(G)\leq c_{T}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+4.

Proof
. The first two inequalities are obvious. To prove the third inequality. Let
\Lambda
be a list assignment to
E(G)\cup V(G)
with
|\Lambda(e)|\geq\Delta(G)+4

(\forall e\in E(G))
and
|\Lambda(v)|\geq\Delta(G)+4

(\forall v\in V(G))
. We may properly colour the vertices of
G
using colours from the lists
\Lambda(v)
. Since
|\Lambda(v)|\geq\Delta(G)+4
, this is clearly possible (by an obvious greedy algorithm, this is possible if
|\Lambda(v)|\geq\Delta(G)+1

(\forall v\in V(G))
. For
e\in E(G)
, let
\Lambda^{*}(e)
be
\Lambda(e)
less the two colours selected to properly colour the end vertices. Then
|\Lambda^{*}(e)|\geq\Delta(G)+2

(\forall e\in E(G))
. By Theorem 5, there is an edge-list colouring of

.

8
The Hall index and the Hall condition index of a simple graph.

We should remark that a large part of this section holds
mutatis mutandis
for graphs in general or for the edge-sets of multigraphs (see Hilton and Johnson
[
15
]
).The connection between Hall’s theorem and list colouring was noticed in 1990 by Hilton and Johnson
[
14
]
, and was also touched on in the survey paper by Woodall
[
22
]
in 2001.

For an edge-list assignment
L
of
G
, a proper
L-edge-colouring
is a mapping
\phi
from
E(G)
to
\bigcup\limits_{e\in E}L(e)
such that
\phi(e)\in L(e)
for all
e\in E(G)
, and if
e,f\in E(G)
have a vertex in common, then
\phi(e)\neq\phi(f)
. For a list assignment
L
to
E(G)
and a colour
\sigma\in\bigcup\limits_{e\in E(G)}L(e)
, let
\alpha^{\prime}(\sigma,L,G)
be the maximum number of independent edges in the subgraph
H
of
G
induced by those edges in
E(G)
such that
\sigma\in L(e)
. Thus
\alpha^{\prime}(\sigma,L,G)
is the size of the largest set of edges of
G
having
\sigma
in their lists and which have pairwise no vertex in common; in other words
\alpha^{\prime}(\sigma,L,G)
is the
matching number
of the subgraph of
G
induced by the edges with
\sigma
in their lists. In the case when each edge has the same list assignment, the notation
\alpha^{\prime}(\sigma,L,G)
is shortened to
\alpha^{\prime}(G)
.

Suppose that
E(G)
has a proper L-edge colouring. Then for each
\sigma\in\bigcup\limits_{e\in E}L(e)
, the set
S_{\sigma}
of edges coloured
\sigma
is independent. Therefore, since each vertex is coloured,

|E(G)|=\sum_{\sigma}|S_{\sigma}|\leq\sum_{\sigma}\alpha^{\prime}(\sigma,L,G),

where the sums are taken over all
\sigma\in\bigcup\limits_{e\in E}L(e)
. Since every subgraph
H
of
G
is also properly coloured, the same inequality holds with
E=E(G)
replaced with
E(H)
and
\alpha^{\prime}(\sigma,L,G)
replaced by
\alpha^{\prime}(\sigma,L,H)
.

Given a simple graph
G
and a list assignment
L
for
E(G)
, we say that
G
satisfies
Hall’s edge-condition
if and only if, for each subgraph
H
of
G
,

|E(H)|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in(\cup L(e):e\in E(H))\end{subarray}}\alpha^{\prime}(\sigma,L,H)\hskip 20.0pt(*)

Note that (*) holds for each induced subgraph
H
of
G
if and only of it holds for each subgraph of
G
.

We define the
Hall edge number
or
Hall index

h^{\prime}(G)
to be the smallest positive integer
l
such that there is a proper L-edge-colouring of
G
whenever
G
and
L
satisfy Hall’s edge-condition and
|L(e)|\geq l
for all
e\in E(G)
.

We define the
Hall edge condition number
, or the
Hall condition index
s^{\prime}(G)
of
G
to be the smallest integer
l
such that
G
and
L
satisfy Hall’s Condition (*) whenever
|L(e)|\geq l
for all
e\in E(G)
. Let
s_{o}{{}^{\prime}}(G)
be the smallest integer
l
such that the assignment of
\{1,...,l\}
to every edge satisfies Hall’s Condition.

Theorem 14.
(Hilton and Johnson
[
15
]
and Johnson
[
16
]
)

\begin{split}s^{\prime}(G)=s_{o}{{}^{\prime}}&=max\Bigg\{{\Biggl\lceil\frac{|E(H)|}{\alpha^{\prime}(H)}\Biggr\rceil}:\text{\it{H is an induced subgraph of G}}\Bigg\}\\
&=max\Bigg\{{\Biggl\lceil\frac{|E(H)|}{\alpha^{\prime}(H)}\Biggr\rceil}:\text{\it{H is an induced subgraph of G with at least one edge}}\Bigg\}.\end{split}

Some further facts about
c^{\prime}(G),s^{\prime}(G),\chi^{\prime}(G)
and
h^{\prime}(G)
are collected together in the following Theorem. (See Hilton and Johnson

]

Theorem 15. Let
G
be a simple graph. Then

A)

Either

1)

\Delta(G)+1=c^{\prime}(G)=\chi^{\prime}(G)=s^{\prime}(G)>h^{\prime}(G)
, or

2)

\Delta(G)+1=c^{\prime}(G)=\chi^{\prime}(G)=h^{\prime}(G)\geq s^{\prime}(G)
, or

\Delta(G)+2\geq h^{\prime}(G)

s^{\prime}(G)-s^{\prime}(G-e)\leq 1

,

If

The most interesting question about the choice index
c^{\prime}(G)
is whether the choice index conjecture is true, i.e. whether
c^{\prime}(G)=\chi^{\prime}(G)
for all simple graphs
G
, or indeed, for all multigraphs. If it is not true, then (A 3) above has to be true for some graph

, and so either
\Delta(G)+2=c^{\prime}(G)=h^{\prime}(G),

\Delta(G)+1\geq\chi(G)\geq s^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)

\Delta(G)+1=c^{\prime}(G)=h^{\prime}(G)

\chi^{\prime}(G)=s^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)

h^{\prime}(G)
.This aspect was looked at by Cropper and Hilton

. They were unable to determine the choice index or the Hall index of

The total Hall number and the total Hall Condition number of a simple graph.

If
H

G
induced by
E(H)\cup V(H)
then let
\alpha_{T}(H)
be the largest independent set of vertices and edge of
G
, or, in other words, let
\alpha_{T}(H)
be the largest set of vertices and edge of
G
such that no two vertices are adjacent, no two edges are incident with the same vertex, and it contains no edge-vertex pair with the edge incident with the vertex.
\alpha_{T}(H)
is called the
total independence number
of

Suppose that
E(G)\cup V(G)
has a proper L-total colouring. For a total list assignment
L

E(G)\cup V(G)

\sigma\in(\bigcup\limits_{e\in E}L(e))\cup(\bigcup\limits_{v\in V}L(v)
). Let
\alpha_{T}(\sigma,L,G)

of

\sigma\in(\bigcup\limits_{e\in E}L(e))\cup\bigcup\limits_{v\in V}L(v))

of edges and vertices is independent. Therefore, since each vertex and edge is coloured,

|E(G)|\cup|V(G)|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\end{subarray}}|T_{\sigma}|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\end{subarray}}\alpha_{T}{(\sigma,L,G),}

where the sum is taken over all
\sigma\in(\bigcup\limits_{e\in E}L(e))\cup(\bigcup\limits_{v\in V}L(v))
.

Since every subgraph
H
of
G
is also properly coloured, the same inequality holds with
E(G)
and
V(G)
replaced by
E(H)
and
V(H)
, and
\alpha_{T}(\sigma,L,G)

\alpha_{T}(\sigma,L,H)

e\in E(H),v\in V(H)\end{subarray}}\alpha_{T}(\sigma,L,H)\hskip 28.45274pt(**)

Marginally trapped surfaces in 𝕃 4 {\mathbb{L}}^{4} and three Weierstrass representations
Hojoo Lee

Abstract
. We construct new integrable systems to present Weierstrass type representations for spacelike surfaces whose mean curvature vector 𝐇 \mathbf{H} satisfies the null condition ⟨ 𝐇 , 𝐇 ⟩ = 0 \langle\mathbf{H},\mathbf{H}\rangle=0 in the four dimensional Lorentz-Minkowski space 𝕃 4 {\mathbb{L}}^{4} . Our new Weierstrass presentations extend simultaneously classical Weierstrass representations (of the first kind and the second kind) for maximal surfaces in 𝕃 3 {\mathbb{L}}^{3} and minimal surfaces in ℝ 3 {\mathbb{R}}^{3} . We solve a linear partial differental equation to construct explicit examples of marginally trapped surfaces with nowhere vanishing mean curvature vector.



1.
Motivation and main results

A spacelike space in the four dimensional Lorentz-Minkowski space
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
is marginally trapped
if its mean curvature vector
\mathbf{H}
verifies the null condition:

\langle\mathbf{H},\mathbf{H}\rangle=0.

We briefly review known conformal representations
[
2
,
3
,
Liu2013
]
for marginally trapped surfaces.

The Aledo-Gálvez-Mira representation
[
2
, Theorem 9]
generates marginally trapped surfaces of
Bryant type
in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
. Their
conformal representation yields marginally trapped surfaces satisfying two geometric conditions that they have flat normal bundle and are locally isometric to
some minimal surfaces in Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
or some maximal surfaces in Lorentz-Minkowski space
{\mathbb{L}}^{3}
. The Aledo-Gálvez-Mira
representation extends simultaneously the Bryant representation
[
Bryant1987
, Theorem A]
for surfaces with mean curvature one in hyperbolic space
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}\subset{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
and the representation
[
1
, Theorem 7.1]
for spacelike surfaces with mean curvature one in de-Sitter space
{\mathbb{S}}^{3}_{1}\subset{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
.

Recently, Dussan, Filho, Simões
[
3
, Section 5]
presented an interesting method to construct explicit examples of marginally trapped surfaces sitting in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}\subset{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
. They solved a Riccati partial differential equation for the triple of a holomorphic function and two
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions. The conformal representation due to Liu
[
Liu2013
, Theorem 4.2]
for general marginally trapped surfaces in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
requires to solve a system of partial differential
equations for the triple of a holomorphic function and two
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions. Our work is strongly motivated by the Liu representation.

We sketch the main results of this paper, the first one in a series of papers on the marginally trapped surfaces. We present the three Weierstrass type
representations (Theorem
7
, Corollary
8
, Corollary
9
) for marginally trapped surfaces in the four dimensional
Lorentz-Minkowski space
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
. Our representations require to solve the
linear
partial differential equations.

Inspired by the Liu integrable system (
[
Liu2013
, Theorem 4.2]
and Lemma
6
) for
the triple of a holomorphic function and two
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions, we construct integrable systems (Definition
2.1
and Defintion
2.2
) for
the triple of a holomorphic function and two
\mathbb{R}
-valued functions. The fundamental features of our integrable systems are investigated in
Section
2
and Section
5
.

The key geometric idea hidden in our algebraic reduction of the Liu integrable system is revealed in our Weierstrass type representation (in Corollary
8
) for marginally trapped
surfaces in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
. Our conformal representation naturally extends the Weierstrass type representation of
the second kind
(due to O.
Kobayashi
[
Kobayashi1983
, Corollary 1.3]
) for maximal surfaces in
{\mathbb{L}}^{3}
. (See Remark
4.1
.)

Let
h(u+iv)
be an arbitrary prescribed nowhere vanishing holomorphic function. Our conformal representation in Corollary
8
indicates that the pair
\left(\mathcal{M}(u,v),\mathcal{N}(u,v)\right)
of the
\mathbb{R}
-valued
functions, which solves the
linear
partial differential equation

{\mathcal{M}}_{uu}+{\mathcal{M}}_{vv}=\textrm{Re}\,h\,\left({\mathcal{N}}_{uu}+{\mathcal{N}}_{vv}\right),

yields the marginally trapped surface in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
. (The holomorphic function
g:=\frac{1}{\,h\,}

can be viewed as a complexified null Gauss map of the marginally trapped surface. See Remark
4.1
.) Our representation in
Corollary
9
can be viewed a natural generalization of classical representations of
the first kind
for maximal surfaces
in
{\mathbb{L}}^{3}
and minimal surfaces in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. (See Remark
4.2
.)

Solving the above linear partial differential equation yields explicit examples of marginally trapped surfaces in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
. In particular, in
Section
6
, we construct two families of marginally trapped surfaces with nowhere vanishing mean curvature vector. Both families
contain a
catenoid cousin
with mean curvature one in the three dimensional hyperbolic space
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}\subset{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
.

2.
Two equivalent integrable systems

Throughout this section,
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\equiv\mathbb{C}
denotes a simply connected domain with the complex coordinate
z
.

Definition 2.1

(
Weierstrass data of the first kind
)
.

We call the triple
\left(g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\right)
the Weierstrass data of the first kind if a function
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}-\left\{0\right\}
and two
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
functions
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy the three conditions

\begin{cases}g_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0,\\
{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={|\,g\,|}^{2}\;{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},\\
{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\neq 0.\end{cases}

Definition 2.2

(
Weierstrass data of the second kind
)
.

We call the triple
\left(h,\,\mathcal{M},\,\mathcal{N}\right)
the Weierstrass data of the second kind if a function
h:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}-\left\{0\right\}
and two
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
functions
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy the three conditions

\begin{cases}h_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0,\\
{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},\\
{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\neq 0.\end{cases}

Theorem 1

(
Equivalence transformation from the first data to the second data
)
.

Let
\left(g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\right)
be a Weierstrass data of the first kind. We define
h:=\frac{1}{\,g\,}
and
\mathcal{N}:=2\mathcal{P}
.
Then, there exists a
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
function
{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

{\mathcal{M}}_{z}=\frac{1}{\,g\,}\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}+g\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}.

Moreover, the triple
\left(h,\,\mathcal{M},\,\mathcal{N}\right)
becomes a Weierstrass data of the second kind such that

\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\,=-\frac{1}{\,\overline{\,g\,}\,}\left(\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\,\right).

Proof.

The function
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}-\left\{0\right\}
and two
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
functions
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy

g_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0,\quad{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={|\,g\,|}^{2}\;{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},\quad{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\neq 0.

It is immediate that
h_{\overline{\,z\,}}={\left(\,\frac{1}{\,g\,}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0
. We introduce the function
\mathcal{E}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
by

\mathcal{E}:=\frac{1}{\,g\,}\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}+g\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}.

We have

{\mathcal{E}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{1}{\,g\,}\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}+g\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\left(\,\frac{1}{\,g\,}\,+\frac{1}{\,\overline{\,g\,}\,}\,\right)\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=2\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right){\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\in\mathbb{R}.

Since the domain
\Omega
is simply connected, by Poincaré’s Lemma, this
implies the existence of an

{\mathbb{R}}
-valued function
{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}
defined on
\Omega
such that

{\mathcal{M}}_{z}=\mathcal{E}.

We have

{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={\mathcal{E}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=2\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right){\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\,\right){\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}

and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}-\frac{1}{\,2\,}\left(\frac{1}{\,g\,}\,+\frac{1}{\,\overline{\,g\,}\,}\right)2{\mathcal{P}}_{z}=\frac{1}{\,g\,}\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}+g\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}-\left(\frac{1}{\,g\,}\,+\frac{1}{\,\overline{\,g\,}\,}\right){\mathcal{P}}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\,\overline{\,g\,}\,}\left(\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\,\right)\neq 0.

We conclude that the triple
\left(h,\,\mathcal{M},\,\mathcal{N}\right)
is a Weierstrass data of the second kind:

h_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0,\quad{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},\quad{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\neq 0.

∎

Theorem 2

(
Equivalence transformation from the second data to the first data
)
.

Let
\left(h,\,\mathcal{M},\,\mathcal{N}\right)
be a Weierstrass data of the second kind. We define
g:=\frac{1}{\,h\,}
and
\mathcal{P}:=\frac{1}{\,2\,}\mathcal{N}
.
Then, there exists a
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
function
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}=h\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{\,h^{2}\,}{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}.

Moreover, the triple
\left(g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\right)
becomes a Weierstrass data of the first kind such that

\frac{1}{\,\overline{\,g\,}\,}\left(\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\,\right)=-\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\,\right).

Proof.

The function
h:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}-\left\{0\right\}
and two
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
functions
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy

h_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0,\quad{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{\,h+\overline{\,h\,}\,}{2}\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},\quad{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{\,h+\overline{\,h\,}\,}{2}\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\neq 0.

It is immediate that
g_{\overline{\,z\,}}={\left(\,\frac{1}{\,h\,}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0
. We introduce the function
\mathcal{E}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
by

\mathcal{E}:=h\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{\,h^{2}\,}{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}.

We have

{\mathcal{E}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=h\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}-\frac{\,h^{2}\,}{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\left(h\frac{\,h+\overline{\,h\,}\,}{2}-\frac{\,h^{2}\,}{2}\right){\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{\,{|\,h\,|}^{2}\,}{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\in\mathbb{R}.

Since the domain
\Omega
is simply connected, by Poincaré’s Lemma, this
implies the existence of an

{\mathbb{R}}
-valued function
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}
defined on
\Omega
such that

{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}=\mathcal{E}.

We have

{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={\mathcal{E}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{\,{|\,h\,|}^{2}\,}{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{1}{\,{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}

and

\frac{1}{\,{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}=\frac{\,{\left|\,h\,\right|}^{2}\,}{\,2\,}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}-\,\left(\,h\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{\,h^{2}\,}{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\,\right)=h\left(\,\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}-{\mathcal{M}}_{z}\,\right)\neq 0.

We conclude that the triple
\left(g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\right)
is a Weierstrass data of the first kind:

g_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0,\quad{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={|\,g\,|}^{2}\;{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},\quad{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\neq 0.

∎

Remark 2.1
.

It is straightforward to check that the transformation in Theorem
2
is the reverse transformation in Theorem
1
using the integrability conditions.

We present three fundamental deformations of Weierstrass data of the first kind and the second kind.

Proposition 3

(
Parabolic Deformations
)
.

Let the triple
\left(g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\right)
be a Weierstrass data of the first kind. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
be a parameter constant
such that
g(z)\neq-\frac{1}{\,i\lambda\,}
for all
z\in\Omega
. We set
g_{{}_{\lambda}}:=\frac{g}{\,1+i\lambda g\,}
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{\lambda}:=\mathcal{P}
.
Then, there exists a
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
function
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the integrability condition

{\left(\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}=\left(\,\frac{1}{\,g\,}+i\lambda\,\right)\left(g{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}-i\lambda{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\right).

Moreover, the triple
\left(g_{{}_{\lambda}},\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\lambda},\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}\right)
becomes a Weierstrass data of the first kind.

First Proof of Proposition
3
.

We observe that both
g_{{}_{\lambda}}
and
g=g_{{}_{0}}
vanishes nowhere. We have

\frac{1}{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,}=\frac{1}{\,g\,}+i\lambda\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{\,\overline{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,}\,}=\frac{1}{\,\overline{g}\,}-i\lambda.

We introduce the function
\mathcal{E}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
by

\mathcal{E}:=\left(\,\frac{1}{\,g\,}+i\lambda\,\right)\left(g{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}-i\lambda{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\right)=\frac{1}{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,}\left(g{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}-i\lambda{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\right).

Note that both
g_{{}_{\lambda}}
and
g=g_{{}_{0}}
are holomorphic and that
{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{1}{\,{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}
. We have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g}{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}+\frac{1}{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,}\left(\,-i\lambda\,\right){\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{g}{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,}\frac{\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\,}{\,g\overline{\,g\,}\,}+\frac{1}{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,}\left(\,-i\lambda\,\right){\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,}\left(\,\frac{\,1\,}{\,\overline{\,g\,}\,}-i\lambda\,\right){\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{1}{\,{\left|\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,\right|}^{2}\,}{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}.

Since the domain
\Omega
is simply connected, by Poincaré’s Lemma, the observation

{\mathcal{E}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{1}{\,{\left|\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,\right|}^{2}\,}{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\in\mathbb{R}

implies the existence of an

{\mathbb{R}}
-valued function
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}
defined on
\Omega
such that

{\left(\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}=\mathcal{E}.

It follows that

{\left(\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={\mathcal{E}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{1}{\,{\left|\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,\right|}^{2}\,}{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{1}{\,{\left|\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,\right|}^{2}\,}{\left({\mathcal{P}}_{\lambda}\right)}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}.

Finally, we have the equality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\,\overline{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,}\,}{\left(\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}-g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,{\left(\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\,\overline{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,}\,}{\left(\,{\mathcal{P}}\,\right)}_{z}-g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,\mathcal{E}=\frac{1}{\,\overline{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,}\,}{\left(\,{\mathcal{P}}\,\right)}_{z}-g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,\left(\,\frac{1}{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,}\left(g{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}-i\lambda{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\right)\,\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\,\frac{1}{\,\overline{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,}\,}+i\lambda\,\right){\mathcal{P}}_{z}-g\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}=\frac{1}{\,\overline{\,g\,}\,}{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-g\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z},

This implies the last condition

{\left(\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}-{|\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,|}^{2}\,{\left(\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}=\frac{\,\overline{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}\,}\,}{\,\overline{\,g\,}\,}\left({\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\right)\neq 0.

∎

Though the first proof of Proposition
4
is self-contained, it does not explain the method how to discover the integrability condition in Proposition
4
:

{\left(\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}=\left(\,\frac{1}{\,g\,}+i\lambda\,\right)\left(g{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}-i\lambda{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\right).

We use the equivalence of the Weierstrass data of the first kind and the Weierstrass data of the second kind to discover the integrability condition.

Second Proof of Proposition
3
.

We set
h=\frac{1}{\,g\,}
and
\mathcal{N}=2\mathcal{P}
. According to Theorem
1
, we can
find a
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
function
{\mathcal{M}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

{\mathcal{M}}_{z}=\frac{1}{\,g\,}\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}+g\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}.

Moreover, the triple
\left(h,\,\mathcal{M},\,\mathcal{N}\right)
is a Weierstrass data of the second kind:

h_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0,\quad{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},\quad{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\neq 0.

It follows from
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
that
\textrm{Re}\left(h+i\lambda\right)=\textrm{Re}\,h
.
We find immediately that the triple

\left(h_{{}_{\lambda}},\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda},\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\lambda}\right)=\left(h+i\lambda,\,\mathcal{M},\,\mathcal{N}\right)

is also a Weierstrass data of the second kind:

{\left(\,h_{{}_{\lambda}}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0,\quad{\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\left(\textrm{Re}\,h_{{}_{\lambda}}\right)\,{\left(\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},\quad{\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h_{{}_{\lambda}}\right)\,{\left(\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}\neq 0.

Theorem
2
guarantees the existence of a
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
function
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

{\left(\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}=h_{{}_{\lambda}}\,{\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}-\frac{\,{h_{{}_{\lambda}}}^{2}\,}{2}{\left(\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}.

It follows from the definitions
g_{{}_{\lambda}}=\frac{g}{\,1+i\lambda g\,}
,
h=\frac{1}{\,g\,}
, and
h_{{}_{\lambda}}=h+i\lambda
that

\frac{1}{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}}=\frac{1}{\,g\,}+i\lambda=h+i\lambda=h_{{}_{\lambda}}.

We set

{\mathcal{P}}_{\lambda}:={\mathcal{P}}=\frac{1}{\,2\,}{\mathcal{N}}=\frac{1}{\,2\,}{\mathcal{N}}_{\lambda}.

Theorem
2
shows that the triple
\left(g_{{}_{\lambda}},\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\lambda},\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}\right)
is a Weierstrass data of the second kind.
It now remains to deduce the integrability condition:

\displaystyle{\left(\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{{}_{\lambda}}\,{\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}-\frac{\,{h_{{}_{\lambda}}}^{2}\,}{2}{\left(\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}=\frac{1}{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}}\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{1}{\,{g_{{}_{\lambda}}}^{2}}{\mathcal{P}}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}}\left(\,\frac{1}{\,g\,}\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}+g\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}-\frac{1}{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}}{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\,\right)=\frac{1}{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}}\left(\,g\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}+\left(\,\frac{1}{\,g\,}-\frac{1}{\,g_{{}_{\lambda}}}\,\right){\mathcal{P}}_{z}\,\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\,\frac{1}{\,g\,}+i\lambda\,\right)\left(g{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}-i\lambda{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\right).

∎

Proposition 4

(
Elliptic Deformations
)
.

Let the triple
\left(h,\,\mathcal{M},\,\mathcal{N}\right)
be a Weierstrass data of the second kind. Given a parameter
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
we set
h_{{}_{\tau}}:=e^{-i\tau}h
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{\tau}:=\mathcal{N}
.
Then, there exists a
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
function
{\mathcal{M}}_{\tau}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the integrability condition

{\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\tau}\,\right)}_{z}=e^{i\tau}\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{\,e^{i\tau}-e^{-i\tau}\,}{2}\,h\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}.

Moreover, the triple
\left(h_{{}_{\tau}},\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\tau},\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\tau}\right)
becomes a Weierstrass data of the second kind.

First Proof of Proposition
4
.

Since
h=h_{{}_{0}}
vanishes nowhere,
h_{{}_{\tau}}=e^{-i\tau}h
also vanishes nowhere.
We introduce the function
\mathcal{E}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
by

\mathcal{E}:=e^{i\tau}\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{\,e^{i\tau}-e^{-i\tau}\,}{2}\,h\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}.

Note that
h
is holomorphic and that
{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{\,h+\overline{\,h\,}\,}{2}\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}
. We have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\tau}\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}-\frac{\,e^{i\tau}-e^{-i\tau}\,}{2}\,h\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\left(\,e^{i\tau}\,\frac{\,h+\overline{\,h\,}\,}{2}-\frac{\,e^{i\tau}-e^{-i\tau}\,}{2}\,h\,\right){\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\,\frac{\,e^{-i\tau}h+e^{i\tau}\overline{\,h\,}\,}{2}\,\right){\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\left(\textrm{Re}\,h_{{}_{\tau}}\right){\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}.

Since the domain
\Omega
is simply connected, by Poincaré’s Lemma, the observation

{\mathcal{E}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=\left(\textrm{Re}\,h_{{}_{\tau}}\right){\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\in\mathbb{R}

implies the existence of an

{\mathbb{R}}
-valued function
{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}
defined on
\Omega
such that

{\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}=\mathcal{E}.

It follows that

{\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={\mathcal{E}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=\left(\textrm{Re}\,h_{{}_{\tau}}\right){\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\left(\textrm{Re}\,h_{{}_{\tau}}\right){\left(\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}.

Finally, we deduce the last condition

\displaystyle{\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h_{{}_{\tau}}\right){\left(\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}-\left(\,\frac{\,e^{-i\tau}h+e^{i\tau}\overline{\,h\,}\,}{2}\,\right){\mathcal{N}}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\tau}\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{\,e^{i\tau}-e^{-i\tau}\,}{2}\,h\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}-\left(\,\frac{\,e^{-i\tau}h+e^{i\tau}\overline{\,h\,}\,}{2}\,\right){\mathcal{N}}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\tau}\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\,\right)\neq 0.

∎

Though the first proof of Proposition
4
is self-contained, it does not explain how to discover the integrability condition in Proposition
4
:

{\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\tau}\,\right)}_{z}=e^{i\tau}\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{\,e^{i\tau}-e^{-i\tau}\,}{2}\,h\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}.

We use the equivalence of the Weierstrass data of the first kind and the Weierstrass data of the second kind to discover the integrability condition.

Second Proof of Proposition
4
.

We set
g=\frac{1}{\,h\,}
and
\mathcal{P}=\frac{1}{\,2\,}\mathcal{N}
. According to Theorem
2
, we can
find a
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
function
{\mathcal{Q}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}=h\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{\,h^{2}\,}{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}.

Moreover, the triple
\left(g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\right)
is a Weierstrass data of the second kind:

g_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0,\quad{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={|\,g\,|}^{2}\;{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},\quad{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\neq 0.

We observe that
|\,e^{i\tau}g\,|=|\,g\,|
. We find immediately that the triple

\left({g}_{{}_{\tau}},\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\tau},\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau}\right)=\left(e^{i\tau}g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\right)

is also a Weierstrass data of the first kind:

{\left(\,{g}_{{}_{\tau}}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0,\quad{\left({\mathcal{P}}_{\tau}\right)}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={|\,g\,|}^{2}\;{\left({\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau}\right)}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},\quad{\left({\mathcal{P}}_{\tau}\right)}_{z}-{|\,{g}_{{}_{\tau}}\,|}^{2}\,{\left({\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau}\right)}_{z}\neq 0.

Theorem
1
guarantees the existence of a
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
function
{\mathcal{M}}_{\tau}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

{\left({\mathcal{M}}_{\tau}\right)}_{z}=\frac{1}{\,{g}_{{}_{\tau}}\,}\,{\left({\mathcal{P}}_{\tau}\right)}_{z}+{g}_{{}_{\tau}}\,{\left({\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau}\right)}_{z}

It follows from the definitions
{h}_{{}_{\tau}}=e^{-i\tau}h
,
g=\frac{1}{\,h\,}
, and
{g}_{{}_{\tau}}=e^{i\tau}g
that

{h}_{{}_{\tau}}=\frac{1}{\,{g}_{{}_{\tau}}\,}.

We set

{\mathcal{N}}_{\tau}:={\mathcal{N}}=2{\mathcal{P}}=2{\mathcal{P}}_{\lambda}.

Theorem
1
shows that the triple
\left(h_{{}_{\tau}},\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\tau},\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\tau}\right)
is a Weierstrass data of the second kind.
It now remains to deduce the integrability condition:

\displaystyle{\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\tau}\,\right)}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\,{g}_{{}_{\tau}}\,}\,{\left({\mathcal{P}}_{\tau}\right)}_{z}+{g}_{{}_{\tau}}\,{\left({\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau}\right)}_{z}=e^{-i\tau}h\frac{\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\,}{2}+\frac{e^{i\tau}\,}{h}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\,e^{-i\tau}h\,}{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}+\frac{e^{i\tau}\,}{h}\left(\,h\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{\,h^{2}\,}{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\,\right)=e^{i\tau}\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{\,e^{i\tau}-e^{-i\tau}\,}{2}\,h\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}.

∎

Proposition 5

(
Hyperbolic Deformations in terms of the Weierstrass data of the first kind
)
.

Let the triple
\left(g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\right)
be a Weierstrass data of the first kind. Let
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
be a parameter.
Then, the triple
\left({g}_{{}_{\eta}},\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\eta},\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\eta}\right):=\left(e^{\eta}g,\,e^{\eta}{\mathcal{P}},\,e^{-\eta}\mathcal{Q}\right)
becomes a Weierstrass data of the first kind.

Proof.

The assumption says that the triple
\left(g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\right)
satisfy

g_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0,\quad{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={|\,g\,|}^{2}\;{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},\quad{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\neq 0.

Hence, the triple
\left({g}_{{}_{\eta}},\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\eta},\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\eta}\right)
satisfy

\displaystyle{\left(\,{g}_{{}_{\eta}}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}={\left(\,e^{\eta}g\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0,

\displaystyle{\left(\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\eta}\,\right)}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={\left(\,e^{\eta}{\mathcal{P}}\,\right)}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=e^{\eta}{|\,g\,|}^{2}\;{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={|\,e^{\eta}g\,|}^{2}\,e^{-\eta}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={|\,{g}_{{}_{\eta}}|}^{2}\;{\left(\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\eta}\,\right)}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}

\displaystyle{\left(\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\eta}\,\right)}_{z}-{|\,{g}_{{}_{\eta}}|}^{2}{\left(\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\eta}\,\right)}_{z}=e^{\eta}\left(\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\,\right)\neq 0.

∎

3.
Reduction of Liu integrable system

The purpose of this section is to explain one way to discover our integrable systems for marginally trapped surfaces in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
. An algebraic reduction of the following version of the Liu
integrable system
[
Liu2013
]
for the triple of a holomorphic function and two
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions yields to our integrable systems for the Weierstrass
triple of a holomorphic function and two
\mathbb{R}
-valued functions. We emphasize that the hidden geometric idea of our algebraic reduction is implicitly contained in our new Weierstrass representations (Theorem
7
, Corollary
8
, and Corollary
9
) for marginally trapped surfaces.

Lemma 6

(
Liu’s integrable system for marginally trapped surfaces in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}

[
Liu2013
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be an open domain in
\mathbb{C}
with the complex coordinate
z
.
We assume that a spacelike surface
\Sigma
in Lorentz-Minkowski space
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
is parameterized by a
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
conformal patch
\mathbf{X}:\Omega\to{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
of the form

{\mathbf{X}}_{z}=\begin{bmatrix}\;{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}\;\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}\end{bmatrix}=\Psi\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}+f_{2}\\[4.0pt]
\;-i\left(f_{1}-f_{2}\right)\;\\[4.0pt]
1-f_{1}f_{2}\\[4.0pt]
1+f_{1}f_{2}\end{bmatrix},

where
\Psi:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}-\{0\}
and
f_{1},f_{2}:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
. Then, the following three integrability conditions hold for all
z\in\Omega
:

(1)

{\Psi}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=\overline{\,{\Psi}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,}
,

(2)

{\left(\,\Psi f_{1}f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=\overline{\,{\left(\,\Psi f_{1}f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,}
,

(3)

{\left(\,\Psi f_{1}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=\overline{\,{\left(\,\Psi f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\overline{\,{\left(\,\Psi f_{1}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,}={\left(\,\Psi f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}
.

If
\Sigma
is marginally trapped, then the following holomorphicity condition holds for each point
z\in\Omega
:

{\left(\,f_{1}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0\quad\text{or}\quad{\left(\,f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0,

Proof.

It follows from
[
Liu2013
, Theorem 4.1 and Theorem 4.2]
. For the reader’s convenience, we sketch a self-contained proof.
We first observe that

\displaystyle\Psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\,{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}+{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}\,}{2},

\displaystyle\Psi f_{1}f_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\,-{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}+{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}\,}{2},

\displaystyle\Psi f_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\,{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}+i{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\,}{2},

\displaystyle\Psi f_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\,{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}-i{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\,}{2}.

(These relations reveal how to prescribe the three functions
\Psi
,
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
in terms of the four component functions of
{\mathbf{X}}_{z}
.)
Since
{\mathbf{x}}_{1}=\overline{\,{\mathbf{x}}_{1}\,}
,
{\mathbf{x}}_{2}=\overline{\,{\mathbf{x}}_{2}\,}
,
{\mathbf{x}}_{3}=\overline{\,{\mathbf{x}}_{3}\,}
,
{\mathbf{x}}_{4}=\overline{\,{\mathbf{x}}_{4}\,}
, we have

\overline{\,{\Psi}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,}=\overline{\,{\left(\,\frac{\,{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}+{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\,}{2}\,\right)}\,}={\left(\,\frac{\,{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}+{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}\,}{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}={\Psi}_{\overline{\,z\,}}

and

\overline{\,{\left(\,\Psi f_{1}f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,}=\overline{\,{\left(\,\frac{\,-{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}+{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\,}{2}\,\right)}\,}={\left(\,\frac{\,-{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}+{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}\,}{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}={\left(\,\Psi f_{1}f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}.

We also have

\displaystyle\overline{\,{\left(\,\Psi f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\,{\left(\,\frac{\,{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}-i{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\,}{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,}={\left(\,\frac{\,{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}+i{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,}{2}\,\right)}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left(\,\frac{\,{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}+i{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\,}{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}={\left(\,\Psi f_{1}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}.

Now, we assume that the spacelike surface
\Sigma
with the mean curvature vector
\mathbf{H}
is marginally trapped. Since
\mathbf{X}
is a conformal patch, we find that
the null condition

\langle\mathbf{H},\,\mathbf{H}\rangle=0

is equivalent to

\langle{\mathbf{X}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},\,{\mathbf{X}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\rangle=0.

We differentiate both sides of

{\mathbf{X}}_{z}=\Psi{\mathbf{F}}_{0},\quad\text{where}\quad{\mathbf{F}}_{0}:=\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}+f_{2}\\
\;-i\left(f_{1}-f_{2}\right)\;\\
1-f_{1}f_{2}\\
1+f_{1}f_{2}\end{bmatrix},

with respect to
\overline{\,z\,}
to obtain the decomposition

{\mathbf{X}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={\Psi}_{\overline{\,z\,}}{\mathbf{F}}_{0}+\Psi{\left(\,f_{1}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}{\mathbf{F}}_{2}+\Psi{\left(\,f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}{\mathbf{F}}_{1},

where we define

{\mathbf{F}}_{2}:=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
\;-i\;\\
-f_{2}\\
f_{2}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathbf{F}}_{1}:=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
i\;\\
-f_{1}\\
f_{1}\end{bmatrix}.

A straightforward computation shows that

\begin{cases}\langle{\mathbf{F}}_{1},\,{\mathbf{F}}_{2}\rangle=2,\\
\langle{\mathbf{F}}_{i},\,{\mathbf{F}}_{j}\rangle=0\;\text{for}\;\left(i,j\right)\neq\left(1,2\right),\left(2,1\right).\end{cases}

It follows that

0=\langle{\mathbf{X}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},\,{\mathbf{X}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\rangle=2{\Psi}^{2}{\left(\,f_{1}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}{\left(\,f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,\langle{\mathbf{F}}_{1},\,{\mathbf{F}}_{2}\rangle=4{\Psi}^{2}{\left(\,f_{1}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}{\left(\,f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}.

We recall the assumption
{\Psi}(z)\neq 0
for all
z\in\Omega
. We conclude that
{\left(\,f_{1}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0
or

{\left(\,f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0
at each point
z\in\Omega
. This completes the proof.
∎

It remains to explain how to use the Liu integrable system for the marginally trapped surface

(1)

{\Psi}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=\overline{\,{\Psi}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,}
,

(2)

{\left(\,\Psi f_{1}f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=\overline{\,{\left(\,\Psi f_{1}f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,}
,

(3)

{\left(\,\Psi f_{1}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=\overline{\,{\left(\,\Psi f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\overline{\,{\left(\,\Psi f_{1}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,}={\left(\,\Psi f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}
,

(4)

{\left(\,f_{1}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,{\left(\,f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0
.

to discover the key equations in our integrable system in Definition
2.2
:

(1)

{h}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0
,

(2)

{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}
,

which were essential in our second Weierstrass representation in Corollary
8
.
We keep the notations in Lemma
6
and consider the case
{\left(\,f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\equiv 0
. We have

\Psi f_{1}=\frac{\,{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}+i{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\,}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi f_{2}=\frac{\,{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}-i{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\,}{2}.\\

We make an additional assumption that
the function
\Psi=\frac{\,{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}+{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}\,}{2}
never vanish on
\Omega
. (This assumption will be not required in
our second Weierstrass representation in Corollary
8
.) We define the three functions

{\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{N}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
and
h:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
by

\quad{\mathcal{M}}:={\mathbf{x}}_{1},\quad{\mathcal{N}}:={\mathbf{x}}_{3}+{\mathbf{x}}_{4},\quad h:=f_{2}=\frac{\,\Psi f_{2}\,}{\Psi}=\frac{\,{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}-i{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\,}{\,{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}+{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}\,}=\frac{\,{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}-i{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\,}{\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\,}.

We note that
h=f_{2}
is holomorphic in
\Omega
and that
{\mathcal{N}}=\overline{\,{\mathcal{N}}\,}
. We observe

\displaystyle 2\Psi f_{2}={\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}-i{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{z}h,

\displaystyle-i{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}+\left(\,{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}-i{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\,\right)=-{\mathcal{M}}_{z}+{\mathcal{N}}_{z}h,

\displaystyle 2\Psi f_{1}={\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}+i{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(-{\mathcal{M}}_{z}+{\mathcal{N}}_{z}h\right)=2{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-{\mathcal{N}}_{z}h.

We conclude that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left(\,\Psi f_{1}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}-\overline{\,{\left(\,\Psi f_{2}\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{1}{\,2\,}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}h\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}-\overline{\,{\left(\,\frac{1}{\,2\,}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}h\,\right)}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}-\frac{1}{\,2\,}{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}h-\frac{1}{\,2\,}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}h_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,\right)-\overline{\,{\left(\,\frac{1}{\,2\,}{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}h+\frac{1}{\,2\,}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}h_{\overline{\,z\,}}\,\right)}\,}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}-\textrm{Re}\,\left(h_{\overline{\,z\,}}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}.

This completes the desired reduction.

.

4.
Three Weierstrass representations

The
4
-dimensional Lorentz-Minkowski space
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
is the real vector space
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
equipped with the Lorentzian metric

\langle\;,\;\rangle={dx_{1}}^{2}+{dx_{2}}^{2}+{dx_{3}}^{2}-{dx_{4}}^{2},

where
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}
denotes the canonical coordinates in
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
. The standard complexificiation of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
induces
the symmetric bilinear form on
{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
.

The purpose of this section is to present various conformal representation for marginally trapped surfaces in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
. A spacelike surface in Lorentz-Minkowski space
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
is called a marginally trapped surface if its mean curvature vector
\mathbf{H}
satisfies the null condition:

\langle\mathbf{H},\mathbf{H}\rangle=0.

Theorem 7

(
First Weierstrass representation for marginally trapped surfaces in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\equiv\mathbb{C}
be a simply connected domain with the complex coordinate
z
.
Let the triple
\left(g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\right)
be a Weierstrass data of the first kind.
The holomorphic function
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}-\left\{0\right\}
and two
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
functions

\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy the equation

{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={|\,g\,|}^{2}\;{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},

and the condition

{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\neq{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}.

Then, there exists a conformal parameterization
\mathbf{X}={\mathbf{X}}_{\left[\,g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\,\right]}:\Omega\to{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
of the marginally trapped surface

\Sigma=\mathbf{X}\left(\Omega\right)
in Lorentz-Minkowski space
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
satisfying the following two equalities:

{\mathbf{X}}_{z}=\begin{bmatrix}\;{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}\;\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}\end{bmatrix}={\mathcal{P}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\,g\,}\\[4.0pt]
\;\frac{i}{\,g\,}\;\\[4.0pt]
1\\[4.0pt]
1\end{bmatrix}+{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}g\\[4.0pt]
-ig\\[4.0pt]
-1\\[4.0pt]
1\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathbf{X}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\begin{bmatrix}2\,\textrm{Re}\,g\\[4.0pt]
2\,\textrm{Im}\,g\\[4.0pt]
-1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\\[4.0pt]
1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\end{bmatrix}.

In particular, we have
{\mathbf{x}}_{3}(z)=\mathcal{P}(z)-\mathcal{Q}(z)
and
{\mathbf{x}}_{4}(z)=\mathcal{P}(z)+\mathcal{Q}(z)
, up to additive constants.
The Gauss map
{\mathcal{N}}
of the marginally trapped surface
\Sigma
defined by

{\mathcal{N}}:=\begin{bmatrix}2\,\textrm{Re}\,g\\[4.0pt]
2\,\textrm{Im}\,g\\[4.0pt]
-1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\\[4.0pt]
1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\end{bmatrix}

satisfies the orthogonality conditions
\langle{\mathbf{X}}_{z},{\mathcal{N}}\rangle=0
and
\langle{\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{N}}\rangle=0
. (In particular,
\mathcal{N}
is
null.) The conformal metric induced by
the patch
{\mathbf{X}}(z)
is

{{ds}_{{}_{\Sigma}}}^{2}=\frac{4}{\,{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}\,{\left|\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\,\right|}^{2}\,{|\,dz\,|}^{2}.

Proof.

The vector
{\mathcal{N}}
is null:

\langle{\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{N}}\rangle={\left(\,2\,\textrm{Re}\,g\,\right)}^{2}+{\left(\,2\,\textrm{Im}\,g\,\right)}^{2}+{\left(\,-1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,\right)}^{2}-{\left(\,1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,\right)}^{2}=0.

We introduce the mappings
{\mathcal{E}},{\mathbf{G}}_{1},{\mathbf{G}}_{2}
defined on the domain
\Omega
:

{\mathbf{G}}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\,g\,}\\[4.0pt]
\;\frac{i}{\,g\,}\;\\[4.0pt]
1\\[4.0pt]
1\end{bmatrix},\;\;{\mathbf{G}}_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}g\\[4.0pt]
-ig\\[4.0pt]
-1\\[4.0pt]
1\end{bmatrix},\;\;{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{P}}_{z}{\mathbf{G}}_{1}+{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}{\mathbf{G}}_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}.

We note that
{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\mathcal{N}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
.
We use the holomorphicity of
g
and the assumption
{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=g\overline{\,g\,}\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}
to find that
the mapping
{\mathcal{E}}_{F}
is
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
-valued. Indeed, we have

{\mathcal{E}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}={\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\,g\,}\\[4.0pt]
\;\frac{i}{\,g\,}\;\\[4.0pt]
1\\[4.0pt]
1\end{bmatrix}+{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\begin{bmatrix}g\\[4.0pt]
-ig\\[4.0pt]
-1\\[4.0pt]
1\end{bmatrix}={\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\begin{bmatrix}2\,\textrm{Re}\,g\\[4.0pt]
2\,\textrm{Im}\,g\\[4.0pt]
-1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\\[4.0pt]
1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\end{bmatrix}={\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}{\mathcal{N}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}.

Since the domain
\Omega
is simply connected, by Poincaré’s Lemma, the observation
{\mathcal{E}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
implies the existence of an

{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
-valued mapping
\mathbf{X}
defined on
\Omega
such that

\begin{bmatrix}\;{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}\;\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}\end{bmatrix}={\mathbf{X}}_{z}={\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{P}}_{z}{\mathbf{G}}_{1}+{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}{\mathbf{G}}_{2}={\mathcal{P}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\,g\,}\\[4.0pt]
\;\frac{i}{\,g\,}\;\\[4.0pt]
1\\[4.0pt]
1\end{bmatrix}+{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}g\\[4.0pt]
-ig\\[4.0pt]
-1\\[4.0pt]
1\end{bmatrix}.

It follows from the equalities
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}={\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}
and
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}={\mathcal{P}}_{z}+{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}
that

{\mathbf{x}}_{3}=\mathcal{P}-\mathcal{Q}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathbf{x}}_{4}=\mathcal{P}+\mathcal{Q},

up to additive constants.
The conformality of the mapping
z\mapsto{\mathbf{X}}(z)
follows from
\langle{\mathbf{X}}_{z},{\mathbf{X}}_{z}\rangle=0
. Indeed, we have

\langle{\mathbf{X}}_{z},{\mathbf{X}}_{z}\rangle={{\mathcal{P}}_{z}}^{2}\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{1},{\mathbf{G}}_{1}\rangle+2{\mathcal{P}}_{z}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{1},{\mathbf{G}}_{2}\rangle+{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{2},{\mathbf{G}}_{2}\rangle=0.

It is straightforward to check the identities

\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{1},{\mathbf{G}}_{1}\rangle={\left(\frac{1}{\,g\,}\right)}^{2}+{\left(\frac{i}{\,g\,}\right)}^{2}+1-1=0,\;\;\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{1},{\mathbf{G}}_{2}\rangle=0,\;\;\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{2},{\mathbf{G}}_{2}\rangle=0,

\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{1},\overline{\,{\mathbf{G}}_{1}\,}\rangle=\frac{2}{\,{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,},\;\;\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{1},\overline{\,{\mathbf{G}}_{2}\,}\rangle=-2,\;\;\langle\overline{\,{\mathbf{G}}_{1}\,},{\mathbf{G}}_{2}\rangle=-2,\;\;\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{2},\overline{\,{\mathbf{G}}_{2}\,}\rangle=2\,{|\,g\,|}^{2}.

Since
\mathbf{X}
is
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
-valued, we have
{\mathbf{X}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}=\overline{\,{\mathbf{X}}_{z}\,}
. To find the conformal metric
{{ds}_{{}_{\Sigma}}}^{2}={\Lambda(z)}\,{|\,dz\,|}^{2}
induced by the patch
{\mathbf{X}}
, we compute the conformal factor
{\Lambda}=2\langle{\mathbf{X}}_{z},{\mathbf{X}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\rangle
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\,2\,}{\Lambda(z)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{X}}_{z},{\mathbf{X}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}\rangle=\langle{\mathbf{X}}_{z},\overline{\,{\mathbf{X}}_{z}\,}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{P}}_{z}{\mathbf{G}}_{1}+{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}{\mathbf{G}}_{2},\overline{\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\,}\,\overline{\,{\mathbf{G}}_{1}\,}+\overline{\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}}\,\overline{\,{\mathbf{G}}_{2}\,}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\,|}^{2}\,\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{1},\overline{\,{\mathbf{G}}_{1}\,}\rangle+{|\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\,|}^{2}\,\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{2},\overline{\,{\mathbf{G}}_{2}\,}\rangle+{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\overline{\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\,}\,\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{1},\overline{\,{\mathbf{G}}_{2}\,}\rangle+\overline{\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\,}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\,\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{1},\overline{\,{\mathbf{G}}_{2}\,}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\,|}^{2}\,\left(\,\frac{2}{\,{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}\,\right)+{|\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\,|}^{2}\,\left(\,2\,{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,\right)+{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\overline{\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\,}\left(\,-2\,\right)+\overline{\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\,}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\left(\,-2\,\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,{\left|\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\frac{1}{\,g\,}-{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\overline{\,g\,}\,\right|}^{2}=\frac{2}{\,{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}\,{\left|\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\,\right|}^{2}>0.

It follows that

{{ds}_{{}_{\Sigma}}}^{2}={\Lambda(z)}\,{|\,dz\,|}^{2}=\frac{4}{\,{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}\,{\left|\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\,\right|}^{2}\,{|\,dz\,|}^{2}

We recall that
{\mathbf{X}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={\mathcal{E}}_{\overline{\,z\,}}={\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}{\mathcal{N}}
.
Since the vector
\mathcal{N}
is null, we find that the mean curvature vector
\mathbf{H}
is also null:

\mathbf{H}:={\triangle}_{{{ds}_{{}_{\Sigma}}}^{2}}\mathbf{X}={\triangle}_{{\Lambda(z)}{|dz|}^{2}}\mathbf{X}=\frac{4}{\,{\Lambda(z)}\,}{\mathbf{X}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{4}{\,{\Lambda(z)}\,}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}{\mathcal{N}}

It remains to verify that
{\mathcal{N}}
is the Gauss map in the sense that
\langle{\mathbf{X}}_{z},{\mathcal{N}}\rangle=0
. We observe that

\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{N}}\rangle=\left\langle\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\,g\,}\\[4.0pt]
\;\frac{i}{\,g\,}\;\\[4.0pt]
1\\[4.0pt]
1\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}2\,\textrm{Re}\,g\\[4.0pt]
2\,\textrm{Im}\,g\\[4.0pt]
-1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\\[4.0pt]
1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle=2\frac{\textrm{Re}\,g+i\textrm{Im}\,g}{g}+\left(\,-1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,\right)-\left(\,1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,\right)=0,

and that

\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{2},{\mathcal{N}}\rangle=\left\langle\begin{bmatrix}g\\[4.0pt]
-ig\\[4.0pt]
-1\\[4.0pt]
1\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}2\,\textrm{Re}\,g\\[4.0pt]
2\,\textrm{Im}\,g\\[4.0pt]
-1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\\[4.0pt]
1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle=2g\left(\textrm{Re}\,g-i\,\textrm{Im}\,g\right)-\left(\,-1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,\right)-\left(\,1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,\right)=0.

It is immediate that

\langle{\mathbf{X}}_{z},{\mathcal{N}}\rangle=\langle{\mathcal{P}}_{z}{\mathbf{G}}_{1}+{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}{\mathbf{G}}_{2},{\mathcal{N}}\rangle={\mathcal{P}}_{z}\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{1},{\mathcal{N}}\rangle+{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\langle{\mathbf{G}}_{2},{\mathcal{N}}\rangle=0.

∎

Corollary 8

(
Second Weierstrass representation for marginally trapped surfaces in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\equiv\mathbb{C}
be a simply connected domain with the complex coordinate
z
.
Let the triple
\left(h,\,\mathcal{M},\,\mathcal{N}\right)
be a Weierstrass data of the second kind
The holomorphic function
h:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}-\left\{0\right\}
and two
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
functions

\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy the equation

{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},

and the condition

{\mathcal{M}}_{z}\neq\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}.

Then, there exists a conformal parameterization
\mathbf{X}={\mathbf{X}}_{\left(\,h,\,\mathcal{M},\,\mathcal{N}\,\right)}:\Omega\to{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
of the marginally trapped surface

\Sigma=\mathbf{X}\left(\Omega\right)
in Lorentz-Minkowski space
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
satisfying

{\mathbf{X}}_{z}=\begin{bmatrix}\;{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}\;\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}\end{bmatrix}={\mathcal{M}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}1\\[4.0pt]
-i\\[4.0pt]
-h\\[4.0pt]
h\end{bmatrix}+{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}0\\[4.0pt]
ih\\[4.0pt]
\frac{1}{\,2\,}\left(1+h^{2}\right)\\[4.0pt]
\frac{1}{\,2\,}\left(1-h^{2}\right)\end{bmatrix}.

In particular, we have
{{\mathbf{x}}_{1}}(z)=\mathcal{M}(z)
and
{\mathbf{x}}_{3}(z)+{\mathbf{x}}_{4}(z)=\mathcal{N}(z)
, up to additive constants.
The conformal metric induced by the patch
{\mathbf{X}}(z)
is

{{ds}_{{}_{\Sigma}}}^{2}=4\,{|\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\,|}^{2}\,{|dz|}^{2}.

Proof.

Using the conditions
h_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{\,h+\overline{\,h\,}\,}{2}\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}
, we deduce

\frac{\partial}{\partial{\overline{\,z\,}}}\left(\,h\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{\,h^{2}\,}{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\,\right)=h\left(\,\frac{\,h+\overline{\,h\,}\,}{2}\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\,\right)-\frac{\,h^{2}\,}{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{\,{|\,h\,|}^{2}\,}{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\in\mathbb{R}.

Since the domain
\Omega
is simply connected, by Poincaré’s Lemma, this implies the existence of an

{\mathbb{R}}
-valued mapping
\mathcal{Q}
defined on
\Omega
satisfying the equalities

{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}=h\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{\,h^{2}\,}{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{\,{|\,h\,|}^{2}\,}{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}.

We define
{\mathcal{P}}:=\frac{1}{\,2\,}{\mathcal{N}}
and
g:=\frac{1}{\,h\,}
. Since
h:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}-\left\{0\right\}
is holomorphic,

g:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}-\left\{0\right\}
is also holomorphic. We deduce remaining conditions in Theorem
7
:

{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{1}{\,2\,}{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{1}{\,{|\,h\,|}^{2}\,}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}={|\,g\,|}^{2}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},

and

{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}=\frac{1}{\,2\,}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}-\frac{1}{\,h\overline{\,h\,}\,}\left(\,h\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\frac{\,h^{2}\,}{2}{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\,\right)=-\frac{1}{\,\overline{\,h\,}\,}\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\,\right)\neq 0.

Taking the triple
\left(g,{\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{Q}}\right)
in Theorem
7
yields the existence of

\mathbf{X}={\mathbf{X}}_{\left[\,g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\,\right]}:\Omega\to{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
of the marginally trapped surface

\Sigma=\mathbf{X}\left(\Omega\right)
in Lorentz-Minkowski space
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
satisfying

\begin{bmatrix}\;{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}\;\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}\end{bmatrix}={\mathbf{X}}_{z}={\mathcal{P}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\,g\,}\\[4.0pt]
\;\frac{i}{\,g\,}\;\\[4.0pt]
1\\[4.0pt]
1\end{bmatrix}+{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}g\\[4.0pt]
-ig\\[4.0pt]
-1\\
1\end{bmatrix}={\mathcal{M}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}1\\[4.0pt]
-i\\[4.0pt]
-h\\[4.0pt]
h\end{bmatrix}+{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}0\\[4.0pt]
ih\\[4.0pt]
\frac{1}{\,2\,}\left(1+h^{2}\right)\\[4.0pt]
\frac{1}{\,2\,}\left(1-h^{2}\right)\end{bmatrix}.

It follows from the equalities
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}={\mathcal{M}}_{z}
and
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}+{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}={\mathcal{N}}_{z}
that

{\mathbf{x}}_{1}(z)=\mathcal{M}(z)\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathbf{x}}_{3}(z)+{\mathbf{x}}_{4}(z)=\mathcal{N}(z),

up to additive constants.
We recall that
g=\frac{1}{\,h\,}
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}=-\frac{1}{\,\overline{\,h\,}\,}\left(\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\,\right)
.
We use Theorem
7
to compute the conformal metric induced by the patch
{\mathbf{X}}(z)
:

{{ds}_{{}_{\Sigma}}}^{2}=\frac{4}{\,{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}\,{\left|\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\,\right|}^{2}\,{|\,dz\,|}^{2}=4\,{|\,{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\,|}^{2}\,{|dz|}^{2}.

∎

Corollary 9

(
Third Weierstrass representation for marginally trapped surfaces in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\equiv\mathbb{C}
be a simply connected domain with the complex coordinate
z
.
Given a holomorphic function

, we consider two
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
functions

\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the equation

{\mathcal{A}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{\,-1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}{\,1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}\;{\mathcal{B}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},

and the condition

{\mathcal{A}}_{z}\neq\frac{\,-1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}{\,1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}\;{\mathcal{B}}_{z}.

Then, there exists a conformal parameterization
\mathbf{X}={\mathbf{X}}_{\left\{\,g,\,\mathcal{A},\,\mathcal{B}\,\right\}}:\Omega\to{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
of the marginally trapped surface

\Sigma=\mathbf{X}\left(\Omega\right)
in Lorentz-Minkowski space
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
satisfying

{\mathbf{X}}_{z}=\begin{bmatrix}\;{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}\;\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}\end{bmatrix}={\mathcal{A}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\,2\,}\left(\frac{1}{\,g\,}-g\right)\\
\frac{i}{\,2\,}\left(\frac{1}{\,g\,}+g\right)\\
1\\
0\end{bmatrix}+{\mathcal{B}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\,2\,}\left(\frac{1}{\,g\,}+g\right)\\
\frac{i}{\,2\,}\left(\frac{1}{\,g\,}-g\right)\\
0\\
1\end{bmatrix}.

We have
{{\mathbf{x}}_{3}}(z)=\mathcal{A}(z)
and
{\mathbf{x}}_{4}(z)=\mathcal{B}(z)
, up to additive constants.
The Gauss map
{\mathcal{N}}
of the marginally trapped surface
\Sigma
defined by

{\mathcal{N}}:=\begin{bmatrix}2\,\textrm{Re}\,g\\[4.0pt]
2\,\textrm{Im}\,g\\[4.0pt]
-1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\\[4.0pt]
1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\end{bmatrix}

satisfies the orthogonality condition
\langle{\mathbf{X}}_{z},{\mathcal{N}}\rangle=0
. The conformal metric induced by the patch
{\mathbf{X}}(z)
is

{{ds}_{{}_{\Sigma}}}^{2}={\left(\,|\,g\,|+\frac{1}{|\,g\,|}\,\right)}^{2}\,{\left|\,{\mathcal{A}}_{z}-\frac{\,-1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}{\,1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}\;{\mathcal{B}}_{z}\,\right|}^{2}\,{|\,dz\,|}^{2}

Proof.

We take
\left(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}\right)=\left(\frac{\,\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\,}{2},\frac{\,-\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\,}{2}\right)
. We obtain

{\mathcal{P}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}\;{\mathcal{Q}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\,\left(1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\right){\mathcal{A}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}-\left(-1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\right){\mathcal{B}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\right)=0,

and

{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\,\left(1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\right){\mathcal{A}}_{z}-\left(-1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\right){\mathcal{B}}_{z}\right)\neq 0.

Taking the triple
\left(g,\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}\right)=\left(g,\frac{\,\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\,}{2},\frac{\,-\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\,}{2}\right)
in
Theorem
7
yields the existence of
{\mathbf{X}}_{\left[\,g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\,\right]}:\Omega\to{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
of the marginally trapped surface

\Sigma={\mathbf{X}}_{\left[\,g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\,\right]}\left(\Omega\right)
in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
satisfying

\frac{\partial}{\,\partial z\,}{{\mathbf{X}}_{\left[\,g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\,\right]}}={\mathcal{P}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\,g\,}\\[4.0pt]
\;\frac{i}{\,g\,}\;\\[4.0pt]
1\\[4.0pt]
1\end{bmatrix}+{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}g\\[4.0pt]
-ig\\[4.0pt]
-1\\[4.0pt]
1\end{bmatrix}.

Setting
{\mathbf{X}}_{\left\{\,g,\,\mathcal{A},\,\mathcal{B}\,\right\}}(z):={\mathbf{X}}_{\left[\,g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\,\right]}(z)
and using
\left({\mathcal{P}}_{z},{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\right)=\left(\frac{\,{\mathcal{A}}_{z}+{\mathcal{B}}_{z}\,}{2},\frac{\,-{\mathcal{A}}_{z}+{\mathcal{B}}_{z}\,}{2}\right)
, we have

\begin{bmatrix}\;{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right)}_{z}\;\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{2}\right)}_{z}\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}\\[4.0pt]
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}\end{bmatrix}=\frac{\partial}{\,\partial z\,}{\mathbf{X}}_{\left\{\,g,\,\mathcal{A},\,\mathcal{B}\,\right\}}=\frac{\partial}{\,\partial z\,}{{\mathbf{X}}_{\left[\,g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\,\right]}}={\mathcal{A}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\,2\,}\left(\frac{1}{\,g\,}-g\right)\\
\frac{i}{\,2\,}\left(\frac{1}{\,g\,}+g\right)\\
1\\
0\end{bmatrix}+{\mathcal{B}}_{z}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\,2\,}\left(\frac{1}{\,g\,}+g\right)\\
\frac{i}{\,2\,}\left(\frac{1}{\,g\,}-g\right)\\
0\\
1\end{bmatrix}.

It follows from the equalities
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{3}\right)}_{z}={\mathcal{A}}_{z}
and
{\left({\mathbf{x}}_{4}\right)}_{z}={\mathcal{B}}_{z}
that

{\mathbf{x}}_{3}(z)=\mathcal{A}(z)\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathbf{x}}_{4}(z)=\mathcal{B}(z),

up to additive constants. It follow from Theorem
7
that

{{ds}_{{}_{\Sigma}}}^{2}=\frac{4}{\,{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}\,{\left|\,{\mathcal{P}}_{z}-{|\,g\,|}^{2}{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}\,\right|}^{2}\,{|\,dz\,|}^{2}={\left(\,|\,g\,|+\frac{1}{|\,g\,|}\,\right)}^{2}\,{\left|\,{\mathcal{A}}_{z}-\frac{\,-1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}{\,1+{|\,g\,|}^{2}\,}\;{\mathcal{B}}_{z}\,\right|}^{2}\,{|\,dz\,|}^{2}.

∎

Proposition 10

(
Geometric meaning of the parameter
\lambda
in the parabolic deformation in Proposition
3
)
.

Let the triple
\left(g,\,\mathcal{P},\,\mathcal{Q}\right)
be a Weierstrass data of the first kind. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
be a parameter constant
such that
g(z)\neq-\frac{1}{\,i\lambda\,}
for all
z\in\Omega
. We set
g_{{}_{\lambda}}:=\frac{g}{\,1+i\lambda g\,}
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{\lambda}:=\mathcal{P}
.
Then, the following statements hold:

(1)

There exists a
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
function
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the integrability condition

{\left(\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}\,\right)}_{z}=\left(\,\frac{1}{\,g\,}+i\lambda\,\right)\left(g{\mathcal{Q}}_{z}-i\lambda{\mathcal{P}}_{z}\right).

Moreover, the triple
\left(g_{{}_{\lambda}},\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\lambda},\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{\lambda}\right)
becomes a Weierstrass data of the first kind.

(2)

The marginally trapped surface

6.
Examples of marginally trapped surfaces

We use our integrable systems and Weierstrass representations to construct explicit examples of the marginally trapped surface in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
with nowhere vanishing mean curvature vector. We adopt the complex coordinate
z=u+iv
with
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
. To use Corollary
8
, we need to prepare the Weierstrass triple
\left(h,\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\right)
of the second kind, which solves the system

\begin{cases}h_{\overline{\,z\,}}=0,\\
{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}=\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}},\\
{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}\neq 0.\end{cases}

We take the normalization with the nowhere vanishing holomorphic function

h(z)=e^{iz}=e^{-v}\left(\cos u+i\sin u\right)\neq 0.

Then, we need to find a pair
\left(\mathcal{M}(u,v),\,\mathcal{N}(u,v)\right)
of
\mathbb{R}
-valued functions solving the Poisson equation of the form

{\mathcal{M}}_{uu}+{\mathcal{M}}_{vv}=e^{-v}\cos u\left({\mathcal{N}}_{uu}+{\mathcal{N}}_{vv}\right).

We observe that this
linear
equation admits the following two independent solutions:

(6.1)

\displaystyle\left({\mathcal{M}}_{1}(u,v),\,{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(u,v)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sinh u\sin u,\,e^{v}\cosh u\right),

(6.2)

\displaystyle\left({\mathcal{M}}_{2}(u,v),\,{\mathcal{N}}_{2}(u,v)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-\cos u\cos v,\,e^{v}\sin v\right).

Using these two solutions and the linear structure of the above Poisson equation, we construct families of marginally trapped surfaces with nowhere vanishing mean curvature vector.

Example 6.1

(
A family of marginally trapped surfaces in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
connecting from a CMC-
1
surface in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
to a CMC-
1
surface in
{\mathbb{S}}^{3}_{1}
)
.

We construct a one parameter family
{\left\{{\Sigma}_{\theta}\right\}}_{\theta\in\left[0,\frac{\,\pi\,}{2}\right]}
of marginally trapped surfaces with nowhere vanishing mean curvature vector field. Moreover, the surface
{\Sigma}_{\theta}
lies in the hypersurface in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
:

{{x}_{1}}^{2}+{{x}_{2}}^{2}+{{x}_{3}}^{2}-{{x}_{4}}^{2}=-\cos\left(2\theta\right).

Given
\theta\in\left[0,\frac{\,\pi\,}{2}\right]
, we choose an open domain
{\Omega}_{\theta}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

satisfying the condition

\cos\theta\cosh u+\sin\theta\cos v\neq 0,

for all
\left(u,v\right)\in{\Omega}_{\theta}
. For instance, if
\theta\in\left[0,\frac{\,\pi\,}{4}\right)
we take

For
\theta=\frac{\,\pi\,}{4}
, we take

{\Omega}_{\frac{\,\pi\,}{4}}=\left\{\,\left(u,v\right)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\;|\;u>0,\;v\in\left(-\pi,\pi\right)\,\right\}.

For
\theta=\frac{\,\pi\,}{2}
, we take

{\Omega}_{\frac{\,\pi\,}{2}}=\left\{\,\left(u,v\right)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\;|\;u\in\mathbb{R},\;v\in\left(-\frac{\,\pi\,}{2},\frac{\,\pi\,}{2}\right)\,\right\}.

Given
\theta\in\left[0,\frac{\,\pi\,}{2}\right]
, we define the triple
\left({h}_{\theta},\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta},\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}\right)
by

\displaystyle{h}_{\theta}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{iz}=e^{-v}\left(\cos u+i\sin u\right)\neq 0,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\theta\sinh u\sin u-\sin\theta\cos u\cos v,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{e}^{v}\left(\cos\theta\cosh u+\beta\sin v\right).

The triple
\left(h,\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\right)=\left({h}_{\theta},\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta},\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}\right)

\displaystyle h_{\overline{\,z\,}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle 4{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{v}\left(2\cos\theta\cosh u+\sin\theta\cos v\right),

\displaystyle 4{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos u\left(2\cos\theta\cosh u+\sin\theta\cos v\right)=\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,\left(4{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\right),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}

to the triple

in

\cosh u\sinh v\\
\cosh u\cosh v\end{bmatrix}+\frac{1}{\,\sqrt{\,2\,}\,}\begin{bmatrix}-\cos u\cos v\\
\sin u\cos v\\
\cosh v\sin v\\
\sinh v\sin v\end{bmatrix},\quad(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}\times\left(-\frac{\,\pi\,}{2},\frac{\,\pi\,}{2}\right).

The surface
{\Sigma}_{\frac{\,\pi\,}{4}}
is null in the sense that it lies in the light cone
{{x}_{1}}^{2}+{{x}_{2}}^{2}+{{x}_{3}}^{2}-{{x}_{4}}^{2}=0
.

Example 6.2

(
Two parameter deformations of a catenoid cousin in
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
to marginally trapped surfaces
in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
)
.

We take
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\equiv\mathbb{C}
and the complex coordinate.
We produce a two parameter family of marginally trapped surfaces with nowhere vanishing mean curvature vector field.
Given a pair
\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
of real constants such that

{\alpha}^{2}+{\beta}^{2}<1
, we define the triple
\left({h}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)},\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)},\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\right)
by

\displaystyle{h}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{iz}=e^{-v}\left(\cos u+i\sin u\right)\neq 0,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha u+\beta v+\sinh u\sin u,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{e}^{v}\cosh u.

The triple
\left(h,\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\right)=\left({h}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)},\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)},\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\right)
is a Weierstrass data of the second kind:

\displaystyle h_{\overline{\,z\,}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle 4{\mathcal{M}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\cos u\cosh u=\left(e^{-v}\cos u\right)\left(2e^{v}\cosh u\right)=\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,\left(4{\mathcal{N}}_{z\overline{\,z\,}}\right),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\alpha+\cosh u\sin u\right)+i\left(-\beta+\cosh u\cos u\right)\neq 0.

The proof of the last statement is given as follows. Assume to the contrary that there exists a pair
\left(u,v\right)
of real numbers such that

{\mathcal{M}}_{z}-\left(\textrm{Re}\,h\right)\,{\mathcal{N}}_{z}=0
, or equivalently, that

\cosh u\sin u=-\alpha\quad\text{and}\quad\cosh u\cos u=\beta.

Combining the assumption
a^{2}+b^{2}<1
and these two conditions yields

1>{\alpha}^{2}+{\beta}^{2}={\left(\cosh u\sin u\right)}^{2}+{\left(\cosh u\cos u\right)}^{2}={\cosh}^{2}u\geq 1,

which is a contradiction. Applying Corollary
8
to the triple
\left(h,\,\mathcal{M},\,\mathcal{N}\right)
, we can find
a conformal patch

{\mathbf{X}}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}:={\mathbf{X}}_{\left(\,h,\,{\mathcal{M}},\,{\mathcal{N}}\,\right)}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
of the marginally trapped surface

{\Sigma}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}
in
{\mathbb{L}}^{4}
:

{\mathbf{X}}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}(u,v)=\alpha\begin{bmatrix}u\\
v\\
-e^{-v}\sin u\\
e^{-v}\sin u\end{bmatrix}+\beta\begin{bmatrix}v\\
-u\\
e^{-v}\cos u\\
-e^{-v}\cos u\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\sinh u\sin u\\
\sinh u\cos u\\
\cosh u\sinh v\\
\cosh u\cosh v\end{bmatrix}.

The metric of the spacelike surface
{\Sigma}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}
induced by the patch
{\mathbf{X}}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}
is

{\Lambda}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}(z){|dz|}^{2}=\left(\,{\left(\alpha+\cosh u\sin u\right)}^{2}+{\left(-\beta+\cosh u\cos u\right)}^{2}\,\right)\left(\,du^{2}+dv^{2}\,\right).

Finally, we claim that the mean curvature vector
\mathbf{H}
vanishes nowhere on the surface
{\Sigma}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}
:

\mathbf{H}={\triangle}_{{\Lambda}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}(z){|dz|}^{2}}{\mathbf{X}}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}=\frac{1}{\,{\Lambda}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}(z)\,}\left(\,{\left(\,{\mathbf{X}}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\,\right)}_{uu}+{\left(\,{\mathbf{X}}_{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\,\right)}_{vv}\,\right).

\;-\sin u\;\\

\cos u
and

can be zero simultaneously.

Continuity of Functional Transducers: A Profinite Study of Rational Functions
Michaël Cadilhac \rsuper a, Olivier Carton \rsuper b, Charles Paperman \rsuper c

Abstract
. A word-to-word function is continuous for a class of languages 𝒱 \mathcal{V} if its inverse maps 𝒱 \mathcal{V} _languages to 𝒱 \mathcal{V} . This notion provides a basis for an algebraic study of transducers, and was integral to the characterization of the sequential transducers computable in some circuit complexity classes. Here, we report on the decidability of continuity for functional transducers and some standard classes of regular languages. To this end, we develop a robust theory rooted in the standard profinite analysis of regular languages. Since previous algebraic studies of transducers have focused on the sole structure of the underlying input automaton, we also compare the two algebraic approaches. We focus on two questions: When are the automaton structure and the continuity properties related, and when does continuity propagate to superclasses?

We build upon the work of Pin and Silva

3.1.
The Preservation Lemma: Continuity is equivalent to preserving
equations

The Preservation Lemma gives us a key characterization in our study: it ties
together continuity and some notion of preservation of equations. This can be
seen as a generalization for functions of the notion of equation satisfaction
for languages. We will need the following technical lemma that extends
[
13
, Proposition VI.3.17]
from morphisms to arbitrary Reg_continuous
functions; interestingly, this relies on a quite different proof.

Lemma 6
.

Let
f\colon A^{*}\to B^{*}
be a Reg_continuous function and
L
a regular
language. The equality
\widehat{f}^{-1}(\overline{L})=\overline{f^{-1}(L)}
holds.

Proof 3.1
.

First note that
f^{-1}(L)\subseteq\widehat{f}^{-1}(\overline{L})
, and that the
right-hand side of this inclusion is closed. Hence

\overline{f^{-1}(L)}\subseteq\widehat{f}^{-1}(\overline{L})
.

For the converse inclusion, first write
D=f^{-1}(B^{*})
, a regular language
by hypothesis. We have that

\widehat{f}^{-1}(\overline{L})=(\widehat{f}^{-1}(\overline{L}^{\text{c}}))^{\text{c}}\cap\overline{D}
,
and similarly,
f^{-1}(L)=(f^{-1}(L^{\text{c}}))^{\text{c}}\cap D
. This latter
equality implies that

\overline{f^{-1}(L)}=\overline{f^{-1}(L^{\text{c}})}^{\text{c}}\cap\overline{D}
, since

f^{-1}(L^{\text{c}})
and
D
are regular.

Hence the inclusion to be shown, that is,

\widehat{f}^{-1}(\overline{L})\subseteq\overline{f^{-1}(L)}
, is equivalent to:

\displaystyle(\widehat{f}^{-1}(\overline{L}^{\text{c}}))^{\text{c}}\cap\overline{D}\subseteq\overline{f^{-1}(L^{\text{c}})}^{\text{c}}\cap\overline{D}\kern 5.0pt,

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\overline{f^{-1}(L^{\text{c}})}\cup\overline{D}^{\text{c}}\subseteq\widehat{f}^{-1}(\overline{L}^{\text{c}})\cup\overline{D}^{\text{c}}\kern 5.0pt.

The inclusion to be shown is thus implied by

\overline{f^{-1}(L^{\text{c}})}\subseteq\widehat{f}^{-1}(\overline{L}^{\text{c}})
, that is, since

L
is regular, by

\overline{f^{-1}(L^{\text{c}})}\subseteq\widehat{f}^{-1}(\overline{L^{\text{c}}})
. As in the
proof of the converse inclusion, the right-hand side being closed, this
inclusion holds.

3.2.
The profinite extension of rational functions

The Preservation Lemma already hints at our intention to see transducers as
computing functions from and to the free profinite monoids. Naturally, if

\tau

Theorem
3
). For
u=\lim u_{n}
a profinite word, we will write

\tau(u)
for
\widehat{\tau}(u)
, i.e., the limit
\lim\tau(u_{n})
, which exists by
continuity. In this section, we develop a slightly more combinatorial approach
to the evaluation of
\widehat{\tau}
, and address two classes of profinite words:
those expressed as
s\cdot u\cdot t
for
s,t
words and
u
a profinite word,
and those expressed as
x^{\omega}
for
x
a word.

Let
\tau
be an unambiguous transducer. Recall that for any state

of

\tau
and any profinite word
u
,

is well defined. As a consequence, if

s
and

are words, then there is at most one initial state
q_{0}
, one

and one

such that
q^{\prime}.t

exist iff
\tau(s\cdot u\cdot f)
is defined. Thus:

Lemma 9
.

Let
\tau
be an unambiguous transducer from
A^{*}
to
B^{*}
,
s,t\in A^{*}

and

. Suppose

be the unique states such that

is initial,
q\in q_{0}.s
,

q^{\prime}\in q.u
, and
q^{\prime}.t
is final. The following holds:

\tau(s\cdot u\cdot t)=\tau_{\bullet,q}(s)\cdot\tau_{q,q^{\prime}}(u)\cdot\tau_{q^{\prime},\bullet}(t)\kern 5.0pt.

Let us now turn to the evaluation of
\omega

Lemma 10
.

Let
\tau
be an unambiguous transducer from
A^{*}
to
B^{*}
and
x\in A^{*}
.
If

is defined, then there are words

such

\tau(x^{\omega})=s\cdot y^{\omega-1}\cdot t\kern 5.0pt.

Proof 3.4
.

Consider a large value

; we study the behavior of
x^{n!}
on
\tau
.
There is an initial state
q_{0}
, a state
q
, and a final state
q_{1}
such
that
x^{n!}
is accepted by a path going from
q_{0}
to
q
reading
x^{i}
,
from
q
to
q
reading

with

, and from
q
to
q_{1}

to

3.3.
The Syncing Lemma: Preservation Lemma applied to transducers

We apply the Preservation Lemma on transducers and deduce a slightly more
combinatorial characterization of transducers describing continuous functions.
This does not provide an immediate decidable criterion, but our decidability
results will often rely on it. The goal of the forthcoming lemma is to
decouple, when evaluating
s\cdot u\cdot t
(with the notations of the
Preservation Lemma), the behavior of the
u
part and that of the
s,t
part.
This latter part will be tested against an
equalizer
set:

{defi}

[Equalizer set]
Let
u,v\in\widehat{A^{*}}
. The
equalizer set
of
u
and
v
in
\mathcal{V}
is:

\mathsf{Equ}_{\mathcal{V}}(u,v)=\{(s,s^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\in{(A^{*})}^{4}\mid s\cdot u\cdot t=_{\mathcal{V}}s^{\prime}\cdot v\cdot t^{\prime}\}\kern 5.0pt.

Remark 11
.

The complexity of equalizer sets can be surprisingly high. For instance,
letting
\mathcal{V}
be the class of languages defined by

\{x^{2}=x^{3}\mid x\in A^{*}\}
, there is a profinite word
u
for which

\mathsf{Equ}_{\mathcal{V}}(u,u)
is undecidable (this relies on the existence of arbitrarily
long square-free words). On the other hand, equalizer sets quickly become
less complex for common varieties; for instance, Lemma
14
will
provide a simple form for the equalizer sets of aperiodic supercancellative
varieties.

{defi}

[Input synchronization]
Let
R,S\subseteq A^{*}\times B^{*}
. The
input synchronization
of
R
and

S
is defined as the relation over
B^{*}\times B^{*}
obtained by
synchronizing the first component of
R
and
S
:

R\bowtie S=\{(u,v)\mid(\exists s)[(s,u)\in R\land(s,v)\in S]\}\;\big(=S\circ R^{-1})\kern 5.0pt.

Naturally, the input synchronization of two rational functions is a rational
relation.

Lemma 12

(Syncing Lemma)
.

Let
\tau
be an unambiguous transducer from
A^{*}
to
B^{*}
and
E

Proof 3.5
.

We rely on the Preservation Lemma, since

(Only if.)
Suppose that

3.4.
A profinite toolbox for the aperiodic setting

In this section, we provide a few lemmas pertaining to our study of aperiodic

We show that the equalizer sets of aperiodic supercancellative varieties are

(2)

Suppose

We will reduce the case

(1)

Suppose

, that is,

Lemma 15
.

Let

Proof 3.8
.

\mathcal{V}

For two aperiodic supercancellative varieties

4.
Intermezzos

We present a few facts of independent interest on continuous rational functions.
Through this, we develop a few examples, showing in particular how the
Preservation and Syncing Lemmas can be used to show (non)continuity. In a first part, we study when the structure of the transducer is relevant to continuity,
and in a second, when the (non)inclusion of variety relates to (non)inclusion of the class of continuous rational functions.

4.1.
Transducer structure and continuity

As noted by Reutenauer and Schützenberger

_realizable rational

4.2.
Variety inclusion and inclusion of classes of continuous functions

In this section, we study the consequence of variety (non)inclusion on the inclusion of the related classes of continuous rational functions. This is
reminiscent of the notion of

where a function is

_continuous for

Proposition 20
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{W}

\mathcal{V}
_continuous rational functions that are not

Proof 4.4
.

Let

as the identity function with domain

_continuous. However,

\mathcal{W}
_continuous.

The remainder of this section focuses on a dual statement:

If

\mathcal{V}
_continuous rational

\mathcal{W}
_continuous?

4.2.1.
The group cases

We first focus on group varieties. Naturally, if 1.

Proof 4.5

It remains to show the case

The aperiodic cases

We now turn to aperiodic varieties. For less expressive varieties, the

5.

5.1.

Reutenauer and Schützenberger showed in

{defi}

•

A transducer

\Big[(\exists x,y)\big[\emptyset\;\neq\;(\tau_{p,\bullet}\bowtie\tau_{q,\bullet})\;\subseteq\;(x,y)\cdot\textit{Id}\big]\Big]\Rightarrow p=q\kern 5.0pt.

Within a transducer, a

p

q^{\prime}

q

a
a
a^{*}

This is regardless of the outputs. The pan is
proper
if
q\neq q^{\prime}
.

•

Within a transducer, and for a letter
a

q
is

a
_recurrent
if
p.a^{\omega}=\{p\}
. The transducer itself is said to be

a
_recurrent if all of its states are, and recurrent if it is

a
_recurrent for all letters.

Lemma 26
.

5.2.
Deciding continuity for aperiodic varieties

We saw in Section

that the approach of the previous section
cannot work: there is no correspondence between continuity and realizability for
aperiodic varieties. Herein, we use the Syncing Lemma to decide continuity in
two main steps. First, note that all of our aperiodic varieties are defined by
an infinite number of equations for each alphabet. The Syncing Lemma would thus
have us check an infinite number of conditions; our first step is to reduce this
to a finite number, which we stress through the forthcoming notion of
“pertaining triplet” of states. Second, we have to show that the inclusion of
the second point of the Syncing Lemma can effectively be checked. This will be
done by simplifying this condition, and showing a decidability property on
rational relations.

\mathsf{Equ}_{\text{$\mathcal{A}$\kern-0.25331pt\scalebox{.6}{$\mathcal{B}$}}}(u,v)=\mathsf{Equ}_{\text{$\mathcal{C}$\kern-0.50664pt\scalebox{.6}{$\mathcal{O}$\kern-0.8445pt$\mathcal{M}$}}}(u,v)
.

Let
\tau\colon A^{*}\to B^{*}
be a given transducer. Consider an equation

ab=ba
and four states
p,p^{\prime},q,q^{\prime}
of
\tau
. Write

u=\tau_{p,p^{\prime}}(ab)
and
v=\tau_{q,q^{\prime}}(ba)
. We ought to
check, by the Syncing Lemma, the inclusion in

\mathsf{Equ}_{\text{$\mathcal{C}$\kern-0.50664pt\scalebox{.6}{$\mathcal{O}$\kern-0.8445pt$\mathcal{M}$}}}(u,v)=\{(s,s^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\mid s\cdot u\cdot t=_{\text{$\mathcal{C}$\kern-0.50664pt\scalebox{.6}{$\mathcal{O}$\kern-0.8445pt$\mathcal{M}$}}}s^{\prime}\cdot v\cdot t^{\prime}\}
of some input synchronization. Now this set is the set of

(s,s^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})
such that

\mathsf{Pkh}(s\cdot u\cdot t)=\mathsf{Pkh}(s^{\prime}\cdot v\cdot t^{\prime})
, and is thus defined by a
simple semilinear property. The input synchronizations themselves, e.g.,

\tau_{\bullet,p}\bowtie\tau_{\bullet,q}
, are rational relations, and
their component-wise Parikh image is thus a semilinear set. Since the
inclusion of semilinear sets is decidable, the inclusion of the second point
of the Syncing Lemma is also decidable.

For
\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{B}

, we should additionally check the equations
a^{\omega}=1
. The
reasoning is similar. Consider three states
(p,p^{\prime},q)
, and write

x\cdot u^{\omega-1}\cdot y
for
\tau_{p,p^{\prime}}(a^{\omega})
. By
commutativity and the fact that
u^{\omega-1}
acts as an inverse of
u
in
the equations holding in
\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{B}

, we have that

(s,s^{\prime},t,t^{\prime})\in\mathsf{Equ}_{\text{$\mathcal{A}$\kern-0.25331pt\scalebox{.6}{$\mathcal{B}$}}}(x\cdot u^{\omega-1}\cdot y,1)
iff

s\cdot t=_{\text{$\mathcal{A}$\kern-0.25331pt\scalebox{.6}{$\mathcal{B}$}}}s^{\prime}\cdot u\cdot t^{\prime}
. This again reduces the inclusion of the
second point of the Syncing Lemma to a decidable semilinear property.

6.
Discussion

We presented a study of continuity in functional transducers, on the one hand
focused on general statements (Section
3
), on the other hand on
continuity for classical varieties. The heart of this contribution resides in
decidability properties (Section
5
), although we also addressed
natural and related questions in a systematic way
(Section
4
). We single out two main research directions.

First, there is a sharp contrast between the genericity of the Preservation and
Syncing Lemma and the technicality of the actual proofs of decidability of
continuity. To which extent can these be unified and generalized? We know of
two immediate extensions: 1. the generic results of Section
3

readily apply to Boolean algebras of languages closed under quotient, a
relaxation of the conditions imposed on varieties, and 2. Proposition
17
and Lemma
28
can be shown to
also hold for the varieties
\mathcal{G}_{p}
of languages recognized by
p
-groups, hence

\mathcal{G}_{p}
_continuity is decidable for transducers. Beyond these two points, we do
not know how to show decidability for
\mathcal{G}_{\text{nil}}
(which is the
join
of the

\mathcal{G}_{p}
), and the surprising complexity of the equalizer sets for some Burnside
varieties (e.g., the one defined by
x^{2}=x^{3}
, see the Remark on
page
11
) leads us to conjecture that continuity may be
undecidable in that case, hence that no unified way to show the decidability of
continuity exists.

Second, the notion of continuity may be extended to more general settings. For
instance, departing from regular languages, it can be noted that every recursive
function is continuous for the class of recursive languages. Another natural
generalization consists in studying
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})
_continuity, that is, the
property for a function to map
\mathcal{W}
_languages to
\mathcal{V}
_languages by inverse
image. This would provide more flexibility for a sufficient condition for
cascades of languages (or stackings of circuits, or nestings of formulas) to be
in a given variety.

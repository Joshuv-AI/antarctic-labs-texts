Positively curved Finsler metrics on vector bundles III Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification 32J25, 32F17. Keywords: Kobayashi positivity, Griffiths positivity, ampleness.
Kuang-Ru Wu

Abstract
The goal of the paper is to extend results about ample or Griffiths positive vector bundles to Kobayashi positive vector bundles. In particular, we show that the quotient bundle of a Kobayashi positive vector bundle is Kobayashi positive, and the tensor product of two Kobayashi positive vector bundles is Kobayashi positive. These results strengthen the conjectural equivalences between ampleness, Griffiths positivity, and Kobayashi positivity. The proofs rely on the convexity of Kobayashi positive Finsler metrics and the duality for convex Finsler metrics.

Introduction

In
[
Har66
]
, Hartshorne proved several properties regarding ampleness of vector bundles; for example, taking quotient and taking tensor product preserve ampleness. Most of these properties are also valid for Griffiths positive Hermitian metrics (
[
Gri69
,
Ume73
,
Dem12
]
). In light of the conjectures of Griffiths and Kobayashi which predict that ampleness, Griffiths positivity, and Kobayashi positivity should all be equivalent, one is led to the question whether these properties are valid for Kobayashi positive Finsler metrics. The main purpose of the paper is to address this question. (For the progress on the conjectures of Griffiths and Kobayashi, see
[
Ume73
,
CF90
,
Ber09
,
MT07
,
HMP10
,
LSY13
,
Nau21
,
FLW20
,
Dem21
,
Pin21
,
Wu22a
,
Wu22b
,
MZ23
]
).

We use
E,E_{1}
, and
E_{2}
for holomorphic vector bundles and
X,Y
for the base compact complex manifolds. A vector bundle is called Kobayashi positive if it carries a strongly pseudoconvex Finsler metric with positive Kobayashi curvature (we will review Finsler metrics in Subsection
2.1
). Our main results are the following.

Theorem 1
.

1.

The quotient bundle of a Kobayashi positive vector bundle is Kobayashi positive.

2.

If
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are Kobayashi positive, then
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
is Kobayashi positive.

3.

If
E
is Kobayashi positive, then the tensor power
E^{\otimes k}
and the symmetric power
S^{k}E
are Kobayashi positive for
k\geq 1
, and the exterior power
\bigwedge^{k}E
is Kobayashi positive for
1\leq k\leq r
where
r=\text{rank }E
.

The second statement in Theorem
1
is perhaps most interesting. It is based on a general theorem in
[
Lem15
, Theorem 2.4]
(similar results in various settings can be found in
[
Roc84
, Theorem 4.2]
,
[
Slo88
, Corollary 4.5]
, and
[
CS93
, Proposition 15.5]
where the focus is on the interpolation problem). This theorem, roughly speaking, says that if a holomorphic homomorphism between Banach bundles decreases curvature, then the logarithm of the operator norm of the homomorphism is plurisubharmonic.

Since every homomorphism from
E_{1}
to
E_{2}^{*}
decreases curvature provided that
E_{1}
is positively curved and
E_{2}^{*}
is negatively curved, the bundle
\Hom(E_{1},E_{2}^{*})
with the operator norm is negatively curved according to the Lempert’s theorem above, so
E_{1}^{*}\otimes E_{2}^{*}
is negatively curved. This is basically the idea behind the proof, but we cannot simply apply the results in
[
Roc84
,
Slo88
,
CS93
,
Lem15
]
to our case. One main catch is that our predecessors all assume convexity on the metrics, whereas the Finsler metrics we consider are not necessarily convex.
Another obstacle concerns dual Finsler metrics. In general, taking duality of Finsler metrics does not flip the sign of curvature (
[
Dem99
, Remark 2.7]
); however, for convex Finsler metrics, we do have the duality (
[
Som78
,
Dem99
]
or see Lemma
8
. In
[
Lem17a
, Lemma 2.1]
, Lempert proved duality for Banach bundles). All these issues can be resolved by our result in
[
Wu22a
, Corollary 2]
: when it comes to Kobayashi positive Finsler metrics, one has convexity for free. The use of our convexity result from
[
Wu22a
, Corollary 2]
is the crux of the paper.

Another feature which does not seem to appear in the literature before is regularization of continuous Finsler metrics. We prove two regularization lemmas in Subsection
2.3
based on
[
GW73
,
GW79
]
.

Besides our application here, the theorem in
[
Lem15
]
is also used in
[
Lem17a
]
to prove an Ohsawa–Takegoshi type extension theorem
[
BL16
]
, in
[
Lem17b
,
Wu20
]
to solve a Dirichlet problem for flat metrics on Hilbert bundles, and in
[
Alb22
]
to prove a Skoda type division theorem.

In
[
Wu22b
]
, as a motivation, we mentioned that if
E
is ample (Griffiths positive) then
E^{*}\otimes\det E
is ample (Griffiths positive), but we did not know whether the statement holds for Kobayashi positivity. We can answer this question here by using the statement 3 in Theorem
1
together with the isomorphism
\bigwedge^{r-1}E\simeq E^{*}\otimes\det E
; therefore, if
E
is Kobayashi positive then so is
E^{*}\otimes\det E
. A related question is that if
E
is ample, can we prove Kobayashi positivity of
E^{*}\otimes\det E
? Since
E\otimes\det E^{*}\simeq\Hom(E^{*},\det E^{*})
and
\det E^{*}
is theoretically more negative than
E^{*}
, it is tempting to use Lempert’s theorem on curvature decreasing, but we have not been able to compare the curvature of
E^{*}
and
\det E^{*}
.

Let us discuss a naive approach to Theorem
1
. It is known that ampleness of
E
is equivalent to Kobayashi negativity of
E^{*}
(
[
Kob75
, Theorem 5.1]
), and that Theorem
1
is true if Kobayashi positivity is replaced with ampleness (
[
Har66
]
), so Theorem
1
is true for Kobayashi negativity (quotient bundle replaced with subbundle in the statement 1). However, one cannot simply take the dual Finsler metrics to conclude Theorem
1
because, again, taking duality does not in general flip the sign of the curvature for Finsler metrics.

There are some results about tensor products and Kobayashi negativity. In
[
Kob75
]
, Kobayashi asked whether one can prove the fact that Kobayashi negativity is preserved under tensor product by using a differential geometric method. The statements in
[
Dem99
, Theorem 5.6 and Corollary 5.7]
can be viewed as an answer to Kobayashi’s question. In
[
BA02
]
, Ben Abdesselem showed that if
E_{1}
is Kobayashi negative and
E_{2}^{*}
is globally generated by holomorphic sections, then
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
is Kobayashi negative (see also
[
BAA16
]
). However, their results do not imply ours because one cannot take duality due to the lack of convexity.

In addition to Theorem
1
, we collect a few more properties of Kobayashi positivity in Theorem
2
below. We use the strategy developed in the proof of Theorem
1
to prove them; pass to the dual bundles, manipulate plurisubharmonicity, and then take dual again using convexity. It seems likely the results in Theorem
2
can also be proved using curvature formulas from
[
Kob75
,
Kob96
]
without passing to the dual bundles.

Theorem 2
.

1.

The direct sum of vector bundles
E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
is Kobayashi positive if and only if
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are both Kobayashi positive.

2.

In the short exact sequence of vector bundles
0\to E_{1}\to E\to E_{2}\to 0
, if
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are Kobayashi positive, then
E
is Kobayashi positive.

3.

If
f:Y\to X
is an immersion, and
E
is a Kobayashi positive vector bundle over
X
, then the pull-back
f^{*}E
is Kobayashi positive.

[
Wu22a

Wu22b

2.1
Finsler metrics

We give a quick review of Finsler metrics here. For more details, we recommend
[
Kob75
,
Kob96
]
. See also
[
Aik98
,
Aik04
,
CW03
,

,

.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
r
over a compact complex manifold
X
of dimension
n
. For a vector
\zeta\in E_{z}
, we will symbolically write
(z,\zeta)\in E
. A Finsler metric
F
on the vector bundle
E\to X
is a real-valued function on
E
such that

\displaystyle(1)\text{ $F$ is continuous on the total space $E$, and $F$ is smooth away from the zero section of $E$}.

\displaystyle(2)\text{ For $(z,\zeta)\in E$},F(z,\zeta)\geq 0,\text{ and equality holds if and only if $\zeta=0$}.

\displaystyle(3)\text{ $F(z,\lambda\zeta)=|\lambda|F(z,\zeta)$, for $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$}.

We denote
F^{2}
by
G
throughout the paper (we will sometimes use
G
to denote a Finsler metric). Regarding (1), if
F
is only continuous on
E
without being smooth on
E\smallsetminus\{0\}
, then we call such an
F
a continuous Finsler metric (in this paper, Finsler metrics are always smooth, unless we write
continuous
Finsler metrics).

Denote by
P(E)
the projectivized bundle of
E
, and by
O_{P(E)}(-1)
the tautological line bundle over
P(E)
. Let
p
be the projection from
P(E)
to
X
, and denote the pull-back bundle
p^{*}E
by
\tilde{E}
. In summary,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces O_{P(E)}(-1)\subset\tilde{E}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P(E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{p}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

For a vector
\zeta\in E_{z}
, we denote by
[\zeta]
the equivalence class of
\zeta
in
P(E_{z})
, and we will write
(z,[\zeta])\in P(E)
. The pull-back bundle
\tilde{E}=\coprod_{(z,[\zeta])\in P(E)}(z,[\zeta])\times E_{z}
, and we will denote an element in
\tilde{E}
by
(z,[\zeta],Z)
with
Z\in E_{z}
. There is a one-to-one correspondence between Finsler metrics on
E
and Hermitian metrics on
O_{P(E)}(-1)
, furnished by
\tilde{p}
.
If
F
is a Finsler metric on
E
with
G=F^{2}
and we denote the corresponding Hermitian metric on
O_{P(E)}(-1)
by
h_{G}
, then the correspondence is

(2.1)

h_{G}(z,[\zeta],\zeta)=G(z,\zeta).

When we need to do local computations, we use
(z_{1},...,z_{n})
for local coordinates on
X
, and
(\zeta_{1},...,\zeta_{r})
for local fiber coordinates on
E
with respect to a holomorphic frame
\{e_{1},...,e_{r}\}
. So
(z_{1},...,z_{n},\zeta_{1},...,\zeta_{r})
is a coordinate system on
E
. Let
F
be a Finsler metric on
E
with
G=F^{2}
. We write

\displaystyle G_{i}=\partial G/\partial\zeta_{i}\,,\text{ }\text{ }G_{\bar{j}}=\partial G/\partial\bar{\zeta}_{j}\,,\text{ }\text{ }G_{i\bar{j}}=\partial^{2}G/\partial\zeta_{i}\partial\bar{\zeta}_{j}\,,

\displaystyle G_{i\alpha}=\partial G_{i}/\partial z_{\alpha}\,,\text{ }\text{ }G_{i\bar{j}\bar{\beta}}=\partial G_{i\bar{j}}/\partial\bar{z}_{\beta}\,,\text{ etc.,}

with Latin letters
i,j
for the fiber direction and Greek letters
\alpha,\beta
for the base direction. From
[
Kob96
, (3.3) and (3.10)]
, we have

(2.2)

\sum_{j}G_{i\bar{j}}(z,\zeta)\bar{\zeta}_{j}=G_{i}(z,\zeta)\text{, and }\sum_{i,j}G_{i\bar{j}}(z,\zeta)\zeta_{i}\bar{\zeta}_{j}=G(z,\zeta).

A Finsler metric
F
is said to be

\displaystyle(1)

\displaystyle\text{ strongly pseudoconvex if the matrix $(F_{i\bar{j}})$ is positive definite on $E\smallsetminus\{\text{0}\}$}.

\displaystyle(2)

\displaystyle\text{ convex if $F$ restricted to each fiber $E_{z}$ is convex},

\displaystyle\text{ namely the fiberwise real Hessian of $F$ is positive semidefinite on $E\smallsetminus\{\text{0}\}$}.

\displaystyle(3)

\displaystyle\text{ strongly convex if the fiberwise real Hessian of $G$ is positive definite on $E\smallsetminus\{\text{0}\}$}.

Note that in (1), the requirement
(F_{i\bar{j}})>0
is equivalent to
(G_{i\bar{j}})>0
. Indeed, since
G=F^{2}
, we have
G_{i\bar{j}}=2F_{i}F_{\bar{j}}+2FF_{i\bar{j}}
and so
(F_{i\bar{j}})>0
implies
(G_{i\bar{j}})>0
. On the other hand, we have

F_{i\bar{j}}=\frac{1}{4}G^{-\frac{3}{2}}(2G_{i\bar{j}}G-G_{i}G_{\bar{j}})=\frac{1}{4}G^{-\frac{3}{2}}(G_{i\bar{j}}G+G_{i\bar{j}}G-G_{i}G_{\bar{j}})

and if
(G_{i\bar{j}})>0
, we have
(\sum_{i,j}G_{i\bar{j}}v_{i}\bar{v}_{j})G-(\sum_{i}G_{i}v_{i})(\sum_{j}G_{\bar{j}}\bar{v}_{j})\geq 0
for any
v\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
by (
2.2
) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, so
(F_{i\bar{j}})>0
.

If
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are two convex Finsler metrics on
E
, then the Finsler metric
(F_{1}^{2}+F_{2}^{2})^{1/2}
is still convex by a direct computation and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.

The next is a lemma characterizing strong pseudoconvexity (
[
Kob96
, Theorem (1)]
).

Lemma 3
.

Let
F
be a Finsler metric on
E
and
h_{G}
be its corresponding Hermitian metric on

. Then
F
is strongly pseudoconvex if and only if the curvature
\Theta(h_{G})
of

restricted to each fiber of

is negative definite.

For a strongly pseudoconvex Finsler metric
F
, there is a natural Hermitian metric
\tilde{G}
on the pull-back bundle
\tilde{E}
(see
[
Kob75
]
). In terms of local coordinates, the Hermitian metric
\tilde{G}
is given by

\tilde{G}_{(z,[\zeta])}(Z,Z)=\sum_{i,j}G_{i\bar{j}}(z,\zeta)Z_{i}\bar{Z}_{j},\text{ for $Z=\sum^{r}_{i=1}Z_{i}s_{i}(z)\in E_{z}$}.

Now since
(\tilde{E},\tilde{G})
is a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle, we can talk about its Chern curvature
\Theta
, an
\End\tilde{E}
-valued
(1,1)
-form on
P(E)
. With respect to the metric
\tilde{G}
, the bundle
\tilde{E}
has a fiberwise orthogonal decomposition
O_{P(E)}(-1)\oplus O_{P(E)}(-1)^{\perp}
, and so
\Theta
can be written as a block matrix. Let
\Theta|_{O_{P(E)}(-1)}
denote the block in the matrix
\Theta
corresponding to
\End(O_{P(E)}(-1))
. Since
O_{P(E)}(-1)
is a line bundle,
\Theta|_{O_{P(E)}(-1)}
is a
(1,1)
-form on
P(E)
.

Definition 4
.

The
(1,1)
-form
\Theta|_{O_{P(E)}(-1)}
is called the Kobayashi curvature of the strongly pseudoconvex Finsler metric
F
. Kobayashi positivity (or negativity) means the positivity (or negativity) of
\Theta|_{O_{P(E)}(-1)}
.

We recall a local expression of the Kobayashi curvature (
[
Wu22a
, Formula (2.4)]
). We focus on a local chart
\{(z,[\zeta]):\zeta_{r}\neq 0\}
of
P(E)
, and let
\zeta_{i}/\zeta_{r}=w_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r-1
. The formula is

(2.3)

\Theta|_{O_{P(E)}(-1)}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{\tilde{G}(R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\zeta,\zeta)}{\tilde{G}(\zeta,\zeta)}\,dz_{\alpha}\wedge d\bar{z}_{\beta},

where
R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}
is an endomorphism of
\tilde{E}
.

We also have the following characterization (
[
Kob96
, Section 5]
).

Lemma 5
.

Let
F
be a strongly pseudoconvex Finsler metric on
E
and
h_{G}
be the corresponding Hermitian metric on
O_{P(E)}(-1)
. Then
F
is Kobayashi positive (or negative) if and only if
\Theta(h_{G})
has signature
(n,r-1)
(or
(0,n+r-1)
).

For a strongly pseudoconvex Finsler metric
F
, one can talk about coordinates normal at a point. Given a point
(z_{0},[\zeta_{0}])\in P(E)
, there exists a holomorphic frame
\{e_{i}\}
for
E
around
z_{0}\in X
such that

(2.4)

G_{\alpha}(z_{0},\zeta_{0})=0,\,\,G_{\alpha\bar{j}}(z_{0},\zeta_{0})=0

(such a frame can be obtained by (5.11) in
[

]
).

Finally, we recall a formula from

, Section 5]
(see also

. For

,

and

K_{v}(\zeta)=-\inf\partial\bar{\partial}\log G(\phi)\bigr|_{z_{0}}(v,\bar{v}),

2.2
Lemmas

We collect some lemmas in this subsection that will be used later.

Lemma 7
.

Let
F
be a strongly pseudoconvex Finsler metric on
E
. The following are equivalent.

\displaystyle(1)

\displaystyle\text{ The Finsler metric $F$ has negative Kobayashi curvature}.

\displaystyle(2)

\displaystyle\text{ $F$ is strongly plurisubharmonic on $E\smallsetminus\{0\}$}.

\displaystyle(3)

\displaystyle\text{ $F^{2}$ is strongly plurisubharmonic on $E\smallsetminus\{0\}$}.

If any of the above happens, then
F
is plurisubharmonic on
E
.

Proof.

For the equivalence between (1) and (2), one can use
[
Dem99
, (3) and (4) in Theorem 1.3]
along with Lemma
5
.

The implication from (2) to (3) is obvious. From (3) to (2), since the Finsler metric
F
is strongly pseudoconvex, we can use the normal coordinates (
2.4
) to compute the complex Hessian of
F
at one point, which gives

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{4}G^{-\frac{3}{2}}(2G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}G-G_{\alpha}G_{\bar{\beta}}),&\frac{1}{4}G^{-\frac{3}{2}}(2G_{\alpha\bar{j}}G-G_{\alpha}G_{\bar{j}})\\
\frac{1}{4}G^{-\frac{3}{2}}(2G_{i\bar{\beta}}G-G_{i}G_{\bar{\beta}}),&F_{i\bar{j}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{4}G^{-\frac{3}{2}}2G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}G,&0\\
0,&F_{i\bar{j}}\end{pmatrix}

a positive matrix. So,
F
is strongly plurisubharmonic. (The equivalence between (1) and (2) can also be proved using Lemma
6
and computing the needed complex Hessian with the normal coordinates (
2.4
)).

The last statement that
F
is plurisubharmonic on
E
is true because
\log F(\phi)
is plurisubharmonic for any local holomorphic section
\phi
by Lemma
6
and then we use
[
Wu23
, Lemma 6.1]
. Alternatively, since
F
is non-negative and is zero on the zero section of
E
, if
F
is strongly plurisubharmonic on
E\smallsetminus\{0\}
, then
F
is plurisubharmonic on
E
for
F
satisfying the mean value inequality at every point in
E
.

It is known that a Hermitian metric is Griffiths positive if and only if its dual Hermitian metric is Griffiths negative. The following lemma is a Finsler analogue, and the core of the lemma is due to
[
Som78
]
and
[
Dem99
, Theorem 2.5]
. The convexity assumption is important, as we are taking dual Finsler metrics in the proof (see
[
Dem99
, Remark 2.7]
). We will call a vector bundle
E
convex Kobayashi positive (negative) if
E
carries a convex and strongly pseudoconvex Finsler metric with positive (negative) Kobayashi curvature. In
[
Lem17a
, Lemma 2.1]
, Lempert also obtained some duality results for Banach bundles.

Lemma 8
.

A vector bundle
E
is convex Kobayashi positive if and only if its dual
E^{*}
is convex Kobayashi negative.

Before proving Lemma
8
, we need another lemma first. This lemma has appeared in Proposition 1.8 in the first version of
[
FLW20
]
on the arXiv. We give a simplified proof here.

Lemma 9
.

A Finsler metric
F
has transversal Levi signature
(r,n)
if and only if
F
is strongly pseudoconvex and has positive Kobayashi curvature.

Proof.

Recall that a Finsler metric
F
is said to have transversal Levi signature
(r,n)
, if at every point
(z,\zeta)\in E\smallsetminus\{\text{zero section}\}
, the Levi form
i\partial\bar{\partial}F
on
E
is positive definite along the fiber
E_{z}
and negative definite on some
n
-dimensional subspace
W\subset T^{1,0}_{(z,\zeta)}E
which is transversal to the fiber
E_{z}
.

For the forward direction, since
F_{i\bar{j}}>0
, the Finsler metric
F
is strongly pseudoconvex. Let
\pi:E\smallsetminus\{\text{zero section}\}\to P(E)
be the quotient map. As before, we denote
F^{2}
by
G
, and the corresponding Hermitian metric on
O_{P(E)}(-1)
by
h_{G}
. It is not hard to verify that
\pi^{*}\Theta(h_{G})=-\partial\bar{\partial}\log G
. For any nonzero
e\in W
,

\displaystyle\Theta(h_{G})(\pi_{*}e,\overline{\pi_{*}e})=\pi^{*}\Theta(h_{G})(e,\overline{e})=-2\partial\bar{\partial}\log F(e,\overline{e})

\displaystyle=-2(\frac{\partial\bar{\partial}F}{F}-\frac{\partial F\wedge\bar{\partial}F}{F^{2}})(e,\overline{e})\geq-2\frac{\partial\bar{\partial}F}{F}(e,\overline{e})>0.

As a consequence, the map
\pi_{*}|_{W}
is injective. So the space
\pi_{*}W
is of dimension
n
, and
\Theta(h_{G})
is positive definite on
\pi_{*}W
. By Lemma
3
and a dimension count,
\Theta(h_{G})
has signature
(n,r-1)
, thus
F
is Kobayashi positive by Lemma
5
.

For the backward direction, we fix a point
(z,\zeta)\in E\smallsetminus\{\text{zero section}\}
. Since
F
is strongly pseudoconvex, the Levi form
i\partial\bar{\partial}F
on
E
is positive definite along the fiber
E_{z}
. Moreover, we can find a holomorphic frame
\{e_{i}\}
for
E
around
z\in X
such that

(2.6)

G_{\alpha}(z,\zeta)=G_{\alpha\bar{j}}(z,\zeta)=0,

namely the normal coordinates as in (
2.4
). With respect this coordinate system, the tangent space
T^{1,0}_{(z,\zeta)}E
has a basis
\{\partial/\partial z_{1},\cdots,\partial/\partial z_{n},\partial/\partial\zeta_{1},\cdots,\partial/\partial\zeta_{r}\}
, and the space
E_{z}=T^{1,0}_{\zeta}E_{z}
has a basis
\{\partial/\partial\zeta_{1},\cdots,\partial/\partial\zeta_{r}\}
. We choose
W
to be the space generated by
\{\partial/\partial z_{1},\cdots,\partial/\partial z_{n}\}
which is transversal to
E_{z}
.

Let us verify that
i\partial\bar{\partial}F
is negative definite on
W
. A straightforward computation gives

\displaystyle\partial\bar{\partial}F=\partial\bar{\partial}\sqrt{G}=\frac{1}{2}G^{-\frac{1}{2}}\partial\bar{\partial}G-\frac{1}{4}G^{-\frac{3}{2}}\partial G\wedge\bar{\partial}G.

Using (
2.6
), we get

\partial\bar{\partial}F(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{\alpha}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}_{\beta}})=\frac{1}{2}G^{-\frac{1}{2}}G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}-\frac{1}{4}G^{-\frac{3}{2}}G_{\alpha}G_{\bar{\beta}}=\frac{1}{2}G^{-\frac{1}{2}}G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}};

the matrix
(G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}})
in the end differs from the Kobayashi curvature of
F
by a negative sign (
[
Kob96
, Formulas (5.10) and (5.16)]
). Since
F
is Kobayashi positive, the form
i\partial\bar{\partial}F
is negative definite on
W
, and hence
F
has transversal Levi signature
(r,n)
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
8
.

Assume the bundle
E
carries a convex and strongly pseudoconvex Finsler metric
F
with positive Kobayashi curvature. By adding a small Hermitian metric, we may assume the Finsler metric
F
is strongly convex. By Lemma
9
, the Finsler metric
F
has transversal Levi signature
(r,n)
. According to
[
Dem99
, Theorem 2.5]
, the dual Finsler metric of
F
is strongly plurisubharmonic on
E^{*}\smallsetminus\{0\}
; in particular, it is strongly pseudoconvex. Therefore, the dual Finsler metric of
F
has negative Kobayashi curvature by Lemma
7
. Since the dual metric is already convex, we conclude that the bundle
E^{*}
is convex Kobayashi negative.

For the other direction, we assume the dual bundle
E^{*}
carries a convex, strongly pseudoconvex Finsler metric
F^{*}
whose Kobayashi curvature is negative. By adding a small Hermitian metric, we may assume the Finsler metric
F^{*}
is strongly convex. By Lemma
7
, the Finsler metric
F^{*}
is strongly plurisubharmonic on
E^{*}\smallsetminus\{0\}
. By
[
Dem99
, Theorem 2.5]
, the dual of
F^{*}
has transversal Levi signature
(r,n)
. Therefore, by Lemma
9
, the dual of
F^{*}
is strongly pseudoconvex and has positive Kobayashi curvature. As a result, the bundle
E
is convex Kobayashi positive.
∎

The next lemma is from
[
Wu22a
, Corollary 2]
, and almost all our arguments start with this lemma. It basically says that when dealing with Kobayashi positive Finsler metrics, we have convexity for free.

Lemma 10
.

If
E
is Kobayashi positive, then
E
is convex Kobayashi positive. Therefore, the dual bundle
E^{*}
is convex Kobayashi negative by Lemma
8
.

The next lemma is from
[

, Theorem 6.1]
. One should notice that a “convex” Finsler metric in
[
Kob75
]
is what we call “strongly pseudoconvex” here. This lemma can also be proved using Lemma
7
.

Lemma 11
.

Assume
E
carries a convex and strongly pseudoconvex Finsler metric
F
whose Kobayashi curvature is negative. If
E_{1}
is a subbundle of
E
, then the restriction of the Finsler metric
F
to

is still convex, strongly pseudoconvex, and Kobayashi negative.

2.3
Regularization of continuous Finsler metrics

Recall that in the definition of Finsler metrics, we include the smoothness of
F
on
E\smallsetminus\{0\}
, so for us Finsler metrics are smooth. If we drop the smoothness assumption, then we call
F
a continuous Finsler metric. In this subsection, we will show that continuous Finsler metrics with extra properties can be approximated by Finsler metrics with the same properties.

First of all, the correspondence (
2.1
) can be extended to the continuous case; namely, there is a one-to-one correspondence between continuous Finsler metrics on
E
and continuous Hermitian metrics on
O_{P(E)}(-1)
.

We fix a background Finsler metric
F
on
E
, and denote
F^{2}
by
G
. We denote the corresponding Hermitian metric on
O_{P(E)}(-1)
by
h
, so according to (
2.1
) we have
h(z,[\zeta],\zeta)=G(z,\zeta)
. Every continuous Hermitian metric on the line bundle
O_{P(E)}(-1)
can be written as
h
times a positive continuous function
m(z,[\zeta])
on
P(E)
. Hence, through the one-to-one correspondence, every continuous Finsler metric on
E
can be written as
G(z,\zeta)m(z,[\zeta])
where
m(z,[\zeta])
is a positive continuous function on
P(E)
. We will simply write
Gm
or
Gm(z,\zeta)
for
G(z,\zeta)m(z,[\zeta])
. (Strictly speaking, we should use
(Gm)^{1/2}
for Finsler metric, but we abuse the notation by using
Gm
.)

For an open set
U
in
P(E)
and a positive continuous function
m(z,[\zeta])
defined on
U
, the continuous Finsler metric
Gm
is defined on some open subset of
E
. We call
Gm
strongly convex if for
\zeta_{0}
in
E_{z_{0}}
, there exist a neighborhood in
E
and a local Hermitian metric
H
on
E
such that
\zeta\mapsto Gm(z,\zeta)-H(\zeta,\zeta)
is convex in the neighborhood of
(z_{0},\zeta_{0})
in
E
. When
m(z,[\zeta])
is smooth, this definition of strong convexity is consistent with the earlier definition in Subsection
2.1
. On the other hand,
Gm
is called strongly plurisubharmonic on the total space
E\smallsetminus\{0\}
if for
\zeta_{0}\in E_{z_{0}}
there exist a coordinate neighborhood in
E
and a positive number
c
such that
Gm(z,\zeta)-c(\|z\|^{2}+\|\zeta\|^{2})
is plurisubharmonic in the neighborhood of
(z_{0},\zeta_{0})
in
E
where
\|z\|^{2}=\sum_{\alpha}|z_{\alpha}|^{2}
and
\|\zeta\|^{2}=\sum_{j}|\zeta_{j}|^{2}
.

We define a presheaf
S
on
P(E)
as follows. For
U
an open set in
P(E)
,
S(U)
is set to be the collection of all positive continuous functions
m(z,[\zeta])
on
U
such that
Gm(z,\zeta)
is strongly plurisubharmonic and strongly convex (in the sense of the previous paragraph). It is not hard to see that
S
satisfies the gluing axioms for sheaves, so
S
is a sheaf.

We are going to use the following theorem from
[
GW79
, Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 1]
.

Theorem 12
.

If
T
is a subsheaf of the sheaf of germs of continuous functions on
P(E)
and if
T
has the local approximation, the
C^{\infty}
stability, and the maximum closure properties, then the smooth global sections of
T
are dense in the global sections of
T
in the
C^{0}
fine topology.

Let us explain the terminologies (they can be found in
[
GW79
, Definitions 1.1, 1.3, and 1.4]
). The
C^{0}
fine topology on the set of continuous functions on
P(E)
is the topology generated by the sets

\{\text{$u$ continuous functions on $P(E)$}:|f-u|<g\text{ on }P(E)\}

where
f,g
are both continuous on
P(E)
and
g
is positive.

The subsheaf
T
is said to have the local approximation property if each point in
P(E)
has a neighborhood
U
such that
T|_{U}
has the semilocal approximation property. For the precise definition of the semilocal approximation, see
[
GW79
, Definition 1.4]
; roughly speaking, it means that local sections of
T
can be approximated by smooth sections of
T
in a neighborhood of a given compact set.

The subsheaf
T
is said to have the
C^{\infty}
stability property if the following holds. Suppose that
U
is an open set in
P(E)
,
K
is a compact set in
U
, and
f
is a real-valued function on
U
such that the germ
\{f\}_{(z,[\zeta])}
at
(z,[\zeta])
is in the stalk
T_{(z,[\zeta])}
for every point
(z,[\zeta])
in
U
. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that every real-valued function
g
smooth in a neighborhood of
K
with
d_{K}(0,g)<\varepsilon
satisfies
\{f+g\}_{(z,[\zeta])}\in T_{(z,[\zeta])}
for
(z,[\zeta])
in
K
. Here
d_{K}(0,g)
is

d_{K}(0,g)=\sup_{K}|g|+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\min(1,\|g\|_{K,i}),

where
\|g\|_{K,i}
is the sup of
i
-th partial derivatives of
g
computed in some coordinate systems covering
K
(see the first displayed formula in
[
GW79
, page 49]
).

The subsheaf
T
is said to have the maximum closure property if for any two germs
\{f\}_{(z,[\zeta])},\{g\}_{(z,[\zeta])}
in
T_{(z,[\zeta])}
, the germ
\{\max(f,g)\}_{(z,[\zeta])}
is in
T_{(z,[\zeta])}
.

For our sheaf
S
, we have the following lemma. In other words, this lemma says that a strongly plurisubharmonic, strongly convex, continuous Finsler metric can be approximated by the smooth ones.

Lemma 13
.

The smooth global sections of
S
are dense in the global sections of
S
in the
C^{0}
fine topology.

Proof.

Let us verify the three properties in Theorem
12
for
S
. For the maximum closure property, we consider two sections
m_{1},m_{2}
of
S
defined in a neighborhood of some point in
P(E)
. The function
\max(m_{1},m_{2})
is obviously positive and continuous. Also, we have
G\max(m_{1},m_{2})=\max(Gm_{1},Gm_{2})
. To show that
\max(Gm_{1},Gm_{2})
is strongly convex, we fix
\zeta_{0}\in E_{z_{0}}
, then there exist local Hermitian metrics
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
on
E
such that
Gm_{1}-H_{1}
and
Gm_{2}-H_{2}
are convex in a neighborhood of
(z_{0},\zeta_{0})
in
E
. By choosing a small positive number
c
, we can make
Gm_{1}-cH_{1}
and
Gm_{2}-cH_{1}
convex in a neighborhood of
(z_{0},\zeta_{0})
in
E
. So
\max(Gm_{1}-cH_{1},Gm_{2}-cH_{1})=\max(Gm_{1},Gm_{2})-cH_{1}
is convex in a neighborhood of
(z_{0},\zeta_{0})
in
E
. Hence,
\max(Gm_{1},Gm_{2})
is strongly convex. Showing that
\max(Gm_{1},Gm_{2})
is strongly plurisubharmonic is similar, and we skip the details. As a result, the function
\max(m_{1},m_{2})
is a section of
S
, and the sheaf
S
has the maximum closure property.

Since strong plurisubharmonicity and strong convexity are open conditions, the sheaf
S
has the
C^{\infty}
stability property. To be precise, let
U
be an open set in
P(E)
,
K
a compact set in
U
, and
m
a section of
S
over
U
. So,
m
is a positive continuous function on
U
such that
Gm
is strongly plurisubharmonic and strongly convex. Let
H
be a Hermitian metric on
E
and
\widetilde{K}
be the compact set
\{(z,\zeta)\in E:1\leq H(\zeta,\zeta)\leq 2\}
. By strong convexity of
Gm
, there exist a finite open cover
\{V_{j}\}
for
\widetilde{K}
and finitely many local Hermitian metrics
H_{j}
such that
Gm-H_{j}
is convex in
V_{j}
for each
j
. We can find
\varepsilon>0
small such that for
g
smooth in a neighborhood of
K
with
d_{K}(0,g)<\varepsilon
,
Gg+H_{j}/2
is convex in
V_{j}
for each
j
. Therefore,

G(m+g)-H_{j}/2=Gm-H_{j}+Gg+H_{j}/2

is convex in
V_{j}
for each
j
. Now for a fixed
(z,\zeta)\in E
with
(z,[\zeta])\in K
, we can find
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
to make
(z,\lambda\zeta)
lie in the interior of
\widetilde{K}
and hence in
V_{j}
for some
j
; since

G(m+g)(z,\lambda\zeta)-\frac{H_{j}}{2}(\lambda\zeta,\lambda\zeta)=|\lambda|^{2}\big(G(m+g)(z,\zeta)-\frac{H_{j}}{2}(\zeta,\zeta)\big),

G(m+g)-H_{j}/2
is convex in a neighborhood of
(z,\zeta)
in
E
. We have thus obtained the strong convexity for
G(m+g)
. For the strong plurisubharmonicity, the argument is similar, and we skip the details.

For the local approximation property, we fix a point
(z_{0},[\zeta_{0}])
in
P(E)
and consider a coordinate neighborhood; we want to verify that
S
restricted to this coordinate neighborhood has the semilocal approximation property.

We assume the coordinate neighborhood has the following coordinate system. Let
\{e_{1},...,e_{r}\}
be a holomorphic frame of
E
around
z_{0}
with fiber coordinates
(\zeta_{1},...,\zeta_{r})
. Around the point
(z_{0},[\zeta_{0}])\in P(E)
, we define the local coordinates
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},w_{1},\ldots,w_{r-1})
by
w_{i}=\zeta_{i}/\zeta_{r}
for
i=1\sim r-1
. So

e:=\frac{\zeta_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+\zeta_{r}e_{r}}{\zeta_{r}}=w_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+w_{r-1}e_{r-1}+e_{r}

is a holomorphic frame for
O_{P(E)}(-1)
.

Let
U
be an open set in this coordinate neighborhood, and
m
be a section of
S
over
U
; namely,
m
is a positive continuous function on
U
such that
Gm
is strongly plurisubharmonic and strongly convex. Especially, the continuous Hermitian metric
h(z,[\zeta],\zeta)m(z,[\zeta])
is plurisubharmonic on the total space
O_{P(E)}(-1)
, so

\log\big(h(z,[\zeta],e)m(z,[\zeta])\big)

is plurisubharmonic on
U
. We will abbreviate
h(z,[\zeta],e)m(z,[\zeta])
as
(h(e)m)(z,w)
or
h(e)m
.

Let
\rho
be a nonnegative radial smooth function with support in the unit ball that has integral one in
\mathbb{C}^{n+r-1}
. For
\delta>0
, we consider
\rho_{\delta}(\cdot)=\delta^{-2(n+r-1)}\rho(\cdot/\delta)
, and the convolution

(2.7)

\big(h(e)m\big)*\rho_{\delta}.

The function

m_{\delta}:=\frac{\big(h(e)m\big)*\rho_{\delta}}{h(e)}

is positive and smooth. We claim that
Gm_{\delta}
is plurisubharmonic. First, each term in the Riemann sum for the integral in the convolution
\big(h(e)m\big)*\rho_{\delta}
is of the form
(h(e)m)(z-s,w-t)\rho_{\delta}(s,t)\Delta s\Delta t
where
s,t
are the sample points and
\Delta s\Delta t
is the volume of a subinterval in the partition, and these terms are log plurisubharmonic as the function
(h(e)m)(z,w)
is log plurisubharmonic. The logarithm of the Riemann sum which we denote by

\log\big(\sum(h(e)m)(z-s,w-t)\rho_{\delta}(s,t)\Delta s\Delta t\big)=\log\big(\sum e^{\log(h(e)m)(z-s,w-t)\rho_{\delta}(s,t)\Delta s\Delta t}\big)

is therefore plurisubharmonic, and it converges to
\log\big[(h(e)m)*\rho_{\delta}\big]=\log(h(e)m_{\delta})
which is plurisubharmonic as a result. Meanwhile, we have

\log(Gm_{\delta})(z,\zeta)=\log\big(|\zeta_{r}|^{2}(h(e)m_{\delta})(z,w)\big)=\log|\zeta_{r}|^{2}+\log(h(e)m_{\delta})(z,w),

where the last two terms are plurisubharmonic, so
Gm_{\delta}
is plurisubharmonic as we claimed.

Next we claim that, for
\zeta
and
\xi
in
E_{z}
and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
,
Gm_{\delta}(z,\theta\zeta+(1-\theta)\xi)\leq\theta Gm_{\delta}(z,\zeta)+(1-\theta)Gm_{\delta}(z,\xi)
. Let us unwind the convolution first,

\displaystyle Gm_{\delta}(z,\zeta)=|\zeta_{r}|^{2}(h(e)m_{\delta})(z,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\zeta_{r}|^{2}\int_{(s,t)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{r-1}}(h(e)m)(z-s,w-t)\rho_{\delta}(s,t)dsdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(s,t)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{r-1}}h\big(z-s,\big[\zeta-\zeta_{r}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}t_{i}e_{i}\big],\zeta-\zeta_{r}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}t_{i}e_{i}\big)m(z-s,w-t)\rho_{\delta}(s,t)dsdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(s,t)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{r-1}}G\big(z-s,\zeta-\zeta_{r}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}t_{i}e_{i}\big)m(z-s,w-t)\rho_{\delta}(s,t)dsdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(s,t)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{r-1}}Gm\big(z-s,\zeta-\zeta_{r}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}t_{i}e_{i}\big)\rho_{\delta}(s,t)dsdt.

where
w=(w_{1},...,w_{r-1})
and
\zeta_{i}/\zeta_{r}=w_{i}
for
i=1\sim r-1
, and
\{e_{i}\}
is the holomorphic frame for
E
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle Gm_{\delta}(z,\theta\zeta+(1-\theta)\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(s,t)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{r-1}}Gm\big(z-s,\theta\zeta+(1-\theta)\xi-(\theta\zeta_{r}+(1-\theta)\xi_{r})\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}t_{i}e_{i}\big)\rho_{\delta}(s,t)dsdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(s,t)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{r-1}}Gm\big(z-s,\theta(\zeta-\zeta_{r}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}t_{i}e_{i})+(1-\theta)(\xi-\xi_{r}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}t_{i}e_{i})\big)\rho_{\delta}(s,t)dsdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{(s,t)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{r-1}}\theta Gm\big(z-s,\zeta-\zeta_{r}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}t_{i}e_{i}\big)+(1-\theta)Gm\big(z-s,\xi-\xi_{r}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}t_{i}e_{i}\big)\rho_{\delta}(s,t)dsdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta Gm_{\delta}(z,\zeta)+(1-\theta)Gm_{\delta}(z,\xi),

where the inequality is due to the convexity of
Gm
.

With the local frame
\{e_{i}\}
for
E
, we can construct a local Hermitian metric
H
on
E
which is Griffiths negative (for example,
H(z,\zeta)=\|\zeta\|^{2}e^{\|z\|^{2}}
). So the Finsler metric
Gm_{\delta}+\delta H
is strongly plurisubharmonic and strongly convex. The function

\frac{Gm_{\delta}+\delta H}{G}(z,\zeta)

is therefore a smooth section of the sheaf
S
, and it approximates
m
in the sense of the conditions (a) and (b) in
[
GW79
, Definition 1.4]
for
\delta
small (with
f
and
F
there replaced with
m
and
(Gm_{\delta}+\delta H)/G
respectively).

As a result,
S
restricted to the coordinate neighborhood has the semilocal approximation property, and hence
S
has the local approximation property. (One could consider the convolution
\big(\log(h(e)m)\big)*\rho_{\delta}
instead of (
2.7
) but we do not know how to prove convexity in this case. Also, taking the convolution of
Gm
on the total space
E
does not seem to work because the homogeneity of Finsler metrics is lost).

∎

There is another sheaf we will use later. Define a presheaf
S^{\prime}
on

as follows. For

an open set in
P(E)
,

is set to be the collection of all positive continuous functions

on

such that
Gm(z,\zeta)
is strongly convex (in the sense of continuous Finsler metrics); moreover, for each point in
X
, there exist a coordinate neighborhood and

in this coordinate system, the function

Lemma 14
.

The smooth global sections of

are dense in the global sections of

in the

fine topology.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
13
. Let us verify the three properties in Theorem

for

. For the maximum closure property, we consider two sections

are plurisubharmonic for any local holomorphic section

\log\max(Gm_{1},Gm_{2})(A(z))-\varepsilon\|z\|^{2}
is plurisubharmonic for any

. Consequently,
\max(m_{1},m_{2})
is a section of

, and the sheaf

has the maximum closure property.

Since the requirements in defining the sheaf

are open conditions, the sheaf

has the

stability property. Indeed, let
U
be an open set in
P(E)
,

a compact set in

, and

a section of

over

. Let

be the image of

under the projection

. Since

is compact, there exist a finite open cover
\{V_{j}\}
for

and
\varepsilon_{\min}>0
such that the condition (

) holds in each

with
\varepsilon_{\min}
. We can find
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
such that for

smooth in a neighborhood of

with

,

\log(1+\frac{g}{m}(z,[A(z)]))+\frac{\varepsilon_{\min}}{2}\|z\|^{2}

is plurisubharmonic for any holomorphic section

V_{j}
for each

(

has to be in the domain of

). Therefore,

\log\big(G(m+g)(A(z))\big)-\frac{\varepsilon_{\min}}{2}\|z\|^{2}=\log Gm(A(z))-\varepsilon_{\min}\|z\|^{2}+\log\big(1+\frac{g}{m}([A(z)])\big)+\frac{\varepsilon_{\min}}{2}\|z\|^{2}

is plurisubharmonic for any holomorphic section

of

defined in

for each

. We have verified the condition (

). The strong convexity is verified the same way as in the proof of Lemma

.

For the local approximation property, we fix a point

around

with fiber coordinates
(\zeta_{1},...,\zeta_{r})
. Around the point

(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},w_{1},\ldots,w_{r-1})

for

. So

is a holomorphic frame for

.

Let

be an open set in this coordinate neighborhood, and

over

. The condition (

Gm_{\delta}+\delta H
, constructed as in the proof of Lemma
13
, is strongly plurisubharmonic and strongly convex. Since we have smoothness for the Finsler metric
Gm_{\delta}+\delta H
, it is Kobayashi negative by Lemma
7
, and so it satisfies the condition (
2.8
) by Lemma
6
. The function

\frac{Gm_{\delta}+\delta H}{G}(z,\zeta)

is therefore a smooth section of the sheaf
S^{\prime}
, and it approximates
m
in the sense of the conditions (a) and (b) in
[
GW79
, Definition 1.4]
for
\delta
small (with
f
and
F
there replaced with
m
and
(Gm_{\delta}+\delta H)/G
respectively).

As a result,
S^{\prime}
restricted to the coordinate neighborhood has the semilocal approximation property, and hence
S^{\prime}
has the local approximation property.
∎

If one does not need convexity and only wants to show that a strongly plurisubharmonic continuous Finsler metric can be approximated by the smooth ones, then a much shorter proof can be given based on the argument above. One can also use Richberg’s regularization theorem
[
Ric68
]
in this case.

Proof of main results

Let us recall the statements of Theorem
1
:

1.

The quotient bundle of a Kobayashi positive vector bundle is Kobayashi positive.

2.

If
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are Kobayashi positive, then
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
is Kobayashi positive.

3.

If
E
is Kobayashi positive, then the tensor power
E^{\otimes k}
and the symmetric power
S^{k}E
are Kobayashi positive for
k\geq 1
, and the exterior power
\bigwedge^{k}E
is Kobayashi positive for
1\leq k\leq\text{rank }E
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

1. Let
E
be a Kobayashi positive vector bundle and
E_{2}
be a quotient bundle of
E
. By Lemma
10
, the dual bundle
E^{*}
is convex Kobayashi negative. Since
E_{2}^{*}
is a subbundle of
E^{*}
, the bundle
E^{*}_{2}
is convex Kobayashi negative by Lemma
11
. Using Lemma
8
, the bundle
E_{2}
is convex Kobayashi positive.

One can give another proof based on
[
Lem17a
, Lemma 2.3]
who uses the quotient norm. However, before applying Lempert’s lemma, one has to make sure the metrics are convex, and this is justified since Kobayashi positivity implies convexity, Lemma
10
.

2.
By Lemma
10
, we assume that
E_{1}
carries a convex and strongly pseudoconvex Kobayashi positive Finsler metric
F_{1}
, and
E_{2}^{*}
carries a convex and strongly pseudoconvex Kobayashi negative Finsler metric
F_{2}
. We have the operator norm on the bundle
\Hom(E_{1},E_{2}^{*})
defined by

F_{3}(z,A):=\sup_{0\neq\zeta\in E_{1}|_{z}}\frac{F_{2}(z,A\zeta)}{F_{1}(z,\zeta)}\text{\,\, for $A\in\Hom(E_{1},E_{2}^{*})|_{z}$ }.

Obviously,
F_{3}
satisfies positivity and homogeneity in the definition of a Finsler metric. Using the continuity of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
, it is not hard to verify that
F_{3}
is continuous on the total space
\Hom(E_{1},E_{2}^{*})
, but we do not know if
F_{3}
is smooth away from the zero section. So
F_{3}
is only a continuous Finsler metric which is sufficient for our proof later. In addition, the continuous Finsler metric
F_{3}
satisfies
F_{3}(z,A+B)\leq F_{3}(z,A)+F_{3}(z,B)
since the Finsler metric
F_{2}
is convex. Later on, if the context is clear, we will simply write

F_{3}(z,A)=F_{3}(A)
.

Let
H
be a Hermitian metric on
\Hom(E_{1},E_{2}^{*})
. Fix a point in
X
and a coordinate neighborhood
U
with coordinates
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
. Since the Finsler metric
F_{2}
is Kobayashi negative, its Kobayashi curvature
\Theta|_{O_{P(E^{*}_{2})}(-1)}
satisfies

\Theta|_{O_{P(E^{*}_{2})}(-1)}\leq-2\varepsilon\sum_{\alpha}dz_{\alpha}\wedge d\bar{z}_{\alpha}

in
P(E_{2}^{*}|_{U})
for some
\varepsilon>0
by using the local formula (
2.3
). Together with Lemma
6
, we get

(3.1)

\partial\bar{\partial}\log F^{2}_{2}(\phi)(v,\bar{v})\geq 2\varepsilon\sum_{\alpha}|v_{\alpha}|^{2},

where
\phi
is any local holomorphic section of
E_{2}^{*}
and
v=\sum v_{\alpha}\partial/\partial z_{\alpha}
is any vector field in
U
. In particular, there exists a positive constant
c_{0}
such that for
0\leq c\leq c_{0}
, we have

(3.2)

\partial\bar{\partial}\log\big[F^{2}_{2}(A(z)\psi(z))+cH(A(z))F^{2}_{1}(\psi(z))\big](v,\bar{v})\geq\varepsilon\sum_{\alpha}|v_{\alpha}|^{2},

where
A(z)
is any local holomorphic section of
\Hom(E_{1},E_{2}^{*})
and
\psi(z)
is any local holomorphic section of
E_{1}
, and
v=\sum v_{\alpha}\partial/\partial z_{\alpha}
is any vector field in
U
.

Now, we fix
c
with
0\leq c\leq c_{0}
and a local holomorphic section
A(z)
of
\Hom(E_{1},E_{2}^{*})
defined in
U
. From (
3.2
), for any local holomorphic section
\psi
of
E_{1}
, the function

(3.3)

\log\big[F^{2}_{2}(A\psi)+cH(A)F^{2}_{1}(\psi)\big]-\varepsilon\|z\|^{2}

is plurisubharmonic, where
\|z\|^{2}=\sum_{\alpha}|z_{\alpha}|^{2}
. Next, we let

\displaystyle h(z):

\displaystyle=\log\big[F^{2}_{3}(A(z))+cH(A(z))\big]-\varepsilon\|z\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\log\sup_{0\neq\zeta\in E_{1}|_{z}}\frac{\big[F^{2}_{2}(A(z)\zeta)+cH(A(z))F_{1}^{2}(\zeta)\big]e^{-\varepsilon\|z\|^{2}}}{F^{2}_{1}(\zeta)}.

We are going to show that the function
h(z)
is plurisubharmonic in
U
. Fix a complex line in
U
. Without loss of generality, we assume the complex line has the direction in
z_{1}
, namely,
\mathbb{C}\ni\lambda\mapsto p_{0}+\lambda(1,0,\cdots,0)
where
p_{0}
is a point in
U
. For a point
z_{0}
on the complex line, we can find a nonzero vector
\zeta_{0}\in E_{1}|_{z_{0}}
such that

(3.4)

h(z_{0})=\log\frac{\big[F^{2}_{2}(A(z_{0})\zeta_{0})+cH(A(z_{0}))F_{1}^{2}(\zeta_{0})\big]e^{-\varepsilon\|z_{0}\|^{2}}}{F^{2}_{1}(\zeta_{0})}.

Since the Finsler metric
F_{1}
is Kobayashi positive, if we choose
v=v_{1}\partial/\partial z_{1}\in T^{1,0}_{z_{0}}X
in Lemma
6
, then

(3.5)

0<-\inf\partial\bar{\partial}\log F_{1}^{2}(\psi)\bigr|_{z_{0}}(v,\bar{v}),

the inf taken over local holomorphic sections
\psi
of
E_{1}
such that
\psi(z_{0})=\zeta_{0}
. We can find such a
\psi
so that

(3.6)

0>\partial\bar{\partial}\log F_{1}^{2}(\psi)\bigr|_{z_{0}}(v,\bar{v})=\frac{\partial^{2}\log F_{1}^{2}(\psi)}{\partial z_{1}\partial\bar{z}_{1}}\Bigr|_{z_{0}}|v_{1}|^{2}.

By the definition of
h(z)
, we have

(3.7)

h(z)\geq\log\frac{\big[F^{2}_{2}(A(z)\psi(z))+cH(A(z))F_{1}^{2}(\psi(z))\big]e^{-\varepsilon\|z\|^{2}}}{F^{2}_{1}(\psi(z))}\text{\,\, for $z$ near $z_{0}$ in $U$}

with equality at
z_{0}
by (
3.4
). Now, we restrict to the complex line and denote by
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(z_{0},r)}h(z)
the average of
h
over a ball centered at
z_{0}
with radius
r
in the complex line. The inequality (
3.7
) yields

\displaystyle\big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(z_{0},r)}h(z)\big)-h(z_{0})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(z_{0},r)}\log\big[F^{2}_{2}(A\psi)+cH(A)F^{2}_{1}(\psi)\big]e^{-\varepsilon\|z\|^{2}}-\log F^{2}_{1}(\psi)\big)

\displaystyle-\big(\log\big[F^{2}_{2}(A(z_{0})\zeta_{0})+cH(A(z_{0}))F^{2}_{1}(\zeta_{0})\big]e^{-\varepsilon\|z_{0}\|^{2}}-\log F^{2}_{1}(\zeta_{0})\big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(z_{0},r)}-\log F^{2}_{1}(\psi)\big)+\log F^{2}_{1}(\zeta_{0}),

where the second inequality is due to the fact
\log\big[F^{2}_{2}(A\psi)+cH(A)F^{2}_{1}(\psi)\big]-\varepsilon\|z\|^{2}
is plurisubharmonic, namely (
3.3
). As a result,

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(z_{0},r)}h(z)-h(z_{0})\big)\geq\liminf_{r\to 0}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(z_{0},r)}\log F^{2}_{1}(\psi)-\log F^{2}_{1}(\zeta_{0})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}\log F^{2}_{1}(\psi)}{\partial z_{1}\partial\bar{z}_{1}}\Bigr|_{z_{0}}>0,

where we use (
3.6
) in the last inequality. Hence
\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(z_{0},r)}h(z)-h(z_{0})\big)\geq 0
, and

is subharmonic on the complex line (for the last argument, one can see
[

,
[

,
[

, or
[

, Lemma 2.2]
).

X
and a coordinate neighborhood
U
, there exist
\varepsilon>0
and a positive constant
c_{0}
such that for
0\leq c\leq c_{0}
and for any holomorphic section
A(z)
of
\Hom(E_{1},E_{2}^{*})
defined in

, the function
\log[F^{2}_{3}(A(z))+cH(A(z))]-\varepsilon\|z\|^{2}
is plurisubharmonic.

Since
X
is compact, we can find
\varepsilon_{\min}>0
and
c_{\min}>0
such that for each point in
X
there exists a coordinate neighborhood so that
the function
\log(F^{2}_{3}+c_{\min}H)(A(z))-\varepsilon_{\min}\|z\|^{2}
is plurisubharmonic for any local holomorphic section

of
\Hom(E_{1},E_{2}^{*})
defined in this neighborhood.

As a result, the continuous Finsler metric
(F_{3}^{2}+c_{\min}H)
on the bundle
\Hom(E_{1},E_{2}^{*})
satisfies the condition (
2.8
) and is strongly convex. By Lemma
14
, there exists a Finsler metric
F_{4}
on
\Hom(E_{1},E_{2}^{*})
that is strongly convex and satisfies the condition (

). By Lemma

, the Finsler metric
F_{4}
is Kobayashi negative. As a consequence, the bundle

is convex Kobayashi negative, hence

is convex Kobayashi positive by Lemma

.

Recall the statements of Theorem
2
:

1.

The direct sum of vector bundles
E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
is Kobayashi positive if and only if
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are both Kobayashi positive.

2.

In the short exact sequence of vector bundles
0\to E_{1}\to E\to E_{2}\to 0
, if
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are Kobayashi positive, then
E
is Kobayashi positive.

3.

If
f:Y\to X
is an immersion, and
E
is a Kobayashi positive vector bundle over
X
, then the pull-back
f^{*}E
is Kobayashi positive.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

1. The forward direction follows from the statement
1
in Theorem
1
. For the backward direction, we have two Kobayashi positive vector bundles
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, hence by Lemma
10
the duals
E_{1}^{*}
and
E_{2}^{*}
are convex Kobayashi negative, and we denote by
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
the convex, strongly pseudoconvex, Kobayashi negative Finsler metrics on
E_{1}^{*}
and
E_{2}^{*}
respectively. Let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be two Hermitian metrics on
E_{1}^{*}
and
E_{2}^{*}
respectively. Choose
\varepsilon>0
small such that the Finsler metrics
F_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon H_{1}
and
F_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon H_{2}
are still Kobayashi negative.

On the direct sum
E^{*}_{1}\oplus E^{*}_{2}
, we define
F
by

(3.8)

F(z,(\xi,\eta))=\big((F_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon H_{1})(z,\xi)+(F_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon H_{2})(z,\eta)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where
\xi
and
\eta
are vectors in
E_{1}^{*}|_{z}
and
E_{2}^{*}|_{z}
respectively. It is obvious that
F
satisfies positivity and homogeneity in the definition of a Finsler metric. However,
F
is smooth only on
(E^{*}_{1}\smallsetminus\{0\})\oplus(E^{*}_{2}\smallsetminus\{0\})
rather than on
E^{*}_{1}\oplus E^{*}_{2}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, but
F
is still continuous on the total space
E^{*}_{1}\oplus E^{*}_{2}
since
F_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon H_{1}
and
F_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon H_{2}
are continuous on
E^{*}_{1}
and
E^{*}_{2}
respectively. Hence
F
is only a continuous Finsler metric. By the convexity of
F_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon H_{1}
and
F_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon H_{2}
, we see that
F^{2}
is convex.

By Lemma
7
, the Finsler metric
F_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon H_{1}
is strongly plurisubharmonic on
E_{1}^{*}\smallsetminus\{0\}
and plurisubharmonic on
E_{1}^{*}
, and the same is true for
F_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon H_{2}
on
E_{2}^{*}
. Therefore, the continuous Finsler metric
F^{2}
is strongly plurisubharmonic on
E^{*}_{1}\oplus E^{*}_{2}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.

Let
H
be a Hermtian metric on
E^{*}_{1}\oplus E^{*}_{2}
. By choosing
c>0
small, the continuous Finsler metric
F^{2}+cH
is strongly plurisubharmonic and strongly convex. By Lemma
13
, there exists a Finsler metric
F_{3}
that is strongly plurisubharmonic and strongly convex. Hence
F_{3}
is Kobayashi negative by Lemma
7
. Therefore, the bundle
E^{*}_{1}\oplus E^{*}_{2}
with the Finsler metric
F_{3}
is convex Kobayashi negative, so the bundle
E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
is convex Kobayashi positive by Lemma
8
.

2. This is practically the content of
[
Ume73
, Lemma 2.2]
, but Umemura dealt with Hermitian metrics only. We will follow closely Umemura’s argument. We consider the sequence of dual bundles
0\to E_{2}^{*}\to E^{*}\to E_{1}^{*}\to 0
. Since
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are Kobayashi positive, the duals
E_{1}^{*}
and
E_{2}^{*}
are convex Kobayashi negative. We borrow from the proof of the statement 1 the Finsler metrics
F_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon H_{1}
and
F_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon H_{2}
, and we denote them by
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
.

Let
E^{*}
be determined by
v\in H^{1}(X,\Hom(E^{*}_{1},E^{*}_{2}))
. Take a sufficiently fine open covering
\{U_{a}\}
for
X
so that the extension
E^{*}
is given by patching
E^{*}_{2}|_{U_{a}}\oplus E^{*}_{1}|_{U_{a}}
and
E^{*}_{2}|_{U_{b}}\oplus E^{*}_{1}|_{U_{b}}
on
U_{a}\bigcap U_{b}
by

\begin{pmatrix}I&A_{ba}\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}

where
A_{ba}\in H^{0}(U_{a}\bigcap U_{b},\Hom(E^{*}_{1},E^{*}_{2}))
. Then the extension
E^{*}_{\lambda}
determined by
\lambda v\in H^{1}(X,\Hom(E^{*}_{1},E^{*}_{2}))
with

\begin{pmatrix}I&-\lambda B_{a}\\

gives rise to a smooth bundle isomorphism

from
E^{*}_{2}\oplus E^{*}_{1}
to

.

On the direct sum
E^{*}_{2}\oplus E^{*}_{1}
, we define as in (

) a continuous Finsler metric

G(z,(\eta,\xi))=G_{2}(z,\eta)+G_{1}(z,\xi)
where
\eta
and

are vectors in
E_{2}^{*}|_{z}

respectively (we use the convention

3. We first consider a more general case where the map
f:X\to Y
is smooth and the bundle
E
carries a Finsler metric
F
. The pull-back bundle
f^{*}E
has the pull-back Finsler metric
f^{*}F
defined as

f^{*}F(x,\zeta)=F(f(x),\zeta)\text{, for $\zeta\in E_{f(x)}$.}

It is not hard to check that if
F
is convex or strongly pseudoconvex, then so is
f^{*}F
.

Now, we consider the case in the statement
3
. We assume that
f
is an immersion and
E
is Kobayashi positive. By Lemma
10
, the dual
E^{*}
is convex Kobayashi negative, and we let
F^{*}
be a convex, strongly pseudoconvex, Kobayashi negative Finsler metric on
E^{*}
. From the discussion in the previous paragraph, the pull-back Finsler metric
f^{*}F^{*}
on
f^{*}E^{*}
is convex and strongly pseudoconvex. By Lemma

, the Finsler metric
F^{*}
is strongly plurisubharmonic on
E^{*}-\{0\}
. Since
f
is an immersion and
f^{*}F^{*}(x,\zeta)=F^{*}(f(x),\zeta)
, a direct computation shows that
f^{*}F^{*}
is strongly plurisubharmonic on

Alternatively, one can use formula (

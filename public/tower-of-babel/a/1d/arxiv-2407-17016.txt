q q -deformed Griffiths polynomials of Racah type
Nicolas Crampé, Luc Frappat, Julien Gaboriaud, Eric Ragoucy

Abstract




Abstract

New bivariate Griffiths polynomials of
q
-Racah type are introduced and characterized.
They generalize the polynomials orthogonal on the multinomial distribution introduced by R. Griffiths fifty years ago.
They also correspond to a
q
-deformation of the Griffiths polynomials of Racah type introduced previously by the authors and collaborators. The latter are recovered from the former by a
q\to 1
limit.
We show that these new polynomials are bispectral and biorthogonal. We also exhibit some symmetry relations
that are essential in the proof of the bispectrality property.

Keywords:
Orthogonal polynomials; Bivariate polynomials;
Bispectral problem

1
Introduction

In the study of special functions, a special class of polynomials, called the
(
q
-)hypergeometric orthogonal polynomials, plays a special role
[
1
,
27
]
.
This class comprises diverse families of polynomials who
are usually arranged in a scheme, called the (
q
-)Askey scheme,
which allows one to visualize the connections between the members of this class (see

[
47
]
for a review).
All these families of polynomials are orthogonal and satisfy a three-term recurrence
relation.
In addition, they satisfy a second order differential equation or a three-term
(
q
-)difference equation.
They are described as bispectral, meaning that they satisfy a recurrence
relation and a difference equation in two distinct variables.
The (
q
-)Racah polynomials sit at the top of this scheme and the various other
polynomials can be obtained from the latter by limits or specializations on the
parameters.
A generalization to the multivariate case is important.

The goal of this paper is to provide a bivariate generalization of the
q
-Racah
polynomials that possesses the bispectrality property amongst many other properties.

Let us emphasize that the generalization introduced here is different from the one
defined in
[
62
]
: that one is obtained as a truncation of the Koornwinder–Macdonald
multivariable generalization of the Askey–Wilson polynomials.

A number of multivariate generalizations of orthogonal polynomials have been studied
throughout the years.
In
[
35
,
23
]
, a
n
variable generalization of the Krawtchouk polynomials (which
are a limiting case of the Racah polynomials) has been introduced through a generating
function, and it has been proven that these polynomials are orthogonal with respect to the
multinomial distribution.
They have also been obtained in the context of a probabilistic model in
[
38
]
(and
have been called Rahman polynomials for a while), expressed in terms of Gel’fand-Aomoto
hypergeometric series
[
51
]
, and appeared as the
3nj
-symbol of the oscillator
algebra
[
61
,
46
,
66
,
17
]
.
These polynomials are usually called the Griffiths polynomials but, in this paper, we
shall call them the
Griffiths polynomials of Krawtchouk type
to distinguish them
from the ones we will construct.

A
n
variable generalization of the (
q
-)Racah polynomials has been obtained in

[
57
,
28
]
; here we shall call it the
Tratnik polynomials of (
q
-)Racah
type
. These multivariate polynomials are bispectral and have been studied intensively in
different contexts (see
e.g.

[
32
,
40
,
56
,
29
,
4
,
36
]
). Taking a limit on
the Tratnik polynomials of Racah type, one obtains a multivariate generalization of the
Krawtchouk polynomials, called here the
Tratnik polynomials of Krawtchouk type
.
These polynomials in
n
variables depend on
n
parameters whereas the Griffiths
polynomials of Krawtchouk type depend on
\tfrac{1}{2}n(n+1)
parameters. In
[
31
]
, a
group theoretical interpretation of the Griffiths polynomials of Krawtchouk type has been
given (see also
[
54
,
42
,
41
]
). This point of view showed that the Tratnik
polynomials of Krawtchouk type can be obtained by specifying some parameters of the
Griffiths polynomials of Krawtchouk type. The latter polynomials were also expressed as
sums of a nested product of univariate Krawtchouk polynomials.

It is then natural to ask whether it is possible to define multivariate polynomials based
on univariate Racah or
q
-Racah polynomials which, in a certain limit, reproduce the
Griffiths polynomials of Krawtchouk type. Recently, studying the representation theory of
the rank 2 Racah algebra, bivariate Griffiths polynomials of Racah type have been computed

[
15
]
and their bispectral property has been obtained. These polynomials are
expressed as a sum of a product of three univariate Racah polynomials and share this
structure with the bivariate Griffiths polynomials of Krawtchouk type. They have
subsequently been studied in more detail in
[
13
]
, where the properties of the
bivariate polynomials have been established using only the properties of the univariate
Racah polynomials.
In addition, it has been proven (see
[
13
]
) that a limit of the
bivariate Griffiths polynomials of Racah type reproduces the bivariate Griffiths
polynomials of Krawtchouk type; this provides a justification for their
designation as “bivariate Griffiths polynomials”.

In this paper, we study a
q
-deformed version of the bivariate Griffiths polynomials of
Racah type. In other words,
we study
bivariate Griffiths polynomials of
q
-Racah type
which
are expressed as a sum of a product of three univariate
q
-Racah polynomials (see
relation (
5.1
)). From these, a limit
q\to 1
leads to the Griffith polynomials of
Racah type just discussed. Using properties of the univariate
q
-Racah polynomial, we
prove numerous properties for the proposed bivariate polynomials: bispectral properties,
biorthogonality, symmetry, duality, etc. The proofs differ from the ones in

[
13
]
since the properties used there seem not to generalize to the

q
-deformed case. Instead, we make use of a symmetry under the exchange of the two
variables, which appears to be crucial and was not yet established in
[
13
]
for
the undeformed case. To establish this symmetry, we use the Racah and Biedenharn–Elliot
relations for the monovariate
q
-Racah polynomials (see relations (
4.1
)
and (
4.6
)). These properties are proven using the
underlying algebra of the bispectral problem which is the rank 2 Askey–Wilson algebra

\mathbf{aw}(4)
. As a byproduct of this construction, we provide finite-dimensional representations
of the
\mathbf{aw}(4)
algebra.

The plan of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
, the univariate
q
-Racah
polynomials are defined in terms of
q
-hypergeometric functions
(whose definition can be found in Appendix
A
) and their well-known
properties, such as bispectrality, duality and orthogonality, are recalled.
Supplementary relations, called contiguity relations, are proven in subsection

2.2
; these are then used to prove various properties of the Tratnik
polynomials of
q
-Racah type in Section
3
.
Section
4
is devoted to the Racah relation and the Biedenharn–Elliot
relation for the univariate
q
-Racah polynomials.
The proofs use the representation theory of the rank
1
Askey–Wilson algebra
\mathbf{aw}(3)

and rank
2
Askey–Wilson algebra
\mathbf{aw}(4)
respectively,
whose definitions and representations are provided in Appendices
B

and
C
respectively.
We then come to the main study of the paper in Section
5
: the definition
and properties of the Griffiths polynomials of
q
-Racah type. These polynomials are
written as a sum of a product of three univariate
q
-Racah polynomials. We show in
subsection
5.1
that these polynomials satisfy a certain symmetry under the
exchange of the two degrees and of the two variables simultaneously. Then, the duality and
biorthogonality relations are established in subsection
5.2
. Subsection

5.3
contains the bispectral properties of these Griffiths polynomials.
One recurrence relation is easily proven knowing the one of the Tratnik polynomials and
the other one is deduced from it by leveraging the symmetry relation. The difference
equations are then obtained thanks to the duality relation.
We conclude the paper in Section
6
by listing some open problems.

2
Properties of the
q
-Racah polynomials

The
q
-Racah polynomials are defined (see
e.g.

[
47
]
) in terms of the

q
-hypergeometric functions as follows, for
N
a nonnegative integer and

0\leq n\leq N
:

\displaystyle R_{n}(\mu(x);\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)={{}_{4}\phi_{3}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{q^{-n},\; \alpha\beta q^{n+1},\; q^{-x},\; \gamma\delta q^{x+1}}{\alpha q,\; \beta\delta q,\; \gamma q}\middle|q;q\right)}\,,

(2.1)

where
\mu(x)=q^{-x}+\gamma\delta q^{x+1}
and either
\alpha
,
\beta\delta
or
\gamma

is equal to
q^{-N-1}
. For this paper to be self-contained, the definitions and notations
pertaining to basic hypergeometric functions and
q
-Pochhammer symbols are
recalled in Appendix
A
.

It is well-known that the
q
-Racah polynomials
R_{n}(\mu(x);\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)

are polynomials of degree
n
with respect to
\mu(x)
and possess nice properties such
as orthogonality and bispectrality. We now recall some of these properties after making a
slight change of notation which will prove useful for the rest of the paper.

Let us write the parameters
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
and
\delta
in the following way:

\displaystyle\alpha=c_{2}\,,\qquad\beta=c_{3}\,,\qquad\gamma=q^{-N-1}\,,\qquad\delta=c_{1}c_{2}q^{N+1}\,.

(2.2)

We emphasize that
\delta
depends on
N
, which is just a choice of parameter here,
but this choice is crucial in the determination of the contiguity relations
(
2.18
), (
2.22
)
and for the definitions of the Tratnik polynomials (
3.1
)
or Griffiths polynomials (
5.1
) of
q
-Racah type.

We also use a particular normalization for the
q
-Racah polynomials

{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)
throughout this paper. For
N
a nonnegative
integer and
0\leq n\leq N
:

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)=\Omega(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,{{}_{4}\phi_{3}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{q^{-n},\; c_2c_3q^{n+1},\; q^{-x},\; c_1c_2q^{x+1}}{q c_2,\; c_1c_2c_3q^{N+2},\; q^{-N}}\middle|q;q\right)}\,,

(2.3)

where

\displaystyle\Omega(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)=\frac{(q^{N-n+1};q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}\frac{(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{2n+1})(qc_{2};q)_{n}(c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}q^{N+2};q)_{n}(qc_{1};q)_{N-n}}{(qc_{2})^{n-N/2}(c_{2}c_{3}q^{n+1};q)_{N+1}(qc_{3};q)_{n}}\,.

(2.4)

The normalization
\Omega(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)
is intimately linked to the weight and the
norm of the
q
-Racah polynomials, as will be shown below.
The function
{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)
is a polynomial of degree
n
with respect to
q^{-x}+c_{1}c_{2}q^{x+1}
.
When
n>N
, this function is also well-defined and satisfies
{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)=0
for
x
nonnegative integer and
x\leq N
.

Lastly, note that replacing
c_{i}\mapsto q^{c_{i}}
and taking the limit
q\to 1
,
we recover the Racah polynomials with the normalization used in
[
13
]
.

2.1
Bispectral property, duality and orthogonality

The
q
-Racah polynomials solve a bispectral problem
i.e.
verify a recurrence
relation and a difference equation
[
47
]
.
They are also orthogonal and satisfy a duality property. Below, these relations
are recalled in the notations used in this paper.

Symmetry relation.

The
q
-Racah polynomials obey the symmetry relation

{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)=\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{3}}\right)^{N}\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(x;c_{1}^{-1},c_{2}^{-1},c_{3}^{-1};N)\Big|_{q\to q^{-1}}.

(2.5)

The notation
q\to q^{-1}
means that the corresponding expression is defined
by replacing
q
by
q^{-1}
everywhere, including the Pochhammer symbols and the basic
hypergeometric functions.
This relation is a direct consequence of the relations

\Omega(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\Big|_{q\to q^{-1},c_{i}\to c_{i}^{-1}}=\left(\frac{c_{3}}{c_{1}}\right)^{N}\Omega(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N),

(2.6)

{{}_{4}\phi_{3}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{q^{-n},\;c_2c_3q^{n+1}, \;q^{-x},\; c_1c_2q^{x+1} }{q c_2,\;c_1c_2c_3 q^{N+2},\;q^{-N} }\middle|q;q\right)}={{}_{4}\phi_{3}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{q^{n},\;(c_2c_3q^{n+1})^{-1}, \;q^{x},\; (c_1c_2q^{x+1})^{-1} }{(qc_2)^{-1},\;(c_1c_2c_3q^{N+2})^{-1},\;q^{N} }\middle|\dfrac{1}{q};\dfrac{1}{q}\right)},

(2.7)

that are themselves checked straightforwardly.

Duality.

Using the invariance of the
q
-hypergeometric functions mentioned in Appendix

A
,
we can show that the
q
-Racah polynomials satisfy the following duality relation,
for
0\leq n,x\leq N
:

\displaystyle\Omega(x;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)=\Omega(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{x}(n;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N)\,.

(2.8)

Orthogonality.

For
0\leq n,m\leq N
,
the orthogonality relation between two
q
-Racah polynomials is:

\displaystyle\sum_{x=0}^{N}\Omega(x;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{m}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)=\delta_{n,m}\,\Omega(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,.

(2.9)

Strictly speaking, the term
orthogonality
is valid only if the weight

\Omega(x;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N)
is strictly positive for
0\leq x\leq N
.
This condition imposes restrictions on the parameters entering in the definition of the

q
-Racah polynomials.

The orthogonality relation composed with the duality relation leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{x=0}^{N}{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{x}(m;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N)=\delta_{n,m}\,.

(2.10)

We call (
2.10
) the
involution relation
.

Recurrence relation.

It is well-known from the classical theory of orthogonal polynomials that
an orthogonal polynomial sequence obeys a three-term recurrence relation.
For the
q
-Racah polynomials, it is given as follows, for
0\leq n,x\leq N
:

\displaystyle\lambda(x;c_{1}c_{2})\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)=\sum_{\varepsilon=0,\pm}\Phi^{\varepsilon}(n+\varepsilon;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n+\varepsilon}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,,

(2.11)

where

\displaystyle\lambda(x;c)

\displaystyle=-(1-q^{-x})(1-cq^{x+1})\,,

(2.12a)

\displaystyle\Phi^{+}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)

\displaystyle=\frac{-c_{2}q^{n-N}(1-q^{n})(1-c_{1}q^{N-n+1})(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{n+N+1})(1-c_{3}q^{n})}{(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{2n})(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{2n+1})}\,,

(2.12b)

\displaystyle\Phi^{-}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-q^{n-N})(1-c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}q^{n+N+2})(1-c_{2}q^{n+1})(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{n+1})}{(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{2n+1})(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{2n+2})}\,,

(2.12c)

\displaystyle\Phi^{0}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)

\displaystyle=-\Phi^{+}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)-\Phi^{-}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,.

(2.12d)

In the above and throughout the paper, the notation
\varepsilon=0,\pm
should be understood
as
\varepsilon=0,\pm 1
whenever the
\varepsilon
gets added or multiplied.
Let us remark that for
n=0
, the recurrence relation involves

{\mathfrak{p}}_{-1}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)
, which vanishes by convention.
For
n=N
, it involves
{\mathfrak{p}}_{N+1}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)
, which is zero as mentioned above.
In the recurrence relation, the denominators appearing in the various coefficients
must not vanish. This leads to the following constraints on the parameters:

\displaystyle c_{2}c_{3}\notin\{q^{-2},q^{-3},\dots,q^{-2N}\}\,.

(2.13)

There exist apparent poles for
c_{2}c_{3}=1,q^{-1},q^{-2N-1},q^{-2N-2}
but the residues on
these points vanish.

We also demand that the coefficients
\Phi^{+}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)
and

\Phi^{-}(n-1;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)
do not vanish for
n=1,\dots,N
. This leads to the
supplementary constraints:

\displaystyle c_{2},q\neq 0\,,\quad q,q^{2},\dots,q^{N}\neq 1\,,\quad c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}q^{N+1}\notin\{q^{-1},q^{-2},\dots,q^{-N}\}\,.

(2.14)

Difference equation.

In addition to the previous recurrence relation, the
q
-Racah polynomials also satisfy a
difference equation which can be deduced from the duality and recurrence relations.
This difference equation reads, for
0\leq n,x\leq N
:

\displaystyle\lambda(n;c_{2}c_{3})\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon=0,\pm}\Phi^{\varepsilon}(x;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(x-\varepsilon;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,.

(2.15)

For
x=0
(resp.
x=N
), the difference equation simplifies since

\Phi^{+}(0;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N)=0
(resp.
\Phi^{-}(N;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N)=0
) which implies that

{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(N+1;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)
or
{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(-1;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)
never appear in the difference
equations.
As for the recurrence relation, the denominators appearing in the coefficients of the
difference equation must not vanish. This leads to the following constraints on the
parameters:

\displaystyle c_{1}c_{2}\notin\{q^{-2},\dots,q^{-2N}\}\,.

(2.16)

Due to the constraints (
2.14
) and (
2.16
),
\Phi^{+}(x;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N)

and
\Phi^{-}(x-1;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N)
do not vanish for
x=1,\dots,N
.

From now on, we suppose that the constraints (
2.13
), (
2.14
) and
(
2.16
) are satisfied.

Remark 2.1
.

Evidently, these relations correspond to the usual recurrence relation and difference
equation of the
q
-Racah polynomials given, for example, in
[
47
]
.
Let us point out that, in order to simplify the presentation and upcoming formulas,
we have moved away from the usual naming conventions of the coefficients in
the literature. The usual names can be recovered as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Phi^{-}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)&=A_{n}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,,\\
\Phi^{+}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)&=C_{n}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,,\end{aligned}\qquad\begin{aligned} \Phi^{-}(x;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N)&=B(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,,\\
\Phi^{+}(x;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N)&=D(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,.\end{aligned}

(2.17)

The notation
\Phi^{\varepsilon}
,
\varepsilon\in\{+,0,-\}
used in this paper makes explicit the
symmetry between the coefficients of the recurrence relation and the difference equation
and allows for more compact expressions, see
e.g.
Section
C.2
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The constraints (
2.13
) and (
2.16
) are necessary and sufficient
conditions to ensure
\lambda(x;c_{1}c_{2})\neq\lambda(y;c_{1}c_{2})
and
\lambda(x;c_{2}c_{3})\neq\lambda(y;c_{2}c_{3})
for

2.2
Contiguity recurrence relations and difference equations

The contiguity relations relate polynomials with contiguous values of
N
.
For Racah polynomials, such relations played a crucial role in constructing the
representations of the rank
2
Racah algebra
[
15
]
. Such contiguity relations were
also used to prove the bispectral properties of the Griffiths polynomials of Krawtchouk
type in
[
12
]
and of Racah type in
[
13
]
. They also appear in the
study of the representations of the factorized Leonard pairs
[
19
]
.
These relations and their proofs for the
q
-Racah polynomials are given below and will be
utilized in the study of the bispectral properties of the Tratnik polynomials of
q
-Racah
type in Section
3
.

Proposition 2.3
.

The contiguity recurrence relations between the
q
-Racah polynomials are

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pm}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N\pm 1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon=0,\pm}{\Phi}^{\varepsilon}_{\pm}(n+\varepsilon;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n+\varepsilon}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,,

(2.18)

where

\displaystyle\lambda_{+}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)

\displaystyle=-(q^{-N-1}/c_{3}-q^{-x})(1-c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}q^{N+x+2})\,,

(2.19a)

\displaystyle\Phi_{+}^{+}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{qc_{2}}c_{2}q^{2n-N-1}\frac{(1-q^{n})(1-c_{1}q^{N-n+1})(1-c_{1}q^{N+2-n})(1-c_{3}q^{n})}{(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{2n})(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{2n+1})}\,,

(2.19b)

\displaystyle\Phi_{+}^{-}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{(1-c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}q^{n+N+2})(1-c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}q^{n+N+3})(1-c_{2}q^{n+1})(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{n+1})}{c_{3}q^{N+1}\sqrt{qc_{2}}(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{2n+1})(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{2n+2})}\,,

(2.19c)

\displaystyle\Phi_{+}^{0}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\varepsilon=\pm}\Phi_{+}^{\varepsilon}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)-\frac{(1-c_{1}c_{2}q^{N+2})(1-c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}q^{N+2})}{c_{3}q^{N+1}\sqrt{qc_{2}}}\,,

(2.19d)

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{-}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)

\displaystyle=-(q^{-N}-q^{-x})(1-c_{1}c_{2}q^{N+x+1})\,,

(2.19e)

\displaystyle\Phi_{-}^{+}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{qc_{2}}\frac{(1-q^{n})(1-c_{3}q^{n})(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{n+N})(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{n+N+1})}{q^{N}(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{2n})(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{2n+1})}\,,

(2.19f)

\displaystyle\Phi^{-}_{-}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)

\displaystyle=-c_{3}\sqrt{qc_{2}}q^{N}\,\frac{(1-q^{n-N})(1-q^{n-N+1})(1-c_{2}q^{n+1})(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{n+1})}{(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{2n+1})(1-c_{2}c_{3}q^{2n+2})}\,,

(2.19g)

\displaystyle\Phi^{0}_{-}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\varepsilon=\pm}\Phi_{-}^{\varepsilon}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)-\sqrt{qc_{2}}(1-c_{3}q^{N})(q^{-N}-1)\,.

(2.19h)

Proof.

The proof is done recursively on
n
. For small
n

3
Tratnik polynomials of
q
-Racah type

A bivariate generalization of the
q
-Racah polynomials has been introduced in

[

,
40
]
and is here called the

q
-Racah type
.
We define these polynomials, recall some of their properties and provide their proofs in
the notation of this paper.

For
i,j,x,y
nonnegative integers and
i+j\leq N

x+y\leq N

the bivariate Tratnik polynomials of
q
-Racah type are defined by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)={\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}(y;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4};N-x)\,,

(3.1)

where the parameter

appearing in the l.h.s. of the above relation is defined through
the following constraints between the parameters:

\displaystyle q^{2N+3}c_{0}c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}=1\,.

(3.2)

The notation
\mathcal{T}_{i,j}(x,y)
is short for
\mathcal{T}_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)

We demand that the constraints (

obeyed for each univariate polynomials,

the parameters must satisfy

\displaystyle c_{i},q\neq 0\,,\qquad q,q^{2},\dots,q^{N}\neq 1\,,

\displaystyle c_{1}c_{2},\,c_{2}c_{3},\,c_{0}c_{3},\,c_{0}c_{4},\,qc_{1}c_{2}c_{3},\,qc_{0}c_{3}c_{4}\notin\{q^{-2},q^{-3},\dots,q^{-2N}\}\,,

(3.3b)

\displaystyle c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}\notin\{q^{-1},q^{-2},\dots,q^{-N}\}\,.

(3.3c)

Let us emphasize that we could multiply the previous expression of

or

All the relations satisfied by

3.1
Polynomiality

We have been calling
\mathcal{T}_{i,j}(x,y)

\mathcal{T}_{i,j}(x,y)

Indeed, definition (

The grid is the domain over which the functions are orthogonal with respect to each other

3.3

It is possible to multiply

More precisely, we show below that, up to transformations only valid on the grid,

To simplify the notations, we define the following two functions:

\displaystyle F^{-}_{x}(c_{1},c_{2})

(3.20)

Proposition 3.5

(
Recurrence relations
)
.

For
i,j,x,y
nonnegative integers such that
x+y\leq N
and
i+j\leq N
,
the Tratnik polynomials obey the following recurrence relations:

\displaystyle\lambda(x;c_{1}c_{2})\,\mathcal{T}_{i,j}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon=0,\pm}\Phi^{\varepsilon}(i+\varepsilon;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)\,\mathcal{T}_{i+\varepsilon,j}(x,y)\,,

(3.21)

\displaystyle\lambda(y;c_{3}c_{0})\,\mathcal{T}_{i,j}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}=0,\pm}A^{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{i+\varepsilon,j+\varepsilon^{\prime}}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)\,\mathcal{T}_{i+\varepsilon,j+\varepsilon^{\prime}}(x,y)\,,

(3.22)

where
\lambda
and
\Phi^{\varepsilon}
are given by (

) and

\displaystyle A_{i,j}^{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)

\displaystyle=-c_{3}q^{N+1}\,F^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{j}(c_{4},c_{0})\,\Phi_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\varepsilon}(i;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)\,,\qquad\text{for $\varepsilon=0,\pm$, $\varepsilon^{\prime}=\pm$\,,}

(3.23a)

\displaystyle A_{i,j}^{\varepsilon,0}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)

\displaystyle=c_{3}q^{N+1}\Big(\!F^{+}_{j}(c_{4},c_{0})+F^{-}_{j}(c_{4},c_{0})\!\Big)\,\Phi^{\varepsilon}(i;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)\,,\quad\text{\,for $\varepsilon=\pm$\,,}

(3.23b)

\displaystyle A_{i,j}^{0,0}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(1-c_{3}q^{N+1-j})\sqrt{qc_{2}}}{1-c_{1}c_{2}q^{N+2-j}}\sum_{\varepsilon=0,\pm}A_{i,j}^{\varepsilon,+}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)

(3.23c)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt-\frac{1-c_{1}c_{2}q^{N+1-j}}{(1-c_{3}q^{N-j})\sqrt{qc_{2}}}\sum_{\varepsilon=0,\pm}A_{i,j}^{\varepsilon,-}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)-\sum_{\varepsilon=\pm}A_{i,j}^{\varepsilon,0}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)\,.

We apply the convention
\mathcal{T}_{-1,j}(x,y)=\mathcal{T}_{i,-1}(x,y)=\mathcal{T}_{-1,-1}(x,y)=0

Proof.

The equation (
3.21
) is a direct consequence of the recurrence equation
(
2.11
) applied to the Racah polynomial
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)
in

\mathcal{T}_{i,j}(x,y)

To prove (
3.22
), apply the recurrence equation to the second polynomial

{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}(y;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4};N-x)
such that the l.h.s. of (

\displaystyle\lambda(y;c_{3}c_{0})\,\mathcal{T}_{i,j}(x,y)={\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)\sum_{\varepsilon=0,\pm}\Phi^{\varepsilon}(j+\varepsilon;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4};N-x)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{j+\varepsilon}(y;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4};N-x)\,.

(3.24)

Remark that for

\displaystyle\Phi^{\varepsilon}(j+\varepsilon;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4};N-x)

\displaystyle=-c_{3}q^{N+1}F^{\varepsilon}_{j+\varepsilon}(c_{4},c_{0})\,\lambda_{\varepsilon}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};{N-j-\varepsilon})\,,

(3.25)

\displaystyle\Phi^{0}(j;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4};N-x)

\displaystyle=c_{3}q^{N+1}\Big(F_{j}^{+}(c_{4},c_{0})+F_{j}^{-}(c_{4},c_{0})\Big)\lambda(x;c_{1}c_{2})+g_{j}\,,

(3.26)

where

(3.27)

Then, apply the recurrence and contiguity recurrence relations (
2.11
) and
(

{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)
in
(

This allows to recognize Tratnik polynomials with shifted indices as written in
(

\displaystyle g_{j}=-\frac{(1-c_{3}q^{N+1-j})\sqrt{qc_{2}}}{1-c_{1}c_{2}q^{N+2-j}}\sum_{\varepsilon=0,\pm}A_{i,j}^{\varepsilon,+}-\frac{1-c_{1}c_{2}q^{N+1-j}}{(1-c_{3}q^{N-j})\sqrt{qc_{2}}}\sum_{\varepsilon=0,\pm}A_{i,j}^{\varepsilon,-}\,,

(3.28)

∎

Note that
\mathcal{T}_{N+1-j,j}(x,y)=0

Remark 3.6

Note that the normalization of the Tratnik polynomials can be changed in such a way that

\displaystyle\sum_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}=0,\pm}\widetilde{A}_{i,j}^{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)=0\,.

The relation (

Proof.

Remark 3.8

(
Generalized Favard theorem
)
.

A multivariate version of the Favard theorem has been established, see

e.g.
Theorem 3.2.8 in
[
25
]
. It provides necessary and sufficient
conditions for a family of multivariate polynomials satisfying recurrence relations to be
orthogonal. For bivariate polynomials
P_{i,j}(x_{1},x_{2})
of total degree
i+j
, the
recurrence relations must be of the following form, for
i=1,2
:

x_{i}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{n}={\mathbb{A}}_{n,i}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{n+1}+{\mathbb{B}}_{n,i}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{n}+{\mathbb{A}}^{T}_{n-1,i}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{n-1}\,.

(3.35)

In the above, we gather the
r_{n}
polynomials of total degree
n
in a vector
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
.
In the r.h.s.,
{\mathbb{A}}_{n,i}
and
{\mathbb{B}}_{n,i}
are matrices of size

r_{n}\times r_{n+1}
and
r_{n}\times r_{n}
respectively.
An additional necessary condition is that
{\mathbb{B}}_{n,i}
must be symmetric.

We now show that these conditions are satisfied for the bivariate Tratnik polynomials.
For nonnegative integers
i,j
such that
i+j\leq N
,
one defines

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{i,j}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{i,j}(x,y)\,/\,\mathcal{T}_{N,0}(x,y)

(3.36)

where
\mathcal{T}_{i,j}(x,y)
is given by (
3.8
).
It is of total degree
N-i
w.r.t.
\lambda(x;c_{1}c_{2})
and
\lambda(y;c_{0}c_{3})
.
Consider the vectors

{\mathbb{T}}_{n}=\Big(\nu_{n,0}\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{N-n,0}\,,\ \nu_{n,1}\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{N-n,1}\,,\ \dots\,,\ \nu_{n,n}\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{N-n,n}\Big)^{T}

(3.37)

containing all polynomials of total degree
n
renormalized by some coefficients
\nu_{i,j}
.
In order to get the recurrence relations (
3.22
) and (
3.21
)
in the form (
3.35
), we need to impose

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\left(\frac{\nu_{i,j+1}}{\nu_{i,j}}\right)^{2}=\frac{A^{0,+}_{N-i,j+1}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}{A^{0,-}_{N-i,j}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}\,,\qquad\left(\frac{\nu_{i+1,j}}{\nu_{i,j}}\right)^{2}=\frac{A^{-,0}_{N-i-1,j}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}{A^{+,0}_{N-i,j}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}\,,\\
\left(\frac{\nu_{i+1,j+1}}{\nu_{i,j}}\right)^{2}=\frac{A^{-,+}_{N-i-1,j+1}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}{A^{+,-}_{N-i,j}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}\,,\qquad\left(\frac{\nu_{i,j+1}}{\nu_{i+1,j}}\right)^{2}=\frac{A^{+,+}_{N-i,j+1}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}{A^{-,-}_{N-i-1,j}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}\,,\\
\left(\frac{\nu_{i+1,j}}{\nu_{i,j}}\right)^{2}=\frac{\Phi^{-}(N-i-1;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)}{\Phi^{+}(N-i;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)}\,,\end{gathered}

where
A_{i,j}^{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)
and
\Phi^{\varepsilon}(i+\varepsilon;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)

are the coefficients introduced in (
3.23
) and (
2.12
).
Using the explicit expression of these coefficients, one can show that in
(
3.8
), the second and third lines are a consequence of the first line, the
latter being sufficient to determine the coefficients
\nu
up to a global constant.
Thus, one concludes from the generalized Favard theorem that the polynomials
\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{i,j}(x,y)
are orthogonal, in accordance with the
result in Proposition
3.3
.
∎

4
Racah and Biedenharn–Elliot relations for
q
-Racah polynomials

Two fundamental relations involving univariate
q
-Racah polynomials are the Racah and
Biedenharn–Elliot relations. These relations will be key ingredients in
the next section.
Both these relations have been obtained in the study of
6j
-symbols of the quantum
algebra
U_{q}(su(2))
, meaning that they were valid only for a restricted range of
parameters.
We present them below in their full generality.
To the best of our knowledge, these general relations have not appeared in the literature
before and the proofs we provide are new.

4.1
Racah relation

The Racah relation appeared in the study of the
6j
-symbol for the Lie algebra
su(2)

(see
e.g.

[
49
]
) and has been related to the Racah algebra
(a limit
\textsc{q}\to 1
of the Askey–Wilson algebra) in
[
33
]
.
As the
6j
-symbol can be expressed in terms of Racah polynomials
[
64
,
44
]
,
the Racah relation leads to an identity for Racah polynomials with particular parameters.
This relation was then generalized through the study of the representations of
the Racah algebra
[
15
]
.
When the
6j
-symbol for the quantum group
U_{q}(su(2))
is instead considered,
there exists a similar relation involving the
q
-Racah polynomials with
particular parameters
[
45
]
and their study is intimately linked to
the Askey–Wilson algebra
\mathbf{aw}(3)

[
34
]
.
In this paper, using the representations of the Askey–Wilson algebra
\mathbf{aw}(3)
introduced
in the Appendix
B
and following the lines of the proof in the non-deformed
case
[
15
]
, we prove the Racah relation for generic
q
-Racah polynomials.

Proposition 4.1
.

The Racah relation between the
q
-Racah polynomials reads,
for
0\leq i,x\leq N
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{n=0}^{N}\ &\frac{\omega_{n}(c_{1}c_{2})}{\xi_{n}(c_{1},c_{2})}\frac{\omega_{i}(c_{2}c_{3})}{\xi_{i}(c_{2},c_{3})}\frac{\omega_{x}(c_{3}c_{1})}{\xi_{x}(c_{3},c_{1})}\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(n;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}(x;c_{3},c_{1},c_{2};N)\\
&=\omega_{N}(qc_{1}c_{2}c_{3})\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(x;c_{1},c_{3},c_{2};N)\,,\end{aligned}

(4.1)

where the functions
\xi
and
\omega
are defined in (
B.3
).

Proof.

Consider the three equivalent representations of
\mathbf{aw}(3)

\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{C}

introduced in the Appendix
B.2
.
The changes of basis between these representations (
B.8
) lead to

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}\mathcal{B}}\,\mathcal{B}_{I}\,\mathcal{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}}=\mathcal{C}_{I}=\mathcal{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{C}\mathcal{A}}\,\mathcal{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}\,\mathcal{B}_{I}\,\mathcal{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{B}\mathcal{A}}\,\mathcal{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{A}\mathcal{C}}\,,\qquad I\in\{12,23,13\}\,.

(4.2)

Since the representations
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
are irreducible,
the above formula implies that there exists (by Schur’s Lemma) a constant
\tau

such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{B}\mathcal{A}}\,\mathcal{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{A}\mathcal{C}}=\tau\,\mathcal{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}}\,.

(4.3)

To determine the constant
\tau
take the component
(0,0)
of the previous equation.
This leads to:

\displaystyle\tau=\left(\frac{qc_{1}c_{2}}{c_{3}}\right)^{\!\frac{N}{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{N}q^{Nk}\,\frac{(q^{-N};q)_{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}\frac{(1-c_{1}c_{2}q^{2k+1})(c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}q^{N+2};q)_{k}(qc_{3};q)_{N-k}}{(c_{1}c_{2}q^{k+1};q)_{N+1}}\,.

(4.4)

The above sum can be computed from the orthogonality relation of the
q
-Racah
polynomials. Starting from (
2.9
) in the case where
n=m=0
,
replace
c_{1}\mapsto c_{1}/t
and
c_{2}\mapsto c_{2}t
and take the limit
t\to\infty
.
This allows to identify

\displaystyle\tau=\omega_{N}(qc_{1}c_{2}c_{3})\,.

(4.5)

Finally, the entry
(i,x)
of relation (
4.3
) is
precisely the Racah relation of the proposition.
∎

4.2
Biedenharn–Elliot relation

Similarly to the Racah relation, the Biedenharn–Elliot relation is a relation between the

6j
-symbols for the Lie algebra
su(2)

[
8
,
26
]
which has been generalized to the
case of
the quantum group
U_{q}(su(2))
in
[
45
,
50
]
. In
[
15
]
, such a relation is also
obtained in the context of the Racah algebra. We provide here this relation for generic

q
-Racah polynomials.

Proposition 4.2
.

For
i,j,x,y
nonnegative integers such that
0\leq i+j\leq N
and
0\leq x+y\leq N
,
the Biedenharn–Elliot relation between the
q
-Racah polynomials is:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{a=0}^{\min(N-j,N-y)}&\;{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(a;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N-j)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}(y;c_{1},c_{0},c_{4};N-a)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{a}(x;c_{4},c_{3},c_{2};N-y)\\[-7.5pt]
&\times\big(\tfrac{c_{0}}{c_{3}}\big)^{\!\frac{i}{2}}\big(\tfrac{c_{3}}{c_{1}}\big)^{\!\frac{j}{2}}\big(\tfrac{c_{1}}{c_{4}}\big)^{\!\frac{a}{2}}\big(\tfrac{c_{4}}{c_{2}}\big)^{\!\frac{y}{2}}\big(\tfrac{c_{2}}{c_{0}}\big)^{\!\frac{x}{2}}\,\xi_{i}(c_{1},c_{2})\,\xi_{j}(c_{4},c_{0})\,\xi_{a}(c_{2},c_{3})\,\xi_{y}(c_{0},c_{1})\,\xi_{x}(c_{3},c_{4})\\[10.00002pt]
&={\mathfrak{p}}_{j}(x;c_{3},c_{4},c_{0};N-i)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(y;c_{0},c_{1},c_{2};N-x)\,.\end{aligned}

(4.6)

The last line of the equation can also be written as a Tratnik polynomial

\mathcal{T}_{j,i}(x,y;c_{3},c_{4},c_{0},c_{2};N)
.

Proof.

Consider the five equivalent representations of
\mathbf{aw}(4)

\mathsf{A}
,
\mathsf{B}
,
\mathsf{C}
,
\mathsf{D}
,
\mathsf{E}

introduced in the Appendix
C.2
.
The changes of basis between these representations (
C.4
) lead to

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{A}\mathsf{B}}\,\mathsf{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{B}\mathsf{C}}\,\mathsf{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{C}\mathsf{D}}\,\mathsf{D}_{I}\,\mathsf{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{D}\mathsf{C}}\,\mathsf{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{C}\mathsf{B}}\,\mathsf{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{B}\mathsf{A}}\,=\mathsf{A}_{I}=\mathsf{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{A}\mathsf{E}}\,\mathsf{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{E}\mathsf{D}}\,\mathsf{D}_{I}\,\mathsf{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{D}\mathsf{E}}\,\mathsf{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{E}\mathsf{A}}\,,\qquad I\in\{12,23,34,123,234\}\,.

(4.7)

Since the representations
\mathsf{A}
,
\mathsf{B}
,
\mathsf{C}
,
\mathsf{D}
and
\mathsf{E}
are irreducible,
the above formula implies that there exists (by Schur’s Lemma) a constant
\tau

such that

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{A}\mathsf{B}}\,\mathsf{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{B}\mathsf{C}}\,\mathsf{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{C}\mathsf{D}}\,=\tau\,\mathsf{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{A}\mathsf{E}}\,\mathsf{P}_{\!\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{E}\mathsf{D}}\,.

(4.8)

To determine the constant
\tau
take the component
(0,0);(0,0)
of
the previous equation. This leads to:

\displaystyle\tau=\frac{\Omega(0;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N)}{\Omega(0;c_{3},c_{4},c_{0};N)\,\Omega(0;c_{0},c_{1},c_{2};N)}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{4}}\Big)^{\!\frac{j}{2}}\xi_{j}(c_{2},c_{3})\,\Omega(0;c_{1},c_{0},c_{4};N-j)\,\Omega(j;c_{4},c_{3},c_{2};N)\,.

(4.9)

The above sum can be computed from the orthogonality relation of the
q
-Racah
polynomials. Starting from (
2.9
) in the case where
n=m=0
,
replace
c_{1}\mapsto 1/(c_{1}q^{N+1})
,
c_{2}\mapsto c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}q^{N+1}
,
c_{3}\mapsto c_{4}
.
We find that
\tau
is precisely equal to

\displaystyle\tau=1\,.

(4.10)

Finally, the entry
(i,j);(y,x)
of relation (
4.8
) is
precisely the Biedenharn–Elliot relation of the proposition.
∎

5
Griffiths polynomials of
q
-Racah type

We now introduce the main functions of this paper, called the

Griffiths polynomials of
q
-Racah type
.
For
i,j,x,y
nonnegative integers such that
i+j,x+y\leq N
, they are defined as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{a=0}^{M}\omega_{a}\Big(\frac{c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}}{c_{4}}\Big)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(a;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}(y;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4};N-a)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{a}(x;c_{4},c_{2},c_{1};N-y)\,,

(5.1)

where
M=\min(N-j,N-y)
. Due to the properties of the polynomials
p_{n}(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N)

(see beginning of Section
2
), the terms with
a>\min(N-j,N-y)

do not
contribute to the sum in (
5.1
), and one can replace
M
by either
N-j
or
N-y
.
Again, the parameter
c_{0}
is fixed through the constraint
equation
c_{0}c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}q^{2N+3}=1
and
\omega_{a}(c)
is given by (
B.3
).

The Griffiths polynomials of
q
-Racah type can be expressed in terms of the
Tratnik polynomials introduced previously:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=0}^{N-y}\omega_{a}\Big(\frac{c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}}{c_{4}}\Big)\,\mathcal{T}_{i,j}(a,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{a}(x;c_{4},c_{2},c_{1};N-y)

(5.2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=0}^{N-j}\omega_{a}\Big(\frac{c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}}{c_{4}}\Big)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(a;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)\,\mathcal{T}_{j,a}(y,x;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4},c_{1};N)\,.

(5.3)

The notation
\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)
is short for the functions

\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)
.

We demand that, for each univariate polynomials in the definition of the Griffiths
polynomials (
5.1
), the constraints (
2.13
), (
2.14
) and
(
2.16
) be obeyed,
i.e.
the parameters must satisfy

\displaystyle c_{i},q\neq 0\,,i=0,1,\dots,4\,,\qquad q,q^{2},\dots,q^{N}\neq 1\,,

(5.4a)

\displaystyle c_{1}c_{2},\,c_{2}c_{3},\,c_{0}c_{3},\,c_{0}c_{4},\,c_{2}c_{4},\,qc_{1}c_{2}c_{3},\,qc_{0}c_{3}c_{4},\,qc_{1}c_{2}c_{4}\notin\{q^{-2},q^{-3},\dots,q^{-2N}\}\,,

(5.4b)

\displaystyle c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}\notin\{q^{-1},q^{-2},\dots,q^{-N}\}\,.

(5.4c)

Replacing
c_{i}\to q^{c_{i}}
and taking the limit
q\to 1
in (
5.1
), we recover the

Griffiths polynomial of Racah type
studied in
[
13
]
:

\lim_{q\to 1}\,\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y;q^{c_{1}},q^{c_{2}},q^{c_{3}},q^{c_{4}};N)=G_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)\,.

(5.5)

The polynomials
G_{i,j}(x,y)
were introduced in
[
15
]
with a different
normalization. As mentioned in the introduction, it has been shown in
[
13
]

that the polynomials
G_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)
tend (in a certain limit) to the
original bivariate Griffiths polynomials introduced in
[
35
]
. This latter fact
justifies the appellation of “Griffiths polynomials” used in this paper for

\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)
.

In the following, we show that the Griffiths polynomials of
q
-Racah type have numerous
interesting properties.

Substituting the expression (
3.8
) for
\mathcal{T}_{j,a}(y,x;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4},c_{1};N)

into (
5.3
),
one gets (for
x,y

x+y\leq N

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\Omega(i;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)\,\frac{(1-c_{0}c_{4}q^{2j+1})}{(q;q)_{j}}\,\frac{(qc_{3};q)_{y}}{(qc_{0};q)_{y}}\left(\frac{c_{3}c_{4}}{c_{0}c_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}(N-y)}\,\left(q^{a-j}\frac{c_{1}}{c_{4}}\right)^{a}\,(q^{2j+1}c_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}N}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{a=0}^{N-j}

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{a-N};q)_{j}(qc_{2};q)_{a}(qc_{0};q)_{N-a}}{(q;q)_{a}(c_{0}c_{4}q^{j+1};q)_{N-a+1}(c_{1}c_{2}q^{a+1})_{a}(qc_{1};q)_{a}}(q^{y-N};q)_{a}(q^{N-y+2}c_{1}c_{2}c_{4};q)_{a}

\displaystyle\times{{}_{4}\phi_{3}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{q^{-i},\;c_{2}c_{3}q^{i+1}, \;q^{-a},\;c_{1}c_{2}q^{a+1}}{qc_2,\; q^{N-j+2}c_{1}c_{2}c_{3},\;q^{j-N}}\middle|q;q\right)}{{}_{4}\phi_{3}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{q^{-a},\;c_{1}c_{2}q^{a+1}, \;q^{-x},\;c_{2}c_{4}q^{x+1}}{qc_2,\; q^{N-y+2}c_{1}c_{2}c_{4},\;q^{y-N}}\middle|q;q\right)}

\displaystyle\times{{}_{4}\phi_{3}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{q^{j+a-N},\;q^{N-j+a+2}c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}, \;q^{y+a-N},\;q^{a+N-y+2}c_{1}c_{2}c_{4}}{c_{1}c_{2}q^{2a+2},\;q^{a-N}c_0^{-1},\;q^{a-N}}\middle|q;q\right)}\,.

(5.6)

Writing the basic hypergeometric functions appearing in (
5.6
) as finite sums, one can show that
\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)
divided by the first line of the r.h.s. of (
5.6
) — a function of the form
f(i,j)g(x,y)
— is a bivariate polynomial of total degree
N-j
w.r.t.
\lambda(x;c_{2}c_{4})
and
\lambda(y;c_{3}c_{0})
.

5.1
Symmetry relations

The Griffiths polynomials (
5.1
) satisfy a symmetry relation (
5.15
)
that links
\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)
to
\mathcal{G}_{j,i}(y,x)
. This relation will play an important role
in the proof of the bispectrality of the Griffiths polynomials: put simply, the
bispectrality properties on
i
and
x
will be easily transposed to the bispectrality
properties on
j
and
y
.

In order to prove this relation, we first need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

The Griffiths polynomials (
5.1
) satisfy the following two relations:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)&=\Big(\frac{c_{0}}{c_{1}}\Big)^{\!\frac{i}{2}}\Big(\frac{c_{3}}{c_{1}}\Big)^{\!\frac{j}{2}}\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{0}}\Big)^{\!\frac{x}{2}}\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{4}}\Big)^{\!\frac{y}{2}}\frac{\xi_{i}(c_{2},c_{3})\xi_{j}(c_{0},c_{4})}{\xi_{x}(c_{2},c_{4})\xi_{y}(c_{0},c_{3})}\frac{\omega_{j}(c_{0}c_{2}c_{4}/c_{1})}{\omega_{i}(c_{3}c_{0}c_{2}/c_{1})}\,\\
&\times\,\omega_{N}(qc_{1}c_{2}c_{3})\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(y,x;c_{1}^{-1},c_{3}^{-1},c_{2}^{-1},c_{0}^{-1};N)\Big|_{q\to q^{-1}}\,,\end{aligned}

(5.7)

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)&=\Big(\frac{c_{0}}{c_{1}}\Big)^{\!\frac{i}{2}}\Big(\frac{c_{3}}{c_{1}}\Big)^{\!\frac{j}{2}}\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{0}}\Big)^{\!\frac{x}{2}}\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{4}}\Big)^{\!\frac{y}{2}}\frac{\xi_{i}(c_{2},c_{3})\xi_{j}(c_{0},c_{4})}{\xi_{x}(c_{2},c_{4})\xi_{y}(c_{0},c_{3})}\frac{\omega_{y}(c_{1}c_{3}c_{0}/c_{2})}{\omega_{x}(c_{2}c_{4}c_{1}/c_{0})}\\
&\times\,\omega_{N}(qc_{1}c_{2}c_{4})\,\mathcal{G}_{j,i}(x,y;c_{1}^{-1},c_{4}^{-1},c_{0}^{-1},c_{2}^{-1};N)\Big|_{q\to q^{-1}}\,,\end{aligned}

(5.8)

where
\xi
and
\omega
are given in (
B.3
).

Proof.

Rewrite the Griffiths polynomials (
5.1
) as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)=\sum_{b=0}^{N-j}\sum_{a=0}^{N-j}&\,\frac{\omega_{a}({c_{1}c_{2}})}{\xi_{a}(c_{1},c_{2})}\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(a;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)\,\delta_{a,b}\,\\
&\times\Big(\frac{c_{3}}{c_{4}}\Big)^{\!\frac{b}{2}}\,\xi_{b}(c_{1},c_{2})\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}(y;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4};N-b)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{b}(x;c_{4},c_{2},c_{1};N-y)\,.\end{aligned}

(5.9)

It is straightforward to check that (
5.9
) reduces to (
5.1
) upon
getting rid of the sum on
a
with the Kronecker delta
\delta_{a,b}
and using the
identities

\displaystyle\xi_{b}(c_{1},c_{2})\,\xi_{b}(c_{2},c_{1})=1\,,\qquad\omega_{a}(cd)=\omega_{a}(c)\,d^{\frac{n}{2}}\,.

(5.10)

Next, use the involution relation (
2.10
) to rewrite the Kronecker delta

\delta_{a,b}
in (
5.9
):

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)&=\sum_{b=0}^{N-j}\sum_{a=0}^{N-j}\sum_{d=0}^{N-j}\frac{\omega_{a}({c_{1}c_{2}})}{\xi_{a}(c_{1},c_{2})}\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(a;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{a}(d;c_{3},c_{1},c_{2};N-j)\,\\
&\times\Big(\frac{c_{3}}{c_{4}}\Big)^{\!\frac{b}{2}}\,\xi_{b}(c_{1},c_{2})\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{d}(b;c_{2},c_{1},c_{3};N-j)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}(y;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4};N-b)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{b}(x;c_{4},c_{2},c_{1};N-y)\,.\end{aligned}

(5.11)

Since the bounds on
a
,
b
,
d
are independent of each other, the sums can be
freely interchanged. Apply the Racah relation (
4.1
) on the first
two polynomials (sum over
a
). This leads to

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)&=\sum_{b=0}^{N-j}\sum_{d=0}^{N-j}\omega_{N-j}(qc_{1}c_{2}c_{3})\,\frac{\xi_{i}(c_{2},c_{3})\xi_{d}(c_{3},c_{1})}{\omega_{i}(c_{2}c_{3})\omega_{d}(c_{3}c_{1})}\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(d;c_{1},c_{3},c_{2};N-j)\,\\
&\times\Big(\frac{c_{3}}{c_{4}}\Big)^{\!\frac{b}{2}}\,\xi_{b}(c_{1},c_{2})\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{d}(b;c_{2},c_{1},c_{3};N-j)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}(y;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4};N-b)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{b}(x;c_{4},c_{2},c_{1};N-y)\,.\end{aligned}

(5.12)

Now use the Biedenharn–Elliot relation (
4.6
) on the last three
polynomials (sum over
b
).
Hence one gets

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)&=\Big(\frac{c_{3}}{c_{2}}\Big)^{\!\frac{j}{2}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{0}}{c_{1}}\Big)^{\!\frac{x}{2}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{4}}\Big)^{\!\frac{y}{2}}\,\frac{\omega_{N-j}(qc_{1}c_{2}c_{3})}{\omega_{i}(c_{2}c_{3})}\,\frac{\xi_{i}(c_{2},c_{3})\xi_{j}(c_{0},c_{4})}{\xi_{x}(c_{2},c_{4})\xi_{y}(c_{0},c_{3})}\,\\
&\times\sum_{d=0}^{N-j}\frac{1}{\omega_{d}(\frac{c_{3}c_{1}c_{0}}{c_{2}})}\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(d;c_{1},c_{3},c_{2};N-j)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}(x;c_{2},c_{4},c_{0};N-d)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{d}(y;c_{0},c_{3},c_{1};N-x)\,.\end{aligned}

(5.13)

Using the symmetry relation (
2.5
) for the Racah polynomials and the equality

\frac{1}{\omega_{d}(c)}=\omega_{d}(c^{-1})\big|_{q\to q^{-1}}
, the sum over
d
in
(
5.13
) can now be written as Griffiths polynomials in the indeterminate

q^{-1}
:

\begin{split}&\sum_{d=0}^{N-j}\frac{1}{\omega_{d}(\frac{c_{3}c_{1}c_{0}}{c_{2}})}\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(d;c_{1},c_{3},c_{2};N-j)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}(x;c_{2},c_{4},c_{0};N-d)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{d}(y;c_{0},c_{3},c_{1};N-x)\,\\
&=\Big(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\Big)^{\!j}\,\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{0}}\Big)^{\!x}\,\sum_{d=0}^{N-j}\Big[\omega_{d}\!\left(\!\frac{c_{1}^{-1}c_{3}^{-1}c_{2}^{-1}}{c_{0}^{-1}}\!\right)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(d;c_{1}^{-1},c_{3}^{-1},c_{2}^{-1};N-j)\,\\
&\hskip 90.00014pt\,\times{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}(x;c_{2}^{-1},c_{4}^{-1},c_{0}^{-1};N-d)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{d}(y;c_{0}^{-1},c_{3}^{-1},c_{1}^{-1};N-x)\,\Big]\Big|_{q\to q^{-1}}\\
&=\Big(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\Big)^{\!j}\,\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{0}}\Big)^{\!x}\,\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(y,x;c_{1}^{-1},c_{3}^{-1},c_{2}^{-1},c_{0}^{-1};N)\Big|_{q\to q^{-1}}\,.\end{split}

(5.14)

Substituting this expression in (
5.13
) and expressing
\omega_{N-j}
in term of

\omega_{N}
and
\omega_{j}
, one gets the result (
5.7
).
The proof of relation (
5.8
) proceeds along the same lines.
∎

The previous lemma allows us to get a nice symmetry relation for the Griffiths polynomials
when the two variables and the two indices are exchanged simultaneously.

Theorem 5.2
.

The Griffiths polynomials (
5.1
) satisfy the following symmetry relation:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)\ \omega_{i}\Big(\dfrac{c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}}{c_{1}}\Big)\,\omega_{x}\Big(\dfrac{c_{4}c_{2}c_{1}}{c_{3}}\Big)\,c_{0}^{N/2}\;\\
=\ &\mathcal{G}_{j,i}(y,x;c_{1},c_{0},c_{4},c_{3};N)\ \omega_{j}\Big(\dfrac{c_{3}c_{0}c_{4}}{c_{1}}\Big)\,\omega_{y}\Big(\dfrac{c_{1}c_{0}c_{3}}{c_{4}}\Big)\,c_{2}^{N/2}\;.\end{aligned}

(5.15)

Proof.

Compare the two expressions of
\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)
given in Lemma

5.1
. This yields a relation between

\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(y,x;c_{1}^{-1},c_{3}^{-1},c_{2}^{-1},c_{0}^{-1};N)|_{q\to q^{-1}}
and

\mathcal{G}_{j,i}(x,y;c_{1}^{-1},c_{4}^{-1},c_{0}^{-1},c_{2}^{-1};N)|_{q\to q^{-1}}
. Replacing

q^{-1}\mapsto q
and
c_{i}^{-1}\mapsto c_{i}
in this expression and relabeling the

c_{i}
’s, one obtains the desired expression (
5.15
).
∎

.

In the limit

(5.16)

\displaystyle G_{i,j}(x,y)

\displaystyle G_{i,j}(x,y)

\displaystyle=G_{j,i}(y,x;c_{1},c_{0},c_{4},c_{3};N)\ (-1)^{x+y+i+j}\,,

(5.18)

∎

5.2
Duality and biorthogonality

The Griffiths polynomials (
5.1
) obey a duality relation that connects two
polynomials with a different set of parameters, as was the case for Tratnik polynomials.

Proposition 5.4
.

The Griffiths polynomials
\mathcal{G}_{i,j}
obey the duality relation

\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)=\frac{\Omega(i;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)\,\Lambda(j;c_{4},c_{0},c_{3},c_{1};N)}{\Omega(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{4};N-y)\,\Lambda(y;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4},c_{1};N)}\,\mathcal{G}_{x,y}(i,j;c_{1},c_{2},c_{4},c_{3};N)

(5.19)

where
\Omega
and
\Lambda
are given respectively by (
2.4
) and
(
3.13
).

Proof.

This is a consequence of the duality relation (
2.8
) for the Racah polynomials
as well as the identities (
3.14
) and (
3.15
).
∎

The Griffiths polynomials (
5.1
) are not orthogonal; rather they are

biorthogonal
. Both functions that are orthogonal with one another are Griffiths
polynomials, but of different parameters.

Proposition 5.5
.

The Griffiths polynomials are biorthogonal:

\displaystyle\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{x,y\geq 0}{x+y\leq N}}\frac{\Omega(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{4};N-y)\,\Lambda(y;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4},c_{1};N)}{\xi_{x}(c_{2},c_{4})\xi_{y}(c_{0},c_{3})(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{0}})^{x/2}\,(\frac{c_{4}}{c_{1}})^{y/2}}\,\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)\,\mathcal{G}_{k,\ell}(y,x;c_{1},c_{3},c_{2},c_{0};N)

\displaystyle\qquad=\delta_{i,k}\,\delta_{j,\ell}\,\frac{\omega_{j}(\frac{c_{2}c_{0}c_{4}}{c_{3}})\,\omega_{N}(qc_{1}c_{2}c_{3})}{\omega_{i}({c_{2}c_{3}})}\,\frac{\Omega(i;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)\,\Lambda(j;c_{4},c_{0},c_{3},c_{1};N)}{\xi_{i}(c_{2},c_{3})\,{\xi_{j}(c_{0},c_{4})}}\,.

(5.20)

The functions
\xi
and
\omega
are given in (
B.3
).

Proof.

Consider the sum

\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{x,y\geq 0}{x+y\leq N}}\frac{\Omega(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{4};N-y)\,\Lambda(y;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4},c_{1};N)}{\Omega(i;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)\,\Lambda(j;c_{4},c_{0},c_{3},c_{1};N)}\,\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)\,\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{k,\ell}(x,y)\,,

(5.21)

where

\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{k,\ell}(x,y)=\sum_{b=0}^{N-\ell}\frac{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}(b;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-\ell)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{\ell}(y;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4};N-b)\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{b}(x;c_{4},c_{2},c_{1};N-y)}{\omega_{b}(\frac{c_{1}c_{2}c_{4}}{c_{3}})}\,.

(5.22)

Expressing
\mathcal{G}_{i,j}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{k,\ell}
in term of univariate
q
-Racah
polynomials
{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
, (
5.21
) is rewritten as a quadruple sum
(on
x
,
y
,
a
and
b
)
of univariate polynomials with the following coefficient entering the sum:

\frac{\Omega(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{4};N-y)\,\Lambda(y;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4},c_{1};N)}{\Omega(i;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-y)\,\Lambda(j;c_{4},c_{0},c_{3},c_{1};N)}\frac{\omega_{a}(\frac{c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}}{c_{4}})}{\omega_{b}(\frac{c_{1}c_{2}c_{4}}{c_{3}})}\,.

(5.23)

Using relation (
3.14
) to rewrite each of the
\Lambda
functions,
the coefficient (
5.23
) is reexpressed as:

\frac{\Omega(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{4};N-y)\,\Omega(a;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N-j)\,\Omega(y;c_{4},c_{0},c_{3};N-a)}{\Omega(a;c_{4},c_{2},c_{1};N-y)\,\Omega(i;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)\,\Omega(j;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4};N-a)}\frac{\Lambda(a;c_{1},c_{2},c_{4},c_{3};N)\omega_{a}(\frac{c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}}{c_{4}})}{\Lambda(a;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)\omega_{b}(\frac{c_{1}c_{2}c_{4}}{c_{3}})}\,.

(5.24)

Now, starting from (
5.21
), perform the sum on
x
and use the orthogonality
relation (
2.9
) for
q
-Racah polynomials to get a
\delta_{a,b}
term that
lets one evaluate the sum on
b
.
By virtue of relation (
3.14
), the term

\frac{\Lambda(a;c_{1},c_{2},c_{4},c_{3};N)\,\omega_{a}({c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}}/{c_{4}})}{\Lambda(a;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)\,\omega_{b}({c_{1}c_{2}c_{4}}/{c_{3}})}
reduces to

.
Then, perform the sum on
y
to obtain a
\delta_{j,\ell}
term through the
orthogonality relation. Lastly, evaluate the sum on
a
, again using the orthogonality
relation, to obtain another
\delta_{i,k}
term. Hence, the expression (
5.21
) is

\delta_{j,\ell}\,\delta_{i,k}
.
Next, notice that equation (
5.14
) shows that

\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{k,\ell}(x,y)=\Big(\frac{c_{3}}{c_{1}}\Big)^{\!\ell}\,\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{4}}\Big)^{\!y}\,\mathcal{G}_{k,\ell}(x,y;c_{1}^{-1},c_{2}^{-1},c_{3}^{-1},c_{4}^{-1};N)\Big|_{q\to q^{-1}}\,.

(5.25)

Using the relation (

\xi_{n}(c,c^{\prime})=1/\xi_{n}(c^{\prime},c)\,,

the final result (

.

Note also that the Griffiths polynomial

Corollary 5.6
.

The Griffiths polynomials
\mathcal{G}_{i,j}
obey an involution relation

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{x,y\geq 0}{x+y\leq N}}\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{0}}\Big)^{\!\frac{x}{2}}\,\Big(\frac{c_{4}}{c_{1}}\Big)^{\!\frac{y}{2}}\,\xi_{x}(c_{2},c_{4})\,\xi_{y}(c_{0},c_{3})\,\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)\,\mathcal{G}_{y,x}(k,\ell;c_{1},c_{3},c_{0},c_{2};N)\,\\
&\qquad=\delta_{i,k}\,\delta_{j,\ell}\ \omega_{N}(qc_{1}c_{2}c_{3})\,\frac{\omega_{j}(\frac{c_{3}c_{0}c_{4}}{c_{2}})}{\omega_{i}({c_{2}c_{3}})}\,\xi_{i}(c_{2},c_{3})\,{\xi_{j}(c_{0},c_{4})}\,.\end{aligned}

(5.26)

Proof.

As a direct consequence of the duality and biorthogonality relations, one gets:

\displaystyle\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{x,y\geq 0}{x+y\leq N}}\frac{\Omega(x;c_{1},c_{2},c_{4};N-y)\,\Lambda(y;c_{3},c_{0},c_{4},c_{1};N)}{\Omega(y;c_{1},c_{3},c_{0};N-x)\,\Lambda(x;c_{2},c_{4},c_{0},c_{1};N)}\,\frac{\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)\,\mathcal{G}_{y,x}(k,\ell;c_{1},c_{3},c_{0},c_{2};N)}{\xi_{x}(c_{2},c_{4})(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{0}})^{x/2}\,\xi_{y}(c_{0},c_{3})(\frac{c_{4}}{c_{1}})^{y/2}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\omega_{j}(\frac{c_{2}c_{0}c_{4}}{c_{3}})\,\omega_{N}(qc_{1}c_{2}c_{3})}{\omega_{i}({c_{2}c_{3}})}\,\frac{\Omega(i;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3};N-j)\,\Lambda(j;c_{4},c_{0},c_{3},c_{1};N)}{\Omega(i;c_{1},c_{3},c_{2};N-j)\,\Lambda(j;c_{0},c_{4},c_{2},c_{1};N)}\,\frac{\delta_{i,k}\,\delta_{j,\ell}}{\xi_{i}(c_{2},c_{3})\,{\xi_{j}(c_{0},c_{4})}}\,.

(5.27)

Then using the explicit expressions of the functions
\Omega
and
\Lambda
,
the result (
5.26
) follows.
∎

5.3
Bispectral property

In this subsection, we obtain the bispectral property of the Griffiths polynomials of

q
-Racah type.

Proposition 5.7
.

The Griffiths polynomials of
q
-Racah type satisfy the following two recurrence
relations:

\displaystyle\lambda(y;c_{3}c_{0})\,\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}=0,\pm 1}\,A^{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{i+\varepsilon,j+\varepsilon^{\prime}}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)\,\mathcal{G}_{i+\varepsilon,j+\varepsilon^{\prime}}(x,y)\,,

(5.28)

\displaystyle\lambda(x;c_{4}c_{2})\,\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}=0,\pm 1}\frac{\omega_{\varepsilon}(q^{2i}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}/c_{1})}{\omega_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(q^{2j}c_{0}c_{3}c_{4}/c_{1})}\,A^{\varepsilon^{\prime},\varepsilon}_{j+\varepsilon^{\prime},i+\varepsilon}(c_{1},c_{0},c_{4},c_{3};N)\,\mathcal{G}_{i+\varepsilon,j+\varepsilon^{\prime}}(x,y)\,,

(5.29)

where
A^{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{i,j}
are given in (
3.23
) and
\omega_{\varepsilon}
in
(
B.3
).

Proof.

The proof of (
5.28
) consists in writing the Griffiths polynomials using the
form (
5.2
) and in using the recurrence relation (
3.22
) of the
Tratnik polynomials.

To simplify the calculation, we define

\rho_{i,j}=\frac{\omega_{j}\Big(\dfrac{c_{3}c_{0}c_{4}}{c_{1}}\Big)\,\omega_{y}\Big(\dfrac{c_{1}c_{0}c_{3}}{c_{4}}\Big)}{\omega_{i}\Big(\dfrac{c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}}{c_{1}}\Big)\,\omega_{x}\Big(\dfrac{c_{4}c_{2}c_{1}}{c_{3}}\Big)}\left(\dfrac{c_{2}}{c_{0}}\right)^{N/2}\,.

(5.30)

To prove (
5.29
), rewrite its l.h.s. using the symmetry relation
(
5.15
) and apply the first recurrence relation (
5.28
)
(up to a suitable change of variables and indices):

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\lambda(x;c_{2}c_{4})\,\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)=\rho_{i,j}\,\lambda(x;c_{4}c_{2})\,\mathcal{G}_{j,i}(y,x;c_{1},c_{0},c_{4},c_{3};N)\\
&\quad=\rho_{i,j}\sum_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}=0,\pm 1}\,A^{\varepsilon^{\prime},\varepsilon}_{j+\varepsilon^{\prime},i+\varepsilon}(c_{1},c_{0},c_{4},c_{3};N)\,\mathcal{G}_{j+\varepsilon^{\prime},i+\varepsilon}(y,x;c_{1},c_{0},c_{4},c_{3};N)\\
&\quad=\sum_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}=0,\pm 1}\frac{\rho_{i,j}}{\rho_{i+\varepsilon,j+\varepsilon^{\prime}}}\,A^{\varepsilon^{\prime},\varepsilon}_{j+\varepsilon^{\prime},i+\varepsilon}(c_{1},c_{0},c_{4},c_{3};N)\,\mathcal{G}_{i+\varepsilon,j+\varepsilon^{\prime}}(x,y;c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)\,.\end{aligned}

(5.31)

Examine the last line of (
5.31
); the r.h.s. of relation
(
5.29
) is recognized by remarking that the ratio of the functions
\rho
is
given by

\frac{\rho_{i,j}}{\rho_{i+\varepsilon,j+\varepsilon^{\prime}}}=\frac{\omega_{i+\varepsilon}\Big(\dfrac{c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}}{c_{1}}\Big)\,\omega_{j}\Big(\dfrac{c_{3}c_{0}c_{4}}{c_{1}}\Big)}{\omega_{i}\Big(\dfrac{c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}}{c_{1}}\Big)\,\omega_{j+\varepsilon^{\prime}}\Big(\dfrac{c_{3}c_{0}c_{4}}{c_{1}}\Big)}=\frac{\omega_{\varepsilon}(q^{2i}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}/c_{1})}{\omega_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(q^{2j}c_{0}c_{3}c_{4}/c_{1})}\,,

(5.32)

which concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.8
.

The Griffiths polynomials of
q
-Racah type satisfy the following two difference
relations:

\displaystyle\lambda(j;c_{0}c_{4})\,\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}=0,\pm 1}\,A^{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{x,y}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{4},c_{3};N)\,\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x-\varepsilon,y-\varepsilon^{\prime})\,,

(5.33)

\displaystyle\lambda(i;c_{2}c_{3})\,\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}=0,\pm 1}\frac{\omega_{-\varepsilon^{\prime}}(q^{2x}c_{1}c_{2}c_{4}/c_{3})}{\omega_{-\varepsilon}(q^{2y}c_{1}c_{0}c_{3}/c_{4})}\,A^{\varepsilon^{\prime},\varepsilon}_{y,x}(c_{1},c_{0},c_{3},c_{4};N)\,\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x-\varepsilon^{\prime},y-\varepsilon)\,,

(5.34)

where
A^{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{x,y}
are given in (
3.23
) and
\omega_{\varepsilon}
in
(
B.3
).

Proof.

The proof can be done mimicking the one of Proposition
5.7
.
Alternatively, one can use the duality property (
5.19
) of the Griffiths
polynomials to obtain it from the recurrence relations.
∎

Remark 5.9

(
Generalized Favard theorem
)
.

We show that the conditions of the generalized Favard theorem (see
Remark
3.8
) are not satisfied for the Griffiths polynomials, which is
consistent with the fact that they are biorthogonal.
For nonnegative integers
i,j
such that
i+j\leq N
, one defines

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{i,j}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)\,/\,\mathcal{G}_{N,0}(x,y)

(5.35)

where
\mathcal{G}_{i,j}(x,y)
is given by (
5.6
).
It is of total degree
N-i
w.r.t.
\lambda(x;c_{4}c_{2})
and
\lambda(y;c_{0}c_{3})
.
Consider

{\mathbb{G}}_{n}=\Big(\chi_{n,0}\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{N-n,0}\,,\ \chi_{n,1}\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{N-n,1}\,,\ \dots\,,\ \chi_{n,n}\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{N-n,n}\Big)^{T}

(5.36)

and look for coefficients
\chi
such that the recurrence relations
(
5.28
) and (
5.29
) can be put in the form of
(
5.36
). This leads to the constraints

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left(\frac{\chi_{i,j+1}}{\chi_{i,j}}\right)^{2}&=\frac{A^{0,+}_{N-i,j+1}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}{A^{0,-}_{N-i,j}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}\,,&\quad\left(\frac{\chi_{i+1,j}}{\chi_{i,j}}\right)^{2}&=\frac{A^{-,0}_{N-i-1,j}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}{A^{+,0}_{N-i,j}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}\,,\\
\left(\frac{\chi_{i+1,j+1}}{\chi_{i,j}}\right)^{2}&=\frac{A^{-,+}_{N-i-1,j+1}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}{A^{+,-}_{N-i,j}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}\,,&\quad\left(\frac{\chi_{i,j+1}}{\chi_{i+1,j}}\right)^{2}&=\frac{A^{+,+}_{N-i,j+1}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}{A^{-,-}_{N-i-1,j}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4};N)}\,,\end{aligned}

(5.37)

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left(\frac{\chi_{i,j+1}}{\chi_{i,j}}\right)^{2}&=\frac{\omega_{-}(q^{2j+2}c_{3}c_{0}c_{4}/c_{1})}{\omega_{+}(q^{2j}c_{3}c_{0}c_{4}/c_{1})}\,\,\frac{A^{+,0}_{j+1,N-i}(c_{1},c_{0},c_{4},c_{3};N)}{A^{-,0}_{j,N-i}(c_{1},c_{0},c_{4},c_{3};N)}\,,\\
\left(\frac{\chi_{i+1,j}}{\chi_{i,j}}\right)^{2}&=\frac{\omega_{-}(q^{2(N-i)}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}/c_{1})}{\omega_{+}(q^{2(N-i-1)}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}/c_{1})}\,\,\frac{A^{0,-}_{j,N-i-1}(c_{1},c_{0},c_{4},c_{3};N)}{A^{0,+}_{j,N-i}(c_{1},c_{0},c_{4},c_{3};N)}\,,\\
\left(\frac{\chi_{i+1,j+1}}{\chi_{i,j}}\right)^{2}&=\frac{\omega_{-}(q^{2(N-i)}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}/c_{1})}{\omega_{+}(q^{2j+2}c_{3}c_{0}c_{4}/c_{1})}\,\frac{\omega_{-}(q^{2j}c_{3}c_{0}c_{4}/c_{1})}{\omega_{+}(q^{2(N-i-1)}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}/c_{1})}\,\frac{A^{+,-}_{j+1,N-i-1}(c_{1},c_{0},c_{4},c_{3};N)}{A^{-,+}_{j,N-i}(c_{1},c_{0},c_{4},c_{3};N)}\,,\\
\left(\frac{\chi_{i,j+1}}{\chi_{i+1,j}}\right)^{2}&=\frac{\omega_{+}(q^{2(N-i-1)}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}/c_{1})}{\omega_{+}(q^{2j}c_{3}c_{0}c_{4}/c_{1})}\,\frac{\omega_{-}(q^{2j+2}c_{3}c_{0}c_{4}/c_{1})}{\omega_{-}(q^{2(N-i)}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}/c_{1})}\,\frac{A^{+,+}_{j+1,N-i}(c_{1},c_{0},c_{4},c_{3};N)}{A^{-,-}_{j,N-i-1}(c_{1},c_{0},c_{4},c_{3};N)}\,.\end{aligned}

(5.38)

From the calculations done for Tratnik polynomials, one knows that in
(
5.37
), the second line is a consequence of the first line. Similarly, the
last two lines of (
5.38
) are a consequence of the first two ones.
However, the equations (
5.37
) and (
5.38
) are inconsistent.
For instance, the ratio of both expressions of

\big(\tfrac{\chi_{i,j+1}}{\chi_{i,j}}\big)^{2}
given in (
5.37
) and
(
5.38
) is equal to

\frac{q^{2j-2N-1}}{c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}}\neq 1\,.

(5.39)

Thus, the Griffiths polynomials of
q
-Racah type cannot be orthogonal. Note that in the limit

c_{i}\mapsto q^{c_{i}}
,
q\to 1
, the expressions become consistent,
in accordance with the orthogonality property of the Griffiths polynomials of Racah type

[
13
]
.
∎

6
Outlook

This paper is devoted to the introduction and the study of new bivariate polynomials, the

Griffiths polynomials of
q
-Racah type
.
From these polynomials, one could perform various limits, so as to get
bivariate functions involving other monovariate polynomials such as the
q
-Hahn or the

q
-Krawtchouk polynomials.
These limits could have interesting uses;
for example, the bivariate Tratnik polynomials of
q
-Krawtchouk type appear naturally in
the study of
q
-rotations
[
30
]
and in the representation theory of
SU_{q}(3)

[
5
]
and hence the obtained polynomials may generalize these results.

The orthogonality property of these polynomials also deserves some studies. Unlike the
Griffiths polynomials of Racah type, which are orthogonal, the polynomials introduced in
this paper are biorthogonal. Clearly, some restriction on the parameter space can lead to
orthogonal polynomials. However, it could be that other constructions lead to truly
orthogonal polynomials; these polynomials would potentially possess a higher number of
parameters. In that case, it
is likely that the underlying algebraic structure would be different from the
Askey–Wilson algebra
\mathbf{aw}(4)
. The existence of this new algebra remains to be
elucidated.

A natural generalization of the results obtained here would be to consider multivariate
polynomials in more than two variables.
These
n
variable polynomials would be a generalization of the
n
variable Tratnik
polynomials introduced and studied in
[
28
,
40
]
.
Their bispectral property should provide a representation of the higher rank Askey–Wilson
algebra
\mathbf{aw}(n+2)
which has been defined in
[
14
]
. It would also be
interesting to study the representation theory of this algebra on its own to obtain
results on these polynomials as a byproduct.

The Leonard theorem has initially been proved in order to try to classify the so-called

P
- and
Q
-polynomial schemes or distance regular graph (see
[
48
,
2
]
).
These different notions have been generalized recently in
[
6
,
3
,
7
]
.
It would be interesting to generalize the Leonard theorem to the multivariate case and to
obtain a classification of bispectral multivariate polynomials.

Appendix A
Basic hypergeometric functions

The basic hypergeometric functions, or
q
-hypergeometric functions, are defined by the
following series, for
r
and
s
nonnegative integers:

{{}_{r}\phi_{s}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{a_1,\;a_2,\;\dots,\;a_r}{b_1,\;b_2,\dots,\;b_s}\middle|q;z\right)}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{r};q)_{k}}{(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{s};q)_{k}}(-1)^{(1+s-r)k}q^{(1+s-r)\binom{k}{2}}\frac{z^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}\,,

(A.1)

where

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{r};q)_{k}=(a_{1};q)_{k}(a_{2};q)_{k}\dots(a_{r};q)_{k}\,,

(A.2)

and the
q
-Pochhammer symbol
(a;q)_{n}
is defined as

\displaystyle(a;q)_{0}=1\,,\qquad(a;q)_{k}=\prod_{\ell=0}^{k-1}(1-aq^{\ell})\,,\quad k=1,2,3,\dots.

(A.3)

The
q
-Pochhammer symbol satisfies the following transformation formula:

(\tfrac{1}{a};q)_{n}=(-\nicefrac{{1}}{{a}})^{n}\,q^{\binom{n}{2}}\,({a}\,q^{-n+1};q)_{n}\,.

(A.4)

When
a_{1}=q^{-n}
for some nonnegative integer
n
, the series in (
A.1
)
truncates and becomes a sum over
k
from
0
to
n
.
Let us emphasize that the
q
-hypergeometric functions are invariant under any permutation
either of its parameters
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{r}
or of its parameters
b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{s}
.
It also satisfies

\displaystyle{{}_{4}\phi_{3}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{q^{n},\;a_2, \;a_3,\; a_4 }{b_1,\;b_2,\;b_3}\middle|q^{-1};q^{-1}\right)}={{}_{4}\phi_{3}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{q^{-n},\; a_2^{-1},\; a_3^{-1},\; a_4^{-1}}{b_1^{-1},\; b_2^{-1},\; b_3^{-1}}\middle|q;\frac{q^{n}a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}}{b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}}\right)}\,.

(A.5)

Limit
q\to 1
.

In the limit
q\to 1
, the
q
-generalized hypergeometric function

{{}_{4}\phi_{3}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{q^{-n},\; q^{a_2}, \;q^{a_3},\; q^{a_4}}{q^{b_1},\; q^{b_2},\; q^{b_3}}\middle|q;q\right)}

tends to the generalized hypergeometric function

{{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{{-n},\; {a_2},\; {a_3},\; {a_4}}{{b_1},\; {b_2},\; {b_3}}\middle|1\right)}
and
the renormalized
q
-Pochhammer symbol tends to the Pochhammer symbol:

\lim_{q\to 1}\frac{(q^{a};q)_{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}=\prod_{n=0}^{k-1}(a+n)=(a)_{k}\,.

(A.6)

Appendix B
Representations of the Askey–Wilson algebra
\mathbf{aw}(3)

In
[
65
]
, it has been shown that the operators defined by the bispectral property of
the
q
-Racah polynomials satisfy an algebra, called the
Askey–Wilson algebra
.
The Askey–Wilson algebra has subsequently found applications in various domains:
association schemes
[
48
,
2
,
59
]
, Leonard pairs
[
60
,
58
]
,
Kauffman bracket skein algebras
[
10
,
9
]
,
the centralizer of
U_{q}(sl_{2})
in tensor products of any three possibly different spin
representations
[
18
,
16
]

and symmetry of physical models
[
43
,
52
,
31
]
.
In addition, its representation theory has been studied in
[
39
]
.
For more details about the Askey–Wilson algebra
\mathbf{aw}(3)
, we refer to the review

[
11
]
.

B.1
Defining relations for
\mathbf{aw}(3)

We first provide the defining relations satisfied by the
\mathbf{aw}(3)
algebra.
We then exhibit a particular symmetry under which these relations are unchanged
and which we will use to obtain multiple representations in the next subsection.

Definition B.1
.

The Askey–Wilson algebra
\mathbf{aw}(3)
is an unital algebra generated by

C_{12}
,
C_{23}
and
C_{13}
, with four central charges

C_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
C_{3}
and
C_{123}
and a parameter
q
.
The generators obey the following three relations:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\textsc{q}^{2}-\textsc{q}^{-2}}[C_{12},C_{23}]_{\textsc{q}}=-C_{13}+\frac{1}{\textsc{q}+\textsc{q}^{-1}}(C_{3}C_{1}+C_{2}C_{123})\,,

(B.1a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\textsc{q}^{2}-\textsc{q}^{-2}}[C_{23},C_{13}]_{\textsc{q}}=-C_{12}+\frac{1}{\textsc{q}+\textsc{q}^{-1}}(C_{1}C_{2}+C_{3}C_{123})\,,

(B.1b)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\textsc{q}^{2}-\textsc{q}^{-2}}[C_{13},C_{12}]_{\textsc{q}}=-C_{23}+\frac{1}{\textsc{q}+\textsc{q}^{-1}}(C_{2}C_{3}+C_{1}C_{123})\,,

(B.1c)

where
[X,Y]_{\textsc{q}}=\textsc{q}XY-\textsc{q}^{-1}YX
.

Remark B.2
.

The Askey–Wilson algebra
\mathbf{aw}(3)
has a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}
symmetry.
In particular, the relations of the Askey–Wilson algebra
\mathbf{aw}(3)

are unchanged under the following cyclical permutations of the generators:

\displaystyle\sigma~:\quad\begin{aligned} C_{1}&\mapsto C_{2}\,,\\
C_{2}&\mapsto C_{3}\,,\\
C_{3}&\mapsto C_{1}\,,\end{aligned}\qquad C_{123}\mapsto C_{123}\,,\qquad\begin{aligned} C_{12}&\mapsto C_{23}\,,\\
C_{23}&\mapsto C_{13}\,,\\
C_{13}&\mapsto C_{12}\,.\end{aligned}

(B.2)

It is immediate to see that
\sigma^{3}=1
.
∎

B.2
Representations of the algebra
\mathbf{aw}(3)

We now provide
(N+1)
-dimensional representations of the Askey–Wilson algebra.

Define the functions
\xi
and
\omega
as

\displaystyle\xi_{n}(c_{1},c_{2})=\frac{(qc_{1};q)_{n}}{(qc_{2};q)_{n}}\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{n/2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\omega_{n}(c)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}c^{n/2}\,q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}\,,

(B.3)

and let
\mathbb{I}
denote the identity matrix of dimension
N+1
.

The
\mathcal{A}
matrices.

Introduce the
(N+1)\times(N+1)
-dimensional matrices

\mathcal{A}_{12}
,
\mathcal{A}_{23}
and
\mathcal{A}_{13}
.
Their non-vanishing entries are given as follows, for
0\leq i\leq N
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\mathcal{A}_{12})_{i,i}&=\lambda(i;c_{1}c_{2})\,,\\
(\mathcal{A}_{23})_{i-\varepsilon,i}&=\Phi^{\varepsilon}(i;c_{3},c_{2},c_{1};N)\,,\\
(\mathcal{A}_{13})_{i-\varepsilon,i}&=\frac{\xi_{i-\varepsilon}(c_{2},c_{1})\omega_{i-\varepsilon}(c_{2}c_{1})}{\xi_{i}(c_{2},c_{1})\omega_{i}(c_{2}c_{1})}\Phi^{\varepsilon}(i;c_{3},c_{1},c_{2};N)\,,\end{aligned}\hskip 40.00006pt\varepsilon\in\{0,\pm\}\,,

(B.4)

In the above and hereinafter, by convention the entries
(-1,i)
and
(N+1,i)
of
any of the
(N+1)\times(N+1)
-dimensional matrices are null,

e.g.

(\mathcal{A}_{12})_{-1,0}=(\mathcal{A}_{13})_{N+1,N}=0
.
The matrix
\mathcal{A}_{12}
is diagonal and both the matrices
\mathcal{A}_{23}
and
\mathcal{A}_{13}
are
tridiagonal matrices.

One observes that the matrices
\mathcal{A}_{12}
and
\mathcal{A}_{23}
encode respectively the
recurrence (
2.11
) and difference (
2.15
) relations
of the
q
-Racah polynomials, in other words their bispectral property.
It is a standard result that the algebraic relations between the matrices that encode the
bispectral property of the
q
-Racah polynomials are those of the Askey–Wilson algebra

[
65
]
.
The explicit correspondence with the relations (
B.1
)
will be given in the later Proposition (
B.3
).

The
\mathcal{B}
matrices.

The
(N+1)\times(N+1)
-dimensional matrices
\mathcal{B}_{12}
,
\mathcal{B}_{23}
and
\mathcal{B}_{13}
,
have the following non-vanishing entries, for
0\leq i\leq N
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\mathcal{B}_{23})_{i,i}&=\lambda(i;c_{2}c_{3})\,,\\

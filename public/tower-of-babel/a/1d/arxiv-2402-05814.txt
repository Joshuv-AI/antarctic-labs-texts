Connectedness of level sets for non-degenerate integrable systems that extend complexity one torus actions Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification . Primary 37J35, 53D20. Secondary 37J39, 53D35. Thanks: Keywords: Completely integrable Hamiltonian systems, non-degenerate singular points, complexity one actions, connected level sets.
Daniele Sepe, Susan Tolman

Abstract
. In this paper we study the connectedness of the level sets of integrable systems that have only non-degenerate singular points and extend complexity one T T -spaces with proper moment maps. Our main result states that if there are no singular points with a hyperbolic block and connected T T -stabilizer, then each level set is connected. Moreover, we prove that the above condition is necessary if either some reduced space is simply connected or the moment map for the integrable system is generic in a natural sense.



1.
Introduction

An important step in studying Hamiltonian group actions is understanding the level sets of the moment map. In this direction, connectedness of the level sets has been established in the following cases:

(1)

Hamiltonian group actions of compact abelian Lie groups with proper moment maps
[
At
,
GS2
,
LMTW
]
; and

(2)

semi-toric systems in dimension four and their natural generalization in dimension six
[
VN
,
Wa
]
.

In both cases, the connectedness of the level sets of the moment map is key to understanding the moment map image and plays an important role in classification.

In this paper we study the connectedness of the level sets of integrable systems that are extensions of complexity one torus actions, and have only non-degenerate singular points. Complexity one torus actions are the simplest after the toric case and have been partially classified in
[
KT1
,
KT2
,
KT3
]
. Non-degenerate singular points of integrable systems are generic, and can be thought of as the symplectic analog of critical points for Morse-Bott functions; they have attracted much interest, both for classical and quantum integrable systems (see
[
BF
,
SV
]
and references therein). In particular, the integrable systems that we study include the semi-toric systems of
[
VN
]
, and their higher dimensional generalization in
[
Wa
]
.

In our main result, Theorem
1.6
we give sufficient conditions for level sets of the moment map to be connected,
generalizing the results in
[
VN
,
Wa
]
.
Morse theory plays a central role in the theorems
(1)
and
(2)
above, and our theorem both uses and generalizes the following simple Morse-theoretic result: Let
N
be a closed, connected 2-dimensional manifold and let
\overline{g}\colon N\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a Morse function. If
\overline{g}
has no singular point of index one, then each level set is connected (see Lemma
7.3
). We also explore the extent to which the hypotheses in our main result are necessary (see Theorem
1.10
).

Before stating our results precisely, we begin with few definitions from integrable systems.

Definition 1.1
.

An
integrable system
is a quadruple
\left(M,\omega,V,f\right)
, where
\left(M,\omega\right)
is a connected

2n
-dimensional symplectic manifold,
V
is an
n
-dimensional real
vector space, and
f\colon M\to V^{*}
is a smooth map
such that:

(1)

the Poisson bracket
\big\{\langle f,\xi_{1}\rangle,\langle f,\xi_{2}\rangle\big\}=0
for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in V
, and

(2)

the function
f
is regular on a dense subset of
M
.

Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon V^{*}\times V\to{\mathbb{R}}
is the natural pairing.

The standard definition of integrable systems takes
V={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. In this paper, we assume that every integrable system is
complete
, that is,
f
is
a moment map for a Hamiltonian
V
-action.

Fix an integrable system
\left(M,\omega,V,f\right)
. A point
p\in M
is
regular
if
D_{p}f
has full rank and
singular
otherwise. Given a point

p\in M
, the
V
-orbit
\mathcal{O}
through
p
is isotropic. Hence, the vector space
\left(T_{p}\mathcal{O}\right)^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathcal{O}
inherits a natural symplectic structure.
Moreover, the stabilizer
W\subseteq V
of
p
acts by linear symplectomorphisms on
T_{p}M
and fixes
T_{p}\mathcal{O}.

We call the induced symplectic action of
W
on
(T_{p}\mathcal{O})^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathcal{O}

the
linearized action
at
p
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
p
be a point in an integrable system
\left(M,\omega,V,f\right)
.
Let
W_{0}\subseteq V
be the Lie algebra of the stabilizer of
p
. The point
p
is
non-degenerate
if there exist identifications
W_{0}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{\dim W}
and
(T_{p}\mathcal{O})^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathcal{O}\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^{\dim W}
such that the homogeneous moment map for the linearized action at
p
is a product of the following “blocks”:

•

(
elliptic
)
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\mapsto\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}
;

•

(
hyperbolic
)
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\mapsto{\mathfrak{I}}(z^{2})
;

•

(
focus-focus
)
(z_{1},z_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\mapsto\left(\frac{1}{2}(|z_{1}|^{2}-|z_{2}|^{2}),{\mathfrak{I}}(z_{1}z_{2})\right)
.

Here
{\mathfrak{I}}(w)
denotes the imaginary part of
w\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
{\mathbb{C}}^{\dim W}
has the standard symplectic structure. If all the blocks are elliptic, we say that
p
has
purely elliptic type
.

Remark 1.3
.

The above definition of non-degeneracy is equivalent to the standard one (see, for instance,
[
SV
, Theorem 4.8]
). In particular,
the focus-focus block in the above definition can be transformed to the one in the
integrable systems literature by applying a linear symplectomorphism.

Remark 1.4
.

The notion of non-degeneracy can be viewed as a “symplectic Morse-Bott” condition. In particular, in dimension two, a point is non-degenerate in the sense of Definition
1.2
if and only if it is non-degenerate in the sense of Morse theory. Moreover, it is hyperbolic if and only if it has index one and elliptic otherwise.

Next we introduce complexity one torus actions. Let
T
be an
(n-1)
-dimensional torus with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{t}}
.

Definition 1.5
.

A
complexity one
\boldsymbol{T}
-space
is
a triple
(M,\omega,\Phi)
, where
(M,\omega)
is a connected
2n
-dimensional
symplectic manifold
and
\Phi\colon M\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
is a moment map for
an effective
T
-action.

Let
(M,\omega,\Phi)
be a complexity one
T
-space. Given
\alpha\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
, the
reduced space
at
\alpha
is the quotient
\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)/T
; if
\alpha=0
, we write
{M/\!/T}
for the reduced space.
A point
p\in M

is
short
if
[p]
is an isolated point in the reduced space
\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(p))/T
and is
tall
otherwise.
By
[
KT1
, Proposition 6.1]
,
if
\Phi
is proper each reduced space
\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)/T

containing more than one point is topologically a closed, connected oriented 2-dimensional manifold; moreover, any two non-empty reduced spaces have the same fundamental group
[
KT1
, Lemma 5.7 and Corollary 9.7]
.

The main objects that we study in this paper are non-degenerate integrable systems that extend complexity one torus actions, i.e., integrable systems
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=(\Phi,g)\right)
such that every singular point is non-degenerate and such that
(M,\omega,\Phi)
is a complexity one
T
-space.
Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=(\Phi,g)\right)
be an integrable system so that
(M,\omega,\Phi)
is a complexity one
T
-space with a proper moment map. Assume that each tall
singular point in
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f\right)
is non-degenerate. If
M
has no non-degenerate singular points with a hyperbolic block and connected
T
-stabilizer, then
the level set
f^{-1}(\alpha,c)
is connected for each
(\alpha,c)\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{R}}
;
moreover, the reduced space
\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)/T
is simply connected.

Remark 1.7
.

Theorem
1.6
implies connectedness of the level sets for the higher dimensional analogs of semi-toric systems that Wacheux studied in
[
Wa
]
: Integrable systems
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=\left(\Phi,g\right)\right)
so that
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is a complexity
one
T
-space with a proper moment map all of whose singular points are non-degenerate without hyperbolic blocks. In particular, our main result generalizes
[
Wa
, Theorem 4.2.4]
to arbitrary dimensions.

Remark 1.8
.

Theorem
1.6
is comparable to
[
PRV
, Theorems 1 – 3]
in that all these results assume that the singular points of an integrable system are non-degenerate, disallow (certain) singular points with a hyperbolic block, and prove that the level sets are connected. However, the results in
[
PRV
]
do not require the first component of the moment map to generate a Hamiltonian circle action, but only hold in dimension four and have additional hypotheses on the moment map.

Unfortunately, Theorem
1.6
is not quite sharp. If a reduced space has positive genus, then
M
must have non-degenerate singular points with a hyperbolic block and connected
T
-stabilizer; see Remark
1.14
.
However, as we show below, it is still possible for
all the level sets of
f
to be connected in this case.

Example 1.9
.

Let
\omega
be the standard symplectic form on the torus
N={\mathbb{R}}^{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.
The function
g\colon N\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined by
g([x,y])=\cos(2\pi x)+\cos(2\pi y)

is Morse and has connected fibers, a unique minimum and maximum, and two points of index one.
Hence,
(N,\omega,{\mathbb{R}},g)
is an integrable system with two hyperbolic singular points.
(Since the compact torus
T
is trivial, all points have connected
T
-stabilizer.)
To form higher dimensional examples, take the product of this integrable system with a compact, connected symplectic toric manifold.

Nevertheless, as our next result shows, Theorem
1.6
is quite close to being sharp.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=(\Phi,g)\right)
be an integrable system so that
(M,\omega,\Phi)
is a complexity one
T
-space with a proper moment map. Assume that each tall
singular point in
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f\right)
is non-degenerate. Moreover, assume that one of the following holds:

•

The reduced space
\Phi^{-1}(\beta)/T
is simply connected for some
\beta\in\Phi(M).

•

Each level set of
f
contains at most one orbit of non-degenerate singular points with a hyperbolic block.

Then the level set
f^{-1}(\alpha,c)
is connected for all
(\alpha,c)\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{R}}
if and only
M
has no non-degenerate singular points with a hyperbolic block and connected
T
-stabilizer.

Remark 1.11
.

If in Theorems
1.6
and
1.10
we additionally assume that
M
is compact, then by a theorem of Hui Li
[
Li
]
, the fundamental group of
M
is equal to that of any reduced space. Hence, in this case, the phrase “the reduced space
\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)/T
” can be changed to “
M
”.

The fundamental idea behind the proofs of
Theorems
1.6
and
1.10
is quite simple:
Since
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f=(\Phi,g)\right)
is an integrable system, the function
g
is
T
-invariant, and hence induces a function
\overline{g}\colon\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)/T\to{\mathbb{R}}
.
By the key technical result in our paper, Proposition
7.1
, each non-trivial component of the reduced space
\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)/T
can be given the structure of a smooth oriented surface so that

\overline{g}\colon\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)/T\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a Morse function. Moreover,
the critical points of index
1
correspond exactly to orbits of non-degenerate singular points with a hyperbolic black and connected
T
-stabilizer.
Since
\Phi
is proper, the reduced space
\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)/T
is compact and connected by Theorem
5.5
.
Therefore, the proofs reduce to standard results in Morse theory; see Lemmas
7.3
and
7.4
.

This method can be used more generally on integrable systems that extend a complexity one torus action but have (sufficiently nice)
degenerate
singular points. For example, in a forthcoming paper, we will prove that the analogs of Theorems
1.6
and
1.10
hold if we also allow a broader class of “ephemeral” singular points; like focus-focus points and hyperbolic points with disconnected
T
-stabilizers, these correspond to regular points on the symplectic quotients.

Remark 1.12
.

The second bullet point in Theorem
1.10
can be thought of as a genericity condition because generic Morse functions have at most one critical point (of index 1) in each level set.

Remark 1.13
.

More generally, Theorems
1.6
and
1.10
both hold if
\Phi
is proper as a map to an open convex subset of
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
, because the analog of Theorem
5.5
still holds in this case
[
LMTW
, Theorem 4.3]
. Additionally, if
f
is proper and the fibers of
\Phi
are connected, then the first statement in Theorem
1.6
still holds. This is because a proper Morse function on a connected surface with no critical points of index
1
has connected fibers (Proposition
5.6
).

Remark 1.14
.

Let
(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{R}},f)
be an integrable system satisfying the assumptions in the first two sentences of Theorem
1.6
, but assume that the reduced space
\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)/T
has positive genus
k
, and hence Euler characteristic
2-2k
. In this case, by calculating the Euler characteristic, we see that

\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)/T
has at least
2k
orbits of non-degenerate singular points with a hyperbolic block and connected
T
-stabilizer because because
\overline{g}
has at least one minimum and maximum. Note that if
\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)/T
has
more
than

2k
such orbits, then the same argument shows that
\overline{g}
must have additional local minima or maxima;
hence the fibers of
f
are not connected by Lemma
7.2
.

Structure of the paper

In Section
2
, we review some facts about tall complexity one local models and
then briefly explore a few additional properties.
Next, in Section
3
we prove that we can endow the symplectic quotient
\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)/T
with a smooth structure so that
\overline{g}
is a Morse function – as long as most singular points satisfy a certain technical condition that guarantees that we can do this locally. Most of the rest of the paper is dedicated to proving that non-degenerate singular points satisfy this condition. For example, in Section
4
we prove that tall points that have purely elliptic type satisfy it, and in Section
5
we show that, if
M
is four-dimensional, focus-focus points satisfy it. At this point, we are able to demonstrate our technique by using it to give a new proof that that level sets of a semi-toric system are connected. In
Section
6
, we prove that all the remaining types of non-degenerate singular points also satisfy this technical condition. Therefore, we can complete the proof of our theorems in Section
7
.

2.
Tall local models

To begin, we review some facts about tall complexity one local models.
In particular, we introduce (tall) complexity one local models, construct the “defining polynomial” for tall local models, and use it to describe the sympletic quotient
{Y/\!/T}
. Moreover, we provide a generating set for
T
-invariant polynomials on these models.

Fix a complexity one
T
-space
(M,\omega,\Phi)
and
let
\mathfrak{O}
be the
T
-orbit through a point
p\in M
.
Since
\mathfrak{O}
is isotropic, the vector space

(T_{p}\mathfrak{O})^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathfrak{O}
inherits a natural symplectic structure.
Moreover, the natural action
of the stabilizer
H\subseteq T
of
p
on
T_{p}M
induces a symplectic representation of
H
on

(T_{p}\mathfrak{O})^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathfrak{O}
, called the
symplectic slice representation
at
p
.
It is isomorphic to the representation of
H
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}

associated to some injective homomorphism
\rho\colon H\to(S^{1})^{h+1}
, where
h=\dim H
.
Let
\eta_{i}\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
be the differential of the
i
’th component of
\rho
.
The (not necessarily distinct) vectors
\eta_{0},\dots,\eta_{h}
are
called the
isotropy weights
at
p
; the multiset of isotropy weights at
p
is
uniquely determined.

Set
Y:=T\times_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
, where
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}
is the annihilator of
{\mathfrak{h}}
in
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
and
H
acts on
T
by multiplication, on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}
trivially, and on
{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
by
h\cdot z=\rho(h^{-1})z
.
Fix an inner product on the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{t}}
once and for all, and use it to identify
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
with
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}.

There exists a canonical symplectic form
\omega_{Y}
on
Y
so that the
T
-action
s\cdot[t,\alpha,z]=[st,\alpha,z]

has homogeneous moment
map
\Phi_{Y}\colon Y\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
given by

\textstyle\Phi_{Y}([t,\alpha,z])=\alpha+\Phi_{H}(z),

where
\Phi_{H}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\eta_{i}|z_{i}|^{2}
is the homogeneous moment
map for the
H
-action on
{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
; see Remark
4.2
.
By the Marle-Guillemin-Sternberg local normal form theorem, there is
a
T
-equivariant symplectomorphism from an invariant neighborhood of
\mathfrak{O}

to an open subset of
Y
taking
p
to
[1,0,0]
. We call
(Y,\omega_{Y},\Phi_{Y})
the

for

. We say
that

is

if
\Phi_{Y}^{-1}(0)
consists of a single

-orbit and

otherwise. By Lemma
2.7
below, the level set
\Phi^{-1}_{Y}(0)
contains exactly one
T
-orbit exactly if
[p]\in\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(p))
is an isolated point. Hence,

is short exactly if its local model is short.

As shown in
[

]
, tall local models have an important invariant, called the

.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
Y=T\times_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
be a local model associated to an injective homomorphism
\rho:H\to(S^{1})^{h+1}
. Then there exists
\xi=(\xi_{0},\ldots,\xi_{h})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{h+1}
such that the homomorphism
(S^{1})^{h+1}\to S^{1}

given by
\lambda\mapsto\prod\limits_{j=0}^{h}\lambda_{j}^{\xi_{j}}
induces a short exact sequence

(2.2)

1\to H\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\to}}(S^{1})^{h+1}\to S^{1}\to 1;

moreover, the vector
\xi
is unique up to sign. Finally,
\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq 0
for all
i,j
if and only if the local model
Y
is tall.

Proof.

Existence of
\xi
and its uniqueness up to sign follow from
[
KT1
, Lemma 5.8]
. The local model
Y
is tall if and only if
\Phi^{-1}_{Y}(0)
consists of more than one orbit. By
[
KT1
, Lemma 5.4]
, the latter occurs exactly if
\Phi_{Y}
is not proper, which, by
[

, Lemma 5.8]
, is equivalent to
\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq 0
for all

.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Let
\eta_{0},\ldots,\eta_{h}\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
be the isotropy weights at

.
By (
2.2
), the homomorphism

given by

\displaystyle 0\to{\mathfrak{h}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho_{*}}}{{\to}}{\mathbb{R}}^{h+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}\to 0;

\displaystyle 0\to{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\to\left({\mathbb{R}}^{h+1}\right)^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho^{*}}}{{\to}}{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\to 0.

In particular,

lies in

exactly if
\sum\limits_{j}b_{j}\xi_{j}=0
, while

vanishes exactly if

.
Hence, if

is tall then
(1,\dots,1)\notin\rho_{*}({\mathfrak{h}})
because

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
Y=T\times_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
be a tall local model
with moment map
\Phi_{Y}\colon Y\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
. The defining polynomial
P\colon Y\to{\mathbb{C}}
induces a homeomorphism
\overline{P}\colon{Y/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{C}}
that restricts to a diffeomorphism on the
complement of
\left\{[1,0,0]\right\}
.

Proof.

Following
[
KT1
]
, we say that an orbit is
exceptional
if every nearby orbit in the same moment level set has strictly smaller stabilizer. Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
2.1
, since
Y
is tall the moment map
\Phi_{Y}
is not proper. The first claim follows at once by
[

, Corollary 6.3]
. By
[

, Lemmas 5.15 and 5.17]
, the orbit of
[1,0,0]
is the only possible exceptional orbit in

. The second claim now follows at once by
[
KT1
, Corollary 7.2]
.
∎

In most cases, the homeomorphism

Let

\textstyle K:=\{\lambda\in(S^{1})^{k+1}\mid\prod_{j=0}^{k}\lambda_{j}^{\xi_{j}}=1\}

{\mathbb{C}}^{k+1}

The inclusion

given by

(z_{0},\ldots,z_{k})\mapsto[1,0,(z_{0},\ldots,z_{k},0,\ldots,0)]

Proof.

On the reduced space

Lemma 2.9
.

Let

Let

Proof.

Let

be the weights for the

The moment map

Thus it suffices to check that

\begin{split}\lvert z\rvert^{2}&=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{h}\lvert z_{j}\rvert^{2}=cN;\\

Remark 3.3

Clearly, every critical point of

g

\Phi

\Psi_{p}([p])

.

Since
\varphi\circ\mu=f=(\Phi,g)
, there exist an
injective Lie group homomorphism
\Lambda\colon T\to(S^{1})^{n}=(S^{1})^{n-h-1}\times(S^{1})^{h+1}
,
\beta\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
, and a smooth map
\varphi_{\mathbb{R}}\colon({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{*}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\varphi=(\Lambda^{*}+\beta,\varphi_{{\mathbb{R}}})
, where
\Lambda^{*}
is the dual of the Lie algebra homomorphism
\Lambda_{*}:{\mathfrak{t}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. By replacing
f
by its pre-composition with the translation by
-\beta
, we may assume that
\beta=0
. Hence, the composite
D_{0}\varphi\circ\varphi^{-1}\colon{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{R}}
is the identity on
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
. Therefore, by replacing
f
by the composition
D_{0}\varphi\circ\varphi^{-1}\circ f=D_{0}\varphi\circ\mu
, we may assume that
\varphi_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is linear.

Let
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
denote the
projections of
\Lambda
onto
(S^{1})^{n-h-1}
and
(S^{1})^{h+1}
, and let
\Lambda_{1}^{*}
and
\Lambda^{*}_{2}
be the dual of the Lie algebra homomorphisms induced by
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
respectively. Then
{\operatorname{ker}}\,\Lambda_{1}=H
and
\Lambda_{2}|_{H}=\rho
. Hence, if
b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{h+1}\smallsetminus\rho_{*}({\mathfrak{h}})
,
then
(0,b)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\smallsetminus\Lambda_{*}({\mathfrak{t}})
.
Thus there exists a linear isomorphism
L
of
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{R}}
that restricts to the identity on
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
so that, by further replacing
f
by the composition
L\circ f
, we may assume that
\varphi_{{\mathbb{R}}}=(0,2b)
. In particular,
g(s,a,w)=\sum_{i=0}^{h}b_{i}|w_{i}|^{2}
.

By construction, the map given by

[t,\alpha,z]\mapsto[t,\alpha+\Phi_{H}(z),z]

is a symplectomorphism between the local model
Y
and the reduced space
(T\times{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1})/\!/H
discussed in Remark
4.2
.

By a straightforward computation, the map
T\times{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}\to T\times{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
given by
\textstyle(t,\beta,z)\mapsto\left(t,\beta-\Lambda^{*}_{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}|z_{0}|^{2},\ldots,\frac{1}{2}|z_{h}|^{2}\right),\Lambda_{2}(t)z\right)

is a symplectomorphism. It induces a symplectomorphism between the reduced space
(T\times{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1})/\!/H
and
T/H\times{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
that is given by

\textstyle[t,\beta,z]\mapsto(tH,\beta-\Lambda^{*}_{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}|z_{0}|^{2},\ldots,\frac{1}{2}|z_{h}|^{2}\right),\Lambda_{2}(t)z).

Here the symplectic form on
T/H\times{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
is the sum of the pullbacks of the canonical symplectic form on
T^{*}(T/H)\cong T/H\times{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}
and of the standard symplectic form on
{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
.

By a dimension count,
\Lambda_{1}
induces an isomorphism between
T/H
and
(S^{1})^{n-h-1}
. Hence it induces a symplectomorphism between
T/H\times{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
and
(S^{1})^{n-h-1}\times\left({\mathbb{R}}^{n-h-1}\right)^{*}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
that is given by

(tH,\alpha,w)\mapsto(\Lambda_{1}(t),(\Lambda^{*}_{1})^{-1}(\alpha),w).

Composing these maps, we obtain a symplectomorphism
\Psi\colon Y\to(S^{1})^{n-h-1}\times\left({\mathbb{R}}^{n-h-1}\right)^{*}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
. Since
\Lambda_{2}|_{H}=\rho
, it is given by

\textstyle\Psi([t,\alpha,z])=\left(\Lambda_{1}(t),(\Lambda_{1}^{*})^{-1}\left(\alpha-\mathrm{pr}_{{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}}\left(\Lambda^{*}_{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}|z_{0}|^{2},\ldots,\frac{1}{2}|z_{h}|^{2}\right)\right)\right),\Lambda_{2}(t)z\right).

Here
\mathrm{pr}_{{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}}\colon{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\to{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}

denotes the projection induced by the fixed choice of metric on
{\mathfrak{t}}
. Clearly this map is
T
-equivariant and so
\Phi\circ\Psi
is a moment map for the
T
-action on
Y
. Since
\Phi\circ\Psi(1,0,0)=0
, it follows that
\Phi\circ\Psi
is the homogeneous moment map
\Phi_{Y}
. Moreover, it is easy check that
g\circ\Psi=g_{b}
. By construction, the integrable system
(Y,\omega_{Y},{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f\circ\Psi=(\Phi_{Y},g_{b}))
is isomorphic to the original one, as desired.
∎

We are finally ready to prove the main result of this section.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=(\Phi,g))

be an integrable system
so that
(M,\omega,\Phi)
is a complexity one
T
-space.
Let
p\in\Phi^{-1}(0)\cap g^{-1}(0)
be a tall critical point
of
g
modulo
\Phi
that has purely elliptic type. After possibly replacing
g
by
-g
, there exist an open neighborhood
U
of
[p]
in
{M/\!/T}

and a map
\Psi\colon U\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
taking
[p]
to
(0,0)
satisfying:

(1)

\Psi\colon U\to\Psi(U)
is a homeomorphism onto an open set;

(2)

\Psi
restricts to a diffeomorphism on
U\smallsetminus\{[p]\}
; and

(3)

(\overline{g}\circ\Psi^{-1})(x,y)=x^{2}+y^{2}
for all
(x,y)\in\Psi(U)
.

Proof.

Since
p
has tall local model
Y=T\times_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
, Remark
2.3
implies that
(1,\ldots,1)\notin\rho_{*}({\mathfrak{h}})
, where
\rho\colon H\to(S^{1})^{h+1}
is the symplectic slice representation at
p
. Hence, by Lemma
4.4
and the Morse Lemma, after possibly replacing
g
by
-g
, we may assume that
M
is a local model
Y=T\times_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
, that
\Phi=\Phi_{Y}
, that
g([t,\alpha,z])=|z|^{2}
, and that
p=[1,0,0]
. Let
N
be the degree of the defining polynomial
P\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}\to{\mathbb{C}}
.
By Lemma
2.7
, the map
\overline{P}\colon{Y/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a
homeomorphism; moreover, it
restricts to a diffeomorphism from
({Y/\!/T})\smallsetminus\{[t,0,0]\}
to
{\mathbb{C}}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.
By Lemma
2.9
, there exists a positive real number
C
such that
g([t,\alpha,z])=C|P(z)|^{\frac{2}{N}}
for all
[t,\alpha,z]\in\Phi_{Y}^{-1}(0)
.
Let
S\colon{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be the homeomorphism that, in polar coordinates,
takes
(r,\theta)
to
(\sqrt{C}\,r^{\frac{1}{N}},\theta)
; it restricts
to a diffeomorphism
{\mathbb{C}}\smallsetminus\{0\}\to{\mathbb{C}}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.
Define
\Psi\colon{Y/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{C}}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by
\Psi([t,\alpha,z])=S(P(z))
. Since

|\Psi([t,\alpha,z])|^{2}=C|P(z)|^{\frac{2}{N}}=\overline{g}([t,\alpha,z]),

for all
[t,\alpha,z]\in{Y/\!/T}
, the map
\Psi
satisfies properties (1) – (3).
∎

Remark 4.6
.

We observe that, by Lemma
2.8
, if

the map

5.
The semitoric case

The goal of this section is to prove that focus-focus points satisfy the hypotheses of Proposition
3.4
(see Lemma
5.4
below). This allows us to provide a short and direct new proof of Vu Ngoc’s theorem that the level sets of semitoric systems are connected
[
VN
]
– see Theorem
5.7
. In both proofs, the key arguments follow from Morse theory; the most significant difference is that we construct a new smooth structure on the reduced spaces, while he uses the natural smooth structure on
M
.

Definition 5.1
.

An integrable system
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f=\left(\Phi,g\right)\right)
is
semitoric
if

•

\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is a 4-dimensional complexity one
T
-space, and

•

every singular point of
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f\right)
is non-degenerate with no hyperbolic blocks.

Note that, by definition, every singular point of a semitoric system either has purely elliptic type or is focus-focus, i.e., it has one focus-focus block.
We begin with an important example of a semitoric system containing one focus-focus point.

Example 5.2
.

Let
Y={\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, let
\omega_{Y}
be the standard symplectic form, and set
\Phi_{Y}(z_{1},z_{2}):=\frac{1}{2}\left(|z_{1}|^{2}-|z_{2}|^{2}\right)
and
g_{Y}(z_{1},z_{2}):={\mathfrak{I}}(z_{1}z_{2})={\mathfrak{I}}(P(z_{1},z_{2}))
, where
P\colon Y\to{\mathbb{C}}
is the defining polynomial for the
S^{1}
-action induced by
\Phi_{Y}
(cf. Example
2.6
).

Locally, near a focus-focus point, every semitoric system is modeled on Example
5.2
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f=(\Phi,g))
be an integrable system
such that
(M,\omega,\Phi)
is a 4-dimensional complexity one
T
-space.
Assume that
p\in M
is focus-focus.
After identifying
T
with
S^{1}
, a neighborhood of
p
is isomorphic to a neighborhood of the origin in the semitoric system
(Y,\omega_{Y},{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f_{Y})
described above.

Proof.

Identify
T
with
S^{1}
. By the local normal form for focus-focus points, we may assume that the integrable system is
(Y,\omega_{Y},{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}},\varphi\circ f_{Y})
and
p
is the origin, where
(Y,\omega_{Y},{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f_{Y}=(\Phi_{Y},g_{Y}))
is as in Example
5.2
and
\varphi\colon{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}
is a diffeomorphism
[
C
,
VW
]
. Since by assumption the first component of
\varphi\circ f_{Y}
induces an effective
S^{1}
-action on
Y
, and since the level sets of
f_{Y}
are not compact, up to a translation
\Phi_{Y}
and the first component of
\varphi\circ f_{Y}
are equal up to sign (cf.
[
HSS
, Proposition 3.9]
). Since the integrable systems
(Y,\omega_{Y},{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f_{Y}=(\Phi_{Y},g_{Y}))
and
(Y,\omega_{Y},{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}},(-\Phi_{Y},g_{Y}))
are isomorphic, the claim follows.
∎

Hence, focus-focus points satisfy the hypotheses of Proposition
3.4
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f=(\Phi,g))
be an integrable system so that
(M,\omega,\Phi)
is a 4-dimensional complexity one
T
-space. Assume that
p\in\Phi^{-1}(0)\cap g^{-1}(0)
is focus-focus.
Then
there exist an open neighborhood
U
of
[p]\in{M/\!/T}

and a map
\Psi\colon U\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
taking
[p]
to
(0,0)
satisfying:

(1)

\Psi\colon U\to\Psi(U)
is a homeomorphism onto an open set;

(2)

\Psi
restricts to a diffeomorphism on
U\smallsetminus\{[p]\}
; and

(3)

(\overline{g}\circ\Psi^{-1})(x,y)=y
for all
(x,y)\in\Psi(U)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.3
we may assume that the integrable system is the system
(Y,\omega_{Y},{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f_{Y}:=(\Phi_{Y},g_{Y}))
described in Example
5.2
, and that

is the origin. Set
\Psi[z_{1},z_{2}]:=z_{1}z_{2}=P(z_{1},z_{2})
. Then the claim follows immediately from Lemma
2.7
.
∎

We are now almost ready to give our proof of Vu Ngoc’s theorem on the connectedness of the level sets of semitoric systems; however,
we also need the following important result, which is proved in
[
At
,
GS2
,
LMTW
]
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
T
act on a connected symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
with moment map
\Phi\colon M\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
. If
\Phi
is
proper then the level set
\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)
is connected for all
\alpha\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}.

We also need the following proposition. Atiyah proved the compact case (see
[
At
]
), and used it to prove the theorem above;
it was later extended to the proper case; see
[
PRV
, Proposition 4.5]
. (For completeness, we provide a variation on the arguments in
[
PRV
]
in Section
7
.)

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
N
be a connected manifold and let
\overline{g}\colon N\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper Morse-Bott function. If no critical point of
\overline{g}
has index or coindex one, then the level set
\overline{g}^{-1}(c)
is connected for all
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

We observe that the result below is a special case of Theorem
1.6
, and the proof below illustrates our general strategy.

Theorem 5.7

(Vũ Ngoc)
.

Let
(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f=(\Phi,g))
be a semitoric system so that
\Phi
is proper.
Then the level set
f^{-1}(\alpha,c)=\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)\cap g^{-1}(c)
is connected for all
(\alpha,c)\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

Fix
\alpha\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
. Assume without loss of generality that
\alpha=0
. Since
\Phi
is proper, the reduced space
{M/\!/T}
is compact and connected by Theorem
5.5
.

If the reduced space
{M/\!/T}
is a single point
then obviously
\Phi^{-1}(0)
consists of a single
T
-orbit. Hence, the level set

f^{-1}(0,c)
is connected for all
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Otherwise, since the orbit of a short point in
\Phi^{-1}(0)
is an isolated point in
\Phi^{-1}(0)/T
, every point in
\Phi^{-1}(0)
is tall. If
p\in\Phi^{-1}(0)
is a critical point of
g
modulo
\Phi
, then
p
is also a singular point of
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f\right)
. So, by assumption,
p
either has purely elliptic type or is focus-focus. By Propositions
3.4
and
4.5
, and Lemma
5.4
,
{M/\!/T}
can be given the structure of a smooth closed, connected, oriented surface such that
\overline{g}:{M/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a Morse function with no critical point of index 1. By Proposition
5.6
, the level set
\overline{g}^{-1}(c)
is connected for all
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Since
T
is connected, it follows that
f^{-1}(0,c)
is also connected.
∎

6.
Non-degenerate singular points

Let
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=\left(\Phi,g\right)\right)
be an integrable system such that
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is a complexity one
T
-space. Let
p\in\Phi^{-1}(0)
be a tall non-degenerate singular point. By Lemma
3.2
and Proposition
4.5
, if
p
has purely elliptic type, then we can put a smooth structure on
{M/\!/T}
near
[p]
so that
\overline{g}
is locally Morse, and hence

satisfies the hypotheses of Proposition
3.4
. The aim of this section is to extend this result to the remaining cases (see Proposition
6.2
). In particular, if
p
either has a focus-focus block, or has a hyperbolic block and disconnected
T
-stabilizer, then
[p]
is a regular point of
\overline{g}
, while if
p
has a hyperbolic block and connected
T
-stabilizer, then
[p]
is a singular point with index one.

We start by recalling some facts about non-degenerate singular points.

Remark 6.1
.

Let
p
be a point in an integrable system
(M,\omega,V,f)
and let
W_{0}\subseteq V
be the Lie algebra of the stabilizer of
p
. Let
\mathcal{O}
be the
V
-orbit through
p
. The linearized action at
p
induces a Lie algebra homomorphism
W_{0}\to\mathfrak{sp}((T_{p}\mathcal{O})^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathcal{O})
. Given
\xi\in W_{0}
, we can calculate the associated symplectic endomorphism as follows: Let
f_{\mathrm{lin}}
be the homogeneous moment map for the linearized action.
We view
\langle f_{\mathrm{lin}},\xi\rangle
as a homogeneous quadratic polynomial on
(T_{p}\mathcal{O})^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathcal{O}
.
We recall that there is a standard isomorphism between homogeneous quadratic polynomials and
\mathfrak{sp}((T_{p}\mathcal{O})^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathcal{O})
that, upon choosing a standard basis for the symplectic vector space
(T_{p}\mathcal{O})^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathcal{O}
, sends a symmetric matrix
A
to
JA
, where
J
is the standard complex structure on
(T_{p}\mathcal{O})^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathcal{O}
. The above Lie algebra homomorphism sends
\xi
to
J\langle f_{\mathrm{lin}},\xi\rangle
.

If, in addition,
p
is non-degenerate, then for a generic element
\xi
in the Lie algebra of
W
, the eigenvalues of the above endomorphism of
(T_{p}\mathcal{O})^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathcal{O}
can be characterized as follows: If
f_{\mathrm{lin}}
can be written as the product of
k_{e}
elliptic blocks,
k_{h}
hyperbolic blocks and
k_{f}
focus-focus blocks, then the above endomorphism has exactly

distinct non-zero imaginary eigenvalues,

distinct non-zero real eigenvalues, and
4k_{f}
distinct non-zero complex eigenvalues that are neither real nor imaginary. In particular, the numbers
k_{e},k_{h}
and
k_{f}
are a well-defined invariant of
p
.

We can now state our main proposition.

.

U
of
[p]\in{M/\!/T}

(0,0)

Let

be the local model for

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=\left(\Phi,g\right)\right)
be an integrable system such that
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is a complexity one

-space. If

For simplicity, assume that

. By the Marle-Guillemin-Sternberg local normal form theorem, we may assume that

is a local model

, and that

,

(T\times{\mathbb{R}})

.

Let
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=\left(\Phi,g\right)\right)

.

Therefore, we review a few lemmas from Morse theory.

Lemma 7.2

Let

be a connected manifold and let

\overline{g}^{-1}(c)

Let

be a critical manifold that is a local extremum. We may assume that it has index

By the Morse lemma, there exists a neighborhood

Lemma 7.3
.

Let

Conversely, assume that the level set
\overline{g}^{-1}(c)

is simply connected. Since

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
N
be a connected manifold and let
h\colon N\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper function such that
h(N)\subseteq[0,\infty)
. Assume that the restriction of
h
to
h^{-1}(0,\infty)
is a Morse-Bott function with no points of index
1
,
and that
0
is an isolated critical value. Then
h^{-1}(0)
is connected.

Proof.

Consider points
x
and
y
in
h^{-1}(0)
. Since
N
is path-connected, there is a path
\gamma
from
x
to
y
. Hence,
x
and
y
are in the same component of
h^{-1}([0,b))
for some
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
. If
C\subseteq h^{-1}((0,\infty))
is a critical submanifold, then since the index of
C
is not
1
, the restriction
H^{0}(h^{-1}([0,f(C)+\epsilon)))\to H^{0}(h^{-1}([0,f(C)-\epsilon)))
is a surjection for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
. In particular, if
x
and
y
are in the same component of
h^{-1}([0,h(C)+\epsilon))
, then they are also in the same component of
h^{-1}([0,h(C)-\epsilon))
. Moreover, since
h
is proper and
0
is an isolated critical value, there are a finite number of such critical submanifolds in
h^{-1}([0,b))
. Hence, by induction,
x
and
y
are in the same component of
h^{-1}(0).

∎

Proof of Proposition
5.6
.

Fix
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
and define
h\colon N\to{\mathbb{R}}
by
h=(\overline{g}-c)^{2}
. On the set
\overline{g}^{-1}(c,\infty)
the function
h
is Morse-Bott; the critical components of
h
and
\overline{g}
coincide and have the same index. On the set
\overline{g}^{-1}(-\infty,c)
the function
h
is Morse-Bott; the critical components of
h
and
\overline{g}
coincide but the index and coindex of each critical manifold are exchanged. Finally, since
h
is a proper Morse-Bott function there exists a neighborhood of
c
that contains no other critical values, so
0
is an isolated critical value. Therefore,
h^{-1}(0)=\overline{g}^{-1}(c)
is connected by Lemma
7.5
.
∎

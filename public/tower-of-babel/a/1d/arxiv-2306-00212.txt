Provably Efficient Generalized Lagrangian Policy Optimization for Safe Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning
Dongsheng Ding, Xiaohan Wei, Zhuoran Yang, Zhaoran Wang, Mihailo R. Jovanović

Abstract
We examine online safe multi-agent reinforcement learning using constrained Markov games in which agents compete by maximizing their expected total rewards under a constraint on expected total utilities. Our focus is confined to an episodic two-player zero-sum constrained Markov game with independent transition functions that are unknown to agents, adversarial reward functions, and stochastic utility functions. For such a Markov game, we employ an approach based on the occupancy measure to formulate it as an online constrained saddle-point problem with an explicit constraint. We extend the Lagrange multiplier method in constrained optimization to handle the constraint by creating a generalized Lagrangian with minimax decision primal variables and a dual variable. Next, we develop an upper confidence reinforcement learning algorithm to solve this Lagrangian problem while balancing exploration and exploitation. Our algorithm updates the minimax decision primal variables via online mirror descent and the dual variable via projected gradient step and we prove that it enjoys sublinear rate OPEN O ⁡ ( ( | X | + | Y | ) ​ L ​ T ⁡ ( | A | + | B | ) ) ) O((|X|+|Y|)L\sqrt{T(|A|+|B|)})) for both regret and constraint violation after playing T T episodes of the game. Here, L L is the horizon of each episode, ( | X | , | A | ) (|X|,|A|) and ( | Y | , | B | ) (|Y|,|B|) are the state/action space sizes of the min-player and the max-player, respectively. To the best of our knowledge, we provide the first provably efficient online safe reinforcement learning algorithm in constrained Markov games.

Introduction

Safe Reinforcement Learning (RL) studies how a single agent learns to maximize its expected total reward subject to safety-concerned constraints by interacting with an unknown environment over time
(
Garcıa and Fernández 2015
;
Thomas 2015
;
Amodei et al. 2016
)
. The constrained Markov decision processes (MDPs) provide a standard class of constraint critical environment models
(
Altman 1999
)
that are utilized in autonomous robots
(
Feyzabadi 2017
;
Fisac et al. 2018
)
, personalized medicine
(
Girard 2018
)
, online advertising
(
Boutilier and Lu 2016
)
, and financial management
(
Abe et al. 2010
)
. General constrained MDPs for two or more agents are often formulated as constrained Markov games (MGs) in which agents compete under constraints
(
Altman and Shwartz 2000
;
Altman et al. 2005
;
Altman et al. 2008
)
, providing an effective model for safe multi-agent RL
(
Nguyen et al. 2014
;
Shalev-Shwartz et al. 2016
;
Zhang et al. 2021
)
.

Considerable recent progress has been made in single-agent safe RL, especially for solving constrained MDP problems with constraint satisfaction guarantees
(
Efroni et al. 2020
;
Brantley et al. 2020
;
Bai et al. 2020a
;
Ding et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2021
;
Singh et al. 2022
;
Ding et al. 2022b
)
. In these references, Lagrangian-based methods have been combined with the optimistic exploration to address exploration-exploitation trade-off under constraints. These constrained MDP learning algorithms are sample-efficient (in achieving both low regret and low constraint violation) and they effectively enhance classical RL methods to attain safety requirements. However, most of these algorithms are limited to the single-agent setting and it is an open question how to balance the exploration-exploitation trade-off under constraints for multiple agents. Another motivation for our work comes from recent advances on the efficient competitive RL algorithms in MGs
(
Wei et al. 2017
;
Bai and Jin 2020
;
Bai et al. 2020b
;
Xie et al. 2020
)
.

In this work, we take initial steps towards developing provably efficient safe multi-agent RL algorithms. We examine perhaps the most basic safe multi-agent RL setup that involves a two-player zero-sum constrained MG with independent state transitions
(
Altman and Shwartz 2000
;
Altman et al. 2005
;
Altman et al. 2008
;
Singh and Hemachandra 2014
)
.
This problem represents a generalization of constrained MDPs to the two-player case with coupled constraints. In such a constrained MG, two players follow their own state transitions independently, take actions simultaneously, and observe the reward and utility functions while competing against each other by maximizing/minimizing the reward while both are restrained by the constraint regarding some utility for safety reasons. The decision-coupling that arises from the constraint is often encountered in multi-agent systems
(
Rosen 1965
;
Li and Marden 2014
;
Kulkarni 2011
;
Kulkarni 2017
;
De Nijs 2019
)
. More specifically, we aim to design an online RL algorithm for solving episodic two-player zero-sum constrained MGs. Here, two players do not know the transition models and have no access to a generative model, but can play the game for multiple episodes using arbitrary policies. The goal is to find an approximate constrained Nash equilibrium of the game in hindsight, a generalization of Nash equilibrium to characterize violating constraints if any unilateral deviations occur. We utilize a notion of regret to quantify the approximation error of the constrained Nash equilibrium and employ a constraint dissatisfaction (which results from violation of any utility constraints) to evaluate the constraint violation.

Problem Setup

In this section, we introduce zero-sum Markov games (MGs) with constraints, which are categorized as constrained Markov/stochastic games
(
Altman and Shwartz 2000
;
Altman et al. 2005
;
Altman et al. 2008
)
.

In an episodic constrained MG there are two players; a
min-player
–
(X,A,P_{1},r,g,T)
, which minimizes the reward, and a
max-player
–
(Y,B,P_{2},r,h,T)
, which maximizes the reward, while adhering to a coupled utility constraint. Here,
T
is the number of episodes,
X
and
Y
are finite state spaces,
A
and
B
are finite action spaces,
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are transition probability measures where
P_{1}(\cdot\,|\,x,a)
is a distribution over
X
if the min-player takes action
a
in state
x
and
P_{2}(\cdot\,|\,y,b)
is a distribution over
Y
if the max-player takes action
b
in state
y
,
r\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{r^{t}\}_{t\,=\,1}^{T}
is a collection of players’ reward functions
r^{t}
:
X\times Y\times A\times B\to[0,1]
, whereas
g\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{g^{t}\}_{t\,=\,1}^{T}
and
h\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{h^{t}\}_{t\,=\,1}^{T}
are collections of players’ utility functions
g^{t}
:
X\times A\to[0,1]
,
h^{t}
:
Y\times B\to[0,1]
. For two independent transitions, players are coupled via the reward function and a constraint on their utility functions.

We utilize layered Markov decision processes to model the environment dynamics. For each player, e.g., the min-player, we assume that the state space
X
has
L+1
layers and that it satisfies the loop-free property: (i)
X\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=X_{0}\cup\cdots\cup X_{L}
and
X_{\ell_{1}}\cap X_{\ell_{2}}=\emptyset
for
\ell_{1}\neq\ell_{2}
; (ii)
X_{0}=\{x_{0}\}
and
X_{L}=\{x_{L}\}
; (iii) if
P_{1}(x^{\prime}\,|\,x,a)>0
, then
x^{\prime}\in X_{\ell+1}
and
x\in X_{\ell}
for some
\ell\in\{0,1,\cdots,L\}
. This assumption is common in loop-free stochastic shortest path problems
(
György et al. 2007
;
Jaksch et al. 2010
;
Neu et al. 2010
;
Rosenberg and Mansour 2019
;
Jin et al. 2020
)
; it is often used to simplify notation/analysis since any episodic MDPs can be reduced to be loop-free.

The min/max players interact with the environment in episode
t
as follows. At the beginning, the environment determines the reward function
r^{t}
and the utility functions
g^{t}
and
h^{t}
. Meanwhile, two players decide their policies
\pi^{t}
:
X\times A\to[0,1]
and
\mu^{t}
:
Y\times B\to[0,1]
, where
\pi^{t}(\cdot\,|\,x)
and
\mu^{t}(\cdot\,|\,y)
are probability distributions over their action spaces
A
and
B
, respectively. Then, given initial states
x_{0}
and
y_{0}
, both players execute their own policies
\pi^{t}
or
\mu^{t}
for
L
steps. At step
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}
, each player only observes its own state
x_{\ell}\in X
or
y_{\ell}\in Y
, takes action
a_{\ell}
or
b_{\ell}
following its own policy
\pi^{t}
or
\mu^{t}
, transits to next state
x_{\ell+1}
or
y_{\ell+1}
according to its own transition
P_{1}(\cdot\,|\,x_{\ell},a_{\ell})
or
P_{2}(\cdot\,|\,y_{\ell},b_{\ell})
, and observes reward
r^{t}
and local utility
g^{t}
or
h^{t}
. Assume there is no dependence between functions
r^{t}
,
g^{t}
, and
h^{t}
and they are independent of the underlying MDPs.

To define the learning objective, for the min-player in episode
t
we introduce the occupancy measure
q_{1}^{t}
:
X\times A\times X\to[0,1]
by
q_{1}^{t}(x,a,x^{\prime})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\text{Prob}(x_{\ell}=x,a_{\ell}=a,x_{\ell+1}=x^{\prime})
for
x\in X_{\ell}
, describing the marginal probability of visiting
(x,a,x^{\prime})
when executing policy
\pi^{t}
under the transition
P_{1}
. Similarly, we introduce the occupancy measure
q_{2}^{t}
:
Y\times B\times Y\to[0,1]
for the max-player. We recall that a function
q
:
X\times A\times X\to[0,1]
is an occupancy measure associated with policy
\pi
and transition
P
if and only if it satisfies two conditions
(
Altman 1999
)
: (i)
\sum_{x\in X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{x^{\prime}\in X_{\ell+1}}q(x,a,x^{\prime})=1
for
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}
; (ii)
\sum_{x\in X_{\ell-1}}\sum_{a\in A}q(x,a,x^{\prime})=\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{x^{\prime\prime}\in X_{\ell+1}}q(x^{\prime},a,x^{\prime\prime})
for
x^{\prime}\in X_{\ell}
and
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L-1\}
. We denote by
\Delta(P)
a set of valid occupancy measures under
P
,

\Delta(P)\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\big\{q\!:\!X\times A\times X\to[0,1]\;|\;q\text{ satisfies (i) and (ii) as shown above}\big\}.

It is worth noting that the occupancy measure set is convex and compact for finite MDPs
(
Altman 1999
)
.
Using an occupancy measure
q
, we can express associated transition
P
and policy
\pi
as

P(x^{\prime}\,|\,x,a)\;=\;\frac{q(x,a,x^{\prime})}{\sum_{x^{\prime\prime}\,\in\,X_{\ell+1}}q(x,a,x^{\prime\prime})}\;\text{ and }\;\pi(a\,|\,x)\;=\;\frac{\sum_{x^{\prime}\,\in\,X_{\ell+1}}q(x,a,x^{\prime})}{\sum_{a\,\in\,A}\sum_{x^{\prime\prime}\,\in\,X_{\ell+1}}q(x,a,x^{\prime\prime})}

(1)

where
x\in X_{\ell}
. Slightly extending the notation
q
, we use it to represent the probability of visiting
(x,a)
, i.e.,
q(x,a)=\sum_{x^{\prime}\,\in\,X_{\ell+1}}q(x,a,x^{\prime})
for
x\neq x_{L}
. These properties imply that the problem of learning a policy equals learning the associated occupancy measure
(
Zimin and Neu 2013
)
.

In episode
t
, given a min-policy
\pi^{t}
and a max-policy
\mu^{t}
, we introduce the expected total reward,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P_{1},P_{2},\pi^{t},\mu^{t}}\left[\,\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}r^{t}(x_{\ell},y_{\ell},a_{\ell},b_{\ell})\,\right]\;=\;\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell},\,y\,\in\,Y_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A,\,b\,\in\,B}q_{1}^{t}(x,a)q_{2}^{t}(y,b)r^{t}(x,y,a,b)\\[5.69046pt]
&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\displaystyle\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\left\langle q_{1}^{t}\cdot q_{2}^{t},r^{t}\right\rangle\end{array}

(2)

where the expectation
\mathbb{E}
is taken over the random state-action sequence
\{(x_{\ell},y_{\ell},a_{\ell},b_{\ell})\}_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}
; the action
a_{\ell}
follows the policy
\pi^{t}(\cdot\,|\,x_{\ell})
in the state
x_{\ell}
and the next state
x_{\ell+1}
follows the transition
P_{1}(\cdot\,|\,x_{\ell},a_{\ell})
; the action
b_{\ell}
follows the policy
\mu^{t}(\cdot\,|\,y_{\ell})
in the state
y_{\ell}
and the next state
y_{\ell+1}
follows the transition
P_{2}(\cdot\,|\,y_{\ell},b_{\ell})
.
Similarly, we can define the expected total utilities as

\mathbb{E}_{P_{1},\pi^{t}}\left[\,\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}g_{x}^{t}(x_{\ell},a_{\ell})\,\right]\;=\;\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}q_{1}^{t}(x,a)g^{t}(x,a)\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\left\langle q_{1}^{t},g^{t}\right\rangle

(3a)

\mathbb{E}_{P_{2},\mu^{t}}\left[\,\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}h^{t}(y_{\ell},a_{\ell})\,\right]\;=\;\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{y\,\in\,Y_{\ell}}\sum_{b\,\in\,B}q_{2}^{t}(y,b)h^{t}(y,b)\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\left\langle q_{2}^{t},h^{t}\right\rangle.

(3b)

In general, reward function
r^{t}
and utility functions
g^{t}
and
h^{t}
all can change arbitrarily, i.e., being adversarial. However, even if we fix the opponent’s policy, there is no algorithm for the player to achieve sublinear regret and constraint violation at the same time when the constraints are changing adversarially
(
Mannor et al. 2009
)
. Hence, we restrict the utility functions to be stochastic:
g^{t}(x,a)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=g(x,a;\xi^{t})
,
h^{t}(y,b)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=h(y,b;\xi^{t})
with
\mathbb{E}\left[g^{t}(x,a)\right]=g(x,a)
and
\mathbb{E}\left[h^{t}(y,b)\right]=h(y,b)
, for any
x\in X
,
a\in A
and
y\in Y
,
b\in B
, where
\xi^{t}
is an independent random variable.

Learning Performance
. We now define the underlying constrained optimization problem and the solution concept for learning constrained MGs. Using the notion of occupancy measure, we formulate a constrained minimax problem in which the objective function is a sum of the expected total rewards over
T
episodes and the constraint is on a sum of two agent’ expected total utilities,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\minimize\limits_{q_{1}\,\in\,\Delta(P_{1})}\,\,\maximize\limits_{q_{2}\,\in\,\Delta(P_{2})}&&\!\!\!\!\displaystyle\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\left\langle q_{1}\cdot q_{2},r^{t}\right\rangle\;\;\;\subject\;\;\;\left\langle q_{1},g\right\rangle\,+\,\left\langle q_{2},h\right\rangle\;\leq\;b\end{array}

(4)

where we take
b\in(0,{2L}]
to avoid trivial cases since we note that
\langle{q_{1}},{g}\rangle
,
\langle{q_{2}},{h}\rangle\in[0,L]
. The coupled constraint is used to model the limited use of budget/resource for two players; multi-agent problems with a common constraint are often called
weakly-coupled
or
non-orthogonal
in the literature on CMDPs
(
Meuleau et al. 1998
;
Boutilier and Lu 2016
;
Wei et al. 2018
;
Salemi Parizi 2018
;
Gagrani and Nayyar 2020
)
and constrained MGs
(
Altman et al. 2008
;
Altman and Solan 2009
;
Kulkarni 2011
;
Singh and Hemachandra 2014
;
Kulkarni 2017
)
. We can generalize it to multiple or local side constraints, e.g.,
\langle{q_{1}},{g}\rangle\leq b_{1}
or
\langle{q_{2}},{h}\rangle\leq b_{2}
. When transitions
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are known, the occupancy measure sets
\Delta(P_{1})
and
\Delta(P_{2})
define convex polytopes on
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
.

Let
(q_{1}^{\star},q_{2}^{\star})
be a solution to Problem (
4
) in hindsight. The existence of
(q_{1}^{\star},q_{2}^{\star})
follows from compactness of the constraint sets
(
Neumann 1928
;
Rosen 1965
)
. It is standard to define an intuitive solution – constrained Nash equilibrium – via two conditions
(
Altman and Shwartz 2000
;
Daskalakis et al. 2021
)
:

(i)

\displaystyle\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\langle q_{1}^{\star}\cdot q_{2}^{\star},r^{t}\rangle\;\leq\;\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\langle q_{1}\cdot q_{2}^{\star},r^{t}\rangle
for any
q_{1}\in\Delta(P_{1})
satisfying
\langle{q_{1}},{g}\rangle+\langle{q_{2}^{\star}},{h}\rangle\leq b
;

(ii)

\displaystyle\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\langle q_{1}^{\star}\cdot q_{2},r^{t}\rangle\;\leq\;\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\langle q_{1}^{\star}\cdot q_{2}^{\star},r^{t}\rangle
for any
q_{2}\in\Delta(P_{2})
satisfying
\left\langle q_{1}^{\star},g\right\rangle+\left\langle q_{2},h\right\rangle\leq b
.

Any unilateral deviation from the constrained Nash equilibrium will either break the constraint, or if it is not, then there is no benefit for this player.
With this solution concept, we define the regret for any algorithm that plays the game for
T
episodes by

\text{Regret}(T)\;=\;\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\left(\left\langle q_{1}^{t}\cdot q_{2}^{\star},r^{t}\right\rangle-\left\langle q_{1}^{\star}\cdot q_{2}^{t},r^{t}\right\rangle\right)

(5)

which adds two side optimality gaps,
\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\left\langle q_{1}^{t}\cdot q_{2}^{\star},r^{t}\right\rangle-\left\langle q_{1}^{\star}\cdot q_{2}^{\star},r^{t}\right\rangle
for the min-player and
\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\langle{q_{1}^{\star}\cdot q_{2}^{\star}},{r^{t}}\rangle-\langle{q_{1}^{\star}\cdot q_{2}^{t}},{r^{t}}\rangle
for the max-player, and two players take policies
\pi^{t}
and
\mu^{t}
in episode
t
and they define occupancy measures
q_{1}^{t}
and
q_{2}^{t}
under the true transitions
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
. This regret works in a notion of weak regret
(
Brafman and Tennenholtz 2002
;
Bai and Jin 2020
;
Xie et al. 2020
)
instead of the single-agent type regret
(
Tian et al. 2020
;
Bai et al. 2020b
)
which is statistically and computationally hard to bound sublinearly.

To measure the constraint satisfaction, we introduce the violation as a non-negative part of accumulated constraint violations
\langle{q_{1}^{t}},{g}\rangle+\langle{q_{2}^{t}},{h}\rangle-b
over
T
episodes,

\text{Violation}(T)\;=\;\left[\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\left(\left\langle q_{1}^{t},g^{t}\right\rangle+\left\langle q_{2}^{t},h^{t}\right\rangle-b\right)\right]_{+}.

(6)

We next assume feasibility that ensures the existence of constrained Nash equilibrium
(
Altman and Shwartz 2000
)
. Feasibility can be verified by a priori knowledge on feasible policies.

Assumption 1

(Feasibility)
.

There exists a joint policy
(\bar{\pi},\bar{\mu})
associated to the occupancy measure
(\bar{q}_{1},\bar{q}_{2})
and a constant
\xi>0
such that
\left\langle\bar{q}_{1},g\right\rangle+\left\langle\bar{q}_{2},h\right\rangle+\xi\leq b
.

Having defined the learning performance, we will work with the occupancy measure in the online learning setting where the two players do not know the transition functions, only observe reward/utility functions at the end of each episode, repeatedly play the game for a fixed number of episodes to learn the constrained Nash equilibrium in hindsight.

Proposed Algorithm

We present a variant of upper confidence reinforcement learning in Algorithm
1
– an
\underline{\text{U}}
pper
\underline{\text{C}}
onfidence
\underline{\text{B}}
ound
\underline{\text{C}}
onstrained
\underline{\text{SA}}
ddle-
\underline{\text{P}}
oint
\underline{\text{O}}
ptimization (UCB-CSAPO) algorithm – for learning constrained MGs. Conceptually, the algorithm works as the primal-dual policy optimization
(
Efroni et al. 2020
;
Ding et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2021
)
in the Lagrangian-based framework, which makes it a simple policy optimization algorithm. However, our primal update exploits the structure of constrained MGs to maintain two players’ occupancy measures. The domain set of occupancy measures builds on the upper confidence bound exploration or optimism
(
Jaksch et al. 2010
)
regarding the estimated transition models using past trajectories. The dual update determines the penalty weight by collecting the possible constraint violation already acquired. In each episode, our algorithm has two key stages: (i) The generalized Lagrangian mirror descent step for updating the occupancy measures with optimism; (ii) The estimation of confidence sets on the occupancy measures.

Generalized Lagrangian Mirror Descent Step
. The main idea of this step is to apply the online primal-dual mirror descent – an algorithmic generalization of online mirror descent to the constrained problems
(
Wei et al. 2020
)

5
Concluding Remarks

We have examined an episodic two-player zero-sum constrained Markov game (MG) with independent transition functions. In our setup, transition functions are unknown to agents, reward functions are adversarial, and utility functions are stochastic. We have proposed the first provably efficient algorithm for playing constrained MGs with
O(\sqrt{T})
regret and constraint violation. Our algorithm provides a principled extension of the upper confidence reinforcement learning to deal with coupled constraints in constrained MGs. We also remark that the developed algorithmic framework can be readily applied to learning other constrained MGs, e.g., the ones that involve a single controller.

Our work opens up many interesting directions for future work, such as sharper algorithms with sample complexity lower bounds, constrained rational algorithms, and how to perform safe exploration in other models of constrained MGs.

Li et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2022a
)
. While these results provide provably efficient algorithms regarding regret and constraint satisfaction in the single-agent setting, they are not applicable to our multi-agent game being played under constraints, because of the
non-convexity
nauture of constrained multi-agent policy optimization and the
non-stationary
environment each agent is facing. An extended line of work on constrained MDPs focuses on cooperative multi-agent learning under constraints and most efforts study the case where multiple agents have independent MDPs with a coupled budget/resource constraint
(
Meuleau et al. 1998
;
Boutilier and Lu 2016
;
Wei et al. 2018
;
de Nijs and Stuckey 2020
;
Gagrani and Nayyar 2020
)
. All these results assume that transition models or system dynamics are known. Only a few studies considered the shared MDP case
(
Diddigi et al. 2019
;
Lu et al. 2020
;
Parnika et al. 2021
;
Gu et al. 2021
)
, but they either lack of theoretical guarantees or do not handle exploration. In contrast, our work focuses on the MG setting with unknown transition models, and attacks the exploration challenge directly.

Single-agent RL in MDPs & multi-agent RL in MGs
.
A considerable literature has provided sample-efficient online RL methods in single-agent and multi-agent unconstrained RL settings; see recent summaries in
Foster et al. 2021
;
Du et al. 2021
;
Jin et al. 2021
for single-agent RL and
Jin et al. 2022b
;
Jin et al. 2022a
;
Song et al. 2021
for multi-agent RL. However, it is largely open to extend those sample-efficient online RL methods to constrained MGs due to several technical challenges. First, since the Bellman optimality fails even in constrained MDPs
(
Piunovskiy and Mao 2000
;
Borkar 2005
)
and the optimal constrained policy is often stochastic
(
Altman 1999
)
, value-based RL methods are not suitable. Second, applying policy-based RL methods often warrants solving constrained policy optimization problems that are not convex
(
Achiam et al. 2017
;
Ding et al. 2020
)
, not mentioning multi-agent policy optimization problems. Third, designing a sample-efficient online RL algorithm for constrained MGs has to deal with the fundamental exploitation/exploration tradeoff under constraints
(
Efroni et al. 2020
;
Brantley et al. 2020
;
Ding et al. 2021
)
. Despite some recent progress in dealing with each technical issue individually, it is crucial to address them together for multi-agent RL in constrained MGs. In this work, we offer the first positive answer by identifying a class of zero-sum constrained MGs, establishing a new policy optimization algorithm with online exploration for learning such games, and proving near-optimal sample efficiency.

Efficient Implementation of (

In this section, we provide an efficient implementation for the primal update (

).

Since the minimax objective in the primal update (
8
) is separable for two players, it is equivalent to update two occupancy measures individually via

\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}\;=\;\displaystyle\argmin_{q_{1}\,\in\,\Delta(k_{1}^{t})}\;V\,\big\langle{q_{1}\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}},{r^{t-1}}\big\rangle\,+\,\lambda^{t-1}\langle{q_{1}},{g^{t-1}}\rangle\,+\,\displaystyle\eta^{-1}D\big(q_{1}\,|\,\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\big)

(12a)

(12b)

Note that
\langle{q_{1}\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}},{r^{t-1}}\rangle=\langle{q_{1}},{\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}\cdot r^{t-1}}\rangle

We can express (

\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}\;=\;\displaystyle\argmin_{q_{1}\,\in\,\Delta(k_{1}^{t})}\;\eta\,\langle{q_{1}},{\phi_{1}^{t-1}}\rangle\,+\,\displaystyle D\big(q_{1}\,|\,\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\big)

(13a)

\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}\;=\;\displaystyle\argmin_{q_{2}\,\in\,\Delta(k_{2}^{t})}\;\eta\,\langle{q_{2}},{\phi_{2}^{t-1}}\rangle\,+\,\displaystyle D\big(q_{2}\,|\,\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}\big)

\alpha(x,a)
,
\lambda_{\ell}
,

,

, and

By the Lagrange duality theory, the strong duality holds. To find the optimal solution to the projection problem in (

for

which is zero if we take

. Using this stationary condition, we simplify the Lagrangian

and

&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\displaystyle\,+\,\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}\sum_{x^{\prime}\,\in\,X_{\ell+1}}\mu^{+}(x,a,x^{\prime})\left((1-\epsilon_{1}^{k_{1}}(x,a))q_{1}(x,a,x^{\prime})-\bar{P}_{1}^{k_{1}}(x^{\prime}\,|\,x,a)\sum_{x^{\prime\prime}\,\in\,X_{\ell+1}}q_{1}(x,a,x^{\prime\prime})\right)\\[5.69046pt]
&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\displaystyle\,+\,\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}\sum_{x^{\prime}\,\in\,X_{\ell+1}}\mu^{-}(x,a,x^{\prime})\left(\bar{P}_{1}^{k_{1}}(x^{\prime}\,|\,x,a)\sum_{x^{\prime\prime}\,\in\,X_{\ell+1}}q_{1}(x,a,x^{\prime\prime})-(1+\epsilon_{1}^{k_{1}}(x,a))q_{1}(x,a,x^{\prime})\right).\end{array}

\beta(x_{0})=\beta(x_{L})=0

(18)

16

. We note that it is not unique to determine

for some

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\beta^{\star},\mu^{+,\star},\mu^{-,\star}&=&\displaystyle\argmax_{\beta,\,\mu^{+},\,\mu^{-}\,\geq\,0}\maximize_{\alpha,\,\lambda}\,\minimize_{q_{1}}\;{\mathcal{L}}(q_{1};\alpha,\lambda,\beta,\mu^{+},\mu^{-})\\[5.69046pt]

where the third equality is due to:
\frac{\partial{\mathcal{L}}}{\partial q_{1}(x,a,x^{\prime})}|_{q_{1}^{\star}(x,a,x^{\prime})}=0
and
\frac{\partial{\mathcal{L}}}{\partial q_{1}(x,a)}|_{q_{1}^{\star}(x,a)}=0
, and we ignore all constants that are independent of
\beta
,
\mu^{+}
, and
\mu^{-}
for the last equality; we note that this minimization problem is a convex optimization problem over the nonnegative orthant. Hence, we have proved the update (
14a
) as an efficient update (
18

Similarly, we have an efficient update (
14b
) for the second problem (

We first present a basic property of the Kullback-Leibler divergence that generalizes similar properties in the literature
(
Nemirovski et al. 2009
;
Tseng 2009
;
Wei et al. 2020
)
to the convex-concave minimax problems. For this purpose, we set some standard notations. Let
{\mathcal{X}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex set with non-empty interior,
{\mathcal{X}}^{\text{int}}\neq\emptyset
. Let
\phi
:
{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function that is is continuously differentiable on
{\mathcal{X}}^{\text{int}}
. Let
\Delta_{x}\subset{\mathcal{X}}
be a compact convex set containing the origin. Denote
\Delta_{x}^{o}=\Delta\cap{\mathcal{X}}^{\text{ int}}
and let
\Delta_{x}^{o}\neq\emptyset
. We define the Kullback-Leibler divergence,
D
:
\Delta_{x}\times\Delta_{x}^{o}\to\mathbb{R}
,

D(x,x^{\prime})\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\phi(x)-\phi(x^{\prime})-\langle\nabla\phi(x^{\prime}),x-x^{\prime}\rangle.

An interesting case is when
\Delta_{x}
becomes a probability simplex.
If
\phi(x)=\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{d}(x_{i}\log x_{i}-x_{i})
, then
D(x,x^{\prime})=\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{d}x_{i}\log(x_{i}/x_{i}^{\prime})-\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{d}(x_{i}-x_{i}^{\prime})
defines the unnormalized Kullback-Leibler divergence
(
Cover 1999
;
Boyd et al. 2004
)
. This is the setup we will discuss later.

Lemma 13
.

Let
f(x,y)
:
{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous differentiable function that is convex in
x
and concave in
y
, where
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
are compact convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose for some
x^{\prime}\in\Delta_{x}^{o}
and
y^{\prime}\in\Delta_{y}^{o}
,

(x^{\star},y^{\star})\;\in\;\argminimax_{x\,\in\,\Delta_{x},\,y\,\in\,\Delta_{y}}\;f(x,y)+\eta^{-1}D(x\,|\,x^{\prime})-\eta^{-1}D(y\,|\,y^{\prime})

and
x^{\star}\in\Delta_{x}^{o}
and
y^{\star}\in\Delta_{y}^{o}
,
where
\eta>0
.
Then, for any
x\in\Delta_{x}
and
y\in\Delta_{y}
,

f(x^{\star},y)+\eta^{-1}\big(D(x^{\star},x^{\prime})+D(y^{\star},y^{\prime})\big)\;\leq\;f(x,y^{\star})+\eta^{-1}\big(D(x,x^{\prime})+D(y,y^{\prime})-D(x,x^{\star})-D(y,y^{\star})\big).

Proof.

For the smooth convex-concave function
f
, it is necessary to have the first-order stationary condition on
(x^{\star},y^{\star})
.
There exist
\nabla_{x}f(x^{\star},y^{\star})
and
\nabla_{y}(x^{\star},y^{\star})
such that

\left\langle\nabla_{x}f(x^{\star},y^{\star})+\eta^{-1}\big(\phi(x^{\star})-\phi(x^{\prime})\big),x-x^{\star}\right\rangle\;\geq\;0,\;\text{ for any }x\in\Delta_{x}

(28a)

\left\langle-\nabla_{y}f(x^{\star},y^{\star})+\eta^{-1}\big(\phi(y^{\star})-\phi(y^{\prime})\big),y-y^{\star}\right\rangle\;\geq\;0,\;\text{ for any }y\in\Delta_{y}.

(28b)

By the definition of
D(\cdot\,|\,\cdot)
,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\eta^{-1}\big(D(x,x^{\prime})-D(x,x^{\star})\big)\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\eta^{-1}\big(\phi(x^{\star})-\phi(x^{\prime})-\langle\nabla\phi(x^{\prime}),x-x^{\prime}\rangle+\langle\nabla\phi(x^{\star}),x-x^{\star}\rangle\big)\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\eta^{-1}\big(\phi(x^{\star})-\phi(x^{\prime})-\eta^{-1}\langle\nabla\phi(x^{\prime}),x^{\star}-x^{\prime}\rangle\big)-\langle\nabla_{x}f(x^{\star},y^{\star}),x-x^{\star}\rangle\\[5.69046pt]
&&\,+\,\langle\nabla_{x}f(x^{\star},y^{\star})+\eta^{-1}\big(\nabla\phi(x^{\star})-\nabla\phi(x^{\prime})\big),x-x^{\star}\rangle\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\eta^{-1}D(x^{\star},x^{\prime})-\langle\nabla_{x}f(x^{\star},y^{\star}),x-x^{\star}\rangle\\[5.69046pt]
&&\,+\,\langle\nabla_{x}f(x^{\star},y^{\star})+\eta^{-1}\big(\nabla\phi(x^{\star})-\nabla\phi(x^{\prime})\big),x-x^{\star}\rangle.\end{array}

Application of (
28a
) leads to

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\eta^{-1}\big(D(x,x^{\prime})-D(x,x^{\star})\big)&\geq&\eta^{-1}D(x^{\star},x^{\prime})-\langle\nabla_{x}f(x^{\star},y^{\star}),x-x^{\star}\rangle\\[5.69046pt]
&\geq&\eta^{-1}D(x^{\star},x^{\prime})+f(x^{\star},y^{\star})-f(x,y^{\star})\end{array}

(29)

where the last inequality is due to the convexity fo
f(x,y^{\star})
in
x
:

f(x,y^{\star})\geq f(x^{\star},y^{\star})+\langle\nabla_{x}f(x^{\star},y^{\star}),x-x^{\star}\rangle
.

Similarly, we work on
\eta^{-1}\big(D(y,y^{\prime})-D(y,y^{\star})\big)
and (
28b
).

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\eta^{-1}\big(D(y,y^{\prime})-D(y,y^{\star})\big)&\geq&\eta^{-1}D(y^{\star},y^{\prime})+f(x^{\star},y)-f(x^{\star},y^{\star}).\end{array}

(30)

Finally, we conclude the proof by adding (
29
) to (
30
) from both sides.

Lemma 14
.

Let
q(x,a,x^{\prime})
and
q^{\prime}(x,a,x^{\prime})
be two occupancy measures, and
q(x,a)
and
q^{\prime}(x,a)
be the associated state-action visitation probability distributions. Then,

D(q,q^{\prime})\;\geq\;\frac{1}{2L}\left\|{q-q^{\prime}}\right\|_{1}^{2}.

Proof.

We recall
q(x,a)
and
q^{\prime}(x,a)
,

q(x,a)\;=\;\sum_{x^{\prime}\,\in\,X_{\ell}}q(x,a,x^{\prime})\;\text{ and }\;q^{\prime}(x,a)\;=\;\sum_{x^{\prime}\,\in\,X_{\ell}}q^{\prime}(x,a,x^{\prime})

where
\ell
is the layer that
x
belongs to. We note that
q(x,a)
and
q^{\prime}(x,a)
define probability laws for each
\ell\in\{0,1,\ldots,L-1\}
, and
\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}q(x,a)=\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}q^{\prime}(x,a)=1
.

By the definition,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}D(q,q^{\prime})&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}q(x,a)\log\frac{q(x,a)}{q^{\prime}(x,a)}\,-\,\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}\big(q(x,a)-q^{\prime}(x,a)\big)\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}q(x,a)\log\frac{q(x,a)}{q^{\prime}(x,a)}\\[5.69046pt]
&\geq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\left\|{q(x,a)-q^{\prime}(x,a)}\right\|_{1}^{2}\\[5.69046pt]
&\geq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2L}\left(\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\left\|{q(x,a)-q^{\prime}(x,a)}\right\|_{1}\right)^{2}\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2L}\left\|{q-q^{\prime}}\right\|_{1}^{2}\end{array}

where we apply the Pinsker’s inequality to
\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}q(x,a)\log\frac{q(x,a)}{q^{\prime}(x,a)}
in the first inequality.

Lemma 15
.

Let
q(x,a,x^{\prime})
and
q^{\prime}(x,a,x^{\prime})
be two occupancy measures, and
q(x,a)
and
q^{\prime}(x,a)
be the associated state-action visitation probability laws.
Define
\widetilde{q}{\,{}^{\prime}}(x,a)=(1-\theta)q^{\prime}(x,a)+\theta\frac{1}{|X_{\ell}||A|}
for
(x,a)\in X_{\ell}\times A
,
\ell\in\{0,1,\ldots,L-1\}
, and
\theta\in(0,1]
. Then,

D(q,\widetilde{q}{\,{}^{\prime}})-D(q,q^{\prime})\;\leq\;\theta L\log(|X||A|)\;\text{ and }\;D(q,\widetilde{q}{\,{}^{\prime}})\;\leq\;L\log(|X||A|/\theta).

Proof.

By the definition,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!D(q,\widetilde{q}{\,{}^{\prime}})-D(q,q^{\prime})\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}q(x,a)\left(\log\frac{q(x,a)}{\widetilde{q}{\,{}^{\prime}}(x,a)}-\log\frac{q(x,a)}{q^{\prime}(x,a)}\right)-\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}\big(q^{\prime}(x,a)-\widetilde{q}{\,{}^{\prime}}(x,a)\big)\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}q(x,a)\left(\log{q^{\prime}(x,a)}-\log{\widetilde{q}{\,{}^{\prime}}(x,a)}\right)\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}q(x,a)\left(\log{q^{\prime}(x,a)}-\log\left((1-\theta)q^{\prime}(x,a)+\theta\frac{1}{|X_{\ell}||A|}\right)\right).\end{array}

By the Jensen’s inequality,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!D(q,\widetilde{q}{\,{}^{\prime}})-D(q,q^{\prime})\\[5.69046pt]
&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}q(x,a)\left(\log{q^{\prime}(x,a)}-(1-\theta)\log q^{\prime}(x,a)-\theta\log{\frac{1}{|X_{\ell}||A|}}\right)\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}\theta q(x,a)\left(\log{q^{\prime}(x,a)}+\log{|X_{\ell}||A|}\right)\\[5.69046pt]
&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}\theta q(x,a)\log{|X_{\ell}||A|}\\[5.69046pt]
&\leq&\displaystyle\theta L\log|X||A|\end{array}

where the second inequality is due to that a negative entropy is non-positive.

We next prove the second inequality. By the definition,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}D(q,\widetilde{q}{\,{}^{\prime}})&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}q(x,a){\log\frac{q(x,a)}{\widetilde{q}{\,{}^{\prime}}(x,a)}}-\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}\big(q(x,a)-\widetilde{q}{\,{}^{\prime}}(x,a)\big)\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}q(x,a)\left(\log{q(x,a)}-\log{\widetilde{q}{\,{}^{\prime}}(x,a)}\right)\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}q(x,a)\left(\log{q(x,a)}-\log\left((1-\theta)q^{\prime}(x,a)+\theta\frac{1}{|X_{\ell}||A|}\right)\right)\\[5.69046pt]
&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}-q(x,a){\log\left((1-\theta)q^{\prime}(x,a)+\theta\frac{1}{|X_{\ell}||A|}\right)}\end{array}

where the last inequality is due to that a negative entropy is non-positive. We note that
-\log(\cdot)
is a non-increasing function. We can simplify the upper bound on
D(q,\widetilde{q}{\,{}^{\prime}})
above by,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}D(q,\widetilde{q}{\,{}^{\prime}})&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}-q(x,a){\log\left(\theta\frac{1}{|X_{\ell}||A|}\right)}\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}q(x,a)\log{\frac{|X_{\ell}||A|}{\theta}}\\[5.69046pt]
&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}q(x,a)\log{\frac{|X||A|}{\theta}}\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\displaystyle L\log\frac{|X||A|}{\theta}.\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!V\,\big\langle{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}},{r^{t-1}}\big\rangle\,+\,\Delta^{t}\,+\,\eta^{-1}\big(D(\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t},\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1})+D(\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t},\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1})\big)\\[5.69046pt]

&&\,+\,\eta^{-1}\big(D(q_{1}^{\star},\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1})+D(q_{2}^{\star},\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1})\,-\,D(q_{1}^{\star},\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t})\,-\,D(q_{2}^{\star},\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t})\big).\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!V\,\big\langle{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}},{r^{t-1}}\big\rangle\,+\,\eta^{-1}\big(D(\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t},\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1})+D(\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t},\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1})\big)\\[5.69046pt]

&\geq&-\,V\,\left\|{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}-\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}\,-\,2\theta VL\,+\,(2\eta L)^{-1}\left\|{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}-\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}^{2}\\[5.69046pt]
&&\,-\,2\theta VL\,-\,V\,\left\|{\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}}\right\|_{1}\,+\,(2\eta L)^{-1}\left\|{\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}-\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}\\[5.69046pt]

where we apply the Hölder’s inequality and Lemma

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\left\|{\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}\left|(1-\theta)\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}(x,a)+\theta\frac{1}{|X_{\ell}||A|}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}(x,a)\right|\\[5.69046pt]

&=&2\theta L\end{array}

and
\|{\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}}\|_{1}\leq 2\theta L
that can be proved similarly, and the last inequality is due to

for any

,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\Delta^{t}\,-\,4\theta VL\,-\,\eta V^{2}L\\[5.69046pt]
&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\leq V\,\big\langle{q_{1}^{\star}\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\cdot q_{2}^{\star}},{r^{t-1}}\big\rangle\,+\,\lambda^{t-1}\big(\langle{q_{1}^{\star}},{g^{t-1}}\rangle+\langle{q_{2}^{\star}},{h^{t-1}}\rangle-b\big)\,+\,2L^{2}\\[5.69046pt]

\begin{array}[]{rcl}D(q_{1}^{\star},\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1})-D(q_{1}^{\star},\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t})&=&D(q_{1}^{\star},\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1})-D(q_{1}^{\star},\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1})+D(q_{1}^{\star},\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1})-D(q_{1}^{\star},\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t})\\[5.69046pt]

and, similarly,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Delta^{t}&\leq&V\,\big\langle{q_{1}^{\star}\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\cdot q_{2}^{\star}},{r^{t-1}}\big\rangle\,+\,\lambda^{t-1}\big(\langle{q_{1}^{\star}},{g^{t-1}}\rangle+\langle{q_{2}^{\star}},{h^{t-1}}\rangle-b\big)\\[5.69046pt]

in Lemma

. Let us denote the event in Lemma

\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\langle q_{1}^{\star}\cdot q_{2},r^{t}\rangle\leq\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\langle q_{1}^{\star}\cdot q_{2}^{\star},r^{t}\rangle
for any
q_{2}\in\Delta(P_{2})
satisfying
\left\langle q_{2},h\right\rangle\leq b_{2}
.
With this solution concept, we define the regret for any algorithm that plays the game for
T
episodes by

\text{Regret}(T)\;=\;\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\left(\left\langle q_{1}^{t}\cdot q_{2}^{\star},r^{t}\right\rangle-\left\langle q_{1}^{\star}\cdot q_{2}^{t},r^{t}\right\rangle\right)

(45)

where two players take policies
\pi^{t}
and
\mu^{t}
in episode
t
and they define occupancy measures
q_{1}^{t}
and
q_{2}^{t}
under the true transitions
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
.

To measure the constraint satisfaction, we introduce the violation as a non-negative part of accumulated constraint violations
\langle{q_{1}^{t}},{g}\rangle-b_{1}
and
\langle{q_{2}^{t}},{h}\rangle-b_{2}
over
T
episodes,

\!\!\!\!\text{Violation}_{1}(T)\,=\,\left[\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\left(\left\langle q_{1}^{t},g^{t}\right\rangle-b_{1}\right)\right]_{+}\,\text{ and }\,\text{Violation}_{2}(T)\,=\,\left[\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\left(\left\langle q_{2}^{t},h^{t}\right\rangle-b_{2}\right)\right]_{+}.

(46)

We next make an assumption that guarantees the existence of constrained Nash equilibrium
(
Altman and Shwartz 2000
)
.

Assumption 2

(Feasibility)
.

There exists a joint policy
(\bar{\pi},\bar{\mu})
associated to the occupancy measure
(\bar{q}_{1},\bar{q}_{2})
and
\xi>0
such that
\left\langle\bar{q}_{1},g\right\rangle+\xi\leq b_{1}
and
\left\langle\bar{q}_{2},h\right\rangle+\xi\leq b_{1}
.

14.1
Algorithm and Performance Guarantees

We now are ready to specialize Algorithm
1
to Problem (
44
). The only change is to replace the primal-dual update (
8
) and (
10
) by the following optimistic primal-dual mirror descent step.

Let us recall that the occupancy measures
q_{1}^{t}
for the min-player and
q_{2}^{t}
for the max-player are defined over the true transitions
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
in episode
t
. The primal update of our algorithm maintains two occupancy measures
\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}
,
\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}
to estimate
q_{1}^{t}
,
q_{2}^{t}
, separately. Although
\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}
,
\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}
do not necessarily come from the true transitions
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
, they propose a min-policy
\pi^{t}
for the min-player and a max-policy
\mu^{t}
for the max-player given by (
7
).

We can revise our Lagrangian-based design to update estimates
\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}
and
\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}
as follows.
Assume that the transitions
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are known. We consider a one-episode constrained minimax problem based on reward/utility functions:
r^{t-1}
,
g^{t-1}
,
h^{t-1}
, revealed at the end of episode
t-1
,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\minimize\limits_{q_{1}\,\in\,\Delta(P_{1})}\,\,\maximize\limits_{q_{2}\,\in\,\Delta(P_{2})}&&\!\!\!\!\left\langle q_{1}\cdot q_{2},r^{t-1}\right\rangle\\[8.5359pt]
\subject&&\!\!\!\!\left\langle q_{1},g^{t-1}\right\rangle\;\leq\;b_{1}\;\text{ and }\;\left\langle q_{2},h^{t-1}\right\rangle\;\leq\;b_{2}\end{array}

where
\Delta(P_{1})
and
\Delta(P_{2})
are sets of valid occupancy measures under
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, respectively. We apply the method of Lagrange multipliers
(
Bertsekas 2014
)
to deal with constraints by formulating a generalized Lagrangian-based function,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}L^{t}(q_{1},q_{2};\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})&\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=&\langle{q_{1}\cdot q_{2}},{r^{t-1}}\rangle\,+\,\lambda_{1}\big(\langle{q_{1}},{g^{t-1}}\rangle-b_{1}\big)\,-\,\lambda_{2}\big(\langle{q_{2}},{h^{t-1}}\rangle-b_{2}\big)\end{array}

where
q_{1}
is the first primal variable for the min-player,
q_{2}
is the second primal variable for the max-player, and
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}\geq 0
work as the Lagrange multiplier or the dual variable in penalizing the min-player/max-player via the first/second
\lambda
-term. Once we update
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{1}^{t-1}
and
\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{2}^{t-1}
from the last episode, we reach a constrained saddle-point problem,

\minimize_{q_{1}\,\in\,\Delta(P_{1})}\,\maximize_{q_{2}\,\in\,\Delta(P_{2})}\;\;L^{t}(q_{1},q_{2};\lambda_{1}^{t-1},\lambda_{2}^{t-1}).

However, it is not feasible to take the domains
\Delta(P_{1})
and
\Delta(P_{2})
since the true transitions
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are unknown. Instead, we use their optimistic estimates
\Delta(k_{1}^{t})
and
\Delta(k_{2}^{t})
in sense that
q_{1}^{t}\in\Delta(k_{1}^{t})
and
q_{2}^{t}\in\Delta(k_{2}^{t})
hold with high probability; see Lemma
1
. Denote
\widehat{q}^{\,t}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t},\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t})
. By the linear approximation of
L^{t}(q_{1},q_{2};\lambda^{t-1})
at the previous iterate
(q_{1}^{t-1},q_{2}^{t-1})
, we update the primal variable via an online mirror descent step over the optimistic domains of
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\widehat{q}^{t}\,\leftarrow\,\displaystyle\argmin_{q_{1}\,\in\,\Delta(k_{1}^{t})}\argmax_{q_{2}\,\in\,\Delta(k_{2}^{t})}\Big(V\,\big\langle{q_{1}\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}+\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\cdot q_{2}},{r^{t-1}}\big\rangle\\[5.69046pt]
&&\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\,+\,\lambda_{1}^{t-1}\langle{q_{1}},{g^{t-1}}\rangle\,-\,\lambda_{2}^{t-1}\langle{q_{2}},{h^{t-1}}\rangle\,+\,\displaystyle\eta^{-1}D\big(q\,|\,\widetilde{q}^{\,t-1}\big)\Big)\end{array}

(47)

where
V
,
\eta>0
are some regularization parameters,
D(\cdot\,|\,\cdot)
is the unnormalized Kullback-Leibler divergence with a slightly abuse in a way that
D(q\,|\,q^{\prime})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=D(q_{1}\,|\,q_{1}^{\prime})-D(q_{2}\,|\,q_{2}^{\prime})
,
\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}
and
\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}
are mixing policies given by (
9
).
The unnormalized Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between two distributions
p
,
q
is defined by
D(p\,|\,q)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{i}p_{i}\ln\frac{p_{i}}{q_{i}}-\sum_{i}(p_{i}-q_{i})
. Moreover, (
47
) has an efficient update that is similar as the one in Appendix
8
.

Once we obtain
\widehat{q}^{\,t}
, we next perform the dual update. We treat two
\lambda
-related regularization terms in
L^{t}(\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t},\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t};\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
, separately. The dual update works for each player in the usual way by adding up all past constraint violations,

\lambda_{1}^{t}\;=\;\max\big(\lambda_{1}^{t-1}\,+\,(\langle{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}},{g^{t-1}}\rangle-b_{1}\,),\,0\big)\;\,\text{ and }\;\,\lambda_{2}^{t}\;=\;\max\big(\lambda_{2}^{t-1}\,+\,(\langle{\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}},{h^{t-1}}\rangle-b_{2}\,),\,0\big).

(48)

The dual update (
48
) increases
\lambda_{1}^{t-1}
when
\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}
violates the approximate constraint
\langle{q_{1}},{g^{t-1}}\rangle\leq b_{1}
; it is similar for
\lambda_{2}^{t-1}
. Once we replace the primal-dual update (
8
) and (
10
) in line 4 of Algorithm
1
by (
47
) and (
48
), we obtain a new version of Algorithm
1
for Problem (
44
).

Similar to Theorem
2
, we have the following bounds on the regret and the constraint violation.

Theorem 16

(Regret Bound and Constraint Violation)
.

Let Assumption
2
hold.
Fix
p\in\left(0,1\right)
and
T\geq\max(|X||A|,|B||Y|)
.
In Algorithm
1
with the primal-dual update (

) and (

), we set

,
\eta=1/(TL)
, and
\theta=1/T

Then, the regret (

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\text{Regret}(T)&\leq&\widetilde{O}\big((|X|+|Y|)L\sqrt{T(|A|+|B|)}\big)\\[5.69046pt]
\text{Violation}_{1}(T),\text{Violation}_{2}(T)&\leq&\widetilde{O}\big((|X|+|Y|)L\sqrt{T(|A|+|B|)}\big)\end{array}

with probability

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Delta_{1}^{t}\,+\,\Delta_{2}^{t}&\leq&V\,\big\langle{q_{1}^{\star}\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\cdot q_{2}^{\star}},{r^{t-1}}\big\rangle\,+\,\lambda_{1}^{t-1}\big(\langle{q_{1}^{\star}},{g^{t-1}}\rangle-b_{1}\big)\,+\,\lambda_{2}^{t-1}\big(\langle{q_{2}^{\star}},{h^{t-1}}\rangle-b_{2}\big)\\[5.69046pt]

t
that

\left\|{\lambda^{t}}\right\|\;\leq\;\displaystyle\Theta+2t_{0}L+t_{0}\frac{64L^{2}}{\xi}\log\left(\frac{128L^{2}}{\xi}\right)+t_{0}\frac{64L^{2}}{\xi}\log\frac{1}{\delta}

or, equivalently,

P\left(\left\|{\lambda^{t}}\right\|\;\geq\;\displaystyle\Theta+2t_{0}L+t_{0}\frac{64L^{2}}{\xi}\log\left(\frac{128L^{2}}{\xi}\right)+t_{0}\frac{64L^{2}}{\xi}\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\;\leq\;\delta.

If we take

c\;=\;2\Theta L+4t_{0}L^{2}+t_{0}\frac{128L^{3}}{\xi}\log\left(\frac{128L^{2}}{\xi}\right)+t_{0}\frac{128L^{3}}{\xi}\log\frac{1}{\delta}\;\text{ and }\;z\;=\;\sqrt{2Tc^{2}\log(1/(\delta T))}

then (
57
) becomes

P\left(\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\left(\lambda_{1}^{t}\big(\langle{q_{1}^{\star}},{g^{t}}\rangle-b_{1}\big)+\lambda_{2}^{t}\big(\langle{q_{2}^{\star}},{h^{t}}\rangle-b_{2}\big)\right)\geq z\right)\;\leq\;2\delta T

which proves the desired result.
∎

We now ready to conclude a bound on
{\widehat{\text{Regret}}(T)}
by combining Lemma
19
and Lemma
17
.

Theorem 20
.

Let Assumption
2
hold. Fix
T\geq\max(|X||A|,|B||Y|)
. Let
V=L\sqrt{T}
,
\eta=1/(TL)
,
t_{0}=\sqrt{T}
, and
\theta=1/T
. Then,
with probability
1-2T\delta
it holds that

{\widehat{\text{Regret}}(T)}\;\leq\;\widetilde{O}\big({(|X|+|Y|)L\sqrt{T}}\big).

Proof.

Using the given parameters
V
,
\eta
,
t_{0}
, and
\theta
for Lemma
17
,
{\widehat{\text{Regret}}(T)}
is upper bounded by

\frac{1}{L\sqrt{T}}\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\left(\lambda_{1}^{t}\big(\langle{q_{1}^{\star}},{g^{t}}\rangle-b_{1}\big)+\lambda_{2}^{t}\big(\langle{q_{2}^{\star}},{h^{t}}\rangle-b_{2}\big)\right)+\widetilde{O}(L\sqrt{T})

with probability
1-\delta
.
We note that
\Theta\leq\widetilde{O}(L^{2}\sqrt{T})
and
T\geq\max(|X||A|,|B||Y|)
. Using parameters in Lemma
19
, with probability
1-2T\delta
,

\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\left(\lambda_{1}^{t}\big(\langle{q_{1}^{\star}},{g^{t}}\rangle-b_{1}\big)+\lambda_{2}^{t}\big(\langle{q_{2}^{\star}},{h^{t}}\rangle-b_{2}\big)\right)\;\leq\;\widetilde{O}(L^{3}T).

We complete the proof by noting
L\leq|X|+|Y|
.
∎

We conclude the regret bound in Theorem
16
by combining Lemma
4
and Theorem
20
, and
\delta=p/(2T)
.

14.3
Constraint Violation Analysis

We begin with a decomposition using the auxiliary occupancy measures
(q_{1}^{t},q_{2}^{t})
. By inserting
\langle\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t},g^{t}\rangle
and
\langle\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t},h^{t}\rangle
into
\text{Violation}_{1}(T)
and
\text{Violation}_{2}(T)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\text{Violation}_{1}(T)&=&\underbrace{\left[\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\left(\left\langle\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t},g^{t}\right\rangle-b_{1}\right)\right]_{+}}_{\widehat{\text{Violation}_{1}}(T)}\,+\,\underbrace{\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\left\langle q_{1}^{t}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t},g^{t}\right\rangle}_{\text{Error}_{3}}\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\text{Violation}_{2}(T)&=&\underbrace{\left[\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\left(\left\langle\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t},h^{t}\right\rangle-b_{2}\right)\right]_{+}}_{\widehat{\text{Violation}_{2}}(T)}\,+\,\underbrace{\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\left\langle q_{2}^{t}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t},h^{t}\right\rangle}_{\text{Error}_{4}}.\end{array}

For
{\text{Error}_{3}}
and
{\text{Error}_{4}}
, we have the same bounds in Lemma
9
.
We next bound
{\widehat{\text{Violation}_{1}}(T)}
and
{\widehat{\text{Violation}_{2}}(T)}
by applying the epoch property
(
Jaksch et al. 2010
)
; see a proof in Appendix
13
.

Theorem 21
.

Let
V=L\sqrt{T}
,
\eta=1/(TL)
,
t_{0}=\sqrt{T}
, and
\theta=1/T
. Then,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\widehat{\text{Violation}_{1}}(T)},{\widehat{\text{Violation}_{2}}(T)}&\leq&\displaystyle\left\|{\lambda^{T}}\right\|\,+\,\frac{2}{T-1}\sum_{t\,=\,1}^{T}\left\|{\lambda^{t-1}}\right\|\,+\,\widetilde{O}\big(L\sqrt{T(|X||A|+|Y||B|)}\big).\end{array}

Proof.

By the dual update (
48
),

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\lambda_{1}^{t}&=&\max\!\Big(\lambda_{1}^{t-1}\,+\,\big(\left\langle\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t},g^{t-1}\right\rangle-b_{1}\,\big),\;0\Big)\\[5.69046pt]
&\geq&\lambda_{1}^{t-1}\,+\,\big(\left\langle\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t},g^{t-1}\right\rangle-b_{1}\,\big)\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\lambda_{1}^{t-1}\,+\,\big(\left\langle\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1},g^{t-1}\right\rangle-b_{1}\,\big)\,+\,\left\langle\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1},g^{t-1}\right\rangle\\[5.69046pt]
&\geq&\lambda_{1}^{t-1}\,+\,\big(\left\langle\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1},g^{t-1}\right\rangle-b_{1}\,\big)\,-\,\left\|{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}\end{array}

(58a)

where the last inequality is due to:
\langle{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}},{g^{t-1}}\rangle\leq\|\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\|_{1}\|g^{t-1}\|_{\infty}
,
and
\|g^{t-1}\|_{\infty}\in[0,1]
. Similarly,

\lambda_{2}^{t}\;\geq\;\lambda_{2}^{t-1}\,+\,\big(\left\langle\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1},h^{t-1}\right\rangle-b_{2}\,\big)\,-\,\left\|{\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}.

(58b)

We note that
\lambda_{1}^{0}=\lambda_{2}^{0}=0
from the initialization. Summing up both sides of (
58a
) from
t=1
to
t=T
leads to

\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\big(\left\langle\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t},g^{t}\right\rangle-b_{1}\,\big)\;\leq\;\lambda_{1}^{T}\,+\,\sum_{t\,=\,1}^{T}\left\|{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}.

(59a)

Similarly,

\sum_{t\,=\,0}^{T-1}\big(\left\langle\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t},h^{t}\right\rangle-b_{2}\,\big)\;\leq\;\lambda_{2}^{T}\,+\,\sum_{t\,=\,1}^{T}\left\|{\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}.

(59b)

Hence,

{\widehat{\text{Violation}_{1}}(T)},{\widehat{\text{Violation}_{2}}(T)}\;\leq\;\left\|{\lambda^{T}}\right\|\,+\,\sum_{t\,=\,1}^{T}\left(\left\|{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}+\left\|{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}\right).

(60)

We recall
\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}\in\Delta(k_{1}^{t})
,
\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}\in\Delta(k_{2}^{t})
in the primal update (
47
) and
\Delta(k_{1}^{t})
and
\Delta(k_{2}^{t})
in the confidence sets (
11
).
To bound
\left\|{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}+\left\|{\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}
, we consider two cases: (i)
k_{1}^{t}=k_{1}^{t-1}
and
k_{2}^{t}=k_{2}^{t-1}
; (ii) either
k_{1}^{t}\neq k_{1}^{t-1}
or
k_{2}^{t}\neq k_{2}^{t-1}
.

Case (i)
. In this case, we have:
\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}
,
\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\in\Delta(k_{1}^{t})
,
\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}
,
\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}\in\Delta(k_{2}^{t})
.
We begin with the primal update (
8
) and apply Lemma
13
with,

f(x,y)|_{x\,=\,q_{1},\,y\,=\,q_{2}}\;=\;V\,\big\langle{q_{1}\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}+\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\cdot q_{2}},{r^{t-1}}\big\rangle\,+\,\lambda_{1}^{t-1}\langle{q_{1}},{g^{t-1}}\rangle\,-\,\lambda_{2}^{t-1}\langle{q_{2}},{h^{t-1}}\rangle

and
x^{\star}=\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}
,
y^{\star}=\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}
,
x^{\prime}=\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}
,
y^{\prime}=\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}
,
x=\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}
, and
y=\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}
. Thus,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!V\,\big\langle{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}+\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\cdot\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}},{r^{t-1}}\big\rangle\,+\,\lambda_{1}^{t-1}\langle{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}},{g^{t-1}}\rangle\,-\,\lambda_{2}^{t-1}\langle{\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}},{h^{t-1}}\rangle\\[5.69046pt]
&&\,+\,\eta^{-1}\big(D(\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t},\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1})+D(\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t},\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1})\big)\\[5.69046pt]
&\leq&V\,\big\langle{\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}+\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}},{r^{t-1}}\big\rangle\,+\,\lambda_{1}^{t-1}\langle{\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}},{g^{t-1}}\rangle\,-\,\lambda_{2}^{t-1}\langle{\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}},{h^{t-1}}\rangle\\[5.69046pt]
&&\,-\,\eta^{-1}\big(D(\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1},\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t})+D(\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1},\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t})\big).\end{array}

or, equivalently,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\eta^{-1}\big(D(\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t},\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1})+D(\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t},\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1})\big)\,+\,\eta^{-1}\big(D(\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1},\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t})+D(\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1},\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t})\big)\\[5.69046pt]
&\leq&V\,\big\langle{(\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t})\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}+\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\cdot(\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}-\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1})},{r^{t-1}}\big\rangle\\[5.69046pt]
&&\,+\,\lambda_{1}^{t-1}\langle{\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}},{g^{t-1}}\rangle\,+\,\lambda_{2}^{t-1}\langle{\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}},{h^{t-1}}\rangle.\end{array}

(61)

We note that
\langle{(\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t})\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}},{r^{t-1}}\rangle\leq\|(\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t})\cdot\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}\|_{1}\|{r^{t-1}}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}\|_{1}
, and, similarly,
\langle\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}\cdot(\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}-\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}),{r^{t-1}}\rangle\leq\|\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}-\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}\|_{1}
. Thus, we can reduce (
61
) into

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\eta^{-1}\big(D(\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t},\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1})+D(\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t},\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1})\big)\,+\,\eta^{-1}\big(D(\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1},\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t})+D(\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1},\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t})\big)\\[5.69046pt]
&\leq&(V+\lambda_{1}^{t-1})\left\|{\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}}\right\|_{1}\,+\,(V+\lambda_{2}^{t-1})\left\|{\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}}\right\|_{1}\\[5.69046pt]
&\leq&(V+\left\|{\lambda^{t-1}}\right\|)\left(\left\|{\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}}\right\|_{1}+\left\|{\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}}\right\|_{1}\right)\end{array}

where the left-hand side can be lower bounded by Lemma
14
,

D(\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t},\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1})+D(\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1},\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t})\;\geq\;L^{-1}\left\|{\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}}\right\|_{1}^{2}

D(\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t},\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1})+D(\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1},\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t})\;\geq\;L^{-1}\left\|{\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}}\right\|_{1}^{2}.

Then, we apply the inequality
(x+y)^{2}\leq 2(x^{2}+y^{2})
and cancel a non-negative term to obtain

\left\|{\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}}\right\|_{1}+\left\|{\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}}\right\|_{1}\;\leq\;2\eta L(V+\left\|{\lambda^{t-1}}\right\|).

(62)

By the definition of
\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}
and
\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}
,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\left\|{\widetilde{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}}\right\|_{1}&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}\left|(1-\theta)\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}(x,a)+\theta\frac{1}{|X_{\ell}||A|}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}(x,a)\right|\\[5.69046pt]
&\geq&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\,=\,0}^{L-1}\sum_{x\,\in\,X_{\ell}}\sum_{a\,\in\,A}\left((1-\theta)\left|\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}(x,a)-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}(x,a)\right|-\theta\left(\frac{1}{|X_{\ell}||A|}+\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}(x,a)\right)\right)\\[5.69046pt]
&=&(1-\theta)\left\|{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}}\right\|_{1}-2\theta L.\end{array}

Similarly, we have
\|{\widetilde{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}}\|_{1}\leq(1-\theta)\|{\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}}\|_{1}-2\theta L
. Thus, we can further reduce (
62
) into

\left\|{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}}\right\|_{1}+\|{\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}}\|_{1}\;\leq\;2\eta(1-\theta)^{-1}L(V+\left\|{\lambda^{t-1}}\right\|)+4\theta(1-\theta)^{-1}L.

(63)

Case (ii)
. In this case, either
\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}
,
\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}
or
\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}
,
\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}
might not have the same domain. For instance, when
k_{1}^{t}>k_{1}^{t-1}
, it is possible that
\Delta(k_{1}^{t})
becomes different from
\Delta(k_{1}^{t-1})
. We note that
k_{1}^{t}>k_{1}^{t-1}
only happens when episode
t
is the first one that belongs to epoch
k_{1}^{t}
. By Lemma
25
,
k_{1}^{T}\leq\sqrt{T|X||A|}\log(8T/(|X||A|))
and
k_{2}^{T}\leq\sqrt{T|Y||B|}\log(8T/(|Y||B|))
if we are given
T\geq\max(|X||A|,|Y||B|)
.

We now combine two cases above for (
60
),

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\displaystyle\sum_{t\,=\,1}^{T}\left(\left\|{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}+\left\|{\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}\right)\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\,\leq\,t\,\leq\,T\\
k_{1}^{t}\,=\,k_{1}^{k-1}\,\wedge\,k_{2}^{t}\,=\,k_{2}^{k-1}\end{subarray}}\left(\left\|{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}+\left\|{\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}\right)\\[5.69046pt]
&&\displaystyle\,+\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\,\leq\,t\,\leq\,T\\
k_{1}^{t}\,=\,k_{1}^{k-1}\,\vee\,k_{2}^{t}\,=\,k_{2}^{k-1}\end{subarray}}\left(\left\|{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}+\left\|{\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}\right)\\[5.69046pt]
&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\,\leq\,t\,\leq\,T\\
k_{1}^{t}\,=\,k_{1}^{k-1}\,\wedge\,k_{2}^{t}\,=\,k_{2}^{k-1}\end{subarray}}\left(\left\|{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}+\left\|{\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}\right)\,+\,2L(k_{1}^{T}+k_{2}^{T})\\[5.69046pt]
&\leq&\displaystyle 2\eta(1-\theta)^{-1}L\sum_{t\,=\,1}^{T}(V+\left\|{\lambda^{t-1}}\right\|)+4\theta(1-\theta)^{-1}LT+2L(k_{1}^{T}+k_{2}^{T})\end{array}

where the first inequality is due to:
\left\|{\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{1}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}\leq 2L
and
\left\|{\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t}-\widehat{q}_{2}^{\,t-1}}\right\|_{1}\leq 2L
, and we apply (
63
) from the case (i) for the last inequality. Using the bounds on
k_{1}^{T}
,
k_{2}^{T}
in the case (ii), we conclude the desired bound for (
60
),

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\displaystyle{\widehat{\text{Violation}_{1}}(T)},{\widehat{\text{Violation}_{2}}(T)}\\[5.69046pt]
&\leq&\displaystyle\left\|{\lambda^{T}}\right\|\,+\,\frac{2\eta L}{1-\theta}\sum_{t\,=\,1}^{T}\left\|{\lambda^{t-1}}\right\|+\frac{2\eta V+4\theta}{1-\theta}LT\\[5.69046pt]
&&\displaystyle+2L\left(\sqrt{T|X||A|}\log(8T/(|X||A|))+\sqrt{T|Y||B|}\log(8T/(|Y||B|))\right).\end{array}

We complete the proof by noting
\lambda_{1}^{0}=\lambda_{2}^{0}=0
,
V=L\sqrt{T}
,
\eta=1/(TL)
, and
\theta=1/T
.
∎

to Theorem

(

The following two lemmas are useful in the epoch analysis.

Lemma 24
.

(
Jaksch et al. 2010
)
Let a sequence of positive numbers be
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
. Assume that
0\leq x_{k}\leq X_{k-1}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\max(1,\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{k-1}x_{i})
for

. Then,

\sum_{k\,=\,1}^{n}\frac{x_{k}}{\sqrt{X_{k-1}}}\;\leq\;(\sqrt{2}+1)\sqrt{X_{n}}.

Lemma 25
.

(
Jaksch et al. 2010
)
Assume that
T\geq\max(|X||A|,|Y||B|)
. Then, the epochs
k_{1}^{T}
and
k_{2}^{T}
for episode
T

k_{1}^{T}\;\leq\;|X||A|\log\left(\frac{8T}{|X||A|}\right)\;\leq\;\sqrt{T|X||A|}\log\left(\frac{8T}{|X||A|}\right)

k_{2}^{T}\;\leq\;|Y||B|\log\left(\frac{8T}{|Y||B|}\right)\;\leq\;\sqrt{T|Y||B|}\log\left(\frac{8T}{|Y||B|}\right).

Variational Analysis of Generalized Games over Banach spaces
Asrifa Sultana, Shivani Valecha

Abstract
We study generalized games defined over Banach spaces using variational analysis. To reformulate generalized games as quasi-variational inequality problems, we will first form a suitable principal operator and study some significant properties of this operator. Then, we deduce the sufficient conditions under which an equilibrium for the generalized game can be obtained by solving a quasi-variational inequality. Based on this variational reformulation, we derive the existence of equilibrium for generalized games with non-ordered (that is, associated weak preference relations need not be complete and transitive) and mid-point continuous preference maps.



Keywords:

Nash equilibrium problem; quasi-variational inequality; binary relations; non-ordered preference; generalized game

49J40, 49J53, 91B06, 91B42

(1)

Then, any vector

A preference relation of any individual is a binary relation which represents his choice in the set
of available options. The preference relation of any individual satisfy the completeness and transitivity property if he is able to compare any pair of available options. Any complete, transitive and continuous preference relation defined on connected and separable topological space admits a numerical representation (see
[

). Hence, one can obtain the above well known version of GNEP with real-valued objective functions if preferences of players satisfy these properties. It is worth mentioning that for numerical representation of preference relations the completeness and transitivity properties are necessary (see

[

[
17

to study generalized games in which players’ preference maps are non-ordered, that is, there are no restrictions like completeness and transitivity of players’ preference relations. Thereafter, Yannelis- Prabhakar
[

[

are non-ordered preference and feasible strategy maps of player

indicates the set of equilibrium points for generalized game

\bar{x}_{\nu}\in K_{\nu}(\bar{x})~\text{and}~P_{\nu}(\bar{x})\cap K_{\nu}(\bar{x})=\emptyset~\text{for every}~\nu\in\Lambda.

(3)

Recently, the authors studied economic equilibrium problems with complete, transitive and continuous preference relations in
[
15
]
using tools of variational analysis. Thereafter, the maximization problem for incomplete and non-transitive preferences has been studied in
[
16
,

]
by reformulating it as a variational inequality problem. In these articles, the principal operator of associated variational inequality is formed by using normal cones to preferred sets. This motivated Beuno et. al.
[

to study existence of equilibrium for generalized games by employing variational approach. Further, Sultana-Valecha deduced the existence of equilibrium and projected solutions for generalized games with not necessarily bounded strategy maps

Our aim is to study the generalized game

Then, we deduce the sufficient conditions under which an equilibrium for the generalized game can be obtained by solving an associated quasi-variational inequality. Based on this variational reformulation, we derive the existence of equilibrium for the proposed generalized games.

Let us recall the classical definitions of variational and quasi-variational inequality problems
[
13
]

\text{there exists}~\bar{y}^{*}\in T(\bar{y})~\text{satisfying}~\langle\bar{y}^{*},y-\bar{y}\rangle\geq 0,~\text{for all}~y\in K(\bar{y}).

If the constraint map

Let

be a Banach space with topological dual

. A preference relation on

\succeq
. As per

-

-

-

-

and
\{z\in X\,|\,y\succeq z\}
are closed for any

;

Any preference relation
\succeq

induces a strict preference relation

as follows: For any pair

, it is known that

y\succeq z

. A vector

admits a numerical representation (see

\succeq

However, the completeness and transitivity of

]
). Hence, we consider a non-ordered preference map

P(x)=\{y\in X|\,y\succeq x~\text{and}~x\nsucceq y\}=\{y\in X|\,y\succ x\}.

(4)

Now the notion of maximal elements can be given as (see

.

Mid-point continuity for Preference Maps

We define the concept of mid-point continuity for non-ordered preference maps motivated by
[
12
]
, which generalizes the concept of continuity for preference relations studied in
[
16
, Definition 2]
:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. A preference map
P\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\rightrightarrows X
fulfils,

-

lower mid-point continuity at any
x\in X
if for any
w\in{P}(x)
, there exists
t\in[0,1)
and open set
V
in
X
such that
tx+(1-t)w\in V
and
V\subset{P}(x)
;

-

upper mid-point continuity at any
x\in X
if for any
w\in P(x)
, there exists
t\in[0,1)
and open set
W
in
X
such that
tx+(1-t)w\in{P}(x^{\prime})
for each
x^{\prime}\in W
;

-

mid-point continuity
[
12
, Definition 1]
at any
x\in X
if it is upper and lower mid-point continuous at
x
, that is, for any
w\in{P}(x)
, there exists
t\in[0,1)
and open sets
V
and
W
in
X
such that

\displaystyle tx+(1-t)w\in V,x\in W~\text{and}~w^{\prime}\in{P}(x^{\prime})~\text{for all}~(w^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in V\times W.

Let
Y
be a Banach space and
(x,y)\in X\times Y
be arbitrary. We say
P\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\times Y\rightrightarrows X
is lower mid-point continuous w.r.t.

at
(x,y)
if for any

, there exists
t\in[0,1)
and open set

in

such that
tx+(1-t)w\in V
and

. Furthermore, we say
P
is upper mid-point continuous w.r.t.

at
(x,y)
if for any

there exists
t\in[0,1)
and open sets

in
X
and

in

such that
tx+(1-t)w\in{P}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
for each
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in W\times O
. Finally, we say

.

In

Example 3.1

Suppose

is defined on

as

u(x)=\begin{cases}x,~&\text{if}~x<\frac{1}{2}\\
2-x,~&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(5)

Then, clearly

but there no open set

such that

. Hence,

is not lower semi-continuous. But it is easy to observe that the corresponding preference map

\displaystyle P(x)=\begin{cases}(x,1],&~\text{if}~x\in[0,\frac{1}{2})\\

.

The generalized games are studied in finite dimensional spaces through variational reformulation in
[

]
under the assumption that preference maps are lower semi-continuous with open convex values. Furthermore, the maximization of preference maps is studied in finite dimensional spaces through variational reformulation in
[

under the lower semi-continuity and openness like assumptions on preference maps. Following result along with Example

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose

is non-empty. A map
P\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Z\rightrightarrows Z
is:

(a)

lower mid-point continuous at

if

is open in

;

(b)

lower mid-point continuous at

if

is convex and any

is an internal point with respect to

;

(c)

upper mid-point continuous at

if it is l.s.c. with convex and open values in

;

(d)

upper mid-point continuous at

if

is l.s.c. at

,

is convex and any

is an internal point with respect to

.

Proof.

To prove (a), suppose

is open valued in

. Then, for any

the lower mid-point continuity holds by assuming

. Further, we notice that

is open if it satisfies the assumptions given in (b) (see

follows by (a). For (c), we assume

is l.s.c. with convex and open values in

. Then, for any

[

and assuming

. Again, the proof of (d) follows from (c) and

∎

Remark 3.1
.

We observe that the converse of the statements in Proposition

need not be true. In fact, it is clear from Example

that

is lower mid-point continuous but it does not admit open values in

is nor lower semi-continuous neither it admits open values but it is upper mid-point continuous for any

Example 3.2

Suppose

is defined as,

\displaystyle P(x)=\begin{cases}(x,1],&~\text{if}~x\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]\\

Clearly,

Normal Cone Operators for Preference Maps

In this section, we derive some important properties of normal cone operators corresponding to non-ordered preference map, which will be later employed to study preference maximization problem and generalized games through variational reformulation.

According to
[
4
]
, the normal cone of the set
C
at some point
x\in X
is

\displaystyle N_{C}(x)=\begin{cases}\{x^{*}\in X^{*}|\,\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle\leq 0~\text{for all}~y\in C\},&\text{if}~C\neq\emptyset\\
X^{*},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(6)

For Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, consider a set-valued map
P\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\times Y\rightrightarrows X
. We define a normal cone operator
N_{P}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\times Y\rightrightarrows X^{*}
as

N_{P}(x,y)=N_{P(x,y)}(x)=(P(x,y)-\{x\})^{\circ},
that is,

\displaystyle N_{P}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\{x^{*}|\,\langle x^{*},z-x\rangle\leq 0~\forall\,z\in P(x,y)\},&\text{if}~{P}(x,y)\neq\emptyset\\
X^{*},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(7)

We derive some properties related to normal cone operators defined as (
7
).

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
N_{P}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\times Y\rightrightarrows X^{*}
is defined as (
7
). If the map
P
is convex valued and lower mid-point continuous w.r.t.
x
at
(x,y)
and
x\notin P(x,y)
then
(N_{P}(x,y)\setminus\{0\})\neq\emptyset
for any
(x,y)\in X\times Y
.

Proof.

In the case
P(x,y)=\emptyset
then we have
N_{P}(x,y)=X^{*}
and
N_{P}(x,y)\setminus\{0\}\neq\emptyset
. Suppose
P(x,y)\neq\emptyset
and
w\in P(x,y)
. Then, by lower mid-point continuity of
P
there exists
t\in[0,1)
and open set
V
such that
tx+(1-t)w\in V\subset P(x,y)
. This shows
P(x,y)
contains an interior point
tx+(1-t)w
. As
x\notin P(x,y)
, we obtain
0\neq x^{*}\in X^{*}
by virtue of
[
1
, Theorem 5.67]
such that

\sup_{w\in P(x)}\langle x^{*},w\rangle\leq\langle x^{*},x\rangle.

(8)

This proves
N_{P}(x,y)\setminus\{0\}\neq\emptyset
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose
N_{P}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\times Y\rightrightarrows X^{*}
is defined as (
7
). If
P
is lower mid-point continuous w.r.t.
x
at
(x,y)
and

N_{P}(x,y)\cap-N_{P}(x,y)=\{0\}.

(9)

Proof.

We observe that,

{N}_{P}(x,y)\cap-{N}_{P}(x,y)=(P(x,y)-\{x\})^{\perp}=\{x^{*}|\,\langle x^{*},w-x\rangle=0,~\forall~w\in P(x,y)\}.

Suppose

, then we have
t\in[0,1)
and open set
V
such that
tx+(1-t)w\in V\subset P(x,y)
by lower mid-point continuity. This implies
int(P(x,y)-\{x\})\neq\emptyset
. Thus,
(P(x,y)-\{x\})^{\perp}=\{0\}
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose

at
(x,y)
. For

∗

∗

Consider a net

such that

converges to

with respect to norm topology and

converges to

with respect to weak

. Since

weak
∗
converges to

and

(10)

As we know

\langle x_{\beta}^{*},z-x_{\beta}\rangle\leq 0~\text{for all}~z\in P(x_{\beta},y_{\beta}).

(11)

To prove (

), we assume

is arbitrary. Since

Proposition 4.4

Suppose

F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\times Y\rightrightarrows X^{*}
with non-empty convex weak
∗
compact values such that
F(x,y)\subseteq N_{P}(x,y)\setminus\{0\}
whenever
P(x,y)\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Suppose
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in(X\times Y)\setminus E
is arbitrary, where
E=\{(x,y)\in X\times Y|\,P(x,y)=\emptyset\}
. We aim to find an open set
O_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}
in
(X\times Y)\setminus E
containing
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and a map
T_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}O_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\rightrightarrows X^{*}
such that
T_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}(x,y)\subseteq N_{P}(x,y)\setminus\{0\}
for all
(x,y)\in O_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}
. Suppose
\bar{w}\in P(\bar{x},\bar{y})
then by mid-point continuity of
P
w.r.t.
\bar{x}
there exists
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}>0
and
t=t_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\in[0,1)
, such that

t\bar{x}+(1-t)\bar{w}+\epsilon_{1}B_{X}\subset P(x,y)~\text{for each}~(x,y)\in(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\epsilon_{2}(B_{X}\times B_{Y}).

(13)

Suppose
2\epsilon=\min\{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}\}
then we have

t\bar{x}+(1-t)\bar{w}+2\epsilon B_{X}\subset P(x,y)~\text{for each}~(x,y)\in(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\epsilon(B_{X}\times B_{Y}).

(14)

For any
(x,y)\in(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\epsilon(B_{X}\times B_{Y})
and
x^{*}\in N_{P}(x,y)=\{x^{*}|\,\langle x^{*},w-x\rangle\leq 0,~\text{for any}~w\in P(x,y)\}
we have,

\langle x^{*},t\bar{x}+(1-t)\bar{w}+2\epsilon u-x\rangle\leq 0~\text{for all}~u\in B_{X}.

(15)

This further implies,

\displaystyle 2\epsilon\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x^{*}\right\rVert}_{*}

\displaystyle=2\epsilon\sup_{u\in B_{X}}\langle x^{*},u\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle x^{*},x-(t_{z}\bar{x}+(1-t_{z})w_{z})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle+(1-t_{z})\langle x^{*},\bar{x}-\bar{w}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x^{*}\right\rVert}+(1-t_{z})\langle x^{*},\bar{x}-\bar{w}\rangle.

Finally, we have
\epsilon\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x^{*}\right\rVert}_{*}\leq(1-t)\langle x^{*},\bar{x}-\bar{w}\rangle
.
Let us assume,

H_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}=\{x^{*}\in X^{*}|\,(1-t)\langle x^{*},\bar{x}-\bar{w}\rangle=\epsilon\}.

Clearly,
N_{P}(x,y)\cap H_{z}\subset N_{P}(x,y)\setminus\{0\}
is weak
∗
compact as it is contained in
B_{X}^{*}
and non-empty as per Proposition
4.1
. Suppose
O_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\epsilon(B_{X}\times B_{Y})
and define
T_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}O_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\rightrightarrows X^{*}
by,

T_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}(x,y)=N_{P}(x,y)\cap H_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}~\text{for any}~(x,y)\in O_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}.

(16)

Now, we construct a map
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\times Y\rightrightarrows X^{*}
as convex combination of the maps like
T_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}
by employing a partition of unity technique. We observe that,

\mathcal{O}=\{O_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={(\bar{x},\bar{y})}+\epsilon(B_{X}\times B_{Y})|\,{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\in(X\times Y)\setminus E\}

forms an open cover for
(X\times Y)\setminus E
. Since
X\times Y
is paracompact, there exists a locally finite open refinement
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\Lambda}
for
\mathcal{O}
such that for each
\nu\in\Lambda
there is
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in X\times Y
satisfying
U_{\nu}\subset O_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}
. Further, we obtain a family of continuous functions
\{f_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\Lambda}
which forms a partition of unity corresponding to
\mathcal{U}
as per
[
1
, Theorem 2.90]
. Then,
f_{\nu}(x,y)=0
if
(x,y)\notin U_{\nu}
and
\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda}f_{\nu}(x,y)=1
for any
(x,y)\in X\times Y
. Further, the set
\lambda(x,y)=\{\nu\in\Lambda|\,f_{\nu}(x,y)>0\}
is non-empty finite. Since for each
\nu\in\Lambda
there exists
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in X
satisfying
U_{\nu}\subset O_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}
, we define
T_{\nu}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}U_{\nu}\rightrightarrows X^{*}
by
T_{\nu}(x,y)=T_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})}(x,y)
.

Define
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(X\times Y)\setminus E\rightrightarrows X^{*}
as
T(x,y)=\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda(x,y)}f_{\nu}(x,y)T_{\nu}(x,y)
. We claim that
T(x,y)\subset N_{P}(x,y)\setminus\{0\}
.On contrary, suppose
0\in T(x,y)
. Then, there exists
x_{\nu}^{*}\in T_{\nu}(x,y)\subset N_{P}(x,y)
for all
\nu\in\Lambda(x,y)
such that

0=\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda(x,y)}f_{\nu}(x,y)x_{\nu}^{*}
. Since
f_{\nu}(x,y)>0
for all
\nu\in\Lambda(x,y)
, we have

-x_{\nu}^{*}=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda(x,y)\setminus\{\nu\}}\frac{f_{\mu}(x,y)}{f_{\nu}(x,y)}x_{\mu}^{*}\in N_{P}(x,y).

In the view of Proposition
4.2
, we have
x_{\nu}^{*}=\{0\}
. But, this contradicts the fact that
x_{\nu}^{*}\in H_{z}
. This proves
T(x,y)\subset N_{P}(x,y)\setminus\{0\}
.

We claim that
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\times Y\rightrightarrows X^{*}
constructed as,

F(x,y)=\begin{cases}B_{X}^{*},&~\text{if}~P(x,y)=\emptyset\\
T(x,y),&~\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(17)

is norm-weak
∗
upper semi-continuous. Since
F(x,y)\subseteq B_{X}^{*}
, it is sufficient to prove that
F
is norm-weak
∗
closed. Consider a net
(x_{\beta},y_{\beta},x_{\beta}^{*})_{\beta}\subseteq Gr(F)
such that
(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})_{\beta}
converges to
(x,y)
with respect to norm topology and
x_{\beta}^{*}
converges to
x^{*}
with respect to weak
∗
topology. We aim to show that
x^{*}\in F(x,y)
. If
(x,y)\in E
, we observe that
x^{*}\in F(x,y)=B_{X}^{*}
as
x_{\beta}^{*}\in F(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})\subset B_{X}^{*}
and
B_{X}^{*}
is weak
∗
closed. Suppose
(x,y)\in(X\times Y)\setminus E
. We notice that
E
is closed set due to mid-point continuity of
P
. Hence, it is not restrictive to consider
(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})_{\beta}\subset(X\times Y)\setminus E
. Since
(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})_{\beta}
converges to
(x,y)
, we can assume without loss of generality that
\Lambda(x,y)\subseteq\Lambda(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})
for each
\beta
. Hence, it appears,

T(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})=\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})\setminus\Lambda(x,y)}f_{\nu}(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})T_{\nu}(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})+\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda(x,y)}f_{\nu}(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})T_{\nu}(x_{\beta},y_{\beta}).

Since
(x^{*}_{\beta})_{\beta}
weak
∗
converges to
x^{*}
, we have nets
(x^{*}_{\nu,\beta})_{\beta}

∗

x^{*}_{\nu}
for each
\nu\in\Lambda(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})
such that,

x^{*}_{\beta}=\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})\setminus\Lambda(x,y)}f_{\nu}(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})x^{*}_{\nu,\beta}+\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda(x,y)}f_{\nu}(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})x^{*}_{\nu,\beta}.

(18)

Since
\{f_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\Lambda}
forms a continuous family of functions we have,

\lim_{\beta}\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})\setminus\Lambda(x,y)}f_{\nu}(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})=1-\lim_{\beta}\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda(x,y)}f_{\nu}(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})=0.

This further implies first term in (
18
) weak
∗
converges to
0
as,

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})\setminus\Lambda(x,y)}f_{\nu}(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})x^{*}_{\nu,\beta}\right\rVert}_{*}\leq\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})\setminus\Lambda(x,y)}f_{\nu}(x_{\beta},y_{\beta}).

In the view of Proposition
4.3
, we observe that each
T_{\nu}
is norm-weak
∗
closed map and
x^{*}_{\nu}\in T_{\nu}(x,y)
. Hence, by taking limits in (

) we have,

x^{*}=\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda(x,y)}f_{\nu}(x,y)x^{*}_{\nu}\in T(x,y).

This proves the map

defined as (

) is norm-weak
∗
u.s.c. with non-empty convex weak
∗
compact values such that
F(x,y)\subseteq N_{P}(x,y)
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

In the case

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose
P
is convex valued lower mid-point continuous map w.r.t.
x
at
(x,y)
. If
x^{*}\in N_{P}(x,y)
and
w\in P(x,y)
satisfy
\langle x^{*},w-x\rangle\geq 0
then
x^{*}=0
.

Proof.

Let
C
be non-empty subset of
X
. We claim that if
y\in int(C)
and
y^{*}\in C^{\circ}
satisfy
\langle y^{*},y\rangle\geq 0
then
y^{*}=0
. Since
y\in int(C)
, we have
\epsilon>0
such that
y+\epsilon B_{X}\subset C
. Hence,

\langle y^{*},y+\epsilon u\rangle\leq 0~\text{for all}~u\in B_{X}.

(19)

Since
y^{*}\in C^{\circ}
, we have
\langle y^{*},y\rangle=0
. From (
19
), we obtain
\langle y^{*},u\rangle\leq 0~\text{for all}~u\in B_{X}.
Thus,
\langle y^{*},u\rangle=0~\text{for all}~u\in B_{X},
which proves
y^{*}=0
.

Suppose
x^{*}\in N_{P}(x,y)
and
w\in P(x,y)
satisfy
\langle x^{*},w-x\rangle\geq 0
. Considering
w_{t}=tx+(1-t)w
, we have

\langle x^{*},w_{t}-x\rangle=(1-t)\langle x^{*},w-x\rangle\geq 0~\text{for any}~t\in[0,1).

(20)

By lower mid-point continuity, we get
t\in[0,1)
such that
w_{t}\in int(P(x,y))
. In the view of (
20
) and the fact that
w_{t}-x\in int(P(x,y)-\{x\})
and
x^{*}\in(P(x,y)-\{x\})^{\circ}
we get

.
∎

5
Existence Results

5.1
Variational Reformulation of Preference Maximization Problems

Let us reconsider the problem of finding maximal elements for non-ordered preference map
P\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\rightrightarrows X
over
K\subset X
, by following the Definition
2.1
. We show that any solution of variational inequality with principal operator
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
defined as (
17
) is maximal element for
P
over
K
. Note that the results derived in the previous section for normal cone operators can be easily adapted for preference maps
P
which have no dependence on
y\in Y
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
K
be non-empty closed convex subset of Banach space
X
. Suppose
P\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\rightrightarrows X
is convex valued mid-point continuous map and
x\notin P(x)
for any
x\in X
. Then,
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{S}(P,K)
if
\bar{x}\in K
solves
VI(F,K)
where
F
is defined as (
17
).

Proof.

Suppose
\bar{x}\in K
solves
VI(F,K)
, that is

\exists\,\bar{x}^{*}\in F(\bar{x}),\langle\bar{x}^{*},y-\bar{x}\rangle\geq 0~\text{for all}~y\in K.

(21)

If
P(\bar{x})=\emptyset
then
\bar{x}
is maximal element. Suppose
P(\bar{x})\neq\emptyset
, then we aim to show that
P(\bar{x})\cap K=\emptyset
. On contrary, suppose
z\in P(\bar{x})\cap K
. From (
21
), it appears that
\bar{x}^{*}\in F(\bar{x})\subseteq N_{P}(\bar{x})\setminus\{0\}
satisfies,

\langle\bar{x}^{*},z-\bar{x}\rangle\geq 0.

(22)

According to Proposition
4.5
, we have
\bar{x}^{*}=0
. But, we know that
P(\bar{x})\neq\emptyset
and
x^{*}\in F(\bar{x})\subset N_{P}(\bar{x})\setminus\{0\}
by following the arguments in proof of Proposition
4.4
. This shows our assumption
P(\bar{x})\cap K\neq\emptyset
is false and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{S}(P,K)
.
∎

Based on the variational reformulation of preference maximization problems obtained in the above result Theorem
5.1
, we now deduce the sufficient conditions for the existence of maximal elements.

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose
P\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\rightrightarrows X
is convex valued mid-point continuous map satisfying
x\notin P(x)
for any
x\in X
and
K\subset X
is non-empty compact and convex. Then, the set of maximal elements
\mathbb{S}(P,K)\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

In the view of Theorem
5.1
, it is sufficient to show that
VI(F,K)
admits a solution. According to Proposition
4.4
, the map
F
is norm-weak
∗
upper semi-continuous with non-empty convex and weak
∗
compact values. Hence, we see that variational inequality
VI(F,K)
admits a solution
\bar{x}\in K
by assuming the constraint map
K
is a constant for all
x
in
[
7
, Theorem 3.3]
. Finally,
\bar{x}\in K
is required maximal element of
P
over
K
.
∎

5.2
Variational Reformulation of Generalized Games

In this section, we aim to show that any solution of quasi-variational inequality with principal operator
\mathcal{F}
(defined as (
23

By following (
7

N_{P_{\nu}}(x_{\nu},x_{-\nu})=\begin{cases}\{x_{\nu}^{*}\in X_{\nu}^{*}|\,\langle x_{\nu}^{*},y_{\nu}-x_{\nu}\rangle\leq 0~\text{for all}~y_{\nu}\in P_{\nu}(x)\},&\text{if}~P_{\nu}(x)\neq\emptyset\\
X_{\nu}^{*},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In addition to notations in (
1
), let

. Suppose
\mathcal{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\rightrightarrows X^{*}

Theorem 5.3
.

For any
\nu\in\Lambda
, assume that,

(a)

X_{\nu}

(b)

\bar{x}

\mathcal{F}
is defined as (
23
).

Proof.

If
\bar{x}

\exists\,\bar{x}^{*}\in\mathcal{F}(\bar{x}),\langle\bar{x}^{*},y-\bar{x}\rangle\geq 0,~\text{for all}~y\in K(\bar{x}).

\bar{x}\in\mathbb{S}(\Gamma).

P_{\nu}(\bar{x})=\emptyset

. On contrary, suppose

\bar{x}_{\nu}^{*}=0

Based on the variational reformulation of generalized games obtained in the above result Theorem

Theorem 5.4

For any

Abstract
. We compute the involutive knot invariants for pretzel knots of the form P ⁡ ( − 2 , m , n ) P(-2,m,n) for m ≥ n ≥ 3 m\geq n\geq 3 .



1.
Introduction

Heegaard Floer homology is a collection of invariants of three-manifolds and knots and links within them introduced by Ozsváth and Szabó
[
OS04c
,
OS04b
,
OS04a
]
and in the knot case independently by Rasmussen
[
Ras03
]
in the early 2000s. The knot version associates to a knot
K\subseteq S^{3}
a
(\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z})
-filtered,
\mathbb{Z}
-graded chain complex over
\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]
called
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K)
. This chain complex recovers the data of the classical Alexander polynomial
[
OS04a
]
and detects the knot genus
[
OS06
]
and whether the knot is fibred
[
Ghi08
,
Ni07
]
. Furthermore, a plethora of interesting invariants of knot concordance have been extracted from it
[
OS06
,
OS08
,
Ras04
,
Hom14
,
OSS17
]
.

In 2015, Hendricks and Manolescu
[
HM17
]
introduced a refinement of Heegaard Floer homology called involutive Heegaard Floer homology, which incorporates the data of a conjugation symmetry on the Heegaard Floer chain complexes. In the knot case, this takes the form of a skew-filtered automorphism

\iota_{K}\colon\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K)\rightarrow\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K)

which is order four up to filtered chain homotopy. From this additional data, they construct two new concordance invariants
\underline{V\mkern-3.0mu}\mkern 3.0mu_{0}(K)
and
\overline{V}_{0}(K)
, which are analogs of a concordance invariant
V_{0}(K)
from the non-involutive setting
[
OS08
,
Ras04
,
Pet10
]
. These invariants are particularly interesting in that, unlike many concordance invariants from Heegaard Floer homology and the related knot homology theory Khovanov homology, they can take nonzero values on knots of finite concordance order. For example,
\underline{V\mkern-3.0mu}\mkern 3.0mu_{0}(4_{1})=1
; the invariants therefore detect the nonsliceness of the figure eight knot. The authors give combinatorial computations of the involutive concordance invariants for
L
-space knots (which include the torus knots) and thin knots (which include alternating and quasi-alternating knots).

Following a similar strategy to Hendricks and Manolescu’s computation for thin knots
[
HM17
, Section 8]
, in this note we compute the involutive concordance invariants of
P(-2,m,n)
pretzel knots for
m
and
n
odd, and their mirrors. The complexes
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(P(-2,m,n))
associated to these knots were computed by Goda, Matsuda, and Morifuji
[
GMM05
]
. The reader should compare the statement of our result below with
[
HM17
, Proposition 8.2]
. We include the values of the ordinary concordance invariant
V_{0}(K)
in the statement for ease of comparison.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m,n
be odd numbers such that
m\geq n\geq 3
. The involutive knot concordance invariants of the pretzel knots
K=P(-2,m,n)
are as follows.

•

If
m\not\equiv n\pmod{4}
, then
V_{0}(K)=\underline{V\mkern-3.0mu}\mkern 3.0mu_{0}(K)=0
and
\overline{V}_{0}(K)=-\frac{m+n}{4}
.

•

If
m\equiv n\pmod{4}
, then
V_{0}(K)=\underline{V\mkern-3.0mu}\mkern 3.0mu_{0}(K)=0
and
\overline{V}_{0}(K)=-\frac{m+n-2}{4}
.

Moreover, the involutive knot concordance invariants of the mirrors
\overline{K}=P(2,-m,-n)
are as follows.

•

If
m\not\equiv n\pmod{4}
, then
V_{0}(\overline{K})=\underline{V\mkern-3.0mu}\mkern 3.0mu_{0}(\overline{K})=\overline{V}_{0}(\overline{K})=\frac{m+n}{4}
.

•

If
m\equiv n\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, then
V_{0}(\overline{K})=\underline{V\mkern-3.0mu}\mkern 3.0mu_{0}(\overline{K})=\overline{V}_{0}(\overline{K})=\frac{m+n-2}{4}
.

•

If
m\equiv n\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, then
\underline{V\mkern-3.0mu}\mkern 3.0mu_{0}(\overline{K})=\frac{m+n+2}{4}
and
V_{0}(\overline{K})=\overline{V}_{0}(\overline{K})=\frac{m+n-2}{4}
.

This computation comes from analyzing four essentially distinct cases for the structure of the chain complex
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K)
, corresponding to the values of
m
and
n
modulo four, as we explain further in Section
3.2
.

Remark 1.2
.

In the case that
n=3
, the involutive concordance invariants were already known. The knots
P(-2,m,3)
are mirrors of
L
-space knots
[
OS05
]
. Hendricks and Manolescu computed the involutive concordance invariants of
L
-space knots and their mirrors
[
HM17
, Section 7]
; we review the results of their computation in Section 2. We include the case
n=3
above for completeness.

Remark 1.3
.

The computation of knot Floer homology for the pretzel knots discussed in this note is particularly simple because they are
(1,1)
knots
[
GMM05
]
. In general,
(1,1)
knots admit Heegaard diagrams depending only on a set of four integer parameters, whose knot Floer homology may be computed combinatorially
[
Ras05
,
Doy05
,
Rac15
]
. A possible future research direction is to attempt to give a general, hopefully combinatorial, formula for the skew-filtered chain homotopy equivalence class of
\iota_{K}
for knots admitting such diagrams.

Organization

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we review some necessary background; in particular, in Section 2.1 we review some properties of Heegaard Floer homology for knots, and in Section 2.2 we recall involutive Heegaard Floer homology for knots and the construction of the involutive concordance invariants. In Section 3.1 we recall the Heegaard Floer complexes associated to the pretzel knots
P(-2,m,n)
and carry out a convenient change of basis. In Section 3.2 we present the computation of the involutive invariants associated to these knots and conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Background on Heegaard Floer homology and involutive Heegaard Floer homology

In this section we recall some background on Heegaard Floer homology for knots and involutive Heegaard Floer homology for knots.

2.1.
Heegaard Floer homology for knots

We begin by briefly reviewing the construction of knot Floer homology, after which we will give a more focused description of some of its algebraic aspects; for a more detailed look, see
[
OS04a
]
, or
[
Hom17
]
for an expository view.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be the field of two elements. Recall that a
doubly-pointed Heegaard diagram
is a tuple
\mathcal{H}=(\Sigma,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta},z,w)
such that

•

\Sigma
is a closed oriented surface of genus
g
;

•

\bm{\alpha}
(respectively
\bm{\beta}
) is a tuple
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{g}\}
of pairwise disjoint circles (respectively
\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{g}\}
) in
\Sigma
which span a
g
-dimensional subspace of
H_{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{F})
.

•

The curves
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{j}
intersect transversely for all
i,j

•

w
and
z
are points in the complement of
\bm{\alpha}
and
\bm{\beta}

Momentarily ignoring the basepoint
z
, the tuple
(\Sigma,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta},w)
specifies a 3-manifold
Y
via thickening
\Sigma
to
\Sigma\times[0,1]
, attaching thickened disks along each
\alpha_{i}\times\{0\}
and
\beta_{j}\times\{1\}
, and capping off each of the two remaining
S^{3}
boundary components with three-balls. A knot
K
inside of
Y
is determined by connecting
w
to
z
in the complement of the
\beta
-disks and
z
to
w
in the complement of the
\alpha
-disks. An example of a Heegaard diagram for the right-handed trefoil appears in Figure
1
.

Figure 1.

\mathcal{H}
for the right-handed trefoil. Here

and the single

\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})

Given a Heegaard diagram
\mathcal{H}
for
K\subset S^{3}
as above, knot Floer homology associates to
\mathcal{H}
a free finitely-generated
(\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z})
-filtered chain complex
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
over
\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]

[
OS04a
,
Ras03
]
. The construction of this complex uses the
g
-fold symmetric product
\mathrm{Sym}^{g}(\Sigma)=\Sigma^{g}/S_{g}
, where here
\Sigma^{g}
denotes the ordinary product of
g
copies of
\Sigma
and the quotient is by the action of the symmetric group
S_{g}
. This points of this symmetric product are unordered
g
-tuples of points on the surface
\Sigma
; moreover,
\mathrm{Sym}^{g}(\Sigma)
has the structure of a
g
-dimensional complex manifold. Inside this symmetric product one may consider the tori
\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}}=\alpha_{1}\times\dots\times\alpha_{g}
and
\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}}=\beta_{1}\times\dots\times\beta_{g}
. The generators of
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
as an
\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]
-complex are the finitely-many intersection points
x\in\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}}\pitchfork\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}}
; concretely, this means that the generators consist of unordered
g
-tuples of intersection points between the curves
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{j}
, such that each curve is used exactly once. The differential is defined by counting pseudoholomorphic curves in the symmetric product; for more detail, see
[
OS04a
]
.

To the set of generators
\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}}\pitchfork\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}}
, Ozsváth and Szabó define maps
A,M:\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}}\pitchfork\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, called the
Alexander
and
Maslov
(or
homological
) gradings respectively. With these maps in hand, the generators of
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
as an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space may be written

U^{-i}x=[x,i,j]\text{ such that }x\in\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}}\pitchfork\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}},(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z},A(x)=j-i.

These generators are conventionally drawn on a plane; the element
[x;i,j]
lies at
(i,j)
and is said to have
planar grading

(i,j)
. One may then extend the functions
A
and
M
to
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
via

\operatorname{gr}([x;i,j])=M([x;i,j])=M(x)+2i\qquad\qquad A([x;i,j])=j

The action of the
U
-variable is now given by

U[x;i,j]=[x;i-1,j-1]

and the effect on the gradings of
U
-multiplication is

M(U[x;i,j])=M([x;i-1,j-1])=M([x;i,j])-2

A(U[x;i,j])=A([x;i-1,j-1])=j-1.

The
(i,j)
level of the
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
filtration is
\mathcal{F}_{(i,j)}=\{[x;i^{\prime},j^{\prime}]\in\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K):(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\leq(i,j)\}
, where
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
is given the dictionary order. The differential
\partial
on
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K)
respects the filtration and is
U
-equivariant; moreover if

\partial([x;i,j])=\sum[y;i^{\prime},j^{\prime}]

where each
[y;i^{\prime}j^{\prime}]
appears at most once (that is, there are no cancelling pairs in the expression) then for each
[y;i^{\prime},j^{\prime}]
we have

M([y;i^{\prime},j^{\prime}])=M([x;i,j])-1.

While the construction of
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
requires a choice of Heegaard diagram, Ozsváth and Szabó show that all such choices produce chain homotopy equivalent chain complexes
[
OS04a
]
; indeed, work of Juhász, Thurston, and Zemke
[
JTZ12
]
shows that these chain homotopies are themselves canonical up to homotopy, from which it follows that there is a well-defined filtered chain homotopy equivalence class of complexes
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K)
. Throughout the paper, we will generally take some representative for the filtered chain homotopy equivalence class of
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K)
; in some cases, such as Example
2.2
below, this representative will not be the chain complex associated to any Heegaard diagram for the knot.

The homology
H_{*}(\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K))
is always isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]

[
OS04c
,
OS04a
,
Ras03
]
.

We will have occasion to consider several special subsets of
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K)
which are chain complexes over either the ring
\mathbb{F}[U]
or over the vector space
\mathbb{F}
. Given a subset
X\in\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
, let
CX
denote the
\mathbb{F}
-vector space with basis consisting of elements with planar grading
(i,j)\in X
. Some examples of particular importance to us include

•

The complex
A_{0}^{-}=C\{i,j\leq 0\}
, consisting of the portion of
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K)
lying in the third quadrant of the plane, which has the structure of a chain complex over
\mathbb{F}[U]
.

•

B^{-}_{0}=C\{i\leq 0\}
, consisting of the portion of
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K)
lying in the second and third quadrants of the plane, which has the structure of a chain complex over
\mathbb{F}[U]
.

•

C\{i=0\}=C\{i\leq 0\}/C\{i<0\}
, consisting of the portion of
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K)
lying on the
j
-axis, which has the structure of a chain complex over
\mathbb{F}
.

Let us begin by considering the first two complexes. The homology
H_{*}(A_{0}^{-})
always admits a (noncanonical) decomposition as a direct sum

\mathbb{F}[U]\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{F}[U]/U^{n_{i}}\right)

for some natural numbers
1\leq n_{1}\leq\dots\leq n_{k}
; this follows from the fact that the homology of
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K)
is the Heegaard Floer three-manifold invariant
\mathit{HF}^{\infty}(S^{3})\simeq\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]

[
OS04c
,
OS04a
,
Ras03
]
. Moreover, the homology
H_{*}(B_{0}^{-})
is isomorphic to a copy of
\mathbb{F}[U]
with the property that
\operatorname{gr}(1)=0
. (In particular,
H_{*}(B_{0}^{-})
is the three-manifold Heegaard Floer invariant
\mathit{HF}^{-}(S^{3})
.) There is a chain map
v_{0}\colon A_{0}^{-}\rightarrow B_{0}^{-}
given by inclusion. For sufficiently large
n
, the induced map on homology
U^{n}H_{*}(A_{0}^{-})\to U^{n}H_{*}(B^{-}_{0})
is a nonzero map
\mathbb{F}[U]\rightarrow\mathbb{F}[U]
which must therefore be given by multiplication by some
U^{V_{0}(K)}
, where
V_{0}(K)
is a nonnegative integer. Since the map
v_{0}
is grading-preserving, the integer
V_{0}(K)
may be computed from the homological degree of the element
1
in the “tower” summand
\mathbb{F}[U]
in any decomposition of
H_{*}(A_{0}^{-})
into a direct sum of an
\mathbb{F}[U]
summand and
U
-torsion summands. In other words, we see that

V_{0}(K)=-\frac{1}{2}\max\{r:\exists\ x\in H_{r}(A_{0}^{-}),\forall\ n,\ U^{n}x\neq 0\}

where
H_{r}(A_{0}^{-})
denotes the homology in homological grading
r
. Peters
[
Pet10
, Proposition 2.1]
and Rasmussen
[
Ras04
, Theorem 2.3]
showed that
V_{0}(K)
is an invariant of knot concordance, in Peters’s case under the name
d(S^{3}_{+1}(K))=-2V_{0}(K)
and in Rasmussen’s case under the name
h_{0}(\overline{K})
. For more on the context of the concordance invariant
V_{0}(K)
, see eg
[
Hom17
, Section 3.2.2]
.

Now consider the complex
C\{i=0\}
, whose chain homotopy equivalence class is called
\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(K)
, which is naturally
\mathbb{Z}
-filtered by the Alexander filtration. The homology of the associated graded object of
C\{i=0\}
is

(2.1)

\begin{split}\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(K)&=\bigoplus_{w\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(K,w)\\
&=\bigoplus_{w\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{*}(C\{i=0,j=w\})\end{split}

and is often referred to as the
knot Floer homology
. If we include the homological grading
s
, we get a further decomposition

\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(K)=\bigoplus_{w\in\mathbb{Z},s\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}_{s}(K,w).

This theory is symmetric in the sense that
\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}_{s}(K,w)\simeq\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}_{s-2w}(K,-w)
and furthermore it detects the knot genus via

g(K)=\mathrm{max}\{w:\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(K,w)\neq 0\}.

Finally, the graded Euler characteristic of the knot Floer homology is the Alexander polynomial of the knot, that is,

\Delta_{K}(t)=\sum_{w}\chi(\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(K,w))t^{w}.

We also consider the vertical and horizontal homologies associated to
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K)
, as follows. Let
\partial=\sum_{i,j}\partial_{ij}
where
\partial_{ij}
is the term in the differential which decreases the two planar gradings by
i
and
j
respectively. Then the vertical differential is
\partial_{\mathrm{vert}}=\sum_{j}\partial_{0,j}
. The vertical homology is the
\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]
-module

H_{*}(\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K),\partial_{\mathrm{vert}})=\bigoplus_{i^{\prime}}H_{*}(C\{i=i^{\prime}\}).

Likewise the horizontal differential is
\partial_{\mathrm{horz}}=\sum_{i}\partial_{i,0}
and the horizontal homology is

H^{*}(\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K),\partial_{\mathrm{horz}})=\bigoplus_{j^{\prime}}H_{*}(C\{j=j^{\prime}\}).

For any knot
K
, the ranks of the vertical and horizontal homologies are one as
\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]
-modules; alternately, if we ignore the action of
U
, the dimensions of each
H_{*}(C\{j=j^{\prime}\})
and
H_{*}(C\{i=i^{\prime}\})
are one as an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space.

The action of a Dehn twist around the knot
K
in

induces a filtered chain homotopy equivalence
\sigma
on
\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K)
with the property that
\sigma^{2}\sim\operatorname{Id}
, where
\sim
denotes filtered chain homotopy
[

]
. Zemke
[
Zem17
]
shows that

admits a simple computation, as follows. Let

\Phi=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
i\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\partial_{ij}\qquad\qquad\Psi=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
j\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\partial_{ij}.

We then have

\sigma=\operatorname{Id}+U^{-1}\Phi\circ\Psi.

K
is said to be

-space
if it admits a positive surgery which is a three-manifold with Heegaard Floer homology of minimal rank (called a

-space
). Ozsváth and Szabó showed
[

that if

is an

-space knot, the filtered chain homotopy equivalence class of the complex

has a particularly simple representative. To describe it, we begin by introducing the following notation.

A
positive staircase complex
is a
(\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z})
-filtered
\mathbb{F}
-chain complex generated by elements
z_{0},z_{r}^{1},z_{r}^{2}
where

ranges from
1
to some integer

. The element
z_{0}
has planar grading
(0,0)
; moreover, the planar gradings of
z^{1}_{v-2w}
and
z^{1}_{v-(2w+1)}
differ only in the

z^{1}_{v-(2w+1)}
and
z^{1}_{v-(2w+2)}
differ only in the
j

z_{r}^{1}
lies at planar grading
(i,j)
then
z_{r}^{2}
lies at planar grading
(j,i)
. If

is even, the nonzero differentials in this complex are

\displaystyle\partial(z_{r}^{s})=z_{r-1}^{s}+z_{r+1}^{s}\text{ for }1<r<v,r\text{ odd }\qquad\qquad\qquad\partial(z_{1}^{s})=z_{0}+z_{2}^{s}

whereas if

is odd they are

\displaystyle\partial(z_{r}^{s})=z_{r-1}^{s}+z_{r+1}^{s}\text{ for }r>0,r\text{ even}\qquad\qquad\qquad\partial(z_{0})=z_{1}^{1}+z_{1}^{2}.

2.2.
Involutive Heegaard Floer homology for knots

Recall that if
C
is a
(\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z})
-filtered complex, a chain map
\alpha\colon C\rightarrow C
is said to be skew-filtered if
\alpha(\mathcal{F}_{j,i})\subset\mathcal{F}_{i,j}
. In
[
HM17
, Section 6]
, Hendricks and Manolescu use a conjugation operation on knot Floer homology to define a skew-filtered automorphism

\iota_{K,\mathcal{H}}\colon\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}).

The the pair
(\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}),\iota_{K,\mathcal{H}})
is an invariant of the knot up to equivariant chain homotopy equivalence, sometimes called
strong equivalence
. Specifically, two pairs
(C_{i},\iota_{i})
for
i=1,2
for which
C_{i}
is a finitely-generated
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
-filtered
\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]
-complex and
\iota_{i}
is a skew-filtered automorphism are said to be strongly equivalent if there are filtered chain maps
f:C_{1}\rightarrow C_{2}
and
g:C_{2}\rightarrow C_{1}
with the property that

gf\sim\operatorname{Id}_{C_{1}}\qquad\qquad gf\sim\operatorname{Id}_{C_{2}}\qquad\qquad f\iota_{1}\sim\iota_{2}f\qquad\qquad\iota_{1}g\sim g\iota_{2}

where the first two equivalences are filtered chain homotopy equivalence and the second two are skew-filtered chain homotopy equivalence.

In general we take some representative for the strong equivalence class
(\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K),\iota_{K})
, not always one arising from a Heegaard diagram. The map
\iota_{K}
is in principle difficult to compute. However, it has the following useful property.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
HM17
]
The square of the map
\iota_{K}
is filtered chain homotopic to the Sarkar involution; that is, we have

{\iota_{K}}^{2}\sim\sigma.

Note that this implies that
{\iota_{K}}^{4}
is filtered chain homotopic to the identity map. In
[
HM17
, Sections 7 and 8]
, Hendricks and Manolescu show that this is enough to uniquely determine
\iota_{K}
up to filtered chain homotopy for
L
-space knots and thin knots.

There are two knot concordance invariants analogous to
V_{0}(K)
arising from this complex, as follows. First, choosing some representative for
(\mathit{CFK}^{\infty}(K),\iota_{K})
, we consider the complex

and the term

We first note that in all cases by Lemma

\mathbb{F}
-complex

\displaystyle t^{g}-t^{g-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-3}(-1)^{k-1}kt^{g-k-2}+\sum_{k=n-g}^{g-n}(-1)^{g-k-1}(n-2)t^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-3}(-1)^{k-1}kt^{k+2-g}-t^{1-g}+t^{-g}.

\sum_{k=1}^{n-4}(-1)^{k}kt^{g-k-3}+\sum_{k=n-g}^{g-n}(-1)^{g-k-1}(n-3)t^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-4}(-1)^{k}kt^{k+3-g}.

This polynomial factors as

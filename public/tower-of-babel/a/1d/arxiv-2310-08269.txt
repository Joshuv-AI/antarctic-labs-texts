The Lattice of Group Topologies on a Group
Dekui Peng

Abstract
. For an infinite group G G , the set of group topologies, ℒ G \mathcal{L}_{G} , forms a complete lattice. It is known that ℒ G \mathcal{L}_{G} is modular if G G is abelian but the same result does not hold for nilpotent groups. We prove that the lattice ℒ G \mathcal{L}_{G} is semi-modular if and only if ℒ G / Z \mathcal{L}_{G/Z} is semi-modular, where Z Z is the centre. As a corollary, for every nilpotent group G G , the lattice ℒ G \mathcal{L}_{G} is semi-modular. Moreover, in the famous Kourovka Notebook, several questions about ℒ G \mathcal{L}_{G} were formalized by Arnautov. We answer two of them in this note.

Introduction

The study of the lattice of all topologies on a given set was started by G. Birkhoff
[
5
]

\mathcal{T}_{X}

The subset

In 1947, R. Vaidyanathaswamy
[
24
]
proved that
\mathcal{T}_{X}
is not distributive.
R. Bagley generalized this result by remarking that
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{X}
is not even modular in his phD thesis in 1954.
However, it is a consequence of Ore
[
20

that the lattice

satisfies dual Birkhoff property

Such lattices are also called

, that is, if

and

And in 1972, R. Larson and W. Thron proved that

has both Birkhoff property and dual Birkhoff property.

For an abstract group

of

With the order inherited from

The study of the lattice

We refer the readers to

It is known that

.
In this case,

This result was greatly extended by D. Dikranjan and I. Protasov

In the 14th section of the famous Kourovka Notebook, Arnautov formalized six questions about the lattice

For a positive integer

Notations, terminology and elementary facts.

Topological group topologies (shortly, group topologies) on a group
G
are not assumed to be Hausdorff in general; thus
\mathcal{L}_{G}
forms a complete lattice.
The identity of a group is denoted by
e
. Through out the paper, a neighbourhood always means an open neighbourhood.

For any group
G
, the following is known as
Pontryagin’s Principle
for a system of subsets of
G
to be a neighbourhood basis at
e
for some group topology.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
2
, Theorem 3.12]
Let
G
be a group and
\mathcal{B}
be a family of subsets of
G
, each contains the identity
e
. Then
\mathcal{B}
is a neighbourhood basis at
e
for some group topology if and only if the following conditions hold:

(i)

for each
U\in\mathcal{B}
, there exists
V\in\mathcal{B}
such that
VV\subseteq U
;

(ii)

for each
U\in\mathcal{B}
, there exists
V\in\mathcal{B}
such that
V^{-1}\subseteq U
;

(iii)

for each
U\in\mathcal{B}
and
x\in U
, there exists
V\in\mathcal{B}
such that
xV\subseteq U
;

(iv)

for each
U\in\mathcal{B}
and
x\in G
, there exists
V\in\mathcal{B}
such that
xVx^{-1}\subseteq U;

(v)

for each pair
U,V\in\mathcal{B}
, there exists
W\in\mathcal{B}
such that
W\subseteq U\cap V
.

Moreover, this topology is Hausdorff if and only if

(vi)

\bigcap\mathcal{B}=\{e\}

is also satisfied.

If
\tau
is a group topology on a group
G
and
N
is a subgroup of
G
, then
\tau\res_{N}
denotes the subspace topology on
N
induced from
(G,\tau)
.
The quotient topology on the left coset space

is denoted by
\tau/N
.
It is evident that if
N
is a normal subgroup, then
\tau/N
is a group topology.
The following lemma is widely used in the study of minimal groups:

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
G
be a group,
N
a (not necessarily normal) subgroup of
G
.
Then for
\tau,\sigma\in\mathcal{L}_{G}
, if

(i)

\sigma\subseteq\tau
;

(ii)

\sigma\res_{N}=\tau\res_{N}
; and

(iii)

\sigma/N=\tau/N
,

then
\sigma=\tau
.

In a lattice
\mathcal{L}
, we say that
a
covers

, or

is a cover of

, if
b<a
and there is no

with
b<c<a
.
We also call the pair
\{b,a\}
a

\mathcal{L}
)
.
In this case, we write

.
The symbol
b\preceq a
means that either
b\prec a
or
b=a
.
Evidently, a covering is an unrefinalbe chain of length 1.
Coverings in the lattice
\mathcal{L}_{G}
are also called

by W. He, M. Tkachenko, Z. Xiao and the author
[
15
,
16
]
.
The supremum and infimum of two elements

and
b
in a lattice
\mathcal{L}
is denoted by
\tau\vee\sigma
and
\tau\wedge\sigma
, respectively.
And for a subset

of
\mathcal{L}
, the supremum (resp. infimum) of

(if exists) is denoted by
\bigvee_{a\in A}a
(resp.
\bigwedge_{a\in A}a
).

Fact 1.6
.

Let
\sigma,\tau
be two group topologies on a group
G
, and
\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}
(resp.,
\mathcal{B}_{\tau}
) is a neighbourhood basis of the identity in
(G,\sigma)
(resp.
(G,\tau)
. Then the following are known and can be easily checked:

(i)

\{U\cap V:U\in\mathcal{B}_{\sigma},V\in\mathcal{B}_{\tau}\}
forms a local basis at
e
in
(G,\sigma\vee\tau)
;

(ii)

if the centre of
G
is open with respect to either
\sigma
or
\tau
, then
\{UV:U\in\mathcal{B}_{\sigma},V\in\mathcal{B}_{\tau}\}
forms a local basis at
e
in
(G,\sigma\wedge\tau)
.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
G
be a group and
N
a subgroup. Then for every pair
\sigma,\tau\in\mathcal{L}_{G}
, one has

(i)

(\sigma\vee\tau)\res_{N}=\sigma\res_{N}\vee\tau\res_{N}
;

(ii)

(\sigma\wedge\tau)/N=\sigma/N\wedge\tau/N
provided that
N
is normal.

Proof.

The item (i) is an immediate corollary of Fact
1.6
(i).

To see (ii), on one hand it is evident that
(\sigma\wedge\tau)/N\leq\sigma/N\wedge\tau/N
.
And on the other hand, let
\lambda
be the group topology on
G
such that a subset is open if and only if it is the preimage of some open set under the canonical mapping

\lambda\leq\sigma\wedge\tau
and hence
(\sigma\wedge\tau)/N\leq\lambda/N=\sigma/N\wedge\tau/N

∎

A lattice
\mathcal{L}
is called
modular
if it satisfies the following: for every triple
a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}
with
a\leq c
, one has
a\vee(b\wedge c)=(a\vee b)\wedge c
.
Distributive lattices and the lattice of normal subgroups of a given group
G
are the most well-known examples of modular lattices.
Modular lattices have important and nice properties.
Among others, they have the following so-called
upper cover condition
:
b\prec a
implies
b\vee c\prec a\vee c
for every
c
.

A lattice
\mathcal{L}
is called
semi-modular
if it satisfies the upper covering condition.
One of the most important properties of semi-modular lattices is that they have the
Jordan-Hölder Chain Condition
(also called
Jordan-Dedekind Chain Condition
).

Definition 1.8
.

[
23
,
\lx@sectionsign 1.9
]
A lattice
\mathcal{L}
is called to have Jordan-Hölder Chain Condition if for every every pair of elements
a,b\in\mathcal{L}
with
a<b
, all maximal chains in the interval
[a,b]
are either of infinite length or of the same finite length.

Theorem 1.9
.

[
23
, Theorem 1.9.1]
Every semi-modular lattice satisfies Jordan-Hölder Chain Condition.

We are going to prove Theorem

We first recall some elementary facts.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

be a finite group and
\tau
a group topology on

.
Then there exists a normal subgroup

of

such that the singleton
\{H\}
forms a local base of

at the identity. So a set in

.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2}
be modular lattices. Then
\mathcal{P}_{1}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}
(the order is defined as:
(a,b)\leq(c,d)
if and only if

and

) is modular.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
F
be a finite non-abelian nilpotent group. Take a prime number

is not divided by

H=\bigoplus_{\omega}\mathbb{Z}(p)

\mathbb{Z}(p)
is the cyclic group of order

.
Then the group

By Lemmas

, it suffices to prove that every group topology

\tau_{H}\in\mathcal{L}_{H}

\tau_{F}\in\mathcal{L}_{F}

.

The closure of
\{e\}

(endowed with the topology

) is denoted by

.
Then we claim that

, where

.
To see this, let

Then

Since

Therefore,

The inclusion

Thus,

.

Denote by

By the above argument,

.
It is evident that

Thus,

Since

Let

be an open neighborhood of

Then

, and hence,

.
This particularly implies that

, where

is an open set in

Lemma 2.4

Let

Suppose

Corollary 2.5

Let

be a group with a central subgroup

\sigma\leq\tau\in\mathcal{L}_{G}

Then
\sigma\prec\tau
if and only if one of the following holds:

(a)

\sigma\res_{N}=\tau\res_{N}

\sigma/N\prec\tau/N

(b)

\sigma\res_{N}\prec\tau\res_{N}

Since

.
The case (b) follows from a similar argument.

Recall that a lattice is called
dually semi-modular
if its dual lattice is semi-modular, or equivalently, if it satisfies the
lower cover condition
:
b\prec a
implies that
b\wedge c\preceq a\wedge c
for every
c
.
One can immediately see that a dually semi-modular lattice also satisfies Jordan-Hölder Chain Condition because Jordan-Hölder Chain Condition is dually preserved.
So one may wonder

Question 4.1
.

Does Theorem
2.10
hold for dual semi-modularity? In particular, is
\mathcal{L}_{G}
dually semi-modular if
G
is nilpotent?

The next question is devoted to consider a wider family of groups than nilpotent ones.

Question 4.2
.

Is the lattice
\mathcal{L}_{G}
for a (super)sovable group
G
semi-modular or dually semi-modular?

We have mentioned in the footnote 1 that there is a weaker property of lattices than being (dually) semi-modular, namely, being satisfying (dual) Birkhoff property.
It is known that a lattice having (dual) Birkhoff property satisfies the so-called
weakly Jordan-Hölder Chain Condition
:

Each pair of unrefinable chains of
finite
length with same endpoints have the same length.

The main difference between Jordan-Hölder Chain Condition and the weak version is that the latter one allows the existence of two chains with same endpoints of which one is finite and the other is infinite.
So if Question
4.2
does not hold. One may ask:

Question 4.3
.

Let
G
be a (super)sovable group. Does
\mathcal{L}_{G}
satisfies Birkhoff property or dual Birkhoff property?

A lattice is called
atomistic
if every element is the join of some atoms; and, dually,
coatomistic
if every elment is the meet of some coatoms.
For every abelian group
G
, there exists a natural coatomistic sublattice of
\mathcal{L}_{G}
, which is consisting of meets of maximal group topologies (recall that again, maximal group topologies are coatoms in
\mathcal{L}_{G}
being also Hausdorff). In other words, it is the subset
\{\bigwedge\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{1}(G)\}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{1}(G)
is the set of all maximal group topologies and
\bigwedge\varnothing
is considered as the discrete topology.
Let us call it the
Prodanov
lattice of
G
and denote it by
\mathcal{P}_{G}
in memory of Iv. Prodanov, who introduced the notion of
submaximal group topology

[
22
]
; the submaximal group topology is the bottom element in the lattice
\mathcal{P}_{G}
, i.e., the infimum of all maximal group topologies.
Among other results, Prodanov showed that the submaximal group topology is finer than the Bohr topology, i.e., the finest precompact topology, by giving a neighbourhood basis of the identity in the submaximal topology.
This result particularly implies that the submaixmal, and also all members in
\mathcal{P}_{G}
, are Hausdorff.
Here we list some questions about the Prodanov lattice for an abelian group.

Question 4.4
.

Let
G
be an infinite albeian group.

(i)

Let
k>1
be an integer. Must every
k
-maximal group topology is contained in
\mathcal{P}_{G}
? This is equivalent to ask: must a
k
-maximal group topology be the infimum of
k
many maximal group topologies?

(ii)

Does every two maximal chains in
\mathcal{P}_{G}
have the same cardinality?

(iii)

What is the largest cardinal
\kappa
such that
\mathcal{P}_{G}
has a chain of cardinality
\kappa
?

On the differential equations of frozen Calogero-Moser-Sutherland particle models
Michael Voit

Abstract
. Multivariate Bessel and Jacobi processes describe Calogero-Moser-Sutherland particle models. They depend on a parameter k k and are related to time-dependent classical random matrix models like Dysom Brownian motions, where k k has the interpretation of an inverse temperature. There are several stochastic limit theorems for k → ∞ k\to\infty were the limits depend on the solutions of associated ODEs where these ODEs admit particular simple solutions which are connected with the zeros of the classical orthogonal polynomials. In this paper we show that these solutions attract all solutions. Moreover we present a connection between the solutions of these ODEs with associated inverse heat equations. These inverse heat equations are used to compute the expectations of some determinantal formulas for the Bessel and Jacobi processes.

\|x(t)-\tilde{x}(t)\|\leq\|x(0)-\tilde{x}(0)\|\cdot\sqrt{\frac{N(N-1)}{\|x(0)\|^{2}}t+1}\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\sqrt{2N(N-1)t+\|x(0)\|^{4}}-\|x(0)\|^{2}\Bigr)}.

In particular, if
x(t)
is any solution of (
2.1
) on
C_{N}^{A}
with
x_{1}(0)+\ldots+x_{N}(0)=0
, then

\displaystyle\Bigl\|x(t)

\displaystyle-\sqrt{2t+\frac{2\|x(0)\|^{2}}{N(N-1)}}\cdot{\bf z}\Bigr\|

(2.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Biggl\|x(0)-\sqrt{\frac{2\|x(0)\|^{2}}{N(N-1)}}\cdot{\bf z}\Biggr\|\cdot\sqrt{\frac{N(N-1)}{\|x(0)\|^{2}}t+1}\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\sqrt{2N(N-1)t+\|x(0)\|^{4}}-\|x(0)\|^{2}\Bigr)}

Proof.

We use the time transform

t=\frac{N(N-1)}{2}\tau^{2}+\|x(0)\|^{2}\tau

from (
2.5
) with
t,\tau\geq 0
. Then

\tau=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\Bigl(\sqrt{2N(N-1)t+\|x(0)\|^{4}}-\|x(0)\|^{2}\Bigr),

and by Proposition
2.9
and Lemmas
2.6
and
2.6
,

\displaystyle\|x(t)

\displaystyle-\tilde{x}(t)\|=\|x(t)\|\cdot\Bigl\|\frac{x(t)}{\|x(t)\|}-\frac{\tilde{x}(t)}{\|\tilde{x}(t)\|}\Bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x(t)\|\cdot\Bigl\|\frac{x(0)}{\|x(0)\|}-\frac{\tilde{x}(0)}{\|\tilde{x}(0)\|}\Bigr\|e^{-\frac{N(N-1)}{2}\tau}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{N(N-1)}{\|x(0)\|^{2}}t+1}\cdot\|x(0)-\tilde{x}(0)\|\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\sqrt{2N(N-1)t+\|x(0)\|^{4}}-\|x(0)\|^{2}\Bigr)}.

This proves the first estimate. The second estimate is then also clear.
∎

We next discuss some connection of the ODE (
2.1
) to the inverse heat equation.
As indicated in the introduction, this is not new; see for instance
[
CSL
,
RT
]
for a connection for
N=\infty

in the context of the Riemannian hypothesis and also
[
HHJK
]
.

We include a proof, as this will be the blueprint for
the proofs of corresponding results for further ODEs below.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
x:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}):[0,\infty[\to C_{N}^{A}
be a differentiable function.
Then
x
is a solution of (
2.1
) in the sense of Theorem
2.1

if and only if the function
H(t,z):=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(z-x_{i}(t))

solves the inverse heat equation
H_{t}+\frac{1}{2}H_{zz}=0
.

Proof.

Assume first that
x(t)
satisfies (
2.1
). Consider
H
as defined in the theorem. For
t>0
and
i,j=1,\ldots,N
with
i\neq j

consider the polynomials
H_{i}
and
H_{i,j}
in
z
with
H_{i}(t,z):=H(t,z)/(z-x_{i}(t))
and
H_{i,j}(t,z):=H(t,z)/((z-x_{i}(t))(z-x_{j}(t)))
.
Then, for
i\neq j
,

\frac{H_{i}(t,z)-H_{j}(t,z)}{x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)}=H_{i,j}(t,z).

(2.11)

Hence, by (
2.1
),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}H(t,z)=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{\prime}(t)\>H_{i}(t,z)=-\sum_{i,j:i\neq j}\frac{1}{x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)}\>H_{i}(t,z)

(2.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j:i\neq j}H_{i,j}(t,z)=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{z}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}H_{i}(t,z)\Bigr)=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{zz}H(t,z)

as claimed. Please notice that the factor
1/2
in this computation comes from the fact that two terms in the first line of (
2.11
)
lead to one in the second line.

Now assume that
H_{t}+\frac{1}{2}H_{zz}=0
. As before, we write
H(t,z)=H_{i}(t,z)(z-x_{i}(t))
for
i=1,\ldots,N
. Hence,

\frac{\partial_{zz}H(t,z)}{\partial_{z}H(t,z)}=\frac{2\partial_{z}H_{i}(t,z)+(z-x_{i}(t))\partial_{zz}H_{i}(t,z)}{H_{i}(t,z)+(z-x_{i}(t))\partial_{z}H_{i}(t,z)}

(2.13)

and thus

\frac{\partial_{zz}H(t,x_{i}(t))}{\partial_{z}H(t,x_{i}(t))}=\frac{2\partial_{z}H_{i}(t,x_{i}(t))}{H_{i}(t,x_{i}(t))}.

(2.14)

Moreover,

0=\frac{d}{dt}H(t,x_{i}(t))=\partial_{t}H(t,x_{i}(t))+\partial_{z}H(t,x_{i}(t))\cdot x_{i}^{\prime}(t)

(2.15)

and the heat equation lead to

x_{i}^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{\partial_{t}H(t,x_{i}(t))}{\partial_{z}H(t,x_{i}(t))}=\frac{\partial_{zz}H(t,x_{i}(t))}{2\partial_{z}H(t,x_{i}(t))}.

(2.16)

Hence, by (
2.14
),

x_{i}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{\partial_{z}H_{i}(t,x_{i}(t))}{H_{i}(t,x_{i}(t))}=\sum_{j:j\neq i}\frac{1}{x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)}

(2.17)

as claimed.
∎

Remark 2.13
.

Solutions of the inverse heat equation
H_{t}+\frac{1}{2}H_{zz}=0
are known in probability theory as space-time harmonic functions.
Polynomial solutions of this type have
the property that for a Brownian motion
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{R}
(with the generator
f\mapsto f^{\prime\prime}/2

as usual in probability), the process
(H(t,B_{t}))_{t\geq 0}
is a martingale w.r.t. the canonical filtration
by Dynkin’s formula; see e.g. Section III.10 of
[
RW
]
.

The most typical examples of such space-time-harmonic functions are the heat polynomials

H_{N}(t,z):=2^{N}\prod_{i=1}^{N}(z-\sqrt{2t}\cdot z_{i})=2^{N}(2t)^{N/2}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\sqrt{2t}}-z_{i}\Bigr)=(2t)^{N/2}\cdot H_{N}(z/\sqrt{2t})

(2.18)

with the Hermite polynomials
H_{N}
with the standard leading coefficients
2^{N}
as in
[
S
]
for
N\geq 0
where we again see the
connection with Lemma
2.3

Theorem
2.12
and the linearity of the inverse heat equation show
that for each solution
x(t)
of the ODE (
2.1
) on
C_{N}^{A}
, the associated polynomial
H(t,z)
from Theorem
2.12

can be written as

H(t,z)=2^{-N}H_{N}(t,z)+\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}c_{l}H_{l}(t,z),

where the
c_{l}
(
l=0,\ldots,N-1
) are determined uniquely by the initial condition:

2^{N}z^{N}+\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}c_{l}2^{l}z^{l}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(z-x_{i}(0)).

We next discuss some application of Theorem
2.12
to some expectations for Dyson Brownian motions
on the Weyl chambers
C_{N}^{A}
for arbitrary parameters
k>0
where

describes some inverse temperature; see e.g.
[
AGZ
]
.
These processes
(X_{t,k}=(X_{t,k}^{1},\ldots,X_{t,k}^{N}))_{t\geq 0}
are also known as Bessel processes on

; see e.g.
[
CGY
,
RV1
,
V3
]
.
Following
[
AGZ
,
CGY
]
, we can describe these processes as the unique strong solutions of the stochastic differential equations

dX_{t,k}^{i}=d\tilde{B}_{t}^{i}+k\sum_{j:\>j\neq i}\frac{1}{X_{t,k}^{i}-X_{t,k}^{j}}dt\quad\quad(i=1,\ldots,N)

(2.19)

with some
N
-dimensional Brownian motion
(\tilde{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
for arbitrary starting points
x\in C_{N}^{A}
where

x
may be even on the singular boundary of
C_{N}^{A}
; see Section 4.3 of
[
AGZ
]
and
[
CL
,
GM

CGY
]
for different approaches.
The processes

have the generators

L_{k}f:=\frac{1}{2}\Delta f+k\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigl(\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\Bigr)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}f

(2.20)

for

,
R2
,
RV1
,

]

Clearly, the renormalized processes

are solutions of

d\tilde{X}_{t,k}^{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}dB_{t}^{i}+\sum_{j:\>j\neq i}\frac{1}{\tilde{X}_{t,k}^{i}-\tilde{X}_{t,k}^{j}}dt\quad\quad(i=1,\ldots,N).

(2.21)

Moreover, for

(

) degenerates into the ODE (

) with the deterministic solutions

.

In
[

]
, several martingales associated with the

were considered which lead to some identities for expectations which generalize identities
for determinants of Gaussian orthogonal/unitary/symplectic ensembles in
[

]
. The key in
[

]
is the observation that the expectations
of elementary symmetric polynomials of

are independent of

. For this recapitulate
that the elementary
symmetric polynomials
e_{l}^{N}(x)
(
l=0,\ldots,N
) in
N
variables satisfy

\prod_{l=1}^{N}(z-x_{l})=\sum_{l=0}^{N}(-1)^{N-l}e^{N}_{N-l}(x)z^{l}\quad\quad(z\in\mathbb{C},\>x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})),

(2.22)

i.e.,

e_{0}^{N}=1,\>e_{1}^{N}(x)=\sum_{l=1}^{N}x_{l},\ldots,e_{N}^{N}(x)=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{N}
. Then, by Corollary 2.5 in
[
KVW
]
:

Lemma 2.14
.

For all
l=0,\ldots,N
,
t\geq 0
,
k\in]0,\infty]
, and fixed starting points

,
the processes
(\tilde{X}_{t,k})_{t\geq 0}

satisfy
\mathbb{E}(e_{l}^{N}(\tilde{X}_{t,k}))=e_{l}^{N}(x(t))
.

Corollary 2.15
.

For fixed starting points
x\in C_{N}^{A}
and
k\in]0,\infty]
, consider the Bessel processes
(\tilde{X}_{t,k})_{t\geq 0}
. Then, for
each
t\geq 0
and each
\mathbb{R}
-valued random variable
Y
with
N
-th moment, which is independent from
\tilde{X}_{t,k}
,

\mathbb{E}\bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{N}(Y-\tilde{X}_{t,k}^{i})\bigr)=\mathbb{E}\bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{N}(Y-x_{l}(t))\bigr).

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{N}(Y-\tilde{X}_{t,k}^{i})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{N}(-1)^{l}\mathbb{E}\bigl(e_{l}^{N}(\tilde{X}_{t,k})Y^{N-l}\bigr)=\sum_{l=0}^{N}(-1)^{l}\mathbb{E}(e_{l}^{N}(\tilde{X}_{t,k})\bigr)\cdot\mathbb{E}(Y^{N-l})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{N}(-1)^{l}e_{l}^{N}(x(t))\cdot\mathbb{E}(Y^{N-l})=\mathbb{E}\bigl(\prod_{l=1}^{N}(Y-x_{l}(t))\bigr).

∎

For
Y=z\in\mathbb{R}
a constant and the starting point

, this, Lemma
2.3
, and the definition of
the Hermite polynomial
H_{N}
yield the following; see
[
KVW
]
:

Corollary 2.16
.

For
k>0
let

(X_{t,k})_{t\geq 0}
be the (original) Bessel process of type A with start in

.
Then, for

and

,

\mathbb{E}\bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{N}(z-X_{t,k}^{i})\bigr)=(tk/2)^{N/2}\cdot H_{N}(z/\sqrt{2kt}).

On the other hand, Corollary
2.15
and Theorem
2.12
imply:

Theorem 2.17
.

Let

,
k>0
, and
(\tilde{X}_{t,k})_{t\geq 0}
a renormalized Bessel process starting in
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in C_{N}^{A}
.
Then, for each 1-dimensional Brownian motion

(starting in 0) independent from

\mathbb{E}\bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{N}(B_{t}+y-\tilde{X}_{t,k}^{i})\bigr)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(y-x_{i}).

(2.23)

Corollary
2.15
with

∎

Theorem 2.18
.

Let

Then

.

Remark 2.19
.

(1)

The proof of the if-part in Theorem
2.18

also works for functions
H
which satisfy (

) with quite arbitary potentials instead of

-N\lambda
. This looks disturbing at a first glance and can be resolved by the fact that by our general

which is possible only for the potential

(2)

For

and

as described in the theorem,
the process

is a martingale. This fact can be used to derive an analogue of Theorem

for

instead of a Brownian motion and an asyptotically stationary version of the Bessel processes

.
However, as in these cases the same distributions appear up to notations, the expectations in this case will be equivalent to those in
(

The Laguerre case

\displaystyle(q-p)\Bigl(\cot\left(\frac{\tau_{i}(t)}{2}\right)-\cot\left(\frac{\tilde{\tau}_{i}(t)}{2}\right)\Bigr)

In order to estimate

we conclude from the mean value theorem that

\displaystyle(q-p)

\displaystyle\Bigl(\cot\Bigl(\frac{\tau_{i}(t)}{2}\Bigr)-\cot\Bigl(\frac{\tilde{\tau}_{i}(t)}{2}\Bigr)\Bigr)+2(p+1-N)(\cot\tau_{i}(t)-\cot\tilde{\tau}_{i}(t))

\displaystyle=-(\tau_{i}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{i}(t))\Bigl(\frac{q-p}{2\sin^{2}(\hat{\tau}_{i}(t)/2)}+\frac{2(p+1-N)}{\sin^{2}(\hat{\tau}_{i}(t))}\Bigr)

In order to handle

\displaystyle A_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l,j:\>l>j}\Bigl(\tau_{j}(t)+\tau_{l}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{j}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{l}(t)\Bigr)\Bigl(\cot\Bigl(\frac{\tau_{j}(t)+\tau_{l}(t)}{2}\Bigr)-\cot\Bigl(\frac{\tilde{\tau}_{j}(t)+\tilde{\tau}_{l}(t)}{2}\Bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{l,j:\>l>j}\Bigl(\tau_{j}(t)-\tau_{l}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{j}(t)+\tilde{\tau}_{l}(t)\Bigr)\Bigl(\cot\Bigl(\frac{\tau_{j}(t)-\tau_{l}(t)}{2}\Bigr)-\cot\Bigl(\frac{\tilde{\tau}_{j}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{l}(t)}{2}\Bigr)\Bigr).

Hence, again by the mean value theorem for the cotangens, for some
\tau_{j,l,1},\tau_{j,l,2}\in[0,\pi]

\displaystyle A_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l,j:\>l>j}\Bigl(\tau_{j}(t)+\tau_{l}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{j}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{l}(t)\Bigr)^{2}\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\tau_{j,l,1})}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l,j:\>l>j}\Bigl(\tau_{j}(t)-\tau_{l}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{j}(t)+\tilde{\tau}_{l}(t)\Bigr)^{2}\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\tau_{j,l,2})}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle\sum_{l,j:\>l\neq j}\Bigl(\Bigl(\tau_{j}(t)+\tau_{l}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{j}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{l}(t)\Bigr)^{2}+\Bigl(\tau_{j}(t)-\tau_{l}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{j}(t)+\tilde{\tau}_{l}(t)\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr).

As

\displaystyle\Bigl(\tau_{j}(t)+

\displaystyle\tau_{l}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{j}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{l}(t)\Bigr)^{2}+\Bigl(\tau_{j}(t)-\tau_{l}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{j}(t)+\tilde{\tau}_{l}(t)\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle=2(\tau_{j}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{j}(t))^{2}+2(\tau_{l}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{l}(t))^{2},

(4.8)

we conclude that

A_{1}(t)\leq-(N-1)\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\tau_{j}(t)-\tilde{\tau}_{j}(t))^{2}.

(4.9)

.
Let
r_{j}(t):=x_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{j}(t)
for
j=1,\ldots,N
, and
D(t):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}r_{j}(t)^{2}
.
We assume w.l.o.g. that

x_{1}(t)+\ldots+x_{N}(t)=\tilde{x}_{1}(t)+\ldots+\tilde{x}_{N}(t)=0\quad\quad(t\geq 0).

(6.7)

Then,

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Bigl(x_{j}^{\prime}(t)-\tilde{x}_{j}^{\prime}(t)\Bigr)\Bigl(x_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{j}(t)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l,j:\>l\neq j}\Bigl(x_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{j}(t)\Bigr)\Bigl(\cot\Bigl(\frac{x_{j}(t)-x_{l}(t)}{2}\Bigr)-\cot\Bigl(\frac{\tilde{x}_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{l}(t)}{2}\Bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l,j:\>l\neq j}\Bigl((x_{j}(t)-x_{l}(t))-(\tilde{x}_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{l}(t))\Bigr)\cdot

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\cdot\Biggl(\cot\Bigl(\frac{x_{j}(t)-x_{l}(t)}{2}\Bigr)-\cot\Bigl(\frac{\tilde{x}_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{l}(t)}{2}\Bigr)\Biggr).

As
x_{j}(t)-x_{l}(t)
and
\tilde{x}_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{l}(t)
have the same sign and are contained in
]-2\pi,2\pi[
, the mean value theorem yields that

\cot\Bigl(\frac{x_{j}(t)-x_{l}(t)}{2}\Bigr)-\cot\Bigl(\frac{\tilde{x}_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{l}(t)}{2}\Bigr)=-\frac{(x_{j}(t)-x_{l}(t))-(\tilde{x}_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{l}(t))}{2\sin^{2}(z_{j,l}(t)/2)}

with some
z_{j,l}(t)
between
x_{j}(t)-x_{l}(t)
and
\tilde{x}_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{l}(t)
.
Moreover, as by (
6.7
),

\sum_{l,j:\>l\neq j}(x_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{j}(t))(x_{l}(t)-\tilde{x}_{l}(t))=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}(x_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{j}(t))^{2},

and as
|\sin y|\leq 1
for
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{l,j:\>l\neq j}\frac{\Bigl((x_{j}(t)-x_{l}(t))-(\tilde{x}_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{l}(t))\Bigr)^{2}}{\sin^{2}(z_{j,l}(t)/2)}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{l,j:\>l\neq j}\Bigl((x_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{j}(t))-(x_{l}(t)-\tilde{x}_{l}(t))\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\Biggl(2(N-1)\sum_{j=1}^{N}(x_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{j}(t))^{2}-2\sum_{l,j:\>l\neq j}(x_{j}(t)-\tilde{x}_{j}(t))(x_{l}(t)-\tilde{x}_{l}(t))\Biggr)

\displaystyle=-N\cdot D(t).

The lemma of Gronwall now implies the claim.
∎

It does not seem to be possible to state an analogue of the connection between the ODEs and the associated inverse heat equations
in the preceding sections for the ODEs (
6.4
) due to their trigonometric form. To obtain such a connection,
we
transfer all data from
\mathbb{R}^{N}
to
\mathbb{T}^{N}
via
w_{j}:=e^{ix_{j}}
for
j=1,\ldots,N
, or for short,

w:=e^{ix}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}
. Then in
w
-coordinates,
L_{k}
is given by

\displaystyle H_{k}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Bigl(w_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{j}}\Bigr)^{2}-k\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{l:\>l\neq j}\frac{w_{j}+w_{l}}{w_{j}-w_{l}}\cdot w_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{j}}

(6.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{j}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial w_{j}^{2}}-(1-k(N-1))\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{j}}-2k\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{l:l\neq j}\frac{w_{j}^{2}}{w_{j}-w_{l}}\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{j}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial w_{j}^{2}}-(1+k(N-1))\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{j}}-2k\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{l:l\neq j}\frac{w_{j}w_{l}}{w_{j}-w_{l}}\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{j}}

where
H_{k}
is applied to permutation invariant functions in

C^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
; see
[
LV
,
RV2
]
.
We now consider
the renormalized operators
\tilde{H}_{k}:=\frac{1}{k}H_{k}
and the associated diffusions

(\tilde{Z}_{t,k}:=e^{i\widetilde{X}_{t,k}})_{t\geq 0}

on
C_{N}^{\mathbb{T}};=\{e^{ix}:\>x\in C_{N}\}
for
k\in]0,\infty[
. Clearly, this also works for
k=\infty
. Hence,
in
w
-coordinates, the ODE (
6.4
)
has the form

w_{j}^{\prime}(t)=-(N-1)w_{j}(t)-2\sum_{l:l\neq j}\frac{w_{j}(t)w_{l}(t)}{w_{j}(t)-w_{l}(t)}\quad\quad(j=1,\ldots,N).

(6.9)

Remark 6.3
.

Besides the generators in (
6.8
), also the operators

D_{k}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{j}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial w_{j}^{2}}+2k\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{l:\>l\neq j}\frac{w_{j}^{2}}{w_{j}-w_{l}}\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{j}}

(6.10)

appear in the literature; see e.g.
[
F
,
St
,
OO
]
.
Here

-H_{k}=D_{k}+E_{k}
with the Euler operator

E_{k}:=(1-k(N-1))\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{j}}

which commutes with
D_{k}
.

We now turn to the connection of (
6.9
) to inverse heat equations.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
w:=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{N}):[0,\infty[\to C_{N}^{\mathbb{T}}
be a differentiable function.
Then
w
is a solution of (
6.9
)
if and only if the function
H(t,z):=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(z-w_{j}(t))

solves the “inverse heat equation”

H_{t}=z^{2}H_{zz}-(N-1)zH_{z}.

(6.11)

Proof.

Assume that
w
satisfies (
6.9
).
Let
H
be as in the theorem, and, for
l\neq j
,

H_{j}(t,z):=H(t,w)/(z-w_{j}(t)),\quad H_{j,l}(t,z):=H(t,z)/((z-w_{j}(t))(z-w_{l}(t))).

Then by (
6.9
),
(
2.11
), and (
4.12
),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}H(t,z)=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{i}^{\prime}(t)\>H_{j}(t,z)

(6.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(N-1)\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{j}(t)H_{j}(t,z)+2\sum_{l,j:\>l\neq j}\frac{w_{j}(t)w_{l}(t)}{w_{j}(t)-w_{l}(t)}\>H_{j}(t,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(N-1)\sum_{j=1}^{N}(z-(z-w_{j}(t))H_{j}(t,z)+\sum_{l,j:\>l\neq j}\frac{w_{j}(t)w_{l}(t)}{w_{j}(t)-w_{l}(t)}(H_{j}(t,z)-H_{l}(t,z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(N-1)\bigl(z\partial_{z}H(t,z)-N\cdot H(t,z)\bigr)+\sum_{l,j:\>l\neq j}w_{j}(t)w_{l}(t)H_{l,j}(t,z).

Hence, by (
4.14
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}H(t,z)=

\displaystyle\bigl((N-1)z\partial_{z}H(t,z)-(N-1)N\cdot H(t,z)\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\bigl((N-1)N\cdot H(t,z)+z^{2}\partial_{zz}H(t,z)-2(N-1)z\partial_{z}H(t,z)\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z^{2}\partial_{zz}H(t,z)-(N-1)z\partial_{z}H(t,z)

(6.13)

as claimed.

Now assume that (
6.11
) holds. As in the proof of Theorem
2.12
, we obtain from
(
2.15
) and (
6.11
) that for
j=1,\ldots,N
,

w_{j}^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{\partial_{t}H(t,w_{j}(t))}{\partial_{z}H(t,w_{j}(t))}=\frac{-w_{j}(t)^{2}\partial_{zz}H(t,w_{j}(t))}{\partial_{z}H(t,w_{j}(t))}+(N-1)w_{j}(t).

Hence, by (
4.16
),

w_{j}^{\prime}(t)=-2w_{j}(t)^{2}\sum_{l:\>l\neq j}\frac{1}{w_{j}(t)-w_{l}(t)}+(N-1)w_{j}(t),

which is just (
6.9
) as claimed.
∎

As in the preceding sections,
Theorem
6.4
leads to some martingales. For this consider the process
(Y_{t}:=exp(i\sqrt{2}\cdot\tilde{B}_{t}+Nt))_{t\geq 0}

for
some one-dimensional Brownian motion

(\tilde{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
which satisfies

dY_{t}=i\sqrt{2}\cdot Y_{t}\>d\tilde{B}_{t}+(N-1)Y_{t}\>dt.

Dynkin’s formula and a comparison of this SDE with the inverse heat equation (
6.11
) imply:

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
w:[0,\infty[\to C_{N}^{\mathbb{T}}
be any solution of (
6.9
). Then for any one-dimensional Brownian motion

(\tilde{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, the process

\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{N}(e^{Nt}e^{i\sqrt{2}\cdot\tilde{B}_{t}}-w_{j}(t))\Bigr)_{t\geq 0}

is a martingale.

Similar to Theorems
2.17
,
3.15
, and
4.5
, this can be used to compute some expectations:

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
k>0
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
, and
w_{0}=(w_{0,1},\ldots w_{0,N})\in C_{N}^{\mathbb{T}}
.
Let

(\tilde{Z}_{t,k}:=e^{i\widetilde{X}_{t,k}})_{t\geq 0}
be a renormalized particle diffusion on
C_{N}^{\mathbb{T}}
as described above starting in
w_{0}
, and let

(\tilde{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be one-dimensional Brownian motion starting in
y
which is independent from
(\tilde{Z}_{t,k})_{t\geq 0}
.
Then

\mathbb{E}\biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{N}(e^{Nt}e^{i\sqrt{2}\cdot\tilde{B}_{t}}-e^{t/k}\tilde{Z}_{t,k}^{j})\biggr)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(e^{i\sqrt{2}\cdot y}-w_{0,j}).

(6.14)

Proof.

We again use the elementary
symmetric polynomials
e_{l}^{N}(x)
. By Corollaries 3.5 and 3.6 in
[
RV2
]
we know that

\mathbb{E}\bigl(e_{l}^{N}(\tilde{Z}_{t,k})\bigr)=e^{-l(N-l+1/k)t}e_{l}^{N}(w_{0})

(6.15)

for
t\geq 0
,
l=0,\ldots,N
, and
k\in]0,\infty]
, where for
k=\infty
,
\tilde{Z}_{t,k}=w(t)
is a deterministic solution of the ODE
(
6.9
).
Hence, by (
6.15
) and Corollary
6.6
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{N}(e^{Nt}e^{i\sqrt{2}\cdot\tilde{B}_{t}}

\displaystyle-e^{t/k}\tilde{Z}_{t,k}^{j})\biggr)=\sum_{l=0}^{N}(-1)^{l}\mathbb{E}\bigl(e^{(N-l)Nt}e^{i\sqrt{2}\cdot(N-l)\cdot\tilde{B}_{t}}\cdot e^{lt/k}e_{l}^{N}(\tilde{Z}_{t,k})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{N}(-1)^{l}\mathbb{E}\bigl(e^{(N-l)Nt}e^{i\sqrt{2}\cdot(N-l)\cdot\tilde{B}_{t}}\bigr)\cdot\mathbb{E}\bigl(e^{lt/k}e_{l}^{N}(\tilde{Z}_{t,k})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{N}(-1)^{l}\mathbb{E}\bigl(e^{(N-l)Nt}e^{i\sqrt{2}\cdot(N-l)\cdot\tilde{B}_{t}}\bigr)\cdot e_{l}^{N}(w(t))\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{N}(e^{Nt}e^{i\sqrt{2}\cdot\tilde{B}_{t}}-w_{j}(t))\bigr)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(e^{i\sqrt{2}\cdot y}-w_{0,j})

as claimed.

The interior-boundary Strichartz estimate for the Schrödinger equation on the half line revisited Thanks: TÖ’s research is partially supported by the Science Academy’s Young Scientist Award Program (BAGEP 2020)
Bİlge Köksal and Türker Özsarı

Abstract
. It was shown by the second author in [ 24 ] for the biharmonic Schrödinger equation and most recently by Himonas and Mantzavinos [ 14 ] for 2D Schrödinger equation that Fokas method based formulas are capable of defining weak solutions of associated nonlinear initial boundary value problems (ibvps) below the Banach algebra threshold. In view of these results, we revisit the theory of interior-boundary Strichartz estimates for the Schrödinger equation posed on the right half line, considering both Dirichlet and Neumann cases. Finally, we apply these estimates to obtain low regularity solutions for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) with Neumann boundary condition and a coupled system of NLS equations defined on the half line with Dirichlet/Neumann boundary conditions.

(1.1)

\displaystyle y_{t}+Py=f(y),\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0,T),

(1.2)

\displaystyle y(x,0)=y_{0}(x),

(1.3)

\displaystyle[By](t)=g(t),

where
P=-i\partial_{x}^{2}
(formal Schrödinger operator),
f
is the complex valued power type function defined by
f(y)=\lambda|y|^{p}y
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
p>0
, and
B
is a trace operator given by
B=\gamma_{0}
(Dirichlet trace) or
B=\gamma_{1}
(Neumann trace) but more general boundary conditions could also be considered. Solutions of the nonlinear problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) can be obtained by applying a fixed point theorem to the linear solution operator, which is constructed based on a formula for solutions of an associated linear ibvp. The latter problem is usually studied via a decompose-and-reunify approach. In this approach, one decomposes the problem into three subproblems (i) a homogeneous Cauchy problem with no interior source, (ii) a nonhomogeneous Cauchy problem with zero initial value, and (iii) an ibvp with zero initial and interior data. Spatial regularity of both types of Cauchy problems for NLS are widely studied in the literature, see for instance Cazenave’s book
[
6
]
- a classical reference on this topic. However, much less effort was given for the temporal regularity of these Cauchy problems as well as the spatial regularity of the ibvp with an inhomogeneous boundary datum. We are aware of some approaches regarding the treatment of the ibvp for the half line problem. Holmer
[
16
]
studied this problem by constructing a boundary forcing operator based on the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral, an approach that was previously applied to the ibvp for the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation posed on the half line
[
8
]
. Some recent papers studied the same ibvp by analyzing the solution formula constructed with one of the traditional integral transforms. For instance, Bona-Sun-Zhang
[
5
]
used the Laplace transform in temporal variable, and Esquivel-Hayashi-Kaikina
[
9
]
used the Fourier sine transform. Finally, Fokas-Himonas-Mantzavinos
[
11
]
introduced an approach utilizing the integral representation formula obtained through the unified transform method of Fokas
[
10
]
.

In
[
11
]
, authors treated (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) at the high regularity level (i.e., in
H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
s>1/2
) and obtained estimates in the
L_{t}^{\infty}(0,T;H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
norm. In this setting,
H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is a Banach algebra, i.e.,

|\phi\psi|_{H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\lesssim|\phi|_{H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}|\psi|_{H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\text{ for }\phi,\psi\in{H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})},

and therefore handling the nonlinearities via contraction is relatively easier. In the low regularity setting
s\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
looses its algebra structure, and estimates in the
L_{t}^{\infty}(0,T;H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
norm are not good enough to perform the associated nonlinear analysis. The classical method in the theory of nonlinear dispersive PDEs for dealing with this difficulty is to prove Strichartz type estimates in mixed norm function spaces
L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T;H_{x}^{{s,r}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
, where
(\lambda,r)
satisfies a special admissibility condition intrinsic to the underlying evolution operator. Holmer
[
16
]
and Bona, et al.
[
5
]
gave proofs of such estimates for the Dirichlet problem in the low regularity setting by analyzing the representation formulas obtained through Riemann–Liouville fractional integral and Laplace transform, respectively.

Strichartz estimates, first noted in
[
27
]
within the framework of the Fourier restriction problem, are a group of inequalities for linear dispersive PDEs that allow us to bound the size and decay of solutions in mixed norm Lebesgue-Sobolev spaces. These estimates are mostly established for Cauchy type problems where the spatial domain is the whole Euclidean space. The results on other geometries are rather limited, and not as strong. In these results, either the given geometry has no boundary (e.g., a boundaryless manifold) or else the boundary conditions are set to zero. Therefore, the estimates are still given with respect to only initial and interior data. On the other hand, Strichartz estimates for inhomogeneous ibvps are rare, and there are relatively much fewer work in this direction.

Strichartz estimates generally rely on local-in-time dispersive estimates. In second author’s work
[
24
]
on the biharmonic nonlinear Schrödinger equation (BNLS) with Dirichlet-Neumann boundary conditions, it was shown that these estimates can also be proven through the analysis of the representation formula obtained via the Fokas method. Most recently, Himonas and Mantzavinos
[
14
]
made use of the same idea for obtaining the low regularity solutions of NLS posed on the half plane with Dirichlet boundary condition. In view of these two papers, one can also expect that recent UTM based
L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
estimates of Fokas, et al.
[
11
]
should extend to UTM based
L_{t}^{\lambda}H_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
estimates in the one dimensional setting. The goal of this paper is to revisit the one dimensional theory, prove Strichartz estimates and apply them to NLS and a system of coupled NLS equations defined on the half line with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions. The Neumann case is a topic which was not treated also with other methods;
[
5
]
and
[
16
]
only considered the Dirichlet case. The second author’s paper
[
3
]
and later Himonas, et. al.
[
15
]
treated the Neumann problem with Laplace transform and UTM based formulas, respectively, however both of these papers handle only the high regularity solutions (i.e.,
s>1/2
).

In this section, we review the decompose-and-reunify algorithm to study (

Abusing the notation, we first write the associated linear nonhomogeneous problem:

(2.1a)

\displaystyle y_{t}+Py=f(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0,T),

(2.1b)

\displaystyle y(x,0)=y_{0}(x),

(2.1c)

\displaystyle[By](t)=g(t),

where

We fix spatial bounded extension operators

\mathbb{R}_{+}

(2.2a)

\displaystyle v_{t}+Pv=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,T),

(2.2b)

\displaystyle v(x,0)=y_{0}^{*}(x),

(2.3a)

\displaystyle z_{t}+Pv=f^{*}(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,T),

(2.3b)

\displaystyle z(x,0)=0,

(2.4a)

\displaystyle u_{t}+Pu=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0,T^{\prime}),

(2.4b)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=0,

(2.4c)

\displaystyle[Bu](t)=h(t).

In the above equations,
y_{0}^{*}
and
f^{*}
are spatial extensions of initial and interior data,

h(t)\equiv g(t)-[Bv](t)-[Bz](t)

in which for convenience we extend the RHS beyond the given time interval
(0,T)
such that it is zero for
t>T^{\prime}
for some
T^{\prime}>T
. The condition,

is convenient for a smooth transition to zero so that the given regularity level of
h

(0,T)
is preserved on the extended interval. Now, the solution of (
2.1a
)-(
2.1c

(2.5)

y=v|_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0,T)}+z|_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0,T)}+u|_{(0,T)}.

Note that we subtract the boundary traces associated with the two Cauchy problems when we define
h
, therefore one needs to know existence of these traces. A representation formula for the solution of the homogeneous Cauchy problem (
2.2a
)-(
2.2b
) is formally given via Fourier transform as

(2.6)

\begin{split}v(x,t)&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{ikx-ik^{2}t}\widehat{y_{0}^{*}}(k)dk.\end{split}

We will use the notation
v(x,t)=e^{-tP}y_{0}^{*}(x)
to denote the solution of the homogeneous Cauchy problem. The above formula is well defined for
y_{0}^{*}\in\mathcal{S},
and one has

\hat{v}(k,t)=e^{-ik^{2}t}\widehat{y_{0}^{*}}(k)
for
y_{0}^{*}\in\mathcal{S}
, which implies the conservation property
|e^{-tP}y_{0}^{*}|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}=|y_{0}^{*}|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}
for
y_{0}^{*}\in\mathcal{S}
. Since
\mathcal{S}
is dense in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
e^{-tP}
easily extends to a group of isometries on
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
(still denoted same). Moreover, we have a Duhamel formulation for the solution of (
2.3a
)-(
2.3b
):

(2.7)

z(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})P}{f^{*}}(x,t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}.

The solution of the ibvp (
2.4a
)-(
2.4c
) with
B=\gamma_{0}
(Dirichlet b.c.), and zero initial and interior data is given by the Fokas method as a complex integral
[
10
]
:

(2.8)

u(x,t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial D^{+}}e^{ikx-ik^{2}t}k\tilde{h}(k^{2},T^{\prime})dk,

where
D^{+}=\{k\in\mathbb{C}_{+}\,|\,\Re(ik^{2})<0\},
its boundary
\partial D^{+}
is positively oriented (see Figure
1
), and
\tilde{h}(k^{2},T^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}e^{ik^{2}s}h(s)ds.

The solution in the case
B=\gamma_{1}
(Neumann b.c.) takes the form

(2.9)

u(x,t)=-\frac{i}{\pi}\int_{\partial D^{+}}e^{ikx-ik^{2}t}\tilde{h}(k^{2},T^{\prime})dk.

We will use the notation
\mathcal{T}_{B}(t)h
to denote the solution of the ibvp, namely to denote the right hand side of (
2.8
) or (
2.9
) (depending on
B
) obtained through the Fokas method.
Therefore, we can rewrite (
2.5
) as follows:

(2.10)

\begin{split}y(t)=\left.e^{-tP}y_{0}^{*}\right|_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0,T)}+\left.\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})P}{f^{*}}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right|_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0,T)}+\left.\mathcal{T}_{B}(t)h\right|_{(0,T)}.\end{split}

Boundary type Strichartz estimates for Fokas formulas

In this section, we state the Strichartz estimates for Fokas method formulas representing solutions of the simplified ibvp.

Theorem 3.5

(Dirichlet b.c.)
.

Let
s\geq 0
,
h\in H_{t}^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}(\mathbb{R})

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
3.5

3.3.1.
Splitting the solution of the ibvp

In order to prove the Strichartz estimates, we first split the ibvp solution
u
in two parts by using the definition and relevant parametrization of the boundary of
D^{+}
. So, we have

(3.7)

\begin{split}u(x,t)&=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial D^{+}}e^{ikx-ik^{2}t}k\tilde{h}(k^{2},T^{\prime})dk\\
&=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\infty}^{0}e^{-kx+ik^{2}t}k\hat{h}(k^{2})dk+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{ikx-ik^{2}t}k\hat{h}(-k^{2})dk\\
&\equiv u_{1}(x,t)+u_{2}(x,t).\end{split}

Sometimes, we will write
\mathcal{T}_{B,i}(t)h
to denote
u_{i}(\cdot,t)
,
i=1,2
.

Note that in the above decomposition, we in addition used the fact that
\tilde{h}(k^{2},T^{\prime})=\hat{h}(-k^{2})
which follows from the support condition
supp\,h\subset[0,T^{\prime})
. This relation is of particular importance for relating the estimates in the next two sections to the Sobolev norm of the boundary input.

3.3.2.
Analysis on the imaginary axis: oscillatory kernel

In this section, we will prove Strichartz estimates for
u_{1}
. To this end, we set a function
H_{1}
which is defined to be the inverse Fourier transform of the function below:

(3.8)

\hat{H}_{1}(k)\equiv\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{\pi}k\hat{h}(k^{2}),&\hbox{if $k\geq 0$;}\\
0,&\hbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Then, upon changing the order of integrals, we can represent
u_{1}
as

(3.9)

\begin{split}u_{1}(x,t)&=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\infty}^{0}e^{-kx+ik^{2}t}k\hat{h}(k^{2})dk=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-kx+ik^{2}t}\hat{H}_{1}(k)dk\\
&=\lim_{b\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{b}e^{-kx+ik^{2}t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-ik\tau}H_{1}(\tau)d\tau dk\\
&=\lim_{b\rightarrow\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ell(\tau;x,t,b)H_{1}(\tau)d\tau,\end{split}

where

(3.10)

\ell(\tau;x,t,b)=\int_{0}^{b}e^{-kx+ik^{2}t-ik\tau}dk=\int_{0}^{b}e^{i\phi(k;\tau,t)}\psi(k;x)dk.

In the oscillatory integral (
3.10
),
\psi(k;x)\equiv e^{-kx}
is the amplitude function and

\phi(k;\tau,t)\equiv k^{2}t-{k}\tau=t\varphi(k)-k\tau

is the phase function with
\varphi(k)=k^{2}
.

Definition 3.9
.

The function
\ell
in (
3.9
) will be referred to as the
kernel of the representation
.

We first recall the following lemma from the oscillatory integral theory:

Lemma 3.10

(
[
18
]
)
.

Let
I(\tau,t,k)=\int_{0}^{k}e^{i(t\varphi(k^{\prime})-k^{\prime}\tau)}dk^{\prime}
with
\varphi(k^{\prime})={(k^{\prime})}^{2}
. Then

|I(\tau,t,k)|\leq c_{\varphi}|t|^{-1/2},\tau,t,k\in\mathbb{R}

where
c_{\varphi}>0
is a constant independent of
\tau,t,k
.

Now, we can state the following decay estimate for the kernel of the representation:

Lemma 3.11
.

The kernel defined by (
3.10
) satisfies the following dispersive estimate:

\left|\ell(\tau;x,t,b)\right|\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{|t|}},\quad t\neq 0,\text{ uniformly in }x,b\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

We set
\Phi(k;\tau,t)\equiv\int_{0}^{k}e^{i\phi(k^{\prime};\tau,t)}dk^{\prime}
. Then,

\ell(\tau;x,t,b)=\int_{0}^{b}\left[\frac{d}{dk}\Phi(k;\tau,t)\right]\psi(k;x)dk.

Integrating in the RHS, using
\Phi(0;\tau,t)=0
, and estimating
|\ell(\tau;x,t,b)|
, we get

\left|\ell(\tau;x,t,b)\right|\leq|\Phi(b;\tau,t)\psi(b;x)|+\int_{0}^{b}|\Phi(k;\tau,t)|\left|\frac{d}{dk}\psi(k;x)\right|dk.

By Lemma
3.10
, we have
|\Phi(k;\tau,t)|\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{|t|}}
for all
k\in[0,b]
, where
c
only depends on
\varphi
and is independent of free parameters and
b
. Moreover, we observe that
|\psi(b;x)|\leq 1
uniformly in
x
and
b
, and

\int_{0}^{b}\left|\frac{d}{dk}\psi(k;x)\right|dk={x}\int_{0}^{b}e^{-{k}x}dk=(1-e^{-{b}x})\leq 1

uniformly in
x
and
b
.
Hence, the result follows.
∎

It is immediate from the definition of
u_{1}
and the above lemma that

(3.11)

|u_{1}(\cdot,t)|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}|H_{1}|_{L_{t}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

The following estimate is due to the fact that the Laplace transform is a bounded operator from
L_{k}^{2}(0,\infty)
into
L_{k}^{2}(0,\infty)
:

(3.12)

|u_{1}(\cdot,t)|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\lesssim|H_{1}|_{L_{t}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

Interpolating between (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) via the Riesz-Thorin Interpolation Theorem, we get

(3.13)

|u_{1}(\cdot,t)|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\lesssim t^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}|H_{1}|_{L_{t}^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})},\quad 2\leq r\leq\infty.

The above estimate plays the key role in establishing the Strichartz estimates. This is rather standard. Indeed, (
3.13
), the admissibility condition (
3.1
) and the Riesz potential inequalities imply

(3.14)

\left|\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\mathcal{T}_{B,1}(t-s)\theta(s)ds\right|_{L^{\lambda}(0,T^{\prime};L^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\lesssim|\theta|_{L^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,T^{\prime};L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}

for any
\theta\in L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,T^{\prime};L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
. Now, let
\psi\in C_{c}([0,T^{\prime});\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))

(3.15)

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\mathcal{T}_{B,1}(t)h,\psi(t))_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}dt\right|\\
=&\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-kx+ik^{2}t}\hat{H}_{1}(k)dk\overline{\psi}(x,t)dxdt\right|\\
=&\lim_{b\rightarrow\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{b}\hat{H}_{1}(k)\overline{\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-kx-ik^{2}t}\psi(x,t)dxdt}dk\right|\\
=&\lim_{b\rightarrow\infty}\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{H}_{1}(\tau)\overline{\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{\ell}(\tau;x,t,b)\psi(x,t)dxdt}d\tau\right|.\end{split}

We prove an auxiliary result now.

Lemma 3.12
.

If
\psi\in C_{c}([0,T^{\prime});\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
and
I(\tau;b)=\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{\ell}(\tau;x,t,b)\psi(x,t)dxdt
, then

(3.16)

\left|I(\tau;b)\right|_{L_{\tau}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq c|\psi|_{L^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,T^{\prime};L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}.

Proof.

We can rewrite the LHS of (
3.16
) as

(3.17)

\begin{split}\left|I(\tau;b)\right|_{L_{\tau}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(x,t)\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{\psi}(y,s)L(x,y,t,s;b)dydsdxdt,\end{split}

where
L(x,y,t,s;b)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\overline{\ell}(\tau;x,t,b){\ell}(\tau;y,s,b)d\tau.
By using the finite line Fourier transform, we have

(3.18)

\begin{split}L(x,y,t,s;b)&=\int_{0}^{b}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{b}e^{-kx-ik^{2}t+ik\tau}e^{-\tilde{k}y+i\tilde{k}^{2}s-i\tilde{k}\tau}dkd\tilde{k}d\tau\\
&=2\pi\int_{0}^{b}e^{-k(x+y)-ik^{2}(t-s)}dk.\end{split}

By using the same arguments in Lemma
3.11
, we deduce that

|L(x,y,t,s;b)|\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{|t-s|}},\quad t\neq s,\text{ uniformly in }x,y,b\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

The above estimate and arguments used in the proof of (
3.14
) give the result.
∎

Using Lemma
3.12
, we find that the RHS of (

) is bounded by

(3.19)

c|H_{1}|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}|\psi|_{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,T^{\prime};L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}.

Hence, by duality we establish the case
s=0
:

(3.20)

|\mathcal{T}_{B,1}(t)h|_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T^{\prime};L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\leq c|H_{1}|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

Remark 3.13
.

The integral representation formula on
\partial D_{+}
obtained via the Fokas method has the remarkable property that one can easily differentiate with respect to

and

.

In view of Remark

, differentiating

into the integrand. Therefore, the above arguments can be repeated for

|\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{B,1}(t)h|_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T^{\prime};L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\leq c|\tilde{H}_{1}|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

where

for

. From (

(3.22)

|\mathcal{T}_{B,1}(t)h|_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T^{\prime};H_{x}^{1,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\leq c|H_{1}|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

Now, we interpolate between (
3.20

(3.23)

|\mathcal{T}_{B,1}(t)h|_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T^{\prime};H_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\leq c|H_{1}|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}

. Finally, we can iterate and derive the same estimate for all

Returning to the original boundary input is easy. Namely,
H_{1}
and

(3.24)

\begin{split}&|H_{1}|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(1+k^{2})^{s}|\hat{H}_{1}(k)|^{2}dk\\

It follows from (

|u_{1}|_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T^{\prime};H_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\lesssim|h|_{H_{t}^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}(\mathbb{R})}.

Analysis on the real axis: ibvp to Cauchy switch

Here, we set a function

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{2}+Pu_{2}=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,T^{\prime}),

\displaystyle u_{2}(x,0)=H_{2}(x).

(3.29)

It follows from (

|u_{2}|_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T;H_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\lesssim|h|_{H_{t}^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}(\mathbb{R})}.

3.5.
Temporal regularity of spatial traces for Cauchy problems

In this section, we overview two theorems regarding the time trace estimates of solutions of the homogeneous and nonhomogeneous Cauchy problems, respectively.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
s\geq 0
and
y_{0}^{*}\in H^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R})
. Then
v(t)=e^{-tP}y_{0}^{*}
defines a solution to (
2.6
) such that
\partial_{x}v\in C(\mathbb{R}_{x},H_{t}^{\frac{2s-1}{4}}(0,T))
for
T\in(0,\infty]
and

(3.43)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}v(x,\cdot)|_{H_{t}^{\frac{2s-1}{4}}(0,T)}\leq\sqrt{2}|y_{0}^{*}|_{H_{x}^{s}}.

Proof.

By the solution representation of the homogeneous Cauchy problem we have

\displaystyle\partial_{x}v(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\xi x-i\xi^{2}t}\xi\widehat{y_{0}^{*}}(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\xi x-i\xi^{2}t}\xi\widehat{y_{0}^{*}}(\xi)d\xi+\frac{i}{2\pi}\int^{0}_{-\infty}e^{i\xi x-i\xi^{2}t}\xi\widehat{y_{0}^{*}}(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle\eqqcolon I(x,t)+II(x,t).

First we estimate
II
. By the change of variable
\xi=-\sqrt{\tau}
we rewrite
II
as

(3.44)

II(x,t)=\frac{i}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-i\sqrt{\tau}x-i\tau t}\widehat{y_{0}^{*}}(-\sqrt{\tau})d\tau.

Then
II
is the inverse (in time) Fourier transform of the function

(3.45)

\widehat{II}^{(t)}(x,\tau)\coloneqq\frac{i}{2}\chi_{[0,\infty)}(\tau)e^{-i\sqrt{\tau}x}\widehat{y_{0}^{*}}(-\sqrt{\tau}).

So by the definition of the Sobolev norm,

\displaystyle|II(x,\cdot)|^{2}_{H_{t}^{\frac{2s-1}{4}}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+\tau^{2})^{\frac{2s-1}{4}}|\widehat{II_{1}}^{(t)}(x,\tau)|^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+\tau^{2})^{\frac{2s-1}{4}}|\widehat{y_{0}^{*}}(-\sqrt{\tau})|^{2}d\tau

(3.46)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{0}(1+\xi^{4})^{\frac{2s-1}{4}}|\widehat{y_{0}^{*}}(\xi)|^{2}\xi d\xi\leq\frac{1}{2}|y_{0}^{*}|^{2}_{H^{s}}.

By a similar argument, one can show that

(3.47)

|I(x,\cdot)|_{H_{t}^{\frac{2s-1}{4}}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|y_{0}^{*}|_{H_{x}^{s}}.

Then (
3.5
) and (
3.47
) together imply the desired result.
∎

Next, we estimate time traces of the solution of the nonhomogeneous Cauchy problem.

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
(\lambda,r)
be Schrödinger admissible,
m\in\{0,1\}
and
z
be defined by (

). Then, the following properties hold:

(1)

Let
f^{*}\in L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r^{\prime}}
,
s\in\mathbb{Z}
, then

\partial_{x}^{m}z\in C(\mathbb{R};\dot{H}_{t}^{\frac{2s+1-2m}{4}}(0,T))

for
T\leq\infty
and

(3.48)

\sup_{x}|\partial_{x}^{m}z(x,\cdot)|_{\dot{H}_{t}^{\frac{2s+1-2m}{4}}(0,T)}\lesssim|f^{*}|_{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r^{\prime}}}.

(2)

Let
f^{*}\in L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}H_{x}^{s,r^{\prime}}
,
s\in[0,\infty)
, then
\partial_{x}^{m}z\in C(\mathbb{R};H_{t}^{\frac{2s+1-2m}{4}}(0,T))
for

and

(3.49)

\sup_{x}|\partial_{x}^{m}z(x,\cdot)|_{H_{t}^{\frac{2s+1-2m}{4}}(0,T)}\lesssim(1+c_{T})|f^{*}|_{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}H_{x}^{s,r^{\prime}}}

Remark 3.17
.

The homogeneous and inhomogeneous time trace estimates in the above theorem can be considered as extensions of estimates in
[
16
]
to a larger range of

Moreover, we refrain from differentiating the solution formula in time to obtain higher order time trace estimates. This generally cause additional time trace terms appearing at the right hand side of (

[
5
]
. Instead, we use an iterative argument whose each step relies on boundedness of

Remark 3.18
.

{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}H_{x}^{s,r^{\prime}}}

{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,T;H_{x}^{s,r^{\prime}}})

Lemma 3.19

(
[
17

19
]

For
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\otimes}\equiv\big\{h\,|\,h(x,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}^{1}(x)h_{i}^{2}(t),h_{i}^{1},h_{i}^{2}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\big\}

(3.50)

\begin{split}2z(x,t)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})P}f(x,t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}-2\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})P}f(x,t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\\
&-\frac{i}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\tau}\Big[\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\epsilon<|\xi^{2}+\tau|<\frac{1}{\epsilon}}e^{ix\xi}\frac{\widehat{f}(\xi,\tau)}{\xi^{2}+\tau}d\xi\Big]d\tau.\end{split}

Proof.

This lemma is an analogue of a proposition that was given by Kenig, et al. in

for the Korteweg-de Vries equation. Its adaptation to the Schrödinger equation was given in Holmer

without proof. We give a proof for completeness. We write the last term in (

(3.51)

-\frac{i}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\xi}\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{\epsilon<|\xi^{2}+\tau|<\frac{1}{\epsilon}}e^{it\tau}\frac{\widehat{f}(\xi,\tau)}{\xi^{2}+\tau}d\tau d\xi.

Now by the Fourier transform of the signum function and a change of variable we have

\displaystyle\int_{\epsilon<|\xi^{2}+\tau|<\frac{1}{\epsilon}}e^{it\tau}\frac{\widehat{f}(\xi,\tau)}{\xi^{2}+\tau}d\tau

4.
Nonlinear Applications

We will prove local well-posedness of the NLS and the coupled system of NLS equations on the halfline utilizing trace and Strichartz estimates given in previous sections. The local wellposedness of the Dirichlet and Neumann problems for the single NLS were studied before in the high regularity setting in
[
11
]
and
[
15
]
, respectively, via Fokas method based formulas. Dirichlet problem was studied in the low regularity setting with other methods (see e.g., see
[
5
]
,
[
16
]
). Therefore, here we will prove the local well-posedness of only the Neumann problem in the low regularity setting
s\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
using Theorem
3.6
. Then we move on to the coupled system of NLS equations and establish its wellposedness, too.

One should not expect a local wellposesness result for negative indices in light of the theory of Cauchy problems. Indeed, let
u
be a solution of the NLS on the real line:

(4.1)

u_{t}+Pu+f(u)=0.

Then,
u_{\epsilon}(x,t)\doteq\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{p}}u(\epsilon^{-1}x,\epsilon^{-2}t)
is also a solution of the same eqution. Moreover,
u
solves (
4.1
) on
(0,T)
iff
u_{\epsilon}
solves (
4.1
) on
(0,\epsilon^{2}T)
. The corresponding initial data satisfy
\displaystyle|u_{\epsilon}(0)|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s}}=\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}-s}|u(0)|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s}}.
It is clear that if
s<s^{*}\doteq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}
, then both
|u_{\epsilon}(0)|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s}}
and the life span of
u_{\epsilon}
tends to zero as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
. This suggests that the problem may be locally illposed for
s<s^{*}
and locally wellposed otherwise. Illposedness is easier to establish for
0\leq s<s^{*}
whenever
s^{*}>0
(
L^{2}
-
supercritical
) or for
s<s^{*}=0
(
L^{2}
-
critical
). For the focusing problems, in the case
s^{*}\geq 0
, one can construct solutions that blow up in given arbitrarily small time. However, if
p<4
, then
s^{*}<0
, in which case an explicit blow-up solution cannot be constructed. So, the question is what is the range of
s
for which local wellposedness fails when
s^{*}<0
(
L^{2}
-
subcritical
). It was shown in
[
7
]
that the solution operator is not uniformly continuous for
s<\max(0,s^{*})
. This motivates us to consider the wellposedness problem for
s\geq\max(0,s^{*})
also in the present context of an ibvp.

{T_{0}}
. Now take
R=2c_{T_{0}}|y_{0}|_{H^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}
. Let
(\lambda,r)
be the particular admissible pair

\lambda\coloneqq\frac{4(p+2)}{p(1-2s)},r\coloneqq\frac{p+2}{1+sp}.

Then by the fractional Leibniz and chain rules:

|f(y^{*})|_{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,{T_{0}};\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq T_{0}^{\theta}|y^{*}|^{p+1}_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,{T_{0}};\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq T_{0}^{\theta}|y|^{p+1}_{X_{T_{0}}},

|f(y^{*})|_{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,{T_{0}};L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq T_{0}^{\theta}|y^{*}|^{p}_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,{T_{0}};\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}))}|y^{*}|_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,{T_{0}};L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq T_{0}^{\theta}|y|^{p+1}_{X_{T_{0}}}

where
\theta\coloneqq 1-\frac{p(1-2s)}{4}
. We have

|\Theta y|_{X_{T_{0}}}\leq\frac{R}{2}+c_{T_{0}}\big(T_{0}^{\theta}R^{p+1}+|g|_{H_{t}^{\frac{2s-1}{4}}(0,{T_{0}})}\big).

From the linear theory,
c_{T_{0}}
is non-increasing as
{T_{0}}
gets smaller. Thus we can take
{T_{0}}
small such that

c_{T_{0}}\big(T_{0}^{\theta}R^{p+1}+|g|_{H_{t}^{\frac{2s-1}{4}}(0,{T_{0}})}\big)\leq\frac{R}{2}

so that we have
\Theta y\in X_{T_{0}}
. Now we have to show that
\Theta
is a contraction. Take
y_{1},y_{2}\in X_{T_{0}}
. Then

\displaystyle d(\Theta y_{1},\Theta y_{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim|f(y_{1}^{*})-f(y_{2}^{*})|_{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,{T_{0}};H_{x}^{s,r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim T_{0}^{\theta}\Big(|y_{1}^{*}|^{p}_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,{T_{0}};\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}))}+|y_{2}^{*}|^{p}_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,{T_{0}};\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}))}\Big)|y_{1}^{*}-y_{2}^{*}|_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,{T_{0}};L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}))}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq T_{0}^{\theta}\big(|y_{1}|^{p}_{X_{T_{0}}}+|y_{2}|^{p}_{X_{T_{0}}}\big)d(y_{1},y_{2}).

So taking
{T_{0}}
small enough we can make
\Theta
a contraction. Then by Banach fixed point theorem
\Theta
has a unique fixed point
y\in X_{T_{0}}
. From the linear theory, it also follows that
y\in C([0,{T_{0}}];H^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
.

Remark 4.3
.

Note that the above arguments apply in the subcritical case
\theta>0
. For the critical case
\theta=0
the term
T_{0}^{\theta}
on the RHS is lost but one can still argue in a similar manner with the additional assumption that initial data is small.

Uniqueness.

Let
y_{1},y_{2}\in L^{\lambda}_{t}([0,{T_{0}}],H_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
be two solutions. Then by (
4.6
) we have

d(y_{1},y_{2})=d(\Theta y_{1},\Theta y_{2})\lesssim T_{0}^{\theta}(|y_{1}|^{p}_{X_{T_{0}}}+|y_{2}|^{p}_{X_{T_{0}}})d(y_{1},y_{2})

Hence
T_{0}
can be taken small enough to guarantee uniqueness for
\theta>0
. For the critical case, a similar argument applies for small initial data.

Continuous Dependence.
Let
B
be bounded subset of
H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\times H_{t}^{\frac{2s-1}{4}}(0,T_{0})
and let
(y_{1},g_{1}),(y_{2},g_{2})\in B
with corresponding solutions
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
in
X_{T_{0}}
, respectively. Then
w\equiv w_{1}-w_{2}
is a solution to the following problem

(4.7a)

\displaystyle iw_{t}+w_{xx}=f(w_{1})-f(w_{2}),\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0,T_{0}),

(4.7b)

\displaystyle w(x,0)=(y_{1}-y_{2})(x)\in H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

(4.7c)

\displaystyle w_{x}(0,t)=(g_{1}-g_{2})(t)\in H_{t}^{\frac{2s-1}{4}}(0,T_{0}).

Then we have

\displaystyle|w|_{X_{T_{0}}}\lesssim

\displaystyle|y_{1}^{*}-y_{2}^{*}|_{H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+|f(w_{1}^{*})-f(w_{2}^{*})|_{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,{T_{0}};H_{x}^{s,r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}))}+c_{T_{0}}|h_{1}-h_{2}|_{H_{t}^{\frac{2s-1}{4}}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|y_{1}-y_{2}|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+T_{0}^{\theta}\Big(|w_{1}|^{p}_{X_{T_{0}}}+{|w_{2}|}^{p}_{X_{T_{0}}}\Big)|w_{1}-w_{2}|_{X_{T_{0}}}+c_{T_{0}}|g_{1}-g_{2}|_{H_{t}^{\frac{2s-1}{4}}(0,{T_{0}})}.

Then taking
{T_{0}}
small enough (provided
\theta>0
) we obtain

|w_{1}-w_{2}|_{Y_{T_{0}}}\lesssim|(y_{1},g_{1})-(y_{2},g_{2})|_{B}.
A similar argument also applies to
\theta=0
case by assuming initial data are small.

Blowup Alternative.
Let
S
be the set of
T_{0}\in(0,T]
such that there is a unique local solution in
L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T_{0};H_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
. We claim that if
T_{max}\coloneqq\sup_{T_{0}\in S}T_{0}<T
then
|y(t)|_{H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\rightarrow\infty
as
t\rightarrow T_{max}
. Suppose for contradiction that there is a sequence
(t_{n})\subset S
such that
t_{n}\rightarrow T
and
|y(t_{n})|_{H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq M
for some
M>0
. Given any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is a unique solution
y_{1}
of the problem on
[0,t_{n}]
. Now consider the following problem

(4.8a)

\displaystyle iy_{t}+y_{xx}=\kappa|y|^{p}y\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(t_{n},T),

(4.8b)

\displaystyle y(x,t_{n})=y_{0}(x),

(4.8c)

\displaystyle y_{x}(0,t)=g(t)

Then (
4.8
) has a unique local solution
y_{2}
on some interval
[t_{n},t_{n}+\delta]
. Now choose
n
such that
t_{n}+\delta>T_{max}
. Define
y
such that
y\equiv y_{1}
on
[0,t_{n})
and
y\equiv y_{2}
on
[t_{n},t_{n}+\delta]
. Then
y
is a local solution on
[0,t_{n}+\delta]
which is a contradiction.
∎

4.2.
Coupled system of NLS equations

In this section we prove local well-posedness for the cubic coupled NLS ibvp on the half line. We consider the following system:

(4.9a)

\displaystyle ip_{t}+p_{xx}

\displaystyle=a|q|^{2}p,

\displaystyle(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0,T),

(4.9b)

\displaystyle p(x,0)

\displaystyle=p_{0}(x),

(4.9c)

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}p(0,t)

\displaystyle=g(t),

(4.9d)

\displaystyle iq_{t}+q_{xx}

\displaystyle=b|p|^{2}q,

\displaystyle(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0,T)

(4.9e)

\displaystyle q(x,0)

\displaystyle=q_{0}(x),

(4.9f)

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}q(0,t)

\displaystyle=h(t),

where
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
and
m,n\in\{0,1\}
.

Theorem 4.4

(Low regularity solutions)
.

Let
s\in[0,1/2)
,
\lambda=\frac{8}{1-2s}
and
r=\frac{4}{1+2s}
. Let
p_{0},q_{0}\in H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and for
m,n\in\{0,1\}
let
g\in H_{t}^{\frac{2s+1-2m}{4}}(0,T)
and
h\in H_{t}^{\frac{2s+1-2n}{4}}(0,T)
. Then there is
T_{0}\in(0,T]
such that the system (
4.9
) has a unique solution

(p,q)\in L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T_{0};H_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))^{2}\cap C([0,T_{0}];H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))^{2}

Moreover the data-to-solution map is locally Lipschitz continuous.

Proof.

We will again use a fixed point argument. Let
\Psi_{m}[y_{0},g,f]
denote the solution operator of
2.1
for
B=\gamma_{m}
.
Then it is easy to see that a solution of (
4.9
)
corresponds to a fixed point of the operator

(4.10)

\Lambda:(p,q)\mapsto\Big(\Psi_{m}[p_{0},g,a|q|^{2}p],\Psi_{n}[q_{0},h,b|p|^{2}q]\Big)

We define the space
X_{T_{0}}
for the sought-after solution as

X_{T_{0}}\coloneqq\{(p,q)\in L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T_{0};H_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))^{2}:|p|_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T_{0};H_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}+|q|_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T_{0};H_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\leq R\}

equipped with the metric

d((p_{1},q_{1}),(p_{2},q_{2}))=|p_{1}-p_{2}|_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T_{0};L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}+|q_{1}-q_{2}|_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T_{0};L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}.

So that
(X_{T_{0}},d)
is a complete metric space. We claim that there is
T_{0}>0
such that
\Lambda
has a fixed point in
X_{T_{0}}
.
We have, similar to the proof of Theorem
4.1
,

\displaystyle|\Lambda(p,q)|_{X_{T_{0}}}\leq

\displaystyle c_{T_{0}}\big(|p_{0}|_{H^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+|a|q|^{2}p|_{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,{T_{0}};H_{x}^{s,r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}+|g|_{H_{t}^{\frac{2s+1-2m}{4}}(0,{T_{0}})}

\displaystyle+|q_{0}|_{H^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+|b|p|^{2}q|_{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,{T_{0}};H_{x}^{s,r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}+|h|_{H_{t}^{\frac{2s+1-2n}{4}}(0,{T_{0}})}\big).

Thus, for the invariance of
X_{T_{0}}
, it will suffice to show the following estimate

(4.11)

||p|^{2}q|_{L^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,T_{0};H^{s,r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+||q|^{2}p|_{L^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,T_{0};H^{s,r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\lesssim c(T_{0},R,{\theta})

with
c(T_{0},R,{\theta})
getting smaller as
T_{0}
gets smaller. To prove that
\Lambda
is a contraction it is enough to show that for
(p_{1},q_{1})
and
(p_{2},q_{2})
in
X_{T_{0}}
, we have

\displaystyle||q_{1}|^{2}p_{1}-|q_{2}|^{2}p_{2}|_{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,T_{0};L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}+||p_{1}|^{2}q_{1}-|p_{2}|^{2}q_{2}|_{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,T_{0};L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle c(T_{0},R,{\theta})|(p_{1},q_{1})-(p_{2},q_{2})|_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T_{0};L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}.

We choose
\mu
and
\rho
such that

\frac{1}{\lambda^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\mu}+\frac{2}{\lambda},

\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}=\frac{2}{\rho}+\frac{1}{r}
.
We have the Sobolev embedding
\dot{H}^{s,r^{\prime}}\xhookrightarrow{}L^{\rho}
and
\mu<\lambda
. Let

(4.13)

\theta\coloneqq\frac{1}{\mu}-\frac{1}{\lambda}>0

and let

(4.14)

\frac{1}{j}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\rho}+\frac{1}{r}.

Then given
(p,q)\in X_{T_{0}}
, by the generalized Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle|D^{s}(|p^{*}(t)|^{2}q^{*}(t))|_{{L}_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|D^{s}(|p^{*}(t)|^{2})|_{L_{x}^{j}}|q^{*}(t)|_{L_{x}^{\rho}}+||p^{*}(t)|^{2}|_{L_{x}^{\frac{\rho}{2}}}|D^{s}q^{*}(t)|_{L_{x}^{r}}.

The first term on the RHS is estimated as

(4.15)

|D^{s}(|p^{*}(t)|^{2})|_{L_{x}^{j}}\lesssim|D^{s}p^{*}(t)|_{L_{x}^{r}}|\overline{p}^{*}(t)|_{L_{x}^{\rho}}\leq|p^{*}(t)|^{2}_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r}}.

Thus we have

(4.16)

|D^{s}(|p^{*}|^{2}q^{*})|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\lesssim|p^{*}|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r}}^{2}|q^{*}|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r}}\lesssim|p|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}|q|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

Now applying Hölder’s inequality in time, using (
4.16
) and (
4.13
), we get

\displaystyle||p|^{2}q|_{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,T;\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}

\displaystyle\leq||p^{*}|^{2}q^{*}|_{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,T;\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\leq\big||p|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}\big|_{L_{t}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}(0,T_{0})}\big||q|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\big|_{L_{t}^{\mu}(0,T_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq T_{0}^{\theta}|p|^{2}_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T_{0};\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}|q|_{L_{t}^{\lambda}(0,T_{0};\dot{H}_{x}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\leq T_{0}^{\theta}R^{3}.

Using the same
\mu
and
\rho
, applying Hölder inequality twice gives

||p|^{2}q|_{L^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,T;L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\leq T_{0}^{\theta}|p|^{2}_{L^{\lambda}(0,T_{0};\dot{H}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}|q|_{L^{\lambda}(0,T_{0};L^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\leq c_{2}T_{0}^{\theta}R^{3}.

Thus we can choose
T_{0}
and
R
to enforce invariance of
X_{T_{0}}
under
\Lambda
. Now observe that we have

\displaystyle|p_{1}|^{2}q_{1}-|p_{2}|^{2}q_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(q_{1}-q_{2})(|p_{1}|^{2}+|p_{2}|^{2})

(4.17)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(q_{1}+q_{2})\big[(p_{1}-p_{2})(\overline{p_{1}}+\overline{p_{2}})+(p_{1}+p_{2})(\overline{p_{1}}-\overline{p_{2}})\big]

from which we obtain

\displaystyle||p_{1}^{*}|^{2}q_{1}^{*}-|p_{2}^{*}|^{2}q_{2}^{*}|_{L_{t}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(0,T_{0};L_{x}^{r^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|q_{1}-q_{2}|_{L_{t}^{\mu}(0,T_{0};L_{x}^{r})}||p_{1}|^{2}+|p_{2}|^{2}|_{L_{t}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}(0,T_{0};L_{x}^{\frac{\rho}{2}})}

\displaystyle+|p_{1}-p_{2}|_{L_{t}^{\mu}(0,T_{0};L_{x}^{r})}|(\overline{p_{1}}+\overline{p_{2}})(q_{1}+q_{2})|_{L_{t}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}(0,T_{0};L_{x}^{\frac{\rho}{2}})}

\displaystyle+|\overline{p_{1}}-\overline{p_{2}}|_{L_{t}^{\mu}(0,T_{0};L_{x}^{r})}|(p_{1}+p_{2})(q_{1}+q_{2})|_{L_{t}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}(0,T_{0};L_{x}^{\frac{\rho}{2}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\theta}_{0}R^{2}d((p_{1},q_{1}),(p_{2},q_{2})).

So we have proved (
4.11
) and (
4.2
). The uniqueness of the fixed point and the local Lipschitz continuity of the data-to-solution map follows as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Now we will prove local well-posedness for high regularity solutions of (
4.9
). Throughout, we will assume the necessary compatibility conditions between initial and boundary data.

Theorem 4.5

(High regularity solutions)
.

Let
s\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2})-\frac{3}{2}
,
p_{0},q_{0}\in H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and for
m,n\in\{0,1\}
let
g\in H_{t}^{\frac{2s+1-2m}{4}}(0,T)
and
h\in H_{t}^{\frac{2s+1-2n}{4}}(0,T)
satisfying necessary compatibility conditions. Then there is
T_{0}\in(0,T]
such that the system (
4.9
) has a unique solution

(p,q)\in C([0,T_{0}];H_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))^{2}

and the data-to-solution map is locally Lipschitz continuous.

Proof.

We have to prove that the operator
\Lambda
in (
4.10
) is continuous from
Y_{T_{0}}
to itself for some
{T_{0}},R>0
where

Y_{T_{0}}\coloneqq\{(p,q)\in C([0,T_{0}];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))^{2}:|p|_{C([0,T_{0}];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}+|q|_{C([0,T_{0}];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\leq R\}

equipped with the distance

d((p_{1},q_{1}),(p_{2},q_{2}))=|p_{1}-p_{2}|_{C([0,T_{0}];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}+|q_{1}-q_{2}|_{C([0,T_{0}];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}

From the proof of local well-posedness for the Neumann and Dirichlet problems for the NLS in the high regularity case (see
[
11
]
for the Dirichlet problem) one can see that it will suffice to deal only with the nonlinear terms
a|q|^{2}p
and
b|p|^{2}q
. This case will be much simpler than the low regularity case because we can make use of the Banach algebra property. First, we have the following proposition

Proposition 4.6

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let
f\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
, then

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Psi[0,0,f]|_{C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\lesssim T\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|f|_{C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}.

It follows from Proposition
4.6
and the submultiplicativity of the norm that

|\Psi[0,0,a|p|^{2}q]|_{Y_{T_{0}}}\leq cT_{0}(|p|_{Y_{T_{0}}}^{2}|q|_{Y_{T_{0}}}+|q|_{Y_{T_{0}}}^{2}|p|_{Y_{T_{0}}})\leq cT_{0}R^{3},

where
c
does not depend on
T_{0}
or
R
. The same argument can be made for
|\Psi[0,0,b|q|^{2}p]|_{Y_{T_{0}}}
. Thus
T_{0}
and
R
can be taken small enough to make
Y_{T_{0}}
invariant under
\Lambda
.

It follows trivially from (
4.2
) and the algebra property that
\Lambda
can also be a contraction, exhibiting a fixed point
(p,q)\in Y_{T_{0}}
. Uniqueness and continuous dependece of this local solution can be proved in the same way as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Remark 4.8

(Global wellposedness with inhomogeneous boundary data)
.

Global well-posedness for the coupled system is a more difficult problem than it is for the single NLS due to the term
a|q|^{2}p\overline{p_{t}}
that arises upon multiplying equation (
4.9a
) by
\overline{p_{t}}
. It is not easy to control this term, however, it is possible to prove global wellposedness for sufficiently small power indices. Consider for example the problem

(4.18a)

\displaystyle ip_{t}+p_{xx}

\displaystyle=a|q|^{\alpha}p

\displaystyle(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0,T)

(4.18b)

\displaystyle p(x,0)

\displaystyle=p_{0}(x)

(4.18c)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}p(0,t)

\displaystyle=g(t)

(4.18d)

\displaystyle iq_{t}+q_{xx}

\displaystyle=b|p|^{\alpha}q

\displaystyle(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0,T)

(4.18e)

\displaystyle q(x,0)

\displaystyle=q_{0}(x)

(4.18f)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}q(0,t)

\displaystyle=h(t)

with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. Assuming a local solution (
H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
in space) exists, one can prove global wellposedness by showing that
H^{1}
norm of the solution, that is
|p(t)|_{H^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+|q(t)|_{H^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}
does not blow up in finite time. We claim that it is controlled by the sum of
H^{1}
norms of initial and boundary inputs uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
.

Upon multiplying (
4.18a
) with
\overline{p}
, taking imaginary parts, integrating in space-time, using Sobolev trace inequality and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality one obtains:

(4.19)

|p(t)|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\lesssim|p_{0}|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+|g|_{L_{t}^{2}(0,T)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|p(s)|_{H^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}.

Similar arguments applied to the second equation yield

(4.20)

|q(t)|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\lesssim|q_{0}|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+|h|_{L_{t}^{2}(0,T)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|q(s)|_{H^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}.

Note that same multipliers also lead to the estimates

\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{t}|p(t)|^{2}dx\lesssim|g|_{H_{t}^{1}(0,T)}|p(t)|_{H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}

and

\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{t}|q(t)|^{2}dx\lesssim|h|_{H_{t}^{1}(0,T)}|q(t)|_{H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

Multiplying (
4.18a
) by
\overline{p}_{t}
, taking real parts, integrating in space-time, using integration by parts, Sobolev embedding
H_{t}^{1}(0,T)\hookrightarrow L_{t}^{\infty}(0,T)
, Sobolev trace theorem and making use of the above inequalities we get

\displaystyle|p_{x}(t)|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle|p_{0}^{\prime}|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+|g(0)|\cdot|p_{0}(0)|+|g|_{H_{t}^{1}(0,T)}|p(t)|_{H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}

\displaystyle+|g|_{H_{t}^{1}(0,T)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|p(s)|_{H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}

(4.21)

\displaystyle+|a|\cdot|g|_{H_{t}^{1}(0,T)}\cdot\int_{0}^{t}|q(s)|_{H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{\alpha}|p(s)|_{H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}ds.

A similar estimate also holds for the second equation:

\displaystyle|q_{x}(t)|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle|q_{0}^{\prime}|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+|h(0)|\cdot|q_{0}(0)|+|h|_{H_{t}^{1}(0,T)}|q(t)|_{H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}

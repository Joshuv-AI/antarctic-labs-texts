On Lattice Points, Short-Time Estimates, and Global Well-posedness of the Quintic NLS on 𝕋 \mathbb{T} Thanks: The first author was partially supported by the NSF grant DMS-2154031.
Ryan McConnell

Abstract
. We prove and utilize an improvement to the short time estimates of Burq, Gérard, & Tzvetkov [ 7 ] on 𝕋 \mathbb{T} via connecting this estimate to the number of lattice points in thin annuli. As a consequence, we enhance the well-posedness level of the periodic quintic Nonlinear Schrödinger equation to s > 131 624 ∼ 0.21 s>\frac{131}{624}\sim 0.21 , which is an improvement on the results of De Silva, Pavlović, Staffilani, & Tzirakis, [ 12 ] , Li, Wu, & Xu, [ 25 ] , and Schippa [ 26 ] . We also present conditional results, dependent on improvements on the count of lattice points in thin annuli.

1.
Introduction

Consider the periodic mass critical Nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) posed on
\mathbb{T}

(1)

\begin{cases}(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u=\pm|u|^{4}u\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}),\end{cases}

where the equation is
defocusing
when
+
is taken, and
focusing
when
-
is taken. Equation (
1
) is mass critical in the sense that the
L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{T})
norm is scale invariant– if
u
solves (
1
), then
u^{\lambda}(x,t):=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}u(\lambda^{-1}x,\lambda^{-2}t)
solves (
1
) posed on
\lambda\mathbb{T}
and

\|u\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{T})}=\|u^{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\lambda\mathbb{T})}.

While this doesn’t respect the geometry of
\mathbb{T}
like it does for
\mathbb{R}
, this still serves to guide intuition.

Well-posedness of (
1
) is immediate for
s>\frac{1}{2}
, while Bourgain
[
4
]
, with his proof of the
L^{6}_{x,t}
Strichartz estimate, proved local well-posedness for
s>0
. Even though the question of local wellposdness is settled, global existence for the full range
0<s<1
has been a lingering question for
30
years.

With an eye towards global well-posedness, we observe that (
1
) satisfies conservation of Mass and Energy

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}},\qquad E(u)(t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx\pm\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|u(t)|^{6}\,dx=E(u_{0}),

which naturally leads to global existence for the defocusing (as well as the small data focusing) case when
s\geq 1
, yet global well-posedness between these two conservation laws, for
0<s<1
, is a more subtle question. Progress first began with the introduction of Bourgain’s High-Low decomposition
[
5
]
, which requires the presence of a smoothing property for high-frequency data. This method was refined by Colliander, Keel, Staffilani, Takaoka, and Tao, who introduced the
I-
method in
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, and which was subsequently applied with varying success to the equation (
1
), in
[
12
,
25
]
. Specifically,
[
12
]
proved global well-posedness for
s>\frac{4}{9}
, while
[
25
]
improved this result to
s>\frac{2}{5}
by utilizing a more refined modified energy (with the obvious caveat of small data for the focusing variant).

Now, the
I
-method is the inclusion of an operator
I
defined by a smooth, decreasing, and radial multiplier:

(2)

m(|n|)=\begin{cases}1&|n|\leq N\\
N^{1-s}|n|^{s-1}&|n|>2N,\end{cases}

whose effect is to place
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
into
H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, where we can attempt to use the energy functional above. The standard
I
-method argument then obtains quantitative bounds (in terms of
N
) on the energy increment associated to the flow of
Iu
, which is used to obtain global well-posedness.

The arguments of De Silva, Pavlović, Staffilani, & Tzirakis
[
12
]
as well as Li, Wu, & Xu
[
25
]
supplement the already available factors of
N
with those following from a refined bilinear estimate of the following form: if
f,g\in L^{2}(\lambda\mathbb{T})
,
M\gg L
, and
\lambda\gg 1
, then:

(3)

\big\|e^{it\Delta{\lambda\mathbb{T}}}P_{M}fe^{it\Delta{\lambda\mathbb{T}}}P_{L}g\big\|_{L^{2}_{x,t}(\lambda\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1])}\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{1+M}\right)^{1/2}\|P_{M}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\lambda\mathbb{T})}\|P_{L}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\lambda\mathbb{T})},

where
\Delta_{\lambda\mathbb{T}}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator associated to the
\lambda-
torus. It’s worth noting that this above result should be compared to the bilinear estimate on
\mathbb{R}
, where we view
\lambda\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

However, due to the resonant structure of the quintic NLS, it appears that one cannot hope to obtain any improvements via the introduction of higher order modified energies as in
[
10
]
. Furthermore, as the the bilinear estimate above is sharp, one appears stuck without the introduction of a different idea, which was originally observed by Hani
[
14
]
in connection to the global well-posedness of the cubic NLS on closed manifolds. The key idea was that the low frequency portion of the scaled short-time estimate should hold for much longer times than
T\sim 1
; specifically, the short-time estimates of Burq, Gérard, & Tzvetkov
[
7
]
could be used in a scaling critical norm to establish longer times of existence that depend on
\lambda
, circumventing some of the blow up when
s\to 0
in
\lambda\sim N^{\frac{1-s}{s}}
. The addition of this idea, together with the fine properties of the structure of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, was observed by Herr & Kwak
1
1

1

They technically did not need to rely on the
I-
method for their proof, as they obtained a short time Strichartz estimate with logarithmically dependent time.

[
15
]
and subsequently by Schippa
[
26
]
, to significantly improve the global well-posedness threshold.

The fundamental building block is the family of estimates given in
[
14
]
, which hold on timescales
\sim N^{-1}
:

(4)

\|e^{it\bigtriangleup_{\mathcal{M}}}P_{\leq N}f\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(N^{-1}\mathbb{T}\times\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\|P_{\leq N}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})},

whenever
(p,q)
is an admissible exponent pair for the Schrödinger equation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d
the dimension of the (Riemannian) compact manifold
\mathcal{M}
. The key insight of
[
15
]
, then, is that the short-time estimates above hold on generic compact manifolds, so we should possibly expect such estimates to hold on longer timescales when restricted to
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, culminating in the estimate:

(5)

\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}P_{\leq N}f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(N^{-\alpha}\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim_{\alpha}\|P_{\leq N}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{2})},\quad\,\forall\alpha>0.

This estimate was utilized by
[
15
]
to obtain small data global well-posedness of the cubic NLS on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
for
s>0
, while (
4
) was used by
[
26
]
to obtain global well-posedness for the quintic NLS on
\mathbb{T}
for
s>\frac{1}{3}
.

However, the threshold
s>\frac{1}{3}
of
[
26
]
is imposed by the duration in (
4
), and is the key avenue along which to attack this problem. This manuscript improves this estimate, which provides the following improvement to the global well-posedness threshold.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\alpha>\frac{131}{208}
and
s>\frac{\alpha}{3}
. Then (
1
) is globally well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for any
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{T})}\ll 1
.

As previously mentioned, Theorem
1
follows from an improvement to the short time estimate (
4
) when posed on
\mathbb{T}
, which is derived from a connection between (
4
) and lattice counting in thin annuli. However, due to scaling considerations discussed in Section
5.2
, Theorem
1
also requires the use of a trilinear estimate, Proposition
7
, over the bilinear estimate (
3
). While this estimate has loss, the loss in many situations can be taken to be in the smallest frequency, and hence we can use this estimate in much the same way as we utilize the bilinear estimate. In fact, much of the construction of the modified energies and pointwise/regularity estimates, outside of a few tighter bounds, follows in exactly the same manner as in
[
26
]
– so we focus first on the proof the Strichartz estimate.

Before stating the Strichartz estimate, we first state Hypothesis

H

(
α
)

, which will be used in the proof of the Strichartz estimate of Theorem
2
.

Hypothesis 1

(Hypothesis
H(\alpha)
)
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We say that
\alpha
satisfies

H

(
α
)

if for all
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
we have

(
H(\alpha)
)

\#\{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\,:\,N^{2}\lesssim|n-\beta|^{2}\lesssim N^{2}+N^{\alpha}\}\lesssim N^{\alpha}.

That is, the number of lattice points in any annulus centered at
\beta
with inner radius
N
and outer radius
\sqrt{N^{2}+N^{\alpha}}
as at most
N^{\alpha}
.

Remark 1
.

We observe that
\alpha>\frac{2}{3}
is clear by classical Fourier analytic techniques. Moreover, if (say)
\frac{2}{3}>\alpha^{\prime}>\alpha
and
H(\alpha)
holds, then a decomposition argument also yields
H(\alpha^{\prime})
.

The above hypothesis is intimately related to the (slightly generalized) circle problem of Gauss, which is to find the best
\theta=\theta(\beta)>\frac{1}{2}
such that

\#\{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\,:\,|n-\beta|^{2}\leq N\}=\pi N^{2}+O(N^{\theta}).

Indeed, if the above holds for some
\theta
and all choices of
\beta
, then

H

(
α
)

holds with
\alpha>\theta
. Results of Huxley
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
lead up to and obtain
\theta=\frac{131}{208}=0.62980...
, and recently Li & Yang
[
24
]
obtained
\theta=0.62896...
at least in the case that
\beta=0
. However, proving that the above holds for any
\alpha<\theta(0)
is nontrivial. Moreover, there is a wealth of work on this kind of problem from a probabilistic perspective, see e.g.
[
18
,
29
,
28
,
11
,
2
,
3
,
1
]
, viewing either the center,
\beta
, or the radius as random variables.

With

H

(
α
)

, we are in a position to state our improvement to (

):

Theorem 2
.

Let
\alpha>0
satisfy

H

(
α
)

. Then for any
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
and

:

(6)

\|P_{\leq N}f\|_{L^{6}_{x,t}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,N^{-\alpha}])}\lesssim\|P_{\leq N}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}.

In particular, Equation
6
holds unconditionally for
\alpha>\frac{131}{208}
.

With

H

(
α
)

we can similarly restate Theorem

to the form that we will actually prove.

2.
Notation

As will be come apparant in the later sections, we will need to work on a scaled torus
\lambda\mathbb{T}
, for some
\lambda>0
. For any function
v\in C^{0}_{t}L^{2}_{x}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
we denote

v^{\lambda}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{1/2}}v\left(\frac{x}{\lambda},\frac{t}{\lambda^{2}}\right),

where
v^{\lambda}
naturally lives in
C^{0}_{t}L^{2}_{x}([0,\lambda^{2}T]\times\lambda\mathbb{T})
. We note that this respects the natural scaling of equation (
1
).

In the scaled space
\lambda\mathbb{T}
, we denote the Fourier transform of
f
by
\widehat{f}
, and is given by

\widehat{f}(k)=\int_{\lambda\mathbb{T}}e^{-ikx}f(x)\,dx.

Letting
(dk)_{\lambda}
denote the normalized counting measure, we naturally have the Fourier inversion formula given by

f(x)=\int e^{ikx}\widehat{f}(k)\,(dk)_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{k\in\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathbb{Z}}e^{ikx}\widehat{f}(k).

If
I\subset\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{R}
, we denote the projection of
\widehat{f}
onto the set
I
by
P_{I}f
, where

P_{I}f:=\int_{I}e^{ikx}\widehat{f}(k)(dk)_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{k\in I}\widehat{f}(k)e^{ikx}.

With these we find the standard definition of the scaled Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\lambda\mathbb{T})
as

\|f\|^{2}_{H^{s}(\lambda\mathbb{T})}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{k\in\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathbb{Z}}\langle k\rangle^{2s}|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}.

We will also require the ability to go between multiple scales. Suppose that
\mu,\lambda,\nu>0
,
v\in C^{0}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(\mu\mathbb{T}\times\lambda\mathbb{T})
, and
Q
is some Fourier multiplier on
L^{2}_{x}(\lambda\mathbb{T})
given by
\widehat{Q}(k)=q(k)
. Defining
Q^{\nu}
to be the Fourier multiplier on
L^{2}(\nu\lambda\mathbb{T})
with
\widehat{Q^{\nu}}(k)=q(\nu k)
, then

(7)

\|Qf\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{t}L^{p_{1}}_{x}(\mu\mathbb{T}\times\lambda\mathbb{T})}=\nu^{-\sigma(p_{1},p_{2})}\|Q^{\nu}f^{\nu}\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{t}L^{p_{1}}_{x}(\nu^{2}\mu\mathbb{T}\times\nu\lambda\mathbb{T})},\quad\sigma(p_{1},p_{2})=\frac{2}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{p_{1}}-\frac{1}{2}.

The above will often be applied in the situation that
Q=P_{I}
for some interval
I\subset\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\mathbb{Z}
, where setting
\nu=\lambda^{-1}
yields

\|P_{I}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{t}L^{p_{1}}_{x}(\mu\mathbb{T}\times\lambda\mathbb{T})}=\lambda^{\sigma(p_{1},p_{2})}\|P_{\lambda I}f^{\lambda^{-1}}\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{t}L^{p_{1}}_{x}(\tfrac{\mu}{\lambda^{2}}\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})},

while setting
\nu=\mu^{-1/2}
yields

\|P_{I}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{t}L^{p_{1}}_{x}(\mu\mathbb{T}\times\lambda\mathbb{T})}=\nu^{\frac{\sigma(p_{1},p_{2})}{2}}\|P_{\sqrt{\mu}I}f^{\mu^{-1/2}}\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{t}L^{p_{1}}_{x}(\mathbb{T}\times\tfrac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\mu}}\mathbb{T})}.

In our setting
\mu\ll\lambda^{2}
, so this will allow us to transfer problems on
\lambda\mathbb{T}
to those on
\mathbb{T}
for small time, and problems on
\mu\mathbb{T}\times\lambda\mathbb{T}
to problems with unit time on very large torii. Additionally, we will always take
p_{1}=p_{2}\in\{2,6\}
– when
p_{1}=p_{2}=6
, we see that lossless
L^{6}_{x,t}
Strichartz for
T\sim\mu/\lambda^{2}
transfer without loss to
L^{6}_{x,t}
Strichartz estimates on
\mu\mathbb{T}\times\lambda\mathbb{T}
.

We will perform Littlewood-Paley decompositions, where it becomes necessary to consider the dyadic numbers
M\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, and define

A_{N_{i}}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,\frac{1}{2}M<|x|<2M\}\qquad\mbox{for }M\in 2^{\mathbb{N}},

together with
A_{1}=[-2,2]
. We may then define the relationship
|k|\sim M
in the standard way, with the understanding that
|k|\sim 2
for
|k|\leq 2
. In this fashion, we may consider the smooth (inhomogenous) Littlewood-Paley projections
P_{M}
for
M\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
. For functions
f\in L^{2}
we write
f_{M}:=P_{M}f
, which affords us the standard decomposition:

\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\lambda\mathbb{T})}^{2}\sim\sum_{M\in 2^{\mathbb{M}}}\|f_{M}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\lambda\mathbb{T})}^{2}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n>1
we also define both the hyperplanes
\Gamma_{n}
and their inherited measures
(d\Gamma_{n})_{\lambda}
by

\Gamma_{n}=\left\{(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\left(\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\mathbb{Z}\right)^{j}\,:\,k_{1}+\dots+k_{n}=0\right\},\quad(d\Gamma_{n})_{\lambda}=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(dk_{j})_{\lambda},

which will be useful not only in our construction of the modified energy, but for handling multilinear estimates. Indeed, if
n>1
and
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
is some collection with
f_{j}\in L^{2}(\lambda\mathbb{T})
, then we find the standard identity

\int_{\lambda\mathbb{T}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(x)\,dx=\frac{1}{\lambda^{n-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{j}\in\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\\
\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}=0\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\widehat{f}_{j}(k_{j})=\int\prod_{j=1}^{n}\widehat{f}_{j}(k_{j})\,(d\Gamma_{n})_{\lambda}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and a collection
(z_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
, we denote
(z_{j}^{*})_{j=1}^{n}
to be a decreasing rearrangement of
(z_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
– that is,

|z_{1}^{*}|\geq|z_{2}^{*}|\geq\dots\geq|z_{n}^{*}|,

and
(z_{j}^{*})
is a permutation of
(z_{j})
. Lastly, for
A,B>0
, we denote
A\vee B=\max(A,B)
and
A\wedge B=\min(A,B).

3.
A Lattice Counting Lemma & Proof of Theorem
2

This section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
2
. For this section, we let
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
be distinct from the
N
used in the
I-
method.

We first perform a reduction as in
[
15
]
, where for fixed
n_{i},m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
1\leq i\leq 3
we define

\Omega=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{3}n_{i}^{2}-m_{i}^{2}\right|.

Additionally, for a fixed
f\in L^{2}_{x}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{Z}
, we write

h(\tau)=h(\tau;N)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{i=1}^{3}n_{i}-m_{i}=0\\
\Omega=\tau,\,|n_{i}|,|m_{i}|\leq 2N\end{subarray}}\left|\prod_{i=1}^{3}\widehat{f}(n_{1})\widehat{f}(m_{i})\right|.

With these notations, we find

\displaystyle\|e^{it\Delta}P_{\leq N}f\|^{6}_{L^{6}_{t,x}([0,N^{-\alpha}]\times\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{N^{-\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|e^{it\Delta}P_{\leq N}f|^{6}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{2N^{-\alpha}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|e^{it\Delta}P_{\leq N}f|^{6}\,dxdtdT

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\alpha}h(0)+\sum_{\tau>0}\min\left(N^{-\alpha},\frac{N^{\alpha}}{\tau^{2}}\right)h(\tau)

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\alpha}h(0)+\sup_{K\in N^{\alpha}2^{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{K\leq|\tau|\leq 2K}h(\tau).

The first term is handled exactly the same way that one handles the
L^{6}_{x,t}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})
estimate, so that for
\alpha>0
it is sufficient to instead estimate

\sup_{K\in N^{\alpha}2^{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{K\leq|\tau|\leq 2K}h(\tau)=\sup_{K\in N^{\alpha}2^{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{i=1}^{3}n_{i}-m_{i}=0\\
K\leq|\sum_{i=1}^{3}n_{i}^{2}-m_{i}^{2}|\leq 2K\\
|n_{i}|,|m_{i}|\leq 2N\end{subarray}}\left|\prod_{i=1}^{3}\widehat{f}(n_{1})\widehat{f}(m_{i})\right|.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\alpha
be such that

H

(
α
)

holds,
K\in N^{\alpha}2^{\mathbb{N}}
, and
f\in L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{T})
. Then

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{i=1}^{3}n_{i}-m_{i}=0\\
K\leq\left|\sum_{i=1}^{3}n_{1}^{2}-m_{i}^{2}\right|\leq 2K\\
|n_{i}|,|m_{i}|\leq 2N\end{subarray}}|\prod_{i=1}^{3}\widehat{f}(n_{1})\widehat{f}(m_{i})|\lesssim\|P_{\leq N}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{T})}^{3}.

Due to the form of the estimate, it becomes necessary to calculate the number of lattice points on a plane intersected with a very thin cylindrical annulus in the direction of the normal to the plane. Before getting to this, we must first introduce notation. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
K\in N^{\alpha}2^{\mathbb{N}}
fixed, let

P_{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:x\cdot(1,1,1)=n\},

which is a plane with normal vector
(1,1,1)
. For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
\xi_{1}=(1,0,-1)
, and
\xi_{2}=(0,1,-1)
, we decompose
[0,6N^{2}]=\bigsqcup_{\ell\geq 0}I_{\ell}
where
I_{\ell}=[\ell K,(\ell+1)K)
and define

\mathcal{C}_{\ell}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,:\,x=t(1,1,1),\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}\\
+\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,:\,x=\xi_{1}r\cos(\theta)+\xi_{2}r\sin(\theta),\,\theta\in[0,2\pi],\,r^{2}\in I_{\ell}\},

which is the cylindrical annulus in the direction of the normal vector for the plane
P_{n}
, with inner radius
\sqrt{\ell K}
and outer radius
\sqrt{(\ell+1)K}
. Observe that

\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap P_{n}\cap\bigsqcup_{\ell\geq 0}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}=\{(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\,:\,\,n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}=n\}.

Lemma 2
.

There exists
c_{n}
, depending only on the equivalence class of
n
modulo 3, such that for any
1\leq\ell\leq 6N^{2}/K
we have:

\#(\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\cap P_{n})=\#(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap A(\ell K,(\ell+1)K)+c_{n}),

where
A(r_{1},r_{2})
is the annulus:

A(r_{1},r_{2}):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,r_{1}\leq\|x\|^{2}<r_{2}\}.

Proof.

This is almost trivial, but included for completeness. Observe that the above is a count of the number of lattice points in the affine lattice given by

\Gamma=\{x\,:\,x=(\lfloor\tfrac{n}{3}\rfloor,\lfloor\tfrac{n}{3}\rfloor,\lfloor\tfrac{n}{3}\rfloor+j)+a\xi_{1}+b\xi_{2},\,a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\},

with

\ell K\leq\|x-(n/3,n/3,n/3)\|^{2}\leq(\ell+1)K.

By a rotation and translation of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we may map
P_{n}
to
\{z=0\}
,
\Gamma
to:

\tilde{\Gamma}=\{x\,:\,x=(a,b,0),\,a,b\in\sqrt{2}\mathbb{Z}\},

and
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\cap P_{n}
to

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,:\,x=c_{n}+(r\cos(\theta),r\sin(\theta),0),\,\theta\in[0,2\pi],\,r^{2}\in 2I_{\ell}\},

for some
c_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\{0\}
depending only on the equivalence class modulo
3
of
n
.

That is, the problem is exactly the same as counting the number of points of

\{x\,:\,x=(a,b),\,a,b\in\sqrt{2}\mathbb{Z}\},

in the annulus:

\sqrt{2}A(\ell K,(\ell+1)K)+c_{n},

an annulus with modulated inner and outer radii centered slightly off
0
.

The number of such points is the same as the number of points in the standard lattice that lie in the annulus

A(\ell K,(\ell+1)K)+c_{n},

for a possibly scaled choice of
c_{n}.

∎

With the problem now reduced to the number of lattice points in an annulus, we are now free to use Gauss Circle Problem bounds to bound this. In particular, Huxley’s work
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
provides the following bound

\#\{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\,:\,|n-\beta|^{2}<M^{2}\}=\pi M^{2}+O(M^{\frac{131}{208}}),

for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. With this in hand, we find via standard differencing that

\#\{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\,:\,\ell K\leq|n-\beta|^{2}\leq(\ell+1)K\}=\pi K+O\left((\ell K)^{\frac{131}{416}}\right)=\pi K+O(N^{\frac{131}{208}}).

More generally, suppose that
\alpha>0
satisfies

H

(
α
)

and we assume that
K\gtrsim N^{\alpha}
. Then
\ell K\lesssim N^{2}
so that Remark
1
yields

\#\{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\,:\,\ell K\leq|n-\beta|^{2}\leq(\ell+1)K\}\lesssim K.

In particular, this yields the following Corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\alpha>0
so that

H

(
α
)

holds,
N^{\alpha}\lesssim K
,
\ell\lesssim N^{2}/K
, and
c\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then

\#(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap(A(\ell K,(\ell+1)K)+c))\lesssim K.

We now have all the pieces necessary to prove the Lemma
1

Proof of Lemma
1
.

Recall that we seek to show:

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{i=1}^{3}m_{i}-\tilde{m}_{i}=0\\
K\leq\left|\sum_{i=1}^{3}m_{1}^{2}-\tilde{m}_{i}^{2}\right|\leq 2K\\
|m_{i}|,|\tilde{m}_{i}|\leq N\end{subarray}}|\prod_{i=1}^{3}\widehat{f}(m_{1})\widehat{f}(\tilde{m}_{i})|\lesssim\|P_{\leq N}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{T})}^{3}.

Now, we first foliate over the linear relationship, and write

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{i=1}^{3}m_{i}-\tilde{m}_{i}=0\\
K\leq\left|\sum_{i=1}^{3}m_{1}^{2}-\tilde{m}_{i}^{2}\right|\leq 2K\\
|m_{i}|,|\tilde{m}_{i}|\leq N\end{subarray}}|\prod_{i=1}^{3}\widehat{f}(m_{1})\widehat{f}(\tilde{m}_{i})|\lesssim\frac{1}{K}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,\tilde{m}\in P_{n}\\
K\leq||m|^{2}-|\tilde{m}|^{2}|\leq 2K\\
|m|,|\tilde{m}|\leq 3N\end{subarray}}|\tilde{f}(m)\tilde{f}(\tilde{m})|,

where, for
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
:

\tilde{f}(m)=\widehat{f}(m_{1})\widehat{f}(m_{2})\widehat{f}(m_{3}).

We now introduce the decomposition into annuli as above. In order to do this, we decompose
\mathbb{R}^{3}=\sqcup_{\ell\geq 0}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
, and denote
\mathcal{C}^{n}_{\ell}=P_{n}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
. By almost-orthogonality in the
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
decomposition, we now write our estimate as:

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,\tilde{m}\in P_{n}\\
K\leq||m|^{2}-|\tilde{m}|^{2}|\leq 2K\\
|m|,|\tilde{m}|\leq 3N\end{subarray}}|\tilde{f}(m)\tilde{f}(\tilde{m})|\ \lesssim\frac{1}{K}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,j\geq 0\\
|l-j|=O(1)\end{subarray}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{\ell}^{n}\cap B(0,10N))}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{j}^{n}\cap B(0,10N))},

where Cauchy-Schwarz and Lemma
2
allows us to write

\|\tilde{f}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{\ell}^{n}\cap B(0,10N))}\lesssim\#(\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\ell}^{n}\cap B(0,10N))^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\ell}^{n}\cap B(0,10N))}\\
\lesssim\#(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap\left(A(\ell K,(\ell+1)K)+c\right)\cap B(0,10N))^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\ell}^{n}\cap B(0,10N))}\\
\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\ell}^{n}\cap B(0,10N))},

for some
c
depending solely on the equivilence class of
n
modulo
3
. Summarizing, we have found:

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,\tilde{m}\in P_{n}\\
K\leq||m|^{2}-|\tilde{m}|^{2}|\leq 2K\\
|m|,|\tilde{m}|\leq 3N\end{subarray}}|\tilde{f}(m)\tilde{f}(\tilde{m})|\lesssim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,j\geq 0\\
|l-j|=O(1)\end{subarray}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\ell}^{n}\cap B(0,10N))}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{j}^{n}\cap B(0,10N))}.

Another application of Cauchy-Schwarz yields:

\sum_{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,j\geq 0\\
|l-j|=O(1)\end{subarray}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\ell}^{n}\cap B(0,10N))}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{j}^{n}\cap B(0,10N))}\lesssim\sum_{n}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\ell^{2}(P_{n})}^{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{6},

as desired.
∎

5.1.
Atomic Space Overview

We will perform our estimates in the atomic spaces of
[
16
]
(see also
[
17
,
23
]
), as these behave well in critical scenarios and preserve the embeddings we enjoy. However, these spaces don’t behave as well as the
L^{2}_{x,t}
based spaces that we normally work with. We may still perform Littlewood-Paley decompositions, but we may not do the crude work we did before when we had that the norms were in terms of the absolute values of the space-time Fourier transforms.

We briefly review their properties below.

Definition 1
.

Let
H
be a seperable HIlbert space over
\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathcal{Z}
be the collection of all partitions
-\infty<t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}\leq\infty
. Then for
1\leq p<\infty
we define
V^{p}X
as the space of all right-continuus functions
u:\mathbb{R}\to X
with
\lim_{t\to-\infty}u(t)=0
satisfying

\|u\|_{V^{p}X}^{p}:=\sup_{\{t_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|u(t_{k})-u(t_{k-1})\|_{X}^{p}<\infty.

Additionally, we define the space
V^{p}_{\bigtriangleup}
as
V^{p}L^{2}
adapted to the linear group
e^{it\bigtriangleup}
as

V^{p}_{\bigtriangleup}=\{u\,:\,\mathbb{R}\times\lambda\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}\,:e^{-it\bigtriangleup}u\in V^{p}L^{2}(\lambda\mathbb{T})\}.

V^{p}
spaces also admit the nice predual
U^{p}
.

Definition 2
.

A
U^{p}
atom is a function
u:\mathbb{R}\times\lambda\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

u=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\chi_{[t_{k-1},t_{k})}a_{k},\quad\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|a_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{p}=1,\quad\{t_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K}\in\mathcal{Z}.

We then define the
U^{p}
norm of
u:\mathbb{R}\times\lambda\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
as

\|u\|_{U^{p}}:=\inf\left(\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\mu_{k}|\,:\,u=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}f_{k},\,\,\{\mu_{k}\}\in\ell^{1},\,f_{k}\mbox{ a }U^{p}-\mbox{atom}\right\}\right).

We similarly define the standard adaptation of
U^{p}
to the linear group as
U^{p}_{\bigtriangleup}
.

Remark 3
.

This definition of
V^{p}
is commonly denoted as
V^{p}_{-,rc}
,
[
13
]
. With this convention it follows that for all
1\leq p<q<\infty
the embeddings
U^{p}\subset U^{q}
,
V^{p}\subset V^{q}
,
U^{p}\subset V^{p}
, and
V^{p}\subset U^{q}
are all continuous.

We define the space that we will work in as
Y^{s}
, which is given in terms of
V^{p}_{\bigtriangleup}
as:

Definition 3
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
. We define
Y^{s}_{\pm}
to be the space of functions
u
for which:

\|u\|_{Y^{s}_{\pm}}^{2}:=\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{k\in\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathbb{Z}}\langle k\rangle^{2s}\left\|e^{\pm it|k|^{2}}\widehat{u}(t,k)\right\|_{V^{2}}^{2}<\infty.

Similarly, we define the the standard adaptation to
t\in[0,T]
as
Y^{s}_{\pm,T}
.

We ignore the presence in the conjugates, and simply call these spaces
Y^{s}
. These spaces enjoy the properties that we demand with respect to the Duhamel operator, as well as both preservation of disjointness and transference of Strichartz estimates. This is summarized in the next proposition.

Proposition 3

(
[
26
,
13
]
)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
T>0
. If
A
and
B
be disjoint subsets of
\lambda\mathbb{Z}
, then

\|P_{A\cup B}u\|_{Y^{s}}^{2}=\|P_{A}u\|_{Y^{s}}^{2}+\|P_{B}u\|_{Y^{s}}^{2}.

Moreover, if
f\in H^{s}(\lambda\mathbb{T})
then

\|\chi_{[0,T)}(t)e^{it\bigtriangleup}f\|_{Y^{s}}\sim\|f\|_{H^{s}(\lambda\mathbb{T})},

and if
\|e^{it\bigtriangleup}f\|_{L^{p}_{x,t}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}
for all
f\in L^{2}
then

\|u\|_{L^{p}_{x,t}}\lesssim\|u\|_{U^{p}_{\bigtriangleup}}.

Lastly, if
F\in L^{1}_{t}H^{s}_{x}
then

\left\|\chi_{[0,T)}(t)\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\bigtriangleup}F(s)\,ds\right\|_{Y^{s}}\lesssim\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in Y^{-s}\\
\|v\|_{Y^{-s}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\lambda\mathbb{T}}\overline{v}F(s)\,dxds\right|.

These spaces enjoy nice interpolation properties, which is the primary way we will obtain multilinear estimates in these spaces.

Proposition 4

(Propositions 2.19 & 2.20,
[
13
]
)
.

Let
T_{0}:L^{2}\times\dots\times L^{2}\mapsto L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{C})
be an
n-
linear operator and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. If

\|T_{0}(e^{\cdot i\Delta}\phi_{1},\dots,e^{\cdot i\Delta}\phi_{n})\|_{L^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{T}))}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{n}\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}},

then there is an extension
T:U^{p}_{\Delta}\times\dots\times U^{p}_{\Delta}\to L^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{T}))
such that
T(u_{1},\dots u_{n})(t)(x)=T_{0}(u_{1}(t),\dots,u_{n}(t))(x)
almost everywhere and

\|T(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\|_{L^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{T}))}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{n}\|u_{j}\|_{U^{p}_{\Delta}}.

Suppose now that
1\leq 2<p
,
j\geq 2
,
E
is a Banach space,
T:(U^{p}_{\Delta})^{j}\to E
is a bounded linear operator with
\|T(\{u_{k}\})\|_{E}\leq C_{p}\prod_{k=1}^{j}\|u_{j}\|_{U^{p}_{\Delta}}
for all
\{u_{k}\}\in(U^{p}_{\Delta})^{j}
, and that there is some
C_{2}\in(0,C_{p}]
such that
\|T(\{u_{k}\})\|_{E}\leq C_{2}\prod_{k=1}^{j}\|u_{j}\|_{U^{2}_{\Delta}}
holds for all
\{u_{k}\}\in(U^{2}_{\Delta})^{j}
. Then
T
also satisfies

\|T(u,u)\|_{E}\lesssim_{p}C_{2}\left(\ln\frac{C_{p}}{C_{2}}+1\right)^{j}\prod_{k=1}^{j}\|u_{k}\|_{V^{2}_{\Delta}},\qquad\{u_{k}\}\in(V^{p}_{\Delta})^{j}.

The first part of the above proposition is the analogue of the usual transference principle utilized for Bourgain spaces. The second enables us to upgrade multilinear estimates for operators,
T
, that are bounded for functions in
U^{p}
, to estimates of
T
considered as operators from
V^{2}_{\Delta}
, which is desirable due to our Strichartz embedding properties for
U^{p}_{\Delta}
. These above results trivially extend to
Y^{s}
.

Before concluding, we also remark the following by Bernstein:

\|P_{N}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,t}([0,T]\times\lambda\mathbb{T})}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}}\|P_{N}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}([0,T]\times\lambda\mathbb{T})}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}}\|P_{N}u\|_{U^{p}_{\Delta}}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}}\|P_{N}u\|_{V^{2}_{\Delta}}\sim\|P_{N}u\|_{Y^{\frac{1}{2}}},

for any, say,
2<p<\infty
. This will be utilized, together with the Strichartz embedding, to yield estimates in

Before proceeding, we state the estimate of Theorem

in this setting, which was the original motivation for the study of short time estimates in connection with global well-posedness.

Proposition 5
.

If
\phi\in L^{2}(\lambda\mathbb{T})
,
N\gg 1
,
\lambda\sim N^{\frac{1-s}{s}}\gg 1
, and
M\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
, then we find the following Strichartz estimate:

\displaystyle\|P_{M}e^{it\Delta}\phi\|_{L^{6}(\mu\mathbb{T}\times\lambda\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\begin{cases}\|P_{M}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\lambda\mathbb{T})}&M\lesssim N\\
(\lambda M)^{0+}\|P_{M}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\lambda\mathbb{T})}&M\gg N.\end{cases}

Proof.

This is a straightforward scaling argument. Observe that

\|P_{M}e^{it\Delta}\phi\|_{L^{6}(\mu\mathbb{T}\times\lambda\mathbb{T})}=\|P_{\lambda M}e^{it\Delta}\phi^{\lambda^{-1}}\|_{L^{6}((\lambda N)^{-\alpha}\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})},

so that if
M\lesssim N
then we are in a position to apply Theorem
2
, while if
M\gg N
we apply the standard
L^{6}
estimate:

\|P_{M}e^{it\Delta}\phi\|_{L^{6}_{x,t}(\mu\mathbb{T}\times\lambda\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\|P_{\lambda M}e^{it\Delta}\phi^{\lambda^{-1}}\|_{L^{6}_{x,t}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})}\\
\lesssim(\lambda M)^{0+}\|P_{\lambda M}\phi^{\lambda^{-1}}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{T})}=(\lambda M)^{0+}\|P_{M}\phi\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{\lambda T})}.

∎

Due to the above transference principle, this space preserves multilinear Strichartz estimates, such as those in

. In that direction, we first state the scale

of

Proposition 6

(Proposition 2.1 & Corollary 2.1,
[

]
)

Let

\widehat{I^{\pm}_{M}(f,g)}(k)=\int_{k=k_{1}+k_{2}}\chi_{|k_{1}\mp k_{2}|\gtrsim M}\widehat{f}(k_{1})\widehat{g}(\pm k_{2})(dk_{1})_{\lambda}.

\|I^{\pm}_{M}(e^{it\bigtriangleup}\phi_{1},e^{it\bigtriangleup}\phi_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}(\mathbb{T}\times\lambda\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{M}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\phi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\lambda\mathbb{T})}\|\phi_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\lambda\mathbb{T})}.

We now observe that if

is as in (

\|I^{\pm}_{M}(e^{it\Delta}\phi_{1},e^{it\Delta}\phi_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}(\mu\mathbb{T}\times\lambda\mathbb{T})}=\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|I^{\pm}_{\sqrt{\mu}M}(e^{it\Delta}\phi_{1}^{\sqrt{\mu}^{-1}},e^{it\Delta}\phi_{2}^{\sqrt{\mu}^{-1}})\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}(\mathbb{T}\times\tfrac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\mu}}\mathbb{T})}\\

This would be acceptable only if

, which we cannot overcome, and is due to the fact that the

The use of trilinear estimates in this exact setting goes back to Bourgain

Theorem 3

\int_{[0,N^{-\alpha}]\times\mathbb{T}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}|e^{it\Delta}f_{j}|\,dxdt\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\beta}\log(N)^{12+\varepsilon}N_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha\beta}{8}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}.

While one could certainly utilize Theorem
3
, one would not be able to obtain the full range

estimate on

Proposition 7
.

Let
\lambda\gg 1
,

be dyadic intervals such that
|I_{1}|\lesssim|I_{2}|\lesssim|I_{3}|

\phi_{j}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
,
1\leq j\leq 3

\|\prod_{j=1}^{3}e^{it\Delta}\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{x,t}(\lambda\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{|I_{2}|}{N_{\max}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\lambda\mathbb{T})}.

Alternatively, if
N_{\max}\gtrsim|I_{2}|
and
|I_{1}|\ll|I_{2}|
,

for some

, and

then we find the enhanced trilinear estimate:

\|\prod_{j=1}^{3}e^{it\Delta}\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{x,t}(\lambda\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{M\vee|I_{1}|}{N_{\max}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\lambda\mathbb{T})}.

Proof.

This follows in a similar manner as in
[
12
]
&
[
25
]
. Specifically, we begin by obtaining a bound on the size of
A
, defined as

A=\{n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}/\lambda\,:\,n_{1}\in I_{1},\,n_{2}\in I_{2},\,n_{3}:=n-n_{1}-n_{2}\in I_{3},\,|n_{1}-n_{3}|\gtrsim N_{13}\\
|n_{2}-n_{3}|\gtrsim N_{23},\tau-n_{1}^{2}-n_{2}^{2}-(n-n_{1}-n_{2})^{2}=O(1)\,\},

in the form of

\sup_{n,\tau}\#A\lesssim\lambda^{2}\frac{K}{N_{\max}}+O(\lambda),

for some

. Once we have this in hand, we observe after taking the supremum and applying Cauchy-Schwarz that

\|\prod_{j=1}^{3}e^{it\Delta}\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{x,t}(\lambda\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\sup_{n,\tau}\#A\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\lambda\mathbb{T})},

and hence we may conclude our estimate.

Turning our attention to
\sup_{n,\tau}\#A
, we follow
[
12
]
in that we seek to transfer this question to a question about lattice points in disk that lie in an elliptical annulus. To that end, we fix an element
(n_{1},n_{2})\in A
, and its corresponding
n_{3}=n-n_{1}-n_{2}
. We then will count the number of
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
(n_{1}+\ell_{1}/\lambda,n_{2}+\ell_{2}/\lambda)\in A
. That is,

\displaystyle n_{1}+\tfrac{\ell_{1}}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\in I_{1},

\displaystyle n_{2}+\tfrac{\ell_{2}}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\in I_{2},

\displaystyle n-n_{1}-n_{2}-\tfrac{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\in I_{3},

\displaystyle|n_{1}-n_{3}-\tfrac{2\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}{\lambda}|

\displaystyle\gtrsim N_{13},

\displaystyle|n_{2}-n_{3}-\frac{\ell_{1}+2\ell_{2}}{\lambda}|

\displaystyle\gtrsim N_{23}

together with the property that

\tau-(n_{1}+\tfrac{\ell_{1}}{\lambda})^{2}-(n_{2}+\tfrac{\ell_{2}}{\lambda})^{2}-(n_{3}-\tfrac{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}{\lambda})^{2}=O(1).

Expanding the above, we find that

(n_{1}+\tfrac{\ell_{1}}{\lambda})^{2}+(n_{2}+\tfrac{\ell_{2}}{\lambda})^{2}+(n_{3}-\tfrac{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}{\lambda})^{2}\\
=n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}+n_{3}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}(\ell_{1}^{2}+\ell_{2}^{2}+(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})^{2})+2\frac{\ell_{1}}{\lambda}(n_{1}-n_{3})+2\frac{\ell_{2}}{\lambda}(n_{2}-n_{3})\\
=\tau+O(1)+\frac{2}{\lambda^{2}}(\ell_{1}^{2}+\ell_{2}^{2}+\ell_{1}\ell_{2})+2\frac{\ell_{1}}{\lambda}(n_{1}-n_{3})+2\frac{\ell_{2}}{\lambda}(n_{2}-n_{3}),

and also that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\ell_{j}}{\lambda}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\ell_{j}}{\lambda}+n_{j}-n_{j}\right|\lesssim|I_{j}|,\quad j=1,2

\displaystyle|n_{1}-n_{3}|

\displaystyle\gtrsim N_{13}

\displaystyle|n_{2}-n_{3}|

\displaystyle\gtrsim N_{23}.

Combining the above and relabeling
\tilde{a}=n_{1}-n_{3}
,
\tilde{b}=n_{2}-n_{3}
, we see that it is sufficient to count
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying:

(15)

\displaystyle\big|x^{2}+y^{2}+xy+

\displaystyle\lambda(x\tilde{a}+y\tilde{b})\big|\leq c_{1}\lambda^{2}

(16)

\displaystyle|x|\leq c_{2}\lambda|I_{1}|

\displaystyle,\quad|y|\leq c_{2}\lambda|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\tilde{a}^{2}+\tilde{b}^{2}

\displaystyle\sim N_{\max}^{2},

for some constants

.

One can view the first condition above as an elliptical annulus– define

,

,

, and

, with which we may rewrite the portion within the absolute value as:

In particular, each such radius defines an ellipse centered at

with major axis in the

direction, minor axis in the

direction, and extending
\sim\lambda N_{\max}
in both directions. That is, it’s morally a circle with radius

.

We now examine the second condition above, which defines a rectangle centered at
(0,0)
. If
|I_{1}|\sim|I_{2}|
, then we can view this as nearly a circle with radius

. However, if

, then this defines a long rectangle with major axis in the direction of the

axis, and minor axis of length
|I_{1}|
. Gains in this region will come from the observation that, under certain conditions, we may shrink the intersection from the full rectangle to a much smaller disk.

We now demonstrate that if
|I_{1}|\ll|I_{2}|
and condition (
14

, and that the region this forms is contained in an

ball around the origin. To that end, let
c_{1},c_{2}
be the constants in equations (
15
) and (
16
), respectively, and assume without loss of generality that

. We let
B(c_{2})=c_{2}|I_{1}|+\tilde{b}
, and observe that substitution of

into equation (

(y+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}B)^{2}=\tfrac{\lambda^{2}B^{2}}{4}\left\{1+4\frac{c_{1}-c_{2}^{2}|I_{1}|^{2}-c_{2}|I_{1}|\tilde{a}}{B^{2}}\right\}.

Where we realize the inner term satisfies

\left|\frac{c_{1}-c_{2}^{2}|I_{1}|^{2}-c_{2}|I_{1}|\tilde{a}}{B^{2}}\right|\lesssim\frac{M}{N_{23}}\ll 1,

and hence we may unwind and utilize a Taylor series bound to conclude

y(c_{1},c_{2})=-\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\{c_{2}|I_{1}|+\tilde{b}-|c_{2}|I_{1}|+\tilde{b}|\right\}+O\left(\lambda M\right)=O\left(\lambda M\right).

A similar argument allows us to conclude that

for any

.

This observation implies that the intersection is contained in the region

, and hence will live in a smaller ball with equation given by:

x^{2}+y^{2}\leq c_{3}\lambda^{2}(|I_{1}|\vee M)^{2},

where we are free to write this in terms of our new variables, up to some multiplicative loss on

.

To summarize, we have reduced our original system to finding the number of
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}
such that:

(17)

\displaystyle(u+\lambda a)^{2}+3(v+\lambda b)^{2}

\displaystyle=R+\lambda^{2}\left(a^{2}+3b^{2}\right)\quad|R|\leq c\lambda^{2}

(18)

\displaystyle u^{2}+v^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}\lambda^{2}K^{2}

(19)

\displaystyle a^{2}+b^{2}

\displaystyle\sim N_{\max}^{2},

where we take

(20)

K:=\begin{cases}|I_{1}|\vee M&\mbox{if }\eqref{Condition: M condition}\mbox{ holds}\\
|I_{2}|&\mbox{else}.\end{cases}

We remark that, pictorially and philosophically, this is only a minor variation of
[

We now seek to count the number of elements in the region formed by conditions (

A^{\lambda}:=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\,:\eqref{Equation: Ellipse Shell}-\eqref{Equation: a b condition}\,\mathrm{holds}\},

and observe that

, where

\,u^{2}+v^{2}\leq c_{3}K^{2},\,a^{2}+b^{2}\sim N_{\max}^{2}\}.

Hence, the desired claim would follow if we showed

|A^{\lambda}|\lesssim\lambda^{2}\frac{K}{N_{\max}}+O(\lambda),

where

It follow that for

.

Let

denote the the sector of the ellipse

whose boundaries are formed by the two tangent lines above and the boundary of the ellipse. In this way,

is a sector of the ellipse with angle given by

.

Combining the above together with a theorem of Gauss (see, e.g.

), we find that

|A^{\lambda}|=|\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap\lambda A^{1}|\leq|\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap\lambda S(c)|-|\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap\lambda S(-c)|=\lambda^{2}|S(c)\setminus S(-c)|+O(\lambda).

However, as the axis are similar in size we have (from, say, a trivial integral calculation) that

and hence

|A^{\lambda}|\lesssim\lambda^{2}\frac{K}{N_{\max}}+O(\lambda),

which is exactly the desired bound, (

Proposition 8
.

6.

With the prior lemmas out of the way, we turn to the construction of the modified energy described in Proposition

. Due to conservation of energy for (

They key theme in what is to follow is that we seek to perform integration by parts to exploit large phases. However, the phases under consideration are of of the form:

\Omega_{n}=i\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}k_{j}^{2},

which have non-trivial

. Even for just

Proposition 9
.

Let

denote intervals such that
|I_{j}|=L_{j}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
. Let

\overline{M}_{I}
, on
\mathbb{R}^{6}

|\partial_{\ell_{j}}^{\alpha}\overline{M}_{I}(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{6})|\lesssim\frac{\cal{M}(I_{1},\dots,I_{6})}{L^{\alpha}_{j}}

and

\overline{M}_{I}(k_{1},\dots,k_{6})=\frac{1}{L_{1}\cdots L_{6}}\sum_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{6}\in\mathbb{Z}}m(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{6})\prod_{j=1}^{6}e^{i\frac{k_{j}\xi_{j}}{L_{j}}}

\overline{\mathcal{M}}(I_{1},\dots,I_{6})\lesssim m(N_{1}^{*})N_{1}^{*}m(N_{3}^{*})N_{3}^{*}.

Observing that

|k_{1}-k_{3}|\sim|k_{2}-k_{4}|\sim|k_{5}-k_{1}|\sim|k_{6}-k_{2}|\sim N_{1}^{*},

we see that we may apply Corollary
2
twice, obtaining

\eqref{Equation: Resonant Base Estimate}\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}\sim N_{4}\gtrsim N\gg N_{5}^{*}\geq N_{6}^{*}}\frac{N^{2s-2}(N_{1})^{-2s}}{N_{5}N_{6}}\left|\int_{\lambda\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]}\prod_{j=1}^{6}w_{N_{j}^{*}}\,dxdt\right|\\
\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{4}^{*}\gtrsim N\gg N_{5}^{*}}\frac{N^{2s-2}(N_{1}^{*})^{-2s}}{N_{5}N_{6}}\|w_{N_{1}}w_{N_{3}}w_{N_{5}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,t}}\|w_{N_{2}}w_{N_{4}}w_{N_{6}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,t}}\\
\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}\sim N_{4}\gtrsim N\gg N_{5}^{*}\geq N_{6}^{*}}\frac{N^{2s-3}(N_{1}^{*})^{-2s+}}{(N_{5}N_{6})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\prod_{j=1}^{6}\|Iu_{N_{j}^{*}}\|_{Y^{1}_{T}}\lesssim N^{-3+}\|Iu\|_{Y^{1}_{T}}^{6}.

Suppose now that we are in case
A_{3213}
, in which case we have

|k_{2}+k_{3}|=|k_{1}+k_{4}+O(N_{5}^{*})|\gtrsim N_{1}^{*}\gtrsim N,

so that
|k_{1}+k_{4}|\gtrsim N_{1}^{*}
. In particular, we have access to two trilinear Strichartz applications and can apply the above argument again after a permutation of the functions.

We are thus left with
A_{3214}
, where we distinguish two (further) cases:

\displaystyle A_{3214a}

\displaystyle:=\{k\in A_{3214}\,:\,|k_{1}+k_{2}|\lesssim|k_{5}^{*}|\}

\displaystyle A_{3214b}

\displaystyle:=\{k\in A_{3214}\,:\,|k_{1}+k_{2}|\gg|k_{5}^{*}|\gtrsim|k_{5}+k_{6}|\}.

In case
A_{3214a}
we have that the linear relationship between
k_{j}
forces
|k_{3}+k_{4}|\lesssim|k_{5}^{*}|
, and hence we may perform the further almost orthogonal decomposition of
k_{j}\in I_{j}
for
j=1,2,3,4
and
|I_{j}|\sim N_{5}^{*}
. Equation (
31
) yields the bound
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{6}\lesssim m(N_{1}^{*})N_{1}^{*}m(N_{5}^{*})N_{5}^{*}
in this scenario, and hence

\eqref{Equation: Resonant Base Estimate}\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}\sim N_{4}\gtrsim N\gg N_{5}^{*}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1}\sim I_{2}\\
I_{3}\sim I_{4}\\
|I_{j}|\sim N_{5}^{*}\end{subarray}}\frac{N^{3s-3}(N_{1}^{*})^{-3s}}{N_{6}^{*}}\left|\int_{\lambda\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]}\prod_{j=1}^{4}P_{I_{j}}w_{N_{j}}\prod_{j=5}^{6}w_{N_{j}}\,dxdt\right|\\
\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}\sim N_{4}\gtrsim N\gg N_{5}^{*}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1}\sim I_{2}\\
I_{3}\sim I_{4}\\
|I_{j}|\sim N_{5}^{*}\end{subarray}}\frac{N^{3s-3}(N_{1}^{*})^{-3s}}{N_{6}^{*}}\prod_{j=1}^{4}\|P_{I_{j}}w_{N_{j}}\|_{L^{6}}\prod_{j=5}^{6}\|w_{N_{j}}\|_{L^{6}}\\
\lesssim N^{-3+}\|Iu\|_{Y^{1}_{T}}^{6}.

This leaves us with the final case of
A_{3214b}
, where
|k_{1}+k_{2}|\gg N_{5}^{*}\gtrsim|k_{5}+k_{6}|
. This forces
|k_{3}+k_{4}|\sim|k_{1}+k_{2}|
, so we perform a dydadic decomposition of
|k_{1}+k_{2}|\sim N_{12}
, and the further almost orthogonal decomposition of
k_{j}\in I_{j}
for
|I_{j}|\sim N_{12}
and
j=1,2,3,4
. We note the bound
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{6}\lesssim m(N_{1}^{*})N_{1}^{*}m(N_{12})N_{12}
of Equation (
32
), which reduces us to bounding

\eqref{Equation: Resonant Base Estimate}\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{4}^{*}\gtrsim N\gg N_{5}^{*}}\sum_{N_{12}\lesssim N_{1}^{*}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1}\sim I_{2}\\
I_{3}\sim I_{4}\\
|I_{j}|\sim N_{12}\end{subarray}}\frac{N^{3s-3}(N_{1}^{*})^{-3s}m(N_{12})N_{12}}{N_{5}N_{6}}\\
\times\left|\int_{\lambda\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]}\prod_{j=1}^{4}P_{I_{j}}w_{N_{j}}\prod_{j=5}^{6}w_{N_{j}^{*}}\,dxdt\right|.

In order to handle the
6
-linear estimate, we consider first the case that

N_{12}^{2}\lesssim N_{5}^{*}N_{1},

and apply the base trilinear estimate of Proposition
8
twice. This affords us

\left|\int_{\lambda\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]}\prod_{j=1}^{4}P_{I_{j}}w_{N_{j}}\prod_{j=5}^{6}w_{N_{j}^{*}}\,dxdt\right|\lesssim(N_{1}^{*})^{0+}\left(\frac{N_{12}}{N_{1}}+\frac{1}{N}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{4}\|P_{I_{j}}w_{N_{j}}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}\prod_{j=5}^{6}\|w_{N_{j}}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}},

where we may further sum to obtain

\sum_{N_{12}^{2}\lesssim N_{5}^{*}N_{1}}(N_{1}^{*})^{0+}m(N_{12})N_{12}\left(\frac{N_{12}}{N_{1}}+\frac{1}{N}\right)\lesssim(N_{1}^{*})^{0+}N_{5}^{*}+N^{-s}N_{1}^{s+},

by Remark
7
and the summation restriction. If, on the other hand, we have

N_{5}^{*}N_{1}\ll N_{12}^{2},

then we may apply the enhanced version of Proposition
8
where

K\lesssim\frac{N_{5}^{*}N_{1}}{N_{12}},

and hence

\left|\int_{\lambda\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]}\prod_{j=1}^{4}P_{I_{j}}w_{N_{j}}\prod_{j=5}^{6}w_{N_{j}^{*}}\,dxdt\right|\lesssim(N_{1}^{*})^{0+}\left(\frac{N_{5}^{*}}{N_{12}}+\frac{1}{N}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{4}\|P_{I_{j}}w_{N_{j}}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}\prod_{j=5}^{6}\|w_{N_{j}}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}.

Summation again yields the bound

\sum_{N_{5}^{*}N_{1}\ll N_{12}^{2}\lesssim N_{1}^{2}}(N_{1}^{*})^{0+}m(N_{12})N_{12}\left(\frac{N_{5}^{*}}{N_{12}}+\frac{1}{N}\right)\lesssim(N_{1}^{*})^{0+}N_{5}^{*}+N^{-s}N_{1}^{s+}.

It follows that in either situation we have

\sum_{N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{4}^{*}\gtrsim N\gg N_{5}^{*}}\sum_{N_{12}\lesssim N_{1}^{*}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1}\sim I_{2}\\
I_{3}\sim I_{4}\\
|I_{j}|\sim N_{12}\end{subarray}}\frac{m(N_{12})N_{12}}{N^{3-3s}N_{1}^{3s-}N_{5}N_{6}}\left|\int_{\lambda\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]}\prod_{j=1}^{4}P_{I_{j}}w_{N_{j}}\prod_{j=5}^{6}w_{N_{j}^{*}}\,dxdt\right|\\
\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{4}^{*}\gtrsim N\gg N_{5}^{*}}N^{-3+}N_{1}^{0-}\prod_{j=1}^{6}\|w_{N_{j}}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}\lesssim N^{-3+}\|Iu\|_{Y^{1}_{T}}^{6},

which is our desired bound.

A_{322}
&

(ii)

&

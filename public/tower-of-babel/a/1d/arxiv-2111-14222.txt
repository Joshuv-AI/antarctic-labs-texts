1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
denote the
C^{\ast}
-algebra of all bounded
linear operators on a complex Hilbert space
\big(\mathcal{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\big)

and
I
stand for the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}
.
Every operator
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
can be represented as
A=\mathfrak{R}(A)+i\mathfrak{I}(A)
,
the Cartesian decomposition, where
\mathfrak{R}(A)=\frac{A+A^{*}}{2}
and
\mathfrak{I}(A)=\frac{A-A^{*}}{2i}

are the real and imaginary parts of
A
, respectively.
For
0\leq\nu\leq 1
, we define the weighted real and imaginary parts of
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
by

\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)=\nu A+(1-\nu)A^{*}
and
\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)=\nu(-iA)+(1-\nu)(-iA)^{*}
, respectively.
When
\nu=1/2
, we clearly have
\mathfrak{R}_{{}_{1/2}}(A)=\mathfrak{R}(A)
and
\mathfrak{I}_{{}_{1/2}}(A)=\mathfrak{I}(A)
.
Let
\|A\|
and
{\|A\|}_{2}
denote the usual operator norm and the Hilbert–Schmidt norm of
A
, respectively.
Recall that
A
belongs to the Hilbert–Schmidt class
\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{H})

if
{\|A\|}_{2}=\sqrt{{\rm tr}(A^{*}A)}
is finite, where the symbol
{\rm tr}
denotes the usual trace.
It is known that for
A,B\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle\left|{\rm tr}\left(AB\right)\right|\leq{\|A\|}_{2}{\|B\|}_{2}.

(1.1)

The numerical radius of
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
is defined by

\displaystyle w(A)=\sup\big\{|\langle Ax,x\rangle|:\,\,x\in\mathcal{H},\|x\|=1\big\}.

This concept is useful in studying linear operators and has attracted the attention of many authors
in the last few decades (e.g., see
[
7
,
9
,
11
,
14
,
19
,
20
,
28
]
, and their references).

An important and useful identity for the numerical radius (see, e.g.,
[
25
]
) is as follows:

\displaystyle w(A)=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}\left\|\mathfrak{R}(e^{i\theta}A)\right\|.

(1.2)

It is also well known that
w(\cdot)
defines a norm on

\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that for all
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|A\|\leq w(A)\leq\|A\|.

(1.3)

The inequalities in (
1.3
) are sharp.
The first inequality becomes an equality if
A
is square-zero (i.e.,
A^{2}=0
)
and the second inequality becomes an equality if
A
is normal in the sense that
A^{*}A=AA^{*}
;
see
[
12
,
13
]
.

Now, let
N(\cdot)
be an arbitrary norm on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
.
The norm
N(\cdot)
is self-adjoint if
N(A^{*})=N(A)
,
unitarily invariant if
N(VAU)=N(A)
and
weakly unitarily invariant if
N(U^{*}AU)=N(A)

for every
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
and unitary
U,V\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
.
The norms
\|\!\cdot\!\|
and
{\|\!\cdot\!\|}_{2}
are typical examples of self-adjoint and unitarily invariant norms.
Further, the norm
w(\cdot)
is self-adjoint and weakly unitarily invariant, but, it is not unitarily invariant.

Based on some operator theory studies on Hilbert spaces, several
generalizations for the concept of numerical radius have recently been introduced
[
1
,
5
,
22
,
29
]
.
One of these generalizations is the
\mathbb{A}
-numerical radius of an operator
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})

defined by

\displaystyle w_{\mathbb{A}}(A)=\sup\big\{|\langle Ax,\mathbb{A}x\rangle|:\,\,x\in\mathcal{H},\langle x,\mathbb{A}x\rangle=1\big\},

see, e.g.,
[
5
,
26
]
. Here,
\mathbb{A}
is a positive bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}
.

In
[
1
]
, in light of the equality (
1.2
), Abu-Omar and Kittaneh introduced the so-called generalized numerical radius,
by introducing the quantity

\displaystyle w_{{}_{N}}(A)=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}(e^{i\theta}A)\right),

where
N(\cdot)
is a given norm on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

Another generalization of the classical numerical radius has been introduced
in
[
29
]
, where the authors defined the numerical radius
r_{{}_{N}}(A)
induced as follows:

\displaystyle r_{{}_{N}}(A)=\sup\big\{|\xi|:\,\xi\in\mathbb{C},\,I\perp^{N}_{B}(A-\xi I)\big\},

in which the notation
\perp^{N}_{B}
refers to Birkhoff–James orthogonality.

Very recently, Sheikhhosseini et al.
[
22
]

introduced the so-called the weighted numerical radius:
If
0\leq\nu\leq 1
, the weighted numerical radius for
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
, denoted by
w_{{}_{\nu}}(A)
, is introduced by

\displaystyle w_{{}_{\nu}}(A)=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}\left\|\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)\right\|.

Hence, one can see that research involving possible generalizations of the
numerical radius with plausible properties has attracted researchers in this
field.

In Section 2 of this paper and inspired by
[
1
]
and
[
22
]
(see also
[
27
]
),
for an arbitrary norm
N(\cdot)
on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
0\leq\nu\leq 1
,
we define the
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\cdot)
as a generalization of the weighted numerical radius
and investigate basic properties of this norm and prove inequalities involving it.
In Section 3, when
N(\cdot)
is the Hilbert–Schmidt norm
{\|\!\cdot\!\|}_{2}
,
we first derive a formula for
w_{{}_{({\|\!\cdot\!\|}_{2},\nu)}}(A)
in terms of
{\|A\|}_{2}
and
{\rm tr}(A^{2})

and we then apply it to obtain several the weighted Hilbert–Schmidt numerical radius inequalities for
2\times 2
operator matrices.
In particular, we prove that

\displaystyle\frac{1+|1-2\nu|}{\sqrt{2}}w_{{}_{({\|\!\cdot\!\|}_{2},\nu)}}(A)\leq w_{{}_{({\|\!\cdot\!\|}_{2},\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)\\
\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}\right)\leq\sqrt{2}w_{{}_{({\|\!\cdot\!\|}_{2},\nu)}}(A).

In Section 4, we give a refinement of the triangle inequality for the Hilbert–Schmidt norm as follows:

\displaystyle{\|A+B\|}_{2}\leq\sqrt{2w_{{}_{({\|\!\cdot\!\|}_{2},\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)-(1-2\nu)^{2}{\|A-B\|}_{2}^{2}}\leq{\|A\|}_{2}+{\|B\|}_{2}.

Our results extend several results in the literature.

2.
A generalization of the weighted numerical radius

In this section, we introduce our new norm on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
which generalizes the weighted numerical radius,
and present basic properties of this norm.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
N(\cdot)
be a norm on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
and let
0\leq\nu\leq 1
.
The function
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\cdot)\colon\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is defined as

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)\right)\qquad(A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})).

Remark 2.2
.

Let
N(\cdot)
be a norm on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
and let
0\leq\nu\leq 1
.
For every
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
, it is easy to see that

w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(ie^{i\theta}A)\right)
.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
N(\cdot)
be the usual operator norm
\|\!\cdot\!\|
and
let
0\leq\nu\leq 1
. For every
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
, we have

w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)=w_{{}_{\nu}}(A)
.
Thus
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\cdot)
generalizes the weighted numerical radius
w_{{}_{\nu}}(\cdot)
,
which has been recently introduced in
[
22
]
.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
N(\cdot)
be a norm on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
and let
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
.
Obviously,
w_{{}_{(N,0)}}(A)=N(A^{*})
,
w_{{}_{(N,1)}}(A)=N(A)
and

w_{{}_{(N,1/2)}}(A)=w_{{}_{N}}(A)
.
Hence
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\cdot)
also generalizes the numerical radius
w_{{}_{N}}(\cdot)
,
which has been introduced in
[
1
]
.

Notation 2.5
.

When
N(\cdot)
is the Hilbert–Schmidt norm
{\|\!\cdot\!\|}_{2}
, the function
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\cdot)

is denoted by
w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(\cdot)
. That is,

w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A)=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}{\left\|\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)\right\|}_{2}
.

In the following theorem, we prove that
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\cdot)
is a norm on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
.
We use some ideas of
[
1
, Theorems 1,2]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
N(\cdot)
be a norm on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
and let
0\leq\nu\leq 1
.
Then
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\cdot)
is a norm on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
and the following inequalities hold for every
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
:

\displaystyle\max\left\{\nu N(A),(1-\nu)N(A^{*})\right\}\leq w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)\leq\max\left\{N(A),N(A^{*})\right\}.

Proof.

Let
X\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Obviously,
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(X)\geq 0
.
Let us now suppose
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(X)=0
.
If
\nu=0
, then by Remark
2.4
we get
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(X)=N(X^{*})=0
. Thus
X^{*}=0
, or equivalently,
X=0
.
Hence, we may assume that
\nu\neq 0
.
Then, by Definition
2.1
,

\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}X)=0
for every
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.
Taking
\theta=0
and
\theta=\pi/2
, we have
\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(X)=\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(iX)=0
.
Thus
X=\frac{\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(X)-i\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(iX)}{2\nu}=0
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. There exists
\varphi\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\alpha=|\alpha|e^{i\varphi}
.
Hence

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\alpha X)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}\alpha X)\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\nu e^{i(\theta+\varphi)}|\alpha|X+(1-\nu)e^{-i(\theta+\varphi)}|\alpha|X^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\phi\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\nu e^{i\phi}|\alpha|X+(1-\nu)e^{-i\phi}|\alpha|X^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=|\alpha|\displaystyle{\sup_{\phi\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\phi}X)\right)=|\alpha|w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(X).

Now, let
Y,Z\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
. We have

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(Y+Z)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}(Y+Z))\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}Y)+\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}Z)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}\left(N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}Y)\right)+N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}Z)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}Y)\right)+\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}Z)\right)

\displaystyle=w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(Y)+w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(Z).

Thus
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\cdot)
is subadditive and so
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\cdot)
is a norm on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

On the other hands, for every
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
, by Definition
2.1
we have

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)\geq N\left(\nu e^{i\theta}A+(1-\nu)e^{-i\theta}A^{*}\right)\qquad(\theta\in\mathbb{R}).

So, by taking
\theta=0
and
\theta=-\pi/2
in the above inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)\geq N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}A\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)\geq N\left(\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}A\right).

(2.1)

Since
\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}A+i\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}A=2\nu A
, by (
2.1
) and the triangle inequality for the norm
N(\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}A\right)+N\left(\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}A\right)}{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}A+i\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}A\right)}{2}=\frac{N\left(2\nu A\right)}{2}=\nu N\left(A\right),

and hence

\displaystyle\nu N\left(A\right)\leq w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A).

(2.2)

Replacing
A
and
\nu
in the above inequality by
A^{*}
and
1-\nu
, respectively, we have

\displaystyle(1-\nu)N\left(A^{*}\right)\leq w_{{}_{(N,(1-\nu))}}(A^{*}).

(2.3)

Since

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(N,(1-\nu))}}(A^{*})=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left((1-\nu)e^{i\theta}A^{*}+\nu e^{-i\theta}A\right)=w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A),

(2.4)

by (
2.3
), it follows that

\displaystyle(1-\nu)N\left(A^{*}\right)\leq w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A).

(2.5)

Now, (
2.2
) and (
2.5
) yield that

\displaystyle\max\left\{\nu N(A),(1-\nu)N(A^{*})\right\}\leq w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A).

(2.6)

Furthermore, by the triangle inequality for the norm
N(\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\nu e^{i\theta}A+(1-\nu)e^{-i\theta}A^{*}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\nu N\left(A\right)+(1-\nu)N\left(A^{*}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\nu\max\left\{N(A),N(A^{*})\right\}+(1-\nu)\max\left\{N(A),N(A^{*})\right\}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{N(A),N(A^{*})\right\},

and so

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)\leq\max\left\{N(A),N(A^{*})\right\}.

(2.7)

From (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we deduce the desired result.
∎

In the following result we state some properties of the norm
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\cdot)
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
N(\cdot)
be a norm on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
and let
0\leq\nu\leq 1
.
For every
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
, the following properties hold:

(i)

w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A^{*})=w_{{}_{(N,1-\nu)}}(A)
.

(ii)

If
A
is self-adjoint, then
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)=N(A)
.

(iii)

w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)=\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta,\varphi\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}((e^{i\theta}-ie^{i\varphi})A)\right)
.

Proof.

(i) It follows immediately from (
2.4
).

(ii) Since
A
is self-adjoint, we have
\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)=\left(\nu e^{i\theta}+(1-\nu)e^{-i\theta}\right)A
.
Thus

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta})A\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}\left|\nu e^{i\theta}+(1-\nu)e^{-i\theta}\right|N(A)

\displaystyle=N(A)\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}\sqrt{1-4\nu(1-\nu)\sin^{2}\theta}=N(A).

(iii)
We have

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)+\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)+\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(e^{i(\theta+\pi/2)}A)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta,\varphi\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)+\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(e^{i\varphi}A)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta,\varphi\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\nu\left(e^{i\theta}-ie^{i\varphi}\right)A+(1-\nu)\left(\left(e^{i\theta}-ie^{i\varphi}\right)A\right)^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta,\varphi\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}\left(\left(e^{i\theta}-ie^{i\varphi}\right)A\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta,\varphi\in\mathbb{R}}}w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}\left(\left(\left(e^{i\theta}-ie^{i\varphi}\right)A\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta,\varphi\in\mathbb{R}}}\left|e^{i\theta}-ie^{i\varphi}\right|w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)}{2}\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta,\varphi\in\mathbb{R}}}\sqrt{2-2\sin(\theta-\varphi)}=w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A),

and so
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)=\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta,\varphi\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}((e^{i\theta}-ie^{i\varphi})A)\right)
.
∎

We now derive some properties of the norm
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\cdot)
when
N(\cdot)
is a self-adjoint norm on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
N(\cdot)
be a self-adjoint norm on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
and let
0\leq\mu,\nu\leq 1
.
Then

(i)

w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\cdot)
is self-adjoint.

(ii)

w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\cdot)
is equivalent to
N(\cdot)
and the following inequalities hold for
every
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
:

\displaystyle\frac{1+|1-2\nu|}{2}N(A)\leq w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)\leq N(A).

(iii)

For every
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
, the function
f(\nu)=w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)
is a convex
continuous function on [0, 1] that attains its minimum at
\nu=1/2
and its maximum at
\nu=0,\nu=1
.

(iv)

For every
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)\leq w_{{}_{(N,\mu)}}(A)
if and only if
|\nu-1/2|\leq|\mu-1/2|
.

Proof.

(i) Let
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Since the norm
N(\cdot)
is self-adjoint, we have

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A^{*})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\nu e^{i\theta}A^{*}+(1-\nu)e^{-i\theta}A\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\nu e^{-i\theta}A+(1-\nu)e^{i\theta}A^{*}\right)=w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A).

(ii) Since
N(A^{*})=N(A)
for every
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
\max\{\nu,1-\nu\}=\frac{1+|1-2\nu|}{2}
,
the desired inequalities follow from Theorem
2.6
.

(iii) Let
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
and let
0\leq t\leq 1
. The following proof is a modification of the one given
by Sheikhhosseini et al.
[
22
, Theorem 2.5]
.
We have

\displaystyle f\left(t\nu+(1-t)\mu\right)

\displaystyle=w_{{}_{(N,\left(t\nu+(1-t)\mu\right))}}(A)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\left(t\nu+(1-t)\mu\right)}(e^{i\theta}A)\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(t\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)+(1-t)\mathfrak{R}_{\mu}(e^{i\theta}A)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}\Big(tN\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)\right)+(1-t)N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\mu}(e^{i\theta}A)\right)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq tw_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)+(1-t)w_{{}_{(N,\mu)}}(A)=tf(\nu)+(1-t)f(\mu),

and hence
f\left(t\nu+(1-t)\mu\right)\leq tf(\nu)+(1-t)f(\mu)
.
Therefore
f
is convex on
[0,1]
and so
f
is continuous on
(0,1)
.
Also, by (ii), we have

\displaystyle 0\leq N(A)-w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)\leq\frac{1-|1-2\nu|}{2}N(A).

Thus
f
is continuous at
\nu=0
and
\nu=1
. Hence
f
is continuous on
[0,1]
.
By (i) and Proposition
2.7
(i), we have
f(\nu)=f(1-\nu)
. Thus
f
is symmetric about
\nu=1/2
.
This shows that
f
attains its minimum at
\nu=1/2
and its maximum at

\nu=0,\nu=1
.

(iv) From (iii), the function
f(\nu)=w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)
is convex on [0, 1] and symmetric around
\nu=1/2
, it follows
that it is decreasing on
[0,1/2]
and increasing on
[1/2,1]
. Now, (iv) is trivial.
∎

Our next result reads as follows.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
N(\cdot)
be a norm on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
and let
0\leq\nu\leq 1
.

(i)

If
N(\cdot)
is weakly unitarily invariant, then so is
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\cdot)
.

(ii)

If
N(\cdot)
is unitarily invariant, then
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)\leq w_{\nu}(A)N(I)
.

Proof.

(i) Let
N(\cdot)
be weakly unitarily invariant and let
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
.
Let
U\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
be unitary. We have

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(U^{*}AU)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}U^{*}AU)\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(U^{*}\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)U\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)\right)=w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A).

Thus
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(\cdot)
is weakly unitarily invariant.

(ii) Let
N(\cdot)
be unitarily invariant and let
A\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Let
\epsilon>0
.
For every
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, since

\left\|\frac{\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)}{\left\|\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)\right\|+\epsilon}\right\|<1
,
by Gardner’s theorem (see, e.g.,
[
10
]
), there exist unitaries
U_{1},U_{2},\cdots U_{n}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)}{\left\|\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)\right\|+\epsilon}=\frac{U_{1}+U_{2}+\cdots+U_{n}}{n}.

Since the norm
N(\cdot)
is unitarily invariant, we have
N\left(U_{k}\right)=N\left(I\right)
for all
1\leq k\leq n
,
and therefore,

\displaystyle N\left(\frac{\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)}{\left\|\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)\right\|+\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{N\left(U_{1}+U_{2}+\cdots+U_{n}\right)}{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N\left(U_{1}\right)+N\left(U_{2}\right)+\cdots+N\left(U_{n}\right)}{n}=N\left(I\right).

From this it follows that

\displaystyle N\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)\right)\leq\left(\left\|\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)\right\|+\epsilon\right)N\left(I\right).

Taking the supremum over
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
in the above inequality, we get

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)\leq\left(w_{\nu}(A)+\epsilon\right)N\left(I\right).

Now, letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, we conclude
w_{{}_{(N,\nu)}}(A)\leq w_{\nu}(A)N(I)
.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

Since the Hilbert–Schmidt norm
{\|\cdot\|}_{2}
is unitarily invariant and

{\|I\|}_{2}=1
, by Proposition
2.9
(ii) for every
A\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
, we have
w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A)\leq w_{\nu}(A)
.

3.
The weighted Hilbert–Schmidt numerical radius inequalities for operator matrices

In this section, we first derive a formula for
w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A)
in terms of
{\|A\|}_{2}
and
{\rm tr}(A^{2})

and we then apply it to obtain several the weighted Hilbert–Schmidt numerical radius inequalities for
2\times 2
operator matrices.
These inequalities generalize known Hilbert–Schmidt numerical radius inequalities (see, e.g.,
[
4
,
6
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
21
,
24
]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
and let
0\leq\nu\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A)=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}(A^{2})\right|.

Proof.

For every
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle{\left\|\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(ie^{i\theta}A)\right\|}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\left(\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(ie^{i\theta}A)\right)^{*}\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(ie^{i\theta}A)\right)

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\Big(\left(\nu e^{-i\theta}A^{*}+(1-\nu)e^{i\theta}A\right)\left(\nu e^{i\theta}A+(1-\nu)e^{-i\theta}A^{*}\right)\Big)

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\Big(\nu^{2}A^{*}A+\nu(1-\nu)e^{2i\theta}A^{2}+e^{-2i\theta}\nu(1-\nu)(A^{*})^{2}+(1-\nu)^{2}AA^{*}\Big)

\displaystyle=\nu^{2}{\rm tr}\left(A^{*}A\right)+\nu(1-\nu)\left(e^{2i\theta}{\rm tr}\left(A^{2}\right)+e^{-2i\theta}{\rm tr}\left((A^{2})^{*}\right)\right)+(1-\nu)^{2}{\rm tr}\left(AA^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\nu^{2}{\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)\mathfrak{R}\left(e^{2i\theta}{\rm tr}\left(A^{2}\right)\right)+(1-\nu)^{2}{\|A\|}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)\mathfrak{R}\left(e^{2i\theta}{\rm tr}\left(A^{2}\right)\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}{\left\|\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(e^{i\theta}A)\right\|}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}\Big((2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)\mathfrak{R}\left(e^{2i\theta}{\rm tr}\left(A^{2}\right)\right)\Big)

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}(A^{2})\right|.

∎

Remark 3.2

[

In fact if

\displaystyle w_{{}_{2}}^{2}(A)=\frac{1}{2}{\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left|{\rm tr}(A^{2})\right|

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
and let

. Then

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1}{\|A\|}_{2}\leq w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A)\leq{\|A\|}_{2}.

Proof.

The proof follows from Theorem
3.1
and the fact that
0\leq\left|{\rm tr}(A^{2})\right|\leq{\|A\|}_{2}^{2}
.
∎

Remark 3.4

The following lemma is known in the literature (see, e.g.,

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
X,Y,Z,W\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
. Then

\displaystyle{\left\|\begin{bmatrix}X&Y\\
Z&W\end{bmatrix}\right\|}_{2}^{2}={\|X\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|Y\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|Z\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|W\|}_{2}^{2}.

In the next theorem we compute the weighted Hilbert–Schmidt numerical
radius for certain
2\times 2
operator matrices defined on
\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
A,B,C,D\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
and let
0\leq\nu\leq 1
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

(i)

w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B&0\end{bmatrix}\right)=w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&B\\
A&0\end{bmatrix}\right)
.

(ii)

w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B&0\end{bmatrix}\right)=w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
e^{i\theta}B&0\end{bmatrix}\right)
.

(iii)

w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&A\\
-A&-A\end{bmatrix}\right)=2\sqrt{2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1}{\|A\|}_{2}
.

(iv)

w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
A&0\end{bmatrix}\right)=\sqrt{2}w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A)
.

(v)

w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\right)=w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A)+(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\|B\|}_{2}^{2}
.

(vi)

If
{\rm tr}(BC)=0
, then

w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&iA\end{bmatrix}\right)=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1)\left(2{\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|B\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|C\|}_{2}^{2}\right)
.

(vii)

If
A,B
are positive, then

w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B&0\end{bmatrix}\right)=\frac{{\|A+B\|}_{2}^{2}+(1-2\nu)^{2}{\|A-B\|}_{2}^{2}}{2}
.

(viii)

If
A,B
are self-adjoint, then

w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\
0&B\end{bmatrix}\right)=w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A)+w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(B)
.

(ix)

If
A,B
are self-adjoint, then

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
B&A\end{bmatrix}\right)=w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A+B)+w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A-B).

Proof.

(i)
Let
U=\begin{bmatrix}0&I\\
I&0\end{bmatrix}
. Then
U
is a unitary operator on
\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}
and by Proposition
2.9
(i) we have

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B&0\end{bmatrix}\right)=w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(U\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B&0\end{bmatrix}U^{*}\right)=w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&B\\
A&0\end{bmatrix}\right).

(ii) It is similar than (i), only we use
\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
0&e^{i\theta/2}I\end{bmatrix}
instead of
\begin{bmatrix}0&I\\
I&0\end{bmatrix}
.

(iii)
Let
U=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}I&I\\
-I&I\end{bmatrix}
. Then
U
is a unitary operator on
\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}
.
Since

{\begin{bmatrix}A&A\\
-A&-A\end{bmatrix}}^{2}=0
, by Theorem
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&A\\
-A&-A\end{bmatrix}\right)=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\left\|\begin{bmatrix}A&A\\
-A&-A\end{bmatrix}\right\|}_{2}^{2}.

Also, since the Hilbert–Schmidt norm
{\|\cdot\|}_{2}
is unitarily invariant, we have

\displaystyle{\left\|\begin{bmatrix}A&A\\
-A&-A\end{bmatrix}\right\|}_{2}={\left\|U\begin{bmatrix}A&A\\
-A&-A\end{bmatrix}U^{*}\right\|}_{2}=2{\left\|\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
A&0\end{bmatrix}\right\|}_{2}.

Now, utilizing Lemma
3.7
, we deduce the desired result.

(iv) From Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
3.7
it follows that

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
A&0\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\left\|\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
A&0\end{bmatrix}\right\|}_{2}^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}\left({\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
A&0\end{bmatrix}}^{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1)\left({\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|A\|}_{2}^{2}\right)+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A^{2}&0\\
0&A^{2}\end{bmatrix}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=2\Big((2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}A^{2}\right|\Big)=2w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A),

which gives (iv).

(v) By Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
3.7
, we have

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\left\|\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\right\|}_{2}^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}\left({\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}}^{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1)\left({\left\|A\right\|}_{2}^{2}+{\left\|B\right\|}_{2}^{2}\right)+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A^{2}&AB\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\|B\|}_{2}^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}(A^{2})\right|

\displaystyle=w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A)+(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\|B\|}_{2}^{2}.

(vi) Let
{\rm tr}(BC)=0
. We have

\displaystyle\left|{\rm tr}\left({\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&iA\end{bmatrix}}^{2}\right)\right|=\left|{\rm tr}\left({\begin{bmatrix}A^{2}+BC&AB+iBA\\
CA+iAC&CB-A^{2}\end{bmatrix}}^{2}\right)\right|=2\left|{\rm tr}(BC)\right|=0.

Also, by Lemma
3.7
, we get

{\left\|\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&iA\end{bmatrix}\right\|}_{2}^{2}=2{\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|B\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|C\|}_{2}^{2}
.
So, by Theorem
3.1
, we deduce the desired result.

(vii) Let
A,B
be positive. Then by Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
3.7
it follows that

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B&0\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\left\|\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B&0\end{bmatrix}\right\|}_{2}^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}\left({\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B&0\end{bmatrix}}^{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1)\left({\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|B\|}_{2}^{2}\right)+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}\left(\begin{bmatrix}AB&0\\
0&BA\end{bmatrix}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1)\left({\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|B\|}_{2}^{2}\right)+4\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}\left(AB\right)\right|

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1)\left({\rm tr}\left(A^{2}\right)+{\rm tr}\left(B^{2}\right)\right)+4\nu(1-\nu){\rm tr}\left(AB\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm tr}\left((A+B)^{2}\right)+(1-2\nu)^{2}{\rm tr}\left((A-B)^{2}\right)}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\|A+B\|}_{2}^{2}+(1-2\nu)^{2}{\|A-B\|}_{2}^{2}}{2}.

Hence
w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B&0\end{bmatrix}\right)=\frac{{\|A+B\|}_{2}^{2}+(1-2\nu)^{2}{\|A-B\|}_{2}^{2}}{2}
.

(viii) Let
A,B
be self-adjoint. Then
A^{2},B^{2}
are positive and so

\displaystyle\left|{\rm tr}(A^{2}+B^{2})\right|=\left|{\rm tr}(A^{2})\right|+\left|{\rm tr}(B^{2})\right|.

Now, by Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
3.7
, we have

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\
0&B\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\left\|\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\
0&B\end{bmatrix}\right\|}_{2}^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}\left({\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\
0&B\end{bmatrix}}^{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1)\left({\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|B\|}_{2}^{2}\right)+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A^{2}&0\\
0&B^{2}\end{bmatrix}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1)\left({\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|B\|}_{2}^{2}\right)+2\nu(1-\nu)\left(\left|{\rm tr}(A^{2})\right|+\left|{\rm tr}(B^{2})\right|\right)

\displaystyle=w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A)+w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(B).

(ix) Let
A,B
be self-adjoint and let

U=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}I&I\\
-I&I\end{bmatrix}
.
So, by Proposition
2.9
(i) and (viii), we obtain

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
B&A\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(U\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
B&A\end{bmatrix}U^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A+B&0\\
0&A-B\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A+B)+w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A-B).

∎

In the following theorem we give an upper bound for the weighted Hilbert–Schmidt numerical radius of the
general
2\times 2
operator matrix

\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
A,B,C,D\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
and let
0\leq\nu\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}\right)\leq w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A)+w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(D)+{\|B\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|C\|}_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
, Lemma
3.7
and the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\left\|\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}\right\|}_{2}^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}\left({\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}}^{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1)\left({\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|B\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|C\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|D\|}_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}\left(A^{2}\right)+{\rm tr}\left(D^{2}\right)+2{\rm tr}\left(BC\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}\left(A^{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1){\|D\|}_{2}^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}\left(D^{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1)\left({\|B\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|C\|}_{2}^{2}\right)+4\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}\left(BC\right)\right|

\displaystyle=w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A)+w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(D)+(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1)\left({\|B\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|C\|}_{2}^{2}\right)+4\nu(1-\nu)\left|{\rm tr}\left(BC\right)\right|.

(3.1)

Hence, by (
3
), (
1.1
) and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, we conclude

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle\leq w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A)+w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(D)+(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1)\left({\|B\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|C\|}_{2}^{2}\right)+4\nu(1-\nu){\|B\|}_{2}{\|C\|}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A)+w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(D)+(2\nu^{2}-2\nu+1)\left({\|B\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|C\|}_{2}^{2}\right)+2\nu(1-\nu)\left({\|B\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|C\|}_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(A)+w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}(D)+{\|B\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|C\|}_{2}^{2}.

.

and let

(i)

(ii)

Our final result in this section is a generalization of

.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
and let
0\leq\nu\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1+|1-2\nu|}{\sqrt{2}}w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A)\leq w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)\\
\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}\right)\leq\sqrt{2}w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A).

Proof.

First, note that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)+i\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)=2\nu A\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)-i\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)=2(1-\nu)A^{*}.

(3.2)

Now, let
U=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}I&I\\
-I&I\end{bmatrix}
.
Then, by Proposition
2.9
(i), (
3.2
),
Corollary
3.11
(i), Theorem
3.8
(ii), and Theorem
3.8
(iv), we have

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)\\
\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=2\nu w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{1}{2\nu}\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)\\
\frac{i}{2\nu}\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=2\nu w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(U\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{1}{2\nu}\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)\\
\frac{i}{2\nu}\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}U^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\nu w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&\frac{1-\nu}{\nu}A^{*}\\
-\frac{1-\nu}{\nu}A^{*}&-A\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\nu w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\
0&-A\end{bmatrix}\right)+\nu w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{1-\nu}{\nu}A^{*}\\
-\frac{1-\nu}{\nu}A^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\nu w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A)+\nu w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{1-\nu}{\nu}A^{*}\\
\frac{1-\nu}{\nu}A^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\nu w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A)+\sqrt{2}\nu w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\frac{1-\nu}{\nu}A^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\nu w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A)+\sqrt{2}(1-\nu)w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A)=\sqrt{2}w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A).

Thus

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)\\
\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}\right)\leq\sqrt{2}w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A).

(3.3)

Also, by (
3.2
), Theorem
3.8
(iv), and Theorem
3.8
(i)-(ii), we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\nu w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)+i\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)+i\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)\\
\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)+i\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)\\
i\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}0&i\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)\\
\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)\\
i\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}\right)+\frac{1}{2}w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&i\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)\\
\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)\\
\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}\right),

and hence

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\nu w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A)\leq w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)\\
\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}\right).

(3.4)

Further, by a similar argument, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}(1-\nu)w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A)\leq w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)\\
\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}\right).

(3.5)

So, by (
3.4
) and (
3.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\max\left\{\sqrt{2}\nu w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A),\sqrt{2}(1-\nu)w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A)\right\}\leq w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)\\
\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}\right),

(3.6)

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\frac{1+|1-2\nu|}{\sqrt{2}}w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}(A)\leq w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}(A)\\
\mathfrak{I}_{\nu}(A)&0\end{bmatrix}\right).

(3.7)

From (
3.3
) and (
3.7
), we deduce the desired result.
∎

4.
An application

In this section we obtain a refinement of the triangle inequality for the Hilbert–Schmidt norm.
In order to achieve our goal, we need the following lemma. It is well-known and can be found in
[
23
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
. Then

\displaystyle 2\left({\|X\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|Y\|}_{2}^{2}\right)={\|X-Y\|}_{2}^{2}+{\|X+Y\|}_{2}^{2}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
and let
0\leq\nu\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle{\|A+B\|}_{2}\leq\sqrt{2w_{{}_{(2,\nu)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)-(1-2\nu)^{2}{\|A-B\|}_{2}^{2}}\leq{\|A\|}_{2}+{\|B\|}_{2}.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
4.1
with
X:=\frac{A+B}{2}
and

Y:=\frac{(1-2\nu)(A-B)}{2}
, we get

\displaystyle 2\left({\left\|\frac{A+B}{2}\right\|}_{2}^{2}+{\left\|\frac{(1-2\nu)(A-B)}{2}\right\|}_{2}^{2}\right)={\left\|\nu A+(1-\nu)B\right\|}_{2}^{2}+{\left\|(1-\nu)A+\nu B\right\|}_{2}^{2}.

This implies

\displaystyle{\left\|A+B\right\|}_{2}^{2}=2{\left\|\nu A+(1-\nu)B\right\|}_{2}^{2}+2{\left\|(1-\nu)A+\nu B\right\|}_{2}^{2}-(1-2\nu)^{2}{\left\|A-B\right\|}_{2}^{2},

and so

\displaystyle{\left\|A+B\right\|}_{2}^{2}=2{\left\|\nu A+(1-\nu)B\right\|}_{2}^{2}+2{\left\|(1-\nu)A^{*}+\nu B^{*}\right\|}_{2}^{2}-(1-2\nu)^{2}{\left\|A-B\right\|}_{2}^{2}.

Therefore, by Lemma
3.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\left\|A+B\right\|}_{2}^{2}=2{\left\|\begin{bmatrix}0&\nu A+(1-\nu)B\\
(1-\nu)A^{*}+\nu B^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right\|}_{2}^{2}-(1-2\nu)^{2}{\left\|A-B\right\|}_{2}^{2},

or equivalently,

\displaystyle{\left\|A+B\right\|}_{2}^{2}=2{\left\|\nu\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}+(1-\nu){\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}}^{*}\right\|}_{2}^{2}-(1-2\nu)^{2}{\left\|A-B\right\|}_{2}^{2}.

Hence

\displaystyle{\left\|A+B\right\|}_{2}^{2}=2{\left\|\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)\right\|}_{2}^{2}-(1-2\nu)^{2}{\left\|A-B\right\|}_{2}^{2}.

(4.1)

Since
{\left\|\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)\right\|}_{2}\leq w_{{}_{(2,v)}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)
, by (
4.1
) it follows that

\displaystyle{\left\|A+B\right\|}_{2}^{2}\leq 2w_{{}_{(2,v)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)-(1-2\nu)^{2}{\left\|A-B\right\|}_{2}^{2}.

(4.2)

Also, by Lemma
3.7
and the triangle inequality for the norm
{\|\cdot\|}_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle 2

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,v)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)-(1-2\nu)^{2}{\left\|A-B\right\|}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}{\left\|\mathfrak{R}_{\nu}\left(e^{i\theta}\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)\right\|}_{2}^{2}-(1-2\nu)^{2}{\left\|A-B\right\|}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}{\left\|\begin{bmatrix}0&\nu e^{i\theta}A+(1-\nu)e^{-i\theta}B\\
(1-\nu)e^{-i\theta}A^{*}+\nu e^{i\theta}B^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right\|}_{2}^{2}-(1-2\nu)^{2}{\left\|A-B\right\|}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\displaystyle{\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}}\left({\left\|\nu e^{i\theta}A+(1-\nu)e^{-i\theta}B\right\|}_{2}^{2}+{\left\|(1-\nu)e^{-i\theta}A^{*}+\nu e^{i\theta}B^{*}\right\|}_{2}^{2}\right)-(1-2\nu)^{2}{\left\|A-B\right\|}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Big(\nu{\left\|A\right\|}_{2}+(1-\nu){\left\|B\right\|}_{2}\Big)^{2}+2\Big((1-\nu){\left\|A\right\|}_{2}+\nu{\left\|B\right\|}_{2}\Big)^{2}-(1-2\nu)^{2}\Big|{\left\|A\right\|}_{2}-{\left\|B\right\|}_{2}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle={\|A\|}_{2}^{2}+2{\|A\|}_{2}{\|B\|}_{2}+{\|B\|}_{2}^{2},

and hence

\displaystyle 2

\displaystyle w_{{}_{(2,v)}}^{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)-(1-2\nu)^{2}{\left\|A-B\right\|}_{2}^{2}\leq\left({\|A\|}_{2}+{\|B\|}_{2}\right)^{2}.

(4.3)

Now, by (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we deduce the desired result.
∎

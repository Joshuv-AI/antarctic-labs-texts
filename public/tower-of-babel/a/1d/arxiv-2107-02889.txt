2.
Curvature tensors in conformally flat
(GR)_{4}

In this section we thoroughly investigate the curvature properties of conformally flat
(GR)_{4}
. We prove the following result without any additional assumption on the spacetime or the associated vectors which is a common practice while studying this topic.

Theorem 2.1
.

A conformally flat
(GR)_{4}
is an Einstein manifold, in particular,

R_{kl}=-\varepsilon\lambda g_{kl}

where
\lambda=A^{i}B_{i}
and
A^{i}A_{i}=\varepsilon=\pm 1
, provided
A^{i}
is a unit vector.
Furthermore, it is either a de Sitter space or an anti-de Sitter space.

Proof.

Since the spacetime is generalized Ricci recurrent, the Ricci curvature tensor
R_{kl}
must satisfy (
1
)
with one additional assumption that
A^{i}
is a unit vector with
A^{i}A_{i}=\varepsilon=\pm 1
.
Contracting
j
and
l
in (
1
) we obtain

\nabla_{i}R=RA_{i}+4B_{i}.

(5)

Contracting
i
and
l
in (
1
) we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{j}R=A_{l}R^{l}_{j}+B_{j}.

(6)

It follows from (
5
) and (
6
) that

2A_{l}R^{l}_{i}=RA_{i}+2B_{i}.

(7)

On the other hand, from the conformal flatness of the spacetime we have

\nabla_{i}R_{kl}-\nabla_{k}R_{il}=\frac{g_{kl}\nabla_{i}R-g_{il}\nabla_{k}R}{6}.

Using (
1
) and (
5
) in the above equation we calculate

6A_{i}R_{kl}-6A_{k}R_{il}=RA_{i}g_{kl}-RA_{k}g_{il}-2B_{i}g_{kl}+2B_{k}g_{il},

(8)

Transvecting (
8
) with
A^{i}
we obtain

\displaystyle 6\varepsilon R_{kl}=\varepsilon Rg_{kl}-2A^{i}B_{i}g_{kl}+2RA_{k}A_{l}+2A_{l}B_{k}+6A_{k}B_{l}.

(9)

Swapping
k
and
l
in (

) we obtain

6{\varepsilon}R_{lk}={\varepsilon}Rg_{lk}-2A^{i}B_{i}g_{lk}+2RA_{l}A_{k}+2A_{k}B_{k}+6A_{l}B_{k}.

These two equations imply

B_{i}={\varepsilon}\lambda A_{i},\quad(\lambda=A^{l}B_{l}),

(10)

Armed with this significant result, using (
9
) we can conclude that the Ricci curvature tensor and the Ricci scalar satisfy

6R_{kl}=(R-2{\varepsilon}\lambda)g_{kl}+(2R+8{\varepsilon}\lambda){\varepsilon}A_{k}A_{l},\quad 2A_{l}R^{l}_{i}=(R+2{\varepsilon}\lambda)A_{i}

(11)

\nabla_{i}R=(R+4{\varepsilon}\lambda)A_{i},

(12)

Covariantly differentiating (
11
) with respect to

we obtain

\displaystyle 6\nabla_{i}R_{kl}=

\displaystyle(\nabla_{i}R-2{\varepsilon}\nabla_{i}\lambda)g_{kl}+(2\nabla_{i}R+8{\varepsilon}\nabla_{i}\lambda){\varepsilon}A_{k}A_{l}+(2R+8{\varepsilon}\lambda){\varepsilon}\nabla_{i}(A_{k}A_{l})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(R+4{\varepsilon}\lambda)A_{i}-2{\varepsilon}\nabla_{i}\lambda\}g_{kl}+\{(2R+8{\varepsilon}\lambda)A_{i}+8{\varepsilon}\nabla_{i}\lambda\}{\varepsilon}A_{k}A_{l}

\displaystyle+(2R+8{\varepsilon}\lambda){\varepsilon}\nabla_{i}(A_{k}A_{l}).

On the other hand, (

) and (

\displaystyle 6\nabla_{i}R_{kl}=

\displaystyle(R+4{\varepsilon}\lambda)A_{i}g_{kl}+(2R+8{\varepsilon}\lambda){\varepsilon}A_{i}A_{k}A_{l}.

These two equations give

\displaystyle-{\varepsilon}g_{kl}\nabla_{i}\lambda+4A_{k}A_{l}\nabla_{i}\lambda+(R+4{\varepsilon}\lambda){\varepsilon}\nabla_{i}(A_{k}A_{l})=0.

Transvecting with
A^{k}
and

we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\lambda=0.

(13)

It follows from these two equations that

\displaystyle(R+4{\varepsilon}\lambda)\nabla_{i}(A_{k}A_{l})=0.

(14)

We consider two cases:
R+4{\varepsilon}\lambda\neq 0
and
R+4{\varepsilon}\lambda=0
.

a:

If
R+4{\varepsilon}\lambda\neq 0
, we have
\nabla_{i}A_{l}=0
and so
A_{l}R_{i}^{l}=0
.
It follows from (

R=-2{\varepsilon}\lambda.

(15)

It follows from this equation and (

\nabla_{i}R=-2{\varepsilon}\nabla_{i}\lambda=0.

After applying this to (

b:

Suppose that

(16)

Therefore, using (

meaning that it is an Einstein space. As a conformaly flat Einstein space is of constant sectional curvature.
We conclude that a conformally flat generalized Ricci recurrent spacetime is either a de Sitter space or an anti-de Sitter space.

Corollary 2.1
.

In a conformally flat
(GR)_{4}
, the Gauss-Bonnet term can be expressed as

\mathcal{G}=\frac{8}{3}\lambda^{2},

provided the unit vector
A^{i}
is not parallel.

Proof.

In any
4
-dimensional conformally flat spacetime,
\mathcal{G}
can be expressed as

\mathcal{G}=-2R_{kl}R^{kl}+\frac{2}{3}R^{2},

(18)

by (
17
) which reduces to

\mathcal{G}=\frac{8}{3}\lambda^{2}.

∎

Theorem 2.2
.

In a conformally flat
(GR)_{4}
satisfying
f(R,\mathcal{G})
-gravity, if the four-velocity vector is identical with
A^{i}
, then the effective pressure
p^{\text{eff}}
and the effective energy density
\rho^{\text{eff}}
are given by

p^{\text{eff}}=p^{(m)}+\frac{1}{\kappa}\left(\frac{f}{2}-\lambda f_{R}-\frac{4\lambda^{2}}{3}f_{\mathcal{G}}-\lambda\right),

\rho^{\text{eff}}=\rho^{(m)}-\frac{1}{\kappa}\left(\frac{f}{2}-\lambda f_{R}-\frac{4\lambda^{2}}{3}f_{\mathcal{G}}-\lambda\right).

Proof.

Using the results of Theorem
2.1
and Corollary
2.1
in the Gauss-Bonnet gravity equations (
4
), after some calculations we obtain

\kappa T^{\text{eff}}_{ik}=\kappa T^{\text{matter}}_{ik}+\left(\frac{f}{2}-\lambda f_{R}-\frac{4\lambda^{2}}{3}f_{\mathcal{G}}-\lambda\right)g_{ik}.

(19)

Hence the result.
∎

f(R,\mathcal{G})
-gravity in a
(GR)_{4}

Several models of
f(R,\mathcal{G})
gravity theories have been proposed in the literature. In this section, we consider two different models of

gravity theories to analyze our results. To examine different energy conditions for both the models we consider the vacuum case for simplicity

]
, that is,
\rho^{(m)}=p^{(m)}=0

Case:I

f(R,\mathcal{G})=R+\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}
. R. Myrzakulov et al
[
28
]
also considered this type of

model. In this case, the effective energy density and the effective pressure for a perfect fluid matter as follows:

\rho^{eff}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\left[2\lambda+\frac{2\lambda^{2}}{3\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}}-\left(\frac{R+\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}}{2}\right)\right]

(20)

and

p^{eff}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\left[\left(\frac{R+\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}}{2}\right)-2\lambda-\frac{2\lambda^{2}}{3\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}}\right]

(21)

We know that the relationship between energy density and pressure given by
\frac{p}{\rho}
is known as the equation of state parameter(EoS). In this case, the equation of state parameter turns out to be
\omega=-1
. Hence the universe is dominated by cosmological constant and the given model is consistent with well established
\Lambda
CDM theory. As weak energy condition(WEC) is the combination of positive density and null energy condition(NEC) and in the present setting NEC is zero, thus we examine the behavior of dominant energy condition(DEC), strong energy condition(SEC), and the density parameter.

4.
Discussion

Nowadays, to describe the observed late-time cosmic acceleration the modified theories of gravity are considered as the most valid candidate. In this article, we have studied the effect of the geometrical constraint of
(GR)_{4}
type spacetime on an interesting gravity theory, namely, the
f(R,\mathcal{G})
-gravity. To examine the self consistencies of these modified theories of gravity, the different energy conditions are favorable candidates and these energy conditions can be obtained via the popular Raychaudhuri equation. In this manuscript, we have investigated the null, dominant, weak, and strong energy conditions under
f(R,\mathcal{G})
-gravity in a conformally flat generalized Ricci recurrent perfect fluid space-time. We have taken two different
f(R,\mathcal{G})
models,
f(R,\mathcal{G})=R+\sqrt{\mathcal{G}}
and another one is
f(R,\mathcal{G})=R^{2}+\mathcal{G}ln\mathcal{G}
. We conclude that NEC is always satisfied in the present setting, weak and dominant energy conditions are satisfied when
-5\leq R\leq 1.5
,
1\leq\mathcal{G}\leq 3.5
and
2\leq R\leq 3
,
2\leq\mathcal{G}\leq 3
for the two models respectively provided with
\lambda>0
while the strong energy condition shows the negative behavior for both the models. Hence both the models have good agreement with the current observational findings which depict the accelerated expansion of the universe.

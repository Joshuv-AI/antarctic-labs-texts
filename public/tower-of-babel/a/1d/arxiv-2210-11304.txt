Maximal amenable subgroups of arithmetic groups
Vadim Alekseev, Alessandro Carderi

Abstract
. By classifying S S -maximal amenable subgroups of algebraic groups over a global field of characteristic zero, we obtain a complete classification of maximal amenable subgroups up to commensurability in the respective arithmetic groups. Futhermore, we prove that these commensurably maximal amenable subgroups are singular and therefore give rise to maximal amenable von Neumann subalgebras.

20G30, 20F65, 43A07, 46L10

1.
Introduction

In this article we are studying amenable subgroups of arithmetic groups and their ambient locally compact groups. By the Tits alternative
[
Tit72
]
, all amenable subgroups of arithmetic groups are virtually solvable and hence structurally easy to understand; similarly, amenable real Lie groups are composed of solvable and compact real Lie groups and hence again structurally easy to handle. However, understanding maximal amenable subgroups of a given group is a much harder task. For real Lie groups this has been accomplished by Moore
[
Moo79
]
. The primary goal of this article is to understand maximal amenable subgroups of arithmetic groups; since the latter ultimately come from algebraic groups, our analysis heavily relies on structure theory of algebraic groups wherefore our results can also be understood as an extension of Moore’s work.

Classifying all maximal amenable subgroups of arithmetic groups is a challenging task due to presence of finite maximal amenable subgroups, for instance,
\mathbb{Z}/6
in
\mathbf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/6\ast_{\mathbb{Z}/2}\mathbb{Z}/4
. However, the situation becomes much more tractable if one works up to finite index sub- and overgroups, thus considering maximal amenable commensurability classes instead of maximal amenable subgroups; every such class contains a maximal amenable subgroup. For example, in the realm of hyperbolic groups the work of Gromov
[
Gro87
]
yields complete classification of infinite maximal amenable subgroups: they are stabilisers of couples of points on the Gromov boundary. In particular, their commensurability classes are maximal amenable.

One of our main results is the complete classification of commensurably maximal amenable subgroups in arithmetic groups over a field of characteristic zero. They correspond to algebraic subgroups satisfying a maximal amenability condition with respect to a given set
S
of places of the ground field. Surprisingly, these algebraic subgroups can be bigger than the Zariski closure of the maximal amenable subgroup, so that establishing the correspondence requires some delicate considerations. The natural counterpart to commensurability in the setting of algebraic groups considered over a given set of places is working up to
S
-cocompact sub- and overgroups, meaning that over every place
v\in S
the group becomes cocompact. With this idea, the relevant condition on the algebraic subgroup
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
turns out to be “
S
-cocompact maximal amenability”, and our classification result describes
S
-cocompactly maximal amenable subgroups
\mathbf{H}
of a given algebraic group
\mathbf{G}
. It turns out that upon fixing the unipotent radical
\mathbf{U}
of such a subgroup the quotient has an
S
-cocompact subtorus. This torus has no
S
-cocompact subgroups and is
S
-cocompact in its centralizer. Together with a rank condition which ensures that the torus normalizes
\mathbf{U}
and not a bigger unipotent subgroup, we get the definition of an
S
-ample torus (see Definition
3.16
) which allows us to formulate the classification result:

Theorem A

(Theorem
3.22
)
.

Let
K
be a global field of characteristic zero,
\mathbf{G}
be a connected
K
-group and
S\subseteq\mathscr{R}
be a set of valuations.
Every
S
-cocompactly maximal amenable subgroup
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
is
S
-cocompactly equivalent to a subgroup
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{T}}\leqslant\mathbf{H}
of the form

\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{T}}\coloneqq\mathbf{T}\ltimes\mathbf{U},

where
\mathbf{U}
is the unipotent radical of the
K
-parabolic subgroup
\mathbf{P}\coloneqq\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{U})
of
\mathbf{G}
with a Levi decomposition
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{L}\ltimes\mathbf{U}
, and
\mathbf{T}\leqslant\mathbf{L}
is an
S
-ample torus. Conversely, the normalizer of every subgroup
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{T}}
as above is
S
-cocompactly maximal amenable.

By taking the
K(S)
-points, we obtain the corresponding classification of commensurably maximal subgroups in arithmetic groups. Let us remark that we don’t assume
S
to be finite; in particular, our result also cover commensurably maximal amenable subgroups of
\mathbf{G}(K)
.

Theorem B

(see Proposition
4.7
)
.

A subgroup
\Lambda<\mathbf{G}(K(S))
is commensurably maximal amenable if and only if there is an
S
-cocompactly maximal amenable subgroup
\mathbf{H}<\mathbf{G}
such that
\Lambda=\mathbf{H}(K(S))
.

Furthermore, we prove that all commensurably maximal amenable subgroups verify a stronger condition: they are singular in the ambient arithmetic groups.

Definition
.

An amenable subgroup
\Lambda<\Gamma
is singular if there is a continuous action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
on a compact space such that for any
\Lambda
-invariant probability measure
\mu
and for all
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\Lambda
, the probability measures
\mu
and
\gamma_{*}\mu
are singular.

The notion of singularity comes from the context of von Neumann algebras, it was introduced in
[
BC15
]
, where it was shown that all singular subgroups give rise to maximal amenable subalgebras. Currently, this is the main source of maximal amenable subalgebras.

In hyperbolic groups every commensurably maximal amenable subgroup is singular with respect to the action on the Gromov boundary
[
BC15
, Proposition 2.1]
. Other examples of singular subgroups were obtained for acylindrically hyperbolic groups in
[
JN20
, Theorem 3.2]
. Beyond hyperbolicity, singularity was known for HNN extensions and amalgamated free products
[
BC15
, Proposition 2.4, Proposition 2.5]
and for certain particular examples of maximal amenable subgroups of lattices in real Lie groups
[
BC15
, Proposition 2.6, Proposition 2.7]
. Our results extend this to all commensurably maximal amenable subgroups in arithmetic groups over a field of characteristic zero, completely classifying singular subgroups in these groups.

Theorem C

(Theorem
4.10
)
.

Let
K
be a global field of characteristic
0
and let
S
be a set of places of
K
satisfying
\mathscr{R}_{\infty}\setminus\mathscr{T}(\mathbf{G})\subseteq S
. Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected
K
-group and
\mathbf{H}
be an
S
-cocompactly maximal amenable subgroup. Then
\Lambda\coloneqq\mathbf{H}(K(S))
is a singular subgroup of
\Gamma\coloneqq\mathbf{G}(K(S))
. In particular, its von Neumann algebra
L\Lambda
is maximal amenable in
L\Gamma
.

The space
X
which is responsible for singularity of
\Lambda
can be easily definied: it is just the product of the Furstenberg boundaries over places in
S
. However, the proof that we present here is much more complicated than in the previous cases: in our context the measure preserved by the amenable subgroup can be diffuse and the support of the translated measure is not necessarily disjoint, as was the case in all previous examples. This will force us to do a much finer analysis of the action on the Furstenberg boundary, heavily using the structure obtained in our classification result.

3.
S
-maximal amenable algebraic subgroups

In this section, we will fix a non-empty subset
S\subseteq\mathscr{R}
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be an algebraic group defined over
K
and let
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
be a
K
-defined subgroup.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
\mathbf{H}
is
S
-
amenable
if
\mathbf{H}(K_{v})
is amenable as locally compact group for every
v\in S
.

Remark that since an infinite direct sum of groups is dense in both the restricted and the unrestricted product,
\mathbf{H}
is
S
-amenable if and only if
\prod_{v\in S}\mathbf{H}(K_{v})
is if and only if
\mathbf{H}_{S}
is.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that
\mathbf{H}
is
S
-
compact
if for every
v\in S
the group
\mathbf{H}(K_{v})
is compact (with respect to the locally compact topology coming from
K_{v}
).

Clearly
S
-compact subgroups are
S
-amenable.

Definition 3.3
.

An
S
-amenable subgroup
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
is called
S
-
maximal amenable
if whenever
\mathbf{H}^{\prime}\geqslant\mathbf{H}
is a
K
-defined
S
-amenable group of
\mathbf{G}
, we have that
\mathbf{H}^{\prime}=\mathbf{H}
.

First, we prove that every
S
-amenable subgroup
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
is contained in an
S
-maximal amenable subgroup.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be an algebraic group. For every
S
-amenable group
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
, there is an
S
-maximal amenable
\mathbf{H}^{\prime}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
containing
\mathbf{H}
.

In order to prove the above proposition, we will need the following consequence of the Jordan-Schur theorem.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be an algebraic group and let
\mathbf{N}\trianglelefteqslant\mathbf{G}
be a normal subgroup. Suppose we have an increasing sequence of subgroups
\{\mathbf{H}_{n}\leqslant\mathbf{G}\}_{n}
satisfying
\mathbf{H}_{n}^{\circ}=\mathbf{N}
. Then the Zariski closure of
\bigcup_{n}\mathbf{H}_{n}
in
\mathbf{G}
is an extension of a virtually abelian group
\mathbf{A}
by
\mathbf{N}
.

Proof.

Let
q\colon\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{N}
be the natural quotient map. By assumption, the image
\Lambda_{n}\coloneqq q(\mathbf{H}_{n})
in
\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{N}
is finite, hence the union
\Lambda\coloneqq\bigcup_{n}\Lambda_{n}
is locally finite. Now, the Jordan–Schur theorem implies
[
Pas77
, Corollary 12.1.12]
that
\Lambda
is virtually abelian, and therefore so is its Zariski closure in
\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{N}
. The claim follows.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.4
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be an
S
-amenable subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
. Clearly there is an
S
-amenable subgroup
\mathbf{H}_{1}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
containing
\mathbf{H}
of maximal dimension. Therefore
\mathbf{H}_{1}
has finite index in any
S
-amenable subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
containing it. Let
\{\mathbf{H}_{n}\}_{n}
be an increasing chain of amenable subgroups containing
\mathbf{H}_{1}
. By assumption, the subgroup
\mathbf{H}_{n}^{\circ}=\mathbf{H}_{1}^{\circ}\eqqcolon\mathbf{N}
is normal in
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{N})
. The group
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
contains
\mathbf{H}_{n}
for every
n
and hence we can apply Lemma
3.5
to obtain that the Zariski closure of
\bigcup_{n}\mathbf{H}_{n}
is
S
-amenable. Since the dimension of
\mathbf{H}_{1}
is already maximal, the sequence
\{\mathbf{H}_{n}\}_{n}
stabilizes.
∎

The aim of this section is to study
S
-maximal amenable subgroups of a given group
\mathbf{G}
. To do so, we perform several reduction steps. First of all, in view of the following lemma we may assume that
\mathbf{G}
is reductive.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a
K
-group. Then every
S
-maximal amenable subgroup
\mathbf{H}
contains the unipotent radical of
\mathbf{G}
, that is
\mathbf{H}\geqslant\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{G})
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
is
S
-amenable. Since
\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{G})
is normal in
\mathbf{G}
, it is normalized by
\mathbf{H}
. Hence
the group generated by
\mathbf{H}_{S}
and
\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{G})_{S}
is amenable.
∎

From now on,
\mathbf{G}
will be reductive and
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
be
S
-maximal amenable. Consider the Levi decomposition
\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{H}^{R}\ltimes\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H})
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume that
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
is
S
-maximal amenable. Let
\mathbf{U}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
be a unipotent subgroup and assume that
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{U})
. Then
\mathbf{U}\leqslant\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H})\leqslant\mathbf{H}
. In particular, given any subgroup
\mathbf{P}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
, if
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathbf{P}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
, then
\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H})\geqslant\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{P})
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathbf{H}
is
S
-MA and denote by
\mathbf{H}^{\prime}
the group generated by
\mathbf{H}
and
\mathbf{U}
. Observe that
\mathbf{U}\leqslant\mathbf{H}^{\prime}
is normal. The group
\mathbf{H}^{\prime}_{S}/\mathbf{U}_{S}=\mathbf{H}_{S}/(\mathbf{U}_{S}\cap\mathbf{H}_{S})
is amenable and hence
\mathbf{H}^{\prime}_{S}
is. Therefore
\mathbf{H}^{\prime}
is
S
-amenable and the maximality implies that
\mathbf{H}^{\prime}=\mathbf{H}
. The subgroup
\mathbf{U}\leqslant\mathbf{H}
is a normal unipotent subgroup which has to be contained in the unipotent radical.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Assume that
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
is
S
-maximal amenable. Then

\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H})))=\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H}),

and moreover
\mathbf{P}\coloneqq\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H}))
is a parabolic subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
containing
\mathbf{H}
.

Proof.

Set
\mathbf{U}\coloneqq\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{P})
. By definition,
\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H})
is a unipotent group normalized by
\mathbf{P}
, so it is contained in its unipotent radical,
\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H})\leqslant\mathbf{U}
.
Since
\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H})
is normal in
\mathbf{H}
, we have that
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H}))=\mathbf{P}
. Lemma
3.7
yields that
\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H})\geqslant\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{P})=\mathbf{U}=\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H})))
.

The moreover part follows from
[
Hum75
, Corollary 30.3.B]
.
∎

Lemma 3.9

(
[
Mar91
, Proposition I.1.3.2, Proposition I.1.6.3, Proposition I.2.3.6]
)
.

Let
k
be a local field. If
\mathbf{G}
is a semisimple
k
-group, then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\rk_{k}\mathbf{G}=0
;

(ii)

\mathbf{G}(k)
is compact;

(iii)

\mathbf{G}(k)
doesn’t contain any unipotent elements;

(iv)

\mathbf{G}(k)
is amenable.

If
\mathbf{G}
is a reductive
k
-group, then the first two conditions are equivalent and the last two conditions are equivalent.

Note that the derived subgroup of a connected reductive group is always semisimple
[
BT65
, Proposition 2.2]
. Therefore Lemma
3.9
implies the following.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be a connected
K
-group with Levi decomposition
\mathbf{H}\cong\mathbf{H}^{R}\ltimes\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H})
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\mathbf{H}
is
S
-amenable;

(ii)

\rk_{K_{v}}\mathbf{H}^{R}=\rk_{K_{v}}\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{H}^{R})
for all
v\in S
;

(iii)

\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{H}^{R})
is
S
-compact.

In particular, if
\mathbf{H}
is
S
-amenable, then all unipotent elements of
\mathbf{H}_{S}
are contained in
\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H})_{S}
.

For infinite
S
, we can say much more.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let us assume that
S
is infinite.
Let
\mathbf{H}
be a connected
K
-group with Levi decomposition
\mathbf{H}\cong\mathbf{H}^{R}\ltimes\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H})
. Then
\mathbf{H}
is
S
-amenable if and only if
\mathbf{H}^{R}
is virtually abelian.

Proof.

The lemma follows from Lemma
3.10
once we observe that the set of places

for which
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{H}^{R})(K_{v})
is compact is finite
[
Mar91
, I.3.2.3]
.
∎

Therefore, we can concentrate on understanding maximal amenable subgroups without unipotent elements, that is, maximal amenable reductive subgroups
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
in a reductive group
\mathbf{G}
.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be a reductive
S
-maximal amenable subgroup of a reductive group
\mathbf{G}
and
\mathbf{Z}
be the maximal
K
-split torus of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{G})
. Then the following holds:

(i)

for every
v\in S
, let
\mathbf{T}_{v}
be a
K_{v}
-defined maximal
K_{v}
-split torus of
\mathbf{H}
. Then
\mathbf{T}_{v}
is central in
\mathbf{H}^{\circ}
.

(ii)

if
\mathbf{H}
is connected, then
\rk_{K}\mathbf{H}=\rk_{K}\mathbf{Z}
. In particular, in this case
\mathbf{Z}
is the maximal
K
-split torus of
\mathbf{H}
.

Proof.

For every
v\in S
, Lemma
3.10
yields that
\mathbf{H}^{\circ}(K_{v})
is the almost direct product of its center with a compact subgroup. Therefore any
K_{v}
-defined
K_{v}
-split subgroup of
\mathbf{H}^{\circ}(K_{v})
is contained in the center, so
(i)
is proven.

Clearly
\mathbf{H}\geqslant\mathbf{Z}
. Assume that we have a
K
-split torus
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}>\mathbf{Z}
properly containing
\mathbf{Z}
. By the first point,
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}
is in the center of
\mathbf{H}
. On the other hand
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}
is not central in
\mathbf{G}
, therefore it has at least one non-trivial root. Then by
[
Mar91
, Proposition I.1.1.3]
,
[
BT65
, Corollaire 3.18]
,
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}
normalises some

\mathbf{U}
in
\mathbf{G}
. Since
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}
is central in
\mathbf{H}
, we have
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{Z}^{\prime})
. This centralizer is contained in a parabolic subgroup
\mathbf{P}
with non-trivial unipotent radical

. But in this case
\mathbf{H}
, being maximal amenable, has to contain
\mathbf{U}

. This contradicts the assumption that

(ii)
.
∎

Note that Item

is not true if

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a reductive group and
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
be a reductive
S
-maximal amenable subgroup. Let
\mathbf{T}
be the central torus of
\mathbf{H}^{\circ}
. Then
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})
. In particular,

•

\mathbf{H}/\mathbf{T}\leqslant\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})/\mathbf{T}
is a semisimple
S
-maximal amenable subgroup of the semisimple group
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})/\mathbf{T}
;

•

for every
v\in S
,
\rk_{K_{v}}(\mathbf{H}/\mathbf{T})=0
;

•

if
S
is finite, the group
(\mathbf{H}/\mathbf{T})_{S}
is compact;

•

if
S
is infinite,
(\mathbf{H}/\mathbf{T})_{S}
is finite.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
be reductive and
S
-maximal amenable and
\mathbf{T}\leqslant\mathbf{H}
be the central torus of
\mathbf{H}^{\circ}
. Then
\mathbf{H}^{\circ}\leqslant\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})
. As
\mathbf{H}^{\circ}
is normal in
\mathbf{H}
and every automorphism of
\mathbf{H}^{\circ}
has to preserve the central torus, we have that
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})
which is a finite index overgroup of the Levi subgroup
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})
. Moreover,
\mathbf{H}
normalizes
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})
and therefore its center
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}\geqslant\mathbf{T}
. Since
\mathbf{H}
is
S
-maximal amenable, it contains
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
which is therefore central in
\mathbf{H}^{\circ}
; hence
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}=\mathbf{T}
which ensures that
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})/\mathbf{T}
is semisimple. The rest follows from Lemmas
3.9
,
3.10
and
3.11
.
∎

Remark 3.14

The above results allow us to deduce maximal amenability in certain important cases.

Definition 3.15
.

Similarly, Item

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a reductive group and let
\mathbf{T}\leqslant\mathbf{G}

-ample torus. Then
\mathbf{T}
is the unique

-ample sub-torus of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{T}_{0}\leqslant\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})
be an
S
-ample torus. Denote by
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})
be an almost complement of
\mathbf{T}
, that is a subgroup such that
\mathbf{H}
and
\mathbf{T}
generate

and have finite intersection. By
(ii’)

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a reductive group and
\mathbf{T}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
be an

-ample torus. Let
\mathbf{H}
be an

-amenable subgroup containing

. Then
\mathbf{H}\leqslant\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{T}
is connected,
\mathbf{T}\leqslant\mathbf{H}^{\circ}
.
Let us first prove that

cannot contain unipotent elements. Indeed, if it does, then the unipotent radical
\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H})
is nontrivial (Lemma

and Lemma

. In particular,
\mathbf{T}
is contained in a proper parabolic subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
and therefore

has to contain a
K
-split subtorus
\mathbf{T}_{0}
which is not central in
\mathbf{G}
.
By condition
(ii)
in Definition
3.16
, this torus

is contained in
\mathbf{T}
; this is, however, in contradiction with the condition
(i)
in Definition

.

Thus,
\mathbf{H}
is reductive and Lemma
3.13

\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
be the central torus of
\mathbf{H}^{\circ}
; by construction,
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}

However, condition

Lemma 4.3
.

Proposition 4.4

(Poor man’s Tits alternative)
.

Let
K
be a global field and
\Lambda\leqslant\mathbf{G}(K)
be an amenable subgroup. Then the Zariski closure of
\Lambda
is virtually solvable.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be the Zariski closure of
\Lambda
. We want to prove that the semisimple group
\mathbf{H}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathbf{H}/\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{H})
is finite. Since
\mathbf{H}^{\prime}
is an almost direct product of almost simple algebraic groups, replacing
\Lambda/(\Lambda\cap\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{H}))
by a finite extension of it, we can assume that
\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{H}_{1}\times\dots\times\mathbf{H}_{r}
. We have to prove the finiteness of every
\mathbf{H}_{i}
.

We let
\Lambda_{i}\leqslant\mathbf{H}_{i}(K)
be the image of
\Lambda
in
\mathbf{H}_{i}
; it is an amenable Zariski dense subgroup. Let
\mathbf{P}_{i}<\mathbf{H}_{i}
be a minimal
K
-parabolic subgroup. Then the quotient
\mathbf{H}_{i}(K_{v})/\mathbf{P}_{i}(K_{v})
is compact for every
v\in S
. The group
\Lambda_{i}\leqslant\mathbf{H}_{i}(K_{v})
is amenable and Zariski dense, so
[
Zim84
, Corollary 3.2.19]
implies that
\Lambda_{i}\leqslant\mathbf{H}_{i}(K_{v})
is precompact. Then we deduce that
\Lambda_{i}
is contained in a restricted product of compact subgroups
\prod_{v\in\mathscr{R}}M_{v}\leqslant\mathbf{H}_{i}(\mathbb{A}_{K})
; this restricted product is a direct limit of an increasing sequence of compact groups. Since
\Lambda_{i}<\mathbf{H}_{i}(K)
and the latter is discrete in
\prod_{v\in\mathscr{R}}\mathbf{H}_{i}(K_{v})
, we deduce that
\Lambda_{i}
is locally finite. Now, the Jordan–Schur theorem implies
[
Pas77
, Corollary 12.1.12]
that
\Lambda_{i}
is virtually abelian. It follows that
\Lambda_{i}
is finite, since its Zariski closure
\mathbf{H}_{i}
is supposed to be semisimple and thus its identity component can’t be abelian unless it is trivial.
∎

We now can establish a good correspondence between

\mathbf{G}
and maximal amenable subgroups of

Every maximal amenable subgroup

\mathbf{H}(K(S))
for some

-maximal amenable subgroup
\mathbf{H}<\mathbf{G}

Proof.

The Zariski closure of
\Lambda
is
K

[

.

The converse of the above corollary is not clear. The sitaution is much easier in the case of
commensurably maximal amenable
subgroups.

Recall that two subgroups of a group are called commensurable if their intersection has finite index in both of them; commensurability is an equivalence relation on the set of subgroups of a group. Clearly all groups in the same commensurability class are either amenable or not. Thus, one can speak of amenable and non-amenable commensurability classes.

Definition 4.6
.

A maximal amenable subgroup
\Lambda\leqslant\Gamma
is called
commensurably maximal amenable
if its commensurability class is maximal.

Since increasing unions of amenable subgroups are amenable, every commensurably maximal amenable subgroup is contained in a maximal amenable subgroup as a subgroup of finite index.

Proposition 4.7
.

A subgroup
\Lambda<\mathbf{G}(K(S))
is commensurably maximal amenable if and only if there is an
S
-cocompactly maximal amenable subgroup
\mathbf{H}<\mathbf{G}
such that
\Lambda=\mathbf{H}(K(S))
.

Proof.

Let us assume that
\Lambda
is commensurably maximal amenable.
By Corollary
4.5
,
\Lambda=\mathbf{H}(K(S))
for some
S
-maximal amenable group
\mathbf{H}
. Set
\mathbf{U}\coloneqq\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H})
. By Theorem
3.22

(i)

\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{H}^{R}\ltimes\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{H})
and
\mathbf{H}^{R}
contains an
S
-cocompact torus
\mathbf{T}
. We can also assume that
\mathbf{T}
has no
S
-cocompact subgroups; then
\mathbf{T}\ltimes\mathbf{U}
has no
S
-cocompact subgroups either. If
\mathbf{H}
is not
S
-cocompactly maximal amenable, by Proposition
3.23
there is an
S
-cocompactly maximal amenable
\mathbf{H}^{\prime}\leqslant\mathbf{G}
such that
\mathbf{H}\cap\mathbf{H}^{\prime}
is
S
-cocompact in
\mathbf{H}
and
\mathbf{H}^{\prime}/(\mathbf{H}\cap\mathbf{H}^{\prime})
is a non-

-compact torus of

. Hence

has finite index in

but infinite index in

which contradicts our hypothesis.

Conversely, if
\mathbf{H}
is
S
-cocompactly maximal amenable, then by Theorem
3.22

(ii)

\Lambda
is commensurable to a group of the form

and the

of

of a finite index subgroup of

∎

4.1.
Singularity

The following definition is borrowed from
[
BC15
]
.

Definition 4.8
.

An amenable subgroup
\Lambda<\Gamma
is

if there is a continuous action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
on a compact space such that for any
\Lambda
-invariant probability measure
\mu
and for all
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\Lambda
, the probability measures
\mu
and
\gamma_{*}\mu
are singular.

Our aim is to prove that all commensurably maximal amenable groups of arithmetic groups are singular, Theorem
4.10
. Before proving it, let us remark that the converse is always true.

Lemma 4.9
.

Assume that the amenable group
\Lambda
is singular in
\Gamma
. Then
\Lambda
is commensurably maximal amenable in
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Consider the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
as in the definition of singularity, let
\Lambda^{\prime}\leqslant\Lambda
be a finite index subgroup and take an amenable group
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\geqslant\Lambda^{\prime}
. Since
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}
is amenable, there is a
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}
-invariant measure
\mu
on
X
. Clearly
\mu
is
\Lambda^{\prime}
-invariant, hence its orbit
\Lambda\mu=\{\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k}\}
is finite, where
\mu_{0}=\mu
. Set
\mu^{\prime}\coloneqq 1/(k+1)\sum_{i}\mu_{i}
. Then
\mu^{\prime}
is
\Lambda
-invariant. Given any
\gamma\in\Lambda^{\prime\prime}
, remark that the measures
\mu^{\prime}
and
\gamma_{*}\mu^{\prime}
are not singular: they are both the convex sum of

with another probability measure. Therefore
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\leqslant\Lambda
and hence
\Lambda
is commensurably maximal amenable.
∎

Our main theorem is the converse of the above lemma for arithmetic lattices.

Theorem 4.10

As described in Theorem

Claim 4.11

, their intersection has strictly lower dimension, hence is of measure zero.
∎

Claim 4.16
.

There are only finitely many
\mathbf{M}_{v}(K_{v})
distinct orbits in
F
.

Proof of Claim.

Fix an arbitrary point
[g]\in F
. Let
\mathbf{S}\leqslant\mathbf{Q}_{v}
be a maximal
K_{v}
-split torus containing
\mathbf{T}_{v}
. As we have already remarked,
g^{-1}\mathbf{T}_{v}(K_{v})g\subseteq\mathbf{Q}_{v}(K_{v})
. Since
\mathbf{T}_{v}
is
K_{v}
-split, there is a unipotent element
u\in\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{Q}_{v})(K_{v})
such that
u^{-1}g^{-1}\mathbf{T}_{v}(K_{v})gu\subseteq\mathbf{S}(K_{v})
. Also note that
[g]=[gu]
. Therefore every point in
F
is represented by an element of the
transporter
of
\mathbf{T}_{v}
into
\mathbf{S}
. By
[
Spr98
, Proof of Proposition 13.3.1]
, this transporter is the union of finitely many
\mathbf{M}_{v}
-cosets.
∎

Since
\mu_{v}
and
\mu^{\prime}_{v}
are not singular, we have that
\mu^{\prime}_{v}
-measure of the support of
\mu_{v}
cannot be zero. By Claim
4.16
, the support of
\mu_{v}
is a finite union of
\mathbf{M}_{v}(K_{v})
-orbits, so there is at least one
\mathbf{M}_{v}(K_{v})
-orbit which has positive measure with respect to both
\mu_{v}
and
\mu^{\prime}_{v}
. Since
\mu_{v}
is supported on
F
, it has to be an
\mathbf{M}_{v}(K_{v})
-orbit of an element
[g]\in F
.
Claim
4.15
implies that there is
y
such that
(\mathbf{T}^{\prime}\ltimes\mathbf{U})(K_{v})[g_{y}]\subseteq\mathbf{M}_{v}(K_{v})[g]\subseteq F
. As
[g_{y}]\in\mathbf{M}_{v}(K_{v})[g]
, we also have that
[g_{y}]\in F
and
(\mathbf{T}^{\prime}\ltimes\mathbf{U})[g_{y}]\subseteq\mathbf{M}_{v}[g_{y}]
.
As in the proof of Claim
4.15
, we consider the parabolic subgroup
\mathbf{R}_{v}
containing
g_{y}\mathbf{Q}_{v}g_{y}^{-1}
whose Levi subgroup is
\mathbf{M}_{v}
; as already observed,
\mathbf{R}_{v}(K_{v})[g_{y}]=\mathbf{M}_{v}(K_{v})[g_{y}]
.

Claim 4.17
.

We have
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{T}\supseteq\mathbf{T}_{v}
; in particular,
\mathbf{T}_{v}=\mathbf{T}^{\prime}_{v}
.

Proof of Claim.

As observed before,
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}[g_{y}]\subseteq\mathbf{M}_{v}[g_{y}]
. Now since
\mathbf{R}_{v}\geqslant g_{y}\mathbf{Q}_{v}g_{y}^{-1}
and
\mathbf{M}_{v}[g_{y}]=\mathbf{R}_{v}[g_{y}]
, we have
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}\leqslant\mathbf{R}_{v}
. Let
\mathbf{N}\leqslant\mathbf{R}_{v}
be the
K
-group generated by
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{T}
. Since
\mathbf{T}
is
S
-ample in
\mathbf{L}
,
\mathbf{T}
cannot normalize any unipotent
K
-subgroup in
\mathbf{L}
. Therefore
\mathbf{N}
is reductive. Then there is an element
u\in\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathbf{R}_{v})(K_{v})
such that
\mathbf{N}\leqslant u\mathbf{M}_{v}u^{-1}
. Since
\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
are
S
-ample, they have to contain the the
K_{v}
-split part of the central torus of
u\mathbf{M}_{v}u^{-1}
. By rank considerations the latter then has to be equal to both
\mathbf{T}_{v}
and
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}_{v}
. ∎

We now deduce that
\gamma_{\ell}
normalizes
\mathbf{T}_{v}
for every
v\in S
. As
\mathbf{T}
is
S
-ample, it coincides with the minimal
K
-defined torus containing all
\mathbf{T}_{v}
, and thus we see that
\gamma_{\ell}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{L}}(\mathbf{T})(K)
. Therefore
\gamma\in(\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{L}}(\mathbf{T})\ltimes\mathbf{U})(K)=\mathbf{H}(K)
. This finishes the proof.
∎

5.
Some examples

Constructing explicit examples of commensurably maximal amenable subgroups of arithmetic groups in classic algebraic groups can be done using number-theoretic input and computer algebra assistance. Implementing algorithms which find
S
-ample tori and giving explicit generators of commensurably maximal amenable subgroups in general arithmetic groups will be subject of forthcoming independent work. In this section we will restrict our attention to giving some easy examples of commensurably maximal amenable subgroups in
\mathbf{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\mathbf{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[1/5])
.

Constructing maximal tori in
\mathbf{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
amount to the study of étale algebras. Recall that an étale algebra
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
is a finite product of algebraic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
:

E=\prod_{k=1}^{\ell}E_{k},

where
E_{k}
is an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
of degree
n_{k}\coloneqq[E_{k}:\mathbb{Q}]
. The degree of
E
is
n\coloneqq[E:\mathbb{Q}]=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}E=\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}n_{k}
, and fixing a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis of
E
defines a matrix representation

\pi\colon E\to\mathbb{M}_{d}(\mathbb{Q})

coming from the regular representation of
E
.

Then there is a unique maximal torus
\mathbf{T}<\mathbf{GL}_{n}
such that

\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Q})=\pi(E^{\times})

and every
\mathbb{Q}
-defined maximal torus in
\mathbf{GL}_{n}
is of this form. Indeed, the algebra generated by
\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Q})
inside
\mathbf{M}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
is easily seen to be étale since
\mathbf{T}
is
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-diagonalizable.

We now want to investigate the
\mathbb{Z}
-points of such a torus. In general, these depend on the choice of the matrix representation
\pi
or, equivalently, on the choice of a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis of
E
.

Given a matrix representation
\pi
as above, we obtain an order of
E

\mathcal{O}\coloneqq\{e\in E\mid\pi(e)\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\}

which clearly satisfies

\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Z})=\pi(\mathcal{O}^{\times}).

Conversely, choosing an order
\mathcal{O}\subset E
and considering the matrix representation
\pi
associated to a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathcal{O}
, we have

\{e\in E\mid\pi(e)\in\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Z})\}=\mathcal{O}^{\times}

Choosing a different
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathcal{O}
yields a torus
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
such that
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Z})
are conjugate inside
\mathbf{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
. Therefore, fixing a maximal
\mathbb{Q}
-torus inside
\mathbf{GL}_{n}
up to conjugacy by
\mathbf{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
amounts to fixing an étale algebra
E
together with an order
\mathcal{O}
. The
\mathbb{Q}
-rank of such a torus
\mathbf{T}
is equal to
\rk_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbf{T}=\ell
.

Every automorphism
\sigma\in\Aut(\mathcal{O})\leqslant\Aut(E)
yields a matrix
\pi(\sigma)\in\mathbf{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
which normalizes
\pi(E)
and therefore lies in
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{G}L_{n}}(\mathbf{T})(\mathbb{Z})
. Conversely, if
g\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{G}L_{n}}(\mathbf{T})(\mathbb{Z})
, then conjugation by
g
defines an automorphism of
\pi(\mathcal{O})\cong\mathcal{O}
, and therefore
g=\pi(\sigma)\cdot\pi(t)
for some
t\in\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Let us begin with the case when
S
consists only of the real valuation. A maximal
\mathbb{Q}
-defined and
\mathbb{Q}
-irreducible torus
\mathbf{T}
of
\mathbf{SL}_{n}
is
S
-ample whenever it is not
\mathbb{R}
-compact. Note that the latter can happen only for
n=2
. Let us give examples of such tori and the corresponding maximal amenable subgroups of
\mathbf{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
. In our examples the order
\mathcal{O}
will always be the maximal order in
E
, so that the generators of
\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Z})
are the generators of the unit group of
E
; they were computed using the
Sage
computer algebra system.

Example 5.1
.

Taking the torus associated to the number field
E=\mathbb{Q}[x]/\langle x^{3}+x-1\rangle
, we obtain a commensurably maximal amenable subgroup of
\mathbf{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
generated by

g=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
1&0&-1\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.

There are no integral elements in the normalizer because the automorphism group of
E
is trivial.

Example 5.2
.

Consider the torus in
\mathbf{SL}_{4}
associated to the number field
E=\mathbb{Q}[x]/\langle x^{4}-8x^{3}+20x^{2}-16x+1\rangle
. Its integer points are generated by the central element
-\mathbf{1}
and

g_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&-2&-4\\
8&16&31&62\\
-6&-12&-24&-49\\
1&2&4&8\\
\end{pmatrix},g_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1&-2&-5\\
9&15&31&78\\
-6&-11&-25&-69\\
1&2&5&15\\
\end{pmatrix},g_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}-4&-1&-1&-1\\
13&12&15&15\\
-7&-7&-8&-5\\
1&1&1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Here the Galois group
\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2
generated by

w=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0\\
-16&-1&-4&-16\\
16&0&1&8\\
-4&0&0&-1\end{pmatrix},w^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0\\
-14&4&10&25\\
12&-4&-9&-20\\
-2&1&2&4\end{pmatrix}.

The group generated by
-\mathbf{1},g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},w
and
w^{\prime}
is commensurably maximal amenable.

By considering the torus of Example
5.1
inside
\mathbf{SL}_{4}
, we obtain an example of a maximal amenable group with unipotent elements.

Example 5.3
.

We let
\tilde{g}\in\mathbf{SL}_{4}(\mathbb{Z})
be the block diagonal matrix

\tilde{g}=\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

where
g
is as in Example
5.1
. If we denote by
E_{i,j}\in\mathbf{SL}_{4}(\mathbb{Z})
the standard elementary matrix, then one has that the group generated by

\tilde{g},-\mathbf{1},E_{1,4},E_{2,4},E_{3,4}

is commensurably maximal amenable.

Let us finally give an example where
S
consists of
2
valuations.

Example 5.4
.

Consider the maximal
\mathbb{Q}
-torus
\mathbf{T}
in
\mathbf{SL}_{2}
associated with the number field
E=\mathbb{Q}[i]
. Then we have
\rk_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{T}=0
(so it is not ample for
S=\{\infty\}
) and
\rk_{\mathbb{Q}_{5}}=1
. In particular,
\mathbf{T}
is
\{\infty,5\}
-ample and
\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Z}[1/5])
is generated by

i=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad g=\begin{pmatrix}4/5&-3/5\\
3/5&4/5\\
\end{pmatrix}

as can be verified by computing
S
-units of norm
1
in
E
. This group is commensurably maximal amenable.

Abstract
. The present note is an essential addition to author’s recent paper [ 14 ] , concerning general multiplicative systems of random variables. Using some lemmas and the methodology of [ 14 ] , we obtain a general extreme inequality, with corollaries involving Rademacher chaos sums and those analogues for multiplicative systems. In particular we prove that a system of functions generated by bounded products of a multiplicative system is a convergence system.

Introduction

A sequence of bounded random variables
\phi_{n}
,
n=1,2,\ldots
(finite or infinite) is said to be multiplicative if the equality

(1.1)

{\textbf{E}}\left[\prod_{j\in A}\phi_{j}\right]=0

holds for all nonempty subsets
A\subset\mathbb{N}
of positive integers. The multiplicative systems were introduced by Alexits (see
[
1
]
, chap 3.2), who also proved in
[
2
]
that the uniformly bounded multiplicative systems are convergence systems, i.e. the condition
\sum_{k}a_{k}^{2}<\infty
implies almost sure convergence of series
\sum_{k}a_{k}\phi_{k}
(see
[
17
,
18
,
9
]
for further extensions of this result). The multiplicative systems were considered also in the context of central limit theorems and law of iterated logarithm (
[
21
,
5
,
8
,
7
]
). Well-known examples of multiplicative sequences are mean zero independent random variables and more general, the martingale-differences. The trigonometric sequences
\{\cos 2n_{k}\pi(x+x_{k})\}
on
(0,1)
with positive integers
n_{k}
, satisfying
n_{k+1}\geq 2n_{k}
, are known to be non-martingale examples of multiplicative systems (see
[
27
]
, chap. 5). A wide class of multiplicative systems was recently introduced in
[
23
]
. Namely, under certain symmetry conditions on
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
, the functions
f(nx)
,
n=1,2,\ldots
form a multiplicative system on
(0,1)
.

Recall the definition of multiplicative error given in
[
14
]
. Denote
\mathbb{Z}_{n}=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and let
\mathbb{Z}_{\infty}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
be the set of all positive integers. We consider systems of random variables
\phi=\{\phi_{k}:\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\}
(here
n
can be either finite or infinite) satisfying

(1.2)

A_{k}\leq\phi_{k}\leq B_{k},\text{ where }A_{k}<0<B_{k}.

Given
d\leq n\leq\infty
the
d
-multiplicative error of the system
\phi
is the quantity

(1.3)

\mu_{d}(\phi)=\sum_{A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{n},\,\#(A)\leq d}\quad\prod_{j\in A}(C_{j})^{-1}\left|{\textbf{E}}\left[\prod_{j\in A}\phi_{j}\right]\right|,

where
C_{k}=\min\{-A_{k},B_{k}\}
. We say
\phi
is
d
-multiplicative if
\mu_{d}(\phi)=0
or equivalently (
1.1
) holds for any finite nonempty
A\subset\mathbb{N}
of cardinality
\#(A)\leq d
. If
d=\infty
this gives the classical definition of multiplicative systems. It was proved in
[
14
]
(see Lemma
2.1
below) that if the system
\phi
is defined on
[0,1]
and
\mu=\mu_{d}(\phi)<\infty
, then the functions of
\phi
can be extended up to
[0,1+\mu]
to a
d
-multiplicative system, obeying same condition (
1.2
). We call a system of random variables
d
-independent if any collection of its
d
elements is independent. It is clear that
d
-independent mean zero random variables are
d
-multiplicative.

The main result of
[
14
]
reads as follows.

Theorem A

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
\Phi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
be a convex function and
\phi=\{\phi_{k}:\,k=1,2,\ldots,n\}
be a system of random variables satisfying (
1.2
). Then for any integer
d\leq n
and a choice of real coefficients
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
it holds the inequality

(1.4)

\textbf{E}\left[\Phi\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\phi_{k}\right)\right]\leq(1+\mu_{d}(\phi))\textbf{E}\left[\Phi\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\xi_{k}\right)\right],

where
\xi_{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,n
, are
\{A_{k},B_{k}\}
- valued
d
-independent mean zero random variables.

Applying this inequality for
d
-multiplicative systems, i.e.
\mu_{d}(\phi)=0
, with the parameters
A_{k}=-1
and
B_{k}=1
, we immediately obtain the following extremal property of Rademacher random variables.

Theorem B

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
\Phi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
be a convex function. If
\phi=\{\phi_{k}:\,k=1,2,\ldots,n\}
, is a multiplicative system of random variables, satisfying
\|\phi_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, then for any choice of coefficients
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
we have

(1.5)

\textbf{E}\left[\Phi\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\phi_{k}\right)\right]\leq{\textbf{E}}\left[\Phi\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}r_{k}\right)\right],

where
r_{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,n
, are Rademacher independent random variables.

Applying inequalities (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), paper
[
14
]
provides extensions of some probability inequalities for general multiplicative systems. In particular it was proved the following generalization of Azuma-Hoeffding’s martingale inequality
[
4
,
11
]

Theorem C

(
[
14
]
)
.

If a system of random variables
\phi=\{\phi_{k}:\,k=1,2,\ldots,n\}
satisfies (
1.2
), then it holds the inequality

(1.6)

\left|\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{k}>\lambda\right\}\right|\leq(1+\mu_{n}(\phi)\exp\left(-\frac{2\lambda^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}(B_{k}-A_{k})^{2}}\right),\quad\lambda>0.

The present note is an addition to paper
[
14
]
. Essentially using the lemmas and the methodology of
[

Theorem 1.1
.

Let a system of random variables
\phi=\{\phi_{k}:\,k=1,2,\ldots,n\}
satisfy (
1.2
),

\textbf{E}\left[G\left(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n}\right)\right]\leq(1+\mu_{d}(\phi))\textbf{E}\left[G\left(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\right)\right],

where
\xi_{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,n
, are
\{A_{k},B_{k}\}
- valued
d
-independent mean zero random variables.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
G:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
be a convex function. If
\phi=\{\phi_{k}:\,k=1,2,\ldots,n\}
, is a system of random variables, satisfying

where

-independent random variables.

Remark 1.1

We remark that if

Remark 1.2

We will mainly be interested in convex functions

3.
Trigonometric subsystems

The Walsh functions have a canonical numeration,
w_{n}
,
n=0,1,2,\ldots
. That is
w_{0}\equiv 1
and for
n\geq 1
with a dyadic decomposition

(3.1)

n=2^{k_{1}}+2^{k_{2}}+\cdots+2^{k_{d}},\quad 0\leq k_{1}<k_{2}<\cdots<k_{d}.

we have

w_{n}(x)=r_{k_{1}+1}(x)r_{k_{2}+1}(x)\ldots r_{k_{d}+1}(x).

We call
d
a power of the number
n
and write
\rho(n)=d
.
Let
L_{\Phi}(0,1)
denote the Orlicz space of functions on
(0,1)
, corresponding to a Young function
\Phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
that is a convex function satisfying

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\Phi(t)}{t}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{t}{\Phi(t)}=0.

The Luxemburg norm of a function
f\in L_{\Phi}(0,1)
is defined by

\|f\|_{\Phi}=\inf\left\{\lambda:\,\lambda>0,\,\int_{0}^{1}\Phi\left(\frac{|f|}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}<\infty.

Corollary 3.1
.

If
\Phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a Young function, then for any finitely supported sequence of coefficients
b_{n}
,
n=1,2,\ldots,
we have the bound

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n:\,\rho(n)\leq d}b_{n}\cos 2\pi n(x+x_{n})\right\|_{\Phi}\leq 2^{d-1}\left\|\sum_{n:\,\rho(n)\leq d}b_{n}w_{n}(x)\right\|_{\Phi},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left\|\max_{m\geq 1}\left|\sum_{n\leq m:\,\rho(n)\leq d}b_{n}\cos 2\pi n(x+x_{n})\right|\right\|_{\Phi}\leq 2^{d-1}\left\|\max_{m\geq 1}\left|\sum_{n\leq m:\,\rho(n)\leq d}b_{n}w_{n}(x)\right|\right\|_{\Phi}

Using the trigonometric sums formulae and decomposition (
3.1

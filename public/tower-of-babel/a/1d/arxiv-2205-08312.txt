Higgsing q ​ q qq -character and irreducibility
Taro Kimura

Abstract
. We show that the q ​ q qq -character of the irreducible highest weight module for finite-type and affine quivers is obtained by Higgsing, specialization of the equivariant parameters of the associated framing space in the quiver variety.

1.2.
Higgs mechanism

Specialization of the weight parameters demonstrated above has a natural interpretation as the Higgs mechanism.
Geometrically realizing the
qq
-character in the eight dimensional setup, called the gauge origami, the weight parameters are interpreted as the Coulomb moduli of the dual gauge theory defined in the transverse surface
[
Nek17
]
.
From this point of view, the gauge symmetry is originally described by
\mathrm{U}(\mathbf{W})=\prod_{i\in\Gamma_{0}}\mathrm{U}(w_{i})
, which is a compact version of
G_{w}=\mathrm{GL}(\mathbf{W})
.
Specialization of the weight parameters implies freezing all the Coulomb moduli except for the center of mass factor.
Therefore, we have the symmetry breaking,
\mathrm{U}(\mathbf{W})\to\mathrm{U}(1)
.
We remark that the Higgs mechanism freezes the non-Abelian part of the gauge group.
The
\mathrm{U}(1)
factors of each
\mathrm{U}(w_{i})
are fixed by the bifundamental mass parameters except for the overall center of mass factor in this context.
It has been established that the
qq
-character has no singularity with respect to this center of mass factor, a.k.a., the compactness theorem
[
Nek16
]
.

Such an interpretation of the parameter specialization as Higgsing has been discussed in the various contexts.
It has been known that one can move on to the root of Higgs branch of the moduli space of supersymmetric vacua by tuning the Coulomb moduli
[
Dor98
,
DHT99
]
.
In the context of topological string, the idea of Higgsing is used to describe the geometric transition
[
GV98
]
.
For the primary example of the transition between the resolved and deformed conifolds, one may discuss the open string degrees of freedom on the Lagrangian submanifold on the deformed conifold side, which is given as the cotangent bundle
T^{*}\!S^{3}
and its Lagrangian
S^{3}
.
From this point of view, the Higgsing condition on the Kähler parameter is interpreted as the flux quantization condition.
Along this direction, we may apply a similar interpretation to the current situation.
Geometrically, the irreducible part is interpreted as a subvariety of the whole quiver variety:
The graded quiver variety is decomposed into direct product under the
q
-segment condition
[
Nak99
, §4]
.
Hence, the Higgsed
qq
-character, which describes the irreducible module, is expected to correspond to this irreducible subvariety of the quiver variety.
This is a geometric representation theoretical interpretation of the Higgsing process.

Then, there exists a canonical specialization of the weight parameters

This Conjecture is for generic modules and for generic quivers.

and

for the tensor product modules of the Fock modules of the quantum toroidal algebras associated with

and
\widehat{A}_{r-1}

We have a more specific Conjecture regarding the KR module of a finite-type quiver.

Conjecture 1.2

(Theorems
3.2
,
3.3

.

Let
\Gamma

Considering the weight dimension vector having a single non-zero entry,

The

(3)

The irreducible

Hence it is not irreducible in general.

2.
Construction of

-character

In this Section, we provide an algorithm to calculate the

We apply the algebraic construction based on the iWeyl reflection.

2.1.
Quiver

Let
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})
be a quiver with the set of nodes
\Gamma_{0}
and the set of edges
\Gamma_{1}
.
We call
\operatorname{rk}\Gamma=|\Gamma_{0}|
the rank of quiver.
We define a decorated quiver
\Gamma_{d}=(\Gamma,d)
with the set of positive integers
d=(d_{i})_{i\in\Gamma_{0}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{\operatorname{rk}\Gamma}
assigned to each node, which would be identified with the relative root length of the corresponding root system.
We denote
d_{ij}=\operatorname{gcd}(d_{i},d_{j})
.

2.2.
Index functor

Let
\mathbf{X}
be the bundle for which we denote the set of Grothendieck roots (exponentials of the Chern roots) by
X=\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1,\ldots\operatorname{rk}\mathbf{X}}

Then, the corresponding character is given by
\operatorname{ch}\mathbf{X}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\operatorname{rk}\mathbf{X}}x_{\alpha}=\sum_{x\in X}x
.
We denote the wedge products of the bundle
\mathbf{X}
by

\displaystyle\wedge\mathbf{X}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\operatorname{rk}\mathbf{X}}(-1)^{k}\wedge^{k}\mathbf{X}\,.

(2.1)

Then, we denote the index functor, which converts the additive class to the multiplicative class, by

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}[\mathbf{X}]=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{\operatorname{rk}\mathbf{X}}(1-x_{\alpha})=\operatorname{ch}\wedge\mathbf{X}\,.

(2.2)

We also use the twisted version.
We denote the twisted sum of the wedge products by

\displaystyle\wedge_{y}\mathbf{X}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\operatorname{rk}\mathbf{X}}(-y)^{k}\wedge^{k}\mathbf{X}\,.

(2.3)

For
\mathbf{X}^{\prime}
a one-dimensional bundle with
\operatorname{ch}\mathbf{X}^{\prime}=x^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}[\mathbf{X}\otimes\mathbf{X}^{\prime}]=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{\operatorname{rk}\mathbf{X}}(1-x_{\alpha}x^{\prime})=\operatorname{ch}\wedge_{x^{\prime}}\mathbf{X}\,,

(2.4)

which is the characteristic polynomial with respect to
X
.

2.3.
\mathsf{Y}
-function

For a quiver

\displaystyle\mathsf{Y}_{i,X}=\mathbb{I}\quantity[ \mathbf{X} \otimes\mathbf{Y}_i ]=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{\operatorname{rk}\mathbf{X}}\mathsf{Y}_{i,x_{\alpha}}\,,\qquad i\in\Gamma_{0}\,,\ x_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\,.

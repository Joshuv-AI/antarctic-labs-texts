The inverse Black-Scholes problem in Radon measures space revisited: towards a new measure of market uncertainty
Nizar Riane

Abstract
In this paper, we revisit the inverse Black-Scholes model, the existence of the solution is proved in more rigorous way, and the empirical study is done using different approach based on finite element method. The article leads to a measure of incertitude in the option market.

Introduction

In 1997, R. Merton and M. Scholes for their works, in particular, the Black-Scholes model
[
BS73
]
, which continue to be a reference for the option pricing.

Despite the genius behind this construction, the model present many weaknesses when he confront the data, in particular, when the model underprices the option out of the money, and overprices in the money
[
GFS99
]
.

Another weakness of the model is that consider that the market to be isotropic in physicians terminology, while the option price can be affected by incertitude about the future, leading to anisotropic reactions.

In
[
RD20b
]
,
[
RD21b
]
, we introduced an upgraded version of this model, satisfying the economic foundations of finance and taking into account an important market factor : incertitude.

In this paper, we revisit the inverse Black-Scholes model, as presented in
[
RD20b
]
, we give a more rigorous justification of the existence of the solution, and we fit the data using different approach based on finite element method.

At the end of this work, we will prove that the measure based Black-Scholes model is a better candidate to fit financial data, in addition, we will get an interpretation in terms of market incertitude.

)

H^{k}\left(E\right)=W^{k,2}\left(E\right)=\left\{f\,\in\,L^{2}\left(E\right)\,,\,\forall\,j\leqslant k\,:\,D^{j}f\,\in\,L^{2}\left(E\right)\right\}\,\cdot

H_{0}^{k}\left(E\right)=W^{k,2}\left(E\right)=\left\{f\,\in\,L^{2}\left(E\right)\,,\,f_{|\partial E}=0\,\text{and: }\forall\,j\leqslant k\,:\,D^{j}f\,\in\,L^{2}\left(E\right)\right\}\,\cdot

The measure based Black-Scholes model was introduced in
[
RD21b
]
in the following variational form:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(t,x)\,d\mu&=&\left(-r(t)\,x\,\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}(t,x)-\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{2}(t,x)}{2}\,x^{2}\,\Delta u(t,x)+r(t)\,u(t,x)\right)\,dx&\quad\forall\,t\,\in\,\left[0,T\right],\,\forall\,x\,\in\,\left[0,M\right[\\
u(T,x)&=&h(x),&\quad\forall\,x\,\in\,\left[0,M\right[\end{array}\right.

where
\sigma>0
represents the volatility,
r>0
the risk-free interest rate,
T>0
the maturity of the option and
u
the option price. The underlying asset price is supposed to be bounded by some strictly positive number
M\gg 1
.

We suppose the measure
\mu
to be any finite Radon measure supported in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}=\left[0,M\right]\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
and satisfies the following assumptions

Assumption 2.1
.

There exists two strictly positive constant
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
such that:

\forall\,u\,\in\,\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{M}):\quad\parallel u\parallel_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{M})}\leqslant C_{0}\,\left\|x\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}\quad\text{and}\quad\parallel u\parallel_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}\leqslant C_{1}\,\parallel u\parallel_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{M})}\,\cdot

where
{\cal D}(\mathcal{M})
denote the space of test functions on
\mathcal{M}=\left[0,M\right[
, i.e. the space of smooth functions with compact support in
\mathcal{M}
.

Remark 2.1
.

We proved In
[
RD21b
]
the first assumption for all finite Radon measures, and we proved the second assumption for all absolute continuous measures with respect to the Lebesgue one.

We recall the following restrictions on the parameters
r
and
\sigma
to ensure existence and uniqueness results:

Assumption 2.2
.

1.

The risk-free interest rate is bounded on
\left[0,T\right]

\displaystyle\forall\,t\,\in\,[0,T]:\quad 0\leqslant r\leqslant r(t)\leqslant R

2.

There exist two positive constants,
\underline{\sigma}
and
\overline{\sigma}
, such that:

\displaystyle\forall\,t\,\in\,[0,T],\,\forall\,x\,\in\,\left[0,M\right]:\quad 0<\underline{\sigma}\leqslant{\sigma}\leqslant\overline{\sigma}\,\cdot

3.

There exists a positive constant
C_{\sigma}
such that:

\displaystyle\forall\,t\,\in\,[0,T],\,\forall\,x\,\in\,\left[0,M\right]\,:\quad\left|x\,\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial x}(t,x)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\sigma}\,\cdot

The existence and uniqueness of the solution proof can be found in
[
RD20b
]

Notations
.

Set:

V_{\mathcal{M}}=\left\{v\,\in\,L^{2}(\mathcal{M})\quad,\quad x\,\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}\,\in\,L^{2}(\mathcal{M})\right\}\quad,\quad W_{\mathcal{M}}=\left\{v\,\in\,L^{2}(\mathcal{M})\quad,\quad x^{2}\,\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial x^{2}}\,\in\,L^{2}(\mathcal{M})\right\}

Proposition 2.3

(
Poincaré’s inequality
)

The space
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{M})
is dense in

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}\leqslant 2\,\left\|x\,\displaystyle\frac{dv}{dx}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}

Proposition 2.4

(Continuity and Gårding inequality)

The bilinear form

is continuous on

, and satisfies the Gårding inequality:

\forall\,u\,\in\,V_{\mathcal{M}}:\quad B(u)\geqslant\displaystyle\frac{\underline{\sigma}^{2}}{4}|u|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}^{2}-\lambda\,\parallel u\parallel_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}

Remark 2.2

Given

\forall\,v\,\in\,\text{dom}\,(L):\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{d}{dt}w(t,x)\,v(x)\,d\mu=B(w,v)+\tilde{\lambda}\,\int_{\mathcal{M}}w(t,x)\,v(x)\,d\mu=\widehat{B}(w,v)\quad\text{and}\quad w(T,x)=h(x)\,\cdot

Without affecting the solution spaces, one obtains the continuity and the coercivity of the form

\displaystyle|\widehat{B}(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,|u|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}\,|v|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}+\tilde{\lambda}\,\parallel u\parallel_{L^{2}_{\mu}}\,\parallel v\parallel_{L^{2}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leqslant(C+\tilde{\lambda}\,C_{0})\,|u|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}\,|v|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle\widehat{B}(u,u)

\displaystyle\geqslant\displaystyle\frac{\underline{\sigma}^{2}}{4}\,|u|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}^{2}-\lambda\parallel u\parallel_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}+\tilde{\lambda}\,\parallel u\parallel_{L^{2}_{\mu}}^{2}

The following result follows directly from

Theorem 2.6
.

L^{2}_{\mu}
Regularity

We have the estimate, for

\displaystyle\left\|h(x)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant e^{-2\tilde{\lambda}\,(T-t)}\left\|u(t,x)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}+\frac{\underline{\sigma}^{2}}{2}\int_{t}^{T}e^{-2\tilde{\lambda}\,(T-s)}|u(s,x)|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}^{2}\,ds

Using the remark before by setting

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{d}{ds}u(s,x)\,e^{-2\tilde{\lambda}\,(T-s)}\,u(s,x)\,d\mu\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{T}\frac{d}{ds}\int_{\mathcal{M}}e^{-2\tilde{\lambda}\,(T-s)}\frac{|u(s,x)|^{2}}{2}\,d\mu\,ds

\displaystyle-\tilde{\lambda}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}e^{-2\tilde{\lambda}\,(T-s)}\,|u(s,x)|^{2}\,d\mu\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\parallel u(T,x)\parallel_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}-\frac{e^{-2\tilde{\lambda}\,(T-t)}}{2}\parallel u(t,x)\parallel_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}

Theorem 2.7
.

V_{\mathcal{M}}
Regularity

For
h\in V_{\mathcal{M}}
, we have the estimate:

\displaystyle|u|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left(\left(\tilde{\lambda}-\frac{\underline{\sigma}^{2}}{2C_{0}}\right)(T-t)\right)|h|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}^{2}

Using the transformation

\displaystyle\frac{\underline{\sigma}^{2}}{4}|w|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}^{2}\leqslant\widehat{B}(w)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\parallel w\parallel_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}\leqslant\frac{C_{0}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|w|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}^{2}

\displaystyle|w|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}^{2}

\displaystyle|u|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}^{2}

If

\mu

Proof.

Set

.

3
The inverse problem

For normalization purpose, we restrict ourselves to finite Radon measure with total mass equals to
M
, this enable ones to take into account the classical Black-Scholes model. For simplicity, we consider probability measures
\mu
on
\mathcal{M}
, then we multiply
\mu
by the constant
M
to get
\mu(\mathcal{M})=M
.

Definition 3.1

(
Inverse problem
)
.

We recall the inverse problem as defined in
[
RD21b
]
, which consists in finding the measure
\mu
associated with the Black-Scholes equation, given a very small parameter
\varepsilon>0
, and a noisy measure of the solution
u_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of the solution
u_{\mu}
such that:

\displaystyle\parallel u_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}-u_{\mu}\parallel

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon\,\cdot

Notation
.

We denote by
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M})
(respectively
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
) the space of finite Radon (resp. probability) measures on
\left[0,M\right]
, and by
C(\mathcal{M})
the space of (uniformly) continuous functions on
\left[0,M\right]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

[
BP13
]

The space of finite Radon measures
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M})
equipped with the
total variation norm

\parallel\cdot\parallel_{\mathcal{TV}}
:

\displaystyle\parallel\mu\parallel_{\mathcal{TV}}=\sup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\mu(E_{i})|\,,\quad\underset{i=1}{\overset{+\infty}{\bigcup}}\,E_{i}=\left[a,b\right],\quad\{E_{i}\}\text{ are disjoint and measurable}\right\}

is complete and separable, and is also the dual space of
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{M})
:

\displaystyle C(\mathcal{M})^{\star}=\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M})\,\cdot

Definition 3.2
.

A sequence of measures
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\,\in\,\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M})^{\mathbb{N}}
converges in the weak sense
⋆
towards a measure
\mu
if and only if

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\phi\,d\mu_{n}\rightarrow\int_{a}^{b}\phi\,d\mu\qquad\forall\phi\in C(\mathcal{M})\,\cdot

Moreover, every bounded sequence in
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M})
has a weak
⋆
convergent subsequence.

Remark 3.1

(
[
BP13
]
)
.

For
d\geqslant 1
, let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be an open non-empty subset. Then:

i
.

the space of all finite linear combinations of
\delta
-peaks

\displaystyle\left\{\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}\,,\quad n\,\in\,{\mathbb{N}},\,v_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\,x_{i}\in\Omega,\,i=1,\ldots,n\right\}

ii
.

the space
L^{2}(\Omega)
, equipped with the injection
w\rightarrow w\,\mu_{\cal L}
, for the Lebesgue measure
\mu_{\cal L}
,

are weakly
⋆
dense subsets of
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The space of probability measures
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
equipped with the
Prokhorov metric

:

\displaystyle d_{P}(\mu,\nu)=\inf\left\{\alpha>0\,,\,\mu(A)\leqslant\nu(A_{\alpha})+\alpha\quad\text{and}\quad\nu(A)\leqslant\mu(A_{\alpha})+\alpha\,,\,\forall A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M})\right\}

where

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}:=\{x\,:\,d(x;A)<\alpha\text{ if }A\neq\emptyset\,;\,\emptyset_{\alpha}:=\emptyset\,;\,\forall\alpha>0\}

and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M})
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\left[0,M\right]
, is a compact metric space for the weak
⋆
topology.

Remark 3.2
.

For normalization purpose, we restrict ourselves to the space
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{M})=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M})\,:\,\mu(\mathcal{M})=M\right\}
, since the total measure of
\mathcal{M}
with respect to Lebesgue measure is

Notation

In the spirit of

]
, we introduce the solution operator of the measure based Black-Scholes problem

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\Psi&:&\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{M})&\rightarrow&V_{\mathcal{M}}\subset L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{M})\\
&&\mu&\mapsto&u_{\mu}(0,\cdot)\\
\end{array}

Notations
.

Given two probability measures
\mu
and
\nu
in
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{M})
, we introduce the respective solutions

,
u_{\nu}
,

of:

\forall\,v\,\in\,{\cal D}({\cal M})\,:\quad\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{M}u_{\mu}\,v\,d\mu=B(u_{\mu},v)\quad,\quad\forall\,v\,\in\,{\cal D}({\cal M})\,:\quad\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{M}u_{\nu}\,v\,d\nu=B(u_{\nu},v)

and:

\forall\,v\,\in\,{\cal D}({\cal M})\,:\quad\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{M}u_{\mu+\nu}\,v\,d(\mu+\nu)=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{M}u_{\mu+\nu}\,v\,d\mu+\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{M}u_{\mu+\nu}\,v\,d\nu\\
=B(u_{\mu+\nu},v)\,\cdot

Remark 3.3
.

1.

Non linearity
: for any
v
in
{\cal D}\left({\cal M}\right)
:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{0}^{M}u_{\mu+\nu}\,v\,d\mu+\int_{0}^{M}u_{\mu+\nu}\,v\,d\nu\right)&=&B(u_{\mu+\nu},v)\\
\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{0}^{M}u_{\mu}\,v\,d\mu+\int_{0}^{M}u_{\nu}\,v\,d\nu\right)&=&B(u_{\mu},v)+B(u_{\nu},v)\\
\end{array}

Then

implies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{0}^{M}u_{\nu}\,v\,d\mu+\int_{0}^{M}u_{\mu}\,v\,d\nu\right)

\displaystyle=0

Choose

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{M})}=-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\left\|u_{\mu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}(\mathcal{M})}

Integrate between

\displaystyle\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{M})}-\left\|u_{\nu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{M})}+\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}(\mathcal{M})}-\left\|u_{\mu}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\nu}(\mathcal{M})}

Moreover, for

\displaystyle\alpha\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{M}u_{\alpha\nu}\,v\,d\mu

\displaystyle=B(u_{\alpha\nu},v)

Multiply the identity for

\displaystyle\alpha\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{M}u_{\mu}\,v\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\alpha B(u_{\mu},v)

Clearly

and the related operator

2.

Weak
⋆
-strong Continuity

\left(\mu_{n}\right)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\,\in\,{\tilde{\mathcal{P}}}({\cal M})^{\mathbb{N}}

(\left\|u_{\mu_{n}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mu_{n}}(\mathcal{M})})

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{0}^{M}w_{\mu_{n}}\,v\,d\mu_{n}\right)=\widehat{B}(w_{\mu_{n}},v)

Subtracting the inequalities defining
w_{\mu_{n}}
and
w_{\mu_{m}}
for
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\widehat{B}(w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{M}\frac{d}{dt}w_{\mu_{n}}(w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}})\,d\mu_{n}-\int_{0}^{M}\frac{d}{dt}w_{\mu_{m}}(w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}})\,d\mu_{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{M}\frac{d}{dt}w_{\mu_{n}}(w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}})\,d(\mu_{n}-\mu_{m})+\int_{0}^{M}\frac{d}{dt}(w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}})(w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}})\,d\mu_{m}

Then

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}\widehat{B}(w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}})\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{T}\int_{0}^{M}\frac{d}{dt}w_{\mu_{n}}(w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}})\,d(\mu_{n}-\mu_{m})\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{T}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu_{n}}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{T}\int_{0}^{M}\frac{d}{dt}w_{\mu_{n}}(w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}})\,d(\mu_{n}-\mu_{m})\,ds-\frac{1}{2}\left\|w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu_{n}}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}

Equivalently

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu_{n}}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\underline{\sigma}^{2}}{4}|w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}}|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\left\|w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu_{n}}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}\widehat{B}(w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}})\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{T}\int_{0}^{M}\frac{d}{dt}w_{\mu_{n}}(w_{\mu_{n}}-w_{\mu_{m}})\,d(\mu_{n}-\mu_{m})\,ds

Since

is bounded in

and

, then it is bounded in

, for all

is a Cauchy sequence in

and then converges to a limit

Definition 3.3

(

)
.

The Tikhonov minimization problem is the solution of

\displaystyle\min_{\mu\in\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{M})}\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}(\mu)=\displaystyle\frac{\parallel\Psi(\mu)-u_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\parallel^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}}{2}+\alpha\parallel\mu\parallel_{\mathcal{TV}}

where

denotes the regularization parameter.

Theorem 3.3
.

Thikhonov regularized problem admit a solution.

Proof.

The operator
\Psi
is weak
⋆
-strong continuous, so is Tikhonov functional from composition properties. Since the space
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{M})
is compact for the weak
⋆
topology, the result follows.

∎

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}^{m}_{j,j+1}

4.2
Convergence and error estimate

Let proceed without affecting the solution spaces, as in the remark of the first section by setting
\tilde{\lambda}=\lambda\,C_{1}
and
u=e^{\tilde{\lambda}\,(T-t)}w
, where
w
is the solution of the transformed problem

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\,\int_{0}^{M}w\,v\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\widehat{B}(w,v)

The bilinear form
\widehat{B}(w,v)=B(w,v)+\tilde{\lambda}\,\int_{\mathcal{M}}w(t,x)\,v(x)\,d\mu
, satisfies continuity and coercivity:

\displaystyle|\widehat{B}(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leqslant(C+\tilde{\lambda}\,C_{0})\,|u|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}\,|v|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle\widehat{B}(u,u)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\underline{\sigma}^{2}}{4}|u|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}^{2}

J. L. Lions and E. Magenes theory
[
LM68
]
implies the existence and uniqueness of the solution for the approximated problem.

We use the decomposition
u^{m}(t,x)=\overline{u^{m}}(t,x)+\beta_{0}(t)\,\phi_{0}(x)+\beta_{m}(t)\,\phi_{m}(x)
to consider the homogeneous part
\overline{u^{m}}(t,x)
.

Define the projector
\pi\,:\,V_{\mathcal{M}}\rightarrow V_{\mathcal{M}}^{h}
by

\displaystyle\pi(v)(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}v(x_{j})\,\phi_{j}(x)

Lemma 4.2
.

There is a constant
C_{2}
independent of
\Delta x
such that, for all
v\in V_{\mathcal{M}}^{h}

\displaystyle|v-\pi(v)|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{2}\,(\Delta x)^{2}\left\|(xv(x))^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}

Proof.

Let
v\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{M})
. For
x\in\left]x_{j},x_{j+1}\right[
:

\displaystyle xv(x)-\pi(xv(x))

\displaystyle=xv(x)-\left(x_{j}v(x_{j})+\frac{x_{j+1}v(x_{j+1})-x_{j}v(x_{j})}{x_{j+1}-x_{j}}\right)(x-x_{j})

\displaystyle=\int_{x_{j}}^{x}\frac{d}{ds}(sv(s))\,ds-\frac{x-x_{j}}{x_{j+1}-x_{j}}\left(\int_{x_{j}}^{x_{j+1}}\frac{d}{ds}(sv(s))\,ds\right)

\displaystyle=(x-{x_{j}})\frac{d}{ds}(sv(s))(x_{j}+\alpha)-(x-x_{j})\frac{d}{ds}(sv(s))(x_{j}+\beta)

\displaystyle=(x-{x_{j}})\int_{x_{j}+\alpha}^{x_{j}+\beta}\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}(sv(s))\,ds

for
0\leqslant\alpha\leqslant x-x_{j}
and
0\leqslant\beta\leqslant\Delta x
. We get from Cauchy-Schwartz inequality

\displaystyle|xv(x)-\pi(xv(x))|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant(\Delta x)^{2}\left(\int_{x_{j}}^{x_{j+1}}|\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}(sv(s))|\,ds\right)^{2}\leqslant(\Delta x)^{3}\left(\int_{x_{j}}^{x_{j+1}}|\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}(sv(s))|^{2}\,ds\right)

integrating over
\left[x_{j},x_{j+1}\right]
and summing

\displaystyle|v-\pi(v)|^{2}_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\int_{x_{j}}^{x_{j+1}}|xv(x)-\pi(xv(x))|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant(\Delta x)^{4}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\left(\int_{x_{j}}^{x_{j+1}}|\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}(sv(s))|^{2}\,ds\right)

\displaystyle=(\Delta x)^{4}\left\|(sv(s))^{\prime\prime}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}

The result follows then by density.

∎

It follows the following theorem

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
u\in V_{\mathcal{M}}
and
u^{m}\in V_{\mathcal{M}}^{h}
be respectively, the solution and the finite element approximation of the measure based Black-Scholes equation. Then the finite element method converges.

In addition, if
h\in W_{\mathcal{M}}
, then there exist a constant
C(T)
, independent of
\Delta x
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|u-u^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C(T)\,(\Delta x)^{2}\left\|(xh(x))^{\prime\prime}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}

Proof.

It suffice to resonate in terms of the transformed problem.

Consider the variational inequalities involving
u\in W_{\mathcal{M}}
and
u^{m}\in V_{\mathcal{M}}^{h}
, by taking
v\in V_{\mathcal{M}}^{h}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\,\int_{0}^{M}u\,v\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\widehat{B}(u,v)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\,\int_{0}^{M}u^{m}\,v\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\widehat{B}(u^{m},v)

subtract the two inequalities and define the error
\mathfrak{e}=u-u^{m}
, this yield to the the formula

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\,\int_{0}^{M}\mathfrak{e}\,v\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\widehat{B}(\mathfrak{e},v)

It follows from coercivity and continuity of
\widehat{B}
that

\displaystyle\left\|u-u^{m}\right\|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{3}\,\inf_{v^{m}\in V_{\mathcal{M}}^{h}}\left\|u-v^{m}\right\|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}

for some constant
C_{3}
. Using the regularity solution estimate, the first assumptions and the lemma before, we get for
0\leqslant t\leqslant T

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{e}(t,x)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{\tilde{\lambda}\,(T-t)}\,\left\|\mathfrak{e}(T,x)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{0}\,e^{\tilde{\lambda}\,T}\,|\mathfrak{e}(T,x)|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{0}\,C_{3}\,e^{\tilde{\lambda}\,T}\,|u(T,x)-\pi(u)(T,x)|_{V_{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{0}\,C_{2}\,C_{3}\,e^{\tilde{\lambda}\,T}\,(\Delta x)^{2}\left\|(xu(T,x))^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C(T)\,(\Delta x)^{2}\left\|(xh(x))^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}

∎

We deduce from the existence and uniqueness of the transformed solution, the existence and uniqueness of the original one, and then it’s finite element approximation.

4.3
Discretization of the Tikhonov problem

Given
N\geqslant 1
,
m\geqslant 1
,
0\leqslant n\leqslant N
and
j\,\in\,\{0,\ldots,m\}
, we denote by
u^{m}_{\mu}(t,j)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\beta_{i}(t)\,\phi_{i}(x_{j})
the finite element approximation of the solution
u_{\mu}(t,x_{j})
. The coefficient vector
\mathbf{B}^{m}(t)=(\beta_{i}(t))_{0\leqslant i\leqslant m}
follows the differential system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{B}^{m}\mathbf{M}^{m}&=\mathbf{B}^{m}\mathbf{K}^{m}+\beta_{m}(t)\mathbf{C}^{m}\\
\mathbf{B}^{m}(T)&=\mathbf{H}\end{cases}

as stated before. Define the approximation measure as a Dirac linear combination

\nu=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}\gamma_{i}\,\delta_{i}

for
\sum_{i=0}^{m}\gamma_{i}=M
, which is dense in
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{M})
. The matrix
\mathbf{M}^{m}
simplifies to a diagonal matrix

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}^{m}_{j,j}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{j}

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}^{m}_{\underset{i\neq j}{j,i}}

\displaystyle=0

Now consider an empirical measure
u_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of the solution, we introduce the finite element approximation operator
\tilde{\Psi}\,:\,\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, such that:

\tilde{\Psi}(\mu)_{i}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\delta_{x_{i}}u^{m}_{\mu}\,dx

This construction justify the discretized Tikhonov problem:

Definition 4.2

(
Discretized Tikhonov problem
)
.

We define the
discretized Tykhonov problem
as

\displaystyle\min_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m},\,\sum\gamma_{i}=M}\mathcal{J}(\mu)=\frac{\parallel\tilde{\Psi}(\mu)-u_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\parallel^{2}}{2}+\alpha\sum_{i=0}^{m}|\gamma_{i}|

where
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}\,\delta_{i}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

The discretized Tikhonov minimization problem admits a unique solution
\mu\in\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

The existence follows from the continuity of
\tilde{\Psi}
, which follows from the continuous dependence of the solution of the linear differential system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{B}^{m}\mathbf{M}^{m}&=\left(\mathbf{K}^{m}\mathbf{B}^{m}+\beta_{m}\mathbf{C}^{m}\right)\\
\mathbf{B}^{m}(T)&=\mathbf{H}\end{cases}

to the matrix
\mathbf{M}^{m}
(continuity of the exponential), combined with the form of
u^{m}

\displaystyle u^{m}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{B}^{m}(t)\cdot\mathbf{\Phi}^{m}(x)+\beta_{m}(t)\phi_{m}(x)

where
\mathbf{\Phi}^{m}=(\phi_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m-1}
, is the finite element basis. The uniqueness follows by strict convexity of Tikhonov functional and the exponential.

5
Discussion

The results established in this work demonstrate the superiority of the measure based Black-Scholes model in term of market data fit, while the classical model underestimate the option price.

The measure based Black-scholes offer a new information on the market consisting in the measure
\mu
which can be interpreted as a market confidence measure : by considering the probability version
\nu=\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{M}=\sum\frac{\gamma_{i}}{M}\,\delta_{i}
, the parameter
\gamma_{i}
represent the option price rigidity at the stock price
x_{i}
, higher is
\gamma_{i}
more stable is the option price.

One remarkable fact is the parameters for the estimated model are lower than the uniform case of the Black-Scholes model, reflecting more instability in the market than the classical theory predict.

The model based on the measure
\mu
open a new field of research by quantifying the market confidence (in opposition with market incertitude) :
\gamma(x)
reflect the market confidence at the stock price
x
, inducing more rigidity and a slow change in the option price. This is just a measure of incertitude in the market, which was considered to be unmeasurable.

1.
Introduction

The resonance method developed by Soundararajan
[
11
]
is a flexible tool for detecting both unusually large and unusually small values in families of
L
-functions. In its original application to the zeta function, this method exploits the oscillatory behavior of
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)
by generating a Dirichlet polynomial

R(t)=\sum_{n\leqslant N}r(n)n^{-it}

that “resonates” with the “louder frequencies” of
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)
on
T\leqslant t\leqslant 2T
for some later optimized choice of real-valued resonator coefficients
r(n)
. The argument proceeds by unpacking, reassembling, and analyzing what is essentially the first moment of

(1)

\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|R(t)|^{2}\approx\sum_{k\leqslant T}k^{-1/2}\sum_{n,m\leqslant N}r(n)r(m)\left(\frac{n}{mk}\right)^{it}

on this interval, where one must balance the length
N
of the resonator — a parameter commensurate with the strength of the resulting bound (
2
) — against the contribution of off-diagonal terms (
n\neq mk
) which are difficult to manage. Comparing the mean value of (
1
) to the mean of
|R(t)|^{2}
and optimizing the resonator coefficients yields

(2)

\max_{T\leqslant t\leqslant 2T}\log\left|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\right|\geqslant(1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{\log T}{\log\log T}}.

The bound (
2
) substantially improved upon the previously best known result
[
1
,
2
]
and is much larger than the bulk of the normal distribution with mean value
0
and variance
(\log\log T)/2
; see
[
13
, §11.13]
. This result is fairly close to predictions from random matrix theory
[
7
]
that suggest the right hand side of (
2
) may be within a factor of
(\log\log T)/\sqrt{2}
to the true maximum.

Bondarenko and Seip
[
4
,
5
]
have since managed to substantially extend the length of the resonator for the zeta function, thus improving (
2
), but at the cost of localization; the full strength of their result requires the entire segment
0\leqslant t\leqslant T
and can not recover information along dyadic ranges.

For a number field
K
, the pure adelic analogue for the set of values of
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)
is the set of central values of Hecke
L
-functions to Hecke characters that ramify only at a single place; see the upcoming Section
2.1
for a brief recollection of Hecke characters. Over
\mathbb{Q}
this presents the additional problem of detecting large values of
L(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi)
for Dirichlet characters
\chi
over prime power moduli. In a related but not identical direction, Hough
[
8
]
used the resonance method to establish a result of quality similar to
(
2
)
within prescribed angular sectors for Dirichlet characters varying on prime moduli, while de la Bretèche and Tenenbaum
[
6
]
have since improved the quality of this bound for such even characters (without the angular sector restriction) to the level of Bondarenko and Seip’s result on the zeta function.

The complexity of Hecke characters expands as one moves to proper extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
. For a fundamental example beyond both the archimedean and Dirichlet character types, when
K
is an imaginary quadratic number field,
angular characters
emerge from sole ramification at the unique complex place. On principal ideals of the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, these characters behave precisely as
\xi_{\ell}((\beta))=e(\ell\arg\beta/2\pi)
for integers
\ell
divisible by
|\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}|
, where
\beta
is an element of a fixed complex embedding of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. The integer
\ell
is said to be the
frequency
of the character and there are multiple angular characters of any given permissible frequency when
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is not a PID; this multiplicity is exactly equal to the size of the class group. The purpose of this article is to apply for the first time the resonance method to Hecke
L
-functions to angular characters to address for the first time the problem of detecting their large values, complementing the progress made on the archimedean and Dirichlet character types over
\mathbb{Q}
. We prove the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic number field and
\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}
be its associated set of angular characters with frequency
\ell
. Then

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\leqslant\ell\leqslant 2X\\
\xi_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\log\left|L(\tfrac{1}{2},\xi_{\ell})\right|\geqslant\left(\sqrt{2}+o_{K}(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}}.

The most substantial contextual difference in our application of the resonance method arises in Sections
4.1
and
4.2
when one seeks to isolate and then analyze the diagonal terms (the analogues of
n=mk
in (
1
)) of the analogous quantity which appears, very roughly, in this article as

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{b}\leqslant N\\
\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k}\ll X\\
\mathfrak{k}\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}\text{ principal}\end{subarray}}\frac{r(\mathfrak{a})r(\mathfrak{b})}{\sqrt{\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k}}}\sum_{k\asymp X}e(k\arg\gamma_{\mathfrak{k}\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}}/2\pi),

where
\gamma_{\mathfrak{n}}
is the generator of the principal ideal
\mathfrak{n}
nearest to the positive real line with respect to rotation about the origin. A similar, but more relaxed, analysis precedes this in Section
4.1
when examining the mean of our resonator polynomial. At this point, one must consider the geometry of the embedding of
K
into
\mathbb{C}
. Indeed, the diagonal condition becomes characterized by whether the product
\mathfrak{k}\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}
is principally generated by a rational element of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. This is the only way the innermost sum above can avoid meaningful cancellation when
N
is bounded appropriately in terms of
X
.

We therefore bound length of the resonator in terms of
X
(as is typical) so that our analysis of the quantity illustrated above depends on an area
\mathbb{C}
where the embedding of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is “sufficiently discretized” such that all
\gamma_{\mathfrak{n}}

near the real line
are
exactly rational
. As a consequence, the off-diagonal terms where the
\mathfrak{k}\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}
are not rationally generated will not meaningfully contribute.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Hecke characters

We now recall the basic properties of Hecke characters; the reader may reference
[
10
, Section 7.6]
for additional details. Let
K
be a general number field and
\mathcal{O}_{K}
its ring of integers. We will denote the group of non-zero fractional ideals as
\mathfrak{I}
; it contains the subgroup
\mathfrak{P}
of non-zero fractional principal ideals. The quotient group
\mathfrak{I}/\mathfrak{P}
is the class group, which has finite size
h_{K}
. For a non-zero integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
, we further denote
\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}
as the set of all fractional ideals coprime to
\mathfrak{m}
.

Suppose
K
has
r_{1}
real embeddings and
r_{2}
complex embeddings up to conjugation. A Hecke character modulo
\mathfrak{m}
is a continuous homomorphism
\xi:\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{m}}\to S^{1}
(when interpreted as a morphism on the idele class group of
K
) for which there are two characters

\chi_{f}:(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}\to S^{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\chi_{\infty}:(\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{r_{1}}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r_{2}}\to S^{1}

such that
\xi((\beta))=\chi_{f}(\beta)\chi_{\infty}(\beta)
for every
\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
where
((\beta),\mathfrak{m})=1
. A pair of characters
(\chi_{f},\chi_{\infty})
is associated to a Hecke character if and only if
\chi_{f}(\epsilon)\chi_{\infty}(\epsilon)=1
for every
\epsilon\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
. The Hecke characters associated to such a pair differ by multiplication of a character of the class group. Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the set of all Hecke characters of
K
.

We now focus our attention to when
K
is an imaginary quadratic number field and fix an embedding into
\mathbb{C}
so that no ambiguity may occur from conjugation. Hence we may use
\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
to mean both the ring element and its image under the embedding. Let
\omega_{K}=|\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}|
and for each integer
\ell
divisible by
\omega_{K}
define the set

(3)

\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}:=\left\{\xi_{\ell}\in\mathcal{H}:\xi_{\ell}\big((\beta)\big)=e\left(\frac{\ell\arg\beta}{2\pi}\right)\text{ for all non-zero }\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}\right\}

of angular characters with frequency
\ell
as in Theorem
1
, where
|\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}|=h_{K}
. In the event that
\omega_{K}\nmid\ell
, we simply take
\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}
to be empty. It will be useful to have a standard choice of generator for principal ideals that we encounter. For a principal ideal
\mathfrak{n}
, let
\gamma_{\mathfrak{n}}
denote the generator of
\mathfrak{n}
whose principal argument resides in
[-\pi\omega_{K}^{-1},\pi\omega_{K}^{-1})
.

2.2.
Functional equation

For any number field
K
, we may associate to any Hecke character its Hecke
L
-function

L(s,\xi)=\sum_{\mathfrak{k}\neq 0}\xi(\mathfrak{k})\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k}^{-s}

which converges absolutely for
\mathrm{Re}\,s>1
and has meromorphic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
. Each such
L
-function possesses a functional equation. In particular, for the
L
-functions of interest in this article,
[
9
, Theorem 3.8]
boils down to the following.

Theorem 2
.

Let
D
denote the discriminant of the imaginary quadratic number field
K
and
\xi_{\ell}
be an angular character of frequency
\ell
. The function

(4)

\Lambda(s,\xi_{\ell})=|D|^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s+|\ell|/2)L(s,\xi_{\ell})

is entire for
\xi_{\ell}
non-trivial and satisfies the functional equation

(5)

\Lambda(s,\xi_{\ell})=\Lambda(1-s,\overline{\xi_{\ell}}).

When examining
L
-functions along the critical line, it is often beneficial to express the
L
-function as a smoothly weighted Dirichlet series in what is known as the approximate functional equation. Roughly speaking, and in general, this weight has approximate value one until its transition point, where it then drops rapidly to zero. One may think of the approximate functional equation as essentially a finite sum. For Hecke
L
-functions, the location of this point and the decay rate are both subject to the complexity of the associated character.

Theorem 3
.

For
K
and
\xi_{\ell}
as in Theorem
2
,

L\left(\tfrac{1}{2},\xi_{\ell}\right)=\sum_{\mathfrak{k}\neq(0)}\frac{\xi_{\ell}(\mathfrak{k})+\overline{\xi_{\ell}}(\mathfrak{k})}{\sqrt{\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k}}}W_{K}\left(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k},|\ell|\right)

where

(6)

W_{K}\bigg(\frac{|D|^{1/2}y}{2\pi},2x\bigg)=V(y,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1)}\rho_{s}(x)y^{-s}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}

and

(7)

\rho_{s}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(x+s+\tfrac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(x+\tfrac{1}{2})}.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda(s,\xi_{\ell})
be as in (
4
) in Theorem
2
. By a contour shift permitted by the exponential decay of the gamma function along vertical strips, we find

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1)}\Lambda(s+\tfrac{1}{2},\xi_{\ell})\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}=\Lambda(\tfrac{1}{2},\xi_{\ell})+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(-1)}\Lambda(s+\tfrac{1}{2},\xi_{\ell})\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}.

On the right side above, applying the substitution
s\mapsto-s
and the functional equation (
5
) from Theorem
2
before substituting (
4
) and integrating term-wise on each side, we obtain

L(\tfrac{1}{2},\xi_{\ell})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{\mathfrak{k}\neq 0}\frac{\xi_{\ell}(\mathfrak{k})+\overline{\xi_{\ell}}(\mathfrak{k})}{\sqrt{\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k}}}\int_{(1)}\rho_{s}(|\ell|/2)\left(2\pi|D|^{-1/2}\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k}\right)^{-s}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s},

as desired.
∎

The following lemmata characterize the behavior of
\rho_{s}(x)
and its derivatives so that we may determine where
V(y,x)
, as defined in (
6
), is “essentially supported” and how quickly it decays. The first of these demonstrates that, in suitable bounded vertical strips,
\rho_{\sigma+it}(x)
has an approximately linear phase in
t
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
x\geqslant 1
and
s=\sigma+it
where
|\sigma|\leqslant x/2
. For
\rho_{s}(x)
as in (
7
), we have

\displaystyle\rho_{s}(x)=(x/e)^{\sigma}\left(1+\sigma/x\right)^{x+\sigma}\exp\left(F_{x+\sigma}(t)+i\psi(t)\right)\left(1+O\left(x^{-1}\right)\right)

with

F_{\kappa}(t)=-\int_{0}^{t}\arctan\left(u/\kappa\right)\mathrm{d}u

and real-valued phase

\psi(t)=t\log\left(x+\sigma\right)+O\left(|t|^{3}x^{-2}\right).

Proof.

We begin by recalling Stirling’s formula
[
12
, Theorem 2.3]
, which states that

\Gamma(s)=\exp(s\log s-s)\left(2\pi/s\right)^{1/2}\left(1+O\left(|s|^{-1}\right)\right)

as
|s|\to\infty
within any sector of
\mathbb{C}
which omits the negative real axis. Applying Stirling’s formula to (
7
) and noting (
9
) shows

\displaystyle\rho_{s}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{(x+s+\tfrac{1}{2})^{x+s}}{(x+\tfrac{1}{2})^{x}}e^{-s}\left(1+O(x^{-1})\right)

(8)

\displaystyle=(x/e)^{s}\left(1+s/x\right)^{x+s}\left(1+O\left(x^{-1}\right)\right)

where the expansion of
\log(1+z)
revealed

\displaystyle(x+s+\tfrac{1}{2})^{x+s}

\displaystyle=(x+s)^{x+s}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{2}(x+s)^{-1}\right)^{x+s}

(9)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{e}(x+s)^{x+s}\left(1+O\left(x^{-1}\right)\right),

and one notes that the error term above is uniformly bounded for
|\mathrm{Re}\,s|\leqslant x/2
. Toward explicating the magnitude and phase of (
8
), we may factor
(1+s/x)^{x+s}
as the product of

\displaystyle(1+s/x)^{x+\sigma}

\displaystyle=(1+\sigma/x)^{x+\sigma}\left(1+\tfrac{it}{x+\sigma}\right)^{x+\sigma}

\displaystyle=(1+\sigma/x)^{x+\sigma}\exp\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(x+\sigma)\log\big(1+\left(\tfrac{t}{x+\sigma}\right)^{2}\big)+i\phi_{1}(t)\right),

where

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=(x+\sigma)\arctan\left(\tfrac{t}{x+\sigma}\right)

(10)

\displaystyle=t+O\left(|t|^{3}x^{-2}\right),

and

\displaystyle(1+s/x)^{it}

\displaystyle=(1+\sigma/x)^{it}\left(1+\tfrac{it}{x+\sigma}\right)^{it}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-t\arctan\left(\tfrac{t}{x+\sigma}\right)+i\phi_{2}(t)\right),

where

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=t\log(1+\sigma/x)+\tfrac{t}{2}\log\big(1+\left(\tfrac{t}{x+\sigma}\right)^{2}\big)

(11)

\displaystyle=t\log(1+\sigma/x)+O(|t|^{3}x^{-2}),

noting that both
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are real-valued. Inserting each of (
10
) and (
11
) back into (
8
) shows

\displaystyle\rho_{s}(x)=

\displaystyle\left(1+O\left(x^{-1}\right)\right)(x/e)^{\sigma}\left(1+\sigma/x\right)^{x+\sigma}

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(x+\sigma)\log\big(1+\left(\tfrac{t}{x+\sigma}\right)^{2}\big)-t\arctan\left(\tfrac{t}{x+\sigma}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(i\phi_{1}(t)+i\phi_{2}(t)+it(-1+\log x)\right)

thus yielding

\rho_{s}(x)=(x/e)^{\sigma}\left(1+\sigma/x\right)^{x+\sigma}\exp\left(\tilde{F}_{x+\sigma}(t)+i\psi(t)\right)\left(1+O\left(x^{-1}\right)\right)

for
\psi(t)
as defined in the statement of Lemma
1
and

(12)

\tilde{F}_{\kappa}(t):=-t\arctan\left(\tfrac{t}{\kappa}\right)+\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\log\left(1+t^{2}/\kappa^{2}\right).

Note that
\tilde{F}_{\kappa}(0)=0
and
\tilde{F}_{\kappa}^{\prime}(t)=-\arctan(t/\kappa)
, so that an application of the fundamental theorem of calculus shows
F_{\kappa}(t)=\tilde{F}_{\kappa}(t)
, finishing the proof.
∎

Lemma 2
.

For
n\geqslant 1
and
\mathrm{Re}\,z\geqslant 1
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}z^{n}}\log\Gamma(z)=\delta_{n}^{1}\log z+O_{n}\left(\mathrm{Re}\,(z)^{-\delta_{n}^{1}-n+1}\right),

where
\delta_{n}^{m}
is the Kronecker delta function.

Proof.

We begin with the well-known identity
[
14
, §12.31]

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\log\Gamma(z)=\log z-\frac{1}{2z}-\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{u}+\frac{1}{e^{u}-1}\right)e^{-uz}\,\mathrm{d}u.

The case
n=1
follows from the exponential decay of the integrand above, which begins at
\mathrm{Re}\,(z)^{-1}
. For
n\geqslant 2
, see that

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}z^{n}}\log\Gamma(z)=(-1)^{n}(n-1)!\left(\frac{1}{nz^{n-1}}+\frac{1}{2z^{n}}+O\left(\mathrm{Re}\,(z)^{-n}\right)\right),

which finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
x\geqslant 1
and
s=\sigma+it
with
|\sigma|\leqslant x/2
. Then, for
n\geqslant 0
,

(13)

\rho_{s}^{(n)}(x)=\rho_{s}(x)\log^{n}(1+s/x)\left(1+O_{n}(x^{-1})\right).

Proof.

The case
n=0
is clear and the case
n=1
follows from the logarithmic derivative

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\rho_{s}(x)=\rho_{s}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\log\rho_{s}(x)

and Lemma
2
. Proceeding inductively for
n\geqslant 2
, note that

\displaystyle\rho_{s}^{(n)}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n-1}}{\mathrm{d}x^{n-1}}\left(\rho_{s}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\log\rho_{s}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{m}\rho_{s}^{(m)}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n-m}}{\mathrm{d}x^{n-m}}\log\rho_{s}(x).

By our inductive hypothesis (
13
) for
m<n
and Lemma
2
, we note the main contribution from
m=n-1
and conclude the proof.
∎

Having established Lemmata
1
and
3
, we are now equipped to understand the general behavior of
V(y,x)
and its derivatives in the approximate functional equation of
L(\tfrac{1}{2},\xi_{\ell})
as introduced in Theorem
3
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
y\geqslant 1
,
x\geqslant 3
,
n\geqslant 0
, and
V(y,x)
be as in (
6
). Then

(14)

\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial x^{n}}V(y,x)=\delta_{n}^{0}+O_{n}\Big(x^{-n/2}(y/x)^{\sqrt{x}}\Big)\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial x^{n}}V(y,x)\ll_{n}x^{-n/2}(x/y)^{\sqrt{x}},

where
\delta_{n}^{m}
is the Kronecker delta function.

Proof.

Let
|\sigma|=x^{1/2}
. Shifting the line of integration in (
6
) to
(\sigma)
(perhaps picking up contribution from the pole at
s=0
), differentiating
n
times with respect to
x
, and substituting
s=\sigma+it
yields

(15)

\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial x^{n}}V(y,x)=\delta_{n}^{0}\Delta_{\sigma}+\frac{y^{-\sigma}}{2\pi\sigma}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\rho^{(n)}_{\sigma+it}(x)y^{-it}G(\sigma,t)\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
\Delta_{\sigma}\in\{0,1\}
indicates whether
\sigma
is negative and

G(\sigma,t)=\frac{\sigma}{\sigma+it}.

We will start by bounding the tails of the integral in (
15
), corresponding to the values
|t|\geqslant x^{1/2+\varepsilon}
. Exercising Lemmata
1
and
3
with
(1+\sigma/x)^{x+\sigma}=\exp(\sigma+O(1))
from our choice
|\sigma|=x^{1/2}
shows these tails are bounded by an absolute constant multiple of

\displaystyle\int_{|t|\geqslant x^{1/2+\varepsilon}}\left|\rho_{\sigma+it}^{(n)}(x)\right|\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\ll_{n}\int_{|t|\geqslant x^{1/2+\varepsilon}}\left|\rho_{\sigma+it}(x)\log^{n}\left(1+\tfrac{\sigma+it}{x}\right)\right|\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\ll x^{\sigma-n}\int_{x^{1/2+\varepsilon}}^{\infty}\exp\left(n\log t-\int_{0}^{t}\arctan\left(\tfrac{u}{x+\sigma}\right)\ \mathrm{d}u\right)\mathrm{d}t.

Noting that
\arctan(v)\geqslant v\pi/4
for
0\leqslant v\leqslant 1
and
\arctan(v)\geqslant\pi/4
for
v\geqslant 1
allows us to bound the right hand side above by

\displaystyle\leqslant x^{\sigma-n}

\displaystyle\int_{x^{1/2+\varepsilon}}^{x+\sigma}\exp\left(\tfrac{-t^{2}\pi}{8x}(1+o_{n}(1))\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+x^{\sigma-n}\exp\left(\tfrac{\pi}{8}(x+\sigma)\right)\int_{x+\sigma}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\tfrac{\pi}{4}t(1+o_{n}(1))\right)\mathrm{d}t

where utilizing the bound
\int_{c}^{\infty}e^{-u^{2}}\mathrm{d}u\ll e^{-c^{2}}/c
then shows that the main contribution above comes from the left summand whose integral is bounded by

\ll_{n}\int_{x^{1/2+\varepsilon}}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-t^{2}\pi}{16x}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\ll x^{1/2-\varepsilon}\exp(-x^{\varepsilon}\pi/16).

We therefore find that the integral in (
15
) becomes

(16)

O_{n}\left(x^{\sigma-n+1/2-\varepsilon}\exp(-x^{\varepsilon}\pi/16)\right)+\int_{-x^{1/2+\varepsilon}}^{x^{1/2+\varepsilon}}\rho^{(n)}_{\sigma+it}(x)y^{-it}G(\sigma,t)\mathrm{d}t.

Another application of lemmata
1
and
3
, keeping in mind
(1+\sigma/x)^{x+\sigma}
is equal to
\exp(\sigma+O(1))
by our choice
|\sigma|=x^{1/2}
, shows that the integral in (
16
) is

(17)

\ll_{n}x^{\sigma}\int_{-x^{1/2+\varepsilon}}^{x^{1/2+\varepsilon}}\exp\left(F_{x+\sigma}(t)\right)\left|\log^{n}\left(1+\tfrac{\sigma+it}{x}\right)\right|\,\mathrm{d}t

where, by (
12
), we may expand

\displaystyle F_{x+\sigma}(t)

\displaystyle=-t\arctan\left(\tfrac{t}{x+\sigma}\right)+\tfrac{1}{2}(x+\sigma)\log\left(1+\tfrac{t^{2}}{(x+\sigma)^{2}}\right)

(18)

\displaystyle=-\frac{t^{2}}{2x}\left(1+O(x^{-1/2}+t^{2}x^{-2})\right).

Inserting (
18
) into (
17
) yields a value

\ll x^{\sigma}\int_{-x^{1/2+\varepsilon}}^{x^{1/2+\varepsilon}}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{4}t^{2}/x)\left|\log^{n}\left(1+\tfrac{\sigma+it}{x}\right)\right|\,\mathrm{d}t\ll x^{\sigma-n+1/2}\sigma^{n}

which can be used as an upper bound for the integral in (
16
) resulting, ultimately, in a bound for (
15
) of the form

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial x^{n}}V(y,x)

\displaystyle=\delta_{n}^{0}\Delta_{\sigma}+O_{n}\left(\frac{y^{-\sigma}}{2\pi\sigma}\left(x^{\sigma-n+1/2}\sigma^{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\delta_{n}^{0}\Delta_{\sigma}+O_{n}\left(x^{-n/2}(x/y)^{\sigma}\right)

as desired.
∎

It will be convenient to produce lower bounds on sums of products involving factors of
W_{K}
by truncating the sums, where all other factors of the summands are non-negative. To do so, we must prove that
V(y,x)
is also non-negative for the input
(y,x)
under consideration. Note that the bounds in (
14
) are insufficient here. We therefore prove the following lemma which happens to also show that
V(y,x)
can be expressed as the ratio of an incomplete Gamma function to a complete Gamma function.

Lemma 5
.

If
y>0
and
x>0
, then
V(y,x)\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

We begin by shifting the contour in the definition of
V(y,x)
to the piece-wise contour
\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{-}+\gamma_{\varepsilon}+\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{+}
, where the
\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}
sit along
\pm i[\varepsilon,\infty)
and
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
forms the right half of the circle with radius
\varepsilon
centered at the origin. Define

I_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}}\rho_{s}(x)y^{-s}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}

and
I_{\varepsilon}
similarly for
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
, where we denote the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
of each
I_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}
and
I_{\varepsilon}
as
I^{\pm}
and
I
, respectively. Note that
I=1/2
and

\displaystyle I^{-}+I^{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi\Gamma(x+\tfrac{1}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Im}\,\Gamma(x+\tfrac{1}{2}+it)y^{-it}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Gamma(x+\tfrac{1}{2})^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{x-1/2}e^{-u}\,\mathrm{sgn}\log(u/y)\,\mathrm{d}u

which implies that
I^{-}+I^{+}\in\mathbb{R}
is strictly less than
1/2
in absolute value. The statement of the lemma follows.
∎

3.
The setup

Fix an imaginary quadratic number field
K
and let
X
be a large positive value. We begin by introducing our notion of a resonator

(19)

R(\xi_{\ell})=\sum_{\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{a}\leqslant N}\xi_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})r(\mathfrak{a})

where
\xi_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}
is a Hecke character as defined in (
3
) and the
r(\mathfrak{a})
are non-negative resonator coefficients. The length of our resonator will be set by a constraint involving the frequencies of the
\xi_{\ell}
under consideration, as is expected within the resonator method, where the goal is to build
R(\xi_{\ell})
to resonate with the oscillatory function
L(\tfrac{1}{2},\xi_{\ell})
for
\xi_{\ell}
varying among the
\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}
with
X\leqslant\ell\leqslant 2X
. One then expects large central values to be pronounced when examining
L(\tfrac{1}{2},\xi_{\ell})|R(\xi_{\ell})|^{2}
. To make this idea precise, let
0\leqslant\Phi(x)\leqslant 1
be a smooth weight function supported in
[1,2]
with uniformly bounded derivatives and note that

(20)

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\leqslant\ell\leqslant 2X\\
\xi_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\left|L(\tfrac{1}{2},\xi_{\ell})\right|\geqslant\frac{\big|\sum_{\ell}\Phi(\ell/X)\sum_{\xi_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}}L(\tfrac{1}{2},\xi_{\ell})|R(\xi_{\ell})|^{2}\big|}{\sum_{\ell}\Phi(\ell/X)\sum_{\xi_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}}|R(\xi_{\ell})|^{2}}.

We make provisions for the resonator coefficients before an explicit choice is later made. Let
r\geqslant 0
be multiplicative,
r(\mathfrak{p})=0
on prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
that are not generated by a factor of a split rational prime, and for all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
,
r(\mathfrak{p})=r(\overline{\mathfrak{p}})
and
r(\mathfrak{p}^{m})=0
for
m\geqslant 2
. These choices do not affect the quality of our result and serve to simplify many calculations that are encountered.

3.1.
Bounding the denominator

To start, we examine the denominator of the ratio in (
20
), upon which we wish to produce an upper bound. By orthogonality of the class group characters and the definition of
\gamma_{\mathfrak{n}}
, both discussed in Section
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega_{K}\mid\ell}\Phi(\ell/X)\sum_{\xi_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}}|R(\xi_{\ell})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\omega_{K}\mid\ell}\Phi(\ell/X)\sum_{\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{b}\leqslant N}r(\mathfrak{a})r(\mathfrak{b})\sum_{\xi_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}}\xi_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}})

(21)

\displaystyle=h_{K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{b}\leqslant N\\
\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}\in P\end{subarray}}r(\mathfrak{a})r(\mathfrak{b})\sum_{k}\Phi(\omega_{K}k/X)e(\omega_{K}k\arg\gamma_{\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}}/2\pi),

where
P
denotes the set of principal ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Application of Poisson summation with a change of variable shows that the above equals

(22)

\displaystyle h_{K}\omega_{K}^{-1}X\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{b}\leqslant N\\
\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}\in P\end{subarray}}r(\mathfrak{a})r(\mathfrak{b})\sum_{j}\hat{\Phi}\left(X(\omega_{K}^{-1}j-\arg\gamma_{\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}}/2\pi)\right).

The rapid decay of
\hat{\Phi}
allows us to profitably split this sum into two cases according to whether
\left|\arg\gamma_{\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}}\right|
passes the threshold
X^{-1+\varepsilon}
. Note the series expansion of
\sin\arg\gamma_{\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}}
in the case
\left|\arg\gamma_{\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}}\right|<X^{-1+\varepsilon}
shows that

(23)

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{Im}\,\gamma_{\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}}\right|\leqslant N\left(X^{-1+\varepsilon}+O\left(X^{-2}\right)\right).

It is here that a constraint on the length of the resonator appears should we wish to avoid off-diagonal contribution in (
22
); later, though, we further restrict the length. Let
P_{0}
denote the set of principal ideals generated by rational numbers and
P^{\prime}
be the set of ideals which contain no
P_{0}
-factors. Enforcing
N\leqslant X^{1-\varepsilon}
guarantees
\left|\mathrm{Im}\,\gamma_{\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}}\right|<1
by (
23
), so that
\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}\in P_{0}
for
X
sufficiently large relative to
\varepsilon
.

By considering the prime factorization of
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
, we may write

(24)

\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}_{0}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}_{0}\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}

uniquely for
\mathfrak{a}_{0},\mathfrak{b}_{0}\in P_{0}
and
\mathfrak{a^{\prime}},\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\in P^{\prime}
to find that
\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}\in P_{0}
if and only if
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
. By the bound
\hat{\Phi}(y)\ll_{n}y^{-n}
(derived from repeated integration by parts) and the deduction above, the total contribution to the sum in (
22
) over all such pairs
(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
is

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{b}\leqslant N\\
\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}\in P_{0}\end{subarray}}r(\mathfrak{a})r(\mathfrak{b})\big(\hat{\Phi}(0)+O(X^{-1})\big)

\displaystyle\ll\hat{\Phi}(0)\sum_{\mathfrak{a}_{0},\mathfrak{b}_{0}\in P_{0}}r(\mathfrak{a}_{0})r(\mathfrak{b}_{0})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\in P^{\prime}\\
(\mathfrak{a}^{\prime},\mathfrak{a}_{0}\mathfrak{b}_{0})=1\end{subarray}}r(\mathfrak{a}^{\prime})^{2}

(25)

\displaystyle=\hat{\Phi}(0)\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{\mathfrak{p}}\left(1+4r(\mathfrak{p})^{2}+r(\mathfrak{p})^{4}\right),

where the primed product above is indexed over arbitrary representatives of conjugate pairs of factors of split rational primes. Again wielding the above bound on
\hat{\Phi}(y)
and exercising the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we find that the off-diagonal contribution (
i.e.
when
\left|\arg\gamma_{\mathfrak{a}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}}\right|\geqslant X^{-1+\varepsilon}
) to the sum in (
22
) is

(26)

\ll_{m}X^{-\varepsilon m}N\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\neq 0}r(\mathfrak{a})^{2}\leqslant X^{1-\varepsilon m}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}(1+r(\mathfrak{p})^{2}).

Choosing
m
sufficiently large relative to
\varepsilon
allows the quantity in (
25
) to dominate that in (
26
). Combining this observation with (
21
) and (
22
) yields

(27)

\sum_{\ell}\Phi(\ell/X)\sum_{\xi_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}}|R(\xi_{\ell})|^{2}\ll X\hat{\Phi}(0)\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{\mathfrak{p}}\left(1+4r(\mathfrak{p})^{2}+r(\mathfrak{p})^{4}\right).

4.
Resonator application

Application of Theorem
3

(28)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\Phi(\ell/X)\sum_{\xi_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}}|R(\xi_{\ell})|^{2}\sum_{\mathfrak{k}}\frac{\xi_{\ell}(\mathfrak{k})+\overline{\xi_{\ell}}(\mathfrak{k})}{\sqrt{\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k}}}W_{K}\big(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k},|\ell|\big)

upon which we wish to place a lower bound. Our analysis of the sum over

. Further, the main contribution to the sum over

28

21
) and (

\displaystyle h_{K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{b}\leqslant N\\

\displaystyle=h_{K}\omega_{K}^{-1}X\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{b}\leqslant N\\

where

denotes the Fourier transform of

. Over the course of this section, we will establish a lower bound on (

We begin by forming a handle on the decay of the transform of the function

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}u^{n}}\Phi(u)W_{K}(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k},uX)=\sum_{0\leqslant m\leqslant n}\binom{n}{m}\Phi^{(n-m)}(u)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathrm{d}u^{m}}W_{K}(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k},uX)

and using (

(30)

\ll_{n}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}X^{n/2}\left(\frac{uX}{c_{K}\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k}}\right)^{\sqrt{uX/2}},&\text{if }u\leqslant c_{K}\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k}/X;\\
1+X^{n/2}\left(\frac{uX}{c_{K}\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k}}\right)^{-\sqrt{uX/2}},&\text{if }u>c_{K}\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k}/X.\end{array}\right.

Repeating integration by parts
n

(31)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{u}[\Phi(u)W_{K}(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k};uX)](v)\ll_{n}v^{-n}X^{n/2}S(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k},X)+v^{-n}\max\left(0,2-\tfrac{c_{K}\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k}}{X}\right)

(32)

Using the substitution

in (

(33)

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k},X)\ll_{n}\begin{cases}X^{-1/2}\left(c_{K}\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k}/X\right)^{\sqrt{X/2}},&\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k}\leqslant X/c_{K};\\

so that the insertion of (

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{u}[\Phi(u)W_{K}(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{k};uX)](v)

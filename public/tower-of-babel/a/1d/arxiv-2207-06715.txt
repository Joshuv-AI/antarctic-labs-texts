On a new concept of stochastic domination and the laws of large numbers
Lê Vǎn Thành

Abstract
Consider a sequence of positive integers { k n , n ≥ 1 } \{k_{n},n\geq 1\} , and an array of nonnegative real numbers { a n , i , 1 ≤ i ≤ k n , n ≥ 1 } \{a_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\} satisfying sup n ≥ 1 ∑ i = 1 k n a n , i = C 0 ∈ ( 0 , ∞ ) . \sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}=C_{0}\in(0,\infty). This paper introduces the concept of { a n , i } \{a_{n,i}\} -stochastic domination. We develop some techniques concerning this concept and apply them to remove an assumption in a strong law of large numbers of Chandra and Ghosal [Acta. Math. Hungarica, 1996]. As a by-product, a considerable extension of a recent result of Boukhari [J. Theoret. Probab., 2021] is established and proved by a different method. The results on laws of large numbers are new even when the summands are independent. Relationships between the concept of { a n , i } \{a_{n,i}\} -stochastic domination and the concept of { a n , i } \{a_{n,i}\} -uniform integrability are presented. Two open problems are also discussed.



∎

Introduction and Motivation

Let
1\leq p<2
. The classical Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund strong law of large numbers (SLLN)
states that for a sequence
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
of independent identically distributed mean zero
random variables, condition
\mathbb{E}(|X_{1}|^{p})<\infty
is
necessary and sufficient for

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}}{n^{1/p}}=0\text{ almost surely (a.s.).}

(1.1)

Now, let us recall a weak dependence structure introduced in Chandra and Ghosal
chandra1996extensions
as follows.
A sequence of random variables
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}

is said to be
asymptotically almost negatively associated
(AANA) if there exists
a sequence of nonnegative real numbers
\{q_{n},n\geq 1\}
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}q_{n}=0
such that

\operatorname{Cov}(f(X_{n}),g(X_{n+1},\ldots,X_{n+k}))\leq q_{n}\left(\operatorname{Var}(f(X_{n}))\operatorname{Var}(g(X_{n+1},\ldots,X_{n+k}))\right)^{1/2},

for all
n\geq 1,k\geq 1
and for all coordinatewise nondecreasing continuous functions
f
and
g

provided the right side of the above inequality is finite. The
q_{n},n\geq 1
are called
mixing coefficients
. The starting point of the current investigation
is the following SLLN established by Chandra and Ghosal
chandra1996extensions
.

Theorem 1.1
(Chandra and Ghosal
chandra1996extensions
)

Let
1\leq p<2
and let
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of AANA mean zero random variables
with the sequence of mixing coefficients
satisfying
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q_{n}^{2}<\infty
. Let

G(x)=\sup_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(|X_{i}|>x),\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

If

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{p-1}G(x)\operatorname{d}x<\infty,

(1.2)

and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n}|^{p}>n)<\infty,

(1.3)

then the Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund SLLN (
1.1
) is obtained.

The above result of Chandra and Ghosal
chandra1996extensions
weakens the assumptions
in the classical Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund SLLN not only
by considering a weak dependence structure, but also by relaxing
the identical distribution condition. We refer to (
1.2
)
and (
1.3
) as the Chandra–Ghosal conditions.
It is clear that for a sequence
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
of random variables with a common
law, (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are equivalent since each of them is equivalent to
\mathbb{E}(|X_{1}|^{p})<\infty
.
This leads to a natural question in this context is whether
(
1.2
) or (
1.3
) can be removed.
The current work is an attempt to answer this
question. More precisely, we shall prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2

Theorem
1.1
holds without Condition (
1.3
).

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we develop some results concerning a new concept of
stochastic domination which leads to the concept of the Cesàro
stochastic domination as a
particular case.

Let
\{k_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of positive integers. An array
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
of
random variables is said to be

stochastically dominated

by a random variable
X
if

\sup_{1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x)\leq\mathbb{P}(|X|>x),\ \text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.4)

This concept was extended to the concept of the so-called Cesàro
stochastic domination by Gut
gut1992complete
as follows.
An array
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
of
random variables is said to be

stochastically dominated in the Cesàro sense
(or
weakly mean dominated
)
by a random variable
X
if

\sup_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{1}{k_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x)\leq C\mathbb{P}(|X|>x),\ \text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.5)

where
C>0
is a constant.
It was shown by Gut
(
gut1992complete
, Example 2.1)
that
(
1.5
) is strictly weaker than (
1.4
).

We will now introduce a new concept of stochastic domination. Let
\{a_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
be an array of nonnegative real numbers
satisfying

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}=C_{0}\in(0,\infty).

(1.6)

An array
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
of
random variables is said to be

\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastically dominated

by a random variable
X
if

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x)\leq C_{0}\mathbb{P}(|X|>x),\ \text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.7)

In view of Gut’s definition in (
1.5
), one may be tempted to give
an apparently weaker definition of
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
being
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastically dominated
by a random variable
Y
, namely that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x)\leq C\mathbb{P}(|Y|>x),\ \text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.8)

for some finite constant
C>0
. However, it will be shown in Theorem
2.2
that
(
1.7
) and (
1.8
) are indeed equivalent.
Therefore, concerning Gut’s definition of the Cesàro stochastic domination, we can simply choose
C=1

in (
1.5
). If
a_{n,i}=1/k_{n},1\leq i\leq k_{n},\ n\geq 1,

then it is obvious that
C_{0}=1
, and the concept of
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastic domination reduces to
the concept of stochastic domination in the Cesàro sense.

If

and

is a sequence of
random variables satisfying (

is valid irrespective of any dependence structure (see Remark 3 in Chandra and Ghosal

Theorem 2.1

Let
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
be an array of random variables, let

\{a_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
be an array of
nonnegative
real numbers satisfying (
1.6
)
and let

F(x)=1-\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\sup\limits_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x),\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

Then
F(\cdot)
is the distribution function of a random variable
X
if and only if
\lim_{x\to\infty}F(x)=1.

In such a case,
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
is
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastically dominated by
X
.

Proof

It is clear that
F(\cdot)
is nondecreasing, and

\lim\limits_{x\to-\infty}F(x)=1-\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}=0.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, let
n_{0}\geq 1
be such that

\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n_{0}}}a_{n_{0},i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n_{0},i}|>a)>\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>a)-\varepsilon/2,

or equivalently,

1-\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n_{0}}}a_{n_{0},i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n_{0},i}|>a)<F(a)+\varepsilon/2.

(2.1)

Since the function

x\mapsto\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n_{0}}}a_{n_{0},i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n_{0},i}|>x),\ x\in\mathbb{R},

is nonincreasing and right continuous, there exists
\delta>0
such that

-\varepsilon/2<\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n_{0}}}a_{n_{0},i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n_{0},i}|>x)-\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n_{0}}}a_{n_{0},i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n_{0},i}|>a)\leq 0\text{ for all $x$ such that }0\leq x-a<\delta.

Therefore, for
x
satisfying
0\leq x-a<\delta
, we have

\displaystyle F(x)-\varepsilon

\displaystyle=1-\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\sup\limits_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x)-\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq 1-\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n_{0}}}a_{n_{0},i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n_{0},i}|>x)-\varepsilon

\displaystyle<1-\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n_{0}}}a_{n_{0},i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n_{0},i}|>a)-\varepsilon/2

\displaystyle<F(a)\ \text{ (by \eqref{df01})}

and so
|F(x)-F(a)|<\varepsilon
. Thus
\lim\limits_{x\to a^{+}}F(x)=F(a)
. Since
a\in\mathbb{R}
is arbitrary, this implies that
F
is right
continuous on
\mathbb{R}
. Since
F(\cdot)
is nondecreasing, right continuous and
\lim\limits_{x\to-\infty}F(x)=0
,
it is the distribution function of a random variable
X
if and only if

\lim\limits_{x\to\infty}F(x)=1
.
By definition of
F(\cdot)
,
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}

is
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastically dominated by
X
.

Example 2.5

Let

Then, for

\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }n\leq x,\\
\dfrac{n-m_{n}}{n}&\text{ if }n>x.\end{cases}

and for

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x)=\sum_{i=m_{n}+1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }n\leq x,\\
\dfrac{n-m_{n}}{n^{2}}\leq\dfrac{1}{n}&\text{ if }n>x.\end{cases}

Thus

as

.

\{a_{n,i}\}

Recall that a real-valued function
L(\cdot)
is said to be

\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{L(\lambda x)}{L(x)}=1.

If

there exists a slowly varying function
\tilde{L}(\cdot)

\lim_{x\to\infty}L(x)\tilde{L}\left(xL(x)\right)=1\ \text{ and }\lim_{x\to\infty}\tilde{L}(x)L\left(x\tilde{L}(x)\right)=1.

(2.5)

The function
\tilde{L}(\cdot)
is called the de Bruijn conjugate of
L(\cdot)
(see Bingham et al.
(
bingham1989regular
, p. 29)
).
For many “nice” slowly varying functions

,
we can choose

. Especially, if
L(x)=(\log x)^{\gamma}
or
L(x)=(\log\log x)^{\gamma}
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
then
\tilde{L}(x)=1/L(x)
.

Let
L(\cdot)
be a slowly varying function and let
\alpha>0
. By using a suitable asymptotic equivalence version
(see Lemma 2.2 and Lemma 2.3 (i) in Anh et al.
anh2021marcinkiewicz
),
we can firstly assume that
L(\cdot)
is positive and
differentiable on
[a,\infty)
, and
x^{\alpha}L(x)
is strictly increasing on
[a,\infty)
for some large
a
. Next, let

L_{1}(\cdot)
be a slowly varying function satisfying
L_{1}(x)=L(a)x/a
if
0\leq x<a
and
L_{1}(x)=L(x)
if
x\geq a
(i.e.,

L_{1}(0)=0
with a linear growth to
L(a)

over
[0,a)
, and
L_{1}(x)\equiv L(x)
on
[a,\infty)
).
Then (i)
L_{1}(x)
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
and differentiable on
[a,\infty)
, and
(ii)
x^{\alpha}L_{1}(x)
is strictly increasing on
[0,\infty)
.
In this paper, we will assume, without loss of generality, that these properties are fulfilled for the underlying slowly varying functions.

The next theorem shows that bounded moment conditions
on an array of random variables
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}

with respect to weights
\{a_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
can accomplish
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastic domination.

Theorem 2.6

Let
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
be an array of random variables,

\{a_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
an array of
nonnegative
real numbers satisfying (
1.6
).
Let
p>0
and let
\nu
be a fixed positive integer. Let
L(\cdot)
be a
slowly varying function. If

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|)\log_{\nu}^{(2)}(|X_{n,i}|)\right)<\infty,

(2.6)

then there exists a random variable
X

with distribution function

F(x)=1-\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x),\ x\in\mathbb{R}

(2.7)

such that
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
is
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastically dominated by
X
, and

\mathbb{E}\left(|X|^{p}L(|X|)\right)<\infty.

(2.8)

Remark 2.7

A weaker version of Theorem
2.6

for stochastic domination (without the appearance of the slowly varying function
L(\cdot)
)
was proved by Rosalsky and Thành
rosalsky2021note
(see Theorem 2.5 (ii) and (iii) in Rosalsky and Thành
rosalsky2021note
).
Typical examples of slowly varying functions

for (

) are

and

L(x)\equiv L_{1}(x)(\log_{\nu}^{(2)}(x))^{-1}
, where
L_{1}(\cdot)

is another slowly varying function.
Theorem
2.6
is proved by employing an idea from Galambos and Seneta
galambos1973regularly
.

Before proving Theorem
2.6
, we recall a simple result on the expectation of a nonnegative random variable, see Rosalsky and Thành
rosalsky2021note

for a proof.

Lemma 2.8

Let
h:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a measurable function with
h(0)=0
which is
bounded on
[0,A]
and differentiable on
[A,\infty)
for some
A\geq 0
.
If
\xi
is a nonnegative random variable, then

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}(h(\xi))&=\mathbb{E}(h(\xi)\mathbf{1}(\xi\leq A))+h(A)+\int_{A}^{\infty}h^{\prime}(x)\mathbb{P}(\xi>x)\operatorname{d}x.\end{split}

Proof of Theorem
2.6
.

Set

g(x)=x^{p}L(x)\log_{\nu}^{(2)}(x),\ \text{ and }h(x)=x^{p}L(x),\ x\geq 0.

Since
\lim_{x\to\infty}g(x)=\infty
and

g(\cdot)
is strictly increasing on
[0,\infty)
as we have assumed before, we have from Markov’s inequality and (
2.6
) that

0\leq\lim_{x\to\infty}\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x)\leq\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{g(x)}\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{E}(g(|X_{n,i}|))=0.

By Theorem
2.1
, the array
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
is

\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastically dominated by a random variable
X

with distribution function
F(\cdot)
given in (
2.7
).

Next, we prove (
2.8
). We firstly consider the case where the slowly varying function
L(\cdot)
is differentiable on an infinite interval far enough from
0
, and

\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{xL^{\prime}(x)}{L(x)}=0.

(2.9)

By (
2.9
), there exists
B\geq 0
such that

\left|\dfrac{xL^{\prime}(x)}{L(x)}\right|\leq\dfrac{p}{2},\ x>B.

It follows that

h^{\prime}(x)=px^{p-1}L(x)+x^{p}L^{\prime}(x)=x^{p-1}L(x)\left(p+\dfrac{xL^{\prime}(x)}{L(x)}\right)\leq\dfrac{3px^{p-1}L(x)}{2},\ x\geq B.

(2.10)

Therefore,
there exists a constant
C_{1}
such that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}(h(X))&=\mathbb{E}(h(X)\mathbf{1}(X\leq B))+h(B)+\int_{B}^{\infty}h^{\prime}(x)\mathbb{P}(X>x)\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C_{1}+\dfrac{3p}{2}\int_{B}^{\infty}x^{p-1}L(x)\mathbb{P}(X>x)\mathrm{d}x\\
&=C_{1}+\dfrac{3p}{2C_{0}}\int_{B}^{\infty}x^{p-1}L(x)\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x)\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C_{1}+\dfrac{3p}{2C_{0}}\int_{B}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{x\log_{\nu}^{(2)}(x)}\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{E}\left(g(|X_{n,i}|)\right)\mathrm{d}x\\
&=C_{1}+\dfrac{3p}{2C_{0}}\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{E}\left(g(|X_{n,i}|)\right)\int_{B}^{\infty}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{x\log_{\nu}^{(2)}(x)}<\infty,\end{split}

where we have applied Lemma
2.8
in the first equality,
(
2.10
) in the first inequality, Markov’s inequality in the second inequality, and
(
2.6
) in the last inequality. Thus we obtain (

) in this case.

For general slowly varying function

a slowly varying function

which is differentiable on

large enough, and satisfies

(2.11)

and

For
n\geq 1
,

, we have from (

) that for all

&\qquad=\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{n,i}|^{p}L_{1}(|X_{n,i}|)\log_{\nu}^{(2)}(|X_{n,i}|)\mathbf{1}(|X_{n,i}|\leq B_{2})\right)\\
&\quad\qquad+\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{n,i}|^{p}L_{1}(|X_{n,i}|)\log_{\nu}^{(2)}(|X_{n,i}|)\mathbf{1}(|X_{n,i}|>B_{2})\right)\\
&\qquad\leq C_{2}+2\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|)\log_{\nu}^{(2)}(|X_{n,i}|)\mathbf{1}(|X_{n,i}|>B_{2})\right),\end{split}

where

3
Relationships between
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastic domination and
\{a_{n,i}\}
-uniform integrability

The concept of
\{a_{n,i}\}
-uniform integrability was introduced by Ordóñez Cabrera
ordonezcabrera1994convergence
.
Let

\{a_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
be an array of
nonnegative
real numbers satisfying (
1.6
).
An array
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
of random variables is said to
be
\{a_{n,i}\}
-uniformly integrable
if

\lim_{a\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{E}(|X_{n,i}|\mathbf{1}(|X_{n,i}|>a))=0.

Similar to the classical characterization of the uniform integrability, it
was proved by Ordóñez Cabrera
ordonezcabrera1994convergence
that an
array of random variables

\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
is

\{a_{n,i}\}
-uniformly integrable
if and only if

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{E}(|X_{n,i}|)<\infty

and for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that whenever
\{A_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
is
an array of events satisfying

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(A_{n,i})<\delta,

then

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{E}(|X_{n,i}|\mathbf{1}(A_{n,i}))<\varepsilon.

If
a_{n,i}=1/k_{n},1\leq i\leq k_{n},\ n\geq 1,
then it reduces to the concept of
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
being

uniformly integrable in the Cesàro sense

which was introduced in
chandra1989uniform
.
The de La Vallée–Poussin criterion for uniform integrability in the Cesàro sense, and
for
\{a_{n,i}\}
-uniform integrability was proved, respectively, by Chandra and Goswami
chandra1992cesaro
, and
Ordóñez Cabrera
ordonezcabrera1994convergence
. The former is a special case of the latter,
which reads as follows:
An array of random variables

\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
is

\{a_{n,i}\}
-uniformly integrable
if and only if there exists a measurable
function
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
g(0)=0
,
g(x)/x\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
, and

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{E}(g(|X_{n,i}|))<\infty.

Moreover,
g
can be selected to be convex and such that
g(x)/x
is nondecreasing.

The next theorem establishes relationships between the concept of
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastic domination and the concept of
\{a_{n,i}\}
-uniform integrability.

Theorem 3.1

Let
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
be an array of random variables and let

\{a_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
be an array of
nonnegative
real numbers satisfying (
1.6
).
Let
p>0
and let
\tilde{L}(\cdot)
be the Brujin conjugate of a
slowly varying function
L(\cdot)
.

(i)

If
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
is
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastically dominated by a random variable

X
with
\mathbb{E}(|X|^{p}L(|X|^{p}))<\infty
, then
\{|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|^{p}),1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
is
\{a_{n,i}\}
-uniformly integrable.

(ii)

If
\{|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|^{p}),1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
is
\{a_{n,i}\}
-uniformly integrable, then
there exists a
random variable
X
with distribution function

F(x)=1-\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x),x\in\mathbb{R},

such that
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
is
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastically dominated by
X
,

\mathbb{E}\left(|X|^{p}L(|X|^{p})(\log_{\nu}^{(2)}(|X|))^{-1}\right)<\infty\text{ for all fixed positive integer }\nu,

(3.1)

and

\lim_{x\to\infty}x\mathbb{P}\left(|X|>x^{1/p}\tilde{L}^{1/p}(x)\right)=0.

(3.2)

Proof

Let
f(x)=x^{p}L(x^{p}),\ g(x)=x^{1/p}\tilde{L}^{1/p}(x),\ x\geq 0
.
Recalling that we assume, without loss of generality, that
f
and
g
are strictly increasing on
[0,\infty)
.

(i)
Since
\mathbb{E}(|X|^{p}L(|X|^{p}))<\infty
, it follows from the classical
de La Vallée Poussin criterion for uniform integrability
that there exists a continuous and strictly increasing function

h:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
h(0)=0
,
\lim_{x\to\infty}h(x)/x=\infty
,
and
\mathbb{E}(h(|X|^{p}L(|X|^{p})))<\infty.
Since
f(x)
is strictly increasing on
[0,\infty)
,
the
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastic domination assumption ensures that for all
n\geq 1
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>f^{-1}(h^{-1}(x)))\leq C_{0}\mathbb{P}(|X|>f^{-1}(h^{-1}(x))),\ x\in\mathbb{R}

or, equivalently,

\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|^{p})>h^{-1}(x))\leq C_{0}\mathbb{P}(|X|^{p}L(|X|^{p})>h^{-1}(x)),\ x\in\mathbb{R}

which, in turn, is equivalent to

\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(h(|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|^{p}))>x)\leq C_{0}\mathbb{P}(h(|X|^{p}L(|X|^{p}))>x),\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

It follows that

\begin{split}\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{E}(h(|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|^{p})))&=\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(h(|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|^{p}))>x)\operatorname{d}x\\
&\leq C_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(h(|X|^{p}L(|X|^{p}))>x)\operatorname{d}x\\
&=C_{0}\mathbb{E}(h(|X|^{p}L(|X|^{p})))<\infty.\end{split}

By the de La Vallée Poussin criterion for
\{a_{n,i}\}
-uniform integrability
(Ordóñez Cabrera
ordonezcabrera1994convergence
),

\{|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|^{p}),1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
is
\{a_{n,i}\}
-uniformly integrable.

(ii) Since
\{|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|^{p}),1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
is
\{a_{n,i}\}
-uniformly integrable,

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|^{p})\right)<\infty,

and so by Theorem
2.6
,

\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq k_{n},n\geq 1\}
is
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastically dominated by a
random variable
X
with
distribution function

F(x)=1-\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x),\ x\in\mathbb{R},

and (
3.1
) holds.

Finally, by using the de La Vallée Poussin criterion for
\{a_{n,i}\}
-uniform integrability again,
there exists a nondecreasing function
h
defined on
[0,\infty)
with
h(0)=0
such that

\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{h(x)}{x}=\infty,

(3.3)

and

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{E}(h(|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|^{p})))<\infty.

(3.4)

By applying Lemma 2.1 in Anh et al.
anh2021marcinkiewicz
, we have
f(g(x))/x\to 1
as
x\to\infty
,
and therefore

f(g(x))>x/2\text{ for all large }x.

(3.5)

We thus have from (
3.3
), (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and Markov’s inequality that

\begin{split}\lim_{x\to\infty}x\mathbb{P}\left(|X|>g(x)\right)&=\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\lim_{x\to\infty}x\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>g(x))\\
&\leq\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\lim_{x\to\infty}x\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(f(|X_{n,i}|)\geq f(g(x)))\\
&\leq\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\lim_{x\to\infty}x\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(f(|X_{n,i}|)\geq x/2)\\
&\leq\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\lim_{x\to\infty}x\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(h(f(|X_{n,i}|))\geq h(x/2))\\
&\leq\dfrac{1}{C_{0}}\lim_{x\to\infty}x\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\dfrac{\mathbb{E}(h(f(|X_{n,i}|)))}{h(x/2)}\\
&=\dfrac{2}{C_{0}}\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}}a_{n,i}\mathbb{E}(h(f(|X_{n,i}|)))\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{x/2}{h(x/2)}=0,\end{split}

thereby proving (
3.2
).
∎

4
Laws of large numbers for triangular arrays and proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3

In this section, we establish strong and weak laws of large numbers for triangular arrays of random variables.
We say that a collection
\{X_{i},1\leq i\leq N\}
of
random variables satisfies condition
(H)
if for all
a>0
,
there exists a constant
C
such that

\mathbb{E}\left(\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=m+1}^{m+k}\left(X_{i}^{(a)}-\mathbb{E}X_{i}^{(a)}\right)\right|\right)^{2}\leq C\sum_{i=m+1}^{m+n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i}^{(a)})^{2},\ m\geq 0,n\geq 1,m+n\leq N,

(4.1)

where

X_{i}^{(a)}=-a\mathbf{1}(X_{i}<-a)+X_{i}\mathbf{1}(|X_{i}|\leq a)+a\mathbf{1}(X_{i}>a),\ 1\leq i\leq N.

An infinite sequence of random variables
\{X_{i},i\geq 1\}
is said to satisfy condition
(H)
if
every finite subsequence satisfies condition
(H)
.
Many dependence structures meet this condition. For example, condition
(H)
holds for negatively associated sequences, negatively superadditive dependent sequences,
AANA sequences with the sequence of mixing coefficients is in

\ell_{2}
(the mixing coefficients
q_{n},n\geq 1
satisfying
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q_{n}^{2}<\infty
).
In
Adler and Matuła
adler2018exact
, the authors used a similar condition to establish
exact SLLNs (see Theorems 3.2 and 4.1 in
adler2018exact
).

Throughout this section, the symbol
C
denotes a positive universal constant which is not necessarily the same in each appearance.
We shall let the indices
k_{n}
in the previous sections be
k_{n}\equiv n
.

The following theorem establishes the rate of convergence in SLLN
for maximal partial sums from triangular arrays of dependent
random variables under the Chandra–Ghosal-type condition (see Condition (
4.2
) below).

Theorem 4.1

Let

\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq n,n\geq 1\}
be an array of mean zero random variables
such that for each
n\geq 1
fixed, the collection
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq n\}
satisfies condition
(H)
, and let

G(x)=\sup_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x),\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
L(\cdot)
be a slowly varying function and let
1\leq p<2
. When
p=1
, we further assume that
L(\cdot)
is nondecreasing and
L(x)\geq 1
for all
x\geq 0
.
If

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{p-1}L^{p}(x)G(x)\operatorname{d}x<\infty,

(4.2)

then

Theorem 4.5

Example 4.7

Let
0<p<1

4.23
) is satisfied since

Let

\hat{G}(x)=\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x),\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

For
n\geq 1
, we have

\begin{split}\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{n,i}|^{p}\log(|X_{n,i}|)\right)&\leq 1+\dfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}(|X_{n,n}|^{p}\log(|X_{n,n}|))\\
&=1+\dfrac{1}{\log(n)}\left(\dfrac{\log(n)-\log(\log(n))}{p}\right)\\

Therefore,
\{|X_{n,i}|^{p},1\leq i\leq n,n\geq 1\}
is uniformly integrable in the Cesàro sense by the de La Vallée Poussin criterion for the Cesàro uniform integrability.
Then by Corollary
3.2
,
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq n,n\geq 1\}
is
stochastically dominated in the Cesàro sense by a random variable
X

) (with

4.24
) (with
b_{n}\equiv n^{1/p}

\begin{split}\dfrac{1}{b_{n}}\max_{j\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}c_{n,i}X_{n,i}\right|&=\dfrac{1}{n^{1/p}}c_{n,n}|X_{n,n}|\\
&=\dfrac{n}{\log^{1/p}(n)}\to\infty\end{split}

Example 4.8

Let
0<p<1
,
b_{n}=n^{1/p},\ n\geq 1
and let
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
be
a sequence of random variables such that

\mathbb{P}(X_{n}=-1)=\mathbb{P}(X_{n}=1)=1/2\ \text{ for }n\not=2^{m},\ m\geq 0,

(4.33)

and

\mathbb{P}\left(X_{2^{m}}=-2^{m/p}/m^{1/p}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(X_{2^{m}}=2^{m/p}/m^{1/p}\right)=1/2\text{ for }m\geq 0.

(4.34)

Then

\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n}|>x)=\sup_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(|X_{2^{m}}|>x)=1\text{ for all }x\geq 1.

Thus there is no random variable
X
such that the sequence
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
is stochastically dominated by
X
,
and so we cannot apply Theorem 2.1 of Boukhari
boukhari2021weak
.

Now, for
n\geq 1
, let
m\geq 0
be such that
2^{m}\leq n<2^{m+1}
. Then

\begin{split}\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{i}|^{p}\log(|X_{i}|)\right)&\leq 1+\dfrac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{2^{i}}|^{p}\log(|X_{2^{i}}|)\right)\\
&=1+\dfrac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\dfrac{2^{i}}{i}\left(\dfrac{i-\log(i)}{p}\right)\\
&\leq 1+\dfrac{2}{p}<\infty.\end{split}

(4.35)

Therefore,
\{|X_{n}|^{p},n\geq 1\}
is uniformly integrable in the Cesàro sense by the de La Vallée Poussin criterion for the Cesàro uniform integrability.
Then by Corollary
3.2
, the sequence
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
is
stochastically dominated in the Cesàro sense by a random variable
X

with distribution function

F(x)=1-\sup_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(|X_{i}|>x):=1-G(x),\ x\in\mathbb{R},

(4.36)

and condition (
1.10
) (with
b_{n}\equiv n^{1/p}
) is satisfied. It thus follows from Theorem

1.3
that the WLLN (
1.11
) holds.

Finally, it is clear that
\mathbb{P}(|X_{n}|^{p}>n)=0
for all
n\geq 1
so
that (
1.3
) holds. We will show that (
1.2
)
(with
0<p<1
) is not satisfied. To see this, for
x\geq 1
, let
n_{x}
be
the smallest integer such that

\dfrac{2^{n_{x}}}{n_{x}}>x^{p}.

Then for
x\geq 1
,

\dfrac{2^{n_{x}}}{n_{x}}>x^{p}\geq\dfrac{2^{n_{x}-1}}{n_{x}-1}

(4.37)

and it follows from (
4.37
) that there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

\dfrac{1}{2^{n_{x}}}>\dfrac{\varepsilon_{0}}{x^{p}\log(x)}\ \text{ for all large }x.

(4.38)

Combining (
4.33
)–(
4.36
) and (
4.38
), we have

G(x)=\sup_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(|X_{i}|>x)\geq\dfrac{1}{2^{n_{x}}}\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n_{x}}}\mathbb{P}(|X_{i}|>x)=\dfrac{1}{2^{n_{x}}}\geq\dfrac{\varepsilon_{0}}{x^{p}\log(x)}\text{ for all large }x

(4.39)

and it follows from (
4.39
) that
(
1.2
)
(with
0<p<1
) fails. Thus we cannot apply Remark 3 of Chandra and Ghosal
chandra1996extensions
to obtain the Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund
SLLN for the case
0<p<1
.

Now, we discuss about the normalizing sequences in the WLLN in Theorem
1.3
. We note that for
0<p<2
and
b_{n}\equiv n^{1/p}
,
(
1.9
) is fulfilled if
0<p<1
but
it fails to hold if
1\leq p<2
. For the case where
1\leq p<2
, we also have to require some dependence structures to obtain the WLLN (see Boukhari
boukhari2021weak

for a counterexample).
Kruglov
kruglov2011generalization
established
a Kolmogorov–Feller-type WLLN for sequences of negatively associated identically distributed
random variables with normalizing sequences
b_{n},n\geq 1
satisfying

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{b_{i}^{2}}{i^{2}}=O\left(\dfrac{b_{n}^{2}}{n}\right).

(4.40)

It was observed by Kruglov
kruglov2011generalization
that (
4.40
) holds for the case
b_{n}\equiv n^{1/p}L(n)
,
where
1\leq p<2
and
L(\cdot)
is a slowly varying function.
The following theorem appears to be new even when the underlying random variables are independent.
It also extends the sufficient part of Theorem 1 of Kruglov
kruglov2011generalization
.
The proof of Theorem
4.9
can be obtained by proceeding in a similar manner as that of Theorem
4.5
:
We firstly use the nondecreasing
truncation as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
, and
use the maximal inequality (
4.1
) instead of
the triangular inequality (in the place of the second inequality in (
4.32
)), and then change the other places accordingly.
We leave the details to the interested reader.

Theorem 4.9

Let
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq n,n\geq 1\}
be an array of random variables
such that for each
n\geq 1
fixed, the collection
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq n\}
satisfies condition
(H)
and let
G(\cdot)

be as in Theorem
4.1
. Let

\{b_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a nondecreasing sequence of positive real numbers satisfying
(
4.40
) and let
\{c_{n,i},1\leq i\leq n,n\geq 1\}
be an array of nonnegative real numbers satisfying

0<A_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{n,i}^{2}\leq Cn,\ n\geq 1.

(4.41)

Let

\hat{G}(x)=\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{n,i}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n,i}|>x),\ x\in\mathbb{R},

where
a_{n,i}=A_{n}^{-1}c_{n,i}^{2},\ 1\leq i\leq n,n\geq 1.

If

\lim_{k\to\infty}kG(b_{k})=0\ \text{ and }\ \lim_{k\to\infty}k\hat{G}(b_{k})=0,

(4.42)

then the WLLN

\dfrac{1}{b_{n}}\max_{j\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}c_{n,i}\left(X_{n,i}-\mathbb{E}(X_{n,i}\mathbf{1}(|X_{n,i}|\leq b_{n}))\right)\right|\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\text{ as }n\to\infty

is obtained.

We now apply Corollary
3.2
and Theorem
4.9
to obtain a WLLN for arrays of random variables
under the Cesàro uniform integrability condition.
For simplicity and since it is just meant to be an illustration, we only consider the unweighted case, i.e., the case where
c_{n,i}\equiv 1
.

Corollary 4.10

Let
1\leq p<2
and let
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq n,n\geq 1\}
be an array of random variables
such that for each
n\geq 1
fixed, the collection
\{X_{n,i},1\leq i\leq n\}
satisfies condition
(H)
. Let
G(\cdot)

be as in Theorem
4.1
and
L(\cdot)
be a slowly varying function. Let
\tilde{L}(\cdot)
be
the Bruijin conjugate of
L(\cdot)
. In the case
p=1
, we further assume that
L(x)
is nondecreasing and
L(x)\geq 1
for all
x\geq 0
.
If
\{|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|^{p}),1\leq i\leq n,n\geq 1\}
is uniformly integrable in the Cesàro sense, that is,

\lim_{a\to\infty}\sup_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|^{p})\mathbf{1}(|X_{n,i}|>a)\right)=0,

(4.43)

then the WLLN

\dfrac{1}{n^{1/p}\tilde{L}^{1/p}(n)}\max_{j\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{n,i}-\mathbb{E}(X_{n,i})\right)\right|\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\text{ as }n\to\infty

(4.44)

is obtained.

Proof

In Theorem
4.9
, if we choose
c_{n,i}\equiv 1
, then (
4.41
) is automatic, and
\hat{G}(x)\equiv G(x)
. By Corollary
3.2
,
it follows from (
4.43
) that (
4.42
) holds with
b_{n}\equiv n^{1/p}\tilde{L}^{1/p}(n)
.
Applying Theorem
4.9
, we obtain

\dfrac{1}{b_{n}}\max_{j\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{n,i}-\mathbb{E}(X_{n,i}\mathbf{1}(|X_{n,i}|\leq b_{n}))\right)\right|\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

To obtain (
4.44
), it remains to show that

\dfrac{1}{b_{n}}\max_{j\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\mathbb{E}(X_{n,i}\mathbf{1}(|X_{n,i}|>b_{n}))\right|\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

(4.45)

Since the function
x^{p-1}L(x)
is nondecreasing, we have

\begin{split}&\dfrac{1}{b_{n}}\max_{j\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\mathbb{E}(X_{n,i}\mathbf{1}(|X_{n,i}|>b_{n}))\right|\leq\dfrac{1}{b_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(|X_{n,i}|\mathbf{1}(|X_{n,i}|>b_{n}))\\
&\leq\dfrac{1}{b_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbb{E}(|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|^{p})\mathbf{1}(|X_{n,i}|>b_{n}))}{b_{n}^{p-1}L(b_{n}^{p})}\\
&=\left(\dfrac{1}{\tilde{L}(n)L\left(n\tilde{L}(n)\right)}\right)\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|^{p})\mathbf{1}(|X_{n,i}|>b_{n})).\end{split}

(4.46)

Now, from the second half of (
2.5
) we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{L}(n)L\left(n\tilde{L}(n)\right)=1
, and from (
4.43
) we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(|X_{n,i}|^{p}L(|X_{n,i}|^{p})\mathbf{1}(|X_{n,i}|>b_{n}))=0.

Therefore (
4.45
) follows from (
4.46
). The proof of the corollary is completed. ∎

5
Conclusions and open problems

In Section
4
, our results on the concept of
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastic domination
are applied to obtain the WLLNs for weighted sums. The results on the Cesàro stochastic domination case
are applied to obtain rate of convergence in the SLLN with general normalizing sequences under the Chandra–Ghosal-type condition,
and these results help us to remove an assumption of a SLLN established by Chandra and Ghosal
chandra1996extensions
.
The results on the concept of
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastic domination may also be useful in proving weighted SLLNs of Chandra and Ghosal
chandra1996strong

as we will describe as follows.

Let
1\leq p<2
and
let
\{a_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of positive real numbers with

A_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\to\infty\ \text{ as }n\to\infty.

Let
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of mean zero random variables which satisfies suitable dependence conditions.
Let
G(x)
be as in Theorem
1.1

and let

\tilde{G}(x)=\sup_{n\geq 1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{1/p}\right)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{1/p}\mathbb{P}(|X_{i}|>x),x\in\mathbb{R}.

Chandra and Ghosal
chandra1996strong
considered the following three conditions (see (2.14)–(2.16) in
chandra1996strong
):

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{p-1}G(x)\operatorname{d}x<\infty,

(5.1)

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{p-1}\tilde{G}(x)\operatorname{d}x<\infty,

(5.2)

and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n}|^{p}>A_{n}/a_{n})<\infty.

(5.3)

Chandra and Ghosal (see Theorems 2.6 and 2.7 in
chandra1996strong
) proved that if
(
5.1
), (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) are all satisfied, then the weighted Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund SLLN

\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{1/p}X_{i}}{A_{n}^{1/p}}\to 0\text{ a.s. as }n\to\infty

is obtained. In view of Theorems
1.2
and
4.5
, we state an open problem as to whether or not
the Chandra and Ghosal result mentioned above still holds without
Condition (
5.3
).

For
n\geq 1
, let

a_{n,i}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{1/p}\right)^{-1}a_{i}^{1/p},\text{ }1\leq i\leq n.

Since
\tilde{G}(x)
is nonincreasing, it follows from (
5.2
) that
\lim_{x\to\infty}\tilde{G}(x)=0
.
By Theorem
2.1
,
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
is
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastically dominated by
a random variable
X
with distribution function
F(x)=1-\tilde{G}(x)
, and (
5.2
) becomes

\mathbb{E}(|X|^{p})<\infty
. In view of the proof of Theorem
4.5
, the results on the concept of
\{a_{n,i}\}
-stochastic domination established in Sections
2

and
3

may help in answering the above open problem.

Finally, we present an open problem concerning Corollary
4.10
.
For the case where
L(x)\equiv\tilde{L}(x)\equiv 1
, we can obtain convergence in mean of order
p
in (
4.44
) (see, e.g.,
Theorem 1 in
chandra1989uniform
,
Theorem 4 in
ordonezcabrera1994convergence
, Theorem 2.1 in
thanh2005lp
).
However, the methods in
chandra1989uniform
;
ordonezcabrera1994convergence
;
thanh2005lp

do not seem to work for general slowly varying function
L(\cdot)
, even with assumption that the underlying random variables are independent.
It is an open problem as to whether or not convergence in mean of order
p
prevails in
the conclusion (
4.44
).

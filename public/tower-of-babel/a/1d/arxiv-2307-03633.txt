The MorseResolutions package for Macaulay2
Trung Chau, Selvi Kara, Augustine O’Keefe

Abstract
. Using discrete Morse theory, Batzies and Welker introduced Morse resolutions of monomial ideals. In this note, we present the Macaulay2 package MorseResolutions for working with two important classes of Morse resolutions: Lyubeznik and Barile-Macchia resolutions. This package also contains procedures to search for a minimal Barile-Macchia resolution of a given monomial ideal.



1.
Introduction

A well-known approach for resolving a monomial ideal over a polynomial ring is to find its Taylor resolution
[
15
]
which are usually non-minimal. Several attempts have been made to find refinements of Taylor resolutions that are closer to the minimal resolutions. Among the most prominent refinements are Lyubeznik resolutions
[
13
]
and Scarf complexes
[
3
]
. Another type of refinement are
Morse resolutions
which were introduced by Batzies and Welker in
[
2
]
. These resolutions are induced from Taylor resolutions by utilizing
homogeneous acyclic matchings
, a tool from discrete Morse theory.

In general, there are several homogeneous acyclic matchings for a given monomial ideal, and each such matching may produce a different Morse resolution. For instance, the homogeneous acyclic matchings described in
[
2
, Theorem 3.2]
yield a class of Morse resolutions which coincide with Lyubeznik resolutions. In this note, we refer to these matchings as
Lyubeznik matchings
. Another class of Morse resolutions called
Barile-Macchia resolutions
are induced by a different class of homogeneous acyclic matchings termed
Barile-Macchia matchings
, as described in
[
5
, Algorithm 2.9]
. While Barile-Macchia resolutions may not always be minimal, they tend to be closer to the minimal resolution compared to the Taylor and Lyubeznik resolutions (in specific cases). Notably, they are minimal for many important classes of monomial ideals, as demonstrated in
[
5
]
.

Morse resolutions serve as powerful tools for describing (minimal) free resolutions of monomial ideals. To facilitate further exploration in this field, we have developed the
MorseResolutions
package for
Macaulay2
, which enables researchers to experiment with Barile-Macchia and Lyubeznik resolutions. Specifically, our package allows users to generate Barile-Macchia matchings and Lyubeznik matchings for a given ideal, considering a specific total order on its minimal generating set. The package can be accessed at:
https://github.com/selvikara/morseResolutions
.

In this note, we provide a review of the necessary mathematical background and summarize the key features of our package, accompanied by illustrative examples.

Mathematical background

In this section, we summarize the relevant background for this note and package. We refer interested readers to

]
for more details on the theory.

Let
R=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]
be the polynomial ring in
N
variables over a field
\Bbbk
. For the remainder of this section, let
I\subset R
be a monomial ideal with a minimal monomial generating set
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\}
. Impose a total order
(>)
on
\mathcal{G}(I)
and treat a subset
\sigma
of
\mathcal{G}(I)
as an ordered set with respect to
(>)
.

Throughout the note, let
[k]
denote the set
\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
for any integer
k
.

2.1.
Taylor resolutions

Consider the full simplex on
n
vertices such that the vertices are labeled elements of
\mathcal{G}(I)
and each face is labeled by the lcm of its vertex labels. This labeled simplex, denoted by
\Delta_{I}
, is called the
Taylor complex
of
R/I
. Let
\lcm(\sigma)
denote the lcm of the elements in
\sigma\subseteq\mathcal{G}(I)
.

Recall that
\Delta_{I}
induces a
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
-graded complex
\mathcal{F}
where
\mathcal{F}_{r}
is the free
R
-module with a basis indexed by all subsets of cardinality
r
of
\mathcal{G}(I)
and the differentials
\partial_{r}\colon\mathcal{F}_{r}\to\mathcal{F}_{r-1}
are defined by

\partial_{r}(\sigma)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma,\\
|\sigma^{\prime}|=r-1\end{subarray}}[\sigma:\sigma^{\prime}]\frac{\lcm(\sigma)}{\lcm(\sigma^{\prime})}\sigma^{\prime}.

The complex
\mathcal{F}
is called the
Taylor resolution
of
R/I
.

2.2.
Discrete Morse theory and Morse resolutions

Discrete Morse theory was developed by Forman in
[
9
]
as a combinatorial counterpart of Morse theory for manifolds, and was reformulated in terms of homogeneous acyclic matchings by Chari in
[
4
]
. In
[
2
]
, Batzies and Welker used Chari’s reformulation to obtain a “trimmed” cellullar resolution from a given cellular resolution induced by a regular CW-complex (for instance, the Taylor resolution) of a monomial ideal. The key idea of their method is to encode faces of the Taylor complex in a graph and then reduce this graph to obtain a smaller object that is homotopy equivalent to
\Delta_{I}
by using homogeneous acyclic matchings.

Consider the directed graph
G_{I}
on the set of cells of the Taylor complex
\Delta_{I}
. The vertices of
G_{I}
are the cells of
\Delta_{I}
, namely subsets of
\mathcal{G}(I)
, and the directed edges of
G_{I}
are given by

E_{I}=\{(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}):\sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma,|\sigma^{\prime}|=|\sigma|-1\}.

For any
A\subseteq E_{I}
, let
G_{I}^{A}
be the graph obtained by reversing the direction of the directed edges in
A
, i.e., the directed graph with the edges

E(G_{I}^{A})=(E_{I}\setminus A)\cup\{(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma)\mid(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\in A\}.

We are now ready to define homogeneous acyclic matchings and critical cells.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A\subseteq E_{I}
be a matching on
G_{I}
, i.e., no two edges in
A
share a vertex. Then:

•

The matching
A
is called
acyclic
if the associated graph
G_{I}^{A}
is acyclic, i.e., it does not contain any directed cycles.

•

The matching
A
is called
homogeneous
if
\lcm(\sigma)=\lcm(\sigma^{\prime})
for any directed edge
(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\in A
.

If
A
is a homogeneous acyclic matching on
G_{I}
, a cell
\sigma\in\mathcal{G}(I)
is called
A
-
critical
if
\sigma
does not appear in any edge of
A
.

In
[
2
]
, Batzies and Welker showed that a homogeneous acyclic matching
A
of
G_{I}
produces a CW-complex which supports a free resolution of
R/I
. See
[
2
]
and
[
5
]
for unexplained terminology and notations.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
2
]

If
A
is a homogeneous acyclic matching on
G_{I}
, then there exists a CW-complex which supports a free resolution of
R/I
. The
i
-cells of this CW-complex are in one-to-one correspondence with the
A
-critical cells of cardinality
i+1
of
\Delta_{I}
. We denote the cellular resolution supported on this CW-complex by
\mathcal{F}_{A}
where
(\mathcal{F}_{A})_{i}
is the free
R
-module with a basis indexed by all critical cells of cardinality
i
and the differentials are the maps
\partial_{i}^{A}:(\mathcal{F}_{A})_{i}\to(\mathcal{F}_{A})_{i-1}
defined by

\partial_{i}^{A}(\sigma)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma,\\
|\sigma^{\prime}|=i-1\end{subarray}}[\sigma:\sigma^{\prime}]\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{\prime\prime}\text{ critical,}\\
|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|=i-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{P}\text{ gradient path}\\
\text{from }\sigma^{\prime}\text{ to }\sigma^{\prime\prime}\end{subarray}}m(\mathcal{P})\frac{\lcm(\sigma)}{\lcm(\sigma^{\prime\prime})}\sigma^{\prime\prime}.

The resulting (cellular) free resolution
\mathcal{F}_{A}
is called the
Morse resolution
of
R/I
associated to
A
.

The resolution
\mathcal{F}_{A}
is minimal if for any two
A
-critical cells
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime\prime}
of
\Delta_{I}
with
|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|=|\sigma|-1
such that there exists a gradient path from
\sigma^{\prime}
to
\sigma^{\prime\prime}
in
G_{I}^{A}
for some
\sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma
with
|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|=|\sigma^{\prime}|
, we have
\lcm(\sigma)\neq\lcm(\sigma^{\prime\prime})
.

2.3.
Lyubeznik resolution

In
[
2
]
, Batzies and Welker showed that Lyubeznik’s resolution from
[
13
]
is an example of a Morse resolution and they provided a recipe for the corresponding homogeneous acyclic matching. We recall the construction below. In the
MorseResolutions
package, we provide a function that produces this Lyubeznik matching of
R/I
with respect to a given total order.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
2
, Theorem 3.2]

For any subset
\sigma=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{q}\}
of
\mathcal{G}(I)
where
m_{1}>\cdots>m_{q}
, we define

v_{L}(\sigma)\coloneqq\sup\big\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\exists m\in\mathcal{G}(I)\text{ such that }m_{k}>m\text{ for some }k\in[q]\text{ and }m\mid\lcm(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})\big\}.

Set
v_{L}(\sigma)=-\infty
if no such
m
exists. If
v_{L}(\sigma)\neq-\infty
, define

m_{L}(\sigma)\coloneqq\min_{>}\{m\in\mathcal{G}(I):m\mid\lcm(m_{1},\dots,m_{v_{L}(\sigma)})\}.

For each
p\in R
, set

A_{p}\coloneqq\{(\sigma\cup m_{L}(\sigma),\sigma\setminus m_{L}(\sigma)):\lcm(\sigma)=p\text{ and }v_{L}(\sigma)\neq-\infty\}.

Then
\displaystyle A=\cup_{p\in R}A_{p}
is a homogeneous acyclic matching. The graded free resolution
\mathcal{F}_{A}
induced by
A
is the
Lyubeznik resolution
of
R/I
with respect to
(>)
.

We call the final matching
A
in this theorem the
Lyubeznik matching
of
R/I
with respect to
(>)
. Recall the following example from
[
5
, Example 5.6]
to see how to find the
v_{L}(\sigma)
and
m_{L}(\sigma)
for a given
\sigma
of
\mathcal{G}(I)
:

Example 2.4
.

Consider the monomial ideal
I=(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},m_{4},m_{5},m_{6})\subseteq\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{8}]
where

m_{1}=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4},~~m_{2}=x_{2}x_{3}x_{5}x_{6},~~m_{3}=x_{1}x_{2}x_{5},~~m_{4}=x_{1}x_{2}x_{7},~~m_{5}=x_{2}x_{3}x_{8},~~m_{6}=x_{7}x_{8}

with the total order
m_{1}>m_{2}>m_{3}>m_{4}>m_{5}>m_{6}
on
\mathcal{G}(I)
.

•

For
\sigma_{1}=(m_{1},m_{4},m_{5})
, we have
v_{L}(\sigma_{1})=3
and
m_{L}(\sigma_{1})=m_{6}
since
m_{6}|\lcm(\sigma_{1})
and
\lcm(m_{1},m_{4})
is not divisible by
m_{5}
or
m_{6}
.

•

For
\sigma_{2}=(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})
, we have
v_{L}(\sigma_{2})=2
and
m_{L}(\sigma_{2})=m_{3}
since
m_{3}|\lcm(m_{1},m_{2})
.

•

For
\sigma_{3}=(m_{2},m_{3},m_{4})
, we have
v_{L}(\sigma_{3})=-\infty
since
\lcm(m_{2},m_{3})
is not divisible by
m_{4},m_{5}
or
m_{6}
and
\lcm(\sigma_{3})
is not divisible by
m_{5}
or
m_{6}
.

Let
A
be the Lyubeznik matching of
R/I
with respect to this total order. Then,
(\sigma_{1}\cup\{m_{6}\},\sigma_{1})
and
(\sigma_{2},\sigma_{2}\setminus\{m_{3}\})
are directed edges in
A
while
A
does not have any edge involving
\sigma_{3}
.

2.4.
Barile-Macchia resolution

The first two authors developed an algorithm to produce a different homogeneous acyclic matching called the
Barile-Macchia matching
of
R/I
in
[
5
]
. Morse resolutions induced by Barile-Macchia matchings are called
Barile-Macchia resolutions
. In the same work, they identified several classes of ideals whose Barile-Macchia resolutions are minimal. In
MorseResolutions
package, we provide a function that produces the Barile-Macchia matching of
R/I
with respect to a given total order.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\sigma
be a subset of
\mathcal{G}(I)
.

•

A monomial
m\in\mathcal{G}(I)
is called a
bridge
of
\sigma
if
m\in\sigma
and
\lcm(\sigma\setminus\{m\})=\lcm(\sigma)
.

•

The
smallest bridge
of
\sigma\subseteq\mathcal{G}(I)
with respect to
(>)
, denoted by
\sbridge_{>}(\sigma)
, is the smallest bridge of
\sigma
in this total order. If
\sigma
has no bridges, then
\sbridge_{>}(\sigma)=\emptyset
.

For simplicity, denote
S\setminus\{s\}
and
S\cup\{s\}
for a set
S
and
s\in S
by
S\setminus s
and
S\cup s
, respectively. Below is the Barile-Macchia algorithm from
[
5
, Algorithm 2.9]
.

Algorithm 2.6

(Barile-Macchia Algorithm)
.

Let
A=\emptyset
. Set

\Omega=\{\text{all subsets of }\mathcal{G}(I)\text{ with cardinality at least }3\}.

(1)

Pick a subset
\sigma
of maximal cardinality in
\Omega
.

(2)

Set

\Omega\coloneqq\Omega\setminus\{\sigma,\sigma\setminus\sbridge(\sigma)\}.

If
\sbridge(\sigma)\neq\emptyset
, add the directed edge
(\sigma,\sigma\setminus\sbridge(\sigma))
to
A
. If
\Omega\neq\emptyset
, return to step (1).

(3)

Whenever there exist distinct directed edges
(\sigma,\sigma\setminus\sbridge(\sigma))
and
(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}\setminus\sbridge(\sigma^{\prime}))
in
A
such that

\sigma\setminus\sbridge(\sigma)=\sigma^{\prime}\setminus\sbridge(\sigma^{\prime}),

then

•

if
\sbridge(\sigma^{\prime})>\sbridge(\sigma)
, remove
(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}\setminus\sbridge(\sigma^{\prime}))
from
A
,

•

otherwise, remove
(\sigma,\sigma\setminus\sbridge(\sigma))
from
A
.

Definition 2.7
.

The set
A
produced by the Barile-Macchia algorithm is called the
Barile-Macchia matching
of
R/I
with respect to
(>)
. The set of edges that appear in
A
before Step (3) of the algorithm is called the
possible edges
of
R/I
.

Barile-Macchia matchings are indeed homogeneous acyclic matchings as shown in
[
5
, Theorem 2.11]
. Recall the following example from
[
5
, Example 2.13]
.

Example 2.8
.

Consider the ideal
I=(wz,wx,xy,yz)\subseteq R=\Bbbk[w,x,y,z]
with the total order
wz>wx>xy>yz
on
\mathcal{G}(I)
. The Barile-Macchia matching of
R/I
with respect to this total order is:

A=\{(\{wz,wx,xy,yz\},\{wz,wx,xy\}),(\{wz,xy,yz\},\{wz,xy\}),(\{wx,xy,yz\},\{wx,yz\})\}.

The possible edges of
A
are

A\cup\{(\{wz,wx,yz\},\{wx,yz\})\}

because the edge
(\{wz,wx,yz\},\{wx,yz\})
is removed from
A
in Step (3) of the algorithm. We will display the same output by using our functions from the
MorseResolutions
package.

A final concept that is needed in this section is the notion of bridge-friendliness from
[
5
, Definition 2.27]
. As it is shown in
[
5
, Theorem 2.29]
, if an ideal is bridge-friendly, then its corresponding Barile-Macchia resolution is minimal. So, this property allows us check the minimality of Barile-Macchia resolutions. In this package, we provide a function to identify whether a given ideal is bridge-friendly while detecting all total orders where this property holds.

Definition 2.9
.

A monomial ideal
I
is called
bridge-friendly
with respect to a total order
(>)
on
\mathcal{G}(I)
if its Barile-Macchia matching with respect to
(>)
is the same as its set of possible edges.

The ideal in Example
2.8
is not bridge-friendly with respect to the given order. More generally, we will show that this ideal is not bridge-friendly with respect to any total order by using our package in the next section.

3.
The package and examples

The
Macaulay2
package
MorseResolutions
was
created as a tool to experiment with Barile-Macchia resolutions from
[
5
]
, Lyubeznik resolutions from
[
2
]
, and the notion of bridge-friendliness from
[
5
]
. In this section, we highlight some of the key features of this package.

Our package has two main parts where the first part focuses on functions related to Barile-Macchia resolutions and the second part involves analogous functions for Lyubeznik resolutions. This package also contains an application of these ideas, trimmed Lyubeznik resolutions, in Subsection
4.2
.

3.1.
Barile-Macchia matchings and Barile-Macchia resolutions

One of the main functions of the package is the
bMMatching

(>)
based on Algorithm
2.6
. As seen from Theorem
2.2

In order to find a Barile-Macchia (or Lyubeznik) matching of

4.1.

Using our package
MorseResolutions

\Bbbk

[
11

, we can check the bridge-minimality of these ideals by comparing their Betti numbers with the ranks of its Barile-Macchia resolutions. The following code is used to check the bridge-minimality of edge ideal of a 9-cycle, which can be modified to check for other cycles:

R=QQ[x1,x2,x3,x4,x5,x6,x7,x8,x9]
I=ideal(x1*x2,x2*x3,x3*x4,x4*x5,x5*x6,x6*x7,x7*x8,x8*x9,x1*x9)
orderings = permutations flatten entries gens I;

);

);
ij = ij+1;
);

4.2.
Trimming Lyubeznik resolutions

In
[
5
]
, the Barile-Macchia matching algorithm is applied to the Taylor complex of a given monomial ideal with respect to a fixed total order. In this set-up, the Taylor complex can be replaced with any regular CW-complex (see
[
2
]
). Since Lyubeznik resolutions are simplicial by
[
14
]
and any simplicial complex is a regular CW-complex, one can apply the Barile-Macchia algorithm to the simplicial complex that supports a Lyubeznik resolution of a given monomial ideal.

In
MorseResolutions
package, we introduced the
trimmedMatching
function, a modified version of the
bMMatching
function. Instead of Taylor cells, this new function considers the critical Lyubeznik cells of a monomial ideal with respect to a total order
S_{1}
and applies the Barile-Macchia algorithm to these cells with respect to
S_{2}
. Consider our running example and let
S_{1}=S_{2}=S=(yz,xy,wx,wz)
. The output of this function is the collection of edges in
G_{I}
with “unmatched” vertices in the critical Lyubeznik cells.

i15 : trimmedMatching(S,S)
o15 = {({y*z, x*y, w*x}, {y*z, w*x})}

In addition, the package contains
criticalTrimmedCells
and
trimmedRanks
functions to print out the critical cells of the trimmed Lyubeznik complex and the ranks of the trimmed Lyubeznik resolution. For our running example, the trimmed Lyubeznik resolution of
R/I
is minimal.

i16 : criticalTrimmedCells(S,S)
o16 = {{}, {{y*z, w*x, w*z}}, {{y*z, x*y}, {x*y, w*x}, {y*z, w*z}, {w*x, w*z}}
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
{{y*z}, {x*y}, {w*x}, {w*z}}}

i17 : trimmedRanks(S,S)
o17 = {1, 4, 4, 1, 0}

In fact, for any total orders
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, the corresponding trimmed Lyubeznik resolution of
R/I
is minimal.

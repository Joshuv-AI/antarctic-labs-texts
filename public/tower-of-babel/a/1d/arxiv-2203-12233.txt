On the Spectrum of the Periodic Anderson-Bernoulli Model Preprint: AIP/123-QED
William Wood

Abstract
We analyze the spectrum of a discrete Schrödinger operator with a potential given by a periodic variant of the Anderson Model. In order to do so, we study the uniform hyperbolicity of a Schrödinger cocycle generated by the S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) SL(2,\mathbb{R}) transfer matrices. In the specific case of the potential generated by an alternating sequence of random values we show that the almost sure spectrum consists of at most 4 intervals.

Introduction

Discrete Schrödinger operators
H_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}V}}:l^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow l^{2}(\mathbb{Z})

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sigma(H_{V})=\{\lambda:H_{V}-\lambda\text{ does not have a bounded inverse}\}.}

The spectral type (absolutely continuous, singular continuous, pure point, or mixed) and the topological structure of the spectrum of these operators are objects of study, see

There are plenty of more complicated examples, such as the Almost Mathieu Operator. If

;

The last example is the Anderson Model, where the potential depends on random variables. If

This paper will address a model inspired by the Anderson Model. If the sequence

II.1
Uniform Hyperbolicity

First, we will need to define what it means that a cocycle or a set of matrices is uniformly hyperbolic

;
y
;

Definition 1
.

A set of matrices
\{A_{n}\}\subset SL(2,\mathbb{R})
is
uniformly hyperbolic
if there exists the constant
\lambda>1
such that, for any product
\prod A_{i}
of
m
matrices, the inequality
||\prod A_{i}||>\lambda^{m}
holds.

In this product, duplicates are allowed. Due to Avila, Bochi, and Yoccoz

, we have the following Theorem.

Theorem 2
.

A finite set of
2\times 2
matrices
\{A_{i}\}
is uniformly hyperbolic if and only if there is a nontrivial open set
C\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{1}
(hereafter referred to as a cone) such that
\overline{C}\subsetneq\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a finite number of intervals and
A_{i}(\overline{C})\subset C
for each matrix
A_{i}
from the set.

For this paper, the projective space,
\mathbb{P},
will be parameterized by
\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\end{bmatrix}\leftrightarrow\frac{x}{y}
. The simplest example of a uniformly hyperbolic set of matrices is when
C
is a principal cone.

Definition 2
.

If the cone
C
consists of a single interval, it is called a
principal cone
.

If the set
\{A_{i}\}
is a set of
2\times 2
matrices, a principal cone forms if and only if all the unstable eigenvectors (here the unstable eigenvector of
A_{i}
will be expressed as
u_{i}
) lie in a single interval in the projective space that contains none of the stable eigenvectors (here the stable eigenvector of
A_{i}
will be expressed as
s_{i}
). The cone,
C
, can be defined as that interval.

Before covering the second definition of uniform hyperbolicity, we need to define a cocyle.

Definition 3
.

Given a metric space
\Omega
, homeomorphism
T:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega
and continuous
B:\Omega\rightarrow SL(2,\mathbb{R})
, a

is the map

(T,B):(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})

(\omega,\vec{v})\mapsto(T(\omega),B(\omega)\vec{v}).

Definition 4
.

Denoting
(T,B)^{n}=(T^{n},B_{n})

B_{n}(\omega)=B(T^{n-1}(\omega))\cdot B(T^{n-2}(\omega))\cdots B(T(\omega))B(\omega).

A cocycle

||B_{n}(\omega)||>C\lambda^{n}

More general definitions of hyperbolicity are available

Johnson’s Theorem and Transfer Matrices

Given a dynamical system
(\Omega,T,\mu)
with compact metric space
\Omega

\mu

(\omega,\vec{v})\mapsto(T\omega,g\circ f(\omega)\cdot\vec{v})

H

\begin{bmatrix}\phi(n+1)\\
\phi(n)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}E-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}V_{\omega}}(n)&-1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}\phi(n)\\
\phi(n-1)\end{bmatrix}

We arrive at the transfer matrix

1&0\end{bmatrix}

where

and

Consider an ergodic family of Schrödinger operators with random potentials expressible as a possible outcome of the distribution

\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}A^{2}_{E,(0,x)}:(0,x)\in\Omega}\}

(\leftarrow)

infinitely often. It would seem possible that for a large enough period, the spectrum may consist of an infinite number of intervals.

III
Key Lemma

Theorem 1 states that if a family of ergodic Schrödinger operators has a potential given by
(2)
, then the almost sure spectrum consists of a finite number of intervals. Applying lemma 1 with
m=2
, we simply need to find a way to calculate the set
\{E:\{A_{E,(0,x)}^{2}\}\text{ is uniformly hyperbolic}\}
. We can calculate the set using the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

The set of matrices

\bigg\{AC,AD,BC,BD:\begin{array}[]{c}A,B,C,D\text{ are transfer matrices and}\\
A_{11}\geq B_{11}\text{ and }C_{11}\geq D_{11}\end{array}\bigg\}

is uniformly hyperbolic if and only if the matrices
AC,AD,BC,\;\&\;BD
are individually hyperbolic.

If the set is uniformly hyperbolic, all the matrices are hyperbolic. In the regions where all the matrices are all hyperbolic, they turn out to form a principal cone. We will identify the principal cones in the projective space in section
4
, proving lemma
2
.

After that, over these intervals the corresponding set of matrices will be shown to have a principal cone, proving the set is uniformly hyperbolic.

Lemma 5
.

Given distinct transfer matrices

AB
and
AC
are hyperbolic, then they cannot share an eigenvector. Similarly,
BA

cannot share an eigenvector if they are hyperbolic.

Proof.

•

To prove the first statement, given matrices
AB
and
AC
, assume they share an eigenvector
\vec{v}
. Then

AB\vec{v}=\xi_{1}AC\vec{v}=\xi_{2}\vec{v}

for constants
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
. Therefore

B\vec{v}=\xi_{1}C\vec{v}\Rightarrow(B-\xi_{1}C)\vec{v}=0

det(B-\xi C)=(1-\xi_{1})^{2}
. For this to be zero,
\xi_{1}=1.
This makes
(B-C)\vec{v}=\begin{bmatrix}B_{11}-C_{11}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\cdot\vec{v}=0
in which case
\vec{v}\propto\begin{bmatrix}0\\
1\end{bmatrix}
. For this to be an eigenvalue of
AB,
we get the equation

AB\cdot\begin{bmatrix}0\\
1\end{bmatrix}=A\cdot\begin{bmatrix}-1\\
0\end{bmatrix}=\xi_{2}\begin{bmatrix}0\\
1\end{bmatrix},

which gives the equations
A=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}
and
\xi_{2}=-1
. This makes
AB
not hyperbolic.

•

To prove the second statement, assume matrices
BA
and
CA
which share an eigenvector
\vec{v}
.

BA\vec{v}=\xi_{1}CA\vec{v}=\xi_{2}\vec{v}

Therefore,

B(A\vec{v})=\xi_{1}C(A\vec{v})\Rightarrow(B-\xi_{1}C)(A\vec{v})=0.

This gives the equation
A\vec{v}\propto\begin{bmatrix}0\\
1\end{bmatrix}
, which implies the equation

1\end{bmatrix}\propto\begin{bmatrix}-1\\
0\end{bmatrix}

1\end{bmatrix},
then the equations

are true, making the product parabolic and proves the lemma.

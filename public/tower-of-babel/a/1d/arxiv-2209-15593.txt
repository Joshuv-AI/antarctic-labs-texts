A simple analytical expression of quantum Fisher and Skew information and their dynamics under decoherence channels
N. Abouelkhir

Abstract




In statistical estimation theory, it has been shown previously that the Wigner-Yanase skew information is bounded by the quantum Fisher information associated with the phase parameter. Besides, the quantum Cramér-Rao inequality is expressed in terms of skew information. Since these two fundamental quantities are based on the concept of quantum uncertainty, we derive here their analytical formulas for arbitrary two qubit
X
-states using the same analytical procedures. A comparison of these two informational quantifiers for two quasi-Werner states composed of two bipartite superposed coherent states is examined. Moreover, we investigated the decoherence effects on such quantities generated by the phase damping, depolarization and amplitude damping channels. We showed that decoherence strongly influences the quantum criteria during the evolution and these quantities exhibit similar dynamic behaviors. This current work is characterized by the fact that these two concepts play the same role and capture similar properties in quantum estimation protocols.

Keywords
: Wigner-Yanase Skew Information, Quantum Fisher Information, Concurrence, Phase-Damping Channel, Depolarizing Channel and Amplitude-Damping Channel.

2.2
Explicit form of quantum Fisher information

Let us consider a quantum state
\rho_{\theta}
that depends on an unknown parameter
\theta
. If we intend to extract information about
\theta
from the density matrix
\rho_{\theta}
, we need to perform a set of quantum measurements
\left\{\Pi_{x}\right\}
. In classical metrology
[
4
,
19
]
, the Fisher information provides the lower limit of the variance of an unbiased estimator for the estimated parameter. It is defined by

F_{c}\left(\theta\right)=\int p\left(x/\theta\right)\left[\frac{\partial\ln p\left(x/\theta\right)}{\partial\theta}\right]^{2}dx,

(8)

where
p\left(x/\theta\right)={\rm Tr}\left[\Pi_{x}\rho_{\theta}\right]
represents the conditional probability of obtaining the measurement outcome
x
when the true value of the parameter is
\theta
. According to classical statistical theory, the ultimate accuracy
{\rm Var}\left(\theta\right)
in estimating
\theta
from
n
measurements is restricted by the Fisher information
F_{c}\left(\theta\right)
via the Cramér-Rao inequality as

{\rm Var}\left(\theta\right)\geq\frac{1}{nF_{c}\left(\theta\right)}.

(9)

The quantum Fisher information is defined as the optimization of the classical Fisher information (Eq.(
8
)) over all possible measurements
\left\{\Pi_{x}\right\}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{\theta}\right)=\max_{\Pi_{x}}F_{c}\left(\theta\right).

(10)

Then we can rewrite the above equation explicitly as

\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{\theta}\right)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\theta}^{2})=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left(\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}\right)\mathcal{L}_{\theta}\right],

(11)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\theta}
is so-called symmetric logarithmic
derivative operator and is determined explicitly by the relation

\frac{\partial\rho_{\theta}}{\partial\theta}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\rho_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\theta}+\mathcal{L}_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}\right].

(12)

Indeed, the quantum Fisher information represents a central role in quantum metrology. It can be used to extract information about some parameters that cannot be measured directly
[
19
]
. Specifically, this quantity is proposed to measure the phase sensitivity of quantum systems.
[
57
,
58
,
60
]
. It is necessary to mention that this quantity can be derived directly when the symmetric logarithmic derivative operator is explicitly calculated. Here, we restrict our description to the explicit expression of the quantum Fisher information for two-qubit
X
states (of the form (
1
)). The solution of the equation (
12
) leads to the analytical formula of the symmetric logarithmic derivative operator in terms of exponentiation of the density matrix as

\mathcal{L}_{\theta}=2\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\rho_{\theta}s}(\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta})e^{-\rho_{\theta}s}ds.

(13)

Using the decomposition
\rho_{\theta}^{n}=\rho_{\theta}^{(1)n}\oplus{\rho_{\theta}^{(2)n}}
(with
n\geq 1
) and
e^{-\rho_{\theta}s}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(-s\right)^{n}}{n!}\rho_{\theta}^{n}=e^{-\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}s}+e^{-\rho_{\theta}^{(2)}s}
, the expression of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\theta}
can then be written as

\mathcal{L}_{\theta}=L_{\theta}^{(1)}+L_{\theta}^{(2)},

(14)

where

L_{\theta}^{(1)}=p^{0}\vartheta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}p^{i}\vartheta_{i},\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274ptL_{\theta}^{(2)}=\tilde{p}^{0}\tilde{\vartheta}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\tilde{p}^{i}\tilde{\vartheta}_{i}.

(15)

In this respect, the quantum Fisher information can be decomposed as
\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{\theta}\right)=\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}\right)+\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(2)}\right)
, where
\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}\right)
(or
\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(2)}\right)
) denotes the quantum Fisher information for
\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}
(or
\rho_{\theta}^{(2)}
) which is expressed as

\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}\right)=\operatorname{Tr}\left((\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}^{(1)})L_{\theta}^{(1)}\right),\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(2)}\right)=\operatorname{Tr}\left((\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}^{(2)})L_{\theta}^{(2)}\right).

(16)

Based on the expression of the symmetric logarithmic derivative operator (
15
) for the state
\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}
and Eq.(
5
), one check that

\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}\right)=p_{0}\left(\partial_{\theta}\chi_{0}\right)+\sum_{i=0}^{3}\left(p_{i}\partial_{\theta}\chi_{i}\right),

(17)

with
p_{0}
and
p_{i}
are completely determined by the following relation

\frac{\partial\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}}{\partial\theta}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}L_{\theta}^{(1)}+L_{\theta}^{(1)}\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}\right),

(18)

By substituting the expression of the density matrix
\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}
(eq.
5
) in the expression of the operator
L_{\theta}^{(1)}
(eq.
15
), we can easily show that

\partial_{\theta}\chi_{0}=\chi_{0}p_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\chi_{i}p_{i},\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\partial_{\theta}\chi_{i}=p_{0}\chi_{i}+\chi_{0}p_{i},

(19)

with
i=0,1,2,3
. By solving the above equations, one gets

p_{0}=\frac{\chi_{0}\partial_{\theta}\chi_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\chi_{i}\partial_{\theta}\chi_{i}}{\chi_{0}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\chi_{i}^{2}},\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274ptp_{i}=\frac{1}{\chi_{0}}\left[\frac{g_{\alpha\beta}\chi^{\alpha}\left(\chi^{\beta}\partial_{\theta}\chi^{i}-\chi^{i}\partial_{\theta}\chi^{\beta}\right)}{g_{\alpha\beta}\chi^{\alpha}\chi^{\beta}}\right],

(20)

where
g_{\alpha\beta}={\rm diag}\left(1,-1,-1,-1\right)
denotes the metric of the Minkowski space-time. Substituting the equations (
20
) and (
19
) into Eq.(
17
),
\mathcal{F}(\rho_{\theta}^{(1)})
can be expressed as

\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}\right)=\frac{\left(\partial_{\theta}\chi_{0}\right)^{2}}{\chi_{0}}+\frac{1}{\chi_{0}}\left[\frac{\left(g_{\alpha\beta}\chi^{\alpha}\partial_{\theta}\chi^{\beta}\right)^{2}}{g_{\alpha\beta}\chi^{\alpha}\chi^{\beta}}-g_{\alpha\beta}\left(\partial_{\theta}\chi^{\alpha}\right)\left(\partial_{\theta}\chi^{\beta}\right)\right].

(21)

The above equation (
21
) is applicable for mixed states (with
\chi_{0}\neq 0
and
g_{\alpha\beta}\chi^{\alpha}\chi^{\beta}\neq 0
). For pure states, we have
\rho_{\theta}^{(1)^{2}}=\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}
and the symmetric logarithmic derivative operator can be directly calculated as

L_{\theta}^{(1)}=2\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}.

(22)

Especially, by substituting Eqs (
22
) and (
5
) into Eq.(
17
), the quantum Fisher information
\mathcal{F}(\rho_{\theta}^{(1)})
(
23
) reduces to

\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}\right)=\left(\chi_{0}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\chi_{i}\right)^{2}.

(23)

Consequently, the quantum Fisher information
\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(2)}\right)
associated with the matrix
\rho_{\theta}^{(2)}
(
3
) can be determined similarly. It writes as

\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(2)}\right)=\frac{\left(\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\chi}_{0}\right)^{2}}{\tilde{\chi}_{0}}+\frac{1}{\tilde{\chi}_{0}}\left[\frac{\left(g_{\alpha\beta}\tilde{\chi}^{\alpha}\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\chi}^{\beta}\right)^{2}}{g_{\alpha\beta}\tilde{\chi}^{\alpha}\tilde{\chi}^{\beta}}-g_{\alpha\beta}\left(\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\chi}^{\alpha}\right)\left(\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\chi}^{\beta}\right)\right],

(24)

and it can be simplified to

\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(2)}\right)=\left(\tilde{\chi_{0}}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\tilde{\chi_{i}}\right)^{2},

(25)

for pure states. unlike calculation methods that rely on the diagonalization of the density matrix, this new expression (
24
) bypasses matrix diagonalization and can be successfully applied in various quantum systems.

2.3
Explicit form of Wigner-Yanase skew information

Typically, skew information is widely regarded as a special type of quantum Fisher information since it falls into the category of monotonic metrics on the state space
[
61
,
62
]
. Moreover, it possesses many geometric features analogous to those of Fisher information, both based on the notion of quantum uncertainty as well as Cramér-Rao inequality can be rewritten in terms of skew information
[
63
,
64
,
65
]
. This motivated us to investigate the similarity of these two quantities. Usually, the total uncertainty of a single observable
K
in a quantum state
\rho_{\theta}
is quantified by the variance as
{\rm Var}\left(\rho_{\theta},K\right)={\rm Tr}\left(\rho_{\theta}K^{2}\right)-\left({\rm Tr}\left(\rho_{\theta}K\right)\right)^{2}
. Due to the classical ignorance on the classical mixture in
\rho_{\theta}
, the uncertainty quantification in terms of variance includes the classical ignorance
{\rm Var}_{c}\left(\rho_{\theta}\right)
and a quantum part
{\rm Var}_{q}\left(\rho_{\theta}\right)
for the mixed states, such that
{\rm Var}\left(\rho_{\theta},K\right)={\rm Var}_{c}\left(\rho_{\theta}\right)+{\rm Var}_{q}\left(\rho_{\theta}\right)
. To deal only with the quantum part of the variance, Wigner and Yanase
[
29
]
introduced the following quantity

\mathcal{I}\left(\rho_{\theta}\right)=-4{\rm Tr}[\sqrt{\rho_{\theta}},K]^{2},

(26)

called the Wigner-Yanase skew information, as a measure of the information content on the values of observables not commuting with
K
. In the situation where the quantum estimation is carried out in an interferometric configuration (i.e., the estimation of an unknown phase shift)
[
59
]
, the density matrix is generated by the unitary dynamics
\rho_{\theta}=e^{-iK\theta}\rho_{AB}e^{iK\theta}
and
\rho_{\theta}
obeys the Landau-von Neumann equation
i\partial_{\theta}\rho_{\theta}=\left[K,\theta_{\theta}\right]
. Thus, the above equation (
26
) becomes

\mathcal{I}\left(\rho_{\theta}\right)=4{\rm Tr}\left(\partial\sqrt{\rho_{\theta}}\right)^{2}.

(27)

This quantity can be regarded as a measure of the non-commutativity degrees between a considered state
\rho_{\theta}
and the observable
K
and satisfies all the known criteria for an information-theoretic measure; It reduces to the variance for pure states and vanishes when the density matrix commutes with the conserved quantity
K
. It is invariant as long as the state changes for isolated systems according to the Landau-von Neumann. Another interesting property of skew information is its additive, i.e. the information content of a multiparty system is independent of the sum of the information provided by the subsystems
[
60
]
. For two qubits
X
-state (
2
), the skew information (
27
) can be equivalently represented as

\mathcal{I}\left(\rho_{\theta}\right)=\mathcal{I}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}\right)+\mathcal{I}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(2)}\right),

(28)

where

\mathcal{I}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}\right)=4\operatorname{Tr}\left(\partial_{\theta}\sqrt{\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}}\right)^{2},\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{I}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(2)}\right)=4{\rm Tr}\left(\partial_{\theta}\sqrt{\rho_{\theta}^{(2)}}\right)^{2}.

(29)

Clearly, to obtain the explicit form of
\mathcal{I}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}\right)
in
terms of the coefficients characterizing the state under consideration, we use the same procedure as the one we adopted to get the quantum Fisher information. First, it easy to see that the matrices
\sqrt{\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}}
and
\sqrt{\rho_{\theta}^{(2)}}
can be expanded as

\sqrt{\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}}=t_{0}\vartheta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}t^{i}\vartheta_{i},\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\sqrt{\rho_{\theta}^{(2)}}=\tilde{t}_{0}\tilde{\vartheta}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\tilde{t}^{i}\tilde{\vartheta}_{i}.

(30)

Based on above equations, one shows that

\mathcal{I}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}\right)=8\left[\left(\partial_{\theta}t_{0}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\partial_{\theta}t^{i}\right)^{2}\right],\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{I}(\rho_{\theta}^{(2)})=8\left[\left(\partial\tilde{t}_{0}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\partial\tilde{t}^{i}\right)^{2}\right],

(31)

So, it becomes clear that to calculate
\mathcal{I}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}\right)
and
\mathcal{I}(\rho_{\theta}^{(2)})
, we need to evaluate the coefficients

t_{0}
(
\tilde{t}_{0}
) and
t_{i}
(
\tilde{t}_{i}
) in terms of
\chi_{0}
(
\tilde{\chi}_{0}
) (
6
) and
\chi_{i}
(
\tilde{\chi}_{i}
) (
7
), respectively. For the matrix
\sqrt{\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}}
, we find

\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}=\left(t_{0}^{2}+\left|t^{2}\right|\right)\vartheta_{0}+2t_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{3}t^{i}\vartheta_{i},

(32)

with
t=(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
. By comparing the result (
32
) with the expanded form of the matrix
\rho_{AB}^{(1)}
(
5
), on obtains

t_{0}^{2}+|t|^{2}=\frac{\chi_{0}}{2},\hskip 56.9055pt2t_{0}t^{i}=\frac{\chi_{i}}{2},

(33)

and the solutions
t_{0}
and
t_{i}
reads as

t_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\chi_{0}+\sqrt{\chi_{0}^{2}-|\chi|^{2}}},\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274ptt^{i}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\chi_{i}}{\sqrt{\chi_{0}+\sqrt{\chi_{0}^{2}-|\chi|^{2}}}},

(34)

with
\chi=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{3})
. We end up with

\partial_{\theta}t_{0}=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\chi_{0}^{2}-|\chi|^{2}}}\left[\sqrt{\chi_{0}+\sqrt{\chi_{0}^{2}-|\chi|^{2}}}\left(\partial_{\theta}\chi_{0}\right)-\frac{\Sigma_{j=1}^{3}\chi_{j}\left(\partial_{\theta}\chi_{j}\right)}{\sqrt{\chi_{0}+\sqrt{\chi_{0}^{2}-|\chi|^{2}}}}\right],

(35)

and

\partial_{\theta}t_{i}=-\frac{\nu}{4}\left(\chi_{i}\partial_{\theta}\chi_{0}\right)+\frac{\mu}{2}\left(\partial_{\theta}\chi_{i}\right)+\frac{\gamma}{4}\chi_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left(\chi_{j}\partial_{\theta}\chi_{j}\right),

(36)

where the quantities
\nu
,
\mu
and
\gamma
are given by

\nu=\left(\chi_{0}^{2}-|\chi|^{2}\right)^{-1/2}\left(\chi_{0}+\sqrt{\chi_{0}^{2}-|\chi|^{2}}\right)^{-1/2},\hskip 28.45274pt\mu=\left(\chi_{0}+\sqrt{\chi_{0}^{2}-|\chi|^{2}}\right)^{-1/2},

(37)

and

\gamma=\left(\chi_{0}^{2}-|\chi|^{2}\right)^{-1/2}\left(\chi_{0}+\sqrt{\chi_{0}^{2}-|\chi|^{2}}\right)^{-3/2}.

(38)

Consequently, the evaluation of
\partial\tilde{t}_{0}
and
\partial\tilde{t}_{i}
, as a linear combination of
\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\chi}_{0}
and
\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\chi}_{i}
, is required to obtain the skew information associated with the Hermitian matrix
\sqrt{\rho_{\theta}^{(2)}}
. For pure states, the skew information reduces to

\mathcal{I}\left(\rho_{\theta}^{(1)}\right)=2\left(|\partial_{\theta}\chi_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\partial_{\theta}\chi_{i}|^{2}\right),\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{I}(\rho_{\theta}^{(2)})=2\left(|\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\chi}_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\chi}_{i}|^{2}\right),

(39)

in term of
\chi_{0}
,
\chi_{i}
,
\tilde{\chi}_{0}
and
\tilde{\chi}_{i}
.

3
Explicit expressions of quantum Fisher and skew information under decoherence effect

3.1
Quantum channels

Open quantum systems, which are coupled to an external environment, are playing a major role in quantum information theory. In this situation, the efficiency of quantum information processing depends critically on the details of decoherence and noise-induced by the environment
[
66
,
67
]
. It is, therefore, necessary to find a concrete mathematical description of the errors that reflects our understanding of the environmental influence
[
68
]
. The Markov dynamics of states evolving in noisy environments are modeled by the maps between the spaces of operators known as quantum channels. Indeed, a quantum channel is a map
\Phi
that acts on the density operators;
\Phi:\rho\longmapsto\Phi\left(\rho\right)
, and incorporates the evolution of a pure quantum state to a mixed quantum state
[
69
,
70
]
. Mathematically, the description of the quantum channel can be completely characterized by the Kraus representation as

\rho_{\rm out}=\Phi\left(\rho\right)=\sum_{i}\mathcal{K}_{i}\rho\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\dagger},\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\sum_{i}\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\dagger}\mathcal{K}_{i}=\mathbb{I},

(40)

where
\mathcal{K}_{i}
are the Kraus operator. For a two-qubit system, described by the density matrix
\rho
, this action can be expressed in the Fano-Bloch representation as

\Phi(\rho)=\sum_{\alpha\beta}(\Phi(\mathcal{T}))_{\alpha\beta}\sigma_{\alpha}\otimes\sigma_{\beta},

(41)

where
\sigma_{\alpha}
(
\alpha=1,2,3
) are the Pauli matrices and the matrix elements
(\Phi(\mathcal{T}))_{\alpha\beta}
are given by

\left(\Phi\left(\mathcal{T}\right)\right)_{\alpha\beta}=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\Phi^{\dagger}\left(\sigma_{\alpha}\right)\otimes\Phi^{\dagger}\left(\sigma_{\beta}\right)\rho\right),

(42)

with

\Phi^{\dagger}\left(\sigma_{\alpha}\right)=\sum_{i}\left(\mathcal{K}_{i}\right)^{\dagger}\sigma_{\alpha}\mathcal{K}_{i}.

(43)

It is clear to check that
\Phi^{\dagger}\left(\sigma_{\alpha}\right)
reads as

\Phi^{\dagger}\left(\sigma_{\alpha}\right)=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}}W_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\sigma_{\alpha^{\prime}},

(44)

where the matrix elements of the transformation
W
are given by

W_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\Phi^{\dagger}\left(\sigma_{\alpha}\right)\sigma_{\alpha^{\prime}}\right).

(45)

Using the relations (
42
) and (
44
), one obtains

\Phi(\mathcal{T})=W\mathcal{T}W^{T}.

(46)

where we denote the transpose of matrix
W
by
W^{T}
. With the above description, we will focus on the dynamics of the Fisher and skew information for two-qubit system under noisy channels (i.e., phase-damping, depolarizing and amplitude-damping channels), which can be modeled in terms of Kraus operators as follows:

(
i
) Phase-damping channel:
The phase-damping channel describes the loss of quantum information without loss of energy. The corresponding Kraus operators are given by

\mathcal{K}_{0}^{PDC}=\sqrt{1-p}\mathbb{I},\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{K}_{1}^{PDC}=\sqrt{\frac{p}{4}}(\mathbb{I}+\sigma_{z}),\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{K}_{2}^{PDC}=\sqrt{\frac{p}{4}}(\mathbb{I}-\sigma_{z}).

(47)

where the decoherence probability
p=1-s=1-e^{-\gamma t}
with
\gamma
is the decay rate.

(
ii
) Depolarizing channel:
The depolarizing channel is a model for decoherence where with a certain probability of
3p/4
the qubit becomes totally mixed. The three Kraus operators are characterized by the set

\mathcal{K}_{0}^{DPC}=\sqrt{1-\frac{3p}{4}}\mathbb{I},\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{K}_{1}^{DPC}=\sqrt{\frac{p}{4}}\sigma_{x},\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{K}_{2}^{DPC}=\sqrt{\frac{p}{4}}\sigma_{y},\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{K}_{3}^{DPC}=\sqrt{\frac{p}{4}}\sigma_{z}.

(48)

(
iii
) Amplitude-damping channel:
The amplitude damping channel can be used to describe, for example, the disintegration of a two-level excited state of an atom due to the spontaneous emission of a photon. Obviously, the Kraus operators are given by

\mathcal{K}_{1}^{ADC}=\sqrt{s}|0\rangle\langle 0|+|1\rangle\left\langle 1\left|,\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{K}_{2}^{ADC}=\sqrt{p}\right|1\right\rangle\langle 0|.

(49)

These quantum channels are the prototype models of dissipation relevant in various experimental systems. Especially, they provide a revealing caricature of decoherence in realistic physical situations, with all inessential mathematical details stripped away. Some of the examples include multiphoton systems, ion traps, atomic ensembles, or solid-state spin systems such as quantum dots.

3.2
Fisher and skew information for evolved two-qubit
X
-states

In this section, we shall discuss the effects of these three different noisy channels on both the quantum Fisher information and the skew information for two-qubit
X
-states. Clearly, under decoherence effect, the evolved density matrix
\Phi(\varrho)
remains a two-qubit
X
-state. Using the linearity property of quantum channels, i.e.,
\Phi\left(\alpha\rho_{1}+\beta\rho_{2}\right)=\alpha\Phi\left(\rho_{1}\right)+\beta\Phi\left(\rho_{2}\right)
, and the decomposition of equation (
2
), we obtain

\Phi(\rho_{AB})=\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(1)})+\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(2)}),

(50)

where
\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(1)})
and
\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(2)})
given by

\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(1)})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha=0}^{3}\Lambda_{\alpha}\vartheta_{\alpha},\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(2)})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha=0}^{3}\tilde{\Lambda}_{\alpha}\tilde{\vartheta}_{\alpha}.

(51)

Therefore, the quantum Fisher information for the state (
50
) can be written as

\mathcal{F}\left(\Phi(\rho_{AB})\right)=\mathcal{F}\left(\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(1)})\right)+\mathcal{F}\left(\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(2)})\right),

(52)

where

\mathcal{F}\left(\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(1)})\right)=\frac{\left(\partial_{\theta}\Lambda_{0}\right)^{2}}{\Lambda_{0}}+\frac{1}{\Lambda_{0}}\left[\frac{\left(g_{\alpha\beta}\Lambda^{\alpha}\partial_{\theta}\Lambda^{\beta}\right)^{2}}{g_{\alpha\beta}\Lambda^{\alpha}\Lambda^{\beta}}-g_{\alpha\beta}\left(\partial_{\theta}\Lambda^{\alpha}\right)\left(\partial_{\theta}\Lambda^{\beta}\right)\right],

(53)

and

\mathcal{F}\left(\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(2)})\right)=\frac{\left(\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\Lambda}_{0}^{2}\right)}{\tilde{\Lambda}_{0}}+\frac{1}{\tilde{\Lambda}_{0}}\left[\frac{\left(g_{\alpha\beta}\tilde{\Lambda}^{\alpha}\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\Lambda}^{\beta}\right)^{2}}{g_{\alpha\beta}\tilde{\Lambda}^{\alpha}\tilde{\Lambda}^{\beta}}-g_{\alpha\beta}\left(\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\Lambda}^{\alpha}\right)\left(\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\Lambda}^{\beta}\right)\right].

(54)

For pure states, the quantum Fisher information reduces to the following explicit form

\mathcal{F}\left(\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(1)})\right)=(\Lambda_{0})^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}(\Lambda_{i})^{2},\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm}\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{F}\left(\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(2)})\right)=(\tilde{\Lambda_{0}})^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}(\tilde{\Lambda_{i}})^{2}.

(55)

As we saw earlier, for the noisy effects under consideration, any two-qubit
X
-state remains
X
-shaped. Further, the states
\sqrt{\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(j)})}
(with
j=1,2
) can be written as a linear combination of the generators
\vartheta_{\alpha}
and
\tilde{\vartheta}_{\alpha}
as

\sqrt{\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(1)})}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha=0}^{3}X_{\alpha}\vartheta_{\alpha},\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\sqrt{\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(2)})}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha=0}^{3}\tilde{X}_{\alpha}\tilde{\vartheta}_{\alpha},

(56)

where
X_{\alpha}
(resp.
\tilde{X}_{\alpha}
) are the components of the Bloch vector of the matrix
\sqrt{\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(1)})}
(resp.
\sqrt{\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(2)})}
). Consequently, it is easy to see that skew information is written as

\mathcal{I}\left(\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(1)})\right)=8\left[\left(\partial_{\theta}X_{0}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\partial_{\theta}X_{i}\right)^{2}\right],\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{I}\left(\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(2)})\right)=8\left[\left(\partial\tilde{X}_{0}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\partial\tilde{X}_{i}\right)^{2}\right].

(57)

For pure states, the skew information is expressed as below:

\mathcal{I}\left(\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(1)})\right)=2\left(|\partial_{\theta}\Lambda_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\partial_{\theta}\Lambda_{i}|^{2}\right),\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{I}\left(\Phi(\rho_{AB}^{(2)})\right)=2\left(|\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\Lambda}_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\Lambda}_{i}|^{2}\right).

(58)

Thus, the analytical expression of quantum Fisher and skew information are obtained for any two-qubit system by deriving the correlation matrix
\Phi(\mathcal{T})
. For phase-damping channel, which is defined by the set of Kraus operators (
47
), the action of the application
\Phi
on the correlation matrix
\mathcal{T}
is

\Phi(\mathcal{T})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathcal{T}_{00}&0&0&\mathcal{T}_{03}\\
0&s^{2}\mathcal{T}_{11}&s^{2}\mathcal{T}_{12}&0\\
0&s^{2}\mathcal{T}_{21}&s^{2}\mathcal{T}_{22}&0\\
\mathcal{T}_{30}&0&0&\mathcal{T}_{33}\end{array}\right).

(59)

and the quantities
\Lambda_{i}
and
\tilde{\Lambda}_{i}
are, respectively, given by

\Lambda_{0}=\chi_{0},\hskip 28.45274pt\Lambda_{1}=s^{2}\chi_{1},\hskip 28.45274pt\Lambda_{2}=s^{2}\chi_{2},\hskip 28.45274pt\Lambda_{3}=\chi_{3},

(60)

and

\tilde{\Lambda}_{0}=\tilde{\chi}_{0},\hskip 28.45274pt\tilde{\Lambda}_{1}=s^{2}\tilde{\chi}_{1},\hskip 28.45274pt\tilde{\Lambda}_{2}=s^{2}\tilde{\chi}_{2},\hskip 28.45274pt\tilde{\Lambda}_{3}=\tilde{\chi}_{3}.

(61)

Likewise, it is simple to verify that the matrix (
46
) for the depolarizing channel is expressed as

\Phi(\mathcal{T})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathcal{T}_{00}&0&0&s\mathcal{T}_{03}\\
0&s^{2}\mathcal{T}_{11}&s^{2}\mathcal{T}_{12}&0\\
0&s^{2}\mathcal{T}_{21}&s^{2}\mathcal{T}_{22}&0\\
s\mathcal{T}_{30}&0&0&s^{2}\mathcal{T}_{33}\end{array}\right),

(62)

and in such a case, we obtain

\Lambda_{0}=\frac{1}{2}((\chi_{0}+\tilde{\chi}_{0})+s^{2}(\chi_{0}-\tilde{\chi}_{0})),\hskip 28.45274pt\Lambda_{1}=s^{2}\chi_{1},\hskip 28.45274pt\Lambda_{2}=s^{2}\chi_{2},\hskip 28.45274pt\Lambda_{3}=s^{2}\chi_{3},

(63)

and

\tilde{\Lambda}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}((\chi_{0}+\tilde{\chi}_{0})-s^{2}(\chi_{0}-\tilde{\chi}_{0})),\hskip 28.45274pt\tilde{\Lambda}_{1}=s^{2}\chi_{1},\hskip 28.45274pt\tilde{\Lambda}_{2}=s^{2}\chi_{2},\hskip 28.45274pt\tilde{\Lambda}_{3}=s^{2}\chi_{3}.

(64)

Further, through the Kraus operator (
49
) that characterizes the amplitude damping channel, we prove that

\Phi(\mathcal{T})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathcal{T}_{00}&0&0&s\mathcal{T}_{03}-p\mathcal{T}_{00}\\
0&s\mathcal{T}_{11}&s\mathcal{T}_{12}&0\\
0&s\mathcal{T}_{21}&s\mathcal{T}_{22}&0\\
s\mathcal{T}_{30}-p\mathcal{T}_{00}&0&0&p^{2}\mathcal{T}_{00}-sp(\mathcal{T}_{30}+\mathcal{T}_{03})+s^{2}\mathcal{T}_{33}\end{array}\right),

(65)

where

\Lambda_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left[(1+p^{2}+s^{2})\chi_{0}-2sp\chi_{3}+(1+p^{2}-s^{2})\tilde{\chi}_{0}\right],\quad\Lambda_{1}=s\chi_{1},\hskip 28.45274pt\Lambda_{2}=s\chi_{2},\quad\Lambda_{3}=s\chi_{3}-p(\chi_{0}+\tilde{\chi}_{0}),

(66)

and

\tilde{\Lambda}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left[(1-p^{2}-s^{2})\chi_{0}+2sp\chi_{3}+(1-p^{2}+s^{2})\tilde{\chi}_{0}\right],\quad\tilde{\Lambda}_{1}=s\tilde{\chi}_{1},\hskip 28.45274pt\tilde{\Lambda}_{2}=s\tilde{\chi}_{2},\hskip 28.45274pt\tilde{\Lambda}_{3}=s\tilde{\chi}_{3},

(67)

Clearly, the correlation matrix
\Phi(\mathcal{T})
given by (
59
), (
62
) and (
65
) corresponds to an
X
-shaped density matrix. Therefore, the three noisy channels preserves the
X
-form of the initial
X
-state. In this respect, the results obtained in the previous section can be adopted to investigate the quantum Fisher and skew information in three different noisy channels for the system under consideration.

4
Application to quasi-Werner states

Coherent states
[
71
,
72
]
, which were discussed by Glauber
[
73
]
to explain the coherence property of laser light, are considered the closest quantum counterpart to classical radiation field states. They have an important role in quantum information processing to storage, process or teleport an unknown quantum state. Actually, the coherent states are eigenstates of the annihilation operator
a
, i.e.,
a\left|\alpha\right\rangle=\alpha\left|\alpha\right\rangle
with the Fock state representation of a single mode coherent state is

\left|\alpha\right\rangle=D\left(\alpha\right)\left|0\right\rangle=\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\mid\alpha\mid^{2}\right]\sum_{n}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\left|n\right\rangle,

(68)

which can be viewed as the unitary displacement action
D\left(\alpha\right)
on the ground state vector
\left|0\right\rangle
, with

D\left(\alpha\right)=\exp\left[-\mid\alpha\mid^{2}/2\right]\exp\left[\alpha a^{\dagger}\right]\exp\left[-\alpha^{*}a\right].

(69)

Besides, the coherent state (
68
) is a typical example of the non-orthogonal state, so it’s easy to see that

\left\langle\alpha_{1}\mid\alpha_{2}\right\rangle=\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\mid\alpha_{1}\mid^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mid\alpha_{2}\mid^{2}+\alpha_{1}^{*}\alpha_{2}\right].

(70)

Moreover, the coherent state family solves unity and allows the over completeness in the space such that

\frac{1}{\pi}\int\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|d^{2}\alpha=\hbox{\small 1\kern-4.3pt\normalsize 1},\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274ptd^{2}\alpha=d\mathcal{R}e\left(\alpha\right)+d\mathcal{I}m\left(\alpha\right).

(71)

If we consider the two coherent states
\left|\alpha\right\rangle
and
\left|\beta\right\rangle
with corresponding coherent amplitudes
\alpha
and
\beta
, we can write the two bipartite superposed coherent states as

\left|\psi^{\pm}\right\rangle=n_{\pm}\left[\left|\alpha,\beta\right\rangle\pm\left|-\alpha,-\beta\right\rangle\right],

(72)

where the states
\left|-\alpha\right\rangle
and
\left|-\beta\right\rangle
are
\pi
-radian out of phase with the corresponding coherent states and
n_{\pm}
is the normalization factor reads

n_{\pm}=\left[2\left(1\pm\chi_{\alpha}^{2}\chi_{\beta}^{2}\right)\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}},

(73)

with
\chi_{\alpha}=\exp\left[-|\alpha|^{2}\right]
and
\chi_{\beta}=\exp\left[-|\beta|^{2}\right]
. As mentioned earlier, the coherent states of the form (
68
) are not orthogonal to each other, however, an orthonormal basis can be achieved by considering the even and odd coherent states
\mid\pm_{\alpha}\rangle
and
\mid\pm_{\beta}\rangle
as

\left|\pm_{\alpha}\right\rangle=N_{\pm}^{\alpha}[|\alpha\rangle\pm|-\alpha\rangle],\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\left|\pm_{\beta}\right\rangle=N_{\pm}^{\beta}[|\beta\rangle\pm|-\beta\rangle],

(74)

where the normalization constants are given by

N_{\pm}^{\alpha}=\left[2\left(1\pm\chi_{\alpha}^{2}\right)\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}},\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274ptN_{\pm}^{\beta}=\left[2\left(1\pm\chi_{\beta}^{2}\right)\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(75)

Reporting the expressions (
74
) and (
75
) in equation (
72
), we rewrite the states
\left|\psi^{\pm}\right\rangle
using the orthonormal basis states as follows

\left|\psi^{+}\right\rangle=\frac{n^{+}}{2}\left[\frac{\left|+_{\alpha},+_{\beta}\right\rangle}{N_{+}^{\alpha}N_{+}^{\beta}}+\frac{|-_{\alpha},-_{\beta}\rangle}{N_{-}^{\alpha}N_{-}^{\beta}}\right],\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\left|\psi^{-}\right\rangle=\frac{n^{-}}{2}\left[\frac{\left|+_{\alpha},-_{\beta}\right\rangle}{N_{+}^{\alpha}N_{-}^{\beta}}+\frac{|-_{\alpha},+_{\beta}\rangle}{N_{-}^{\alpha}N_{+}^{\beta}}\right].

(76)

Respectively, two quasi-Werner states
[
74
]
based on the bipartite superposed coherent states are written as

\rho(\psi^{+},q)=(1-q)\frac{\hbox{\small 1\kern-4.3pt\normalsize 1}}{4}+q|\psi^{+}\rangle\langle\psi^{+}|,\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\rho(\psi^{-},q)=(1-q)\frac{\hbox{\small 1\kern-4.3pt\normalsize 1}}{4}+q|\psi^{-}\rangle\langle\psi^{-}|,

(77)

where
q
being the mixing parameter ranging from
0
(for maximal mixed states) to
1
(for pure states). The density matrices
\rho\left(\psi^{\pm},q\right)
, associated to the quasi-Werner states
\left|\psi^{\pm}\right\rangle
, take the following forms

\rho\left(\psi^{+},p\right)=\frac{1}{4}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1+q\left(\frac{n_{+}^{2}}{\left(N_{+}^{\alpha}\right)^{2}\left(N_{+}^{\beta}\right)^{2}}-1\right)&0&0&\frac{qn_{+}^{2}}{N_{+}^{\alpha}N_{+}^{\beta}N_{-}^{\alpha}N_{-}^{\beta}}\\
0&1-q&0&0\\
0&0&1-q&0\\
\frac{qn_{+}^{2}}{N_{+}^{\alpha}N_{+}^{\beta}N_{-}^{\alpha}N_{-}^{\beta}}&0&0&1+q\left(\frac{n_{+}^{2}}{\left(N_{-}^{\alpha}\right)^{2}\left(N_{-}^{\beta}\right)^{2}}-1\right)\end{array}\right),

(78)

and

\rho\left(\psi^{-},q\right)=\frac{1}{4}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1-q&0&0&0\\
0&1+q\left(\frac{n_{-}^{2}}{\left(N_{+}^{\alpha}\right)^{2}\left(N_{-}^{\beta}\right)^{2}}-1\right)&\frac{qn_{-}^{2}}{N_{+}^{\alpha}N_{+}^{\beta}N_{-}^{\alpha}N_{-}^{\beta}}&0\\
0&\frac{qn_{-}^{2}}{N_{+}^{\alpha}N_{+}^{\beta}N_{-}^{\alpha}N_{-}^{\beta}}&1+q\left(\frac{n_{-}^{2}}{\left(N_{-}^{\alpha}\right)^{2}\left(N_{+}^{\beta}\right)^{2}}-1\right)&0\\
0&0&0&1-q\end{array}\right),

(79)

in the computational basis

\{|+_{\alpha},+_{\beta}\rangle,|+_{\alpha},-_{\beta}\rangle,|-_{\alpha},+_{\beta}\rangle,|-_{\alpha},-_{\beta}\rangle\}
. The non-vanishing matrix elements
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha\beta}
(with
\alpha,\beta=0,1,2,3
) are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{00}^{+}=1,\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{T}_{11}^{+}=\frac{-qn_{+}^{2}}{2N_{+}^{\alpha}N_{+}^{\beta}N_{-}^{\alpha}N_{-}^{\beta}},\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{T}_{30}^{+}=\frac{qn_{+}^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{1}{(N_{\alpha}^{+}N_{\beta}^{+})^{2}}-\frac{1}{(N_{\alpha}^{-}N_{\beta}^{-})^{2}}\right),

(80)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{33}^{+}=q,\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{T}_{22}^{+}=\frac{qn_{+}^{2}}{2N_{+}^{\alpha}N_{+}^{\beta}N_{-}^{\alpha}N_{-}^{\beta}},\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{T}_{03}^{+}=\frac{qn_{+}^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{1}{(N_{\alpha}^{+}N_{\beta}^{+})^{2}}-\frac{1}{(N_{\alpha}^{-}N_{\beta}^{-})^{2}}\right),

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{00}^{-}=1,\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{T}_{11}^{-}=\frac{qn_{-}^{2}}{2N_{+}^{\alpha}N_{+}^{\beta}N_{-}^{\alpha}N_{-}^{\beta}},\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{T}_{30}^{-}=\frac{qn_{-}^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{1}{(N_{\alpha}^{+}N_{\beta}^{-})^{2}}-\frac{1}{(N_{\alpha}^{-}N_{\beta}^{+})^{2}}\right),

(81)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{33}^{-}=-q,\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{T}_{22}^{-}=\frac{qn_{-}^{2}}{2N_{+}^{\alpha}N_{+}^{\beta}N_{-}^{\alpha}N_{-}^{\beta}},\hskip 28.45274pt\mathcal{T}_{03}^{-}=\frac{-qn_{-}^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{1}{(N_{\alpha}^{+}N_{\beta}^{-})^{2}}-\frac{1}{(N_{\alpha}^{-}N_{\beta}^{+})^{2}}\right),

for the states
\rho\left(\psi^{+},q\right)
and
\rho\left(\psi^{-},q\right)
, respectively. Then we decompose these density matrices as

\rho\left(\psi^{\pm},q\right)\equiv\rho_{\pm}=\varrho_{1}^{\pm}+\varrho_{2}^{\pm},

(82)

where

\displaystyle\varrho_{1}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(\chi_{0}^{\pm}\vartheta_{0}+\chi^{\pm}\vartheta_{1}),\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm and}\hskip 28.45274pt\varrho_{2}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{\chi}_{0}^{\pm}\tilde{\vartheta}_{0}+\tilde{\chi}_{1}^{\pm}\tilde{\vartheta}_{1}).

(83)

The non-vanishing quantities
\chi_{i}^{\pm}
and
\tilde{\chi}_{i}^{\pm}
are given by

\displaystyle\chi_{0}^{\pm}=\frac{1\pm q}{2},\hskip 28.45274pt\chi_{1}^{+}=\frac{qn_{+}^{2}}{2N_{+}^{\alpha}N_{+}^{\beta}N_{-}^{\alpha}N_{-}^{\beta}},\hskip 28.45274pt\chi_{3}^{+}=\frac{qn_{+}^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{1}{(N_{\alpha}^{+}N_{\beta}^{+})^{2}}-\frac{1}{(N_{\alpha}^{-}N_{\beta}^{-})^{2}}\right),

(84)

\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}_{0}^{\pm}=\frac{1\mp q}{2},\hskip 28.45274pt\tilde{\chi}_{1}^{-}=\frac{qn_{-}^{2}}{2N_{+}^{\alpha}N_{+}^{\beta}N_{-}^{\alpha}N_{-}^{\beta}},\hskip 28.45274pt\tilde{\chi}_{3}^{-}=\frac{qn_{-}^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{1}{(N_{\alpha}^{+}N_{\beta}^{-})^{2}}-\frac{1}{(N_{\alpha}^{-}N_{\beta}^{+})^{2}}\right).

In order to investigate the role of quantum entanglement in quantum metrology for the bipartite quasi-Werner states, we employ the most accepted bona fide entanglement measure called concurrence
{\cal C}\left(\rho\right)
, which was introduced by Wootters
[
75
]
. For our states
\rho\left(\psi^{\pm},q\right)
, the analytical expression of concurrence takes the form

{\cal C}\left(\rho_{\pm}\right)=\max\left\{0,\frac{qn_{\pm}^{2}-\left(1-q\right)N_{+}^{\alpha}N_{-}^{\alpha}N_{+}^{\beta}N_{-}^{\beta}}{2N_{+}^{\alpha}N_{-}^{\alpha}N_{+}^{\beta}N_{-}^{\beta}}\right\}.

(85)

To get an insight into the influence of the mixedness on the entanglement dynamics of two quasi-Werner states, we display the concurrence (

This shows that skew information fulfills the same role as quantum Fisher information in quantum estimation theory.

5
Concluding Remarks

To summarize, the skew information as a measure of the theoretical information content in a given quantum state, and the quantum Fisher information defined via symmetric logarithmic derivatives, have some common properties, both playing an important role in quantum information theory and establishing the interpretation in quantum uncertainty. They are both useful in the quantification of quantum resources such as quantum coherence
[
76
,
77
]
, asymmetry
[
78
,
79
]
and quantum correlation
[
80
,
81
,
82
]
. Moreover, understanding these informational quantifiers in quantum systems has practical significance in quantum estimation strategies. However, the interplay between these two concepts appears to have attracted little attention.

Our main result provides a complete characterization of such notions and gives a detailed analytical derivation for an arbitrary two-qubit
X
-state. As an illustration, we applied these derived analytical expressions in the two quasi-Werner states composed of two bipartite superposed coherent states. As well, we investigated their dynamics when exposed to various decoherence effects via phase damping, amplitude damping and depolarization channels. We have proved that quantum Fisher information and skew information perform the same behavior under decoherence channels and exhibit somewhat similar properties. Further, the maximum skew information coincides with the maximum amount of entanglement in the two quasi-Werner states, which is quantified via the Wootters concurrence. These results are in agreement with the results of the quantum Fisher information and show the role of quantum entanglement to improve the efficiency and accuracy of quantum metrology protocols.

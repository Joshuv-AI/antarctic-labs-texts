Abstract
We propose a time-implicit, finite-element based space-time discretization of the necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for the stochastic linear-quadratic optimal control problem with the stochastic heat equation driven by linear noise of type [ X ⁡ ( t ) + σ ⁡ ( t ) ] ​ d ​ W ​ ( t ) [X(t)+\sigma(t)]{\rm d}W(t) , and prove optimal convergence w.r.t. both, space and time discretization parameters. In particular, we employ the stochastic Riccati equation as a proper analytical tool to handle the linear noise, and thus extend the applicability of the earlier work [ 16 ] , where the error analysis was restricted to additive noise.

Introduction

Let
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a bounded domain with
C^{2}
boundary and
T>0
be given.
Our goal is to numerically approximate the
{\mathbb{F}}
-adapted control process

U^{*}\equiv\{U^{*}(t);\,t\in[0,T]\}
on the filtered probability space

(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
that minimizes the quadratic functional (
\alpha\geq 0
)

{\mathcal{J}}(X,U)=\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}\|X(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|U(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,{\rm d}t+\alpha\|X(T)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\Bigr]

(1.1)

subject to the (controlled forward) stochastic PDE (
SPDE
, for short) of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm d}X(t)=\bigl[\Delta X(t)+U(t)\bigr]\,{\rm d}t+\big[X(t)+\sigma(t)\big]{\rm d}W(t)&\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,,\\
X(0)=X_{0}\,,\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

for a proper function
\sigma:[0,T]\rightarrow{\mathbb{L}}^{2}
, a suitable initial datum
X_{0}
,
homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, and a Wiener process

W:=\{W(t):\,t\in[0,T]\}
, which here is
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued for the sake of simplicity.
A unique (strong) minimizer
(X^{*},U^{*})

of the stochastic optimal control problem: ‘minimize (
1.1
) subject to (
1.2
)’ may then be deduced — which we below refer to as problem
SLQ
; see e.g.
[
2
,
13
]
, and Section
2
for a further specification of the data.

is a prototypic stochastic optimization problem on (infinite-dimensional) Hilbert spaces, for which the numerical analysis so-far is rare in the literature;
cf.
the references in
[
16
]
. In the foregoing work
[
16
]
, optimal strong error estimates were shown for a space-time discretization of a corresponding problem where the equation (
1.2
)
1
was driven by
additive
noise. On the level of Pontryagin’s maximum principle that we apply below to develop numerical methods for
SLQ
this difference, in particular, simplified a lot the (numerical analysis of the) backward
stochastic PDE (

, for short), which for the current problem

with solution tuple

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm d}Y(t)=\bigl[-\Delta Y(t)-Z(t)+X(t)\bigr]{\rm d}t+Z(t){\rm d}W(t)&\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,,\\
Y(T)=-\alpha X(T)\,.\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

Note that in the case of

noise in
(
1.2

in
(
1.3

[

]

For

, the optimality system consists of (

), (
1.3
) and

(1.4)

which then uniquely determines the optimal process tuple denoted by

of problem

.

Based on Pontryagin’s maximum principle, problem

1.2

1.3
) and (

(
1.3
) within (the finite element version of) the coupled optimality system

.
In this work, we use the stochastic Riccati equation (

(see (

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Notations and assumptions — involved processes and the finite element method

Let
\bigl({\mathbb{K}},(\cdot,\cdot)_{{\mathbb{K}}}\bigr)
be a separable Hilbert space.
By
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}
resp.
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}
,
we denote the norm resp. the scalar product in Lebesgue space
{\mathbb{L}}^{2}:=L^{2}(D)
.
By
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}
,
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
,
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}}
, we denote norms in Sobolev spaces
{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}:=H_{0}^{1}(D)
,
\mathbb{H}^{2}:=H^{2}(D)
,
\mathbb{H}^{3}:=H^{3}(D)
respectively.
Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
be a complete filtered probability space, where

{\mathbb{F}}=\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
is the filtration generated by the
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued Wiener process
W
, which is augmented by
all the
{\mathbb{P}}
-null sets. The space of all
{\mathbb{F}}
-adapted processes

X:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}
satisfying

{\mathbb{E}}[\int_{0}^{T}\|X(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{K}}}\,{\rm d}t]<\infty
is denoted by

L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{K}})
; the space of all
{\mathbb{F}}
-adapted processes

X:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}
with continuous path satisfying

{\mathbb{E}}[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{K}}]<\infty
is denoted by

L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{K}})\bigr)
;
for any
t\in[0,T]
, the space of
\mathbb{K}
-valued
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable random variables
\eta
satisfying
\mathbb{E}[\|\eta\|^{2}_{\mathbb{K}}]<\infty

is denoted by
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}(\Omega;\mathbb{K})
.

We partition the bounded domain
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
via a regular triangulation
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
into elements
K
with maximum mesh size

h:=\max\{{\rm diam}(K):\,K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}
, and consider space

\displaystyle{\mathbb{V}}_{h}:=\left\{\phi\in{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}:\,\phi\bigl|_{K}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{1}(K)\quad\forall\,K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\right\}\,,

where
{\mathbb{P}}_{1}(K)
denotes the space of polynomials of degree
1
; see
e.g.

[
4
]
.
We define the discrete Laplacian
\Delta_{h}:{\mathbb{V}}_{h}\rightarrow{\mathbb{V}}_{h}
by
(-\Delta_{h}\xi_{h},\phi_{h})_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}=(\nabla\xi_{h},\nabla\phi_{h})_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}
for all
\xi_{h},\phi_{h}\in{\mathbb{V}}_{h}
, the
{\mathbb{L}}^{2}
-projection
\Pi_{h}:{\mathbb{L}}^{2}\rightarrow{\mathbb{V}}_{h}
by
(\Pi_{h}\xi-\xi,\phi_{h})_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}=0
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{2},\,\phi_{h}\in{\mathbb{V}}_{h}
, and the Ritz-projection
{\cal R}_{h}:\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}

by
(\nabla[{\cal R}_{h}\xi-\xi],\nabla\phi_{h})_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}=0
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1},\,\phi_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
.
Via definition of
{\cal R}_{h}
, it is easy to get that

\|\nabla{\cal R}_{h}\xi\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leq\|\nabla\xi\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\qquad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}\,.

(2.1)

We denote by
I_{\tau}=\{t_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}\subset[0,T]
a time mesh with maximum step size
\tau:=\max\{t_{n+1}-t_{n}:\,n=0,1,\cdots,N-1\}
, and

\Delta_{n}W:=W(t_{n})-W(t_{n-1})
for all
n=1,\cdots,N
. For given time mesh
I_{\tau}
, we can define
\mu(\cdot),\,\nu(\cdot)
by

\mu(t):=t_{n+1}\,,\qquad\nu(t):=t_{n}\qquad\forall\,t\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}),\,n=0,1,\cdots,N-1\,.

(2.2)

Throughout this work, we assume that
\tau\leq 1
.
For simplicity, we choose a uniform partition, i.e.
\tau=T/N
.
The results in this work still hold for quasi-uniform partitions.
Throughout this work, we shall make use of the following

Assumption (A)
:
X_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}\cap\mathbb{H}^{3}
, and
\sigma\in C^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}\cap\mathbb{H}^{3}).

2.2
The stochastic heat equation and rates of strong convergence for its space-time discretization

Under Assumption
(A)
, in particular,
and given

U\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1})

in

SPDE
(
1.2
), there exists a unique strong solution
X\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0})\bigr)\cap L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(0,T;{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\bigr)

which satisfies
the following estimate (see
e.g.

[
5
]
),

{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X(t)\|_{{\mathbb{H}}_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|X(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}\,{\rm d}t\Big]\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\|X_{0}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{H}}_{0}^{1}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|U(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\|\sigma(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}\,{\rm d}t\Big]\,.

(2.3)

Obviously,
X
satisfies the following variational form
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\big(X(t),\phi\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\big(X_{0},\phi\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\nabla X(s),\nabla\phi\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\big(U(s),\phi\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,{\rm d}s

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\big(X(s)+\sigma(s),\phi\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}W(s)\qquad\forall\,\phi\in{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}\,.

A finite element discretization of (
2.4
) which we later refer to as
SPDE
h
then reads: For all
t\in[0,T]
, find

X_{h}\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{V}}_{h})\bigr)
such that
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. and for all times
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\big(X_{h}(t),\phi_{h}\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\big(X_{h}(0),\phi_{h}\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\nabla X_{h}(s),\nabla\phi_{h}\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\big(U(s),\phi_{h}\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s

(2.5)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\big(X_{h}(s)+{\cal R}_{h}\sigma(s),\phi_{h}\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\qquad\forall\,\phi_{h}\in{\mathbb{V}}_{h}\,.

Equation (
2.5
) may be recast into the following stochastic differential equation,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &dX_{h}(t)=\bigl[\Delta_{h}X_{h}(t)+\Pi_{h}U(t)\bigr]{\rm d}t+\big[X_{h}(t)+{\cal R}_{h}\sigma(t)\big]{\rm d}W(t)\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,,\\
&X_{h}(0)={\cal R}_{h}X_{0}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.6)

Thanks to this equivalence, we do not distinguish between
SPDE
h
(
2.5
) and equation (
2.6
)
throughout this paper.

The derivation of an error estimate is well-known (see
e.g.

[
21
]
), which uses the improved (spatial) regularity properties of the strong solution to deduce

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|X_{h}(t)-X(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]+{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla\bigl[X_{h}(t)-X(t)\bigr]\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,{\rm d}t\Bigr]\leq Ch^{2}\,.

(2.7)

We now consider a time-implicit discretization of (
2.5
) on a partition
I_{\tau}
of
[0,T]
. The problem then reads: For every
0\leq n\leq N-1
, find a solution
X^{n+1}_{h}\in L^{2}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{n+1}}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{V}}_{h})
such that
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s.

\big(X^{n+1}_{h}-X^{n}_{h},\phi_{h}\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\tau\Big[\big(\nabla X_{h}^{n+1},\nabla\phi_{h}\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\bigl(U(t_{n}),\phi_{h}\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\Big]=\big(X_{h}^{n},\phi_{h}\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\Delta_{n+1}W\,.

(2.8)

The verification of the error estimate (see
[
21
]
)

\max_{0\leq n\leq N}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|X_{h}(t_{n})-X^{n}_{h}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]+\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\|\nabla\bigl[X_{h}(t_{n})-X^{n}_{h}\bigr]\bigr\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\Bigr]\leq C\tau

(2.9)

rests on
stability properties of the implicit Euler, Assumption
(A)
, as well as the further assumption that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|U(t)-U(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]\leq C\tau\,.

When solving problem
SLQ
, we select the
optimal control
U^{*}
for
U
, in particular, which inherits improved regularity conditions from the solution of the adjoint equation to the optimal control
U^{*}
via the optimality condition.

2.3
The backward stochastic heat equation — a finite element based spatial discretization

Let
Y_{T}\in L^{2}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}\bigl(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}\bigr)
and
f\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,T;{\mathbb{L}}^{2})
. A strong solution to the backward stochastic heat equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm d}Y(t)=\bigl[-\Delta Y(t)-Z(t)+f(t)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}t+Z(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t)&\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,,\\
Y(T)=Y_{T}\end{array}\right.

(2.10)

with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary data
is a pair of square integrable
{\mathbb{F}}
-adapted processes

(Y,Z)\in\bigl(L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0})\cap L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigl(0,T;{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}\cap{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\bigr)\bigr)\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigl(0,T;{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}\bigr)
that satisfies the following variational form

{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\bigl(Y_{T},\phi\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\big(Y(t),\phi\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\int_{t}^{T}\bigl(\nabla Y(s),\nabla\phi\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\big(Z(s),\phi\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\bigl(f(s),\phi\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,{\rm d}s

(2.11)

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{T}\bigl(Z(s),\phi\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,{\rm d}W(s)\qquad\forall\,\phi\in{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}\,.

The existence of a strong solution to (
2.10
), as well as its uniqueness are shown in
[
7
]
; moreover,
there exists a constant
C\equiv C(D,T)>0
such that

{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}\bigr]+{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}\|Y(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}+\|Z(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\|Y_{T}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]\,.

(2.12)

We may consider a finite element discretization of the
BSPDE
(
2.10
).
Let
Y_{T,h}={\cal R}_{h}Y_{T}\in L^{2}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{V}}_{h})
,
an approximation of
Y_{T}
. The problem
BSPDE
h
then reads:
Find a pair
(Y_{h},Z_{h})\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{V}}_{h})\bigr)\times L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(0,T;{\mathbb{V}}_{h}\bigr)
such that
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. for all
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\big(Y_{T,h},\phi_{h}\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\big(Y_{h}(t),\phi_{h}\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}\big(\nabla Y_{h}(s),\nabla\phi_{h}\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\big(Z(s),\phi_{h}\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\big(f(s),\phi_{h}\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,{\rm d}s

(2.13)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\big(Z_{h}(s),\phi_{h}\big)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,{\rm d}W(s)\qquad\forall\,\phi_{h}\in{\mathbb{V}}_{h}\,.

Actually, equation (
2.13
) is equivalent to the following BSDE:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\rm d}Y_{h}(t)=\bigl[-\Delta_{h}Y_{h}(t)-Z_{h}(t)+\Pi_{h}f(t)\bigr]{\rm d}t+Z_{h}(t){\rm d}W(t)\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,,\\
&Y_{h}(T)=Y_{T,h}\,,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.14)

and we do not distinguish between
BSPDE
h
(
2.13
) and BSDE (
2.14
)
throughout this paper.
The existence and uniqueness of a solution tuple
(Y_{h},Z_{h})
follows from
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
. Moreover, there exists
C\equiv C(T)>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|\nabla Y_{h}(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]+{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}\|\Delta_{h}Y_{h}(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+\|\nabla Z_{h}(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,{\rm d}t\Bigr]\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\left\|\nabla Y_{T,h}\right\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]\,;\end{aligned}

cf.
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
. The following result is taken from
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
, whose proof exploits the bounds (
2.12
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume
Y_{T}\in L^{2}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0})
and

(Y,Z)
be the solution of (

, we may take

(

),

, and

2.4
Temporal discretization of problem
SLQ
— the role of Malliavin derivatives

The numerical analysis of a temporal discretization of problem
SLQ
requires Malliavin calculus to bound temporal increments such as
\mathbb{E}[\|Z_{h}(t)-Z_{h}(s)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}]
in terms of
|t-s|
, where
s,t\in[0,T]
,
and
Z_{h}
is the second component of BSDE (
2.14
) with
\Pi_{h}f=X_{h}^{*}
. We therefore briefly recall the definition and results of the Malliavin derivative
of processes, which will be applied below. For further details, we refer to
[
15
,
9
]
.

We define the Itô isometry
\mathbb{W}:L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}})\to L^{2}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}})
by

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}(g)=\int_{0}^{T}g(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}W(t)\,.

For
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we denote by
C_{p}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
the space of all smooth functions
s:\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
s
and all of its partial derivatives have polynomial growth.
Let
{\cal P}
be the set of
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued random variables of the form

\displaystyle F=s\bigl(\mathbb{W}(g_{1}),\mathbb{W}(g_{2}),\cdots,\mathbb{W}(g_{\ell})\bigr)

for some
s\in C_{p}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
g_{1},\ldots,g_{\ell}\in L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}})
.
To any
F\in{\cal P}
we define its
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued Malliavin derivative

DF:=\{D_{\theta}F;\,0\leq\theta\leq T\}
process via

\displaystyle D_{\theta}F=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{\partial s}{\partial x_{i}}(\mathbb{W}(g_{1}),\mathbb{W}(g_{2}),\cdots,W(g_{\ell}))g_{i}(\theta)\,.

In general, we can define the
k
-th iterated derivative of
F
by
D^{k}F=D(D^{k-1}F)
, for any

k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Note that, generally, for any
\theta\in[0,T]
,
D_{\theta}F
is
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable; and if
F
is

\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable, then
D_{\theta}F=0
for any
\theta\in(t,T]
.

Now we can extend the derivative operator to
\mathbb{K}
-valued variables. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
u
in the set of
\mathbb{K}
-valued variables:

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{\mathbb{K}}=\Bigl\{u=\sum_{j=1}^{n}F_{j}\phi_{j}:F_{j}\in{\cal P},\,\phi_{j}\in\mathbb{K},\,n\in\mathbb{N}\Bigr\}\,,

we can define the
k
-th iterated derivative of
u
by

D^{k}u=\sum_{j=1}^{n}D^{k}F_{j}\otimes\phi_{j}\,.

For
p\geq 1
, we define the norm
\|\cdot\|_{k,p}
via

\displaystyle\|u\|_{k,p}:=\Big(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|u\|_{\mathbb{K}}^{p}\bigr]+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\left\|D^{j}u\right\|_{\left({L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}})}\right)^{\otimes j}\otimes\mathbb{K}}^{p}\bigr]\Big)^{1/p}\,.

Then
\mathbb{D}^{k,p}({\mathbb{K}})
is the completion of
{\cal P}_{\mathbb{K}}
under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{k,p}
.

3
Rates of convergence for a spatio-temporal discretization of problem SLQ

3.1
The discrete maximum principle and main results

In this part, we discretize the original problem
SLQ
within two steps,
starting with its semi-discretization in space (which is referred to as
SLQ
h
), which is then followed by a
discretization in space and time (which is referred to as
SLQ
hτ
).
Our goal in this section is to prove rates of convergence in both
cases. By
[
13
]
, problem
SLQ
is uniquely solvable, and its optimal pair
(X^{*},U^{*})
may be
characterized by the following coupled
FBSPDE
(supplemented by homogeneous Dirichlet data for
X^{*},Y
) with a unique solution

(X^{*},Y,Z,U^{*})
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\rm d}X^{*}(t)=\big[\Delta X^{*}(t)+U^{*}(t)\big]{\rm d}t+\big[X^{*}(t)+\sigma(t)\big]{\rm d}W(t)\qquad\forall\,t\in(0,T)\,,\\
&{\rm d}Y(t)=\big[-\Delta Y(t)-Z(t)+X^{*}(t)\big]{\rm d}t+Z(t){\rm d}W(t)\qquad\forall\,t\in(0,T)\,,\\
&X^{*}(0)=X_{0}\,,\qquad Y(T)=-\alpha X^{*}(T)\,,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

with the condition

U^{*}(t)-Y(t)=0\qquad\forall\,t\in(0,T)\,.

(3.2)

By (
3.2
) and the fact that
Y\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}))
, we find that the optimal control
U^{*}
has continuous paths, taking zero values on the boundary
\partial D
.

The spatial semi-discretization
SLQ
h
of problem
SLQ
reads as follows: Find an optimal pair

(X_{h}^{*},U^{*}_{h})\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{V}}_{h}))\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{V}}_{h})
that minimizes the functional

{\cal J}(X_{h},U_{h})=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}\|X_{h}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\|U_{h}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,{\rm d}t\Bigr]+\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|X_{h}(T)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\bigr]\,,

(3.3)

subject to the equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm d}X_{h}(t)=\big[\Delta_{h}X_{h}(t)+U_{h}(t)\big]\,{\rm d}t+\big[X_{h}(t)+{\cal R}_{h}\sigma(t)\big]{\rm d}W(t)&\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,,\\
X_{h}(0)={\cal R}_{h}X_{0}\,.\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

Note the use of the Ritz projection, instead of the
{\mathbb{L}}^{2}
-projection, which will be useful in Section
3.3
.
The existence of a unique optimal pair
(X_{h}^{*},U^{*}_{h})
follows from
[
22
]
,
thanks to its characterization via Pontryagin’s maximum principle,
i.e.
,

0=U^{*}_{h}(t)-Y_{h}(t)\qquad\forall\,t\in(0,T)\,,

(3.5)

where the adjoint
(Y_{h},Z_{h})\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{V}}_{h})\bigr)\times L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(0,T;{\mathbb{V}}_{h}\bigr)
solves the
BSPDE
h

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm d}Y_{h}(t)=\big[-\Delta_{h}Y_{h}(t)-Z_{h}(t)+X^{*}_{h}(t)\big]{\rm d}t+Z_{h}(t){\rm d}W(t)&\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,,\\
Y_{h}(T)=-\alpha X^{*}_{h}(T)\,.\end{array}\right.

(3.6)

In
[
8
]
, error estimates have been obtained for

(X_{h}^{*},Y_{h},Z_{h})
with the help of a fixed point argument — which crucially exploits
T>0
to be
sufficiently small
. One main goal in this work is to derive
corresponding estimates for
(X_{h}^{*},Y_{h},Z_{h},U_{h}^{*})
for
arbitrary

T>0
via a variational argument which
exploits properties of the cost functional
{\cal J}
in (
3.3
):
once an estimate for
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\int_{0}^{T}\|U^{*}(s)-U^{*}_{h}(s)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,{\rm d}s\bigr]
stands,
we use the results from Sections
2.2
and
2.3
to derive estimates for
the remaining processes in
(X_{h}^{*},Y_{h},Z_{h},U_{h}^{*})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under Assumption
(A)
, let
(X^{*},U^{*})
be the solution to problem
SLQ
, and
(X^{*}_{h},U^{*}_{h})
solve
problem
SLQ
h
.
Then, there exists
C\equiv C(X_{0},\sigma,T)>0
independent of
h>0
such that

\displaystyle{\rm(i)}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}\|U^{*}(t)-U_{h}^{*}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]\leq Ch^{2}\,,

\displaystyle{\rm(ii)}

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|X^{*}(t)-X_{h}^{*}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}\|X^{*}(t)-X_{h}^{*}(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]\leq Ch^{2}\,,

\displaystyle{\rm(iii)}

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|Y(t)-Y_{h}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]+\int_{0}^{T}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\|Y(t)-Y_{h}(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\|Z(t)-Z_{h}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\Bigr]\,\mathrm{d}t\leq Ch^{2}\,.

We postpone its proof to Section
3.2
. —
In a second step, we propose a temporal discretization of problem
SLQ
h
which will be analyzed in
Section
3.4
. For this purpose, we use
a mesh

covering

, and consider step size processes
(X_{h\tau},U_{h\tau})\in{\mathbb{X}}_{h\tau}\times{\mathbb{U}}_{h\tau}\subset L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigl(0,T;\mathbb{V}_{h}\bigr)\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigl(0,T;\mathbb{V}_{h}\bigr)
, where

\displaystyle{\mathbb{X}}_{h\tau}

\displaystyle:=\left\{X\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{V}_{h}):\,X(t)=X(t_{n}),\,\,\forall t\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}),\,\,n=0,1,\cdots,\,N-1\right\}\,,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{U}}_{h\tau}

\displaystyle:=\left\{U\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{V}_{h}):\,U(t)=U(t_{n}),\,\,\forall t\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}),\,\,n=0,1,\cdots,\,N-1\right\}\,,

and define for any

\displaystyle\|X\|_{\mathbb{X}_{h\tau}}:=\Big(\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\big[\|X(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big]\Big)^{1/2}\,,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|U\|_{\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}}:=\Big(\tau\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|U(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big]\Big)^{1/2}.

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\tau}(X_{h\tau},U_{h\tau})=\frac{1}{2}\big[\|X_{h\tau}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{X}_{h\tau}}+\|U_{h\tau}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}}^{2}\big]+\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|X_{h\tau}(T)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]\,,

(3.7)

subject to a forward difference equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &X_{h\tau}(t_{n+1})-X_{h\tau}(t_{n})=\tau\big[\Delta_{h}X_{h\tau}(t_{n+1})+U_{h\tau}(t_{n})\big]+\big[X_{h\tau}(t_{n})+{\cal R}_{h}\sigma(t_{n})\big]\Delta_{n+1}W\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad n=0,1,\cdots,N-1\,,\\
&X_{h\tau}(0)={\cal R}_{h}X_{0}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.8)

The following result states a (discrete) Pontryagin-type maximum principle for the uniquely solvable problem

hτ
. We mention the appearing
mapping

in (

)
2

In the sequel, this principle is
used to verify

towards

.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle A_{0}:=\left(\mathds{1}-\tau\Delta_{h}\right)^{-1}
.
The unique optimal pair
(X^{*}_{h\tau},U^{*}_{h\tau})\in{\mathbb{X}}_{h\tau}\times{\mathbb{U}}_{h\tau}
of
problem

hτ
solves the following equalities for
n=0,1,\cdots,N-1
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} X_{h\tau}^{*}(t_{n+1})=&A_{0}^{n+1}\prod_{j=1}^{n+1}\left(1+\Delta_{j}W\right)X_{h\tau}({0})+\tau\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{0}^{n+1-j}\prod_{k=j+2}^{n+1}\left(1+\Delta_{k}W\right)U_{h\tau}^{*}(t_{j})\\
&+\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{0}^{n+1-j}\prod_{k=j+2}^{n+1}\left(1+\Delta_{k}W\right){\cal R}_{h}\sigma(t_{j})\Delta_{j+1}W\,,\\
\left(K_{h\tau}X^{*}_{h\tau}\right)(t_{n}):=&-\tau\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{j=n+1}^{N}A_{0}^{j-n}\prod_{k={n+2}}^{j}(1+\Delta_{k}W)X^{*}_{h\tau}(t_{j})\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}\Big]\\
&-\alpha\mathbb{E}\Big[A_{0}^{N-n}\prod_{k=n+2}^{N}(1+\Delta_{k}W)X^{*}_{h\tau}(T)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}\Big]\,,\\
X^{*}_{h\tau}(0)=&{\cal R}_{h}X_{0}\,,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.9)

together with the discrete optimality condition

U^{*}_{h\tau}(t_{n})-\left(K_{h\tau}X^{*}_{h\tau}\right)(t_{n})=0\,.

(3.10)

We verify this characterization of the unique minimizer of problem

in Section

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{J}}:L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,T;{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\qquad\mbox{via}\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{J}}(U)={\mathcal{J}}\bigl({\cal S}(U),U\bigr)\,,

Lemma 3.5
.

For every
U\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{L}^{2}})
, the Fréchet derivative

D\widehat{\cal J}(U)
is a bounded operator on
L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,T;{\mathbb{L}^{2}})

which takes the form

D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}(U)=U-{\cal T}^{1}\bigl({\cal S}(U)\bigr)\,.

(3.13)

Proof.

By (
2.3
), the stability of
SPDE
(
3.1
)
1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|{\cal S}^{0}(V)(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\bigr]\leq C\|V\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}\qquad\forall~t\in[0,T],\,V\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})\,.

Hence, for any
U,\,V\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})
, applying the fact
{\cal S}(U+V)={\cal S}(U)+{\cal S}^{0}(V)
, we can get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\widehat{\cal J}(U+V)-\widehat{\cal J}(U)\\
&\quad-\Big[({\cal S}(U),{\cal S}^{0}(V))_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}+\bigl((\alpha{\cal S}(U)(T)),{\cal S}^{0}(V)(T)\bigr)_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{2})}+(U,V)_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}\Big]\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\big\|{\cal S}^{0}(V)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}+\|V\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}+\alpha\big\|{\cal S}^{0}(V)(T)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{2})}\Big].\end{aligned}

On the other side, Itô’s formula to
({\cal S}^{0}(V),{\cal T}^{1}({\cal S}(U)))_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}
yields to

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bigl({\cal S}(U),{\cal S}^{0}(V)\bigr)_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}+\bigl(\alpha{\cal S}(U)(T),{\cal S}^{0}(V)(T)\bigr)_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{2})}=-\bigl({\cal T}^{1}\left({\cal S}(U)\right),V\bigr)_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}.\end{aligned}

Subsequently, by the definition of Fréchet derivative and above three inequalities,
we prove the desired
result.

By the unique solvability property of (

), we associate to this equation the bounded solution operator

(3.14)

where

is defined in (

).

) may be written as

, where

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{1}_{h}:L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\big(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{V}}_{h})\big)\rightarrow L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\big(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{V}}_{h})\big)\,,\quad{\cal T}^{2}_{h}:L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\big(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{V}}_{h})\big)\rightarrow L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,T;{\mathbb{V}}_{h})\,.

We are now in a position to prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

For every
U_{h}\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,T;{{\mathbb{V}}_{h}})
, the Fréchet derivative

{D\widehat{\cal J}_{h}(U_{h})}
is a bounded operator (uniformly in
h
) on
L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,T;\mathbb{V}_{h})
, and has the form

D{\widehat{\cal J}}_{h}(U_{h})=U_{h}-{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\bigl({\mathcal{S}}_{h}(U_{h})\bigr)\,,

(3.15)

which can be deduced by the similar procedure as that in Lemma
3.5
.
Let
U_{h}\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,T;{{\mathbb{V}}_{h}})
be arbitrary; it is due to the quadratic structure of the reduced functional (

) that

\bigl(D^{2}\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h}(U_{h})R_{h},R_{h}\bigr)_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}\geq\|R_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{L}}^{2})}\quad\forall\,R_{h}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{V}_{h})\,.

As a consequence, on putting
R_{h}=U^{*}_{h}-\Pi_{h}U^{*}
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|U^{*}_{h}-\Pi_{h}U^{*}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{L}}^{2})}^{2}\leq\big(D^{2}\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h}(U_{h})(U^{*}_{h}-\Pi_{h}U^{*}),U^{*}_{h}-\Pi_{h}U^{*}\big)_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}\\
&\qquad=\bigl(D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h}(U^{*}_{h}),U^{*}_{h}-\Pi_{h}U^{*}\bigr)_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}-\bigl(D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h}(\Pi_{h}U^{*}),U^{*}_{h}-\Pi_{h}U^{*}\bigr)_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}\,.\end{aligned}

Note that
D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h}(U^{*}_{h})=0
by (
3.5
), as well as
D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}(U^{*})=0
by (

\displaystyle NU^{*}_{h\tau}+H({{\cal R}_{h}X_{0}},f)=0\,.

Therefore, for any

such that

,

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\tau}(X_{h\tau},U_{h\tau})-{\cal J}_{\tau}(X^{*}_{h\tau},U^{*}_{h\tau})=\frac{1}{2}\big(N(U_{h\tau}-U^{*}_{h\tau}),U_{h\tau}-U^{*}_{h\tau}\big)_{L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}>0\,,

which means that

is the unique optimal control, and
(X^{*}_{h\tau},U^{*}_{h\tau})
is the unique optimal pair.

Finally, by the definition of

, and properties (
3.41
) and
(
3.38

\displaystyle 0=NU^{*}_{h\tau}+H({{\cal R}_{h}X_{0}})=U^{*}_{h\tau}+\big[L^{*}X^{*}_{h\tau}+\alpha\widehat{L}^{*}X^{*}_{h\tau}(T)\big]\,.

By the definition of

in (

)
2
, we can arrive at

\displaystyle L^{*}X^{*}_{h\tau}+\alpha\widehat{L}^{*}X^{*}_{h\tau}(T)=-K_{h\tau}X^{*}_{h\tau}\,.

Then (
3.10

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\left\|Y_{h}(t;{\cal S}_{h\tau}(\Pi_{\tau}U^{*}_{h}))-Y_{h}(t_{n};{\cal S}_{h\tau}(\Pi_{\tau}U^{*}_{h}))\right\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq C\tau\left[\|X_{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\sigma\|^{2}_{C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{2})}\right]\,.

4
The gradient descent method to solve problem
SLQ
hτ

By Theorem
3.2
, solving problem
SLQ
hτ
is
equivalent to solving the system of the coupled forward-backward difference equations
(
3.9
) and (
3.10
). We may exploit the variational
character of problem
SLQ
hτ
to construct a gradient descent method
where approximate iterates of the optimal control
U^{*}_{h\tau}
in the
Hilbert space
\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}
are obtained; see also
[
14
,
12
]
for more details.

Algorithm 4.1
.

Let
U_{h\tau}^{(0)}\in\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}
, and fix
\kappa>0
. For any
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, update
U_{h\tau}^{(\ell)}\in{\mathbb{U}}_{h\tau}
as follows:

1.

Compute
X_{h\tau}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{X}_{h\tau}
by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &[\mathds{1}-\tau\Delta_{h}]X^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n+1})=X^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n})+\tau U^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n})+\big[X^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n})+{\cal R}_{h}\sigma(t_{n})\big]\Delta_{n+1}W\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad n=0,1,\cdots,N-1\,,\\
&X_{h\tau}^{(\ell)}(0)={\cal R}_{h}X_{0}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

2.

Use
X_{h\tau}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{X}_{h\tau}
to compute
Y_{h\tau}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{X}_{h\tau}
via

\displaystyle Y_{h\tau}^{(\ell)}(t_{n})=

\displaystyle-\tau\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{j=n+1}^{N}A_{0}^{j-n}\prod_{k={n+2}}^{j}(1+\Delta_{k}W)X^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{j})\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}\Big]

\displaystyle-\alpha\mathbb{E}\Big[A_{0}^{N-n}\prod_{k=n+2}^{N}(1+\Delta_{k}W)X^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(T)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}\Big]\quad n=0,1,\cdots,N-1\,.

3.

Update
U_{h\tau}^{(\ell+1)}\in{\mathbb{U}}_{h\tau}
via

\displaystyle U^{(\ell+1)}_{h\tau}=U^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}-\frac{1}{\kappa}\big[U^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}-Y_{h\tau}^{(\ell)}\big]\,.

If compared with (
3.9
)-(
3.10
), steps 1 and 2 are now decoupled: the first step requires to solve a space-time discretization of
SPDE
h
(
3.1
)
1
, while the second requires to solve a space-time discretization of the
BSPDE
(
3.1
)
2
which is not the numerical solution by the implicit Euler method (see Lemmas
3.10
and
3.11
for the difference).
A similar method to solve problem
SLQ
hτ

has been proposed in
[
8
,
16
]
.
We refer to related works on how to approximate conditional expectations
(e.g.
[
3
,
10
,
1
,
18
,
17
]
).

In the below,
we tend to present a lower bound for
\kappa
and show convergence rate of Algorithm
4.1
.
For this purpose, we first recall Lipschitz continuity of

D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h\tau}
.
By the definition of Fréchet derivative,

D^{2}\widehat{\cal J}_{h\tau}(U_{h\tau})=\big[\mathds{1}+L^{*}L+\alpha\widehat{L}^{*}\widehat{L}\big]U_{h\tau}\,,

where operators
L,\,\widehat{L}
are defined in (
3.38
)-(
3.39
). Next, we can find
K:=\|\mathds{1}+L^{*}L+\alpha\widehat{L}^{*}\widehat{L}\|_{{\cal L}(\mathbb{U}_{h\tau};\mathbb{U}_{h\tau})}
, such that

\displaystyle\|D\widehat{\cal J}_{h\tau}\left(U^{1}_{h\tau}\right)-D\widehat{\cal J}_{h\tau}\left(U^{2}_{h\tau}\right)\|_{\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}}\leq K\left\|U^{1}_{h\tau}-U^{2}_{h\tau}\right\|_{\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}}\,.

Indeed,
noting that
\|(\mathds{1}-\tau\Delta_{h})^{-1}\|_{{\cal L}(\mathbb{U}_{h\tau},\mathbb{U}_{h\tau})}\leq 1
,
we find that

\displaystyle\|LU_{h\tau}\|_{\mathbb{X}_{h\tau}}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau\mathbb{E}\|\tau\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left[(\mathds{1}-\tau\Delta_{h})^{-1}\right]^{n-j}\prod_{k=j+2}^{n}\left(1+\Delta_{k}W\right)U_{h\tau}(t_{j})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq T^{2}e^{T}\|U_{h\tau}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}}^{2}\,.

In a similar vein, we can prove that

\displaystyle\|\widehat{L}U_{h\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}\leq Te^{T}\|U_{h\tau}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}}^{2}\,.

Hence

\displaystyle K=\|\mathds{1}+L^{*}L+\alpha\widehat{L}^{*}\widehat{L}\|_{{\cal L}(\mathbb{U}_{h\tau};\mathbb{U}_{h\tau})}\leq 1+\alpha Te^{T}+T^{2}e^{T}\,.

Since Algorithm
4.1
is the gradient descent method for
SLQ
hτ
, we have the following estimates.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
\kappa\geq K
.
Let
\displaystyle\{U^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}\}_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\subset{\mathbb{U}}_{h\tau}
be generated by Algorithm
4.1
, and
U^{*}_{h\tau}
solve
SLQ
hτ
. Then for
\ell=1,2,\cdots
,

\displaystyle{\rm(i)}

\displaystyle\|U^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}-U^{*}_{h\tau}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}}^{2}\leq\Big(1-\frac{1}{\kappa}\Big)^{\ell}\|U^{(0)}_{h\tau}-U^{*}_{h\tau}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}}^{2}\,;

\displaystyle{\rm(ii)}

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal J}_{h\tau}(U^{(\ell)}_{h\tau})-\widehat{\cal J}_{h\tau}(U^{*}_{h\tau})\leq\frac{2\kappa\|U^{(0)}_{h\tau}-U^{*}_{h\tau}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}}^{2}}{\ell}\,;

\displaystyle{\rm(iii)}

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq n\leq N}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|X_{h\tau}^{*}(t_{n})-X^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]+\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|X^{*}_{h\tau}(t_{n})-X^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\Big(1-\frac{1}{\kappa}\Big)^{\ell}\|U^{(0)}_{h\tau}-U^{*}_{h\tau}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}}^{2}\,,

where
C
is independent of
h,\,\tau,\,\ell
.

Proof.

The estimates (i) and (ii) are standard for the gradient descent method (see e.g.
[
14
, Theorem 1.2.4]
); more details can also be found in
[
16
, Section 5]
. In the following, we restrict to assertion (iii).

For all
n=0,1,\cdots,N
, define
\bar{e}_{X}^{n,\ell}=X^{*}_{h\tau}(t_{n})-X^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n})
. Subtracting (
4.1
)
from (
3.8
) (where
U_{h\tau}=U^{*}_{h\tau}
) leads to

\displaystyle\bar{e}_{X}^{n+1,\ell}-\bar{e}_{X}^{n,\ell}=

\displaystyle\tau\Delta_{h}\bar{e}_{X}^{n+1,\ell}+\bar{e}_{X}^{n,\ell}\Delta_{n+1}W+\tau\big[U_{h\tau}^{*}(t_{n})-U^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n})\big]\,.

Multiplication with
e_{X}^{n+1,\ell}
, and then taking expectations, as well as applying Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we arrive at

\displaystyle(1-\tau)\mathbb{E}\big[\|\bar{e}_{X}^{n+1,\ell}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\big]+2\tau\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla\bar{e}_{X}^{n+1,\ell}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq(1+\tau)\mathbb{E}\big[\|\bar{e}_{X}^{n,\ell}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\big]+\tau\mathbb{E}\big[\|U_{h\tau}^{*}(t_{n})-U^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big]\,.

By the discrete Gronwall’s inequality, then taking the sum over all
0\leq n\leq N-1

,
we find that

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\bar{e}_{X}^{n,\ell}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla\bar{e}_{X}^{n,\ell}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]\leq C\tau\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|U_{h\tau}^{*}(t_{n})-U^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]\,,

which, together with (i), imply assertion (iii).

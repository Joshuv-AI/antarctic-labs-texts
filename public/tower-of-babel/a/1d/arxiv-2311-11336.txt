Error estimate of a quasi-Monte Carlo time-splitting pseudospectral method for nonlinear Schrödinger equation with random potentials
Zhizhang Wu, Zhiwen Zhang, Xiaofei Zhao

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the numerical solution of a nonlinear Schrödinger equation with spatial random potential. The randomly shifted quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) lattice rule combined with the time-splitting pseudospectral discretization is applied and analyzed. The nonlinearity in the equation induces difficulties in estimating the regularity of the solution in random space. By the technique of weighted Sobolev space, we identify the possible weights and show the existence of QMC that converges optimally at the almost-linear rate without dependence on dimensions. The full error estimate of the scheme is established. We present numerical results to verify the accuracy and investigate the wave propagation. Keywords: nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation; random potential; quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) method; time splitting; pseudospectral method; error estimate. AMS Subject Classification: 65C30; 65D32; 65M15; 65M70; 82B44.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following one-space-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation with a spatial random potential on the torus:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}\psi=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\psi+V(\omega,x)\psi+\alpha|\psi|^{2}\psi,\quad x\in{\mathbb{T}},\ \omega\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
&\psi(t=0,\omega,x)=\psi_{in}(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{T}},\ \omega\in\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Here
t\geq 0
is the time variable,
x\in{\mathbb{T}}
is the space variable with
{\mathbb{T}}
the one-dimensional torus (periodic boundary condition in
x
),
\omega\in\Omega
is the random sample with
\Omega
the sample space,
\psi=\psi(t,\omega,x)
is the complex-valued unknown with
\psi_{in}
the given initial data,
V(\omega,x)
is a given real-valued random potential, and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a given parameter describing the strength of the nonlinearity. We refer to the NLS equation (
1.1
) as the random NLS.

It is well known that in the one-space-dimensional case in the presence of a random potential and in the absence of nonlinearity (i.e.,
\alpha=0
) with probability one all the states are exponentially localized
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
17
,
38
]
. With
\alpha=0
the equation in (
1.1
) is a linear Schrödinger equation with random potentials, which belongs to the family of the Anderson models named after P. Anderson. In 1958, P. Anderson considered the discrete linear Schrödinger equations and discovered the localization of waves due to the disorder induced by the random potential
[
3
]
. Ever since then, the Anderson localization effect has been widely considered in many applications such as semiconductors and acoustic waves.

When
\alpha\neq 0
, the interplay between disorder and nonlinear effects leads to new interesting physics.
In spite of the extensive research, many fundamental problems still remains open. A long-lasting question in mathematics and physics is whether a nonlinearity especially the defocusing nonlinearity, i.e.,
\alpha>0
in (
1.1
), could break the localization
[
19
]
. Many analytical and numerical investigations have been done to address this question. The study of the long-time behavior of wave propagation so far still relies on numerical simulations, which are performed on the discrete NLS models
[
18
,
44
]
. Our model problem (
1.1
) is the continuous version of the random NLS. It has been considered physically for modeling the Anderson localization of Bose-Einstein Condensates
[
40
,
45
]
and for the study of nonlinear dispersive wave dynamics in a disordered medium
[
9
,
22
,
33
]
. For theoretical results, we refer readers to
[
13
,
14
]
for the well-posedness results of (
1.1
) and to
[
15
,
20
,
27
]
for other related mathematical properties. Here we consider the one-space-dimensional case and the torus domain in (
1.1
) for simplicity.

In this work, we consider the parametrization of the random potential in the manner of the Karhunen-Loève expansion
[
25
,
26
,
37
,
54
]
:

\displaystyle V(\omega,x)=v_{0}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\xi_{j}(\omega)v_{j}(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{T}},\ \omega\in\Omega,

(1.2)

where
v_{0}(x)
is a deterministic function,
\{v_{j}(x)\}_{j\geq 1}
are the physical components,
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\cdots>0
are the corresponding strengths and
\bm{\xi}(\omega)=(\xi_{1}(\omega),\xi_{2}(\omega),\ldots)^{T}
are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) uniform random variables on
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]
.
Thus,

\bm{\xi}\in\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{\mathbb{N}}=:U.

According to the Doob-Dynkin lemma
[
32
]
, the solution
\psi
can be represented by parametric functions parameterized by
\bm{\xi}
. Hence, (
1.1
) can be rewritten as the following parametric nonlinear Schrödinger equation with a random potential
V(\bm{\xi},x)
given by (
1.2
):

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}\psi=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\psi+V(\bm{\xi},x)\psi+\alpha|\psi|^{2}\psi,\quad x\in{\mathbb{T}},\ \bm{\xi}\in U,\ t>0,\\
&\psi(t=0,\bm{\xi},x)=\psi_{in}(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{T}},\ \bm{\xi}\in U,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

with
\psi=\psi(t,\bm{\xi},x)
,
\psi_{in}
being deterministic, and
we shall consider that
\alpha\neq 0
.

Along the numerical aspect of (
1.1
) or (
1.3
), let us mention several related works. The work of Henning and Peterseim
[
29
]
considered the fully deterministic case of (
1.1
) and addressed the convergence issue of a Crank-Nicolson finite element discretization. Zhao later addressed the corresponding convergence of an exponential integrator spectral method in
[
55
]
and numerically investigated the stochastic case. The work of Kachman, Fishman and Soffer
[
33
]
proposed an iterative integrator in time for the linear model. However, to our best knowledge, the existing studies on (
1.1
) so far are yet to address the discretization in the random variable. The sampling method in the aforementioned works as well as in the physical works
[
18
,
40
,
44
]
all consider the classical Monte-Carlo method. It is known that the classical Monte-Carlo offers only a half-order convergence rate and so practical computing would require a large number of samples for accurately capturing the statistical quantities of interest. To increase the efficiency and accuracy of quantifying the uncertainty, methods like the quasi-Monte Carlo sampling
[
8
,
12
,
53
]
, polynomial chaos expansion
[
10
,
21
,
30
,
54
]
and stochastic collocation method
[
6
,
42
,
52
]
have later been developed for PDEs with random inputs.

In this work, we shall consider the quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) sampling method for the NLS (
1.3
). The origin of QMC dates back to around 1960, which proposes to sample deterministically by using the low-discrepancy sequences
[
28
,
48
]
.
The convergence order of QMC with respect to the number of samples is higher than that of the classical Monte Carlo method; however, it is dependent on the dimension of the random variable
[
8
,
41
]
. To overcome this problem, some state-of-art randomization techniques of different kinds have been further introduced to the QMC. We refer the interested readers to
[
12
,
36
]
and the references therein for a detailed review. In this work, we would follow the randomly shifted QMC lattice rule
[
11
,
43
,
47
]
. In such a way, Graham, Kuo, Schwab and Sloan et al. proposed the QMC method with finite element discretizations to solve linear elliptic equations
[
23
,
25
,
26
,
37
]
. Via the technique of the weighted Hilbert space with the carefully chosen weights
[
25
,
37
]
, they established the almost-linear convergence rates in
N
without dependence on the dimension.

By combining the randomly shifted QMC lattice rule with the popular time-splitting Fourier pseudospectral (TSFP) method
[
4
,
7
,
31
]
, we propose an efficient numerical method to solve (
1.3
). Moreover, we aim to analyze for the full convergence result of the scheme. The difficulty/novelty of the analysis mainly come from the following facts: i) the nonlinearity in the equation (
1.3
) makes it complicated to estimate of the regularity of the solution in the parametric space; ii) the physical observable of interest is a nonlinear functional of the solution; iii) the full scheme involves the discretizations in time, physical space and parametric space. Under suitable assumptions on the decaying rate of the potential (
1.2
), we point out the possible choice of the weights in the framework of the weighted Sobolev space, and we show the existence of a randomly shifted QMC lattice rule which can achieve an almost-linear convergence rate without dependence on dimensions for the expectation of the physical observable. The optimal root-mean-square error estimate of the QMC-TSFP scheme in time, physical space, and random space will then be established and verified numerically. Finally, we numerically investigate the wave propagation in the random NLS models.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present the detailed QMC-TSFP scheme and our main result about its convergence theorem. Section
3
and Section
4
are devoted to rigorously establishing the theorem by analyzing the error on the PDE and on the scheme in a sequel. Numerical results are presented in Section
5
. Concluding remarks are made in Section
6
.

2.
Numerical method

In this section, we shall first present the numerical discretization of the NLS (
1.3
). Then, we shall give the convergence result of the scheme.

2.1.
Dimension truncation and discretization

First of all, for practical computation we need to truncate the Karhunen-Loève expansion (
1.2
) of the random potential
V(\bm{\xi},x)
into a finite sum. That is to say,
we choose an integer
m>0
large enough and truncate the parameterized random potential as:

\displaystyle V_{m}(\bm{\xi},x)=v_{0}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\xi_{j}(\omega)v_{j}(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{T}},\ {\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})^{T}}\in U_{m}:=\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{m}.

(2.1)

We emphasize that the approximation of the truncated random potential
V_{m}(\bm{\xi},x)
to
V(\bm{\xi},x)
depends on the decaying rates of the strengths. With the above finite dimensional random potential, we consider the following truncated NLS problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}\psi_{m}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\psi_{m}+V_{m}(\xi,x)\psi_{m}+\alpha|\psi_{m}|^{2}\psi_{m},\quad x\in{\mathbb{T}},\ \xi\in U_{m},\ t>0,\\
&\psi_{m}(t=0,\xi,x)=\psi_{in}(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{T}},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.2)

which is an approximation to (
1.3
).
Here we have
\psi_{m}(t,\xi,x)=\psi(t,(\bm{\xi}_{\{1:m\}},\mathbf{0}),x)
with
\xi=\bm{\xi}_{\{1:m\}}
.

With one precise sample for
\xi
, (
2.2
) reads as a cubic NLS with the given potential
V_{m}
, and so it can be numerically solved by any of the classical algorithms. Concerning the periodic boundary condition, we shall consider in this paper the time-splitting Fourier pseudospectral method, which is simple to begin with but undoubtedly one of the most popular numerical methods for NLS
[
7
,
31
,
49
]
. It begins by splitting (
2.2
) into two subflows
\Psi^{\mathrm{k}}_{s}
and
\Psi^{\mathrm{p}}_{s}
as

\displaystyle\Psi^{\mathrm{k}}_{s}:i\partial_{t}\psi_{m}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\psi_{m},\quad t\in(0,s];

(2.3a)

\displaystyle\Psi^{\mathrm{p}}_{s}:i\partial_{t}\psi_{m}

\displaystyle=V_{m}\psi_{m}+\alpha|\psi_{m}|^{2}\psi_{m},\quad t\in(0,s].

(2.3b)

Note that
V_{m}
and
\alpha
are real-valued, so both of the above equations can be integrated exactly in time, and then the scheme composes as
\psi_{m}(t_{n+1},\xi,x)\approx\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}\circ\Psi_{\tau}^{\mathrm{p}}\circ\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}(\psi_{m}(t_{n},\xi,x))
, where
\tau=t_{n+1}-t_{n}
is the time step. Let us briefly present the detailed scheme below.

Denote
{\mathbb{T}}=[-L,L]
with some
L>0
and choose some even integer
M>0
. The physical space is discretized as
x_{k}=-L+kh
with
h=2L/M
for
k=0,1,\ldots,M
. For the time axis, we take
\tau=\Delta t>0
and denote
t_{n}=n\tau
for
n=0,1,\ldots
. Denote the numerical solution as
\psi_{m,k}^{n}=\psi_{m,k}^{n}(\xi)\approx\psi_{m}(t_{n},\xi,x_{k})
with
\psi_{m,k}^{0}=\psi_{in}(x_{k})
, the scheme of the
time-splitting Fourier pseudospectral method (TSFP)
for solving (
2.2
) reads for
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\psi_{m,k}^{n+1}=\sum_{l=-M/2}^{M/2-1}\widetilde{(\psi_{m,k}^{n+1})}_{l}\mathrm{e}^{iu_{l}(x_{k}+L)},\quad\widetilde{(\psi_{m,k}^{n+1})}_{l}=\mathrm{e}^{-iu_{l}^{2}\tau/4}\widetilde{(\psi_{m,k}^{**})}_{l},\quad u_{l}=\frac{\pi l}{L},

(2.4a)

\displaystyle\psi_{m,k}^{**}=\mathrm{e}^{-i[V_{m}(\xi,x_{k})+\alpha|\psi_{m,k}|^{2}]\tau}\psi_{m,k}^{*},\quad\widetilde{(\psi_{m,k}^{*})}_{l}=\mathrm{e}^{-iu_{l}^{2}\tau/4}\widetilde{(\psi_{m,k}^{n})}_{l},\quad 0\leq k\leq M,

(2.4b)

with
\widetilde{(\phi)}_{l}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\phi_{k}\mathrm{e}^{-iu_{l}(x_{k}+L)}
being the discrete Fourier coefficients of a discrete function
\phi_{k}
. With the discrete values, we can define a continuous version of the numerical solution as the interpolation:

I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}(x)=\sum_{l=-M/2}^{M/2-1}\widetilde{(\psi_{m,k}^{n})}_{l}\mathrm{e}^{iu_{l}(x+L)},\quad x\in{\mathbb{T}},\ n\geq 0.

(2.5)

The TSFP (
2.4
) is fully explicit in time and efficient with the computational cost
\mathcal{O}(M\log M)
per time level under the help of fast Fourier transform. Moreover, it is time symmetric and preserves the discrete
l^{2}
-norm of the solution. These properties offer it unconditional stability and the reliable long-time performance
[
16
]
. Thus, it has been used in
[
19
,
44
]
to simulate the nonlinear Anderson models. For simulating wave propagation, the torus domain setup for (
1.3
) or (
2.2
) is a valid truncation of the whole space problem provided that the domain size
L>0
is taken large enough and the initially localized solution in
{\mathbb{T}}
is away from the boundary within the time of computation.

2.2.
Quasi-Monte Carlo sampling

With the solution
\psi
of (
1.3
), the physical observables in applications are some functionals of
\psi
, i.e.,
G(\psi(t,\bm{\xi},\cdot)),\ t\geq 0,\ \bm{\xi}\in U,

for some functional
G
in the energy space of the solution.
Then, the expected value with respect to the random parameter is given by the following integral in infinite dimensions:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[G](t):=\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{U_{m}}G(\psi_{m}(t,\xi,\cdot))d\xi,

(2.6)

with the truncated solution
\psi_{m}
of (
2.2
). Therefore, for some sufficiently large
m>0
,
\mathbb{E}[G]
can be approximated by

\mathbb{E}[G](t)\approx\int_{U_{m}}G(\psi_{m}(t,\xi,\cdot))d\xi.

Note that
|\psi|^{2}
represents the probability density function in quantum physics. Many widely concerned physical observables are linear functionals of
|\psi|^{2}
. One typical example is the center of mass, which is frequently considered in the context of Anderson localization
[
19
]
.
Therefore, in the following, we shall focus on the expected value of
G(|\psi|^{2})
, where
G
is a linear functional. In order to estimate the convergence of QMC for computing the expected value of
G(|\psi|^{2})
, we shall analyze the parametric regularity of
G(|\psi|^{2})
or equivalently the parametric regularity of
|\psi|^{2}
in Section
3.3
, which is one of our main contributions in this work. Let us denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t,\bm{\xi}):=G(|\psi(t,\bm{\xi},\cdot)|^{2}),\qquad\mathcal{G}^{m}(t,\xi):=G(|\psi_{m}(t,\xi,\cdot)|^{2}),

(2.7)

for short in the following, and so we look for
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}](t)
.

To evaluate the expectation of a general function
F(\xi)
under a fixed
m
, the strategy of a quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) method is an equal weight quadrature rule, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{m}[F]:=\int_{U_{m}}F(\xi)d\xi\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{p=1}^{N}{F}(\xi^{(p)})=:Q_{m,N}[{F}],

(2.8)

where
\xi^{(p)}\in U_{m}
is the quadrature point and
N>0
is the total number of samples.
Classical QMC is done by constructing some deterministic quadrature points, such as the Halton sequence
[
28
]
or the Sobol sequence
[
48
]
. However, a drawback is that the approximation error of (
2.8
) depends on the dimension
m
. When
m
is large, the QMC might lose its efficiency over the standard Monte Carlo method.

To provide a practical error estimate and reduce the biased error, some randomization techniques have been developed to construct the quadrature points. Here, we adopt the so-called
shifted rank-one lattice rule

[
12
]
to get
\xi^{(p)}
. That is

\xi^{(p)}=\mathrm{frac}\left(\frac{pz}{N}+\Delta\right)-\frac{1}{2},\quad p=1,\ldots,N,

(2.9)

where
\mathrm{frac}(w)
means to take the fractional part of each component of a vector
w
,
\Delta\in[0,1]^{m}
is a uniformly distributed random shift and
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
is known as the generating vector. A specific generating vector
z
can be constructed by the component-by-component construction (CBC) approach
[
12
]
to minimize the shifted-averaged worst-case error function, which will be detailed in our analysis later in Section
3.4
. For every
\xi^{(p)}
, we solve the NLS (
2.2
) using the TSFP scheme (
2.4
). Then, we obtain
\{\psi_{m}^{n}(\xi^{(p)})\}_{p=1}^{N}
to compute the numerical expectation of some quantity of interest. The whole numerical scheme shall be referred to as QMC-TSFP in the rest of the paper. The practical computing of
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}]
via the randomly shifted QMC-TSFP method is then implemented as:

(1)

Given the truncated dimension
m
in (
2.1
), choose
M,\tau
for discretizations in TSFP, and take
N
as the number of samples for one random shift and
R
as the number of random shifts;

(2)

Construct the generating vector
z
using the CBC approach;

(3)

Generate i.i.d. random shifts
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{R}
from the uniform distribution on
[0,1]^{m}
. For each
\Delta_{r}
with
r=1,\ldots,R
, obtain the sample set
\{\xi^{(r,p)}=\mathrm{frac}\left(\frac{pz}{N}+\Delta_{r}\right)-\frac{1}{2}:p=1,\ldots,N\}
;

(4)

For each
\xi^{(r,p)}
with
r=1,\ldots,R
and
p=1,\ldots,N
, solve (
2.2
) via (
2.4
) and obtain
I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}(\xi^{r,p},x)
in (
2.5
);

(5)

Compute
\{Q^{(r)}_{m,N}(G(|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}|^{2})):r=1,\ldots,R\}
, where

\displaystyle Q^{(r)}_{m,N}(G(|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}|^{2}))=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{p=1}^{N}G(|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}(\xi^{(r,p)},\cdot)|^{2})

(2.10)

is the approximation of
\mathbb{E}_{m}[G(|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}|^{2})]
with one single shift
\Delta_{r}
in the QMC rule;

(6)

Take the average over all the random shifts to get

\displaystyle\overline{Q}_{m,N,R}(G(|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}|^{2}))=\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^{R}Q^{(r)}_{m,N}(G(|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}|^{2}))

(2.11)

as the final approximation of
\mathbb{E}[G(|\psi(t_{n},\cdot,\cdot)|^{2})]
.

With a precise shift
\Delta_{r}
, the total computational cost of QMC-TSFP is
\mathcal{O}(NM\log M)
. The number of shifts
R
is usually kept small in practice, e.g.,
10
-
50

[
12
]
.

2.3.
Main result

We shall present here the main convergence result. We first introduce the Bochner spaces we will consider for the potential
V
and the wave functions
\psi
. For the potential
V
, we will consider the Bochner space
L^{\infty}(U;H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}))
with the norm

\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}))}=\sup\limits_{\bm{\xi}\in U}\|V(\bm{\xi})\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})}.

For the wave function
\psi
, we will consider the Bochner space
L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}))
with the norm

\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}))}=\sup\limits_{t\in(0,T),\bm{\xi}\in U}\|\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})}.

Assume that the functional, the potential and the initial data of (
1.3
) satisfy the following conditions.

Assumption 2.1
.

Assume the linear functional
G\in(H^{1}({\mathbb{T}}))^{\prime}
, which is the dual space of
H^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
. For some fixed
s\geq 1
, assume that
\psi_{in}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})
,
V\in L^{\infty}(U;H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}))
and

\|V_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{s})}\leq\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{s})}
for any
m>0
. Concerning the relevant physical context, assume further that
\psi_{in}
is localized in the torus domain
{\mathbb{T}}=[-L,L]
and
L>0
is large enough such that the wave is yet to reach the boundary within the time of interest.

In addition, to achieve the optimal convergence rate of QMC, we would further ask for the following technical assumption on
V
, which shows the property of the decaying rates of the terms in the series (
2.1
).

Assumption 2.2
.

Assume that the potential
V
in the form of (
1.2
) satisfies:

\|V_{m}-V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{1})}\leq Cm^{-\chi},\qquad\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left[3^{j}\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1}}\right]^{1/2}<\infty,

(2.12)

for some constants
C,\chi>0
.

Then, we have the following error estimate result for the QMC-TSFP method.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume that Assumption
2.1
, Assumption
2.2
hold and
\psi_{in}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})
with
s\geq 5
. Then, a randomly shifted QMC lattice rule can be constructed to solve (
2.2
) till any fixed
T>0
, and there exist constants
\tau_{0},h_{0}>0
independent of
m
so that with the numerical solution
I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}
from the QMC-TSFP scheme (
2.4
) for
\tau\leq\tau_{0}
and
h\leq h_{0}
, the root-mean-square error satisfies: for any
\delta\in(0,1/2)
and
N\leq 10^{30}
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{\Delta}\left[\Big|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}](t_{n})-Q_{m,N}[G(|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}|^{2})]\Big|^{2}\right]}\leq C(\tau^{2}+h^{s-1}+m^{-\chi}+N^{\delta-1}),

(2.13)

for all
t_{n}\in[0,T]
and some constant
C>0
independent of
m,\tau,h,N
, where
\mathbb{E}^{\Delta}
denotes the expectation with respect to the random shift.

The rest of the paper is devoted to rigorously establishing the above theorem. It will be done in a sequel by analyzing the QMC error on the PDE in Section
3
and the error of the TSFP scheme in Section
4
.

Remark 1
.

The condition
N\leq 10^{30}
is purely a technical condition since we use in the proof of Theorem
2.3
the property that the Euler totient function
\varphi(N)
satisfies
1/\varphi(N)\leq 9/N
for
N\leq 10^{30}

[
25
]
; see also the proof of Lemma
3.7
. In practice, the number of samples will seldom exceed
10^{30}
.

Remark 2
.

The Assumption
2.2
basically requires that the eigenpairs in (
1.2
) are exponentially decaying, which is stronger than the usual algebraic ones in the literature for linear model problems, e.g.,
[
25
,
37
]
. Such exponential decay assumption is a technical condition for the rigorous proof by means of analysis in the weighted Sobolev space. It is essentially raised by the nonlinearity. In practice, it could be satisfied if the random potential
V(\bm{\xi},x)
is a smooth and periodic (or Schwartz for the whole space case) function in terms of
x
uniformly for all
\bm{\xi}
. Generally, the decaying speed of the eigenvalues depend on the regularity of the covarivance kernel. We refer interested readers to
[
46
]
for more details.

Remark 3
.

Under weaker assumptions than those in Theorem
2.3
, weaker convergence rates can be established similarly. For instance, if we only have
s\geq 3
then the temporal accuracy of TSFP will drop down to one (see
[
39
]
for the deterministic case). On the other hand, if the decaying rate in Assumption
2.2
is slower, then the sampling error from QMC might drop down to
1/2

[
37
]
. Here we omitted these cases for simplicity.

Remark 4
.

The physical context of Anderson localization in NLS is associated with many physical parameters, including the time interval, spatial domain size, strength of nonlinearity, and randomness of the potential. Our primary focus in Theorem
2.3
is on the convergence rates with respect to numerical parameters. The dependence of the error estimate on physical parameters is encapsulated in the error constant
C>0
in (
2.13
). Determining the explicit and particularly optimal dependence of the constant
C
on these physical parameters requires substantial additional efforts and will be a subject of our future research.

3.
QMC on the PDE

In this section, we aim to analyze the convergence of the QMC sampling for the NLS model (
1.3
) or (
2.2
) on the continuous level. We shall largely follow the framework in
[
37
]
.

3.1.
Regularity in physical space

Lemma 3.1
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, for each
\bm{\xi}\in U
the solution
\psi(t,\bm{\xi},x)
of (
1.3
) is globally well-posed and uniformly bounded in
H^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
. Moreover, for any fixed
0<T<\infty
, we have
\psi\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}))
.

Proof.

Following the framework from
[
24
]
, let us briefly go through the proof to check mainly the effect from the random potential. For simplicity, we shall omit the spatial variable
x
in the functions.
By the Duhamel formula, (
1.3
) reads

\psi(t,\bm{\xi})=\mathrm{e}^{it\partial_{x}^{2}/2}\psi_{in}-i\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{i(t-\rho)\partial_{x}^{2}/2}\left[V(\bm{\xi})\psi(\rho,\bm{\xi})+\alpha|\psi(\rho,\bm{\xi})|^{2}\psi(\rho,\bm{\xi})\right]d\rho,\quad t\geq 0.

(3.1)

Note that under condition
s\geq 1
, we have the algebraic property of the Sobolev space
H^{s}
in the 1D
[
5
, Chapter 4]
. Therefore, by taking the
H^{s}
-norm on both sides of (
3.1
), we get

\|\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|\psi_{in}\|_{H^{s}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\left[\|V(\bm{\xi})\|_{H^{s}}\|\psi(\rho,\bm{\xi})\|_{H^{s}}+\|\psi(\rho,\bm{\xi})\|_{H^{s}}^{3}\right]ds,

for some constant
C>0
from the Sobolev embedding which depends only on
s
. Then, a bootstrap-type argument
[
51
]
will give the local well-posedness of (
1.3
) in
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})
for each
\bm{\xi}
(see Appendix
A
for details of the bootstrap-type argument). Moreover, there exists a
T_{0}>0
such that
\psi\in L^{\infty}((0,T_{0})\times U;H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}))
owning to the fact that
\psi_{in}
is deterministic and the assumption
V\in L^{\infty}(U;H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}))
.

On the other hand, since
V
is real-valued, the model (
1.3
) enjoys the mass conservation

\displaystyle M(t):=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}|\psi(t,\bm{\xi},x)|^{2}dx\equiv\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}|\psi_{in}(x)|^{2}dx=M(0),\quad t\geq 0,

(3.2)

where the mass is independent of
\bm{\xi}
. Also, we have the energy conservation:

\displaystyle E(t,\bm{\xi}):=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left[\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}\psi(t,\bm{\xi},x)|^{2}+V(\bm{\xi},x)|\psi(t,\bm{\xi},x)|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|\psi(t,\bm{\xi},x)|^{4}\right]dx

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left[\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}\psi_{in}(x)|^{2}+V(\bm{\xi},x)|\psi_{in}(x)|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|\psi_{in}(x)|^{4}\right]dx=E(0,\bm{\xi}),\quad t\geq 0,\ \bm{\xi}\in U.

(3.3)

Note by the Sobolev embedding that
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})\subseteq L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
, then the condition
V\in L^{\infty}(U;H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}))
guarantees a uniform upper bound of the initial energy for all the
\bm{\xi}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle|E(0,\bm{\xi})|\leq\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left[\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}\psi_{in}(x)|^{2}+\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U\times{\mathbb{T}})}|\psi_{in}(x)|^{2}+\frac{|\alpha|}{2}|\psi_{in}(x)|^{4}\right]dx=:E_{0}<\infty,\quad\bm{\xi}\in U.

(3.4)

In the defocusing case, i.e.,
\alpha>0
, it can be seen from the definition of
E(t,\bm{\xi})
in (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle E(t,\bm{\xi})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left[\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}\psi(t,\bm{\xi},x)|^{2}+V(\bm{\xi},x)|\psi(t,\bm{\xi},x)|^{2}\right]dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U\times{\mathbb{T}})}\|\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(3.5)

which together with the mass conservation (
3.2
), the energy conservation (
3.1
) and the bound of
E(0,\bm{\xi})
in (
3.4
) shows that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2|E(t,\bm{\xi})|+2\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U\times{\mathbb{T}})}\|\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2E_{0}+2\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U\times{\mathbb{T}})}\|\psi_{in}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad\forall\,t\geq 0,\,\bm{\xi}\in U.

(3.6)

In the focusing case, i.e.,
\alpha<0
, we derive by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
\|\psi\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\leq C\|\partial_{x}\psi\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{3}
and the Young’s inequality that

\displaystyle\|\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\leq C\|\partial_{x}\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{L^{2}}^{3}\leq\frac{1}{-2\alpha}\|\partial_{x}\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{-\alpha C^{2}}{2}\|\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{L^{2}}^{6},

(3.7)

which together with the definition of
E(t,\bm{\xi})
in (
3.1
) shows that

\displaystyle E(t,\bm{\xi})\geq\frac{1}{4}\|\partial_{x}\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U\times{\mathbb{T}})}\|\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{2}C^{2}}{4}\|\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{L^{2}}^{6}.

(3.8)

Then the inequality (
3.8
) together with the mass conservation (
3.2
), the energy conservation (
3.1
) and the bound of
E(0,\bm{\xi})
in (
3.4
) shows that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4|E(t,\bm{\xi})|+4\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U\times{\mathbb{T}})}\|\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha^{2}C^{2}\|\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{L^{2}}^{6}

\displaystyle\leq 4E_{0}+4\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U\times{\mathbb{T}})}\|\psi_{in}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha^{2}C^{2}\|\psi_{in}\|_{L^{2}}^{6},\quad\forall\,t\geq 0,\,\bm{\xi}\in U.

(3.9)

Thus,
\|\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{H^{1}}
for any
t\geq 0,\,\bm{\xi}\in U
is always bounded from above by a uniform constant depending only on
{\|\psi_{in}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}
,
E_{0}
and
\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U\times{\mathbb{T}})}
. Such generic upper bound for
\|\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{H^{1}}
together with the local well-posedness leads to the global well-posedness of (
1.3
) in
H^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
for each
\bm{\xi}
, and
\psi\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times U;H^{1}({\mathbb{T}}))\subseteq L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times U\times{\mathbb{T}})
.
Then, (
3.1
) by the product lemma
[
51
]
(see Appendix
A
for details of the product lemma) gives

\|\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|\psi_{in}\|_{H^{s}}+C\left[\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{s})}+\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times U\times{\mathbb{T}})}^{2}\right]\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi(\rho,\bm{\xi})\|_{H^{s}}d\rho,

and the last assertion of the lemma follows from the Gronwall inequality for any fixed
T>0
.
∎

Remark 5
.

For the truncated NLS problem (
2.2
), note that the truncation does not change the regularity of the random potential, as stated in Assumption
2.1
. Therefore, by the same procedure in Lemma
3.1
, we can have
\psi_{m}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U_{m};H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}))
for any finite
T>0
with
\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U_{m};H^{s})}\leq\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}
.

3.2.
Error of dimension truncation

Lemma 3.2
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, the difference between the solution of (
2.2
) and the solution of the parameterized approximation (
1.3
) is given by

\|\psi_{m}-\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{1})}\leq C\|V_{m}-V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{1})},

with a constant
C>0
depending on
\alpha
,
T
, the norm of
V
in
L^{\infty}(U;H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}))
and the norm of
\psi
in
L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}))
.

Proof.

Denoting
\delta\psi=\psi_{m}-\psi
and taking the difference between (
2.2
) and (
1.3
), we get

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}\delta\psi=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\delta\psi+V\delta\psi+(V_{m}-V)\psi_{m}+\alpha\left[|\psi_{m}|^{2}\psi_{m}-|\psi|^{2}\psi\right],\quad x\in{\mathbb{T}},\ t>0,\\
&\delta\psi(t=0)=0,\quad x\in{\mathbb{T}}.\end{aligned}\right.

The Duhamel formula gives

\displaystyle\delta\psi(t,\bm{\xi})=-i\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{i(t-\rho)\partial_{x}^{2}/2}\Big[

\displaystyle V(\bm{\xi})\delta\psi(\rho,\bm{\xi})+(V_{m}(\bm{\xi})-V(\bm{\xi}))\psi_{m}(\rho,\bm{\xi})

\displaystyle+\alpha(|\psi_{m}(\rho,\bm{\xi})|^{2}\psi_{m}(\rho,\bm{\xi})-|\psi(\rho,\bm{\xi})|^{2}\psi(\rho,\bm{\xi}))\Big]d\rho,\quad t\geq 0,

which by the product lemma gives

\displaystyle\|\delta\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\left[\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{s})}+\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}^{2}+\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U_{m};H^{s})}^{2}\right]\|\delta\psi(\rho,\bm{\xi})\|_{H^{1}}d\rho

\displaystyle+tC\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U_{m};H^{s})}\|V_{m}-V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{1})},\quad 0\leq t\leq T,

with some
C>0
depending on
\alpha
only. Note that the function

q(t):=tC\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U_{m};H^{s})}\|V_{m}-V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{1})}

is non-decreasing in
t
and the constant

K:=C\left[\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{s})}+\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}^{2}+\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U_{m};H^{s})}^{2}\right]\geq 0.

Then the Gronwall inequality leads to

\displaystyle\|\delta\psi(t,\bm{\xi})\|_{H^{1}}\leq

\displaystyle q(t)+K\exp(Kt)\int_{0}^{t}\exp(-K\rho)q(\rho)d\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q(t)\left(1+K\exp(Kt)\int_{0}^{t}\exp(-K\rho)d\rho\right)=q(t)\exp(Kt)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle tC\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U_{m};H^{s})}\|V_{m}-V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{1})}

\displaystyle\times\exp{\left\{tC\left[\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{s})}+\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}^{2}+\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U_{m};H^{s})}^{2}\right]\right\}},

where we use the fact that
q(t)
is non-decreasing in
t
in the second inequality, and the uniform right-hand-side in
\bm{\xi}
gives the assertion.
∎

Under the condition
\|V_{m}-V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{1})}\leq Cm^{-\chi}
in Assumption
2.2
, Lemma
3.2
tells that
\psi_{m}\to\psi
in
L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{1}({\mathbb{T}}))
as
m\to\infty
.
Recall that with the given linear functional
G
, we are interested in computing
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}]
which after the truncation is approximated by

\mathbb{E}_{m}[\mathcal{G}^{m}]=\int_{U_{m}}G(|\psi_{m}(t,\xi,\cdot)|^{2})d\xi.

Its error is given as follows.

Lemma 3.3
.

Under Assumption
2.1
and Assumption
2.2
, with
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}^{m}
defined in (
2.7
), we have the estimate

|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}]-\mathbb{E}_{m}[\mathcal{G}^{m}]|\leq Cm^{-\chi},

where
C
is independent of
m
.

Proof.

By the definition (
2.6
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}]-\mathbb{E}_{m}[\mathcal{G}^{m}]=\lim_{p\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{p}[\mathcal{G}^{p}]-\mathbb{E}_{m}[\mathcal{G}^{m}]
. Note that when
p\geq m
,
\mathbb{E}_{p}[\mathcal{G}^{m}]=\mathbb{E}_{m}[\mathcal{G}^{m}]
, so we have

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}]-\mathbb{E}_{m}[\mathcal{G}^{m}]|=\lim_{p\to\infty}|\mathbb{E}_{p}[\mathcal{G}^{p}-\mathcal{G}^{m}]|.

(3.10)

By the assumptions and Lemma
3.2
, we have for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}^{p}(t,\bm{\xi}_{\{1:p\}})-\mathcal{G}^{m}(t,\xi)|\leq

\displaystyle\|G\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{T}})^{\prime}}\||\psi_{p}(t,\bm{\xi}_{\{1:p\}},\cdot)|^{2}-|\psi_{m}(t,\xi,\cdot)|^{2}\|_{H^{1}}.

(3.11)

With the estimates in Lemma
3.1
and Remark
5
, we get by the triangle inequality and the algebraic property of
H^{1}
,

\||\psi_{p}(t,\bm{\xi}_{\{1:p\}},\cdot)|^{2}-|\psi_{m}(t,\xi,\cdot)|^{2}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|\psi_{p}(t,\bm{\xi}_{\{1:p\}},\cdot)-\psi_{m}(t,\xi,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}}.

Plugging it into (
3.11
) and then using triangle inequality and Lemma
3.2
, we find

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}^{p}(t,\bm{\xi}_{\{1:p\}})-\mathcal{G}^{m}(t,\xi)|\leq

\displaystyle C\|\psi_{p}(t,\bm{\xi}_{\{1:p\}},\cdot)-\psi(t,\bm{\xi},\cdot)\|_{H^{1}}+C\|{\psi(t,\bm{\xi},\cdot)}-\psi_{m}(t,\xi,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|V_{p}-V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{1})}+\|V_{m}-V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{1})})\leq C(m^{-\chi}+p^{-\chi}).

Note here
C>0
is uniform constant in
\bm{\xi},p,m
. Then, we find

|\mathbb{E}_{p}[\mathcal{G}^{p}-\mathcal{G}^{m}]|\leq\mathbb{E}_{p}[|\mathcal{G}^{p}-\mathcal{G}^{m}|]\leq C(m^{-\chi}+p^{-\chi}),

and so by (
3.10
) we have the assertion of the lemma.
∎

3.3.
Regularity in parametric space

As one key to determine the convergence of the QMC method, we need the regularity of the solution in the parametric space. We now start to estimate the mixed first derivatives of
\psi_{m}
with respect to
\xi_{j}
. In the following, we shall denote

\Gamma:=\{\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{m})\,|\,\nu_{j}=0\,\mbox{ or }\,1,\,j=1,\ldots,m\}

with
|\nu|=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\nu_{j}
, and the mixed first derivative reads

\partial^{\nu}\psi_{m}=\partial_{\xi_{1}}^{\nu_{1}}\ldots\partial_{\xi_{m}}^{\nu_{m}}\psi_{m},\quad\nu\in\Gamma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Under Assumption
2.1
,
for any fixed
T>0,\ m>0
and multi-index
0\neq\nu\in\Gamma
, we have

\|\partial^{\nu}\psi_{m}(t,\xi,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}}\leq 3^{(|\nu|+2)(|\nu|-1)/2}C_{T}^{2|\nu|-1}\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1}}\right)^{\nu_{j}},\ \forall\,\xi\in U_{m},\ t\in[0,T],

(3.12)

where
C_{T}=\max\{C_{T}^{0},1\}
with

\displaystyle C_{T}^{0}:=

\displaystyle 2\max\{1,|\alpha|\}C_{0}T\max\left\{1,\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}\right\}

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(T\max\left\{1,C_{0}\left[\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{s})}+3|\alpha|\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}^{2}\right]\right\}\right)

for some generic constant
C_{0}>0
independent of
\nu
and
m
.

Proof.

We prove (
3.12
) by an induction on
|\nu|
.
When
|\nu|=1
, we differentiate (
1.3
) with respect to
\xi_{j}
. By denoting

\partial_{j}\psi_{m}=\partial_{\xi_{j}}\psi_{m}
and
\partial_{j}V_{m}=\partial_{\xi_{j}}V_{m}
for short, we get for
j=1,\ldots,m
,

i\partial_{t}\partial_{j}\psi_{m}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\partial_{j}\psi_{m}+\partial_{j}V_{m}\psi_{m}+V_{m}\partial_{j}\psi_{m}+\alpha\left(2|\psi_{m}|^{2}\partial_{j}\psi_{m}+\psi_{m}^{2}\overline{\partial_{j}\psi_{m}}\right).

Omitting the spatial variable for brevity, the Duhamel formula of the above shows

\displaystyle\partial_{j}\psi_{m}(t,\xi)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{it\partial_{x}^{2}/2}\partial_{j}\psi_{in}-i\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{i(t-\rho)\partial_{x}^{2}/2}\Big[\partial_{j}V_{m}(\xi)\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)+V_{m}(\xi)\partial_{j}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)

\displaystyle+\alpha\left(2|\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)|^{2}\partial_{j}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)+\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)^{2}\overline{\partial_{j}\psi_{m}}(\rho,\xi)\right)\Big]d\rho.

Since the initial function is deterministic, so
\partial_{j}\psi_{in}=0
. Then we find

\displaystyle\|\partial_{j}\psi_{m}(t,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\leq C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\Big[

\displaystyle\|\partial_{j}V_{m}(\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\|\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}+\|V_{m}(\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial_{j}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle+3|\alpha|\|\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|\partial_{j}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\Big]d\rho,

with a generic constant
C_{0}>0
coming from the Sobolev embedding.
By the fact
\partial_{j}V_{m}=\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}v_{j}
, and the boundedness

\|\psi_{m}(t,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U_{m};H^{1})}\leq\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})},\quad t\in[0,T],

as stated in Remark
5
, we further find for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{j}\psi_{m}(t,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}t\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1}}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}

\displaystyle+C_{0}\left[\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{s})}+3|\alpha|\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}^{2}\right]\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{j}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}d\rho.

By Gronwall’s inequality, we get for
t\in[0,T],\ \xi\in U_{m}
,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{j}\psi_{m}(t,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(tC_{0}\left[\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{s})}+3|\alpha|\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}^{2}\right]\right)C_{0}t\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1}},

which verifies (
3.12
) for
|\nu|=1
.

When
|\nu|\geq 2
, by the Leibniz rule we have

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\partial^{\nu}\psi_{m}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial_{x}^{2}}{2}\partial^{\nu}\psi_{m}+\sum_{\mu\preceq\nu}\binom{{\nu}}{\mu}\partial^{{\nu}-{\mu}}V_{m}\partial^{\mu}\psi_{m}+\alpha\sum_{{\mu}\preceq{\nu}}\binom{{\nu}}{{\mu}}\partial^{{\nu}-{\mu}}|\psi_{m}|^{2}\partial^{\mu}\psi_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial_{x}^{2}}{2}\partial^{\nu}\psi_{m}+V_{m}\partial^{\nu}\psi_{m}+\alpha\left(2|\psi_{m}|^{2}\partial^{\nu}\psi_{m}+\psi_{m}^{2}\partial^{\nu}\overline{\psi_{m}}\right)+\sum_{|{\nu}-{\mu}|=1,\mu\prec\nu}\partial^{{\nu}-{\mu}}V_{m}\partial^{\mu}\psi_{m}

\displaystyle+\alpha\sum_{0\neq{\mu}\prec{\nu}}\sum_{\eta\preceq\nu-\mu}\partial^{{\nu}-{\mu}-\eta}\psi_{m}\partial^{\mu}\psi_{m}\partial^{\eta}\overline{\psi_{m}}.

Here
\binom{\nu}{\mu}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\binom{\nu_{j}}{\mu_{j}}
.
Similarly as before, we can deduce

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\nu}\psi_{m}(t,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\left[\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{s})}+3|\alpha|\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}^{2}\right]\|\partial^{\nu}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}d\rho

\displaystyle+C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{|{\nu}-{\mu}|=1,\mu\prec\nu}\|\partial^{{\nu}-{\mu}}V_{m}\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial^{\mu}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}d\rho

\displaystyle+C_{0}|\alpha|\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{0\neq{\mu}\prec{\nu}}\sum_{\eta\preceq\nu-\mu}\|\partial^{{\nu}-{\mu}-\eta}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial^{\mu}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial^{\eta}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}d\rho.

By the Gronwall inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\nu}\psi_{m}(t,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{C}\bigg[\sum_{|{\nu}-{\mu}|=1,\mu\prec\nu}\|\partial^{{\nu}-{\mu}}V_{m}\|_{H^{1}}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial^{\mu}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}d\rho

(3.13)

\displaystyle+\sum_{0\neq{\mu}\prec{\nu}}\sum_{\eta\preceq\nu-\mu}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial^{{\nu}-{\mu}-\eta}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial^{\mu}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial^{\eta}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}d\rho\bigg],

with

\tilde{C}=\max\{1,|\alpha|\}C_{0}\exp\left(T\max\left\{1,C_{0}\left[\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{s})}+3|\alpha|\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}^{2}\right]\right\}\right).

For the first summation term above, noting that
|\mu|=|\nu|-1
and using an induction argument with the assumption (
3.12
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{|{\nu}-{\mu}|=1,\mu\prec\nu}\|\partial^{{\nu}-{\mu}}V_{m}\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial^{\mu}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\leq|\nu|3^{(|\nu|+1)(|\nu|-2)/2}C_{T}^{2|\nu|-3}\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1}}\right)^{\nu_{j}}.

(3.14)

On the other hand, for the double summation part in (
3.13
), by the induction assumption (
3.12
) we find

\displaystyle\sum_{0\neq{\mu}\prec{\nu}}\sum_{\eta\preceq\nu-\mu}\|\partial^{{\nu}-{\mu}-\eta}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial^{\mu}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial^{\eta}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{0\neq{\mu}\prec{\nu}}\sum_{\eta\preceq\nu-\mu}C_{T}^{2|\nu|-2}\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1}}\right)^{\nu_{j}}3^{\frac{|\nu|^{2}+|\nu|-6}{2}+|\mu|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}-|\nu||\mu|-|\nu||\eta|-|\mu||\eta|},

and it is direct to check here that

\frac{|\nu|^{2}+|\nu|-6}{2}+|\mu|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}-|\nu||\mu|-|\nu||\eta|-|\mu||\eta|\leq\frac{|\nu|^{2}-|\nu|-2}{2}.

So by
noting that the double summation contains
3^{|\nu|}-3
terms in total, we further get

\displaystyle\sum_{0\neq{\mu}\prec{\nu}}\sum_{\eta\prec\nu-\mu}\|\partial^{{\nu}-{\mu}-\eta}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial^{\mu}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial^{\eta}\psi_{m}(\rho,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3^{|\nu|}C_{T}^{2|\nu|-2}\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1}}\right)^{\nu_{j}}3^{(|\nu|+1)(|\nu|-2)/2}.

(3.15)

By plugging (
3.15
) and (
3.14
) into (
3.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\nu}\psi_{m}(t,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{C}TC_{T}^{2|\nu|-2}3^{(|\nu|+1)(|\nu|-2)/2}\left(|\nu|+3^{|\nu|}\right)\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1}}\right)^{\nu_{j}}.

Further noting that
\tilde{C}T\leq C_{T}^{0}/2
and
|\nu|<3^{|\nu|}
, we find

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\nu}\psi_{m}(t,\xi)\|_{H^{1}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}C_{T}^{2|\nu|-1}3^{(|\nu|+1)(|\nu|-2)/2}\left(|\nu|+3^{|\nu|}\right)\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1}}\right)^{\nu_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{T}^{2|\nu|-1}3^{(|\nu|+2)(|\nu|-1)/2}\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1}}\right)^{\nu_{j}}.

∎

With the established regularity of the solution in the parametric space, we can now estimate the mixed derivative of the physical observable
\mathcal{G}^{m}(t,\xi)=G(|\psi_{m}(t,\xi,\cdot)|^{2})
with respect to
\xi
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, for any multi-index
{\nu}\in\Gamma
with the constant
C_{\nu}:=2^{|\nu|}C_{T}^{2|\nu|-1}3^{(|\nu|+2)(|\nu|-1)/2}
,
we have

|\partial^{\nu}\mathcal{G}^{m}(t,\xi)|\leq C_{0}C_{\nu}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1}}\right)^{\nu_{j}}\|G\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{T}})^{\prime}},\quad\forall\,\xi\in U_{m},\ t\in[0,T].

(3.16)

Proof.

For any
\xi\in U_{m}
and
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial^{\nu}\mathcal{G}^{m}(t,\xi)|

\displaystyle=|G\left(\partial^{\nu}|\psi_{m}|^{2}\right)|\leq\|G\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{T}})^{\prime}}\|\partial^{\nu}|\psi_{m}|^{2}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}.

By the algebraic property of
H^{1}
and Lemma
3.4
, we find

\displaystyle|\partial^{\nu}\mathcal{G}^{m}(t,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\|G\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{T}})^{\prime}}\sum_{{\mu}\preceq{\nu}}C_{0}\left\|\partial^{{\nu}-{\mu}}\overline{\psi_{m}}\right\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial^{\mu}\psi_{m}\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}C_{\nu}\|G\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{T}})^{\prime}}\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1}}\right)^{\nu_{j}}.

∎

3.4.
Analysis of the QMC integration error

With the preparation before, we now specify the QMC method, i.e., the quadrature points in (
2.9
), and then we shall derive its error bound on the PDE level.

We adopt the weighted Sobolev space technique
[
37
,
47
]
here.
Consider the weighted and unanchored Sobolev space
\mathcal{W}_{m,\gamma}:=\{F(\xi):U_{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}\ |\ \|F\|_{W_{m,\gamma}}<\infty\}
with the norm

\|F\|_{W_{m,\gamma}}:=\left[\sum_{{\nu\in\Gamma}}\normalsize\gamma_{\nu}^{-1}\int_{U_{|\nu|}}\left(\int_{U_{m-|\nu|}}\partial^{\nu}F(\xi)d\xi_{\nu}^{\dagger}\right)^{2}d\xi_{\nu}\right]^{1/2},

(3.17)

where by the notation,
\xi
is split into the active part
\xi_{\nu}
for differentiations and the inactive part
\xi_{\nu}^{\dagger}
, i.e.,
\xi_{\nu}
consists of
\xi_{j}
with
j
such that
\nu_{j}=1
and
\xi_{\nu}^{\dagger}
consists of
\xi_{k}
with
k
such that
\nu_{k}=0
. The notation
\gamma_{\nu}
denotes the product-type weight, i.e.,

\gamma_{\nu}=\Pi_{\nu_{j}=1}\gamma_{j}
with
\gamma_{\mathbf{0}_{\{1:m\}}}=1
,
which will be chosen later, and
\gamma:=\{\gamma_{\nu}\,|\,\nu\in\Gamma\}
. With the chosen
\gamma_{j}
(
j=1,\ldots,m
) and the following worst-case error function

e^{wt}_{s}(z):=-1+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{p=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\left[1+\gamma_{j}B\left(\mathrm{frac}(pz_{j}/N)\right)\right],\quad z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}),\ 1\leq s\leq m,

where
B(x)=-1/(2\pi^{2})\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi ilx}/l^{2}
is the Bernoulli polynomial, the generating vector
z
in (
2.9
) is computed by the CBC algorithm
[
12
]
. That is to set
z_{1}=1
, and then determine each
z_{s}
for
s=2,\ldots,m
in a sequel by minimizing
e^{wt}_{s}(z)
for
z_{s}\in\mathbb{U}_{N}=\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,|\,1\leq x\leq N-1,\,\mathrm{gcd}(x,N)=1\}
. An explicit error bound of the QMC with such constructed quadrature points can be established (see e.g.,
[
36
]
). Here let us quote it as the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

(
[
36
, Theorem 4.1]
)
With fixed
m,\,N,\,\gamma
and some
F(\xi)\in W_{m,\gamma}
, the QMC method (
2.9
) given by the CBC algorithm satisfies

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{\Delta}\left[|E_{m}(F)-Q_{m,N}[F]|^{2}\right]}\leq\left[\sum_{\nu\in\Gamma\setminus\mathbf{0}_{\{1:m\}}}\gamma_{\nu}^{\lambda}\left(\frac{2\zeta(2\lambda)}{(2\pi^{2})^{\lambda}}\right)^{|\nu|}\right]^{1/(2\lambda)}\varphi(N)^{-1/(2\lambda)}\|F\|_{W_{m,\gamma}},

(3.18)

for any
\lambda\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
. Here
\varphi(N)=|\mathbb{U}_{N}|
is the Euler totient function and
\zeta(\cdot)
is the Riemann zeta function.

The possible choice of the weight
\gamma
to provide the optimal convergence rate for the QMC is specified in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

With fixed
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+}
and
N\leq 10^{30}
, choose
\lambda=1/(2-2\delta)
for any
\delta\in(0,1/2)
. Then under Assumption
2.1
and Assumption
2.2
, a QMC method (
2.9
) can be constructed by the CBC algorithm which satisfies

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{\Delta}\left[|E_{m}[\mathcal{G}^{m}](t)-Q_{m,N}[\mathcal{G}^{m}](t)|^{2}\right]}\leq CN^{\delta-1},\quad t\in[0,T],

(3.19)

where
C>0
is some constant independent of
m
.

Proof.

It is direct to compute from the definition of the weighted norm (
3.17
) and Lemma
3.5
that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}^{m}(t,\cdot)\|_{W_{m,\gamma}}=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{{\nu\in\Gamma}}\normalsize\gamma_{\nu}^{-1}\int_{U_{|\nu|}}\left(\int_{U_{m-|\nu|}}\partial^{\nu}\mathcal{G}^{m}(t,\xi)d\xi_{\nu}^{\dagger}\right)^{2}d\xi_{\nu}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\|G\|_{H^{1}(D)^{\prime}}\left[\sum_{\nu\in\Gamma}\gamma_{\nu}^{-1}C_{\nu}^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}^{\nu_{j}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1}}^{2\nu_{j}}\right]^{1/2}.

Meanwhile, it is known that the Euler totient function satisfies
1/\varphi(N)\leq 9/N
for
N\leq 10^{30}

[
25
]
. Thus, by (
3.18
) and choosing
\lambda=1/(2-2\delta)
we find

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{\Delta}\left[|E_{m}[\mathcal{G}^{m}](t)-Q_{m,N}[\mathcal{G}^{m}](t)|^{2}\right]}\leq 9C_{\gamma}C_{0}\|G\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{T}})^{\prime}}N^{\delta-1},

with

C_{\gamma}=\left[\sum_{\nu\in\Gamma}\gamma_{\nu}^{-1}C_{\nu}^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}^{\nu_{j}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1}}^{2\nu_{j}}\right]^{1/2}\left[\sum_{\nu\in\Gamma}\gamma_{\nu}^{\lambda}\left(\frac{2\zeta(2\lambda)}{(2\pi^{2})^{\lambda}}\right)^{|\nu|}\right]^{1/(2\lambda)}.

Denote
b_{j}=\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1}}
and
\rho(\lambda)=2\zeta(2\lambda)/(2\pi^{2})^{\lambda}
for simplicity.
Now we take

\gamma_{\nu}=\left[C_{\nu}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{b_{j}^{\nu_{j}}}{\rho(\lambda)^{\nu_{j}/2}}\right]^{2/(1+\lambda)},

and it is direct to deduce that

C_{\gamma}=(A_{\lambda})^{\frac{\lambda+1}{2\lambda}}
with

A_{\lambda}=\sum_{\nu\in\Gamma}C_{\nu}^{2\lambda/(1+\lambda)}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left[b_{j}^{2\lambda\nu_{j}}\rho(\lambda)^{\nu_{j}}\right]^{1/(1+\lambda)}.

It remains to show that
A_{\lambda}
is bounded
\forall\nu\in\Gamma,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

By the constant
C_{\nu}
from Lemma
3.5
and using Hölder’s inequality, we find

\displaystyle A_{\lambda}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu\in\Gamma}\left[3^{|\nu|^{2}+|\nu|-1/2}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}^{\nu_{j}}\right]^{2\lambda/(1+\lambda)}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left[\frac{(2C_{T}^{2})^{2\lambda}b_{j}^{2\lambda}\rho(\lambda)}{\alpha_{j}^{2\lambda}}\right]^{\nu_{j}/(1+\lambda)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\nu\in\Gamma}\prod_{j=1}^{m}3^{\nu_{j}s_{j}}\alpha_{j}^{\nu_{j}}\right)^{2\lambda/(1+\lambda)}\left(\sum_{\nu\in\Gamma}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left[\frac{(2C_{T}^{2})^{2\lambda}b_{j}^{2\lambda}\rho(\lambda)}{\alpha_{j}^{2\lambda}}\right]^{\nu_{j}/(1-\lambda)}\right)^{(1-\lambda)/(1+\lambda)},

where
s_{j}:=\sum_{l=1}^{j}\nu_{l}
and
\alpha_{j}>0
is chosen as follows. Note the fact that

\sum_{\nu\in\Gamma}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{j}^{\nu_{j}}\leq\exp(\sum_{j\geq 1}\beta_{j})
holds for any
m\geq 1
and any
\beta_{j}>0
with
\sum_{j\geq 1}\beta_{j}<\infty
(see
[
37
]
), then we have:
\forall\nu\in\Gamma,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle A_{\lambda}\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{2\lambda}{1+\lambda}\sum_{j\geq 1}3^{j}\alpha_{j}\right)\exp\left(\frac{1-\lambda}{1+\lambda}\sum_{j\geq 1}\left[\frac{(2C_{T}^{2})^{2\lambda}b_{j}^{2\lambda}\rho(\lambda)}{\alpha_{j}^{2\lambda}}\right]^{1/(1-\lambda)}\right)=:A_{max}.

By taking
\alpha_{j}=\sqrt{b_{j}/3^{j}}
and under Assumption
2.2
, we have
\sum_{j\geq 1}3^{j}\alpha_{j}<1
. Moreover, with
\lambda\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
we have

\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\geq 1
and so

\sum_{j\geq 1}(b_{j}/\alpha_{j})^{2\lambda/(1-\lambda)}=\sum_{j\geq 1}(3^{j}b_{j})^{\lambda/(1-\lambda)}<\infty.

Thus, we find the upper bound
A_{max}
is finite.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Under the same assumption of Lemma
3.7
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
m
such that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{\Delta}\left[\left|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}](t)-Q_{m,N}[\mathcal{G}^{m}](t)\right|^{2}\right]}\ \leq C(m^{-\chi}+N^{\delta-1}),\quad t\in[0,T],

(3.20)

for any
\delta\in(0,1/2)
.

Proof.

Firstly, by triangle inequality we have

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}](t)-Q_{m,N}[\mathcal{G}^{m}](t)|\leq|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}](t)-\mathbb{E}_{m}[\mathcal{G}^{m}]|+|\mathbb{E}_{m}[\mathcal{G}^{m}]-Q_{m,N}[\mathcal{G}^{m}](t)|.

The first part of right-hand-side is independent of the random shift
\Delta
, so

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\Delta}\left[|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}](t)-Q_{m,N}[\mathcal{G}^{m}](t)|^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle 2\left|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}](t)-\mathbb{E}_{m}[\mathcal{G}^{m}](t)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\mathbb{E}^{\Delta}\left[|\mathbb{E}_{m}[\mathcal{G}^{m}](t)-Q_{m,N}[\mathcal{G}^{m}](t)|^{2}\right].

Then by Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.7
, we obtain the result.
∎

Remark 6
.

We remark the choice of the weight
\gamma
and the error constant given in our analysis above may be far away from optimal. Our result theoretically serves to show the existence of the randomly shifted QMC lattice rule that can converge at the desired almost-linear and dimension-independent rate for solving the random NLS (
1.3
).

4.
Error of the scheme

In this section, we continue the analysis to complete the full error estimate result given in Section
2.3
. To do so, we first carry out the error estimate of the TSFP scheme on the truncated problem (
2.2
), which is stated as the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\psi_{m}^{n}
be the numerical solution from the TSFP scheme (
2.4
) for solving the truncated problem (
2.2
) till any fixed
T>0
with any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Under the same assumption in Theorem
2.3
, there exist constants
\tau_{0},h_{0}>0
independent of
m
such that when
\tau\leq\tau_{0}
and
h\leq h_{0}
, the following error bound holds

\|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}-\psi_{m}(t_{n},\xi,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}}\leq C(\tau^{2}+h^{s-1}),\quad\forall\,0\leq t_{n}\leq T,\ \xi\in U_{m},

for some constant
C>0
independent of
\xi,m,\tau
and
h
.

Proof.

The proof largely follows the framework of analysis for the Strang splitting in
[
7
,
39
]
. Here we shall go through it mainly to check the impact from the parametric space. Let us omit the variables
x,\xi
and denote
\psi_{m}(t)=\psi_{m}(t,\xi,x)
for simplicity.

We begin with the estimate of the temporal truncation error. Denoting
v^{n}(\rho):=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}i\partial_{x}^{2}\rho}\psi_{m}(t_{n})
for short,
by Taylor’s expansion we find

\displaystyle\Psi_{\tau}^{\mathrm{p}}\circ\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}(\psi_{m}(t_{n}))=\left[1+i\tau(V_{m}+\alpha|v^{n}(\tau/2)|^{2})-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}(V_{m}+\alpha|v^{n}(\tau/2)|^{2})^{2}\right]v^{n}(\tau/2)+r_{1}^{n},

where
r_{1}^{n}
depends on
V_{m}
and
v^{n}
. By Remark
5
, we have
\|r_{1}^{n}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\tau^{3}
with
C
depending on the
H^{1}
-norm of
V_{m},\psi_{m}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}\circ\Psi_{\tau}^{\mathrm{p}}\circ\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}(\psi_{m}(t_{n}))=

\displaystyle v^{n}(\tau)+i\tau\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}i\partial_{x}^{2}\tau/2}\left(V_{m}+\alpha|v^{n}(\tau/2)|^{2}\right)v^{n}(\tau/2)

\displaystyle-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\left(V_{m}+\alpha|v^{n}(\tau/2)|^{2}\right)^{2}v^{n}(\tau/2)+r_{2}^{n},

(4.1)

where

r_{2}^{n}=\mathrm{e}^{i\partial_{x}^{2}\tau/4}r_{1}^{n}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\frac{i}{4}\partial_{x}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{i\partial_{x}^{2}s/4}\left(V_{m}+\alpha|v^{n}(\tau/2)|^{2}\right)^{2}v^{n}(\tau/2)ds.

Under the assumption
s\geq 5
, we have
\|r_{2}^{n}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\tau^{3}
with
C
depending on the
H^{3}
-norm of
V_{m}
and
\psi_{m}
.
On the other hand, by the Duhamel formula of (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\psi_{m}(t_{n+1})=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}i\tau\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{m}(t_{n})+i\int_{0}^{\tau}g_{\tau}^{n}(\rho)d\rho,\quad n\geq 0,

(4.2)

with
g_{\tau}^{n}(\rho):=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}i(\tau-\rho)\partial_{x}^{2}}[V_{m}+\alpha|\psi_{m}(t_{n}+\rho)|^{2}]\psi_{m}(t_{n}+\rho)
. By plugging

\psi_{m}(t_{n}+\rho)=v^{n}(\rho)+i\int_{0}^{\rho}g_{\rho}^{n}(s)ds

into (
4.2
) we find

\displaystyle\psi_{m}(t_{n+1})=

\displaystyle v^{n}(\tau)+i\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}i(\tau-\rho)\partial_{x}^{2}}\left[V_{m}+\alpha|v^{n}(\rho)|^{2}\right]v^{n}(\rho)d\rho

\displaystyle+i\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}i(\tau-\rho)\partial_{x}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\rho}\left[i(V_{m}+2\alpha|v^{n}(\rho)|^{2})g_{\rho}^{n}(s)-i\alpha v^{n}(\rho)^{2}\overline{g_{\rho}^{n}(s)}\right]dsd\rho+r_{3}^{n},

where clearly
\|r_{3}^{n}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\tau^{3}
with
C
depending on the
H^{1}
-norm of
V_{m},\psi_{m}
.
Note
g_{\rho}^{n}(s)=[V_{m}+\alpha|v^{n}(\rho)|^{2}]v^{n}(\rho)+\mathcal{O}(\tau\partial_{x}^{2})
for
0\leq s\leq\rho\leq\tau
,
we further get

\displaystyle\psi_{m}(t_{n+1})=

\displaystyle v^{n}(\tau)+i\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}i(\tau-\rho)\partial_{x}^{2}}\left[V_{m}+\alpha|v^{n}(\rho)|^{2}\right]v^{n}(\rho)d\rho

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\rho(V_{m}+\alpha|v^{n}(\rho)|^{2})^{2}v^{n}(\rho)d\rho+r_{3}^{n}+r_{4}^{n},

where
\|r_{4}^{n}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\tau^{3}
with
C
depending on the
H^{3}
-norm of
V_{m},\psi_{m}
.
Subtracting the above from (
4
) and then using the quadrature error formula of the midpoint rule, we find

\displaystyle\|\psi_{m}(t_{n+1})-\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}\circ\Psi_{\tau}^{\mathrm{p}}\circ\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}(\psi_{m}(t_{n}))\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|r_{2}^{n}\|_{H^{1}}+\|r_{3}^{n}\|_{H^{1}}+\|r_{4}^{n}\|_{H^{1}}+\tau^{3}\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(\theta)\|f^{\prime\prime}(\theta\tau)\|_{H^{1}}d\theta,

where
\kappa(\theta)
is the Peano kernel of the midpoint rule and

f(\rho):=i\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}i(\tau-\rho)\partial_{x}^{2}}\left[V_{m}+\alpha|v^{n}(\rho)|^{2}\right]v^{n}(\rho)-\rho(V_{m}+\alpha|v^{n}(\rho)|^{2})^{2}v^{n}(\rho).

Therefore, we find in total

\displaystyle\|\psi_{m}(t_{n+1})-\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}\circ\Psi_{\tau}^{\mathrm{p}}\circ\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}(\psi_{m}(t_{n}))\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\tau^{3},\quad 0\leq n<T/\tau,

(4.3)

with
C
depending on the
H^{5}
-norm of
V_{m},\psi_{m}
.
By the uniform bounds of
V_{m}
and
\psi_{m}
in
H^{s}
with
s\geq 5
from the assumption and the result of Lemma
3.1
and Remark
5
, we find the error constant
C>0
in (
4.3
) is eventually a constant depending only on
\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}
and

\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{s})}
.

Next, by the triangle inequality and the projection error (denote by
P_{M}
the projection operator to cutoff the Fourier modes of a function larger than
M/2
)
[
50
]
, we find

\displaystyle\|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n+1}-\psi_{m}(t_{n+1})\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n+1}-P_{M}\psi_{m}(t_{n+1})\|_{H^{1}}+Ch^{s-1},

with
C
here depends only on
\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U_{m};H^{s})}
and so only on
\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}
.
Noting that
I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n+1}=\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}\circ I_{M}[\Psi_{\tau}^{\mathrm{p}}\circ\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}(I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n})]
and
\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}
is isometric in
H^{1}
, we find by the triangle inequality and (
4.3
),

\displaystyle\|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n+1}-P_{M}\psi_{m}(t_{n+1})\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|I_{M}\Psi_{\tau}^{\mathrm{p}}\circ\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}(I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n})-P_{M}\Psi_{\tau}^{\mathrm{p}}\circ\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}(\psi_{m}(t_{n}))\|_{H^{1}}+C\tau^{3}.

(4.4)

Then by an induction on the boundedness of
I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}
in
H^{1}
-space and the difference between the flows, we find with some
C
depending only on
\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}
and

\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{s})}
,

\displaystyle\left\|I_{M}\Psi_{\tau}^{\mathrm{p}}\circ\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}(I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n})-P_{M}\Psi_{\tau}^{\mathrm{p}}\circ\Psi_{\frac{\tau}{2}}^{\mathrm{k}}(\psi_{m}(t_{n}))\right\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}-P_{M}\psi_{m}(t_{n})\|_{H^{1}}+C\tau\|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}-P_{M}\psi_{m}(t_{n})\|_{H^{1}}+C\tau h^{s-1}.

Plugging it into (
4.4
), summing the inequalities up till
n=0
and noting

I_{M}\psi_{m}^{0}=I_{M}\psi_{m}(0)
, the Gronwall inequality gives

\displaystyle\|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n+1}-\psi_{m}(t_{n+1})\|_{H^{1}}\leq C(\tau^{2}+h^{s-1}),

for some
C
depending only on
T,

\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}
and

\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(U;H^{s})}
.
Thus, there exist constants
\tau_{0},h_{0}>0
uniformly in
m
such that for
\tau\leq\tau_{0}
and
h\leq h_{0}
,
\|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n+1}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times U;H^{s})}+1
for all
t_{n+1}\leq T
.
∎

With all the obtained results so far, we are now ready to get the full error bound stated in our main theorem in Section
2.3
for the QMC-TSFP scheme (
2.4
). The proof is given as follows.

Proof of the Theorem
2.3
:
By the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\Delta}\left[\left|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}](t_{n})-Q_{m,N}[G(|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}|^{2})]\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}^{\Delta}\left[\left|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}](t_{n})-Q_{m,N}[\mathcal{G}^{m}](t_{n})\right|^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}^{\Delta}\left[\left|Q_{m,N}[G(|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}|^{2})]-Q_{m,N}[\mathcal{G}^{m}](t_{n})\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(m^{-\chi}+N^{\delta-1})^{2}+2\mathbb{E}^{\Delta}\left[\left|Q_{m,N}[G(|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}(\xi,\cdot)|^{2})-G(|\psi_{m}(t_{n},\xi,\cdot)|^{2})]\right|^{2}\right],

(4.5)

where we used Proposition
3.8
. By (
2.8
) and Assumption
2.1
, we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\Delta}\left[\left|Q_{m,N}[G(|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}(\xi,\cdot)|^{2})-G(|\psi_{m}(t_{n},\xi,\cdot)|^{2})]\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi\in U_{m}}\left|G(|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}(\xi,\cdot)|^{2})-G(|\psi_{m}(t_{n},\xi,\cdot)|^{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|G\|_{H^{1}(D)^{\prime}}\sup_{\xi\in U_{m}}\left\||I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}(\xi,\cdot)|^{2}-|\psi_{m}(t_{n},\xi,\cdot)|^{2}\right\|_{H^{1}}.

Then, by the error estimate in Proposition
4.1
which is uniform in
\xi
and
m
, we plug the above into (
4.5
) to get

\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{\Delta}\left[\Big|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}](t_{n})-Q_{m,N}[G(|I_{M}\psi_{m}^{n}|^{2})]\Big|^{2}\right]}\leq C(\tau^{2}+h^{s-1}+m^{-\chi}+N^{\delta-1}),\ t_{n}\in[0,T],

for some constant
C>0
independent of
m
. This completes the proof. ∎

6.
Conclusion

We proposed to combine the randomly shifted QMC lattice rule with the time-splitting Fourier pseudospectral method for solving the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with random potential. We analysed the convergence in random space by using the technique of the weighted Sobolev space. The nonlinearity in the equation introduces difficulties in estimating the parametric regularity of the solution, and the physical observable considered here is typically a nonlinear functional of the solution. We propose sufficient conditions to show the existence of a QMC rule that can achieve the almost-linear and dimension-independent convergence rate for the expected value of the physical observable. The full error estimate of the scheme is established which also covers the convergence in time and in the physical space. Numerical examples are presented to verify the theoretical result and simulations are done to investigate the wave propagation in the random NLS.

Some useful tools for studying NLS

To study the regularity of the solution of (
1.3
) in the physical space and analyze the error resulting from dimension truncation, we need two useful tools introduced in
[
51
]
. We briefly review these two tools in this section.

The bootstrap-type argument

The bootstrap-type argument is an application of the bootstrap principle or the continuity method, which can be viewed as a continuous analogue of the principle of mathematical induction. The abstract bootstrap principle works in the following way.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
I
be a time interval, and for each
t\in I
suppose we have two statements, a “hypothesis”
\mathbf{H}(t)
and a “conclusion”
\mathbf{C}(t)
. Suppose we can verify the following four assertions:

(a)

(Hypothesis implies conclusion) If
\mathbf{H}(t)
is true for some time
t\in I
, then
\mathbf{C}(t)
is also true for for that time
t
.

(b)

(Conclusion is stronger than hypothesis) If
\mathbf{C}(t)
is true for some
t\in I
, then
\mathbf{H}(t^{\prime})
is true for all
t^{\prime}\in I
in a neighbourhood of
t
.

(c)

(Conclusion is closed) If
t_{1},t_{2},\ldots
is a sequence of times in
I
which converges to another time
t\in I
, and
\mathbf{C}(t_{n})
is true for all
t_{n}
, then
\mathbf{C}(t)
is true.

(d)

(Base case)
\mathbf{H}(t)
is true for at least one time
t\in I
.

Then
\mathbf{C}(t)
is true for all
t\in I
.

The proof of this proposition can be found in
[
51
, Chapter 1.3]
. Moreover, for an illustration of how the bootstrap-type argument is applied to proving the local well-posedness of NLS, we refer readers to the proof of Proposition 3.8 in
[
51
]
.

The product lemma

As a useful tool from harmonic analysis, the product lemma reads as follows:

Lemma A.2
.

For all
f,g\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})
with
s\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq C_{s}\left(\|f\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\|g\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})}\right),

(A.1)

where
C_{s}>0
is a constant dependent on
s
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbb{T}}=(0,2\pi)
for simplicity, and the Fourier expansion reads
f(x)=\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\hat{f}_{l}\mathrm{e}^{ilx}
where
\hat{f}_{l}
denotes the Fourier coefficient. For some
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we consider

\displaystyle(1+l^{2})^{s/2}|\widehat{(fg)}_{l}|=(1+l^{2})^{s/2}\left|\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}=l}\hat{f}_{l_{1}}\hat{g}_{l_{2}}\right|\leq\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}=l}\left[1+(l_{1}+l_{2})^{2}\right]^{s/2}|\hat{f}_{l_{1}}\hat{g}_{l_{2}}|.

It is direct to check that

\displaystyle\left[1+(l_{1}+l_{2})^{2}\right]^{s/2}\leq 2^{s/2}\left[\sqrt{1+{l_{1}}^{2}}+\sqrt{1+{l_{2}}^{2}}\right]^{s}\leq C_{s}\left[(1+{l_{1}}^{2})^{s/2}+(1+{l_{2}}^{2})^{s/2}\right],

where the last inequality used the convexity of the function
x^{s}
for
x>0,\,s\geq 1
. In the following, the constant
C_{s}>0
may be different at each occurrence. Combining the above, we deduce

(1+l^{2})^{s/2}|\widehat{(fg)}_{l}|\leq C_{s}\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}=l}(1+l_{1}^{2})^{s/2}|\hat{f}_{l_{1}}\hat{g}_{l_{2}}|+C_{s}\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}=l}(1+l_{2}^{2})^{s/2}|\hat{f}_{l_{1}}\hat{g}_{l_{2}}|.

By taking the square, we find

\displaystyle(1+l^{2})^{s}|\widehat{(fg)}_{l}|^{2}\leq C_{s}\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}=l}(1+l_{1}^{2})^{s}|\hat{f}_{l_{1}}\hat{g}_{l_{2}}|^{2}+C_{s}\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}=l}(1+l_{2}^{2})^{s}|\hat{f}_{l_{1}}\hat{g}_{l_{2}}|^{2}.

By Parseval’s identity and note
\|fg\|_{H^{s}}^{2}=\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(1+l^{2})^{s}|\widehat{(fg)}_{l}|^{2}
, we get

\|fg\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq C_{s}(\|f\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

The assertion of the lemma is obtained by noting
\|f\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
and Sobolev’s embedding
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{s}\|f\|_{H^{s}}
.
For more general statement of product lemma, see e.g.,
[
51
]
.

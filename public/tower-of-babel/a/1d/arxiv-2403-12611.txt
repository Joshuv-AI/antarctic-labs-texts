MOCCA: A Fast Algorithm for Parallel MRI Reconstruction Using Model Based Coil Calibration
Gerlind Plonka, Yannick Riebe

Abstract
Abstract. We propose a new fast algorithm for simultaneous recovery of the coil sensitivities and of the magnetization image from incomplete Fourier measurements in parallel MRI. Our approach is based on a parameter model for the coil sensitivities using bivariate trigonometric polynomials of small degree. The derived MOCCA algorithm has low computational complexity of 𝒪 ⁡ ( N c ​ N 2 ​ log ⁡ N ) {\mathcal{O}}(N_{c}N^{2}\log N) for N × N N\times N images and N c N_{c} coils and achieves very good performance for incomplete MRI data. We present a complete mathematical analysis of the proposed reconstruction method. Further, we show that MOCCA achieves similarly good reconstruction results as ESPIRiT with a considerably smaller numerical effort which is due to the employed parameter model. Our numerical examples show that MOCCA can outperform several other reconstruction methods.



Key words.
parallel MRI, deconvolution, discrete Fourier transform, bivariate trigonometric polynomials, structured matrices, regularization

Introduction

One of the biggest innovations in magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) within the last years is the concept of parallel MRI.
In this setting, the use of multiple receiver coils allows the reconstruction of high-resolution images from undersampled Fourier data such that the acquisition time can be substantially reduced.
Assume we have
N_{c}
receiver channels and (incomplete) discrete measurements, so-called
k
-space data, on a cartesian grid
given in the form

\textstyle y_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{(j)}:=y^{(j)}(\frac{\boldsymbol{\nu}}{N})=\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}s^{(j)}(\mathbf{x})\,m(\mathbf{x})\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\nu}}{N}\cdot\mathbf{x}}d\mathbf{x}+\textstyle n^{(j)}(\frac{\boldsymbol{\nu}}{N}),\quad j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1,

(1.1)

for
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{N}:=\{-\frac{N}{2},\ldots,\frac{N}{2}-1\}\times\{-\frac{N}{2},\ldots,\frac{N}{2}-1\}
, with
N\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
, in the
2D
-case,
and with
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})^{T}
.
Here,
m
denotes the complex-valued unknown magnetization image and
s^{(j)}
are the complex valued sensitivity profiles of the
N_{c}
individual coils. The signal is disturbed by noise
n^{(j)}(\frac{\boldsymbol{\nu}}{N})
. Further,
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is the bounded area of interest. We assume for simplicity that
\Omega
is a square centered around zero.
To achieve the wanted acceleration of the acquisition time, the goal is to
reconstruct the high-resolution magnetization image
m
from a subsampled amount of data
y_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{(j)}
,
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}}\subset\Lambda_{N}
, thereby exploiting the information from the parallel receiver channels.

Unfortunately, in general, the coil sensitivity functions
s^{(j)}
are also not a priori known and have to be recovered from the measured data. Indeed, the acquisition process produces unpredictable correlations between the magnetization image and the coil sensitivities on the one hand and correlations between different coil sensitivities on the other hand such that

s^{(j)}
cannot be accurately estimated beforehand.
Therefore, the parallel MRI reconstruction problem can be seen as a multi-channel blind deconvolution problem.

In this paper, we present a new

Our new method employs the assumption that the coil sensitivities

Therefore, our MOCCA algorithm has low computational complexity of

If the employed discrete models for

The parameter vectors determining the coil sensitivities

Our approach is conceptionally different from ESPIRiT

where the sensitivities are computed based on a low-rank assumption of structured

This low-rank assumption is not used in our method, instead, we assume that the sensitivities can be well represented by bivariate trigonometric polynomials of small degree.

Starting with the model (

), we propose a completely different procedure to compute the coil sensitivities

. The low complexity of our MOCCA algorithm stems from the fact that all parameters needed to determine the sensitivities can be recovered by computing just one eigenvector of a moderately sized MOCCA-matrix.
By contrast, in ESPIRiT

[

This problem is entirely avoided in the MOCCA algorithm.

We provide a complete mathematical analysis of the proposed MOCCA reconstruction algorithm.
In the upcoming paper
[
21
]
, we will give deeper insights into the relation between the model-based reconstruction of coil sensitivities based on trigonometric polynomials in MOCCA and the assumption that the

[

]

This relation shows that our model assumption is equally suitable.
At the same time, the new model allows a significantly faster computation of the sensitivities from the ACS region by the MOCCA algorithm since it requires neither
the high effort of computing the coil sensitivities pixel-wise from many eigenvalue problems
as in
[

,

While we will focus here on modeling the coil sensitivities using bivariate trigonometric polynomials, the approach can

algebraic polynomials or of splines. Moreover, the model can be directly incorporated into a variational

Organization of the Paper

In Section 2, we

Throughout the paper, we restrict ourselves to the 2D-case, while all ideas can be directly transferred to 3D.
We assume for simplicity that
the measurement data are given on a cartesian grid. More exactly, assume that we have given the data as in (
1.1
)
for
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{N}
(or for
\boldsymbol{\nu}
from a subset

In other words, the

\frac{1}{N}\Lambda_{N}
is a cartesian grid of equidistant points in
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{2}
with cardinality

We apply the notation

{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}:=(y^{(j)}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{N}}

Here and throughout the paper, the index set

\textstyle{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}={\mathcal{F}}\,(\mathbf{m}\circ{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}),\qquad j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1.

(2.1)

2.1
Model for Coil Sensitivities

In practice, the coil sensitivities
{s}^{(j)}
are also unknown for
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
,
but we can suppose that these functions are smooth.
The idea is now to model the
s^{(j)}
such that they can be reconstructed from a small number of parameters.
Therefore, we propose to present the coil sensitivities as bivariate trigonometric polynomials, i.e.,
s^{(j)}
have small support in
k
-space. Let
\Lambda_{L}:=\{-n,\ldots,n\}\times\{-n,\ldots,n\}
, i.e.,
|\Lambda_{L}|=L^{2}
with
L=2n+1\ll N
. Now let

\textstyle s^{(j)}_{\mathbf{n}}:=s^{(j)}(\mathbf{n})=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}}c_{\mathbf{r}}^{(j)}\,\omega_{N}^{-\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{n}},\qquad j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1,\;\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}.

(2.2)

In our numerical experiments, it has been sufficient to use
L=5
(or
L=7
), such that every
s^{(j)}
is already determined by
25
(or
49

Let
\mathbf{W}:=({\omega}_{N}^{-\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{n}})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N},\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}}
denote a partial matrix of the inverse (scaled) Fourier matrix
N^{2}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}
with only
L^{2}
columns corresponding to indices in

\Lambda_{L}
.
Then, with
{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}:=(c_{\mathbf{r}}^{(j)})_{\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}}
, we obtain

{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}:=(s^{(j)}_{\mathbf{n}})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}=\mathbf{W}\,\mathbf{c}^{(j)},\qquad j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1.

(2.3)

In other words, filling up
(c_{\mathbf{r}}^{(j)})_{\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}}
by zeros to get
\tilde{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}=(\tilde{c}_{\mathbf{r}}^{(j)})_{\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{N}}
with
\tilde{c}_{\mathbf{r}}^{(j)}=c_{\mathbf{r}}^{(j)}
for
\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}
and
\tilde{c}_{\mathbf{r}}^{(j)}=0
otherwise, we have
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}=N^{2}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}
.

2.4
Generalized Model and Ambiguities

Model (
2.2
) for the coil sensitivities can simply be generalized to

\tilde{s}^{(j)}_{\mathbf{n}}:=\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}\,s^{(j)}_{\mathbf{n}}=\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}}c_{\mathbf{r}}^{(j)}\,\omega_{N}^{-\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{n}}\qquad j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1,\;\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N},

(2.4)

where
s^{(j)}_{\mathbf{n}}
in (
2.2
) is multiplied with the (nonvanishing) sample
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}=\gamma(\mathbf{n})
of a smooth window function
\gamma
, and where

does not depend on

.
Then, (

) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}^{(j)}={\mathcal{F}}({\mathbf{m}}\circ{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})={\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{m}\circ{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{-1}\circ{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\circ{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})={\mathcal{F}}(\tilde{\mathbf{m}}\circ\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})

(2.5)

with
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}:=(\gamma_{\mathbf{n}})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}
,
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{-1}:=(\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}^{-1})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}
,

\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}:={\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}\circ{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
, and
\tilde{\mathbf{m}}:=\mathbf{m}\circ{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{-1}
. In other words, having found
\mathbf{m}
and
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
,
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
, satisfying (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) for all acquired data, we obtain many further solutions

and

Analogously as in

In our ”classical” MOCCA model (

are indeed samples of bivariate trigonometric polynomials. Then we are left with only one global unavoidable ambiguity factor

and

we also find the solution

and

Applying the generalized model (

2.5
Sum-of-Squares Condition

Many recovery algorithms in parallel MRI (see e.g.
[
8
,
22
,
42
]
) employ the following strategy to recover
\mathbf{m}
from the (incomplete) acquired data
{\mathcal{P}}{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}
. In a first step, the unacquired
k
-space data
({y}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{(j)})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{N}\setminus\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}}}
are approximated from the acquired data
({y}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{(j)})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}}}
. Having the complete data
\mathbf{y}^{(j)}
in
k
-space, one applies the inverse Fourier transform
\check{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\mathbf{y}^{(j)}
,
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
, and computes
the components
m_{\mathbf{n}}
of
{\mathbf{m}}
using the sos condition,

\displaystyle\textstyle m_{\mathbf{n}}:=\big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}|\check{y}_{\mathbf{n}}^{(j)}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}.

(2.6)

At the first glance, this procedure completely disregards the sensitivity vectors
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
. We shortly explain, how (
2.6
) relates to our setting using the generalized model (
2.4
).
For
\mathbf{m}=(m_{\mathbf{n}})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}
and
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}=(s^{(j)}_{\mathbf{n}})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}
,
satisfying (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) for
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
, we define

\displaystyle \textstyle\gamma_{\mathbf{n}} :=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha_{\mathbf{n}}\big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}|s^{(j)}_{\mathbf{n}}|^{2}\big)^{-1/2},&\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N},\,\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}|s^{(j)}_{\mathbf{n}}|^{2}>0,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

with
\alpha_{\mathbf{n}}:=\mathrm{sign}(m_{\mathbf{n}})\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\mathrm{sign}(m_{\mathbf{n}}):=\frac{m_{\mathbf{n}}}{|m_{\mathbf{n}}|}
for
|m_{\mathbf{n}}|\neq 0
and
\mathrm{sign}(m_{\mathbf{n}}):=0
for
|m_{\mathbf{n}}|=0
.
Then, we obtain for
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}={\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\circ{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}=(\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}s_{\mathbf{n}}^{(j)})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}
the relation

\sum_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}|\tilde{s}_{\mathbf{n}}^{(j)}|^{2}=1
for
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}} \neq 0
,
and
\sum_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}|\tilde{s}_{\mathbf{n}}^{(j)}|^{2}=0
for
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}=0
, where
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
satisfies the generalized model (
2.4
).
According to (
2.5
), we set
\tilde{m}_{\mathbf{n}}=\frac{1}{\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}}m_{\mathbf{n}}
for
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}\neq 0
and
\tilde{m}_{\mathbf{n}}=0
for
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}=0
,
such that
\check{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)} ={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)} =\tilde{\mathbf{m}}\circ\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
. Then we conclude for all
\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}
with
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}\neq 0

\displaystyle\textstyle\tilde{m}_{\mathbf{n}}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\frac{\mathrm{sign}(m_{\mathbf{n}})|m_{\mathbf{n}}|}{\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}}=\frac{\mathrm{sign}(m_{\mathbf{n}})}{\alpha_{\mathbf{n}}} \big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}|{s}_{\mathbf{n}}^{(j)}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}|m_{\mathbf{n}}|=|m_{\mathbf{n}}| \big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}|{s}_{\mathbf{n}}^{(j)}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\textstyle=\big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}|{s}^{(j)}_{\mathbf{n}}{m}_{\mathbf{n}}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}|\check{y}_{\mathbf{n}}^{(j)}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence, the sos solution (
2.6
) can be obtained from our generalized model (
2.4
) by choosing
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}
as in (
2.5
).
This observation shows that the application of the sos condition is another way to resolve the ambiguity problem in the discrete parallel MRI setting. Finally, we note that
m_{\mathbf{n}}
can only be determined if
\sum_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}|s_{\mathbf{n}}^{(j)}|^{2}>0
.

Remark 2.1

In subspace algorithms as
[
42
]
and
[
26
]
, where the sensitivities are computed from the
k
-space data in the ACS region in a first step by solving separate eigenvalue problems at every pixel index
\mathbf{n}
, the sos condition is inherently employed by computing normalized eigenvectors
(s_{\mathbf{n}}^{(j)})_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}
. However, then still the phase factors for every
\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}
need to be suitably chosen. This problem is avoided in our approach by taking the model (
2.2
) or (
2.4
).

3
MOCCA Algorithm for Reconstruction from Incomplete
k
-space Data

In this section we will derive the MOCCA algorithm that reconstructs
\mathbf{m}=(m_{\mathbf{n}})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}
and the sensitivities

{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
(which are by (
2.2
) already determined by

{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}=(c_{\mathbf{r}}^{(j)})_{\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}}
) from the acquired subset
{\mathcal{P}}{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}
,
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
. As seen before, (
2.1
) with (
2.2
) requires to determine only
N^{2}+L^{2}N_{c}
parameters.
Therefore, we should be able to recover these parameters from incomplete
k
-space measurements.

3.1
MOCCA Algorithm

Our approach consists of two steps.
First we recover the sensitivities
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
from the acquired
k
-space data in the (ACS) region using only the discretization model (
2.1
) and the sensitivity model (
2.2
). That is, we have to recover

{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}
,
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
, determining
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
via (
2.2
).
In the second step of the algorithm we will reconstruct
\mathbf{m}
.

Step 1: Reconstruction of
{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}
.

First, we derive a relation between
{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}
and the
k
-space data
\mathbf{y}^{(j)}
that we can apply to recover
{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}
only from the ACS region.

Theorem 3.1

Assume that the vectorized coil sensitivities
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N^{2}}
satisfy
(\ref{sjk})
, i.e.,
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}={\mathbf{W}}{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}
for
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
, with
\mathbf{c}^{(j)}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{L^{2}}
as in
(\ref{sj})
.
If the model
(\ref{discmodel})
is satisfied then

\displaystyle\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}-(\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq 0}}^{N_{c}-1}{\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(\ell)})&{\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(0)}&\ldots&{\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(0)}\\
{\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(1)}&-(\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq 1}}^{N_{c}-1}{\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(\ell)})&\ldots&{\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(1)}\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
{\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(N_{c}-1)}&\ldots&{\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(N_{c}-1)}&-(\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq N_c-1}}^{N_{c}-1}{\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(\ell)})\end{array}\!\!\!\right)\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}{\mathbf{c}}^{(0)}\\
{\mathbf{c}}^{(1)}\\
{\mathbf{c}}^{(2)}\\
\vdots\\
{\mathbf{c}}^{(N_{c}-1)}\end{array}\!\!\!\right)={\mathbf{0}},

where
{\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(j)}:=({y}^{(j)}_{(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\mathbf{r})\scriptsize{{\mathrm{mod}}}\,\Lambda_{N}})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{N},\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N^{2}\times L^{2}}
.
The matrix in
(\ref{gross})
is called the MOCCA-matrix
{\mathbf{A}}_{N}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N^{2}N_{c}\times L^{2}N_{c}}
, such that
(\ref{gross})
reads
{\mathbf{A}}_{N}{\mathbf{c}}={\mathbf{0}}
.

Proof.

Model (
2.1
) together with (
2.2
) (resp. (
2.3
)) leads to

\check{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}:={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}={\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}\circ\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{m}\circ({\mathbf{W}}{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)})=({\mathbf{W}}{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)})\circ\mathbf{m}

(3.10)

for
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
. Therefore, we also find

\textstyle\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}\check{\mathbf{y}}^{(\ell)}=\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}({\mathbf{W}}{\mathbf{c}}^{(\ell)})\circ\mathbf{m}=({\mathbf{W}}\,\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}{\mathbf{c}}^{(\ell)})\circ\mathbf{m}

and it follows from (
3.10
) that

\displaystyle{\check{\mathbf{y}}}^{(j)}\circ({\mathbf{W}}\,\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}{\mathbf{c}}^{(\ell)})

\displaystyle=\textstyle({\mathbf{W}}\,\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}{\mathbf{c}}^{(\ell)})\circ\check{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}=({\mathbf{W}}\,\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}{\mathbf{c}}^{(\ell)})\circ({\mathbf{W}}\,{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)})\circ\mathbf{m}

\displaystyle=\textstyle({\mathbf{W}}\,\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}{\mathbf{c}}^{(\ell)})\circ\mathbf{m}\circ({\mathbf{W}}{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)})=(\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}\check{\mathbf{y}}^{(\ell)})\circ({\mathbf{W}}\,{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}).

(3.11)

This is equivalent with

-\mathrm{diag}\Big(\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}\check{\mathbf{y}}^{(\ell)}\Big)\,{\mathbf{W}}{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}+\mathrm{diag}({\check{\mathbf{y}}}^{(j)})\,{\mathbf{W}}\,\Big(\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}{\mathbf{c}}^{(\ell)}\Big)={\mathbf{0}},\qquad j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1.

Application of the
2D
-Fourier matrix
{\mathcal{F}}
to the vectorized images yields

\textstyle[-{\mathcal{F}}\,\mathrm{diag}(\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}\check{\mathbf{y}}^{(\ell)})\,{\mathbf{W}},\,{\mathcal{F}}\,\mathrm{diag}({\check{\mathbf{y}}}^{(j)})\,{\mathbf{W}}]\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}\\
\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}{\mathbf{c}}^{(\ell)}\end{array}\right)={\mathbf{0}},\qquad j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1.

(3.12)

For the
{\mathbf{r}}
-th column of the matrix
{\mathcal{F}}\,\mathrm{diag}(\check{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}){\mathbf{W}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N^{2}\times L^{2}}
we observe that

\displaystyle\textstyle{\mathcal{F}}\,\mathrm{diag}(\check{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)})(\omega_{N}^{-\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{r}})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}

\displaystyle=(\omega_{N}^{\boldsymbol{\nu}\cdot\mathbf{n}})_{\boldsymbol{\nu},\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}\,(\omega_{N}^{-\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\check{y}^{(j)}_{\mathbf{n}})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\big(\sum\limits_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}\check{y}^{(j)}_{\mathbf{n}}\,\omega_{N}^{\mathbf{n}\cdot(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\mathbf{r})}\big)_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{N}}=\big({y}^{(j)}_{(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\mathbf{r})\scriptsize{{\mathrm{mod}}}\,\Lambda_{N}}\big)_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{N}}.

(3.13)

Hence, the
\boldsymbol{\nu}
-th row of
{\mathcal{F}}\,\mathrm{diag}(\check{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}){\mathbf{W}}
contains the
L^{2}
components
y^{(j)}_{(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\mathbf{r})\scriptsize{{\mathrm{mod}}}\,\Lambda_{N}}
for
\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}
, i.e., all components of
\mathbf{y}^{(j)}
in the
(L\times L)
-block around pixel
\boldsymbol{\nu}
in the matrix representation of
\mathbf{y}^{(j)}
(using periodic boundary conditions).
Thus, (
3.12
) implies for
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
,

\displaystyle\Big(-\big(\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}y^{(\ell)}_{(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\mathbf{r})\scriptsize{{\mathrm{mod}}}\,\Lambda_{N}}\big)_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{N},\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}},({y}^{(j)}_{(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\mathbf{r})\scriptsize{{\mathrm{mod}}}\,\Lambda_{N}})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{N},\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}}\Big)\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}\\
\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}{\mathbf{c}}^{(\ell)}\end{array}\right)={\mathbf{0}}.

Equivalently, with

{\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(j)}:=({y}^{(j)}_{(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\mathbf{r})\scriptsize{{\mathrm{mod}}}\,\Lambda_{N}})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{N},\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N^{2}\times L^{2}}
we obtain (
3.1
).
∎

If the nullspace of
{\mathbf{A}}_{N}
is one-dimensional, then
\mathbf{c}
is uniquely determined up to a global constant.
Obviously, it is sufficient to use a much smaller number of rows of the matrix
{\mathbf{A}}_{N}
above to recover
\mathbf{c}
.
Therefore, we just need to employ the given
k
-space data from the ACS region and consider only the matrix blocks

{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(j)}:=({y}^{(j)}_{(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\mathbf{r})\scriptsize{{\mathrm{mod}}}\,\Lambda_{N}})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{M},\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{M^{2}\times L^{2}}
.
We construct the MOCCA-matrix
{\mathbf{A}}_{M}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{M^{2}N_{c}\times L^{2}N_{c}}
similarly as in (
3.1
), where the block matrices
{\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(j)}
are replaced by their submatrices
{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(j)}
with only
M^{2}
rows.
If the nullspace of
{\mathbf{A}}_{M}
is still one-dimensional, then
{\mathbf{A}}_{M}\,\mathbf{c}={\mathbf{0}}
can be solved, and
{\mathbf{c}}
is uniquely determined up to a global (complex) factor.
We choose
\mathbf{c}
with
\|\mathbf{c}\|_{2}=1
.
Then, we find the coil sensitivity vectors
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}=\mathbf{W}\,\mathbf{c}^{(j)}
,
j=0,\ldots,{N_{c}-1}
.

Step 2: Reconstruction of
\mathbf{m}
.

Having determined
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
, we compute
\mathbf{m}
using the SENSE approach
[
34
]
.
In a first step, we normalize
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
.
We apply now the generalized model (
2.4
) and compute
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}={\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\circ{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
with
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}} =(\gamma_{\mathbf{n}})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}
as in (
2.5
),
where
\alpha_{\mathbf{n}}:=1
. We obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{\mathbf{n}}^{(j)}:=\left\{ \begin{array}[]{ll}\Big(\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{N_{c}-1}|s_{\mathbf{n}}^{(\ell)}|^{2}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}s_{\mathbf{n}}^{(j)}&\text{if}\;\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{N_{c}-1}|s_{\mathbf{n}}^{(\ell)}|^{2}>\epsilon,\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

where theoretically
\epsilon=0
, and numerically
\epsilon
is a small threshold larger than
0
.
Then, model (
2.1
) still holds in the form

{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}={\mathcal{F}}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}\circ\tilde{\mathbf{m}})
for
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
with
\tilde{\mathbf{m}}:={\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{-1}\circ\mathbf{m}
.
To reconstruct
\tilde{\mathbf{m}}
, we solve the minimization problem

\displaystyle\textstyle\tilde{\mathbf{m}}:=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}\limits_{{\mathbf{m}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N^{2}}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}\|{\mathcal{P}}{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}-({\mathcal{P}}\,{\mathcal{F}}\,\mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}))\,{\mathbf{m}}\|_{2}^{2}\right),

(3.18)

which leads to the linear system

\displaystyle\textstyle\Big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}({\mathbf{B}}^{(j)})^{*}\,{\mathbf{B}}^{(j)}\Big)\,\tilde{\mathbf{m}}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}({\mathbf{B}}^{(j)})^{*}\,{\mathcal{P}} {\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}

(3.19)

with
{\mathbf{B}}^{(j)}:={\mathcal{P}} {\mathcal{F}} \mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})
.
Thus,
\tilde{\mathbf{m}}
is uniquely determined if the positive semidefinite coefficient matrix
\sum_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}({\mathbf{B}}^{(j)})^{*}\,{\mathbf{B}}^{(j)}
is invertible.
The obtained solution set
(\tilde{\mathbf{m}},\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})
with
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
in (
3.1
) and
\tilde{\mathbf{m}}
in (
3.18
) now refers to the generalized coil sensitivity model (
2.4
) (with
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
as in (
2.5
) and
\alpha_{\mathbf{n}}=1
), while the solution set
(\mathbf{m},{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})
with
\mathbf{m}={\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\circ\tilde{\mathbf{m}}

refers to (
2.2
).
Replacing now the components of
\tilde{\mathbf{m}}
by its modulus,
|\tilde{\mathbf{m}}|:=(|\tilde{m}_{\mathbf{n}}|)_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}
, is equivalent with replacing the coefficients
\alpha_{\mathbf{n}}=1
by
\alpha_{\mathbf{n}} =\mathrm{sign}(m_{\mathbf{n}})
as in (
2.5
),
such that the resulting solution
|\tilde{\mathbf{m}}|
satisfies the sos condition (
2.6
).
Finally, there is still one ambiguity, which can be used to normalize the image
\tilde{\mathbf{m}}
.

The complete reconstruction algorithm to compute
(\tilde{\mathbf{m}},\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})
is summarized in Algorithm
3.2
.

Algorithm 3.2

[MOCCA algorithm for reconstruction of
\mathbf{m}
and
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
]

Input:
Incomplete
k
-space data
{\mathcal{P}}{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}
,
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
, with

({\mathcal{P}}{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}=y_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{(j)}

for
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}}
and

({\mathcal{P}}{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}=0
for
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{N}\setminus\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}}
.

L=2n+1
determining the support of the trig. polynomials
s^{(j)}
in
(\ref{sjk})
in
k
-space.

\Lambda_{M+L-1}\subset\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}}\subset\Lambda_{N}
, where
M\geq L
.
Regularization parameter
\beta\geq 0
.

1.

Build the matrices
{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(j)}:=({y}^{(j)}_{(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\mathbf{r})\scriptsize{{\mathrm{mod}}}\,\Lambda_{N}})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{M},\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}}
,
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
, and

\displaystyle{\mathbf{A}}_{M}:=\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}-(\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq 0}}^{N_{c}-1}{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(\ell)})&{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(0)}&\ldots&{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(0)}\\
{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(1)}&\!\!\!-(\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq 1}}^{N_{c}-1}{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(\ell)})&\ldots&{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(1)}\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(N_{c}-1)}&\ldots&{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(N_{c}-1)}&\!\!\!-(\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq N_c-1}}^{N_{c}-1}{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(\ell)})\end{array}\!\!\!\right).

2.

Compute a singular vector
{\mathbf{c}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{c}L^{2}}
of
{\mathbf{A}}_{M}
of norm
1
corresponding to the smallest singular value of
{\mathbf{A}}_{M}
.

3.

Extract the vectors
{\mathbf{c}}^{(j)}
, from
\mathbf{c}=((\mathbf{c}^{(0)})^{T},(\mathbf{c}^{(1)})^{T},\ldots,(\mathbf{c}^{(N_{c}-1)})^{T})^{T}
.

Compute
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}={\mathbf{W}}\,\mathbf{c}^{(j)}
,
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
by FFT, where
{\mathbf{W}} :=(\omega_{N}^{-\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{r}})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N},\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}}
.

4.

Compute
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{\mathbf{n}})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}:=\sum_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}\overline{{{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}}}\circ{{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}}
, where
\circ
is the pointwise product.
Set
{\mathbf{d}}^{+}=(d_{\mathbf{n}}^{+})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}
with
d_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}:=\frac{1}{d_{\mathbf{n}}}
for
|d_{\mathbf{n}}|>\epsilon
and
d_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}=0
otherwise.

5.

For
j=0:N_{c}-1
compute

\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}:=({\mathbf{d}}^{+})^{\frac{1}{2}}\circ{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}=(\sqrt{d}^{+}_{\mathbf{n}}s_{\mathbf{n}}^{(j)})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}
.

6.

Solve the equation system

\displaystyle\textstyle\Big(\beta\,{\mathbf{I}} +\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}({\mathbf{B}}^{(j)})^{*}{\mathbf{B}}^{(j)}\Big)\,\tilde{\mathbf{m}}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}({\mathbf{B}}^{(j)})^{*}\,{\mathcal{P}}\,{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}

(3.24)

with
{\mathbf{B}}^{(j)}:={\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{F}}\,\mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})
, either iteratively or directly,
to compute
\tilde{\mathbf{m}}
.

7.

Set
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}:=(\mathrm{sign}(\tilde{m}_{\mathbf{n}})\tilde{s}^{(j)}_{\mathbf{n}})_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}
for
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
and

\tilde{\mathbf{m}}:=(|\tilde{m}_{\mathbf{n}}|)_{\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}}
.

Output:

(\tilde{\mathbf{m}}
,
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}),

j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
satisfying
(\ref{models1})
,
(\ref{general})
, and
(\ref{sos1})
.

Remark 3.3

1. To compute the singular vector to the smallest singular value
\sigma_{\min}
of
{\mathbf{A}}_{M}

one can employ an inverse power iteration to
{\mathbf{A}}_{M}^{*}{\mathbf{A}}_{M}
which converges fast in our case.

2. Beside the MOCCA matrix
{\mathbf{A}}_{M}
in
(\ref{AM})
, there exist many other matrices that serve the same purpose to
determine the vectors
\mathbf{c}=({\mathbf{c}}^{(j)})_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}
.
Taking for example

{\mathbf{A}}_{M,0}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}-{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(1)}&{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(0)}&{\mathbf{0}}&\ldots&{\mathbf{0}}\\
{\mathbf{0}}&-{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(2)}&{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(1)}&\ldots&{\mathbf{0}}\\
\vdots&&&\ddots&\vdots\\
{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(N_{c}-1)}&\ldots&{\mathbf{0}}&{\mathbf{0}}&-{\mathbf{Y}}_{M,L}^{(0)}\end{array}\right),

we conclude similarly as in
(\ref{reljj})
that
{\mathbf{A}}_{M,0}{\mathbf{c}}={\mathbf{0}}
.

3. Relations of the type
\check{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}\circ{\mathbf{s}}^{(\ell)}-\check{\mathbf{y}}^{(\ell)}\circ{\mathbf{s}}^{{(j)}}={\mathbf{0}}
for
j,\,\ell\in\{0,\ldots,N_{c}-1\}
have been used also in other papers, see e.g.
[
12
]
. But without using a suitable model for
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
, these relations are not sufficient to derive a reconstruction method for
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
.

4. Note that for the special case
\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}} =\Lambda_{N}
(completely acquired
k
-space data), the coefficient matrix of the system (
3.24
) simplifies to

\textstyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}({\mathbf{B}}^{(j)})^{*}{\mathbf{B}}^{(j)}\!=\!\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}\mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})^{*}{\mathcal{F}}^{*}{\mathcal{F}}\mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})\!=\!N^{2}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}\mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})^{*}\mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})\!=\!N^{2}{\mathbf{I}}_{N^{2}},

such that

\mathbf{m}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}\mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})^{*}{\mathcal{F}}^{*}{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}=\sum_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}\mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})^{*}\check{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}
.

5. To improve the condition number of the coefficient matrix in (
3.19
),
it can
be replaced by the positive definite matrix
\beta\,{\mathbf{I}}+\sum_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}({\mathbf{B}}^{(j)})^{*}\,{\mathbf{B}}^{(j)}
with small
\beta>0
and the identity matrix
{\mathbf{I}}
of size
N^{2}\times N^{2}
. This regularization is often used for least square methods, see e.g.
[
28
]
.

6. In step 2 of the MOCCA algorithm to reconstruct the magnetization image
\mathbf{m}
the
least squares problem (
3.18
) can be simply extended by incorporating a
further regularization term for
\mathbf{m}
and considering instead the minimization problem

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{m}}=\textstyle\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}\limits_{{\mathbf{m}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N^{2}}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathcal{P}} {\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}-({\mathcal{P}}\,{\mathcal{F}}\,\mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}))\,{\mathbf{m}}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\Phi({\mathbf{m}})\right).

(3.25)

Here,
\Phi(\mathbf{m})
denotes a suitable constraint on the image
\mathbf{m}
, and
\lambda>0
is the regularization parameter. As usual in image processing applications, we can for example force that
\mathbf{m}
has a sparse representation in a suitably chosen transformed domain, i.e.,
\Phi(\mathbf{m}):=\|{\mathbf{T}}\mathbf{m}\|_{1}
, where
{\mathbf{T}}
denotes the transform matrix and
\|\cdot\|_{1}
is the usual 1-norm of a vector. Note that in
[
19
]
, actually two regularization functionals for
\mathbf{m}
have been proposed, including the total variation regularization. In
[

]

3.2
Complexity of the MOCCA Algorithm

Algorithm
3.2
requires

{\mathcal{O}}(N_{c}\,N^{2}\log N)
operations. In step 2, the singular vector corresponding to the smallest singular value of the MOCCA matrix
{\mathbf{A}}_{M}
can be obtained by an SVD of
{\mathbf{A}}_{M}
with at most
{\mathcal{O}}((N_{c}M^{2})^{2}N_{c}L^{2})
operations (disregarding the block Hankel structure of
{\mathbf{A}}_{M}
). We can assume here that
N_{c}M^{2}L<{\mathcal{O}}(N\log N)
such that this effort is less than
{\mathcal{O}}(N_{c}\,N^{2}\log N)
. As we will see in the numerical experiments, small numbers
L
and
M
can be chosen, e.g.
L=5
and
M=20
such that the needed ACS region is covered by
24
ACS lines.
The
N_{c}
matrix-vector multiplications in step 3 require
{\mathcal{O}}(N_{c}\,N^{2}\,L^{2})
operations.
Step 4 needs
{\mathcal{O}}(N_{c}\,N^{2})
operations. Finally, step 5 is the most expensive step, since it requires to solve a large linear system. However, for regular sampling patterns
{\mathcal{P}}
usually taken in parallel MRI (e.g. each second, third, or fourth row/column is acquired), the structure of the coefficient matrix can be employed to derive fast solvers with
{\mathcal{O}}(N_{c}N^{2}\log N)
operations, see Section
3.3.2
.
Furthermore, in Section
3.3.1
we propose an iterative algorithm to solve (
3.24
) efficiently.

3.3
Efficient Solution of the Least-Squares Problem

Note that the coefficient matrix
\beta\,{\mathbf{I}}+\sum_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}({\mathbf{B}}^{(j)})^{*}\,{\mathbf{B}}^{(j)}
(with
\beta\geq 0
)
in (

) is of size
N^{2}\times N^{2}
.
Therefore, despite its special structure, it is very large for large
N
.
To solve (

) efficiently, we propose
an iterative algorithm and a direct algorithm, where the latter one can be applied in case of structured acquired data.

3.3.1
Weighted Jacobi-Richardson Iteration

We propose the following simple iteration algorithm to solve (

).

Algorithm 3.4

[Weighted Jacobi-Richardson iteration for

]

Input:

{\mathcal{P}}\,{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
,

,

,

\epsilon>0

Remark 3.5

Algorithm

For accelerated convergence in Algorithm

[

3.4
Nonlinear Local Smoothing

As a post-processing step, we use a local nonlinear smoothing scheme as proposed e.g. in
[
32
]
. To every value
m_{\mathbf{n}}
,
\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{N}
, of the reconstructed magnetization image we apply the following procedure,

\displaystyle m_{\mathbf{n}}^{s}:=m_{\mathbf{n}}+\tau_{\mathbf{n}}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in(\Lambda_{1}\setminus\{{\mathbf{0}}\})\cap\Lambda_{N}}\frac{g(|m_{\mathbf{n}}-m_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{r}}|)}{\|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{r}\|^{2}}(m_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{r}}-m_{\mathbf{n}}),

where
\Lambda_{1}:=\{-1,0,1\}\times\{-1,0,1\}
,
\tau_{\mathbf{n}}:=\big(\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in(\Lambda_{1}\setminus\{{\mathbf{0}}\})\cap\Lambda_{N}}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{r}\|^{2}}\big)^{-1}
, and with
a function
g
which is a so-called diffusivity function. In our experiments, we have applied the Perona-Malik diffusivity
g(s)=\frac{1}{1+s^{2}/\lambda}
with
\lambda
as given in Section
5
.
This procedure is only one step of the smoothing algorithm in
[
32
]
, which can be interpreted as a discretized nonlinear diffusion smoothing.

{\mathbf{c}}:=((\mathbf{c}^{(0)})^{T},\ldots,(\mathbf{c}^{(N_{c}-1)})^{T})^{T}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{c}L^{2}}
.
By Lemma
4.1
, each further vector
{\mathbf{v}}=(({\mathbf{v}}^{(0)})^{T},({\mathbf{v}}^{(1)})^{T},\ldots,({\mathbf{v}}^{(N_{c}-1)})^{T})^{T}
with
{\mathbf{v}}^{(j)}=(v_{\mathbf{r}}^{(j)})_{\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{L^{2}}
in the nullspace of
{\mathbf{A}}_{N}
satisfies (
4.3
).
Condition (
4.2
) ensures that
c^{(j)}(\mathbf{z})
,
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
, have full degree
2(L-1)
, such that the (bivariate) rational functions

\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}\frac{c^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{z})}{c^{(j)}(\mathbf{z})}=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}\frac{z_{1}^{n}z_{2}^{n}c^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{z})}{z_{1}^{n}z_{2}^{n}c^{(j)}(\mathbf{z})}
are of type
(2(L-1),2(L-1))
.
This type is almost surely minimal, i.e.,
\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}z_{1}^{n}z_{2}^{n}c^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{z})
and
z_{1}^{n}z_{2}^{n}c^{(j)}(\mathbf{z})
almost surely have no common zeros.
For each
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
we conclude: If
v^{(j)}(\mathbf{z})
is not the zero polynomial, then (
4.3
) implies that

\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}\frac{c^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{z})}{c^{(j)}(\mathbf{z})}=\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\ell=0}{\ell\neq j}}^{N_{c}-1}\frac{v^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{z})}{v^{(j)}(\mathbf{z})}

almost everywhere, and since
v^{(j)}(\mathbf{z})
is by construction a Laurent polynomial of degree at most
2(L-1)
,
it follows that
v^{(j)}(\mathbf{z})=\mu c^{(j)}(\mathbf{z})
with some global constant
\mu
which does not depend on
j
.
Hence,
{\mathbf{A}}_{N}
in
(\ref{gross})
has a nullspace of dimension 1.
∎

Remark 4.3

The conditions of Lemma

can be easily satisfied.
For example,

already satisfies the rank assumption, if

\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{2L-1}
.
If

If (

In practice, the considered models (

in step 4 of Algorithm

5
.

Proof.

(4.6)

The iteration procedure in step 2 of Algorithm

\displaystyle\textstyle\tilde{\mathbf{m}}_{\kappa+1}\!

\displaystyle=\textstyle\!\Big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}\!\!\! \mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})^{*}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}((1-\frac{\beta}{N^{2}}){\mathbf{I}}-{\mathcal{P}}){\mathcal{F}} \mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})\!\Big)\tilde{\mathbf{m}}_{\kappa}\!+\!\!\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}\!\! \mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})^{*}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}{\mathbf{y}}_{0}^{(j)}

To derive a direct efficient algorithm to solve (

To avoid confusion, in this section, we explicitly distinguish between the

{\mathbf{y}}^{(j)}
,
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
,
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
,
\mathbf{m}
,

For properties of the Kronecker product of matrices and the discrete Fourier transform, we refer to Section 3.4 in
[
33

.
In matrix form, the Fourier operator

denotes the centered 2D DFT, and in vector form it can be written as

With these notations, (

We present exemplarily the case, where

8

contains

where, besides the ACS region, only every fourth column of the
N\times N

is acquired. Let

is the
N\times N
unitary matrix. For the

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}({\mathbf{B}}^{(j)})^{*}{\mathbf{B}}^{(j)}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{vec}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}))^{*}({\mathbf{F}}_{N}\otimes{\mathbf{F}}_{N})^{*}({\mathbf{P}}_{1}\otimes{\mathbf{I}}_{N})({\mathbf{F}}_{N}\otimes{\mathbf{F}}_{N})\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{vec}(\tilde{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}))

and defining
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}:=\mathrm{vec}(({\mathbf{d}}^{+})^{\frac{1}{2}})\circ{\mathbf{g}}=\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{vec}(({\mathbf{d}}^{+})^{\frac{1}{2}})){\mathbf{g}}
with
{\mathbf{d}}^{+}
as in step 4 of Algorithm
3.2
we find

\textstyle{\mathbf{B}}^{(j)}{\mathbf{g}} ={\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{F}}\big(\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{vec}({\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}\circ({\mathbf{d}}^{+})^{\frac{1}{2}})){\mathbf{g}}\big)={\mathcal{P}}{\mathcal{F}}\,\mathrm{vec}({\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})\circ\tilde{\mathbf{g}}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}{\mathcal{P}}\big(({\mathcal{F}}\mathrm{vec}({\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}))*({\mathcal{F}}\tilde{\mathbf{g}})\big)={\mathbf{0}},

where
*
denotes the 2D discrete
N
-periodic convolution.
From
\mathrm{vec}({\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})={\mathbf{W}}\mathbf{c}^{(j)}
we obtain

\mathrm{vec}(\hat{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}):={\mathcal{F}}\mathrm{vec}({\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})=N^{2}({c}^{(j)}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{N}}
is the zero-extension of
\mathbf{c}^{(j)}
from
\Lambda_{L}
to
\Lambda_{N}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{F}}\mathrm{vec}({\mathbf{s}}^{(j)})*{\mathcal{F}}\tilde{\mathbf{g}})

\displaystyle=\big(\hat{s}_{(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\mathbf{r})\scriptsize{{\mathrm{mod}}}\,\Lambda_{N}}^{(j)}\big)_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}},\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{N}}\,({\mathcal{F}}\tilde{\mathbf{g}})

\displaystyle=N^{2}\big({c}_{(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\mathbf{r})\scriptsize{{\mathrm{mod}}}\,\Lambda_{N}}^{(j)}\big)_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}},\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{N}}\,({\mathcal{F}}\tilde{\mathbf{g}})={\mathbf{0}}

for
j=0,\ldots,N_{c}-1
.
Thus,
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}={\mathbf{0}}
, if and only if the matrix in (
4.5
)
has full rank
N^{2}
. Therefore, also
{\mathbf{g}}={\mathbf{0}}
, since
{\mathbf{d}}^{+}
has only positive components. In particular, the invertibility of
\sum_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}({\mathbf{B}}^{(j)})^{*}\,{\mathbf{B}}^{(j)}
implies
N_{c}|\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}}|\geq N^{2}
and

\Lambda_{N}\subset(\boldsymbol{\nu}+\Lambda_{L})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}}}
.
∎

Similarly, we obtain a condition for other regular patterns of acquired data, as e.g. for the special example that each second row and each second column of the
k
-space data is acquired.

Corollary 4.1

Assume that the index set of acquired measurements
\Lambda_{\mathcal{P}}
contains beside the ACS region index set
\Lambda_{M+L-1}
the set
2\Lambda_{N/2}
, i.e., every second index in
x
-direction and
y
-direction. Then, the matrix

\sum_{j=0}^{N_{c}-1}({\mathbf{B}}^{(j)})^{*}\,{\mathbf{B}}^{(j)}
is invertible, if
the
(N_{c}\times 4)
-matrices

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{n}}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}s_{\mathbf{n}+(\frac{N}{4},\frac{N}{4})}^{(0)}&s_{\mathbf{n}+(\frac{N}{4},-\frac{N}{4})}^{(0)}&s_{\mathbf{n}+(-\frac{N}{4},\frac{N}{4})}^{(0)}&s_{\mathbf{n}+(-\frac{N}{4},-\frac{N}{4})}^{(0)}\\
s_{\mathbf{n}+(\frac{N}{4},\frac{N}{4})}^{(1)}&s_{\mathbf{n}+(\frac{N}{4},-\frac{N}{4})}^{(1)}&s_{\mathbf{n}+(-\frac{N}{4},\frac{N}{4})}^{(1)}&s_{\mathbf{n}+(-\frac{N}{4},-\frac{N}{4})}^{(1)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
s_{\mathbf{n}+(\frac{N}{4},\frac{N}{4})}^{(N_{c}-1)}&s_{\mathbf{n}+(\frac{N}{4},-\frac{N}{4})}^{(N_{c}-1)}&s_{\mathbf{n}+(-\frac{N}{4},\frac{N}{4})}^{(N_{c}-1)}&s_{\mathbf{n}+(-\frac{N}{4},-\frac{N}{4})}^{(N_{c}-1)}\end{array}\right)

have full rank
4
for each
\mathbf{n}\in\Lambda_{\frac{N}{2}}
.

Proof.

The proof follows directly from the observations in this section and in Section
3.3.2
, Remark 3.3, where the matrices
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathbf{n}} ={\mathbf{G}}_{(k,\ell)}
with
k,\ell=-\frac{N}{4},\ldots,\frac{N}{4}-1
need to be invertible to recover the components of
\mathbf{m}
.
∎

6
Conclusions

Previous sub-space methods in parallel MRI
[
38
,
42
,
10
,
23
]
are usually based on the assumption that the block Hankel matrix
({\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(0)},{\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(1)},\ldots,{\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(N_{c}-1)})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N^{2}\times L^{2}N_{c}}
, where
{\mathbf{Y}}_{N,L}^{(j)}=(y_{(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\mathbf{r})\scriptsize{{\mathrm{mod}}}\,\Lambda_{N}}^{(j)})_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{N},\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda_{L}}
with
L
being a suitable small window size, has low rank. More exactly, it is assumed that the rank of this matrix is essentially smaller than
L^{2}N_{c}
. The application of these methods based on (structured) low-rank matrix approximations and the study the reconstructed sensitivities
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
leads to the observation that the
{\mathbf{s}}^{(j)}
usually have (approximately) a small support in
k
-space, see e.g.
[
38
,
42
]
. Our new MOCCA approach directly computes the sensitivities and the magnetization image based on the a priori fixed model (
2.2
) (resp. (
2.4
)) and can therefore be seen as a counterpart of the known algorithms. Our different view provides several advantages: We can provide simple and fast reconstruction algorithms for incomplete k-space data in parallel MRI achieving similarly good reconstruction results as the best sub-space methods.

One question, which still stays to be open regarding the MOCCA approach is to determine a suitable support index set
\Lambda_{L}
for the coil sensitivities in practice. A larger support index set may lead to many singular values of the MOCCA matrix
{\mathbf{A}}_{M}
in (
1
) being close to zero. In this case, the choice of a suitable linear combination of singular vectors is crucial to achieve satisfying sensitivities and image reconstructions.
In this paper, we have exemplarily shown that the MOCCA algorithm can outperform several other methods, while the problem of finding an optimal linear combination of singular vectors in case of overestimated support set is still under investigation.

There is a close connection between the MOCCA approach and the subspace methods ESPIRiT and SAKE which we study in a forthcoming paper
[
21
]
.
A better understanding of the relation between subspace methods and sensitivity modelling will help us to answer the question of optimal recovering of sensitivity profiles with small
k
-space support and to find other sensitivity models being appropriate for parallel MRI reconstructions thereby still allowing fast reconstruction procedures.

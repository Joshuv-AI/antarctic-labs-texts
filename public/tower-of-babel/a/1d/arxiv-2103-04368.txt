Introduction

Fourier multipliers are by far the most important operators in analysis. One can say that classical harmonic analysis has been developed around Fourier multipliers. The main task there is to find criteria for the boundedness of Fourier multipliers on various function spaces, notably on
L_{p}
-spaces. However, it is in general impossible to characterize their
L_{p}
-boundedness for finite
p\neq 2
. In this regard, the most fundamental result is the celebrated Hörmander-Mikhlin multiplier theorem which asserts that if
m
is a
C^{[\frac{d}{2}]+1}
function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
such that

\sup_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq[\frac{d}{2}]+1}\,\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}}|\xi|^{|\alpha|}|\partial^{\alpha}m(\xi)|<\infty,

where
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d})
denotes a multi-index of nonnegative integers and
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{d}
,
then the associated Fourier multiplier
T_{m}
, defined via Fourier transform by
\widehat{T_{m}(f)}=m\widehat{f}
, is a bounded map on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

for all
1<p<\infty
. A similar statement holds for the periodic case, i.e., for
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
instead of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. More precisely, if
m:{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{C}}
satisfies

(1)

\|m\|_{{\rm HM}}=\sup_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq[\frac{d}{2}]+1}\,\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|k|^{|\alpha|}|\partial^{\alpha}m(k)|<\infty,

where
\partial^{\alpha}=\partial_{k_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{k_{d}}^{\alpha_{d}}
denotes the partial discrete difference operator of order
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d})
,
then the associated Fourier multiplier
T_{m}
is a bounded map on
L_{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
for all
1<p<\infty
.

In noncommutative harmonic analysis and operator algebras, the study Fourier multipliers on general groups is of paramount importance. These multipliers are also called Herz-Schur multipliers in the literature. The underlying
L_{p}
-spaces are then the noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces on the group von Neumann algebras. This line of investigation finds one of its origins in the study of approximation properties of operator algebras inaugurated by Haagerup’s pioneering work
[
6
]
on Fourier multipliers on free groups in which he solved the longstanding problem on Grothendieck’s approximation property for the reduced C*-algebra of a free group: this algebra has the completely bounded approximation property. Later, together with De Cannière
[
2
]
and Cowling
[
5
]
, Haagerup studied completely bounded multipliers on the Fourier algebras of locally compact groups.

One of the remarkable recent achievements in the interplay between multipliers and noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces is the result of Lafforgue and de la Salle
[
19
]
asserting that the noncommutative
L_{p}
-space with

associated to the von Neumann algebra of

fails the completely bounded approximation property. Haagerup and coauthors
[
9

10

8

extended their result to connected simple Lie groups; later, de Laat and de la Salle
[
18

discovered that

multipliers are very tightly connected with Banach space geometry and group representations. Other striking illustrations of this interplay are Junge and Parcet’s work
[
16

on the Maurey-Rosenthal factorization for noncommutative

-spaces which relies on Schur multiplier estimates. Multipliers are also intimately related to functional calculus in von Neumann algebras as shown by the resolution of Krein’s celebrated Lipschitz continuity problem in noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces by Potapov and Sukochev
[
32
]
, see also

3

where the crucial use of Fourier multipliers is more apparent.

In a recent series of articles

, Haagerup and co-authors obtained a beautiful handy characterization of the completely bounded radial multipliers on the von Neumann algebras (i.e.

For a complex function

being the unit circle equipped with normalized Haar measure. In this case, every function

{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
satisfying (
1

L_{p}
-multiplier on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

Throughout the article,

will denote a free group on infinite generators

(2)

Given a complex function

{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

\displaystyle M_{m}(\lambda(g))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}m(k_{1},\cdots,k_{d})\,\lambda(g)&\textrm{ if}&d\leq n,\\
m(k_{1},\cdots k_{n},0,\cdots,0)\,\lambda(g)&\textrm{ if}&d>n\end{array}\right.

for every

as in (
2

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

and
1<p<\infty
. If

is a completely bounded

-multiplier on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, then
M_{m}
extends to a completely bounded map on
L_{p}(\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{\infty})
.
In particular, the conclusion holds if

satisfies (
1
).

To achieve Theorem
1.1
, we establish a new platform to transfer the
L_{p}
-complete boundedness of Fourier multipliers on tori to Fourier multipliers on free groups. The key tool is a group of unitary actions on
L_{2}(\widehat{}\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
and its complete boundedness on
L_{p}(\widehat{}\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
for all

. Given
z=(z_{j,i})_{1\leq j\leq d,\,1\leq i<\infty}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\infty}
, let

\alpha^{Ld}_{z}(\lambda(g))=z_{1,i_{1}}^{k_{1}}z_{2,i_{2}}^{k_{2}}\cdots z_{d,i_{d}}^{k_{d}}\lambda(g)

for

as in (

) (with

if

).
It is obvious that
\alpha^{Ld}
extends to a unitary action of the group
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\infty}
on
L_{2}(\widehat{}\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

The above action
\alpha^{Ld}
of
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{\infty}
on
L_{2}(\widehat{}\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
extends to a uniformly completely bounded action on
L_{p}(\widehat{}\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
for every
1<p<\infty
with constant depending only on
d
and
p
.

Theorem
1.1
will follow from Theorem
1.2
and a standard transference argument. Our initial proof of Theorem

is very long and technical. We have then luckily found a new and shorter argument; the new one depends, in a crucial way, on an analogous result on amalgamated free products of von Neumann algebras. The new approach has a more advantage that it allows us to easily extend Theorems

In this context, let
(\mathcal{A}_{i},\tau_{i})_{i\in I}

be the conditional expectation from

to

Assume that we are given maps

We can define a map
T^{Ld}
as follows.
T^{Ld}(b)=b

\displaystyle T^{Ld}(a)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}T_{1,i_{1}}(a_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes T_{d,i_{d}}(a_{d})\otimes a_{d+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}&\textrm{if}&d<n,\\

for

The following theorem and its proof contain the main novelty of the article, it gives a surprisingly simple condition to ensure the complete
boundedness of the map

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Assume that the

and on

uniformly in

Then

with

\|T^{Ld}\|_{{\rm cb}(L_{p}(\mathcal{A}))}\lesssim_{p,d}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\sup_{i\in I}\Big(\|T_{j,i}\|_{{\rm cb}(L_{p}(\mathcal{A}_{i}))}+\|T_{j,i}\|_{{\rm cb}(L_{2}(\mathcal{A}_{i}))}\Big).

It is clear that the assumption above is necessary for the validity of the conclusion. Thus the theorem gives a characterization of the complete boundedness of

on

. Another aspect to be emphasized is the fact that

is a single fixed index. One of the main ingredients in our argument is a length reduction formula for the

-norms associated with amalgamated free products that we state as Theorem

Preliminaries

This section presents some preliminaries on noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces and amalgamated free products.

Murray and von Neumann’s work
[
25
]
demonstrates von Neumann algebras as a natural framework to do noncommutative analysis. The elements in a von Neumann algerba
\mathcal{M}
can be integrated over the equipped trace
\tau
and measured by the associated
L_{p}
-norms. In the sequel,
\mathcal{M}
will denote a von
Neumann algebra with a normal faithful semifinite trace
\tau
. For each

let
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
be the noncommutative
L_{p}
-space associated with the pair
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Note that
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{M}
with the operator norm. We refer to
[
31
]
for details and more historical references.

All
L_{p}
-spaces in this article will be equipped with their natural operator space structure introduced by Pisier in
[
29
,
30
]
. We also refer to
[
30
]
for operator space theory.
In fact, what we will need from the latter theory is only
[
29
, Lemma 1.7]
that asserts a linear map

is completely bounded iff
{\rm Id}_{S_{p}}\otimes T:L_{p}(B(\ell_{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})\to L_{p}(B(\ell_{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})
is bounded; in this case, the completely bounded norm of

is equal to the usual norm of
{\rm Id}_{S_{p}}\otimes T
and denoted by
\|T\|_{{\rm cb}(L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))}
or simply
\|T\|_{{\rm cb}}
when no ambiguity is possible. Note that
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
is an operator Hilbert space and every bounded map on it is automatically completely bounded with the same norm.

We will often be concerned with group von Neumann algebras. Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group. Recall the definition of its left regular representation
\lambda
: for any
g\in\Gamma
,
\lambda(g)
is just the left translation by
g
on
\ell_{2}(\Gamma)

\lambda(g)(f)(h)=f(g^{-1}h)
for any

and

is the Dirac mass at

denotes the linear span of

is the weak* closure of

in

. The canonical trace of
\widehat{\Gamma}
is the vector state induced by

with

the identity of
\Gamma
. When
\Gamma
is abelian (e.g.

coincides with the usual

-space on the dual group of
\Gamma
.

We turn to the second part on amalgamated free products.
Let

be a family of finite von Neumann algebras equipped with normal faithful normalized traces. Let

be a common von Neumann subalgebra of

For any

\underline{i}=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})\in I^{n}

\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{B}\oplus\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})\in I^{n}\\

is a

. The natural projection

from

onto

is a conditional expectation.

is a trace on

is the finite von Neumann algebra obtained by the GNS construction from

For

For

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}=\bigoplus_{\underline{i}:i_{1}=k}\mathcal{W}_{\underline{i}}\;\textrm{ and }\;\mathcal{R}_{k}=\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}.

We denote the associated orthogonal projections on

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a von Neumann subalgebra of a semifinite von Neumann algebra
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
such that the restriction of
\tau
to
\mathcal{B}
is semifinite too. We need to introduce several norms on tensor products related to
L_{p}
-modules as in
[
17
]
.

Let
E
be a right Hilbert
\mathcal{B}
-module with
\mathcal{B}
-inner product
\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\rangle
(or
\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\rangle_{E}
if we deal with several modules).
E
is equipped with the norm induced by its inner product. However, we do not assume that
E
is complete.
A typical example is, given an index set
\mathcal{I}
, the module

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathcal{B})=\big\{(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}\subset\mathcal{B}\;:\;\sup_{S\subset\mathcal{I}\textrm{ finite}}\big\|\sum_{\alpha\in S}x_{\alpha}^{*}x_{\alpha}\big\|_{\mathcal{B}}<\infty\big\}

with inner product
\langle x,\,y\rangle=\lim_{S}\sum_{\alpha\in S}x_{\alpha}^{*}y_{\alpha}
which is well defined as a weak* limit over finite subsets of
\mathcal{I}
for inclusion. By
[
20
,
28
]
, a general module
E
can be embedded into a self-dual Hilbert module and there exist an index set
\mathcal{I}
and a right
\mathcal{B}
-module map
u=(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}:E\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathcal{B})
so that
\langle x,\,y\rangle=\langle u(x),\,u(y)\rangle.

Given
2\leq p<\infty
, we introduce a norm on the amalgamated tensor product
E\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
as follows:

\|x\|_{E^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}=\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i}^{*}\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle a_{j}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}

for
x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\otimes a_{i}\in E\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
. Equipped with this norm,
E\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
is denoted by
E^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
(the superscript
c
refers to column). In
[
17
]
, this norm is denoted by

\big\|\sum_{i}|x_{i}\rangle a_{i}\big\|_{p}.

As explained in section 3 there, via the above concrete embedding of
E
into
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathcal{B})
, the map

u\otimes{\rm Id}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}:E^{c}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathcal{B})\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\subset L_{p}\big(B(\ell_{2}(\mathcal{I}))\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})\big)

is then an isometry that allows us to view

E^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
as a subspace of the column
subspace of the last space. This also fully justifies that

\|\cdot\|_{E^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}
is indeed a norm.

Similarly, given
F
a left Hilbert
\mathcal{B}
-module, we define a norm on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}F
by

\|y\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\otimes_{p}F^{r}}=\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}b_{i}\langle y_{i},\,y_{j}\rangle b_{j}^{*}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}

for
y=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\otimes y_{i}
. This norm is denoted by
\big\|\sum_{i}b_{i}\langle y_{i}|\,\big\|_{p}
in
[
17
]
. As above,
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\otimes_{p}F^{r}
can be identified with a subspace of the row subspace of
L_{p}\big(B(\ell_{2}(\mathcal{J}))\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)
for some index set
\mathcal{J}
.

We can gather the two definitions together and introduce a norm on
E\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}F
; the resulting space is denoted by
E^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\otimes_{p}F^{r}
, it isometrically embeds into
L_{p}\big(B(\ell_{2}(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{I}))\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)
. This is independent of the choice of the sets
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}
.

We will need extra notions when we assume that
E
and
F
are
also bimodules (i.e.,
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(E)
, the algebra of
adjointable right
\mathcal{B}
-modular maps on
E
, and
\mathcal{B}\subset\,_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{L}(F)
, the algebra of adjointable left
\mathcal{B}
-modular maps
on
F
). Given
E^{\prime}
another right Hilbert
\mathcal{B}
-module, we can consider

E^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}E
as a right
\mathcal{B}
-module with the internal inner
product; consequently, we have the space
(E^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}E)^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
. Note that the norm of
(E^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}E)^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})

coincides with that of
E^{\prime c}\otimes_{p}(E^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))

when viewing
E^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
in the column subspace of

L_{p}\big(B(\ell_{2}(\mathcal{I}))\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)
as above. Similar
constructions apply to the row case too. It is clear that all these
operations are naturally associative.

Remark 3.1
.

We will often use without any reference that the above norms are injective. We mean that if
E^{\prime}\subset E
,
F^{\prime}\subset F
are submodules and
(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\tau)\subset(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is a semifinite von Neumann subalgebra, then
E^{\prime c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})\otimes_{p}F^{\prime r}
isometrically sits in
E^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\otimes_{p}F^{r}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that
T:E\to E
is a bounded right
\mathcal{B}
-modular map. Then

\big\|T\otimes{\rm Id}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}:E^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\to E^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\big\|\leq\|T\|.

If additionally
E
is equipped with a left
\mathcal{B}
-action and
T
is
\mathcal{B}
-bimodular, then for any right Hilbert
\mathcal{B}
-module
E^{\prime}

\big\|{\rm Id}_{E^{\prime}}\otimes T\otimes{\rm Id}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}\big\|_{B\big((E^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}E)^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\big)}\leq\|T\|.

Similar statements hold for left modules too.

Proof.

By
[
28
, Theorem 2.8]
, we have
\langle T(x),\,T(x)\rangle\leq\|T\|^{2}\langle x,\,x\rangle
for
x\in E
. Let
x_{i}\in E
and
b_{i}\in\mathcal{B}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Then

\langle\sum_{i}T(x_{i})b_{i},\,\sum_{i}T(x_{i})b_{i}\rangle\leq\|T\|^{2}\langle\sum_{i}x_{i}b_{i},\,\sum_{i}x_{i}b_{i}\rangle,

so we can reinterpret this inequality in the matrix algebra
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathcal{B})
as

0\leq\big(\langle T(x_{i}),\,T(x_{j})\rangle\big)_{i,j}\leq\|T\|^{2}\big(\langle x_{i},\,x_{j}\rangle\big)_{i,j}.

The latter immediately implies

\Big(\sum_{i,j}a_{i}^{*}\langle T(x_{i}),T(x_{j})\rangle a_{j}\Big)^{1/2}\leq\|T\|^{2}\Big(\sum_{i,j}a_{i}^{*}\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle a_{j}\Big)^{1/2}

for any
a_{i}\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
. This yields the first assertion.

The second follows from the same argument by noticing that
{\rm Id}_{E^{\prime}}\otimes T
is bounded on the right Hilbert
\mathcal{B}
-module
E^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}E
with norm
\|T\|
. Indeed, for finite families
x_{i}\in E
and
y_{i}\in E^{\prime}
with
1\leq i\leq n

\langle\sum_{i}y_{i}\otimes T(x_{i}),\,\sum_{i}y_{i}\otimes T(x_{i})\rangle_{E^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}E}=\sum_{i,j}\langle T(x_{i}),\langle y_{i},\,y_{j}\rangle_{E^{\prime}}T(x_{j})\rangle_{E}.

As a positive element in
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathcal{B})
, the matrix
\big(\langle y_{i},\,y_{j}\rangle_{E^{\prime}}\big)_{i,j}
can be written as

\big(\langle y_{i},\,y_{j}\rangle_{E^{\prime}}\big)_{i,j}=\sum_{k}\big(b_{k,i}^{*}\,b_{k,j}\big)_{i,j}\;\text{ with }\;b_{k,j}\in\mathcal{B}.

Thus we deduce

\displaystyle\langle\sum_{i}y_{i}\otimes T(x_{i}),\,\sum_{i}y_{i}\otimes T(x_{i})\rangle_{E^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}E}

\displaystyle\leq\|T\|^{2}\sum_{k}\langle\sum_{i}b_{k,i}x_{i},\,\sum_{i}b_{k,i}x_{i}\rangle_{E}

\displaystyle=\|T\|^{2}\langle\sum_{i}y_{i}\otimes x_{i},\,\sum_{i}y_{i}\otimes x_{i}\rangle_{E^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}E}.

This is the desired boundedness of
{\rm Id}_{E^{\prime}}\otimes T
on
E^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}E
. Thus the proposition is proved.
∎

Thanks to the identifications recalled at the beginning of this section, the previous proposition immediately implies the following

Remark 3.3
.

If
T:E\to E
is a bounded right
\mathcal{B}
-modular map, then for any left
\mathcal{B}
-module
F

\big\|T\otimes{\rm Id}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}\otimes{\rm Id}_{F}:E^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\otimes_{p}F^{r}\to E^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\otimes_{p}F^{r}\big\|\leq\|T\|.

A similar statement holds for bimodules, namely,
{\rm Id}_{E^{\prime}}\otimes T\otimes{\rm Id}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}\otimes{\rm Id}_{F}
is bounded on
(E^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}E)^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\otimes_{p}F^{r}
with norm less than or equal to
\|T\|
if additionally
E
is equipped with a left
\mathcal{B}
-action and
T
is
\mathcal{B}
-bimodular.

Remark 3.4
.

Proposition
3.2
is the modular version of the fact that the row and column
p
-operator spaces are homogeneous (i.e., when
\mathcal{B}=\mathbb{C}
and
E
and
F
are just Hilbert spaces).

A typical situation to which we will apply the previous results is the case where
E=F=\mathcal{A}
with
\mathcal{A}
a finite von Neumann algebra containing
\mathcal{B}
. The right inner product on
\mathcal{A}
is
\langle x,\,y\rangle=\mathbb{E}(x^{*}y)
and the left
\langle\langle x,\,y\rangle\rangle=\mathbb{E}(xy^{*})
,
\mathbb{E}
being the trace preserving conditional expectation from
\mathcal{A}
onto
\mathcal{B}
. In this case, we have
\mathcal{A}^{c}\otimes_{2}L_{2}(\mathcal{B})=L_{2}(\mathcal{A})
isometrically. Thus if
T:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
is right
\mathcal{B}
-modular and bounded for
\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\rangle
, it automatically extends to a map
\widetilde{T}:L_{2}(\mathcal{A})\to L_{2}(\mathcal{A})
with
\|\widetilde{T}\|_{B(L_{2}(\mathcal{A}))}\leq\|T\|
. Since
\|\langle x,\,x\rangle\|_{\mathcal{B}}=\sup_{b\in L_{2}(\mathcal{B}),\|b\|_{2}\leq 1}\|xb\|_{2}
for
x\in\mathcal{A}
, we actually have
\|\widetilde{T}\|_{B(L_{2}(\mathcal{A}))}=\|T\|
. We may still write
T
instead of
\widetilde{T}
.

We state this fact as a lemma for later use.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\tau)
be a finite von Neumann and
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
a von Neumann subalgebra with the associated conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}
. Assume that
\mathcal{A}
is equipped with the right
\mathcal{B}
-module inner product
\langle x,\,y\rangle=\mathbb{E}(x^{*}y)
. Then any bounded right
\mathcal{B}
-modular map
T:\mathcal{A}\to A
extends to a bounded map on
L_{2}(\mathcal{A})
with
\|T\|_{B(L_{2}(\mathcal{A}))}=\|T\|
.

If
T:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
is completely positive and leaves
\mathcal{B}
invariant, then it satisfies the above conditions. If additionally
\mathbb{E}\circ T\leq\mathbb{E}
, then
T
also extends to a completely bounded map on
L_{p}(\mathcal{A})
.

We will need the following

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
T:L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\to L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
be a completely bounded map. Then for any right Hilbert
\mathcal{B}
-module
E
and left Hilbert
\mathcal{B}
-module
F
we have

\big\|{\rm Id}_{E}\otimes T\otimes{\rm Id}_{F}\big\|_{{\rm cb}(E^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\otimes_{p}F^{r})}\leq\|T\|_{{\rm cb}}.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the fact that identifying
E^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\otimes F^{r}
with a subspace of
L_{p}\big(B(\ell_{2}(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{I}))\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\big)
, then
{\rm Id}_{E}\otimes T\otimes{\rm Id}_{F}
acts like
{\rm Id}\otimes T
.
∎

4.
Multipliers on amalgamated free products

This section is the core of the article and contains our major
novelty. The principal result is Theorem
4.9
that will imply
Theorem
1.3
by iterations. Throughout the section,

(\mathcal{A}_{i},\tau_{i})_{i\in I}
will denote a family of finite von Neumann
algebras containing
\mathcal{B}
as a common subalgebra. We will use notation
introduced in section
2
on amalgamated free
products, in particular,
(\mathcal{A},\tau)=*_{i\in I,\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{A}_{i},\tau_{i})
.

4.1.
An intermediate result

Given a family
\pi=(\pi_{i})_{i\in I}
of
*
-representations
\pi_{i}:\mathcal{A}_{i}\to\mathcal{A}_{i}
such that
\pi_{i}(b)=b
for all
b\in\mathcal{B}
and
\mathbb{E}\circ\pi_{i}=\mathbb{E}
, we introduce a linear map
T_{\pi}
on
\mathcal{W}
by

T_{\pi}(b)=b
for
b\in\mathcal{B}
and

T_{\pi}(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})=\pi_{i_{1}}(a_{1})\otimes a_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}

for
n\geq 1
and
a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}\in\mathcal{W}_{\underline{i}}
where
\underline{i}=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})
and
i_{1}\neq\cdots\neq i_{n}
. Define
T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}
on
\mathcal{W}
as
T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(x)=T_{\pi}(x^{*})^{*}
. Note that both
T_{\pi}
and
T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}
commute with the projections
P_{n}
.

We aim to show that
T_{\pi}
extends to a bounded map on
L_{p}(\mathcal{A})
for
1<p<\infty
. To this end, we introduce some paraproducts on
\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{W}
in the manner of
[
23
]
: for
x,y\in\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle x\overset{1,0}{{\dagger}}y

\displaystyle=xy-\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}[H_{\varepsilon}^{{\rm op}}(xH^{{\rm op}}_{\varepsilon}(y))],

\displaystyle x\overset{0,1}{{\dagger}}y

\displaystyle=xy-\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}[H_{\varepsilon}(H_{\varepsilon}(x)y)],

\displaystyle x\overset{1,1}{{\dagger}}y

\displaystyle=xy-x\overset{1,0}{{\dagger}}y-x\overset{0,1}{{\dagger}}y,

where
H_{\varepsilon}
and
H^{{\rm op}}_{\varepsilon}
are the free Hilbert transform defined in (
5
) and
\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}
is the conditional expectation over all possible choices of symmetric independent signs
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{i})
. Note that when
x
and
y
are elementary tensors, then
x\overset{1,0}{{\dagger}}y
collects in

xy
the parts that do not end in the same algebra as
y
, that is, all letters in
y
must have been simplified. Similarly,
x\overset{0,1}{{\dagger}}y
collects in
xy
the parts that do not start in the same algebra as
x
.

We will need some elementary free algebraic facts.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{W}_{l}
,
h\in\mathcal{W}_{n}
with
l,n\geq 0
.

i)

If
l>n
, then
T_{\pi}(gh)=T_{\pi}(g)h
.

ii)

If
n>l
, then
T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(gh)=gT_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(h)
.

iii)

If
l=n
, then
P_{\geq 2}[T_{\pi}(gh)]=P_{\geq 2}[T_{\pi}(g)h]
and
P_{\geq 2}[T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(gh)]=P_{\geq 2}[gT_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(h)]
.

Proof.

One can assume that
g
and
h
are elementary tensors. The first item is then clear as the first letter of
g
cannot be cancelled if
l>n
. The second is obtained by passing to adjoints. Similarly, if
n=l
,
P_{\geq 2}(gh)
is a sum of elementary tensors that all start with the first letter of
g
and end with the last letter of
h
, up to a multiplication by an element of
\mathcal{B}
.
∎

The following Cotlar type formula immediately follows from the previous lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
g,h\in\mathcal{W}
we have

(6)

P_{\geq 2}\big[T_{\pi}(g)T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(h)\big]=P_{\geq 2}\big[T_{\pi}(gT_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(h))+T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(T_{\pi}(g)h)-T_{\pi}T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(gh)\big].

Proof.

By linearity, it suffices to show the formula for
g\in\mathcal{W}_{l}
,
h\in\mathcal{W}_{n}
. Then it remains to check it by using Lemma
4.1
according to the different cases. We omit the details.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
g=g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{l}\in\mathcal{W}_{l}
,
h=h_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{1}\in\mathcal{W}_{n}
with
l,n\geq 0
, and let
g^{\prime}=g_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{l}
and
h^{\prime}=h_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{2}
.

i)

If
n>l
, then
P_{1}(gh)=\delta_{n,l+1}\mathbb{E}(gh^{\prime})h_{1}
.

ii)

If
l>n
, then
P_{1}(gh)=\delta_{n+1,l}\,g_{1}\mathbb{E}(g^{\prime}h)
.

iii)

If
l=n
, then
P_{1}(gh)=P_{1}(g_{1}\mathbb{E}(g^{\prime}h^{\prime})h_{1})
.

Proof.

This proof is easy. Let us verify only i). If
n>l+1
, then both
P_{1}(gh)
and
\mathbb{E}(gh^{\prime})h_{1}
vanish. The case
n=l+1
is checked by induction on
l
and
n
thanks to the following simplification formula:

gh=x\otimes\overset{\smash{\lower 4.01749pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\mathaccent 0{\accentclass@}017{}$}}}{\wideparen{g_{n}h_{l}}}\otimes y+x\,\mathbb{E}(g_{l}h_{n})y,

where
x=g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{l-1}
and
y=h_{n-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{1}
.
It then follows that
P_{1}(gh)=P_{1}(x\,\mathbb{E}(g_{l}h_{n})y)
.
∎

The following is a complement to the Cotlar formula (
6
).

Lemma 4.4
.

For
g,h\in\mathcal{W}
we have

(7)

\displaystyle P_{1}\big(T_{\pi}(g)T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(h)\big)=T_{\pi}\big[P_{1}\big(g\overset{1,0}{{\dagger}}T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(h)\big)\big]+T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}\big[P_{1}\big(T_{\pi}(g)\overset{0,1}{{\dagger}}h\big)\big]+T_{\pi}\big[P_{1}\big(g\overset{1,1}{{\dagger}}h\big)\big].

Proof.

We will check the formula according to the three cases in Lemma
4.3
and use notation there.

For i), we can assume
n=l+1
, otherwise all terms are 0. Then

P_{1}\big(T_{\pi}(g)T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(h)\big)=\mathbb{E}\big(T_{\pi}(g)h^{\prime}\big)\,\pi_{j_{1}}(h_{1}).

On the other hand, for elementary tensors
x\in\mathcal{W}_{(i_{1},\cdots,i_{l})}
and
y\in\mathcal{W}_{(j_{n},\cdots,j_{1})}
, we have
x\overset{1,0}{{\dagger}}y=0
as the last letter of
y
in
xy
is not cancelled. To deal with
x\overset{0,1}{{\dagger}}y
, note that

H_{\varepsilon}[P_{1}(H_{\varepsilon}(x)y)]=\varepsilon_{i_{1}}\varepsilon_{j_{1}}P_{1}(xy).

It thus follows that
P_{1}(x\overset{0,1}{{\dagger}}y)=P_{1}(xy)
since
\mathbb{E}(xy^{\prime})=0
unless

i_{1}=j_{2}
but then
j_{1}\neq j_{2}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon_{i_{1}}\varepsilon_{j_{1}})=0
. Hence the right hand
side of (
7
) is exactly
T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}\big[P_{1}\big(T_{\pi}(g)h\big)\big]=\mathbb{E}\big(T_{\pi}(g)h^{\prime}\big)\pi_{j_{1}}(h_{1})
.

The case ii) is obtained from i) by passing to adjoints.

For iii), let
x\in\mathcal{W}_{(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})}
and
y\in\mathcal{W}_{(j_{n},\cdots,j_{1})}
be elementary tensors. Then
P_{1}(xy)=0
unless
i_{k}=j_{k}
for all
1\leq k\leq n
. Hence we can assume that
i_{1}=j_{1}
. Then noting that

H_{\varepsilon}[P_{1}(H_{\varepsilon}(x)y)]=\varepsilon_{i_{1}}^{2}P_{1}(xy)=P_{1}(xy),

we deduce
P_{1}(x\overset{0,1}{{\dagger}}y)=0
; by symmetry,
P_{1}(x\overset{1,0}{{\dagger}}y)=0
too. Thus the right hand side of (
7
) becomes

T_{\pi}\big[P_{1}\big(g\overset{1,1}{{\dagger}}h\big)\big]=T_{\pi}\big[P_{1}\big(g_{1}\mathbb{E}(g^{\prime}h^{\prime})h_{1}\big)\big]=\pi_{i_{1}}\big[P_{1}\big(g_{1}\mathbb{E}(g^{\prime}h^{\prime})h_{1}\big)\big].

However, the left hand side is

P_{1}\big[\pi_{i_{1}}(g_{1})\mathbb{E}(g^{\prime}h^{\prime})\pi_{i_{1}}(h_{1})\big]=P_{1}\big[\pi_{i_{1}}\big(g_{1}\mathbb{E}(g^{\prime}h^{\prime})h_{1}\big)\big]=\pi_{i_{1}}\big[P_{1}\big(g_{1}\mathbb{E}(g^{\prime}h^{\prime})h_{1}\big)\big]

for
\pi_{i_{1}}
is a
*
-representation, leaves the elements of
\mathcal{B}
invariant and
P_{1}\pi_{i_{1}}=\pi_{i_{1}}P_{1}
. (
7
) is thus proved in the case
l=n
too.
∎

The following is an intermediate result to Theorem
4.9
, it will be the key for the reduction formula in Theorem
4.6
below.

Theorem 4.5
.

The map
T_{\pi}
extends to a completely bounded map on
L_{p}(\mathcal{A})
for all
2\leq p<\infty
with cb-norm majorized by a constant depending only on
p
.

Proof.

As the
\pi_{i}
’s are trace preserving and leave
\mathcal{B}
invariant,

T_{\pi}
is an isometry on
\mathcal{W}
for the
L_{2}(\mathcal{A})
-norm, thus it
extends to an isometry on
L_{2}(\mathcal{A})
. We need only
to prove the boundedness of
T_{\pi}
since the complete boundedness
will then be automatic thanks to the usual trick of replacing
\mathcal{B}
by
the matrix algebra
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathcal{B})
.

As in
[
23
]
, we show that the
L_{p}
-boundedness of
T_{\pi}
implies its
L_{2p}
-boundedness for
2\leq p<\infty
. Starting with
p=2
, using iteration and interpolation, we will deduce the assertion for the full range
2<p<\infty
. In the following, we will denote by
\gamma_{p}
the norm of
T_{\pi}
and
T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}
on
\mathcal{W}
equipped with the
L_{p}
-norm.

For
x\in\mathcal{W}
we write

\displaystyle T_{\pi}(x)T_{\pi}(x)^{*}

\displaystyle=T_{\pi}(x)T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(x^{*})

\displaystyle=P_{\geq 2}[T_{\pi}(x)T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(x^{*})]+P_{1}[T_{\pi}(x)T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(x^{*})]+\mathbb{E}[T_{\pi}(x)T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(x^{*})].

By (
6
) and the fact that
P_{\geq 2}
has norm less than 5 on
L_{p}(\mathcal{A})
, we have

\big\|P_{\geq 2}[T_{\pi}(x)T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(x^{*})]\big\|_{p}\leq 5\,\big[2\gamma_{p}\|x\|_{2p}\|T_{\pi}(x)\|_{2p}+\gamma_{p}^{2}\|x\|_{2p}^{2}\big].

On the other hand, by
[
23
, Proposition 3.14]
or Lemma
2.1
, the paraproducts
\overset{i,j}{{\dagger}}
are bounded from
L_{2p}(\mathcal{A})\times L_{2p}(\mathcal{A})
to
L_{p}(\mathcal{A})
with norm less than
\eta_{p}
. Thus using (
7
) and the fact that
P_{1}
has norm less than 3 on
L_{p}(\mathcal{A})
, we get

\big\|P_{1}[T_{\pi}(x)T_{\pi}^{{\rm op}}(x^{*})]\big\|_{p}\leq 3\eta_{p}\gamma_{p}\big[2\|x\|_{2p}\|T_{\pi}(x)\|_{2p}+\|x\|_{2p}^{2}\big].

Clearly,

\big\|\mathbb{E}[T_{\pi}(x)T_{\pi}^{op}(x^{*})]\big\|_{p}=\|\mathbb{E}(xx^{*})\|_{p}\leq\|x\|_{p}^{2}.

So combining all the estimates yields

\|T_{\pi}(x)\|_{2p}^{2}\leq\gamma_{p}\big(10+6\eta_{p})\|x\|_{2p}\|T_{\pi}(x)\|_{2p}+(1+3\eta_{p}\gamma_{p}+5\gamma_{p}^{2})\|x\|_{p}^{2}.

It thus follows that

\|T_{\pi}(x)\|_{2p}\leq C\gamma_{p}\eta_{p}\|x\|_{2p}

for some absolute constant
C
, whence
\gamma_{2p}\leq C\gamma_{p}\eta_{p}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

4.2.
A length reduction formula

We show here how to recursively estimate the
L_{p}
-norm of an element in

\mathcal{W}
in the spirit of
[
17
]
. The space
\mathcal{W}
is naturally a right Hilbert
\mathcal{B}
-module with inner product
\langle x,\,y\rangle=\mathbb{E}(x^{*}y)
and also a left Hilbert
\mathcal{B}
-module with
\langle\langle x,\,y\rangle\rangle=\mathbb{E}(xy^{*})
. The same holds for
\mathcal{W}_{1}
and
\mathring{\mathcal{W}}
as submodules. A typical element in
\mathcal{W}
can be written as a finite sum

(8)

x=x_{0}+x_{1}+\sum_{i,\alpha}a_{i}(\alpha)\otimes b_{i}(\alpha){\mathop{=}^{\rm def}}x_{0}+x_{1}+z,

where
x_{0}\in\mathcal{B}
,
x_{1}\in\mathcal{W}_{1}
and
a_{i}(\alpha)\in\mathring{\mathcal{A}_{i}}
and

b_{i}(\alpha)\in\mathring{\mathcal{W}}
with
L_{i}(b_{i}(\alpha))=0
.

The following result extends the main result of
[
17
]
on homogeneous polynomials to any polynomials, which is the key tool for the argument in the next subsection.

Theorem 4.6
.

With the notation above, for
2\leq p<\infty
, we have
(
considering
z\in\mathcal{W}_{1}\otimes\mathring{\mathcal{W}})

(9)

\|x\|_{p}\approx_{p}\|x_{0}\|_{p}+\|x_{1}\|_{p}+\|z\|_{\mathcal{W}_{1}^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{A})}+\|z\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{p}\mathring{\mathcal{W}}^{r}}.

Proof.

By the boundedness of the projections
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
, it suffices to prove the estimate
for
z
. To this end, we consider two copies of
\mathcal{A}
, and put a superscript
to distinguish them. We use associativity of the free product to write

\mathcal{A}^{(1)}*_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{A}^{(2)}=\big(*_{i\in I,\,\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{(1)}\big)*_{\mathcal{B}}\big(*_{i\in I,\,\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{(2)}\big)=*_{i\in I,\,\mathcal{B}}\big(\mathcal{A}_{i}^{(1)}*_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{(2)}\big){\mathop{=}^{\rm def}}*_{i\in I,\,\mathcal{B}}\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i}.

Since the traces are compatible,

\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{A})}=\|x^{(1)}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}*_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{A}^{(2)})}
for every
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{A})
.

For each
i
, we define the swap map
\pi_{i}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
by

\displaystyle a_{1}^{(1)}\otimes a_{2}^{(2)}\otimes a_{3}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\mapsto a_{1}^{(2)}\otimes a_{2}^{(1)}\otimes a_{3}^{(2)}\otimes\cdots

\displaystyle a_{1}^{(2)}\otimes a_{2}^{(1)}\otimes a_{3}^{(2)}\otimes\cdots\mapsto a_{1}^{(1)}\otimes a_{2}^{(2)}\otimes a_{3}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots

for
\mathcal{B}
-centered elements
a_{k}^{(j)}
and by
\pi_{i}(b)=b
for
b\in\mathcal{B}
. It is clear that
\pi_{i}
is a
*
-representation,
\mathbb{E}
-preserving and leaves the elements of
\mathcal{B}
invariant. Thus, noticing that
\pi_{i}^{2}={\rm Id}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{i}}
, we use Theorem
4.5
to get

\Big\|\sum_{i,\alpha}a_{i}(\alpha)\otimes b_{i}(\alpha)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{A})}\simeq_{p}\Big\|\sum_{i,\alpha}a_{i}(\alpha)^{(2)}\otimes b_{i}(\alpha)^{(1)}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}*_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{A}^{(2)})}.

The element

\sum_{i,\alpha}a_{i}(\alpha)^{(2)}\otimes b_{i}(\alpha)^{(1)}
is
homogeneous of degree 2 with respect to the length of
\mathcal{A}^{(1)}*_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{A}^{(2)}
. Thus by
[
17
, Theorem B]

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{i,\alpha}a_{i}(\alpha)^{(2)}\otimes b_{i}(\alpha)^{(1)}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}*_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{A}^{(2)})}\simeq\Big\|\sum_{i,\alpha}|a_{i}(\alpha)^{(2)}\rangle b_{i}^{(1)}(\alpha)\Big\|_{p}+\Big\|\sum_{i,\alpha}a_{i}(\alpha)^{(2)}\langle b_{i}^{(1)}(\alpha)|\Big\|_{p}\,.

Since taking copies clearly does not change norms, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{i,\alpha}|a_{i}(\alpha)^{(2)}\rangle b_{i}^{(1)}(\alpha)\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{i,\alpha}a_{i}(\alpha)^{(2)}\otimes b_{i}^{(1)}(\alpha)\Big\|_{(\mathcal{W}_{1}^{(2)})^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}*_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{A}^{(2)})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{i,\alpha}a_{i}(\alpha)\otimes b_{i}(\alpha)\Big\|_{\mathcal{W}_{1}^{c}\otimes_{p}L_{p}(\mathcal{A})}\,.

Similarly,

\Big\|\sum_{i,\alpha}a_{i}(\alpha)^{(2)}\langle b_{i}^{(1)}(\alpha)|\Big\|_{p}=\Big\|\sum_{i,\alpha}a_{i}(\alpha)\otimes b_{i}(\alpha)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{p}{\mathring{\mathcal{W}}}^{r}}.

This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

,

]
for

since the Schatten

is
a UMD space. Note that valid for general UMD spaces, the results in

than the condition
(

Our arguments rely on the

A.1.
Bounds on homogeneous polynomials

We work in the setting of amalgamated free products of von Neumann algebras as in section
4
. Let
H_{\varepsilon}^{(j)}
be the maps introduced in Corollary
4.10
with
\varepsilon
a family of signs.

The case

of the following theorem follows from
[
17

.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
1\leq j\leq d
. Then for any
x\in\mathcal{W}_{d}
we have

\|H_{\varepsilon}^{(j)}(x)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\min\big\{\log(j+2),\,\log(d-j+2)\big\}\|x\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

For
z\in\mathbb{T}
, let
U_{z}
be the unitary on
L_{2}({\mathcal{A}})
sending
w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{n}
to
z^{n}w
for
n\geq 0
. Given
x\in{\mathcal{W}}_{d},y\in L_{2}({\mathcal{A}})
and
0\leq k\leq 2d
, let

x\bot^{k}y=\mathbb{E}_{z}[z^{d-k}U_{\overline{z}}(xU_{z}(y))].

It is easy to verify that

xy=\sum_{k=0}^{2d}x\bot^{k}y\;\;\text{and }\;\;\|x\bot^{k}y\|_{2}\leq\|x\|_{\infty}\|y\|_{2}.

Moreover, we easily show

H_{\varepsilon}^{(j)}(x)\bot^{k}y=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}H_{\varepsilon}^{(j)}(x\bot^{k}y)&\textrm{if}&k\leq 2(d-j),\\
x\bot^{k}H_{\varepsilon}^{(d+1-j)}(y)&\textrm{if}&k>2(d-j).\end{array}\right.

Thus

H_{\varepsilon}^{(j)}(x)y=\sum_{0\leq k\leq 2(d-j)}H_{\varepsilon}^{(j)}(x\bot^{k}y)+\sum_{2(d-j)<k\leq 2d}x\bot^{k}H_{\varepsilon}^{(d+1-j)}(y).

On the other hand, using the elementary estimate

\big\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}z^{k}\big\|_{L_{1}({\mathbb{T}})}\simeq\log(n+2),

we get

\big\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}x\bot^{k}y\big\|_{2}+\big\|\sum_{k=n}^{2d}x\bot^{k}y\big\|_{2}\lesssim\min\big\{\log(n+2),\,\log(2d-n+2)\big\}\|x\|_{\infty}\|y\|_{2}.

Therefore, since
H_{\varepsilon}^{(j)}
is isometric on
L_{2}(\mathcal{A})
, we deduce

\displaystyle\big\|H_{\varepsilon}^{(j)}(x)y\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\sum_{k=0}^{2(d-j)}x\bot^{k}y\big\|_{2}+\big\|\sum_{k=2(d-j)+1}^{2d}x\bot^{k}H_{\varepsilon}^{(d+1-j)}(y)\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\min\big\{\log(j+2),\,\log(d-j+2)\big\}\|x\|_{\infty}\|y\|_{2},

whence the desired estimate on
\|H_{\varepsilon}^{(j)}(x)\|_{\infty}
.
∎

where

\displaystyle\|x\|_{{\rm BMO}^{c}(S)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t>0}\big\|S_{t}\big[|x-S_{t}(x)|^{2}\big]\big\|_{\infty}^{1/2},

\displaystyle\|x\|_{{\rm bmo}^{c}(S)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t>0}\big\|S_{t}(|x|^{2})-|S_{t}(x)|^{2})\big\|_{\infty}^{1/2}.

Similarly, we define the row versions
{\rm BMO}^{r}(S)
and
{\rm bmo}^{r}(S)
by passing to adjoints. One of the main results of
[
13
]
states that the intersection space
{\rm BMO}^{c}(S)\cap{\rm BMO}^{r}(S)
behaves well with complex interpolation, i.e., it replaces
L_{\infty}
as an endpoint space in the complex interpolation scale
\big\{L_{p}(\widehat{}\mathbb{F}_{\infty})\big\}_{p>1}
.

Let
H_{\varepsilon}
denote the free Hilbert transform of the first letters associated to a sequence of signs, see Lemma
2.1
. It is easy to see that
\|H_{\varepsilon}(x)\|_{{\rm bmo}^{c}(S)}=\|x\|_{{\rm bmo}^{c}(S)}
for
x\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{F}_{\infty}]
. We will explain why one cannot hope the boundedness of
H_{\varepsilon}
from
L_{\infty}(\widehat{}\mathbb{F}_{\infty})=\widehat{}\mathbb{F}_{\infty}
to any of
{\rm BMO}^{r}(S)
,
{\rm BMO}^{c}(S)
or
{\rm bmo}^{r}(S)
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{\infty}
be two free elements. Let
z
be a finite sum
z=\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}\lambda_{a^{k}}
and
z_{n}=z\lambda(b^{n})
. Then

i)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|z_{n}\|_{{\rm bmo}^{r}(S)}=\|z\|_{\infty}
,

ii)

\displaystyle(e^{-1}-e^{-2})\|z\|_{\infty}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}\|_{{\rm BMO}^{\alpha}(S)}\leq 2\|z\|_{\infty},\;\alpha\in\{c,r\}.

Proof.

i) It is clear that

\|z_{n}\|_{{\rm bmo}^{r}(S)}\leq\|z_{n}\|_{\infty}=\|z\|_{\infty}.

For a fixed
t>0
, we have
\lim\|S_{t}(z_{n})\|_{\infty}=0
. Thus,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|z_{n}\|_{{\rm bmo}^{r}(S)}^{2}\geq\sup_{t}\big\|S_{t}(z_{n}z_{n}^{*})\big\|_{\infty}=\|z\|_{\infty}^{2}.

ii) The upper bound is clear. For the lower, we use the Kadison inequality to ensure

\big\|S_{t}|z_{n}^{*}-S_{t}(z_{n}^{*})|^{2}\big\|_{\infty},\;\big\|S_{t}|z_{n}-S_{t}(z_{n})|^{2}\big\|_{\infty}\geq\big\|(S_{t}-S_{2t})z_{n}\big\|_{\infty}^{2}.

But in
\widetilde{}\mathbb{F}_{\infty}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big((S_{\frac{1}{n}}-S_{\frac{2}{n}})z_{n}\big)\lambda_{b^{-n}}=(e^{-1}-e^{-2})z.

This finishes the proof.
∎

As
H_{\varepsilon}(z_{n})=H_{\varepsilon}(z)\lambda_{b^{n}}
, we get

Corollary A.3
.

The transform
H_{\varepsilon}
is unbounded from
L_{\infty}(\widehat{}\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
to any of
{\rm BMO}^{r}(S)
,
{\rm BMO}^{c}(S)
and
{\rm bmo}^{r}(S)
.

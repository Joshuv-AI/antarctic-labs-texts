1.
Introduction and main results

For
0<p<\infty
,
\Psi:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
and a finite positive Borel measure
\mu
, the Lebesgue-Zygmund space
L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}
consists of all measurable functions
f
in the unit disc
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
such that

\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f|^{p}\Psi(|f|)\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

In this study we assume that the inducing function
\Psi
satisfies certain hypotheses. We say that it belongs to the class
\mathcal{L}
if it is essentially monotonic and satisfies the property

\Psi(x)\asymp\Psi(x^{2}),\quad 0\leq x<\infty.

(1.1)

Here,
\Psi
is essentially increasing if there exists an absolute constant
C=C(\Psi)>0
such that
\Psi(x)\leq C\Psi(y)
for all
x\leq y
. The essentially decreasing functions are defined in an analogous manner. Besides constants, for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the logarithmic function
x\mapsto(\log(e+x))^{\alpha}
is a typical member of
\mathcal{L}
. In fact, we will show in Section
2
that each essentially increasing function in
\mathcal{L}
cannot grow faster than a certain logarithmic function while the decay of each essentially decreasing function in
\mathcal{L}
is restricted in a similar manner. It is worth mentioning that either requirement for a function to belong to
\mathcal{L}
does not imply the other, see Section
2
for details.

If
d\mu=\omega\,dA
, where
dA(z)=\frac{dx\,dy}{\pi}
is the element of the normalized Lebesgue area measure on
\mathbb{D}
, then we write
L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}=L_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}
. For
\Psi(x)=\left(\log(e+x)\right)^{\beta}
with
0<\beta<\infty
, the corresponding Lebesgue-Zygmund space becomes the classical Zygmund space, see
[
3
,
4
,
6
]
and references therein for further information. The Lebesgue-Zygmund space
L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}
is not necessarily a normed space even in the case
p\geq 1
, because
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}_{\mu,\Psi}}
does not necessarily satisfy the absolute homogeneity. However, it can be proved that it is a quasinorm by standard methods.

Let
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
denote the space of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
. An integrable function
\omega:\mathbb{D}\to[0,\infty)
is called a weight. The weighted Bergman-Zygmund space
A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}
consists of all analytic functions in
L_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}
, that is,
A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}=L_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}\cap\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
. The definition of the weighted Bergman-Zygmund spaces seems similar to that of certain weighted Bergman-Orlicz spaces. More precisely, if the function
t\mapsto t^{p}\Psi(t)
happens to be non-decreasing and convex, then the corresponding Bergman-Orlicz space has the same elements as the Bergman-Zygmund space induced by
\Psi
and
\omega
. However, it is easy to see that their quasi-norms are not comparable. For more information on the weighted Bergman-Orlicz spaces, see
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in the Bergman-Zygmund spaces induced by radial weights satisfying certain doubling properties. To be precise, some more notation is needed. We say that
\omega
is radial if
\omega(z)=\omega(|z|)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and we write
\widehat{\omega}(z)=\int_{|z|}^{1}\omega(s)\,ds
for short. We assume
\widehat{\omega}>0
on
\mathbb{D}
for otherwise
A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}=\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
.
A radial weight
\omega
belongs to the class
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
if there exists a constant
C=C(\omega)\geq 1
such that the tail integral
\widehat{\omega}
satisfies the doubling condition
\widehat{\omega}(r)\leq C\widehat{\omega}(\frac{1+r}{2})
for all
0\leq r<1
. Moreover, if there exist constants
K=K(\omega)>1
and
C=C(\omega)>1
such that
\widehat{\omega}(r)\geq C\widehat{\omega}\left(1-\frac{1-r}{K}\right)
for all
0\leq r<1
, then we write
\omega\in\widecheck{\mathcal{D}}
. Finally, the intersection
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}\cap\widecheck{\mathcal{D}}
is denoted by
\mathcal{D}
. The definitions of
these classes of weights are of geometric in nature, and the classes themselves arise
naturally in the operator theory related to Bergman spaces, see for example
[
14
,
19
]
.

For
\Phi,\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
, we say that
\mu
is a
(q,\Phi)
-Carleson measure for
A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}
if the identity mapping
I:A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}\to L_{\mu,\Phi}^{q}
is bounded. Here an operator is called bounded if it maps bounded sets into bounded sets, and further, it is compact if it sends a neighborhood of the origin onto a relatively compact set in the target space.

In the paper, we will characterize the
(q,\Phi)
-Carleson measures for
A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}
, provided
0<p\leq q<\infty
,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
\Phi,\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
. To give the precise statement some basic definitions are in order. The Carleson square associated with
a\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
is the polar rectangle

S(a)=\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\,|z|\geq|a|,\,|\arg a-\arg z|<\frac{1}{2}(1-|a|)\right\}.

Further, we set
S(0)=\mathbb{D}
for convenience, and let
\omega(S)=\int_{S}\omega dA
for measurable
S\subset\mathbb{D}
.
Our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
0<p\leq q<\infty
,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
\Phi,\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

\mu
is a
(q,\Phi)
-Carleson measure for
A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}
if and only if

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{\mu(S(a))\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a)))}\right)}{\left(\omega(S(a))\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}}<\infty;

(1.2)

(ii)

I:A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}\to L^{q}_{\mu,\Phi}
is compact if and only if

\lim_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\frac{\mu(S(a))\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)}{\left(\omega(S(a))\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}}=0.

(1.3)

In the statement of Theorem
1
we may replace the Carleson square
S(a)
by the pseudohyperbolic disc
\Delta(a,r)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|(a-z)/(1-\overline{a}z)|<r\}
centered at
a\in\mathbb{D}
and of radius
r\in(0,1)
. This is due to the hypothesis
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and standard arguments, see Section
3
for details.
This phenomenon was first noticed in
[
10
]
for the Bergman space and also appears in the case of
q
-Carleson measures for Bergman space
A^{p}_{\omega}
induced by
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
by
[
8
,
9
]
. Theorem
1
reduces to
[
8
, Theorem 2]
when
\Phi
and
\Psi
are constants, and the choices
\Phi(x)=\left(\log(e+x)\right)^{\gamma}
and
\Psi(x)=\left(\log(e+x)\right)^{\beta}
give
[
6
, Theorem C]
when the weight involved is
\omega(z)=(1-|z|)^{\alpha}
.

As a byproduct of the proof of Theorem
1
and the theorem itself we deduce that for each
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
, the weight
W=\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|\cdot|}\right)\omega
belongs to
\mathcal{D}
and
A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}
equals
A^{p}_{W}
as a set. This is the main content of Proposition
11
which is proved at the end of the paper. However, it should be underlined here that even though the spaces have the same elements, the quasinorms are not in general comparable.

En route to the proof of Theorem
1
we develop several tools that allow us to study integral operators on weighted Bergman–Zygmund spaces. The integral operator
T_{g}
, induced by a
g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
, is defined by

T_{g}(f)(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(\zeta)g^{\prime}(\zeta)\,d\zeta,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

This operator was initially considered by Pommerenke
[
20
]
, and it began to be extensively studied after the appearance of the works by Aleman, Cima and Siskakis
[
1
,
2
]
. In the paper, we will characterize the boundedness and compactness of
T_{g}:A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}\to A^{q}_{\nu,\Phi}
in terms of neat conditions on
g
, provided
0<p\leq q<\infty
and
\omega,\nu\in\mathcal{D}
. Our results in this direction are stated as follows.

Theorem 2
.

Let
0<p\leq q<\infty
,
\omega,\nu\in\mathcal{D}
and
\Psi,\Phi\in\mathcal{L}
,
g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

T_{g}:A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}\to A^{q}_{\nu,\Phi}
is bounded if and only if

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{|g^{\prime}(a)|(1-|a|)\left(\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\nu(S(a))\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}}{\left(\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\omega(S(a))\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}<\infty;

(1.4)

(ii)

T_{g}:A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}\to A^{q}_{\nu,\Phi}
is compact if and only if

\lim_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\frac{|g^{\prime}(a)|(1-|a|)\left(\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\nu(S(a))\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}}{\left(\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\omega(S(a))\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}=0.

(1.5)

We make couple of observations on Theorem
2
. First, as in the case of Carleson measures and because of the same reason, pseudohyperbolic discs can be substituted for the Carleson squares. Second, the special case
p=q
and
\omega=\nu
in Theorem
2
shows that
T_{g}:A^{p}_{\omega}\to A^{q}_{\omega}
is bounded (resp. compact) if and only if
g
belongs to the (resp. little) Bloch space
[
12
, Theorem 6.4]
.

We finish the introduction with a couple of words about the notation used in this paper. The letter
C=C(\cdot)
will denote an absolute constant whose value depends on the parameters indicated in the parenthesis and may change from one occurrence to another. If there exists a constant

C=C(\cdot)>0
such that
a\leq Cb
, then we write either
a\lesssim b
or
b\gtrsim a
. In particular, if
a\lesssim b
and

a\gtrsim b
, then we denote
a\asymp b
and say that
a
and
b
are comparable.

2.
Class
\mathcal{L}

Recall that a function
\Psi:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
belongs to the class
\mathcal{L}
if it is essentially monotonic and satisfies the property
\Psi(x)\asymp\Psi(x^{2})
for all
0\leq x<\infty
.
It is worth mentioning that either requirement for a function to belong to
\mathcal{L}
does not imply the other. Namely, the essential monotonicity obviously does not imply the condition (
1.1
) while a non essentially monotonic function satisfying (
1.1
) can be constructed as follows. Choose an increasing and unbounded function
f
and a decreasing function
g
such that
f(x)\asymp f(x^{2})
and
g(x)\asymp g(x^{2})
for all
[0,\infty)
. Fix
x_{1}
and
y_{1}
such that
x_{1}^{2}>y_{1}>x_{1}>1
, and set
x_{n+1}=x_{n}^{2}
and
y_{n+1}=y_{n}^{2}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Consider
\Psi
defined by

\Psi(x)=\begin{cases}g(x),\quad x\in\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}(x_{n},y_{n}),\\
f(x),\quad x\in[0,\infty)\setminus\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}(x_{n},y_{n}).\end{cases}

Then,
\Psi(x)\asymp\Psi(x^{2})
for all
x\in[0,\infty)
, but
\Psi
is not essentially monotonic. Natural choices of
f
and
g
are adequate powers of logarithmic functions.

In this section we provide some basic properties of functions in the class
\mathcal{L}
that will be later on used in the proof of the main theorem. We begin with pointwise growth/decay estimates.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
. Then there exist constants
c_{1}=c_{1}(\Psi),C_{1}=C_{1}(\Psi)>0
and
c_{2}=c_{2}(\Psi),C_{2}=C_{2}(\Psi)\in\mathbb{R}
such that

c_{1}(\log(e+x))^{c_{2}}\leq\Psi(x)\leq C_{1}(\log(e+x))^{C_{2}},\quad 0\leq x<\infty.

Proof.

Let

a=\inf_{0\leq x\leq 4}\Psi(x)\quad\mbox{and}\quad b=\sup_{0\leq x\leq 4}\Psi(x).

Since
\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
, both
a
and
b
are strictly positive and finite, and thus the statement is valid for all
0\leq x\leq 4
.
Let
4<x<\infty
, and choose
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{2^{n}}\leq x<2^{2^{n+1}}
. Then, by (
1.1
), there exist constants

\widetilde{c}_{2},\widetilde{C}_{2}>0
such that
\widetilde{c}_{2}^{n}a\leq\Psi(x)\leq\widetilde{C}_{2}^{n}b
. Moreover, since
n\leq\log_{2}\log_{2}x<n+1
, there exist constants
c_{2},C_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\widetilde{c}_{2}^{n}\asymp(\log(e+x))^{c_{2}},\quad\textrm{and}\quad\widetilde{C}_{2}^{n}\asymp(\log(e+x))^{C_{2}}.

The assertion follows from the estimates established.
∎

The functions in the class
\mathcal{L}
admit the following pointwise estimates which are useful for our purposes.

Proposition 4
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
\Phi,\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

\Psi(x)\asymp\Psi(y)
, when
x\asymp y
;

(ii)

\Psi(x)\asymp\Psi(x^{p})
for all
0\leq x<\infty
;

(iii)

\Psi(x/\Phi(x))\asymp\Psi(x)
for all
0\leq x<\infty
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that (i) and (ii) hold, while (iii) follows from (ii) and Lemma
3
.
∎

As an immediate corollary to Lemma
3
we obtain another handy result. Its essential content is that the product of
\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
and any power of the identity function results an essentially increasing function. Observe that this statement can not be proved by using only the essential monotonicity of
\Psi
. Namely, for an example the function defined by
\Psi(x)=2^{-2^{n}}
for
n\leq x<n+1
is decreasing but
x\mapsto x^{p}\Psi(x)
is not essentially increasing.

Lemma 5
.

Let
0<p,\beta<\infty
and
\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
. Then
\Theta:x\mapsto x^{p}\Psi(x)
is essentially increasing on
[0,\infty)
, and
\theta:x\mapsto(1-x)^{\beta}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-x}\right)
is essentially decreasing on
[0,1)
.

Proof.

We prove the first statement only, the latter one can be proved similarly. So we will show that there exists
C=C(\Psi,p)>0
such that
\Theta(x)\leq C\Theta(y)
for all
0\leq x\leq y<\infty
. For
0\leq x\leq 2
, this follows trivially by Lemma
3
. Moreover, if
2<x<\infty
and
x\leq y<x^{2}
, then the inequality is a direct consequence of Proposition
4
(ii). Let now
2<x<\infty
,
x^{2}\leq y
and write
y=x^{t}
.
Then, for each
\alpha>0
there exists
C_{1}=C_{1}(\alpha)>0
such that

\displaystyle\left(\log(e+x)\right)\left(\log(e+y)\right)\leq t\left(\log(e+x)\right)^{2}\leq C_{1}x^{\alpha(t-1)}=C_{1}\left({y\over x}\right)^{\alpha}.

By Lemma
3
there are
C_{2}=C_{2}(\Psi),\beta=\beta(\Psi)>0
such that

\displaystyle{\Psi(y)\over\Psi(x)}\geq{C_{2}\over\left(\log(e+x)\right)^{\beta}\left(\log(e+y)\right)^{\beta}}.

Therefore, by choosing
\alpha>0
sufficiently small such that
\alpha\beta<p
, we deduce

\displaystyle{y^{p}\Psi(y)\over x^{p}\Psi(x)}\geq\left({y\over x}\right)^{p}{C_{2}\over\left(\log(e+x)\right)^{\beta}\left(\log(e+y)\right)^{\beta}}>C

for some
C=C(\Psi)>0
.
∎

3.
Proof of the main theorems

For
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
and
0<r<1
, set

M_{p}(r,f)=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad 0<p<\infty,

and
M_{\infty}(r,f)=\sup_{|z|=r}|f(z)|
. The following lemma gives a growth estimate for functions in the weighted Bergman-Zygmund space. We will use this estimate in the proof of Theorem
1
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
and
\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
. Then, for each fixed
0<\varepsilon<p
,

|f(z)|^{p-\varepsilon}\lesssim\frac{\lVert f\rVert^{p}_{A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}}+1}{\omega(S(z))},\quad f\in A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

(3.1)

Proof.

It follows from
[
11
, Lemma 1.3]
that

M_{\infty}(r,f)\lesssim\frac{M_{p}(\frac{1+r}{2},f)}{(1-r)^{\frac{1}{p}}},\quad f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}),\quad 0\leq r<1.

This estimate together with Lemma
5
and the hypotheses
\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
and
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
yields

\displaystyle|f(z)|^{p-\varepsilon}\omega(S(z))

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{\infty}^{p-\varepsilon}(|z|,f)(1-|z|)\widehat{\omega}(z)\lesssim M_{p-\varepsilon}^{p-\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1+|z|}{2}\right)\widehat{\omega}\left(\frac{1+|z|}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\frac{1+|z|}{2}}^{1}M_{p-\varepsilon}^{p-\varepsilon}(r,f)\omega(r)r\,dr\leq\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(\zeta)|^{p-\varepsilon}\omega(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{|f|\leq 1}|f(\zeta)|^{p-\varepsilon}\omega(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)+\int_{|f|>1}|f(\zeta)|^{p-\varepsilon}\frac{\Psi(|f(\zeta)|)}{\Psi(|f(\zeta)|)}\omega(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\int_{|f|>1}|f(\zeta)|^{p}\Psi(|f(\zeta)|)\omega(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)\leq 1+\lVert f\rVert^{p}_{A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}}.

For the case where
\Psi
is essentially increasing, we not need to use Lemma
5
, and we can actually choose
\varepsilon=0
and remove the added constant
1
.
∎

Next, we show that the quantity that defines the weighted Lebesgue-Zygmund space satisfies the quasi triangular inequality and the scalar multiplication is continuous. Observe that we do not require absolute homogeneity for a set to be a quasinormed vector space.

Proposition 7
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
, and let
\mu
be a finite positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
. Then the space
L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}
is a quasinormed topological vector space with the quasinorm

\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f|^{p}\Psi(|f|)d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Proof.

To prove that
\lVert\,\cdotp\rVert_{L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}}
is a quasinorm, we only need to show that

\lVert f+g\rVert_{L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}}+\lVert g\rVert_{L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}}.

By symmetry, we may assume that
|g|\leq|f|
. Then, by Lemma
5
and Proposition
4
, we have

\displaystyle|f+g|^{p}\Psi(|f+g|)\lesssim 2^{p}|f|^{p}\Psi(|f|)\lesssim|f|^{p}\Psi(|f|)+|g|^{p}\Psi(|g|).

This clearly gives us

\displaystyle\lVert f+g\rVert_{L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f+g|^{p}\Psi(|f+g|)\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq(2C)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f|^{p}\Psi(|f|)\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g|^{p}\Psi(|g|)\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(2C)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}}+\lVert g\rVert_{L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}}\right)

for some constant
C=C(p,\Psi)>0
. Thus
\lVert\,\cdotp\rVert_{L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}}
satisfies the quasi-triangle inequality. This trivially implies that addition is continuous, so it remains to show that the scalar multiplication is continuous. Assume first that
\Psi
is essentially increasing. When
|c|<1
, we have
\Psi(|cf|)\lesssim\Psi(|f|)
. If
|c|>1
, choose
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that
2^{k}\leq|c|<2^{k+1}
. Then Proposition
4
(i) implies

\Psi(|cf|)\lesssim\Psi(2^{k+1}|f|)\leq C^{k}\Psi(|f|)<C^{\log_{2}|c|}\Psi(|f|),

which gives us

\lVert cf\rVert^{p}_{L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}}\lesssim|c|^{p}(1+C^{\log_{2}|c|})\lVert f\rVert^{p}_{L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}}.

Assume next that
\Psi
is essentially decreasing. When
|c|\geq 1
, we clearly have
\Psi(|cf|)\lesssim\Psi(|f|)
. When
0<|c|<1
, we have
2^{-(k+1)2^{k+1}}\leq|c|<2^{-k2^{k}}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Then by definition of class
\mathcal{L}
we have

\Psi(|cf|)\lesssim\Psi(2^{-(k+1)2^{k+1}}|f|)\lesssim C^{k}\Psi(|f|)\lesssim C^{\log_{2}\log_{2}\frac{1}{|c|}}\Psi(|f|),

giving us

\lVert cf\rVert^{p}_{L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}}\lesssim|c|^{p}(1+C^{\log_{2}\log_{2}\frac{1}{|c|}})\lVert f\rVert^{p}_{L_{\mu,\Psi}^{p}}

and the statement follows.
∎

The following characterization for the compactness on the weighted Bergman space setting is well-known but we include a proof for the convenience of the readers.

Proposition 8
.

Let
0<p\leq q<\infty
,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
\Phi,\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
.
Then the canonical inclusion map
I:A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}\to L^{q}_{\mu,\Phi}
is compact if and
only if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{q}_{\mu,\Phi}}=0
for each bounded sequence
\{f_{n}\}
in
A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}
that converges to 0
uniformly on compact subsets on
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

Suppose
I
is compact and
\{f_{n}\}
is a bounded sequence in
A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}
that converges to
0
uniformly on compact subsets on
\mathbb{D}
.
Let
\{g_{n}\}
be a subsequence of
\{f_{n}\}
. Since
I
is compact, there exists a subsequence
\{I(g_{n_{k}})\}
converging to
G

in
L^{q}_{\mu,\Phi}
. Note that
G=0
since
I(g_{nk})=g_{n_{k}}
and
\{g_{n}\}
converges to
0
uniformly on compact subsets on
\mathbb{D}
.
Therefore every subsequence of
\{f_{n}\}
has a subsequence converging to
0
in
L^{q}_{\mu,\Phi}
. This implies that
\{f_{n}\}
itself converges to
0
in
L^{q}_{\mu,\Phi}
.

For the sufficiency, let
\|f_{n}\|_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}\leq M_{0}
for some
M_{0}
.
Then
\{f_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
by Lemma
6
.
Therefore Montel’s theorem guarantees the existence of a subsequence
\{f_{n_{k}}\}
which converges uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
to some analytic function
f
. By Proposition
7
, for each
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{D(0,r)}|f|^{p}\Psi(|f|)\omega\,dA\leq C\int_{D(0,r)}|f-f_{n_{k}}|^{p}\Psi(|f-f_{n_{k}}|)\omega\,dA+C\|f_{n_{k}}\|_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}^{p}\leq C_{1}

for some
C_{1}=C_{1}(p,\omega,\Psi,M_{0})
taking
n_{k}
sufficiently large.
Therefore
f\in A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}
by Fatou’s lemma.
Since the subsequence converges uniformly on compact subsets, by the assumption
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|I(f-f_{n_{k}})\|_{L^{q}_{\mu,\Phi}}=0
.
Hence
I:A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}\to L^{q}_{\mu,\Phi}
is compact.
∎

The following lemma contains useful characterizations of weights in the class
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
. For a proof, see
[
12
, Lemma 2.1]
, for example.

Lemma A
.

Let
\omega
be a radial weight. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
;

(ii)

There exist
C=C(\omega)>0
and
\beta_{0}=\beta_{0}(\omega)>0
such that

\widehat{\omega}(r)\leq C\left(\frac{1-r}{1-t}\right)^{\beta}\widehat{\omega}(t),\quad 0\leq r\leq t<1,

for all
\beta\geq\beta_{0}
;

(iii)

There exists
\lambda=\lambda(\omega)\geq 0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\omega(z)}{|1-\bar{\zeta}z|^{\lambda+1}}dA(z)\asymp\frac{\widehat{\omega}(\zeta)}{(1-|\zeta|)^{\lambda}},\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{D}.

With the aid of Lemma
A
we can now construct suitable testing functions for the proof of the main result.

Lemma 9
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
and
\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
. Then there exists
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{0}(\omega,\Psi)
such that, for each
\gamma>\gamma_{0}
, the function

f_{a}(z)=\left(\left(\frac{1-|a|}{1-\overline{a}z}\right)^{\gamma}\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

belongs to
A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}
and
\lVert f_{a}\rVert^{p}_{A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}}\asymp 1
for all
a\in\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

For the lower bound, by Proposition
4
we have

\displaystyle\lVert f_{a}\rVert^{p}_{A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}}

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{1-|a|}{|1-\overline{a}z|}\right)^{\gamma}\frac{\omega(z)}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\Psi\left(\left(\frac{1-|a|}{|1-\overline{a}z|}\right)^{\gamma}\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\right)\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int_{S(a)}\frac{\omega(z)}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\right)\,dA(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\right)}{\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\asymp 1,\quad a\in\mathbb{D}.

For the upper bound, assume first that
\Psi
is essentially increasing. By Proposition
4
(ii), (iii) and Lemma
A
(iii), we have

\displaystyle\lVert f_{a}\rVert^{p}_{A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}}

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{1-|a|}{|1-\overline{a}z|}\right)^{\gamma}\frac{\omega(z)}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\Psi\left(\left(\frac{1-|a|}{|1-\overline{a}z|}\right)^{\gamma}\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\right)\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{1-|a|}{|1-\overline{a}z|}\right)^{\gamma}\frac{\omega(z)}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\right)\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\right)}{\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\asymp 1,\quad a\in\mathbb{D}.

Next, assume that
\Psi
is essentially decreasing. Let
N=N(a)\in\mathbb{N}
be the smallest integer such that
2^{N}(1-|a|)\geq 1
. Define

\displaystyle S_{0}(a)=S(a),\quad S_{k}(a)

\displaystyle=S((1-2^{k}(1-|a|))e^{i\text{arg}a}),\quad k=1,\ldots,N-1,

and set
S_{k}(a)=\mathbb{D}
if
k\geq N
. Then

|1-\overline{a}z|\asymp 2^{k}(1-|a|),\quad z\in S_{k}(a)\setminus S_{k-1}(a),\quad k=1,\ldots,N.

(3.2)

Since
\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
, by Proposition
4
(i) there exists a constant
C=C(\Psi)>0
such that

\frac{1}{C^{k}}\Psi(x)\leq\Psi\left(\frac{x}{2^{k}}\right)\leq C^{k}\Psi(x),\quad 0\leq x<\infty.

(3.3)

Therefore, by employing (
3.2
), (
3.3
), Proposition
4
(ii)-(iii) and the hypothesis
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
, we deduce the existence of
C_{1}=C_{1}(\Psi)>0
such that

\displaystyle\lVert f_{a}\rVert^{p}_{A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}}

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{1-|a|}{|1-\overline{a}z|}\right)^{\gamma}\frac{\omega(z)}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\Psi\left(\left(\frac{1-|a|}{|1-\overline{a}z|}\right)^{\gamma}\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\right)\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{S_{k}(a)\setminus S_{k-1}(a)}\left(\frac{1-|a|}{|1-\overline{a}z|}\right)^{\gamma}\frac{\omega(z)}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\Psi\left(\left(\frac{1-|a|}{|1-\overline{a}z|}\right)^{\gamma}\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\right)\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{S(a)}\left(\frac{1-|a|}{|1-\overline{a}z|}\right)^{\gamma}\frac{\omega(z)}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\Psi\left(\left(\frac{1-|a|}{|1-\overline{a}z|}\right)^{\gamma}\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\right)\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\asymp\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{S_{k}(a)\setminus S_{k-1}(a)}\frac{1}{2^{k\gamma}}\frac{\omega(z)}{\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{2^{k\gamma}\omega(S(a))\Psi(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))})}\right)\,dA(z)+1

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{N}\left(\frac{C_{1}}{2^{\gamma}}\right)^{k}\frac{\omega(S_{k}(a))}{\omega(S(a))},\qquad a\in\mathbb{D}.

Proof is completed by taking
\gamma
sufficiently large since
\omega(S_{k}(a))\leq C^{k}\omega(S(a))
by Lemma
A
(ii).
∎

We will also use the following characterization of the class
\widecheck{\mathcal{D}}
, the proof of which can be found in
[
17
]
.

Lemma B
.

Let
\omega
be a radial weight. Then
\omega\in\widecheck{\mathcal{D}}
if and only if there exist
C=C(\omega)>0
and
\beta=\beta(\omega)>0
such that

\widehat{\omega}(t)\leq C\left(\frac{1-t}{1-r}\right)^{\beta}\widehat{\omega}(r),\quad 0\leq r\leq t<1.

In the proof of the main result we need to understand the behavior of the auxiliary weight
W=\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|\cdot|}\right)\omega
. To do this, we will use the next result.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
. Then

\int_{r}^{1}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)\frac{\widehat{\omega}(s)}{1-s}\,ds\lesssim\int_{r}^{1}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)\omega(s)\,ds,\quad 0\leq r<1.

(3.4)

Proof.

Assume first that
\Psi
is essentially decreasing. On one hand, since
\omega\in\mathcal{D}\subset\widecheck{\mathcal{D}}
, by Lemma
B
, there exists a
\beta=\beta(\omega)>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{1}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)\frac{\widehat{\omega}(s)}{1-s}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\int_{r}^{1}\frac{\widehat{\omega}(s)}{(1-s)^{1-\beta+\beta}}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\frac{\widehat{\omega}(r)}{(1-r)^{\beta}}\int_{r}^{1}\frac{1}{(1-s)^{1-\beta}}\,ds\lesssim\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\widehat{\omega}(r).

On the other hand, it follows from Lemma
5
that
s\mapsto\frac{1}{(1-s)^{\beta}}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)
is essentially increasing. Then, by integrating by parts we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{1}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)\omega(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{r}^{1}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)\omega(s)\frac{(1-s)^{\beta}}{(1-s)^{\beta}}\,ds

\displaystyle\gtrsim\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\frac{1}{(1-r)^{\beta}}\int_{r}^{1}(1-s)^{\beta}\omega(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\frac{1}{(1-r)^{\beta}}\left((1-r)^{\beta}\widehat{\omega}(r)+\int_{r}^{1}\beta(1-s)^{\beta-1}\widehat{\omega}(s)\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\geq\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\widehat{\omega}(r),\quad 0\leq r<1.

This finishes the proof of the case when
\Psi
is essentially decreasing.

Assume next that
\Psi
is essentially increasing. Then, by Lemma
B
, there exists an
\alpha=\alpha(\omega)>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{1}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)\frac{\widehat{\omega}(s)}{1-s}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\widehat{\omega}(r)}{(1-r)^{\alpha}}\int_{r}^{1}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)\frac{1}{(1-s)^{1-\alpha}}\,ds,\quad 0\leq r<1.

Now, for
\beta<\alpha
, the function
s\mapsto(1-s)^{\beta}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)
is essentially decreasing by Lemma
5
. Thus

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{\omega}(r)}{(1-r)^{\alpha}}\int_{r}^{1}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)\frac{(1-s)^{\beta}}{(1-s)^{1-\alpha+\beta}}\,ds\lesssim\widehat{\omega}(r)\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\lesssim\int_{r}^{1}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)\omega(s)\,ds

for all
0\leq r<1
. The claim follows.
∎

It should be noted that it can easily be proven that
\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\widehat{\omega}(r)\asymp\int_{r}^{1}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)\omega(s)\,ds
using similar methods as in the above proof. We will use this fact in the proof of Proposition

.

\begin{split}\int_{E_{\varepsilon}}|f(z)|^{q}\Phi(|f(z)|)\,d\mu(z)&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\widehat{\omega}(\zeta)^{\frac{q}{p}}(1-|\zeta|)^{\frac{q}{p}}}{(1-|\zeta|)^{\eta+q\varepsilon+2}}\,dA(\zeta)\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\widehat{\omega}(r)}{(1-r)^{\eta+q\varepsilon+1}}\,dr\\
&\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dr}{(1-r)^{\eta+q\varepsilon+1-\beta}}\end{split}

for some
\beta=\beta(\omega)>0
. By choosing
\eta>0
and
\varepsilon>0
small enough, the last integral converges. To estimate the second integral, we use the subharmonicity of
|f|^{p}
, Minkowski’s inequality and (

) to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus E_{\varepsilon}}|f(z)|^{q}\Phi(|f(z)|)\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus E_{\varepsilon}}|f(z)|^{q}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(z))}\right)\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus E_{\varepsilon}}\left(\int_{\Delta(z,r)}\frac{|f(\zeta)|^{p}}{(1-|\zeta|)^{2}}\,dA(\zeta)\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(z))}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f(\zeta)|^{p}}{(1-|\zeta|)^{2}}\left(\int_{\Delta(\zeta,r)}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(z))}\right)\,d\mu(z)\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dA(\zeta)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f(\zeta)|^{p}}{(1-|\zeta|)^{2}}\left(\mu(\Delta(\zeta,r))\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(\zeta))}\right)\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,dA(\zeta)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(\zeta)|^{p}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(\zeta))}\right)\frac{\omega(\Delta(\zeta,r))}{(1-|\zeta|)^{2}}\,dA(\zeta)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(\zeta)|^{p}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(\zeta))}\right)\omega(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

By combining the estimates above we have

\lVert f\rVert_{L_{\mu,\Phi}^{q}}^{p}\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(\zeta)|^{p}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(\zeta))}\right)\omega(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}+1.

If
\Psi
is essentially increasing (resp. decreasing), we may finish the proof by arguing as in Case I (resp. Case II). The proof of Theorem
1
(i) is now complete.
\Box

(3.10)

Proposition 11
.

Let
0<p<\infty,\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
and
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
. Then there exists a weight
W=W(\omega,\Psi)\in\mathcal{D}
such that
A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}=A_{W}^{p}
when considered as sets. Also the identity mappings
I:A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}\to A_{W}^{p}
and
I:A_{W}^{p}\to A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}
are bounded. Moreover,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}\|_{A^{p}_{W}}=0
if and only if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}\|_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}=0
.

Proof.

Define
W(z)=\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\omega(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. We first show that
W\in\mathcal{D}
. By the hypothesis
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
, Lemma
4
(i) and the afternote of Lemma
10
, we have

\widehat{W}(r)\asymp\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\widehat{\omega}(r)\lesssim\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-\frac{1+r}{2}}\right)\widehat{\omega}\left(\frac{1+r}{2}\right)\asymp\widehat{W}\left(\frac{1+r}{2}\right),\quad 0\leq r<1.

Therefore
W\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
by the definition. Let
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\omega)\in(0,\beta)
, where
\beta=\beta(\omega)
is that of Lemma
B
. Then Lemmas
B
and
5
imply

\begin{split}\widehat{W}(t)&\asymp\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-t}\right)\widehat{\omega}(t)\lesssim\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-t}\right)\left(\frac{1-t}{1-r}\right)^{\beta}\widehat{\omega}(r)\\
&=\frac{\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-t}\right)(1-t)^{\varepsilon}}{\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)(1-r)^{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{1-t}{1-r}\right)^{\beta-\varepsilon}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\widehat{\omega}(r)\\
&\lesssim\left(\frac{1-t}{1-r}\right)^{\beta-\varepsilon}\widehat{W}(r),\quad 0\leq r<t<1,\end{split}

which shows
W\in\widecheck{\mathcal{D}}
by Lemma
B
. Thus
W\in\mathcal{D}
.

By Proposition
4
(i) and the afternote of Lemma
10
we have

\displaystyle\frac{W(S(a))}{\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|a|}\right)\omega(S(a))}\asymp 1,\quad a\in\mathbb{D}.

Hence Theorem
1
(i) implies that the identity mappings between
A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}
and
A_{W}^{p}
are bounded, and thus
A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}=A_{W}^{p}
as sets.

For the last statement, assume first that
\Psi
is essentially increasing. Let first
\{f_{n}\}
be a bounded sequence in
A^{p}_{W}
. By the first part of the proof we know that
\{f_{n}\}
is also a bounded sequence in
A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}
. By Lemma
6
we have

\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{n}(z)|\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\omega(z)\,dA(z)=\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{W}},

which shows that if
\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{W}}\to 0
, then
\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}\to 0
.

Let now
\{f_{n}\}
be a bounded sequence in
A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}
for which
\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}\to 0
. Let
E^{n}_{\varepsilon}=E^{n}_{\varepsilon}(f)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|f_{n}(z)|<\frac{1}{(1-|z|)^{\varepsilon/p}}\}
, where
\varepsilon>0
is fixed such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{1}{(1-|z|)^{\varepsilon}}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\omega(z)\,dA(z)<\infty.

Then

\begin{split}\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{W}}&=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{n}(z)|\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\omega(z)\,dA(z)\\
&=\int_{E^{n}_{\varepsilon}}|f_{n}(z)|\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\omega(z)\,dA(z)+\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus E^{n}_{\varepsilon}}|f_{n}(z)|\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\omega(z)\,dA(z)\\
&\lesssim\int_{E^{n}_{\varepsilon}}|f_{n}(z)|\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\omega(z)\,dA(z)+\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}.\end{split}

By the proof of Lemma
6
, we know that there is no ‘
+1
’ in the numerator on the right hand side of the statement of the said lemma when
\Psi
is essential increasing. Hence
|f_{n}|\to 0
pointwise in
\mathbb{D}
when
\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}\to 0
. Thus the dominated convergence theorem yields

\int_{E^{n}_{\varepsilon}}|f_{n}(z)|\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\omega(z)\,dA(z)\to 0,

(3.15)

when
n\to\infty
. Therefore
\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{W}}\to 0
if
\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}\to 0
.

Assume next that
\Psi
is essentially decreasing. Then

\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{W}}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{n}(z)|\Psi\left(|f_{n}(z)|\right)\omega(z)\,dA(z)=\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}},

which shows that if
\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}\to 0
, then
\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{W}}\to 0
.
Moreover, for the set
E^{n}_{\varepsilon}=E^{n}_{\varepsilon}(f)
, defined above, we have

\begin{split}\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}&=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{n}(z)|^{p}\Psi\left(|f_{n}(z)|\right)\omega(z)\,dA(z)\\
&\lesssim\int_{E^{n}_{\varepsilon}}|f_{n}(z)|^{p}\Psi\left(|f_{n}(z)|\right)\omega(z)\,dA(z)+\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus E^{n}_{\varepsilon}}|f_{n}(z)|\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\omega(z)\,dA(z)\\
&\leq\int_{E^{n}_{\varepsilon}}|f_{n}(z)|^{p}\Psi\left(|f_{n}(z)|\right)\omega(z)\,dA(z)+\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{W}}.\end{split}

Since
|f_{n}|\to 0
pointwise in
\mathbb{D}
when
\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{W}}\to 0
by Lemma
6
and its proof, we deduce

\int_{E^{n}_{\varepsilon}}|f_{n}(z)|^{p}\Psi\left(|f_{n}(z)|\right)\omega(z)\,dA(z)\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
. Therefore we have
\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{W}}\to 0
when
\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}\to 0
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Recall that in Proposition
6
we provided a rough growth estimate for functions in
A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}
when
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
. If

, then we can give a more precise growth estimate, which is needed to prove Theorem

. A sharp bound for the constant

Lemma 12
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
\Psi\in\mathcal{L}
. Then, for each
f\in A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}
, there exists a constant
C=C(p,\omega,\Psi,\|f\|_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}})
such that

|f(z)|^{p}\leq\frac{C}{\omega(S(z))\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(z))}\right)},\quad f\in A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

(3.16)

Proof.

By the subharmonicity of
|f|^{p}
, and integration by parts with Lemma
10
, we deduce

\begin{split}|f(z)|^{p}&\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|)^{2}}\int_{\Delta(z,r)}|f(\zeta)|^{p}\frac{\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|\zeta|}\right)}{\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|\zeta|}\right)}\frac{\widehat{\omega}(\zeta)}{\widehat{\omega}(\zeta)}\,dA(\zeta)\\
&\asymp\frac{1}{\omega(S(z))\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)}\int_{\Delta(z,r)}|f(\zeta)|^{p}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|\zeta|}\right)\frac{\widehat{\omega}(\zeta)}{1-|\zeta|}\,dA(\zeta)\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{\omega(S(z))\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(\zeta)|^{p}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|\zeta|}\right)\omega(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.\end{split}

(3.17)

Since
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
by the hypothesis, we are able to find a sufficiently small
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\omega)
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{1}{(1-|z|)^{\varepsilon}}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|\zeta|}\right)\omega(z)\,dA(z)<\infty.

(3.18)

When
\Psi
is essentially increasing, let
E_{\varepsilon,p}=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|f(z)|<\frac{1}{(1-|z|)^{\varepsilon/p}}\}
. Then (
3.17
) and (
3.18
) yield

\begin{split}|f(z)|^{p}&\lesssim\frac{1}{\omega(S(z))\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)}\left(\int_{E_{\varepsilon,p}}+\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus E_{\varepsilon,p}}\right)|f(\zeta)|^{p}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|\zeta|}\right)\omega(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{\omega(S(z))\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)}\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus E_{\varepsilon,p}}|f(\zeta)|^{p}\Psi\left(|f(\zeta)|\right)\omega(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)\right)\\
&\leq\frac{\lVert f\rVert^{p}_{A_{\omega,\Psi}^{p}}+1}{\omega(S(z))\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.\end{split}

(3.19)

When
\Psi
is essentially decreasing, then (
3.1
), (
3.17
) and Proposition
4
(i),(ii) yield

\begin{split}|f(z)|^{p}&\lesssim\frac{1}{\omega(S(z))\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(\zeta)|^{p}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|\zeta|}\right)\omega(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{\omega(S(z))\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(\zeta)|^{p}\Psi\left(\frac{|f(\zeta)|^{p-\delta}}{\|f\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}+1}\right)\omega(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)\\
&\lesssim\frac{\|f\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}}{\omega(S(z))\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},\end{split}

(3.20)

where the suppressed constant depends on
p,\omega
,
\Psi
and
\|f\|_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}
.
The proof will be completed by combining (
3.19
) and (
3.20
).
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
2
. Before giving the proof, we should notice that the proof of Proposition
8
with minor modification implies that
T_{g}:A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}\to A^{q}_{\nu,\Phi}
is compact if and
only if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T_{g}(f_{n})\|_{L^{q}_{\mu,\Phi}}=0
for any bounded sequence
\{f_{n}\}
in
A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}
that converges to 0
uniformly on compact subsets on
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof of
Theorem
2
(i)
.
Assume first that (
1.4
) holds. By Lemma
5
, Lemma
12
, (
1.4
) and Lemma
3
, we derive that there exists a
\beta=\beta(\nu,q)
such that

\begin{split}|T_{g}(f)(z)|&=\left|\int_{0}^{z}f(\zeta)g^{\prime}(\zeta)\,d\zeta\right|\leq\int_{0}^{z}|f(\zeta)g^{\prime}(\zeta)|\,|d\zeta|\\
&\lesssim\int_{0}^{|z|}\left(\frac{\lVert f\rVert^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}+1}{\omega(S(\zeta))\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|\zeta|}\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\frac{\left(\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(\zeta))}\right)\omega(S(\zeta))\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(1-|\zeta|)\left(\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(\zeta))}\right)\nu(S(\zeta))\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}}\,|d\zeta|\\
&\lesssim\frac{(\lVert f\rVert^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}+1)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(1-|z|)^{\beta}},\quad f\in A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},\end{split}

(3.21)

where the suppressed constant depends on
p,q,\omega,\Psi,\Phi
and
\|f\|_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}
. This estimate enables us to prove that for any
\nu\in\mathcal{D}
and
\Phi\in\mathcal{L}
, we have

\|T_{g}(f)\|^{q}_{A^{q}_{\nu,\Phi}}\lesssim 1+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|T_{g}(f)(z)|^{q}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\nu(z)\,dA(z),\quad g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}).

(3.22)

Indeed, if
\Phi
is essentially increasing, then (
3.21
) and Proposition
4
(ii) yields (
3.22
) directly. If
\Phi
is essentially decreasing, define
E_{\varepsilon}=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|T_{g}(f)(z)|<\frac{1}{(1-|z|)^{\varepsilon/q}}\}
, where
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(q,\nu)>0
will be fixed in such a way that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\nu_{[-\varepsilon]}(z)\,dA(z)<\infty.

This estimate together with Proposition
4
(ii) yields

\begin{split}\lVert T_{g}(f)\rVert_{A^{q}_{\nu,\Phi}}^{q}&=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|T_{g}(f)(z)|^{q}\Phi\left(|T_{g}(f)(z)|\right)\nu(z)\,dA(z)\\
&=\int_{E_{\varepsilon}}|T_{g}(f)(z)|^{q}\Phi\left(|T_{g}(f)(z)|\right)\nu(z)\,dA(z)\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus E_{\varepsilon}}|T_{g}(f)(z)|^{q}\Phi\left(|T_{g}(f)(z)|\right)\nu(z)\,dA(z)\\
&\lesssim 1+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|T_{g}(f)(z)|^{q}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\nu(z)\,dA(z),\end{split}

which proves (
3.22
). An integration by parts together with Lemma
10

(3.23)

Since

, standard arguments imply

, and hence
\Phi\left(\frac{1}{1-|\cdot|}\right)\frac{\widehat{\nu}}{1-|\cdot|}\in\mathcal{D}
by Proposition

[
14

), (
3.23
) and (
1.4
), we get

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{\mathbb{D}}|T_{g}(f)(z)|^{q}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\nu(z)\,dA(z)\\
&\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}|T_{g}(f)(z)|^{q}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\frac{\widehat{\nu}(z)}{1-|z|}\,dA(z)\\
&\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|)^{q}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\frac{\widehat{\nu}(z)}{1-|z|}\,dA(z)\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{q}\left(\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\frac{(\omega(S(z)))^{\frac{q}{p}}}{(1-|z|)^{2}}\,dA(z)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}|f(z)|^{q-p}\left(\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\frac{(\omega(S(z)))^{\frac{q}{p}}}{(1-|z|)^{2}}\,dA(z).\end{split}

(3.24)

Applying the growth estimate of
|f|^{q-p}
based on Lemma
12
into the above estimate and (
3.22
), we derive

\lVert T_{g}(f)\rVert_{A^{q}_{\nu,\Phi}}^{q}\lesssim 1+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\frac{\omega(S(z))}{(1-|z|)^{2}}\,dA(z).

From here we follow the same steps as were done in the proof of the sufficiency of Theorem
1
(i).

Assume next that
T_{g}:A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}\to A^{q}_{\nu,\Phi}
is bounded. By the subharmonicity of
|g^{\prime}|^{q}
and Proposition
4
(ii), we have

\begin{split}&\quad\frac{|g^{\prime}(a)|^{q}(1-|a|)^{q}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\nu(S(a))\right)}{\left(\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\omega(S(a))\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}}\\
&\lesssim\frac{(1-|a|)^{q-2}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\nu(S(a))}{\left(\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\omega(S(a))\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}}\int_{\Delta(a,r)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}\,dA(z)\\
&\asymp\int_{\Delta(a,r)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}\frac{(1-|z|)^{q-2}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(z))}\right)\nu(S(z))}{\left(\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\omega(S(a))\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}}\,dA(z)\\
&\asymp\int_{\Delta(a,r)}\left(\frac{1-|a|}{|1-\overline{a}z|}\right)^{\frac{\gamma q}{p}}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}\frac{(1-|z|)^{q-2}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(z))}\right)\nu(S(z))}{\left(\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\omega(S(a))\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}}\,dA(z)\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{a}(z)|^{q}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|)^{q}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\frac{\widehat{\nu}(z)}{1-|z|}\,dA(z),\quad a\in\mathbb{D},\end{split}

where
f_{a}
is exactly the same test function defined in Lemma
9
. By similar reasoning as was done on the proof sufficiency, it follows from the afternote of Lemma
10
,
[
14
, Theorem 5]
and an integration by parts that

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{a}(z)|^{q}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|)^{q}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\frac{\widehat{\nu}(z)}{1-|z|}\,dA(z)\\
&\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}|T_{g}(f_{a})(z)|^{q}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\frac{\widehat{\nu}(z)}{1-|z|}\,dA(z)\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|T_{g}(f_{a})(z)|^{q}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\nu(z)\,dA(z).\end{split}

Note that the omitted constant depends on
\|T_{g}(f_{a})\|_{A^{q}_{\nu,\Phi}}
. As
\|f_{a}\|_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}
is uniformly bounded, we can use the assumption that
T_{g}:A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}\to A^{q}_{\nu,\Phi}
is bounded together with Proposition
11
to show that the constant is actually universal. By following the steps used in Cases I and II in the proof of Theorem
1
, we finish the proof.

Proof of
Theorem
2
(ii)

Let
g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
and assume
T_{g}
is compact. On one hand, since
f_{a}
is uniformly bounded in
A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}
and
f_{a}\to 0
as
|a|\to 1^{-}
, we have by Proposition
8
,

\lim_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\|T_{g}(f_{a})\|_{A^{q}_{\nu,\Phi}}=0,\quad g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}).

(3.25)

On the other hand, a straightforward consequence from the proof of the necessity of (i) gives us

\begin{split}\frac{|g^{\prime}(a)|^{q}(1-|a|)^{q}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\nu(S(a))\right)}{\left(\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\omega(S(a))\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}}&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|T_{g}(f_{a})(z)|^{q}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\nu(z)\,dA(z),\end{split}

(3.26)

for
a\in\mathbb{D}
. The proof is complete by Proposition
11

and (
3.25
).

Conversely, assume (
1.5
) holds. Let
\{f_{n}\}
be a norm bounded sequence in
A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}
such that
\|f_{n}\|_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}<M
and
f_{n}\to 0
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
, as
n\to\infty
. To prove the assertion, by Proposition
8
, it suffices to show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T_{g}(f_{n})\|_{A^{q}_{\mu,\Phi}}=0.

(3.27)

To this end, let
\epsilon>0
. Then, the same proof as was used to verify (
3.22
) together with the dominated convergence theorem implies that there exists a
N_{1}=N_{1}(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n>N_{1}
,

\|T_{g}(f_{n})\|^{q}_{A^{q}_{\nu,\Phi}}\lesssim\epsilon+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|T_{g}(f_{n})(z)|^{q}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\nu(z)\,dA(z),\quad g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}),

where the suppressed constant depends on
p,q,\omega,\Psi,\Phi
and
M
. Hence (
3.24
) indicates that for any
R\in(0,1)

\begin{split}\lVert T_{g}(f_{n})\rVert_{A^{q}_{\nu,\Phi}}^{q}&\lesssim\epsilon+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|T_{g}(f_{n})(z)|^{q}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\nu(z)\,dA(z)\\
&\asymp\epsilon+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{n}(z)g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|)^{q}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\frac{\widehat{\nu}(z)}{1-|z|}\,dA(z)\\
&=\epsilon+\left(\int_{D(0,R)}+\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus D(0,R)}\right)|f_{n}(z)g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|)^{q}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\frac{\widehat{\nu}(z)}{1-|z|}\,dA(z)\\
&=\epsilon+I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

(3.28)

Since (
1.5
) holds by the hypothesis, for the above
\epsilon
, there exists a
R=R(\epsilon)\in(0,1)
such that for any
R<|a|<1

\frac{|g^{\prime}(a)|^{q}(1-|a|)^{q}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\nu(S(a))\right)}{\left(\Psi\left(\frac{1}{\omega(S(a))}\right)\omega(S(a))\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}}<\epsilon.

(3.29)

Therefore, for the above fixed
R
and
\epsilon
, the dominated convergence theorem implies the existence of a natural number
N_{2}=N_{2}(R,\epsilon)>N_{1}
such that for all
n>N_{2}
, the inequality
I_{1}\lesssim\epsilon
holds. Moreover, by (
3.29
) and Lemma
6
, we deduce

I_{2}\lesssim\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{n}(z)|^{p}\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\right)\frac{\omega(S(z))}{(1-|z|)^{2}}\,dA(z),

and therefore
I_{2}\lesssim\epsilon
by the proof of the sufficiency of
Theorem
1

and the fact that
\|f_{n}\|_{A^{p}_{\omega,\Psi}}<M
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Applying the estimates of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
into (
3.28
), we prove (
3.27
). The proof is now complete.

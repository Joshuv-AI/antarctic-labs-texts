Uniform distribution of polynomially-defined additive function to varying moduli
Agbolade Akande

Abstract




1
Introduction

Let
q
be a positive integer and
f
be an integer-valued arithmetic function. We say
f
is uniformly distributed modulo
q
if for every residue class
a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)

\displaystyle\#\{n\leq x:f(n)\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\}\sim\frac{x}{q}

\displaystyle\text{as $x\rightarrow\infty$}.

One way to prove that
f
is uniformly distributed is by considering the mean value of
f
composed with additive characters modulo
q

[
1
, Proposition 1.1]
:

f
is uniformly distributed modulo
q
if and only if for all
r=1,2,\ldots,q-1
, one has
\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{x}\sum\limits_{n\leq x}\exp\left(\frac{2\pi irf(n)}{q}\right)=0
.

Pillai in 1940
[
2
]
used this approach to show
\Omega(n)
is uniformly distributed modulo
q
for every positive integer
q
.
\Omega(n)
is an example of an
additive
function: an integer-valued arithmetic function
f
with the property
f(mn)=f(m)+f(n)
for coprime
m
and
n
. In 1969, Delange gave a general criterion
[
3
]
for when additive functions
f
are uniformly distributed modulo
q
for fixed
q
. Delange’s criterion is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Define two conditions for every integer
t>1
:

(A_{t})
The series
\sum\limits_{\underset{t\nmid f(p)}{p}}p^{-1}
diverges,

and

(B_{t})
For all
k\geq 1
, the number
\frac{2f(2^{k})}{t}
is an odd integer.

An additive function
f
is uniformly distributed modulo
q
in the following 3 cases:

•

q
is odd and
A_{p}
holds for every prime divisor
p
of
q
.

•

2\mid\mid q
,
A_{p}
holds for every odd prime divisor
p
of
q
and either
A_{2}
or
B_{2}
hold.

•

4\mid q
,
A_{p}
holds for every odd prime divisor
p
of
q
, and either
A_{2}
holds or both
B_{2}
and
A_{4}
hold.

Moreover, these conditions are necessary for
f
to be uniformly distributed modulo
q
.

We are interested in proving uniform distribution of a class of additive functions where the modulus is allowed to vary. To illustrate, consider the sum of prime divisors function
A(n)=\sum\limits_{p^{k}\mid\mid n}kp
. It was proved by Pollack and Singha Roy that for a fixed
K\geq 1
and any residue class
a\mod{q}
,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\overset{n\leq x}{A(n)\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}1\sim\frac{x}{q}

as
x\rightarrow\infty
,

for
q\leq(\log x)^{K}
. This was a byproduct of their work on the distribution of multiplicative functions in coprime residue classes
[
4
]
.

In this paper, we will consider
polynomially-defined additive
functions
f
: Additive functions
f
such that
f(p)=F(p)
for all primes
p
, where
F(x)
is a nonconstant polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
. In the next section, given a polynomially-defined additive function
f
satisfying mild conditions, we explicitly describe the collection of integers
q
such that
f
is uniformly distributed modulo
q
. We will then set up the same framework used in
[
4
]
by Pollack and Singha Roy. (See also the paper of Katai
[
5
]
, where a broadly similar approach is taken.) A new ingredient is the use of estimates for the size of sets of the form:

\mathcal{V}_{F,q,J}(w)=\left\{(v_{1},\cdots,v_{J})\mod{q}:(v_{1},\ldots,v_{J},q)=1,\sum\limits_{j=1}^{J}F(v_{j})\equiv w\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\right\}.

These estimates require bounds on exponential sums adopted from Cochrane
[
6
]
and Loh
[
7
]
. The end result is as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
K\geq 1
and
\epsilon\in(0,1]
. Let
f
be a polynomially-defined additive function such that
f(p)=F(p)
for all primes
p
where
F(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
with degree
d
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{f}
be the set of all positive integers
q
such that
f
is uniformly distributed modulo
q
. Then as
x\rightarrow\infty
,

\sum\limits_{\overset{n\leq x}{f(n)\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}1\sim\frac{x}{q},

for all residue classes
a\mod{q}
and
q\in\mathcal{S}_{f}\cap[1,(\log x)^{K}]
provided that either

•

d=1
, or

•

q\leq(\log x)^{\left(1-\epsilon\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{d}\right)^{-1}}
.

As we now explain, the extra condition on
q
in Theorem
1.2
when
d>1
is essentially optimal. Fix an integer
d\geq 2
and define
F(x)=(x-1)^{d}+1
. Let
f
be the completely additive function where
f(p)=F(p)
for all primes
p
and let
q=q_{1}^{d}
. By Theorem
1.1
,
f
is uniformly distributed for all
q
. Notice that
F(p)\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
if and only if
p\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q_{1})
. Hence, by Siegel-Walfisz’s theorem, there are at least
\frac{1+o(1)}{\varphi(q_{1})}\frac{x}{\log x}
primes
p\leq x
with
F(p)\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
. Therefore, the residue class
1\mod q
is overrepresented if
\frac{1}{\varphi(q_{1})}\frac{x}{\log x}
is much bigger than
\frac{x}{q}
, for example if
q^{1-1/d}>(\log x)^{(1+\delta)}
for some fixed
\delta>0
. This means that for uniform distribution we want
q
to be no more than
\approx(\log x)^{(1-\frac{1}{d})^{-1}}
. So the condition is essentially optimal.

2
Polynomial-defined arithmetic functions that are uniformly distributed modulo
q

Given a polynomially-defined additive function, we will use Delange’s criterion in Theorem
1.1
to determine the collection of integers
q
such that
f
is uniformly distributed modulo
q
. To check condition
B_{2}
, we will impose the restriction:
f(2^{k})
is odd for all
k\geq 2
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Fix
q\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{1\}
. Let
f
be a polynomially-defined additive function, such that
f(p)=F(p)
for all primes
p
,
F(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
. Suppose
f(2^{k})
is an odd integer for all
k\geq 2
. If there exist an odd prime
p
such that
p\mid q
and
F(x)\mod{p}
has a factor of

\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}(x-i),

then
f
is not uniformly distributed modulo
q
.

If
2\mid\mid q
and
F
has an even constant and
F(x)\mod{2}
has a factor of
(x-1)
, then
f
is not uniformly distributed modulo
q
.

If
4\mid q
and the alternating sum of coefficients of F is either
0
or
2\mod{4}
, then
f
is not uniformly distributed modulo
q
.

If neither of these conditions hold, then
f
is uniformly distributed modulo
q
.

Proof.

First, let
p
be an odd prime divisor
p
of
q
. By Theorem
1.1
,
f
is not uniformly distributed modulo
q
if
A_{p}
does not hold. Now we will use the fact that, for all
d\geq 2
,

A_{d}
holds if and only if there exist
a
such that
(a,d)=1
and
f(a)\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ d)

This follows from Dirichlet’s theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions. Therefore, the only polynomials where
A_{p}
does not hold are the polynomials
F
such that
F\mod{p}
has a factor of
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}(x-i)
.

Suppose
2\mid\mid q
. By Theorem
1.1
,
f
is not uniformly distributed modulo
q
if
A_{2}
and
B_{2}
both fail. The
A_{2}
case is similar to the last proof: the polynomials where
A_{2}
does not are the polynomials with the sum of its coefficients equal to 0 modulo 2. This is equivalent to polynomials
F
where
F\mod{2}
has a factor of
(x-1)
. The
B_{2}
case fails when the constant term is even.

Suppose
4\mid q
. By Theorem
1.1
,
f
is not uniformly distributed modulo
q
if
A_{4}
does not hold. Let
F(x)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}x^{i}
where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
F(1)\equiv F(3)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
. Then
\sum\limits_{\overset{0\leq i\leq d}{\text{$i$ even}}}a_{i}+\sum\limits_{\overset{0\leq i\leq d}{\text{$i$ odd}}}a_{i}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
and
\sum\limits_{\overset{0\leq i\leq d}{\text{$i$ even}}}a_{i}-\sum\limits_{\overset{0\leq i\leq d}{\text{$i$ odd}}}a_{i}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
. The only solutions to these system of congruences are
\sum\limits_{\overset{0\leq i\leq d}{\text{$i$ even}}}a_{i}\equiv\sum\limits_{\overset{0\leq i\leq d}{\text{$i$ odd}}}a_{i}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
and
\sum\limits_{\overset{0\leq i\leq d}{\text{$i$ even}}}a_{i}\equiv\sum\limits_{\overset{0\leq i\leq d}{\text{$i$ odd}}}a_{i}\equiv 2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
.

If
F
does not satisfy any of those conditions mentioned, the criteria hold for all the cases in Theorem
1.1
. Thus,
f
is uniformly distributed modulo
q
.
∎

3
Bounds on polynomials composed with additive characters

We will use bounds from Cochrane
[
6
]
and Loh
[
7
]
. The first bound in
[
6
]
applies to polynomials that are nonconstant modulo
\ell
for
\ell
prime; a polynomial
F
is
nonconstant modulo
\ell
if and only if the map
F:\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}\mapsto\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
is nonconstant.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all primes
\ell
and polynomials
F(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
that are nonconstant modulo
\ell
,

\left\lvert\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{k}}{(v,\ell)=1}}{\exp\left(\frac{2\pi iF(v)}{\ell^{k}}\right)}\right\rvert\leq 8.82\ell^{k(1-\frac{1}{d+1})}.

Lemma
3.1
cannot be applied to all polynomials we are interested in. For example, let
F(x)=x^{2}+x+1
and
f
be the completely additive function such that
f(p)=F(p)
for all primes
p
. By Theorem
1.1
,
f
is uniformly distributed modulo
q
for all positive integers
q
but
F
is constant modulo 2. So, in the rest of this section, we will focus on the polynomials Lemma
3.1
does not apply to.

Firstly, notice that
\left\lvert\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{k}}{(v,\ell)=1}}{\exp\left(\frac{2\pi iF(v)}{\ell^{k}}\right)}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{k}}{(v,\ell)=1}}{\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i(F(v)+t)}{\ell^{k}}\right)}\right\rvert
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
so the constant term of
F
does not play a role. Also, if
F
is constant modulo
\ell
with
d\geq 1
, there have to be at least 2 terms in the polynomial.

Next, we will consider the polynomials where
\ell
does not divide the content of
F
: We define the
content of
F
to be the greatest common divisor of the coefficients of
F
. The following lemma, which gives an upper bound on the exponential sum with a condition on the content of
F
, is stated in
[
7
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
F(x)
be a polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
such that

F(x)=a_{s}x^{d_{s}}+a_{s-1}x^{d_{s-1}}+\cdots+a_{2}x^{d_{2}}+a_{1}x^{d_{1}}

with
s\geq 2
and
d_{s}>d_{s-1}>\cdots>d_{1}\geq 1
, and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
.

Suppose
\ell
is prime and
k
is a positive integer.

If
\ell>d_{s}
and
\ell
does not divide the content of
F
, then

\left\lvert\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{k}}{(v,\ell)=1}}{\exp\left(\frac{2\pi iF(v)}{\ell^{k}}\right)}\right\rvert\leq(d_{s}-1)\ell^{m(1-\frac{1}{s})}.

If
\ell\leq d_{s}
and
\ell
does not divide the content of
F
, then

\left\lvert\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{k}}{(v,\ell)=1}}{\exp\left(\frac{2\pi iF(v)}{\ell^{k}}\right)}\right\rvert\leq R(\ell^{-\tau}F^{\prime})\ell^{\frac{\tau+1}{s}}\ell^{m(1-\frac{1}{s})}

where
\tau
is the largest nonnegative integer such that
\ell^{\tau}
divides the content of
F^{\prime}(x)
and
R(F)
is the number of roots of the congruence

F(X)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \ell).

Since
\tau
and
R(\ell^{-\tau}F^{\prime})
depend only on
F
, they are both
O_{F}(1)
. Also, if
d
is the degree of
f
, then
s<d+1
. Hence, the inequalities in Lemma
3.2
can be combined into a single inequality so that a uniform upper bound on the exponential sum is
O_{F}(\ell^{m(1-\frac{1}{d+1})})
. Still, there are polynomials that Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
cannot be applied to, the polynomials
F
where
\ell
does divide the content of
F
without its constant term and
F
is constant modulo
\ell
. For instance,
F(x)=2x+1
is constant modulo 2 and 2 divides the content of
F(x)-1
but the completely additive function
f
such that
f(p)=F(p)
for all primes
p
is uniformly distributed modulo
q
for all positive integers
q
.

Let
t
be the largest nonnegative integer such that
\ell^{t}
divides the content of
F(x)
without its constant term
c_{0}
. Then,
F(x)=\ell^{t}G(x)+c_{0}
where
G(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
and
\ell
does not divide the content of
G(x)
. Then for
k>t
,

\left\lvert\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{k}}{(v,\ell)=1}}{\exp\left(\frac{2\pi iF(v)}{\ell^{k}}\right)}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{k}}{(v,\ell)=1}}{\exp\left(\frac{2\pi iG(v)}{\ell^{m-t}}\right)}\right\rvert=\ell^{t}\left\lvert\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{m-t}}{(v,\ell)=1}}{\exp\left(\frac{2\pi iG(v)}{\ell^{m-t}}\right)}\right\rvert.

We now apply the bounds that we already have on the last sum. Since
\ell^{t}
divides the content of
F
without its constant term,
\ell^{t}
is
O_{F}(1)
. Combining all the bounds that we have, we can form a corollary:

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
F(x)
be a nonconstant polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
. Then, for all positive integers
k
and primes
\ell
,

\left\lvert\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{k}}{(v,\ell)=1}}{\exp\left(\frac{2\pi iF(v)}{\ell^{k}}\right)}\right\rvert\leq C_{F}\ell^{k(1-\frac{1}{d+1})}

where
C_{F}
is a constant that depends on
F
.

4
Proof of Theorem
1.2
: the notion of convenient numbers

Fix
K\geq 1
and
\epsilon>0
. Let
f
be a polynomially-defined additive function such that
f(p)=F(p)
for all primes
p
and
F(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
. We will focus on integers
n\leq x
and varying
q\in\mathcal{S}_{f}\cap[1,(\log x)^{K}]
. Similar to Pollack and Singha Roy’s paper
[
4
]
, we will consider the
n
with many large non-repeated prime factors and we will show that the count of everything else is negligible compared to the count of these special integers.

We will first define parameters
J=\lfloor\log\log\log x\rfloor
and
y=\exp((\log x)^{\frac{\epsilon}{2}})
. We call a positive integer
n

convenient
if the following conditions hold:

•

n\leq x
,

•

the
J
largest prime factors of
n
with multiplicity exceed
y
,

•

none of these
J
primes are repeated in
n
.

To summarize,
n
is convenient if and only if
n
admits an expression:
n=mP_{1}\cdots P_{J}
, where
P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}
are primes with

\displaystyle\max\{P(m),y\}<P_{J}<\cdots<P_{1},

\displaystyle P_{1}\cdots P_{J}\leq x/m.

Lemma 4.1
.

The number of inconvenient
n\leq x
is at most
o(x)
.

Proof.

We follow the proof in
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
. Suppose
n\leq x
is inconvenient. We can assume
P(n)>z=x^{1/\log\log x}
(
P(n)
is the largest prime factor of
n
) because the number of exceptional
n\leq x
, by theorems on smooth numbers
[
8
, Theorem 5.13 and Corollary
5.19, Chapter III.5]
, is
\frac{x}{(\log x)^{(1+o(1))\log\log\log x}}=o(x)
. We can also assume that
n
has no repeated prime factors exceeding
y
because the number of exceptional
n\leq x
is
O(x/y)=o(x)
.

We will denote
n=PAB
where
P=P(n)
and
A
is the largest divisor of
n/P
supported on primes exceeding
y
. Then
z<P\leq x/AB
. If
A
and
B
are given, the number of possibilities of
P
is bounded by
\pi(x/AB)\ll\frac{x}{AB\log z}\ll\frac{x\log\log x}{AB\log x}
. We sum on
A
and
B
to bound the number of inconvenient
n
. As
n
has no repeated primes exceeding
y
and
n
is inconvenient,
\Omega(A)<J
. Thus
\sum\frac{1}{A}\leq\left(1+\sum\limits_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p}\right)^{J}\leq(2\log\log x)^{J}\leq\exp\left(O((\log\log\log x)^{2})\right)
. Since
B
is
y
-smooth,
\sum\frac{1}{B}=\prod\limits_{p\leq y}\left(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{j}}\right)\ll\exp\left(\sum\limits_{p\leq y}\frac{1}{p}\right)\ll\log y=(\log x)^{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}
. Combining all the results, the number of the remaining inconvenient
n\leq x
is at most

\displaystyle\frac{x\log\log x}{(\log x)^{1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}}\exp(O((\log\log\log x)^{2}))=o(x).

Next, we show that the count of inconvenient integers
n\leq x
with the condition
f(n)\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
, for polynomially-defined additive function
f
, is negligible compared to the count of convenient integers with the same condition:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f
be a polynomially-defined function, such that
f(p)=F(p)
for all primes and
F(x)
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
with degree
d
. If
q
satisfies the conditions in Theorem
1.2
, the number of inconvenient
n\leq x
where
f(n)\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
is
o(x/q)
for all residue classes
a\mod q
.

Proof.

We follow the the verification of Hypothesis B in section 5 of
[
4
]
. Suppose
n\leq x
is inconvenient and
f(n)\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
. From the proof in Lemma
4.1
, we can assume
P(n)>z=x^{1/\log\log x}
and that
n
has no repeated prime factors exceeding
y
because the number of exceptional
n\leq x
is
o(x/q)
.

We will denote
n=PAB
where
P=P(n)
and
A
is the largest divisor of
n/P
supported on primes exceeding
y
. Then
z<P\leq x/AB
and
f(P)+f(AB)\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
. Let
\xi(q)
be the maximum number of roots
v\mod{q}
of any congruence
F(v)\equiv a^{\prime}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
, where the maximum is over all residue classes
a^{\prime}\mod{q}
. By the Brun–Titchmarsh inequality, given
A
and
B
there are
\ll\xi(q)\frac{x}{\varphi(q)AB\log(z/q)}\ll\xi(q)\frac{x\log\log x}{\varphi(q)AB\log x}
corresponding values of
P
. Summing over
A
and
B
, using the same process as Lemma
4.1
, the number of remaining inconvenient
n\leq x
such that
f(n)\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
is at most

\xi(q)\frac{x\log\log x}{\varphi(q)(\log x)^{1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}}\exp(O((\log\log\log x)^{2})).

Since
\frac{1}{\log\log 3q}\ll\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\ll 1
for all
q\geq 2
, in our range of
q
the ratio
\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}
is negligible compared to any power of
\log x
. Hence, it is enough to show
\xi(q)=o\left((\log x)^{1-\frac{3}{4}\epsilon}\right)
.

When
d=1
,
\xi(q)=O_{F}(1)
. (In fact, it is bounded above by the absolute value of leading coefficient of
F
.) Hence, let
d\geq 2
. We will first consider when
q
is prime. Then,
\xi(q)
is at most
d
expect for finitely many primes. (If
F
does not reduce to a constant modulo
q
,
\xi(q)
is at most
d
). Extending to when
q
is squarefree,
\xi(q)=O_{F}(d^{\omega(q)})
. Using the asymptotic formula
\omega(q)\ll\frac{\log q}{\log\log q}
from
[
9
]
, in our range of
q
,
d^{\omega(q)}=\exp\left(O_{F}\left(\frac{\log\log x\log d}{\log\log\log x}\right)\right)
which is smaller than the bound we want. Otherwise, by a result of Konyagin in
[
10
]
, each congruence
F(v)\equiv a^{\prime}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
has
O_{F}(q^{1-1/d})
roots modulo
q
for any residue class
a^{\prime}\mod{q}
. Using the condition in Theorem
1.2
,
q\leq(\log x)^{\left(1-\epsilon\right)\left(1-1/d\right)^{-1}}
will satisfy our bound.
∎

Therefore, by Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, it suffices to show

\sum\limits_{\overset{\text{convenient $n\leq x$}}{f(n)\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}1\sim\frac{1}{q}\sum\limits_{\text{convenient $n\leq x$}}1.

(1)

We will now rewrite the number of ways we can count the number of convenient numbers as follows:

\sum\limits_{\text{convenient $n\leq x$}}1=\frac{1}{J!}\sum\limits_{m\leq x}\sum\limits_{\overset{P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}\text{ distinct}}{\overset{P_{1}\cdots P_{J}\leq x/m}{{\text{each }P_{J}>L_{m}}}}}1

(2)

where
L_{m}=\max\{y,P(m)\}
. We will define for an arbitrary class
w\mod q

\mathcal{V}(w)=\{(v_{1},\cdots,v_{J})\mod{q}:(v_{1},\ldots,v_{J},q)=1,\sum\limits_{j=1}^{J}F(v_{j})\equiv w\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\}

so we can write

\sum\limits_{\overset{\text{convenient $n\leq x$}}{f(n)\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}1=\frac{1}{J!}\sum\limits_{m\leq x}\sum\limits_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{m}}\sum\limits_{\overset{P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}\text{ distinct}}{\overset{P_{1}\cdots P_{J}\leq x/m}{\overset{\text{each }P_{J}>L_{m}}{\text{each }P_{j}\equiv v_{j}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}}}1

(3)

where
L_{m}=\max\{y,P(m)\}
and
\mathcal{V}_{m}=\mathcal{V}(a-f(m))
.

We will proceed as in
[
4
]
to remove the congruence in the innermost sum over the
J
largest primes in (
3
). Write

\sum\limits_{\overset{P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}\text{ distinct}}{\overset{P_{1}\cdots P_{J}\leq x/m}{\overset{\text{each }P_{j}>L_{m}}{\text{each }P_{j}\equiv v_{j}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}}}1=\sum\limits_{\overset{P_{2},\ldots,P_{J}\text{ distinct}}{\overset{P_{2}\cdots P_{J}\leq x/mL_{m}}{\overset{\text{each }P_{j}>L_{m}}{\text{each }P_{j}\equiv v_{j}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}}}\sum\limits_{\overset{P_{1}\neq P_{2},\ldots,P_{J}\text{ distinct}}{\overset{L_{m}<P_{1}\leq x/(mP_{2}\cdots P_{J})}{P_{1}\equiv v_{1}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}}1.

Since
L_{m}\geq y
and
q\leq(\log x)^{K}=(\log y)^{2K}
, the Siegel-Walfisz theorem implies that

\sum\limits_{\overset{P_{1}\neq P_{2},\ldots,P_{J}\text{ distinct}}{\overset{L_{m}<P_{1}\leq x/(mP_{2}\cdots P_{J})}{P_{1}\equiv v_{1}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}}1=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum\limits_{\overset{P_{1}\neq P_{2},\ldots,P_{J}\text{ distinct}}{{L_{m}<P_{1}\leq x/(mP_{2}\cdots P_{J})}}}1+O\left(\frac{x}{mP_{2}\cdots P_{J}}\exp(-C_{K}\sqrt{\log y})\right).

Thus,

\sum\limits_{\overset{P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}\text{ distinct}}{\overset{P_{1}\cdots P_{J}\leq x/m}{\overset{\text{each }P_{j}>L_{m}}{\text{each }P_{j}\equiv v_{j}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}}}1=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum\limits_{\overset{P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}\text{ distinct}}{\overset{P_{1}\cdots P_{J}\leq x/m}{\overset{\text{each }P_{j}>L_{m}}{\text{$(\forall j\geq 2)$ }P_{j}\equiv v_{j}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}}}1+O\left(\frac{x}{m}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}C_{K}(\log x)^{\epsilon/4}\right)\right).

Repeating the same steps for
P_{2},\ldots,P_{J}
, we have

\sum\limits_{\overset{P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}\text{ distinct}}{\overset{P_{1}\cdots P_{J}\leq x/m}{\overset{\text{each }P_{j}>L_{m}}{\text{each }P_{j}\equiv v_{j}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}}}1=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)^{J}}\sum\limits_{\overset{P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}\text{ distinct}}{\overset{P_{1}\cdots P_{J}\leq x/m}{{\text{each }P_{j}>L_{m}}}}}1+O\left(\frac{x}{m}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4}C_{K}(\log x)^{\epsilon/4}\right)\right).

Putting this back into (
3
) and noting that
\#\mathcal{V}_{m}\leq(\log x)^{KJ}
,

\sum\limits_{\overset{\text{convenient $n\leq x$}}{f(n)\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}1=\sum\limits_{m\leq x}\frac{\#\mathcal{V}_{m}}{\varphi(q)^{J}}\left(\frac{1}{J!}\sum\limits_{\overset{P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}\text{ distinct}}{\overset{P_{1}\cdots P_{J}\leq x/m}{{\text{each }P_{j}>L_{m}}}}}1\right)+O\left(x\exp\left(-\frac{1}{8}C_{K}(\log x)^{\epsilon/4}\right)\right).

(4)

We will find an asymptotic formula for
\#\mathcal{V}_{m}
using exponential sums. We can write
\#\mathcal{V}_{m}=\#\mathcal{V}(w)=\prod\limits_{\ell^{e}\mid\mid q}\#V_{\ell^{e}}
for each prime power
\ell^{e}\mid\mid q
where
V_{\ell^{e}}
can be written as follows:

V_{\ell^{e}}=\{(v_{1},\cdots,v_{J})\mod{\ell^{e}}:(v_{1},\ldots,v_{J},\ell)=1,\sum\limits_{j=1}^{J}F(v_{j})\equiv w\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \ell^{e})\}.

By the orthogonality relations for exponential sums,

\ell^{e}\#V_{\ell^{e}}=\sum\limits_{r\mod\ell^{e}}\sum\limits_{\overset{v_{1},\ldots,v_{J}}{(v_{1}\cdots v_{J},\ell)=1}}\exp\left(\frac{2\pi ir\left[\sum\limits_{j=1}^{J}F(v_{j})-w\right]}{\ell^{e}}\right)

=\varphi(\ell^{e})^{J}+\sum\limits_{0<r<\ell^{e}}\exp\left(\frac{-2\pi irw}{\ell^{e}}\right)\left[\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{e}}{(v,\ell)=1}}\exp\left(\frac{2\pi irF(v)}{\ell^{e}}\right)\right]^{J}

=\varphi(\ell^{e})^{J}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{e}(\ell^{J})^{e-k}\left(\sum\limits_{\overset{0<r<\ell^{k}}{\ell\nmid r}}\exp\left(\frac{-2\pi irw}{\ell^{k}}\right)\left[\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{k}}{(v,\ell)=1}}\exp\left(\frac{2\pi irF(v)}{\ell^{k}}\right)\right]^{J}\right).

We can rewrite this as
\varphi(\ell^{e})^{J}\left[1+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{e}H(\ell,k,J)\right]
, where

H(\ell,k,J)=\sum\limits_{\overset{0<r<\ell^{k}}{\ell\nmid r}}\exp\left(\frac{-2\pi irw}{\ell^{k}}\right)\left[\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{k}}{(v,\ell)=1}}\frac{\exp\left(\frac{2\pi irF(v)}{\ell^{k}}\right)}{\varphi(\ell^{k})}\right]^{J}.

Let
\ell
be a prime dividing
q
with
e=e(\ell,q)
satisfying
\ell^{e}\mid\mid q
. Define
S_{\ell}=\mathbb{N}\cap[1,e]
and three subsets
X_{\ell}
,
Y_{\ell}
, and
Z_{\ell}
such that

•

X_{\ell}=\left\{k\in S_{\ell}:\left\lvert\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{k}}{(v,\ell)=1}}\exp\left(\frac{2\pi irF(v)}{\ell^{k}}\right)\right\rvert={\varphi(\ell^{k})}\right\}
,

•

Y_{\ell}=\{k\in S_{\ell}\backslash X_{\ell}:\frac{C_{F}\ell^{k(1-\frac{1}{d+1})}}{\varphi(\ell^{k})}<1\}
where
C_{F}
is the constant in Corollary
3.3
, and

•

Z_{\ell}=\{k\in S_{\ell}\backslash(X_{\ell}\cup Y_{\ell})\}
.

We will show that for each
\ell
, the sums over
Y_{\ell}
and
Z_{\ell}
are vanishing as
J\rightarrow\infty
, that
X_{\ell}
and
Z_{\ell}
are empty for sufficiently large
\ell
, and that the asymptotic bounds on
\#\mathcal{V}(w)
come from
X_{\ell}
for small
\ell
.

Define
M=M(F,q)
to be the largest positive integer dividing
q
such that
F(v)\equiv F(1)\ (\mathrm{mod}\ M)
for all
v
coprime to
M
.
M
is well defined and
M\ll_{F}1
because
F
is a nonconstant polynomial. Let
D_{\ell}
be the largest positive integer such that
\ell^{D_{\ell}}\mid M
.

Lemma 4.3
.

A closed form of the sum over
X_{\ell}
is as follows:

\sum\limits_{k\in X_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $\ell\nmid M$},\\
\ell^{D_{\ell}}-1&\text{if $\ell\mid M$ and $F(1)J\equiv w\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \ell^{D_{\ell}})$},\\
-1&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
k\in X_{\ell}
,
\exp\left(\frac{2\pi irF(v)}{\ell^{k}}\right)=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi irF(1)}{\ell^{k}}\right)
for all
v
coprime to
\ell
. Thus,
F(v)\equiv F(1)\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \ell^{k})
and

\ell^{k}\mid M
. It follows that
X_{\ell}=\{k\in\mathbb{N}:k\leq D_{\ell}\}
; in particular,
X_{\ell}=\emptyset
if
D_{\ell}=0
. This gives the evaluation of the sum when
\ell\nmid M
. Otherwise,

\sum\limits_{k\in X_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{D_{\ell}}\sum\limits_{\overset{0<r<\ell^{k}}{\ell\nmid r}}\exp\left(\frac{-2\pi ir(w-F(1)J)}{\ell^{k}}\right)

=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{D_{\ell}}\left[\sum\limits_{r\mod{\ell^{k}}}\exp\left(\frac{-2\pi ir(w-F(1)J)}{\ell^{k}}\right)-\sum\limits_{r\mod{\ell^{k-1}}}\exp\left(\frac{-2\pi ir(w-F(1)J)}{\ell^{k-1}}\right)\right]

=-1+\left[\sum\limits_{r\mod{\ell^{D_{\ell}}}}\exp\left(\frac{-2\pi ir(w-F(1)J)}{\ell^{D_{\ell}}}\right)\right].

By the orthogonality relation for additive characters, the sum on
r
is
\ell^{D_{\ell}}
if
F(1)J\equiv w\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \ell^{D_{\ell}})
and 0 otherwise.
∎

To study the sum over
Y_{\ell}
and
Z_{\ell}
, we consider
B(\ell,k)=\frac{C_{F}\ell^{k(1-\frac{1}{d+1})}}{\varphi(\ell^{k})}
as a continuous function on the domain
\{(\ell,k):\text{$\ell\geq 2$ and $k\geq 1$}\}
. It is easy to see that
B(\ell,k)
is a monotonically decreasing function of both
\ell
and
k
. Thus, for every fixed
\ell
,
B(\ell,k)<1
for all large
k
. We record the following consequences:

•

For every fixed
\ell
, there exist a smallest positive integer
E_{\ell}
not in
X_{\ell}
such that
B(\ell,E_{\ell})<1
. Then
Y_{\ell}=\{k\in\mathbb{N}:E_{\ell}\leq k\leq e\}
.

•

There exists a value of
k
, bounded in terms of
F
, with
B(\ell,k)<1
for all
\ell\geq 2
. Thus,
E_{\ell}\ll_{F}1
.

•

For large
\ell
, we have
B(\ell,1)<1
. Since
X_{\ell}
is empty when
\ell>M
,
E_{\ell}=1
for all large
\ell
. For these
\ell
, we have
Y_{\ell}=S_{\ell}
and
Z_{\ell}=\emptyset
. We fix
\ell_{0}=\ell_{0}(F)
such that
Y_{\ell}=S_{\ell}
and
2C_{F}\leq\ell^{\frac{1}{2(d+1)}}
for all
\ell>\ell_{0}
.

If
k\not\in X_{\ell}
,
\left\lvert\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{k}}{(v,\ell)=1}}\frac{\exp\left(\frac{2\pi irF(v)}{\ell^{k}}\right)}{\varphi(\ell^{k})}\right\rvert<1
. So we may find a positive
\eta\gg_{F}1
, satisfying for all
\ell\leq\ell_{0}
,

(1-\eta)\geq\begin{cases}B(\ell,k)&\text{if $k\geq E_{\ell}$},\\
\left\lvert\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{k}}{(v,\ell)=1}}\frac{\exp\left(\frac{2\pi irF(v)}{\ell^{k}}\right)}{\varphi(\ell^{k})}\right\rvert&\text{if $D_{\ell}<k<E_{\ell}$}.\end{cases}

Lemma 4.4
.

As
x\rightarrow\infty
(which coincides with
J\rightarrow\infty
),

\sum\limits_{\ell\mid q}\left\lvert\sum\limits_{k\in Y_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right\rvert=o(1).

Proof.

Using Corollary
3.3
, as
J\rightarrow\infty
,

\left\lvert\sum\limits_{k\in Y_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right\rvert\leq\sum\limits_{k=E_{\ell}}^{\infty}\left[\frac{C_{F}\ell^{k(1-\frac{1}{d+1})}}{\varphi(\ell^{k})}\right]^{J}\varphi(\ell^{k})\ll\left[\frac{C_{F}\ell^{E_{\ell}(1-\frac{1}{d+1})}}{\varphi(\ell^{E_{\ell}})}\right]^{J}\ell^{E_{\ell}}\leq\left[\frac{C_{F}\ell^{(1-\frac{1}{d+1})}}{\ell-1}\right]^{J}\ell^{E_{\ell}}.

We used the fact that
\sum\limits_{k=E_{\ell}}^{\infty}\left[\frac{C_{F}\ell^{k(1-\frac{1}{d+1})}}{\varphi(\ell^{k})}\right]^{J}\varphi(\ell^{k})
is a geometric series with ratio
\ell^{(1-\frac{J}{d+1})}
. As
J
gets large,
\ell^{(1-\frac{J}{d+1})}\leq 2^{(1-\frac{J}{d+1})}\leq\frac{1}{2}
so the sum of the geometric series is at most twice the first term.

If we assume that
\ell>\ell_{0}
, then
E_{\ell}=1
and
\frac{C_{F}\ell^{(1-\frac{1}{d+1})}}{\ell-1}\leq\ell^{-\frac{1}{2(d+1)}}
so that

\left\lvert\sum\limits_{k\in Y_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right\rvert=O\left(\ell^{1-\frac{J}{2(d+1)}}\right)=O(\ell^{-\frac{J}{4(d+1)}}).

(5)

For the exceptional
\ell\leq\ell_{0}
, as
J\rightarrow\infty
,

\left\lvert\sum\limits_{k\in Y_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right\rvert\ll\left[\frac{C_{F}\ell^{E_{\ell}(1-\frac{1}{d+1})}}{\varphi(\ell^{E_{\ell}})}\right]^{J}\ell^{E_{\ell}}=O_{F}((1-\eta)^{J})

(6)

recalling that
E_{\ell}\ll_{F}1
. It follows from (
5
) and (
6
) that
\sum\limits_{\ell\mid q}\left\lvert\sum\limits_{k\in Y_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right\rvert=o(1)
as
J\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

As
x\rightarrow\infty
,

\sum\limits_{\ell\mid q}\left\lvert\sum\limits_{k\in Z_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right\rvert=o(1).

Proof.

Since
Z_{\ell}
is empty for
\ell>\ell_{0}
, we may restrict attention to
\ell\leq\ell_{0}
. By definition of
D_{\ell}
and
E_{\ell}
, we can write
Z_{\ell}=\{k\in\mathbb{N}:D_{\ell}<k<E_{\ell}\}
. Thus, recalling that
E_{\ell}\ll_{F}1
,

\left\lvert\sum\limits_{k\in Z_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right\rvert\leq(E_{\ell}-D_{\ell})\max\limits_{k\in Z_{\ell}}\left\{\left\lvert\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{k}}{(v,\ell)=1}}\frac{\exp\left(\frac{2\pi irf(v)}{\ell^{k}}\right)}{\varphi(\ell^{k})}\right\rvert^{J}\varphi(\ell^{k})\right\}

<(\ell^{E_{\ell}}\cdot E_{\ell})\cdot\max\limits_{D_{\ell}<k<E_{\ell}}\left\{\left\lvert\sum\limits_{\overset{v\mod\ell^{k}}{(v,\ell)=1}}\frac{\exp\left(\frac{2\pi irf(v)}{\ell^{k}}\right)}{\varphi(\ell^{k})}\right\rvert\right\}^{J}.

=O\left(\ell^{E_{\ell}}\cdot E_{\ell}\cdot(1-\eta)^{J}\right)=O_{F}((1-\eta)^{J}).

Hence,
\sum\limits_{\ell\mid q}\left\lvert\sum\limits_{k\in Z_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right\rvert=o(1)
as
J\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

We can write,

\#V_{\ell^{e}}=\frac{\varphi(\ell^{e})^{J}}{\ell^{e}}\Big[1+\sum\limits_{k\in X_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)+\sum\limits_{k\in Y_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)+\sum\limits_{k\in Z_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\Big].

Multiplying over all
\ell

,

\#\mathcal{V}(w)=\frac{\varphi(q)^{J}}{q}\prod\limits_{\ell\mid q}\left[1+\sum\limits_{k\in X_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)+\sum\limits_{k\in Y_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)+\sum\limits_{k\in Z_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right].

To proceed, we observe that
\#\mathcal{V}(w)=0
unless

for all
\ell

. Indeed, since

. Thus, using Lemma

\#\mathcal{V}(w)=\mathbbm{1}_{F(1)J\equiv w\ (\mathrm{mod}\ M)}\frac{\varphi(q)^{J}}{q}\prod\limits_{\overset{\ell\mid q}{\ell\mid M}}\left[\ell^{D_{\ell}}+\sum\limits_{k\in Y_{\ell}\cup Z_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right]\prod\limits_{\overset{\ell\mid q}{\ell\nmid M}}\left[1+\sum\limits_{k\in Y_{\ell}\cup Z_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right]

=\mathbbm{1}_{F(1)J\equiv w\ (\mathrm{mod}\ M)}\frac{M\varphi(q)^{J}}{q}\prod\limits_{\overset{\ell\mid q}{\ell\mid M}}\left[1+\frac{1}{\ell^{D_{\ell}}}\sum\limits_{k\in Y_{\ell}\cup Z_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right]\prod\limits_{\overset{\ell\mid q}{\ell\nmid M}}\left[1+\sum\limits_{k\in Y_{\ell}\cup Z_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right].

By Lemmas

\sum\limits_{\ell\mid q}\left\lvert\sum\limits_{k\in Y_{\ell}\cup Z_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right\rvert=o(1)

. Therefore,

\prod\limits_{\overset{\ell\mid q}{\ell\mid M}}\left[1+\frac{1}{\ell^{D_{\ell}}}\sum\limits_{k\in Y_{\ell}\cup Z_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right]\prod\limits_{\overset{\ell\mid q}{\ell\nmid M}}\left[1+\sum\limits_{k\in Y_{\ell}\cup Z_{\ell}}H(\ell,k,J)\right]=1+o(1),

and

\#\mathcal{V}(w)=(1+o(1))\mathbbm{1}_{F(1)J\equiv w\ (\mathrm{mod}\ M)}\frac{M\varphi(q)^{J}}{q}.

Hence, using this asymptotic in (

\sum\limits_{\overset{\text{convenient $n\leq x$}}{f(n)\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}}1=\sum\limits_{m\leq x}\frac{\#\mathcal{V}_{m}}{\varphi(q)^{J}}\left(\frac{1}{J!}\sum\limits_{\overset{P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}\text{ distinct}}{\overset{P_{1}\cdots P_{J}\leq x/m}{\overset{\text{each }P_{J}>L_{m}}{F(1)J\equiv w\mod{M}}}}}1\right)+O\left(x\exp\left(-\frac{1}{8}C_{K}(\log x)^{\epsilon/4}\right)\right)

=(1+o(1))\frac{M}{q}\left(\frac{1}{J!}\sum\limits_{m\leq x}\sum\limits_{\overset{P_{1},\ldots,P_{J}\text{ distinct}}{\overset{P_{1}\cdots P_{J}\leq x/m}{\overset{\text{each }P_{J}>L_{m}}{f(P_{1}\cdots P_{J})\equiv a-f(m)\mod{M}}}}}1\right)+O\left(x\exp\left(-\frac{1}{8}C_{K}(\log x)^{\epsilon/4}\right)\right).

M
. Since

(7)

Abstract
. In [ KL22 ] , the authors studied the mean-square of certain sums of the divisor function d k ​ ( f ) d_{k}(f) over the function field 𝔽 q ​ [ T ] \mathbb{F}_{q}[T] in the limit as q → ∞ q\to\infty and related these sums to integrals over the ensemble of symplectic matrices, along similar lines as previous work of Keating, Rodgers, Roditty-Gershon and Rudnick [ KRRGR18 ] for unitary matrices. We present an analogous problem yielding an integral over the ensemble of orthogonal matrices and pursue a more detailed study of both the symplectic and orthogonal matrix integrals, relating them to symmetric function theory. The function field results lead to conjectures concerning analogous questions over number fields.

1.
Introduction

The goal of this article is to study the connection between arithmetic sums involving the divisor function and integrals over the ensembles of unitary symplectic and orthogonal matrices. Consider the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
q
is an odd prime power. For a monic polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, define the divisor function
d_{k}(f)
, analogously to the number field case, via

d_{k}(f):=\#\{(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})\,:\,f=f_{1}\cdots f_{k},\,f_{j}\mbox{ monic}\}.

In
[
KRRGR18
]
, Keating, Rodgers, Roditty-Gershon, and Rudnick consider the distribution of

\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};n;Q}(A):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\,\text{monic},\deg(f)=n\\
f\equiv A\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)\end{subarray}}d_{k}(f).

For
Q
a square-free polynomial and
n\leq k(\deg(Q)-1)
, they prove in
[
KRRGR18
, Theorem 3.1]

that the variance of
\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};n;Q}
, defined by

\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};n;Q}):=\frac{1}{\Phi(Q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)\\
\mathrm{gcd}(A,Q)=1\end{subarray}}\left|\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};n;Q}(A)-\left\langle\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};n;Q}\right\rangle\right|^{2},

is given by

(1)

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};n;Q})}{q^{n}/|Q|}=\int_{\mathrm{U}(\deg(Q)-1)}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=n\\
0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\leq\deg(Q)-1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{1}}(U)\cdots\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{k}}(U)\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}U,

where the integral ranges over complex unitary matrices of dimension
\deg(Q)-1
, and the
\mathrm{Sc}_{j}(U)
are the secular coefficients, defined for a
N\times N
matrix
U
via

\det(I+Ux)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\mathrm{Sc}_{j}(U)x^{j}.

Letting
c=\frac{m}{N}\in[0,k]
, Keating et al. further prove in
[
KRRGR18
, Theorem 1.5]
that

\int_{\mathrm{U}(N)}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=m\\
0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\leq N\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{1}}(U)\cdots\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{k}}(U)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}U=\gamma_{k}(c)N^{k^{2}-1}+O_{k}(N^{k^{2}-2}),

where

(2)

\gamma_{k}(c)=\frac{1}{k!G(1+k)^{2}}\int_{[0,1]^{k}}\delta_{c}(w_{1}+\cdots+w_{k})\prod_{i<j}(w_{i}-w_{j})^{2}d^{k}w.

Here
\delta_{c}(w)=\delta(w-c)
is the delta distribution translated by
c
, and
G
is the Barnes
G
-function, defined for positive integers
k
as
G(1+k)=1!\cdot 2!\cdots(k-1)!
.

Keating at al. study various properties of
\gamma_{k}(c)
, proving in particular that it is a piecewise polynomial function of
c
. More specifically,
\gamma_{k}(c)
is a fixed polynomial for
r\leq c<r+1
(with
r
an integer), and each time the value of
c
passes through an integer, it becomes a different polynomial.

In the integer setting, let
Q\in\mathbb{Z}
be prime and define for
A\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};X;Q}(A)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv A\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n),

where
d_{k}(n)
is the number of ways of writing

n
as a product of
k
positive integers. The variance is given by

\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};X;Q}):=\frac{1}{\Phi(Q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)\\
\mathrm{gcd}(A,Q)=1\end{subarray}}\left|\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};X;Q}(A)-\left\langle\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};X;Q}\right\rangle\right|^{2}.

Based on their result over function fields and their study of the integral in (
1
), Keating et al. conjecture in
[
KRRGR18
, Conjecture 3.3]
that for
Q^{1+\varepsilon}<X<Q^{k-\varepsilon}
, as
X\rightarrow\infty
,

(3)

\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};X;Q})\sim\frac{X}{Q}a_{k}\gamma_{k}\left(\frac{\log X}{\log Q}\right)(\log Q)^{k^{2}-1},

where
a_{k}
is given by

a_{k}=\prod_{p}\left(\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{(k-1)^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{j}^{2}\frac{1}{p^{j}}\right)

and
\gamma_{k}
is given by (
2
). This conjecture is consistent with work of Lester
[
Les16
]
, and Bettin and Conrey
[
BC21
]
also showed that the Shifted moments conjecture for the Riemann zeta function implies that the main term of the variance of
\Delta_{k}(x,H)
has coefficient
a_{k}\gamma_{k}(c)
, as predicted by (
3
). Properties of
\gamma_{k}(c)
have been further studied, among other places, in
[
BGR18
]
.

In
[
KL22
]
, the authors considered the distribution of
d_{k}(f)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
when restricted to quadratic residues modulo an irreducible polynomial
P
. In other words, we defined

\mathcal{S}^{S}_{d_{k},n}(P):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\,\text{monic},\deg(f)=n\\
f\equiv\square\,(\mathrm{mod}\,P)\\
P\nmid f\end{subarray}}d_{k}(f),

where
P
is a monic irreducible polynomial of degree
2g+1
.
They prove in
[
KL22
, Theorem 1.1]
that for
n\leq 2gk
, as
q\rightarrow\infty
,

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\Big|\mathcal{S}^{S}_{d_{k},n}(P)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
P\nmid f\end{subarray}}d_{k}(f)\Big|^{2}\sim

\displaystyle\frac{q^{n}}{4}I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;g),

where

(5)

I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N):=\int_{\mathrm{Sp}(2N)}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=n\\
0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\leq 2N\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{1}}(U)\cdots\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{k}}(U)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}U.

This result, while analogous to formula (
1
), involves an integral over the set of unitary symplectic matrices, and crucially uses a monodromy argument of Katz. In order to interpret (
4
), one must understand the integral
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)
, which we study extensively in this paper.

As in the case of formula (
1
),
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)
is a piecewise polynomial function with transition points whenever
\frac{n}{N}
passes an integer. In order to better understand
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)
, we have the following result about its behavior when
n
is of the same order as
N
, which we obtain by further developing some results from Medjedovic’s MSc thesis
[
Med21
]
over symmetric functions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
c=\frac{a}{b}
be a fixed rational number and
k
be a fixed integer. If
2N
is a multiple of
b
, then
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c2N;N)
is a polynomial of degree
{2k^{2}+k-2}
in
N
.

This is done by expressing a generating function involving
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)
in terms of a sum of Schur functions over certain even partitions. This allows us to interpret
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)
as a function counting points inside a polytope, and combining this with Ehrhart theory.

Of particular interest is the leading coefficient of
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c2N;N)
, which is a function in
c
. Let
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)
denote the leading coefficient of
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c2N;N)
. By using complex analysis techniques to analyze
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n,N)
, we get a description of
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}
as a piecewise polynomial function of degree at most
2k^{2}+k-2
for any real number, made precise in the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

The function
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)
is given by

\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq b\leq c\\
0\leq a\leq 2c-b\end{subarray}}g_{a,b}^{S}(c),

and each
g_{a,b}^{S}(t)
is a polynomial of degree
2k^{2}+k-2
.

Remark 1.3
.

This result exhibits the transition points of the piecewise polynomial
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)
. In particular, whenever
c
passes an integer or a half-integer, the number of terms in the sum in Theorem
1.2
increases, since the number of pairs
(a,b)
with
0\leq b\leq c
and
0\leq a\leq 2c-b
increases. These are the transition points.

Both Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
indicate that the degree of
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}
is at most
2k^{2}+k-2
. We expect equality to hold, but our results do not eliminate the possibility that the degree could be smaller due to possible cancellation of the main coefficients of
g_{a,b}^{S}(c)
. The situation is nearly identical to that of the unitary case, where in
[
KRRGR18
]
, Theorem 1.6 shows that
\gamma_{k}(c)
is a sum of polynomials of degree
k^{2}-1
whose main coefficients may cancel. It would be interesting to investigate this question further.

Conjecture 1.4
.

The degree of
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)
is
2k^{2}+k-2
.

In the second half of this paper, we consider quantities related to orthogonal matrix integrals. We will discuss number-theoretic problems which lead us to consider the integral

I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N):=\int_{\mathrm{O}(2N+1)}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=n\\
0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\leq 2N+1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{1}}(U)\cdots\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{k}}(U)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}U,

as well as the main coefficient
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}
of the highest power of
N
.

We proceed to further study
I_{d_{k},2}^{O}
and
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}
and prove results analogous to our results in the symplectic case.
More precisely, we prove the following results.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
c=\frac{a}{b}
be a fixed rational number and
k
be a fixed integer. If
2N+1
is a multiple of
b
, then
I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(c(2N+1);N)
is a polynomial of degree
{2k^{2}-k-2}
in
N
.

Note that the proof of Theorem
1.5
, presented in Section
7
, also applies when to the integral analogous to
I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N)
but defined over the even-dimensional group
\mathrm{O}(2N)
.

Theorem 1.6
.

The constant
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}(c)
is given by

\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}(c)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq b\leq c\\
0\leq a\leq 2c-b\end{subarray}}g_{a,b}^{O}(c),

and each
g_{a,b}^{O}(t)
is a polynomial of degree
2k^{2}-k-2
.

Remark 1.7
.

Just as in the symplectic case, this result exhibits the transition points of the piecewise polynomial
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}(c)
whenever
c
passes an integer or a half-integer.

Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
imply that

\deg\left(\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}\right)\leq 2k^{2}-k-2,

and suggest that equality should hold.

Conjecture 1.8
.

The degree of
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}(c)
is
2k^{2}-k-2
.

Based on our understanding of
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}
, we formulate conjectures concerning questions on the distribution of
d_{k}
over number fields that are analogous to the Gaussian integer setting explored over the function fields.

To begin with we have the following conjecture, which is analogous to the conjecture given by (
3
).

Conjecture 1.9
.

Let
p
be a prime and define

\mathcal{S}^{S}_{d_{k};x}(p)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\equiv\square\,(\mathrm{mod}\,p)\\
p\nmid n\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n).

Consider the variance defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{p\in[y,2y]}\left(\mathcal{S}^{S}_{d_{k};x}\right)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{y}\sum_{y<p\leq 2y}\log p\Big(\mathcal{S}^{S}_{d_{k};x}(p)-\left\langle\mathcal{S}^{S}_{d_{k};x}\right\rangle_{p}\Big)^{2},

where
\langle\cdot\rangle_{p}
denotes a refined average given by

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{S}^{S}_{d_{k};x}\right\rangle_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p\nmid n\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n).

Then, for
x^{1/k+\varepsilon}\leq y
, we have,

(6)

\mathrm{Var}_{p\in[y,2y]}\left(\mathcal{S}^{S}_{d_{k};x}\right)\sim a_{k}^{S}(\mathcal{S})\frac{x}{4}\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}\left(\frac{\log x}{\log y}\right)(\log y)^{2k^{2}+k-2},

where
a_{k}^{S}(\mathcal{S})
is a certain arithmetic constant dependent only on
k
and
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)
is a piecewise polynomial of degree
2k^{2}+k-2
given by (
3.5
).

Remark 1.10
.

The statement of this conjecture differs slightly from the published version in the definition the variance, which has now been defined relative to the more refined average value
\langle\mathcal{S}^{S}_{d_{k};x}\rangle_{p}
, which is dependent on the prime
p
.

For
p\nmid n
, the constraint that
n\equiv\square\bmod p
is equivalent to multiplying by
\frac{1+\chi_{p}(n)}{2}
in the sum. One could alternatively consider the sum without the condition

p\nmid n
, namely, where the
n
that are divisible by
p
are counted with weight
\frac{1}{2}
. One could also study the related problem of the variance of
\sum_{n\leq x}d_{k}(n)\chi_{p}(n)
when ranging over primes
p
. This problem is perhaps more direct from the perspective of the
L
-functions involved, but is less immediately accessible as a number theoretic question. For this reason and for consistency with
[
KL22
]
, we have stated Conjecture
1.9
for a restricted sum over quadratic residues mod
p
.

Constants such as
a_{k}^{S}(\mathcal{S})
can be derived by following the recipe of
[
CFK+05
]
or by considering
y
large respect to
x
, in which case the main term is expected to come from the diagonal contribution, and hence arises from a residue computation. However, Conjecture
1.9
and the constant
a_{k}^{S}(\mathcal{S})
are somewhat more subtle because of the constraint that the variance is running only over primes, rather than a wider average. We can also make an analogous conjecture for the variance of the divisor function weighted by quadratic characters where the variance is taken over all positive fundamental discriminants, and not just over primes. In this setting, we conjecture the following.

Conjecture 1.11
.

For a positive fundamental discriminant
m
, define

\mathcal{T}^{S}_{d_{k};x}(m)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(m,n)=1\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)\chi_{m}(n),

\chi_{m}

.
Let

\mathcal{T}^{S}_{d_{k};x}(m)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{m\in[y,2y]}\left(\mathcal{T}^{S}_{d_{k};x}\right)

\mathbb{E}^{*}

Then, for

(7)

\mathrm{Var}_{y<m\leq 2y}\left(\mathcal{T}^{S}_{d_{k};x}\right)\sim a_{k}^{S}(\mathcal{T})x\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}\left(\frac{\log x}{\log y}\right)(\log y)^{2k^{2}+k-2},

where

is an arithmetic constant given by

a_{k}^{S}(\mathcal{T})=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{k(2k+1)}\left(\frac{1}{p+1}\left(1+\frac{p}{2}\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right)^{-2k}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right)^{-2k}\right)\right)\right),

and
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)
is a piecewise polynomial of degree

).

The constant

can be derived from the recipe of
[
CFK+05
]

It should be related to the constant

A crucial piece of information for writing formula (

In
[
KL22
]
, the authors made use of a model of the Gaussian integers in the function field context which was considered by Rudnick and Waxman in
[

]
and initially developed by Bary-Soroker, Smilansky, and Wolf in

\mathcal{N}^{S}_{d_{\ell},k,n}(u)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\,\text{monic},\deg(f)=n\\

37
)) that can be interpreted as the function field analogue of having the argument of a complex number lying in certain specific sector of the unit circle.

The authors proved in
[
KL22
, Theorem 1.2]
that for
n\leq\ell(2\kappa-2)
with

\langle\mathcal{N}^{S}_{d_{\ell},k,n}\rangle\sim q^{n-\kappa}\binom{\ell+n-1}{\ell-1},

and

(8)

\frac{1}{q^{\kappa}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{k}^{1}}\left|\mathcal{N}^{S}_{d_{\ell},k,n}(u)-\langle\mathcal{N}^{S}_{d_{\ell},k,n}\rangle\right|^{2}\sim\frac{q^{n}}{q^{\kappa}}\int_{\mathrm{Sp}(2\kappa-2)}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{\ell}=n\\
0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{\ell}\leq 2\kappa-2\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{1}}(U)\cdots\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{\ell}}(U)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}U.

We can also use our understanding of
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)
to formulate a conjecture for an analogous problem in the integers.
Following Rudnick and Waxman
[
RW19
]
, for an ideal
\mathfrak{a}=(\alpha)
of
\mathbb{Z}[i]
we associate a direction vector

in the unit circle and write

. Since the generator of the ideal is defined up to multiplication by a unit
\{\pm 1,\pm i\}
, the direction vector is well defined on ideals, while the angle
\theta_{\mathfrak{a}}
is well-defined modulo
\pi/2
.

For a given
\theta

I_{K}(\theta)=[\theta-\frac{\pi}{4K},\theta+\frac{\pi}{4K}]

\mathfrak{a}
such that

\mathcal{N}^{S}_{d_{\ell},K;x}(\theta)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}\,\text{ideal}\\

Its average is given by

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{N}^{S}_{d_{\ell},K;x}\right\rangle:=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}\,\text{ideal}\\

\theta_{\mathfrak{a}}\in I_{K}(\theta)\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})d\theta=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}\,\text{ideal}\\

N(\mathfrak{a})\leq x\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a}),

and its variance is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left(\mathcal{N}^{S}_{d_{\ell},K;x}\right):=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}\,\text{ideal}\\
N(\mathfrak{a})\leq x\\
\theta_{\mathfrak{a}}\in I_{K}(\theta)\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})-\left\langle\mathcal{N}^{S}_{d_{\ell},K;x}\right\rangle\big)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta.

We get the following conjecture by replacing

by

and

by

.

Let

Finally, we consider a number-theoretic problem, in both the function field and integer setting, whose behavior is connected to an orthogonal matrix integral. Let

\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},k,n}^{O}(u):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
f(0)\neq 0\\
U(f)\in\mathrm{Sect}(u,k)\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(f)\left(\frac{1+\chi_{2}(f)}{2}\right),

where
\chi_{2}
is the quadratic character over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
defined by
\chi_{2}(f):=\chi_{2}(f(0))
, where for
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
\chi_{2}(x):=1
if
x
is a non-zero square,
-1
if
x
is a non square, and
0
if
x=0
. As we show in Lemma
6.4
, the average value of
\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},k,n}^{O}(u)
is

\langle\mathcal{N}^{O}_{d_{\ell},k,n}\rangle:=\frac{1}{q^{\kappa}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{k}^{1}}\mathcal{N}^{O}_{d_{\ell},k,n}(u)=\frac{q^{n-\kappa}}{2}\binom{\ell+n-1}{\ell-1}+O(q^{n-\kappa-1}).

We estimate the variance of
\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},k,n}^{O}(u)
in the following result.

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
n\leq\ell(2\kappa-1)
with
\kappa=\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor\geq 3
. As
q\rightarrow\infty
,

\frac{1}{q^{\kappa}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{S}_{k}^{1}}\left|\mathcal{N}^{O}_{d_{\ell},k,n}(u)-\langle\mathcal{N}^{O}_{d_{\ell},k,n}(u)\rangle_{S}\right|^{2}\sim\frac{q^{n}}{4q^{\kappa}}\int_{\mathrm{O}(2\kappa-1)}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{\ell}=n\\
0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{\ell}\leq 2\kappa-1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{1}}(U)\cdots\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{\ell}}(U)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}U,

where
\langle\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},k,n}^{O}(u)\rangle_{S}
is a refined average given by

\langle\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},k,n}^{O}(u)\rangle_{S}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
f(0)\neq 0\\
U(f)\in\mathrm{Sect}(u,k)\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(f).

Both Theorem
1.13
as well as the result (
8
) from
[
KL22
, Theorem 1.2]
rely on monodromy theorems due to Katz
[
Kat17
]
. Surprisingly, straightforward rational analogs of
\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},k,n}^{O}(u)
are related to families of
L
-functions that do not have orthogonal symmetry, so Theorem
1.13
does not naturally lead to a corresponding conjecture for its rational analog. However, we expect the orthogonal integral
I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(c(2N+1);N)
and the quantity
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}(c)
to appear in other problems involving orthogonal families of rational
L
-functions; see Section
10
for more discussion.

More generally, for any family
\mathcal{F}
of rational
L
-functions with symmetry type
G
, we expect

\mathbb{E}_{f\in\mathcal{F},\mathrm{conductor}(f)\leq y}\left|\sum_{m\leq x}d_{k}(m)f(m)\right|^{2}

to be asymptotically proportional to

I_{d_{k},2}^{G}:=\int_{G}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{1}}(U)\cdots\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{k}}(U)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}U,

where the sum in the integrand is taken over
j_{1},\dots,j_{k}
with
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}
constant. The proportionality constant may be extracted by considering
y
sufficiently large with respect to
x
, as then only the diagonal contribution to
\mathbb{E}_{f\in\mathcal{F},\mathrm{conductor}(f)\leq y}\left|\sum_{m\leq x}d_{k}(m)f(m)\right|^{2}
is relevant.

We also remark that in the symplectic and orthogonal cases one could also consider

\mathbb{E}_{f\in\mathcal{F},\mathrm{conductor}(f)\leq y}\sum_{m\leq x}d_{k}(m)f(m)

without the absolute value. This is analogous to the study of moments of
L
-functions versus the Riemann zeta function. In the Riemann zeta function case, which has unitary symmetry, one considers the
2k
th moments of the absolute value, since the
k
th moments without the absolute value are highly oscillatory. However, in the
L
-functions cases with symplectic or ortogonal symmetry, it is possible to study the
k
th moments without taking absolute values. We keep the absolute values in this article to better compare with the original number theoretic questions.

While the methods in this article are quite close to the ones employed in
[
KRRGR18
]
, they are technically more involved. One central difference is that in the context of random matrix theory, it is much less natural to consider integrals of squares of functions in the symplectic and orthogonal cases than it is in the unitary case, where the eigenvalues are complex and the square of the absolute value is essentially unavoidable. However, the problems that we consider are the variances of various arithmetic functions, so the squares in the integral are inherent to our setting. The presence of the square brings considerable difficulty in the study of
\gamma_{d_{k},2}
both in the symplectic and the orthogonal cases. The symmetric function theory in the unitary case considered in
[
KRRGR18
]
requires the consideration of semi-standard Young tableaux arising from a single rectangular Ferrer diagram, while here we must consider a sum including more general shapes. The orthogonal case is particularly more involved, and it requires developing machinery for
\mathrm{O}(2N+1)
which we have not found in the literature. Also, at the level of the complex integrals we find considerable challenges, again coming from the square introduced in the symplectic and orthogonal cases.

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
we develop some results about determinants that will be useful for computations with symmetric functions. In Sections
3
and
7
we express generating functions involving
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)
and
I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N)
, respectively, in terms of sums of Schur functions and combine this with Ehrhart theory to deduce the degrees of
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}
and
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}
and prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.5
. We combine the results of those sections in Sections
4
and
8
to obtain some specific formulas for
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}
and
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}
. We treat the generating functions of
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)
and
I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N)
as complex integrals in Sections
5
and
9
, providing further insight on the shapes of
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}
and
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}
and proving Theorems
1.2
and
1.6
. Section
6
includes the function field setting for the Gaussian integers, as well as the proof of Theorem
1.13
. Finally, in Section
10
we discuss the motivation for Conjectures
1.9
and Conjecture
1.12
.

Some results on determinants

This section covers some results on determinants that will be needed later.

Let
p_{j}(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x]
for
j=1,\dots,2k
and consider the
2k
-variable polynomial given by

P(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k})=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq 2k}[p_{j}(x_{i})].

This is an alternating polynomial, and thus divisible by the Vandermonde determinant
\Delta(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k}):=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq 2k}(x_{i}-x_{j})
. We are interested in finding
\frac{P(x,\dots,x,y,\dots,y)}{\Delta(x,\dots,x,y,\dots,y)}
, or more precisely, the limit of
\frac{P(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k})}{\Delta(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k})}
as
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\rightarrow x
and
x_{k+1},\dots,x_{2k}\rightarrow y
.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have

(9)

\left.\frac{P(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k})}{\Delta(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k})}\right|_{(x,\dots,x,y,\dots,y)}=\frac{1}{(y-x)^{k^{2}}}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq k\\
1\leq j\leq 2k\end{subarray}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{1}{(i_{1}-1)!}\frac{\partial^{i_{1}-1}}{\partial x^{i_{1}-1}}p_{j}(x)\\
\\
\frac{1}{(i_{2}-1)!}\frac{\partial^{i_{2}-1}}{\partial y^{i_{2}-1}}p_{j}(y)\end{array}\right],

where the vector
(x,\dots,x,y,\dots,y)
in (
9
) has
k
coordinates equal to
x
and
k
coordinates equal to
y
.

The proof of this lemma follows from the same ideas as
[
Med21
, Lemma 1]
, which considers the case of a
k\times k
matrix and
\frac{P(x,\dots,x)}{\Delta(x,\dots,x)}
.

Proof.

First consider the case

(10)

P(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k})=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq 2k}[x_{i}^{a_{j}}]=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{2k}}\sgn(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{2k}x_{i}^{a_{\sigma(i)}},

where the
a_{j}
are non-negative integers. By applying L’Hôpital’s rule, the limit that we seek to compute becomes

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i}\rightarrow x,\,1\leq i\leq k\\
x_{j}\rightarrow y,\,k+1\leq j\leq 2k\end{subarray}}\frac{P(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k})}{\Delta(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k})}=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{(1!\cdots(k-1)!)^{2}}\frac{1}{\prod_{1\leq i\leq k}\prod_{k+1\leq j\leq 2k}(x_{i}-x_{j})}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\times(-1)^{\frac{(k-1)k}{2}}\frac{\partial^{k-1}}{\partial x_{2k}^{k-1}}\cdots\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k+2}}\frac{\partial^{k-1}}{\partial x_{k}^{k-1}}\cdots\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}P(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k})\right]\right|_{(x,\dots,x,y,\dots,y)}.

Introducing the expansion (
10
) in the above identity yields

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i}\rightarrow x,\,1\leq i\leq k\\
x_{j}\rightarrow y,\,k+1\leq j\leq 2k\end{subarray}}\frac{P(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k})}{\Delta(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k})}=\left[\frac{1}{(1!\cdots(k-1)!)^{2}}\frac{1}{\prod_{1\leq i\leq k}\prod_{k+1\leq j\leq 2k}(x_{i}-x_{j})}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\times\frac{\partial^{k-1}}{\partial x_{2k}^{k-1}}\cdots\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k+2}}\frac{\partial^{k-1}}{\partial x_{k}^{k-1}}\cdots\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{2k}}\sgn(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{2k}x_{i}^{a_{\sigma(i)}}\right]\right|_{(x,\dots,x,y,\dots,y)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.\frac{1}{(y-x)^{k^{2}}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{2k}}\sgn(\sigma)\prod_{i_{1}=1}^{k}\binom{a_{\sigma(i_{1})}}{i_{1}-1}x_{i_{1}}^{a_{\sigma(i_{1})}-i_{1}+1}\prod_{i_{2}=k+1}^{2k}\binom{a_{\sigma(i_{2})}}{i_{2}-k-1}x_{i_{2}}^{a_{\sigma(i_{2})}-i_{2}+k+1}\right|_{(x,\dots,x,y,\dots,y)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(y-x)^{k^{2}}}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq k\\
1\leq j\leq 2k\end{subarray}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{1}{(i_{1}-1)!}\frac{\partial^{i_{1}-1}}{\partial x^{i_{1}-1}}x^{j}\\
\\
\frac{1}{(i_{2}-1)!}\frac{\partial^{i_{2}-1}}{\partial y^{i_{2}-1}}y^{j}\end{array}\right],

as desired. For the general case, apply multi-linearity of the determinant to split it into a sum of determinants involving monomials, and apply the above result to each individual term. The proof is completed by adding the terms together.

∎

The right-hand side of (
9
) is a polynomial. It can be computed from a particular case of the generalized Vandermonde matrix in
[
Kal84
]
, which we state in Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any positive integer
k
,

\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1},i_{2},\leq k\\
1\leq j\leq 2k\end{subarray}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{(j-1)!}{(j-i_{1})!}x^{j-i_{1}}\\
\\
\frac{(j-1)!}{(j-i_{2})!}y^{j-i_{2}}\end{array}\right]=G(1+k)(y-x)^{k^{2}},

where
G(1+k)=0!\cdot 1!\cdots(k-1)!
is the Barnes
G
-function.

Subsequently we will need the following generalization of Lemma
2.2
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{2k}
be non-negative integers and let

M=\begin{bmatrix}x^{\alpha_{1}}&x^{\alpha_{2}}&\cdots&x^{\alpha_{2k}}\\
\alpha_{1}x^{\alpha_{1}-1}&\alpha_{2}x^{\alpha_{2}-1}&\cdots&\alpha_{2k}x^{\alpha_{2k}-1}\\
\alpha_{1}(\alpha_{1}-1)x^{\alpha_{1}-2}&\alpha_{2}(\alpha_{2}-1)x^{\alpha_{2}-2}&\cdots&\alpha_{2k}(\alpha_{2k}-1)x^{\alpha_{2k}-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{1}\cdots(\alpha_{1}-k+2)x^{\alpha_{1}-k+1}&\alpha_{2}\cdots(\alpha_{2}-k+2)x^{\alpha_{2}-k+1}&\cdots&\alpha_{2k}\cdots(\alpha_{2k}-k+2)x^{\alpha_{2k}-k+1}\\
y^{\alpha_{1}}&y^{\alpha_{2}}&\cdots&y^{\alpha_{2k}}\\
\alpha_{1}y^{\alpha_{1}-1}&\alpha_{2}y^{\alpha_{2}-1}&\cdots&\alpha_{2k}y^{\alpha_{2k}-1}\\
\alpha_{1}(\alpha_{1}-1)y^{\alpha_{1}-2}&\alpha_{2}(\alpha_{2}-1)y^{\alpha_{2}-2}&\cdots&\alpha_{2k}(\alpha_{2k}-1)y^{\alpha_{2k}-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{1}\cdots(\alpha_{1}-k+2)y^{\alpha_{1}-k+1}&\alpha_{2}\cdots(\alpha_{2}-k+2)y^{\alpha_{2}-k+1}&\cdots&\alpha_{2k}\cdots(\alpha_{2k}-k+2)y^{\alpha_{2k}-k+1}.\end{bmatrix}

Then
(y-x)^{k^{2}}
divides
\det M
.

Proof.

The first
k
rows consist of the successive derivatives of the first row; then the last
k
rows follow the same structure, but with
y
’s instead of
x
’s.

Every term in
\det M
has degree
\sum_{j=1}^{2k}\alpha_{j}-(k-1)k
or is identically zero, so
\det M
must be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\sum_{j=1}^{2k}\alpha_{j}-(k-1)k
(or identically zero).

Subtracting the
k+1
st row from the first row does not change the determinant, and the difference is a row of terms of the form
x^{\alpha_{j}}-y^{\alpha_{j}}
. Each entry of this new row is divisible by
y-x
, so
y-x
must divide the determinant. Moreover, the same argument applies for any matrix with two rows which have the same polynomial entries, one in the variable
x
and the other in the variable
y
; any such matrix has determinant divisible by
y-x
. We will proceed by showing that
(y-x)
also divides each of the first
k^{2}-1
derivatives (in
x
) of
\det M
.

For a
k
-tuple
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}
, let
M_{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}}
denote the matrix
M
where the
i
th row has been differentiated
\beta_{i}
times in
x
. So,
M_{0,\dots,0}=M
. If for any
i
, we have
\beta_{i}\leq k-i
, then the
i
th row of
M_{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}}
is the
i-1+\beta_{i}
’th derivative of
\begin{bmatrix}x^{\alpha_{1}}&x^{\alpha_{2}}&\cdots&x^{\alpha_{2k}}\end{bmatrix}
and thus identical to the
k+i-1+\beta_{i}
th row, but with
x
’s instead of
y
’s. By the argument above, if
\beta_{i}\leq k-i
for any
i
, then
(y-x)
divides
\det M_{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}}
.
Also, if for any
1\leq i\leq k-1
we have
\beta_{i}=\beta_{i+1}+1
, then the
i
th row and the
i+1
st row will be identical, so that
\det M_{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}}=0
. By extension, if for any
1\leq i,j\leq k-1
we have
\beta_{i}=\beta_{i+j}+j
, then
\det M_{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}}=0
.

In general for an
\ell\times\ell
matrix
F(x)
of functions of
x
whose rows are
r_{1}(x),\dots,r_{\ell}(x)
, the derivative of
\det F(x)
is given by

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\det F(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\det\begin{bmatrix}r_{1}(x)\\
\vdots\\
r_{i-1}(x)\\
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}r_{i}(x)\\
r_{i+1}(x)\\
\vdots\\
r_{\ell}(x)\end{bmatrix}.

In our case, the last
k
rows are constant in
x
and they do not have to be considered. So the derivative of
\det M_{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}}
is

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\det M_{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}}=\det M_{\beta_{1}+1,\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k}}+\det M_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}+1,\dots,\beta_{k}}+\cdots+\det M_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k}+1}.

Beginning with
\det M=\det M_{0,\dots,0}
, its first derivative is
\det M_{0,\dots,0,1}
, which is divisible by
(y-x)
; all other terms are
0
. The second derivative is then
\det M_{0,\dots,1,1}+\det M_{0,\dots,0,2}
, which for
k\geq 2
is still divisible by
(y-x)
. Inductively, the
j
th derivative of
\det M_{0,\dots,0}
is

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{increasing paths $P$}\\
\text{of length $j$}\end{subarray}}\det M_{P(j)},

where the sum is over all paths
P
beginning at
(0,\dots,0)\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, of length
j
, where each step has nondecreasing entries. If
\beta_{1}\leq k-1
, then
(y-x)
divides
\det M_{P(j)}
. Otherwise,
\beta_{1}\geq k
, and either
\det M_{P(j)}=0
or
\beta_{i}\geq k
, leading to
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{k}\geq k^{2}
, which is a contradiction if we are taking the
j
th derivative with

j\leq k^{2}-1
. Thus the first
k^{2}-1
derivatives are all divisible by
(y-x)
, so
\det M
is a multiple of
(y-x)^{k^{2}}
, as desired.
∎

(i,j)\in U^{S}
,

(2)

for
u_{1}^{(k)}:=c-(u_{2}^{(k)}+\cdots+u_{k}^{(k)})
, we have
0\leq u_{1}^{(k)}\leq 1
,

(3)

for
u_{1}^{(2k)}:=2c-(u_{2}^{(2k)}+\cdots+u_{2k}^{(2k)})
, we have
0\leq u_{1}^{(2k)}\leq 1
,

(4)

u_{r+1}^{(s+1)}\leq u_{r}^{(s)}
, and

(5)

u_{r}^{(s)}\leq u_{r}^{(s+1)}
.

Finally, let
A_{k}^{S}
be the set defined by the last two conditions above.

The region
V^{S}_{c}
is convex because it is the intersection of half-planes. Note that for
c\in[0,k]
,
V^{S}_{c}
is contained in
[0,1]^{2k^{2}+k-2}
and therefore contained in a closed ball of diameter
\sqrt{2k^{2}+k-2}
. Let
\mathbb{Z}^{2k^{2}-k-1,2k-1}_{\text{e}}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2k^{2}-k-1}\times(2\mathbb{Z})^{2k-1}
, where the even coordinates correspond to the elements
(i,2k)\in U^{S}
for
i=2,\dots,2k
. Then

(13)

I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)=\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{2k^{2}-k-1,2k-1}_{\text{e}}\cap(2N\cdot V^{S}_{c})\right),

where
2N\cdot V^{S}_{c}=\{2Nx:x\in V_{c}\}
is the dilate of
V^{S}_{c}
by a factor of
2N
.

We now use the well-known principle that the count of lattice points in a region can be approximated by the volume of the region. Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
be a lattice. The shape of
\Lambda
is roughly described by the successive minima
\mu_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{\ell}
of
\Lambda
.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
Sch95
, Lemma 1]

If
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
is a convex region contained in a closed ball of radius
\rho
, and if
\mu_{\ell-1}\leq\rho
, then

\#(\Lambda\cap S)=\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{\ell}(S)}{\det\Lambda}+O\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell}\rho^{\ell-1}}{\det\Lambda}\right).

Applying this to
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{2k^{2}-k-1,2k-1}_{\text{e}}
, we get

I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)=\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{2k^{2}+k-2}(2N\cdot V^{S}_{c})}{2^{2k-1}}+O_{k}\left(N^{2k^{2}+k-3}\right).

The volume of the region is given by

(14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{2k^{2}+k-2}(2N\cdot V^{S}_{c})

\displaystyle=(2N)^{2k^{2}+k-2}\int_{[0,1]^{2k^{2}+k}}\delta_{c}(u_{1}^{(k)}+\cdots+u_{k}^{(k)})\delta_{2c}(u_{1}^{(2k)}+\cdots+u_{2k}^{(2k)})\mathds{1}_{A_{k}^{S}}(u)d^{2k^{2}+k}u.

To confirm that
I^{S}_{d_{k},2}(n;N)
is a polynomial, we rely on Ehrhart theory
[
Ehr67
]
(see also
[
Bre15
]
for details).

Theorem 3.2
.

[
Ehr67
]
Let
E
be a convex lattice polytope in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
(that is, a polytope whose vertices have all integral coordinates). Then there is a polynomial
P
of degree
\ell
such that for all positive integers
m
,

\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}\cap(m\cdot E)\right)=P(m).

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
c=\frac{a}{b}
be a fixed rational number and
k
be a fixed integer. If
2N
is a multiple of
b
, then
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c2N;N)=P_{c,k}(N)
, where
P_{c,k}
is a polynomial of degree
{2k^{2}+k-2}
.

Proof.

Writing
2N=bm
and applying (
13
),

I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c2N;N)=\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{2k^{2}-k-1,2k-1}_{\text{e}}\cap(m\cdot\left[b\cdot V^{S}_{c}\right])\right).

Since
b\cdot V^{S}_{c}
is a convex lattice polytope in
\mathbb{R}^{(2k^{2}-k-1)+(2k-1)}
, Theorem
3.2
implies that
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c2N;N)
is a polynomial of degree
{2k^{2}+k-2}
in
m
, and therefore in
N
.
∎

For what follows we will need the following result of
[
KRRGR18
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

[
KRRGR18
, Lemma 4.6]

For
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
satisfying
\beta_{1}\leq\beta_{2}\leq\cdots\beta_{k+1}
, define

I(\beta)=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\,:\,\beta_{1}\leq\alpha_{1}\leq\beta_{2}\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\cdots\alpha_{k}\leq\beta_{k+1}\}.

Then

\int_{\alpha\in I(\beta)}\Delta(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k-1},\dots,\alpha_{1})d^{k}\alpha=\frac{1}{k!}\Delta(\beta_{k+1},\dots,\beta_{1}),

where
\Delta
denotes the Vandermonde determinant.

Proposition 3.5
.

The coefficient of
(2N)^{2k^{2}+k-2}
in the asymptotics for
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c(2N),N)
is given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{-2k+1}}{G(1+k)}\int_{[0,1]^{\frac{3}{2}k^{2}+\frac{3}{2}k}}\delta_{c}(u_{1}^{(k)}+\cdots+u_{k}^{(k)})\delta_{2c}(u_{1}^{(2k)}+\cdots+u_{2k}^{(2k)})

(15)

\displaystyle\times\mathds{1}\begin{bmatrix}&&u_{1}^{(k)}&\leq&\dots&\leq&u_{1}^{(2k)}\\
&\iddots&&&&\iddots&\\
u_{k}^{(k)}&\leq&\dots&\leq&u_{k}^{(2k)}&\\
\vdots&&\iddots&&&&\\
u_{2k}^{(2k)}&&&&&&\end{bmatrix}\Delta(u_{1}^{(k)},u_{2}^{(k)},\dots,u_{k}^{(k)})d^{\frac{3}{2}k^{2}+\frac{3}{2}k}u,

where
\mathds{1}_{X}
denotes the characteristic function of the set
X
.

Proof.

From the volume formula (
14
), we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)=

\displaystyle 2^{-2k+1}\int_{[0,1]^{2k^{2}+k}}\delta_{c}(u_{1}^{(k)}+\cdots+u_{k}^{(k)})\delta_{2c}(u_{1}^{(2k)}+\cdots+u_{2k}^{(2k)})

(16)

\displaystyle\times\mathds{1}\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}^{(1)}&\leq&u_{1}^{(2)}&\leq&\dots&\leq&u_{1}^{(2k-1)}&\leq&u_{1}^{(2k)}\\
\rotatebox{90.0}{$\,\leq$}&&\rotatebox{90.0}{$\,\leq$}&&&&\rotatebox{90.0}{$\,\leq$}&&\\
u_{2}^{(2)}&\leq&u_{2}^{(3)}&\leq&\dots&\leq&u_{2}^{(2k)}&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\iddots&&&&\\
u_{2k}^{(2k)}&&&&&&&&\end{bmatrix}d^{2k^{2}+k}u.

Integrating with respect to
u_{1}^{(1)}
in (
3
) and applying Lemma
3.4
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)=

\displaystyle 2^{-2k+1}\int_{[0,1]^{2k^{2}+k-1}}\delta_{c}(u_{1}^{(k)}+\cdots+u_{k}^{(k)})\delta_{2c}(u_{1}^{(2k)}+\cdots+u_{2k}^{(2k)})

\displaystyle\times\mathds{1}\begin{bmatrix}&&u_{1}^{(2)}&\leq&\dots&\leq&u_{1}^{(2k-1)}&\leq&u_{1}^{(2k)}\\
&&\rotatebox{90.0}{$\,\leq$}&&&&\rotatebox{90.0}{$\,\leq$}&&\\
u_{2}^{(2)}&\leq&u_{2}^{(3)}&\leq&\dots&\leq&u_{2}^{(2k)}&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\iddots&&&&\\
u_{2k}^{(2k)}&&&&&&&&\end{bmatrix}\frac{\Delta(u_{1}^{(2)},u_{2}^{(2)})}{1!}d^{2k^{2}+k-1}u.

We proceed inductively and get

\displaystyle\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)=

\displaystyle 2^{-2k+1}\int_{[0,1]^{\frac{3}{2}k^{2}+\frac{3}{2}k}}\delta_{c}(u_{1}^{(k)}+\cdots+u_{k}^{(k)})\delta_{2c}(u_{1}^{(2k)}+\cdots+u_{2k}^{(2k)})

\vdots&&\iddots&&&&\\
u_{2k}^{(2k)}&&&&&&\end{bmatrix}\frac{\Delta(u_{1}^{(k)},u_{2}^{(k)},\dots,u_{k}^{(k)})}{1!2!\cdots(k-1)!}d^{\frac{3}{2}k^{2}+\frac{3}{2}k}u.

∎

4.
The determinant expression in the symplectic case

Consider in full generality

I_{d_{k},\ell}^{S}(n;N):=\int_{\mathrm{Sp}(2N)}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=n\\
0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\leq 2N\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{1}}(U)\cdots\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{k}}(U)\Big|^{\ell}\mathrm{d}U,

and write

I_{d_{k},\ell}^{S}(n;N)\sim\gamma_{d_{k},\ell}^{S}(c)(2N)^{m},

where
m
is the appropriate exponent of
N
such that the above asymptotic formula exists.

The case of
I_{d_{k},1}^{S}(n;N)
and
\gamma_{d_{k},1}^{S}(c)
was studied in detail by Medjedovic
[
Med21
]
. However, for the purposes of understanding the asymptotics given in (
4
), we need to consider
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)
and
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)
instead. The goal of this section is to further develop the results of Medjedovic to adapt them to our case. Following
[
Med21
]
, the Désarménien–Stembridge–Proctor formula
[
BK72
,
BW16
,
Pro90
]
gives

(17)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda_{1}\leq 2N\\
\lambda\,\text{even}\end{subarray}}s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})=\frac{1}{\Delta(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}^{2}}\prod_{i<j}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}x_{j}}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq r}\left[x_{i}^{2N+2r+1-j}-x_{i}^{j-1}\right].

(See also equation (53) in
[
BG06
]
.)

Combining equations (
11
), (
12
), and (
17
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{2Nk}x^{m}y^{n}J_{d_{k},2}^{S}(m,n;N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x^{2})^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(1-y^{2})^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(1-xy)^{k^{2}}}\left.\frac{\det_{1\leq i,j\leq 2k}\left[x_{i}^{2N+4k+1-j}-x_{i}^{j-1}\right]}{\Delta(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k})}\right|_{(x,\dots,x,y,\dots,y)}.

Applying Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{2Nk}x^{m}y^{n}J_{d_{k},2}^{S}(m,n;N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x^{2})^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(1-y^{2})^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(1-xy)^{k^{2}}(y-x)^{k^{2}}}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq k\\
1\leq j\leq 2k\end{subarray}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\binom{2N+4k+1-j}{i_{1}-1}x^{2N+4k+2-j-i_{1}}-\binom{j-1}{i_{1}-1}x^{j-i_{1}}\\
\\
\binom{2N+4k+1-j}{i_{2}-1}y^{2N+4k+2-j-i_{2}}-\binom{j-1}{i_{2}-1}y^{j-i_{2}}\end{array}\right].

It is not clear a priori that the right-hand side of (
4
) leads to a polynomial or even a Taylor series, due to the term
(y-x)^{k^{2}}
in the denominator, but this follows from Proposition
2.3
.

There is a symmetry for the variable
n
in
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)
as illustrated next.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
0\leq n\leq 2Nk
the following functional equation holds:

I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)=I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(2Nk-n;N).

Proof.

The functional equation of the characteristic polynomial of a symplectic matrix is given by

\det(I+Ux)=x^{2N}\det(I+Ux^{-1}).

Then, from equation (
12
) we have

\sum_{m,n=0}^{2Nk}x^{m}y^{n}J_{d_{k},2}^{S}(m,n;N)=x^{2Nk}y^{2Nk}\sum_{m,n=0}^{2Nk}x^{-m}y^{-n}J_{d_{k},2}^{S}(m,n;N).

By making the changes of variables
m\rightarrow 2Nk-m
,
n\rightarrow 2Nk-n
, we immediately obtain

J_{d_{k},2}^{S}(m,n;N)=J_{d_{k},2}^{S}(2Nk-m,2Nk-n;N),

and we deduce the result from setting
m=n
.
∎

Now we focus on computing
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)
for the case
n\leq N+\frac{1+k}{2}
.

Before stating the result we make the following definition. A
quasi-polynomial

P(m)
of period
r
is a function on integer numbers for which there exist polynomials
P_{0}(m),\dots,P_{r-1}(m)
such that

P(m)=\begin{cases}P_{0}(m)&m\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,r),\\
P_{1}(m)&m\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,r),\\
\vdots&\\
P_{r-1}(m)&m\equiv r-1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,r).\end{cases}

Proposition 4.2
.

For
n\leq N+\frac{1+k}{2}
, we have

I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)=\frac{1}{G(1+k)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=0\\
\ell\equiv n\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}^{n}\binom{\frac{n-\ell}{2}+\binom{k+1}{2}-1}{\binom{k+1}{2}-1}^{2}\binom{\ell+k^{2}-1}{k^{2}-1}.

Moreover,
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)
is a quasi-polynomial in
n
of period 2 and degree
2k^{2}+k-2
(provided that
n\leq N+\frac{1+k}{2}
).

Proof.

From the right-hand side of equation (
4
), the contribution to
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)
, that is, the coefficient of
x^{n}y^{n}
, for
n\leq N+\frac{1+k}{2}
, comes from the determinant that takes exclusively terms of the form
\binom{j-1}{i-1}x^{j-i}
and similarly with
y
. In other words,
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)
comes exclusively from the diagonal terms of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x^{2})^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(1-y^{2})^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(1-xy)^{k^{2}}(y-x)^{k^{2}}}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq k\\
1\leq j\leq 2k\end{subarray}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\binom{j-1}{i_{1}-1}x^{j-i_{1}}\\
\\
\binom{j-1}{i_{2}-1}y^{j-i_{2}}\end{array}\right].

Lemma
2.2
implies that we must consider the diagonal terms in

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G(1+k)(1-x^{2})^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(1-y^{2})^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(1-xy)^{k^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G(1+k)}\sum_{\ell_{1}=0}^{\infty}\binom{\ell_{1}+\binom{k+1}{2}-1}{\binom{k+1}{2}-1}x^{2\ell_{1}}\sum_{\ell_{2}=0}^{\infty}\binom{\ell_{2}+\binom{k+1}{2}-1}{\binom{k+1}{2}-1}y^{2\ell_{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{m+k^{2}-1}{k^{2}-1}(xy)^{m}.

We obtain

\displaystyle I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)=\frac{1}{G(1+k)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\binom{\ell+\binom{k+1}{2}-1}{\binom{k+1}{2}-1}^{2}\binom{n-2\ell+k^{2}-1}{k^{2}-1}.

This is a quasi-polynomial in
n
, since the upper bound in the sum depends on the parity of
n
. Each term in the above sum has total degree
2\left(\binom{k+1}{2}-1\right)+k^{2}-1=2k^{2}+k-3
in the variable
\ell
. By summing over
\ell
, each term leads to a polynomial of degree
2k^{2}+k-2
on
n
. Moreover, the coefficients are all positive, which guarantees that there is no cancelation that could lower the final degree.
∎

4.1.
Some low degree cases

Here we specialize Proposition
4.2
for low values of

. For

we have

I_{d_{1},2}^{S}(n;N)=\left\lfloor\frac{n+2}{2}\right\rfloor=\frac{2n+3}{4}+\frac{(-1)^{n}}{4}.

Combining with Lemma

, this recovers
[

, Corollary 5.12]
:
If

, then

I_{d_{1},2}^{S}(n,N):=\int_{\mathrm{Sp}(2N)}\left|\mathrm{Sc}_{n}(U)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}U=\begin{cases}\left\lfloor\frac{n+2}{2}\right\rfloor&0\leq n\leq N,\\
\left\lfloor\frac{2N-n+2}{2}\right\rfloor&N+1\leq n\leq 2N.\end{cases}

As

,

\frac{(2-c)^{8}}{215040}&\frac{3}{2}\leq c\leq 2.\end{cases}

5.
A complex analysis approach in the symplectic case

In this section we further study the asymptotic coefficient
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)
by studying the integral (
12
) with tools from complex analysis.

First, recall equation (3.36) from
[
CFK+03
]
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{Sp}(2N)}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\det(I-Ue^{-\alpha_{j}})\mathrm{d}U=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{r(r-1)/2}2^{r}}{(2\pi i)^{r}r!}e^{-N\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}}\oint\cdots\oint\prod_{1\leq\ell\leq m\leq r}(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{\ell}})^{-1}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\dots,z_{r}^{2})^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{r}z_{j}e^{N\sum_{j=1}^{r}z_{j}}\mathrm{d}z_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}z_{r}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{j=1}^{r}(z_{j}-\alpha_{i})(z_{j}+\alpha_{i})}.

By (
12
), we are interested in the diagonal coefficients of the polynomial

\displaystyle P_{k}(x,y):=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{Sp}(2N)}\det(I-Ux)^{k}\det(I-Uy)^{k}\mathrm{d}U

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}2^{2k}}{(2\pi i)^{2k}(2k)!}x^{Nk}y^{Nk}\oint\cdots\oint\prod_{1\leq\ell\leq m\leq 2k}(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{\ell}})^{-1}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\dots,z_{2k}^{2})^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}z_{j}e^{N\sum_{j=1}^{2k}z_{j}}\mathrm{d}z_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}z_{2k}}{\prod_{j=1}^{2k}(z_{j}-\log(x))^{k}(z_{j}+\log(x))^{k}(z_{j}-\log(y))^{k}(z_{j}+\log(y))^{k}}.

In order to compute the integral
P_{k}(x,y)
, first shrink the contour of the integrals into small circles centered at
\pm\log(x)
and
\pm\log(y)
. More precisely, let
\epsilon_{j}=\log x
or
\log y
and
\delta_{j}=\pm 1
for
j=1,\dots,2k
. Consider

\displaystyle P_{k}(x,y,\delta_{1}\epsilon_{1},\dots\delta_{2k}\epsilon_{2k}):=

\displaystyle\oint\cdots\oint\prod_{1\leq\ell\leq m\leq 2k}(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{\ell}})^{-1}

(21)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\dots,z_{2k}^{2})^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}z_{j}e^{N\sum_{j=1}^{2k}z_{j}}\mathrm{d}z_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}z_{2k}}{\prod_{j=1}^{2k}(z_{j}-\log(x))^{k}(z_{j}+\log(x))^{k}(z_{j}-\log(y))^{k}(z_{j}+\log(y))^{k}},

where the
j
th integral is around a small circle centered at
\delta_{j}\epsilon_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,2k
. This gives
4^{2k}
multiple integrals, and

P_{k}(x,y)=\frac{(-1)^{k}2^{2k}}{(2\pi i)^{2k}(2k)!}x^{Nk}y^{Nk}\sum_{\delta_{j},\epsilon_{j}}P_{k}(x,y,\delta_{1}\epsilon_{1},\dots\delta_{2k}\epsilon_{2k}),

where the sum is taken over all the possible
4^{2k}
choices for
\delta_{j}
and
\epsilon_{j}
.

The following lemma shows that the only nonzero terms in the above sum are those where
\epsilon_{j}=\log x
for exactly half of the
j
.

Lemma 5.1
.

(Equivalent of
[
KRRGR18
, Lemma 4.11]
) Let
P_{k}(x,y,\delta_{1}\epsilon_{1},\dots\delta_{2k}\epsilon_{2k})
be defined by (
5
). Then

P_{k}(x,y,\delta_{1}\epsilon_{1},\dots\delta_{2k}\epsilon_{2k})=0
unless exactly half of the
\epsilon_{j}
’s equal
\log x
.

Proof.

Assume that
k+1
of the
\epsilon_{j}
’s are
\log x
, and without loss of generality that
\epsilon_{1}=\cdots=\epsilon_{k+1}=\log x
. Let

\displaystyle G(z_{1},\dots,z_{k+1})=

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq\ell\leq m\leq 2k}(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{\ell}})^{-1}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\dots,z_{2k}^{2})\prod_{j=1}^{2k}z_{j}e^{N\sum_{j=1}^{2k}z_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}(z_{j}-\delta_{j}\log(x))^{k}}{\prod_{j=1}^{2k}(z_{j}-\log(x))^{k}(z_{j}+\log(x))^{k}(z_{j}-\log(y))^{k}(z_{j}+\log(y))^{k}}.

Poles arising from
z_{i}=-z_{j}
cancel with the Vandermonde determinant in the numerator, so
G(z_{1},\dots,z_{k+1})
is analytic around
(\delta_{1}\log x,\dots,\delta_{k+1}\log x)
. Now use the residue theorem to compute the original integral in (
5
), whose integrand is

\frac{G(z_{1},\dots,z_{k+1})\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\dots,z_{2k}^{2})}{\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}(z_{j}-\delta_{j}\log(x))^{k}}.

To compute the residue we need to compute the coefficient of
\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}(z_{j}-\delta_{j}\log(x))^{k-1}
in the numerator. By properties of the Vandermonde,

\displaystyle\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\dots,z_{2k}^{2})=

\displaystyle\Delta(z_{1}^{2}-(\log x)^{2},\dots,z_{2k}^{2}-(\log x)^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{2k}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{2k}\left(z_{i}^{2}-(\delta_{j}\log x)^{2}\right)^{\sigma(i)-1},

and it is impossible to have
\sigma(i)-1\leq k-1

\displaystyle\times\oint J({\bf v})\frac{\prod_{1\leq\ell<m\leq k}(\delta_{\ell}v_{\ell}-\delta_{m}v_{m})^{2}\prod_{k<\ell<m\leq 2k}(\delta_{\ell}v_{\ell}-\delta_{m}v_{m})^{2}}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell\leq m\leq k\\

(in the

and evaluate at

is of the form

, where

is a certain linear function of the

. After differentiating

\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{\ell,m}\geq 0\\

where

is a polynomial of degree

. But then as
{\bf v}\to 0
, the derivative of

is a polynomial in

Thus by taking residues in (

\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)(2N)^{2k^{2}+k-2},

where

and each

Thus

is a piecewise polynomial function of degree at most

6.
The Rudnick-Waxman Orthogonal setting

In this section we consider a problem involving an arithmetic sum in functions fields that is described by an integral over the ensemble of orthogonal matrices. More precisely, we follow a model of Gaussian integers in the function field context that was considered by Rudnick and Waxman in
[
RW19
]
and initially developed by Bary-Soroker, Smilansky, and Wolf in
[
BSSW16
]
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the set of monic polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, and let
\mathcal{M}_{n}
denote the elements of
\mathcal{M}
of degree
n
. Similarly define
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}
the set of monic irreducible polynomials, and the corresponding subset of elements of degree
n
. For a
P(T)\in\mathcal{P}
, there exist
A(T),B(T)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
such that

(36)

P(T)=A(T)^{2}+TB(T)^{2}

if and only if
P(0)
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\chi_{2}
the character on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
defined by

\chi_{2}(x)=\begin{cases}0&x=0,\\
1&x\text{ is a nonzero square in }\mathbb{F}_{q},\\
-1&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Extend
\chi_{2}
to
\mathcal{M}
by defining
\chi_{2}(f):=\chi_{2}(f(0))
. Equation (
36
) is solvable if and only if
\chi_{2}(P)=1
.

Set
S:=\sqrt{-T}
so that
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}[S]
naturally.
Equation (
36
) becomes

P(T)=(A+BS)(A-BS)=\mathfrak{p}\overline{\mathfrak{p}}

in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[S]
. There are two automorphisms of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[S]
fixing
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
: the identity and (an analogue of) complex conjugation, which may be extended to the ring of formal power series:

\sigma:\mathbb{F}_{q}[[S]]\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}[[S]],\qquad\sigma(S)=-S.

The norm map is then given by

\mathrm{Norm}:\mathbb{F}_{q}[[S]]^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}[[T]]^{\times},\qquad\mathrm{Norm}(f)=f\sigma(f)=f(S)f(-S).

The group of formal power series with constant term 1 and unit norm is

\mathbb{S}^{1}:=\{g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[[S]]^{\times}\,:\,g(0)=1,\mathrm{Norm}(g)=1\},

and can be seen as an analogue of the unit circle in this context.

For
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[[S]]
let
\mathrm{ord}(f)=\max\{j\,:\,S^{j}\mid f\}
and
|f|:=q^{-\mathrm{ord}(f)}
, the absolute value associated with the place at infinity. Now consider sectors of the unit circle:

(37)

\mathrm{Sect}(v;k):=\{w\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\,:\,|w-v|\leq q^{-k}\}.

The sector
\mathrm{Sect}(v;k)
can be described modulo
S^{k}
since

w\in\mathrm{Sect}(v;k)
if and only if
w\equiv v\,(\mathrm{mod}\,S^{k})
. The following modular group then parametrizes the different sectors:

\mathbb{S}^{1}_{k}:=\{f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[S]/(S^{k})\,:\,f(0)=1,\,\mathrm{Norm}(f)\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,S^{k})\}.

Lemma 6.1
.

[
Kat17
, Lemma 2.1]
,
[
RW19
, Lemma 6.1]
,

(1)

The cardinality of
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{k}
is given by

\#\mathbb{S}^{1}_{k}=q^{\kappa},\mbox{ with }\kappa:=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor.

(2)

There is a direct product decomposition

\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[S]/(S^{k})\right)^{\times}=H_{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}_{k},

where

H_{k}:=\{f\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[S]/(S^{k})\right)^{\times}\,:\,f(-S)\equiv f(S)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,S^{k})\},

and

\#H_{k}=(q-1)q^{\left\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rfloor}.

For
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[S]
coprime to
S
, define

U(f):=\sqrt{\frac{f}{\sigma(f)}}.

The square-root is well defined for

\frac{f}{\sigma(f)}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, since
v\mapsto v^{2}
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
by Hensel’s Lemma. Also,
U(cf)=U(f)
for scalars
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.

The modular counterpart of
U
is given by

U_{k}:\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[S]/(S^{k})\right)^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}_{k},\qquad f\mapsto\sqrt{\frac{f}{\sigma(f)}}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,S^{k})

and is a surjective homomorphism whose kernel is
H_{k}
(
[
RW19
, Lemma 6.2]
).

We now define the analogues of the Hecke characters in this setting, following
[
RW19
]
and Katz
[
Kat17
]
. A
super-even
character modulo
S^{k}
is a Dirichlet character

\Xi:\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[S]/(S^{k})\right)^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}

which is trivial on
H_{k}
. Super-even characters modulo
S^{k}
can be seen as the characters of

\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[S]/(S^{k})\right)^{\times}/H_{k}\cong\mathbb{S}_{k}^{1}
.

The super-even characters modulo
S
satisfy these orthogonality relations, found in the proof of Lemma 6.8 in
[
RW19
, page 192]
:

(38)

\sum_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{k}^{1}}\overline{\Xi_{1}(v)}\Xi_{2}(v)=\begin{cases}q^{\kappa}&\Xi_{1}=\Xi_{2},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proposition 6.2
.

[
RW19
, Proposition 6.3]

For
f\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[S]/(S^{k})\right)^{\times}
and
v\in\mathbb{S}_{k}^{1}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

U_{k}(f)\in\mathrm{Sect}(v;k)
,

(2)

U_{k}(f)=U_{k}(v)
,

(3)

fH_{k}=vH_{k}
,

(4)

\Xi(f)=\Xi(v)
for all super-even characters
\,(\mathrm{mod}\,S^{k})
.

The
Swan conductor
of
\Xi
is the maximal integer
d=d(\Xi)<k
such that
\Xi
is nontrivial on the subgroup

\Gamma_{d}:=\left(1+(S^{d})\right)/(S^{k})\subset\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[S]/(S^{k})\right)^{\times}.

Thus
\Xi
is a primitive character modulo
S^{d(\Xi)+1}
. The Swan conductor of a super-even character is necessarily odd, since these characters are automatically trivial on
\Gamma_{d}
for
d
even.

Consider the twists
\Xi\chi_{2}
of the super-even characters
\Xi
. The

L
-function associated to
\Xi\chi_{2}
is given by

\mathcal{L}(u,\Xi\chi_{2})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}\\
f(0)\not=0\end{subarray}}\Xi(f)\chi_{2}(f)u^{\deg(f)}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}\\
P(0)\not=0\end{subarray}}\left(1-\Xi(P)\chi_{2}(P)u^{\deg(P)}\right)^{-1},\quad|u|<1/q.

This is a polynomial of degree
d(\Xi)
when
\Xi
is nontrivial. The Riemann hypothesis, which is known in this case, implies that the reciprocals of the roots of
\mathcal{L}(u,\Xi\chi_{2})
have absolute value
q^{1/2}
. More precisely,

\mathcal{L}(u,\Xi\chi_{2})=\det\left(I-uq^{1/2}\Theta_{\Xi\chi_{2}}\right),

with
\Theta_{\Xi\chi_{2}}\in\mathrm{U}(N)
and
N=d(\Xi)
.

Katz
[
Kat17
, Theorem 7.1]
showed that for
q\rightarrow\infty
the set of Frobenius classes

\{\Theta_{\Xi\chi_{2}}\,:\,\Xi\mbox{ primitive super-even }\,(\mathrm{mod}\,S^{k})\}

becomes uniformly distributed in
\mathrm{O}(2\kappa-1)
if
2\kappa\geq 6
, and that the same holds for
2\kappa=4
if the characteristic is coprime to 10.

6.1.
A problem leading to orthogonal distributions

Our goal is to study the following sum:

\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},k,n}^{O}(v)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
f(0)\neq 0\\
U(f)\in\mathrm{Sect}(v,k)\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(f)\left(\frac{1+\chi_{2}(f)}{2}\right),

and to prove Theorem
1.13
about its variance.

The divisor function naturally appears when considering powers of the
L
-function, since

\mathcal{L}(u,\Xi\chi_{2})^{\ell}=\mathrm{det}\left(1-uq^{1/2}\Theta_{\Xi\chi_{2}}\right)^{\ell}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}\\
f(0)\not=0\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(f)\Xi(f)\chi_{2}(f)u^{\deg(f)}.

This suggests that we define

M_{0}(n;d_{\ell}\Xi\chi_{2}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
f(0)\not=0\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(f)\Xi(f)\chi_{2}(f).

A Dirichlet character
\chi
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
is
even
if
\chi(\alpha f)=\chi(f)
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and odd otherwise. In our case,
\Xi
is even while
\Xi\chi_{2}
is odd.

By
[
KL22
, Lemma 2.1]
, the following result holds.

Lemma 6.3
.

For
n\leq\ell d(\Xi)
,

M_{0}(n;d_{\ell}\Xi\chi_{2})=(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{\ell}=n\\
0\leq j_{1},\dots j_{\ell}\leq d(\Xi)\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{1}}(\Theta_{\Xi\chi_{2}})\cdots\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{\ell}}(\Theta_{\Xi\chi_{2}})

and
M_{0}(n;d_{\ell}\Xi\chi_{2})=0
otherwise.

We start our analysis by looking at the mean value of
\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},k,n}^{O}
averaged over all the directions of
v\in\mathbb{S}_{k}^{1}
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Define

\langle\mathcal{N}^{O}_{d_{\ell},k,n}\rangle:=\frac{1}{q^{\kappa}}\sum_{v\in\mathbb{S}_{k}^{1}}\mathcal{N}^{O}_{d_{\ell},k,n}(v).

\langle\mathcal{N}^{O}_{d_{\ell},k,n}\rangle=\frac{1}{q^{\kappa}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\

By
[

\frac{1}{q^{\kappa}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
f(0)\not=0\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(f)=q^{n-\kappa}\binom{\ell+n-1}{\ell-1}+O(q^{n-\kappa-1}).

This can be seen by considering the generating function for
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}\\
f(0)\not=0\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(f)u^{\deg(f)}
, namely
\left(\frac{1-u}{1-qu}\right)^{\ell}
, and by comparing the coefficients of

in both expressions.

The result follows from the fact that

(39)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
f(0)\neq 0\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(f)\chi_{2}(f)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }n>0,\\
1&\text{ if }n=0.\end{cases}

This fact is true because the generating function of the terms in (
39
) is given by

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}\\
f(0)\neq 0\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(f)\chi_{2}(f)u^{\deg(f)}=\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}\\
f(0)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\chi_{2}(f)u^{\deg(f)}\Big)^{\ell},

and at the same time,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
f(0)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\chi_{2}(f)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }n>0,\\
1&\text{ if }n=0.\end{cases}

∎

Note that in proving Lemma
6.4
, we also showed that
\langle\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},k,n}^{O}\rangle
is the average sum of the divisor function over all sectors, unweighted by
\chi_{2}
, and then divided by the number of sectors considered. That is,

\langle\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},k,n}^{O}\rangle=\frac{1}{2q^{\kappa}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
f(0)\neq 0\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(f).

However, the quantity
\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},k,n}^{O}(v)
exhibits two kinds of fluctuations away from this average: one is simply the fluctuations of the divisor function itself in sectors, and the other is the fluctuations coming from the twist by the character
\chi_{2}
. We are interested in isolating the latter fluctuations, which are somewhat more delicate, so we will consider a more refined “average” of
\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},k,n}^{O}
, defined as:

\langle\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},k,n}^{O}(v)\rangle_{S}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
f(0)\neq 0\\
U(f)\in\mathrm{Sect}(v,k)\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(f).

Our next goal is to obtain
\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},k,n}^{O}
in terms of the characters
\Xi
and
\chi_{2}
. By Proposition
6.2
and the orthogonality relations (

\frac{1}{q^{\kappa}}\sum_{\Xi\text{ super-even}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,S^{k})}\overline{\Xi(v)}\Xi(f)=\begin{cases}1&U(f)\in\mathrm{Sect}(v;k),\\

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{O}_{d_{\ell},k,n}(v)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2q^{\kappa}}\sum_{\Xi\text{ super-even}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,S^{k})}\overline{\Xi(v)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\

f(0)\not=0\\

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{O}_{d_{\ell},k,n}(v)-\langle\mathcal{N}^{O}_{d_{\ell},k,n}(v)\rangle_{S}=

\mathrm{Var}_{S}(\mathcal{N}^{O}_{d_{\ell},k,n})=\frac{q^{n}}{4q^{2\kappa}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Xi\text{ super-even}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,S^{k})\\
\Xi\neq\Xi_{0}\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{\ell}=n\\
0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{\ell}\leq d(\Xi)\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{1}}(\Theta_{\Xi\chi_{2}})\cdots\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{\ell}}(\Theta_{\Xi\chi_{2}})\Big|^{2}.

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
n\leq\ell(2\kappa-1)
with
\kappa=\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor\geq 3
. As
q\rightarrow\infty
,

\mathrm{Var}_{S}(\mathcal{N}^{O}_{d_{\ell},k,n})\sim\frac{q^{n}}{4q^{\kappa}}\int_{\mathrm{O}(2\kappa-1)}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{\ell}=n\\
0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{\ell}\leq 2\kappa-1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{1}}(U)\cdots\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{\ell}}(U)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}U.

Proof.

In equation (
42
), separate the characters according to their Swan conductor, which is necessarily an odd integer
d(\Xi)<k
with maximal value
2\kappa-1
. The characters with maximal conductor are primitive; the contribution from the others is negligible. Thus, we can consider the sum only over the primitive characters, and the result follows from Katz
[
Kat17
, Theorem 7.1]
.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

In fact, the sum analogous to
\mathcal{N}^{O}_{d_{\ell},k,n}
defined with
\left(\frac{1-\chi_{2}(f)}{2}\right)
instead of
\left(\frac{1+\chi_{2}(f)}{2}\right)
has the same variance by the same arguments as in this section. Similarly, the distribution of the divisor function along quadratic non-residues modulo an irreducible polynomial is symplectic, just as the distribution along quadratic residues is.

7.
The symmetric function theory context in the orthogonal case

In this section we describe a model for
I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N)
using symmetric function theory. Several ideas translate from the symplectic case treated in Section
3
.

We begin with a lemma connecting orthogonal matrix integrals to Schur functions. The
conjugate partition

\mu^{\prime}
of a partition
\mu
is defined by the condition that the Ferrer diagram of
\mu^{\prime}
is the transpose of the Ferrer diagram of
\mu
. We have the following expression.

Lemma 7.1
.

(43)

\int_{\mathrm{O}(2N+1)}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\det(1+Ux_{j})\mathrm{d}U=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu_{1}\leq 2N+1\\
\mu^{\prime}\text{ even}\end{subarray}}s_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r}),

where the sum takes place over all the partitions with even conjugate partition.

Remark 7.2
.

The above result is very similar to equation (102) in
[
BG06
]
, which gives

\int_{\mathrm{O}(2N)}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\det(1+Ux_{j})\mathrm{d}U=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda_{1}\leq 2N\\
\lambda^{\prime}\,\text{even}\end{subarray}}s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r}).

Their results do not immediately extend to the orthogonal group of odd dimension. However, this result is nevertheless well-known to experts; for completeness, we provide a short proof.

Proof.

We begin by using the dual Cauchy identity (see
[
Sta99
, Theorem 7.14.3, page 332]
), which says that for any vectors
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
and
(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m})
,

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+\alpha_{i}\beta_{j})=\sum_{\mu}s_{\mu}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})s_{\mu^{\prime}}(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}),

where the sum takes place over all partitions
\mu
. Then if
U
has eigenvalues
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{r}\det(1+Ux_{j})

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+\alpha_{i}x_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}s_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})s_{\mu^{\prime}}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}).

Integrating this identity gives that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{O}(2N+1)}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\det(1+Ux_{j})\mathrm{d}U

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}s_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})\int_{\mathrm{O}(2N+1)}s_{\mu^{\prime}}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\mathrm{d}U.

By
[
DG06
, equation (56)]
(see also the arguments leading to
[
Mac15
, equation 3.21]
for more explanation),

\int_{\mathrm{O}(2N+1)}s_{\mu^{\prime}}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\mathrm{d}U=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\mu^{\prime}\text{ is even and }\ell(\mu^{\prime})\leq 2N+1\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Noting that
\ell(\mu^{\prime})\leq 2N+1
if and only if
\mu_{1}\leq 2N+1
, this completes the proof.
∎

In our case,

(44)

\int_{\mathrm{O}(2N+1)}\det(1+Ux)^{k}\det(1+Uy)^{k}\mathrm{d}U=\sum_{m,n=0}^{(2N+1)k}x^{m}y^{n}J_{d_{k},2}^{O}(m,n;N),

and we are interested in the diagonal terms

I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N):=J_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n,n;N).

Thus

I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N)=2\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu_{1}\leq 2N+1\\
\mu^{\prime}\text{ even}\end{subarray}}s_{\mu}(\underbrace{x,\dots,x}_{k},\underbrace{y,\dots,y}_{k})\right]_{x^{n}y^{n}}.

These terms count the number of SSYTs
T
such that
\mu_{1}\leq 2N+1
,
\mu^{\prime}
is even, and

a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}=n,\qquad a_{k+1}+\cdots+a_{2k}=n.

Parametrize such
T
using the same parametrization as before, where
y^{(s)}_{r}=y^{(s)}_{r}(T)
is the rightmost position of the entry
s
in the row
r
. We obtain an array of shape

\begin{bmatrix}y_{1}^{(1)}&y_{1}^{(2)}&\dots&y_{1}^{(2k)}\\
0&y_{2}^{(2)}&\dots&y_{2}^{(2k)}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&y_{2k}^{(2k)}\end{bmatrix}

and satisfying the following properties:

(1)

y_{r}^{(s)}\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,2N+1]
,

(2)

y_{r+1}^{(s+1)}\leq y_{r}^{(s)}
,

(3)

y_{r}^{(s)}\leq y_{r}^{(s+1)}
,

(4)

y_{1}^{(k)}+\cdots+y_{k}^{(k)}=n
,

(5)

y_{1}^{(2k)}+\cdots+y_{2k}^{(2k)}=2n
, and

(6)

y_{2\ell-1}^{(2k)}=y_{2\ell}^{(2k)}
for
\ell=1,\dots,k
.

The last condition codifies the fact that
\mu^{\prime}
is even.
The combination of (6) with items (2) and (3) imposes the extra conditions
y_{2\ell-1}^{(2k-1)}=y_{2\ell-1}^{(2k)}
for
\ell=1,\dots,k
.

Let
c=\frac{n}{2N+1}
. As in the symplectic case, consider
U^{O}=\{(i,j):1\leq i\leq j\leq 2k-1,(i,j)\not=(1,k),(1,2k-1)\}
. Let
V^{O}_{c}
be the convex region contained in
\mathbb{R}^{2k^{2}-k-2}=\{(u_{i}^{(j)})_{(i,j)\in U^{O}}:u_{i}^{(j)}\in\mathbb{R}\}
defined by the following inequalities:

(1)

0\leq u_{i}^{(j)}\leq 1
for
(i,j)\in U^{O}
,

(2)

for
u_{1}^{(k)}:=c-(u_{2}^{(k)}+\cdots+u_{k}^{(k)})
, we have
0\leq u_{1}^{(k)}\leq 1
,

(3)

for
u_{1}^{(2k-1)}:=c-(u_{3}^{(2k-1)}+\cdots+u_{2k-1}^{(2k-1)})
, we have
0\leq u_{1}^{(2k-1)}\leq 1
,

(4)

u_{r+1}^{(s+1)}\leq u_{r}^{(s)}
, and

(5)

u_{r}^{(s)}\leq u_{r}^{(s+1)}
.

Let
A_{k}^{O}
be the set defined by the last two conditions above.

As in the symplectic case, the region
V^{O}_{c}
is convex because it is the intersection of half-planes. For
c\in[0,k]
,
V^{O}_{c}
is contained in
[0,1]^{2k^{2}-k-2}
and is therefore contained in a closed ball of diameter
\sqrt{2k^{2}-k-2}
.
Then

I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N)=2\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{2k^{2}-k-2}\cap((2N+1)\cdot V^{O}_{c})\right),

where
(2N+1)\cdot V^{O}_{c}=\{(2N+1)x:x\in V^{O}_{c}\}
is the dilate of
V^{O}_{c}
by a factor of
2N+1
.
By Theorem
3.1
,

I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N)=2\mathrm{vol}_{2k^{2}-k-2}((2N+1)\cdot V^{O}_{c})+O\left(N^{2k^{2}-k-3}\right).

The volume of the corresponding region is given by

(45)

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{2k^{2}-k-2}((2N+1)\cdot V^{O}_{c})

\displaystyle=(2N+1)^{2k^{2}-k-2}\int_{[0,1]^{2k^{2}-k}}\delta_{c}(u_{1}^{(k)}+\cdots+u_{k}^{(k)})\delta_{c}(u_{1}^{(2k-1)}+u_{3}^{(2k-1)}+\cdots+u_{2k-1}^{(2k-1)})\mathds{1}_{A_{k}^{O}}(u)d^{2k^{2}-k}u.

By applying Theorem
3.2
, we obtain Theorem
1.5
, which is an analogue to Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
c=\frac{a}{b}
be a fixed rational number and
k
be a fixed integer. If
2N+1
is a multiple of
b
, then
I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(c(2N+1);N)=P_{c,k}(N)
, where
P_{c,k}
is a polynomial of degree
{2k^{2}-k-2}
.

We also have the analogue to Proposition
3.5
, with a formula for
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}
itself.

Proposition 7.4
.

The coefficient for
(2N+1)^{2k^{2}-k-2}
in the asymptotics for
I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(c(2N+1),N)
is given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}(c)=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{G(1+k)}\int_{[0,1]^{\frac{3}{2}k^{2}-\frac{k}{2}}}\delta_{c}(u_{1}^{(k)}+\cdots+u_{k}^{(k)})\delta_{c}(u_{1}^{(2k-1)}+\cdots+u_{2k-1}^{(2k-1)})

\displaystyle\times\mathds{1}\begin{bmatrix}&&u_{1}^{(k)}&\leq&\dots&\leq&u_{1}^{(2k)}\\
&\iddots&&&&\iddots&\\
u_{k}^{(k)}&\leq&\dots&\leq&u_{k}^{(2k)}&\\
\vdots&&\iddots&&&&\\
u_{2k}^{(2k)}&&&&&&\end{bmatrix}\Delta(u_{1}^{(k)},u_{2}^{(k)},\dots,u_{k}^{(k)})d^{\frac{3}{2}k^{2}-\frac{k}{2}}u.

Proof.

From the volume formula (
45
), we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}(c)=

\displaystyle 2\int_{[0,1]^{2k^{2}-k}}\delta_{c}(u_{1}^{(k)}+\cdots+u_{k}^{(k)})\delta_{c}(u_{1}^{(2k-1)}+u_{3}^{(2k-1)}+\cdots+u_{2k-1}^{(2k-1)})

(46)

\displaystyle\times\mathds{1}\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}^{(1)}&\leq&u_{1}^{(2)}&\leq&\dots&\leq&u_{1}^{(2k-2)}&\leq&u_{1}^{(2k-1)}\\
\rotatebox{90.0}{$\,\leq$}&&\rotatebox{90.0}{$\,\leq$}&&&&\rotatebox{90.0}{$\,\leq$}&&\\
u_{2}^{(2)}&\leq&u_{2}^{(3)}&\leq&\dots&\leq&u_{2}^{(2k-1)}&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\iddots&&&&\\
u_{2k-1}^{(2k-1)}&&&&&&&&\end{bmatrix}d^{2k^{2}-k}u.

Integrating with respect to
u_{1}^{(1)}
in (
7
) and applying Lemma
3.4
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}(c)=

\displaystyle 2\int_{[0,1]^{2k^{2}-k-1}}\delta_{c}(u_{1}^{(k)}+\cdots+u_{k}^{(k)})\delta_{c}(u_{1}^{(2k-1)}+u_{3}^{(2k-1)}+\cdots+u_{2k-1}^{(2k-1)})

\displaystyle\times\mathds{1}\begin{bmatrix}&&u_{1}^{(2)}&\leq&\dots&\leq&u_{1}^{(2k-2)}&\leq&u_{1}^{(2k-1)}\\
&&\rotatebox{90.0}{$\,\leq$}&&&&\rotatebox{90.0}{$\,\leq$}&&\\
u_{2}^{(2)}&\leq&u_{2}^{(3)}&\leq&\dots&\leq&u_{2}^{(2k-1)}&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\iddots&&&&\\
u_{2k-1}^{(2k-1)}&&&&&&&&\end{bmatrix}\frac{\Delta(u_{1}^{(2)},u_{2}^{(2)})}{1!}d^{2k^{2}-k-1}u.

We proceed inductively and get

\displaystyle\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}(c)=

\displaystyle 2\int_{[0,1]^{\frac{3}{2}k^{2}-\frac{k}{2}}}\delta_{c}(u_{1}^{(k)}+\cdots+u_{k}^{(k)})\delta_{c}(u_{1}^{(2k-1)}+u_{3}^{(2k-1)}+\cdots+u_{2k-1}^{(2k-1)})

\displaystyle\times\mathds{1}\begin{bmatrix}&&u_{1}^{(k)}&\leq&\dots&\leq&u_{1}^{(2k-1)}\\
&\iddots&&&&\iddots&\\
u_{k}^{(k)}&\leq&\dots&\leq&u_{k}^{(2k-1)}&\\
\vdots&&\iddots&&&&\\
u_{2k-1}^{(2k-1)}&&&&&&\end{bmatrix}\frac{\Delta(u_{1}^{(k)},u_{2}^{(k)},\dots,u_{k}^{(k)})}{1!2!\cdots(k-1)!}d^{\frac{3}{2}k^{2}-\frac{k}{2}}u.

∎

8.
The determinant expression in the orthogonal case

As in the symplectic case, consider in full generality

I_{d_{k},\ell}^{O}(n;N):=\int_{\mathrm{O}(2N+1)}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=n\\
0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\leq 2N+1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{1}}(U)\cdots\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{k}}(U)\Big|^{\ell}\mathrm{d}U,

and write

I_{d_{k},\ell}^{O}(n;N)\sim\gamma_{d_{k},\ell}^{O}(c)(2N+1)^{m},

where
m
is the appropriate exponent of
N
such that the above asymptotic formula exists.

Similar formulas for
\mathrm{O}(2N)
and
\mathrm{SO}(2N)
with
\ell=1
were studied in detail by Medjedovic
[
Med21
]
.
Recall that

(47)

\int_{\mathrm{O}(2N+1)}\det(1+Ux)^{k}\det(1+Uy)^{k}\mathrm{d}U=\sum_{m,n=0}^{2Nk}x^{m}y^{n}J_{d_{k},2}^{O}(m,n;N),

and we are interested in the diagonal terms

I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N):=J_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n,n;N).

From (81) and (83) in
[
BG06
]
,

(48)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{SO}(2N+1)}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\det(1-Ux_{j})\mathrm{d}U=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}x_{j}}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq r}\left[x_{i}^{2N+2r-j}-x_{i}^{j-1}\right].

Combining equations (
43
), (
47
), and (
48
), yields

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{(2N+1)k}x^{m}y^{n}J_{d_{k},2}^{O}(m,n;N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x^{2})^{\binom{k}{2}}(1-y^{2})^{\binom{k}{2}}(1-xy)^{k^{2}}}\left.\frac{\det_{1\leq i,j\leq 2k}\left[x_{i}^{2N+4k-j}-x_{i}^{j-1}\right]}{\Delta(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k})}\right|_{(x,\dots,x,y,\dots,y)}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(1-x^{2})^{\binom{k}{2}}(1-y^{2})^{\binom{k}{2}}(1-xy)^{k^{2}}}\left.\frac{\det_{1\leq i,j\leq 2k}\left[x_{i}^{2N+4k-j}-x_{i}^{j-1}\right]}{\Delta(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k})}\right|_{(-x,\dots,-x,-y,\dots,-y)}

Applying Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{(2N+1)k}x^{m}y^{n}J_{d_{k},2}^{O}(m,n;N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(1-x^{2})^{\binom{k}{2}}(1-y^{2})^{\binom{k}{2}}(1-xy)^{k^{2}}(y-x)^{k^{2}}}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq k\\
1\leq j\leq 2k\end{subarray}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\binom{2N+4k-j}{i_{1}-1}x^{2N+4k-j-i_{1}+1}-\binom{j-1}{i_{1}-1}x^{j-i_{1}}\\
\\
\binom{2N+4k-j}{i_{2}-1}y^{2N+4k-j-i_{2}+1}-\binom{j-1}{i_{2}-1}y^{j-i_{2}}\end{array}\right].

Proposition
2.3
implies that the right-hand side of (
8
) is at least a Taylor series.

As in the symplectic case, we have a functional equation for the integral
I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N)
.

Lemma 8.1
.

For
0\leq n\leq(2N+1)k
the following functional equation holds:

I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N)=I_{d_{k},2}^{O}((2N+1)k-n;N).

Proof.

The characteristic polynomial of a matrix in
\mathrm{SO}(2N+1)
satisfies

\det(I+Ux)=x^{2N+1}\det(I+Ux^{-1}).

Then by equation (
44
),

\sum_{m,n=0}^{(2N+1)k}x^{m}y^{n}J_{d_{k},2}^{O}(m,n;N)=x^{(2N+1)k}y^{(2N+1)k}\sum_{m,n=0}^{(2N+1)k}x^{-m}y^{-n}J_{d_{k},2}^{O}(m,n;N).

Change variables
m\rightarrow(2N+1)k-m
,
n\rightarrow(2N+1)k-n
to obtain

J_{d_{k},2}^{S}(m,n;N)=J_{d_{k},2}^{S}((2N+1)k-m,(2N+1)k-n;N),

and deduce the result from setting
m=n
.
∎

Proposition 8.2
.

For
n\leq N+\frac{k}{2}
,

I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N)=\frac{2}{G(1+k)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=0\\
\ell\equiv n\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}^{n}\binom{\frac{n-\ell}{2}+\binom{k}{2}-1}{\binom{k}{2}-1}^{2}\binom{\ell+k^{2}-1}{k^{2}-1}.

Moreover,
I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N)
is a quasi-polynomial in
n
of degree
2k^{2}-k-2
(if
n\leq N+\frac{k}{2}
).

Proof.

Looking at the right-hand side of equation (
8
), we must take exclusively terms of the form

\binom{j-1}{i-1}x^{j-i}
and similarly with
y
. In other words, the
I_{d_{k},2}^{O}
comes exclusively from the diagonal terms of

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(1-x^{2})^{\binom{k}{2}}(1-y^{2})^{\binom{k}{2}}(1-xy)^{k^{2}}(y-x)^{k^{2}}}\det_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq k\\
1\leq j\leq 2k\end{subarray}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\binom{j-1}{i_{1}-1}x^{j-i_{1}}\\
\\
\binom{j-1}{i_{2}-1}y^{j-i_{2}}\end{array}\right].

Lemma
2.2
implies that we must consider the diagonal terms in

\displaystyle\frac{2}{G(1+k)(1-x^{2})^{\binom{k}{2}}(1-y^{2})^{\binom{k}{2}}(1-xy)^{k^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{G(1+k)}\sum_{\ell_{1}=0}^{\infty}\binom{\ell_{1}+\binom{k}{2}-1}{\binom{k}{2}-1}x^{2\ell_{1}}\sum_{\ell_{2}=0}^{\infty}\binom{\ell_{2}+\binom{k}{2}-1}{\binom{k}{2}-1}y^{2\ell_{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{m+k^{2}-1}{k^{2}-1}(xy)^{m}.

Then

\displaystyle I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N)=\frac{2}{G(1+k)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\binom{\ell+\binom{k}{2}-1}{\binom{k}{2}-1}^{2}\binom{n-2\ell+k^{2}-1}{k^{2}-1}.

This is a quasi-polynomial in
n
. Each term in the above sum has total degree
2\left(\binom{k}{2}-1\right)+k^{2}-1=2k^{2}-k-3
in the variable
\ell
. By summing over
\ell
, each term leads to a polynomial of degree
2k^{2}-k-2
on
n
. Moreover, the coefficients are all positive, which guarantees that there is no cancelation that could lower the final degree.

∎

8.1.
Some low degree cases

As in the symplectic case, Proposition
8.2
can be used to compute
I_{d_{k},2}^{O}(n;N)
for low values of
k
. For
k=1
,
I_{d_{1},2}^{O}(n;N)=0.

For
k=2
,

\displaystyle I_{d_{2},2}^{O}(n;N)=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=0\\
\ell\equiv n\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}^{n}\binom{\ell+3}{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left\lfloor\frac{((n+3)^{2}-1)((n+3)^{2}-3)}{48}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{48}(2n^{4}+24n^{3}+100n^{2}+168n+93)+\frac{(-1)^{n}}{16}.

This leads to

I_{d_{2},2}^{O}(n;N)\sim\gamma_{d_{2},2}^{O}(c)(2N+1)^{4},

where

\gamma_{d_{2},2}^{O}(c)=\begin{cases}\frac{c^{4}}{24}&0\leq c\leq\frac{1}{2},\\
\frac{(2-c)^{4}}{24}&\frac{3}{2}\leq c\leq 2.\end{cases}

9.
A complex analysis approach in the orthogonal case

The goal of this section is to further understand the asymptotic coefficient
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{O}(c)
by studying the integral (
44
) with tools from complex analysis.

By applying equation (3.35) from
[
CFK+03
, Lemma 4.3]
to equation (81) from
[
BG06
]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{SO}(2N+1)}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\det(I-Ue^{-\alpha_{j}})\mathrm{d}U=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{r(r-1)/2}2^{r}}{(2\pi i)^{r}r!}e^{-\left(N+\frac{1}{2}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}}\oint\cdots\oint\prod_{1\leq\ell<m\leq r}(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{\ell}})^{-1}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\dots,z_{r}^{2})^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}e^{\left(N+\frac{1}{2}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{r}z_{j}}\mathrm{d}z_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}z_{r}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{j=1}^{r}(z_{j}-\alpha_{i})(z_{j}+\alpha_{i})}.

Since we work with the full orthogonal group, we need to consider

(52)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{O}(2N+1)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\det(I-Ue^{-\alpha_{j}})\mathrm{d}U=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{SO}(2N+1)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\det(I-Ue^{-\alpha_{j}})\mathrm{d}U+\int_{\mathrm{SO}(2N+1)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\det(I+Ue^{-\alpha_{j}})\mathrm{d}U.

This leads to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{O}(2N+1)}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}\det(I+Ux_{j})\mathrm{d}U=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{SO}(2N+1)}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}\det(I-Ux_{j})\mathrm{d}U+\int_{\mathrm{SO}(2N+1)}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}\det(I+Ux_{j})\mathrm{d}U

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k(2k-1)}2^{2k}}{(2\pi i)^{2k}(2k)!}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}x_{j}^{N+\frac{1}{2}}\oint\cdots\oint\prod_{1\leq\ell<m\leq 2k}(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{\ell}})^{-1}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\dots,z_{2k}^{2})^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}(-\log(-x_{j}))e^{\left(N+\frac{1}{2}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{2k}z_{j}}\mathrm{d}z_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}z_{2k}}{\prod_{i=1}^{2k}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}(z_{j}-\log(-x_{i}))(z_{j}+\log(-x_{i}))}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k(2N+1)}\frac{(-1)^{k(2k-1)}2^{2k}}{(2\pi i)^{2k}(2k)!}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}x_{j}^{N+\frac{1}{2}}\oint\cdots\oint\prod_{1\leq\ell<m\leq 2k}(1-e^{-z_{m}-z_{\ell}})^{-1}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Delta(z_{1}^{2},\dots,z_{2k}^{2})^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}(-\log(x_{j}))e^{\left(N+\frac{1}{2}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{2k}z_{j}}\mathrm{d}z_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}z_{2k}}{\prod_{i=1}^{2k}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}(z_{j}-\log(x_{i}))(z_{j}+\log(x_{i}))}.

By (
44
), we are interested in the diagonal coefficients of the polynomial

\displaystyle P_{k}(x,y):=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{O}(2N+1)}\det(I-Ux)^{k}\det(I-Uy)^{k}\mathrm{d}U

Improved uniform error bounds on time-splitting methods for the long-time dynamics of the Dirac equation with small potentials
Weizhu Bao, Yue Feng, Jia Yin

Abstract
We establish improved uniform error bounds on time-splitting methods for the long-time dynamics of the Dirac equation with small electromagnetic potentials characterized by a dimensionless parameter ε ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \varepsilon\in(0,1] representing the amplitude of the potentials. We begin with a semi-discritization of the Dirac equation in time by a time-splitting method, and then followed by a full-discretization in space by the Fourier pseudospectral method. Comparisons on the accuracy of different time discretizations for the Dirac equation are also provided.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the Dirac equation in one or two dimensions (1D or 2D), which can be represented in the two-component form with wave function
\Phi:=\Phi(t,{\mathbf{x}})=(\phi_{1}(t,{\mathbf{x}}),\phi_{2}(t,{\mathbf{x}}))^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
as

,

,

i\partial_{t}\Phi=\left(-i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{j}\partial_{j}+\sigma_{3}\right)\Phi+\varepsilon\left(V(\mathbf{x})I_{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j}(\mathbf{x})\sigma_{j}\right)\Phi,\quad\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,

(1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

(d=1,2)

,

\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\ \ 1\\
1&\ \ 0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\ \ -i\\
i&\ \ 0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\ \ 0\\

(2)

In order to study the dynamics of the Dirac equation (
1

\Phi(t=0,\mathbf{x})=\Phi_{0}(\mathbf{x}),\quad{\bf x}\in\overline{\Omega}.

(3)

For the Dirac equation (

\refcite

However, to our knowledge, much less research has been done for the long-time dynamics when
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. With a small
\varepsilon
, we can study the long-time dynamics of the Dirac equation (
1
) for

t\in[0,T_{\varepsilon}]
with increasing
T
. On the other hand, when
T
is fixed, we are interested in analyzing the dependency of errors for different numerical methods on the parameter
\varepsilon
for
t\in[0,T_{\varepsilon}]
. In our recent works, we examined the long-time error bounds for finite difference time domain (FDTD) methods and finite difference Fourier pseudospectral (FDFP) methods, where the finite difference was applied to discretize the Dirac equation (
1
) in time and different discretizations were taken in space. We rigorously proved that the FDTD methods share an error bound at
O(\frac{h^{2}}{\varepsilon}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{\varepsilon})

T_{\varepsilon}
with

the spatial mesh size and

O(h^{m}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{\varepsilon})
up to the long-time
[
18

.
The uniform spatial error bound with respect to
\varepsilon
for the FDFP methods indicate that these methods have better spatial resolution than the FDTD methods to solve the Dirac equation with small potentials in the long-time regime. Nevertheless, both types of methods face severe numerical burdens when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
due to the
\varepsilon
-dependence of the temporal error. To deal with this problem, we adopted the exponential wave integrator (EWI) method for time discretization, and obtained a uniform error bound

O(h^{m}+\tau^{2})
up to the long-time
[

]

To further improve the temporal error for

,

[
11
,
12
]
. In addition, the unitary flow property of the time-splitting methods contributes to the time-dependent uniform error bounds for the Schrödinger equation and the improved uniform error bounds for the Schrödinger/nonlinear Schrödinger equation with the help of the

[
2
,
3

. This improves the analysis in the previous studies and obtains surprising error estimates for the time-splitting methods
[
2
,
3
]
. The aim of this paper is to establish the improved uniform error bounds on time-splitting methods for the long-time dynamics of the Dirac equation with small potentials. First, we prove a uniform error bound where the error constant grows linearly with respect to the time

and time step

2
The time-splitting methods

For simplicity, in the following sections, we focus on the Dirac equation (
1
) in 1D, i.e.,
d=1
in (
1
), for the numerical methods and corresponding analysis. The methods and results can be easily generalized to (
1
) in 2D, i.e.,
d=2
, and to the four-component Dirac equation given in Refs.
\refcite
BCJT and
\refcite
BCJY.

The Dirac equation (
1
) in 1D on the bounded computational domain
\Omega=(a,b)
with periodic boundary condition collapses to

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\Phi=(-i\sigma_{1}\partial_{x}+\sigma_{3})\Phi+\varepsilon(V(x)I_{2}-A_{1}(x)\sigma_{1})\Phi,\qquad x\in\Omega,\quad t>0,

(4)

\displaystyle\Phi(t,a)=\Phi(t,b),\quad t\geq 0;\qquad\Phi(0,x)=\Phi_{0}(x),\quad x\in\overline{\Omega},

(5)

where
\Phi:=\Phi(t,x)
and
\Phi_{0}(a)=\Phi_{0}(b)
.

2.1
The semi-discretization

Define the operators

\mathbf{T}:=-\sigma_{1}\partial_{x}-i\sigma_{3},\quad\mathbf{V}:=-i(V(x)I_{2}-A_{1}(x)\sigma_{1}),

(6)

then (
4
) can be expressed by

\partial_{t}\Phi(t,x)=(\mathbf{T}+\varepsilon\mathbf{V})\Phi(t,x),\ x\in\Omega,\ t>0.

(7)

Take a time step size
\tau>0
and denote the time grids as
t_{n}=n\tau
for
n=0,1,\ldots
. As both
\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{V}
are time-independent, the solution to (
7
) with (
5
) can be propagated through

\Phi(t_{n+1},x)=e^{\tau(\mathbf{T}+\varepsilon\mathbf{V})}\Phi(t_{n},x),\quad n=0,1,\ldots.

(8)

To approximate the operator
e^{\tau(\mathbf{T}+\varepsilon\mathbf{V})}
, we apply the second-order time-splitting (Strang splitting)
[
32
]
, which gives

e^{\tau(\mathbf{T}+\varepsilon\mathbf{V})}\approx e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\mathbf{T}}e^{\varepsilon\tau\mathbf{V}}e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\mathbf{T}}.

(9)

Therefore, the semi-discretization of the Dirac equation (
4
) via Strang splitting can be expressed as

\Phi^{[n+1]}(x)=\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(\Phi^{[n]}(x)):=e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\mathbf{T}}e^{\varepsilon\tau\mathbf{V}}e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\mathbf{T}}\Phi^{[n]}(x),\quad n=0,1,\ldots,

(10)

where we take the initial condition
\Phi^{[0]}(x):=\Phi_{0}(x)
for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
. Here,
\Phi^{[n]}(x)
is the approximation of
\Phi(t_{n},x)
.

2.2
The full-discretization

By noticing the definition of
\mathbf{V}
in (
6
), it is easy to derive that

e^{\varepsilon\tau\mathbf{V}}\Phi(t,x)=e^{-i\varepsilon\tau\left(V(x)I_{2}-A_{1}(x)\sigma_{1}\right)}\Phi(t,x),\quad x\in\overline{\Omega},\quad t>0.

(11)

On the other hand, to get
e^{\tau\mathbf{T}}\Phi(t,x)
, we can discretize (
7
) in space by the Fourier spectral method, and then it is possible to integrate the operator analytically in the phase space. We take
M+1
uniformly sampled grid points in
\overline{\Omega}
with
M
a positive even integer

x_{j}=a+jh,\quad h=\frac{b-a}{M},\quad j=0,1,\ldots,M,

(12)

and we denote the sets
X_{M}
,
Y_{M}
,
Z_{M}
as

\displaystyle X_{M}=\left\{U=(U_{0},U_{1},\ldots,U_{M})^{T}~|~U_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{2},\,j=0,1,\ldots,M,\,U_{0}=U_{M}\right\},

\displaystyle Y_{M}=Z_{M}\times Z_{M},\quad Z_{M}={\rm{span}}\{\phi_{l}(x)=e^{i\mu_{l}(x-a)},\,l\in\mathcal{T}_{M}\},

where the index set
\mathcal{T}_{M}=\{l~|~l=-M/2,-M/2+1,\ldots,M/2-1\}
, and
\mu_{l}=2\pi l/(b-a)
for
l\in\mathcal{T}_{M}
. The projection operator
P_{M}:\left(L^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{2}\rightarrow Y_{M}
is defined as

\left(P_{M}U\right)(x):=\sum_{l\in\mathcal{T}_{M}}\widehat{U}_{l}e^{i\mu_{l}(x-a)},\quad U(x)\in\left(L^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{2},

where

\widehat{U}_{l}=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}U(x)e^{-i\mu_{l}(x-a)}dx,\quad l\in\mathcal{T}_{M};

(13)

and by taking
\left(C_{\rm per}(\overline{\Omega})\right)^{2}=\left\{U\in\left(C(\overline{\Omega})\right)^{2}~|~U(a)=U(b)\right\}
, the interpolation operator
I_{M}:\left(C_{\rm per}(\overline{\Omega})\right)^{2}\rightarrow Y_{M}
or
I_{M}:X_{M}\rightarrow Y_{M}
is defined as

\left(I_{M}U\right)(x):=\sum_{l\in\mathcal{T}_{M}}\widetilde{U}_{l}e^{i\mu_{l}(x-a)},\qquad U(x)\in\left(C_{\rm per}(\overline{\Omega})\right)^{2}\quad{\rm{or}}\quad U\in X_{M},

where

\widetilde{U}_{l}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}U_{j}e^{-2ijl\pi/M},\quad l\in\mathcal{T}_{M}.

(14)

Here we take
U_{j}=U(x_{j})
if
U
is a function.

Let
\Phi_{j}^{n}
be the numerical approximation of
\Phi(t_{n},x_{j})
and denote
\Phi^{n}=\left(\Phi_{0}^{n},\Phi_{1}^{n},\ldots,\Phi_{M}^{n}\right)^{T}\in X_{M}
as the solution vector at
t=t_{n}
. Take the initial value
\Phi_{j}^{0}=\Phi_{0}(x_{j})
for
j=0,\ldots,M
, then the time-splitting Fourier pseudospectral (TSFP) method for discretizating the Dirac equation (
4
) is given as

\begin{split}&\Phi^{(1)}_{j}=\sum_{l\in\mathcal{T}_{M}}e^{-i\frac{\tau\Gamma_{l}}{2}}\widetilde{(\Phi^{n})}_{l}\;e^{i\mu_{l}(x_{j}-a)}=\sum_{l\in\mathcal{T}_{M}}Q_{l}\;e^{-i\frac{\tau D_{l}}{2}}(Q_{l})^{T}\widetilde{(\Phi^{n})}_{l}\;e^{\frac{2ijl\pi}{M}},\\
&\Phi^{(2)}_{j}=e^{-i\varepsilon\tau G(x_{j})}\Phi^{(1)}_{j}=Pe^{-i\varepsilon\Lambda_{j}}P^{T}\Phi^{(1)}_{j},\\
&\Phi^{n+1}_{j}=\sum_{l\in\mathcal{T}_{M}}e^{-i\frac{\tau\Gamma_{l}}{2}}\widetilde{(\Phi^{(2)})}_{l}\;e^{i\mu_{l}(x_{j}-a)}=\sum_{l\in\mathcal{T}_{M}}Q_{l}\;e^{-i\frac{\tau D_{l}}{2}}(Q_{l})^{T}\widetilde{(\Phi^{(2)})}_{l}\;e^{\frac{2ijl\pi}{M}},\end{split}

(15)

for
n=0,1,\ldots
, where
\Gamma_{l}=\mu_{l}\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{3}=Q_{l}D_{l}(Q_{l})^{T}
with
\delta_{l}=\sqrt{1+\mu^{2}_{l}}
,
(Q_{l})^{T}
is the transpose of
Q_{l}
and

\Gamma_{l}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\ \mu_{l}\\
\mu_{l}&\ -1\end{pmatrix},\quad Q_{l}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1+\delta_{l}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{l}(1+\delta_{l})}}&\ -\frac{\mu_{l}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{l}(1+\delta_{l})}}\\
\frac{\mu_{l}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{l}(1+\delta_{l})}}&\ \frac{1+\delta_{l}}{\sqrt{2\delta_{l}(1+\delta_{l})}}\end{pmatrix},\quad D_{l}=\begin{pmatrix}\delta_{l}&\ 0\\
0&\ -\delta_{l}\end{pmatrix},

(16)

and
G(x_{j})=V(x_{j})I_{2}-A_{1}(x_{j})\sigma_{1}=P\Lambda_{j}P^{T}
with
\Lambda_{j}=\textrm{diag}(\Lambda_{j,+},\Lambda_{j,-})
and
\Lambda_{j,\pm}=V(x_{j})\pm A_{1}(x_{j})
,
P=I_{2}
if
A_{1}(x_{j})=0
and otherwise

P=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\ &\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\ &\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

If the electromagnetic potential

3
Uniform error bounds

In this section, we prove the uniform error bounds for the second-order time-splitting method in propagating the Dirac equation with small potentials in the long-time regime up to
T_{\varepsilon}=T/\varepsilon
for any given
T>0
. We will start with the results for the semi-discretized scheme, and then extend it to the full-discretization.

3.1
For semi-discretization

Suppose there exists a positive integer
m\geq 2
, such that for the potentials, we have

\textrm{(A)}\qquad\qquad V(x)\in{W^{m,\infty}_{\rm per}}(\Omega),\quad A_{1}(x)\in{W^{m,\infty}_{\rm per}}(\Omega),

where
W^{m,\infty}_{\rm per}(\Omega):=\{u|u\in W^{m,\infty}(\Omega),\,\partial^{l}_{x}u(a)=\partial^{l}_{x}u(b),\,l=0,\,\ldots,\,m-1\}
.
In addition, we assume that the exact solution
\Phi:=\Phi(t,x)
of the Dirac equation (
4
) up to the long-time
T_{\varepsilon}=T/\varepsilon
satisfies

\textrm{(B)}\qquad\qquad\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{\varepsilon}];(H_{\rm per}^{m}(\Omega))^{2})}\lesssim 1,

where similarly,
H^{m}_{\rm per}(\Omega):=\{u|u\in H^{m}(\Omega),\,\partial^{l}_{x}u(a)=\partial^{l}_{x}u(b),\,l=0,\,\ldots,\,m-1\}
, with the equivalent
H^{m}
-norm on
H^{m}_{\rm per}(\Omega)
given as

\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{m}}=\left(\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+\mu_{l}^{2})^{m}|\widehat{\phi}_{l}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(17)

Taking into account the assumptions (A) and (B), we can easily derive

\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{\varepsilon}];(H_{\rm per}^{m-1}(\Omega))^{2})}\lesssim 1,

(18)

from the Dirac equation (
1
).
By taking the semi-discretized second-order time-splitting given by (
10
) for the Dirac equation (
7
) with the operators
\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{V}
defined in (
6
), we have the following error estimate.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Phi^{[n]}(x)
be the numerical approximation obtained from the semi-discretized second-order time-splitting (
10
) for the Dirac equation (
7
), then under the assumptions (A) and (B), for any
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, we have the uniform error estimate

\|\Phi(t_{n},x)-\Phi^{[n]}(x)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{1}\varepsilon t_{n}\tau^{2}\leq C_{1}T\tau^{2},\quad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau},

(19)

where
C_{1}
is a positive constant independent of
h,\tau,n
and
\varepsilon
.

Proof 3.2
.

We notice that
\mathbf{T}
generates a unitary group in
(H^{k}_{\rm per}(\Omega))^{2}

(k\geq 0)
. Denote the exact solution flow
\Phi(t_{n})\to\Phi(t_{n+1})
as

\Phi(t_{n+1})=\mathcal{S}_{e,\tau}(\Phi(t_{n})),\quad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau},

(20)

where we take
\Phi(t_{n}):=\Phi(t_{n},x)
for simplicity.

In order to prove the convergence, we adopt the approach via formal Lie calculus introduced in Ref.
\refcite
Lubich and split the proof into the following two steps.

Step 1
(Bounds for local truncation error). We begin with the local truncation error, i.e., to estimate the error generated by one time step computed via (
10
). By using Taylor expansion for
e^{\varepsilon\tau\mathbf{V}}
, we have

\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(\Phi_{0})=e^{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}\Phi_{0}+\varepsilon\tau e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}{\mathbf{V}}e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}\Phi_{0}+\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{2}\int^{1}_{0}(1-\theta)e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}e^{\varepsilon\theta\tau{\mathbf{V}}}{\mathbf{V}}^{2}e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}\Phi_{0}d\theta.

By Duhamel’s principle, we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{e,\tau}(\Phi_{0})=

\displaystyle\ e^{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}\Phi_{0}+\varepsilon\int^{\tau}_{0}e^{(\tau-s)\mathbf{T}}{\mathbf{V}}e^{s\mathbf{T}}\Phi_{0}ds

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{2}\int^{\tau}_{0}\int^{s}_{0}e^{(\tau-s)\mathbf{T}}{\mathbf{V}}e^{(s-w)\mathbf{T}}{\mathbf{V}}\Phi(w)dwds.

Denote

Y(s)=e^{(\tau-s)\mathbf{T}}{\mathbf{V}}e^{s\mathbf{T}}\Phi_{0},\quad B(s,w)=e^{(\tau-s)\mathbf{T}}{\mathbf{V}}e^{(s-w)\mathbf{T}}{\mathbf{V}}e^{w\mathbf{T}}\Phi_{0},

(21)

then the local truncation error can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(\Phi_{0})-\mathcal{S}_{e,\tau}(\Phi_{0})=

\displaystyle\ \varepsilon\tau Y\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)-\varepsilon\int^{\tau}_{0}Y(s)ds+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{2}}{2}B\left(\frac{\tau}{2},\frac{\tau}{2}\right)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{2}\int^{\tau}_{0}\int^{s}_{0}B(s,w)dsdw+\varepsilon^{2}R_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}R_{2},

with

\begin{split}&R_{1}=\tau^{2}\int^{1}_{0}(1-\theta)e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}e^{\varepsilon\theta\tau{\mathbf{V}}}{\mathbf{V}}^{2}e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}\Phi_{0}d\theta-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}B\left(\frac{\tau}{2},\frac{\tau}{2}\right),\\
&R_{2}=-\int^{\tau}_{0}\int^{s}_{0}e^{(\tau-s)\mathbf{T}}{\mathbf{V}}e^{(s-w)\mathbf{T}}{\mathbf{V}}\Phi(w)-B(s,w)dwds.\end{split}

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle\|R_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{2}\max_{\theta\in(0,1)}\{\|\partial_{\theta\theta}((1-\theta)e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}e^{\varepsilon\theta\tau{\mathbf{V}}}{\mathbf{V}}^{2}e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}\Phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\tau^{3}\left(\|{\mathbf{V}}^{3}\Phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\tau\|{\mathbf{V}}^{4}\Phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\lesssim\varepsilon\tau^{3}.

In view of the properties of
\mathbf{T}
and
B(s,w)
, the quadrature rule implies

\displaystyle\|R_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{3}\max_{s,w\in(0,\tau)}\{\|e^{(\tau-s){\mathbf{T}}}{\mathbf{V}}e^{(s-w)\mathbf{T}}{\mathbf{V}}\partial_{w}\Phi(w)\|_{L^{2}}\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{3}\|\partial_{s}\Phi(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\tau];({L^{2}})^{2})}\lesssim\tau^{3},

and

\left\|\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}B\left(\frac{\tau}{2},\frac{\tau}{2}\right)-\int^{\tau}_{0}\int^{s}_{0}B(s,w)dwds\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\tau^{3}\max_{0\leq w\leq s\leq\tau}(\|\partial_{s}B\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{w}B\|_{L^{2}})\lesssim\tau^{3}.

Finally, we estimate the last term, which contains the major part of the local error

\mathcal{F}(\Phi_{0}):=\varepsilon\tau{Y}(\frac{\tau}{2})-\varepsilon\int^{\tau}_{0}Y(s)ds=\varepsilon\tau^{3}\int^{1}_{0}\text{ker}(\theta){Y}^{\prime\prime}(\theta\tau)d\theta,

(22)

where
\text{ker}(\theta)
is the Peano kernel for midpoint rule. In addition, we have

Y^{\prime\prime}(s)=e^{(\tau-s)\mathbf{T}}[{\mathbf{T}},[\mathbf{T},{\mathbf{V}}]]e^{s\mathbf{T}}\Phi_{0}.

For the double commutator
[{\mathbf{T}},[{\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{V}}]]
, we have

\left\|[{\mathbf{T}},[\mathbf{T},{\mathbf{V}}]]\Psi\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left(\|V(\cdot)\|_{W^{2,\infty}}+\|A_{1}(\cdot)\|_{W^{2,\infty}}\right)\|\Psi\|_{H^{1}}.

Combining all the results above, we find the one step local error as

\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(\Phi_{0})-\mathcal{S}_{e,\tau}(\Phi_{0})=\mathcal{F}(\Phi_{0})+\mathcal{R}^{0},

(23)

where
\|\mathcal{R}^{0}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{3}
and

\mathcal{F}(\Phi_{0})=\varepsilon\tau^{3}\int^{1}_{0}\rm{ker}(\theta)e^{\tau(1-\theta){\mathbf{T}}}[{\mathbf{T}},[{\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{V}}]]e^{\tau\theta{\mathbf{T}}}\Phi_{0}d\theta.

(24)

Define the local truncation error at
t_{n}
for
0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1
as

\mathcal{E}^{n}(x)=\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(\Phi(t_{n},x))-\mathcal{S}_{e,\tau}(\Phi(t_{n},x)),\quad a\leq x\leq b,

(25)

then from the above computation, we can get

\mathcal{E}^{n}(x)=\mathcal{F}(\Phi(t_{n}))+\mathcal{R}^{n},\quad a\leq x\leq b,\quad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1,

(26)

where for
0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1
,

\|\mathcal{F}(\Phi(t_{n}))\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon\tau^{3}\|\Phi(t_{n})\|_{H^{1}},\quad\|\mathcal{R}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{3}.

(27)

Step 2
(Bounds for the global error). We are going to prove the error bound (
19
). Denote
{\bf e}^{[n]}(x)=\Phi^{[n]}-\Phi(t_{n})
, then
\|{\bf e}^{[0]}(x)\|_{L^{2}}=0
by definition. For
0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1
, we have

{\bf e}^{[n+1]}=\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(\Phi^{[n]})-\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(\Phi(t_{n}))+\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(\Phi(t_{n}))-\mathcal{S}_{e,\tau}(\Phi(t_{n})).

(28)

By the error bound (
27
) for the local truncation error, we obtain for
0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1
,

\left\|{\bf e}^{[n+1]}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|{\bf e}^{[n]}\right\|_{L^{2}}+C_{1}\varepsilon\tau^{3},

(29)

where
C_{1}>0
is a constant and independent of
h,n,\tau
and
\varepsilon
.
Then it is straightforward to derive

\left\|{\bf e}^{[n+1]}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|{\bf e}^{[0]}\right\|_{L^{2}}+C_{1}(n+1)\varepsilon\tau^{3}\leq C_{1}\varepsilon t_{n+1}\tau^{2},

(30)

which completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Remark 3.3
.

According to Theorem
3.1
, the uniform error bound on the time-splitting method for the Dirac equation grows linearly with respect to
t
. In fact, given an accuracy bound
\delta_{0}>0
, the time (for simplicity, assume
\varepsilon=1
here) for the second-order splitting method to violate the accuracy requirement
\delta_{0}
is
O(\delta_{0}/\tau^{2})
. The results can be extended to other time-splitting methods. For the first-order Lie-Trotter splitting and fourth-order compact splitting or partitioned Runge-Kutta splitting (PRK4)
[
9
,
34
]
, the times are at
O(\delta_{0}/\tau)
and
O(\delta_{0}/\tau^{4})
, respectively. In other words, higher order splitting method performs much better in the long-time simulation not only regarding the higher accuracy but also longer simulation time to produce accurate solutions. In addition, extensions to 2D/3D are straightforward.

3.2
For full-discretization

For the full-discretization given in (
15
) by the second-order time-splitting method for the Dirac equation (
7
), we could further derive the following uniform error estimate.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\Phi^{n}
be the approximation obtained from the TSFP (
15
) for the Dirac equation (
7
). Under the assumptions (A) and (B), for any
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, we have

\|\Phi(t_{n},x)-I_{M}\Phi^{n}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(t_{n})\left(h^{m}+\tau^{2}\right),\qquad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau},

(31)

where
C(t)=C_{0}+C_{1}\varepsilon t\leq C_{0}+C_{1}T
with

C_{0}
and
C_{1}
two positive constants independent of
h,\tau,n
and
\varepsilon
.

Proof.
Noticing that

I_{M}\Phi^{n}-\Phi(t_{n})=I_{M}\Phi^{n}-P_{M}\Phi(t_{n})+P_{M}\Phi(t_{n})-\Phi(t_{n}),

(32)

under the assumption (B), we get from the standard Fourier projection properties
[
31
]

\left\|I_{M}\Phi^{n}-\Phi(t_{n})\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|I_{M}\Phi^{n}-P_{M}\Phi(t_{n})\right\|_{L^{2}}+C_{2}h^{m},\ 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}.

(33)

Thus, it suffices to consider the error function
{\bf e}^{n}\in Y_{M}
at
t_{n}
as

{\bf e}^{n}:={\bf e}^{n}(x)=I_{M}\Phi^{n}-P_{M}\Phi(t_{n}),\quad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}.

(34)

Since
\Phi^{0}_{j}=\Phi_{0}(x_{j})
, it is obvious from (B) that
\|{\bf e}^{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{3}h^{m}
.
From the local truncation error (
25
), we have the error equation for
{\bf e}^{n}
(
0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1
),

\displaystyle{\bf e}^{n+1}=I_{M}\Phi^{n+1}-P_{M}\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(\Phi(t_{n}))+P_{M}(\mathcal{E}^{n}).

(35)

Recall the semi-discretization (
10
) and the full-discretization (
15
), we have

\begin{split}&I_{M}\Phi^{n+1}=e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}(I_{M}\Phi^{(2)}),\ I_{M}\Phi^{(2)}=I_{M}\left(e^{\varepsilon\tau\mathbf{V}}\Phi^{(1)}\right),\ I_{M}\Phi^{(1)}=e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}(I_{M}\Phi^{n}),\\
&P_{M}(\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(\Phi(t_{n})))=e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}(P_{M}\Phi^{\langle 2\rangle}),\ \Phi^{\langle 2\rangle}:=e^{\varepsilon\tau\mathbf{V}}\Phi^{\langle 1\rangle},\ \Phi^{\langle 1\rangle}:=e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}\Phi(t_{n}).\end{split}

In view of the facts that
I_{M}
and
P_{M}
are identical on
Y_{M}
and
e^{\tau{\mathbf{T}}/2}
preserves the
H^{k}
-norm (
k\geq 0
), using the Taylor expansion
e^{\varepsilon\tau{\mathbf{V}}}=1+\varepsilon\tau{\mathbf{V}}\int^{1}_{0}e^{\varepsilon\tau\theta{\mathbf{V}}}d\theta
and assumptions (A) and (B), we have

\displaystyle\left\|I_{M}\Phi^{n+1}-P_{M}(\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(\Phi(t_{n})))\right\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|I_{M}\Phi^{(2)}-P_{M}\Phi^{\langle 2\rangle}\right\|_{L^{2}},

(36)

\displaystyle\left\|P_{M}\Phi^{\langle 2\rangle}-I_{M}\Phi^{\langle 2\rangle}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{4}\varepsilon\tau h^{m}.

(37)

In addition, noticing
\mathbf{V}=-i(V(x)I_{2}-A_{1}(x)\sigma_{1})
, by direct computation and Parseval’s identity, we have

\displaystyle\left\|I_{M}\Phi^{(2)}-I_{M}\Phi^{\langle 2\rangle}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{h\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}\left|\Phi^{(2)}_{j}-\Phi^{\langle 2\rangle}(x_{j})\right|^{2}}=\sqrt{h\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}\left|\Phi^{(1)}_{j}-\Phi^{\langle 1\rangle}(x_{j})\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|I_{M}\Phi^{(1)}-I_{M}\Phi^{\langle 1\rangle}\right\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|I_{M}\Phi^{n}-P_{M}\Phi(t_{n})\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\bf e}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

(38)

From (
26
), (
27
) and the assumption (B), it is clear that there exists
C_{5}>0
such that
\|\mathcal{E}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{5}\varepsilon\tau^{3}
for
0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1
. Taking the
L^{2}
-norm on both sides of (
35
) and combining the above estimates, we obtain for
0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1
,

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf e}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|I_{M}\Phi^{(2)}-P_{M}\Phi^{\langle 2\rangle}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\mathcal{E}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|I_{M}\Phi^{(2)}-I_{M}\Phi^{\langle 2\rangle}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|P_{M}\Phi^{\langle 2\rangle}-I_{M}\Phi^{\langle 2\rangle}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\mathcal{E}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\bf e}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}+C_{6}\left(\varepsilon\tau h^{m}+\varepsilon\tau^{3}\right),

(39)

where
C_{6}=\max\{C_{4},C_{5}\}
.
Thus, we arrive at

\left\|{\bf e}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{6}\varepsilon t_{n+1}\left(h^{m}+\tau^{2}\right)+C_{3}h^{m},\quad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1,

(40)

which completes the proof of Theorem
3.4
by taking
C_{0}=C_{2}+C_{3}
and
C_{1}=C_{6}
.

\square

4
Improved uniform error bounds

In this section, we establish the improved uniform error bounds for the time-splitting methods applied to the Dirac equation (
4
) up to the long-time
T_{\varepsilon}
under the assumptions (A) and (B).

4.1
For semi-discretization

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Phi^{[n]}(x)
be the numerical approximation obtained from the semi-discretized second-order time-splitting (
10
) for the Dirac equation (
7
). Under the assumptions (A) and (B), for
0<\tau_{0}<1
sufficiently small and independent of
\varepsilon
, when
0<\tau<\alpha\frac{\pi(b-a)\tau_{0}}{\sqrt{\tau_{0}^{2}(b-a)^{2}+4\pi^{2}(1+\tau_{0})^{2}}}
for a fixed constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we have the following improved uniform error bound for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]

\|\Phi(t_{n},x)-\Phi^{[n]}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon\tau^{2}+\tau^{m}_{0},\quad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}.

(41)

In particular, if the exact solution is smooth, e.g.,
\Phi(t,x)\in L^{\infty}([0,T_{\varepsilon}];(H^{\infty}_{\rm per}(\Omega))^{2})
, the last term
\tau_{0}^{m}
decays exponentially fast and could be ignored practically for small enough
\tau_{0}
, where the improved uniform error bound for sufficiently small
\tau
will be

\|\Phi(t_{n},x)-\Phi^{[n]}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon\tau^{2},\quad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}.

(42)

Proof 4.2
.

From the local truncation error (
25
) and the error equation (
28
), we have

{\bf e}^{[n+1]}=\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(\Phi^{[n]})-\mathcal{S}_{\tau}(\Phi(t_{n}))+\mathcal{E}^{n}=e^{\tau\mathbf{T}}{\bf e}^{[n]}+W^{n}+\mathcal{E}^{n},\quad n\geq 0,

(43)

where
W^{n}:=W^{n}(x)
(
n=0,1,\ldots
) is given by

W^{n}(x)=\varepsilon\tau e^{\frac{\tau\mathbf{T}}{2}}{\mathbf{V}}\int^{1}_{0}e^{\varepsilon\tau\theta\mathbf{V}}d\theta\;e^{\frac{\tau\mathbf{T}}{2}}{\bf e}^{[n]}.

(44)

Under the assumption (A), we have the following estimate

\left\|W^{n}(x)\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon\tau\left\|{\bf e}^{[n]}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

(45)

Based on (
43
), we arrive at

{\bf e}^{[n+1]}=e^{(n+1)\tau\mathbf{T}}{\bf e}^{[0]}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}e^{(n-k)\tau\mathbf{T}}\left(W^{k}+\mathcal{E}^{k}\right),\quad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1.

(46)

Combining (
26
), (
27
) and (
45
), noticing
{\bf e}^{[0]}={\bm 0}
, we have the estimates for
0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1
,

\left\|{\bf e}^{[n+1]}\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon\tau^{2}+\varepsilon\tau\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left\|{\bf e}^{[k]}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}e^{(n-k)\tau\mathbf{T}}\mathcal{F}(\Phi(t_{k}))\right\|_{L^{2}}.

(47)

In order to obtain the improved uniform error bounds, we shall employ the
regularity compensation oscillation
(RCO) technique
[
2
,
3
]
to deal with the last term in the RHS of the inequality (
47
).

The key idea is a summation-by-parts procedure combined with spectrum cut-off and phase cancellation. First, we employ the spectral projection on
\Phi(t_{k})
such that only finite Fourier modes of
\Phi(t_{k})
need to be considered and the projection error could be controlled by the regularity of
\Phi(t_{k})
. Then, we apply the summation-by parts formula to the low Fourier modes such that the phase could be cancelled for small
\tau
(the terms of the type
\sum_{k=0}^{n}e^{(n-k)\tau\mathbf{T}}
) and an extra order of
\varepsilon
could be gained from the terms like
\mathcal{F}(\Phi(t_{k}))-\mathcal{F}(\Phi(t_{k+1}))
.

From the Dirac equation (
7
) and the assumption (A), we notice that
\partial_{t}\Phi(t,x)-\mathbf{T}\Phi(t,x)=O(\varepsilon)
. In order to gain an extra order of
\varepsilon
, it is natural to introduce the ‘twisted variable’ as

\Psi(t,x)=e^{-t\mathbf{T}}\Phi(t,x),\quad t\geq 0,

(48)

which satisfies the equation

\partial_{t}\Psi(t,x)=\varepsilon e^{-t\mathbf{T}}\left(\mathbf{V}e^{t\mathbf{T}}\Psi(t,x)\right),\quad t\geq 0.

(49)

Noticing
\mathbf{T}=-\sigma_{1}\partial_{x}-i\sigma_{3}
, under the assumptions (A) and (B), we can prove that

\|\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{\varepsilon}];(H_{\rm per}^{m}(\Omega))^{2})}\lesssim 1,\quad\|\partial_{t}\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{\varepsilon}];(H_{\rm per}^{m}(\Omega))^{2})}\lesssim\varepsilon

(50)

and

\left\|\Psi(t_{n+1})-\Psi(t_{n})\right\|_{H^{m}}\lesssim\varepsilon\tau,\quad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1.

(51)

The RCO technique will be used to force
\partial_{t}\Psi
to appear with the gain of order
\varepsilon
for the summation-by-parts procedure in the last term
\sum_{k=0}^{n}e^{(n-k)\tau\mathbf{T}}\mathcal{F}(\Phi(t_{k}))
, where the small
\tau
is required to control the accumulation of the frequency of the type
e^{(n-k)\tau\mathbf{T}}
.

Step 1.
As introduced in Ref.
\refcite
BCF1, we choose the cut-off parameter
\tau_{0}\in(0,1)
and
M_{0}=2\lceil 1/\tau_{0}\rceil\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
(
\lceil\cdot\rceil
is the ceiling function) with
1/\tau_{0}\leq M_{0}/2<1+1/\tau_{0}
. Under the assumptions (A) and (B), we have the following estimate

\left\|P_{M_{0}}\mathcal{F}(P_{M_{0}}\Phi(t_{n}))-\mathcal{F}(\Phi(t_{n}))\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon\tau\tau_{0}^{m}.

(52)

Based on the above estimates, (
47
) would imply for
0\leq n\leq T_{\varepsilon}/\tau-1
,

\left\|{\bf e}^{[n+1]}\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\tau_{0}^{m}+\varepsilon\tau^{2}+\varepsilon\tau\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left\|{\bf e}^{[k]}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\mathcal{L}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}},

(53)

where

\mathcal{L}^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}e^{-(k+1)\tau\mathbf{T}}P_{M_{0}}\mathcal{F}\left(e^{t_{k}\mathbf{T}}(P_{M_{0}}\Psi(t_{k}))\right).

(54)

Step 2.
Now, we concentrate on
\mathcal{L}^{n}
, which represents the summation of low Fourier modes. For
l\in\mathcal{T}_{M_{0}}
, define the index set
\mathcal{I}_{l}^{M_{0}}
associated to
l
as

\mathcal{I}_{l}^{M_{0}}=\left\{(l_{1},l_{2})\ |\ l_{1}+l_{2}=l,\ l_{1}\in\mathbb{Z},\,l_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{M_{0}}\right\}.

(55)

Following the notations in (
16
), we denote

\Pi_{l}^{+}=Q_{l}\,\text{diag}(1,0)(Q_{l})^{T},\quad\Pi_{l}^{-}=Q_{l}\,\text{diag}(0,1)(Q_{l})^{T},

(56)

where
\Pi_{l}^{\pm}
are the projectors onto the eigenspaces of
\Gamma_{l}
corresponding to the eigenvalues
\pm\delta_{l}
, respectively. Moreover, we have
(\Pi_{l}^{\pm})^{T}=\Pi_{l}^{\pm}
,
\Pi_{l}^{+}+\Pi_{l}^{-}=I_{2}
,
(\Pi_{l}^{\pm})^{2}=\Pi_{l}^{\pm}
,
\Pi_{l}^{\pm}\Pi_{l}^{\mp}={\bf 0}
.
By direct computation, we have

e^{t{\bm T}}P_{M_{0}}\Psi(t_{k})=\sum\limits_{l\in\mathcal{T}_{M_{0}}}\left(e^{-it\delta_{l}}\Pi_{l}^{+}+e^{it\delta_{l}}\Pi_{l}^{-}\right)\widehat{\Psi}_{l}(t_{k})e^{i\mu_{l}(x-a)}.

(57)

According to the definition of
\mathcal{F}
in (
22
), the expansion below follows

\displaystyle e^{-(k+1)\tau{\bm T}}P_{M_{0}}\left(e^{(\tau-s)\mathbf{T}}\mathbf{V}e^{(t_{k}+s)\mathbf{T}}P_{M_{0}}\Psi(t_{k}))\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{l\in\mathcal{T}_{M_{0}}}\sum\limits_{(l_{1},l_{2})\in\mathcal{I}_{l}^{M_{0}}}\sum\limits_{\nu_{j}=\pm,j=1,2}\mathcal{G}_{k,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(s)e^{i\mu_{l}(x-a)},

(58)

where
\mathcal{G}_{k,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(s)
is a function of
s
defined as

\mathcal{G}_{k,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(s)=e^{i(t_{k}+s)\delta^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}_{l,l_{2}}}\Pi_{l}^{\nu_{1}}\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_{l_{1}}\Pi_{l_{2}}^{\nu_{2}}\widehat{\Psi}_{l_{2}}(t_{k}),

(59)

with
\delta_{l,l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}=\nu_{1}\delta_{l}-\nu_{2}\delta_{l_{2}}
.
Then, the remainder term
\mathcal{L}^{n}
in (
53
) reads

\mathcal{L}^{n}=\varepsilon\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\sum\limits_{l\in\mathcal{T}_{M_{0}}}\sum\limits_{(l_{1},l_{2})\in\mathcal{I}_{l}^{M_{0}}}\sum\limits_{\nu_{j}=\pm,j=1,2}\lambda_{k,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}e^{i\mu_{l}(x-a)},

(60)

where the coefficients
\lambda_{k,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}
given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{k,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}

\displaystyle=\tau\mathcal{G}_{k,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(\tau/2)-\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{G}_{k,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=r_{l,l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}e^{it_{k}\delta^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}_{l,l_{2}}}c_{k,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}},

(61)

and

\displaystyle c_{k,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}=\Pi_{l}^{\nu_{1}}\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_{l_{1}}\Pi_{l_{2}}^{\nu_{2}}\widehat{\Psi}_{l_{2}}(t_{k}),

(62)

\displaystyle r_{l,l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}=\tau e^{i\tau\delta^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}_{l,l_{2}}/2}-\int^{\tau}_{0}e^{is\delta^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}_{l,l_{2}}}\;ds=O(\tau^{3}(\delta^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}_{l,l_{2}})^{2}).

(63)

We only need to consider the case
\delta^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}_{l,l_{2}}\neq 0
as
r_{l,l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}=0
if
\delta^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}_{l,l_{2}}=0
. For
l\in\mathcal{T}_{M_{0}}
and
(l_{1},l_{2})\in\mathcal{I}_{l}^{M_{0}}
, we have

|\delta^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}_{l,l_{2}}|\leq 2\delta_{M_{0}/2}=2\sqrt{1+\mu_{{M_{0}}/2}^{2}}<2\sqrt{1+\frac{4\pi^{2}(1+\tau_{0})^{2}}{\tau_{0}^{2}(b-a)^{2}}},

(64)

which implies for
0<\tau\leq\alpha\frac{\pi(b-a)\tau_{0}}{\sqrt{\tau_{0}^{2}(b-a)^{2}+4\pi^{2}(1+\tau_{0})^{2}}}
with
0<\alpha,\tau_{0}<1
, there holds
\frac{\tau}{2}|\delta_{l,l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}|\leq\alpha\pi
.
Denoting
S^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}_{n,l,l_{2}}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}e^{it_{k}\delta_{l,l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}}
(
n\geq 0
), for
0<\tau\leq\alpha\frac{\pi(b-a)\tau_{0}}{\sqrt{\tau_{0}^{2}(b-a)^{2}+4\pi^{2}(1+\tau_{0})^{2}}}
, we obtain

|S^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}_{n,l,l_{2}}|\leq\frac{1}{|\sin(\tau\delta_{l,l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}/2)|}\leq\frac{C}{\tau|\delta_{l,l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}|},\quad C=\frac{2\alpha\pi}{\sin(\alpha\pi)},\quad\forall n\geq 0.

(65)

Using the summation-by-parts, we find that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lambda_{k,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}=

\displaystyle\ r_{l,l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}S^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}_{k,l,l_{2}}(c_{k,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}-c_{k+1,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}})+S_{n,l,l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}c_{n,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}\right],

(66)

with

c_{k,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}-c_{k+1,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}=\Pi_{l}^{\nu_{1}}\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_{l_{1}}\Pi_{l_{2}}^{\nu_{2}}\left(\widehat{\Psi}_{l_{2}}(t_{k})-\widehat{\Psi}_{l_{2}}(t_{k+1})\right).

(67)

Combining (
63
), (
65
), (
66
), and (
67
), we have

\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lambda_{k,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}\right|\lesssim\tau^{2}|\delta_{l,l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}|\left|\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_{l_{1}}\right|\left[\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\left|\widehat{\Psi}_{l_{2}}(t_{k})-\widehat{\Psi}_{l_{2}}(t_{k+1})\right|+\left|\widehat{\Psi}_{l_{2}}(t_{n})\right|\right].

(68)

Step 3.
Now, we are ready to give the improved estimates. For
l\in\mathcal{T}_{M_{0}}
and
(l_{1},l_{2})\in\mathcal{I}_{l}^{M_{0}}
, simple calculations show (
l=l_{1}+l_{2}
)

|\delta_{l,l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}|\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{2}(1+\mu_{l_{j}}^{2})^{1/2}.

(69)

Based on (
60
), (
68
) and (
69
), using Cauchy inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{L}^{n}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

(70)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\sum\limits_{l\in\mathcal{T}_{N_{0}}}\big|\sum\limits_{(l_{1},l_{2})\in\mathcal{I}_{l}^{N_{0}}}\sum\limits_{\nu_{j}=\pm,j=1,2}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\lambda_{k,l,l_{1},l_{2}}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{4}\bigg\{\sum_{l\in\mathcal{T}_{M_{0}}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{(l_{1},l_{2})\in\mathcal{I}_{l}^{N_{0}}}\left|\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_{l_{1}}\right|\left|\widehat{\Psi}_{l_{2}}(t_{n})\right|\prod_{j=1}^{2}(1+\mu_{l_{j}}^{2})^{1/2}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+n\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigg[\sum_{l\in\mathcal{T}_{M_{0}}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{(l_{1},l_{2})\in\mathcal{I}_{l}^{N_{0}}}\left|\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_{l_{1}}\right|\left|\widehat{\Psi}_{l_{2}}(t_{k})-\widehat{\Psi}_{l_{2}}(t_{k+1})\right|\prod_{j=1}^{2}(1+\mu_{l_{j}}^{2})^{1/2}\bigg)^{2}\bigg]\bigg\}.

In order to estimate each term in the above inequality, we use the auxiliary function
\Theta(x)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+\mu_{l}^{2})^{1/2}\left|\widehat{\Psi}_{l}(t_{n})\right|e^{i\mu_{l}(x-a)}
, where
\Theta(x)\in\left(H_{\rm per}^{m-1}(\Omega)\right)^{2}
which can be verified from the assumption (B) and we can prove
\|\Theta(x)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|\Psi(t_{n})\|_{H^{s+1}}
(
s\leq m-1
). Similarly, introduce the function
\mathbf{U}(x)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+\mu_{l}^{2})^{1/2}\left|\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_{l}\right|e^{i\mu_{l}(x-a)}
, where
\mathbf{U}(x)\in{W^{m-1,\infty}_{\rm per}}(\Omega)
as can be derived from the assumption (A). We can prove directly that
\|\mathbf{U}(x)\|_{W^{0,\infty}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{V}(x)\|_{W^{2,\infty}}
. Expanding

\mathbf{U}(x)\Theta(x)=\sum\limits_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{l_{1}+l_{2}=l}\prod_{j=1}^{2}(1+\mu_{l_{j}}^{2})^{1/2}\left|\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_{l_{1}}\right|\left|\widehat{\Psi}_{l_{2}}(t_{n})\right|e^{i\mu_{l}(x-a)},

we can obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\mathcal{T}_{M_{0}}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{(l_{1},l_{2})\in\mathcal{I}_{l}^{M_{0}}}\left|\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_{l_{1}}\right|\left|\widehat{\Psi}_{l_{2}}(t_{n})\right|\prod_{j=1}^{2}(1+\mu_{l_{j}}^{2})^{1/2}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\ \|\mathbf{U}(x)\Theta(x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{V}(x)\|_{W^{2,\infty}}^{2}\|\Psi(t_{n})\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

(71)

which together with the assumption (A), (
50
) and (
51
) gives

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{L}^{n}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{4}\|\mathbf{V}(x)\|_{W^{2,\infty}}^{2}\bigg(\|\Psi(t_{n})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+n\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\|\Psi(t_{k})-\Psi(t_{k+1})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{4}+n^{2}\varepsilon^{4}\tau^{6}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{4}.

(72)

for
0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1
, where the same trick is applied to the rest terms. Combining (
53
) and (
72
), we have

\|{\bf e}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\tau_{0}^{m}+\varepsilon\tau^{2}+\varepsilon\tau\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\|{\bf e}^{k}\|_{L^{2}},\quad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1.

(73)

Discrete Gronwall’s inequality would yield
\|{\bf e}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon\tau^{2}+\tau_{0}^{m}

(0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1)
, and the proof for the improved uniform error bound (
41
) in Theorem
42
is completed.

Remark 4.3
.

\tau_{0}\in(0,1)
is a parameter introduced in analysis and the requirement on
\tau
(essentially
\tau\lesssim\tau_{0}
) enables the improved estimates on the low Fourier modes
|l|\leq 1/\tau_{0}
, where the error constant depends on
\alpha
.
\tau_{0}
can be arbitrary as long as the assumed relation between
\tau
and
\tau_{0}
holds, i.e.
\tau_{0}
could be either fixed, or dependent on
\tau
, e.g.
\tau_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{16\pi^{2}+(b-a)^{2}}}{\alpha(b-a)\pi}\tau
.

Remark 4.4
.

The improved uniform error bounds are established for the second-order Strang splitting method. Under appropriate assumptions of the exact solution, the improved uniform error bounds can be extended to the first-order Lie-Trotter splitting and the fourth-order PRK splitting method with improved uniform error bounds at
\varepsilon\tau
and
\varepsilon\tau^{4}
, respectively.

4.2
For full-discretization

For the TSFP method (
15
), we could establish the following improved uniform error bounds.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\Phi^{n}
be the approximation obtained from the TSFP (
15
) for the Dirac equation (
7
). Under the assumptions (A) and (B), for
0<\tau_{0}<1
sufficiently small and independent of
\varepsilon
, when
0<\tau\leq\alpha\frac{\pi(b-a)\tau_{0}}{\sqrt{\tau_{0}^{2}(b-a)^{2}+4\pi^{2}(1+\tau_{0})^{2}}}
for a fixed constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we have

\left\|\Phi(t_{n},x)-I_{M}\Phi^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim h^{m}+\varepsilon\tau^{2}+\tau^{m}_{0},\quad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}

(74)

for any
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
. In particular, if the exact solution is smooth, e.g.,
\Phi(t,x)\in L^{\infty}([0,T_{\varepsilon}];(H^{\infty}_{\rm per}(\Omega))^{2})
, the improved uniform error bounds for sufficiently small
\tau
will be

\left\|\Phi(t_{n},x)-I_{M}\Phi^{n}\right\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim h^{m}+\varepsilon\tau^{2},\quad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}.

(75)

Proof 4.6
.

From the error estimates in previous sections, we have for
0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}
,

\left\|\Phi(t_{n},x)-\Phi^{[n]}\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon\tau^{2}+\tau^{m}_{0},\quad\left\|\Phi^{[n]}-P_{M}\Phi^{[n]}\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim h^{m}.

(76)

Since
\Phi(t_{n},x)-I_{M}\Phi^{n}=\Phi(t_{n},x)-\Phi^{[n]}+\Phi^{[n]}-P_{M}\Phi^{[n]}+P_{M}\Phi^{[n]}-I_{M}\Phi^{n}
, we derive

\left\|\Phi(t_{n},x)-I_{M}\Phi^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|P_{M}\Phi^{[n]}-I_{M}\Phi^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}+C_{1}\left(h^{m}+\varepsilon\tau^{2}+\tau^{m}_{0}\right),

(77)

where
C_{1}
is a constant independent of
h
,
\tau
,
n
,
\varepsilon
and
\tau_{0}
. As a result, it remains to establish the estimates on the error function
{\widetilde{\bf e}}^{n}:={\widetilde{\bf e}}^{n}(x)\in Y_{M}
given as

{\widetilde{\bf e}}^{n}(x):=P_{M}\Phi^{[n]}-I_{M}\Phi^{n},\quad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}.

From (
15
) and (
10
), we get

\displaystyle I_{M}\Phi^{n+1}=e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}\left(I_{M}\left(e^{\varepsilon\tau\mathbf{V}}e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}(I_{M}\Phi^{n})\right)\right),

\displaystyle P_{M}\Phi^{[n+1]}=e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}\left(P_{M}\left(e^{\varepsilon\tau\mathbf{V}}e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}(P_{M}\Phi^{[n]})\right)\right),

which lead to

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bf e}^{n+1}=e^{\tau\mathbf{T}}\widetilde{\bf e}^{n}+W^{n}(x),

(78)

where

W^{n}(x)=e^{\frac{\tau\mathbf{T}}{2}}\left[P_{M}\left((e^{\varepsilon\tau\mathbf{V}}-1)e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}(P_{M}\Phi^{[n]})\right)-I_{M}\left((e^{\varepsilon\tau\mathbf{V}}-1)e^{\frac{\tau{\mathbf{T}}}{2}}(I_{M}\Phi^{n})\right)\right].

Similar to the error estimates in Ref.
\refcite
BCJY, we have the following error bounds

\left\|W^{n}(x)\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon\tau\left(h^{m}+\left\|\widetilde{\bf e}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right),

(79)

Thus, we could obtain

\left\|\widetilde{\bf e}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|\widetilde{\bf e}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}+C_{2}\varepsilon\tau\left(h^{m}+\left\|\widetilde{\bf e}^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right),\quad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1,

(80)

where
C_{2}
is a constant independent of
h
,
\tau
,
n
and
\varepsilon
. Since
\widetilde{\bf e}^{0}=P_{M}\Phi_{0}-I_{M}\Phi_{0}
, we have
\left\|\widetilde{\bf e}^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim h^{m}
and discrete Gronwall’s inequality implies
\left\|\widetilde{\bf e}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim h^{m}
for
0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau}-1
. Combining the above estimates with (
77
), we derive

\left\|\Phi(t_{n},x)-I_{M}\Phi^{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim h^{m}+\varepsilon\tau^{2}+\tau^{m}_{0},\quad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T/\varepsilon}{\tau},

which shows the improved uniform error bound (
74
) and the proof for Theorem
75
is completed.

6
Conclusions

Improved uniform error bounds for the time-splitting methods for the long-time dynamics of the Dirac equation with small electromagnetic potentials were rigorously established. With the help of the unitary property of the solution flow in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, the linear growth of the uniform error bound for the time-splitting methods was strictly proven. By employing the regularity compensation oscillation (RCO) technique, the improved uniform error bounds were proved to be
O(\varepsilon\tau^{2})
and
O(h^{m}+\varepsilon\tau^{2})
up to the long-time at
O(1/\varepsilon)
for the semi-discretization and full-discretization, respectively. Numerical results were shown to validate our error bounds and to demonstrate that they are sharp. Finally, comparisons of different time discretizations were presented to illustrate the superior property of the time-splitting methods for the numerical simulation of the long-time dynamics of the Dirac equation.

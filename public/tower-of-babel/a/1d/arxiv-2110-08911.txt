Divisibility conditions on the order of the reductions of algebraic numbers
Pietro Sgobba

Abstract
. Let K K be a number field, and let G G be a finitely generated subgroup of K × K^{\times} . Without relying on the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis we prove an asymptotic formula for the number of primes 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} of K K such that the order of ( G mod 𝔭 ) (G\bmod\mathfrak{p}) is divisible by a fixed integer. We also provide a rational expression for the natural density of this set. Furthermore, we study the primes 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} for which the order is k k -free, and those for which the order has a prescribed ℓ \ell -adic valuation for finitely many primes ℓ \ell . An additional condition on the Frobenius conjugacy class of 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} may be considered. In order to establish these results, we prove an unconditional version of the Chebotarev density theorem for Kummer extensions of number fields.

1.
Introduction

Consider a number field
K
and let
G
be a finitely generated subgroup of
K^{\times}
. If
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime of
K
such that
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(g)=0
for all
g\in G
, then the reduction
(G\bmod\mathfrak{p})
is a well-defined subgroup of
k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
, where
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the residue field at
\mathfrak{p}
and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}
the
\mathfrak{p}
-adic valuation over
K
. In this paper we investigate the set consisting of the primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
such that the order of
(G\bmod\mathfrak{p})
is well-defined and it satisfies some divisibility conditions.

More precisely, denote by
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)
the order of
(G\bmod\mathfrak{p})
. In Theorem
1
we prove an asymptotic formula for the number of primes
\mathfrak{p}
such that
m\mid\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)
, where
m
is any given positive integer. We also consider the primes
\mathfrak{p}
such that
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)
is
k
-free, i.e. it is not divisible by
k
-th powers (greater than
1
), where
k\geqslant 2
. In Theorem
2
, relying on the previous result, we prove an asymptotic formula for the number of primes satisfying this condition.
Given a finite Galois extension of
K
, an additional condition on the conjugacy class of the Frobenius automorphisms of the primes lying above
\mathfrak{p}
may also be considered.
Notice that in this paper we do not rely on the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH). In fact, in Theorem
10
, which will be essential in the proof of Theorem
1
, we give an unconditional version of the Chebotarev density theorem for cyclotomic-Kummer extensions of number fields.

The mathematical questions addressed in this paper are closely related to Artin’s Conjecture on primitive roots, and hence are part of an active research area; see Moree’s survey
[
7
]
. The density of rational primes
p
such that
m\mid\ord_{p}(g)
, where
g\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\setminus\{\pm 1\}
, has been recently studied by Pappalardi
[
10
,
11
]
(also replacing
g
with a group of rational numbers), by Moree
[
6
]
, and previously by Wiertelak
[
23
]
. Our paper provides generalizations and variants of various results by Pappalardi and Moree, as described in the next sections. Over a number field, Debry and Perucca considered the density of the primes
\mathfrak{p}
such that
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)
, where
G
is a group consisting of algebraic numbers, is not divisible by some fixed prime number (and described how this permits to treat general divisibility conditions); see
[
2
,
13
]
. Under the assumption of GRH, more general results over number fields hold, e.g. for
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)
satisfying a given modular congruence; see
[
24
]
by Ziegler and
[
14
]
by Perucca and the author. For more references and historical background we refer to
[
7
, Sect. 9.2 and 9.3]
.

1.1.
Notation

If
m\geqslant 1
is an integer, then we denote by
\zeta_{m}
a primitive
m
-th root of unity, and by
m^{\infty}
the supernatural number
\prod_{p\mid m\text{ prime}}p^{\infty}
. As customary,
\mu
is the Möbius function.

We fix an algebraic closure
\overline{K}
of
K
.
For
m,n\geqslant 1
with
n\mid m
, we write
K_{m,n}:=K(\zeta_{m},G^{1/n})
for the
n
-th Kummer extension related to
G
over
K(\zeta_{m})
, i.e. the subextension of
\overline{K}/K(\zeta_{m})
obtained by adding the
n
-th roots of all elements in
G
. If
F/K
is a finite Galois extension, and
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime of
K
which does not ramify in
F
, then we denote by
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)
the conjugacy class of the Frobenius elements at the primes of
F
above
\mathfrak{p}
. If
\mathcal{S}
is a set of primes of
K
, then we let
\pi_{\mathcal{S}}(x)
be the number of primes in
\mathcal{S}
with norm up to
x
.

1.2.
Outline of the main results

The main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
F/K
be a finite Galois extension of number fields, and let
C
be a conjugacy-stable subset of
\Gal(F/K)
. Let
G
be a finitely generated and torsion-free subgroup of
K^{\times}
of positive rank
r
. Let
m
be a positive integer, and consider the set of primes of
K
given by

\mathcal{P}_{m}=\left\{\mathfrak{p}:m\mid\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(G),\,\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{p}\\
F/K\end{pmatrix}\subseteq C\right\}

(where we are tacitly discarding the finitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
that ramify in
F
or such that
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(g)\neq 0
for some
g\in G
).
Then we have

(1)

\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{m}}(x)=\frac{x}{\log x}\varrho_{C,m}+O\left(x\bigg(\frac{(\log\log x)^{2}}{\log x}\bigg)^{1+\frac{1}{r+2}}\right),

where

(2)

\varrho_{C,m}:=\sum_{n\mid m^{\infty}}\,\sum_{d\mid m}\frac{\mu(d)c(mn,dn)}{[F_{mn,dn}:K]}

and where, for all positive integers
a,b
with
b\mid a
, we set

(3)

c(a,b):=|C\cap\Gal(F/F\cap K_{a,b})|.

The constant implied by the
O
-term depends only on
F
,
K
,
G
.

The assumption that
G
is torsion-free allows some simplifications in the proofs, and in Remark
16
we explain how to deal with the general case. Also notice that the series in (
2
) is convergent by Proposition
15
. The coefficient
c(a,b)
in (
3
) is always at most
\left\lvert C\right\rvert\leqslant[F:K]
, and it is equal to
1
if the condition on the Frobenius is trivial.

For the case
F=K=\mathbb{Q}
, Theorem
1
provides a new version of
[
11
, Theorem 1]
and
[
6
, Lemma 1]
. The main challenge for the generalization of Pappalardi’s method consists in proving a certain unconditional version of the Chebotarev density theorem for cyclotomic-Kummer extensions of number fields, as mentioned above. In Section
2
we will argue that this is not difficult if the base field
K
is normal over
\mathbb{Q}
. However, for the general case we need an improvement on the upper bound of a possible Landau–Siegel zero of the Dedekind zeta function of
K_{m,n}
, which is achieved in Proposition
7
. Notice that Section
2
is self-contained and also of independent interest.

Section
3
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1
, whereas in Section
4
we justify that the natural density
\varrho_{C,m}
is a positive rational number, and for
C=\Gal(F/K)
(e.g. if
F=K
) we express it in terms of finite sums and products, see Theorem
18
.

Sections
5
and
6
are devoted to proving applications of Theorem
1
. In Section
5
we apply Theorem
1
to prove the following result on the primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
for which
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)
is
k
-free.

Theorem 2
.

Let
F/K
be a finite Galois extension of number fields, and let
C
be a conjugacy-stable subset of
\Gal(F/K)
. Let
G
be a finitely generated and torsion-free subgroup of
K^{\times}
of positive rank
r
. Let
k\geqslant 2
be an integer, and consider the following set of primes of
K
:

\mathcal{N}_{k}:=\left\{\mathfrak{p}:\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)\text{ is $k$-free},\,\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{p}\\
F/K\end{pmatrix}\subseteq C\right\}

(where we are tacitly discarding the primes
\mathfrak{p}
that ramify in
F
or such that
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(g)\neq 0
for some
g\in G
). Then we have

(4)

\pi_{\mathcal{N}_{k}}(x)=\frac{x}{\log x}\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\mu(m)\varrho_{C,m^{k}}+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{1+\frac{k-1}{(r+2)k+1}}}\right),

where
\varrho_{C,m^{k}}
is as in (
2
). The set
\mathcal{N}_{k}
has natural density

\beta_{C,k}:=\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\,\sum_{n\mid m^{\infty}}\,\sum_{d\mid m}\frac{\mu(m)\mu(d)c(nm^{k},dn)}{[F_{nm^{k},dn}:K]},

where
c(a,b)
is as in (
3
). The constant implied by the
O
-term depends only on
F
,
K
,
G
.

Notice that the convergence of the series
\beta_{C,k}
follows from Proposition
15
.
For the case
F=K=\mathbb{Q}
we refer to Pappalardi
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
11
, Remark (8), p.388]
.
In Section
6
, from Theorem
1
we also derive Theorem
21
, which concerns the primes
\mathfrak{p}
for which the
\ell
-adic valuation of
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)
has a prescribed value for finitely many prime numbers
\ell
.

In Section
7
, under GRH, we derive some improvements on the error terms of formulas (
1
) and (
4
). Finally, in Section
8
we provide several numerical examples for the densities considered in this paper.

2.
Chebotarev’s density theorem for cyclotomic-Kummer extensions

In this section we prove an effective version of the Chebotarev density theorem for cyclotomic-Kummer extensions of number fields which is “unconditional”, i.e. it does not rely on GRH. Let us first introduce some notation (in addition to the notation of Section
1.1
).

2.1.
Notation

Given a finite Galois extension
L/K
of number fields and a conju-gacy-stable subset
C
of
\Gal(L/K)
, we denote by
\pi_{L/K,C}(x)
the number of primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
with
\N\mathfrak{p}\leqslant x
which are unramified in
L
and such that
(\mathfrak{p},L/K)\subseteq C
.

Also,
d_{K}
denotes the absolute discriminant of
K
,
\mathcal{O}_{K}
the ring of integers of
K
, and
\zeta_{K}
the Dedekind zeta function of
K
.
For a finitely generated subgroup
G
of
K^{\times}
,
\mathcal{P}(G)
is the set of primes
\mathfrak{p}
in
K
such that
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(g)\neq 0
for some
g\in G
(notice that this set is finite).

Also, we use the following standard notation:
\varphi
is Euler’s totient function; given
m\geqslant 1
,
\tau(m)
is the number of positive divisors of
m
, and
\rad(m)
is the radical of
m
, i.e. the largest squarefree integer dividing
m
;
\Li(x)=\int_{2}^{x}\frac{dx}{\log x}
is the logarithmic integral function.

2.2.
Chebotarev’s density theorem

We start by stating a recent effective and unconditional version of Chebotarev’s density theorem by Thorner and Zaman
[
22
]
. This result refines previous versions by Lagarias and Odlyzko
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
, Serre
[
19
, Theorem 2]
, and V.K. Murty
[
8
, Section 4]
.

Theorem 3

(
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
L/K
be a finite Galois extension of number fields with
L\neq\mathbb{Q}
, and let
C
be a conjugacy-stable subset of
\Gal(L/K)
. There exist absolute and effective constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that, if

(5)

x\geqslant\big(\left\lvert d_{L}\right\rvert\cdot[L:\mathbb{Q}]^{[L:\mathbb{Q}]}\big)^{c_{1}},

then
\pi_{L/K,C}(x)
is given by

(6)

\frac{\left\lvert C\right\rvert}{[L:K]}\big(\Li(x)-\theta\Li(x^{\beta})\big)\left(1+O\left(e^{-\sqrt{\frac{c_{2}\log x}{[L:\mathbb{Q}]}}}+e^{-\frac{c_{2}\log x}{\log(\left\lvert d_{L}\right\rvert[L:\mathbb{Q}]^{[L:\mathbb{Q}]})}}\right)\right)

where
\theta=\theta(C)\in\{-1,0,1\}
, and
\beta
is a possible Landau–Siegel zero of the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{L}(s)
, i.e. the unique real zero in the range

1-\frac{1}{4\log|d_{L}|}\leqslant\alpha\leqslant 1

(if it exists, otherwise
\theta=0
).

In the formula that we aim to derive, the term
\frac{|C|}{[L:K]}\Li(x)
should be dominant.
Thus the difficulty in applying Theorem
3
to cyclotomic-Kummer extensions consists in estimating the term
\Li(x^{\beta})
, and hence bounding the value of
\beta
. As of today, the best known bound on
\beta
is provided by Stark in
[
20
, Proof of Theorem 1’, p.148]
. In fact, if
K/\mathbb{Q}
is normal, then that bound is good enough for our purpose, see Remark
8
. However, for the general case we need to deduce from Stark’s results some improvement which is suitable for our goal.

2.3.
On a possible Landau–Siegel zero of the Dedekind zeta function

Lemma 4

(
[
20
, Lemma 3]
)
.

Let
L\neq\mathbb{Q}
be a number field. The Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{L}(s)
has at most one zero in the region

(7)

\left\{s\in\mathbb{C}:1-\frac{1}{4\log\left\lvert d_{L}\right\rvert}\leqslant\re(s)<1\text{ and }\left\lvert\im(s)\right\rvert\leqslant\frac{1}{4\log\left\lvert d_{L}\right\rvert}\right\}.

If such a zero exists, then it is real and simple.

Lemma 5

(
[
20
, Lemma 8]
)
.

Let
L\neq\mathbb{Q}
be a number field, and set
c_{0}(L)=4
if
L/\mathbb{Q}
is normal,
c_{0}(L)=4\cdot[L:\mathbb{Q}]!
otherwise. If
\zeta_{L}
has a real zero
\beta
such that
1-(c_{0}(L)\cdot\log|d_{L}|)^{-1}\leqslant\beta<1
, then there is a quadratic number field
M
inside
L
such that
\zeta_{M}(\beta)=0
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
L/K
be a normal extension of number fields with
L\neq\mathbb{Q}
. If
\zeta_{L}
has a real zero
\beta
such that

(8)

1-\frac{1}{4(2[K:\mathbb{Q}])!\cdot\log\left\lvert d_{L}\right\rvert}\leqslant\beta<1,

then there is a quadratic number field
M
inside
L
such that
\zeta_{M}(\beta)=0
.

The Lemma says, in particular, that if
L
has no quadratic subfields, then
\zeta_{L}(s)
has no real zero in the range (
8
).

Proof.

If
K=\mathbb{Q}
, then the statement holds by Lemma
5
, hence we suppose
K\neq\mathbb{Q}
. If
\zeta_{L}(s)
has a zero with real part lying in the range (
8
), then by Lemma
4
it must be real, simple, and unique in that range. Since
L/K
is normal, by
[
20
, Theorem 3]
there is a subextension
F
of
L/K
, which is either trivial or quadratic over
K
, such that
\zeta_{E}(\beta)=0
for every field
F\subseteq E\subseteq L
. Therefore we have that
\zeta_{K}(\beta)=0
or
\zeta_{F}(\beta)=0
for some quadratic extension
F/K
with
F\subseteq L
. We conclude by applying Lemma
5
either to
K
or
F
, noticing that the range of the lemma contains the interval (
8
).
∎

Proposition 7
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields with
L\neq\mathbb{Q}
. Then the possible unique zero
\beta
of the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{L}(s)
in the region (
7
) is real and simple, and we have

(9)

\frac{1}{2}\leqslant\beta\leqslant\max\left\{1-\frac{1}{4(2[K:\mathbb{Q}])!\log\left\lvert d_{L}\right\rvert},1-\frac{1}{c_{3}\left\lvert d_{L}\right\rvert^{1/[L:\mathbb{Q}]}}\right\},

where
c_{3}>0
is an effective absolute constant.

Proof.

Clearly
\beta\geqslant 1/2
as
4\log\left\lvert d_{L}\right\rvert\geqslant 2
. It suffices to show that if
\beta
is in the range (
8
), then
\beta
satisfies (
9
). We follow the same argument as in
[
20
, Proof of Theorem 1’, p.148]
. If
L
has no quadratic subfields, then, as mentioned above,
\zeta_{L}(s)
has no real zero in the range (
8
) by Lemma
6
and hence (
9
) is satisfied. If
L
contains a quadratic field and
\zeta_{L}(\beta)=0
for some
\beta
in the range (
8
), then by Lemma
6
there must be a quadratic subfield
M
of
L
such that
\zeta_{M}(\beta)=0
. By
[
20
, Lemma 11]
we must have
\beta<1-(c_{3}\left\lvert d_{M}\right\rvert^{1/2})^{-1}
, for an effective absolute constant
c_{3}>0
. We may conclude because we have
\left\lvert d_{L}\right\rvert\geqslant\left\lvert d_{M}\right\rvert^{[L:\mathbb{Q}]/2}
.
∎

Remark 8
.

In fact, if in the proof of Lemma
6
we have
\zeta_{K}(\beta)=0
, then the lower bound of (
8
) may be taken as
1-(4[K:\mathbb{Q}]!\log\left\lvert d_{L}\right\rvert)^{-1}
. Moreover, if
L
is normal over
\mathbb{Q}
or if there is a tower of normal extensions
\mathbb{Q}=k_{0}\subset k_{1}\subset\cdots\subset k_{m}=K
, then the lower bound of (
8
) may be taken to be
1-(4\log\left\lvert d_{L}\right\rvert)^{-1}
by Lemma
5
, or
1-(16\log\left\lvert d_{L}\right\rvert)^{-1}
by
[
20
, Lemma 10]
, respectively. Accordingly, the factor
4(2[K:\mathbb{Q}])!
in (
9
) can be replaced by
4
or
16
in the respective cases, by following the same argument of the proof of Proposition
7
.

2.4.
Chebotarev’s density theorem for cyclotomic-Kummer extensions

Proposition 9
.

Let
K
be a number field, and let
G
be a finitely generated and torsion-free subgroup of
K^{\times}
of positive rank
r
. Then, for
m,n\geqslant 1
with
n\mid m
, we have

\frac{\log\left\lvert d_{K_{m,n}}\right\rvert}{[K_{m,n}:\mathbb{Q}]}\leqslant\log(\varphi(m)mn^{r})+\log\big(\sigma_{G}\left\lvert d_{K}\right\rvert\big),

where
\sigma_{G}
is the product of the rational primes lying below the primes in
\mathcal{P}(G)
.

Proof.

Given a finite extension
L/K
we write
d_{L/K}
for the relative discriminant. We have

(10)

d_{K_{m,n/\mathbb{Q}}}=\N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(d_{K_{m,n}/K})\cdot d_{K/\mathbb{Q}}^{[K_{m,n}:K]}\,,

see
[
9
, Ch.III, Corollary 2.10]
. By
[
19
, Proposition 5]
, since
K_{m,n}/K
is Galois, we have

(11)

\log\left\lvert\N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(d_{K_{m,n}/K})\right\rvert\leqslant[K_{m,n}:\mathbb{Q}]\bigg(\log[K_{m,n}:K]+\sum_{p\in P(K_{m,n}/K)}\log p\bigg)

where
P(K_{m,n}/K)
is the set of rational primes
p
lying below the primes of
K
that ramify in
K_{m,n}
. These prime numbers divide
m
or
\sigma_{G}
, as they lie below the primes
\mathfrak{p}
that divide
d_{K_{m,n}/K}
, and an estimate for this relative discriminant is
[
14
, Formula (4.7)]
:

d_{K_{m,n}/K}\mid\Big(mn^{r}\prod_{i=1}^{r}(\alpha_{i}\beta_{i})^{2}\Big)^{n^{r}\varphi(m)}\mathcal{O}_{K}\,,

where
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
are such that the elements
\gamma_{i}:=\alpha_{i}/\beta_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
form a basis of
G
as a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module. Since
n\mid m
, we have

(12)

\sum_{p\in P(K_{m,n}/K)}\log p\leqslant\log(m\sigma_{G})\,.

We conclude by taking the natural logarithm of
\left\lvert d_{K_{m,n}}\right\rvert
, making use of (
10
), and by applying the bounds (
11
), (
12
) and
[K_{m,n}:K]\leqslant\varphi(m)n^{r}
.
∎

We are now ready to prove an effective unconditional Chebotarev density theorem for cyclotomic-Kummer extensions of number fields.

Theorem 10
.

Let
F/K
be a Galois extension of number fields, and let
G
be a finitely generated and torsion-free subgroup of
K^{\times}
of positive rank
r
. Let
C
be a conjugacy-stable subset of
\Gal(F/K)
, and for all integers
m,n\geqslant 1
with
n\mid m
, define

(13)

C_{m,n}:=\{\sigma\in\Gal(F_{m,n}/K):\sigma|_{F}\in C,\,\sigma|_{K_{m,n}}=\id\}\,,

which is a conjugacy-stable subset of
\Gal(F_{m,n}/K)
. Then there exist constants
c_{4},c_{5}>0
, which depend only on
F
and
G
, such that, for all
b>0
and uniformly for

(14)

m\leqslant c_{4}\left(\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{b}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r+2}}\,,

we have

(15)

\pi_{F_{m,n}/K,C_{m,n}}(x)=\frac{\left\lvert C_{m,n}\right\rvert}{[F_{m,n}:K]}\Li(x)+O_{F,G}\left(\frac{x}{\log x\cdot e^{c_{5}(\log\log x)^{b}}}\right).

We first prove an upper bound for
\pi_{F_{m,n}/K,C_{m,n}}(x)
.

Proposition 11
.

With the notation of Theorem
10
, there exists a constant
c_{6}>0
(depending only on
F
and
G
), such that, for all
b>0
and uniformly for

(16)

m\leqslant c_{6}\left(\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{1+b}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r+1}},

we have

\pi_{F_{m,n}/K,C_{m,n}}(x)\leqslant(2+o(1))\frac{\left\lvert C_{m,n}\right\rvert}{[F_{m,n}:K]}\Li(x).

Proof.

We apply Theorem
3
to
F_{m,n}/K
and
C_{m,n}
. By Proposition
9
and since
[F_{m,n}:F]\leqslant m^{r+1}
we have

\log\left\lvert d_{F_{m,n}}\right\rvert+[F_{m,n}:\mathbb{Q}]\log[F_{m,n}:\mathbb{Q}]\\
\leqslant[F:\mathbb{Q}]m^{r+1}\big(\log\big([F:\mathbb{Q}]m^{r+2}\big)+\log\big(\sigma_{G}\left\lvert d_{F}\right\rvert\big)\big)\leqslant c_{7}\cdot m^{r+1}\log m,

where
\sigma_{G}
is as in Proposition
9
.
Thus, for (
5
) to be satisfied, we should have at most
m\ll(\frac{\log x}{\log\log x})^{\frac{1}{r+1}}
. However, for the second error term in (
6
) to be
o(1)
, we require the bound (
16
) on
m
, which results in the estimate

m^{r+1}\log m\ll_{F,G}\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{b}}.

Notice that the constant
c_{6}
is chosen in such a way that
c_{1}c_{7}\cdot m^{r+1}\log m\leqslant\log x
.
As for the first error term of (
6
), we have

\exp\left(-\sqrt{\frac{c_{2}\log x}{[F_{m,n}:\mathbb{Q}]}}\right)\leqslant\exp\Big(-c_{8}(\log\log x)^{\frac{1+b}{2}}\Big)=o(1)\,.

Applying the inequality

yields the result. The constants

are as in Theorem

depend only on
F
and
G
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
10
.

We apply Theorem
3
to
F_{m,n}/K
and
C_{m,n}
. Since the bound (

) is smaller than (

), the condition (

) is satisfied by the proof of Proposition

is chosen similarly as for

\exp\left(-\sqrt{\frac{c_{2}\log x}{[F_{m,n}:\mathbb{Q}]}}\right)\leqslant\exp\Big(-c_{10}(\log x)^{\frac{1}{2(r+2)}}\cdot(\log\log x)^{\frac{b}{4}}\Big)\,.

We now bound the term

3.2.
Proof of Theorem

Recall that if

F/K\end{pmatrix}\subseteq C\right\}\right\rvert,

(where we only consider primes of degree

Lemma 12

If

\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{m}}(x)=\sum_{n\mid m^{\infty}}\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(d)\pi^{1}_{mn,dn}(x)+O\left(\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\log x}\right).

Proof.

The proof is a variation of
[

. The

.
Let

. Then we have

\mathcal{B}_{n}:=\left\{\mathfrak{p}:p\equiv 1\bmod mn,\,n\mid\ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G),\,\Big(\frac{\ind_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)}{n},m\Big)=1,\,\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{p}\\

Remark 13

It follows from the definition of

Remark 14

Remark 17
.

4.

As a special case of

In the rest of the paper,

Theorem 18
.

Let
K
be a number field and let
G
be a finitely generated and torsion-free subgroup of
K^{\times}
of positive rank

.
Let
m\geqslant 1
be an integer and let
\varrho_{m}
be the natural density of the set of primes

of

(where

Then there is an integer

, which depends only on

and

, such that

(25)

\varrho_{m}=\frac{1}{\varphi(m)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\mid m\\
\ell\nmid z\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell(\ell^{r}-1)}{\ell^{r+1}-1}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\mid z\\
\rad(g)\mid(m,z)\mid g\end{subarray}}\sum_{h\mid g}\frac{p(g,h)}{[K_{g,h}:K]},

where we set

if and only if at least one of the following conditions holds:

•

\ell\mid g
,
\ell\nmid h
such that

,

•

there is
\ell\mid h
such that

and

,

•

\ell\mid h

;

else we define

p(g,h)=\frac{\varphi(g)}{h}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\mid h,\ v_{\ell}(\frac{z}{g})>0\\
v_{\ell}(\frac{g}{h})=v_{\ell}(m)-1\end{subarray}}-\ell\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\mid h,\ v_{\ell}(\frac{z}{g})=0\\

v_{\ell}(\frac{z}{h})=0\end{subarray}}\frac{-\ell^{r+1}(\ell-1)}{\ell^{r+1}-1}.

Notice that the formula for

The rational number characterized by

The correction factors arising in problems related to Artin’s primitive root conjecture have been the object of study of several articles; see, for instance,

Proof of Theorem

By

[K_{m,n}:K]=\frac{\varphi(m)n^{r}}{\varphi((m,z))(n,z)^{r}}\cdot[K_{(m,z),(n,z)}:K]\,.

Therefore, we have

(26)

\varrho_{m}=\sum_{n\mid m^{\infty}}\sum_{d\mid m}\frac{\mu(d)}{[K_{mn,dn}:K]}=\frac{1}{\varphi(m)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\mid z\\
h\mid g\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(g)h^{r}}{[K_{g,h}:K]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\mid m^{\infty}\\
(mn,z)=g\end{subarray}}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid m\\
(dn,z)=h\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(d)}{n^{r+1}d^{r}}.

First of all, for all

we have that
\rad(mn,z)=\rad(m,z)
, hence we may restrict the sum on
g\mid z
to the divisors such that
(m,z)\mid g
and
\rad(g)=\rad((m,z))
both hold. To simplify the notation, let us write

, and similarly for
z_{\ell},g_{\ell},h_{\ell}
. Then, by properties of the multiplicative functions, from (
26

\varrho_{m}=\frac{1}{\varphi(m)}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\mid z\\
\rad(g)\mid(m,z)\mid g\end{subarray}}\sum_{h\mid g}\frac{\varphi(g)h^{r}}{[K_{g,h}:K]}\cdot\prod_{\ell\mid m}p_{\ell}(g,h)

where for
\ell\mid m
we define

(27)

p_{\ell}(g,h):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\geqslant 0\\
\min(m_{\ell}+s,z_{\ell})=g_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\{0,1\}\\
\min(s+e,z_{\ell})=h_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(\ell^{e})}{\ell^{s(r+1)}\ell^{er}}.

If
\ell\mid m
and
\ell\nmid z
, then the two conditions on the indices are trivial and we have

p_{\ell}(g,h)=\frac{\ell(\ell^{r}-1)}{\ell^{r+1}-1}.

This computation already justifies the first product in (

). Next, we take

and compute

depending on the prime factors
\ell
of
g
(equivalently, of
(m,z)
).

Case 1:
\ell\mid g
and
\ell\nmid h
.
Since
\ell\nmid h
, the conditions on the indices in (

) hold only for
s=e=0
, and so
p_{\ell}(g,h)=1
if
\min(m_{\ell},z_{\ell})=g_{\ell}
, and
p_{\ell}(g,h)=0
otherwise.

Case 2:
\ell\mid h
and
g_{\ell}<z_{\ell}
.

Since
1\leqslant h_{\ell}<z_{\ell}
, the conditions on the indices hold only for

. Therefore, if

, then

; if
g_{\ell}=m_{\ell}+h_{\ell}-1
, then
p_{\ell}(g,h)=-\ell/\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)}
; otherwise
p_{\ell}(g,h)=0
.

Case 3:
\ell\mid h
and

.

The conditions on the indices hold only for

and
m_{\ell}+s\geqslant z_{\ell}=g_{\ell}
. Therefore, if
m_{\ell}+h_{\ell}-1\geqslant z_{\ell}
, then
p_{\ell}(g,h)=-(\ell-1)/\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)}
; if
m_{\ell}+h_{\ell}=z_{\ell}
, then
p_{\ell}(g,h)=1/\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)}
; otherwise
p_{\ell}(g,h)=0
.

Case 4:
\ell\mid h
and

Since
h_{\ell}=g_{\ell}=z_{\ell}\geqslant 1
, the conditions on the indices hold if and only if

. Therefore, we obtain

p_{\ell}(g,h)=-\frac{1}{\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)}}\frac{\ell^{r+1}(\ell-1)}{\ell^{r+1}-1}.\qed

5.
The order being
k
-free

In this section we prove Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

If
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime of
K
with
\mathfrak{p}\notin\mathcal{P}(G)
, then
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)
is
k
-free if and only if for every rational prime
q
we have
q^{k}\nmid\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)
. Therefore, by the inclusion-exclusion principle we have

\pi_{\mathcal{N}_{k}}(x)=\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\mu(m)\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{m^{k}}}^{1}(x)+O\left(\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\log x}\right),

where
\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{m}}^{1}(x)
counts the primes of degree
1
in
\mathcal{P}_{m}
with norm up to
x
(the
O
-term estimates the primes not of degree 1). Notice that for
\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{m}}^{1}(x)
we may take the same asymptotic formula (
1
) as for
\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{m}}(x)
. Then, for
0<a<1
and
z:=\log^{a}x
we have

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathcal{N}_{k}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\leqslant z}\mu(m)\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{m^{k}}}^{1}(x)+\bigg(\sum_{m>z}\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{m^{k}}}^{1}(x)\bigg)+O\left(\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\log x}\right)

(28)

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{\log x}\beta_{C,k}+\bigg(\frac{x}{\log x}\sum_{m>z}\varrho_{C,m^{k}}\bigg)+\bigg(\sum_{m>z}\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{m^{k}}}^{1}(x)\bigg)

(29)

\displaystyle\quad+O\Bigg(\sum_{m\leqslant z}x\bigg(\frac{(\log\log x)^{2}}{\log x}\bigg)^{1+\frac{1}{r+2}}\Bigg)+O\left(\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\log x}\right)\,.

By Theorem
1
we have

\sum_{m>z}\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{m^{k}}}^{1}(x)\ll_{F,K,G}\frac{x}{\log x}\sum_{m>z}\varrho_{C,m^{k}}.

By Proposition
15
and recalling that
m/\varphi(m)=O_{\eta}(m^{\eta})
, we have
\varrho_{C,m^{k}}\ll_{\eta}1/m^{k-\eta}
for every
0<\eta<1
. Hence both
O
-terms in (

) are bounded by

(30)

\frac{x}{\log x}\sum_{m>z}\frac{1}{m^{k-\eta}}\ll_{\eta}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{1+a(k-1-\eta)}}.

Next, we can bound the first error term in (
29
) by

(31)

(\log x)^{a}\cdot x\bigg(\frac{(\log\log x)^{2}}{\log x}\bigg)^{1+\frac{1}{r+2}}\leqslant\frac{x(\log\log x)^{3}}{(\log x)^{1+\frac{1}{r+2}-a}},

and we may choose
a<\frac{1}{(r+2)(k-\eta)}
so that (
31
) can be bounded by (
30
). Thus, we may decrease the exponent in the denominator of (
30
) until
1+\frac{k-1}{(r+2)k+1}
, taking, e.g.
\eta=1/(r+4)
.
Collecting the errors yields the result.
∎

s+1\geqslant z_{\ell}
, hence
p_{\ell}(g,h)=-1/(\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)}(\ell-1))
if
g_{\ell}-h_{\ell}=1
, and
p_{\ell}(g,h)=0
otherwise.

Case 1.4:
\ell\mid h
and
g_{\ell}<z_{\ell}
.
We must have
s=g_{\ell}-1
. Thus, we obtain:
p_{\ell}(g,h)=-1/(\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)}(\ell-1))
if
g_{\ell}-h_{\ell}=1
;
p_{\ell}(g,h)=\ell/(\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)}(\ell-1))
if
g_{\ell}=h_{\ell}
;
p_{\ell}(g,h)=0
otherwise.

Case 2:
\ell\mid k
and
a_{\ell}>0
.
Recall that
g
is such that
g_{\ell}\geqslant\min(a_{\ell},z_{\ell})
. We set

p_{\ell}(g,h):=\sum_{e\in\{0,1\}}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\geqslant 0\\
\min(e+a_{\ell}+s,z_{\ell})=g_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\{0,1\}\\
\min(s+t,z_{\ell})=h_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(\ell^{e})\mu(\ell^{t})}{\ell^{s(r+1)+e+tr}}.

If
\ell\nmid z
, then we have
p_{\ell}(g,h)=\frac{(\ell-1)(\ell^{r}-1)}{\ell^{r+1}-1}
.
Before dealing with the case
\ell\mid z
, we set
q_{2}(g,h):=\prod_{\ell\mid(k,z),\,a_{\ell}>0}p_{\ell}(g,h)\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)}
, and we compute
p_{\ell}(g,h)
depending on the prime factor
\ell
of
(k,z)
.

Case 2.1:
\ell\nmid h
.
We must have
s=t=0
and hence we have:
p_{\ell}(g,h)=0
if
g_{\ell}\notin\{z_{\ell},a_{\ell},a_{\ell}+1\}
, or
g_{\ell}=z_{\ell}>a_{\ell}+1
;
p_{\ell}(g,h)=1
if
g_{\ell}=a_{\ell}<z_{\ell}
;
p_{\ell}(g,h)=-1/\ell
if
g_{\ell}=a_{\ell}+1
;
p_{\ell}(g,h)=1-1/\ell
if
g_{\ell}=z_{\ell}\leqslant a_{\ell}
.

Case 2.2:
\ell\mid h
and
h_{\ell}=g_{\ell}=z_{\ell}
.
The conditions on the indices hold only for
s\geqslant h_{\ell}
, or
s=h_{\ell}-1
and
t=1
(as
a_{\ell}\geqslant 1
). We obtain

p_{\ell}(g,h)=\Big(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\Big)\Big(\Big(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{r}}\Big)\frac{1}{\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)}}\sum_{s\geqslant 0}\frac{1}{\ell^{s(r+1)}}-\frac{\ell}{\ell^{h_{\ell}}}\Big)=-\frac{\ell^{r}(\ell-1)^{2}}{\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)}(\ell^{r+1}-1)}.

Case 2.3:
\ell\mid h
and
h_{\ell}<g_{\ell}=z_{\ell}
. Then the conditions are satisfied only if
s+e+a_{\ell}\geqslant z_{\ell}
. We deduce that
p_{\ell}(g,h)
equals:
-(\ell-1)^{2}/\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)+1}
if
g_{\ell}-h_{\ell}<a_{\ell}
;
(2\ell-1)/\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)+1}
if
g_{\ell}-h_{\ell}=a_{\ell}
;
-1/\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)+1}
if
g_{\ell}-h_{\ell}=a_{\ell}+1
;
0
if
g_{\ell}-h_{\ell}>a_{\ell}+1
.

Case 2.4:
\ell\mid h
and
g_{\ell}<z_{\ell}
. The conditions are satisfied only if
a_{\ell}+s+e=g_{\ell}
. Thus,
p_{\ell}(g,h)
equals:
0
if
g_{\ell}-h_{\ell}\notin\{a_{\ell}-1,a_{\ell},a_{\ell}+1\}
;
-\ell/\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)}
if
g_{\ell}-h_{\ell}=a_{\ell}-1
;
2/\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)}
if
g_{\ell}-h_{\ell}=a_{\ell}
;
-1/\ell^{h_{\ell}(r+1)+1}
if
g_{\ell}-h_{\ell}=a_{\ell}+1
.

Finally, take
p(g,h):=\frac{\varphi(g)}{h}\cdot q_{1}(g,h)q_{2}(g,h)
.
∎

Corollary 23
.

Let
K
be a number field and let
G
be a torsion-free subgroup of
K^{\times}
of positive rank
r
.
Let
k\geqslant 1
be a squarefree integer. The natural density of the set of primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
such that
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(G)
is coprime to
k
and
(\mathfrak{p},F/K)\subseteq C
(where
\mathfrak{p}\notin\mathcal{P}(G)
and
\mathfrak{p}
does not ramify in
F
) is given by

\sum_{f\mid k}\mu(f)\varrho_{C,f},

where, for
t\geqslant 1
,
\varrho_{C,t}
is as in (
2
).
Moreover, supposing that the condition on the Frobenius is trivial, there is an integer
z
, which depends only on
K
and
G
, such that this density equals

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\mid k\\
\ell\nmid z\end{subarray}}\bigg(1-\frac{\ell(\ell^{r}-1)}{(\ell^{r+1}-1)(\ell-1)}\bigg)\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\mid z\\
\rad(g)\mid k\end{subarray}}\sum_{h\mid g}\mu\left(\frac{g}{h}\right)\frac{p(g,h)}{[K_{g,h}:K]},

where, for
h\mid g
, we set

p(g,h)=\frac{\varphi(g)}{\varphi(g/h)h}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\mid h\\
\ell\nmid\frac{z}{h}\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell^{r+1}}{\ell^{r+1}-1}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\mid h\\
v_{\ell}(h)=v_{\ell}(g)<v_{\ell}(z)\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}.

Proof.

The first part of the statement follows directly from Theorem
1
and it is a special case of Theorem
21
.
As for the second part, it suffices to take
m=1
in Theorem
22
. Clearly we have
q_{2}(g,h)=1
. We obtain that
p(g,h)=0
if and only if
g/h
is not squarefree, and this together with the factor
\prod_{\ell\mid\frac{g}{h}}(-1)
in
q_{1}(g,h)
yields the term
\mu(g/h)
in the formula. Also, the factor
\prod_{\ell\mid\frac{g}{h}}1/(\ell-1)
in
q_{1}(g,h)
is equal to
1/\varphi(g/h)
.
∎

7.
Conditional results assuming GRH

In this section we show how Theorems
1
and
2
can be improved if we assume GRH for the Dedekind zeta functions of number fields of the type
K_{m,n}
. In fact, in this case we can make use of the stronger version of the Chebotarev density theorem, namely
[
19
, Théorème 4]
or
[
24
, Theorem 2]
, and we obtain smaller error terms. Let us first apply this theorem to cyclotomic-Kummer extensions of
K
.

Lemma 24
.

Assume GRH.
Let
F/K
,
G
,
C
and
\pi_{F_{m,n}/K,C_{m,n}}(x)
be as in Theorem
10
. Then we have

(34)

\pi_{F_{m,n}/K,C_{m,n}}(x)=\frac{\left\lvert C_{m,n}\right\rvert}{[F_{m,n}:K]}\Li(x)+O_{F,G}\left(\sqrt{x}\log(mx)\right).

Proof.

Applying
[
19
, Théorème 4]
we have

\frac{\left\lvert C_{m,n}\right\rvert}{[F_{m,n}:K]}\Big(\Li(x)+O\left(\sqrt{x}\log\big(\left\lvert d_{F_{m,n}}\right\rvert x^{[F_{m,n}:\mathbb{Q}]}\big)\right)\Big).

Recalling that
\left\lvert C_{m,n}\right\rvert\leqslant[F:K]
and applying Proposition
9
, we can reduce the error term to

O_{F}\bigg(\sqrt{x}\cdot\frac{\log\left\lvert d_{F_{m,n}}\right\rvert}{[F_{m,n}:\mathbb{Q}]}+\sqrt{x}\log x\bigg)=O_{F,G}\left(\sqrt{x}\log m+\sqrt{x}\log x\right).\qed

Theorem 25
.

Assume GRH.
With the setup of Theorem
1
, we have

\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{m}}(x)=\Li(x)\varrho_{C,m}+O_{F,K,G}(x^{3/4}\log x).

Proof.

We start as in the proof of Theorem
1
. Recall (
19
), where
\mathcal{B}_{n}
was defined in (
18
). Applying first Lemma
12
, and then Lemma
24
to the functions
\pi^{1}_{mn,dn}(x)
(notice that (
34
) also holds if we restrict to the primes of
K
of degree
1
), setting
y:=x^{1/4}
we obtain:

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathcal{P}_{m}}(x)=

\displaystyle\ \Li(x)\varrho_{C,m}+O\Bigg(\sum_{n\leqslant y/m}\,\sum_{d\mid m}\sqrt{x}\log(mnx)\Bigg)+O\left(\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\log x}\right)

\displaystyle\ +O\Bigg(\Li(x)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\mid m^{\infty}\\
nm>y\end{subarray}}\,\sum_{d\mid m}\frac{\mu(d)c(mn,dn)}{[F_{mn,dn}:K]}\Bigg)+O\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\mid m^{\infty}\\
nm>y\end{subarray}}\pi_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(x)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \Li(x)\varrho_{C,m}+O\left(y\sqrt{x}\log x\right)+O\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{y\log x}\right)+O\Bigg(\frac{\Li(x)}{\varphi(m)}\sum_{n>y/m}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigg).

For the last error term, we used that

\pi_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(x)\leqslant\pi_{K_{mn,n}/K,\id}(x)\ll_{K,G}\frac{1}{[K_{mn,n}:K]}\Li(x)

by Lemma
24
, and the estimate
[K_{mn,n}:K]\gg_{K,G}\varphi(m)n^{2}
; see
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
. All error terms are hence estimated by
x^{3/4}\log x
.
∎

Corollary 26
.

Assume GRH.
With the setup of Theorem
2
, we have

\pi_{\mathcal{N}_{k}}(x)=\Li(x)\beta_{C,k}+O_{F,K,G}\Bigg(\frac{x^{1-\frac{k-1}{4k+1}}}{\log x}\Bigg).

Moreover, with the setup of Theorem
21
, we have

\pi_{\mathcal{V}}(x)=\Li(x)\gamma_{C,k,m}+O_{F,K,G}\big(\tau(k)x^{3/4}\log x\big).

Proof.

As for the first assertion, it is sufficient to follow the proof of Theorem
2
, making use of Theorem
25
instead of Theorem
1
. Here we set
z=x^{a}
for some
0<a<1/4
. This yields

\pi_{\mathcal{N}_{k}}(x)=\Li(x)\beta_{C,k}+O\left(\frac{x}{x^{a(k-1-\eta)}\log x}+x^{3/4+a}\log x\right).

Taking
a<\frac{1}{4(k-\eta)}
we can bound the second error term with the first one. Hence we may decrease the exponent
a(k-1-\eta)
to
\frac{k-1}{4k+1}
(e.g. taking
\eta=1/10
), resulting in the error term of the statement.
The second assertion is a direct consequence of Theorem
25
.
∎

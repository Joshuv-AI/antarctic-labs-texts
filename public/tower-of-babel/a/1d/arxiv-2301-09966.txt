Abstract
Sequences of numbers (either natural integers, or integers or rational) of level k ∈ ℕ k\in\bbbn have been defined in [ Fra05 , FS06 ] as the sequences which can be computed by deterministic pushdown automata of level k k . This definition has been extended to sequences of words indexed by words in [ Sén07 , FMS14 ] . We characterise here the sequences of level 3 as the compositions of two HDT0L-systems. Two applications are derived: - the sequences of rational numbers of level 3 are characterised by polynomial recurrences - the equality problem for sequences of rational numbers of level 3 is decidable.



Keywords:
Iterated pushdown automata ; recurrent sequences of numbers; equivalence problems.

The following properties are equivalent:

2.1
Sets-Relations

Given a set
E
, we denote by
{\mathcal{P}}(E)
the set of its subsets and by
{\mathcal{P}}_{f}(E)
the set of its
finite
subsets.

A
binary relation
from a set
E
into a set
F
is a subset
R
of
E\times F
.
The domain and image of
R
are defined by:

{\rm dom}(R):=\{x\in E\mid\exists y\in F,(x,y)\in R\},\;\;{\rm im}(R):=\{y\in F\mid\exists x\in E,(x,y)\in R\}.

We denote by
\circ
the composition of binary relations:
if
R\subseteq E\times F,R^{\prime}\subseteq F\times G
then:

R\circ R^{\prime}:=\{(x,z)\in E\times G\mid\exists y\in F,(x,y)\in R\;\wedge\;(y,z)\in R^{\prime}\}

We denote by
\operatorname{BR}(Q)
the set of binary relations over
Q
.

A
function
from the set
E
into the set
F
is a binary relation

f\subseteq E\times F
such that,

\forall(x,y),\forall(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in f,x=x^{\prime}\Rightarrow y=y^{\prime}

Note that, when using a functional notation, we still use the composition operator
\circ
as above i.e.

(f\circ g)(x):=g(f(x)).

We call
mapping
(or simply,
map
) from
E
to
F
any function
f:E\rightarrow F
such that
{\rm dom}(f)=E
.

The empty set
\emptyset
is also denoted by the symbol
\varepsilon
when it is viewed as the empty word.

2.3
Monoids

We recall a monoid is a triple
\langle M,\cdot,{\bf 1}\rangle
such that,

M
is a set (the carrier of the monoid),
\cdot
is a composition law which is associative and
{\bf 1}
is a neutral element for
\cdot
(on both sides).
Given two monoids

{\mathbb{M}}_{1}:=\langle M_{1},\cdot,{\bf 1}_{1}\rangle,\;\;{\mathbb{M}}_{2}:=\langle M_{2},\cdot,{\bf 1}_{2}\rangle
a
monoid-homomorphism from
{\mathbb{M}}_{1}
to
{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
is a map
h:M_{1}\rightarrow M_{2}

fulfilling: for every
x,y\in M_{1}

h(x\cdot y)=h(x)\cdot h(y)\mbox{ and }h({\bf 1}_{1})={\bf 1}_{2}.

We denote by
\operatorname{HOM}({\mathbb{M}}_{1},{\mathbb{M}}_{2})
the set of all monoid-homomorphism from
{\mathbb{M}}_{1}
to
{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
.
For every monoid
{\mathbb{M}}
, the set
\operatorname{HOM}({\mathbb{M}},{\mathbb{M}})
, endowed with the composition law
\circ
and the identity map
{\rm Id}_{M}
is a monoid.

Given a set
X
(that we see as an “alphabet”), we denote by
X^{*}
the set of all
finite words labelled on this set
X
. We denote by
\cdot
the binary operation
of concatenation over
X^{*}

and denote by
\varepsilon
the empty word.
The structure
\langle X^{*},\cdot,\varepsilon\rangle
is the
free monoid

over the alphabet
X
.
For every integer
k\geq 0
, by
X^{\leq k}
we mean the set
\{u\in X^{*}\mid|u|\leq k\}
.

Partial monoids

We call
partial-monoid
every triple
{\mathbb{M}}=\langle M,\cdot,{\bf 1}\rangle
such that,

M
is a set (the carrier of the partial-monoid),
\cdot
is a function from
M\times M
into
M
such that:
for every
x,y,z\in M

[(x,y)\in{\rm dom}(\cdot)\mbox{ and }(x\cdot y,z)\in{\rm dom}(\cdot)]\Leftrightarrow[(y,z)\in{\rm dom}(\cdot)\mbox{ and }(x,y\cdot z)\in{\rm dom}(\cdot)]

and, if
x,y,z
fulfill the above two equivalent prerequisite, then

(x\cdot y)\cdot z=x\cdot(y\cdot z)

and, for every
x\in M

x\cdot{\bf 1}={\bf 1}\cdot x=x.

Note that, if
\langle M,\cdot,{\bf 1}\rangle
is a partial monoid, then

\langle{\mathcal{P}}(M),\cdot,\{{\bf 1}\}\rangle

is a monoid
(here , for
X,Y\subseteq M,X\cdot Y=\{x\cdot y\mid x\in X,y\in Y,(x,y)\in{\rm dom}(\cdot)\}
). Let
us consider the monoid
{\mathbb{M}}^{0}
defined by

{\mathbb{M}}^{0}:=\langle{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(M),\cdot,\{{\bf 1}\}\rangle

where
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(M)
denotes the set of subsets of
M
with at most one element.

{\mathbb{M}}^{0}
is a sub-monoid of the monoid
\langle{\mathcal{P}}(M),\cdot,\{{\bf 1}\}\rangle
. When
{\mathbb{M}}
is a partial-monoid which fails to be a monoid, there is a bijection between
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(M)
and
M\cup\{0\}
,
where
0
is a new element not in
M
. Most assertions about a partial monoid
{\mathbb{M}}
can thus be deduced from the similar assertions about the monoid

{\mathbb{M}}^{0}
.

Congruences

An equivalence relation
\sim
over
M
is called a
right-regular
equivalence
if and only if, for every
x,y,z\in M

x\sim y\Rightarrow x\cdot z\sim y\cdot z.

(the notion of left-regular equivalence is defined analogously).

An equivalence over the monoid
M
is called a
congruence
iff it is both right-regular and left-regular.

Given an homomorphism
h:{\mathbb{M}}_{1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
, the kernel of
h
is the congruence
\equiv
over
{\mathbb{M}}_{1}
defined by:

x\equiv y\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;h(x)=h(y).

We shall use the following construction.
Let
{\mathbb{M}}_{1},{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
be monoids and let us consider their free product
{\mathbb{M}}_{1}*{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
.
Let
h:{\mathbb{M}}_{1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
be an homomorphism. Let
\hat{h}:{\mathbb{M}}_{1}*{\mathbb{M}}_{2}\rightarrow M_{2}
be the unique monoid-homomorphism such that:

\forall x_{1}\in M_{1},\;\;\hat{h}(x_{1})=h(x_{1}),\;\;\forall x_{2}\in M_{2},\;\;\hat{h}(x_{2})=x_{2}.

The definition of
\hat{h}
shows that:

\forall x,y\in M_{1},(x,y)\in\ker(\hat{h})\Leftrightarrow(x,y)\in\ker(h).

Therefore
\ker(\hat{h})
is an extension of the congruence
\ker(h)
to the monoid
{\mathbb{M}}_{1}*{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
.

Starting with a congruence
\equiv
over
{\mathbb{M}}_{1}
, we can define
{\mathbb{M}}_{2}:={\mathbb{M}}_{1}/\equiv
, consider the projection

h:{\mathbb{M}}_{1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
and define an extension
\hat{\equiv}
of
\equiv
over the monoid
{\mathbb{M}}_{1}*({\mathbb{M}}_{1}/\equiv)

by

\hat{\equiv}:=\ker(\hat{h}).

(1)

We call
\hat{\equiv}
the self-extension of
\equiv
.
The monoid
{\mathbb{M}}_{1}/\equiv
is isomorphic with
({\mathbb{M}}_{1}*({\mathbb{M}}_{1}/\equiv))/\hat{\equiv}
, by the map

[x]_{\equiv}\mapsto[x]_{\hat{\equiv}}.

Intuitively, the elements of
{\mathbb{M}}_{1}*({\mathbb{M}}_{1}/\equiv)
are “mixed products” of “genuine” elements of
M_{1}
with “fake” elements
that are merely congruence-classes. When we map the genuine elements onto their classes, what we obtain is
a product of classes. The value of this product is the class of the
“mixed product” for the self-extension
\hat{\equiv}
.

This construction will be useful for extending some natural congruences over ordinary pushdowns of order 3 to
pushdowns of order 3
extended with undeterminates
(see subsection
3.5
), where the undeterminates
are classes of ordinary pushdowns modulo some congruence.

General Automata

General automaton

We introduce in this paragraph a notion of automaton over
an arbitrary memory structure, which is essentially the one defined by
[

]

Let us call

every tuple

\mathbb{D}=\langle D,F,\operatorname{OP},\operatorname{tops}\rangle
consisting of a set

D
, a finite set
F
, a set
\operatorname{OP}
of unary operations (

\forall\operatorname{op}\in\operatorname{OP}
,
\operatorname{op}
is a total map :

)
and a total map
\operatorname{tops}:D\rightarrow F

Definition 1
(Automaton over
\mathbb{D}
)

An automaton over the data-structure
\mathbb{D}
and
the terminal alphabet
B
is a 4-tuple

\mathcal{A}=(Q,B,\mathbb{D},\delta)

where

Q
is a finite set of states,

\mathbb{D}=\langle D,F,\operatorname{OP},\operatorname{tops}\rangle
is a data-structure,

the transition function,
\delta
, is a map from
Q\times(B\cup\{\varepsilon\})\times F

into
{\cal P}(Q\times\operatorname{OP}(\mathbb{D}))
.

A configuration is a triple
(q,u,w)\in Q\times B^{*}\times D
.
The
direct computation relation

{\vdash}_{\mathcal{A}}\;
, is defined as usual:
it is a binary relation over the set of configurations.For every
q,q^{\prime}\in Q,u,u^{\prime}\in B^{*},d,d^{\prime}\in D
,
we let

(q,u,d){\vdash}_{\mathcal{A}}\;(q^{\prime},u^{\prime},d^{\prime})

iff

•

u=\bar{b}\cdot u^{\prime}
for some
\bar{b}\in B\cup\{\varepsilon\}

•

\exists\operatorname{op}\in\operatorname{OP},(q^{\prime},\operatorname{op})\in\delta(q,\bar{b},\operatorname{tops}(d))
and
d^{\prime}=\operatorname{op}(d)
.

Pushdowns

Given a data-structure
\mathbb{D}
and a non-empty alphabet
\Gamma
, one constructs a new data-structure, denoted by
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{d}}(\Gamma,\mathbb{D})
and called the
set of
pushdowns over

(\Gamma,\mathbb{D})
. It is defined as follows:

{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{d}}(\Gamma,\mathbb{D}):=\langle P(\Gamma,D),F^{\prime},\operatorname{OP}^{\prime},\operatorname{tops}^{\prime}\rangle

where

\displaystyle P(\Gamma,D)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(\Gamma[D])^{*},\;\;F^{\prime}:=\Gamma\cdot F\cup\Gamma\cup\{\varepsilon\},\;\;\operatorname{OP}^{\prime}:=\operatorname{OP}\cup\{\operatorname{pop}\}\cup\{\operatorname{push}(h)\mid h\in\Gamma^{+}\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{tops}^{\prime}(\gamma[d]w):=\gamma\cdot\operatorname{tops}(d),\;\;\operatorname{tops}(\varepsilon):=\varepsilon.

The maps
\operatorname{push}(h):P(\Gamma,D)\rightarrow P(\Gamma,D)
and
\operatorname{pop}:P(\Gamma,D)\rightarrow P(\Gamma,D)

are defined by: for every
h\in\Gamma^{+},d\in D,w\in D^{*}
:if
h=h_{1}\cdots h_{n}
with
h_{i}\in\Gamma

\operatorname{push}(h)(\gamma[d]w):=h_{1}[d]\cdots h_{n}[d]w,\;\;\operatorname{pop}(\gamma[d]w):=w,

\;\;\operatorname{push}(h)(\varepsilon):=\varepsilon,\;\;\operatorname{pop}(\varepsilon):=\varepsilon.

A
pushdown automaton

of level
k
, for some integer
k\geq 0
, is an automaton over the data-structure
\mathbb{D}_{k}
defined by:

\mathbb{D}_{0}:=\langle\{\emptyset\},\{\emptyset\},\emptyset,\{(\emptyset,\emptyset)\}\rangle\;\;\mbox{ and }\;\;\forall i\in{\mathbb{N}},\;\;\mathbb{D}_{i+1}:={\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{d}}(\Gamma,\mathbb{D}_{i}).

The usual notion of finite automaton (resp. pushdown-automaton) coincides with the above notion of pda of level
0
(resp.
1
).

Derivations versus computations

\mathbb{D}
be some data-structure and

\mathcal{A}=(Q,B,{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{d}}(\Gamma,\mathbb{D}),\delta)

.
We associate with

V_{\mathcal{A}}=\{(p,\omega,q)\mid p,q\in Q,\omega\in P(\Gamma,D)-\{\varepsilon\}\}.

(2)

whose elements are called

.
The set of

2.5
Pushdown automata of level
k

pda

Beside the usual notions of finite automaton and pushdown automaton, we shall
consider here the notion of
pushdown automaton of level
k
:
this is an automaton (in the sense of Definition
1
)
over the data-structure
\mathbb{D}_{k}
(as defined in subsection
2.4
).
Let us describe in more details these automata:

Definition 2
(
k
-iterated pushdown store)

Let
\Gamma
be a set. We define inductively the set of

k
-iterated pushdown-stores over

\Gamma
by:

0-\operatorname{pds}(\Gamma)=\{\varepsilon\}\;\;(k+1)-\operatorname{pds}(\Gamma)=(\Gamma[k-\operatorname{pds}(\Gamma)])^{*}\;\;\operatorname{it}-\operatorname{pds}(\Gamma)=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}k-\operatorname{pds}(\Gamma).

The elementary operations that a
k
-pda can perform are:

-
\operatorname{pop}_{j}
of level
j
(where
1\leq j\leq k
), which consists of
popping the leftmost letter of level
j
and all the bracket just on its right

-
\operatorname{push}_{j}(h)
of level
j
(where
1\leq j\leq k,h\in\Gamma^{+}
), which consists of
pushing successively all the symbols of
h
as new heads of the leftmost pushdown of level
j
, thus copying this pushdown
in each bracket following each new head on its right(see example
1
below).

An operation of level
j
(whether
\operatorname{pop}_{j}
or
\operatorname{push}_{j}(h)
) applied on a pushdown where this leftmost pushdown of level
j
is
\varepsilon
,
leaves the pushdown invariant.

Example 1

Let
\Gamma=\{A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},B_{1},B_{2},B_{3},C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},D_{1},D_{2},D_{3}\}.

Let

\omega=A_{1}[A_{2}[A_{3}[\varepsilon]C_{3}[\varepsilon]]B_{2}[D_{3}[\varepsilon]C_{3}[\varepsilon]]]B_{1}[B_{2}[B_{3}[\varepsilon]D_{3}[\varepsilon]]].

We shall (abusively) write:

\omega=A_{1}[A_{2}[A_{3}C_{3}]B_{2}[D_{3}C_{3}]]B_{1}[B_{2}[B_{3}D_{3}]].

i.e. we use a short notation where the bracketed empty words
[\varepsilon]
that occur at the most internal level of the pushdown (here level 4) are removed.

The reading operation, applied on the above example gives:

\operatorname{topsyms}(\omega)=A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}

The
\operatorname{pop}
operations, applied on the above example give:

\operatorname{pop}_{1}(\omega)=B_{1}[B_{2}[B_{3}D_{3}]]

\operatorname{pop}_{2}(\omega)=A_{1}[B_{2}[D_{3}C_{3}]]B_{1}[B_{2}[B_{3}D_{3}]]

\operatorname{pop}_{3}(\omega)=A_{1}[A_{2}[C_{3}]B_{2}[D_{3}C_{3}]]B_{1}[B_{2}[B_{3}D_{3}]]

The
\operatorname{push}
operations, applied on the above example gives:

\operatorname{push}_{1}(AB)(\omega)=A[A_{2}[A_{3}C_{3}]B_{2}[D_{3}C_{3}]]B[A_{2}[A_{3}C_{3}]B_{2}[D_{3}C_{3}]]B_{1}[B_{2}[B_{3}D_{3}]]

\operatorname{push}_{2}(AB)(\omega)=A_{1}[A[A_{3}C_{3}]B[A_{3}C_{3}]B_{2}[D_{3}C_{3}]]B_{1}[B_{2}[B_{3}D_{3}]]

\operatorname{push}_{3}(AB)(\omega)=A_{1}[A_{2}[ABC_{3}]B_{2}[D_{3}C_{3}]]B_{1}[B_{2}[B_{3}D_{3}]]

A transition of the automaton consists, given the word
\gamma
made of all the leftmost letters
of the
k
-pushdown (the one of level 1, followed by the one of level 2, …, followed by the one of level
k
),
the state
q
and the leftmost letter
b
(or, possibly, the empty word
\varepsilon
) on the input tape,
in performing one of the above elementary operations. More formally,

Definition 3
(operations on
k
-
pds
)

Let
k\geq 1
, let
\operatorname{POP}(k):=\{\operatorname{pop}_{j}|j\in[1,k]\}
,

\operatorname{PUSH}(k,\Gamma):=\{\operatorname{push}_{j}(\gamma)|\gamma\in\Gamma^{+},j\in[1,k]\}
,

\forall i\in I,\forall k,\ell\in[1,d],\forall a\in A,\forall i\in I,\;\;u_{i,k,\ell}(aw))=P_{i,k,\ell}(\vec{u}(w))

(55)

where
\vec{u}(w)
is the
|I|\cdot d^{2}
-tuple of integers
(u_{*,*,*}(w))
.
Finally, if we note
M(u_{i})

(d,d)

K

we get that:

\forall i\in I,\forall w\in A^{*},\;\;f_{i}(w)=K(\vec{u}(w)).

(56)

By (

are polynomially recurrent and by (
56

f_{i}
are polynomially recurrent too.

The extension of the proof, hence of the result, to an arbitrary finite alphabet

5
Examples and counter-examples

We examine here four examples of mappings
A^{*}\rightarrow\bbbn
and
locate them in the classes
\mathbb{S}_{k}(A^{*},{\mathbb{N}})
or some related classes.

Example 4

The Fibonacci sequence
F_{n}
defined by

F_{0}=1,F_{1}=1,F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_{n}\mbox{ for all }n\geq 0

is clearly in
\mathbb{S}_{2}
since it fulfills a linear recurrence relation with
coefficients in
\bbbn
(i.e. a catenative recurrence where the alphabet has size
1
).

Example 5

Let
G:\{0,1\}^{*}\rightarrow\bbbn
be defined by

G(w)=F_{\nu(w)}

where
\nu(w)
is the natural number expressed by
w
in base 2.
Since
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\nu(w)w
is a rational series,
G
fulfils point 3 of our characterisation of
\mathbb{S}_{3}(\{0,1\}^{*},\bbbn)
.

Example 6

Factorial sequence:
FC(n):=(n+1)!
.

i.e.
FC(n)
denotes “factorial
(n+1)
”.

It has level
3
.

Proof

Let us define the auxiliary sequence
L(n):=n+2.

These sequences fulfill the polynomial recurrence relations:

L(0)=2\;\;FC(0)=1\;\;L(n+1)=L(n)+1\;\;FC(n+1)=L(n)\cdot FC(n).

Hence
FC
is a sequence in
\mathbb{S}_{3}
.

By Theorem
3.1
,
FC
is the composition of two word-sequences of order
2
.

An explicit such decomposition is the following:

\displaystyle u(0)=b\;\;v(0)=ab\;\;

\displaystyle u(n+1)=u(n)\cdot v(n)

\displaystyle\;\;v(n+1)=a\cdot v(n)

\displaystyle U(\varepsilon)=1\;\;V(\varepsilon)=0\;\;

\displaystyle U(a\cdot w)=U(w)+V(w)\;\;

\displaystyle V(a\cdot w)=V(w)\;\;

\displaystyle U(b\cdot w)=U(w)\;\;V(b\cdot w)=V(w)\;\;

We get:

u(n)=babaab\cdots ba^{n}b\;\;\;\;v(n)=a^{n+1}b

U(u(n))=(n+1)!

We thus have decomposed
FC
into two recurrences of level 2 (going
“through” words):

\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}},\;\;FC(n)=U(u(n)).

Nevertheless,
FC
is
not
the composition of two integer sequences of order
2
:
this can be deduced from the rate of growth of
FC
, which is strictly larger than any exponential function, but strictly smaller than
any double-exponential function. The technically subtle point consists in showing that no composition of
integer sequences in
\mathbb{S}_{2}
can posess such a growth rate (work in preparation).

Example 7

Let us consider the sequence
(D_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that

D_{0}=0\mbox{ and }\forall n\geq 1,\;\;D_{n}=n^{n}.

This sequence belongs to
\mathbb{S}_{4}
and does not belong to
{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{S}_{3})
.

Proof for level 4

The maps
f(n):=n
and
g(n):=n
have level
1
, hence
f\in\mathbb{S}_{4}
and
g\in\mathbb{S}_{3}
.
By Proposition 66 of
[
FS06
]
, if
f\in\mathbb{S}_{k+1}
and
g\in\mathbb{S}_{k}
with
k\geq 3
, then

n\mapsto f(n)^{g(n)}\in\mathbb{S}_{k+1}
. It follows that, for the above maps
f,g
,
D=f^{g}\in\mathbb{S}_{4}
.

Decomposition

We take below the convention that
0^{0}=0
.

Let us exhibit two mappings
f\in\mathbb{S}_{2}({\mathbb{N}},\{a,b,c\}^{*}),g\in\mathbb{S}_{3}(\{a,b,c\}^{*},{\mathbb{N}})

such that

\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}},D_{n}=g(f(n)).

(57)

We define these mappings by two systems of recurrent relations.
Let
f,A,C:{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow\{a,b,c\}^{*}
such that

\displaystyle f(0)=b,

\displaystyle A(0)=a,

\displaystyle C(0)=c,

\displaystyle f(n+1)=A(n)\cdot f(n)\cdot B(n),

\displaystyle A(n+1)=A(n),

\displaystyle C(n+1)=C(n).

(58)

Let us introduce a finite alphabet
X=\{x,y\}
and define mappings
H,K,K^{\prime},P:\{a,b,c\}^{*}\rightarrow\operatorname{HOM}(X^{*},X^{*})

by the recurrence relations:

\displaystyle H(\varepsilon)={\rm Id},

\displaystyle K(\varepsilon)=[x,xy],

\displaystyle K^{\prime}(\varepsilon)=[x,\varepsilon],\;\;P(\varepsilon)=[y,x],

\displaystyle H(au)=H(u)\circ H(u),

\displaystyle H(bu)=P(u)\circ H(u)\circ K^{\prime}(u),

\displaystyle H(cu)=H(u)\circ K(u),

\displaystyle K(au)=K(u),

\displaystyle K(bu)=K(u),

\displaystyle K(cu)=K(u),

\displaystyle K^{\prime}(au)=K^{\prime}(u),

\displaystyle K^{\prime}(bu)=K^{\prime}(u),

\displaystyle K^{\prime}(cu)=K^{\prime}(u),

\displaystyle P(au)=P(u),

\displaystyle P(bu)=P(u),

\displaystyle P(cu)=P(u).

(59)

where the bracketed notation
[w,w^{\prime}]
designates the homorphism
X^{*}\to X^{*}
that maps
x
to
w
and
y
to
w^{\prime}
.
We finally define
g
by:

\forall u\in\{a,b,c\}^{*},\;\;g(u)=H(u)(x).

One can check that, for every integers
p,q,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
:

H(c^{q})=[x,x^{q}y],\;\;H(bc^{q})=[x^{q},x],\;\;H(a^{p}bc^{q})=[x^{q^{p}},x]

f(n)=a^{n}bc^{n}.

g(a^{n}bc^{n})=H(a^{n}bc^{n})(x)=x^{n^{n}}

g(f(n))=x^{n^{n}}\equiv n^{n}

The system (
5
) shows that
f
has level 2. The system (
5
) is a system of recurrent relations in
\langle\operatorname{HOM}(\{a,b,c\}^{*},\{a,b,c\}^{*}),\circ,{\rm Id}\rangle
, which implies, by point (2) of Theorem
3.1
, that
g
has level 3.

This decomposition also proves that
D
has level
4
: by an adaptation of Proposition 70 of
[
FS06
]
to arbitrary alphabets),
the composition
D=f\circ g
of a map of level
2
by a map of level
3
, has level
4
.

Proof for not level 3:

The proof of theorem 16 of
[
CMP+20
]
shows that, for every
k\geq 1
, the sequences
D_{n},D_{n+1},\ldots,D_{n+k-1}
are algebraically independent over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Theorem
4.2
then shows that it cannot have level 3, in the strong sense that:
D\notin{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{S}_{3}(\bbbn,\bbbn))
.

6.2
Perspectives

All the open problems mentionned at the end of
[
FS06
]
remain unsolved at the moment and deserve interest.

Let us mention some open problems which are specific to level 3.

1- The convolution operation is known to preserve
\mathbb{S}_{2}
and it is shown in Proposition 67 of
[
FS06
]

that, for every integer
k\geq 2
, if
f\in\mathbb{S}_{k+1},g\in\mathbb{S}_{k}
then

f*g\in\mathbb{S}_{k+1}
.

We wonder if the level
3
is closed under convolution. The same question is open for all levels
k\geq 3
.

2- The equality problem for sequences in
\mathbb{S}_{3}(A^{*},{\mathbb{N}})
(for every finite alphabet
A
) is decidable (
[
Sén07
]
).
Let us say that
(u(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}},(v(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are
almost
-equal (which we denote by
u=_{a}v
) iff

\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid u_{n}\neq v_{n}\}\mbox{ is finite}.

The following decision problem is not known to be decidable nor undecidable:

Instance
: two sequences
u,v\in\mathbb{S}_{3}

Question
:
u=_{a}v?

Note that the so-called “Skolem problem” (see below) is recursively reducible to this problem.
Skolem problem (usually credited to
[
Sko34
]
) is the following decision problem:

Instance
: two sequences
u,v\in\mathbb{S}_{2}

Question
:
\exists n\in{\mathbb{N}},u(n)=v(n)?

Skolem’s problem is reducible (by a many-one recursive reduction) to the almost-equivalence problem for sequences of level 3,
by the following arguments.

Given
u,v\in\mathbb{S}_{2}
, let us consider the sequence

w(n):=\prod_{i=0}^{n}(u(n)-v(n)).

This sequence
w
belongs to
{\cal D}_{3}
i.e. has the form

w(n)=w^{+}(n)-w^{-}(n)

where
w^{+},w^{-}\in\mathbb{S}_{3}
(see the sketch of proof below).

But

[\exists n\in{\mathbb{N}},\;u(n)=v(n)]\Leftrightarrow[w^{+}=_{a}w^{-}].

Sketch of proof
:
The sequence
w
fulfills the recurrence relation

w(n+1)=w(n)\cdot(u(n+1)-v(n+1)).

which has the form

w(n+1)=P(w(n))

for some polynomial
P
with coefficients in
{\cal D}_{3}
.
From Theorem 96 of
[
FS06
]
, step 1 of the proof, it follows that
this sequence
w
belongs to
{\cal D}_{3}
.

\Box

3- A natural decision problem is the following:

Instance
: a sequences
u\in\mathbb{S}_{k+1}

Question
:
u\in\mathbb{S}_{k}?

which we can name the
level-minimization problem
.
A positive solution is given in
[
Dou23
]
for several sub-hierarchies of
(\operatorname{D}\operatorname{PT}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
.
It is not known whether this problem is decidable or not for
(\operatorname{D}\operatorname{PT}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
nor for
(\mathbb{S}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
.

Example
7
suggests that there is no simple property that characterizes level 3 inside
\mathbb{S}_{4}
; for example, a low growth-rate does not imply
a low level in the hierarchy
(\mathbb{S}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
.

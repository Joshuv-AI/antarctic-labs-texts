Out-of-Distribution-Aware Electric Vehicle Charging
Tongxin Li, Chenxi Sun

Abstract
We tackle the challenge of learning to charge Electric Vehicles (EVs) with Out-of-Distribution (OOD) data. Traditional scheduling algorithms typically fail to balance near-optimal average performance with worst-case guarantees, particularly with OOD data. Model Predictive Control (MPC) is often too conservative and data-independent, whereas Reinforcement Learning (RL) tends to be overly aggressive and fully trusts the data, hindering their ability to consistently achieve the best-of-both-worlds. To bridge this gap, we introduce a novel OOD-aware scheduling algorithm, denoted OOD-Charging . This algorithm employs a dynamic “awareness radius”, which updates in real-time based on the Temporal Difference (TD)-error that reflects the severity of OOD. The OOD-Charging algorithm allows for a more effective balance between consistency and robustness in EV charging schedules, thereby significantly enhancing adaptability and efficiency in real-world charging environments. Our results demonstrate that this approach improves the scheduling reward reliably under real OOD scenarios with remarkable shifts of EV charging behaviors caused by COVID-19 in the Caltech ACN-Data.

Problem Formulation

II-A

Preliminary: Markov Decision Process

The general EV charging scheduling problem considered in this work is formulated as a finite Markov Decision Process (MDP).
Denote by
T>0
the length of the scheduling time horizon and write
[T]\coloneqq\{0,\ldots,T-1\}
. Let
s_{t}\in\mathcal{S}
and
a_{t}\in\mathcal{A}
be the system
state
and
scheduling action
at time
t\in[T]
where
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
are safety state and action spaces defined by operational constraints. Let
P_{t}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{S}}
be the charging transition probability, where
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{S}}
is a set of probability measures on
\mathcal{S}
. Given a charging action
a_{t}
at time
t\in[T]
, the EV state evolves as

s_{t+1}\sim P_{t}(\cdot|s_{t},a_{t}),\ t\in[T]
. We denote a scheduling policy by
\pi\coloneqq(\pi_{t}:t\in[T])
where each
\pi_{t}:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
decides a charging action
a_{t}
, after observing the EV state
s_{t}
at time
t\in[T]
.
We consider a time-varying charging cost (or negative reward)

with variability induced by solar generation and electricity costs. Overall, our goal is to find a policy

that minimizes the following expected costs:

\displaystyle J(\pi)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t\in[T]}c_{t}\left(s_{t},\pi_{t}(s_{t})\right)\right],

(1)

where the randomness in the expectation

is from the transition dynamics
\mathbb{P}=(P_{t}:t\in[T])

IV
Theoretical Guarantees

We present theoretical guarantees of the
OOD-Charging
policy.
Consider the error metric
\varepsilon
defined in (
9
).
The goal of the OOD-aware scheduling is to achieve (1). near-optimal total cost when the Q-value error
\varepsilon
is small, and (2). close to the scheduling baseline when the Q-value error
\varepsilon
is high due to distribution shifts in a stochastic setting. This motivates us to consider the following performance benchmark that generalizes the concept of competitive ratio to evaluate the optimality of scheduling policies.

Definition
IV.1
.

Given
\mathsf{MDP}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},T,\mathbb{P},c)
, the
ratio of expectations

of a policy
\pi=\left(\pi_{t}:t\in[T]\right)
is defined as
\mathsf{RoE}(\pi)\coloneqq{J(\pi)}/{J^{\star}}
where
J(\pi)
is defined in (
1
) and
J^{\star}
is the optimal expected cost.

Now, we are ready to introduce our definition of consistency and robustness with respect to the ratio of expectations, similar to the growing literature on learning-augmented algorithms
[
18
,
26
,
33
]
. We write the ratio of expectations
\mathsf{RoE}(\varepsilon)
of a policy
\pi
as a function of the Q-value advice error
\varepsilon
in terms of the
\ell_{\infty}
norm, defined in (
9
).

Definition
IV.2

(Consistency and Robustness)
.

An algorithm
\pi
is said to be
k
-consistent
if its worst-case (with respect to the MDP) ratio of expectations satisfies
\mathsf{RoE}(\varepsilon)\leq k
for
\varepsilon=0
. On the other hand, it is
l
-robust
if
\mathsf{RoE}(\varepsilon)\leq l
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

The result below indicates that for the non-stationary dynamics in (
15
),
OOD-Charging
achieves a near-optimal consistency and robustness trade-off.

Theorem
IV.1
.

Consider the EV charging problem in Section
II
with Assumption
1
and
2

3

10

such that

is
\left(1+\mathcal{O}(\overline{W})\right)

\displaystyle\mathsf{RoE}(\mathsf{MPC})\leq\frac{2\xi C^{2}(1+C^{2})(1+\overline{A}^{2}+\overline{B}^{2})}{\mu(1-\overline{\lambda})^{2}}

(12)

bounds the ratio of expectations of the MPC baseline (Procedure

VI
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we present an OOD-aware policy,
OOD-Charging
, to tackle the challenge of learning to charge EVs under OOD conditions.
Experimental results show that
OOD-Charging
significantly improves the effectiveness and reliability of EV charging scheduling in unpredictable real-world environments, particularly in OOD contexts. It has several limitations
that motivate extensions.
In future work, it would be interesting to assess the performance of the proposed approach under more stringent real-world constraints such as three-phase network models and customized user preferences. Additionally, we plan to implement the proposed OOD-aware algorithm at real charging sites with solar cells installed to verify the applicability of the proposed algorithm.

Step 2. Consistency Bound.
Now we show that the per-step expected dynamic regret of
OOD-Charging
is less than
\mathbb{E}\left[\mu_{t}\right]+L_{Q}\mathbb{E}\left[\eta_{t}\left(\widetilde{s}_{t}\right)+2\overline{W}-R_{t}\right]

\displaystyle J(\pi)-J^{\star}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\mu_{t}\right]+L_{Q}\mathbb{E}\left[\eta_{t}\left(\widetilde{s}_{t}\right)+2\overline{W}-R_{t}\right],

(32)

where

We apply the projection lemma (Lemma 3) in
[

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{t}\left(s_{t},a_{t}\right)\leq

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{t}\left(s_{t},\widetilde{\pi}_{t}\left(\widetilde{s}_{t}\right)\right)+L_{Q}\left\|\widetilde{\pi}_{t}\left(\widetilde{s}_{t}\right)-a_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{t}\left(s_{t},\widetilde{\pi}_{t}\left(\widetilde{s}_{t}\right)\right)+L_{Q}\left(\eta_{t}\left(s_{t}\right)-R_{t}\right)+2L_{Q}\overline{W}

where

Therefore, the result can be derived by using

\displaystyle Q_{t}^{\star}\left(s_{t},a_{t}\right)-\inf_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}Q_{t}^{\star}\left(s_{t},a^{\prime}\right)\leq

\displaystyle L_{Q}\left[\eta_{t}\left(s_{t}\right)-R_{t}\right]^{+}+\mu_{t}

Step 3. Robustness Bound.

Since the cost functions
(c_{t}:t\in[T])
are Lipschitz continuous with a Lipschitz constant
L_{\mathrm{C}}
, using the Kantorovich Rubinstein duality theorem, since
{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu)\leq{W}_{q}(\mu,\nu)
for all
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
, for all
t\in[T]
,

\displaystyle J(\pi)-J(\overline{\pi})=

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in[T]}\mathbb{E}\left[c_{t}\left(s,a\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[c_{t}\left(s,a\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in[T]}\sup_{\|f\|_{L}\leq L_{\mathrm{C}}}\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(s,a\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(s,a\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{C}}\overline{C}\sum_{t\in[T]}\mathbb{E}\left[R_{t}+2C\overline{W}\right],

[
34

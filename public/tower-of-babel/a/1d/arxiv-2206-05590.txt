Quantum Heisenberg model on a sawtooth-chain lattice: Rotation-invariant Green’s function method
Taras Hutak, Taras Krokhmalskii, Oleg Derzhko, Johannes Richter

Abstract
We apply the rotation-invariant Green’s function method (RGM) to study the spin S = 1 / 2 S=1/2 Heisenberg model on a one-dimensional sawtooth lattice, which has two nonequivalent sites in the unit cell. We check the RGM predictions for observable quantities by comparison with the exact-diagonalization and finite-temperature-Lanczos calculations. We discuss the thermodynamic and dynamic properties of this model in relation to the mineral atacamite Cu 2 Cl(OH) 3 complementing the RGM outcomes by results of other approaches.

The finite-temperature properties of frustrated quantum spin systems are interesting for a broad community of solid-state researchers.
On the other hand, the theoretical description of these quantum many-body systems is challenging.
Moreover, the powerful quantum Monte Carlo approach suffers from the infamous sign problem and exact solutions are notoriously rare.

It is a universal and straightforward way to investigate quantum lattice spin systems

Such a double-time Green’s function approach is known as the rotation-invariant Green’s function method (RGM):

Model and quantities of interest

We consider a quantum

Heisenberg model on a sawtooth-chain lattice of
N=2{\cal N}

{\cal N}

The lattice sites are unambiguously given by two integer numbers

The first one determines the unit cell whereas the second one specifies the site in the cell.
The Hamiltonian of the model reads

\displaystyle H\!=\!\sum_{j=1}^{\cal{N}}\!\left[J_{1}{\bf S}_{j,1}\!\cdot\!{\bf S}_{j+1,1}\!+\!J_{2}\left({\bf S}_{j,1}\!\cdot\!{\bf S}_{j,2}\!+\!{\bf S}_{j,2}\!\cdot\!{\bf S}_{j+1,1}\right)\right],\,\,\,

(2.1)

and periodic boundary conditions are imposed for convenience.
Here the spin-1/2 operators satisfy the relations

S^{+}S^{z}=-S^{+}/2

S^{z}S^{+}=S^{+}/2
,

S^{-}S^{z}=S^{-}/2
,

S^{z}S^{-}=-S^{-}/2

S^{+}S^{-}=1/2+S^{z}
,

S^{-}S^{+}=1/2-S^{z}
etc.,
if they are attached to the same site,
but they commute if they are attached to different sites.

Let us briefly discuss the physical quantities of interest.
The internal energy (per cell) of the model follows immediately from Eq. (

\displaystyle e(T)=\frac{3}{2}J_{1}c_{10}+3J_{2}c_{01},

\displaystyle c_{10}=\langle S_{j,1}^{-}S_{j+1,1}^{+}\rangle,\;\;\;c_{01}=\langle S^{-}_{j,2}S^{+}_{j+1,1}\rangle

(2.2)

[we have used the lattice symmetry and the relations

\langle S^{x}_{A}S^{x}_{B}\rangle=\langle S^{y}_{A}S^{y}_{B}\rangle=\langle S^{z}_{A}S^{z}_{B}\rangle

\displaystyle c(T)=\frac{3}{2}J_{1}\frac{\partial c_{10}}{\partial T}+3J_{2}\frac{\partial c_{01}}{\partial T},

(2.3)

which in turn yields the entropy

\displaystyle s(T)=s(\infty)+\frac{e(T)}{T}-\int\limits_{T}^{\infty}{\rm d}{\sf T}\frac{e({\sf T})}{{\sf T}^{2}}

(2.4)

with

The corresponding equations for the uniform susceptibility will be given below.

III
RGM equations

To determine the Green’s functions we write down the equations of motion for them.
Differentiating the definition of
G_{q\alpha\beta}(t)
gives

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}G_{q\alpha\beta}(t)}{{\rm{d}}t}\!=\!-2{\rm{i}}\delta(t)\delta_{\alpha\beta}\langle S^{z}\rangle\!-\!{\rm{i}}\theta(t)\!\left\langle\!\left[\!\frac{{\rm{d}}S^{+}_{q\alpha}}{{\rm{d}}t}\!(t),S^{-}_{q\beta}\!\right]\!\right\rangle\!.

(3.1)

Since we seek for rotation-invariant Green’s functions (i.e.,
\langle S^{z}\rangle=0
),
the first term in Eq. (
3.1
) drops out.
We need to take the derivative with respect to time
t
once more arriving at

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}^{2}G_{q\alpha\beta}(t)}{{\rm{d}}t^{2}}=-{\rm{i}}\delta(t)\left\langle\!\left[\frac{{\rm{d}}S^{+}_{q\alpha}}{{\rm{d}}t},S^{-}_{q\beta}\right]\!\right\rangle

\displaystyle-{\rm{i}}\theta(t)\left\langle\!\left[\frac{{\rm{d}}^{2}S^{+}_{q\alpha}}{{\rm{d}}t^{2}}(t),S^{-}_{q\beta}\right]\!\right\rangle.

(3.2)

Let us introduce the moment matrix:

\displaystyle M_{q\alpha\beta}={\rm{i}}\left\langle\!\left[\frac{{\rm{d}}S^{+}_{q\alpha}}{{\rm{d}}t},S^{-}_{q\beta}\right]\!\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cal{N}}}\sum_{j=1}^{\cal{N}}{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}qj}\left\langle\!\left[\left[S_{j,\alpha}^{+},H\right],S^{-}_{q\beta}\right]\!\right\rangle.

(3.3)

By straightforward calculations in the second line of Eq. (
III
) we immediately obtain

\displaystyle M_{q11}=-4J_{1}c_{10}\left(1-\cos q\right)-4J_{2}c_{01},

\displaystyle M_{q12}={\sf M}_{q12}\left(1+{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}q}\right)=\left(M_{q21}\right)^{*},\;\;\;{\sf M}_{q12}=2J_{2}c_{01},

\displaystyle M_{q22}=-4J_{2}c_{01}\,\,\,\,\,

(3.4)

with
c_{10}
and
c_{01}
defined in Eq. (
II
).

The second term in the right-hand side of Eq. (
III
) can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle{\rm{i}}\theta(t)\left\langle\!\left[\frac{{\rm{d}}^{2}S^{+}_{q\alpha}}{{\rm{d}}t^{2}}(t),S^{-}_{q\beta}\right]\!\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\!{\rm{i}}\theta(t)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cal N}}\sum_{j=1}^{\cal N}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}qj}\left\langle\!\left[\left[\left[S^{+}_{j,\alpha},H\right],H\right](t),S^{-}_{q\beta}\right]\!\right\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Kondo-Yamaji decoupling}}}{{\approx}}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{2}F_{q\alpha\gamma}G_{q\gamma\beta}(t).

(3.5)

Here
F_{q\alpha\beta}
are the elements of the frequency matrix.
To arrive at the last line of Eq. (
III
) we follow the strategy of J. Kondo and K. Yamaji
Kondo1972

and utilize the Kondo-Yamaji decoupling:

\displaystyle S_{A}^{-}S_{B}^{+}S_{C}^{+}\to\tilde{\alpha}_{AB}S_{C}^{+}+\tilde{\alpha}_{AC}S_{B}^{+},

\displaystyle S_{A}^{z}S_{B}^{z}S_{C}^{+}\to\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\alpha}_{AB}S_{C}^{+},

(3.6)

where
A
,
B
, and
C
denote different lattice sites,

\tilde{\alpha}_{AB}=\alpha_{AB}c_{AB}
,

c_{AB}\equiv\langle S^{-}_{A}S^{+}_{B}\rangle=\langle S^{+}_{A}S^{-}_{B}\rangle=2\langle S^{z}_{A}S^{z}_{B}\rangle
,
and
\alpha_{AB}
are the vertex parameters introduced to improve the decoupling of higher-order correlators.
After all,
c_{AB}
and
\alpha_{AB}
must be determined self-consistently.
Besides the first-neighbor correlations
c_{10}
and
c_{01}
(
II
),
we introduce the second-neighbor correlations

\displaystyle c_{20}\!=\!\langle S^{-}_{i,1}S^{+}_{i+2,1}\rangle\!,c_{11}\!=\!\langle S^{-}_{i,2}S^{+}_{i+2,1}\rangle\!,c_{02}\!=\!\langle S^{-}_{i,2}S^{+}_{i+1,2}\rangle\!,\,\,\,\,\,

(3.7)

see Fig.
1
.
After tedious but straightforward calculations in the second line of Eq. (
III
) with accounting Eq. (
III
)
we find the elements of the frequency matrix
F_{q\alpha\beta}
,

\displaystyle F_{q11}\!=\!J_{1}^{2}\!\left(\!1-2\tilde{\alpha}_{10}\!+\!2\tilde{\alpha}_{20}\!\right)\!+\!J_{2}^{2}\!\left(\!1\!+\!2\tilde{\alpha}_{02}\!\right)+4J_{1}\!J_{2}\!\left(\!\tilde{\alpha}_{01}\!+\!\tilde{\alpha}_{11}\!\right)

\displaystyle\!+\!\left[\!-J_{1}^{2}\!\left(\!1\!+\!2\tilde{\alpha}_{10}\!+\!2\tilde{\alpha}_{20}\!\right)\!+\!2J_{2}^{2}\!\tilde{\alpha}_{01}\!-\!2J_{1}\!J_{2}\!\left(\!3\tilde{\alpha}_{01}\!+\!\tilde{\alpha}_{11}\!\right)\!\right]\cos\!q

\displaystyle\!+\!4J_{1}^{2}\!\tilde{\alpha}_{10}\cos^{2}\!q,

\displaystyle F_{q12}={\sf F}_{q12}\left(1+{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}q}\right),

\displaystyle{\sf F}_{\!q12}\!\!=\!\!-J_{2}^{2}\!\left(\!\!\frac{1}{2}\!+\!\tilde{\alpha}_{10}\!+\!\tilde{\alpha}_{01}\!\!\right)\!-\!2J_{1}\!J_{2}\tilde{\alpha}_{10}\!+\!2J_{1}\!J_{2}\tilde{\alpha}_{10}\cos\!q,

\displaystyle F_{q21}={\sf F}_{q21}\left(1+{\rm e}^{{\rm i}q}\right),

\displaystyle{\sf F}_{\!q21}\!\!=\!\!-\!J_{2}^{2}\!\left(\!\!\frac{1}{2}\!+\!\tilde{\alpha}_{01}\!+\!\tilde{\alpha}_{02}\!\!\right)\!\!-\!\!J_{1}\!J_{2}\!\left(\!\tilde{\alpha}_{01}\!\!+\!\!\tilde{\alpha}_{11}\!\right)\!+\!2J_{1}\!J_{2}\tilde{\alpha}_{01}\cos\!q,

\displaystyle F_{q22}=J_{2}^{2}\left(1+2\tilde{\alpha}_{10}+2\tilde{\alpha}_{01}\cos q\right).\,\,\,\,\,

(3.8)

As a result, Eq. (
III
) becomes a closed set of equations for the Green’s functions
and after utilizing the representation for the
\delta
-function

2\pi\delta(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\omega{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\omega t}
,
we arrive at the
2\times 2
set of equations:

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma=1}^{2}\left(\omega^{2}\delta_{\alpha\gamma}-F_{q\alpha\gamma}\right)G_{q\gamma\beta}(\omega)=M_{q\alpha\beta}.

(3.9)

Importantly, in all previous RGM studies the moment matrix and the frequency matrix were real and symmetric

;
Mueller2018
.
Both matrices (
III
) and (
III
) are complex.
Moreover, while the moment matrix is Hermitian, the frequency matrix is not.
This is a direct result of the two nonequivalent sites in the unit cell.

Inverting the matrix
\omega^{2}{\bf I}-{\bf F}_{q}
one gets
{\bf G}_{q}(\omega)
.
The final result reads:

\displaystyle G_{q\alpha\beta}(\omega)=\frac{{\sf A}_{q\alpha\beta}(f_{+})}{\omega^{2}-f_{+}}-\frac{{\sf A}_{q\alpha\beta}(f_{-})}{\omega^{2}-f_{-}},

\displaystyle{\sf A}_{q11}(\omega^{2})=\frac{\left(\omega^{2}-F_{q22}\right)M_{q11}+F_{q12}M_{q21}}{f_{+}-f_{-}},

\displaystyle{\sf A}_{q12}(\omega^{2})=\frac{\left(\omega^{2}-F_{q22}\right)M_{q12}+F_{q12}M_{q22}}{f_{+}-f_{-}},

\displaystyle{\sf A}_{q21}(\omega^{2})=\frac{F_{q21}M_{q11}+\left(\omega^{2}-F_{q11}\right)M_{q21}}{f_{+}-f_{-}},

\displaystyle{\sf A}_{q22}(\omega^{2})=\frac{F_{q21}M_{q12}+\left(\omega^{2}-F_{q11}\right)M_{q22}}{f_{+}-f_{-}},

\displaystyle f_{\pm}=\frac{F_{q11}\!+\!F_{q22}}{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\!\frac{F_{q11}\!-\!F_{q22}}{2}\!\right)^{\!2}\!+\!F_{q12}F_{q21}}.

(3.10)

Here
f_{\pm}
stand for the eigenvalues of the frequency matrix
{\bf F}_{q}
.
We search for non-negative eigenvalues
f_{\pm}\geq 0
for all
-\pi\leq q<\pi
.
The solution (
III
) of the set of equations (
3.9
) for the Green’s functions is not the final result yet,
since
G_{q\alpha\beta}(\omega)
(
III
) contain five unknown correlation functions and vertex parameters
which must be determined self-consistently.

To proceed further,
we calculate the correlation function
\langle S_{q\beta}^{-}S_{q\alpha}^{+}\rangle
(
II
) doing standard manipulations.
Namely,
we rewrite the Green’s function (
III
) in the form

\displaystyle G_{q\alpha\beta}(\omega)\!=\!\frac{{\sf A}_{q\alpha\beta}(f_{+})}{2\sqrt{f_{+}}}\!\left(\!\frac{1}{\omega\!-\!\sqrt{f_{+}}}\!-\!\frac{1}{\omega\!+\!\sqrt{f_{+}}}\!\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{{\sf A}_{q\alpha\beta}(f_{-})}{2\sqrt{f_{-}}}\!\left(\!\frac{1}{\omega\!-\!\sqrt{f_{-}}}\!-\!\frac{1}{\omega\!+\!\sqrt{f_{-}}}\!\right),

(3.11)

use the Sokhotski-Plemelj theorem to calculate

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to+0}\left(G_{q\alpha\beta}(\omega+{\rm i}\epsilon)-G_{q\alpha\beta}(\omega-{\rm i}\epsilon)\right)

\displaystyle\!=\!-\frac{{\rm i}\pi{\sf A}_{q\alpha\beta}(f_{+})}{\sqrt{f_{+}}}\left(\!\delta(\omega\!-\!\sqrt{f_{+}})\!-\!\delta(\omega\!+\!\sqrt{f_{+}})\!\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{{\rm i}\pi{\sf A}_{q\alpha\beta}(f_{-})}{\sqrt{f_{-}}}\left(\!\delta(\omega\!-\!\sqrt{f_{-}})\!-\!\delta(\omega\!+\!\sqrt{f_{-}})\!\right),

(3.12)

and finally obtain

\displaystyle\langle S_{q\beta}^{-}S_{q\alpha}^{+}\rangle\!=\!\frac{{\sf A}_{q\alpha\beta}(f_{+})}{2\sqrt{f_{+}}}\!\coth\!\frac{\sqrt{f_{+}}}{2T}\!-\!\frac{{\sf A}_{q\alpha\beta}(f_{-})}{2\sqrt{f_{-}}}\!\coth\!\frac{\sqrt{f_{-}}}{2T}.

Thus, we are in position to calculate correlation functions, see Eq. (
2.13
),
and in particular the ones that were introduced while deriving the closed-set of equations of motion (
3.9
).
That is,

\displaystyle c_{10}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm d}q{\rm e}^{{\rm i}q}\langle S_{q1}^{-}S^{+}_{q1}\rangle,

\displaystyle c_{01}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm d}q{\rm e}^{{\rm i}q}\langle S_{q2}^{-}S^{+}_{q1}\rangle,

\displaystyle c_{20}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm d}q{\rm e}^{2{\rm i}q}\langle S_{q1}^{-}S^{+}_{q1}\rangle,

\displaystyle c_{11}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm d}q{\rm e}^{2{\rm i}q}\langle S_{q2}^{-}S^{+}_{q1}\rangle,

\displaystyle c_{02}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm d}q{\rm e}^{{\rm i}q}\langle S_{q2}^{-}S^{+}_{q2}\rangle.

(3.14)

Moreover,
we have two more equations
\langle S_{j,1}^{z}\rangle=0
and
\langle S_{j,2}^{z}\rangle=0
(sum rules),
which read:

\displaystyle\langle S^{-}_{j,1}\!S^{+}_{j,1}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm d}q\langle S_{q1}^{-}S^{+}_{q1}\rangle=\frac{1}{2},

\displaystyle\langle S^{-}_{j,2}\!S^{+}_{j,2}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm d}q\langle S_{q2}^{-}S^{+}_{q2}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}.

(3.15)

Note that we have two sum rules (
III
) since there are two nonequivalent sites in the unit cell,
whereas in previous RGM studies with one site in the unit cell only one sum rule could be exploited.

We adopt a generalization of the minimal RGM scheme setting

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}_{10}=\alpha_{1}c_{10},\;\;\;\tilde{\alpha}_{20}=\alpha_{1}c_{20},

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}_{01}=\alpha_{2}c_{01},\;\;\;\tilde{\alpha}_{11}=\alpha_{2}c_{11},\;\;\;\tilde{\alpha}_{02}=\alpha_{2}c_{02},

(3.16)

i.e.,
the correlators which contain only the site
1
are improved by the vertex parameter
\alpha_{1}
,
whereas the correlators which contain the site
2
are improved by the vertex parameter
\alpha_{2}
,
see Fig.
1
.
Two vertex parameters go hand in hand with two sum rules for two nonequivalent sites in the unit cell (
III
).
We note in passing that the described scheme reproduces the results of the Kondo-Yamaji paper
Kondo1972
in the limit
J_{1}/J_{2}\to\infty
,
i.e., for the
S=1/2
Heisenberg chain.

To summarize this section,
we face a set of seven coupled equations (
III
), (
III
), and (
III
)
for the determination of the correlators
c_{10}
,
c_{01}
,
c_{20}
,
c_{11}
,
c_{02}
and two vertex parameters
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}

In the next section (and in the Appendix) we discuss how to solve these self-consistent nonlinear equations numerically.
It is worth reminding that in the previous RGM studies of frustrated quantum spin systems
there was a smaller number of unknown quantities and equations to determine them.
For instance,
for the

pyrochlore-lattice Heisenberg antiferromagnet
one deals with three correlation functions and one vertex parameter to be found from a set of four equations

This means that now we are facing a more challenging computational task.

the finite-size lattice calculations explain pretty well the temperature profile of the specific heat
c
(upper panel)
but fail to reproduce the temperature dependence of the uniform susceptibility
\chi_{0}^{zz}
(lower panel).
Thus,
the RGM underestimates the experimental value of
\chi_{0}^{zz}
at
T=0.1
by about 39%,
whereas the ED (Padé approximant
[5,5]
) overestimates it by about 100% (45%).
It should be underlined that from the theoretical side we deal with a pure one-dimensional-system susceptibility
whereas in experiments three-dimensional interactions may be relevant
Scalapino1975
;
Schulz1996
.

VI
Summary

In conclusion,
we have used the RGM approach to investigate the properties of a
S=1/2
antiferromagnetic Heisenberg sawtooth-chain model.
Exact statements are rather scarce for frustrated quantum spin models
and the analytical or numerical methods available to study these systems involve approximations.
Therefore, it is valuable to obtain results by different techniques.
Although various aspects of the
S=1/2
antiferromagnetic Heisenberg sawtooth chain have been studied by many authors
using various approximate techniques,
a consistent analysis of the thermodynamic and dynamic properties still remains an interesting issue.

The sawtooth-chain lattice geometry corresponds to two nonequivalent sites in the unit cell.
We have elaborated the RGM approach for the calculation of the thermodynamic and dynamic quantities of the
S=1/2
Heisenberg model on such a lattice.
A unit cell with nonequivalent sites has less symmetry.
The frequency matrix is complex and non-Hermitian.
Nevertheless,
its eigenvalues are real (but not necessarily nonnegative)
and the application of a minimal RGM scheme
(i.e., with the number of vertex parameters equals to the number of nonequivalent sites in the unit cell)
gives reasonable results.
Although the basic RGM equations (
III
) – (
III
) are valid for any set of parameters,
we discuss in detail how to derive relevant solutions for
J_{2}=1
and
J_{1}
slightly larger than
J_{2}

and illustrate that the RGM approach gives reasonable results up to very low temperatures.
In particular,
the RGM is applicable for atacamite Cu
2
Cl(OH)
3
with
J_{2}=1
,
J_{1}=3.294

Heinze2018
;
Heinze2021
.
As it was mentioned in Sec.
V
,
the RGM results violate three sum rules: Two for the specific heat
c(T)
and one for the static structure factor
S_{q}
.
It might be interesting to check
whether these sum rules (with
e_{0}
as an extra input parameter) may be used to introduce more vertex parameters
this way improving the minimal RGM scheme results.
We hope the gained experience would be useful for other RGM studies in the case of nonequivalent sites in the unit cell,
in particular,
for the square-kagome
S=1/2
Heisenberg antiferromagnet compounds
Fujihala2020
;
Yakubovich2021
;
Liu2022
.

Summarizing this appendix,
we emphasize
that a numerical solution of the self-consistent equations emerging after the Kondo-Yamaji approximation is an important ingredient of the RGM approach.
While in the previous studies this issue has not been discussed in great detail,
in the case of nonequivalent sites in the unit cell, when the number of equations increases,
a controlled solving of this set of equations is vitally necessary to make possible a successful application of the RGM approach.

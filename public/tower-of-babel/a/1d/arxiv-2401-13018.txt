Abstract
. We consider a Leibniz algebra 𝔏 = ℑ ⊕ 𝔙 {\mathfrak{L}}={\mathfrak{I}}\oplus{\mathfrak{V}} over an arbitrary base field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} , being ℑ {\mathfrak{I}} the ideal generated by the products [ x , x ] , x ∈ 𝔏 [x,x],x\in{\mathfrak{L}} . This ideal has a fundamental role in the study presented in our paper. A basis ℬ = { v i } i ∈ I \mathcal{B}=\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I} of 𝔏 {\mathfrak{L}} is called multiplicative if for any i , j ∈ I i,j\in I we have that [ v i , v j ] ∈ 𝔽 ​ v k [v_{i},v_{j}]\in{\mathbb{F}}v_{k} for some k ∈ I k\in I . We associate an adequate graph Γ ⁡ ( 𝔏 , ℬ ) \Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}) to 𝔏 {\mathfrak{L}} relative to ℬ \mathcal{B} . By arguing on this graph we show that 𝔏 {\mathfrak{L}} decomposes as a direct sum of ideals, each one being associated to one connected component of Γ ⁡ ( 𝔏 , ℬ ) \Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}) . Also the minimality of 𝔏 {\mathfrak{L}} and the division property of 𝔏 {\mathfrak{L}} are characterized in terms of the weak symmetry of the defined subgraphs Γ ⁡ ( 𝔏 , ℬ ℑ ) \Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{I}}) and Γ ⁡ ( 𝔏 , ℬ 𝔙 ) \Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{V}}) . 2020MSC : 05C50, 05C25, 17A32, 17A60, 17B65. Keywords : Leibniz algebra, multiplicative basis, graph, structure theory.



1.
Introduction

Leibniz algebras were presented by Bloh
[
6
]
, who called them the
D
-algebras. Two decades later, Loday introduced them in
[
19
]
as a non-antisymmetric analogue of Lie algebras and naming them Leibniz algebras because it was Gottfried W. Leibniz who discovered the
Leibniz rule
for differentiation of functions. This formula is realized when the adjoint map is a derivation and generalizes the well-known Jacobi identity in such a way that any Lie algebra is a Leibniz algebra.

Recently, the structure of Leibniz algebras has been considered in the frameworks of low dimensional algebras, nilpotency and related problems
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
7
,
11
,
17
,
18
]
. We finally have to mention the recent book
[
4
]
, where a complete state of the art on Leibniz algebras theory can be found. The inner structure of Leibniz algebras admitting a multiplicative basis
\mathcal{B}
has been recently studied in
[
8
]
, where the focus is on the characterization of the
\mathcal{B}
-semisimplicity and of the
\mathcal{B}
-simplicity of the algebra.

An interesting problem in graph theory and in abstract algebra consists in characterizing the structure of an algebraic object by the properties satisfied for some graph associated with it (see for instance
[
5
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
). The paper
[
9
]
is devoted to the study of the structure of linear spaces under a bilinear map, by associating to this data an adequate graph.

The main goal of the present paper is to use properties of graphs to study Leibniz algebras
{\mathfrak{L}}
with multiplicative bases
\mathcal{B}
, in order to obtain results about their algebraic structure.
Given a Leibniz algebras
{\mathfrak{L}}
, the ideal
{\mathfrak{I}}
generated by the products
[x,x]
with
x\in{\mathfrak{L}}
plays a relevant role in our study, so we have to adapt the ideas of
[
9
]
to this situation.
By arguing on the associated graph, we show that a Leibniz algebra decomposes as a direct sum of ideals, each one being associated to one connected component of the mentioned graph.
In the last section, we approach the minimality property for this class of algebras by using the subgraphs
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{I}})
and
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{V}})
, related to a given (vector space) decomposition
{\mathfrak{L}}={\mathfrak{I}}\oplus{\mathfrak{V}}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we introduce the (directed) graph associated with a Leibniz algebra
{\mathfrak{L}}
admitting a multiplicative basis
\mathcal{B}
, denoted as
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B})
. By using this graph we prove that
{\mathfrak{L}}
decomposes as a direct sum

{\mathfrak{L}}=\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{I}_{k}

of ideals with a multiplicative basis contained in
\mathcal{B}
, each one being associated with a connected component of
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B})
. In the next section we discuss the relations among various decompositions of
{\mathfrak{L}}
given by different choices of bases. Finally, in Section 4 we relate the weak symmetry of two concrete subgraphs with some properties of
{\mathfrak{L}}
.
The minimality of
{\mathfrak{L}}
and the division property of
\mathcal{B}
are characterized. It is shown that
{\mathfrak{L}}
is minimal if and only if
\mathcal{B}
is of division if and only if the two graphs
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{I}})
and
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{V}})
are weakly symmetric.

All of the Leibniz algebras considered are of arbitrary (finite) dimension over an arbitrary base field
\mathbb{F}
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
Leibniz algebra

{\mathfrak{L}}
is a vector space over a
field
𝔽
endowed with a bilinear product
[\cdot,\cdot]
satisfying the
Leibniz identity

[[y,z],x]=[[y,x],z]+[y,[z,x]],

for any
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{L}}
.

Clearly, Lie algebras are examples of Leibniz algebras. For any
x\in{\mathfrak{L}}
, consider the adjoint map
{\rm ad}_{x}:{\mathfrak{L}}\to{\mathfrak{L}}
defined by
{\rm ad}_{x}(y)=[y,x]
, with
y\in{\mathfrak{L}}
. Observe that the Leibniz identity is equivalent to the assertion that
{\rm ad}_{x}
is a derivation of
{\mathfrak{L}}
, for all
x\in{\mathfrak{L}}
.
A
subalgebra

S
of a Leibniz algebra
{\mathfrak{L}}
is a vector subspace of
{\mathfrak{L}}
such that
[S,S]\subset S
, and an
ideal

I
of
{\mathfrak{L}}
is a subalgebra such that
[I,{\mathfrak{L}}]+[{\mathfrak{L}},I]\subset I
.

The ideal
{\mathfrak{I}}
generated by
\{[x,x]:x\in{\mathfrak{L}}\}
plays an important role in the theory since it determines the (possible) non-Lie character of the Leibniz algebra
{\mathfrak{L}}
. From the Leibniz identity, this ideal satisfies

(1)

[{\mathfrak{L}},{\mathfrak{I}}]=0.

Observe that we can write

{\mathfrak{L}}={\mathfrak{I}}\oplus{\mathfrak{V}},

where
{\mathfrak{V}}
is a linear complement of
{\mathfrak{I}}
in
{\mathfrak{L}}
(as a vector space,
{\mathfrak{V}}
is isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{L}}/{\mathfrak{I}}
, the so-called corresponding Lie algebra of
{\mathfrak{L}}
). Hence, by taking bases
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{I}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{V}}
of
{\mathfrak{I}}
and
{\mathfrak{V}}
, respectively, we get

{\mathcal{B}}={\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{I}}\;\dot{\cup}\;{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{V}},

a basis of
{\mathfrak{L}}
.

.

A decomposition of a Leibniz algebra

3.
Relating the graphs given by different choices of bases

In general, two different multiplicative bases of
{\mathfrak{L}}
determine different associated graphs, which can give rise to different decompositions of
{\mathfrak{L}}
as an orthogonal direct sum of ideals (see Theorem
2.1
). For instance, consider the next example. First we recall that two graphs
(V,E)
and
(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
are
isomorphic
if there exists a bijection
f:V\to V^{\prime}
such that
(v_{i},v_{j})\in E
if and only if
(f(v_{i}),f(v_{j}))\in E^{\prime}
.

We recall that an

satisfying
[f(x),f(y)]=f([x,y])

Definition 3.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{L}}
be a Leibniz algebra. Two bases
\mathcal{B}=\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\{w_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of

are

if there exists an automorphism
f:{\mathfrak{L}}\to{\mathfrak{L}}
satisfying
f(\mathcal{B})=\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{L}}

{\mathfrak{L}}

Proof.

Let us suppose that
\mathcal{B}=\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}

{\mathfrak{L}}

Let us denote by

\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B})
and
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
, respectively. Taking into account that
V=\mathcal{B}
and
V^{\prime}=\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, and the fact
f(\mathcal{B})=\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, we have that the map
f
defines a bijection from
V
to
V^{\prime}
.

Given
x,y\in V
, we want to show that
(x,y)\in E
if and only if
(f(x),f(y))\in E^{\prime}
. Suppose that
(x,y)\in E
. Then there are
z\in V
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
such that either
[x,z]=\lambda y
or
[z,x]=\lambda y
. Applying
f
to these relations we get that either
[f(x),f(z)]=\lambda f(y)
or
[f(z),f(x)]=\lambda f(y)
. Thus,
(f(x),f(y))\in E^{\prime}
. The same argument using
f^{-1}
shows that if
(f(x),f(y))\in E^{\prime}
then
(x,y)\in E
, which concludes the proof that
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B})
and
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
are isomorphic via
f
.

∎

Consider a graph

. Given two vertices

, a

is

if for every
(v_{j},v_{i})\in E
there exists a directed path from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
.

In particular, we have that every symmetric graph is weakly symmetric.

Definition 4.2
.

Given a graph
(V,E)
, we say that two vertices
v_{i},v_{j}\in V
are
strongly connected
if there exists a directed path from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
and one from
v_{j}
to
v_{i}
. (As usual, we consider that there is a directed path using no edges from any vertex to itself.)
Additionally, we say that the graph
(V,E)
is

if any two vertices of

Remark 4.1
.

Note that a graph is strongly connected if and only if it is connected and weakly symmetric. Indeed, if any pair of vertices of a graph is strongly connected, then clearly the graph is connected and weakly symmetric. Conversely, suppose that
(V,E)
is connected and weakly symmetric. Given vertices
v_{i},v_{j}\in V
, let
(v_{i}=v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{n}}=v_{j})
be an undirected path from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
. For each
1\leq r\leq n-1
, if
(v_{i_{r}},v_{i_{r+1}})\not\in E
, then we must have
(v_{i_{r+1}},v_{i_{r}})\in E
and by weak symmetry there is a directed path
(v_{i_{r}}=v_{k_{1}},\dots,v_{k_{m}}=v_{i_{r+1}})
from
v_{i_{r}}
to
v_{i_{r+1}}
; thus we can replace in the given undirected path from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
the edge
(v_{i_{r+1}},v_{i_{r}})
with the directed path
(v_{i_{r}}=v_{k_{1}},\dots,v_{k_{m}}=v_{i_{r+1}})
. Proceeding in this fashion for all
1\leq r\leq n-1
, we end up with a directed path from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
, as required.

In the case of Leibniz algebras, there can be no edges of type
(e_{i},u_{j})
, with
e_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{I}}
,
u_{j}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{V}}
. So the notion of weak symmetry is not well adjusted to the graph associated to a Leibniz algebra
{\mathfrak{L}}
. Due to this fact, we consider two subgraphs, one related to the ideal
{\mathfrak{I}}
and the other to
{\mathfrak{V}}
, isomorphic to the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{L}}/{\mathfrak{I}}
.

.

Let

. By a

Let

by removing the edges

with

and

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{L}}
be a Leibniz algebra with a multiplicative basis
\mathcal{B}:=\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.
Given
v_{i},v_{j}\in\mathcal{B}_{K}
, we have that
v_{j}\in\mathcal{I}(v_{i})
if and only if there exists a directed path from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
in
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{K})
, for whatever
K\in\{{\mathfrak{I}},{\mathfrak{V}}\}
.

Proof.

We consider the direct implication first. For
x,y\in{\mathfrak{L}}
, write
f(x,y)
to denote either one of the elements
[x,y]
or
[y,x]
. Then

(5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(v_{i})=\sum\mathbb{F}f(f(\cdots f(f(v_{i},w_{1}),w_{2}),\cdots),w_{k}),

where the sum is over all
k\geq 0
(in case
k=0
, by convention, the corresponding subspace is
\mathbb{F}v_{i}
), all
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\mathcal{B}
and all possible choices for
f(x,y)=[x,y]
or
f(x,y)=[y,x]
. Indeed, the right-hand side of (
5
) is a subspace of
{\mathfrak{L}}
which is closed under left and right products with elements of
{\mathfrak{L}}
, thus it is an ideal. Since it contains
v_{i}
, it contains
\mathcal{I}(v_{i})
and in fact it must equal
\mathcal{I}(v_{i})
as every summand from the right-hand side of (
5
) is contained in
\mathcal{I}(v_{i})
. Observe also that, since
\mathcal{B}
is multiplicative and
v_{i},w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\mathcal{B}
, each term on the right-hand side of (
5
) is either
0
or a nonzero multiple of
v_{l}
, for some
v_{l}\in\mathcal{B}
. It follows that if
v_{i},v_{j}\in\mathcal{B}
and
v_{j}\in\mathcal{I}(v_{i})
, then there exist
k\geq 0
,
w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\in\mathcal{B}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
such that

\displaystyle f(f(\cdots f(f(v_{i},w_{1}),w_{2}),\cdots),w_{k})=\lambda v_{j},

where above each occurrence of
f(x,y)
has been assigned a specific interpretation as
[x,y]
or
[y,x]
, which we fix. For each
1\leq l\leq k
, let
v_{i_{l}}
be the unique element of
\mathcal{B}
such that
f(f(\cdots f(f(v_{i},w_{1}),w_{2}),\cdots),w_{l})
is a nonzero multiple of
v_{i_{l}}
. Then there is a directed path
(v_{i},v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k-1}},v_{j})
from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
in
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B})
. To finish, we just need to argue that this path is contained in the subgraph
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{K})
, assuming that
v_{i},v_{j}\in\mathcal{B}_{K}
. This is clear as
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{I}}\,\dot{\cup}\,\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{V}}
and there are no edges in
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B})
from a vertex in
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{I}}
to a vertex in
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{V}}
.

For the converse implication, assume that
(v_{i}=v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}}=v_{j})
is a directed path from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
. Since
(v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}})
is an edge, there exist
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
and
w_{1}\in\mathcal{B}
such that
f(v_{i_{1}},w_{1})=\lambda v_{i_{2}}
, so
v_{i_{2}}=f(v_{i_{1}},\lambda^{-1}w_{1})\in\mathcal{I}(v_{i})
. Proceeding successively as above along the path, we conclude that
v_{i_{l}}\in\mathcal{I}(v_{i})
for all
1\leq l\leq k
, so
v_{j}\in\mathcal{I}(v_{i})
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
{\mathfrak{L}}={\mathfrak{I}}\oplus{\mathfrak{V}}
be a Leibniz algebra with a multiplicative basis
\mathcal{B}={\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{I}}\;\dot{\cup}\;{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{V}}
, where
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{I}}=\{e_{k}\}_{k\in K}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{V}}=\{u_{j}\}_{j\in J}
. It is said that
\mathcal{B}
is of
weak division
if

•

0\neq[e_{i},u_{j}]=\lambda e_{k}
implies
e_{i}\in\mathcal{I}(e_{k})
.

•

0\neq[u_{i},u_{k}]=\lambda u_{p}
implies
u_{i},u_{k}\in\mathcal{I}(u_{p})
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{L}}
be a Leibniz algebra admitting a multiplicative basis
\mathcal{B}={\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{I}}\;\dot{\cup}\;{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{V}}
, where
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{I}}=\{e_{k}\}_{k\in K}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{V}}=\{u_{j}\}_{j\in J}.
Under these conditions,
\mathcal{B}
is of weak division if and only if the subgraph
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{K})
is weakly symmetric, for
K\in\{{\mathfrak{I}},{\mathfrak{V}}\}
.

Proof.

Let us suppose that the multiplicative basis
\mathcal{B}
is of weak division. First we prove that
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{V}})
is weakly symmetric. Given an edge
(u_{i},u_{j})
of
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{V}})
we have that there exist
u_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{V}}
and
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
such that
[u_{i},u_{k}]=\lambda_{j}u_{j}
or
[u_{k},u_{i}]=\lambda_{j}u_{j}
. Since
\mathcal{B}
is of weak division we get
u_{i}\in\mathcal{I}(u_{j})
, and by Lemma
4.1
there exists a directed path from
u_{j}
to
u_{i}
, so

\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{V}})
is weakly symmetric.

Now we prove that
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{I}})
is weakly symmetric. Let us take an edge
(e_{i},e_{j})
of
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{I}})
. So there exists
u_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{V}}
such that
[e_{i},u_{k}]=\lambda_{j}e_{j}
, for some
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
. Since
\mathcal{B}
is of weak division we get
e_{i}\in\mathcal{I}(e_{j})
. As in the previous case, by Lemma
4.1
we conclude
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{I}})
is weakly symmetric.

Conversely, let us suppose that the subgraph
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{K})
is weakly symmetric, for
K\in\{{\mathfrak{I}},{\mathfrak{V}}\}
. We want to show that
\mathcal{B}
is of weak division.
Given
e_{i},e_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{I}},u_{j}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{V}}
such that
0\neq[e_{i},u_{j}]=\lambda e_{k}
. Then
(e_{i},e_{k})
is an edge of
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{I}})
. Since
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{I}})
is weakly symmetric, there exists a directed path from
e_{k}
to
e_{i}
. Then, by Lemma
4.1
it follows that
e_{i}\in\mathcal{I}(e_{k})
. In a similar way, for
u_{i},u_{k},u_{p}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{V}}
such that
0\neq[u_{i},u_{k}]=\lambda u_{p}
, we get that
(u_{i},u_{p})
and
(u_{k},u_{p})
are edges of
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{V}})
. Analogously to the previous case, Lemma
4.1
lets us assert that
u_{i},u_{k}\in\mathcal{I}(u_{p})
. In conclusion,
\mathcal{B}
is of weak division, as required.

.

Let
{\mathfrak{L}}
be a Leibniz algebra admitting a multiplicative basis
\mathcal{B}

\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{I}})

{\mathfrak{L}}

Let

e_{i_{0}}

\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{I}})

. Applying Lemma

e_{i}\in{\mathcal{I}}(e_{i_{0}})\subset I
, therefore

{\mathfrak{I}}\subset I.

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
{\mathfrak{L}}
be a Leibniz algebra admitting a multiplicative basis
\mathcal{B}
. If the subgraph
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{V}})

{\mathfrak{L}}

We will prove that

By hypothesis, there exists a directed path from

(6)

Definition 4.5
.

A Leibniz algebra
{\mathfrak{L}}
admitting a multiplicative basis
\mathcal{B}
is
minimal
if its only non-zero ideals admitting a multiplicative basis contained in
\mathcal{B}
are
{\mathfrak{I}}
and
{\mathfrak{L}}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{L}}
be a Leibniz algebra with multiplicative basis
\mathcal{B}={\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{I}}\;\dot{\cup}\;{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathfrak{V}}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

i.

{\mathfrak{L}}
is minimal.

ii.

\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{K})
is strongly connected, for
K\in\{{\mathfrak{I}},{\mathfrak{V}}\}
.

iii.

\mathcal{B}
is of weak division and
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{K})
is connected, for
K\in\{{\mathfrak{I}},{\mathfrak{V}}\}
.

Proof.

The equivalence between ii. and iii. follows by Proposition
4.1
and Remark
4.1
.
Now, we prove that ii. implies i. Let

be a non-zero ideal of
{\mathfrak{L}}
admitting a multiplicative basis contained in

. We have two possibilities, either

or

. In the first case by Proposition
4.2
we infer that

, and in the second case by Proposition

we have

; consequently
{\mathfrak{L}}
is minimal.

It remains to show that i. implies ii. Suppose that
{\mathfrak{L}}
is minimal. First we take care of the case

. Let

. The minimality of
{\mathfrak{L}}
implies that
{\mathcal{I}}(e_{i})={\mathfrak{I}}
, so in particular

. Then, by Lemma

, there exists a directed path from

to

in
\Gamma({\mathfrak{L}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{I}})
, proving that the latter is strongly connected.
Similarly, given

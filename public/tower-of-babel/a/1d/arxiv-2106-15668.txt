Two extremal problems in the light of Lex graphs
Kristina Dedndreaj

Abstract
Extremal problems involving independent sets are much studied. Two of the most important extremal problems in this context are concerned with the sharp upper bounds for the number of independent sets of fixed size and the independence number. In literature, these sharp upper bounds are derived in completely different contexts. In this paper, we show that both of these sharp upper bounds can be derived by considering Lex graphs. More exactly, they depend on two parameters of a sequence defined on them.

Lemma

Let

If

Proof.

The proof can be done by induction on

Definition 2
.

[
4

Let

be two natural numbers such that

Remark 2

From Definition

is the smallest number in the set
\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
which satisfies

m\leq\frac{k(2n-k-1)}{2}.

(9)

Therefore the value for

is

\left\lceil n-\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+n^{2}-n-2m}\right\rceil.

(10)

The value for

is

m-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}n-i=m-\frac{(k-1)(2n-k)}{2}.

(11)

Let

be a lex graph on
n

m

. The depth of

. Let

A(i)=\{v\in[n]:v<i\}

N(i)=\begin{cases}V\setminus\{i\}&\text{ for }i\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\},\\
A(i)\cup B&\text{ for }i=k,\\
A(k+1)&\text{ for }i\in\{k+1,\ldots,k+p_{k}\},\\
A(k)&\text{ for }i\in\{k+p_{k}+1,\ldots,n\}.\end{cases}

For given

{\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}}

Theorem 4
.

Let
L(n,m)
be the lex graph with
n
vertices,
m
edges and depth
k
. Then the maximum independent sets of
L(n,m)
have size
n-k
.

Proof.

Let
A=\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
,
B=\{k,p_{k}+1,\ldots,n\}
and
C=\{k+1,\ldots,n\}
. Notice that
A\cup B\cup C=V
and
B^{\complement}\cap C^{\complement}\subset A
. Also, notice that no element of
A
can belong to an independent set with cardinality greater than one because for such elements we have
N(i)=V\setminus\{i\}
. We claim that every independent set with cardinality greater than one is a subset of
B
or a subset of
C
. Assume the opposite, that there exists an independent set
I
with cardinality greater than one which is not a subset of
B
nor a subset of
C
. This means that

I\cap B^{\complement}\neq\emptyset\text{ and }I\cap C^{\complement}\neq\emptyset,

(12)

therefore,
I\cap A\neq\emptyset
. As a consequence, there exists an element of
A
which belongs to an independent set with cardinality greater than one, a contradiction.

Next we will show that the sets
B,C
are independent dominating sets and therefore maximal independent sets. That they are independent it is clear from the construction of the graph
L(n,m)
. The set

B
is dominating because every element of
B^{\complement}
is adjacent to an element of
B
. In the same way we conclude that the set
C
is dominating.

Note that
|B|=n-k-p_{k}+1
and
|C|=n-k
. If
p_{k}>1
, the set
C
is the only maximum independent set of the graph
L(n,m)
. If
p_{k}=1
then
|B|=|C|
and the graph has two maximum independent sets,
B
and
C
.
∎

Since the lex graph
L(n,m)
maximizes the number of independent sets of fixed size for
G(n,m)
then
n-k
is a sharp upper bound for the independence number
\alpha
of
G(n,m)
-graphs. Therefore by combining Remark
2

Theorem 5
.

Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges and with independence number
\alpha
then

\alpha\leq\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+n^{2}-n-2m}\right\rfloor

(13)

and the bound is sharp.

Proof.

Note that for real numbers
a
and
b
we have:
a-(b+1)<a-\lceil b\rceil\leq a-b
. This imples that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+n^{2}-n-2m}

\displaystyle<n-\left\lceil n-\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+n^{2}-n-2m}\right\rceil

(14)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+n^{2}-n-2m}

(15)

and therefore by the definition of the floor function

n-\left\lceil n-\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+n^{2}-n-2m}\right\rceil=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+n^{2}-n-2m}\right\rfloor.

(16)

By Theorem
4
we have that
\alpha\leq n-k
. By inserting the solution for
k
from Remark
2
we have:

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\leq n-\left\lceil n-\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+n^{2}-n-2m}\right\rceil

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+n^{2}-n-2m}\right\rfloor.

∎

Theorem 6
.

Let
L(n,m)
be the lex graph with
n
vertices,
m
edges and depth
k
. Then the number of independent sets of size
r>2
in
L(n,m)
, denoted
i_{r}
is

\binom{n-k-p_{k}}{r-1}+\binom{n-k}{r}.

Proof.

There are two types of independent sets of size
r
:

1.

Independent sets which contain the vertex
k
. There are

\binom{n-k-p_{k}}{r-1}

(17)

such sets.

2.

Independent sets which do not contain the element
k
. There are

\binom{n-k}{r}

(18)

such sets.

∎

By using the extremality of
L(n,m)
, Theorem
6
and Remark

Corollary 1
.

Let
G(n,m)
be a graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges and let
i_{r}
be its number of independent sets of size
r
then

i_{r}\leq\binom{n-k-p_{k}}{r-1}+\binom{n-k}{r}

(19)

where
k
and
p_{k}
are as in Remark
2

and the bound is sharp.

Remark 3
.

When

and the Inequality (

i_{r}\leq\binom{n-k}{r}=\binom{n-k-1}{r}+\binom{n-k-1}{r-1}.

(20)

Note that

1.

m=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}n-i\Longleftrightarrow\binom{n}{2}-m=\binom{s}{2}+s
and

2.

\left\{\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+n^{2}-n-2m}\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\Longleftrightarrow\binom{n}{2}-m=\binom{s}{2}+s

where

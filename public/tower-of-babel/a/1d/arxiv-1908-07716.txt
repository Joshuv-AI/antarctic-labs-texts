Existence of Large deviations rate function for any S S -unimodal map Thanks: Keywords : Large Deviation Principle; S S -unimodal map; renormalization
Hiroki Takahasi and Masato Tsujii

Abstract
. For an arbitrary negative Schwarzian unimodal map with a non-flat critical point, we establish the level-2 Large Deviation Principle for empirical distributions. We also give an example of a bimodal map for which the level-2 Large Deviation Principle does not hold.

1.
Introduction

A main objective of the ergodic theory of smooth dynamical systems is to understand long-term behaviors of typical orbits for a majority of systems. Much effort has been dedicated to
constructing physically relevant invariant measures which statistically predict
typical asymptotic behaviors. A refined description of the dynamics requires the analysis of
atypical, or transient behaviors before orbits settle down
to equilibrium. The theory of large deviations is concerned with such rare events.
The Large Deviation Principle (LDP) asserts the existence of the rate function
which controls probabilities of rare events on exponential scale.

It is now classical that a transitive uniformly hyperbolic (Axiom A) attractor supports a unique
Sinaĭ-Ruelle-Bowen measure
[
2
,
32
]
, and Lebesgue almost every orbit in the basin of attraction is asymptotically
distributed with respect to this measure.
The LDP for Axiom A attractors was established by Kifer
[
20
]
, Orey
\&
Pelikan
[
25
]
and Takahashi
[
34
]
.
For one-dimensional non-hyperbolic systems,
after several progresses
[
7
,
27
,
28
]
, a major advance was made in

[
8
]
which establishes the LDP
for an arbitrary
C^{1+\alpha}
multimodal map with non-flat critical points
that is topologically exact.
The aim of this paper is to treat
what is left off in
[
8
]
:
the LDP for renormalizable unimodal maps, including infinitely renormalizable ones.
The conclusion is that the LDP holds for an arbitrary
S
-unimodal map
with a non-flat critical point.

We introduce our setting and results in more precise terms.
Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}
be a compact non-degenerate interval.
A
C^{1}
map
f\colon X\to X
is called
unimodal

if it has a unique critical point
c
, which is contained in
{\rm int}(X)
and is an extremum, and satisfies
f(\partial X)\subset\partial X
.
An
S
-unimodal map

f
is a unimodal map of class
C^{3}
on
X\setminus\{c\}
with
negative Schwarzian derivative
D^{3}f/Df-(3/2)(D^{2}f/Df)^{2}<0

such that if
x\in\partial X
is a fixed point of
f
then
|Df(x)|>1
.
We say the critical point
c
is
non-flat

if there exist
\ell>1
and

C^{3}
diffeomorphisms
\varphi
and
\psi
defined on a neighborhood of
c
and
f(c)

respectively such that
\varphi(c)=0=\psi(f(c))
and
|\varphi(x)|^{\ell}=|\psi(f(x))|
for all
x
near
c
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
denote the space of Borel probability measures on
X
endowed with the weak* topology.
The empirical measure at time
n
with initial point
x\in X
is
the uniform probability distribution on the orbit
\{x,f(x),\ldots,f^{n-1}(x)\}
, denoted by

\delta_{x}^{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{f^{k}(x)}\in\mathcal{M},

where
\delta_{f^{k}(x)}
denotes the unit point mass at
f^{k}(x)
.
For a Borel set
A\subset X
, we write
|A|
for its Lebesgue measure. Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem A

(level-2 LDP)
.

For an arbitrary
S
-unimodal map
f\colon X\to X
with a non-flat critical point,
the level-2 LDP holds, namely, there exists a convex lower
semicontinuous function
I\colon\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty]
such that

(\text{\rm lower bound})\ \ \ \ \displaystyle{\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left|\{x\in X\colon\delta_{x}^{n}\in\mathcal{G}\}\right|\geq-\inf_{\mathcal{G}}I}

for any open set
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{M}
, and

(\text{\rm upper bound})\ \ \ \ \displaystyle{\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left|\{x\in X\colon\delta_{x}^{n}\in\mathcal{C}\}\right|\leq-\inf_{\mathcal{C}}I}

for any closed set
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{M}
.

Hereafter we follow the convention

\sup\emptyset=-\infty
,
\inf\emptyset=\infty
,
\log 0=-\infty
.
The function
I
is called
the rate function
.
Since
\mathcal{M}
is a metrizable space, the LDP determines the rate function uniquely
[
29
, Theorem 2.13]
.

The rate function
I
in Theorem A is given as follows.
Let
\mathcal{M}(f)
denote the set of elements of
\mathcal{M}
which are
f

For each
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f)
, the limit

\chi(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|Df^{n}(x)|

exists for

and belongs to
\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
, because

is uniformly bounded from above. We set

(1.1)

\chi^{+}(x)=\max\{\chi(x),0\}\in\mathbb{R}

and define the
Lyapunov exponent
of

by

(1.2)

\chi(\mu)=\int\chi^{+}(x)d\mu(x).

Let
h(\mu)
denote the measure-theoretic entropy of
\mu
with respect to
f
. Define a
free energy

F\colon\mathcal{M}\to[-\infty,0]
by

(1.3)

F(\mu)=\begin{cases}h(\mu)-\chi(\mu)\ &\text{if
$\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f)$,}\\
-\infty\ &\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The rate function
I
in Theorem A is defined to be the minus of the upper semicontinuous regularization of
F
:

(1.4)

I(\mu)=-\inf_{\mathcal{G}\ni\mu}\sup_{\mathcal{G}}F(\nu).

Here, the infimum is taken over all open sets
\mathcal{G}
in
\mathcal{M}
containing
\mu
.
Note that the entropy and the Lyapunov exponent are upper semicontinuous as functions of measures.
In cases (I) and (II), the Lyapunov exponent is in fact continuous (see Lemma
2.11
) and thus

I=-F.

In case (III),
the Lyapunov exponent may fail to be lower semicontinuous
[
4

I\neq-F
.

The next theorem asserts that Theorem A cannot be extended to maps with multiple critical points.

Theorem B

(Breakdown of the LDP)
.

There exists a
C^{3}
interval map with exactly two non-degenerate critical points for which the level-2 LDP does not hold.

The rest of this paper consists of four sections.
In Section 2, we introduce several basic definitions and results on
S
-unimodal maps.
In Section 3, we prove the lower bound for open sets in Theorem A.
In Section 4, we prove the upper bound for closed sets in Theorem A.
In Section 5
we prove Theorem B.

For only finitely renormalizable maps, the dynamics of typical orbits consist of two stages: transition to the deepest
renormalization cycle,
and circulation within that cycle.
The LDP restricted to the deepest renormalization cycle mostly
follows from the known result
[
8
]
.
For infinitely renormalizable maps, orbits contained in sufficiently deep cycles are
approximated by the post-critical measure supported on the attracting Cantor set.
Therefore, all we have to do is to analyze
the transitions between renormalization cycles.
The main point of Section 3 is to prove that any
f
-invariant measure supported on a hyperbolic set
is approximated with orbit segments
from finitely many subintervals of
X
(see Proposition
3.1
).
This claim is standard if the measure is ergodic, and if non-ergodic then we glue orbit segments together which
approximate its ergodic components.

The main technique used in Section 4 to derive the upper bound is a ‘coarse graining approach’.
Here the set of orbits with prescribed time averages of continuous functions are coarse grained
(see Section
4.3
), and estimates on the resultant ‘clusters’
are transferred to the large deviations upper bound on empirical measures (see Propositions
4.1
and
4.4
).
The estimate on each cluster consists of contributions from the uniformly hyperbolic dynamics
on each renormalization cycle and the transitions between them.

A counterexample of a bimodal map we construct in the proof of Theorem B is non-transitive and
has two non-degenerate critical points, one of which is non-recurrent.
With a slight modification of our construction, one can find
a counterexample in polynomial maps of degree
3
,
see Remark
5.5
.
It is plausible that there is an
S
-unimodal map with a non-recurrent flat critical point for which the level-2 LDP fails. For a transitive
S
-unimodal map with a non-recurrent flat critical point and all periodic orbits hyperbolic repelling, the level-2 LDP was shown in

[
9
]
under an assumption that the criticality increases at some specific rates.

2.1.
Classification of attracting periodic orbits

Let
f\colon X\to X
be a unimodal map, and let

\{f^{k}(x)\}_{k=0}^{p-1}

be a periodic orbit of
f
with prime period
p
.
We say

\{f^{k}(x)\}_{k=0}^{p-1}
is:

hyperbolic attracting
if
|Df^{p}(x)|<1
;

neutral
if
|Df^{p}(x)|=1
;
hyperbolic repelling
if
|Df^{p}(x)|>1
.
The
basin
of the periodic orbit
\{f^{k}(x)\}_{k=0}^{p-1}
is the set of points in
X
whose omega-limit set is contained in the set
\{f^{k}(x)\}_{k=0}^{p-1}
.
We say
\{f^{k}(x)\}_{k=0}^{p-1}
is

attracting
if its basin contains an open set. In this case, the union of the
connected components of the basin containing a point from
\{f^{k}(x)\}_{k=0}^{p-1}

is called the
immediate basin

of
\{f^{k}(x)\}_{k=0}^{p-1}
.
Each connected component of the immediate basin
contains exactly one point from
\{f^{k}(x)\}_{k=0}^{p-1}
.

If
f:X\to X
is
S
-unimodal, a neutral periodic point
x
with prime period
p
satisfies either

(a)
D^{2}f^{p}(x)\neq 0
,  or (b)
D^{2}f^{p}(x)=0
and
D^{3}f^{p}(x)/Df^{p}(x)<0
.

In case (a), the periodic point
x
is locally attracting
from only one side and in case (b) it is attracting from both sides.
Hence a periodic point is attracting if and only if it is hyperbolic attracting or neutral. The immediate basin of an attracting periodic orbit
contains the critical point
[
24
, Chapter II, Lemma 6.1, Theorem 6.1]
. Therefore, there is at most one attracting periodic orbit,
denoted by
O(f)
. Let
B(f)
denote the immediate basin of
O(f)
. If
f
has no attracting periodic orbit, we put
B(f)=\emptyset
for convenience.
Let
\delta_{O(f)}
denote the element of
\mathcal{M}(f)
that is supported on
O(f)
.
An attracting periodic orbit
\delta_{O(f)}
is called
two-sided attracting

if it is hyperbolic attracting or neutral in case (b).
Otherwise (
i.e.
neutral in case (a)), an attracting periodic orbit
\{f^{k}(x)\}_{k=0}^{p-1}
is called
one-sided attracting
.

2.2.
Renormalization of
S
-unimodal maps

Let
f\colon X\to X
be a unimodal map with a non-flat critical point
c

A proper closed subinterval
J

of

if the following hold (c.f.

\circ

the interiors of

\circ

f^{p}(J)\subset J

f^{p}(\partial J)\subset\partial J

\circ

one of the intervals

\circ

J
is maximal with respect to these properties: if
J^{\prime}\supset J
is a closed interval which is strictly contained in

J^{\prime}=J

We define a strictly decreasing sequence of closed intervals

X=J_{0}\supsetneq J_{1}\supsetneq J_{2}\supsetneq\cdots

which contain the critical point

is restrictive of period

for

, inductively as follows.
Given
J_{m}
and
p_{m}

If

If

If this inductive definition continues for arbitrarily large

\bar{m}(f)=\infty

We say

\bar{m}(f)=\infty

We assume

We classify them into the following cases:

(A)

(B)

(C)

Let

One can check this by combining

In case (B), we have

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to X
be a renormalizable
S
-unimodal map, and let

be a restrictive interval with period

Let

Since the renormalized map

0\leq k<p_{m}
, which form a hyperbolic repelling periodic orbit of prime period
p_{m}
.

For each
m\in\{0,\ldots,m(f)-1\}

we define

K_{m,m+1}={\rm cl}(K_{m}\setminus K_{m+1}).

We also define

\Gamma_{m}=\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}f^{-n}\left(\bigcup_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{m}}P\right).

Note that
\Gamma_{m}
for
1\leq m<m(f)
may contain an attracting periodic orbit only if
m=m(f)-1
,
and this periodic orbit is one-sided attracting.
In this case,
K_{m}

is disjoint from the interior of the basin of the attracting periodic orbit.
Set
\Gamma_{-1}=\partial X.

For convenience, in the case
m(f)<\infty

we further define

\Gamma_{m(f)}=\begin{cases}O(f)&\quad\text{if $f$ has a two-sided attracting periodic orbit, }\\
\bigcup_{k=0}^{p_{m(f)}-1}f^{k}(L_{m(f)})&\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The sets
\Gamma_{m}
are non-empty, pairwise disjoint closed sets. For each
m\in\{-1,0,\ldots,m(f)\}
we set

\mathcal{M}_{m}(f)=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f)\colon{\rm supp}(\mu)\subset\Gamma_{m}\},

where
{\rm supp}(\mu)
denotes the smallest closed subset of
X
with full
\mu
-measure.

The sets
\mathcal{M}_{m}(f)
are pairwise disjoint,
and if
m(f)<\infty
then the convex hull of
\bigcup_{m=-1}^{m(f)}\mathcal{M}_{m}(f)
is
\mathcal{M}(f)
.
If
m(f)<\infty
and
f
has a one-sided attracting periodic orbit, then

L_{m(f)}
is contained in the basin of the attracting periodic orbit,
and so
\mathcal{M}_{m(f)}(f)=\emptyset
.

2.4.
Symbolic dynamics on each cycle

Let
f\colon X\to X
be an
S
-unimodal map such that
m(f)\geq 1
.
For each
m\in\{0,\ldots,m(f)-1\}
,
there is a topological Markov chain over the finite alphabet
\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{m}
determined by the transition matrix

(M_{PQ})_{P,Q\in\mathscr{P}_{m}},\quad M_{PQ}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $f(P)\supset Q$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer
and let
P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-1}\in\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{m}
.
The word
P_{0}P_{1}\cdots P_{n-1}
of length
n
is
admissible
if

M_{P_{k}P_{k+1}}=1
holds for
0\leq k\leq n-2
.
Let

E^{n}_{m}
denote the set of admissible words of elements of
\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{m}

of length
n
.
Let
\Sigma_{m}
denote the set of one-sided infinite
sequences
\{P_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
of elements of
\mathscr{P}_{m}

such that
P_{0}\cdots P_{n-1}\in E_{m}^{n}
holds for all
n\geq 2
.
We endow
\Sigma_{m}
with the
the restriction of the product topology of the discrete topology of
\mathscr{P}_{m}
.
Let
\sigma_{m}\colon\Sigma_{m}\to\Sigma_{m}
denote the left shift:
\sigma_{m}(\{P_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty})=\{P_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
.
For each
P_{0}P_{1}\cdots P_{n-1}\in E^{n}_{m}
, we set

I_{P_{0}P_{1}\cdots P_{n-1}}=\bigcap_{k=0}^{n-1}f^{-k}(P_{k})
,
and define

\pi_{m}\colon\Sigma_{m}\to\Gamma_{m}
by

(2.4)

\pi_{m}(\{P_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty})\in\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}I_{P_{0}\cdots P_{n-1}}.

By
[
22
, Main Theorem]
,
f
has no wandering interval, and so any homterval is contained in the basin of an attracting periodic orbit.
Since
\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}I_{P_{0}\cdots P_{n-1}}

is not contained in the basin of an attracting periodic orbit, it is not a homterval, namely, a singleton.
Hence,
\pi_{m}
is well-defined by (
2.4
).

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
f\colon X\to X
be an
S
-unimodal map with a non-flat critical point such that
m(f)\geq 1
.
For each
m\in\{0,\ldots,m(f)-1\}
,
the restriction
f|_{\Gamma_{m}}:\Gamma_{m}\to\Gamma_{m}
is topologically conjugate by the conjugacy map
\pi_{m}
to the topological Markov chain
\sigma_{m}\colon\Sigma_{m}\to\Sigma_{m}
.

Proof.

By definition,
\pi_{m}
is continuous and surjective. Since the elements of
\mathscr{P}_{m}
are pairwise disjoint,
\pi_{m}
is injective and has a continuous inverse.
For each
P_{0}P_{1}\cdots P_{n-1}\in E^{n}_{m}
,
the Markov property of
\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{m}
implies

f^{k-1}(I_{P_{0}\cdots P_{n-1}})=I_{P_{k-1}\cdots P_{n-1}}
for
1\leq k\leq n
.
Hence
f\circ\pi_{m}=\pi_{m}\circ\sigma_{m}
holds.
We have verified that
f|_{\Gamma_{m}}
is topologically conjugate
to
\sigma_{m}
by
\pi_{m}
.
∎

2.5.
Distortion estimate on each cycle

Let
Y
be a non-gedegenerate compact interval in
X
and let
g\colon Y\to X
be a
C^{1}
map.
Let
J
be a subinterval of
Y
such that the restriction of
g
to
J
is a diffeomorphism onto its image. By a
distortion of
g
on
J
we mean the quantity

\sup_{x,y\in J}\frac{|Dg(x)|}{|Dg(y)|}.

We will frequently use the following distortion estimates on each cycle.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
f\colon X\to X
be an
S
-unimodal map with a non-flat critical point such that
m(f)\geq 1
. For each

m\in\{0,\ldots,m(f)-1\}
the following hold:

(a)

if
\Gamma_{m}
does not contain a neutral periodic orbit, then
there exists a constant
\gamma_{m}\geq 1
such that
if
n\geq 1
then the distortion of
f^{n}
on any
connected component of
\bigcap_{k=0}^{n-1}f^{-k}(K_{m,m+1})
is bounded by
\gamma_{m}
.

(b)

if

\Gamma_{m}
contains a neutral periodic orbit, then for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N\geq 1
such that
if
n\geq N
then the distortion of
f^{n}
on any connected component of

\bigcap_{k=0}^{n-1}f^{-k}(K_{m,m+1})
is bounded by
e^{\varepsilon n}
.

Proof.

Since
f
is
C^{2}
, the restriction of
\log|Df|
to
the union of the elements of
\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{m}

not containing a point
from an attracting periodic orbit
is Lipschitz continuous. Moreover, by Mañé’s theorem
[
23
, Theorem A]
,
the maximal
f
-invariant set in this union is a hyperbolic set.
Hence (a) holds.

As for (b),
if
\Gamma_{m}
contains a neutral periodic orbit, then

m=m(f)-1
and
\partial J_{m(f)}
contains a point from the neutral periodic orbit. We need the following lemma, under the notation in Section
2.4
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For each

m\in\{0,\ldots,m(f)-1\}

we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup\{\left|I_{\omega}\right|\colon\omega\in E^{n}_{m}\}=0.

Proof.

Suppose there exist
\varepsilon>0
and an infinite subset
F
of

\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E^{n}_{m}
such that

|I_{\omega}|>\varepsilon
for all
\omega\in F
.
Since
X
has a finite diameter,
we can choose a sequence
(\omega^{(n)})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
F

such that
(I_{\omega^{(n)}})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a nested sequence.
Since
\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}I_{\omega^{(n)}}
is not a singleton,
it is a homterval. However,
it is not contained in the basin of an attracting periodic orbit. We obtain a contradiction to
[
22
, Main Theorem]
.
∎

Let
\varepsilon>0
, let
n\geq 2
and let

W
be a connected component of
\bigcap_{k=0}^{n-1}f^{-k}(K_{m,m+1})
. There exists

\omega\in E^{n}_{m}
such that

W=I_{\omega}
.
Since
K_{m,m+1}
does not contain the critical point of
f
, the infimum of
|Df|
over this set is positive.
For all
x,y\in I_{\omega}

we have

\begin{split}\log\frac{|Df^{n}(x)|}{|Df^{n}(y)|}&\leq\left(\sup_{K_{m,m+1}}\frac{|D^{2}f|}{|Df|}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|f^{k}(I_{\omega})|\leq\left(\sup_{K_{m,m+1}}\frac{|D^{2}f|}{|Df|}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sup_{\omega\in E^{n-k}_{m}}|I_{\omega}|.\end{split}

By Lemma
2.6
, the last number is bounded by
\varepsilon n
for a sufficiently large
n
.
The proof of Proposition
2.5
is complete.
∎

2.6.
Topological results

Let
Y
be a non-degenerate interval in
X
and
g\colon Y\to Y
be a continuous map.
Let
U
be a non-degenerate subinterval of
Y
and
n\geq 1
an integer. Any connected component of
g^{-n}(U)

is called
a pullback of
U
by
g^{n}
.
If
V
is a pullback of
U
by
g^{n}
and

g^{n}|_{V}
is a diffeomorphism, then
V
is called
a diffeomorphic pullback of
U
by
g^{n}
.

Results in this subsection will be used for the proof of the lower bound in Theorem A, in order to glue different orbits together to form one orbit with required properties.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
f\colon X\to X
be an
S
-unimodal map with a non-flat critical point such that
m(f)\geq 1
, and
let
0\leq m<m(f)
be such that
p_{m+1}/p_{m}\neq 2
. For any open interval

J\subset L_{m}
that intersects
\Gamma_{m}
, there exist
n\geq 1

and a pullback
W
of
L_{m}
by
f^{p_{m}n}

that is contained in
J
.

Proof.

Since
m<m(f)
,

f^{p_{m}}(c)\neq f^{2p_{m}}(c)
and
L_{m}
is a non-degenerate interval.
Since
J
intersects
\Gamma_{m}
, it is not contained in
K_{m+1}
. Since
\Gamma_{m}\cap B(f)=\emptyset
and
f(\Gamma_{m})=\Gamma_{m}
,
J
is not a homterval of
f^{p_{m}}
. Hence
U=\bigcup\{f^{p_{m}n}(J)\colon n\geq 0,c\in f^{p_{m}n}(J)\}
intersects
\Gamma_{m}
.
If
n\geq 0
and
c\in f^{p_{m}n}(J)
, then

f^{p_{m}}(c)
is contained in the boundary of
f^{p_{m}(n+1)}(J)
. Hence,
there exists an increasing sequence
\{n_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
of non-negative integers such that
c\in f^{p_{m}n_{i}}(J)
for all
i
, and
\{f^{p_{m}(n_{i}+1)}(J)\}_{i\geq 1}
is increasing and

f^{p_{m}}(U)=\bigcup_{i\geq 1}f^{p_{m}(n_{i}+1)}(J).

We claim that
c\in{\rm int}(f^{p_{m}k}(U))
for some
k\geq 1
implies
f^{p_{m}k}(U)\subset U
.
Indeed,
for each
x\in f^{p_{m}k}(U)
there exists
n\geq 0
such that
c\in f^{p_{m}n}(J)
and
c,x\in f^{p_{m}(n+k)}(J)
. Hence
x\in U
, and
the claim holds.

Set
r=\inf\{k\geq 1\colon c\in{\rm int}(f^{p_{m}k}(U))\}
. Since
J
is not a homterval of
f^{p_{m}}
,

f^{p_{m}}(U)
is not a homterval of
f^{p_{m}}
either, and so
r
is finite.
By the above claim,
if
r\geq 2
then
f^{p_{m}}
is renormalizable with period
r
and we have

U\subset K_{m+1}
, which is a contradiction. Hence
r=1
, namely
c\in{\rm int}(f^{p_{m}}(U))
. This implies that the closed interval bordered by

c
and
f^{p_{m}}(c)
is contained in
f^{p_{m}n}(J)
for some
n\geq 0
, and hence
L_{m}\subset f^{p_{m}(n+1)}(J)
.
This implies the existence of a pullback with the desired properties.
∎

.

Let
f\colon X\to X

.
∎

Notation

Let
C(X)
denote the set of real-valued continuous functions on
X
.
For
\phi\in C(X)
and an integer
n\geq 1
, we write
S_{n}\phi
for the sum

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\phi\circ f^{k}
.
For an integer
l\geq 1
define

C(X)^{l}=\{\vec{\phi}=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{l})\colon\phi_{j}\in C(X)\text{ for every }j\in\{1,\ldots,l\}\}.

holds for every

.
For an integer

, we set

(2.5)

A_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha})=\left\{x\in X\colon\int\vec{\phi}d\delta_{x}^{n}>\vec{\alpha}\right\},

and

For

and

, we write

\vec{a}=(a,a,\ldots,a)\in\mathbb{R}^{l}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\vec{v}\|=\max_{1\leq j\leq l}|v_{j}|.

For
\vec{v},\vec{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
, the expression

.

2.8.
Infinitely renormalizable maps

Let
f\colon X\to X
be an infinitely renormalizable
S
-unimodal map with a non-flat critical point.
The omega limit set of Lebesgue almost every initial point
x\in X
coincides with the closed invariant set

\Lambda=\bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}K_{m}

and the empirical measure
\delta_{x}^{n}
converges to
the post-critical measure

\mu_{\infty}
supported on
\Lambda
. The restriction of
f
to
\Lambda
is uniquely ergodic and the post-critical measure
\mu_{\infty}
is the unique invariant measure on
\Lambda
. Moreover,
h(\mu_{\infty})=\chi(\mu_{\infty})=0
holds (see

[
4
, Theorem 3.4(b)]
).

In order to ‘approximate’
\mu_{\infty}
, we will use the following lemma.

Lemma 2.10
.

(
[
35
, Lemma 2.3]
)

Let
f\colon X\to X
be an
S
-unimodal map with a non-flat critical point such that
m(f)=\infty
.
Let

l\geq 1
,
\vec{\phi}\in C(X)^{l}
,
\vec{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
there exist integers

and

such that the following hold:

and

(b)

if

\sup_{K_{m_{*}}}\left\|\frac{1}{n}S_{n}\vec{\phi}-\int\vec{\phi}d\mu_{\infty}\right\|<\varepsilon.

For infinitely renormalizable maps,

.

(

3.
Large deviations lower bound

In this section we complete the proof of the lower bound in Theorem A.
In Section
3.1
we show that this follows from a key lower bound stated in Proposition
3.1
.
The rest of this section is dedicated to the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

3.1.
Key lower bound

The next proposition allows us to approximate a given invariant measure with finitely many intervals
in a particular sense.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to X
be an
S
-unimodal map with a non-flat critical point.
Let
l\geq 1
,
\vec{\phi}\in C(X)^{l}
,
\vec{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
and
let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f)
satisfy
\int\vec{\phi}d\mu>\vec{\alpha}
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N\geq 1
such that
for each integer
n\geq N
, there exists
a finite set
\mathscr{A}_{n}
of closed intervals in

A_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha})
with pairwise disjoint interiors such that

(a)

\#\mathscr{A}_{n}>\exp\left((h(\mu)-\varepsilon)n\right),\quad and

(b)

|A|>\exp\left(-(\chi(\mu)+\varepsilon)n\right)
for all
A\in\mathscr{A}_{n}
.

In order to prove Proposition
3.1
, we approximate the
given measure
\mu

with a finite convex combination of measures each supported on some
\Gamma_{m}
or
\Lambda
, and then for each of these measures construct a collection of finitely many intervals. We then glue orbits from these intervals together to construct a collection intervals with the desired properties.

The proof of Proposition
3.1
is given in Section
3.3
.
Below we deduce a corollary to Proposition
3.1
and complete the proof of
the lower bound in
Theorem A.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
f\colon X\to X
be an
S
-unimodal map with a non-flat critical point.
Let
l\geq 1
,
\vec{\phi}\in C(X)^{l}
,
\vec{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
.
If
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f)
satisfies
\int\vec{\phi}d\mu>\vec{\alpha}
, then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|A_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha})|\geq F(\mu).

Proof.

From Proposition
3.1
, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N\geq 1
such that

|A_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha})|>e^{(F(\mu)-2\varepsilon)n}
holds for all
n\geq N
.
Taking logarithms of both sides, dividing by
n
and letting
n\to\infty
and then

\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

Proof of the lower bound in Theorem A.

Subsets of
\mathcal{M}
of the form

\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}\colon\int\vec{\phi}d\mu>\vec{\alpha}\}

with
l\geq 1
,
\vec{\phi}\in C(X)^{l}
,
\vec{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}

constitute a base of the weak* topology of
\mathcal{M}
.
Therefore any non-empty open subset

\mathcal{G}
of
\mathcal{M}
is written as the union

\mathcal{G}=\bigcup_{\lambda}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
of sets
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
of this form.
For each
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
, Corollary
3.2
gives

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\{x\in X\colon\delta_{x}^{n}\in\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}\}|\geq\sup_{\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}F.

Hence we obtain

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\{x\in X\colon\delta_{x}^{n}\in\mathcal{G}\}|\geq\sup_{\lambda}\sup_{\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}F=\sup_{\mathcal{G}}F=-\inf_{\mathcal{G}}I,

as required.
∎

3.2.

.
Therefore,
l\geq 1
,

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{B\in\tilde{\mathscr{B}}_{p_{m}r}(P)}\sup_{x,y\in B}\frac{1}{p_{m}r}\left|S_{p_{m}r}\varphi(x)-S_{p_{m}r}\varphi(y)\right|=0.

We start the proof of Lemma

with the case where

4.1.
Key upper bound

Let

Define

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

-unimodal map with a non-flat critical point.

|\bar{A}_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha})|\leq\exp\left((F(\mu)+\varepsilon)n\right)\quad and

(b)

We finish the proof of the upper bound in Theorem A assuming Proposition
4.1
.

Proof of the upper bound in Theorem A.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-empty closed
subset of
\mathcal{M}
.
Let
\mathcal{G}
be an arbitrary open set containing
\mathcal{C}
.
Since
\mathcal{M}
is metrizable and
\mathcal{C}
is compact, we can choose
\varepsilon>0
and finitely many closed sets
\mathcal{C}_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq s
of the form

\mathcal{C}_{i}=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}\colon\int\vec{\phi}_{i}d\mu\geq\vec{\alpha}_{i}\}

with

,
\vec{\phi}_{i}\in C(X)^{l_{i}}
,
\vec{\alpha}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{l_{i}}

so that

(4.1)

\mathcal{C}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{C}_{i}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{C}_{i}(\varepsilon)\subset\mathcal{G},

where

\mathcal{C}_{i}(\varepsilon)=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}\colon\int\vec{\phi}_{i}d\mu>\vec{\alpha}_{i}-\vec{\varepsilon}\}
.
Since
F(\mu)\leq-I(\mu)
for

,

implies

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\{x\in X\colon\delta_{x}^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}\}|\leq-\inf_{\mathcal{C}_{i}(\varepsilon)}I+\varepsilon

for each

.
These and the relation (

) give

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\{x\in X\colon\delta_{x}^{n}\in\mathcal{C}\}|\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq s}\left(-\inf_{\mathcal{C}_{i}(\varepsilon)}I\right)+\varepsilon\leq-\inf_{\mathcal{G}}I+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary and

is an arbitrary open set containing

, it follows that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\{x\in X\colon\delta_{x}^{n}\in\mathcal{C}\}|\leq\inf_{\mathcal{G}\supset\mathcal{C}}(-\inf_{\mathcal{G}}I)=-\inf_{\mathcal{C}}I,

as required. The last equality is due to the lower semicontinuity of

.
∎

4.2.
Escape estimate in a single renormalization cycle

Assume

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume
m(f)\geq 1
,
and let
m\in\{0,\ldots,m(f)-1\}
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N\geq 1
such that
if
\vec{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
,
P\in\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{m}
,

satisfy

,
then there exists a measure

\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f)
such that

(a)

|A_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\beta},P)|\leq\exp\left((F(\mu)+\varepsilon)n\right)|P|\quad and

(b)

\int\vec{\phi}d\mu>\vec{\beta}-\vec{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
P\in\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{m}
.
If
p_{m+1}/p_{m}=2
, we can verify the claim easily,
taking the measure
\mu
to be the equidistribution on the orbit of the periodic point in

.
Suppose

.
We split the rest of the proof of Lemma

into two cases,

or

.

Case 1:

.

We perform an escape estimate relative to

.
Let

be such that
A_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\beta},P)\neq\emptyset
.
Let
\mathscr{P}_{m,n}(P)
denote the collection of diffeomorphic pullbacks of elements of
\mathscr{P}_{m}
by

which are contained in

and intersect
\bar{A}_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\beta})
.
The union of elements of

\mathscr{P}_{m,n}(P)
contain
A_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\beta},P)
.
Using
Corollary
2.8
,
we fix an integer multiple

of
p_{m}
such that for any
Q\in\mathscr{P}_{m}
there is a diffeomorphic pullback of
P
by
f^{M_{m}}
that is contained in
Q
. For each
Q\in\mathscr{P}_{m}
,
we pick such a pullback and denote it by
W_{Q}
.

Sublemma 4.3
.

For any
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0

there exists
N^{\prime}\geq 1
such that for any

Q\in\mathscr{P}_{m}
,

n\geq N^{\prime}
and any diffeomorphic pullback

W
of
Q
by
f^{n}

contained in
P
, there exists a diffeomorphic pullback
Y
of
P
by
f^{n+M_{m}}
that is contained in
W
and satisfies

|Y|\geq e^{-\varepsilon^{\prime}n}|W|.

Proof.

Define
Y
to be the diffeomorphic pullback of
W_{Q}
by
f^{n}
that is contained in
W
.
Then
Y
is a diffeomorphic pullback of
P
by
f^{n+M_{m}}
. By
Proposition
2.5
with
\varepsilon
replaced by
\varepsilon^{\prime}/2
, if

is sufficiently large we have

\frac{|Y|}{|W|}\geq e^{-\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{2}n}\frac{|f^{n}(Y)|}{|f^{n}(W)|}\geq e^{-\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{2}n}\min_{Q\in\mathscr{P}_{m}}\frac{|W_{Q}|}{|Q|}\geq e^{-\varepsilon^{\prime}n},

as required.
∎

In view of
Sublemma
4.3
,
for each
W\in\mathscr{P}_{m,n}(P)

we fix a diffeomorphic pullback
Y_{W}
of
P
by
f^{n+M_{m}}
that is contained in

and satisfies

|Y_{W}|\geq e^{-\varepsilon n/3}|W|.

Set

\mathscr{P}_{m,n}^{\prime}(P)=\{Y_{W}\colon W\in\mathscr{P}_{m,n}(P)\}.

We have

(4.2)

Y\subset A_{n+M_{m}}\left(\vec{\phi},\vec{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\vec{\varepsilon}\right)\quad\text{for }Y\in\mathscr{P}_{m,n}^{\prime}(P),\text{ and }

(4.3)

|A_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha},P)|\leq\sum_{W\in\mathscr{P}_{m,n}(P)}|W|\leq e^{\frac{\varepsilon}{3}n}\sum_{Y\in\mathscr{P}_{m,n}^{\prime}(P)}|Y|.

Set
\tilde{n}=n+M_{m}
.
The restriction of
f^{\tilde{n}}

to
\bigcup_{Y\in\mathscr{P}_{m,n}^{\prime}(P)}Y

induces a fully branched expanding Markov map onto
P

with finitely many branches.
Let
\Delta\subset\Gamma_{m}
denote its maximal invariant set, namely

\Delta=\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}(f^{\tilde{n}})^{-k}\left(\bigcup_{Y\in\mathscr{P}_{m,n}^{\prime}(P)}Y\right).

The map

is topologically conjugate to the full shift
\sigma\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma
over the finite alphabet
\mathscr{P}_{m,n}^{\prime}(P)
. We write
\pi\colon\Sigma\to\Delta
for the conjugacy map, and define
the induced potential
\Phi\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
by
\Phi(\omega)=-\log|Df^{\tilde{n}}(\pi(\omega))|
.
From Lemma
2.6
,

\Phi
is continuous with respect to the shift metric.
The variational principle
[
2
, p.40, 2.17]
gives

(4.4)

\sup_{\tilde{\nu}\in\mathcal{M}(\sigma)}\left(h(\sigma,\tilde{\nu})+\int\Phi d\tilde{\nu}\right)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\log\left(\sum_{\omega\in\sigma^{-k}(\omega^{\prime})}\exp{\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\Phi(\sigma^{i}\omega)}\right)

for any fixed
\omega^{\prime}\in\Sigma
, where
\mathcal{M}(\sigma)
denotes the space of
\sigma
-invariant Borel probability measures endowed with the weak* topology, and
h(\sigma,\tilde{\nu})
denotes the entropy of
\tilde{\nu}
with respect to
\sigma
.
By the distortion estimates in Proposition
2.5
, if
n\geq 1
is sufficiently large then
for all
Y\in\mathscr{P}_{m,n}^{\prime}(P)
and
\omega\in\Sigma

such that

we have

\exp\left(\Phi(\omega)\right)\geq e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}n}\frac{|Y|}{|P|}.

Hence, the series inside the logarithm in (

) is bounded from below as follows:

\sum_{\omega\in\sigma^{-k}(\omega^{\prime})}\exp\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\Phi(\sigma^{i}\omega)\geq\left(\inf_{\omega^{\prime}\in\Sigma}\sum_{\omega\in\sigma^{-1}(\omega^{\prime})}\exp\left(\Phi(\omega)\right)\right)^{k}\geq\left(e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}n}\sum_{Y\in\mathscr{P}_{m,n}^{\prime}(P)}\frac{|Y|}{|P|}\right)^{k}.

Taking logarithms of both sides, dividing by
k
and letting
k\to\infty
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\log\left(\sum_{\omega\in\sigma^{-k}(\omega^{\prime})}\exp\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\Phi(\sigma^{i}\omega)\right)\geq\log\sum_{Y\in\mathscr{P}_{m,n}^{\prime}(P)}\frac{|Y|}{|P|}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}n.

Plugging this inequality into (
4.4
) yields

(4.5)

\sup_{\tilde{\nu}\in\mathcal{M}(\sigma)}\left(h(\sigma,\tilde{\nu})+\int\Phi d\tilde{\nu}\right)\geq\log\sum_{Y\in\mathscr{P}_{m,n}^{\prime}(P)}\frac{|Y|}{|P|}-\frac{\varepsilon n}{2}.

Since
\mathcal{M}(\sigma)
is compact in the weak* topology and the entropy is upper semicontinuous on it,
there exists a measure
\tilde{\mu}\in\mathcal{M}(\sigma)

which attains the supremum in (
4.5
).
The measure
\tilde{\mu}\circ\pi^{-1}
on
\Delta
is
f^{n}
-invariant and its spread

\mu=\frac{1}{\tilde{n}}\sum_{Y\in\mathscr{P}_{m,n}^{\prime}(P)}\sum_{k=0}^{\tilde{n}-1}(\tilde{\mu}|_{Y})\circ(f^{k}\circ\pi)^{-1}

belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{m}(f)
.
Although
f^{\tilde{n}}
may not be the first return map to
\Delta
,
Abramov’s formula, connecting the entropies of
\mu
and
\tilde{\mu}\circ\pi^{-1}
, and Kac’s formula, connecting the integrals of

and
\Phi
, still hold
[
26
, Theorem 2.3]
and
we have

(4.6)

h(\sigma,\tilde{\mu})+\int\Phi d\tilde{\mu}=F(\mu)\tilde{n}.

From (
4.5
) and (

) we have

From this inequality and (
4.3
), for all sufficiently large

as required in Lemma
4.2
(a).
Item (b) follows from (
4.2
).

) we have

(4.15)

|A_{n}(t,\mathbf{B})\cap V_{t}|\leq\frac{C^{2}|X|}{|f^{t}(V_{t})|}\exp\left(\left(F(\nu_{t,\mathbf{B}})+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\right)n\right)|P|.

Let
\mu
be a measure in
\{\nu_{t,\mathbf{B}}\colon\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{M}_{2,l}(t,\varepsilon\mathbb{Z})\}\cup\{\delta_{z}^{p_{m}}\}
which maximizes the free energy within this finite set. Clearly we have

(4.16)

\#\mathbb{M}_{2,l}(t,\varepsilon\mathbb{Z})\leq\prod_{j=1}^{l}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\sup\phi_{j}-\inf\phi_{j}\right)+2\right)^{2}.

If
n
is sufficiently large, then (
4.15
)
and (
4.16
) together imply

(4.17)

|A_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\beta},V_{t})|\leq\sum_{\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{M}_{2,l}(t,\varepsilon\mathbb{Z})}|A_{n}(t,\mathbf{B})\cap V_{t}|\leq\exp\left(\left(F(\mu)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)n\right)|P|.

To finish, from (
4.8
) and (
4.17
) we have

\begin{split}I&=\sum_{t=1}^{n-N}|A_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\beta},V_{t})|+\sum_{t=n-N+1}^{n-1}|A_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\beta},V_{t})|\\
&\leq\frac{n}{p_{m}}\exp\left(\left(F(\mu)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)n\right)|P|+\frac{n}{p_{m}}C\exp\left(F(\mu)(n-N)\right)|P|.\end{split}

Combining this estimate with that of
I\!I
, for all sufficiently large
n
we obtain

\begin{split}|A_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\beta},P)|=I+I\!I\leq\exp\left((F(\mu)+\varepsilon)n\right)|P|,\end{split}

as required in Lemma
4.2
(a).
Item (b) is a consequence of (
4.14
).
∎

4.3.
Coarse graining decomposition into clusters

In this subsection,
we decompose the set
\bar{A}_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha})
into a finite number of clusters consisting of points which share the same ‘itinerary’ up to time
n
, and share almost the same average values of
\vec{\phi}
along the segments of orbits each contained in a single cycle.

Let
\varepsilon>0
.
If
m(f)=\infty
, then let
m_{*}\geq 1
and
N_{*}\geq 1
be integers for which the conclusion of Lemma
2.10
holds
with
\varepsilon
replaced by
\varepsilon/2
.
We set

(4.18)

M=\begin{cases}m_{*}&\text{ if $m(f)=\infty$}\\
m(f)&\text{ if $1\leq m(f)<\infty$.}\end{cases}

For each
x\in X
, we define by induction two (possibly infinite) increasing sequences
\{\hat{n}_{i}(x)\}_{i=0}^{d_{*}(x)}
,

\{\hat{m}_{i}(x)\}_{i=0}^{d_{*}(x)}
of non-negative integers
that successively record the time and position at which the orbit of
x
falls into deeper renormalization cycles.
Start with

\hat{n}_{0}(x)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{m}_{0}(x)=\max\{m\in\{0,\ldots,M\}\colon x\in K_{m}\}.

Let
i\geq 0
and
suppose
\hat{n}_{i}(x)
and
\hat{m}_{i}(x)
are defined. If
\hat{m}_{i}(x)=M
, or if
\hat{m}_{i}(x)\leq M-1
and

f^{k}(x)\in K_{\hat{m}_{i}(x),\hat{m}_{i}(x)+1}

for all
k>\hat{n}_{i}(x)
, then we stop the inductive definition by setting
d_{*}(x)=i
. Otherwise, we define

\hat{n}_{i+1}(x)=\min\{k\geq\hat{n}_{i}(x)+1\colon f^{k}(x)\notin K_{\hat{m}_{i}(x),\hat{m}_{i}(x)+1}\}\quad\text{and}

\hat{m}_{i+1}(x)=\max\{m\in\{\hat{m}_{i}(x)+1,\ldots,M\}\colon f^{\hat{n}_{i+1}(x)}(x)\in K_{m}\}.

For two integers
n
,
d

with
0\leq d\leq\min\{n-1,M\}
we define

I_{n}(d)=\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{l}$\mathbf{t}=((n_{0},m_{0}),(n_{1},m_{1}),\ldots,(n_{d},m_{d}))\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{d+1}\colon$\\
$0=n_{0}<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{d}<n$,\\
$0\leq m_{0}<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{d}\leq M$\end{tabular}\right\}.

Let
\mathbb{t}=((n_{0},m_{0}),(n_{1},m_{1}),\ldots,(n_{d},m_{d}))\in I_{n}(d)
. For convenience, we set

n_{d+1}=n\ \text{ and }\ m_{d+1}=m_{d},

and call

n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{d+1}

transition times
.
For each
i\in\{0,\ldots,d\}
we set

t_{i}=n_{i+1}-n_{i}.

Let
R(\mathbf{t})
denote the set of
x\in X
such that
d_{*}(x)\geq d
,
(\hat{n}_{i}(x),\hat{m}_{i}(x))=(n_{i},m_{i})
for every
i\in\{0,\ldots,d\}
,
and
\hat{n}_{d+1}(x)\geq n
if
d_{*}(x)\geq d+1
. We have

X\subset\bigcup_{d=0}^{M}\bigcup_{\mathbf{t}\in I_{n}(d)}R(\mathbf{t}).

We decompose the set
\bar{A}_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha})\cap R(\mathbf{t})
with respect to coarse grained values at scale
\varepsilon
of time averages of
\vec{\phi}
between two consecutive transition times.
For each
\mathbf{t}\in I_{n}(d)
,
let

\mathbb{M}_{d+1,l}(\mathbb{t},\varepsilon\mathbb{Z})

denote the set of
(d+1)\times l
matrices
\mathbf{A}=(\alpha_{ij})_{i\in\{0,\ldots,d\},j\in\{1,\ldots,l\}}

with entries in
\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}
such that
the following hold for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
:

(4.19)

\sum_{i=0}^{d}\alpha_{ij}t_{i}>\left(\alpha_{j}-\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\right)n,

(4.20)

\inf\phi_{j}-\varepsilon<\alpha_{ij}\leq\sup\phi_{j}\quad\text{for every }i\in\{0,\ldots,d\}.

For a matrix
\mathbf{A}=(\alpha_{ij})\in\mathbb{M}_{d+1,l}(\mathbb{t},\varepsilon\mathbb{Z})
,
let
\vec{\alpha}_{i}=(\alpha_{i1},\ldots,\alpha_{il})
denote the
i
-th row of
\mathbf{A}
.
Define

R(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{A})=\{x\in R(\mathbf{t})\colon S_{t_{i}}\vec{\phi}(f^{n_{i}}(x))\geq t_{i}\vec{\alpha}_{i}\quad\text{for every }i\in\{0,\ldots,d\}\}.

Similarly to the argument in the proof of
Lemma
4.2
in Case 2, we obtain

\bar{A}_{n}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha})\cap R(\mathbf{t})\subset\bigcup_{\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{M}_{d+1,l}(\mathbb{t},\varepsilon\mathbb{Z})}R(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{A}).

4.4.
Lebesgue measures of clusters

We are in position to state and prove a main technical estimate on the Lebesgue measure of each cluster
R(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{A})
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume
m(f)\geq 1
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N\geq 1
such that if
n\geq N
,

\mathbf{t}\in\bigcup_{d=0}^{M}I_{n}(d)
,

\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{M}_{d+1,l}(\mathbb{t},\varepsilon\mathbb{Z})
and

R(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{A})\neq\emptyset
, then
there exists a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f)
satisfying

(a)

\left|R(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{A})\right|\leq\exp\left(\left(F(\mu)+\varepsilon\right)n\right)\quad\text{and}

(b)

\int\vec{\phi}d\mu>\vec{\alpha}-\vec{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
, and
let
\delta>0
be such that

(4.21)

\|\vec{\phi}(x)-\vec{\phi}(y)\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\ \text{
for $x,y\in X$ with $|x-y|<\delta$.}

In view of Lemma
2.6
,
let
N_{0}\geq 1
be an integer such that for any
m\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}
and
any connected component
W
of
\bigcap_{k=0}^{N_{0}-1}f^{-k}(K_{m,m+1})
, we have
|W|<\delta
.
The rest of the proof of Proposition
4.4
consists of three steps.
In the course of the proof we will require
N_{0}
to be sufficiently large.

Step 1 (Estimate of error bound in transition).

Let
n\geq 1
,
d\in\{0,\ldots,M\}
,
\mathbf{t}\in I_{n}(d)
,

\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{M}_{d+1,l}(\mathbb{t},\varepsilon\mathbb{Z})
satisfy

R(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{A})\neq\emptyset
.
We set

R_{0}(\mathbf{A})=K_{m_{0},m_{0}+1}
,
and let
\mathscr{R}_{0}(\mathbf{A})
denote the collection of connected components of
R_{0}(\mathbf{A})
.
For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,d+1\}
,
let
\mathscr{R}_{i}(\mathbf{A})

denote the collection of
diffeomorphic pullbacks of the connected components of
K_{m_{i}}

by
f^{n_{i}}
which are contained in
R_{0}(\mathbf{A})

and intersect
R(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{A})
.
Let
R_{i}(\mathbf{A})
denote the
union of elements of

\mathscr{R}_{i}(\mathbf{A})
. Note that

(4.22)

R(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{A})\subset R_{d+1}(\mathbf{A})\subset\cdots\subset R_{1}(\mathbf{A})\subset R_{0}(\mathbf{A}).

Among all transition times,
for our purpose it suffices to concentrate on
consecutive ones which are well-separated.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any
i\in\{0,\ldots,d\}
with
t_{i}>N_{0}
we have

f^{n_{i}}(R_{i+1}(\mathbf{A}))\subset\bar{A}_{t_{i}}\left(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\vec{\varepsilon}\right).

Proof.

Let
R\in\mathscr{R}_{i+1}(\mathbf{A})
. Fix
x_{0}\in R\cap R(\mathbb{t},\mathbb{A})
.
The definition of
\mathscr{R}_{i+1}(\mathbf{A})
gives

S_{t_{i}}\vec{\phi}(f^{n_{i}}(x_{0}))\geq t_{i}\vec{\alpha}_{i}.

For each
k\in\{0,\ldots,t_{i}-N_{0}+1\}
we have
t_{i}-k\geq N_{0}-1
, and so

f^{n_{i}}(R)\subset\bigcap_{j=0}^{N_{0}-1}f^{-j}(K_{m_{i},m_{i+1}})
.
The choice of
N_{0}
gives
|f^{n_{i}+k}(R)|<\delta
.
By (
4.21
),
for any
x\in R
we have

\|S_{t_{i}-N_{0}}\vec{\phi}(f^{n_{i}}(x))-S_{t_{i}-N_{0}}\vec{\phi}(f^{n_{i}}(x_{0}))\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}(t_{i}-N_{0}).

For the remaining
N_{0}
iterations, clearly we have

\|S_{N_{0}}\vec{\phi}(f^{n_{i+1}-N_{0}}(x))-S_{N_{0}}\vec{\phi}(f^{n_{i+1}-N_{0}}(x_{0}))\|\leq 2N_{0}\sup\|\vec{\phi}\|.

Since
t_{i}>N_{0}
,
combining these three displayed estimates we obtain

\frac{1}{t_{i}}S_{t_{i}}\vec{\phi}(f^{n_{i}}(x))\geq\vec{\alpha}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\vec{\varepsilon},

provided
N_{0}
is sufficiently large.
Since
x\in R
is arbitrary and
R\in\mathscr{R}_{i+1}(\mathbf{A})
is arbitrary, the desired inclusion follows.
∎

Step 2 (Estimate of
i
-step conditional probability).

The next lemma provides estimates of conditional probabilities at well-separated transition times.

Lemma 4.6
.

For any
i\in\{0,\ldots,d\}
with
t_{i}>N_{0}
, there exists a measure
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{M}(f)
such that

(a)

\frac{|R_{i+1}(\mathbf{A})|}{|R_{i}(\mathbf{A})|}\leq\exp\left(\left(F(\mu_{i})+\varepsilon\right)t_{i}\right)\quad\text{and}

(b)

\int\vec{\phi}d\mu_{i}>\vec{\alpha}_{i}-\vec{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

We first treat the case
m_{i}\leq M-1
.
Let
R\in\mathscr{R}_{i}(\mathbf{A})
.
Let
\mathscr{P}_{m_{i}}(\mathbf{A},R)
denote the collection of
diffeomorphic pullbacks of elements of
K_{m_{i}}
by
f^{t_{i}}

that intersect
f^{n_{i}}(R\cap R_{i+1}(\mathbf{A}))
.
By the assumption
t_{i}>N_{0}
and Lemma
4.5
, each
element
P\in\mathscr{P}_{m_{i}}(\mathbf{A},R)
intersect

\bar{A}_{t_{i}}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha}_{i}-(1/2)\vec{\varepsilon})
, and
by Lemma
4.2

there exists
\nu_{R,P}\in\mathcal{M}(f)
such that

(4.23)

\left|A_{t_{i}}\left(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\vec{\varepsilon},P\right)\right|\leq\exp\left(\left(F(\nu_{R,P})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)t_{i}\right)|P|\quad\text{and}

(4.24)

\int\vec{\phi}d\nu_{R,P}>\vec{\alpha}_{i}-\vec{\varepsilon}.

Let
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{M}(f)
be a measure
in
\{\nu_{R,P}\colon R\in\mathscr{R}_{i}(\mathbf{A}),\ P\in\mathscr{P}_{m_{i}}(\mathbf{A},R)\}
which maximizes the free energy within this finite set.
By (
4.23
) we have

(4.25)

\begin{split}|f^{n_{i}}(R\cap R_{i+1}(\mathbf{A}))|&\leq\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{m_{i}}(\mathbf{A},R)}\left|A_{t_{i}}\left(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha}_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\vec{\varepsilon},P\right)\right|\\
&\leq\sum_{P\in\mathscr{P}_{m_{i}}(\mathbf{A},R)}\exp\left(\left(F(\nu_{R,P})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)t_{i}\right)|P|\\
&\leq\exp\left(\left(F(\mu_{i})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)t_{i}\right)|f^{n_{i}}(R)|.\end{split}

If
i\geq 1
, then for
0\leq j<i
,

f^{n}(R)
is contained in
K_{n_{j},n_{j}+1}

for
n_{j}\leq n\leq n_{j+1}-n_{j}-1
.
By Proposition
2.5
(a), the distortion of the composition
f^{n_{i}}=f^{t_{i-1}}\circ\cdots\circ f^{t_{1}}\circ f^{t_{0}}
on
R
is bounded by

(4.26)

D_{i}=\gamma_{m_{i-1}}\cdots\gamma_{m_{1}}\gamma_{m_{0}}.

From (
4.25
) we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{|R\cap R_{i+1}(\mathbf{A})|}{|R|}\leq D_{i}\frac{|f^{n_{i}}(R\cap R_{i+1}(\mathbf{A}))|}{|f^{n_{i}}(R)|}&\leq\exp\left(\left(F(\mu_{i})+\varepsilon\right)t_{i}\right),\end{split}

provided
N_{0}
is sufficiently large. Since
n_{0}=0
, the same upper bound remains valid for
i=0
. Consequently
we obtain

\frac{|R_{i+1}(\mathbf{A})|}{|R_{i}(\mathbf{A})|}\leq\max_{R\in\mathscr{R}_{i}(\mathbf{A})}\frac{|R\cap R_{i+1}(\mathbf{A})|}{|R|}\leq\exp\left((F(\mu_{i})+\varepsilon)t_{i}\right),

as required in Lemma
4.6
(a).
Lemma
4.6
(b) is a consequence of
(
4.24
).

To complete the proof of Lemma
4.6
, it is left to treat the case

m_{i}=M
. In this case we have
i=d
, and there are four subcases.

Case 1:
m(f)=\infty
.

We have
M=m_{*}
by (
4.18
).
Set
\mu_{d}=\mu_{\infty}
.
Clearly Lemma
4.6
(a) holds
since
F(\mu_{d})=0
.
Since
t_{d}>N_{0}
and

f^{n_{d}}(R_{d+1}(\mathbf{A}))\subset\bar{A}_{t_{d}}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha}_{d}-(1/2)\vec{\varepsilon})\cap K_{M}

by Lemma
4.5
,
Lemma
4.6
(b) follows from
Lemma
2.10
if
N_{0}
is sufficiently large.

Case 2:
m(f)=M
and

{\rm int}(J_{m(f)})
contains an attracting periodic point (Case C-I in Remark
2.2
).
Note that

K_{M}={\rm cl}(B(f))

by (
2.3
) and Lemma
2.1
.
Let
z
denote the point from
O(f)
that is contained in
{\rm int}(J_{M})
.
Fix a closed interval
J\subset J_{M}
such that

c,z\in{\rm int}(J)
,
f^{p_{M}}(J)\subset J
and
|K_{M}\setminus K_{M+1}|<\delta
where
K_{M+1}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{p_{M}-1}f^{k}(J).

Let
\nu
denote the element of
\mathcal{M}_{M-1}(f)
supported on the orbit of the hyperbolic repelling periodic point in
\partial J_{M}
.

The rest of the argument is similar in spirit to that in Case 2 in the proof of Lemma
4.2
. For each
R\in\mathscr{R}_{d}(\mathbf{A})
and
t\in\{0,\ldots,t_{d}\}
, define

V_{R,t}=\begin{cases}f^{n_{d}}(R)\cap K_{M+1}&\text{for }t=0,\\
f^{n_{d}}(R)\cap\left(f^{-t}(K_{M+1})\setminus\bigcup_{j=0}^{t-1}f^{-j}(K_{M+1})\right)&\text{for }t\in\{1,\ldots,t_{d}-1\},\\
f^{n_{d}}(R)\setminus\bigcup_{j=0}^{t_{d}-1}f^{-j}(K_{M+1})&\text{for }t=t_{d}.\end{cases}

We have
f^{n_{d}}(R)=\bigcup_{t=0}^{t_{d}}V_{R,t}
.
If
V_{R,t}\neq\emptyset
, then
V_{R,t}
is the union of at most two intervals.
Let
W_{R,t}
denote the pullback of
V_{R,t}
by
f^{n_{d}}
that is contained in
R
. Define
\nu_{R,t}\in\mathcal{M}(f)
by

\nu_{R,t}=\frac{t}{t_{d}}\nu+\left(1-\frac{t}{t_{d}}\right)\delta_{O(f)}.

There exists a constant
C_{0}\geq 1
depending only
f
,
\delta
such that
if
x\in X
and
k\geq 1
satisfy
x,\ldots,f^{k-1}(x)\in K_{M}\setminus K_{M+1}
then
|(f^{k})^{\prime}x|\geq C_{0}^{-1}e^{\chi(\nu)k}
. Hence

(4.27)

\frac{|V_{R,t}|}{|f^{n_{d}}(R)|}\leq C_{0}\exp\left(-\chi(\nu)t\right)=C_{0}\exp\left(F(\nu)t\right).

Since
F(\delta_{O(f)})=0
we have
F(\nu)\leq F(\nu_{R,t})
.
As in the proof for the first case,
by Proposition
2.5
the distortion of
f^{n_{d}}
on
R
is bounded by the constant
D_{d}
in (
4.26
) so that

(4.28)

\frac{|W_{R,t}|}{|R|}\leq C_{0}D_{d}\exp\left(F(\nu_{R,t}\right)t_{d}).

For each
R\in\mathscr{R}_{d}(\mathbf{A})

put

T_{R}=\{t\in\{0,\ldots,t_{d}\}\colon W_{R,t}\cap\mathscr{R}_{d+1}(\mathbf{A})\neq\emptyset\}
.
Let
\mu_{d}
be a measure in
\{\nu_{R,t}\colon R\in\mathscr{R}_{d}(\mathbf{A}),t\in T_{R}\}
which maximizes the free energy within this finite set. Since
t_{d}>N_{0}
, using (
4.28
) we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{|R_{d+1}(\mathbf{A})|}{|R_{d}(\mathbf{A})|}&\leq\max_{R\in\mathscr{R}_{d}(\mathbf{A})}\frac{|R\cap R_{d+1}(\mathbf{A})|}{|R|}\leq\max_{R\in\mathscr{R}_{d}(\mathbf{A})}\sum_{t\in T_{R}}\frac{|W_{R,t}|}{|R|}\\
&\leq(t_{d}+1)\exp\left(\left(F(\mu_{d})\right)t_{d}\right)\leq\exp\left(\left(F(\mu_{d})+\varepsilon\right)t_{d}\right),\end{split}

provided
N_{0}
is sufficiently large.
This implies Lemma
4.6
(a).

For a proof of Lemma
4.6
(b), it suffices to show that

(4.29)

\int\vec{\phi}d\nu_{R,t}>\vec{\alpha}_{d}-\vec{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ for }R\in\mathscr{R}_{d}(\mathbf{A})\text{ and }t\in T_{R}.

We may assume
t_{d}-t\geq p_{M}
, for otherwise (
4.29
) holds if
N_{0}
is sufficiently large.
For each
t\in T_{R}

we fix
x_{R,t}\in\bar{A}_{t_{d}}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha}_{d})\cap V_{R,t}\subset K_{M}
, and let
y_{R,t}
denote the point from the periodic orbit supporting the measure
\nu

that is contained
in the connected component of
K_{M}\setminus K_{M+1}
containing
x_{R,t}
.
Take
k\in\{0,\ldots,p_{M}-1\}
with
f^{t}(x_{R,t})\in f^{k}(J)
.
The choice of
\delta
in (
4.21
) and that of
J
imply

(4.30)

S_{t}\vec{\phi}(x_{R,t})\leq S_{t}\vec{\phi}(y_{R,t})+\frac{t_{d}}{3}\vec{\varepsilon},

and there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
depending only on
f
,
\delta
,
\phi
such that

(4.31)

\|S_{t_{d}-t}\vec{\phi}(f^{t}(x_{R,t}))-S_{t_{d}-t}\vec{\phi}(f^{k}(z))\|<\frac{t_{d}-t}{3}\varepsilon+C_{1}.

Write
t_{d}-t=p_{M}q+r
where
q
,
r
are non-negative integers with
0\leq r\leq p_{M}-1
. Since
z
is of period
p_{M}
, (
4.31
) gives

(4.32)

\begin{split}\int\vec{\phi}d\delta_{O(f)}&=\frac{1}{p_{M}q}\left(S_{t_{d}-t}\vec{\phi}(f^{k}(z))-S_{r}\vec{\phi}(f^{p_{M}q+k}(z))\right)\\
&>\frac{1}{p_{M}q}\left(S_{t_{d}-t}\vec{\phi}(f^{t}(x_{R,t}))-\frac{t_{d}-t}{3}\vec{\varepsilon}-C_{1}\vec{1}-S_{r}\vec{\phi}(f^{p_{M}q+k}(z))\right).\end{split}

Meanwhile, (
4.30
) gives
S_{t_{d}-t}\vec{\phi}(f^{t}(x_{R,t}))=S_{t_{d}}\vec{\phi}(x_{R,t})-S_{t}\vec{\phi}(x_{R,t})>t_{d}\vec{\alpha}_{d}-S_{t}\vec{\phi}(y_{R,t})-(t_{d}/3)\vec{\varepsilon}.

Plugging this inequality into (
4.32
) gives

\int\vec{\phi}d\delta_{O(f)}>\frac{1}{p_{M}q}\left(t_{d}\vec{\alpha}_{d}-S_{t}\vec{\phi}(y_{R,t})-\frac{2t_{d}-t}{3}\vec{\varepsilon}-C_{1}\vec{1}-S_{r}\vec{\phi}(f^{p_{M}q+k}(z))\right).

Note that there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
depending only on
f
,
\delta
,
\phi
such that

\int\vec{\phi}d\nu>\frac{1}{t}(S_{t}\vec{\phi}(y_{R,t})-C_{2}\vec{1})\quad\text{for }t\in\{1,\ldots,t_{d}\}.

The last two inequalities
together imply (
4.29
) provided
N_{0}
is sufficiently large.

Case 3:
m(f)=M
and
\partial J_{m(f)}
contains an attracting periodic point (Case C-II in Remark
2.2
).

Set
\mu_{d}=\delta_{O(f)}
. Clearly
Lemma
4.6
(a) holds because
F(\mu_{d})=0
. Lemma
4.5
gives

f^{n_{d}}(R_{d+1}(\mathbf{A}))\subset\bar{A}_{t_{d}}(\vec{\phi},\vec{\alpha}_{d}-(1/2)\vec{\varepsilon}).

The uniform convergence in (
2.2
) implies
Lemma
4.6
(b) if
N_{0}
is sufficiently large.

Case 4:
m(f)=M
and
f
has no attracting periodic point
(Case A in Remark
2.2
).

Recall that

with
|a-a(j)|<\Delta(j)
.
Iterating this construction yields the four sequences with the required properties. ∎

Remark 5.5
.

The counterexample in Theorem B can be found in polynomial maps of degree
3
, by considering an appropriate one-parameter family
f_{a}
of polynomial of degree
3
and applying the argument parallel to the proof of
Proposition
5.2
above. Below we explain the construction of the one-parameter family
f_{a}
, but leave the details to the interested readers.

First we take a polynomial
f
of degree
3
so that there exist

\circ

two non-degenerate critical points
c_{0}<c_{1}
and non-empty compact intervals
K
and
X
with
K\subsetneq X
,
c_{0}\in X\setminus K
and
c_{1}\in K
, and

\circ

a non-trivial hyperbolic basic set
\Lambda\subset K
, and a hyperbolic repelling periodic point
q\in\Lambda
,

satisfying the conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) with
p=f(c_{1})
. This is possible by the combinatorial argument on the dynamics of continuous piecewise monotone maps
[
24
, Chapter II, Sections 4-6]
.

Second we consider a one-parameter family
f_{a}
of polynomial of degree
3
with
f_{0}=f
.
Clearly the condition (iv) is not true for
f_{a}
in this case, unless the family
f_{a}
is trivial. But this is not an essential problem as we put the condition (iv) just to make computations easier. The critical points
c_{0}<c_{1}
and periodic points
p
,
q
extend continuously for
f_{a}
with
a
close to
0
, and we write
c_{0}(a)<c_{1}(a)
and
p(a)
,
q(a)
for them respectively. In the place of the condition (v), we require

(5.5)

\frac{d}{da}(f_{a}(c_{0}(a))-p(a))\big|_{a=0}\neq 0.

Though this condition is likely to hold for generic family
f_{a}
with
f_{0}=f
, it is not easy to make sure of the condition rigorously for a family of polynomials of degree
3
. Here we exploit the theory of Douady-Hubbard-Thurston on the dynamics of complex polynomials.
Consider the two-parameter family

f_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=f(x)+\alpha x+\beta

and regard it as a family of complex dynamical systems with complex parameters
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. We apply
[
33
, Main Theorem 1.1]
to this family, restricting the parameter
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
to a small neighborhood
V
of
(0,0)
. To this end, we have to check that the family
f_{\alpha,\beta}
is a
normalized family
(see
[
33
]
), that is, no affine transformation conjugates two maps in the family
f_{\alpha,\beta}
with different parameters in
V
. This is not difficult: compute
L^{-1}\circ f_{\alpha,\beta}\circ L
for an affine map
L(z)=Az+B
and use the fact that the coefficients of
f_{\alpha,\beta}
with degree
2
and
3
do not depend on the parameter
(\alpha,\beta)
. As the conclusion, we obtain that
the differential of

\Psi:(\alpha,\beta)\in V\mapsto(f_{\alpha,\beta}(c_{0}(\alpha,\beta))-p(\alpha,\beta),f^{3}_{\alpha,\beta}(c_{1}(\alpha,\beta))-c_{1}(\alpha,\beta))\in\mathbb{C}^{2}

at
(0,0)
is injective, where
c_{0}(\alpha,\beta)
,
c_{1}(\alpha,\beta)
,
p(\alpha,\beta)
denote the continuous extension of the non-degenerate critical points
c_{0}
,
c_{1}
and the hyperbolic periodic point
p
for
f_{0}
to those for
f_{\alpha,\beta}
respectively. Clearly we can restrict the map
\Psi
to
V\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}
and see that
D\Psi_{(0,0)}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is injective.
Thus we can define the family
f_{a}=f_{\alpha(a),\beta(a)}
for
a
with small modulus so that
\Psi(\alpha(a),\beta(a))=(a,0)
. Then
the condition (
5.5
) holds for such a family
f_{a}
.

For the family
f_{a}
constructed as above, we have

\partial_{a}(f^{n}_{a}(c_{0})-f^{n-1}_{a}(p(a)))=Df^{n-1}_{a}(f_{a}(c_{0}))\cdot(\partial_{a}(f_{a}(c_{0})-p(a)))

at the parameter
a=0
, correspondingly to (
5.4
).
Since
\partial_{a}(f^{n}_{a}(p(a)))

discussed above, we can follow the argument in

5.2

.

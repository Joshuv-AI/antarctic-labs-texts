Stochastic six-vertex models, Hall-Littlewood positivity and t t -deformed Schensted insertions
Konstantin Matveev

Abstract
. We prove a positivity theorem for a certain family of operators defined in terms of the stochastic six-vertex model. We explore connections of this result with other vertex models and t t -deformed Schensted insertions.

1.1.
Positivity phenomena

The main result of this paper is theorem
1.6
. It belongs to the following class of theorems. Suppose
P(h_{1},h_{2},\ldots,h_{m})
is some polynomial with many positive and negative coefficients. Suppose each
h_{i}
is itself a polynomial in
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}
. Then a priori there is no reason to expect that
P
as a polynomial in
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}
will have positive coefficients. If it happens to be the case, it might be an indication that there is some structure underlying this phenomenon. Here is a relevant example. Denote by
\Lambda_{n}
the commutative algebra of symmetric polynomials in
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}
over
\mathbb{R}
. Define the
r
-th
complete symmetric polynomial

(1.1)

\displaystyle h_{r}:=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\cdots\leq i_{r}\leq n}a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{r}}\in\Lambda_{n},\qquad h_{0}=1,\qquad h_{r}=0\quad\text{for }r<0.

Proposition 1.1
.

For a partition
\lambda=\{\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{\ell}\}
,
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
, take polynomial

to be
\det\left[h_{\lambda_{i}-i+j}\right]_{i,j=1}^{\ell}

In this case

(1.2)

\displaystyle S_{\lambda}=\sum_{\bm{\lambda}\text{ of shape }\lambda}a^{\bm{\lambda}}=\sum_{\bm{\lambda}\text{ of shape }\lambda}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}(i)},

equal to

[

Hall-Littlewood symmetric polynomial

Definition 1.3

\displaystyle\mathsf{i}\cdot S=\begin{cases}S,\quad\text{if }i\in S,\\

1.5.
Statement of the main result

Our main result is the following

Theorem 1.6
.

For
0\leq t<1
and any partition
\lambda
all matrix elements of
\Theta(P_{\lambda})
with respect to basis

are polynomials in
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}
with non-negative coefficients.

Corollary 1.7
.

For

and any partition
\lambda
with
\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\leq n
the matrix of
\frac{\Theta(P_{\lambda})}{P_{\lambda}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})}
with respect to basis

Denote by
\Lambda

\left\{P_{\lambda}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots;q,t)\right\}_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}

In particular, the one-row Macdonald functions are

(2.1)

\displaystyle g_{r}:=Q_{(r)}=\sum_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},\ldots\geq 0:\ r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3}+\cdots=r}\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{(t;q)_{r_{i}}}{(q,q)_{r_{i}}}x_{i}^{r_{i}},\quad\text{where}\quad(a;q)_{k}:=\prod_{m=1}^{k}\left(1-aq^{m-1}\right)

is the

, the one-column Macdonald function. Note that for

of subsection

. Then

Corollary 2.2

Suppose

Proof of corollary

.

\theta

\theta(a_{i}):=\alpha_{i}\geq 0
is non-negative on all Hall-Littlewood polynomials due to equality (

). For the reverse direction first define

by setting

for

\pi(e_{r})=e_{r}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})
for

for

.
There is a homomorphism

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\tilde{\theta}(g_{r}(t,0))z^{r}=e^{\gamma z}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\alpha_{i}z;t)_{\infty}}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\beta_{j}z\right)

.

∎

3.2.
Schensted’s insertions and interacting particle systems

Schensted’s row and column insertions (

of deterministic long-range pulling and pushing, which involve only two consecutive levels of an array.

Definition 3.3
.

(Deterministic long-range pulling, Fig.

Let

,

Define

\displaystyle\nu=\mathsf{pull}(\lambda\mid\bar{\lambda}\to\bar{\nu}):=\begin{cases}\lambda+\mathrm{e}_{i},&\text{if $\bar{\lambda}_{i}=\lambda_{i}$};\\

does not encounter any blocking issues.

Definition 3.6
.

The Schensted’s row insertion is an algorithm that takes a semistandard tableau
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathbb{GT}^{(n)}
, and an integer
1\leq x\leq n
, and constructs a new tableau
\bm{\lambda}\leftarrow x
according to the following procedure:

\bullet
If
x
is at least as large as all the entries in the first row of
\bm{\lambda}
, add
x
in a new box to the end of the first row. In this case the algorithm terminates.

\bullet
Otherwise find the leftmost entry
y
in the first row that is strictly larger than
x
and replace it by
x
.

\bullet
Repeat the same steps with
y
and the second row, then with the replaced entry
z
and the third row, …, and so on until the replaced entry can be put in the end of the next row, possibly by forming a new row of one entry. Then the algorithm terminates.

In terms of arrays and long-range pulling we can describe this insertion in the following way:

\bullet
Levels
\lambda^{(1)},\ldots,\lambda^{(x-1)}
remain unchanged.

\bullet
Rightmost particle on the level
x
moves by
1
to the right, i.e
\lambda^{(x)}\to\lambda^{(x)}+\bar{\mathrm{e}}_{1}
.

\bullet
Then
\mathsf{pull}
operations are consecutively performed for
j=x+1,\ldots,n
.

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}\leftarrow X:=\bm{\lambda}\leftarrow x_{1}\leftarrow x_{2}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow x_{\ell}

Definition 3.7
.

The Schensted’s column insertion is an algorithm that takes a semistandard tableau
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathbb{GT}^{(n)}
, and an integer
1\leq x\leq n
, and constructs a new tableau

according to the following procedure:

\bullet
If
x
is strictly larger than all the entries in the first column of
\bm{\lambda}
, add
x
in a new box at the bottom of the first column. In this case the algorithm terminates.

\bullet
Otherwise find the topmost entry
y
in the first column that is at least large as
x
and replace it by
x
.

\bullet
Repeat the same steps with
y
and the second column, then with the replaced entry
z
and the third column, …, and so on until the replaced entry can be put at the bottom of the next column, possibly by forming a column of one entry. Then the algorithm terminates.

In terms of arrays and long-range pushing we can describe this insertion in the following way:

\bullet
Levels
\lambda^{(1)},\ldots,\lambda^{(x-1)}
remain unchanged.

\bullet
Leftmost particle on the level
x
moves by
1
to the right, i.e
\lambda^{(x)}\to\lambda^{(x)}+\bar{\mathrm{e}}_{x}
.

\bullet
Then
\mathsf{push}
operations are consecutively performed for
j=x+1,\ldots,n
.

-th precisely one particle moves to the right by

in a tableau
\bm{\lambda}
by consecutively inserting its entries one by one in reverse order:

\displaystyle X\to\bm{\lambda}:=x_{1}\to x_{2}\to\cdots\to x_{\ell}\to\bm{\lambda}

3.3.
Plactic Algebra

To a semistandard tableau
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathbb{GT}^{(n)}
one can associate an element
w(\bm{\lambda})\in Pl_{n}
represented by a word obtained by reading entry letters of
\bm{\lambda}
first in the bottom row from left to right, then in the second row from the bottom from left to right, and so on. For instance, for a tableau on Fig.
8
the corresponding word will be
\mathsf{554433322255551111344}
. The following proposition explains basic connections between the plactic monoid and Schensted insertions.

Proposition 3.8
.

(see
[
Lot02
]
).

(1)

For every
a\in Pl_{n}
there exists a unique tableau
\bm{\lambda}
, such that
a=w(\bm{\lambda})
.

(2)

w(\bm{\lambda}\leftarrow x)=w(\bm{\lambda})x
.

(3)

w(x\to\bm{\lambda})=xw(\bm{\lambda})
.

Hence consecutively inserting
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r}
via the Schensted’s row insertion into a tableau
\bm{\lambda}
leads to the the same result as multiplication of
w(\bm{\lambda})
by
X=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{r}
on the right, while multiplication of
w(\bm{\lambda})
by
X
on the left amounts to the same result as consecutively inserting
x_{r},\ldots,x_{2},x_{1}
in
\bm{\lambda}
via the Schensted’s column insertion.

The plactic algebra is noncommutative for
n\geq 2
, but it contains nice families of commuting elements. More precisely, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{GT}^{+}_{n}
and variables
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}
define the
plactic Schur polynomial

(3.2)

\displaystyle S_{\lambda}^{Pl}(a_{1}\mathsf{1},a_{2}\mathsf{2},,\ldots,a_{n}\mathsf{n}):=\sum_{\bm{\lambda}\in\mathbb{GT}^{(n)}(\lambda)}w(\bm{\lambda})\cdot a_{1}^{|\lambda^{(1)}|}a_{2}^{|\lambda^{(2)}|-|\lambda^{(1)}|}\cdots a_{n}^{|\lambda^{(n)}|-|\lambda^{(n-1)}|}.

Proposition 3.9
.

(see
[
Lot02
]
).

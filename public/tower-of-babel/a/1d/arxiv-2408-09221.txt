Maurer–Cartan elements in symplectic cohomology from compactifications
Matthew Strom Borman, Mohamed El Alami, and Nick Sheridan

Abstract
. We prove that under certain conditions, a normal crossings compactification of a Liouville domain determines a Maurer–Cartan element for the L ∞ L_{\infty} structure on its symplectic cohomology; and deforming by this element gives the quantum cohomology of the compactification.

1.2.
Maurer–Cartan element

We briefly recall the formalism of Maurer–Cartan elements in
L_{\infty}
algebras, following
[
Get09
]

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\ell^{*})
be an
L_{\infty}
algebra over
\mathbf{k}
, and
(R,Q_{\geq\bullet})
a graded filtered
\mathbf{k}
-algebra.
We define
\mathfrak{g}^{R}
to be the degreewise completion of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes R
; it carries an
L_{\infty}

Define the Maurer–Cartan set

(1.4)

MC(\mathfrak{g},R)\vcentcolon=\left\{\beta\in Q_{\geq 1}\mathfrak{g}^{R}\ |\ \deg(\beta)=2\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{F}(\beta)=0\right\},

where
\mathcal{F}(\beta)\in\mathfrak{g}^{R}
is the curvature term

(1.5)

\mathcal{F}(\beta)=\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{d!}\ell^{d}(\beta,\dots,\beta).

(The appearance of

\mathbf{k}
has characteristic zero.)

Elements
\beta\in MC(\mathfrak{g},R)
are used to define deformed
L_{\infty}
operations,

(1.6)

\ell_{\beta}^{d}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\ell^{k+d}(\beta^{\otimes k},x_{1},\dots,x_{d}),

see
[
Get09
, Proposition 4.4]
.
Given an element
\gamma\in Q_{\geq 1}\mathfrak{g}^{R}
of degree
1

\alpha\mapsto\ell^{1}_{\alpha}(\gamma)
which is tangent to
MC(\mathfrak{g},R)
; Maurer–Cartan elements which lie on a common flowline of such a vector field are said to be gauge equivalent, and give rise to quasi-isomorphic deformed
L_{\infty}
algebras.

The main construction of this paper is:

Construction A

We construct a Maurer–Cartan element

\beta\in MC(SC^{*}(X;\mathbf{k}),R^{+}).

where

Under assumptions where sphere bubbling is ruled out (e.g., if there is no spherical class

1.3.
Deforming by the Maurer–Cartan element

Our main result says that the deformation of symplectic cohomology by the Maurer–Cartan element
\beta

Hypothesis B

(Hypothesis A of
[

]
)
.

For all

\lambda_{j}\leq 2

We then have:

Theorem C
.

Under Hypothesis
B

R

QH^{*}(M;R)\cong H^{*}\left(SC^{*}(X;\mathbf{k})\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbf{k}}R,\ell^{1}_{\beta}\right).

Note that we have abused notation slightly by continuing to use the same notation

We remark that Pomerleano–Seidel have proved an analogue of Theorem

In fact, Pomerleano–Seidel prove a more precise result: they identify the cohomology of

They also allow

1.5.
Implications for mirror symmetry in the log Calabi–Yau case

Following
[
Ton19
]
and
[
BSV22
, Section 1.6]
, we explain how our main result, together with the conjectures of the previous section, give an alternative perspective on Auroux’ work on mirror symmetry in the log Calabi–Yau case
[
Aur07
]
.
Thus, we assume that
D
is anticanonical, meaning
\lambda_{j}=2
for all
j
.

Suppose that
Y
is a smooth
\Bbbk
-variety mirror to
X
, in the sense that we have a quasi-equivalence

\mathcal{W}(X;\mathbf{k})\simeq\mathrm{DCoh}(Y).

Hacking and Keating have proved such a result for surfaces
[
HK22
]
; in higher dimensions, one may expect it to be an output of the ‘intrinsic mirror symmetry’ programme, see
[
GS18
,
Pom21
]
.

The mirror quasi-equivalence induces
L_{\infty}
quasi-isomorphisms

(1.8)

SC^{*}(X;\mathbf{k})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{CO}}CC^{*}(\mathcal{W}(X;\mathbf{k}))\simeq CC^{*}(\mathrm{DCoh}(Y))\simeq C^{*}(Y,\wedge^{*}TY).

Here
C^{*}(Y,\wedge^{*}TY)
is the DG Lie algebra of derived global sections of the sheaf
(\wedge^{*}TY,0,[-,-])
of DG Lie algebras with vanishing differential, and Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket (see e.g.
[
VdB07
, Appendix B]
for more on the general construction of a structure of DG Lie algebra on a chain complex underlying derived global sections of a sheaf of DG Lie algebras; in the complex case, a specific model using Dolbeault cohomology was used in
[
BK98
]
).
The main step in the construction of the right-hand quasi-equivalence is Konstevich’s Formality Theorem
[
Kon03
,
Kon01
]
.

Because
SC^{*}(X;\mathbf{k})
is concentrated in non-negative degrees, and the generators
q_{j}
of
R^{+}
have degree
2
, the Maurer–Cartan element
\beta\in MC(SC^{*}(X;\mathbf{k}),R^{+})
has only linear terms, and its gauge equivalence class is completely determined by

D\beta(0)=\sum_{j}q_{j}\beta_{j},

where
\beta_{j}\in SH^{0}(X;\mathbf{k})
.
Under (
1.8
), the Maurer–Cartan element
\beta
corresponds to a Maurer–Cartan element
w\in MC(C^{*}(Y,\wedge^{*}TY),R^{+})
, which is similarly determined by the linear terms

Dw(0)=\sum_{j}q_{j}w_{j}

where
w_{j}\in H^{0}(\wedge^{0}TY)=\mathcal{O}(Y)
.
In other words, the gauge equivalence class of the Maurer–Cartan element is determined by a ‘multi-potential’
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{N}):Y\to\mathbb{A}^{n}
, in the terminology of
[
Lee24
]
.

The Maurer–Cartan element
w
determines an
A_{\infty}
deformation of
\mathrm{DCoh}(Y)
over
R^{+}
, which we denote by
\mathrm{DCoh}(Y,w)
; it is immediate that we have a filtered quasi-equivalence of
R^{+}
-linear curved
A_{\infty}
categories

\mathcal{W}(M,D)\simeq\mathrm{DCoh}(Y,w).

In order to get equivalences of uncurved
A_{\infty}
categories, we pass to categories of weak bounding cochains in the sense of
[
FOOO09
]
(see also the exposition in
[
She16
, Section 4]
).
We could do this over
R^{+}
, but in order to get a more familiar mirror symmetry statement, we first pass to the single-variable Novikov ring
R^{+}_{q}=\mathbf{k}[q]
with
|q|=2
, via the graded algebra homomorphism

\displaystyle R^{+}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle R^{+}_{q}

\displaystyle q_{j}

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle q\quad\text{for all $j$.}

We denote the resulting Maurer–Cartan elements by
\beta_{q}=q\sum\beta_{j}
, and
w_{q}=qW
with
W=\sum w_{j}
.
We obtain a filtered quasi-equivalence of
R^{+}_{q}
-linear curved
A_{\infty}
categories

\mathcal{W}(M,D)_{q}\simeq\mathrm{DCoh}(Y,w_{q}).

This induces a filtered quasi-equivalence of the corresponding uncurved categories of weak bounding cochains with curvature
\lambda q
:

\mathcal{W}(M,D)^{wbc}_{\lambda q}\simeq\mathrm{DCoh}(Y,w_{q})^{wbc}_{\lambda q}

for all
\lambda\in\mathbf{k}
(see
[
PS23a
, Theorem 2.12]
for the case
\lambda=0
). Setting
q=1
, we obtain quasi-equivalences of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded
\mathbf{k}
-linear
A_{\infty}
categories, which we denote by

\mathcal{W}(M,D)^{wbc}_{\lambda}\simeq\mathrm{DCoh}(Y,W)^{wbc}_{\lambda}.

We expect that
\mathrm{DCoh}(Y,W)^{wbc}_{\lambda}
coincides with the category of matrix factorizations
MF(Y,W-\lambda)
defined in
[
Orl12
,
LP13
]
(which is equivalent to Orlov’s triangulated category of singularities
\mathrm{DSing}(Y_{\lambda})

[
Orl04
]
, where
Y_{\lambda}
is the fibre of
W
over
\lambda
, by
[
Orl12
, Theorem 3.5]
).
Roughly, we expect that deforming by the Maurer–Cartan element
qW
has the simple effect of adding a curvature term
qW\cdot\mathrm{id}
to each object.
A matrix factorization

E_{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}}E_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}E_{0},\qquad\text{satisfying}\qquad d_{0}d_{1}=(W-\lambda)\cdot\text{id}_{E_{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad d_{1}d_{0}=(W-\lambda)\cdot\mathrm{id}_{E_{0}},

can then be considered as a complex of coherent sheaves

E_{0}[-1]\xrightarrow{d_{0}}E_{1},

equipped with a weak bounding cochain
qd_{1}
, where the only non-vanishing terms in the Maurer–Cartan equation

\mathfrak{m}^{0}+\mathfrak{m}^{1}(qd_{1})+\mathfrak{m}^{2}(qd_{1},qd_{1})+\ldots=q\lambda\cdot\mathrm{id}

are
\mathfrak{m}^{0}=qW\cdot\mathrm{id}
and
\mathfrak{m}^{1}(qd_{1})=q(d_{0}d_{1}+d_{1}d_{0})=q(W-\lambda)\cdot\text{id}_{E_{0}[-1]\oplus E_{1}}
.

Assuming this holds, we obtain a quasi-equivalence

\mathcal{W}(M,D)^{wbc}_{\lambda}\simeq MF(W-\lambda)

for all
\lambda
, which is the usual statement of homological mirror symmetry, albeit with an enlarged Fukaya category.
Of course, if the subcategory
\mathcal{F}(M,D)^{wbc}_{\lambda}\subset\mathcal{W}(M,D)^{wbc}_{\lambda}
split-generates, we may replace
\mathcal{W}
with the more familiar
\mathcal{F}
.

Example 1.7
.

Let
M
be a smooth Fano toric variety, and
D
its toric boundary divisor.
Then the completion of
X=M\setminus D
is
T^{*}T^{n}
, which is mirror to
Y=\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
.
It follows from
[
Ton19
, Theorem 6.5]
and the computation of disc potentials for monotone torus fibres in Fano toric varieties
[
CO06
]
that
w_{j}=z^{v_{j}}\in\mathcal{O}(Y)=\mathbf{k}[z_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,z_{n}^{\pm 1}]
where
v_{j}
is the ray of the fan of
M
corresponding to component
D_{j}
of
D
.
Thus
W=\sum_{j}w_{j}
is the usual Hori–Vara mirror potential, so we get the expected homological mirror symmetry result for Fano toric varieties; note that this is a case where we expect
\mathcal{F}(M,D)^{wbc}_{\lambda}\subset\mathcal{W}(M,D)^{wbc}_{\lambda}
to split-generate, as each component of the Fukaya category is split-generated by some local system on the monotone torus fibre by
[
EL19
, Corollary 1.3.1]
.

In a slightly different direction, we may deform the quasi-isomorphism (
1.8
) by the respective Maurer–Cartan elements, tensor with
R_{q}:=\mathbf{k}[q^{\pm 1}]
, then take cohomology. The left-hand side becomes

by Theorem

, while the right-hand side becomes
\mathcal{O}(Z^{h})\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}R_{q}
, where
\mathcal{O}(Z^{h})
is the algebra of functions on the derived critical locus of
W
, as argued in
[
BSV22
, Section 1.6.4]
.
In the case that the critical locus of
W
is isolated, this is the ordinary algebra of functions on the critical locus, which decomposes as the direct sum of Jacobian algebras of the critical points of
W
.
This gives us the familiar closed-string mirror isomorphism between quantum cohomology of
M
and the Jacobian algebra of
W
, on the level of vector spaces – in order to upgrade it to respect more algebraic structure, one would need to correspondingly upgrade Theorem

(cf.
[
PS23b
]
), as well as the Formality Theorem (cf.
[
Tam98
]
and
[
Pre12
, Section 7]
).

1.6.
Outline

Let us first motivate Theorem
C
.
It is shown in
[
BSV22
]
how to compute the quantum cohomology in terms of Hamiltonian Floer theory of a sequence of Hamiltonians which become infinite along the divisor; and that one may safely ‘ignore’ the orbits which are not contained in
X
for this computation.
Each relevant Floer trajectory, then, passes between orbits disjoint from
D
.
We now imagine ‘stretching the neck’ along
\partial X
, in the sense of symplectic field theory
[
EGH00
]
.
Floer trajectories will degenerate into components contained in
X
, which can be identified with
L_{\infty}
operations; and ‘caps’ lying outside
X
, whose count should define the Maurer–Cartan element
\beta
.
Thus we expect neck-stretching to give a chain homotopy between the Floer differential (which counts Floer trajectories), and the deformed differential
\ell^{1}_{\beta}
.

Rather than implement this approach, we use a different approach which substitutes algebra for neck-stretching analysis.
Recall that the symplectic cochains on
X
are defined in
[
BEAS24
]
as the telescope complex of an increasing sequence of Hamiltonians:

SC^{*}(X;\mathbf{k})=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}CF^{*}(X,H_{n};\mathbf{k})[t],

where
t
is a formal variable satisfying
t^{2}=0
.
We enlarge this telescope complex to include formal generators
x^{D_{j}}
for each component
D_{j}
of the divisor, which we think of as carrying weight
n=0
:

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{N}\mathbf{k}[t]\cdot x^{D_{j}}\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}CF^{*}(X,H_{n};\mathbf{k})[t].

We extend the
L_{\infty}
structure to this enlarged complex by counting pseudoholomorphic curves
u
in
M
, where weight-zero inputs
x^{D_{j}}
or
t\cdot x^{D_{j}}
correspond bijectively to intersection points of
u
with
D_{j}
.

Remark 1.8
.

In fact, we are not able to prove that the extended ‘
L_{\infty}
operations’ satisfy the
L_{\infty}
relations, due to breakings which may occur along orbits lying outside of
X
.
Instead, we show that they satisfy the
L_{\infty}
operations when all inputs are drawn from a certain subspace, and show that this suffices for our purposes.
Although this is the most technically involved part of the paper, it is not conceptually enlightening so we will elide it for the remainder of this section.

The extended
L_{\infty}
products are defined to vanish if all inputs are formal generators
x^{D_{j}}
; this is valid because when the corresponding marked points bubble off in our moduli spaces they form a sphere bubble, which we may arrange to appear in codimension
2
rather than codimension
1
as with the other boundary components contributing to the
L_{\infty}
relations.
As a result, we have a Maurer–Cartan element

\alpha_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}q_{j}\cdot x^{D_{j}}\in MC(\mathfrak{g},R^{+})

(it satisfies the Maurer–Cartan equation (
1.5
) because every term vanishes).
From
\alpha_{0}
, we construct a one-parameter family of gauge-equivalent Maurer–Cartan elements
\alpha_{\tau}
by flowing along the vector field determined by
\gamma=t\cdot\alpha_{0}
.
The weight-zero part of
\alpha_{\tau}
is
(1-\tau)\cdot\alpha_{0}
, and in particular,
\alpha_{1}\in MC(SC^{*}(X;\mathbf{k}),R^{+})
.
The Maurer–Cartan element
\beta
from Construction
A
is defined to be
\alpha_{1}
.

We now outline the proof of Theorem
C
.
We define a telescope complex

QC^{*}(M;R)\vcentcolon=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}CF^{*}(M,H_{n})[t]

associated to the sequence of Hamiltonians
H_{n}
, extended to
M
.
We have an isomorphism

QH^{*}(M;R)\cong H^{*}(QC^{*}(M;R),d^{QC}),

simply because each chain complex in the directed system computes
QH^{*}(M;R)
by the PSS isomorphism, and the continuation maps between them are isomorphisms.

In
[
BSV22
]
, a map

\displaystyle\Phi:SC^{*}(X;\mathbf{k})\otimes R

\displaystyle\to QC^{*}(M;R)

is constructed, sending

\gamma\mapsto[\gamma,u]\otimes q_{1}^{u\cdot D_{1}}\ldots q_{N}^{u\cdot D_{N}}

for an arbitrary cap
u
for
\gamma
.
We arrange that this
\Phi
is a chain map:

(1.9)

\Phi(\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{0}}(\gamma))=d^{QC}(\Phi(\gamma)),

because the differentials on the two sides of the equation count isomorphic moduli spaces.

Remark 1.9
.

In order for the infinite sum defining the differential
\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{0}}
to be defined in general, and for our subsequent application of standard arguments from Maurer–Cartan theory, we need our complexes to be degreewise complete, i.e. to work with
SC^{*}(X;\mathbf{k})\widehat{\otimes}R
rather than
SC^{*}(X;\mathbf{k})\otimes R
.
Under Hypothesis
B
however,
SC^{*}(X;\mathbf{k})
is concentrated in non-negative degrees, which implies that the degreewise completion has no effect:
SC^{*}(X;\mathbf{k})\widehat{\otimes}R=SC^{*}(X;\mathbf{k})\otimes R
.

Let us now justify (
1.9
).
On the RHS, the differential counts Floer trajectories and continuation maps
u
in
M
between orbits
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}
in
X
; while on the LHS, we arrange that the terms

\frac{1}{d!}\ell^{1+d}\left(\gamma_{1},q_{j_{1}}x^{D_{j_{1}}},\ldots,q_{j_{d}}x^{D_{j_{d}}}\right)

count precisely the same curves
u
with the same weights.
Thus (up to a harmless passage to a subquotient complex to remove orbits outside of
X
, which we elide for the purposes of this section), we obtain a chain isomorphism

(1.10)

\Phi:\left(SC^{*}(X;\mathbf{k})\otimes R,\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{0}}\right)\xrightarrow{\sim}\left(QC^{*}(M;R),d^{QC}\right).

We also have that the inclusions

(1.11)

\left(SC^{*}(X;\mathbf{k})\otimes R,\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{\tau}}\right)\hookrightarrow\left(\mathfrak{g}\otimes R,\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{\tau}}\right)

are quasi-isomorphisms for all
\tau
, as the quotient complexes are manifestly acyclic.

On the other hand, the Maurer–Cartan elements
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{1}=\beta
determine quasi-isomorphic deformations

(1.12)

\left(\mathfrak{g}\otimes R,\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{0}}\right)\simeq\left(\mathfrak{g}\otimes R,\ell^{1}_{\beta}\right)

due to the fact they are gauge equivalent, by a standard argument from Maurer–Cartan theory.
Theorem
C
now follows by composing the isomorphisms (
1.10
), (
1.11
) for
\tau=0
, (
1.12
), and (
1.11
) for
\tau=1
.

2.
Geometric Background

In this section we introduce our conventions concerning quantum cohomology and Floer cohomology (we refer to
[
MS04
,
Sal99
]
for additional background on these areas), and introduce the Hamiltonians which will play a key role in our main argument.

2.1.
Quantum Cohomology

Recall that we assume
(M,\omega)
to be a compact symplectic manifold which is positively monotone, so that

[\omega]=2\kappa c_{1}(M)\quad\text{in}\ H^{2}(M,\mathbb{R}),\quad\kappa>0.

We define the graded Novikov ring
\Lambda^{\text{gr}}_{\omega}
to be the group ring
\mathbf{k}[\pi_{2}(M)]
, i.e., the algebra generated by formal symbols
\{e^{A}\ |\ A\in\pi_{2}(M)\}
, subject to the relation
e^{A}\cdot e^{B}=e^{A+B}
. We equip it with the integer grading
\lvert e^{A}\rvert=2c_{1}(A)
.
We observe that it is
degreewise
complete with respect to the action functional

(2.1)

A_{\omega}(e^{A})=\omega(A)

(because the action is constant, by monotonicity).
We note that
\Lambda^{\text{gr}}_{\omega}
is similar to the Novikov ring in
[
Tia95
, §5]
.
It is however different from the more common Novikov
\Lambda_{\omega}
obtained by taking the full completion, as decribed in
[
PSS96
, §2]
; the latter is not a graded ring.

Let
H:S^{1}\times M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a time-dependent Hamiltonian. Its corresponding vector field
X_{H}
is defined by the equation

(2.2)

\iota_{X_{H}}\omega=-dH.

We note that our convention here is identical to
[
Sei08
]
, but different from other references we use such as
[
PSS96
]
or
[
BSV22
]
.

The Hamiltonian
H
is said to be non-degenerate if the time
1
flow
\phi_{1}^{X_{H}}:M\rightarrow M
has a graph
\text{Graph}(\phi_{1}^{X_{H}})\subseteq M\times M
which is transverse to the diagonal
\Delta_{M}\subseteq M\times M
. This property ensures that the set of
1
-periodic orbits is a transversely cut compact manifold of dimension
0
.

A
capped orbit
is a pair
(\gamma,u)
where
\gamma
is a
1
-periodic orbit of
X_{H}
, and
u
is an equivalence class of maps
u:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow M
such that
u|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}=\gamma
, where two such maps
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are equivalent if

u_{1}\#-u_{2}=0

in
\pi_{2}(M)
.
We denote the collection of all capped
1
-periodic orbits by
\mathcal{P}_{H}
. It is exactly the set of critical points of the action functional

(2.3)

A_{H}(\gamma,u)=-\int_{\mathbb{D}}u^{*}\omega+\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\gamma^{*}H.

Assuming that
H
is non-degenerate, we can associate with each capped orbit
x=(\gamma,u)
an orientation line
o_{x}
and its
\mathbf{k}
-normalization
\lvert o_{x}\rvert_{\mathbf{k}}
, see
[
BEAS24
]
.
Floer’s complex associated with
H
is the
graded
completion of
\bigoplus_{x\in\mathcal{P}_{H}}\lvert o_{x}\rvert_{\mathbf{k}}
with respect to the action filtration
A_{H}
,

(2.4)

CF^{*}(M,H)=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{P}_{H}\\
\lvert x\rvert=i\end{subarray}}\lvert o_{x}\rvert_{\mathbf{k}}}^{A_{H}}.

It is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded module over the graded Novikov ring
\Lambda^{\text{gr}}_{\omega}
, where the module structure is

(2.5)

e^{A}\cdot(\gamma,u)=(\gamma,u\#-A).

We note in particular that

(2.6)

\begin{cases}&\lvert e^{A}\cdot(\gamma,u)\rvert=\lvert(\gamma,u)\rvert+2c_{1}(A)\\
&A_{H}(e^{A}\cdot(\gamma,u))=A_{H}(\gamma,u)+\omega(A).\end{cases}

Floer’s complex is also equipped with a differential which counts maps
u:\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}\rightarrow M
satisfying the pseudo-holomorphic curve equation

(2.7)

\begin{cases}&\partial_{s}u+J_{t}(\partial_{t}u-X_{H}(u))=0\\
&E(u)\vcentcolon=\int\lvert\partial_{s}u\rvert^{2}<+\infty,\end{cases}

where
J_{t}
is a generic
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure.
For each
distinct
pair
x_{-},x_{+}\in\mathcal{P}_{H}
, define the moduli space

(2.8)

\mathcal{M}(x_{-},x_{+})=\left\{u\ \text{satisfies}\ \eqref{Floer trajectory},\ u_{-}\#[u]=u_{+}\ \text{and}\ \ \lim_{s\rightarrow\pm\infty}u(s,t)=\gamma_{\pm}\right\}/\mathbb{R},

where the action of
\mathbb{R}
is by translation in the
s
-direction. Floer’s differential on (
2.4
) is

(2.9)

dx_{+}=\sum_{x_{-}\neq x_{+}}\#\mathcal{M}(x_{-},x_{+})\cdot x_{-}.

Here,
\#\mathcal{M}(x_{-},x_{+})
is notation for the signed count of isolated points in this moduli space.
Moreover, for any
\Lambda^{\text{gr}}_{\omega}
-module
R
, we define a Floer complex with coefficients in
R
,

(2.10)

CF^{*}(M,H;R)=CF^{*}(M,H)\otimes_{\Lambda^{\text{gr}}_{\omega}}R.

Its cohomology
HF^{*}(M,H;R)
is independent of
H
and
J
, and we have a PSS-isomorphism (see
[
PSS96
]
)

(2.11)

HF^{*}(M,H;R)\cong H^{*}(M,R).

Under this isomorphism, the quantum product on
H^{*}(M,\Lambda^{\text{gr}}_{\omega})
corresponds to the pair-of-pants product on
HF^{*}(M,H)
. Throughout this work, we refer to Floer cohomology of a
compact positive
symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
as quantum cohomology.

2.2.
Simple crossings divisors

Recall that a symplectic divisor
D\subseteq M
is a (real) codimension 2 compact submanifold such that
\omega|_{D}
is non-degenerate. A collection
(D_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
of symplectic divisors is said to be transverse if, for any subset
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
, the intersection

(2.12)

D_{I}=\bigcap_{i\in I}D_{i}

is a transversely cut smooth submanifold. In particular, the normal bundle of
D_{I}
is given by

(2.13)

N_{M}D_{I}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}N_{M}D_{i}|_{D_{I}}.

For each
I
, the normal bundle
N_{M}D_{I}
acquires a symplectic orientation using the normal bundle sequence

(2.14)

0\rightarrow TD_{I}\rightarrow TM|_{D_{I}}\rightarrow N_{M}D_{I}\rightarrow 0,

where
TD_{I}
and
TM|_{D_{I}}
are oriented using the non-degenerate
2
-form
\omega
. However, this collection of orientations need not be coherent. Following
[
TMZ18
, §2]
, the union
D=(D_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
is said to be

(i)

a simple crossings divisor if the isomorphisms (
2.13
) respect the symplectic orientation for all
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
.

(ii)

orthogonal if for every
i\neq j
, and every
x\in D_{i}\cap D_{j}
, the
\omega
-normal bundle
(T_{x}D_{i})^{\perp\omega}\subseteq TM
is a vector subspace of
T_{x}D_{j}
.

Let
D=(D_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
be an orthogonal simple crossings divisor such that

(2.15)

2c_{1}(M)=\lambda_{1}\textnormal{pd}(D_{1})+\dotsi+\lambda_{N}\textnormal{pd}(D_{N}),

where
\textnormal{pd}:H_{2n-2}(M,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Q})
is Poincaré duality and
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
are positive rational numbers. This condition essentially says that
D
supports (a multiple of) an effective anti-canonical divisor.

Consider the exact symplectic manifold
X=M\backslash D
. By Lefschetz duality, the homology group
H_{2}(M,X,\mathbb{Z})
is freely generated by small discs
(u_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
where each
u_{i}:(\mathbb{D},\partial\mathbb{D})\rightarrow(M,X)
intersects
D_{i}
once transversely at
0\in\mathbb{D}
, and
u_{i}
is disjoint from all
(D_{j})_{j\neq i}
. Using the universal coefficient theorem, we have an isomorphism

(2.16)

H^{2}(M,X,\mathbb{R})\cong H_{2}(M,X,\mathbb{R})^{\vee}.

Let
\textnormal{pd}^{\text{rel}}(D_{i})\in H^{2}(M,X,\mathbb{R})
be the dual basis to
(u_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
, and let

(2.17)

[\omega]^{\text{rel}}=\kappa_{1}\textnormal{pd}^{\text{rel}}(D_{1})+\dotsi+\kappa_{n}\textnormal{pd}^{\text{rel}}(D_{N}),\quad\text{where}\ \kappa_{i}=\kappa\cdot\lambda_{i}.

The class
[\omega]^{\text{rel}}
is a lift of
[\omega]
, and similarly the classes
\textnormal{pd}^{\text{rel}}(D_{i})
are lifts of
\textnormal{pd}(D_{i})
under the map

(2.18)

H^{2}(M,X,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow H^{2}(M,\mathbb{R}).

Recall that we also have a de Rham chain model for relative cohomology groups given by

(2.19)

\Omega(M,X,\mathbb{R})=\text{Cone}(\Omega(M,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\Omega(X,\mathbb{R}))[-1].

In particular,
\omega^{\text{rel}}
may be thought of as a pair
(\omega,-\theta^{\prime})
where
\theta^{\prime}\in\Omega^{1}(X)
such that
d\theta^{\prime}=\omega
. By definition of this relative class, we have

(2.20)

\kappa_{i}=\int_{u_{i}}\omega-\int_{\partial u_{i}}\theta^{\prime}.

This is called the wrapping number of
\omega^{\text{rel}}
relative to the divisor
D_{i}
and it is invariant under exact perturbations of
\theta^{\prime}
. In the terminology of McLean (see
[
McL12
, Lemma 5.17, Appendix A]
),
(X,\theta^{\prime})
is a convex symplectic manifold because all
\kappa_{i}
are positive.

2.3.
The Liouville structure

2.3.1.
When
D
is smooth

Let
D\subseteq M
be a
smooth
symplectic divisor. By a Moser type argument (see
[
McL12
, Lemma 5.9]
), there is a neighbourhood
UD\subseteq M
of
D
which comes with a symplectic fibration map
\pi:UD\rightarrow D
, such that the fibers are symplectomorphic to a standard disc
D_{R}=\{r\leq R\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
of
area

R
. In our notation,
\mathbb{C}
is given the symplectic form

(2.21)

\omega_{\mathbb{C}}=\rho d\rho\wedge d\theta\vcentcolon=dr\wedge d\phi,

where
(\rho,\theta)
are the usual polar coordinates, whereas
r=\pi\rho^{2}
and
\phi=\theta/2\pi
.
The radial coordinate
r:UD\rightarrow[0,R)
generates a Hamiltonian vector field
X_{r}=\partial_{\phi}
which satisfies the following properties:

(i)

The field
X_{r}
integrates to an
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
-action on
UD
.

(ii)

The Hamiltonian action fixes all points of
D
.

(iii)

The Hamiltonian action is free on
UD\backslash D
.

With this setup, let
\theta^{\prime}
be a primitive for
\omega
in
M\backslash D
, and let
F
be a fiber of
\pi:UD\rightarrow D
. Since
F\backslash\{0\}\cong\mathbb{C}^{*}
, we must have a constant
\kappa
, and a function
f:F\backslash\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.22)

\theta^{\prime}|_{F\backslash\{0\}}=rd\phi-\kappa d\phi+df.

This constant
\kappa
is the same as the wrapping number from (
2.20
).
Therefore, if
Z
is the Liouville field of
\theta=\theta^{\prime}-df
, we have

(2.23)

Z(r)=\theta(\partial_{\phi})=r-\kappa.

It follows that the Liouville field
Z
points outwards along the boundary of
M\backslash UD
, provided that
\kappa>0
and
R
is sufficiently small.

2.3.2.
When
D
is orthogonal simple crossings

We recall the construction of the Liouville structure from
[
McL12
, §5]
and
[
BSV22
, §2.3]
. Assume that we have a lift
\omega^{\text{rel}}=(\omega,-\theta^{\prime})
of
\omega
such that

(2.24)

[\omega^{\text{rel}}]=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}\textnormal{pd}^{\text{rel}}(D_{i})\quad\text{in}\ H^{2}(M,X,\mathbb{R}),

where all
\kappa_{i}
are positive.

By
[
McL12
, Lemma 5.14]
, it is possible to construct open neighbourhoods
UD_{i}
of
D_{i}
with radial functions
r_{i}:UD_{i}\rightarrow[0,R)
such that for all
i,j
,

(i)

The intersection
UD_{i}\cap UD_{j}
is invariant under the
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
-action of
X_{r_{i}}
.

(ii)

Along the intersection
UD_{i}\cap UD_{j}
, the Hamiltonians
r_{i}
and
r_{j}
Poisson commute.

The assumptions on
D
ensure that we can construct compatible neighbourhoods
UD_{I}
of
D_{I}
for each subset
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
. The fiber of the projection
\pi_{I}:UD_{I}\rightarrow D_{I}
is a polydisc
F_{I}=\prod_{i\in I}D_{R}
which carries a torus action by the commuting vector fields
(X_{r_{i}})_{i\in I}
. McLean’s lemma constructs an exact perturbation
\theta
of
\theta^{\prime}
such that on each punctured fiber
F_{I}^{*}=\prod_{i\in I}(D_{R}\backslash\{0\})
,

(2.25)

\theta|_{F_{I}^{*}}=\sum_{i\in I}(r_{i}-\kappa_{i})d\phi_{i}.

In particular, if
Z
is the Liouville vector field of
\theta
, we have that

(2.26)

Z(r_{i})=r_{i}-\kappa_{i}\quad\text{on }UD_{i},\text{ for all }i=1,2,\dots,N.

Since all
\kappa_{i}
are positive, there is a positive parameter
R_{0}\in[0,\min_{i}\kappa_{i})
such that
Z
points outwards along the boundary of
M\backslash\cup_{i=1}^{N}\{r_{i}\leq R\}
for all
R\in[0,R_{0})
.

Let
\mathbb{L}
be the
Z
-skeleton of
X
, i.e. the collection of points
p\in X
such that the flow
\phi^{Z}_{t}(p)
is defined for all time
t
. Any point
p\in X\backslash\mathbb{L}
has a Liouville flow that eventually enters one of the open sets
UD_{i}
. Define
UD_{i}^{\max}
to be the open set consisting of
D_{i}
together with all the points in
X
whose Liouville flow enters
UD_{i}
. Note that
\cup_{i}UD_{i}^{\max}=M\backslash\mathbb{L}
, and that each
r_{i}:UD_{i}\rightarrow[0,R)
extends smoothly to
r_{i}^{\max}:UD_{i}^{\max}\rightarrow[0,\kappa_{i})
using the Liouville flow. One checks that the Hamiltonians
r_{i}^{\max}
still define commuting
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
-actions on
UD_{i}^{\max}
.

2.4.
The radial coordinate

Next, we briefly recall a construction from
[
BSV22
, §4]
of a
smooth
radial function
\rho:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.27)

Z(\rho)=\rho.

A natural candidate
\rho^{0}
is given by
e^{-t}
, where
t(x)
is the blow-up time of the flow
\phi^{Z}_{t}(x)
. This is well-defined for any
x\in X\backslash\mathbb{L}
where
\mathbb{L}
is the
Z
-skeleton of
X
. For instance if
x\in UD_{i}
, then
\rho^{0}(x)\leq 1-r_{i}(x)/\kappa_{i}
. In fact, it is not difficult to see that

(2.28)

\rho^{0}(x)=\max\left\{1-\frac{r_{i}(x)}{\kappa_{i}}\ \bigg|\ 1\leq i\leq N\ \text{such that}\ x\in UD_{i}^{\max}\right\}.

We also extend
\rho^{0}
continuously to all of
M\backslash\mathbb{L}
by setting
\rho^{0}|_{D}=1
.

In
[
BSV22
, §4]
, the authors construct a regularization
\rho^{R}
of
\rho^{0}
depending on a smoothing parameter
R\in(0,R_{0})
such that each
\rho^{R}:M\backslash\mathbb{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function satisfying
Z(\rho^{R})=\rho^{R}
on
X\backslash\mathbb{L}
, and
\rho|_{D}\geq 1
. The construction of this smoothing is rather technical, so we only recall its most important properties and we refer the reader to
[
BSV22
]
for proofs.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
BSV22
, Lemma 4.14]

There exist smooth functions
\nu_{i}:M\backslash\mathbb{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
for

, such that
\nu_{i}
is supported in
UD_{i}^{\max}
and for all
p\in M

(2.29)

d_{p}\rho=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nu_{i}(p)d_{p}r_{i}^{\max}.

Moreover,
d\nu_{i}(X_{r_{j}})=0
for all

.

The last property in the Lemma above ensures that
\nu_{i}
is constant along the Hamiltonian orbits of
\rho
. More generally, let
h:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function which is constant near

. Then

\nu^{h}\vcentcolon=(\nu_{1}^{h},\dots,\nu_{N}^{h}):M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}

(2.30)

(2.31)

\Phi^{h(\rho)}_{1}(p)=-\nu^{h}(p)\cdot p,

where
\nu^{h}(p)
is projected to

and it acts on

Corollary 2.2
.

[
BSV22

The

correspond to points

such that for all

(2.32)

\nu_{i}^{h}(p)\in\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{or}\quad p\in D_{i}.

(2.39)

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(\epsilon_{n})_{n}
be a sequence of positive real numbers. There is a sequence

3.
Pseudo-holomorphic curve theory

In this section we introduce the moduli spaces of pseudoholomorphic curves which we use to construct the Maurer–Cartan element.

3.1.
Rational curves with aligned framings

Let
\mathcal{R}_{0,1+d}
be the Deligne–Mumford space of smooth genus
0
curves with
1+d
marked points, and
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0,1+d}
its compactification. A

framing
on a curve
(C,z_{0},\dots,z_{d})\in\mathcal{R}_{0,1+d}
is the choice of a tangent direction
\theta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}(T_{z_{i}}C)
for each one of its marked points. The framing is said to be
aligned
if for each
i=1,\dots,d
, there is an isomorphism
\phi:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that
\phi(z_{0})=\infty
,
\phi(z_{i})=0
, and both
\phi_{*}(\theta_{0})
and
\phi_{*}(\theta_{i})
point along the positive real direction. The moduli space of smooth genus
0
curves with
1+d
marked points and aligned framings is

(3.1)

\mathcal{R}^{\text{al}}_{d}=\text{Conf}_{d}(\mathbb{C})/\text{Aff}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{R}_{>0}),

where
\text{Aff}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{R}_{>0})=\{z\mapsto az+b\ |\ a\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\ \text{and}\ b\in\mathbb{C}\}
.

The moduli space
\mathcal{R}^{\text{al}}_{d}
admits a Deligne–Mumford type compactification
\overline{\mathcal{R}}^{\text{al}}_{d}
which is a smooth manifold with corners. It is an
S^{1}
-bundle over the real blowup of the Deligne–Mumford space
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0,1+d}
over the normal crossings divisor
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0,1+d}\backslash\mathcal{R}_{0,1+d}
, see
[
BEAS24
, §2]
.

Given a map

, we can also construct a moduli space

of smooth genus
0
curves with aligned framings and
\mathbf{p}
-flavors. The
\mathbf{p}
-flavor datum is a map

such that for each

,

(3.2)

\psi(f)(z_{0})=\infty,\quad\psi(f)(z_{\mathbf{p}(f)})=0,

and

points along the positive real direction. The parametrization

is called a sprinkle for the marked point

A set of weights for a curve

is a tuple of

non-negative

such that

(3.3)

n_{0}=n_{1}+\dots+n_{d}+\lvert F\rvert.

The moduli space of
\mathbf{p}
-flavored smooth genus
0
curves with aligned framings in weight

. It admits a compactification

modelled on semi-stable

[

]
, and this compactification is a smooth manifold with corners.

We will call a marked point

if it has weight
0
and carries no sprinkle; these points play a special role in our constructions, analogous to
[

, Section 7]
to which we refer for a more detailed account of an analogous setup.
By forgetting all forgettable marked points, the triple

reduces to a triple
(d^{\text{red}},\mathbf{p}^{\text{red}},\mathbf{n}^{\text{red}})
.
Moreover, we have a forgetful map

(3.4)

\overline{\mathcal{R}}^{\text{al}}_{d,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{n}}\xrightarrow{\text{forgetful}}\overline{\mathcal{R}}^{\text{al}}_{d^{\text{red}},\mathbf{p}^{\text{red}},\mathbf{n}^{\text{red}}}.

This is well-defined unless

,

We must treat this special case separately throughout our constructions.

Example 3.1
.

The framed cap

Recall that a positive/negative cylindrical end for a curve

is a holomorphic embedding

of a positive/negative semi-infinite cylinder

3.2.
Coherent perturbation data

A perturbation datum for

consisting of a Hamiltonian perturbation and an almost complex structure, both of which are domain-dependent on the associated Riemann surface

. Suppose now that we have a nodal curve

glued at the points

\mathscr{F}_{r_{\pm}}
for each component

for

as follows:

-

If
z_{+}
has positive weight, then
\mathscr{F}_{r}
agrees with
\mathscr{F}_{r_{\pm}}
on the component

.

-

If
z_{+}
has weight
0
, then
\mathscr{F}_{r}
agrees with
\mathscr{F}_{r_{+}}
on the component
r_{+}
, but it equals
(0,J_{r_{-}}|_{z_{-}})
on the component

This construction easily generalizes to nodal curves

which have multiple components (compare the
(Constant on spheres)
condition on perturbation data in
[
PS23a
, Definition 5.4]

In this section, we construct perturbation data varying smoothly in

satisfying the following conditions:

(Consistency)
If
r_{1},\dots,r_{c}
are the irreducible components of
r
, then
\mathscr{F}_{r}
is the perturbation datum induced by
(\mathscr{F}_{r_{i}})_{i=1}^{c}
as described above.

(Forgetfulness)
The perturbation data
\mathscr{F}_{r}
are constant along the fibers of the forgetful map (
3.4
), whenever the latter is well-defined; in the case where forgetting all forgettable marked points gives a Floer cylinder, the perturbation data coincide with the Floer data, and in particular are independent of the position of the forgettable marked points.

(Equivariance)
The perturbation data
\mathscr{F}_{r}
are
\text{Sym}(d,\mathbf{p})
-invariant.

We emphasize that the requirement of
(Consistency)
is slightly different from the corresponding requirement described in
[

, §9]
(necessarily so, as the latter would be incompatible with
(Forgetfulness)
). Indeed, we do not always require compatibility of perturbation data with gluing operations along cylindrical ends near the boundary
\partial\overline{\mathcal{R}}^{\text{al}}_{d,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{n}}

We do not specify perturbation data on the moduli spaces where all the weights
n_{i}
are
0
; these moduli spaces do not appear in our construction (we deal with sphere bubbles in a different way: as we have already seen, when a sphere bubble develops along an edge with weight zero, it is equipped with an almost complex structure which is constant over the sphere, but this constant value may depend on where the sphere bubble was attached).

is defined by

(4.4)

A^{SH}_{H_{n}}(\gamma)=-\int_{S^{1}}\gamma^{*}\theta+\int_{S^{1}}H_{n}(\gamma(t))dt.

It is related to our previous action functional (
2.3
) by the formula

(4.5)

A^{SH}_{H_{n}}(\gamma)=A_{H_{n}}(\gamma,u)+\kappa\cdot u\cdot D^{\bm{\lambda}}.

In
[
BSV22
, §1.4]
, the authors introduce a
fractional cap
which is written formally as

(4.6)

u_{in}=u-u\cdot D^{\bm{\lambda}},

where
u
is any cap for
\gamma
in
M
. In terms of the fractional cap, we have

(4.7)

\lvert\gamma\rvert_{SH}=\lvert(\gamma,u_{in})\rvert\quad\text{and}\quad A^{SH}_{H_{n}}(\gamma)=A_{H_{n}}(\gamma,u_{in}).

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have a continuation chain map

(4.8)

c:CF^{*}(X_{\sigma},H_{n})\rightarrow CF^{*}(X_{\sigma},H_{n+1}).

The homotopy direct limit of continuation maps is the chain complex

(4.9)

SC^{*}(X_{\sigma},\mathbf{H})=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}CF^{*}(X_{\sigma},H_{n})[t],

where
t
is a formal variable of degree
-1
such that
t^{2}=0
. It is equipped with the differential

(4.10)

d^{SC}(\gamma+t\gamma^{\prime})=(-1)^{\lvert\gamma\rvert}d\gamma+(-1)^{\lvert\gamma^{\prime}\rvert}(td\gamma^{\prime}+c(\gamma^{\prime})-\gamma^{\prime}).

The cohomology of this complex is called symplectic cohomology and it is denoted by
SH^{*}(X_{\sigma})
. It only depends on the deformation type of the Liouville domain
X_{\sigma}
.

Remark 4.1
.

When the weights
\lambda_{j}
are not integers, but only rational numbers, the degree
\lvert\gamma\rvert_{SH}
may not be an integer, so we need to explain the meaning of the sign
(-1)^{\lvert\gamma\rvert}
. In this case we actually equip our complex with a
(\mathbb{Q}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2)
-grading, where the
\mathbb{Q}
-component is as defined above, and the
\mathbb{Z}/2
-component is the parity of the
\mathbb{Z}
-grading defined using any cap (which is independent of the choice of cap by (
2.6
)). Then if
\lvert\gamma\rvert=(q,\sigma)
, we define
(-1)^{\lvert\gamma\rvert}:=(-1)^{\sigma}
.

4.2.
Partial
L_{\infty}
operations

Consider a triplet
(d,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{n})
, and a tuple
\mathbf{x}=(x_{0},\dots,x_{d})
such that for all
i\in\{0,\dots,d\}
:

-

If
i\in Z^{c}(\mathbf{n})
, then
x_{i}
is an
SH
-orbit of
H_{n_{i}}
.

-

If
i\in Z(\mathbf{n})
, then
x_{i}=x^{D_{j_{i}}}
is a label for one of the components of
D
.

We introduce notation

\mathcal{M}^{\text{al}}_{d,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x})\vcentcolon=\left\{(r,u)\in\mathcal{M}^{\text{al}}_{d,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\ \bigg|\ [u]\cdot D=\lvert Z(\mathbf{n})\rvert\right\},

where
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
is obtained from
\mathbf{x}
by removing all
x_{i}
with
i\in Z(\mathbf{n})
, and the tangency data for such
i
are given by
m_{ik}=\delta_{k,j_{i}}
.

By Lemma
3.4
,
\mathcal{M}^{\text{al}}_{d,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x})
is Fredholm regular of dimension

(4.11)

\dim\mathcal{M}^{\text{al}}_{d,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x})=\dim\mathcal{R}^{\text{al}}_{d,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{n}}+\lvert x_{0}\rvert-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lvert x_{i}\rvert.

In our previous work on compactness, we used formal symbols
x_{e}
associated to inputs or nodes of weight zero, and these were given index
2
and action
0
. Morally, these generators can be thought of as constant orbits equipped with constant caps. In (
4.11
) and afterwards however, we give the analogous generators
x^{D_{j}}
their
SH
-index and
SH
-action, which are

(4.12)

\lvert x^{D_{j}}\rvert=2-\lambda_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad A(x^{D_{j}})=-\kappa\lambda_{j}.

Morally, this corresponds to equipping these constant orbits with their fractional cap
u_{in}
.

Each isolated element
\mathbf{u}=(r,u)\in\mathcal{M}^{\text{al}}_{d,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x})
determines a linear isomorphism

(4.13)

\lvert o_{\mathbf{u}}\rvert:t^{i_{1}}\lvert o_{x_{1}}\rvert\otimes\dots\otimes t^{i_{d}}\lvert o_{x_{d}}\rvert\rightarrow\lvert o_{x_{0}}\rvert\ [3-2d],

where
i_{j}=\lvert\mathbf{p}^{-1}(j)\rvert
for each
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
(cf.
[
BEAS24
, Section 6]
).
We remark that for the formal generators
x^{D_{j}}
, we define
\lvert o_{x^{D_{j}}}\rvert_{\mathbf{k}}\vcentcolon=\mathbf{k}
to be a trivial line.

By Lemma
3.6
, Lemma
3.8
, and Remark
3.7
, the moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}^{\text{al}}_{d,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x})
are compact in dimension
0
, provided that

(4.14)

\mathscr{P}(x_{j})>0\quad\text{ for all }j\in Z^{c}(\mathbf{n}).

Therefore, we can add up the contributions of (
4.13
) to produce maps

(4.15)

\tilde{\ell}^{d}_{0}:\mathfrak{g}_{>0}^{\otimes d}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathscr{P}_{>0}CF^{*}(X_{\sigma},H_{n}),

where for any real
a\in\mathbb{R}
,

(4.16)

\mathfrak{g}_{>a}=\mathscr{P}_{>a}SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{N}\mathbf{k}[t]\cdot\lvert o_{x^{D_{j}}}\rvert_{\mathbf{k}}.

Remark 4.2
.

Recall that the moduli space is undefined in the case that all inputs have weight
0
and no sprinkle.
In this case, we define the output to be
0
:

\tilde{\ell}^{d}\left(x^{D_{j_{1}}},\ldots,x^{D_{j_{d}}}\right)=0.

Morally, this is the correct definition because these generators correspond to constant orbits, on which the loop rotation action is trivial and hence the
L_{\infty}
operations vanish; but we will see the precise justification in Lemma
4.3
below.

These maps have unique
\partial_{t}
-linear extensions

(4.17)

\tilde{\ell}^{d}:\mathfrak{g}_{>0}^{\otimes d}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{>0}.

See
[
BEAS24
]
for more details on the construction of
\tilde{\ell}^{d}
.

Finally, we set

(4.18)

\ell^{d}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=\begin{cases}\tilde{\ell}^{d}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})&if $d\geq 2$\\
\tilde{\ell}^{1}(x_{1})+(-1)^{\lvert x_{1}\rvert}\partial_{t}x_{1}&if $d=1$.\end{cases}

We note that the outputs of the operations
\ell^{d}
are always linear combinations of
1
-periodic orbits, except when
d=1
.

Lemma 4.3
.

The
L_{\infty}
equations

(4.19)

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{\sigma\in\text{Unsh}(d,j)}(-1)^{\epsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})}\ell^{d-j+1}(\ell^{j}(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(j)}),x_{\sigma(j+1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(d)})=0

hold whenever
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\in\mathfrak{g}_{>1}
.
Here
\text{Unsh}(d,j)
is the subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
of permutations of
\{1,\dots,d\}
which satisfy

(4.20)

\sigma(1)<\dots<\sigma(j)\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma(j+1)<\dots<\sigma(d),

and

(4.21)

\epsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
\sigma(i)>\sigma(j)\end{subarray}}\lvert x_{i}\rvert\cdot\lvert x_{j}\rvert.

Proof.

The argument is as in
[
BEAS24
]
, using the compactness results of Lemma
3.6
and Lemma
3.8
.
The key novelty, compared with the argument in
op. cit.
, concerns boundary components where a sphere bubble carrying several marked points of weight zero and no sprinkle develops.
Such boundary components do not appear in the boundary of a
1
-dimensional moduli space by Lemma
3.8
; this justifies the definition given in Remark
4.2
, as it makes the corresponding terms in (
4.19
) vanish.
∎

We emphasize that
(\mathfrak{g}_{>1},(\ell^{d})_{d\geq 1})
is not an
L_{\infty}
algebra because it is not preserved by
\ell^{1}
. Instead, we have
\ell^{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{>1})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{>0}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

The operations
\ell^{d}
satisfy the following property

(4.22)

\mathscr{P}(\ell^{d}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}))\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathscr{P}(x_{i})+2d-3.

Proof.

Indeed, if a
0
-dimensional moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\text{al}}_{d,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x})
is non-empty, we must have

\displaystyle 2d-3+\lvert F\rvert+\lvert x_{0}\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lvert x_{i}\rvert

\displaystyle-(C+\kappa^{-1}C_{\mathbf{n}})\lvert F\rvert+Cn_{0}+\kappa^{-1}A(x_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d}Cn_{i}+\kappa^{-1}A(x_{i}).

We subtract the first equation from the second inequality. The result is

(4.23)

\mathscr{P}(x_{0})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathscr{P}(x_{i})+2d-3+(1+C+\kappa^{-1}C_{\mathbf{n}})\lvert F\rvert.

The lemma follows from the fact that
1+C+\kappa^{-1}C_{\mathbf{n}}>0
.
∎

The operations
(\ell^{d})
are closely related to the
L_{\infty}
structure on symplectic cohomology

(4.24)

\ell_{SC}^{d}:SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})^{\otimes d}\rightarrow SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})[3-2d]

which we constructed in
[
BEAS24
]
:

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose that we have inputs
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\in\mathscr{P}_{>0}SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})
. Then,

(4.25)

\ell_{SC}^{d}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=\begin{cases}\ell^{d}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\quad&\text{if }d\geq 2\\
\ell^{1}(x_{1})\quad&\text{if }d=1\text{ and }\mathscr{P}(x_{1})>1.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
x_{0}\in\mathscr{P}_{>0}SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})
, then we have

\langle\ell^{d}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}),x_{0}\rangle=\langle\ell_{SC}^{d}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}),x_{0}\rangle

as a direct consequence of the integrated maximum principle of Lemma
3.3
.
If
x_{0}\notin\mathscr{P}_{>0}SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})
, then
\langle\ell^{d}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}),x_{0}\rangle=0
by definition.
On the other hand, if
\langle\ell_{SC}^{d}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}),x_{0}\rangle\neq 0
and either
d\geq 2
or
d=1
and
\mathscr{P}(x_{1})>1
, then
\mathscr{P}(x_{0})>0
by the analogue of Lemma
4.4
for
\ell^{d}_{SC}
.
∎

4.3.
Maurer–Cartan theory

Let
(R,Q_{\geq\bullet})
be a graded filtered commutative
\mathbf{k}
-algebra. This means that
R
has a decomposition into pure degree components

(4.26)

R=\bigoplus_{a\in\mathbb{Q}}R_{a},\quad R_{a}\cdot R_{b}\subseteq R_{a+b}.

Moreover, we have a decreasing collection of
\mathbf{k}
-subspaces
Q_{\geq p}R\subseteq R
for each
p\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(4.27)

Q_{\geq p_{1}}R\cdot Q_{\geq p_{2}}R\subseteq Q_{\geq p_{1}+p_{2}}R.

We assume that the
Q
-filtration is exhaustive and bounded below.

For each
\mathbf{k}
-vector space
V
, the tensor product
V\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}R
inherits a filtration

(4.28)

Q_{\geq p}(V\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}R)\vcentcolon=V\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}Q_{\geq p}R.

The completion with respect to this filtration is denoted
\overline{V\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}R}^{Q}
.

If
V
is a graded vector space over
\mathbf{k}
, we define the degreewise completed tensor product as the graded
R
-module

(4.29)

V\widehat{\otimes}R=\bigoplus_{a\in\mathbb{Q}}\overline{(V\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}R)_{a}}^{Q}.

Example 4.6
.

Let
R=\mathbf{k}[q]
be a polynomial ring such that
\lvert q\rvert=1
, and let
V
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space. Then,

(4.30)

(V\widehat{\otimes}R)_{i}=\left\{\sum_{k=-i}^{\infty}v_{-k}q^{i+k}\ \bigg|\ v_{-k}\in V_{-k}\right\}.

In particular, if
V
is concentrated in degrees above some constant
c\in\mathbb{Z}
, we simply have
V\widehat{\otimes}R=V\otimes R
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\ell^{*})
be an
L_{\infty}
algebra over
\mathbf{k}
. We linearly extend the
L_{\infty}
operations on
\mathfrak{g}
to obtain an
L_{\infty}
structure on
\mathfrak{g}^{R}=\mathfrak{g}\widehat{\otimes}R
. Define the Maurer–Cartan set

(4.31)

MC(\mathfrak{g},R)=\left\{\alpha\in Q_{\geq 1}\mathfrak{g}^{R}\ |\ \deg(\alpha)=2\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{F}(\alpha)=0\right\},

where
\mathcal{F}(\alpha)\in\mathfrak{g}^{R}
is the curvature term

(4.32)

\mathcal{F}(\alpha)=\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{d!}\ell^{d}(\alpha,\dots,\alpha).

Elements
\alpha\in MC(\mathfrak{g},R)
are used to define deformed
L_{\infty}
operations,

(4.33)

\ell_{\alpha}^{d}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\ell^{k+d}(\alpha^{\otimes k},x_{1},\dots,x_{d}).

It is straightforward to check that
(\ell^{d}_{\alpha})
satisfy the
L_{\infty}
equations on
\mathfrak{g}^{R}
(see
[
Get09
, Proposition 4.4]
).

Consider the
L_{\infty}
algebra given by

(4.34)

\mathfrak{g}_{\tau}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}\mathbf{k}[\tau,d\tau],

where
\mathbf{k}[\tau,d\tau]
is a graded commutative dg-algebra with generators

(4.35)

\deg(\tau)=0,\quad\deg(d\tau)=1,\quad\text{and}\quad d(\tau)=d\tau.

The
L_{\infty}
operations on
\mathfrak{g}_{\tau}
are given by

(4.36)

\underline{\ell}^{d}(x_{1}\otimes\theta_{1},\dots,x_{d}\otimes\theta_{d})=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\dagger}\ell^{d}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\otimes(\theta_{1}\dotsi\theta_{d})\quad&\text{ if }d\geq 2,\\
\ell^{1}(x_{1})\otimes\theta_{1}+(-1)^{\lvert x_{1}\rvert}x_{1}\otimes d\theta_{1}\quad&\text{ if }d=1,\end{cases}

where the missing sign is
\dagger=\sum_{k<l}\lvert x_{k}\rvert\lvert\theta_{l}\rvert
. The
L_{\infty}
equations for
(\underline{\ell}^{*})
follow from a straightforward computation using the
L_{\infty}
equations for
(\ell^{*})
, together with the following sign identity.

Lemma 4.7
.

With each
\bm{y}=(x_{1}\otimes\theta_{1},\dots,x_{d}\otimes\theta_{d})
, we associate the sign

(4.37)

h(\bm{y})=\sum_{k<l}\lvert x_{k}\rvert\lvert\theta_{l}\rvert\quad\mod 2.

It is related to the Koszul signs defined in (
4.21
) by

(4.38)

\epsilon(\sigma;\bm{y})-\epsilon(\sigma;\mathbf{x})-\epsilon(\sigma;\bm{\theta})=h(\sigma\cdot\bm{y})-h(\bm{y})\quad\mod 2,

where
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
, and
\bm{\theta}=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d})
.

Proof.

Observe that both sides of the equation satisfy the equation

(4.39)

\psi(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2};\mathbf{x})\equiv\psi(\sigma_{1};\sigma_{2}\mathbf{x})+\psi(\sigma_{2};\mathbf{x})\quad\mod 2.

Therefore, it suffices to check that they agree for transpositions of the type
\sigma=(i\ i+1)
. In this case, both sides reduce to
\lvert x_{i}\rvert\lvert\theta_{i+1}\rvert+\lvert x_{i+1}\rvert\lvert\theta_{i}\rvert
.
∎

The
L_{\infty}
algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{\tau},\underline{\ell}^{d})
comes with evaluation maps for any
c\in\mathbf{k}
,

(4.40)

ev_{c}:\mathfrak{g}_{\tau}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}:\quad\quad ev_{c}(\tau)=c\ \text{ and }\ ev_{c}(d\tau)=0,

which are clearly
L_{\infty}
homomorphisms, and in fact quasi-isomorphisms, see Lemma
4.9
below.

Two Maurer–Cartan elements
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}\in MC(\mathfrak{g},R)
are said to be gauge-equivalent if there is a Maurer–Cartan element
\alpha_{\tau}\in MC(\mathfrak{g}_{\tau},R)
such that
ev_{0}(\alpha_{\tau})=\alpha_{0}
and
ev_{1}(\alpha_{\tau})=\alpha_{1}
. We think of
\alpha_{\tau}
as a homotopy between the two Maurer–Cartan elements. With that in mind, the following result can be thought of as a path lifting property for Maurer–Cartan elements.

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose we have
\alpha_{0}\in MC(\mathfrak{g},R)
and
\gamma_{\tau}\in(\mathfrak{g}^{R})^{1}\widehat{\otimes}\mathbf{k}[\tau]
. There is a unique
c_{\tau}\in(\mathfrak{g}^{R})^{2}\widehat{\otimes}\mathbf{k}[\tau]
such that
\alpha_{\tau}=c_{\tau}+\gamma_{\tau}d\tau
is a Maurer–Cartan element, and
ev_{0}(\alpha_{\tau})=\alpha_{0}
.

Proof.

The Maurer–Cartan equation for
\alpha_{\tau}
in
\mathfrak{g}^{R}_{\tau}
decomposes into

(4.41)

\displaystyle\frac{dc_{\tau}}{d\tau}=-\sum_{d\geq 0}\frac{1}{d!}\ell^{1+d}(\gamma_{\tau},c_{\tau},\dotsi,c_{\tau}),\quad c_{0}=\alpha_{0}.

(4.42)

\displaystyle\sum_{d\geq 1}\frac{1}{d!}\ell^{d}(c_{\tau},\dotsi,c_{\tau})=0.

In the equations above, we clarify that unlike
\underline{\ell}^{1}(c_{\tau})
, the expression
\ell^{1}(c_{\tau})
is an extension of
\ell^{1}
to
\mathfrak{g}\widehat{\otimes}\mathbf{k}[\tau]
which is linear in
\tau
.

The ODE (
4.41
) can be solved order-by-order in
\tau
, and it remains to check that the solution satisfies (
4.42
). Indeed,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{F}(c_{\tau})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{d,e\geq 0}\frac{1}{d!e!}\ell^{1+d}(\ell^{1+e}(\gamma_{\tau},c_{\tau},\dots,c_{\tau}),c_{\tau},\dots,c_{\tau})

\displaystyle=\sum_{d,e\geq 0}\ell^{1+d}(\ell^{1+e}(c_{\tau},\dots,c_{\tau}),\gamma_{\tau},c_{\tau},\dots,c_{\tau})\quad\quad(L_{\infty}-\text{equations})

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\geq 0}\frac{1}{d!}\ell^{1+d}(\mathcal{F}(c_{\tau}),\gamma_{\tau},c_{\tau},\dots,c_{\tau}).

This is a first-order ODE in
\tau
with initial condition
\mathcal{F}(c_{0})=0
. It follows that
\mathcal{F}(c_{\tau})
vanishes identically as desired.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

If
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{1}
are gauge-equivalent, then the deformed
L_{\infty}
algebras
(\mathfrak{g},\ell^{*}_{\alpha_{0}})
and
(\mathfrak{g},\ell^{*}_{\alpha_{1}})
are quasi-isomorphic.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for any
c\in\mathbf{k}
, the evaluation map

(4.43)

ev_{c}:(\mathfrak{g}^{R}_{\tau},\underline{\ell}^{1}_{\alpha_{\tau}})\rightarrow(\mathfrak{g}^{R},\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{c}})

is a quasi-isomorphism. Indeed, the chain complexes in (
4.43
) come with exhaustive and bounded below
Q
-filtrations which are respected by the map
ev_{c}
. Because
\alpha_{\tau}\in Q_{\geq 1}\mathfrak{g}^{R}_{\tau}
, the induced map at the
E_{1}
-page is

(4.44)

(Gr(\mathfrak{g}^{R}),\ell^{1})\otimes(\mathbf{k}[\tau,d\tau],d)\xrightarrow{\text{id}\otimes ev_{c}}(Gr(\mathfrak{g}^{R}),\ell^{1})\otimes(\mathbf{k},0),

where
Gr(\mathfrak{g}^{R})
is the associated graded of
\mathfrak{g}^{R}
with respect to the
Q
-filtration. The latter is a quasi-isomorphism because
ev_{c}:(\mathbf{k}[\tau,d\tau],d)\rightarrow(\mathbf{k},0)
is a quasi-isomorphism. By classical spectral sequence convergence and comparison theorems (see
[
Wei94
, Theorems 5.2.12 and 5.5.1]
), we deduce that (
4.43
) is a quasi-isomorphism.
∎

4.4.
Maurer–Cartan construction

We now carry out the constructions of the previous section in our geometric setup. The (partial)
L_{\infty}
algebra of interest is
\mathfrak{g}_{>0}
as defined in (
4.16
), and we study
L_{\infty}
deformations over the filtered
\mathbf{k}
-algebra from the Introduction:

\displaystyle R^{+}=\mathbf{k}[q_{1},\dots,q_{N}],\quad\deg(q_{j})=\lambda_{j}.

\displaystyle Q_{\geq p}R^{+}=\bigoplus_{i\geq p}R^{+}_{i},\quad p\in\mathbb{Z}.

The partial
L_{\infty}
algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{R^{+}}_{>0}
has a tautological Maurer–Cartan element

(4.45)

\alpha_{0}=\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{D}\vcentcolon=\sum_{j=1}^{N}q_{j}x^{D_{j}}

(it satisfies the Maurer–Cartan equation because all terms vanish, see Remark
4.2
).

Following the proof of Lemma
4.8
, we can homotope
\alpha_{0}
through the constant path
\gamma_{\tau}=t\cdot\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{D}
by solving the ODE

(4.46)

c_{0}=\alpha_{0},\quad\frac{d}{d\tau}c_{\tau}=\sum_{d\geq 0}\frac{1}{d!}\ell^{1+d}(\gamma,c_{\tau},\dots,c_{\tau}).

This ODE can be solved order-by-order in
\tau
. However, since
\mathfrak{g}_{>0}
is not exactly an
L_{\infty}
algebra, more care is neeeded in checking that
\alpha_{\tau}=c_{\tau}+\gamma_{\tau}d\tau
satisfies the Maurer–Cartan equations for
(\mathfrak{g}^{R^{+}}_{\tau,>0},\underline{\ell}^{*})
.

Lemma 4.10
.

We have
c_{\tau}\in\mathfrak{g}^{R^{+}}_{\tau,>1}
.

Proof.

We start by looking at the first order term
c_{1}
from the power series expansion
c_{\tau}=c_{0}+c_{1}\tau+\dotsi
of
c_{\tau}
. It is computed from the formula

(4.47)

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\geq 0}\frac{1}{d!}\ell^{1+d}(\gamma,\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{D},\dots,\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{D})

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{D}+\sum_{d\geq 0}\frac{1}{d!}\tilde{\ell}^{1+d}(\gamma,\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{D},\dots,\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{D}).

Let
x_{0}
be an orbit contributing to
\tilde{\ell}^{1+d}(\gamma,\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{D},\dots,\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{D})
. By (
4.23
) and (
3.14
),

(4.48)

\mathscr{P}(x_{0})\geq-1-2d+2(1+d)-3+(1+C+\kappa^{-1}C_{(1,0,\dots,0)})>1.

It follows that
c_{1}\in\mathfrak{g}^{R^{+}}_{\tau,>1}
. For higher order terms, we proceed by induction. First, note that because of (
4.47
), we can write

(4.49)

c_{\tau}=(1-\tau)\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{D}+\overline{c}_{\tau},

where
\overline{c}_{\tau}\in\mathscr{P}_{>0}SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})\otimes\mathbf{k}[\tau]\widehat{\otimes}R^{+}
is a combination of
1
-periodic orbits, and a solution to the ODE

(4.50)

\overline{c}_{0}=0,\quad\frac{d}{d\tau}\overline{c}_{\tau}=\sum_{d\geq 0}\frac{1}{d!}\tilde{\ell}^{1+d}(\gamma,(1-\tau)\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{D}+\overline{c}_{\tau},\dots,(1-\tau)\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{D}+\overline{c}_{\tau}).

Let
i>1
be an integer. Then
c_{i}=\overline{c}_{i}
is computed from operations

(4.51)

\tilde{\ell}^{1+j+k}(\gamma,c_{i_{1}},\dots,c_{i_{j}},\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{D},\dots,\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{D}),

where
i_{1},\dots,i_{j}<i
. If
x_{0}
is an output contributing to this operation, then

(4.52)

\mathscr{P}(x_{0})>-1+j+(-2)\cdot k+2(1+j+k)-3=3j-2.

Therefore,
\mathscr{P}(x_{0})>1
unless
j=0
. But the case of
j=0
follows by the same argument (using (
4.23
) and (
3.14
)) presented above for
c_{1}
.
∎

Corollary 4.11
.

The element
\alpha_{\tau}=c_{\tau}+\gamma_{\tau}d\tau\in\mathfrak{g}^{R^{+}}_{\tau,>1}
satisfies the Maurer–Cartan equation. In particular,
\alpha_{1}\in SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})\widehat{\otimes}R^{+}
satisfies the Maurer–Cartan equation.
The Maurer–Cartan element
\alpha
from Construction
A
is defined to be
\alpha_{1}
.

Proof.

Since
\gamma_{\tau},c_{\tau}\in\mathfrak{g}^{R^{+}}_{>1}\widehat{\otimes}\mathbf{k}[\tau]
, the
L_{\infty}
equations needed for the proof of the Maurer–Cartan equation in Lemma
4.8
hold, so that the same argument works here as well.
∎

4.5.
Maurer–Cartan deformation

For any
1
-periodic orbit
x
, define

(4.53)

\mathcal{F}(x)=C\cdot n_{x}+\kappa^{-1}A(x).

It was shown in
[
BSV22
, Proposition 1.14]
that the inclusions
\mathcal{F}^{X}_{\geq p}\subseteq SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})
are quasi-isomorphisms, where

(4.54)

\mathcal{F}^{X}_{\geq p}=\mathcal{F}_{\geq p}SC^{*}(X_{\sigma}),\quad p\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
\alpha_{\tau}\in\mathfrak{g}^{R^{+}}_{\tau,>1}
be the Maurer–Cartan element previously constructed.

Lemma 4.12
.

The subspace
\mathcal{F}_{\geq p}\mathfrak{g}^{R^{+}}_{\tau,>0}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{R^{+}}_{\tau,>0}
is preserved by
\underline{\ell}^{1}_{\alpha_{\tau}}
.

Proof.

Let
x
be a
1
-periodic orbit such that
\mathscr{P}(x)>0
. We have

(4.55)

\underline{\ell}^{1}_{\alpha_{\tau}}(x)=\sum_{d\geq 0}\frac{1}{d!}\underline{\ell}^{1+d}(x,\alpha_{\tau},\dotsi,\alpha_{\tau}).

If
x_{0}
is an orbit which contributes to the term
\underline{\ell}^{1+d}(x,\alpha_{\tau},\dotsi,\alpha_{\tau})
, then

\displaystyle C\cdot n_{x_{0}}+\kappa^{-1}A(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\mathscr{P}(x_{0})+\lvert x_{0}\rvert

\displaystyle\geq\mathscr{P}(x)-2d+2(1+d)-3+\lvert x\rvert+1

\displaystyle=C\cdot n_{x}+\kappa^{-1}A(x).

∎

For a graded complex
C^{\bullet}
, we define its degree-truncation at
p\in\mathbb{R}
,

(4.56)

\sigma_{<p}C^{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{i<p}C^{i}.

It is equivalent to the quotient complex
C^{\bullet}/C^{\bullet\geq p}
and has the property that

(4.57)

H^{j}(\sigma_{<p}C^{\bullet})=H^{j}(C^{\bullet})\quad\text{for all}\ j<p-1.

Lemma 4.13
.

We have an isomorphism

(4.58)

H^{j}(\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{X}_{\geq p}\widehat{\otimes}R^{+},\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{1}})\cong H^{j}(\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{X}_{\geq p}\widehat{\otimes}R^{+},\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{0}})

for all
p\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Using the
L_{\infty}
relations on
\mathfrak{g}_{>1}
, we deduce that
\underline{\ell}^{1}_{\alpha_{\tau}}
is a differential when restricted to
\sigma_{<p-1}(\mathcal{F}^{X}_{\geq p}\widehat{\otimes}R^{+})\widehat{\otimes}\mathbf{k}[\tau,d\tau]
. As a consequence, the result follows by the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
4.9
.
∎

Lemma 4.14
.

For any
j<p-2
, we have an isomorphism

(4.59)

H^{j}(\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{X}_{\geq p}\widehat{\otimes}R^{+},\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{1}})\cong H^{j}(SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})\widehat{\otimes}R^{+},\ell_{\alpha_{1}}^{1}).

Proof.

Using the isomorphism in (
4.57
), it is enough to show that the inclusion

(4.60)

(\mathcal{F}^{X}_{\geq p}\widehat{\otimes}R^{+},\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{1}})\rightarrow(SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})\widehat{\otimes}R^{+},\ell_{\alpha_{1}}^{1})

is a quasi-isomorphism. This is true by a spectral sequence comparison argument similar to the proof of Lemma
4.9
. Indeed, using the
Q
-filtration on both sides, together with the fact that
\alpha_{1}\in Q_{\geq 1}\mathfrak{g}_{>1}^{R^{+}}
, the induced map on the
E_{1}
-page is the inclusion

(4.61)

(\mathcal{F}^{X}_{\geq p}\otimes Q_{\geq j}R^{+}/Q_{\geq j+1}R^{+},\ell^{1})\rightarrow(SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})\otimes Q_{\geq j}R^{+}/Q_{\geq j+1}R^{+},\ell^{1}),

which is an isomorphism by
[
BSV22
, Proposition 1.14]
.
∎

4.6.
Recovering Quantum Cohomology

In this section we complete the proof of Theorem
C
.

For this we need to pass to the localization
R
of
R^{+}
at the product
q_{1}\dotsi q_{N}
,

(4.62)

R=\mathbf{k}[q_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,q_{N}^{\pm 1}].

It has the structure of an
R^{+}
-algebra by the localization homomorphism, and of a
\Lambda^{\text{gr}}_{\omega}
-algebra by the ring homomorphism

(4.63)

\Lambda^{\text{gr}}_{\omega}\rightarrow R,\quad e^{A}\mapsto\mathbf{q}^{A\cdot\bm{D}}\vcentcolon=q_{1}^{A\cdot D_{1}}\dotsi q_{N}^{A\cdot D_{N}}.

Note that the filtrations on
R
and
\Lambda^{\text{gr}}_{\omega}
are related,

(4.64)

A_{\omega}(e^{A})=2\kappa\lvert\mathbf{q}^{A\cdot\bm{D}}\rvert.

By
[
BSV22
, Proposition 1.15]
, Hypothesis
B
implies that
\mathfrak{g}_{>0}
is concentrated in non-negative degrees, and therefore that

(4.65)

SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})\widehat{\otimes}R=SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})\otimes R.

As
R
is a flat
R^{+}
-module, the isomorphisms of Lemmas
4.13
and
4.14
hold true over
R
:

(4.66)

\displaystyle H^{j}(\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{X}_{\geq p}\otimes R,\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{1}})

\displaystyle\cong H^{j}(\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{X}_{\geq p}\otimes R,\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{0}}).

(4.67)

\displaystyle H^{j}(\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{X}_{\geq p}\otimes R,\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{1}})

\displaystyle\cong H^{j}(SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})\otimes R,\ell_{\alpha_{1}}^{1}).

Consider the telescoping complex

(4.68)

QC^{*}(M;R)=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}CF^{*}(M,H_{n};R)[t].

It is equipped with a differential
\tilde{d}^{QC}
which counts Floer trajectories (
2.9
) and continuation maps using a generic
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure
(\tilde{J}_{n})_{n}
, and perturbation data for the continuation maps, on
M
. We emphasize that we require
all
Floer trajectories and continuation maps for
(\tilde{J}_{n})_{n}
, and the corresponding perturbation data for the continuation maps, to be Fredholm regular. This is in contrast to the family
(J_{n})_{n}
and perturbation data from §
3.4
, for which we do not have regularity of Floer trajectories and continuation maps between
D
-orbits. Nonetheless, counts using the family
(J_{n})_{n}
can be used to define a diffential
d^{QC}
on the complexes
\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{M}_{p}
, where

(4.69)

\mathcal{F}^{M}_{p}=\mathcal{F}_{\geq p}QC^{*}(M;R),\quad p\in\mathbb{R}.

Lemma 4.15
.

For any
j<p-2
, we have an isomorphism

(4.70)

H^{j}(\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{M}_{p},d^{QC})\cong H^{j}(\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{M}_{p},\tilde{d}^{QC}).

Proof.

This follows from the usual TQFT style argument explained in
[
Sei08
, §8]
, where one constructs a chain map

(4.71)

(\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{M}_{p},d^{QC})\rightarrow(\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{M}_{p},\tilde{d}^{QC})

from a generic homotopy
J_{s,t}
between
J_{t}
and
\tilde{J}_{t}
. The difficulty in our case is that we do not have transversality for Floer trajectories between
D
-orbits. Instead, the compactness results needed to define (
4.71
) and to show it is a chain map are Lemma
3.6
and Lemma
3.8
.
∎

The complex
(\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{M}_{p},d^{QC})
only involves
1
-periodic orbits in
X_{\sigma}
, but unlike
\ell^{1}=d^{SC}
, the differential
d^{QC}
counts Floer trajectories that interesect the divisor
D
.

Define a morphism of
R
-modules

(4.72)

\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{X}_{\geq p}\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}R\rightarrow\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{M}_{p},\quad\Phi(\gamma)=[\gamma,u]\otimes q_{1}^{u\cdot D_{1}}\dotsi q_{N}^{u\cdot D_{N}}.

It is not difficult to check that the image
\Phi(\gamma)
does not depend on the choice of the cap
u
for
\gamma
. For simplicity of notation, we write
\Phi(\gamma)=[\gamma,u]\otimes\mathbf{q}^{u\cdot\bm{D}}
.

Lemma 4.16
.

The map
\Phi
is an isomorphism of chain complexes

(4.73)

(\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{X}_{\geq p}\otimes R,\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{0}})\rightarrow(\sigma_{<p-1}\mathcal{F}^{M}_{p},d^{QC}).

Proof.

By definition, both chain complexes are built from
1
-periodic orbits in
X_{\sigma}
, so
\Phi
is a vector space isomorphism. It remains to check that for any
1
-periodic orbit
\gamma
in
X_{\sigma}
, we have

(4.74)

\Phi(\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{0}}(\gamma_{1}))=d^{QC}(\Phi(\gamma_{1})).

We explain this in the simpler case when
N=1
; the general case is identical.

Let
u_{1}
be a cap for
\gamma_{1}
, and set
x_{1}=(\gamma_{1},u_{1})
. Recall that

(4.75)

d^{QC}(x_{1})=\sum_{x_{0}\neq x_{1}}\#\mathcal{M}(x_{0},x_{1})x_{0}.

On the other hand,

(4.76)

\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{0}}(\gamma_{1})=\sum_{\gamma_{0}}\sum_{d\geq 0}\frac{q^{d}}{d!}\langle\ell^{1+d}(\gamma_{1},x^{D},\dots,x^{D}),\gamma_{0}\rangle.

To relate the two differentials, notice that by our
(Forgetfulness)
assumption on the choice of perturbation data,
for any cap
u_{1}
for
\gamma_{1}
we have a tautological map

(4.77)

\mathcal{M}^{\text{al}}_{1+d,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{n}}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},x^{D},\dots,x^{D})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}([\gamma_{0},u_{0}],[\gamma_{1},u_{1}]),

where
\mathbf{p}
is empty,
\mathbf{n}=(n_{0},n_{1},0,\dots,0)
, and
u_{0}
is the unique cap for
\gamma_{0}
such that
(u_{1}-u_{0})\cdot D=d
.
Our regularity assumptions on the perturbation data (Lemma
3.4
) ensure that over the
0
-dimensional components of the moduli spaces, the map (
4.77
) is
d!
-to-1, with the fibre over
(r,u)
in bijection with the set of labellings of the
d
intersection points of the curve
u
with
D
by the labels
\{2,\ldots,d+1\}
. It follows that

(4.78)

\frac{1}{d!}\langle\ell^{1+d}(\gamma_{1},x^{D},\dots,x^{D}),\gamma_{0}\rangle=\langle d^{QC}x_{1},x_{0}\rangle,

where
x_{i}=[\gamma_{i},u_{i}]
.
Hence

\displaystyle\langle d^{QC}(x_{1}\otimes q^{u_{1}\cdot D}),x_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{d}}{d!}\langle\ell^{1+d}(\gamma_{1},x_{D},\dots,x_{D}),\gamma_{0}\rangle\otimes q^{-d+u_{1}\cdot D}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{d}}{d!}\langle\ell^{1+d}(\gamma_{1},x_{D},\dots,x_{D}),\gamma_{0}\rangle\otimes q^{u_{0}\cdot D}

\displaystyle=\langle\Phi(\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{0}}(\gamma_{1})),x_{0}\rangle

as required.
∎

By combining the isomorphisms in (
4.66
), (
4.67
), together with the isomorphisms in Lemma
4.15
and Lemma
4.16
, we obtain our main result:

Theorem D
.

We have an isomorphism of
R
-modules

(4.79)

H^{*}(SC^{*}(X_{\sigma})\otimes R,\ell^{1}_{\alpha_{1}})\cong QH^{*}(M,R).

Interior over-penalized enriched Galerkin methods for second order elliptic equations
Jeonghun J. Lee, Omar Ghattas

Abstract
. In this paper we propose a variant of enriched Galerkin methods for second order elliptic equations with over-penalization of interior jump terms. The bilinear form with interior over-penalization gives a non-standard norm which is different from the discrete energy norm in the classical discontinuous Galerkin methods. Nonetheless we prove that optimal a priori error estimates with the standard discrete energy norm can be obtained by combining a priori and a posteriori error analysis techniques. We also show that the interior over-penalization is advantageous for constructing preconditioners robust to mesh refinement by analyzing spectral equivalence of bilinear forms. Numerical results are included to illustrate the convergence and preconditioning results.

Primary: 65N30

1.
Introduction

It is well known that numerical fluxes of Galerkin methods with Lagrange finite elements (CG) do not satisfy local mass conservation.
Since local mass conservation is an important physical principle in numerical simulations,
numerical methods providing locally mass conservative numerical flux with/without postprocessing
have been intensively studied. These include post-processing of the CG methods
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, the mixed method
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
, and a class of discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
), to name a few.

Enriched Galerkin (EG) methods
[
13
]
are proposed to achieve locally mass conservative numerical methods with low computational costs.
Compared to classical DG methods for the primal formulation, EG methods have
much
fewer degrees of freedom and provide a simple local post-processing for reconstruction of locally mass conservative numerical fluxes. For efficient solvers, block diagonal preconditioners based on algebraic multigrid methods have been proposed as efficient preconditioners for EG methods
[
14
]
. However, the preconditioning analysis in this work relies on the full elliptic regularity assumption which requires restrictive conditions on the domain geometry and material coefficients. In fact, numerical experiments show that the block preconditioners in
[
14
]
are not scalable with mesh refinement when the boundary condition is not a pure Neumann boundary condition.

The purpose of this paper is to develop new EG methods that retain the advantages of local mass conservation
while
also providing more robust fast solvers that do not need
restrictive
assumptions on domain geometry and boundary conditions. The key in the development of these new EG methods is to replace the original bilinear form with a new bilinear form that has an
over-penalized
interior jump term. As such, we call them interior
over-penalized
enriched Galerkin methods (
IOP-EG
). The
over-penalization
parameters depend on the mesh sizes, so the
over-penalized
jump term and other terms in the new bilinear form do not have the same scaling property. Because of these incompatible scaling properties, the standard techniques for error analysis are not available for
the
optimal error estimates
of
the new EG methods.

The first main result in this paper is an error analysis with optimal error estimates.
To overcome the difficulty
caused by the bilinear forms with
different
scalings
, we use a medius analysis idea utilizing a posteriori error estimate results for our a priori error estimates.

Through this idea
we can obtain optimal error estimates for
IOP-EG
methods.
The second main result is
the
construction of scalable preconditioners for the
IOP-EG
methods.
More precisely, we propose an abstract form of preconditioners based on appropriate mesh-dependent norms and carry out spectral equivalence analysis for the abstract preconditioners following the framework in
[
15
]
. It turns out that
over-penalization
is crucial for construction of scalable preconditioners via an analysis of spectral equivalence.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce notation and define the
IOP-EG
methods.
In Section
3
, we prove optimal error estimates of the
IOP-EG
methods with a minimal regularity assumption using a medius error analysis.
Finally, numerical results illustrating our theoretical results and concluding remarks will be given in Sections
5
and
6
.

Notations

For a set

.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded polygonal/polyhedral domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with

or
3
.
For a nonnegative real number

,

denotes the standard Sobolev spaces based on the

We use
\mathcal{T}_{h}
to denote a shape-regular triangulation of

.
We also use
\mathcal{E}_{h}
to denote the set of facets in
\mathcal{T}_{h}

-dimensional simplices in the triangulation determined by

. In particular,

and

, respectively.

For

and functions

we define

. For a set

which is a union of facets in

, we define

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{\Gamma}=\sum_{e\subset\Gamma}\langle{f},{g}\rangle_{e}.

This notation is naturally extended to
\left<\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{g}\right>_{\Gamma}
for vector-valued functions
\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{g}\in L^{2}(e;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with the natural inner product on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

For an integer
k\geq 0
and for each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
\mathcal{P}_{k}(T)
is the space of polynomials of degree
\leq k
on
T
, and
\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
denotes the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})=\begin{cases}\left\{q\in H^{1}(\Omega)\;:\;q|_{T}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(T),\;T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\}\quad\text{if }k\geq 1\\
\left\{q\in L^{2}(\Omega)\;:\;q|_{T}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(T),\;T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\}\quad\text{if }k=0\\
\end{cases}.

We use
X\lesssim Y
to denote an inequality
X\leq CY
with a constant
C
which depends only on the shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and polynomial degree

,
and
X\sim Y
stands for
X\lesssim Y
and
Y\lesssim X
.

2.2.
Enriched Galerkin methods with interior
over-penalization

In this subsection we introduce the interior
over-penalized
enriched Galerkin methods.

For
V_{h}^{c}=\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
with
k\geq 1
and
V_{h}^{0}=\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, we define
V_{h}
as the linear space

\displaystyle V_{h}=\{v^{c}+v^{0}\,:\,v^{c}\in V_{h}^{c},v^{0}\in V_{h}^{0}\}.

A function
v\in V_{h}
with
v=v^{c}+v^{0}
is determined by the standard degrees of freedom of
V_{h}^{c}
and
V_{h}^{0}
.
However, the degrees of freedom of
V_{h}
is not the union of the two sets of degrees of freedom, of
V_{h}^{c}
and of
V_{h}^{0}
. In fact, a piecewise polynomial function
v\in V_{h}
may have more than one expression as a sum of elements in
V_{h}^{c}
and
V_{h}^{0}
.
If we use
v=v^{c}+v^{0}
with
v^{c}\in V_{h}^{c}
,
v^{0}\in V_{h}^{0}
to denote an element in
V_{h}
, the zero function can have infinitely many expressions by choosing
v^{c}=-v^{0}=C
for any constant
C
. However, such non-unique expressions are unique up to constant addition. To see it, assume that
v=v^{c}_{1}+v^{0}_{1}=v^{c}_{2}+v^{0}_{2}
. Then
v^{c}_{1}-v^{c}_{2}=v^{0}_{2}-v^{0}_{1}\in V_{h}^{c}\cap V_{h}^{0}
, so
v^{c}_{1}
and
v^{c}_{2}
are the same up to constant addition. So are
v^{0}_{1}
and
v^{0}_{2}
.

We use
\mathbb{V}_{h}
to denote the space
V_{h}^{c}\times V_{h}^{0}
.
Note that one element in
\phi\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
uniquely determines
\phi_{c}\in V_{h}^{c}
and
\phi_{0}\in V_{h}^{0}
.
Let
\mathbb{V}_{h}^{ker}
be the one dimensional subspace
\mathbb{V}_{h}^{ker}:=\{\phi\in\mathbb{V}_{h}\,:\,\phi_{c}=-\phi_{0}\}
in
\mathbb{V}_{h}
.
If we denote the quotient space
\mathbb{V}_{h}/\mathbb{V}_{h}^{ker}
by
\mathbb{V}_{h}^{\perp}
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between
V_{h}
and
\mathbb{V}_{h}^{\perp}
.

To introduce the bilinear form for the enriched Galerkin method, we define the jump and average operators of functions which have well-defined traces on edges/faces in the context of classical discontinuous Galerkin methods.
For
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\partial}

\displaystyle[\![{q}]\!]|_{e}=q|_{e}\boldsymbol{n},\quad\left\{\!\!\left\{{q}\right\}\!\!\right\}|_{e}=q|_{e},\qquad[\![{\boldsymbol{q}}]\!]|_{e}=\boldsymbol{q}|_{e}\cdot\boldsymbol{n},\quad\left\{\!\!\left\{{\boldsymbol{q}}\right\}\!\!\right\}|_{e}=\boldsymbol{q}|_{e}

where
q
and
\boldsymbol{q}
are
{\mathbb{R}}
- and
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
-valued functions such that their traces on
e
are well-defined, and
\boldsymbol{n}
is the outward unit normal vector field on
e
. For
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}
, let
T_{+}
and
T_{-}
be the two elements sharing
e
as
e=\partial T_{+}\cap\partial T_{-}
. If
q
is a scalar function on
T_{+}\cup T_{-}
, then we use
q^{\pm}
and
\nabla q^{\pm}
to denote the restrictions of
q
and
\nabla q
on
T_{+}
and
T_{-}
. We use
\boldsymbol{n}^{\pm}
to denote the unit outward normal vector fields of
T_{\pm}
and we again omit the restriction
|_{e}
if it is clear in context. Then the jumps and the averages of
q
and
\boldsymbol{q}
on
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}
are defined by

\displaystyle[\![{q}]\!]|_{e}

\displaystyle=q^{+}\boldsymbol{n}^{+}+q^{-}\boldsymbol{n}^{-},

\displaystyle\left\{\!\!\left\{{q}\right\}\!\!\right\}|_{e}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(q^{+}+q^{-}),

\displaystyle[\![{\boldsymbol{q}}]\!]|_{e}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{q}^{+}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}^{+}+\boldsymbol{q}^{-}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}^{-},

\displaystyle\left\{\!\!\left\{{\boldsymbol{q}}\right\}\!\!\right\}|_{e}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{q}^{+}+\boldsymbol{q}^{-}).

If
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
is clear in context, we will use
[\![{q}]\!]
instead of
[\![{q}]\!]|_{e}
for the jump of
q
on
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
. The same simplification will apply to other quantities.

We consider a model second order elliptic equation

(1)

\displaystyle-\Delta u=f\qquad\text{ in }\Omega,

(2)

\displaystyle u|_{\Gamma_{D}}=u_{D}\text{ on }\Gamma_{D},\quad\nabla u\cdot\boldsymbol{n}|_{\Gamma_{N}}=u_{N}\text{ on }\Gamma_{N}

where
\Gamma_{D}
,
\Gamma_{N}
are disjoint open subsets of
\partial\Omega
, the boundary of
\Omega
, such that
\overline{\Gamma_{D}}\cup\overline{\Gamma_{N}}=\partial\Omega
.

Let
\gamma>0
be a sufficiently large positive constant. We use
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}
to denote the set
\{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\partial}\,:\,e\subset\Gamma_{D}\}
. For
V:=H^{1}(\Omega)
,
let
a:V\times V_{h}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be

(3)

\displaystyle a(v,w)

\displaystyle=\left(\nabla v,\nabla w\right)+\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}{v},{w}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}

where

\displaystyle\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}{v},{w}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}v,w\right>_{e}

with
h_{e}
, the diameter of
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
.
We also define the bilinear form
a_{h}
on
V_{h}\times V_{h}
as

(4)

\displaystyle a_{h}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\left(\nabla v,\nabla w\right)-\left(\left<\left\{\!\!\left\{{\nabla v}\right\}\!\!\right\},[\![{w}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}+\left<[\![{v}]\!],\left\{\!\!\left\{{\nabla w}\right\}\!\!\right\}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1-\alpha}[\![{v}]\!],[\![{w}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}+\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}[\![{v}]\!],[\![{w}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}

for
\alpha\geq 0
. Here
\nabla
for
v\in V_{h}
is the element-wise gradient operator. If
\alpha=0
, this is the bilinear form of the symmetric interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method (SIPG), which is used in the enriched Galerkin (EG) method in
[
14
]
.
In this paper we are interested in the cases
\alpha\geq 1
such that the interior penalization terms are overly
penalized
. We will call these methods interior
over-penalized
enriched Galerkin methods (
IOP-EG
). For example, if
\alpha=1
, then
a_{h}
is

\displaystyle a_{h}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\left(\nabla v,\nabla w\right)-\left(\left<\left\{\!\!\left\{{\nabla v}\right\}\!\!\right\},[\![{w}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}+\left<[\![{v}]\!],\left\{\!\!\left\{{\nabla w}\right\}\!\!\right\}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-2}[\![{v}]\!],[\![{w}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}+\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}[\![{v}]\!],[\![{w}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}.

In
IOP-EG
methods we seek
u_{h}\in V_{h}
such that

(5)

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v)=\mathcal{F}(v)\qquad\forall v\in V_{h}

for
a_{h}
in (
4
) with

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(v):=(f,v)+\left<u_{N},v\right>_{\Gamma_{N}}+\left<u_{D},\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\nabla v\right>_{\Gamma_{D}}+\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}u_{D},v\right>_{\Gamma_{D}}.

To show the stability of (
5
), let us define a norm on
V_{h}
associated with
a_{h}
as

(7)

\displaystyle\left\|v\right\|_{a_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\left(\nabla v,\nabla v\right)+\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1-\alpha}[\![{v}]\!],[\![{v}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}+\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}[\![{v}]\!],[\![{v}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}.

For later use, we also define

(8)

\displaystyle\left\|v\right\|_{a_{h},0}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\left(\nabla v,\nabla v\right)+\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1-\alpha}[\![{v}]\!],[\![{v}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}},

\displaystyle\left\|v\right\|_{a_{h},\partial}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}[\![{v}]\!],[\![{v}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}.

By the inverse trace inequality and the standard argument in DG methods, it is not difficult to show

\displaystyle|a_{h}(v,w)|\lesssim\left\|v\right\|_{a_{h}}\left\|w\right\|_{a_{h}}

for
v,w\in V_{h}
. We can obtain that

(9)

\displaystyle|\left<\left\{\!\!\left\{{\nabla v}\right\}\!\!\right\},[\![{v}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla v,\nabla v\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}[\![{v}]\!],[\![{v}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

for sufficiently large
\gamma
.
Since
h\leq 1
, if
\gamma
is sufficiently large, then we can use (
9
) to derive a coercivity inequality

(10)

\displaystyle C_{\text{\rm{co}}}\left\|v\right\|_{a_{h}}^{2}\leq a_{h}(v,v)

with
C_{\text{\rm{co}}}>0
independent of
0<h\leq 1
. Therefore (
5
) is stable.

3.
The a priori error estimates

In this section we prove the a priori error analysis of the
IOP-EG
methods.
For the analysis we adopt the idea of
[
17
]
utilizing estimates of the a posteriori error analysis.
As a consequence, we prove optimal a priori error estimates with minimal regularity assumption of exact solutions.

We recall efficiency estimates of the a posteriori error analysis in Nitsche’s method
[
18
,
19
]
.
We take into account only the data oscillation of
f
in the discussion below for simplicity.
For detailed discussion with data oscillations of boundary conditions, we refer to
[
19
]
.
For
v\in V_{h}^{c}
the local efficiency estimates

(11)

\displaystyle h_{T}^{2}\left\|f+\Delta v\right\|_{0,T}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\nabla(u-v),\nabla(u-v))_{0,T}^{2}+(\operatorname{osc}_{T}(f))^{2},

(12)

\displaystyle h_{e}\left\|[\![{\nabla v}]\!]\right\|_{0,e}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},e\subset T}\left((\nabla(u-v),\nabla(u-v))_{0,T}^{2}+(\operatorname{osc}_{T}(f))^{2}\right)

are proved in
[
19
]
with

\displaystyle\operatorname{osc}_{T}(f)

\displaystyle:=h_{T}\left\|f-f_{h}\right\|_{0,T},

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname{osc}(f)

\displaystyle:=\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\operatorname{osc}_{T}(f))^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where
f_{h}
is the
L^{2}
projection of
f
into
V_{h}^{c}
.

Here we give some definitions and results which are necessary in our a priori error analysis.
We will use
V_{h}^{00}
to denote the space of mean-value zero piecewise constant functions, i.e.,

\displaystyle V_{h}^{00}:=\{v\in V_{h}^{0}\;:\;\int_{\Omega}v\,{\rm d}x=0\}.

Suppose that
D
is a union of (closed) simplices in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
\operatorname{int}D
is the interior of
{D}
.
A discrete seminorm
|v|_{H_{h}^{1}(D)}
for element-wise
H^{1}
function
v
is defined as

(13)

\displaystyle|v|_{H_{h}^{1}(D)}^{2}=\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0},e\subset\operatorname{int}D}h_{e}^{-1}\|[\![{v}]\!]\|_{0,e}^{2}.

If
D=\overline{\Omega}
, we simply use
H_{h}^{1}
instead of
H_{h}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
.

For a vertex
z
in the triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
, we define

(14)

\displaystyle M_{z}=\bigcup_{z{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\in}}T}\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\,:\,z\in T\},\quad M_{T}=\bigcup_{z{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\in}}T}M_{z},\quad M_{e}=\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},e\subset\partial T}M_{T}.

Geometrically, these are the union of simplices which become proper neighborhoods whose interiors contain
z
,
T
, and
e
, respectively.
For
v\in V_{h}^{0}
it is known that there is a linear interpolation operator
I_{h}:V_{h}^{0}\rightarrow V_{h}^{c}
such that,

(15)

\displaystyle\left\|v-I_{h}v\right\|_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{T}|v|_{H_{h}^{1}(M_{T})}

\displaystyle\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

(16)

\displaystyle\left\|v-I_{h}v\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{e}^{\frac{1}{2}}|v|_{H_{h}^{1}(M_{e})}

\displaystyle\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D},

(17)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla I_{h}v\right\|_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|v|_{H_{h}^{1}(M_{T})}

\displaystyle\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}

hold, where the implicit constants in these estimates are independent of the mesh sizes (see
[
20
, Theorem 3.1]
).

We define
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{D}
and
\mathcal{E}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{D})
as

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{h}^{D}

\displaystyle:=\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\,:\,\partial T\cap\partial\Omega\subset\Gamma_{D}\},

(19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{D})

\displaystyle:=\{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\,:\,e\subset\partial T,\text{ for some }T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{D}\}.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
v^{0}\in V_{h}^{00}
it holds that

(20)

\displaystyle\left\|v^{0}\right\|_{a_{h},\partial}\leq C\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\{h_{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}\{h_{e}^{\alpha/2}\}\left\|v^{0}\right\|_{a_{h},0}

with
C
depending on the shape regularity, the constants of the trace theorem and the discrete Poincaré inequality.

Proof.

Suppose that
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{D}
and
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}
with
e\subset\partial T
, are fixed. If we set
z\in V_{h}^{00}
as
z=h_{e}^{-1/2}v^{0}
, then, by (
16
),

\displaystyle\left<h_{e}^{-1}v^{0},v^{0}\right>_{e}=\left\|z\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|I_{h}z\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}+Ch_{e}|z|_{H_{h}^{1}(M_{e})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2h_{e}^{-1}\left\|I_{h}v^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}+C|v^{0}|_{H_{h}^{1}(M_{e})}^{2}.

Applying a similar argument for every such pair
(T,e)
,
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{D}
,

(21)

\displaystyle\left\|v^{0}\right\|_{a_{h},\partial}^{2}\leq 2\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\{h_{e}^{-1}\}\gamma\left\|I_{h}v^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{D})}^{2}+C\gamma|v^{0}|_{H_{h}^{1}}^{2}.

By the trace theorem and the triangle inequality,
we have

\displaystyle\left\|I_{h}v^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{D})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|I_{h}v^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\nabla I_{h}v^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|I_{h}v^{0}-v^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|v^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\nabla I_{h}v^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

By a discrete Poincaré inequality (cf.
[
21
, Remark 1.1]
),
\left\|v^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim|v^{0}|_{H_{h}^{1}}
because
v^{0}\in V_{h}^{00}
. Applying this discrete Poincaré inequality, (
15
), and (
17
), to the above inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left\|I_{h}v^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{D})}\lesssim|v^{0}|_{H_{h}^{1}}.

From this, (
21
), and

|v^{0}|_{H_{h}^{1}}\leq\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}\gamma^{-1/2}h_{e}^{\alpha/2}\left\|v^{0}\right\|_{a_{h},0}
,
we can obtain

\displaystyle\left\|v^{0}\right\|_{a_{h},\partial}\leq C\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\{h_{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}\{h_{e}^{\alpha/2}\}\left\|v^{0}\right\|_{a_{h},0}.

∎

In the theorem below, we assume that
\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\{h_{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}\{h_{e}^{\alpha/2}\}
is bounded by
Ch^{(\alpha-1)/2}
with
C>0
independent of the mesh sizes.
This condition is fulfilled, for example, if
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is quasi-uniform.
Under this assumption, we prove the a priori error estimates for solutions
u\in H^{s}(\Omega)
,
s\geq 1
, of (
1
)–(
2
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
u
and
u_{h}
are the solutions of (
1
)–(
2
) and (
5
). Assume that
\alpha\geq 1
and

\displaystyle\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\{h_{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}\{h_{e}^{\alpha/2}\}\leq Ch^{(\alpha-1)/2}

with
C>0
independent of the mesh sizes. Then,

(22)

\displaystyle\left\|u-u_{h}\right\|_{a_{h}}\leq C(\inf_{v\in V_{h}^{c}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{1}+\operatorname{osc}(f))

with

independent of the mesh sizes. In addition, the interior jump terms satisfy

\displaystyle|u_{h}^{0}|_{H_{h}^{1}}\lesssim h^{\alpha}\left(\inf_{v\in V_{h}^{c}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{1}+\operatorname{osc}(f)\right).

Proof.

We define

such that

\displaystyle a(w_{h}^{c},v)=\mathcal{F}(v)\qquad\forall v\in V_{h}^{c}.

We first claim that for

\displaystyle a_{h}(\Pi_{h}^{0}u,v^{0})

\displaystyle=a_{h}(\Pi_{h}^{c}u,v^{0}-I_{h}v^{0})-\mathcal{F}(v^{0}-I_{h}v^{0})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(f+\nabla\cdot(\nabla\Pi_{h}^{c}u),v^{0}-I_{h}v^{0})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\left<[\![{\nabla\Pi_{h}^{c}u}]\!],{v^{0}-I_{h}v^{0}}\right>_{e}.

By (
15

16

\displaystyle\left(f+\Delta\Pi_{h}^{c}u,v^{0}-I_{h}v^{0}\right)_{T}

Recall that the flux is

Definition 3.3

Theorem 3.4

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{z}_{h},\boldsymbol{q})_{T}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{q}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{k-2}(T),

4.
Preconditioning

In this section we propose an abstract form of block diagonal preconditioners.
Through
the operator preconditioning approach, we show that the preconditioners are spectrally equivalent to the matrix given by (
5
) independent of mesh sizes.

We define a norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
on
\mathbb{V}_{h}
as

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(v^{c},v^{0})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}

\displaystyle:=a_{h}(v^{c},v^{c})+a_{h}(v^{0},v^{0})

(33)

\displaystyle={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(\nabla v^{c},\nabla v^{c}\right)}+\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}v^{c},v^{c}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1-\alpha}[\![{{v^{0}}}]\!],[\![{{v^{0}}}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}+\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}v^{0},v^{0}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}

for

,

. Note that this is a norm on

, not only on

. For an element

the decomposition

is not unique, so

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

Proof.

\displaystyle|[\![{\phi_{v^{0},T_{0}}}]\!]|_{e_{j}}|=\left|\frac{1}{N(T_{0})+1}\sum_{i=1}^{N(T_{0})}(p_{i}-p_{0})\right|\leq\frac{1}{N(T_{0})+1}\sum_{i=1}^{N(T_{0})}|p_{i}-p_{0}|

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

Theorem 4.3
.

hold. If
\alpha\geq 1
, then there exists

(39)

\displaystyle\inf_{v=v^{c}+v^{0}\in V_{h}}\sup_{w={w^{c}}+{w^{0}}\in V_{h}}\frac{a_{h}(v,w)}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(v^{c},v^{0})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|({w^{c}},{w^{0}})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}\geq C>0.

Proof.

For
v\in V_{h}
let
v=v^{c}+v^{0}
for
v^{c}\in V_{h}^{c}
and
v^{0}\in V_{h}^{00}
. Suppose that
\bar{v}^{0}
and
\tilde{v}^{0}
are defined as in Lemma
4.2
.
By (
35
), (
36
), (
37
), and (
20
) there exists
C_{1}
independent of
h
satisfying

(40)

\displaystyle\left\|\bar{v}^{0}\right\|_{a_{h},\partial},\left\|\tilde{v}^{0}\right\|_{a_{h},0},\left\|\tilde{v}^{0}\right\|_{a_{h},\partial},\left\|v^{0}\right\|_{a_{h},\partial}\leq C_{1}\left\|v^{0}\right\|_{a_{h},0}.

We take

\displaystyle w:={w^{c}}+{w^{0}},\qquad{w^{c}}:=v^{c},\quad{w^{0}}:=\delta\tilde{v}^{0}=v^{0}+\delta(v^{0}+\bar{v}^{0})

with
\delta>0
which will be determined later. From the definition (
33
) and (
35
), it holds that

(41)

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|({w^{c}},{w^{0}})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(v^{c},v^{0}+\delta\tilde{v}^{0})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v^{c}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v+\delta\tilde{v}^{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\leq C{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(v^{c},v^{0})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}

with
C
which is uniformly bounded for
\delta\leq 1
.

From the definition of
w
and the coercivity of
a_{h}

\displaystyle a_{h}(v,w)

\displaystyle=a_{h}(v,v)+\delta a_{h}(v,\tilde{v}^{0})

\displaystyle\geq C_{\text{\rm{co}}}\left(\left\|v^{c}\right\|_{a_{h},0}^{2}+\left\|v^{0}\right\|_{a_{h},0}^{2}+\left\|v^{0}+v^{c}\right\|_{a_{h},\partial}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\delta\left<\left\{\!\!\left\{{\nabla v^{c}}\right\}\!\!\right\},[\![{\tilde{v}^{0}}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}+\delta\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1-\alpha}[\![{v^{0}}]\!],[\![{\tilde{v}^{0}}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}

\displaystyle\quad+\delta\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}(v^{0}+v^{c}),\tilde{v}^{0}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}

\displaystyle=C_{\text{\rm{co}}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(v^{c},v^{0})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+2C_{\text{\rm{co}}}\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}v^{c},v^{0}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}

\displaystyle\quad-\delta\left<\left\{\!\!\left\{{\nabla v^{c}}\right\}\!\!\right\},[\![{\tilde{v}^{0}}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}+\delta\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1-\alpha}[\![{v^{0}}]\!],[\![{\tilde{v}^{0}}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}

\displaystyle\quad+\delta\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}v^{0},\tilde{v}^{0}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}+\delta\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}v^{c},v^{0}+\bar{v}^{0}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\qquad(\text{because }\tilde{v}^{0}=v^{0}+\bar{v}^{0})

\displaystyle=C_{\text{\rm{co}}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(v^{c},v^{0})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+(2C_{\text{\rm{co}}}+\delta)\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}v^{c},v^{0}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}+\delta\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}v^{c},\bar{v}^{0}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}

\displaystyle\quad-\delta\left<\left\{\!\!\left\{{\nabla v^{c}}\right\}\!\!\right\},[\![{\tilde{v}^{0}}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}+\delta\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1-\alpha}[\![{v^{0}}]\!],[\![{\tilde{v}^{0}}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}+\delta\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}v^{0},\tilde{v}^{0}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}

\displaystyle=:C_{\text{\rm{co}}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(v^{c},v^{0})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+(2C_{\text{\rm{co}}}+\delta)I_{1}+\delta(I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+I_{5})

with

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle:=\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}v^{c},v^{0}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle:=\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}v^{c},\bar{v}^{0}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle:=\left<\left\{\!\!\left\{{\nabla v^{c}}\right\}\!\!\right\},[\![{\tilde{v}^{0}}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}},

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle:=\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1-\alpha}[\![{v^{0}}]\!],[\![{\tilde{v}^{0}}]\!]\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}

\displaystyle I_{5}

\displaystyle:=\left<\gamma h_{e}^{-1}v^{0},\tilde{v}^{0}\right>_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}.

\displaystyle X_{c,\partial}=\left\|v^{c}\right\|_{a_{h},\partial},\quad X_{c,0}=\left\|v^{c}\right\|_{a_{h},0},\quad X_{0,\partial}=\left\|v^{0}\right\|_{a_{h},\partial},\quad X_{0,0}=\left\|v^{0}\right\|_{a_{h},0}.

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq X_{c,\partial}X_{0,\partial}\leq C_{1}X_{c,\partial}X_{0,0},\quad\text{(by \eqref{eq:aux-ineq1})}

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq X_{c,\partial}\left\|\bar{v}^{0}\right\|_{a_{h},\partial}\leq C_{1}X_{c,\partial}X_{0,0},\quad\text{(by \eqref{eq:aux-ineq1})}

\displaystyle|I_{3}|

\displaystyle a_{h}(v,w)

. If we use

\displaystyle\geq\left(C_{\text{\rm{co}}}\frac{\delta}{2+\delta}-9\delta^{2}C_{1}^{2}\right)X_{c,\partial}^{2}+C_{\text{\rm{co}}}X_{c,0}^{2}+\frac{C_{\text{\rm{co}}}\delta}{2}X_{0,\partial}^{2}+\frac{3C_{\text{\rm{co}}}}{4}X_{0,0}^{2}.

The conclusion follows by combining it with (

).

To construct preconditioners recall that
\tilde{{\mathcal{A}_{h}}}
is given by the bilinear form

(42)

\displaystyle a_{h}(v^{c},{w^{c}})+a_{h}(v^{0},{w^{0}}),\qquad v,w\in V_{h},

and its matrix form is

(43)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbb{M}}_{c}&0\\
0&{\mathbb{M}}_{0}\end{pmatrix}

where
{\mathbb{M}}_{c}
and
{\mathbb{M}}_{0}
are matrices obtained from
a_{h}(v^{c},{w^{c}})
and
a_{h}(v^{0},{w^{0}})
, respectively.

To construct preconditioners in practice, we use algebraic multigrid methods to obtain
an approximate inverse of this block diagonal matrix.
It is known that algebraic multigrid methods give good preconditioners for
{\mathbb{M}}_{c}
.
Since
{\mathbb{M}}_{0}
is a weakly diagonally dominant matrix
[
14
]
,
standard algebraic multigrid methods give an efficient preconditioner for this as well.
Therefore it is feasible to use a preconditioner of the form

(44)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\text{AMG}({\mathbb{M}}_{c})&0\\
0&\text{AMG}({\mathbb{M}}_{0})\end{pmatrix},

where
\text{AMG}({\mathbb{M}})
is a preconditioner of
{\mathbb{M}}
constructed by algebraic multigrid methods.
We will see in the next section that this form of preconditioner gives robust numerical results.

6.
Conclusion

In this paper we propose interior over-penalized enriched Galerkin (IOP-EG) methods for second order elliptic equations. From theoretical point of view, a new medius error analysis and a new spectral equivalence analysis, are developed for optimal convergence of errors and for construction of robust iterative solvers.
In numerical experiment results comparing preconditioned IOP-EG and EG methods, we observe that preconditioned IOP-EG methods show very robust iterative solver performances for mesh refinement and for the anisotropy of permeability coefficients. In conclusion, the IOP-EG methods can be a good replacement of the original EG methods providing parameter-robust scalable iterative solvers.

Some applications of the Lagrange inversion formula for the k k -Fibonacci numbers
Bakir FARHI

Abstract
The aim of this paper consists of providing summation formulas for the k k -Fibonacci numbers ( k ∈ ℤ k\in{\mathbb{Z}} , k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 ) and their asymptotic equivalents in terms of generalized binomial coefficients. Our main tools are the Lagrange inversion formula and one of its consequences due to Hermite.



MSC 2020:
Primary 11B39; Secondary 11B83, 11B65, 11B37.

Keywords:

k
-Fibonacci numbers, Fibonacci numbers, linear recurrent sequences, binomial coefficients, the Lagrange inversion formula.

1
Introduction and Notation

Throughout this paper, we let
{\mathbb{N}}
denote the set of positive integers and
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:={\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
the set of nonnegative integers. Next, the (compositional) inverse function of a function
f
is denoted by
f^{<-1>}
and the coefficient of
z^{n}
(
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
) in a power series
\varphi(z)
is denoted by
[z^{n}]\varphi(z)
. Further, we let
\binom{a}{b}
(
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
) denote the generalized binomial coefficient defined by:

\binom{a}{b}:=\begin{cases}\frac{a(a-1)\cdots(a-b+1)}{b!}&\text{if }b\geq 0\\
\binom{a}{a-b}&\text{if }a\geq b\\
0&\text{if }a<b<0\end{cases}.

(1.1)

Using this definition, we easily cheek the following formulas:

\displaystyle\binom{a}{b}

\displaystyle=\binom{a-1}{b-1}+\binom{a-1}{b}~~~~~~~~~~(\forall(a,b)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\binom{a}{b}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(-1)^{b-1}\binom{b-a-1}{b}&\text{if }b\leq a<0\\
(-1)^{b}\binom{b-a-1}{b}&\text{else}\end{cases}~~~~~~~~~~(\forall(a,b)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}).

(1.3)

For a given integer
k\geq 2
, the
k
-Fibonacci sequence is the integer sequence
{(F_{n}^{(k)})}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
given by the recurrence relation

F_{n+k}^{(k)}=F_{n}^{(k)}+F_{n+1}^{(k)}+\dots+F_{n+k-1}^{(k)}~~~~~~~~~~(\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}),

(1.4)

with the initial values
F_{0}^{(k)}=F_{1}^{(k)}=\dots=F_{k-2}^{(k)}=0
and
F_{k-1}^{(k)}=1
. The special case
k=2
corresponds to the usual Fibonacci sequence, simply denoted by
{(F_{n})}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
and given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}F_{0}=0,F_{1}=1,\\
F_{n+2}=F_{n}+F_{n+1}~~~~(\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})\end{array}\right..

The above linear recurrence relation (
1.4
) of order
k
for the
k
-Fibonacci sequence easily implies the following simpler linear recurrence relation of order
(k+1)
:

F_{n+k+1}^{(k)}=2F_{n+k}^{(k)}-F_{n}^{(k)}~~~~~~~~~~(\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})

(1.5)

(see
[
4
]
for the details). By relying on (
1.5
), we easily check that we have

\begin{split}F_{n}^{(k)}&=2^{n-k}~~~~~~~~~~\text{for }k\leq n<2k,\\
F_{2k}^{(k)}&=2^{k}-1.\end{split}

(1.6)

The
k
-Fibonacci sequence plays an important role in many areas of Mathematics, including Discrete Mathematics and Combinatorics. Here is a simple combinatorial interpretation: given an integer
k\geq 2
, the number of possible ways of writing a positive integer
n
as a sum of numbers, all belonging to the set
\{1,2,\dots,k\}
(taking into account the permutations), is exactly
F_{n+k-1}^{(k)}
. To find a closed form of
F_{n}^{(k)}
, the theory of linear recurrence sequences associates to the linear recurrence (
1.4
) the polynomial equation (known as its
characteristic equation
)

x^{k}=x^{k-1}+x^{k-2}+\dots+x+1.

(
{\mathbb{E}}_{k}
)

Multiplying the both sides of (

E
k

) by
(x-1)
and rearranging, we obtain the simpler polynomial equation

x^{k+1}-2x^{k}+1=0,

(
{\mathbb{E}}_{k}^{\prime}
)

which is nothing else the characteristic equation associated to the linear recurrence (
1.5
). The closed form of
F_{n}^{(k)}
then depends on the complex roots of Equation (

E
k

) and their multiplicities. Furthermore, in order to establish a simple asymptotic equivalent for
F_{n}^{(k)}
(as
n\rightarrow+\infty
), it is necessary to locate the complex roots of Equation (

E
k

) in the complex plane, a topic that has been investigated by several authors. Using the famous Rouché theorem of complex analysis, Miles
[
6
]
proved that Equation (

E
k

) has exactly
(k-1)
pairwise distinct complex roots in the open unit disk
D(0,1)
of the complex plane and a unique complex root
\rho_{k}
of modulus greater than
1
; precisely,
\rho_{k}
is real and belongs to the interval
(1,2)
. In
[
10
]
, Wolfram showed that we have more precisely

2-\frac{2}{2^{k}}<\rho_{k}<2.

(1.7)

For the special case
k=2
,
\rho_{k}
is nothing else the famous
golden ratio
, commonly denoted by
\phi
(
\phi=\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}=1.618\dots
). In
[
7
]
, Miller founded an elementary way to prove the Miles result, which relies on Descartes’s rules of signs. Denoting by
\alpha_{k,1},\alpha_{k,2},\dots,\alpha_{k,k}=\rho_{k}
the complex roots of Equation (

E
k

), the theory of linear recurrence sequences shows that
F_{n}^{(k)}
has the closed form
F_{n}^{(k)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\lambda_{k,\ell}\alpha_{k,\ell}^{n}
(
\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
), where the
\lambda_{k,\ell}
’s are complex numbers (independent on
n
) which can be calculated by relying on the initial values of the sequence
{(F_{n}^{(k)})}_{n}
. In
[
8
]
, Spickerman and Joyner showed that

\lambda_{k,\ell}=\frac{\alpha_{k,\ell}^{k+1}-\alpha_{k,\ell}^{k}}{\alpha_{k,\ell}\left[2\alpha_{k,\ell}^{k}-(k+1)\right]}~~~~~~~~~~(\forall\ell\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}),

and in
[
3
]
, Dresden simplified this to

\lambda_{k,\ell}=\frac{\alpha_{k,\ell}-1}{\alpha_{k,\ell}\left[2+(k+1)(\alpha_{k,\ell}-2)\right]}=\frac{\alpha_{k,\ell}-1}{\alpha_{k,\ell}\left[(k+1)\alpha_{k,\ell}-2k\right]}~~~~~~~~~~(\forall\ell\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}).

So we have for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
:

F_{n}^{(k)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{\alpha_{k,\ell}-1}{(k+1)\alpha_{k,\ell}-2k}\,\alpha_{k,\ell}^{n-1}.

Then, since
\rho_{k}
is the largest root of (

E
k

) in modulus, we derive the following asymptotic equivalence (as
n\rightarrow+\infty
):

F_{n}^{(k)}\sim_{+\infty}\frac{\rho_{k}-1}{(k+1)\rho_{k}-2k}\,\rho_{k}^{n-1}.

(1.8)

Let us denote by
\mathrm{As}_{n}^{(k)}
the expression of the right-hand side of (
1.8
); that is

\mathrm{As}_{n}^{(k)}:=\frac{\rho_{k}-1}{(k+1)\rho_{k}-2k}\,\rho_{k}^{n-1}

(1.9)

(for all integer
k\geq 2
and all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
). Next, for all integer
k\geq 2
, define

\varepsilon_{k}:=2-\rho_{k}.

From (
1.7
), we have that
\varepsilon_{k}\in(0,2^{1-k})\subset(0,1/2)
. Furthermore, from the fact that
\rho_{k}
is a root of Equation (

E
k

′

), we derive that

\varepsilon_{k}=\frac{1}{(2-\varepsilon_{k})^{k}},

(1.10)

which is a crucial equality for our purpose.

The goal of this paper is first to represent
\rho_{k}^{n}
and
\mathrm{As}_{n}^{(k)}
as series involving binomial coefficients. Then, by truncating judiciously the series related to
\mathrm{As}_{n}^{(k)}
, we derive a representation of
F_{n}^{(k)}
as a finite sum involving binomial coefficients. Our results
are essentially obtained by employing the Lagrange inversion formula and one of its consequences due to Hermite (see §
2
). While some of these results have been previously pointed out by other authors, they were often accompanied by
ad hoc
proofs, and in some instances, contained mistakes (see §
3
for detailed discussion).

2
The Lagrange inversion formula

The Lagrange inversion formula is one of the most important formulas of combinatorics. In its general form, it can be stated as follows

Theorem 1

(The Lagrange inversion formula)
.

Let
\alpha,z_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
f
be a complex analytic function of
z
at a neighborhood of
z_{0}
. Let also
w=w(z)
be a complex analytic function of
z
at a neighborhood of
z_{0}
, which is implicitly defined by
:

w=z_{0}+\alpha f(w).

(2.1)

Then for every complex analytic function
g
of
z
at a neighborhood of
z_{0}
, we have
(
for
|\alpha|
sufficiently small
)

g(w)=g(z_{0})+\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!}\left[\left(\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{n-1}\!\!\!\left(f^{n}g^{\prime}\right)\right](z_{0}).

Taking in particular
g(z)=z
, we get
(
for
|\alpha|
sufficiently small
)

w=z_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!}\left[\left(\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{n-1}\!\!\!\left(f^{n}\right)\right](z_{0}).

Note that the last part of this important theorem was proved in 1770 by Lagrange
[
5
]
, who applied it to solve the Kepler equation
y=x+\alpha\sin{y}
, while its generalization (i.e., the first part of Theorem
1
) was obtained by Bürmann
[
1
]
in 1798. One simple way to prove Theorem
1
uses contour integration in the complex plane, while other more elementary approaches employ algebraic techniques on formal series (see e.g.,
[
2
,
9
]
). From Theorem
1
, we derive the following interesting corollary, which is due to Hermite (see
[
2
]
):

Corollary 2

(Hermite)
.

Let
\varphi
be a complex analytic function at a neighborhood of
0
such that
\varphi(0)\neq 0
and let
\psi
be the compositional inverse function of the function
z\rightarrow\frac{z}{\varphi(z)}
at a neighborhood of
0

(
\psi
exists because
\left(\frac{z}{\varphi(z)}\right)^{\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{\varphi(0)}\neq 0
)
. Then for every complex function
g
, which is analytic at a neighborhood of
0
, we have

\frac{z\cdot(g\circ\psi)(z)}{\psi(z)\cdot\left(\left(\frac{z}{\varphi(z)}\right)^{\prime}\circ\psi(z)\right)}=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}z^{n}[z^{n}]\left(g(z)\varphi(z)^{n}\right).

Since this last result of Hermite is unfamiliar, we have preferred to include its proof in this paper.

Proof of Corollary
2
.

Let f be a primitive of the function
\frac{g}{\varphi}
at a neighborhood of
0
. By definition of
\psi
, we have
\left(\frac{z}{\varphi(z)}\circ\psi\right)(z)=z
; that is
\psi
is implicitly given by:

\psi(z)=0+z\cdot\left(\varphi\circ\psi\right)(z).

(2.2)

So by applying the first part of Theorem
1
with (
2.2
) filling the role of (
2.1
); that is with
w
replaced by
\psi
,
f
by
\varphi
,
\alpha
by
z
,
z_{0}
by
0
, and
g
by
f
, we get

\displaystyle\left(f\circ\psi\right)(z)

\displaystyle=f(0)+\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\left[\left(\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{n-1}\!\!\!\left(\varphi(z)^{n}f^{\prime}(z)\right)\right](0)

\displaystyle=f(0)+\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n}\left[z^{n-1}\right]\left(g(z)\varphi(z)^{n-1}\right)~~~~~~~~~~(\text{since }f^{\prime}=\frac{g}{\varphi})

\displaystyle=f(0)+\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{n+1}}{n+1}\left[z^{n}\right]\left(g(z)\varphi(z)^{n}\right).

By differentiating with respect to
z
, we get

\psi^{\prime}(z)\cdot\left(f^{\prime}\circ\psi\right)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}z^{n}\left[z^{n}\right]\left(g(z)\varphi(z)^{n}\right).

But since

\psi^{\prime}(z)=\left(\left(\frac{z}{\varphi(z)}\right)^{<-1>}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\left(\frac{z}{\varphi(z)}\right)^{\prime}\circ\psi(z)}

and

\left(f^{\prime}\circ\psi\right)(z)=\left(\frac{g}{\varphi}\circ\psi\right)(z)=\frac{(g\circ\psi)(z)}{(\varphi\circ\psi)(z)}=\frac{1}{\psi(z)}\cdot\frac{\psi(z)}{(\varphi\circ\psi)(z)}\cdot\left(g\circ\psi\right)(z)\\
=\frac{1}{\psi(z)}\cdot\underbrace{\left(\frac{z}{\varphi(z)}\circ\psi(z)\right)}_{=z}\cdot\left(g\circ\psi\right)(z)=\frac{z}{\psi(z)}\cdot\left(g\circ\psi\right)(z),

it follows that:

\frac{z\cdot(g\circ\psi)(z)}{\psi(z)\cdot\left(\left(\frac{z}{\varphi(z)}\right)^{\prime}\circ\psi(z)\right)}=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}z^{n}[z^{n}]\left(g(z)\varphi(z)^{n}\right),

as required.
∎

3
The results and the proofs

We begin with the following result which express the powers of
\rho_{k}
(
k\geq 2
) as real series involving binomial coefficients.

Theorem 3.1
.

For every positive integers
k
and
n
, with
k\geq 2
, we have

\rho_{k}^{n}=2^{n}-n2^{n-k-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\ell+1}\binom{k(\ell+1)+\ell-n}{\ell}\frac{1}{2^{(k+1)\ell}}.

In particular, we have

\rho_{k}=2-\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\ell+1}\binom{k(\ell+1)+\ell-1}{\ell}\frac{1}{2^{(k+1)\ell}}.

Proof.

Let
k
and
n
be two fixed positive integers with
k\geq 2
. By applying the first part of Theorem
1
with (
1.10
) filling the role of (
2.1
); that is with
w=\varepsilon_{k}
,
f(z)=(2-z)^{-k}
,
\alpha=1
,
z_{0}=0
, and
g(z)=(2-z)^{n}
, we get

\displaystyle\left(2-\varepsilon_{k}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=2^{n}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\ell!}\left[\left(\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{\ell-1}\!\!\!\left((2-z)^{-k\ell}(-n(2-z)^{n-1})\right)\right](0)

\displaystyle=2^{n}-n\sum_{\ell=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\ell!}\left[\left(\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{\ell-1}\!\!\!(2-z)^{-k\ell+n-1}\right](0)

\displaystyle=2^{n}-n\sum_{\ell=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\ell}\left[z^{\ell-1}\right](2-z)^{-k\ell+n-1}.

But according to the generalized binomial formula, we have for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
:

\left[z^{\ell-1}\right](2-z)^{-k\ell+n-1}=\binom{-k\ell+n-1}{\ell-1}2^{-k\ell+n-\ell}(-1)^{\ell-1}=\binom{k\ell-n+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\frac{2^{n}}{2^{(k+1)\ell}}

(according to (
1.3
)). Thus

\displaystyle\left(2-\varepsilon_{k}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=2^{n}-n\sum_{\ell=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\ell}\binom{k\ell-n+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\frac{2^{n}}{2^{(k+1)\ell}}

\displaystyle=2^{n}-n\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\ell+1}\binom{k(\ell+1)+\ell-n}{\ell}\frac{2^{n}}{2^{(k+1)(\ell+1)}};

that is

\rho_{k}^{n}=2^{n}-n2^{n-k-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\ell+1}\binom{k(\ell+1)+\ell-n}{\ell}\frac{1}{2^{(k+1)\ell}},

as required.
∎

Remarks 3.2
.

1.

In the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we have omitted the verification of the convergence of the series
\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\ell+1}\binom{k(\ell+1)+\ell-n}{\ell}\frac{1}{2^{(k+1)\ell}}
. Note that this convergence can be easily confirmed by using for example the elementary upper bounds
\binom{m}{\ell}\leq\frac{2^{m}}{\sqrt{m+2}}
(for
1\leq\ell\leq m
).

2.

Actually, the formula of
\rho_{k}
in the second part of Theorem
3.1
has already been discovered by Wolfram
[
10
]
, who obtained it in a more complicated way.

By specializing in Theorem
3.1
the integer
k
to
2
, we derive important formulas for the golden ration
\phi
and its powers. Precisely, we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 3.3
.

For every positive integer
n
, we have

\phi^{n}=2^{n}-n2^{n-3}\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\ell+1}\binom{3\ell+2-n}{\ell}\frac{1}{2^{3\ell}}.

In particular, we have

\phi=2-\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\ell+1}\binom{3\ell+1}{\ell}\frac{1}{2^{3\ell+2}}.

\square

More interestingly, we are going to find representations as series (analogous to those of Theorem
3.1
) for the asymptotic equivalent
\mathrm{As}_{n}^{(k)}
of
F_{n}^{(k)}
. To achieve this, we rely on the following key theorem:

Theorem 3.4
.

For every integers
k
and
a
, with
k\geq 2
, we have

\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\binom{(k+1)\ell+a}{\ell}2^{-(k+1)\ell}=2^{a+1}\cdot\frac{\rho_{k}^{-a}}{(k+1)\rho_{k}-2k}.

Proof.

Let
k
and
a
be fixed integers, with
k\geq 2
. We apply Corollary
2
for the functions
\varphi(z)=(2-z)^{-k}
and
g(z)=(2-z)^{-a-1}
, which are both analytic at a neighborhood of
0
and we have
\varphi(0)=2^{-k}\neq 0
. In this situation, the compositional inverse function
\psi
of the function
z\mapsto\frac{z}{\varphi(z)}=z(2-z)^{k}
(at a neighborhood of
0
) satisfies (according to (
1.10
)):
\psi(1)=\varepsilon_{k}
. So by specializing in formula of Corollary
2
the variable
z
to
1
, we get

\frac{1\cdot(g\circ\psi)(1)}{\psi(1)\cdot\left(\left(z(2-z)^{k}\right)^{\prime}\circ\psi\right)(1)}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}1^{\ell}[z^{\ell}](2-z)^{-k\ell-a-1}.

But since
\psi(1)=\varepsilon_{k}
(as clarified above),
\left(z(2-z)^{k}\right)^{\prime}=(2-z)^{k-1}\left(2-(k+1)z\right)
, and for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
:
[z^{\ell}](2-z)^{-k\ell-a-1}=\binom{-k\ell-a-1}{\ell}2^{-k\ell-a-1-\ell}(-1)^{\ell}=\binom{(k+1)\ell+a}{\ell}2^{-(k+1)\ell-a-1}
(by using the generalized binomial formula and then (
1.3
)), it follows that

\frac{(2-\varepsilon_{k})^{-a-1}}{\varepsilon_{k}(2-\varepsilon_{k})^{k-1}\left(2-(k+1)\varepsilon_{k}\right)}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\binom{(k+1)\ell+a}{\ell}2^{-(k+1)\ell-a-1}.

Finally, since
\varepsilon_{k}(2-\varepsilon_{k})^{k}=1
(according to (
1.10
)),
2-\varepsilon_{k}=\rho_{k}
, and
2-(k+1)\varepsilon_{k}=

2-(k+1)(2-\rho_{k})=(k+1)\rho_{k}-2k
, we conclude to

\frac{\rho_{k}^{-a}}{(k+1)\rho_{k}-2k}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\binom{(k+1)\ell+a}{\ell}2^{-(k+1)\ell-a-1},

which immediately gives the required formula of the theorem.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The convergence of the series in the formula of Theorem
3.4
can be verified by using for example the Stirling formula
n!\sim_{+\infty}n^{n}e^{-n}\sqrt{2\pi n}
.

Relying on Theorem
3.4
, we are now able to provide a series representation (involving binomial coefficients) for the asymptotic equivalent
\mathrm{As}_{n}^{(k)}
of
F_{n}^{(k)}
. This is the subject of the following theorem:

Theorem 3.6
.

For every nonnegative integers
k
and
n
, with
k\geq 2
and
n\neq 1
, we have

\mathrm{As}_{n}^{(k)}=2^{n-2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\left[\binom{(k+1)\ell-n}{\ell}-\binom{(k+1)\ell-n}{\ell-1}\right]2^{-(k+1)\ell}.

Proof.

Let
k
and
n
be two fixed nonnegative integers such that
k\geq 2
and
n\neq 1
. By applying Theorem
3.4
for
a=-n
, we get

\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\binom{(k+1)\ell-n}{\ell}2^{-(k+1)\ell}=2^{-n+1}\cdot\frac{\rho_{k}^{n}}{(k+1)\rho_{k}-2k}.

(3.1)

Similarly, by applying Theorem
3.4
for
a=k+1-n
, we get

\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\binom{(k+1)\ell+k+1-n}{\ell}2^{-(k+1)\ell}=2^{k+2-n}\cdot\frac{\rho_{k}^{n-k-1}}{(k+1)\rho_{k}-2k}.

(3.2)

But we remark that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\binom{(k+1)\ell+k+1-n}{\ell}2^{-(k+1)\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\binom{(k+1)(\ell+1)-n}{\ell}2^{-(k+1)\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{+\infty}\binom{(k+1)\ell-n}{\ell-1}2^{-(k+1)(\ell-1)}

\displaystyle=2^{k+1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\binom{(k+1)\ell-n}{\ell-1}2^{-(k+1)\ell}

(since
\binom{(k+1)\ell-n}{\ell-1}=0
for
\ell=0
, because
n\neq 1
). Thus Formula (
3.2
) is simplified to

\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\binom{(k+1)\ell-n}{\ell-1}2^{-(k+1)\ell}=2^{-n+1}\frac{\rho_{k}^{n-k-1}}{(k+1)\rho_{k}-2k}.

(3.3)

Next, by subtracting side by side Eq. (
3.3
) from Eq. (
3.1
) and then multiplying by
2^{n-2}
, we get

\displaystyle 2^{n-2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\left[\binom{(k+1)\ell-n}{\ell}-\binom{(k+1)\ell-n}{\ell-1}\right]2^{-(k+1)\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\rho_{k}^{n}-\rho_{k}^{n-k-1}}{(k+1)\rho_{k}-2k}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\rho_{k}-\rho_{k}^{-k}}{(k+1)\rho_{k}-2k}\cdot\rho_{k}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\rho_{k}-(2-\rho_{k})}{(k+1)\rho_{k}-2k}\cdot\rho_{k}^{n-1}~~~~~~(\text{in view of \eqref{eq9}})

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt=\frac{\rho_{k}-1}{(k+1)\rho_{k}-2k}\rho_{k}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt=\mathrm{As}_{n}^{(k)}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~(\text{according to \eqref{eq8}}),

as required.
∎

Observing Formula of Theorem
3.6
, and because
As_{n}^{(k)}
is close to
F_{n}^{(k)}
for
n
large, a natural question arises: can replacing the series therein with its truncation at some positive integer
N(n,k)
(depending on
n
and
k
) yield the
k
-Fibonacci numbers? Working in this direction, we have established the following curious result:

Theorem 3.7
.

For every integers
n,k\geq 2
, we have

F_{n+k-2}^{(k)}=2^{n-2}\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n-1}{k+1}}\left[\binom{(k+1)\ell-n}{\ell}-\binom{(k+1)\ell-n}{\ell-1}\right]2^{-(k+1)\ell}.

To prepare for the proof of Theorem
3.7
, let us provisionally define (for every integers
n,k\geq 2
)

G_{n}^{(k)}:=2^{n-2}\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n-1}{k+1}}\left[\binom{(k+1)\ell-n}{\ell}-\binom{(k+1)\ell-n}{\ell-1}\right]2^{-(k+1)\ell}.

We first establish the two following propositions:

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
n,k\geq 2
be two integers. Then we have

\begin{split}G_{n}^{(k)}&=2^{n-2}~~~~~~~~~~\text{for }2\leq n\leq k+1,\\
G_{k+2}^{(k)}&=2^{k}-1.\end{split}

(3.4)

Proof.

For
2\leq n\leq k+1
, the only integer
\ell
in the range
[0,\frac{n-1}{k+1}]
is
\ell=0
. So the definition of
G_{n}^{(k)}
gives

G_{n}^{(k)}=2^{n-2}\left[\binom{-n}{0}-\binom{-n}{-1}\right]=2^{n-2}

(since
\binom{-n}{0}=1
and
\binom{-n}{-1}=0
), as required.

Next, for
n=k+2
, the only integers in the range
[0,\frac{n-1}{k+1}]=[0,1]
are
0
and
1
. So the definition of
G_{k+2}^{(k)}
gives

\displaystyle G_{k+2}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=2^{k}\left\{\left[\underbrace{\binom{-k-2}{0}}_{=1}-\underbrace{\binom{-k-2}{-1}}_{=0}\right]+\left[\underbrace{\binom{-1}{1}}_{=-1}-\underbrace{\binom{-1}{0}}_{=1}\right]2^{-k-1}\right\}

\displaystyle=2^{k}\left(1-2^{-k}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{k}-1,

as required. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

For all integers
n,k\geq 2
, we have

G_{n+k+1}^{(k)}=2G_{n+k}^{(k)}-G_{n}^{(k)}.

Proof.

Let
n,k\geq 2
be two fixed integers. We have by definition

2G_{n+k}^{(k)}=2^{n+k-1}\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n+k-1}{k+1}}\left[\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k}{\ell}-\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k}{\ell-1}\right]2^{-(k+1)\ell}.

(3.5)

But summing on
\ell
such that
0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n+k-1}{k+1}
is equivalent to sum on
\ell
such that
0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n+k}{k+1}
, except perhaps in the case where
\frac{n+k}{k+1}
is an integer. Suppose that we are in the last case (i.e.,
\frac{n+k}{k+1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
). Then since
\frac{n+k}{k+1}\geq\frac{k+2}{k+1}>1
, we have
\frac{n+k}{k+1}\geq 2
. Thus for
\ell=\frac{n+k}{k+1}\geq 2
, we find that
\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k}{\ell}-\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k}{\ell-1}=\binom{0}{\ell}-\binom{0}{\ell-1}=0
, implying that the sum for
0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n+k-1}{k+1}
in (
3.5
) can be replaced by the sum for
0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n+k}{k+1}
even in the case where
\frac{n+k}{k+1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. So we have in all cases

2G_{n+k}^{(k)}=2^{n+k-1}\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n+k}{k+1}}\left[\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k}{\ell}-\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k}{\ell-1}\right]2^{-(k+1)\ell}.

(3.6)

Next, we have by definition

\displaystyle G_{n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=2^{n-2}\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n-1}{k+1}}\left[\binom{(k+1)\ell-n}{\ell}-\binom{(k+1)\ell-n}{\ell-1}\right]2^{-(k+1)\ell}

\displaystyle=2^{n-2}\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq\frac{n+k}{k+1}}\left[\binom{(k+1)(\ell-1)-n}{\ell-1}-\binom{(k+1)(\ell-1)-n}{\ell-2}\right]2^{-(k+1)(\ell-1)}

\displaystyle=2^{n+k-1}\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq\frac{n+k}{k+1}}\left[\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k-1}{\ell-1}-\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k-1}{\ell-2}\right]2^{-(k+1)\ell}.

But since
\ell=0
gives
\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k-1}{\ell-1}-\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k-1}{\ell-2}=\binom{-n-k-1}{-1}-\binom{-n-k-1}{-2}=0
(because
-n-k-1\leq-5
), we have even

G_{n}^{(k)}=2^{n+k-1}\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n+k}{k+1}}\left[\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k-1}{\ell-1}-\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k-1}{\ell-2}\right]2^{-(k+1)\ell}.

(3.7)

By subtracting (side to side) (
3.7
) from (
3.6
) and using the formulas

\displaystyle\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k}{\ell}-\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k-1}{\ell-1}=\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k-1}{\ell}

and

\displaystyle\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k}{\ell-1}-\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k-1}{\ell-2}=\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k-1}{\ell-1}

that result from (
1.2
), we get

\displaystyle 2G_{n+k}^{(k)}-G_{n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=2^{n+k-1}\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n+k}{k+1}}\left[\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k-1}{\ell}-\binom{(k+1)\ell-n-k-1}{\ell-1}\right]2^{-(k+1)\ell}

\displaystyle=G_{n+k+1}^{(k)},

as required.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
3.7
.

Proof of Theorem
3.7
.

Let
k\geq 2
be a fixed integer. We have to show that
F_{n+k-2}^{(k)}=G_{n}^{(k)}
for all integer
n\geq 2
. Since the two sequences
{(F_{n+k-2}^{(k)})}_{n\geq 2}
and
{(G_{n}^{(k)})}_{n\geq 2}
satisfy the same linear recurrence relation of order
(k+1)
, which is

u_{n+k+1}=2u_{n+k}-u_{n}

(according to (
1.5
) and to Proposition
3.9
) then it suffices to show that
F_{n+k-2}^{(k)}
and
G_{n}^{(k)}
coincide at the first
(k+1)
consecutive values of
n
; that is

F_{n+k-2}^{(k)}=G_{n}^{(k)}~~~~~~~~~~\text{for }2\leq n\leq k+2.

(3.8)

To confirm (
3.8
), we distinguish the two following cases:

•
1
st
case
: (if
2\leq n\leq k+1
). In this case, we have
k\leq n+k-2\leq 2k-1
, implying (according to (
1.6
)) that
F_{n+k-2}^{(k)}=2^{n-2}
. Since we have also (according to (
3.4
))
G_{n}^{(k)}=2^{n-2}
then
F_{n+k-2}^{(k)}=G_{n}^{(k)}
for this case.

•
2
nd
case
: (If
n=k+2
). In this case, we have
F_{n+k-2}^{(k)}=F_{2k}^{(k)}=2^{k}-1
(according to (
1.6
)) and
G_{n}^{(k)}=G_{k+2}^{(k)}=2^{k}-1
(according to (
3.4
)). Hence
F_{n+k-2}^{(k)}=G_{n}^{(k)}
also for this case. This confirms (
3.8
) and completes this proof.
∎

By shifting the index
n
to
(n-k+2)
in Theorem
3.7
, we derive the following corollary:

Corollary 3.10
.

For every integers
n
and
k
such that
n\geq k\geq 2
, we have

F_{n}^{(k)}=2^{n-k}\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n-k+1}{k+1}}\left[\binom{(k+1)\ell-n+k-2}{\ell}-\binom{(k+1)\ell-n+k-2}{\ell-1}\right]2^{-(k+1)\ell}.

\square

By taking
k=2
in Corollary
3.10
, we derive in particular an expression of the usual Fibonacci numbers as finite sums involving binomial coefficients and nonpositive powers of
8
. This is given by the following corollary:

Corollary 3.11
.

For all integer
n\geq 2
, we have

F_{n}=2^{n-2}\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n-1}{3}}\left[\binom{3\ell-n}{\ell}-\binom{3\ell-n}{\ell-1}\right]2^{-3\ell}.

\square

We end this paper by deriving from Corollary
3.10
a result which corrects that of Howard and Cooper
[
4
, Theorem 2.4]
.

Corollary 3.12
.

For every integers
n
and
k
such that
n\geq k\geq 2
and
n\neq 2k-1
, we have

F_{n}^{(k)}=2^{n-k}+\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq\frac{n-k+1}{k+1}}(-1)^{\ell}\left[\binom{n-(\ell+1)k+2}{\ell}-\binom{n-(\ell+1)k}{\ell-2}\right]2^{n-(k+1)\ell-k}.

Proof.

Let
n,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be fixed with
n\geq k\geq 2
and
n\neq 2k-1
. According to Formula (
1.2
), we have for all
(a,\ell)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0),(0,1)\}
:

\binom{a}{\ell}-\binom{a}{\ell-1}=\binom{a-1}{\ell}-\binom{a-1}{\ell-2}.

By applying this last formula for each of the couples
(a,\ell)=((k+1)\ell-n+k-2,\ell)
(
0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n-k+1}{k+1}
), which are all different from
(0,0)
and
(0,1)
, we transform Formula of Corollary
3.10
to

F_{n}^{(k)}=2^{n-k}\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n-k+1}{k+1}}\left[\binom{(k+1)\ell+k-n-3}{\ell}-\binom{(k+1)\ell+k-n-3}{\ell-2}\right]2^{-(k+1)\ell}.

Then, by applying Formula (
1.3
) for all the binomial coefficients occurring in the last formula, we conclude to

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=2^{n-k}\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq\frac{n-k+1}{k+1}}(-1)^{\ell}\left[\binom{n-(\ell+1)k+2}{\ell}-\binom{n-(\ell+1)k}{\ell-2}\right]2^{-(k+1)\ell}

\displaystyle=2^{n-k}+\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq\frac{n-k+1}{k+1}}(-1)^{\ell}\left[\binom{n-(\ell+1)k+2}{\ell}-\binom{n-(\ell+1)k}{\ell-2}\right]2^{n-(k+1)\ell-k},

as required.
∎

Proof of Vogan’s conjecture on Arthur packets: irreducible parameters of p p -adic general linear groups
Clifton Cunningham and Mishty Ray

Abstract
. In this paper we prove Vogan’s conjecture on local Arthur packets, as recalled in [ 5 ] *Section 8.3, Conjecture 1(a), for irreducible Arthur parameters of p p -adic general linear groups. This result shows that these Arthur packets may be characterized by properties of simple perverse sheaves on a moduli space of Langlands parameters.



1.
Introduction

The local Langlands correspondence for a connected reductive algebraic group
G
over a
p
-adic field
F
partitions the set of equivalence classes of smooth irreducible representations of
G(F)
into
L
-packets using equivalence classes of Langlands parameters.
Following
[
14
,
1
]
, the correspondence may be viewed as a bijection between smooth irreducible representations that share a common infinitesimal parameter of a
p
-adic group
G(F)
, along with its pure inner forms, and simple objects in the category of equivariant perverse sheaves on a moduli space of Langlands parameters.
This perspective leads to the notion of an ABV-packet, as articulated in
[
5
]
*Section 8, which conjecturally generalizes the local Arthur packet - we call this as Vogan’s conjecture on local Arthur packets. In this paper, we prove this conjecture for irreducible Arthur parameters of the
p
-adic group
GL_{n}(F)
.

1.1.
Simple and irreducible parameters

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field with residue characteristic
p
and residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, so
q=p^{f}
. Let
W_{F}
be the Weil group of
F
and
W_{F}^{\prime}
denote the group
W_{F}\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.
Recall that a Langlands parameter is a homomorphism

\phi:W^{\prime}_{F}\to\prescript{L}{}{G}=\widehat{G}\rtimes W_{F}

commuting with the natural projections to
W_{F}
, satisfying conditions adapted from
[
4
]
.
In particular, this means that all the information of a Langlands parameter
\phi
is captured by a
1
-cocycle

\phi^{0}:W^{\prime}_{F}\to\widehat{G}

such that
\phi(w,g)=\phi^{0}(g,w)\rtimes w
.
In the case
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}
, the cocycle
\phi^{0}
is a representation of the group
W^{\prime}_{F}
.

Now set
W_{F}^{\prime\prime}:=W_{F}\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and recall that an Arthur parameter
[
3
]
*Chapter 1 is a homomorphism

\psi:W^{\prime\prime}_{F}\to\prescript{L}{}{G}=\widehat{G}\rtimes W_{F},

likewise defined by a
1
-cocycle

\psi^{0}:W^{\prime}_{F}\to\widehat{G}

satisfying certain conditions, notably that the restriction of
\psi^{0}
to
W_{F}
is bounded.
Again, when
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}
,
\psi^{0}
is a representation.
We say that
\psi
is a
simple
Arthur parameter of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(F)
if
\psi^{0}
is an irreducible representation and the restriction of
\psi^{0}
to
W_{F}
is trivial. We say that
\psi
is
irreducible
if
\psi^{0}
is an irreducible representation of
W_{F}^{\prime\prime}
.

Every Arthur parameter determines a Langlands parameter by the rule

\phi_{\psi}(w,x)=\psi\left(w,x,\begin{pmatrix}|w|^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\\
0&|w|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}\right).

The infinitesimal parameter
\lambda_{\phi}
of a Langlands parameter
\phi
is a homomorphism

\lambda_{\phi}:W_{F}\xrightarrow{}\prescript{L}{}{G}

defined by the rule

\lambda_{\phi}(w)=\phi\left(w,\begin{pmatrix}|w|^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\\
0&|w|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}\right).

See
[
5
]
*Section 4.1 for definition. As before, it is determined by

\lambda_{\phi}^{0}:W_{F}\to\widehat{G}

which is a representation when
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}
. The function
\phi\mapsto\lambda_{\phi}
defined on
\widehat{G}
-equivalence classes of these parameters is not injective in general. The infinitesimal parameters
\lambda_{\phi_{\psi}}
for a simple Arthur parameter
\psi
is unramified as a representation of
W_{F}
,
i.e.
, it are trivial on the inertia group
I_{F}
.

1.2.
Local Langlands correspondence

The local Langlands conjecture for a connected reductive algebraic group
G
over
F
gives a map between the set
\Pi(G(F))
of equivalence classes of smooth irreducible representations of
G(F)
and the set
\Phi(\prescript{L}{}{G})
of equivalence classes of Langlands parameters. This map can be refined
[
14
,
1
]
as a bijection between
\Pi(G(F))
and the set
\Xi(\prescript{L}{}{G})
of equivalence classes of pairs
\xi=(\phi,\epsilon)
, called enhanced Langlands parameters, where
\epsilon
is an irreducible representation of the finite group

S_{\phi}:=Z_{\widehat{G}}(\phi)/Z_{\widehat{G}}(\phi)^{0}Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma_{F}},

where
\Gamma_{F}
is the absolute Galois group of
F
.
We emphasize the distinction between Langlands parameters and
L
-parameters - Langlands parameters are maps and
L
-parameters are
\widehat{G}
-equivalence classes of Langlands parameters. These
L
-parameters partition
\Pi(G(F))
into disjoint finite sets
\Pi_{\phi}(G(F))
called
L
-packets. This correspondence must satisfy a list of functoriality properties and should be compatible with
L
-functions and
\epsilon
-factors on both sides (See
[
4
]
or
[
9
]
, for example).
When
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}
, the local Langlands correspondence is a theorem
[
8
,
7
]
. In this case,
S_{\phi}=1
(see
[
3
]
, for example) and the correspondence can be viewed as a bijection

\Pi(G(F))\xrightarrow{}\Phi(G),

where
\Phi(G)
is the set of
L
-parameters for
G
. Thus,
L
-packets are singletons for
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
.

1.3.
A-packets

In 1989, Arthur introduced
[
2
]
what are now known as Arthur packets, or
A
-packets, which enjoy some nice properties that non-tempered
L
-packets fail to satisfy. This was a global theory and the local analogue was clarified by Arthur’s subsequent work
[
3
]
in 2013.
Local
A
-packets
\Pi_{\psi}(G(F))
are finite sets of smooth irreducible representations of
G(F)
which are not necessarily disjoint and consist of unitary admissible representations. The representations that appear in
A
-packets are said to be of Arthur type. Every A-packet
\Pi_{\psi}(G(F))
contains a distinguished
L
-packet
\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}(G(F))
through the injection injection
\psi\mapsto\phi_{\psi}
.
In the 1990s, Adams, Barbasch, and Vogan suggested
[
1
]
a geometric characterization of
A
-packets by using microlocal analysis on certain stratified complex varieties built out of Langlands parameters. When
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}
,
A
-packets are singletons
[
3
]
; in particular, the
A
-packet for
\psi
coincides with the
L
-packet for
\phi_{\psi}
.

1.4.
Vogan-Langlands correspondence

Vogan’s perspective
[
14
]
on the local Langlands correspondence suggests a beautiful geometric approach to
A
-packets. This perspective concerns a moduli space
X_{\lambda}
of Langlands parameters that have the same infinitesimal parameter
\lambda
. Vogan observed that simple objects in the category
\operatorname{Per}_{\widehat{G}}(X_{\lambda})
of equivariant perverse sheaves on
X_{\lambda}
are naturally identified with the pairs
(\phi,\epsilon)
, when we properly interpret the finite group attached to
\phi
and consider representations of
G(F)
together with its pure inner forms. Viewed in this way, the local Langlands correspondence takes the form of a bijection

\Pi_{\lambda}(G/F)\to\operatorname{Per}_{H_{\lambda}}(V_{\lambda})_{\operatorname{/iso}}^{\operatorname{simple}}

between the set
\Pi_{\lambda}(G/F)
of equivalence classes of irreducible objects in the category
\operatorname{Rep}_{\lambda}(G/F)
of smooth representations of
G(F)
and its pure rational forms which have a matching infinitesimal parameter
\lambda
and the set
\operatorname{Per}_{H_{\lambda}}(V_{\lambda})_{\operatorname{/iso}}^{\operatorname{simple}}
of isomorphism classes of simple objects in the category
\operatorname{Per}_{\widehat{G}}(X_{\lambda})
.

Instead of working with
\operatorname{Per}_{\widehat{G}}(X_{\lambda})
, we work with an equivalent category
\operatorname{Per}_{H_{\lambda}}(V_{\lambda})
(see
[
5
]
*Section 4.5). The variety
V_{\lambda}
of Langlands parameters and the group
H_{\lambda}
are described in
2
.
In the case
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}
, the correspondence can be viewed as a bijection

\Pi_{\lambda}(G(F))\to\operatorname{Per}_{H_{\lambda}}(V_{\lambda})_{\operatorname{/iso}}^{\operatorname{simple}},

where
\Pi_{\lambda}(G(F))
is the set of equivalence classes of smooth irreducible representations of
G(F)
with a matching infinitesimal parameter
\lambda
and

\operatorname{Per}_{H_{\lambda}}(V_{\lambda})_{\operatorname{/iso}}^{\operatorname{simple}}=\{\mathcal{IC}({\mathbbm{1}}_{C}):C\subseteq V_{\lambda}\text{ is an }H_{\lambda}\text{-orbit}\},

where
{\mathbbm{1}}_{C}
denotes the constant local system on
C
.
In this way, every
\pi\in\Pi_{\lambda}(G(F))
corresponds to
\mathcal{P}(\pi)=\mathcal{IC}({\mathbbm{1}}_{C})\in\operatorname{Per}_{H_{\lambda}}(V_{\lambda})_{\operatorname{/iso}}^{\operatorname{simple}}
, for a unique
H_{\lambda}
-orbit
C\subseteq V_{\lambda}
. In other words, in this case there is a bijection between elements of
\Pi_{\lambda}(G(F))
and
H_{\lambda}
-orbits
C
in
V_{\lambda}
.

1.5.
Vogan’s conjecture on A-packets

Following an approach similar to
[
1
]
, Vogan attached to any Langlands parameter
\phi
the set of irreducible representations of
G(F)
and its pure inner forms for which the characteristic cycles of the corresponding simple perverse sheaf contains the conormal bundle of the
\widehat{G}
-orbit of
\phi
in the moduli space mentioned above. This notion is revised in
[
5
]
in terms of vanishing cycles. This set is referred to as the ABV-packet
\Pi_{\phi}^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}(G/F)
.

Vogan’s perspective on the local Langlands correspondence introduces pure A-packets
\Pi_{\psi}(G/F)
which are unions of
A
-packets for
G
and its pure inner forms. This perspective also identifies
\Pi_{\psi}(G/F)
with a finite set of simple perverse sheaves on
V_{\lambda_{\psi}}
, where
\lambda_{\psi}
is the infinitesimal parameter of
\phi_{\psi}
.
What property do these perverse sheaves share, and how are they determined by the Arthur parameter
\psi
?
This question is answered by the functor

\operatorname{Evs}_{\psi}:\operatorname{Per}_{H_{\lambda_{\psi}}}(V_{\lambda_{\psi}})\xrightarrow{}\operatorname{Rep}(A_{\psi})

where
A_{\psi}=Z_{\widehat{G}}(\psi)/Z_{\widehat{G}}(\psi)^{0}
,
constructed in
[
5
]
*Section 7.10.
In fact, this functor is defined in terms of a more general functor
\operatorname{Ev}_{C_{\psi}}:\operatorname{D}_{H_{\lambda_{\psi}}}(V_{\lambda_{\psi}})\to\operatorname{D}_{H_{\lambda_{\psi}}}(\Lambda^{\text{reg}}_{C_{\psi}})

where
\Lambda_{C_{\psi}}\subset\Lambda_{\lambda_{\psi}}
is the conormal bundle above
C_{\psi}
in the conormal variety for
V_{\lambda}

[
5
]
*Section 7.3.
This
\operatorname{Ev}_{C_{\psi}}
functor is defined in such a way that

\left(\operatorname{Ev}_{C_{\psi}}\mathcal{P}\right)_{(x,\xi)}=\left(\operatorname{R\hskip-1.0pt\Phi}_{\xi}\mathcal{P}\right)_{x},

for all
(x,\xi)\in\Lambda^{\text{reg}}_{C_{\psi}}
, where
\operatorname{R\hskip-1.0pt\Phi}_{\xi}
is Deligne’s vanishing cycles functor for
\xi:V_{\lambda_{\psi}}\to\mathbb{C}

[
5
]
*Equation 7.6.
When restricted from
\operatorname{D}_{H_{\lambda_{\psi}}}(V_{\lambda_{\psi}})
to
\operatorname{Per}_{H_{\lambda_{\psi}}}(V_{\lambda_{\psi}})
, the functor
\operatorname{Ev}_{C_{\psi}}
lands in
\operatorname{Per}_{H_{\lambda_{\psi}}}(\Lambda^{\text{reg}}_{C_{\psi}})
, which is equivalent to the semisimple category
\operatorname{Loc}_{H_{\lambda_{\psi}}}(\Lambda^{\text{reg}}_{C_{\psi}})
of equivariant local systems on
\Lambda^{\text{reg}}_{C_{\psi}}
, by
[
5
]
*Proposition 6.9.
This proposition also gives an equivalence

\operatorname{Loc}_{H_{\lambda_{\psi}}}(\Lambda^{\text{reg}}_{C_{\psi}})\cong\operatorname{Rep}(A_{\psi});

see
[
5
]
*Equation 7.30.
As explained in
[
5
]
*Equations 7.22, 7.24, the precise relation between
\operatorname{Evs}_{\psi}
and
\operatorname{Ev}_{C_{\psi}}
is given by

(1)

\operatorname{Evs}_{\psi}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{H}^{1+\operatorname{codim}C_{\psi}}_{(x_{\psi},\xi_{\psi})}\left(\operatorname{Ev}_{C_{\psi}}\mathcal{P}\right),

as a vector space with
A_{\psi}
-action, where
(x_{\psi},\xi_{\psi})\in\Lambda_{\lambda_{\psi}}
is defined in
[
5
]
*Equation 6.12, 6.13.
For
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}
,
A_{\psi}=1
by
[
3
]
*Chapter 1, for example; so in this case,
\operatorname{Rep}(A_{\psi})
is simply the category of vector spaces.
Following
[
5
]
, the ABV-packet for
\psi
is defined by

(2)

\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}}(G(F)):=\{\pi\in\Pi_{\lambda_{\psi}}(G(F)):\operatorname{Evs}_{\psi}(\mathcal{P}(\pi))\neq 0\},

where
\mathcal{P}(\pi)
is the simple perverse sheaf on
V_{\lambda_{\psi}}
corresponding to
\pi\in\Pi_{\lambda_{\psi}}(G(F))
.

The main result of this paper, below, gives a proof of
[
5
]
*Section 8.3, Conjecture 1(a) adapted to general linear groups, for irreducible Arthur parameters:

Theorem 1

(Theorem
6.4
)
.

Let
\psi
be an irreducible Arthur parameter of
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}(F)
, let
\phi_{\psi}
be its corresponding Langlands parameter, let
\Pi_{\psi}(G(F))
be the Arthur packet attached to
\psi
.
Then

\Pi_{\psi}(G(F))=\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}}(G(F)).

We briefly overview the structure of the paper. Henceforth,
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}
. In Section
2
, we write down the
Vogan variety

V_{\lambda}
, the group
H_{\lambda}
, and the explicit action of
H_{\lambda}
on
V_{\lambda}
. Our perspective on the local Langlands correspondence is that of representations corresponding to orbits in the Vogan variety. Both sides of this correspondence are encoded in combinatorial objects - multisegments for representations and rank triangles for orbits. We reiterate these notions and write down an algorithm to go from multisegments to rank triangles and vice-verse in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we discuss the Zelevinsky involution on multisegments and explain how it corresponds to an involution on orbits. We write down the Moeglin-Waldspurger algorithm to compute the involution on a multisegment, and use this notion to prove Lemma
4.8
. In section
5
, we adapt Lemma
4.8
to a statement about orbits in Lemma
5.1
. Using this lemma and the properties of the functor
\operatorname{Evs_{\psi}}
, we prove the conjecture for simple
A
-parameters in Theorem
5.3
. In section
6
, we use the process of hyper-unramification to generalize the result to an irreducible
A
-parameter.

In upcoming work, we expect to generalize Theorem
1
to arbitrary
A
-parameters for
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(F)
for a non-archimedean field
F
.

Since ABV-packets are expected to generalize Arthur packets, it is natural to ask - are all ABV-packets are singletons for
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
? The answer is no; one can see an ABV-packet of size
2
for a non Arthur-type Langlands parameter of
\operatorname{GL}_{16}
in
[
6
]
.

2.
A moduli space of Langlands parameters

Recall that
F
is a non-archimedean local field of residue characteristic
q=p^{f}
with
p
-adic absolute value denoted by
|\cdot|
and the uniformizer denoted by
\varpi
. The Weil group is
W_{F}=I_{F}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
, where
I_{F}
is the inertia subgroup and
\mathbb{Z}
is identified with the cyclic subgroup generated by the frobenius. From local class field theory, we have the local Artin map
\alpha:W_{F}\xrightarrow{}F^{\times}
that maps
I_{F}
to the group of units
\mathfrak{o}^{\times}
in
F^{\times}
and the geometric frobenius
\mathfrak{f}
to
1/\varpi
. We abuse notation and use
|\cdot|
to denote the absolute value on
W_{F}
, where
|w|:=|\alpha(w)|
. Thus,
|\mathfrak{f}|=|1/\varpi|=q
. We have that
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}(F)
, and we can work with
\widehat{G}=\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
instead of
\prescript{L}{}{G}
.

We consider
simple
Arthur parameters,
i.e.
, of the form

\displaystyle\psi:W_{F}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})

(3)

\displaystyle(w,x,y)

\displaystyle\mapsto\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(x)\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{a}(y),

where
a
and
d
are positive integers
1
1

1

Here we use
d
and
a
in our notation to refer to the fact that the first
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
in
W_{F}^{\prime\prime}=W_{F}\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is the “Deligne-
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
”,
i.e.
, it comes from Weil-Deligne representations, and the second
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
is the “Arthur-
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
”,
i.e.
, it comes from the definition of Arthur parameters.
.

For
w\in W_{F}
, let
d_{w}
denote the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}|w|^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\\
0&|w|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}
. The corresponding Langlands parameter is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{\psi}:W_{F}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})

(4)

\displaystyle(w,x)

\displaystyle\mapsto\psi(w,x,d_{w}).

One can simplify
\psi(w,x,d_{w})
to get

\phi_{\psi}(w,x)=|w|^{\frac{a}{2}}\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(x)\oplus|w|^{\frac{a-2}{2}}\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(x)\cdots\oplus|w|^{\frac{-a}{2}}\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(x).

The infinitesimal parameter associated to this Langlands parameter is given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{\phi_{\psi}}:W_{F}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})

(5)

\displaystyle(w,x)

\displaystyle\mapsto\phi_{\psi}(w,d_{w}).

One can simplify
\phi_{\psi}(w,d_{w})
to get

(6)

\lambda_{\phi_{\psi}}(w)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{a}\bigoplus_{j=0}^{d}|w|^{\frac{a+d}{2}-(i+j)}

We fix this infinitesimal parameter for the rest of this work and call it
\lambda
for simplicity. The element
\lambda(\mathfrak{f})\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is semisimple with eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}:=q^{e_{i}}
occurring with multiplicities
m_{i}
, where
0\leq i\leq k
, with
e_{i}=e_{i-1}-1
. Let us rewrite the matrix
\lambda(\mathfrak{f})
in a more instructive way:

(7)

\lambda(\mathfrak{f})=\begin{bmatrix}q^{e_{0}}I_{m_{0}}&0&\dots\\
0&q^{e_{1}}I_{m_{1}}&\dots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\\
0&&q^{e_{k}}I_{m_{k}}\end{bmatrix}.

We denote the corresponding eigenspaces by
E_{i}
. If we consider the
n
-dimensional vector space which is the standard representation of
\operatorname{Lie}(\widehat{G})
spanned by the usual basis
\{f_{1},...,f_{n}\}
, then
E_{0}
is spanned by
\{f_{1},...,f_{m_{0}}\}
,
E_{1}
by
\{f_{m_{0}+1},...,f_{m_{1}}\}
, and in general
E_{i}
by
\{f_{m_{i-1}+1},...,f_{m_{i}}\}
.

Following
[
14
]
and
[
5
]
, we describe the variety
V_{\lambda}
and group
H_{\lambda}
below:

(8)

V_{\lambda}:=\{X\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C}):\operatorname{Ad}(\lambda(w))X=|w|X,\text{ }\forall w\in W_{F}\}.

As the absolute value on
I_{F}
is trivial, any
X\in V_{\lambda}
is determined by the equation

(9)

\operatorname{Ad}(\lambda(\mathfrak{f}))X=qX.

V_{\lambda}
is referred to as the
Vogan variety
. In this case, it is easy to see the structure of this variety. Any matrix in
V_{\lambda}
is strictly upper triangular or trace zero so it can be identified as a vector subspace of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
.
\lambda(\mathfrak{f})
is an element inside the maximal torus in
\operatorname{SL}_{n}
. Using the Cartan decomposition of
\mathfrak{sl_{n}}
,
V_{\lambda}
can be identified as a sum of root spaces of
\mathfrak{sl_{n}}
such that the roots evaluate to
q
at
\lambda(\mathfrak{f})
. This shows that
V_{\lambda}
is simply an affine space. This fact is true more generally, as seen in
[
5
]
*Lemma 5.5.

Lemma 2.1
.

The Vogan variety
V_{\lambda}
can be decomposed as

(10)

V_{\lambda}=\operatorname{Hom}(E_{k},E_{k-1})\times\operatorname{Hom}(E_{k-1},E_{k-2})\times\cdots\times\operatorname{Hom}(E_{1},E_{0}).

Proof.

Let
X\in V_{\lambda}
and
v\in E_{i}
. By definition,
\lambda(\mathfrak{f})v=q^{e_{i}}v
and
\lambda(\mathfrak{f})X\lambda(\mathfrak{f})^{-1}=qX
. We show that
Xv\in E_{i-1}
. Indeed

\displaystyle\lambda(\mathfrak{f})Xv

\displaystyle=\lambda(\mathfrak{f})X\lambda(\mathfrak{f})^{-1}\cdot\lambda(\mathfrak{f})v,

\displaystyle=qX\cdot q^{e_{i}}v,

\displaystyle=q^{e_{i}+1}Xv.

As
E_{i-1}
is the eigenspace for
q^{e_{i}+1}
,

V_{\lambda}\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(E_{k},E_{k-1})\times\operatorname{Hom}(E_{k-1},E_{k-2})\times\cdots\times\operatorname{Hom}(E_{1},E_{0}).

Now we show the reverse containment. Let
T\in\operatorname{Hom}(E_{i},E_{i-1})
. If we view this as an endomorphism of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, then the
m_{i-1}\times m_{i}
matrix associated with
T
can be thought of as an
n\times n
matrix
(a_{ij})
such that
a_{ij}\neq 0
if
m_{i-2}+1\leq i\leq m_{i-1}\text{ and }m_{i-1}+1\leq j\leq m_{i}
, and
a_{ij}=0
otherwise. However, the adjoint (conjugation) action of
\lambda(\mathfrak{f})
simply multiplies all the nonzero entries
a_{ij}
by
q
. Thus,
\operatorname{Ad}(\lambda(\mathfrak{f}))\cdot(a_{ij})=q\cdot(a_{ij})
and the matrix
(a_{ij})
can be identified with an element of
V_{\lambda}
. This shows the reverse containment.
∎

The group
H_{\lambda}
is
Z_{\widehat{G}}(\lambda)
, the centralizer of the image of
\lambda
in
\widehat{G}
. Again, any
g\in H_{\lambda}
is determined by the equation

(11)

\operatorname{Ad}(\lambda(\mathfrak{f}))\cdot g=g.

Using the description of
\lambda(\mathfrak{f})
in
(
7
)
, we can directly see that

Z_{\widehat{G}}(\lambda(\mathfrak{f}))=\begin{bmatrix}\operatorname{GL}_{m_{0}}(\mathbb{C})&0&\dots\\
0&\operatorname{GL}_{m_{1}}(\mathbb{C})&\dots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\\
0&&\operatorname{GL}_{m_{k}}(\mathbb{C})\end{bmatrix}

We may identify
H_{\lambda}
as follows.

(12)

H_{\lambda}=\operatorname{Aut}(E_{k})\times\operatorname{Aut}(E_{k-1})\times\cdots\times\operatorname{Aut}(E_{0})

H_{\lambda}
acts on
V_{\lambda}
via conjugation. Using the interpretation from
(
10
)
and
(
12
)
, we describe the action as follows.
For elements in
V_{\lambda}
let us use the notation

x=(x_{kk-1},\cdots,x_{21},x_{10}),\text{ where }x_{ii-1}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{m_{i-1},m_{i}}(\mathbb{C}).

For elements of
H_{\lambda}
, we write

h=(h_{k},\cdots h_{1},h_{0}),\text{ where }h_{i}\in\operatorname{GL}_{m_{i}}(\mathbb{C}).

In this notation, the action
H_{\lambda}
on
V_{\lambda}
is given by

(13)

3.1.
Multisegments

The Langlands classification using multisegments is summarized beautifully in
[
11

*Section 1. We recall this classification in more detail following the notation in

. For any representation

\operatorname{GL}_{m}(F)

A multisegment is a collection of segments with repetitions allowed. The Langlands classification theorem tells us that any smooth irreducible representation of

3.3.
Correspondence between rank triangles and multisegments

We write down an algorithm to compute a rank triangle from a multisegment and vice-versa.

To each segment

with

A multisegment
\underline{m}
then determines the triangle

T_{\underline{m}}=\sum_{\Delta\in\underline{m}}T_{\Delta}.

We now explain how to pass from a rank triangle with eigenvalues

and multiplicities (top row)

to a multisegment with support

by the following inductive rule:.

(1)

Set
\underline{m}:=\emptyset
.

(2)

Let

be the lowest and most to the left non-zero entry in the rank triangle. Note that

.

(3)

Add the segment
\Delta=[e_{i},e_{j}]
to
\underline{m}
.

(4)

Replace

by

for each value of

with
j\leq l<k\leq i
.
Replace
m_{k}
by
m_{k}-1
for each

. That is, subtract
T_{\Delta}
from the rank triangle.

(5)

Repeat steps (ii) through (iv) until all

are zero.

(6)

If any

, add the singleton
\{e_{k}\}
with multiplicity
m_{k}
to
\underline{m}
.

Now
\underline{m}
is the multisegment determined by the rank triangle. These procedures,

establish a bijection between rank triangles and multisegments.

Let
\alpha

, where

4.1.
Moeglin-Waldspurger algorithm

In this section, write down the Moeglin-Waldpurger algorithm
[
12
]
to compute the Zelevinsky involution on multisegments for

. For this we recall what it means for one segment to precede the other. Let
\Delta_{1}=[b_{1},e_{1}]
and
\Delta_{2}=[b_{2},e_{2}]
be two segments.

Definition 4.1
.

\Delta_{1}
is said to
precede

\Delta_{2}
if and only if

b_{1}<b_{2}\text{, }e_{1}<e_{2},\text{ and }b_{2}\leq e_{1}+1.

We list the steps to compute
\tilde{\alpha}
below.

First, we compute the segment
M(\alpha)
associated to
\alpha
:

(1)

Set
e
to be the largest end value that appears in any segment. Set
m:=e
.

(2)

Consider all segments in
\alpha
with end value
m
. Among these, choose a segment with the largest base value and call it
\Delta_{m}
.

(3)

Consider the set of all segments in
\alpha
that precede
\Delta_{m}
with end value
m-1
. If this is empty, go to step
(5)
. Otherwise, choose a segment from this set with the largest base value and call it
\Delta_{m-1}
.

(4)

Set
m:=m-1
and go to step (3).

(5)

Return
M(\alpha):=[m,e]
.

Next, we inherit the following notation from the above procedure: for any
i\in\{m,m+1,\cdots,e\}
, set
\Delta_{i}
as the segment whose base value is the highest among segments with end value
i
. Define
\alpha\setminus M(\alpha)
to be the multisegment obtained by removing

from
\Delta_{i}
for all
m\leq i\leq e
. Then, the dual
\tilde{\alpha}
is recursively defined via

\tilde{\alpha}:=(M(\alpha),\widetilde{\alpha\setminus M(\alpha)}).

The fact that this procedure gives you an involution comes from
[

]
. In particular, we have the useful property that
\tilde{\tilde{\alpha}}=\alpha
.

4.2.
Lemma about simple multisegments

The aim of this section is to prove Lemma
4.8
. We establish some terminology and notation associated to a multisegment
\alpha
. The length of a segment
[b,e]
is simply the value
e-b+1
.

L_{\alpha}
:= Length of the longest segment in
\alpha
.

n_{\alpha}
:= number of segments in
\alpha

c_{\alpha}
:= minimum number of segments that
\bigcup_{\Delta\in\alpha}\Delta
can be broken into.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be any multisegments. If
\alpha\leq\beta
, then

(1)

L_{\alpha}\leq L_{\beta}
.

(2)

n_{\alpha}\geq n_{\beta}.

(3)

n_{\tilde{\alpha}}\geq L_{\alpha}
and
n_{\alpha}\geq L_{\tilde{\alpha}}
.

Proof.

(1)

Since
\beta
is obtained by performing elementary operations on segments in
\alpha
, the longest segment in
\beta
is either a segment from
\alpha
or a union of two segments in
\alpha
. Therefore,
L_{\beta}\geq L_{\alpha}.

(2)

Every pair of segments in
\alpha
is replaced by at most two segments to form
\beta
. Therefore
n_{\beta}\leq n_{\alpha}
.

(3)

Fix a segment in
\alpha
. From the Moeglin-Waldspurger algorithm, one can observe that each element of that segment appears in a distinct segment in
\tilde{\alpha}
. In particular, this is true for the longest segment of
\alpha
. Thus, we get
n_{\tilde{\alpha}}\geq L_{\alpha}
. Since
\tilde{\tilde{\alpha}}=\alpha
, we get the other inequality.

∎

Definition 4.4
.

\alpha
is a simple multisegment if it is of the form

\{[b,e],[b+1,e+1],\cdots,[b+n-1,e+n-1]\}.

Observe that
n
in the definition above is equal to
n_{\alpha}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\alpha
is a simple multisegment, then

(1)

\tilde{\alpha}
is simple.

(2)

n_{\tilde{\alpha}}=L_{\alpha}
.

(3)

c_{\alpha}=1
.

Proof.

(1)

Applying the Moeglin-Waldspurger algorithm, one can explicitly compute

\tilde{\alpha}=\{[e-k,e+n_{\alpha}-k-1]:0\leq k\leq e-b\}.

Clearly, it is simple.

(2)

Observe that the length of every segment in
\alpha
is the same, and
L_{\alpha}=e-b+1
. Observing how the index
k
varies in part
(1)
tells us that
n_{\alpha}=e-b+1
.

(3)

c_{\alpha}=1
because

\bigcup_{\Delta\in\alpha}\Delta=[b,e+n-1].

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

If
L_{\tilde{\alpha}}=n_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\leq\beta
, and
\tilde{\alpha}\leq\tilde{\beta}
, then

n_{\alpha}=n_{\beta}=L_{\tilde{\alpha}}=L_{\tilde{\beta}}.

The above statement also holds true if
\alpha
is interchanged with
\tilde{\alpha}
and
\beta
with
\tilde{\beta}
.

Proof.

By hypothesis,
\tilde{\alpha}\leq\tilde{\beta}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle L_{\tilde{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\tilde{\beta}},\text{ (from Lemma \ref{lemma1})}

\displaystyle\leq n_{\tilde{\tilde{\beta}}},\text{ (from Lemma \ref{lemma1}) }

\displaystyle=n_{\beta},\text{ (since $\tilde{\tilde{\beta}}=\beta$)}

\displaystyle\leq n_{\alpha},\text{ (hypothesis and Lemma \ref{lemma1})}

\displaystyle=L_{\tilde{\alpha}}.\text{ (hypothesis)}

∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\alpha
be a simple multisegment and
\beta
any multisegment such that
\alpha\leq\beta
and
L_{\alpha}\leq L_{\beta}
. Then,
\alpha=\beta
.

Proof.

Suppose
\alpha<\beta
. Then, there is a segment in
\beta
of the form
\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}
strictly containing
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
, where
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}\in\alpha
. Now
\alpha
is simple, so every segment has the same length and that length is equal to

. Thus, the strict containment above implies

L_{\beta}\geq\text{ length of }\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}>\text{ length of }\Delta_{1}=L_{\alpha},

which contradicts the hypothesis.
∎

Now we are ready to state the primary result of this section.

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose

is a simple multisegment and
\beta

Proof.

\alpha

. Lemma

\Lambda\subseteq T^{*}(V)
, is defined as the kernel of the map
[\cdot,\cdot]:T^{*}(V)\xrightarrow{}\operatorname{Lie}(H)
.

By
[
5
]
*Proposition 6.3.1, for each
H
-orbit
C
in
V
,

(17)

\Lambda_{C}:=\{(x,y)\in\Lambda:x\in C\}

is the conormal bundle to
C
. By identifying
V^{**}\simeq V
, for an
H
-orbit
C^{*}
in
V^{*}
we may write

\Lambda_{C^{*}}=\{(x,y)\in\Lambda:x\in C^{*}\}

where
\Lambda_{C^{*}}
is the conormal bundle to
C^{*}
.

For any
C
, there exists a unique orbit
C^{*}
in

with
\bar{\Lambda}_{C}\simeq\bar{\Lambda}_{C^{*}}
; see
[
13
]
*Corollary 2. The map

defines a bijection the set of

-orbits in

and the set of
H
-orbits in

. Using this, we may define

\widehat{C}:=\prescript{t}{}{C^{*}},

where

is matrix transposition. Then

In section

, we wrote down the Zelevinsky involution on multisegments and in this section we see an involution on orbits. Since multisegments and orbits correspond to each other via rank triangles as seen in Section

[

Example 4.9
.

5.
Vogan’s conjecture for simple Arthur parameters

Recall the simple Arthur parameter
\psi
defined in (
2
), the Langlands parameter
\phi_{\psi}
coming from it defined in (
2
), and its infinitesimal parameter
\lambda
defined in (
2
). We remind the reader that
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}
. Now,
\pi\in\Pi(G(F))
is said to have the infinitesimal parameter
\lambda
if its corresponding Langlands parameter
\phi
has the infinitesimal parameter
\lambda
. Let
\Pi_{\lambda}(G(F))
denote all such
\pi
and
\Phi_{\lambda}(G(F))
denote equivalence classes of all such
\phi
. As outlined in sections
1.4
and
2
, equivalence classes of smooth irreducible representations of
G(F)
with infinitesimal parameter
\lambda
correspond to equivalence classes of simple objects in
\operatorname{Per}_{H}(V)
. This bijection is summarized below:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Pi_{\lambda}(G(F))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Phi_{\lambda}(G(F))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H\operatorname{-orbits}\operatorname{in}V}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Perv}_{H}(V)^{\operatorname{simple}}_{/\operatorname{iso}},}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi\hskip 5.69054pt}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\hskip 5.69054ptC_{\phi}\hskip 5.69054pt}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\hskip 5.69054pt\mathcal{P}(\pi)=\mathcal{IC}(\mathbb{1}_{C_{\phi}}).}}

Thus, there is a unique orbit
C_{\phi_{\psi}}
in
V
attached to
\phi_{\psi}
. We shorten this notation to
C_{\psi}
.
This orbit in turn corresponds to a simple multisegment - one can see this directly by writing down the multisegment for the Langlands parameter
\phi_{\psi}
when
\psi(w,x,y)=\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(x)\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{a}(y)
:

(18)

\left\{\left[\tfrac{-(d+a)}{2},\tfrac{-(d+a)}{2}+d\right],\left[\tfrac{-(d+a)}{2}+1,\tfrac{-(d+a)}{2}+d+1\right],\ldots,\left[\tfrac{-(d+a)}{2}+a,\tfrac{-(d+a)}{2}+d+a\right]\right\}.

The dictionary between Langlands parameters and multisegments is written down in many places, see
[
11
]
*Section 4.2, for example. In Section
3.3
we saw that rank triangles and multisegments correspond to each other and this correspondence respects the partial order on both sides. In section
4.3
, we see that this correspondence respects the involution on both sides. Thus, we may adapt Lemma
4.8
as follows:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
C_{\psi}
be the orbit corresponding to a simple Arthur parameter as explained above. Let
C
be any
H
-orbit in
V
. If
C_{\psi}\leq C
and
\widehat{C}_{\psi}\leq\widehat{C}
, then
C_{\psi}=C
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
C_{\psi}
be the orbit corresponding to a simple Arthur parameter. Then

\widehat{C}_{\psi}=C_{\widehat{\psi}},

where
{\widehat{\psi}}
is the the simple Arthur parameter obtained by interchanging the role of the two
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
’s in
W_{F}^{\prime\prime}
:

\widehat{\psi}(w,x,y):=\psi(w,y,x).

Proof.

Since
\psi
is simple,
C_{\psi}
corresponds to the multisegment written in (
18
). The dual of this multisegment, in the sense of
4.1
, is

\left\{\left[\tfrac{-(d+a)}{2},\tfrac{-(d+a)}{2}+a\right],\left[\tfrac{-(d+a)}{2}+1,\tfrac{-(d+a)}{2}+a+1\right],\ldots,\left[\tfrac{-(d+a)}{2}+d,\tfrac{-(d+a)}{2}+d+a\right]\right\}.

By inspection, this multisegment comes from the simple Arthur parameter
(w,x,y)\mapsto\operatorname{Sym}^{a}(x)\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(y)
. From the discussion in
4.3
, this multisegment also corresponds to
\widehat{C}_{\psi}
, as required.
∎

We recall the functor defined in (
1.5
):

\operatorname{Evs}_{\psi}:\operatorname{Per}_{H_{\lambda_{\psi}}}(V_{\lambda_{\psi}})\xrightarrow{}\operatorname{Rep}(A_{\psi}),.

For the Langlands parameter
\phi_{\psi}
, we recall the ABV-packet from (
2
):

\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}}(G(F)):=\{\pi\in\Pi_{\lambda}(G(F)):\operatorname{Evs}_{\psi}(\mathcal{P}(\pi))\neq 0\}.

We now state and prove the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\psi
be a simple Arthur parameter of
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}
,
\phi_{\psi}
its corresponding Langlands parameter,
\Pi_{\psi}(G(F))
the Arthur packet attached to
\psi
, and
\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}}(G(F))
the ABV-packet attached to
\phi_{\psi}
. Then,

\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}}(G(F))=\Pi_{\psi}(G(F)).

Proof.

The local Langlands correspondence tells us that
L
-packets are singletons. As discussed in
1.3
, the Arthur packet
\Pi_{\psi}(G(F))
is a singleton and it coincides with the
L
-packet for
\phi_{\psi}
. We may write

\Pi_{\psi}(G(F))=\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}(G(F))=\{\pi_{\psi}\}.

Let
C_{\psi}
be the unique orbit associated to the Langlands parameter
\phi_{\psi}
. By
[
5
]
*Theorem 7.19,
\operatorname{Evs}_{\psi}\mathcal{IC}(\mathbb{1}_{C_{\psi}})\neq 0
. By definition, this implies

\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}(G(F))\subseteq\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}}(G(F)),

and therefore

\Pi_{\psi}(G(F))\subseteq\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}}(G(F)).

To prove the theorem, it is sufficient to prove that
\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}}(G(F))
is a singleton,
i.e.
, it contains no representation other that
\{\pi_{\psi}\}
.

Suppose we have a representation
\pi\in\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}}(G(F))
. By definition, this means

(19)

\operatorname{Evs}_{\psi}(\mathcal{P}(\pi))\neq 0.

From the discussion in sections
1.4
and
2
, we know that
\pi
corresponds to an
H
-orbit
C
in
V
and
\mathcal{P}(\pi)=\mathcal{IC}(\mathbb{1}_{C})
. We may rephrase (
19
) as

(20)

\operatorname{Evs}_{\psi}\mathcal{IC}({\mathbbm{1}}_{C})\neq 0.

Using
[
6
]
*Proposition 3.2.1 and Lemma
5.2
, (
19
) also implies that

(21)

\operatorname{Evs}_{\widehat{\psi}}\mathcal{IC}({\mathbbm{1}}_{\widehat{C}})\neq 0.

Again from
[
6
]
*Proposition 3.2.1, (
20
) implies that
C_{\psi}\leq C
and (
21
) implies
\widehat{C}_{\psi}\leq\widehat{C}
. The hypothesis of Lemma
5.1
is satisfied, so we may conclude that
C_{\psi}=C
. The corresponding representations are the same, as required.

∎

6.
Vogan’s conjecture for irreducible Arthur parameters

We now extend the result in Theorem
5.3
to an
irreducible
Arthur parameter of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
,
i.e.
,
of the form

(22)

\psi(w,x,y)=\rho(w)\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(x)\operatorname{Sym}^{a}(y),

where
\rho
is an irreducible admissible representation of
W_{F}
of dimension
m
, and
a
and
d
are non-negative integers. First note that
\rho=\rho_{0}\otimes|\cdot|^{s}
, where
\rho_{0}
is of Galois type and
|\cdot|
is the absolute value on the Weil group as explained in section
2
.

We apply the procedure of hyper-unramification as explained in
[
5
]
*Section 5 on the infinitesimal parameter
\lambda
. Note that
\lambda(w)=\rho_{0}(w)\otimes|w|^{s}\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(d_{w})\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{a}(d_{w})
where
d_{w}
is defined in Section
2
. For any
X\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, set
\eta(X):=\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(X)\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{a}(X)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

The semisimple element
\lambda(\mathfrak{f})
has the decomposition
\lambda(\mathfrak{f})=s_{\lambda}t_{\lambda}
where

s_{\lambda}=\rho_{0}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\otimes|\mathfrak{f}|^{\operatorname{Re}(s)}\otimes\eta(d_{\mathfrak{f}})

is the hyperbolic part and

t_{\lambda}=\rho_{0}(\mathfrak{f})\otimes|\mathfrak{f}|^{i\operatorname{Im}(s)}\otimes\eta\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

is the elliptic part.

Proof.

Clearly,
s_{\lambda}t_{\lambda}=t_{\lambda}s_{\lambda}=\lambda(\mathfrak{f})
. To show that
s_{\lambda}
is hyperbolic, it suffices to show that its eigenvalues are positive real numbers. Indeed, after
s_{\lambda}
is identified with a matrix, its eigenvalues are of the form
q^{\operatorname{Re}s+\frac{a+d}{2}+j}
where
j
is an integer. This can be seen from direct calculation aided by Equation
6
. To show that
t_{\lambda}
is elliptic, it suffices to show that its eigenvalues have complex norm 1. Indeed,
t_{\lambda}
can be identified with the (block) matrix

\begin{bmatrix}\rho_{0}(\mathfrak{f})q^{i\operatorname{Im}s}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\rho_{0}(\mathfrak{f})q^{i\operatorname{Im}s}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\\
0&\cdots&0&\rho_{0}(\mathfrak{f})q^{i\operatorname{Im}s}\end{bmatrix},

so complex norm of its eigenvalues are determined by those of
\rho_{0}(\mathfrak{f})
. However,
\rho_{0}
is of Galois-type which means it has finite image. Thus
\rho_{0}(\mathfrak{f})
is a matrix of finite order, whose eigenvalues must be roots of unity.

∎

The process of hyper-unramification described in
[
5
]
*Section 5.3 adapted to our case gives us an unramified parameter

\lambda_{nr}:W_{F}\xrightarrow{}J_{\lambda}
,
where
J_{\lambda}
is
Z_{\widehat{G}}(\lambda|_{I_{F}},t_{\lambda})
whose image of frobenius is the hyperbolic element
s_{\lambda}
. Using
[
5
]
*Lemma 5.3 we have that

V_{\lambda_{nr}}=V_{\lambda},\text{ and }H_{\lambda_{nr}}=H_{\lambda}^{0}.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose
\psi
is an irreducible Arthur parameter as defined in Equation
22
. Then,

(1)

J_{\lambda}=\operatorname{GL}_{n/m}(\mathbb{C})
.

(2)

H_{\lambda_{nr}}=H_{\lambda}

Proof.

(1)

By definition,
J_{\lambda}=Z_{\hat{G}}(\lambda|_{I_{F}},t_{\lambda})
. Now
\lambda(w)|_{I_{F}}=\rho(w)|_{I_{F}}\otimes\eta(d_{w})|_{I_{F}}
. Since
I_{F}
consists of elements of absolute value 1,
\lambda(w)|_{I_{F}}=\rho_{0}(w)|_{I_{F}}\otimes\eta\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
. As
\rho_{0}
is of Galois-type, it is determined by a finite list of elements. In particular, there are elements
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{k}
in
I_{F}
so that their images (block matrices) of the form

\begin{bmatrix}\rho_{0}(\sigma_{i})&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\rho_{0}(\sigma_{i})&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\\
0&0&\cdots&\rho_{0}(\sigma_{i})\end{bmatrix}

determine
\rho_{0}|_{I_{F}}
.

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=Z_{\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}(\lambda(w)|_{I_{F}},t_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\{X\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}):t_{\lambda}Xt_{\lambda}^{-1}=X\text{ and }\lambda(\sigma_{i})X\lambda(\sigma_{i})^{-1}=X\text{ for }1\leq i\leq k\}

\displaystyle=\{X=(X_{rs})_{r,s}\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\text{ where }X_{rs}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{m}(\mathbb{C}):\rho_{0}(\mathfrak{f})X_{rs}\rho_{0}(\mathfrak{f})^{-1}=X_{rs}\text{ and }\rho_{0}(\sigma_{i})X_{rs}\rho_{0}(\sigma_{i})^{-1}=X_{rs}\}

\displaystyle=\{X=(X_{rs})_{r,s}\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\text{ where }X_{rs}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{m}(\mathbb{C}):X_{rs}\in Z_{\operatorname{Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}(\rho_{0})\}

\displaystyle=\{X=(c_{rs}\operatorname{Id}_{m})_{r,s}\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\text{ where }c_{rs}\in\mathbb{C}\}

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{GL}_{n/m}(\mathbb{C}).

The first equality is by definition, the second by the fact that
\lambda|_{I_{F}}
is determined by
\sigma_{i}s
as explained above, the third follows by writing down
X,t_{\lambda}
, and
\lambda(\sigma_{i})s
as block matrices, the fourth follows from the fact that
\rho_{0}
is completely determined by
\mathfrak{f}
and
\sigma_{i}
s, and the fifth follows from Schur’s lemma applied to the irreducible representation
\rho_{0}
for every
r,s
. The isomorphism in the sixth step comes from realizing
(c_{rs}\operatorname{Id}_{m})_{r,s}
as
(c_{rs})_{r,s}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{m}
whose determinant is
\operatorname{det}((c_{rs})_{r,s})^{m}
using the determinant of the Kronecker product of two matrices.

(2)

As centralizers in
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
are connected,
H_{\lambda}
is connected so
H_{\lambda_{nr}}=H_{\lambda}^{0}=H_{\lambda}
.

∎

The above lemma shows that if we replace
J_{\lambda}
with the isomorphic general linear group, we may interpret the unramified infinitesimal parameter as a homomorphism
W_{F}\xrightarrow{}\operatorname{GL}_{n/m}(\mathbb{C})
whose image at the frobenius can now be thought of as
|\mathfrak{f}|^{\operatorname{Re}s}\otimes\eta(d_{\mathfrak{f}})
, and whose Vogan variety and group acting on it remain the same as that of
\lambda
. Also observe that removing the factor
|\mathfrak{f}|^{\operatorname{Re}s}
from this infinitesimal parameter does not change the variety and the group acting on it for trivial reasons. We collect these observations on hyper-unramification of
\psi
as defined in
22
in the following lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose
\psi
is an
irreducible
Arthur parameter of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
,
i.e.
,

\psi(w,x,y)=\rho(w)\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(x)\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{a}(y),

where
\rho
is an irreducible representation of
W_{F}
in
\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
. We obtain the unramified parameter

\lambda_{nr}:W_{F}\xrightarrow{}\operatorname{GL}_{n/m}(\mathbb{C})

defined via
w\mapsto\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(d_{w})\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{a}(d_{w})
, with

V_{\lambda_{nr}}\simeq V_{\lambda},\text{ and }H_{\lambda_{nr}}\simeq H_{\lambda},

where these isomorphisms are obtained by an application of Schur’s lemma, as in the proof of Lemma
6.2
(1).

The upshot is that
\lambda_{nr}
can be thought of as the infinitesimal parameter of
\psi_{nr}(w,x,y):=\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(x)\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{a}(y)
, after an application of the isomorphisms
V_{\lambda_{nr}}\simeq V_{\lambda}
and
H_{\lambda_{nr}}\simeq H_{\lambda}
that are induced by taking scalar matrices to their respective scalars in a smaller matrix group.
Since the variety and group action are unchanged, the category
\operatorname{Per}_{H_{\lambda_{nr}}}(V_{\lambda_{nr}})=\operatorname{Per}_{H_{\lambda}}(V_{\lambda})
. As a result, we directly see that the isomorphisms give us a bijection
C\mapsto C_{nr}
of orbits which preserves the ordering and the Zelevinsky involution. The geometric side of the Vogan-Langlands correspondence can thus be reduced to the study of the unramified infinitesimal parameter. This paves the way for generalizing Theorem
5.3
from simple Arthur parameters to irreducible Arthur parameters.

Theorem 6.4
.

Suppose
\psi
is an irreducible Arthur parameter of
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}
, i.e.,

\psi(w,x,y)=\rho(w)\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(x)\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{a}(y),

where
\rho
is an irreducible representation of
W_{F}
on
\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
. Let
\Pi_{\psi}(G(F))
denote the local Arthur packet of
\psi
and
\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}}(G(F))
denote the ABV-packet attached to
\phi_{\psi}
. Then,

\Pi_{\psi}(G(F))=\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}}(G(F)).

Proof.

From Lemma
6.3
,
V_{\lambda}\simeq V_{\lambda_{nr}}
and
H_{\lambda}\simeq H_{\lambda_{nr}}
. We have a bijection
C\mapsto C_{nr}
between orbits in
V_{\lambda}
and
V_{\lambda_{nr}}
which preserves partial order and the Zelevinsky involution on orbits. This gives a bijection
\pi\mapsto\pi_{nr}
between
\Pi_{\lambda}(G(F))
and
\Pi_{\lambda_{nr}}(G(F))
preserving the partial order and involution on multisegments such that
(C_{\pi})_{nr}=C_{\pi_{nr}}
. In particular,
(C_{\psi})_{nr}=C_{\psi_{nr}}
. Since
\operatorname{Evs}_{\psi}
is purely dependent on the geometry, we have that
\operatorname{Evs}_{\psi}\mathcal{IC}(\mathbb{1}_{C_{\pi}})\neq 0
for
\pi\in\Pi_{\lambda}(G(F))
if and only if
\operatorname{Evs}_{\psi_{nr}}\mathcal{IC}(\mathbb{1}_{C_{\pi_{nr}}})\neq 0
for the corresponding
\pi_{nr}\in\Pi_{\lambda_{nr}}(G(F))
. Since the ABV-packet for
\psi_{nr}
is a singleton from Theorem
5.3
, same is true for the ABV packet for
\psi
.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

The process of hyper-unramification taking
\psi\mapsto\psi_{nr}
gives us a bijection between
\Pi_{\lambda}(G(F))
and
\Pi_{\lambda_{nr}}(G(F))
which can be written down explicitly in terms of multisegments. Let
\pi_{\rho}
be the supercuspidal representation of
\operatorname{GL}_{m}(F)
corresponding to the irreducible representation
\rho
. The correspondence at the level of multisegments is induced by taking any segment of the form
[\pi_{\rho}(b),\pi_{\rho}(e)]
to the segment
[b,e]
.

1.
introduction and preliminary

Let
\mathbb{M}_{n}
denote the set of
n\times n
complex matrices and
A^{*}
denote the conjugate transpose of
A
for

A\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
.

A Hermitian matrix
A\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
is called positive semidefinite (denoted by
A\geqslant 0
) if
\langle Ax,x\rangle\geqslant 0
for all vectors
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. If in addition
A
is invertible, then
A
is called positive definite (denoted by

A>0
).

A matrix
A\in M_{n}
is called accretive if its real part
\Re A=\frac{A+A^{*}}{2}
is positive definite.

If
A
is accretive matrix and
W(A)
is the numerical range of
A
, then

W(A)\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\Re z>0,|\Im z|\leqslant(\Re z)\tan\theta\}.

for some
0\leqslant\theta<\frac{\pi}{2}
. In this case, we will write
A\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta}
.

By definition, it is easily seen that
A\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta}
is invertible and we have the following well-known inequality
[
13
,
14
]
:

\Re(A^{-1})\leqslant\Re^{-1}(A)\leqslant\sec^{2}\theta\ \Re(A^{-1}).

(1.1)

An operator mean
\sigma
in the sense of Kubo-Ando
[
12
]
is defined by an
operator monotone function
f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
with
f(1)=1
(briefly we write
f\in\mathbf{m}
) as

A\sigma B=A^{1/2}f(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})A^{1/2},

for positive invertible matrices
A
and
B
. The function
f
is called the representing function of
\sigma
. Recently, Bedrani, Kittaneh and sababheh in
[
3
]
proved that this definition can be used for accretive matrices too.

For
0\leqslant v\leqslant 1
, the
v
-weighted arithmetic, geometric and Harmonic means are defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle A\nabla_{v}B=vA+(1-v)B,

\displaystyle A\sharp_{v}B=A^{1/2}(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})^{v}A^{1/2},

\displaystyle A!_{v}B=(vA^{-1}+(1-v)B^{-1})^{-1}.

Let
\sigma
be an operator mean with representing function
f.
The operator
mean with representing function
f(t^{-1})^{-1}
is called the
adjoint of
\sigma
and denoted by
\sigma^{*}.
It follows
from definition that

A\sigma^{*}B=(A^{-1}\sigma B^{-1})^{-1}.

For two operator means
\sigma_{f}
and
\sigma_{g}
with representing functions
f
and
g
, we write
\sigma\leqslant\tau
if
A\sigma B\leqslant A\tau B
for every two positive matrices
A
and
B
or equivalently if
f(t)\leqslant g(t)
for all positive
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

A linear map
\Phi:M_{n}\rightarrow M_{n}
is called positive if

\Phi(A)\geq 0
whenever
A\geq 0
. If
\Phi(I)=I
, where
I
denoted the identity matrix, then we say that
\Phi
is unital.

Khosravi et al. in
[
11
]
prove that if
0<mI\leqslant A,B\leqslant MI
, then for every positive unital linear map
\Phi
and operator means
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
between some
\sigma
and
\sigma^{*}
,

\Phi^{p}(A\sigma B)\leqslant\alpha^{p}\Phi^{p}(A\sigma^{*}B),

(1.2)

where
p>0
,
\alpha=\max\{K,4^{1-\frac{2}{p}}K\}
and
K=\frac{(M+m)^{2}}{4mM}
is the Kantorovich constant.

In addition, they showed that if
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
between
\nabla_{\nu}
and
!_{\nu}
, then

f\left(\Phi(A))\sigma_{1}f(\Phi(B)\right)\leqslant Kf\left(\Phi(A\sigma_{2}B)\right),

(1.3)

for every nonzero operator monotone function
f
on
[0,\infty)
.

The main purpose of this article is to present the sectorial version of these inequalities.

Throughout,
K
considered as the Kantorovich constant
\frac{(M+m)^{2}}{4mM}
.

2.
main results

We start this section with some lemmas which are necessary for proving our main results.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
3
]

Let
A,B\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta}
. Then
A\sigma B\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta}
and

\Re A\sigma\Re B\leqslant\Re(A\sigma B)\leqslant\sec^{2}\theta(\Re A\sigma\Re B).

Also we have the
famous Choi’s inequality:

Lemma 2.2
.

[
19
]

Let
A\in B(H)
be positive. Then, for every positive unital linear map
\Phi
,

\displaystyle\Phi^{-1}(A)\leqslant\Phi(A^{-1}).

By these lemmas, we can prove the next proposition.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta}
for some
0\leqslant\theta\leqslant\pi/2
such that
0<mI\leqslant\Re A,\Re B\leqslant MI
. If
\sigma
is an arbitrary mean and
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
are two operator means between
\sigma
and
\sigma^{*}
, then

\cos^{2}\theta\Phi\Re(A\sigma_{1}B)+mM\Phi^{-1}\Re(A\sigma_{2}B)\leqslant(M+m)I

(2.1)

for every positive unital linear map
\Phi
.

Proof.

From
0<mI\leqslant\Re A\leqslant MI
, it follows that

(MI-\Re A)(\frac{1}{m}I-\Re^{-1}A)\geq 0,

which is equivalent to

\Re A+Mm\Re^{-1}(A)\leqslant(M+m)I.

Similarly

\Re B+Mm\Re^{-1}(B)\leqslant(M+m)I.

Since every operator mean is subadditive and monotone, we have

\displaystyle\Re A\sigma\Re B+mM(\Re^{-1}(A)\sigma\Re^{-1}(B))

\displaystyle\leqslant(\Re A+Mm\Re^{-1}(A))\sigma(\Re B+Mm\Re^{-1}(B))

\displaystyle\leqslant(M+m)I\sigma(M+m)I

\displaystyle=(M+m)I

Thus, for a positive unital linear map
\Phi,
we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi(\Re A\sigma\Re B)+mM\Phi(\Re^{-1}(A)\sigma\Re^{-1}(B))\leqslant(M+m)I.

Let
\sigma^{*}\leqslant\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\leqslant\sigma
. Using Lemma
2.1
and Choi’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta\Phi\Re(A\sigma_{1}B)+mM\Phi^{-1}\Re(A\sigma_{2}B)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Phi(\Re A\sigma_{1}\Re B)+mM\Phi^{-1}(\Re A\sigma_{2}\Re B)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Phi(\Re A\sigma\Re B)+mM\Phi^{-1}(\Re A\sigma^{*}\Re B)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Phi(\Re A\sigma\Re B)+mM\Phi((\Re A\sigma^{*}\Re B)^{-1})

\displaystyle=\Phi(\Re A\sigma\Re B)+mM\Phi(\Re^{-1}A\sigma\Re^{-1}B)

\displaystyle\leqslant(M+m)I,

and this complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

[
6
]

Let
A,B\geq 0
. Then

\|AB\|\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\|A+B\|^{2}.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
12
]

For each
A,B>0
and
p>1,

\|A^{p}+B^{p}\|\leqslant\|(A+B)^{p}\|.

Now we are ready to prove our main results which is an extension of (
1.2
) for accretive matrices.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta}
such that
0<mI\leqslant\Re A,\Re B\leqslant MI
and
\Phi
be a positive unital linear map. If
\sigma
is an operator mean and
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
are two operator means between
\sigma
and
\sigma^{*}
, then

\displaystyle\Re^{p}\Phi(A\sigma_{1}B)\leqslant\sec^{2p}\theta\ K^{p}\Re^{p}\Phi(A\sigma_{2}B),

\displaystyle(p\leqslant 2)

\displaystyle\Re^{p}\Phi(A\sigma_{1}B)\leqslant\sec^{2p}\theta 4^{p-2}K^{p}\Re^{p}\Phi(A\sigma_{2}B).

\displaystyle(p\geq 2)

Proof.

For the first inequality, by Lowner-Heinz inequality, it is enough to prove the inequality for
p=2
.

Applying Lemma
2.4
and inequality (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\|\cos^{2}\theta\Phi\Re(A\sigma_{1}B)

\displaystyle\Phi^{-1}\Re(A\sigma_{2}B)\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{4mM}\|\cos^{2}\theta\Phi\Re(A\sigma_{1}B)+mM\Phi^{-1}\Re(A\sigma_{2}B)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{(M+m)^{2}}{4mM}=K,

which is equivalent to

\cos^{4}\theta\Phi^{2}\Re(A\sigma_{1}B)\leqslant K^{2}\Phi^{2}\Re(A\sigma_{2}B).

(2.2)

Now, if
p\geqslant 2,
by Lemmas
2.1
,
2.5
and inequality (
2.1
), respectively

\displaystyle\|\cos^{p}\theta

\displaystyle\Phi^{\frac{p}{2}}\Re(A\sigma_{1}B)M^{\frac{p}{2}}m^{\frac{p}{2}}\Phi^{-\frac{p}{2}}\Re(A\sigma_{2}B)\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\left\|\cos^{p}\theta\Phi^{\frac{p}{2}}\Re(A\sigma_{1}B)+M^{\frac{p}{2}}m^{\frac{p}{2}}\Phi^{-\frac{p}{2}}\Re(A\sigma_{2}B)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\left\|\cos^{2}\theta\Phi\Re(A\sigma_{1}B)+Mm\Phi^{-1}\Re(A\sigma_{2}B)\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{4}(M+m)^{p}.\

Hence,

\|\cos^{p}\theta\Phi^{\frac{p}{2}}\Re(A\sigma_{1}B)\Phi^{-\frac{p}{2}}\Re(A\sigma_{2}B)\|\leqslant\left(\dfrac{(M+m)^{2}}{4^{\frac{2}{p}}Mm}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

This means that

\cos^{2p}\theta\Phi^{p}\Re(A\sigma_{1}B)\leqslant\left(\dfrac{(M+m)^{2}}{4^{\frac{2}{p}}Mm}\right)^{p}\Phi^{p}\Re(A\sigma_{2}B).

∎

This result was proved in
[
3
]
for
p=2
with coefficient
\sec^{12}\theta
and we find a sharper inequality with coefficient
\sec^{4}\theta
.

Furthermore, if in Theorem
2.6
, we put
p=2
,
\sigma_{1}=\nabla_{v}
and
\sigma_{2}=\sharp_{v}
, we get a refinement of some results in
[
18
]
and
[
21
]
.

Going back to the proof of Proposition
2.3
, if we put
\sigma_{1}=\nabla_{v}
, then
\Re A\sigma_{1}\Re B=\Re(A\sigma_{1}B)
. Thus the coefficient
\cos\theta
does not appear. Therefore, we have the following result.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta}
such that
0<mI\leqslant\Re A,\Re B\leqslant MI
and
\Phi
be a positive unital linear map. If
!_{v}\leqslant\sigma\leqslant\nabla_{v}
, then

\displaystyle\Re^{p}\Phi(A\nabla_{v}B)\leqslant\alpha^{p}\Re^{p}\Phi(A\sigma B),

where
\alpha=\max\{K,4^{1-\frac{2}{p}}K\}
.

In addition, (
1.1
), helps us to prove another version of Theorem
2.6
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta}
such that
0<mI\leqslant\Re A^{-1},\Re B^{-1}\leqslant MI
and
\sigma
be an arbitrary mean. Then for every positive unital linear map
\Phi
and
\sigma^{*}\leqslant\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\leqslant\sigma
,

\Phi^{p}\Re(A\sigma_{1}B)\leqslant\alpha^{p}\sec^{4p}\theta\Phi^{p}\Re(A\sigma_{2}B),

where
\alpha=\max\{K,4^{1-\frac{2}{p}}K\}
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove

Mm\Phi\Re(A\sigma_{1}B)+\Phi^{-1}\Re(A\sigma_{2}B)\leqslant\sec^{2}\theta\ (M+m)I.

(2.3)

The rest of proof is similar to Theorem
2.6
.

As in the proof of Proposition
2.3
,

\Re A^{-1}\sigma\Re B^{-1}+mM(\Re^{-1}A^{-1}\sigma\Re^{-1}B^{-1})\leqslant(M+m)I.

From this inequality and (
1.1
), it follows that

\Re A^{-1}\sigma\Re B^{-1}+Mm(\Re A\sigma\Re B)\leqslant(M+m)I.

Lemma
2.1
leads to

\Re(A\sigma_{1}B)\leqslant\sec^{2}\theta(\Re A\sigma_{1}\Re B)\leqslant\sec^{2}\theta(\Re A\sigma\Re B).

(2.4)

In addition,

\displaystyle\Phi^{-1}\Re(A\sigma_{2}B)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Phi^{-1}(\Re A\sigma_{2}\Re B)\leqslant\Phi^{-1}(\Re A\sigma^{*}\Re B)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Phi(\Re A\sigma^{*}\Re B)^{-1}=\Phi(\Re^{-1}A\sigma\Re^{-1}B)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sec^{2}\theta\Phi(\Re A^{-1}\sigma\Re B^{-1}).

(2.5)

Combining (
2.4
) and (
2
), we get (
2.3
).
∎

.

If we substitute

,

,

with

,

Lemma 2.10
.

[
3
]

Let
f\in\mathbf{m}
and
A,B\in{\mathcal{S}_{\theta}}
for some
0\leqslant\theta<\frac{\pi}{2}
. Then

f(\Re A)\leqslant\Re(f(A))\leqslant\sec^{2}\theta f(\Re A).

Lemma 2.11
.

[
16
, Proposition 2.1]

If
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are two means with
\sigma_{1}\leqslant\sigma_{2}
and
A,B\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}
, then

\displaystyle(a)\quad\Re(A\sigma_{1}B)\leqslant\sec^{2}\theta\ \Re(A\sigma_{2}B)

\displaystyle(b)\quad\Re(A\sigma_{2}B)^{-1}\leqslant\sec^{2}\theta\ \Re(A\sigma_{1}B)^{-1}.

The following theorem is an analogue of Lemma
2.11
involving operator monotone functions.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta}
such that
0<mI\leqslant\Re A,\Re B\leqslant MI
,
\sigma
be an arbitrary mean,
\Phi
be a positive unital linear map and
f\in\mathbf{m}
. Then

\Re f\left(\Phi(A\sigma_{1}B)\right)\leqslant\sec^{2}\theta\ \Re f\left(K\sec^{2}\theta\ \Phi(A\sigma_{2}B)\right)\leqslant K\sec^{4}\theta\ \Re f\left(\Phi(A\sigma_{2}B)\right)

for every operator means
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
between
\sigma
and
\sigma^{*}
.

Moreover, if
\sigma_{1}\leqslant\sigma_{2}
, the constant
K
can be omitted.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.10
and Theorem
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta\Re f\left(\Phi(A\sigma_{1}B)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant f\left(\Re\Phi(A\sigma_{1}B)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant f\left(K\sec^{2}\theta\ \Re\Phi(A\sigma_{2}B)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Re f\left(K\sec^{2}\theta\Phi(A\sigma_{2}B)\right).

This prove the first inequality.

The second inequality follows from the fact that if
\lambda\geq 1
then
f(\lambda x)\leqslant\lambda f(x)
.

If
\sigma_{1}\leqslant\sigma_{2}
, we can use Lemma
2.11
instead of Theorem
2.6
and avoid the constant
K
.
∎

Similarly using Theorem
2.7
, we have this Theorem.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta}
such that
0<mI\leqslant\Re A,\Re B\leqslant MI
,
\Phi
be a positive unital linear map and
f\in\mathbf{m}
. Then

\Re f\left(\Phi(A\nabla_{v}B)\right)\leqslant\sec^{2}\theta\ \Re f\left(K\Phi(A\sigma B)\right)\leqslant K\sec^{2}\theta\ \Re f\left(\Phi(A\sigma B)\right)

where
!_{v}\leqslant\sigma\leqslant\nabla_{v}
.

The next theorem is the sectorial version of (
1.3
).

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\theta}
such that
0<mI\leqslant\Re A,\Re B\leqslant MI
. Then for every positive unital linear map
\Phi
and
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
between
\nabla_{\nu}
and
!_{\nu}
,

\Re\left(f(\Phi(A))\sigma_{1}f(\Phi(B))\right)\leqslant K\sec^{4}\theta\ \Re f\left(\Phi(A\sigma_{2}B)\right),

for
f\in\mathbf{m}
.

Proof.

By Lemmas
2.1
and
2.10
,

\displaystyle\Re\left(f(\Phi(A))\sigma_{1}f(\Phi(B))\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sec^{2}\theta\ \left(\Re f(\Phi(A))\sigma_{1}\Re f(\Phi(B))\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sec^{4}\theta f(\Re(\Phi(A)))\sigma_{1}f(\Re(\Phi(B)))

\displaystyle=\sec^{4}\theta f(\Phi(\Re(A)))\sigma_{1}f(\Phi(\Re(B)))

Now applying (
1.3
) and Lemmas
2.1
and
2.10
, we get

\displaystyle f(\Phi(\Re(A)))\sigma_{1}f(\Phi(\Re(B)))

\displaystyle\leqslant Kf\left(\Phi(\Re A\sigma_{2}\Re B)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant K\ f\left(\Phi(\Re(A\sigma_{2}B))\right)

\displaystyle=Kf\left(\Re(\Phi(A\sigma_{2}B))\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant K\Re f(\Phi(A\sigma_{2}B)).

∎

Remark 2.15
.

In this Theorem, consider
\sigma_{2}=\nabla_{v}
. Since
f\in\mathbf{m}
is operator concave, the last part of the proof can be written as

\displaystyle f(\Phi(\Re A))\sigma_{1}f(\Phi(\Re B))

\displaystyle\leqslant f(\Phi(\Re A))\nabla_{v}f(\Phi(\Re B))

\displaystyle\leqslant f\left(\Phi(\Re A)\nabla_{v}\Phi(\Re B)\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(\Phi(\Re A\nabla_{v}\Re B)\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(\Re(\Phi(A\nabla_{v}B))\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Re f(\Phi(A\nabla_{v}B)).

This leads to

\Re\left(f(\Phi(A))\sigma_{1}f(\Phi(B))\right)\leqslant\sec^{4}\theta\ \Re f\left(\Phi(A\nabla_{v}B)\right),

This result was proved in
[
3
]
where
\sigma_{1}
is geometric mean.

\sigma\leqslant\nabla
. Then

\mid\det(A\sigma B)\mid\leqslant\frac{\sec^{4n}\theta}{2^{n}}\mid\det(I_{n}+A)\mid.\mid\det(I_{n}+B)\mid.

and

\|A\sigma B\|\leqslant\frac{\sec^{4}\theta}{2}\|I_{n}+A\|.\|I_{n}+B\|.

Proof.

Compute

\displaystyle\mid\det(A\sigma B)\mid

\displaystyle\leqslant\sec^{n}\theta\det(\Re(A\sigma B))

\displaystyle(\text{by Lemma \ref{67} })

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\sec^{3n}\theta}{2^{n}}\det(\Re(A+B))

\displaystyle(\text{by Lemma \ref{2.10} })

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\sec^{3n}\theta}{2^{n}}\mid\det(\Re(A+B)\mid

\displaystyle(\text{by Lemma \ref{67} })

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\sec^{4n}\theta}{2^{n}}\mid\det(I_{n}+A)\mid.\mid\det(I_{n}+B)\mid.

\displaystyle(\text{by \eqref{80} })

and

\displaystyle\|A\sigma B\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sec\theta\|\Re(A\sigma B))\|

\displaystyle(\text{by Lemma \ref{32}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\sec^{3}\theta}{2}\|\Re(A+B)\|

\displaystyle(\text{by Lemma \ref{2.10}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\sec^{3}\theta}{2}\|A+B\|

\displaystyle(\text{by Lemma \ref{32} })

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\sec^{4}\theta}{2}\|I_{n}+A\|.\|I_{n}+B\|.

\displaystyle(\text{by \eqref{81} })

∎

.

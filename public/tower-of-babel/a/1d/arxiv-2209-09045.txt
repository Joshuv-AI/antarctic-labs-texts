Borel summability of the 𝟏 / 𝐍 \mathbf{1/N} expansion in quartic O ⁡ ( 𝐍 ) \mathbf{\mathrm{O}(N)} -vector models
L. Ferdinand, R. Gurau, C.I. Perez-Sanchez, F. Vignes-Tourneret

Abstract
We consider a quartic O ⁡ ( N ) \mathrm{O}(N) -vector model. Using the Loop Vertex Expansion, we prove the Borel summability in 1 / N 1/N along the real axis of the partition function and of the connected correlations of the model. The Borel summability holds uniformly in the coupling constant, as long as the latter belongs to a cardioid like domain of the complex plane, avoiding the negative real axis.



1
Introduction

The Loop Vertex Expansion (LVE) was introduced by Rivasseau in 2007
[
Riv07
]
as a new tool in constructive field theory in order to deal with matrix fields. It was then successfully applied to general tensor fields
[
Gur13
,
DR16
,
RVT18
]
.
For a general exposition in zero dimensions, close to the topic of this
article, see
[
Riv09
]
. The outcome of the LVE is
an expression of the free energy, as well as the generating function
of connected moments (or cumulants) as a sum over trees instead of
connected graphs.
As the number of trees increases only exponentially with the number of vertices and the contribution
of each tree is exponentially bounded, the resulting series is convergent. The two
ingredients of this expansion are the Hubbard–Stratonovich

[
Hub59
,
Str57
]
intermediate field
representation and the Brydges-Kennedy–Abdesselam-Rivasseau (BKAR) formula

[
BK87
,
AR95
]
.

In this paper we study the Borel summability in
1/N
of the free
energy and the cumulants of the quartic
\mathrm{O}(N)
-vector model in
zero dimensions using the LVE (see Section
2
for the definition of the model). Note that here we are not interested
in the pertubative expansion (the expansion at small coupling
constant), which is well-understood for the quartic
\mathrm{O}(N)
-vector model and is Borel summable in
0
dimensions
[
Riv07
]
and in
2
dimensions
[
EMS74
]
. On the contrary, Borel summability in
1/N
is less explored. The associated two-dimensional Euclidean quantum field theory was studied in
[
BR82
]
, where the authors prove the
Borel summability of the partition function and of the moments of the

\frac{g}{N}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert}^{4}_{2}
measure. But they discuss neither the free energy
nor the cumulants. Passing between the two is rather non trivial as one
needs to take a logarithm. The raison d’être of the LVE is to take this
logarithm rigorously and uniformly in
N
. A related model, the spherical
\mathrm{O}(N)
model (or
non-linear
\sigma
-model), has been studied in
[
Kup80a
,
FMR82
]
where the authors
showed that the partition function and the correlation functions
at high enough temperature are Borel summable in
1/N
.
However, contrary to the model we study here, the spherical
\mathrm{O}(N)
model does not have any
issues of convergence at large field as the field is restricted to belong to
the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
.

Techniques similar to the ones we use in this paper have been introduced
in
[
GK15
]
for
N\times N
matrices. However, only the Borel summability of the
perturbative expansion in the coupling constant has been established
in
[
GK15
]
: the status of the
1/N
series has not been analyzed. The generalization of Borel summability results in
1/N
to the case of matrices is not
straightforward: contrary to the vector case, we do not have a representation of the partition function
(with sources) in which
N
is just a parameter. Consequently it has been impossible so far to prove that such functions can be extended to analytic functions in
1/N
in some domain.

In this article the free energy and the generating function of cumulants
of the quartic
\mathrm{O}(N)
vector model are considered
as functions of the coupling constant
g
and of
1/N
. We
look for the largest domain in the

(g,1/N)
-plane allowing their bivariate analytic continuation.
After introducing the model in Section
2
, we present both the main tools
and the two main results in Section
3
, namely the analyticity (Thm.
3.6
) and the Borel summability (Thm.
3.8
) domains of the free energy and the cumulants. We obtain that if
\lvert\arg g+\arg 1/N\rvert<3\pi/2
, the free energy and the cumulants are analytic in a cardioid shaped domain in
g
and, for
\lvert\arg g\rvert<{\pi}
they are Borel summable in
1/N
along
the real axis uniformly in
g
for
g
in a slightly smaller cardioid domain. The proofs of these theorems are presented in Sections
4
and
5
,
respectively. In order to keep this
article self-contained, we recall
BKAR formula in Lemma
3.5
, but other useful tools also appear in the appendix.

The model and the partition function

Before introducing the model, let us adopt the following notation:

•

We denote by
I_{n}
the identity matrix on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and by
\mathbbm{1}_{n}
the
n\times n
matrix with all entries equal to
1
.

•

Let
C\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be symmetric positive and
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We write

for
\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}X_{i}C_{ij}Y_{j}

denote

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X\right\rVert}^{2}
. Whenever
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the argument of a function
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\langle\partial,\partial\rangle_{C}F(X)=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}C_{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial{x_{i}}\partial{x_{j}}}(X)\;.

•

Let
C\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be symmetric positive semi-definite. We denote by

\mu_{C}
the centered Gaussian probability distribution of
covariance
C
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that it exists and is unique
(see appendix
B.1
) even if
C
is degenerate.

•

If
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, we denote
\mathbb{E}_{C}[F(X)]
the expectation of
F
with respect to
\mu_{C}
:

\mathbb{E}_{C}[F(X)]=\int d\mu_{C}(X)F(X)=[e^{\frac{1}{2}\langle\partial,\partial\rangle_{C}}F(X)]_{X=0}\;.

•

We write
a\lesssim b
if there is a constant
K>0
such
that
a\leq Kb
. If we want to specify that
K
depends on some parameter

\alpha

a\lesssim_{\alpha}b
.

•

Throughout this paper, we denote
1/N
by
\epsilon
when promoted to a complex variable.

Let
N
be a positive integer and
g\in\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\Re z>0\}
. The
zero-dimensional quartic
\mathrm{O}(N)
-vector model is a probability distribution

\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
defined as a perturbed Gaussian distribution in the following way: denoting by
\mathbb{E}
the expectation with respect
to
\nu
, for all
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

\nu
-mesurable, the
expectation of
F
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(X)]=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{I_{N}}[e^{-\frac{g}{8N}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X\right\rVert}^{4}}F(X)]}{\mathbb{E}_{I_{N}}[e^{-\frac{g}{8N}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X\right\rVert}^{4}}]}\;.

The Fourier-Laplace transform of the measure, also known in the physics literature as the

partition function with sources

J\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, denoted
Z(g,1/N;J)
, is:

\displaystyle Z\big(g,\frac{1}{N};J\big)=\mathbb{E}_{I_{N}}[e^{-\frac{g}{8N}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X\right\rVert}^{4}}]\,\mathbb{E}[e^{\sqrt{N}\langle J,X\rangle}]=\mathbb{E}_{I_{N}}[e^{-\frac{g}{8N}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X\right\rVert}^{4}+\sqrt{N}\langle J,X\rangle}]\;.

In particular, the
partition function
of
\nu
,
Z(g,1/N;0)=\mathbb{E}_{I_{N}}[e^{-\frac{g}{8N}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X\right\rVert}^{4}}]
, is the normalisation constant of that measure.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that we have made a particular choice of scaling of the
sources
J
with
N
. This scaling ensures that all the cumulants (see below for details) are non-trivial
in the large
N
limit. In the absence of this scaling, in the large
N
limit
the
\mathrm{O}(N)
-vector model is a Gaussian model with a
complicated covariance corresponding to the resummation of the dominant diagrams in the large
N
limit, the so-called cactus diagrams.

From now on, we will switch to an integral notation, more adapted to
the LVE, and more reminiscent of the functional integration in quantum
field theory. In particular, in accordance to the usual notation of
quantum field theory, we denote
\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
the random vector so
that the partition function with sources rewrites:

\displaystyle Z\big(g,\frac{1}{N};J\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{d^{N}\phi}{(2\pi)^{N/2}}\;e^{-\frac{1}{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert}^{2}-\frac{g}{8N}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert}^{4}+\sqrt{N}\langle J,\phi\rangle}=\int d\mu_{I_{N}}(\phi)\;e^{-\frac{g}{8N}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert}^{4}+\sqrt{N}\langle J,\phi\rangle}\;.

(1)

Our aim is to study the expansion in
1/N
of
the partition function and the cumulants of the measure
\nu
. At fixed

N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, the integral in eq. (
1
) is absolutely
convergent iff
\Re g\geq 0
and defines
Z(g,\tfrac{1}{N};J)
as a holomorphic
function of
g
for all
g\in\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\Re z>0\}
.

In
[
Riv07
]
it was noted that performing a change of variables
(known as the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation, or intermediate field
representation) one can obtain a convergent expansion for the logarithm of the partition function. We thus insert in eq. (
1
) the Hubbard-Stratonovich intermediate field representation (
\imath=\sqrt{-1}
):

e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}={\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy\;e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}+\imath xy}=\int d\mu_{1}(y)\;e^{\imath xy}\;,

for the quartic interaction term,
x=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{g/N}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert}^{2}
and obtain:

\displaystyle Z\big(g,\frac{1}{N};J\big)

\displaystyle=\int d\mu_{{I}_{N}}(\phi)\int d\mu_{1}(\sigma)\;e^{\imath\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{g/N}\,\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert}^{2}\sigma+\sqrt{N}\langle J,\phi\rangle}

\displaystyle=\int d\mu_{1}(\sigma)\;e^{\frac{N}{2}\ln R(\sigma,g/N)+\frac{N}{2}R(\sigma,g/N)\,\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int d\mu_{\frac{1}{N}}(\sigma)\;e^{\frac{N}{2}\ln R(\sigma,g)+\frac{N}{2}R(\sigma,g)\,\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}}

where
R\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{(\frac{1}{\imath\sqrt{z}},z)\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}}z\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
R(\sigma,z)={(1-\imath\sqrt{z}\sigma)}^{-1}
is called the resolvent.

Remark 2.2
.

This transformation renders the O(
N
) invariance explicit: the partition function depends only on the norm of the sources.

We observe that, at fixed
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
Z
can
be analytically continued in
g
to all
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{-}
. The intermediate field representation also makes

1/N
an explicit parameter
[
Kup80
]
in an integral representation of
Z(g,1/N;J)
, contrary to eq. (
1
) where
N
is also present implicitly in the dimension of the integral. One can then study the analyticity properties of
Z(g,1/N;J)
seen as a function of the variable
\epsilon=1/N
that, since we are interested in Borel summability along the positive real axis, we promote to
\mathrm{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\Re z>0\}
. We parameterize
\mathbb{C}^{*}
as
\{(\lvert z\rvert,\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times(-\pi,\pi]\}
and for
z=(\lvert z\rvert,\alpha)\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, we write
\arg z=\alpha
, and we use the same parametrization for
\mathrm{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\mid\lvert\arg z\rvert<\pi/2\}
. Regarding

, since it appears in a square root in the resolvent, it is wiser to take it to be an element of the Riemann surface of the square root, whose basic properties are recall hereafter:

Definition 2.3

(Riemann surface of the square root)
.

Let us denote by
\sqrt{\phantom{z}}
the principal branch of the complex square root defined on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{-}

\Sigma

\sqrt[\sim]{\phantom{\omega}}

\Sigma

is a

\mathbb{C}^{*}

\{(\lvert z\rvert,\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times(-2\pi,2\pi]\}

\liftarg z=\alpha

\mathrm{I}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}z=(\lvert z\rvert,\alpha)\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{-}\mapsto(\lvert z\rvert,\alpha)\in\Sigma
be the canonical injection of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{-}
into
\Sigma
and
\Pi

(but note that

\Pi z
). We will denote by

(resp.
\Sigma^{-}
) the sheet of
\Sigma

\sqrt{\phantom{z}}
(resp. to

Therefore, from now on, the coupling constant

is an element of
\Sigma
, and we aim to find the maximal domain of analyticity of the free energy and the cumulants as functions of
(g,\epsilon)\in\Sigma\times\mathrm{H}
. Since this will bring us to constantly deal with with the arguments of

\text{we will use indifferently $\liftarg g$ and $\varphi$, as well as $\arg\epsilon$ and $\theta$}.

At this point, partition function rewrites as

\displaystyle Z(g,\epsilon;J)

\displaystyle=\int d\mu_{\epsilon}(\sigma)\;e^{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\ln\widetilde{R}(\sigma,g)+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\widetilde{R}(\sigma,g)\,\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}}\,,

which enables to analytically continue it from
\mathrm{I}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*})\times\{1/N\mid N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\}
to
\mathrm{I}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{-})\times\mathrm{H}

\displaystyle Z_{\psi}(g,\epsilon;J)

\displaystyle=\int_{e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}}\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\sigma}{\sqrt{2\pi\epsilon}}\;e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2\epsilon}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\ln\widetilde{R}(\sigma,g)+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\widetilde{R}(\sigma,g)\,\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\sigma}{e^{-\imath\frac{\psi}{2}}\sqrt{2\pi\epsilon}}\;e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2\epsilon e^{-\imath\psi}}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\ln\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)\,\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int d\mu_{\epsilon e^{-\imath\psi}}(\sigma)\;e^{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\ln\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)\,\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}}\;.

(2)

The integral is convergent and, furthermore,
Z_{\psi}(g,\epsilon;J)=Z(g,\epsilon;J)
is independent of
\psi
. Indeed, let
s_{g,\epsilon,J}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\sigma\in\mathbb{C}\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\epsilon}}\;e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2\epsilon}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\ln\widetilde{R}(\sigma,g)+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\widetilde{R}(\sigma,g)\,\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}}
so that
Z_{\psi}(g,\epsilon;J)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}}s_{g,\epsilon,J}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}})d\sigma
. We then have
\frac{d}{d\psi}Z_{\psi}(g,\epsilon;J)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\imath}{2}e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}}d\sigma[s_{g,\epsilon,J}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}})+\sigma{s^{\prime}}_{g,\epsilon,J}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}})]=0
by integration by part.

Proposition 2.4

The partition function with sources of the zero-dimensional

-vector model,

Remark 2.5
.

In the sequel, to prove Borel summability of the free energy or the cumulants of

[

]
. One important hypothesis of this theorem is analyticity in a disk tangent to the imaginary axis and centered at a positive real number. We call such a domain a Sokal disk, see remark

In order to prove Proposition

Lemma 2.6

For all

\lvert\widetilde{R}(\sigma,g)\rvert\leq\frac{1}{\lvert\cos({\arg\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\liftarg{g}})\rvert}\,.

This bound is trivial for

Proof.

Directly stems from

Remark 2.7
.

This analytic continuation is the largest one that can be found thanks to the tilt of the contour of integration, since for
|\psi-\theta|\geq\pi/2
, the integral (
2
) becomes divergent.

Remark 2.8
.

In order to clarify why the tilting of the contour was needed,
consider the following. Suppose we are interested
in the function
h\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{\Re z>0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},z\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-zt}dt
and ignore that
f(z)=1/z
. Clearly,
h
is
analytic on its domain of definition. We aim to analytically continue
h
to
some maximal domain. To this end, we observe that
h_{\psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}z=\lvert z\rvert e^{\imath\alpha}\mapsto\int_{e^{\imath\psi}\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-zt}dt
is analytic iff

\lvert\alpha+\psi\rvert<\pi/2
. Moreover, if

\lvert\psi\rvert<\pi
, the domains of analyticity of
h
and
h_{\psi}

overlap, and
h=h_{\psi}
where they are both analytic. Thus
h_{\psi}
is an
analytic continuation of
f
to a Riemann sheet. One needs to check whether this analytic
continuation has a discontinuity at the real negative
axis (in which case
0
is a branch point) or a pole. In our case
one gets a pole, but applying the same strategy to
z\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-zt^{2}}dt
one obtains a branch point of order
2
. We apply the same strategy to
Z
.

Our aim is to obtain similar results for the free energy
and the cumulants. As such quantities depend on the logarithm of the partition
function
Z
and
Z
has zeroes, they will not simply inherit the analyticity properties of

Z
: we expect that the domain of analyticity of
\ln Z
is smaller than the one of
Z
. Since
Z(0,\epsilon;J)
is non vanishing, for
g
close to
0
,
Z(g,\epsilon;J)
is non vanishing too. However, for
g
real negative
Z
is discontinuous and
g=0
does not belong to the domain of analyticity of
Z
or
\ln Z
.
In order to identify some domain of analyticity of
\ln Z

we will rewrite it as a uniformly convergent series of
analytic functions. This series is indexed by trees and converges for a small enough
coupling constant thereby defining
\ln Z
in some domain. This is in contrast with the perturbative expansion which writes the partition function and the cumulants as divergent series.

The core of our arguments heavily relies on the
Loop Vertex Expansion
(LVE)
[
MR07
,
Riv07
]
, which we now present.

3
The Loop Vertex Expansion of the cumulants

In this section, we perform the LVE of the cumulants defined hereafter:

Definition 3.1

(The rescaled cumulants)
.

For all
k\geq 1
, one defines
the
rescaled cumulant
of order
2k
,

\mathfrak{K}^{2k}(g,\epsilon)
, by the following relation:

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}(2k)}\prod_{(a_{i},a_{j})\in\pi}\delta_{a_{i},a_{j}}\right)\mathfrak{K}^{2k}_{\psi}(g,\epsilon)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\epsilon\frac{\partial^{2k}}{\partial_{J_{a_{1}}}...\partial_{J_{a_{2k}}}}\left.\ln{Z_{\psi}(g,\epsilon;J)}\right|_{J=0},

(5)

where
P_{2}(2k)
is the set of pairings of
2k
elements and let
\mathfrak{K}^{2k}(g,\epsilon)=\mathfrak{K}^{2k}_{\psi=0}(g,\epsilon)
.

This scaling is chosen as to have a well-defined large-
N
limit
and the advantage of using
\mathfrak{K}^{2k}
over the RHS of (
5
)
is that the former is manifestly O(
N
) invariant. Since only
\mathfrak{K}^{2k}
will
appear below, we refer to them as the cumulants.

Before going to the Loop Vertex Expansion, let us introduce a few notations. First of all, in the following, we will denote by
\mathcal{T}_{n}
be the set of all labelled trees with
n
vertices. To a tree
T\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
, we associate the symmetric
n\times n
matrix
W^{T}(u)
with diagonal
entries equal to
1
and off diagonal ones
W_{ij}^{F}(u)=W_{ji}^{F}(u)=w_{ij}^{F}(u)

as given by eq. (
10
). Then, the LVE is written in terms of

C^{n}_{k}=\big\{(i_{1},...,i_{k})\in\{1,...,n\}^{k}\mid\text{for all }a,b\in\{1,...,k\},a\neq b\Rightarrow i_{a}\neq i_{b}\big\}\;.

The notation comes from the fact that
C_{k}^{n}
is the
configuration space of
k
particles on the discrete
n
-point space. With this notation at hand, the LVE allows us to express to cumulants as a sum over
ciliated
trees
, for which we adopt the following convention:

\text{couples $(T,\mathfrak{c})$
made of a tree $T$ (with $n$ vertices)
and \emph{cilia} $\mathfrak{c}\in C^{n}_{k}$ are denoted by $T_{\mathfrak{c}}$ }.

(
\star
)

For
i\in\{1,...,n\}
, we also denote
d_{i}(T_{\mathfrak{c}})=c(i)+d_{i}(T)
with
c(i)=\textbf{1}_{i\in\mathfrak{c}}
the coordination (or degree) of the vertex
i
in
T_{\mathfrak{c}}
including the cilia. The Loop Vertex expansion of the cumulants is given by the following proposition:

Proposition 3.2

(Loop Vertex expansion of the cumulants)
.

For all
1\leq k\leq n
and
\psi\in(\theta-\pi/2,\theta+\pi/2)
, the cumulants are given by the series

\mathfrak{K}_{\psi}^{2k}(g,\epsilon)=2^{k-1}\sum_{n\geq k}\frac{1}{n!}{\left(\frac{-\Pi g}{2}\right)}^{n-1}\\
\times\sum_{T_{\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\times C^{n}_{k}}\int du_{T}\int d\mu_{\epsilon e^{-\imath\psi}W^{T}(u)}(\sigma)\bigg\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}(T_{\mathfrak{c}})-1)!\,\widetilde{R}^{d_{i}(T_{\mathfrak{c}})}(\sigma^{(i)}e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)\bigg\}\;,

for all
(g,\epsilon)\in\Sigma\times\mathrm{H}
such that it converges (in particular for
(g,\epsilon)\in\mathrm{I}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*})\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
).

Proof.

To perform the LVE, we first expand the partition function with sources as expressed in
eq. (
2
). From now on, we fix

\psi\in(\theta-\pi/2,\theta+\pi/2)
and for
(g,\epsilon)\in\mathfrak{B}
we start from:

\displaystyle Z_{\psi}(g,\epsilon;J)

\displaystyle=\int d\mu_{\epsilon e^{-\imath\psi}}(\sigma)\;e^{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\ln\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)\,\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}}\,.

We expand the exponential inside the integral and, using Fubbini’s theorem, we exchange the sum and the
integral to obtain:

Z_{\psi}(g,\epsilon;J)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2\epsilon)^{n}n!}\int d\mu_{\epsilon e^{-\imath\psi}}(\sigma)\big[\ln\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)+\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}\big]^{n}\;.

(6)

The use of Fubbini’s theorem is justified by the following lemma:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
a\in(0,1/2]
,
(g,\epsilon)\in\Sigma\times\mathrm{H}
and
\psi\in(\theta-\pi/2,\theta+\pi/2)
. Then, if
(g,\epsilon)\in\mathfrak{A}^{+}_{\psi}
(see eq.
4
), there exist two non negative reals
C_{1},C_{2}
independent of
\lvert g\rvert
and
\lvert\epsilon\rvert
such that for
n
large enough:

A_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{(2\lvert\epsilon\rvert)^{n}n!}\bigg|\int d\mu_{\epsilon e^{-\imath\psi}}(\sigma)\big[{\ln\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)+\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}}\big]^{n}\bigg|\leq\frac{C_{1}^{n}}{\lvert\epsilon\rvert^{n}n!}+\frac{C_{2}^{n}\lvert g\rvert^{an}}{{(\lvert\epsilon\rvert^{n}n!)}^{(1-a)}}\,.

In particular, at fixed
\psi\in(\theta-\pi/2,\theta+\pi/2)
, the sum of the
A_{n}
’s has infinite radius of convergence in both
\lvert g\rvert
and
1/\lvert\epsilon\rvert
for all
(g,\epsilon)\in\mathfrak{A}_{\psi}^{+}
.

Proof.

It is convenient to perform the change of variable
\sigma\rightarrow\sigma\sqrt{\lvert g\rvert}
so that
A_{n}
rewrites:

\frac{1}{(2\lvert\epsilon\rvert)^{n}n!}\bigg|\int d\mu_{\lvert g\rvert\epsilon e^{-\imath\psi}}(\sigma)[\ln\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},\frac{g}{\lvert g\rvert})+\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},\frac{g}{\lvert g\rvert})\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}]^{n}\bigg|.

Then, thanks to the bound in eq. (
3
), if
\lvert\psi+\varphi\rvert<\pi
,

\lvert\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},\frac{g}{\lvert g\rvert})\rvert\cdot\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}\leq\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}}{\cos{(\frac{\psi+\varphi}{2})}}
. Furthermore:

\big|{\ln{\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},\frac{g}{\lvert g\rvert})}}\big|\leq\big[\big(-\frac{1}{2}\ln\lvert\widetilde{R}^{-2}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},\frac{g}{\lvert g\rvert})\rvert\big)^{2}+\pi^{2}\big]^{1/2}.

Then, using
\lvert\widetilde{R}^{-2}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},\frac{g}{\lvert g\rvert})\rvert=1+2\sin{\frac{\psi+\varphi}{2}}\lvert\sigma\rvert+\sigma^{2}\leq(1+\lvert\sigma\rvert)^{2}
, we get
\ln{\lvert\widetilde{R}^{-2}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},\frac{g}{\lvert g\rvert})\rvert}\leq\ln{(1+\lvert\sigma\rvert)^{2}}\leq 2(1+\lvert\sigma\rvert)^{2a}
for
0<a<1/2
implying
|{\ln{\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},\frac{g}{\lvert g\rvert})}}|\leq\sqrt{(1+\lvert\sigma\rvert)^{4a}+\pi^{2}}\leq(1+\lvert\sigma\rvert)^{2a}+\pi
as
\pi>1
so that:

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\lvert\epsilon\rvert)^{n}n!\sqrt{\cos(\psi-\theta)}}\int d\mu_{\frac{\lvert g\epsilon\rvert}{\cos(\psi-\theta)}}(\sigma)\big[(1+\lvert\sigma\rvert)^{2a}+\pi+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}\cos^{-1}{(\frac{\psi+\varphi}{2})}\big]^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\lvert\epsilon\rvert)^{n}n!\sqrt{\cos(\psi-\theta)}}\int d\mu_{\frac{\lvert g\epsilon\rvert}{\cos(\psi-\theta)}}(\sigma)\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(1+\lvert\sigma\rvert)^{2ak}\big[\pi+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}\cos^{-1}{(\frac{\psi+\varphi}{2})}\big]^{n-k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\big[\pi+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}\cos^{-1}{(\frac{\psi+\varphi}{2})}\big]^{n}}{\lvert\epsilon\rvert^{n}n!\sqrt{\cos(\psi-\theta)}}\int d\mu_{\frac{\lvert g\epsilon\rvert}{\cos(\psi-\theta)}}(\sigma)(1+\lvert\sigma\rvert)^{2an}\,,

where we used the fact that
\pi+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}\cos^{-1}{(\frac{\psi+\varphi}{2})}
and
1+\lvert\sigma\rvert
are greater than one. At this stage, rewriting:

\displaystyle\int d\mu_{\frac{\lvert g\epsilon\rvert}{\cos(\psi-\theta)}}(\sigma)(1+\lvert\sigma\rvert)^{2an}

\displaystyle=\int_{\lvert\sigma\rvert<1}d\mu_{\frac{\lvert g\epsilon\rvert}{\cos(\psi-\theta)}}(\sigma)(1+\lvert\sigma\rvert)^{2an}+\int_{\lvert\sigma\rvert>1}d\mu_{\frac{\lvert g\epsilon\rvert}{\cos(\psi-\theta)}}(\sigma)(1+\lvert\sigma\rvert)^{2an}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\lvert\sigma\rvert<1}d\mu_{\frac{\lvert g\epsilon\rvert}{\cos(\psi-\theta)}}(\sigma)2^{2an}+\int_{\lvert\sigma\rvert>1}d\mu_{\frac{\lvert g\epsilon\rvert}{\cos(\psi-\theta)}}(\sigma)(2\lvert\sigma\rvert)^{2an}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2an}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}d\mu_{\frac{\lvert g\epsilon\rvert}{\cos(\psi-\theta)}}(\sigma)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}d\mu_{\frac{\lvert g\epsilon\rvert}{\cos(\psi-\theta)}}(\sigma)\lvert\sigma\rvert^{2an}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 4^{an}\bigg(1+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\big[\frac{\cos{(\psi-\theta)}}{2\lvert g\epsilon\rvert}\big]^{1/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-\cos{(\psi-\theta)}\frac{t}{2\lvert g\epsilon\rvert}}t^{an-1/2}dt\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 4^{an}\bigg(1+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\big[\frac{2\lvert g\epsilon\rvert}{\cos{(\psi-\theta)}}\big]^{an}\Gamma\big(an+\frac{1}{2}\big)\bigg)\,,

and using the asymptotic of the gamma function we get
A_{n}\leq\frac{C_{1}^{n}}{\lvert\epsilon\rvert^{n}n!}+\frac{C_{2}^{n}\lvert g\rvert^{an}}{{(\lvert\epsilon\rvert^{n}n!)}^{(1-a)}}
.∎

We now use the copies trick as stated in Lemma

(see Appendix

rewrites:

Lemma 3.4

(The copies trick)
.

Let
n
be a positive integer,
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re z>0
and

F\in L^{n}(\mathbb{R},\mu_{\lvert z\rvert^{2}/\Re z})
a
\mathbb{C}
-valued function. Then
F^{\otimes n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
,
X=(X_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\mapsto\prod_{1=n}^{n}F(X_{i})
is in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu_{{\lvert z\rvert^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{n}/\Re z}})
and furthermore we
have:

\displaystyle\int d\mu_{z}(x)F^{n}(x)=\int d\mu_{z\mathbbm{1}_{n}}(X)F^{\otimes n}(X)=\int d\mu_{z\mathbbm{1}_{n}}(X)\prod_{i=1}^{n}F(X_{i})\;.

(7)

This lemma produces for an integration variable
\sigma
,
n

variables of integration that we call the copies of
\sigma
and that
we denote by
(\sigma^{(i)})_{1\leq i\leq n}
, where we use parenthesis
to avoid the confusion with the O(
N
) indices. With this notation, using eq. (
7
) in
eq. (
6
) we obtain that:

\displaystyle Z_{\psi}(g,\epsilon;J)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{(2\epsilon)^{n}n!}\int d\mu_{\epsilon e^{-\imath\psi}\mathbbm{1}_{n}}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\bigg\{\ln\widetilde{R}(\sigma^{(i)}e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)+\widetilde{R}(\sigma^{(i)}e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}\bigg\}

(8)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{(2\epsilon)^{n}n!}\left[\exp\Big(\frac{\epsilon e^{-\imath\psi}}{2}\langle\partial,\partial\rangle_{X(x)}\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\bigg\{\ln\widetilde{R}(\sigma^{(i)}e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)+\widetilde{R}(\sigma^{(i)}e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}\bigg\}\right]_{\sigma^{(i)}=0,x_{ij}=1}\,,

where
[X(x)]_{ii}=1
and
[X(x)]_{ij}=x_{ij}
for
i\neq j
.

We now rewrite the above expansion as a sum indexed by forests over labelled vertices of analytic functions and consequently its logarithm as a sum indexed by trees over labelled vertices of analytic functions. The crucial point is that at order
n
the number of
such trees is of order
O(1)^{n}n!
, much less than the number of Feynman diagrams which is of order

O(1)^{n}(2n)!
, and the expansion is convergent.

This rewriting is obtained thanks to the BKAR formula
[
BK87
,
AR95
]
, which will be applied to the

Lemma 3.5

(BKAR formula)
.

Let
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a
smooth function. If
x\in\mathbb{R}^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}
, we denote its
components by
x_{ij}
for
i,j=1,2,\dots,n
,
i<j
. Let
F
be a
forest with vertex set
V(F)=\{1,2,\dotsc,n\}
. If there is an edge between vertices
i
and
j

(
i<j
), we write
(i,j)\in E(F)
. If both
i
and
j
belong to
the same connected component of
F
, we let
P_{i\leftrightarrow j}^{F}
stand for the unique path in
F
between
i
and

j
. Then we have:

f(x)\big|_{x=\mathbf{1}}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}}\Big(\prod_{(i,j)\in E(F)}\int_{0}^{1}du_{ij}\Big)\,\frac{\partial^{|E(F)|}f(x)}{\prod_{(l,m)\in E(F)}\partial x_{lm}}\bigg|_{x=w^{F}}\,,

(9)

where
\mathbf{1}
is the
\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
-vector with all the components equal to
1
,
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is the set of all forests with
n
labelled vertices,

|E(F)|
is the number of edges of
F
, and

w^{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}
is given by:

w^{F}_{ij}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\inf_{(k,l)\in P_{i\leftrightarrow j}^{F}}u_{kl}&\text{if $i$ and $j$ belong to the same
component of $F$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(10)

Notice that
\prod_{(a,b)\in E(F)}\partial_{x_{ab}}={\epsilon}^{\lvert E(F)\rvert}e^{-\imath\psi\lvert E(F)\rvert}\prod_{(ab)\in E(F)}\partial_{\sigma^{(a)}}\partial_{\sigma^{(b)}}
when acting on the quantity in square brackets in (
8
).
Thus, by the BKAR formula (
9
), the partition function with sources rewrites as

Z_{\psi}(g,\epsilon;J)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{(2\epsilon)^{n}n!}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}}\int du_{F}\int d\mu_{\epsilon e^{-\imath\psi}W^{F}(u)}(\sigma){\epsilon}^{\lvert E(F)\rvert}e^{-\imath\psi\lvert E(F)\rvert}\\
\times\prod_{(a,b)\in E(F)}\partial_{\sigma^{(a)}}\partial_{\sigma^{(b)}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\bigg\{\ln\widetilde{R}(\sigma^{(i)}e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)+\widetilde{R}(\sigma^{(i)}e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2}\bigg\}\;,

where we recall that for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
,
W^{F}(u)
is the symmetric
n\times n
matrix with diagonal
entries equal to
1
and off diagonal ones
W_{ij}^{F}(u)=W_{ji}^{F}(u)=w_{ij}^{F}(u)

as given by eq. (
10
),
du_{F}=\prod_{(ij)\in{F}}\int_{0}^{1}du_{ij}
. The matrix
W^{F}(u)
is positive.

The logarithm of the partition function with sources is the generating
function of the cumulants. Now that the partition function with sources
is expressed as a sum over forests with the amplitude of a forest factorizing over the trees of this forest, its logarithm writes as a sum over trees. As
\lvert E(T)\rvert=|V(T)|-1
, we get:

\epsilon\ln{Z}_{\psi}(g,\epsilon;J)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{e^{-\imath(n-1)\psi}}{2^{n}n!}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\int du_{T}\int d\mu_{\epsilon e^{-\imath\psi}W^{T}(u)}(\sigma)\prod_{(a,b)\in E(T)}\partial_{\sigma^{(a)}}\partial_{\sigma^{(b)}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2k}}}{k!}\\
\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1},...,i_{k}\leq n\\
a\neq b\Rightarrow i_{a}\neq i_{b}\end{subarray}}\prod_{c=1}^{k}\widetilde{R}(\sigma^{(i_{c})}e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)\prod_{j\neq i_{1},...,i_{k}}\ln{\widetilde{R}(\sigma^{(j)}e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g})\,,

were we recall that
\mathcal{T}_{n}
stands for the set of all trees with
n
labelled vertices. At this stage, we can rewrite the sum above in terms of
C^{n}_{k}
and of
ciliated trees
(recall the convention (

⋆

‣
3
)). Indeed, repeatedly using the fact
that
\partial_{\sigma}^{k}\ln\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)={(\imath e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}}\sqrt[\sim]{g})}^{k}(k-1)!\,\widetilde{R}^{k}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)

\partial_{\sigma}^{k}\widetilde{R}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)={(\imath e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}}\sqrt[\sim]{g})}^{k}k!\,\widetilde{R}^{k+1}(\sigma e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)

\epsilon\ln{Z}_{\psi}(g,\epsilon;J)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n!}{\left(\frac{-\Pi g}{2}\right)}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2k}}{k!}\sum_{T_{\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\times C^{n}_{k}}\int du_{T}\int d\mu_{\epsilon e^{-\imath\psi}W^{T}(u)}(\sigma)\\
\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\big\{(d_{i}(T_{\mathfrak{c}})-1)!\,\widetilde{R}^{d_{i}(T_{\mathfrak{c}})}(\sigma^{(i)}e^{\imath\frac{\psi}{2}},g)\big\}.

Using

\frac{\partial^{2k}}{\partial_{J_{a_{1}}}...\partial_{J_{a_{2k}}}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert J\right\rVert}^{2k}=2^{k}k!\sum_{\pi\in P_{2}(2k)}\prod_{(a_{i},a_{j})\in\pi}\delta_{a_{i},a_{j}}
, we obtain the following expression, that holds true as long as both the individual integrals converge and the overall series is convergent (recall that with the convention (

\mathfrak{K}_{\psi}^{2k}(g,\epsilon)=2^{k-1}\sum_{n\geq k}\frac{1}{n!}{\left(\frac{-\Pi g}{2}\right)}^{n-1}\\

(11)

Theorem 3.6

(Main Theorem 1: Analyticity)
.

For all

\mathrm{O}(N)

\epsilon

\displaystyle\lvert g\rvert

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{4}(1+\cos{(\liftarg g+\psi)})\sqrt{\cos{(\psi-\arg\epsilon)}}\;,

(12a)

\displaystyle\lvert\liftarg g+\psi\rvert

\displaystyle<\pi\;,

(12b)

\displaystyle\lvert\psi-\arg\epsilon\rvert

\displaystyle<\frac{\pi}{2}\;.

(12c)

The proof of this theorem is given is Section
4
.

Corollary 3.7

(Domain as a Riemann sheet)
.

At fixed
g\in\Sigma
, the domain of analyticity in
\epsilon
is independent of its modulus, and contains all
\epsilon\in\mathrm{H}
such that
-3\pi/2-\liftarg g<\arg\epsilon<3\pi/2-\liftarg g
. In particular, for all

\lvert\epsilon\rvert\geq 0

\mathfrak{C}
if

3.10

\mathfrak{C}
includes a Sokal disk

\epsilon

) of an

Proof.

The two conditions on
\varphi

\theta

\begin{cases}\lvert\varphi+\psi\rvert<\pi\\
\lvert\psi-\theta\rvert<\frac{\pi}{2}\end{cases}\Leftrightarrow\begin{cases}-\pi<\varphi+\psi<\pi\\

Remark 3.10

From now on we fix

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

with
\Re z>0

We apply this lemma with

and

C=W^{T}(u)
. Then, we bound the integration over the

u_{T}
parameters by one. Finally, we also have to
notice that
\frac{1}{\cos{(\frac{\varphi+\psi}{2})}}
appears at the
power
\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}(T_{\mathfrak{c}})=k+2(n-1)
. Thus,

\lvert\mathfrak{K}_{\psi}^{2k}(g,\epsilon)\rvert\lesssim_{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cos{(\psi-\theta)}}}\frac{1}{\cos^{k}{(\frac{\varphi+\psi}{2})}}\\
\times\sum_{n\geq k}\frac{1}{n!}{\left(\frac{\lvert g\rvert}{2\cos^{2}{(\frac{\varphi+\psi}{2})}\sqrt{\cos{(\psi-\theta)}}}\right)}^{n-1}\sum_{T_{\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\times C^{n}_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}(T_{\mathfrak{c}})-1)!\;,

We conclude using combinatorial arguments, that are gathered
in the following lemma:

Lemma 4.2
.

For all
n\geq k
, the sum over
k
-ciliated (i.e.
\mathfrak{c}\in C_{k}^{n}
) trees with
n
-vertices verifies

\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{T_{\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\times C^{n}_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}(T_{\mathfrak{c}})-1)!=\binom{2n-1}{n-k}\binom{2n+k-3}{2n-1}\times(k-2)!\;.

Proof.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{T_{\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\times C^{n}_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}(T_{\mathfrak{c}})-1)!

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}(T)-1)!\sum_{\mathfrak{c}\in C^{n}_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(d_{i}(T)+\mathbf{1}_{i\in\mathfrak{c}}-1)!}{(d_{i}(T)-1)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}(T)-1)!\sum_{\mathfrak{c}\in C^{n}_{k}}\prod_{i\in\mathfrak{c}}d_{i}(T)\,.

Here, to count the number of trees, we use Cayley’s theorem that states that:

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}(T)-1)!=(n-2)!\sum^{n}_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},\dotsc,d_{n}=1\\
\sum_{i}d_{i}=2(n-1)\end{subarray}}1\,,

(16)

which yields

\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{T_{\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\times C^{n}_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}(T_{\mathfrak{c}})-1)!=\frac{(n-2)!}{n!}\sum^{n}_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},\dotsc,d_{n}=1\\
\sum_{i}d_{i}=2(n-1)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}\in C^{n}_{k}}\prod_{i\in\mathfrak{c}}d_{i}\,.

The sum over the
d_{i}
’s can be computed by the following trick. Let us consider the function
f
of
n
variables:

f(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})=\sum_{d_{1},\dotsc,d_{n}=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{d_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}}{1-x_{i}}\;.

Applying the following differential operator to
f
, and evaluating it
at
(x,\dotsc,x)
gives the expression of the sum as a Taylor coefficient:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},\dotsc,d_{n}=1\\
\sum_{i}d_{i}=2(n-1)\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\prod_{i\in\mathfrak{c}}d_{i}

\displaystyle=[x^{2(n-1)}]\big(\prod_{i\in\mathfrak{c}}x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\big)f(x,\dotsc,x)

\displaystyle=[x^{2(n-1)}]\frac{x^{n}}{(1-x)^{n+k}}=\binom{2n+k-3}{n-2}.

With this result at hand, and using
\sum_{\mathfrak{c}\in C^{n}_{k}}1=n!/(n-k)!
, we finally obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{T_{\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\times C^{n}_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}(T_{\mathfrak{c}})-1)!

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-2)!}{n!}\times\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}\binom{2n+k-3}{n-2}=\binom{2n-1}{n-k}\binom{2n+k-3}{2n-1}\times(k-2)!\,.

∎

Combining this with the trigonometric identity

2\cos^{2}{(x/2)}=(1+\cos x)
, we obtain the following bound on the cumulants:

\lvert\mathfrak{K}_{\psi}^{2k}(g,\epsilon)\rvert\lesssim_{k}\frac{(k-2)!}{\sqrt{\cos{(\psi-\theta)}}}{\frac{1}{\cos^{k}{(\frac{\varphi+\psi}{2})}}}\\
\times\sum_{n\geq k}\binom{2n-1}{n-k}\binom{2n+k-3}{2n-1}{\left(\frac{\lvert g\rvert}{(1+\cos{(\varphi+\psi}))\sqrt{\cos{(\psi-\theta)}}}\right)}^{n-1}.

By Stirling’s formula,
\binom{2n-1}{n-k}\lesssim_{k}4^{n}
and

\binom{2n+k-3}{2n-1}(k-2)!=\frac{(2n+k-3)!}{(2n-1)!}\lesssim_{k}n^{k-2}
so that we can finally bound the cumulants by:

\lvert\mathfrak{K}_{\psi}^{2k}(g,\epsilon)\rvert\lesssim_{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cos{(\psi-\theta)}}}{\frac{1}{\cos^{k}{(\frac{\varphi+\psi}{2})}}}\sum_{n\geq k}{\left(\frac{4\lvert g\rvert}{(1+\cos{(\varphi+\psi}))\sqrt{\cos{(\psi-\theta)}}}\right)}^{n-1}n^{k-2}\;.

We conclude on the analyticity of the cumulants thanks to two classical
theorems: the first one states that the integral of a function that
depends analytically on a paramater defines an analytic function as long as it converges;
the second one states that a uniformly convergent series of analytic
functions is analytic.

The cumulants are expressed as a uniformly convergent series of analytic functions
both in
\Pi g
and
\epsilon
. Since the theorems above apply in the bivariate
analytic case
\mathfrak{K}_{\psi}^{2k}(g,\epsilon)
is analytic on the domain of

yields
an analytic continuation of
\mathfrak{K}^{2k}(g,\epsilon)
to the
subdomain
\mathfrak{C}
of
\Sigma\times\mathrm{H}
which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3

k\geq 1
:

\displaystyle f(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq k}\binom{2n+2q+k-3}{2n-1}{\gamma}^{n-1}\leq 4q\sum_{n\geq k}\binom{2n+2q+k-3}{2n-2}{\gamma}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq 4q\sum_{n\geq k}\binom{2n+2q+k-3}{2n-2}{(\sqrt{\gamma})}^{2n-2}\leq 4q\sum_{n\geq 2k-2}\binom{n+2q+k-1}{n}{(\sqrt{\gamma})}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq 4q\sum_{n\geq 0}\binom{n+2q+k-1}{n}{(\sqrt{\gamma})}^{n}\;.

In the second line we bound the even part of the series by the total series, using the positivity of the odd part. Observing that
\sum_{n\geq 0}\binom{n+2q+k-1}{n}\sqrt{\gamma}^{n}=\frac{1}{{(1-\sqrt{\gamma})}^{2q+k}}
we are done.
∎

Combining this lemma with eq. (

f
uniformly as

f(\gamma)\lesssim_{k}\frac{4q}{\alpha^{2q+k}}
for all
\gamma\in[0,1-\alpha\mathopen{]}
and denoting

and
K_{\alpha}=6\alpha^{-4}
at

\displaystyle\lvert R^{2k}_{q,\psi}(g,\epsilon)\rvert\lesssim_{k}C_{\alpha}K_{\alpha}^{q}{\lvert\epsilon\rvert}^{q}q!\;,

with
C_{\alpha}
and
K_{\alpha}
independent of

lemma (

).∎

The Nevalinna-Sokal theorem

A formal power series
A(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
such that
B(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}/k!t^{k}
is absolutely convergent in some disk centered at zero an admits an analytic continuation along the real axis such that
|B(t)|<Ke^{-t/R}
for some
K,R\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is called a
Borel summable series
. The function
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}/k!t^{k}
is called the
Borel transform
of
A(z)
and the
Borel sum
of
A(z)
is the Laplace transform of its Borel transform:

\displaystyle f(z)=\frac{1}{z}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\;e^{-t/z}B(t)\;.

The Borel sum of a series, if it exists, is unique.

A function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
which is analytic in a disk tangent to the imaginary axis in
0
and
has an asymptotic series in
0
(which can have zero radius of convergence) such that the Taylor rest term of order
q
in
0
of
f
grows no faster than
q!
is called a
Borel summable function

[
Sok79
]
.

These two notions are intimately related: the Borel sums of Borel summable series are Borel summable functions (this is straightforward to prove). The asymptotic series of Borel summable functions are Borel summable series
[
Sok79
]
.
We present here a slightly modified version
[
Riv07
]
of the (optimal) Nevanlinna-Sokal theorem on Borel summability which introduces the notion of uniform Borel summability with respect to some parameter.

Theorem A.1

(Nevanlinna-Sokal)
.

Let
f
be a function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{C}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
(z,w)\mapsto f(z,w)
and let
(f_{k}(w))_{k\geq 0}
be the coefficients of the asymptotic series of
f
in
z=0
, at fixed
w\in\mathbb{C}
. If
f
is analytic in its first variable
z
in a domain
{\rm Disk}_{R}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\Re{(1/z)}>1/R\}
with
R>0
independent of
w
and there is a domain
\cal D\subset\mathbb{C}
such that for
all
(z,w)\in{\rm Disk}_{R}\times{\cal D}
, there exist some
constants
C,K>0
independent of
w
for which the following bound on the rest term of
f
holds for
q
large enough:

\left|f(z,w)-\sum_{k=0}^{q}f_{k}(w)z^{k}\right|\leq CK^{q}\lvert z\rvert^{q}q!,

then
f
is called a Borel summable function in
z
uniformly in
w
in
{\rm Disk}_{R}\times{\cal D}
.

Under these conditions, for all
w\in\cal D
, the Borel transform in
z
of the asymptotic series of
f
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(t,w)\mapsto\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{f_{k}(w)}{k!}t^{k},

is analytic in a disk of radius
K^{-1}
in
t
and can be analytically continued to the strip
\{t\in\mathbb{C}\mid\lvert\Im t\rvert<K^{-1}\}
and in this strip obeys the exponential bound
|\mathcal{B}(t)|<e^{t/R}
. Moreover, for all
(z,w)\in{\rm Disk}_{R}\times{\cal D}
we can reconstruct the function
f(z,w)
by:

\displaystyle f(z,w)=\frac{1}{z}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dt\;e^{-t/z}\mathcal{B}(t,w).

Definition B.1

(Gaussian distributions in dimension one)

For all

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}[F(x)]=[e^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}}F(x)]_{x=0}\;.

Furthermore, if
\varepsilon\neq 0

\mu_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{N}(0,\varepsilon)

\mu_{\varepsilon}=\delta

The previous definition immediately implies that for all

B.3
The copies trick

Lemma B.5

(The copies trick)
.

Let
n
be a positive integer,
z\in\{\Re z>0\}
and

F\in L^{n}(\mathbb{R},\mu_{\lvert z\rvert^{2}/\Re z})
a
\mathbb{C}

F^{\otimes n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}

(X_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\mapsto\prod_{1=n}^{n}F(X_{i})

L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu_{{\lvert z\rvert^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{n}/\Re z}})
and furthermore we

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{z}[F^{n}(x)]=\mathbb{E}_{z\mathbbm{1}_{n}}[F^{\otimes n}(X)]

Proof.

For simplicity we take
z=1
. We denote by
u
the vector of

\mathbb{R}^{n}
with all entries
1/\sqrt{n}
, such that
\mathbbm{1}_{n}=nu\otimes u
. Let
v_{2},...,v_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(u,v_{2},...,v_{n})
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We aim to
understand the action of
\mu_{\mathbbm{1}_{n}}
on a test function
G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. For
\varepsilon>0
, let us define

C_{\varepsilon}
by
C_{\varepsilon}=\mathbbm{1}_{n}+\varepsilon\sum_{i=2}^{n}v_{i}\otimes v_{i}
so that
C_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\mathbbm{1}_{n}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbbm{1}_{n}}[G(X)]

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{n}\det{C_{\varepsilon}}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle X,X\rangle_{C_{\varepsilon}^{-1}}}G(X)d^{n}X

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{n}n\varepsilon^{n-1}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(n^{-1}y_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon^{-1}\sum_{i=2}^{n}y_{i}^{2})}G(y_{1}u+\sum_{i=2}^{n}y_{i}v_{i})\,dy_{1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}dy_{i}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{n}\varepsilon^{n-1}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(y_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon^{-1}\sum_{i=2}^{n}y_{i}^{2})}G(y_{1}\sqrt{n}u+\sum_{i=2}^{n}y_{i}v_{i})\,dy_{1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}dy_{i}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}_{1\otimes\varepsilon^{\otimes(n-1)}}[G(y_{1}\sqrt{n}u+\sum_{i=2}^{n}y_{i}v_{i})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{1\otimes 0^{\otimes(n-1)}}[G(y_{1}\sqrt{n}u+\sum_{i=2}^{n}y_{i}v_{i})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{1}[G(y_{1},...,y_{1})]

To go from the first to the second line, we perform a change of

X

whose Jacobian
determinant is 1, and to go from the second line to the third line, we
perform the change of variable

becomes
\sqrt{n}y_{1}
. Line four

Applying the previous equality to

and substituting

concludes the proof.
∎

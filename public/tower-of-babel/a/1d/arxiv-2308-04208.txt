Growth of ( α , β , γ ) (\alpha,\beta,\gamma) -order solutions of linear differential equations with entire coefficients
Benharrat Belaïdi ∗ and Tanmay Biswas

Abstract
. The main aim of this paper is to study the growth of solutions of higher order linear differential equations using the concepts of ( α , β , γ ) (\alpha,\beta,\gamma) -order and ( α , β , γ ) (\alpha,\beta,\gamma) -type. We obtain some results which improve and generalize some previous results of Kinnunen [ 22 ] , Long et al. [ 27 ] as well as Belaïdi [ 3 ] , [ 5 ] .

Introduction

Throughout this paper, we assume that the reader is familiar with the

\nu(r,f)

M\left(r,f\right)=\underset{\left|z\right|=r}{\max}\left|f\left(z\right)\right|

\log^{+}x=\max\left(0,\log x\right)

2.
Definitions and Notations

We denote the Lebesgue linear measure of a set
F\subset\left[0,+\infty\right)
by
m\left(F\right)=\underset{F}{\int}dt,
and the
logarithmic measure of a set
E\subset\left(1,+\infty\right)
by
m_{l}\left(E\right)=\underset{E}{\int}\frac{dt}{t}.
Furthermore, let
throughout this paper
E
represents a set of finite logarithmic measure,
I
represents a set of infinite logarithmic measure and
F
represents a set
of finite linear measure in proof of our results. For
x\in[0,+\infty)
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
where
\mathbb{N}
is the set of all positive integers, define iterations of the
exponential and logarithmic functions as
\exp^{[k]}x=\exp(\exp^{[k-1]}x)
and
\log^{[k]}x=\log(\log^{[k-1]}x)
with convention that
\log^{[0]}x=x
,
\log^{[-1]}x=\exp{x}
,
\exp^{[0]}x=x
and
\exp^{[-1]}x=\log x
. Now, let
L
be a class of continuous non-negative on
(-\infty,+\infty)
function
\alpha
such that
\alpha(x)=\alpha(x_{0})\geq 0
for
x\leq x_{0}
and
\alpha(x)\uparrow+\infty
as
x_{0}\leq x\rightarrow+\infty
. We say that
\alpha\in L_{1}
, if
\alpha\in L
and
\alpha(a+b)\leq\alpha(a)+\alpha(b)+c
for all
a,b\geq R_{0}

and fixed
c\in(0,+\infty)
. Further, we say that
\alpha\in L_{2}
, if
\alpha\in L
and
\alpha(x+O(1))=(1+o(1))\alpha(x)
as
x\rightarrow+\infty
. Finally,
\alpha\in L_{3}
, if
\alpha\in L
and
\alpha(a+b)\leq\alpha(a)+\alpha(b)
for all
a,b\geq R_{0}
i.e.,
\alpha
is
subadditive. Clearly
L_{3}\subset L_{1}
.

Particularly, when
\alpha\in L_{3}
, then one can easily verify
that
\alpha(mr)\leq m\alpha(r),

m\geq 2
is an integer. Up to a
normalization, subadditivity is implied by concavity. Indeed, if
\alpha(r)

is concave on
[0,+\infty)
and satisfies
\alpha(0)\geq 0
, then for
t\in[0,1]
,

\alpha(tx)=\alpha(tx+(1-t)\cdot 0)\geq t\alpha(x)+(1-t)\alpha(0)\geq t\alpha(x)\text{,}

so that by choosing
t=\frac{a}{a+b}
or
t=\frac{b}{a+b}
,

\displaystyle\alpha(a+b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a}{a+b}\alpha(a+b)+\frac{b}{a+b}\alpha(a+b)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\Big(\frac{a}{a+b}(a+b)\Big)+\alpha\Big(\frac{b}{a+b}(a+b)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(a)+\alpha(b)\text{, \ \ }a,b\geq 0\text{.}

As a non-decreasing, subadditive and unbounded function,
\alpha(r)

satisfies

\alpha(r)\leq\alpha(r+R_{0})\leq\alpha(r)+\alpha(R_{0})

for any
R_{0}\geq 0
. This yields that
\alpha(r)\sim\alpha(r+R_{0})
as

r\rightarrow+\infty
.

Now we add two conditions on
\alpha,

\beta
and
\gamma
: (i)
Always
\alpha\in L_{1},

\beta\in L_{2}
and
\gamma\in L_{3}
; and
(ii)
\alpha(\log^{[p]}x)=o(\beta(\log\gamma(x))),

p\geq 2,

\alpha(\log x)=o(\alpha\left(x\right))
and
\alpha^{-1}(kx)=o\left(\alpha^{-1}(x)\right)

\left(0<k<1\right)
as
x\rightarrow+\infty
.

Throughout this paper, we assume that
\alpha,

\beta
and
\gamma
always satisfy the above two conditions unless otherwise specifically
stated.

Heittokangas et al.
[
17
]
have introduced a new concept of
\varphi
-order of entire and meromorphic function considering
\varphi
as
subadditive function. For details one may see
[
17
]
. Extending this
notion, recently Belaïdi et al.
[
6
]
introduce the definition of
the
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-order of a meromorphic function in the
following way:

Definition 1
.

(
[
6
]
) The
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-order denoted by
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]
of a meromorphic function
f
is
defined by

\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]=\underset{r\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim\sup}\frac{\alpha\left(\log T\left(r,f\right)\right)}{\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)}\text{.}

By the inequality in
[
14
]

T(r,f)\leq\log^{+}M(r,f)\leq\frac{R+r}{R-r}T(R,f)\text{ }(0<r<R)

for an entire function
f(z)
, one can easily verify that
[
6
]

\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]=\underset{r\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim\sup}\frac{\alpha\left(\log T\left(r,f\right)\right)}{\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)}=\underset{r\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim\sup}\frac{\alpha(\log^{[2]}M(r,f))}{\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)}\text{.}

Proposition 1
.

(
[
6
]
) If
f
is an entire function, then

\sigma_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)}[f]=\underset{r\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim\sup}\frac{\alpha(\log^{[2]}T(r,f))}{\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)}=\underset{r\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim\sup}\frac{\alpha(\log^{[3]}M(r,f))}{\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)}\text{,}

where
(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)
-order denoted by
\sigma_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)}[f]
.

Now to compare the relative growth of two meromorphic functions
having same non zero finite
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-order, one may
introduce the definition of
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-type in the following
manner:

Definition 2
.

The
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-type denoted by
\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]
of a meromorphic function
f
with
0<\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]<+\infty
is defined by

\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]=\underset{r\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim\sup}\frac{\exp(\alpha\left(\log T\left(r,f\right)\right))}{\left(\exp\left(\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)\right)\right)^{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]}}\text{.}

If
f
is an entire function with
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]\in(0,+\infty)
, then the
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-type of
f
is defined by

\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma),M}[f]=\underset{r\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim\sup}\frac{\exp(\alpha(\log^{[2]}M(r,f)))}{\left(\exp\left(\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)\right)\right)^{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]}}\text{.}

Similar to Definition
2
, we can also define the
(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)
-type of a meromorphic function
f
in the following way:

Definition 3
.

The
(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)
-type denoted by
\tau_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)}[f]
of a meromorphic function
f
with
0<\sigma_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)}[f]<+\infty
is defined by

\tau_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)}[f]=\underset{r\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim\sup}\frac{\exp(\alpha(\log^{[2]}T(r,f)))}{\left(\exp\left(\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)\right)\right)^{\sigma_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)}[f]}}\text{.}

If
f
is an entire function with
\sigma_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)}[f]\in(0,+\infty)
, then the
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-type of
f
is
defined by

\tau_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma),M}[f]=\underset{r\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim\sup}\frac{\exp(\alpha(\log^{[3]}M(r,f)))}{\left(\exp\left(\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)\right)\right)^{\sigma_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)}[f]}}\text{.}

Proposition 2
.

(
[
6
]
) Let
f_{1},

f_{2}
be nonconstant meromorphic
functions with
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]
and
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]

-order. Then

(i)
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]\leq\max\{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}],

\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]\};

(ii)
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\cdot f_{2}]\leq\max\{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}],

\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]\};

(iii) If
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]\neq\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]
, then
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]=\max\{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}],

\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]\};

(iv) If
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]\neq\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]
, then
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}\cdot f_{2}]=\max\{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}],

\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]\}
.

Proposition 3
.

(
[

\sigma_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]=\max\{\sigma_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}],

\sigma_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]\};

(iv) If
\sigma_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]\neq\sigma_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]

By using the properties

and

,

Proposition 4
.

Let
f
be a nonconstant meromorphic function with finite
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-order. Then

(i)
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[\frac{1}{f}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]

Proposition 5
.

Let
f_{1},

f_{2}
be two nonconstant meromorphic functions.
Then the following statements hold:

(i) If
0<\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]<\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]<+\infty
and
\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]<\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]
, then
\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]=\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\cdot f_{2}]=\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}];

(ii) If
0<\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]<+\infty
, then
\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]\leq\max\{\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}],

\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]\}
. Moreover if
\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]\neq\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]
, then
\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]=\max\{\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}],

\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]\};

(iii) If
0<\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\cdot f_{2}]<+\infty
, then
\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\cdot f_{2}]\leq\max\{\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}],

\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]\}
. Moreover if
\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]\neq\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]
, then
\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\cdot f_{2}]=\max\{\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}],

\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]\}
.

Proof.

(i) The definition of the
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-type implies that for
any given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a sequence
\{r_{n}
,
n\geq 1\}

tending to infinity such that

T(r_{n},f_{2})\geq\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}(\log((\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]-\varepsilon)(\exp(\beta(\log\gamma(r_{n}))))^{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]}))\right\}

and for sufficiently large values of

We know that
T(r,f_{1}\pm f_{2})\geq T(r,f_{2})-T(r,f_{1})-\log 2
, we
obtain from above two inequalities that

\displaystyle T(r_{n},f_{1}\pm f_{2})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}(\log((\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]-\varepsilon)(\exp(\beta(\log\gamma(r_{n}))))^{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]}))\right\}

\displaystyle-\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}(\log((\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]+\varepsilon)(\exp(\beta(\log\gamma(r_{n}))))^{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]}))\right\}-\log 2

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}(\log((\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]-2\varepsilon)(\exp(\beta(\log\gamma(r_{n}))))^{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]}))\right\}

provided
\varepsilon
such that
0<2\varepsilon<\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]-\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]
. It follows from

2
that
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]

\alpha
and above, we obtain that

\frac{\exp(\alpha(\log T(r_{n},f_{1}\pm f_{2})))}{\left(\exp\left(\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r_{n}\right)\right)\right)\right)^{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]}}\geq\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]-2\varepsilon\text{,}

(2.1)

It remains to prove the converse inequality. Indeed, by applying (

)

\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]>\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[-f_{1}]

(2.2)

We deduce from (

\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]=\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]

Now, we prove that
\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\cdot f_{2}]=\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]

T(r,f_{1}\cdot f_{2})\geq T(r,f_{1})-T(r,f_{2})+O(1)
and a similar

\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\cdot f_{2}]\geq\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]\text{.}

(2.3)

Since
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\cdot f_{2}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]>\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}\left(\frac{1}{f_{1}}\right)

and therefore
\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\cdot f_{2}]=\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]
.

(ii) The definition of the
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-type implies that for
any given
\varepsilon>0
and for all
r
sufficiently large, we have

T(r,f_{i})\leq\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}(\log((\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{i}]+\varepsilon)(\exp\left(\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)\right))^{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{i}]}))\right\}\text{, }i=1,2\text{.}

(2.4)

By the assumption
0<\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]<+\infty
, we get that

\displaystyle T(r,f_{1}\pm f_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T(r,f_{1})+T(r,f_{2})+\log 2

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}(\log((\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]+\varepsilon)(\exp\left(\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)\right))^{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]}))\right\}

\displaystyle+\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}(\log((\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]+\varepsilon)(\exp\left(\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)\right))^{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]}))\right\}+\log 2

\leq\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}(\log((\max(\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}],\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}])+3\varepsilon)(\exp\left(\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)\right))^{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]}))\right\}\text{.}

By the monotonicity of
\alpha
and above, we obtain that

\frac{\exp(\alpha(\log T(r,f_{1}\pm f_{2})))}{\left(\exp\left(\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)\right)\right)^{\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]}}\leq\max(\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}],\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}])+3\varepsilon\text{.}

Since
\varepsilon>0
can be chosen arbitrarily, then we get that

\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]\leq\max\{\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}],\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]\}\text{.}

(2.5)

Without loss of generality, we may suppose
\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]<\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]
, then by (
2.5
) and
since
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[-f_{1}]
, it
follows that

\displaystyle\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}-f_{1}]

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\{\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}],\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]\}=\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]\text{.}

We deduce from (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) that

\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\pm f_{2}]=\max\{\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}],\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]\}\text{.}

(iii) By a similar discussion as in the above proof and the fact that
T(r,f_{1}\cdot f_{2})\leq T(r,f_{2})+T(r,f_{1})
, one can prove that

\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\cdot f_{2}]\leq\max\{\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}],\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]\}\text{.}

(2.7)

On the other hand, if we suppose that
\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]<\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]
, then by (
2.7
) and
since
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\cdot f_{2}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}\left(\frac{1}{f_{1}}\right)
, we get

\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]=\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}\left(f_{1}\cdot f_{2}\frac{1}{f_{1}}\right)\leq\max\{\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\cdot f_{2}],\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}]\}=\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\cdot f_{2}]\text{.}

(2.8)

It follows from (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) that
\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}\cdot f_{2}]=\max\{\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{1}],\tau_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f_{2}]\}
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
f
be a meromorphic function of order
\sigma_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)}[f]

=\sigma
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, for any
given
\varepsilon>0
,

m\left(r,\frac{f^{(k)}}{f}\right)=O(\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}((\sigma+\varepsilon)\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right))\right\})\text{,}

outside, possibly, an exceptional set
F\subset\left[0,+\infty\right)

of finite linear measure.

Proof.

Let
k=1
. The definition of
\sigma_{(\alpha(\log),\beta,\gamma)}[f]

implies that for any given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
r_{0}>1
, such
that for all
r>r_{0}
,

T(r,f)\leq\exp^{[2]}\left\{\alpha^{-1}((\sigma+\varepsilon)\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right))\right\}\text{.}

(4.1)

It follows from (
4.1
) and the lemma of logarithmic derivative and the
condition
\alpha(\log^{[2]}x)=o(\beta(\log\gamma(x)))
as
x\rightarrow+\infty
that

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(\log T(r,f)+\log r)

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}((\sigma+\varepsilon)\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right))\right\}\right),\text{ }r\notin F,

where
F\subset\left[0,+\infty\right)
is of finite linear measure.

Now, we assume that for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

m\left(r,\frac{f^{(k)}}{f}\right)=O\left(\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}((\sigma+\varepsilon)\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right))\right\}\right),\text{ }r\notin F.

(4.3)

Since
N(r,f^{(k)})\leq(k+1)N(r,f)
, we deduce

\displaystyle T(r,f^{(k)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m(r,f^{(k)})+N(r,f^{(k)})

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{f^{(k)}}{f}\right)+m(r,f)+(k+1)N(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(k+1)T(r,f)+O\left(\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}((\sigma+\varepsilon)\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right))\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O\left(\exp^{[2]}\left\{\alpha^{-1}((\sigma+\varepsilon)\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right))\right\}\right)\text{.}

It follows from (
4.2
) and (
4.4
) that

m\left(r,\frac{f^{(k+1)}}{f^{(k)}}\right)=m\left(r,\frac{\left(f^{(k)}\right)^{\prime}}{f^{(k)}}\right)=O(\log T(r,f^{\left(k\right)})+\log r)

=O\left(\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}((\sigma+\varepsilon)\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right))\right\}\right),r\notin F.

Thus

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{f^{(k+1)}}{f}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{f^{(k+1)}}{f^{(k)}}\right)+m\left(r,\frac{f^{(k)}}{f}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}((\sigma+\varepsilon)\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right))\right\}\right),\text{ }r\notin F\text{.}

Lemma 3
.

Let
f
be a meromorphic function. Then
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f^{\prime}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]
.

Proof.

Set
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]=\sigma
. From the definition of
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-order, for any given
\varepsilon>0
, there
exists
r_{0}>1
, such that for all
r\geq r_{0}
,

\log T(r,f)\leq\alpha^{-1}((\sigma+\varepsilon)\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right))\text{.}

Obviously,
T(r,f^{\prime})\leq 2T(r,f)+m\left(r,\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)
. By the lemma of logarithmic derivative (p. 34 in
[
14
]
) and
the condition
\alpha(\log^{[2]}x)=o(\beta(\log\gamma(x)))
as
x\rightarrow+\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\log T(r,f^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log T(r,f)+\log\left\{O\left(\log r+\log T\left(r,f\right)\right)\right\}+O\left(1\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log T(r,f)+\log\left(\log r\right)+\log\log T\left(r,f\right)+O\left(1\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha^{-1}\left(\left(\sigma+4\varepsilon\right)\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)\right),\text{ }r\notin F,

where
F\subset[0,+\infty)
is a set of finite linear measure. By
Lemma
2
, for
d=2
and all
r>r_{0},
we have

\log T(r,f^{\prime})\leq\alpha^{-1}\left(\left(\sigma+4\varepsilon\right)\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(2r\right)\right)\right).

Therefore, by using
\gamma(2r)\leq 2\gamma(r)
and
\beta(x+O(1))=(1+o(1))\beta(x)
as
x\rightarrow+\infty

\alpha\left(\log T(r,f^{\prime})\right)\leq\left(\sigma+4\varepsilon\right)\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(2r\right)\right)\leq\left(\sigma+4\varepsilon\right)\beta\left(\log\left(2\gamma\left(r\right)\right)\right)

=\left(\sigma+4\varepsilon\right)\beta\left(\log 2+\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)=\left(\sigma+4\varepsilon\right)(1+o(1))\beta\left(\log\left(\gamma\left(r\right)\right)\right).

It is implies that
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]\geq\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f^{\prime}]
.

On the other hand, we prove the inequality
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]\leq\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f^{\prime}]
. The
definition of
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f^{\prime}]=\sigma^{\prime}
implies that for any given above
\varepsilon>0
and for all
r

sufficiently large, we have

T(r,f^{\prime})\leq\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}((\sigma^{\prime}+\varepsilon)\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right))\right\}\text{.}

Since
T(r,f)\leq O(T(2r,f^{\prime})+\log r)
,
r\rightarrow+\infty
(cf.

[
36
]
), then by using
\gamma(2r)\leq 2\gamma(r)
,
\beta(x+O(1))=(1+o(1))\beta(x)
and
\alpha(\log^{[2]}x)=o(\beta(\log\gamma(x)))
as
x\rightarrow+\infty,
we can get that

\displaystyle T(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O\left(\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}((\sigma^{\prime}+\varepsilon)\beta(\log\gamma(2r))\right\}\right)+\log r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}\left(\left(\sigma^{\prime}+2\varepsilon\right)\beta\left(\log\left(\gamma\left(2r\right)\right)\right)\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\alpha^{-1}\left(\left(\sigma^{\prime}+2\varepsilon\right)\left(1+o\left(1\right)\right)\beta\left(\log\left(\gamma\left(r\right)\right)\right)\right)\right\},

i.e.,~\log T(r,f))\leq\alpha^{-1}((\sigma^{\prime}+2\varepsilon)\left(1+o\left(1\right)\right)\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right))),

i.e.,~\alpha\left(\log T\left(r,f\right)\right)\leq(\sigma^{\prime}+2\varepsilon)\left(1+o\left(1\right)\right)\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right),\text{ }r\rightarrow+\infty\text{.}

Since
\varepsilon>0
is an arbitrary number, we obtain that
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]\leq\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f^{\prime}]
and therefore
\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f^{\prime}]=\sigma_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}[f]
. Hence the proof
follows.

Lemma 6
.

f

(<\sigma_{0})

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-order implies that there exists

(R_{j})_{j=1}^{+\infty}

\left(1+\frac{1}{j}\right)R_{j}<R_{J+1},\text{ \ \ \ \ \ }\underset{j\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}\frac{\alpha(\log T(R_{j},f))}{\beta(\log\gamma(R_{j}))}=\sigma_{0}\text{.}

\varepsilon\in(0,\sigma_{0}-\mu)

j_{1}

\alpha(\log T(R_{j},f))>(\sigma_{0}-\varepsilon)\beta(\log\gamma(R_{j}))\text{.}

\frac{\sigma_{0}-\varepsilon}{\mu}>\frac{\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{j}\right)R_{j}\right)\right)}{\beta(\log\gamma(R_{j}))}\text{.}

It follows from this inequality and (

and for any

\displaystyle\alpha\left(\log T\left(r,f\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\alpha(\log T(R_{j},f))>(\sigma_{0}-\varepsilon)\beta(\log\gamma(R_{j}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{0}-\varepsilon}{\mu}\mu\frac{\beta(\log\gamma(R_{j}))}{\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)}\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{0}-\varepsilon}{\mu}\frac{\beta(\log\gamma(R_{j}))}{\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{j}\right)R_{j}\right)\right)}\mu\beta\left(\log\gamma\left(r\right)\right)

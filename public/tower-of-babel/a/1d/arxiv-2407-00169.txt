On Relative Cohomology for Lie Groupoids and Lie Algebroids
María Amelia Salazar

Abstract
. Motivated by our attempt to understand characteristic classes of Lie groupoids and geometric structures, we are brought back to the fundamentals of the cohomology theories of Lie groupoids and algebroids. One element that was missing in the literature was the notion of relative cohomology in this setting. The main aim of this paper is to develop the structural theory of this notion, the relation between the relative cohomology of groupoids and that of algebroids via van Est maps, and to indicate how it can be used to provide an intrinsic definition of characteristic classes.



1.
Introduction

The cohomology of Lie groups and Lie algebras has been used successfully to study the interaction between the algebraic and geometric properties of these objects. Lie algebra cohomology was first introduced by Élie Cartan in
[
6
]
, and later extended by Chevalley and Eilenberg in
[
7
]
. It was originally used to study the topology of Lie groups and homogeneous spaces by connecting de Rham cohomology with the properties of the Lie algebra. On the other hand, Lie group cohomology is closely related to the cohomology of the classifying space (see the work of Stasheff
[
24
]
). Its relation to Lie algebra cohomology has been extensively studied and produced many classical results. In particular, van Est
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
in one of his results produces a map
VE_{G}
from the cochain complex that computes the differentiable cohomology of a Lie group
G
to the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of its Lie algebra; under some connectivity assumptions on
G
, this induces an isomorphism in cohomology.

Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids are a far-reaching generalization of Lie groups and Lie algebras respectively, and have been used to codify geometric structures and their symmetries (foliations, Poisson manifolds and (other) related structures, PDEs, etc). Their cohomologies are natural extensions of those for Lie groups and Lie algebras, and have been relevant when applied to geometric structures such as foliated cohomology and Poisson cohomology (see, e.g.
[
4
,
12
]
for foliated cohomology and
[
13
,
9
,
10
,
21
]
for Poisson cohomology). The relation between the cohomology of Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids started with the work of Weinstein and Xu in
[
30
]
, where they first extended the van Est map to a map
VE_{G}:\mathsf{C}(G)\to\mathsf{C}(A)
from the cochain complex that computes the differentiable cohomology of a Lie groupoid
G
to the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of its Lie algebroid
A
. Later Crainic
[
13
]
produced other versions of van Est results; in
op. cit.
he also defined characteristic classes of representations of Lie algebroids, extending the classical characteristic classes of flat vector bundles
[
1
,
18
]
, and the modular class of Poisson manifolds
[
14
]
. (See also
[
8
,
15
]
for characteristic classes of Lie algebroids.)

Motivated by understanding characteristic classes of representations of Lie algebroids intrinsically and by our attempt to develop a parallel theory of characteristic classes of representations of Lie groupoids, we are brought back to understanding the fundamentals of the cohomology theories of Lie groupoids and algebroids. One element that was notably missing in the literature was the notion of
relative cohomology
for Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids. The main aim of this paper is to introduce and study this notion, as well as to indicate how it can be used to define characteristic classes for both Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids. (These will be studied in more detail in future work.) This approach generalizes that for Lie groups and Lie algebras, where relative cohomology is used to define characteristic classes of flat bundles in various contexts (see
[
18
, Chapter 6]
). Throughout we pay special attention to “van Est type” results, which relate the notions at the global (Lie groupoid) and infinitesimal (Lie algebroid) level. Using the language of groupoids, we find novel and explicit constructions. In what follows, we illustrate the structure of the paper and the main results.

Relative cohomology of Lie algebroids (Section
3
).
In order to define relative cohomology in the context of Lie algebras and Lie groups, one deals with the
\mathfrak{k}
-basic subcomplex
\mathsf{C}(\mathfrak{g})_{\mathfrak{k}-\mathrm{basic}}
of the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex
\mathsf{C}(\mathfrak{g})
of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}
, consisting of cochains that are
\mathfrak{k}
-horizontal and
\mathfrak{k}
-invariant under the coadjoint action when restricted to
\mathfrak{k}^{*}
. Similarly, when
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{k}
are the Lie algebras of a Lie group
G
and a Lie subgroup
K
respectively, one considers the
K
-basic subcomplex
\mathsf{C}(\mathfrak{g})_{K-\mathrm{basic}}
; in this case, the relevant action is the restriction of the coadjoint action on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
to
K
. Generalizing these notions to Lie algebroids and Lie groupoids is not obvious as, in general, a Lie algebroid
A
does not act on itself, nor does a Lie groupoid act on its Lie algebroid. To solve this issue, we use the key observation that when
B\subseteq A
is a Lie subalgebroid over the same base, there is a natural action of
B
on the quotient
A/B
; similarly, if
K\subseteq G
is a Lie subgroupoid integrating
B
, then
K
acts naturally on
A/B
. This allows us to define and give the main properties of the
B
-basic subcomplex and the
K
-basic subcomplex respectively

\mathsf{C}(A)_{B-\mathrm{basic}}\subseteq\mathsf{C}(A),\ \ \mathsf{C}(A)_{K-\mathrm{basic}}\subseteq\mathsf{C}(A),

which coincide with the standard notions in the context of Lie algebras and Lie groups.

The transitive case (Section
4
).
Here we assume the existence of a wide transitive Lie subgroupoid
K\subseteq G
. We show that there is a natural extension map

\mathrm{ext}\colon\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(G_{z})^{K_{z}}\to\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(G)^{K},

where
K_{z}
,
G_{z}
are the isotropy Lie groups of
K
and
G
respectively, over a point
z
of the base. This map is an isomorphism of chain complexes with inverse given by the restriction (see Theorem
4.2
). An analogous result holds true (see Theorem
4.4
) when looking at the infinitesimal counterpart:

\mathrm{ext}\colon\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}_{z})_{K_{z}\mathrm{-basic}}\to\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(A)_{K\mathrm{-basic}},

where
A
is the Lie algebroid of
G
and
\mathfrak{g}_{z}
is the isotropy Lie algebra. Our theory shows that, in this case, the relative cohomology of Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids is the same as the relative cohomology of their isotropy Lie groups and Lie algebras.

Van Est maps (Section
5
).
In this section we study the van Est maps in the context of relative cohomology, and pay special attention to the case where the Lie subgroupoid
K
is proper. We make use of
tubular structures
and
Cartan decompositions
of
(G,K)
(see Definitions
5.1
and
5.2
) to obtain van Est integration maps (i.e., cochain maps going from the relative cohomology of the Lie algebroid to the relative cohomology of the Lie groupoid). As a consequence, we conclude that the van Est differentiation map (see equation (
39
))

VE_{G/K}\colon\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(G)\to\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(A)_{K\mathrm{-basic}}

is a homotopy equivalence when
K
is a proper subgroupoid (see Theorem
5.14
). For example, for a Lie group
G
with finitely many connected components and
K\subseteq G
a maximal compact subgroup, tubular structures always exist (see e.g.
[
2
, Chapter VII]
). To prove the results we use the Perturbation Lemma, treated in the Appendix. We apply the previous results to conclude that in the transitive case, the differentiable cohomology of the Lie groupoid is homotopy equivalent to the cohomology of its isotropy Lie algebra relative to the maximal compact subgroup of its isotropy Lie group (see Theorem
5.19
).

Characteristic classes (Section
6
).
As an application of our theory we introduce the definition of characteristic classes of a representation of a Lie groupoid.
This also allows us to give an alternative, simple and direct definition
of the existing notion of characteristic classes of a representation of a Lie algebroid (see
[
13
,
8
,
15
]
), and to relate them with our definition of characteristic classes for Lie groupoids.
This represents the first step to study characteristic classes of Lie groupoids endowed with a geometric structure, e.g., symplectic, symplectic-Nijenhuis, presymplectic, Pfaffian, or contact groupoids.
At the infinitesimal level, we also hope that our approach will bring a new insight into existing characteristic classes of Poisson manifolds (the modular class), foliations (Bott’s characteristic classes), and other types of geometries encoded by Lie algebroids. This will be studied in a future paper.

Conventions

All the groupoids appearing in this paper are assumed to be Hausdorff. Moreover, any Lie subgroupoid
K
of a Lie groupoid
G
is assumed to be wide, i.e. the inclusion
K\hookrightarrow G
is an injective immersion of Lie groupoids and
K
has the same base as
G
. Analogously, any Lie subalgebroid
B
of a Lie algebroid
A
has the same base as
A
.

2.
Relative cohomology of Lie groupoids

We will review the cochain complexes for Lie groupoids, and define the relative cohomology with respect to a Lie subgroupoid. We will give the main properties of these notions and exhibit equivalences between cochain complexes. In Theorem
2.6
, we show that the cohomology of a Lie groupoid relative to a proper subgroupoid computes its differentiable cohomology.

The inclusion

By Lemma

4.
The transitive case

In this section, we construct extension maps from the basic subcomplexes of the isotropy Lie groups and isotropy Lie algebras (see Remark
3.5
), to the relative cochain complexes of transitive Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids respectively. The outcome is that in the transitive case, the relative cochain complexes of Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids are naturally isomorphic to the cochain basic subcomplexes of their isotropy Lie groups and Lie algebras respectively (see Theorems

Throughout this section, let

be a Lie groupoid over

Lie subgroupoid, i.e., it has only one orbit:
\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{s}^{-1}(m))=M

is also transitive.

4.1.
Lie groupoid cohomology relative to a transitive subgroupoid

Fix an element

for any function

. Hence,

(38)

\mathcal{L}_{\big(C_{b,*}(\xi_{s(1)})\big)^{(1)}}\cdots\mathcal{L}_{\big(C_{b,*}(\xi_{s(p)})\big)^{(p)}}(f)|_{(C_{b}(m),\ldots C_{b}(m))}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{s(1)}^{(1)}}\cdots\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{s(p)}^{(p)}}(f)|_{(m,\ldots,m)}

Putting together (
37
) and (
38
) we obtain that
VE_{G}(f)\in\mathsf{C}^{p}(A)^{K}
.

Finally, to see that when restricted to
\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}^{\bullet}(G)^{K}

becomes an algebra map, recall the following: When considering the normalized subcomplex
\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}^{\bullet}(G)\subseteq\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(G)

]

\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(A)_{K-\mathrm{basic}}\subseteq\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(A)

∎

5.4.

The manifold

\big(\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(A)_{K\mathrm{-basic}},{\mathsf{d}}_{CE}\big)\simeq\big(\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}}(G/K)^{L},{\mathsf{d}}_{Rh}\big).

Similarly, elements in the double complex

(47)

\beta\colon B_{p}G\to\Omega^{q}_{\mathcal{F}}(G/K)

({\mathsf{d}}\beta)(g_{1},\ldots,g_{p})=(-1)^{p}{\mathsf{d}}_{Rh}\beta(g_{1},\ldots,g_{p}),

(49)

\displaystyle(\delta\beta)(g_{1},\ldots,g_{p+1})

\displaystyle=\beta(g_{2},\ldots,g_{p+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}(-1)^{i}\beta(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i}g_{i+1},\ldots,g_{p+1})

(For

The horizontal augmentation map
{\mathsf{i}}
is the inclusion of the invariant forms (

\mathsf{j}

with

\lambda\colon[0,1]\times G/K\to G/K,\ (t,gK)\mapsto\lambda_{t}(gK).

\mathsf{D}(G,K)_{K\mathrm{-basic}}

\mathsf{k}
, hence implying that

lands in
\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}^{\bullet}(G)^{K}
. To see that in this case

is an algebra map in cohomology, note that in this case

VE_{G}|_{\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}^{\bullet}(G)^{K}}\circ R_{G/K}=\mathrm{id}.

As

is an algebra map by Theorem

,
we then obtain that, when passing to cohomology,

is an isomorphism with inverse the algebra map
VE_{G}|_{\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}^{\bullet}(G)^{K}}
. This implies that in cohomology

is an algebra map.
∎

5.6.

Let

and let

at the cochain level, described in terms of cochains in

In the first part, we find explicit generators for

and

to find the generators of

being the

Note that the inclusion map

For the second step, we use the

First, we identify the quotient

6.
Characteristic classes

In this section, we introduce the definition of characteristic classes of a representation of a Lie groupoid, and relate them with the existing notion of characteristic classes of a representation of a Lie algebroid (see
[
13
,
8
,
15
]
).

Throughout, we deal with cohomologies with real coefficients.

6.1.
Background on representations

A
representation
of a Lie groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
is a (left)
G
-action on a (real or complex) vector bundle
V\to M
so that for any
g\in G
with
\mathsf{s}(g)=m,\mathsf{t}(g)={m^{\prime}}
, the induced map
V_{m}\to V_{m^{\prime}},\ e\mapsto ge
is a linear isomorphism. Alternatively, a representation can be encoded as follows:
Associated to any vector bundle
V\to M
, we consider the gauge groupoid
GL(V)\rightrightarrows M
of linear isomorphism between the fibers

GL(V)=\{\phi\colon V_{m}\to V_{m^{\prime}}\mid m,m^{\prime}\in M\ \mathrm{and}\ \phi\ \mathrm{is\ a\ linear\ isomorphism}\},

with source and target
\mathsf{s}(\phi)=m,\mathsf{t}(\phi)=m^{\prime}
, and multiplication given by composition.
A representation of a Lie groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
is equivalently described as a Lie groupoid morphism

\Phi\colon G\to GL(V)

over the identity map of
M
.

Passing to the infinitesimal picture, a representation of a Lie algebroid
A\to M
on a vector bundle
V\to M
is a connection-like operator
\nabla:\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(V)\to\Gamma(V)
, satisfying the Leibniz rule w.r.t. the anchor map, and so that it is flat. In analogy with the groupoid case a representation can be alternatively described as a Lie algebroid morphism

\Psi\colon A\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)

over the identity map, where
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is the Lie algebroid of
GL(V)
.

If
A
is the Lie algebroid of
G
, then any representation
\Phi:G\to GL(V)
induces a representation of
A
by

(59)

\Psi:=\mathrm{Lie}(\Phi)\colon A\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)

with
\mathrm{Lie}(\Phi)=d\Phi|_{A}.

The following result is used and proved in Section
6.3
, and may be interesting in its own right. We remind the reader that we are dealing with real coefficient in the cohomology.

Corollary 6.1
.

For a complex vector bundle
V,
the van Est map
VE_{GL(V)}\colon H(GL(V))\to H(\mathfrak{gl}(V))
is injective.

6.2.
Characteristic classes for algebroids revisited

In this section we focus on an alternative, simple and direct definition of the characteristic classes of representations of Lie algebroids first defined in
[
13
]
(see also
[
8
,
15
]

Let

be a Lie algebroid over

,

where the

be a double complex, concentrated in non-negative degrees.
Let
(\mathsf{X}^{\bullet},{\mathsf{d}})
be a cochain complex. A morphism of double complexes

{\mathsf{i}}\colon\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{D}

(where
\mathsf{X}^{\bullet}
is regarded as a double complex concentrated in bidegrees
(0,\bullet)
) will be called a
horizontal augmentation map
.
Passing to total complexes,
{\mathsf{i}}
becomes a cochain map from
X^{\bullet}
to the cochain complex
(\operatorname{Tot}^{\bullet}(\mathsf{D}),{\mathsf{d}}+\delta)
. The Perturbation Lemma, due to Brown
[
3
]
and Gugenheim
[
16
]
,
allows us to turn a homotopy operator for the horizontal differential
\delta
into a homotopy operator with respect to the total differential
{\mathsf{d}}+\delta
.

Lemma A.1

(Perturbation Lemma)
.

Suppose
\mathsf{h}\colon\mathsf{D}\to\mathsf{D}
is a linear map of bidegree
(-1,0)
such that

[\mathsf{h},\delta]=1-{\mathsf{i}}\circ\mathsf{p}

for some degree
0
map

\mathsf{p}\colon\mathsf{D}^{0,\bullet}\to\mathsf{X}^{\bullet}
.
Put

\mathsf{h}^{\prime}=\mathsf{h}(1+{\mathsf{d}}\mathsf{h})^{-1}
and
\mathsf{p}^{\prime}=\mathsf{p}(1+{\mathsf{d}}\mathsf{h})^{-1}
.
Then

[\mathsf{h}^{\prime},{\mathsf{d}}+\delta]=1-{\mathsf{i}}\circ\mathsf{p}^{\prime}.

Here,
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the
graded
commutator, e.g.
[\mathsf{h},\delta]=\mathsf{h}\delta+\delta\mathsf{h}
.

We shall assume from now on that
\mathsf{p}\circ{\mathsf{i}}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathsf{X}}
, so that
{\mathsf{i}}
is injective and
{\mathsf{i}}\circ\mathsf{p}
is a projection onto the image of
{\mathsf{i}}
. Then also
\mathsf{p}^{\prime}\circ{\mathsf{i}}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathsf{X}}
, and
{\mathsf{i}}\circ\mathsf{p}^{\prime}
is again a projection.
In other words,
{\mathsf{i}}\colon\mathsf{X}^{\bullet}\to\operatorname{Tot}^{\bullet}(\mathsf{D})
is a homotopy equivalence, with
\mathsf{p}^{\prime}
a homotopy inverse.

In our applications, there is another cochain complex
(\mathsf{Y}^{\bullet},\delta)
, with a

vertical augmentation map

\mathsf{j}\colon\mathsf{Y}\to\mathsf{D}

(thus
\mathsf{Y}^{\bullet}
is regarded as a double complex concentrated in bidegrees
(\bullet,0)
). The horizontal homotopy
\mathsf{h}
allows us to ‘invert’ the second cochain map in

\mathsf{Y}^{\bullet}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{j}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Tot}^{\bullet}(\mathsf{D})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathsf{i}}}}{{\longleftarrow}}\mathsf{X}^{\bullet},

thereby producing a cochain map

\mathsf{p}^{\prime}\circ\mathsf{j}=\mathsf{p}\circ(1+{\mathsf{d}}\mathsf{h})^{-1}\circ\mathsf{j}\colon\mathsf{Y}^{\bullet}\to\mathsf{X}^{\bullet}
.
On elements of degree
p
, this is given by

(69)

(-1)^{p}\mathsf{p}\circ({\mathsf{d}}\mathsf{h})^{p}\circ\mathsf{j}\colon\mathsf{Y}^{p}\to\mathsf{X}^{p},

Consider now the situation that the vertical differential has a homotopy operator

\mathsf{k}\colon\mathsf{D}^{p,q}\to\mathsf{D}^{p,q-1},\ \ [{\mathsf{d}},\mathsf{k}]=1-\mathsf{j}\circ\mathsf{q},

where

\mathsf{q}\colon\mathsf{D}^{0,\bullet}\to\mathsf{Y}^{\bullet}
is a cochain map for
{\mathsf{d}}
with
\mathsf{q}\circ\mathsf{j}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathsf{Y}}
. Then we can apply the Perturbation Lemma
A.1
to
this vertical homotopy, and we obtain a cochain map
\mathsf{q}\circ(1+\delta\mathsf{k})^{-1}\circ{\mathsf{i}}\colon\mathsf{X}^{\bullet}\to\mathsf{Y}^{\bullet}
given on degree
p
elements by

(70)

(-1)^{p}\mathsf{q}\circ(\delta\mathsf{k})^{p}\circ{\mathsf{i}}\colon\mathsf{X}^{p}\to\mathsf{Y}^{p}.

The following result will be used to relate van Est ‘integration’ and ‘differentiation’ maps.

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose the homotopy operators
\mathsf{h},\mathsf{k}
satisfy

(71)

\mathsf{h}\circ\mathsf{k}=0,\ \ \ \ \mathsf{p}\circ\mathsf{k}=0.

Then (
70
) followed by
(
69
) is the identity map of
\mathsf{X}^{p}
.

For our application we start with a Lie groupoid
G
with Lie algebroid
A
. The two complexes
\mathsf{X}=\mathsf{C}(A),\ \,\mathsf{Y}=\mathsf{C}(G)
are related by a double complex, due to Crainic
[
13
]
. Recall from the discussion around (
5
) that

\kappa\colon EG\to BG

is a simplicial principal bundle, where for each
p
, the
G
-action on
E_{p}G
happens along the map
\pi_{p}\colon E_{p}G\to M
. The
double complex

(72)

(\mathsf{D}^{\bullet,\bullet}(G),\delta,{\mathsf{d}})

is defined as follows.

•

The bigraded summands of the double complex
are

(73)

\mathsf{D}^{p,q}(G)=\Gamma\big(\pi_{p}^{*}(\wedge^{q}A^{*})\big).

•

\delta
is the simplicial differential on sections of the simplicial vector bundle

\pi_{\bullet}^{*}(\wedge^{q}A^{*})\to E_{\bullet}G.

•

{\mathsf{d}}=(-1)^{p}{\mathsf{d}}_{CE}
on elements of bidegree
(p,q)
, is the Chevalley-Eilenberg differential on

(74)

\mathsf{D}^{p,\bullet}(G)\cong\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(\pi_{p}^{*}A)

Specifically, let
\mathcal{F}
be the foliation of
E_{p}G
given by the
\kappa_{p}
-fibers; thus
T_{\mathcal{F}}E_{p}G
is the vertical bundle. The isomorphism

\pi_{p}^{*}A\cong T_{\mathcal{F}}E_{p}G
defines an algebroid structure on

\pi_{p}^{*}A
.

Furthermore, the double complex comes with horizontal and vertical augmentation maps:

•

{\mathsf{i}}\colon\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(A)\to\mathsf{D}^{0,\bullet}(G)
is given in degree
q
by the pullback
\pi_{0}^{*}
(using (
74
) for
p=0
).

•

\mathsf{j}\colon\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(G)\to\mathsf{D}^{\bullet,0}(G)
is given in degree
p
by the pullback map
\kappa_{p}^{*}
(using (
5
)).

The augmentation maps define cochain maps to the total complex

\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{j}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Tot}^{\bullet}(\mathsf{D}(G))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathsf{i}}}}{{\longleftarrow}}\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(A).

Differentiation

The double complex
(\mathsf{D}^{\bullet,\bullet}(G),\delta,{\mathsf{d}})
has a horizontal homotopy. Consider the maps

h_{p}\colon E_{p}G\to E_{p+1}G,\ (g_{1},\ldots,g_{p};g)\mapsto(g_{1},\ldots,g_{p},g;m),

where
m=\mathsf{s}(g)
. Since
\pi_{p+1}\circ h_{p}=\pi_{p}
, these lift to fiberwise isomorphisms of vector bundles
\pi_{p}^{*}(\wedge^{q}A^{*})\to\pi_{p+1}^{*}(\wedge^{q}A^{*})
, defining a pullback map on sections.
The map

\mathsf{h}\colon\mathsf{D}^{p,q}(G)\to\mathsf{D}^{p-1,q}(G),\ \ \psi\mapsto(-1)^{p}h_{p-1}^{*}\psi

satisfies

[\mathsf{h},\delta]=1-{\mathsf{i}}\circ\mathsf{p},

where
\mathsf{p}\colon\mathsf{D}^{0,\bullet}(G)\to\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(A)
is the
left inverse to
{\mathsf{i}}=\pi_{0}^{*}

given by pullback under the inclusion
u\colon M\hookrightarrow E_{0}G=G
:

\mathsf{p}={\mathsf{u}}^{*}\colon\Gamma(\pi_{0}^{*}(\wedge^{q}A^{*}))\to\Gamma(\wedge^{q}A^{*}).

The Perturbation Lemma
A.1
gives a new projection
\mathsf{p}^{\prime}=\mathsf{p}\circ(1+{\mathsf{d}}\mathsf{h})^{-1}
, which is a cochain map for the total differential
{\mathsf{d}}+\delta
, with
\mathsf{p}^{\prime}\circ{\mathsf{i}}=\operatorname{id}
. Thus,
{\mathsf{i}}
is a homotopy equivalence, with
\mathsf{p}^{\prime}
a homotopy inverse. As a result from
[
22
]
, we have the following:

Theorem A.3
.

The van Est differentiation map (
36
) is obtained as

VE_{G}=\mathsf{p}\circ(1+{\mathsf{d}}\mathsf{h})^{-1}\circ\mathsf{j}\colon\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(G)\to\mathsf{C}^{\bullet}(A).

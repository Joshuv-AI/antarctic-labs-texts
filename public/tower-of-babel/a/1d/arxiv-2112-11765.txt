A Degree Six Inequality on Convex Quadrilaterals
Mazen Bou Khuzam

Abstract
We prove a degree-six inequality on convex quadrilaterials. This inequality originated from work on the Atiyah-Sutcliffe conjectures on configurations of points in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} .



\bf{Keywords}
: Atiyah determinant ; Atiyah-Sutcliffe conjectures

This degree-six inequality seems to lie at the heart of the problem and will be used in a subsequent paper to prove the second Atiyah-Sutcliffe conjecture on convex quadrilaterals. We hope that this work would help in finding similar inequalities for

The Multiplicity-Two Terms

Proof of The Inequality

Now that we have simplified each group of terms in the previous two sections, we turn to the proof of inequality (1). We first prove the that the sum of the
X
,
Y
,
W
-terms, and
P_{1}-\frac{1}{2}
(which is the first line of Lemma 2) is non-negative. In particular, using the simplifications obtained in the previous two sections, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 2

: For the convex quadrilateral presented in figure 1 where
\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{3}\leq\pi

and
\gamma_{3}+\gamma_{4}\leq\pi
, we have

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sin X\sin\frac{W^{\prime}}{2}\sin\frac{Y}{2}\sin(\frac{\alpha_{1}-\beta_{4}}{2})&&&\\
+\sin Y\sin\frac{W}{2}\sin\frac{X}{2}\sin(\frac{\beta_{1}-\alpha_{2}}{2})&\begin{array}[]{c}-2\sin^{2}\frac{X}{2}\cos^{2}\frac{W}{2}\cos^{2}\frac{Y}{2}\\
\\
-2\cos^{2}\frac{X}{2}\cos^{2}\frac{W^{\prime}}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{Y}{2}\end{array}&\geq&0\\
+\sin W\cos\frac{X}{2}\cos\frac{Y}{2}\sin(\frac{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{3}}{2})&&&\end{array}

(4)

The first column in this inequality is nothing but the simplified expressions of the sum of the
X
,
Y
, and
W
-terms, whereas the second column is the first line in Lemma 2 (which is
P_{1}-\frac{1}{2}
). To prove Theorem 2, we first need the following corollary:

Corollary 1

: On the convex quadrilateral depicted in figure 1, we have the following 3 inequalities:

\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}-\beta_{4}}{2}\right)\right|\leq\sin\left(\frac{W^{\prime}-Y}{2}\right)

\left|\sin\left(\frac{\beta_{1}-\alpha_{2}}{2}\right)\right|\leq\sin\left(\frac{W-X}{2}\right)

\sin\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{3}}{2}\right)\geq\sin\left(\frac{X+Y}{2}\right)

Proof: By using
\sin(\frac{\alpha_{3}-\beta_{2}}{2})+\sin(\frac{\alpha_{3}+\beta_{2}}{2})=2\sin\frac{\alpha_{3}}{2}\cos\frac{\beta_{2}}{2}
and noticing that

\alpha_{3}-\beta_{2}=\alpha_{1}-\beta_{4}
and
\alpha_{3}+\beta_{2}=W^{\prime}-Y
, we conclude:

\sin(\frac{\alpha_{1}-\beta_{4}}{2})=\sin(\frac{Y-W^{\prime}}{2})+2\sin\frac{\alpha_{3}}{2}\cos\frac{\beta_{2}}{2}\geq\sin(\frac{Y-W^{\prime}}{2})

In addition,
\sin(\frac{\alpha_{3}-\beta_{2}}{2})\leq\sin(\frac{\alpha_{3}+\beta_{2}}{2})
implies that
\sin(\frac{\alpha_{1}-\beta_{4}}{2})\leq\sin(\frac{W^{\prime}-Y}{2})
which concludes the first inequality in this corollary.

The other two inequalities can be proved in a similar way by using

\sin(\frac{\beta_{3}-\alpha_{4}}{2})+\sin(\frac{\beta_{3}+\alpha_{4}}{2})=2\sin\frac{\beta_{3}}{2}\cos\frac{\alpha_{4}}{2}
for the second one and

\sin(\frac{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{3}}{2})-\sin(\frac{\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{3}}{2})=2\cos\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{\gamma_{3}}{2}
for the third one.

We also have to use that
\beta_{3}-\alpha_{4}=\beta_{1}-\alpha_{2}
and
\beta_{3}+\alpha_{4}=W-X
for the second inequality and
\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{3}=X+Y
for the last one.

\bf{Proof\,\,of\,\,Theorem\,2}
: Based on Corollary 1, the left hand side of (4) is not less than:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\sin X\sin\frac{W^{\prime}}{2}\sin\frac{Y}{2}\sin(\frac{Y-W^{\prime}}{2})&&\\
+\sin Y\sin\frac{W}{2}\sin\frac{X}{2}\sin(\frac{X-W}{2})&\begin{array}[]{c}-2\sin^{2}\frac{X}{2}\cos^{2}\frac{W}{2}\cos^{2}\frac{Y}{2}\\
-2\cos^{2}\frac{X}{2}\cos^{2}\frac{W^{\prime}}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{Y}{2}\end{array}&\\
+\sin W\cos\frac{X}{2}\cos\frac{Y}{2}\sin(\frac{X+Y}{2})&&\end{array}

Expanding
\displaystyle{\sin(X+Y)/2=\sin X/2\cos Y/2+\cos X/2\sin Y/2}
in the third line of the first column and combining the terms with the ones in the second column yields:

\begin{array}[]{cc}2\sin\frac{X}{2}\cos\frac{X}{2}\sin\frac{W^{\prime}}{2}\sin\frac{Y}{2}\sin(\frac{Y-W^{\prime}}{2})&\\
+2\sin\frac{Y}{2}\cos\frac{Y}{2}\sin\frac{W}{2}\sin\frac{X}{2}\sin(\frac{X-W}{2})&\\
+2\cos\frac{W}{2}\sin\frac{X}{2}\cos^{2}\frac{Y}{2}\left(\sin\frac{W}{2}\cos\frac{X}{2}-\cos\frac{W}{2}\sin\frac{X}{2}\right)&\\
+2\sin\frac{W}{2}\cos^{2}\frac{X}{2}\sin\frac{Y}{2}\left(\sin\frac{W^{\prime}}{2}\cos\frac{Y}{2}-\cos\frac{W^{\prime}}{2}\sin\frac{Y}{2}\right)&\end{array}

Note that the last two quatities in parentheses are nothing but
\displaystyle{\sin(W-X)/2}
and
\displaystyle{\sin(W^{\prime}-Y)/2}
. Combined with the first two rows gives us

2\cos\frac{X}{2}\sin\frac{Y}{2}\sin\left(\frac{W^{\prime}-Y}{2}\right)\left(-\sin\frac{X}{2}\cos\frac{W}{2}+\sin\frac{W}{2}\cos\frac{X}{2}\right)

+2\sin\frac{X}{2}\cos\frac{Y}{2}\sin(\frac{W-X}{2})\left(-\sin\frac{Y}{2}\cos\frac{W^{\prime}}{2}+\cos\frac{Y}{2}\sin\frac{W^{\prime}}{2}\right)

=2\cos\frac{X}{2}\sin\frac{Y}{2}\sin\left(\frac{W^{\prime}-Y}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{W-X}{2}\right)+2\sin\frac{X}{2}\cos\frac{Y}{2}\sin\left(\frac{W-X}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{W^{\prime}-Y}{2}\right)

=2\sin\left(\frac{W^{\prime}-Y}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{W-X}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{X+Y}{2}\right)\,\geq 0

This last expression is nonnegative because
W^{\prime}-Y=\alpha_{3}+\beta_{2}
,

, and these angles will be between

when divided by 2 (in addition to

), which conlcudes the proof of Theorem 2.

In order to finish the proof of the quadilaterial inequality (1), we need to show that the remaining terms give a positive answer when added to the second line of Lemma 2, which is

. Well, if the quadrilateral at hand satisfies

\sin\frac{\beta_{4}-\alpha_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{\alpha_{2}-\beta_{1}}{2}\geq 0

\displaystyle{2\sin\frac{W^{\prime}-Y}{2}\sin\frac{W-X}{2}\sin\frac{X+Y}{2}-2\sin\frac{\beta_{4}-\alpha_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{\alpha_{2}-\beta_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{\gamma_{3}+\gamma_{1}}{2}}

\displaystyle{\geq 2\sin\frac{\beta_{4}-\alpha_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{\alpha_{2}-\beta_{1}}{2}\left(\sin\frac{X+Y}{2}-\sin\frac{\gamma_{3}+\gamma_{1}}{2}\right)}

where the first inequality follows from Corollary 1 and the last equality comes from the fact that

. We can see that, under our assumption

\displaystyle{4\sin\frac{W}{2}\cos\frac{W}{2}\cos\frac{X}{2}\cos\frac{Y}{2}\cos\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{\gamma_{3}}{2}}

\displaystyle{-4\sin\frac{\beta_{4}-\alpha_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{\alpha_{2}-\beta_{1}}{2}\cos\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}\sin\frac{\gamma_{3}}{2}}

This is easily seen to be positive: We factor out

Diego DOMINICI

Abstract
We present some families of polynomials related to the moments of weight functions of hypergeometric type. We also consider different types of generating functions, and give several examples.

Introduction

Let
\{\mu_{n}\}
be a sequence of complex numbers and

\mathcal{L}\colon\mathbb{C}[x]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a linear
functional defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\big(x^{n}\big)=\mu_{n},\qquad n=0,1,\ldots.

Then,
\mathcal{L}
is called the
moment functional
determined by the
formal moment sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}
. The number
\mu_{n}
is
called the
moment
of order
n
. The task of finding an explicit
representation for the functional
\mathcal{L}
is called a
moment
problem

[
1
,
24
,
32
]
.

A sequence
\{\Pi_{n}(x)\}\subset\mathbb{C}[x]
, with
\deg(\Pi_{n})=n
is called an

orthogonal polynomial sequence
with respect to
\mathcal{L}
provided
that
[
4
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\Pi_{n}\Pi_{m})=K_{n}\delta_{n,m},\qquad n,m=0,1,\ldots,

where
K_{n}\neq 0
and
\delta_{n,m}
is Kronecker’s delta.

The moments play a fundamental role in the theory of orthogonal polynomials
since, among other results, we have the determinantal representation

\displaystyle\Pi_{n}(x)=C_{n}\left|\begin{matrix}\mu_{0}&\mu_{1}&\cdots&\mu_{n}\\
\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\cdots&\mu_{n+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mu_{n-1}&\mu_{n}&\cdots&\mu_{2n-1}\\
1&x&\cdots&x^{n}\end{matrix}\right|,

for some normalization constant
C_{n}\neq 0
, with

\displaystyle\left|\begin{matrix}\mu_{0}&\mu_{1}&\cdots&\mu_{n}\\
\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\cdots&\mu_{n+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mu_{n}&\mu_{n+1}&\cdots&\mu_{2n}\end{matrix}\right|\neq 0,\qquad n=0,1,\ldots.

Given their importance, it is very striking that they are not explicitly
listed in the standard books on orthogonal polynomials, or even in
encyclopedic texts such as
[

found a comprehensive enumeration of the moments of classical orthogonal
polynomials was the recent article
[

In this paper, we focus our attention on linear functionals defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{x=0}^{\infty}}f(x)\rho\big(x;\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta},c\big),

(1)

\rho(x;\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta},c)
is of the form

\displaystyle\rho\big(x;\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta},c\big)=\frac{(\vec{\alpha})_{x}}{(\vec{\beta}+1)_{x}}\frac{c^{x}}{x!},

(2)

\displaystyle(\vec{\alpha})_{x}=(\alpha_{1})_{x}(\alpha_{2})_{x}\cdots(\alpha_{p})_{x},\qquad(\vec{\beta}+1)_{x}=(\beta_{1}+1)_{x}(\beta_{2}+1)_{x}\cdots(\beta_{q}+1)_{x},

(a)_{x}
denotes the Pochhammer symbol (also called

[

\displaystyle(a)_{0}=1,\qquad(a)_{x}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+x-1),\qquad x\in\mathbb{N},

or by

\displaystyle(a)_{x}=\frac{\Gamma(a+x)}{\Gamma(a)},\qquad a+x\neq 0,-1,\ldots,

\Gamma(z)
is the Gamma function. Unless stated otherwise,

\displaystyle\beta_{i}>-1,\qquad 1\leq i\leq q,

and we will use the notation

\displaystyle\vec{\alpha}=\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p},\qquad\vec{\beta}+1=\beta_{1}+1,\beta_{2}+1,\ldots,\beta_{q}+1.

Note that we have

\displaystyle\frac{\rho(x;\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta},c)}{\rho(x;\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta},c)}=\frac{\eta(x)}{\phi(x+1)},

with

\displaystyle\phi(x)=x(x+\beta_{1})(x+\beta_{2})\cdots(x+\beta_{q}),\qquad\eta(x)=c(x+\alpha_{1})(x+\alpha_{2})\cdots(x+\alpha_{p}).

2
Main results

The function
\mu_{0}(c)
satisfies the differential equation

[
29
, (16.8.3)]

\displaystyle\big[\vartheta(\vartheta+\beta_{1})\cdots(\vartheta+\beta_{q})-c(\vartheta+\alpha_{1})\cdots(\vartheta+\alpha_{p})\big]\mu_{0}=0,

(13)

where
\vartheta
was defined in (
6
). We can rewrite the ODE (
13
) as

\displaystyle\vartheta^{q+1}\mu_{0}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{q}\sigma_{k}(c)\vartheta^{k}\mu_{0},\qquad q>p-1,

\displaystyle(1-c)\vartheta^{q+1}\mu_{0}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{q}\sigma_{k}(c)\vartheta^{k}\mu_{0},\qquad q=p-1,

(14)

\displaystyle c\vartheta^{p}\mu_{0}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{p-1}\sigma_{k}(c)\vartheta^{k}\mu_{0},\qquad q<p-1,

where the coefficients
\sigma_{k}(c)
are linear functions of
c
.

Introducing the quantities

\displaystyle\vec{\mu}(c)=\begin{bmatrix}\mu_{0}(c)\\
\mu_{1}(c)\\
\vdots\\
\mu_{\xi}(c)\end{bmatrix},\qquad\xi=\max\{p-1,q\},

and

\displaystyle(\lambda,\tau)=\begin{cases}(1,0),&q+1>p,\\
(1,-1),&q+1=p,\\
(0,1),&q+1<p,\end{cases}

we can rewrite (
14
) as

\displaystyle(\lambda+c\tau)\mu_{\xi+1}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\xi}\sigma_{k}(c)\mu_{k}.

If we define the
(\xi+1)\times(\xi+1)
matrix

\mathbf{M}(c)
by

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\sigma_{j},&i=\xi+1,\quad 0\leq j\leq\xi,\\
\lambda+c\tau,&j=i+1,\quad 0\leq i\leq\xi-1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}\,\vec{\mu}=(\lambda+c\tau)\vartheta\vec{\mu}.

(15)

We can now state our main result.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let the
(\xi+1)
-vector polynomials

\vec{P}_{n}(c)
be defined by

\displaystyle\vec{P}_{0}(c)=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{bmatrix}

(16)

and

\displaystyle\vec{P}_{n+1}=c(\lambda+c\tau)\frac{d\vec{P}_{n}}{dc}+\big(\mathbf{M}^{T}-n\tau c\mathbf{I}\big)\vec{P}_{n},\qquad n=0,1,\ldots,

(17)

where
\mathbf{I}
is the
(\xi+1)\times(\xi+1)

identity matrix. Then,

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(c)=(\lambda+c\tau)^{-n}\vec{P}_{n}(c)\cdot\vec{\mu}(c),\qquad n=0,1,\ldots.

(18)

Proof 2.2
.

Using (
15
) and (
17
), we have

\displaystyle\vec{P}_{n+1}\cdot\vec{\mu}=(\lambda+c\tau)\vartheta\vec{P}_{n}\cdot\vec{\mu}+\big(\mathbf{M}^{T}-n\tau c\mathbf{I}\big)\vec{P}_{n}\cdot\vec{\mu}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\vec{P}_{n+1}\cdot\vec{\mu}}{}=(\lambda+c\tau)\vartheta\vec{P}_{n}\cdot\vec{\mu}+\vec{P}_{n}\cdot(\mathbf{M}-n\tau c\mathbf{I})\vec{\mu}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\vec{P}_{n+1}\cdot\vec{\mu}}{}=(\lambda+c\tau)\vartheta\vec{P}_{n}\cdot\vec{\mu}+\vec{P}_{n}\cdot(\lambda+c\tau)\vartheta\vec{\mu}-n\tau c\vec{P}_{n}\cdot\vec{\mu}.

Multiplying by
(\lambda+c\tau)^{-n-1}
we get

\displaystyle(\lambda+c\tau)^{-n-1}\vec{P}_{n+1}\cdot\vec{\mu}=(\lambda+c\tau)^{-n}\vartheta\vec{P}_{n}\cdot\vec{\mu}+\vec{P}_{n}\cdot(\lambda+c\tau)^{-n}\vartheta\vec{\mu}-n\tau c(\lambda+c\tau)^{-n-1}\vec{P}_{n}\cdot\vec{\mu}

\displaystyle\hphantom{(\lambda+c\tau) ^{-n-1} \vec{P}_{n+1}\cdot\vec{\mu}}{}=\vartheta\big[(\lambda+c\tau)^{-n}\vec{P}_{n}\cdot\vec{\mu}\big].

Thus, the sequence

\displaystyle r_{n}=(\lambda+c\tau)^{-n}\vec{P}_{n}\cdot\vec{\mu}

satisfies the recurrence
r_{n+1}=\vartheta r_{n}
with initial condition

\displaystyle r_{0}=\vec{P}_{0}\cdot\vec{\mu}=\mu_{0}.

From (
5
), we conclude that
r_{n}=\mu_{n}
.

2.1
Generating functions

Let’s consider the exponential generating function for the moments, defined by
the formal power series

\displaystyle G_{\mu}(c,w)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}(c)\frac{w^{n}}{n!}.

Given that

\displaystyle\mu_{n+1}=c\mu_{n}^{\prime},

we have
[
33
]

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G_{\mu}}{\partial w}=c\frac{\partial G_{\mu}}{\partial c},

with general solution

\displaystyle G_{\mu}(c,w)=H\big(ce^{w}\big)

for some function
H(w)
. But since

\displaystyle G_{\mu}(c,0)=\mu_{0}(c),

we conclude that

\displaystyle G_{\mu}(c,w)=\mu_{0}\big(ce^{w}\big).

(19)

Using (
18
), we see that the exponential generating function for the
polynomials
\vec{P}_{n}(c)

\displaystyle\vec{G}_{P}(c,w)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\vec{P}_{n}(c)\frac{w^{n}}{n!}

satisfies

\displaystyle G_{\mu}(c,(\lambda+\tau c)w)=\vec{G}_{P}(c,w)\cdot\vec{\mu}(c),

or, using (
19
),

\displaystyle\vec{G}_{P}(c,w)\cdot\vec{\mu}(c)=\mu_{0}\big(ce^{(\lambda+\tau c)w}\big).

(20)

2.2
Stieltjes transform

A different type of generating function for the moments that is very important
in the theory of orthogonal polynomials is the Stieltjes transform (or
Z

transform), that can be defined by the formal Laurent series

\displaystyle S_{\mu}(c,z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{n}(c)}{z^{n+1}}.

We have

\displaystyle S_{\mu}(c,z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{n}(c)}{z^{n+1}}=\sum\limits_{n=-1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{n+1}(c)}{z^{n+2}}=\frac{1}{z}\sum\limits_{n=-1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{n+1}(c)}{z^{n+1}}=\frac{1}{z}\left[\mu_{0}(c)+\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{n+1}(c)}{z^{n+1}}\right].

Hence,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{n+1}(c)}{z^{n+1}}=zS_{\mu}(c,z)-\mu_{0}(c)

(21)

and using (
5
), we get

\displaystyle c\frac{\partial S_{\mu}}{\partial c}=zS_{\mu}-\mu_{0}(c).

(22)

From (
4
), we have

\displaystyle S_{\mu}(0,z)=\frac{1}{z}

and solving (
22
) we obtain

\displaystyle S_{\mu}(c,z)=-c^{z}\int_{0}^{c}\frac{\mu_{0}(x)}{x^{z+1}}dx=-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mu_{0}(ct)}{t^{z+1}}dt.

Using the recurrence relation for the Gamma function, we can write

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mu_{0}(ct)}{t^{z+1}}dt=\frac{1}{z}\frac{\Gamma(1-z)}{\Gamma(1)\Gamma(-z)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mu_{0}(ct)}{t^{z+1}}dt,

and therefore

\displaystyle S_{\mu}(c,z)=\frac{1}{z}\,{}_{p+1}F_{q+1}\left[\begin{matrix}-z,\ \vec{\alpha}\\
1-z,\ \vec{\beta}+1\end{matrix};c\right],

(23)

where we have used the integral representation
[
29
, (16.5.2)]

\displaystyle{}_{p+1}F_{q+1}\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha_{0},\ \vec{\alpha}\\
\beta_{0},\ \vec{\beta}\end{matrix};c\right]=\frac{\Gamma(\beta_{0})}{\Gamma(\alpha_{0})\Gamma(\beta_{0}-\alpha_{0})}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\alpha_{0}-1}(1-t)^{\beta_{0}-\alpha_{0}-1}\,{}_{p}F_{q}\left[\begin{matrix}\vec{\alpha}\\
\vec{\beta}\end{matrix};ct\right]dt.

We derived (
23
) in
[
12
]
using a different approach.

If we define the Stieltjes transform of
\vec{P}_{n}(c)
by

\displaystyle\vec{S}_{P}(c,z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\vec{P}_{n}(c)}{z^{n+1}},

then it follows from (
18
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda+\tau c}S_{\mu}\left(c,\frac{z}{\lambda+\tau c}\right)=\vec{S}_{P}(c,z)\cdot\vec{\mu}(c),

or

\displaystyle\vec{S}_{P}(c,z)\cdot\vec{\mu}(c)=\frac{1}{z}\,{}_{p+1}F_{q+1}\left[\begin{matrix}-\frac{z}{\lambda+\tau c},\ \vec{\alpha}\\
1-\frac{z}{\lambda+\tau c},\ \vec{\beta}+1\end{matrix};c\right].

(24)

\displaystyle\rho_{M}(x;\alpha,c)=(\alpha)_{x}\frac{c^{x}}{x!},\qquad 0<c<1,\qquad\alpha>0.

(27)

In this case, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{0}^{M}(c;\alpha)={}_{1}F_{0}\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha\\
-\end{matrix};c\right]=(1-c)^{-\alpha},

(28)

and hence

\displaystyle\xi=0,\qquad(\lambda,\tau)=(1,-1).

From (
13
) we see that
\mu_{0}^{M}(c;\alpha)
satisfies
the ODE

\displaystyle[\vartheta-c(\vartheta+\alpha)]\mu_{0}^{M}=0,

which implies

\displaystyle(1-c)\mu_{1}^{M}=\alpha c\mu_{0}^{M}.

Thus,
\sigma_{0}(c)=\alpha c
, and Proposition
2.1
gives

\displaystyle\mu_{n}^{M}(c;\alpha)=(1-c)^{-n}P_{n}^{M}(c)\mu_{0}^{M}(c;\alpha),

(29)

with
P_{n}^{M}(c)
defined by
P_{0}^{M}(c)=1

and (see also
[
27
, equation (5.68)]
)

\displaystyle P_{n+1}^{M}=c(1-c)\frac{dP_{n}^{M}}{dc}+(\alpha+n)cP_{n}^{M}.

(30)

From (
10
), we have (see also
[
28
, equation (43)]
)

\displaystyle\mu_{n}^{M}(c;\alpha)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}c^{k}(\alpha)_{k}(1-c)^{-(\alpha+k)},

and therefore

\displaystyle P_{n}^{M}(c)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}(\alpha)_{k}c^{k}(1-c)^{n-k}.

(31)

Using (
19
) and (
20
), we see that the generating functions of

\mu_{n}^{M}(c;\alpha)
and
P_{n}^{M}(c)
are
given by (see also
[
28
, equation (49)]
)

\displaystyle G_{\mu}(c,w)=\mu_{0}\big(ce^{w}\big)=\big(1-ce^{w}\big)^{-\alpha},

and

\displaystyle G_{P}(c,w)=\frac{\big[1-ce^{(1-c)w}\big]^{-\alpha}}{(1-c)^{-\alpha}}=\left[\frac{1-ce^{(1-c)w}}{1-c}\right]^{-\alpha}.

3.2.1
Krawtchouk polynomials

The Krawtchouk polynomials
[
29
, (18.20.6)]

\displaystyle K_{n}(x;\mathfrak{p,}N)={}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}-n,-x\\
-N\end{matrix};\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}}\right),

are orthogonal with respect to the weight function
[
9
]

\displaystyle\rho_{K}(x;\mathfrak{p,}N)=\binom{N}{x}\mathfrak{p}^{x}(1-\mathfrak{p})^{N-x},\qquad N\in\mathbb{N},\qquad 0<\mathfrak{p}<1.

It is related to the weight function of the Meixner polynomials (
27
) by

\displaystyle\rho_{K}(x;\mathfrak{p,}N)=(1-\mathfrak{p})^{N}\frac{(-N)_{x}}{x!}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathfrak{p}-1}\right)^{x}=(1-\mathfrak{p})^{N}\rho_{M}\left(x;-N,\frac{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathfrak{p}-1}\right).

(32)

Using (
32
) in (
28
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{0}^{K}(\mathfrak{p})=(1-\mathfrak{p})^{N}\mu_{0}^{M}\left(-N,\frac{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathfrak{p}-1}\right)=1.

and using (
29
) and (
31
), we get
\mu_{n}^{K}(\mathfrak{p})=P_{n}^{K}(\mathfrak{p})
, with (see
also
[
28
, equation (42)]
)

\displaystyle P_{n}^{K}(\mathfrak{p})=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}\binom{N}{k}k!\mathfrak{p}^{k}.

From (
30
) and (
32
), we have

\displaystyle P_{n+1}^{K}=\mathfrak{p}(1-\mathfrak{p})\frac{dP_{n}^{K}}{d\mathfrak{p}}+N\mathfrak{p}P_{n}^{K}.

To obtain the generating function of
P_{n}^{K}(\mathfrak{p})
,
we can use
[
29
, (26.8.12)]

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}\frac{w^{n}}{n!}=\frac{(e^{w}-1)^{k}}{k!},

(33)

and we get (see also
[
28
, equation (48)]
)

\displaystyle G_{P}(\mathfrak{p},w)=\big[1+\big(e^{w}-1\big)\mathfrak{p}\big]^{N}.

3.3
Generalized Charlier

These polynomials are orthogonal with respect to the weight function
[
17
]

\displaystyle\rho(x;\beta,c)=\frac{1}{(\beta+1)_{x}}\frac{c^{x}}{x!},\qquad c>0,\qquad\beta>-1.

In this case, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(c)={}_{0}F_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}-\\
\beta+1\end{matrix};c\right]=c^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\Gamma(\beta+1)I_{\beta}\big(2\sqrt{c}\big),

where
I_{\beta}(z)
is the modified Bessel function of the
first kind
[
29
, (10.39.9)]
, and hence

\displaystyle\xi=1,\qquad(\lambda,\tau)=(1,0).

From (
13
) we see that
\mu_{0}(c)
satisfies the ODE

\displaystyle\vartheta(\vartheta+\beta)\mu_{0}-c\mu_{0}=0,

which implies

\displaystyle\mu_{2}=c\mu_{0}-\beta\mu_{1},

and therefore

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(c)=c,\qquad\sigma_{1}(c)=-\beta.

Proposition
2.1
gives

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(c)=\vec{P}_{n}(c)\cdot\vec{\mu}(c),

with
\vec{P}_{n}(c)
defined by (
16
) and

\displaystyle\vec{P}_{n+1}=c\frac{d\vec{P}_{n}}{dc}+\begin{bmatrix}0&c\\
1&-\beta\end{bmatrix}\vec{P}_{n}.

(34)

To obtain the Stieltjes transform of
\vec{P}_{n}(c)
, let’s write

\displaystyle\vec{P}_{n}(c)=\left[\begin{matrix}Q_{n}(c)\\
R_{n}(c)\end{matrix}\right],\qquad\vec{S}_{P}(c,z)=\left[\begin{matrix}U(c,z)\\
V(c,z)\end{matrix}\right].

From (
34
), we have

\displaystyle Q_{n+1}=cQ_{n}^{\prime}+cR_{n},\qquad R_{n+1}=cR_{n}^{\prime}-\beta R_{n}+Q_{n},

with
Q_{0}=1
,
R_{0}=0
. Using (
21
), we get

\displaystyle zU-1=c\frac{\partial U}{\partial c}+cV,\qquad zV=c\frac{\partial V}{\partial c}-\beta V+U.

(35)

The solution of the system (
35
) is given by

\displaystyle U(c,z)=\frac{1}{z}\,{}_{1}F_{2}\left[\begin{matrix}1\\
1-z,\ -\beta-z\end{matrix};c\right],\qquad V(c,z)=\frac{1}{z(z+\beta)}\,{}_{1}F_{2}\left[\begin{matrix}1\\
1-z,\ 1-\beta-z\end{matrix};c\right].

In this case

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(c)={}_{0}F_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}-\\
\beta+1\end{matrix};c\right],\qquad\mu_{1}(c)=\frac{c}{\beta+1}\,{}_{0}F_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}-\\
\beta+2\end{matrix};c\right],

and since

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z}\,{}_{1}F_{2}\left[\begin{matrix}1\\
1-z,\ -\beta-z\end{matrix};c\right]\,{}_{0}F_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}-\\
\beta+1\end{matrix};c\right]

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\frac{1}{z(z+\beta)}\,{}_{1}F_{2}\left[\begin{matrix}1\\
1-z,\ 1-\beta-z\end{matrix};c\right]\frac{c}{\beta+1}\,{}_{0}F_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}-\\
\beta+2\end{matrix};c\right]=\frac{1}{z}\,{}_{1}F_{2}\left[\begin{matrix}-z\\
1-z,\ \beta+1\end{matrix};c\right],

we see that (
24
) is satisfied.

3.4
Generalized Meixner

These polynomials are orthogonal with respect to the weight function
[
3
]

\displaystyle\rho(x;\alpha,\beta,c)=\frac{(\alpha)_{x}}{(\beta+1)_{x}}\frac{c^{x}}{x!},\qquad c,\alpha>0,\qquad\beta>-1.

In this case, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(c)={}_{1}F_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha\\
\beta+1\end{matrix};c\right]=M(\alpha,\beta+1,c),

where
M(a,b,c)
is Kummer’s (confluent hypergeometric)
function
[
29
, (13.2.2)]
, and hence

\displaystyle\xi=1,\qquad(\lambda,\tau)=(1,0).

From (
13
) we see that
\mu_{0}(c)
satisfies the ODE

\displaystyle\vartheta(\vartheta+\beta)\mu_{0}-c(\vartheta+\alpha)\mu_{0}=0,

which implies

\displaystyle\mu_{2}=c\alpha\mu_{0}+(c-\beta)\mu_{1},

and therefore

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(c)=\alpha c,\qquad\sigma_{1}(c)=c-\beta.

Proposition
2.1
gives

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(c)=\vec{P}_{n}(c)\cdot\vec{\mu}(c),

with
\vec{P}_{n}(c)
defined by (
16
) and

\displaystyle\vec{P}_{n+1}=c\frac{d\vec{P}_{n}}{dc}+\begin{bmatrix}0&\alpha c\\
1&c-\beta\end{bmatrix}\vec{P}_{n}.

To obtain the Stieltjes transform of
\vec{P}_{n}(c)
, let’s write

\displaystyle\vec{P}_{n}(c)=\left[\begin{matrix}Q_{n}(c)\\
R_{n}(c)\end{matrix}\right],\qquad\vec{S}_{P}(c,z)=\left[\begin{matrix}U(c,z)\\
V(c,z)\end{matrix}\right].

From (
34
), we have

\displaystyle Q_{n+1}=cQ_{n}^{\prime}+\alpha cR_{n},\qquad R_{n+1}=cR_{n}^{\prime}+(c-\beta)R_{n}+Q_{n},

with
Q_{0}=1
,
R_{0}=0
. Using (
21
), we get

\displaystyle zU-1=c\frac{\partial U}{\partial c}+\alpha cV,\qquad zV=c\frac{\partial V}{\partial c}+(c-\beta)V+U.

(36)

If we represent the functions
U
,
V
as

\displaystyle U(c,z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}u_{n}(z)c^{n},\qquad V(c,z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}v_{n}(z)c^{n},

then (
36
) gives

\displaystyle zu_{n}-\delta_{n,0}=nu_{n}+\alpha v_{n-1},\qquad zv_{n}=nv_{n}+v_{n-1}-\beta v_{n}+u_{n}.

(37)

We have (assuming that
u_{n}=v_{n}=0
for
n<0)

\displaystyle u_{0}=\frac{1}{z},\qquad v_{0}=\frac{1}{z(z+\beta)},

and

\displaystyle u_{n}=(z+\beta-n)v_{n}-v_{n-1}.

(38)

Using (
38
) in (
37
), we obtain

\displaystyle v_{n}=\frac{z+\alpha-n}{(z-n)(z+\beta-n)}v_{n-1},\qquad n=1,2,\ldots,

and therefore

\displaystyle v_{n}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{z(z+\beta)}\frac{(1-\alpha-z)_{n}}{(1-z)_{n}(1-\beta-z)_{n}},\qquad n=0,1,\ldots.

From (
38
), it follows that

\displaystyle u_{n}=\alpha\frac{(-1)^{n}}{z(z+\alpha)}\frac{(-\alpha-z)_{n}}{(1-z)_{n}(-\beta-z)_{n}},\qquad n=1,2,\ldots.

Thus,

\displaystyle U(c,z)=\frac{1}{z}+\frac{\alpha}{z(z+\alpha)}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\alpha-z)_{n}}{(1-z)_{n}(-\beta-z)_{n}}(-c)^{n}

\displaystyle\hphantom{U(c,z)}{}=\frac{1}{z}-\frac{\alpha}{z(z+\alpha)}+\frac{\alpha}{z(z+\alpha)}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\alpha-z)_{n}}{(1-z)_{n}(-\beta-z)_{n}}(-c)^{n}

\displaystyle\hphantom{U(c,z)}{}=\frac{1}{z+\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{z(z+\alpha)}\,{}_{2}F_{2}\left[\begin{matrix}1,-\alpha-z\\
1-z,\ -\beta-z\end{matrix};-c\right],

and

\displaystyle V(c,z)=\frac{1}{z(z+\beta)}\,{}_{2}F_{2}\left[\begin{matrix}1,\ 1-\alpha-z\\
1-z,\ 1-\beta-z\end{matrix};-c\right].

In this case

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(c)={}_{1}F_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha\\
\beta+1\end{matrix};c\right],\qquad\mu_{1}(c)=\frac{\alpha c}{\beta+1}\,{}_{1}F_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha+1\\
\beta+2\end{matrix};c\right],

and since

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{z+\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{z(z+\alpha)}\,{}_{2}F_{2}\left[\begin{matrix}1,-\alpha-z\\
1-z,\ -\beta-z\end{matrix};-c\right]\right)\,{}_{1}F_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha\\
\beta+1\end{matrix};c\right]

\displaystyle\!\qquad{}+\frac{1}{z(z+\beta)}\,{}_{2}F_{2}\left[\begin{matrix}1,\ 1-\alpha-z\\
1-z,\ 1-\beta-z\end{matrix};-c\right]\frac{\alpha c}{\beta+1}\,{}_{1}F_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha+1\\
\beta+2\end{matrix};c\right]=\frac{1}{z}\,{}_{2}F_{2}\left[\begin{matrix}-z,\ \alpha\\
1-z,\ \beta+1\end{matrix};c\right],

we see that (
24
) is satisfied.

3.5
Generalized Krawtchouk

These polynomials are orthogonal with respect to the weight function
[
15
]

\displaystyle\rho(x;\alpha,N,c)=(\alpha)_{x}(-N)_{x}\frac{c^{x}}{x!},\qquad c<0,\qquad\alpha>0,\qquad N\in\mathbb{N}.

In this case, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(c)={}_{2}F_{0}\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha,\ -N\\
-\end{matrix};c\right]=C_{N}\big({-}\alpha;-c^{-1}\big),

where
C_{N}(x;\mu)
is the Charlier polynomial

[
6
]
, and hence

\displaystyle\xi=1,\qquad(\lambda,\tau)=(0,1).

From (
13
) we see that
\mu_{0}(c)
satisfies the ODE

\displaystyle\vartheta\mu_{0}-c(\vartheta+\alpha)(\vartheta-N)\mu_{0}=0,

which implies

\displaystyle c\mu_{2}=\alpha Nc\mu_{0}+(Nc-\alpha c+1)\mu_{1},

and therefore

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(c)=\alpha Nc,\qquad\sigma_{1}(c)=(N-\alpha)c+1.

Proposition
2.1
gives

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(c)=c^{-n}\vec{P}_{n}(c)\cdot\vec{\mu}(c),

with
\vec{P}_{n}(c)
defined by (
16
) and

\displaystyle\vec{P}_{n+1}=c^{2}\frac{d\vec{P}_{n}}{dc}+\begin{bmatrix}-nc&\alpha Nc\\
c&\left(N-\alpha-n\right)c+1\end{bmatrix}\vec{P}_{n}(c).

3.6
Generalized Hahn polynomials of type I

These polynomials are orthogonal with respect to the weight function
[
15
]

\displaystyle\rho(x;\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta;c)=\frac{(\alpha_{1})_{x}(\alpha_{2})_{x}}{(\beta+1)_{x}}\frac{c^{x}}{x!},

(39)

where
0<c<1
,
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>0
,
\beta>-1
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(c)={}_{2}F_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha_{1},\ \alpha_{2}\\
\beta+1\end{matrix};c\right],

and hence

\displaystyle\xi=1,\qquad(\lambda,\tau)=(1,-1).

From (
13
) we see that
\mu_{0}(c)
satisfies the ODE

\displaystyle\vartheta(\vartheta+\beta)\mu_{0}-c(\vartheta+\alpha_{1})(\vartheta+\alpha_{2})\mu_{0}=0,

which implies

\displaystyle(1-c)\mu_{2}=\allowbreak\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}c\mu_{0}+\big[(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})c-\beta\big]\mu_{1},

and therefore

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(c)=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}c,\qquad\sigma_{1}(c)=(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})c-\beta.

Proposition
2.1
gives

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(c)=(1-c)^{-n}\vec{P}_{n}(c)\cdot\vec{\mu}(c),

with
\vec{P}_{n}(c)
defined by (
16
) and

\displaystyle\vec{P}_{n+1}=c(1-c)\frac{d\vec{P}_{n}}{dc}+\begin{bmatrix}nc&\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}(1-c)c\\
1-c&(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+n)c-\beta\end{bmatrix}\vec{P}_{n}.

3.7
Hahn polynomials

The Hahn polynomials
[
29
, (18.20.5)]

\displaystyle H_{n}(x;\alpha,\beta,N)={}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-n,-x,n+\alpha+\beta+1\\
-N,\alpha+1\end{matrix};1\right)

are orthogonal with respect to the weight function
[
29
, (18.19)]

\displaystyle\rho_{H}(x;\alpha,\beta,N)=\frac{(\alpha+1)_{x}}{x!}\frac{(\beta+1)_{N-x}}{(N-x)!},\qquad\alpha,\beta\notin[-N,-1],\qquad N\in\mathbb{N},

or

\displaystyle\rho_{H}(x;\alpha,\beta,N)=\binom{\alpha+x}{x}\binom{\beta+N-x}{N-x}.

The relation between
\rho_{H}(x;\alpha,\beta,N)
and the
weight function of the generalized Hahn polynomials (
39
) is
given by

\displaystyle\rho_{H}(x;\alpha,\beta,N)=\frac{(\beta+1)_{N}}{N!}\rho(x;\alpha+1,-N,-N-\beta-1;1).

From (
10
), we get (see also
[
28
, equation (41)]
)

\displaystyle\mu_{n}^{H}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}(\alpha+1)_{k}\frac{(\alpha+\beta+2+k)_{N-k}}{(N-k)!},

where we have used

\displaystyle\mu_{0}^{H}=\frac{(\alpha+\beta+2)_{N}}{N!}.

To obtain the generating function of
\mu_{n}^{H}
, we can use (
33
), and we get

\displaystyle G_{\mu^{H}}(z)=\frac{(\alpha+\beta+2)_{N}}{N!}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}-N,\ \alpha+1\\
\alpha+\beta+2\end{matrix};1-e^{z}\right].

This generating function seems not to have been considered before.

Remark 3.1
.

Except for the Bell polynomials (
26
), all the other families

\vec{P}_{n}(c)
associated to the moments
\mu_{n}(c)
seem to be new.

Remark 3.2
.

Stieltjes transforms of the generalized Krawtchouk and generalized Hahn
polynomials of type I have been omitted because of the complexity of the formulas.

4
Conclusion

We have developed a technique for computing the moments of weight functions of
hypergeometric type. We have shown that the moments are linear combinations of
the first
\xi+1
moments with polynomial coefficients in the parameter
c
.
We have also constructed generating functions for both the moments and the
polynomials associated with them.

All the results in Sections
3.3
–
3.5
are new, and give efficient ways of
computing the moments of the discrete semiclassical polynomials of class
1
.
The same method can be used to find the moments of polynomials of class
s>1
.

In a previous work
[
16
]
, we found the asymptotic zero distribution
of polynomial families satisfying first-order differential-recurrence
relations of the form (
12
). It would be interesting to know if our
results could be extended to include the polynomials
\vec{P}_{n}(c)
studied in this paper.

Constructive S4 modal logics with the finite birelational frame property
Philippe Balbiani, Martín Diéguez, David Fernández-Duque, Brett McLean

Abstract
The logics 𝖢𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{CS4} and 𝖨𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{IS4} are the two leading intuitionistic variants of the modal logic 𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{S4} . Whether the finite model property holds for each of these logics have been long-standing open problems. It was recently shown that 𝖨𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{IS4} has the finite frame property and thus the finite model property. In this paper, we prove that 𝖢𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{CS4} also enjoys the finite frame property. Additionally, we investigate the following three logics closely related to 𝖨𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{IS4} . The logic 𝖦𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{GS4} is obtained by adding the Gödel–Dummett axiom to 𝖨𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{IS4} ; it is both a superintuitionistic and a fuzzy logic and has previously been given a real-valued semantics. We provide an alternative birelational semantics and prove strong completeness with respect to this semantics. The extension 𝖦𝖲𝟦 𝖼 \mathsf{GS4^{c}} of 𝖦𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{GS4} corresponds to requiring a crisp accessibility relation on the real-valued semantics. We give 𝖦𝖲𝟦 𝖼 \mathsf{GS4^{c}} a birelational semantics corresponding to an extra confluence condition on the 𝖦𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{GS4} birelational semantics and prove strong completeness. Neither of these two logics have the finite model property with respect to their real-valued semantics, but we prove that they have the finite frame property for their birelational semantics. Establishing the finite birelational frame property immediately establishes decidability, which was previously open for these two logics. Our proofs yield NEXPTIME upper bounds for 𝖢𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{CS4} , 𝖦𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{GS4} , and 𝖦𝖲𝟦 𝖼 \mathsf{GS4^{c}} . The logic 𝖲𝟦𝖨 \mathsf{S4I} is obtained from 𝖨𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{IS4} by reversing the roles of the modal and intuitionistic relations in the birelational semantics. We also prove the finite frame property, and thereby decidability, for 𝖲𝟦𝖨 \mathsf{S4I} , although our proof does not yield an elementary complexity bound. In summary, we prove that 𝖢𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{CS4} , 𝖲𝟦𝖨 \mathsf{S4I} , 𝖦𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{GS4} , and 𝖦𝖲𝟦 𝖼 \mathsf{GS4^{c}} all have the finite birelational frame property, with the latter two having a NEXPTIME validity problem.

Introduction

Intuitionistic logic, with its roots in constructive reasoning, has found important and profound applications in computer science, most notably through the Curry–Howard correspondence. This correspondence, often termed the “propositions-as-types” principle, establishes a deep connection between logic and the type theory of programming languages.

In the context of intuitionistic
modal
logic, this correspondence has led to the development of expressive and powerful frameworks for reasoning about computation and program behavior. For example, modal types can represent computations that are guaranteed to terminate or those that might diverge. More generally, modal type theory allows modalities to capture program invariants, preconditions, and postconditions, allowing for a more fine-grained and expressive specification of program behavior.

Davis and Pfenning
[
16
]
extended the Curry–Howard correspondence to the case of a constructive version of the well known modal logic
\sf S4
in order to achieve a modal account of
staged computation
.
1
1

1

A staged computation is a computation that proceeds as a sequence of multiple stages, where each stage produces the input for the next. These stages can be carried out on different machines by different users—think, for example, of the (various) compilation and execution stages that are necessary to arrive at the final output of a program.
The resulting system,
\mathsf{Mini}\text{-}\mathsf{ML}^{\square}
, can be used to introduce the notion of partially evaluated functions in functional programming.
Similarly, Choudhury and Krishnaswami
[
13
]
proposed extending the constructive
\sf S4
typed calculus to obtain a modal type system for impure functional programming languages that can distinguish between expressions with and without side effects. To do so, the authors use modalities to capture important notions such as
safety
or
purity
in a comonadic type framework.

Within the context of computer security, Miyamoto and Igarashi
[
31
]
used a constructive
\sf S4
to propose a typed
\lambda
-calculus to capture typed-based information flow analysis. In their approach, security levels are mapped to Kripke worlds and the intuitionistic accessibility relation simulates the heritage between the security levels, i.e. any information available at a lower level is also available at higher levels.

Another important field of application for constructive
\sf S4
is the so-called Fitch-style modal
\lambda
-calculus
[
9
,
28
]
, where Fitch-style calculi
[
32
]
are extended with two functions, ‘later’ and ‘constant’, which can be captured in terms of the
\Box
modality in intuitionistic modal logic. In
[
14
]
, Clouston studies a variation of the modal
\mathsf{S4}
Fitch-style calculus where the
\Box
modality is idempotent (i.e.
\Box\varphi
and
\Box\Box\varphi
are not only logical equivalent but also identical).
Most of the applications we have mentioned focus on the
\Diamond
-free fragment of constructive modal logic, but following on previous work of Fitch
[
21
]
and Wijesekera
[
38
]
, constructive and intuitionistic modal logic may be enriched with the
\Diamond
‘possiblity’ operator.
Unlike in the classical setting, the modalities
\Box
and
\Diamond
are not inter-definable, and whereas
\Box
allows us to reason about properties that are ensured throughout a computation,
\Diamond
instead indicates outcomes that
may
happen, including, for example, the possibility of a crash or of finding a solution to an
np
-complete problem.

Regarding
\mathsf{S4}
specifically, there are (at least) three prominent constructive/intuitionistic variants in the literature.
This paper investigates one of them, known as
\mathsf{CS4}

[
4
]
, along with some variants of a second, known as
\mathsf{IS4}

[
35
]
.
Each of these logics has a natural axiomatisation and is sound and
strongly complete
for a class of Kripke structures based on two preorders: one preorder
\preccurlyeq
for the intuitionistic implication, and another preorder
\sqsubseteq
for the modalities
\Diamond
and
\Box
(so the structures are particular types of
birelational
frames).
However, the questions of whether the logics
\mathsf{CS4}
and
\mathsf{IS4}
enjoy the
finite model property
remained open for twenty years or more: in the case of
\mathsf{CS4}
since at least 2001
[
4
]
, and of
\mathsf{IS4}
at least since 1994
[
35
]
. The question for
\mathsf{IS4}
was recently resolved in the positive in
[
23
]
. In this paper we do the same for
\mathsf{CS4}
, solving the other main open problem concerning non-classical variants of
\mathsf{S4}
.
The third preexisting constructive/intuitionistic variant of
\mathsf{S4}
, which we do not investigate in this paper, is the logic
\mathsf{IntS4}
studied (along with related variants of other modal logics) by Wolter and Zakharyaschev
[
39
,
40
]
.
In contrast to
\mathsf{CS4}
and
\mathsf{IS4}
, the logic
\mathsf{IntS4}
was already known to have the finite model property, although the semantics are quite different, in particular employing different binary relations for each of
\Diamond
and
\Box
.

Along with
\mathsf{CS4}
, we also study three variants of
\mathsf{IS4}
and show that they too all enjoy the finite model property.
2
2

2

For all of these results, including for
\mathsf{CS4}
, we prove the stronger
finite frame property
.

The first two variants are modal extensions of
Gödel(–Dummett) logic,
a type of fuzzy logic. According to Caicedo and Ramírez
[
11
]
, such fuzzy versions of modal logic may be used to model situations where modal notions may be applied to vague predicates.
One may consider propositions such as
“The weather is always warm in Tulum”
or
“Alice knows that Bob is trustworthy”,
where to be ‘warm’ or ‘trustworthy’ are treated as vague (they can be true to varying degrees), and ‘always’ and ‘knows’ are treated as modalities.

Among many approaches for dealing with vagueness, Gödel logics
[
24
]
are particularly appealing versions of fuzzy logic, because they can alternatively be viewed as superintuitionistic logics
[
26
]
.
Indeed, propositional Gödel logic lies strictly between intuitionistic propositional logic and classical propositional logic, and is characterised by a semantics that assigns to each proposition a truth value in the interval
[0,1]
.
If we further assume that modalities are interpreted using a
crisp
accessibility relation (that is, expressions of the form
x\mathrel{R}y
take values in
\{0,1\}
), then Gödel modal logic may be seen as a specialisation of intuitionistic modal logic
[
19
,
35
]
, where semantically the intuitionistic partial order validates an
upward linearity
condition.

Some modal extensions of Gödel logic have already been studied in the literature, including combinations of Gödel logic with
\mathsf{K}

[
30
,
29
,
10
]
,
\mathsf{S4}

[
11
]
,
\mathsf{S5}
[
11
,
25
, Chapter 8], and with linear temporal logic
[
3
]
.
In the specific case of Gödel
\mathsf{K}
, it has been proved that the complexity of the validity problem is
pspace
-complete for the box fragment
[
29
]
(where the logics of the
accessibility-crisp
and accessibility-fuzzy frames coincide) and also the diamond fragment
[
30
]
(for both
accessibility-crisp
frames and accessibility-fuzzy frames). Modal logics over accessibility-fuzzy frames have been axiomatised by Caicedo and Rodríguez
[
11
,
12
]
, and over
accessibility-crisp
frames by Rodríguez and Vidal
[
33
]
.

Our first
\mathsf{IS4}
variant,
\mathsf{GS4}
, which we prove to be equivalent to Gödel
\mathsf{S4}
, has semantics defined over a subclass of
\mathsf{IS4}
frames: those where the intuitionistic relation is
upward linear
, so that the logic validates the Gödel–Dummett axiom
(p\mathbin{\rightarrow}q)\vee(q\mathbin{\rightarrow}p)
. A second variant,
\mathsf{GS4^{c}}
, is defined by imposing a
‘forth–down’ confluence
condition on
\mathsf{GS4}
frames, and
\mathsf{GS4^{c}}
is equivalent to
accessibility-crisp
Gödel
\mathsf{S4}
.

Our third
\mathsf{IS4}
variant,
\mathsf{S4I}
, is defined over the same class of frames as
\mathsf{IS4}
, except that the roles of the intuitionistic and the modal preorders are interchanged.
These frame conditions for
\mathsf{S4I}
are natural from a technical point of view, as they are the minimal conditions required so that both modalities
\Diamond
and
\Box
can be evaluated ‘classically’, i.e. using (there exists an accessible world…) and (for all accessible worlds…).

Our finite frame property proofs involve first showing that each logic is sound and complete for a class of birelational frames.
We do this via canonical model constructions. However, after this the proofs diverge, and we use three different methods to obtain the finite frame property for our four logics, choosing the method that give the simplest argument and best complexity bound for each logic.

In the case of
\mathsf{CS4}
, the canonical model may be
relativised
to a finite set of formulas, directly yielding the finite frame property.
In the case for the two Gödel logics
\mathsf{GS4}
and
\mathsf{GS4^{c}}
, the canonical model cannot be relativised in this way.
However, the associated birelational semantics is restrictive enough that we can obtain finite models directly by taking an appropriate
bisimulation quotient
of a model.
The case of
\mathsf{S4I}
is somewhat more involved, as we can neither relativise the canonical model nor apply a bisimulation quotient directly.
Instead, we first show that it is sufficient that the logic enjoys the
shallow
frame property
, meaning that any non-
valid
formula may be falsified on a frame where the length of any
\prec
-chain is bounded (as usual,
w\prec v
means that
w\preccurlyeq v
but
v\not\preccurlyeq w
).
3
3

3

The descriptor
shallow
has been used in a similar way in the context of classical modal logic
[
34
]
.

While
shallow
models may in principle be infinite, their quotients modulo bisimulation are always finite, thus reducing the problem of proving the finite frame property to that of proving the
shallow
frame property, which we then proceed to do.

Syntax and semantics

In this section we first introduce the propositional modal language shared by all the logics we are interested in. Then we define the logics themselves. Finally, we present the various birelational or real-valued semantics for the logics.

Fix a

2.2
Semantics

We will consider several semantics for our intuitionistic variants of
\mathsf{S4}
, some of which are intuitionistic semantics (that is, featuring
intuitionistic frames
) and some of which are real-valued semantics. For the various intuitionistic semantics, it will be convenient to introduce a general class of structures that includes each of these semantics as special cases.

Definition 3
.

An
intuitionistic frame
is a triple
\mathcal{F}=(W,W^{\bot},{\preccurlyeq})
, where
W
is a set,
\preccurlyeq
is a preorder (that is, a reflexive and transitive binary relation) on
W
, and
W^{\bot}\subseteq W
is closed under
\preccurlyeq
, that is, whenever
w\in W^{\bot}
and
w\preccurlyeq v
, we also have that
v\in W^{\bot}

[
5
]
.
We say that
\mathcal{F}
is
upward linear
if
w\preccurlyeq u
and
w\preccurlyeq v
implies that
u\preccurlyeq v
or
v\preccurlyeq u
.

A
birelational frame
is a quadruple
\mathcal{F}=(W,W^{\bot},{\preccurlyeq},R)
, where
(W,W^{\bot},\allowbreak{\preccurlyeq})
is an
intuitionistic frame
and
(W,W^{\bot},R)
is a Kripke frame in which
W^{\bot}
is closed under
R
. It is a
bi-preorder
if both
(W,W^{\bot},{\preccurlyeq})
and
(W,W^{\bot},R)
are
intuitionistic frames
(i.e., if
R
is also a preorder), in which case we will write
\sqsubseteq
instead of
R
.
We call the
birelational frame

\mathcal{F}
upward linear if
(W,W^{\bot},\allowbreak{\preccurlyeq})
is
upward linear
.

The set
W^{\bot}
is called the set of
fallible worlds
, as in
[
4
,
5
,
37
,
27
]
.
Note that in a
birelational frame
,
W^{\bot}
is closed under both
\preccurlyeq
and
R
.
When
W^{\bot}=\varnothing
we omit it, and view
\mathcal{F}
as a triple
(W,{\preccurlyeq},R)
.
In this case, we say that
\mathcal{F}
is
infallible
.

Definition 4
.

Given a
birelational frame

\mathcal{F}=(W,W^{\bot},{\preccurlyeq},R)
, a
valuation
on
\mathcal{F}
is a function
V\colon\mathbb{P}\to 2^{W}
that is
monotone
in the sense that each
V(p)
is upward closed with respect to
\preccurlyeq
and includes
W^{\bot}
(to ensure the validity of
\bot\mathbin{\rightarrow}\varphi
).

We define the satisfaction relation
\models
recursively by

•

(\mathcal{M},w)\models p\in\mathbb{P}
if
w\in V(p)
;

•

(\mathcal{M},w)\models\bot
if
w\in W^{\bot}
;

•

(\mathcal{M},w)\models\varphi\wedge\psi
if
(\mathcal{M},w)\models\varphi
and
(\mathcal{M},w)\models\psi
;

•

(\mathcal{M},w)\models\varphi\vee\psi
if
(\mathcal{M},w)\models\varphi
or
(\mathcal{M},w)\models\psi
;

•

(\mathcal{M},w)\models\varphi\mathbin{\rightarrow}\psi
if for all
v\succcurlyeq w
,
(\mathcal{M},v)\models\varphi
implies
(\mathcal{M},v)\models\psi
;

•

(\mathcal{M},w)\models\Diamond\varphi
if for all
u\succcurlyeq w
there exists
v
such that
u\mathrel{R}v
and
(\mathcal{M},v)\models\varphi
;

•

(\mathcal{M},w)\models\Box\varphi
if for all
u,v
such that
w\preccurlyeq u\mathrel{R}v
,
(\mathcal{M},v)\models\varphi
.

It can be easily proved by induction on
\varphi
that
persistence
(or
monotonicity
) holds: for all
w,v\in W
, if
w\preccurlyeq v
and
(\mathcal{M},w)\models\varphi
then
(\mathcal{M},v)\models\varphi
.

A
birelational model
is a
birelational frame
equipped with a
valuation
; if the frame is a
bi-preorder
, then we speak of a
bi-preordered model
.
If
\mathcal{M}=(W,W^{\bot},{\preccurlyeq},R,V)
is any model and
\varphi

\mathcal{M}\models\varphi
if
(\mathcal{M},w)\models\varphi
for every

.

Validity is defined as one would expect, but it is worth being precise about the surrounding terminology and notation.

Definition 5
.

Let

be any class of models or class of frames. Let

Downward-linear

First we argue that without loss of generality, models based on

\displaystyle W^{\prime}

Theorem 6

(

Suppose

(|\Sigma|+1)\cdot 2^{|\Sigma|(|\Sigma|+1)+1}

\mathsf{GS4}

\mathsf{GS4}

6.4
Shallow frames

Our finite frame property proof for
\mathsf{S4I}
follows some the same general pattern as those for Gödel–Dummett logics, but there are a few subtleties.

First, once branching is involved, it is no longer the case that every model will be bisimilar to a finite one.
This is illustrated in

Let

The following inequality will be useful in order to simplify some expressions involving superexponentials.

Lemma 21
.

For all

2^{m}\cdot 2_{k}^{(n-1)m}\leq 2_{k}^{nm}.

Proof.

Proceed by induction on

If

m+2_{k-1}^{(n-1)m}\leq(m+1)2_{k-1}^{(n-1)m}\leq 2^{m}\cdot 2_{k-1}^{(n-1)m}\mathrel{{\mathop{\leq}\limits}}2_{k-1}^{nm}.

Then

2^{m}\cdot 2_{k}^{(n-1)m}=2^{m}\cdot 2^{2_{k-1}^{(n-1)m}}=2^{m+2_{k-1}^{(n-1)m}}\leq 2^{2_{k-1}^{nm}}=2_{k}^{nm},

as needed.

Given a

\Phi_{k+1}\sqsubseteq_{\mathrm{c}}\Upsilon\succcurlyeq_{\mathrm{c}}\Theta
.
If
\Delta_{r}\cap\Sigma=\Upsilon\cap\Sigma
, we observe that
\Delta_{r}\preccurlyeq^{0}_{\Sigma}\Upsilon
.
Hence
\Phi_{k+1}\sqsubseteq^{0}_{\Sigma}\Delta_{r}
, so that
(\Phi_{i})_{i\leq k+1}\sqsubseteq^{1}_{\Sigma}(\Delta_{i})_{i\leq r}
.
In this case, we may simply set
r^{\prime}=r
.
Otherwise, we set
r^{\prime}=r+1
and
\Delta_{r+1}=\Upsilon
.
In this case, we also have that
(\Phi_{i})_{i\leq k}\sqsubseteq^{1}_{\Sigma}(\Delta_{i})_{i\leq r}
.

For
forth–down
confluence, as above, if
\Gamma\sqsubseteq^{1}_{\Sigma}\Delta
, then there is a non-decreasing sequence
j_{1},\ldots j_{m}
with
j_{m}=n
such that
\Gamma_{i}\sqsubseteq^{0}_{\Sigma}\Delta_{j_{i}}
for
i\leq m
.
Then, any
\Gamma^{\prime}\preccurlyeq_{\Sigma}\Gamma
is of the form
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{0},\ldots,\Gamma_{m^{\prime}})
with
m^{\prime}\leq m
, and setting
\Delta^{\prime}=(\Delta_{0},\ldots,\Delta_{j_{m^{\prime}}})
, we readily see that
\Gamma^{\prime}\sqsubseteq^{1}_{\Sigma}\Delta^{\prime}\preccurlyeq_{\Sigma}\Delta
.
∎

Lemma 26
.

Let
\Gamma\in W_{\Sigma}
.

1.

If
\Diamond\varphi\in\Sigma
then
\Diamond\varphi\in\ell(\Gamma)
if and only if there is
\Delta\sqsupseteq_{\Sigma}\Gamma
such that
\varphi\in\ell(\Delta)
.

2.

If
\Box\varphi\in\Sigma
then
\Box\varphi\in\ell(\Gamma)
if and only if for every
\Delta\sqsupseteq_{\Sigma}\Gamma
,
\varphi\in\ell(\Delta)
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{i})_{i\leq n}
.
For the first claim, we first prove the easier right-to-left direction.
Suppose that
\Delta=(\Delta_{i})_{i\leq m}\sqsupseteq^{1}_{\Sigma}\Gamma
is such that
\varphi\in\ell(\Delta)
, meaning that
\varphi\in\Delta_{m}
.
Then
\Gamma_{n}\sqsubseteq^{0}_{\Sigma}\Delta_{m}
, which means that for some
\Theta
,
\Gamma_{n}\sqsubseteq_{\mathrm{c}}\Theta\succcurlyeq^{0}_{\Sigma}\Delta_{m}
.
Then
\varphi\in\Theta
; hence
\Diamond\varphi\in\Gamma_{n}=\ell(\Gamma)
by the truth lemma.
If instead
\Delta\sqsupseteq_{\Sigma}\Gamma
, then there is a sequence

\Gamma=\Upsilon_{0}\sqsubseteq^{1}_{\Sigma}\Upsilon_{1}\sqsubseteq^{1}_{\Sigma}\ldots\sqsubseteq^{1}_{\Sigma}\Upsilon_{n}=\Delta,

and backwards induction on
i
shows that
\Diamond\varphi\in\ell(\Upsilon_{i})
, so that in particular
\Diamond\varphi\in\ell(\Gamma)
.

For the left-to-right direction, suppose that
\Diamond\varphi\in\ell(\Gamma)
.
By backwards induction on
k\leq n
we prove that there exists some sequence
(\Theta_{i})_{i=k}^{n}
such that
\Gamma_{i}\sqsubseteq_{\mathrm{c}}\Theta_{i}
for each
i\in[k,n]
and
\Theta_{i}\preccurlyeq_{\mathrm{c}}\Theta_{i+1}
if
i\in[k,n)
(interval notation should be interpreted over the natural numbers).
In the base case
k=n
and, by the truth lemma, there is
\Theta_{n}\sqsupseteq_{\mathrm{c}}\Gamma_{n}
such that
\varphi\in\Theta_{n}
.
So, suppose that
(\Theta_{i})_{i=k+1}^{n}
has been constructed with the desired properties.
Since the canonical model is
forth–down confluent
, there is some
\Theta_{k}
such that
\Gamma_{k}\sqsubseteq_{\mathrm{c}}\Theta_{k}\preccurlyeq_{\mathrm{c}}\Theta_{k+1}
, yielding the desired
\Theta_{k}
.

The problem is that the sequence
\Theta=(\Theta_{i})_{i\leq n}
may not be an element of
W_{\Sigma}
.
We instead choose a suitable subsequence
\Delta=(\Theta_{j_{i}})_{i\leq m}
, where
j_{k}
is defined by (forward) induction on
k
.
For the base case, we set
j_{0}=0
, and
m_{0}=0
(meaning that
\Delta
currently has
m_{0}+1
elements).
Now, suppose that
m_{k}
has been defined as has been
j_{i}
for
i\leq m_{k}
, in such a way that
(\Theta_{j_{i}})_{i\leq m_{k}}\in W_{\Sigma}
and
(\Gamma_{i})_{i\leq k}\sqsubseteq_{\Sigma}(\Theta_{j_{i}})_{i\leq m_{k}}
.
To define
m_{k+1}\geq m_{k}
and
(\Theta_{j_{i}})_{i\leq m_{k+1}}
, we consider two cases.
First assume that there is
\psi\in\Sigma
such that
\psi\in\Theta_{k+1}\setminus\Theta_{j_{k}}
.
In this case, setting
m_{k+1}=m_{k}+1
and
j_{m_{k+1}}=k+1
we see that
(\Theta_{j_{i}})_{i\leq m_{k+1}}
has all desired properties.
In particular, the existence of the formula
\psi
guarantees that
(\Theta_{j_{i}})_{i\leq m_{k+1}}\in W_{\Sigma}
.
Otherwise, set
m_{k+1}=m_{k}
.
In this case we see that
\Gamma_{k+1}\sqsubseteq_{\mathrm{c}}\Theta_{k+1}\succcurlyeq^{0}_{\Sigma}\Theta_{j_{m_{k}}}
, so that
\Gamma_{k+1}\sqsubseteq^{0}_{\Sigma}\Theta_{j_{m_{k}}}
and thus
(\Gamma_{i})_{i\leq k+1}\sqsubseteq_{\Sigma}(\Theta_{j_{i}})_{i\leq m_{k+1}}
.
That
(\Theta_{j_{i}})_{i\leq m_{k+1}}\in W_{\Sigma}
follows from the fact that
m_{k+1}=m_{k}
and the induction hypothesis.
The claim then follows by setting
m=m_{n}
and
\Delta=(\Theta_{j_{i}})_{i\leq m}
.

The second claim is proven similarly, but by contraposition. We leave the details to the reader.
∎

7
Concluding remarks

We have settled the long-standing problems of the finite frame property for
\mathsf{CS4}
and the decidability of
\mathsf{GS4}
and
\mathsf{GS4^{c}}
.
We also introduced a logic
\mathsf{S4I}
closely related to
\mathsf{IS4}
that enjoys the finite frame property as well.
The logics we considered correspond to classes of models with combinations of the
forth–up
,
back–up
, and
forth–down confluence
properties.
We have already sketched in the discussion regarding
Figure

10
why we do not obtain the same result for
\mathsf{IS4}
, but there are a handful of other logics that may be defined in this fashion for which our techniques should be applicable.

We could also consider logics with the
forth–up confluence
property alone, the basis of what we call
\Diamond
-regular
logics.
We have not provided an axiomatisation for the logic of reflexive transitive
\Diamond
-regular
frames, as our completeness proofs rely on either Axiom
2.1
or Axiom
2.1
being available.
Note that this class does not validate
\Box p\to\Box\Box p
. First results on logics of frames satisfying only forth–up confluence were recently presented in
[
8
]
, where the
\mathsf{K}
version was axiomatised and shown to be decidable.

Similarly, we may consider the class of
forth–down confluent
frames.
While Axiom
2.1
holds on frames that are
forth–up

and

forth–down confluent
, it is unclear how the class of
forth–down confluent
frames may be axiomatised.
On the other hand, it seems that the
shallow
model construction we have provided for
\mathsf{S4I}
should readily adapt to frames satisfying only the
forth–up confluence
or the
forth–down confluence
property, so our techniques should be applicable to these classes of frames.
All that is needed is to find the respective axiomatisations.

In total, there are eight logics that could be obtained from combinations of the three confluence frame conditions, and eight more for their
upward-linear
variants.
The study of
\mathsf{S4I}
-like logics whose frames satisfy both
forth–up
and
forth–down confluence
has recently been expanded to the enriched
bi
-intuitionistic modal language
[
18
]
.
Note that the decidability results in that paper are not obtained directly via the
shallow
model property, given that models can no longer be assumed to be forest-like in the bi-intuitionistic setting.

It is worth noting that Gödel modal logics are of independent interest (see for example
[
11
,
29
]
), with the Gödel variants of the modal logics
\mathsf{K}
and
\mathsf{S5}
enjoying the finite model property
[
10
]
.
However, the decidability of
\mathsf{GS4}
and
\mathsf{GS4^{c}}
remained challenging problems, since these logics do not enjoy a finite model property for real-valued semantics.
We have shown how birelational semantics do not have this issue, and indeed the logics enjoy the finite frame property for this semantics, and hence are decidable.
We believe that this work could lead to a systematic treatment of other prominent Gödel modal logics whose decidability was previously unknown, by first providing them with a birelational interpretation and then establishing the finite frame property under this semantics.

Finally, an interesting question left open is that of the complexity of the logics we have studied.
The exponential bound on the size of
\mathsf{GS4}
and
\mathsf{GS4^{c}}
models yields a
nexptime
upper bound on complexity, but it is possible that validity is in fact
pspace
-complete since this is the case of Gödel linear temporal logic defined by similar frame conditions
[
2
]
.
In the case of
\mathsf{CS4}
, the only known lower bound is
pspace
, inherited from the intuitionistic propositional fragment
[
36
]
.
Closing this gap remains an interesting open problem.
Our conjecture is that
\mathsf{CS4}
is indeed
pspace
-complete.
Meanwhile, the finite models we have produced for
\mathsf{S4I}
are superexponential in size, but it may be possible to obtain smaller models via a subsequent selection.
In view of the non-primitive-recursive complexity of bimodal logics with similar frame properties
[
22
]
, it is unclear whether an elementary upper bound may be found.
We expect the results we have presented to be an important step in determining the complexity of these four logics.

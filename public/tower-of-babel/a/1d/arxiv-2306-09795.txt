Parabolic α \alpha -Riesz flows and limit cases α → 0 + \alpha\to 0^{+} , α → d − \alpha\to d^{-}
L. De Luca, M. Morini, M. Ponsiglione, E. Spadaro

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce the notion of parabolic α \alpha -Riesz flow, for α ∈ ( 0 , d ) \alpha\in(0,d) , extending the notion of s s -fractional heat flows to negative values of the parameter s = − α 2 s=-\frac{\alpha}{2} . Then, we determine the limit behaviour of these gradient flows as α → 0 + \alpha\to 0^{+} and α → d − \alpha\to d^{-} . To this end we provide a preliminary Γ \Gamma -convergence expansion for the Riesz interaction energy functionals. Then we apply abstract stability results for uniformly λ \lambda -convex functionals which guarantee that Γ \Gamma -convergence commutes with the gradient flow structure. Keywords: Gagliardo seminorms; Γ \Gamma -convergence; Fractional heat flow AMS subject classifications: 49J45 35R11 35K20



Introduction

Fractional versions of local operators attracted much attention in the last decades. For
s\in(0,1)
the so-called
s
-fractional Laplacians and

s
-fractional perimeters represent natural and interesting nonlocal counterparts of the classical Laplacian operator and Euclidean perimeter.
It is also well known
[
17
,
26
]
that for negative values of the parameter
s
, and specifically for
s=-\frac{\alpha}{2}
with
\alpha\in(0,d)
(
d
being the dimension of the ambient space) , the
\alpha
-Riesz potentials represent the natural continuation of the one-parameter family of
s
-fractional Laplacians (as well as of
s
-fractional perimeters and curvatures
[
7
]
) for negative values of
s
.

Much analysis has been devoted to studying the limit cases of
s
-fractional perimeters and Laplacians as
s\to 0^{+}
and
s\to 1^{-}
; remarkably, as
s\to 1^{-}
one recovers the local canonical objects, while the limit
s\to 0^{+}
is somehow more degenerate. The analogous analysis for Riesz type operators seems to be less investigated, with recent results only for what concerns geometric flows
[
7
]
. This paper gives a further contribution in this direction, introducing the notion of

parabolic
\alpha
-Riesz flows
and
studying the limit cases as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
and
\alpha\to d^{-}
.

We will now revisit the literature concerning the limit analysis of
s
-fractional operators as
s\to 0^{+}

(referring the interested reader to
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
9
,
10
,
18
,
24
,
19
]
for the case
s\to 1^{-}
).

In
[
20
]
the authors show that, as
s\to 0^{+}
, the squared
s
-fractional Gagliardo seminorms multiplied by
s
pointwise converge to (a multiple of) the squared
L^{2}
norm.
A
\Gamma
-convergence expansion (as
s\to 0^{+}
) with respect to the
L^{2}
topology has been derived in
[
9
]
.
The first term of such an expansion is, according with
[
20
]
, the squared
L^{2}
norm divided by
s
, whereas the next order term can be understood as a kind of
0
-fractional Gagliardo seminorm. The first variation of the zero-order
\Gamma
-limit is trivial, while the first variation of the first order
\Gamma
-limit gives an interesting elliptic operator, referred to as
0
-fractional Laplacian
, which is a slight variant of the logarithmic Laplacian introduced in
[
8
]
.
Still in
[
9
]
, the minimizing movements approach was used as a bridge between the
\Gamma
-convergence analysis and the stability of the corresponding fractional parabolic flows:
After scaling the time by
s
, the
s
-fractional parabolic flows converge, as
s\to 0^{+}
,
to the degenerate parabolic flow of the squared
L^{2}
norm (with respect to the
L^{2}
metric, itself); this is nothing but a trivial ODE corresponding to the exponential decay of the initial datum. If, instead of scaling in time, we renormalize the parabolic flow by subtracting the forcing leading term (corresponding to the first variation of the squared
L^{2}
norm, scaled by
s
), we end up with a much more interesting dynamics:
the heat flow governed by the
0
-fractional Laplacian, namely,
the parabolic flow of the first order
\Gamma
-limit of the
s
-fractional Gagliardo seminorms.

A first task of this paper is to provide a similar analysis as
s\to 0^{-}
. As already explained, for negative values of
s
the analogue of fractional Laplacians is given by
\alpha
-Riesz potentials
[
17
,
26
]
, with
\alpha=-2s
. The first step in our analysis is to formalize such an analogy introducing, for negative values of
s
, explicit counterparts of
s
-fractional Gagliardo seminorms, Laplacians, and the corresponding parabolic flows, referred to as parabolic
\alpha
-Riesz flows.
Such a track has already been pursued in the geometric framework
[
7
]
; there, suitable notions of
\alpha
-Riesz perimeters, curvatures and geometric flows have been introduced and analyzed as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
, leading to the geometric flow of the
0
-fractional perimeter introduced in
[
13
]
. (In fact, such a flow also coincides with the limit as
s\to 0^{+}
of the
s
-fractional mean curvature flows
[
7
]
.)

As in
[
9
,
7
]
, our approach is purely variational, in the sense that our stability analysis relies on the gradient flow structure of the parabolic
\alpha
-Riesz flows.
We consider an open bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and,
for every
\alpha\in(0,d)
, we define the
\alpha
-Riesz functionals

\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
on
L^{2}(\Omega)
as

(0.1)

\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}(u):=-\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x)u(y)}{|x-y|^{d-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

We stress that the first variation of
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
gives back the opposite of the
\alpha
-Riesz potentials
(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}

[
26
,
17
]
.
In terms of a more genuine variational viewpoint,
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
can be understood as a continuous version of the
XY
energy functional with long-range interactions and real-valued spin variable.
Oversimplifying, in classical
XY
scalar systems the spin variable
u
takes values
\pm 1
, and, for nearest neighbor interactions, the energy can be seen as a discrete perimeter of the phase
\{u=1\}
, as well as a discrete Dirichlet energy for functions defined on a lattice and taking only two values. Clearly, long-range variants of the
XY
model are naturally related with nonlocal perimeters; specifically, with fractional type perimeters, when the interaction kernel is non-integrable, and with Riesz type perimeters, when the kernel is locally integrable as in (
0.1
).
Although such analogies are part of the motivations for our study, here we will not push further considerations within such a statistical mechanics framework; in fact, we focus our analysis on real-valued functions, adopting tools and terminology of fractional seminorms and operators.

Now we describe in detail our results, starting from the asymptotic behavior of
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
.
In Theorem
2.1
we prove that the functionals
-\alpha\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}

\Gamma
-converge as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
to a multiple of
\|\cdot\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
with respect to the weak
L^{2}
topology. Some considerations about the signs of the energy functionals are in order: the functionals
-\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
penalize more and more, as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
, functions with large
L^{2}
norm, which, instead, are favored by the functionals
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
. Indeed, also after the natural scaling, the functionals
\alpha\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
would
\Gamma
-converge to the relaxation (with respect to the weak
L^{2}
topology) of
-\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
, which is constantly equal to
-\infty
(see Remark
2.2
). On the other hand, renormalizing the (otherwise asymptotically ill-posed) family of functionals
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
, by setting
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}(u):=\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}(u)+\frac{d\omega_{d}}{\alpha}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
, it turns out that the energies
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}
penalize oscillations. In fact, in Theorem
2.3
we prove that the
\Gamma
-limit of
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}
(as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
), with respect to the strong
L^{2}
topology,
is a nonlocal coercive Dirichlet type energy functional
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}
, which is nothing but
the
\Gamma
-limit derived in
[
9
]
for the renormalized
s
-fractional Gagliardo seminorms (as
s\to 0^{+}
).
Similarly to what observed above, here there is only one option in choosing the signs in order to deal with coercive functionals: reversing all the signs, one would end up with a kind of relaxation of
-\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}
, which would give back once again
-\infty
(Remark
2.5
). Similar considerations can be done in the setting of nonlocal geometric flows, but, trying to draw such a parallelism, a relevant difference emerges:
in the geometrical context
u
is a characteristic function, so that oscillations relaxing the energy to
-\infty
are automatically prevented. A relevant consequence is that,
contrarily to the case discussed above, in the geometric setting, the right sign is the negative one, since
the functionals
\alpha\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
computed on characteristic functions, together with their limit, provide a true perimeter, whose first variation is a nonlocal curvature satisfying natural comparison principles
[
7
]
.
These different paths of geometrical and linear framework rejoin when looking at the next order, where
we fix the negative sign for the
\alpha
-fractional Laplacians.

In this framework, we introduce the parabolic
\alpha
-Riesz flows as the
L^{2}
gradient flows of the functionals
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
, scaled or renormalized according with the
\Gamma
-convergence analysis. As mentioned above, the first variation of the functional
-\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
(for
\alpha\in(0,d)
) is already known in the literature
[
26
,
17
]
under the name of
\alpha
-Riesz potential
(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
.
Therefore, the fractional parabolic flows considered here are nothing but fractional heat flows governed by
s
-fractional Laplacians for negative values of the fractional parameter
s\in(-\frac{d}{2},0)
.

As one may expect from the static asymptotic analysis, scaling the time by
\alpha
, the
\alpha
-parabolic flows governed by the operator
(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
converge (Theorem
4.2
) as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
, to a degenerate ODE describing the exponential decay of the initial datum, namely,
to the gradient flow of the (up to a prefactor)
\Gamma
-limit
\|\cdot\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
; this result agrees with the analysis of
s
-fractional heat flows as
s\to 0^{+}
done in
[
9
]
. In analogy with the geometric case of fractional curvature flows
[
7
]
and following the discussions on the signs done above, one could wonder what happens reversing all the signs. In fact, the parabolic flows governed by
-\alpha(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
is actually well defined; however, as explained in Remark
4.3
, our variational methods based on
\Gamma
-convergence do not provide its convergence (as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
) to the parabolic flow
u_{t}=2u
. The latter describes the exponential growth of the initial datum, and is consistent with the corresponding geometric flows, where balls tend to expand (instead of shrinking) faster and faster as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
.

Following the first order
\Gamma
-convergence analysis, the gradient flow of the functional
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}
is a parabolic flow governed by
-(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
plus the forcing leading linear term (diverging as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
). This flow converges (Theorem
4.4
), as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
, to the gradient flow of
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}
, namely, to the parabolic flow governed by the
0
-fractional Laplacian.
Again, this result agrees with the analogous analysis done for
s\to 0^{+}
in
[
9
]
.
In fact,
the proofs of the stability results above are obtained as an application of the abstract approach developed in
[
9
]
; loosely speaking, the latter consists in showing that uniform
\lambda
-convexity and
\lambda
-positivity on an Hilbert setting is a sufficient condition to guarantee that
\Gamma
-convergence of the underlying functionals commutes with the gradient flow structure. We refer to Subsection
4.2
for a short recap on such an abstract setting.

The same approach, namely, first analyzing the asymptotic behavior of the functionals
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
as
\alpha\to d^{-}
and then using the abstract result in
[
9
]
to derive the convergence of the corresponding parabolic flows, allows to study also the limit as
\alpha\to d^{-}
for the parabolic
\alpha
-Riesz flows. In such a case, the analysis is simpler. Indeed, the interaction kernel in (
0.1
) converges to
1
(strongly in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
), whence, for every
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we deduce that
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}(u)
converges (as
\alpha\to d^{-}
) to
\mathcal{J}^{d}(u):=-\big(\int_{\Omega}u\big)^{2}
. Actually, in Theorem
3.1
, we prove that
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n})\to\mathcal{J}^{d}(u)
for every
\alpha_{n}\to d^{-}
and for every
u^{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, which provides in particular the
\Gamma
-convergence of the functionals
\pm\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
to
\pm\mathcal{J}^{d}
. As a consequence, in Theorem
4.5
we prove that the
\alpha
-parabolic flows governed by the operator
\pm(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
converge (Theorem
4.2
) , as
\alpha\to d^{-}
, to a degenerate ODE describing now the exponential growth/decay of the average of the initial datum, namely,
to the gradient flow of the
\Gamma
-limit
\mp\big(\int_{\Omega}u\big)^{2}
.
Moreover, for every
u^{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and for every
\alpha_{n}\to d^{-}
, the renormalized functionals

\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n}):=\frac{\mathcal{J}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n})-\mathcal{J}^{d}(u^{n})}{d-\alpha_{n}}\,

converge to the Riesz energy functional with logarithmic kernel

\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{d}(u):=-\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}u(x)u(y)\log\frac{1}{|x-y|}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

In particular, as
\alpha\to d^{-}
, the functionals
\pm\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}

\Gamma
-converge to the functional
\pm\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{d}
with respect to the weak
L^{2}
convergence (see Theorem
3.4
).
Finally, Theorem
4.6
establishes that the gradient flows of
\pm\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}
converge, as
\alpha\to d^{-}
, to the parabolic flow governed by
the operator
\pm(-\Delta)^{-\frac{d}{2}}
, namely, by the first variation of
\pm\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{d}
.

Summarizing, we have introduced the parabolic
\alpha
-Riesz flows, i.e., the flows governed by the
\alpha
-Riesz potential
(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
, for
\alpha\in(0,d)
, and we have analyzed their convergence as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
and
\alpha\to d^{-}
.
Along the way, we have focused on the interesting class of integral functionals

(0.2)

-\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x)u(y)}{|x-y|^{p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,,

representing, for
p\in(0,d)
, natural extensions of Gagliardo-type energies. In particular, through rigorous renormalization procedures, we have given a meaning to the critical case
p=d
.
It may be of some interest to extend our methods to
p\geq d
, adopting suitable renormalization procedures; in this direction, a popular method is the so-called

core radius approach
, already used in
[
12
]
in the geometric framework. The general case
p\in\mathbb{R}
, accounting also for negative values of
p
, could deserve further investigations. Ultimately, our analysis invites to a
methodical comparison between the one-parameter family (
0.2
) of generalized Riesz functionals/potentials and the (generalized) Gagliardo seminorms/fractional Laplacians.

1.
Description of the problem

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded and open set with Lipschitz continuous boundary.
For every
\alpha\in(0,d)
, we define the functional
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to(-\infty,+\infty)
as

(1.1)

\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}(u):=-\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x)u(y)}{|x-y|^{d-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

We first show that the functionals
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
are well defined and actually finite in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Let
k^{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty)
be the function defined by

(1.2)

k^{\alpha}(z):=\frac{1}{|z|^{d-\alpha}}\,.

Then,
k^{\alpha}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Moreover, for every
R>0
we set

(1.3)

k^{\alpha}_{R}:=\chi_{B_{R}(0)}k^{\alpha}\,;

it is immediate to check that

(1.4)

\|k^{\alpha}_{R}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\frac{d\omega_{d}}{\alpha}R^{\alpha}\,.

Now, for any
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and for every
R>\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)
, we have

(1.5)

\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}(u)=-\langle\tilde{u},\tilde{u}\ast k^{\alpha}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=-\langle\tilde{u},\tilde{u}\ast k^{\alpha}_{R}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,,

where, here and throughout the paper,
\tilde{u}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by
\tilde{u}=u
in
\Omega
and
\tilde{u}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.

Therefore, by (
1.5
) and (
1.4
), using Hölder and convolution’s Young inequalities, we obtain that for every
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)

(1.6)

|\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}(u)|\leq\|k^{\alpha}_{R}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\tilde{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{d\omega_{d}}{\alpha}R^{\alpha}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

which shows that
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
takes values in
(-\infty,+\infty)
.

We highlight that for any
\alpha\in(0,d)
the Fourier transform
\mathscr{F}[k^{\alpha}]
of
k^{\alpha}
is given by

(1.7)

\mathscr{F}[k^{\alpha}](\xi)=2^{\alpha}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{d-\alpha}{2})}k^{d-\alpha}(\xi)\,,

where
\Gamma
is the Euler Gamma function, defined by
\Gamma(\beta):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{\beta-1}e^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t
, for
\beta>0
.
Indeed, let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
belong to the Schwarz class. Then, setting
\beta:=d-\alpha
, by the very definition of Euler Gamma function and by Fubini Theorem, we have that

(1.8)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)k^{\alpha}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\frac{\beta}{2})}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\,\mathrm{d}t\,t^{\frac{\beta}{2}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\!\,\mathrm{d}x\,\varphi(x)e^{-t|x|^{2}}\,.

Moreover, since

\mathscr{F}[e^{-t|\cdot|^{2}}](\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-t|x|^{2}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,\mathrm{d}x=\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4t}}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}}},

by Parseval identity we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)e^{-t|x|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathscr{F}[\varphi](\xi)\frac{e^{-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4t}}}{t^{\frac{d}{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,,

which, plugged into (
1.8
), yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)k^{\alpha}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\frac{\beta}{2})}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\,\mathrm{d}t\,t^{\frac{\beta-d}{2}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathscr{F}[\varphi](\xi)e^{-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4t}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}2^{\alpha}\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{\alpha}{2}\big)}{\Gamma(\frac{d-\alpha}{2})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\mathcal{F}[\varphi](\xi)}k^{d-\alpha}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,,

where we have used first the change of variable
t=s|\xi|^{2}
and then
u=\frac{1}{4s}
to get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{\frac{\beta-d}{2}-1}e^{-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4t}}\,\mathrm{d}t=

\displaystyle|\xi|^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\frac{\beta-d}{2}-1}e^{-\frac{1}{4s}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\xi|^{-\alpha}2^{d-\beta}\int_{0}^{+\infty}u^{\frac{d-\beta}{2}-1}e^{-u}\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\xi|^{-\alpha}2^{\alpha}\Gamma\big(\frac{\alpha}{2}\big)\,.

Therefore, (
1.7
) holds true and hence
the kernels
k^{\alpha}
are positive definite, i.e.,

(1.9)

\langle v,v\ast k^{\alpha}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\geq 0\qquad\textrm{for any }v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,

which, by (
1.5
), yields

(1.10)

\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}(u)\leq 0\qquad\textrm{for any }u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\,.

Moreover, by (
1.5
), using the symmetry of
k^{\alpha}_{R}
, for any
u,v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
we have that

(1.11)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}(u)-\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}(v)|=

\displaystyle\Big|\langle u-v,(u+v)\ast k^{\alpha}_{R}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{d\omega_{d}}{\alpha}R^{\alpha}\big(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big)\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

whence we deduce the continuity of
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
with respect to the strong convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Now, for every
\alpha\in(0,d)
and for every
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
we set

(1.12)

\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}(u):=\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}(u)+\frac{d\omega_{d}}{\alpha}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{1}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|x-y|\leq 1\}
; for every
\alpha\in[0,d)
we define the functionals
\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{1}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty]
and
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}_{1}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
as

\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{1}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}\frac{|\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,,\qquad\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}_{1}(u):=-\iint_{(\Omega\times\Omega)\setminus\mathcal{D}_{1}}\frac{u(x)u(y)}{|x-y|^{d-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

We highlight that for every
\alpha\in(0,d)
and for every

u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
it holds

(1.13)

\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}(u)=\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{1}(u)+\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}_{1}(u)\,,

Notice that, by Hölder inequality, for every
\alpha\in[0,d)
and for every
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)

(1.14)

|\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}_{1}(u)|\leq\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}|u(x)u(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\|u\|^{2}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq|\Omega|\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

and hence
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}_{1}
is finite on
L^{2}(\Omega)
also for
\alpha=0
.
In full analogy with (
1.13
) we can define the functional
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}
also for
\alpha=0
by setting

(1.15)

\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}(u):=\mathcal{G}^{0}_{1}(u)+\mathcal{J}^{0}_{1}(u)\,,

for every
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. In view of (
1.14
), the functional
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is well defined.
Notice that
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}
in (
1.15
) coincides with the functional
\hat{F}^{0}
defined in
[
9
, formula (1.9)]
.

Remark 1.1
.

In view of its very definition (
1.12
) and of
(
1.11
), the functionals
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,d)
are continuous with respect to the strong convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, for every
\alpha\in[0,d)
and
for all
u,\,v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
we have

(1.16)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}_{1}(u)-\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}_{1}(v)|\leq

\displaystyle(\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)})\|u-v\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\Omega|(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

which shows, in particular, that
the functionals
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}
are lower semicontinuous with respect to the strong convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

The following result will be used throughout the paper.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
k\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\mathrm{supp}\;k\subset B_{R}(0)
for some
R>0
.
Let moreover
\{v^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
\mathrm{supp}\;v^{n}\subset B_{R}(0)
and

v^{n}\rightharpoonup v
for some
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then,
v^{n}\ast k\to v\ast k
(strongly) in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

We set
g^{n}:=v^{n}-v
, so that
g^{n}\rightharpoonup 0
in
L^{2}(B_{R}(0))
.
For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
[g^{n}\ast k](x)\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
. Moreover, let
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
; by Young’s convolution inequality, we have

(1.17)

\Big\|[g^{n}\ast k](\cdot+h)-[g^{n}\ast k](\cdot)\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|g^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|k(\cdot+h)-k(\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,;

since the right-hand side of (
1.17
) tends to
0
as
h\to 0
, uniformly with respect to
n
, by the Fréchet-Kolmogorov criterion we get the claim.
∎

2.
\Gamma
-convergence for the functionals
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}
as
\alpha\to 0^{+}

We start by studying the asymptotic behavior of the functionals
-\alpha\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,d)
be such that
\alpha_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to+\infty
.

(i)

(
\Gamma
-liminf inequality) For every
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and for every
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
with
u^{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, it holds

{d\omega_{d}}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}-\alpha_{n}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n}).

(ii)

(
\Gamma
-limsup inequality) For every
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}-\alpha_{n}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha_{n}}(u)={d\omega_{d}}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

Proof.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we set
\bar{k}^{\alpha_{n}}:=\alpha_{n}k^{\alpha_{n}}
with
k^{\alpha}
defined in (
1.2
) and, for every
R>\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)
we set
\bar{k}^{\alpha_{n}}_{R}:=\bar{k}^{\alpha_{n}}\chi_{B_{R}(0)}=\alpha_{n}k^{\alpha_{n}}_{R}
.

Proof of (i).

Since
\bar{k}^{\alpha_{n}}_{R}\,\mathrm{d}z\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}{d\omega_{d}}\delta_{0}
we have that
\tilde{u}\ast\bar{k}^{\alpha_{n}}_{R}\to d\omega_{d}\tilde{u}
(strongly) in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, and hence

(2.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\langle\tilde{u},(\tilde{u}^{n}-\tilde{u})\ast\bar{k}^{\alpha_{n}}_{R}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\langle\tilde{u}\ast\bar{k}^{\alpha_{n}}_{R},\tilde{u}^{n}-\tilde{u}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0\,,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\langle\tilde{u}^{n},\tilde{u}\ast\bar{k}^{\alpha_{n}}_{R}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\omega_{d}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

Moreover, by (
1.5
), we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle-\alpha_{n}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n})=

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{u}^{n}-\tilde{u},(\tilde{u}^{n}-\tilde{u})\ast\bar{k}^{\alpha_{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\langle\tilde{u},(\tilde{u}^{n}-\tilde{u})\ast\bar{k}^{\alpha_{n}}_{R}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle+\langle\tilde{u}^{n},\tilde{u}\ast\bar{k}^{\alpha_{n}}_{R}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,,

and hence, by (
1.10
), (
2.1
), and (
2.2
), we obtain

(2.4)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to+\infty}-\alpha_{n}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n})\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\langle\tilde{u}^{n}-\tilde{u},(\tilde{u}^{n}-\tilde{u})\ast\bar{k}^{\alpha_{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle+\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\langle\tilde{u},(\tilde{u}^{n}-\tilde{u})\ast\bar{k}^{\alpha_{n}}_{R}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\langle\tilde{u}^{n},\tilde{u}\ast\bar{k}^{\alpha_{n}}_{R}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle d\omega_{d}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

Proof of (ii).
The claim follows directly by (
2.2
) and by the first equality in (
1.5
).
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,d)
with
\alpha_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to+\infty
.
By (
2.1
)-(
2.3
), for any
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and for any sequence
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
with
u^{n}\to u
(strongly) in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\alpha_{n}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n})=-d\omega_{d}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

which shows, in particular, that the
\Gamma
-limit of the functionals
\alpha\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
(as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
) with respect to the strong convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
is the functional
-d\omega_{d}\|\cdot\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.
This fact implies that the
\Gamma
-limit of
\alpha\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
(as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
) with respect to the weak convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
equals to
-\infty
.

In Theorem
2.3
below we show that the functional
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}

\Gamma
-converges to the functional
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}
.
To this end, following
[
9
, Remark 1.3]
, we introduce the space

H^{0}_{0}(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\,:\,\mathcal{G}^{0}_{1}(u)<+\infty\}\,.

Such a functional space could be endowed with a
0
-Gagliardo type norm defined as

\|u\|_{H_{0}^{0}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\big(2\mathcal{G}^{0}_{1}(u)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,d)
be such that
\alpha_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to+\infty
. The following
\Gamma
-convergence result holds true.

(i)

(Compactness) Let
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
be such that

(2.5)

\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n})+(|\Omega|+m)\|u^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq M,

for some constants
m,M>0
independent of
n
. Then, up to a subsequence,
u^{n}\to u
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
for some
u\in H_{0}^{0}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

(
\Gamma
-liminf inequality) For every
u\in H^{0}_{0}(\Omega)
and for every
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
with
u^{n}\to u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, it holds

\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}(u)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n})\,.

(iii)

(
\Gamma
-limsup inequality) For every
u\in H_{0}^{0}(\Omega)
there exists
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
with
u^{n}\to u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}(u)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n})\,.

In order to prove (i) of Theorem
2.3
we adopt a strategy similar to that used in the proof of
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
. To this end we prove the following result.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,d)
be such that
\alpha_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to+\infty
.
Let moreover
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)

with

(2.6)

\|u^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\mathcal{G}^{\alpha_{n}}_{1}(u^{n})\leq C\,,

for some constant
C>0
(independent of
n
) . Then, up to a subsequence,
u^{n}\to u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
for some
u\in H^{0}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

By (
2.6
), there exists
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that, up to a (not-relabeled) subsequence,
u^{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

For every
\alpha\in[0,d)

and for every
0<\delta<1
, we define
j_{\delta}^{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(0,+\infty)
as

j_{\delta}^{\alpha}(z):=\frac{\chi_{A_{\delta,1}(0)}(z)}{|z|^{d-\alpha}}\,,

where we have set

A_{r,R}(\xi):=B_{R}(\xi)\setminus\overline{B}_{r}(\xi)
for every
0<r<R
and for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Furthermore, for every
\alpha\in[0,d)
and for every
0<\delta<1
we set
w_{\delta}^{\alpha}:=\frac{j_{\delta}^{\alpha}}{\|j_{\delta}^{\alpha}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}
.

Notice that

(2.7)

\|j_{\delta}^{\alpha_{n}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=d\omega_{d}\frac{1-\delta^{\alpha_{n}}}{\alpha_{n}}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|j_{\delta}^{\alpha_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\omega_{d}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\delta^{d-\alpha_{n}}}=\frac{\omega_{d}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\delta^{d}}\,.

We observe that for every
0<\delta<1

(2.8)

j^{\alpha_{n}}_{\delta}\to j^{0}_{\delta}\textrm{ in }C^{k}(\overline{A_{\delta,1}(0)})\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}\textrm{ and }\|j^{\alpha_{n}}_{\delta}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to d\omega_{d}|\log\delta|=\|j^{0}_{\delta}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,,

and, by Lemma
1.2
,

(2.9)

\|w^{0}_{\delta}\ast(u^{n}-u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to 0\qquad\textrm{as }n\to+\infty.

In turn, by triangular and convolution’s Young inequalities, using (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we get

(2.10)

\|w^{\alpha_{n}}_{\delta}\ast u^{n}-w^{0}_{\delta}\ast u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\leq\|w^{\alpha_{n}}_{\delta}-w^{0}_{\delta}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|u^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|w^{0}_{\delta}\ast(u^{n}-u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to 0\qquad\textrm{ as }n\to+\infty\,.

Now, following
[
16
, Lemma 2.2]
, using Jensen’s inequality,
we get

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|u^{n}-w_{\delta}^{\alpha_{n}}\ast u^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u^{n}(x+z)-u^{n}(x))w_{\delta}^{\alpha_{n}}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u^{n}(x+z)-u^{n}(x)|^{2}w_{\delta}^{\alpha_{n}}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\|j^{\alpha_{n}}_{\delta}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\mathcal{G}^{\alpha_{n}}_{1}(u^{n})\leq\frac{C}{|\log\delta|}\,,

where in the last inequality we have used (
2.6
) and (
2.8
). Moreover, since
w^{0}_{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}z\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\delta_{0}
as
\delta\to 0
, we have that

(2.12)

\|w^{0}_{\delta}\ast u-u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to 0\qquad\textrm{as }\delta\to 0\,.

Therefore, by triangular inequality, we obtain that, for every
0<\delta<1
,

\displaystyle\|u^{n}-u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|u^{n}-w_{\delta}^{\alpha_{n}}\ast u^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|w_{\delta}^{\alpha_{n}}\ast u^{n}-w_{\delta}^{0}\ast u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|w^{0}_{\delta}\ast u-u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,,

whence the strong convergence of
u^{n}
to
u
follows by and by (
2.10
), (
2.11
), and (
2.12
), sending first
n\to+\infty
and then
\delta\to 0
.
Finally, by Fatou Lemma and by (
2.6
),

(2.13)

\mathcal{G}_{1}^{0}(u)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{1}^{\alpha_{n}}(u_{n})\leq C\,,

whence we deduce that
u\in H^{0}_{0}(\Omega)
, thus concluding the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

We start by proving compactness.
By (
2.5
), (
1.13
) and (
1.14
), we have

M\geq\mathcal{G}^{\alpha_{n}}_{1}(u^{n})+m\|u^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

which, in view of Lemma
2.4
, yields (i).
Moreover, in view of (
1.13
), the proof of (ii) follows by (
2.13
) and by (

).
Finally, the proof of (iii) follows by using standard density arguments in

-convergence (see for instance the proof of

).
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Analogously to what already observed in Remark
2.2
, one can easily prove that the functionals
-\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}

\Gamma
-converge to
-\infty
(as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
) with respect to the strong
L^{2}
convergence. For example, assume that
0\in\Omega
and consider
v_{n}:=\frac{n^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\log^{\frac{1}{4}}n}\chi_{B_{\frac{1}{n}}(0)}
. Then, one can easily check that
v_{n}\to 0
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Moreover, for
n
large enough,

\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{1}(v_{n})\geq C\frac{1-n^{-\alpha}}{\alpha}\frac{1}{\log^{\frac{1}{2}}n}
; since

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\frac{1-n^{-\alpha}}{\alpha}\frac{1}{\log^{\frac{1}{2}}n}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}n=+\infty\,,

by a standard diagonal argument there exists a sequence
\{v_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}=\{v_{n(\alpha)}\}
with
\|v_{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0
and such that
\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{1}(v_{\alpha})\to+\infty
(as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
). As a consequence, for every
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
u+v_{\alpha}\to u
(strongly) in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{1}(u+v_{\alpha})\to+\infty
.
Recalling (
1.13
) and recalling Remark
1.1
, we conclude that
-\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}(u+v_{\alpha})\to-\infty
for every
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

3.
\Gamma
-convergence for the functionals
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
as
\alpha\to d^{-}

We define the functional
\mathcal{J}^{d}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to(-\infty,0]
as
\mathcal{J}^{d}(u):=-\Big(\int_{\Omega}u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{2}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,d)
be such that
\alpha_{n}\to{d}^{-}
as
n\to+\infty
.
For every
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and for every
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
with
u^{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
it holds

(3.1)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n})=\mathcal{J}^{d}(u)\,.

In particular, as
\alpha\to d^{-}
, the functionals
\pm\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}

\Gamma
-converge to the functional
\pm\mathcal{J}^{d}
with respect to the weak
L^{2}
convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We first notice that for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

(3.2)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|k^{\alpha_{n}}-1\|_{L^{1}(K)}=0\,,

where
k^{\alpha_{n}}
is defined in (
1.2
) .
By (
1.5
), we thus have that

(3.3)

\mathcal{J}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n})=-\langle\tilde{u}^{n},\tilde{u}^{n}\ast(k^{\alpha_{n}}-1)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}-\langle\tilde{u}^{n},\tilde{u}^{n}\ast 1\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,.

Now, taking
R>\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)
, by Young’s convolution inequality and by (
3.2
), we have

(3.4)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\langle\tilde{u}^{n},\tilde{u}^{n}\ast(k^{\alpha_{n}}-1)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\|u^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|k^{\alpha_{n}}-1\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(0))}=0\,,

and, since
u^{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
,

(3.5)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\langle\tilde{u}^{n},\tilde{u}^{n}\ast 1\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\langle\tilde{u},\tilde{u}\ast 1\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=-\mathcal{J}^{d}(u)\,.

By (
3.3
), (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we obtain (
3.1
).

∎

For every
\alpha\in(0,d)

we define the functional
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
as

\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}(u):=\frac{\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}(u)-\mathcal{J}^{d}(u)}{d-\alpha}\,;

moreover,
and for every
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
we define

(3.6)

\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{d}(u):=-\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}u(x)u(y)\tilde{k}^{d}(|x-y|)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,,

where we have set

\tilde{k}^{d}(z):=\log\frac{1}{|z|}
.
Notice that

(3.7)

\tilde{k}^{d}\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\qquad\textrm{for any }p\in[1,+\infty)\,;

therefore, for every
R>\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)
, by Young’s convolution inequality, we get

(3.8)

|\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{d}(u)|\leq\|\tilde{k}^{d}\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(0))}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\textrm{for every }u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\,,

which shows, in particular, that the functional
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{d}
defined by (
3.6
) takes values in
\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
k\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
\mathscr{J}_{k}
be the functional defined on
L^{2}(\Omega)
by

\mathscr{J}_{k}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}u(x)u(y)k(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad\textrm{for every }u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\,.

Let
R\geq\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)
.
Then, by Hölder and Young’s convolution inequalities,

|\mathscr{J}_{k}(u)|\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|k\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(0))}\,.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
\{k^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|k^{n}\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(0))}\leq C
, for some
C>0
and
R>\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)
. Then, by Remark
3.2
the functionals
\mathscr{J}_{k^{n}}
are
\lambda
-positive, for any
\lambda>2C
. Therefore, for any sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
with

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{J}_{k^{n}}(u^{n})+\lambda\|u^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<+\infty\,,

we have that, up to a subsequence,
u^{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, for some
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

In particular, this applies to any sequence of functionals
\{\mathcal{J}^{\alpha_{n}}\}_{n}
with
\alpha_{n}\to d^{-}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,d)
be such that
\alpha_{n}\to d^{-}
as
n\to+\infty
.

For every
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and for every
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
with
u^{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
it holds

(3.9)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n})=\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{d}(u)\,.

In particular, as
\alpha\to d^{-}
, the functionals
\pm\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}

\Gamma
-converge to the functional
\pm\mathcal{J}^{d}
with respect to the weak
L^{2}
convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
we set
\tilde{k}^{\alpha_{n}}:=\frac{k^{\alpha_{n}}-1}{d-\alpha_{n}}
, with
k^{\alpha_{n}}
defined in (
1.2
).
For any
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, it holds

(3.10)

\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}(v)=-\langle\tilde{v},\tilde{v}\ast\tilde{k}^{\alpha_{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,,\qquad\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{d}(v)=-\langle\tilde{v},\tilde{v}\ast\tilde{k}^{d}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,,

and hence

(3.11)

\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}(v)=-\langle\tilde{v},\tilde{v}\ast(\tilde{k}^{\alpha_{n}}-\tilde{k}^{d})\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{d}(v)\\

Moreover, by Taylor expansion, for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, we have

(3.12)

\tilde{k}^{\alpha_{n}}(z)=\tilde{k}^{d}(z)+\mathrm{O}\Big((d-\alpha_{n})\log^{2}\frac{1}{|z|}\Big)\,,

with
\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{O}(t)}{t}\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds

(3.13)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|\tilde{k}^{\alpha_{n}}-\tilde{k}^{d}\|_{L^{p}(K)}=0\qquad\textrm{for any }p\in[1,+\infty)\,.

Let
R>\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)
.

We start by proving (a).
By Young’s convolution inequality and by (
3.13
), we have

(3.14)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\langle\tilde{u}^{n},\tilde{u}^{n}\ast(\tilde{k}^{\alpha_{n}}-\tilde{k}^{d})\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\|u^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\tilde{k}^{\alpha_{n}}-\tilde{k}^{d}\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(0))}=0\,,

and, by Lemma
1.2
,

(3.15)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{d}(u^{n})=\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{d}(u)\,.

By (
3.11
), (
3.14
), and (
3.15
), we thus obtain

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n})=\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{d}(u)\,,

i.e., (
3.9
).

∎

Remark 3.5
.

By Remark
3.3
and by (
3.11
), (
3.13
) and (
3.8
), it immediately follows that any sequence
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}\}_{n}
with
\alpha_{n}\to d^{-}
is uniformly
\lambda
-positive and hence weakly equi-coercive in
L^{2}(\Omega)
(in the sense of Remark
3.3
).

4.
Convergence of the
\alpha
-Riesz flows

4.1.
The
\alpha
-Riesz potential for
\alpha\in(0,d)
and for
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=d

For every
\alpha\in(0,d)
and for every
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the
\alpha
-Riesz potential is formally defined by

(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}v(x):=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{v(y)}{|x-y|^{d-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,,\qquad\qquad\textrm{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

Notice that such a definition coincides, up to multiplicative constants depending on
\alpha
and
d
, with
[
26
, formula (4), p. 117]
.
By
[
26
, Theorem 1, p. 119]
the above definition is well posed, and there exists a constant
C(d,\alpha)
such that

\|(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}v\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C(d,\alpha)\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\qquad\qquad\textrm{for all }v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,

where
q=\frac{2d}{d-\alpha}
. Since
q\geq 2
, by Hölder inequality, we deduce that

(4.1)

\|(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(d,\alpha,\Omega)\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})};

we also notice that, if
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
with
v\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega
, then (
4.1
) is an easy consequence of convolution’s Young inequality.
With a little abuse of notation, for every
\alpha\in(0,d)
and for every

u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
we set
(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}u:=(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\tilde{u}
.

One can easily check that the
\alpha
-Riesz potential is nothing but the first variation of the
\alpha
-Riesz functionals
-\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
, i.e., for every
u,\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
we have

(4.2)

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}(u+t\varphi)-\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}(u)}{t}=-\langle(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}u,\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

In analogy with
[
9
, Subsection 4.1]
,
for every
\psi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we define the
0
-fractional laplacian of
\psi
as

(-\Delta)^{0}\psi(x):=\int_{B_{1}}\frac{2\psi(x)-\psi(x+z)-\psi(x-z)}{|z|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}z-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{B}_{1}}\frac{\psi(x+z)}{|z|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

Furthermore, for every
u\in H^{0}_{0}(\Omega)

we define
0
-fractional laplacian of
u
by duality as

(4.3)

\langle(-\Delta)^{0}u,\varphi\rangle:=\langle u,(-\Delta)^{0}\tilde{\varphi}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,\qquad\textrm{for all }\varphi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)\,.

Clearly, the
0
-fractional laplacian is the first variation of the functional
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}
, as shown in
[
9
, Proposition 3.2]
.

Finally, for every
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
we set

(-\Delta)^{-\frac{d}{2}}v(x):=2\int_{\Omega}v(y)\log\frac{1}{|x-y|}\,\mathrm{d}y\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,

and we notice that
(-\Delta)^{-\frac{d}{2}}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

It is easy to see that
(-\Delta)^{-\frac{d}{2}}
is the first variation of the functional
-\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{d}
defined in (
3.6
) , i.e., that for any
u,\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega)

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{d}(u+t\varphi)-\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}^{d}(u)}{t}=-\langle(-\Delta)^{-\frac{d}{2}}u,\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

4.2.
Abstract stability results for parabolic flows

It is well known that, under suitable uniform convexity assumptions,
\Gamma
-convergence commutes with gradient flows. This fact, first exploited in
[
6
]
, has been generalized in many respects, such as for instance in metric spaces
[
11
,
2
,
25
]
. For the specific framework of uniform
\lambda
-convex functionals we refer for instance to
[
21
,
22
,
23
,
9
]
. Here, we recall the stability result as appeared in
[
9
, Theorem 3.8]
,
that best suits our purposes.
We start by recalling the notion of
\lambda
-positive and
\lambda
-convex functions.
Let
\mathscr{H}
be a Hilbert space and let
\lambda>0
. We say that a function
\mathcal{F}:\mathscr{H}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
is
\lambda
-convex if the function
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)+\frac{\lambda}{2}|\cdot|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}
is convex.
Moreover, we say that
\mathcal{F}
is
\lambda
-positive if

\mathcal{F}(x)+\frac{\lambda}{2}|x|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\geq 0
for every
x\in\mathscr{H}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be a Hilbert space. Let
\{\mathcal{F}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\mathcal{F}^{n}:\mathscr{H}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
be a sequence of proper, strongly lower semicontinuous functions which are
\lambda
-convex and
\lambda
-positive, for some
\lambda>0
independent of
n
. Let
\{x_{0}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathscr{H}
be such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{F}^{n}(x_{0}^{n})<+\infty
and
x_{0}^{n}\to x^{\infty}_{0}

for some
x^{\infty}_{0}\in\mathscr{H}
. Assume that one of the following statements is satisfied:

(a)

The functions
\mathcal{F}^{n}

\Gamma
-converge to some proper function
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}
with respect to the weak
\mathscr{H}
-convergence.
Moreover,
the
\Gamma
-limsup inequality is satisfied with respect to the strong
\mathscr{H}
-convergence, i.e., for every
y\in\mathscr{H}
there exists a sequence
\{y^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
y^{n}\overset{\mathscr{H}}{\to}y
such that
\mathcal{F}^{n}(y^{n})\to\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(y)
as
n\to+\infty
.

(b)

The functions
\mathcal{F}^{n}

\Gamma
-converge to some proper function
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}
with respect to the strong
\mathscr{H}
-convergence (as
n\to+\infty
) and every sequence
\{y^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathscr{H}
with

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{F}^{n}(y^{n})+\frac{\lambda}{2}|y^{n}|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}<+\infty

admits a strongly convergent subsequence.

Let
T>0
.
Then,
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(x_{0}^{\infty})<+\infty
and the unique solutions
x^{n}\in H^{1}([0,T];\mathscr{H})
of the Cauchy problem

(4.4)

\begin{cases}\dot{x}(t)\in-\partial\mathcal{F}^{n}(x(t))\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }t\in(0,T)\,,\\
x(0)=x_{0}^{n}\end{cases}

weakly converge, as
n\to+\infty
, in
H^{1}([0,T];\mathscr{H})
to the unique solution
x^{\infty}
of the problem

(4.5)

\begin{cases}\dot{x}(t)\in-\partial\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(x(t))\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }t\in(0,T)\,,\\
x(0)=x^{\infty}_{0}\,.\end{cases}

Furthermore, if

(4.6)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}^{n}(x^{n}_{0})=\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(x^{\infty}_{0})\,,

then, we have that

(4.7)

x^{n}\to x^{\infty}\qquad\textrm{(strongly) in }H^{1}([0,T];\mathscr{H})\,,

(4.8)

x^{n}(t)\overset{\mathscr{H}}{\to}x^{\infty}(t)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathcal{F}^{n}(x^{n}(t))\to\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(x^{\infty}(t))\qquad\textrm{for every }t\in[0,T]\,.

4.3.
Convergence of the
\alpha
-parabolic flows as
\alpha\to 0^{+}

Here we state and prove the convergence results for the parabolic flows corresponding to the (either scaled or renormalized)
\alpha
-Riesz functionals as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
. These results follow by applying our
\Gamma
-convergence analysis together with the stability result Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,d)
be such that
\alpha_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to+\infty
. Let
u^{\infty}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\{u_{0}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
be such that

u^{n}_{0}\to u^{\infty}_{0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Let
T>0
; then,
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a unique solution
u^{n}\in H^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
of

(4.9)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(t)=-\alpha_{n}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2}}u(t)\qquad\textrm{for a.e.\ }t\in(0,T)\\
u(0)=u^{n}_{0}\,.\end{cases}

Moreover,
u^{n}\to u^{\infty}
in
H^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
as
n\to+\infty
, where
u^{\infty}

is the unique solution of

(4.10)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(t)=-2d\omega_{d}u(t)\qquad\textrm{for a.e.\ }t\in(0,T)\,,\\
u(0)=u^{\infty}_{0}\,,\end{cases}

and

\|u^{n}(t)-u^{\infty}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0\quad\textrm{and}\quad-\alpha_{n}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n}(t))\to d\omega_{d}\|u^{\infty}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\textrm{for every }t\in[0,T]\,.

Proof.

Recall that,
by (
1.6
), the functionals
-\alpha_{n}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha_{n}}
are finite on
L^{2}(\Omega)
and, by (
1.10
) and Remark
3.2
, they are also positive and convex.
Furthermore, by (
1.11
), the functionals
-\alpha_{n}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha_{n}}
are continuous with respect to the strong convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, for every
u,\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,

(4.11)

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{d\omega_{d}\|u+t\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-d\omega_{d}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{t}=2\langle d\omega_{d}u,\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

Finally, by Remark
2.2
,
\lim_{n\to+\infty}-\alpha_{n}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n}_{0})=d\omega_{d}\|u^{\infty}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.
Hence, the conclusion follows by applying Theorem
4.1
with
\mathscr{H}=L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\mathcal{F}^{n}=-\alpha_{n}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha_{n}}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}(\cdot)=d\omega_{d}\|\cdot\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, once noticed that, in view of Theorem
2.1
, assumption (a) is satisfied.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
u^{n}
be the unique solution to the Cauchy problem (
4.9
) and let
u
be the solution to (
4.10
) with
-2d\omega_{d}
replaced by
2d\omega_{d}
. Existence and uniqueness of the solutions for the problems above are consequence of the classical gradient flow theory for
\lambda
-convex functionals (see, for instance,
[
2
,
21
]
or
[
9
, Theorem 3.2]
). It is a natural question if
u^{n}
converge to
u
. In the geometric context of characteristic functions this is the case for the corresponding curvature flows. In the present setting, unfortunately, Theorem
4.1
does not apply. In fact, on the one hand,
compactness can be achieved only in the weak
L^{2}
topology, so that assumption (b) does not hold true.
On the other hand, in view of Remark
2.2
, the
\Gamma
-limit of the functionals
\alpha\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}
(as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
) is
-d\omega_{d}\|\cdot\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
only with respect to the strong
L^{2}
topology, so that neither assumption (a) is satisfied.
More in general, it would be interesting
to detect natural assumptions weaker than (a) and (b) that guarantee convergence for similar parabolic flows,
when the
\Gamma
-convergence property holds true with respect to a topology that is stronger than that for which equicoercivity can be provided.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,d)
be such that
\alpha_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to+\infty
. Let
u^{\infty}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and let
\{u^{n}_{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
be such that
sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n}_{0})<+\infty

and

u^{n}_{0}\to u^{\infty}_{0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Let
T>0
; then,
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a unique solution
u^{n}\in H^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
of

(4.12)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(t)=-\Big[-(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2}}u(t)+2\frac{d\omega_{d}}{\alpha_{n}}u(t)\Big]\qquad\textrm{for a.e.\ }t\in(0,T)\,,\\
u(0)=u^{n}_{0}\,.\end{cases}

Moreover,
u^{\infty}_{0}\in H^{0}_{0}(\Omega)
and
u^{n}\rightharpoonup u^{\infty}
in
H^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
as
n\to+\infty
, where

u^{\infty}
is the unique solution of

(4.13)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(t)=-(-\Delta)^{0}u(t)\qquad\textrm{for a.e.\ }t\in(0,T)\\
u(0)=u^{\infty}_{0}\,.\end{cases}

Furthermore, if

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n}_{0})=\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}(u^{\infty}_{0})\,,

then,
u^{n}\to u^{\infty}
(strongly) in
H^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
and

\|u^{n}(t)-u^{\infty}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0\quad\textrm{and}\quad\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n}(t))\to\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}(u^{\infty}(t))\qquad\textrm{for every }t\in[0,T]\,.

Proof.

By (
1.13
) and (
1.14
), the functionals

\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}
are
\lambda
-convex and
\lambda
-positive for every
\lambda>2|\Omega|
. Moreover, by Remark
1.1
, they are lower semicontinuous with respect to the strong convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Now, by (
4.2
) and (
4.11
), using the very definition of
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}
in (
1.12
), we have that, for every
u,\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega)

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}(u+t\varphi)-\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}(u)}{t}=\Big\langle-(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2}}u+2\frac{d\omega_{d}}{\alpha_{n}}u,\varphi\Big\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

Hence, the stability claim follows by applying Theorem
4.1
with
\mathscr{H}=L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\mathcal{F}^{n}=\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha_{n}}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}=\widehat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}
, once noticed that, in view of Theorem
2.3
, assumption (b) is satisfied.
∎

.

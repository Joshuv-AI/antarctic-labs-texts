Abstract
. We introduce a new curvature-pinching condition, which is weaker than the positive sectional curvature or PIC1, and then we prove several rigidity results for rotationally symmetric solutions of steady Ricci solitons, i.e., the Bryant Ricci solitons.

0.
Introduction

In
[
4
]
, Brendle solved the following Perelman’s conjecture
[
34
]
:

Any
3
-dimensional (complete) non-flat
\kappa
-noncollapsed steady gradient soliton is rotationally. Namely, it is isometric to the Bryant Ricci soliton up to scaling.

For general dimensional steady gradient solitons, Brendle also proved
[
5
]

Theorem 0.1
.

Any steady gradient Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g)

(n\geq 3)
with positive sectional curvature must be isometric to the Bryant Ricci soliton up to scaling if it is asymptotically cylindrical.

Since any
3
-dimensional non-flat steady gradient soliton has always positive sectional curvature
[
11
]
and it is also asymptotically cylindrical by the
\kappa
-noncollapsed property
[
34
]
, Theorem
0.1
in particular also confirms the Perelman’s conjecture.

The asymptotically cylindrical behavior is an asymptotic property for a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)

[
5
]
(also see Definition
1.2
). In this paper, we purpose to weaken the global condition of positive sectional curvature to generalize Theorem
0.1
.

Let
{\rm Rm}_{g}
be the curvature operator tensor and
R=R(x)
the scalar curvature of
(M,g)
. We introduce the following curvature-pinching condition,

(0.1)

\displaystyle 2P<R,

where the function
P=P(x)
is defined on
M
by

(0.2)

\displaystyle P(\cdot)=\sup_{h}\frac{{\rm Rm}_{g}(h,h)}{|h|^{2}}=\sup_{h}\frac{{\rm R}_{ijkl}(\cdot)h^{ik}h^{jl}}{h_{ik}h^{ik}},~\forall~{\rm symmetric}~{\rm 2-tensor}~h\neq 0.

The operator
{\rm Rm}_{g}(h,\cdot)
on the space of symmetric
2
-tensors
h
arises from the Lichnerowicz Laplacian
[
3
]
(also see (
4.1
)). When the operator is restricted to the traceless
h
, its negative is usually called the second kind curvature operator (cf.
[
32
,
10
,
29
]
).

The following is our main result in this paper.

Theorem 0.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)

(n\geq 4)
be a steady gradient Ricci soliton which satisfies

(\ref{pinching-condition})
. Then
(M,g)
must be isometric to the Bryant Ricci soliton up to scaling if it is asymptotically cylindrical.

We will discuss a class of
(M^{n},g)
(
n\geq 4
) called with PIC0, which satisfy (
0.1
) in Section 1 (cf. Proposition
1.6
). This class consists of
all
(M^{n},g)
with positive sectional curvature or PIC1.
Thus, Theorem
0.2
is in particular a generalization of Theorem
0.1
for the steady gradient solitons with positive sectional curvature.

It seems that PIC0 is very closely related to PIC (cf. (
1.8
)). The notion of PIC was first introduced by Micallef and Moore
[
31
]
in their work on the index of minimal two-spheres. The PIC condition is preserved under the Ricci flow, proved by Hamilton
[
25
]
for
4
-dimension, and Nguyen
[
33
]
and Brendle-Schoen
[
8
]
independently for higher dimensions.
There are important classification results for differential structures of compact manifolds with PIC via surgery method, we refer the reader to
[
25
,
12
,
13
,
6
]
, etc.

As an application of Theorem
0.2
, we prove the following rigidity of the Bryant soliton with respect to the linear decay curvature.

Corollary 0.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)

(n\geq 4)
be a
\kappa
-noncollapsed steady gradient Ricci soliton with positive Ricci curvature. Suppose that there exists
C_{0}
and
r_{0}
such that for any
x\in M
with
\rho(x)\geq r_{0}
it holds

(0.3)

\displaystyle{\rm Rm}_{g}(x)\geq 0~{\rm and}~R(x)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\rho(x)}.

Then
(M,g)
is isometric to the Bryant Ricci soliton if it satisfies
(\ref{pinching-condition})
. In particular, any

n
-dimensional
\kappa
-noncollapsed steady gradient Ricci soliton with PIC0 must be isometric to the Bryant Ricci soliton up to scaling under the curvature decay
(\ref{decay-condition})
.

The above corollary generalizes a result of Deng-Zhu
[
19
]
instead of the positive sectional curvature by the pinching-curvature (
0.1
) or PIC0.
We note that the global condition of positive Ricci curvature and the condition (
0.3
) near the infinity are both necessary according to examples of
\kappa
-noncollapsed steady gradient Ricci soliton constructed in
[
18
,
2
]
, which are not isometric to the Bryant Ricci soliton up to scaling. But we guess that the positive Ricci curvature condition can be removed under (
0.1
). We also note that just assuming the PIC0 is not sufficient to prove the rigidity of steady gradient Ricci solitons, thanks for the new examples of Lai about
\kappa
-noncollapsed steady gradient Ricci solitons of dimension
n\geq 4
with the positive curvature operator which are not rotationally symmetric
[
27
]
.

Definition 0.4
.

(M^{n},g)
is called
C^{2,\tau}
-asymptotic to the
n
-dimensional Bryant Ricci soliton
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0})
if
there are a ball
B_{R}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a diffeomorphism

F:(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R},g_{0})\rightarrow(M,g)

such that
\hat{g}=F^{*}g
satisfies

(0.4)

\displaystyle|\hat{g}-g_{0}|_{g_{0}}(x)\leq O(\rho(x)^{-\tau-\frac{1}{2}}),\quad|\nabla^{k}\hat{g}|_{g_{0}}\leq O(\rho(x)^{-\tau-k}),

where
k=1,2,\tau\in(0,1)
.

By Corollary
0.3
, we can get the following rigidity theorem for the steady gradient Ricci solitons.

Corollary 0.5
.

Any steady gradient Ricci soliton with the curvature condition
(\ref{pinching-condition})
, which is
C^{2,\tau}
-asymptotic to the
n
-dimensional Bryant Ricci soliton
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0})
, must be isometric to
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0})
.

In general, there is no rigidity for the steady soliton under the metric
C^{l,\tau}
-asymptotic behavior if there is no curvature restriction. For examples, Conlon and Deruelle have constructed a complete steady gradient Kähler-Ricci soliton in every Kähler class of a crepant resolution over a Calabi-Yau cone, which unique up to the flow of the soliton vector field converges at a polynomial rate to the Cao’s steady gradient Kähler-Ricci soliton on the cone
[
17
]
.

It is interesting to mention that a similar version of Corollary
0.5
has been established for the shrinking gradient Ricci soliton
[
26
]
and the expending gradient Ricci soliton
[
21
,
22
]
, respectively. We hope that the condition (
0.1
) can be weakened by the positive Ricci curvature. Actually, we have the following conjecture.

Conjecture 0.6
.

Any steady gradient Ricci soliton with positive Ricci curvature, which is
C^{2,\tau}
-asymptotic to the
n
-dimensional Bryant Ricci soliton
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0})
, must be isometric to
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0})
.

To prove Theorem
0.2
, we will follow an approach of Brendle to construct
\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
Killing vector fields (VFs) on the steady Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g)
as in the proof of Theorem
0.1

[
5
]
,
where the positivity of sectional curvature plays a crucial role in his decay estimate for the solutions of Lichnerowicz-type equation via the maximum principle (also see Remark
4.5
).
However, in our case with the curvature condition (
0.1
), we use the heat flow method to do the decay estimate by solving the parabolic version of Lichnerowicz-type equation (cf. Section 5). Thus we need to show that the Lie derivatives of metric

associated to the constructed

approximate Killing VFs have fast polynomial decay (cf. Section 4).

We now outline the main steps in the proof of Theorem

.

Step 1 (cf. Section 2, 3). As in

4
,
5
]
, we construct a collection of approximate Killing VFs

satisfy (cf. Proposition

(0.5)

\displaystyle\left|\mathscr{L}_{U_{a}}(g)\right|\leq O(\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}\delta})~{\rm and}~|\Delta U_{a}+D_{X}U_{a}|\leq O(\rho^{-1-\frac{1}{4}\delta}).

The above estimate allows us to get global solutions of the following elliptic equation associated to
U_{a}
on
(M,g)
,

(0.6)

\displaystyle\Delta W_{a}+D_{X}W_{a}=0.

The method is to
solve the Dirichlet problem via perturbation and exhausting as in
[

, Section 3]
.

Step 2 (cf. Section 4). We shall do the decay estimate of

for solutions of
Lichnerowicz-type equation associated to the steady Ricci soliton
(M,g)
,

(0.7)

\displaystyle\Delta_{L}h_{a}+\mathscr{L}_{X}(h_{a})=0.

We prove that there exists a constant
\lambda
such
that
|\tilde{h}_{a}|
, where
\tilde{h}_{a}=h_{a}-\lambda\operatorname{Ric}(g)
, decays to zero at any polynomial rate (cf. Theorem
4.3
).

Step 3 (cf. Section 5). We prove that the uniqueness of solutions of (
0.7
) and

\tilde{h}_{a}
is in fact zero then we will finish the proof of Theorem
0.2
. Our method is to consider the following parabolic Lichnerowicz equation for the initial value
\tilde{h}_{a}
constructed in Step 2,

(0.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h=\Delta_{L,g(t)}h,

where

is the Ricci flow solution induced by

via transformations group

generated by the soliton VF.
By modifying the pinching estimate of Anderson-Chow for the solution of (

We will show that there exists a bounded positive super-solution of (

1.
Preliminary

A complete Riemannian metric
g
on
M
is called a gradient Ricci soliton if there exists a smooth function
f
( which is called a defining function) on
M
such that

(1.1)

R_{ij}(g)+\rho g_{ij}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}f,

where
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant. The gradient Ricci soliton is called expanding, steady and shrinking according to the sign
\rho>,=,<0
, respectively. These three types of Ricci solitons correspond to three different blow-up solutions of Ricci flow
[
24
]
.

In case of steady Ricci solitons, we can rewrite (
1.1
) as

(1.2)

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Ric}(g)=\mathscr{L}_{X}g,

where
\mathscr{L}_{X}
is the Lie operator along the gradient VF
X=\nabla f
generalized by
f
.
Let
\{\Phi^{*}_{t}\}_{t\in(-\infty,\infty)}
be a 1-ps of transformations generated by
-X
. Then

g(t)=\Phi^{*}_{t}(g)
(
t\in(-\infty,\infty))
is a solution of Ricci flow. Namely,
g(t)
satisfies

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}=-2{\rm Ric}(g),~g(0)=g.

For simplicity, we call
g(t)
the soliton Ricci flow of
(M,g)
.

By (
1.2
), we have

\langle\nabla R,\nabla f\rangle=-2\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f),

where
R
is the scalar curvature of
g
.
It follows

R+|\nabla f|^{2}={\rm Const.}

Since
R
is alway positive (
[
36
,
9
]
), the above equation can be normalized by

(1.4)

\displaystyle R+|\nabla f|^{2}=1.

In dimension
n=2
, it is classified that the ciger is only solution
[
25
,
16
]
. Brendle prove that the Bryant soliton is only
3
-dimensional non-flat
\kappa
-noncollapsed steady gradient soliton up to scaling
[
4
]
. Here we call a steady gradient soliton as
the Bryant Ricci soliton if it is rotational and is normalized by (

). In this paper, we always assume

, if there is no special emphasis.

The following result has been proved in
[

,
14
]

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a steady gradient soliton. Suppose that the scalar curvature decays uniformly and have positive Ricci curvature outside of a compact set

. Then there exists positive constants

,
C_{1}

C_{2}
such that for all

C_{1}\rho(x)\leq f(x)\leq C_{2}\rho(x),

1.1.
Asymptotically cylindrical steady Ricci solitons

Recall (cf.
[
5
]
)

Definition 1.2
.

We say that a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is asymptotically cylindrical if the following holds:

(i) The scalar curvature satisfies
\frac{C_{1}}{\rho(x)}\leq R(x)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\rho(x)}
as
\rho(x)>>1
, where
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
are two positive constants.

(ii) Let
p_{m}
be an arbitrary sequence of marked points going to infinity. Consider the rescaled metrics

\hat{g}^{(m)}(t)=r_{m}^{-1}\Phi^{*}_{r_{m}t}(g),

where
r_{m}R(p_{m})=\frac{n-1}{2}+o(1)
as

(M,\hat{g}^{(m)}(t),p_{m})
converges in the Cheeger-Gromov sense to a family of shrinking cylinders
(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R},~\bar{g}(t))
,

. The metric
\bar{g}(t)
is given by

(1.6)

\displaystyle\bar{g}(t)=(n-2)(2-2t)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(1)}+dr^{2},

where
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(1)
is the unit sphere in the Euclidean space.

Under the condition (ii) in Definition
1.2
, the following curvature behavior estimate for the steady gradient solitons has been proved in
[

.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a noncompact steady Ricci soliton, which satisfies the condition (ii) in Definition
1.2
. For
p\in M
, let
\{e_{1},e_{2},...,e_{n}\}
be an orthonormal basis of

with respect to metric

, and
e_{n}=\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{|\operatorname{Hess}R|+|\Delta\mathrm{Ric(g)}|}{R^{2}}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{ as }\quad\rho(x)\rightarrow\infty,

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{Ric}\left(e_{i},e_{j}\right)}{R}\rightarrow\frac{\delta_{ij}}{(n-1)},\quad\text{ as }\quad\rho(x)\rightarrow\infty,

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{Ric}\left(e_{i},e_{n}\right)}{R}\rightarrow 0\text{, as }\quad\rho(x)\rightarrow\infty,

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{Rm}\left(e_{k},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l}\right)}{R}\rightarrow\frac{(1-\delta_{jk})\delta_{jl}}{(n-1)(n-2)},\quad\text{ as }\quad\rho(x)\rightarrow\infty,

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{Rm}\left(e_{n},e_{i},e_{j},e_{k}\right)}{R}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{ as }\quad\rho(x)\rightarrow\infty.

In particular, there exists a compact set
K\subset M
such that

has positive sectional curvature outside of

By the above lemma (also see
[

Lemma

\displaystyle R_{ikik}+R_{jkjk}+\lambda^{2}(R_{ilil}+R_{jljl})-2\lambda R_{ijkl}>0~for~all~\lambda\in[0,1].

In case that (

, namely,

\displaystyle R_{\{i,j,k\}}=R_{ikik}+R_{jkjk}>0,

Recall that the function

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm Rm}(h,h)}{|h|^{2}}(x)=\frac{\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}R_{ijij}h_{ii}h_{jj}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}h_{ii}^{2}}.

Lemma 1.5

\displaystyle+M_{jk}(h_{j}-h_{l})^{2}+M_{jl}(h_{j}-h_{l})^{2}+M_{kl}(h_{k}-h_{l})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 0

2.
Fine estimates of curvature behavior

In this section, we modify the argument in
[
5
, Section 2]
to improve the estimate for the curvature behavior around
level sets of the steady Ricci soliton with asymptotically cylindrical property. The result will be used to construct the solutions of elliptic equation (
0.6
) for VFs in Section 3.

For a fixed point
p\in M
, we choose an orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},e_{2},...,e_{n}\}
with
e_{n}=\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}
.
By
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
(also see Lemma
1.3
), we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}\left(e_{i},e_{j}\right)=\frac{1}{n-1}R\delta_{ij}+o\left(r^{-1}\right),~i,j\in\{1,2,...,n-1\}.

and

(2.2)

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Ric}\left(e_{i},X\right)=-\left\langle e_{i},\nabla R\right\rangle=o\left(r^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right).

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-1}RR_{ijkl}+O\left(r^{-\frac{5}{2}-4\delta}\right).

Then, by Lemma
2.6

\displaystyle-D_{X}R_{ijkl}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n-1}RR_{ijkl}+O\left(r^{-\frac{5}{2}-4\delta}\right)

\displaystyle=f^{-1}R_{ijkl}+\left(r^{-\frac{5}{2}-\delta}\right).

It follows

(2.32)

\displaystyle|D_{X}(fR_{ijkl})|\leq O(r^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}).

Let

\displaystyle S_{ijkl}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(n-2)}\left(g_{ik}-\partial_{i}f\partial_{k}f\right)\left(g_{jl}-\partial_{j}f\partial_{l}f\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2(n-2)}\left(g_{il}-\partial_{i}f\partial_{l}f\right)\left(g_{jk}-\partial_{j}f\partial_{k}f\right).

It is easy to see

|D_{X}S_{ijkl}|\leq C|\operatorname{Ric}(X,\cdot)|.

Note that by (
2.2
) and (
2.7
) it holds

\operatorname{Ric}(X,\cdot)\leq O(r^{-\frac{3}{2}-8\delta}).

Then

(2.33)

\displaystyle|D_{X}S_{ijkl}|\leq O(r^{-\frac{3}{2}-8\delta}).

Hence, combining (
2.32
) and (
2.33
), we obtain

(2.34)

\displaystyle|D_{X}(fR_{ijkl}-S_{ijkl})|\leq O(r^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}).

Since

at infinity, by the integration along the integral curves of
X

2.34

|fR_{ijkl}-S_{ijkl}|\leq O(r^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}),

which implies (
2.7

By the asymptotically cylindrical property,

Lemma 4.1

It suffices to show that

(4.5)

\displaystyle f^{l}h\leq\max\{\rho^{l}\sup_{\{f=\rho\}}|h|,\rho_{0}^{l}\sup_{\{f=\rho_{0}\}}|h|\}g.

Then
-h
will be also true since

\displaystyle\theta\langle X,\nabla(f^{-l})\rangle-(D_{X}h)(e_{1},e_{1})=0.

\displaystyle 0

where the function
P(\cdot,t)=\Phi^{*}_{t}P(\cdot)
.
We notice that by (
0.1
) there is a
\delta_{2}>0
such that

(5.21)

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{t}(p,D)}[2P(\cdot,t)-R(\cdot,t)]=\sup_{B_{0}(p,D)}[2P(\cdot)-R(\cdot)]\leq-\delta_{2}.

Let

G_{D}(t)=\int_{B_{0}(p,D)}\int_{M}G(x,t;y,0)dv_{g(t)}xdv_{g(0)}y.

Then by (
5
), we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}G_{D}(t)

(5.22)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{0}(p,D)}\int_{M}[2P(x,t)-R(x,t)]G(x,t;y,0)dv_{g(t)}xdv_{g(0)}y<0.

More precisely, by
(\ref{uD-L1-bound})
and
(\ref{2P-R})

we obtain

\partial_{t}G_{D}(t)\leq-\delta_{1}\delta_{2},~t\in[t_{i},t_{i}+\delta_{1}].

Since
G
is everywhere positive and so as
G_{D}(t)>0
, we derive

\displaystyle-G_{D}(0)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}G_{D}(t)-G_{D}(0)

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i}+\delta_{1}}\delta_{1}\delta_{2}dt=-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\delta_{1}^{2}\delta_{2}.

The above relation implies that
G_{D}(0)=\infty
, which is impossible!. Therefore (
5.15
) is true and the lemma is proved.

∎

Inspired by a pinching estimate of Anderson-Chow for the solution of (
0.8
) associated to the
3
-dimensional Ricci flow
[
1
]
, we have the following pinching estimate for the solution of (
0.8
) in higher dimensions.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))

(t\in(0,T])
be an
n\geq 3
-dimension Ricci flow. Let
H
be a positive solution of
(\ref{H-equation})
associated to the flow
g(t)
with the function
P(\cdot,t)
given by

(5.23)

\displaystyle P(\cdot,t)=\sup_{h}\frac{{\rm Rm}_{g(t)}(\cdot)(h,h)}{|h|^{2}}=\sup_{h}\frac{{\rm R}_{t,ijkl}(\cdot)h^{ik}h^{jl}}{h_{ik}h^{ik}},~\forall~t\in~(0,T].

Then any solution
h
of
(\ref{para-L})

satisfies the following pinching estimate,

(5.24)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\frac{|h|^{2}}{H^{2}})\leq\Delta(\frac{|h|^{2}}{H^{2}})+\frac{2}{H}\nabla H\cdot\nabla(\frac{|h|^{2}}{H^{2}}),

where
\Delta=\Delta_{g(t)}
and
|\cdot|=|\cdot|_{g(t)}
.

Proof.

By a direct computation with help of (
0.8
) and
(\ref{H-equation})
, we have the following identities

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\frac{|h|^{2}}{H^{2}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{H^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{ij}h^{i}_{k}h^{kj}+\frac{2}{H^{2}}(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h)\cdot h-2\frac{|h|^{2}}{H^{3}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H

\displaystyle=\frac{4\operatorname{Ric}(h,h)}{H^{2}}+\frac{2}{H^{2}}\left(\Delta h_{ij}+2R_{ikjl}h^{kl}-R_{ik}h^{k}_{j}-R_{jk}h^{k}_{i}\right)h^{ij}-2\frac{|h|^{2}}{H^{3}}(\Delta H+2PH),

and

\displaystyle\Delta(\frac{|h|^{2}}{H^{2}})

\displaystyle=2\frac{\Delta h\cdot h}{H^{2}}+2\frac{|\nabla h|^{2}}{H^{2}}-8\frac{h}{H^{3}}\langle\nabla h,\nabla H\rangle-2\frac{|h|^{2}\Delta H}{H^{3}}+6\frac{|h|^{2}|\nabla H|^{2}}{H^{4}}.

Note

\frac{1}{H}\nabla H\cdot\nabla(\frac{|h|^{2}}{H^{2}})=2\frac{h}{H^{3}}\langle\nabla h,\nabla H\rangle-2\frac{|h|^{2}|\nabla H|^{2}}{H^{4}}.

Then combining the above three relations, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\frac{|h|^{2}}{H^{2}})

\displaystyle=\Delta(\frac{|h|^{2}}{H^{2}})+\frac{2}{H}\nabla H\cdot\nabla(\frac{|h|^{2}}{H^{2}})+\frac{4}{H^{2}}(R_{ijkl}h^{ik}h^{jl}-P|h|^{2})

\displaystyle-2\frac{|H\nabla_{i}h_{jk}-(\nabla_{i}H)h_{jk}|^{2}}{H^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta(\frac{|h|^{2}}{H^{2}})+\frac{2}{H}\nabla H\cdot\nabla(\frac{|h|^{2}}{H^{2}}).

The last inequality follows from the condition (
5.23
).

∎

By the above three lemmas, we are able to get an asymptotic behavior estimate for the solution of (

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
(M^{n},~g(t))
be the soliton Ricci flow of
g
with dimension

in Lemma
5.1
. Let

(t\in[0,\infty))

\displaystyle|h|(x,0)\leq O(\rho^{-l}),

for some

.
Then

(5.27)

\displaystyle\lim_{t}\sup_{B_{t}(p,D)}|h|(\cdot,t)=0.

Proof.

Let

be the heat kernel in (

Then

.
Thus by Lemma

By the maximum principle, we see that

\displaystyle|h|(x,t)\leq\int_{M}G(x,t;y,0)|h|(y,0)d_{g(0)}y.

On the other hand, by (

A Carleman estimate and an energy method for a first-order symmetric hyperbolic system


Abstract
For a symmetric hyperbolic system of the first order, we prove a Carleman estimate under some positivity condition concerning the coefficient matrices. Next, applying the Carleman estimate, we prove an observability L 2 L^{2} -estimate for initial values by boundary data. Key words. Carleman estimate, symmetric hyperbolic system, energy estimate AMS subject classifications. 35R30, 35L40

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary

\partial\Omega
, and let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and

Q:=\Omega\times(0,T).

For any
k\in\{0,1,2,...,d\}
, let

H^{k}=H^{k}(x,t)
be
N\times N
matrices whose elements belong to

C^{1}(\overline{Q})
, and
P
be an
N\times N
matrix with elements in

L^{\infty}(Q)
.
We write
x=(x_{1},...,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

\partial_{0}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\quad\partial_{k}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}},\quad k=1,...,d.

Here and henceforth
\cdot^{T}
means the transpose of vectors and
matrices under consideration.

Throughout this article, we
assume that
H^{k}(x,t)
,
k=0,1,...,d

that is,

(H^{k})^{T}=H^{k},\quad k=0,1,...,d.

Let an

be in
L^{2}(Q)
.

We consider a symmetric hyperbolic system of the first order:

Lu(x,t):=H^{0}(x,t)\partial_{t}u+\sum_{k=1}^{d}H^{k}(x,t)\partial_{k}u+P(x,t)u=F(x,t),\quad x\in\Omega,\,0<t<T,

[formula]

where
u=(u_{1},...,u_{N})^{T}
.

Whenever there is no fear of confusion, we understand
that
(U\cdot V)
denotes a scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
or

for

and
V=(V_{1},...,V_{N})^{T}

(U\cdot V):=\sum_{j=1}^{N}U_{j}V_{j}\quad\mbox{or}\quad(U\cdot V):=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j=1}^{N}U_{j}(x)V_{j}(x)dx.

By
\|\cdot\|_{X}
we denote the norm in a normed space

for specifying

\|\cdot\|
mean

\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
if we do not
specify extra, and
|U|_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}:=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}U_{j}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

The system (1.1) can describe several important equations in mathematical
physics such as Maxwell’s equations and the elasticity equations.

\partial\Omega_{+}(t):=\left\{x\in\partial\Omega;\,\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}H^{k}(x,t)\nu_{k}\right)v\,\cdot\,v\right)>0\quad\mbox{for all $v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}\right\}

[formula]

\partial\Omega_{-}(t):=\left\{x\in\partial\Omega;\,\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}H^{k}(x,t)\nu_{k}\right)v\,\cdot\,v\right)\leq 0\quad\mbox{for all $v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}\right\}.

[formula]

For

We remark that

Let
\varphi=\varphi(x,t)\in C^{1}(\overline{Q})
.
Now we can state our first main result.

Theorem 1 (Carleman estimate for a symmetric hyperbolic system of
the first order)
.

We assume that there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that

\left(\left(\sum_{k=0}^{d}(\partial_{k}\varphi)H^{k}(x,t)\right)v\,\cdot\,v\right)\geq\delta|v|_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{2}\quad\mbox{for all $(x,t)\in Q$ and $v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.

[formula]

Then there exist constants
C>0
and
s_{0}>0
such that

s\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,0)u(x,0)\,\cdot\,u(x,0))e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx+s^{2}\int_{Q}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt

[formula]

+s\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega_{-}(t)}\left|\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}H^{k}(x,t)\nu_{k}\right)u\,\cdot\,u\right)\right|e^{2s\varphi}dSdt

\leq C\int_{Q}|F|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt+Cs\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega_{-}(t)}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dSdt

+s\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,T)u(x,T)\,\cdot\,u(x,T))e^{2s\varphi(x,T)}dx

for all
s\geq s_{0}
and
u\in H^{1}(Q)
satisfying (1.1) in
Q
.

Next we apply Theorem 1 to the observability inequality of

First in Theorem 1, we choose the weight function
\varphi(x,t)
by

\varphi(x,t)=\eta(x)-\beta t,

where
\beta>0
and
\eta\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
are chosen later.
We assume that there exist constants
\delta_{0}>0
,

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}(\partial_{k}\eta)(x)H^{k}(x,t)v\,\cdot\,v\right)\geq\delta_{0}|v|_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{2}\quad\mbox{for all $(x,t)\in Q$ and $v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.

[formula]

and

\delta_{1}|v|_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{2}\leq(H^{0}(x,t)v\,\cdot\,v)\leq M|v|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\quad\mbox{for all $(x,t)\in Q$ and $v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.

[formula]

Theorem 2 (Observability inequality).

We further assume (1.6), (1.7) and

T>\frac{M}{\delta_{0}}\left(\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\eta(x)-\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\eta(x)\right).

[formula]

Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|u(\cdot,0)\|\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))}

for all
u\in H^{1}(Q)
satisfying

H^{0}(x,t)\partial_{t}u+\sum_{k=1}^{d}H^{k}(x,t)\partial_{k}u+Pu=0
in

.

2
Proof of Theorem 1

The proof is based on integration by parts, which is applicable also for
direct proofs of Carleman estimates for
parabolic and hyperbolic equations. The main steps are described as
follows.

Step I.

We transform system (1.1) in
u
to a system in terms of

w:=ue^{s\varphi}
.

Step II.

Calculating the squared
L^{2}
-norms of the transformed system,
we make a lower estimate of the cross terms composed of products
of
w
and
\partial_{k}w
for
k=0,1,...,d
.

Step III.

The lower estimate in Step II cannot dierctly produce
the Carleman estimate, which is the same for parabolic and hypebolic
equations. Therefore, we take scalar products of the transformed system by

B(x,t)w
with suitably chosen matrix function
B(x,t)
and we add
the resulting equality to the lower estimate obtained in Step II, which
leads us to the conclusion.

Before starting the proof, we remark that it is sufficient to prove
Theorem 1 for
P\equiv 0
. Indeed let Theorem 1 be proved in the case of

P\equiv 0
.
Then

\displaystyle s\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,0)u(x,0)\,\cdot\,u(x,0))e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx+s^{2}\int_{Q}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle s\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega_{-}(t)}\left|\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}H^{k}(x,t)\nu_{k}\right)u\,\cdot\,u\right)\right|e^{2s\varphi}dSdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}|F-Pu|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega_{-}(t)}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dSdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle s\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,T)u(x,T)\,\cdot\,u(x,T))e^{2s\varphi(x,T)}dx.

Since

|F-Pu|^{2}\leq 2|F|^{2}+2|Pu|^{2}\leq 2|F|^{2}+2\|P\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}^{2}|u|^{2}

in
Q
, we can estimate
\int_{Q}|F-Pu|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt
by

C_{0}\int_{Q}|F|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt+C_{0}\int_{Q}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt

with some constant
C_{0}>0
. Choosing
s>0
large, we can absorb the term

C_{0}\int_{Q}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt
into

s^{2}\int_{Q}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt
on the left-hand side.
Therefore, (1.5) with
P\in L^{\infty}(Q)
is derived from (1.5) in the case
of
P\equiv 0
.

Moreover we show

(R(x,t)\partial_{k}w\,\cdot\,w)_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{k}(Rw\,\cdot\,w)_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{k}Rw\cdot w)_{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\quad k=0,1,...,d\quad\mbox{in $Q$},

[formula]

where
R\in C^{1}(\overline{Q})
is an
N\times N
symmetric matrix and

w=(w_{1},...,w_{N})^{T}\in H^{1}(Q)
.

Indeed, by the symmetry of
R
, we have

(R\partial_{k}w\cdot w)_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}=(\partial_{k}w\cdot Rw)_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
, and so

\displaystyle\partial_{k}(Rw\cdot w)_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}=(R\partial_{k}w\cdot w)_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}+(Rw\cdot\partial_{k}w)_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}+((\partial_{k}R)w\cdot w)_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(R\partial_{k}w\cdot w)_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}+((\partial_{k}R)w\cdot w)_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\quad\mbox{in $Q$},

which verifies (2.1).

Step I.

Not only for functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, but also
for
u=(u_{1},...,u_{N})^{T},\,v=(v_{1},...,v_{N})^{T}\in L^{2}(Q)
, we use the
notation

(u\cdot v):=\int_{Q}\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}(x,t)v_{j}(x,t)dxdt.

We set

Lu:=H^{0}(x,t)\partial_{t}u+\sum_{k=1}^{d}H^{k}(x,t)\partial_{k}u=F\quad\mbox{in $Q$}.

[formula]

We set

w:=e^{s\varphi}u,\quad Pw:=e^{s\varphi}L(e^{-s\varphi}w).

Then

Pw=H^{0}\partial_{t}w+\sum_{k=1}^{d}H^{k}\partial_{k}w-s\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}(\partial_{k}\varphi)H^{k}+(\partial_{t}\varphi)H^{0}\right)w

in
Q
. For short description, we set

A:=\sum_{k=0}^{d}(\partial_{k}\varphi)H^{k}\quad\mbox{in $Q$},

where
\partial_{0}\varphi=\partial_{t}\varphi
. Then we can write (1.1) in terms of
the transformed system in terms of
w
:

Pw=H^{0}\partial_{t}w+\sum_{j=1}^{n}H^{k}\partial_{k}w-sAw=Fe^{s\varphi}\quad\mbox{in $Q$}.

[formula]

Step II.

By the definition, we have

\|Pw\|^{2}=\int_{Q}|Lw|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt=\int_{Q}|F|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt.

Therefore, we have to make a lower estimate of
\|Pw\|^{2}
and
extract the terms on the left-hand side of (1.5).
Direct calculations yield

\displaystyle\|Pw\|^{2}=2\left(\left(H^{0}\partial_{t}w+\sum_{k=1}^{d}H^{k}\partial_{k}w\right)\,\cdot\,-sAw\right)+s^{2}\|Aw\|^{2}+\left\|H^{0}\partial_{t}w+\sum_{k=1}^{d}H^{k}\partial_{k}w\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-2s\int_{Q}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{d}H^{k}\partial_{k}w\cdot Aw\right)dxdt+s^{2}\|Aw\|^{2}.

Hence,

-2s\int_{Q}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{d}H^{k}\partial_{k}w\cdot Aw\right)dxdt+s^{2}\|Aw\|^{2}\leq\|Fe^{s\varphi}\|^{2}.

[formula]

The last term
s^{2}\|Aw\|^{2}
can estimate
s^{2}\|w\|^{2}

suitably, but we cannot directly estimate the cross term

-2s\int_{Q}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{d}H^{k}\partial_{k}w\cdot Aw\right)dxdt.

The difficulty comes from the non-symmetry in the form

\left(\sum_{k=0}^{d}H^{k}\partial_{k}w\cdot Aw\right)
, so that we cannot
treat the derivatives
\partial_{k}w
.
We remark that
H^{k}
and
A
are symmetric but

AH^{k}
and
H^{k}A
are not necessarily symmetric.
Thus we need some argument in Step III.

Step III.

For compensating for the above cross term, we take the scalar product
of (2.3) with
B(x,t)w
, where we choose
B
later:

\left(\sum_{k=0}^{d}H^{k}\partial_{k}w\,\cdot\,Bw\right)-s(Aw\cdot Bw)=(Fe^{s\varphi}\cdot Bw).

For adjusting the power of
s
, we multiply by
2s
:

2s\left(\sum_{k=0}^{d}H^{k}\partial_{k}w\,\cdot\,Bw\right)-2s^{2}(Aw\cdot Bw)=2s(Fe^{s\varphi}\cdot Bw).

[formula]

Adding (2.4) and (2.5), we obtain

\displaystyle 2s\left(\sum_{k=0}^{d}H^{k}\partial_{k}w\,\cdot\,(B-A)w\right)+s^{2}(\|Aw\|^{2}-2(Aw\cdot Bw))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|Fe^{s\varphi}\|^{2}+2s(Fe^{s\varphi}\cdot Bw).

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the right-hand side and
using

2s(Fe^{s\varphi}\cdot Bw)=2\left(\frac{Fe^{s\varphi}}{\varepsilon}\,\cdot\,s\varepsilon Bw\right)\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\|Fe^{s\varphi}\|^{2}+s^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\|Bw\|^{2},

we obtain

2s\left(\sum_{k=0}^{d}H^{k}\partial_{k}w\,\cdot\,(B-A)w\right)+s^{2}(\|Aw\|^{2}-2(Aw\cdot Bw))

\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\|Fe^{s\varphi}\|^{2}+s^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\|Bw\|^{2}.

[formula]

In order to apply (2.1) to the first term on the left-hand side of (2.6)
for estimating the cross term, we choose
B
suitably, for example, such that

B-A=-E_{N},

where
E_{N}
is the
N\times N
identity matrix.
Then

-2s\left(\sum_{k=0}^{d}H^{k}\partial_{k}w\,\cdot\,w\right)+s^{2}(2(Aw\,\cdot\,w)-\|Aw\|^{2})

\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\|Fe^{s\varphi}\|^{2}+s^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\|(A-E_{N})w\|^{2}.

[formula]

With a parameter
\mu>0
, we set

\widetilde{\varphi}:=\mu\varphi,\quad\widetilde{A}(x,t):=\sum_{k=0}^{d}(\partial_{k}\widetilde{\varphi})H^{k}=\mu A\quad\mbox{in $Q$}.

Henceforth
C>0
denotes generic constants which are independent of

s>0
and
\mu>0
.
Setting
\widetilde{w}:=ue^{s\widetilde{\varphi}}
, we repeat the calculations to
obtain

-2s\left(\sum_{k=0}^{d}H^{k}\partial_{k}\widetilde{w}\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w}\right)+s^{2}(2(\widetilde{A}\widetilde{w}\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w})-\|\widetilde{A}\widetilde{w}\|^{2})

\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\|Fe^{s\widetilde{\varphi}}\|^{2}+s^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\|(\widetilde{A}-E_{N})\widetilde{w}\|^{2}.

[formula]

We can directly verify

\|\widetilde{A}\widetilde{w}\|^{2}=\mu^{2}\|A\widetilde{w}\|^{2}\leq C\mu^{2}\|\widetilde{w}\|^{2}

and

\|(\widetilde{A}-E_{N})\widetilde{w}\|^{2}\leq 2\|\widetilde{A}\widetilde{w}\|^{2}+2\|E_{N}\widetilde{w}\|^{2}\leq 2(C\mu^{2}+1)\|\widetilde{w}\|^{2}.

Moreover, by (1.4), we see
(\widetilde{A}\widetilde{w}\cdot\widetilde{w})\geq\mu\delta\|\widetilde{w}\|^{2}
.
Hence, (2.8) yields

-2s\sum_{k=0}^{d}(H^{k}\partial_{k}\widetilde{w}\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w})+s^{2}(2\delta\mu-C\mu^{2})\|\widetilde{w}\|^{2}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\|Fe^{s\widetilde{\varphi}}\|^{2}+2s^{2}\varepsilon^{2}(C\mu^{2}+1)\|\widetilde{w}\|^{2}.

[formula]

Since
H^{k}
,
k=0,1,...,d
are symmetric, by (2.1) we have

-\sum_{k=1}^{d}(H^{k}\partial_{k}\widetilde{w}\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w})=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{Q}\partial_{k}(H^{k}\widetilde{w}\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w})dxdt+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{Q}((\partial_{k}H^{k})\widetilde{w}\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w})dxdt

[formula]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)}\sum_{k=1}^{d}((H^{k}\nu_{k})\widetilde{w}\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w})dSdt+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{Q}((\partial_{k}H^{k})\widetilde{w}\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w})dxdt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega_{-}(t)}\sum_{k=1}^{d}|H^{k}\nu_{k}||\widetilde{w}|^{2}dSdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega_{-}(t)}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{d}((H^{k}\partial_{k})\widetilde{w}\cdot\widetilde{w})\right|dSdt-C\|\widetilde{w}\|^{2}.

Moreover,

-(H^{0}\partial_{t}\widetilde{w}\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w})=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q}\partial_{t}(H^{0}\widetilde{w}\cdot\widetilde{w})dxdt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q}((\partial_{t}H^{0})\widetilde{w}\cdot\widetilde{w})dxdt

[formula]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,0)\widetilde{w}(x,0)\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w}(x,0))dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,T)\widetilde{w}(x,T)\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w}(x,T))dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q}((\partial_{t}H^{0})\widetilde{w}\cdot\widetilde{w})dxdt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,0)\widetilde{w}(x,0)\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w}(x,0))dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,T)\widetilde{w}(x,T)\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w}(x,T))dx-C\|\widetilde{w}\|^{2}.

Applying (2.10) and (2.11) in (2.9), we obtain

\displaystyle s^{2}[(2\delta\mu-C\mu^{2})-2\varepsilon^{2}(C\mu^{2}+1)]\|\widetilde{w}\|^{2}-Cs\|\widetilde{w}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle s\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega_{-}(t)}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{d}((H^{k}\nu_{k})\widetilde{w}\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w})\right|dSdt+s\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,0)\widetilde{w}(x,0)\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w}(x,0))dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\|Fe^{s\widetilde{\varphi}}\|^{2}+Cs\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega_{-}(t)}|\widetilde{w}|^{2}dSdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle s\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,T)\widetilde{w}(x,T)\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w}(x,T))dx.

We choose
\mu>0
small so that
2\delta\mu-C\mu^{2}>0
.
For this
\mu>0
, we choose
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small such that

(2\delta\mu-C\mu^{2})-2\varepsilon^{2}(C\mu^{2}+1)>0.

Then, for
\mu>0
and
\varepsilon>0
, we can find a constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(\mu,\varepsilon)>0

such that

\displaystyle C_{0}s^{2}\|\widetilde{w}\|^{2}-Cs\|\widetilde{w}\|^{2}+Cs\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega_{-}(t)}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{d}((H^{k}\nu_{k})\widetilde{w}\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w})\right|dSdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle s\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,0)\widetilde{w}(x,0)\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w}(x,0))dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\|Fe^{s\widetilde{\varphi}}\|^{2}+Cs\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega_{-}(t)}|\widetilde{w}|^{2}dSdt+s\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,T)\widetilde{w}(x,T)\,\cdot\,\widetilde{w}(x,T))dx.

Setting
\widetilde{s}_{0}:=\frac{2C}{C_{0}(\mu,\varepsilon)}
, we obtain

C_{0}s^{2}\|\widetilde{w}\|^{2}-Cs\|\widetilde{w}\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{0}s^{2}\|\widetilde{w}\|^{2}

for all
s>\widetilde{s}_{0}
. Rewriting in terms of
\widetilde{w}=e^{s\widetilde{\varphi}}u=e^{s\mu\varphi}u
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}C_{0}s^{2}\int_{Q}|u|^{2}e^{2s\mu\varphi}dxdt+s\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega_{-}(t)}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{d}((H^{k}\nu_{k})u\,\cdot\,u)\right|e^{2s\mu\varphi}dSdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle s\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,0)u(x,0)\,\cdot\,u(x,0))e^{2s\mu\varphi(x,0)}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\|F\|^{2}e^{2s\mu\varphi}dxdt+Cs\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega_{-}(t)}|u|^{2}e^{2s\mu\varphi}dSdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle s\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,T)u(x,T)\,\cdot\,u(x,T))e^{2s\mu\varphi(x,T)}dx

for all
s>\widetilde{s}_{0}
. Therefore, rewriting
\mu\varphi
by
\varphi
in the above
inequality, we complete the proof of Theorem 1.

\blacksquare

Proof of Theorem 2

It is known that a relevant Carleman estimate produces an
L^{2}
-estimate for
initial value (e.g., Kazemi and Klibanov
[
17
]
, Klibanov and Timonov

[
21
]
). Here we apply a method in Huang, Imanuvilov and Yamamoto

[
12
]
which simplifies the argument based on Carleman estimate
in
[
17
]
,
[
21
]
.

First Step.

Let
H^{k}\in C^{1}(\overline{Q})
,
k=0,1,...,d
be symmetric matrices.
We prove

Lemma 1 (energy estimate for an initial value problem)
.

We assume (1.7). Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|u(\cdot,t)\|^{2}+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega_{+}(\xi)}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}(H^{k}\nu_{k})u\,\cdot\,u\right)dSd\xi

\leq C\left(\|u(\cdot,0)\|^{2}+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega_{+}(\xi)}|u|^{2}dSd\xi\right)

[formula]

for all
u\in H^{1}(Q)
satisfying

H^{0}\partial_{t}u+\sum_{k=1}^{d}H^{k}\partial_{k}u+Pu=0\quad\mbox{in $Q$}.

[formula]

Proof.

The proof is by the following standard way. We take the scalar product
(3.2) with
u
and integrate in
x\in\Omega
to have

\displaystyle(H^{0}(\cdot,\xi)\partial_{\xi}u(\cdot,\xi)\,\cdot u(\cdot,\xi))+\sum_{k=1}^{d}(H^{k}(\cdot,\xi)\partial_{k}u(\cdot,\xi)\,\cdot u(\cdot,\xi))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(P(\cdot,\xi)u(\cdot,\xi)\,\cdot u(\cdot,\xi))=0,\quad 0\leq\xi\leq t.

In the same way as (2.10) and (2.11), we see

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\xi}\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,\xi)u(x,\xi)\,\cdot u(x,\xi))dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\nu_{k}H^{k}(x,\xi)\,\cdot u(x,\xi)\right)dS

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}((\partial_{t}H^{0}(x,\xi))u(x,\xi)\,\cdot u(x,\xi))dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\partial_{k}H^{0}(x,\xi)\right)u(x,\xi)\,\cdot u(x,\xi)\right)dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(P(x,\xi)u(x,\xi)\,\cdot u(x,\xi))dx=0,\quad 0\leq\xi\leq t.

We integrate in
\xi\in(0,t)
to have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,t)u(x,t)\,\cdot u(x,\xi))dx+\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega_{+}(\xi)}\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\nu_{k}H^{k}\right)u(x,\xi)\,\cdot u(x,\xi)\right)dSd\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(H^{0}(x,0)u(x,0)\,\cdot u(x,0))dx-\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega_{+}(\xi)}\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\nu_{k}H^{k}\right)u(x,\xi)\,\cdot u(x,\xi)\right)dSd\xi

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}H^{0}(x,\xi)u(x,\xi)\,\cdot\,u(x,\xi))dxd\xi

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k=1}^{d}((\partial_{k}H^{k})u(x,\xi)\,\cdot u(x,\xi))dxd\xi-2\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\Omega}(Pu(x,\xi)\,\cdot u(x,\xi))dxd\xi,\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

We set
E(t)=\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)|^{2}dx
.
By (1.7),
H^{k}\in C^{1}(\overline{Q})
, and
P\in L^{\infty}(Q)
, we obtain

E(t)+\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega_{+}(\xi)}\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\nu_{k}H^{k}\right)u(x,\xi)\,\cdot u(x,\xi)\right)dSd\xi

[formula]

\leq CE(0)+C\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega_{+}(\xi)}|u(x,\xi)|^{2}dSd\xi+C\int^{t}_{0}E(\xi)d\xi,\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

In particular,

E(t)\leq CE(0)+C\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega_{+}(\xi)}|u(x,\xi)|^{2}dSd\xi+C\int^{t}_{0}E(\xi)d\xi,\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

The Gronwall inequality yields

E(t)\leq CE(0)+C\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega_{+}(\xi)}|u(x,\xi)|^{2}dSd\xi,\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

Substituting this into (3.3), we reach

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega_{+}(\xi)}\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\nu_{k}H^{k}\right)u(x,\xi)\,\cdot u(x,\xi)\right)dSd\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CE(0)+C\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega_{+}(\xi)}|u|^{2}dSd\xi,\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

The proof of Lemma 1 is complete.

Second Step.

We complete the proof of Theorem 2. By (1.8) we can choose
\beta\in\left(0,\,\frac{\delta_{0}}{M}\right)
which is close to

\frac{\delta_{0}}{M}
such that

T>\frac{1}{\beta}(\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\eta(x)-\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\eta(x)),

that is,

\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi(x,0)-\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi(x,T)>\delta_{2}>0,

[formula]

where
\delta_{2}>0
is some constant.

Moreover, by
\beta<\frac{\delta_{0}}{M}
, (1.6) and (1.7), we see that

\sum_{k=0}^{d}((\partial_{k}\varphi)H^{k}v\cdot v)=-\beta(H^{0}v\cdot v)+\sum_{k=1}^{d}((\partial_{k}\eta)H^{k}v\cdot v)\geq(-\beta M+\delta_{0})|v|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}},

which verifies (1.4) with
\delta:=\delta_{0}-\beta M
.
Therefore, Theorem 1 is applicable with the weight function

\varphi(x,t):=\eta(x)-\beta t
.

With this
\delta_{2}
, we apply Theorem 1. In terms of (1.7), the first term
on the left-hand side of (1.5) is replaced by

s\delta_{1}\int_{\Omega}|u(x,0)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx\geq s\delta_{1}e^{2s\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi(x,0)}\|u(\cdot,0)\|^{2}.

We neglect the second and the third terms on the left-hand side of (1.5)
and further replace the second term on the right-hand side by

Cse^{C_{1}s}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))}
where

C_{1}:=\max_{(x,t)\in\overline{Q}}\varphi(x,t)
.

Thus

se^{2s\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi(x,0)}\|u(\cdot,0)\|^{2}\leq Ce^{C_{1}s}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))}+Cse^{2s\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi(x,T)}\|u(\cdot,T)\|^{2}

for all
s\geq s_{0}
.
Applying (3.4), we obtain

\|u(\cdot,0)\|^{2}\leq Ce^{C_{1}s}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))}+Ce^{-2s\delta_{2}}\|u(\cdot,T)\|^{2}

[formula]

for all
s\geq s_{0}
.

Lemma 1 implies

\|u(\cdot,T)\|^{2}\leq C(\|u(\cdot,0)\|^{2}+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))}).

Substituting this into (3.5), we reach

\|u(\cdot,0)\|^{2}\leq Ce^{Cs}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))}+Ce^{-2s\delta_{2}}\|u(\cdot,0)\|^{2}+Ce^{-2s\delta_{2}}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))}

for all
s\geq s_{0}
. Choosing
s>0
sufficicently large, we can
absorb the second term on the right-hand side into the
left-side hand, and so we complete the proof of Theorem 2.

\blacksquare

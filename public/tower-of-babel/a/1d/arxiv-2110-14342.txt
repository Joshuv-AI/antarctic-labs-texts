On radiuses of convergence of q q -metallic numbers and related q q -rational numbers
Xin Ren

Abstract
The q q -rational numbers and the q q -irrational numbers are introduced by S. Morier-Genoud and V. Ovsienko. In this paper, we focus on q q -real quadratic irrational numbers, especially q q -metallic numbers and q q -rational sequences which converge to q q -metallic numbers, and consider the radiuses of convergence of them when we assume that q q is a complex number. We construct two sequences given by recurrence formula as a generalization of the q q -deformation of Fibonacci numbers and Pell numbers which are introduced by S. Morier-Genoud and V. Ovsienko. We give an estimation of radiuses of convergence of them, and we solve on conjecture of the lower bound expected which is introduced by L. Leclere, S. Morier-Genoud, V. Ovsienko, and A. Veselov for the metallic numbers and its convergence rational sequence. In addition, we obtain a relationship between the radius of convergence of the [ n , n , … , n , … ] q [n,n,\ldots,n,\dots]_{q} and [ n , n , … , n ] q [n,n,\ldots,n]_{q} in the case of n = 3 n=3 and n = 4 n=4 .

Introduction

It is well-known that the Euler
q
-integer is a polynomial with integer coefficients of
q
, which is a kind of quantization of integers. S. Morier-Genoud and V. Ovsienko expand integers to rational numbers and real numbers, and define
q
-rationals
[
9
]
and
q
-irrationals
[
10
]
based on some combinatorial properties of rational numbers and number-theoretic properties of irrational numbers. The
q
-rationals and the
q
-irrationals are related to mathematical physics, combinatorics, number theory, quantum algebra, and knot theory, such as calculation recipes of the Jones polynomial and Alexander polynomial of rational knots (see
[
5
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
11
]
), quantum Teichmüller spaces
[
1
]
, and the Markov-Hurwitz approximation theory (see
[
7
]
and
[
2
]
).

We know that a positive rational number
\displaystyle\frac{r}{s}
has regular and negative continued fraction expansions as
\displaystyle\frac{r}{s}=[a_{1},\ldots,a_{2m}]=[[c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}]]
. S. Morier-Genoud and V. Ovsienko
[
9
]
defined their
q
-deformations
\displaystyle[a_{1},\ldots,a_{2m}]_{q}
and
\displaystyle[[c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}]]_{q}
, respectively. These
q
-deformations are equal, and thus one can define
\displaystyle\left[\frac{r}{s}\right]_{q}:=[a_{1},\ldots,a_{2m}]_{q}=[[c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}]]_{q}
as
q
-rationals. On the other hand, the
q
-rationals can also be considered as a formal power series in
q
. Furthermore, given an arbitrary rational sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
that converges to an irrational number
x>1
, one can consider the
q
-rational sequence
([x_{k}]_{q})_{k\geq 1}
. S. Morier-Genoud and V. Ovsienko proved that the Taylor series of
[x_{k}]_{q}
stabilizes, as
k
grows. This series does not depend on the choice of the sequence
[x_{k}]_{q}
. Hence, for an irrational
x>1
, the
q
-deformation of
x
is defined as the following power series in
q
:

[x]_{q}=\sum^{\infty}_{s=0}\varkappa_{s}q^{s}

where
\displaystyle\varkappa_{s}

(s\in\mathbb{N})
are integers
[
10
]
.

On the study of
q
-deformation of the real quadratic irrational numbers, L. Leclere and S. Morier-Genoud
[
6
]
proved some properties of
q
-deformation of them corresponding to classical real quadratic irrational numbers. On the other hand, since the
q
-real numbers are defined as power series in

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

For any real number

, the radius of convergence of the series
[x]_{q}
is greater than or equal to
\displaystyle R_{(1)}=\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}
.

For convenience, we use the notation
\displaystyle R_{(1)}
instead of
R_{\ast}
in
[
7
]
. It is shown that

is the radius of convergence of the
q
-golden number
\displaystyle\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right]_{q}

,

) and

Theorem 1.2

For the metallic number

Theorem 1.3

If

is greater than
or equal to
\displaystyle R_{(1)}
.

It is worth noting that the ideas for the proof of Theorems

-metallic number to consider the zeros of its discriminant, then the palindrome of the discriminant of the

In addition, for some relationship between the radius of convergence of the

-metallic number

For

At the beginning, we briefly describe the definitions of the

2.1
q
-continued fractions and
q
-rational numbers

A positive rational number
\displaystyle\frac{r}{s}>1
with coprime integers
r
and
s\neq 0
has regular and negative continued fraction expansions as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{r}{s}=a_{1}+\frac{1}{a_{2}+\cfrac{1}{\ddots+\cfrac{1}{a_{n}}}}=c_{1}-\frac{1}{c_{2}-\cfrac{1}{\ddots-\cfrac{1}{c_{k}}}}

with
a_{i}\geq 1
and
c_{j}\geq 2
. These expansions are denoted by
[a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}]
and
[[c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{k}]]
, respectively.
Since
[a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}]=[a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}-1,1]
, we always assume that
n=2m
is even. In this way, we have the unique regular and negative continued fraction expansion of
\displaystyle\frac{r}{s}
.

Let
q
be a formal parameter. For a non-negative integer
n
we denote the Euler
q
-integer by

\displaystyle[n]_{q}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{
cl}0&\text{for}\ n=0,\\
\displaystyle\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}=1+q+q^{2}+\cdots+q^{n-1}&\text{for}\ n\geq 1.\\
\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

We note that
\displaystyle q^{n}[n]_{q^{-1}}=\frac{q^{n}(1-q^{-n})}{1-q^{-1}}=q[n]_{q}
.

The
q
-deformations of regular and negative continued fraction expansions are defined by Morier-Genoud and Ovsienko
[
9
]
as follows:

[a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{2m}]_{q}:=[a_{1}]_{q}+\cfrac{q^{a_{1}}}{[a_{2}]_{q^{-1}}+\cfrac{q^{-a_{2}}}{[a_{3}]_{q}+\cfrac{q^{a_{3}}}{[a_{4}]_{q^{-1}}+\cfrac{q^{-a_{4}}}{\cfrac{\ddots}{[a_{2m-1}]_{q}+\cfrac{q^{a_{2m-1}}}{[a_{2m}]_{q^{-1}}}}}}}}

(2.2)

[[c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{k}]]_{q}:=[c_{1}]_{q}-\cfrac{q^{c_{1}-1}}{[c_{2}]_{q}-\cfrac{q^{c_{2}-1}}{[c_{3}]_{q}-\cfrac{q^{c_{3}-1}}{[c_{4}]_{q}-\cfrac{q^{c_{4}-1}}{\cfrac{\ddots}{[c_{k-1}]_{q}-\cfrac{q^{c_{k-1}-1}}{[c_{k}]_{q}}}}}}}.

(2.3)

Theorem 2.1

(Morier-Genoud and Ovsienko
[
9
]
)
.

If a positive rational number
\displaystyle\frac{r}{s}
is given in the form

\displaystyle\frac{r}{s}=[a_{1},\ldots,a_{2m}]=[[c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}]]
, then

[a_{1},\dots,a_{2m}]_{q}=[[c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}]]_{q}.

(2.4)

Hence, the
q
-rational number
\displaystyle\left[\frac{r}{s}\right]_{q}
is defined as

\displaystyle\left[\frac{r}{s}\right]_{q}:=[a_{1},\ldots,a_{2m}]_{q}=[[c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}]]_{q}.

2.2
q
-irrationals

Let
x>1
be an irrational number. Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a rational number sequence that converges to
x
, and consider the sequence
([x_{k}]_{q})_{k\geq 1}
of
q
-deformation. For
k\geq 1
, we express
[x_{k}]_{q}
as the formal power series :

\displaystyle[x_{k}]_{q}=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\varkappa_{k,s}q^{s}.

(2.5)

Then the
q
-deformed irrational number
x
is defined as the following formal power series in
q
:

\displaystyle[x]_{q}=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\varkappa_{s}q^{s}\ \ \ \ (\varkappa_{s}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\varkappa_{k,s})

(2.6)

We can guarantee that the existence of the limit and its independence of the choice of the converging sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
by the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2

(Morier-Genoud and Ovsienko
[
10
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Given an irrational real number
x\geq 1
, for every
k\geq 0
, the coefficients
\varkappa_{k,s}
of (
2.5
) are stabilizing, as
k
grows. Moreover, the limit
\varkappa_{s}
of coefficients(
2.6
) are integers, and independent of the choice of the converging sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
.

By the above proposition, we may always construct
q
-irrational numbers using continued fractions as follows:
for
x=[a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\ldots]>1
, the sequence of rational numbers which converges to
x
can be chosen as

\displaystyle x_{k}=[a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}].

2.3
q
-metallic numbers and its discriminant

Definition 2.3

(
q
-metallic numbers)
.

Let
n
be a natural number. The metallic number
x
can be represented as follows :

\displaystyle x=\displaystyle\frac{n+\sqrt{n^{2}+4}}{2}=[n,n,\ldots,n,\ldots].

We call that the
q
-deformation of
x
(denoted by
[x]_{q}
) the (
n
th)
q
-metallic number. Particularly, when
n=1
, the
[x]_{q}
is called the
q
-golden number (the
1
st
q
-metallic number).

By (
2.2
), we can obtain the next formula.

\displaystyle\left[\frac{n+\sqrt{n^{2}+4}}{2}\right]_{q}=[n]_{q}+\cfrac{q^{n}}{[n]_{q^{-1}}+\cfrac{q^{-n}}{[n]_{q}+\cfrac{q^{n}}{[n]_{q^{-1}}+\cfrac{q^{-n}}{\ddots}}}}

(2.7)

The
q
-metallic number is a solution of the algebraic equation

\displaystyle q[x]_{q}^{2}-((q-1)(q^{n}+1)+q[n]_{q})[x]_{q}-1=0,

(2.8)

which is a
q
-deformation of
x^{2}-nx-1=0
. This can be deduced from (
2.7
).

Definition 2.4
.

We denote by
\mathcal{D}([\overline{n}]_{q})
the discriminant which is satisfied as the quadratic equation (
2.8
) for
[x]_{q}
. More precisely, it is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}([\overline{n}]_{q})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{
cl}1+2q-q^{2}+2q^{3}+{q}^{4}&\text{for}\ n=1,\\
1+2q+3q^{2}+3q^{4}+2q^{5}+{q}^{6}&\text{for}\ n=2,\\
D(q)&\text{for}\ n\geq 3,\\
\end{array}\right.

(2.9)

where

\displaystyle D(q)=1+2q^{2}+\sum^{n-1}_{t=3}(t-1)q^{t}+(n+1)q^{n}+(n-4)q^{n+1}+(n+1)q^{n+2}+\sum^{2n-1}_{t=n+3}(2n-t+1)q^{t}+2q^{2n}+q^{2n+2}.

Then
\mathcal{D}([\overline{n}]_{q})
is said to be the discriminant of the
q
-metallic number.

We note that all of the coefficients of
\mathcal{D}([\overline{n}]_{q})
are positive integers for
n\geq 4
. The discriminant
\mathcal{D}([\overline{n}]_{q})
has the following property. This property is very helpful for us to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 2.5
.

(the palindromic of discriminant)

The discriminant of
q
-metallic numbers
\mathcal{D}([\overline{n}]_{q})
are palindromic.

Proof. It is obvious that
\mathcal{D}([\overline{1}]_{q})
and
\mathcal{D}([\overline{2}]_{q})
are palindromes. For
n\geq 3
, one can check
q^{2n+2}\mathcal{D}([\overline{n}]_{q^{-1}})=\mathcal{D}([\overline{n}]_{q})
by direct calculation.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

For general
q
-real quadratic irrational numbers, the palindromic of discriminant has been proved by considering a
q
-deformation of the elements of the modular group
PSL(2,\mathbb{Z})
(see
[
6
]
).

3
The radius of convergence of
q
-metallic numbers

Since
q
-irrational is defined as power series in
q
, we can consider its radiuses of convergence. In this section, we always assume that
q
is a complex number.

3.1
Some observations

For
q
-metallic numbers

\displaystyle[x]_{q}=\left[\displaystyle\frac{n+\sqrt{n^{2}+4}}{2}\right]_{q}=[n,n,\ldots,n,\ldots]_{q},

with
n\geq 1
,
we denote the radiuses of convergence of
[x]_{q}
by
R_{(n)}
. In the cases of
n=1
and
n=2
the following has already been known.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
7
, Propositions 3.1 and 4.1]
)
.

\displaystyle R_{(1)}=\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}=1-\frac{1}{\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}}\approx 0.38197,\ R_{(2)}=\frac{1+\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{2\sqrt{2}-1}}{2}\approx 0.53101.

In complex analysis, it is well-known that the radius of convergence of a power series
[x]_{q}
centered on a point
0
is equal to the distance from
0
to the nearest point so that
[x]_{q}
cannot be defined in a way that makes it holomorphic. Since

\displaystyle[x]_{q}=\frac{q[n]_{q}+(q^{n}+1)(q-1)+\sqrt{\mathcal{D}([\overline{n}]_{q})}}{2q},

we can calculate
R_{(n)}
by finding the roots of
\mathcal{D}([\overline{n}]_{q})
.

Since the discriminant
\mathcal{D}([\overline{n}]_{q})
is a palindromic polynomial, we can find the roots of it by using the next lemma.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
4
, Corollary 2]
)
.

Let
P(q)\in\mathbb{Q}[q]
be a palindromic polynomial given by

P(q)=a_{0}+a_{1}q+a_{2}q^{2}+\cdots+a_{n-2}q^{n-2}+a_{n-1}q^{n-1}+a_{n}q^{n},

having even degree
n
. If

\displaystyle\max\{\left|a_{k}\right|:k\in{0,1,2,\ldots,\frac{n}{2}-1}\}\geq\left|a_{\frac{n}{2}}\right|,

then
P(q)
has roots on the unit circle.

Since
\mathcal{D}([\overline{n}]_{q})
satisfies Lemma
3.2
. it has roots on the unit circle. Indeed, for any positive integer
n
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}([\overline{n}]_{q})=(1-q+q^{2})P_{n}(q),

where

\displaystyle P_{n}(q):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1+3q+q^{2}&\text{for}\ n=1,\\
1+q+4q^{2}+q^{3}+q^{4}&\text{for}\ n=2,\\
\displaystyle 1+\sum^{n-1}_{t=1}tq^{t}+(n+2)q^{n}+\sum^{2n-1}_{t=n+1}(2n-t)q^{t}+q^{2n}&\text{for}\ t=n\geq 3.\\
\end{array}\right.

Hence, we obtain
R_{(n)}\leq 1
, and
R_{(n)}=\min\{|q|:P_{n}(q)=0\}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

A polynomial
P(q)\in\mathbb{Q}[q]
is palindromic if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(i)

1
is a root of even multiplicity (possibly zero),

(ii)

if
q\neq\pm 1
is a root, then
\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}
is a root with the same multiplicity.

For a proof of Lemma
3.3
, see
[
8
, Theorem 1.1.9]
.

3.2
Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We prove Theorem
1.2
by reductive absurdum. Let
n\geq 3
, and assume that
q=re^{i\theta}
(
0<r\leq R_{(1)}
,
0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi
) is a root of
P_{n}(q)
. By Lemma
3.3
, since
P_{n}(q)
is palindrome, it follows that
q^{-1}=r^{-1}e^{-i\theta}
is also a root of
P_{n}(q)
. Hence,
P_{n}(q)
has a root can be expressed by
q=rz
(
\displaystyle r\geq\frac{1}{R_{(1)}}>\frac{5}{2}
,
|z|=1
). Then,
z
is a zero of the polynomial

\displaystyle\sum^{2n}_{t=0}\mu_{t}z^{t},\ \ \displaystyle\mu_{t}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{
cl}1&\text{for}\ t=0,\\
tr^{t}&\text{for}\ 0<t<n,\\
(n+2)r^{n}&\text{for}\ t=n,\\
(2n-t)r^{t}&\text{for}\ n<t<2n,\\
r^{2n}&\text{for}\ t=2n.\\
\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

Lemma 3.4
.

For
n\geq 3
, the sequence
\{\mu_{t}\}^{2n}_{t=0}
with
\mu_{t}>0
for
t=0,1,2,\ldots,2n
, is strictly increasing and
\displaystyle\mu_{2n-1}<\frac{2}{5}\mu_{2n}
.

Proof. Since
\displaystyle r>\frac{5}{2}
, then
\displaystyle\mu_{2n-1}=r^{2n-1}<\frac{2}{5}r^{2n}=\frac{2}{5}\mu_{2n}
. ∎

We are now turning to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Let us consider the polynomial

\displaystyle F(z)=(1-z)\sum^{2n}_{t=0}\mu_{t}z^{t}=-\mu_{2n}z^{2n+1}+(\mu_{2n}-\mu_{2n-1})z^{2n}+\cdots+(\mu_{1}-\mu_{0})z+\mu_{0}.

By Lemma
3.4
and
|z|=1
, one has

\begin{split}\displaystyle\left|F(z)\right|&=\left|-\mu_{2n}z^{2n+1}+\mu_{2n}z^{2n}-\frac{2}{5}\mu_{2n}z^{2n}+(\frac{2}{5}\mu_{2n})z^{2n}+\cdots+(\mu_{1}-\mu_{0})z+\mu_{0}\right|\\
&\geq\left|\mu_{2n}\right|\left|z\right|^{2n}\left|z-\frac{3}{5}\right|-\left|z\right|^{2n}\left|(\frac{2}{5}\mu_{2n}-\mu_{2n-1})+\frac{(\mu_{2n-1}-\mu_{2n-2})}{z}+\cdots+\frac{(\mu_{1}-\mu_{0})}{z^{2n-1}}+\frac{\mu_{0}}{z^{2n}}\right|\\
&\geq\left|\mu_{2n}\right|\left|z\right|^{2n}\left|z-\frac{3}{5}\right|-\left|z\right|^{2n}(\left|\frac{2}{5}\mu_{2n}-\mu_{2n-1}\right|+\frac{\left|\mu_{2n-1}-\mu_{2n-2}\right|}{\left|z\right|}+\cdots+\frac{\left|\mu_{1}-\mu_{0}\right|}{\left|z\right|^{2n-1}}+\frac{\left|\mu_{0}\right|}{\left|z\right|^{2n}})\\
&=\mu_{2n}(\left|z-\frac{3}{5}\right|-\frac{2}{5})=r^{2n}(\left|z-\frac{3}{5}\right|-\frac{2}{5})\geq 0.\end{split}

Note that the equality holds if and only if
z=1
, but
z=1
is not a zero of (
3.1
). It turns out that (
3.1
) has no zero on the unit circle. This leads to a contradiction.∎

By the part (ii) of Lemma
3.3
and the proof of Theorem
1.2
, it follows that
P_{n}(q)
has no zeros inside
\displaystyle\{q\in\mathbb{C}:|q|>\frac{1}{R_{(1)}}=\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}\}
. Then we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.5
.

All zeros of
P_{n}(q)
lie in the annulus
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}:=\{q\in\mathbb{C}:\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}\leq|q|\leq\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}\}
.

4
q
-rational number sequence with convergence on
q

4.1
Two sequences
\{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)\}_{k=0,1,2,\ldots}
and
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{k}(n)\}_{k=0,1,2,\ldots}

Such as the golden number, that is the
1
st metallic number, can be represented by the limit of the quotient of the adjacent two terms of the Fibonacci sequence, the
n
th metallic number also has a sequence of numbers that is the limit of the quotient of the adjacent two terms. In this section, we first consider such a sequence and its
q
-deformation.

Fix a natural number
n
, and consider the sequence
\{A_{k}(n)\}_{k=0,1,2,\ldots}
defined by
A_{0}(n)=0
,
A_{1}(n)=1
, and
A_{k+2}(n)=nA_{k+1}(n)+A_{k}(n)
. The sequence
\{A_{k}(n)\}_{k=0,1,2,\ldots}
satisfies the following recurrence formula:

A_{k+4}(n)=(n^{2}+2)A_{k+2}(n)-A_{k}(n)\ (k=0,1,2,\ldots).

(4.1)

We consider the rational sequence
\frac{A_{2}(n)}{A_{1}(n)},\frac{A_{3}(n)}{A_{2}(n)},{\frac{A_{4}(n)}{A_{3}(n)},\ldots}
, which converges the
n
th metallic number, and their
q
-deformations
{\left[\frac{A_{2}(n)}{A_{1}(n)}\right]_{q},\left[\frac{A_{3}(n)}{A_{2}(n)}\right]_{q},\left[\frac{A_{4}(n)}{A_{3}(n)}\right]_{q},\ldots}
.

It is well-known that
A_{k}(1)
is the
k
th Fibonacci number, and
A_{k}(2)
is the
k
th Pell number. Since the
q
-deformation of Fibonacci numbers and Pell numbers are defined in
[
9
]
. S. Morier-Genoud and V. Ovsienko
[
9
]
introduced two sequences which converge to
q
-golden number and
q
-silver number. These sequence can also be viewed as a
q
-deformation of Fibonacci numbers and Pell numbers, respectively. Inspired by their study, we introduce two sequences which are generalizations of them as follows.

Proposition 4.1
.

Consider the following four recurrence formulas:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{M}_{2l+1}(n)=q[n]_{q}\mathcal{M}_{2l}(n)+\mathcal{M}_{2l-1}(n),\\
&\mathcal{M}_{2l+2}(n)=[n]_{q}\mathcal{M}_{2l+1}(n)+q^{2n}\mathcal{M}_{2l}(n),\\
\end{split}

(4.2)

\begin{split}\displaystyle&\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{2l+1}(n)=[n]_{q}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{2l}(n)+q^{2n}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{2l-1}(n),\\
&\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{2l+2}(n)=q[n]_{q}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{2l+1}(n)+\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{2l}(n),\\
\end{split}

(4.3)

with
l=1,2,\cdots
, and

\mathcal{M}_{0}(n)=0,\ \mathcal{M}_{1}(n)=1,\ \mathcal{M}_{2}(n)=[n]_{q},\ \mathcal{M}_{3}(n)=1+q([n]_{q})^{2},

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(n)=0,\ \widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{1}(n)=1,\ \widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{2}(n)=[n]_{q},\ \widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{3}(n)=([n]_{q})^{2}+q^{2n-1}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\left[\frac{A_{k+1}(n)}{A_{k}(n)}\right]_{q}=\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{k+1}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}\ (k=1,2,3,\ldots).

(4.4)

Proof. We show this proposition by using the
q
-deformed continued fraction and weighted triangulations. For a detailed definition of weighted triangulation, it can be referred to
[
9
]
(see, Sections 2.1 and 2.3).

Then
[n,n,\ldots,n,\ldots]_{q}
corresponds to the weighted triangulation depicted in Figure 1, where the weights of the unmarked edges are
1
and the convergence of the continued fraction corresponds to the black vertices. By direct deduction, the numerator and denominator of
\displaystyle\left[\frac{A_{k+1}(n)}{A_{k}(n)}\right]_{q}
are satisfy (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), respectively.

Figure 1:

The weighted triangulation corresponding to
[n,n,\ldots,n,\ldots]_{q}

∎

Both
\{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)\}_{k=0,1,2,\ldots}
and
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{k}(n)\}_{k=0,1,2,\ldots}
can be viewed as the
q
-deformation of the sequence
\{A_{k}(n)\}_{k=0,1,2,\ldots}
. From (
4.2
) and Proposition
4.1
, we can obtain the following proposition, which can be viewed as a
q
-deformation of (
4.1
).

Proposition 4.2
.

The
\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)
is determined by the recurrence formula

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{k+4}(n)=f(q,n)\mathcal{M}_{k+2}(n)-q^{2n}\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)\ (k=1,2,3,\ldots),

(4.5)

where

\displaystyle f(q,n):=1+q([n]_{q})^{2}+q^{2n}=1+\sum^{n}_{t=1}tq^{t}+\sum^{2n-1}_{t=n+1}(2n-t)q^{t}+q^{2n},

and the initial data

\mathcal{M}_{1}(n)=1,\ \mathcal{M}_{2}(n)=[n]_{q},\ \mathcal{M}_{3}(n)=1+q([n]_{q})^{2},\ \mathcal{M}_{4}(n)=(q^{2n}+1)[n]_{q}+q([n]_{q})^{3}.

Note that
f(q,n)+2q^{n}=P_{n}(q)
, and so
f(q,n)
is also a palindromic polynomial.

4.2
Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

For any
n
, the equation

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k+2}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}=f(q,n)-q^{2n}\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}

(4.6)

can be implied by (
4.5
). We set
C_{n}=\{q\in\mathbb{C}:|q|=R_{(n)}\}
and
D_{n}=\{q\in\mathbb{C}:|q|<R_{(n)}\}
. Before starting the proof, we will prepare 3 lemmas as follows.

Lemma 4.3
.

On the circle
C_{1}
, one has

\displaystyle\left|f(q,n)\right|_{C_{1}}>1-R_{(1)}.

Proof. On the circle
C_{1}
, one has

\displaystyle\left|f(q,n)\right|_{C_{1}}\geq f(-R_{(1)},n)=1+\sum^{n}_{t=1}(-1)^{t}tR_{(1)}^{t}+\sum^{2n-1}_{t=n+1}(-1)^{t}(2n-t)R_{(1)}^{t}+R_{(1)}^{2n}.

Furthermore, the
f(-R_{(1)},n)
in the above inequality can also be written as

\displaystyle f(-R_{(1)},n)=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\left\{0,\ldots,n\right\}\\
2\mathrel{|}t\end{subarray}}R_{(1)}^{t}(t-(t+1)R_{(1)})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\{n+1,\ldots,2n-2\}\\
2\mathrel{|}t\end{subarray}}R_{(1)}^{t}((2n-t)-(2n-t-1)R_{(1)})+R_{(1)}^{2n}.

Note that for
t\geq 1
, it follows that
t-(t+1)R_{(1)}>0
and
t-(t-1)R_{(1)}>0
. Then, we have

\displaystyle f(-R_{(1)},n)>1-R_{(1)}.

Hence, the lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

If
\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}\right|_{C_{1}}<\frac{1}{R_{(1)}}
, then
\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k+2}(n)}\right|_{C_{1}}<\frac{1}{R_{(1)}}
.

Proof. From (
4.6
), one has

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k+2}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}\right|_{C_{1}}\geq\left|f(q,n)\right|_{C_{1}}-R_{(1)}^{2n}\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}\right|_{C_{1}}.

By Lemma
4.3
and
\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}\right|_{C_{1}}<\frac{1}{R_{(1)}}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k+2}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}\right|_{C_{1}}\geq\left|f(q,n)\right|_{C_{1}}-R_{(1)}^{2n}\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}\right|_{C_{1}}>1-R_{(1)}-R_{(1)}^{2n-1}>R_{(1)}\ \text{with}\ n\geq 2.

On the other hand, the case of
n=1
, has been proved (see
[
7
, Proposition 3.1]
).∎

Lemma 4.5
.

\displaystyle f(q,n)
has no zeros inside
D_{1}
.

Proof. The proof is almost the same as the proof of Theorem
1.2
by the major change being the substitution of

\displaystyle\sum^{2n}_{t=0}\mu_{t}z^{t},\ \ \displaystyle\mu_{t}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{
cl}1&\text{for}\ t=0,\\
tr^{t}&\text{for}\ 0<t\leq n,\\
(2n-t)r^{t}&\text{for}\ n<t<2n,\\
r^{2n}&\text{for}\ t=2n\\
\end{array}\right.

(4.7)

for (
3.1
). We may also prove this lemma by using Lemma
4.3
and the Rouché theorem. Note that
P_{n}(q)=f(q,n)+2q^{n}
and
P_{n}(q)
has no zero inside
D_{1}
. Then, we can check that
1-R_{(1)}>2R_{(1)}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|f(q,n)\right|_{C_{1}}>2R_{(1)}^{n}.
∎

We are now turning to the proof of Theorem
1.3
. We prove that
\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)
has no zeros inside
D_{1}
by induction on
k
.

Assume that
\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)
has no zeros inside
D_{1}
. By Lemmas
4.3
and
4.4
, the following inequality

\displaystyle\left|f(q,n)\right|_{C_{1}}\geq R_{(1)}^{2n}\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}\right|_{C_{1}}

holds. Since
f(q,n)
has zeros inside
D_{1}
, by the Rouché theorem,
\mathcal{M}_{k+2}(n)
has no zeros inside
D_{n}
. Hence, by induction on
k
, the proof can be completed.
∎

Since
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{k}(n)
also satisfies (
4.5
), by the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.6
.

The zeros of the polynomials
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{k}(n)
and
\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)
lie in the annulus
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}
.

4.3
Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.4
, we need the following two lemmas.

Lemma 4.7
.

On the circles
C_{3}
and
C_{4}
, one has

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|f(q,3)\right|_{C_{3}}

\displaystyle>R_{(3)}^{4}+R_{(3)}^{3}+R_{(3)}^{2},

(4.8)

\displaystyle\left|f(q,4)\right|_{C_{4}}

\displaystyle>R_{(4)}^{6}+R_{(4)}^{5}+R_{(4)}^{4}+R_{(4)}^{3}+R_{(4)}^{2}.

(4.9)

Proof. By direct calculation, one has

\displaystyle\displaystyle R_{(3)}^{6}-R_{(3)}^{5}+R_{(3)}^{4}-4R_{(3)}^{3}+R_{(3)}^{2}-R_{(3)}+1

\displaystyle>0,

\displaystyle R_{(4)}^{8}-R_{(4)}^{7}+R_{(4)}^{6}-4R_{(4)}^{5}+3R_{(4)}^{4}-4R_{(4)}^{3}+R_{(4)}^{2}-R_{(4)}+1

\displaystyle>0.

Then

\begin{split}\left|f(q,3)\right|_{C_{3}}&\geq f(-R_{(3)},3)=R_{(3)}^{6}-R_{(3)}^{5}+2R_{(3)}^{4}-3R_{(3)}^{3}+2R_{(3)}^{2}-R_{(3)}+1\\
&>R_{(3)}^{6}-R_{(3)}^{5}+2R_{(3)}^{4}-3R_{(3)}-(R_{(3)}^{6}-R_{(3)}^{5}+R_{(3)}^{4}-4R_{(3)}^{3}+R_{(3)}^{2}-R_{(3)}+1)\\
&=R_{(3)}^{4}+R_{(3)}^{3}+R_{(3)}^{2},\end{split}

\begin{split}\left|f(q,4)\right|_{C_{4}}&\geq f(-R_{(4)},4)=R_{(4)}^{8}-R_{(4)}^{7}+2R_{(4)}^{6}-3R_{(4)}^{5}+4R_{(4)}^{4}-3R_{(4)}^{3}+2R_{(4)}^{2}-R_{(4)}+1\\
&>R_{(4)}^{8}-R_{(4)}^{7}+2R_{(4)}^{6}-3R_{(4)}^{5}+4R_{(4)}^{4}-3R_{(4)}^{3}+2R_{(4)}^{2}-R_{(4)}+1\\
&\ \ \ \ -(R_{(4)}^{8}-R_{(4)}^{7}+R_{(4)}^{6}-4R_{(4)}^{5}+3R_{(4)}^{4}-4R_{(4)}^{3}+R_{(4)}^{2}-R_{(4)}+1)\\
&=R_{(4)}^{6}+R_{(4)}^{5}+R_{(4)}^{4}+R_{(4)}^{3}+R_{(4)}^{2}.\end{split}

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

For
n=3,4
, if
\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}\right|_{C_{n}}<\frac{1}{R_{(n)}}
, then
\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k+2}(n)}\right|_{C_{n}}<\frac{1}{R_{(n)}}
.

Proof.
From (
4.6
), one has

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k+2}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}\right|_{C_{n}}\geq\left|f(q,n)\right|_{C_{n}}-R_{(n)}^{2n}\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}\right|_{C_{n}}.

By Lemma
4.7
and
\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}\right|_{C_{n}}<\frac{1}{R_{(n)}}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k+2}(3)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(3)}\right|_{C_{3}}

\displaystyle>R_{(3)}^{4}+R_{(3)}^{3}+R_{(3)}^{2}-R_{(3)}^{5}>R_{(3)}

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k+2}(4)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(4)}\right|_{C_{4}}

\displaystyle>R_{(4)}^{6}+R_{(4)}^{5}+R_{(4)}^{4}+R_{(4)}^{3}+R_{(4)}^{2}-R_{(4)}^{7}>R_{(4)}.

Hence the lemma is proved.∎

We are now turning to the proof of Theorem
1.4
. By induction on
k
, we prove that
\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)
has no zeros inside
D_{n}
for
n=3,4
.

Assume that
\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)
has no zeros inside
D_{n}
. Since
R_{(n)}\leq 1
, Lemma
4.7
implies that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|f(q,3)\right|_{C_{3}}

\displaystyle>R_{(3)}^{4}+R_{(3)}^{3}+R_{(3)}^{2}>2R_{(3)}^{3}\geq\left|2q^{3}\right|_{C_{3}},

\displaystyle\left|f(q,4)\right|_{C_{4}}

\displaystyle>R_{(4)}^{6}+R_{(4)}^{5}+R_{(4)}^{4}+R_{(4)}^{3}+R_{(4)}^{2}>2R_{(4)}^{4}\geq\left|2q^{3}\right|_{C_{4}}.

Since
f(q,n)+2q^{n}=P_{n}(q)
, and
P_{n}(q)
has no zeros inside
D_{n}
, we see that
f(q,n)
has no zeros inside
D_{n}
, by using the Rouché theorem.

On the other hand, by Lemmas
4.7
and
4.8
, the following inequality holds.

\displaystyle\left|f(q,n)\right|_{C_{n}}\geq R_{(n)}^{2n}\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}\right|_{C_{n}}

Again, by the Rouché theorem,
\mathcal{M}_{k+2}(n)
has no zeros inside
D_{n}
. By induction on
k
, the proof can be completed.
∎

Since
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{k}(n)
also satisfies (
4.5
), by the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we have the following corollary which is better than Corollary
4.6
where
n=3,4
.

Corollary 4.9
.

For
n=3,4
, the zeros of the polynomials
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{k}(n)
and
\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)
lie in the annulus
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}:=\{q\in\mathbb{C}:R_{(n)}\leq|q|\leq R_{(n)}^{-1}\}
.

Remark 4.10
.

Note that for the case of
n
is a specific integer, we can find an inequality to estimate
\left|f(q,n)\right|_{C_{n}}
by direct calculation such as Lemma
4.7
. However, for the general case (
n\geq 5
), there are some limitations to this approach to construct an inequality of
\left|f(q,n)\right|_{C_{n}}
to check the following conditions.

(I)

\left|f(q,n)\right|_{C_{n}}\geq\left|2q^{n}\right|_{C_{n}}
;

(II)

If
\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}\right|_{C_{n}}<\frac{1}{R_{(n)}}
, then
\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k}(n)}{\mathcal{M}_{k+2}(n)}\right|_{C_{n}}<\frac{1}{R_{(n)}}
.

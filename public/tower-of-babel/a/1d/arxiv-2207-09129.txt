On the symmetric rearrangement of the gradient of a Sobolev function
Vincenzo Amato, Andrea Gentile

Abstract
In this paper, we generalize a classical comparison result for solutions to Hamilton-Jacobi equations with Dirichlet boundary conditions, to solutions to Hamilton-Jacobi equations with non-zero boundary trace. As a consequence, we prove the isoperimetric inequality for the torsional rigidity (with Robin boundary conditions) and for other functionals involving such boundary conditions. MSC 2020: 46E30, 35A23, 35J92. Keywords: Rearrangements, Robin boundary conditions.

Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, open and Lipschitz set and let
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, for some
p\geq 1
, be a non-negative function.

In this paper, we deal with the problem of comparing a function
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
with a radial function having the modulus of the gradient equi-rearranged with
{\left|\nabla u\right|}
.
Hence, we aim to extend the results contains in and Nunziante
[
GN84
]
to a more general setting.

Throughout this article,
{\left|\cdot\right|}
will denote both the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure and the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, the meaning will be clear by the context.

If
A
is a bounded and open set with the same measure as
\Omega
, we say that a function
f^{\star}\in L^{p}(A)
is equi-rearranged to
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
if they have the same distribution function, i.e.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f:\Omega\to\R
be a measurable function, the
distribution function
of
f
is the function
\mu_{f}:[0,+\infty[\,\to[0,+\infty[
defined by

\mu_{f}(t)={\left|\Set{x\in\Omega\,:\,{\left|f(x)\right|}>t}\right|}.

In order to state our results, we recall some definitions

Definition 1.2
.

Let
f:\Omega\to\R
be a measurable function:

•

the
decreasing rearrangement
of
f
, denoted by
f^{\ast}
, is the distribution function of
\mu_{f}
. Moreover, we can write

f^{\ast}(s)=\inf\{t\geq 0\,|\,\mu_{f}(t)<s\};

•

the
increasing rearrangement
of
f
is defined as

f_{\ast}(s)=f^{\ast}({\left|\Omega\right|}-s);

•

the
spherically symmetric decreasing rearrangement
of
f
, defined in
\Omega^{\sharp}
i.e. the ball centered at the origin with the same measure as
\Omega
, is the function

f^{\sharp}(x)=f^{\ast}(\omega_{n}{\left|x\right|}^{n}),

where
\omega_{n}
is the measure of the
n
-dimensional unit-ball of
\R^{n}
;

•

the
spherically symmetric increasing rearrangement
of
f
, defined in
\Omega^{\sharp}
, is

f_{\sharp}(x)=f_{\ast}(\omega_{n}{\left|x\right|}^{n}).

Clearly, we can construct several rearrangements of a given function
f
, but the one we will refer to is the spherically symmetric increasing rearrangement defined in
\Omega^{\sharp}
.

The starting point of our work, and many others, is
[
GN84
, Theorem 2.2]

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
f\colon\Omega\to\R
,
H\colon\R^{n}\to\R
be measurable non-negative functions and let
K\colon[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be a strictly increasing real-valued function such that

0\leq K({\left|y\right|})\leq H(y)\qquad\forall y\in\R^{n}\qquad\text{ and }K^{-1}(f)\in L^{p}(\Omega)

Let
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
be a function that satisfy

\begin{cases}H(\nabla v)=f(x)&\text{a.e. in }\Omega\\
v=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

then, denoting with
\overline{v}
the unique decreasing spherically symmetric solution to

\begin{cases}K({\left|\nabla\overline{v}\right|})=f_{\sharp}(x)&\text{a.e. in }\Omega^{\sharp}\\
\overline{v}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega^{\sharp}\end{cases}

it holds

{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq{\left\|\overline{v}\right\|}_{L^{1}(\Omega^{\sharp})}

(1.1)

They give also a similar result for the spherically symmetric decreasing rearrangement of the gradient, with an
L^{\infty}
comparison.

In recent decades, many authors studied this kind of problems, in particular in
[
ALT89
]
Alvino, Lions and Trombetti proved the existence of a spherically symmetric rearrangement of the gradient of
v
which gives a
L^{q}
comparison as in (
1.1
) for a fixed
q
.

Moreover, Cianchi in
[
Cia96
]
gives a characterization of such rearrangement; clearly, the rearrangement found by Cianchi is different both from the spherically symmetric increasing and decreasing rearrangement if
q\in(1,\infty)
.

Furthermore, in
[
FP91
]
and
[
FPV93
]
the authors studied the optimization of the norm of a Sobolev function in the class of functions with fixed rearrangement of the gradient.

Incidentally, let us mention that the case where the
L^{q,1}
Lorentz norm, see Section
2
for its definition, takes the place of the
L^{q}
norm in (
1.1
) has been studied in
[
Tal
]
. In particular, he stated the following

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u
be a real-valued function defined in
\R^{n}
. Suppose
u
is nice enough - e.g. Lipschitz continuous - and the support of
u
has finite measure. Let
M
and
V
denote the distribution function of
{\left|\nabla u\right|}
and the measure of the support of
u
, respectively.

Let
v
the real-valued function defined in
\R^{n}
that satisfies the following conditions:

1.

{\left|\nabla v\right|}
is a rearrangement of
{\left|\nabla u\right|}
;

2.

the support of
v
has the same measure of the support of
u
;

3.

v
is radially decreasing and
{\left|\nabla v\right|}
is radially increasing.

Then

{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{p,1}(\Omega)}\leq{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{p,1}(\Omega^{\sharp})}\quad\text{ if }n=1\text{ or }0<p\leq\frac{n}{n-1},

furthermore

{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{p,1}(\Omega^{\sharp})}=\frac{p^{2}}{\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}(n+p)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[V^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{n}}-(V-M(t))^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{n}}\right]\,dt.

On the other hand, the problem of studying the rearrangement of the Laplacian has been widely studied by several authors. The bibliography is extensive; for the sake of completeness, let us recall some of the works:
[
Tal76
]
for the Dirichlet boundary conditions,
[

As we already said, we focus on the case in which the functions do not vanish on the boundary. Our main theorem is the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be a bounded, open and Lipschitz set and let
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
be a non-negative function. If we denote with
\Omega^{\sharp}
the ball centered at the origin with same measure as
\Omega
, then there exists a non-negative function
u^{\star}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega^{\sharp})
that satisfies

\begin{cases}\lvert\nabla u^{\star}\rvert={\left|\nabla u\right|}_{\sharp}(x)&\text{a.e. in }\Omega^{\sharp}\\[4.30554pt]
u^{\star}=\cfrac{\displaystyle{\int_{\partial\Omega}u\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}}{\displaystyle{\lvert\partial\Omega^{\sharp}\rvert}}&\text{ on }\partial\Omega^{\sharp}.\end{cases}

(1.2)

and such that

\displaystyle{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq{\left\|u^{\star}\right\|}_{L^{1}(\Omega^{\sharp})}.

(1.3)

Remark 1.4
.

By the explicit expression of
u^{*}
on the boundary and the Hölder inequality, we can estimate the
L^{p}
norm of the trace:

\lvert\partial\Omega^{\sharp}\rvert^{p-1}\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}(u^{\star})^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\left(\int_{\partial\Omega}u\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)^{p}\leq{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}^{p-1}\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\qquad\forall p\geq 1.

(1.4)

This result allows us to compare solutions to PDE with Robin boundary conditions with solutions to their symmetrized.

Precisely we are able to compare solutions to

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=1&\text{in }\Omega\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial{u}}{\partial{\nu}}+\beta{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}\,u=0}&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(1.5)

with the solution to

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=1&\text{in }\Omega^{\sharp}\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial{v}}{\partial{\nu}}+\beta\lvert\partial\Omega^{\sharp}\rvert\,v=0}&\text{on }\partial\Omega^{\sharp}\end{cases}

(1.6)

In particular we get

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\beta>0,
let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be a bounded, open and Lipschitz set. If we denote with
\Omega^{\sharp}
the ball centered at the origin with same measure as
\Omega
, it holds

T(\Omega,\beta)\geq T(\Omega^{\sharp},\beta)

(1.7)

where

T(\Omega,\beta)=\inf_{w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\cfrac{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla w\right|}^{2}\,dx+\beta{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}\,\int_{\partial\Omega}w^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}}{\displaystyle{\biggl(\int_{\Omega}w\,dx\biggr)^{2}}}\qquad\text{for }w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega).

(1.8)

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall some basic notions, definitions and
classical results and we prove Theorem
1.3
. Eventually, Section

is dedicated to the application to the Robin torsional rigidity and in Section
4
we get a comparison between Lorentz norm of
u
and

.

2
Notations, Preliminaries and proof of the main result

Observe that obviously
\forall p\geq 1

\displaystyle{{\left\|f\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}={\left\|f^{*}\right\|}_{L^{p}([0,{\left|\Omega\right|}])}=\lVert{f^{\sharp}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\sharp})}={\left\|f_{*}\right\|}_{L^{p}([0,{\left|\Omega\right|}])}=\lVert{f_{\sharp}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\sharp})}},

moreover, the Hardy-Littlewood inequalities hold true:

\int_{\Omega}{\left|f(x)g(x)\right|}\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}f^{*}(s)g^{*}(s)\,ds=\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}f^{\sharp}(x)g^{\sharp}(x)\,dx,

\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}f^{\sharp}(x)g_{\sharp}(x)\,dx=\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}f^{*}(s)g_{*}(s)\,ds\leq\int_{\Omega}{\left|f(x)g(x)\right|}\,dx.

Finally, the operator which assigns to a function its symmetric decreasing rearrangement is a contraction in
L^{p}
, see (
[
Chi79
]
), i.e.

{\left\|f^{*}-g^{*}\right\|}_{L^{p}([0,{\left|\Omega\right|}])}\leq{\left\|f-g\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

(2.1)

One can find more results and details about rearrangements for instance in
[
HLP88
]
and in
[
Tal
]
.

Other powerful tools are the pseudo-rearrangements. Let
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and let
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, as in
[
AT78
]

\forall s\in[0,{\left|\Omega\right|}]
, there exists a subset
D(s)\subseteq\Omega
such that

1.

{\left|D(s)\right|}=s
;

2.

D(s_{1})\subseteq D(s_{2})
if
s_{1}<s_{2}
;

3.

D(s)=\Set{x\in\Omega\,}{\,{\left|u(x)\right|}>t}
if
s=\mu(t)
.

So the function

\int_{D(s)}f(x)\,dx

is absolutely continuous, therefore it exists a function
F
such that

\int_{0}^{s}F(t)\,dt=\int_{D(s)}f(x)\,dx

(2.2)

We will use the following propriety (
[
AT78
, Lemma 2.2]
)

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\in L^{p}
for
p>1
and let
D(s)
be a family described above. If
F
is defined as in (
2.2
), then there exists a sequence
\Set{F_{k}}
such that
F_{k}
has the same rearrangement as
f
and

F_{k}\rightharpoonup F\qquad\text{in }L^{p}([0,{\left|\Omega\right|}])

If

it follows that

\lim_{k}\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}F_{k}(s)g(s)\,ds=\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}F(s)g(s)\,ds

for each function
g\in BV([0,{\left|\Omega\right|}])
.

Moreover, for sake of completeness, we will recall the definition of the Lorentz norm.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\R^{n}
a measurable set,

and

. Then a function

belongs to the Lorentz space

{\left\|g\right\|}_{L^{p,q}(\Omega)}=\biggl(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\bigl[t^{\frac{1}{p}}g^{*}(t)\bigr]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{q}}<+\infty.

(2.3)

Let us notice that for

the Lorentz space

coincides with the Lebesgue space

by the Cavalieri’s principle.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let us consider
\varepsilon

\delta:=\delta_{\varepsilon}
and the sets

\displaystyle\Omega_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\Set{x\in\R^{n}}{d(x,\Omega)<\varepsilon}\qquad

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega,

\displaystyle\Omega^{\sharp}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\Set{x\in\R^{n}}{d(x,\Omega^{\sharp})<\delta}\qquad

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\sharp}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{\sharp}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\Omega^{\sharp},

\displaystyle{\left|\Omega_{\varepsilon}\right|}

\displaystyle=\lvert\Omega^{\sharp}_{\varepsilon}\rvert\qquad

\displaystyle{\left|\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\right|}

\displaystyle=\lvert\Sigma_{\varepsilon}^{\sharp}\rvert,

{\left|\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\right|}/\varepsilon\to{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}
and
\lvert\Sigma_{\varepsilon}^{\sharp}\rvert/\delta\to\lvert\partial\Omega^{\sharp}\rvert

\displaystyle{u_{\varepsilon}\rvert_{\Omega}=u}

(b)

(c)

\displaystyle{{\left\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq{\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right|}(y)}

(d)

\displaystyle{\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}{\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right|}\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}u\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.}

\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\nabla\bigl(u(p(x))\bigr)\biggl[1-\frac{d(x,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}\biggr]-u\bigl(p(x)\bigr)\frac{\nabla d(x,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}

For the first term, we can notice

\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\bigl\lvert\nabla\bigl(u(p(x))\bigr)\bigr\rvert\biggl[1-\frac{d(x,\partial\Omega)}{\varepsilon}\biggr]\,dx\leq L\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\,dx=L{\left|\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\right|}

. Now we deal with the second term and, keeping in mind that
{\left|\nabla d\right|}=1

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}u(p(x))\,dx=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\,dt\int_{\Gamma_{t}}(u\circ p)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

For every

\begin{cases}{\left|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\right|}(x)={\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right|}_{\sharp}(x)&\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\sharp}\\

{\left|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\right|}(x)={\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right|}_{\sharp}(x)={\left|\nabla u\right|}_{\sharp}(x)\qquad\forall x\in\Omega^{\sharp}.

We can see

{\left|\nabla u_{k}\right|}_{*}={\left|\nabla u_{k}^{\star}\right|}_{*}
and

\displaystyle{\left\|u_{k}\right\|}_{L^{1}(\Omega_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert u_{k}^{\star}\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega_{k}^{\sharp})}

(2.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{k}}u_{k}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega_{k}^{\sharp}}u_{k}^{\star}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

(2.13)

Therefore, since

, for all

\Set{u_{k}^{\star}}
is equibounded in
W^{1,p}(\Omega^{\sharp})
and it has a subsequence which converges strongly in

and weakly in

to a function

.

Let us prove that
{\left|\nabla u\right|}
and
{\left|\nabla w\right|}
has the same rearrangement.

\limsup_{k}\,\bigl\lVert{\left|\nabla u_{k}^{\star}\right|}-{\left|\nabla u\right|}_{\sharp}\bigr\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\sharp})}\leq\lim_{k}\,\bigl\lVert(f_{k})_{\sharp}-f_{\sharp}\bigr\rVert_{L^{p}(\R^{n})}

where

f(x)=\begin{cases}{\left|\nabla\tilde{u}\right|}&\text{in }\Omega\\
\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\R^{n})}&\text{in }\R^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{cases}\qquad\text{ and }f_{k}=\begin{cases}{\left|\nabla u_{k}\right|}&\text{in }\Omega_{k}\\
\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\R^{n})}&\text{in }\R^{n}\setminus\Omega_{k}\end{cases}

So using (
2.1
) we have

\bigl\lVert(f_{k})_{\sharp}-f_{\sharp}\bigr\rVert_{L^{p}(\R^{n})}\leq\lVert f_{k}-f\rVert_{L^{p}(\R^{n})}=\lVert f_{k}-f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{k}\setminus\Omega)}\leq 2\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\R^{n})}{\left|\Omega_{k}\setminus\Omega\right|}\

that tends to

as

by the fact that
{\left|\Omega_{k}\triangle\Omega\right|}\to 0
.

Hence, the functions
\nabla w
and
\nabla u
has the same rearrangement, by the uniqueness of the weak limit in
\Omega^{\sharp}
.

In the end, passing to limit

in (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), we have

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert w\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega^{\sharp})}

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}u\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}w\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Hence
w=u^{\star}
.

Step 4

Finally, we proceed by removing the assumption

\displaystyle\forall k\in\mathbb{N}

(2.14)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{k}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}u_{k}^{\star}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.15)

Arguing as the previous step, there exists a function

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}u\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

3
An application to torsional rigidity

Let
\beta>0
, let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be a bounded and open set with Lipschitz boundary and let us consider the functional

\mathcal{F}_{\beta}(\Omega,w)=\cfrac{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla w\right|}^{2}\,dx+\beta{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}\,\int_{\partial\Omega}w^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}}{\displaystyle{\biggl(\int_{\Omega}w\,dx\biggr)^{2}}}\qquad w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)

(3.1)

and the associate minimum problem

T(\Omega,\beta)=\min_{w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\mathcal{F}_{\beta}(w)

(3.2)

The minimum
u
is a weak solution to

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=1&\text{in }\Omega\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial{u}}{\partial{\nu}}+\beta{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}\,u=0}&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(3.3)

Our aim is to compare
T(\Omega,\beta)
with

T(\Omega^{\sharp},\beta):=\min_{v\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\mathcal{F}_{\Omega,\beta}(v)=\min_{v\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\cfrac{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}{\left|\nabla v\right|}^{2}\,dx+\beta\lvert\partial\Omega^{\sharp}\rvert\,\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}v^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}}{\displaystyle{\biggl(\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}v\,dx\biggr)^{2}}}

where the minimum is a weak solution to

\begin{cases}-\Delta z=1&\text{in }\Omega^{\sharp}\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial{z}}{\partial{\nu}}+\beta\lvert\partial\Omega^{\sharp}\rvert\,z=0}&\text{on }\partial\Omega^{\sharp}\end{cases}

(3.4)

Proof of Corollary
1.5
.

Let
w\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, by Theorem
1.3
and Remark
1.4
there exists
w^{\star}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega^{\sharp})
radial such that

\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla w\right|}^{2}\,dx=\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}\lvert\nabla w^{\star}\rvert^{2}\,dx\qquad\int_{\Omega}{\left|w\right|}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}\lvert w^{\star}\rvert\,dx\qquad\lvert\partial\Omega^{\sharp}\rvert\,\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}(w^{\star})^{2}\leq{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}\,\int_{\partial\Omega}w^{2}

Therefore

\mathcal{F}_{\beta}(w)\geq\mathcal{F}_{\beta}(w^{\star})

Passing to the infimum on right-hand side and successively to the left-hand side, we obtain

T(\Omega,\beta)\geq T(\Omega^{\sharp},\beta)

∎

Remark 3.1
.

We highlight that all the arguments work also in the non-linear case, where the functional

\mathcal{F}_{\beta,p}(w)=\cfrac{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla w\right|}^{p}\,dx+\beta{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}^{p-1}\,\int_{\partial\Omega}w^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}}{\displaystyle{\biggl(\int_{\Omega}w\,dx\biggr)^{p}}}\qquad\text{for }w\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).

(3.5)

is considered.

4
A weighted
L^{1}
comparison

Let us check how extend the result by
[
Tal
]
to the case of function non vanishing on the boundary.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be a bounded, open and Lipschitz set. Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a function such that

f^{*}(t)\geq\biggl(1-\frac{1}{n}\biggr)\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)\,ds\qquad\forall t\in[0,{\left|\Omega\right|}].

(4.1)

If
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
u^{\star}
is the function given by Theorem
1.3
, then

\int_{\Omega}f(x)u(x)\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}f^{\sharp}(x)u^{\star}(x)\,dx.

(4.2)

Proof.

If
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, the result is contained in
[
Tal
]
. We recall it, for sake of completeness.

By
[
GN84
, eq. 2.7]
it is known

u^{*}(s)\leq\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}}\int_{s}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}\frac{F(t)}{t^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}\,dt

(4.3)

where
F
is a function such that

\int_{0}^{s}F(t)\,dt=\int_{D(s)}{\left|\nabla u\right|}_{*}(s)\,ds

with
D(s)
defined in Section
2
.

Setting
\displaystyle{g(t):=\frac{1}{t^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)\,ds}
, multiplying both terms of (
4.3
) for
f^{*}(s)
, integrating from
0
to
{\left|\Omega\right|}
and using Fubini’s Theorem we get

\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}f^{*}(s)u^{*}(s)\,ds\leq\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}}\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}f^{*}(s)\biggl(\int_{s}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}\frac{F(t)}{t^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}\,dt\biggr)\,ds=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}}\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}F(t)g(t)\,dt

(4.4)

Let us suppose that
g(t)
is non-decreasing, so
g_{*}(s)=g(s)
and by Lemma
2.1
there exists a sequence
\{F_{k}\}
such that
(F_{k})_{*}=(\nabla u)_{*}
and
F_{k}\rightharpoonup F
in
BV
. Therefore

\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}F(t)g(t)\,dt=\lim_{k}\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}F_{k}(t)g(t)\,dt

Using Hardy-Littlewood’s inequality we have

\lim_{k}\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}F_{k}(t)g(t)\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}{\left|\nabla u\right|}_{*}(t)g_{*}(t)\,dt=\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}{\left|\nabla u\right|}_{*}(t)g(t)\,dt

Hence, by (
4.4
) and Fubini’s Theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}f^{*}(t)u^{*}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}}\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}{\left|\nabla u\right|}_{*}(t)\,g(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}}\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}{\left|\nabla u\right|}_{*}(t)\Biggl(\frac{1}{t^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)\,ds\Biggr)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}f^{*}(s)\biggl(\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}}\int_{s}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}\frac{{\left|\nabla u\right|}_{*}(t)}{t^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}\,dt\biggr)\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}f^{*}(s)(u^{\star})^{*}(s)\,ds

Therefore, by Hardy-Littlewood inequality, we have

\int_{\Omega}f(x)u(x)\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}f^{*}(t)u^{*}(t)\leq\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}f^{*}(s)(u^{\star})^{*}(s)\,ds=\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}f^{\sharp}(x)\,u^{\star}(x)\,dx

(4.5)

But we have to deal with the assumption that
g
is non-decreasing, that is

g^{\prime}(t)\geq 0\iff\frac{d}{dt}\biggl(\frac{1}{t^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)\,ds\biggr)=-\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{t^{2-\frac{1}{n}}}\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)\,ds\biggr)+\frac{1}{t^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}f^{*}(t)\geq 0,

hence, if and only if

f^{*}(t)\geq\biggl(1-\frac{1}{n}\biggr)\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)\,ds.

Now let us deal with

. Suppose that

is a non-negative function, that

has

Corollary 4.4

{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{p,1}(\Omega)}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{\frac{1}{p}-1}u^{*}(t)\,dt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}u^{*}(t)\,dt

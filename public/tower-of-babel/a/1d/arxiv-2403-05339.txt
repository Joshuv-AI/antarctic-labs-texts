Manin triples and bialgebras of Left-Alia algebras associated to invariant theory
Chuangchuang Kang, Guilai Liu, Zhuo Wang, Shizhuo Yu

Abstract
. A left-Alia algebra is a vector space together with a bilinear map satisfying symmetric Jocobi identity. Motivated by invariant theory, we first construct a class of left-Alia algebras induced by twisted derivations. Then, we introduce the notion of Manin triples and bialgebras of left-Alia algebras. Via specific matched pairs of left-Alia algebras, we figure out the equivalence between Manin triples and bialgebras of left-Alia algebras.

Introduction and Main Statements

1.1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group and
\mathbb{K}
an algebraic closed field of characteristic zero.
Suppose that
V
is an
n
-dimensional faithful representation of
G
and

S=\mathbb{K}[V]=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

is the coordinate ring of
V
.

The goal of invariant theory is to study the structures of the ring of invariants

S^{G}=\{f\in S:a\cdot f=f,\ \forall a\in G\},

in which the group action is extended from the representation of
G
(see Section
2.1
for more details).
In particular, Hilbert proved that
S^{G}
is always a finite generated
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
[
17
]
and Chevalley
[
10
]
, Shephard and Todd
[
27
]
proved that
S^{G}
is a polynomial algebra if and only if
G
is generated by pseudo-reflections (see Section
2.2
for precise definition).

Twisted derivations
[
14
]
(also named
\sigma
-derivations) play an important role in the study of deformations of Lie algebras. Motivated by the above Chevalley’s Theorem, we apply pseudo-reflections to induce a class of twisted derivations on
S
(see Theorem
2.7
for more details). Based on twisted derivations on commutative associative algebras, we obtain a class of left-Alia (left anti-Lie-admissible) algebras
[
12
]
, which appears in the study of a special class of algebras with a skew-symmetric identity of degree three. Furthermore, we construct Manin triples and bialgebras of left-Alia algebras. Via specific matched pairs of left-Alia algebras, we figure out the equivalence between Manin triples and bialgebras.

Throughout this paper, unless otherwise specified, all vector spaces are finite-dimensional over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero and all
\mathbb{K}
-algebras are commutative and associative with the finite Krull dimension, although many results and notions remain valid in the infinite-dimensional case.

1.2.
Left-Alia Algebras Associated with Invariant Theory

The notion of a left-Alia algebra was defined for the first time in the table in the Introduction of
[
12
]
.

Definition 1.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

A
left-Alia algebra
(also named a 0-Alia algebra) is a vector space
A
together with a bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
satisfying the
symmetric Jacobi identity
:

[[x,y],z]+[[y,z],x]+[[z,x],y]=[[y,x],z]+[[z,y],x]+[[x,z],y],\;\forall x,y,z\in A.

(1)

There are some typical examples of left-Alia algebras. Firstly, when the bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]
is skew-symmetric,
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Lie algebra. By contrast, any commutative algebra is a left-Alia algebra and, in particular, a mock-Lie algebra
[
30
]
(also known as a Jacobi–Jordan algebra in
[
8
]
) with a symmetric bilinear map that satisfies the Jacobi identity is a left-Alia algebra.
Secondly, the notion of an anti-pre-Lie algebra
[
22
]
was recently studied as a left-Alia algebra with an additional condition. Anti-pre-Lie algebras are the underlying algebra structures of nondegenerate commutative
2
-cocycles
[
13
]
on Lie algebras and are characterized as Lie-admissible algebras whose negative multiplication operators compose representations of commutator Lie algebras. Condition (
1
) of the identities of an anti-pre-Lie algebra is just to guarantee
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Lie-admissible algebra. Additionally, we also studied left-Alia algebras in terms of their relationships with Leibniz algebras
[
24
]
and Lie triple systems
[
15
]
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a commutative associative algebra and
R:A\rightarrow A
a linear map on
A
. For brevity, the operation
\cdot
will be omitted. A linear map
D:A\rightarrow A
is called
a twisted derivation
with respect to an
R
(also named a
\sigma
-derivation in
[
14
]
) if
D
satisfies the twisted Leibniz rule:

D(fg)=D(f)g+R(f)D(g),\ f,g\in A.

(2)

Non-trivial examples of twisted derivations can be constructed in invariant theory. In particular, each pseudo-reflection
R
on a vector space
V
induces a twisted derivation
D_{R}
on the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[V]
(see Section
2.2
for details).

Define

[f,g]_{R}=D(f)g-R(f)D(g).

We then obtain a class of left-Alia algebras in Theorem A.

Theorem A
(Theorem
2.7
and Theorem
3.4
)

(a)
For each twisted derivation
D
on
A
,
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{R})
is a left-Alia algebra.

(b)
Each pseodo-reflection
R
on
V
induces a left-Alia algebra
(\mathbb{K}[V],[\cdot,\cdot]_{R})
.

This applies when
R=I
,
[f,g]_{R}
is skew-symmetric and
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{R})
is a Lie algebra of the Witt type
[
28
]
. Moreover, Theorem A also provides a class of left-Alia algebras on polynomial rings from invariant theory. As a corollary of Theorem A, we see that when
S^{G}
is a polynomial algebra, each generator
g\in G
corresponds to a left-Alia algebra
(S,[\cdot,\cdot]_{R_{g}})
. The collection of left-Alia algebras is also an interesting research object for further study.

In addition, if we define that
[f,h]=D_{R}(f)h-fD_{R}(h)
on
S
,
(S,[\cdot,\cdot])
, then it is not a left-Alia algebra in general. However, when
[\cdot,\cdot]
is restricted to
V^{*}
, we obtain a finite-dimensional Lie algebra, which induces a linear Poisson structure on
V
, and figure out the entrance to the study of twisted relative Poisson structures on graded algebras. See
[
19
]
for reference.

1.3.
Manin Triples and Bialgebras of Left-Alia Algebras

A bialgebra structure is a vector space equipped with both an
algebra structure and a coalgebra structure satisfying certain
compatible conditions. Some well-known examples of such structures
include Lie bialgebras
[
9
,
11
]
, which are closely
related to Poisson–Lie groups and play an important role in the infinitesimalization of quantum
groups, and antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
26
]
as equivalent structures of double constructions of Frobenius algebras which are widely
applied in the 2d topological field and string theory
[
20
,
21
]
.
Recently, the notion of anti-pre-Lie bialgebras was studied in
[
23
]
, which serves as a preliminary to supply a reasonable bialgebra theory for transposed Poisson algebras
[
5
]
.
The notions of mock-Lie bialgebras
[
6
]
and Leibniz bialgebras
[
25
,
29
]
were also introduced with different motivations.
These bialgebras have a common property in that they can be equivalently characterized by Manin triples which correspond to nondegenerate invariant bilinear forms on the algebra structures.
In this paper, we follow such a procedure to study left-Alia bialgebras.

To develop the bialgebra theory of left-Alia algebras, we first define a representation of a left-Alia algebra
to be a triple
(l,r,V)
, where
V
is a vector space and
l,r:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
are linear maps such that the following equation holds:

l([x,y])v-l([y,x])v=r(x)r(y)v-r(y)r(x)v+r(y)l(x)v-r(x)l(y)v,\;\forall x,y\in A,v\in V.

A representation
(\rho,V)
of a Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
renders representations
(\rho,-\rho,V)
and
(\rho,2\rho,V)
of
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
as left-Alia algebras.

Furthermore, we introduce the notion of a quadratic left-Alia algebra, defined as a left-Alia algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
equipped with a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
which is invariant in the sense that

\mathcal{B}([x,y],z)=\mathcal{B}(x,[z,y]-[y,z]),\;\forall x,y,z\in A.

A quadratic left-Alia algebra gives rise to the equivalence between the adjoint representation and the coadjoint representation.

Last, we introduce the notions of a matched pair (Definition
3.23
) of left-Alia algebras, a Manin triple of left-Alia algebras (Definition
4.1
) and a left-Alia bialgebra (Definition
4.6
). Via specific matched pairs of left-Alia algebras, we figure out the equivalence between Manin triples and bialgebras in Theorem B.

Theorem B
(Theorem
4.3
and Theorem
4.7
)

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a left-Alia algebra. Suppose that there is a left-Alia algebra structure
(A^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\})
on the dual space
A^{*}
, and
\delta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
is the dual of
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

There is a Manin triple of left-Alia algebras
\big((A\oplus A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{d},\mathcal{B}_{d}),A,A^{*}\big)
,
where,

[(x,u),(y,v)]_{d}=([x,y],l(x)v+r(y)u),\;\forall x,y\in A,u,v\in A^{*},\ \ \ and

\mathcal{B}_{d}((x,u),(y,v))=\langle x,v\rangle+\langle y,u\rangle,\;\forall x,y\in A,u,v\in A^{*}.

(b)

(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\delta)
is a left-Alia bialgebra.

Theorem B naturally leads to the study of Yang–Baxter equations and relative Rota–Baxter operators for left-Alia algebras
[
18
]
.

2.
Pseudo-Reflections and Twisted Deviations in Invariant Theory

2.1.
Preliminary on Invariant Theory

Let
G
be a finite group and
\mathbb{K}
an algebraic closed field of characteristic zero.
Suppose that
(\rho,V)
is an
n
-dimensional faithful representation of
G
and its dual representation is denoted by
(\rho,V^{*})
.
Let
S=\mathbb{K}[V]=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the coordinate ring of
V
. Define a
G
-action on
S
as

g\cdot\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}k_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}x_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{i_{n}}:=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}k_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}(\rho(g)x_{1})^{i_{1}}\ldots(\rho(g)x_{n})^{i_{n}},\;\forall g\in G.

(3)

Define the ring of invariants as

S^{G}=\{f\in S:a\cdot f=f,\ \forall a\in G\}.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
7
,
17
]
)
.

(a)

S^{G}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{K}
-algebra.

(b)

S
is a finitely generated
S^{G}
-module.

Definition 2.2

(
[
7
]
)
.

A linear automorphism
R\in Aut(V)
is called a
pseudo-reflection
if
R^{m}=I
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
Im(I-R)
is one-dimensional.

In invariant theory, the following theorem gives the equivalent condition that
S^{G}
is a polynomial algebra:

Theorem 2.3

(
[
10
,
27
]
)
.

S^{G}
is a polynomial algebra if and only if
G\cong\rho(G)
is generated by pseudo-reflections.

Then, we figure out the relation between a pseudo-reflection on
V
and a twisted deviation on
S=\mathbb{K}[V]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
R
be a pseudo-reflection on
V
. Then,
R
induces a pseudo-reflection on
V^{*}
(also denoted by
R
).

Proof.

Let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be a basis of
V
such that
W={\rm Span}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-1}\}
is fixed by
R
.
By
R^{m}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&&&&a_{1}\\
&1&&&a_{2}\\
&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
&&&1&a_{n-1}\\
&&&&a_{n}\\
\end{array}\right)^{m}=I
, we see that
R
is given by the diagonal matrix
diag(1,\ldots,1,\omega)
, where
\omega\neq 1
is an
m
-th primitive root over
\mathbb{K}
. Denote
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, the dual basis of
V^{*}
, such that
x_{i}(e_{j})=\delta_{ij}
. Thus, the induced automorphism on
V^{*}
, defined by
R(x_{i})(e_{j}):=x_{i}(R^{-1}(e_{j}))
, satisfies that

R(x_{i})=x_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
R(x_{n})=(1/\omega)x_{n}
. Therefore,
R
is a pseudo-reflection on
V^{*}
.
∎

2.2.
Pseudo-Reflections Induced by Twisted Deviations

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a commutative associative algebra and
R:A\rightarrow A
a linear map on
A
. Recall from
[
14
]
the definition of a twisted derivation (also named a
\sigma
-derivation).

Definition 2.5
.

A linear map
D:A\rightarrow A
is called a
twisted derivation
with respect to
R
if
D
satisfies the twisted Leibniz rule:

D(fg)=D(f)g+R(f)D(g),\ f,g\in A.

(4)

Remark 2.6
.

When
R=I
,
D
is a derivation on
A
.

Recall from Section
2.1
that for a fixed non-zero
v_{R}\in Im(I-R)\subset V
, there exists a
\Delta_{R}\in V^{*}
such that

(I-R)v=\Delta_{R}(v)v_{R},\ \ \forall v\in V.

(5)

By Lemma
2.4
, for a fixed non-zero
l_{R}\in Im(I-R)\subset V^{*}
, there also exists a
\Delta_{R}\in V
such that

(I-R)x=\Delta_{R}(x)l_{R},\ \ \forall x\in V^{*}.

(6)

Also, denote
R:S\rightarrow S
as an extension of
R\in{\rm Aut}(V)
satisfying

R(k_{1}f+k_{2}h)=k_{1}R(f)+k_{2}R(h)\ {\rm and}\ R(fh)=R(f)R(h).

Theorem 2.7
.

For each
f\in S
, there exists a twisted derivation
D_{R}:S\rightarrow S
with respect to
R

such that

R(f)=f-D_{R}(f)l_{R}.

(7)

Proof.

First, we prove that
R(f)
can be uniquely written as
R(f)=f-D_{R}(f)l_{R}
for some
D_{R}:S\rightarrow S
.
It follows from (
6
) that, for
1\leq a_{i}\leq n
,

\displaystyle R(x_{a_{1}}\ldots x_{a_{k}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(Rx_{a_{1}})\ldots(Rx_{a_{k}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{a_{1}}-\Delta_{R}(x_{a_{1}})l_{R})\ldots(x_{a_{k}}-\Delta_{R}(x_{a_{k}})l_{R})

which can be expressed as

x_{a_{1}}\ldots x_{a_{k}}-D_{R}(x_{a_{1}}\ldots x_{a_{k}})l_{R},

where
D_{R}
maps the monomial to a polynomial in
S
.
As a consequence,
R(f)
can be written as

R(f)=f-D_{R}(f)l_{R},

(8)

where
D_{R}:S\rightarrow S
is a linear map.
Then, we prove that
D_{R}
is a twisted derivation on
S
with respect to
R
.
On the one hand,

R(fh)=fh-D_{R}(fh)l_{R}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle R(f)R(h)=R(f)(h-D_{R}(h)l_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f-D_{R}(f)l_{R})h-R(f)D_{R}(h)l_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle fh-(D_{R}(f)h+R(f)D_{R}(h))l_{R}.

Therefore,
R(fh)=D_{R}(f)h+R(f)D_{R}(h)
.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

When restricting
D_{R}
to
V^{*}
,
D_{R}=\Delta_{R}
on
V^{*}
. When restricting
(I-D_{R})
to
V^{*}
,
(I-D_{R})
is a pseudo-reflection on
V^{*}
.

3.
Left-Alia Algebras and Their Representations

3.1.
Left-Alia Algebras and Twisted Derivations

Definition 3.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

A
left-Alia algebra
is a vector space
A
together with a bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]:A\times A\rightarrow A
satisfying the symmetric Jacobi property:

[[x,y],z]+[[y,z],x]+[[z,x],y]=[[y,x],z]+[[z,y],x]+[[x,z],y],\;\forall x,y,z\in A.

Remark 3.2
.

A left-Alia algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Lie algebra if and only if the bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]
is skew-symmetric.
On the other hand, any commutative algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
in the sense that
[\cdot,\cdot]
is symmetric is a left-Alia algebra.

We can obtain a class of left-Alia algebras from twisted derivations.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
D:A\rightarrow A
be a twisted derivation of the commutative associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
. Then,
D
satisfies

xD(y)-D(x)y=R(x)D(y)-D(x)R(y),\quad\forall~x,y\in A.

(9)

Proof.

By the commutative property of
(A,\cdot)
and (
4
), we have

\displaystyle D(xy)-D(yx)=D(x)y+R(x)D(y)-D(y)x-R(y)D(x)=0.

Therefore, (
9
) holds.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a commutative associative algebra and
D
be a twisted derivation. For all
x,y\in A
, define the bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]_{R}:A\times A\rightarrow A
by

[x,y]_{R}:=[x,y]_{D}=xD(y)-R(y)D(x).

(10)

Then,
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{R})
is a left-Alia algebra.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z\in A
. By (
10
), we have

\displaystyle[x,y]_{R}-[y,x]_{R}

\displaystyle~=xD(y)-R(y)D(x)-yD(x)+R(x)D(y)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:d-use}}{=}2(xD(y)-yD(x)),

and

\displaystyle D(xD(y)-yD(x))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:s-der}}{=}D(x)D(y)-R(y)D^{2}(x)-D(y)D(x)+R(x)D^{2}(y)

\displaystyle=R(x)D^{2}(y)-R(y)\ D^{2}(x).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\circlearrowleft_{x,y,z}[[x,y]_{R}-[y,x]_{R},z]_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\circlearrowleft_{x,y,z}2[xD(y)-yD(x),z]_{R}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:R-der}}{=}

\displaystyle\circlearrowleft_{x,y,z}2\big(xD(y)D(z)-yD(x)D(z)-R(x)D^{2}(y)R(z)+R(y)D^{2}(x)R(z)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~0.

Therefore, the conclusion holds.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Theorem
3.4
can also be verified in the following way.
Let
(A,\cdot)
be a commutative associative algebra with linear maps
f,g:A\rightarrow A
.
By
[
12
]
, there is a left-Alia algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
given by

[x,y]=x\cdot f(y)+g(x\cdot y),\;\forall x,y\in A,

(11)

which is called a
special left-Alia algebra
with respect to
(A,\cdot,f,g)
.
If
D
is a twisted derivation of
(A,\cdot)
with respect to
R
, then we see that
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{R})
satisfies (
11
) for

f=2D,g=-D.

Hence,
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{R})
is left-Alia.

Since

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be an
n
-dimensional (
n\geq 2
) left-Alia algebra and
\left\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\right\}
be a basis of
A
. For all positive integers
1\leq i,j,t\leq n
and structural constants
C_{ij}^{t}\in\mathbb{C}
, set

[e_{i},e_{j}]=\sum_{t=1}^{n}C_{ij}^{t}e_{t}.

(12)

Then,
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a left-Alia algebra if and only if the structural constants
C_{ij}^{t}
satisfy the following equation:

\sum_{k,m=1}^{n}\left((C_{ij}^{k}-C_{ji}^{k})C_{kl}^{m}+(C_{jl}^{k}-C_{lj}^{k})C_{ki}^{m}+(C_{li}^{k}-C_{il}^{k})C_{kj}^{m}\right)=0,\;\forall 1\leq i,j,l\leq n.

(13)

Proof.

By (
1
), for all
e_{i},e_{j},e_{l}\in\left\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\right\}
,

\displaystyle[[e_{i},e_{j}]-[e_{j},e_{i}],e_{l}]+[[e_{j},e_{l}]-[e_{l},e_{j}],e_{i}]+[[e_{l},e_{i}]-[e_{i},e_{l}],e_{j}]=0.

(14)

Set

[e_{i},e_{j}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{ij}^{k}e_{k},~[e_{j},e_{l}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{jl}^{k}e_{l},~[e_{l}\cdot e_{i}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{li}^{k}e_{k},~~~~C_{ij}^{k},C_{jl}^{k},C_{li}^{k}\in\mathbb{C}.

Therefore, Equation (
13
) holds.
∎

As a direct consequence, we obtain the following:

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
A
be a two-dimensional vector space
over the complex field
\mathbb{C}
with a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
. Then, for any bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]

on
A
,
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a left-Alia algebra.

Next, we give some example of three-dimensional left-Alia algebras.

Example 3.9
.

Let
A
be a three-dimensional vector space
over the complex field
\mathbb{C}
with a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}

[\cdot,\cdot]:A\times A\rightarrow A

\displaystyle[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{1},~~[e_{1},e_{3}]=e_{1},~~[e_{2},e_{1}]=e_{2},~~[e_{3},e_{1}]=e_{3},

\displaystyle[e_{1},e_{1}]=[e_{2},e_{2}]=[e_{3},e_{3}]=[e_{2},e_{3}]=[e_{3},e_{2}]=e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}.

Remark 3.10
.

A right-Leibniz algebra
[
24
]
is a vector space

together with a bilinear operation

then

3.5.
Representations and Matched Pairs of left-Alia Algebras

Definition 3.16
.

A
representation
of a left-Alia algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a triple
(l,r,V)
, where
V
is a vector space and
l,r:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
are linear maps such that the following equation holds:

l([x,y])-l([y,x])=r(x)r(y)v-r(y)r(x)+r(y)l(x)v-r(x)l(y),\;\forall x,y\in A,v\in V.

(22)

Two representations,
(l,r,V)
and
(l^{\prime},r^{\prime},V^{\prime})
, of a left-Alia algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
are called
equivalent
if there is a linear isomorphism
\phi:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
such that

\phi\big(l(x)v\big)=l^{\prime}(x)\phi(v),\;\phi\big(r(x)v\big)=r^{\prime}(x)\phi(v),\;\forall x\in A,v\in V.

(23)

Example 3.17
.

Let
(\rho,V)
be a representation of a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
, that is,
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
is a linear map such that

\rho([x,y])v=\rho(x)\rho(y)v-\rho(y)\rho(x)v,\;\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},v\in V.

Then, both
(\rho,-\rho,V)
and
(\rho,2\rho,V)
satisfy (
22
) and, hence, are representations of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
as a left-Alia algebra.

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a left-Alia algebra,
V
be a vector space and
l,r:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
be linear maps.
Then,
(l,r,V)
is a representation of
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
if and only if there is a left-Alia algebra on the direct sum
d=A\oplus V
of vector spaces (
the semi-direct product
) given by

[x+u,y+v]_{d}=[x,y]+l(x)v+r(y)u,\;\forall x,y\in A,u,v\in V.

(24)

In this case, we denote
(A\oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{d})=A\ltimes_{l,r}V
.

Proof.

This is the special case of matched pairs of left-Alia algebras where
B=V
is equipped with the zero multiplication in Proposition
3.24
.
∎

For a vector space
A
with a bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]:A\times A\rightarrow A
, we set linear maps
\mathcal{L}_{[\cdot,\cdot]},\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(A)
using

\mathcal{L}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(x)y=[x,y]=\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(y)x,\;\forall x,y\in A.

Example 3.19
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a left-Alia algebra.
Then,
(\mathcal{L}_{[\cdot,\cdot]},\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]},A)
is a representation of
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
, which is called an
adjoint representation
.
In particular, for a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
with the adjoint representation
\mathrm{ad}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g})
given by
\mathrm{ad}(x)y=[x,y],\;\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(\mathcal{L}_{[\cdot,\cdot]},\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]},\mathfrak{g})=(\mathrm{ad},-\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{g})

is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
as a left-Alia algebra.

Let
A
and
V
be vector spaces. For a linear map
l:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
, we set a linear map
l^{*}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V^{*})
using

\langle l^{*}(x)u^{*},v\rangle=-\langle u^{*},l(x)v\rangle,\;\forall x\in A,u^{*}\in V^{*},v\in V.

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
(l,r,V)
be a representation of a left-Alia algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
.
Then,
(l^{*},l^{*}-r^{*},V^{*})
is also a representation of
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
.
In particular,
(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]},A^{*})
is a representation of
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
, which is called the
coadjoint representation
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in A,u^{*}\in V^{*},v\in V
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\langle\big(l^{*}[x,y]-l^{*}[y,x]+(l^{*}-r^{*})(x)l^{*}(y)-(l^{*}-r^{*})(x)(l^{*}-r^{*})(y)

\displaystyle+(l^{*}-r^{*})(y)(l^{*}-r^{*})(x)-(l^{*}-r^{*})(y)l^{*}(x)\big)u^{*},v\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(l^{*}[x,y]-l^{*}[y,x]+(l^{*}-r^{*})(x)r^{*}(y)-(l^{*}-r^{*})(y)r^{*}(x)\big)u^{*},v\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u^{*},\big(l[y,x]-l[x,y]+r(y)(l-r)(x)-r(x)(l-r)(y)\big)v\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:rep 0-Alia}}{=}0.

Hence, the conclusion follows. ∎

Example 3.21
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Lie algebra.
Then, the coadjoint representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
as a left-Alia algebra is

(\mathrm{ad}^{*},\mathrm{ad}^{*}-(-\mathrm{ad}^{*}),\mathfrak{g}^{*})=(\mathrm{ad}^{*},2\mathrm{ad}^{*},\mathfrak{g}^{*}).

Hence, there is a left-Alia algebra structure
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\mathrm{ad}^{*},2\mathrm{ad}^{*}}\mathfrak{g}^{*}

of vector spaces.

Remark 3.22
.

In
[
12
]
, there is also the notion of a

, defined as a vector space
A
together with a bilinear map

satisfying

[x,[y,z]^{\prime}]^{\prime}+[y,[z,x]^{\prime}]^{\prime}+[z,[x,y]^{\prime}]^{\prime}=[x,[z,y]^{\prime}]^{\prime}+[y,[x,z]^{\prime}]^{\prime}+[z,[y,x]^{\prime}]^{\prime},\;\forall x,y,z\in A.

(25)

It is clear that

is a right-Alia algebra if and only if the opposite algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
of

Thus, our study on left-Alia algebras can straightforwardly generalize a parallel study on right-Alia algebras.
Consequently, if
(l,r,V)

\}\supset\{

then we say
\big((A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}),(B,[\cdot,\cdot]_{B}),l_{A},r_{A},l_{B},r_{B}\big)
is a

.

.

Let

and

is a representation of

, the triple

(l_{B},r_{B},A)
is a representation of

and the following equations hold:

\displaystyle r_{B}(a)([x,y]_{A}-[y,x]_{A})

\displaystyle=(l_{B}-r_{B})(a)[y,x]_{A}+(r_{B}-l_{B})(a)[x,y]_{A}

\displaystyle+l_{B}\big((r_{A}-l_{A})(y)a\big)x+l_{B}\big((l_{A}-r_{A})(x)a\big)y,

(26)

\displaystyle r_{A}(x)([a,b]_{B}-[b,a]_{B})

\displaystyle=(l_{A}-r_{A})(x)[b,a]_{B}+(r_{A}-l_{A})(x)[a,b]_{B}

\displaystyle+l_{A}\big((r_{B}-l_{B})(b)x\big)a+l_{A}\big((l_{B}-r_{B})(a)x\big)b,

(27)

for all

.

Proof.

The proof follows from a straightforward computation. ∎

3.6.
Quadratic Left-Alia Algebras

Definition 3.25
.

A
quadratic left-Alia algebra
is a triple
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\mathcal{B})
, where
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a left-Alia algebra and
\mathcal{B}
is a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form on
A
which is invariant in the sense that

\mathcal{B}([x,y],z)=\mathcal{B}(x,[z,y]-[y,z]),\;\forall x,y,z\in A.

(28)

Remark 3.26
.

Since
\mathcal{B}
is symmetric, it follows from Definition
3.25
that

\mathcal{B}([x,y],z)+\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z])=0,\;\forall x,y,z\in A.

(29)

Lemma 3.27
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\mathcal{B})
be a quadratic left-Alia algebra.
Then,
(\mathcal{L}_{[\cdot,\cdot]},\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]},A)
and
(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]},A^{*})
are equivalent as representations of
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
.

Proof.

We set a linear isomorphism
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}:A\rightarrow A^{*}
using

\langle\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(x),y\rangle=\mathcal{B}(x,y).

(30)

Then, by (
29
) we have

\langle\mathcal{B}^{\natural}\big(\mathcal{L}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(x)y\big),z\rangle=\mathcal{B}([x,y],z)=-\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z])=-\langle\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y),[x,z]\rangle=\langle\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(x)\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y),z\rangle,

that is,
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}\big(\mathcal{L}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(x)y\big)=\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(x)\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y)
.
Similarly, by (
28
), we have
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}\big(\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(x)y\big)=\linebreak(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]})(x)\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y)
. Hence, the conclusion follows.
∎

Proposition 3.28
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a commutative associative algebra and
f:A\rightarrow A
be a linear map.
Let
\mathcal{B}
be a nondegenerate symmetric invariant bilinear form on
(A,\cdot)

and
\hat{f}:A\rightarrow A
be the adjoint map of
f
with respect to
\mathcal{B}
, given by

\mathcal{B}\big(\hat{f}(x),y\big)=\mathcal{B}\big(x,f(y)\big),\;\forall x,y\in A.

Then, there is a quadratic left-Alia algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\mathcal{B})
, where
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is the special left-Alia algebra with respect to
(A,\cdot,f,-\hat{f})
, that is,

[x,y]=x\cdot f(y)-\hat{f}(x\cdot y).

(31)

Proof.

For all
x,y,z\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}([x,y],z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\big(x\cdot f(y)-\hat{f}(x\cdot y),z\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\big(x,z\cdot f(y)-y\cdot f(z)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\Big(x,z\cdot f(y)-\hat{f}(z\cdot y)-\big(y\cdot f(z)-\hat{f}(y\cdot z)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x,[z,y]-[y,z]).

Hence, the conclusion follows.
∎

Example 3.29
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a left-Alia algebra and
(\mathcal{L}_{[\cdot,\cdot]},\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]},A)
be the adjoint representation of
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
.
By Propositions
3.18
and
3.20
, there is a left-Alia algebra
A\ltimes_{\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}}A^{*}
on
d=A\oplus A^{*}
, given by (
24
).
There is a natural nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\mathcal{B}_{d}
on
A\oplus A^{*}
, given by

\mathcal{B}_{d}(x+a^{*},y+b^{*})=\langle x,b^{*}\rangle+\langle a^{*},y\rangle,\;\forall x,y\in A,a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}.

(32)

For all
x,y,z\in A,a^{*},b^{*},c^{*}\in A^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{d}([x+a^{*},y+b^{*}]_{d},z+c^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{d}\big([x,y]+\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(x)b^{*}+(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]})(y)a^{*},z+c^{*}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle[x,y],c^{*}\rangle+\langle\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(x)b^{*}+(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]})(y)a^{*},z\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle[x,y],c^{*}\rangle-\langle[x,z],b^{*}\rangle+\langle a^{*},[z,y]-[y,z]\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{d}\big(x+a^{*},[z+c^{*},x+b^{*}]_{d}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle[z,y],a^{*}\rangle-\langle[z,x],b^{*}\rangle+\langle c^{*},[y,x]-[x,y]\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{d}\big(x+a^{*},[y+b^{*},z+c^{*}]_{d}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle[y,z],a^{*}\rangle-\langle[y,x],c^{*}\rangle+\langle b^{*},[z,x]-[x,z]\rangle.

Hence, we have

\mathcal{B}_{d}\big([x+a^{*},y+b^{*}]_{d},z+c^{*}\big)=\mathcal{B}_{d}\big(x+a^{*},[z+c^{*},y+b^{*}]_{d}-[y+b^{*},z+c^{*}]_{d}\big),

and, thus,
(A\ltimes_{\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}}A^{*},\mathcal{B}_{d})
is a quadratic left-Alia algebra.

Remark 3.30
.

By Example

We study the tensor forms of nondegenerate symmetric invariant bilinear forms on left-Alia algebras.

Definition 3.31
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a left-Alia algebra and

h:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(A\otimes A)
be a linear map given by

h(x)=(\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}-\mathcal{L}_{[\cdot,\cdot]})(x)\otimes\mathrm{id}-\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(x),\;\forall x\in A.

(33)

An element

is called
invariant
on
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
if

for all

.

Proposition 3.32
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a left-Alia algebra.
Suppose that
\mathcal{B}
is a nondegenerate bilinear form on
A
and
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}:A\rightarrow A^{*}

is the corresponding map given by (
30
).
Set
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}\in A\otimes A
using

\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{B}},a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{B}^{\natural^{-1}}(a^{*}),b^{*}\rangle,\;\forall a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}.

(34)

Then,
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\mathcal{B})
is a quadratic left-Alia algebra if and only if
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
is symmetric and invariant on
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathcal{B}
is symmetric if and only if
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
is symmetric.
Let
x,y,z\in A
and
a^{*}=\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(x),c^{*}=\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(z)
.
Under the symmetric assumption, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}([x,y],z)=\langle[x,y],\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(z)\rangle=\langle[\mathcal{B}^{\natural^{-1}}(a^{*}),y],c^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\mathcal{B}^{\natural^{-1}}(a^{*}),\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(y)c^{*}\rangle=-\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{B}},a^{*}\otimes\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(y)c^{*}\rangle=\langle\big(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(y)\big)\widetilde{\mathcal{B}},a^{*}\otimes c^{*}\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x,[z,y]-[y,z])=\langle\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(x),[z,y]-[y,z]\rangle=\langle a^{*},[\mathcal{B}^{\natural^{-1}}(c^{*}),y]-[y,\mathcal{B}^{\natural^{-1}}(c^{*})]\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]})(y)a^{*},\mathcal{B}^{\natural^{-1}}(c^{*})\rangle=\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{B}},c^{*}\otimes(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]})(y)a^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\big((\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}-\mathcal{L}_{[\cdot,\cdot]})(y)\otimes\mathrm{id}\big)\widetilde{\mathcal{B}},a^{*}\otimes c^{*}\rangle,

that is, (
28
) holds if and only if
h(y)\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}=0
for all
y\in A
.
Hence, the conclusion follows.
∎

4.
Manin Triples of Left-Alia Algebras and Left-Alia Bialgebras

In this section, we introduce the notions of Manin triples of left-Alia algebras and left-Alia bialgebras.
We show that they are equivalent structures via specific matched pairs of left-Alia algebras.

4.1.
Manin Triples of Left-Alia Algebras

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A})
and
(A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}})
be left-Alia algebras.
Assume that there is a left-Alia algebra structure
(d=A\oplus A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{d})
on
A\oplus A^{*}
which contains
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A})
and
(A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}})
as left-Alia subalgebras.
Suppose that the natural nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\mathcal{B}_{d}
, given by (
32
), is invariant on
(A\oplus A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{d})
, that is,
(A\oplus A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{d},\mathcal{B}_{d})
is a quadratic left-Alia algebra.
Then, we say that
\big((A\oplus A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{d},\mathcal{B}_{d}),A,A^{*}\big)
is a
Manin triple of left-Alia algebras
.

Recall
[
4
]
that a
double construction of commutative Frobenius algebras

\big((A\oplus A^{*},\cdot_{d}
,

\mathcal{B}_{d}),A,A^{*}\big)
is a commutative associative algebra
(A\oplus A^{*},\cdot_{d})
containing
(A,\cdot_{A})
and
(A^{*},\cdot_{A^{*}})
as commutative associative subalgebras, such that the natural nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\mathcal{B}_{d}
given by (
32
) is invariant on
(A\oplus A^{*},\cdot_{d})
.
Now, we show that double constructions of commutative Frobenius algebras with linear maps naturally give rise to Manin triples of left-Alia algebras.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\big((A\oplus A^{*},\cdot_{d},\mathcal{B}_{d}),A,A^{*}\big)
be a double construction of commutative Frobenius algebras.
Suppose that
P:A\rightarrow A
and
Q^{*}:A^{*}\rightarrow A^{*}
are linear maps.
Then, there is a Manin triple of left-Alia algebras

\big((A\oplus A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{d},\mathcal{B}_{d}),A,A^{*}\big)
given by

\displaystyle[x+a^{*},y+b^{*}]_{d}=(x+a^{*})\cdot_{d}\big(P(y)+Q^{*}(b^{*})\big)-(Q+P^{*})\big((x+a^{*})\cdot_{d}(y+b^{*})\big),

\displaystyle[x,y]_{A}=x\cdot_{A}P(y)-Q(x\cdot_{A}y),\;[a^{*},b^{*}]_{A^{*}}=a^{*}\cdot_{A^{*}}Q^{*}(b^{*})-P^{*}(a^{*}\cdot_{A^{*}}b^{*}),

for all
x,y\in A,a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}
.

Proof.

The adjoint map of
P+Q^{*}
with respect to
\mathcal{B}_{d}
is
Q+P^{*}
.
Hence, the conclusion follows from Proposition
3.28
by taking
f=P+Q^{*}
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A})
and
(A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}})
be left-Alia algebras.
Then, there is a Manin triple of left-Alia algebras
\big((A\oplus A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{d},\mathcal{B}_{d}),A,A^{*}\big)
if and only if

\big((A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}),(A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}),\mathcal{L}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}^{*},\mathcal{L}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}^{*}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}\big)

is a matched pair of left-Alia algebras.

Proof.

Let
\big((A\oplus A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{d},\mathcal{B}_{d}),A,A^{*}\big)
be a Manin triple of left-Alia algebras.
For all
x,y\in A,a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{d}([x,b^{*}]_{d},y)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:quad}}{=}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{B}(b^{*},[x,y]_{A})=-\langle b^{*},[x,y]_{A}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}(x)b^{*},y\rangle=\mathcal{B}_{d}\big(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}(x)b^{*},y\big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{d}([x,b^{*}]_{d},a^{*})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:quad}}{=}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{d}(x,[a^{*},b^{*}]_{A^{*}}-[b^{*},a^{*}]_{A^{*}})=\langle x,[a^{*},b^{*}]_{A^{*}}-[b^{*},a^{*}]_{A^{*}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}})(b^{*})x,a^{*}\rangle=\mathcal{B}_{d}\big((\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}})(b^{*})x,a^{*}\big).

Thus,

\mathcal{B}_{d}([x,b^{*}]_{d},y+a^{*})=\mathcal{B}_{d}\big((\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}})(b^{*})x+\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}(x)b^{*},y+a^{*}\big)

and, by the nondegeneracy of
\mathcal{B}_{d}
, we have

[x,b^{*}]_{d}=(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}})(b^{*})x+\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}(x)b^{*}.

Similarly,

[y,a^{*}]_{d}=(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}})(y)a^{*}+\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}(a^{*})y.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle[x+a^{*},y+b^{*}]_{d}

\displaystyle=[x,y]_{A}+\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}(a^{*})y+(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}})(b^{*})x

\displaystyle+[a^{*},b^{*}]_{A^{*}}+\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}(x)b^{*}+(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}})(y)a^{*}.

(35)

Hence,
\big((A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}),(A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}),\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}\big)
is a matched pair of left-Alia algebras.

Conversely, if
\big((A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}),(A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}),\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}\big)
is a matched pair of left-Alia algebras, then it is straightforward to check that
\mathcal{B}_{d}
is invariant on the left-Alia algebra
(A\oplus A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{d})
given by (
35
).
∎

4.2.
Left-Alia Bialgebras

Definition 4.4
.

A
left-Alia coalgebra
is a pair,
(A,\delta)
, such that
A
is a vector space and
\delta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
is a co-multiplication satisfying

(\mathrm{id}^{\otimes 3}+\xi+\xi^{2})(\tau\otimes\mathrm{id}-\mathrm{id}^{\otimes 3})(\delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\delta=0,

(36)

where
\tau(x\otimes y)=y\otimes x
and
\xi(x\otimes y\otimes z)=y\otimes z\otimes x
for all
x,y,z\in A
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
A
be a vector space and
\delta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
be a co-multiplication.
Let
[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}:A^{*}\otimes A^{*}\rightarrow A^{*}
be the linear dual of
\delta
, that is,

\langle[a^{*},b^{*}]_{A^{*}},x\rangle=\langle\delta^{*}(a^{*}\otimes b^{*}),x\rangle=\langle a^{*}\otimes b^{*},\delta(x)\rangle,\;\;\forall a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*},x\in A.

(37)

Then,
(A,\delta)
is a left-Alia coalgebra if and only if
(A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}})
is a left-Alia algebra.

Proof.

For all
x\in A,a^{*},b^{*},c^{*}\in A^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle[[a^{*},b^{*}]_{A^{*}},c^{*}]_{A^{*}}-[[b^{*},a^{*}]_{A^{*}},c^{*}]_{A^{*}},x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\delta^{*}(\delta^{*}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\mathrm{id}^{\otimes 3}-\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\otimes c^{*},x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\otimes c^{*},(\mathrm{id}^{\otimes 3}-\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})(\delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\delta(x)\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle[[b^{*},c^{*}]_{A^{*}},a^{*}]_{A^{*}}-[[c^{*},b^{*}]_{A^{*}},a^{*}]_{A^{*}},x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle b^{*}\otimes c^{*}\otimes a^{*},(\mathrm{id}^{\otimes 3}-\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})(\delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\delta(x)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\otimes c^{*},\xi^{2}(\mathrm{id}^{\otimes 3}-\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})(\delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\delta(x)\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle[[c^{*},a^{*}]_{A^{*}},b^{*}]_{A^{*}}-[[a^{*},c^{*}]_{A^{*}},b^{*}]_{A^{*}},x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle c^{*}\otimes a^{*}\otimes b^{*},(\mathrm{id}^{\otimes 3}-\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})(\delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\delta(x)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\otimes c^{*},\xi(\mathrm{id}^{\otimes 3}-\tau\otimes\mathrm{id})(\delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\delta(x)\rangle.

Hence, (
1
) holds for
(A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}})

if and only if (
36
) holds.
∎

Definition 4.6
.

A
left-Alia bialgebra
is a triple
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\delta)
, such that

(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a left-Alia algebra,
(A,\delta)
is a left-Alia coalgebra and the following equation holds:

(\tau-\mathrm{id}^{2})\big(\delta([x,y]-[y,x])+(\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(x)\otimes\mathrm{id})\delta(y)-(\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(y)\otimes\mathrm{id})\delta(x)\big)=0,\;\forall x,y\in A.

(38)

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A})
be a left-Alia algebra. Suppose that there is a left-Alia algebra structure
(A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}})
on the dual space
A^{*}
, and
\delta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
is the linear dual of
[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}
.
Then,
\big((A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}),(A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}),\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}\big)
is a matched pair of left-Alia algebras if and only if
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},\delta)
is a left-Alia bialgebra.

Proof.

For all
x,y\in A,a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}})(a^{*})([x,y]_{A}-[y,x]_{A}),b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle[x,y]_{A}-[y,x]_{A},[b^{*},a^{*}]_{A^{*}}-[a^{*},b^{*}]_{A^{*}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(\tau-\mathrm{id}^{\otimes 2})\delta([x,y]_{A}-[y,x]_{A}),a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle[\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}(a^{*})y,x]_{A},b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}(a^{*})y,\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}(x)b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle y,[\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}(x)b^{*},a^{*}]_{A^{*}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\big(\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}(x)\otimes\mathrm{id}\big)\delta(y),b^{*}\otimes a^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\tau\big(\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}(x)\otimes\mathrm{id}\big)\delta(y),a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\rangle,

\displaystyle-\langle[\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}(a^{*})y,x]_{A},b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\tau\big(\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}(y)\otimes\mathrm{id}\big)\delta(x),a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\rangle,

\displaystyle-\langle\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}\big(\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}(y)a^{*}\big)x,b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle x,[\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}(y)a^{*},b^{*}]_{A^{*}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\big(\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}(y)\otimes\mathrm{id}\big)\delta(x),a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}\big(\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}(x)a^{*})y\big),b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\big(\mathcal{R}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}(x)\otimes\mathrm{id}\big)\delta(y),a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\rangle.

Thus, (
38
) holds if and only if (
26
) holds for
l_{A}=\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}},\;r_{A}=\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}},\linebreak\;l_{B}=\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}},\;r_{B}=\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}
.
Similarly, (
38
) holds if and only if (
27
) holds for
l_{A}=\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}},\;r_{A}=\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}},\;l_{B}=\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}},\;r_{B}=\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}
.
Hence, the conclusion follows.
∎

Summarizing Theorems
4.3
and
4.7
, we have the following corollary:

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A})
be a left-Alia algebra. Suppose that there is a left-Alia algebra structure
(A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}})
on the dual space
A^{*}
, and
\delta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
is the linear dual of
[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

There is a Manin triple of left-Alia algebras
\big((d=A\oplus A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{d},\mathcal{B}_{d}),A,A^{*}\big)
.

(b)

\big((A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}),(A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}),\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}\big)
is a matched pair of left-Alia algebras.

(c)

(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},\delta)
is a left-Alia bialgebra.

Example 4.9
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A})
be the three-dimensional left-Alia algebra given in Example
3.9
.

Then, there is a left-Alia bialgebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},\delta)
with a non-zero co-multiplication
\delta
on
A
, given by

\delta(e_{1})=e_{1}\otimes e_{1}.

(39)

Then, by Corollary
4.8
, there is a Manin triple
\big((A\oplus A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot],\mathcal{B}_{d}),A,A^{*}\big)
. Here, the multiplication
[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}
on
A^{*}
is given through
\delta
by (
39
), that is,

[e^{*}_{1},e^{*}_{1}]_{A^{*}}=e^{*}_{1},

and the multiplication
[\cdot,\cdot]
on
A\oplus A^{*}
is given by (
35
).
Moreover,
\big((A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}),(A^{*},[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}),\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}
,

\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}},\mathcal{L}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}-\mathcal{R}^{*}_{[\cdot,\cdot]_{A^{*}}}\big)
is a matched pair of left-Alia algebras.

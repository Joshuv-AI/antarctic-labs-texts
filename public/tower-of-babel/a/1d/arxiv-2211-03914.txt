1.1.
Main results

To state the theorem precisely introduce the normed spaces:

L^{p,s}(\mathbb{R})
defined with

\|q\|_{L^{p,s}(\mathbb{R})}:=\|\langle x\rangle^{s}q\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})},

where
\langle x\rangle=\sqrt{1+x^{2}}
and

H^{k,s}(\mathbb{R}):=H^{k}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2,s}(\mathbb{R}).

The following theorem is our main results of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\{r(z),R(z)\}
and
\{z_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N-1}
be, respectively, the
reflection coefficient and the discrete spectrum associated with the weighted Sobolev initial data
q_{0}\in\tanh(x)+H^{4,4}(\mathbb{R})
.
Then the long-time asymptotics of the solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) for the
defocusing NLS equation in two transition regions are given by the following formulas.

Case I.

For
(x,t)\in\mathcal{P}_{-1}(x,t)
,

(1.4)

q(x,t)=e^{i\alpha(\infty)}\left(1+\tau^{-1/3}\beta(-1)\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-1/2}\right),

where

(1.5)

\displaystyle\alpha(\infty)=-2i\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\log\bar{z}_{j}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\nu(\zeta)}{\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta,\ \ \nu(\zeta)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log(1-|r(\zeta)|^{2}),

\displaystyle\beta(-1)=-\frac{i}{2}\left(u(s)e^{i\varphi_{0}}+\int_{s}^{\infty}u^{2}(\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta\right),\ \ s=\frac{8}{3}(\xi+1)\tau^{\frac{2}{3}},\ \tau=\frac{3}{4}t.

Here
\varphi_{0}=\arg(r(-1))
and
u(s)
is a solution of the Painlevé II equation

(1.6)

u^{\prime\prime}(s)=2u^{3}(s)+su(s)

with the asymptotics

(1.7)

\displaystyle u(s)\sim-|r(-1)|\rm{Ai}(s)\sim-|r(-1)|\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}s^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{2}{3}s^{3/2}},\ s\to+\infty,

where
\rm{Ai}(s)
is the classical Airy function.

Case II.

For
(x,t)\in\mathcal{P}_{+1}(x,t)
,

(1.8)

q(x,t)=e^{i\alpha(\infty)}\left(1+\tau^{-1/3}\beta(1)\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-1/2}\right),

where

(1.9)

\displaystyle\alpha(\infty)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\nu(\zeta)}{\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta,\ \ \beta(1)=\frac{i}{2}\left(u(s)e^{i\varphi_{0}}+\int_{s}^{\infty}u^{2}(\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta\right),

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}=\arg(R(1)),\ \ s=-\frac{8}{3}(\xi-1)\tau^{\frac{2}{3}},\ \tau=\frac{3}{4}t.

Here, the function
R(z)
is defined by (
4.28
) and
u(s)
is a solution of the Painlevé equation (
1.6
) with the asymptotics

(1.10)

u(s)\sim|R(1)|\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}s^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{2}{3}s^{3/2}},\ s\to+\infty.

Remark 1.2
.

Comparing Case I and Case II in Theorem
1.1
,
we find that the formulae (
1.4
) and (
1.8
) have a similar asymptotic form.
Their difference is that the discrete spectrum contributes to the phase
\alpha(\infty)
in (
1.5
),
while the reflection coefficient contributes to the phase
\alpha(\infty)
in (
1.9
).

Remark 1.3
.

In the case of the KdV and Camassa-Holm equation
[
44
,
26
,
45
]
, a given RH problem can be reduced into a local RH problem
at a phase point
z_{0}
with the nonlinear steepest method. For the non-generic case
|r(0)|\not=1
, the local RH problem
can then be analyzed and controlled by the norm

(1-|r(z_{0})|^{2})^{-1}
. While for the generic case
|r(0)|=1
,
it turns out that the norm
(1-|r(z_{0})|^{2})^{-1}
blow up as
z_{0}\to 0
. This indicates the presence of a new collisionless shock region for
|r(0)|=1
.

Remark 1.4
.

By contrast to the results in Remark
1.3
,
in the case of the focusing NLS equation, Vartanian in
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
obtained the long-time asymptotics for (
1.1
)-(
1.2
)
under the hypothesis
\|r\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}<1
including
|r(\pm 1)|<1
, which is the non-generic case.
However, for the generic case
r(\pm 1)=1
,
Cuccagna and Jerkins proposed a new way in
[

This method allows to proceed as in the similar region, whose asymptotics matches the Painlevé asymptotics in self-similar region
and thus no new shock wave asymptotic forms appear.
Because of this, the Painlevé asymptotics given in Theorem
1.1
are still effective for the case
|r(\pm 1)|=1
.

2.1.
Jost functions

The NLS equation (
1.1
) admits a Lax pair

(2.1)

\displaystyle\psi_{x}=L\psi,\quad L=L(z;x,t)=i\sigma_{3}\left(Q-\lambda\left(z\right)\right),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\psi_{t}=T\psi,\quad T=T(z;x,t)=-2\lambda(z)L+i(Q^{2}-I)\sigma_{3}+Q_{x},

where

Q=Q(x,t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\bar{q}(x,t)\\
q(x,t)&0\end{array}\right),\quad\lambda(z)=\frac{1}{2}\left(z+z^{-1}\right).

We define the Jost solutions of Lax pairs (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) with asymptotics

\psi^{\pm}(z)\sim Y_{\pm}e^{-it\theta(z)\sigma_{3}},\quad x\to\pm\infty,

where we denote
\psi^{\pm}(z):=\psi^{\pm}(z;x,t)
and

(2.3)

\displaystyle Y_{\pm}=I\pm\sigma_{1}z^{-1},\quad\text{det}Y_{\pm}=1-z^{-2},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\theta(z)=\zeta(z)\big(xt^{-1}-2\lambda(z)\big),\quad\zeta(z)=\frac{1}{2}(z-z^{-1}).

Making a transformation

(2.5)

m^{\pm}(z)=\psi^{\pm}(z)e^{it\theta(z)\sigma_{3}},

then
m^{\pm}(z)
satisfy the Volterra integral equations

\displaystyle m^{\pm}(z)=Y_{\pm}+\int_{\pm\infty}^{x}\left(Y_{\pm}e^{-i\zeta(z)(x-y)\hat{\sigma}_{3}}Y^{-1}_{\pm}\right)\left(\Delta L_{\pm}m^{\pm}(z;y)\right)\mathrm{d}y,\ z\not=\pm 1,

\displaystyle m^{\pm}(z)=Y_{\pm}+\int_{\pm\infty}^{x}\left(I+(x-y)L_{\pm}\right)\Delta L_{\pm}(\pm 1;y)m^{\pm}(\pm 1;y)\mathrm{d}y,\ z=\pm 1,

where
\Delta L_{\pm}(z;y)=i\sigma_{3}(Q\mp\sigma_{1})
.

The existence, analyticity and differentiability of

2.3.
A basic Riemann-Hilbert problem

Denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{+}=\{z_{j}|s_{11}(z_{j})=0,\ z_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{+},\ |z_{j}|=1,\ j\in H\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{-}=\{\bar{z}_{j}|s_{22}(\bar{z}_{j})=0,\ \bar{z}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{-},\ |\bar{z}_{j}|=1,\ j\in H\}.

Moreover,

(2.12)

\displaystyle s_{11}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{z-z_{j}}{z-\bar{z}_{j}}\exp\left(-i\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\nu(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\right),

where
z_{j}\in\mathcal{Z}^{+}
and

(2.13)

\displaystyle\nu(\zeta)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\left(1-|r(\zeta)|^{2}\right).

Based on the properties of the scattering data

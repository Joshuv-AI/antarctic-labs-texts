Abstract
We present an Oppenheim type determinantal inequality for positive definite block matrices. Recently, Lin [Linear Algebra Appl. 452 (2014) 1–6] proved a remarkable extension of Oppenheim type inequality for block matrices, which solved a conjecture of Günther and Klotz. There is a requirement that two matrices commute in Lin’s result. The motivation of this paper is to obtain another natural and general extension of Oppenheim type inequality for block matrices to get rid of the requirement that two matrices commute.

Oppenheim’s inequality;
Fischer’s inequality;

Introduction

We use the following standard notation.
The set of
m\times n
complex matrices is denoted by
\mathbb{M}_{m\times n}(\mathbb{C})
,
or simply by
\mathbb{M}_{m\times n}
.
When
m=n
, we write
\mathbb{M}_{n}
for
\mathbb{M}_{n\times n}
.
The identity matrix of order
n
is denoted by
I_{n}
, or
I
for short.
Given two matrices
A=[a_{ij}]
and
B=[b_{ij}]
with the same order,
the
Hadamard product
of
A
and
B
is defined as
A\circ B=[a_{ij}b_{ij}]
.
It is easy to verify that

(A\circ B)\circ{C}=A\circ(B\circ{C})
,
so the Hadamard product of
A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(m)}
could be denoted by

\prod_{i=1}^{m}\circ A^{(i)}
.
By convention, the
\mu\times\mu
leading principal submatrix of
A

is denoted by
A_{\mu}
, i.e.,
A_{\mu}=[a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{\mu}
.

Let
A=[a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
be positive semidefinite.
The Hadamard inequality says that

\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{ii}\geq\det A.

(1)

If both
A
and
B
are positive definite (semidefinite),
it is well-known that
A\circ B
is positive definite (semidefinite);
see, e.g.,
[
12
, p. 479]
.
Moreover,
the celebrated Oppenheim inequality (see
[
19
]
or
[
12
, p. 509]
) states that

\det(A\circ B)\geq\det A\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}b_{ii}\geq\det(AB).

(2)

By setting
B=I_{n}
, then (
2
) is reduced to (
1
).
Note that
A\circ B=B\circ A
, thus (
2
) also implies

\det(A\circ B)\geq\det B\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{ii}\geq\det(AB).

(3)

The following inequality (
4
) not merely generalized Oppenheim’s result,
but also presented a well connection between (
2
) and (
3
);
see
[
21
, Theorem 3.7]
or
[
12
, pp. 509–510]
for more details.

\det(A\circ B)+\det(AB)\geq\det A\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}b_{ii}+\det B\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{ii}.

(4)

Inequality (
4
) is usually called Oppenheim-Schur’s inequality.
Furthermore, Chen
[
3
]
generalized (
4
) and proved
the following implicit improvement.
If
A
and
B
are
n\times n
positive definite matrices, then

\det(A\circ B)\geq\det(AB)\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}\left(\frac{a_{\mu\mu}\det A_{\mu-1}}{\det A_{\mu}}+\frac{b_{\mu\mu}\det B_{\mu-1}}{\det B_{\mu}}-1\right),

(5)

where
A_{\mu}=[a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{\mu}
and
B_{\mu}=[b_{ij}]_{i,j}^{\mu}

for every
\mu=1,2,\ldots,n
.

The significance and applicability of Hadamard product are well known in the
literature. For example, this product is used to communication and information theory in correcting codes of satellite transmissions, signal processing and
pattern recognition, and is also
used to discrete combinatorial geometry and graph theory in
interrelations between Hadamard matrices and different combinatorial
configurations of block designs and Latin square. Applications
can also be found in statistical analysis. For more details and
applications, we refer the reader to the survey papers
[

,

]

Now, Lin’s result could be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(see
[
16
]
)
Let
{\bm{A}}=[A_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
and
{\bm{B}}=[B_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{M}_{k})

be positive definite matrices such that

of
{\bm{A}}
commutes with every
B_{rs}
of
{\bm{B}}
. Then

\displaystyle\det(\bm{A}\,\Box\,\bm{B})\geq\det(\bm{A}\bm{B})\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}\left(\frac{\det A_{\mu\mu}\det\bm{A}_{\mu-1}}{\det\bm{A}_{\mu}}+\frac{\det B_{\mu\mu}\det\bm{B}_{\mu-1}}{\det\bm{B}_{\mu}}-1\right),

where
{\bm{A}}_{\mu}=[A_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{\mu}
and
{\bm{B}}_{\mu}=[B_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{\mu}
denote
the
\mu\times\mu
leading principal block submatrices of
{\bm{A}}
and
{\bm{B}}
, respectively.

Clearly, when
k=1
, Theorem
1.1
reduces to Chen’s result (
5
).

Motivated by Theorem
1.1
,
we will give another natural and general extension of (
5
)
for positive definite block matrices.
The condition in Theorem
1.1
that
every
A_{ij}
of
{\bm{A}}
commutes with every
B_{rs}
of
{\bm{B}}
is harsh and strong when
the blocks are of order at least two.
Our extension (Theorem
1.2
) has no requirement on the commutation assumptions.
It also can be viewed as a complement of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2

Let
{\bm{A}}^{(i)}=\bigl[A^{(i)}_{rs}\bigr]_{r,s=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{M}_{k}),i=1,\ldots,m

be positive definite. Then

\displaystyle\det\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\circ\bm{A}^{(i)}\right)\geq\det\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\bm{A}^{(i)}\right)\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\det A^{(i)}_{\mu\mu}\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu-1}}{\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu}}-(m-1)\right),

where
{\bm{A}}^{(i)}_{\mu}
stands for
the
\mu\times\mu
leading principal block submatrix of
{\bm{A}}^{(i)}
.

Additionally, based on Theorem
1.2
and
a numerical inequality,
we will give the following Theorem
1.3
,
which is an extension of Oppenheim type inequality (
4
).

Theorem 1.3

Let
{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{M}_{k}),i=1,2,\ldots,m
be positive semidefinite. Then

\displaystyle\det\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)+(m-1)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\det{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{m}\det{\bm{A}}^{(j)}\cdot\prod_{\mu=1}^{n}\det A^{(i)}_{\mu\mu}.

For two Hermitian matrices

A/A_{11}:=A_{22}-A_{21}A_{11}^{-1}A_{12}

It is obvious that
\det(A/A_{11})=(\det A)/(\det A_{11})

Lemma 2.1

Let
A=[a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
and
B=[b_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
be positive definite matrices. Then

\frac{\det(A_{p}\circ B_{p})}{\det(A_{p-1}\circ B_{p-1})}+\frac{\det(A_{p}B_{p})}{\det(A_{p-1}B_{p-1})}\geq\frac{a_{pp}\det B_{p}}{\det B_{p-1}}+\frac{b_{pp}\det A_{p}}{\det A_{p-1}},

where
A_{p}=[a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{p}
and
B_{p}=[b_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{p}

for every
p=1,2,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

First, we denote
\alpha:=[a_{1p},\ldots,a_{p-1,p}]^{T}
and

\beta:=[b_{1p},\ldots,b_{p-1,p}]^{T}
. Setting

\widehat{A_{p}}:=\begin{bmatrix}A_{p-1}&\alpha\\
{\alpha}^{*}&a_{pp}-\frac{\det A_{p}}{\det A_{p-1}}\end{bmatrix},\quad\widehat{B_{p}}:=\begin{bmatrix}B_{p-1}&\beta\\
{\beta}^{*}&b_{pp}-\frac{\det B_{p}}{\det B_{p-1}}\end{bmatrix}.

It is easy to see that both
\widehat{A_{p}}
and
\widehat{B_{p}}
are singular positive semidefinite,
then
\widehat{A_{p}}\circ\widehat{B_{p}}
is positive semidefinite. By taking determinant, it follows that

\det(\widehat{A_{p}}\circ\widehat{B_{p}})=\det\begin{bmatrix}A_{p-1}\circ B_{p-1}&\alpha\circ\beta\\
\alpha^{*}\circ{\beta}^{*}&\left(a_{pp}-\frac{\det A_{p}}{\det A_{p-1}}\right)\!\left(b_{pp}-\frac{\det B_{p}}{\det B_{p-1}}\right)\end{bmatrix}\geq 0.

By a direct computation, we get

\det(A_{p}\circ B_{p})+\det(A_{p-1}\circ B_{p-1})\left(-\frac{a_{pp}\det B_{p}}{\det B_{p-1}}-\frac{b_{pp}\det A_{p}}{\det A_{p-1}}+\frac{\det(A_{p}B_{p})}{\det(A_{p-1}B_{p-1})}\right)\geq 0.

This completes the proof.

Proposition 2.4

Let
{\bm{A}}=[A_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
and
{\bm{B}}=[B_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
be positive definite. Then

\displaystyle\det\bigl(({\bm{A}}_{\mu}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu})/({\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})\bigr)+\det\bigl(({\bm{A}}_{\mu}/{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1})\circ({\bm{B}}_{\mu}/{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad\geq\det\bigl(A_{\mu\mu}\circ({\bm{B}}_{\mu}/{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})\bigr)+\det\bigl(B_{\mu\mu}\circ({\bm{A}}_{\mu}/{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1})\bigr),

where
{\bm{A}}_{\mu}=[A_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{\mu}
and
{\bm{B}}_{\mu}=[B_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{\mu}

for every
\mu=1,2,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

We first denote
\bm{X}:=\begin{bmatrix}A_{1\mu}\\
\vdots\\
A_{\mu-1,\mu}\end{bmatrix}
,

\bm{Y}:=\begin{bmatrix}B_{1\mu}\\
\vdots\\
B_{\mu-1,\mu}\end{bmatrix}
and define

\widehat{{\bm{A}}_{\mu}}:=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}&\bm{X}\\
\bm{X}^{*}&\bm{X}^{*}\bm{A}_{\mu-1}^{-1}\bm{X}\end{bmatrix},\quad\widehat{{\bm{B}}_{\mu}}:=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1}&\bm{Y}\\
\bm{Y}^{*}&\bm{Y}^{*}\bm{B}_{\mu-1}^{-1}\bm{Y}\end{bmatrix}.

It is easy to see by computing Schur complement that
\widehat{{\bm{A}}_{\mu}}

and
\widehat{{\bm{B}}_{\mu}}
are singular positive semidefinite.
Therefore
\widehat{{\bm{A}}_{\mu}}\circ\widehat{{\bm{B}}_{\mu}}
is positive semidefinite
and so

(\bm{X}^{*}\bm{A}_{\mu-1}^{-1}\bm{X})\circ(\bm{Y}^{*}\bm{B}_{\mu-1}^{-1}\bm{Y})\geq(\bm{X}^{*}\circ\bm{Y}^{*})({\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})^{-1}(\bm{X}\circ\bm{Y}),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\bigl(A_{\mu\mu}-(\bm{A}_{\mu}/{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1})\bigr)\circ\bigl(B_{\mu\mu}-(\bm{B}_{\mu}/{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad\geq A_{\mu\mu}\circ B_{\mu\mu}-({\bm{A}}_{\mu}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu})/({\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1}).

Expanding the above inequality gives

\displaystyle({\bm{A}}_{\mu}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu})/({\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})+({\bm{A}}_{\mu}/{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1})\circ({\bm{B}}_{\mu}/{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})

(6)

\displaystyle\geq A_{\mu\mu}\circ({\bm{B}}_{\mu}/{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})+B_{\mu\mu}\circ({\bm{A}}_{\mu}/{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}).

On the other hand, since
B_{\mu\mu}\geq{\bm{B}}_{\mu}/{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1}
, then
by (
6
), we have

\displaystyle({\bm{A}}_{\mu}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu})/({\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})-A_{\mu\mu}\circ({\bm{B}}_{\mu}/{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})

\displaystyle\geq B_{\mu\mu}\circ({\bm{A}}_{\mu}/{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1})-({\bm{A}}_{\mu}/{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1})\circ({\bm{B}}_{\mu}/{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})

\displaystyle=(B_{\mu\mu}-{\bm{B}}_{\mu}/{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})\circ({\bm{A}}_{\mu}/{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1})\geq 0.

Therefore,

({\bm{A}}_{\mu}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu})/({\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})\geq A_{\mu\mu}\circ({\bm{B}}_{\mu}/{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})\geq({\bm{A}}_{\mu}/{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1})\circ({\bm{B}}_{\mu}/{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1}).

(7)

Similarly, we could obtain

({\bm{A}}_{\mu}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu})/({\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})\geq B_{\mu\mu}\circ({\bm{A}}_{\mu}/{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1})\geq({\bm{A}}_{\mu}/{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1})\circ({\bm{B}}_{\mu}/{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1}).

(8)

Lemma 2.6

Let
{\bm{A}}=[A_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
and
{\bm{B}}=[B_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
be positive definite. Then

\frac{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu})}{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})}+\frac{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu}{\bm{B}}_{\mu})}{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})}\geq\frac{\det A_{\mu\mu}\det{\bm{B}}_{\mu}}{\det{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1}}+\frac{\det B_{\mu\mu}\det{\bm{A}}_{\mu}}{\det{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}},

where
{\bm{A}}_{\mu}=[A_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{\mu}
and
{\bm{B}}_{\mu}=[B_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{\mu}

for every
\mu=1,2,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

We first can see from (
7
) and (
8
) that

\frac{\det(A_{p}\circ B_{p})}{\det(A_{p-1}\circ B_{p-1})}\geq\frac{a_{pp}\det B_{p}}{\det B_{p-1}}\geq\frac{\det(A_{p}B_{p})}{\det(A_{p-1}B_{p-1})},

and

\frac{\det(A_{p}\circ B_{p})}{\det(A_{p-1}\circ B_{p-1})}\geq\frac{b_{pp}\det A_{p}}{\det A_{p-1}}\geq\frac{\det(A_{p}B_{p})}{\det(A_{p-1}B_{p-1})}.

By Lemma
2.1
and Corollary
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{p=(\mu-1)k+1}^{\mu k}\frac{\det(A_{p}\circ B_{p})}{\det(A_{p-1}\circ B_{p-1})}+\prod_{p=(\mu-1)k+1}^{\mu k}\frac{\det(A_{p}B_{p})}{\det(A_{p-1}B_{p-1})}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\geq\prod_{p=(\mu-1)k+1}^{\mu k}\frac{a_{pp}\det B_{p}}{\det B_{p-1}}+\prod_{p=(\mu-1)k+1}^{\mu k}\frac{b_{pp}\det A_{p}}{\det A_{p-1}}.

Note that
A_{\mu k}=[a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{\mu k}=[A_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{\mu}={\bm{A}}_{\mu}
,
then the above inequality could be written as

\displaystyle\frac{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu})}{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})}+\frac{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu}{\bm{B}}_{\mu})}{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\left(\prod_{p=(\mu-1)k+1}^{\mu k}a_{pp}\right)\frac{\det{\bm{B}}_{\mu}}{\det{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1}}+\left(\prod_{p=(\mu-1)k+1}^{\mu k}b_{pp}\right)\frac{\det{\bm{A}}_{\mu}}{\det{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}},

Theorem 2.7

Let
{\bm{A}}=[A_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
and
{\bm{B}}=[B_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
be positive definite. Then

\displaystyle\det(\bm{A}\circ\bm{B})\geq\det(\bm{A}\bm{B})\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}\left(\frac{\det A_{\mu\mu}\det\bm{A}_{\mu-1}}{\det\bm{A}_{\mu}}+\frac{\det B_{\mu\mu}\det\bm{B}_{\mu-1}}{\det\bm{B}_{\mu}}-1\right),

where
{\bm{A}}_{\mu}=[A_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{\mu}
and
{\bm{B}}_{\mu}=[B_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{\mu}

for every
\mu=1,2,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.6
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu})}{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu}{\bm{B}}_{\mu})}{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\frac{\det A_{\mu\mu}\det{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}}{\det{\bm{A}}_{\mu}}+\frac{\det B_{\mu\mu}\det{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1}}{\det{\bm{B}}_{\mu}}-1\right).

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}\frac{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu})}{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}\circ{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})}

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}\frac{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu}{\bm{B}}_{\mu})}{\det({\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\frac{\det A_{\mu\mu}\det{\bm{A}}_{\mu-1}}{\det{\bm{A}}_{\mu}}+\frac{\det B_{\mu\mu}\det{\bm{B}}_{\mu-1}}{\det{\bm{B}}_{\mu}}-1\right).

Note that Oppenheim’s inequality (
2
) leads to

\det({\bm{A}}_{1}\circ{\bm{B}}_{1})\geq\det({\bm{A}}_{1}{\bm{B}}_{1}).

Thus, the required inequality
now immediately follows.

m-1
, that is

\displaystyle\det\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)\geq\det\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{\det A^{(i)}_{\mu\mu}\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu-1}}{\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu}}-(m-2)\right).

Now we consider the case
m>2
. Then we have

\displaystyle\det\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)

\displaystyle=\det\left(\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\Bigr)\circ{\bm{A}}^{(m)}\right)~~~\hbox{(by Theorem \ref{thm26})}

\displaystyle\geq\det\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\Bigr)\left(\det{\bm{A}}^{(m)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}\left(\frac{\det\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\!\!\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)_{\!\!\mu\mu}\!\!\!\det\Bigl(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\!\!\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\Bigr)_{\!\!\mu\!-\!1}}{\det\Bigl(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\!\!\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\Bigr)_{\!\!\mu}}+\frac{\det A^{(m)}_{\mu\mu}\det{\bm{A}}^{(m)}_{\mu-1}}{\det{\bm{A}}^{(m)}_{\mu}}-1\right)

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\det{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)\times\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{\det A^{(i)}_{\mu\mu}\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu-1}}{\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu}}-(m-2)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}\left(\frac{\det\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\!\!\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)_{\!\!\mu\mu}\!\!\!\det\Bigl(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\!\!\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\Bigr)_{\!\!\mu\!-\!1}}{\det\Bigl(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\!\!\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\Bigr)_{\!\!\mu}}+\frac{\det A^{(m)}_{\mu\mu}\det{\bm{A}}^{(m)}_{\mu-1}}{\det{\bm{A}}^{(m)}_{\mu}}-1\right).

For notational convenience, we denote

R_{\mu}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{\det A^{(i)}_{\mu\mu}\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu-1}}{\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu}}-(m-2),

and

S_{\mu}:=\frac{\det\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\!\!\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)_{\!\!\mu\mu}\!\!\det\Bigl(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\!\!\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\Bigr)_{\!\!\mu-1}}{\det\Bigl(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\!\!\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\Bigr)_{\!\!\mu}}+\frac{\det A^{(m)}_{\mu\mu}\det{\bm{A}}^{(m)}_{\mu-1}}{\det{\bm{A}}^{(m)}_{\mu}}-1.

By Fischer’s inequality (Lemma
2.5
), we can see that

\det A^{(i)}_{\mu\mu}\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu-1}\geq\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,m,

which leads to

R_{\mu}=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{\det A^{(i)}_{\mu\mu}\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu-1}}{\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu}}-(m-2)\geq 1.

(9)

On the other hand, by Fischer’s inequality (Lemma
2.5
) again, we have

\displaystyle\det\Bigl(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\!\!\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\Bigr)_{\!\!\mu\mu}\det\Bigl(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\!\!\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\Bigr)_{\!\!\mu-1}\geq\det\Bigl(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\!\!\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\Bigr)_{\!\!\mu}.

Therefore, we obtain

S_{\mu}\geq\frac{\det A^{(m)}_{\mu\mu}\det{\bm{A}}^{(m)}_{\mu-1}}{\det{\bm{A}}^{(m)}_{\mu}}\geq 1.

(10)

Since
R_{\mu}\geq 1
and
S_{\mu}\geq 1
, this leads to

R_{\mu}S_{\mu}\geq R_{\mu}+S_{\mu}-1.

Hence, we get from (
9
) and (
10
) that

\displaystyle\det\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\det{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}R_{\mu}\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}S_{\mu}

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\det{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}(R_{\mu}+S_{\mu}-1)

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\det{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\det A^{(i)}_{\mu\mu}\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu-1}}{\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu}}-(m-1)\right).

This completes the proof.

At the end of this paper, we are going to prove Theorem
1.3
.
First, we need to introduce a numerical inequality,
which could be found in
[
6
]
.
For completeness, we here include a proof for the convenience.

Lemma 3.1

If
\bigl(a^{(i)}_{1},a^{(i)}_{2},\ldots,a^{(i)}_{n}\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}^{n},i=1,\ldots,m
and

a_{\mu}^{(i)}\geq 1
for all
i
and
\mu
, then

\prod_{\mu=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a^{(i)}_{\mu}-(m-1)\right)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{\mu=1}^{n}a^{(i)}_{\mu}-(m-1).

Proof.

We apply induction on
n
.
When
n=1
, there is nothing to show.
Suppose that the required inequality is true for
n=k
. Then we consider the case
n=k+1
,

\displaystyle\prod_{\mu=1}^{k+1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a^{(i)}_{\mu}-(m-1)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a^{(i)}_{k+1}-(m-1)\right)\cdot\prod_{\mu=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a^{(i)}_{\mu}-(m-1)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a^{(i)}_{k+1}-(m-1)\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{\mu=1}^{k}a^{(i)}_{\mu}-(m-1)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(a_{k+1}^{(i)}-1\right)\!\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\prod_{\mu=1}^{k}a_{\mu}^{(j)}-(m-1)\biggr)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{\mu=1}^{k}a_{\mu}^{(i)}-(m-1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(a_{k+1}^{(i)}-1\right)\!\biggl(\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{m}\prod_{\mu=1}^{k}a_{\mu}^{(j)}-(m-1)\biggr)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(a_{k+1}^{(i)}-1\right)\prod_{\mu=1}^{k}a_{\mu}^{(i)}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{\mu=1}^{k}a_{\mu}^{(i)}-(m-1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(a_{k+1}^{(i)}-1\right)\!\biggl(\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{m}\prod_{\mu=1}^{k}a_{\mu}^{(j)}-(m-1)\biggr)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{\mu=1}^{k+1}a_{\mu}^{(i)}-(m-1)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{\mu=1}^{k+1}a_{\mu}^{(i)}-(m-1).

Thus, the required inequality holds for
n=k+1
, so the proof of induction step is complete.

Remark.
When
m=2
, Lemma
3.1
implies that for every
a_{\mu},b_{\mu}\geq 1
, then

\prod_{\mu=1}^{n}(a_{\mu}+b_{\mu}-1)\geq\prod_{\mu=1}^{n}a_{\mu}+\prod_{\mu=1}^{n}b_{\mu}-1.

(11)

This inequality (
11
) plays an important role in
[
16
]

11
) that Chen’s result (
5
)
is indeed an improvement of (
4
).
On the other hand, (
11
) could be obtained from Corollary
2.3
.
The above proof of Lemma
3.1
is by induction on
n
.
In fact, combining (
11
) and by induction on
m
,
one could get another way to prove Lemma
3.1
.

Now, we are ready to present a proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Without loss of generality,
we may assume by a standard perturbation argument that all
{\bm{A}}^{(i)}
are positive definite.
Thus, the required inequality could be rewritten as

\det\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)\geq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\det{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\prod_{\mu=1}^{n}\det A^{(i)}_{\mu\mu}}{\det\bm{A}^{(i)}}-(m-1)\right).

(12)

By Fischer’s inequality (Lemma
2.5
), we have

\det A^{(i)}_{\mu\mu}\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu-1}\geq\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu}.

Therefore, it follows from Theorem
1.2
and Lemma
3.1
that

\displaystyle\det\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\circ{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\det{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\det A^{(i)}_{\mu\mu}\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu-1}}{\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu}}-(m-1)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\det{\bm{A}}^{(i)}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}\frac{\det A^{(i)}_{\mu\mu}\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu-1}}{\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu}}-(m-1)\right).

Observe that

\prod_{\mu=2}^{n}\frac{\det A^{(i)}_{\mu\mu}\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu-1}}{\det\bm{A}^{(i)}_{\mu}}=\frac{\prod_{\mu=1}^{n}\det A^{(i)}_{\mu\mu}}{\det\bm{A}^{(i)}}.

Hence, the proof of (
12
) is complete.

Abstract
. This is the third in a series of papers on standard monomial theory and invariant theory of arc spaces. For any algebraically closed field K K , we prove the arc space analogue of the first and second fundamental theorems of invariant theory for the special linear group. This is more subtle than the results for the general linear and symplectic groups obtained in the first two papers because the arc space of the corresponding affine quotients can be nonreduced.



1.
Introduction

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field,
G
be an algebraic group over
K
, and
W
be a finite-dimensional
G
-module over
K
. A basic problem of classical invariant theory is to describe the ring of invariant polynomial functions
K[W]^{G}
. One also considers
K[V]^{G}
, where
V=W^{\oplus p}\bigoplus{W^{*}}^{\oplus q}
is the direct sum of
p
copies of
W
and
q
copies of the dual
G
-module
W^{*}
. According to Weyl’s terminology
[
19
]
, a first fundamental theorem of invariant theory (FFT) for the pair
(G,W)
is a generating set for
K[V]^{G}
, and a second fundamental theorem (SFT) for
(G,W)
is a generating set for the ideal of relations among these generators.

In this paper,
G
will be the special linear group
SL_{h}(K)
over
K
, and
W=K^{\oplus h}
will be its standard module. Then
V=W^{\oplus p}\bigoplus{W^{*}}^{\oplus q}
has affine coordinate ring

K[V]=K[a^{(0)}_{il},b^{(0)}_{jl}|\ 0\leq i\leq p,\ 1\leq j\leq p,\ 1\leq l\leq h].

Theorem 1.1
.

(FFT and SFT for
SL_{h}(K)
and
W=K^{\oplus h}
)
The invariant ring
K[V]^{SL_{h}(K)}
is generated by

(1.1)

\begin{split}&X^{(0)}_{ij}=\sum_{l=1}^{h}a^{(0)}_{il}b^{(0)}_{jl},\quad\quad 1\leq i\leq p,\qquad 1\leq j\leq q;\\
&Y^{(0)}_{u_{1},\dots,u_{h}}=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{u_{1}1}^{(0)}&a_{u_{1}2}^{(0)}&\cdots&a_{u_{1}h}^{(0)}\\
a_{u_{2}1}^{(0)}&a_{u_{2}2}^{(0)}&\cdots&a_{u_{2}h}^{(0)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
a_{u_{h}1}^{(0)}&a_{u_{h}2}^{(0)}&\cdots&a_{u_{h}h}^{(0)}\\
\end{array}\right|,\quad\quad Z^{(0)}_{v_{1},\dots,v_{h}}=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}b_{v_{1}1}^{(0)}&b_{v_{1}2}^{(0)}&\cdots&b_{v_{1}h}^{(0)}\\
b_{v_{2}1}^{(0)}&b_{v_{2}2}^{(0)}&\cdots&b_{v_{2}h}^{(0)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
b_{v_{h}1}^{(0)}&b_{v_{h}2}^{(0)}&\cdots&b_{v_{h}h}^{(0)}\\
\end{array}\right|,\\
&1\leq u_{1}<u_{2}<\cdots<u_{h}\leq p,\quad\quad\quad 1\leq v_{1}<v_{2}<\cdots<v_{h}\leq q.\end{split}

Let

Y^{(0)}_{u,u,u_{3},\dots,u_{h}}=0=Z^{(0)}_{v,v,v_{3}\dots,v_{h}}.
For a permutation
\sigma
of
1,2,\dots,h
, let

Y^{(0)}_{u_{\sigma(1)},\dots,u_{\sigma(h)}}=\sign(\sigma)Y^{(0)}_{u_{1},\dots,u_{h}},\quad Z^{(0)}_{v_{\sigma(1)},\dots,v_{\sigma(h)}}=\sign(\sigma)Z^{(0)}_{v_{1},\dots,v_{h}};

The ideal of relations among the generators is
I_{h}^{+}
generated by

(1.2)

\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}X^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{1}}&X^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{2}}&\cdots&X^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{h}}\\
X^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{1}}&X^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{2}}&\cdots&X^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{h}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
X^{(0)}_{u_{h}v_{1}}&X^{(0)}_{u_{h}v_{2}}&\cdots&X^{(0)}_{u_{h}v_{h}}\\
\end{array}\right|-Y^{(0)}_{u_{1},\dots,u_{h}}Z^{(0)}_{v_{1},\dots,v_{h}},

(1.3)

\sum_{i=0}^{h}(-1)^{i}X^{(0)}_{u_{i}v}Y^{(0)}_{u_{0},\dots,u_{i-1},u_{i+1},\dots,u_{h}},\quad\sum_{j=0}^{h}(-1)^{j}X^{(0)}_{uv_{j}}Z^{(0)}_{v_{0},\dots,v_{j-1},v_{j+1},\dots,v_{h}},

(1.4)

\sum_{i=1}^{h}(-1)^{i}Y^{(0)}_{u^{\prime}_{h},\dots,u^{\prime}_{2},u_{i}}Y^{(0)}_{u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1},u^{\prime}_{1},u_{i+1},\dots,u_{h}},\quad\sum_{j=1}^{h}(-1)^{j}Z^{(0)}_{v^{\prime}_{h},\dots,v^{\prime}_{2},v_{j}}Z^{(0)}_{v_{1},\dots,v_{j-1},v^{\prime}_{1},v_{j+1},\dots,v_{h}}.

In the case
K=\mathbb{C}
, these theorems are classical and were proven by Weyl
[
19
]
. In the general case, the FFT is due to de Concini and Procesi
[
3
]
, and the SFT is due to Lakshmibai and Shukla
[
9
]
; see also the book
[
8
]
of Lakshmibai and Raghavan for a uniform approach to these theorems for all the classical groups based on standard monomial theory.

Standard monomial theory

Let

X^{(0)}_{u_{1},\dots,u_{r};v_{1},\dots,v_{r}}=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}X^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{1}}&X^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{2}}&\cdots&X^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{r}}\\
X^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{1}}&X^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{2}}&\cdots&X^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{r}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
X^{(0)}_{u_{r}v_{1}}&X^{(0)}_{u_{r}v_{2}}&\cdots&X^{(0)}_{u_{r}v_{r}}\\
\end{array}\right|.

There is a partial order for the set

\{Y^{(0)}_{u_{1},\dots,u_{h}},Z^{(0)}_{v_{1},\dots,v_{h}},X^{(0)}_{u_{1},\dots,u_{r};v_{1},\dots,v_{r}}|\ r\leq h,u_{i}<u_{i+1},v_{j}<v_{j+1}\}

given by

(1)

Y^{(0)}_{u_{1},\dots,u_{h}}\leq Y^{(0)}_{u^{\prime}_{1},\dots,u^{\prime}_{h}}
if
u_{i}\leq u^{\prime}_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq h
;

(2)

Z^{(0)}_{v_{1},\dots,v_{h}}\leq Z^{(0)}_{v^{\prime}_{1},\dots,v^{\prime}_{h}}
if
u_{i}\leq u^{\prime}_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq h
;

(3)

Y^{(0)}_{u_{1},\dots,u_{h}}\leq X^{(0)}_{u^{\prime}_{1},\dots,u^{\prime}_{r};v^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,v^{\prime}_{r}}
if
u_{i}\leq u^{\prime}_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq r
;

(4)

Z^{(0)}_{v_{1},\dots,v_{h}}\leq X^{(0)}_{u^{\prime}_{1},\dots,u^{\prime}_{r};v^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,v^{\prime}_{r}}
if
v_{i}\leq v^{\prime}_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq r
;

(5)

X^{(0)}_{u_{1},\dots,u_{r};v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}}\leq X^{(0)}_{u^{\prime}_{1},\dots,u^{\prime}_{s};v^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,v^{\prime}_{s}}
if
s\leq r
,
u_{i}\leq u^{\prime}_{i}
and
v_{i}\leq v^{\prime}_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq s
.

The invariant ring
K[V]^{SL_{h}(K)}
has a standard monomial basis (chapter 11 in
[
8
]
) with respect to this partially ordered set: the ordered products
A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{k}
of the elements
A_{i}
in the partial order set with
A_{i}\leq A_{i+1}
form a basis of
K[V]^{SL_{h}(K)}
. This is needed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
in
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
.

Arc spaces

Our main goal is to prove the arc space analogue of Theorem
1.1
. Recall that for a scheme
X
of finite type over
K
, the arc space
J_{\infty}(X)
is defined as the inverse limit of the finite jet schemes
J_{n}(X)

[
4
]
. It is generally of infinite type, and is determined by its functor of points by Corollary 1.2 of
[
1
]
. For every
K
-algebra
A
, we have a bijection

\Hom(\Spec A,J_{\infty}(X))\cong\Hom(\Spec A[[t]],X).

If
f:X\to Y
is a morphism of schemes, there is an induced morphism of schemes
f_{\infty}:J_{\infty}(X)\to J_{\infty}(Y)
.

If
\text{char}\ K=0
,
J_{\infty}(X)
is irreducible whenever
X
is irreducible, by a theorem of Kolchin
[
6
]
. However, even if
X
is irreducible and reduced,
J_{\infty}(X)
need not be reduced. An ideal
\mathcal{I}\subset K[J_{\infty}(X)]
is said to be
differentially finitely generated
if there exists a finite subset
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\}\subset\mathcal{I}
such that all normalized derivatives
{\bar{\partial}}^{i}f_{j}
lie in
\mathcal{I}
, and
\mathcal{I}
is generated by
\{{\bar{\partial}}^{i}f_{j}|\ j=1,\dots,m,\ i\geq 0\}
. If
\text{char}\ K=0
, the nilradical
\mathcal{N}\subset K[J_{\infty}(X)]
is always a differential ideal, that is,
\partial\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{N}
. The question of when
\mathcal{N}
is differentially finitely generated has been raised by several authors
[
2
,
17
,
18
,
7
]
. There are examples, such as the homogeneous coordinate ring of the flag varieties in
[
5
]
[
14
]
, but little is known about this question in general. In our main examples, we will give an explicit differential finite generating set for
\mathcal{N}
when
\text{char}\ K=0
.

Invariant theory for arc spaces

Let
G
be an algebraic group over
K
and let
V
be a finite-dimensional
G
-module. Then
J_{\infty}(G)
is again an algebraic group, and there is an induced action of
J_{\infty}(G)
on
J_{\infty}(V)
.
The quotient morphism
V\to V/\!\!/G
induces a morphism
J_{\infty}(V)\to J_{\infty}(V/\!\!/G)
, so we have a morphism

(1.5)

J_{\infty}(V)/\!\!/J_{\infty}(G)\to J_{\infty}(V/\!\!/G).

In particular, we have a ring homomorphism

(1.6)

K[J_{\infty}(V/\!\!/G)]\rightarrow K[J_{\infty}(V)]^{J_{\infty}(G)}.

In the case
K=\mathbb{C}
, if
V/\!\!/G
is smooth or a complete intersection, it was shown in
[
13
]
that under mild hypotheses, (
1.6
) is an isomorphism, although in general it is neither injective nor surjective.

As above, let
W=K^{\oplus h}
be the standard representation of
SL_{h}(K)
and let
V=W^{\oplus p}\bigoplus{W^{*}}^{\oplus q}
. Then
J_{\infty}(V)
has affine coordinate ring

(1.7)

K[J_{\infty}(V)]=K[a^{(k)}_{il},b^{(k)}_{jl}|\ 1\leq i\leq p,\ 1\leq j\leq q,\ 1\leq l\leq h,\ k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}],

which has an induced action of
J_{\infty}(SL_{h}(K))
.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

K[J_{\infty}(V)]^{J_{\infty}({SL}_{h}(K))}
be the ring of invariants under the action of
J_{\infty}(SL_{h}(K))
on
K[J_{\infty}(V)]
.

(1)

K[J_{\infty}(V)]^{J_{\infty}({SL}_{h}(K))}
is generated by

\displaystyle{\bar{\partial}}^{k}X^{(0)}_{ij},\quad{\bar{\partial}}^{k}Y^{(0)}_{u_{1},\dots,u_{h}},\quad{\bar{\partial}}^{k}Z^{(0)}_{v_{1},\dots,v_{h}},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

with
1\leq i\leq p,\ 1\leq j\leq q,\ 1\leq u_{1}<u_{2}<\cdots<u_{h}\leq p,\ 1\leq v_{1}<v_{2}<\cdots<v_{h}\leq q
.

(2)

The ideal of relations among the generators in (1) is generated by

(1.8)

{\bar{\partial}}^{n}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}X^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{1}}&X^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{2}}&\cdots&X^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{h}}\\
X^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{1}}&X^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{2}}&\cdots&X^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{h}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
X^{(0)}_{u_{h}v_{1}}&X^{(0)}_{u_{h}v_{2}}&\cdots&X^{(0)}_{u_{h}v_{h}}\\
\end{array}\right|-\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\bar{\partial}}^{k}Y^{(0)}_{u_{1},\dots,u_{h}}{\bar{\partial}}^{n-k}Z^{(0)}_{v_{1},\dots,v_{h}},\qquad n\geq 0,

(1.9)

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{h}(-1)^{i}{\bar{\partial}}^{k}X^{(0)}_{u_{i}v}{\bar{\partial}}^{n-k}Y^{(0)}_{u_{0},\dots,u_{i-1},u_{i+1},\dots,u_{h}},\qquad n\geq 0,\\
&\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{h}(-1)^{j}{\bar{\partial}}^{k}X^{(0)}_{uv_{j}}{\bar{\partial}}^{n-k}Z^{(0)}_{v_{0},\dots,v_{j-1},v_{j+1},\dots,v_{h}},\qquad n\geq 0,\end{split}

and for any
0\leq l<i\leq h
,

(1.10)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{\sigma}C_{k}^{l}\frac{\sign(\sigma)}{h!i!}{\bar{\partial}}^{n-k}Y^{(0)}_{u^{\prime}_{h},\dots,u^{\prime}_{i+1},u_{\sigma(h+i)},\dots,u_{\sigma(h+1)}}{\bar{\partial}}^{k}Y^{(0)}_{u_{\sigma(h)},\dots,u_{\sigma(1)}};

(1.11)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{\sigma}C_{k}^{l}\frac{\sign(\sigma)}{h!i!}{\bar{\partial}}^{n-k}Z^{(0)}_{v^{\prime}_{h},\dots,v^{\prime}_{i+1},v_{\sigma(h+i)},\dots,v_{\sigma(h+1)}}{\bar{\partial}}^{k}Z^{(0)}_{v_{\sigma(h)},\dots,v_{\sigma(1)}}.

Here the summations are over all permutations
\sigma
of
1,2,\dots,h+i
, and

(1.12)

C_{k}^{l}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{k!}{l!(k-l)!},&0\leq l\leq k,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(3)

There is a standard monomial basis for
K[J_{\infty}(V)]^{J_{\infty}(SL_{h}(K))}
.

This generalizes the results in Section 4 of
[
13
]
, which deal with the following special cases for
K=\mathbb{C}
.

(1)

p<n
and
q\leq n
, or
p=n
and
q<n
, so that
V/\!\!/SL_{h}(\mathbb{C})
is an affine space,

(2)

p=n=q
, so that
V/\!\!/SL_{h}(\mathbb{C})
is a hypersurface,

(3)

p=n+1
and
q<n
, or
p<n
and
q=n+1
, so that
V/\!\!/SL_{h}(\mathbb{C})
is a complete intersection.

The first statement in Theorem
1.2
shows that the map (
1.6
) is surjective.
Relations (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) are obtained from relations (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), and relations (
1.10
) and (
1.11
) for
l=0

are obtained from relations (
1.4
), by taking
{\bar{\partial}}^{n}
. However, relations (
1.10
) and (
1.11
) for
0<l<h
are not consequences of (
1.2
), (
1.3
), and (
1.4
), and their derivatives. Therefore (
1.6
) is not injective if
\text{max}(p,q)-2>h
. We will see later that when
\text{char}\ K=0
, the nilradical
\mathcal{N}
in the affine coordinate ring
K[J_{\infty}(V/\!\!/SL_{h}(K)]
coincides with the kernel of (
1.6
). Therefore
\mathcal{N}
is generated by the relations (
1.10
) and (
1.11
) in the case
n=0
and
0<l<h
, together with their normalized derivatives; in particular,
\mathcal{N}
is differentially finitely generated. When
p=0
or
q=0
, only the relations (
1.10
) and (
1.11
) appear respectively; they are the semi-infinite Plücker relations in
[
5
]
for the semi-infinite Grassmannians.

The main idea in the proof is to extend the standard monomial basis for
K[V]^{SL_{h}(K)}
to a standard monomial basis for
K[J_{\infty}(V)]^{J_{\infty}({SL}_{h}(K))}
. In Section
2
, we will introduce an integral form of this ring which we denote by
\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{h}
. It is the quotient of the polynomial ring over
\mathbb{Z}
generated by
x^{(k)}_{ij}
,
{\bar{\partial}}^{k}(u_{h},\dots,u_{2},u_{1}|
, and
{\bar{\partial}}^{k}|v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{h})
, by the ideal generated by the relations (
1.8
)-(
1.11
), where we replace the variables
{\bar{\partial}}^{k}X^{(0)}_{ij}
,
{\bar{\partial}}^{k}Y^{(0)}_{u_{1},\dots,u_{h}}
, and
{\bar{\partial}}^{k}Z^{(0)}_{v_{1},\dots,v_{h}}
with
x^{(k)}_{ij}
,
{\bar{\partial}}^{k}(u_{h},\dots,u_{2},u_{1}|
and
{\bar{\partial}}^{k}|v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{h})
, respectively. We will define the set of standard monomials in
\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{h}
, and show (modulo the technical Lemma
2.9
, whose proof will be given in Section
6
), that the standard monomials span
\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{h}
over
\mathbb{Z}
.

In Section
3
, we will prove that the standard monomials form a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{h}
; see Theorem
3.3
. The proof makes use of a ring
\mathfrak{B}
which is an integral form of
K[J_{\infty}(V)]
, i.e.,
K[J_{\infty}(V)]\cong\mathfrak{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}K
, and a homomorphism
Q^{+}_{h}:\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{B}
which we show is injective. In Section
4
, we will show that after tensoring with
K
, the image of
Q^{+}_{h}
can be identified with
K[J_{\infty}(V)]^{J_{\infty}({SL}_{h}(K))}
; see Theorem
4.2
. We will also show that
\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{h}
is the quotient of a larger ring
\tilde{\mathfrak{R}}^{+}_{h}
which is an integral form of
K[J_{\infty}(V/\!\!/SL_{h}(K)]
. So after tensoring with
K
, the composition of the maps
\tilde{\mathfrak{R}}^{+}_{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{h}
and
Q^{+}_{h}:\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{B}
is exactly the natural map
K[J_{\infty}(V/\!\!/SL_{h}(K)]\rightarrow K[J_{\infty}(V)]^{J_{\infty}({SL}_{h}(K))}
, which proves Theorem
1.2
. Finally, Section
5
is devoted to establishing some technical properties of standard monomials which are needed in the proof of Lemma
2.9
.

Theorem
1.2
has significant applications to vertex algebras which we develop in
[
11
,
12
]
. First, it allows a complete description of certain cosets of affine vertex algebras inside free field algebras that are related to the classical Howe pairs. These cosets appear in some new level-rank dualities involving affine vertex superalgebras. Next, for any smooth manifold
X
in either the algebraic, complex analytic or smooth settings, the
chiral de Rham complex

\Omega^{\text{ch}}_{X}
is a sheaf of vertex algebras on
X
. It was introduced by Malikov, Schechtman and Vaintrob in
[
15
]
, and has attracted considerable attention in both physics and mathematics. Theorem
1.2
is essential in the description of the vertex algebra of global sections
\Gamma(X,\Omega^{\text{ch}}_{X})
for a
d
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold
X
with holonomy group
SU(d)
. This algebra turns out to be isomorphic to the algebra with central charge
3d
constructed by Odake in
[
16
]
. It is an important building block in the structure of
\Gamma(X,\Omega^{\text{ch}}_{X})
for an arbitrary compact Ricci-flat Kähler manifold
X
.

2.
Standard monomials

A ring and its derivation

Fix integers
p,q\geq 1
, and let

\mathfrak{R}=\mathfrak{R}^{p,q}=\mathbb{Z}[x^{(k)}_{ij}|\ 1\leq i\leq p,\ 1\leq j\leq q,\ k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}].

This is related to the affine coordinate ring of the arc space of the space
M_{p,q}=M_{p,q}(K)
of
p\times q
matrices over a field
K
as follow:
K[J_{\infty}(M_{p,q})]\cong\mathfrak{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}K
.

For
h>0
, let
\mathcal{J}_{h}^{L}
be the set of expressions

(2.1)

J^{L}={\bar{\partial}}^{k}(u_{h},\dots,u_{2},u_{1}|,\quad\text{with }1\leq u_{1}<u_{2}<\cdots<u_{h}\leq p,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

and
\mathcal{J}_{h}^{R}
be the set of expressions

(2.2)

J^{R}={\bar{\partial}}^{k}|v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{h}),\quad\text{with }1\leq v_{1}<v_{2}<\cdots<v_{h}\leq q,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

We call
wt(J^{L})=wt(J^{R})=k
the weights of
J^{L}
and
J^{R}
.

For a permutation
\sigma
of
1,2,\dots,h
, let

{\bar{\partial}}^{k}|u_{\sigma(h)},\dots,u_{\sigma(2)},u_{\sigma(1)})=\sign(\sigma)J^{L},\quad{\bar{\partial}}^{k}|v_{\sigma(1)},v_{\sigma(2)},\dots,v_{\sigma(h)})=\sign(\sigma)J^{R}.

If there is
1\leq i<j\leq h
with
u_{i}=u_{j}
, let
{\bar{\partial}}^{k}(u_{h},\dots,u_{2},u_{1}|=0
.
If there is
1\leq i<j\leq h
with
v_{i}=v_{j}
, let

{\bar{\partial}}^{k}|v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{h})=0
.

There is a derivation
\partial
on the polynomial ring
\mathfrak{R}^{+}=\mathfrak{R}[\mathcal{J}_{h}^{L}\cup\mathcal{J}_{h}^{R}]
given by

\displaystyle\partial x^{(k)}_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+1)x^{(k+1)}_{ij},

\displaystyle\partial\,{\bar{\partial}}^{k}(u_{h},\dots,u_{2},u_{1}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+1){\bar{\partial}}^{k+1}(u_{h},\dots,u_{2},u_{1}|,

\displaystyle\partial\,{\bar{\partial}}^{k}|v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+1){\bar{\partial}}^{k+1}|v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{h}).

Let
{\bar{\partial}}^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\partial^{k}
be the normalized
k
-derivation. The following propositions are easy to verify.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
a,b\in\mathfrak{R}^{+}
,

{\bar{\partial}}^{l}(ab)=\sum_{i=0}^{l}{\bar{\partial}}^{i}a\,{\bar{\partial}}^{l-i}b

and
{\bar{\partial}}^{l}a\in\mathfrak{R}^{+}
.

Let

(2.3)

B=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}x^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{1}}&x^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{2}}&\cdots&x^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{h}}\\
x^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{1}}&x^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{2}}&\cdots&x^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{h}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x^{(0)}_{u_{h}v_{1}}&x^{(0)}_{u_{h}v_{2}}&\cdots&x^{(0)}_{u_{h}v_{h}}\\
\end{array}\right|.

Proposition 2.2
.

(2.4)

{\bar{\partial}}^{n}B=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}+\cdots+n_{h}=n\\
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\sigma}\sign(\sigma)x^{(n_{1})}_{u_{1}v_{\sigma(1)}}x^{(n_{2})}_{u_{2}v_{\sigma(2)}}\cdots x^{(n_{h})}_{u_{h}v_{\sigma(h)}}.

Let

(2.5)

\mathfrak{R}^{+}_{h}=\mathfrak{R}^{+}/\mathcal{I}_{h}^{+},

where
\mathcal{I}_{h}^{+}
is the ideal generated by

(2.6)

{\bar{\partial}}^{n}B-{\bar{\partial}}^{n}({\bar{\partial}}^{0}(u_{h},\dots,u_{2},u_{1}|\,\,\,{\bar{\partial}}^{0}|v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{h}))=0.

(2.7)

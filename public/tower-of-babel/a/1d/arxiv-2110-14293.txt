Virtual Artin groups
Paolo Bellingeri, Luis Paris, Anne-Laure Thiel

Abstract
Starting from the observation that the standard presentation of a virtual braid group mixes the standard presentation of the corresponding braid group with the standard presentation of the corresponding symmetric group and some mixed relations that mimic the action of the symmetric group on its root system, we define a virtual Artin group VA ⁡ [ Γ ] {\rm VA}[\Gamma] of a Coxeter graph Γ \Gamma mixing the standard presentation of the Artin group A ⁡ [ Γ ] A[\Gamma] with the standard presentation of the Coxeter group W ⁡ [ Γ ] W[\Gamma] and some mixed relations that mimic the action of W ⁡ [ Γ ] W[\Gamma] on its root system. By definition we have two epimorphisms π K : VA ⁡ [ Γ ] → W ⁡ [ Γ ] \pi_{K}:{\rm VA}[\Gamma]\to W[\Gamma] and π P : VA ⁡ [ Γ ] → W ⁡ [ Γ ] \pi_{P}:{\rm VA}[\Gamma]\to W[\Gamma] whose kernels are denoted by KVA ⁡ [ Γ ] {\rm KVA}[\Gamma] and PVA ⁡ [ Γ ] {\rm PVA}[\Gamma] respectively. We calculate presentations for these two subgroups. In particular KVA ⁡ [ Γ ] {\rm KVA}[\Gamma] is an Artin group. We prove that the center of any virtual Artin group is trivial. In the case where Γ \Gamma is of spherical type or of affine type, we show that each free of infinity parabolic subgroup of KVA ⁡ [ Γ ] {\rm KVA}[\Gamma] is also of spherical type or of affine type, and we show that VA ⁡ [ Γ ] {\rm VA}[\Gamma] has a solution to the word problem. In the case where Γ \Gamma is of spherical type we show that KVA ⁡ [ Γ ] {\rm KVA}[\Gamma] satisfies the K ⁡ ( π , 1 ) K(\pi,1) conjecture and we infer the cohomological dimension of KVA ⁡ [ Γ ] {\rm KVA}[\Gamma] and the virtual cohomological dimension of VA ⁡ [ Γ ] {\rm VA}[\Gamma] . In the case where Γ \Gamma is of affine type we determine upper bounds for the cohomological dimension of KVA ⁡ [ Γ ] {\rm KVA}[\Gamma] and for the virtual cohomological dimension of VA ⁡ [ Γ ] {\rm VA}[\Gamma] .

Introduction

Virtual braid groups were introduced by Kauffman
[
25
]
in his founding paper on the theory of virtual knots and links.
They play the same role for virtual links as the braid groups for classical links.
We know by Kamada
[
24
]
and Vershinin
[
35
]
that the virtual braid group on
n
strands, denoted
{\rm VB}_{n}
, admits a presentation with generators
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n-1},\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n-1}
, and relations:

(v1)

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}
for
|i-j|\geq 2
,
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}
for
|i-j|=1
,

(v2)

\tau_{i}\tau_{j}=\tau_{j}\tau_{i}
for
|i-j|\geq 2
,
\tau_{i}\tau_{j}\tau_{i}=\tau_{j}\tau_{i}\tau_{j}
for
|i-j|=1
,
\tau_{i}^{2}=1
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
,

(v3)

\tau_{j}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i}\tau_{j}
for
|i-j|\geq 2
,
\tau_{i}\tau_{j}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j}\tau_{i}\tau_{j}
for
|i-j|=1
.

The relations (v1) are the relations of Artin’s presentation for the braid group
B_{n}
, and they determine an embedding of
B_{n}
into
{\rm VB}_{n}
.
The relations (v2) are the relations of the Coxeter type presentation of the symmetric group
S_{n}
, and they determine an embedding of
S_{n}
into
{\rm VB}_{n}
.
As for the relations (v3), called
mixed relations
, they mimic the action of the symmetric group on its associated root system.
This situation extends naturally to all Artin groups and Coxeter groups, which explains the forthcoming definition of virtual Artin group.

A
Coxeter matrix
on a countable set
S
is a symmetric square matrix
M=(m_{s,t})_{s,t\in S}
, indexed by the elements of
S
, with entries in
\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, satisfying
m_{s,s}=1
for all
s\in S
and
m_{s,t}=m_{t,s}\geq 2
for all
s,t\in S
,
s\neq t
.
Such a matrix is usually represented by a labeled graph
\Gamma
, called
Coxeter graph
, defined as follows.
The set
S
is the set of vertices of
\Gamma
.
Two vertices
s,t
are joined by an edge if
m_{s,t}\geq 3
, and this edge is labeled with
m_{s,t}
if
m_{s,t}\geq 4
.
We say that
\Gamma
(or
M
) is
finite
if
S
is finite.
In most of the studies on Coxeter groups and/or Artin groups the Coxeter graph is assumed to be finite.
Here we will not assume that
\Gamma
is necessarily finite since, from a given Coxeter graph
\Gamma
, we will construct another Coxeter graph
\hat{\Gamma}
which is not finite in general, even when
\Gamma
is.
However, we will always assume the set of vertices to be countable.

If
a,b
are two letters and
m
is an integer
\geq 2
, then we denote by
{\rm Prod}_{L}(a,b,m)
the word
aba\cdots
of length
m
and by
{\rm Prod}_{R}(b,a,m)
the word
\cdots aba
of length
m
.
If
a,b
are two elements of a group
G
, then we denote again by
{\rm Prod}_{L}(a,b,m)
(resp.
{\rm Prod}_{R}(b,a,m)
) the element of
G
represented by
{\rm Prod}_{L}(a,b,m)
(resp.
{\rm Prod}_{R}(b,a,m)
).
Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph and let
M=(m_{s,t})_{s,t\in S}
be its Coxeter matrix.
The
Artin group
associated with
\Gamma
, denoted
A[\Gamma]
, is the group defined by the following presentation:

A[\Gamma]=\langle S\mid{\rm Prod}_{R}(t,s,m_{s,t})={\rm Prod}_{R}(s,t,m_{s,t})\text{ for }s,t\in S\,,\ s\neq t\,,\ m_{s,t}\neq\infty\rangle\,.

The
Coxeter group
associated with
\Gamma
, denoted
W[\Gamma]
, is the quotient of
A[\Gamma]
by the relations
s^{2}=1
,
s\in S
.

Definition
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph and let
M=(m_{s,t})_{s,t\in S}
be its Coxeter matrix.
Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma_{s}\mid s\in S\}
and
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau_{s}\mid s\in S\}
be two abstract sets in one-to-one correspondence with
S
.
The
virtual Artin group
associated with
\Gamma
, denoted
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
, is the group defined by the presentation with generating set
\mathcal{S}\sqcup\mathcal{T}
and relations:

(v1)

{\rm Prod}_{R}(\sigma_{t},\sigma_{s},m_{s,t})={\rm Prod}_{R}(\sigma_{s},\sigma_{t},m_{s,t})
for
s,t\in S
,
s\neq t
and
m_{s,t}\neq\infty
;

(v2)

{\rm Prod}_{R}(\tau_{t},\tau_{s},m_{s,t})={\rm Prod}_{R}(\tau_{s},\tau_{t},m_{s,t})
for
s,t\in S
,
s\neq t
and
m_{s,t}\neq\infty
;
\tau_{s}^{2}=1
for
s\in S
;

(v3)

{\rm Prod}_{R}(\tau_{s},\tau_{t},m_{s,t}-1)\,\sigma_{s}=\sigma_{r}\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(\tau_{s},\tau_{t},m_{s,t}-1)
, where
r=s
if
m_{s,t}
is even and
r=t
if
m_{s,t}
is odd, for
s,t\in S
,
s\neq t
and
m_{s,t}\neq\infty
.

Like for virtual braid groups, the relations (v1) are the relations that define the Artin group
A[\Gamma]
, and we have a natural homomorphism
\iota_{A}:A[\Gamma]\to{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
which sends
s
to
\sigma_{s}
for all
s\in S
.
On the other hand, the relations (v2) are the relations that define the Coxeter group
W[\Gamma]
, and we have a natural homomorphism
\iota_{W}:W[\Gamma]\to{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
which sends
s
to
\tau_{s}
for all
s\in S
.
We will see in Section
2
that these two homomorphisms are injective.
Finally, the relations (v3) mimic the action of
W[\Gamma]
on its root system (see Lemma
2.2
).

Artin groups were introduced in the early 1970s by Brieskorn
[
9
]
, Brieskorn–Saito
[
10
]
and Deligne
[
17
]
as natural generalizations of braid groups.
This generalization goes beyond the presentations themselves since a large part of the theory of braid groups extends to some Artin groups, especially those of spherical type, and a large part of the study of braid groups is now made in this more general framework.
We expect that the same phenomenon will hold for virtual Artin groups viewed as generalizations of virtual braid groups.

Notice that the third author
[
33
]
proposed a notion of virtual braid group of type
B_{n}
by extending the diagrammatic description provided by tom Dieck for Artin groups of type
B

and which turns out to be a quotient of the virtual Artin group of type
B_{n}
defined above.

The virtual braid group on
n
strands
{\rm VB}_{n}
admits two surjective homomorphisms onto the symmetric group
S_{n}
,
\pi_{K}:{\rm VB}_{n}\to S_{n}
and
\pi_{P}:{\rm VB}_{n}\to S_{n}
.
Actually, when
n\geq 5
, by Bellingeri–Paris
[
6
]
, these are the only surjective homomorphisms from
{\rm VB}_{n}
onto
S_{n}
up to automorphism of
S_{n}
.
The kernel of
\pi_{P}
is called the
pure virtual braid group
and it is denoted by
{\rm PVB}_{n}
, and the kernel of
\pi_{K}
is called the
kure virtual braid group
and it is denoted by
{\rm KVB}_{n}
.
The homomorphisms
\pi_{K}
and
\pi_{P}
admit a common section
\iota_{n}:S_{n}\to{\rm VB}_{n}
, hence we have the semi-direct product decompositions
{\rm VB}_{n}={\rm KVB}_{n}\rtimes S_{n}
and
{\rm VB}_{n}={\rm PVB}_{n}\rtimes S_{n}
.
A presentation for
{\rm PVB}_{n}
was calculated by Bardakov
[
1
]
.
Furthermore,
{\rm PVB}_{n}
coincides with a group studied by Bartholdi–Enriquez–Etingof–Rains
[
3
]
in relation with the Yang–Baxter equations.
By Bartholdi–Enriquez–Etingof–Rains
[
3
]
,
{\rm PVB}_{n}
admits a finite classifying space, that is,
{\rm PVB}_{n}
is of type F, which implies also that it is torsion free.
On the other hand, the group
{\rm KVB}_{n}
is an Artin group (see Rabenda
[
31
]
and Bardakov–Bellingeri
[
2
]
) and it is quite well-understood thanks to Godelle–Paris
[
23
]
.
In particular
{\rm KVB}_{n}
is also of type F, therefore it is torsion free and it has a solution to the word problem.
Since
{\rm KVB}_{n}
is of finite index in
{\rm VB}_{n}
, we can therefore use
{\rm KVB}_{n}
to solve the word problem in
{\rm VB}_{n}
(see Bellingeri–Cisneros-de-la-Cruz–Paris
[
5
]
).

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph.
Like for virtual braid groups, we have two surjective homomorphisms
\pi_{P}:{\rm VA}[\Gamma]\to W[\Gamma]
and
\pi_{K}:{\rm VA}[\Gamma]\to W[\Gamma]
that admit a common section
\iota_{W}:W[\Gamma]\to{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
.
Let
{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]
denote the kernel of
\pi_{P}
and
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
denote the kernel of
\pi_{K}
.
Then we have semi-direct product decompositions
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]={\rm PVA}[\Gamma]\rtimes W[\Gamma]
and
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]={\rm KVA}[\Gamma]\rtimes W[\Gamma]
.
In a first step we compute presentations for
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
and
{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]
(see Theorem
2.3
and Theorem
2.6
).
These presentations are generalizations of the presentations of
{\rm PVB}_{n}
and
{\rm KVB}_{n}
found by Bardakov
[
1
]
, Rabenda
[
31
]
and Bardakov–Bellingeri
[
2
]
, and provide a new insight into these groups.
For example
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
as well as
{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]
has a generating set naturally in one-to-one correspondence with the root system of
W[\Gamma]
, and the action of
W[\Gamma]
on
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
and/or
{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]
is given by the action of
W[\Gamma]
on its root system.
As a first application we show that the natural homomorphism
\iota_{A}:A[\Gamma]\to{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
which sends
s
to
\sigma_{s}
for all
s\in S
is injective (see Corollary
2.4
).
Note that the homomorphism
\iota_{W}:W[\Gamma]\to{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
is necessarily injective since it is a section of both
\pi_{P}
and
\pi_{K}
(see Proposition
2.1
).

From Theorem
2.3
it follows that
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
is an Artin group whose Coxeter graph, denoted
\hat{\Gamma}
, is determined by the root system of
W[\Gamma]
.
We use this in the rest of the paper.
In Section
3
we prove that the center of
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
is trivial whatever
\Gamma
is (see Corollary
3.4
).
This result is rather surprising since determining the center of all Artin groups is an open question.
In Sections
4
,
5
and
6
we assume that the Coxeter graph
\Gamma
is of spherical type or of affine type.
There is a general principle which says that, if one understands the free of infinity parabolic subgroups of a given Artin group, then we can understand the Artin group itself (see Godelle–Paris
[
22
]
).
The aim of Section
4
is to show that we can apply this principle to
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
when
\Gamma
is of spherical type or of affine type (see Theorem
4.1
).
In particular we show that each free of infinity parabolic subgroup of
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
is of spherical type or of affine type and its spherical dimension is bounded above by the spherical dimension of
A[\Gamma]
.
The definition of “spherical dimension” of an Artin group is given in the beginning of Section
4
.
In the good cases, that include Artin groups of spherical type and of affine type, it coincides with the cohomological dimension of the group.
In Section
5
we show that, if
\Gamma
is a Coxeter graph of spherical type or of affine type, then
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
has a solution to the word problem (see Theorem
5.1
).
Section
6
concerns some (co)homological properties of
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
and
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
when
\Gamma
is of spherical type or of affine type.
We show that, if
\Gamma
is of spherical type, then
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
satisfies the so-called
K(\pi,1)
conjecture (see Theorem
6.3
), hence it is of type F, its cohomological dimension is finite, and it is torsion free (see Corollary
6.5
).
It also follows that the virtual cohomological dimension of
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
is equal to the number of vertices of
\Gamma
.
For the case where
\Gamma
is of affine type we show that the cohomological dimension of
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
and the virtual cohomological dimension of
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
are bounded above (see Theorem
6.6
).

In order to avoid possible misunderstandings let us finish this section with a caveat. The term ”virtual” has different meanings in this text: a virtual Artin group is called in such way because
it is a natural extension of the virtual braid group
{\rm VB}_{n}
, while ”virtual cohomogical dimension” and ”virtually torsion free” are referred to combinatorial properties of finite index subgroups
of virtual Artin groups. However, when
\Gamma
is of spherical type, the fact that
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
is an Artin group and that
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]={\rm KVA}[\Gamma]\rtimes W[\Gamma]

can be rephrased saying that the virtual Artin group
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
is virtually an Artin group.

The groups
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
and
{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph and let
M=(m_{s,t})_{s,t\in S}
be its Coxeter matrix.
Recall that
\Gamma
is not assumed to be finite in the sense that the set of vertices of
\Gamma
can be a countable infinite set.
For
X\subset S
we set
M_{X}=(m_{s,t})_{s,t\in X}
and we denote by
\Gamma_{X}
the Coxeter graph of
M_{X}
.
Note that
\Gamma_{X}
is the full Coxeter subgraph of
\Gamma
spanned by
X
.
We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{\rm fin}(S)
the set of finite subsets of
S
.
If
X,Y\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm fin}(S)
are such that
X\subset Y
, then, by Van der Lek
[
27
, Lemma 4.11]
and Bourbaki
[
8
, Corollaire 1, Page 19]
, the homomorphisms
A[\Gamma_{X}]\to A[\Gamma_{Y}]
and
W[\Gamma_{X}]\to W[\Gamma_{Y}]
induced by the inclusion are injective.
It follows that
A[\Gamma]
and
W[\Gamma]
are the following direct limits:

A[\Gamma]=\varinjlim_{X\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm fin}(S)}A[\Gamma_{X}]\text{ and }W[\Gamma]=\varinjlim_{X\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm fin}(S)}W[\Gamma_{X}]\,.

So, most of the results on Artin and Coxeter groups that we will use in this paper, although proved within the framework of finite Coxeter graphs, extend immediately to Coxeter graphs with countable many vertices.
We will point out when and where this is not the case.

We see in the presentations of
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
and
W[\Gamma]
that there are surjective homomorphisms
\pi_{K}:{\rm VA}[\Gamma]\to W[\Gamma]
and
\pi_{P}:{\rm VA}[\Gamma]\to W[\Gamma]
defined by:

\displaystyle\pi_{K}(\sigma_{s})=1\text{ and }\pi_{K}(\tau_{s})=s\text{ for all }s\in S\,,

\displaystyle\pi_{P}(\sigma_{s})=\pi_{P}(\tau_{s})=s\text{ for all }s\in S\,.

We set
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]={\rm Ker}(\pi_{K})
and
{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]={\rm Ker}(\pi_{P})
.
Recall the homomorphism
\iota_{W}:W[\Gamma]\to{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
which sends
s
to
\tau_{s}
for all
s\in S
.
Observe that
\pi_{K}\circ\iota_{W}={\rm id}
and
\pi_{P}\circ\iota_{W}={\rm id}
, hence
\iota_{W}
is a section of both epimorphisms.
So:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph.
The homomorphism
\iota_{W}:W[\Gamma]\to{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
is injective and we have the following semi-direct product decompositions:

{\rm VA}[\Gamma]={\rm KVA}[\Gamma]\rtimes W[\Gamma]\text{ and }{\rm VA}[\Gamma]={\rm PVA}[\Gamma]\rtimes W[\Gamma]\,.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph and let
M=(m_{s,t})_{s,t\in S}
be its Coxeter matrix.
Let
\Pi=\{\alpha_{s}\mid s\in S\}
be an abstract set in one-to-one correspondence with
S
.
The elements of
\Pi
are called
simple roots
.
Let
V
be the real vector space having
\Pi
as a basis and let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:V\times V\to\mathbb{R}
be the symmetric bilinear form, called the
canonical bilinear form
, defined by:

\langle\alpha_{s},\alpha_{t}\rangle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-2\cos(\pi/m_{s,t})&\text{if }m_{s,t}\neq\infty\,,\\
-2&\text{if }m_{s,t}=\infty\,.\end{array}\right.

There is a faithful linear representation
W[\Gamma]\hookrightarrow{\rm GL}(V)
, called the
canonical linear representation
of
W[\Gamma]
, preserving the bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, and defined by:

s(v)=v-\langle v,\alpha_{s}\rangle\alpha_{s}\,,\quad\text{for }v\in V\,,\ s\in S\,.

We will assume
W[\Gamma]
to be embedded into
{\rm GL}(V)
via this linear representation.
We set
\Phi[\Gamma]=\{w(\alpha_{s})\mid s\in S\text{ and }w\in W[\Gamma]\}
, and we denote by
\Phi^{+}[\Gamma]
(resp.
\Phi^{-}[\Gamma]
) the set of elements
\beta\in\Phi[\Gamma]
that can be written
\beta=\sum_{s\in S}\lambda_{s}\alpha_{s}
with
\lambda_{s}\geq 0
(resp.
\lambda_{s}\leq 0
) for all
s\in S
.
By Deodhar
[
16
]
we have the disjoint union
\Phi[\Gamma]=\Phi^{+}[\Gamma]\sqcup\Phi^{-}[\Gamma]
and
\Phi^{-}[\Gamma]=\{-\beta\mid\beta\in\Phi^{+}[\Gamma]\}
.
The set
\Phi[\Gamma]
is called the
root system
of
\Gamma
and the elements of
\Phi^{+}[\Gamma]
(resp.
\Phi^{-}[\Gamma]
) are called
positive roots
(resp.
negative roots
).

For each
\beta\in\Phi[\Gamma]
we denote by
r_{\beta}:V\to V
the linear reflection defined by
r_{\beta}(v)=v-\langle v,\beta\rangle\beta
.
Note that, if
s\in S
and
w\in W[\Gamma]
are such that
\beta=w(\alpha_{s})
, then
r_{\beta}=wsw^{-1}
.
So
r_{\beta}\in W[\Gamma]
for all
\beta\in\Phi[\Gamma]
and
r_{\alpha_{s}}=s
for all
s\in S
.
Note also that
r_{\beta}=r_{-\beta}
for all
\beta\in\Phi[\Gamma]
.

We denote by
\cdot
the action of
W[\Gamma]
on
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
(resp.
{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]
).
In other words, for
w\in W[\Gamma]
and
g\in{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
(resp.
g\in{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]
), we set
w\cdot g=\iota_{W}(w)\,g\,\iota_{W}(w)^{-1}
.
The following lemma gives its real meaning to the remark “the relations (v3) mimic the action of
W[\Gamma]
on the root system of
W[\Gamma]
” made in the introduction.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph and let
M=(m_{s,t})_{s,t\in S}
be its Coxeter matrix.
Let
u,v\in W[\Gamma]
and
s,t\in S
.
If
u(\alpha_{s})=v(\alpha_{t})
, then
u\cdot\sigma_{s}=v\cdot\sigma_{t}
(in
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
) and
u\cdot(\tau_{s}\sigma_{s})=v\cdot(\tau_{t}\sigma_{t})
(in
{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]
).

Proof.

We prove the equality
u\cdot\sigma_{s}=v\cdot\sigma_{t}
.
The other equality can be proved in the same way.
We denote by
\lg:W[\Gamma]\to\mathbb{N}
the word length with respect to
S
.
The proof is based on the following result which can be found in Deodhar
[
16
]
.

Claim 1.

(1)

Let
w\in W[\Gamma]
and
s\in S
.
We have
w(\alpha_{s})\in\Phi^{+}[\Gamma]
if and only if
\lg(ws)=\lg(w)+1
.

(2)

Let
w\in W[\Gamma]
and
s,t\in S
,
s\neq t
, such that
\lg(ws)=\lg(wt)=\lg(w)-1
.
Then
m_{s,t}\neq\infty
and
\lg(w\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(t,s,m_{s,t})^{-1})=\lg(w)-m_{s,t}
.

(3)

Let
s,t\in S
,
s\neq t
, such that
m_{s,t}\neq\infty
.
Then
{\rm Prod}_{R}(s,t,m_{s,t}-1)(\alpha_{s})=\alpha_{r}
, where
r=s
if
m_{s,t}
is even and
r=t
if
m_{s,t}
is odd.

Let
u,v\in W[\Gamma]
and
s,t\in S
such that
u(\alpha_{s})=v(\alpha_{t})
.
Upon replacing
u
with
v^{-1}u
, we may assume that
v=1
, that is,
u(\alpha_{s})=\alpha_{t}
.
We show that
u\cdot\sigma_{s}=\sigma_{t}
by induction on
\lg(u)
.
If
\lg(u)=0
, then
\alpha_{s}=\alpha_{t}
, hence
s=t
, and therefore
\sigma_{s}=\sigma_{t}
.
So, we can assume that

and that the induction hypothesis holds.
Since

, hence
us=tu
.
Moreover,

(by Claim 1).
Set

.
We have

\lg(us)=\lg(u)+1
and
\lg(ur)=\lg(u)-1
.
Moreover,
\lg(ws)=\lg(wr)=\lg(w)-1
hence, by Claim 1,
m_{r,s}\neq\infty
and
\lg(w^{\prime})=\lg(w)-m_{r,s}
, where
w^{\prime}=w\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(s,r,m_{r,s})^{-1}
.
We set
x=s
if
m_{r,s}
is even and
x=r
if
m_{r,s}
is odd.
Since
w=us=w^{\prime}\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(s,r,m_{r,s}-1)\,s
, we have
u=w^{\prime}\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(s,r,m_{r,s}-1)
, hence, by Claim 1,

\alpha_{t}=u(\alpha_{s})=w^{\prime}\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(s,r,m_{r,s}-1)(\alpha_{s})=w^{\prime}(\alpha_{x})\,.

Since
\lg(w^{\prime})=\lg(w)-m_{r,s}\leq\lg(w)-2<\lg(w)-1=\lg(u)

.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle{\rm Prod}_{R}(s,r,m_{r,s}-1)\cdot\sigma_{s}={\rm Prod}_{R}(\tau_{s},\tau_{r},m_{r,s}-1)\,\sigma_{s}\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(\tau_{s},\tau_{r},m_{r,s}-1)^{-1}=

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(\tau_{s},\tau_{r},m_{r,s}-1)\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(\tau_{s},\tau_{r},m_{r,s}-1)^{-1}=\sigma_{x}\,,

hence:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph.
Let

.
Let
m=\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma}
and let
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}\in\Phi[\Gamma]
be the roots such that

Then
Z(\beta,\gamma,\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma})
is the reverse word of
Z(\gamma,\beta,\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma})
, that is,
Z(\beta,\gamma,\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma})=\hat{\zeta}_{\beta_{1}}\hat{\zeta}_{\beta_{2}}\cdots\hat{\zeta}_{\beta_{m}}

Proof.

We set
\beta_{1}=\beta
,
\gamma_{1}=\gamma
and, for
k\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\beta_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\gamma},r_{\beta},k-1)(\gamma)&\text{if }k\text{ is even}\,,\\
{\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\beta},r_{\gamma},k-1)(\beta)&\text{if }k\text{ is odd}\,,\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\beta},r_{\gamma},k-1)(\beta)&\text{if }k\text{ is even}\,,\\
{\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\gamma},r_{\beta},k-1)(\gamma)&\text{if }k\text{ is odd}\,.\end{array}\right.

We have:

Z(\gamma,\beta,\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma})=\hat{\zeta}_{\beta_{m}}\cdots\hat{\zeta}_{\beta_{2}}\hat{\zeta}_{\beta_{1}}\text{ and }Z(\beta,\gamma,\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma})=\hat{\zeta}_{\gamma_{m}}\cdots\hat{\zeta}_{\gamma_{2}}\hat{\zeta}_{\gamma_{1}}\,.

We need to show that
\beta_{k}=\gamma_{m+1-k}
for all
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.
We assume that
m
is even.
The case where
m
is odd can be proved in the same way.
Recall that, by Deodhar
[
16
]
, we have:

\gamma={\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\gamma},r_{\beta},m-1)(\gamma)\text{ and }\beta={\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\beta},r_{\gamma},m-1)(\beta)\,.

If
k
is even, then
m+1-k
is odd, hence:

\displaystyle\gamma_{m+1-k}={\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\gamma},r_{\beta},m-k)(\gamma)={\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\gamma},r_{\beta},m-k)\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\gamma},r_{\beta},m-1)(\gamma)=

\displaystyle{\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\gamma},r_{\beta},m-k)\,{\rm Prod}_{L}(r_{\beta},r_{\gamma},m-1)(\gamma)={\rm Prod}_{L}(r_{\beta},r_{\gamma},k-1)(\gamma)=

\displaystyle{\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\gamma},r_{\beta},k-1)(\gamma)=\beta_{k}\,.

If
k
is odd, then
m+1-k
is even, hence:

\displaystyle\gamma_{m+1-k}={\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\beta},r_{\gamma},m-k)(\beta)={\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\beta},r_{\gamma},m-k)\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\beta},r_{\gamma},m-1)(\beta)=

\displaystyle{\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\beta},r_{\gamma},m-k)\,{\rm Prod}_{L}(r_{\gamma},r_{\beta},m-1)(\beta)={\rm Prod}_{L}(r_{\beta},r_{\gamma},k-1)(\beta)=

\displaystyle{\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\beta},r_{\gamma},k-1)(\beta)=\beta_{k}\,.\qquad\proved

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph.
Then the map
\{\hat{\zeta}_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\Phi[\Gamma]\}\to\{\zeta_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\Phi[\Gamma]\}
,
\hat{\zeta}_{\beta}\mapsto\zeta_{\beta}
, induces an isomorphism
\varphi:\widehat{{\rm PVA}}[\Gamma]\to{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
2.3
.
One can easily show that
\{\zeta_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\Phi[\Gamma]\}
generates
{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]
in the same way as we shown that
\{\delta_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\Phi[\Gamma]\}
generates
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
in the proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Now, we show that we have a homomorphism
\varphi:\widehat{{\rm PVA}}[\Gamma]\to{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]
which sends
\hat{\zeta}_{\beta}
to
\zeta_{\beta}
for all
\beta\in\Phi[\Gamma]
.
Let
\beta,\gamma\in\Phi[\Gamma]
such that
\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma}\neq\infty
.
There exist
w\in W
and
s,t\in S
such that
\beta=w(\alpha_{s})
,
\gamma=w(\alpha_{t})
, and
m_{s,t}=\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma}
.
Recall the definition of
Z(\gamma,\beta,\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma})
.
Let
m=\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma}=m_{s,t}
and let
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}\in\Phi[\Gamma]
be the roots defined by
\beta_{1}=\beta
and, for
k\geq 2
,

\beta_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\gamma},r_{\beta},k-1)(\gamma)&\text{if }k\text{ is even}\,,\\
{\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\beta},r_{\gamma},k-1)(\beta)&\text{if }k\text{ is odd}\,.\end{array}\right.

Then
Z(\gamma,\beta,\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma})=\hat{\zeta}_{\beta_{m}}\cdots\hat{\zeta}_{\beta_{2}}\hat{\zeta}_{\beta_{1}}
.
Let
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.
Suppose that
k
is odd.
Then:

\beta_{k}={\rm Prod}_{R}(r_{\beta},r_{\gamma},k-1)(\beta)=(w\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(s,t,k-1)\,w^{-1}w)(\alpha_{s})=(w\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(s,t,k-1))(\alpha_{s})\,,

hence:

\displaystyle\zeta_{\beta_{k}}=w\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(s,t,k-1)\cdot\zeta_{\alpha_{s}}=

\displaystyle\iota_{W}(w)\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(\tau_{s},\tau_{t},k-1)\,(\tau_{s}\sigma_{s})\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(\tau_{s},\tau_{t},k-1)^{-1}\iota_{W}(w)^{-1}=

\displaystyle\iota_{W}(w)\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(\tau_{t},\tau_{s},k)\,\sigma_{s}\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(\tau_{s},\tau_{t},k-1)^{-1}\iota_{W}(w)^{-1}\,.

We show in the same way that, if
k
is even, then:

\zeta_{\beta_{k}}=\iota_{W}(w)\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(\tau_{s},\tau_{t},k)\,\sigma_{t}\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(\tau_{t},\tau_{s},k-1)^{-1}\iota_{W}(w)^{-1}\,.

Thus:

\zeta_{\beta_{m}}\cdots\zeta_{\beta_{2}}\zeta_{\beta_{1}}=\iota_{W}(w)\,{\rm Prod}_{L}(\tau_{s},\tau_{t},m)\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(\sigma_{t},\sigma_{s},m)\,\iota_{W}(w)^{-1}\,.

Similarly, if
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m}
are the roots such that
Z(\beta,\gamma,\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma})=\hat{\zeta}_{\gamma_{m}}\cdots\hat{\zeta}_{\gamma_{2}}\hat{\zeta}_{\gamma_{1}}
, then:

\zeta_{\gamma_{m}}\cdots\zeta_{\gamma_{2}}\zeta_{\gamma_{1}}=\iota_{W}(w)\,{\rm Prod}_{L}(\tau_{t},\tau_{s},m)\,{\rm Prod}_{R}(\sigma_{s},\sigma_{t},m)\,\iota_{W}(w)^{-1}\,.

So,
\zeta_{\beta_{m}}\cdots\zeta_{\beta_{2}}\zeta_{\beta_{1}}=\zeta_{\gamma_{m}}\cdots\zeta_{\gamma_{2}}\zeta_{\gamma_{1}}
.

The action of
W[\Gamma]
on
\{\hat{\zeta}_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\Phi[\Gamma]\}
defined by
w\cdot\hat{\zeta}_{\beta}=\hat{\zeta}_{w(\beta)}
extends to an action of
W[\Gamma]
on
\widehat{{\rm PVA}}[\Gamma]
.
Thus, we can consider the semi-direct product

determined by this action.
Now, we show that the homomorphisms
\varphi:\widehat{{\rm PVA}}[\Gamma]\to{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]\subset{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
and
\iota_{W}:W[\Gamma]\to{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
induce a homomorphism
\tilde{\varphi}:G\to{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
.
To do this, we just need to check that
\varphi(w\cdot\hat{\zeta}_{\beta})=\iota_{W}(w)\,\varphi(\hat{\zeta}_{\beta})\,\iota_{W}(w)^{-1}
for all

and all
\beta\in\Phi[\Gamma]
, and this equality is easily verified since both terms are by definition equal to
\zeta_{w(\beta)}
.

Let
\psi:\mathcal{S}\sqcup\mathcal{T}\to G
be the map defined by
\psi(\sigma_{s})=s\hat{\zeta}_{\alpha_{s}}
and
\psi(\tau_{s})=s
for all
s\in S
.
Now, we show that this map induces a homomorphism
\psi:{\rm VA}[\Gamma]\to G
.
Let
s,t\in S
,
s\neq t
, such that

.
Then:

{\rm Prod}_{R}(\psi(\tau_{t}),\psi(\tau_{s}),m_{s,t})={\rm Prod}_{R}(t,s,m_{s,t})={\rm Prod}_{R}(s,t,m_{s,t})={\rm Prod}_{R}(\psi(\tau_{s}),\psi(\tau_{t}),m_{s,t})\,.

Let
s\in S
.
Then:

\psi(\tau_{s})^{2}=s^{2}=1\,.

Let
s,t\in S
,
s\neq t
, such that
m_{s,t}\neq\infty
.
Let
m=m_{s,t}
and let
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}\in\Phi[\Gamma]
be the roots defined by
\beta_{1}=\alpha_{s}
and, for

\beta_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm Prod}_{R}(t,s,k-1)(\alpha_{t})&\text{if }k\text{ is even}\,,\\
{\rm Prod}_{R}(s,t,k-1)(\alpha_{s})&\text{if }k\text{ is odd}\,.\end{array}\right.

Then:

\displaystyle{\rm Prod}_{R}(\psi(\sigma_{t}),\psi(\sigma_{s}),m_{s,t})=\cdots s\hat{\zeta}_{\alpha_{s}}t\hat{\zeta}_{\alpha_{t}}s\hat{\zeta}_{\alpha_{s}}={\rm Prod}_{R}(t,s,m_{s,t})\,\hat{\zeta}_{\beta_{m}}\cdots\hat{\zeta}_{\beta_{2}}\hat{\zeta}_{\beta_{1}}=

\displaystyle{\rm Prod}_{R}(t,s,m_{s,t})\,Z(\alpha_{t},\alpha_{s},\hat{m}_{\alpha_{s},\alpha_{t}})\,.

Similarly,

{\rm Prod}_{R}(\psi(\sigma_{s}),\psi(\sigma_{t}),m_{s,t})={\rm Prod}_{R}(s,t,m_{s,t})\,Z(\alpha_{s},\alpha_{t},\hat{m}_{\alpha_{s},\alpha_{t}})\,.

Hence:

{\rm Prod}_{R}(\psi(\sigma_{t}),\psi(\sigma_{s}),m_{s,t})={\rm Prod}_{R}(\psi(\sigma_{s}),\psi(\sigma_{t}),m_{s,t})\,.

Let
s,t\in S
,

, such that

Set

Then the following equality can be proved in the same way as the corresponding equality in the proof of Theorem

Finally, we prove that

in the same way as in the proof of Theorem

3
Center of
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]

We start this section by recalling some results on centers of Coxeter groups.
If
\Gamma
is a Coxeter graph and
M=(m_{s,t})_{s,t\in S}
is its Coxeter matrix, then we denote by
\lg:W[\Gamma]\to\mathbb{N}
the word length in
W[\Gamma]
with respect to
S
.
The following can be found in Bourbaki
[
8
, Exercice 22, Page 22]
.

Proposition 3.1

(Bourbaki
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph and let
M=(m_{s,t})_{s,t\in S}
be its Coxeter matrix.
The following conditions on an element
w_{0}\in W[\Gamma]
are equivalent:

(a)

For all
u\in W[\Gamma]
,
\lg(w_{0})=\lg(u)+\lg(u^{-1}w_{0})
.

(b)

For all
s\in S
,
\lg(w_{0})>\lg(sw_{0})
.

Moreover,
w_{0}
exists if and only if
W[\Gamma]
is finite.
If
w_{0}
satisfies (a) and/or (b), then
w_{0}
is unique,
w_{0}
is an involution, and
w_{0}Sw_{0}=S
.

The element
w_{0}
of Proposition
3.1
, if it exists, is called
the longest element
of
W[\Gamma]
.
The center of a group
G
will be denoted by
Z(G)
.
The following can be found in Bourbaki
[
8
, Exercice 3, Page 130]
.

Proposition 3.2

(Bourbaki
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected Coxeter graph.

(1)

If
W[\Gamma]
is infinite, then
Z(W[\Gamma])=\{1\}
.

(2)

Suppose that
W[\Gamma]
is finite.
Let
w_{0}
be the longest element of
W[\Gamma]
.
Then
Z(W[\Gamma])=\{1,w_{0}\}
if
w_{0}
is central, and
Z(W[\Gamma])=\{1\}
otherwise.

Now, we return to the study of virtual Artin groups.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph.
Assume that
W[\Gamma]
is a subgroup of
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
via the embedding
\iota_{W}:W[\Gamma]\hookrightarrow{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
.
Then the centralizer of
W[\Gamma]
in
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
is
Z(W[\Gamma])
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{X}
is a subset of
\Phi[\Gamma]
, then we denote by
\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}
the full subgraph of
\hat{\Gamma}
spanned by
\mathcal{X}
.
By Van der Lek
[
27
, Lemma 4.11]
we can identify
A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}]
with the subgroup of
A[\hat{\Gamma}]={\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
generated by
\{\delta_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\mathcal{X}\}
.
Moreover, again by Van der Lek
[
27
, Lemma 4.12]
, if
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\mid i\in I\}
is a family of subsets of
\Phi[\Gamma]
, then:

\bigcap_{i\in I}A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}_{i}}]=A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\cap_{i\in I}\mathcal{X}_{i}}]\,.

(3.1)

It is clear that
Z(W[\Gamma])
is contained in the centralizer of
W[\Gamma]
in
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
.
So, we just have to prove that the centralizer of
W[\Gamma]
in
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
is contained in
Z(W[\Gamma])
.
Let
g
be an element of the centralizer of
W[\Gamma]
in
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
.
We write
g
in the form
g=hw
with
h\in{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
and
w\in W[\Gamma]
.
The element
\pi_{K}(g)=w
must lie in the center of
W[\Gamma]
, hence
h
also lies in the centralizer of
W[\Gamma]
in
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
.
Let
\beta\in\Phi^{+}[\Gamma]
.
We set
\mathcal{X}^{+}_{\beta}=\Phi[\Gamma]\setminus\{-\beta\}
,
\mathcal{X}^{-}_{\beta}=\Phi[\Gamma]\setminus\{\beta\}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}=\Phi[\Gamma]\setminus\{\beta,-\beta\}
.
Since
\hat{m}_{\beta,-\beta}=\infty
,
\mathcal{X}^{+}_{\beta}\cup\mathcal{X}^{-}_{\beta}=\Phi[\Gamma]
and
\mathcal{X}^{+}_{\beta}\cap\mathcal{X}^{-}_{\beta}=\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}
, by Bellingeri–Paris
[
6
, Lemma 3.3]
,

{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]=A[\hat{\Gamma}]=A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}^{+}_{\beta}}]*_{A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}}]}A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}^{-}_{\beta}}]\,.

Since
r_{\beta}(\beta)=-\beta
and
r_{\beta}(-\beta)=\beta
, we have
r_{\beta}(\mathcal{X}^{+}_{\beta})=\mathcal{X}^{-}_{\beta}
,
r_{\beta}(\mathcal{X}^{-}_{\beta})=\mathcal{X}^{+}_{\beta}
and
r_{\beta}(\mathcal{Y}_{\beta})=\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}
, hence
r_{\beta}\cdot A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}^{+}_{\beta}}]=A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}^{-}_{\beta}}]
,
r_{\beta}\cdot A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}^{-}_{\beta}}]=A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}^{+}_{\beta}}]
and
r_{\beta}\cdot A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}}]=A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}}]
.
Since
r_{\beta}\in W[\Gamma]
and
h
lies in the centralizer of
W[\Gamma]
in
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
,
r_{\beta}
and
h
commute, which means in terms of actions that
r_{\beta}\cdot h=h
.
By Bellingeri–Paris
[
6
, Lemma 3.6]
, it follows that
h\in A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}}]
.
We have
\cap_{\beta\in\Phi^{+}[\Gamma]}\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}=\emptyset
, hence, by Equation (
3.1
),

h\in\bigcap_{\beta\in\Phi^{+}[\Gamma]}A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{Y}_{\beta}}]=A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\emptyset}]=\{1\}\,.

Thus,
h=1
and
g=w\in Z(W[\Gamma])
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph.
Then
Z({\rm VA}[\Gamma])=\{1\}
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{\ell}
be the connected components of
\Gamma
.
We have
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]={\rm VA}[\Gamma_{1}]\times\cdots\times{\rm VA}[\Gamma_{\ell}]
, hence:

Z({\rm VA}[\Gamma])=Z({\rm VA}[\Gamma_{1}])\times\cdots\times Z({\rm VA}[\Gamma_{\ell}])\,.

So, we can assume that
\Gamma
is connected.
Note that the center of
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
is contained in the centralizer of
W[\Gamma]
in
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
.
If either
W[\Gamma]
is infinite or
W[\Gamma]
is finite and the longest element
w_{0}
is not central, then
Z({\rm VA}[\Gamma])=\{1\}
by an easy combination of Proposition
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
.
Suppose
W[\Gamma]
is finite and
w_{0}
is central.
Then, by Proposition
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
,
Z({\rm VA}[\Gamma])\subset\{1,w_{0}\}
.
Let
s\in S
.
By Deodhar
[
16
]
we have
w_{0}(\alpha_{s})=-\alpha_{s}
, hence
w_{0}\cdot\delta_{\alpha_{s}}=\delta_{-\alpha_{s}}\neq\delta_{\alpha_{s}}
, thus
w_{0}
and
\delta_{\alpha_{s}}=\sigma_{s}
do not commute.
So,
w_{0}\not\in Z({\rm VA}[\Gamma])
, hence
Z({\rm VA}[\Gamma])=\{1\}
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

\Gamma

Let
\Gamma

be its Coxeter matrix.

\mathcal{P}_{\rm sph}(S)
the set of subsets

such that

is of spherical type.
Then the

of

is defined to be
n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])=\max\{|X|\mid X\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm sph}(S)\}
.
We know by Charney–Davis
[
12
]
that, if

satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture, then

is equal to the cohomological dimension of
A[\Gamma]
, and it is conjectured that all Artin groups satisfy the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture.
This conjecture will be recalled in the beginning of Section

Note however that, by Deligne
[
17
]
and Paolini–Salvetti
[
30
]
,

satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture if
\Gamma
is of spherical type or of affine type.

Example
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is of spherical type or of affine type.
Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:V\times V\to\mathbb{R}
be the canonical bilinear form of
W[\Gamma]
.
Then
n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])
is equal to the rank of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
In particular, if
\Gamma
is of spherical type, then
n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])
is equal to the number of vertices of
\Gamma
, and if
\Gamma
is connected of affine type, then
n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])
is equal to the number of vertices of
\Gamma
minus
1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph and let
M=(m_{s,t})_{s,t\in S}
be its Coxeter matrix.
A subset
X\subset S
is called
free of infinity
if
m_{s,t}\neq\infty
for all
s,t\in X
.
We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{<\infty}(S)
the set of free of infinity subsets of
S
.
Note that
\mathcal{P}_{\rm sph}(S)\subset\mathcal{P}_{<\infty}(S)
, hence:

n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])=\max\{n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma_{X}])\mid X\in\mathcal{P}_{<\infty}(S)\}\,.

The following theorem is the main result of the present section.
Recall the general principle which says that, if we understand
A[\Gamma_{X}]
for all
X\in\mathcal{P}_{<\infty}(S)
, then we can understand
A[\Gamma]
(see Godelle–Paris
[
22
]
).
Since we understand the Artin groups of spherical type and the Artin groups of affine type, this theorem enables to apply this principle and therefore to understand
A[\hat{\Gamma}]={\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
when
\Gamma
is of spherical type or of affine type.
Since
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]={\rm KVA}[\Gamma]\rtimes W[\Gamma]
and Coxeter groups are well-understood, we can therefore deduce results on
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
itself.
We will apply this strategy in Section
5
and Section
6
to prove that
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
has a solution to the word problem and to compute the virtual cohomological dimension of
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
when
\Gamma
is of spherical type or of affine type.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph of spherical type or of affine type, and let
\mathcal{X}
be a free of infinity subset of
\Phi[\Gamma]
.
Then
\mathcal{X}
is finite,
\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}
is of spherical type or of affine type, and
n_{\rm sph}(A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}])\leq n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])
.

Proof.

A symmetric matrix
A=(a_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
is called
decomposable
if there exists a non-trivial partition of the set of indices,
\{1,\dots,n\}=I\sqcup J
, such that
a_{i,j}=a_{j,i}=0
for all
i\in I
and
j\in J
.
We say that
A
is
indecomposable
otherwise.
The following claim is well-known and can be found for example in Bourbaki
[
8
, Lemme 4, Page 78]
.

Claim 1.

Let
A=(a_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
be a positive symmetric matrix but not positive definite.
Suppose that
A
is indecomposable, that
a_{i,i}>0
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, and that
a_{i,j}\leq 0
for all
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
i\neq j
.

(1)

The rank of
A
is
n-1
.

(2)

If
X
is a proper subset of
\{1,\dots,n\}
, then the matrix
A_{X}=(a_{i,j})_{i,j\in X}
is positive definite.

Now take a Coxeter graph
\Gamma
of spherical type or of affine type, and denote by
M=(m_{s,t})_{s,t\in S}
its Coxeter matrix.
Let
\hat{\Pi}=\{\hat{E}_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\Phi[\Gamma]\}
be a set in one-to-one correspondence with
\Phi[\Gamma]
, and let
\hat{V}=\oplus_{\beta\in\Phi[\Gamma]}\mathbb{R}\,\hat{E}_{\beta}
be a real vector space having
\hat{\Pi}
as a basis.
Define the symmetric bilinear form
\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle:\hat{V}\times\hat{V}\to\mathbb{R}
by
\langle\!\langle\hat{E}_{\beta},\hat{E}_{\gamma}\rangle\!\rangle=-2\cos(\pi/\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma})
if
\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma}\neq\infty
and
\langle\!\langle\hat{E}_{\beta},\hat{E}_{\gamma}\rangle\!\rangle=-2
if
\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma}=\infty
.

Claim 2.

Let
\beta,\gamma\in\Phi[\Gamma]
such that
\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma}\neq\infty
.
Then
\langle\!\langle\hat{E}_{\beta},\hat{E}_{\gamma}\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle
.

Proof of Claim 2.

If
\beta=\gamma
, then
\langle\!\langle\hat{E}_{\beta},\hat{E}_{\gamma}\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle=2
.
Suppose
\beta\neq\gamma
.
By definition there exist
w\in W
and
s,t\in S
such that
m_{s,t}\neq\infty
,
\beta=w(\alpha_{s})
,
\gamma=w(\alpha_{t})
and
\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma}=m_{s,t}
.
Then:

\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle=\langle w(\alpha_{s}),w(\alpha_{t})\rangle=\langle\alpha_{s},\alpha_{t}\rangle=-2\cos(\pi/m_{s,t})=-2\cos(\pi/\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma})=\langle\!\langle\hat{E}_{\beta},\hat{E}_{\gamma}\rangle\!\rangle\,.

This completes the proof of Claim 2.

Now take a free of infinity finite subset
\mathcal{X}
of
\Phi[\Gamma]
.
We turn to show that
A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}]
is of spherical type or of affine type, that
|\mathcal{X}|\leq 2\,n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])
, and that
n_{\rm sph}(A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}])\leq n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])
. The existence of this uniform bound on the cardinality of
\mathcal{X}
in particular implies that there exists no free of infinity infinite subset of
\Phi[\Gamma]
.
This will prove the theorem.
We denote by
\hat{V}_{\mathcal{X}}
the linear subspace of
\hat{V}
spanned by
\{\hat{E}_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\mathcal{X}\}
and by
\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}:\hat{V}_{\mathcal{X}}\times\hat{V}_{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathbb{R}
the restriction of
\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle
to
\hat{V}_{\mathcal{X}}
.

Let
\hat{u}\in\hat{V}_{\mathcal{X}}
.
We write
\hat{u}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{X}}\lambda_{\beta}\hat{E}_{\beta}
with
\lambda_{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}
for all
\beta\in\mathcal{X}
.
Let
u=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{X}}\lambda_{\beta}\beta\in V
.
Then, by Claim 2,

\langle\!\langle\hat{u},\hat{u}\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{X}}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{X}}\lambda_{\beta}\lambda_{\gamma}\langle\!\langle\hat{E}_{\beta},\hat{E}_{\gamma}\rangle\!\rangle=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{X}}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{X}}\lambda_{\beta}\lambda_{\gamma}\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle=\langle u,u\rangle\geq 0\,.

This shows that
\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}
is positive, hence
A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}]
is of spherical type or of affine type.

Let
\hat{\Gamma}_{1},\dots,\hat{\Gamma}_{m}
be the connected components of
\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}
.
For each
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
we denote by
\mathcal{X}_{k}
the set of vertices of
\hat{\Gamma}_{k}
and we consider the matrix
A_{k}=(\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma})_{\beta,\gamma\in\mathcal{X}_{k}}
.
The matrix
A_{k}
is symmetric, positive and indecomposable, hence, by Claim 1, the rank of
A_{k}
is either
|\mathcal{X}_{k}|
or
|\mathcal{X}_{k}|-1
.
In the second case we necessarily have
|\mathcal{X}_{k}|-1\geq 1
.
Thus, we have
m\leq{\rm rank}(\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathcal{X}})
, and therefore:

|\mathcal{X}|=\sum_{k=1}^{m}|\mathcal{X}_{k}|=m+\sum_{k=1}^{m}(|\mathcal{X}_{k}|-1)\leq m+{\rm rank}(\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathcal{X}})\leq 2\,{\rm rank}(\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathcal{X}})\,.

Since both
\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}
and
\Gamma
are of spherical type or of affine type, we have
n_{\rm sph}(A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}])={\rm rank}(\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathcal{X}})
and
n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])={\rm rank}(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, hence it remains to show that
{\rm rank}(\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathcal{X}})\leq{\rm rank}(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
.

By applying again Claim 1 to the matrices
A_{k}
we see that there exists a subset
\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{X}
such that
|\mathcal{Y}|={\rm rank}(\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathcal{X}})
and
\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}
is positive definite.
Let
\{\lambda_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in\mathcal{Y}}
be a collection of real numbers such that
\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{Y}}\lambda_{\beta}\beta=0
(in
V
).
Then, by Claim 2,

\displaystyle 0=\langle 0,0\rangle=\langle\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{Y}}\lambda_{\beta}\beta,\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{Y}}\lambda_{\beta}\beta\rangle=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{Y}}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}}\lambda_{\beta}\lambda_{\gamma}\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{Y}}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}}\lambda_{\beta}\lambda_{\gamma}\langle\!\langle\hat{E}_{\beta},\hat{E}_{\gamma}\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{Y}}\lambda_{\beta}\hat{E}_{\beta},\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{Y}}\lambda_{\beta}\hat{E}_{\beta}\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}\,.

Since
\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}
is positive definite and
\{\hat{E}_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\mathcal{Y}\}
is a basis of
\hat{V}_{\mathcal{Y}}
, it follows that
\lambda_{\beta}=0
for all
\beta\in\mathcal{Y}
, hence
\mathcal{Y}
is a linearly independent subset of
V
.
Let
V_{\mathcal{Y}}
be the linear subspace of
V
spanned by
\mathcal{Y}
and let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}:V_{\mathcal{Y}}\times V_{\mathcal{Y}}\to\mathbb{R}
be the restriction of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to
V_{\mathcal{Y}}
.
The matrix of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}
in the basis
\mathcal{Y}
is the same as the matrix of
\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}
in the basis
\{\hat{E}_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\mathcal{Y}\}
and
\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}
is positive definite, hence
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}
is positive definite.
So,
{\rm rank}(\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathcal{X}})=|\mathcal{Y}|=\dim(V_{\mathcal{Y}})={\rm rank}(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}})\leq{\rm rank}(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
.
∎

5
Word problem

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph of spherical type or of affine type.
Then
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
has a solution to the word problem.

The proof of Theorem
5.1
relies on the following proposition which we state and prove in a general framework, without assuming that the Coxeter graph is of spherical type or of affine type.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite Coxeter graph.
Let
\mathcal{S}\sqcup\mathcal{T}
be the standard generating set of
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
, where
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma_{s}\mid s\in S\}
and
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau_{s}\mid s\in S\}
.
There exists an algorithm which, given a word
w
on
(\mathcal{S}\sqcup\mathcal{T})^{\pm 1}
which represents an element
g\in{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
, determines:

(i)

a finite subset
\mathcal{X}\subset\Phi[\Gamma]
such that
g\in A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}]
,

(ii)

the Coxeter matrix
\hat{M}_{\mathcal{X}}
of
\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}
,

(iii)

a word
\hat{w}
over
\{\delta_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\mathcal{X}\}^{\pm 1}
which represents
g
.

Proof.

Let
M=(m_{s,t})_{s,t\in S}
be the Coxeter matrix of
\Gamma
.
For
s,t\in S
we set
a_{s,t}=-2\cos(\pi/m_{s,t})
if
m_{s,t}\neq\infty
and
a_{s,t}=-2
if
m_{s,t}=\infty
.
The number
a_{s,t}
is an algebraic number for all
s,t\in S
, hence we can consider a number field
\mathbb{K}
containing all
a_{s,t}
and make all linear calculations with
\mathbb{K}
in place of
\mathbb{R}
.
This makes them algorithmically computable.
Take an abstract set
\Pi=\{\alpha_{s}\mid s\in S\}
in one-to-one correspondence with
S
and denote by
V
the vector space over
\mathbb{K}
having
\Pi
as a basis.
Moreover, consider the symmetric bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:V\times V\to\mathbb{K}
defined by
\langle\alpha_{s},\alpha_{t}\rangle=a_{s,t}
for all
s,t\in S
and the embedding
W[\Gamma]\hookrightarrow{\rm GL}(V)
defined by
s(v)=v-\langle v,\alpha_{s}\rangle\alpha_{s}
for
s\in S
and
v\in V
.
Set
\Phi[\Gamma]=\{w(\alpha_{s})\mid s\in S\text{ and }w\in W[\Gamma]\}
.

In this context a root
\beta
is given by a word
w=s_{1}\cdots s_{\ell}\in S^{*}
and an element
s\in S
such that
\bar{w}(\alpha_{s})=\beta
, where
\bar{w}
is the element of
W[\Gamma]
represented by
w
.
The word problem in
W[\Gamma]
is solvable (see Tits
[
34
]
) and the linear operations in
V
can be down with algorithmic means, hence we can decide whether two pairs
(w,s)
and
(w^{\prime},s^{\prime})
in
S^{*}\times S
represent the same root or not.

Claim 1.

Let
\beta,\gamma\in\Phi[\Gamma]
such that
\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle\leq 0
and let
s,t\in S
such that
m_{s,t}\neq\infty
.
There exists
u\in W[\Gamma]
such that
\{u(\alpha_{s}),u(\alpha_{t})\}=\{\beta,\gamma\}
if and only if
st
and
r_{\beta}r_{\gamma}
are conjugate in
W[\Gamma]
.

Proof of Claim 1.

Note that
ts
and
st
are conjugate, since
ts=s\,st\,s
, hence we can replace
st
with
ts
if necessary.
Suppose first there exists
u\in W[\Gamma]
such that
\{u(\alpha_{s}),u(\alpha_{t})\}=\{\beta,\gamma\}
, say
u(\alpha_{s})=\beta
and
u(\alpha_{t})=\gamma
.
Then
r_{\beta}=ur_{\alpha_{s}}u^{-1}=usu^{-1}
and
r_{\gamma}=ur_{\alpha_{t}}u^{-1}=utu^{-1}
, hence
r_{\beta}r_{\gamma}=u\,st\,u^{-1}
.

Now, suppose that
r_{\beta}r_{\gamma}
and
st
are conjugate in
W[\Gamma]
.
Let
w\in W[\Gamma]
such that
wr_{\beta}r_{\gamma}w^{-1}=st
.
Upon replacing
\beta
with
w(\beta)
and
\gamma
with
w(\gamma)
, we can assume that
r_{\beta}r_{\gamma}=st
.
By Baumeister–Dyer–Stump–Wegener
[
4
, Theorem 1.4]
,
r_{\beta}
and
r_{\gamma}
lie in the subgroup
W[\Gamma_{\{s,t\}}]
of
W[\Gamma]
generated by
\{s,t\}
, and they are conjugate in
W[\Gamma_{\{s,t\}}]
to elements of
\{s,t\}
.
Since, furthermore,
r_{\beta}r_{\gamma}=st
, there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
r_{\beta}=(st)^{k+1}s
and
r_{\gamma}=(st)^{k}s
.
If
k
is even, say
k=2\ell
, then, upon conjugating by
s(ts)^{\ell}
, we may assume that
r_{\beta}=t
and
r_{\gamma}=s
.
If
k
is odd, say
k=2\ell-1
, then, upon conjugating by
(ts)^{\ell}
, we may assume that
r_{\beta}=s
and
r_{\gamma}=t
.
So, since we can swap
s
and
t
, we may assume that
r_{\beta}=s
and
r_{\gamma}=t
.
This means in terms of roots that
\beta\in\{\alpha_{s},-\alpha_{s}\}
and
\gamma\in\{\alpha_{t},-\alpha_{t}\}
.
If
\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle=0
, then
\langle\alpha_{s},\alpha_{t}\rangle=0
.
In this case
s(\alpha_{s})=-\alpha_{s}
,
s(\alpha_{t})=\alpha_{t}
,
t(\alpha_{s})=\alpha_{s}
and
t(\alpha_{t})=-\alpha_{t}
.
By using these equalities we easily reduce to the case where
\beta=\alpha_{s}
and
\gamma=\alpha_{t}
, which concludes the proof in this case.
Suppose
\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle<0
.
Then
m_{s,t}\geq 3
,
\langle\alpha_{s},\alpha_{t}\rangle=\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle
, and we have two possibilities: either
\beta=\alpha_{s}
and
\gamma=\alpha_{t}
, or
\beta=-\alpha_{s}
and
\gamma=-\alpha_{t}
.
If
\beta=\alpha_{s}
and
\gamma=\alpha_{t}
, then there is nothing to prove.
So, we can assume
\beta=-\alpha_{s}
and
\gamma=-\alpha_{t}
.
We know by Deodhar
[
16
]
that:

\displaystyle{\rm Prod}_{R}(s,t,m_{s,t})(\alpha_{s})=-\alpha_{s}\text{ and }{\rm Prod}_{R}(s,t,m_{s,t})(\alpha_{t})=-\alpha_{t}\text{ if }m_{s,t}\text{ is even}\,,

\displaystyle{\rm Prod}_{R}(s,t,m_{s,t})(\alpha_{s})=-\alpha_{t}\text{ and }{\rm Prod}_{R}(s,t,m_{s,t})(\alpha_{t})=-\alpha_{s}\text{ if }m_{s,t}\text{ is odd}\,,

hence, upon applying
{\rm Prod}_{R}(s,t,m_{s,t})
, we may assume that
\beta=\alpha_{s}
and
\gamma=\alpha_{t}
, which finishes the proof of Claim 1.

Claim 2.

There exists an algorithm which, for given
\beta,\gamma\in\Phi[\Gamma]
, computes
\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma}
.

Proof of Claim 2.

If
\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle\not\in]-2,0]
, then
\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma}=\infty
.
Thus, we can assume
\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle\in]-2,0]
.
As shown in the beginning of the proof of Proposition 1.4.7 in Krammer
[
26
]
, this implies that
r_{\beta}r_{\gamma}
is of finite order.
Let
s,t\in S
such that
m_{s,t}\neq\infty
.
Then the algorithm of Krammer
[
26
, Proposition 3.3.2]
determines whether
r_{\beta}r_{\gamma}
and
st
are conjugate.
By Claim 1 this algorithm also decides whether there exists
u\in W[\Gamma]
such that
\{u(\alpha_{s}),u(\alpha_{t})\}=\{\beta,\gamma\}
.
Then we compute
\hat{m}_{\beta,\gamma}
by applying this algorithm to all the pairs
(s,t)\in S\times S
such that
s\neq t
and
m_{s,t}\neq\infty
.
This concludes the proof of Claim 2.

End of the proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Let
g\in{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
given by a word over
(\mathcal{S}\sqcup\mathcal{T})^{\pm 1}
.
From this word we can easily write
g
in the form:

g=\iota_{W}(w_{0})\sigma_{s_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\iota_{W}(w_{1})\cdots\sigma_{s_{p}}^{\varepsilon_{p}}\iota_{W}(w_{p})\,,

where
w_{0},w_{1},\dots,w_{p}\in W[\Gamma]
,
s_{1},\dots,s_{p}\in S
and
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{p}\in\{\pm 1\}
.
For
i=1,\dots,p
set
\beta_{i}=(w_{0}\cdots w_{i-1})(\alpha_{s_{i}})
.
Set also
w=w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{p}
.
Then
g=\delta_{\beta_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\delta_{\beta_{2}}^{\varepsilon_{2}}\cdots\delta_{\beta_{p}}^{\varepsilon_{p}}\iota_{W}(w)
.
But
w=\pi_{K}(g)=1
, since
g\in{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
, hence
g=\delta_{\beta_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\delta_{\beta_{2}}^{\varepsilon_{2}}\cdots\delta_{\beta_{p}}^{\varepsilon_{p}}
.
This proves Part (i) and Part (iii) if we set
\mathcal{X}=\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{p}\}
.
Then Part (ii) immediately follows from Claim 2.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph of spherical type or of affine type.
Let
g\in{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
given by a word over
(\mathcal{S}\sqcup\mathcal{T})^{\pm 1}
.
We can calculate
\pi_{K}(g)\in W[\Gamma]
and the word problem in
W[\Gamma]
is solvable (see Tits
[
34
]
), hence we can decide whether
\pi_{K}(g)
is trivial or not.
If
\pi_{K}(g)\neq 1
, then
g\neq 1
and the algorithm stops here.
So, we can assume
\pi_{K}(g)=1
, that is,
g\in{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
.
The algorithm of Proposition
5.2
gives a finite subset
\mathcal{X}\subset\Phi[\Gamma]
and a word
\hat{w}
over
\{\delta_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\mathcal{X}\}^{\pm 1}
such that
g\in A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}]
and
\hat{w}
represents
g
.
It also gives the matrix
\hat{M}_{\mathcal{X}}
of
\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}
.
We know by Godelle–Paris
[
22
]
that, if, for each free of infinity subset
\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{X}
the group
A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{Y}}]
has a solution to the word problem, then
A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}]
itself has a solution to the word problem.
On the other hand, by Theorem
4.1
,
A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{Y}}]
is of spherical type or of affine type for each free of infinity subset
\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{X}
, by Deligne
[
17
]
and Brieskorn–Saito
[
10
]
the Artin groups of spherical type have a solvable word problem, and by McCammond–Sulway
[
29
]
the Artin groups of affine type have a solvable word problem.
So,
A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}]
has a solution to the word problem.
This means that there is an algorithm which decides whether
g
is trivial or not.
∎

6
K(\pi,1)
conjecture and cohomological dimension

We start by recalling the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture for Artin groups.
Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph and let
M=(m_{s,t})_{s,t\in S}
be its Coxeter matrix.
Recall that
\Pi=\{\alpha_{s}\mid s\in S\}
is the set of simple roots, that
V
is the real vector space having
\Pi
as a basis, and that
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:V\times V\to\mathbb{R}
is the canonical bilinear form.
We have an embedding
W[\Gamma]\hookrightarrow{\rm GL}(V)
defined by
s(v)=v-\langle v,\alpha_{s}\rangle\alpha_{s}
for
s\in S
and
v\in V
, and
W[\Gamma]
, viewed as a subgroup of
{\rm GL}(V)
, is generated by linear reflections.

Suppose
\Gamma
is finite.
There exists a non-empty open cone
I\subset V
, called
Tits cone
, on which
W[\Gamma]
acts properly discontinuously.
The set of reflections in
W[\Gamma]
is
R=\{wsw^{-1}\mid w\in W[\Gamma]\text{ and }s\in S\}
and
W[\Gamma]
acts freely and properly discontinuously on
I\setminus\cup_{r\in R}H_{r}
, where, for
r\in R
,
H_{r}
denotes the hyperplane of
V
fixed by
r
(see Bourbaki
[
8
]
).
Set:

\mathcal{M}[\Gamma]=(I\times I)\setminus\left(\bigcup_{r\in R}(H_{r}\times H_{r})\right)\,.

This is a connected manifold of dimension
2\,|S|
on which
W[\Gamma]
acts freely and properly discontinuously.
A key result in the domain is the following.

Theorem 6.1

(Van der Lek
[
27
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite Coxeter graph.
Then the fundamental group of
\mathcal{M}[\Gamma]/W[\Gamma]
is isomorphic to
A[\Gamma]
.

Recall that a CW-complex
X
is a
classifying space
for a (discrete) group
G
if
\pi_{1}(X)=G
and the universal covering of
X
is contractible.
These spaces play a major role in the computation of cohomology of groups (see Brown
[
11
]
).
The following is one of the main questions in the theory of Artin groups.

Conjecture 6.2

(
K(\pi,1)
Conjecture)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite Coxeter graph.
Then
\mathcal{M}[\Gamma]/W[\Gamma]
is a classifying space for
A[\Gamma]
.

We say that an Artin group
A[\Gamma]
with
\Gamma
finite
satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture
if
\mathcal{M}[\Gamma]/W[\Gamma]
is a classifying space for
A[\Gamma]
.
So, the above conjecture says that any Artin group
A[\Gamma]
with
\Gamma
finite satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture.
Note also that, by Deligne
[
17
]
and Paolini–Salvetti
[
30
]
,
A[\Gamma]
satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture if
\Gamma
is of spherical type or of affine type.

The following makes sense only when
\Gamma
is of spherical type since this is the only case where
\hat{\Gamma}
is finite.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph of spherical type.
Then
A[\hat{\Gamma}]
satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture.

Theorem
6.3
is a direct consequence of the following proposition combined with Theorem
4.1
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite Coxeter graph and let
M=(m_{s,t})_{s,t\in S}
be its Coxeter matrix.
Suppose that, for each free of infinity subset
X\subset S
, the Coxeter graph
\Gamma_{X}
is of spherical type or of affine type.
Then
A[\Gamma]
satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture.

Proof.

By Deligne
[
17
]
, if
\Gamma
is of spherical type, then
A[\Gamma]
satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture, and by Paolini–Salvetti
[
30
]
, if
\Gamma
is of affine type, then
A[\Gamma]
satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture.
On the other hand, by Ellis–Sköldberg
[
21
]
(see also Godelle–Paris
[
23
]
), if
A[\Gamma_{X}]
satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture for every free of infinity subset
X\subset S
, then
A[\Gamma]
satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture.
So, if for each free of infinity subset
X\subset S
the Coxeter graph
\Gamma_{X}
is of spherical type or of affine type, then
A[\Gamma]
satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.3
.

It is a straightforward consequence of Proposition
6.4
and Theorem
4.1
.
∎

For a group
G
we denote by
{\rm cd}(G)
the cohomological dimension of
G
and by
{\rm vcd}(G)
the virtual cohomological dimension of
G
.

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph of spherical type and let
n
be the number of vertices of
\Gamma
.
Then
{\rm cd}({\rm KVA}[\Gamma])={\rm vcd}({\rm VA}[\Gamma])=n
.
In particular,
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
is torsion free and
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
is virtually torsion free.

Proof.

We have
n=n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])
by definition.
By Theorem
6.3
,
A[\hat{\Gamma}]
satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture, hence, by Charney–Davis
[
12
]
,
{\rm cd}({\rm KVA}[\Gamma])={\rm cd}(A[\hat{\Gamma}])=n_{\rm sph}(A[\hat{\Gamma}])
.
By Theorem
4.1
it follows that
{\rm cd}({\rm KVA}[\Gamma])\leq n
.
On the other hand,
A[\Gamma]
is a subgroup of
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
(see Corollary
2.4
) and, by Deligne
[
17
]
,
{\rm cd}(A[\Gamma])=n
, hence
n\leq{\rm cd}({\rm KVA}[\Gamma])
, and therefore
{\rm cd}({\rm KVA}[\Gamma])=n
.
Finally, since
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]={\rm KVA}[\Gamma]\rtimes W[\Gamma]
and
W[\Gamma]
is finite,
{\rm vcd}({\rm VA}[\Gamma])={\rm cd}({\rm KVA}[\Gamma])=n
.
∎

Remark
.

It follows from Bartholdi–Enriquez–Etingof–Rains
[
3
]
that the cohomological dimension of
{\rm PVB}_{n}
is
n-1
, which implies also that
{\rm PVB}_{n}
is torsion free.
We think that this extends to all virtual Artin groups of spherical type in the sense that, for a Coxeter graph
\Gamma
of spherical type, we think that the cohomological dimension of
{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]
is equal to the number of vertices of
\Gamma
, and therefore that
{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]
is torsion free as well.
Note that, since in this case
{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]
is of finite index in
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
and by Corollary
6.5
the virtual cohomological dimension of
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
is equal to the number of vertices of
\Gamma
, by a theorem of Serre
[
32
]
it would suffice to prove that
{\rm PVA}[\Gamma]
is torsion free.

We are not able to give precise values for the cohomological dimension of
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
and for the virtual cohomological dimension of
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
when
\Gamma
is of affine type, but we can give bounds:

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Coxeter graph of affine type.

(1)

{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
is torsion free and
{\rm cd}({\rm KVA}([\Gamma])\leq n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])+1
.

(2)

{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
is virtually torsion free and
{\rm vcd}({\rm VA}[\Gamma])\leq 2\,n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])+1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a finite subset of
\Phi[\Gamma]
.
We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{<\infty}(\mathcal{X})
the set of free of infinity subsets of
\mathcal{X}
.
Recall that
n_{\rm sph}(A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}])=\max\{n_{\rm sph}(A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{Y}}])\mid\mathcal{Y}\in\mathcal{P}_{<\infty}(\mathcal{X})\}
and, by Theorem
4.1
,
n_{\rm sph}(A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{Y}}])\leq n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])
for all
\mathcal{Y}\in\mathcal{P}_{<\infty}(\mathcal{X})
, hence
n_{\rm sph}(A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}])\leq n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])
.
Furthermore, by Proposition
6.4
and Theorem
4.1
,
A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}]
satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture, hence, by Charney–Davis
[
12
]
,
{\rm cd}(A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}])=n_{\rm sph}(A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}])\leq n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])
.
Recall that
\mathcal{P}_{\rm fin}(\Phi[\Gamma])
denotes the set of finite subsets of
\Phi[\Gamma]
.
Since
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]=A[\hat{\Gamma}]=\varinjlim_{\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm fin}(\Phi[\Gamma])}A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}]
and
{\rm cd}(A[\hat{\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}])\leq n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])
for all
\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm fin}(\Phi[\Gamma])
, by Bieri
[
7
, Theorem 4.7]
we conclude that
{\rm cd}({\rm KVA}[\Gamma])\leq n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])+1
.
This also implies that
{\rm KVA}[\Gamma]
is torsion free.

We know by Bourbaki
[
8
, Chapitre VI]
that
W[\Gamma]
admits a semi-direct product decomposition
W[\Gamma]=\mathbb{Z}^{m}\rtimes W_{0}
where
m\leq n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])
and
W_{0}
is a finite Coxeter group.
Let
H=\pi_{K}^{-1}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})
.
Then
H
is a finite index subgroup of
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
which admits a semi-direct product decomposition
H={\rm KVA}[\Gamma]\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{m}
.
By Brown
[
11
, Proposition 2.4]
it follows that:

{\rm cd}(H)\leq{\rm cd}({\rm KVA}[\Gamma])+{\rm cd}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})\leq n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])+1+m\leq 2\,n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])+1\,,

hence
{\rm VA}[\Gamma]
is virtually torsion free and
{\rm vcd}({\rm VA}[\Gamma])={\rm cd}(H)\leq 2\,n_{\rm sph}(A[\Gamma])+1
.
∎

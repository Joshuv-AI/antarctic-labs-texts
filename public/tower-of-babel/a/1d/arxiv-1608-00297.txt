Panyue Zhou, Bin Zhu

Abstract
Extriangulated categories were introduced by Nakaoka and Palu by extracting the similarities between exact categories and triangulated categories. A notion of mutation of subcategories in an extriangulated category is defined in this article. Let 𝒜 \mathcal{A} be an extension closed subcategory of an extriangulated category 𝒞 \mathscr{C} . Then the quotient category ℳ := 𝒜 / 𝒳 \mathcal{M}:=\mathcal{A}/\mathscr{X} carries naturally a triangulated structure whenever ( 𝒜 , 𝒜 ) (\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}) forms an 𝒳 \mathscr{X} -mutation pair. This result unifies many previous constructions of triangulated quotient categories, and using it gives a classification of thick triangulated subcategories of pretriangulated category 𝒞 / 𝒳 \mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X} , where 𝒳 \mathscr{X} is functorially finite in 𝒞 \mathscr{C} . When 𝒞 \mathscr{C} has Auslander-Reiten translation τ \tau , we prove that for a functorially finite subcategory 𝒳 \mathscr{X} of 𝒞 \mathscr{C} containing projectives and injectives, 𝒞 / 𝒳 \mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X} is a triangulated category if and only if ( 𝒞 , 𝒞 ) (\mathscr{C},\mathscr{C}) is 𝒳 − \mathscr{X}- mutation, and if and only if τ ​ 𝒳 ¯ = 𝒳 ¯ . \tau\underline{\mathscr{X}}=\overline{\mathscr{X}}. This generalizes a result by Jørgensen who proved the equivalence between the first and the third conditions for triangulated categories. Furthermore, we show that for such a subcategory 𝒳 \mathscr{X} of the extriangulated category 𝒞 \mathscr{C} , 𝒞 \mathscr{C} admits a new extriangulated structure such that 𝒞 \mathscr{C} is a Frobenius extriangulated category. Applications to exact categories and triangulated categories are given. From the applications we present examples that extriangulated categories are neither exact categories nor triangulated categories. Key words: Extriangulated category; Mutation; Quotient triangulated category 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 18E10; 18E30; 18E40

Introduction

Exact and triangulated categories are two fundamental structures
in algebra and geometry. A first looking at their construction shows that these two kinds categories have many different points:
while modules or sheaves are forming
abelian exact categories, complexes lead to homotopy or derived categories
that are triangulated. But if we look carefully at the construction inside the categories, they share many similarities: while exact categories admit short exact sequences, triangulated categories admit triangles.
The similarity between short exact sequences and triangles makes that it is possible to put these two notations into a unified form. This was carried out recently by Nakaoka and Palu [NP].

After a careful looking what is necessary to define a cotorsion pair on exact categories or triangulated category, Nakaoka and Palu [NP] introduced the notion of an extriangulated category by extracting those properties of
\mbox{Ext}^{1}
on exact categories and on triangulated categories that seem relevant from the point-of-view of cotorsion pairs. Using the frame of extriangulated category, they succeed in unifying and extending the construction on twin cotorsion pairs in triangulated and exact categories. The class of extriangulated categories not only contains exact categories and
extension closed subcategories of triangulated categories as examples, but it is also
closed under taking some quotients. The term “extriangulated” stands for externally triangulated by means of a bifunctor. It can also be viewed as the mixing of exact and triangulated, or as an
abbreviation of Ext-triangulated.

We want to show further the similarity and difference between exact categories and triangulated categories from the point of view of construction of quotient triangulated categories.

Quotients of exact or triangulated categories have been extensively studied in representation theory. It is in particular very much of interest to know which structure of the quotient inherits from the exact or triangulated category one starts with. Well-studied examples include:

The main aim of this article is to give a way to unify the existing different constructions
of triangulated quotient categories and study the properties of the quotients. We define a notion of mutation pairs of subcategories in
an extriangulated category, and show that the corresponding quotient categories carry triangulated structures (see Theorem
3.15
). As applications, our result unifies the triangulated quotient category construction considered by Happel [Ha], Beligiannis [B1, B2], Iyama-Yoshino [IY], Jørgensen [J], and Liu-Zhu [LZ]. We note that there are some other constructions of quotients triangulated categories by using model theory [Li] which is not covered by our construction. An immediate application of our result is to give a classification (see Theorem
3.24
) of thick triangulated subcategories of pre-triangulated category
\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}
, where
\mathscr{X}
is a functorially finite in
\mathscr{C}
.
Let
\mathscr{X}
be a functorially finite subcategory of a triangulated category
\mathscr{C}
with the
Auslander-Reiten translation
\tau

\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}
is a triangulated category if and only if

\mathscr{X}-

We then prove that for such a subcategory
\mathscr{X}
of an extriangulated category
\mathscr{C}

\mathscr{C}

\mathscr{C}

(see Theorem

If applying to triangulated categories, we not only recover Jørgensen theorem, but also get examples of Frobenius extriangulated categories which are not exact nor triangulated (see Remark

This article is organized as follows: In Section 2, we recall the definition of an extriangulated category and some basic properties which are needed in the proof of our main results. In Section 3, we show certain quotient categories of extriangulated categories form triangulated categories (Theorem

In Section 4, we show that an extriangulated version Jørgensen theorem (Theorem

3
Triangulated quotient categories

Given two objects
A
and
B
of a category
\mathscr{C}
, we denote by
\mathscr{X}(A,B)
the set of morphisms from
A
to
B
of
\mathscr{C}
which
factor through an object of
\mathscr{X}
. It is well known that
\mathscr{X}(A,B)
is a subgroup of
\mathscr{C}(A,B)
, and that the family of
these subgroups
\mathscr{X}(A,B)
forms an ideal of
\mathscr{C}
. Thus we
have the category
\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}
whose objects are objects of

\mathscr{C}
and whose morphisms are elements of
\mathscr{C}(A,B)/\mathscr{X}(A,B)
. The composition of
\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}
is induced
canonically by the composition of
\mathscr{C}
. We then have an additive functor
\pi\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}
,

\pi(A)=\overline{A}
and
\pi(f)=\overline{f}
. Such category
\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}
is called the
quotient category
of
\mathscr{C}

by
\mathscr{X}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a subcategory of an extriangulated category
\mathscr{C}
. A morphism
f\colon A\to B
in
\mathscr{C}
is called

\mathscr{X}
-monic
, if for any object
X\in\mathscr{X}
, we have that

\mathscr{C}(B,X)\xrightarrow{\mathscr{C}(f,X)}\mathscr{C}(A,X)\to 0

is exact.
Dually, a morphism
g\colon C\to D
in
\mathscr{C}
is called

\mathscr{X}
-epic
, if for any object
X\in\mathscr{X}
, we have that

\mathscr{C}(X,C)\xrightarrow{\mathscr{C}(X,g)}\mathscr{C}(X,D)\to 0

Obviously,
any left
\mathscr{X}
-approximation is
\mathscr{X}
-monic and any right

\mathscr{X}
-approximation is
\mathscr{X}
-epic.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathscr{X},\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be subcategories of an extriangulated category

, and
\mathscr{X}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
and
\mathscr{X}\subseteq\mathcal{B}

-
mutation pair

(1)

For any

-triangle

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\delta}

where

,

-approximation of

\beta

\mathscr{X}
-approximation of

(2)

\mathbb{E}
-triangle

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Corollary 4.9
.

[KIWY, Proposition 2.16]

\tau^{-1}

\mathscr{X}

\mathcal{P}

\mathcal{I}
, which satisfies

. Assume that there exists a functorial isomorphism

Then the following statements hold.

(1)

Let
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\mathscr{X}

(2)

(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{S}^{\prime})

Proof.

Remark 4.11
.

When
\mathscr{X}\not=\{0\}
, the new Frobenius extriangulated category
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E}^{\prime},\mathfrak{s}^{\prime})
in Corollary above is not triangulated, since
\mathscr{X}
is projectives and injectives and non-zero. When
\mathscr{X}\not=\mathscr{C}
, it is easy to see that the new Frobenius extriangulated category
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E}^{\prime},\mathfrak{s}^{\prime})
is not exact. Otherwise any
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}-
extension splits and then any object in
\mathscr{C}
is projective and injective. Then
\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{C}
, a contradiction. We take a subcategory
\{0\}\not=\mathscr{X}\varsubsetneqq\mathscr{C}
satisfying the condition in Corollary above, Then the new Frobenius extriangulated category
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E}^{\prime},\mathfrak{s}^{\prime})
is neither exact nor triangulated. This provides many new extriangulated categories which are not exact or triangulated, see [NP].

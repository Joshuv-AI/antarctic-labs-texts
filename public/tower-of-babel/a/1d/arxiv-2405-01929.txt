Novel Local Characteristic Decomposition Based Path-Conservative Central-Upwind Schemes
Shaoshuai Chu, Michael Herty, Alexander Kurganov

Abstract
We introduce local characteristic decomposition based path-conservative central-upwind schemes for (nonconservative) hyperbolic systems of balance laws. The proposed schemes are made to be well-balanced via a flux globalization approach, in which source terms are incorporated into the fluxes: This helps to enforce the well-balanced property when the resulting quasi-conservative system is solved using the local characteristic decomposition based central-upwind scheme recently introduced in [ A. Chertock, S. Chu, M. Herty, A. Kurganov, and M. Lukáčová-Medvi d’ ová , J. Comput. Phys., 473 (2023), Paper No. 111718]. Nonconservative product terms are also incorporated into the global fluxes using a path-conservative technique. We illustrate the performance of the developed schemes by applying them to one- and two-dimensional compressible multifluid systems and thermal rotating shallow water equations.



Key words:
Local characteristic decomposition, path-conservative central-upwind schemes, flux globalization, compressible multifluids,
thermal rotating shallow water equations.

AMS subject classification:
76M12, 65M08, 35L65, 86-08, 76T99.

1
Introduction

This paper is focused on the development of highly accurate and robust numerical methods for the nonconservative hyperbolic systems of
balance laws, which in the one-dimensional (1-D) case read as

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}=B(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{x}+\bm{S}(\bm{U}),

(1.1)

where
x
is a spatial variable,
t
is the time,
\bm{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a vector of unknown functions,
\bm{F}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are
nonlinear fluxes,
B(\bm{U})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, and
\bm{S}(\bm{U}):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are source terms.

Designing a numerical scheme for (
1.1
) is a challenging task for a number of reasons. First, it is well-known that solutions of
(
1.1
) may develop complicated nonsmooth structures even for smooth initial data. Second, the presence of nonconservative products (when

B\neq 0
) brings many challenges for studying these systems both theoretically and numerically. In general, when
\bm{U}
is discontinuous, weak
solutions of (
1.1
) cannot be understood in the sense of distributions. Instead, one can introduce Borel measure solutions

[
19
,
30
,
31
]
. This concept of weak solutions was utilized to develop path-conservative finite-volume (FV) methods;
see, e.g.,
[
35
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
36
,
38
,
1
,
9
]
and reference therein. Third, a novel scheme should be well-balanced (WB)
in the sense that it should be capable of exactly preserving some of the physically relevant steady-state solutions satisfying

\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}-B(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{x}-\bm{S}(\bm{U})=M(\bm{U})\bm{E}(\bm{U})_{x}=\bm{0}.

Here,
M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
\bm{E}
is the vector of equilibrium variables, which are constant at the steady states provided
M

is invertible; see
[
26
]
.

In this paper, we focus on one of the popular classes of FV methods, the Riemann-problem-solver-free central-upwind (CU) schemes, which were
originally introduced in
[
28
,
29
,
25
]
as a “black-box” solver for general multidimensional hyperbolic systems
of conservation laws and then successfully applied to a variety of hyperbolic systems. In
[
8
]
, the CU schemes were first reformulated
in a path-conservative framework and then extended to the 1-D nonconservative systems (
1.1
), resulting in the second-order
path-conservative CU (PCCU) schemes. These schemes are WB in the sense that they are capable of preserving simple steady-state solutions
like “lake-at-rest” of several shallow water models.

In order to design PCCU schemes capable of preserving more complicated steady states, path-conservative techniques were incorporated into
the flux globalization framework: This results in a new class of flux globalization based WB PCCU schemes introduced in
[
26
]
. A
flux globalization approach, which was introduced in
[
14
,
5
,
20
,
21
,
33
]
, relies on the following quasi-conservative
form of (
1.1
):

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{K}(\bm{U})_{x}=\bm{0},

(1.2)

where
\bm{K}(\bm{U})
is a global flux

\bm{K}(\bm{U})=\bm{F}(\bm{U})-\bm{W}(\bm{U}),\quad\bm{W}(\bm{U})=\int\limits^{x}_{\widehat{x}}B(\bm{U}){\bm{U}}_{\xi}(\xi,t)+S(\bm{U}(\xi,t))\,{\rm d}\xi,

(1.3)

and
{\widehat{x}}
is an arbitrary number. The flux globalization based WB PCCU schemes are obtained by applying the CU numerical flux to
the semi-discretization of (
1.2
) and by using the path-conservative technique for the evaluation of the integrals in (
1.3
). These
schemes have been applied to a variety of hyperbolic systems in
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
10
,
26
,
18
]
.

Even though the flux globalization based WB PCCU schemes from
[
26
]
are accurate, efficient, and robust, higher resolution of the
numerical solutions can be achieved by reducing numerical dissipation present in those schemes. This can be done, for example, by using the
local characteristic decomposition (LCD) based CU (LCD-CU) numerical fluxes instead of the original CU ones employed in

[
2
,
3
,
4
,
10
,
26
]
. The LCD-CU schemes have been recently introduced in
[
12
]
for hyperbolic systems of
conservation laws and we use the same LCD based framework to enhance the performance of the flux globalization based WB PCCU schemes.
The proposed scheme combines for the first time the modifications enhancing the performance of the CU schemes for both conservative and
nonconservative hyperbolic systems in multiple space dimensions. We derive the new LCD-PCCU schemes and apply them to two particular
systems. First, we consider the
\gamma
-based compressible multifluid systems, which can be, in principle, written in the conservative
form, but we use its nonconservative formulation to achieve high resolution of material interfaces. Second, we consider a challenging
thermal rotating shallow water (TRSW) model, which contains geometric source term due to a nonflat bottom topography, source terms due to
Coriolis forces, and generically nonconservative momentum exchange terms.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In §
2
, we briefly describe the 1-D second-order LCD-CU scheme. In §
3
, we
propose the novel scheme by considering the nonconservative system (
1.1
) and design a 1-D flux globalization based WB LCD-PCCU scheme.
In §
4
, we extend the 1-D LCD-PCCU scheme to the two-dimensional (2-D) case. Finally, in §
5
, we apply the developed
LCD-PCCU schemes to the
\gamma
-based compressible multifluid systems and TRSW equations. We illustrate the performance of the scheme on a
number of numerical examples.

2
1-D LCD-CU Scheme

In this section, we briefly describe the second-order semi-discrete LCD-CU scheme, which is a slightly modified version of the scheme from

[
12
]
. For the difference between the schemes, we refer the reader to Remarks
2.1
–
2.3
below.

We consider a 1-D hyperbolic system of conservative laws

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}=\bm{0},

(2.1)

cover the computational domain with the uniform cells
C_{j}:=[x_{j-\frac{1}{2}},x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}]
of size
\Delta x
centered at
x_{j}=(x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+x_{j+\frac{1}{2}})/2
, and denote
by
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}(t)
the computed cell averages of
\bm{U}(\cdot,t)
,

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}(t):\approx\frac{1}{\Delta x}\int\limits_{C_{j}}\bm{U}(x,t)\,{\rm d}x,\qquad j=1,\ldots,N,

which are assumed to be available at a certain time
t\geq 0
. Note that both
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}(t)
and many other indexed quantities below are
time-dependent, but from here on, we suppress their time-dependence for the sake of brevity.

The computed cell averages (
2.1
) are evolved in time by numerically solving the following system of ODEs:

\frac{{\rm d}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{\bm{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm LCD}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Delta x},

where the LCD-CU numerical fluxes are given by

\bm{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}P^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\bm{F}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}M^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\bm{F}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}Q^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big).

(2.2)

Here,
\bm{F}^{\pm}_{j}=\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j})
and
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are the one-sided values of a piecewise linear interpolant

\widetilde{\bm{U}}(x)=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+(\bm{U}_{x})_{j}(x-x_{j}),\quad x\in C_{j},

(2.3)

at the cell interface
x=x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, namely

\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+\frac{\Delta x}{2}(\bm{U}_{x})_{j},\quad\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}-\frac{\Delta x}{2}(\bm{U}_{x})_{j+1}.

(2.4)

In order to ensure the non-oscillatory nature of this reconstruction, we compute the slopes
(\bm{U}_{x})_{j}
in (
2.4
) using a generalized
minmod limiter
[
32
,
34
,
40
]
:

(\bm{U}_{x})_{j}={\rm minmod}\left(\theta\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-1}}{\Delta x},\,\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-1}}{2\Delta x},\,\theta\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}}{\Delta x}\right),\quad\theta\in[1,2],

(2.5)

applied in the component-wise manner. Here, the minmod function is defined as

{\rm minmod}(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots):=\begin{cases}\min_{j}\{z_{j}\}&\mbox{if}~z_{j}>0\quad\forall\,j,\\
\max_{j}\{z_{j}\}&\mbox{if}~z_{j}<0\quad\forall\,j,\\
0&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

(2.6)

and the parameter
\theta
can be used to control the amount of numerical viscosity present in the resulting scheme. We have taken

\theta=1.3
in all of the numerical experiments reported in §
5
.

The diagonal matrices
P^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
,
M^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, and
Q^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are given by

\displaystyle P^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}={\rm diag}\big((P^{\rm LCD}_{1})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,(P^{\rm LCD}_{d})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big),\quad M^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}={\rm diag}\big((M^{\rm LCD}_{1})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,(M^{\rm LCD}_{d})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big),

(2.7)

\displaystyle Q^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}={\rm diag}\big((Q^{\rm LCD}_{1})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,(Q^{\rm LCD}_{d})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)

with

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\big((P^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},(M^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},(Q^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)

(2.8)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\Big((\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},-(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Big)&&\mbox{if}~(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}>\varepsilon_{0},\\[1.29167pt]
&\frac{1}{a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\Big(a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Big)&&\hskip-99.58464pt\mbox{if}~(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}~~\mbox{and}~~a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}>\varepsilon_{0},\\[1.29167pt]
&\Big({\frac{1}{2}},{\frac{1}{2}},0\Big)&&\hskip-99.58464pt\mbox{if}~a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\leq\varepsilon_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where

\begin{aligned} &(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\max\left\{\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,0\right\},\\
&(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\min\left\{\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,0\right\},\end{aligned}\quad i=1,\ldots,d.

Here,
A(\bm{U})=\frac{\partial\bm{F}}{\partial\bm{U}}(\bm{U})
is the Jacobian and the matrices
R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are such that

R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
is diagonal, where
\widehat{A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=A(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})
and
\widehat{\bm{U}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
is either a simple
average
(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1})/2
or another type of average of the
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}
and
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}
states. The one-sided local
speeds of propagation
a^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
can be estimated using the largest and the smallest eigenvalues of
A
, for example, by taking

\displaystyle a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\max\left\{\lambda_{d}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\lambda_{d}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),0\right\},\quad a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\min\left\{\lambda_{1}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\lambda_{1}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),0\right\}.

Finally,
\varepsilon_{0}
in (
2.8
) is a very small desingularization constant, which is taken
\varepsilon_{0}=10^{-18}
in all of the
numerical examples reported in §
5
.

Remark 2.1

The numerical flux (
2.2
) is identical to the LCD-CU numerical flux in
[
12
]
, which we have simplified using the fact that

P^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+M^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=I.

(2.9)

Remark 2.2

We perform a piecewise linear reconstruction for the conservative variables
\bm{U}
rather than the local characteristic variables as it was
done in
[
12
]
. As no substantial spurious oscillations have been observed in any of the conducted numerical examples, there is no
need to switch to the local characteristic variables in this case. This allows the developed scheme to be more efficient than the original
LCD-CU scheme from
[
12
]
.

Remark 2.3

The desingularization in (
2.8
) is performed differently than in
[
12
]
. Our numerical experiments indicate that the current
desingularization is more robust compared with the previous desingularization strategy.

3
1-D Flux Globalization Based LCD-PCCU Scheme

In this section, we propose an extension of the LCD-CU scheme from §
2
to the nonconservative system (
1.1
) and design a 1-D
flux globalization based LCD-PCCU scheme.

We apply the LCD-CU scheme to the quasi-conservative system (
1.2
)–(
1.3
) to obtain

\frac{{\rm d}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{\bm{\mathcal{K}}^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{\mathcal{K}}^{\rm LCD}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Delta x},

(3.1)

where the numerical fluxes
\bm{\mathcal{K}}^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are given by (compare with (
2.2
))

\bm{\mathcal{K}}^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}P^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\bm{K}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}M^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\bm{K}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}Q^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\breve{\bm{U}}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\breve{\bm{U}}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big).

(3.2)

Here,
\bm{K}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are the global fluxes, which are computed as the same as in
[
3
, §2]
.

The global fluxes
\bm{K}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are computed using the point values
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, which, in order to ensure the WB property of the
resulting scheme, are to be obtained with the help of a piecewise linear reconstruction (
2.3
)–(
2.6
) applied to the equilibrium
variables
\bm{E}
(instead of
\bm{U}
):

\bm{E}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{E}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+\frac{\Delta x}{2}(\bm{E}_{x})_{j},\quad\bm{E}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{E}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}-\frac{\Delta x}{2}(\bm{E}_{x})_{j+1},

where
\bm{E}_{j}:=\bm{E}(\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j})
. We then obtain the corresponding values
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
by solving the (nonlinear systems of)
equations

\bm{E}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})=\bm{E}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bm{E}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})=\bm{E}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}

(3.3)

for
\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, respectively. We emphasize that the values
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
cannot be used in (
3.2
) instead of

\breve{\bm{U}}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
as
\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
may not be equal to
\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
at discrete steady states. We therefore use another set of point
values
\breve{\bm{U}}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, which are obtained by solving modified versions of (
3.3
):

\breve{\bm{E}}(\breve{\bm{U}}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})=\bm{E}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\breve{\bm{E}}(\breve{\bm{U}}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})=\bm{E}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}.

Here, the functions
\breve{\bm{E}}
are the modifications of the functions
\bm{E}
, which are made in such a way that

\breve{\bm{U}}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\breve{\bm{U}}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
as long as
\bm{E}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{E}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and, at the same time,

\breve{\bm{U}}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+{\cal O}((\Delta x)^{2})
and
\breve{\bm{U}}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+{\cal O}((\Delta x)^{2})
for smooth solutions; see

[
10
,
3
,
2
,
4
,
26
]
for examples of such modifications.

The diagonal matrices
P^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
,
M^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, and
Q^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
in (
3.2
) are defined by (
2.7
)–(
2.8
),
but with

\begin{aligned} (\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}&=\max\left\{\lambda_{i}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,\lambda_{i}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,0\right\},\\
(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}&=\min\left\{\lambda_{i}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,\lambda_{i}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,0\right\},\end{aligned}\quad i=1,\ldots,d,

where
{\cal A}(\bm{U})=\frac{\partial\bm{F}}{\partial\bm{U}}(\bm{U})-B(\bm{U})
and the matrices
R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are such that

R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{\cal A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
is diagonal for
\widehat{\cal A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}={\cal A}(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})
. Finally, the one-sided local
speeds of propagation
a^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are estimated by

\displaystyle a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\max\left\{\lambda_{d}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\lambda_{d}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,0\right\},\quad a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\min\left\{\lambda_{1}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\lambda_{1}\big({\cal A}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,0\right\}.

Remark 3.1

The introduced 1-D semi-discrete flux globalization based LCD-PCCU scheme is WB. In order to prove this, we only need to show that if the
discrete data are at a steady state, namely, if
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.51114pt$E$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}\equiv\widehat{E}=\texttt{Const}
, then the RHS of (
3.1
) vanishes. Following
the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
, we obtain

\bm{K}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{K}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{K}^{+}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}=\widehat{\bm{K}}=\texttt{Const},\quad\forall j.

(3.4)

Substituting (
3.4
) and (
2.9
) into (
3.2
) and taking into account the fact that at the steady states

\breve{\bm{U}}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\breve{\bm{U}}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},~\forall j
, yields

\bm{\mathcal{K}}^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\widehat{\bm{K}}+R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}Q^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\breve{\bm{U}}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\breve{\bm{U}}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)=\widehat{\bm{K}},\quad\forall j,

which ensures that the RHS of (
3.1
) vanishes.

4
2-D Flux Globalization Based LCD-PCCU Scheme

In this section, we propose a novel 2-D LCD-PCCU scheme for

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}+\bm{G}(\bm{U})_{y}=B(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{x}+C(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{y}+\bm{S}^{x}(\bm{U})+\bm{S}^{y}(\bm{U}),

(4.1)

where
\bm{G}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
C(\bm{U})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
\bm{S}^{x}(\bm{U}):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

\bm{S}^{y}(\bm{U}):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. To this end, we design the 2-D scheme in a “dimension-by-dimension” manner.

As in the 1-D case, we first rewrite the general 2-D nonconservative system (
4.1
) in the following quasi-conservative form:

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{K}(\bm{U})_{x}+\bm{L}(\bm{U})_{y}=\bm{0},\quad\bm{K}(\bm{U})=\bm{F}(\bm{U})-\bm{W}^{x}(\bm{U}),\quad\bm{L}(\bm{U})=\bm{G}(\bm{U})-\bm{W}^{y}(\bm{U}),

(4.2)

where

\bm{W}^{x}(\bm{U}):=\int\limits^{x}_{\widehat{x}}\left[B(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{\xi}(\xi,t)+\bm{S}^{x}(\bm{U}(\xi,t))\right]{\rm d}\xi,\quad\bm{W}^{y}(\bm{U}):=\int\limits^{y}_{\widehat{y}}\left[C(\bm{U})\bm{U}_{\eta}(\eta,t)+\bm{S}^{y}(\bm{U}(\eta,t))\right]{\rm d}\eta,

with
\widehat{x}
and
\widehat{y}
being arbitrary numbers.

Applying the 2-D version of the semi-discrete LCD-CU scheme from §
2
to the quasi-conservative system (
4.2
) results in the
following system of ODEs:

\frac{{\rm d}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{\bm{\mathcal{K}}^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\bm{\mathcal{K}}^{\rm LCD}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}}{\Delta x}-\frac{\bm{\mathcal{L}}^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\bm{\mathcal{L}}^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}}{\Delta y},

(4.3)

where the numerical fluxes
\bm{\mathcal{K}}^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
\bm{\mathcal{L}}^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are given by

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{K}}^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}

\displaystyle=R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}P^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}R^{-1}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\bm{K}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}M^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}R^{-1}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\bm{K}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}

(4.4)

\displaystyle+R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}Q^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}R^{-1}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big(\breve{\bm{U}}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\breve{\bm{U}}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big),

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{L}}^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=R_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}P^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}R^{-1}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\bm{L}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+R_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}M^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}R^{-1}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\bm{L}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle+R_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}Q^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}R^{-1}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big(\breve{\bm{U}}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\breve{\bm{U}}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big).

All of the terms in (
4.4
) are computed as in §
3
separately in the
x
- and
y
-directions.

The global fluxes
\bm{K}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
\bm{L}^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are computed using the WB path-conservative technique as in
[
2
]
. The
LCD matrices
P^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
M^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
Q^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
P^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
,
M^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
, and

Q^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are computed as in
[
12
]
, but based on the eigensystems of

\widehat{\cal A}^{\,\bm{F}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}={\cal A}^{\bm{F}}(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}):=\frac{\partial\bm{F}}{\partial\bm{U}}(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})-B(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})
and

\widehat{\cal A}^{\,\bm{G}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}={\cal A}^{\bm{G}}(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}):=\frac{\partial\bm{G}}{\partial\bm{U}}(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})-C(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})
. As in §
2
, we do not employ local characteristic
variables in the piecewise linear reconstruction and perform the desingularization of
P^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
M^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,

Q^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
P^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
,
M^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
, and
Q^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
as in (
2.8
).

Remark 4.1

The introduced 2-D semi-discrete flux globalization based LCD-PCCU scheme is WB in the sense that it is capable of exactly preserving
discrete steady states, for which
\bm{K}(\bm{U})
is independent of
x
and
\bm{L}(\bm{U})
is independent of
y
. We omit the proof for the sake of
brevity.

\big((P_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},(M_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},(Q_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\Big(a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Big)&&\mbox{if}~a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}>\varepsilon_{0},\\[1.29167pt]

5.1
\gamma
-Based Compressible Multifluid Equations

In this section, we show the applications of the developed LCD-PCCU schemes introduced in §
3
and §
4
to the 1-D and 2-D

\gamma
-based compressible multifluid systems
[
11
,
39
]
, which in the 2-D case can be written as (
4.1
) with

\bm{U}=\big(\rho,\rho u,\rho v,E,\Gamma,\Pi\big)^{\top}
,
\bm{F}(\bm{U})=\big(\rho u,\rho u^{2}+p,\rho uv,u(E+p),0,0\big)^{\top}
,

\bm{G}(\bm{U})=\big(\rho v,\rho uv,\rho v^{2}+p,v(E+p),0,0)^{\top}
,
\bm{S}^{x}(\bm{U})=\bm{S}^{y}(\bm{U})=\bm{0}
,
B(\bm{U})={\rm diag}(0,0,0,0,u,u)
, and

C(\bm{U})={\rm diag}(0,0,0,0,v,v)
. Here,
\rho
is the density,
u
and
v
are the
x
- and
y
-velocities,
p
is the pressure, and
E
is
the total energy. The system is closed through the following (stiff) equation of state (EOS) for each of the fluid components:

p=(\gamma-1)\left[E-\frac{\rho}{2}(u^{2}+v^{2})\right]-\gamma\pi_{\infty},

(5.1)

where the parameters
\gamma
and
\pi_{\infty}
represent the specific heat ratio and stiffness parameter, respectively (when

\pi_{\infty}\equiv 0
for all of the components, the system reduces to the ideal gas multicomponent case). The variables
\Gamma
and
\Pi

are defined as
\Gamma:=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}
and
\Pi:=\frac{\gamma\pi_{\infty}}{\gamma-1}
.

Remark 5.1

We note that the studied
\gamma
-based compressible multifluid equations contain no source terms and thus there is no need to numerically
enforce the WB property. We therefore do not need to introduce any equilibrium variables—instead, we reconstruct the conservative
quantities
\bm{U}
and replace
\breve{\bm{U}}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
,
\breve{\bm{U}}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
, and
\breve{\bm{U}}^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
in (
3.2
) and (
4.4
) by

\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
,
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
, and
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
, respectively.

Remark 5.2

In Appendix
A
, we provide the expressions for the matrices
\widehat{\cal A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
\widehat{\cal A}^{\,\bm{F}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and the
corresponding matrices
R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
for the 1-D and 2-D
\gamma
-based compressible multifluid systems, respectively. The
matrices
\widehat{\cal A}^{\,\bm{G}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
and
R_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
can be obtained in an analogous way.

denotes the thickness of the fluid layer,

and

\rho

\rho_{0}

\theta
and
\theta_{0}

and
\beta
are positive constants.

,

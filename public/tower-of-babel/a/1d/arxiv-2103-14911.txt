Abstract
. We will give a general criterion — the existence of an F F -obstruction — for showing that a subgroup of PL + ​ I \mathrm{PL}_{+}I does not embed into Thompson’s group F F . An immediate consequence is that Cleary’s “golden ratio” group F τ F_{\tau} does not embed into F F . Our results also yield a new proof that Stein’s groups F p , q F_{p,q} [ 18 ] do not embed into F F , a result first established by Lodha using his theory of coherent actions. We develop the basic theory of F F -obstructions and show that they exhibit certain rigidity phenomena of independent interest. In the course of establishing the main result of the paper, we prove a dichotomy theorem for subgroups of PL + ​ I \mathrm{PL}_{+}I . In addition to playing a central role in our proof, it is strong enough to imply both Rubin’s Reconstruction Theorem restricted to the class of subgroups of PL + ​ I \mathrm{PL}_{+}I and also Brin’s Ubiquity Theorem.

1.
Introduction

In this article, we aim to give a partial answer to the following question:

When does a group of piecewise linear homeomorphisms of the unit interval
fail to embed into Richard Thompson’s group
F
?

Thompson’s group
F
is the subgroup of
\mathrm{PL}_{+}I

consisting of those functions whose breakpoints occur at dyadic rationals and whose slopes are powers of
2
.
We isolate the notion of an
F
-obstruction
based on Poincaré’s
rotation number
and
show that subgroups of
\mathrm{PL}_{+}I
which contain
F
-obstructions do not embed into
F
.

In the course of proving the main result of the paper, we establish a dichotomy theorem for subgroups
of
\mathrm{PL}_{+}I
.
This result seems likely to be of independent interest as it is already sufficiently powerful to prove both
Brin’s Ubiquity Theorem
[
4
]
and the restriction of
Rubin’s Reconstruction Theorem
[
17
, Corollary 3.5(c)]
to the class of subgroups of
\mathrm{PL}_{+}I

(see also
[
16
, Theorem 4]
and
[
1
, E16.3]
which were precursors to
[
17
]
).

1.1.
Rotation numbers and
F
-obstructions

Recall that if
\gamma
is a homeomorphism of the circle
\mathbf{R}/\mathbf{Z}
, then the
rotation number
of

\gamma
is defined to be

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\widetilde{\gamma}^{n}(x)-x}{n}

modulo
1

where
\widetilde{\gamma}:\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}
is a lift of
\gamma

(this limit always exists and, modulo 1, does not depend on
x
or the choice of
\widetilde{\gamma}
).
Observe that if
\gamma
is a rotation of
\mathbf{R}/\mathbf{Z}
by
r\in(0,1)
, then we can take

\widetilde{\gamma}(x)=x+r
and the rotation number of
\gamma
is
r
.
In fact Poincaré showed that if
\gamma
is any homeomorphism such that no finite power has a fixed point,

\gamma
is semiconjugate to the irrational rotation specified by its rotation number.
On the other hand, if
\gamma^{q}
has a fixed point for some
q
, then we can take
x\in\mathbf{R}
and
\widetilde{\gamma}

such that
\widetilde{\gamma}^{q}(x)=x+p
for some
p\in\mathbf{Z}
with
0\leq p<q
.
It follows that the rotation number is
p/q
.

If
f,g\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{+}I
and
s\in I
are such that

s<f(s)\leq g(s)<f(g(s))=g(f(s))

then the
rotation number
f
modulo
g
at
s
is the rotation number of the
function on
[s,g(s))
defined by
x\mapsto g^{-m}(f(x))

where
m
is such that
s\leq g^{-m}(f(x))<g(s)
.
This map is a homeomorphism of a circle when
[s,g(s))
is given a suitable topology.

A pair
(f,g)
of elements of
\mathrm{PL}_{+}I
is an
F
-obstruction
if there is an
s
such that either:

•

s<f(s)\leq g(s)<f(g(s))=g(f(s))

and the rotation number of
f
modulo
g
at
s
is irrational;

•

s>f(s)\geq g(s)>f(g(s))=g(f(s))

and the rotation number of
f^{-1}
modulo
g^{-1}
at
f(g(s))
is irrational.

It follows from work of Ghys and Sergiescu
[
12
]
that the standard way of representing

F
in
\mathrm{PL}_{+}I
does not contain any
F
-obstructions
(see Section
3
).

The main result of this paper is that
the property of being an
F
-obstruction is preserved by monomorphisms into
\mathrm{PL}_{+}I
.

Theorem 1
.

If
(f,g)
is an
F
-obstruction
and
\phi:\langle f,g\rangle\to\mathrm{PL}_{+}I
is a monomorphism,
then
(\phi(f),\phi(g))
is an
F
-obstruction.
In particular, if
G\leq\mathrm{PL}_{+}I
contains an
F
-obstruction,
then
G
does not embed into
F
.

1.2.
A dichotomy for subgroups of
\mathrm{PL}_{+}I

Theorem
1
is first established for
F
-obstructions which generate a group
with a single
orbital
— a component of support.
The general case is then handled by way of a dichotomy theorem for subgroups of
\mathrm{PL}_{+}I
.
This dichotomy is strong enough to imply both Brin’s Ubiquity Theorem
[
4
]

and a form of Rubin’s Reconstruction Theorem
[
17
, Corollary 3.5(c)]
for subgroups of
\mathrm{PL}_{+}I

(see Section
5
).

If

, then we say that

is a
resolvable

if

is an orbital of
G

\{\operatorname{supt}(g)\cap J\mid g\in G\}

If

Our dichotomy theorem can now be stated as follows:

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that

and

is a resolvable orbital of
G
.
If

(i<n)
is a sequence of orbitals of

then exactly one of the following is true:

(1)

There is a

(2)

There is an

The reader is referred to

]

3.

In this section we’ll prove the following proposition.

Proposition 2

No pair of elements of the standard model of

Proof.

Recall that Thompson’s group

consists of all piecewise linear homeomorphisms
of
\mathbf{R}/\mathbf{Z}

to a dyadic rational, whose breakpoints are dyadic rationals,

Ghys and Sergiescu
[

Let

,

be a dyadic rational such that

.
By conjugating by an element of

and revising

By further conjugating by an element

Proof of Theorem

(i<n)
list the orbitals of

is an injection,

can not hold.
Therefore there is an

has no isolated points and

is somewhere dense.
It is easily checked that if

Rubin’s Theorem asserts that

and

are locally compact and if

is nonempty with closure contained in

.
Let
K_{i}

(i<n)
list the orbitals of

which intersect
\operatorname{supt}(\phi(g_{0}))
— there are only finitely many such

Since

G
,
the only element of

which commutes with every conjugate of

is the identity.
Apply Theorem
2
to the group
\Gamma
and observe that
the first alternative cannot hold since if

is not the identity,

fails to commute with

for some

Since the support of

is contained in

, it must be that

\phi(g)|_{K_{i}}
is nontrivial for some

.
Thus there is an
i<n
and a
\Gamma
-equivariant continuous surjection

\theta:K_{i}\to J
.
Similarly, there is a
\Gamma
-equivariant continuous surjection

from some orbital

of

By the observation made at the start of the proof,

In particular

∎

Remark
.

It should be noted that Theorem

For example, since

It would be interesting to know if there are broader contexts in which Theorem

(i<n)

.

to the group

There is a subset

Remark
.

While we used Brin’s Ubiquity Theorem to prove Theorem

Even so, the purpose of deriving Corollaries

7.
Some examples

In this section, we will prove Corollaries
1
–
3
.
Recall that Cleary’s group
F_{\tau}
is the subgroup of
\mathrm{PL}_{+}I
consisting of those
elements whose singularities are in
\mathbf{Z}[\tau]
and whose slopes are powers of
\tau
,
where
\tau
is the solution to
\tau^{2}=\tau+1
with
\tau>1
.
If
1<p<q
are relatively prime integers, Stein’s group
F_{p,q}
is the subgroup of
\mathrm{PL}_{+}I
consisting
of those elements whose singularities are in
\mathbf{Z}[\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}]
and whose slopes are the product of a power of
p
and
a power of
q
.

The following observations will allow us to show that Cleary’s and Stein’s groups contain

F
-obstructions.

Observation 1
.

Suppose that
f,g\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{+}I
and for some
s
and
0<\xi<\eta
,

xf=x+\xi
and
xg=x+\eta
whenever
s\leq x\leq s+\eta
.
Then the rotation number of
f
modulo
g
at
s
is defined and equals
\xi/\eta
.

Observation 2
.

Suppose that
f,g\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{+}I
and for some
s_{0}<s_{1}
and
1<a<b
,

xf=a(x-s_{0})+s_{0}
and
xg=b(x-s_{0})+s_{0}
whenever

s_{0}\leq x\leq s_{1}
.
If
s\in(s_{0},s_{1})
is such that
sg\leq s_{1}
, then the rotation number of
f

modulo
g
at
s
is defined and equals
\log_{b}(a)

The second observation is a consequence of the first by conjugating

f
and
g
by
\log_{b}\frac{x-s_{0}}{s}
.
If
1<p<q
are relatively prime integers, then
\log_{q}(p)
is irrational.
Since
F_{p,q}
contains elements which have slope
p
and
q
near
0
, Corollary
2
follows
from Observation
2
and Theorem
1
.

We now turn to Cleary’s group
F_{\tau}
.
Define
f,g\in F_{\tau}
by

xf:=\begin{cases}x\tau&\textrm{ if }0\leq x\leq\tau^{-3}\\
x+\tau^{-2}-\tau^{-3}&\textrm{ if }\tau^{-3}\leq x\leq\tau^{-1}\\
x\tau^{-1}+\tau^{-2}&\textrm{ if }\tau^{-1}\leq x\leq 1\end{cases}

xg:=\begin{cases}x\tau^{2}&\textrm{ if }0\leq x\leq\tau^{-4}\\
x+\tau^{-2}-\tau^{-4}&\textrm{ if }\tau^{-4}\leq x\leq\tau^{-1}\\
x\tau^{-2}+\tau^{-1}&\textrm{ if }\tau^{-1}\leq x\leq 1\end{cases}

If we set
s:=\tau^{-3}
, then

sf=\tau^{-2}<\tau^{-2}+\tau^{-3}-\tau^{-4}=\tau^{-1}-\tau^{-4}=sg<\tau^{-1}.

It follows from Observation
1
that the rotation number of
f
modulo
g
at

s
is defined and equals

\frac{\tau^{-2}-\tau^{-3}}{\tau^{-2}-\tau^{-4}}=\frac{\tau^{2}-\tau}{\tau^{2}-1}=\tau^{-1}.

Since
\tau^{-1}
is irrational,
(f,g)
is an
F
-obstruction.

Finally, we wish to show that the group generated by
F\cup F^{t\mapsto t-\xi}
contains an
F
-obstruction
whenever
0<\xi<1
.
Recall that
F
is the subgroup of
\mathrm{PL}_{+}I
consisting of those elements whose singularities occur at
dyadic rationals and whose slopes are powers of
2
.
Let
\xi
be given and let
n
be such that
2^{-n}<\xi<1-2^{-n+2}
.
Observe that the following functions
f
,
g_{0}
, and
g_{1}

are in either
F
or
F^{t\mapsto t-\xi}
:

xf:=\begin{cases}2x+\xi&\textrm{ if }-\xi\leq x\leq 2^{-n}-\xi\\
x+2^{-n}&\textrm{ if }2^{-n}-\xi\leq x\leq 1-2^{-n+1}-\xi\\
2^{-1}(x+1-\xi)&\textrm{ if }1-2^{-n+1}-\xi\leq x\leq 1-\xi\\
x&\textrm{ otherwise}\end{cases}

xg_{0}:=\begin{cases}2x&\textrm{ if }0\leq x\leq 2^{-1}-2^{-n-1}\\
x+2^{-1}-2^{-n-1}&\textrm{ if }2^{-1}-2^{-n-1}\leq x\leq 2^{-1}\\
2^{-n}x+1-2^{-n}&\textrm{ if }2^{-1}\leq x\leq 1\\
x&\textrm{ otherwise}\end{cases}

xg_{1}:=\begin{cases}2^{n}(x+\xi)-\xi&\textrm{ if }-\xi\leq x\leq 2^{-n-1}-\xi\\
x+2^{-1}-2^{-n-1}&\textrm{ if }2^{-n-1}-\xi\leq x\leq 2^{-n}-\xi\\
2^{-1}(x+\xi)+2^{-1}-\xi&\textrm{ if }2^{-n}-\xi\leq x\leq 1-\xi\\
x&\textrm{ otherwise}\end{cases}

Set
g:=g_{0}g_{1}
.
Observe that by our choice of

0\leq x\leq(1-\xi)/2
, then:

xf=x+2^{-n}\qquad xg=x+(1-\xi)/2.

Since
0f=2^{-n}<(1-\xi)/2=0g
, it follows from Observation
2

that the rotation number of

modulo

at
0
is defined and equals

2^{-n+1}/(1-\xi)
, which is irrational.
Hence
(f,g)
is an
F
-obstruction.

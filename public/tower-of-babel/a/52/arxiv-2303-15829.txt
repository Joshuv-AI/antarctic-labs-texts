Abstract
. Given an elliptic curve E E over a perfect defectless henselian valued field ( F , val ) (F,\mathrm{val}) with perfect residue field k F \textbf{k}_{F} and valuation ring 𝒪 F \mathcal{O}_{F} , there exists an integral separated smooth group scheme ℰ \mathcal{E} over 𝒪 F \mathcal{O}_{F} with ℰ × Spec ​ 𝒪 F Spec ​ F ≅ E \mathcal{E}\times_{\text{Spec }\mathcal{O}_{F}}\text{Spec }F\cong E . If char ⁡ ( k F ) ≠ 2 , 3 \mathrm{char}(\textbf{k}_{F})\neq 2,3 then one can be found over 𝒪 F a ​ l ​ g \mathcal{O}_{F^{alg}} such that the definable group ℰ ⁡ ( 𝒪 ) \mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}) is the maximal generically stable subgroup of E E . We also give some partial results on general Abelian varieties over F F . The construction of ℰ \mathcal{E} is by means of generating a birational group law over 𝒪 F \mathcal{O}_{F} by the aid of a generically stable generic type of a definable subgroup of E E .



1.
Introduction

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over the field of fractions
F=\mathrm{Frac}(R)
of a valuation ring
R
. A
model
of
E
is a scheme
\mathcal{E}
(of finite type) over
R
for which
\mathcal{E}\times_{\Spec R}\Spec F\cong E
. There are many different models one can consider. For example, if
E
is given by a Weierstrass equation over
R
then we can take the projective closure in
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{R}
; we get a proper model over
R
but it might not be smooth. If we now take the smooth locus over
R
the resulting model is smooth but we might lose properness. We might also desire the model to be a group scheme over
R
.

When
R
is a discrete valuation ring, for any abelian variety
A
over
F
, Néron constructed a model
\mathcal{A}
over
R
which can be seen as a best possible model. Néron relaxed the assumption of properness and concentrated on smoothness and the group structure. The model Néron constructed (
the Néron model of
A
over
R
) is a smooth separated group scheme of finite type over
R
satisfying that
\mathcal{A}\times_{\Spec R}\Spec F\cong A
and the
Néron mapping property
. This latter property is extremely important, but will not be important here; we just remark that it implies that
\mathcal{A}(R)=\mathcal{A}_{F}(F)
.

It is well known that if
R
is a valuation subring of an algebraically closed field then a Néron model might not necessarily exist, even for an elliptic curve over
R
(see, e.g., Remark
5.19
). Nevertheless, one might hope to find well-behaved models. See
[
Nér64
,
BLR90
]
for more information on models over DVRs.

The main aim of this paper is to give a model theoretic approach for constructing such models by means of finding birational group laws (in the sense of
[
BLR90
]
) over
R
. We build heavily on results from
[
HRK19
]
and
[
Hal19
]
, where generically stable groups in algebraically closed valued fields and stably pointed varieties were studied, respectively. Before stating the main theorems we elaborate on the former.

Let
(K,\val)
be an algebraically closed valued field. A type-definable subgroup
H\subseteq G
of an algebraic group
G
over
K
is said to be a connected generically stable group if there exists a generically stable type
p
, concentrated on
H
, satisfying that
gp=p
for any
g\in H
(so
p
is the unique generic type of
H
). We recall that a global
K
-invariant type
p
concentrated on
G
is generically stable if for any open affine subvariety
V\subseteq G
, on which
p
is concentrated and
L\succ K
,
\val(f(c))\in\Gamma_{L}
for any
f\in L[V]
and
c\models p|L
.

We now give the main theorem for elliptic curves. Let
F
be a perfect defectless henselian (non-trivially) valued field with a perfect residue field
\textbf{k}_{F}
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{F}
be its valuation ring and let
\mathcal{O}_{F^{alg}}
be its extension to the algebraic closure
F^{alg}
of
F
. Below the types and definable groups are objects in the algebraically closed valued field
F^{alg}
.

Theorem

(Theorem
5.15
, Theorem
5.21
)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve given by a Weierstrass equation over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
.

(1)

There exists a smooth integral separated
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-group scheme
\mathcal{E}
of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
with geometrically integral fibers satisfying that
\mathcal{E}_{F}\cong E
and

(a)

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{F^{alg}})
is a connected generically stable subgroup of
\mathcal{E}(F^{alg})=E(F^{alg})
,

(b)

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{F^{alg}})=E(F^{alg})
if and only if
\mathcal{E}
is an Abelian scheme over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
if and only if
\Delta\in\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
, where
\Delta
is the discriminant of
E
.

(2)

Assuming
\mathrm{char}(\textbf{k}_{F})\neq 2,3
, there exists
\gamma_{\infty}\in\Gamma_{F^{alg}}
satisfying:

(a)

For any
\gamma\leq\gamma_{\infty}
there is a smooth integral separated
\mathcal{O}_{F^{alg}}
-group scheme
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{F^{alg}}
with integral fibers and
(\mathcal{E}_{\gamma})_{F^{alg}}\cong E_{F^{alg}}
.

(b)

For any
\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}\leq\gamma_{\infty}
,
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma_{1}}(\mathcal{O}_{F^{alg}})\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{\gamma_{2}}(\mathcal{O}_{F^{alg}})
and both are generically stable subgroups of
E(F^{alg})
.

(c)

Conversely, for any integral group scheme
\mathcal{G}
of finite type
\mathcal{O}_{F^{alg}}
with integral special fiber and
\mathcal{G}_{F^{alg}}\cong E_{F^{alg}}
, there exists
\gamma\leq\gamma_{\infty}
with
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{F^{alg}})=\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}(\mathcal{O}_{F^{alg}})
, and

(d)

For any connected generically stable type-definable subgroup
H\leq G
there is
\gamma\leq\gamma_{\infty}
with
H=\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}(\mathcal{O}_{F^{alg}})
.

If
F
has discrete value group and
E
is given by a minimal Weierstrass equation then
\mathcal{E}
is isomorphic to the identity component of the Néron model of
E
(Proposition
5.20
).

For general Abelian varieties over an algebraically closed valued field
K
, with valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, we have the following partial result.

Theorem

(Theorem
4.8
)
.

Let
A
be an Abelian variety over
K
and assume that
A
is a connected generically stable group with a unique generic type
p
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists an open affine subvariety
V\subseteq A
such that

\{f\in K[V]:p\vdash\val(f(x))\geq 0\}

is a finitely generated algebra over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

(2)

There exists an integral Abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}
over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with
\mathcal{A}_{K}\cong A
.

Our hope is that this theorem may be generalized to the generic type of the maximal generically stable subgroup, in the sense of
[
HRK19
]
, and that condition (1) always holds.

The key model theoretic input is the following, stated for algebraically closed valued fields just for simplicity; it holds for valued fields as above but requires some more assumptions. It should be seen as an analog of
[
HRK19
, Theorem 6.11]
for non-affine algebraic groups. We do not know if the finite generation assumption can be dropped.

Proposition

(Proposition
4.7
)
.

Let
G
be an algebraic group over
K
and let
H
be a connected generically stable Zariski dense type-definable subgroup with
p
its generically stable generic. Further assume that there exists an open affine subvariety
V\subseteq G
with

\{f\in K[V]:p\vdash\val(f(x))\geq 0\}

a finitely generated algebra over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

Then there exists a smooth integral separated
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-group scheme
\mathcal{G}
of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with geometrically integral fibers satisfying that
\mathcal{G}_{K}\cong G
and that under this isomorphism

Valued fields

Let
(F,\val)
be a non-trivially valued field with value group
\Gamma_{F}
. The valuation ring of
F
is the ring
\mathcal{O}_{F}=\{x\in F:\val(x)\geq 0\}
. It is a local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{F}=\{x\in F:\val(x)>0\}
. The residue field is the quotient
\textbf{k}_{F}=\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{m}_{F}
and the quotient map
\res:\mathcal{O}_{F}\to\textbf{k}_{F}
is called the residue map.

Let ACVF be the theory of non trivially valued algebraically closed fields in the three sorted language: the valued field sort which will be denoted by
\mathrm{VF}
, the value group by
\Gamma
and the residue field by
k
.

We will treat the valued field sort and the model as interchangeable, e.g. when we say that
K
is a model of ACVF we really mean that
K=\mathrm{VF}(M)
for some
M\models
ACVF. For any definable set
D
and set
A
, we will write

for
\dcl(A)\cap D
. So

\Gamma(A):=\dcl(A)\cap\Gamma
, and
\textbf{k}(A):=\dcl(A)\cap\textbf{k}

We work in some large (larger than any set or field in question) saturated model
\mathbb{K}
of ACVF (technically
\mathbb{K}=\mathrm{VF}(\mathbb{U})
for some saturated model
\mathbb{U}
).

Generically stable groups

We review some definitions from
[
HRK19
]
.

Let
G
be a type-definable group,
p
an
A
-definable type on
G
and
g\in G
. The
left translate
of
p
by

, is the definable type such that for any

d\models p|A^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow gd\models gp|A^{\prime}.

Similarly the right translate
pg
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a type-definable group. A definable type

concentrated on
G
is

if for any
A=\acl(A)
over which it is defined and
g\in G
,

is definable over
A
. Similarly right generic.

Any generically stable left generic is also right generic.

Definition 2.3
.

A type-definable group will be called

if it has a generically stable generic.

For a generically stable group

, let

be the intersection of all definable subgroups of finite index. It is of bounded index and called the
connected component
of

. We have the following:

is the unique generic type of

if and only if for all

,

if and only if

. If

.

We will assume basic knowledge of schemes and group schemes, see
[

]

For a field

By an algebraic group over

of finite type over

For an affine scheme

we denote by

the

defined by

For any variety

over

to

. Since

is geometrically integral over

is still a variety over

.

Let
F
be a valued field. Most of our schemes over

will be quasi-compact integral and faithfully flat separated dominant over

. For a valued field extension

, the base change
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{O}_{L}}=\mathcal{V}\times_{\Spec\mathcal{O}_{F}}\Spec\mathcal{O}_{L}
is still quasi-compact and faithfully flat separated dominant over
\mathcal{O}_{L}
. If we further assume that
\mathcal{V}_{F}
is a variety over
F
then by Fact
3.11
,
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{O}_{L}}
is also an integral scheme. As
\mathcal{V}
is separated over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, by the valuative criterion for separatedness
[

, 01KZ]
, we have
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{O}_{L}}(\mathcal{O}_{L})\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{L}(L)
for any valued field extension

.

We recall that by
[
Nag66
]
, every flat scheme of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
is finitely presented over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
; we will use this fact freely.

We denote by
V
the definable set
V_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathbb{K})
, and by
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O})
the pro-definable set
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}}}(\mathcal{O})
, hopefully without causing too much confusion.

Given such a scheme
\mathcal{V}
with
\mathcal{V}_{F}
a variety, we can view

as a type-definable subset of the definable set

or as a pro-definable subset of an inverse limit of powers of

, and then

2.1.
The Functor

We review some necessary definitions and results from
[
Hal21
]
. The results of this section will be tacitly assumed throughout the paper without further reference.

Let
K
be an algebraically closed valued field. The category
(\text{SPVar}/{K})
, of
stably pointed varieties
, is the category of pairs
(V,p)
, where
V
is a variety over
K
and
p
is a Zariski dense generically stable type over
K
, concentrated on
V
. Maps are morphisms between varieties that map the types accordingly. The category
(\text{SPVar}^{\text{aff}}/{K})
is the restriction of
(\text{SPVar}/{K})
to affine varieties.

We define a functor from
(\text{SPVar}^{\text{aff}}/{K})
to the category of affine schemes over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
given by
\Phi(V,p)=\Spec K[V]^{p}
, where

K[V]^{p}=\{f\in K[V]:p\vdash\val(f(x))\geq 0\}.

The scheme
\mathcal{V}:=\Phi(V,p)
is an affine integral scheme flat over
\mathcal{O}_{K}

[
Hal21
, Proposition 4.2.4]
. Note that by definition
p
is concentrated on
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O})
. Moreover,
V\cong\mathcal{V}_{K}:=\mathcal{V}\times_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}K
and
\mathcal{V}
enjoys the maximum modulus principle with respect to
p
(mmp w.r.t.
p
):

For every regular function
f
on
\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{K}}
there is some
\gamma_{f}\in\Gamma
such that
p\vdash\val(f(x))=\gamma_{f}
and for any
h\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O})
,
\val(f(h))\geq\gamma_{f}
.
As a result if
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
is an open affine subscheme with
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{O})\neq\emptyset
then
p
is concentrated on
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{O})
. For the definition of the maximum modulus principle for general quasi-compact separated schemes over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
see
[
Hal21
, Definition 4.3.1]
.

If either

is sufficiently saturated or
\mathcal{V}
is of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
then
\mathcal{V}
has an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-point. Thus
\Phi(V_{\mathbb{K}},p)
is faithfully flat and dominant over
\mathcal{O}

[
Hal21
, Proposition 3.1.4]
. On the other hand, by
[

, Proposition 4.2.15]

\Phi(V,p)\times_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\mathcal{O}=\Phi(V_{\mathbb{K}},p)
and thus by faithfully flat descent

is faithfully flat over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
as well
[
GW10
, Corollary 14.12]
(though might not have an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-point) and dominant over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
by
[
DG67
, Corollaire IV.2.6.4]
.

. However, by passing to an open subvariety of
U
we can always assume that this is the case (taking
W=U
below):

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
F
be a gracious valued field. Let
(V,p),(U,q)\in(\text{SPVar}^{\text{aff}}/{F})
,
W\subseteq U
an open affine subvariety and
\varphi:W\to V
an open immersion with
\varphi_{*}q=p
. Then there exist
f\in F[U]
and
g\in F[V]
, with
q\vdash\val(f(x))=0
and
p\vdash\val(g(x))=0
, such that

(1)

D_{U}(f)\subseteq W
and
\varphi
restricts to an isomorphism
\varphi^{\prime}
between
D_{U}(f)
and
D_{V}(g)

(2)

For
\mathcal{V}=\Phi(V,p)
and
\mathcal{U}=\Phi(U,q)
,
\Phi(D_{U}(f),q)=D_{\mathcal{U}}(f)
and
\Phi(D_{V}(g),p)=D_{\mathcal{V}}(g)

(3)

\Phi(\varphi^{\prime}):D_{\mathcal{U}}(f)\to D_{\mathcal{V}}(g)
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

By a standard argument, there are
f\in F[U]
and
g\in F[V]
such that
D_{U}(f)\subseteq W
,
D_{V}(g)\subseteq f(W)
and that
\varphi
restricts to an isomorphism between
D_{U}(f)
and
D_{V}(g)
. This gives (1). As
p
and
q
are strictly based on
F
we may further assume that
q\vdash\val(f(x))=0
and
p\vdash\val(g(x))=0
(since
\val(f(c))\in\Gamma_{F}
for
c\models q|F
, and likewise for
g
and
p
). In particular,
(F[U]_{f})^{q}=(F[U]^{q})_{f}
and likewise for
g
. Thus if we set
\mathcal{U}=\Phi(U,q)
and
\mathcal{V}=\Phi(V,p)
we get (2).

Item (3) follows since
\Phi
obviously preserves isomorphisms.
∎

3.
Some more on the functor

For the entirety of this section we let
F
be a gracious valued field and
K
an algebraically closed valued field containing
F
. The aim of this section is to prove some more properties of
\Phi
.

3.1.
Integrality of the Fibers

In this subsection we prove that given
(V,p)\in(\text{SPVar}^{\text{aff}}/{F})
, the fibers of
\Phi(V,p)\to\Spec\mathcal{O}_{F}
are geometrically integral. We then deduce, assuming
\Phi(V,p)
is of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, that
\Phi(V,p)
is generically smooth over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
.

We denote by
(K,\val)^{sh}
the Shelah expansion of
(K,\val)
, see
[
Sim15
, Section 3.3]
and Appendix
A
. We will require the following result whose proof we postpone to the appendix, see Proposition
A.4
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
T
be a complete NIP theory in a first order language
\mathcal{L}
and
M\models T
. Let
\mathbb{U}\succ M
be a large saturated model of
T
and
\mathbb{U}^{*}
an expansion to a saturated model of
\mathrm{Th}(M^{sh})
(so
\mathbb{U}^{*}\restriction\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{U}
).

For every global type
p
, if
p
is generically stable over
M
then there exists a unique extension
p\subseteq q\in S^{\mathcal{L}^{sh}}(\mathbb{U}^{*})
which is generically stable over
M
.

The following observation is simple, yet useful:

Fact 3.2
.

Let
R\subseteq S
be (possibly trivial) valuation rings with fraction field
F
. Then for every
S
-algebras
A
and
B

A\otimes_{R}B=A\otimes_{S}B.

Proof.

Let
a\in A
,
b\in B
and let
r\in S\setminus R
, so
r^{-1}\in R
.

(ra)\otimes b=(ra)\otimes(r^{-1}rb)=(rr^{-1})a\otimes(rb)=a\otimes(rb),

as needed.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(V,p)\in(\text{SPVar}^{\text{aff}}/{F})
and let
\mathcal{V}=\Phi(V,p)
. All the fibers of
\mathcal{V}\to\Spec\mathcal{O}_{F}
are geometrically integral.

Proof.

After base change, we may assume that
F=K
.

Let
v
be the valuation on
K
,
\mathcal{O}_{v}:=\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
\Gamma_{v}:=\Gamma_{K}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}_{w}\in\Spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
be a prime ideal corresponding to a coarsening
w
of
v
with value group
\Gamma_{w}:=\Gamma_{v}/\Delta_{w}
and residue field
\textbf{k}_{w}
; so
\mathcal{O}_{w}:=(\mathcal{O}_{v})_{\mathfrak{p}_{w}}
is the corresponding valuation ring.

If
\mathfrak{p}_{w}
corresponds to the generic point, the generic fiber is just
\mathcal{V}\times_{\Spec\mathcal{O}_{v}}\Spec K=V
so integral. Otherwise,
w
is a non-trivial coarsening of
v
.

Let
(K,\val)^{sh}
be the Shelah expansion of
(K,\val)
. Let
\tilde{p}
be the (unique) generically stable extension of
p
living in the saturated expansion
(\mathbb{K},\val)^{*}
, as given by Proposition
3.1
. Possibly after passing to
T^{eq}
, which we can do by Fact
A.3
, the valuation
w
is now definable in
(K,v)^{sh}
and thus defines a coarsening of
v
in
\mathbb{K}
as well, which we will also denote by
w
. Let
p_{w}
be the restriction of
\tilde{p}
to
(\mathbb{K},w)
;
p
is just the restriction of
\tilde{p}
to
(\mathbb{K},v)
. Note that
p_{w}
is still
K
-definable and Zariski dense in
V
. It is generically stable by Fact
A.3
.
Let
K[V]^{p_{w}}=\{f\in K[V]:w(f(c))\geq 0,\text{ for }c\models p_{w}|K\}
.

Claim
.

Viewing
K[V]^{p_{v}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{v}}\mathcal{O}_{w}
as a subset of
K[V]^{p_{w}}
,

K[V]^{p_{w}}=K[V]^{p_{v}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{v}}\mathcal{O}_{w}.

Proof.

Let
c\models\tilde{p}|K
, then obviously
c\models p_{v}|K
and
c\models p_{w}|K
.

Let
f\in K[V]
be a regular function satisfying
w(f(c))\geq 0
. As
p_{w}
is generically stable,
w(f(c))\in\Gamma_{w}
. Since
w
is a coarsening of
v
, there are two possibilities for
v(f(c))
, either
v(f(c))\geq 0
or
0>v(f(c))\in\Delta_{w}
. If
v(f(c))\geq 0
there is nothing show. If
0>v(f(c))\in\Delta_{w}
then let
a\in K
be such that
v(f(c))=v(a)
. Such an element exists seen
p_{v}
is orthogonal to
\Gamma_{v}
. Writing
f(c)=a\cdot\frac{f(c)}{a}
concludes the proof since
v\left(\frac{f(c)}{a}\right)=0
and
v(a)\in\Delta_{w}
and hence
w(a)=0
.
∎

The fiber over
\mathfrak{p}_{w}
is by definition
\mathcal{V}\times_{\Spec\mathcal{O}_{v}}\Spec\textbf{k}_{w}
, or in other words

\Spec\left(K[V]^{p}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{v}}\textbf{k}_{w}\right).

As
\textbf{k}_{w}=\mathcal{O}_{w}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{w}}\textbf{k}_{w}
, by Fact
3.2
this is equal to

\Spec\left(K[V]^{p_{v}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{v}}\mathcal{O}_{w}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{v}}\textbf{k}_{w}\right).

By the claim above and Fact
3.2
again we get that it is equal to

\Spec\left(K[V]^{p_{w}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{w}}\textbf{k}_{w}\right).

It is thus sufficient to prove the following claim:

Claim
.

For any
(V,p)\in(\text{SPVar}^{\text{aff}}/{K})
,
\Phi(V,p)
has an integral special fiber.

Proof.

We show that the special fiber
K[V]^{p}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\textbf{k}_{K}\cong K[V]^{p}/(\mathfrak{m}_{K}K[V]^{p})
is an integral domain. Let
f,g\in K[V]^{p}
and assume that
fg\in\mathfrak{m}_{K}K[V]^{p}
. In particular
\val(f(c)g(c))>0
for
c\models p|K
and thus without loss of generality
\val(f(c))>0
. Since
p
is generically stable, there is some
a\in K
such that
\val(f(c))=\val(a)>0
so
\val(f(c)/a)=0
and hence
f(c)/a\in K[V]^{p}
. Consequently,
f=a\cdot f/a\in\mathfrak{m}_{K}K[V]^{p}
, as needed.
∎

Apply the last claim to
(K,w)
and
p_{w}
and conclude.
∎

We now turn to showing smoothness.

By
[
Nag66
, Theorem 3]
, a flat scheme of finite type over a valuation ring is finitely presented. Actually the proof gives a bit more (see
[
Nag66
, page 159]
):

Fact 3.4
.

Let
R
be a valuation ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and let
R[X]
be the polynomial ring over
R
with variables
X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Then for every
I\trianglelefteq R[X]
such that
R[X]/I
is flat over
R
, if
f_{1}+\mathfrak{m}[X],\dots,f_{r}+\mathfrak{m}[X]
generate
(I+\mathfrak{m}[X])/\mathfrak{m}[X]
then
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
generate
I
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be an integral affine scheme of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-point. If the special fiber
\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}
is reduced then the smooth locus of
\mathcal{V}
over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is open non-empty and has an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-point.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{V}=\Spec\mathcal{O}_{K}[X]/I
. Since
\mathcal{V}
has an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-point it is faithfully flat over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
by
[
Hal21
, Proposition 3.1.4]
, finitely presented over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
by
[
Nag66
, Theorem 3]
and
\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}(\textbf{k}_{K})
is non empty.

The coordinate ring of the special fiber is isomorphic to

(\mathcal{O}_{K}[X]/I)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\textbf{k}_{K}\cong\mathcal{O}_{K}[X]/(I+\mathfrak{m}_{K}[X])\cong\left(\nicefrac{{\mathcal{O}_{K}[X]}}{{\mathfrak{m}_{K}[X]}}\right)/\left(\nicefrac{{I+\mathfrak{m}_{K}[X]}}{{\mathfrak{m}_{K}[x]}}\right);

by Fact
3.4
there are generators
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
of
I
such that
\overline{f_{1}},\dots,\overline{f_{r}}
, with
\overline{f_{i}}=f_{i}+\mathfrak{m}_{K}[X]
, are non-zero and generate
\nicefrac{{I+\mathfrak{m}_{K}[X]}}{{\mathfrak{m}_{K}[x]}}
.

As
\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}
is reduced (and of finite type over
\textbf{k}_{K}
) and
\textbf{k}_{K}
is perfect, by
[
GW10
, Theorem 6.19]
the singular locus of
\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}
is a proper closed subvariety. Thus there is some
a\in\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}(\textbf{k}_{K})
and a minor
\det\overline{M}(a)
of
\left(\frac{\partial\overline{f_{i}}}{\partial x_{j}}(a)\right)_{1\leq i\leq r,\,1\leq i\leq n}
of order
n-d
with
d=\dim\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}
and
\det\overline{M}(a)\neq 0
.

By
[
Hal21
, Theorem 3.2.4]
, there is some
b\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
with
r(b)=a
, where
r:\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\to\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}(\textbf{k}_{K})
is the reduction map. Viewing
b
as an element of
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{n}
we exactly get that
\res(b)=a
. Hence
\val(\det M(b))=0
where
\det M(b)
is the corresponding minor of
\left(\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(b)\right)_{1\leq i\leq r,\,1\leq i\leq n}
. This exactly means that the prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{b}=\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[X]/I:\val(f(b))>0\}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}[X]/I
is a smooth point of
\mathcal{V}
over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of relative dimension
d
.

Thus the smooth locus is open non-empty and contains the
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-point
b
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(V,p)\in(\text{SPVar}^{\text{aff}}/{F})
. If
\Phi_{F}(V,p)
is of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
then there exists an open affine subvariety
U\subseteq V
such that
\Phi_{F}(U,p)
is an open affine subscheme of
\Phi_{F}(V,p)
and
\Phi_{F}(U,p)
is smooth over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
.

Proof.

The
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-scheme
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}=\Phi_{K}(V_{K},p)
satisfies the assumptions of Proposition
3.5
(by Proposition
3.3
). As
\mathcal{V}
is affine, the smooth locus of
\mathcal{V}
over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
is a finite union of principal open affine subschemes of
\mathcal{V}
(given by Jacobian determinants). Base changing this open subscheme to
\Spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
we get the smooth locus of
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
.

Hence there exists some
f\in F[V]^{p}
such that
D_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}}(f)
is smooth over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
D_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}}(f)(\mathcal{O}_{K})\neq\emptyset
. As smoothness descends under faithfully flat descent
[
DG67
, IV.17.7.3]
,
\mathcal{U}:=D_{\mathcal{V}}(f)
is smooth over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
.

Since
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{O})
is non empty,
p
is concentrated on
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{O})
, i.e.
\val(f(c))=0
for
c\models p|F
. Consequently,
(F[V]^{p})_{f}=(F[V]_{f})^{p}
and thus, setting
U=\Spec F[V]_{f}
,
\Phi_{F}(U,p)=\mathcal{U}
.
∎

3.2.
Strongly Stably dominated types

We recall the definition of a strongly stably dominated type (at least an equivalent definition, see
[
HL16
, Proposition 8.1.2]
).

Definition 3.7
.

Let
L
be a valued field and
p
an
L
-definable type on a variety
V
over
L
. We say that
p
is
strongly stably dominated
if there exists some
L
-definable map
g
into a variety over the residue field for which
\dim(p)=\dim(g_{*}p)
.

Every strongly stably dominated type is generically stable; not every generically stable type is strongly stably dominated, but every generically stable type concentrated on an algebraic curve is strongly stably dominated
[
HL16
, Lemma 8.1.4 and Remark 8.1.5]
.

Fact 3.8
.

Let
L
be a valued field,
V
an affine variety over
L
, with
n=\dim(V)
, and
p
a Zariski dense
L
-definable type concentrated on
V
. If
p
is strongly stably dominated then there exist open affine subvarieties
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
,
U\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{n}_{L}
and a finite morphism
f:V^{\prime}\to U
with
f_{*}p=p_{\mathcal{O}}^{\otimes n}
.

Proof.

By
[
HL16
, Lemma 8.1.2]
, we can find such
f:V_{0}\to\mathbb{A}^{n}_{L}
,
V_{0}\subseteq V
open affine subvariety, with
f
quasi-finite. By
[
Sta23
, Tag 02NW]
, we may further reduce (the target and domain of)
f
to achieve finiteness.
∎

The purpose of this section is to present some results on strongly stably dominated types. We start with some known results.

Fact 3.9
.

[
HHS21
, Lemmas 2.13, 2.14]

(1)

The generic types of a
definable
generically stable group are strongly stably dominated.

(2)

If the unique generically stable type of a type-definable connected generically stable subgroup
H
of an algebraic group
G
is strongly stably dominated then
H
is definable.

For the next result we will need the following, which is hidden inside the proof of
[
Hal21
, Theorem 3.2.4]
.

Fact 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be an affine integral scheme flat and dominant over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. If
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O})=\emptyset
then
\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}=\emptyset
. If
\mathcal{V}
is of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
then
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O}_{K})=\emptyset
implies that
\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}(\textbf{k}_{K})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{V}=\Spec\mathcal{O}_{K}[X]/I
be the given affine scheme. Assume that
\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}\neq\emptyset
, after base-changing we also assume that
\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}(\textbf{k}_{K})\neq\emptyset
. By
[
Hal21
, Lemma 3.2.2(2)]
, there exists
b\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O})
. If
\mathcal{V}
is of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
(so finitely presented over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
) then
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\neq\emptyset
as well.
∎

We observe the following:

Fact 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a scheme which is flat over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
and assume that
\mathcal{V}_{F}
is geometrically integral, then
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{V}\times_{\Spec\mathcal{O}_{F}}\Spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
are integral schemes.

Proof.

We may assume that
\mathcal{V}=\Spec A
is affine.
Since
A
is flat over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
,

A\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}\mathcal{O}_{K}\hookrightarrow A\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}K.

We also have
A\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}K\cong A\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}(F\otimes_{F}K)\cong(A\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}F)\otimes_{F}K
, and since the latter is an integral domain (by assumption),
A\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}\mathcal{O}_{K}
is an integral domain.

Likewise, since
A
is flat over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
,

A\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}\mathcal{O}_{F}\hookrightarrow A\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}\mathcal{O}_{K},

so
A
is an integral domain as well.
∎

In the following, it is convenient to view
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O})
as a pro-definable subset of an inverse limit of (powers of)
\mathcal{O}
. Thus
b
might in general be an infinite tuple.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be an affine integral scheme flat over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, with
\mathcal{V}_{K}
geometrically integral, and assume that it has the mmp w.r.t.
p
, a generically stable type over
K
concentrated on
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O})
. If
\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}
is integral then for any
b\models p|K
,
\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}=\Spec(\textbf{k}_{K}[\res(b)])
. In particular,
r_{*}p
is the unique generic type of
\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{V}=\Spec\mathcal{O}_{K}[X]/I
. Since
p
is concentrated on
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O})
,
\mathcal{V}
has an
\mathcal{O}
-point so flat over
\mathcal{O}
. Thus, as
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{O}}
is integral by Fact
3.11
,
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{O}}
is dominant and faithfully flat over
\mathcal{O}

[
Hal21
, Proposition 3.1.4 and its proof]
; by faithfully flat descent so is
\mathcal{V}
over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

Let
b\models p|K
. By
[
Hal21
, Lemma 3.2.2(1)]
,
\mathcal{O}_{K}[X]/I\cong\mathcal{O}_{K}[b]
, given by
X\mapsto b
. Now let
\mathcal{O}_{K}[b]\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\textbf{k}_{K}\to\textbf{k}_{K}[\res(b)]
be the surjection given by the residue map; we claim it is injective. Note that every element of
\mathcal{O}_{K}[b]\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\textbf{k}_{K}
is of the form
f(b)\otimes 1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[X]/I
and assume that
\overline{f}(\res(b))=0
, i.e.
\val(f(b))>0
. Thus
\val(f(c))>0
for any
c\models p|L
and any large enough model
L
containing
K
. By the maximum modulus principle,
D_{\mathcal{V}}(f)(\mathcal{O})=\emptyset
, where
D_{\mathcal{V}}(f)
is the principal open subscheme of
\mathcal{V}
given by
f
.

Note that
D_{\mathcal{V}}(f)
is also integral, flat and dominant over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
as an open subscheme of such; so by Fact
3.10
we get that
D_{\mathcal{V}}(f)_{\textbf{k}}(\textbf{k})=\emptyset
which gives
f=0
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}[b]\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\textbf{k}_{K}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}[b]\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\textbf{k}
.
∎

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
(V,p)\in(\text{SPVar}^{\text{aff}}/{K})
. The type
p
is strongly stably dominated if and only if
\dim V=\dim\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}
, for
\mathcal{V}=\Phi(V,p)
. Furthermore, in this situation
p
is stably dominated via
r:\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O})\to\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\dim V=\dim\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}
. Since
\mathcal{V}\to\Spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
has integral fibers (Proposition
3.3
), by Lemma
3.12

r_{*}p
is the generic type of
\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}
, so
\dim(p)=\dim V=\dim\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}=\dim(r_{*}p)
, so
p
is strongly stably dominated. By
[
Hal21
, Proposition 4.1.4]
,
p
is stably dominated via
r
.

For the other direction, by Fact
3.8
there exist open affine subvarieties
U\subseteq V
,
W\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}
and a finite morphism
f:U\to W
satisfying
f_{*}p=p_{\mathcal{O}}^{\otimes n}
for
n=\dim V
. Writing
f=(f_{i})
, then
f_{i}\in K[U]^{p}
for all
i
. Letting
\overline{f_{i}}=\res\circ f_{i}
, by Lemma
3.12
we have that for
c\models p|K

\overline{f_{i}}(c)=\res(f_{i}(c))\in\textbf{k}_{K}[\res(c)]\cong K[U]^{p}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\textbf{k}_{K}.

Since
f(c)\models p_{\mathcal{O}}^{\otimes n}
this implies that
\dim\textbf{k}_{K}[\res(c)]=n
, i.e.
\dim\mathcal{V}_{\textbf{k}_{K}}=n
.
∎

The following can be seen as a “strengthening” of
[
HRK19
, Lemma 5.1]
.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
A
be a commutative algebraic group over
K
and
p
be a strongly stably dominated type concentrated on
A
, which is definable over
K
. If
p^{n}
is Zariski dense in
A
then it is the generic of a coset of a generically stable connected definable subgroup of
A
, where
n=\dim A
.

Proof.

We may assume that
n>0
.
By
[
HRK19
, Lemma 5.1]
, there exists a type-definable generically stable connected subgroup
H
of
A
with unique generic
p^{\mp 2n}
, where

p^{\mp 2n}=f_{*}p^{\otimes 2n}

for

3.3.
A finiteness condition

The purpose of this section is to provide a condition that guarantees that
\Phi(V,p)
is of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, for
(V,p)\in(\text{SPVar}^{\text{aff}}/{F})
. Then we show that generic types of Abelian schemes over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
satisfy this condition.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
(V,p)\in(\text{SPVar}^{\text{aff}}/{F})
.
If there exists a finite morphsim
f:V\to W
, with
W\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{n}_{F}
an open subvariety, satisfying that

and for

, the valuation on
(F(f(c)),\val)
extends uniquely to
F(c)
, then there exists an open subvariety
U\subseteq V
such that
\Phi(U,p)
is of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
.

Proof.

Let
c\models p|F
and let
f:V\to W
be a finite morphism of varieties over
F
satisfying the assumptions. By restricting
W
(and
V
) there is no harm in assuming that
W=D_{\mathbb{A}^{n}_{F}}(h)
is a principal open subvariety of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{F}
; as
p_{\mathcal{O}}^{\otimes n}
is generically stable we may further assume that
\val(h(d))=0
for
d\models p_{\mathcal{O}}^{\otimes n}|F
.

Assuming
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
, we identify
F[D_{\mathbb{A}^{n}_{F}}(h)]
with
F[f_{1},\dots,f_{n}]_{h}\subseteq F[V]
.

Identifying
F[V]
with
F[c]
we have that
f_{i}(c)\in F[c]
for
1\leq i\leq n
, and as
f(c)\models p_{\mathcal{O}}^{\otimes n}|F
they constitute a valuation transcendence basis of

over
F
, i.e. for any (multi-)index set
I\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and
g\in F[f(c)]
, if
g=\sum_{\nu\in I}a_{\nu}f(c)^{\nu}
then

\val(g)=\min\{\val(a_{\nu})\}.

Since
(F,\val)
is a defectless valued field, by the generalized stability theorem
[
Kuh10
, Theorem 1.1]
,
(F(f(c)),\val)
is also defectless. So
F(c)/F(f(c))
is finite defectless. Since, by the assumptions, the valuation on
F(f(c))
extends uniquely to
F(c)
, by
[
Kuh24
, Lemma 11.15]
,
(F(c)/F(f(c)),\val)
has a separating basis (as a valued vector space). I.e., since
p
is strictly based on
F
,
\Gamma_{F(c)}=\Gamma_{F}
, there are basis elements
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}\in F(V)
, with
\val(g_{i}(c))=0
, such that for any
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\in F(f(c))
,

\val(\sum_{i}a_{i}g_{i}(c))=\min_{i}\{\val(a_{i})\}.

As
f:V\to W
is finite,
\mathrm{Frac}(F[f_{1},\dots,f_{n}]_{h})\otimes_{F}F[V]=F(V)
, so there exists
r_{0}\in F[f_{1},\dots,f_{n}]_{h}
for which
g_{i}\in F[V]_{r_{0}}
for all
1\leq i\leq d
; note that
F[V]_{r_{0}}
is still finite over
F[f_{1},\dots,f_{n}]_{hr_{0}}
. We may thus find
r_{1}\in F[f_{1},\dots,f_{n}]_{hr_{0}}
for which
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}
generate
F[V]_{r_{0}r_{1}}
as a module over
F[f_{1},\dots,f_{n}]_{hr_{0}r_{1}}
. Let
r=r_{0}r_{1}
and let

. As before, there is no harm in assuming that
\val(r(c))=0
.

We now show that
1/h,1/r,\,g_{i},\,f_{j}
, for
1\leq i\leq d,\,1\leq j\leq n
generate

as an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-algebra.

Let

; there exist
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\in F[f_{1},\dots,f_{n}]_{hr}
such that

. Write each

as

for
b_{i}\in F[f_{1},\dots,f_{n}]
. Each

can be written as
\sum_{\nu\in I}t_{i,\nu}f^{\nu}
, with

.

As
\val(s(c))\geq 0
, by the choice of the

we get that
\val(b_{i}(c))=\val(b_{i}(c)/(hr)(c)^{n_{i}})\geq 0
for

. So
\val(t_{i,\nu})\geq 0
for all the

∎

4.
Getting a Group Scheme

Let
F
be a gracious valued field and
K\supseteq F
an algebraically closed valued field.

In
[
HRK19
, Theorem 6.11]
, Hrushovksi and Rideau-Kikuchi proved that given an
affine
algebraic group
G
over
K
and a generically stable type
p
concentrating on
G
satisfying
p^{2}=p
there exists an affine group scheme
\mathcal{G}
over
\mathcal{O}
and a pro-definable isomorphism
G\cong\mathcal{G}_{K}
under which
\mathrm{Stab}(p)
is mapped to
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O})
.

We still do not have a full analog for general algebraic groups over
K
. The purpose of this section is to answer this to the affirmative when the functor
\Phi
(locally) gives a scheme of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. We then apply this to give a model theoretic criterion for good reduction of (some) Abelian varieties over
K
.

We review some definitions and results from
[
BLR90
]
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be any scheme. Recall the definition of a schematically dense open subscheme
[
Sta23
, Tags 01RB and 01RE]
; for reduced schemes this definition coincides with Zariski denseness
[
Sta23
, Tag 056D]
.

Definition 4.1
.

An open subscheme
\mathcal{U}
of a scheme
\mathcal{X}
over
\mathcal{S}
is called
\mathcal{S}
-dense if
\mathcal{U}\times_{\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is schematically dense in
\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
for all morphisms
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{S}
.

Fact 4.2
.

[

, IV.11.10.10]

If
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{S}
is flat and of finite presentation then an open subscheme
\mathcal{U}
of

is

-dense if and only if for all

is the residue class field of

\mathcal{S}

universally schematically dense relative to

and in SGA3 as

\mathcal{X}
relative to
\mathcal{S}
. Furthermore, in
[
BLR90

-schemes and in return they only require that

be Zariski dense in

. The reason for this is the following.

\mathcal{X}
is smooth and of finite presentation over

and let

-dense if and only if

As

if flat and of finite presentation, if

for all

. In particular, for any such

,

and hence reduced so being Zariski dense is equivalent to being schematically dense by

Appendix A
Generically stable types and the Shelah expansion

Let
T
be a complete NIP theory in a first order language
\mathcal{L}
and let
\mathbb{U}
be a large saturated model.

Definition A.1
.

A global type
p
is said to be
generically stable
if it is definable and finitely satisfiable in some small model
M
. We then say that
p
is
generically stable over
M
.

Remark A.2
.

(1)

For every set
B
for which a generically stable type
p
is
B
-invariant,
p
is the unique
B
-invariant extension of
p|B
.

(2)

A global
A
-invariant type is generically stable if and only if every Morley sequence of
p
is totally indiscernible if and only if there exists a Morley sequence of
p
which is totally indiscernible.

Fact A.3
.

(1)

Let
p
be a generically stable type. Then
p
is still generically stable in any reduct.

(2)

There is a unique expansion of any type
p
to
T^{eq}
. If
p
is generically stable then this expansion is still generically stable.

Proof.

(1)
Assume that
p
is definable and finitely satisfiable in some small model
M
. Let
\tilde{p}
be the reduct of the type. Since
\tilde{p}
is still finitely satisfiable in
M
it is
M
-invariant. Now, we use the fact that every Morley sequence of
p
is totally indiscernible to show that there exists such a Morley sequence in
\tilde{p}
.

(2)
Straightforward.
∎

Let
M\models T
be a small model and
M\prec N
an
|M|^{+}
-saturated model. The Shelah expansion of
M
, denoted by
M^{sh}
, is a structure with universe
M
and language
\mathcal{L}^{sh}=\mathcal{L}\cup\{R_{\varphi(x,c)}:\varphi(x,y)\in\mathcal{L},c\in N\}
, where for every
a\in M
,
M^{sh}\models R_{\varphi(x,c)}(a)\iff N\models\varphi(a,c)
. The definable sets of
M^{sh}
do not depend on the
|M|^{+}
-saturated model from which we take the parameters for the externally definable sets. Since
T
is NIP,
\mathrm{Th}(M^{sh})
eliminates quantifiers in
\mathcal{L}^{sh}

[
She09
]
.

By resplendency of saturated models, we may expand
\mathbb{U}
to a saturated model of
\mathrm{Th}(M^{sh})
in the language
\mathcal{L}^{sh}
. We denote this expansion by
\mathbb{U}^{*}
.

The following is probably well known but we could not find a proof of it anywhere, a version for measures appears in
[
BK23
, Theorem 6.2]
.

Proposition A.4
.

Let
p
be a global type. If
p
is definable and finitely satisfiable in
A\subseteq M
. Then there exists a unique extension
p\subseteq q\in S^{\mathcal{L}^{sh}}(\mathbb{U}^{*})
which is definable and finitely satisfiable in
A
.

As a result, if
p
is generically stable over
M
then so is
q
.

Proof.

Let
M^{sh}\prec L^{*}
be an
|M|^{+}
-saturated extension and
(L^{*},M^{sh})\prec(\mathbb{V}^{*},\mathbb{U}^{**})
a saturated extension of cardinality
|\mathbb{U}|
. By uniqueness of saturated models we may assume that
\mathbb{U}^{**}=\mathbb{U}^{*}
. Let
L,\mathbb{U},\mathbb{V}
be their reducts to
\mathcal{L}
, respectively.

As
p
is definable and finitely satisfiable in
A
, there exists an extension
\tilde{p}\in S(\mathbb{V})
which is still definable and finitely satisfiable in
A
(and with the same definition). Seen as a partial type over
\mathbb{V}^{*}
,
\tilde{p}
is still finitely satisfiable in
A
. Let
\tilde{p}^{sh}
be a completion of
\tilde{p}
in
S^{\mathcal{L}^{sh}}(\mathbb{V}^{*})
which is still finitely satisfiable in
A
. We will show that
\tilde{p}^{sh}|\mathbb{U}^{*}
is definable over
A
.

For each
c\in N
choose some
\tilde{c}\in L
for which
\tp(c/M)=\tp(\tilde{c}/M)
. In particular, for each
\mathcal{L}
-formula
\psi(x,y,z)
and
c\in N
, we have that

\psi(M,c)=\psi(M,\tilde{c}).

We first show the following:

Claim
.

R_{\varphi(x,y,c)}(x,b)\in\tilde{p}^{sh}|\mathbb{U}^{*}\iff\varphi(x,b,\tilde{c})\in\tilde{p}.

Proof.

Assume that
(\star)
is not true. Thus there exists
b\in\mathbb{U}^{*}
with

\neg R_{\varphi(x,y,c)}(x,b)\wedge\varphi(x,b,\tilde{c})\in\tilde{p}^{sh}.

Since
\tilde{p}^{sh}
is finitely satisfiable in
A
there exists
m\in A\subseteq M^{sh}
with

\mathbb{V}^{*}\models\neg R_{\varphi(x,y,c)}(m,b)\wedge\varphi(m,b,\tilde{c}),

but since we also have

(\mathbb{V}^{*},\mathbb{U}^{*})\models\forall(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathbb{U}^{*}\left(R_{\varphi(x,y,c)}(a_{1},a_{2})\leftrightarrow\varphi(a_{1},a_{2},\tilde{c})\right),

we arrive to a contradiction.
∎

We now show that
\tilde{p}^{sh}|\mathbb{U}^{*}
is definable over
A
. Since
\tilde{p}
is definable over
A
, we have
\varphi(x,b,\tilde{c})\in\tilde{p}\iff\mathbb{V}\models\psi(b,\tilde{c})
for some
\mathcal{L}
-formula over
A
. By the choice of
\tilde{c}
, we have that
\psi(M,\tilde{c})=\psi(M,c)
, thus

(L^{*},M^{sh})\models\forall a\in M^{sh}\left(R_{\psi(x,c)}(a)\leftrightarrow\psi(a,\tilde{c})\right).

Since
(L^{*},M^{sh})\prec(\mathbb{V}^{*},\mathbb{U}^{*})
, for all
a\in\mathbb{U}^{*}

\mathbb{U}^{*}\models R_{\psi(x,c)}(a)\iff\mathbb{V}^{*}\models\psi(a,\tilde{c}).

Together with the claim this combines to

R_{\varphi(x,y,c)}(x,b)\in\tilde{p}^{sh}|\mathbb{U}^{*}\iff\mathbb{U}^{*}\models R_{\psi(x,c)}(b).

Uniqueness follows from
(\star)
.
∎

An explicit computation of the Hecke operator and the ghost conjecture
Nha Xuan Truong

Abstract




1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number, and
N
be a positive integer co-prime to
p
. For an integer
k\geq 3
, we use
S_{k}\big(\Gamma_{1}(pN)\big)
to denote the space of modular cuspforms of weight
k
, level
\Gamma_{1}(pN)
. A modular form
f
in
S_{k}\big(\Gamma_{1}(pN)\big)
can be written as

f(\tau)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(f)q^{n},\ q=e^{2\pi i\tau}.

For each prime
l
, one can define a
Hecke operator

T_{l}
on
S_{k}\big(\Gamma_{1}(pN)\big)
. When
l=p
, the Hecke operator is often denoted by
U_{p}
instead. For an
eigenform

f
, after normalization, one can deduce that

U_{p}f=a_{p}f.

The
p
-adic valuation of
a_{p}
is called the
slope of the eigenform
. The study of slopes plays an important role in understanding the geometry of the so-called eigencurve introduced by Coleman and Mazur using
p
-adic interpolation of overconvergent eigenforms
[
CM98
]
. The eigencurve has many application in
p
-adic number theory, for example Kisin’s proof of Fontaine-Mazur conjecture for
\GL_{2}

[
Kis09
]
.

The first numerical data of the slopes was due to Gouvêa and Mazur in
[
Go92
]
using computer calculations. Buzzard and his co-authors computed in the case with small primes
p
and small level
[
BC04
]
,
[
BK05
]
,
[
Ja04
]
. Liu, Wan and Xiao found the slopes for the
U_{p}
-operator over the boundary of the weight space to be certain unions of arithmetic progressions
[
LWX17
]
.
Recently, Bergdall and Pollack proposed the ghost conjecture which predicts the slopes over the entire the weight space. In
[
BP

⁢
19
a

]
[
BP

⁢
19
b

]
, the authors defined a formal power series, called the
ghost series
, and conjectured that the slope of
U_{p}
action is the same as the slopes of the Newton polygon of the ghost series.

We consider a variant of the ghost conjecture for overconvergent forms on definite quaternion algebras.The result can than be translate to modular forms using Jacquet-Langlands correspondence. The upshot is that, this circumvented the difficulties posed by the geometry of a modular curve, traded with the arithmetic complication of a quarternion algebra, which turns out to be more accessible by our method.

One simple and nice computation in this direction is due to Jacobs in
[
Ja04
]
. In his thesis, Jacobs studied the case when
p=3
with a particular level
[
Ja04
]
and computed the slopes of the
U_{3}
-operation. The method is later refined by Wan–Xiao–Zhang
[
WXZ17
]
.

In this paper, we investigate the Hecke operator
U_{5}
and show that the
n\times n
upper left minors of the matrix have non zero corank and, interestingly, follow that unimodal pattern in the ghost conjecture
[
BP

⁢
19
a

]
[
BP

⁢
19
b

]
. This seems to give the ghost series of Bergdall and Pollack some theoretic explanation. We expect that the slopes of
U_{5}
-action in this case can be computed using an appropriate variant of the
ghost series
, defined in (
8.1.1
). Assume this result, we achieve an upper bound for the slopes that is similar to the Gouêa’s
\frac{k-1}{p+1}
conjecture.

The result can be generalized in my ongoing project with Ruochuan Liu, Liang Xiao and Bin Zhao to prove the ghost conjecture under a certain mild technical hypothesis.

2.2.
Level structure

We define the level structure using adeles.
The ring of finite adeles
\mathbb{A}_{f}
is defined as

\mathbb{A}_{f}:=\{\left(x_{\ell}\right)_{\ell}\in\prod_{\ell\textrm{ prime}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}|\textrm{ all but finitely many }x_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\}.

We recall that
D
splits at all odd prime
\ell
, i.e
D\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}\cong\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
. We fix such an isomorphism for each
\ell
and define

D_{f}=D\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{A}_{f}

Then,

D_{f}^{\times}=(D\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{A}_{f})^{\times}:=\{\left(x_{\ell}\right)_{\ell}\in(D\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{2})^{\times}\prod_{\ell\textrm{ prime},\ell\not=2}\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})|\textrm{ all but finitely many }x_{\ell}\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\}.

For
\ell=2
, we use
D^{\times}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})
to denote the maximal compact subgroup of
(D\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{2})^{\times}
.

We consider the following open compact subgroup of
D^{\times}_{f}
:

\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(5)=D^{\times}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})\times\prod_{\ell\neq 2,5}\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\times\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{Z}_{5}^{\times}&\mathbb{Z}_{5}\\
5\mathbb{Z}_{5}&1+5\mathbb{Z}_{5}\end{pmatrix}

and the maximal open compact subgroup

\widehat{\GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})=D^{\times}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})\times\prod_{\ell\neq 2}\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}).

We have the following useful lemma, which explains the choice of
\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(5).

Lemma 2.3
.

The following natural map is bijective.

(2.3.1)

\textstyle{D^{\times}\times\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(5)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{D_{f}^{\times}}
\textstyle{(\delta,u)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\delta u.}

Proof.

We follows the same argument in
[
WXZ17
]
.
By Lemma 1.22 in
[
Ja04
]
,

D_{f}^{\times}=D^{\times}\cdot\widehat{\GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{5}).

Since
D^{\times}\cap\widehat{\GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})=\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}
, taking into account of the duplication, we have

D_{f}^{\times}=D^{\times}\times^{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}\widehat{\GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{5}).

So it suffices to check that the image of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}
in
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{5})
forms a full set of coset representatives of
\widehat{\GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})/\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(5)\cong\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{5})/\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{F}_{5}^{\times}&\mathbb{F}_{5}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

This further follows from the fact that
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{5})
and
\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{F}_{5}^{\times}&\mathbb{F}_{5}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\times\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}
both have
24\cdot 20
elements and
\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{F}_{5}^{\times}&\mathbb{F}_{5}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\cap\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}=\{1\}
.
∎

2.4.
Overconvergent automorphic forms

In this section, we define the space of overconvergent automorphic forms for a definite quaternion algebra and describe the Hecke actions explicitly.

Fix an integer
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, consider the right action of the Iwahori subgroup
\mathrm{Iw}_{5,1}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{Z}_{5}^{\times}&\mathbb{Z}_{5}\\
5\mathbb{Z}_{5}&1+5\mathbb{Z}_{5}\end{pmatrix}
of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})
on
\mathbb{Q}_{5}\big<z\big>
given by

(2.4.1)

\textrm{for }\gamma=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\big)\in\mathrm{Iw}_{5,1}\textrm{ and }f(z)\in\mathbb{Q}_{5}\big<z\big>,\quad(f||_{k}\gamma)(z):=(cz+d)^{k-2}f\big(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\big).

We define the space of
overconvergent automorphic forms of weight
k
and level
\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(5)
to be

S_{k}^{D,\dagger}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(5)):=\Big\{\varphi:D^{\times}\backslash D_{f}^{\times}\to\mathbb{Q}_{5}\big<z\big>\;\Big|\;\varphi(gu)=\varphi(g)||_{k}u_{5},\textrm{ for any }g\in D_{f}^{\times},u\in\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(5)\Big\},

where
u_{5}
is the
5
-component of
u
.

Using Lemma
2.3
, we have the isomorphism:

(2.4.2)

\textstyle{S_{k}^{D,\dagger}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(5))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Q}_{5}\big<z\big>}
\textstyle{\varphi\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\varphi(1).}

Remark 2.5
.

The isomorphism gives a simpler description of the space of overconvergent automophic form,
S_{k}^{D,\dagger}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(5))
, which allows us to compute explicitly the matrix of the Hecke action.

2.6.
U_{5}
-operator

The space
S_{k}^{D,\dagger}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(5))
carries actions of Hecke operators
U_{5}
, defined as follows.

We write

(2.6.1)

\mathrm{Iw}_{5,1}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}5&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)\mathrm{Iw}_{5,1}=\coprod_{i=0}^{4}\mathrm{Iw}_{5,1}v_{i},\quad\textrm{with }v_{i}=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}5&0\\
5i&1\end{smallmatrix}\big).

Then the action of the operator
U_{5}
on
S_{k}^{D,\dagger}\big(\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(5)\big)
is defined to be

(2.6.2)

U_{5}(\varphi)=\sum_{i=0}^{4}\varphi|_{k}v_{i},\quad\textrm{with }(\varphi|_{k}v_{i})(g):=\varphi(gv_{i}^{-1})||_{k}v_{i}.

Explicit computation of the infinite matrix of
U_{5}

In terms of the explicit description of the space of overconvergent automorphic forms (
2.4.2
), the
U_{5}
-operators can be described by the following commutative diagram.

\textstyle{S_{k}^{D,\dagger}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(5))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi\mapsto\varphi(1)}
\scriptstyle{\tiny\varphi\mapsto U_{5}(\varphi)}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Q}_{5}\big<z\big>\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tiny\mathfrak{U}_{5}}
\textstyle{S_{k}^{D,\dagger}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(5))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi\mapsto\varphi(1)}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Q}_{5}\big<z\big>.}

Lemma 3.1
.

The
\mathfrak{U}_{5}
is given by

\mathfrak{U}_{5}=||_{\kappa}\delta_{1}+||_{\kappa}\delta_{2}+||_{\kappa}\delta_{3}+||_{\kappa}\delta_{4}+||_{\kappa}\delta_{5}
, where

\delta_{1}=\mathbf{j}-\mathbf{2},\ \delta_{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{i}-3\mathbf{j}-3\mathbf{k}),\ \delta_{3}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{1}+3\mathbf{i}-3\mathbf{j}+\mathbf{k}),\ \delta_{4}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{1}-3\mathbf{i}-3\mathbf{j}-\mathbf{k}),\ \textrm{and }\delta_{5}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{i}-3\mathbf{j}+3\mathbf{k}).

Their images in
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{5})
are given by

\begin{pmatrix}\ -2+\nu_{5}&0\\
0&-(2+\nu_{5})\end{pmatrix},\quad\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1-3\nu_{5}&1+3\nu_{5}\\
-1+3\nu_{5}&1+3\nu_{5}\end{pmatrix},\quad\quad\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1-3\nu_{5}&3-\nu_{5}\\
-(3+\nu_{5})&1+3\nu_{5}\end{pmatrix},\quad

\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1-3\nu_{5}&-3+\nu_{5}\\
3+\nu_{5}&1+3\nu_{5}\end{pmatrix},\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1-3\nu_{5}&-(1+3\nu_{5})\\
1-3\nu_{5}&1+3\nu_{5}\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

We can compute
U_{5}
explicitly

U_{5}(\varphi)(1)=\sum_{j=1}^{5}\varphi(v_{j}^{-1})||_{\kappa}v_{j},\quad\textrm{for }v_{j}=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}5&0\\
5j&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)

By Lemma
2.3
, we can write each
v_{j}^{-1}
uniquely as
\delta_{j}^{-1}u_{j}
for
\delta_{j}\in D^{\times}
and
u_{j}\in\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(5)
.
Then

\varphi(v_{j}^{-1})||_{\kappa}v_{j}=\varphi(\delta_{j}^{-1}u_{j})||_{\kappa}v_{j}=\varphi(1)||_{\kappa}(u_{j,5}v_{j})=\varphi(1)||_{\kappa}\delta_{j,5},

where
u_{j,5}
and
\delta_{j,5}
denote the
5
-components of
u_{j}
and
\delta_{j}
, respectively.

We see that

\delta_{j,5}=u_{j,5}v_{j,5}\in D^{\times}\cap\mathrm{Iw}_{5,1}v_{j}\subseteq D^{\times}\cap\mathrm{Iw}_{5,1}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}5&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)\mathrm{Iw}_{5,1}\subseteq D^{\times}\cap U^{*}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}5&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\big),

where
U^{*}
is a subgroup of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})
such that the lower right entry belongs to

If we put
\delta_{j}=\delta^{\prime}_{j}(1+2\mathbf{j})
, then we have

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}_{j}

\displaystyle\in D^{\times}\cap U^{*}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}5&0\\

We know
D^{\times}\cap\widehat{\GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})=\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}
and all

are distinct, so by taking modulo 5, and comparing to the list of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}
, we have

\delta^{\prime}_{j}\in\big\{\mathbf{j},-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}+\mathbf{k}),-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}+\mathbf{k}),-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j}-\mathbf{k}),\frac{1}{2}(-\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{i}-\mathbf{j}+\mathbf{k})\big\}.

It is then clear that all
\delta_{j}

\mathbf{1}+2\mathbf{j}

4.
Computation

In this section we compute the matrix for the operator
U_{5}
explicitly and explore its upper left
n\times n
principal minor.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(P_{i,j})_{i,j=0,1,\cdots}
denote the matrix for the operator
U_{5}
on
\mathbb{Q}_{5}\langle z\rangle
, defined in (
2.6.2
), with respect to the power basis
1,z,z^{2},\dots
, then

•

When
i=j

P_{ij}=\left(\frac{1+3\nu_{5}}{2}\right)^{k-i-2}\left(\frac{1-3\nu_{5}}{2}\right)^{i}\left(4\sum_{n=0}^{i}(-1)^{i-n}\binom{j}{n}\binom{k-j-2}{i-n}+(\nu_{5}-1)^{k-2}(\nu_{5})^{i}\right);

•

When
i\neq j
but
i\equiv j
mod 4

P_{i,j}=4\left(\frac{1+3\nu_{5}}{2}\right)^{k-i-2}\left(\frac{1-3\nu_{5}}{2}\right)^{i}\ \ \sum_{n=0}^{\min\{i,j\}}(-1)^{i-n}\binom{j}{n}\binom{k-j-2}{i-n};

•

Otherwise
P_{ij}=0.

Proof.

The following argument is originally due to Jacobs
[
Ja04
]
.
For a matrix
P=(P_{i,j})
, we define a generating series as the formal power series
H_{P}(x,y):=P_{i,j}x^{i}y^{i}.

We have an explicit expression of the generating series for the operator
U_{p}
on
\mathbb{Q}_{5}\langle z\rangle
with respect to the power basis

H_{P}(x,y):=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}P_{i,j}x^{i}y^{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{5}\frac{(c_{i}x+d_{i})^{k-1}}{c_{i}x+d_{i}-a_{i}xy-b_{i}y},

where
\delta_{i}={\big(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{i}&b_{i}\\
c_{i}&d_{i}\end{smallmatrix}\big)}
are listed as in Lemma
3.1
.

We include a proof here for the convenience of the readers. It’s enough to compute the generating series of the operator
||_{k}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
acting on
\mathbb{Q}_{5}\langle z\rangle
with respect to the power basis. By definition,

H(x,y)=\sum_{i\geq 0}y^{i}\left(cx+d\right)^{k-2}\left(\frac{ax+b}{cx+d}\right)^{i}=\frac{\left(cx+d\right)^{k-2}}{1-y\cdot\frac{ax+b}{cx+d}}=\frac{\left(cx+d\right)^{k-1}}{cx+d-axy-by}.

H_{\delta_{1}}(x,y)=\frac{(-2-\nu_{5})^{k-1}}{-2-\nu_{5}+(2-\nu_{5})xy}=\frac{(-2-\nu_{5})^{k-2}}{1-\frac{2-\nu_{5}}{2+\nu_{5}}xy}=(-2-\nu_{5})^{k-2}\sum_{i\geq 0}(\frac{2-\nu_{5}}{2+\nu_{5}})^{i}x^{i}y^{i}.

Let
\alpha=\frac{1+3\nu_{5}}{2}
then

\delta

H_{\delta}(x,y)

\delta_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\ 1-\alpha&\alpha\\
\alpha-1&\alpha\end{pmatrix}

H_{\delta_{2}}(x,y)=\frac{((\alpha-1)x+\alpha)^{k-1}}{(\alpha-1)x+\alpha+(\alpha-1)xy-\alpha y}

\delta_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}\ 1-\alpha&-\alpha\nu_{5}\\
(\alpha-1)\nu_{5}&\alpha\end{pmatrix}

H_{\delta_{3}}(x,y)=\frac{((\alpha-1)\nu_{5}x+\alpha)^{k-1}}{(\alpha-1)\nu_{5}x+\alpha+(\alpha-1)xy+\alpha\nu_{5}y}

\delta_{4}=\begin{pmatrix}\ 1-\alpha&\alpha\nu_{5}\\
(1-\alpha)\nu_{5}&\alpha\end{pmatrix}

H_{\delta_{4}}(x,y)=\frac{((1-\alpha)\nu_{5}x+\alpha)^{k-1}}{(1-\alpha)\nu_{5}x+\alpha+(\alpha-1)xy-\alpha\nu_{5}y}

\delta_{5}=\begin{pmatrix}\ 1-\alpha&-\alpha\\
1-\alpha&\alpha\end{pmatrix}

H_{\delta_{5}}(x,y)=\frac{((1-\alpha)x+\alpha)^{k-1}}{(1-\alpha)x+\alpha+(\alpha-1)xy-\alpha y}

We observe that

H_{\delta_{3}}(x,y)=H_{\delta_{2}}(\nu_{5}x,-\nu_{5}y),H_{\delta_{4}}(x,y)=H_{\delta_{2}}(-\nu_{5}x,\nu_{5}y),\textrm{and }H_{\delta_{5}}(x,y)=H_{\delta_{2}}(-x,y).

Hence, in the formal power series of the sum
\sum_{i=2}^{5}H_{\delta_{i}}(x,y)
, the coefficient of
x^{i}y^{j}
is nonzero if and only if
i\equiv j
(mod 4), and in this case it is equal to 4 times the coefficient of
x^{i}y^{j}
in
H_{\delta_{2}}(x,y)
. We are left to compute the power series
\sum a_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j}
of
H_{\delta_{2}}(x,y)
. Rewriting
H_{\delta_{2}}(x,y)
in the formal power series form, we have

Ghost conjecture and its application

8.1.
Ghost conjecture

In this section, we will state a version of the ghost conjecture and one useful theorem that can be used in the proof of the ghost conjecture.

As in
[
LWX17
]
, we can define the characteristic power series of
U_{5}
on
S_{k,a}^{D,\dagger}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(5))
as

\textrm{Char}(U_{5},k,a)=\textrm{det}(I-XP(k,a)).

Our goal is to compute the 5-adic valuation of the
U_{5}
-eigenvalue, namely
the slope
, by finding the Newton polygon of the power series
\textrm{Char}(U_{5},k,a)
. The ghost conjecture states that there is an explicitly defined power series that has the same Newton polygon.

Set
w_{k}=\exp(5(k-2))-1
. We can find power series in
w
such that when evaluating at
w=w_{k}
, we get the entries of the matrix. Note that the power series coefficients are not in
\mathbb{Z}_{5}
but as in
[
LWX17
]
, there is a power series
\textrm{Char}(U_{5},w,t)\in\mathbb{Z}_{5}[[w]][[t]]
such that
{Char}(U_{5},w_{k},a)={Char}(U_{5},k,a)
for all weights
k
.

Following
[
BP

16

]
, we define the
ghost series
for each
a=0,\dots,3
to be the formal power series

(8.1.1)

G^{\left(a\right)}(w,t)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g^{\left(a\right)}_{n}(w)t^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{5}[w][[t]],

where each coefficient
g^{\left(a\right)}_{n}(w)
is a product

g^{\left(a\right)}_{n}(w)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\geq 2\\
l\equiv 2a-2\bmod 4\end{subarray}}(w-w_{\ell})^{m^{\left(a\right)}_{n}(\ell)}\in\mathbb{Z}_{5}[w]

with exponents
m^{\left(a\right)}_{n}(\ell)
given by the following recipe

m^{\left(a\right)}_{n}(\ell)=\begin{cases}\min\big\{n-d_{\ell,a}^{\mathrm{unr}},d_{\ell,a}^{\mathrm{Iw}}-d_{\ell,a}^{\mathrm{unr}}-n\big\}&\textrm{ if }d_{\ell,a}^{\mathrm{unr}}<n<d_{\ell,a}^{\mathrm{Iw}}-d_{\ell,a}^{\mathrm{unr}}\\
0&\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that the exponents
m^{\left(a\right)}_{n}(l)
has the same pattern as the corank of
P_{n}(k,a)
. We expect that the ghost conjecture is correct in this case.

Conjecture 8.2
.

(Ghost conjecture)

\textrm{Char}(U_{5},w,a)
and
G^{(a)}(w,t)
have the same Newton polygon for every
w\in\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}_{5}}
.

There are more technical difficulties to prove this conjecture. However, we believe the following theorem is one among many ingredients.

Theorem 8.1
.

The determinant of the upper left
n\times n
minor
P_{n}(k,a)
is divisible by
g^{\left(a\right)}_{i}(w_{k})
.

Proof.

As noted above, each coefficients of the matrix is a power series in
w
. Then we can find a power series
f_{a,n}(w)\in\mathbb{Z}_{5}[[w]]
such that
f_{a,n}(w_{\ell})=\textrm{det}P_{n}(\ell,a)
. By the conrank bound in
7.0.1
,
f_{n}(w,a)
is divisible by
(w-w_{\ell})^{m^{\left(a\right)}_{n}(\ell)}
, so is
g^{\left(a\right)}_{i}(w_{k})
.
∎

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j\leq i}\mathrm{Dig}\left(A_{n}\right)+\mathrm{Dig}\left(B_{n}\right)-\mathrm{Dig}\left(C_{n}\right)-\mathrm{Dig}\left(C_{n}+1\right)\leq 4i^{2}+2i

We have the following relations:

B_{n}=6C_{n}-A_{n}+3

.

For
j=1,2,...,d^{\mathrm{unr}}_{k_{0}}
, we write
A_{d_{k_{0}}^{\mathrm{unr}}-j},B_{d_{k_{0}}^{\mathrm{unr}}-j},C_{d_{k_{0}}^{\mathrm{unr}}-j}
in terms of
A=A_{d_{k_{0}}^{\mathrm{unr}}-1}
,and
C=C_{d_{k_{0}}^{\mathrm{unr}}-1}
.

Note that
A_{d_{k_{0}}^{\mathrm{unr}}-1}=k_{0\bullet}-6(d_{k_{0}}^{\mathrm{unr}}-1)-a-3=k_{0\bullet}-a-1-\lfloor\frac{k_{0\bullet}-a-1}{6}\rfloor-1\in\{0,1,2,3\}.

A_{d_{k_{0}}^{\mathrm{unr}}-j}=A+6(j-1)\textrm{ and }C_{d_{k_{0}}^{\mathrm{unr}}-j}=C+2(j-1)

\textrm{ Hence, }B_{d_{k_{0}}^{\mathrm{unr}}-j}=6(C+j-1)+3-A

Then using the inequality
\textrm{Dig}(A+B)\leq\textrm{Dig}(A)+\textrm{Dig}(B)
and
\textrm{Dig}(6k)\leq 2\textrm{Dig}(k)\leq 2k
, we conclude that:

\textrm{Dig}\big(A_{d_{k_{0}}^{\mathrm{unr}}-j}\big)\leq\mathrm{Dig}(A)+2(j-1)=A+2(j-1)

\textrm{Dig}\big(B_{d_{k_{0}}^{\mathrm{unr}}-j}\big)\leq\mathrm{Dig}\left(6(C+j-1)\right)+\mathrm{Dig}(3-A)\leq\mathrm{Dig}\left(6(C+j-1)\right)+3-A

Adding these inequalities:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j\leq i}{\big(\textrm{Dig}A_{d_{k_{0}}^{\mathrm{unr}}-j}+\textrm{Dig}B_{d_{k_{0}}^{\mathrm{unr}}-j}}\big)\leq i^{2}+2i+\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j\leq i}{\textrm{Dig}\big(6(C+j-1)\big)}.

We need the following lemma

Lemma 8.10
.

For all
k,C\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

\textrm{Dig}\big((p+1)C\big)\leq\textrm{Dig}(C)+\textrm{Dig}(C+k)+k(p-2)

Proof.

We prove by induction on the number of the digit of
C
written in
p
-base.

If C has only one digit, we have

\textrm{Dig}(C)+\textrm{Dig}(C+k)+k(p-2)\geq\textrm{Dig}(C)+p-1\geq 2\textrm{Dig}(C)=2C=\textrm{Dig}\big((p+1)C\big).

Now we assume that the inequality is true for any
C
with at most
n-1
digits, and prove for the case C has
n
digits.

If
k
has at least
n
digits then
k(p-2)\geq p^{n-1}(p-2)\geq(n-1)p(p-2)\geq(n+1)(p-1)
. The last inequality holds for
n\geq 2
, and
p\geq 5
. We observe that either (p+1)C has
n+1
digits or it has (n+2) digits and start with 10, so in both case the sum of its digit is at most
(n+1)(p-1)
. The lemma follows.

Now we assume
k
has
r\leq n-1
digits and consider 2 cases.

Case 1:
If the first
n-r
digits of C are all equal
p-1
, we write
C=p^{r}D+E
where
0\leq E<p^{r}.

If
E+k<p^{r}
, then
\textrm{Dig}(C+k)=\textrm{Dig}(D)+\textrm{Dig}(E+k)
. By the inductive hypothesis on E, we deduce

\displaystyle\textrm{Dig}(C)+\textrm{Dig}(C+k)+k(p-2)=2\textrm{Dig}(D)+\textrm{Dig}(E)+\textrm{Dig}(E+k)+k(p-2)\geq

\displaystyle\textrm{Dig}\big((p+1)D\big)+\textrm{Dig}\big((p+1)E\big)\geq\textrm{Dig}\big((p+1)C\big)

If
E+k\geq p^{r}
, we can compute
\textrm{Dig}(C+k)=\textrm{Dig}(E+k),\textrm{Dig}(C)=(n-r)(p-1)+\textrm{Dig}(E)
and
\textrm{Dig}\big((p+1)C\big)\leq(n-r)(p-1)+\textrm{Dig}\big((p+1)E\big)
. The lemma follows from the inductive hypothesis on E.

Case 2:

C
has a digit other than
p-1
in the first
n-r
digit, let say it happen at
p^{r^{\prime}}
. We write
C=p^{r^{\prime}-1}D+E
, where
0\leq E<p^{r^{\prime}-1}
. In this case either
E+k\geq p^{r^{\prime}}
or
E+k<p^{r^{\prime}}
, we have
\textrm{Dig}(C+k)=\textrm{Dig}(D)+\textrm{Dig}(E+k)
. This is the same situation as in Case 1.

∎

Applying the lemma for
C,C+1,...,C+i-1
,
k=i
and
p=5
, then summing up the inequalities, we deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j\leq i}{\mathrm{Dig}\left(6(C+j)\right)}\leq\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j\leq 2i}{\textrm{Dig}\big((C+j-1)\big)}+3i^{2}).

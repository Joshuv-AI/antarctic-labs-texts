Total positivity and conjugacy classes
Xuhua He, George Lusztig

Abstract
. In this paper we study the interaction between the totally positive monoid G ⩾ 0 G_{\geqslant 0} attached to a connected reductive group G G with a pinning and the conjugacy classes in G G . In particular we study how a conjugacy class meets the various cells of G ⩾ 0 G_{\geqslant 0} . We also state a conjectural Jordan decomposition for G ⩾ 0 G_{\geqslant 0} and prove it in some special cases.

15B48, 20E45, 20G20

Introduction

Let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
G_{>0}\subset G_{\geqslant 0}
be the totally positive submonoids of
G
associated to a pinning of
G
in
[
8
]
. (In the case where
G=GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, the definition of
G_{>0},G_{\geqslant 0}
goes back to Schoenberg and to Gantmacher-Krein in the 1930’s.) In this paper we are mainly interested in the interaction between these monoids and the conjugacy classes of
G
. Here are some examples of such interaction in the earlier literature.

In
[
4
]
, Gantmacher and Krein showed that when
G=GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, any matrix in
G_{>0}
has
n
distinct eigenvalues which are all in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. This result was generalized in
[
8
]
to a general
G
by showing that

(a) any
g\in G_{>0}
is regular semisimple and contained in a
\mathbb{R}
-split maximal torus.

In
[
2
]
, it is shown that when
G=GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, any matrix in
G_{\geqslant 0}
has all eigenvalues in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. This result was generalized in
[
10
]
to a general
G
by showing that

(b) any
g\in G_{\geqslant 0}
acts on any finite dimensional representation of
G
with all eigenvalues in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

In
[
8
]
it is shown that
G_{\geqslant 0}
contains “relatively few” unipotent elements in the sense that

(c) if
C
is a unipotent class in
G
then
C\cap G_{\geqslant 0}
has dimension less than or equal to half the dimension of the set of real points of
C
(and it can be empty).

Let
G^{{\mathrm{reg}}}
be the open subset of
G
consisting of regular elements in the sense of Steinberg. Let
G^{{\mathrm{reg}},\text{ss}}
be the open subset of
G
consisting of regular semisimple elements.

In
[
8
]
a partition of
G_{\geqslant 0}
into cells indexed by two Weyl group elements was defined. In this paper we show that each cell of
G_{\geqslant 0}
contains regular elements (and even regular semisimple elements). We describe explicitly the cells of
G_{\geqslant 0}
which are entirely contained in
G^{reg}
and also the cells which are entirely contained in
G^{{\mathrm{reg}},\text{ss}}
.

We show that if
G
is almost simple, then the set of regular unipotent elements in
G_{\geqslant 0}
is contained in the union of the unipotent radicals of the two Borel subgroups which are part of the pinning.

If
g\in G
, we have a Jordan decomposition
g=g_{s}g_{u}=g_{u}g_{s}
with
g_{u}
unipotent and
g_{s}
semisimple. Let
H
be the centralizer of
g_{s}
in
G
(by
[
10
]
,
H
is a connected reductive group). If
g\in G_{\geqslant 0}
then
g_{u},g_{s}
are not necessarily contained in
G_{\geqslant 0}
.

As a substitute, in this paper we state a conjecture which says that
H
has something close to a pinning with respect to which we can define
H_{\geqslant 0}
and we have
g_{u}\in H_{\geqslant 0},g_{s}\in H_{\geqslant 0}
. This conjecture is proved in some cases in this paper.

A consequence of this conjecture and of (c) is that, if
C^{\prime}
is a non-semisimple conjugacy class in
G
, defined over
\mathbb{R}
, then
C^{\prime}\cap G_{\geqslant 0}
has dimension less than the dimension of the set of real points of
C^{\prime}
.

1.
Pinnings and total positivity

1.1.
Totally nonnegative monoid

We assume that
G
is defined and split over
\mathbb{R}
. We fix a pinning
\mathbf{P}=(B^{+},B^{-},T,x_{i},y_{i};i\in I)
. Let
W=N_{G}(T)/T
be the Weyl group of
G
and
\{s_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the set of simple reflection in
W
. Let
G_{\geqslant 0}
be the totally nonnegative submonoid of
G
introduced in
[
8
]
. Let
w\in W
and
w=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
be a reduced expression of
w
. We set

\displaystyle U^{+}_{w,>0}

\displaystyle=\{x_{i_{1}}(a_{1})x_{i_{2}}(a_{2})\cdots x_{i_{n}}(a_{n});a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}>0\};

\displaystyle U^{-}_{w,>0}

\displaystyle=\{y_{i_{1}}(a_{1})y_{i_{2}}(a_{2})\cdots y_{i_{n}}(a_{n});a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}>0\}.

For any
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
, we set
G_{w_{1},w_{2},>0}=U^{+}_{w_{1},>0}T_{>0}U^{-}_{w_{2},>0}
. By
[
8
, §2.11]
,
G_{w_{1},w_{2},>0}=U^{-}_{w_{2},>0}T_{>0}U^{+}_{w_{1},>0}
and

G_{\geqslant 0}=\sqcup_{w_{1},w_{2}\in W}G_{w_{1},w_{2},>0}.

For any
i\in I
, let
\alpha_{i}
be the corresponding simple root. Set
\dot{s}_{i}=x_{i}(1)y_{i}(-1)x_{i}(1)
. For any
w\in W
, it is known that for any reduced expression
w=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\cdots
of
w
, the element
\dot{s}_{i_{1}}\dot{s}_{i_{2}}\cdots
is independent of the choice of the reduced expression. We denote this element by
\dot{w}
. For any element
w\in W
, let
\supp(w)
be the set of simple reflections occurring in some (or equivalently, any) reduced expression of
w
.

For any
J\subset I
, let
P^{+}_{J}\supset B^{+}
be the standard parabolic subgroup of
G
,
P^{-}_{J}\supset B^{-}
be the opposite parabolic subgroup and
L_{J}=P^{+}_{J}\cap P^{-}_{J}
be the standard Levi subgroup. For any parabolic subgroup
P
, let
U_{P}
be its unipotent radical. Then
P^{\pm}_{J}=L_{J}U_{P^{\pm}_{J}}
. Let
\Phi
be the root system of
G
and
\Phi_{J}\subset\Phi
be the subsystem attached to
L_{J}
. The Weyl group
W_{J}
of
L_{J}
is the subgroup of
W
generated by
s_{j}
for
j\in J
. We denote by
w_{J}
the longest element in
W_{J}
. We simply write
U^{\pm}_{>0}
for
U^{\pm}_{w_{I},>0}
and write
G_{>0}
for
G_{w_{I},w_{I},>0}
. The set
G_{>0}
is the totally positive part of
G
.

For any
J\subset I
, let
W^{J}
(resp.
{}^{J}W
) be the set of minimal length elements in their cosets in
W/W_{J}
(resp.
W_{J}\backslash W
). For any
J,J^{\prime}\subset I
, we simply write
{}^{J}W^{J^{\prime}}
for
{}^{J}W\cap W^{J^{\prime}}
.

1.2.
Weak pinnings

We define an equivalence relation on the set of pinnings of
G
. Two pinnings
\mathbf{P}=(B^{+},B^{-},T,x_{i},y_{i};i\in I)
,
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}=(B^{\prime}{}^{+},B^{\prime}{}^{-},T^{\prime},x^{\prime}_{i},y^{\prime}_{i};i\in I^{\prime})
are said to be equivalent if
T=T^{\prime},I=I^{\prime}
and if there exists
c=(c_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{I}
such that for any almost simple “factor”
G_{1}
of the derived subgroup of
G
we have

•

either
B^{+}\cap G_{1}=B^{\prime}{}^{+}\cap G_{1}
,
B^{-}\cap G_{1}=B^{\prime}{}^{-}\cap G_{1}
,
x^{\prime}_{i}(a)=x_{i}(c_{i}a)
,
y^{\prime}_{i}(a)=y_{i}(c_{i}^{-1}a)
for all
i\in I,a\in\mathbb{C}
;

•

or
B^{+}\cap G_{1}=B^{\prime}{}^{-}\cap G_{1}
,
B^{-}\cap\ G_{1}=B^{\prime}{}^{+}\cap G_{1}
,
x^{\prime}_{i}(a)=y_{i}(c_{i}a)
,
y^{\prime}_{i}(a)=x_{i}(c_{i}^{-1}a)
for all
i\in I,a\in\mathbb{C}
.

This is an equivalence relation on the set of pinnings of
G
. An equivalence class of pinnings is called a
weak pinning
of
G
. We denote by
\underline{\mathbf{P}}
the weak pinning containing a pinning
\mathbf{P}
.

The monoids
G_{\geqslant 0},G_{>0}
attached in
[
8
]
to a pinning of
G
depend only on the equivalence class of that pinning hence can be viewed as being associated to a weak pinning of
G
. To show that the monoid
G_{\geqslant 0}
determines a unique weaking pinning of
G
, we first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 1.1
.

Suppose that
G
is almost simple. Let
G^{{\mathrm{reg}},\text{uni}}
be the set of regular unipotent elements in
G
. Then

G^{{\mathrm{reg}},\text{uni}}\cap G_{\geqslant 0}=\bigsqcup_{w\in W,\supp(w)=I}U^{+}_{w,>0}\sqcup\sqcup_{w\in W,\supp(w)=I}U^{-}_{w,>0}.

In particular,
G^{{\mathrm{reg}},\text{uni}}\cap G_{\geqslant 0}
has two connected components and they are dense in the unipotent radical of two opposite Borel subgroups
B^{+}
and
B^{-}
.

Proof.

Let
G^{\text{uni}}
be the set of unipotent elements in
G
. By
[
8
, Theorem 6.6]
,

G^{\text{uni}}\cap G_{\geqslant 0}=\bigsqcup_{w_{1},w_{2}\in W;\supp(w_{1})\cap\supp(w_{2})=\emptyset}U^{+}_{w_{1},>0}U^{-}_{w_{2},>0}.

Suppose that
G
is almost simple. Let
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
and
g\in U^{+}_{w_{1},>0}U^{-}_{w_{2},>0}
. Let
J_{i}=\supp(w_{i})
for
i=1,2
. We assume furthermore that
J_{1}\cap J_{2}=\emptyset
. Then
\dot{w}_{J_{2}}g\dot{w}_{J_{2}}^{-1}\in U^{+}
.
If
J_{2}\neq\emptyset
and
J_{2}\neq I
, then there exists
i\in I-J_{2}
such that in the Dynkin diagram of
G
, the vertex corresponds
i
is connected to some vertex in
J_{2}
. In this case,
w_{J_{2}}(\Phi_{J_{1}})\cap\Phi_{\{i\}}=\emptyset
and
\dot{w}_{J_{2}}g\dot{w}_{J_{2}}^{-1}\in U_{P^{+}_{I-\{i\}}}
. Hence
g
is not regular.

Similarly, if
J_{1}\neq\emptyset
and
J_{1}\neq I
, then
g
is not regular. It is obvious that if
J_{1}=J_{2}=\emptyset
, then
g=1
and hence is not regular. Finally if
(J_{1},J_{2})=(I,\emptyset)
or
(\emptyset,I)
, then
g
is regular.

The “in particular” part follows from that the fact that
\sqcup_{w\in W,\supp(w)=I}U^{\pm}_{w,>0}
is connected and dense in
U^{\pm}
.
∎

1.3.
Uniqueness of weak pinnings

Let
G_{\geqslant 0}
be the totally nonnegative part of
G
associated to pinnings
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}=(B^{\prime}{}^{+},B^{\prime}{}^{-},T^{\prime},x^{\prime}_{i},y^{\prime}_{i};i\in I^{\prime})
. We show that
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}
are equivalent.

Let
G_{\text{der}}
be the derived subgroup of
G
. Let
G_{k}
for
1\leqslant k\leqslant l
be the almost simple factors of
G_{\text{der}}
.

For any
k
,
G_{k}\cap G_{\geqslant 0}
is a totally nonnegative part of
G_{k}
associated to the pinnings
(B^{+}\cap G_{k},B^{-}\cap G_{k},T\cap G_{k},\ldots)
,
(B^{\prime}{}^{+}\cap G_{k},B^{\prime}{}^{-}\cap G_{k},T^{\prime}\cap G_{k},\ldots)
of
G_{k}
. By Lemma
1.1
,
G_{k}\cap G^{\prime}_{\geqslant 0}
has two connected components and the closure of these connected components are the unipotent radicals of two opposite Borel subgroups
\{B^{+}\cap G_{k},B^{-}\cap G_{k}\}=\{B^{\prime}{}^{+}\cap G_{k},B^{\prime}{}^{-}\cap G_{k}\}
. In particular,

T\cap G_{k}=(B^{+}\cap G_{k})\cap(B^{-}\cap G_{k})=(B^{\prime}{}^{+}\cap G_{k})\cap(B^{\prime}{}^{+}\cap G_{k})=T^{\prime}\cap G_{k}.

Hence
T=(T\cap G_{1})\cdots(T\cap G_{l})Z(G)=(T^{\prime}\cap G_{1})\cdots(T^{\prime}\cap G_{l})Z(G)=T^{\prime}
.
Recall that
\Phi
is the set of roots of
G
with respect to
T
. Then
\Phi=\Phi_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\Phi_{l}
, where
\Phi_{k}
is the set of roots of
G_{k}
with respect to
T\cap G_{k}
. Let
\Sigma
be the set of positive roots determined by
B^{+}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
be the set of positive roots determined by
B^{\prime}{}^{+}
. Since
\{B^{+}\cap G_{k},B^{-}\cap G_{k}\}=\{B^{\prime}{}^{+}\cap G_{k},B^{\prime}{}^{-}\cap G_{k}\}
, we have
\Sigma^{\prime}\cap\Phi_{k}=\Sigma\cap\Phi_{k}
or
-\Sigma\cap\Phi_{k}
. In particular, we may identify
I
with
I^{\prime}
. If
B^{\prime}{}^{+}\cap G_{k}=B^{+}\cap G_{k}
, then for any
i\in I
with
\alpha_{i}\in\Phi_{K}
, we have
x^{\prime}_{i}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})=x_{i}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
and thus there exists
c_{i}>0
such that
x^{\prime}_{i}(a)=x_{i}(c_{i}a)
for all
a\in\mathbb{C}
. Similarly, if
B^{\prime}{}^{+}\cap G_{k}=B^{-}\cap G_{k}
, then for any
i\in I
with
\alpha_{i}\in\Phi_{K}
, we have
x^{\prime}_{i}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})=y_{i}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
and thus there exists
c_{i}>0
such that
x^{\prime}_{i}(a)=y_{i}(c_{i}a)
for all
a\in\mathbb{C}
.

Thus
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{P}
are equivalent.

1.4.
Weak bases

Let
V
be a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space. A
half line
in
V
is a subset of
V
of the form
\{av;a\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\}
for some
v\in V-\{0\}
. Assume now that
\dim(V)<\infty
. A
weak basis
of
V
is a collection of half lines in
V
such that if we choose a nonzero vector on each of these half lines, the resulting nonzero vectors form a basis of
V
. Note that any basis of
V
gives rise to a weak basis of
V
, which consists of the half lines containing the various elements in that basis.

Let
\underline{\beta}
be a weak basis of
V
. Let
V_{\underline{\beta},\geqslant 0}
be the set of
\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
-linear combination of vectors in the half lines in
\beta
. This subset is closed under addition and under scalar multiplication by
\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
. Let
V_{\underline{\beta},>0}
be the set of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-linear combination of nonzero vectors in the half lines in
\beta
. This subset of
V
is closed under addition and under scalar multiplication by
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. If
\underline{\beta},\underline{\beta}^{\prime}
are two weak bases of
V
, we have

V_{\underline{\beta},\geqslant 0}=V_{\underline{\beta}^{\prime},\geqslant 0}\Rightarrow V_{\underline{\beta},>0}=V_{\underline{\beta}^{\prime},>0}\Rightarrow\underline{\beta}=\underline{\beta}^{\prime}.

We now assume that
G
is simply laced.

We fix a weak pinning
\underline{\mathbf{P}}
of
G
. Let
X_{\underline{\mathbf{P}}}
be the set of Borel subgroups
B
of
G
such that
B=B^{+}
for some pinning
\mathbf{P}=(B^{+},B^{-},\ldots)
in
\underline{\mathbf{P}}
.

Let
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
be the collection of irreducible finite dimensional rational representations of
G
. Let
V\in\mathrm{Irr}G
. For any Borel subgroup
B
of
G
we denote by
V_{B}
the unique
B
-invariant
\mathbb{C}
-line in
V
.

For
B\in X_{\underline{\mathbf{P}}}
let
e_{B}
be the set of half lines in
V_{B}
. For any pinning
\mathbf{P}=(B^{+},B^{-},\ldots)
in
\underline{\mathbf{P}}
and any
v\in V_{B^{+}}-\{0\}
we denote by
\mathbb{B}_{V,\mathbf{P},v}
the canonical basis of
V
containing
v
defined in terms of
\mathbf{P}
in
[
6
]
.

If
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}=(B^{\prime}{}^{+},B^{\prime}{}^{-},\ldots)
is another pinning in
\underline{\mathbf{P}}
and if
v^{\prime}\in V_{B^{\prime}{}^{+}}-\{0\}
is defined by the condition that
v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{V,\mathbf{P},v}
then, using
[
7
, Proposition 21.1.2]
, we see that the canonical basis
\mathbb{B}_{V,\mathbf{P}^{\prime},v^{\prime}}
of
V
gives rise to the same weak basis of
V
as
\mathbb{B}_{V,\mathbf{P},v}
; moreover, the half line in
V_{B^{\prime}{}^{+}}
containing
v^{\prime}
depends only on the half line containing
v
. Thus the sets
e_{B}
for various
B\in X_{\underline{\mathbf{P}}}
can be identified with a single set
e_{V,\underline{\mathbf{P}}}
. We also see that
\underline{\mathbf{P}}
together with a choice of an element
\epsilon\in e_{V,\underline{\mathbf{P}}}
give rise to a weak basis
\beta_{V,\underline{\mathbf{P}},\epsilon}
of
V
.

Hence the subsets
V_{\beta_{V,\underline{\mathbf{P}},\epsilon},\geqslant 0}
,
V_{\beta_{V,\underline{\mathbf{P}},\epsilon},>0}
are defined.

According to
[
8
, §3.2]
, if
g\in G_{\underline{\mathbf{P}},>0}
then
g:VV
maps
V_{\beta_{V,\underline{\mathbf{P}},\epsilon},\geqslant 0}
into
V_{\beta_{V,\underline{\mathbf{P}},\epsilon},>0}
. Conversely, according to
[
3
, Theorem 1.11]
, if
g\in G
is such that for any
V\in\text{Irr}(G)
,
g:VV
maps
V_{\beta_{V,\underline{\mathbf{P}},\epsilon},\geqslant 0}
into
V_{\beta_{V,\underline{\mathbf{P}},\epsilon},>0}
(for some

), then
g\in G_{>0}
.

2.
Regular elements

2.1.
The main results

Recall that
G^{{\mathrm{reg}}}
is the set of regular elements in
G
and
G^{{\mathrm{reg}},\text{ss}}
is the set of regular semisimple elements in
G
. Now we state the main results of this section.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
. Then

(1)
G_{w_{1},w_{2},>0}\cap G^{{\mathrm{reg}},\text{ss}}\neq\emptyset
.

(2)
G_{w_{1},w_{2},>0}\subset G^{{\mathrm{reg}},\text{ss}}
if and only if
\supp(w_{1})=\supp(w_{2})=I
.

(3) Suppose that
G
is almost simple. Then
G_{w_{1},w_{2},>0}\subset G^{{\mathrm{reg}}}
if and only if
\supp(w_{1})=I
or
\supp(w_{2})=I
.

2.2.
Some semigroups

Let
\ast:W\times WW
is the monoid product (see
[
10
, §2.11]
. Then for any
w,w^{\prime}\in W
, we have
\supp(w\ast w^{\prime})=\supp(w)\cup\supp(w^{\prime})
. By
[
10
, §2.11(d)]
, we have
G_{w_{1},w_{2},>0}G_{w_{3},w_{4},>0}=G_{w_{1}\ast w_{3},w_{2}\ast w_{4},>0}
. Now as a consequence of Theorem
2.1
, we have

(a)
the union of the totally nonnegative cells consisting of regular semisimple elements is an open sub semigroup of
G_{\geqslant 0}
;

(b)
the union of the totally nonnegative cells consisting of regular elements is an open sub semigroup of
G_{\geqslant 0}
.

2.3.
Oscillatory elements

We call an element
g\in G_{\geqslant 0}

oscillatory
if
g^{m}\in G_{>0}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By
[
8
, Proposition 2.19]
, the subset of oscillatory elements is
G^{I,I}_{\geqslant 0}
. By
[
8
, Corollary 5.7]
, any oscillatory element is regular and semisimple. This is the “if” part of Theorem
2.1
(2).

We have the following result, which generalizes
[
8
, Corollary 8.10]
from totally positive elements to oscillatory elements.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
g
be an oscillatory element. Then there exists a unique element
u\in U^{-}_{>0}
such that
u^{-1}gu\in B^{+}
. Moreover,
g=uu^{\prime}tu^{-1}
for some
u^{\prime}\in U^{+}_{\geqslant 0}
and
t\in T
with
\alpha_{i}(t)>1
for all
i\in I
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}=G/B^{+}
be the flag variety of
G
. We denote the action of
G
on
\mathcal{B}
by
g\cdot B:=gBg^{-1}
. Let
\mathcal{B}_{>0}=U^{-}_{>0}\cdot B^{+}\subset\mathcal{B}
be the totally positive flag manifold and
\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}=\overline{\mathcal{B}_{>0}}
be the totally nonnegative flag manifold. By
[
8
, Corollary 8.11]
, there exists
B\in\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}
with
g\in B
. We have
g^{m}\in G_{>0}
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By
[
8
, Lemma 8.2]
, there exists a unique
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}
with
g^{n}\in B^{\prime}
. Since
g\in B
, we have
g^{n}\in B
. Thus
B=B^{\prime}
. By
[
8
, Lemma 8.5]
,
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{>0}
. Hence
B=uB^{+}u^{-1}
for some
u\in U^{-}_{>0}
. So
u^{-1}gu\in B^{+}
. The uniqueness of
u
follows from the uniqueness of
B
.

Let
u^{-1}gu=u^{\prime}t
with
u^{\prime}\in U^{+}
and
t\in T
. We then have
uu^{\prime}t=gu\in G_{\geqslant 0}
. Therefore we have
u^{\prime}\in U^{+}_{\geqslant 0}
and
t\in T_{>0}
. Moreover,
g^{m}=u(u^{\prime}t)^{m}u^{-1}=uu^{\prime}_{m}t^{m}u^{-1}
for some
u^{\prime}_{m}\in U^{+}_{>0}
. By
[
8
, Corollary 8.10]
,
\alpha_{i}(t^{m})>1
for all
i\in I
. Hence
\alpha_{i}(t)>1
for all
i\in I
.
∎

2.4.
Projection maps

For
J\subset I
, let
\pi^{\pm}_{J}:P^{\pm}_{J}L_{J}
be the projection map. Then the restriction of
\pi^{\pm}_{J}
to
T_{>0}
is the identity map. Moreover,
\pi^{+}_{J}(y_{i}(a))=y_{i}(a)
and
\pi^{-}_{J}(x_{i}(a))=x_{i}(a)
for
i\in J
. We also have

\pi^{+}_{J}(x_{i}(a))=\begin{cases}x_{i}(a),&\text{ if }i\in J\\
1,&\text{ if }i\notin J\end{cases}\text{ and }\pi^{-}_{J}(y_{i}(a))=\begin{cases}y_{i}(a),&\text{ if }i\in J;\\
1,&\text{ if }i\notin J.\end{cases}

In particular,
\pi^{\pm}_{J}(P^{\pm}_{J}\cap G_{\geqslant 0})=L_{J,\geqslant 0}
.
We define a morphism of monoids
\pi_{J}:WW_{J}
by
s_{i}s_{i}
if
i\in J
and
s_{i}1
if
i\notin J
. Although not needed in this paper, it is worth pointing out that the map

Before proving the “only if” part of Theorem

Lemma 2.3

Suppose that

2.8.
Regularity for
GL_{n}

Now we study the “if” part of Theorem
2.1
(3). The key part is to show that any element in
B^{+}_{>0}=T_{>0}U^{+}_{>0}
is regular. In this subsection, we prove this claim via linear algebra.

Let
G=GL_{n}
and
g=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in[1,n]}
in
B^{+}_{>0}
. In particular,
a_{ij}=0
if
i<j
,
a_{ij}>0
if
i\geqslant j
and various minors are
>0
. For any
J_{1},J_{2}\subset\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, let
\Delta_{J_{1},J_{2}}
be the
(J_{1},J_{2})
-minor of
g
, i.e., the determinant of the submatrix
(a_{ij})_{i\in J_{1},j\in J_{2}}
. To show that
g
is regular, it suffices to show that any eigenspace of
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\mathbb{R}^{n}
is one-dimensional.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
. Set

V_{k}=\{(A_{1},...,A_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\sum_{j}a_{ij}A_{j}=a_{kk}A_{i}\text{ for all }i\in[1,n]\}.

Then
\dim V_{k}=1
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
n
.

If
n=1
, the result is obvious.

Assume now that
n\geqslant 2
. Let
c=a_{kk}
and
I_{c}=\{i\in[1,n];a_{ii}=c\}
. Let
(A_{1},...,A_{n})\in V_{k}
. If
A_{n}=0
, then
(A_{1},...,A_{n-1})
satisfy a condition similar to that defining
V_{k}
. In this case, the statement follow from the induction hypothesis.

If
n\notin I_{c}
, then
(a_{nn}-c)A_{n}=0
and
A_{n}=0
.

We assume that
n\in I_{c}
. If
n-1\in I_{c}
, then
a_{n-1,n}A_{n}=0
. Since
a_{n-1,n}\neq 0
, we have
A_{n}=0
and the statement follows again from the induction hypothesis.

Now we assume that
n\in I_{c},n-1\notin I_{c}
. If
n-2\in I_{c}
, then we have the linear system

\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{n-2,n-1}A_{n-1}+a_{n-2,n}A_{n}&=0\\
(a_{n-1,n-1}-c)A_{n-1}+a_{n-1,n}A_{n}&=0\end{aligned}\right.

The determinant of this linear system is

a_{n-2,n-1}a_{n-1,n}-(a_{n-1,n-1}-c)a_{n-2,n}=\Delta_{\{n-2,n-1\},\{n-1,n\}}+ca_{n-2.n}>0.

It follows that
A_{n-1}=A_{n}=0
. In particular
A_{n}=0
and we are done.

We then assume that
n\in I_{c}
,
n-1\notin I_{c}
,
n-2\notin I_{c}
. If
n-3\in I_{c}
, we have the linear system

\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{n-3,n-2}A_{n-2}+a_{n-3,n-1}A_{n-1}+a_{n-3,n}A_{n}&=0\\
(a_{n-2,n-2}-c)A_{n-2}+a_{n-2,n-1}A_{n-1}+a_{n-2,n}A_{n}&=0\\
(a_{n-1,n-1}-c)A_{n-1}+a_{n-1,n}A_{n}&=0\end{aligned}\right.

The determinant of this system of linear equations is

\displaystyle a_{n-3,n-2}a_{n-2,n-1}a_{n-1,n}+(a_{n-2,n-2}-c)(a_{n-1,n-1}-c)a_{n-3,n}

\displaystyle-a_{n-3,n-1}(a_{n-2,n-2}-c)a_{n-1,n}-a_{n-3,n-2}(a_{n-1,n-1}-c)a_{n-2,n}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\{n-3,n-2,n-1\},\{n-2,n-1,n\}}+c(\Delta_{\{n-3,n-1\},\{n-1,n\}}+\Delta_{\{n-3,n-2\},\{n-2,n\}})+c^{2}a_{n-3,n}.

Again the determinant is
>0
. It follows that
A_{n-2}=A_{n-1}=A_{n}=0
. In particular
A_{n}=0
and we are done.

Thus we can assume
n\in I_{c},n-1\notin I_{c},n-2\notin I_{c},n-3\notin I_{c}
.
Continuing in this way we see that we can assume
n\in I_{c}
and
n-1,n-2,...,1
are not in
I_{c}
.

Then we can choose
A_{n}
at random. From

(a_{n-1,n-1}-c)A_{n-1}+a_{n-1,n}A_{n}=0
where
a_{n-1,n-1}-c\neq 0
we determine uniquely
A_{n-1}
.
From
(a_{n-2,n-2}-c)A_{n-2}+a_{n-2,n-1}A_{n-1}+a_{n-2,n}A_{n}=0
where
a_{n-2,n-2}-c\neq 0
we
determine uniquely
A_{n-2}
.
Continuing in this way we see that
A_{n-1},A_{n-2},...,A_{1}
are uniquely determined
by
A_{n}
. Hence
\dim V_{k}=1
.
∎

For any

H
so that the associated totally nonnegative part
H_{\underline{\mathbf{P}_{H}},\geqslant 0}
of
H
contains the unipotent part
g_{u}
of
g
(and hence contains
g
itself). Moreover, the desired weak pinning
\underline{\mathbf{P}_{H}}
of
H
has a representation-theoretic interpretation.

To do this, we use the positivity property of the canonical basis for the simply-laced groups. For arbitrary group, we may apply the “folding method” of
[
8
]
once the desired result is proved for the simply-laced groups.

By
[
10
, §9]
, if
g\in G_{\geqslant 0}
, then
g_{s}
is conjugate by an element in
G(\mathbb{R})
to an element in
T_{>0}
. Let
\lambda
be a dominant weight of
G
and
V_{\lambda}
be the corresponding irreducible finite dimensional representation of
G
. We have
V_{\lambda}=\oplus_{c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}V_{\lambda}(g,c)
, where
V_{\lambda}(g,c)
is the generalized eigenspace of
g:V_{\lambda}V_{\lambda}
(see
[
10
, §9]
). Now
H=Z_{G}(g_{s})
acts naturally on each
V_{\lambda}(g,c)
. Let
c_{M}=\max\{c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0};V_{\lambda}(c)\neq 0\}
. We show that

(a)
For any
g\in G_{\geqslant 0}
and any dominant weight
\lambda
of
G
,
V_{\lambda}(g,c_{M})
is an irreducible representation of
H
with highest weight
\lambda
.

We call an element
t\in T_{>0}
a
standard element
if
\alpha_{i}(t)\geqslant 1
for all
i\in I
. If
g_{s}
is a standard element, then
H
is a standard Levi subgroup
L_{J}
for some
J\subset I
. By definition,
V_{\lambda}(g,c_{M})
is spanned by the weight vectors with
\mu
such that
\mu(g_{s})=\lambda(g_{s})
. Note that the condition
\mu(g_{s})=\lambda(g_{s})
holds if and only if
\lambda-\mu\in\sum_{i\in J}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
. In this case,
V_{\lambda}(g,c_{M})
is the
L_{J}
-subrepresentation of
V_{\lambda}
generated by the highest weight vectors of
V_{\lambda}
. In particular,
V_{\lambda}(g,c_{M})
is an irreducible finite dimensional representation of
H
with the highest weight
\lambda
.

Note that any element in
T_{>0}
is conjugate to a standard element. Thus for any
g\in G_{>0}
, there exists
h\in G(\mathbb{R})
such that
hg_{s}h^{-1}\in T_{>0}
and
\alpha_{i}(hg_{s}h^{-1})\geqslant 1
for all
i\in I
. Set
g^{\prime}=hgh^{-1}
. Then
g^{\prime}_{s}=hg_{s}h^{-1}
,
Z_{G}(g^{\prime}_{s})=hHh^{-1}
and
V_{\lambda}(g^{\prime},c_{M})=h\cdot V_{\lambda}(g,c_{M})
. Hence (a) is proved.

Now we state the conjectural totally nonnegative Jordan decomposition.

Conjecture 3.1
.

Assume that
G
is simply laced. Let
g\in G_{\geqslant 0}
and
H=Z_{G}(g_{s})
. Then there exists a weak pinning
\underline{\mathbf{P}_{H}}
of
H
such that

(1)

g_{u}\in H_{\underline{\mathbf{P}_{H}},\geqslant 0}
;

(2)

V(g,c_{M})_{\underline{\mathbf{P}_{H}},\geqslant 0}=V(g,c_{M})\cap V_{\geqslant 0}
for any irreducible representation
V
of
G
.

By §
1.4
and §
1.2
, the weak pinning on
H
, if it exists, is uniquely determined by
V(g,c_{M})\cap V_{\geqslant 0}
. Moreover, as the totally nonnegative part of
H
is determined by a representation-theoretic method, it only depends on the semisimple part
g_{s}
, but not on the element
g
itself. In particular, Conjecture
3.1
claims that the totally nonnegative part
H_{\geqslant 0}
is “canonical”.

3.2.
Non simply-laced groups

We follow
[
8
, §1.6]
. Let
\tilde{G}
be the simply connected (algebraic) covering of the derived group of
G
and
\pi:\tilde{G}G
be the covering homormorphism. The pinning on
G
induces a pinning on
\tilde{G}
. Then there exists a semisimple, simply connected, simply laced group
\dot{G}
, a pinning on
\dot{G}
and an automorphism
\sigma:\dot{G}\dot{G}
compatible with the pinning such that there is an isomorphism
\dot{G}^{\sigma}\tilde{G}
so that the associated pinning on
\dot{G}^{\sigma}
(defined in
[
8
, §1.5]
) is compatible with the pinning on
\tilde{G}
. In this case, we have
G_{\geqslant 0}=(Z_{G})_{>0}\,\pi(\dot{G}^{\sigma}_{\geqslant 0})
.

Let
g\in G_{\geqslant 0}
. Then there exists
z\in(Z_{G})_{\geqslant 0}
and
\dot{g}\in\dot{G}_{\geqslant 0}^{\sigma}
such that
g=z\pi(\dot{g})
. Then
g_{s}=z\pi((\dot{g})_{s})
and
g_{u}=\pi((\dot{g})_{u})
. Set
H=Z_{G}(g_{s})
and
\dot{H}=Z_{\dot{G}}((\dot{g})_{s})
. Then
H=Z_{G}\,\pi(\dot{H}^{\sigma})
.

We assume that Conjecture
3.1
holds for
\dot{G}
. Since
\dot{H}=s(\dot{H})
and the totally nonnegative part
\dot{H}_{\geqslant 0}
is “canoncial”, we have in particular that
H_{\geqslant 0}
is
\sigma
-stable. Set
H_{\geqslant 0}=(Z_{G})_{>0}\,\pi(\dot{H}^{\sigma}_{\geqslant 0})
. By Conjecture
3.1
,
\dot{g}_{u}\in\dot{H}_{\geqslant 0}^{\sigma}
. Thus

g_{u}=\pi(\dot{g}_{u})\in\pi(\dot{H}^{\sigma}_{\geqslant 0})\subset H_{\geqslant 0}.

As a summary, we have

(a)
Suppose that Conjecture
3.1
holds for
\dot{G}
. Then for any Levi subgroup
H
of a parabolic subgroup of
G
, there is a “canonical” totally nonnegative part
H_{\geqslant 0}
such that for any
g\in G_{\geqslant 0}
with
Z_{G}(g_{s})=H
, we have
g_{u}\in H_{\geqslant 0}
.

3.3.
Some evidence of Conjecture
3.1

In this subsection, we verify Conjecture
3.1
in some special cases. We start with a lemma on
V_{\geqslant 0}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
V
be an irreducible finite dimensional representation of
G
and
\beta
be the canonical basis of
V
. Let
J\subset I
and
V_{J}\subset V
be the
L_{J}
-submodule spanned by the highest weight vector of
V
. Let
V_{\geqslant 0}=\sum_{b\in\beta}\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}b
and
(V_{J})_{\geqslant 0}=V_{J}\cap V_{\geqslant 0}
. Then for any
w\in W^{J}
and
u\in U^{-}_{w,>0}
, we have

u\cdot(V_{J})_{\geqslant 0}=(u\cdot V_{J})\cap V_{\geqslant 0}.

Proof.

By
[
8
, §3.2]
,
u\cdot V_{\geqslant 0}\subset V_{\geqslant 0}
. Thus
u\cdot(V_{J})_{\geqslant 0}\subset(u\cdot V_{J})\cap V_{\geqslant 0}
. Now we prove the other direction. By definition,
(V_{J})_{\geqslant 0}=\sum_{b\in\beta\cap V_{J}}\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}b
. We recall the following result.

(a)
Let
i\in I
and
b\in\beta
such that
x_{i}(a)\cdot b=b
for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
\dot{s}_{i}\cdot b\in\beta
.

This is a special case of the braid group action on the canonical basis
[
9
]
. See also
[
1
, Lemma 2.8]
.

Let
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
be a reduced expression. Since
w\in W^{J}
, for any
j
we have
(s_{i_{j+1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}})^{-1}\alpha_{i_{j}}\notin\Phi_{J}
. By definition,
x_{i_{j}}(a)(s_{i_{j+1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}})\cdot b=(s_{i_{j+1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}})\cdot b
for any
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
b\in V_{J}
. Thus by (a), for any
b\in\beta\cap V_{J}
, we have
\dot{s}_{i_{n}}\cdot b\in\beta
,
\dot{s}_{i_{n-1}}\dot{s}_{i_{n}}\cdot b\in\beta
, …,
\dot{w}\cdot b\in\beta
.

By definition,

u\cdot b\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\dot{w}\cdot b+\sum_{b^{\prime}\in\beta,\text{wt}(b^{\prime})>\text{wt}(b),\text{wt}(b^{\prime})-\text{wt}(b)\in\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{N}s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{j-1}}\alpha_{i_{j}}}\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}b^{\prime}.

Since
w\in W^{J}
,
w(\Phi_{J})\cap\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{N}s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{j-1}}\alpha_{i_{j}}=\{0\}
. In other words, the only element in
\{\dot{w}\cdot b^{\prime};b^{\prime}\in\beta\cap V_{J}\}
that occurs in the right hand of (b) with positive coefficient is
\dot{w}\cdot b
.

Let
\sum_{b\in\beta\cap V_{J}}c_{b}b\in V_{J}
with
u\cdot\sum_{b\in\beta\cap V_{J}}c_{b}b\in V_{\geqslant 0}
. Note that

u\cdot\sum_{b\in\beta\cap V_{J}}c_{b}b=\sum_{b\in\beta\cap V_{J}}c_{b}u\cdot b\in\sum_{b\in\beta\cap V_{J}}c_{b}\mathbb{R}_{>0}\dot{w}\cdot b+\sum_{b^{\prime}\in\beta,b^{\prime}\notin\dot{w}\cdot V_{J}}\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}b^{\prime}.

Then
c_{b}\geqslant 0
for all
b\in\beta\cap V_{J}
. Thus
\sum_{b\in\beta\cap V_{J}}c_{b}b\in(V_{J})_{\geqslant 0}
. The Lemma is proved.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
with
\supp(w_{2})\subset\supp(w_{1})
. Then for any
g\in G_{w_{1},w_{2},>0}
or
g\in G_{w_{2},w_{1},>0}
, conjecture
3.1
holds.

Proof.

We prove the case where
g\in G_{w_{1},w_{2},>0}
. The case
g\in G_{w_{2},w_{1},>0}
is proved in the same way.

Let
J_{i}=\supp(w_{i})
. By Lemma
2.2
, there exists
u_{1}\in U^{-}_{w_{J_{2}},>0}
such that
u_{1}^{-1}gu_{1}\in tU^{+}_{w_{1},>0}
for some
t\in T_{>0}
with
\alpha_{i}(t)>1
for all
i\in J_{2}
. By Lemma
2.5
, there exists
u_{2}\in U^{-}_{w_{t,J_{1}}^{-1},>0}
such that
u_{2}^{-1}u_{1}^{-1}gu_{1}u_{2}\in\bar{t}_{J_{1}}U^{+}_{w_{1},>0}
. By definition,
w_{t,J_{1}}\in W_{J_{1}}\cap W^{J_{2}}
. Thus
\ell(w_{J_{2}}w_{t,J_{1}}^{-1})=\ell(w_{J_{2}})+\ell(w_{t,J_{1}})
. We set
u_{-}=u_{1}u_{2}\in U^{-}_{w_{J_{2}}w_{t,J_{1}}^{-1},>0}
. It is also easy to see that there exists
u_{+}\in L_{J_{1}}\cap U_{P^{+}_{I(\bar{t}_{J_{1}})}}
such that

•

the semisimple part
u_{+}^{-1}u_{-}^{-1}g_{s}u_{-}u_{+}
of
u_{+}^{-1}u_{-}^{-1}gu_{-}u_{+}
is
\bar{t}_{J_{1}}
;

•

the unipotent part
u_{+}^{-1}u_{-}^{-1}g_{u}u_{-}u_{+}
of
u_{+}^{-1}u_{-}^{-1}gu_{-}u_{+}
is in
U^{+}_{\geqslant 0}\cap L_{I(\bar{t}_{J_{1}})}
.

Let
\bar{t}=\bar{t}_{I}
and
J=I(\bar{t})
. Let
w
be the unique element in
{}^{J_{1}}W^{J}
with
\bar{t}=\dot{w}^{-1}\bar{t}_{J_{1}}\dot{w}
. Hence the semisimple part of
\dot{w}^{-1}u_{+}^{-1}u_{-}^{-1}gu_{-}u_{+}\dot{w}
is
\bar{t}
. Let
H=Z_{G}(g_{s})
. Since
Z_{G}(\bar{t})=L_{J}
, we have
H=(u_{-}u_{+}\dot{w})L_{J}(u_{-}u_{+}\dot{w})^{-1}
. The pinning
\mathbf{P}_{H}
of

is defined to be the conjugate of the standard pinning of

by the element
u_{-}u_{+}\dot{w}
. In particular,
H_{\underline{\mathbf{P}_{H}},\geqslant 0}=(u_{-}u_{+}\dot{w})L_{J,\geqslant 0}(u_{-}u_{+}\dot{w})^{-1}
.

Since
\bar{t}=\dot{w}^{-1}\bar{t}_{J_{1}}\dot{w}
, we have
\Phi_{I(\bar{t}_{J_{1}})}=w(\Phi_{J})\cap\Phi_{J_{1}}
. Since
w\in{}^{J_{1}}W^{J}
, we have
w^{-1}(I(\bar{t}_{J_{1}}))\subset J
. Therefore
\dot{w}^{-1}(U^{+}_{\geqslant 0}\cap L_{I(\bar{t}_{J_{1}})})\dot{w}\subset U^{+}_{\geqslant 0}\cap L_{J}
. Thus

g_{u}\in(u_{-}u_{+})(U^{+}_{\geqslant 0}\cap L_{I(\bar{t}_{J_{1}})})(u_{-}u_{+})^{-1}\subset(u_{-}u_{+}\dot{w})L_{J,\geqslant 0}(u_{-}u_{+}\dot{w})^{-1}=H_{\underline{\mathbf{P}_{H}},\geqslant 0}.

Part (1) of Conjecture
3.1
is proved.

Let
V
be an irreducible finite dimensional representation of
G
and
v
be a highest weight vector of
V
. Let
V_{J}\subset V
be the
L_{J}
-submodule generated by
v
. Then
V(\bar{t},c_{M})=V_{J}
and
V(g,c_{M})=u_{-}u_{+}\dot{w}\cdot V_{J}
. The nonnegative part
V(g,c_{M})_{\underline{\mathbf{P}_{H}},\geqslant 0}
of

determined by the pinning

of

equals
u_{-}u_{+}\dot{w}\cdot V_{J,\geqslant 0}
. Since
\Phi_{I(\bar{t}_{J_{1}})}=w(\Phi_{J})\cap\Phi_{J_{1}}
and
w\in{}^{J_{1}}W
, we have
\dot{w}^{-1}u_{+}\dot{w}\in U_{P^{+}_{J}}
. Thus
u_{-}u_{+}\dot{w}\cdot V_{J}=u_{-}\dot{w}u\cdot V_{J}=u_{-}\dot{w}\cdot V_{J}
for some

. Thus

V(g,c_{M})_{\underline{\mathbf{P}_{H}},\geqslant 0}=u_{-}\dot{w}\cdot V_{J,\geqslant 0}.

By the proof of Lemma
3.2
,
\dot{w}\cdot V_{J,\geqslant 0}\subset V_{\geqslant 0}
. Since
u_{-}\in U^{-}_{\geqslant 0}
, we have
u_{-}\cdot V_{\geqslant 0}\subset V_{\geqslant 0}
. Thus
V(g,c_{M})_{\underline{\mathbf{P}_{H}},\geqslant 0}\subset V_{\geqslant 0}
.

On the other hand, let
\beta
be the canonical basis of
V
. Suppose that
\sum_{b\in\beta\cap V_{J}}c_{b}b\in V_{J}
with
u_{-}\dot{w}\cdot\sum_{b\in\beta\cap V_{J}}c_{b}b\in V_{\geqslant 0}
. By (b) in the proof of Lemma

, we have

u_{-}\dot{w}\cdot\sum_{b\in\beta\cap V_{J}}c_{b}b\in\sum_{b\in\beta\cap V_{J}}c_{b}\mathbb{R}_{>0}\dot{w}^{\prime}\cdot b+\sum_{b^{\prime}\in\beta,\beta^{\prime}\notin\dot{w}^{\prime}\cdot V_{J}}\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}b^{\prime}.

Thus

for all

and
\sum_{b\in\beta\cap V_{J}}c_{b}b\in(V_{J})_{\geqslant 0}
.

Therefore
V(g,c_{M})_{\underline{\mathbf{P}_{H}},\geqslant 0}=u_{-}\dot{w}\cdot V_{J,\geqslant 0}=u_{-}\dot{w}\cdot V_{J}\cap V_{\geqslant 0}=V(g,c_{M})\cap V_{\geqslant 0}
. Part (2) of Conjecture
3.1
is proved.
∎

3.4.
Verification of conjecture
3.1
for
GL_{3}

In this subsection, we prove conjecture
3.1
for
G=GL_{3}
. Let
W=S_{3}
be the Weyl group of
G
and
\{s_{1},s_{2}\}
be the set of simple reflections of
W
. By Proposition
3.3
, if
g\in G_{\geqslant 0}
but
g\notin G_{s_{1},s_{2},>0}\cup G_{s_{2},s_{1},>0}
, then the conjecture
3.1
holds for
g
. Now we prove the conjecture
3.1
for
g\in G_{s_{1},s_{2},>0}
. The elements in
G_{s_{2},s_{1},>0}
are handled in the same way.

The element
g
can be written as
g=tu_{1}u_{2}
for some
u_{1}\in U^{+}_{s_{1},>0}
,
u_{2}\in U^{-}_{s_{2},>0}
and
t\in T_{>0}
.

If
\alpha_{1}(t)\neq 1
and
\alpha_{2}(t)\neq 1
, then
g
is regular semisimple and the conjecture
3.1
is obvious for
g
.

If
\alpha_{1}(t)=\alpha_{2}(t)=1
, then
g_{s}=t
and
H=G
. In this case, the weak pinning on
H
is the equivalance class of the pinning on
G
we fixed in the beginning. The conjecture
3.1
holds for
g
.

It remains to consider the case where
\alpha_{1}(t)=1
and
\alpha_{2}(t)\neq 1
. (The case where
\alpha_{1}(t)\neq 1
and
\alpha_{2}(t)=1
is proved in the same way.) In this case, there exists
a\in\mathbb{R}
such that
y_{2}(-a)gy_{2}(a)=tu_{1}
. We have
Z_{G}(t)=L_{\{1\}}
and
H=y_{2}(a)L_{\{1\}}y_{2}(-a)
. The pinning on
G
induces the pinning
\mathbf{P}_{\{1\}}=(T,B^{+}\cap L_{\{1\}},B^{-}\cap L_{\{1\}},x_{1},y_{1})
on the Levi subgroup
L_{\{1\}}
. The pinning
\mathbf{P}_{H}
on
H
is obtained from
\mathbf{P}_{\{1\}}
by conjugating
y_{2}(a)
. Moreover
V(g_{s},c_{M})=y_{2}(a)\cdot V(t,c_{M})
and
V(g_{s},c_{M})_{\underline{\mathbf{P}_{H}},\geqslant 0}=y_{2}(a)\cdot V(t,c_{M})_{\geqslant 0}
.

If
\alpha_{2}(t)<1
, then
V(t,c_{M})
is the
L_{\{1\}}
-submodule of
V
generated by the lowest weight vector of
V
and
V(g_{s},C_{M})=V(t,c_{M})
. In this case,
V(g_{s},c_{M})_{\underline{\mathbf{P}_{H}},\geqslant 0}=V(t,c_{M})_{\geqslant 0}=V(g_{s},C_{M})\cap V_{\geqslant 0}
.

If
\alpha_{2}(t)>1
, then
V(t,c_{M})
is the
L_{\{1\}}
-submodule of
V
generated by the highest weight vector of
V
. Moreover, we have
a>0
since
u_{2}\in U^{-}_{s_{2},>0}
. By Lemma
3.2
,
V(g_{s},c_{M})_{\underline{\mathbf{P}_{H}},\geqslant 0}=y_{2}(a)\cdot V(t,c_{M})_{\geqslant 0}=V(g_{s},C_{M})\cap V_{\geqslant 0}
.

This finishes the verification of the conjecture
3.1
for
G=GL_{3}
.

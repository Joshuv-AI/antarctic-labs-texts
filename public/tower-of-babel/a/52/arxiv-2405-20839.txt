We use two different types of quadratic variations, weak sense and strong sense which our two main results deal with. For weak sense quadratic variations we show stability when f ∈ C 1 f\in C^{1} , { X n } n , X \{X^{n}\}_{n},X are Dirichlet processes defined as in Coquet, Mémin and Słomiński (2003) X n → a . s . 1
Introduction

Stability in quadratic variation, namely how
[f(X^{n})-f(X)]\to 0
when
X^{n}\to X
for certain cadlag processes
\{X^{n}\}_{n}
,
X
is intimately tied to convergence of
\int Y_{-}df(X^{n})\to\int Y_{-}df(X)
by the integration by parts and the Kunita-Watanabe inequality. Under assumptions on uniform convergence and that
f\in C^{1}
results can be readily achieved by elementary means. Our focus in this manuscript is then to relax both of these conditions, i.e. assume pointwise convergence of
X^{n}\to X
instead of uniform and relax the
C^{1}
assumption on
f
. Most results will be concerning Dirichlet processes, although some more elementary result concern general cadlag processes with quadratic variation. We consider two types of quadratic variations, one which we term weak-sense is the type of quadratic variation that has typically used in the study of Dirichlet processes. This is indeed a weak type of quadratic variation as it only assumes that the limit (in probability) of the squared differentials only exists along a certain refining sequence (a sequence of fixed-time partitions with mesh tending to zero). The second kind of quadratic variation, we term quadratic variation in the strong sense assumes that approximating sum of squared differentials exists as uniform limit in probability along
any
all sequences of stopping time partitions with mesh tending to zero. It should be pointed out that if
f
is a transform that preserves the presence of quadratic variation for a process
X
along its given fixed time refining sequence, then if
X
has quadratic variation along all fixed time refining sequences so will
f(X)
. This however has no bearing on stopping time refining sequences.

Continuous Dirichlet processes were first defined in
Föllmer (1981)
, as a sum of a continuous local martingale and a continuous process with zero quadratic variation (along some given sequence of refining partitions). Discontinuous Dirichlet processes were later defined in
Stricker (1988)
as the sum of a semimartingale and a process of zero quadratic variation (along some sequence of refining partitions). In
Coquet, Mémin and Słomiński (2003)
it was shown that a Dirichlet process transformed by a
C^{1}
map is still a Dirichlet process.
Lowther (2010)
, working with a different definition of Dirichlet processes (assuming strong quadratic variations) than previous authors showed that this space of processes was closed under a wide family of locally Lipschitz continuous functions and provided an Itô formula for such transforms.

The results of
Lowther (2010)
and
Coquet, Mémin and Słomiński (2003)
are quite different, both in the sense that they use different notions of quadratic variation and the fact that the decompositions are quite different. The decomposition in
Coquet, Mémin and Słomiński (2003)
gives a more detailed description of the non-continuous part. In the study of our problem, decompositions of the kind proved in
Coquet, Mémin and Słomiński (2003)
will be indispensable. We shall develop an analogous Itô decomposition to this one, but in the setting of strong quadratic variation and with relaxed assumptions on the transform,
f
. It turns out however that one of the key components will be the results of
Lowther (2010)
for proving this formula. In the setting of weak quadratic variation we show stability under the assumption of pointwise convergence
X^{n}\to X
and
f\in C^{1}
. In the setting of strong quadratic variation we prove stability with a relaxed assumption on
f
(being a primitive function of a cadlag function), pointwise convergence of
X^{n}\to X
, but some additional assumptions on
X
(
X_{s}
having a non-atomic distribution for each
s\in[0,t]
, and the continuous part and discontinuous part of
X
being independent as stochastic processes).

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose

are processes with quadratic variations along the same refining sequence and assume that

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{k=1}^{m+1}X^{k}\right]_{t}=\left[\sum_{k=1}^{m}X^{k}\right]_{t}+2\left[\sum_{k=1}^{m}X^{k},X^{m+1}\right]_{t}+\left[X^{m+1}\right]_{t}\leq\left[\sum_{k=1}^{m}X^{k}\right]_{t}+

3
Stability in quadratic variation

If a sequence of cadlag processes
\{X^{n}\}_{n}
converges in some sense to another cadlag process
X
while we also have convergence of
\left[f(X^{n})-f(X)\right]\to 0
, for some function
f
, we informally refer to this (as we have not specified in what sense this converges) as stability in quadratic variation. While we feel that stability in quadratic variation, in the sense we will explore, is interesting in its own right, it is also intimately tied to stability of integrators. One might be concerned with integrals of the form
\int Ydf(X^{n})
, for some continuous function
f
and wish to study the convergence of this integral as
X^{n}\to X
in some sense. Due to the integration by parts formula

\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s-}df(X_{s})=f(X_{t})Y_{t}-f(X_{0})Y_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}f(X_{s-})dY_{s}-[f(X),Y]_{t}

and by the Kunita-Watanabe inequality we have that if
[f(X)-f(X^{n})]_{t}\to 0
then
\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s-}df(X^{n}_{s})
converges to
\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s-}df(X_{s})
if and only if
\int_{0}^{t}f(X^{n}_{s-})dY_{s}\to\int_{0}^{t}f(X_{s-})dY_{s}
.

Let us first consider a quite useful but elementary result regarding stability in quadratic variation.
The first very elementary result can be proven directly from first principles.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
\{X^{n}\}_{n}
and
X
have quadratic variations in either the weak (or the strong sense),
f\in C^{1}
,
(X^{n}-X)^{*}_{t}\to 0
in probability and
[X^{n}-X]_{t}\to 0
in probability. Then
[f(X^{n})-f(X)]_{t}\to 0
or in probability, with the quadratic variation being in the weak (strong) sense.

Proof.

We the proof for weak sense quadratic variations, the strong sense case is analogous. By localization, we may without loss of generality assume that
X^{*}_{t}\leq R
for some
R\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
. Let
\delta>0
be so small that if
x,y\in[-(R+\delta),R+\delta]
and
|x-y|<\delta
then
|f(x)-f(y)|<\epsilon
. Let
N
be so large that
n\geq N
implies that
[X^{n}-X]_{t}\leq\epsilon
and
(X^{n}-X)^{*}_{t}<\delta
. For any partition of
[0,t]
such that
\max_{i}|t_{i}-t_{i-1}|<\delta
and
n\geq N
we have,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left|f(X_{t_{i}})-f(X^{n}_{t_{i}})-\left(f(X_{t_{i-1}})-f(X^{n}_{t_{i-1}})\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\left(f^{\prime}\left(X_{t_{i}}+\theta\left(X_{t_{i}}-X_{t_{i-1}}\right)\right)\left(X_{t_{i}}-X_{t_{i-1}}\right)-f^{\prime}\left(X^{n}_{t_{i}}+\theta\left(X^{n}_{t_{i}}-X^{n}_{t_{i-1}}\right)\right)\left(X^{n}_{t_{i}}-X^{n}_{t_{i-1}}\right)\right)d\theta\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i}\left|\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}\left(X_{t_{i}}+\theta\left(X_{t_{i}}-X_{t_{i-1}}\right)\right)d\theta\left(X_{t_{i}}-X^{n}_{t_{i}}-(X_{t_{i-1}}-X^{n}_{t_{i-1}})\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\left(f^{\prime}\left(X_{t_{i}}+\theta\left(X_{t_{i}}-X_{t_{i-1}}\right)\right)-f^{\prime}\left(X^{n}_{t_{i}}+\theta\left(X^{n}_{t_{i}}-X^{n}_{t_{i-1}}\right)\right)\right)d\theta\left(X^{n}_{t_{i}}-X^{n}_{t_{i-1}}\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i}M^{2}\left|X_{t_{i}}-X^{n}_{t_{i}}-(X_{t_{i-1}}-X^{n}_{t_{i-1}})\right|^{2}+2\sum_{i}\epsilon^{2}\left|X^{n}_{t_{i}}-X^{n}_{t_{i-1}}\right|^{2}.

If we now let the mesh of the partition tend to zero in the left-most and right-most side above have have the following limits in probability

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left([f(X)-f(X^{n})]_{t}\geq a\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(2M^{2}\left(\left[X-X^{n}\right]_{t}+\epsilon\right)+2\epsilon^{2}[X^{n}]_{t}\geq a\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(2M^{2}\epsilon+4\epsilon^{2}[X]_{t}+[X-X^{n}]_{t}\left(2M^{2}+4\epsilon^{2}\right)\geq a\right),

for any
a>0
. Letting
\epsilon\to 0
we have
[f(X)-f(X^{n})]_{t}\leq 2M^{2}[X-X^{n}]_{t}
which converges to zero as
n\to\infty
.
∎

Due to the fact that the above argument makes explicit use of refining sequences (which converge in probability) we are tied to convergence in probability. In section
5
we aim to find situations when we may improve to almost sure convergence. We shall also relax the conditions
(X^{n}-X)^{*}_{t}\to 0
and/or
f\in C^{1}
. It turns out that in many (but a bit less general in terms of what processes it applies to) situations it suffices that
X^{n}_{s}\to X_{s}
for
s\leq t
.

The second stability result, while more elementary than our main results has a more elaborate proof, but it is one that is more or less just a simplified version of those. This result relaxes the condition of uniform convergence of
X^{n}\to X
on compacts to point-wise convergence while requiring a stronger condition on
f
,
f\in C^{2}
. We are also able to improve the convergence to almost sure convergence since we are not relying on arguments making explicit use of the refining sequences. Key to this result is the following decomposition due to
Föllmer (1981)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
X
is a process in with quadratic variation in the weak sense,

is a

is a refining sequence then

\displaystyle f(X_{t})=f(X_{0})+\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\Delta f(X_{s})-\Delta X_{s}f^{\prime}(X_{s_{-}})\right)+\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime}(X_{s_{-}})dX_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime\prime}(X_{s_{-}})d[X]^{c}_{s},

(3.3)

where
\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime}(X_{s_{-}})dX_{s}
is the point-wise limit of the series
\sum_{t_{i}\in D_{k}}f^{\prime}(X_{t_{i-1}})(X_{t_{i}}-X_{t_{i-1}})

Proposition 3.3

Suppose

4
Itô Decompositions and lemmas

In this section we gather the necessary tools for our main results. These consists of our Itô decomposition and some lemmas regarding independence of stochastic processes.
The following assumption was originally used by
Lowther (2010)
and since we will use one of his results we will need it.

Assumption 4.1
.

Suppose that
f
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function and
X
is a dirichlet process such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{s:X_{s}\not\in\mathsf{diff}(f)\}}d[X]^{c}_{s}=0\hskip 5.69054pta.s.,

(4.4)

where
\mathsf{diff}(f)
is the set of points where
f
is differentiable

Throughout the remainder of this paper, whenever we write

we will be referring to the right-hand derivative of

, if

is in-fact differentiable at

then this becomes, as we all know, the usual derivative. The following Lemma is an application of (a slight modification of) Theorem 2.1 in
Lowther (2010)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
f
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function that has right-hand derivatives everywhere and
X
is a Dirichlet process such that they satisfy Assumption
4.1
then

[f(X)]_{s}=\int_{0}^{s}f^{\prime}(X_{u-})^{2}d[X]^{c}_{s}+\sum_{u\leq s}\Delta f(X_{u})^{2}.

Proof.

We first note that Theorem 2.1. in
Lowther (2010)
works just as well if we assume that
f
has pointwise right-hand derivatives everywhere and use this as a definition of
D_{x}
in place of the supremum-derivative (in Lemma 2.7. we merely let
\xi
be be the corresponding limit for the right-derivative instead of a supremum). According to Theorem 2.1. in
Lowther (2010)
we have that if
X
is sum of a semimartingale and a process admitting a quadratic variation, with zero continuous quadratic variation then

\displaystyle f(X_{s})=\int_{0}^{s}f^{\prime}(X_{u-})dZ_{u}+V_{s},

(4.5)

where
V_{s}
is a cadlag process that has a quadratic variation and zero continuous quadratic variation. Since
X=Z+C
and
[C]^{c}=[C]=0
,
X
does indeed satisfy this assumption. Note that since
f(X)
has a quadratic variation, we must have that
[f(X)]_{s}=[f(X)]^{c}_{s}+\sum_{u\leq s}\Delta f(X_{u})^{2}
. On the other hand, if we compute the quadratic variation of
4.5
we get

\displaystyle[f(X)]_{s}=\int_{0}^{s}f^{\prime}(X_{u-})^{2}d[Z]^{c}_{s}+\sum_{u\leq s}f^{\prime}(X_{u-})^{2}(\Delta Z_{u})^{2}+2\sum_{u\leq s}f^{\prime}(X_{u-})^{2}\Delta Z_{u}\Delta V_{u}+\sum_{u\leq s}\Delta V_{u}^{2}

(4.6)

and therefore we may conclude that
[f(X)]^{c}_{s}=\int_{0}^{s}f^{\prime}(X_{u-})^{2}d[Z]^{c}_{s}=\int_{0}^{s}f^{\prime}(X_{u-})^{2}d[X]^{c}_{s}
from which the result follows.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If
C
is a continuous process with zero quadratic variation in the strong sense,
Z
is a semimartingale and
g
is a continuous function then
\int_{0}^{.}Z_{-}g(C)dC
is a well-defined ucp limit of its approximating Riemann-Stieltjes sums and it has zero quadratic variation in the strong sense.

Proof.

First we establish the fact that if
C^{\prime}
is a process with strong quadratic variation and
Z^{\prime}
is a semimartingale then the integral
\int Z^{\prime}_{-}dC^{\prime}
exists as the ucp limit of

\sum_{\tau_{i}\in P^{n}}Z^{\prime}_{\tau_{i-1}}\left(C^{\prime}_{\tau_{i}}-C^{\prime}_{\tau_{i-1}}\right),

for any sequence of partitions
\{P_{n}\}_{n}
on
[0,t]
with
\|P_{n}\|\to 0
. Moreover
\int_{0}^{.}Z_{-}dC^{\prime}
has zero quadratic variation in the strong sense. By the usual re-arrangement of the Riemann sums for integration by parts formula, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau_{i}\in P^{n}}Z^{\prime}_{\tau_{i-1}}\left(C^{\prime}_{\tau_{i}}-C^{\prime}_{\tau_{i-1}}\right)=Z^{\prime}C^{\prime}-\sum_{\tau_{i}\in P^{n}}\left(Z^{\prime}_{\tau_{i}}-Z^{\prime}_{\tau_{i-1}}\right)\left(C^{\prime}_{\tau_{i}}-C^{\prime}_{\tau_{i-1}}\right)-\sum_{\tau_{i}\in P^{n}}C^{\prime}_{\tau_{i-1}}\left(Z^{\prime}_{\tau_{i}}-Z^{\prime}_{\tau_{i-1}}\right)

which converges in ucp to
Z^{\prime}C^{\prime}-[Z^{\prime},C^{\prime}]-\int_{0}^{.}C^{\prime}_{-}dZ^{\prime}
, so this left-hand side is well-defined and a.s. finite and we therefore have

\int_{0}^{s}Z^{\prime}_{u-}dC^{\prime}_{u}=Z^{\prime}C^{\prime}-[Z^{\prime},C^{\prime}]-\int_{0}^{s}C^{\prime}_{u}dZ^{\prime}_{u}.

We will now show that
\int Z^{\prime}_{-}dC^{\prime}
has zero quadratic variation.
By pre-local stopping we may assume
Z^{\prime}\leq L
on
[0,t]
. Given any sequence of partitions of
[0,t]
,
\{P^{n}\}_{n}
with

, such that
\|P^{n}\|\to 0
as

, we have

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
X
is a process in with quadratic variation in the strong sense and
f
is a

\displaystyle f(X_{t})=f(X_{0})+\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\Delta f(X_{s})-\Delta X_{s}f^{\prime}(X_{s_{-}})\right)+\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime}(X_{s_{-}})dX_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime\prime}(X_{s_{-}})d[X]^{c}_{s},

where
\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime}(X_{s_{-}})dX_{s}
is a process with quadratic variation in the strong sense and the ucp limit of
\sum_{\tau_{i}\in P^{n}}f^{\prime}(X_{\tau_{i-1}})(X_{\tau_{i}}-X_{\tau_{i-1}})
where
P^{n}
is any sequence of partitions of
[0,t]
such that
\|P^{n}\|\to 0
.

Proof.

We know that since
f\in C^{2}
,
f(X)
has a quadratic variation in the strong sense. Re-arranging, we have that the formula stated in the theorem is equivalent to

\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime}(X_{-})dX=f(X_{t})-f(X_{0})-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime\prime}(X_{s-})d[X]^{c}_{s}-\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\Delta f(X_{s})-\Delta X_{s}f^{\prime}(X_{s_{-}})\right).

All terms on the right-hand side have quadratic variation in the strong sense (the second one is zero) and therefore so will the left-hand side if we can establish this identity. This follows from the standard arguments in the proof of the Itô-formula since we do not establish any semimartingale property and we work in the ucp setting.
∎

\displaystyle[f(X^{n})-f(X)]^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0^{+}}^{t}f^{\prime}(X^{n}_{-})^{2}d\left[X-X^{n}\right]_{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\int_{0^{+}}^{t}\left(f^{\prime}(X^{n}_{s_{-}})-f^{\prime}(X_{s_{-}})\right)^{2}d[X]_{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\left[\sum_{s\leq t}\Delta\left(f(X_{s})-f(X^{n}_{s})\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left[\sum_{s\leq t}\Delta X_{s}\left(f^{\prime}(X_{s})-f^{\prime}(X^{n}_{s})\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left[\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\Delta X_{s}-\Delta X^{n}_{s}\right)f^{\prime}(X^{n}_{s-})\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(5.33)

The first two terms can be dealt with just as in the proof of
5.1
. For the third term, letting
L(r)
be as in the proof of Theorem
5.1
,

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{s\leq t}\Delta\left(f(X_{s})-f(X^{n}_{s})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\Delta\left(f(X_{s})-f(X^{n}_{s})\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\Delta f(X_{s})\right)^{2}1_{|\Delta X_{s}|\leq L(r)}+2\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\Delta f(X^{n}_{s})\right)^{2}1_{|\Delta X_{s}|\leq L(r)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\Delta\left(f(X_{s})-f(X^{n}_{s})\right)\right)^{2}1_{|\Delta X_{s}|>L(r)}.

Since

\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\Delta f(X^{n}_{s})\right)^{2}1_{|\Delta X_{s}|\leq L(r)}=\sum_{s\leq t}(\Delta X^{n}_{s})^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}\left(X^{n}_{s}+\theta\Delta X^{n}_{s}\right)d\theta\right)^{2}1_{|\Delta X_{s}|\leq L(r)},

by a localization argument and the fact that
\{(X^{n})^{*}_{t}\}_{n}
is bounded in probability one may readily deduce that
\left(\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}\left(X^{n}_{s}+\theta\Delta X^{n}_{s}\right)d\theta\right)^{2}\leq M
, for some
M\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
. Employing the same trick as in (
5.27
) now shows that we may make the term
\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\Delta f(X^{n}_{s})\right)^{2}1_{|\Delta X_{s}|\leq L(r)}
uniformly small over all
n
. Similarly for some
M^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
, we may bound

\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\Delta f(X^{n}_{s})\right)^{2}1_{|\Delta X_{s}|\leq L(r)}\leq M^{\prime}\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\Delta X_{s}\right)^{2}1_{|\Delta X_{s}|\leq L(r)},

which goes to zero as
r\to 0
. The sum
\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\Delta\left(f(X_{s})-f(X^{n}_{s})\right)\right)^{2}1_{|\Delta X_{s}|>L(r)}
only contains a finite of terms and by the assumption on point wise convergence, this sum vanishes. For the fourth term of (
5.33
),

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{s\leq t}\Delta X_{s}\left(f^{\prime}(X_{s})-f^{\prime}(X^{n}_{s})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\leq t}(\Delta X_{s})^{2}\left(f^{\prime}(X_{s})-f^{\prime}(X^{n}_{s})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\leq t}(\Delta X_{s})^{2}1_{|\Delta X_{s}|<r}2(M^{\prime}+M)+\sum_{s\leq t}(\Delta X_{s})^{2}\left(f^{\prime}(X_{s})-f^{\prime}(X^{n}_{s})\right)^{2}1_{|\Delta X_{s}|<r}.

For every
r>0
the second sum above contains a finite number of terms that will vanish as
n\to\infty
. Letting
r\to 0
makes the first term vanish. For the fifth and final term of (
5.33
), we have

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\Delta X_{s}-\Delta X^{n}_{s}\right)f^{\prime}(X^{n}_{s-})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\leq t}\left(\Delta\left(X^{n}-X\right)\right)^{2}f^{\prime}(X^{n}_{s-})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\leq t}M\left(\Delta\left(X^{n}-X\right)\right)^{2}\leq M[X^{n}-X]_{t},

which converges to zero.
∎

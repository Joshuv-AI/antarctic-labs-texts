On the Hardness of Sampling from Mixture Distributions via Langevin Dynamics
Xiwei Cheng, Kexin Fu, Farzan Farnia

Abstract
The Langevin Dynamics (LD), which aims to sample from a probability distribution using its score function, has been widely used for analyzing and developing score-based generative modeling algorithms. While the convergence behavior of LD in sampling from a uni-modal distribution has been extensively studied in the literature, the analysis of LD under a mixture distribution with distinct modes remains underexplored in the literature. In this work, we analyze LD in sampling from a mixture distribution and theoretically study its convergence properties. Our theoretical results indicate that for general mixture distributions of sub-Gaussian components, LD could fail in finding all the components within a sub-exponential number of steps in the data dimension. Following our result on the complexity of LD in sampling from high-dimensional variables, we propose Chained Langevin Dynamics (Chained-LD) , which divides the data vector into patches of smaller sizes and generates every patch sequentially conditioned on the previous patches. Our theoretical analysis of Chained-LD indicates its faster convergence speed to the components of a mixture distribution. We present the results of several numerical experiments on synthetic and real image datasets, validating our theoretical results on the iteration complexities of sample generation from mixture distributions using the vanilla and chained LD algorithms.

Introduction

Langevin dynamics (LD) is a well-established methodology with a wide range of applications to various areas, including Bayesian learning
(

)
, non-convex optimization
(
2
,
3
)
, and molecular-dynamics simulations
(

P({\mathbf{x}})
, defined as the gradient of the PDF logarithm
\nabla\log P({\mathbf{x}})
, to perform the following iterative process whose output follows the probability model characterized by
P({\mathbf{x}})

{\mathbf{x}}_{t}={\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t},

where
\delta_{t}
is the step size and
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{d},{\bm{I}}_{d})
is Gaussian noise. Recently, the LD sampling methodology has found central applications in generative modeling tasks, such as image generation
(
6
,
7
)
, adversarial training
(
8
,
9
)
, and imitation learning
(
10
,
11
)
, which have inspired many theoretical and empirical studies of the LD methodology.

Specifically, several references
(
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
)
have studied the convergence properties of the LD sampling process to characterize the iteration complexity of LD sampling from the target PDF
P(\mathbf{x})
. The existing theoretical results mostly focus on demonstrating the satisfactory and speedy convergence of LD assuming a unimodal target distribution consisting of only one distribution component. However, the work of Song and Ermon
(
6
)
has highlighted examples of mixture distributions with multiple separated modes where the vanilla LD sampling struggles in capturing the mode frequencies correctly. This reference
(
6
)
proposes a variant of LD, called
annealed Langevin dynamics (Annealed-LD)
, to address the challenges of LD in sampling from a mixture distribution.

While the Annealed-LD sampling approach provides a satisfactory solution to cover the modes of a mixture model, the theoretical analysis of LD sampling applied to mixture distributions has remained underexplored in the literature. However, a reliable application of LD methods to sample from real-world distributions requires a more solid understanding of their convergence behavior for a target multi-modal distribution, which is commonly present in real-world data due to the different background features of real-world objects and phenomena.

In our work, we aim to study the convergence properties of the LD framework in sampling from a mixture distribution. As displayed in Figure
1
, we observe that the convergence of LD to capture all three underlying modes becomes more challenging when the dimension
d
of the sampling space is growing. Our main theoretical result provides a family of mixture distributions, where the LD framework is unlikely to find all the mixture components within a sub-exponential number of iterations in the data dimension
d
.

Specifically, we consider mixture distributions with a low-probability yet high-variance in-between mode, which we refer to as the zeroth mode
P^{(0)}
(illustrated in Figure
2
). Despite a significantly smaller probability mass compared to the other low-variance modes, the in-between mode
P^{(0)}
surrounds the other low-variance modes and fills the space between them. As a result, Mode 0 dominates the score function in the low-density region, disrupting and slowing down the convergence of the noisy local search in LD to the low-variance modes with greater probability masses.

To mitigate the exponential iteration complexity, we introduce a complementary method,
Chained Langevin Dynamics (Chained-LD)
, with convergence guarantees in a polynomial number of iterations. Following our theoretical results on the role of high dimensionality in the convergence of LD, we propose applying dimensionality reduction through the Chain Rule: for
{\mathbf{x}}=[x^{1},x^{2},\cdots,x^{d}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

P({\mathbf{x}})=P(x^{1})P(x^{2}|x^{1})\cdots P(x^{d}|x^{1},\cdots,x^{d-1}).

Chained-LD sequentially samples every element
{\mathbf{x}}^{i}
for all
i\in[d]
from the conditional distribution given previous elements, i.e.,
P(x^{i}\mid x^{1},\cdots x^{i-1})
. Therefore, Chained-LD reduces the effective dimensionality of the sampled variable, which can accelerate the search for missing modes in sampling from a mixture distribution. Furthermore, for mixture distributions
P
such that
-\log P(x^{i}|x^{1},\cdots,x^{i-1})
is
L_{Q}

m_{Q}
-strongly convex for
|x^{i}|>R_{Q}

\varepsilon
total variation distance in
{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{L_{Q}^{2}d^{3}}{m_{Q}^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}\exp(32L_{Q}R_{Q}^{2})\log\frac{d^{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)={\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{d^{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log\frac{d^{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)
iterations.

Finally, we present the results of several numerical experiments to validate our theoretical findings. In synthetic experiments, we consider high-dimensional Gaussian mixture models, where LD could not find all components within a million steps, whereas Chained-LD could capture all components with correct frequencies in
{\mathcal{O}}(10^{4})
steps. Also, we test the application of Chained-LD as a sampling algorithm in score-based generative modeling for an underlying mixture distribution. In the case of a mixture of original images from the MNIST/Fashion-MNIST dataset (black background and white digits/objects) and flipped images (white background and black digits/objects), our numerical results suggest that Chained-ALD could find both the modes in
{\mathcal{O}}(10^{5})
iterations. We summarize the contributions of this work as follows:

•

Analyzing the iteration complexity of Langevin dynamics under high-dimensional mixture distributions,

•

Proposing Chained Langevin Dynamics (Chained-LD) with sequential sampling to improve LD’s convergence in sampling from mixture distributions,

•

Providing a theoretical analysis of the convergence of Chained-LD,

•

Presenting numerical results validating our theoretical findings on the convergence of LD and Chained-LD.

Related Works

Convergence Guarantees of Langevin Dynamics:

The convergence guarantees of Langevin diffusion, a continuous version of Langevin dynamics, are classical results that have been extensively studied in the literature
(
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
)

,
12

,
15

(
20

studied Langevin dynamics under mixtures of Gaussians with equal variance and showed that the iteration complexity of Langevin dynamics is
\text{poly}(d,1/\varepsilon)
. For more general distributions,
(
21
)
and
(
22
)
analyzed target distributions
p
that are strongly log-concave outside of a region of radius
R
, proving that the iteration complexity of Langevin dynamics is
\exp(cR^{2})\text{poly}(d,1/\varepsilon)
, which can become exponential in
d
when the radius
R
scales as
{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{d})
.

Hardness of Langevin Dynamics in Mixture Distributions:

For continuous Langevin diffusion,
(
23
,
24
,
25
)
studied the mean hitting time and provided a lower bound on the transition time between two modes, e.g., two local maxima. In the context of Langevin dynamics,
(
20
)
proved the existence of a mixture of two Gaussian distributions with covariance matrices differing by a constant factor, wherein Langevin dynamics cannot find both modes in polynomial time.
(
6
)
studied the slow mixing and incorrect relative weight recovery of Langevin dynamics in bi-modal distributions separated by low-density regions. Additionally,
(
26
)
studied the role of noise levels in annealed Langevin dynamics, showing their effect on sample diversity in multi-modal distributions.

Connections between Langevin Dynamics and Score-based Generative Modeling:

Langevin dynamics and its annealed variant serve as the backbone of score-based generative modeling, which aims to learn the underlying probability distribution of training data and efficiently generate new data from the learned distribution.
(
6
)
proposed learning Noise Conditional Score Networks (NCSN) via score matching to estimate the perturbed score function of the underlying distribution from training data and applied annealed Langevin dynamics with NCSN as the sampling method.
(

unified anneal Langevin dynamics and Denoising diffusion probabilistic modeling (DDPM)
(
27

via a stochastic differential equation (SDE) and proposed utilizing score-based Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approaches, e.g., Langevin dynamics, to sample from the SDE.

3.1
Langevin Dynamics

Langevin dynamics aims to produce samples such that their distribution is close to the underlying true distribution
P
. For a continuously differentiable probability density
P({\mathbf{x}})
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, its score function is defined as the gradient of the log probability density function (PDF)
\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}})
. Langevin diffusion is a stochastic process defined by the stochastic differential equation (SDE)

{\,\mathrm{d}}{\mathbf{x}}_{t}=\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{t}){\,\mathrm{d}}t+\sqrt{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}{\mathbf{w}}_{t},

where
{\mathbf{w}}_{t}
is the Wiener process on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Langevin dynamics, a discretization of the SDE for
T
iterations, is applied to sample from the target distribution. Each iteration of Langevin dynamics is defined as

{\mathbf{x}}_{t}={\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t},

(1)

where
\delta_{t}
is the step size and
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{d},{\bm{I}}_{d})
is Gaussian noise. It has been widely recognized that the continuous Langevin diffusion could take an exponential time to mix without additional assumptions on the probability density
(
23
,
24
,
25
)
. To combat the slow mixing,
(
6
)
proposed annealed Langevin dynamics by perturbing the probability density with Gaussian noise of variance
{\sigma}^{2}
, i.e.,

P_{\sigma}({\mathbf{x}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int P({\mathbf{z}}){\mathcal{N}}({\mathbf{x}}\mid{\mathbf{z}},{\sigma}^{2}{\bm{I}}_{d}){\,\mathrm{d}}{\mathbf{z}},

(2)

and applying Langevin dynamics on the perturbed data distribution
P_{{\sigma}_{t}}({\mathbf{x}})
with gradually decreasing noise levels
{\sigma}_{1}\geq{\sigma}_{2}\geq\cdots\geq{\sigma}_{T}
, i.e.,

{\mathbf{x}}_{t}={\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P_{{\sigma}_{t}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t},

(3)

where
\delta_{t}
is the step size and
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{d},{\bm{I}}_{d})
is Gaussian noise. When the noise level
{\sigma}
is vanishingly small, the perturbed distribution is close to the true distribution, i.e.,
\lim_{{\sigma}\to 0}P_{\sigma}({\mathbf{x}})\approx P({\mathbf{x}})
.

Remark 1
.

In our theoretical analysis, we assume the sampler has access to the underlying score function
\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P_{{\sigma}}({\mathbf{x}})
. For generative modeling tasks in real-world datasets, since we do not have direct access to the (perturbed) score function,
(
6
)
proposed the Noise Conditional Score Network (NCSN)
{\mathbf{s}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}},{\sigma})
to jointly estimate the scores of all perturbed data distributions, i.e.,
\forall{\sigma}\in\left\{{{\sigma}_{t}}\right\}_{t\in[T]},\;{\mathbf{s}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}},{\sigma})\approx\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P_{{\sigma}}({\mathbf{x}})
.

3.2
Multi-Modal Distributions

In this work, we focus on the analysis of Langevin dynamics in multi-modal distributions. We highlight that our work studies Langevin dynamics under multi-modal distributions in a slightly different setting from the standard theory literature on sampling. The existing theoretical literature commonly considers a mixture of well-separated modes with bounded variance. On the other hand, in our analysis, we consider a low-density high-variance mode (referred to as Mode 0 or
P^{(0)}
) surrounding the other modes and filling the low density region between the modes. Specifically, as illustrated in Figure
2
.(a), we formulate a
symmetric 3-Gaussian model
as a hard example for Langevin dynamics, defined as following

Definition 1
.

For any given frequency
w\in(0,1)
and variance
\nu^{2}>1
of the in-between mode
P^{(0)}
, and any mean vector
{\bm{\mu}}
of the low-variance mode
P^{(1)}
, a symmetric 3-Gaussian model is defined as

P_{w,\nu,{\bm{\mu}}}=w{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0},\nu^{2}{\bm{I}})+\frac{1-w}{2}{\mathcal{N}}({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{I}})+\frac{1-w}{2}{\mathcal{N}}(-{\bm{\mu}},{\bm{I}}).

More generally, we use
P=w_{0}P^{(0)}+\sum_{i\in[k]}w_{i}P^{(i)}
to represent a mixture of
k+1
modes, where
P^{(0)}
is the in-between mode with high variance as illustrated in Figure
2
.(b). Here each mode
P^{(i)}
is a probability density with frequency
w_{i}
such that
w_{i}>0
for all
i\in[k]
and
w_{0}+\sum_{i\in[k]}w_{i}=1
.

4
Theoretical Analysis of the Hardness of Langevin Dynamics

In this section, we theoretically investigate the iteration complexity of Langevin dynamics. We first introduce a notation
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|_{\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}}
to measure the distance between a sample
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
to the linear span of mean vectors
\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}
of the mixture components in a multi-modal distribution.

Definition 2
.

For a sample
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and a set of vectors
{\bm{\mu}}_{1},\cdots,{\bm{\mu}}_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we define
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|_{\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}}
as the distance from
{\mathbf{x}}
to the span of
\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}
, i.e., the minimum distance from
{\mathbf{x}}
to any linear combination of
\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}
:

\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|_{\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\min_{\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k}}\big\|{{\mathbf{x}}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\big\|.

(4)

We aim to show that in a mixture distribution
P
with a high-variance mode
P^{(0)}
, the sampled vector
{\mathbf{x}}
is likely to be far from the low-variance modes
P^{(1)},\cdots,P^{(k)}
in terms of the
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|_{\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}}
metric.

4.1
Langevin Dynamics in Symmetric 3-Gaussian Model

We begin our theoretical analysis with a simple case: a symmetric 3-Gaussian model consisting of two symmetric Gaussian modes
P^{(1)}={\mathcal{N}}({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{I}}_{d})
and
P^{(2)}={\mathcal{N}}(-{\bm{\mu}},{\bm{I}}_{d})
, and an in-between mode
P^{(0)}={\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{d},\nu^{2}{\bm{I}}_{d})
with high variance
\nu^{2}\geq 3
, as illustrated in Figure

.(a). In the following Theorem
1

{\mathbf{x}}_{T}
fails to find the symmetric modes

within a sub-exponential number of iterations. The proof of Theorem
1
is deferred to Appendix
A.1

Theorem 1
.

Consider a distribution
P_{w,\nu,{\bm{\mu}}}=w{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{d},\nu^{2}{\bm{I}}_{d})+\frac{1-w}{2}{\mathcal{N}}({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{I}}_{d})+\frac{1-w}{2}{\mathcal{N}}(-{\bm{\mu}},{\bm{I}}_{d})
by Definition
1
in dimension
d\geq 250
, such that
w\geq 0.01
,
\nu^{2}\geq 3
, and
\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}}\right\|^{2}\leq 0.2d
. We initialize the sample
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
such that
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{0}}\right\|_{{\bm{\mu}}}^{2}\geq\frac{3\nu^{2}+1}{4}d
and apply Langevin dynamics for
T
iterations, then we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{T}}\right\|_{{\bm{\mu}}}^{2}\geq\frac{\nu^{2}+1}{2}d\right)\geq 1-T\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{d}{300}\right).

For example, for a symmetric 3-Gaussian model
P_{0.01,\sqrt{3},0.2\cdot\bm{1}_{d}}
, Theorem
1
indicates that the sampled vector
{\mathbf{x}}_{T}
within
T\leq\exp(d/300)
iterations cannot be
\sqrt{2d}
close to the center of any low-variance modes with high probability. To interpret Theorem
1
, we first note that in a high-dimensional space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the probability mass of a Gaussian distribution
{\mathcal{N}}({\bm{\mu}},{\bm{I}}_{d})
concentrates inside a ball of radius
\sqrt{d}
centered at
{\bm{\mu}}
, i.e.,
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}}\right\|^{2}\leq d
. On the other hand, the high probability bound
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{T}}\right\|_{{\bm{\mu}}}^{2}\geq\frac{\nu^{2}+1}{2}d
in Theorem
1
implies that
{\mathbf{x}}_{T}
is far from the center of both symmetric Gaussian modes, i.e.,
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{T}-{\bm{\mu}}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{\nu^{2}+1}{2}d\geq 2d
. This observation allows us to translate the bound on
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{T}}\right\|_{{\bm{\mu}}}
into a lower bound in other standard metrics such as total variation distance, as shown in the following Corollary
1
.

Corollary 1
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
1
, the distribution
\widehat{P}_{T}
of the sampled vector
{\mathbf{x}}_{T}
by Langevin dynamics satisfies

\text{TV}(\widehat{P}_{T},P)\geq 0.99-w-\frac{T}{\exp(-d/300)}.

4.2
Langevin Dynamics in Gaussian Mixture Models

We further extend Theorem
1
to a general Gaussian mixture setting. As illustrated in Figure
2
.(b), we consider a Gaussian mixture with an in-between mode
P^{(0)}
with high variance. To intuitively understand our theoretical results, we first note that the probability density

of a Gaussian distribution
{\mathcal{N}}({\bm{\mu}},\nu^{2}{\bm{I}}_{d})
decays exponentially in terms of
\frac{\left\|{{\mathbf{z}}-{\bm{\mu}}}\right\|^{2}}{\nu^{2}}

has a higher variance, the probability density of

and the gradient information from

will be masked by

Theorem 2
.

Consider a data distribution
P=w_{0}{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{d},{\nu}_{0}^{2}{\bm{I}}_{d})+\sum_{i\in[k]}w_{i}{\mathcal{N}}({\bm{\mu}}_{i},{\nu}_{i}^{2}{\bm{I}}_{d})
in dimension
d\geq 250
. For all low-variance modes
P^{(1)},\cdots,P^{(k)}
, we assume
\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|\leq 0.2d
and denote
{\nu}_{\max}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max_{i\in[k]}{\nu}_{i}
. For in-between mode
P^{(0)}
, assume
w_{0}\geq 0.01
and
{\nu}_{0}^{2}\geq 3{\nu}_{\max}^{2}
. We initialize the sample
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
such that
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{0}}\right\|_{\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}}^{2}\geq\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{4}d
and apply Langevin dynamics for
T
iterations, then we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{T}}\right\|_{\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}}\geq\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{2}d\right)\geq 1-T\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{d}{300}\right).

Chained Langevin Dynamics

To reduce the exponential complexity of Langevin dynamics, we propose Chained Langevin Dynamics (Chained-LD) in Algorithm
1
. While Langevin dynamics apply gradient updates to all coordinates of the variable at every step, we decompose the variable into patches of constant size and sample each patch sequentially to alleviate the exponential dependency on the dimensionality. More precisely, we divide a vector
{\mathbf{x}}
into
d/Q
patches
{\mathbf{x}}^{(1)},\cdots{\mathbf{x}}^{(d/Q)}
of some constant size
Q
, and apply Langevin dynamics to sample each patch
{\mathbf{x}}^{(q)}
(for
q\in[d/Q]
) from the conditional distribution
P({\mathbf{x}}^{(q)}\mid{\mathbf{x}}^{(1)},\cdots{\mathbf{x}}^{(q-1)})
. Intuitively, vanilla Langevin dynamics needs to explore the entire space (of volume exponentially large in
d
) to find the missing modes, while Chained-LD could significantly lower the volume by dimensionality reduction.

Conclusion

In this work, we theoretically and numerically studied the hardness of Langevin dynamics sampling methods under a multi-modal distribution. We characterized Gaussian and sub-Gaussian mixture models under which Langevin dynamics are unlikely to find all the components within a sub-exponential number of iterations. To reduce the exponential iteration complexity of Langevin dynamics, we proposed Chained Langevin Dynamics (Chained-LD), as a complementary solution to Annealed-LD in
(
6
)
and analyzed its convergence behavior. Further investigation on the applications of Chained-LD in generative models will be an interesting topic for future exploration. Another future direction could be to study the convergence of Chained-LD under an imperfect score estimation.

Lemma 1

. Recall that equation
6
gives

\mathbf{n}_{t}=\mathbf{n}_{t-1}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}{\mathbf{N}}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}.

We notice that the difficulty of solving
\mathbf{n}_{t}
exhibits in the dependence of
\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})
on
\mathbf{r}_{t-1}
. Since
P=\sum_{i=0}^{2}w_{i}P^{(i)}=\sum_{i=0}^{2}w_{i}{\mathcal{N}}({\bm{\mu}}_{i},{\nu}_{i}^{2}{\bm{I}}_{d})
, we can rewrite the score function as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}=-\sum_{i=0}^{2}\frac{w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\cdot({\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i})

\displaystyle=-\frac{{\mathbf{x}}}{\nu^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\left(\frac{{\mathbf{x}}}{\nu^{2}}-({\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i})\right).

(11)

Now, instead of directly working with
\mathbf{n}_{t}
, we consider a surrogate recursion
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
such that
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{0}=\mathbf{n}_{0}
and for all
t\geq 1
,

\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}=\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t-1}-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2\nu^{2}}\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t-1}+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}.

(12)

The advantage of the surrogate recursion is that
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
is independent of
\mathbf{r}
, thus we can obtain the closed-form solution to
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
. Before we proceed to bound
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
, we first show that
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
is sufficiently close to the original recursion
\mathbf{n}_{t}
in the following lemma.

Lemma 6
.

For any
t\geq 1
, given that
\delta_{j}\leq\nu^{2}
and
\frac{\nu^{2}+1}{2}d\leq\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|^{2}\leq 36\nu^{2}d
for all
j\in[t]
and
\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}}\right\|^{2}\leq 0.2d
, we have
\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}-\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|\leq\frac{t}{\exp(0.04d)}\sqrt{d}
.

Proof of Lemma
6
.

Upon comparing equation
6
and equation
12
, by equation
11
we have that for all
j\in[t]
,

\displaystyle\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j}-\mathbf{n}_{j}}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\frac{\delta_{j}}{2\nu^{2}}\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\mathbf{n}_{j-1}-\frac{\delta_{j}}{2}{\mathbf{N}}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|{\left(1-\frac{\delta_{j}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)(\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\mathbf{n}_{j-1})+\frac{\delta_{j}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}{P({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}\left(1-\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\right)\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{\delta_{j}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\delta_{j}}{2}\frac{w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}{P({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}\left(1-\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\right)\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\delta_{j}}{2}\frac{w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}{w_{0}P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}\left(1-\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\right)6\nu\sqrt{d}.

By Lemma
4
, we have
\frac{P^{(1)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}{P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}\leq\exp(-0.06d)
and
\frac{P^{(2)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}{P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}\leq\exp(-0.06d)
, hence we obtain a recursive bound

\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j}-\mathbf{n}_{j}}\right\|\leq\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|+\frac{1}{\exp(0.04d)}\sqrt{d}.

Finally, by
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{0}=\mathbf{n}_{0}
, we have

\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}-\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|=\sum_{j\in[t]}\left(\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j}-\mathbf{n}_{j}}\right\|-\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|\right)\leq\frac{t}{\exp(0.04d)}\sqrt{d}.

Hence we obtain Lemma
6
.
∎

We then proceed to analyze
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
, The following lemma gives us the closed-form solution of
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
. We slightly abuse the notations here, e.g.,
\prod_{i=c_{1}}^{c_{2}}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)=1
and
\sum_{j=c_{1}}^{c_{2}}\delta_{j}=0
for
c_{1}>c_{2}
.

Lemma 7
.

For all
t\geq 0
,
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}\sim{\mathcal{N}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)\mathbf{n}_{0},\;\sum_{j=1}^{t}\prod_{i=j+1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}\delta_{j}{\bm{I}}_{d-1}\right)
, where the mean and covariance satisfy
\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{t}\prod_{i=j+1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}\delta_{j}\geq 1
.

Proof of Lemma
7
.

We prove the two properties by induction. When
t=0
, they are trivial. Suppose they hold for
t-1
, then for the distribution of
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t-1}-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2\nu^{2}}\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t-1}+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}

\displaystyle\sim{\mathcal{N}}\left(\left(1-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)\mathbf{n}_{0},\;\left(1-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\prod_{i=j+1}^{t-1}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}\delta_{j}{\bm{I}}_{d-1}+\delta_{t}{\bm{I}}_{d-1}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{N}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)\mathbf{n}_{0},\;\sum_{j=1}^{t}\prod_{i=j+1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}\delta_{j}{\bm{I}}_{d-1}\right).

For the second property,

\displaystyle\quad\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{t}\prod_{i=j+1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}\delta_{j}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\prod_{i=j+1}^{t-1}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}\delta_{j}\right)+\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\delta_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\delta_{t}=1+\frac{\delta_{t}^{2}}{4\nu^{4}}\geq 1.

Hence we finish the proof of Lemma
7
.
∎

Armed with Lemma
7
, we are now ready to establish the lower bound on
\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}}\right\|
. For simplicity, denote
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}
and
\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{t}\prod_{i=j+1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}\delta_{j}
. By Lemma
7
we know
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\alpha\mathbf{n}_{0},\beta\nu^{2}{\bm{I}}_{d-1})
, so we can write
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}=\alpha\mathbf{n}_{0}+\sqrt{\beta}\nu\bm{\epsilon}
, where
\bm{\epsilon}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{d-1},{\bm{I}}_{d-1})
.

Lemma 8
.

Given that
\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{0}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{3\nu^{2}+1}{4}d
, we have
\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{5\nu^{2}+3}{8}d
with probability at least
1-\exp\left(-d/300\right)
.

Proof of Lemma
8
.

By
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}=\alpha\mathbf{n}_{0}+\sqrt{\beta}\nu\bm{\epsilon}
we have

\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}}\right\|^{2}=\alpha^{2}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|^{2}+\beta\nu^{2}\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}+2\alpha\sqrt{\beta}\nu\langle\mathbf{n}_{0},\bm{\epsilon}\rangle

By Lemma
1
we can bound

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{3\nu^{2}+1}{4\nu^{2}}d\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}\leq d-2\sqrt{d\cdot\left(\frac{\nu^{2}-1}{8\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}d}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}\leq(d-1)-2\sqrt{(d-1)\left(\frac{\nu^{2}-1}{8\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{d}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\left(\frac{\nu^{2}-1}{8\nu^{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{d}{2}\right)\leq\exp(-d/288).

Since
\bm{\epsilon}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{d-1},{\bm{I}}_{d-1})
, we know
\frac{\langle\mathbf{n}_{0},\bm{\epsilon}\rangle}{\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
. Therefore by Lemma
2
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{\langle\mathbf{n}_{0},\bm{\epsilon}\rangle}{\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|}\leq-\frac{\nu^{2}-1}{4\nu\sqrt{3\nu^{2}+1}}\sqrt{d}\right)\leq\frac{4\nu\sqrt{3\nu^{2}+1}}{\sqrt{2\pi}(\nu^{2}-1)\sqrt{d}}\exp\left(-\frac{(\nu^{2}-1)^{2}d}{32\nu^{2}(3\nu^{2}+1)}\right)\leq\exp(-0.004d).

Conditioned on
\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{0}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{3\nu^{2}+1}{4}d
,
\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}>\frac{3\nu^{2}+1}{4\nu^{2}}d
and
\frac{1}{\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|}\langle\mathbf{n}_{0},\bm{\epsilon}\rangle>-\frac{\nu^{2}-1}{4\nu\sqrt{3\nu^{2}+1}}\sqrt{d}
, since Lemma
7
gives
\alpha^{2}+\beta\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|^{2}+\beta\nu^{2}\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}+2\alpha\sqrt{\beta}\nu\langle\mathbf{n}_{0},\bm{\epsilon}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|^{2}+\beta\nu^{2}\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}-2\alpha\sqrt{\beta}\nu\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|\frac{\nu^{2}-1}{4\nu\sqrt{3\nu^{2}+1}}\sqrt{d}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|^{2}+\beta\nu^{2}\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}-2\alpha\sqrt{\beta}\nu\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|\cdot\frac{\nu^{2}-1}{6\nu^{2}+21}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{\nu^{2}-1}{6\nu^{2}+21}\right)\left(\alpha^{2}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|^{2}+\beta\nu^{2}\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{5\nu^{2}+3}{6\nu^{2}+21}\left(\alpha^{2}+\beta\right)\cdot\frac{3\nu^{2}+1}{4}d

\displaystyle\geq\frac{5\nu^{2}+3}{8}d.

Hence by union bound, we complete the proof of Lemma
8
.
∎

Upon having all the above lemmas, we are now ready to establish Theorem
1
by induction. Suppose the theorem holds for all
T
values of
1,\cdots,T-1
. We consider the following 3 cases:

•

1-\exp(-d/25)

\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{3\nu^{2}+1}{4}d
, thus the problem reduces to the two sub-arrays
\mathbf{n}_{0},\cdots,\mathbf{n}_{t-1}
and
\mathbf{n}_{t},\cdots,\mathbf{n}_{T}

•

Suppose
\delta_{t}\leq\nu^{2}
for all
t\in[T]
. If there exists some
t\in[T]
such that
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}>36\nu^{2}d

we know that with probability at least
1-\exp(-d/50)

\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|^{2}\geq\nu^{2}d>\frac{3\nu^{2}+1}{4}d
, thus the problem similarly reduces to the two sub-arrays
\mathbf{n}_{0},\cdots,\mathbf{n}_{t-1}
and
\mathbf{n}_{t},\cdots,\mathbf{n}_{T}

•

Suppose

and

Combining the two inequalities implies the desired bound

.

Proof of Corollary

.

By the definition of total variation distance, we have

\text{TV}(\widehat{P}_{T},P)=\sup_{A}|\widehat{P}_{T}(A)-P(A)|.

Specifically, by choosing the event

\left\{{{\mathbf{x}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|_{{\bm{\mu}}}^{2}\geq\frac{\nu^{2}+1}{2}d}\right\}
, from Theorem

we know
\widehat{P}_{T}(A)\geq 1-T\cdot\exp(-d/300)

\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|_{{\bm{\mu}}}^{2}\geq\frac{\nu^{2}+1}{2}d

\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{\nu^{2}+1}{2}d

\displaystyle\text{TV}(\widehat{P}_{T},P)

\displaystyle\geq\widehat{P}_{T}(A)-P(A)\geq 1-T\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{d}{300}\right)-P(A)

\displaystyle\geq 1-T\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{d}{300}\right)-\left(w+(1-w)\exp\left(-\frac{d}{10}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq 0.99-w-\frac{T}{\exp(-d/300)}.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

The proof of Theorem
2
follows from a similar framework to the proof of Theorem
1
. Let
r
and
n
respectively denote the rank and nullity of the vector space
\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}
, then we have
r+n=d
and
0\leq r\leq k=o(d)
. Denote
{\mathbf{R}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times r}
an orthonormal basis of the vector space
\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}
, and denote
{\mathbf{N}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times n}
an orthonormal basis of the null space of
\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}
. Now consider decomposing the sample
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}
by

\mathbf{r}_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\mathbf{R}}^{T}{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\text{, and }\mathbf{n}_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\mathbf{N}}^{T}{\mathbf{x}}_{t},

where
\mathbf{r}_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
,
\mathbf{n}_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then we have

{\mathbf{x}}_{t}={\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{r}_{t}+{\mathbf{N}}\mathbf{n}_{t}.

Similarly, we decompose the noise
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}
into

\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{r})}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\mathbf{R}}^{T}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\text{, and }\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\mathbf{N}}^{T}\bm{\epsilon}_{t},

where
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{r})}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
,
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then we have

\bm{\epsilon}_{t}={\mathbf{R}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{r})}+{\mathbf{N}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}.

Since a linear combination of a Gaussian random variable still follows Gaussian distribution, by
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{d},{\bm{I}}_{d})
,
{\mathbf{R}}^{T}{\mathbf{R}}={\bm{I}}_{r}
, and
{\mathbf{N}}^{T}{\mathbf{N}}={\bm{I}}_{n}
we obtain

\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{r})}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{r},{\bm{I}}_{r})\text{, and }\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{n},{\bm{I}}_{n}).

By the definition of Langevin dynamics in equation
1
,
\mathbf{n}_{t}
follow from the update rule:

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{n}_{t-1}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}{\mathbf{N}}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}.

(13)

By Definition
2
, since
\mathbf{n}_{t}
is the projection onto the null space of
\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}
, we have

\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}}\right\|_{\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}}=\min_{\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k}}\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|=\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|.

Then, with the assumption that the initialization satisfies
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{4}d
, the objective is to show that
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|
remains large with high probability.

To establish a lower bound on
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|
, we consider different cases of the step size
\delta_{t}
. Intuitively, when
\delta_{t}
is large enough,
\mathbf{n}_{t}
will be too noisy due to the introduction of random noise
\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}
in equation
13
. While for small
\delta_{t}
, the update of
\mathbf{n}_{t}
is bounded and thus we can iteratively analyze
\mathbf{n}_{t}
. We first handle the case of large
\delta_{t}
in the following lemma.

Lemma 9
.

If
\delta_{t}>{\nu}_{0}^{2}
, with probability at least
1-\exp(-0.04d)
, for
\mathbf{n}_{t}
satisfying equation
13
, we have
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{4}d
regardless of the previous state
{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}
.

Proof of Lemma
9
.

Denote
{\mathbf{v}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbf{n}_{t-1}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}{\mathbf{N}}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})
for simplicity. Note that
{\mathbf{v}}
is fixed for any given
{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}
. We decompose
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}
into a vector aligning with
{\mathbf{v}}
and another vector orthogonal to
{\mathbf{v}}
. Consider an orthonormal matrix
{\mathbf{M}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times(n-1)}
such that
{\mathbf{M}}^{T}{\mathbf{v}}=\bm{0}_{n-1}
and
{\mathbf{M}}^{T}{\mathbf{M}}={\bm{I}}_{n-1}
. By denoting
{\mathbf{u}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}-{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{M}}^{T}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}
we have
{\mathbf{M}}^{T}{\mathbf{u}}=\bm{0}_{n-1}
, thus we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}{\mathbf{u}}+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{M}}^{T}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}{\mathbf{u}}}\right\|^{2}+\left\|{\sqrt{\delta_{t}}{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{M}}^{T}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|{\sqrt{\delta_{t}}{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{M}}^{T}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq{\nu}_{0}^{2}\left\|{{\mathbf{M}}^{T}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}}\right\|^{2}.

Since
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{n},{\bm{I}}_{n})
and
{\mathbf{M}}^{T}{\mathbf{M}}={\bm{I}}_{n-1}
, we obtain
{\mathbf{M}}^{T}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{n-1},{\bm{I}}_{n-1})
. Therefore, by Lemma
1
we can bound

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{4}d\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|{{\mathbf{M}}^{T}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{4{\nu}_{0}^{2}}d\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|{{\mathbf{M}}^{T}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}}\right\|^{2}\leq d-2\sqrt{d\cdot\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{8{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}d}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|{{\mathbf{M}}^{T}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}}\right\|^{2}\leq(n-1)-2\sqrt{(n-1)\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{8{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{d}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{8{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{d}{2}\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{d}{24}\right),

Hence we complete the proof of Lemma
9
.
∎

We then consider the case when
\delta_{t}\leq{\nu}_{0}^{2}
. We first show that when
\left\|{\mathbf{n}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{2}d
,
P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})
is exponentially smaller than
P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})
for all
i\in[k]
in the following lemma.

Lemma 10
.

Given that
\left\|{\mathbf{n}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{2}d
and
\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}\leq 0.2d
for all
i\in[k]
, we have
\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}\leq\exp(-0.06d)
for all
i\in[k]
.

Proof of Lemma
10
.

For all
i\in[k]
, define
\rho_{i}({\mathbf{x}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}
, then

\displaystyle\rho_{i}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}=\frac{(2\pi{\nu}_{i}^{2})^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}\right)}{(2\pi{\nu}_{0}^{2})^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)^{d/2}\exp\left(\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)^{d/2}\exp\left(\left(\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\left\|{{\mathbf{N}}\mathbf{n}}\right\|^{2}+\left(\frac{\left\|{{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{r}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{\left\|{{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{r}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)^{d/2}\exp\left(\left(\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\left\|{\mathbf{n}}\right\|^{2}+\left(\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{r}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{r}-{\mathbf{R}}^{T}{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\right),

where the last step follows from the definition that
{\mathbf{R}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times r}
an orthonormal basis of the vector space
\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}
and
{\mathbf{N}}^{T}{\mathbf{N}}={\bm{I}}_{n}
. Since
{\nu}_{0}^{2}>{\nu}_{i}^{2}
, the quadratic term
\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{r}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{r}-{\mathbf{R}}^{T}{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}
is maximized at
\mathbf{r}=\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}{\mathbf{R}}^{T}{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{i}^{2}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{r}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{r}-{\mathbf{R}}^{T}{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{4}\left\|{{\mathbf{R}}^{T}{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}({\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{i}^{2})^{2}}-\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{i}^{2}}-1\right)^{2}\left\|{{\mathbf{R}}^{T}{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}=\frac{\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2({\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{i}^{2})}.

Hence, for
\left\|{\mathbf{n}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{2}d
and
\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}\leq 0.2d\leq\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{2}\left(\log\left(\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)-\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}+\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)d
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{i}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)^{d/2}\exp\left(\left(\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\left\|{\mathbf{n}}\right\|^{2}+\left(\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{r}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{r}-{\mathbf{R}}^{T}{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)^{d/2}\exp\left(\left(\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{2}d+\frac{\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2({\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{i}^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\left(\log\left(\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)-\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}+\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\frac{d}{2}+\frac{\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2({\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{i}^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\left(\log\left(\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)-\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}+\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\frac{d}{4}\right)\leq\exp(-0.06d).

Therefore we finish the proof of Lemma
10
.
∎

Lemma
10
implies that when
\left\|{\mathbf{n}}\right\|
is large, the Gaussian mode
P^{(0)}
dominates other modes
P^{(i)}
. To bound
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|
, we first consider a simpler case that
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|
is large. Intuitively, the following lemma proves that when the previous state
\mathbf{n}_{t-1}
is far from a mode, a single step of Langevin dynamics with bounded step size is not enough to find the mode.

Lemma 11
.

Suppose
\delta_{t}\leq{\nu}_{0}^{2}
and
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}>36{\nu}_{0}^{2}d
, then for
\mathbf{n}_{t}
following from equation
13
, we have
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|^{2}\geq{\nu}_{0}^{2}d
with probability at least
1-\exp(-0.02d)
.

Proof of Lemma
11
.

From the recursion of
\mathbf{n}_{t}
in equation
13
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{n}_{t-1}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}{\mathbf{N}}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{n}_{t-1}-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}{P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}\cdot\frac{{\mathbf{N}}^{T}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i})}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}{P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}\cdot\frac{1}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\mathbf{n}_{t-1}+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}.

(14)

By Lemma
10
, we have
\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}{P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}\leq\exp(-0.06d)
for all
i\in[k]
, therefore

1-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}{P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}\cdot\frac{1}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\geq 1-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{\delta_{t}(1-w)}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}w}\exp(-0.06d)>\frac{1}{3}.

(15)

On the other hand, from
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{n},{\bm{I}}_{n})
we know
\frac{\langle\mathbf{n}_{t-1},\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}\rangle}{\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
for any fixed
\mathbf{n}_{t-1}\neq\bm{0}_{n}
, hence by Lemma
2
we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{\langle\mathbf{n}_{t-1},\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}\rangle}{\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|}\geq\frac{\sqrt{d}}{4}\right)={\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{\langle\mathbf{n}_{t-1},\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}\rangle}{\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|}\leq-\frac{\sqrt{d}}{4}\right)\leq\frac{4}{\sqrt{2\pi d}}\exp\left(-\frac{d}{32}\right)

(16)

Combining equation
14
, equation
15
and equation
16
gives that

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{2}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}-2{\nu}_{0}\lvert\langle\mathbf{n}_{t-1},\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}\rangle\rvert

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{9}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}-\frac{{\nu}_{0}\sqrt{d}}{2}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{9}\cdot 36{\nu}_{0}^{2}d-\frac{{\nu}_{0}\sqrt{d}}{2}\cdot 6{\nu}_{0}\sqrt{d}

\displaystyle={\nu}_{0}^{2}d

with probability at least
1-\frac{8}{\sqrt{2\pi d}}\exp\left(-\frac{d}{32}\right)\geq 1-\exp(-0.02d)
. This proves Lemma
11
.
∎

We then proceed to bound
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|
iteratively for
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}\leq 36{\nu}_{0}^{2}d
. Recall that equation
13
gives

\mathbf{n}_{t}=\mathbf{n}_{t-1}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}{\mathbf{N}}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}.

We notice that the difficulty of solving
\mathbf{n}_{t}
exhibits in the dependence of
\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})
on
\mathbf{r}_{t-1}
. Since
P=\sum_{i=0}^{k}w_{i}P^{(i)}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}w_{i}{\mathcal{N}}({\bm{\mu}}_{i},{\nu}_{i}^{2}{\bm{I}}_{d})
, we can rewrite the score function as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}=-\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\cdot\frac{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}=-\frac{{\mathbf{x}}}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}+\sum_{i\in[k]}\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\left(\frac{{\mathbf{x}}}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right).

(17)

Now, instead of directly working with
\mathbf{n}_{t}
, we consider a surrogate recursion
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
such that
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{0}=\mathbf{n}_{0}
and for all
t\geq 1
,

\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}=\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t-1}-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t-1}+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}.

(18)

The advantage of the surrogate recursion is that
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
is independent of
\mathbf{r}
, thus we can obtain the closed-form solution to
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
. Before we proceed to bound
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
, we first show that
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
is sufficiently close to the original recursion
\mathbf{n}_{t}
in the following lemma.

Lemma 12
.

For any
t\geq 1
, given that
\delta_{j}\leq{\nu}_{0}^{2}
and
\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{2}d\leq\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|^{2}\leq 36{\nu}_{0}^{2}d
for all
j\in[t]
and
\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}\leq 0.2d
for all
i\in[k]
, we have
\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}-\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|\leq\frac{t}{\exp(0.04d)}\sqrt{d}
.

Proof of Lemma
12
.

Upon comparing equation
13
and equation
18
, by equation
17
we have that for all
j\in[t]
,

\displaystyle\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j}-\mathbf{n}_{j}}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\frac{\delta_{j}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\mathbf{n}_{j-1}-\frac{\delta_{j}}{2}{\mathbf{N}}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|{\left(1-\frac{\delta_{j}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)(\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\mathbf{n}_{j-1})+\frac{\delta_{j}}{2}\sum_{i\in[k]}\frac{w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}{P({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}\left(\frac{1}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}-\frac{1}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{\delta_{j}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|+\sum_{i\in[k]}\frac{\delta_{j}}{2}\frac{w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}{P({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}\left(\frac{1}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}-\frac{1}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|+\sum_{i\in[k]}\frac{\delta_{j}}{2}\frac{w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}{w_{0}P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}\left(\frac{1}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}-\frac{1}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)6{\nu}_{0}\sqrt{d}.

By Lemma
10
, we have
\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}{P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}\leq\exp(-0.06d)
for all
i\in[k]
, hence we obtain a recursive bound

\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j}-\mathbf{n}_{j}}\right\|\leq\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|+\frac{1}{\exp(0.04d)}\sqrt{d}.

Finally, by
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{0}=\mathbf{n}_{0}
, we have

\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}-\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|=\sum_{j\in[t]}\left(\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j}-\mathbf{n}_{j}}\right\|-\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|\right)\leq\frac{t}{\exp(0.04d)}\sqrt{d}.

Hence we obtain Lemma
12
.
∎

We then proceed to analyze
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
, The following lemma gives us the closed-form solution of
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
. We slightly abuse the notations here, e.g.,
\prod_{i=c_{1}}^{c_{2}}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)=1
and
\sum_{j=c_{1}}^{c_{2}}\delta_{j}=0
for
c_{1}>c_{2}
.

Lemma 13
.

For all
t\geq 0
,
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}\sim{\mathcal{N}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)\mathbf{n}_{0},\;\sum_{j=1}^{t}\prod_{i=j+1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\delta_{j}{\bm{I}}_{n}\right)
, where the mean and covariance satisfy
\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{t}\prod_{i=j+1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\delta_{j}\geq 1
.

Proof of Lemma
13
.

We prove the two properties by induction. When
t=0
, they are trivial. Suppose they hold for
t-1
, then for the distribution of
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t-1}-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t-1}+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}

\displaystyle\sim{\mathcal{N}}\left(\left(1-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)\mathbf{n}_{0},\;\left(1-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\prod_{i=j+1}^{t-1}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\delta_{j}{\bm{I}}_{n}+\delta_{t}{\bm{I}}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{N}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)\mathbf{n}_{0},\;\sum_{j=1}^{t}\prod_{i=j+1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\delta_{j}{\bm{I}}_{n}\right).

For the second property,

\displaystyle\quad\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{t}\prod_{i=j+1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\delta_{j}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\prod_{i=j+1}^{t-1}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\delta_{j}\right)+\frac{1}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\delta_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\delta_{t}=1+\frac{\delta_{t}^{2}}{4{\nu}_{0}^{4}}\geq 1.

Hence we finish the proof of Lemma
13
.
∎

Armed with Lemma
13
, we are now ready to establish the lower bound on
\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}}\right\|
. For simplicity, denote
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}
and
\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{t}\prod_{i=j+1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{i}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\delta_{j}
. By Lemma
13
we know
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\alpha\mathbf{n}_{0},\beta{\nu}_{0}^{2}{\bm{I}}_{n})
, so we can write
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}=\alpha\mathbf{n}_{0}+\sqrt{\beta}{\nu}_{0}\bm{\epsilon}
, where
\bm{\epsilon}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{n},{\bm{I}}_{n})
.

Lemma 14
.

Given that
\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{0}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{4}d
, we have
\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{5{\nu}_{0}^{2}+3{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{8}d
with probability at least
1-\exp\left(-d/300\right)
.

Proof of Lemma
14
.

By
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}=\alpha\mathbf{n}_{0}+\sqrt{\beta}{\nu}_{0}\bm{\epsilon}
we have

\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}}\right\|^{2}=\alpha^{2}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|^{2}+\beta{\nu}_{0}^{2}\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}+2\alpha\sqrt{\beta}{\nu}_{0}\langle\mathbf{n}_{0},\bm{\epsilon}\rangle

By Lemma
1
we can bound

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{4{\nu}_{0}^{2}}d\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}\leq d-2\sqrt{d\cdot\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{8{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}d}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}\leq n-2\sqrt{n\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{8{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{d}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{8{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{d}{2}\right)\leq\exp(-d/288),

where the second last step follows from the assumption
d-n=r=o(d)
. Since
\bm{\epsilon}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{n},{\bm{I}}_{n})
, we know
\frac{\langle\mathbf{n}_{0},\bm{\epsilon}\rangle}{\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
. Therefore by Lemma
2
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{\langle\mathbf{n}_{0},\bm{\epsilon}\rangle}{\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|}\leq-\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{4{\nu}_{0}\sqrt{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}}\sqrt{d}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4{\nu}_{0}\sqrt{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}({\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{\max}^{2})\sqrt{d}}\exp\left(-\frac{({\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{\max}^{2})^{2}d}{32{\nu}_{0}^{2}(3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-0.004d).

Conditioned on
\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{0}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{4}d
,
\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}>\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{4{\nu}_{0}^{2}}d
and
\frac{1}{\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|}\langle\mathbf{n}_{0},\bm{\epsilon}\rangle>-\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{4{\nu}_{0}\sqrt{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}}\sqrt{d}
, since Lemma
13
gives
\alpha^{2}+\beta\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|^{2}+\beta{\nu}_{0}^{2}\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}+2\alpha\sqrt{\beta}{\nu}_{0}\langle\mathbf{n}_{0},\bm{\epsilon}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|^{2}+\beta{\nu}_{0}^{2}\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}-2\alpha\sqrt{\beta}{\nu}_{0}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{4{\nu}_{0}\sqrt{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}}\sqrt{d}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|^{2}+\beta{\nu}_{0}^{2}\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}-2\alpha\sqrt{\beta}{\nu}_{0}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|\cdot\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{6{\nu}_{0}^{2}+2{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{6{\nu}_{0}^{2}+2{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}\right)\left(\alpha^{2}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{0}}\right\|^{2}+\beta{\nu}_{0}^{2}\left\|{\bm{\epsilon}}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{5{\nu}_{0}^{2}+3{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{6{\nu}_{0}^{2}+2{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}\left(\alpha^{2}+\beta\right)\cdot\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{4}d

\displaystyle\geq\frac{5{\nu}_{0}^{2}+3{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{8}d.

Hence by union bound, we complete the proof of Lemma
14
.
∎

Upon having all the above lemmas, we are now ready to establish Theorem
2
by induction. Suppose the theorem holds for all
T
values of
1,\cdots,T-1
. We consider the following 3 cases:

•

If there exists some
t\in[T]
such that
\delta_{t}>{\nu}_{0}^{2}
, by Lemma
9
we know that with probability at least
1-\exp(-d/25)
, we have
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{4}d
, thus the problem reduces to the two sub-arrays
\mathbf{n}_{0},\cdots,\mathbf{n}_{t-1}
and
\mathbf{n}_{t},\cdots,\mathbf{n}_{T}
, which can be solved by induction.

•

Suppose
\delta_{t}\leq{\nu}_{0}^{2}
for all
t\in[T]
. If there exists some
t\in[T]
such that
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}>36{\nu}_{0}^{2}d
, by Lemma
11
we know that with probability at least
1-\exp(-d/50)
, we have
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|^{2}\geq{\nu}_{0}^{2}d>\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{4}d
, thus the problem similarly reduces to the two sub-arrays
\mathbf{n}_{0},\cdots,\mathbf{n}_{t-1}
and
\mathbf{n}_{t},\cdots,\mathbf{n}_{T}
, which can be solved by induction.

•

Suppose

regardless of the previous state
{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}
. We then consider the case when
\delta_{t}\leq{\nu}_{0}^{2}
. Intuitively, we aim to prove that the score function is close to
-\frac{{\mathbf{x}}}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}
when
\left\|{\mathbf{n}}\right\|^{2}\geq\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{2}+\frac{{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{2(1-c_{{\nu}})}\right)d
. Towards this goal, we first show that
P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})
is exponentially larger than
P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})
for all
i\in[k]
in the following lemma:

Lemma 16
.

Suppose
P
satisfies Assumption
1
. Then for any
\left\|{\mathbf{n}}\right\|^{2}\geq\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{2}+\frac{{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{2(1-c_{{\nu}})}\right)d
, we have
\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}\leq\exp(-\Omega(d))
and
\frac{\left\|{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}\right\|}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\leq\exp(-\Omega(d))
for all
i\in[k]
.

Proof of Lemma
16
.

We first give an upper bound on the sub-Gaussian probability density. For any vector
{\mathbf{v}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, by considering some vector
{\mathbf{m}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, from Markov’s inequality and the definition in equation
19
we can bound

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbf{z}}\sim P^{(i)}}\left({\mathbf{m}}^{T}({\mathbf{z}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i})\geq{\mathbf{m}}^{T}({\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{z}}\sim P^{(i)}}\left[\exp\left({\mathbf{m}}^{T}({\mathbf{z}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i})\right)\right]}{\exp\left({\mathbf{m}}^{T}({\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i})\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}\left\|{{\mathbf{m}}}\right\|^{2}}{2}-{\mathbf{m}}^{T}({\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i})\right).

Upon optimizing the last term at
{\mathbf{m}}=\frac{{\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}
, we obtain

{\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbf{z}}\sim P^{(i)}}\left(({\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i})^{T}({\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}})\leq 0\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right).

(20)

Denote
{\mathbb{B}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{{{\mathbf{z}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}({\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i})^{T}({\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}})\leq 0}\right\}
. To bound
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbf{z}}\sim P^{(i)}}({\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{B}})
, we first note that

\displaystyle\log P^{(i)}({\mathbf{v}})-\log P^{(i)}({\mathbf{z}})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\langle{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}},\nabla\log P^{(i)}({\mathbf{v}}+\lambda({\mathbf{z}}-{\mathbf{v}}))\rangle{\,\mathrm{d}}\lambda

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}},\nabla\log P^{(i)}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle+\int_{0}^{1}\langle{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}},\nabla\log P^{(i)}({\mathbf{v}}+\lambda({\mathbf{z}}-{\mathbf{v}}))-\nabla\log P^{(i)}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle{\,\mathrm{d}}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}}}\right\|\left\|{\nabla\log P^{(i)}({\mathbf{v}})}\right\|+\int_{0}^{1}\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}}}\right\|\left\|{\nabla\log P^{(i)}({\mathbf{v}}+\lambda({\mathbf{z}}-{\mathbf{v}}))-\nabla\log P^{(i)}({\mathbf{v}})}\right\|{\,\mathrm{d}}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}}}\right\|\cdot L_{i}\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|+\int_{0}^{1}\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}}}\right\|\cdot L_{i}\left\|{\lambda({\mathbf{z}}-{\mathbf{v}})}\right\|{\,\mathrm{d}}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L_{i}c_{{\nu}}}{2c_{L}}\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}+\left(\frac{c_{L}+c_{{\nu}}}{2c_{{\nu}}}\right)L_{i}\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}}}\right\|^{2},

where the second last inequality follows from Assumption
1
.
ii.
that
\nabla\log P^{(i)}({\bm{\mu}}_{i})=\bm{0}_{d}
and Assumption
1
.
iii.
that the score function
\nabla\log P^{(i)}
is
L_{i}
-Lipschitz. Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbf{z}}\sim P^{(i)}}({\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{B}})=\int_{{\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{B}}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{z}}){\,\mathrm{d}}{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{B}}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{v}})\exp\left(-\frac{L_{i}c_{{\nu}}}{2c_{L}}\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}-\frac{c_{L}+c_{{\nu}}}{2c_{{\nu}}}L_{i}\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}}}\right\|^{2}\right){\,\mathrm{d}}{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=P^{(i)}({\mathbf{v}})\exp\left(-\frac{L_{i}c_{{\nu}}}{2c_{L}}\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}\right)\int_{{\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{B}}}\exp\left(-\frac{c_{L}+c_{{\nu}}}{2c_{{\nu}}}L_{i}\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}}}\right\|^{2}\right){\,\mathrm{d}}{\mathbf{z}}.

(21)

By observing that
g\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{B}}\to\left\{{{\mathbf{z}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}({\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i})^{T}({\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}})\geq 0}\right\}
with
g({\mathbf{z}})=2{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}}
is a bijection such that
\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}}}\right\|=\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-g({\mathbf{z}})}\right\|
for any
{\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{B}}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{B}}}\exp\left(-\frac{c_{L}+c_{{\nu}}}{2c_{{\nu}}}L_{i}\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}}}\right\|^{2}\right){\,\mathrm{d}}{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\exp\left(-\frac{c_{L}+c_{{\nu}}}{2c_{{\nu}}}L_{i}\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}}}\right\|^{2}\right){\,\mathrm{d}}{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2\pi c_{{\nu}}}{(c_{L}+c_{{\nu}})L_{i}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}.

(22)

Hence, by combining equation
20
, equation
21
, and equation
22
, we obtain

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbf{z}}\sim P^{(i)}}\left(({\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i})^{T}({\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{z}})\leq 0\right)

\displaystyle\geq P^{(i)}({\mathbf{v}})\exp\left(-\frac{L_{i}c_{{\nu}}}{2c_{L}}\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2\pi c_{{\nu}}}{(c_{L}+c_{{\nu}})L_{i}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}.

By Assumption
1
.
iii.
that
L_{i}\leq\frac{c_{L}}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}
we obtain the following bound on the probability density:

P^{(i)}({\mathbf{v}})\leq 2\left(\frac{2\pi c_{{\nu}}{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{(c_{L}+c_{{\nu}})c_{L}}\right)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1-c_{{\nu}}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}\right).

(23)

Then we can bound the ratio of
P^{(i)}
and
P^{(0)}
. For all
i\in[k]
, define
\rho_{i}({\mathbf{x}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}
, then we have

\displaystyle\rho_{i}({\mathbf{x}})=\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}\leq\frac{2(2\pi c_{{\nu}}{\nu}_{i}^{2}/(c_{L}^{2}+c_{{\nu}}c_{L}))^{-d/2}\exp\left(-(1-c_{{\nu}})\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}/2{\nu}_{i}^{2}\right)}{(2\pi{\nu}_{0}^{2})^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|^{2}/2{\nu}_{0}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{(c_{L}^{2}+c_{{\nu}}c_{L}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}{c_{{\nu}}{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(\frac{\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}})\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{(c_{L}^{2}+c_{{\nu}}c_{L}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}{c_{{\nu}}{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(\left(\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1-c_{{\nu}}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\left\|{{\mathbf{N}}\mathbf{n}}\right\|^{2}+\left(\frac{\left\|{{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{r}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}})\left\|{{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{r}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{(c_{L}^{2}+c_{{\nu}}c_{L}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}{c_{{\nu}}{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(\left(\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1-c_{{\nu}}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\left\|{\mathbf{n}}\right\|^{2}+\left(\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{r}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}})\left\|{\mathbf{r}-{\mathbf{R}}^{T}{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\right),

where the last step follows from the definition that
{\mathbf{R}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times r}
an orthogonal basis of the vector space
\left\{{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}
and
{\mathbf{N}}^{T}{\mathbf{N}}={\bm{I}}_{n}
. Since
{\nu}_{i}^{2}<(1-c_{{\nu}}){\nu}_{0}^{2}
, the quadratic term
\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{r}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}})\left\|{\mathbf{r}-{\mathbf{R}}^{T}{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}
is maximized at
\mathbf{r}=\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}}){\nu}_{0}^{2}{\mathbf{R}}^{T}{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}{(1-c_{{\nu}}){\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{i}^{2}}
. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|{\mathbf{r}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}})\left\|{\mathbf{r}-{\mathbf{R}}^{T}{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\leq\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}})\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2((1-c_{{\nu}}){\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{i}^{2})}.

Hence, for
\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}-{\bm{\mu}}_{0}}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}}){\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{2(1-c_{{\nu}})}\left(\log\frac{c_{{\nu}}{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{(c_{L}^{2}+c_{{\nu}}c_{L}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{2(1-c_{{\nu}}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}+\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)d
and
\left\|{\mathbf{n}}\right\|^{2}\geq\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{2}+\frac{{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{2(1-c_{{\nu}})}\right)d
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{i}({\mathbf{x}})\leq 2\left(\frac{(c_{L}^{2}+c_{{\nu}}c_{L}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}{c_{{\nu}}{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(\left(\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1-c_{{\nu}}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\left\|{\mathbf{n}}\right\|^{2}+\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}})\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2((1-c_{{\nu}}){\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{i}^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{(c_{L}^{2}+c_{{\nu}}c_{L}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}{c_{{\nu}}{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(\left(\frac{1}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1-c_{{\nu}}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{2}+\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{2(1-c_{{\nu}})}\right)d+\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}})\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2((1-c_{{\nu}}){\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{i}^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=2\exp\left(-\left(\log\frac{c_{{\nu}}{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{(c_{L}^{2}+c_{{\nu}}c_{L}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{2(1-c_{{\nu}}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}+\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\frac{d}{2}+\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}})\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2((1-c_{{\nu}}){\nu}_{0}^{2}-{\nu}_{i}^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-\left(\log\frac{c_{{\nu}}{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{(c_{L}^{2}+c_{{\nu}}c_{L}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{2(1-c_{{\nu}}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}+\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\frac{d}{4}\right).

From Lemma
15
, we obtain
\rho_{i}({\mathbf{x}})\leq\exp(-\Omega(d))
.

To show
\frac{\left\|{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}\right\|}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\leq\exp(-\Omega(d))
, from Assumptions
1
.
ii.
and
1
.
iii.
we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\frac{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|{\frac{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}-\frac{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\bm{\mu}}_{i})}{P^{(i)}({\bm{\mu}}_{i})}}\right\|=\left\|{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(i)}({\bm{\mu}}_{i})}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{i}\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|\leq\frac{c_{L}}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|.

Therefore, we can bound
\frac{\left\|{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}\right\|}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\leq\frac{c_{L}}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\rho_{i}({\mathbf{x}})\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|
. When
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|=\exp(o(d))
is small, by
\rho_{i}({\mathbf{x}})\leq\exp(-\Omega(d))
we directly have
\frac{\left\|{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}\right\|}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\leq\exp(-\Omega(d))
. When
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|=\exp(\Omega(d))
is exceedingly large, from equation
23
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}\right\|}{P({\mathbf{x}})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2c_{L}}{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\left(\frac{(c_{L}^{2}+c_{{\nu}}c_{L}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}{c_{{\nu}}{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(\frac{\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}})\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|.

Since
{\nu}_{0}^{2}>\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{1-c_{{\nu}}}
, when
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|=\exp(\Omega(d))\gg\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|
we have

\exp\left(\frac{\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}})\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)=\exp(-\Omega(\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2})).

Therefore
\frac{\left\|{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}\right\|}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\leq\exp(-\Omega(d))
. Thus we complete the proof of Lemma
16
.
∎

Similar to Lemma
11
, the following lemma proves that when the previous state
\mathbf{n}_{t-1}
is far from a mode, a single step of Langevin dynamics with bounded step size is not enough to find the mode.

Lemma 17
.

Suppose
\delta_{t}\leq{\nu}_{0}^{2}
and
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}>36{\nu}_{0}^{2}d
, then we have
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|^{2}\geq{\nu}_{0}^{2}d
with probability at least
1-\exp(-\Omega(d))
.

Proof of Lemma
17
.

For simplicity, denote
{\mathbf{v}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbf{n}_{t-1}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}{\mathbf{N}}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})
. Since
P=\sum_{i=0}^{k}w_{i}P^{(i)}
and
P^{(0)}={\mathcal{N}}({\bm{\mu}}_{0},{\nu}_{0}^{2}{\bm{I}}_{d})
, the score function can be written as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}=\frac{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}w_{0}P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}+\sum_{i\in[k]}\frac{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{w_{0}P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\cdot\frac{{\mathbf{x}}}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}+\sum_{i\in[k]}\frac{w_{i}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{{\mathbf{x}}}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}+\sum_{i\in[k]}\frac{w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\cdot\frac{{\mathbf{x}}}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}+\sum_{i\in[k]}\frac{w_{i}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}.

(24)

For
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}>36{\nu}_{0}^{2}d
by Lemma
16
we have
\frac{\left\|{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}\right\|}{P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}\leq\exp(-\Omega(d))
. Since
\delta_{t}\leq{\nu}_{0}^{2}
, we can bound the norm of
{\mathbf{v}}
by

\displaystyle\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}{\mathbf{N}}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\mathbf{n}_{t-1}+\sum_{i\in[k]}\frac{w_{i}\delta_{t}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}{P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}\mathbf{n}_{t-1}+\sum_{i\in[k]}\frac{w_{i}\delta_{t}}{2}\frac{{\mathbf{N}}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}{P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\left\|{\left(1-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}+\sum_{i\in[k]}\frac{w_{i}\delta_{t}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}{P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}\right)\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|-\sum_{i\in[k]}\frac{w_{i}\delta_{t}}{2}\frac{\left\|{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}\right\|}{P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|-\sum_{i\in[k]}\frac{w_{i}\delta_{t}}{2}\exp(-\Omega(d))

\displaystyle>2{\nu}_{0}\sqrt{d}.

On the other hand, from
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\bm{0}_{n},{\bm{I}}_{n})
we know
\frac{\langle{\mathbf{v}},\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}\rangle}{\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}}\right\|}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
for any fixed
{\mathbf{v}}\neq\bm{0}_{n}
, hence by Lemma
2
we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{\langle{\mathbf{v}},\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}\rangle}{\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}}\right\|}\geq\frac{\sqrt{d}}{4}\right)={\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{\langle{\mathbf{v}},\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}\rangle}{\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}}\right\|}\leq-\frac{\sqrt{d}}{4}\right)\leq\frac{4}{\sqrt{2\pi d}}\exp\left(-\frac{d}{32}\right)

Combining the above inequalities gives

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}}\right\|^{2}\geq\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}}\right\|^{2}-2{\nu}_{0}\lvert\langle{\mathbf{v}},\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}\rangle\rvert\geq\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}}\right\|^{2}-\frac{{\nu}_{0}\sqrt{d}}{2}\left\|{{\mathbf{v}}}\right\|>{\nu}_{0}^{2}d

with probability at least
1-\frac{8}{\sqrt{2\pi d}}\exp\left(-\frac{d}{32}\right)=1-\exp(-\Omega(d))
. This proves Lemma
17
.
∎

When
\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}\leq 36{\nu}_{0}^{2}d
, similar to Theorem
2
, we consider a surrogate recursion
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
such that
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{0}=\mathbf{n}_{0}
and for all
t\geq 1
,

\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}=\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t-1}-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t-1}+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}^{(\mathbf{n})}.

(25)

The following Lemma shows that
\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}
is sufficiently close to the original recursion
\mathbf{n}_{t}
.

Lemma 18
.

For any
t\geq 1
, given that for all
j\in[t]
,
\delta_{j}\leq{\nu}_{0}^{2}
and
\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{2}+\frac{{\nu}_{\max}^{2}}{2(1-c_{{\nu}})}\right)d\leq\left\|{\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|^{2}\leq 36{\nu}_{0}^{2}d
, if
{\bm{\mu}}_{i}
satisfies Assumption
1
.
v.
for all

.

Proof of Lemma
18
.

By equation
24
we have that for all

\displaystyle\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j}-\mathbf{n}_{j}}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\mathbf{n}_{j-1}-\frac{\delta_{j}}{2{\nu}_{0}^{2}}\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\frac{\delta_{j}}{2}{\mathbf{N}}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\mathbf{n}_{j-1}-\sum_{i\in[k]}\frac{w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}P({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}\mathbf{n}_{j-1}-\sum_{i\in[k]}\frac{w_{i}{\mathbf{N}}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}{P({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}}\right\|

, we have
\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}{P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}_{j-1})}\leq\exp(-\Omega(d))

, hence from

we obtain a recursive bound

Finally, by

, we have

\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{t}-\mathbf{n}_{t}}\right\|=\sum_{j\in[t]}\left(\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j}-\mathbf{n}_{j}}\right\|-\left\|{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{j-1}-\mathbf{n}_{j-1}}\right\|\right)\leq\frac{t}{\exp(\Omega(d))}\sqrt{d}.

Hence we obtain Lemma
18
.
∎

•

If there exists some

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{w_{0}P({\mathbf{x}})}\cdot\frac{\nabla P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{w_{0}P({\mathbf{x}})}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{w_{0}P({\mathbf{x}})}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{w_{0}P({\mathbf{x}})}

From Lemma
19
we know
\frac{\left\|{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}\right\|}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\leq\exp(-\Omega(d))
. It remains to show
\left\|{\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}\right\|\leq\exp(-\Omega(d))
. Since by Assumption
3
the score function of
P^{(0)}
is
L_{0}
-Lipschitz, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\left(\left\|{\nabla\log P^{(0)}(0)}\right\|+L_{0}\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}L_{0}\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|+\exp(-\Omega(d))

When
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|=\exp(o(d))
is small, by
\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\leq\exp(-\Omega(d))
we directly have
\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|\leq\exp(-\Omega(d))
. When
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|=\exp(\Omega(d))
is exceedingly large, by equation
32
we have

\displaystyle\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{(c_{L}^{2}+c_{{\nu}}c_{L}){\nu}_{0}^{2}}{c_{{\nu}}{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(\frac{(1+c_{0})(\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}+\left\|{{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2})}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}-\frac{(1-c_{{\nu}})\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}\right)\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|

Since
\frac{1-c_{{\nu}}}{2{\nu}_{i}^{2}}>\frac{1+c_{0}}{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}
, when
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|=\exp(\Omega(d))
we have
\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|\leq\exp(-\Omega(d))
. Therefore, by combining the above we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{w_{i}}{w_{0}}\left\|{\frac{P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})}\right\|+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{w_{i}}{w_{0}}\left\|{\frac{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P^{(i)}({\mathbf{x}})}{P({\mathbf{x}})}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-\Omega(d))

which finishes the proof of Lemma
20
.
∎

We consider an auxiliary trajectory such that
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{\prime}={\mathbf{x}}_{0}
and

{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{\prime}={\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}^{\prime}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}^{\prime})+\sqrt{\delta_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}.

Since the update rule of the auxiliary trajectory is independent of the modes
P^{(1)},\cdots,P^{(k)}
and
{\bm{\mu}}_{i}
is uniformly randomly initialized in a ball of radius
r
, for any given
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{\prime}
we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{\prime}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}>\left(\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{4}+\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{4(1-c_{{\nu}})}\right)d\right)\leq\exp\left(-d\log\frac{4r^{2}}{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{i}^{2}/(1-c_{{\nu}})}\right)

Hence, by the union bound, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{\prime}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}>\left(\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{4}+\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{4(1-c_{{\nu}})}\right)d\;\forall t\in\{0\}\cup[T],i\in[k]\right)

(34)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sum_{t=0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{\prime}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}>\left(\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{4}+\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{4(1-c_{{\nu}})}\right)d\right)

(35)

\displaystyle\geq 1-(T+1)k\exp\left(-d\log\frac{4r^{2}}{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}+{\nu}_{i}^{2}/(1-c_{{\nu}})}\right).

(36)

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
7
. Notice that concavity and
L_{0}
-smoothness of
\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})
imply that the gradients are co-coercive, i.e.,

\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}),\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\right\rangle\leq-\frac{1}{L_{0}}\left\|{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})}\right\|^{2}.

Therefore, for step size
\delta\leq\frac{4}{L_{0}}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}+\frac{\delta}{2}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})-{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}-\frac{\delta}{2}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})}\right\|^{2}

(37)

\displaystyle=\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}}\right\|^{2}+\delta\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}),\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\right\rangle+\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}\left\|{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})}\right\|^{2}

(38)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}}\right\|^{2}+\left(\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}-\frac{\delta}{L_{0}}\right)\left\|{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})}\right\|^{2}

(39)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}}\right\|^{2}

(40)

If
{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}
satisfies
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}\geq\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{2}+\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{2(1-c_{{\nu}})}\right)d
for all
i\in[k]

20

\displaystyle\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{\prime}}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})-{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}^{\prime}-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}^{\prime})}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}+\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})-{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}^{\prime}-\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}^{\prime})}\right\|

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{\delta_{t}}{2}\left\|{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P^{(0)}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1})}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}}\right\|+\exp(-\Omega(d))

Assuming
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{\prime}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}>\left(\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{4}+\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{4(1-c_{{\nu}})}\right)d
for all
t\in\{0\}\cup[T]
and
i\in[k]
, which holds with probability
1-T\cdot\exp(-\Omega(d))
due to equation
36
, by induction we can easily obtain that when

T\cdot\exp(-\Omega(d))\leq\sqrt{\left(\frac{3{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{4}+\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{4(1-c_{{\nu}})}\right)d}-\sqrt{\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{2}+\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{2(1-c_{{\nu}})}\right)d}

we have
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{\prime}}\right\|\leq T\cdot\exp(-\Omega(d))
and
\left\|{{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-{\bm{\mu}}_{i}}\right\|^{2}>\left(\frac{{\nu}_{0}^{2}}{2}+\frac{{\nu}_{i}^{2}}{2(1-c_{{\nu}})}\right)d
for all

and

, which completes the proof of Theorem

.

First, for

Proposition 3
.

Consider a sampler algorithm taking the first

\text{TV}\left(\widehat{P}({\mathbf{x}}),P({\mathbf{x}})\right)\leq\varepsilon.

Proof of Proposition
3
.

For simplicity, denote
{\mathbf{x}}^{[q]}=\left\{{{\mathbf{x}}^{(1)},\cdots,{\mathbf{x}}^{(q)}}\right\}
. By the definition of total variation distance, for all
q\in[d/Q]
we have

\displaystyle\text{TV}\left(\widehat{P}\left({\mathbf{x}}^{[q]}\right),P\left({\mathbf{x}}^{[q]}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\left\lvert\widehat{P}\left({\mathbf{x}}^{[q]}\right)-P\left({\mathbf{x}}^{[q]}\right)\right\rvert{\,\mathrm{d}}{\mathbf{x}}^{[q]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\left\lvert\widehat{P}\left({\mathbf{x}}^{(q)}\mid{\mathbf{x}}^{[q-1]}\right)\widehat{P}\left({\mathbf{x}}^{[q-1]}\right)-P\left({\mathbf{x}}^{(q)}\mid{\mathbf{x}}^{[q-1]}\right)P\left({\mathbf{x}}^{[q-1]}\right)\right\rvert{\,\mathrm{d}}{\mathbf{x}}^{[q]}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int\left\lvert\widehat{P}\left({\mathbf{x}}^{(q)}\mid{\mathbf{x}}^{[q-1]}\right)P\left({\mathbf{x}}^{[q-1]}\right)-P\left({\mathbf{x}}^{(q)}\mid{\mathbf{x}}^{[q-1]}\right)P\left({\mathbf{x}}^{[q-1]}\right)\right\rvert{\,\mathrm{d}}{\mathbf{x}}^{[q]}

D.2
Score Function Estimator

In realistic scenarios, since we do not have direct access to the (perturbed) score function,
[
6
]
proposed the Noise Conditional Score Network (NCSN)
{\mathbf{s}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}},{\sigma})
to jointly estimate the scores of all perturbed data distributions, i.e.,

\forall{\sigma}\in\left\{{{\sigma}_{t}}\right\}_{t\in[T]},\;{\mathbf{s}}_{\bm{\theta}}({\mathbf{x}},{\sigma})\approx\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P_{{\sigma}}({\mathbf{x}}).

To train the NCSN,
[
6
]
adopted denoising score matching, which minimizes the following loss

{\mathcal{L}}\left(\bm{\theta};\left\{{{\sigma}_{t}}\right\}_{t\in[T]}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2T}\sum_{t\in[T]}{\sigma}_{t}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}\sim P}\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}\sim{\mathcal{N}}({\mathbf{x}},{\sigma}_{t}^{2}{\bm{I}}_{d})}\biggl[\left\|{{\mathbf{s}}_{\bm{\theta}}(\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}},{\sigma}_{t})-\frac{\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}-{\mathbf{x}}}{{\sigma}_{t}^{2}}}\right\|^{2}\biggr].

Assuming the NCSN has enough capacity and sufficient training samples,
{\mathbf{s}}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}({\mathbf{x}},{\sigma})
minimizes the loss
{\mathcal{L}}\left(\bm{\theta};\left\{{{\sigma}_{t}}\right\}_{t\in[T]}\right)
if and only if
{\mathbf{s}}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}({\mathbf{x}},{\sigma}_{t})=\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log P_{{\sigma}_{t}}({\mathbf{x}})
almost surely for all
t\in[T]
.

In Chained Langevin dynamics, an ideal conditional score function estimator
{\mathbf{s}}_{\bm{\theta}}
could jointly estimate the scores of all perturbed conditional patch distribution, i.e.,
\forall{\sigma}\in\left\{{{\sigma}_{t}}\right\}_{t\in[TQ/d]},q\in[d/Q]
,

{\mathbf{s}}_{\bm{\theta}}\left({\mathbf{x}}^{(q)}\mid{\sigma},{\mathbf{x}}^{(1)},\cdots,{\mathbf{x}}^{(q-1)}\right)\approx\nabla_{{\mathbf{x}}^{(q)}}\log P_{{\sigma}}({\mathbf{x}}^{(q)}\mid{\mathbf{x}}^{(1)},\cdots{\mathbf{x}}^{(q-1)}).

Following from
[
6
]
, we use the denoising score matching to train the estimator. For a given
{\sigma}
, the denoising score matching objective is

\ell(\bm{\theta};{\sigma})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}\sim P}\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}\sim{\mathcal{N}}({\mathbf{x}},{\sigma}^{2}{\bm{I}}_{d})}\sum_{q\in[d/Q]}\left[\left\|{{\mathbf{s}}_{\bm{\theta}}\left({\mathbf{x}}^{(q)}\mid{\sigma},{\mathbf{x}}^{(1)},\cdots,{\mathbf{x}}^{(q-1)}\right)-\frac{\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{(q)}-{\mathbf{x}}^{(q)}}{{\sigma}^{2}}}\right\|^{2}\right].

Then, combining the objectives gives the following loss

{\mathcal{L}}\left(\bm{\theta};\left\{{{\sigma}_{t}}\right\}_{t\in[TQ/d]}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{d}{TQ}\sum_{t\in[TQ/d]}{\sigma}_{t}^{2}\ell(\bm{\theta};{\sigma}_{t}).

As shown in
[
34
]
, an estimator
{\mathbf{s}}_{\bm{\theta}}
with enough capacity and sufficient training samples minimizes the loss
{\mathcal{L}}
if and only if
{\mathbf{s}}_{\bm{\theta}}
outputs the scores of all perturbed conditional patch distribution almost surely.

D.3
Image Datasets

Our implementation and hyperparameter selection are based on
[
6
]
and
[
26
]
. During training, we i.i.d. randomly flip an image with probability 0.5 to construct the two modes (i.e., original and flipped images). All models are optimized by Adam with learning rate 0.001 and batch size 128 for a total of 200000 training steps, and we use the model at the last iteration to generate the samples. We perform experiments on MNIST
[
28
]
(CC BY-SA 3.0 License) and Fashion-MNIST
[
29
]
(MIT License) datasets and we set the patch size as
Q=14
. All experiments were run with one RTX3090 GPU.

For the score networks of chained annealed Langevin dynamics (Chained-ALD), we use the official PyTorch implementation of an LSTM network
[
35
]
followed by a linear layer. For MNIST and Fashion-MNIST datasets, we set the input size of the LSTM as
Q=14
, the number of features in the hidden state as 1024, and the number of recurrent layers as 2. The inputs of LSTM include inputting tensor, hidden state, and cell state, and the outputs of LSTM include the next hidden state and cell state, which can be fed to the next input. To estimate the noisy score function, we first input the noise level
{\sigma}
(repeated for
Q
times to match the input size of LSTM) and all-0 hidden and cell states to obtain an initialization of the hidden and cell states. Then, we divide a sample into
d/Q
patches and input the sequence of patches to the LSTM. For every output hidden state corresponding to one patch, we apply a linear layer of size
1024\times Q
to estimate the noisy score function of the patch.

T\in\left\{{10000,30000,100000}\right\}

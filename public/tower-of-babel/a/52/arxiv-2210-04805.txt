Phenotype switching in chemotaxis aggregation models controls the spontaneous emergence of large densities
Kevin J Painter, Michael Winkler

Abstract
We consider a phenotype-switching chemotaxis model for aggregation, in which a chemotactic population is capable of switching back and forth between a chemotaxing state (performing chemotactic movement) and a secreting state (producing the attractant). We show that the switching rate provides a powerful mechanism for controlling the densities of spontaneously emerging aggregates. Specifically, in two- and three-dimensional settings it is shown that when both switching rates coincide and are suitably large, then the densities of both the chemotaxing and the secreting population will exceed any prescribed level at some points in the considered domain. This is complemented by two results asserting the absence of such aggregation phenomena in corresponding scenarios in which one of the switching rates remains within some bounded interval. Key words: chemotaxis; indirect signal production; singularity formation MSC 2020: 35B44 (primary); 35B36, 35B45, 35K55, 92C17 (secondary)

Introduction

The Keller-Segel system for chemotactic movement
[
12
]
constitutes a classical model of mathematical biology, noted for its capacity to exhibit self-organisation. When formulated with two variables – a chemotactic population and its diffusive chemoattractant – and making the assumption that the population secretes its own attractant, positive feedback is inserted into this system that allows clusters to form through self-reinforcement. Studies of this system form a significant literature, ranging from biological and sociological applications of the model
[
19
]
to studies into the intricate analytical properties
[
8
,
2
]
. In terms of the latter, resolving the question of whether solutions to the system exist globally-in-time or blow up has been of particular interest.

This simple model implicitly assumes homogeneity across the population, i.e. that all members have equivalent capacity to perform chemotaxis and/or produce attractant. These assumptions of homogeneity, however, can lie at odds with the heterogeneity observed in natural systems, where a population will often span a spectrum of
phenotypes
, such as variable chemotactic sensitivity in the context of chemotactic behaviour
[
22
]
. The presence of phenotypic heterogeneity may confer numerous advantages, such as adaptability within fluctuating environments, but must also be considered within the wider context of energy balances
[
11
]
: performing one activity (e.g. movement) has an inevitable energy cost, meaning less resources may be available for other activities (e.g. protein synthesis and transport), i.e. there exists a
trade-off
. In light of this, recent models have been developed that extend the Keller and Segel framework to include phenotypic heterogeneity and trade-offs, either through a population that spans a continuous spectrum of phenotypes
[
17
]
or a population subdivided into discrete phenotypic states
[
18
]
. The latter considered a self-organisation type scenario, specifically focusing on a trade-off in which the population either performed chemotaxis (
chemotaxing state
) or produced the attractant (
secreting state
), but could not perform both simultaneously. Linear stability analysis of this model demonstrated that pattern formation is possible, but that the rate of switching between the two states becomes a crucial factor in the dynamics. Numerical analyses confirmed these predictions and, moreover, hinted that solutions exist globally-in-time, yet a formal analysis was not attempted.

Motivated by this, we will focus on a family of problems based on the system introduced in
[
18
]
. In the following,
u_{\alpha\beta}
represents the chemotaxing phenotype,
w_{\alpha\beta}
represents the secreting phenotype and
v_{\alpha\beta}
is the chemoattractant. Specifically, we shall consider the problems

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u_{\alpha\beta}=\Delta u_{\alpha\beta}-\nabla\cdot(u_{\alpha\beta}\nabla v_{\alpha\beta})-\alpha u_{\alpha\beta}+\beta w_{\alpha\beta},&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
\partial_{t}v_{\alpha\beta}=\Delta v_{\alpha\beta}-v_{\alpha\beta}+w_{\alpha\beta},&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
\partial_{t}w_{\alpha\beta}=\Delta w_{\alpha\beta}-\beta w_{\alpha\beta}+\alpha u_{\alpha\beta},&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{\partial u_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial w_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\ t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
u_{\alpha\beta}(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad v_{\alpha\beta}(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\quad w_{\alpha\beta}(x,0)=w_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

with
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
representing the rate of switching from chemotaxing to secreting and secreting to chemotaxing, respectively.

Viewed from a mathematical perspective, (
1.1
) differs from the corresponding initial-boundary value problem for the classical Keller-Segel system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v),\\[2.84526pt]
v_{t}=\Delta v-v+\mu u,\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

by including a form of indirectness with respect to the signal production process.
Whereas the classical system is well-known for its ability to generate unbounded densities within the chemotaxing population
(
[
7
]
,
[
26
]
), the inclusion of indirectness within the signal dynamics has been found to significantly reduce a trend toward singular destabilisation. In particular, in
[
6
]
the presence of additional diffusion-induced dissipation of the form in (
1.1
)
was shown to suppress any blow-up phenomena in a closely related problem in three (and lower)-dimensional settings. Meanwhile, a substantial collection of additional studies has provided comparable findings for a number of nearby extensions
(
[
3
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
32
]
). Lacking, as far as we are aware, is a rigorous understanding into the extent to which regularised systems of this nature retain core features of taxis-driven destabilisation, despite the absence of unboundedness phenomena.

Main results.

The purpose of the present manuscript is to confirm that the coefficients
\alpha
and
\beta
of the zero
order contributions to (
1.1
) play a key role in this regard. In particular, simultaneously increasing these parameters can result in the spontaneous emergence of structures which, while not singular, do lead to population densities of arbitrary size.

As a preliminary step, in Section
3
we confirm that, within frameworks of physically relevant dimension, blow-up does not occur in any of the populations in (
1.1
), in the sense that for any choice of the parameters therein, and for all reasonably regular nonnegative initial data,
a global bounded classical solution can always be found:

Proposition 1.1

Let
n\leq 3
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary, let
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
, and suppose that

u_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega),v_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\mbox{ and }w_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{are nonnegative}.

(1.3)

Then there exist uniquely determined nonnegative functions

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{\alpha\beta}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,\infty))\cap C^{2,1}(\overline{\Omega}\times(0,\infty)),\\[2.84526pt]
v_{\alpha\beta}\in\bigcap_{q>n}C^{0}([0,\infty);W^{1,q}(\Omega))\cap C^{2,1}(\overline{\Omega}\times(0,\infty))\qquad\mbox{and}\\[2.84526pt]
w_{\alpha\beta}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,\infty))\cap C^{2,1}(\overline{\Omega}\times(0,\infty))\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

such that
(u_{\alpha\beta},v_{\alpha\beta},w_{\alpha\beta})
forms a classical solution of (
1.1
).
Moreover, this solution is bounded in the sense that for each
q>n
there exists

such that

\|u_{\alpha\beta}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|v_{\alpha\beta}(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}+\|w_{\alpha\beta}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C(q)\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{for all }t>0.

In the light of a corresponding result for a relative of (

), obtained in
[

]
, this
observation is not in itself particularly surprising. The approach taken to derive this, however, is designed in such a way that it can be artlessly further developed so as to reveal that the bounds in (

(0,\infty)^{2}
.

In fact, as a first statement in this direction, we
show that provided

is located in a fixed interval, then the inequality in (

Proposition 1.2

Let
n\leq 3
, and assume (
1.3
).
Then for each
q>n
and any
\alpha^{\star}>0
and
\delta>0
, there exists
C(q,\alpha^{\star},\delta)>0
such that

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\|u_{\alpha\beta}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|v_{\alpha\beta}(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}+\|w_{\alpha\beta}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C(q,\alpha^{\star},\delta)

for all
t>0
and any choice of
\alpha\in(0,\alpha^{\star}]
and
\beta\geq\delta
.

(1.6)

Likewise, under the corresponding scenario that
\beta
is located in a fixed interval, unboundedness phenomena can also be ruled out in the limit
\alpha\to\infty
:

Proposition 1.3

Let
n\leq 3
, and suppose that (
1.3
) holds.
Then whenever
q>n,\beta^{\star}>0
and
\delta\in(0,\beta^{\star})
, one can find
C(q,\beta^{\star},\delta)>0
with the property that

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\|u_{\alpha\beta}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|v_{\alpha\beta}(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}+\|w_{\alpha\beta}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C(q,\beta^{\star},\delta)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{for all $t>0$, any $\alpha>0$ and each }\beta\in[\delta,\beta^{\star}].

(1.7)

In stark contrast to the above results, however, our final result will indicate that when
both
parameters in (
1.1
)
simultaneously diverge, then arbitrarily large population densities may spontaneously emerge in two- and three-dimensional
balls.
To address this in as simple as possible setting, we shall concentrate here on the prototypical case when the large numbers
\alpha
and
\beta
precisely coincide, and hence
consider the one-parameter sub-family of (
1.1
) given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u_{\gamma}=\Delta u_{\gamma}-\nabla\cdot(u_{\gamma}\nabla v_{\gamma})-\gamma u_{\gamma}+\gamma w_{\gamma},&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
\partial_{t}v_{\gamma}=\Delta v_{\gamma}-v_{\alpha\beta}+w_{\gamma},&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
\partial_{t}w_{\gamma}=\Delta w_{\gamma}-\gamma w_{\gamma}+\gamma u_{\gamma},&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{\partial u_{\gamma}}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v_{\gamma}}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial w_{\gamma}}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\ t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
u_{\gamma}(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad v_{\gamma}(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\quad w_{\gamma}(x,0)=w_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

with
\gamma>0
.

Within this context, for some fixed initial data the corresponding solutions can be seen to undergo a quite drastic unboundedness phenomenon, as to be confirmed in Section
4
:

Theorem 1.4

Let
n\in\{2,3\},R>0
and
\Omega=B_{R}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then there exist
T>0
and radially symmetric initial data which satisfy (
1.3
), and which are such that whenever

(\gamma_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\infty)
is unbounded, one can find a subsequence
(\gamma_{j_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with the property
that for the corresponding solutions of (
1.8
) from Proposition
1.1
we have

\|u_{\gamma_{j_{k}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))}\to\infty\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{as }k\to\infty

(1.9)

and

\|w_{\gamma_{j_{k}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))}\to\infty\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{as }k\to\infty.

(1.10)

We remark here that the taxis-driven spontaneous emergence of large densities has been discovered within extreme parameter constellations for several Keller-Segel type systems. However, unlike the majority of preceding literature in this regard
(cf., e.g.
[
15
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
27
]
or
[
28
]
),
the observation that leads to Theorem
1.4
does not refer to parameter limits which are singular in the sense that either fast or slow diffusion limits are involved; indeed, the underlying core mechanism here appears to be more complex, in that the dominance of chemotaxis over diffusion is enforced by an enhanced zero order interaction, despite the marked non-degeneracy of all migration processes.

Numerical motivation

To motivate Propositions
1.1
-
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
we compare numerical solutions of the switching model (
1.8
) with those of the formulation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u_{\infty}=\Delta u_{\infty}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(u_{\infty}\nabla v_{\infty}),&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
\partial_{t}v_{\infty}=\Delta v_{\infty}-v_{\infty}+\frac{1}{2}u_{\infty},&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{\partial u_{\infty}}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v_{\infty}}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\ t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
u_{\infty}(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad v_{\infty}(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

The above can be regarded as the classical companion to (
1.8
), featuring a homogeneous population,
u_{\infty}
, that secretes its own chemoattractant,
v_{\infty}
. A standard linear stability analysis of the uniform steady state solutions for (
2.1
) predicts that self-organisation can occur, given sufficient population mass. However, subsequent dynamics vary according to the dimension,
n
: when
n=1
solutions exist globally-in-time, yet when
n\geq 2
finite-time blow-up can occur. Intuitively, (
2.1
) could conceivably arise in the limit
\gamma\rightarrow\infty
of (
1.8
): switching between chemotaxis-only and secreting-only states becomes instantaneous, effectively generating a single-state population that simultaneously produces the attractant and performs chemotaxis; the half-factors reflect a division of time between the two states.

To substantiate this intuition we perform a numerical exploration for
n=1,2,3
. Generally, numerical studies in
n=2,3
are compromised by computational cost, particularly if high accuracy is required. To circumvent this such that simulations in
n=1,2,3
can be performed to an equivalent degree of resolution, each of the cases
n=2,3
are restricted to an assumed radial symmetry. Specifically, for
n=1
we consider the interval
\Omega=[0,R]
, while in
n=2,3
we consider
\Omega={\cal{B}}_{R}^{n}
(the
n
-dimensional ball of radius

) and radially symmetric initial data, allowing reduction to the radial line
[0,R]
. All simulations utilise the initial distributions
u_{\gamma}(x,0)=w_{\gamma}(x,0)=\rho/2
,
v_{\gamma}(x,0)=\rho(1+0.001e^{-x^{2}})/2
for (
1.8
) or

,

for (

); here,

is used to refer to either the position along the interval

in

, or the radial position in

. The parameter

measures the population mass and linear stability analysis (see
[

]
) can be used to determine the

-space for (

) and

-space for (

) in which the uniform steady state is unstable to the inhomogeneous perturbation and aggregations are predicted to emerge. Regarding the numerical implementation itself, we utilise the

). The above initial conditions bias high density aggregate emergence to the origin, which can then be exploited by performing a spatial discretisation on a non-uniform mesh. Specifically we discretise
[0,R]
into a mesh of

, where

. This concentrates grid points near the origin, where high resolution is desirable for the potentially steep gradients forming at the point of mass concentration. We set

at which the numerical scheme fails.

3.1
Local existence. Basic properties of

]
,

1.3
), and let

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{\alpha\beta}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}\times(0,T_{max}(\alpha,\beta)))\cap C^{2,1}(\overline{\Omega}\times(0,T_{max}(\alpha,\beta))),\\[2.84526pt]

,   then

\displaystyle\hskip 17.07164pt\limsup_{t\nearrow T_{max}(\alpha,\beta)}\Big\{\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}+\|w(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big\}=\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all $q>n$.}

(3.4)

while Lemma

implies that both

\displaystyle(u_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in S}\mbox{ is bounded in $L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))$}

and

\displaystyle(w_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in S}\mbox{ is bounded in $L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))$}.

A straightforward extraction procedure that relies on the Aubin-Lions lemma (
[
23
]
), the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem and the
unboundedness of
S
thus yields
(\gamma_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset S
such that
\gamma_{j}\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
,
and that with some nonnegative functions
u_{\infty},v_{\infty},w_{\infty}
and
z_{\infty}

fulfilling (
4.19
) as well as
u_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))
and
w_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))
,
besides (
4.21
), (
4.22
), (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) we have

u_{\gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}u_{\infty}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptw_{\gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}w_{\infty}\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{in }L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))

(4.28)

as
\gamma=\gamma_{j}\to\infty
.
Since thus, in particular,
w_{\gamma}-u_{\gamma}\rightharpoonup w_{\infty}-u_{\infty}
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))

as
\gamma=\gamma_{j}\to\infty
,
and since on the other hand from Lemma
4.8
we know that
w_{\gamma}-u_{\gamma}\to 0
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))

as
S\ni\gamma\to\infty
, we obtain that necessarily

w_{\infty}=u_{\infty}\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{a.e.~in }\Omega\times(0,T).

(4.29)

Independently, a combination of (
4.24
) with the definition of
(z_{\gamma})_{\gamma>0}
shows that

u_{\gamma}+w_{\gamma}=z_{\gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}z_{\infty}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T))
as
\gamma=\gamma_{j}\to\infty
, and that therefore (
4.28
)
implies that
u_{\infty}+w_{\infty}=z_{\infty}
a.e. in
\Omega\times(0,T)
.
From (
4.29
) it thus follows that
u_{\infty}=w_{\infty}=\frac{1}{2}z_{\infty}
a.e. in
\Omega\times(0,T)
, and that hence,
due to (
4.28
), also (
4.20
) and (
4.23
) hold
as
\gamma=\gamma_{j}\to\infty
.

A derivation of the identities in (
4.26
) and (
4.27
) can therefore be achieved on the basis of (
4.20
)-(
4.25
)
and the corresponding weak formulations associated with (
3.5
) and the second equation in (
1.8
) in a standard manner.

\Box

In fact, any such limit pair solves the considered chemotaxis system even in the classical sense:

Lemma 4.10

Let
n\leq 3
, and suppose that (
4.3
) holds with some
T>0
and some unbounded set
S\subset(0,\infty)
,
Then the functions obtained in Lemma
4.9
have the additional properties that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}z_{\infty}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T])\cap C^{2,1}(\overline{\Omega}\times(0,T))\qquad\mbox{and}\\[2.84526pt]
v_{\infty}\in\bigcap_{q>n}C^{0}([0,T];W^{1,q}(\Omega))\cap C^{2,1}(\overline{\Omega}\times(0,T)),\end{array}\right.

(4.30)

and
(v_{\infty},z_{\infty})
solves

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}z_{\infty}=\Delta z_{\infty}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(z_{\infty}\nabla v_{\infty}),&x\in\Omega,\ t\in(0,T),\\[2.84526pt]
\partial_{t}v_{\infty}=\Delta v_{\infty}-v_{\infty}+\frac{1}{2}z_{\infty},&x\in\Omega,\ t\in(0,T),\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{\partial z_{\infty}}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v_{\infty}}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\ t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
z_{\infty}(x,0)=u_{0}(x)+w_{0}(x),\quad v_{\infty}(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(4.31)

in the classical sense.

Proof.
As Lemma
4.9
ensures that
v_{\infty}
solves the second sub-problem in (
4.31
) in the standard weak sense addressed,
e.g., in
[
14
]
, the Hölder continuity property of
z_{\infty}
asserted by (
4.19
) enables us to employ classical
parabolic regularity theory of Schauder type (
[
14
]
) to conclude that
v_{\infty}
indeed has the smoothness features
in (
4.30
) and solves its respective part in (
4.31
) in the claimed pointwise sense.
The corresponding properties of

can thereupon be verified in a quite similar manner.

As a final ingredient to our proof of Theorem
1.4
, let us perform a simple comparison argument in order to
make sure that all the above statements continue to hold if, instead of (
4.3
), a corresponding bound on the functions

u_{\gamma}
is assumed:

Lemma 4.11

Let
n\leq 3
, and assume that
T>0
and
S\subset(0,\infty)
are such that

\sup_{\gamma\in S}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|u_{\gamma}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty.

(4.32)

Then (
4.3
) holds.

Proof.
In line with (
4.32
), we let
c_{1}>0
be such that
u_{\gamma}\leq c_{1}
in
\Omega\times(0,T)
for all
\gamma\in S
, and writing

c_{2}:=\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
we define
\overline{w}(x,t):=\max\{c_{1}\,,\,c_{2}\}
for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
t\geq 0
.
Then
\overline{w}(x,0)\geq c_{2}\geq\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\geq w_{\gamma}(x,0)
for all
x\in\Omega
, and furthermore the inequality

\overline{w}\geq c_{1}
ensures that

\displaystyle\overline{w}_{t}-\Delta\overline{w}+\gamma\overline{w}-\gamma u_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\gamma\overline{w}-\gamma u_{\gamma}\geq\gamma c_{1}-\gamma u_{\gamma}\geq 0\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{in }\Omega\times(0,T)

according to our choice of
c_{1}
. As clearly
\frac{\partial\overline{w}}{\partial\nu}=0
on
\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty)
, the comparison principle
guarantees that
w_{\gamma}\leq\overline{w}
in
\Omega\times(0,T)
for all
\gamma\in S
, and hence confirms (
4.3
).

\Box

in (

).

Proof
of Theorem
1.4
.

,

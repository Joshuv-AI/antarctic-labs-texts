Derivations, extensions, and rigidity of subalgebras of the Witt algebra
Lucas Buzaglo

Abstract
. Let 𝕜 \Bbbk be an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0. We study some cohomological properties of Lie subalgebras of the Witt algebra W = Der ⁡ ( 𝕜 ⁡ [ t , t − 1 ] ) W=\Der(\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]) and the one-sided Witt algebra W ≥ − 1 = Der ⁡ ( 𝕜 ⁡ [ t ] ) W_{\geq-1}=\Der(\Bbbk[t]) . In the first part of the paper, we consider finite codimension subalgebras of W ≥ − 1 W_{\geq-1} . We compute derivations and one-dimensional extensions of such subalgebras. These correspond to Ext U ⁡ ( L ) 1 ⁡ ( M , L ) \Ext_{U(L)}^{1}(M,L) , where L L is a subalgebra of W ≥ − 1 W_{\geq-1} and M M is a one-dimensional representation of L L . We find that these subalgebras exhibit a kind of rigidity: their derivations and extensions are controlled by the full one-sided Witt algebra. As an application of these computations, we prove that any isomorphism between finite codimension subalgebras of W ≥ − 1 W_{\geq-1} extends to an automorphism of W ≥ − 1 W_{\geq-1} . The second part of the paper is devoted to explaining the observed rigidity. We define a notion of “completely non-split extension” and prove that W ≥ − 1 W_{\geq-1} is the universal completely non-split extension of any of its subalgebras of finite codimension. In some sense, this means that even when studying subalgebras of W ≥ − 1 W_{\geq-1} as abstract Lie algebras, they remember that they are contained in W ≥ − 1 W_{\geq-1} . We also consider subalgebras of infinite codimension, explaining the similarities and differences between the finite and infinite codimension situations. Almost all of the results above are also true for subalgebras of the Witt algebra. We summarise results for W W at the end of the paper.

Introduction

Throughout, we let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0. All vector spaces are
\Bbbk
-vector spaces.

Since its inception in the late 1960s
[
GF68
,
GF69
]
, the study of cohomology of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras has had a rich history. The main focus has been on the Witt algebra and related Lie algebras
[
Gon73
,
FF80
,
DZ98
]
. Another class of Lie algebras that has been of particular interest in this context are
Krichever–Novikov algebras

[
Mil97
,
Wag99
,
Wag12
]
(for the definition and general theory of Krichever–Novikov algebras, see
[
Sch14
]
). Other examples can be found in
[
FL96
,
FM06
]
and in Fuks’s book on the cohomology of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras
[
Fuk86
]
. The computation of these cohomology groups is an interesting and challenging problem with connections to various areas of mathematics and physics, including representation theory and deformation theory.

In this paper, we compute the first cohomology of some infinite-dimensional Lie algebras with coefficients in the adjoint representation, which is closely related to computing derivations of these Lie algebras: for a Lie algebra
L
, we have

H^{1}(L;L)\cong\Der(L)/\Inn(L),

where
\Der(L)
is the space of derivations of
L
and
\Inn(L)
is the space of inner derivations of
L
. The study of derivations of Lie algebras dates back to the pioneering works of Jacobson, Hochschild, Leger, Dixmier, and others
[
Jac37
,
Hoc42
,
Leg53
,
DL57
]
, and continues to be an active area of research to this day (see, for example,
[
GJP11
,
FSW13
,
MSS13
,
SSM17
]
).

Let
W_{\geq-1}=\Der(\Bbbk[t])
be the
one-sided Witt algebra
and let
W=\Der(\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}])
be the
Witt algebra
(or
centerless Virasoro algebra
). These are infinite-dimensional Lie algebras which are naturally modules over
\Bbbk[t]
and
\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]
, respectively. We are interested in infinite-dimensional subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
and
W
. Of particular interest are those subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
and
W
which are also
\Bbbk[t]
- or
\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]
-submodules, which we call
submodule-subalgebras
. They are denoted by
W_{\geq-1}(f)\coloneqq fW_{\geq-1}
and
W(g)\coloneqq gW
for
f\in\Bbbk[t]
and
g\in\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]
. All subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension are “essentially” submodule-subalgebras: if
L
is such a subalgebra, then there exists
f\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
W_{\geq-1}(f)\subseteq L\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))
, where
\rad(f)=\prod_{\xi\in V(f)}(t-\xi)

[
PS23
, Proposition 3.2.7]
. Furthermore, by
[
BB
]
, any infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
is isomorphic to a subalgebra of finite codimension. We can therefore extract a lot of information about infinite-dimensional subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
by first studying submodule-subalgebras.

Of the submodule-subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
, those of the form
W_{\geq n}\coloneqq W_{\geq-1}(t^{n+1})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
are of particular significance and have been extensively studied. For example,
W_{\geq 1}
is sometimes called the
positive Witt algebra
, which appeared in
[
Fia83
]
in the context of deformation theory, where the cohomology group
H^{2}(W_{\geq 1};W_{\geq 1})
was computed. Similar results for the Lie algebras
W_{\geq 2}
and
W_{\geq 3}
were established in
[
FP01
]
and
[
Koc06
]
.

Submodule-subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
and
W
form a large and interesting class of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras of linear growth. More generally, one could study submodule-subalgebras of
\Der(A)
, where
A
is an associative
\Bbbk
-algebra. These first appeared in
[
Gra78
]
, and subsequently in
[
Gra93
]
and
[
Sie96
]
. In particular, Siebert showed that if
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are submodule-subalgebras of
\Der(\Bbbk[X_{1}])
and
\Der(\Bbbk[X_{2}])
, respectively, where
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are irreducible affine varieties, then
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are isomorphic if
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are isomorphic, provided
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
satisfy certain additional properties
[
Sie96
, Theorem 3]
.

We start off by computing derivations of infinite-dimensional subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
.

Theorem 0.1

(Theorems
2.2
and
6.7
)
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
. Then

\Der(L)=\{\ad_{w}\mid w\in W_{\geq-1}\text{ such that }\ad_{w}(L)\subseteq L\}.

In other words, derivations of
L
are restrictions of derivations of
W_{\geq-1}
. Consequently,
H^{1}(L;L)\cong N_{W_{\geq-1}}(L)/L
, where
N_{W_{\geq-1}}(L)
is the normaliser of
L
in
W_{\geq-1}
.

In particular,

\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f))=\{\ad_{w}\mid w\in W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))\}\cong W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))

for
f\in\Bbbk[t]
, where
\rad(f)=\prod_{\xi\in V(f)}(t-\xi)
. As a result,

\dim H^{1}(W_{\geq-1}(f);W_{\geq-1}(f))=\deg(f)-\deg(\rad(f)).

For a Lie algebra
L
, we have
H^{1}(L;L)=\Ext_{U(L)}^{1}(\Bbbk,L)
, where
\Bbbk
is the one-dimensional trivial representation of
L
. We can generalise Theorem
0.1
by computing

\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}^{1}(M,W_{\geq-1}(f)),

where
f\in\Bbbk[t]
and
M
is an arbitrary one-dimensional representation of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
. An element of
\Ext_{U(L)}^{1}(M,L)
can be viewed as a non-split short exact sequence of
L
-representations

0\to L\to\overline{L}\to M\to 0.

We study such short exact sequences in Section
3
for
L
a subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension. In particular, we prove that
\overline{L}
can be canonically embedded in
W_{\geq-1}
(see Proposition
3.7
).

The above results suggest that subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension are “rigid”, in the sense that they inherit their properties from
W_{\geq-1}
. We explain this by showing that
W_{\geq-1}
can be intrinsically built from any of its subalgebras of finite codimension as the universal
completely non-split extension
, which is an abstract Lie-algebraic property (see Definition
5.1
).

Theorem 0.2

(Theorem
5.3
)
.

Let
L
be a subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension. Then
W_{\geq-1}
is the universal completely non-split extension of
L
, in the following sense:
W_{\geq-1}
is a completely non-split extension of
L
, and if
\overline{L}
is another completely non-split extension of
L
, then
\overline{L}
can be uniquely embedded in
W_{\geq-1}
such that the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{\geq-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{L}}

commutes.

Given Siebert’s result, the rigidity noted above, and the close relationship between subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension and submodule-subalgebras, it is natural to ask whether any isomorphism between subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension extends to an automorphism of
W_{\geq-1}
. If we knew of this universal property in advance, this would be immediate. In fact, we do prove this on the way to proving the universal property.

Theorem 0.3

(Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension and suppose there is an isomorphism of Lie algebras
\varphi\colon L_{1}\to L_{2}
. Then
\varphi
extends to an automorphism of
W_{\geq-1}
.

The idea of the proof of Theorem
0.3
is as follows: let
\varphi\colon L_{1}\to L_{2}
be an isomorphism between proper subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension. We show that there exist subalgebras
\overline{L}_{i}
of
W_{\geq-1}
with
L_{i}\subseteq\overline{L}_{i}\subseteq W_{\geq-1}
and
\dim(\overline{L}_{i}/L_{i})=1
for
i=1,2
such that
\varphi
extends to an isomorphism
\overline{\varphi}\colon\overline{L}_{1}\to\overline{L}_{2}
. The proof then follows by induction on
\codim_{W_{\geq-1}}(L_{1})
.

In order to prove the existence of the isomorphism
\overline{\varphi}\colon\overline{L}_{1}\to\overline{L}_{2}
, we apply our study of non-split short exact sequences of the form

0\to L\to\overline{L}\to\overline{L}/L\to 0

where
L
is a subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension and
\dim(\overline{L}/L)=1
. In other words, Theorem
0.3
follows from the computation of
\Ext_{U(L)}^{1}(M,L)
, where
M
is a one-dimensional representation of
L
.

We now explain the structure and proofs of the paper in more detail. There are two main goals: the first is to compute
\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}^{1}(M,W_{\geq-1}(f))
, where
f
and
M
are as above, and the second is to provide an explanation for the observed rigidity.

Sections
1
and
2
are devoted to computing derivations of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
for
f\in\Bbbk[t]
, which corresponds to the case where
M=\Bbbk
is the trivial representation. This is because

\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}^{1}(\Bbbk,W_{\geq-1}(f))=H^{1}(W_{\geq-1}(f);W_{\geq-1}(f))\cong\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f))/\Inn(W_{\geq-1}(f))

is the first cohomology of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
with coefficients in the adjoint representation.

It is easy to show that all derivations of
W_{\geq-1}
are inner. Given the rigidity of the situation, it is natural to ask if derivations of submodule-subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
also arise from the adjoint action of elements of
W_{\geq-1}
, that is, if

\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f))=\{\ad_{w}\mid w\in W_{\geq-1}\text{ and }\ad_{w}(W_{\geq-1}(f))\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(f)\},

where
f\in\Bbbk[t]
. It is easy to see that
\ad_{w}(W_{\geq-1}(f))\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(f)
if and only if
w\in W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))
, where
\rad(f)=\prod\{t-\xi\mid f(\xi)=0\}
. Therefore, the question is equivalent to asking whether
\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f))=\{\ad_{w}\mid w\in W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))\}
, which is precisely what Theorem
0.1
says.

As the Lie algebra
W_{\geq-1}
is graded, the proof of Theorem
0.1
starts by considering derivations of graded submodule-subalgebras, that is, Lie algebras of the form
W_{\geq n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For this situation, we can use results from
[
Far88
]
. In particular, the space of derivations of a graded Lie algebra is itself graded, which greatly simplifies our computations. However, computing derivations of ungraded submodule-subalgebras requires more work. Since submodule-subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
are filtered, this is achieved by an associated graded argument, allowing us to compute derivations of ungraded submodule-subalgebras from derivations of graded ones.

In Section
3
, we move on to computing
\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}^{1}(M,W_{\geq-1}(f))
, where
f\in\Bbbk[t]
and
M
is an arbitrary one-dimensional representation of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
. A key observation is that the derived subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
is
W_{\geq-1}(f^{2})
, so the action of
W_{\geq-1}(f^{2})
on any one-dimensional representation of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
is trivial. Consequently, there are many one-dimensional representations of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
, corresponding to the representations of the abelian Lie algebra
W_{\geq-1}(f)/W_{\geq-1}(f^{2})
. In other words, the one-dimensional representations of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
are parametrised by
\mathbb{A}^{\deg(f)}
. However, the only one-dimensional modules
M
which give rise to non-split extensions

0\to W_{\geq-1}(f)\to L\to M\to 0

are of the form
M=\Bbbk
or
M=W_{\geq-1}/W_{\geq-1}(t-\xi)
for
\xi\in V(f)
a root of order 1. The
U(W_{\geq-1}(f))
-module structure on
W_{\geq-1}/W_{\geq-1}(t-\xi)
is induced from
W_{\geq-1}(f)
acting by its adjoint action on both
W_{\geq-1}
and
W_{\geq-1}(t-\xi)
, noting that
W_{\geq-1}(f)
is a Lie subalgebra of both
W_{\geq-1}
and
W_{\geq-1}(t-\xi)
.

Theorem 0.4

(Theorem
3.3
)
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]
. If
M\cong W_{\geq-1}/W_{\geq-1}(t-\xi)
for some
\xi\in\Bbbk
a root of
f
of multiplicity 1, then

\dim(\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}^{1}(W_{\geq-1}/W_{\geq-1}(t-\xi),W_{\geq-1}(f)))=1.

In this case, the unique non-split extension of
W_{\geq-1}/W_{\geq-1}(t-\xi)
by
W_{\geq-1}(f)
is
W_{\geq-1}(\frac{f}{t-\xi})
. If
M\cong\Bbbk
is trivial, we have

\dim(\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f)}^{1}(\Bbbk,W_{\geq-1}(f)))=\deg(f)-\deg(\rad(f)).

Otherwise,
\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}^{1}(M,W_{\geq-1}(f))=0
.

Although it is not immediately obvious, the proof is closely related to the computation of derivations of submodule-subalgebras: we consider a short exact sequence of
U(W_{\geq-1}(f))
-modules

0\to W_{\geq-1}(f)\to L\to M\to 0

as a sequence of
U(W_{\geq-1}(f^{2}))
-modules. Since
M
is trivial as a representation of
W_{\geq-1}(f^{2})
, the short exact sequence gives an element of

\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f^{2}))}^{1}(\Bbbk,W_{\geq-1}(f))\cong\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f^{2}),W_{\geq-1}(f))/\Inn(W_{\geq-1}(f^{2}),W_{\geq-1}(f)),

which can be computed using the techniques from Theorem
0.1
.

In Section
5
, we prove Theorem
0.2
, providing an explanation for the aforementioned rigidity. We first show that
W_{\geq-1}
is a completely non-split extension of any of its subalgebras of finite codimension, which we achieve by proving that any such subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
has a one-dimensional extension contained in
W_{\geq-1}
. We then prove the universal property using results from Section
3
about one-dimensional extensions of subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
.

We then briefly consider subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
of infinite codimension. Any infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
is isomorphic to a subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension
[
BB
]
, so our results in finite codimension can be translated to infinite codimension. However, there are also some key differences when it comes to the universal property, which we highlight at the end of Section
5
.

We conclude the paper by establishing analogous results to Theorems
0.1
–
0.4
for submodule-subalgebras of the Witt algebra. The proofs for submodule-subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
go through for
W
with the obvious changes. In fact, the situation for the Witt algebra is slightly easier: the associated graded algebra to
W(f)
is
W
, where
f\in\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]
. Consequently, in order to use the associated graded argument to compute derivations of
W(f)
, we only need to consider derivations of
W
. Thanks to results from
[
Far88
]
, it is easy to see that
\Der(W)=\Inn(W)
.

1.
Derivations of graded submodule-subalgebras

We begin by recalling the notions of derivations of algebras, and defining the Lie algebras of interest in this paper.

Definition 1.1
.

A
derivation
of a
\Bbbk
-algebra
A
is a
\Bbbk
-linear map
d\colon A\to A
such that

d(ab)=ad(b)+d(a)b

for
a,b\in A
. We write
\Der(A)
for the set of derivations of
A
.

We write
W_{\geq-1}=\Der(\Bbbk[t])=\Bbbk[t]\partial
and
W=\Der(\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}])=\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]\partial
for the
one-sided Witt algebra
and the
Witt algebra
, respectively, where
\partial=\frac{d}{dt}
. The Witt algebra is sometimes called the
centerless Virasoro algebra
.

We let
e_{n}=t^{n+1}\partial\in W
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The Lie bracket is given by

[f\partial,g\partial]=(fg^{\prime}-f^{\prime}g)\partial,\quad[e_{n},e_{m}]=(m-n)e_{n+m},

where
f,g\in\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]
and
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Note that
W_{\geq-1}
is spanned by
\{e_{n}\mid n\geq-1\}
.

For
f\in\Bbbk[t]
, we write
W_{\geq-1}(f)=fW_{\geq-1}=f\Bbbk[t]\partial
under the obvious notation. It is clear that
W_{\geq-1}(f)
is a subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
, as well as a
\Bbbk[t]
-submodule. We call
W_{\geq-1}(f)
a
submodule-subalgebra
of
W_{\geq-1}
. We write
W_{\geq n}
for
W_{\geq-1}(t^{n+1})
.

We will also consider the Lie algebra
\Der(\Bbbk(t))=\Bbbk(t)\partial
, and view
W_{\geq-1}
and
W
as subalgebras of
\Bbbk(t)\partial
.

Our main goal in the first part of the paper is to compute
\Ext^{1}_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}(M,W_{\geq-1}(f))
, where
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
and
M
is a one-dimensional
U(W_{\geq-1}(f))
-module. We devote the first two sections to studying the easiest case, where
M=\Bbbk
is the trivial one-dimensional representation of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
. In this case,
\Ext^{1}_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}(\Bbbk,W_{\geq-1}(f))
is a well-known object.

Definition 1.2
.

For a Lie algebra
L
and a representation
M
of
L
, the
cohomology of
L
with values in
M
, denoted
H^{n}(L;M)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is defined as

H^{n}(L;M)=\Ext^{n}_{U(L)}(\Bbbk,M).

We are therefore interested in computing
H^{1}(W_{\geq-1}(f);W_{\geq-1}(f))
for
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
. It is a standard fact that, for a Lie algebra
L
and a representation
M
of
L
, the cohomology group
H^{1}(L;M)
is isomorphic to the quotient
\Der(L,M)/\Inn(L,M)
, defined below.

Definition 1.3
.

A
derivation
of a Lie algebra
L
with values in an
L
-module
M
is a linear map
d\colon L\to M
such that

d([x,y])=x\cdot d(y)-y\cdot d(x)

for
x,y\in L
. We write
\Der(L,M)
for the set of derivations
L
with values in
M
, and simply write
\Der(L)
instead of
\Der(L,L)
. A derivation
d\in\Der(L,M)
is
inner
if there exists
m\in M
such that
d(x)=x\cdot m
. We write
\Inn(L,M)
for the set of inner derivations of
L
with values in
M
. As before, we write
\Inn(L)
instead of
\Inn(L,L)
.

If
L=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{n}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie algebra and
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded representation of
L
, then for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we write

\Der(L,M)_{k}=\{d\in\Der(L,M)\mid d(L_{n})\subseteq M_{n+k}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

If
d\in\Der(L,M)_{k}
, we say that
d
is a
graded derivation of degree
k
.

If
L
is a Lie algebra and
M
is a representation of
L
, we can construct elements of
\Ext_{U(L)}^{1}(\Bbbk,M)
from derivations of
L
with values in
M
as follows: given a derivation
d\in\Der(L,M)
, consider the vector space
X=M\oplus\Bbbk d
with
L
-action given by the usual action of
L
on
M
and
w\cdot d=-d(w)
for
w\in L
. Note that
X/M
is trivial as a representation of
L
, so we get a short exact sequence of representations of
L
given by

(1.3.1)

0\to M\to X\to\Bbbk\to 0.

Furthermore, we easily see that (
1.3.1
) splits if and only if
d\in\Inn(L,M)
.

We therefore see that computing
H^{1}(W_{\geq-1}(f);W_{\geq-1}(f))=\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}^{1}(\Bbbk,W_{\geq-1}(f))
is equivalent to computing derivations of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
. Thanks to the following result of Farnsteiner, studying derivations of graded Lie algebras is easier than ungraded ones, since we can exploit the graded structure of the space of derivations.

Proposition 1.4

(
[
Far88
, Proposition 1.1]
)
.

If
L=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{n}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded finitely generated Lie algebra and
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded representation of
L
, then

\Der(L,M)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Der(L,M)_{n}.

The Lie algebras
W
and
W_{\geq-1}
are
\mathbb{Z}
-graded, with
e_{n}
having degree
n
. However, submodule-subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
are not graded in general: the graded ones are
W_{\geq n}
for
n\geq-1
. Still, ungraded submodule-subalgebras are filtered, with associated graded algebras isomorphic to subalgebras of the form
W_{\geq n}
. It is therefore natural to begin our study of derivations of submodule-subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
by considering the graded ones, and later attempt to extract as much information as we can for general submodule-subalgebras via an associated graded argument.

The goal for this section is to compute
\Der(W_{\geq n},W)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where we view
W
as a representation of
W_{\geq-1}
under the adjoint action. We start with the cases
n=-1,0,1
. The computation of derivations of
W_{\geq-1}
was already a well-known folklore result, but we still give a proof here. However, derivations of
W_{\geq n}
for arbitrary
n
were not known before.

Proposition 1.5
.

We have
\Der(W_{\geq-1},W)=\Inn(W_{\geq-1},W)
. Consequently,
\Der(W_{\geq-1})=\Inn(W_{\geq-1})\cong W_{\geq-1}
and
H^{1}(W_{\geq-1};W_{\geq-1})=0
.

We will use a theorem of Farnsteiner to prove Proposition
1.5
.

Theorem 1.6

(
[
Far88
, Proposition 1.2]
)
.

Let
L=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{n}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded finitely generated Lie algebra and let
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}
be a graded representation of
L
such that

(1)

H^{1}(L_{0};M_{n})=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.

(2)

\Hom_{L_{0}}(L_{n},M_{m})=0
for
n\neq m
.

Then
\Der(L,M)=\Der(L,M)_{0}+\Inn(L,M)
.

In order to compute
\Der(W_{\geq-1},W)
, we show that
\Der(W_{\geq-1},W)_{0}\subseteq\Inn(W_{\geq-1},W)
. It will then be clear that
\Der(W_{\geq-1},W)=\Inn(W_{\geq-1},W)
, provided the conditions of Theorem
1.6
are satisfied.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
n\geq-1
. We have
\Der(W_{\geq n},W)_{0}=\Bbbk\ad_{e_{0}}\subseteq\Inn(W_{\geq-1},W)
.

Proof.

Let
d\in\Der(W_{\geq-1},W)_{0}
. Then for all
n\geq-1
there exists
\lambda_{n}\in\Bbbk
such that
d(e_{n})=\lambda_{n}e_{n}
. We have

d([e_{n},e_{m}])=[e_{n},d(e_{m})]+[d(e_{n}),e_{m}]=(\lambda_{n}+\lambda_{m})[e_{n},e_{m}]=(m-n)(\lambda_{n}+\lambda_{m})e_{n+m}.

On the other hand,

d([e_{n},e_{m}])=(m-n)d(e_{n+m})=(m-n)\lambda_{n+m}e_{n+m}.

Combining the above, we conclude that
\lambda_{n}+\lambda_{m}=\lambda_{n+m}
for all
n\neq m
. Now,
\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{1}
, so
\lambda_{0}=0
. Furthermore,
\lambda_{-1}+\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{0}=0
, so
\lambda_{-1}=-\lambda_{1}
.

Now consider
2\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3}
:

2\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}=\lambda_{5}=\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}.

Hence,
\lambda_{2}=2\lambda_{1}
. Inductively, we deduce that
n\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{n}
for all
n\geq-1
. Letting
\lambda=\lambda_{1}
, it follows that
d=\lambda\ad_{e_{0}}\in\Inn(W_{\geq-1},W)
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

By Lemma
1.7
, it suffices to check the conditions for Theorem
1.6
are satisfied when
L=W_{\geq-1}
and
M=W
.

First, we must show that

H^{1}(\Bbbk e_{0};\Bbbk e_{n})\cong\Der(\Bbbk e_{0},\Bbbk e_{n})/\Inn(\Bbbk e_{0},\Bbbk e_{n})=0

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. We have

\Inn(\Bbbk e_{0},\Bbbk e_{n})=\Bbbk\ad_{e_{n}}\subseteq\Der(\Bbbk e_{0},\Bbbk e_{n})\subseteq\Hom_{\Bbbk}(\Bbbk e_{0},\Bbbk e_{n}).

By a dimension count,
H^{1}(\Bbbk e_{0};\Bbbk e_{n})=0
.

To end the proof, we can easily see that
\Hom_{\Bbbk e_{0}}(\Bbbk e_{n},\Bbbk e_{m})=0
for
n\neq m
, where
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-1}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, since
e_{n}
and
e_{m}
have different eigenvalues with respect to the action of
e_{0}
.
∎

The computation of derivations of
W_{\geq 0}
is almost identical to derivations of
W_{\geq-1}
. We therefore omit the proof.

Proposition 1.8
.

We have
\Der(W_{\geq 0},W)=\Inn(W_{\geq 0},W)
. Consequently,
\Der(W_{\geq 0})=\Inn(W_{\geq 0})\cong W_{\geq 0}
and
H^{1}(W_{\geq 0};W_{\geq 0})=0
. ∎

Proposition
1.8
shows that
\Ext_{U(W_{\geq 0})}^{1}(\Bbbk,W_{\geq 0})=0
. However,
W_{\geq 0}
does have nontrivial extensions by one-dimensional representations: consider the short exact sequence

(1.8.1)

0\to W_{\geq 0}\to W_{\geq-1}\to W_{\geq-1}/W_{\geq 0}\to 0.

This is because the
U(W_{\geq 0})
-module
W_{\geq-1}/W_{\geq 0}
is not the trivial one-dimensional module, since
e_{0}
acts as
-\id
. In fact, we will see later that
\dim(\Ext_{U(W_{\geq 0})}^{1}(W_{\geq-1}/W_{\geq 0},W_{\geq 0}))=1
and that (
1.8.1
) is the unique nontrivial one-dimensional extension of
W_{\geq 0}
(see Theorem
3.3
).

Computing
\Der(W_{\geq 1},W)
requires more work, since
e_{0}\not\in W_{\geq 1}
, so we cannot use Theorem
1.6
. Instead, we can use the graded structure of
\Der(W_{\geq 1})
from Proposition
1.4
.

Proposition 1.9
.

We have
\Der(W_{\geq 1},W)=\Inn(W_{\geq 1},W)
. Consequently,
\Der(W_{\geq 1})=\{\ad_{w}\mid w\in W_{\geq 0}\}\cong W_{\geq 0}
and
\dim(H^{1}(W_{\geq 1};W_{\geq 1}))=1
.

Proof.

By Proposition
1.4
, it suffices to prove that
\Der(W_{\geq 1},W)_{n}\subseteq\Inn(W_{\geq 1},W)
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The same proof as in Lemma
1.7
works to show that
\Der(W_{\geq 1},W)_{0}=\Bbbk\ad_{e_{0}}
.

We start by considering
\Der(W_{\geq 1},W)_{1}
. Note that, since
W_{\geq 1}
is generated by
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
as a Lie algebra, any derivation
d\in\Der(W_{\geq 1},W)
is uniquely determined by
d(e_{1})
and
d(e_{2})
. Let
d\in\Der(W_{\geq 1},W)_{1}
, so
d(e_{1})=\lambda e_{2}
and
d(e_{2})=\mu e_{3}
for some
\lambda,\mu\in\Bbbk
. Let
d^{\prime}=d-\mu\ad_{e_{1}}\in\Der(W_{\geq 1},W)
, so that
d^{\prime}(e_{1})=\lambda e_{2}
and
d^{\prime}(e_{2})=0
. We have

\displaystyle d^{\prime}(e_{3})

\displaystyle=d^{\prime}([e_{1},e_{2}])=[e_{1},d^{\prime}(e_{2})]+[d^{\prime}(e_{1}),e_{2}]=\lambda[e_{2},e_{2}]=0,

\displaystyle d^{\prime}(e_{4})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}d^{\prime}([e_{1},e_{3}])=\frac{1}{2}([e_{1},d^{\prime}(e_{3})]+[d^{\prime}(e_{1}),e_{3}])=\frac{1}{2}\lambda[e_{2},e_{3}]=\frac{1}{2}\lambda e_{5},

\displaystyle d^{\prime}(e_{5})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}d^{\prime}([e_{1},e_{4}])=\frac{1}{3}([e_{1},d^{\prime}(e_{4})]+[d^{\prime}(e_{1}),e_{4}])=\frac{1}{3}(\frac{1}{2}\lambda[e_{1},e_{5}]+\lambda[e_{2},e_{4}])=\frac{4}{3}\lambda e_{6}.

On the other hand,

d^{\prime}(e_{5})=d^{\prime}([e_{2},e_{3}])=[e_{2},d^{\prime}(e_{3})]+[d^{\prime}(e_{2}),e_{3}]=0.

Therefore,
\lambda=0
, so
d^{\prime}=0
. It follows that
d=\mu\ad_{e_{1}}
, so
\Der(W_{\geq 1},W)_{1}\subseteq\Inn(W_{\geq 1},W)
.

Now let
d\in\Der(W_{\geq 1},W)_{2}
, so
d(e_{1})=\lambda e_{3}
and
d(e_{2})=\mu e_{4}
for some
\lambda,\mu\in\Bbbk
. Consider the subalgebra
V_{2}
of
W_{\geq-1}
spanned by
e_{2},e_{4},e_{6},\ldots
. Certainly,
d
restricts to a derivation of
V_{2}
. Note that
V_{2}
is isomorphic to
W_{\geq 1}
via the map
e_{2n}\mapsto 2e_{n}
. Under this isomorphism,
d
induces a derivation
\widetilde{d}
of
W_{\geq 1}
of degree 1. By the above computation of
\Der(W_{\geq 1},W)_{1}
, we know that
\widetilde{d}
is a scalar multiple of
\ad_{e_{1}}
, and therefore
{\left.\kern-1.2ptd\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{V_{2}}}
is a scalar multiple of
\ad_{e_{2}}
. In particular, we see that
d(e_{2})=0
, so that
\mu=0
. It follows that
d=-\lambda\ad_{e_{2}}
, so
\Der(W_{\geq 1},W)_{2}\subseteq\Inn(W_{\geq 1},W)
.

Finally, let
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,1,2\}
and consider
d\in\Der(W_{\geq 1},W)_{n}
. Then
d(e_{1})=\lambda e_{n+1}
and
d(e_{2})=\mu e_{n+2}
for some
\lambda,\mu\in\Bbbk
. Taking Lie brackets up to
e_{5}
and using the Leibniz rule for
d
as above, we get

(\lambda-\mu)n^{3}-\lambda n^{2}+(4\lambda-5\mu)n-12\lambda+6\mu=0.

Let
\alpha=\frac{1}{n-1}\lambda
and
\beta=\frac{1}{n-2}\mu
. Substituting
\alpha
and
\beta
into the equation above,

(n^{4}-2n^{3}+5n^{2}-16n+12)(\alpha-\beta)=0.

The quartic equation in
n
does not vanish for
n\geq 3
(its only integer solutions are
n=1,2
), so we conclude that
\alpha=\beta
. Therefore,
d(e_{1})=\alpha(n-1)e_{n+1}
and
d(e_{2})=\alpha(n-2)e_{n+2}
. It follows that
d=-\alpha\ad_{e_{n}}
, so
\Der(W_{\geq 1},W)_{n}\subseteq\Inn(W_{\geq 1},W)
for all
n\geq 3
. This finishes the proof.
∎

We now move on to computing
\Der(W_{\geq n},W)
for
n\geq 2
.

Proposition 1.10
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\Der(W_{\geq n},W)=\Inn(W_{\geq n},W)
. Consequently,
\Der(W_{\geq n})=\{\ad_{w}\mid w\in W_{\geq 0}\}\cong W_{\geq 0}
and
\dim(H^{1}(W_{\geq n};W_{\geq n}))=n
.

In order to prove Proposition
1.10
, it suffices to compute graded derivations of
W_{\geq n}
, by Proposition
1.4
. The idea of the proof is similar to that of Proposition
1.5
. In that proof, we used the fact that we can take brackets up to
e_{5}
in two different ways, which puts restrictions on the space of derivations of
W_{\geq 1}
. In other words, we exploited the relation

[e_{1},[e_{1}[e_{1},e_{2}]]]+6[e_{2},[e_{1},e_{2}]]=0

in
W_{\geq 1}
. For the proof of Proposition
1.10
, we first consider derivations of
W_{\geq n}
of degree 0 and then find a relation in
W_{\geq n}
. It is not difficult to show that any derivation of
W_{\geq n}
of degree 0 must be a multiple of
\ad_{e_{0}}
.

Lemma 1.11
.

Letting
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\Der(W_{\geq n},W)_{0}=\Bbbk\ad_{e_{0}}
.

Proof.

The result has already been shown for
n=0,1
, so we may assume that
n\geq 2
.

Let
d\in\Der(W_{\geq n},W)_{0}
. Then for all
k\geq n
there exists
\lambda_{k}\in\Bbbk
such that
d(e_{k})=\lambda_{k}e_{k}
. For all
\ell\geq n
, we have

d([e_{k},e_{\ell}])=[e_{k},d(e_{\ell})]+[d(e_{k}),e_{\ell}]=(\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{\ell})[e_{k},e_{\ell}]=(\ell-k)(\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{\ell})e_{k+\ell}.

On the other hand,

d([e_{k},e_{\ell}])=(\ell-k)d(e_{k+\ell})=(\ell-k)\lambda_{k+\ell}e_{k+\ell}.

Combining the above, we conclude that
\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{\ell}=\lambda_{k+\ell}
for all
k\neq\ell
.

Consider
2\lambda_{k}
for some
k\geq n
. Let
\ell>2k
. Then

2\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{\ell}=\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{k+\ell}=\lambda_{2k+\ell}=\lambda_{2k}+\lambda_{\ell}.

Therefore,
2\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{2k}
. Inductively, we deduce that
m\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{mk}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now let
\mu=\lambda_{n}+\frac{\lambda_{n}}{n}
. Then

n\mu=\lambda_{n}+n\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{n}+\lambda_{n^{2}}=\lambda_{n(n+1)}=n\lambda_{n+1},

so
\mu=\lambda_{n+1}
. Now, we have
\lambda_{n}+\frac{\lambda_{n}}{n}=\lambda_{n+1}
, so

\frac{\lambda_{n}}{n}=\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n},

and thus
\lambda_{n}=n(\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n})
. Let
\lambda=\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}
. We claim that
d=\lambda\ad_{e_{0}}
.

Then, for
m\geq n
,

\lambda_{m}+\lambda=\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{n+m+1}-\lambda_{n}.

But we also know that
\lambda_{n+m+1}=\lambda_{m+1}+\lambda_{n}
, so

\lambda_{m}+\lambda=\lambda_{m+1}+\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{m+1}.

Since
\lambda_{n}=n\lambda
and
\lambda_{m+1}=\lambda_{m}+\lambda
for all
m\geq n
, it follows by induction that
\lambda_{m}=m\lambda
for all
m\geq n
, proving the claim.
∎

We are nearly ready to prove Proposition
1.10
: we only need to find a relation in
W_{\geq n}
.

Lemma 1.12
.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
. We have the following relation on
e_{n}
and
e_{m}
:

nm[[e_{n},e_{m}],[[e_{n},e_{m}],[[e_{n},e_{m}],[e_{m},[e_{n},[e_{n},e_{m}]]]]]]\\
+3(m-n)(n+m)[[e_{m},[e_{n},[e_{n},e_{m}]]],[[e_{m},[e_{n},[e_{n},e_{m}]]],[e_{n},e_{m}]]]=0.

Proof.

We have

(1.12.1)

\displaystyle[e_{n},e_{m}]

\displaystyle=(m-n)e_{n+m},

\displaystyle[e_{n},[e_{n},e_{m}]]=(m-n)[e_{n},e_{n+m}]

\displaystyle=(m-n)me_{2n+m},

(1.12.2)

\displaystyle[e_{m},[e_{n},[e_{n},e_{m}]]]=(m-n)m[e_{m},e_{2n+m}]

\displaystyle=2(m-n)nme_{2n+2m}.

We can easily find a relation between
e_{n+m}
and
e_{2n+2m}
:

(1.12.3)

\displaystyle[e_{n+m},[e_{n+m},[e_{n+m},e_{2n+2m}]]]

\displaystyle=6(n+m)^{3}e_{5n+5m},

(1.12.4)

\displaystyle[e_{2n+2m},[e_{2n+2m},e_{n+m}]]

\displaystyle=-(n+m)^{2}e_{5n+5m}.

Therefore,
[e_{n+m},[e_{n+m},[e_{n+m},e_{2n+2m}]]]+6(n+m)[e_{2n+2m},[e_{2n+2m},e_{n+m}]]=0
. We can now find a relation between
e_{n}
and
e_{m}
by substituting (
1.12.1
) and (
1.12.2
) into (
1.12.3
) and (
1.12.4
):

\displaystyle[[e_{n},e_{m}],[[e_{n},e_{m}],[[e_{n},e_{m}],[e_{m},[e_{n},[e_{n},e_{m}]]]]]]

\displaystyle=12nm(m-n)^{4}(n+m)^{3}e_{5n+5m},

\displaystyle[[e_{m},[e_{n},[e_{n},e_{m}]]],[[e_{m},[e_{n},[e_{n},e_{m}]]],[e_{n},e_{m}]]]

\displaystyle=-4n^{2}m^{2}(m-n)^{3}(n+m)^{2}e_{5n+5m}.

The lemma follows immediately.
∎

We can now prove Proposition
1.10
.

Proof of Proposition
1.10
.

The result has already been shown for
n=0,1
, so fix
n\geq 2
. By Lemma
1.11
, we already know that
\Der(W_{\geq n},W)_{0}=\Bbbk\ad_{e_{0}}
. So, let
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
and consider
d\in\Der(W_{\geq n},W)_{k}
. For
m\geq n
, write
d(e_{m})=\lambda_{m}e_{m+k}
, where
\lambda_{m}\in\Bbbk
. We claim that

for some
\alpha\in\Bbbk
, or in other words, that
\lambda_{m}=(m-k)\alpha
for all
m\geq n
.

Letting
r,s\geq n
with

, Lemma
1.12
implies that

d\Bigl(rs[[e_{r},e_{s}],[[e_{r},e_{s}],[[e_{r},e_{s}],[e_{s},[e_{r},[e_{r},e_{s}]]]]]]\\

2.
Derivations of general submodule-subalgebras

We now consider derivations of general submodule-subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
. It is standard that
\Ext
groups of a filtered object are related to
\Ext
groups of the associated graded object (see, for example,
[
Bjö79
, Proposition 2.6.10]
). We will see that this is also the case in our situation: by an associated graded argument, we will be able to extract a lot of information about derivations of ungraded submodule-subalgebras from the results of Section
1
.

We begin with some definitions.

Definition 2.1
.

For an element
w=\sum_{i=m}^{n}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\in W
, where
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n>m
, and
\lambda_{i}\in\Bbbk
with
\lambda_{n}\neq 0
, we call
n
the
degree
of
w
and write
\deg(w)=n
. Furthermore, we call
\lambda_{n}e_{n}
the
leading term
of
w
and write
\LT(w)=\lambda_{n}e_{n}
. Note that if
\deg(u)\neq\deg(v)
, we have
\deg([u,v])=\deg(u)+\deg(v)
, where
u,v\in W
.

For
f=\alpha\prod_{i=0}^{n}(t-\lambda_{i})^{k_{i}}\in\Bbbk[t]
, where
\alpha,\lambda_{i}\in\Bbbk
,
\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{j}
for
i\neq j
and
k_{i}\geq 1
for all
i
, the
radical
of
f
is
\rad(f)=\prod_{i=0}^{n}(t-\lambda_{i})
. If
k_{i}=1
for all
i
, we say
f
is
reduced
. The
vanishing locus
of
f
is
V(f)=\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk(t)\setminus\{0\}
and write
f=\frac{p}{q}
, where
p,q\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
. Let
\xi\in\Bbbk
and write

p=(t-\xi)^{k}r,\quad q=(t-\xi)^{\ell}s,

where
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
r,s\in\Bbbk[t]
do not vanish at
\xi
. The
order of vanishing of
f
at
\xi
is
\ord_{\xi}(f)=k-\ell
. If
\ord_{\xi}(f)<0
, we say
f
has a
pole of order
-\ord_{\xi}(f)
. By convention,
\ord_{\xi}(0)=\infty
for all
\xi\in\Bbbk
. Furthermore, for the derivation
f\partial\in\Der(\Bbbk(t))
, we simply write
\ord_{\xi}(f\partial)=\ord_{\xi}(f)
.

The goal of this section is to compute
\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f))
for
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
, thus generalising Proposition
1.10
to arbitrary submodule-subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
. We have
\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f))=\{\ad_{w}\mid w\in W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))\}
. Consequently,
\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f))\cong W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))
, and

\dim H^{1}(W_{\geq-1}(f);W_{\geq-1}(f))=\deg(f)-\deg(\rad(f)).

Remark 2.3
.

A
complete
Lie algebra is a Lie algebra with trivial center whose derivations are all inner. Theorem
2.2
implies that
W_{\geq-1}(f)
is complete if and only if
f\in\Bbbk[t]
is reduced.

In order to understand derivations of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
, we will construct associated graded derivations, which are graded derivations of
W_{\geq n}
, where
n\coloneqq\deg(f\partial)
. This will essentially be done by only considering the leading terms of elements of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
. The following result is the first step in the construction of associated graded derivations.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
. For
d\in\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f),W)
, the set

\{\deg(d(x))-\deg(x)\mid x\in W_{\geq-1}(f)\}

is bounded above.

Proof.

Let
d\in\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f),W)\setminus\{0\}
. Let
n=\deg(f)
and
x_{i}=t^{i-1}f\partial
, so that
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
generate
W_{\geq-1}(f)
as a Lie algebra. Let

N=\max\{\deg(d(x_{i}))-\deg(x_{i})\mid i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\}.

We claim that
\deg(d(x))-\deg(x)\leq N
for all
x\in W_{\geq-1}(f)
. Note that

d([x,y])=[x,d(y)]+[d(x),y].

Therefore, provided
\deg(d(x))\leq\deg(x)+N
and
\deg(d(y))\leq\deg(y)+N
, we see that
\deg(d([x,y]))\leq\deg([x,y])+N
. The claim now follows by an easy induction on
\deg(x)
.
∎

Lemma
2.4
gives us a notion of degree for derivations
d\in\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f),W)
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
. For
d\in\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f),W)\setminus\{0\}
, the
degree
of
d
is

\deg(d)=\max\{\deg(d(x))-\deg(x)\mid x\in W_{\geq-1}(f)\}.

By convention, the degree of the zero derivation is
-\infty
.

We now consider leading terms of elements
x\in W_{\geq-1}(f)
such that
\deg(d(x))=\deg(x)+\deg(d)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
,
d\in\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f),W)
, and
x\in W_{\geq-1}(f)
such that
\deg(d(x))=\deg(x)+\deg(d)
. Then
\deg(d(y))=\deg(y)+\deg(d)
for all
y\in W_{\geq-1}(f)
such that
\deg(y)=\deg(x)
. Furthermore, if
\LT(x)=\LT(y)
then
\LT(d(x))=\LT(d(y))
.

Proof.

Let
y\in W_{\geq-1}(f)
such that
\deg(y)=\deg(x)
. Rescaling
y
if necessary, we may assume that
\LT(y)=\LT(x)
. Let
z=x-y
, so
\deg(z)<\deg(x)
and
d(y)=d(x)-d(z)
. By definition of
\deg(d)
, we know that

\deg(d(z))\leq\deg(z)+\deg(d)<\deg(x)+\deg(d)=\deg(d(x)).

It follows that
\LT(d(y))=\LT(d(x)-d(z))=\LT(d(x))
and that

\deg(d(y))=\deg(d(x))=\deg(x)+\deg(d)=\deg(y)+\deg(d),

as required.
∎

Given Lemma
2.6
, we can now make the following definition.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
,
d\in\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f),W)
, and
n=\deg(f\partial)
. We say that
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq n}
is
d
-compatible
if
\deg(d(x))=\deg(x)+\deg(d)
for some (and therefore all)
x\in W_{\geq-1}(f)
such that
\deg(x)=k
.

Example 2.8
.

Let
f=t^{2}+1
and let
d=\ad_{e_{5}+e_{3}}\in\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f))
. Then
\deg(d)=5
. Therefore, 5 is not
d
-compatible, while every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\setminus\{5\}
is
d
-compatible.

We now complete the construction of associated graded derivations.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
and
d\in\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f),W)
. Define a
\Bbbk
-linear map
\gr(d)\colon W_{\geq n}\to W
by

\gr(d)(e_{k})=\begin{cases}\LT(d(x)),&\text{where }x\in W_{\geq-1}(f)\text{ and }\LT(x)=e_{k},\text{ if }k\text{ is }d\text{-compatible,}\\
0,&\text{if }k\text{ is not }d\text{-compatible,}\end{cases}

for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq n}
. Then
\gr(d)=\lambda\ad_{e_{N}}\in\Der(W_{\geq n},W)
for some
\lambda\in\Bbbk
and
N\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

First of all, by Lemma
2.6
,
\gr(d)
is well-defined.

If we prove that
\gr(d)\in\Der(W_{\geq n},W)
, then the result will follow from Proposition
1.10
. Therefore, it suffices to show that
\gr(d)
is a derivation. In other words, it is enough to show that

\gr(d)([e_{k},e_{\ell}])=[e_{k},\gr(d)(e_{\ell})]+[\gr(d)(e_{k}),e_{\ell}],

for all
k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq n}
. Let
k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq n}
be distinct, and let
u,v\in W_{\geq-1}(f)
such that
\LT(u)=e_{k}
and
\LT(v)=e_{\ell}
. We have

(2.9.1)

d([u,v])=[u,d(v)]+[d(u),v].

The rest of the proof now follows by a straightforward case-by-case analysis, depending on whether
k
and
\ell
are
d
-compatible. We explicitly show the case where
k
and
\ell
are both
d
-compatible and leave the rest of the cases to the reader. Let
N=\deg(d)
. Since
\deg(d(u))=k+N
, we have
\deg([d(u),v])=k+\ell+N
if
k+N\neq\ell
, but
\deg([d(u),v])<k+\ell+N
if
k+N=\ell
. We will therefore need to divide the proof into two cases.

Case 1:
Either
k+N=\ell
or
\ell+N=k
.

Without loss of generality, say
k+N=\ell
and
\ell+N\neq k
. Then
k+\ell
is
d
-compatible (which can be seen by looking at the degrees in (
2.9.1
)), and
[\gr(d)(e_{k}),e_{\ell}]=[\LT(d(x)),e_{\ell}]=0
, since
\deg(d(x))=k+N=\ell
. Furthermore,

\gr(d)([e_{k},e_{\ell}])=\LT(d([u,v]))=\LT([u,d(v)])=[\LT(u),\LT(d(v))]=[e_{k},\gr(d)(e_{\ell})].

Therefore,
\gr(d)([e_{k},e_{\ell}])=[e_{k},\gr(d)(e_{\ell})]+[\gr(d)(e_{k}),e_{\ell}]
.

Case 2:

k+N\neq\ell
and
\ell+N\neq k
.

We have

\displaystyle[e_{k},\gr(d)(e_{\ell})]+[\gr(d)(e_{k}),e_{\ell}]

\displaystyle=[\LT(u),\LT(d(v))]+[\LT(d(u)),\LT(v)]

\displaystyle=\LT([u,d(v)])+\LT([d(u),v]).

Suppose
\LT([u,d(v)])+\LT([d(u),v])\neq 0
. Then
k+\ell
is
d
-compatible and

[e_{k},\gr(d)(e_{\ell})]+[\gr(d)(e_{k}),e_{\ell}]=\LT([u,d(v)]+[d(u),v])=\LT(d([u,v]))=\gr(d)([e_{k},e_{\ell}]).

Otherwise,
\LT([u,d(v)])+\LT([d(u),v])=0
, in which case
k+\ell
is not
d
-compatible, so

\gr(d)([e_{k},e_{\ell}])=0=[e_{k},\gr(d)(e_{\ell})]+[\gr(d)(e_{k}),e_{\ell}].

This completes the proof.
∎

We now study
\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f),W_{\geq-1})
. This is not strictly necessary to prove Theorem
2.2
, which only considers
\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f))
, but it is not much more difficult than considering
\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f))
, and will be useful for the next section.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
and
d\in\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f),W_{\geq-1})
. Then
d=\ad_{\frac{h}{f}\partial}
for some
h\in\Bbbk[t]
.

Proof.

Consider
\gr(d)\in\Der(W_{\geq n},W_{\geq-1})
. By Proposition
2.9
,
\gr(d)=\lambda_{0}\ad_{e_{k_{0}}}
for some
\lambda_{0}\in\Bbbk
,
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Now let
d_{1}=d-\lambda_{0}\ad_{e_{k_{0}}}\in\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f),W)
and note that
\deg(d_{1})<\deg(d)
. Similarly to before,
\gr(d_{1})=\lambda_{1}\ad_{e_{k_{1}}}
for some
\lambda_{1}\in\Bbbk
and
k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
, so we can consider
d_{2}=d_{1}-\lambda_{1}\ad_{e_{k_{1}}}\in\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f),W)
with
\deg(d_{2})<\deg(d_{1})
. Continuing inductively, we get
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},\ldots\in\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f),W)
such that
\deg(d)>\deg(d_{1})>\deg(d_{2})>\deg(d_{3})>\ldots
, and

d=d_{m+1}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}\lambda_{i}\ad_{e_{k_{i}}},

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, where we set
\lambda_{i}=0
if
d_{i}=0
. Let
g=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}t^{k_{i}+1}\in\Bbbk((t^{-1}))
and consider
\ad_{g\partial}\in\Der(\Bbbk((t^{-1}))\partial)
, so that
d={\left.\kern-1.2pt\ad_{g\partial}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{W_{\geq-1}(f)}}
. In other words,
[g\partial,hf\partial]\in W_{\geq-1}
for all
h\in\Bbbk[t]
. Hence,
[g\partial,f\partial]\in W_{\geq-1}
, so
f^{\prime}g-fg^{\prime}\in\Bbbk[t]
, and thus

tf^{\prime}g-tfg^{\prime}\in\Bbbk[t].

Similarly,
[g\partial,tf\partial]\in W_{\geq-1}
, so

fg+tf^{\prime}g-tfg^{\prime}\in\Bbbk[t].

Combining the above, we conclude that
h\coloneqq fg\in\Bbbk[t]
, so
g=\frac{h}{f}\in\Bbbk(t)
.
∎

If
d\in\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f))
, then Proposition
2.10
tells us that
d=\ad_{g\partial}
for some
g\in\Bbbk(t)
. For the proof of Theorem
2.2
, we need to show that
g\in\rad(f)\Bbbk[t]
, in other words, that
g\in\Bbbk[t]
and that
g
vanishes at all the roots of
f
. In order to achieve this, we will consider orders of vanishing of
[f\partial,g\partial]
at roots of
f
. The following easy lemma computes
\ord_{\xi}([f\partial,g\partial])
for
\xi\in\Bbbk
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\xi\in\Bbbk
,
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
, and
g\in\Bbbk(t)\setminus\{0\}
. Then

\ord_{\xi}([f\partial,g\partial])=\ord_{\xi}(f)+\ord_{\xi}(g)-1

if
\ord_{\xi}(g)\neq\ord_{\xi}(f)
and
\ord_{\xi}([f\partial,g\partial])\geq 2\ord_{\xi}(f)
if
\ord_{\xi}(g)=\ord_{\xi}(f)
.

Proof.

Write
f=(t-\xi)^{k}p
and
g=(t-\xi)^{\ell}q
, where
p\in\Bbbk[t],q\in\Bbbk(t)
and
\ord_{\xi}(p)=\ord_{\xi}(q)=0
, so that
k=\ord_{\xi}(f)
and
\ell=\ord_{\xi}(g)
. Differentiating
f
and
g
, we get

f^{\prime}=(t-\xi)^{k-1}(kp+(t-\xi)p^{\prime}),\quad g^{\prime}=(t-\xi)^{\ell-1}(\ell q+(t-\xi)q^{\prime}).

Therefore,

[f\partial,g\partial]=(fg^{\prime}-f^{\prime}g)\partial=(t-\xi)^{k+\ell-1}((\ell-k)pq+(t-\xi)(pq^{\prime}-p^{\prime}q)).

Let
r=(\ell-k)pq+(t-\xi)(pq^{\prime}-p^{\prime}q)
. It is clear that
\ord_{\xi}(r)=0
if
k\neq\ell
. Therefore,

\ord_{\xi}(fg^{\prime}-f^{\prime}g)=\ord_{\xi}((t-\xi)^{k+\ell-1}r)=k+\ell-1

if
k\neq\ell
. If
k=\ell
, then
\ord_{\xi}(r)\geq 1
, so

\ord_{\xi}(fg^{\prime}-f^{\prime}g)=\ord_{\xi}((t-\xi)^{2k-1}r)\geq 2k,

which concludes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
2.2
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

If
w\in W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))
, then by Lemma
2.11
, it follows that
\ad_{w}\in\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f))
.

Let
d\in\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f))
. By Proposition
2.10
, we know that
d=\ad_{\frac{h}{f}\partial}
for some
h\in\Bbbk[t]
. Let
g=\frac{h}{f}
. Since
[g\partial,f\partial]\in W_{\geq-1}(f)
,
f
divides
f^{\prime}g-fg^{\prime}
. Therefore,

\ord_{\xi}(f^{\prime}g-fg^{\prime})\geq\ord_{\xi}(f)

for all
\xi\in V(f)
. Assume, for a contradiction, that
\ord_{\xi}(g)\leq 0
for some
\xi\in V(f)
. Then, by Lemma
2.11
,

\ord_{\xi}(f)\leq\ord_{\xi}(f^{\prime}g-fg^{\prime})=\ord_{\xi}(f)+\ord_{\xi}(g)-1\leq\ord_{\xi}(f)-1,

a contradiction. Therefore,
\ord_{\xi}(g)\geq 1
for all
\xi\in V(f)
. It follows that
g\in\Bbbk[t]
(since
g
can only have poles at roots of
f
), and that
\rad(f)
divides
g
, so
g\partial\in W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))
.
∎

3.
Extensions of submodule-subalgebras

Having computed
\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}^{1}(M,W_{\geq-1}(f))
for
M=\Bbbk
, we move on to the computation of the
\Ext
group when
M
is an arbitrary one-dimensional representation of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
. We may view an element of
\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}^{1}(M,W_{\geq-1}(f))
as a non-split short exact sequence of
U(W_{\geq-1}(f))
-modules

(3.0.1)

0\to W_{\geq-1}(f)\to X\to M\to 0.

Although it is not immediately obvious, this computation is closely related to the results from previous sections on derivations. This is because
M
is trivial as a representation of
W_{\geq-1}(f^{2})=[W_{\geq-1}(f),W_{\geq-1}(f)]
(see Lemma
3.4
). Therefore, regarding (
3.0.1
) as a sequence of
U(W_{\geq-1}(f^{2}))
-modules, we get an element of

\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f^{2}))}^{1}(\Bbbk,W_{\geq-1}(f))\cong\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f^{2}),W_{\geq-1}(f))/\Inn(W_{\geq-1}(f^{2}),W_{\geq-1}(f))

(recall the isomorphism
\Ext_{U(L)}^{1}(\Bbbk,M)\cong\Der(L,M)/\Inn(L,M)
from the beginning of Section
1
). We can use Proposition
2.10
to compute
\Der(W_{\geq-1}(f^{2}),W_{\geq-1}(f))
, which then allows us to determine the structure of
X
as a representation of
W_{\geq-1}(f^{2})
. This will give a lot of information into the structure of
X
as a representation of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
.

First of all, it is straightforward to show that
X
has the structure of a Lie algebra in a natural way.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
0\to L\to\overline{L}\xrightarrow{\pi}M\to 0
be a short exact sequence of
U(L)
-modules, where
L
is a Lie algebra and
\dim(M)=1
. Writing
\overline{L}=L\oplus\Bbbk x
as a vector space (where
\pi(\Bbbk x)=M
), there is a unique Lie bracket on
\overline{L}
extending the action of
L
on
\overline{L}
. This is given by
[u,v]_{\overline{L}}=[u,v]_{L}
and
[u,x]_{\overline{L}}=u\cdot x
for
u,v\in L
.

Proof.

It suffices to check that

(3.1.1)

[u,[v,x]]+[v,[x,u]]+[x,[u,v]]=0

for
u,v\in L
. We have

[[u,v],x]=[u,v]\cdot x=u\cdot(v\cdot x)-v\cdot(u\cdot x),

since
\overline{L}
is a
U(L)
-module. Therefore,

[[u,v],x]=[u,[v,x]]-[v,[u,x]],

so (
3.1.1
) holds.
∎

Lemma
3.1
says that studying the group
\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}^{1}(W_{\geq-1}(f),M)
, where
M
is a one-dimensional
U(W_{\geq-1}(f))
-module, is essentially the same as studying Lie algebras
\overline{L}
containing
W_{\geq-1}(f)
such that
\dim(\overline{L}/W_{\geq-1}(f))=1
. Thus, we make the following definition.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
L
be a Lie algebra. A
one-dimensional extension
of
L
is a Lie algebra
\overline{L}
containing
L
such that
\dim(\overline{L}/L)=1
and the short exact sequence of
U(L)
-modules

0\to L\to\overline{L}\to\overline{L}/L\to 0

is non-split.

We therefore consider one-dimensional extensions of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
, where
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
. There are two types of “obvious” one-dimensional extensions of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
. Firstly, suppose
f
is non-reduced, so that there exists
w\in W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))\setminus W_{\geq-1}(f)
. Since
W_{\geq-1}(f)
is an ideal of
W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))
, it follows that
\overline{L}\coloneqq W_{\geq-1}(f)\oplus\Bbbk w
is a one-dimensional extension of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
. Note that
\overline{L}/W_{\geq-1}(f)
is trivial as a representation of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
, since
W_{\geq-1}(f)
is an ideal of
\overline{L}
. Hence, this construction corresponds to an element of
\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}^{1}(\Bbbk,W_{\geq-1}(f))
.

The other “obvious” one-dimensional extensions come from removing non-repeated roots of
f
. In other words, if

\xi\in V(f)
with
\ord_{\xi}(f)=1
, then
W_{\geq-1}(\frac{f}{t-\xi})
is a one-dimensional extension of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
which is not contained in
W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))
. In this case, the quotient
W_{\geq-1}(\frac{f}{t-\xi})/W_{\geq-1}(f)
is isomorphic to
W_{\geq-1}/W_{\geq-1}(t-\xi)
as a
U(W_{\geq-1}(f))
-module, where the module structure on
W_{\geq-1}/W_{\geq-1}(t-\xi)
is induced from
W_{\geq-1}(f)
acting on
W_{\geq-1}
and
W_{\geq-1}(t-\xi)
, noting that
W_{\geq-1}(f)
is a subalgebra of both
W_{\geq-1}
and
W_{\geq-1}(t-\xi)
. Therefore, this corresponds to an element of

\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}^{1}(W_{\geq-1}/W_{\geq-1}(t-\xi),W_{\geq-1}(f)).

The main goal of this section is to prove that all one-dimensional extensions of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
are one of these two types.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
and let
M
be a one-dimensional
U(W_{\geq-1}(f))
-module. If
M\cong W_{\geq-1}/W_{\geq-1}(t-\xi)
for some
\xi\in V(f)
with
\ord_{\xi}(f)=1
, then

\dim(\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}^{1}(W_{\geq-1}/W_{\geq-1}(t-\xi),W_{\geq-1}(f)))=1.

In this case, the unique non-split extension of
W_{\geq-1}/W_{\geq-1}(t-\xi)
by
W_{\geq-1}(f)
is
W_{\geq-1}(\frac{f}{t-\xi})
. If
M\cong\Bbbk
is trivial, we have

\dim(\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1}(f))}^{1}(\Bbbk,W_{\geq-1}(f)))=\deg(f)-\deg(\rad(f)).

Otherwise,

Having shown that all one-dimensional extensions of

Proof of Theorem

.

If

Let

3.7

\overline{L}\subseteq W_{\geq-1}

\overline{L}=W_{\geq-1}(\frac{f}{t-\xi})
for some
\xi\in V(f)
with
\ord_{\xi}(f)=1
.

Write
\overline{L}=W_{\geq-1}(f)\oplus\Bbbk g\partial
as a vector space, where

g\partial\in W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))

\overline{L}\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))

by Lemma

. It follows that

\ord_{\xi}(f)=1
.

For

, write

. Evaluating

4.
Isomorphisms between subalgebras of finite codimension

As an application of our results on extensions of subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
, we study isomorphisms between subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension. We prove that any isomorphism between subalgebras of finite codimension extends to an automorphism of
W_{\geq-1}
, which allows us to classify submodule-subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
up to isomorphism and compute automorphism groups of submodule-subalgebras. This will be done by an inductive argument: if we have an isomorphism
\varphi\colon L_{1}\to L_{2}
between subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
, we will show that
\varphi
extends to an isomorphism between one-dimensional extensions of
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension and suppose there is an isomorphism of Lie algebras
\varphi\colon L_{1}\to L_{2}
. Then
\varphi
extends to an automorphism of
W_{\geq-1}
.

In particular, if
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
, then Theorem
4.1
implies that automorphisms of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
are restrictions of automorphisms of
W_{\geq-1}
. This is similar to Theorem
2.2
, which says that derivations of
W_{\geq-1}(f)
are restrictions of derivations of
W_{\geq-1}
.

Theorem
4.1
also implies that if
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are isomorphic subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension, then
\codim_{W_{\geq-1}}(L_{1})=\codim_{W_{\geq-1}}(L_{2})
, which is not obvious a priori.

Remark 4.2
.

Theorem
4.1
is not true for arbitrary infinite-dimensional subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
. Taking
f\in\Bbbk[t]
of degree at least 2, consider the Lie algebra
L(f)
from
[
Buz22
]
(see Notation
6.1
). In particular,
L(f)
has infinite codimension in
W_{\geq-1}
, and there exists
h\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
L(f)\cong W_{\geq-1}(h)
, by
[
Buz22
, Lemma 4.12]
. However, this isomorphism clearly does not extend to an automorphism of
W_{\geq-1}
. In fact, it does not even extend to an endomorphism of
W_{\geq-1}
, since any nonzero endomorphism of
W_{\geq-1}
is an automorphism
[
Du04
]
.

If we have a submodule-subalgebra
W_{\geq-1}(f)
, where
f\in\Bbbk[t]
is non-constant, then we know that
W_{\geq-1}(f)
has one-dimensional extensions (for example, we can take
W_{\geq-1}(\frac{f}{t-\xi})
, where
f(\xi)=0
). However, it is not immediately clear whether an arbitrary subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension has any one-dimensional extensions. Our next goal is to show that any such subalgebra has one-dimensional extensions. We will requite notation for the derived series of a Lie algebra.

Notation 4.3
.

For a Lie algebra
L
, let
D(L)=[L,L]
. We write
D^{0}(L)=L
and

D^{n+1}(L)=D(D^{n}(L))=[D^{n}(L),D^{n}(L)]

for the derived series of
L
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 4.4
.

Analogously to residual nilpotence, we say a Lie algebra
L
is
residually solvable
if
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}D^{n}(L)=0
. By Lemma
3.4
, we can see that if
f\in\Bbbk[t]
is non-constant, then
W_{\geq-1}(f)
is residually solvable.

Note that residual solvability is, in some sense, a weak property. For example, a free Lie algebra is residually solvable. On the other hand,
W_{\geq-1}
is not residually solvable, since
D^{n}(W_{\geq-1})=W_{\geq-1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In fact, we will see that
W_{\geq-1}(f)
satisfies a stronger property defined below, provided
f\in\Bbbk[t]
is non-constant.

Definition 4.5
.

We say that a Lie algebra
L
is
strongly residually solvable
if
L
is residually solvable and every finite-dimensional quotient of
L
is solvable.

Given a subalgebra
L\subseteq W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension, the next result shows that
L
is strongly residually solvable. Lie’s theorem will then allow us to deduce that all finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
L
are one-dimensional.

Lemma 4.6
.

If
L
is a proper subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension then
L
is strongly residually solvable.

Proof.

Let
I
be an ideal of
L
of finite codimension. By Proposition
3.6
, there exist
f,g\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\{0\}
such that

W_{\geq-1}(f)\subseteq L\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f)),\quad W_{\geq-1}(g)\subseteq I\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(\rad(g)).

Since
W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))
is residually solvable, it follows that
L
is also residually solvable. It remains to show that
L/I
is solvable.

Note that
W_{\geq-1}(g)\subseteq I\subseteq L\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))
, so
\rad(f)
divides
\rad(g)
. Suppose
\rad(g)\neq\rad(f)
. Then there exists
\xi\in V(g)\setminus V(f)
. Let
w\in I\setminus\{0\}
such that
\ord_{\xi}(w)
is minimal. Then
[f\partial,w]\in I
, since
f\partial\in L
and
I
is an ideal of
L
. By Lemma
2.11
,

\ord_{\xi}([f\partial,w])=\ord_{\xi}(f)+\ord_{\xi}(w)-1=\ord_{\xi}(w)-1,

contradicting the minimality of
\ord_{\xi}(w)
. Therefore,
\rad(g)=\rad(f)
. In particular, this means that there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
g
divides
\rad(f)^{n}
, in other words, we have
W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f)^{n})\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(g)
. We know that the derived series of
W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))
is

W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))\supseteq W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f)^{2})\supseteq W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f)^{4})\supseteq W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f)^{8})\supseteq\ldots.

Since
L\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f))
, we have
D^{k}(L)\subseteq D^{k}(\rad(f))=W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f)^{2^{k}})
. Let

such that

, so that
W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f)^{2^{m}})\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(\rad(f)^{n})\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(g)

Corollary 4.7

Let

be a subalgebra of

Proof.

Remark 4.8

5.
Universal property

Up until now, all our results suggest that submodule-subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
are very rigid, in the sense that their Lie algebraic properties are controlled by
W_{\geq-1}
. Indeed, we have shown that their derivations and automorphisms are restrictions of derivations and automorphisms of
W_{\geq-1}
, and that their one-dimensional extensions are all contained in
W_{\geq-1}
. Therefore, even when studying submodule-subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
as abstract Lie algebras, they still seem to remember
W_{\geq-1}
.

This rigidity is further demonstrated in
[
PS23
]
, where the authors focused on the Poisson algebra structure of symmetric algebras of various infinite-dimensional Lie algebras. In particular, they show that the only elements of
W_{\geq-1}^{*}
which vanish on a nontrivial Poisson ideal of
S(W_{\geq-1})
are what they call
local functions
; these are functions given by linear combinations of derivatives at a finite set of points. They also show that the same result holds for
S(W_{\geq-1}(f))
, where
f\in\Bbbk[t]
: the only functions in
W_{\geq-1}(f)^{*}
which vanish on a nontrivial Poisson ideal of
S(W_{\geq-1}(f))
are restrictions of local functions.

In this section, we show that
W_{\geq-1}
can be intrinsically reconstructed from any of its subalgebras of finite codimension, purely by considering their Lie algebraic structure. In particular, we prove that
W_{\geq-1}
is, in the appropriate sense, a universal finite-dimensional extension of any of its subalgebras of finite codimension, which provides an explanation for the rigidity observed above.

Our goal is to state and prove the universal property satisfied by
W_{\geq-1}
as an extension of any of its subalgebras of finite codimension. We first define the types of extensions we want to consider.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
L
be a Lie algebra. We say that
\overline{L}
is a
completely non-split extension
of
L
if
\overline{L}
is a Lie algebra containing
L
such that there exists a chain of Lie algebras

L_{0}=L\subseteq L_{1}\subseteq L_{2}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq L_{n}=\overline{L},

where for all
i
, the representation
L_{i+1}/L_{i}
of
L_{i}
is finite-dimensional and irreducible, and the following short exact sequence of
L_{i}
-representations is non-split:

0\to L_{i}\to L_{i+1}\to L_{i+1}/L_{i}\to 0.

Applying Proposition
4.9
inductively, we can easily see that
W_{\geq-1}
is a completely non-split extension of any of its subalgebras of finite codimension.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
L
be a subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension. Then
W_{\geq-1}
is a completely non-split extension of
L
. ∎

Note that the trivial one-dimensional representation is the only irreducible finite-dimensional representation of

, since

is simple. By Proposition

\Ext_{U(W_{\geq-1})}^{1}(\Bbbk,W_{\geq-1})=0,

so

does not have any completely non-split extensions. Given a subalgebra

of

of finite codimension, it is therefore reasonable to expect

to be the unique maximal completely non-split extension of

Thanks to Corollary

is a Lie subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
and
L(f,g)\cong W_{\geq-1}(h)
.

In some sense, these are infinite codimension analogues of submodule-subalgebras: all infinite-dimensional subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
are very similar to subalgebras of the form
L(f,g)
, just like subalgebras of finite codimension are very similar to submodule-subalgebras (cf. Proposition
3.6
).

Theorem 6.3

(
[
BB
]
)
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
. Then there exist
f,g\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
f^{\prime}g\in\Bbbk[f]
and

L(f,g)\subseteq L\subseteq L(f).

In particular,
L
has finite codimension in
L(f)
, so
L
is isomorphic to a subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension.

Theorem
6.3
implies that our results on derivations and extensions can be translated to arbitrary infinite-dimensional subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
. Therefore, there are many similarities between infinite codimension and finite codimension subalgebras. However, there are also some key differences, which we highlight at the end of the section.

For example, Lemma
4.6
and Theorem
6.3
immediately imply:

Corollary 6.4
.

If
L
is a proper infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
, then
L
is strongly residually solvable. ∎

As a consequence of our study of derivations of submodule-subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
, we can deduce that
L(f)
has no outer derivations. In order to see this, we require the following result, which shows that for any
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\Bbbk
, the subalgebra
L(f)
is isomorphic to a submodule-subalgebra
W_{\geq-1}(h)
with
h\in\Bbbk[t]
a reduced polynomial.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\Bbbk
and let
h\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
f^{\prime}g_{f}=h(f)\in\Bbbk[f]
. Then
h
is reduced (i.e.
h
is a scalar multiple of
\rad(h)
).

Proof.

Certainly, in order to have
f^{\prime}g_{f}=h(f)
, it must be the case that
f^{\prime}
divides
h(f)
. In particular, this implies that
h
must vanish on
f(V(f^{\prime}))=\{f(\lambda)\mid f^{\prime}(\lambda)=0\}
. We claim that these are all the roots of
h
, in other words,
h
is a scalar multiple of the polynomial

\widetilde{h}=\prod_{\lambda\in f(V(f^{\prime}))}(t-\lambda).

This will certainly imply that
h
is reduced, as required.

In the proof of
[
Buz22
, Proposition 4.13]
, it was shown that
h
is the generator of the ideal

I(f)\coloneqq\{p\in\Bbbk[t]\mid f^{\prime}\text{ divides }p(f)\}

of
\Bbbk[t]
. As mentioned in the first paragraph, any element of
I(f)
must vanish on
f(V(f^{\prime}))
, so
\widetilde{h}
divides
h
. Therefore, it suffices to show that
\widetilde{h}\in I(f)=(h)
, that is,
f^{\prime}
divides
\widetilde{h}(f)=\prod_{\lambda\in f(V(f^{\prime}))}(f-\lambda)
.

Let
\lambda\in V(f^{\prime})
. Then
(t-f(\lambda))
divides
\widetilde{h}
by definition, so
f_{\lambda}\coloneqq f-f(\lambda)
divides
\widetilde{h}(f)
. Note that
\ord_{\lambda}(f_{\lambda}^{\prime})=\ord_{\lambda}(f_{\lambda})-1
, since
f_{\lambda}
vanishes at
\lambda
. But
f_{\lambda}^{\prime}=f^{\prime}
, so this means that
\ord_{\lambda}(f^{\prime})=\ord_{\lambda}(f_{\lambda})-1
. Therefore,

\ord_{\lambda}(\widetilde{h}(f))\geq\ord_{\lambda}(f_{\lambda})>\ord_{\lambda}(f^{\prime})

for all
\lambda\in V(f^{\prime})
. This implies that
f^{\prime}
divides
\widetilde{h}(f)
, as claimed.
∎

Theorem
2.2
, Lemma
6.2
, and Proposition
6.5
immediately imply that
L(f)
has no outer derivations.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]\setminus\Bbbk
. Then all derivations of
L(f)
are inner. In other words,
H^{1}(L(f);L(f))=0
. ∎

Furthermore, we can compute derivations of arbitrary infinite-dimensional subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
, generalising Corollary
3.8
. This further highlights the rigidity exhibited by
W_{\geq-1}
: derivations of any infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
are controlled by
W_{\geq-1}
.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
. Then

\Der(L)=\{\ad_{w}\mid w\in W_{\geq-1}\text{ such that }\ad_{w}(L)\subseteq L\}\cong N_{W_{\geq-1}}(L).

In other words, derivations of
L
are restrictions of derivations of
W_{\geq-1}
. Consequently,
H^{1}(L;L)\cong N_{W_{\geq-1}}(L)/L
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
L
has finite codimension in
L(f)
, which exists by Theorem
6.3
. Let
h\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
f^{\prime}g_{f}=h(f)
, so that
L(f)\cong W_{\geq-1}(h)
. Let
\varphi\colon L(f)\to W_{\geq-1}(h)
be an isomorphism, and let
\widetilde{L}=\varphi(L)\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(h)
, which is a subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension.

Let
d\in\Der(L)
, and let
\widetilde{d}=\varphi\circ d\circ\varphi^{-1}\in\Der(\widetilde{L})
. By Corollary
3.8
,
\widetilde{d}=\ad_{\widetilde{w}}
for some
\widetilde{w}\in W_{\geq-1}
such that
\ad_{\widetilde{w}}(\widetilde{L})\subseteq\widetilde{L}
. We claim that
\widetilde{w}\in W_{\geq-1}(h)
.

By Proposition
3.6
, there exists
g\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
W_{\geq-1}(g)\subseteq\widetilde{L}\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(\rad(g))
. Furthermore,
W_{\geq-1}(g)\subseteq\widetilde{L}\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(h)
, so
h
divides
g
. By Proposition
6.5
,
h
is reduced, so
h
divides
\rad(g)
. Therefore,

W_{\geq-1}(g)\subseteq\widetilde{L}\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(\rad(g))\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(h).

Hence, in order to prove the claim, it suffices to prove that
\widetilde{w}\in W_{\geq-1}(\rad(g))
, in other words, that
\ord_{\xi}(\widetilde{w})\geq 1
for all
\xi\in V(g)
.

Assume, for a contradiction, that
\ord_{\xi}(\widetilde{w})=0
for some
\xi\in V(g)
. Let
\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{L}
such that
\ord_{\xi}(\widetilde{x})
is minimal. Note that
\ord_{\xi}(\widetilde{x})\geq 1
, since
\widetilde{x}\in W_{\geq-1}(\rad(g))
. By Lemma
2.11
,
\ord_{\xi}([\widetilde{w},\widetilde{x}])=\ord_{\xi}(\widetilde{x})-1
. But
[\widetilde{w},\widetilde{x}]\in\widetilde{L}
, since
\widetilde{w}\in N_{W_{\geq-1}}(\widetilde{L})
, contradicting the minimality of
\ord_{\xi}(\widetilde{x})
. This proves the claim.

Now, we have

d=\varphi^{-1}\circ\widetilde{d}\circ\varphi=\varphi^{-1}\circ\ad_{\widetilde{w}}\circ\varphi.

Let
w=\varphi^{-1}(\widetilde{w})\in L(f)
. Then,

d(x)=\varphi^{-1}([\widetilde{w},\varphi(x)])=[w,x]=\ad_{w}(x)

for all
x\in L
. Hence,
d=\ad_{w}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

In the proof of Theorem
6.7
, it was shown that if
L
is a subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
, and
f\in\Bbbk[t]
is a reduced polynomial such that
L\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(f)
, then
N_{W_{\geq-1}}(L)\subseteq W_{\geq-1}(f)
.

Another consequence of our previous work is that any infinite-dimensional subalgebra
L\subseteq W_{\geq-1}
has a universal completely non-split extension isomorphic to
W_{\geq-1}
, which follows by combining Theorems
5.3
and
6.3
.

Corollary 6.9
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
. Then
L
has a universal completely non-split extension
\overline{L}
: if
X
is another completely non-split extension of
L
then
X
embeds in
\overline{L}
such that the following diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{L}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{L}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

.

Furthermore,
\overline{L}\cong W_{\geq-1}
as Lie algebras. ∎

We now highlight the difference between the situations in finite and infinite codimension. Consider an infinite-dimensional subalgebra
L\subseteq W_{\geq-1}
of infinite codimension. Theorem
6.3
shows that
L
is isomorphic to a subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension. Considering the universal property of Theorem
5.3
, it is natural to ask if the universal completely non-split extension
\overline{L}
of
L
embeds in
W_{\geq-1}
in a way that makes the diagram

(6.9.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{\geq-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{L}}

commute. We show that this is not the case.

Proposition 6.10
.

Let
L
be an infinite-dimensional subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
of infinite codimension. Then the universal completely non-split extension
\overline{L}
of
L
cannot be embedded in
W_{\geq-1}
in a way that makes (
6.9.1
) commute.

Proof.

Suppose we can embed
\overline{L}
in
W_{\geq-1}
such that (
6.9.1
) commutes. Certainly, the image of
\overline{L}
in
W_{\geq-1}
must have infinite codimension in
W_{\geq-1}
. Therefore, the map

W_{\geq-1}\cong\overline{L}\hookrightarrow W_{\geq-1}

is a nonzero endomorphism of
W_{\geq-1}
which is not an automorphism. By
[
Du04
, Theorem 2.3]
, this is impossible.
∎

7.
Finite codimension subalgebras of the full Witt algebra

We define submodule-subalgebras of
W
analogously to those of
W_{\geq-1}
: they are Lie subalgebras of
W
which are also submodules over
\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]
. They are denoted by
W(f)\coloneqq fW
, where
f\in\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]
. Since submodule-subalgebras of
W
correspond to ideals of
\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]
, we will usually assume that
f\in\Bbbk[t]
and that
f(0)\neq 0
.

In this section, we prove results about derivations and extensions of submodule-subalgebras of the Witt algebra similar to those of
W_{\geq-1}
. Most proofs are omitted, since they are identical to those in Sections
2
–
5
.

7.1.
Derivations

We start by considering derivations of submodule-subalgebras of the Witt algebra, similarly to Section
2
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
f(0)\neq 0
. We have
\Der(W(f))=\{\ad_{w}\mid w\in W(\rad(f))\}
. Consequently,
\Der(W(f))\cong W(\rad(f))
, and

\dim H^{1}(W(f);W(f))=\deg(f)-\deg(\rad(f)).

We firstly note that the notions of degree and
d
-compatibility are still well-defined for derivations of submodule-subalgebras of
W
. In other words, for
d\in\Der(W(f),W)
, we define

\deg(d)=\max\{\deg(d(x))-\deg(x)\mid x\in W(f)\}.

Since
W(f)
is a finitely generated Lie algebra,
\deg(d)
is well-defined by a similar proof to Lemma
2.4
. Similarly, for
d
-compatibility we simply note that an analogous result to Lemma
2.6
holds in this situation.

One key difference between submodule-subalgebras of
W
and of
W_{\geq-1}
is that the associated graded algebra of
W(f)
is
W
. Therefore, in order to compute the associated graded derivation of
d\in\Der(W(f),W)
, we only need to know about
\Der(W)
, rather than
\Der(W_{\geq n},W)
as was the case in Section
2
, resulting in a much simpler picture.

The computation of
\Der(W)
is similar to that of
\Der(W_{\geq-1})
in Proposition
1.5
: we simply apply Theorem
1.6
.

Proposition 7.2
.

We have
\Der(W)=\Inn(W)
. ∎

This has been known for a long time. For example, a proof can be found in
[
IK90
]
, where the authors compute derivations of a class of Lie algebras known as
generalised Witt algebras
.

Now we can define associated graded derivations similarly to Section
2
. The proof is identical to Proposition
2.9
.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
d\in\Der(W(f),W)
and define a
\Bbbk
-linear map
\gr(d)\colon W\to W
by

\gr(d)(e_{k})=\begin{cases}\LT(d(x)),&\text{where }x\in W(f)\text{ and }\LT(x)=e_{k},\text{ if }k\text{ is }d\text{-compatible,}\\
0,&\text{if }k\text{ is not }d\text{-compatible,}\end{cases}

for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
\gr(d)=\lambda\ad_{e_{k}}\in\Der(W)
for some
\lambda\in\Bbbk
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. ∎

The next result is analogous to Proposition
2.10
.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
d\in\Der(W(f),W)
. Then
d=\ad_{\frac{h}{f}\partial}
for some
h\in\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]
. ∎

We can now prove Theorem
7.1
.

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

The proof follows in a similar way to Theorem
2.2
. The only difference is that in
W
we allow poles at 0, so we need to be a bit more careful. Note that the proof of Theorem
2.2
only considers
\ord_{\xi}
for
\xi\in V(f)
. Since we assumed that
f(0)\neq 0
, we have
0\not\in V(f)
, so we do not run into any problems by allowing poles at 0.
∎

7.2.
Extensions

We proceed similarly to Section
3
to compute one-dimensional extensions of submodule-subalgebras of the Witt algebra. Just like submodule-subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
, one-dimensional extensions of
W(f)
are either contained in
W(\rad(f))
or they come from non-split extensions of
W/W(t-\xi)
by
W(f)
for
\xi\in V(f)
with
\ord_{\xi}(f)=1
. We explain the proof of this result, with emphasis on the difference between this situation and the proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
f\in\Bbbk[t]
with
f(0)\neq 0
. If
M\cong W/W(t-\xi)
for some
\xi\in V(f)
with
\ord_{\xi}(f)=1
, then

\dim(\Ext_{U(W(f))}^{1}(W/W(t-\xi),W(f)))=1.

In this case, the unique non-split extension of
W/W(t-\xi)
by
W(f)
is
W(\frac{f}{t-\xi})
. If
M\cong\Bbbk
is trivial, we have

\dim(\Ext_{U(W(f))}^{1}(\Bbbk,W(f)))=\deg(f)-\deg(\rad(f)).

Otherwise,
\Ext_{U(W(f))}^{1}(M,W(f))=0
.

As in Section
3
, we need to compute the derived subalgebra of
W(f)
for
f\in\Bbbk[t]
. Just like in Lemma
3.4
, we can easily see that
[W(f),W(f)]=W(f^{2})
.

Lemma 7.6
.

For
f\in\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]
, the derived subalgebra of
W(f)
is
W(f^{2})
. ∎

We now need to show that one-dimensional extensions of submodule-subalgebras of
W
are contained in
W
. The proof of this is nearly identical to Proposition
3.7
.

Proposition 7.7
.

Let
L
be a Lie subalgebra of
W
of finite codimension, and let
\overline{L}
be a one-dimensional extension of
L
. Then
\overline{L}
can be uniquely embedded in
W
such that the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{L}}

commutes. ∎

The only subtlety in the proof of Proposition
7.7
is that, when applying
[
PS23
, Proposition 3.2.7]
to deduce that there exists
f\in\Bbbk[t]
such that

W(f)\subseteq L\subseteq W(\rad(f)),

we need to assume that
f(0)\neq 0
. After this, the proof is exactly the same as Proposition
3.7
, since we never consider any orders of vanishing at 0. The proof of Theorem
7.5
now follows similarly to Theorem
3.3
.

As we did with
W_{\geq-1}
, we summarise the computation of one-dimensional extensions of submodule-subalgebras of
W
.

Corollary 7.8
.

Suppose
\overline{L}
is a one-dimensional extension of
W(f)
, where
f\in\Bbbk[t]
and
f(0)\neq 0
. Then either
\overline{L}\subseteq W(\rad(f))
or
\overline{L}=W(\frac{f}{t-\xi})
, where
\xi\in V(f)
with
\ord_{\xi}(f)=1
. ∎

7.3.
Isomorphisms between subalgebras of the Witt algebra of finite codimension

As we did in Section
4
, we explain how any isomorphism between subalgebras of
W
of finite codimension extends to an automorphism of
W
, analogously to Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 7.9
.

Let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be subalgebras of
W
of finite codimension and suppose there is an isomorphism of Lie algebras
\varphi\colon L_{1}\to L_{2}
. Then
\varphi
extends to an automorphism of
W
.

The proof of Theorem
7.9
follows by showing that any subalgebra of
W
of finite codimension has a one-dimensional extension contained in
W
. As we did with
W_{\geq-1}
, we can achieve this by showing that any such subalgebra of
W
is strongly residually solvable. The proof is exactly the same as Lemma
4.6
.

Lemma 7.10
.

If
L
is a proper subalgebra of
W
of finite codimension then
L
is strongly residually solvable. ∎

Applying Lie’s theorem, we deduce that all finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
L
are one-dimensional, just like Corollary
4.7
.

Corollary 7.11
.

Let
L
be a subalgebra of
W
of finite codimension. Then all finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
L
are one-dimensional. ∎

We can now show that any subalgebra of
W
of finite codimension has a one-dimensional extension. The proof is identical to that of Proposition
4.9
.

Proposition 7.12
.

Let
L
be a proper subalgebra of
W
of finite codimension. Then
L
has a one-dimensional extension
\overline{L}
such that the short exact sequence of
U(L)
-modules

0\to L\to\overline{L}\to\overline{L}/L\to 0

is non-split. Furthermore, by Proposition
7.7
, all such extensions can be uniquely embedded in
W
such that the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{L}}

commutes. ∎

Automorphisms of
W
are different to those of
W_{\geq-1}
: in
W
, we no longer have access to the map
e_{n}\mapsto e_{n}(x)
for
x\in\Bbbk\setminus\{0\}
, since
(t-x)^{n}\partial\not\in W
for
n<0
. Furthermore, in
\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]
we have an automorphism
t\mapsto t^{-1}
, which induces an automorphism of
W
.

Notation 7.13
.

For
\alpha\in\Bbbk^{*}
. Define linear maps
\sigma_{\alpha},\tau\colon W\to W
by

\sigma_{\alpha}(e_{n})=\alpha^{n}e_{n},\quad\tau(e_{n})=-e_{-n},

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Similarly to
W_{\geq-1}
, these give rise to all automorphisms of
W
.

Proposition 7.14

(
[
Bav16
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

We have
\Aut(W)=\{\sigma_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Bbbk^{*}\}\rtimes\{\tau^{\pm 1}\}
.

This allows us to determine when two submodule-subalgebras of the Witt algebra are isomorphic. It is worth noting that, unlike submodule-subalgebras of
W_{\geq-1}
, we have
W(f)=W(t^{n}f)
for
f\in\Bbbk[t,t^{-1}]
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Corollary 7.15
.

Let
f,g\in\Bbbk[t]
such that
f(0)\neq 0
and
g(0)\neq 0
and suppose
W(f)\cong W(g)
as Lie algebras. Then one of the following is true:

(1)

There exist
\alpha,\beta\in\Bbbk^{*}
such that
f(\alpha t)=\beta g(t)
;

(2)

There exist
\alpha,\beta\in\Bbbk^{*}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
t^{n}f(\alpha t^{-1})=\beta g(t)
. ∎

7.4.
Universal property

We proceed similarly to Section
5
to prove that the Witt algebra satisfies a similar universal property to
W_{\geq-1}
. We easily see that
W
is a completely non-split extension of any of its subalgebras of finite codimension by applying Proposition
7.12
inductively.

Corollary 7.16
.

Let
L
be a subalgebra of
W
of finite codimension. Then
W
is a completely non-split extension of
L
. ∎

Just like
W_{\geq-1}
, the Witt algebra can be reconstructed from any of its subalgebras of finite codimension as the universal completely non-split extension.

Theorem 7.17
.

Let
L
be a subalgebra of
W_{\geq-1}
of finite codimension. Then
W_{\geq-1}
is the universal completely non-split extension of
L
, in the following sense: if
\overline{L}
is another completely non-split extension of
L
, then
\overline{L}
can be uniquely embedded in
W_{\geq-1}
such that the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{\geq-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{L}}

commutes.

Proof.

The proof follows by an inductive application of Proposition
7.7
, similarly to the proof of Theorem
5.3
. As before, uniqueness of the embedding
\overline{L}\hookrightarrow W
follows from Theorem
7.9
.
∎

We remark that we do not have a classification of subalgebras of
W
of infinite codimension, so we do not have any analogous results to Section
6
for the Witt algebra. This is the subject of ongoing research.

Imaginaries, products and the adele ring
Jamshid Derakhshan, Ehud Hrushovski

Abstract
. We describe the imaginary sorts of infinite products in terms of imaginary sorts of the factors (Theorem 3.1 ). We extend the result to certain reduced powers (Theorem 4.1 ) and then to infinite products ∏ i ∈ I M i \prod_{i\in I}M_{i} enriched with a predicate for the ideal of finite subsets of I I (Theorem 5.1 ). As a special case, using the Hils-Rideau-Kikuchi uniform p p -adic elimination of imaginaries, we find the imaginary sorts of the ring of rational adeles (Example 5.4 ). Our methods include the use of the Harrington-Kechris-Louveau Glimm-Efros dichotomy both for transitioning from monadic second order imaginaries to first-order reducts, and for proving a certain “one-way” model-theoretic orthogonality within the adelic imaginaries.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Products and Boolean-valued models

The theory of a power
M^{I}
of an algebraic structure
M
was determined by Mostowski
[
21
]
,
both for full and weak powers (such as direct powers and direct sums of a group.) It was systematized and generalized to reduced products by Feferman and Vaught
[
9
]
. To understand such structures it is best to add to the picture the Boolean algebra
B
of all subsets of
I

(or all finite or cofinite subsets of
I
, in the case of weak products),
and view
M^{I}
as a
B
-valued model of the theory of
M
; see §
2.1
. If the Boolean algebra is enriched with an ideal
J
, it becomes possible
to interpret restricted powers (see §
5.4
), weak products or reduced products. In fact under weak conditions on
M
, the Boolean algebra and attendant
ideal are already interpretable in
M^{I}
, so it is more a matter of displaying than of really adding the Boolean apparatus.

An interesting example is
the structure
({\mathbb{N}},\cdot)
of multiplication on the integers; the fundamental theorem of arithmetic identifies it with the weak power of Pressburger arithmetic

({\mathbb{N}},+)
. The study of
({\mathbb{N}},\cdot)
, and a procedure for interpreting
Boolean algebra operations within it, go back to
[
20
]
. In modern terms,
({\mathbb{N}},\cdot)
interprets in the first-order sense the Boolean algebra of finite and cofinite subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
, via square-free integers and divisibility. Leibniz makes use not of this directly, but of a closely related ind-definable
free
Boolean algebra, generated by the primes. The mathematical reason for this choice is clear: his goal was to reduce all valid Aristotelian syllogisms
to numerical calculations of multiplication and greatest common divisor operations on natural numbers; it is the free Boolean algebra on given unary predicates that captures Aristotelian logic. The first-order theory of
({\mathbb{N}},\cdot)
was fully analyzed by Skolem in 1930, following on his 1919 work on the theory of

\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
- equivalently the theory of infinite atomic Boolean algebra. (References to Skolem’s work can be found in
[
21
]
.)

The Boolean-valued approach fits into a series of richer examples, including the Scott-Solovay-Shoenfield Boolean-valued approach to forcing, and the theory of the algebraic integers viewed as a Boolean-valued algebraically closed valued field. It was also a component of Keisler’s theory of randomization
[
19
]
, later transposed into
continuous logic by Itaï
Ben Yaacov and Keisler. In the present paper we treat only plain Booleanizations, enriched at most by an ideal.
Guided by the case of the ring of adeles, we consider the zero ideal or ideals
J
such that the algebra is atomless modulo
J
.

Many thanks are due to Mariana Vicaria for her reading and comments on a previous version of this paper.

1.2.
Imaginaries

An imaginary sort of a first-order structure
M
is a possible universe of a structure interpretable in
M
. The significance of imaginary sorts was established in the abstract setting of Classification Theory by Shelah
[
23
]
; subsequently all notions of stability theory, especially algebraic closure, were interpreted
“in
T^{eq}
", i.e. with imaginary sorts taken into account. They were
first studied for specific theories by Poizat
[
22
]
; he showed notably that the theories of algebraically closed fields and of
differentially closed fields
eliminate imaginaries
, i.e. each imaginary sort is in definable bijection with a definable set of
n
-tuples.
Poizat also
pointed out that elimination of imaginaries for algebraically closed fields encapsulates, modulo general principles, classical Galois theory for fields.

Other theories have imaginary sorts that differ qualitatively from definable sets; an example is the value group of a valued field such as
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
In this case it is a question of finding a useful
basis
for the imaginary sorts, meaning every imaginary sort can be embedded into a product of the basis elements. In the case of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, these would be the
geometric sorts

GL_{n}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})/GL_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})

of lattices in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n}
.

The theory of an infinite set
V
, with no relations other than equality, does not admit elimination of imaginaries; since one has the imaginary sorts

V^{n}/H
, for any subgroup
H
of the symmetric group
Sym(n)
; they code finite subsets of
V^{n}
.
However these are the only imaginary sorts one needs. The same situation occurs in theories of vector spaces over a given field.

In general, a theory with universe
V
has
weak elimination of imaginaries
if the sorts
V^{n}/H
form a basis for the imaginaries.

At this point we can give a formulation of our main theorems. Let
T
be any theory. Let

be an infinite index set,
and let

be a family of models of

with the natural structure
(explained precisely in Example

For an equivalence relation

on a finite product

and

be equivalence relations on

be the expansion of

Theorem

Weak elimination of imaginaries for Skolem arithmetic,

An earlier result of Wencel

In §

we will prove our first general result reducing imaginaries in product structures to imaginaries of the individual structures. The fundamental case in our treatment
is
\Omega^{\mathbb{N}}
, where

is a finite set with no additional structure. While it may well be possible to handle this case explicitly, we handle it with tools
of liaison groups originating in stability theory, whose appearance and effectiveness in this second-order environment initially surprised us. In particular, we give some structure theorems for interpretable groups over the infinite atomic Boolean algebra.
This can be compared to the determination of connected
\infty

Along with liaison groups, we will use tools from descriptive set theory. A Borel equivalence relation

on a Polish space

is called

if there exists a

to a Polish space, such that

iff

. In work of Becker and Kechris

]
, this is shown to give a very robust criterion for a Borel subgroup to be closed. Roughly speaking, this will allow us to move from questions on imaginaries of a product

to questions on
reducts of a co-product
\coprod_{i\in I}M_{i}
. The latter remains stable in the critical case where the

are finite, allowing the use of tools from geometric stability theory.

These descriptive-set theoretic ideas will also give us an important model-theoretic orthogonality principle. Let
I_{fin}
be the ideal of finite subsets
of the Boolean algebra
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm P}}}({\mathbb{N}})
. Consider a product of structures

, and define an equivalence relation

using the ideal

that a Borel equivalence relation is non-smooth if and only if

can be reduced to it. In the adelic case, where

is a local field, or more generally an uncountable Borel structure,

gives the equivalence relation known in descriptive set theory as

(which is even less smooth.) On the other hand, we will see that the equivalence relations definable on a single

It will follow from this that there are no nontrivial definable maps or finitely multivalued maps from the reduced power
M/E_{fin}

into

(Proposition

); this conclusion will hold in any model. This will allow us to study the imaginaries separately in each

- class on various definable sets, equivalence relation, and in the quotient, and glue together the information.

The elimination of imaginaries can presumably also be achieved in a more hands-on manner, along the lines of
[
26
]
,
[
24
]
.
But our purpose here is to develop tools that may serve in more complex environments. In

, the same descriptive set-theoretic methods
are used to show in far greater generality that smoothness coincides with an appropriately generalized notion of 2nd-order elimination of imaginaries.

Our test case at present was the ring of rational adeles
\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
, and the above general results lead in particular to describe the imaginary sorts of the adele ring in §

They consist of the uniform imaginary sorts for the

, but each appearing also in a second blurred version, identifying two sequences that agree for almost all primes. Thanks to work of Hils and Rideau-Kikuchi, a uniform description of the imaginaries of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
in a natural language,
roughly speaking the field language along with certain canonical generators for certain tame Galois groups. We thus obtain
a description of the adelic imaginaries in the same language.

It would be very interesting to find a basis for the imaginaries for the algebraic integers, where a ’discrete’ field is also endowed from structure
arising from embedding into an adele ring (see Problem

5.4.
Adeles

In this section we find a basis for the imaginary sorts of the rational adele ring. This will be a consequence of Theorem
5.1
combined with a uniform description of the
p
-adic imaginaries due to Hils and Rideau-Kikuchi.

For further
model-theoretic properties of the adele rings of number fields, decidability and complexity of definable sets, we refer the reader to
[
8
]
.
Notably the formulas

[\phi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})]\in I_{fin}
and

Let
{\mathbb{A}}^{fin}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}=\prod^{\prime}_{p\in P}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
be the ring of finite rational adeles, where
P
is the primes. Then
{\mathbb{A}}^{fin}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
is interpretable in the product
(\prod_{p\in P}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p},B,I_{fin})

and the ideal

consisting of finite unions of atoms. In fact

\prod_{p\in P}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
by the multiplicative set

\{x\in\prod_{p\in P}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}:[x=0]=0\wedge[x\neq 1]\in I_{fin}\}

Conversely,

is interpretable as the set of idempotent finite adeles, and by
[
8
, Theorem 5.2]
the set

is definable from the ring structure on

Now
(\prod_{p\in P}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p},{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm P}}}(I),I_{fin})\models T_{Mixed}
,
where
{T}
is the theory of the valuation rings of Henselian valued fields of characteristic zero, with finite or pseudofinite residue field
and
{\mathbb{Z}}
-group value group.

The full theory of

includes the statement that

is the least element of the value group.

For any imaginary sort

Random walks with bounded first moment on finite-volume spaces
Timothée Bénard, Nicolas de Saxcé

Abstract
Let G G be a real Lie group, Λ ≤ G \Lambda\leq G a lattice, and Ω = G / Λ \Omega=G/\Lambda . We study the equidistribution properties of the left random walk on Ω \Omega induced by a probability measure μ \mu on G G . It is assumed that μ \mu has a finite first moment, and that the Zariski closure of the group generated by the support of μ \mu in the adjoint representation is semisimple without compact factors. We show that for every starting point x ∈ Ω x\in\Omega , the μ \mu -walk with origin x x has no escape of mass, and equidistributes in Cesàro averages toward some homogeneous measure. This extends several fundamental results due to Benoist-Quint and Eskin-Margulis for walks with finite exponential moment.



1
Introduction

A homogeneous Markov chain on a finite-volume real homogeneous space is formally incarnated by a triple
(G,\Lambda,\mu)
where
G
is a real Lie group,
\Lambda
a discrete subgroup of finite covolume in
G
, and
\mu
a Borel probability measure on
G
. The chain in question then corresponds to the left
\mu
-random walk on the quotient
\Omega=G/\Lambda
. In other words, the transition law at a point
x\in\Omega
is given by the convolution
\mu\ast\delta_{x}
, image of
\mu
under
g\mapsto gx
.

In the last 20 years, the study of walks on finite-volume spaces has known spectacular advances, which were achieved in analogy with Ratner’s theorems describing the dynamics of Ad-unipotent flows on
\Omega
.
The first milestone was set by Eskin-Margulis who proved that the
n
-th step distribution of a homogeneous chain essentially remains in a compact set
[
14
]
. A few years later, Benoist-Quint managed to classify all the
\mu
-invariant probability measures
[
3
,
6
]
, and extrapolated in
[
7
]
that almost every trajectory of the random walk equidistributes in some finite-volume homogeneous subspace.
However, all these statements require stringent moment assumptions on the measure
\mu
, such as
compact support
or
finite exponential moment
, the latter meaning that for some
\alpha>0
, we have

\int_{G}\lVert\Ad g\rVert^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(g)<\infty.

In a recent paper, Eskin and Lindenstrauss
[
13
]
extended the techniques developed by Benoist and Quint, and showed that their measure classification was still valid in the case where
\mu
only has a
finite first moment
.
Our goal is to weaken in the same way the moment assumptions in the Eskin-Margulis recurrence theorem and in the Benoist-Quint equidistribution theorems.
This answers a question formulated by Benoist-Quint in the
10^{\text{th}}
Takagi Lectures
[
4
, Question 2]
.

1.1
Main results

Let
G
be a real Lie group,
\Lambda
a lattice in
G
, and set
\Omega=G/\Lambda
. We fix a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
G
and denote by
\Gamma
the semigroup generated by the support of
\mu
. The algebraic group generated by its adjoint representation is denoted by
H=\overline{\Ad\Gamma}^{Z}
, and we call
H^{nc}
its non-compact part, defined as the smallest normal algebraic subgroup of
H
such that
H/H^{nc}
is compact.
All the theorems to follow are presented under the next two assumptions.

1.

The non-compact part
H^{nc}
of
H
is semisimple.

2.

The measure
\mu
has a
finite first moment
:

\int_{G}\log\lVert\Ad g\rVert\,\mathrm{d}\mu(g)<\infty

To state our results, we need to introduce the notion of homogeneous subspace.

Definition 1.1
.

A closed subset
Y
of
\Omega
is
homogeneous
if its stabilizer
G_{Y}=\{g\in G,\,gY=Y\}
acts transitively on
Y
. We add that
Y
has
finite volume
if the action of
G_{Y}
on
Y
preserves a Borel probability measure on
Y
. Such a measure is then unique and denoted by
\nu_{Y}
. If the semigroup
\Gamma
is included in
G_{Y}
(and acts ergodically on
(Y,\nu_{Y})
), we say that
Y
is
\Gamma
-invariant
(and
\Gamma
-
ergodic
).

We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}(\Gamma)
the set of
\Gamma
-invariant ergodic finite-volume closed homogeneous subsets of
\Omega
.

Our first result states that the
\mu
-walk on
\Omega
essentially evolves in a compact subset.
Such statements originate in the work of Eskin-Margulis-Mozes
[
15
]
on the quantitative Oppenheim conjecture and can be seen as an analog for random walks of the Dani-Margulis recurrence theorem on Ad-unipotent flows
[
12
]
.

Theorem A

(Non-escape at infinity)
.

For every compact set
K\subset\Omega
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a compact set
K^{\prime}\subset\Omega
such that for every
x\in K
,

(i)

for every
n\geq 0
,
(\mu^{*n}*\delta_{x})(K^{\prime})>1-\varepsilon
;

(ii)

for
\mu^{\otimes\mathbb{N}^{*}}
-almost every instructions
(g_{i})_{i\geq 1}
,

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\card\{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\ |\ g_{k}\dots g_{1}x\in K^{\prime}\}>1-\varepsilon.

Conclusion (i) means the mass of the
n
-th step distribution of the walk does not escape at infinity. Conclusion (ii) expresses some positive recurrence of the walk’s trajectories.
The result actually holds under the slightly weaker assumption that the image measure
(\Ad_{\mathfrak{s}})_{*}\mu
of
\mu
under the adjoint representation on the largest semisimple quotient of
\mathfrak{g}
without compact factors generates an algebraic group with semisimple non-compact part (see
Theorem A′
in Section
4
). This theorem generalizes
[
5
]
and
[
14
]
, which assume that
\mu
has a finite exponential moment.

Our second result states that the
\mu
-walk on
\Omega
does not accumulate on a
\mu
-invariant homogeneous subspace, unless it is trapped inside it.

Theorem B

(Unstability of invariant homogeneous subspaces)
.

Let
Y\in\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}(\Gamma)
and consider a compact subset
K\subseteq\Omega\smallsetminus Y
.
For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a neighborhood
O^{\prime}
of
Y
in
\Omega
such that for all
x\in K
,

(i)

for every
n\geq 0
,
(\mu^{*n}*\delta_{x})(O^{\prime})<\varepsilon
;

(ii)

for
\mu^{\otimes\mathbb{N}^{*}}
-almost every instructions
(g_{i})_{i\geq 1}
,

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\card\{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\ |\ g_{k}\dots g_{1}x\in O^{\prime}\}<\varepsilon.

For the purpose of studying the equidistribution of
\mu
-trajectories, we prove a more general version (
Theorem B′
) where
Y
is replaced by its orbit
L_{0}Y
under a compact subset
L_{0}
of the centralizer of
H^{nc}
in
G
.
Conclusion
(ii)
is obtained in
[
7
]
under the assumption that
\mu
has a finite exponential moment. If the space
\Omega=G/\Lambda
is compact, item
(i)
can be readily deduced from the arguments given in
[
6
, Section 6]
, but it appears to be new when
\Omega
is not compact, even under exponential moments assumptions.

Our third result states that each
\mu
-walk trajectory on
\Omega
equidistributes in its closure toward some homogeneous measure. It can be seen as an analog for random walks of Ratner’s equidistribution theorem
[
20
]
for Ad-unipotent flows on
\Omega
. As observed in
[
6
,
13
]
, the assumption on
H
must be strengthened in order to guarantee homogeneity.
We ask that
H
be Zariski connected semisimple without compact factors, or equivalently
H=H^{nc}
semisimple.

Theorem C

(Equidistribution in Cesàro-averages)
.

Suppose as well
H=H^{nc}
. For every
x\in\Omega
, we have:

(i)

The orbit closure
Y=\overline{\Gamma\cdot x}\subset\Omega
is a
\Gamma
-invariant ergodic finite-volume closed homogeneous subset.

(ii)

The sequence of measures
(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mu^{*k}*\delta_{x})_{n\geq 1}
converges to
\nu_{Y}
in the weak-
\ast
topology.

(iii)

For
\mu^{\otimes\mathbb{N}^{*}}
-almost every instructions
(g_{i})_{i\geq 1}
, the sequence of empirical measures
(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{g_{k}\dots g_{1}x})_{n\geq 1}
converges to
\nu_{Y}
in the weak-
\ast
topology.

Theorem
C
follows from
[
7
]
in the case where
\mu
has a compact support, which implies in particular that
\Gamma
is compactly generated. It is a well-known conjecture that the Cesàro-averages in the second item should not be necessary. This will be proven in a follow-up paper
[
1
]
in the case where two powers of
\mu
are not mutually singular.

1.2
Dynamics of the proofs

In order to prove Theorem
A
, we use a variant of Foster’s recurrence criterion for walks with a negative drift, applied to an appropriate proper drift function on the space of lattices.
This strategy is generally credited to Margulis, and goes back to the works
[
15
]
and
[
14
]
.
It was further developed by Benoist and Quint
[
5
]
.
In those papers, the authors make an exponential moment assumption, and use the elementary Proposition
1.2
below

To be more accurate, those papers rather use a contraction property of the form
\mathbb{E}_{x}[F(X_{1})]\leq aF(x)+b
, for some
a\in(0,1)
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
It is not hard to see that under the assumptions of the above proposition, the function
F(x)=e^{\delta f(x)}
where
\delta>0
is very small, has the desired contraction property.

, or

exponential recurrence criterion

Proposition 1.2

(Eskin-Margulis, Benoist-Quint)
.

Let
\Omega
be a measurable space,
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
a measurable function and
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
a measurable Markov chain on
\Omega
. Assume

(\mathbf{EM})

\exists\alpha_{0}>0
,

\,\sup_{x\in\Omega}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(e^{\alpha_{0}|f(X_{1})-f(x)|}\right)<\infty

(\mathbf{D})

\exists R_{0},\lambda_{1}>0,

\forall x\in f^{-1}(R_{0},+\infty)
,

\,\mathbb{E}_{x}(f(X_{1}))\leq f(x)-\lambda_{1}

Then for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
R>0
such that for
x\in\Omega
,

(i)

\forall n\geq n_{x},\hskip 11.74988pt\mathbb{P}_{x}[f(X_{n})>R]\,<\,\varepsilon

(ii)

\mathbb{P}_{x}-\mbox{almost surely},

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\card\{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\ |\ f(X_{k})>R\}<\varepsilon.

In order to remove the exponential moment condition in the Benoist-Quint theory, our most important task will be to obtain an analogous recurrence criterion that applies to homogeneous random walks with only a finite first moment assumption.

A first approach would be to simply try weakening the requirement (
\mathbf{EM}
) above.
And indeed we shall see that Proposition
1.2
is still true if we replace (
\mathbf{EM}
) by the condition that the increments of the walk are uniformly bounded in
L^{1+\eta}
for some
\eta>0
:

(\mathbf{M}_{\eta})

\exists M>0
,
\forall x\in\Omega
,

\,\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(|f(X_{1})-f(x)|^{1+\eta}\right)<M.

However, carefully constructed (continuous) Markov chains on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
show that
conclusions (i) and (ii) may not hold for walks with negative drift and increments uniformly bounded in
L^{1}
, in other words if (
\mathbf{EM}
) above is replaced by (
\mathbf{M}_{0}
) (see Section
2
, Examples
1
and
2
). We need a new point of view, which reflects more refined properties of homogeneous chains than the drift in expectation expressed by
(\mathbf{D})
. Our crucial claim is that the height of a homogeneous walk evolving in the cusps of a finite-volume space
\Omega=G/\Lambda
must decrease faster than a chain on
\mathbb{R}
with i.i.d. increments of negative mean. We formalize this concept by the notion of stochastic dominance.

Definition 1.3
.

Given real random variables
Z
and
(Z_{i})_{i\in I}
, we say that
(Z_{i})_{i\in I}
is stochastically dominated by
Z
if for every
i\in I
,
t\in\mathbb{R}

\mathbb{P}(Z_{i}>t)\,\leq\,\mathbb{P}(Z>t).

The abstract version of Proposition
1.2
we shall need is the following.

Theorem D

(Recurrence of Markov chains with stochastic dominance)
.

Let
\Omega
be a measurable space,
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
a measurable function, and
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
a measurable Markov chain on
\Omega
.

Assume there exist a sublevel set
K:=\{f\leq R_{0}\}
and integrable real random variables
Z_{0}
,
Z_{1}
with
\mathbb{E}[Z_{1}]:=-\lambda_{1}<0
such that

1.

(f(X_{1})-f(X_{0})|X_{0}=x)_{x\in K}
is stochastically dominated by
Z_{0}
;

2.

(f(X_{1})-f(X_{0})|X_{0}=x)_{x\in\Omega\smallsetminus K}
is stochastically dominated by
Z_{1}
.

Then for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
R>0
such that for all
x\in\Omega
,

(i)

\forall n\geq 1,\hskip 11.74988pt\mathbb{P}_{x}[f(X_{n})>R]\,<\,\varepsilon+\frac{f(x)}{n\lambda_{1}}.

(ii)

\mathbb{P}_{x}-\mbox{almost surely},

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\card\{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\ |\ f(X_{k})>R\}<\varepsilon.

It is pleasant to note that conclusion (i) gives an
optimal bound
on the time
n
at which the distribution
X_{n}
starts to accumulate on the sublevel set
\{f\leq R\}
(see discussion below Proposition
2.5
).

Once Theorem
D
is established, we use it to show that homogeneous random walks do not escape at infinity, namely Theorem
A
.
Theorem
D
will be applied to a proper drift function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
extracted from Benoist-Quint’s paper
[
5
]
. Benoist-Quint’s function is itself inspired by a former construction due to Eskin, Margulis, and Mozes
[
14
,
15
]
. Checking the stochastic condition of Theorem
D
will require some work, mostly done in Section
3
.

The proof of Theorem
B
is inspired by
[
6
, Section 6]
which obtains item (ii) when
\mu
has a finite exponential moment. However, several important changes are needed. The approach of
[
6
]
consists in showing that the first return random walk induced on a large compact subset
Q
drifts away from
Y\cap Q
in expectation. It is not possible to extract information on the original
n
-th step distribution
\mu^{n}\ast\delta_{x}
from this induced random walk, so conclusion (i) cannot be obtained with such a strategy. Moreover, checking that the induced walk does satisfy the new conditions of stochastic dominance formulated in Theorem
D
would raise other significant difficulties.

Our solution instead is to construct a global drift function for every closed invariant finite-volume homogeneous subset
Y
; this is done by gluing together the Benoist-Quint drift function with a function that drifts away from
Y
on a compact subset.
We believe this new technique could be useful to construct drift functions in other contexts.

Finally, the equidistribution of each
\mu
-trajectory in its closure stated in Theorem
C
results from a combination of Theorems
A
,
B
, the Eskin-Lindenstrauss classification of stationary probability measures
[
13
, Theorem 1.3]
, and the general strategy of
[
7
]
. The proof roughly goes as follows. Consider a starting point
x\in\Omega
, a typical sequence of instructions
(g_{i})_{i\geq 1}
and write for
n\geq 1
,

\nu_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{g_{k}\dots g_{1}x}.

Breiman’s law of large numbers and Theorem
A
imply that any weak-
\ast
limit
\nu
of
(\nu_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a
\mu
-stationary probability measure. By Theorem
B
,
\nu
does not give mass to the
\Gamma
-invariant homogeneous subspaces which do not contain
x
.
The Eskin-Lindenstrauss classification of stationary measures implies that
\nu=\nu_{Y}
for some
Y\in S_{\Omega}(\Gamma)
and necessarily
Y:=\overline{\Gamma.x}
. This yields the equidistribution statement.

2
Markov chains with a negative drift

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
D
stating that Markov chains on a stratified state space and satisfying some stochastic dominance condition have
neither escape of mass nor escape of empirical measures
.
We also note that these conclusions are still valid if the chain is assumed to have a negative drift and increments uniformly bounded in
L^{1+\eta}
for some
\eta>0
.
Counterexamples are given if
\eta=0
.

Recurrent aspects of general Markov chains is a classical subject in probability theory, and the books
[
21
]
and
[
19
]
will provide a thorough introduction to the subject to the interested reader.
We note that many recurrence results for Markov chains on continuous state spaces make some irreducibility assumption such as
\psi
-irreducibility, or Harris-recurrence.
Those are natural assumptions that remove the dependency of the Markov chain on the starting point, and they are certainly necessary to obtain some convergence statement such as
[
19
, Theorem 13.0.1]
, but they are not satisfied by random walks on homogeneous spaces.

However, in this section, we shall only be interested in weaker statements such as non-escape of mass, and for that, one can compensate the lack of irreducibility by making a stronger moment assumption, or a stochastic dominance assumption.
It is only through the classification of stationary measures
[
6
,
13
]
, a result that relies heavily on the specific structure of homogeneous spaces, that we shall be able to derive an equidistribution theorem from the result on non-escape of mass.

For the whole section, we fix a measurable space
\Omega
, a non-negative measurable function
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and a Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on
\Omega
. Given a point
x\in\Omega
, the notations
\mathbb{E}_{x}
and
\mathbb{P}_{x}
will refer to the expectation and probability conditional to
X_{0}=x
.

2.1
Dominance condition and return times

We first give some perspective to the stochastic dominance assumptions in Theorem

(\mathbf{SD})

There exist a sublevel set

, so the non-escape estimates are equally valid in this case.
This latter observation will not be used in the other sections, but seems general enough to be of independent interest.
It also extends the results based on exponential moment conditions used in
[
14
,
7
]
.

Lemma 2.3

(Return times)
.

Suppose
(\Omega,f,(X_{n}))
satisfy condition
(\mathbf{SD})
, or conditions
(\mathbf{D})
and
(\mathbf{M}_{\eta})
for some
\eta>0
. Denote by

\tau=\inf\{n\geq 1\ |\ X_{n}\in K\}

the first return time to
K
.
Then

\sum_{n\geq 1}\sup_{x\in K}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau\geq n)<\infty.

A weaker version of this result can be directly deduced from the so-called Foster’s recurrence criterion, which only assumes negative drift and bounds the expectation of the return time
\tau
. This criterion, or at least an exponential variant, was used in
[
6
,
7
]
. It will also play a role in several proofs below so we record the precise statement.

Lemma 2.4

(Foster’s criterion)
.

Suppose
(\Omega,f,(X_{n}))
satisfy conditions
(\mathbf{D})
and
(\mathbf{M}_{0})
. Then for every
x\in\Omega\smallsetminus K
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau)\leq\frac{f(x)}{\lambda_{1}}.

In particular, the expectation of
\tau
is uniformly bounded when the starting point of the chain varies in
K
, or in other terms
\sup_{x\in K}\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau\geq n)<\infty
. The goal of Lemma
2.3
is precisely to strengthen this inequality by allowing to switch the supremum and the sum.

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

It is a classical result, see for instance
[
19
, Proposition 11.3.2]
, or Foster’s original paper
[
16
]
. We recall the proof for completeness. Let
x\in\Omega\smallsetminus K
be a starting point. For
n\geq 0
, set
Y_{n}=\mathbbm{1}_{\tau>n}f(X_{n})
, and
\mathcal{F}_{n}
the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
. We can bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}[Y_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\mathbbm{1}_{\tau>n+1}f(X_{n+1})|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{x}[\mathbbm{1}_{\tau>n}f(X_{n+1})|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{\tau>n}\mathbb{E}_{x}[f(X_{n+1})|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{1}_{\tau>n}(f(X_{n})-\lambda_{1}).

where the case
n=0
uses that
x\notin K
. Taking expectation, we obtain

0\leq\mathbb{E}_{x}[Y_{n+1}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{x}[Y_{n}]-\lambda_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau>n)\leq\dots\leq\mathbb{E}_{x}[Y_{0}]-\lambda_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau>k)

and passing to the limit, we conclude that

\mathbb{E}_{x}[\tau]=\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau>k)\leq\frac{f(x)}{\lambda_{1}}.

∎

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Case 1:
(\mathbf{SD})
is satisfied
.

Denote by
(Y_{n})
the Markov chain on
\mathbb{R}
with i.i.d. increments given by
Z_{1}
and set

\tau^{(Y)}=\{\inf n\geq 1,\,Y_{n}\leq R_{0}\}

the first return time of
(Y_{n})
to
(-\infty,R_{0}]
. Using the dominance condition
(\mathbf{SD})
, one sees by induction on
n
that for every
x\in\Omega
such that
f(x)>R_{0}
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau\geq n)\geq\mathbb{P}_{f(x)}(\tau^{(Y)}\geq n).

Now for
n\geq 2
,
x\in K
, we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau\geq n)

\displaystyle=\int_{(R,+\infty)}\mathbb{P}_{X_{1}}(\tau\geq n-1)\,d\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(R,+\infty)}\mathbb{P}_{f(X_{1})}(\tau^{(Y)}\geq n-1)\,d\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(R,+\infty)}\mathbb{P}_{R+Z_{0}}(\tau^{(Y)}\geq n-1)\,d\mathbb{P}(Z_{0})

where the last inequality is obtained using assumption
(\mathbf{SD})
and the fact that
t\mapsto\mathbb{P}_{t}(\tau^{(Y)}\geq n-1)
is increasing on
(R,+\infty)
. Observing that the right-hand side no longer depends on
x
and summing over
n
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 2}\sup_{x\in K}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau\geq n)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(R,+\infty)}\sum_{n\geq 2}\mathbb{P}_{R+Z_{0}}(\tau^{(Y)}\geq n-1)\,d\mathbb{P}(Z_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{(R,+\infty)}\mathbb{E}_{R+Z_{0}}(\tau^{(Y)})\,d\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}).

However, Foster’s criterion (Lemma
2.4
) gives for every
t>R
, the bound
\mathbb{E}_{t}(\tau^{(Y)})\leq(t-R)/\lambda_{1}
. Hence we may conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 2}\sup_{x\in K}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau\geq n)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\int_{(R,+\infty)}Z_{0}\,d\mathbb{P}(Z_{0})

\displaystyle<\infty.

Case 2:
(\mathbf{D})
and
(\mathbf{M_{\eta}})
are satisfied from some
\eta>0
.

We can assume
\eta\in(0,1)
.
Since
\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau\geq n)\leq\frac{1}{n^{1+\eta}}\mathbb{E}_{x}[\tau^{1+\eta}]
, it is enough to prove that the family of return times
(\tau\,|\,X_{0}=x)_{x\in K}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{1+\eta}
. More generally, we show that for every
x\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau^{1+\eta})\leq\left(\frac{f(x)}{\lambda_{1}}\right)^{1+\eta}+M\frac{f(x)}{\lambda_{1}}+M_{1}

(1)

where
M_{1}=1+4\left((M^{\frac{1}{1+\eta}}+R_{0})^{1+\eta}+\frac{M(M+R_{0})}{\lambda_{1}}\right)
.

We first bound the return time for a starting point oustide of
K
. On the one hand, notice that we already know by Foster’s criterion that for every
x\in\Omega\smallsetminus K
,
\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau)\leq\frac{f(x)}{\lambda_{1}}
.
In particular, Jensen’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau^{\eta})\leq\left(\frac{f(x)}{\lambda_{1}}\right)^{\eta}.

(2)

On the other hand, the combination of
(\mathbf{D})
and
(\mathbf{M_{\eta}})
yields a strong negative drift for
f(X_{1})^{1+\eta}
as we now explain. Observe that the inequality

(1+t)^{1+\eta}\leq 1+(1+\eta)t+\lvert t\rvert^{1+\eta}

where
-1\leq t\leq+\infty
,
applied to
t=\frac{f(X_{1})-f(x)}{f(x)}

gives

f(X_{1})^{1+\eta}\leq f(x)^{1+\eta}+(1+\eta)\left(f(X_{1})-f(x)\right)f(x)^{\eta}+\lvert f(X_{1})-f(x)\rvert^{1+\eta},

Taking expectation, we obtain for every
x\in\Omega\smallsetminus K
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}(f(X_{1})^{1+\eta})\leq f(x)^{1+\eta}-(1+\eta)\lambda_{1}f(x)^{\eta}+M.

(3)

Inequalities (
2
) and (
3
) allow to apply a general result of Tweedie
[
22
, Theorem 3(iii)]
(with
A=K
,
\psi(k)=(1+\eta)\lambda^{1+\eta}_{1}k^{\eta}-M
,
g(x)=f(x)^{1+\eta}
,
g_{1}(x)=(1+\eta)\lambda_{1}f(x)^{\eta}-M
) to obtain for all
x\in\Omega\smallsetminus K
,

\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau^{1+\eta})\leq\left(\frac{f(x)}{\lambda_{1}}\right)^{1+\eta}+M\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau)

so by Foster’s criterion again, for
x\in\Omega\smallsetminus K
,

\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau^{1+\eta})\leq\left(\frac{f(x)}{\lambda_{1}}\right)^{1+\eta}+M\frac{f(x)}{\lambda_{1}}.

The general bound announced in (
1
) for every
x
in
\Omega
follows using the moment condition
(\mathbf{M_{\eta}})
.

∎

2.2
Conservation of mass

We show conclusion
(i)
in Theorem
D
: random walks on
\Omega
satisfying the stochastic dominance assumption
(\mathbf{SD})
, or having a negative drift and increments uniformly bounded in
L^{1+\eta}
, have no escape of mass.

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose
(\Omega,f,(X_{n}))
satisfy condition
(\mathbf{SD})
, or conditions
(\mathbf{D})
and
(\mathbf{M}_{\eta})
for some
\eta>0
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
R>0
such that for
x\in\Omega
,
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}[f(X_{n})>R]\,<\,\varepsilon+\frac{f(x)}{n\lambda_{1}}

Remark
.

Choosing
\varepsilon
very small, we see that the first time
n\geq 1
such that
\mathbb{P}_{x}[f(X_{n})>R]<1
is bounded above by
\frac{f(x)}{\lambda_{1}(1-\varepsilon)}\simeq\frac{f(x)}{\lambda_{1}}
. When
f(x)\to+\infty
, this bound is essentially optimal, as one can see by considering a deterministic walk by translation of
-\lambda_{1}
, for which the return time is equal to
\frac{f(x)-R}{\lambda_{1}}
.

The key is to use Lemma
2.3
to show the following renewal estimate.

Lemma 2.6

(Renewal estimate)
.

Suppose
(\Omega,f,(X_{n}))
satisfy condition
(\mathbf{SD})
, or conditions
(\mathbf{D})
and
(\mathbf{M}_{\eta})
for some
\eta>0
. Then for any
\alpha>0
, there exists
\ell\geq 0
, such that for all
x\in K
,
n\geq\ell
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\exists i\in\{n-\ell,\dots,n\}:\,X_{i}\in K)>1-\alpha.

In words, this lemma states that for any interval of integers
I\subseteq\mathbb{N}
of large length
\ell
, the walk starting from an arbitrary point in
K
has a very good chance to come back to
K
during
I
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha>0
.
According to Lemma
2.3
, we may choose
\ell\geq 0
such that

\sum_{n\geq\ell}\sup_{x\in K}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau>n)<\alpha.

Let us check that
\ell
satisfies the conclusion of the lemma. Given
n\geq\ell
, it is convenient to set
I_{n}=\{n-\ell,\dots,n\}
. Assume that
A_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a constant such that for
x\in K
,
k\in\{\ell,\dots,n\}
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\forall i\in I_{k},\,X_{i}\notin K)\leq A_{n}.

We can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(\forall i\in I_{n+1},\,X_{i}\notin K)\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\mathbb{P}_{x}(\forall i\in I_{n+1},\,X_{i}\notin K;\tau\leq n)+\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau>n)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n-\ell}\,\mathbb{P}_{x}(\forall i\in I_{n+1},\,X_{i}\notin K;\tau=k)+\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau>n)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n-\ell}\,A_{n}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau=k)+\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau>n)

\displaystyle\leq A_{n}+\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau>n).

Hence, defining a sequence
(A_{n})_{n\geq\ell}
by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}A_{\ell}=0\\
\forall n\geq\ell,\ A_{n+1}=A_{n}+\sup_{x\in K}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau>n)\end{array}\right.

we can see by induction on
n
using the above inequality that for all
x\in K
,
n\geq\ell
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\forall i\in I_{n},\,X_{i}\notin K)\leq A_{n}<\alpha.

∎

Let us now prove the conservation of mass anounced above.

Proof of Proposition
2.5
.

We first deal with the case where
x\in K
. We denote by
\ell\geq 0
the constant given by Lemma
2.6
for
\alpha=\varepsilon/2
, then choose the constant
R>0
large enough so that for every
x\in K
and every
n\leq\ell
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}(f(X_{n})>R)<\varepsilon/2.

By Lemma
2.6
, this yields for
x\in K
, and arbitrary
n\geq\ell

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(f(X_{n})>R)

\displaystyle<\varepsilon/2+\mathbb{P}_{x}(f(X_{n})>R;\,\,\exists i\in\{n-\ell,\dots,n\},X_{i}\in K)

\displaystyle<\varepsilon.

To deal with the complementary case
x\in\Omega\smallsetminus K
, denote by
\tau
the first return time to
K
and notice that for
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(f(X_{n})>R)

\displaystyle<\mathbb{P}_{x}(f(X_{n})>R;\,\tau\leq n)+\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau>n)

\displaystyle<\varepsilon+\frac{\mathbb{E}_{x}[\tau]}{n}.

In view of Foster’s criterion recalled in Lemma
2.4
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{x}[\tau]\leq\frac{f(x)}{\lambda_{1}}
as soon as
f(x)>R_{0}
. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(f(X_{n})>R\right)<\varepsilon+\frac{f(x)}{n\lambda_{1}}

which finishes the proof of Proposition
2.5

∎

We conclude Section
2.2
by a counterexample to Proposition
2.5
when the walk
(\Omega,f,X_{n})
is only assumed to satisfy
(\mathbf{D})
and
(\mathbf{M}_{0})
.

Example 1

(Escape of mass)
.

Let
[0,\frac{1}{2}]\rightarrow[0,1),x\mapsto\alpha_{x}
be a continuous map such that
\alpha^{-1}(0)=0
. We consider the Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on
\Omega=[0,+\infty)
whose transition probability measures are given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mu_{0}=\delta_{0}\\
\mu_{x}=(1-\alpha_{x})\delta_{x}+\alpha_{x}\delta_{\alpha^{-1}_{x}}&\text{if $0<x\leq 1/2$}\\
\mu_{x}=(2x-1)\delta_{0}+(2-2x)\mu_{\frac{1}{2}}&\text{if $1/2\leq x\leq 1$}\\
\mu_{x}=\delta_{x-1}&\text{if $x\geq 1$.}\\
\end{array}\right.

Note that
\mu_{x}
depends continously on
x
.
Letting
f=\text{Id}
, the random walk
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfies

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\mathbb{E}_{x}[|f(X_{1})-f(x)|]\leq 1\hskip 11.74988pt\text{and}\hskip 11.74988pt\sup_{x\in(1,+\infty)}\mathbb{E}_{x}[f(X_{1})-f(x)]=-1.

However, given a fixed
x_{0}\in(0,1/2]
, one can choose the family
(\alpha_{x})_{x\in[0,1/2]}
so that for every
R>0
, there exists
n\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x_{0}}(f(X_{n})>R)\geq 1/2.

(4)

To see this, first observe that a trajectory with origin
x_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
stays at
x_{0}
for some time, then jumps out of
[0,1]
and comes back to it with constant increments of
-1
until reaching a point
x_{1}\in[0,1]
. Then, if
x_{1}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, the process repeats itself to give some point
x_{2}\in[0,1]
, and so on. Arguing step by step, we can choose the coefficients
\alpha_{x}
such that the return points
x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots
form an infinite sequence in
(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, satisfy
x_{i}>x_{i+1}
,
(x_{i})_{i\geq 0}\to 0
, and so that the sequence
(\alpha_{x_{i}})_{i\geq 0}
decreases to
0
fast enough to have for every
i\geq 1
, some
n_{i}\geq 1
for which

\mathbb{P}_{x_{0}}(X_{n_{i}}=x_{i})\geq 1-1/i.

Let us now check the property (
4
) announced above. Fix
R>0
.
For
i,k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x_{0}}(X_{n_{i}+k}\leq R)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x_{0}}(X_{n_{i}+k}\leq R;X_{n_{i}}=x_{i})+\mathbb{P}_{x_{0}}(X_{n_{i}+k}\leq R;X_{n_{i}}\neq x_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x_{i}}(X_{k}\leq R)+1/i.

(5)

Assume
i
to be large enough so that
\alpha_{x_{i}}^{-1}>R
and set
k_{i}=\lfloor\alpha^{-1}_{x_{i}}-R\rfloor
the greatest integer smaller than
\alpha^{-1}_{x_{i}}-R
.
We can then write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x_{i}}(X_{k_{i}}\leq R)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x_{i}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k_{i}}=x_{i})

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{x_{i}})^{k_{i}}

\displaystyle=e^{k_{i}\log(1-\alpha_{x_{i}})}\underset{i\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}e^{-1}.

(6)

Equations (
1
) and (
1
) give that for large
i
,

\mathbb{P}_{x_{0}}(X_{n_{i}+k_{i}}\leq R)\leq 1/2

as announced in (
4
).

2.3
Recurrence of empirical measures

We show conclusion
(ii)
in Theorem
D
: random walks on
\Omega
satisfying the stochastic dominance assumption (
\mathbf{SD}
), or having a negative drift and increments uniformly bounded in
L^{1+\eta}
, must have positive recurrent trajectories.

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose
(\Omega,f,(X_{n}))
satisfy condition
(\mathbf{SD})
, or conditions
(\mathbf{D})
and
(\mathbf{M}_{\eta})
for some
\eta>0
. For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
R>0
such that for every
x\in\Omega
,
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost every
(X_{n})
,

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\card\{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\ |\ f(X_{k})>R\}<\varepsilon.

Proof.

We start with some notation.
Let
\tau_{i}
be the
i
-th return time in
K
. More precisely,
\tau_{0}=0
by convention, and for all
i\geq 1
,

\tau_{i}=\inf\{n>\tau_{i-1}\ |\ X_{n}\in K\}.

We also let
Z_{i}=\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}
be the length of the
i
-th excursion and

Z^{ext}_{i}=\card\{n\in\{\tau_{i-1},\dots,\tau_{i}-1\}\ |\ f(X_{n})>R\}

the time spent outside of
\{f\leq R\}
during the excursion.

To prove Proposition
2.7
, it is sufficient to show that if
R>0
is large enough, then for any
x\in K
,
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely, one has for large
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z^{ext}_{i}<\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}Z_{i}.

(7)

We explain how to obtain (
7
). The assumption
(\mathbf{SD})
, or the conditions
(\mathbf{D})
and
(\mathbf{M}_{\eta})
, both guarantee that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in K}\mathbb{P}_{x}(Z^{ext}_{1}\neq 0)\underset{R\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

(8)

Indeed, we can write for
x\in K
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(Z^{ext}_{1}\neq 0)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}(Z^{ext}_{1}\neq 0;\,\tau\leq N)+\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau\geq N)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}(\exists i\leq N,f(X_{i})>R;\,\tau\leq N)+\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau\geq N)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\leq N}\mathbb{P}_{x}(f(X_{i})>R)+\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau\geq N)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N(R_{0}+NM)}{R}+\sup_{y\in K}\mathbb{P}_{y}(\tau\geq N)

where
M>0
is a fixed uniform
L^{1}
-bound on the positive increment of the walk, i.e. such that
\mathbb{E}_{x}[\max\left(0,f(X_{1})-f(x)\right)]<M
. The right-hand side of the inequality does not depend on
x\in K
. In view of Lemma
2.3
, we can choose
N
large, then
R
even larger, to have the right-hand side arbitrarily close to
0
, whence (
8
).

Observing that
\mathbb{E}_{x}(Z^{ext}_{1})\leq\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau\mathbbm{1}_{Z^{ext}_{1}\neq 0})
and using the uniform integrability of the variables
(\tau\,|\,X_{0}=x)_{x\in K}
given by Lemma
2.3
, we deduce from (
8
) that

\sup_{x\in K}\mathbb{E}_{x}(Z^{ext}_{1})\underset{R\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

In particular, we can choose
R
so that
\sup_{x\in K}\mathbb{E}_{x}(Z^{ext}_{1})<\varepsilon/2
.
Fix
x\in K
. Denote by
\mathcal{F}_{i}
the sub
\sigma
-algebra of
\Omega
generated by
(X_{0},\dots,X_{\tau_{i}})
. The random variables
Z^{ext}_{i}
,
i\geq 1
, statisfy for
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}(Z^{ext}_{i}\geq n\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{i-1})\leq\sup_{y\in K}\mathbb{P}_{y}(\tau\geq n).

As the right-hand side of the inequality is summable by Lemma
2.3
, we may apply Kolmogorov’s law of large numbers for dependent variables
[
9
, Theorem A.6]
and obtain that
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z^{ext}_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{x}[Z^{ext}_{i}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]+o(1).

Moreover, by our choice for
R
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{x}[Z^{ext}_{i}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]<\varepsilon/2
.
We infer that for large
n\geq 0
,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z^{ext}_{i}<\varepsilon\,\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}Z_{i}

keeping in mind that by definition
Z_{i}\geq 1
. This concludes the proof of inequality (
7
), whence the proposition.
∎

We conclude with a counterexample to Proposition
2.5
when the walk
(\Omega,f,X_{n})
is only assumed to satisfy
(\mathbf{D})
and
(\mathbf{M}_{0})
.

Example 2

(Escape of empirical measures)
.

Given a real number
r\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\lfloor r\rfloor
the largest integer
k
such that
k\leq r
.
Fix a sequence
(x_{i})_{n\geq 3}\in(0,1)^{\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}}
decreasing to
0
, and consider the Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on
\Omega=[0,+\infty)
whose transition probability measures are given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mu_{0}=\delta_{0}&\\
\mu_{x_{i}}=(1-\frac{1}{i\log i})\delta_{x_{i+1}}+\frac{1}{i\log i}\delta_{x_{i+1}+\lfloor i\sqrt{\log i}\rfloor}&\text{ for $i\geq 3$}\\
\mu_{x}=\delta_{x-1}&\text{ if $x>1$}\\
x\mapsto\mu_{x}\ \text{affine on each segment}\ [x_{i+1},x_{i}].&\end{array}\right.

The chain
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
has a continuous family of transition probability measures and satisfies, for
f=\text{Id}
,

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\mathbb{E}_{x}[|f(X_{1})-f(x)|]\leq 3\hskip 11.74988pt\text{and}\hskip 11.74988pt\sup_{x\in(1,+\infty)}\mathbb{E}_{x}[f(X_{1})-f(x)]=-1.

However, for
\mathbb{P}_{x_{3}}
-almost every trajectory
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, for every
\varepsilon,R>0
, there exists
n_{0}\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{0}}\card\{k\in\{1,\dots,n_{0}\}\ |\ f(X_{k})\leq R\}<\varepsilon.

(9)

To see this, denote by
\tau_{j}
the
j
-th return time to
[0,1]
. In other words,
\tau_{0}=0
by convention, and for all
j\geq 1
,

\tau_{j}=\inf\{n>\tau_{j-1}\ |\ X_{n}\in[0,1]\}.

The random variables
(\tau_{j}-\tau_{j-1})_{j\geq 1}
varying under
\mathbb{P}_{x_{3}}
are independent of respective laws

\displaystyle(1-\frac{1}{(j+2)\log(j+2)})\delta_{1}+\frac{1}{(j+2)\log(j+2)}\delta_{\lfloor(j+2)\sqrt{\log(j+2)}\rfloor+1}

(
j\geq 1
)

As the sequence
(\frac{1}{n\log n})_{n\geq 3}
is not summable, the converse of the Borel-Cantelli lemma yields that
\mathbb{P}_{x_{3}}
-almost surely we have
\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\frac{\tau_{j}-\tau_{j-1}}{j}=+\infty
.
In particular, given
\varepsilon,R>0
, there exists
j_{0}\geq 1
such that

j_{0}<\frac{\varepsilon}{R+1}\,\tau_{j_{0}}.

Setting
n_{0}=\tau_{j_{0}}
, we get

\card\{k\in\{1,\dots,n_{0}\}\ |\ X_{k}\leq 1\}<\frac{\varepsilon}{R+1}n_{0}.

Finally, observing that for any
k\geq 0
such that
X_{k}\leq R
there exists a time
j\in\{k,\dots,k+\lfloor R\rfloor\}
for which
X_{j}\in[0,1]
, we conclude

\card\{k\in\{1,\dots,n_{0}\}\ |\ X_{k}\leq R\}<\varepsilon n_{0}

as announced in (
9
).

3
Recurrence criterion for homogeneous chains

We apply the results of the previous section to the particular case of homogeneous random walks and obtain a handy criterion to check that the mass or the empirical measures of the walk do not accumulate near a given subset (Proposition
3.3
below).
This criterion will be applied in two different contexts, first to prove non-escape at infinity, and later to derive non-accumulation on invariant homogeneous subspaces.

3.1
The controlled drift condition

In the whole section we fix
\Omega
a locally compact topological space,
\Gamma
a topological semigroup acting continously on
\Omega
, and
\mu
a Borel probability measure on
\Gamma
. We call
\mu
-walk on
\Omega
the Markov chain on
\Omega
whose law of transition at
x\in\Omega
is
\mu\ast\delta_{x}
, image of
\mu
under
g\mapsto gx
.
We denote by
\overline{\Omega}=\Omega\cup\{\infty\}
the Alexandroff compactification of
\Omega
, and write
\mathscr{P}(\overline{\Omega})
the collection of its subsets. Given
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(\overline{\Omega})\times\mathscr{P}(\overline{\Omega})
, we have a decomposition
\mathcal{S}=\cup_{Y\in p_{1}(\mathcal{S})}\{Y\}\times\mathcal{S}_{Y}
where
p_{1}
is the first coordinate projection map and
\mathcal{S}_{Y}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(\overline{\Omega})
is the fibre above
Y
in
\mathcal{S}
. Finally, given a finite-dimensional real vector space
V
, we set
\SL^{\pm}(V)=\{g\in\GL(V)\ |\ \det g=\pm 1\}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(\overline{\Omega})\times\mathscr{P}(\overline{\Omega})
,
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(\Omega)
. We say that the
\mu
-walk has a
controlled drift away from
\mathcal{S}
on
\mathcal{A}
if:

•

There exists a linear representation
\Phi:\Gamma\rightarrow\SL^{\pm}(V)
on a finite-dimensional normed real vector space
V
such that

\int_{G}\log\|\Phi(g)\|\,d\mu(g)<+\infty.

•

There exist constants
\lambda,C>0
such that for any
\alpha>0
, there exists
N\geq 1
such that for
n\geq N
, for any
Y\in p_{1}(\mathcal{S})
, any compact subset
K\subseteq\Omega
such that
K\cap Y=\varnothing
, any
Q\in\mathcal{A}
, there is
Y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{Y}
, and a measurable function
f:\Omega\to[0,+\infty]
which is bounded on
K
, whose upper level sets are neighborhoods of
Y^{\prime}
, and which satisfies

(i)

\exists\,T\in[0,+\infty)
,
\forall x\in Q\cap f^{-1}[T,+\infty)
,

\displaystyle\mu^{*n}(g\in G\ |\ f(gx)\leq f(x)-n\lambda)\geq 1-\alpha

(probable decrease)

(ii)

\forall x\in f^{-1}[0,+\infty)
,
\forall g\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle f(gx)-f(x)\leq C\log\lVert\Phi(g)\rVert.

(control of variations)

Remark
.

1) Strictly speaking, the definition expresses a drift away from some
Y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{Y}
which could have nothing to do with
Y
. However, in all the applications below, we will always have that
Y\subseteq Y^{\prime}
for all
Y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{Y}
, so in particular, the walk does drift away from
Y
too. We choose to sum it up using the terminology of drift away from
\mathcal{S}
.

3) The condition
(ii)
implies that
f^{-1}[0,+\infty)
is
\Gamma
-invariant.

2) If
\mathcal{A}=\{\Omega\}
then we simply say that the
\mu
-walk has a
controlled drift away from
\mathcal{S}
. If additionally,
\mathcal{S}=\{\infty,\infty\}
, we talk about
controlled drift away from infinity
. Note that
f
is proper in this case: its sublevel sets have compact closure in
\Omega
.

4) The map
\Phi
will later be referred to as the
control function
for the drift. Indeed, its role is to bound the growth of
f
in the unlikely event its value goes up along the walk.

To get more familiar with the notion of controlled drift, we start by exploring some stability properties. Given
\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(\overline{\Omega})\times\mathscr{P}(\overline{\Omega})
, we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{0}\vee\mathcal{S}_{1}
the set of pairs
(Y,Y^{\prime}_{0}\cup Y^{\prime}_{1})
where
Y\in p_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{0})\cap p_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{1})
,
Y^{\prime}_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{0,Y}
,
Y^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{1,Y}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

a)

If the
\mu
-walk on
\Omega
has a controlled drift away from both
\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1}
, then it has a controlled drift away from
\mathcal{S}_{0}\vee\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

b)

If the
\mu
-walk on
\Omega
has a controlled drift away from infinity and a controlled drift away from
\mathcal{S}
on compact sets of
\Omega
, then it has a controlled drift away from
\mathcal{S}
.

We explain
a)
. The proof of the second assertion is much more involved and postponed to Section
5
where it will be used.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
a).

We respectively index by
0
and
1
the notations referring to
\mathcal{S}_{0}
,
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Let
C_{i},\lambda_{i}
be the constants given by the controlled drift assumption
3.1
for
\mathcal{S}_{i}
. Set
C=\max(C_{0},C_{1})
,
\lambda=\min(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1})
. Let
\alpha>0
, then
N_{i}
as in
3.1
. Set
N=\max(N_{0},N_{1})
,
n\geq N
. Let
Y\in p_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{0})\cap p_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{1})
and consider a compact set
K\subset\Omega\smallsetminus Y
.
Let
Y^{\prime}_{i},f_{i},T_{i}
be given by
3.1
. We set
Y^{\prime}=Y^{\prime}_{0}\cup Y^{\prime}_{1}
,

f=\max(f_{0},f_{1})

and claim that the map
f
satisfies all the requirements in Definition
3.1
(with
2\alpha
instead of
\alpha
). Indeed, all the conditions are obvious except the one entitled “probable decrease”. To check it, we choose
T\geq\max(T_{0},T_{1})
and large enough so that

\mu^{*n}(g\in\Gamma\,|\,T_{0}+C_{0}\Phi_{0}(g)\leq T-n\lambda)\geq 1-\alpha

and the same holds when we replace
0
by
1
.
Now let

. If
f_{1}(x)=f(x)
, then

\mu^{*n}(g\in\Gamma\,|\,f_{1}(gx)\leq f(x)-n\lambda_{1})\geq 1-\alpha

On the other hand, distinguishing the cases
f_{0}(x)<T_{0}
and
f_{0}(x)\geq T_{0}
, the condition on

guarantees that

\mu^{*n}(g\in\Gamma\,|\,f_{0}(gx)\leq f(x)-n\lambda)\geq 1-\alpha

so combining these inequalities

\mu^{*n}(g\in\Gamma\,|\,f(gx)\leq f(x)-n\lambda)\geq 1-2\alpha.

The same holds if
f_{0}(x)=f(x)
by symmetry.

∎

3.2
Application to recurrence

The proposition below states that a walk that drifts away from
\mathcal{S}
does not accumulate on
\mathcal{S}
.
In particular, when
\mathcal{S}=\{\infty,\infty\}
, the conclusion exactly means that the walk has neither escape of mass nor escape of empirical measures at infinity.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(\Omega,G,\mu)
be as above and
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(\overline{\Omega})\times\mathscr{P}(\overline{\Omega})
. Assume that the
\mu
-walk on
\Omega

\mathcal{S}
. Then for every
Y\in p_{1}(\mathcal{S})

(i)

for every

;

(ii)

for

-almost every instructions

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\card\{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\ |\ g_{k}\dots g_{1}x\in O^{\prime}\}<\varepsilon.

The proof can be summarized as follows. If
\alpha>0
is small enough,

is large enough, then for every

, every compact set

for the

-walk on

which can be extended to the
\mu
-walk.

We begin with a lemma that will allow to check condition
\emph{3.}
in Lemma

.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Phi:\Gamma\rightarrow\SL^{\pm}(V)
be a linear representation of
\Gamma
on a finite-dimensional normed vector space

such that

\int_{G}\log\|\Phi(g)\|\,d\mu(g)<+\infty

Then for all
\eta>0
, there exists
\alpha>0,N^{\prime}\geq 0
such that for all

, if

denotes the standard realisation of

for

\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{\prime}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\alpha]}]\leq n\eta.

Proof.

The law of large numbers
[
9
, Lemma 4.27]
guarantees that there exists a constant
\ell\geq 0
such that the sequence of real random variables
(\frac{1}{n}Z_{n})_{n\geq 1}
converges to

in

. This convergence also holds for

as it has the same law. Now let

,

.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{\prime}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\alpha]}]

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\ell+\mathbb{E}[\lvert\frac{1}{n}Z^{\prime}_{n}-\ell\rvert]

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(\ell+1)

if
n
is large enough. The result follows if we specify

above.
∎

We now apply the results of Section

.

Proof.

Let

be given by Definition

.

We show that the

Proof of Proposition

.

Fix

,

4
Non-escape at infinity

We now apply the results of the previous section to show that random walks on finite-volume homogeneous spaces have neither escape of mass nor escape of empirical measures at infinity.
In what follows,
G
denotes a real Lie group,
\mathfrak{g}
its Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{r}
its amenable radical,
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{r}
the largest quotient of
\mathfrak{g}
which is semisimple without compact factors, and we denote by
\Ad_{\mathfrak{s}}:G\to\Aut\mathfrak{s}
the adjoint action of
G
on
\mathfrak{s}
.
Given a probability measure
\mu
on
G
, we let
H_{\mathfrak{s}}\subset\Aut\mathfrak{s}
be the real algebraic group generated by the support of
(\text{Ad}_{\mathfrak{s}})_{\ast}\mu
and call
H^{nc}_{\mathfrak{s}}
its non-compact part, i.e. the smallest normal algebraic subgroup of
H_{\mathfrak{s}}
such that
H_{\mathfrak{s}}/H^{nc}_{\mathfrak{s}}
is compact.

Our goal is to show the following theorem, which is due to Benoist and Quint
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
in the case where
\mu
has a finite exponential moment on
\mathfrak{s}
.

Theorem A
′
.

(Recurrence of semisimple random walks)

Let
G
be a real Lie group,
\Lambda
a lattice in G, and
\Omega=G/\Lambda
.
Assume that
\mu
is a probability measure on
G
such that

1.

\mu
has a finite first moment on
\mathfrak{s}
:
\int_{G}\log\lVert\Ad_{\mathfrak{s}}g\rVert\ d\mu(g)<+\infty

;

2.

H^{nc}_{\mathfrak{s}}
is semisimple.

Then, for every compact set
K\subset\Omega
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a compact set
K^{\prime}\subset\Omega
such that for every
x\in K
,

(i)

for every
n\geq 0
,
(\mu^{*n}*\delta_{x})(K^{\prime})>1-\varepsilon
;

(ii)

for
\mu^{\otimes\mathbb{N}^{*}}
-almost every instructions
(g_{i})_{i\geq 1}
,

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\card\{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\ |\ g_{k}\dots g_{1}x\in K^{\prime}\}>1-\varepsilon.

Remark
.

In particular, every Radon
\mu
-stationary measure on
\Omega
must have finite mass.

By Proposition
3.3
, the proof of
Theorem A′
reduces to showing that the
\mu
-walk on
\Omega
has a controlled drift away from infinity.
We first check that this is the case if
\Omega=\SL_{d}(\mathbb{R})/\SL_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
and then deduce the result for general quotients
\Omega=G/\Lambda
.

4.1
The Benoist-Quint drift function on
\SL_{d}(\mathbb{R})/\SL_{d}(\mathbb{Z})

We prove
Theorem A′
in the particular case where
G=\SL_{d}(\mathbb{R})
,
\Lambda=\SL_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
for some
d\geq 2
.
The drift functions we use were defined (in an exponential form) by Benoist and Quint in
[
5
]
. They are inspired by former works of Eskin, Margulis, Mozes
[
14
,
15
]
. We begin by recalling their construction.

Fix a probability measure
\mu
on
\SL_{d}(\mathbb{R})
, and let
H
be the algebraic group generated by the support of
\mu
.
We assume that the non-compact part
H^{nc}
of
H
is semisimple, or equivalently that
H
is reductive with compact center. Consider the representation of
H
on the exterior algebra
V=\wedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
and decompose it into its isotypical components for the induced action of
H^{nc}
:

V=\oplus_{j\in J}V^{(j)}.

Alternatively, denoting by
H^{\circ}
the identity component of
H
in the Zariski topology, each
V^{(j)}
is obtained by summing together all the irreducible subrepresentations of
H^{\circ}
on
V
with a given highest weight. The action of
H
on
V
then induces an action of the group of connected components
F=H/H^{\circ}
on the set
\{V^{(j)},j\in J\}
which stabilizes the subspace
V^{H^{nc}}
of
H^{nc}
-invariant vectors. By the law of large numbers
[
9
, Theorem 10.9, Corollary 10.12]
, we can associate to each representation
V^{(j)}
a Lyapunov exponent
\lambda_{1}^{(j)}\in\mathbb{R}
, that quantifies the exponential growth rate of the walk on
V^{(j)}
and only depends on the
F
-orbit of
V^{(j)}
. Moreover, by semisimplicity of
H^{nc}
, we have
\lambda^{(j)}_{1}\geq 0
, with
\lambda_{1}^{(j)}=0
if and and only if
V^{(j)}=V^{H^{nc}}

[
9
, Theorem 10.9 (f)]
.

Fix
H^{c}\subseteq H
a maximal compact subgroup of
H
and some
H^{c}
-invariant Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
This induces a natural
H^{c}
-invariant Euclidean norm on the exterior algebra
V
.
Given
v\in V
, write
v=\sum_{j\in J}v^{(j)}
according to the above decomposition of
V
, and define

\lvert v\rvert=\max_{j\in J}\lVert v^{(j)}\rVert^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}^{(j)}}}

with the convention that
\frac{1}{0}=+\infty
and

\lVert v^{(j)}\rVert^{+\infty}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{if}\ \lVert v^{(j)}\rVert<1\\
+\infty&\mbox{if}\ \lVert v^{(j)}\rVert\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

The following lemma expresses that the
\mu
-walk on
V
uniformly expands the quasi-norm of vectors
v
whose
H^{nc}
-invariant component is not too weighty.

Lemma 4.1
.

Given
k\geq 1
,
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
\alpha>0
, there exists
n_{0}\geq 0
such that for every
n\geq n_{0}
, for every finite family
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
of non-zero vectors in
\wedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying
0<\lvert v_{\ell}\rvert<+\infty
for each
\ell
, one has

\mu^{*n}\left(g\in H\ \left|\ \min_{1\leq\ell\leq k}\log\frac{\lvert gv_{\ell}\rvert}{\lvert v_{\ell}\rvert}\geq n\lambda\right.\right)\geq 1-\alpha.

Proof.

Up to replacing
\alpha
by
\alpha/k
, we may assume that
k=1
. For
v\in V
such that
0<\lvert v\rvert<+\infty
, write

v=\sum_{j\in J}v^{(j)},

and let

J_{v}=\{j\in J\ |\ \lvert v^{(j)}\rvert>0\}.

Then, for every
g\in H
, one can bound

\displaystyle\min_{j\in J_{v}}\log\frac{\lvert gv^{(j)}\rvert}{\lvert v^{(j)}\rvert}\leq\log\frac{\lvert gv\rvert}{\lvert v\rvert}\leq\max_{j\in J_{v}}\log\frac{\lvert gv^{(j)}\rvert}{\lvert v^{(j)}\rvert}.

(10)

For the left inequality, just consider
j
such that
\lvert v\rvert=\lvert v^{(j)}\rvert
, and for the right inequality, choose
j
for which
\lvert gv\rvert=\lvert gv^{(j)}\rvert
.

By the law of large numbers for product of random matrices
[
9
, Theorem 4.28 (b)]
, on each representation
V^{(j)}
such that
\lambda_{1}^{(j)}>0
, the sequence of functions

(g_{i})_{i\geq 1}\mapsto\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{\lvert g_{n}\dots g_{1}v^{(j)}\rvert}{\lvert v^{(j)}\rvert}

converges to
1
in

From local drift to global drift

Proof of Lemma

b).

\frac{c\lambda_{0}}{2}>2C_{1}\lambda_{1,\infty}

and a threshold

as in

containing

Y^{\prime}\in S_{Y}
,

associated to

3.1
for the drift away from

We are now all set up to define our drift function: for

c\cdot\frac{f_{0}(x)-T_{0}}{T^{\prime}-T_{0}}&\mbox{if}\ T_{0}\leq f_{0}(x)\leq T^{\prime}\\

Probable decrease

We prove that if the parameter

in

, with

.
We study separately different cases, according to the values of

imposes that

or

.

(a)

In this case, the conditions

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}c_{gx}f_{0}(gx)\leq cf_{0}(x)-nc\lambda_{0}\\
C_{1}\log\lVert\Phi_{1}(g)\rVert\leq\frac{nc\lambda_{0}}{2}\end{array}\right.

are simultaneously satisfied with

(b)

(c)

6
Equidistribution

The goal of this section is to establish Theorem
C
. We shall need the following lemma, obtained by Benoist and Quint
[
7
, Proposition 2.1]
in the greater generality of
S
-adic Lie groups, but with the additional assumption that
\Gamma
is compactly generated.
The proof is almost the same as in
[
7
]
, but we include it to explain in detail how to modify their argument to avoid this assumption.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
G
be a real Lie group,
\Lambda
a lattice in
G
,
\Gamma
a subgroup of
G
and denote by
L
be the centralizer of
\Gamma
in
G
.
Assume that
\overline{\Ad\Gamma}^{Z}
is Zariski connected semisimple and without compact factors.
Then the set
\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}(\Gamma)
is a countable union of
L
-orbits.

Proof.

Fix a dense countable subset
D\subset\Gamma
.
Given
Y\in\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}(\Gamma)
, there exists a finite family of elements
g_{1},\dots,g_{s}
in
D
generating a group
\Gamma_{f}
such that
\overline{\Ad\Gamma_{f}}^{Z}=\overline{\Ad\Gamma}^{Z}
and that moreover
\Gamma_{f}
acts transitively on the (finite) set of connected components of
Y
.
Let
g\in G
be such that
g\Lambda\in Y
, and write

H=g^{-1}\Gamma_{f}G_{Y}^{\circ}g.

Since
H\Lambda=g^{-1}Y
has finite volume,
H\cap\Lambda
is a lattice in
H
, and
H^{\circ}\cap\Lambda
is a lattice in
H^{\circ}
.
Because
H
and
H^{\circ}
are compactly generated, the groups
\Sigma=H\cap\Lambda
and
\Delta=H^{\circ}\cap\Lambda
are both finitely generated, and since
\Lambda
contains only countably many finitely generated subgroups, we may assume that they are fixed.
(The finitely generated group
\Gamma_{f}
was introduced precisely to ensure that
H
be compactly generated, so we can use the argument of
[
7
]
.)
Then,
H
belongs to the set
\mathcal{T}(G,\Delta,\Sigma)
of closed subgroups such that

(i)

\Sigma
is a lattice in
H
;

(ii)

\Delta=\Sigma\cap H^{\circ}
is a lattice in
H^{\circ}
;

(iii)

there exists a subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}
in
G
such that
\overline{\Ad\Gamma^{\prime}}^{Z}
is connected semisimple without compact factors and acts ergodically on
H/\Sigma
.

By
[
7
, Lemma 2.5]
, this set is countable, so we may assume that
H
is fixed.
Let
L_{f}
denote the centralizer of
\Gamma_{f}
in
G
, and note that
L_{f}^{\circ}=L^{\circ}
as their Lie algebras are given by the sets of invariant vectors of
\overline{\Ad{\Gamma_{f}}}^{Z}=\overline{\Ad{\Gamma}}^{Z}
.
By
[
7
, Lemma 2.2]
, the set of fixed points of
\Gamma_{f}
in
G/H
is a countable union of
L_{f}^{\circ}
-orbits, and therefore also a countable union of
L
-orbits.
Since
Y=gH\Lambda
and
gH
is a fixed point of
\Gamma_{f}
in
G/H
, the lemma follows.
∎

7
Conclusion

To conclude, we mention a few problems that were not discussed here, but might lead to interesting continuations of our study.

Compact factors.

When the group
H=\overline{\Ad\Gamma}^{Z}
is semisimple but is allowed to have compact factors, it is still possible to describe all ergodic
\mu
-stationary measures on
\Omega
.
Some might not be homogeneous, but they can nevertheless be written as an integral average of homogeneous measures
[
13
]
. It should be possible to obtain a generalization of Theorem
C
to this setting.

Drift function on
G
.

On a general quotient
G/\Lambda
, the Benoist-Quint drift function is essentially constructed by embedding
G/\Lambda
into some space of lattices
\SL_{d}(\mathbb{R})/\SL_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
.
It would be desirable to have an intrinsic construction of such a function, at least in the case where
G
is a linear algebraic group defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\Lambda=G(\mathbb{Z})
an arithmetic subgroup.
For example, this would lead to simple formulas for the optimal return time to a compact set, similar to those available for
\SL_{d}(\mathbb{R})/\SL_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Unipotency assumption.

It was originally suggested in
[
14
]
that Theorem
A
could hold under the assumption that
H=\overline{\Ad\Gamma}^{Z}
is generated by unipotents.
As observed by Emmanuel Breuillard
[
11
, Proposition 10.4]
, this is not true for conclusion
(i)
in general.
But this might be the case for
(ii)
, and
(i)
would then hold for the Cesàro averages
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mu^{*k}*\delta_{x}
.
In a slightly different direction, the theorem could be valid as stated if
H
is assumed to be perfect, i.e. if its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
satisfies
\mathfrak{h}=[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}]
.

No moment assumption.

As suggested at the end of
[
2
]
, one may hope that the results on homogeneous random walks obtained here, and in particular Theorem
A
, are still valid without any moment assumption.

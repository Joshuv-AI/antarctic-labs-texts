Bifurcations of robust features on surfaces in the Minkowski 3-space
Marco Antônio do Couto Fernandes

Abstract
We obtain the bifurcation of some special curves on generic 1-parameter families of surfaces in the Minkowski 3-space. The curves treated here are the locus of points where the induced pseudo metric is degenerate, the discriminant of the lines principal curvature, the parabolic curve and the locus of points where the mean curvature vanishes.

Introduction

We consider generic 1-parameter families of surfaces in the Minkowski 3-space
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
. The Lorentzian metric in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
induces a pseudo metric on a surface in
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
. The locus of points on
M
where the induced pseudo metric is degenerate is called the
Locus of Degeneracy
and is denoted by the
LD
for short.
The
LD
separates the surface into regions where the induced metric is Riemannian or Lorentzian.
On such regions the Gaussian and mean curvature of the surface are well defined. The zero sets of the Gaussian and mean curvature functions extends to the
LD
and are called, respectively, the
parabolic curve
and
mean curvature null curve
(
PC
and
MCNC
for short).

The equation of the lines of principal curvature also extends to the
LD
(
[
14
]
). In the Riemannian region the discriminant of that equation consists of the umbilic points. In the Lorentzian region it is the locus of points where two principal directions coincide and become lightlike and is labelled
Lightlike Principal Locus
,
LPL
for short.

We obtain in this paper the catalogue of all possible local bifurcations of the
LD
, the
LPL
, the
MCNC
and the
PC
when the surface is deformed in generic 1-parameter families of surfaces.
We deal in §
4
with the bifurcations at points in the Lorentzian region and in §
5
at points on the
LD
. We give some preliminaries in §
2
and define the notion of genericity and codimension in §
3
. We observe that at points on the Riemannian region of the surface the situation is identical to that of surfaces in the Euclidean space. Families of such surfaces are studied in, for example,
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
10
,
11
]
.

2
Preliminaries

The
Minkowski space

(\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3},\langle,\rangle)
is the vector
space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
endowed with the metric induced by the pseudo-scalar product

\langle\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\rangle=u_{0}v_{0}+u_{1}v_{1}-u_{2}v_{2}
, for any vectors
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2})
and
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=(v_{0},v_{1},v_{2})
in

\mathbb{R}^{3}
(see for example
[
7
,
15
]
for a treatment of the geometry of surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
).
A non-zero vector
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
is said to be
spacelike
if

\langle\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rangle>0
,
lightlike
if

\langle\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rangle=0
and
timelike
if
\langle\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rangle<0
.
The norm of a vector
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\in\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
is defined by

\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|=\sqrt{|\langle\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\rangle|}.

Let
M
be a smooth and regular surface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
and let

\mbox{\boldmath$x$}:U\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
be a local parametrisation of
M
.
We shall simplify notation and write
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}(U)=M
. Let

E=\langle{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{u},{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{u}\rangle,\quad F=\langle{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{u},{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{v}\rangle,\quad G=\langle{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{v},{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{v}\rangle

denote the coefficients of the first fundamental form of
M
with respect to the parametrisation
x
, where subscripts denote partial derivatives. The induced (pseudo) metric on
M
is Lorentzian (resp. Riemannian, degenerate) at
{\rm p}=\mbox{\boldmath$x$}(u,v)
if and only if
\delta(u,v)=(F^{2}-EG)(u,v)>0

(resp.
<0
,
=0
). The locus of points on the surface where the metric is degenerate
is called the
locus of degeneracy
and is denoted by
LD
.
We identify the
LD
on
M
with its pre-image in
U
by
x
. Then the
LD
(in
U
) is given by

LD=\{(u,v)\in U\,|\,\delta(u,v)=0\}.

A direction
(du,dv)\in T_{\rm p}M
is lightlike if and only if

Edu^{2}+2Fdudv+Gdv^{2}=0.

(1)

Equation (
1
) has two (resp. no) solutions when
{{\rm p}}
is in the Lorentzian (resp. Riemannian) region of
M
.
At points on the
LD
, there is a unique (double) solution of the equation.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
14
]
)

There is a local parametrization
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}:U\subset\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{2}\to\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{3}_{1}
of
M
at
p\in LD
such that the lightlike directions in
T_{q}M
is given by
\mathbb{R}.x_{u}
for every
q\in LD
, i.e.,
E=F=0
on the
LD
.

If
q\in M
belongs to the Lorentzian region, there is a local parametrization of
M
at
p
such that
\mathbb{R}.x_{u}
and
\mathbb{R}.x_{v}
are the lightlike directions in
T_{q}M
for every
q\in\mbox{\boldmath$x$}(U)
, i.e.,
E=G=0
on
U
.

At
{{\rm p}}\in M\setminus LD
, we have a well defined unit normal vector (the Gauss map)
\mbox{\boldmath$N$}=\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{u}\times\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{v}/||\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{u}\times\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{v}||
,
which is timelike (resp. spacelike) if
{\rm p}
is in the Riemannian (resp. Lorentzian) region of
M
.
(See
[
16
]
for a definition of an
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
-valued Gauss map.) The map
A_{{\rm p}}=-d\mbox{\boldmath$N$}_{{\rm p}}:T_{{\rm p}}M\to T_{{\rm p}}M
is a self-adjoint
operator on
M\setminus LD
. We denote by

l=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$N$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{uu}\rangle,\quad m=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$N$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{uv}\rangle,\quad n=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$N$},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{vv}\rangle

the coefficients of the second fundamental form on
M\setminus LD
.

When the eigenvalues
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
of
A_{p}
are real, they are called the
principal curvatures
and their associated eigenvectors the
principal directions

of
M
at
{{\rm p}}
. There are always two principal curvatures at each point on the Riemannian part of
M
but this is not always
true on its Lorentzian part.
A point
{\rm p}
on
M
is called an
umbilic point
if
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}
at
{\rm p}
(i.e., if
A_{p}
is a multiple of the identity map). It is called a
spacelike umbilic point
(resp.
timelike umbilic point
) if
{\rm p}
is in the Riemannian (resp. Lorentzian)
part of
M
.

The
lines of principal curvature
, which are the integral curves of the principal directions, are the solutions of the binary differential equation (BDE)

(Fn-Gm)dv^{2}+(En-Gl)dvdu+(Em-Fl)du^{2}=0.

(2)

The discriminant of BDE (
2
)

\{(u,v)\in U\,|\left((En-Gl)^{2}-4(Fn-Gm)(Em-Fl)\right)(u,v)=0\}

consists of the umbilic points in the Riemannian region of
M
and is the locus of points in the Lorentzian region where two principal directions coincide and become lightlike. It is labelled
Lightlike Principal Locus

(LPL)
in
[
13
]
. The principal directions are orthogonal when there are two of them at a given point; in particular one is spacelike and the other is timelike if the point is in the Lorentzian region.

The
Gaussian curvature
and
mean curvature
of
M
at
p
are
K=\det(A_{p})
and
H=-\frac{1}{2}tr(A_{p})
, respectively. We have

K=\frac{ln-m^{2}}{EG-F^{2}},\qquad H=\frac{lG-2mF+nE}{2(EG-F^{2})}.

The locus of points where the Gaussian and mean curvature are zero are called the
parabolic curve
(
PC
) and
mean curvature null curve
(
MCNC
), respectively.

One can extend the lines of principal curvature, the
LPL
, the
PC
and the
MCNC
across the
LD
as follows (
[
14
]
).
As equation (
2
) is homogeneous in
l,m,n
,
we can multiply these coefficients by
||\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{u}\times\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{v}||
and substitute them in the equation by

\bar{l}=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{u}\times\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{v},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{uu}\rangle,\quad\bar{m}=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{u}\times\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{v},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{uv}\rangle,\quad\bar{n}=\langle\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{u}\times\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{v},\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{vv}\rangle.

The new equation

(G\bar{m}-F\bar{n})dv^{2}+(G\bar{l}-E\bar{n})dudv+(F\bar{l}-E\bar{m})du^{2}=0

(3)

is defined at points on the
LD
and
its solutions are the same as those of equation (
2
) in the Riemannian and Lorentzian regions of
M
. A
lightlike umbilic point
is defined as a point on the
LD
where all the coefficients of equation (
3
) vanish (see
[
18
]
).
This occurs if and only if the
LD
is singular (
[
18
]
). The discriminant of the BDE
(\ref{eq:principalLD})
extends the
LPL
to points on the
LD
. Thus, a point
p=\mbox{\boldmath$x$}(u,v)
belongs to the
LPL
of
M
when

\tilde{\delta}(u,v)=\left((E\bar{n}-G\bar{l})^{2}-4(F\bar{n}-G\bar{m})(E\bar{m}-F\bar{l})\right)(u,v)=0.

For the
PC
, note that
K=0
if and only if
\bar{l}\bar{n}-\bar{m}^{2}=0
. Thus, the
PC
extends to points on the
LD
as the zero set of the function
\bar{K}=\bar{l}\bar{n}-\bar{m}^{2}
. Similarly, the
MCNC
extends to the
LD
as the zero set of the function
\bar{H}=\bar{l}G-2\bar{m}F+\bar{n}E
.

We use here some concepts from singularity theory (see
[
1
,
19
]
). Two germs of functions
f_{i}:(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{m},0),i=1,2
are said to be
\mathcal{R}
-equivalent
if
f_{2}=f_{1}\circ h^{-1}
for some germ of a diffeomorphism
h:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{n},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{n},0)
. Similarly,
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are said to be
\mathcal{L}
-
equivalent
when there is a germ of a diffeomorphism
k:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{m},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{m},0)
such that
f_{2}=k\circ f_{1}
. Finally,
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are
\mathcal{A}
-
equivalent
if there exist germs of diffeomorphisms
h
and
k
such that
f_{2}=k\circ f_{1}\circ h^{-1}
.

The simple singularities of germs of functions (
m=1
) are classified by Arnold
[
1
]
. Representatives of their
\mathcal{R}
-orbits when
n=2
are as follows

A_{k}:\pm(x^{2}\pm y^{k+1}),k\geq 1,\,D_{k}:x^{2}y\pm y^{k-1},k\geq 4,\,E_{6}:x^{3}+y^{4},\,E_{7}:x^{3}+xy^{3},\,E_{8}:x^{3}+y^{5}.

For
n\geq 3
one adds the quadratic form
\pm x_{3}^{2}\pm\cdots\pm x_{n}^{2}
to the above normal forms.

We denote by
j^{k}_{q}f
the
k
-jet
of a germ of a smooth/analytic function
f:(\mathbb{R}^{n},q)\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e., its Taylor polynomial of order
k
at
q
, and by
J^{k}(n,1)
the space of all
k
-jets of germs of functions. If
q
is the origin, then we denote the
k
-jet by
j^{k}f
. When
n=2
, we write the
k
-jet, at the origin, of a function
f
in the form
j^{k}f=\sum_{s=0}^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{s}a_{si}x^{s-i}y^{i}

and identify it with the coefficients
(a_{si})\in\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{(k+1)(k+2)/2}
.

3
Genericity and codimension

In this section, we present some concepts used in the rest of the paper. Let
G(3,1)
be the group generated by Lorentzian rotations, reflection, translations and homotheties with the composition operation.
Any
\sigma\in G(3,1)
preserves the
LD
,
LPL
,
PC
and
MCNC
on surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
.

Let
p\in M
be a point in Lorentzian region.
After composing by elements of
G(3,1)
if necessary,
we can parametrise
M
locally in Monge-form

\mbox{\boldmath$x$}:U\subset\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{2}\to\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{3}_{1}
, with
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}(x,z)=(x,f(x,z),z)
,
so that
p=\mbox{\boldmath$x$}(0,0)
and

j^{k}f=\sum_{i=2}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{i}a_{ij}x^{i-j}z^{j}.

(4)

Then,
p
is an umbilic point if and only if
a_{20}=-a_{22}
and
a_{21}=0
.

Similarly, if
p\in M
belongs to
LD
, then after composing by elements of
G(3,1)
if necessary, we can parametrise
M
locally in Monge-form
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}:U\subset\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{2}\to\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{3}_{1}
with
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}(x,y)=(x,y,f(x,y))
, so that
p=\mbox{\boldmath$x$}(0,0)
and

j^{k}f=x+\sum_{i=2}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{i}a_{ij}x^{i-j}y^{j}.

(5)

With the above setting,
p\in LPL
if and only if
a_{20}=0
, and
p
is an umbilic point if and only if
a_{20}=a_{21}=0
.

In
[
2
]
, Bruce described a technique for studying local properties of surfaces
in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
called Monge-Taylor map.
We follow the ideas in
[
2
]
but, as our study is local,
we choose a fixed coordinate system
\{{\bf u},{\bf v},{\bf w}\}
in a neighbourhood
W
of a point
{\rm p}\in M
with
{\bf w}
transverse to
M
at points in
W
.
For each
{\rm p}\in W
, consider the coordinate system
\{{\bf u},{\bf v},{\bf w}\}
with origin at
{\rm p}
and take a local parametrization of
M
in Monge form
(x,y)\mapsto\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{{\rm p}}(x,y)=(x,y,f_{{\rm p}}(x,y))
.
We define the
Monge-Taylor map

\theta:W\to J^{k}(2,1)
by
\theta({{\rm p}})=j^{k}f_{{\rm p}}
.

3.1
Generic properties of surfaces

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the set of germs
(M,p)
where
M\subset\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{3}_{1}
is a smooth surface and
p\in M
. A germ
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{2},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{3}_{1},p)
is a
local parametrization
if
x
is smooth, has a continuous inverse and
d\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{(0,0)}:\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{2}\to\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{3}
is one-to-one. A
local parametrization of
M
is a local parametrization whose image is contained in
M
. Consider

\mathcal{F}=\{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{2},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{3}_{1},p):p\in\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{3}_{1},\,\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\textit{ is a local parameterization}\}

with the Whitney topology. Note that
\sim_{\mathcal{R}}
is an equivalence relation on
\mathcal{F}
and denote by
[\mbox{\boldmath$x$}]
the equivalence class of
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\mathcal{F}
relative to
\sim_{\mathcal{R}}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{F}/\sim_{\mathcal{R}}
is a topological space with the quotient topology.

The map
f:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{F}/\sim_{\mathcal{R}}
that associates with
(M,p)\in\mathcal{G}
the equivalence class
[\mbox{\boldmath$x$}:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{2},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{3}_{1},p)]
of a local parameterization of
M
is well defined and is a bijection. Therefore, we provide
\mathcal{G}
with the only topology that makes
f
a homeomorphism. A local property
P
is said to be
generic
in
\mathcal{G}
when the subset
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{G}
formed by the pairs
(M,p)
such that the point
p
of
M
satisfies the property
P
is open and dense in
\mathcal{G}
. Given a subset
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\subset\mathcal{G}
we consider the induced topology of
\mathcal{G}
in
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
and define the concept of generic property in
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
as in
\mathcal{G}
. For example, if

\tilde{\mathcal{G}}=\{(M,p)\in\mathcal{G}:p\textit{ is a timelike umbilic point}\},

then the property
P:``LPL\textit{ has a singularity }A_{1}^{-}\textit{ at }p"
is generic in
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}

[
9
]
.

A
m
-parameters family of surfaces
M_{t}
deforming
(M,p)\in\mathcal{G}
is a family of surfaces whose parameterizations
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}_{t}:U\to\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{3}_{1}
of
M_{t}
smoothly depend of
t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})\in(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{m},0)
and
M_{0}=M
. Let
z=f(x,y)
be a Monge parameterization of the surface
M
. Then any
m
-parameter family of surfaces
M_{t}
with
M_{0}=M
can be given by

z=f(x,y)+h(x,y,t_{1},\dots,t_{m})

with
h
differentiable and
h(x,y,0,\dots,0)=0
for all
(x,y)\in U
. We say that the family
M_{t}
is generic when

satisfies generic conditions (open and dense conditions).

Remark 3.1

When the surface
M
is given in Monge form
x=f(y,z)
or
y=f(x,z)
, the definition of a generic family of surfaces is analogous.

We denote by

the set of points on the surface
M_{t}
where the metric is degenerate. Thus,

is the
LD
of the surface
M
and the family of curves

, with

, is a deformation of the

of

. Similarly, the

, the

and the

of

are deformed along the family of surfaces. We denote the

, the

and the

of

by

,

and

, respectively.

3.2
Codimension of a property

Let
M\subset\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{3}_{1}
be a surface. A point at
M
with a property
(P)
has
codimension
n
when the submanifold in
J^{k}(2,1)
, for
k
sufficiently large, obtained by the conditions imposed by
(P)
on the coefficients of the Monge parameterization has codimension
n+2
. Generically, a
n
-parameter family of surfaces has points with properties of codimension
\leq n
.
We are interested in the bifurcations of the
LD
, the
LPL
, the
PC
and the
MCNC
in generic 1-parameter families of surfaces, that is, in properties on these curves with codimension 0 and 1.

Properties of codimension 0 with respect to these curves are stable and known. For example, regular points of the
LD
have codimension 0. In fact, taking
M
in Monge form
z=f(x,y)
, with

j^{2}f=a_{00}+a_{10}x+a_{11}y+a_{20}x^{2}+a_{21}xy+a_{22}y^{2}
. Then
p=\mbox{\boldmath$x$}(0,0)
is a regular point of the
LD
when

a_{10}^{2}+a_{11}^{2}=1,\quad a_{11}a_{21}+2a_{10}a_{20}=0,\quad 2a_{11}a_{22}+a_{10}a_{21}\neq 0.

(6)

As the submanifold of
J^{2}(2,1)
defined by (
6
) has codimension 2, regular points of the
LD
have codimension 0. Similarly, regular points of the
LPL
,
PC
and
MCNC
have codimension 0. Also, the Morse singularities of the
LPL
have codimension 0 (
[
14
,
17
]
).

Given two curves
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
on
M
, we denote by
m(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}:p)
the order of contact between then at
p
. At points of codimension 0, the
LPL
has ordinary tangencies with the
LD
and with the
PC

[
14
]
, the
LD
is transversal to the
PC
(see §
5.2
) and the
MCNC
is transversal to all other curves (see §
4.1
and §
5.1
).

We study here properties of codimension 1. We separete the cases when the point is considered in Lorentzian region (§
4
) or on the
LD
(§
5
). The case when the point is in the Riemannian region are already studied elsewhere. At points in the Lorentzian region, codimension 1 cases can occur as follows:

(i)

Degenerate contact between regular relevant curves (§
4.1
);

(ii)

flat timelike umbilic points (§
4.2
);

(iii)

singularities of the relevant curves (§
4.3
, §
4.4
).

At points on the
LD
, we get codimension 1 cases when

(i)

Degenerate contact between regular relevant curves with
LD
(§
5.1
,
5.2
);

(ii)

singularities of the
LD
(§
5.3
).

4
Bifurcations at points in the Lorentzian region

4.1
Bifurcations at
\textbf{LPL}\cap\textbf{MCNC}\cap\textbf{PC}

In this subsection, we will discuss the intersection between the
LPL
and the
PC
when the
LPL
is a regular curve (the singular cases are considered in §
4.2
and §
5.3
). The intersection between the
LPL
and the
PC
at points of the
LD
are always singularities of the
LPL
(see §
5.3
). In the Riemannian region, the
LPL
consist of the umbilic points and is singular at such points. Therefore, the intersection between the
PC
and regular points of the
LPL
occurs only in the Lorentzian region.

Theorem 4.1

Let
M\subset\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{3}_{1}
be a smooth surface and
p\in M\setminus LD
. Then
p
belongs to the intersection of two of three curves: the
LPL
, the
PC
and the
MCNC
if and only if
p
belongs to the intersection of the three of then. Furthemore, if
p
is a regular point on the
LPL
, then
m(LPL,PC:p)=2m(LPL,MCNC:p)
.

Let
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}:U\to\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{3}_{1}
be the local parametrization of
M
given by Theorem
2.1
with
p=\mbox{\boldmath$x$}(0,0)
. Since
E=G=0
on
U
, then the
LPL
, the
PC
and the
MCNC
are given by
\bar{l}\bar{n}=0
,
\bar{m}^{2}-\bar{l}\bar{n}=0
and
\bar{m}=0
, respectively, and this proves the first part of the statement.

Consider a local parametrization
\gamma:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{2},0)
of the

The function

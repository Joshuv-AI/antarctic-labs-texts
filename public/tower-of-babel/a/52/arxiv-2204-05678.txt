First integrals for Finsler metrics with vanishing χ \chi -curvature
Ioan Bucataru, Oana Constantinescu, Georgeta Creţu

Abstract
. We prove that in a Finsler manifold with vanishing χ \chi -curvature (in particular with constant flag curvature) some non-Riemannian geometric structures are geodesically invariant and hence they induce a set of non-Riemannian first integrals. Two alternative expressions of these first integrals can be obtained either in terms of the mean Berwald curvature, or as functions of the mean Cartan torsion and the mean Landsberg curvature.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
53C60, 53B40, 53D25, 70H06

1.
Introduction.

An important geometric structure that encodes informations about the non-Riemannian, Finslerian setting is given by the (mean) Cartan torsion. This structure has been used intensively to prove rigidity results in Finsler geometry, first by Akbar-Zadeh in
[
2
]
, and very recently by Álvarez-Paiva in
[
1
]
.

Another important non-Riemannian quantity in Finsler geometry is the so-called
\chi
-curvature, which has been introduced by Shen in
[
19
]
. Sprays with vanishing
\chi
-curvature were studied in a recent paper
[
20
]
by Shen. In Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
we show that Finsler metrics with vanishing
\chi
-curvature have some geodesically invariant structures: the mean Berwald curvature and a
1
-form
\alpha
that depends on the mean Cartan torsion and the mean Landsberg curvature. We use these geodesically invariant structures to construct geodesically invariant functions and hence first integrals. One of these first integrals has been obtained previously in
[
7
, (1.1)]
and it reduces to the first integral obtained by Foulon and Ruggiero for
k
-basic Finsler surfaces in
[
10
]
. The fact that the
\chi
-curvature can be expressed in terms of the mean Cartan torsion is a key aspect for obtaining rigidity results in Finsler geometry.

In this work we provide two alternative expressions for a set of
(n-1)
non-Riemannian first integrals for Finsler metrics of vanishing
\chi
-curvature, in particular for Finsler metrics of constant flag curvature.

We fix our framework by considering a smooth
n
-dimensional manifold
M
, with
n\geq 2
, and
TM
its
tangent bundle. A
Finsler structure
is given by a function
F:TM\to[0,+\infty)
that is continuous, positively
1
-homogeneous in the fiber coordinates, smooth on
T_{0}M=TM\setminus\{0\}
, and whose metric tensor:

(1.1)

\displaystyle g_{ij}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}F^{2}}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}}(x,y)

is non-degenerate on
T_{0}M
. Here
(x,y)\in TM
, with
x\in M
and

y\in T_{x}M
.

On a Finsler manifold
(M,F)
, we consider
B^{k}_{ijl}
the Berwald curvature, its mean Berwald curvature

E_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}B^{k}_{ijk}
, given by (
2.6
), and the
0
-homogeneous,
(1,1)
-type tensor:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{i}_{j}=2Fg^{ik}E_{kj}.

On a Finsler manifold
(M,F)
, the Finsler structure
F
and the contravariant metric tensor
g^{ik}
are covariant constant along the geodesic flow. Therefore, the tensor (
1.2
) and all its geometric invariants will be constant along the geodesic flow if the mean Berwald curvature will also have this property.

For the
(1,1)
-type tensor (
1.2
), we consider its characteristic polynomial

(1.3)

\displaystyle P(\Lambda)=\det\left(\mathcal{E}^{i}_{j}+\Lambda\delta^{i}_{j}\right)=\sum_{a=0}^{n}g_{a}\Lambda^{n-a}=\Lambda^{n}+g_{1}\Lambda^{n-1}+\cdots+g_{n-1}\Lambda.

Since
E_{ij}y^{j}=0
, see
[

\mathcal{E}^{i}_{j}y^{j}=0
and hence
\det\left(\mathcal{E}^{i}_{j}\right)=0
. Therefore,
P(\Lambda)
has no free term,
g_{n}=0
, the leading terms is
g_{0}=1
and the other coefficients
g_{a}
,
a\in\{1,2...,n-1\}
, are
0
-homogeneous functions.

We formulate now the main theorem of our work.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a Finsler metric of vanishing
\chi
-curvature, the following
n-1
,
0
-homogeneous functions:

(1.4)

\displaystyle f_{a}=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathcal{E}^{a}\right),\quad a\in\{1,2,...,n-1\},

and the coefficients functions
g_{a}
of the characteristic polynomial (
1.3
) are first integrals for the geodesic spray
G
, which means that
G(f_{a})=0
and
G(g_{a})=0
.

In formula (
1.4
),
\operatorname{Tr}
is the semi-basic trace,
[
23
]
, for the
a
-th power of the tensor (
1.2
).
The two sets of first integrals
\{f_{1},...,f_{n-1}\}
and
\{g_{1},...,g_{n-1}\}
are related by Newton’s identities (
3.6
).

An important class of Finsler metrics having the first integrals (
1.4
) is given by Finsler metrics of constant flag curvature, see Corollary
4.1
.

Using the expression (
4.1
) of the mean Berwald curvature in terms of the mean Cartan torsion and the mean Landsberg curvature,
[

, we can reformulate the expression of the tensor (
1.2
) and hence of the first integrals (
1.4
) in terms of the mean Cartan torsion and the mean Landsberg curvature, see (
4.1
) and (
4.2
). In the
2
-dimensional case, the function
f_{1}
becomes a function of the Cartan scalar and the Landsberg scalar, it was known from Berwald,
[
3
, (8.7)]
, and it is the first integral obtained by Foulon and Ruggiero in
[
10

.

We provide two proofs for the Theorem
1.1

For the first proof, where we show that the functions

are first integrals, the key aspect that we will use relies on the fact that for a Finsler metric of vanishing

-curvature, the mean Berwald curvature is covariant constant along the geodesic flow (its dynamical covariant derivative vanishes). Therefore, the tensor (
1.2
) and all its powers are covariant constant and consequently the functions (
1.4
) are first integrals.

We will prove a more general result in Lemma

, by providing necessary and sufficient conditions for
\nabla E_{ij}=0
. One of these conditions can be expressed in terms of the
\chi
-curvature and was obtained by Li and Shen in
[
14
, Theorem 1.2]
.

For the second proof of the Theorem

, where we show that the functions

are first integrals, we will use a
1
-form
\alpha

3.1
), which is geodesically invariant (it is a dynamical symmetry) if and only if the Finsler metric has vanishing
\chi
-curvature, see Lemma

. The
1
-form

depends on the mean Cartan torsion and the mean Landsberg curvature and can be used to provide rigidity results in Finsler geometry. For a
k
-basic Finsler surfaces, this
1

[
10

. Using similar techniques developed in
[
4
]
, we will use the
0
-homogeneous

-form

to construct a set of
(2n-1)
-forms
\Omega_{a}
,

, on the projective sphere bundle

(2n-1)
-forms are geodesically invariant and hence their proportionality factors

, which are also the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial (

), are first integrals if the the
\chi
-curvature vanishes.

The second proof of Theorem

has been inspired from the techniques that were used in
[

to obtain a set of first integrals for geodesically equivalent Finsler metrics. For projectively equivalent Riemannian or Finslerian metrics there are various methods that can be used to obtain first integrals, see
[

17
,

]
.

2.
Finsler metrics and induced geometric structures.

For a Finsler manifold
(M,F)
, the metric tensor (
1.1
) and its regularity condition can be expressed in terms of the angular metric
h_{ij}
:

\displaystyle g_{ij}=h_{ij}+\frac{\partial F}{\partial y^{i}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial y^{j}}=h_{ij}+F_{y^{i}}F_{y^{j}},\quad h_{ij}=F\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}}=FF_{y^{i}y^{j}}.

The metric tensor
g_{ij}
is non-degenerate if and only if the angular metric
h_{ij}
has rank
n-1
.

For a Finsler manifold
(M,F)
, its geometric setting can be derived from its geodesic spray
G\in\mathfrak{X}(T_{0}M)
. The spray
G
is uniquely determined by the Euler-Lagrange equation:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}F^{2}-dF^{2}=0.

Here
J=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}\otimes dx^{i}
is the tangent structure (vertical endomorphism),
d_{J}
is the induced derivation of degree
1
and
\mathcal{L}_{G}
is the Lie derivative with respect to the geodesic spray
G
. Throughout this work we will use the Frölicher-Nijenhuis theory of derivations to describe the geometric setting on a Finsler manifold
[
8
,
11
,
12
,
21
,
23
]
.

The geodesic spray
G
is positively
2
-homogeneous, which means that it satisfies
[\mathcal{C},G]=S
, for
\mathcal{C}=y^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}
the Liouvile (dilation) vector field. Locally, the spray
G
can be expressed as:

\displaystyle G=y^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}-2G^{i}(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}},

where the
2
-homogeneous functions
G^{i}
can be deduced from the Euler-Lagrange equations (
2.1
), and they are given by:

(2.2)

\displaystyle G^{i}(x,y)=\frac{1}{4}g^{ij}(x,y)\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F^{2}}{\partial y^{j}\partial x^{k}}(x,y)y^{k}-\frac{\partial F^{2}}{\partial x^{j}}(x,y)\right).

The spray
G
induces a nonlinear connection (horizontal distribution) on
T_{0}M
, with the horizontal and vertical projectors given by
[
11
]
:

\displaystyle h=\frac{1}{2}\left(\operatorname{Id}-[G,J]\right),\quad v=\frac{1}{2}\left(\operatorname{Id}+[G,J]\right).

Locally, the two projectors can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle h=\frac{\delta}{\delta x^{i}}\otimes dx^{i},\ v=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}\otimes\delta y^{i},\textrm{ \ where \ }\frac{\delta}{\delta x^{i}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}-N^{j}_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{j}},\ \delta y^{i}=dy^{i}+N^{i}_{j}dx^{j},\ N^{j}_{i}=\frac{\partial G^{j}}{\partial y^{i}}.

The local frame
\{\frac{\delta}{\delta x^{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}\}
is adapted to the horizontal and vertical distributions. The geodesic spray
G
of a Finsler metric
F
is a horizontal vector field, which means that
hG=G=y^{i}\frac{\delta}{\delta x^{i}}
.

From the Euler-Lagrange equations (
2.1
) we obtain that the Finsler metric
F
is geodesically invariant,
G(F)=0
, and
d_{h}F=0
, which means:

\displaystyle dF=d_{v}F=\frac{\partial F}{\partial y^{i}}\delta y^{i}.

For a Finsler manifold
(M,F)
, we consider the projective sphere bundle
SM=T_{0}M/\mathbb{R}_{+}
that can be identified with the indicatrix bundle
IM=F^{-1}\{1\}
. The Hilbert
1
and
2
-forms:

\displaystyle d_{J}F=\frac{\partial F}{\partial y^{i}}dx^{i},\quad dd_{J}F=\frac{1}{F}h_{ij}\delta y^{i}\wedge dx^{j}

can be restricted to the sphere bundle
SM
,
d_{J}F\in\Lambda^{1}(SM)
,
dd_{J}F\in\Lambda^{2}(SM)
, since they are
0
-homogeneous,
i_{\mathcal{C}}d_{J}F=0
and
i_{\mathcal{C}}dd_{J}F=0
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\operatorname{rank}(dd_{J}F)=2\operatorname{rank}(h_{ij})=2(n-1).

The regularity condition of a Finsler metric
F
ensures that its Hilbert
1
-form is a contact structure on
SM
, which means that
d_{J}F\wedge\left(dd_{J}F\right)^{(n-1)}\neq 0
. Therefore, a Finsler metric induces a canonical volume form on
SM
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Omega_{F}=\frac{(-1)^{(n-1)(n-2)/2}}{(n-1)!}d_{J}F\wedge\left(dd_{J}F\right)^{(n-1)}\in\Lambda^{2n-1}(SM).

The regularity condition of a Finsler metric
F
ensures also that the following
2
-form is a symplectic form on
T_{0}M
:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}dd_{J}F^{2}=g_{ij}\delta y^{i}\wedge dx^{j}=Fdd_{J}F+dF\wedge d_{J}F=Fdd_{J}F+d_{v}F\wedge d_{J}F.

The symplectic form (
2.4
) induces a canonical volume form on
T_{0}M
:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}_{F}=\frac{(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}}{n!}\left(\frac{1}{2}dd_{J}F^{2}\right)^{(n)}=\det g\ dx\wedge dy\in\Lambda^{2n}(T_{0}M).

Using formulae (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we have that the two volume forms
\Omega_{F}\in\Lambda^{2n-1}(SM)
and
\Omega^{\prime}_{F}\in\Lambda^{2n}(T_{0}M)
are related by:

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}_{F}=(-1)^{n-1}F^{n-1}dF\wedge\Omega_{F}=(-1)^{n-1}F^{n-1}d_{v}F\wedge\Omega_{F},\quad\Omega_{F}=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{F^{n}}i_{\mathcal{C}}\Omega^{\prime}_{F}.

Among the many curvature tensors that exist in Finsler geometry, we recall first the Jacobi endomorphism and the curvature of the nonlinear connection.

The Jacobi endomorphism is the
(1,1)
-type tensor field:

\displaystyle\Phi=v\circ[G,h]=R^{i}_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}\otimes dx^{j},\quad R^{i}_{j}=2\frac{\partial G^{i}}{\partial x^{j}}-G\left(N^{i}_{j}\right)-N^{i}_{k}N^{k}_{j}.

The curvature of the nonlinear connection can be defined as the Nijenhuis tensor of the horizontal projector
h
, which gives the obstruction to the integrability of the horizontal distribution:

\displaystyle R=\frac{1}{2}[h,h]=\frac{1}{2}R^{i}_{jk}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}\otimes dx^{k}\wedge dx^{j},\quad R^{i}_{jk}=\frac{\delta N^{i}_{j}}{\delta x^{k}}-\frac{\delta N^{i}_{k}}{\delta x^{j}}.

A Finsler metric has scalar flag curvature if there exists a function
\kappa\in C^{\infty}(T_{0}M)
such that its Jacobi endomorphism has the form:

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\left(F^{2}J-Fd_{J}F\otimes\mathcal{C}\right)\Longleftrightarrow R^{i}_{j}=\kappa\left(\delta^{i}_{j}g_{sl}-\delta^{i}_{s}g_{jl}\right)y^{s}y^{l}.

When the
0
-homogeneous function
\kappa
is constant, we say that the Finsler metric has constant flag curvature. A Schur’s Lemma for Finsler metrics is true: in dimension
n>2
, if the scalar flag curvature
\kappa
does not depend on the fibre coordinates
y
then
\kappa
is constant and the Finsler metric has constant flag curvature,
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
,
[
21
, Theorem 9.4.1]
.

While the Jacobi endomorphism and the curvature of the nonlinear connection have Riemannian correspondents (depend quadratic and respectively linear on the Riemannian curvature tensor), the Berwald curvature
B^{i}_{jkl}
and the mean Berwald curvature
E_{ij}
are purely non-Riemannian tensors, and they are given by:

(2.6)

\displaystyle B^{i}_{jkl}=\frac{\partial^{3}G^{i}}{\partial y^{j}\partial y^{k}\partial y^{l}},\quad E_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}B^{k}_{ijk}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{3}G^{k}}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}\partial y^{k}}.

The mean Berwald curvature can be expressed in terms of the
S
-function, introduced by Shen,
[
18
, §5.2]
. For a fixed vertically invariant volume form
\sigma(x)dx\wedge dy
on TM,
[
21
, p.490]
, we consider the
S
-
function
and the
distortion

\tau
:

(2.7)

\displaystyle S=G(\tau),\quad\tau=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\det g}{\sigma}.

The mean Berwald curvature can be expressed as follows,
[
18
, (6.13)]
:

(2.8)

\displaystyle E_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}},\ \textrm{hence \ }2E_{ij}\delta y^{i}\wedge dx^{j}=d_{v}d_{J}S.

Another important non-Riemannian quantity, the
\chi
-curvature, was introduced by Shen,
[
19
, (1.10)]
. This can be defined as:

(2.9)

\displaystyle\chi=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{G}S,\quad\textrm{where \ }\delta_{G}S=\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}S-dS=\left\{G\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial y^{i}}\right)-\frac{\partial S}{\partial x^{i}}\right\}dx^{i},

is the Euler-Lagrange semi-basic
1
-form associated to the spray
G
and the
S
-function.

The dynamical covariant derivative
\nabla
is a derivation of degree zero along the geodesic spray. It can be defined through its action on functions and vector fields and then extended to arbitrary tensor fields and forms,
[
8
]
:

\displaystyle\nabla f=G(f),\forall f\in C^{\infty}(T_{0}M),\quad\nabla X=h[G,hX]+v[G,vX],\forall X\in\mathfrak{X}(T_{0}M).

For a Finsler structure
F
, the dynamical covariant derivative of its metric tensor vanishes,
\nabla g_{ij}=0
,
[
8
, (44)]
and the same happens to the Finsler function
\nabla F=G(F)=0
. Therefore, the tensor (
1.2
) and all its geometric invariants are covariant constant along the geodesic flow if
\nabla E_{ij}=0
.

3.
Proofs of Theorem
1.1
.

The most important aspects that we will use in the proof of Theorem
1.1
are based on the geodesic invariance of some geometric structures on Finsler manifolds with vanishing
\chi
-curvature. For the first proof we will use the fact that the mean Berwald curvature is covariant constant,
\nabla E_{ij}=0
, and therefore the tensor (
1.2
) is covariant constant along the geodesic flow if the
\chi
-curvature vanishes. For the second proof we show in Lemma
3.2
that a
1
-form (
3.1
) is geodesically invariant if and only if the
\chi
-curvature vanishes. We use the form
\alpha
to construct a set of
(2n-1)
-forms on the projective sphere bundle
SM
that are geodesically invariant, and whose proportionality factors are first integrals.

3.1.
First proof of Theorem
1.1

For the first proof, we provide in Lemma
3.1
necessary and sufficient conditions for the mean Berwald curvature, and hence for the tensor (
1.2
), to be covariant constant along the geodesic flow. These conditions will allow to obtain various characterisations for a class of Finsler metrics (that includes Finsler metrics of constant flag curvature) which have the functions (
1.4
) as a set of first integrals.

Lemma 3.1
.

On a Finsler manifold
(M,F)
, the following conditions are equivalent:

i)

\nabla E_{ij}=0
;

ii)

d_{h}d_{J}S
is a basic
2
-form;

iii)

there exists a basic
2
-form
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}(M)
such that
2\chi=i_{G}\omega
.

Proof.

We prove i)
\Longrightarrow
ii). Using the expression (
2.8
) of the mean Berwald curvature in terms of the
S
-function, the assumption
\nabla E_{ij}=0
reads
\nabla d_{v}d_{J}S=0
. To this equation we apply derivation
d_{J}
and use the commutation rule
[
5
, (2.11)]
, which gives:

\displaystyle 0=d_{J}\nabla d_{v}d_{J}S=\nabla d_{J}d_{v}d_{J}S+d_{h}d_{v}d_{J}S+2i_{R}d_{v}d_{J}S=-d_{v}d_{h}d_{J}S.

Here we made use of the following
d_{J}d_{v}=-d_{v}d_{J}
and
d^{2}_{J}=0
which gives
d_{J}d_{v}d_{J}S=0
. We also used that
2R=[h,h]=[h,\operatorname{Id}-v]=-[h,v]
and hence
d_{h}d_{v}d_{J}S+d_{v}d_{h}d_{J}S=-2d_{R}d_{J}S=-2i_{R}d_{v}d_{J}S
. The fact that
d_{v}d_{h}d_{J}S=0
implies that
d_{h}d_{J}S
is a basic
2
-form (
d_{h}d_{J}S
is already a semi-basic
2
-form).

We prove ii))
\Longrightarrow
iii). We assume that there is a basic
2
-form
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}(M)
such that
d_{h}d_{J}S=\omega
. We apply the inner product
i_{G}
to both sides of this equality and use the commutation rule for the two derivations
i_{G}
and
d_{h}
,
[
12
, p.205]
,

\displaystyle i_{G}\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i_{G}d_{h}d_{J}S=-d_{h}i_{G}d_{J}S+\mathcal{L}_{hG}d_{J}S+d_{[h,G]}d_{J}S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-d_{h}S+\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}S-d_{v}S=\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}S-dS\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{chi}}}{{=}}2\chi.

The equality of the first and the last terms is the condition iii).

We prove the last implication iii)
\Longrightarrow
i). Formula (
2.9
) allows to write our assumption as
\delta_{G}S=i_{G}\omega
. Using formulae
[
5
, (2.13)]
, which provide alternative expressions for the Euler-Lagrange
1
-form, we can rewrite this assumption as
\nabla d_{J}S-d_{h}S=i_{G}\omega
. If we apply the derivation
d_{J}
to both sides of the last formula and use again the commutation rule
[
5
, (2.11)]
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\nabla d_{J}^{2}S+d_{h}d_{J}S+2i_{R}d_{J}S-d_{J}d_{h}S=d_{J}i_{G}\omega\Longleftrightarrow d_{h}d_{J}S=\omega.

We apply the differential operator
d
to both sides of our assumption and then:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{G}dd_{J}S=di_{G}\omega\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{h}d_{J}S+\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{v}d_{J}S=\mathcal{L}_{G}\omega-i_{G}d\omega\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{v}d_{J}S=-i_{G}d\omega.

We replace the Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{G}
in terms of the covariant derivative
\nabla
,
[
8
, (27)]
and get:

\displaystyle\nabla d_{v}d_{J}S-d_{\Phi}d_{J}S=-i_{G}d\omega.

In the above equation, we separate the semi-basic part from the non-semi-basic part. This implies
\nabla d_{v}d_{J}S=0
(the non-semi-basic part), which in view of formula (
2.8
) gives
\nabla E_{ij}=0
and hence the condition i) is true. We also obtain the equality of the semi-basic parts
d_{\Phi}d_{J}S=i_{G}d\omega
. ∎

The equivalence of i) and iii) has been established by Li and Shen in
[
14
, Theorem 1.2]
.

The first proof of Theorem
1.1
follows immediately from Lemma
3.1
. For a Finsler metric of vanishing
\chi
-curvature, formula (
2.9
) and the condition iii) of Lemma
3.1
show that the assumption
\chi=0
implies the condition i) of Lemma
3.1
,
\nabla E_{ij}=0
. Therefore, the tensor (
1.2
) and all its powers are covariant constant,
\nabla\mathcal{E}^{a}=0
,
\forall a\in\mathbb{N}
. Since the dynamical covariant derivative commutes with the semi-basic trace, it follows that

\displaystyle G(f_{a})=\nabla(f_{a})=\nabla\left(\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal{E}^{a}\right)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\nabla\mathcal{E}^{a}\right)=0,\forall a\in\{1,...,n-1\},

which means that all the
0
-homogeneous functions
f_{a}=\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal{E}^{a}
are first integrals for the geodesic spray
G
.

3.2.
Second proof of Theorem
1.1

For a Finsler metric
F
, its reducibility to a Riemannian metric is encoded into its mean Cartan torsion,
[
9
, Deicke’s Theorem]
:

\displaystyle I_{k}=\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial y^{k}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{k}}\left(\ln\sqrt{\det g}\right)=\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial y^{k}},\quad I=I_{k}dx^{k}=d_{J}\left(\ln\sqrt{\det g}\right)=d_{J}\tau.

The
0
-homogeneity of the distortion function
\tau
implies
i_{G}I=y^{i}I_{i}=\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial y^{i}}y^{i}=0
.

An important ingredient we will use for the second proof of Theorem
1.1
is the following
1
-form:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i_{[J,G]}\mathcal{L}_{G}I=\nabla I_{k}dx^{k}-I_{k}\delta y^{k}=\nabla d_{J}\tau-d_{v}\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{J}\nabla\tau-d_{h}\tau-d_{v}\tau=d_{J}\nabla\tau-d\tau=d_{J}S-d\tau,

which in the
2
-dimensional case has been introduced in
[
10
, §2]
. We can easily see that
i_{\mathcal{C}}\alpha=y^{i}I_{i}=0
and
i_{G}\alpha=y^{i}\nabla I_{i}=0
.

Lemma 3.2
.

On a Finsler manifold
(M,F)
, the
\chi
-curvature vanishes if and only if one of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:

i)

\mathcal{L}_{G}\alpha=0
: the
1
-form
\alpha
is geodesically invariant (it is a dual symmetry);

ii)

d_{h}d_{J}S=0
: the
S
-function is a Hamel function;

iii)

d\alpha=2E_{ij}\delta y^{i}\wedge dx^{j}
: the horizontal and vertical distributions are Lagrangian distributions for
d\alpha
.

Proof.

Formulae (
3.1
) allow to express the geodesic variation of the
1
-form
\alpha
in terms of the
\chi
-curvature:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{G}\alpha=\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}S-\mathcal{L}_{G}d\tau=\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}S-d\mathcal{L}_{G}\tau\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{sgt}}}{{=}}\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}S-dS\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{chi}}}{{=}}2\chi,

and hence
\chi=0
if and only if
\mathcal{L}_{G}\alpha=0
.

The expression of the
\chi
-curvature, given by formula (
2.9
), can be reformulated using the dynamical covariant derivative,
[
5
, (2.13)]
, as follows:

\displaystyle 2\chi=\nabla d_{J}S-d_{h}S.

If we apply to both sides of this formula the derivation
d_{J}
and use the commutation rule
[
5
, (2.11)]
, we obtain:

\displaystyle 2d_{J}\chi=d_{J}\nabla d_{J}S-d_{J}d_{h}S=\nabla d^{2}_{J}S+d_{h}d_{J}S+2i_{R}d_{J}S-d_{J}d_{h}S=2d_{h}d_{J}S.

Above formulae give
d_{J}\chi=d_{h}d_{J}S
. We apply to these semi-basic
2
-forms the inner product
i_{G}
. Therefore, if we use the commutation rule for the derivations
i_{G}
and
d_{h}
,
[
12
, p.205]
, we have:

\displaystyle i_{G}d_{J}\chi=i_{G}d_{h}d_{J}S=-d_{h}i_{G}S+\mathcal{L}_{hG}d_{J}S+d_{[h,G]}S=-d_{h}S+\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}S-d_{v}S=\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}S-dS=2\chi.

The last formulae allow to conclude that
\chi=0
if and only if
d_{J}\chi=0
if and only if
d_{h}d_{J}S=0
.

From formulae (
3.1
) and (
2.8
) we obtain:

\displaystyle d\alpha=dd_{J}S=d_{h}d_{J}S+d_{v}d_{J}S=d_{h}d_{J}S+2E_{ij}\delta y^{i}\wedge dx^{j}.

Therefore,
d\alpha=2E_{ij}\delta y^{i}\wedge dx^{j}
if and only if
d_{h}d_{J}S=0
if and only if
\chi=0
.
∎

For a Finsler surface, the
\chi
-curvature vanishes if and only if we have a
k
-basic Finsler structure,
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
. Therefore, Lemma
3.2
allows to recover the geodesic invariance of the
1
-form
\alpha
for
k
-basic Finsler surfaces obtained in
[

, Lemma 2.2]
.

For the second proof of Theorem

we use the characterizations established in Lemma

for Finsler metrics of vanishing

The
1
-form
\alpha
is

(2n-1)

\displaystyle\Omega_{a}=\frac{(-1)^{(n-1)(n-2)/2}}{(a-1)!(n-a)!}d_{J}F\wedge\left(dd_{J}F\right)^{(a-1)}\wedge\left(d\alpha\right)^{(n-a)}\in\Lambda^{2n-1}(SM).

We can seen that
\Omega_{n}=\Omega_{F}
is the canonical volume (

) of the contact manifold
(SM,d_{J}F)
.

The Reeb vector field of the contact manifold
(SM,d_{J}F)
is the normalized geodesic field
G/F
. Using

it follows that the Hilbert

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{G/F}d_{J}F=\frac{1}{F}\left(\mathcal{L}_{G}d_{J}F-dF\right)=0\ \textrm{and}\ \mathcal{L}_{G/F}dd_{J}F=0.

Since

, we also have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{G/F}\alpha=\frac{1}{F}\mathcal{L}_{G}\alpha+i_{G}\alpha\ d\left(\frac{1}{F}\right)=\frac{1}{F}\mathcal{L}_{G}\alpha\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lgalpha}}}{{=}}0,

which means that the form

(2n-1)

) are invariant as well:

It follows that the proportionality factors of these

\displaystyle\Omega_{a}=g_{n-a}\Omega_{n},\quad\forall a\in\{1,...,n-1\},

are also preserved by the action of the Reeb vector field. Therefore, the functions

,

We prove now that the first integrals

, given by formula (

).

Together with the
(2n-1)

3.3
) on

, we consider the following
2n
-forms on

(3.5)

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}_{a}=(-1)^{n-1}F^{n-1}dF\wedge\Omega_{a}=(-1)^{n-1}F^{n-1}d_{v}F\wedge\Omega_{a},\quad a\in\{1,...,n\}.

It is easy to see that
\Omega^{\prime}_{n}=\Omega^{\prime}_{F}
is the volume form (

are uniquelly determined also by

. First, we can write the characteristic polynomial (

\displaystyle P(\Lambda)=\det\left(g^{ik}2FE_{kj}+\Lambda\delta^{i}_{j}\right)=\frac{1}{{\det g}}\det\left(2FE_{kj}+\Lambda g_{kj}\right).

Therefore, we have:

\displaystyle P(\Lambda)\Omega^{\prime}_{n}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{o'n}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\det g}}\det\left(2FE_{kj}+\Lambda g_{kj}\right)\det g\ dx\wedge dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}}{n!}\left(\left(2FE_{kj}+\Lambda g_{kj}\right)\delta y^{k}\wedge dx^{j}\right)^{(n)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ddjf2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}}{n!}\left(Fd\alpha+\Lambda\left(Fdd_{J}F+dF\wedge d_{J}F\right)\right)^{(n)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}}{n!}\left(\sum_{a=0}^{n}\frac{n!}{a!(n-a)!}\Lambda^{a}\left(Fdd_{J}F+dF\wedge d_{J}F\right)^{(a)}\wedge(Fd\alpha)^{(n-a)}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{n}\frac{\Lambda^{a}}{(a-1)!(n-a)!}dF\wedge d_{J}F\wedge\left(Fdd_{J}F\right)^{(a-1)}\wedge\left(Fd\alpha\right)^{(n-a)}\right)

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}F^{n-1}dF\wedge\left(\sum_{a=1}^{n}\Lambda^{a}\frac{(-1)^{(n-1)(n-2)/2}}{(a-1)!(n-a)!}d_{J}F\wedge\left(dd_{J}F\right)^{(a-1)}\wedge\left(d\alpha\right)^{(n-a)}\right)

4.1.
Particular cases.

Since for Finsler metrics of constant curvature, the
\chi
-curvature vanishes,
[

, Theorem 1.1]
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.1
.

Consider
F
a Finsler metric of constant curvature. Then the functions (
1.4
) and the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial (
1.3
) are constant along the geodesics of the Finsler metric
F
.

We will show now that the first integral
g_{n-1}
coincides with the first integral
[
7
, (1.1)]
. Indeed, using formulae (
3.3
) and (
3.5
), for
a=1
, we have:

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}}{(n-1)!}dF\wedge d_{J}F\wedge\left(Fd\alpha\right)^{(n-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}}{n!}\left(d_{v}F\wedge d_{J}F+Fd\alpha\right)^{(n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}}{n!}\left(\left(2FE_{ij}+\frac{\partial F}{\partial y^{i}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial y^{j}}\right)\delta y^{i}\wedge dx^{j}\right)^{(n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\left(2FE_{ij}+\frac{\partial F}{\partial y^{i}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial y^{j}}\right)dx\wedge dy=\frac{1}{\det g}\det\left(2FE_{ij}+\frac{\partial F}{\partial y^{i}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial y^{j}}\right)\Omega^{\prime}_{n}.

These arguments and formula (
3.3
) for
a=1
give the first integral:

\displaystyle g_{n-1}=\frac{1}{\det g}\det\left(2FE_{ij}+\frac{\partial F}{\partial y^{i}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial y^{j}}\right)=\frac{-1}{\det g}\begin{vmatrix}2FE_{ij}&\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial y^{i}}\vskip 5.69054pt\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial y^{j}}&0\end{vmatrix},

which is the first integral
[
7
, (1.1)]
.

4.2.
Alternative expressions for the first integrals.

The mean Berwald curvature can be expressed in terms of the mean Cartan torsion and the mean Landsberg curvature,
[
18
, Lemma 6.2.4]
:

(4.1)

\displaystyle E_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{I_{j;i}+J_{i\cdot j}\right\},

where:
J_{i}=\nabla I_{i}
is the mean Landsberg curvature,
J_{i\cdot j}=\frac{\partial J_{i}}{\partial y^{j}}
is the vertical covariant derivative of the mean Landsberg curvature and

\displaystyle I_{j;i}=\frac{\delta I_{j}}{\delta x^{i}}-I_{l}\frac{\partial^{2}G^{l}}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}}

is the horizontal covariant derivative of the mean Cartan torsion with respect to the Berwald connection.

It follows that the function

(4.2)

\displaystyle g_{1}=f_{1}=\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\left\{I_{j;i}+J_{i\cdot j}\right\}

is a first integral for a Finsler metric of vanishing
\chi
-curvature.
For a
k
-basic Finsler surface, the first integral (
4.2
) reduces to the first integral obtained in
[
10
, Theorem B]
as a function of the Cartan scalar
I
and the Landsberg scalar
J
.

In this section we present an example of a Finsler metrics of constant curvature and the corresponding first integrals obtained using Theorem

Consider

\displaystyle F(x,y)=\frac{\left(\sqrt{|y|^{2}-(|x|^{2}|y|^{2}-\langle x,y\rangle^{2})}+\langle x,y\rangle\right)^{2}}{\left(1-|x|^{2}\right)^{2}\sqrt{|y|^{2}-(|x|^{2}|y|^{2}-\langle x,y\rangle^{2})}}.

The Finsler metric
F
is
R

\displaystyle P(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{|y|^{2}-(|x|^{2}|y|^{2}-\langle x,y\rangle^{2})}}{1-|x|^{2}}+\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{1-|x|^{2}},\ y\in T_{x}B^{3},

being the Funk metric,
[
18
, (2.48)]
. The spray coefficients (
2.2
) are given by
G^{i}(x,y)=P(x,y)y^{i}
and hence the mean Berwald curvature (
2.6
) is give by:

\displaystyle E_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{3}G^{l}}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}\partial y^{l}}=\frac{n+1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial y^{i}\partial y^{j}}.

Therefore, the tensor (
1.2
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{i}_{j}=(n+1)Fg^{ik}\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial y^{k}\partial y^{j}},

and its characteristic polynomial (

) gives the following
2
first integrals:

\displaystyle g_{1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\left(-2\langle x,y\rangle\sqrt{|y|^{2}-|x|^{2}|y|^{2}+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}-2\langle x,y\rangle^{2}-|y|^{2}(1-|x|^{2})\right)\cdot\left(|y|^{2}-|x|^{2}|y|^{2}+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}\right)^{2})}{8\left(\frac{1}{2}|y|^{2}+|x|^{2}|y|^{2}-2\langle x,y\rangle^{2}\right)|y|^{2}\left(\langle x,y\rangle+\sqrt{|y|^{2}-|x|^{2}|y|^{2}+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}\right)^{2}},

\displaystyle g_{2}

\displaystyle=1+\dfrac{\left(2|y|^{2}+|x|^{2}|y|^{2}-\langle x,y\rangle^{2}\right)\cdot\left(|x|^{2}|y|^{2}-\langle x,y\rangle^{2}\right)}{2|y|^{2}\left(-\frac{1}{2}|y|^{2}+|y|^{2}(1+|x|^{2})-\langle x,y\rangle^{2}\right)}.

To compute the Poisson bracket of these two first integrals, we use the software package, to obtain:

\displaystyle\left\{g_{1},g_{2}\right\}=\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\dfrac{\partial g_{1}}{\partial y^{j}}\dfrac{\delta g_{2}}{\delta x^{i}}-\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\dfrac{\partial g_{2}}{\partial y^{j}}\dfrac{\delta g_{1}}{\delta x^{i}}=0.

Poisson commute with respect to the flat metric and also with respect to the Berwald metric.

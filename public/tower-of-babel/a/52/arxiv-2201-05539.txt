Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to obtain new inequalities for a large family of generalizations of the Wiener Index and to characterize the set of extremal graphs with respect to them. Our main results provide upper and lower bounds for these topological indices on unicyclic graphs.



1.
Introduction

A topological descriptor is a single number that it is computed on the molecular graph of a compound and represents some chemical structure in terms of this graph. These descriptors are common and relevant in the field of mathematical chemistry and especially in the QSPR/QSAR investigations.

When a topological descriptor correlates with a molecular property of certain chemical compounds, then it is called a topological index. Thus, topological indices capture some essential physicochemical property into a single number and this can be used to analyze those properties. This can be very interesting for practitioners. For example, although only about 1000 benzenoid hydrocarbons are known, the number of theoretically
possible benzenoid hydrocarbons is huge. If we consider, for instance, the number of
possible benzenoid hydrocarbons with 35 benzene rings, it is
5.85\times 10^{21}

[
26
]
.
Therefore, a good model capable of predicting physico-chemical properties of currently unknown species is extremely useful.

The main reason for the use of topological indices is to obtain predictions of some property of certain molecules (see, e.g.,
[
9
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
24
]
).
Whenever a topological descriptor shows a better correlation for some property on some group of chemical compounds a new topological index appears. This way hundreds of topological indices have been defined and studied, starting with the seminal work by Wiener,
[
27
]
, who found a correlation between his index and paraffin boiling points.

The
Wiener index
of
G
is defined as

W(G)=\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(G)}d(u,v),

where
\{u,v\}
runs over every pair of vertices in
G
.

Following this work, several versions of this index have been appearing since then, each one better adapted for its purpose. Let us mention, for example, the hyper-Wiener index, see
[
19
,
23
]
or the Harari index
[
15
,
22
]
.

A natural problem in the study of topological indices is, given some fixed parameters, to find the graphs that minimize (or maximize) their value on a certain set of graphs satisfying the restrictions given by the parameters (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
).

Herein, instead of doing this individually on each modified version of the Wiener index we consider a natural generalization of it which encompasses those mentioned above and try to work as generally as possible. In previous works we used the same strategy with trees, see
[
20
]
. Now, we are considering unicyclic graphs. A
unicyclic
graph is a graph containing exactly one cycle
[
16
, p.41]
.
It is well known that if
G
is a unicyclic graph with
n
vertices, then
G
has
n
edges.

The aim of this paper is to obtain new inequalities for a large family of topological indices restricted to unicyclic graphs, fixing or not the number of pendant vertices, and to characterize the extremal unicyclic graphs with respect to them. This problem, for other type of indices, was also addressed in
[
21
]
.

Throughout this work,
G=(V(G),E(G))
denotes a (non-oriented) finite connected simple (without multiple edges and loops) non-trivial (
E(G)\neq\emptyset
) graph.
Note that the connectivity of
G
is not an important restriction, since every molecular graph is connected.

2.
Wiener index and its generalizations

Motivated by the Wiener index, Randić introduced in
[
23
]
an extension of the Wiener index for trees, and this has come
to be known as the
hyper-Wiener index
. In
[
19
]
, this extension was generalized to graphs as

WW(G)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(G)}d(u,v)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(G)}d(u,v)^{2}.

WW(G)
has been useful in correlations (see, e.g.,
[
11
]
and the references therein).
For information about the hyper-Wiener index in mathematics see, e.g.,
[
3
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
17
]
.

Also, it is interesting to generalize the Wiener index in the following way

W^{\lambda}(G)=\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(G)}d(u,v)^{\lambda},

with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Obviously, if
\lambda=1
, then
W^{\lambda}

coincides with the ordinary Wiener index
W
.
Note that
W^{-2}
is the Harary index;
W^{-1}
is the reciprocal Wiener index; the quantity
W^{2}
is closely related to the hyper-Wiener index,
since
WW=(W^{1}+W^{2})/2
. Another topological index, proposed in
[
25
]
is expressed in terms of
W^{1}
,
W^{2}
and
W^{3}
as
(2W^{1}+3W^{2}+W^{3})/6
.
See
[
18
]
for more connections of the same kind.

Three different variants of the
q
-Wiener index (
q>0,\,q\neq 1
) were defined in
[
28
]
as

\displaystyle W_{1}(G,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(G)}[d(u,v)]_{q},

\displaystyle W_{2}(G,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(G)}[d(u,v)]_{q}\,q^{L-d(u,v)},

\displaystyle W_{3}(G,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(G)}[d(u,v)]_{q}\,q^{d(u,v)},

where
L
is the diameter of
G
, and

[k]_{q}=\frac{1-q^{k}}{1-q}=1+q+q^{2}+\cdots+q^{k-1}.

Since
\lim_{q\to 1}[k]_{q}=k
, we have

\lim_{q\to 1}W_{1}(G,q)=\lim_{q\to 1}W_{2}(G,q)=\lim_{q\to 1}W_{3}(G,q)=W(G).

Given any function
h:\mathbb{Z}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the
h
-
Wiener index
of
G
is defined as

W_{h}(G)=\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(G)}h\big(d(u,v)\big),

This general approach allows to study in a unified way the previous indices.

If
P_{n}
is the path graph with
n
vertices, then

W_{h}(P_{n})=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}h(j-i)=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(n-k)h(k).

If
C_{n}
is the cycle graph with
n
vertices, then

•

if
n
is odd,

W_{h}(C_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}nh(j),

•

if
n
is even,

W_{h}(C_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}nh(j)+\frac{n}{2}h\Big(\frac{n}{2}\Big).

Given
n\geq 4
, let
J_{n}
be the graph obtained by identifying a vertex from a cycle
C_{3}
and the vertex with degree
n-3
of a star graph with
n-2
vertices,
S_{n-2}
. Then,

W_{h}(J_{n})=nh(1)+\frac{1}{2}n(n-3)h(2).

Given
3\leq r\leq n
and a function
h:\mathbb{Z}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, let us define the function
F_{h}(r,n)
as follows:

•

if
r
is odd,

(2.1)

F_{h}(r,n):=\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}rh(j)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-r}(n-r+1-j)h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(k+j),

•

if
r
is even,

(2.2)

F_{h}(r,n):=\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}rh(j)+\frac{r}{2}h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-r}(n-r+1-j)h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(k+j)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}h\Big(\frac{r}{2}+k\Big).

Let
G_{r,n}
be the graph obtained by identifying a vertex from a cycle
C_{r}
and a vertex with degree 1 of a path graph
P_{n-r+1}
.
Note that
G_{n,n}=C_{n}
.

As usual, if
a<b
, we use the convention

\sum_{j=b}^{a}A(j)=0.

Proposition 2.1
.

Given
3\leq r\leq n
and a function
h:\mathbb{Z}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, then

W_{h}(G_{r,n})=F_{h}(r,n).

Proof.

Consider the graph
G_{r,n}
, let
w_{0}
be the identified vertex which belongs to the cycle and the path and let
w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n-r}
be the vertices in the path
P_{n-r+1}
with
d(w_{0},w_{k})=k
. See Figure
1
.

Then,

W_{h}(G_{r,n})=W_{h}(C_{r})+W_{h}(P_{n-r+1})+\sum_{w_{0}\neq v\in C_{r},\,w_{0}\neq w_{i}\in P_{n-r+1}}h(d(v,w_{i})).

Suppose
r
is odd. Then, notice that for every
1\leq k\leq n-r
,

\sum_{w_{0}\neq v\in C_{r}}h(d(v,w_{k}))=2\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(j+k).

Thus,

W_{h}(G_{r,n})=\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}rh(j)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-r}(n-r+1-j)h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(k+j).

In particular,

F_{h}(3,n)=3h(1)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-3}(n-2-j)h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-3}h(k+1)=nh(1)+\sum_{j=2}^{n-2}(n-j)h(j).

Suppose
r
is even. Then, notice that for every
1\leq k\leq n-r
,

\sum_{w_{0}\neq v\in C_{r}}h(d(v,w_{k}))=2\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(j+k)+h\Big(\frac{r}{2}+k\Big).

Thus,

W_{h}(G_{r,n})=\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}rh(j)+\frac{r}{2}h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-r}(n-r+1-j)h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(k+j)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}h\Big(\frac{r}{2}+k\Big).

∎

Remark 2.2
.

Since any graph with
n
vertices has
\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)
pairs of vertices, we have

\frac{1}{2}\,n(n-1)=n+\sum_{j=2}^{n-2}(n-j)=\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}r+\sum_{j=1}^{n-r}(n-r+1-j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}1

if
r
is odd, and

\frac{1}{2}\,n(n-1)=n+\sum_{j=2}^{n-2}(n-j)=\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}r+\frac{r}{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-r}(n-r+1-j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}1

if
r
is even.

Lemma 2.3
.

Given
4\leq r\leq n
with
r
even and a strictly increasing function
h:\mathbb{Z}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, then
F_{h}(3,n)>F_{h}(r,n)
. If
h
is a strictly decreasing function, then
F_{h}(3,n)<F_{h}(r,n)
.

Proof.

First of all, note that the second statement is a consequence of the first one, if we consider the function
-h
.
Hence, we can assume that
h
is a strictly increasing function.
We have

\displaystyle F_{h}(3,n)>F_{h}(r,n)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\sum_{j=2}^{n-r}(n-j)h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{n-2}(n-j)h(j)

\displaystyle>\sum_{j=2}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}rh(j)+\frac{r}{2}h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-\frac{r}{2}}h(j)+\sum_{j=2}^{n-r}(n-r+1-j)h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(k+j).

Case 1. Suppose
n-r<\frac{r}{2}-1
. Then,

\displaystyle F_{h}(3,n)>F_{h}(r,n)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{n-2}(n-j)h(j)

\displaystyle>\sum_{j=2}^{n-r}h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}rh(j)+\frac{r}{2}h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-\frac{r}{2}}h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(k+j)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad(n-r)h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-\frac{r}{2}}(n-j-1)h(j)+\sum_{j=n-\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-2}(n-j)h(j)

\displaystyle>\sum_{j=2}^{n-r}h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}(r-n+j)h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(k+j).

Since
n-r<\frac{r}{2}-1
,

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(k+j)

\displaystyle=2h(2)+4h(3)+\cdots+2(n-r)h(n-r+1)+\cdots+2(n-r)h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+2(n-r-1)h\Big(\frac{r}{2}+1\Big)+\cdots+2h\Big(n-\frac{r}{2}-1\Big).

Let

M=(n-r-1)+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{3r}{2}-n-1\Big)\Big(\frac{3r}{2}-n\Big)+(n-r)(2r-n-1).

Then, since
h
is strictly increasing, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{n-r}h(j)\,+

\displaystyle\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}(r-n+j)h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(k+j)

\displaystyle<Mh\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+2(n-r-1)h\Big(\frac{r}{2}+1\Big)+\cdots+2h\Big(n-\frac{r}{2}-1\Big)

\displaystyle=Mh\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-\frac{r}{2}-1}2\Big(n-\frac{r}{2}-j\Big)h(j).

Therefore, since in

(n-r)h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-\frac{r}{2}}(n-j-1)h(j)+\sum_{j=n-\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-2}(n-j)h(j)

the function
h
is evaluated always in numbers greater or equal than
\frac{r}{2}

and Remark
2.2
gives

(n-r)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-\frac{r}{2}}(n-j-1)+\sum_{j=n-\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-2}(n-j)=\sum_{j=2}^{n-r}1+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}(r-n+j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}1,

it suffices to check that

\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-\frac{r}{2}-1}2\Big(n-\frac{r}{2}-j\Big)h(j)<\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-\frac{r}{2}-1}(n-j-1)h(j).

Hence, it suffices to show for every
\frac{r}{2}+1\leq j\leq n-\frac{r}{2}-1
that

2n-r-2j<n-j-1,

and this is equivalent to
n-r<j-1
, which follows from

j-1\geq\frac{r}{2}>\frac{r}{2}-1>n-r.

This finishes the proof of Case 1.

Case 2. Suppose
\frac{r}{2}-1=n-r
. Then,

\displaystyle F_{h}(3,n)>F_{h}(r,n)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\sum_{j=2}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}\Big(\frac{3r}{2}-1-j\Big)h(j)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}}^{\frac{3r}{2}-3}\Big(\frac{3r}{2}-1-j\Big)h(j)

\displaystyle>\sum_{j=2}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}\Big(\frac{3r}{2}-j\Big)h(j)+\frac{r}{2}h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{r-1}h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(k+j)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}}^{\frac{3r}{2}-3}\Big(\frac{3r}{2}-1-j\Big)h(j)

\displaystyle>\sum_{j=2}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(j)+\frac{r}{2}h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{r-1}h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(k+j).

Notice that

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(k+j)

\displaystyle=2h(2)+4h(3)+\cdots+2\Big(\frac{r}{2}-1\Big)h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+2\Big(\frac{r}{2}-2\Big)h\Big(\frac{r}{2}+1\Big)+\cdots+2h(r-2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{\frac{r}{2}}2(j-1)h(j)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{r-2}2(r-1-j)h(j).

Since
h
is strictly increasing, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(j)+\frac{r}{2}h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{r-1}h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(k+j)

\displaystyle<\Big(\frac{r}{2}-1\Big)\Big(\frac{r}{2}+2\Big)h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{r-1}h(j)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{r-2}2(r-1-j)h(j).

Thus, it suffices to check that for every
\frac{r}{2}+1\leq j
we have
2r-1-2j<\frac{3r}{2}-1-j
, which is direct.

Case 3. Suppose
\frac{r}{2}-1<n-r
. Then,

\displaystyle F_{h}(3,n)>F_{h}(r,n)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\sum_{j=2}^{n-r}(r-1)h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{n-2}(n-j)h(j)

\displaystyle>\sum_{j=2}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}rh(j)+\frac{r}{2}h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-\frac{r}{2}}h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(k+j)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\Big(\frac{r}{2}-1\Big)h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-r}(r-2)h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{n-2}(n-j)h(j)

\displaystyle>\sum_{j=2}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{n-\frac{r}{2}}h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(k+j).

Since
\frac{r}{2}-1<n-r
,

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(k+j)

\displaystyle=2h(2)+4h(3)+\cdots+2\Big(\frac{r}{2}-1\Big)h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\cdots+2\Big(\frac{r}{2}-1\Big)h(n-r+1)

\displaystyle+2\Big(\frac{r}{2}-2\Big)h(n-r+2)+\cdots+2h\Big(n-\frac{r}{2}-1\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{\frac{r}{2}}2(j-1)h(j)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-r}(r-2)h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{n-\frac{r}{2}-1}(2n-r-2j)h(j).

Since
h
is strictly increasing, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{n-\frac{r}{2}}h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r}{2}-1}h(k+j)

\displaystyle<\Big(\frac{r^{2}}{4}-2\Big)h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{n-\frac{r}{2}}h(j)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-r}(r-2)h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{n-\frac{r}{2}-1}(2n-r-2j)h(j).

Therefore, since in

\Big(\frac{r}{2}-1\Big)h\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=\frac{r}{2}+1}^{n-r}(r-2)h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{n-2}(n-j)h(j)

the function
h
is evaluated always in numbers greater or equal than
\frac{r}{2}
, it suffices to check that for every
n-r+1\leq j
we have
2n-r-2j+1\leq n-j
, which is immediate.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Given
5\leq r\leq n
with
r
odd and a strictly increasing function
h:\mathbb{Z}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, then
F_{h}(3,n)>F_{h}(r,n)
. If
h
is a strictly decreasing function, then
F_{h}(3,n)<F_{h}(r,n)
.

Proof.

The second statement is a consequence of the first one, if we consider the function
-h
.
Therefore, we can assume that
h
is a strictly increasing function.

Let us recall that

F_{h}(3,n)=nh(1)+\sum_{j=2}^{n-2}(n-j)h(j).

Case 1. Suppose
n-r<\frac{r-1}{2}
. Then,

\displaystyle F_{h}(r,n)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n-r}(n+1-j)h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}rh(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(k+j).

Therefore,

\displaystyle F_{h}(3,n)>F_{h}(r,n)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{n-2}(n-j)h(j)>\sum_{j=2}^{n-r}h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}rh(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(k+j)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\sum_{j=\frac{r+1}{2}}^{n-2}(n-j)h(j)>\sum_{j=2}^{n-r}h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(j-n+r)h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(k+j).

Since
n-r<\frac{r-1}{2}
,

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(k+j)

\displaystyle=2h(2)+\cdots+2(n-r)h(n-r+1)+\cdots

\displaystyle+2(n-r)h\Big(\frac{r+1}{2}\Big)+\cdots+2h\Big(n-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\Big).

Let

N=n-r-1+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(3r-2n-1)}{2}\frac{(3r-2n+1)}{2}+(n-r)(n-r+1)+(3r-2n-1)(n-r).

Therefore, since
h
is strictly increasing,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{n-r}h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(j-n+r)h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(k+j)

\displaystyle<Nh\Big(\frac{r+1}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=\frac{r+3}{2}}^{n-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}2\Big(n-\frac{r}{2}+\frac{1}{2}-j\Big)h(j).

Thus, it suffices to check that for every
\frac{r+3}{2}\leq j\leq n-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{1}{2}
we have

2n-r+1-2j<n-j,

and this is equivalent to
n-r<j-1
,
which is immediate since

j-1\geq\frac{r+1}{2}>n-r.

Case 2. Suppose
\frac{r-1}{2}\leq n-r
. Then,

\displaystyle F_{h}(r,n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}rh(j)+\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(n-r+1-j)h(j)+\sum_{j=\frac{r+1}{2}}^{n-r}(n-r+1-j)h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(k+j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(n+1-j)h(j)+\sum_{j=\frac{r+1}{2}}^{n-r}(n-r+1-j)h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(k+j).

Therefore,

\displaystyle F_{h}(3,n)>F_{h}(r,n)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\sum_{j=\frac{r+1}{2}}^{n-2}(n-j)h(j)>\sum_{j=2}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(j)+\sum_{j=\frac{r+1}{2}}^{n-r}(n-r+1-j)h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(k+j)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\sum_{j=\frac{r+1}{2}}^{n-r}(r-1)h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{n-2}(n-j)h(j)>\sum_{j=2}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(k+j).

Since
\frac{r-1}{2}\leq n-r
,

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(k+j)

\displaystyle=2h(2)+\cdots+2\Big(\frac{r-1}{2}\Big)h\Big(\frac{r+1}{2}\Big)+\cdots

\displaystyle+2\Big(\frac{r-1}{2}\Big)h(n-r+1)+\cdots+2h\Big(n-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\Big).

Let
N^{\prime}=\frac{r^{2}+2r-7}{4}
. Therefore, since
h
is strictly increasing,

\sum_{j=2}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(j)+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}h(k+j)<N^{\prime}h\Big(\frac{r+1}{2}\Big)+\sum_{j=\frac{r+3}{2}}^{n-r}(r-2)h(j)+\sum_{j=n-r+1}^{n-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}(2n-r+1-2j)h(j).

Thus, it suffices to check that for every
\frac{r+3}{2}\leq j\leq n-r
we have
r-2\leq r-1
, and for every
n-r+1\leq j\leq n-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{1}{2}
we have

2n-r+1-2j\leq n-j\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad n-r+1\leq j,

which is trivial.
∎

Example 2.5

Let us recall the following definitions from

.

Given a unicyclic graph

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
G
be a unicyclic graph with
n\geq 6
vertices.

(1)

If

is a strictly increasing function, then

n\,h(1)+\frac{1}{2}n(n-3)\,h(2)\leq W_{h}(G)\leq F_{h}(3,n),

the lower bound is attained if and only if
G=J_{n}
and the upper bound is attained if and only if
G=G_{3,n}
.

(2)

If
h
is a strictly decreasing function, then

F_{h}(3,n)\leq W_{h}(G)\leq n\,h(1)+\frac{1}{2}n(n-3)\,h(2),

the lower bound is attained if and only if
G=G_{3,n}
and the upper bound is attained if and only if
G=J_{n}
.

Proof.

The second statement is a consequence of the first one, if we consider the function
-h
.
Hence, we can assume that
h
is a strictly increasing function.

Since
G
is a unicyclic graph with
n
vertices, there are
n
edges and
n
pairs of adjacent vertices.
Thus, there are
\binom{n}{2}-n=\frac{1}{2}n(n-3)
pairs of vertices at distance at least 2.
The lower bound is attained if and only if
T
has diameter 2.
Notice that this means that the cycle has at most 5 vertices and since
n\geq 6
and any vertex which is not in the cycle is at distance at most 2 from any vertex in it, the cycle is necessarily
C_{3}
.
Therefore, the lower bound is attained if and only if
G=J_{n}
.

Assume that
G
is a unicyclic graph with
n
vertices such that
W_{h}(G)
is maximal and let
C
be the cycle.

Seeking for a contradiction, consider any vertex
v\in G
such that
v
is not in the unique cycle and
deg(v)\geq 3
or
v
is in the cycle and
deg(v)\geq 4
. Then, there is an exterior major vertex
w
in
T_{v}
(where possibly
v=w
). Hence, consider two terminal vertices of
w
,
u_{1},u_{2}
with
d(w,u_{1})=k
. Let us define new vertices
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}
and let

G^{\prime}:=\big(G\setminus[w,u_{1}]\big)\cup u_{2}v_{1}\cup v_{1}v_{2}\cup\cdots\cup v_{k-1}v_{k}.

Thus, it is immediate to see that, since
h
is strictly increasing,
W_{h}(G^{\prime})>W_{h}(G)
leading to contradiction.
Therefore,
deg(v)\leq 2
for every vertex
v\notin C
, and
deg(v)\leq 3
for every vertex
v\in C
.

Suppose there exist
v_{1},v_{2}\in C
with
deg(v_{i})=3
for
i=1,2
. Then,
T_{v_{i}}
is a path
[v_{i},u_{i}]
with length
d([v_{i},u_{i}])=l_{i}
. Let
D_{i}:=\sum_{v\in G\setminus\{T_{v_{1}}\cup T_{v_{2}}\}}d(v_{i},v)
for

. If

and

, let

. Then, let

Since

and

is strictly increasing, it follows that

leading to contradiction. Thus, we may assume, relabeling if necessary, that

Hence, let us define new vertices

Therefore,

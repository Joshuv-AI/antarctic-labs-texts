Abstract
In this article, using the weighted discrete least-squares, we propose a patch reconstruction finite element space with only one degree of freedom per element. As the approximation space, it is applied to the discontinuous Galerkin methods with the upwind scheme for the steady-state convection-diffusion-reaction problems over polytopic meshes. The optimal error estimates are provided in both diffusion-dominated and convection-dominated regimes. Furthermore, several numerical experiments are presented to verify the theoretical error estimates, and to well approximate boundary layers and/or internal layers.

Introduction

In this work, we consider the following convection-diffusion-reaction problem:

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta u+\nabla\cdot(\bm{b}u)+cu

\displaystyle=f\qquad\text{in}~\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.1)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=g\qquad\text{on}~\Gamma=\partial\Omega,

(1.2)

where
d=2
or
3
,
\Omega
is a bounded open polygonal (when
d=2
) or polyhedral (when
d=3
) domain with boundary
\Gamma
,
\nu
is a positive constant diffusivity coefficient,
\bm{b}
is the velocity vector field defined on
\overline{\Omega}=\Omega\cup\Gamma
with
\bm{b}\in[W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)]^{d}
,

is the reaction coefficient,

is a source term, and

is a function describing the

Though (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) looks simple, it is well known that the equation (
1.1
) shows the hyperbolic characteristics when its Péclet number
Pe=|\bm{b}|/\nu\gg 1
. It results to the famous convection-dominated regime. If we let
|\bm{b}|\sim O(1)
, then the condition
Pe\gg 1
is equivalent to
0<\nu\ll 1
. The above case originates from amounts of physical applications, such as chemical engineering, fluid mechanics, aerodynamics, etc., so it has been attracted many engineers and scientists from different fields over nearly half a century. Mathematically, in a convection-dominated regime, the solution and/or its derivatives change rapidly near the boundary lines, which is called boundary layer phenomenons. In addition, the thickness of boundary layers becomes thinner as the value of
\nu
decreases. Because we require a numerical solution to fit the exact solution in all cases, the dominated convection needs more numerical innovations added to the standard finite element methods. If only the standard finite element methods are applied, terrible spurious oscillations will appear near boundary layers unless the computational mesh is the magnitude of boundary layers
[
30
]
.

In the last two decades, the discontinuous Galerkin (DG) finite element methods
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
17
,
33
,
40
]
, firstly introduced in the early 1970’s, have prompted vigorous developments. DG methods have prominent advantages of high-order accuracy, high parallelizability, local mass conservation, allowed hanging nodes and easy handling of complicated geometries due to discontinuous functions employed. However, one of the shortcomings is well-known more degrees of freedom than the standard finite element methods. Effectively reducing degrees of freedom, several variants of DG methods such as HDG
[
18
,
32
,
36
]
and weak Galerkin methods
[
16
,
30
]
have been developed to solve (
1.1
)–(
1.2
). We note that most numerical experiments in the above works are implemented over traditional triangulations or several constructed meshes.

Recently, there are quite a few works concerning the DG method on polytopic (polygonal or polyhedral) meshes
[
1
,
14
,
15
,
19
,
20
,
23
,
25
]
, which provide more flexibility in implementation, in particular for domain with microstructures or problems with certain physical constraints. Among these beautiful works, the patch reconstruction finite element space adopting to general polytopic meshes, which is proposed by Li et al.
[
25
]
, attracts our attentions because of its arbitrary-order accuracy and only one degree of freedom per element. They will help reducing the scales of the linear equation system. However, following the rules introduced in
[

The rest of this article is organized as follows. In Section

, using the approximation space proposed in Section

, we present a symmetric interior penalty DG method with the upwind scheme to solve the convection-diffusion-reaction problem (

)–(

2.2
Element patch

If given a polytopic mesh

, for each element

, using the Von Neumann neighbor (adjacent face-neighboring elements) technique

which is an agglomeration of elements that contain

To fix ideas, we denote by

\begin{split}S_{0}(K)&:=\{K\},\\
S_{j}(K)&:=\big\{K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}~|~K^{\prime}\notin S_{j-1}(K),~\text{and}\\
&\qquad\exists E\in S_{j-1}(K)~\text{such~that~}K^{\prime}\cap E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\big\},\quad j=1,2,\cdots.\end{split}

(2.7)

Then, we set a uniform threshold

for the cardinality of

so that there always exists some integer

\bigcup_{j=0}^{N_{0}-1}S_{j}(K)\subseteq S(K)\subsetneqq\bigcup_{j=0}^{N_{0}}S_{j}(K).

(2.8)

Generally speaking, we enlarge
S(K)
element by element, and stop the recursive procedure (

) if the unique integer

satisfies (

2.3
Constructing reconstruction operator with one unknown per element

To ease the presentation we shall restrict ourselves
to the two-dimensional case, although the results here presented also hold in three

For any polygonal element

, we denote by

,

where the double-index,

\bm{\alpha}

Hence, we can define a matrix

DG with the reconstruction operator for the model problem

3.1
Setting of the problem

We first define the inflow and outflow parts of
\Gamma
as follows:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{-}

\displaystyle:=\big\{\bm{x}\in\Gamma:~\bm{b}(\bm{x})\cdot\bm{n}(\bm{x})<0\big\}=\text{inflow},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{+}

\displaystyle:=\big\{\bm{x}\in\Gamma:~\bm{b}(\bm{x})\cdot\bm{n}(\bm{x})\geqslant 0\big\}=\text{outflow},

(3.2)

where
\bm{n}(\bm{x})
is the unit outward normal vector to
\Gamma
at
\bm{x}\in\Gamma
. We also define an “effective" reaction function
r(\bm{x})
, and assume there exists a constant
r_{0}
such that

r(\bm{x})=c(\bm{x})+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\bm{b}(\bm{x})\geqslant r_{0}\geqslant 0,\quad\forall\bm{x}\in\Omega.

(3.3)

The well-posedness of the model problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) has been proved in
[
21
]
under homogeneous boundary conditions. Further if the domain
\Omega
is convex,
f\in H^{s}(\Omega),~s\geqslant 0
,
g=0
, and (
3.3
) also holds, then (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) has the unique solution
u
satisfies
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{2+s}(\Omega)
, and the following priori estimates
[
35
]
:

\displaystyle\nu^{3/2}\|u\|_{2,\Omega}+\nu^{1/2}\|u\|_{1,\Omega}+\|u\|_{0,\Omega}\leqslant C(\bm{b},r,d,\Omega)\|f\|_{0,\Omega},

\displaystyle\nu^{2}\|u\|_{2+s,\Omega}\leqslant C(d,\Omega)(\nu+\|\bm{b}\|_{1,\infty,\Omega}+\|r\|_{0,\infty,\Omega})\|f\|_{s,\Omega},\quad s>0.

Let
L
be the diameter of
\Omega
. In order to keep homogeneity of dimensions, we define

\|v\|_{k,\infty,\Omega}:=\sum_{s=0}^{k}L^{s}|v|_{s,\infty,\Omega}\quad\forall v\in W^{k,\infty}(\Omega).

(3.4)

For the convection velocity
\bm{b}
, we shall provide the following assumptions
[
4
]
:

1.

there exists
\xi\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
such that

\bm{b}\cdot\nabla\xi\geqslant 2b_{0}:=2\frac{\|\bm{b}\|_{0,\infty,\Omega}}{L};

2.

there exists
C_{b}>0
such that

|\bm{b}(\bm{x})|\geqslant C_{b}\|\bm{b}\|_{1,\infty,\Omega},\quad\forall\bm{x}\in\Omega;

3.

there exists
C_{r}>0
such that

\|r\|_{0,\infty,K}\leqslant C_{r}\big(\min_{\bm{x}\in K}r(\bm{x})+b_{0}\big),\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

In addition, it follows from (
3.4
) and (H1)–(H2) that

C_{b}\frac{\|\bm{b}\|_{1,\infty,\Omega}}{L}\leqslant b_{0}\leqslant\frac{\|\bm{b}\|_{1,\infty,\Omega}}{L},\quad|\bm{b}|_{1,\infty,\Omega}\leqslant\frac{\|\bm{b}\|_{1,\infty,\Omega}}{L}\leqslant\frac{b_{0}}{C_{b}}.

(3.5)

Let
K^{1}
and
K^{2}
be two adjacent elements of the partition
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We write
\bm{n}^{1}
and
\bm{n}^{2}
to denote the outward unit normal vectors on
e=\partial K^{1}\cap\partial K^{2}
, relative to
\partial K^{1}
and
\partial K^{2}
, respectively. Let
v
and
\bm{q}
be a scalar-valued function and a vector-valued function, respectively. Both of them are smooth inside
K^{1}
and
K^{2}
. Then, we write
v^{i}
and
\bm{q}^{i}
to denote the restrictions of
v
and
\bm{q}
to
K^{i}
,
i=1,2
, respectively. The averages of
v
and
\bm{q}
on
e
are given by

\{\!\{v\}\!\}=\frac{1}{2}(v^{1}+v^{2}),\quad\{\!\{\bm{q}\}\!\}=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{q}^{1}+\bm{q}^{2}),\quad\text{on}~e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}.

(3.6)

The jumps of
v
and
\bm{q}
across
e
are given by

\llbracket v\rrbracket=v^{1}\bm{n}^{1}+v^{2}\bm{n}^{2},\quad\llbracket\bm{q}\rrbracket=\bm{q}^{1}\cdot\bm{n}^{1}+\bm{q}^{2}\cdot\bm{n}^{2},\quad\text{on}~e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}.

(3.7)

On a boundary edge/face
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}\subset\partial\Omega
, we set

\{\!\{v\}\!\}=v,\quad\{\!\{\bm{q}\}\!\}=\bm{q},\quad\llbracket v\rrbracket=v\bm{n},\quad\llbracket\bm{q}\rrbracket=\bm{q}\cdot\bm{n}\quad\text{on}~e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}.

(3.8)

For future purposes we shall replace the average
\{\!\{\bm{b}v\}\!\}
on each interior edge/face
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}
by the upwind value of
\bm{b}v
, which is defined as follows
[
13
]
:

\{\!\{\bm{b}v\}\!\}_{up}=\begin{cases}\bm{b}v^{i},&\text{if}~\bm{b}\cdot\bm{n}^{i}>0,\\
\bm{b}v^{j},&\text{if}~\bm{b}\cdot\bm{n}^{i}<0,\\
\bm{b}\{\!\{v\}\!\},&\text{if}~\bm{b}\cdot\bm{n}^{i}=0,\\
\end{cases}

(3.9)

where
i,j\in\{1,2\}
and
i\neq j
. However, on each boundary edge/face
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}
, we already have
\{\!\{\bm{b}v\}\!\}=\bm{b}v
, and we leave it unchanged. Further one can find that the definition (
3.9
) misses conciseness and convenience for our analysis and implementations. To solve it, we have the following formula via some simple derivations.

\{\!\{\bm{b}v\}\!\}_{up}\cdot\bm{n}^{i}=\Big(\{\!\{\bm{b}v\}\!\}+\frac{|\bm{b}\cdot\bm{n}^{i}|}{2}\llbracket v\rrbracket\Big)\cdot\bm{n}^{i},\quad i=1,2.

(3.10)

The following crucial formula
[
3
]
will be extensively employed:

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial K}v\bm{q}\cdot\bm{n}ds=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\!\{\bm{q}\}\!\}\cdot\llbracket v\rrbracket ds+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}}\int_{e}\llbracket\bm{q}\rrbracket\{\!\{v\}\!\}ds,

(3.11)

where both
\bm{q}
and
v
are piecewise function over
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We shall also extensively use the following Poincaré–Friedrichs inequality for piecewise
H^{1}
functions
[
11
,
34
]
:

\|v\|_{0,\Omega}\leqslant LC_{P}\bigg(|v|_{1,h}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\frac{1}{|e|^{1/(d-1)}}\|\llbracket v\rrbracket\|_{0,e}^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(3.12)

where
C_{P}
is a positive constant depending on the minimum angle of sub-decomposition
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, and
|\cdot|_{1,h}
is the broken
H^{1}
-seminorm, which is defined as:

|v|_{1,h}=\bigg(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla v|^{2}d\bm{x}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

3.2
DG variational problem with the reconstructed operator

Motivated by the statements of Remark
2.1
, we define a piecewise bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{h}:\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as:

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{h}(v,w)=&\int_{\Omega}(cvw+\nu\nabla_{h}v\cdot\nabla_{h}w-v\bm{b}\cdot\nabla_{h}w)\\
&+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\backslash\Gamma^{-}}\int_{e}\{\!\{\bm{b}v\}\!\}_{up}\cdot\llbracket w\rrbracket+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\frac{\sigma_{e}\nu}{|e|^{1/(d-1)}}\int_{e}\llbracket v\rrbracket\cdot\llbracket w\rrbracket\\
&-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\big(\{\!\{\nu\nabla_{h}v\}\!\}\cdot\llbracket w\rrbracket+\{\!\{\nu\nabla_{h}w\}\!\}\cdot\llbracket v\rrbracket\big),\end{split}

(3.13)

where
\nabla_{h}
denotes the gradient element by element, and
\sigma_{e}
is the penalty parameter satisfying
0<\eta_{0}\leqslant\sigma_{e}\leqslant c_{0}
,
\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
. In addition, both
c_{0}
and
\eta_{0}
are global constants independent of any edge/face
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
(see
[
3
]
). We also define a piecewise linear form
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as:

\mathcal{L}(v)=\int_{\Omega}fv+\sum_{e\in\Gamma}\int_{e}\bigg(\frac{c_{e}\nu}{|e|^{1/(d-1)}}v-\nu\nabla v\cdot\bm{n}\bigg)g-\sum_{e\in\Gamma^{-}}\int_{e}\bm{b}\cdot\bm{n}gv.

(3.14)

Then, the general DG variational problem with respect to the model problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) is: to find
u\in\mathcal{V}
such that

\mathcal{A}_{h}(u,v)=\mathcal{L}(v),\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{V}.

(3.15)

Now, using the reconstructed operator
\mathcal{P}^{k}
defined in (
2.30
), the discrete DG variational problem is: to find
u_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
such that

\mathcal{A}_{h}(\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h},v)=\mathcal{L}(v),\quad\forall v\in V_{h}^{k}.

(3.16)

Let
u
be the solution of (
3.15
), and it is a simple matter to check the consistency in the sense that

\mathcal{A}_{h}(u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h},v)=0,\quad\forall v\in V_{h}^{k}.

(3.17)

Remark 3.1
.

We note that
\mathcal{P}^{k}V_{h}^{0}
is equivalent to
V_{h}^{k}
because
(\mathcal{P}^{k}v)|_{K}
is a
k
-th polynomial for any
v\in V_{h}^{0}
.

Next, we define the DG-energy norm as:

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{D}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{RC}^{2},

(3.18)

with

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{D}^{2}

\displaystyle=\nu|v|_{1,h}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\frac{\nu}{|e|^{1/(d-1)}}\|\llbracket v\rrbracket\|_{0,e}^{2},

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{RC}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(\overline{r}+b_{0})^{1/2}v\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\||\bm{b}\cdot\bm{n}|^{1/2}\llbracket v\rrbracket\|_{0,e}^{2},

where
\overline{r}
is the piecewise constant function defined as:

\overline{r}(\bm{x})|_{K}=\min_{\bm{x}\in K}r(\bm{x}),\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

(3.19)

Inspired by the definition of the DG-energy norm (
3.18
), we can also separate the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{h}
into two parts. To fix ideas,

\mathcal{A}_{h}(v,w)=a_{h}^{D}(v,w)+a_{h}^{RC}(v,w),

with the diffusion part:

\begin{split}a_{h}^{D}(v,w)=&\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla_{h}v\cdot\nabla_{h}w+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\frac{c_{e}\nu}{|e|^{1/(d-1)}}\int_{e}\llbracket v\rrbracket\cdot\llbracket w\rrbracket\\
&-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\big(\{\!\{\nu\nabla_{h}v\}\!\}\cdot\llbracket w\rrbracket+\{\!\{\nu\nabla_{h}w\}\!\}\cdot\llbracket v\rrbracket\big),\end{split}

(3.20)

and the reaction-convection part:

a_{h}^{RC}(v,w)=\int_{\Omega}(\gamma vw-v\bm{b}\cdot\nabla_{h}w)+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\backslash\Gamma^{-}}\int_{e}\{\!\{\bm{b}v\}\!\}_{up}\cdot\llbracket w\rrbracket.

(3.21)

desire to have a control also on the streamline derivative. It means that the stability is needed in another norm including a term of SUPG type. Hence, based on the norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
, we define a new DG-energy norm of SUPG type as:

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{S}=\big({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\|v\|_{b}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\|v\|_{b}=\bigg(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{h_{K}}{\|\bm{b}\|_{0,\infty,K}}\|\mathcal{P}^{k}(\bm{b}\cdot\nabla v)\|_{0,K}^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.35)

In addition, when the convection dominates, i.e.,
0<\nu\ll 1
described in Section
1
, we assume that

\nu<\frac{h_{K}\|\bm{b}\|_{0,\infty,K}}{2},\quad\|c+\nabla\cdot\bm{b}\|_{0,\infty,K}\leqslant\frac{\|\bm{b}\|_{0,\infty,K}}{h_{K}},\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

(3.36)

Remark 3.2
.

We must stress that the assumption (
3.36
) is valid only in a convection-dominated regime, while it does not hold when
\nu
is not a small parameter.

The stability in the norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{S}
can again be achieved through an inf-sup condition. In
C
we provide proofs of Lemma

and Theorem

.

), there exist two positive constants

both independent of

,

First, we stress that the error estimates shown below in the

.

Let

\|\psi\|_{2,\Omega}\leqslant C_{3}(\nu^{-2}\|\bm{b}\|_{0,\infty,\Omega}+\nu^{-2}\|r\|_{0,\infty,\Omega}+\nu^{-1})\|u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h}\|_{0,\Omega},

(4.4)

where

is a positive constant independent of
\nu
,
\bm{b}
, and
c
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
u
and
u_{h}
be the solutions of (
3.15
) and (
3.16
), respectively. In the hypotheses of (A4), there exist two different positive constants

and

such that

C_{5}h^{k+\frac{1}{2}}&\text{if convection dominates}.\end{cases}

(4.5)

Remark 4.2
.

If the convection dominates, the norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
should be replaced by the norm

ensures the results (

Remark 4.3
.

In spite of the suboptimal error order when the convection dominates, we shall show that the optimal

-error order can be still achieved in the numerical examples of Section

if the exact solution is smooth enough.

Remark 4.4
.

As mentioned above, we can just prove the error estimates in the
L^{2}
-norm with the divergence-free
\bm{b}
in theory. If
\bm{b}
is not divergence free, the auxiliary problem (
4.3
) first becomes: to find
\psi\in H^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\begin{cases}-\nu\Delta\psi+\nabla\cdot(\bm{b}\psi)+c\psi=u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h},&\text{in}~\Omega,\\
\psi=0,&\text{on}~\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Due to the asymmetry of
\mathcal{A}_{h}
, (
D.12
) in
D.3
has to be written as:

\displaystyle\|u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle=(-\nu\Delta\psi+\bm{b}\cdot\psi+c\psi,u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h})_{\Omega}+(\psi\nabla\cdot\bm{b},u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{h}(u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h},\psi)+(\psi\nabla\cdot\bm{b},u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h})_{\Omega}.

Without any projection or approximation property, the additional term
(\psi\nabla\cdot\bm{b},u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h})_{\Omega}
is too tricky to reach our desirable approximation results.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we provide the comprehensive process of constructing a patch reconstruction finite element space, and solve a weighted discrete least-square problem to derive a novel discontinuous finite element method over polytopic meshes. Together with the standard
symmetric interior penalty Galerkin method introduced in Section 2.6 of
[
34
]
, we propose a new DG approximation for the steady-state convection-diffusion-reaction problems. The most prominent characteristic is that arbitrary order accuracy can be achieved with only one degree of freedom per element. Not only reduce the scale of finial linear systems, it provides enough flexibility of designing polytopic meshes.

A variety of numerical experiments are introduced to demonstrate the theoretical results and to show performance of the reconstruction operator. In particular, it approximates the singularities well over refined polygonal meshes for the problem in a convection-dominated regime. It would be interesting to consider an improved operator to approximate boundary and internal layers well over polytopic meshes without showing spurious oscillations. We shall leave this issue to further exploration.

Appendix A
Interpretations of (A4)

In fact, the assumption (A4) is proposed literally from the problem (
2.32
). More specifically, (A4) requires that the number
\#\mathcal{I}_{K}
(note that
\#\mathcal{I}_{K}=\#S(K)=M
) cannot be small, and it is up to at least
n_{k}=\dim(P_{k})
to ensure the unisolvence of (
2.32
). However, compared with the above qualitative statement, we prefer employing matrix analysis to discuss the unisolvence of the equivalent problem (
2.12
).

The existence of
(\underline{\bm{X}}_{k}^{\top}\underline{\bm{X}}_{k})^{-1}
is equivalent to that all singular values of
\underline{\bm{X}}_{k}
are positive. We gather all singular values of
\underline{\bm{X}}_{k}
into a sequence
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{l}
,
l=\min\{M,n_{k}\}
, and the sequence satisfies that

\sigma_{1}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\sigma_{r}>0=\sigma_{r+1}=\cdots=\sigma_{l}

for some
r\leqslant l
. So roughly speaking,
the demand
M\geqslant n_{k}
ensures the possibility that each singular value of
\underline{\bm{X}}_{k}
is positive. Then for the reason that bigger value of
M
is better, we propose the following lemma to be an appropriate interpretation:

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose that
m\geqslant n+1
,
n\geqslant 2
, and
\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n}
are all singular values of a matrix
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with

\sigma_{1}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\sigma_{n}\geqslant 0
. Then we randomly delete a row of
\bm{A}
to obtain another matrix
\widetilde{\bm{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m-1)\times n}
. In addition, we denote all singular values of
\widetilde{\bm{A}}
by
\widetilde{\sigma}_{1},\cdots,\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}
with
\widetilde{\sigma}_{1}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}\geqslant 0
. Then the following results hold:

\sigma_{i}\geqslant\widetilde{\sigma}_{i}\geqslant\sigma_{i+1},~i=1,\cdots,n-1,\quad\sigma_{n}\geqslant\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}.

(A.1)

Proof 1
.

We denote by
\mathcal{U}_{r}
the set of all
r\times r
unitary matrices.
Let

\bm{H}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\bm{0}&\bm{A}\\
\bm{A}^{\top}&\bm{0}\\
\end{array}\right],

then
\bm{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m+n)\times(m+n)}
is a Hermite matrix. We let
\bm{A}=\bm{U}_{1}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^{\top}
be the singular value decomposition (SVD) of
\bm{A}
, where
\bm{U}=[\bm{U}_{1},\bm{U}_{2}]\in\mathcal{U}_{m}
,
\bm{V}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
,
\bm{U}_{1}
is the first
n
columns of
\bm{U}
, and
\bm{\Sigma}=\text{diag}\{\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n}\}
.
Let

\bm{Q}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\bm{U}_{1}&\sqrt{2}\bm{U}_{2}&\bm{U}_{1}\\
\bm{V}&\bm{0}&-\bm{V}\\
\end{array}\right],

and we note that
\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{U}_{m+n}
. Then by some derivations, we obtain that

\bm{Q}^{\top}\bm{H}\bm{Q}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\bm{\Sigma}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}&-\bm{\Sigma}\\
\end{array}\right].

(A.2)

It follows from (
A.2
) that the eigenvalue set of
\bm{H}
is

\lambda(\bm{H})=\big\{\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n},0,\cdots,0,-\sigma_{n},\cdots,-\sigma_{1}\big\}.

(A.3)

In (
A.3
) it is easy to observe that the first
n
numbers of
\lambda(\bm{H})
are all singular values of
\bm{A}
.

On the other hand, we let

\widetilde{\bm{H}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\bm{0}&\widetilde{\bm{A}}\\
\widetilde{\bm{A}}^{\top}&\bm{0}\\
\end{array}\right],

then
\widetilde{\bm{H}}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m+n-1)\times(m+n-1)}
is a Hermite matrix. We note that
\widetilde{\bm{H}}
can be regarded as a matrix given by deleting the
j
th row and the
j
th column from
\bm{H}
for a random number
j
. Following the same techniques of deriving (
A.3
), we obtain the eigenvalue set of
\widetilde{\bm{H}}
is

\lambda(\widetilde{\bm{H}})=\big\{\widetilde{\sigma}_{1},\cdots,\widetilde{\sigma}_{n},0,\cdots,0,-\widetilde{\sigma}_{n},\cdots,-\widetilde{\sigma}_{1}\big\}.

(A.4)

Similarly, in (
A.4
) the first
n
numbers of
\lambda(\widetilde{\bm{H}})
are all singular values of
\widetilde{\bm{A}}
.

Finally, it follows from (
A.3
)–(
A.4
), and the separation theorem originating from the famous “min-max" theorem for eigenvalues of Hermite matrices that the result (
A.1
) holds.

The above lemma implies that the smallest singular value (always nonnegative) of
\underline{\bm{X}}_{k}
becomes bigger as the value of
M
increasing. So the demand is reasonable and necessary that the number
M
should be greater than
n_{k}
to approach the assumption (A4).

Proof 13
.

For any

, it follows from (
4.3
), integration by parts, the fact that

, and (

) that

\begin{split}\|u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}&=(-\nu\Delta\psi+\bm{b}\cdot\nabla\psi+c\psi,u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h})_{\Omega}=\mathcal{A}_{h}(u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h},\psi)\\
&=\mathcal{A}_{h}(u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h},\psi-v_{h})\\
&=a_{h}^{D}(u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h},\psi-v_{h})+a_{h}^{RC}(u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h},\psi-v_{h}),\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}.\end{split}

(D.12)

Let
\zeta=u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h}
and
v_{h}=\widetilde{\psi}
, where with respect to
\psi
,

is an approximation polynomial satisfying (

For the diffusion part, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (

) and (

\begin{split}&a_{h}^{D}(u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h},\psi-v_{h})=a_{h}^{D}(\zeta,\psi-\widetilde{\psi})\\
=&\nu\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\nabla\zeta\cdot\nabla(\psi-\widetilde{\psi})-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\!\{\nu\nabla(\psi-\widetilde{\psi})\}\!\}\cdot\llbracket\zeta\rrbracket\\
\leqslant&\big(\kappa_{2}+\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\sqrt{C_{g}\mathcal{N}_{0}}\big)\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}h\|\psi\|_{2,\Omega}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\zeta\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.\end{split}

(D.13)

For the reaction-convection part, following the technique to rewrite
a_{h}^{RC}(v_{h},w_{h})
in Lemma
3.6
, and using the fact that

is divergence free, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (

\begin{split}&a_{h}^{RC}(u-\mathcal{P}^{k}u_{h},\psi-v_{h})=a_{h}^{RC}(\zeta,\psi-\widetilde{\psi})\\

Cavity as Radio Telescope for Galactic Dark Photon
Yanjie Zeng, Yuxin Liu, Chunlong Li, Yuxiang Liu, Bo Wang, Zhenxing Tang, Yuting Yang, Liwen Feng, Peng Sha, Zhenghui Mi, Weimin Pan, Tianzong Zhang, Zhongqing Ji, Yirong Jin, Jiankui Hao, Lin Lin, Fang Wang, Huamu Xie, Senlin Huang, Yifan Chen, Jing Shu, SHANHE Collaboration

Abstract
Dark photons, as a minimal extension of the Standard Model through an additional Abelian gauge group, may propagate relativistically across the galaxy, originating from dark matter decay or annihilation, thereby contributing to a galactic dark photon background. The generation of dark photons typically favors certain polarization modes, which are dependent on the interactions between dark matter and dark photons. We introduce a framework in which a resonant cavity is utilized to detect and differentiate these polarizations, leveraging the daily variation in expected signals due to the anisotropic distribution of dark photons and the rotation of the Earth. We conduct an experimental search using superconducting radio-frequency cavities, noted for their exceptionally high quality factors, proving them to be effective telescopes for observing galactic dark photons. This approach establishes the most stringent limits yet on the kinetic mixing coefficient between dark photons and electromagnetic photons, thereby unveiling a novel avenue for the indirect search for dark matter via multi-messenger astronomy. Keywords: Dark photon, Resonant cavity, Dark matter indirect detection, Multi-messenger astronomy



1. Introduction

Dark photon, a new vector field posited as a minimal extension to the Standard Model (SM), arises from speculative new physics theories such as extra dimensions or grand unification
Svrcek and Witten 2006
;
Abel et al. 2008
;
Arvanitaki et al. 2010
;
Goodsell et al. 2009
. When present in large occupation numbers, dark photons can form a wave-like background, inducing an effective electric current through mixing with electromagnetic photons
Horns et al. 2013
;
Chaudhuri et al. 2015
. To probe this intricate interaction, various experimental approaches have been undertaken. Notably, superconducting radio-frequency (SRF) cavities have shown exceptional promise, offering the most stringent constraints thus far
Cervantes et al. 2024
;
Romanenko et al. 2023
;
Tang et al. 2024
due to their extremely high quality factor.

The dark photon can itself serve as a dark matter candidate, characterized by its wave-like nature at sub-eV mass scales
Nelson and Scholtz 2011
;
Arias et al. 2012
. Beyond constituting the majority of cold dark matter in a standard halo distribution, dark photons may also arise in diverse cosmic scenarios
Chen et al. 2022a
, such as forming an isotropic cosmological background.
Anisotropic dark photons can originate from galactic sources including supernovae
Raffelt 1999
;
Eby and Takhistov 2024
;
de Giorgi and Jaeckel 2024
, bursts from boson clumps
Eby et al. 2016
;
Levkov et al. 2017
;
Budker et al. 2023
;
Du et al. 2024
, or ionization processes
Baumann et al. 2022
;
Duque et al. 2024
, and dipole radiation emitted by binary systems
Cardoso et al. 2016
;
Dror et al. 2021a
. Notably, dark photons produced via dark matter decay or annihilation contribute to a galactic background with a distinct directional flow from the galactic center.
The detection of these galactic dark photons thus serves as an indirect detection of dark matter. Their anisotropic distribution on Earth results in diurnal signal modulation in direction-sensitive detectors
Nitta et al. 2023
, which can be utilized as distinctive indicators of their galactic origins. Moreover, analyzing the polarization and spectral characteristics of dark photons offers in-depth insights into the nature of dark matter interactions.

In this work, we initiate the search for galactic dark photons using an SRF cavity. We demonstrate that projecting dark photon wavefunctions onto the cavity’s modes results in polarization-dependent modulation of signatures, effectively transforming the cavity into a radio-telescope for dark photons. Utilizing a tunable SRF cavity, cooled in a liquid helium environment to approximately
\displaystyle 2\,
K and connected to several amplifiers, we establish pioneering constraints on galactic dark photons across two distinct polarization modes. Our analytical method is broadly applicable to anisotropic dark photon fluxes, focusing specifically on two benchmark models of dark matter decay: a flat spectrum from four-body cascade decay
Nitta et al. 2023
and a narrow spectrum,
\displaystyle 1/500
of the central frequency, associated with two-body parametric decay. We explore a vast, previously untapped parameter space, leveraging the exceptional quality factor of the SRF cavity, which provides unique advantages over traditional copper cavities
Nitta et al. 2023
.

2. Galactic Dark Photon Flux

We begin by characterizing the wavefunctions and correlations inherent to a general dark photon background. The wavefunction
\displaystyle X^{\mu}
is formulated in the frequency domain as:

X^{\mu}(t,\bm{x})=\int\sum_{P}\,X_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})\,e^{\mu}_{P}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\omega t-\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x})}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{d}^{2}\hat{\bm{k}},

(1)

where the frequency
\displaystyle\omega
and spatial momentum
\displaystyle\bm{k}
adhere to the dispersion relation
\displaystyle\omega^{2}=|\bm{k}|^{2}+m_{X}^{2}
for the dark photon mass
\displaystyle m_{X}
.
\displaystyle X_{P}
signifies the amplitude for each polarization mode
\displaystyle P
, incorporating one longitudinal (L) and two transverse (T) components, while
\displaystyle e^{\mu}_{P}
is the associated polarization vector basis.

Considering the non-coherent nature of dark matter decay, galactic dark photons are expected to manifest as a stochastic Gaussian field. This is quantified by the two-point correlation function:

\langle X_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})X_{P^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime},\hat{\bm{k}}^{\prime})^{*}\rangle=\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\delta^{2}(\hat{\bm{k}}-\hat{\bm{k}}^{\prime})\delta_{PP^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}}).

(2)

Here,
\displaystyle\langle\cdots\rangle
denotes the ensemble average and
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})
is the power spectral density (PSD) of the
\displaystyle P
-mode dark photon from direction
\displaystyle\hat{\bm{k}}
. The total energy density of the dark photon is obtained by integrating
\displaystyle\omega^{2}\mathcal{S}_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})
over the frequency spectrum.

The polarization states and angular distribution of the dark photon spectrum depend on its production mechanisms, as detailed in the Supplemental Material. We examine scenarios where either longitudinal or transverse modes are predominant. Specifically, the longitudinal mode often arises in dark Higgs models, where a scalar field
\displaystyle\Phi
decays into two relativistic dark photons through an interaction Lagrangian proportional to
\displaystyle\Phi X^{\mu}X_{\mu}
. Conversely, transverse modes are typically generated through axion-photon-like interactions, denoted by a term proportional to
\displaystyle\Phi X_{\mu\nu}\tilde{X}^{\mu\nu}
, where
\displaystyle X_{\mu\nu}
represents the dark photon field tensor and
\displaystyle\tilde{X}^{\mu\nu}
its dual.

We consider two benchmark scenarios in which dark matter decays produce such fluxes, corresponding to two extreme source bandwidths relevant for resonant cavity detection.
For the interaction models under consideration, the decay of a massive scalar field into two dark photons can induce parametric instability, leading to rapid depletion of the scalar field through Bose-enhanced dark photon production
Dror et al. 2021b
. In conventional virialized scalar halos, such instabilities might be moderated by gravitational redshift when couplings are sufficiently weak
Arza et al. 2020
, albeit at the cost of reducing the decay products to near indetectable levels. To address this, we consider that a fraction of the scalar dark matter aggregates into dense clumps nested within broader, more diffuse miniclusters
Hogan and Rees 1988
;
Kolb and Tkachev 1994
. Within these dense regions, an equilibrium between dark photon production and escape, limited by the clumps’ size, culminates in a saturated state of dark photons. The clumps achieve stability via a balance of parametric decay and accretion, resulting in a steady and substantial dark photon flux that eclipses that from mere perturbative decay, akin to saturated superradiant clouds in axion-photon systems
Rosa and Kephart 2018
;
Spieksma et al. 2023
. The parametric instability band predominantly shapes the resulting dark photon spectrum, related to the scalar clumps’ core size
Hertzberg and Schiappacasse 2018
;
Levkov et al. 2020
. Assuming a virialized velocity distribution with a dispersion of
\displaystyle 10^{-3}\,c
, the produced dark photons exhibit a bandwidth approximately
\displaystyle 1/500
of the central frequency. An alternative production mechanism involves a scalar dark matter field decaying into two intermediate states,
\displaystyle\Phi
, which then undergo cascade decay into four dark photons
Nitta et al. 2023
. Here, the dark photon spectrum is broadly flat, confined within a frequency range dictated by the dark matter mass.

In both scenarios, local dark photon flux densities may soar to
\displaystyle 1000\,\rho_{\gamma}
, compatible with cosmological constraints on dark matter decay
Dror et al. 2021b
;
Nitta et al. 2023
, where
\displaystyle\rho_{\gamma}
signifies the local electromagnetic photon density
Workman and Others 2022
.
The angular distribution of the incoming dark photon flux corresponds to the line-of-sight integral of the dark matter density, as depicted in Fig.
1
a. This parameter is commonly referred to as the J-factor in the literature on indirect dark matter detection
Cirelli et al. 2011
. It is important to note that certain nearby sources, indicated by the orange circle in Fig.
1
a, may produce significantly stronger transient fluxes.

3. Cavity as a Dark Photon Telescope

When dark photon fluxes transit through a cavity, they can potentially excite a cavity mode if their frequency aligns with the resonant frequency of the cavity, a process facilitated by kinetic mixing with electromagnetic photons
Tang et al. 2024
. In the interaction picture, dark photons contribute to an effective electric current
\displaystyle\bm{J}_{\text{eff}}=\epsilon\,m_{X}^{2}\bm{X}
, where
\displaystyle\epsilon
denotes the kinetic mixing coefficient, and
\displaystyle\bm{X}
represents the spatial component of the dark photon wavefunction. As elucidated in Supplemental Material, the rate of excitation depends on the overlap between
\displaystyle\bm{X}
and the cavity mode’s electric field
\displaystyle\bm{E}_{0}
, quantified by the parameter:

C_{P}(\hat{\bm{k}})\equiv\frac{1}{V}\left|\int_{V}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}\,\hat{\bm{e}}_{P}(\hat{\bm{k}})\cdot\bm{E}_{0}(\bm{x}\,)\,\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{x}\,\right|^{2},

(3)

where
\displaystyle\hat{\bm{e}}_{P}
is the spatial unit directional vector of the polarization basis
\displaystyle e_{P}^{\mu}
, and the integral is taken over the cavity’s volume
\displaystyle V
. Typically, the phase factor
\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}
is approximated as
\displaystyle 1
for the overlap function for DPDM
Tang et al. 2024
, due to the de-Broglie wavelength
\displaystyle 1/|\bm{k}|
of the non-relativistic field being considerably longer than the cavity scale. For axisymmetric cavity modes, the angular dependence reduces to the polar angle
\displaystyle\Psi\equiv-\hat{\bm{k}}\cdot\hat{\bm{l}}_{\rm cav}
, with
\displaystyle\hat{\bm{l}}_{\rm cav}
representing the cavity’s symmetry axis, as depicted in Fig.
1
b. We numerically evaluate the overlap factor
\displaystyle C_{P}(\Psi)
for the TM
010
mode of a single-cell
\displaystyle 1.3
GHz elliptical cavity utilized in this study, illustrated in Fig.
1
c. The longitudinal mode, with
\displaystyle\hat{\bm{e}}_{L}(\hat{\bm{k}})
aligned with
\displaystyle\hat{\bm{k}}
, achieves maximum overlap at
\displaystyle\Psi=0
and diminishes to zero at
\displaystyle\Psi=\pi/2
. The two transverse circular polarized modes, indistinct from one another, display an inverse variation compared to the longitudinal mode. These evaluations assume a relativistic limit for the dark photon, where
\displaystyle|\bm{k}|\approx\omega_{0}
.

The PSD absorbed by the cavity from dark photon fields bears resemblance to that from DPDM
Tang et al. 2024
:

S_{\mathrm{sig}}^{P}(\omega,t)=\frac{\epsilon^{2}\,m_{X}^{4}\,V\,|\bm{e}_{P}|^{2}}{4(\omega-\omega_{0})^{2}+\omega_{0}^{2}/Q_{L}^{2}}\int\mathcal{S}_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})C_{P}(\Psi(t))\mathrm{d}^{2}\hat{\bm{k}}.

(4)

The distinction comes with the factor
\displaystyle|\bm{e}_{P}|^{2}
denotes the magnitude square of the spatial component of the polarization basis
\displaystyle e_{P}^{\mu}
. This factor equates to
\displaystyle 1
for transverse modes and escalates to
\displaystyle\omega^{2}/m_{X}^{2}
for the longitudinal mode, suggesting that a relativistic longitudinal mode could significantly enhance signal power compared to transverse modes, given the same spectra. Here,
\displaystyle Q_{L}
indicates the cavity’s loaded quality factor, determining the resonance bandwidth. In the Galactic frame, the Earth’s rotation leads to temporal variations in the cavity’s symmetry axis
\displaystyle\hat{\bm{l}}_{\rm cav}(t)
and the associated
\displaystyle\Psi(t)
. Consequently, these changes result in polarization-dependent modulations in the PSD, which can be leveraged as distinct signatures for the detection of anisotropic dark photons.

4. Diurnal Modulation Analysis

We now embark on a search for the diurnal modulation of signals stemming from galactic dark photons, leveraging a single-cell elliptical niobium SRF cavity
Tang et al. 2024
. The cavity was equipped with a frequency tuning mechanism
Pischalnikov et al. 2015
;
Pischalnikov et al. 2019
and an amplifier circuit, and maintained in liquid helium at a temperature of approximately
\displaystyle 2
K. The experimental platform
Mi et al. 2015
;
Liu et al. 2016
;
Zhou et al. 2018
;
Zhang et al. 2022
;
Yang et al. 2022
;
Wang et al. 2023
facilitated the calibration of relevant parameters, including the loaded quality factor
\displaystyle Q_{L}
, coupling factor
\displaystyle\beta
, net amplification factor
\displaystyle G_{\rm net}
, and the resonant frequency of the TM
010
mode, centered around
\displaystyle\omega_{0}/(2\pi)\approx 1.3
GHz, along with its stability range for each scanning process. Each scan step was approximately
\displaystyle 5
minutes, with
\displaystyle 100
seconds dedicated to data acquisition and the remainder to frequency tuning and parameter calibration. During two continuous operational periods from April 12-14 and 23-26, 2023, we completed
\displaystyle N_{\rm bin}=350
and
\displaystyle 800
scan steps respectively, covering a total frequency range of
\displaystyle 1.37
MHz.

In our analysis, only the received power at the resonant frequency bin with a bandwidth of
\displaystyle\Delta\omega_{0}/(2\pi)=11.5
Hz
Tang et al. 2024
, denoted by
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{i}
for the
\displaystyle i
-th step, was considered. The dominant noise contribution typically comes from the amplifier. For each run, we computed the average and standard deviation of
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{i}
, denoted as
\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{P}}
and
\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}
, respectively, and determined the normalized power excess
\displaystyle\delta^{i}\equiv(\mathcal{P}^{i}-\overline{\mathcal{P}})/{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}}
. These are shown as black dots in Fig.
2
, aligning closely with a standard normal distribution with a mean of
\displaystyle<10^{-4}
, a standard deviation of
\displaystyle 0.9991
, and a p-value of
\displaystyle 0.80
, with no
\displaystyle 4\sigma
excess observed.

Unlike in the scan search for dark photon dark matter, where a constant fit and normalized power excess were calculated across every
\displaystyle 50
contiguous bins to ensure environmental stability
Tang et al. 2024
, the current search combines all bins from both runs without deliberately suppressing potential diurnal variations.

5. Conclusion —

In this study, we have investigated galactic dark photons as decay products from scalar dark matter, demonstrating the SRF cavity’s sensitivity to their largely unexplored parameter space in terms of mass and kinetic mixing coefficient.
The two production mechanisms we analyzed allow for ultralight bosonic dark matter decay without exponential depletion of the background, thus providing a novel avenue for the indirect detection of scalar dark matter via dark photons. Due to their anisotropic distribution, the induced signal in a cavity at rest relative to the Earth exhibits diurnal modulation, a feature previously used to search for Earth-scattering effects from heavier, boosted dark matter
Ge et al. 2021
;
Fornal et al. 2020
;
Chen et al. 2023a
;
Xia et al. 2022
;
Cui et al. 2022
;
Qiao et al. 2023
. This modulation pattern is sensitive to the polarization mode of dark photons, with constraints being particularly stringent for the longitudinal polarization mode.
The exceptional sensitivity of our study is attributed to the extremely high quality factor of the SRF cavity. This high quality factor results in a narrow bandwidth, which inherently supports low noise levels, thus enabling the exclusion of galactic dark photons across a spectrum bandwidth substantially broader than the cavity’s own bandwidth. This approach mirrors the methodology employed in DPDM searches
Cervantes et al. 2024
;
Romanenko et al. 2023
;
Tang et al. 2024
, showcasing the effectiveness of SRF cavities in probing wide spectral ranges of dark photon signatures.

Looking ahead, as more cavities become operational, a network of detectors with extensive long-baseline separation
Foster et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2022a
;
Afach et al. 2023
;
Jiang et al. 2024
will enable detailed analysis and precise localization of dark photons, including their polarization modes and chiral components
Chen et al. 2022a
. This approach not only enhances our understanding of dark photon properties at both macroscopic and microscopic levels but also, when integrated with other astrophysical observations such as gravitational waves, opens new frontiers in multi-messenger astronomy and cosmology
Dailey et al. 2021
. Beyond dark photon detection, the versatility of SRF cavities extends to axion searches and high-frequency gravitational wave detection through heterodyne upconversion
Goryachev et al. 2019
;
Berlin et al. 2020
;
Nitta et al. 2023
. Moreover, enhancements in SRF cavity sensitivity can be achieved through advanced readout systems
Dixit et al. 2021
;
Agrawal et al. 2024
;
Backes et al. 2021
;
Li et al. 2020
;
Chen et al. 2022b
;
Wurtz et al. 2021
;
Jiang et al. 2023
;
Chen et al. 2023b
.

I: Cavity Response to General Dark Photon Background

This section delves into a comprehensive calculation of the signal generated in a cavity by a general background of kinetically mixing dark photons. The background is characterized by an angular-dependent power spectral density (PSD) function. Unlike non-relativistic dark photon dark matter (DPDM), a relativistic dark photon can exhibit a position-dependent phase factor in the cavity signal, owing to its de Broglie wavelength being comparable to the cavity size.

A. General Dark Photon Background

We examine a dark photon
\displaystyle X^{\mu}
with mass
\displaystyle m_{X}
and momentum
\displaystyle\bm{k}
in the Galactic frame. As a massive vector boson, it encompasses three degrees of freedom: the longitudinal (L) and two transverse modes (left/right-hand circularly polarized states,
\displaystyle T_{l}
and
\displaystyle T_{r}
, respectively). Polarization (P) basis vectors
\displaystyle e^{\mu}_{P}

are introduced, conforming to the unitary gauge and normalization condition
\displaystyle e^{\mu}_{P}e_{\mu}^{P}=-1
, and takes the forms:

\displaystyle e^{\mu}_{T_{l}}

\displaystyle=(0,\left(\hat{\bm{e}}_{T_{1}}-\mathrm{i}\hat{\bm{e}}_{T_{2}}\right)/\sqrt{2}),

(S1)

\displaystyle e^{\mu}_{T_{r}}

\displaystyle=(0,\left(\hat{\bm{e}}_{T_{1}}+\mathrm{i}\hat{\bm{e}}_{T_{2}}\right)/\sqrt{2}),

\displaystyle e^{\mu}_{L}

\displaystyle=(k/m_{X},\omega\,\hat{\bm{k}}/m_{X}).

Here,
\displaystyle\hat{\bm{k}}
,
\displaystyle\hat{\bm{e}}_{T_{1}}
and
\displaystyle\hat{\bm{e}}_{T_{2}}
form a Cartesian orthogonal basis parameterized in the Galactic frame as:

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\sin\theta_{k}\cos\phi_{k},\sin\theta_{k}\sin\phi_{k},\cos\theta_{k}\right),

(S2)

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{e}}_{T_{1}}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\cos\theta_{k}\cos\phi_{k},\cos\theta_{k}\sin\phi_{k},-\sin\theta_{k}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{e}}_{T_{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(-\sin\phi_{k},\cos\phi_{k},0\right),

using spherical coordinate
\displaystyle(\theta_{k},\phi_{k})
. The dark photon energy
\displaystyle\omega
satisfies the dispersion relation
\displaystyle\omega^{2}=|\bm{k}|^{2}+m_{X}^{2}
. It’s notable that the spatial components of the transverse basis,
\displaystyle\bm{e}_{T_{l/r}}\equiv\left(\hat{\bm{e}}_{T_{1}}\mp\mathrm{i}\hat{\bm{e}}_{T_{2}}\right)/\sqrt{2}
, are unit vectors, while the longitudinal one is
\displaystyle|\bm{e}_{L}|=\omega/m_{X}
. Therefore, for relativistic dark photons, the longitudinal mode can produce significantly larger signals in a cavity, as will be discussed further.

A general time-dependent dark photon field is a sum of dark photons with various momenta:

X^{\mu}(t,\bm{x})=\int\sum_{P}\,X_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})\,e^{\mu}_{P}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\omega t-\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x})}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{d}^{2}\hat{\bm{k}},

(S3)

where
\displaystyle X_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})
represents the corresponding field amplitude. As the production mechanisms considered in this study are based on incoherent decay of dark matter from different locations in the galaxy, the dark photon field should be a sum of incoherent waves. Thus, we treat
\displaystyle X_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})
as a Gaussian stochastic field, characterized by its two-point correlation function:

\langle X_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})X_{P^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime},\hat{\bm{k}}^{\prime})^{*}\rangle=\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\delta^{2}(\hat{\bm{k}}-\hat{\bm{k}}^{\prime})\delta_{PP^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}}).

(S4)

In this expression,
\displaystyle\langle\cdots\rangle
denotes the ensemble average.
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})
is the dark photon PSD, reflecting the dark photon spectrum from the direction
\displaystyle\hat{\bm{k}}
of polarization mode
\displaystyle P
. The normalization of the PSD leads to the dark photon energy density

\rho_{X}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int\omega^{2}\,\sum_{P}\mathcal{S}_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})\,\mathrm{d}\omega\,\mathrm{d}^{2}\hat{\bm{k}}.

(S5)

B. Cavity Response to Dark Photons

We analyze the behavior of a cavity during the transit of a dark photon, particularly in the context of kinetic mixing with electromagnetic photons
\displaystyle A^{\mu}
. The interaction Lagrangian in the interaction picture is given by
\displaystyle\epsilon m_{X}^{2}A^{\mu}X_{\mu}
, where
\displaystyle\epsilon
represents the kinetic mixing coefficient. In this scenario, the dark photon functions as an effective current, altering the electromagnetic field equation:

\Box\bm{E}(t,\bm{x})=\epsilon\,m_{X}^{2}\partial_{t}\bm{X}.

(S6)

Here, the electric field
\displaystyle\bm{E}
can be expressed as a sum of orthogonal eigenmodes within the cavity:

\bm{E}(t,\bm{x})=\sum_{n}\xi_{n}(t)\bm{E}_{n}(\bm{x}).

(S7)

The mode basis functions
\displaystyle\bm{E}_{n}
comply with:

(\nabla^{2}+\omega_{n}^{2})\bm{E}_{n}(\bm{x})=0,\qquad\int_{V}\bm{E}_{n}\cdot\bm{E}_{m}^{*}\,\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{x}=\delta_{mn},

(S8)

where
\displaystyle\omega_{n}
are the resonant frequencies, and
\displaystyle\xi_{n}(t)
describe their temporal evolutions. Integrating Eq. (
S7
) and (
S8
) into Eq. (
S6
) and considering cavity dissipation, we can solve for
\displaystyle\xi_{0}
in the frequency domain as detailed in
Tang et al. 2024

\xi_{0}(\omega)=\frac{\mathrm{i}\,\epsilon\,m_{X}^{2}\,\omega}{\omega^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2}+\mathrm{i}\omega_{0}\omega/Q_{L}}\sum_{P}\int X_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})\,\mathrm{d}^{2}\hat{\bm{k}}\int_{V}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}\,\bm{e}_{P}\cdot\bm{E}_{0}(\bm{x})\,\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{x}.

(S9)

Here,
\displaystyle Q_{L}
is the loaded quality factor of the
\displaystyle\bm{E}_{0}
mode. The two-point correlation function of
\displaystyle\xi_{i}(\omega)
contributes to the signal power in the cavity as
\displaystyle\langle\xi_{0}^{P}(\omega)\xi_{0}^{P^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})^{*}\rangle=\delta_{PP^{\prime}}\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})S_{\mathrm{sig}}^{P}(\omega)
. Utilizing Eq. (
S4
), we obtain the expression for
\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{sig}}^{P}
:

S_{\mathrm{sig}}^{P}(\omega)=\frac{\epsilon^{2}\,m_{X}^{4}\,V\,|\bm{e}_{P}|^{2}}{4(\omega-\omega_{0})^{2}+\omega_{0}^{2}/Q_{L}^{2}}\int\mathcal{S}_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})C_{P}(\hat{\bm{k}})\mathrm{d}^{2}\hat{\bm{k}},

(S10)

where we apply
\displaystyle Q_{L}\gg 1
to simplify the Lorentz resonant factor. The dark photon overlapping form factor
\displaystyle C_{P}(\hat{\bm{k}})
is defined as:

C_{P}(\hat{\bm{k}})\equiv\frac{1}{V}\left|\int_{V}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}\,\hat{\bm{e}}_{P}(\hat{\bm{k}})\cdot\bm{E}_{0}(\bm{x}\,)\,\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{x}\,\right|^{2}.

(S11)

As previously discussed, the factor
\displaystyle|\bm{e}_{P}|^{2}
in Eq. (
S10
) enhances the longitudinal mode’s contribution to the signal for relativistic dark photons. The form factor is maximized when the dark photon wavefunction, proportional to
\displaystyle\hat{\bm{e}}_{P}
, aligns with the electric field
\displaystyle\bm{E}_{0}
. Compared to non-relativistic DPDM
Tang et al. 2024
, the form factor here includes an additional phase factor
\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}
, which could potentially suppress the signal. As the TM
010
mode of the axis-symmetric cavity in our study lacks an azimuthal component, the two transverse modes,
\displaystyle T_{l}
and
\displaystyle T_{r}
, have identical form factors.

II: Galactic Dark Photons from Dark Matter Decay

This section delves into the production of galactic dark photons as a result of dark matter decay. We initially explore various coupling types, each leading to distinct dominant polarization modes. When the coupling strength is significant, two-body decay can exponentially deplete the scalar dark matter background; otherwise, it may yield a dark photon flux too weak for detection. We then introduce two alternative production mechanisms, detailing how they can generate observable galactic dark photons. It should be noted that the constraints for DPDM may not apply universally to galactic dark photons. Only constraints that do not presuppose dark photons as the predominant component of dark matter should be considered relevant.

B. Two-body Parametric Decay

In scenarios with a coherent oscillating scalar background
\displaystyle\Phi=\Phi_{0}\cos m_{\Phi}t
, the decay rate of a scalar into dark photons with the same momentum as previously generated particles is enhanced due to Bose enhancement in the final state phase space, fostering exponential growth in dark photon density. This phenomenon is evident from the dark photon’s equation of motion:

\ddot{X}+\left(k^{2}+m_{X}^{2}+q\cos m_{\Phi}t\right)X=0,

(S12)

where
\displaystyle q
is a parameter dependent on the coupling between
\displaystyle\Phi
and the dark photon. For example,
\displaystyle q=g_{a}\Phi_{0}km_{\Phi}
in the case of axion-photon coupling, and
\displaystyle q=g_{H}\Phi_{0}
or
\displaystyle g_{H}\Phi_{0}m_{\Phi}^{2}/(2m_{X}^{2})
for dark Higgs-like coupling with transverse and longitudinal modes of dark photon, respectively. The above equation (
S12
) is analogous to the Mathieu equation, which predicts several instability bands in momentum space
Mathieu 1868
;
Kovacic et al. 2018
.
Within these bands, one can expect a significant exponential increase in occupation number, a process typically referred to as parametric resonance.
The first band typically has the broadest bandwidth and can be analyzed either numerically or through semi-analytic methods like Floquet theory
Stoker 1950
;
Acar and Feeny 2016
;
Hertzberg and Schiappacasse 2018
or non-adiabatic condition checks
Chen et al. 2023c
, yielding the range:

\frac{m_{\Phi}^{2}}{4}-q\leq k^{2}+m_{X}^{2}\leq\frac{m_{\Phi}^{2}}{4}+q.

(S13)

Under the assumption that
\displaystyle m_{\Phi}\gg m_{X}
, the first instability band centers around
\displaystyle m_{\Phi}
with a bandwidth
\displaystyle\Delta k=g_{a}\Phi_{0}m_{\Phi}
for axion-photon coupling, and
\displaystyle\Delta k=2g_{H}\Phi_{0}/m_{\Phi}
and
\displaystyle g_{H}\Phi_{0}m_{\Phi}/m_{X}^{2}
for the transverse and longitudinal modes in dark Higgs coupling. Both numerical and Floquet theory analyses suggest that the exponential growth rate approximately equals the bandwidth
\displaystyle\Delta k
. Consequently, the production of dark photons is predominantly in the longitudinal mode from dark-Higgs coupling, consistent with perturbative analysis.

Alternatively, this exponential growth can be effectively described in kinetic theory, where the decay rate is significantly amplified by the dark photon occupation number
\displaystyle f_{X}

\frac{\mathrm{d}n_{X}}{\mathrm{d}t}=2n_{\Phi}(1+2f_{X})\Gamma(\Phi\rightarrow XX)+\cdots.

(S14)

Here,
\displaystyle n_{X}

\displaystyle n_{\Phi}

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\Delta k\ll m_{\Phi}/2

\displaystyle f_{X}\approx 2\pi^{2}n_{X}/(k^{2}\Delta k)

\displaystyle 8\pi^{2}n_{\Phi}\Gamma(\Phi\rightarrow XX)/(k^{2}\Delta k)

\displaystyle\Delta k

\displaystyle n_{\Phi}\approx\rho_{\Phi}/m_{\Phi}\approx m_{\Phi}\Phi_{0}^{2}/2

\displaystyle k\approx m_{\Phi}/2

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{crit}}

\displaystyle f_{X}

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle X^{\mu}

\displaystyle m_{\Phi}\ll m_{\rm DM}

\displaystyle m_{\rm DM}

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle m_{\rm DM}/2

;
Elor et al. 2016

\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{\text{CD}}}{\mathrm{d}\omega}=\frac{8}{m_{\rm DM}}\Theta\left(\frac{m_{\rm DM}}{2}-\omega\right).

\displaystyle\chi

D. Angular Spectral Density

\displaystyle m_{\rm DM}=m_{\chi}

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{k}}\equiv(\theta_{k},\phi_{k})

\displaystyle\rho^{\rm DM}(r)

\displaystyle r_{\odot}=8.33

\mathcal{S}_{P}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})=\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\omega}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\Phi_{X}}{\mathrm{d}^{2}\hat{\bm{k}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega}.

(S26)

This relationship forms the basis for two benchmark angular spectral densities discussed in the maintext:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{P}^{\text{PD}}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi\,Q\,\rho_{X}}{\omega\,m_{\rm DM}^{2}\,\bar{J}}\,J(\theta_{k})\,\Theta\left(\frac{m_{\rm DM}}{4Q}-|\omega-\frac{m_{\rm DM}}{2}|\right),

(S27)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{P}^{\text{CD}}(\omega,\hat{\bm{k}})=

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi\,\rho_{X}}{\omega\,m_{\rm DM}^{2}\,\bar{J}}\,J(\theta_{k})\,\Theta\left(\frac{m_{\rm DM}}{2}-\omega\right),

where
\displaystyle\bar{J}\equiv\int J(\hat{\bm{k}})\mathrm{d}^{2}\hat{\bm{k}}/(4\pi)\approx 2.19
.

To calculate the modulation of galactic dark photon signals, it is necessary to consider the variation of the cavity’s symmetry axis in the Galactic frame, denoted as
\displaystyle\hat{\bm{l}}_{\rm cav}(t)\equiv(\theta_{\rm cav}(t),\phi_{\rm cav}(t))
. For simplicity, we set the azimuthal angle of the Earth’s rotation axis at
\displaystyle 0
, leading to the following geometric relationships:

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{\rm cav}(t)=

\displaystyle\cos\,\delta_{\rm GC}\,\cos\,\varphi_{\rm cav}\,\cos\left(2\pi t/24+\lambda_{\rm cav}-\alpha_{\rm GC}\right)+\sin\,\delta_{\rm GC}\,\sin\,\varphi_{\rm cav},

(S28)

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{\rm cav}(t)\sin\phi_{\rm cav}(t)=

\displaystyle\cos\varphi_{\rm cav}\sin\left(2\pi t/24+\lambda_{\rm cav}-\alpha_{\rm GC}\right).

In this context,
\displaystyle(\varphi_{\rm cav},\lambda_{\rm cav})=(39.9^{\circ},116.4^{\circ})
represent the latitude and longitude of the cavity, which is oriented towards the Earth’s center throughout the experiment. The coordinates
\displaystyle(\delta_{\rm GC},\alpha_{\rm GC})=(-29.0^{\circ},266.4^{\circ})
specify the declination and right ascension of the GC in the celestial sphere. The time variable
\displaystyle t
is expressed in hours, based on the J2000 standard time system
Board
. Practically, due to the axial symmetry of the dark photon angular spectrum, the modulation of the cavity signal does not exhibit sensitivity to variations in
\displaystyle\phi_{\rm cav}(t)
.

III: Experimental Operations

This section outlines the experimental procedures and the calibration of relevant parameters.

We conducted two continuous operational periods from April 12-14 and April 23-26, 2023, executing
\displaystyle N_{\rm bin}=350
and
\displaystyle 800
scan steps respectively. In each step, the I/Q mode of the spectrum analyzer recorded the complex voltage signals from the readout antenna, encompassing a data recording phase and dual calibration phases for the resonant frequency.

During each scan step labeled by
\displaystyle i

\displaystyle\omega_{0}^{i}
by identifying the peak in the power spectral density (PSD). Subsequently, the noise source was deactivated, and the time-domain voltage signal was recorded over an integration time of
\displaystyle t_{\rm int}=100\,\mathrm{s}
, converting into the received power
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{i}
. We recorded the end time
\displaystyle t^{i}
of each integration phase via a computer linked to the spectrum analyzer. Assuming minimal variation in
\displaystyle\hat{\bm{l}}_{\rm cav}(t)
within the short integration period, we evaluated the expected signal
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{i}_{\rm sig}
at
\displaystyle t^{i}
. Post-recording, we recalibrated the frequency to mitigate any shifts smaller than
\displaystyle\Delta\omega_{0}
and adjusted the cavity using a step motor-controlled tuning arm
Pischalnikov et al. 2015

Pischalnikov et al. 2019

\displaystyle 1.3\,\mathrm{kHz}

IV: Data Analysis

The data for this study are presented in Fig. 2 in the main text. Given the Gaussian nature of the measured data, we utilize the following probability function:

\mathrm{Pr}\left(\delta^{i}\mid\epsilon,m_{X}\right)\propto\prod_{i}\exp\left(\frac{-\left(\delta^{i}/\tilde{\delta}_{\rm sig}^{i}-\left(\epsilon/\epsilon_{\rm ref}\right)^{2}\right)^{2}}{2(\sigma_{\rm tot}^{i})^{2}}\right),

(S29)

where
\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}_{\rm sig}^{i}\equiv\delta_{\rm sig}^{i}|_{\epsilon=\epsilon_{\rm ref}}
is introduced alongside an arbitrary reference value
\displaystyle\epsilon_{\rm ref}
to account for both measurement error and uncertainties from calibrated parameters. Employing the error propagation formula, we deduce the combined uncertainty for
\displaystyle\delta^{i}/\tilde{\delta}_{\rm sig}^{i}
, labeled as
\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm tot}^{i}
:

\left(\sigma_{\rm tot}^{i}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{\left(\tilde{\delta}_{\rm sig}^{i}\right)^{2}}+\frac{(\delta^{i})^{2}}{\left(\tilde{\delta}_{\rm sig}^{i}\right)^{4}}\sum_{j}\left(\frac{\partial\tilde{\delta}_{\rm sig}^{i}}{\partial j}\sigma_{j}\right)^{2}.

(S30)

Here,
\displaystyle j
represents various calibrated parameters with
\displaystyle\sigma_{j}
as their respective uncertainties. The first term in Eq. (
S30
) signifies the measurement error, assuming a standard deviation of
\displaystyle 1
for
\displaystyle\delta^{i}
.
Substituting Eq. (
S30
) into Eq. (
S29
) simplifies the probability function to:

\mathrm{Pr}\left(\delta^{i}\mid\epsilon,m_{X}\right)\propto\prod_{i}\exp\left(\frac{-\left(\delta^{i}-\delta_{\rm sig}^{i}\right)^{2}}{2+2(\delta^{i})^{2}\sum_{j}F_{j}^{2}}\right),

(S31)

as utilized in the main text. The fractional uncertainty is defined as:

F_{j}\equiv\frac{\partial\tilde{\delta}_{\rm sig}^{i}}{\partial j}\frac{\sigma_{j}}{\tilde{\delta}_{\rm sig}^{i}}.

(S32)

The relevant contributions include
\displaystyle j=G_{\rm net}
,
\displaystyle\beta
,
\displaystyle V
, and
\displaystyle C_{P}
:

\sum_{j}F_{j}^{2}=\frac{\sigma_{V}^{2}}{V^{2}}+\frac{\sigma_{C_{P}}^{2}}{C_{P}^{2}}+\left(\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{1}{1+\beta}\right)^{2}\sigma_{\beta}^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{G_{\text{net}}}^{2}}{G_{\text{net}}^{2}}

(S33)

with mean values and uncertainties presented in Table.
S2
. Note that
\displaystyle G_{\rm net}
is evaluated as
\displaystyle(57.30\pm 0.14)
dB
\displaystyle\rightarrow 10^{(5.730\pm 0.014)}
.

Using the probability function (
S31
), we can determine the 90% exclusion limit for the kinetic mixing parameter, denoted as
\displaystyle\epsilon_{90\%}
, by solving:

\frac{\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{90\%}^{2}}\mathrm{Pr}\left(\delta^{i}\mid\epsilon,m_{X}\right)\mathrm{d}\epsilon^{2}}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Pr}\left(\delta^{i}\mid\epsilon,m_{X}\right)\mathrm{d}\epsilon^{2}}=90\%.

(S34)

In addition to the detailed analysis provided, we also estimate the sensitivity using the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) to demonstrate our detection capability. We employ the Dicke radiometer equation
Dicke 1946
for the normalized power excess:

{\rm SNR}^{2}=\sum_{i}{\rm SNR}^{2}(t^{i})=\frac{t_{\rm int}\Delta\omega_{0}}{2\pi}\sum_{i}(\delta_{\rm sig}^{i})^{2}.

(S35)

We introduce a modulation factor,
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{C_{P}}
, to assess the power excess over the average:

\mathcal{F}_{C_{P}}=\frac{\sqrt{\sum_{i}[S_{\mathrm{sig}}^{P}(\omega_{0},t^{i})-\bar{S}_{\mathrm{sig}}^{P}(\omega_{0})]^{2}/N_{\rm bin}}}{\bar{S}_{\mathrm{sig}}^{P}(\omega_{0})}.

(S36)

Here,
\displaystyle\bar{S}_{\mathrm{sig}}^{P}(\omega_{0})
represent the mean signal values, accounting for the angular distribution of the dark photon source and Earth’s rotation.
In our experiment, the cavity’s
\displaystyle z
-axis at time
\displaystyle t_{i}
, denoted as
\displaystyle\hat{\bm{l}}_{\rm cav}^{i}
, can deviate from the vertical direction by up to
\displaystyle 3^{\circ}
, based on engineering resolution. To assess the impact of this deviation on the form factor
\displaystyle C_{P}
, we varied
\displaystyle\hat{\bm{l}}_{\rm cav}^{i}
within a
\displaystyle 3^{\circ}
cone. This resulted in a maximum deviation of the modulation factor
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{C_{P}}
of
\displaystyle 0.88\%
for the longitudinal mode and
\displaystyle 0.59\%
for the transverse mode. Combined with the volume uncertainty of approximately
\displaystyle 0.5\%
, the fractional uncertainty of
\displaystyle C_{P}(\hat{\bm{k}})
is conservatively estimated as
\displaystyle\sqrt{(0.5\%)^{2}+(0.88\%)^{2}}\approx 1.0\%
.

Notably, the average daily overlap factor,
\displaystyle\bar{C}_{P}
, is
\displaystyle 0.135
(
\displaystyle 0.066
) for the transverse (longitudinal) mode of dark photon flux from dark matter decay. From these values, the SNR for
\displaystyle N_{\rm bin}
independent tests distributed evenly over the experimental period is given by:

{\rm SNR}^{2}=\frac{\pi^{3}\epsilon^{4}m_{X}^{8}\rho_{X}^{2}Q_{X}^{2}V^{2}|\bm{e}_{P}|^{4}{N_{\rm bin}}t_{\rm int}\bar{C}_{P}^{2}\mathcal{F}_{C_{P}}^{2}}{8\omega_{0}^{6}T_{N}^{2}\Delta\omega_{0}}.

(S37)

The effective noise temperature,
\displaystyle T_{N}

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}=T_{N}\Delta\omega_{0}/(2\pi)

\displaystyle\eta_{\rm bin}

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle 1

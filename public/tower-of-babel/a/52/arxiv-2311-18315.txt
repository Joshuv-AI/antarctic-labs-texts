Cylindrical Symplectic Representation and Global Regular Solution of Incompressible Navier-Stokes Equations in ℝ 3 {\mathbb{R}}^{3}
Yongqian Han

Abstract
. The existence and uniqueness of global regular solution of incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in ℝ 3 {\mathbb{R}}^{3} are derived provided the initial velocity vector field holds a special structure.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
35Q30, 76D05, 76F02, 37L20

1.
Introduction

The Navier-Stokes equations in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with initial data are given by

(1.1)

u_{t}-\nu\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla P=0,

(1.2)

\nabla\cdot u=0,

(1.3)

u|_{t=0}=u_{0},

where
u=u(t,x)=\big(u^{1}(t,x),u^{2}(t,x),u^{3}(t,x)\big)
and
P=P(t,x)

stand for the unknown velocity vector field of fluid and its
pressure,
u_{0}=u_{0}(x)=\big(u^{1}_{0}(x),u^{2}_{0}(x),u^{3}_{0}(x)\big)
is
the given initial velocity vector field satisfying
\nabla\cdot u_{0}=0
,

\nu>0
is the coefficient of viscosity. Here
\partial_{x_{j}}
denotes
by
\partial_{j}
(
j=1,2,3
).

For the mathematical setting of this problem, we introduce
Hilbert space

H({\mathbb{R}}^{3})=\big\{u\in\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\big)^{3}\big|\nabla\cdot u=0\big\}

endowed with
\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\big)^{3}
norm (resp. scalar product
denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)
). For simplicity of presentation, space

\big(H^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\big)^{3}
denotes by
H^{m}
, where
m\geq 0
. In what
follows we use the usual convention and sum over the repeated indices.

The Fourier transformation of
u(t,x)
with respect to
x
denotes by

\hat{u}(t,\xi)
. Then equation (
1.2
) can be rewritten as follows

(1.4)

\xi_{j}\hat{u}^{j}=\xi_{1}\hat{u}^{1}+\xi_{2}\hat{u}^{2}+\xi_{3}\hat{u}^{3}=0.

It is that
\hat{u}(t,\xi)
is perpendicular to
\xi
. Denote by
\xi\bot\hat{u}
. Equivalently
\hat{u}(t,\xi)\in T_{\xi}{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
.

Let
A=(a,b,c)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}-\{0\}
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
. The vector

\{A\times\xi\}\in T_{\xi}{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
is called the 1st order
incompressible symplectic symmetry. Take
3\times 3
matrix
M=\big(m^{ij}\big)
. The vector
\{\xi\times(M\xi)\}\in T_{\xi}{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
is called the 2nd order incompressible symplectic symmetry.
Generally let
T=\big(T^{i_{1}\cdots i_{n}}\big)
be
n
th order tensor
and
T(\xi\cdots\xi)=T^{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{n}}\xi_{i_{2}}\cdots\xi_{i_{n}}
.
The vector
\{\xi\times T(\xi\cdots\xi)\}\in T_{\xi}{\mathbb{S}}^{2}

is called the
n
th order incompressible symplectic symmetry.

Let vectors
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}-\{0\}
and
B\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}-\{0\}
be linear
independent. Then
A\times\xi
and
B\times\xi
are linear independent
for any
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}-\{0\}
, and form the basis of space
T_{\xi}{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
which is so-called moving frame. There exist
\hat{\phi}(\xi)
and
\hat{\psi}(\xi)
such that

\hat{u}(\xi)=\hat{\phi}(\xi)\cdot\{A\times\xi\}+\hat{\psi}(\xi)\cdot\{B\times\xi\},\;\;\forall\hat{u}(\xi)\in T_{\xi}{\mathbb{S}}^{2}.

For any velocity vector
u
satisfying the equation(
1.2
), there
exist real scalar functions
\phi
and
\psi
such that

(1.5)

\begin{split}u(t,x)=&(A\times\nabla)\phi(t,x)+\{(A\times\nabla)\times\nabla\}\psi(t,x),\\
\end{split}

where vector
A=(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}-\{0\}
. The formulation
(
1.5
) is called (1,2)-symplectic representation of the
velocity vector
u
.

Let

(1.6)

\begin{split}\omega(t,x)=&\nabla\times u(t,x)\\
=&-\{(A\times\nabla)\times\nabla\}\phi(t,x)+(A\times\nabla)\Delta\psi(t,x).\\
\end{split}

Taking curl with equation (
1.1
), we drive

(1.7)

\omega_{t}-\nu\Delta\omega+(u\cdot\nabla)\omega-(\omega\cdot\nabla)u=0.

Thanks the following observations

(1.8)

\begin{split}(A\times\nabla)\cdot u(t,x)&=(A\times\nabla)\cdot(A\times\nabla)\phi(t,x)\\
&=\big\{(A\cdot A)\Delta-(A\cdot\nabla)^{2}\big\}\phi(t,x),\\
\end{split}

(1.9)

\begin{split}(A\times\nabla)\cdot\omega(t,x)&=(A\times\nabla)\cdot(A\times\nabla)\Delta\psi(t,x)\\
&=\big\{(A\cdot A)\Delta-(A\cdot\nabla)^{2}\big\}\Delta\psi(t,x),\\
\end{split}

taking scalar product of equation (
1.1
) with
A\times\nabla
,
we obtain

(1.10)

\big\{(A\cdot A)\Delta-(A\cdot\nabla)^{2}\big\}\{\phi_{t}-\nu\Delta\phi\}+(A\times\nabla)\cdot\{(u\cdot\nabla)u\}=0.

And taking scalar product of equation (
1.7
) with

A\times\nabla
, we get

(1.11)

\big\{(A\cdot A)\Delta-(A\cdot\nabla)^{2}\big\}\Delta\{\psi_{t}-\nu\Delta\psi\}+(A\times\nabla)\cdot\{(u\cdot\nabla)\omega-(\omega\cdot\nabla)u\}=0.

In this paper, we study the global regular solutions of the problem

Now we assume that
\phi
and
\psi
are cylindrical symmetric
functions with respect to space variable
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. It is that

\phi(t,x)=\phi(t,r,x_{3})
,
\psi(t,x)=\psi(t,r,x_{3})
and
r^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}
. Letting
A=e_{3}=(0,0,1)
and
\phi=0
, inserting
(\phi,\psi)=\big(0,\psi(t,r,x_{3})\big)
into equations (
1.10
)
(
1.11
), by long long straightforward calculation,
we derive

(1.13)

\begin{split}\partial_{r}\Delta(\psi_{t}-\nu\Delta\psi)-\Delta_{r}\psi\cdot\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\Delta\psi+r\partial_{r}(\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\Delta\psi)\cdot\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\psi=0,\end{split}

where
\Delta_{r}=\partial^{2}_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}=r\partial_{r}(\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r})+\frac{2}{r}\partial_{r}
,
\Delta=\Delta_{r}+\partial_{3}^{2}
.

Suppose that the initial data
u_{0}
holds symplectic representation
as follows

(1.14)

u_{0}(x)=\{(A\times\nabla)\times\nabla\}\psi_{0}(r,x_{3}),

and
\psi
satisfies the following initial condition

(1.15)

\psi|_{t=0}=\psi_{0}(r,x_{3}).

Let

u(t,x)=\{(A\times\nabla)\times\nabla\}\psi(t,r,x_{3}).

Then the problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) is equivalent to the equation
(
1.13
) with initial data (
1.15
).

Firstly we assume that fluid flows in ring cylinder domain

(1.16)

{\mathbb{D}}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})|0<r_{0}<r<R_{0}<\infty,x_{3}\in{\mathbb{R}}\},

where
r_{0}
and
R_{0}
are positive constants. Applying equation
(
1.13
), we construct the global solution of problem
(
1.1
)–(
1.3
) in
{\mathbb{D}}
which satisfies a priori
estimate

(1.17)

\begin{split}&\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{D}})}\leq C,\;\;\;\;\;\;\forall t\geq 0.\\
\end{split}

Here the constant
C
is independent of
r_{0}
and
R_{0}
.

Finally let
r_{0}\rightarrow 0
and
R_{0}\rightarrow\infty
, we derive
the main result.

Theorem 1.3

(Global Solution)
.

Provided that
m\geq 1
,
u_{0}=\big(\partial_{1}\partial_{3}\psi_{0},\partial_{2}\partial_{3}\psi_{0},-(\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2})\psi_{0}\big)
,

\psi_{0}=\psi_{0}(r,x_{3})
satisfies

(1.18)

\begin{split}&\sum_{j\geq 0,\;2j\leq m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\{(\Delta_{r}\Delta^{j}\psi_{0})^{2}+(\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\Delta^{j}\psi_{0})^{2}\}rdrdx_{3}\\
&+\sum_{k\geq 0,\;2k+1\leq m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{\infty}_{0}(\partial_{r}\Delta\Delta^{k}\psi_{0})^{2}rdrdx_{3}\\
&+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{\infty}_{0}(\partial_{r}\Delta\psi_{0})^{2}r^{-1}drdx_{3}<\infty.\\
\end{split}

Then there exists a unique global solution
u
of the problem
(
1.1
)–(
1.3
) such that
u=\big(\partial_{1}\partial_{3}\psi,\partial_{2}\partial_{3}\psi,-(\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2})\psi\big)
,
\psi=\psi(t,r,x_{3})
and

(1.19)

\begin{split}&u\in C([0,T];H^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap H({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))\cap L^{2}([0,T];H^{m+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})),\;\;\;\;\;\;\forall T\geq 0.\\
\end{split}

2.
Fluid in Ring Cylinder Domain

In this section, assume that fluid flows in ring cylinder domain

{\mathbb{D}}
.

We consider the equation (
1.13
) with initial data
(
1.15
) and boundary condition

(2.1)

\begin{split}&\partial_{r}\psi|_{r=r_{0}}=\partial_{r}\psi|_{r=R_{0}}=0,\\
&\partial_{r}\Delta\psi|_{r=r_{0}}=\partial_{r}\Delta\psi|_{r=R_{0}}=0.\\
\end{split}

The equation (
1.13
) can be rewritten as

(2.2)

\begin{split}&\Delta(\psi_{t}-\nu\Delta\psi)-\int^{r}_{r_{0}}\Delta_{s}\psi\cdot\partial_{s}\partial_{3}\Delta\psi(t,s,x_{3})ds\\
&+\int^{r}_{r_{0}}s\partial_{s}(\frac{1}{s}\partial_{s}\Delta\psi)\cdot\partial_{s}\partial_{3}\psi(t,s,x_{3})ds=0.\end{split}

Moreover the problem (
2.2
) (
2.1
) is
equivalent to

(2.3)

\begin{split}&w_{t}-\nu\Delta w-\int^{r}_{r_{0}}\Delta_{s}\psi\cdot\partial_{s}\partial_{3}\Delta\psi(t,s,x_{3})ds\\
&+\int^{r}_{r_{0}}s\partial_{s}(\frac{1}{s}\partial_{s}\Delta\psi)\cdot\partial_{s}\partial_{3}\psi(t,s,x_{3})ds=0,\\
&\Delta\psi=w,\\
&\frac{\partial}{\partial n}\psi|_{\partial{\mathbb{D}}}=0,\;\;\frac{\partial}{\partial n}w|_{\partial{\mathbb{D}}}=0,\\
\end{split}

where
n
is the outer normal of
\partial{\mathbb{D}}
. Therefore the
existence of local solution of problem (
2.2
)
(
1.15
) (
2.1
) can be established by standard method.

To prove existence of global solution of problem (
1.13
)
(
1.15
) (
2.1
), it is sufficient to give some a priori
estimates.

Lemma 2.1
.

For the solutions of problem
(
1.13
) (
1.15
) (
2.1
), we have

(2.4)

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\{(\Delta_{r}\psi)^{2}+(\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\psi)^{2}\}rdrdx_{3}+2\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}(\partial_{r}\Delta\psi)^{2}rdrdx_{3}=0,\\
\end{split}

where
\Delta_{r}=\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}
.

Proof.

Taking inner product of equation (
1.13
)
with
r\partial_{r}\psi
, we have

(2.5)

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\partial_{r}\Delta(\psi_{t}-\nu\Delta\psi)\partial_{r}\psi rdrdx_{3}\\
&-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\Delta_{r}\psi\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\Delta\psi\partial_{r}\psi rdrdx_{3}\\
&+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}r^{2}\partial_{r}(\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\Delta\psi)\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\psi\partial_{r}\psi drdx_{3}=0.\end{split}

By part integration, we derive

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\partial_{r}\Delta\psi_{t}\partial_{r}\psi rdrdx_{3}\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\{(\Delta_{r}\psi)^{2}+(\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\psi)^{2}\}rdrdx_{3},\\
\end{split}

\begin{split}&-\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\partial_{r}\Delta^{2}\psi\partial_{r}\psi rdrdx_{3}\\
=&-\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}(\partial_{r}\Delta_{r}\psi)^{2}rdrdx_{3}\\
&-2\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}(\Delta_{r}\partial_{3}\psi)^{2}rdrdx_{3}\\
&-\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}(\partial_{r}\partial_{3}^{2}\psi)^{2}rdrdx_{3}\\
=&-\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}(\partial_{r}\Delta\psi)^{2}rdrdx_{3},\\
\end{split}

\begin{split}&-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\Delta_{r}\psi\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\Delta\psi\partial_{r}\psi rdrdx_{3}\\
&+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}r^{2}\partial_{r}(\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\Delta\psi)\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\psi\partial_{r}\psi drdx_{3}\\
=&0.\end{split}

Therefore (
2.4
) is proved.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For the solutions of problem
(
1.13
) (
1.15
) (
2.1
), we have

(2.6)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}(\partial_{r}\Delta\psi)^{2}r^{-1}drdx_{3}\\
&+\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\{(\partial^{2}_{r}-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r})\Delta\psi\}^{2}r^{-1}drdx_{3}\\
&+\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}(\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\Delta\psi)^{2}r^{-1}drdx_{3}=0.\\
\end{split}

Proof.

Taking inner product of equation (
1.13
)
with
r^{-1}\partial_{r}\Delta\psi
, we have

(2.7)

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\partial_{r}\Delta(\psi_{t}-\nu\Delta\psi)\partial_{r}\Delta\psi r^{-1}drdx_{3}\\
&-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\Delta_{r}\psi\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\Delta\psi\partial_{r}\Delta\psi r^{-1}drdx_{3}\\
&+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\partial_{r}(\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\Delta\psi)\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\psi\partial_{r}\Delta\psi drdx_{3}=0.\end{split}

By part integration, we derive

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\partial_{r}\Delta\psi_{t}\partial_{r}\Delta\psi r^{-1}drdx_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}(\partial_{r}\Delta\psi)^{2}r^{-1}drdx_{3},\\
\end{split}

\begin{split}&-\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\partial_{r}\Delta^{2}\psi\partial_{r}\Delta\psi r^{-1}drdx_{3}\\
=&\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\{\partial^{2}_{r}\Delta\psi\}^{2}r^{-1}drdx_{3}\\
&-\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\{\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\Delta\psi\}^{2}r^{-1}drdx_{3}\\
&+\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}(\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\Delta\psi)^{2}r^{-1}drdx_{3},\\
\end{split}

\begin{split}&-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\Delta_{r}\psi\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\Delta\psi\partial_{r}\Delta\psi r^{-1}drdx_{3}\\
&+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\partial_{r}(\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\Delta\psi)\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\psi\partial_{r}\Delta\psi drdx_{3}\\
=&0.\\
\end{split}

Therefore we have

(2.8)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}(\partial_{r}\Delta\psi)^{2}r^{-1}drdx_{3}\\
&+\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\{\partial^{2}_{r}\Delta\psi\}^{2}r^{-1}drdx_{3}\\
&-\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}\{\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\Delta\psi\}^{2}r^{-1}drdx_{3}\\
&+\nu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}(\partial_{r}\partial_{3}\Delta\psi)^{2}r^{-1}drdx_{3}\\
=&0.\\
\end{split}

Note that

(2.9)

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}2\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\Delta\psi\partial^{2}_{r}\Delta\psi r^{-1}drdx_{3}\\
=&2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int^{R_{0}}_{r_{0}}(\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\Delta\psi)^{2}r^{-1}drdx_{3}.\\
\end{split}

By (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), (
2.6
) is proved.
∎

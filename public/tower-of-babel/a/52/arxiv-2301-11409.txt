Self-testing composite measurements and bound entangled state in a single quantum network
Shubhayan Sarkar, Chandan Datta, Saronath Halder, Remigiusz Augusiak

Abstract
Within the quantum networks scenario we introduce a single scheme allowing to certify three different types of composite projective measurements acting on a three-qubit Hilbert space: one constructed from genuinely entangled GHZ-like states, one constructed from fully product vectors that exhibit the phenomenon of nonlocality without entanglement (NLWE), and a hybrid measurement obtained from an unextendible product basis (UPB). Noticeably, we certify a basis exhibiting NLWE in the smallest dimension capable of supporting this phenomenon. On the other hand, the possibility of certification of a measurement obtained from a UPB has an interesting implication that one can also self-test a bound entangled state in the considered quantum network. Such a possibility does not seem to exist in the standard Bell scenario. Furthermore, we also analyse the robustness of our scheme towards experimental errors.



Introduction.—
With the advancement of new technologies such as quantum cryptography
Gisin et al. 2002
, device-independent (DI) certification of quantum devices is becoming increasingly important, allowing one to certify certain features of an underlying device in a “black-box” scenario
Acín et al. 2007
;
Bancal et al. 2011
;
Brunner et al. 2008
, which requires basically no assumptions about the device except that it is governed by quantum theory. The key ingredient for DI certification is Bell nonlocality, i.e., the existence of quantum correlations that cannot be explained by local hidden variable models
Bell 1964
;
Bell 1966
.
The most comprehensive form of DI certification is self-testing
Mayers and Yao 1998
, which allows for almost complete characterization of the underlying quantum state and the measurements performed on it. From an application standpoint, this type of certification is crucial as it allows to verify whether a quantum device works as expected without knowing its internal mechanism.

Since its introduction in
Mayers and Yao 1998
, self-testing has been investigated in various scenarios and shown to have numerous advantages
Šupić and Bowles 2020
. However, while much attention has been paid to the self-testing of bipartite and multipartite states
Mayers and Yao 2004
;
McKague et al. 2012
;
Reichardt et al. 2013
;
McKague 2014
;
Wu et al. 2014
;
Bamps and Pironio 2015
;
Wang et al. 2016
;
Šupić et al. 2016
;
Coladangelo et al. 2017
;
Kaniewski et al. 2019
;
Mančinska et al. 2021
;
Tavakoli et al. 2021
;
Sarkar et al. 2021
;
Sarkar and Augusiak 2022
;
Yang and Navascués 2013
, the problem of certifying quantum measurements, in particular those acting on composite Hilbert spaces, has been
largely unexplored. Apart from a few classes of local measurements
Tavakoli et al. 2021
;
Kaniewski et al. 2019
;
Woodhead et al. 2020
and a few composite ones
Renou et al. 2018
;
Zhou et al. 2022
;
Šupić and Brunner 2022
, no general scheme exists allowing to certify composite measurements even in the simplest case of multi-qubit Hilbert spaces.

Our aim here is to fill this gap and introduce a unified scheme in the quantum networks scenario that allows for self-testing, in a single experiment, three types of projective measurements: one composed of genuinely entangled states, one constructed from fully product states exhibiting nonlocality without entanglement (NLWE)
Bennett et al. 1999a
, and a hybrid one which is constructed from an unextendible product basis (UPB)
Bennett et al. 1999b
and a projector onto the completely entangled subspace orthogonal to the UPB. Notice that UPBs are interesting mathematical objects that have found numerous applications, for instance, in constructing bound entangled states
Bennett et al. 1999b
or Bell inequalities with no quantum violation
Augusiak et al. 2011
.

While a network-based self-testing scheme of a two-qutrit product basis exhibiting NLWE was previously introduced in
Šupić and Brunner 2022
, our approach enables one to self-test such a basis in the smallest possible dimension capable of supporting the notion of NLWE, which is eight. Furthermore, our scheme utilizes fewer measurements and is thus more efficient as compared to
Šupić and Brunner 2022
. Finally, it also allows to simultaneously self-test a measurement of a hybrid type, which is constructed from a UPB. An interesting implication of this fact is that one can
also (indirectly) certify in the network, a mixed bound entangled state
constructed from the UPB. While a network-based certification scheme for pure states
has already been introduced in Ref.
Šupić et al. 2022
, our work seems to be the first to address the question of certification of mixed entangled states (see nevertheless Refs.
Baccari et al. 2020a
;
Frérot and Acín 2021
).

Composite measurements.—
From each of the considered sets of vectors one can construct a projective measurement acting on
\mathcal{H}_{3}=(\mathbbm{C}^{2})^{\otimes 3}
. First, the product basis
|\delta_{i}\rangle

gives rise to a separable eight-outcome measurement
M_{\mathrm{NLWE}}=\{|\delta_{i}\rangle\!\langle\delta_{i}|\}_{i=0}^{7}
. This measurement cannot be implemented in terms of the
LOCC, and, simultaneously, cannot produce entanglement if applied to a state. On the other extreme, we have the eight-outcome measurement

M_{\mathrm{GHZ}}=\{|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|\}_{l=0}^{7}

constructed from the GHZ-like states which are all entangled. Thus, unlike
M_{\mathrm{NLWE}}
, this measurement leads to entangled states for all outcomes
l
.

Let us finally move to the UPB in Eq. (
3
). It allows constructing a five-outcome hybrid measurement
M_{\mathrm{UPB}}=\{|\tau_{i}\rangle\!\langle\tau_{i}|\}_{i=0}^{3}\cup\{\Gamma\}
that lies in between the GHZ measurement and the separable measurement. In fact, four of its outcomes correspond to projections onto fully product states

|\tau_{i}\rangle
, whereas the last outcome is represented by
\Gamma
that projects onto a completely (but not genuinely) entangled four-dimensional subspace.

Setting the scenario.—
We consider a quantum network scenario consisting of three external parties Alice, Bob and Charlie and a central party Eve (see Fig.
2
). The scenario also comprises of three independent sources
P_{i}
that distribute bipartite quantum states among the parties. We denote these states by
\rho_{s\overline{s}}
with

,

,

\overline{B}

,
B_{y}
,
C_{z}
and
E_{e}
, where the measurement choices are labelled

. We assume that each of the external party’s measurements has two outcomes, denoted
a,b,c=0,1
. The first two Eve’s measurements yield eight outputs, whereas the third one results in five outcomes. During the experiment, the parties cannot communicate classically.

The correlations obtained by repeatedly performing these measurements are captured by a set of probability distributions
\vec{p}=\{p(abcl|xyze)\}
, where each
p(abcl|xyze)
is the probability of observing outcomes
a
,
b
,
c
and
l
by Alice, Bob, Charlie and Eve after performing measurements labeled by

,
y
,
z
, and
e
, respectively; it is given by the well-known formula

p(abcl|xyze)=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{ABCE}N^{A}_{a|x}\otimes N^{B}_{b|y}\otimes N^{C}_{c|z}\otimes N^{E}_{l|e}\right],

(5)

where

,

etc. are the
measurement elements representing the measurements of the observers; these are positive semi-definite and satisfy

It will be beneficial to use another representation of the observed correlations, that is, in terms of the expectation values of observables of the external parties, which are defined as

\langle A_{x}B_{y}C_{z}N^{E}_{l|e}\rangle=\sum_{a,b,c=0,1}(-1)^{a+b+c}p(abcl|xyze).

A_{x}
,
B_{y}
and

are quantum operators that are defined through the measurement elements as

s=A,B,C
and

. In the particular case of projective measurements these operators

become unitary and thus represent the standard quantum observables.

Self-testing.—

The quantum networks scenario has recently been harnessed to propose self-testing schemes for few quantum measurements defined in composite Hilbert spaces such as the measurement corresponding to the two-qubit Bell basis composed of four maximally entangled vectors
Renou et al. 2018
, or the nine-outcome projective measurement corresponding to a complete basis in
\mathbbm{C}^{3}\otimes\mathbbm{C}^{3}
that exhibits NLWE
Šupić and Brunner 2022
. Our aim here is to employ these ideas to design a general framework for quantum networks-based device-independent (NDI) certification of various interesting types of quantum measurements, concentrating on the particular case of three-qubit Hilbert spaces.

Outlook.—
Inspired by the notion of NLWE, we can identify a larger set of orthogonal projectors which can be referred to as nonlocality without distillable entanglement (NLWDE). NLWDE is defined as a set of projectors that cannot be perfectly distinguished using local operations and classical communication, such that their normalized versions are positive under partial transpose with respect to every bipartition. In this work, the measurement composed of UPB and bound entanglement falls under this category. It will be interesting to further analyze the properties of such sets.

Several other follow-up problems arise from our work. Designing certification schemes for GHZ bases and product bases exhibiting NLWE for
N
qubits will be straightforward from our work. A more interesting question would be to generalize our scheme to certify any hybrid or even any composite projective measurement in the case of any number of qubits. An even more challenging problem will be to propose a quantum-networks-based scheme for non-projective composite measurements. Our work also establishes a way to self-test mixed entangled states. A natural question here would be to construct schemes to self-test any mixed entangled state; a possibility that does not seem to exist in the standard Bell scenario. This has now been achieved in
Sarkar et al. 2024
.

Appendix A
General results

Before proceeding to the proofs of the main results, we introduce an important lemma that is required to derive our results.

Lemma 1
.

Consider a positive semi-definite matrix
M
such that
M\leq\mathbbm{1}
. Also, consider a density matrix
\rho
that satisfies
\mathrm{Tr}(M\rho)=1
. Then,
M
is an identity matrix acting on the support of
\rho
.

Proof.

Let us consider the eigendecomposition of
\rho
,
\rho=\sum_{k}p_{k}|\psi_{k}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{k}|
such that
p_{k}>0
. After putting it into the condition
\mathrm{Tr}(M\rho)=1
, one obtains

\displaystyle\sum_{k}p_{k}\langle\psi_{k}|M|\psi_{k}\rangle=1,

(19)

which by employing the fact that
\sum_{k}p_{k}=1
can further be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{k}p_{k}(1-\langle\psi_{k}|M|\psi_{k}\rangle)=0.

(20)

Due to the facts that
0\leq M\leq\mathbbm{1}
and
p_{k}>0
, the above equation can hold true only if
\langle\psi_{k}|M|\psi_{k}\rangle=1
. Thus, every
|\psi_{k}\rangle
is an eigenstate of
M
with eigenvalue
1
. As a result,
M=\sum_{k}|\psi_{k}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{k}|=\mathbbm{1}_{\rho}
,
where
\mathbbm{1}_{\rho}
is an identity acting on the support of
\rho
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Consider two unitary observables
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
such that
A_{i}^{2}=\mathbbm{1}
acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Consider then projections
\Pi_{j}
onto
k
subspaces
\mathcal{K}_{j}
of
\mathcal{H}
such that
\Pi_{1}+\dots+\Pi_{k}>0
. Let us denote
\overline{A}_{i}^{(j)}=\Pi_{j}A_{i}\Pi_{j}
and assume that

\overline{A}_{0}^{(j)}\overline{A}_{1}^{(j)}+\overline{A}_{1}^{(j)}\overline{A}_{0}^{(j)}=0\qquad(j=1,\ldots,k)

(21)

and that
\overline{A}_{i}^{(j)}
are unitary on the subspaces
\mathcal{K}_{j}
.
Then, the matrices
A_{i}
anticommute,

\{A_{0},A_{1}\}=0.

(22)

Proof.

Let us first show that
A_{i}=\overline{A}_{i}^{(j)}\oplus\widetilde{E}_{i,j}
, where
\widetilde{E}_{i,j}
with
i=0,1
are matrices acting on the complementary subspace to
\mathcal{K}_{j}
. For this purpose, let us fix
j=1
and express the observable
A_{0}
as

A_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}\overline{A}_{0}^{(1)}&B\\
C&\widetilde{E}_{0,1}\end{pmatrix},

(23)

where
\widetilde{E}_{0,1}
,
B
and
C
are some matrices; in particular,

\widetilde{E}_{0,1}
acts on
\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\perp}
.

Now, exploiting the conditions that
A_{1}A_{1}^{\dagger}=\mathbbm{1}
and
\overline{A}_{0}^{(1)}
is unitary we can conclude that
B=0
. Similarly, using
A_{0}^{\dagger}A_{0}=\mathbbm{1}
one obtains that
C=0
, which gives us the desired form of
A_{0}
. Similar analyses with any

gives us
A_{i}=\overline{A}_{i}^{(j)}\oplus\tilde{E}_{i,j}
.

The facts that
A_{i}
decompose into blocks with respect to the
decompositions
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{K}_{j}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{j}^{\perp}
and that with respect to the same decompositions,
\Pi_{j}
can be expressed as

\Pi_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{K_{j}}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},

(24)

where
\mathbbm{1}_{K_{j}}
is the identity on
\mathcal{K}_{j}
, we can
rewrite the conditions (
21
) as

\{\overline{A}_{0}^{(j)},\overline{A}_{1}^{(j)}\}=\Pi_{j}\{A_{0},A_{1}\}=0.

(25)

After summing (
25

(\Pi_{1}+\ldots+\Pi_{k})\{A_{0},A_{1}\}=0,

(26)

which by using the fact that
\Pi_{1}+\ldots+\Pi_{k}
is a full rank positive semi-definite matrix, implies that

and

anticommute on
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

\displaystyle(-1)^{l_{1}+l_{2}}Z_{A^{\prime}}\otimes Z_{B^{\prime}}|\tilde{\psi}_{l}\rangle=|\tilde{\psi}_{l}\rangle,

(58b)

\displaystyle(-1)^{l_{1}+l_{3}}Z_{A^{\prime}}\otimes Z_{C^{\prime}}|\tilde{\psi}_{l}\rangle=|\tilde{\psi}_{l}\rangle,

(58c)

where
|\tilde{\psi}_{l}\rangle=U_{A}\otimes U_{B}\otimes U_{C}|\psi_{l}\rangle
. As already concluded above each local Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{s}

(s=A,B,C)
decomposes as
\mathcal{H}_{s}=(\mathbbm{C}^{2})_{s^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{s^{\prime\prime}}
. Thus,
|\tilde{\psi}_{l}\rangle
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle|\tilde{\psi}_{l}\rangle=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}=0,1}|i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}\rangle_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}}|\phi^{l}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\rangle_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}G},

(59)

where the normalisation factors are included in
|\phi^{l}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\rangle
. Putting the above form of
|\tilde{\psi}_{l}\rangle
in Eqs. (
58b
) and (
58c
), we obtain

\displaystyle(-1)^{l_{1}+l_{2}}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}=0,1}(-1)^{i_{1}+i_{2}}|i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}\rangle|\phi^{l}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\rangle=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}=0,1}|i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}\rangle|\phi^{l}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\rangle,

(60)

and

\displaystyle(-1)^{l_{1}+l_{3}}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}=0,1}(-1)^{i_{1}+i_{3}}|i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}\rangle|\phi^{l}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\rangle=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}=0,1}|i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}\rangle|\phi^{l}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\rangle.

(61)

For brevity, we dropped subscripts denoting the subsystems. Projecting both the above formulas on
\langle i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}|
, we obtain the following relations

\displaystyle(-1)^{l_{1}+l_{2}}(-1)^{i_{1}+i_{2}}|\phi^{l}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\rangle=|\phi^{l}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\rangle\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad(-1)^{l_{1}+l_{3}}(-1)^{i_{1}+i_{3}}|\phi^{l}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\rangle=|\phi^{l}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\rangle,

(62)

which allow us to conclude that
|\phi^{l}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\rangle=0
whenever
(l_{1}+l_{2}+i_{1}+i_{2})
mod
2=1
and
(l_{1}+l_{3}+i_{1}+i_{3})
mod
2=1
.
Thus, the state in Eq. (
59
) that satisfies the conditions in Eq. (
62
) must be of the form

\displaystyle|\tilde{\psi}_{l}\rangle=|l_{1}l_{2}l_{3}\rangle|\phi_{l_{1}l_{2}l_{3}}\rangle+|\overline{l}_{1}\overline{l}_{2}\overline{l}_{3}\rangle|\phi_{\overline{l}_{1}\overline{l}_{2}\overline{l}_{3}}\rangle

(63)

where
l_{i}=0,1
for any
i=1,2,3
and
\overline{l}_{i}=1-l_{i}
. Now, putting this state in Eq. (
58a
), we obtain the following relation

\displaystyle(-1)^{l_{1}}|\overline{l}_{1}\overline{l}_{2}\overline{l}_{3}\rangle|\phi_{l_{1}l_{2}l_{3}}\rangle+(-1)^{l_{1}}|l_{1}l_{2}l_{3}\rangle|\phi_{\overline{l}_{1}\overline{l}_{2}\overline{l}_{3}}\rangle=|l_{1}l_{2}l_{3}\rangle|\phi_{l_{1}l_{2}l_{3}}\rangle+|\overline{l}_{1}\overline{l}_{2}\overline{l}_{3}\rangle|\phi_{\overline{l}_{1}\overline{l}_{2}\overline{l}_{3}}\rangle,

(64)

which implies that

\displaystyle(-1)^{l_{1}}|\phi_{l_{1}l_{2}l_{3}}\rangle=|\phi_{\overline{l}_{1}\overline{l}_{2}\overline{l}_{3}}\rangle.

(65)

Thus, from Eq. (
63
) we conclude that the state
|\tilde{\psi}_{l}\rangle
, by putting the appropriate normalisation constant, is given by

\displaystyle|\tilde{\psi}_{l}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[|l_{1}l_{2}l_{3}\rangle+(-1)^{l_{1}}|\overline{l}_{1}\overline{l}_{2}\overline{l}_{3}\rangle\right]_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}}\otimes|\phi_{l_{1}l_{2}l_{3}}\rangle_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}E}.

(66)

Tracing out the ancillary subsystem
E
, we finally obtain

\displaystyle U_{A}\otimes U_{B}\otimes U_{C}\,\rho^{l}_{ABC}\,(U_{A}\otimes U_{B}\otimes U_{C})^{\dagger}=|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}},

(67)

where
\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}
denotes the auxiliary state acting on
\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C^{\prime\prime}}
. Putting the above relation back into Eq. (
38
) and taking the unitaries to the right-hand side, we see that

\displaystyle|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}=8\ \mathrm{Tr}_{\overline{ABC}}\left[\left(\mathbbm{1}_{ABC}\otimes R_{l}\right)\bigotimes_{s=A,B,C}|\tilde{\psi}_{s\overline{s}}\rangle\!\langle\tilde{\psi}_{s\overline{s}}|\right],

(68)

where we denoted
|\tilde{\psi}_{s\overline{s}}\rangle=U_{s}|\psi_{s\overline{s}}\rangle
and used the fact that
\overline{P}(l)=1/8
.

(b) States
|\psi_{s\overline{s}}\rangle

generated by the sources.

Let us now exploit the relations in Eq. (
68
) to determine the form of the the states
|\tilde{\psi}_{s\overline{s}}\rangle

(s=A,B,C)
. To this end, we first express them using their Schmidt decompositions,

\displaystyle|\tilde{\psi}_{s\overline{s}}\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{d_{s}-1}\alpha_{i}^{s}|e_{i}\rangle_{s}|f_{i}\rangle_{\overline{s}},

(69)

where
d_{s}
denotes the dimension of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{s}
and
\{|e_{i}\rangle_{s}\}
and
\{|f_{i}\rangle_{\overline{s}}\}
are some local bases corresponding to the subsystems
s
and
\overline{s}
, respectively; recall that, as proven above,
d_{s}
is even for any
s
as the Hilbert spaces of the external parties decompose as
\mathcal{H}_{s}=\mathbbm{C}^{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{s^{\prime\prime}}
. Moreover, the Schmidt coefficients satisfy
\alpha_{i}^{s}>0
and
\sum_{i}(\alpha_{i}^{s})^{2}=1
.

Let us now consider a unitary operation
V_{\overline{s}}:\mathcal{H}_{\overline{s}}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\overline{s}}
such that
V_{\overline{s}}|f_{i}\rangle_{\overline{s}}=|e_{i}^{*}\rangle_{\overline{s}}
for any
i
, where the asterisk stands for the complex conjugation. By using this unitary we can bring Eq. (
69
) to the following form

\displaystyle|\overline{\psi}_{s\overline{s}}\rangle=\mathbbm{1}_{s}\otimes V_{\overline{s}}|\tilde{\psi}_{s\overline{s}}\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{d_{s}-1}\alpha^{s}_{i}|e_{i}\rangle_{s}|e_{i}^{*}\rangle_{\overline{s}}.

(70)

Introducing then the following matrix

\displaystyle P_{\overline{s}}=\sum_{i=0}^{d_{s}-1}\alpha_{i}^{s}|e_{i}^{*}\rangle\!\langle e_{i}^{*}|_{\overline{s}},

(71)

we can rewrite the state in Eq. (
70
) as

\displaystyle|\overline{\psi}_{s\overline{s}}\rangle=\sqrt{d_{s}}\ (\mathbbm{1}_{s}\otimes P_{\overline{s}})\,|\phi^{+}_{d_{s}}\rangle_{s\overline{s}}\qquad(s=A,B,C),

(72)

where
|\phi^{+}_{d_{s}}\rangle_{s\overline{s}}
denotes the maximally entangled state of local dimension
d_{s}
, that is,

\displaystyle|\phi^{+}_{d_{s}}\rangle_{s\overline{s}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{s}}}\sum_{i=0}^{d_{s}-1}|e_{i}\rangle_{s}|e_{i}^{*}\rangle_{\overline{s}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{s}}}\sum_{i=0}^{d_{s}-1}|i\rangle_{s}|i\rangle_{\overline{s}},

(73)

where
|e_{i}^{*}\rangle
is the complex conjugate of
|e_{i}\rangle
and the last equality follows from the fact that the maximally entangled state is invariant under the action of
U\otimes U^{*}
for any unitary operation
U
.

Putting now Eq. (
73
) back into Eq. (
68
), we conclude that

\displaystyle|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}=8\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left[\left(\mathbbm{1}_{ABC}\otimes\overline{R}_{l}\right)\bigotimes_{s}|\phi^{+}_{d_{s}}\rangle\!\langle\phi^{+}_{d_{s}}|_{s\overline{s}}\right],

(74)

where we denoted

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{l}=d_{A}d_{B}d_{C}\left(\bigotimes_{s}P_{\overline{s}}\,V_{\overline{s}}\right)\ R_{l}\ \left(\bigotimes_{s}V_{\overline{s}}^{\dagger}P_{\overline{s}}\right).

(75)

Now, notice that the tensor product of the three maximally entangled states appearing in (
74
) can also be understood as a single maximally entangled state across the bipartition
ABC|\overline{A}\overline{B}\overline{C}
whose local dimension is
d_{A}d_{B}d_{C}
, that is,

\displaystyle|\phi^{+}_{d_{s}}\rangle_{A\overline{A}}\otimes|\phi^{+}_{d_{s}}\rangle_{B\overline{B}}\otimes|\phi^{+}_{d_{s}}\rangle_{C\overline{C}}=|\phi^{+}_{d_{A}d_{B}d_{C}}\rangle_{ABC|\overline{A}\overline{B}\overline{C}},

(76)

\displaystyle|\phi^{+}_{d_{A}d_{B}d_{C}}\rangle_{ABC|\overline{ABC}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{A}d_{B}d_{C}}}\sum_{i=0}^{d_{A}d_{B}d_{C}-1}|i\rangle_{ABC}|i\rangle_{\overline{ABC}}.

(77)

Substituting Eq. (
76
) in Eq. (
74
) and using the well known fact that
\mathbbm{1}_{A}\otimes Q_{B}|\phi^{+}_{D}\rangle_{A|B}=Q^{T}_{A}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B}|\phi^{+}_{D}\rangle_{A|B}
for any matrix
Q
, we can rewrite (
74
) as

\displaystyle|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}=8\mathrm{Tr}_{\overline{ABC}}\left[\left(\overline{R}^{T}_{l}\right)_{ABC}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\overline{ABC}}|\phi^{+}_{d_{A}d_{B}d_{C}}\rangle\!\langle\phi^{+}_{d_{A}d_{B}d_{C}}|_{ABC|\overline{ABC}}\right],

(78)

which implies that

\displaystyle|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{8}{d_{A}d_{B}d_{C}}\overline{R}^{T}_{l}.

(79)

Now, applying the transposition map to both sides and then using Eq. (
75
), we arrive at

\displaystyle|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}}\otimes\left(\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}\right)^{T}=8\left(\bigotimes_{s}P_{s}V_{\overline{s}}\right)\ R_{l}\ \left(\bigotimes_{s}V_{\overline{s}}^{\dagger}P_{s}\right),

(80)

which after summing over
l
and
using the fact that
\sum_{l=0}^{7}R_{l}=\mathbbm{1}
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{7}|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}}\otimes\left(\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}\right)^{T}=8\,P_{A}^{2}\otimes P_{B}^{2}\otimes P_{C}^{2}.

(81)

We can now employ the fact that
\mathrm{Tr}(P_{B}^{2})=\mathrm{Tr}(P^{2}_{C})=1
to
take a partial trace of the above expression over the subsystems
BC
and obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime}}}{2}\otimes\sigma_{A^{\prime\prime}}=P_{A}^{2},

(82)

which directly implies that

\displaystyle P_{A}=\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{A^{\prime\prime}}}{2}},

(83)

where

\displaystyle\sigma_{A^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{l=0}^{7}\mathrm{Tr}_{B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}\left[\left(\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}\right)^{T}\right].

(84)

Analogously we can determine the other matrices
P_{s}
with
s=B,C
. Precisely, by taking partial traces of Eq. (
81
)
over all subsystems except the
s
th one, one obtains

\displaystyle P_{s}=\mathbbm{1}_{s^{\prime}}\otimes\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{s^{\prime\prime}}}{2}}\qquad(s=A,B,C)

(85)

with

\displaystyle\sigma_{s^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{l=0}^{7}\mathrm{Tr}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}\setminus\{s^{\prime\prime}\}}\left[\left(\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}\right)^{T}\right],

(86)

where
\mathrm{Tr}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}\setminus\{s^{\prime\prime}\}}
represents the partial trace over the systems
A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}
except the one labelled by
s^{\prime\prime}
; For instance
\mathrm{Tr}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}\setminus\{A^{\prime\prime}\}}\equiv\mathrm{Tr}_{B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}
.

Now, we can substitute
P_{s}
given by Eq. (
85
) into Eq. (
72
) and also use the fact that
d_{s}=2d_{s}^{\prime\prime}
for some positive integer
d_{s}^{\prime\prime}
to obtain

\displaystyle|\overline{\psi}_{s\overline{s}}\rangle=\sqrt{d_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\ \mathbbm{1}_{s^{\prime}\overline{s}^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{s^{\prime\prime}}\otimes\sqrt{\sigma_{\overline{s}^{\prime\prime}}}\,|\phi^{+}_{d_{s}}\rangle_{s\overline{s}}

(87)

for any
s
. Using again the fact that
|\phi^{+}_{d_{s}}\rangle_{s\overline{s}}=|\phi^{+}\rangle_{s^{\prime}\overline{s}^{\prime}}|\phi^{+}_{d_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle_{s^{\prime\prime}\overline{s}^{\prime\prime}}
, we finally conclude that
the states generated by the sources admit the following form

\displaystyle U_{s}\otimes V_{\overline{s}}|\psi_{s\overline{s}}\rangle=|\overline{\psi}_{s\overline{s}}\rangle=|\phi^{+}\rangle_{s^{\prime}\overline{s}^{\prime}}|\xi_{s^{\prime\prime}\overline{s}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle\qquad(s=A,B,C),

(88)

where the auxiliary state

|\xi_{s^{\prime\prime}\overline{s}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle
is given by

\displaystyle|\xi_{s^{\prime\prime}\overline{s}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle=\sqrt{d_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\,\mathbbm{1}_{s^{\prime\prime}}\otimes\sqrt{\sigma_{\overline{s}^{\prime\prime}}}\,|\phi^{+}_{d_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle_{s^{\prime\prime}\overline{s}^{\prime\prime}}.

(89)

(c) Entangled measurement
E_{0}
.
Let us now characterise the measurement
E_{0}=\{R_{l}\}
. For this purpose, we notice
that the states
\sigma_{s^{\prime\prime}}
are invertible because we assumed
all the reduced density matrices of the joint states to be full rank.
This implies that the matrices
P_{s}
[cf. Eq. (
85
)] are invertible too and therefore we can act with

on both sides of Eq. (
80
)
to bring to the following form

\displaystyle\left(\bigotimes_{s}V_{\overline{s}}\right)\ R_{l}\ \left(\bigotimes_{s}V_{\overline{s}}^{\dagger}\right)=|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}}\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{s}\sigma^{-1/2}_{s^{\prime\prime}}\right)\tilde{\rho}^{l,T}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}\left(\bigotimes_{s}\sigma^{-1/2}_{s^{\prime\prime}}\right).

(90)

We thus conclude that

\displaystyle\left(\bigotimes_{s}V_{\overline{s}}\right)\ R_{l}\ \left(\bigotimes_{s}V_{\overline{s}}^{\dagger}\right)=|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}}\otimes\left(\tilde{R}_{l}\right)_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}},

(91)

for all

, where
\tilde{R}_{l}
is defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{l}=\left(\bigotimes_{s}\sigma^{-1/2}_{s^{\prime\prime}}\right)\left(\tilde{\rho}^{l}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}\right)^{T}\left(\bigotimes_{s}\sigma^{-1/2}_{s^{\prime\prime}}\right).

(92)

Notice that
\tilde{R}_{l}\geq 0
. Moreover, the fact that
R_{l}\leq\mathbbm{1}
implies via Eq. (
91
) that
\tilde{R}_{l}\leq\mathbbm{1}
. Taking then the sum over
l
on both sides of Eq. (
91
) and employing the fact that
\sum_{l}R_{l}=\mathbbm{1}
allows us to conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{ABC}=\sum_{l=0}^{7}|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}}\otimes\left(\tilde{R}_{l}\right)_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}.

(93)

Now, we can take advantage of the fact that
the GHZ-like states are mutually orthogonal
and therefore by projecting the
A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}

subsystems onto
|\phi_{k}\rangle
we
deduce that for every
k
,

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{k}=\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}.

(94)

After plugging the above relation into Eq. (
91
) we finally conclude that there exist local unitary transformations such that the measurement operators
R_{l}
admit the following form

\displaystyle(V_{\overline{A}}\otimes V_{\overline{B}}\otimes V_{\overline{C}})\,R_{l|0}\,(V_{\overline{A}}\otimes V_{\overline{B}}\otimes V_{\overline{C}})^{\dagger}=|\phi_{l}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{l}|_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}\qquad(l=0,\ldots,7),

(95)

which is exactly what we promised in Eq. (
36
). This completes the proof.
∎

.

.

Assume that the sources

) and the measurements of all the parties are certified as in Eqs. (

) and (

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle A^{\prime}_{2}\otimes B^{\prime}_{2}\otimes X_{C^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{C^{\prime\prime}}\rangle_{\overline{\rho}^{000}_{ABC}},

\displaystyle(X_{A^{\prime}}\otimes X_{B^{\prime}}\otimes X_{C^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}})\,\varrho_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varrho_{0},

(103)

\displaystyle(X_{A^{\prime}}\otimes B^{\prime}_{2}\otimes C^{\prime}_{2}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime\prime}})\ \varrho_{0}

\displaystyle-\varrho_{0},

(104)

\displaystyle(A^{\prime}_{2}\otimes X_{B^{\prime}}\otimes C^{\prime}_{2}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B^{\prime\prime}})\ \varrho_{0}

\displaystyle-\ \varrho_{0},

(105)

\displaystyle(A^{\prime}_{2}\otimes B^{\prime}_{2}\otimes X_{C^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{C^{\prime\prime}})\ \varrho_{0}

\displaystyle-\ \varrho_{0}.

Appendix E
Robustness

Let us now comment on the robustness of our self-testing scheme. Even if there exist a few techniques to analyze robustness of self-testing schemes, most of them are restricted to the simplest situations both in the standard Bell scenario with two-outcome measurements
McKague et al. 2012
;
Wu et al. 2014
;
Baccari et al. 2020b
;
Yang and Navascués 2013
or the network one
Renou et al. 2018
. Although interesting from a theoretical perspective,
turning a self-testing statement into one that takes into account robustness to noises is an extremely difficult challenge, in particular in the network scenario. Roughly speaking, a robust self-testing statement corresponds to a situation in which the value of a Bell functional is slightly lower than the maximal one, that is,
\mathcal{B}\geq\beta_{Q}-\varepsilon
where
\mathcal{B},\beta_{Q}
denote a Bell functional and its maximal quantum value respectively and
\varepsilon
is a very small positive number, and allows to asses how far the state and the measurements depart, in some norm, from the optimal quantum realization.

McKague et al. 2012
when Eve’s input is

. For this purpose, we first utilise the fidelity bounds of the ideal post-measurement states with the external parties to the actual ones that can be obtained as shown in
Sarkar et al. 2025
. We do not find them here, but one can straightaway employ it here
Sarkar et al. 2025
. Consequently, the robustness of the ideal post-measurement states to the actual ones can be stated as

)

\varepsilon

A q q -Dwork-type generalization of Rodriguez-Villegas’ supercongruences Thanks: The author gratefully acknowledges the support from the Natural Science Foundation of the Higher Education Institutions of Jiangsu Province (20KJB110023) and National Natural Science Foundation of China (12001279).
He-Xia Ni

Abstract
. Guo and Zudilin [Adv. Math. 346 (2019), 329–358] developed an analytical method, called ‘creative microscoping’, to prove many supercongruences by establishing their q q -analogues. In this paper, we apply this method to give a q q -Dwork-type generalization of Rodriguez-Villegas’ supercongruences, which was recently conjectured by Guo and Zudilin.

1.
Introduction

Let
p>3
be a prime and
\big(\frac{\cdot}{p}\big)
be the Legendre symbol modulo
p
. In 2003, E. Morterson
[
13
,
14
]
proved the following supercongruences involving hypergeometric functions and Calabi-Yau manifolds,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\frac{\binom{2k}{k}^{2}}{16^{k}}\equiv

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p^{2}),

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\frac{\binom{3k}{k}\binom{2k}{k}}{27^{k}}\equiv

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{3}\right)\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p^{2}),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\frac{\binom{4k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}}{64^{k}}\equiv

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-2}{p}\right)\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p^{2}),

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\frac{\binom{6k}{3k}\binom{3k}{k}}{432^{k}}\equiv

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p^{2}),

(1.4)

which were first conjectured by F. Rodriguez-Villegas
[
17
]
. In 2014, Z.-H. Sun
[
18
]
gave an elementary proof of (
1.1
)–(
1.4
)
by showing that

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\binom{-x}{k}\binom{x-1}{k}\equiv(-1)^{\langle-x\rangle_{p}}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p^{2})

for any
p
-adic integer
x
,
where
\langle x\rangle_{m}
denotes the least nonnegative residue of
x
modulo
m
. In 2017, J.-C. Liu
[
11
]
stated that for any
x\in\{1/2,1/3,1/4,1/6\}
and any positive integer
n
,

\sum_{k=0}^{pn-1}\binom{-x}{k}\binom{x-1}{k}\equiv(-1)^{\langle-x\rangle_{p}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{-x}{k}\binom{x-1}{k}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p^{2}).

In recent years,
q
-analogues of supercongruences were widely investigated, and
a variety of techniques, such as asymptotic estimate, basic hypergeometric transformation, creative microscoping,
q
-WZ pair and
q
-Zeilberger algorithm etc., were involved.
For example, in
[
8
]
, Guo and Zudilin introduced a new method called creative microscoping,
and used this method to proved several new Ramanujan-type
q
-congruences in a unifed way.
For more related results and the latest progress, the reader is referred to
[
3
,
4
,
5
,

,

,

,
9
,
10
,
15
,
21
,
20
,

,
23
]
.

In 1969, Dwork
[
1
]
studied a question of continuing analytical solutions
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{k}z^{k}
of linear differential equations via
p
-adic analysis.
A general strategy was to prove that the truncated sums
f_{r}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r}-1}A_{k}z^{k},
where
r=0,1,2,\ldots,
satisfy the so-called Dwork congruences
[
12
]

\displaystyle\frac{f_{r+1}(z)}{f_{r}(z^{p})}\equiv\frac{f_{r}(z)}{f_{r-1}(z^{p})}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p^{r}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[z]])\quad\text{for $r=1,2,\ldots$}.

(1.5)

Moreover, for some
m\geq 2,
provided the congruences (
1.5
) hold modulo a higher power of
p
, such as,

\frac{f_{r+1}(z)}{f_{r}(z^{p})}\equiv\frac{f_{r}(z)}{f_{r-1}(z^{p})}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p^{mr}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[z]])\quad\text{for $r=1,2,\ldots$}.

We refer to this type of congruences as Dwork-type supercongruences. Recently, Guo and Zudilin
[
9
]
proved some Dwork-type supercongruences by establishing their
q
-ananlogues. For example, they proved that,
for any odd positive integer
n>1
and integer
r\geq 1
, modulo
\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Phi_{n^{j}}(q)^{2}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(n^{r}-1)/d}\frac{2(q;q^{2})_{k}^{2}q^{2k}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{k}^{2}(1+q^{2k})}\equiv\left(\frac{-1}{n}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{(n^{r-1}-1)/d}\frac{2(q^{n};q^{2n})_{k}^{2}q^{2nk}}{(q^{2n};q^{2n})_{k}^{2}(1+q^{2nk})},

(1.6)

where
d=1,2.
Here and in what follows, the
q
-shifted factorial

[
2
]
is defined by

(x;q)_{n}=\begin{cases}(1-x)(1-xq)\cdots(1-xq^{n-1})&\text{if }n\geq 1,\\
1&\text{if }n=0,\end{cases}

and the
n
-th cyclotomic polynomial
is defined as

\Phi_{n}(q):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq n\\
(n,k)=1\end{subarray}}(q-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}\cdot\frac{k}{n}}).

Moreover, for polynomials
A_{1}(q),A_{2}(q),P(q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q],
we say that
A_{1}(q)/A_{2}(q)\equiv 0\ ({\rm{mod}}\ P(q))
if
P(q)
divides
A_{1}(q)
and is relatively prime to
A_{2}(q).

More generally, if the numerator of the reduced form of the difference between two rational functions
B(q)
and
C(q)
is divisible by
P(q),
we say that
B(q)\equiv C(q)\ ({\rm{mod}}\ P(q)).

Motivated by Guo and Zudilin’s work
[
9
]
, we shall give the
following result, which was originally conjectured by Guo and Zudilin
[
9
, Conjecture 3.13]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m
and
s
be positive integers with
s<m
. Let
n>1
be an odd integer with
n\equiv\pm 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m)
. Then, for
r\geq 2
, modulo
\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Phi_{n^{j}}(q)^{2}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n^{r}-1}\frac{2(q^{s};q^{m})_{k}(q^{m-s};q^{m})_{k}q^{mk}}{(q^{m};q^{m})_{k}^{2}(1+q^{mk})}\equiv(-1)^{\langle-s/m\rangle_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n^{r-1}-1}\frac{2(q^{sn};q^{mn})_{k}(q^{mn-sn};q^{mn})_{k}q^{mnk}}{(q^{mn};q^{mn})_{k}^{2}(1+q^{mnk})}.

(1.7)

Clearly, the
q
-supercongruence (
1.6
) is just the
(m,s)=(2,1)
case of (
1.7
).

Note that

\Phi_{d}(1)=\begin{cases}p&\text{if }d=p^{k}\text{ for some prime }p,\\
1&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Letting
n=p
be a prime and
q\rightarrow 1
in (
1.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r}-1}\binom{-s/m}{k}\binom{-(m-s)/m}{k}-(-1)^{\langle-s/m\rangle_{p}}\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r-1}-1}\binom{-s/m}{k}\binom{-(m-s)/m}{k}\right)\bigg/p^{2r}

is a
p
-adic integer.
Moreover, since
\gcd(m,p)=1
and
1\bigg/\left(\binom{-s/m}{p^{r-1}}\binom{-(m-s)/m}{p^{r-1}}\right)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the number

W_{p,p^{r-1}}=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r}-1}\binom{-s/m}{k}\binom{-(m-s)/m}{k}-(-1)^{\langle-s/m\rangle_{p}}\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r-1}-1}\binom{-s/m}{k}\binom{-(m-s)/m}{k}}{p^{2r}\binom{-s/m}{p^{r-1}}\binom{-(m-s)/m}{p^{r-1}}}

is a
p
-adic integer. This partially confirms the
n=p^{r-1}
case of a conjecture of Z.-W. Sun
[
19
, Conjecture 10]
.
On the other hand, the
n=p
and
q\to 1
case of (
1.7
) with
m=3,4,6
also confirms some predictions of Roberts and Rodriguez-Villegas from
[
16
]
.

The rest of the paper is arranged as follows. The proof of Theorem
1.1
will be given in Section 2
using the creative microscoping method developed by Guo and Zudilin
[
8
]
.
More precisely, to prove Theorem
1.1
, we shall prove its generalization with an extra parameter
a
so that the corresponding congruence holds modulo
\prod_{j=0}^{n^{r-1}-1}(1-aq^{n(mj+s)})\prod_{j=0}^{n^{r-1}-1}(a-q^{n(mj+m-s)}).
Since the polynomials
1-aq^{n(mj+s)}
and
a-q^{n(mj+m-s)}
are pairwise relatively prime for any
j
with
0\leq j\leq n^{r-1}-1,
this generalized
q
-congruence can be established modulo these polynomials individually. Finally, by
taking the limit
a\rightarrow 1
, we obtain the desired
q
-supercongruence in Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We need the following lemma, which was proved by Guo
[
3
, Corollary 1.4]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
m,n
and
s
be positive integers with
\gcd(m,n)=1
and
n
odd. Then, modulo
(1-aq^{s+m\langle-s/m\rangle_{n}})(a-q^{m-s+m\langle(s-m)/m\rangle_{n}}),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{2(aq^{s};q^{m})_{k}(q^{m-s}/a;q^{m})_{k}q^{mk}}{(q^{m};q^{m})_{k}^{2}(1+q^{mk})}\equiv(-1)^{\langle-s/m\rangle_{n}}.

(2.1)

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we need to establish the following two parametric
generalizations.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
m
,
n
and
s
be positive integers with
s<m
,
n\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m)
and
n
odd. Let
r\geq 2
be an integer and
a
an indeterminate. Then, modulo

\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{n^{r-1}-1}(1-aq^{n(mj+s)})(a-q^{n(mj+m-s)}),

(2.2)

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n^{r}-1}\frac{2(aq^{s};q^{m})_{k}(q^{m-s}/a;q^{m})_{k}q^{mk}}{(q^{m};q^{m})_{k}^{2}(1+q^{mk})}

\displaystyle\quad\equiv(-1)^{\langle-s/m\rangle_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n^{r-1}-1}\frac{2(aq^{sn};q^{mn})_{k}(q^{mn-sn}/a;q^{mn})_{k}q^{mnk}}{(q^{mn};q^{mn})_{k}^{2}(1+q^{mnk})}.

(2.3)

Proof.

It suffices to show that both sides of (
2.3
) are identical when we take
a=q^{-n(mj+s)}
for any
j
with
0\leq j\leq n^{r-1}-1
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n^{r}-1}\frac{2(q^{s-n(mj+s)};q^{m})_{k}(q^{m-s+n(mj+s)};q^{m})_{k}q^{mk}}{(q^{m};q^{m})_{k}^{2}(1+q^{mk})}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{\langle-s/m\rangle_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n^{r-1}-1}\frac{2(q^{-nmj};q^{mn})_{k}(q^{mn+nmj};q^{mn})_{k}q^{mnk}}{(q^{mn};q^{mn})_{k}^{2}(1+q^{mnk})},

(2.4)

or
a=q^{n(mj+m-s)}
for any
j
with
0\leq j\leq n^{r-1}-1
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n^{r}-1}\frac{2(q^{s+n(mj+m-s)};q^{m})_{k}(q^{m-s-n(mj+m-s)};q^{m})_{k}q^{mk}}{(q^{m};q^{m})_{k}^{2}(1+q^{mk})}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{\langle-s/m\rangle_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n^{r-1}-1}\frac{2(q^{mjn+mn};q^{mn})_{k}(q^{-mjn};q^{mn})_{k}q^{mnk}}{(q^{mn};q^{mn})_{k}^{2}(1+q^{mnk})}.

(2.5)

It is easy to see that
n^{r}-1\geq nj+s(n-1)/m
for
0\leq j\leq n^{r-1}-1
, and
n^{r}-1\geq nj+(m-s)(n-1)/m
for
0\leq j\leq n^{r-1}-1
.
Since
n\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m)
, we know
\langle-s/m\rangle_{n}=s(n-1)/m
and
\langle(s-m)/m\rangle_{n}=(m-s)(n-1)/m
. By Lemma
2.1
the left-hand side of (
2.3
) is equal to

(-1)^{nj+s(n-1)/m}.

Likewise, the right-hand side of (
2.3
) is equal to

(-1)^{s(n-1)/m}(-1)^{(mj+s-s)/m}=(-1)^{nj+s(n-1)/m}.

This proves (
2.4
).
Similarly, we can also prove the identity (
2.5
) is true. Namely, the
q
-congruence (
2.3
) is true modulo (
2.2
).
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
m
,
n
and
s
be positive integers with
s<m
,
n\equiv-1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m)
and
n
odd. Then, for
r\geq 2
, modulo
(
2.2
)
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n^{r}-1}\frac{2(q^{s}/a;q^{m})_{k}(aq^{m-s};q^{m})_{k}q^{mk}}{(q^{m};q^{m})_{k}^{2}(1+q^{mk})}

\displaystyle\quad\equiv(-1)^{\langle-s/m\rangle_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n^{r-1}-1}\frac{2(aq^{sn};q^{mn})_{k}(q^{mn-sn}/a;q^{mn})_{k}q^{mnk}}{(q^{mn};q^{mn})_{k}^{2}(1+q^{mnk})}.

(2.6)

Proof.

For
a=q^{-n(mj+s)}
with
0\leq j\leq n^{r-1}-1
, by Lemma
2.1
, the left-hand side of (
2.6
) is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n^{r}-1}\frac{2(q^{s+n(mj+s)};q^{m})_{k}(q^{m-s-n(mj+s)};q^{m})_{k}q^{mk}}{(q^{m};q^{m})_{k}^{2}(1+q^{mk})}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{nj+s(n+1)/m-1}=(-1)^{j-1+s(n+1)/m},

Similarly, the right-hand side of (
2.6
) is equal to

(-1)^{\langle-s/m\rangle_{n}}(-1)^{j}=(-1)^{n-(n+1)s/m+j}=(-1)^{j-1+s(n+1)/m},

where we use the fact that
\langle-s/m\rangle_{n}=n-s(n+1)/m
since
n\equiv-1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m).
And so the
q
-congruence (
2.6
) is true modulo
\prod_{j=0}^{n^{r-1}-1}(1-aq^{n(mj+s)})
.

For
a=q^{n(mj+m-s)}
with
0\leq j\leq n^{r-1}-1
,
the left-hand side of (
2.6
) is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n^{r}-1}\frac{2(q^{s-n(mj+m-s)};q^{m})_{k}(q^{(m-s)+n(mj+m-s)};q^{m})_{k}q^{mk}}{(q^{m};q^{m})_{k}^{2}(1+q^{mk})}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{nj+n-s(n+1)/m}=(-1)^{j-1+s(n+1)/m},

which is the same as the right-hand side of (
2.6
). This proves (
2.6
) modulo
\prod_{j=0}^{n^{r-1}-1}(a-q^{n(mj+m-s)})
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The limit of (
2.2
) as
a\rightarrow 1
has the factor

\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Phi_{n^{j}}(q)^{2n^{r-j}},

where we use the fact that the sets

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\{n(mj+s):j=0,\ldots,n^{r-1}-1\},&\text{ }\\
\{n(mj+m-s):j=0,\ldots,n^{r-1}-1\},\\
\end{cases}

in total contain exactly
2n^{r-j}
multiples of
n^{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,r.

On the other hand, the least common denominator of both sides of (
2.3
) is at most equal to

(q^{m};q^{m})^{2}_{n^{r}-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n^{r}-1}(1+q^{mk})

and its factor related to
\Phi_{n}(q),\Phi_{n^{2}}(q),\ldots
is just

\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Phi_{n^{j}}(q)^{2\left\lfloor(n^{r}-1)/n^{j}\right\rfloor}=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Phi_{n^{j}}(q)^{2(n^{r-j}-1)}.

Hence, letting
a\rightarrow 1
in (
2.3
) we conclude that the
n\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m)
case of (
1.7
) is true modulo
\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Phi_{n^{j}}(q)^{2}.

Similarly, letting
a\rightarrow 1
in (
2.6
), we deduce
that the
n\equiv-1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ m)
case of (
1.7
) is also true. Namely, the
q
-congruence (
1.7
) is true modulo
\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Phi_{n^{j}}(q)^{2}
.
∎

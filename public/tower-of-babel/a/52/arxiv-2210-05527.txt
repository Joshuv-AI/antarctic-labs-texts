1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

Two of the most prominent examples of topological cohomology theories have a one dimensional formal group associated: ordinary cohomology is associated to the additive formal group, while complex
K
-theory is associated to the multiplicative one. More generally one can associate to every complex orientable cohomology theory a one dimensional formal group
[
Qui69
]
. A great source of formal groups is the formal completion of a one dimensional algebraic group at the identity. If we are working over an algebraically closed field for example, we are soon in short supply of one dimensional algebraic groups, except for the additive and multiplicative ones all the others are elliptic curves. These more exotic theories whose associated formal group arise as the formal completion of an elliptic curve
\mathcal{C}
around the identity are called elliptic cohomology theories, and were first constructed in
[
Lan88
]

[
LRS95
]
.

Given a compact Lie group
G
, it is only natural to seek for a
G
-equivariant counterpart of elliptic cohomology. If one takes complex K-theory as a model, than we can expect a good theory of equivariant elliptic cohomology to encode the full algebraic group
\mathcal{C}
, in contrast with the non-equivariant theory simply encoding the formal completion of
\mathcal{C}
around the identity. This is in analogy to how equivariant K-theory works. By the Atiyah-Segal completion theorem
[
AS69
]
if we complete the equivariant K-theory of the point at its augmentation ideal, we obtain the K-theory of
BG
, the classifying space of
G
:

KU_{G}(*)^{\wedge}_{I}\cong KU(BG).

Equivariant elliptic cohomology was first constructed by Grojnowski in
[
Gro07
]
with complex coefficients, and the specific motivation to study certain elliptic algebras. The subject has thrived since then and has found numerous applications to representation theory (for
G
connected compact Lie group
[
Gro07
]
,
[
GKV95
]
), to organize moonshine phenomena (especially for
G
finite
[
Dev96
]
,
[
Dev98
]
), and also quite surprisingly to physics
[
ST11
]
,
[
Ber21
]
.

Given the complexity of defining Equivariant elliptic cohomology (
[
Lur18
]
,
[
Lur18a
]
,
[
Lur19
]
,
[
GM20
]
), and its algebraic origin also in the non-equivariant case, it is quite difficult to compute
G
-equivariant elliptic cohomology of meaningful
G
-spaces. Computations are often limited to fixed
G
-spaces or spheres of complex representations
[
Gre05
]
,
[
Bar22
]
to check that the theory defined is indeed elliptic cohomology. This paper has the goal to enhance computations at least for rational
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology, and in particular given an elliptic curve
\mathcal{C}
and a finite dimensional complex
\mathbb{T}
-representation
V
to compute rational
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology of
\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)
: the
\mathbb{T}
-space of complex lines in
V
.

More precisely we will prove the following which is the main theorem of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every elliptic curve
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{C}
, if
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}
(
3.14
) is the rational
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology theory built in
[

]

is a finite dimensional complex representation of

(1)

If

with

.

(2)

If

,

E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}^{k}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V))\cong\begin{cases}0&$k$ \text{even}\\
\mathbb{C}^{d}&$k$ \text{odd}.\end{cases}

where

, and

Part (1) of this theorem simply checks our methods on previously done computations since

with the trivial

-action, and can be found in the paper as Lemma

Notice that there are different representations, with different complex projective spaces that nonetheless have the same value for

and therefore rational
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology does not distinguish them. For example if
\varepsilon
is
\mathbb{C}
with the trivial action,

and

have

, or
W=\varepsilon\oplus 16z
,

and

. Therefore they have the same elliptic cohomology even if their complex projective spaces are quite different.

In Theorem

we use the construction of rational
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology of Greenlees
[
Gre05
, Theorem 1.1]
with algebraic models. Algebraic models have the advantage to simplify the picture and to maintain a more evident bond with the geometry of the curve

, which are essential aspects when performing computations. This construction of rational equivariant elliptic cohomology using algebraic models has been generalized for the

1.2.
Algebraic Models

Algebraic models are a useful tool to study equivariant cohomology theories: equivariant
K
-theory, equivariant cobordism, Bredon and Borel cohomology just to name a few examples. For every compact Lie group
G
one can construct a category of
G
-spectra where every cohomology theory
E_{G}^{*}(\_)
is represented by a
G
-spectrum
E
, in the sense that for any based
G
-space
X
we have
E_{G}^{*}(X)=[\Sigma^{\infty}X,E]^{G}_{*}
. To simplify the picture one can restrict the focus to rational
G
-equivariant cohomology theories, represented by rational
G
-spectra: the localization of
G
-spectra at the rational sphere spectrum.

The main idea of algebraic models
[
Gre99
]
is for
G
a compact Lie group to define an abelian category
\mathcal{A}(G)
and an homology functor

(1.2)

\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}:G\Spectra_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathcal{A}(G)

together with a strongly convergent Adams spectral sequence to compute stable maps in rational
G
-spectra:

(1.3)

\Ext^{*,*}_{\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}(X),\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}(Y))\Longrightarrow[X,Y]^{G}_{*}

Furthermore for certain groups the homology functor
\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}
can be lifted to a Quillen-equivalence

(1.4)

G\Spectra_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq_{Q}d\mathcal{A}(G)

between rational
G
-spectra and the category
d\mathcal{A}(G)
of differential graded objects in
\mathcal{A}(G)
. The Adams spectral sequence (
1.3
) is a powerful tool to compute values of known theories, while the Quillen-equivalence (
1.4
) can be used to build entirely new theories, simply constructing objects in
d\mathcal{A}(G)
. This latest method is precisely the one used in
[
Gre05
]
and
[
Bar22
]
: building an object
E\mathcal{C}_{G}
in
d\mathcal{A}(G)
and using the Quillen-equivalence (
1.4
) for the circle
G=\mathbb{T}
and the
2
-torus
G=\mathbb{T}^{2}
to define a
G
-equivariant elliptic cohomology theory. This method works since Greenlees and Shipley have extended the Quillen-equivalence (
1.4
) to tori of any rank
\mathbb{T}^{r}

[
GS18
, Theorem 1.1]
.

1.3.
The method of this paper

We start from an elliptic curve
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{C}
together with a coordinate function
t_{e}
in the local ring at the identity
e
of
\mathcal{C}
. This gives us a rational
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology theory

[
Gre05
, Theorem 1.1]
, as well as a rational
\mathbb{T}^{2}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology theory

[

, Theorem 1.4]
. We have also the complex abelian surface

.

Given a complex
\mathbb{T}
-representation

E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)).

We start pointing out the isomorphism of
\mathbb{T}
-spaces:

(1.5)

\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)\cong S(V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}w)/H_{1}

where

is the natural complex representation of
H_{1}=1\times\mathbb{T}<\mathbb{T}^{2}
and
S(V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}w)
is the
\mathbb{T}
-space of vectors of unit norm in the complex vector space
V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}w
. Notice that

is a complex representation of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
of the same dimension of
V
, where
H_{1}
acts on the second factor of the tensor product. The computation is made possible since

and
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
are
H_{1}
-split, where we view
\mathbb{T}
as the quotient group
\mathbb{T}^{2}/H_{1}
:

Theorem 1.6
.

There is a natural transformation of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
-cohomology theories

(1.7)

\varepsilon:\Inflation_{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathbb{T}^{2}}E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}\longrightarrow E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}

which induces an isomorphism

(1.8)

[\mathchoice{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{2}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\textstyle\mathbb{T}^{2}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{T}^{2}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{T}^{2}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}_{+},\Inflation_{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathbb{T}^{2}}E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}]^{\mathbb{T}^{2}}_{*}\cong[\mathchoice{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{2}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\textstyle\mathbb{T}^{2}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{T}^{2}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{T}^{2}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}_{+},E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}]^{\mathbb{T}^{2}}_{*}

for every subgroup

of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
such that

.

We prove this Theorem in Sections

and

As an immediate Corollary:

Corollary 1.9
.

For any
H_{1}
-free
\mathbb{T}^{2}
-space

(1.10)

E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}^{*}(X)\cong E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(X/H_{1})

We can apply this corollary to (

), reducing the computation of
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology to a computation of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology:

E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)_{+})\cong E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(\mathchoice{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\displaystyle S(V\otimes w)_{+}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle H_{1}$}}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\textstyle S(V\otimes w)_{+}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\textstyle H_{1}$}}{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptstyle S(V\otimes w)_{+}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\scriptstyle H_{1}$}}{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle S(V\otimes w)_{+}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H_{1}$}})\cong E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}^{*}(S(V\otimes w)_{+}).

The
\mathbb{T}^{2}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology of

is easier to compute in view of the cofibre sequence of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
-spaces:

S(V\otimes w)_{+}\longrightarrow S^{0}\longrightarrow S^{V\otimes w}

(1.12)

E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}^{*}(S^{V\otimes w})\longrightarrow E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}^{*}(S^{0})\longrightarrow E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}^{*}(S(V\otimes w)_{+}).

The first two terms of (

[

, Theorem 1.4]
that we recall here for easy reference:

Theorem 1.13
.

For every elliptic curve
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{C}
and coordinate

, there exists an object

whose associated rational
\mathbb{T}^{2}
-equivariant cohomology theory

\mathbb{T}^{2}

E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}^{n}(S^{W})\cong\begin{cases}H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(-D_{W}))\oplus H^{2}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(-D_{W}))&$n$ even\\

from compact abelian Lie groups to complex manifolds. If

Where we are considering group homomorphisms, and

is a subvariety of

of the same dimension of

of

that is the associated divisor of

. If

(1.15)

D_{W}:=\sum_{z^{\prime}}\alpha_{z^{\prime}}\mathfrak{X}(\Ker(z^{\prime})),

where in both sums

runs over all the non-trivial characters of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, and
\alpha_{z^{\prime}}
is a non-negative integer.

Remark 1.16
.

One might argue that to compute rational
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology of
\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)
a more straightforward approach can be taken. Namely we have
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T})
and if we explicitly compute the algebraic model
\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T})}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)_{+})
, then we can use the Adams spectral sequence for the circle to compute directly
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)_{+})
. Originally this was the method tried on this project, with a double motivation: first as a warm-up in the circle case before generalizing to higher tori, and second, once
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
has been constructed, compute
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}^{*}(S(V\otimes w)_{+})
, and see if the two values match. If indeed
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)_{+})\cong E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}^{*}(S(V\otimes w)_{+})
, then one might expect that
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}
and
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
are
H_{1}
-split (Theorem
1.6
). The problem encountered with this method is that after computing the algebraic model
\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T})}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)_{+})
then computing maps in
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
from this object into an injective resolution of
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}
still retains a lot of complexity. More precisely it is still difficult to compute
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}
-module maps between arbitrary
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}
-module when none of them is a suspension of the base ring
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}
. Therefore this more conceptual approach of proving
H_{1}
-splitness and computing
E\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}^{*}(S(V\otimes w)_{+})
has been taken. Moreover we hope to replicate and generalise this to other spaces such as Grassmannians of
n
-planes
\Gr_{n}(V)
of a complex
\mathbb{T}
-representation.

1.5.
Notation and Conventions

The most important convention of the paper is that everything is rationalized without comment. In particular all spectra are meant localized at the rational sphere spectrum
S^{0}\mathbb{Q}
and all the homology and cohomology is meant with
\mathbb{Q}

are meant over
\mathbb{Q}
, or over the graded ring with only

in degree zero and zero elsewhere. By subgroup of a compact Lie group we always mean closed subgroup. With the symbol

we mean the torus of rank

. We denote

the

subgroups of

is
\{H_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
indexed with

, and with
H_{1}=1\times\mathbb{T}
and
H_{2}=\mathbb{T}\times 1
being the two privileged subgroups. We denote

the quotient group. We denote

the subgroup of
G
with

: we will refer to the subgroups with identity component

as being along the

-direction. We denote with

a generic finite subgroup of

and

G
of any dimension. Given a module

we will denote

the two periodic version of

: it is a graded module with

: this identifies the element

in the direct sum that has

.

\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H_{i}$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H_{i}$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H_{i}$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H_{i}$}}}

2.
Elliptic cohomology of CP(V)

Recall that
G=\mathbb{T}^{2}
, and
H_{1}=1\times\mathbb{T}
and
H_{2}=\mathbb{T}\times 1
are the two privileged subgroups. We have also the quotient
\bar{G}:=G/H_{1}\cong H_{2}\cong\mathbb{T}
as group of equivariance. We have fixed an elliptic curve
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{C}
together with a coordinate
t_{e}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C},e}
. This gives us a rational
\bar{G}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology theory
E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}\in\mathcal{A}(\bar{G})
(
3.14
), as well as a rational
G
-equivariant elliptic cohomology theory
E\mathcal{C}_{G}\in\mathcal{A}(G)
(Theorem
1.13
). We have also the complex abelian surface
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{G}=\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}
associated to
G
and
\mathcal{C}
.

Given a
\bar{G}
complex representation
V
the main goal of the paper is to compute the reduced cohomology of the pointed space:

E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{*}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V))

where
\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)
is the
\bar{G}
-space of complex lines in
V
. First of all notice the isomorphism of
\bar{G}
-spaces:

(2.1)

\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)\cong S(V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}w)/H_{1}

where
w
is the natural complex representation of
H_{1}
and
S(V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}w)
is the
\bar{G}
-space of vectors of unit norm in the complex vector space
V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}w
. Notice that
V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}w
is a complex representation of
G
of the same dimension of
V
, where
H_{1}
acts on the second factor of the tensor product, while
\bar{G}
acts on the first one. The computation is made possible since
E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}
and
E\mathcal{C}_{G}
are
H_{1}
-split:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
E\mathcal{C}_{G}
be
G
-elliptic cohomology (Theorem
1.13
), and
E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}
be
\bar{G}
-elliptic cohomology (
3.14
). Then there is a natural transformation of
G
-cohomology theories

\varepsilon:\Inflation_{\bar{G}}^{G}E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}\longrightarrow E\mathcal{C}_{G}

which induces an isomorphism

(2.3)

[\mathchoice{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\displaystyle G$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\textstyle G$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptstyle G$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle G$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}_{+},\Inflation_{\bar{G}}^{G}E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}]^{G}_{*}\cong[\mathchoice{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\displaystyle G$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\textstyle G$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptstyle G$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle G$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}_{+},E\mathcal{C}_{G}]^{G}_{*}

for every subgroup
H
of
G
such that
H\cap H_{1}=\{1\}
.

We are going to prove this Theorem in the remaining sections of this paper, in particular in Section
4
we build the map
\varepsilon
while in Section
5
we prove the
H
-equivalence (
2.3
). In the interest of clarity we now present how to compute elliptic cohomology of
\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)
starting from
H_{1}
-splitness.

We have an immediate Corollary:

Corollary 2.4
.

For any
H_{1}
-free
G
-space
X
:

(2.5)

E\mathcal{C}_{G}^{*}(X)\cong E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{*}(X/H_{1})

Since
X
is
H_{1}
-free, it is built using cells
G/H_{+}
with
H\cap H_{1}=\{1\}
, for which we have the equivalence (
2.3
). Therefore:

E\mathcal{C}_{G}^{*}(X)=[X,E\mathcal{C}_{G}]^{G}_{*}\cong[X,\Inflation_{\bar{G}}^{G}E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}]^{G}_{*}\cong[X/H_{1},E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}]^{\bar{G}}_{*}=E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{*}(X/H_{1})

where the second isomorphism is the orbits-inflation adjunction.
∎

Applying this corollary to (
2.1
) allows us to reduce the computation of
\bar{G}
-equivariant elliptic cohomology to a computation of
G
-equivariant elliptic cohomology:

(2.6)

E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{*}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)_{+})\cong E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{*}(\mathchoice{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\displaystyle S(V\otimes w)_{+}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle H_{1}$}}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\textstyle S(V\otimes w)_{+}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\textstyle H_{1}$}}{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptstyle S(V\otimes w)_{+}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\scriptstyle H_{1}$}}{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle S(V\otimes w)_{+}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H_{1}$}})\cong E\mathcal{C}_{G}^{*}(S(V\otimes w)_{+}).

Remark 2.7
.

Notice we have to add a disjoint basepoint to
S(V\otimes w)
, leading us to compute
E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{*}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)_{+})
. To obtain
\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)
without the added basepoint notice that stably:

\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)_{+}\cong\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)\vee S^{0}

and therefore

(2.8)

E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{*}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)_{+})\cong E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{*}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V))\oplus E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{*}(S^{0})\cong E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{*}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V))\oplus\mathbb{C},

since by
[
Gre05
, Theorem 1.1]
we have

E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{k}(S^{0})\cong\begin{cases}H^{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})\cong\mathbb{C}&$k$ \text{even}\\
H^{1}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})\cong\mathbb{C}&$k$ \text{odd}.\end{cases}

G
-equivariant elliptic cohomology of
S(V\otimes w)_{+}
is easier to compute in view of the cofibre sequence of
G
-spaces:

(2.9)

S(V\otimes w)_{+}\longrightarrow S^{0}\longrightarrow S^{V\otimes w}

inducing a long exact sequence:

(2.10)

E\mathcal{C}_{G}^{*}(S^{V\otimes w})\longrightarrow E\mathcal{C}_{G}^{*}(S^{0})\longrightarrow E\mathcal{C}_{G}^{*}(S(V\otimes w)_{+}).

The first two terms of (
2.10
) are computed in (
1.14
), therefore we can deduce the third term from kernel and cokernel of the first map.

To find the associated divisor
D_{V\otimes w}
(
1.15
) decompose
V
as a sum of one dimensional complex representations
V=\bigoplus_{n}\alpha_{n}z^{n}
where
z
is the natural representation of
\bar{G}
and
\alpha_{n}\geq 0
. Notice
z^{n}\otimes w
is a one dimensional representation of
G
with kernel the connected codimension
1
subgroup:

H_{d_{n}}:=\Ker(z^{n}\otimes w)=\{(x,y)\in G=\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}\mid x^{n}y=1\}

and corresponding divisor:

(2.11)

D_{d_{n}}=\mathfrak{X}(H_{d_{n}})=\{(P,Q)\in\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\mid nP+Q=e\}.

Therefore
V\otimes w=\bigoplus_{n}\alpha_{n}(z^{n}\otimes w)
is a complex representation of
G
of the same dimension as
V
, whose associated divisor is:

(2.12)

D_{V\otimes w}=\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}D_{d_{n}}.

We have two distinct proves depending if the divisor (
2.12
) has only one coefficient different from zero, or more than one coefficient different from zero.

When
V
has only one isotypic component, then
\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)\cong\mathbb{C}\text{P}^{\alpha-1}
with trivial
\mathbb{T}
-action. We can prove part (1) of Theorem
1.1
:

Lemma 2.13
.

If
V
has only one isotypic component:
V=\alpha z^{n}
with
\alpha\geq 1
then

(2.14)

E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{k}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V))\cong\mathbb{C}^{\alpha-1}

for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Combining (
2.6
) and (
2.10
) we only need to understand kernel and cokernel of the map

(2.15)

E\mathcal{C}_{G}^{*}(S^{V\otimes w})\longrightarrow E\mathcal{C}_{G}^{*}(S^{0}).

By (
2.12
) the divisor associated to
V\otimes w
is
D_{V\otimes w}=\alpha D_{d_{n}}
, denote
D:=D_{d_{n}}
the smooth irreducible curve. By (
1.14
) it is enough to understand kernel and cokernel for the map

(2.16)

H^{*}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(-\alpha D))\longrightarrow H^{*}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})

induced by the inclusion of sheaves
\mathcal{O}(-\alpha D)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
. We compute this map for the various degrees.

In degree zero
H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(-\alpha D))=0
, since
\mathcal{X}
is a compact complex abelian surface, therefore a function regular on all the surface is constant and since it has a zero at
D
it is the constant zero. Therefore (
2.16
) in degree zero has zero kernel and cokernel
\mathbb{C}
.

In degree
2
by Serre duality

(2.17)

H^{2}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(-\alpha D))\cong H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(\alpha D))^{\vee}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\alpha}

where the second isomorphism is obtained as follows. Consider the divisor
D^{\prime}=\alpha(e)
on the single elliptic curve
\mathcal{C}
, defining the line bundle
\mathcal{L}:=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}(\alpha(e))
. The projective morphism
f:=\pi_{d_{n}}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{C}
is such that
f^{*}\mathcal{L}\cong\mathcal{O}(\alpha D)
, and
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
by
[
Liu02
, Exercise 3.12 Chapter 5]
. As a consequence

(2.18)

f_{*}f^{*}\mathcal{L}=f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}f^{*}\mathcal{L})\cong f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathcal{L}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathcal{L}\cong\mathcal{L}

by the projection formula
[
Har77
, exercise 5.1]
. Therefore

(2.19)

H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(\alpha D))\cong H^{0}(\mathcal{X},f^{*}\mathcal{L})\cong H^{0}(\mathcal{C},f_{*}f^{*}\mathcal{L})\cong H^{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{L})\cong\mathbb{C}^{\alpha},

where the second isomorphism is due to the equality of functors
\Gamma(\mathcal{X},\_)=\Gamma(\mathcal{C},f_{*}(\_))
, while the last one is Riemann-Roch for the elliptic curve
\mathcal{C}
.
Moreover the map

\mathbb{C}\cong H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})\rightarrowtail H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(\alpha D))\cong\mathbb{C}^{\alpha}

is injective being the inclusion of the constant functions. By Serre duality the dual map, (
2.16
) in degree 2, is surjective with kernel
\mathbb{C}^{\alpha-1}
.

In degree 1 by Serre duality

H^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(-\alpha D))\cong H^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(\alpha D))^{\vee}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\alpha}

since by Riemann-Roch
[
Bea96
, Theorem I.12]

(2.20)

h^{0}(\mathcal{X},\alpha D)-h^{1}(\mathcal{X},\alpha D)=\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}(D.D)=0.

We can see
(D.D)=0
since
D
is linearly equivalent to the translated
D+\lambda
, which is an irreducible curve disjoint from
D
, or alternatively simply using the genus formula
[
Bea96
, Theorem I.15]
since
D
is irreducible of genus 1 being isomorphic to the elliptic curve
\mathcal{C}
. By Lemma
2.21
the image of the map (
2.16
) in degree 1 is precisely
H^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(-D))\cong\mathbb{C}
, combining this with the computations (
2.16
) we obtain that the kernel in degree 1 of (
2.16
) is
\mathbb{C}^{\alpha-1}
while the cokernel is
\mathbb{C}
.

In conclusion we obtain that
E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{k}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)_{+})
has in even degrees the degree zero cokernel and the degree one kernel of (
2.16
), therefore is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{\alpha}
. In odd degrees we have the degree 1 cokernel and the degree 2 kernel of (
2.16
), so
\mathbb{C}^{\alpha}
as well. By (
2.8
)
E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{k}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V))\cong\mathbb{C}^{\alpha-1}
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
D=D_{d_{n}}
be any of the smooth divisors (
2.11
). For every integer
\alpha\geq 1
the image of the map

(2.22)

H^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(-\alpha D))\to H^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})

is precisely
H^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(-D))\subset H^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})
.

When
\alpha=1
we have that
D
is a subvariety of
\mathcal{X}
whose ideal sheaf is precisely
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(-D)
, and structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{D}
that we can see as a sheaf on
\mathcal{X}
via the inclusion map
\iota:D\to\mathcal{X}
. We have the closed subscheme short exact sequence
[
Vak17
, 14.3.B]
:

(2.23)

0\to\mathcal{O}(-D)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\to\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D}\to 0

inducing a long exact sequence in cohomology. Since
H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})\xrightarrow{\cong}H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D})\cong\mathbb{C}
then
H^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(-D))\to H^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})
is injective.

When
\alpha\geq 2
we can tensor the exact sequence (
2.23
) with the invertible sheaf
\mathcal{O}(-(\alpha-1)D)
obtaining the exact sequence of sheaves:

(2.24)

0\to\mathcal{O}(-\alpha D)\to\mathcal{O}(-(\alpha-1)D)\to\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D}(-(\alpha-1)D)\to 0

inducing a long exact sequence in cohomology. By the adjunction formula
[
Liu02
, Theorem 1.37]
:

(\omega_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes\mathcal{O}(D))\restriction_{D}=\omega_{D}

where in our case
\omega_{\mathcal{X}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
and
\omega_{D}\cong\mathcal{O}_{D}
, resulting in
\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D}(D)\cong\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D}
and by induction
\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D}(\beta D)\cong\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D}
for every integer
\beta
.

Therefore we have that the last term of (
2.24
) is
\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D}
, and we can analyse the degree 2 piece of the long exact sequence induced:

(2.25)

H^{1}(\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D})\to H^{2}(\mathcal{O}(-\alpha D))\to H^{2}(\mathcal{O}(-(\alpha-1)D))\to H^{2}(\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D})=0.

The first map in (
2.25
) is injective simply by dimension computation:
H^{1}(\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D})\cong\mathbb{C}
, and the other two terms are computed in (
2.17
). Consequently the map
H^{1}(\mathcal{O}(-\alpha D))\twoheadrightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{O}(-(\alpha-1)D))
is surjective. In conclusion we have the chain of maps:

H^{1}(\mathcal{O}(-\alpha D))\twoheadrightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{O}(-(\alpha-1)D))\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{O}(-D))\rightarrowtail H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})

where all the maps are surjective except the last one that is injective, giving us the desired result.
∎

When
V
has more than one isotypic component, then the proof of part (2) of Theorem
1.1
has a different approach:

Theorem 2.26
.

If
V
has more than one isotypic component:
V=\bigoplus_{n}\alpha_{n}z^{n}
with
\alpha_{n}\geq 0
then

(2.27)

E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{k}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V))\cong\begin{cases}0&$k$ even\\
\mathbb{C}^{d}&$k$ odd.\end{cases}

where
d=\sum_{i<j}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}(i-j)^{2}
.

Exactly as in the proof of Theorem
2.13
we only need to understand kernel and cokernel of the map

(2.28)

H^{*}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(-D))\longrightarrow H^{*}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})

where
D:=D_{V\otimes w}=\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}D_{d_{n}}
is the divisor associated to
V\otimes w
(
2.12
).

When
V
has more than one isotypic components, the associated divisor
D
is an ample divisor. To show this we can use the Nakai-Moishezon criterion
[
Har77
, Theorem V.1.10]
. This criterion states that
D
is an ample divisor on
\mathcal{X}
if and only if
D.D>0
and
D.\mathcal{C}^{\prime}>0
for every irreducible curve
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{X}
. First let us prove that
D_{d_{r}}.D_{d_{s}}=(r-s)^{2}
for any two integers
r
and
s
. If
r=s
then
D_{d_{r}}.D_{d_{r}}=0
since
D_{d_{r}}
is linearly equivalent to the translated
D_{d_{r}}+\lambda
, which is an irreducible curve disjoint from
D_{d_{r}}
, or alternatively simply using the genus formula
[
Bea96
, Theorem I.15]
since
D_{d_{r}}
is irreducible of genus 1 being isomorphic to the elliptic curve
\mathcal{C}
. If
r\neq s
than the two curves
D_{d_{r}}
and
D_{d_{s}}
are transverse in each point of intersection therefore we simply need to count the intersection points
[
Bea96
, Definition I.3]
. There is an autoisogeny of
\mathcal{X}
bringing
D_{d_{r}}
to
D_{d_{0}}=D_{2}=\{(P,Q)\in\mathcal{X}\mid Q=e\}
. Under this isogeny
D_{d_{s}}
is brought to
D_{d_{s-r}}=\{(P,Q)\in\mathcal{X}\mid(s-r)P+Q=e\}
. The intersection of these last two curves is easily computed to be
\mathcal{C}[s-r]
which has cardinality
(s-r)^{2}
. Therefore:

(2.29)

D.D=\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}(D_{d_{i}}.D_{d_{j}})=2\sum_{i<j}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}(i-j)^{2}>0

since there are at least two different integers
r\neq s
such that
\alpha_{r},\alpha_{s}>0
(
V
has more than one isotypic component). It remains to show that for every irreducible curve
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{X}
, the curve
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
intersects either
D_{d_{r}}
or
D_{d_{s}}
, so that
D.\mathcal{C}^{\prime}>0
. If
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cap D_{d_{r}}=\emptyset
than
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is necessarily parallel to
D_{d_{r}}
, meaning
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=D_{d_{r}}+\lambda
is a translated of
D_{d_{r}}
, but all these curves intersect
D_{d_{s}}
since
s\neq r
.

The sheaf
\mathcal{O}(D)
is invertible and ample, so we can use Kodaira vanishing theorem
[
Har77
, Remark III.7.15]
obtaining
H^{i}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(D))=0
for
i\geq 1
. Therefore only the zeroth cohomology is nonzero and we can compute it with Riemann-Roch
[
Bea96
, Theorem I.12]
:

h^{0}(\mathcal{X},D)=\frac{1}{2}(D.D)=\sum_{i<j}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}(i-j)^{2}=d

where
(D.D)
is computed in (
2.29
). Moreover the map

\mathbb{C}\cong H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})\rightarrowtail H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}(D))\cong\mathbb{C}^{d}

is injective being the inclusion of the constant functions. By Serre duality the dual map, (
2.28
) in degree 2, is surjective with kernel
\mathbb{C}^{d-1}
.

In conclusion we obtain that
E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{k}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V)_{+})
has in even degrees the degree zero cokernel of (
2.28
):
H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})\cong\mathbb{C}
. In odd degrees we have the degree 1 cokernel and the degree 2 kernel of (
2.28
), so
\mathbb{C}^{d+1}
. By (
2.8
)
E\mathcal{C}_{\bar{G}}^{k}(\mathbb{C}\text{P}(V))
has zero in even degrees and
\mathbb{C}^{d}
in odd degrees.
∎

3.
The circle case revisited

We revise the construction of circle equivariant elliptic cohomology from
[
Gre05
]
. We begin by recalling the definition of the TP-topology for the single elliptic curve
[
Gre05
, Definition 7.1]
:

Definition 3.1
.

Over the set
\mathcal{C}
define the torsion point topology
\mathcal{C}^{\text{TP}}
with generating closed subsets
\{\mathcal{C}\langle n\rangle\}_{n\geq 1}
, where
\mathcal{C}\langle n\rangle
are the elements of exact order
n
in
\mathcal{C}
.

This is a ringed topological space with the pushforward of the structure sheaf:
\mathcal{O}^{\text{TP}}_{\mathcal{C}}
. Likewise
[
Gre05
, Definition 8.2]
choose a coordinate for
\mathcal{C}
at
e
:

Choice 3.2
.

Choose
t_{e}\in\mathcal{O}^{\text{TP}}_{\mathcal{C},e}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C},e}
vanishing to the first order at
e
.

Since we are working in characteristic zero there is a unique logarithm for the formal group law of
\mathcal{C}
(see for example
[
Str19
, Proposition 3.1]
), namely a strict isomorphism with the additive formal group law:

{\hat{t}_{e}}=f(t_{e})\in(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C},e}^{\text{TP}})^{\wedge}_{m_{e}}\cong\mathbb{C}[[t_{e}]],

where
m_{e}<\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C},e}^{\text{TP}}
is the ideal of those functions vanishing at
e
.

Denoting
[n]:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
the multiplication by
n
map in the elliptic curve, we can pullback coordinate for the various
\mathcal{C}\langle n\rangle
:

Definition 3.3
.

For every integer
n\geq 1
define the coordinate and completed coordinate:

(3.4)

\begin{split}t_{n}&:=[n]^{*}(t_{e})\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C},e}^{\text{TP}}\\
{\hat{t}_{n}}&:=[n]^{*}({\hat{t}_{e}})=f(t_{n})\in(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C},e}^{\text{TP}})^{\wedge}_{m_{e}}\end{split}

Remark 3.5

Lemma 5.37
.

If
F\subseteq G
is a finite subgroup of

, then

Suppose

in
G

. This is immediate to see since every element in
F^{\prime}
has order
p
, therefore

and they have the same cardinality. This gives us a contradiction since
|F^{\prime}\cap H_{1}|=p>1
.

To prove the existence of such an

let us think about
G
as the quotient

. Every connected codimension 1 subgroup

is determined by a point

for a pair of coprime integers

and
\mu_{i}
, namely

is the image in the quotient

of the line in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
connecting the origin to the point
P_{i}
. For example
H_{1}
is determined by the point
P_{1}=(0,1)
. By the first part of the Lemma

is cyclic and generated by a point

with

and where

is the order of

. Since

we have

and that

and

are coprimes, otherwise we can find a non-trivial multiple of

but that lies in

. Therefore we can find

such that

. For obvious reason

and

are coprimes, so the

Appendix A
Algebraic models

In this section we present a self-contained account of algebraic models for tori of any rank, therefore in all this section
G=\mathbb{T}^{r}
is an r-dimensional torus, with
r\geq 0
. We also specify that all the modules over graded rings are graded and all the maps between graded modules are graded maps. As in the rest of the paper everything is rationalized without comment.

Algebraic models are a useful tool to study rational equivariant cohomology theories. The main idea is to define an abelian category
\mathcal{A}(G)
and homology functor from the category of rational
G
-equivariant orthogonal spectra to
\mathcal{A}(G)
:

(A.1)

\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}:G\Spectra_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathcal{A}(G)

equipped with an Adams spectral sequence to compute maps in the homotopy category of rational
G
-spectra. More precisely the values of the theory may be calculated by a spectral sequence:

(A.2)

\Ext^{*,*}_{\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}(X),\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}(Y))\Longrightarrow[X,Y]^{G}_{*}.

In the case of tori we have a zig-zag of Quillen-equivalences
[
GS18
, Theorem 1.1]
):

(A.3)

G\Spectra_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq_{Q}d\mathcal{A}(G),

where
d\mathcal{A}(G)
is the model category of differential graded objects in
\mathcal{A}(G)
. Therefore we can build rational
G
-equivariant cohomology theories simply constructing objects in
d\mathcal{A}(G)
.

) on the
F

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}

(A.24)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H_{i}$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H_{i}$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H_{i}$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H_{i}$}}}=\prod_{j\geq 1}H^{*}(B\mathbb{T}^{2}/{H_{i}^{j}})\to H^{*}(B\mathbb{T}^{2}/{H_{i}^{n_{i}}})\rightarrowtail H^{*}(B\mathbb{T}^{2}/F).

The first map of (

) is the projection onto the
n_{i}
-th component since

by definition of the index

. The second map of (

-algebras sending the generator

to

A.4.
Description of
\mathcal{A}(G)

We briefly recap the description of
\mathcal{A}(G)

[
Gre08
, Definition 3.9]
. The objects of
\mathcal{A}(G)
are sheaves of modules over the poset of connected subgroups of
G
with inclusions.

Definition A.25
.

An object
X\in\mathcal{A}(G)
is specified by the following pieces of data:

(1)

For every connected subgroup
H
an
\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}
-module
\varphi^{H}X
.

(2)

For every containment of connected subgroups
K\subseteq H
an
\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle K$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle K$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle K$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle K$}}}
-modules map:

(A.26)

\varphi^{K}X\to\mathcal{E}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle H$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle K$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle H$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle K$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle K$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle K$}}}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle K$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle K$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle K$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle K$}}}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\displaylimits_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}}}\varphi^{H}X.

Then
X
is a sheaf over the space of connected subgroups of
G
. This specifically means that for every connected subgroup
H
, the sheaf
X
has value the
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}
-module:

(A.27)

X(H):=\mathcal{E}_{H}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\displaylimits_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}}}\varphi^{H}X,

and that for every containment
K\subseteq H
of connected subgroups,
X
has a structure map of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}
-modules:

(A.28)

\beta_{K}^{H}:X(K)\to X(H).

The map (
A.28
) is obtained tensoring the
\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle K$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle K$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle K$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle K$}}}
-modules map (
A.26
) with the
\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle K$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle K$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle K$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle K$}}}
-module
\mathcal{E}_{K}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}
. Moreover
X
satisfies the condition that for every connected subgroup
H
the
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}
-modules structure map
\beta_{1}^{H}:X(1)\to X(H)
is the map inverting the multiplicatively closed subset of Euler classes
\mathcal{E}_{H}
(
A.15
).

\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}(S^{V})(H)=\mathcal{E}_{H}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\displaylimits_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}}}\Sigma^{V^{H}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}.

To describe the structure maps it is convenient to use the suspension of the units:

\iota_{V^{H}}:=\Sigma^{V^{H}}(1)\in\Sigma^{V^{H}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}

so that for every inclusion of connected subgroups
K\subseteq H
the structure map
\beta_{K}^{H}
is determined by the suspended unit:

(A.50)

\beta_{K}^{H}(\iota_{V^{K}})=e(V^{K}-V^{H})^{-1}\otimes\iota_{V^{H}}

where the difference of the two representations simply means the orthogonal complement:

V^{K}=V^{H}\oplus(V^{K}-V^{H}).

Remark A.51
.

The content of this section applies also in the case of a virtual complex representation
V=V_{0}-V_{1}
. The only thing to specify is the Euler class
e(V)=e(V_{0})/e(V_{1})
. As a result (
A.49
) becomes:

\pi_{*}^{\mathcal{A}}(S^{V})(H)=\mathcal{E}_{H}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\displaylimits_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.64583pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.64583pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.21529pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}}}\Sigma^{V_{0}^{H}-V_{1}^{H}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\displaystyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\displaystyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\textstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\textstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle H$}}{\raisebox{0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{F}$}\mkern-1.0mu/\mkern-1.0mu\raisebox{-0.3014pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle H$}}}.

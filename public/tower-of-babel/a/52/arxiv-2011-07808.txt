Dual variational methods for a nonlinear Helmholtz equation with sign-changing nonlinearity
Rainer Mandel, Dominic Scheider, Tolga Yeşil

Abstract
We prove new existence results for a Nonlinear Helmholtz equation with sign-changing nonlinearity of the form − Δ ​ u − k 2 ​ u = Q ⁡ ( x ) ​ | u | p − 2 ​ u , u ∈ W 2 , p ​ ( ℝ N ) -\Delta u-k^{2}u=Q(x)|u|^{p-2}u,\quad u\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}) with k > 0 , k>0, N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 , p ∈ [ 2 ​ ( N + 1 ) N − 1 , 2 ​ N N − 2 ) p\in\left[\left.\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1},\frac{2N}{N-2}\right)\right. and Q ∈ L ∞ ​ ( ℝ N ) Q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}) . Due to the sign-changes of Q Q , our solutions have infinite Morse-Index in the corresponding dual variational formulation.



1
Introduction

In the present article, we consider nonlinear Helmholtz equations of the form

(1.1)

-\Delta u-k^{2}u=Q(x)|u|^{p-2}u\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{N}

for
p\in\left[\left.\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1},\frac{2N}{N-2}\right)\right.
and
k>0
with a weight function
Q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
that may change sign. To allow for the latter is nontrivial given that one of the
main tools for proving the existence of solutions is the dual variational method that, in its classical
form, relies on the nonnegativity of the weight function. In the context of Nonlinear Helmholtz equations it
was first implemented in a paper by Evéquoz and Weth
[
4
]
.
To highlight the role of the
nonnegativity of
Q
we briefly recapitulate the approach.

Instead of (
1.1
) one considers a reformulation as the integral equation

(1.2)

u=\textbf{R}(Q|u|^{p-2}u)\qquad u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
R
is the real part of a resolvent type operator
{\mathcal{R}}
, i.e., a right inverse of the
Helmholtz operator
-\Delta-k^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the operator
{\mathcal{R}}
is given by
{\mathcal{R}}(f)=\Phi\ast f
where

\Phi(x):=\frac{\text{i}}{4}\left(\frac{k}{2\pi|x|}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}H^{(1)}_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(k|x|),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}

is the fundamental solution of the Helmholtz equation associated with
Sommerfeld’s outgoing radiation
condition

(1.3)

\left|\nabla\Phi(x)-k\,\text{i}\Phi(x)\frac{x}{|x|}\right|=o(|x|^{\frac{1-N}{2}}),\quad\text{ as }|x|\to\infty.

Here,
H^{(1)}_{\frac{N-2}{2}}
denotes the Hankel function of the first kind and order
\frac{N-2}{2}
.
So the operator
R
from (
1.2
) is given by
\textbf{R}(f)=\Psi\ast f
where

\Psi:=\text{Re}(\Phi)
is given by

(1.4)

\Psi(x)=-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{k}{2\pi|x|}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}Y_{\frac{2-N}{2}}(k|x|),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}

It is
known
[
7
, Theorem 2.3]
that
\mathcal{R}
extends as a continuous linear map from

L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
precisely for
p\in\left[\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1},\frac{2N}{N-2}\right]
.
One then introduces the dual variable
\tilde{u}:=Q^{1/p^{\prime}}|u|^{p-2}u
and observes that solutions of
(
1.2
) are precisely the critical points of the (dual) energy functional
I:L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
given by

I(\tilde{u}):=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\left\|\tilde{u}\right\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tilde{u}{\mathcal{K}}\tilde{u}\penalty\ dx.

Here,
{\mathcal{K}}:L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\tilde{u}\mapsto Q^{\frac{1}{p}}\textbf{R}(Q^{\frac{1}{p}}\tilde{u})
is a symmetric operator in the sense of

(1.5)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f\,{\mathcal{K}}g\penalty\ dx=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g\,{\mathcal{K}}f\penalty\ dx\qquad\text{for all }f,g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

see
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
. Under the additional assumption that
Q
vanishes at
infinity, one obtains that
I
is an odd functional of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
that has the Mountain Pass Geometry and
satisfies the Palais-Smale Condition. So the existence of an unbounded sequence of solutions
to (
1.2
) follows from the Symmetric Mountain Pass Theorem.
Inverting the transformation
u\mapsto\tilde{u}
one thus obtains an unbounded sequence of solutions to the
nonlinear Helmholtz equation (
1.1
). This is the strategy proposed by Evéquoz and
Weth
[
4
]
for the focusing nonlinear Helmholtz equation
Q\geq 0
. We refer
to
[
9
]
for analogous results in the defocusing case
Q\leq 0
, where the dual variational
approach was implemented for the dual variable
\tilde{u}:=|Q|^{1/p^{\prime}}|u|^{p-2}u
. In view of these two
results it is natural to ask for a dual variational approach work in the intermediate case of sign-changing

Q
. In this paper, we provide a solution for this problem.

To treat sign-changing coefficients
Q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
we have to come up with a new idea to make the
dual variation approach work. We write
Q=Q_{+}-Q_{-}
where
Q_{\pm}:=|Q|\mathds{1}_{A_{\pm}}
and

(1.6)

A_{+}:=\{Q>0\},\qquad A_{-}:=\{Q\leq 0\}.

In fact we will consider
Q_{\lambda}:=\lambda Q_{+}-Q_{-}
for
\lambda>0
in the following. Our main
idea is to introduce a tuple of dual variables
(\varphi,\psi)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})\times L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
associated with

(u|_{A_{+}},u|_{A_{-}})
and to derive a coupled system of nonlinear integral equations the solutions of which
are precisely the critical points of an associated strongly indefinite dual energy
functional. We will see that the indefiniteness comes from the presence of
Q_{-}
and thus vanishes in the case
of a nonnegative function
Q\geq 0
. In particular, the critical points of this dual energy functional
will have infinite Morse index, which clearly distinguishes these solutions from the dual bound and ground
states obtained in
[
4
]
in the case
Q\geq 0
. We will explain the dual variational framework in detail in
Section
2
.
Our conditions for the existence of critical points involve the linear operator

{\mathcal{K}}:L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),f\mapsto|Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}\textbf{R}(|Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}f)
as well as the numbers

(1.7)

\alpha:=\max\limits_{\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p^{\prime}}=1,\atop\supp(\varphi)\subset A_{+}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi{\mathcal{K}}\varphi\penalty\ dx,\qquad\beta:=\max\limits_{\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p^{\prime}}=\left\|\psi\right\|_{p^{\prime}}=1,\atop\supp(\varphi)\subset A_{+},\supp(\psi)\subset A_{-}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi{\mathcal{K}}\psi\penalty\ dx.

Since we will assume
{\mathcal{K}}
to be compact, both values are indeed attained.
Moreover we have
\beta\geq 0

and
[
4
, Lemma 4.2(ii)]
gives
\alpha>0
once we assume that
A^{+}
has positive measure, i.e.,
Q^{+}\not\equiv 0
. Our
main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\in[\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1},\frac{2N}{N-2})
and
Q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
Q^{+}\not\equiv 0
. Moreover assume that

(1.8)

{\mathcal{K}}:L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\text{ is compact and }\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi{\mathcal{K}}\psi\penalty\ dx\geq 0\text{ for all }\psi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-}).

Then for almost all
\lambda>\lambda_{0}:=(2\beta\alpha^{-1})^{p}
there is a nontrivial strong solution
u\in W^{2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
q\in\left(\frac{2N}{N-1},\infty\right)
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
of

(1.9)

-\Delta u-k^{2}u=Q_{\lambda}(x)|u|^{p-2}u\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The proof relies on a combination of a saddle-point reduction and the abstract monotonicity trick by
Jeanjean-Toland
[
6
]
, which provides bounded Palais-Smale sequences (only) for almost all
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
. It would clearly be desirable
to extend our result to all
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
, but related a priori bounds seem to be out of reach. Notice also that

[
9
, Theorem 1.4]
suggests the existence of nontrivial solutions also for small
\lambda>0
,
possibly assuming the set
\{Q>0\}
to be small enough and following a different variational approach.
Let us point out that
\lambda_{0}
is small provided that the number
\beta
, which is the same as the
operator norm
\|\mathds{1}_{A_{+}}{\mathcal{K}}(\mathds{1}_{A_{-}})\|_{p^{\prime}\to p}
, is small compared to
\alpha>0
.
In the case
p>\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}
this can be achieved by considering coefficient functions
Q
such that

\dist(A_{-},A_{+})
is large enough, see
[
5
, Lemma 2.6]
.

In the following Corollary, we show that the abstract conditions (
1.8
) hold for a
reasonable class of sign-changing functions
Q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. If for instance
Q
vanishes at infinity,
then
[
4
, Lemma 4.1(ii)]
applied to
|Q|
implies that
{\mathcal{K}}:L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is
compact. It is less immediate to verify the non-negativity assumption on the bilinear form
(
1.8
).
From
[
2
, Corollary 5.4]
we infer that this condition holds for measurable sets
A_{-}
with
small enough diameter. To be more precise, if
y_{\frac{N-2}{2}}
denotes the first (positive) zero of the
Bessel function
Y_{\frac{N-2}{2}}
, then the
condition
\diam(A_{-})\leq k^{-1}y_{\frac{N-2}{2}}
is sufficient. To put this condition into
perspective, note that for
N=3
we have
Y_{\frac{1}{2}}(t)=-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi t}}\cos{t}
, thus
y_{1/2}=\pi/2
and
y_{\frac{N-2}{2}}>y_{1/2}
for
N>3
(see
[
1
, Section 9.5]
).
We thus conclude as follows.

Corollary 1.2
.

Assume
p\in[\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1},\frac{2N}{N-2})
and
Q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),Q^{+}\not\equiv 0
. Moreover assume

(1.10)

\lim_{R\to\infty}\esssup_{|x|\geq R}|Q(x)|=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\diam{(A_{-})}\leq k^{-1}y_{\frac{N-2}{2}}.

Then for almost all
\lambda>\lambda_{0}:=(2\beta\alpha^{-1})^{p}
there is a nontrivial strong
solution
u\in W^{2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
q\in\left(\frac{2N}{N-1},\infty\right)
and

\gamma\in(0,1)
of (
1.9
).

The regularity results in Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
are direct
consequences of
[
4
, Lemma 4.3]
and of the iteration procedure from Step 3 in the proof of

[
8
, Theorem 1]
. Notice that
[
8
, Theorem 1]
provides solutions
to far more general Nonlinear Helmholtz equations than (
1.1
) regardless of whether sign-changes occur or not,
but the constructed solutions are small.
This result relies on a fixed point approach. Let us also mention
[
3
]
where nontrivial solutions
of Nonlinear Helmholtz equations are constructed for rather general and possibly sign-changing nonlinearities
vanishing identically outside some compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Our method is entirely different from any of
these approaches.

This paper is organzied as follows: In Section 2 we introduce our basic tools and develop the dual
variational framework by reformulating the indefinite Nonlinear Helmholtz equation as a coupled system of
integral equations. Then we prove that nontrivial critical points of the associated energy functional

J_{\lambda}
are indeed nontrivial solutions
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of the integral equation

u=\textbf{R}(Q_{\lambda}|u|^{p-2}u)
. This motivates the search for critical points of
J_{\lambda}
.
In Section 3 we perform the saddle-point reduction of
(\varphi,\psi)\mapsto J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi)
leading to a
reduced function
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}
that depends on
\varphi
only. In Section 4 we establish the existence of
bounded Palais-Smale sequences for these reduced functionals for almost all
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
. As
mentioned above, this step entirely relies on the monotonicity trick by Jeanjean and
Toland
[
6
]
. Finally, we combine all the auxiliary results to prove Theorem
1.1

and Corollary
1.2
in Section 5.

Let us close this introduction by fixing some notation: Throughout the paper we denote by
B_{r}(x)
the open
ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with radius
r>0
and center at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Moreover, we set
B_{r}=B_{r}(0)
for any
r>0
. For
1\leq s\leq\infty
, we abbreviate the standard norm
on
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{s}
. The Schwartz-class of rapidly decreasing functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is
denoted by
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. For any
p\in(1,\infty)
we always denote by
p^{\prime}:=\frac{p}{p-1}
the Hölder
conjugate of
p
. The indicator function of a measurable set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is
\mathds{1}_{B}
. By
\diam{(B)}
we
always denote the diametere of a set. We will always use the symbols

\varphi,\psi
to denote
L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})-
and
L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
-functions that are continued trivially to the whole
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

2
Dual variational formulation

In this section we will formulate a variational framework to the equation (
1.9
).
We recall from the introduction that solutions of our problem are obtained as solutions of the integral
equation

(2.1)

u=\textbf{R}(Q_{\lambda}|u|^{p-2}u),\qquad\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

where
\textbf{R}(f)=\Psi\ast f
for the function
\Psi
introduced in (
1.4
) and

(2.2)

\left\|\textbf{R}(f)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

for all
p\in\left[\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1},\frac{2N}{N-2}\right]
and some constant
C>0
.

To obtain the dual variational formualation of (
2.1
) we introduce
v:=\mathds{1}_{A_{+}}u
and
w:=\mathds{1}_{A_{-}}u
. Then (
2.1
) is equivalent to the system

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} v&=\lambda\mathds{1}_{A_{+}}\textbf{R}\left[Q_{+}|v|^{p-2}v\right]-\mathds{1}_{A_{+}}\textbf{R}\left[Q_{-}|w|^{p-2}w\right],\\
w&=\lambda\mathds{1}_{A_{-}}\textbf{R}\left[Q_{+}|v|^{p-2}v\right]-\mathds{1}_{A_{-}}\textbf{R}\left[Q_{-}|w|^{p-2}w\right].\end{aligned}

Setting

\varphi:=\lambda Q_{+}^{1/p^{\prime}}|v|^{p-2}v\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+}),\qquad\psi:=Q_{-}^{1/p^{\prime}}|w|^{p-2}w\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})

we deduce

\displaystyle\lambda^{1-p^{\prime}}|\varphi|^{p^{\prime}-2}\varphi

\displaystyle=Q_{+}^{1/p}v

\displaystyle=\lambda Q_{+}^{1/p}\textbf{R}\left[Q_{+}|v|^{p-2}v\right]-Q_{+}^{1/p}\textbf{R}\left[Q_{-}|w|^{p-2}w\right]

\displaystyle=Q_{+}^{1/p}\textbf{R}\left[Q_{+}^{1/p}\varphi\right]-Q_{+}^{1/p}\textbf{R}\left[Q_{-}^{1/p}\psi\right]

\displaystyle=Q_{+}^{1/p}\textbf{R}\left[|Q|^{1/p}(\varphi-\psi)\right].

Similarly

\displaystyle|\psi|^{p^{\prime}-2}\psi

\displaystyle=Q_{-}^{1/p}\textbf{R}\left[|Q|^{1/p}(\varphi-\psi)\right].

In terms of the Birman-Schwinger operator
{\mathcal{K}}:f\mapsto|Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}\textbf{R}\big(|Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}f\big)

introduced above this can be reformulated as

\displaystyle\lambda^{1-p^{\prime}}|\varphi|^{p^{\prime}-2}\varphi

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}_{A_{+}}{\mathcal{K}}(\varphi-\psi),

\displaystyle|\psi|^{p^{\prime}-2}\psi

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}_{A_{-}}{\mathcal{K}}(\varphi-\psi)

and therefore carries a variational structure through the (dual) energy functional
J_{\lambda}
on
L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})\times L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
given by

(2.3)

J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi):=\frac{\lambda^{1-p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\left\|\varphi\right\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\left\|\psi\right\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\varphi-\psi){\mathcal{K}}(\varphi-\psi)\penalty\ dx.

This functional is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
with

\displaystyle\partial_{1}J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi)[h_{1}]

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\lambda^{1-p^{\prime}}|\varphi|^{p^{\prime}-2}\varphi-{\mathcal{K}}(\varphi-\psi)\right)h_{1}\penalty\ dx,

\displaystyle h_{1}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})

\displaystyle\partial_{2}J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi)[h_{2}]

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(-|\psi|^{p^{\prime}-2}\psi-{\mathcal{K}}(\varphi-\psi)\right)h_{2}\penalty\ dx,

\displaystyle h_{2}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-}).

Here
\partial_{1},\partial_{2}
standard for partial derivatives with respect to
\varphi
and
\psi
.
For this reason we will look for critical points of
J_{\lambda}
. These solve the integral equation (
2.1
). Thus by the regularity results
[
4
, Lemma 4.3]
and
[
8
, p.13]
these are indeed strong solutions to our original problem (
1.9
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(\varphi,\psi)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})\times L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})\setminus\{(0,0)\}
be a critical point of
J_{\lambda}

where
\lambda>0
. Then

u:=\mathrm{{\bf R}}\left(|Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}(\varphi-\psi)\right)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

is a nontrivial solution of (
2.1
).

Proof.

Let
(\varphi,\psi)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})\times L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})\setminus\{(0,0)\}
be a critical point of
J_{\lambda}
. Thus we have

\lambda^{1-p^{\prime}}|\varphi|^{p^{\prime}-2}\varphi=\mathds{1}_{A_{+}}{\mathcal{K}}(\varphi-\psi),\qquad|\psi|^{p^{\prime}-2}\psi=\mathds{1}_{A_{-}}{\mathcal{K}}(\varphi-\psi)

as well as

\displaystyle Q_{\lambda}|u|^{p-2}u

\displaystyle=(\lambda\mathds{1}_{A_{+}}-\mathds{1}_{A_{-}})|Q||u|^{p-2}u

\displaystyle=(\lambda\mathds{1}_{A_{+}}-\mathds{1}_{A_{-}})|Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot||Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}u|^{p-2}\cdot|Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}u

\displaystyle=(\lambda\mathds{1}_{A_{+}}-\mathds{1}_{A_{-}})|Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot||Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}\textbf{R}\left[|Q|^{1/p}(\varphi-\psi)\right]|^{p-2}\cdot|Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}\textbf{R}\left[|Q|^{1/p}(\varphi-\psi)\right]

\displaystyle=(\lambda\mathds{1}_{A_{+}}-\mathds{1}_{A_{-}})|Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot|{\mathcal{K}}\left[\varphi-\psi\right]|^{p-2}\cdot{\mathcal{K}}\left[\varphi-\psi\right]

\displaystyle=(\lambda\mathds{1}_{A_{+}}-\mathds{1}_{A_{-}})|Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\left|\lambda^{1-p^{\prime}}|\varphi|^{p^{\prime}-2}\varphi+|\psi|^{p^{\prime}-2}\psi\right|^{p-2}\cdot\left(\lambda^{1-p^{\prime}}|\varphi|^{p^{\prime}-2}\varphi+|\psi|^{p^{\prime}-2}\psi\right)

\displaystyle=(\lambda\mathds{1}_{A_{+}}-\mathds{1}_{A_{-}})|Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\left(\lambda^{(1-p^{\prime})(p-1)}\varphi+\psi\right)

\displaystyle=|Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\left(\varphi-\psi\right).

Applying
R
then gives

\textbf{R}\left(Q_{\lambda}|u|^{p-2}u\right)=\textbf{R}\left(|Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}(\varphi-\psi)\right)=u.

Hence
u
solves (
2.1
).
∎

So we conclude that it remains to find nontrivial critical points of the functionals
J_{\lambda}
for as many

\lambda>0
as possible. This will be achieved with the Mountain Pass Theorem for families of

{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
-functionals by Jeanjean and Toland
[
6
]
.

3
Saddle-point reduction

In this section we perform the saddle-point reduction of
J_{\lambda}
with respect to the
\psi
-variable. To
this end, we prove that for any fixed
\varphi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})
the functional
\psi\mapsto J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi)
attains its maximum at some uniquely defined function in
L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
that we will call
Z(\varphi)
in the
following.
We shall see that the positivity assumption
\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi{\mathcal{K}}\psi\penalty\ dx\geq 0
for all
\psi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
ensures that the functional
\psi\mapsto J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi)
is
strictly concave so that the global maximization with respect to
\psi
is the only reasonable approach to
perform a saddle point reduction. We introduce the reduced functional
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}:L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})\to\mathbb{R}
via

(3.1)

\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi):=\sup\limits_{\psi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})}J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi).

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{K}}:L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is compact and that
\int\limits_{A_{-}}\psi{\mathcal{K}}\psi\penalty\ dx\geq 0
for all
\psi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
. Then for every
\varphi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})
there exists a unique

Z(\varphi)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
such that for all
\lambda>0
we have

\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi)=J_{\lambda}(\varphi,Z(\varphi)).

Moreover:

(i)

For any
\varphi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})
the corresponding maximizer
Z(\varphi)
satisfies

(3.2)

\left\|Z(\varphi)\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq\left(p^{\prime}\beta\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}-1}}

where
\beta
is defined in (
1.7
).

(ii)

The map
Z:L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})\to L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
is continuous.

(iii)

The reduced functional
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}:L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})\to\mathbb{R}
is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
with derivative

\tilde{J}_{\lambda}^{\prime}[h]=\partial_{1}J_{\lambda}(\varphi,Z(\varphi))[h].

Proof.

We first establish the existence of a maximizer.
So fix
\varphi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})
and consider a maximizing
sequence
(\psi_{n})_{n}\subset L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
. Using
J_{\lambda}(\varphi,0)\leq\sup\limits_{\psi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})}J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi)=J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi_{n})+o(1)
as
n\to\infty
we obtain

o(1)\leq-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\|\psi_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\mathcal{K}\psi_{n}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{n}\mathcal{K}\psi_{n}\leq-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\|\psi_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}+\beta\|\varphi\|_{p^{\prime}}\|\psi_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}\quad(n\to\infty).

Here we used the nonnegativity assumption on
{\mathcal{K}}
as well as (
2.2
). Hence,

(3.3)

\|\psi_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq(p^{\prime}\beta\|\varphi\|)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}-1}}+o(1)\qquad(n\to\infty),

so
(\psi_{n})_{n}
is bounded. Passing to a subsequence we find
\psi^{\ast}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
such that

\psi_{n}\rightharpoonup\psi^{\ast}
in
L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
as
n\to\infty.
Using the compactness of
{\mathcal{K}}
and
the weak lower semicontinuity of the norm we find

\displaystyle\sup_{\psi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})}J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\lambda^{1-p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\|\varphi\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\|\psi_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\mathcal{K}\varphi\penalty\ dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\mathcal{K}\psi_{n}\penalty\ dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{n}\mathcal{K}\psi_{n}\penalty\ dx+o(1)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\lambda^{1-p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\|\varphi\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\|\psi_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\mathcal{K}\varphi\penalty\ dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\mathcal{K}\psi^{\ast}\penalty\ dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi^{\ast}\mathcal{K}\psi^{\ast}\penalty\ dx+o(1)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{\lambda^{1-p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\|\varphi\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\|\psi^{\ast}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\mathcal{K}\varphi\penalty\ dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\mathcal{K}\psi^{\ast}\penalty\ dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi^{\ast}\mathcal{K}\psi^{\ast}\penalty\ dx+o(1)

\displaystyle\quad=J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi^{\ast})+o(1).

Hence the supremum is attained at
\psi^{\ast}
. Since equality must hold in the above estimate we conclude

\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\to\left\|\psi^{\ast}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}
, whence
\psi_{n}\to\psi^{\ast}
in

L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
as
n\to\infty
. This shows the existence of a maximizer satisfying the estimate stated
in (i). So (i) is proved once we have established the uniqueness of the maximizer.

To this end assume that
\psi^{\ast},\psi^{\dagger}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
are maximizers. Then we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi^{\ast})+\frac{1}{2}J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi^{\dagger})-J_{\lambda}\left(\varphi,\frac{1}{2}(\psi^{\ast}+\psi^{\dagger})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\left(\left\|\frac{\psi^{\ast}+\psi^{\dagger}}{2}\right\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\|\psi^{\ast}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\|\psi^{\dagger}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\psi^{\ast}+\psi^{\dagger}}{2}\mathcal{K}\left[\frac{\psi^{\ast}+\psi^{\dagger}}{2}\right]\>\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi^{\ast}\mathcal{K}\psi^{\ast}\>\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi^{\dagger}\mathcal{K}\psi^{\dagger}\>\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\left(\left\|\frac{\psi^{\ast}+\psi^{\dagger}}{2}\right\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\|\psi^{\ast}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\|\psi^{\dagger}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}\right)-\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\psi^{\ast}-\psi^{\dagger})\mathcal{K}[\psi^{\ast}-\psi^{\dagger}]\>\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\left(\left\|\frac{\psi^{\ast}+\psi^{\dagger}}{2}\right\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\|\psi^{\ast}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\|\psi^{\dagger}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 0,

where we have used the non-negativity condition in the second last step and the convexity of
z\mapsto|z|^{p^{\prime}}

in the last step. So we have equality in each estimate and conclude
\psi^{\ast}=\psi^{\dagger}
.
Note that the maximizer does not depend on
\lambda
since the only
\lambda
-dependent term in

J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi)
is
\frac{\lambda^{1-p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\|\varphi\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}
, which is independent of
\psi
.

We now prove (ii), i.e., the continuity of the map
Z
: Assume
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
in
L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})
and let

(\psi_{n})_{n}:=(Z(\varphi_{n}))_{n}\subset L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
be the associated maximizers. By (
3.3
), the
sequence
(\psi_{n})_{n}
is bounded and after passing to a subsequence we may assume
\psi_{n}\rightharpoonup\psi_{0}
in

L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
as
n\to\infty
. Arguing as above we deduce

(3.4)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi_{n})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}J_{\lambda}(\varphi_{n},\psi_{n})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi_{n})\leq J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi_{0})\leq\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi).

using weak lower semicontinuity and
\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\geq\left\|\psi_{0}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}
. On the other hand, with the special choice
\psi=Z(\varphi)
we obtain

(3.5)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi_{n})\geq J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi)=J_{\lambda}(\varphi,Z(\varphi))=\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi).

Combining both estimates gives
\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\to\left\|\psi_{0}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}
as well as

\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi_{n})\to\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi)
as
n\to\infty
. Thus we have equality in
(
3.4
), (
3.5
). Since maximizers are unique, we obtain
\psi_{0}=Z(\varphi)
and
in particular
Z(\varphi_{n})=\psi_{n}\to\psi_{0}=Z(\varphi_{0})
in
L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
as
n\to\infty
.

We are left to prove (iii). Let
h\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})
be arbitrary. We can estimate the difference quotients as follows:

\displaystyle\liminf_{\tau\to 0}\frac{\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi+\tau h)-\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi)}{\tau}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\tau\to 0}\frac{J_{\lambda}(\varphi+\tau h,Z(\varphi))-J_{\lambda}(\varphi,Z(\varphi))}{\tau}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\tau\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{1}J_{\lambda}(\varphi+\tau\sigma h,Z(\varphi))[h]\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}J_{\lambda}(\varphi,Z(\varphi))[h],

\displaystyle\limsup_{\tau\to 0}\frac{\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi+\tau h)-\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi)}{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\tau\to 0}\frac{J_{\lambda}(\varphi+\tau h,Z(\varphi+\tau h))-J_{\lambda}(\tilde{\varphi},Z(\varphi+\tau h))}{\tau}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\tau\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{1}J_{\lambda}(\varphi^{*}+\tau\sigma h,Z(\varphi+\tau h))[h]\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}J_{\lambda}(\varphi,Z(\varphi))[h].

Here we used that
Z
is continuous and that
\partial_{1}J_{\lambda}
is continuous,
see
[
10
, Proposition 9]
for a similar computation. We
conclude that
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}
is Gâteaux-differentiable with continuous derivative
\varphi\mapsto\partial_{1}J_{\lambda}(\varphi,Z(\varphi))[\cdot]
, see Proposition
3.1
(iii).
Hence, the reduced functional
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}
is continuously (Fréchet-)differentiable with

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\varphi)[h]=\partial_{1}J_{\lambda}(\varphi,Z(\varphi))[h]\qquad\forall h\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})

as claimed.
∎

Notice that the condition
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi\mathcal{K}\psi\>\mathrm{d}x\geq 0
is also necessary for the existence of
a global maximizer of
\psi\mapsto J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi)
because otherwise this functional is unbounded from above.

4
Palais-Smale sequences for the reduced functional

In view of the results of the previous sections, we obtain a solution to our problem by
proving the existence of a nontrivial critical point of the reduced functional
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}:L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})\to\mathbb{R}
introduced in (
3.1
). This will be done via Mountain-pass techniques for monotone
families of functionals originating from the work of Jeanjean and Toland
[
6
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
M\subset\mathbb{R}
a compact interval. Then the family
(I_{\nu})_{\nu\in M}
of
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
-functionals on
X
is said to have the Mountain Pass Geometry if there exist
v_{1},v_{2}\in X

such that for all
\nu\in M
it holds

c_{\nu}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}I_{\nu}(\gamma(t))>\max\{I_{\nu}(v_{1}),I_{\nu}(v_{2})\},

where
\Gamma:=\{\gamma\in C([0,1],X):\gamma(0)=v_{1},\gamma(1)=v_{2}\}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

(Jeanjean, Toland)
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]

Assume that
X
is a Banach space,
M\subset\mathbb{R}
a compact interval and

(I_{\nu})_{\nu\in M}
a family of
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
-functionals on
X
having the Mountain Pass Geometry. Assume
further that
(I_{\nu})_{\nu\in M}
has the following property:

(
H
)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\text{For every sequence }(\nu_{n},\varphi_{n})\in M\times X\text{ with }\nu_{n}\nearrow\nu_{*}\in M\text{ and with }\\
&\quad-I_{\nu_{*}}(\varphi_{n}),\quad I_{\nu_{n}}(\varphi_{n}),\quad\frac{I_{\nu_{n}}(\varphi_{n})-I_{\nu_{*}}(\varphi_{n})}{\nu_{*}-\nu_{n}}\quad\text{ bounded from above, }\\
&\text{the sequence }(\varphi_{n})\text{ is bounded itself, and }\limsup_{n\to\infty}(I_{\nu_{*}}(\varphi_{n})-I_{\nu_{n}}(\varphi_{n}))\leq 0.\end{aligned}

Then for almost all
\nu\in M
there is a bounded Palais-Smale sequence (BPS) for
I_{\nu}
at the level

c_{\nu}
.

We shall apply this result to
X=L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})
and the family of
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
-functionals

I_{\lambda}:=\tilde{J}_{\lambda}:X\to\mathbb{R}
. We first verify the Mountain Pass Geometry for
parameters
\lambda\in(\lambda_{0},\infty)
where
\lambda_{0}=(2\beta\alpha^{-1})^{p}
.
Let us recall that
\alpha,\beta
were defined as

\alpha:=\max\limits_{\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p^{\prime}}=1,\atop\supp(\varphi)\subset A_{+}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi{\mathcal{K}}\varphi\penalty\ dx,\qquad\beta:=\max\limits_{\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p^{\prime}}=\left\|\psi\right\|_{p^{\prime}}=1,\atop\supp(\varphi)\subset A_{+},\supp(\psi)\subset A_{-}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi{\mathcal{K}}\psi\penalty\ dx.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}:L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be compact and assume
\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi{\mathcal{K}}\psi\penalty\ dx\geq 0
for
all
\psi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
. Then, for any given compact subinterval
M\subset(\lambda_{0},\infty)
, the
family of functionals
(\tilde{J}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in M}
has the Mountain Pass Geometry according to
Definition
4.1
.

Proof.

For
\lambda\in M
we define
r_{\lambda}:=(\lambda^{p^{\prime}-1}\alpha)^{1/(p^{\prime}-2)}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\inf_{\|\varphi\|_{p^{\prime}}=r_{\lambda}}\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\|\varphi\|_{p^{\prime}}=r_{\lambda}}\sup_{\psi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})}J_{\lambda}(\varphi,\psi)\geq\inf_{\|\varphi\|_{p^{\prime}}=r_{\lambda}}J_{\lambda}(\varphi,0)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\|\varphi\|_{p^{\prime}}=r_{\lambda}}\frac{\lambda^{1-p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\|\varphi\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\mathcal{K}\varphi\>\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{1-p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}r_{\lambda}^{p^{\prime}}-\frac{\alpha}{2}r_{\lambda}^{2}=\alpha\left(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)(\lambda^{p^{\prime}-1}\alpha)^{\frac{2}{p^{\prime}-2}}

\displaystyle>0.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(0)=\sup_{\psi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})}J_{\lambda}(0,\psi)=\sup_{\psi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})}\left[-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\|\psi\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi\mathcal{K}\psi\>\mathrm{d}x\right]=0.

According to Definition
4.1
it therefore remains to find some

\varphi^{*}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})
with
\|\varphi^{*}\|_{p^{\prime}}\geq r_{\lambda_{0}}
such that

\tilde{J}_{\lambda_{0}}(\varphi^{*})\leq 0
holds. Notice that in this case we actually have
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi^{*})<\tilde{J}_{\lambda_{0}}(\varphi^{*})\leq 0
for all
\lambda\in M\subset(\lambda_{0},\infty)
.
To achieve this we estimate
\tilde{J}_{\lambda_{0}}
from above as follows

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{\lambda_{0}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=J_{\lambda_{0}}(\varphi,0)-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\|Z(\varphi)\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi{\mathcal{K}}(Z(\varphi))-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Z(\varphi)\mathcal{K}(Z(\varphi))

\displaystyle\leq J_{\lambda_{0}}(\varphi,0)-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\|Z(\varphi)\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}+\beta\|\varphi\|_{p^{\prime}}\|Z(\varphi)\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq J_{\lambda_{0}}(\varphi,0)+\frac{\beta^{p}}{p}\|\varphi\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p}

where we have used that
\max\limits_{c\geq 0}\left(-\frac{c^{p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}+\beta\left\|\varphi\right\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}c\right)=\frac{\beta^{p}}{p}\|\varphi\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p}.
We choose
\varphi^{*}=r_{0}\varphi_{0}
where the function
\varphi_{0}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+}),\|\varphi_{0}\|_{p^{\prime}}=1
attains the maximum
\alpha=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi_{0}\mathcal{K}\varphi_{0}\>\mathrm{d}x>0
. Then the choice
R:=(\frac{1}{2}\alpha\beta^{-p})^{1/(p-2)}
yields after some computations
(recall
\lambda_{0}=(2\beta\alpha^{-1})^{p}
)

\|\varphi^{*}\|_{p^{\prime}}=R=\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha\beta^{-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}\geq(\lambda_{0}^{p^{\prime}-1}\alpha)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}-2}}>(\lambda^{p^{\prime}-1}\alpha)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}-2}}=r_{\lambda}\qquad\text{for all }\lambda\in M.

Using again the explicit formulas for
R,\lambda_{0}
we find

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{\lambda_{0}}(R\varphi_{0})

\displaystyle\leq J_{\lambda_{0}}(R\varphi_{0})+\frac{\beta^{p}R^{p}}{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{0}^{1-p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}R^{p^{\prime}}-\frac{\alpha}{2}R^{2}+\frac{\beta^{p}}{p}R^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\cdot\left(\lambda_{0}^{1-p^{\prime}}-\frac{p\alpha}{2(p-1)}R^{2-p^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta^{p}}{p-1}R^{p-p^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\cdot\left(\lambda_{0}^{1-p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha R^{2-p^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\cdot\left((2\beta\alpha^{-1})^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha\beta^{-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)

\displaystyle=0

and thus the claim holds with
v_{1}=0
and
v_{2}=\varphi^{*}=R\varphi_{0}
.
∎

Having established the Mountain Pass Geometry of our functionals we now verify the
condition (
H
) in order to use Theorem
4.2

Proposition 4.4
.

For any compact subinterval
M\subset(\lambda_{0},\infty)
the family of
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}-
functionals

(\tilde{J}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in M}
satisfies the condition (
H
).

Proof.

Consider a sequence
(\varphi_{n},\lambda_{n})\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})\times M
with
\lambda_{n}\nearrow\lambda_{*}
and

\displaystyle-\tilde{J}_{\lambda_{*}}(\varphi_{n})\leq C,\quad\tilde{J}_{\lambda_{n}}(\varphi_{n})\leq C,\quad\frac{\tilde{J}_{\lambda_{n}}(\varphi_{n})-\tilde{J}_{\lambda_{*}}(\varphi_{n})}{\nu_{*}-\nu_{n}}\leq C

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we have

C\geq\frac{\tilde{J}_{\lambda_{n}}(\varphi_{n})-\tilde{J}_{\lambda^{*}}(\varphi_{n})}{\lambda_{*}-\lambda_{n}}=\frac{\lambda_{n}^{1-p^{\prime}}-\lambda_{*}^{1-p^{\prime}}}{\lambda_{*}-\lambda_{n}}\|\varphi_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}=\left((p^{\prime}-1)\lambda_{*}^{-p^{\prime}}+o(1)\right)\|\varphi_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}\qquad(n\to\infty)

So we conclude that
(\varphi_{n})
is bounded. Furthermore,
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda^{*}>0
gives

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,(I_{\lambda_{*}}(\varphi_{n})-I_{\lambda_{n}}(\varphi_{n}))=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,(\lambda_{n}^{1-p^{\prime}}-\lambda_{*}^{1-p^{\prime}})\|\varphi_{n}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}=0,

which is all we had to show.
∎

We thus conclude that Theorem
4.2
applies in our context and yields BPS sequences for

\tilde{J}_{\lambda}
at the corresponding Mountain pass levels
c_{\lambda}
for almost all
\lambda\in(\lambda_{0},\infty)
. From the existence of BPS sequences we deduce rather easily the existence of critical
points at the corresponding Mountain Pass level.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}:L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be compact and assume
\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi{\mathcal{K}}\psi\penalty\ dx\geq 0
for
all
\psi\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{-})
. Then for all
\lambda\in(\lambda_{0},\infty)
every BPS sequence of
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}
at its Mountain Pass level
c_{\lambda}

converges to a critical point of
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}
at the level
c_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

Let
(\varphi_{j})_{j}
in
L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})
be a BPS sequence for
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}
, i.e.,

\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi_{j})\to c>0
and
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\varphi_{j})\to 0
.
We may thus assume w.l.o.g.
\varphi_{j}\rightharpoonup\varphi^{*}
. Moreover,
Proposition
3.1
(i) implies the
boundedness of
(\psi_{j})_{j}:=(Z(\varphi_{j}))_{j}
and hence w.l.o.g. also weak convergence. For all
h\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})
we then
have, in view of the formula for
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}^{\prime}
from Proposition
3.1
(iii),

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\varphi_{j}|^{p^{\prime}-2}\varphi_{j}h-|\varphi_{k}|^{p^{\prime}-2}\varphi_{k}h\>\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\quad=\left|\tilde{J}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\varphi_{j})h-\tilde{J}^{\prime}(\varphi_{k})h+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h\mathcal{K}[\varphi_{j}-\varphi_{k}]\>\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h\mathcal{K}[\psi_{j}-\psi_{k}]\>\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|h\|_{p^{\prime}}\cdot\left[\|\tilde{J}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\varphi_{j})\|+\|\tilde{J}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\varphi_{k})\|+\|\mathcal{K}[\varphi_{j}-\varphi_{k}]\|_{p}+\|\mathcal{K}[\psi_{j}-\psi_{k}]\|_{p}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\|h\|_{p^{\prime}}\cdot o(1)\qquad(j,k\to\infty).

We infer that
(|\varphi_{j}|^{p^{\prime}-2}\varphi_{j})_{j}
converges strongly in
L^{p}(A_{+})
. By
uniqueness of weak limits, we infer
|\varphi_{j}|^{p^{\prime}-2}\varphi_{j}\to|\varphi^{*}|^{p^{\prime}-2}\varphi^{*}

strongly in
L^{p}(A_{+})
and hence in particular
\|\varphi_{j}\|_{p^{\prime}}\to\|\varphi^{*}\|_{p^{\prime}}
. This finally
implies
\varphi_{j}\to\varphi^{*}
strongly in
L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})
. A standard computation finally shows
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}(\varphi^{*})=c_{\lambda}
as well as
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\varphi^{*})=0
.
∎

5
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2

We finally combine all auxiliary results to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

From Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
3.1
we infer that
for almost all
\lambda\in(\lambda_{0},\infty)
a nontrivial solution
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of the nonlinear
Helmholtz equation (
1.9
) is found once we have proved the existence of nontrivial critical points of the
reduced functional
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}
for almost all
\lambda\in M
where
M
is an arbitrary compact
subinterval of
(\lambda_{0},\infty)
.
From Proposition
4.3
we infer that the family
(\tilde{J}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in M}
has the Mountain
Pass Geometry. Moreover, by Proposition
4.4
, condition (
H
) holds.
So Theorem
4.2
yields for almost all
\lambda\in M
a BPS sequence for

\tilde{J}_{\lambda}
at the corresponding Mountain Pass level. By Proposition
4.5
each of these
BPS sequences converges to a critical point
\varphi_{\lambda}
of
\tilde{J}_{\lambda}
at the Mountain Pass level.
Since this critical point is necessarily nontrivial, we have thus obtained the desired claim for

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-solutions of (
1.9
). From
[
4
, Lemma 4.3]
we infer that
each of these solutions belongs to
W^{2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
p\leq q<\infty
and

\alpha\in(0,1)
. Arguing as in Step 3 and Step 4
[
8
, p.13]
one even obtains that these
solutions belong to
W^{2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
q\in(\frac{2N}{N-1},p)
.
In particular, these solutions are strong solutions of (
1.9
), which
finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Collorary
1.2
.

In order to apply Theorem
1.1
we show that (
1.10
)
implies (
1.8
). In the special case
k=1
the compactness of
{\mathcal{K}}
was shown in
Lemma 4.2 in
[
4
]
. So the general case follows by rescaling. It therefore remains to show that

\delta:=\diam{(A_{-})}\leq k^{-1}y_{\frac{N-2}{2}}
implies
\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi{\mathcal{K}}\psi\penalty\ dx\geq 0

.
Due to (

),
\mathcal{K}=|Q|^{1/p}\mathcal{\textbf{R}}(|Q|^{1/p}\cdot)
and

it suffices to prove

(5.1)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi\textbf{R}\psi\penalty\ dx\geq 0,\qquad\text{ for all }\psi\in\mathcal{S}(A_{-}).

Using that

) and hence the Corollary.

Sobolev regularity theory for stochastic reaction-diffusion-advection equations with spatially homogeneous colored noises and infinitesimal generators of subordinate Brownian motions
Jae-Hwan Choi, Beom-Seok Han, Daehan Park

Abstract
. This article investigates the existence, uniqueness, and regularity of solutions to nonlinear stochastic reaction-diffusion-advection equations (SRDAEs) with spatially homogeneous colored noises and infinitesimal generators of subordinate Brownian motions in mixed norm L q ​ ( L p ) L_{q}(L_{p}) -spaces. We introduce a new condition—strongly reinforced Dalang’s condition—on colored noise, which facilitates a deeper understanding of the complicated relation between nonlinearities and stochastic forces. Additionally, we establish the space-time Hölder type regularity of solutions.

1.1.
Goal and Settings

Consider the following non-linear stochastic reaction-diffusion-advection equations (SRDAEs):

\partial_{t}u=\phi(\Delta)u+\zeta F(u)+\vec{b}\cdot\nabla_{x}(B(u))+\xi\varphi(u)\dot{W}\quad\text{on }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\quad u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}.

(1.1)

Here,

•

the operator

represents a pseudo-differential operator defined by

\phi(\Delta)f(x):=-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(|\xi|^{2})f(y)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(x-y)\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}y,

where
\phi

•

the random noise
\dot{W}
is a spatially homogeneous colored noise with a nonnegative definite correlation
\pi

•

,

and

and

Definition 1.1

An infinitely differentiable function

(-1)^{n}D^{n}\phi(\lambda)\leq 0,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\,\lambda\in(0,\infty).

Definition 1.2

(i)

(\pi,\phi\ast\bar{\tilde{\phi}})\geq 0,\quad\tilde{\phi}(x):=\phi(-x).

Here

(ii)

\mathbb{E}[W(\psi_{1})W(\psi_{2})]=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\pi,\psi_{1}(t,\cdot)\ast\bar{\tilde{\psi}}_{2}(t,\cdot))\mathrm{d}t.

Remark 1.3
.

The concept of spatially homogeneous colored noise

can be elucidated through an infinite series of Itô stochastic integrals. For any Schwartz function

W(\psi)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(t,x)(\pi\ast e_{k})(x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w_{t}^{k}.

Here,

denotes a sequence of independent one-dimensional Wiener processes, and

forms a real-valued complete orthonormal basis of the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
, which is derived from

.
Consequently, the noise term

can be extended to an

W(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}x):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\pi\ast e_{k})(x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w_{t}^{k}.

This formulation allows us to interpret equation (
1.1

\displaystyle(u(t,\cdot),\eta)

\displaystyle=(u_{0},\eta)+\int_{0}^{t}(u(s,\cdot),\phi(\Delta)\eta)+(\zeta(s,\cdot)F(s,u(s,\cdot)),\eta)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}(B(s,u(s,\cdot)),\vec{b}(s,\cdot)\cdot\nabla_{x}\eta)\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}(\xi(s,\cdot)\varphi(s,u(s,\cdot))\pi\ast e_{k},\eta)\mathrm{d}w_{s}^{k}.

Therefore, adopting Itô’s differential notation, the equation simplifies to

\mathrm{d}u=(\phi(\Delta)u+\zeta F(u)+\vec{b}\cdot\nabla_{x}(B(u)))\mathrm{d}t+\xi\varphi(u)(\pi\ast e_{k})\mathrm{d}w_{t}^{k};\quad u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}.

1.2.
Background

The study of the solvability of SRDAEs poses significant challenges due to the irregular perturbation from noise.
As such, the solvability of SRDAEs has attracted substantial interest from the mathematical research community.
This section revisits various contributions regarding the non-linear SRDAEs.

Case 1: Stochastic reaction-diffusion equations with a solely stochastic non-linear reaction term

\partial_{t}u=\mathcal{L}u+|u|^{1+\lambda_{\text{s.m.}}}\dot{W}.

When
d=1
,
\mathcal{L}
is a second-order elliptic operator, and
\dot{W}
is the space-time white noise, C. Mueller
[
44
]
established that for
0\leq\lambda_{\text{s.m.}}<\frac{1}{2}
, a mild solution exists globally in time on a finite spatial interval with periodic boundary conditions.
Moreover, C. Mueller
[
45
]
also demonstrated that for
\lambda_{\text{s.m.}}>\frac{1}{2}
, the mild solution exhibits finite-time explosion with non-zero probability under the same conditions.
In the critical scenario where
\lambda_{\text{s.m.}}=\frac{1}{2}
, M. Salins
[
54
]
showed that solution explosions are precluded within a periodic domain.
In instances where non-explosive solutions are considered, for
0\leq\lambda_{\text{s.m.}}<\frac{1}{2}
, significant results have emerged.
Notably, N.V. Krylov
[
39
, Section 8.4]
established the existence and Sobolev regularity of the strong solution over
\mathbb{R}
.
B.-S. Han
[
26
]
confirmed the existence, uniqueness, Sobolev regularity, and boundary behavior of strong solutions on a finite interval with Dirichlet boundary conditions.
Additionally, B.-S. Han
[
27
]
further substantiated the existence, uniqueness, and Sobolev regularity of strong solutions over
\mathbb{R}
with
\mathcal{L}=a\Delta^{\alpha/2}+bD+c
.
These results predominantly address the dynamics influenced by space-time white noise.
J.-H. Choi and B.-S. Han
[
9
]
demonstrated the existence, uniqueness, and Sobolev regularity of strong solutions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with spatially homogeneous colored noise
\dot{W}
and
\mathcal{L}=a^{ij}D^{ij}+b^{i}D^{i}+c
.
For further discussions on the range
-1<\lambda_{\text{s.m.}}<0
, the reader is referred to
[
4
,
46
,
47
]
.

Case 2: Stochastic reaction-diffusion equations with deterministic and stochastic reaction terms

\partial_{t}u=\mathcal{L}u+F(u)+\varphi(u)\dot{W}.

Assuming
\mathcal{L}
as a second-order elliptic operator and
\dot{W}
as spatially homogeneous colored noise, the scenario where

exhibits linear growth and

has polynomial growth with a negative leading coefficient has been addressed by S. Cerrai, G. Da Prato, and F. Flandoli
[
5
,
6
,
7
]

They proved the existence and uniqueness of mild solutions over bounded open sets in

with regular boundaries.
Additionally, in the case where

[

established the existence and uniqueness of the mild solution with space-time white noise.
L. Chen and J. Huang
[
8

extended these works to settings with spatially homogeneous colored noise under reinforced Dalang’s condition.
When

and

[

Inspired by the result of Salins
[

]
, which suggests that larger values of

[

validated the existence, uniqueness, and Sobolev regularity of strong solutions with

Recently, A. Agresti and M.C. Veraar
[

Case 3: Stochastic reaction-diffusion-advection equations

\partial_{t}u=\mathcal{L}u+F(u)+\vec{b}\cdot\nabla_{x}(B(u))+\varphi(u)\dot{W}.

Assuming that

Strong solutions have also been extensively investigated.

Their findings were later generalized to bounded convex domains in

J.A. León, D. Nualart, and R. Pettersson

P. Lewis and D. Nualart

Most recently, B.-S. Han

Further developments with the time-fractional derivative were reported in

For an abstract semigroup approach to SRDAEs, we refer the reader to

Despite significant advancements in the study of SRDAEs as evidenced by the results discussed previously, several fundamental and intriguing questions remain unaddressed.
These questions include:

(1)

Non-local operators
: Beyond the findings of B.-S. Han
[
27
]
, research has predominantly focused on second-order elliptic operators.
This observation prompts the question:
Is it feasible to establish solvability for SRDAEs that involve infinitesimal generators of subordinate Brownian motions?

(2)

Regularity theory in mixed Norm
L_{q}(L_{p})
-Spaces
: Aside from the contributions of A. Agresti and M.C. Veraar
[
1
,
2
]
, existing research primarily addresses regularity within
L_{p}
-spaces.
This situation leads to the question:
Is it possible to extend solvability results to SRDAEs in mixed norm
L_{q}(L_{p})
-spaces?

(3)

Nonlinear terms
: Prior studies have not considered the presence of three polynomial growth nonlinear terms,
F
,
B
, and
\varphi
.
Consequently, we ask:
Is it feasible to establish solvability for SRDAEs featuring three polynomial growth nonlinear terms
F
,
B
, and
\varphi
?

(4)

Spatially homogeneous colored noise
: Most results concerning spatially homogeneous colored noise rely on the reinforced Dalang’s condition.
This consideration leads to the questions:
Can we characterize the reinforced Dalang’s condition effectively?
If so, how can this characterization facilitate the resolution of SRDAEs?

This article is dedicated to addressing the above questions, aiming to fill the gaps identified in current research on SRDAEs.

On (4),
rather than assuming the reinforced Dalang condition abstractly, we
characterize
it via an explicit, verifiable integrability criterion on the correlation measure—Assumption
1.5
(\delta_{0},\gamma,r)
(equivalently,
\nu_{\delta_{0},\gamma,r}<\infty

This parameterized form, through the noise smoothness

1.3.
Conditions on spatially homogeneous colored noise

This subsection details the conditions imposed on spatially homogeneous colored noise
W
characterized by a nonnegative definite correlation
\pi
.
Here is our main assumption on correlation
\pi
.
Throughout this article, we always set

\delta_{0}\in(0,1],\quad\gamma\in(0,1)\quad\text{and}\quad r\in[1,d/(d-\delta_{0}(1-\gamma))).

Assumption 1.5

(
\delta_{0},\gamma,r
)
.

The spatially homogeneous colored noise

(i)

The correlation

(ii)

\text{no specific conditions on $\pi$,}\quad&\text{if }\alpha_{\delta_{0},\gamma,r}>d/2,\end{cases}

Conversely, since
|x|<1
, utilizing (
1.7
),

\displaystyle(R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}^{r}\ast R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}^{r})(x)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|x|/2<|y|<3|x|/2}R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-y)^{r}R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(y)^{r}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq N|x|^{\alpha_{\delta_{0},\gamma,r}-d}\int_{|x|/2<|y|<3|x|/2}R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-y)^{r}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq N|x|^{\alpha_{\delta_{0},\gamma,r}-d}\int_{|x-y|<|x|/2}R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-y)^{r}\mathrm{d}y=N|x|^{2\alpha_{\delta_{0},\gamma,r}-d}.

Case 3.
Consider the case where
\alpha_{\delta_{0},\gamma,r}=d/2
.
The objective in this scenario is to demonstrate the existence of positive constants

and

such that

c\log(1/|x|)\leq(R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}^{r}\ast R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}^{r})(x)\leq C(1+\log(1/|x|)),\quad\forall|x|<1.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle(R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}^{r}\ast R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}^{r})(x)

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{|y|\leq 2}|y|^{r(\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)-d)}|R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-y)|^{r}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad+N\int_{|y|>2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|y|}{2}}|R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-y)|^{r}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{|y|\leq 2}|y|^{-d/2}|R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-y)|^{r}\mathrm{d}y+N\|R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}\|_{L_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Given that
\|R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}\|_{L_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty
, our analysis focuses predominantly on

\int_{|y|\leq 2}|y|^{-d/2}|R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-y)|^{r}\mathrm{d}y.

Since
|x|<1
, we have
|x-y|\leq 3
, and thus

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\leq 2}|y|^{-d/2}|R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-y)|^{r}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{|y|\leq 2}|y|^{-d/2}|x-y|^{-d/2}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=N\int_{|y|\leq|x|/2}\cdots\mathrm{d}y+N\int_{|x|/2<|y|\leq 2|x|}\cdots\mathrm{d}y+N\int_{2|x|<|y|\leq 2}\cdots\mathrm{d}y.

By (

\int_{|y|\leq|x|/2}|y|^{-d/2}|x-y|^{-d/2}\mathrm{d}y+\int_{|x|/2<|y|\leq 2|x|}|y|^{-d/2}|x-y|^{-d/2}\mathrm{d}y\leq N,

where

remains independent of

For

, it holds that

\frac{|y|}{2}\leq|y|-|x|\leq|x-y|\leq|x|+|y|<\frac{3|y|}{2}.

Thus,

\int_{2|x|<|y|\leq 2}|y|^{-d/2}|x-y|^{-d/2}\mathrm{d}y\simeq\int_{2|x|<|y|\leq 2}|y|^{-d}\mathrm{d}y=N\log(1/|x|).

(1.13)

Consequently, for

Conversely, employing (

\displaystyle(R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}^{r}\ast R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}^{r})(x)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|x|/2<|y|\leq 2}R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-y)^{r}R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(y)^{r}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq N\int_{|x|/2<|y|\leq 2}|y|^{\alpha_{\delta_{0},\gamma,r}-d}|x-y|^{\alpha_{\delta_{0},\gamma,r}-d}\mathrm{d}y\simeq\log(1/|x|).

∎

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

,

(2.1)

(iii)

Let

denote the set of functions

where

are pairwise disjoint, and each

\bm{\mathrm{H}}_{c,0}^{\infty}

(iv)

Let
\bm{\mathrm{H}}_{c}^{\infty}

\tau_{i-1}\leq\tau_{i}
and

\bm{\mathrm{H}}_{c}^{\infty}
is dense in
\mathbb{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}
.

(v)

Let
\bm{\mathrm{H}}_{c}^{\infty}(\ell_{2})
the set of functions

,

g^{k}(\omega,t,x):=\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{n}1_{(\tau_{i-1}^{n},\tau_{i}^{n}]}(\omega,t)g_{i}^{k}(x),&\text{if }k\leq n,\\
0,&\text{if }k>n,\end{cases}

where
\tau_{i}^{n}
are bounded stopping times such that

and

Proof.

Assertions
(i)
and
(ii)
follow directly as corollaries of Theorem
3.12
, as detailed in
[
35
, Theorem 6.4]
.
For assertions
(iii)
through
(v)
, due to similarity, we demonstrate the proof explicitly for
(iii)
only.
Considering the isometry

(1-\phi(\Delta))^{\gamma/2}:U_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma+2}\to U_{p,q}^{\phi,2}\subseteq L_{q}(\Omega,\mathbb{F}_{0};L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),

and acknowledging that
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in the interpolation space
(H_{p}^{\phi,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))_{1/q,q}

\bm{\mathrm{H}}_{c,0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
L_{q}(\Omega,\mathbb{F}_{0},\mathbb{P};L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

Assuming the contrary, by the Hahn-Banach theorem (
e.g.

[
52
, Theorem 5.19]
), there exists a nonzero element
h\in L_{q^{\prime}}(\Omega,\mathbb{F}_{0},\mathbb{P};L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for which

\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(\omega,x)g(\omega,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega)=0,\quad\forall g\in\bm{\mathrm{H}}_{c,0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Here

and

Specifically,

\int_{\Omega}1_{A}(\omega)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(\omega,x)g(x)\mathrm{d}x\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega)=0,\quad\forall g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,A\in\mathbb{F}_{0}.

Given that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(\omega,x)g(x)\mathrm{d}x=0,\quad\forall g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

Consequently, this infers that

The proof is completed.

2.2.
Semigroups and generators of Subordinate Brownian Motions

This subsection introduces the concept of Subordinate Brownian Motions (SBMs), and discusses some properties of their semigroups and generators.
We start with the definition of subordinators.

Definition 2.2
.

A real-valued Lévy process
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, is termed a
subordinator
if

\mathbb{P}(S_{t}\geq 0,\,\forall t\geq 0)=1.

It is widely recognized that there is a one-to-one correspondence between Bernstein functions and subordinators.
Thus, given either a subordinator
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
or a Bernstein function
\phi
, there also exists the other object such that the following holds:

\left(\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda S_{t}(\omega)}\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega)=:\right)\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda S_{t}}]=\mathrm{e}^{-t\phi(\lambda)},\quad\forall(t,\lambda)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

(2.2)

A more detailed explanation of the relationship between Bernstein functions and SBMs is found in
e.g.

[
10
,
57
]
.
We say that
S
is a subordinator with Laplace exponent
\phi
if (
2.2
) is satisfied.

We now present the concept of SBMs.

Definition 2.3
.

The process
X=(\mathfrak{B}_{S_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
is termed a
Subordinate Brownian Motion
(SBM) with the subordinator
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
if
\mathfrak{B}=(\mathfrak{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion that is independent of the subordinator
S
.

Characteristic exponent of SBM.
It is well known (
e.g.

[
57
, Theorem 3.2]
) that Bernstein function
\phi
has a unique representation

\phi(\lambda)=b_{\phi}\lambda+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\right)\,m_{\phi}(\mathrm{d}t),

where
b_{\phi}\geq 0
and

m_{\phi}
is a nonnegative measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1\wedge t\right)m_{\phi}(\mathrm{d}t)<\infty\,.

(2.3)

We say that
b_{\phi}
is the
drift
and the nonnegative measure
m_{\phi}
satisfying (
2.3
) is the
Lévy measure
of
\phi
.
According to
[
56
, Theorem 30.1]
, the SBM
X
is a
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued Lévy process, and its Lévy triplet
(a,D,\nu)
is as follows:

\begin{gathered}a=b_{\phi}\cdot 0+\int_{(0,\infty)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}xp(t,|x|)\mathrm{d}x\,m_{\phi}(\mathrm{d}t)=0,\\
D=b_{\phi}I_{d\times d},\\
\nu(B)=b_{\phi}\cdot 0+\int_{(0,\infty)}\int_{B}p(t,|x|)\mathrm{d}x\,m_{\phi}(\mathrm{d}t)=\int_{B}j(|x|)\mathrm{d}x,\end{gathered}

where

is the
d\times d

p(t,r):=\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2t}},\quad j(r):=\int_{(0,\infty)}p(t,r)m_{\phi}(\mathrm{d}t),

(2.4)

b_{\phi}
and
m_{\phi}
are the drift and the Lévy measure of
\phi
, respectively.
By making use of
[
56

, we get

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}X_{t}\cdot\xi}]=\exp\left(-tb_{\phi}|\xi|^{2}+t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\xi\cdot y}-1-\mathrm{i}y\cdot\xi 1_{|y|\leq 1})j(|y|)\mathrm{d}y\right).

(2.5)

Applying Fubini’s theorem, we can simplify the above as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\xi\cdot y}-1-\mathrm{i}y\cdot\xi 1_{|y|\leq 1})j(|y|)\mathrm{d}y

(2.6)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\cos(\xi\cdot y)-1)j(|y|)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,\infty)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\xi\cdot y}-1-\mathrm{i}y\cdot\xi 1_{|y|\leq 1})p(s,|y|)\mathrm{d}y\,m_{\phi}(\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,\infty)}(\mathrm{e}^{-s|\xi|^{2}}-1)m_{\phi}(\mathrm{d}s),

which conclusively leads to:

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}X_{t}\cdot\xi}]=\mathrm{e}^{-t\phi(|\xi|^{2})}.

(2.7)

We say that
X
is a SBM with characteristic exponent
\phi
if (

) is satisfied.

Definition 2.4

Lemma 2.5

Let

Given that

2.3.
Functional calculus

First, we introduce the concept of
H^{\infty}
-calculus.
For
\eta\in(0,\pi)
, denote

\Sigma_{\eta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}:|\arg(z)|<\eta\}.

We denote
H^{p}(\Sigma_{\eta})
the complex Banach space of all holomorphic functions
f:\Sigma_{\eta}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying

\|f\|_{H^{p}(\Sigma_{\eta})}:=\sup_{|\nu|<\eta}\|f(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\nu}t)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathrm{d}t/t)}<\infty.

Let
A
be a linear operator on a Banach space
V
.

(i)

(Resolvent of
A
) We say that
z
is in the resolvent set
\rho(A)
of
A
if

•

the range of
A_{z}:=z-A
is dense in
V
.

•

A_{z}
has a continuous inverse.

Here, for
z\in\rho(A)
, we can define
R(z,A):=(z-A)^{-1}
.

(ii)

(Sectorial operator) We say that a linear operator
A
is sectorial if

•

there exists
\eta\in(0,\pi)
such that the spectrum
\sigma(A):=\mathbb{C}\setminus\rho(A)
is contained in
\overline{\Sigma_{\eta}}
.

•

the operator
zR(z,A)
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\eta}}
,
i.e.
,

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\eta}}}\|zR(z,A)\|_{V\to V}<\infty.

In this case, we say
A
is
\eta
-sectorial.
We say that the constant

\eta(A):=\inf\{\eta\in(0,\pi):A\text{ is $\eta$-sectorial}\}

is called the angle of sectoriality of
A
.

(iii)

(Bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus) Let
A
be a sectorial operator with the angle of sectoriality
\eta(A)
.
For functions
f\in H^{1}(\Sigma_{\nu})
, denote

f(A):=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\nu}}f(z)R(z,A)\mathrm{d}z,

where
\eta(A)<\nu<\sigma
is chosen arbitrarily.
We remark that the definition of
f(A)
is independent of the choice of
\nu
(see
e.g.

[
34
, Chapter 10.2]
).
For a constant
\sigma\in(\eta(A),\pi)
, we say that the operator
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\sigma})
-calculus if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|f(A)\|_{V\to V}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{\infty}},\quad f\in H^{1}(\Sigma_{\sigma})\cap H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\sigma}).

We define

\eta_{H^{\infty}}(A):=\inf\{\sigma\in(\eta(A),\pi):A\text{ has a bounded $H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\sigma})$-calculus}\},

and we say that
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
\eta_{H^{\infty}}(A)
.

Definition 2.6

(Analytic semigroup)
.

Let
(L(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
V
.

(i)

(L(t))_{t\geq 0}
is called analytic on
\Sigma_{\eta}
if for all
x\in V
, the function
t\mapsto L(t)x
extends analytically to
\Sigma_{\eta}
and satisfies

\lim_{z\in\Sigma_{\eta},z\to 0}L(z)x=x.

We call
(L(t))_{t\geq 0}
an analytic
C_{0}
-semigroup if
(L(t))_{t\geq 0}
is analytic on
\Sigma_{\eta}
for some
\eta\in(0,\pi)
.

(ii)

We say that
\{L(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
a bounded analytic
C_{0}
-semigroup if
\{L(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is analytic and uniformly bounded on
\Sigma_{\eta}
for some
\eta\in(0,\pi)
.

We are now prepared to employ functional calculus within the framework

(V,A,L(t))=(L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),-\phi(\Delta),T_{t}).

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\phi
be a Bernstein function,
X
be a SBM with characteristic exponent
\phi
, and

(i)

the operator

-calculus on

(ii)

Proof.

For

For part

r(u)\in H^{\phi,1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and

\|\phi^{1/2}(\Delta)(r(u))\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|\phi^{1/2}(\Delta)u\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(3.13)

(iii)

Suppose that
u,v\in H_{2}^{\phi,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Then for any defining sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the sesquilinear functional

\mathcal{I}(u_{n},v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{v(x)}\phi(\Delta)u_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}x

converges to

b_{\phi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{\nabla v(x)}\nabla u(x)\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\overline{v(x)}-\overline{v(y)}\right)\left(u(x)-u(y)\right)j(|x-y|)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

Proof.

(i)
Since
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
H^{\phi,1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{\phi,1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\simeq\|u\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\phi^{1/2}(\Delta)u\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

it suffices only to prove for
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, (
3.12
) holds.
Using Plancherel’s theorem and definition of
\phi^{1/2}(\Delta)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\phi^{1/2}(\Delta)u(x)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(|\xi|^{2})\overline{\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)}\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(b_{\phi}|\xi|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(1-\cos{(\xi\cdot y)}\right)j(|y|)\mathrm{d}y\right)\overline{\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)}\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi,

where the last equality follows from (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
).
Employing the relation
(1-\cos{z})=|1-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z}|^{2}/2
, and applying Plancherel’s theorem again, we achieve

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\phi^{1/2}(\Delta)u(x)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}\left|1-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\xi\cdot y}\right|^{2}\overline{\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)}\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)j(|y|)\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}y+b_{\phi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{2}||\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\xi\cdot y}\right)\overline{\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\xi\cdot y}\right)\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)j(|y|)\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}y+b_{\phi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathrm{i}\xi\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|u(x)-u(x-y)\right|^{2}j(|y|)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+b_{\phi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{2}j(|x-y|)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+b_{\phi}\|\nabla u\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

(ii)
Since
u\in H^{\phi,1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it can be easily checked that
r(u)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Due to
(i)
, we only need to prove (
3.13
).
Since
|r(u(x))-r(u(y))|\leq N|u(x)-u(y)|
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|r(u(x))-r(u(y))\right|^{2}j(|x-y|)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+b_{\phi}\|\nabla(r(u))\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{2}j(|x-y|)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+N\|\nabla u\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=N\|\phi^{1/2}(\Delta)u\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}<\infty.

(iii)
Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a sequence of functions satisfying
u_{n}\to u
in
H^{\phi,1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Then,
\mathcal{I}(u_{n},v)
is well-defined.
In addition, by following the argument in
(i)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(u_{n},v)=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(|\xi|^{2})\overline{\mathcal{F}[v](\xi)}\mathcal{F}[u_{n}](\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\overline{v(x)}-\overline{v(y)}\right)\left(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)\right)j(|x-y|)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle-b_{\phi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{\nabla v(x)}\nabla u_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}x.

By Lemma
3.3
-
(i)
and the Cauchy-Bunyakovsky-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{I}(u_{n},v)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\overline{v(x)}-\overline{v(y)}\right)\left(u(x)-u(y)\right)j(|x-y|)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+b_{\phi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{\nabla v(x)}\cdot\nabla u(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq N\|\phi^{1/2}(\Delta)v\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\phi^{1/2}(\Delta)(u_{n}-u)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to 0

as
n\to\infty
.
Therefore, by defining
\mathcal{I}(u,v)
as the limit of
\mathcal{I}(u_{n},v)
, we prove the lemma.
∎

Now, we prove the maximum principle for semilinear stochastic integro-differential equations.

Theorem 3.5

(Maximum principle)
.

Let
\tau\leq T
be a bounded stopping time.
Suppose that

(i)

u=u(t,\cdot)
and
f=f(t,\cdot)
are
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-valued
\mathbb{F}_{t}
-adapted, jointly measurable functions.

(ii)

g=g(t,\cdot)=(g^{1}(t,\cdot),g^{2}(t,\cdot),\cdots)
is a
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\ell_{2})
-valued
\mathbb{F}_{t}
-adapted, jointly measurable function.

(iii)

u,f,g
satisfy

\mathbb{E}\left[\|u(0,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\||f(s,\cdot)|+|g(s,\cdot)|_{\ell_{2}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right]<\infty,

and

\mathrm{d}u=(\phi(\Delta)u+f)\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}g^{k}\mathrm{d}w^{k}_{t}.

Suppose also that
u(0,\cdot)\leq 0
and for any
\omega\in\Omega
,
k=1,2,\cdots

1_{u(t,\cdot)>0}f(t,\cdot)\leq 0,\quad 1_{u(t,\cdot)>0}g^{k}(t,\cdot)=0,

(3.14)

for almost
t\in(0,\tau(\omega))
.
Then almost surely
\omega\in\Omega
,
u(t,\cdot)\leq 0
for all
t\in[0,\tau(\omega)]
.

Proof.

The aim of this proof is to establish that, for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{+}(t\wedge\tau,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq 0,

(3.15)

where
u_{+}(t,x):=\max(u(t,x),0)
.

Step 1.
In this step, we prove
u\in\mathcal{H}_{2,2}^{\phi,1}(\tau)
and

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq\tau}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right]\leq N\mathbb{E}\left[\|u(0,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\||f(s,\cdot)|+|g(s,\cdot)|_{\ell_{2}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right].

(3.16)

One can check that
f\in\mathbb{L}_{2,2}(\tau)\subseteq\mathbb{H}_{2,2}^{\phi,-1}(\tau)
and
(H_{2}^{\phi,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),H_{2}^{\phi,-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))_{1/2,2}=L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, thus, by Theorem
2.10
,
u\in\mathcal{H}_{2,2}^{\phi,1}(\tau)
.
Consider a nonnegative function
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta(z)\mathrm{d}z=1
, and define

\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x):=\varepsilon^{-d}\zeta(x/\varepsilon).

For
\chi=u,f,g^{k}
, let

\displaystyle\chi_{\varepsilon}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi(t,y)\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y,

then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the equality

u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=u_{\varepsilon}(0,x)+\int_{0}^{t}(\phi(\Delta)u_{\varepsilon}(s,x)+f_{\varepsilon}(s,x))\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}g^{k}_{\varepsilon}(s,x)\mathrm{d}w_{s}^{k}

holds almost surely for all
t\leq\tau
.
By Itô’s formula,

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{\varepsilon}(0,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}(s,x)(\phi(\Delta)u_{\varepsilon}(s,x)+f_{\varepsilon}(s,x))\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g_{\varepsilon}(s,x)|^{2}_{\ell_{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s+2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}(s,x)g_{\varepsilon}^{k}(s,x)\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w_{s}^{k}

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s}\mathrm{d}s.

By Young’s inequality,

2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)f_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\leq\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

By Lemma
3.3
-
(i)
and Remark
3.4
,

\begin{gathered}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}(s,x)\phi(\Delta)u_{\varepsilon}(s,x)\mathrm{d}x=-\|\phi^{1/2}(\Delta)u_{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq 0.\end{gathered}

By the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality,

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq\tau}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}(s,x)g_{\varepsilon}^{k}(s,x)\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda s}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w_{s}^{k}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq N\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}\|u_{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\|g_{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\ell_{2})}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2\lambda s}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq\tau}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\|u_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{2,2}(\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq\tau}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\|u_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right]+N_{\delta}\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{2,2}(\tau)}^{2}.

By taking
\lambda\geq 1
and sufficiently small
\delta>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq\tau}\|u_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right]

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq N\mathbb{E}\left[\|u_{\varepsilon}(0,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\||f_{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot)|+|g_{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot)|_{\ell_{2}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq N\mathbb{E}\left[\|u(0,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\||f(s,\cdot)|+|g(s,\cdot)|_{\ell_{2}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right],

where
N=N(d,\delta,\lambda,T)
.
For achieving (
3.16
), the remaining part of step 1 is only to prove

\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq\tau}\|u_{1/m}(t,\cdot)-u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right]=0.

(3.18)

By the dominated convergence theorem,

\mathbb{E}\left[\|u_{\varepsilon_{1}}(0,\cdot)-u_{\varepsilon_{2}}(0,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\||f_{\varepsilon_{1}}(s,\cdot)-f_{\varepsilon_{2}}(s,\cdot)|+|g_{\varepsilon_{1}}(s,\cdot)-g_{\varepsilon_{2}}(s,\cdot)|_{\ell_{2}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right]

converges to
0
as
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\downarrow 0
.
Due to (
3.17
), it can be easily checked that
\{u_{1/m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L_{2}(\Omega;L_{\infty}([0,\tau];L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})))
.
Therefore, there exists a limit
\bar{u}
such that
u_{1/m}\to\bar{u}
in
L_{2}(\Omega;L_{\infty}([0,\tau];L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})))
.
One can also easily check that
u_{1/m}(t,\cdot)\to u(t,\cdot)
in the sense of distribution for all
t\leq\tau
almost surely.
Therefore, with probability
1
,
u(t,\cdot)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
t\leq\tau
.
This certainly implies that
\bar{u}(t,\cdot)=u(t,\cdot)
in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
t\leq\tau
, thus (
3.18
) holds.

Step 2.
Now, we prove (
3.15
).
Let
r(z):=(\max(z,0))^{2}
for
z\in\mathbb{R}
.
Drawing upon
[
40
, Remark 3.1]
, we introduce a sequence of real-valued convex functions
\{r_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that satisfy:

•

r_{n}
is infinitely differentiable.

•

|r_{n}(z)|\leq N|z|^{2}
,
|r_{n}^{\prime}(z)|\leq N|z|
, and
|r_{n}^{\prime\prime}(z)|\leq N
, where
N
is independent of
z
and
n
.

•

r_{n},r_{n}^{\prime},r_{n}^{\prime\prime}\to r,r^{\prime},r^{\prime\prime}
as
n\to\infty
.
Here
r^{\prime\prime}(z)=2\times 1_{z>0}
.

Applying Itô’s formula, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r_{n}(u_{1/m}(\tau\wedge t,x))\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r_{n}(u_{1/m}(0,x))\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r_{n}^{\prime}(u_{1/m}(s,x))(\phi(\Delta)u_{1/m}(s,x)+f_{1/m}(s,x))\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r_{n}^{\prime\prime}(u_{1/m}(s,x))|g_{1/m}(s,x)|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r_{n}^{\prime}(u_{1/m}(s,x))g^{k}_{1/m}(s,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w_{s}^{k}.

Due to (
3.18
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\leq\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|r_{n}(u_{1/m}(s,x))-r_{n}(u(s,x))|\mathrm{d}x\right]

\displaystyle\leq N\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\leq\tau}\|u(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|u_{1/m}(s,\cdot)-u(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right]\to 0

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\leq\tau}\|r_{n}^{\prime}(u_{1/m}(s,\cdot))-r_{n}^{\prime}(u(s,\cdot))\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right]

\displaystyle\leq N\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\leq\tau}\|u_{1/m}(s,\cdot)-u(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right]\to 0,

as
m\to\infty
.
Then, with probability 1, the equality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r_{n}(u(\tau\wedge t,x))\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r_{n}(u(0,x))\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r_{n}^{\prime}(u(s,x))(\phi(\Delta)u(s,x)+f(s,x))\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r_{n}^{\prime\prime}(u(s,x))|g(s,x)|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r_{n}^{\prime}(u(s,x))g^{k}(s,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w_{s}^{k}

holds for all
t\leq\tau
.
Taking the expectation both-sides, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r_{n}(u(\tau\wedge t,x))\mathrm{d}x\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r_{n}(u(0,x))\mathrm{d}x\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r_{n}^{\prime}(u(s,x))(\phi(\Delta)u(s,x)+f(s,x))\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r_{n}^{\prime\prime}(u(s,x))|g(s,x)|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\right].

By the dominated convergence theorem and (
3.14
), we infer that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{+}(\tau\wedge t,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{+}(0,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right]

(3.19)

\displaystyle+2\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{+}(s,x)(\phi(\Delta)u(s,x)+f(s,x))\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}1_{u(s,x)>0}|g(s,x)|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{+}(s,x)\phi(\Delta)u(s,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\right].

By Lemma
3.3
-(
ii
) and Remark
3.4
,

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{+}(s,x)\phi(\Delta)u(s,x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u_{+}(s,x)-u_{+}(s,y))(u(s,x)-u(s,y))j(|x-y|)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle-2b_{\phi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla u_{+}(x)\nabla u(x)\mathrm{d}x.

Observe that

\big(u_{+}(s,x)-u_{+}(s,y)\big)\big(u(s,x)-u(s,y)\big)\geq 0,

and for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\nabla u_{+}(x)=1_{\{u>0\}}(x)\nabla u(x).

It follows that

2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{+}(s,x)\,\phi(\Delta)u(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 0.

(3.20)

Combining (
3.19
) and (
3.20
), we obtain the desired inequality (
3.15
).
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Theorem
3.5
is stated as a

principle.
The
nonnegativity
version is equivalent by a sign change.
Indeed, let
u
solve

\mathrm{d}u=(\phi(\Delta)u+f)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}g^{k}\,\mathrm{d}w_{t}^{k},

and assume

u(0,\cdot)\geq 0,\qquad 1_{\{u<0\}}\,f\geq 0,\qquad 1_{\{u<0\}}\,g^{k}=0.

Set
v:=-u
.
Then
v
solves

\mathrm{d}v=(\phi(\Delta)v-f)\,\mathrm{d}t-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}g^{k}\,\mathrm{d}w_{t}^{k},

Applying Theorem
3.5
to
v
yields
v\leq 0
, hence
u\geq 0
.
Thus, the nonpositivity and nonnegativity formulations are equivalent.

.

.

(3.21)

u_{m}^{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{2,2}^{\phi,1}(\tau)\cap\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}(\tau)
for the equation

\mathrm{d}u_{m}^{n}=(\phi(\Delta)u_{m}^{n}+\zeta F_{m}(u_{m}^{n})+\vec{b}\cdot\nabla_{x}(B_{m}(u_{m}^{n})))\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\xi\varphi_{m}(u_{m}^{n})(\pi\ast e_{k})\mathrm{d}w_{t}^{k},\quad t\in(0,\tau],

with initial condition
u_{m}^{n}(0,\cdot)=u_{0,n}
.
It is verifiable that

f_{m}^{n}:=(\zeta F_{m}(u_{m}^{n})+\vec{b}\cdot\nabla_{x}(B_{m}(u_{m}^{n})))1_{\text{$[$\kern-3.02948pt$[$}0,\tau\text{$]$\kern-1.19579pt$]$}}\in L_{2}(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and
f_{m}^{n}\leq 0
on
\{(\omega,t,x):u_{m}^{n}(\omega,t,x)\leq 0\}
.
Similarly,

g_{m}^{n}:=\xi\varphi_{m}(u_{m}^{n})\bm{\pi}_{n}1_{\text{$[$\kern-3.02948pt$[$}0,\tau\text{$]$\kern-1.19579pt$]$}}\in L_{2}(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\ell_{2}),\quad\bm{\pi}_{n}:=(\pi\ast e_{1},\cdots,\pi\ast e_{n},0,\cdots),

and
g_{m}^{n}=0
on
\{(\omega,t,x):u_{m}^{n}(\omega,t,x)\leq 0\}
.
Applying Theorem
3.5
, we conclude
u_{m}^{n}\geq 0
for all
t\in[0,\tau]
almost surely.
The final step of the proof is to establish that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{m}^{n}-u_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}(T)}=0.

(3.22)

Here,
u_{m}
is the solution in Theorem
3.1
-
(i)
.
According to Corollary
3.2
,

\displaystyle\|u_{m}^{n}-u_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}(\tau\wedge t)}

\displaystyle\leq N\|u_{0,n}-u_{0}\|_{U_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}}+N\|u_{m}^{n}-u_{m}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,q}(\tau\wedge t)}

\displaystyle\quad+N\|(\bm{\pi}-\bm{\pi}_{n})u_{m}\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma-1}(\tau\wedge t,\ell_{2})},

where
N
is a constant independent of
n
and
t
.
Employing the inequality (
2.1
) and Grönwall’s inequality, we derive

\displaystyle\|u_{m}^{n}-u_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}(\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq N\|u_{0,n}-u_{0}\|_{U_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}}+N\|(\bm{\pi}-\bm{\pi}_{n})u_{m}\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma-1}(\tau,\ell_{2})}.

With (
3.11
) in consideration,

\displaystyle\|(\bm{\pi}-\bm{\pi}_{n})u_{m}\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma-1}(\tau,\ell_{2})}^{q}\leq N\|u_{m}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,q}(\tau)},

leading to the conclusion via the dominated convergence theorem and (
3.21
) that (
3.22
) holds true.
Thus, the theorem is proven.
∎

3.3.
Space-time Hölder type regularity of local solutions: Proof of Theorem
3.1
-(iii)

The proof of Theorem
3.1
-(iii), which concerns the space–time Hölder-type regularity of local solutions, follows as an immediate corollary of the theorem stated below. We begin by recalling the definition of parabolic Hölder spaces adapted to our setting.

Definition 3.8

(Parabolic Hölder space)
.

Let
\delta_{0}\in(0,1]
,
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
,
p\in[2,\infty)
,
q\in(2,\infty)
, and let
\phi
be a Bernstein function satisfying Assumption
1.12
(
\delta_{0}
).
Assume that

\frac{1}{q}\leq\alpha<\beta\leq\frac{1}{2},\qquad\delta_{0}(\gamma-2\beta)-\frac{d}{p}>0.

For an integer
L>\gamma-\frac{d}{p}
, define the
L
-th order difference in the parabolic direction
(\tau,h)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

\mathcal{D}^{0}_{(\tau,h)}u:=u,\qquad\mathcal{D}^{L}_{(\tau,h)}u(t,x):=\mathcal{D}^{1}_{(\tau,h)}\mathcal{D}^{L-1}_{(\tau,h)}u(t,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{L}(-1)^{L-k}\binom{L}{k}\,u(t+k\tau,x+kh).

For
T>0
, set

\mathbb{R}^{d}_{T,L,\tau}:=\Big\{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ (t+L\tau,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\Big\}.

We denote by
C_{t,x}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q},\,\phi_{\gamma-2\beta}-\frac{d}{p}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
the set of continuous functions
u
on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the norm

|u|_{C_{t,x}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q},\,\phi_{\gamma-2\beta}-\frac{d}{p}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=|u|_{C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sup_{(\tau,h)\neq(0,0)}\frac{\|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{(\tau,h)}u\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{T,L,\tau})}}{|\tau|^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}+\phi(|h|^{-2})^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\beta}\,|h|^{-\frac{d}{p}}}

is finite.

Proposition 3.9
.

The space
C_{t,x}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q},\,\phi_{\gamma-2\beta}-\frac{d}{p}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be characterized as follows:

C_{t,x}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q},\,\phi_{\gamma-2\beta}-\frac{d}{p}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})=C(\mathbb{R}^{d};C^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}([0,T]))\cap C([0,T];C^{\phi_{\gamma-2\beta}-\frac{d}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),

where

\displaystyle|u|_{C(\mathbb{R}^{d};C^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}([0,T]))}:=|u|_{C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{t,s\in[0,T]}\frac{|u(t,x)-u(s,x)|}{|t-s|^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}},

\displaystyle|u|_{C([0,T];C^{\phi_{\gamma-2\beta}-\frac{d}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}:=|u|_{C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
|h|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{(0,h)}u(t,x)|}{\phi(|h|^{-2})^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\beta}\,|h|^{-\frac{d}{p}}}.

Proof.

First, note that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{(\tau,h)}u(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{(\tau,h)}u(t,x)-\mathcal{D}^{L}_{(0,h)}u(t,x)|+|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{(0,h)}u(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq N(L)\sum_{j=1}^{L}|u(t+j\tau,x+jh)-u(t,x+jh)|+|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{(0,h)}u(t,x)|.

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{(\tau,h)}u\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{T,L,\tau})}}{|\tau|^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}+\phi(|h|^{-2})^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\beta}|h|^{-\frac{d}{p}}}

\displaystyle\leq N(\alpha,L,q)\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{t,s\in[0,T]}\frac{|u(t,x)-u(s,x)|}{|t-s|^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
|h|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{(0,h)}u(t,x)|}{\phi(|h|^{-2})^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\beta}|h|^{-\frac{d}{p}}}.

This shows that

C(\mathbb{R}^{d};C^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}([0,T]))\cap C([0,T];C^{\phi_{\gamma-2\beta}-\frac{d}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\subseteq C_{t,x}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q},\,\phi_{\gamma-2\beta}-\frac{d}{p}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Conversely, note that

\frac{|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{(\tau,0)}u(t,x)|}{|\tau|^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}}+\frac{|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{(0,h)}u(t,x)|}{\phi(|h|^{-2})^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\beta}|h|^{-\frac{d}{p}}}\leq\sup_{(\tau,h)\neq(0,0)}\frac{\|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{(\tau,h)}u\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{T,L,\tau})}}{|\tau|^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}+\phi(|h|^{-2})^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\beta}|h|^{-\frac{d}{p}}}.

It remains to prove

\sup_{t,s\in[0,T]}\frac{|u(t,x)-u(s,x)|}{|t-s|^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}}\leq N_{1}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|u(t,x)|+N_{2}\sup_{t>0}\frac{\omega_{L}(u(\cdot,x),t)}{t^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}},

(3.23)

where
N_{1}=N_{1}(\alpha,L,q,T)
,
N_{2}=N_{2}(\alpha,L,q)

\omega_{L}(u(\cdot,x),t):=\sup_{0<|\tau|\leq t}\big\|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{(\tau,0)}u(\cdot,x)\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathrm{T}_{L,\tau})},\qquad\mathrm{T}_{L,\tau}:=\big\{s\in[0,T]:\,s+L\tau\in[0,T]\big\}.

Since (
3.23
) is pointwise in
x
, it suffices to prove it for scalar functions on
[0,T]
.
Thus, let
f:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
and set

\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau}f(t):=\sum_{k=0}^{L}(-1)^{L-k}\binom{L}{k}f(t+k\tau),\qquad\omega_{L}(f,t):=\sup_{0<|\tau|\leq t}\big\|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau}f\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathrm{T}_{L,\tau})},

and

[f]_{C^{\beta}_{L}([0,T])}:=\sup_{t>0}\frac{\omega_{L}(f,t)}{t^{\beta}}.

Claim 1.
Let
a_{j}:=\omega_{L}(f,2^{-j}t)
and
b_{j}:=\omega_{L+1}\!\left(f,2^{-j}t\right)
. Then,

a_{0}\leq 2^{L}(1+2^{-LJ})\,a_{J}+L2^{L-1}\sum_{j=0}^{J}(1+2^{-Lj})\,b_{j},\quad\forall\,J\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.24)

Proof of Claim 1.

Let
I
be the identity and
T_{\tau}f(t):=f(t+\tau)
. Then

\mathcal{D}_{\tau}=(I+T_{\tau/2})\mathcal{D}_{\tau/2},

hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau}-\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau/2}

\displaystyle=(I+T_{\tau/2})^{L}\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau/2}-\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau/2}=\sum_{k=1}^{L}\binom{L}{k}(T_{\tau/2}^{k}-2^{-L}I)\,\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau/2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{L}\binom{L}{k}\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}2^{-\frac{L(k-1-r)}{k}}T_{\tau/2}^{\,r}\,\mathcal{D}^{L+1}_{\tau/2}.

Therefore,

\big\|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau}f-\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau/2}f\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathrm{T}_{L,\tau})}\leq L2^{L-1}\,\big\|\mathcal{D}^{L+1}_{\tau/2}f\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathrm{T}_{L+1,\tau/2})}.

Then for integers
0\leq k<J
, define

S_{k,J}:=\sum_{j=k}^{J-1}2^{-Lj}\Big(\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau/2^{j}}-\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau/2^{j+1}}\Big),

which telescopes to

S_{k,J}=2^{-Lk}\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau/2^{k}}-(2^{L}-1)\sum_{j=k+1}^{J-1}2^{-Lj}\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau/2^{j}}-2^{-L(J-1)}\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau/2^{J}}.

Taking sup–norms on the appropriate domains yields

\displaystyle 2^{-Lk}\big\|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau/2^{k}}f\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathrm{T}_{L,\tau/2^{k}})}

\displaystyle\leq(2^{L}-1)\sum_{j=k+1}^{J-1}2^{-Lj}\big\|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau/2^{j}}f\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathrm{T}_{L,\tau/2^{j}})}

\displaystyle+2^{-L(J-1)}\big\|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\tau/2^{J}}f\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathrm{T}_{L,\tau/2^{J}})}

\displaystyle+L2^{L-1}\sum_{j=k}^{J-1}2^{-Lj}\big\|\mathcal{D}^{L+1}_{\tau/2^{j+1}}f\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathrm{T}_{L+1,\tau/2^{j+1}})}.

Hence, we obtain the discrete Volterra inequality: for
k=0,1,2,\cdots,J-2
,

a_{k}\leq 2^{-L(J-1-k)}a_{J}+(2^{L}-1)\sum_{j=k+1}^{J-1}2^{-L(j-k)}a_{j}+L2^{L-1}\sum_{j=k}^{J-1}2^{-L(j-k)}b_{j+1}.

(3.25)

By summing over
k=0,1,\cdots,J-2
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{J-2}a_{k}

\displaystyle\leq a_{J}\sum_{k=0}^{J-2}2^{-L(J-1-k)}+(2^{L}-1)\sum_{k=0}^{J-2}\sum_{j=k+1}^{J-1}2^{-L(j-k)}a_{j}+L2^{L-1}\sum_{k=0}^{J-2}\sum_{j=k}^{J-1}2^{-L(j-k)}b_{j+1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{L}a_{J}}{2^{L}-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}(1-2^{-Lj})a_{j}+\frac{L2^{L-1}}{2^{L}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}b_{j+1}.

Since
a_{k+1}\leq a_{k}
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{J-1}a_{k}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{J-2}a_{k}\leq\frac{2^{L}a_{J}}{2^{L}-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}(1-2^{-Lj})a_{j}+\frac{L2^{L-1}}{2^{L}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}b_{j+1},

thus

\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}2^{-Lj}a_{j}\leq\frac{2^{L}a_{J}}{2^{L}-1}+\frac{L2^{L-1}}{2^{L}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}b_{j+1}

(3.26)

Putting
k=0
in (
3.25
) and using (
3.26
) yields (
3.24
).
∎

Claim 2.
For all
t\in(0,T]
,

\omega_{L}(f,t)\leq N(L)t^{L}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T])}+N(\alpha,L,q)t^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}[f]_{C_{L+1}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}([0,T])}.

(3.27)

Proof of Claim 2.

Note that for any
J\in\mathbb{N}
,

1=\frac{1}{\log 2}\int_{2^{-J}t}^{2^{-(J-1)}t}\frac{1}{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,\qquad 2^{-LJ}=\frac{t^{L}}{L}\int_{2^{J}t}^{\infty}\lambda^{-L-1}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Since
\lambda\mapsto\omega_{L}(f,\lambda)
is nondecreasing, we have

(1+2^{-LJ})a_{J}\leq\frac{1}{\log 2}\int_{0}^{2^{-(J-1)}t}\frac{\omega_{L}(f,\lambda)}{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\lambda+\frac{t^{L}}{L}\int_{2^{J}t}^{\infty}\frac{\omega_{L}(f,\lambda)}{\lambda^{L+1}}\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Using

\omega_{L}(f,\lambda)\leq\min\left(2^{L}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T])},\lambda^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}[f]_{C_{L}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}([0,T])}\right),

we obtain, for all
J\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle(1+2^{-LJ})a_{J}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{[f]_{C_{L}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}([0,T])}}{\log 2}\int_{0}^{2^{-(J-1)}t}\lambda^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}-1}\mathrm{d}\lambda+\frac{2^{L}t^{L}}{L}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T])}\int_{2^{J}t}^{\infty}\lambda^{-L-1}\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq N(\alpha,q)t^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}2^{-J(\alpha-\frac{1}{q})}[f]_{C_{L}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}([0,T])}+N(L)t^{L}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T])}\int_{2^{J}t}^{\infty}\lambda^{-L-1}\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Taking the limit superior as
J\to\infty
gives

\limsup_{J\to\infty}(1+2^{-LJ})a_{J}\leq N(L)t^{L}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T])}\limsup_{J\to\infty}\int_{2^{J}t}^{\infty}\lambda^{-L-1}\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Since

\limsup_{J\to\infty}\int_{2^{J}t}^{\infty}\lambda^{-L-1}\mathrm{d}\lambda\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\lambda^{-L-1}\mathrm{d}\lambda=N(L),

we have

\limsup_{J\to\infty}(1+2^{-LJ})a_{J}\leq N(L)t^{L}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T])}.

(3.28)

Next, since
\lambda\mapsto\omega_{L+1}(f,\lambda)
is nondecreasing,

b_{j+1}=\frac{1}{\log 2}\int_{2^{-(j+1)}t}^{2^{-j}t}\frac{\omega_{L+1}\left(f,2^{-(j+1)}t\right)}{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\leq\frac{1}{\log 2}\int_{2^{-(j+1)}t}^{2^{-j}t}\frac{\omega_{L+1}(f,\lambda)}{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,

and thus, for any
J\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}(1+2^{-Lj})b_{j+1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\log 2}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}(1+2^{-Lj})\int_{2^{-(j+1)}t}^{2^{-j}t}\frac{\omega_{L+1}(f,\lambda)}{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\log 2}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{2^{-(j+1)}t}^{2^{-j}t}\left(1+\left(\frac{2\lambda}{t}\right)^{L}\right)\frac{\omega_{L+1}(f,\lambda)}{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log 2}\int_{2^{-J}t}^{t}\left(1+\left(\frac{2\lambda}{t}\right)^{L}\right)\frac{\omega_{L+1}(f,\lambda)}{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1+2^{L})}{\log 2}\int_{2^{-J}t}^{t}\frac{\omega_{L+1}(f,\lambda)}{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Hence,

\displaystyle\limsup_{J\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}(1+2^{-Lj})b_{j+1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1+2^{L})}{\log 2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\omega_{L+1}(f,\lambda)}{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\lambda

(3.29)

\displaystyle\leq[f]_{C_{L+1}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}([0,T])}\frac{L2^{L-1}}{\log 2}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}-1}\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=N(\alpha,L,q)t^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}[f]_{C_{L+1}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}([0,T])}.

Starting from (
3.24
) and using (
3.28
) and (
3.29
), we conclude

\displaystyle\omega_{L}(f,t)=a_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{J\to\infty}\left(2^{L}(1+2^{-LJ})a_{J}+L2^{L-1}\sum_{j=0}^{J}(1+2^{-Lj})b_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N(L)t^{L}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T])}+N(\alpha,L,q)t^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}[f]_{C^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}_{L+1}([0,T])},

which is (
3.27
).
∎

Now, we prove (
3.23
).
Dividing both sides of (
3.27
) by
t^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}
and taking the supremum over
t\in(0,T]
, we obtain

\displaystyle[f]_{C^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}_{L}([0,T])}\leq N(L)\,T^{L-\alpha+\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T])}+N(\alpha,L,q)\,[f]_{C^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}_{L+1}([0,T])}.

(3.30)

Since

\displaystyle\sup_{t,s\in[0,T]}\frac{|f(t)-f(s)|}{|t-s|^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}}\leq\sup_{t>0}\frac{\omega_{1}(f,t)}{t^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}}=:[f]_{C^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}_{1}([0,T])},

(
3.30
) yields

\displaystyle\sup_{t,s\in[0,T]}\frac{|f(t)-f(s)|}{|t-s|^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}}

\displaystyle\leq[f]_{C^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}_{1}([0,T])}

\displaystyle\leq N(L)[f]_{C^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}_{2}([0,T])}+N(\alpha,L,q)\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T])}

\displaystyle\leq\cdots\leq N_{1}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T])}+N_{2}[f]_{C^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}_{L}([0,T])}.

Finally, applying this with
f(\cdot)=u(\cdot,x)
gives (
3.23
).
This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

The choice of
L
in Definition
3.8
is immaterial for the definition of

C_{t,x}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q},\,\phi_{\gamma-2\beta}-\frac{d}{p}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Indeed, by Proposition
3.9
, the parameter
L
appears only in the definition of

C^{\phi_{\gamma-2\beta}-\frac{d}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Moreover, by
[
11
, Theorem 1.5]
,

|u|_{C^{\phi_{\gamma-2\beta}-\frac{d}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\simeq\|u\|_{B_{\infty,\infty}^{\phi_{\gamma,\beta,p,d}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

\phi_{\gamma,\beta,p,d}(\lambda):=\phi(\lambda^{2})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}-\beta}\lambda^{-\frac{d}{p}}
.
It is straightforward to verify that the definition of

B_{\infty,\infty}^{\phi_{\gamma,\beta,p,d}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

does not depend on the choice of
L
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\delta_{0}\in(0,1]
,
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
,
p\in[2,\infty)
,
q\in(2,\infty)
, and suppose Assumption
1.12
(
\delta_{0}
) holds.
If

\frac{1}{q}\leq\alpha<\beta\leq\frac{1}{2},\qquad\delta_{0}(\gamma-2\beta)-\frac{d}{p}>0,

then the space
\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}(T)
is continuously embedded into

L_{q}\left(\Omega;\,C_{t,x}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q},\,\phi_{\gamma-2\beta}-\frac{d}{p}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)
.
Equivalently, there exists a constant
N=N(d,N_{0},N_{1},N_{2},p,q,T)
such that for all
u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}(T)
,

\mathbb{E}\left[|u|_{C_{t,x}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q},\,\phi_{\gamma-2\beta}-\frac{d}{p}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\,q}\right]\leq N\,\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}(T)}^{\,q}.

Proof.

Utilizing
[
11
, Theorem 5.3 and Remark 5.4]
, for the condition
\delta_{0}\gamma-d/p>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}|D^{i}u|_{C(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|D^{n}u|_{C^{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq N\left(|u|_{C(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sup_{|h|\leq 1}\left(\frac{|\mathcal{D}_{h}^{L}u|_{C(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\phi(|h|^{-2})^{-\gamma/2}|h|^{-d/p}}\right)\right)

(3.31)

\displaystyle\leq N\|u\|_{H_{p}^{\phi,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
\mathcal{D}^{L}_{h}u(x):=\mathcal{D}_{h}(\mathcal{D}^{L-1}_{h}u)(x)
,
\mathcal{D}_{h}u(x):=u(x+h)-u(x)
,
L
is an integer greater than
\gamma-d/p
, and
\delta_{0}\gamma-d/p=n+\nu
for
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
\nu\in(0,1]
.
By applying (
3.31
) and considering the continuous embedding given by Theorem
3.12
,
i.e.
,

\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}(T)\hookrightarrow L_{q}(\Omega;C^{\alpha-1/q}([0,T];H_{p}^{\phi,\gamma-2\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))),

it follows that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}(T)}^{q}

\displaystyle\geq N\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{H_{p}^{\phi,\gamma-2\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{\|u(t,\cdot)-u(s,\cdot)\|_{H_{p}^{\phi,\gamma-2\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{(t-s)^{\alpha-1/q}}\right)^{q}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{m}(t,x)|^{\frac{p}{r}+p\lambda_{\mathrm{b.}}}h_{m}(u_{m}(t,x))\mathrm{d}x\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{t\leq\tau_{m}(S)}\|u_{m}(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}-1}\times\left(\sup_{t\leq\tau_{m}(S)}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{m}(t,x)|\right)^{\frac{q}{r}+q\lambda_{\mathrm{b.}}-\frac{q}{p}-\lambda_{\mathrm{s.d.}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{0}^{\tau_{m}(S)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{m}(t,x)|^{1+\lambda_{\mathrm{s.d.}}}h_{m}(u_{m}(t,x))\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq S^{\frac{q}{p}}\left(\sup_{t\leq\tau_{m}(S)}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{m}(t,x)|\right)^{\frac{q}{r}+q\lambda_{\mathrm{b.}}-\frac{q}{p}-\lambda_{\mathrm{s.d.}}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\vec{b}\cdot\nabla_{x}(B_{m}(u_{m}))\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma-2}(\tau_{m}(S))}

\displaystyle\leq NS^{1+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sup_{t\leq\tau_{m}(S)}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{m}(t,x)|\right)^{\frac{q}{r}+q\lambda_{\mathrm{b.}}-\frac{q}{p}-\frac{q\lambda_{\mathrm{s.d.}}}{p\vee q}}\right]^{1/q}.

Due to (
3.50
), we have

\|\vec{b}\cdot\nabla_{x}(B_{m}(u_{m}))\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma-2}(\tau_{m}(S))}\leq NS^{1+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}}\mathbb{E}[|u_{m}|_{C_{t,x}([0,\tau_{m}(S)]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}]^{\frac{1}{q}\left(\frac{1}{r}+\lambda_{\mathrm{b.}}-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{s.d.}}}{p\vee q}\right)}.

Step 2.
Estimation of
\xi\varphi_{m}(u_{m})\bm{\pi}
, where
\bm{\pi}:=(\pi\ast e_{1},\pi\ast e_{2},\cdots)
.

We modify the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 4.4]
.
One can check that

\|\xi(t,\cdot)\varphi_{m}(u_{m}(t,\cdot))\bm{\pi}\|_{H_{p}^{\phi,\gamma-1}(\ell_{2})}^{p}\leq N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-\cdot)|u_{m}(t,\cdot)|^{1+\lambda_{\mathrm{s.m.}}}h_{m}(u_{m}(t,\cdot))\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{p}\mathrm{d}x,

where

\displaystyle\|R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-\cdot)f\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-(y-z))R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x+z)f(y-z)\overline{f(-z)}\mathrm{d}z\pi(\mathrm{d}y).

By Hölder’s inequality, for
t\leq\tau_{m}(S)
,

\displaystyle\|R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-\cdot)|u_{m}(t,\cdot)|^{1+\lambda_{\mathrm{s.m.}}}h_{m}(u_{m}(t,\cdot))\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{m}(t,y-z)|^{\frac{1}{2}}|u_{m}(t,-z)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}z\frac{\pi(\mathrm{d}y)}{(1+|y|^{2})^{k/2}}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-(y-z))R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-z)|^{r}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times|u_{m}(t,y-z)|^{\frac{r+1}{2}+r\lambda_{\mathrm{s.m.}}}|u_{m}(t,-z)|^{\frac{r+1}{2}+r\lambda_{\mathrm{s.m.}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times h_{m}(u_{m}(t,y-z))h_{m}(u_{m}(t,-z))(1+|y|^{2})^{k(r-1)/2}\mathrm{d}z\pi(\mathrm{d}y)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq A_{\pi}^{1-\frac{1}{r}}\|u_{m}(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1-\frac{1}{r}}\Gamma(x)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq A_{\pi}^{1-\frac{1}{r}}S^{1-\frac{1}{r}}\Gamma(x)^{\frac{1}{r}},

where
A_{\pi}
is the constant defined in (
1.11
) and

\displaystyle\Gamma(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-(y-z))R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}(x-z)|^{r}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times|u_{m}(t,y-z)|^{\frac{r+1}{2}+r\lambda_{\mathrm{s.m.}}}|u_{m}(t,-z)|^{\frac{r+1}{2}+r\lambda_{\mathrm{s.m.}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times h_{m}(u_{m}(t,y-z))h_{m}(u_{m}(t,-z))(1+|y|^{2})^{k(r-1)/2}\mathrm{d}z\pi(\mathrm{d}y).

Since
r<p/2
, Minkowski’s inequality yields that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Gamma(x)^{\frac{p}{2r}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}^{r}\ast R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}^{r})(y)(1+|y|^{2})^{k(r-1)/2}\pi(\mathrm{d}y)\right)^{\frac{p}{2r}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{m}(t,x)|^{\frac{p(r+1)}{2r}+p\lambda_{\mathrm{s.m.}}}h_{m}(u_{m}(t,x))\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle=:I^{\frac{p}{2r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{m}(t,x)|^{\frac{p(r+1)}{2r}+p\lambda_{\mathrm{s.m.}}}h_{m}(u_{m}(t,x))\mathrm{d}x.

Since
(R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}^{r}\ast R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}^{r})
decays exponentially as
|x|\to\infty
,

I<\infty\Longleftrightarrow\int_{|y|<1}(R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}^{r}\ast R_{\delta_{0}(1-\gamma)}^{r})(y)\pi(\mathrm{d}y)<\infty.

Due to Proposition
1.7
,
I<\infty
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau_{m}(S)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{m}(t,x)|^{\frac{p(r+1)}{2r}+p\lambda_{\mathrm{s.m.}}}h_{m}(u_{m}(t,x))\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq NS^{1+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sup_{t\leq\tau_{m}(S)}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{m}(t,x)|\right)^{\frac{q(r+1)}{2r}+q\lambda_{\mathrm{s.m.}}-\frac{q}{p}-\frac{q\lambda_{\mathrm{s.d.}}}{p\vee q}}\right]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq NS^{1+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}}\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{m}|_{C_{t,x}([0,\tau_{m}(S)]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}\right]^{\frac{1}{q}\left(\frac{r+1}{2r}+\lambda_{\mathrm{s.m.}}-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{s.d.}}}{p\vee q}\right)}.

Step 3.
Conclusion.

Therefore, by Theorem
3.2
, equation (
3.48
) has a unique solution
v_{m}\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}(\tau_{m}(S))
with estimate

\displaystyle\|v_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}(\tau_{m}(S))}

\displaystyle\leq N\bigg(\|u_{0}\|_{U_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}}+\mathbb{E}[|u_{m}|_{C_{t,x}([0,\tau_{m}(S)]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}]^{\frac{1}{q}\left((1+\lambda_{\mathrm{s.m.}})-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{s.d.}}}{p\vee q}\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\mathbb{E}[|u_{m}|_{C_{t,x}([0,\tau_{m}(S)]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}]^{\frac{1}{q}\left((1+\lambda_{\mathrm{b.}})-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{s.d.}}}{p\vee q}\right)}\bigg),

where
N=N(S)
.
If we put
z_{m}:=u_{m}-v_{m}
, then
z_{m}
satisfies

\mathrm{d}z_{m}=(\phi(\Delta)z_{m}+\zeta F_{m}(u_{m}))\mathrm{d}t,\quad z_{m}(0)=0.

It can be easily checked that

z_{m}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}T_{t-s}(\zeta F_{m}(u_{m}))\mathrm{d}s\leq 0.

This implies that
u_{m}\leq v_{m}
, thus, by Young’s inequality

\|v_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}(\tau_{m}(S))}\leq N(S)\|u_{0}\|_{U_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}}+N(\varepsilon,S)+\varepsilon\mathbb{E}[|v_{m}|_{C_{t,x}([0,\tau_{m}(S)]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}]^{1/q}.

By Theorem
3.11
,

\|v_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}(\tau_{m}(S))}\leq N(S)\|u_{0}\|_{U_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}}+N(\varepsilon,S)+N\varepsilon\|v_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}(\tau_{m}(S))}.

If we choose sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, then we have

\|v_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}(\tau_{m}(S))}\leq N(S)\|u_{0}\|_{U_{p,q}^{\phi,\gamma}}+N(\varepsilon,S)=:N_{S}.

(3.51)

By making use of Chebyshev’s inequality, Theorem
3.11
and (
3.51
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\leq\tau}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{m}(t,x)|\geq R\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\leq\tau_{m}(S)\wedge\tau}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v_{m}(t,x)|\geq R\right)+\mathbb{P}(\tau_{m}(S)\leq\tau)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N_{S}}{R}+\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\leq\tau}\|u_{m}(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{m}(t,x)|^{1+\lambda_{\mathrm{s.d.}}}h_{m}(u_{m}(t,x))\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\geq S\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N_{S}}{R}+\frac{N_{\alpha}}{S^{\alpha}}.

Note that
N_{\alpha}
is independent of
m
and
S
.
Since
N_{S}
and
N_{\alpha}
are independent of
m
, the theorem is proved.
∎

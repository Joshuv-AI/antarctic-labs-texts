Modular invariant holographic correlators for 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SYM with general gauge group


Abstract
We study the stress tensor four-point function for 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SYM with gauge group G = S ​ U ​ ( N ) G=SU(N) , S ​ O ​ ( 2 ​ N + 1 ) SO(2N+1) , S ​ O ​ ( 2 ​ N ) SO(2N) or U ​ S ​ p ​ ( 2 ​ N ) USp(2N) at large N N . When G = S ​ U ​ ( N ) G=SU(N) , the theory is dual to type IIB string theory on A ​ d ​ S 5 × S 5 AdS_{5}\times S^{5} with complexified string coupling τ s \tau_{s} , while for the other cases it is dual to the orbifold theory on A ​ d ​ S 5 × S 5 / ℤ 2 AdS_{5}\times S^{5}/\mathbb{Z}_{2} . In all cases we use the analytic bootstrap and constraints from localization to compute 1-loop and higher derivative tree level corrections to the leading supergravity approximation of the correlator. We give perturbative evidence that the localization constraint in the large N N and finite complexified coupling τ \tau limit can be written for each G G in terms of Eisenstein series that are modular invariant in terms of τ s ∝ τ \tau_{s}\propto\tau , which allows us to fix protected terms in the correlator in that limit. In all cases, we find that the flat space limit of the correlator precisely matches the type IIB S-matrix. We also find a closed form expression for the S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) 1-loop Mellin amplitude with supergravity vertices. Finally, we compare our analytic predictions at large N N and finite τ \tau to bounds from the numerical bootstrap in the large N N regime, and find that they are not saturated for any G G and any τ \tau , which suggests that no physical theory saturates these bootstrap bounds.

2.1
Basics

Let us denote the bottom component of the stress tensor multiplet by
S

{\bf 20}^{\prime}

SU(4)_{R}\cong SO(6)_{R}

. We are interested in studying the four-point function
\langle SSSS\rangle
, which is fixed by conformal and
SU(4)

\begin{split}&\langle S(\vec{x}_{1},Y_{1})S(\vec{x}_{2},Y_{2})S(\vec{x}_{3},Y_{3})S(\vec{x}_{4},Y_{4})\rangle=\frac{Y^{2}_{12}Y^{2}_{34}}{{x}_{12}^{4}{x}_{34}^{4}}\mathcal{S}(U,V;\sigma,\tau)\,,\end{split}

(2.1)

where we define

\begin{split}U\equiv\frac{{x}_{12}^{2}{x}_{34}^{2}}{{x}_{13}^{2}{x}_{24}^{2}}\,,\qquad V\equiv\frac{{x}_{14}^{2}{x}_{23}^{2}}{{x}_{13}^{2}{x}_{24}^{2}}\,,\qquad\sigma\equiv\frac{(Y_{1}\cdot Y_{3})(Y_{2}\cdot Y_{4})}{(Y_{1}\cdot Y_{2})(Y_{3}\cdot Y_{4})}\,,\qquad\tau\equiv\frac{(Y_{1}\cdot Y_{4})(Y_{2}\cdot Y_{3})}{(Y_{1}\cdot Y_{2})(Y_{3}\cdot Y_{4})}\,.\end{split}

(2.2)

The constraints of superconformal symmetry are given by the Ward identity in
[
47
]
, whose solution can be formally solved in two different ways. The first solution takes the form

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}(U,V;\sigma,\tau)&=\mathcal{S}_{\text{free}}(U,V;\sigma,\tau)+\Theta(U,V;\sigma,\tau)\mathcal{T}(U,V)\,,\\
\Theta(U,V;\sigma,\tau)&\equiv\tau+[1-\sigma-\tau]V+\tau[\tau-1-\sigma]U+\sigma[\sigma-1-\tau]UV+\sigma V^{2}+\sigma\tau U^{2}\,,\end{split}

(2.3)

where
\mathcal{S}_{\text{free}}(U,V;\sigma,\tau)
is the free theory correlator

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{\text{free}}(U,V;\sigma,\tau)=1+U^{2}\sigma^{2}+\frac{U^{2}}{V^{2}}\tau^{2}+\frac{1}{c}\left(U\sigma+\frac{U}{V}\tau+\frac{U^{2}}{V}\sigma\tau\right)\,,\end{split}

(2.4)

so that all non-trivial interacting information is given by the
R
-symmetry invariant correlator
\mathcal{T}(U,V)
. The second solution takes the form
[
43
]

\begin{split}&\mathcal{S}(U,V;\sigma,\tau)=\Theta(U,V;\sigma,\tau){\mathcal{G}}(U,V)+\Phi_{1}(z,\bar{z};\sigma,\tau)f_{1}(z)+\Phi_{3}(z,\bar{z};\sigma,\tau)f_{2}(z)\\
&+(\Phi_{1}(\bar{z},z;\sigma,\tau)-\Phi_{2}(U;\sigma,\tau))f_{1}(\bar{z})+(\Phi_{3}(\bar{z},z;\sigma,\tau)-\Phi_{2}(U;\sigma,\tau))f_{2}(\bar{z})+\Phi_{2}(U;\sigma,\tau)f_{3}(z)\,,\\
\end{split}

(2.5)

where
\Theta
is the same as in (
2.3
) and we define

\begin{split}U&=z\bar{z}\,,\quad V=(1-z)(1-\bar{z})\,,\quad\Phi_{1}\equiv\frac{z\bar{z}}{z-\bar{z}}+\frac{z^{2}\bar{z}\tau}{(z-\bar{z})(1-z)}+\frac{z\bar{z}^{2}\sigma}{\bar{z}-z}\,,\quad\Phi_{2}\equiv\sigma U\,,\\
\Phi_{3}&\equiv\frac{z(\bar{z}-1)}{z-\bar{z}}+\frac{z^{2}\bar{z}\sigma^{2}}{\bar{z}-z}+\frac{z^{2}\bar{z}\tau^{2}+z^{2}\bar{z}(z-2)\sigma\tau+z(z+\bar{z}-2z\bar{z})\tau}{(z-1)(z-\bar{z})}+\frac{z(z+\bar{z}-\bar{z}^{2})\sigma}{z-\bar{z}}\,.\\
\end{split}

(2.6)

If we then equate (

\begin{split}f_{1}(z)=2+\frac{1}{c}-\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{z-1}\,,\qquad f_{2}(z)=1+\frac{1}{c}-z+\frac{1}{1-z}\,,\qquad f_{3}(z)=\frac{1}{c}+z+\frac{1}{z}\,,\end{split}

(2.7)

while the remaining free part contributes to

\begin{split}{\mathcal{T}}(U,V)={\mathcal{G}}(U,V)-\left[1+\frac{1}{V^{2}}+\frac{1}{c}\frac{1}{V}\right]\,.\end{split}

We can expand

\begin{split}{\mathcal{G}}(U,V)=U^{-2}\sum_{\Delta,\ell}\lambda^{2}_{\Delta,\ell}G_{\Delta+4,\ell}(U,V)+\mathcal{F}^{(0)}_{\text{short}}(z,\bar{z})+c^{-1}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{\text{short}}\,,\end{split}

\begin{split}G_{\Delta,\ell}(U,V)&=\frac{z\bar{z}}{z-\bar{z}}(k_{\Delta+\ell}(z)k_{\Delta-\ell-2}(\bar{z})-k_{\Delta+\ell}(\bar{z})k_{\Delta-\ell-2}(z))\,,\\

(2.10)

while the short multiplets OPE coefficients do not depend on the coupling and so can be computed from the free theory to give the exact expressions

and

[

Appendix D
Lorentzian inversion

In this Appendix we explain how to extract the CFT-data to one-loop from the double discontinuity for the
SU(N)
and orbifold 1-loop terms. We will follow a streamlined version of the method of
[

. The function that describes the CFT-data is given by

c(h,\bar{h})=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dz}{z^{2}}k_{1-h}(z)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\bar{z}}{\bar{z}^{2}}\frac{r_{\bar{h}}^{2}}{\bar{h}-1/2}k_{\bar{h}}(\bar{z})\frac{dDisc[(z\bar{z})(\bar{z}-z)\mathcal{G}(U,V)]}{4\pi^{2}}\,,

where
h=\frac{\Delta-\ell+2}{2}
,
\bar{h}=\frac{\Delta+\ell+4}{2}
and

and we have also introduced

r_{h}=\frac{\Gamma(h)^{2}}{\Gamma(2h-1)}\,.

(D.2)

A given double trace operator with quantum numbers
\Delta=4+2n+\ell+\gamma
and
\ell
produces a pole at
h=3+n+\gamma/2
,
\bar{h}=4+n+\ell+\gamma/2
. For small

h-

z
in that limit yield different twists. The integral over
\bar{z}

\bar{h}
and provides the spin dependence for each twist.

We will consider the inversion integral in a

their contribution to the correlator will be of the form

(D.4)

z

z

where we disregard all poles in
\bar{h}
except the ones at
\bar{h}=1
and

\displaystyle R_{0}(\bar{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\bar{z}}{\bar{z}^{2}}\frac{r_{\bar{h}}^{2}}{\bar{h}-1/2}k_{\bar{h}}(\bar{z})(2\bar{z}^{4}h_{0}(\bar{z}))\,,

(D.6)

\displaystyle R_{1}(\bar{h})

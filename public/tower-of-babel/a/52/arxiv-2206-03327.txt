The Yang-Mills-Higgs functional on complex line bundles: Γ \Gamma -convergence and the London equation
Giacomo Canevari, Federico Luigi Dipasquale, Giandomenico Orlandi

Abstract
We consider the Abelian Yang-Mills-Higgs functional, in the non-self dual scaling, on a complex line bundle over a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . This functional is the natural generalisation of the Ginzburg-Landau model for superconductivity to the non-Euclidean setting. We prove a Γ \Gamma -convergence result, in the strongly repulsive limit, on the functional rescaled by the logarithm of the coupling parameter. As a corollary, we prove that the energy of minimisers concentrates on an area-minimising surface of dimension n − 2 n-2 , while the curvature of minimisers converges to a solution of the London equation.



Introduction

Let
(M,\,g)
be a smooth, compact, connected, oriented, Riemannian manifold
without boundary, of dimension
n\geq 3
. Let
E\to M
be a Hermitian complex
line bundle on
M
, equipped with a (smooth) reference connection
\D_{0}
.
For any
1
-form
A\in W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, we denote by
\D_{A}:=\D_{0}-iA

the associated connection and by
F_{A}
the curvature
2
-form of
\D_{A}
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small parameter.
For any section
u\in W^{1,2}(M,\,E)
of
E
and
any
1
-form
A\in W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
,
we consider the
Ginzburg-Landau
or

Abelian Yang-Mills-Higgs
functional

G_{\varepsilon}(u,\,A):=\int_{M}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|\D_{A}u\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}(1-\left|u\right|^{2})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left|F_{A}\right|^{2}\right)\vol_{g}

(1)

In this paper, we prove a convergence result for minimisers in the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
:
given a sequence of minimisers
\{(u_{\varepsilon}^{\min},\,A_{\varepsilon}^{\min})\}
,
the energy density of
\{(u_{\varepsilon}^{\min},\,A_{\varepsilon}^{\min})\}
concentrates
on an
(n-2)
-dimensional surface
S_{*}
, which is area-minimising
in a distinguished homology class, determined
by the topology of the bundle

E\to M
(i.e., the Poincaré-dual to the first
Chern class
c_{1}(E)\in H^{2}(M;\,\mathbb{Z})
).
On the other hand, the curvature of
A_{\varepsilon}^{\min}

converges to a solution of the London equation,
with a singular source term carried by
S_{*}
.
This convergence result for minimisers is stated in Corollary
B

and it is deduced from our main result, Theorem
A
below, which
provides a full
\Gamma
-convergence theorem for the functionals
G_{\varepsilon}
. Moreover, and in agreement with known results in the Euclidean setting (c.f., e.g.,
[
41
,
2
]
), Theorem
A
shows that energy concentration on topological singular sets is not unique to minimisers but is a general feature for sequences
\{(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\}
satisfying a (natural) logarithmic asymptotic bound on the energy.

Functionals of the form (
1
) were originally
proposed by V. Ginzburg and L. Landau in 1950
[
33
]
as a model of superconductors subject to a magnetic field
(where
u
is an order parameter such that
\left|u\right|^{2}
is
proportional to the density of electronic Cooper pairs,
while
A
is the vector potential for the magnetic field).
The theory accounts for most commonly observed effects
(such as the quantisation of magnetic flux,
the Meissner effect, and the emergence of Abrikosov vortex lattices,
see e.g.
[
62
]
); moreover, it can be justified
as a suitable limit of Bardeen, Cooper and Schrieffer’s
microscopic theory
[
8
]
.
Ginzburg-Landau functionals, or variants thereof,
arise in other areas of physics — for instance,
superfluidity
(see e.g.
[
29
]
)
and particle physics, for (
1
)
is the Abelian version of Yang-Mills-Higgs
action functional in gauge theory (see e.g.
[
39
]
).

Being invariant under gauge transformations is, indeed,
one of the most prominent features of the functional (
1
).
In a Euclidean domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
the functional (
1
) reduces to

\mathcal{GL}^{\text{magn}}_{\varepsilon}(u,\,A):=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla u-iAu\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}(1-\left|u\right|^{2})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left|\d A\right|^{2}\right)\d x,

(2)

where
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,\,\mathbb{C})
is a complex-valued map and
A\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}\Omega)
is a real-valued one-form on
\Omega
.
Assuming for simplicity that
\Omega
is simply connected,
the invariance of (
2
) under gauge-transformations
can be expressed as follows:
for any
(u,\,A)\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,\,\mathbb{C})\times W^{1,2}(\Omega,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}\Omega)

and any
\theta\in W^{2,2}(\Omega,\,\mathbb{R})
, there holds

\mathcal{GL}_{\varepsilon}^{\text{magn}}(u,\,A)=\mathcal{GL}_{\varepsilon}^{\text{magn}}(e^{i\theta}u,\,A+\d\theta).

(3)

The property (
3
) suggests that Ginzburg-Landau functionals
are naturally set in the context of complex line bundles over a manifold.
Indeed, the gauge group
\mathbb{S}^{1}\simeq\mathrm{U}(1)
acts
on a pair
(u,\,A)
precisely in the same way as transition functions of
a bundle act (locally) on sections and connection forms.
In this general setting, gauge-invariance takes the following general form:
for any
(u,\,A)\in W^{1,2}(M,\,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

and
\Phi\in W^{2,2}(M,\,\mathbb{S}^{1})
, consider the transformation

(u,\,A)\mapsto(\Phi u,A-i\,\Phi^{-1}\,\d\Phi),

(4)

where
\Phi u
is defined by the fibre-wise action
of the structure group
\mathrm{U}(1)\simeq\mathbb{S}^{1}
on
E
. Then
there holds

G_{\varepsilon}(u,\,A)=G_{\varepsilon}(\Phi u,\,A-i\,\Phi^{-1}\,\d\Phi)

(5)

Gauge-invariance will play a crucial rôle in this paper. Physically, each observable quantity must be gauge-invariant and the energy is only one
of them. For instance, each term in the energy density of
G_{\varepsilon}
is gauge-invariant, as well as
F_{A}
(whose physical counterpart in superconductivity is, in fact, the magnetic field).

Minimisers of gauge-invariant functionals on manifolds,
such as (
1
), include several examples
of physically relevant objects (such as Bogomolnyi monopoles, vortices, instantons, Hermite-Einstein connections on Kälher manifolds).
Moreover, such objects play an important rôle in topology and complex geometry.
For example, Yang-Mills minimisers are used in the classification
of stable holomorphic vector bundles on complex manifolds
(this is the content of Donaldson, Uhlenbeck and Yau’s
theorem
[
28
,
63
]
; for generalisations
to Yang-Mills-Higgs minimisers,
see e.g.
[
18
,
19
,
31
]
).
Functionals such as (
1
)
may also be coupled to Einstein equations;
this approach is relevant to cosmology, as it provides
a model for gravity-driven spontaneous symmetry breaking
[
60
]
.

We are interested in
the asymptotic regime as
\varepsilon\to 0
, which is known as
the
London limit
within the context of superconductivity, or the
strongly repulsive limit
,
within the context of particle physics.
This limit is characterised by the emergence of

topological singularities
—
the energy of minimisers concentrates,
to leading order, on
(n-2)
-dimensional sets, whose
global structure depends on the topology of the bundle
E\to M
.
We are interested in providing a variational characterisation,
in the sense of
\Gamma
-convergence,
of those singularities as being area-minimising in a homology class.
We expect that this variational characterisation
will provide indications on the dynamics
of singularities arising in the limit of the
corresponding time-dependent models.
For instance, for a generalisation of (
1
)
on a Lorentzian manifold, we expect that the energy
concentrate on time-like relativistic strings or
M
-branes
(see e.g.
[
40
,
9
,
10
]

for the analysis of related problems in Minkowski space-time).
Moreover, we expect the heat flow of (
1
)
to be related to motion by mean curvature
(see
[
14
]
for the asymptotics
of a non-gauge-invariant problem, with no dependence on
A
,
in the Euclidean setting).

The asymptotic analysis of (
1
) in the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
heavily relies on
analogous results for the simplified Ginzburg-Landau functional, i.e.

\mathcal{GL}_{\varepsilon}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}(1-\left|u\right|^{2})^{2}\right)\d x

(6)

The functional (
6
) was first considered
by Bethuel, Brezis and Hélein

11

, in case

is a two-dimensional Euclidean domain.

\Gamma

were proved in
[

,

]

These results show that energy concentration on area-minimising surfaces
(of codimension two) is already a feature of the
simplified functionals (
6

— cf. Modica and Mortola’s
\Gamma
-convergence result
in the real-valued case,
[
45
]
.
In fact, both the functionals (
2
), (
6
)
and the corresponding heat flows
have been extensively studied in Euclidean domains
(see e.g.
[
55
,
50
]
),
as well as on Riemannian manifolds
[
24
,

,
26
]
.

In case

is the tangent bundle on a closed Riemann surface
and the connection

is fixed, asymptotics for minimisers

[

,

Minimisers of (

were studied in case the base manifold

]
.
Other results address a
self-dual
variant of the
functional (
1
), in which the curvature term
\left|F_{A}\right|^{2}
is replaced by
\varepsilon^{2}\left|F_{A}\right|^{2}

— see e.g.
[
36
]
for the two-dimensional case
and
[
53
,
52
]
for the higher-dimensional case.
In a way, self-duality is a sort of additional symmetry in the Yang-Mills-Higgs functionals, with applications in the theory of minimal surfaces, as explored e.g. in the recent papers
[
53
,
25
,
52
]
.
However, the non self-dual scaling (

original physical motivation of the Ginzburg-Landau functional.
To better explain this point, let us introduce the

gauge-invariant Jacobian

J(u,\,A)
of a pair
(u,\,A)
, defined pointwise (when the right-hand-side of (
7
) below makes sense) as the
2
-form

J(u,\,A)(X,\,Y):=\left\langle i\D_{A,X}u,\,\D_{A,Y}u\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\left|u\right|^{2}\right)F_{A}(X,\,Y)

(7)

for any smooth test field
X
,
Y
on
M
. Here
\left\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\right\rangle
is the real-valued scalar product induced by the Hermitian form on
E
.
Plainly,

is well-defined for any
(u,\,A)\in W^{1,2}(M,\,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
such that
G_{\varepsilon}(u,\,A)<+\infty
. In superconductivity, there is a physical observable associated with gauge-invariant Jacobians: the
supercurrent vorticity
(cf., e.g.,
[
62
, Chapter 5]
and
[
39
, Chapter 1]
).

As it is easy to check, for minimisers

(u_{\varepsilon}^{\min},\,A_{\varepsilon}^{\min})

of the non-self dual functional (
1

the curvatures
F_{\varepsilon}:=F_{A_{\varepsilon}^{\min}}
satisfy the (gauge-invariant)

-\Delta F_{\varepsilon}+F_{\varepsilon}=2J_{\varepsilon},

(8)

a distinctive feature of superconductivity (see, e.g.,
[
62
, Chapters 1 and 5]
).
In Corollary
B
below, we show that
the curvatures
F_{\varepsilon}=F_{A^{\min}_{\varepsilon}}

converge, up to extraction of a subsequence, to a
solution

of the (limiting) London equation

-\Delta F_{*}+F_{*}=2\pi J_{*}.

(9)

The right-hand side
J_{*}
of (
9
) is the
limit of the Jacobians, i.e.
J_{\varepsilon}\to\pi J_{*}

(cf. Theorem
A
).
J_{*}
is a singular measure,
with values in
2
-forms, carried by an area-minimising set of dimension
n-2
.

By contrast, minimisers of the self-dual functionals do
not
converge to solutions of (
9
);
in the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, Moreover, while minimisers of the self-dual functionals have uniformly bounded energy as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
minimisers
(u_{\varepsilon}^{\min},\,A_{\varepsilon}^{\min})

of (
1

G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}^{\min},\,A_{\varepsilon}^{\min})\to+\infty

\varepsilon\to 0

, preventing compactness even in weak topologies in Sobolev spaces and making the proof of Theorem

quite challenging, as in fact is for its Euclidean counterpart in

]
.

One might expect that critical points
of (
1
) converge, in a suitable sense,
to minimal surfaces, as is the case for (
14
) (see
[
12
]
)
and for the self-dual Yang-Mills-Higgs energies (see

Here, we prove this result for sequences of minimisers of (

Main results.

We consider sequences
\{(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})\}\subset W^{1,2}(M,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

that satisfy a
logarithmic energy bound

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\frac{G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}<+\infty

(10)

Our main result, Theorem
A
below,
is a
\Gamma
-convergence result for the rescaled
functionals
\frac{G_{\varepsilon}}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}
.
The topology of the
\Gamma
-convergence is defined in terms of
the Jacobian of
(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})
.

We denote by
\star
the Hodge dual operator, regarded
as a map from
k
-forms to
(n-k)
-currents and from
k
-currents
to
(n-k)
-forms (upon composition with the natural isomorphism
between vectors and covectors, induced by the metric
— see (
1.1
) below and
Appendix

We consider a distinguished, non-empty class
\mathcal{C}

of integer-multiplicity, rectifiable
(n-2)
-currents with no boundary.
The class
\mathcal{C}
is uniquely defined in terms of
the topology of the bundle
E\to M
; more precisely,

\mathcal{C}\in H_{n-2}(M;\,\mathbb{Z})
is Poincaré-dual to
the first Chern class
c_{1}(E)\in H^{2}(M;\,\mathbb{Z})
of the bundle
(see (
1.6
) below for more details).

.

The following statements hold.

(i)

Let

, with values in
2

\pi\left|J_{*}\right|\!(M)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}

(11)

Moreover,
\star J_{*}
is an integer-multiplicity, rectifiable
(n-2)
-cycle
in the class
\mathcal{C}
.

(ii)

Let
S_{*}
be an integer-multiplicity, rectifiable
(n-2)
-cycle

\mathcal{C}

Remark 1
.

Not every pair
(u,\,A)\in W^{1,2}(M,\,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

has finite energy. Conversely, not every Sobolev pair

(u,\,A)\in W^{1,1}(M,\,E)\times W^{1,1}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
with
G_{\varepsilon}(u,\,A)<+\infty
is such that
(u,\,A)\in W^{1,2}(M,\,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
(for instance, it may or may not be true that
\left|\D_{0}u\right|=\left|\D_{A}u+iAu\right|\in L^{2}(M)
). For simplicity, we have chosen to state our results in terms of
W^{1,2}
-pairs, but our results extend to all Sobolev pairs of finite energy, with no significant change in the proofs.

Since the class

As a corollary, we obtain a variational characterisation

Given

,

as

\mu_{\varepsilon}(u,\,A):=\frac{1}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|\D_{A}u\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}(1-\left|u\right|^{2})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left|F_{A}\right|^{2}\right)

(12)

.

Let
(u_{\varepsilon}^{\min},\,A_{\varepsilon}^{\min})
be a

G_{\varepsilon}
in
W^{1,2}(M,\,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
.
Then, there exist bounded measures

, with values in

.

depends heavily on

-convergence results obtained in the Euclidean

More precisely, given a sequence
\{(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\}

we construct a sequence of

Specifically, the sections

1
Preliminaries

We will frequently encounter Sobolev spaces of sections of a bundle.
For the convenience of the reader, we provide the definitions
and state some basic properties in Appendix
A
.
The notation we use is fairly self-explanatory.
However, we stress that the symbols such as
W^{1,p}(M,\,E)

denote Sobolev spaces of
W^{1,p}
-
sections
of the bundle
E\to M
.
In case
E
is a trivial bundle,
E=M\times\mathbb{C}
or
E=M\times\mathbb{R}
,
we write
W^{1,p}(M,\,\mathbb{C})
,
W^{1,p}(M,\,\mathbb{R})

instead of
W^{1,p}(M,\,M\times\mathbb{C})
,
W^{1,p}(M,\,M\times\mathbb{R})
.

The notation we use for differential forms is rather standard, too.
For instance, we denote as
\#\colon\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\to\Lambda_{k}\mathrm{T}M
,

\flat\colon\Lambda_{k}\mathrm{T}M\to\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M
the isometric
isomorphisms between vectors and forms induced by the metric on
M
,
and by
*\colon\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\to\Lambda^{n-k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M

the Hodge dual operator induced by the metric and the orientation of
M
.
We define an operator

\star\colon\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\to\Lambda_{n-k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M,\qquad\star\colon\Lambda_{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\to\Lambda^{n-k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M

as follows: for any
k
-form
\omega
and any
k
-vector
v
,

\star\omega:=(*\omega)^{\#},\qquad\star v:=*(v^{\flat})

(1.1)

The operator
\star
can be extended to
an operator between currents and form-valued distributions;
see Appendix
B
for details.
We denote by
\d
,
\d^{*}
the exterior differential and codifferential
of forms, respectively. The codifferential of a
k
-form
\omega
is
defined by
\d^{*}\omega:=(-1)^{n(k-1)+1}*\d*\,\omega
.

We recall a few basic notions about currents in Appendix
B
.
Given a current
S
, we will write
\partial S
for its boundary
(defined as in (
B.1
) below),
\mathbb{M}(S)
for its mass
(see (
B.4
) below) and
\mathbb{F}(S)
for its (integer-multiplicity) flat norm
(see (
B.7
) below).

It is convenient to revisit the definition of Jacobian, (
7
).
Given a pair
(u,\,A)\in W^{1,2}(M,\,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

such that
\D_{A}u\in L^{2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)
,
we define the
gauge-invariant pre-Jacobian
as the
1
-form

j(u,\,A):=\left\langle\D_{A}u,\,iu\right\rangle=\left\langle\D_{0}u,\,iu\right\rangle-\left|u\right|^{2}A

(1.2)

Under the assumptions above,
j(u,\,A)
is integrable,
so it makes sense to consider its differential
in the sense of distributions.
We define the (distributional)
gauge-invariant Jacobian
as

J(u,\,A):=\frac{1}{2}\d j(u,\,A)+\frac{1}{2}F_{A}

(1.3)

In case the pair
(u,\,A)
is smooth,
an explicit computation shows that the Jacobian
as defined by (
1.3
) agrees with (
7
).
The same remains true if
(u,\,A)
satisfies
G_{\varepsilon}(u,\,A)<+\infty
,
by a truncation and density argument.
However, (
1.3
) allows us to define the Jacobian
for a broader class of pairs
(u,\,A)

— for instance, when
u\in W^{1,1}(M,\,E)\cap L^{\infty}(M,\,E)

and
A\in L^{1}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
.

Remark 1.1
.

Since, for any vector field
X
on
M
,
(\D_{A,X}u)(p)
depends only on
X(p)
and the values of
u
along any smooth curve representing
X(p)

[
44
, p. 501]
, it follows that
j(u,\,A)
is a local operator and commutes with restrictions. Moreover, since the local representations
F_{A}
are independent of any chosen local trivialization because
E\to M
is of rank 1
[
27
, Chapter V, Remark 3.2]
, we see that also
J(u,A)
is a local operator and commutes with restrictions.

Remark 1.2
.

Let
u\colon M\to E
,
A\colon M\to\mathrm{T}^{*}M
,
B\colon M\to\mathrm{T}^{*}M

be such that
J(u,\,A)
,
J(u,\,B)
are well-defined
— for instance,
u\in(W^{1,1}\cap L^{\infty})(M,\,E)
,

A\in L^{1}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
,
B\in L^{1}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
.
As an immediate consequence of (
1.2
)–(
1.3
),
we have

\displaystyle j(u,A)-j(u,B)=(B-A)\left|u\right|^{2}

(1.4)

\displaystyle J(u,A)-J(u,B)=\frac{1}{2}\d((A-B)(1-\left|u\right|^{2})).

In particular, if
\left|u\right|=1
a.e. then
J(u,\,A)=J(u,\,B)
.

The distributional Jacobian
is continuous with respect to suitable notions of weak convergence.
For the convenience of the reader, we recall a result
in this direction. We denote by
j(u)
,
J(u)
the pre-Jacobian
and Jacobian with respect to the reference connection
\D_{0}
, i.e.

j(u):=j(u,\,0)=\left\langle\D_{0}u,\,iu\right\rangle,\qquad J(u):=J(u,\,0)=\frac{1}{2}\d j(u)+\frac{1}{2}F_{0}

(1.5)

where
F_{0}
is the curvature of the reference connection
\D_{0}
.

j(u)
and
J(u)
are well-defined for any
u\in(W^{1,p}\cap L^{q})(M,\,E)
,
with
p\in[1,\,+\infty]
and
q:=p^{\prime}\in[1,\,+\infty]

such that
1/p+1/q=1
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
p\in[1,\,+\infty]
and
q:=p^{\prime}\in[1,\,+\infty]

be such that
1/p+1/q=1
.
If the sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}\subset(W^{1,p}\cap L^{q})(M,\,E)

is such that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
weakly in
W^{1,p}(M)
and strongly in
L^{q}(M)
,
then
J(u_{\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup^{*}J(u)
weakly
∗
in the sense of distributions.

Proof.

Thanks to (
1.5
), it suffices to show that

j(u_{\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup^{*}j(u)
weakly
∗
in the sense of distributions.
This follows from the Hölder inequality.
∎

In fact, the Jacobian
J(u,\,A)
satisfies suitable
continuity properties as a function
of both
u
and
A
(see e.g.
[
52
, Proposition 3.1]
).

Finally, we introduce a distinguished
(homology) class of
(n-2)
currents,
\mathcal{C}\in H_{n-2}(M;\,\mathbb{Z})
,
as follows. Let
w\colon M\to E
be a
smooth
section of
E
.
We assume that
w
is transverse to the zero-section of
E
;
that means, for any point
p\in M
such that
w(p)=0
,
the differential
\d_{p}w
induces a
surjective
linear map

\d_{p}w\colon\mathrm{T}_{p}M\to E_{p}\simeq\mathbb{C}

where
E_{p}
is the fibre of
E
at
p
. Such a section
w
exists;
in fact, by Thom’s transversality theorem
(see e.g.
[
20
, Theorem 14.6]
), any smooth section
can be approximated (e.g., uniformly) by transverse sections.
Transversality implies that the inverse image
Z:=w^{-1}(0)

is a smooth manifold without boundary, of dimension
n-2
.
As both
M
and
E
are oriented manifolds (the orientation on
E

is the one induced by the complex structure on each fibre),
the manifold
Z
can be given an orientation, in a natural way

[
17
, Proposition 12.7]
. As a consequence,
there is a well-defined
(n-2)
-current
\llbracket Z\rrbracket
,
carried by
Z
, with unit multiplicity.
We have
\partial\llbracket Z\rrbracket=0
,
because
Z
is manifold without boundary. We define

\begin{split}\mathcal{C}:=\big\{\llbracket Z\rrbracket+\partial R&\colon R\textrm{ is an integer-multiplicity, rectifiable $(n-1)$-current}\\
&\qquad\textrm{with }\mathbb{M}(R)+\mathbb{M}(\partial R)<+\infty\big\}\end{split}

(1.6)

The class
\mathcal{C}
does not depend on the choice
of
w
(see
[
17
, Proposition 12.8]
).
Moreover, by the boundary rectifiability theorem
(see e.g.
[
58
, Theorem 6.3]
), all the elements
of
\mathcal{C}
are integer-multiplicity, rectifiable

(n-2)
-currents with no boundary.
In the topological jargon,
\mathcal{C}\in H_{n-2}(M;\,\mathbb{Z})

is Poincaré-dual to the first Chern class
c_{1}(E)\in H^{2}(M;\,\mathbb{Z})
.
Equivalently,
\mathcal{C}
is Poincaré-dual to the Euler class of
E
,
regarded as a real bundle over
M
with the orientation
induced by the complex structure.
The following statement motivates our interest in the class
\mathcal{C}
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
u\in W^{1,1}(M,\,E)
be such that

\left|u\right|=1
a.e. If
J(u)
is a bounded measure, then

\frac{1}{\pi}\star J(u)
is an integer-multiplicity rectifiable

(n-2)
-current and

\frac{1}{\pi}\star J(u)\in\mathcal{C}

Proposition
1.4
establishes a
topological relationship between the singular set of a unit-norm section

M\to E
and the zero set of a generic
smooth
section.
A similar principle applies to several different contexts;
see, for instance,
[
1
, Theorem 3.8]

and
[
2
, Proposition 5.3]
for results in the Euclidean setting,

[
4
, Proposition 2.8]
for sections of real line bundles,

[
6
]
for sections of a principal
\mathbb{Z}
- or
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
-bundle,

[
52
, Corollary 3.3]
for sections of
complex line bundles. For the reader’s convenience, we reproduce below
the key steps of the proof in our context, emphasising the topological aspects.

Sketch of the proof of Proposition
1.4
.

Let
w
be a smooth section of
E\to M
that is transverse
to the zero section of
E
, as above. Let
u_{0}:=w/\left|w\right|
.
The section
u_{0}
is well-defined and smooth away from
Z:=w^{-1}(0)
.
Moreover,
u_{0}\in W^{1,p}(M,\,E)
for any
p\in[1,\,2)
.
Indeed, since
w
is transverse to the zero section of
E
,
the differential
\d w
restricted to the normal bundle
\mathrm{N}Z
of
Z

is a fibre-wise isomorphism
\mathrm{N}Z\to E
. As
M
is compact,
it follows that there exists a constant
C
such that, for any
x\in M
,

\dist(x,\,Z)\geq C\left|w(x)\right|

(1.7)

Therefore,

\int_{M}\left|\D_{0}u_{0}\right|^{p}\,\vol_{g}\lesssim\int_{M}\frac{\left|\D_{0}w\right|^{p}}{\left|w\right|^{p}}\,\vol_{g}\lesssim\left\|\D_{0}w\right\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}^{p}\int_{M}\frac{\vol_{g}}{\dist^{p}(\cdot,\,Z)}

(1.8)

The integral at the right-hand side is finite for any
p\in[1,\,2)
,
because
Z
has codimension
2
(see e.g.
[
2
, Lemma 8.3]
).
Moreover, for a suitable orientation of
Z

(as in
[
17
, Proposition 12.7]
), there holds

\star J(u_{0})=\pi\llbracket Z\rrbracket

(1.9)

As the Jacobian is a local operator (by Remark
1.1
),
it suffices to check that (
1.9
) is satisfied in an arbitrary
coordinate neighbourhood
U\subseteq M
. Due to Remark
1.2
,

J(u_{0|U})
is equal to the Jacobian of
u_{0|U}
with respect to the
flat connection,
\bar{J}:=\frac{1}{2}\d\langle\d u_{0},iu_{0}\rangle
.
For the latter, we have
\star\bar{J}=\pi\llbracket Z\rrbracket
in
U

(this computation is similar to,
e.g.,
[
42
, Example 3.4]
)
and hence (
1.9
) follows.

Let
u\in W^{1,1}(M)
be such that
\left|u\right|=1
a.e. in
M
.
We define a map
\Phi\colon M\to\mathbb{C}
as

\Phi:=\left\langle u,\,u_{0}\right\rangle+i\,\left\langle u,\,iu_{0}\right\rangle

so that
u=\Phi u_{0}
. As
\left|u\right|=\left|u_{0}\right|=1
a.e.,
we have
\left|\Phi\right|=1
a.e. in
M
. Moreover,
\Phi\in W^{1,1}(M)

and, by explicit computation,

\star J(u)=\star\bar{J}(\Phi)+\star J(u_{0})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{homology3}}}{{=}}\star\bar{J}(\Phi)+\pi\llbracket Z\rrbracket

(1.10)

where
\bar{J}(\Phi):=\frac{1}{2}\d\left\langle\d\Phi,\,i\Phi\right\rangle
.
Let
E\subseteq M
be the set of points where
\Phi
is not approximately
differentiable. Then,
E
is negligible (with respect to the volume measure)
and
M\setminus E
can be written as a finite union
of closed sets,
M\setminus E=\bigcup_{j=1}^{+\infty}F_{j}
, such that

\Phi
is Lipschitz-continuous on each
F_{j}
(see e.g.
[
30
, 3.1.18]
,

[
35
, Theorems 11 and 12]
).
As a consequence, for
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.
y\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, the
set
N_{y}:=\Phi^{-1}(y)\setminus E
is countably
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-rectifiable
and, by the coarea formula,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(N_{y})<+\infty

(the details of the argument are as in
[
1
, Section 7.5]
).
For a.e.
y\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, we have

\star\bar{J}(\Phi)=\pi\,(-1)^{n-1}\,\partial\llbracket N_{y}\rrbracket

(1.11)

Indeed, for each coordinate neighboorhood
U\subseteq M
,
we have
\star\bar{J}(\Phi)=\pi\,(-1)^{n-1}\,\partial\llbracket N_{y}\rrbracket

in
U
due to
[
1
, Theorem 3.8]
. As the Jacobian is a local
operator, (
1.11
) follows by partition of unity.
In particular,
\frac{1}{\pi}\star\bar{J}(\Phi)

is the boundary of an integer-multiplicity, rectifiable
(n-1)
-current.
Suppose now that
J(u)
is a bounded measure. Then,

\bar{J}(\Phi)
is a bounded measure, too.
By combining (
1.10
) and (
1.11
),
we obtain that
\frac{1}{\pi}\star\bar{J}(\Phi)
belongs to
the class
\mathcal{C}
defined by (
1.6
).
By Federer and Fleming’s boundary rectifiability
theorem
[
30
, 4.2.16(2)]
,
if
J(u)
has finite mass, then
\frac{1}{\pi}\star J(u)

is an integer-multiplicity rectifiable
(n-2)
-current.
This completes the proof.
∎

2.1
Compactness for the Jacobians

2.1.1
A truncation argument

In some of the arguments below, it will be useful to assume
that sequence
u_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(M,\,E)
.
Fortunately, it is possible to reduce to this case
by means of a classical truncation argument.
We formulate this argument in terms of
pairs
(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})
, for later use.
Let
\{(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\}\subset W^{1,2}(M,\,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

be a sequence that satisfies

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\frac{G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}<+\infty

(2.1)

For each
\varepsilon>0
, we define
v_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,2}(M,\,E)
as

v_{\varepsilon}:=\begin{cases}u_{\varepsilon}&\textrm{where }\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|\leq 1\\[8.0pt]
\dfrac{u_{\varepsilon}}{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|}&\textrm{where }\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|>1.\end{cases}

(2.2)

By construction,
\left|v_{\varepsilon}\right|\leq 1
in
M
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The sequence
\{v_{\varepsilon}\}
defined by (
2.2
) satisfies

G_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\leq G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})

(2.3)

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover, under the assumption (
2.1
)
there holds

J(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})-J(v_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\to 0\qquad\textrm{in }W^{-1,p}(M)\quad\textrm{for any }p\textrm{ such that }1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}

(2.4)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

In the proof of Lemma
2.1
and in the sequel,
we will make repeated use of the following observation.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
u\in W^{1,2}(M,\,E)
and any
A\in W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
,
there holds

\D_{A}u=\frac{\d(\left|u\right|)}{\left|u\right|}u+\frac{j(u,\,A)}{\left|u\right|^{2}}\,iu

a.e. in the set
\{u\neq 0\}
.

Proof.

In
\{u\neq 0\}
, the pair
(u,\,iu)
is an orthogonal
frame for (the real bundle associated with)
E
. Therefore,
keeping in mind that the connection
\D_{A}

is compatible with the metric (i.e., (
A.21
)), we obtain

\D_{A}u=\left\langle\D_{A}u,\,u\right\rangle\frac{u}{\left|u\right|^{2}}+\left\langle\D_{A}u,\,iu\right\rangle\frac{iu}{\left|u\right|^{2}}=\d\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|u\right|^{2}\right)\frac{u}{\left|u\right|^{2}}+j(u,\,A)\frac{iu}{\left|u\right|^{2}}

in
\{u\neq 0\}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Towards the proof of (

), we observe that

2.2
implies

\begin{split}\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}v_{\varepsilon}=-\frac{\d(\left|v_{\varepsilon}\right|)}{\left|v_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|}\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}=j(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\frac{iu_{\varepsilon}}{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{3}}\end{split}

(2.5)

in the set
\{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|>1\}
. As a consequence,

\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}v_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}=\frac{\left|j(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\right|^{2}}{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{4}}\leq\left|\d(\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|)\right|^{2}+\frac{\left|j(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\right|^{2}}{\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}}=\left|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}

in

&\hskip-4.83691pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{trunc2}}}{{\leq}}\left\|\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}-1\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\left\|\D_{A_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\end{split}

\left\|j(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})-j(v_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{1}(M)}\lesssim\varepsilon\left|\log\varepsilon\right|

\left\|J(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})-J(v_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{W^{-1,1}(M)}\lesssim\varepsilon\left|\log\varepsilon\right|

2.1.2
The Jacobians are compact in

Throughout the rest of this section, we consider
a sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}\subset W^{1,2}(M,\,E)

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\frac{E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}<+\infty

(2.11)

We work with the reference connection
\D_{0}
on
E
and
denote by
j(u_{\varepsilon}):=j(u_{\varepsilon},\,0)

J(u_{\varepsilon}):=J(u_{\varepsilon},\,0)

Our first goal is to prove that

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,2}(M,\,E)

Then, there exists a (non-relabelled) subsequence and a

In the Euclidean setting, compactness results analogue to

[

,
2
]
.
We will deduce Lemma
2.3
from its Euclidean counterparts
by a localisation argument.
Let
U\subset M
be a smooth, contractible domain in
M
,
all contained in a coordinate chart of
M
.
By working in local coordinates, we identify
U
with
a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, equipped with a smooth Riemannian metric

.
Moreover, as
U

the bundle
E\to M
trivialises over

.
Therefore, we can (and do) identify sections of

U\to\mathbb{C}
.
The reference connection
\D_{0}
, restricted to

\left\|\d(\left|w_{\varepsilon}\right|)\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}\to 0\qquad\textrm{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

(2.42)

Indeed, let

S_{\varepsilon}:=\left\{x\in M\colon\left|\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|-1\right|\geq\varepsilon^{1/2}\right\}

By construction,
\left|w_{\varepsilon}\right|=\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|f_{\varepsilon}(\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|)

in
M
and
\left|w_{\varepsilon}\right|=1
in

, so

\left\|\d(\left|w_{\varepsilon}\right|)\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq\left(\left\|f_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\|f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\left\|\d(\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|)\right\|_{L^{p}(S_{\varepsilon})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{compu32}}}{{\lesssim}}\left\|\d(\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|)\right\|_{L^{p}(S_{\varepsilon})}

Lemma
2.2
, the Hölder inequality
and the energy estimate (
2.11
) imply

\left\|\d(\left|w_{\varepsilon}\right|)\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}\lesssim\left\|\D_{0}u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{p}(S_{\varepsilon})}\lesssim\left(\vol_{g}(S_{\varepsilon})\right)^{1/p-1/2}\left|\log\varepsilon\right|^{1/2}

(2.43)

On the other hand,
(\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}-1)^{2}\geq\varepsilon
on
S_{\varepsilon}
,
so the energy estimate (
2.11
) implies

\vol_{g}(S_{\varepsilon})\lesssim\varepsilon\left|\log\varepsilon\right|

Combining (
2.43
) with (
2.44
), we obtain (
2.42
).

Step 5
.

We claim that

\left\|\D_{0}w_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq C_{p}

(2.45)

Once (
2.45
) is proved, the lemma follows.
Indeed, if (
2.45
) holds, then we extract a subsequence
such that
w_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup w_{*}
weakly in
W^{1,p}(M)
.
The limit
w_{*}\in W^{1,p}(M,\,E)
satisfies
J(w_{*})=\pi J_{*}
,
due to (
2.39
) and Proposition
1.3
.
Moreover, the estimate (
2.41
) implies
that
\left|w_{*}\right|=1
, as required.

We proceed to the proof of (
2.45
).
Let

T_{\varepsilon}:=\left\{x\in M\colon\left|w_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\right\}=\left\{x\in M\colon\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\right\}

On the one hand, Lemma
2.2
gives

\begin{split}\left\|\D_{0}w_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{p}(M\setminus T_{\varepsilon})}\lesssim\left\|\d(\left|w_{\varepsilon}\right|)\right\|_{L^{p}(M\setminus T_{\varepsilon})}+\left\|j(w_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{p}(M\setminus T_{\varepsilon})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{compu34},\,\eqref{compu41}}}{{\lesssim}}C_{p}\end{split}

(2.46)

On the other hand, the energy estimate (
2.11
)
implies that
\vol_{g}(T_{\varepsilon})\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\left|\log\varepsilon\right|
.
By applying the Hölder inequality, we obtain

\left\|\D_{0}u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{p}(T_{\varepsilon})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2/p-1}\left|\log\varepsilon\right|^{1/p}

(2.47)

Now, (
2.46
) and (
2.47
) imply (
2.45
).
∎

As a byproduct of the arguments above, we have proved the following statement.

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,2}(M,\,E)
be such that

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\left(\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}+\frac{E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}\right)<+\infty

(2.48)

Then, there exists functions
\rho_{\varepsilon}\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
,
maps
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\colon M\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
and a (non-relabelled)
subsequence such that
w_{\varepsilon}:=\rho_{\varepsilon}\,\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}

converges weakly in
W^{1,p}(M)
, for any
p\in[1,\,n/(n-1))
,
to a limit map
w_{*}\in W^{1,p}(M,\,E)

with
\left|w_{*}\right|=1
a.e.
Moreover,
J(u_{\varepsilon})-J(w_{\varepsilon})\to 0
in
W^{-1,p}(M)

for any
p\in[1,\,n/(n-1))
.

Thus, any sequence

\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
bounded in
L^{\infty}(M)
and with
E_{\varepsilon}
-energy of order

\left|\log\varepsilon\right|
can be split into a compact and a non-compact part (with respect to the weak
W^{1,p}
-topology). Moreover, Corollary
2.12
asserts that the compact part stores the information necessary to determine the topological energy-concentration set, while the non-compact part of the sequence is, in this sense, “topologically irrelevant”.

On a qualitative level, the lack of compactness is due to wild
oscillations in the phases made possible by the large amount of energy at
disposal. At first glance, this inconvenient seems to be “cured” by the gauge transformations
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
. However, we must emphasise that
the “penalisations”
\rho_{\varepsilon}
, even if small in uniform norm as
\varepsilon\to 0
, play a subtle rôle. Indeed, Remark
2.13
below points out that gauge transformations alone are in general not sufficient to perform the splitting and obtain compactness.

Remark 2.13
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,2}(M,\,E)
be a sequence that
satisfies (
2.48
). In general, it may
not

be possible to find maps
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\colon M\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
such
that
\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
W^{1,p}(M)
.
(A counterexample, when

and

is the trivial bundle

, is given by the sequence

u_{\varepsilon}(x):=1+\varepsilon\sin(\left|\log\varepsilon\right|^{1/2}x/\varepsilon)
).
However, if
u_{\varepsilon}
are solutions of the Ginzburg-Landau
equations (i.e., critical points of
E_{\varepsilon}
) in Euclidean domains,
then it is possible to obtain compactness for a sequence
of the form
\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}
— see
[
16
, Section 4]

and
[
7
, Lemma 2.2]
,

[
13
, Proposition 2.13]
.

In the rest of this section,

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\delta>0
be smaller than the injectivity radius of
M
.
Let
U\subset M
be a smooth, contractible domain, entirely contained in
a geodesic ball of radius
\delta
. Then,

\pi\left|J_{*}\right|\!(U)\leq\left(1+\mathrm{O}(\delta^{2})\right)\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};\,U)}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}

Proof.

We identify
U
with a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by local coordinates,
and write
\D_{0}=\d-i\gamma_{0}
, where
\d
is the flat connection on
U

and
\gamma_{0}
is a smooth
1
-form that depends on
\D_{0}
only.
We consider again the functional
\bar{E}_{\varepsilon}

given by (
2.14
). Thanks to (
A.1
)
(which we can apply as
U
is supposed to be contained in a geodesic ball),
we can write

\begin{split}\bar{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};\,U)&=\int_{U}\frac{1}{2}\left|\d u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\left(1-\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)^{2}\,\d x\\
&=\int_{U}\frac{1}{2}\left|\D_{0}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+\left\langle\D_{0}u_{\varepsilon},\,i\gamma_{0}u_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle+\left|\gamma_{0}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\left(1-\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)^{2}\,\d x\\
&\leq(1+\mathrm{O}(\delta^{2}))E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};U)+(1+\mathrm{O}(\delta^{-2}))\int_{U}\left|\gamma_{0}u\right|^{2}\vol_{g}\,,\end{split}

The last line follows by Young’s inequality (which gives, for each choice of
\sigma>0
, the pointwise bound
\left\langle\D_{0}u_{\varepsilon},\,i\gamma_{0}u_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\leq\frac{\sigma}{2}\left|\D_{0}u\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left|\gamma_{0}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}
, and then the previous inequality follows by choosing
\sigma=\delta^{2}
).
Consequently, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{\bar{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};\,U)}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}\leq&(1+\mathrm{O}(\delta^{2}))\frac{E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};\,U)}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}+\frac{1+C\delta^{-2}}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}\int_{U}\left|\gamma_{0}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\vol_{g}.\end{split}

Since this holds for every
\varepsilon>0
, we can pass both sides to the
\liminf
as
\varepsilon\to 0
keeping the inequality. On doing so, the last term on the right above vanishes, because the energy estimate (
2.11
) implies that

is bounded in

. Therefore, we get the desired estimate recalling (

) of Theorem
2.5
.
∎

We deduce Proposition

Theorem 2.16

(Federer,
[
30
]
)
.

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold. Let
\mathcal{F}

be a collection of
closed
geodesic balls in

, such that

\inf\left\{r>0\colon\bar{B}_{r}(x_{0})\in\mathcal{F}\right\}=0\qquad\textrm{for any }x_{0}\in M.

(2.49)

Let
\mu
be a non-negative Borel measure on
M
and
V\subset M

an open set. Then, there exists a countable
subfamily
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{F}

of pairwise-disjoint balls such that

\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}B\subset V,\qquad\mu\left(V\setminus\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}B\right)=0

The proof of Theorem
2.16
can be found
in
[
30
, Theorem 2.8.14, Corollary 2.8.15]
. The statements
in
[
30
]
apply not only to compact Riemannian manifolds,
but also to a more general class of metric spaces,
i.e. ‘directionally limited’ metric spaces.
(Moreover, they apply to outer measures as well as measures.)
However, the statement given here is sufficient
for our purposes.

The next lemma is an immediate consequence of
Theorem
2.16
.

Lemma 2.17
.

For any
\delta>0
and any open set
V\subset M
, there exists a countable
family
\{U_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of pairwise-disjoint,
open
geodesic balls,
of radius less than
\delta
, such that
U_{j}\subset V
for any

and

\left|J_{*}\right|\!\left(V\setminus\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}U_{j}\right)=0

Proof.

We apply Theorem
2.16
to the bounded measure
\mu:=\left|J_{*}\right|

and the collection of (closed) balls

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}:=\left\{\bar{B}_{r}(x_{0})\colon 0<r<\delta,\ x_{0}\in M\ \textrm{ such that }\ \left|J_{*}\right|(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))=0\right\}\end{split}

Since
\left|J_{*}\right|
is a finite measure, we have
\left|J_{*}\right|(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))=0

for a.e.

. Then, the assumptions

are satisfied, and the lemma follows.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.14
.

Let

By Lemma

Remark 2.19

If

We consider smooth triangulations on

Remark 2.21

Let
\mathcal{T}

Lemma 2.22

If

. By definition
(see
[

Lemma 3.1

For all

Since

, and let

Proposition 3.2

For every choice of a reference smooth connection

To shorten notations, let us omit the dependence on

A

\mathcal{F}
in place of
\mathcal{F}(\,\cdot\,;u,A,\D_{0})

Step 1:
\mathcal{F}
is sequentially coercive.
As

is sequentially weakly closed in the reflexive space

\mathcal{F}(B)\geq C_{1}\left\|B\right\|_{W^{1,2}(M)}^{2}-C_{2}

B=A+\d^{*}\psi

&=\int_{M}\left\{\left|\D_{A}u\right|^{2}-2\left\langle\D_{A}u,\,i(\d^{*}\psi)u\right\rangle+\left|(\d^{*}\psi)u\right|^{2}\right.\\
&\left.\quad+\left|F_{A}\right|^{2}+2\left\langle F_{A},\,\d\d^{*}\psi\right\rangle+\left|\d\d^{*}\psi\right|^{2}\right\}\,\vol_{g}\\
&\geq C_{1}\left\|\Delta\psi\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}-\mathcal{F}(A)\\
&\geq C_{1}\left(\left\|A\right\|_{W^{1,2}(M)}^{2}+\left\|\d^{*}\psi\right\|_{W^{1,2}(M)}^{2}\right)-C_{2}(u,A,\D_{0})\\

, for every choice of

by (

). Secondly, we replace each
A_{\varepsilon}
with a corresponding minimiser
B_{\varepsilon}
of
\mathcal{F}(\,\cdot\,;v_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon},\D_{0})
over
[A_{\varepsilon}]
, where
[A_{\varepsilon}]
is defined as in (
3.2
). We call
\{(v_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon})\}
an
optimised sequence
associated with
\{(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})\}
.

Lemma 3.5

(Reduction lemma)
.

For any sequence
\{(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})\}\subset W^{1,2}(M,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, let
\{(v_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon})\}\subset(L^{\infty}\cap W^{1,2})(M,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
be an associated optimised sequence (defined in Definition
3.1
). Then,
\{(v_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon})\}
satisfies

G_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon})\leq G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})

(3.10)

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Consequently,

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{G_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon})}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}.

(3.11)

Moreover, under the assumption (
3.7
) there holds

J(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})-J(v_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon})\to 0\quad\mbox{in }W^{-1,p}(M)\mbox{ for any }p\mbox{ such that }1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}

(3.12)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

Inequality (
3.10
) follows by construction. Indeed, by (
3.4
) and (
2.3
) we have

G_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon})\leq G_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})\leq G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})

for all
\varepsilon>0
. Then, obviously, the inequality remains true dividing both sides by
\left|\log\varepsilon\right|
and passing both sides to the
\liminf
, i.e., inequality (
3.11
) holds.

In view of Lemma
2.1
and, more specifically, of (
2.4
), to prove (
3.12
) it is enough to prove that

J(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})-J(u_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon})\to 0\quad\mbox{in }W^{-1,p}(M)\mbox{ for any }p\mbox{ such that }1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. The case
p=1
is proved in Lemma
3.4
. We will rely on that case and interpolation to extend the same conclusion to all
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
. To this purpose, we notice that (
2.9
), (
3.7
) and Hölder’s inequality imply

\left\|J(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{1}(M)}\lesssim\left|\log\varepsilon\right|.

(3.13)

On the other hand, from (
2.9
), (
3.7
), and (
3.9
) it follows

\left\|J(u_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{1}(M)}\lesssim\left|\log\varepsilon\right|.

(3.14)

Thus, by Lemma
3.4
, (
3.13
), (
3.14
), and Lemma
C.1
we get

\left\|J(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})-J(u_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(M)}\to 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

In view of (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), it is always possible to pass to an optimised sequence in the proof of Statement (i) of Theorem
A
(i.e., to replace, in the proof, the given sequence
\{(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})\}
by any associated optimised sequence). As we will see later, a decisive advantage of doing so is that the curvature of the connections of an optimised sequence satisfy the London equation (
8
).

It is convenient to introduce the following notation: if
\Phi\in W^{2,2}(M,\,\mathbb{S}^{1})
is a gauge transformation and
A\in W^{1,2}(M,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, we set (cf. Remark
2.9
)

\Phi\cdot A:=A+\Phi^{*}(\vol_{\mathbb{S}^{1}})=A-i\Phi^{-1}\d\Phi.

(3.15)

The next technical lemma produces a family of gauge transformations playing a crucial rôle in the whole rest of this section.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\{(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})\}\subset(L^{\infty}\cap W^{1,2})(M,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
be an optimised sequence satisfying (
3.7
). Then, for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists gauge transformations
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\in W^{2,2}(M,\,\mathbb{S}^{1})
so that there holds

\left\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(M)}\lesssim\left|\log\varepsilon\right|^{1/2}

(3.16)

for all
\varepsilon>0
, whence

E_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})\lesssim\left|\log\varepsilon\right|

(3.17)

for all
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.10
, for all
\varepsilon>0
we find a gauge transformation such that we can write
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}=\d^{*}\psi_{\varepsilon}+\zeta_{\varepsilon}
, where
\zeta_{\varepsilon}\in\Harm^{1}(M)
. Indeed, denote
A_{\varepsilon}=\d\varphi_{\varepsilon}+\d^{*}\psi_{\varepsilon}+\xi_{\varepsilon}
the Hodge decomposition of
A_{\varepsilon}
, where
\varphi\in W^{2,2}(M)
is co-exact,
\psi\in W^{2,2}(M,\Lambda^{2}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
is exact, and
\xi\in\Harm^{1}(M)
. Then, Lemma
2.10
provides us, for each
\varepsilon>0
, with a gauge transformation
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\in W^{2,2}(M,\,\mathbb{S}^{1})
and a harmonic 1-form
\bar{\xi}_{\varepsilon}
so that
\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{*}(\vol_{\mathbb{S}^{1}})=-\d\varphi_{\varepsilon}-\bar{\xi}_{\varepsilon}
. In addition, by Remark
2.11
,
\|\zeta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C_{M}
, where
\zeta_{\varepsilon}:=\xi_{\varepsilon}-\bar{\xi}_{\varepsilon}
and
C_{M}
is a constant depending only on
M
(whence of course
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\left\|\zeta_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C_{M}
). Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary, and recall that we denote
F_{0}
the curvature 2-form of the reference connection
\D_{0}
. As
\psi_{\varepsilon}
is exact and of class
W^{2,2}
, there holds

-\Delta\psi_{\varepsilon}=\d\d^{*}\psi_{\varepsilon}=\d(\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})=\d A_{\varepsilon}=F_{A_{\varepsilon}}-F_{0},

(3.18)

by (
A.26
). In particular,
F_{A_{\varepsilon}}-F_{0}
is an exact
2
-form (hence, orthogonal to every harmonic
2
-form) and therefore we deduce from Lemma
A.15
that for all
\varepsilon>0

\left\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{W^{2,2}(M)}\lesssim\left\|F_{\varepsilon}-F_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\lesssim\left|\log\varepsilon\right|^{1/2},

where the last inequality follows from (
3.7
). Hence, recalling (
A.4
),

\left\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(M)}\lesssim\left|\log\varepsilon\right|^{1/2}

for all
\varepsilon>0
; i.e., (
3.16
) is proved. From (
3.16
), (
3.7
), and the gauge-invariance of
G_{\varepsilon}
,

\begin{split}E_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})&=\int_{M}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|\D_{0}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\left(1-\left|\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)^{2}\right)\,\vol_{g}\\
&\leq\int_{M}\left(\left|\D_{\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})\right|^{2}+\left|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\left(1-\left|\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)^{2}\right)\,\vol_{g}\\
&\leq\int_{M}\left(\left|\D_{\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})\right|^{2}+\left|F_{\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\left(1-\left|\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)^{2}\right)\,\vol_{g}+\int_{M}\left|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}\\
&\leq 2G_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})+\left\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}\\
&=2G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})+\left\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}\lesssim\left|\log\varepsilon\right|\end{split}

for all
\varepsilon>0
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

In general, we cannot assert that the original sequence
\{A_{\varepsilon}\}
satisfies (
3.16
), as we have no control on the co-exact part of the Hodge decomposition of the maps
A_{\varepsilon}
(i.e., on
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
).

We immediately make use of Lemma
3.7
to prove the following corollary.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
\{(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})\}\subset(L^{\infty}\cap W^{1,2})(M,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
be an optimised sequence satisfying (
3.7
). Then, up to extraction of a (not relabelled) subsequence,
J(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})\to\pi J_{*}
in
W^{-1,p}(M)
for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, where
J_{*}
is a bounded measure with values in
2
-forms. In addition,
\star J_{*}
is an integer-multiplicity rectifiable
(n-2)
-cycle belonging to
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Let
\{\Phi_{\varepsilon}\}
be the family of gauge transformations in Lemma
3.7
. In view of (
3.17
), we can apply Theorem
C
to the sequence
\{\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\}
. By Statement (i) of Theorem
C
, we obtain a (not relabelled) subsequence
\{\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\}
and a bounded measure
J_{*}
with values in
2
-forms such that
J(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})\to\pi J_{*}
in
W^{-1,p}(M)
for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Since we already know from Statement (i) of Theorem
C
that
\star J_{*}
is a current with the desired properties, it only remains to prove that
J(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})\to\pi J_{*}
in
W^{-1,p}(M)
for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

To prove this, we observe that, by the gauge-invariance of the Jacobians, we have
J(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})=J(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})
for all
\varepsilon>0
. Thus,

\begin{split}J(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})-J_{*}&=J(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})-J_{*}\\
&=J(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})-J(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})+J(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})-J_{*}.\end{split}

for all
\varepsilon>0
. By definition of
J_{*}
, we need only to show that

J(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})-J(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})\to 0\quad\mbox{in }W^{-1,p}(M)\mbox{ for any }1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}\mbox{ as }\varepsilon\to 0.

(3.19)

To this purpose, we recall that from (
1.4
) we have

J(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})-J(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})=-\frac{1}{2}\d\left((\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})(1-\left|\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2})\right)

for all
\varepsilon>0
. By (
3.16
) and the energy estimate (
3.17
) (or, equivalently, (
3.7
), since
\left|\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\right|=\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|
a.e.), for each
\varepsilon>0
it holds

\left\|(\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})(1-\left|\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2})\right\|_{L^{1}(M)}\leq\left\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\left\|1-\left|\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\lesssim\varepsilon\left|\log\varepsilon\right|,

whence

\left\|J(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})-J(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{W^{-1,1}(M)}\lesssim\varepsilon\left|\log\varepsilon\right|,

for all
\varepsilon>0
. Then (
3.19
) follows by using Lemma
C.1
exactly as in the last part of Lemma
2.1
. The conclusion is now immediate by triangle inequality.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

In view of (
3.12
), Corollary
3.9
proves a half of Statement (i) of Theorem
A
.

The following proposition is the last piece of information we need to combine the above results with Theorem
C
to deduce the lower bound (
11
), concluding the proof of Theorem
A
. Here we will use in a crucial way the fact that the curvatures
F_{A_{\varepsilon}}
satisfy the London equation (
8
), as alluded in Remark

.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\{(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})\}\subset(L^{\infty}\cap W^{1,2})(M,\,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
be an optimised sequence satisfying (
3.7
). Then, up to extraction of a (not relabelled) subsequence:

1.

\{F_{A_{\varepsilon}}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
W^{1,p}(M)
for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
.

2.

If
\{\Phi_{\varepsilon}\}
is the family of gauge transformations in Lemma
3.7
, the sequence
\{\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\}
is a bounded sequence in
W^{2,p}(M,\,T^{*}M)
for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
.

3.

Up to a further (not relabelled) subsequence,
\{\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\}
converges strongly in
W^{2,p}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
, to some
A_{*}
, where
A_{*}
writes

A_{*}=\d^{*}\psi_{*}+\zeta_{*}

(3.20)

for
\psi_{*}\in W^{3,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{2}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
an exact 2-form and
\zeta_{*}
a harmonic 1-form.

Proof.

Let us set
F_{\varepsilon}:=F_{A_{\varepsilon}}
for convenience. Since each of the curvatures
F_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the London equation (
8
), we have

-\Delta(F_{\varepsilon_{n}}-F_{\varepsilon_{m}})+(F_{\varepsilon_{n}}-F_{\varepsilon_{m}})=2J(u_{\varepsilon_{n}},A_{\varepsilon_{n}})-2J(u_{\varepsilon_{m}},A_{\varepsilon_{m}})

for all indexes
n
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By Lemma
C.3
, we deduce the crucial estimate

\left\|F_{\varepsilon_{n}}-F_{\varepsilon_{m}}\right\|_{W^{1,p}(M)}\lesssim\left\|J(u_{\varepsilon_{n}},A_{\varepsilon_{n}})-J(u_{\varepsilon_{m}},A_{\varepsilon_{m}})\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(M)},

where the right hand side tends to zero as
n,m\to\infty
as a trivial consequence of Corollary
3.9
, and this holds for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
. Thus,
\{F_{A_{\varepsilon}}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
W^{1,p}(M)
for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
. This proves (i).

To prove (ii), arguing as in Lemma
3.7
, by the choice of
\{\Phi_{\varepsilon}\}
, for each gauge-transformed connection
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}
we have the decomposition
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}=\d^{*}\psi_{\varepsilon}+\zeta_{\varepsilon}
for an appropriate exact
2
-form
\psi_{\varepsilon}
satisfying (
3.18
) and a suitable harmonic
2
-form
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
. Furthermore, it holds
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\left\|\zeta_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}<+\infty
(cf. Remark
2.11
). By (
3.18
), Part (i) and elliptic regularity (i.e., Lemma
A.15
) it follows that
\{\psi_{\varepsilon}\}
is a bounded sequence in
W^{3,p}(M)
for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
. Hence, again by (
3.18
) and
L^{p}
-Hodge decomposition (Proposition
A.13
), the sequence
\{\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\}
is bounded in
W^{2,p}(M)
for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
.

(iii) Writing again
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}=\d^{*}\psi_{\varepsilon}+\zeta_{\varepsilon}
exactly as in (ii), from (
3.18
) we have

-\Delta(\psi_{\varepsilon_{n}}-\psi_{\varepsilon_{m}})=F_{\varepsilon_{n}}-F_{\varepsilon_{m}}

for any
n
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, by Part (i) it follows that
\{\Delta\psi_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
W^{1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{2}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
and, since each
\psi_{\varepsilon_{n}}
is exact, by Lemma
A.15
and Remark
A.16
it follows that
\{\psi_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}
is Cauchy sequence in
W^{3,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{2}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
. Hence, by the completeness of
W^{3,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{2}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
,
\{\psi_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}
converges strongly in
W^{3,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{2}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
, to some
\psi_{*}
. By the
L^{p}
-Hodge-decomposition (Proposition
A.13
) and the strong
W^{3,p}
-convergence,
\psi_{*}
is still an exact 2-form.

Next, since the space
\Harm^{1}(M)
is finite-dimensional and
\{\zeta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}
is bounded in the
L^{\infty}(M)
-norm,
\{\zeta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}
is also bounded with respect to the
W^{2,p}(M)
-norm. In addition, we can also extract from
\{\zeta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}
a (not relabelled) Cauchy sequence, which is
a fortiori
a Cauchy sequence in
W^{2,p}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
, and hence it converges in
W^{2,p}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
to some
\zeta_{*}
. Again, by Hodge-decomposition and strong convergence,
\zeta_{*}
is still a harmonic 1-form.

Thus, up to a not relabelled subsequence, and letting
A_{*}:=\d^{*}\psi_{*}+\zeta_{*}
, we have
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\to A_{*}
in
W^{2,p}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
.
∎

Remark 3.12

Lemma

, and Proposition

We now have at disposal everything we need to prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
A
.

(i) As emphasized in Remark
3.6
, we can always pass to an optimised sequence associated with
\{(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})\}
. Hence, we may assume, for notational convenience, that
\{(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})\}
is already optimised. Let
\{\Phi_{\varepsilon}\}\subset W^{2,2}(M,\,\mathbb{S}^{1})
be, once again, the family of gauge transformations of Lemma
3.7
. By (
3.17
), we can apply Statement (i) of Theorem
C
to the sequence
\{\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\}
. Let
J_{*}
be the bounded measure with values in
2
-forms associated with
\{\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\}
by Statement (i) of Theorem
C
. The claimed convergence of the gauge-invariant Jacobians
J(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})
to
J_{*}
follows from Corollary
3.9
(and, back to the original sequence, Remark
3.10
). Therefore, we still have to prove only the lower bound (
11
).

To infer (
11
), we note that, by the gauge-invariance of
G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})
, it suffices to prove that

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{E_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{G_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|},

(3.21)

Once we proved (
3.21
), the conclusion follows immediately from Statement (i) of Theorem
C
.

Towards the proof of (
3.21
), we notice that, by interpolation, Proposition
3.11
, and the (continuous) Sobolev embedding
W^{1,2}(M,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}(M,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

[
56
, Theorem 1.3.3]
, for each
\varepsilon>0
we have

\begin{split}\left\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}&\leq\left\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{1}(M)}^{\frac{2}{n+2}}\left\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2^{*}}(M)}^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\\
&\lesssim\left\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\right\|^{\frac{2}{n+2}}_{W^{2,1}(M)}\left\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(M)}^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\lesssim\left|\log{\varepsilon}\right|^{\frac{n}{2(n+2)}},\end{split}

whence
\left\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}=\mathrm{o}\left(\left|\log\varepsilon\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Since

\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\left|\D_{\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}\\
=\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\left|\D_{0}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})\right|^{2}+\left\langle\D_{0}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}),\,i(\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left|(\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g},

and

\left|\int_{M}\left\langle\D_{0}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}),\,i(\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left|(\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})\right|^{2}\,\vol_{g}\right|\\
\lesssim\left\|\D_{0}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\left\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}+\left\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2},

we deduce

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{E_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{G_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot A_{\varepsilon})}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}.

(3.22)

As remarked, the claimed conclusion now follows from (i) of Theorem
C
(and, back to the original, not necessarily optimised, sequence
\{(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})\}
, inequality (
3.11
)).

(ii) Given any
S_{*}\in\mathcal{C}
, let
J_{*}:=\star S_{*}
be the corresponding measure with values in
2
-forms
J_{*}
and denote
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}\subset W^{1,2}(M,E)
the recovery sequence given by (ii) of Theorem
C
. Then the sequence
\{(u_{\varepsilon},0)\}\subset W^{1,2}(M,E)\times W^{1,2}(M,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
satisfies
J(u_{\varepsilon},0)\to\pi J_{*}
in
W^{-1,p}(M)
for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
(simply because
J(u_{\varepsilon},0)=J(u_{\varepsilon})
by definition). In addition, there holds

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},0)}{\left|\log{\varepsilon}\right|}\leq\pi\left|J_{*}\right|(M),

because the inequality holds for
E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
by (ii) of Theorem
C
and because for all
\varepsilon>0
we have
G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},0)=E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\left\|F_{0}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}
, where as usual
F_{0}
is the curvature of the reference connection
\D_{0}
. The proof is finished.

Remark 3.13
.

There is a local analogue of the

-lower inequality
for
G_{\varepsilon}
. More precisely, let

) and let

in

for any

. Then, there holds

\pi\left|J_{*}\right|\!(V)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\,A_{\varepsilon};\,V)}{\left|\log\varepsilon\right|}

(3.23)

The proof of (

) is completely analogous
to the proof of (

) above and is based on
the local
\Gamma
-lower inequality for

(Proposition

).

Corollary
B
is now an almost immediate
consequence of Theorem

.

Proof of Corollary
B
.

Let

be a minimiser of

in

, for any

.
As the class

for some

.
By applying Theorem

. Moreover,

Appendix A
Bundles on a closed Riemannian manifold

A.1
Sobolev spaces of sections and differential forms

We recall below the main definitions and facts concerning several spaces of differential forms and, more broadly, of section of Hermitian vector bundles. We shall do so in a slightly greater generality than strictly needed in this work to make the presentation more transparent and the comparison with the relevant literature easier. The main reference for this appendix is
[
51
]
, especially Chapters 4, 5, 9, and 19, where the abstract framework is developed in a much more general context (and using the language of category theory, that we avoid for reader’s convenience). A more recent useful reference is
[
34
, Chapter 1]
. Both in
[
51
]
and in
[
34
]
equivalence with other approaches to Sobolev spaces of sections is discussed. (See also
[
48
, Chapter 10]
,
[
56
, Chapter 1]
,
[
65
, Appendix B]
.)

Let
\mathbb{K}
be either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, and let
\pi:E\to M
be a
\mathbb{K}
-vector bundle of rank
\ell
over a
C^{\infty}
-smooth compact orientable Riemannian manifold
M=(M^{n},g)
without boundary. We assume the differentiable structure of
M
is fixed once and for all.

Regular bundle atlases.

Using the compactness of
M
, it is easily shown that it is always possible to find finite bundle atlases
\mathcal{A}=\{(U_{i},\varphi_{i},\chi_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
, where
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the maps
\varphi_{i}\colon U_{i}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
are local charts and
\chi_{i}\colon\pi^{-1}(U_{i})=:E|_{U_{i}}\to U_{i}\times\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
local trivialisations, so that:

(A1)

For all
i\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
,
U_{i}
is contractible and, moreover,
\varphi_{i}(U_{i})=:\Omega_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded contractible open set with smooth boundary.

(A2)

For all
i\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
,
(U_{i},\varphi_{i})
can be extended to a smooth chart
(V_{i},\psi_{i})
contained in the differentiable structure of
M
, so that
\overline{U_{i}}\subset V_{i}
and
\varphi_{i}=\psi_{i}|_{U_{i}}
. This ensures that, for all
i,j\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
, whenever
U_{i}\cap U_{j}\neq\varnothing
, each coordinate change
\varphi_{i}\circ\varphi_{j}^{-1}:\varphi_{j}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})\to\varphi_{i}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})
is smooth up to the boundary of
\varphi_{j}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})
(hence, all its derivatives are bounded).

(A3)

For all
i\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
,
E\to M
trivialises over
U_{i}
, i.e, we have
E|_{U_{i}}\overset{\chi_{i}}{\simeq}U_{i}\times\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
(actually, this comes for free, as the
U_{i}
are contractible). In addition, each local trivialisation
\chi_{i}:\pi^{-1}(U_{i})\to U_{i}\times\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
extends to a smooth map over the corresponding coordinates patches
V_{i}
associated with
U_{i}
as in (A2) (up to slightly shrinking
V_{i}
, if necessary).
Consequently, for all
i,j\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
such that
U_{i}\cap U_{j}\neq\varnothing
, we have
\chi_{i}\circ\chi_{j}^{-1}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{(U_{i}\cap U_{j})}\times\mathbb{K}^{\ell},(U_{i}\cap U_{j})\times\mathbb{K}^{\ell})
. Recall that

\widehat{\chi_{i}}:=(\varphi_{i},{\rm id}_{\mathbb{K}^{\ell}})\circ\chi_{i}:E|_{U_{i}}\to\Omega_{i}\times\mathbb{K}^{\ell}\qquad(i\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}),

are the charts of
E
(as an
(n+\ell)
-manifold if
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
, and as an
(n+2\ell)
real manifold if
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
) and notice that, under our assumptions, all the derivatives of all coordinate changes
\widehat{\chi_{i}}\circ\widehat{\chi_{j}}^{-1}:\Omega_{j}\times\mathbb{K}^{\ell}\to\Omega_{i}\times\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
are bounded. Consequently, denoting
{\rm pr}_{2}
the projection onto the second factor of a product of the type
\Omega\times\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
, with
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the maps
{\rm pr}_{2}\circ\widehat{\chi_{i}}\circ\widehat{\chi_{j}}^{-1}:\Omega_{j}\times\mathbb{K}^{\ell}\to\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
are smooth and all their derivatives are bounded.

For convenience, we refer to atlases of
M
satisfying (A1) and (A2) as
regular atlases
and to bundle atlases satisfying (A1)-(A3) as
regular bundle atlases
. We call
regular
the charts of regular atlases and of regular bundle atlases.

Remark A.1
.

Since we always cover
M
by contractible open sets
U_{i}
,
every
vector bundle over
M
, and not only the given bundle
\pi:E\to M
, trivialises over them.

Remark A.2

(Normal coordinates)
.

As it is well-know (see, e.g.,
[
59
, pp. 166-167]
), around any point
x_{0}\in M
, one can choose
normal coordinates
so that in the geodesic ball
B_{\delta}(x_{0})
centered at
x_{0}
, it holds

g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}g_{ij}}{\partial x^{k}\partial x_{l}}(x_{0})x^{k}x^{l}+\mathrm{o}(\left|x\right|^{2})\,,

whence
\sqrt{\det g}=1+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,k,l=1}^{n}\partial^{2}_{kl}g_{ii}(x_{0})x^{k}x^{l}+\mathrm{o}(\left|x\right|^{2})
, and consequently

\vol_{g}=(1+\mathrm{O}(\delta^{2}))\d x\quad\mbox{in }B_{\delta}(x_{0})\,.

(A.1)

Given any atlas of
M
, by compactness it can be refined so to have a regular atlas in which the local coordinates are normal coordinates.

Sobolev spaces of sections of vector bundles

A
measurable section
of a
\mathbb{K}
-vector bundle
E\to M
of rank
\ell
is a Borel measurable function
u\colon M\to E
so that the equation
\pi\circ u(x)=x
holds for almost every
x\in M
with respect to the measure induced by
\vol_{g}
. Let us denote
\Gamma(M,\,E)
the linear space of all such sections.

Fix a regular bundle atlas
\mathcal{A}
for
E\to M
and a partition of unity
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
subordinate to
\mathcal{A}
. For
m\geq 0
an integer and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we say (according to
[
51
, Chapter 4]
) that a measurable section
u\in\Gamma(M,\,E)
belongs to
W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,\,E)
if and only if every local representation

u_{i}:={\rm pr}_{2}\circ\widehat{\chi_{i}}\circ(\rho_{i}u)\circ\varphi^{-1}_{i}:\Omega_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{K}^{\ell}\qquad(i\in{1,2,\dots,N})

of
u
belongs to the usual Sobolev space
W^{m,p}(\Omega_{i},\,\mathbb{K}^{\ell})
. We endow
W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,E)
with the norm

\left\|u\right\|_{W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,E)}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{0\leq\left|\alpha\right|\leq m}\left\|D^{\alpha}u_{i}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{i},\mathbb{K}^{\ell})},

(A.2)

where the inner sum runs over all multi-indexes
\alpha
of length at most
m
. Of course, we let
L^{p}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,E):=W^{0,p}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,E)
.

Armed with the above definition and (A1)–(A3), it is not difficult to prove that (cf. e.g.,
[
51
,
34
,
56
,
65
]
)

(S1)

W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,E)
is Banach space (Hilbert if
p=2
), separable if
p\in[1,\infty)
, and reflexive if
p\in(1,\infty)
.

(S2)

C^{\infty}(M,E)
(i.e., the space of classical smooth sections of
E\to M
) is dense into
W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,E)
for the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,E)}
for every integer
m\geq 0
and every
p\in[1,\infty)
.

(S3)

The classical embedding (including compact embeddings) theorems hold.

(S4)

Any two partitions of unity subordinate to
\mathcal{A}
induce equivalent norms, hence different choices for the partition of unity yield equivalent Banach spaces
W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,E)
, for which properties (S1)–(S3) hold.

(S5)

For any regular bundle atlases
\mathcal{A}_{1}
,
\mathcal{A}_{2}
of
E\to M
, the sets
W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}(M,E)
and
W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}(M,E)
coincide. Moreover, the corresponding norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}}
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}}
, defined by (
A.2
), induce equivalent norms on
\Gamma(M,E)
. Then,
W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}(M,E)
and
W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}(M,E)
are actually equivalent Banach spaces, for which properties (S1)–(S4) hold.

Remark A.3
.

In view of (S1)–(S5), we can identify a linear subspace
W^{m,p}(M,E)
of
\Gamma(M,E)
, the members of which have finite
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}}}
-norm, independently of the chosen regular bundle atlas
\mathcal{A}
(and of subordinate partitions of unity). All the norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}}}
are equivalent on
W^{m,p}(M,E)
, and hence induce the same topology on
W^{m,p}(M,E)
, that is therefore independent of the regular bundle atlas
\mathcal{A}
chosen for
E\to M
(and of subordinate partitions of unity). In particular, properties (S1)–(S3) hold for
W^{m,p}(M,E)
, and in view of (S4)-(S5) we can choose for computations any regular bundle atlas and subordinate partitions of unity.

Definition A.1
.

Let
M
be a smooth, compact, connected oriented, Riemannian manifold without boundary, of dimension
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
E\to M
be a
\mathbb{K}
-vector bundle over
M
of rank
\ell
. For
m\geq 0
an integer and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we denote by
W^{m,p}(M,E)
the linear space of sections of
E\to M
having finite
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,E)}
-norm for some, and hence all (see Remark
A.3
), choices of a regular bundle atlas
\mathcal{A}
. We provide the space
W^{m,p}(M,E)
with the topology induced by any of the (equivalent) norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}(M,E)}}
. This topology does not depend on the chosen regular bundle atlas
\mathcal{A}
(see again Remark
A.3
).

We denote by
W^{-m,q}(M,\,E^{\prime})
the topological dual of
W^{m,p}(M,\,E)
, i.e.,

W^{-m,q}(M,\,E^{\prime}):=\left(W^{m,p}(M,\,E)\right)^{\prime}.

Here,
q:=p^{\prime}
is the Hölder-conjugate exponent of
p
.

Remark A.4
.

To simplify notations, we will henceforth drop the subscript
\mathcal{A}
when we denote the norm of a section
u\in W^{m,p}(M,E)
. More precisely, we will write
\left\|u\right\|_{W^{m,p}(M,E)}
to mean actually that we have fixed a regular bundle atlas
\mathcal{A}
(and a subordinate partition of unity) and we are evaluating
\left\|u\right\|_{W^{m,p}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,E)}
accordingly to (
A.2
).

Remark A.5
.

If
E\to M
is a trivial
\mathbb{K}
-bundle of rank
\ell
, i.e., if
E=M\times\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
, we can identify
W^{m,p}(M,E)
and
W^{m,p}(M,\mathbb{K}^{\ell})
. Indeed, if
u:M\to M\times\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
is a measurable section, then
\tilde{u}:={\rm pr_{2}}\circ\widehat{\chi}\circ u:M\to\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
is a measurable function. Vice versa, if
\tilde{u}:M\to\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
is a measurable function, the map
x\mapsto(x,\tilde{u}(x))
gives rise to a section
u\in\Gamma(M,E)
. Moreover, the local representations of
u
and
\tilde{u}
coincide a.e. with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Hence,
u\in W^{m,p}(M,M\times\mathbb{K}^{\ell})
if and only if
\tilde{u}\in W^{m,p}(M,\mathbb{K}^{\ell})
, and the norms are the same. Therefore, we can identify these two spaces, and we shall do so when convenient even without explicit mention.

Sobolev spaces of differential forms

Let
0\leq k\leq n
be an integer and
E=\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M
, the bundle of
k
-covectors over
M
. For
m\geq 0
an integer and
p\in[1,\infty]
, let the space
W^{m,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
be defined according to Definition
A.1
. Then, the spaces
W^{m,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
agree with the Sobolev spaces of differential
k
-forms over
M
considered in
[
46
,
57
,
21
,
38
]
. For the purpose of exposition, it is convenient to refer this definition of Sobolev spaces of differential
k
-forms over
M
to as the
classical
definition.

Remark A.6
.

Recall that the bundles
\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\to M
are constructed canonically starting from any atlas of
M
. In particular, any regular atlas
\{(U_{i},\varphi_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
for
M
induces a regular bundle atlas
\mathcal{A}_{k}
for
\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\to M
for every
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
. Thus, for convenience, in this paragraph we use the symbol
\mathcal{A}
to denote the given atlas for
M
, i.e.,
\mathcal{A}=\{(U_{i},\varphi_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
, although in the previous paragraph it has been used to denote bundle atlases.

We set

(\omega,\eta):=\int_{M}\left\langle\omega,\,\eta\right\rangle\,\vol_{g}:=\int_{M}\omega\wedge*\eta,

for any two measurable maps
\omega
,
\eta\colon M\to\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M
such that the right hand side is well-defined. We say that
\omega\in L^{1}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
has
weak exterior differential

\d\omega
if there exists
\Omega\in L^{1}(M,\Lambda^{k+1}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
such that it holds

(\omega,\d^{*}\eta)=(\Omega,\eta)

for all smooth test forms
\eta\in C^{\infty}(M,\Lambda^{k+1}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
. In such case, we set
\d\omega:=\Omega
. Symmetrically, we say that
\omega\in L^{1}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
has
weak exterior codifferential

\d^{*}\omega
if there exists
\Psi\in L^{1}(M,\Lambda^{k-1}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
such that the equation

(\omega,\d\eta)=(\Psi,\eta)

holds for every
\eta\in C^{\infty}(M,\Lambda^{k-1}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, and we set
\d^{*}\omega:=\Psi
. Clearly, when they exists,
\d\omega
and
\d^{*}\omega
are unique, and they coincide with the classical exterior differential and codifferential of
\omega
if
\omega
is smooth.

Thus, for
m\geq 1
, the operators

\displaystyle\d:W^{m,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\to W^{m-1,p}(M,\Lambda^{k+1}\mathrm{T}^{*}M),

\displaystyle\d^{*}:W^{m,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\to W^{m-1,p}(M,\Lambda^{k-1}\mathrm{T}^{*}M),

are well-defined, linear and continuous. For
p\in(1,\infty)
, they are the unique extensions by linearity and density of the classical exterior differential and co-differential. Thus, by density, we have the following “integration by parts” formula: if
p,q\in(1,\infty)
satisfy
1/p+1/q=1
, then

(\d\omega,\eta)\equiv\int_{M}\left\langle\d\omega,\,\eta\right\rangle\,\vol_{g}\equiv\int_{M}\d\omega\wedge*\eta=\int_{M}\omega\wedge*\d^{*}\eta\equiv(\omega,\d^{*}\eta)

(A.3)

for all
\omega\in W^{1,p}(M,\Lambda^{k-1}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
and all
\eta\in W^{1,q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
. On the other hand, if
\omega\in W^{1,\infty}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, then
\omega
is differentiable almost everywhere on
M
and, since
M
is compact, by Hölder inequality
\omega
belongs to
W^{1,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
for every
p\in[1,\infty]
. Furthermore, for every form
\omega
of class at least
W^{2,2}
,

\d\d\omega=0,\qquad\d^{*}\d^{*}\omega=0\quad\mbox{a.e. on }M.

The explicit expressions of
\d
,
\d^{*}
in coordinates will not be needed in this work. Formally, they are the same as in the classical case (see, e.g.,
[
46
, Chapter 7]
). They can be expressed in terms of the metric
g
of
M
on the Levi-Civita connection on
\mathrm{T}M
(which classically induce canonical Riemannian metrics and linear connections over all tensor bundles of
M
), see e.g.
[
56
, Chapter 1]
.

Remark A.7
.

When defined,
\d
,
\d^{*}
satisfy the same properties as in the classical case. For instance, they are local and commute with restrictions.

It has been firstly shown in
[
57
]
that, for
M
compact and without boundary, the classical definition of
W^{1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
is equivalent to the following
geometrical
one
[
57
, Proposition 4.11]
:

\mathcal{W}^{1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)=\left\{\omega\in L^{p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M):\d\omega\in L^{p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k+1}M),\,\d^{*}\omega\in L^{p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k-1}M)\right\}\,.

The continuous embedding of
W^{1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
into
\mathcal{W}^{1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
is obvious, as the pointwise inequality (Eq. (2.6) in
[
57
]
)

\left|\omega\right|^{p}+\left|\d\omega\right|^{p}+\left|\d^{*}\omega\right|^{p}\leq C(U,p)\left|\nabla\omega\right|^{p}

holds a.e. within any open region
U\subset M
compactly contained in a regular coordinate chart. Here, we are using the notation of
[
57
]
, according to which
\left|\nabla\omega\right|^{p}:=\left(\sum\left|\frac{\partial\omega_{I}}{\partial x^{k}}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}
,
\omega=\sum\omega_{I}\d x^{I}
,
I=\{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots i_{k}\leq n\}
, and
\varphi\equiv(x^{1},\dots,x^{n})
are the local (regular) coordinates on
U
.

The proof that
W^{1,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)=\mathcal{W}^{1,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
then amounts to prove the reverse continuous embedding. The latter stems on the following
L^{p}
-version of
Gaffney’s inequality
(
[
57
, Proposition 4.10]
).

Proposition A.8

(Gaffney’s inequality)
.

Let
M
be a compact orientable smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary. Then a regular bundle atlas
\mathcal{A}
of
M
can be found so that the following happens: there exists a positive constant
C_{p}
, depending on
p
,
n
, and
\mathcal{A}
, so that

\|\omega\|_{W^{1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)}\leq C_{p}(\|\omega\|_{L^{p}}+\|\d\omega\|_{L^{p}}+\|\d^{*}\omega\|_{L^{p}})

(A.4)

for all
\omega\in W^{1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
and all integers
0\leq k\leq n
.

Gaffney’s-type inequalities hold, for compact manifolds without boundary, also for higher order derivatives. More precisely, we have the following result.

Proposition A.9
.

Let
M
be a compact oriented smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary, endowed with the regular atlas
\mathcal{A}
in Proposition
A.8
. Then, for every
p\in(1,\infty)
and every integer
m\geq 0
there is a constant
C_{m,p}
, depending on
p
,
n
,
m
and
\mathcal{A}
, so that

\left\|\omega\right\|_{W^{m,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)}\leq C_{m,p}\sum_{s=0}^{m}\left\|(\d+\d^{*})^{s}\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}

(A.5)

for all
\omega\in W^{m,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
and all integers
0\leq k\leq n
.

Sketch of the proof.

Take
\omega\in C^{\infty}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
. For each open set
U_{i}
of the covering provided by
\mathcal{A}
, inequality (
A.5
) is proved for the local representation of
\omega
in
[
21
, Theorem 4.2.1]
(see also
[
21
, Proposition 4.2.2]
). Then the local inequalities can be glued together (using crucially the fact that
\mathcal{A}
is regular) to give (
A.5
) as in
[
57
, Proposition 4.10]
. This concludes the proof if
\omega
is smooth. For general
\omega\in W^{m,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, the result follows by density.
∎

Remark A.10
.

In particular, the classical Sobolev spaces
W^{2,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
coincides, when
M
is as in Proposition
A.9
, with the space of measurable differential
k
-forms
\omega
such that

\omega\in W^{1,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M),\quad\d\omega\in W^{1,p}(M,\Lambda^{k+1}\mathrm{T}^{*}M),\quad\d^{*}\omega\in W^{1,p}(M,\Lambda^{k-1}\mathrm{T}^{*}M).

A
k
-form
\omega\in L^{p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
such that
\d\omega
,
\d^{*}\omega
exist in the weak sense and

\d\omega=0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\d^{*}\omega=0

(A.6)

is called

(or a

). Harmonic

-forms are automatically smooth
[
57
, Proposition 5.2]
, hence Eqs. (
A.6
) actually hold in the classical sense. For each integer
k\geq 0
, we let

\Harm^{k}(M):=\{h\in C^{\infty}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M):\d h=\d^{*}h=0\}.

(A.7)

Letting

-\Delta:=\d\d^{*}+\d^{*}\d

(A.8)

be the Laplace-Beltrami operator, we have the equivalent characterization (e.g.,
[
32
, Proposition 5.2.4.3]
)

\omega\in\Harm^{k}(M)\iff\Delta\omega=0.

Remark A.11
.

Viewed as an operator from
W^{2,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
into
L^{p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
,
\Delta
is a bounded (linear) operator for every
p\in(1,\infty)
and any integer
k\geq 0
, with kernel
\Harm^{k}(M)
.

For every integer
k\geq 0
,
\Harm^{k}(M)
is a finite dimensional linear space (e.g.,
[
38
, Remark 4.9]
). Hence, any two norms are equivalent on
\Harm^{k}(M)
. We denote (cf.
[
57
, (5.4)]
)

(\Harm^{k}(M))^{\perp}:=\left\{\omega\in L^{1}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\colon\forall h\in\Harm^{k}(M),\,\,(\omega,h)=0\right\}.

(A.9)

As established in
[
57
, Lemma 5.6]
, for every
\omega\in L^{1}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
there is a unique
H(\omega)\in\Harm^{k}(M)
such that

(\omega-H(\omega),h)=0\quad\mbox{for all }h\in\Harm^{k}(M).

We call
H(\omega)
the
harmonic part
of
\omega
. The assignment
\omega\mapsto H(\omega)
defines a surjective linear operator (a projection, in fact)
H\colon L^{1}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\to\Harm^{k}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, called
harmonic projection
, which is bounded regardless of the norm on
\Harm^{k}(M)

[
57
, Proposition 5.9]
.

From the classical
L^{2}
-theory of differential forms as developed for instance in
[
64
, Chapter 6]
, there is a linear operator, called
Green’s operator
,

G:C^{\infty}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\to C^{\infty}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\cap(\Harm^{k}(M))^{\perp}

providing the unique solution in
(\Harm^{k}(M))^{\perp}
to the equation

\Delta G(\omega)=\omega-H(\omega)\qquad\textrm{with }G(\omega)\in\Harm^{k}(M)^{\perp}

(A.10)

With Gaffney’s inequality (
A.4
) at hands, C. Scott
[
57
]
extended, for every
p\in(1,\infty)
,
G
to a bounded linear operator, still called
Green’s operator
(and denoted with the same letter),

G\colon L^{p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\to W^{2,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\cap(\Harm^{k}(M))^{\perp}

(A.11)

which still satisfies (
A.10
) (
[
57
, Proposition 6.1]
).

Remark A.12
.

For future reference, we notice that

\Delta G(\omega)=G(\Delta\omega)=\omega-H(\omega)

(A.12)

for every
\omega\in W^{2,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
,
p\in(1,\infty)
, and
0\leq k\leq n
integer. For smooth forms, this is well-known (e.g.,
[
64
, Proposition 6.10]
). For forms of class
W^{2,p}
, see
[
21
, Proposition 6.24]
or argue directly by density of
C^{\infty}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
in
L^{p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
and the continuity of
G\colon L^{p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\to W^{2,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\cap(\Harm^{k})^{\perp}
and
\Delta:W^{2,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\to L^{p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
.
Equation (
A.12
) and the continuity of
G
imply

\left\|\omega\right\|_{W^{2,p}(M)}\leq C\left(\left\|\Delta\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}+\left\|H(\omega)\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}\right)

(A.13)

for any
\omega\in W^{2,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

and some constant
C
that depends only on
M
,
k
and
p
.

Finally, the following fundamental result is proved in
[
57
]
.

Proposition A.13

(
L^{p}
-Hodge decomposition,
[
57
, Proposition 6.5]
)
.

Let
M
be a smooth compact oriented Riemannian manifold without boundary and
1<p<\infty
. For any integer
0\leq k\leq n
, we have

\begin{split}L^{p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)&=\Delta G(L^{p})\oplus\Harm^{k}(M)\\
&=\d\d^{*}G(L^{p})\oplus\d^{*}\d G(L^{p})\oplus\Harm^{k}(M).\end{split}

(A.14)

Moreover,
\d(W^{1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k-1}\mathrm{T}^{*}M))=\d\d^{*}G(L^{p})
and
\d^{*}(W^{1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k+1}\mathrm{T}^{*}M))=\d^{*}\d G(L^{p})
.

Consequently, any
\omega\in L^{p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
can be uniquely written as

\omega=\d\varphi+\d^{*}\psi+\xi,

(A.15)

where
\varphi\in W^{1,p}(M,\Lambda^{k-1}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
is co-exact,
\psi\in W^{1,p}(M,\Lambda^{k+1}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
is exact and
\xi\in\Harm^{k}(M)
is a harmonic
k
-form. In addition, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
p
,
k
and
M
, such that there holds

\left\|\varphi\right\|_{W^{1,p}(M)}+\left\|\psi\right\|_{W^{1,p}(M)}+\left\|\xi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C\left\|\omega\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}

(A.16)

for every
\omega\in L^{p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, where
\varphi
,
\psi
and
\xi
are as in (
A.15
).

Remark A.14
.

The choice of using the
L^{\infty}
-norm for the harmonic part of

in estimate (

) is somewhat arbitrary. However, since
\Harm^{k}(M)
has finite dimension, the

-norm can be replaced by any other norm (up to enlarging

, if necessary).

For later reference, we point out the following elliptic regularity lemma, immediate consequence of Proposition
A.13
and the Open Mapping Theorem.

Lemma A.15
.

Let
j\geq 0
and integer and
p\in(1,\infty)
. For every
f\in W^{j,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
such that
(f,\xi)=0
for any

, there exists a unique

such that

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=f&\textrm{in the sense of }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)\\
\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\langle v,\,\xi\right\rangle\vol_{g}=0&\textrm{for any }\xi\in\Harm^{k}(M)\end{cases}

(A.17)

Moreover,

satisfies

\left\|v\right\|_{W^{j+2,p}(M)}\leq C_{p,j}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{j,p}(M)}

(A.18)

for some constant

depending only on

,

,

,

.

Proof.

Existence and uniqueness readily follow from Proposition

. Thus, for any

\Delta\colon W^{j+2,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\cap(\Harm^{k}(M))^{\perp}\to W^{j,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\cap(\Harm^{k}(M))^{\perp}

is a continuous bijection, hence a Banach space isomorphism by the Open Mapping Theorem. Estimate (
A.18
) is an immediate consequence of this latter fact.
∎

Remark A.16
.

Clearly, if

. (If

A.2
Hermitian line bundles, connections, and weak covariant derivatives of Sobolev sections

A
Hermitian metric
on a complex line bundle
E\to M
is an assignment, for each
x\in M
, of a positive definite Hermitian form
h_{x}:E_{x}\times E_{x}\to\mathbb{C}
that is smooth in the sense that, for all sections
u_{1}
,
u_{2}\in C^{\infty}(M,\,E)
, the function
x\mapsto h_{x}(u_{1}(x),u_{2}(x))
is smooth. In this case we say that
E\to M
is a
Hermitian line bundle
. The typical fibre of
E\to M
is of course
\mathbb{C}
. The structure group of a Hermitian line bundle
E\to M
automatically reduces to
\U(1)

[
44
, pp. 280-1]
. Naturally, the metric allows to identify
E^{\prime}
and
E
. Associated with a Hermitian metric, there is a canonical scalar product, i.e., its real part, that we denote
\left\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\right\rangle
. In other words, we set

\left\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\right\rangle:=\frac{1}{2}(h+\bar{h}).

A (smooth)
connection

\D
on a vector bundle
E\to M
is a linear map

\D\colon C^{\infty}(M,\,E)\to C^{\infty}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)

satisfying Leibniz’ rule:

\forall f\in C^{\infty}(M),\,\,\forall u\in C^{\infty}(M,\,E),\quad\D(fu)=\d f\otimes u+f\D u.

(A.20)

For every fixed
u\in C^{\infty}(M,E)
, we can view
\D u
a map taking a vector field on
M
as argument and giving back a section
\D u(X)
of
E\to M
. We set
\D_{X}u:=\D u(X)
and call
\D_{X}u
the
weak covariant derivative of
u
with respect to
X
.

A
metric connection
on a Hermitian line bundle
E\to M
is a connection
\D
that is compatible with the metric, i.e., satisfying
\D h\equiv 0
. This implies

\forall u,\,v\in C^{\infty}(M,E),\quad\d\left\langle u,\,v\right\rangle=\left\langle\D u,\,v\right\rangle+\left\langle u,\,\D v\right\rangle.

(A.21)

Explicitly, (
A.21
) means that, for every pair of sections
u
,
v\in C^{\infty}(M,\,E)
and every smooth vector field
X\in C^{\infty}(M,\,\mathrm{T}M)
, there holds

X\left\langle u,\,v\right\rangle=\left\langle\D_{X}u,\,v\right\rangle+\left\langle u,\,\D_{X}v\right\rangle.

We recall the following important facts:

•

For every
u\in C^{\infty}(M,\,E)
and every
X\in C^{\infty}(M,\,\mathrm{T}M)
, the value
(\D_{X}u)(x)
of
\D_{X}u
at each
x\in M
depends only on
X(x)
and the values of
u
along any smooth curve representing
X(x)

[
44
, p. 502]
. In fact,
\D_{A}
is a local operator and behaves naturally with respect to restrictions
[
44
, Section 12.1]
.

•

Let
U\subset M
be an open set so that
E\to M
is trivial over
U
,
\chi_{U}:E|_{U}\to U\times\mathbb{C}
a corresponding local trivialisation, and
e_{U}
a reference section for
E\to M
over
U
. Then every
u\in C^{\infty}(M,\,E)
writes as
u=\widetilde{u}e_{U}
for some smooth complex-valued function
\widetilde{u}
and we have, with respect to the local trivialisation
\chi_{U}
,

\D u=\left(\d\widetilde{u}+A_{U}\widetilde{u}\right)\otimes e_{U}\qquad\mbox{in }U,

where
A_{U}\in C^{\infty}(U,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}U)
is a complex-valued 1-form, called the
connection 1-form
of
\D
over
E|_{U}
with respect to
\chi_{U}
. If
\widetilde{\chi}_{U}:E|_{U}\to U\times\mathbb{C}
is another local trivialisation for
E\to M
over
U
, set
g_{U}:=\widetilde{\chi}_{U}\circ\chi_{U}^{-1}
(notice that
g_{U}\in C^{\infty}(U\times\mathbb{C},\,U\times\mathbb{C})
). Denoting
\widetilde{A}_{U}
the connection 1-form of
\D
with respect to
\widetilde{\chi}_{U}
, then the transformation law

A_{U}=\widetilde{A}_{U}+g_{U}^{-1}\d g_{U}

(A.22)

holds. From (
A.22
) it is readily seen that
\d A_{U}
does
not
depend on the local trivialisation.

•

It is a general fact that connections on a vector bundle
E\to M
form an affine space modelled over
C^{\infty}(M,\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes{\rm End}(E))
, where
{\rm End}(E)
is the bundle of endomorphisms of
E
. This means that, upon choosing a reference connection
\D_{0}
, any other connection on
E\to M
writes as

\D_{A}:=\D_{0}+A,

(A.23)

for some
A\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes{\rm End}(E))
. In the case of a Hermitian line bundle with a reference metric connection
\D_{0}
,
\D_{A}
is still a (smooth) metric connection if and only if
A
belongs to the smaller space
C^{\infty}(M,\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes{\rm Ad}(E))
. Here,
{\rm Ad}(E)
denotes the bundle of endomorphisms of
E
which are skew Hermitian on each fiber. Thus, the typical fiber of
{\rm Ad}(E)
is the Lie algebra of the structure group of
E
. In our case,
{\rm Ad}(E)
is a trivial bundle with typical fiber
\U(1)
. As the latter can be identified with
i\mathbb{R}
,
A
should take purely imaginary values in any local trivialisation. In addition, we have

C^{\infty}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes{\rm Ad}(E))\simeq C^{\infty}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M),

with canonical isomorphism. Thus, we can identify
A
with a 1-form with purely imaginary coefficients. However, it is customary to assume instead that
A
is real valued, writing
-iA
in place of
A
. We then rewrite (
A.23
) as

\D_{A}:=\D_{0}-iA.

(A.24)

Explicitly, (
A.24
) means that, for every
u\in C^{\infty}(M,\,E)
and every smooth vector field
X
on
M
, we have

\D_{A,\,X}u=\D_{0,\,X}u-iA(X)u.

The
curvature

\D^{2}_{A}
of a connection
\D_{A}
is given by the following formula: for all
u\in C^{\infty}(M,\,E)
and all
X,Y\in C^{\infty}(M,\,\mathrm{T}M)
,

\D_{A}^{2}u(X,Y):=\D_{A,\,X}\D_{A,\,Y}u-\D_{A,\,Y}\D_{A,\,X}u-\D_{A,\,[X,Y]}u.

(A.25)

One easily checks
[
44
, Section 12.5]
that there exists a
closed

{\rm End}(E)
-valued 2-form
F_{A}
, called the
curvature form
of
\D_{A}
, such that

\forall u\in C^{\infty}(M,\,E),\,\,\forall X,Y\in C^{\infty}(M,\,\mathrm{T}M),\quad\D^{2}_{A}u(X,Y)=F_{A}(X,Y)u.

As for
A
, if
\D_{A}
is a metric connection on a Hermitian line bundle,
F_{A}
is an
{\rm Ad}(E)
-valued 2-form taking purely imaginary values in any local trivialisation. Thus,
F_{A}
is identified with a 2-form on
M
which is assumed to be real-valued, replacing
F_{A}
with
-iF_{A}
in the above formula. Then, denoting
F_{0}
the curvature form of the reference connection, it holds

F_{A}=F_{0}+\d A.

(A.26)

So far, we have dealt with smooth sections and smooth connections. We now extend the previous discussion to Sobolev sections and connections. To this end, we have to define the concept of weak covariant derivative of a non-smooth section
u:M\to E
. For the moment being, we still assume
A
is a smooth 1-form on
M
.

The first ingredient we need is the extension, for every integer
0\leq k\leq n
, of
\D_{A}
to an operator from
C^{\infty}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)
into
C^{\infty}(M,\,\Lambda^{k+1}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)
. This is standardly done by introducing the
exterior covariant derivative
induced by
\D_{A}
, which we denote
\d^{A}
. For the definition of
\d^{A}
, we address the reader to
[
44
, Section 12.9]
. The properties of
\d^{A}
are formally similar to those of
\D_{A}
and they are summarised in
[
44
, Theorem 12.57]
. Here we only stress that, obviously,
\d^{A}
coincides with
\D_{A}
on
C^{\infty}(M,\,E)
, i.e., if
k=0
.

Next, we extend
*
to an operator from
\Gamma(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)
to
\Gamma(M,\,\Lambda^{n-k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)
, which we still denote
*
. To this purpose, it is enough to define the action of
*
on simple elements of
\Gamma(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)
by letting

*(\omega\otimes u):=(*\omega)\otimes u

(A.27)

for
u\in\Gamma(M,\,E)
and
\omega\in\Gamma(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
. The rule (
A.27
), extended linearly, gives a meaning to
*\sigma
for every
\sigma\in\Gamma(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)
and
0\leq k\leq n
integer. Using the scalar product associated with the metric of
E
and the scalar product of
k
-forms induced by the metric, we define

\langle\langle\omega_{1}(x)\otimes u_{1}(x),\omega_{2}(x)\otimes u_{2}(x)\rangle\rangle\vol_{g}:=\left\langle u_{1}(x),\,u_{2}(x)\right\rangle(\omega_{1}(x)\wedge*\omega_{2}(x)),

(A.28)

for every
u_{1}
,
u_{2}\colon M\to E
measurable sections and measurable
k
-forms
\omega_{1}
,
\omega_{2}
, and a.e.
x\in M
. From (
A.28
) we define a corresponding
L^{2}
-product

((\omega_{1}\otimes u_{1},\omega_{2}\otimes u_{2})):=\int_{M}\langle\langle\omega_{1}\otimes u_{1},\omega_{2}\otimes u_{2}\rangle\rangle\,\vol_{g}

(A.29)

anytime the right hand side of (
A.29
) exists. Once again, extending (bi)linearly the rule (
A.29
), we can define the
L^{2}
-product of arbitrary
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}\in\Gamma(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)
by letting

((\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})):=\int_{M}\langle\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\rangle\rangle\,\vol_{g}

(A.30)

anytime the integral at right hand side exists.

With all this machinery at disposal, we can define the
formal adjoint
of
\d^{A}
as the operator

(\d^{A})^{*}:C^{\infty}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)\to C^{\infty}(M,\,\Lambda^{k-1}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)

which is formally adjoint to
\D_{A}
with respect to the
L^{2}
-product (
A.30
). An explicit computation yields (cf., e.g.,
[
43
, Section 4.2]
)

(\d^{A})^{*}:=(-1)^{n(k+1)+1}*\d^{A}*=(-1)^{n(k+1)+1}*(\d^{0}-iA)*,

where
\d^{0}
denotes the exterior covariant derivative induced by the reference connection
\D_{0}
. For
k=1
, we set
\D_{A}^{*}:=(\d^{A})^{*}
.

We can finally define the concept of weak covariant derivative of a section
u\in L^{1}(M,\,E)
. We say that
u\in L^{1}(M,\,E)
has
weak covariant derivative

\D_{A}u
if there exists a section
\sigma_{u}\in L^{1}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)
such that

((\sigma_{u},\tau))=((u,\D_{A}^{*}\tau))

(A.31)

for every
\tau\in C^{\infty}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)
. In such case,
\sigma_{u}
is uniquely determined, and we set
\D_{A}u:=\sigma_{u}
. In particular, if
u\in W^{1,2}(M,\,E)
, then (
A.31
) defines a linear bounded operator

\D_{A}\colon W^{1,2}(M,\,E)\to L^{2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E).

We are now in position to weaken the requirement
A\in C^{\infty}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
in force so far. Indeed, if
u\in W^{1,2}(M,\,E)
and
\D_{0}
is a smooth reference connection, then
\D_{0}u
is well-defined through (
A.31
) and belongs to
L^{2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)
(and in turn to
L^{1}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)
, by the compactness of
M
). For
A\in W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, we have
Au\in L^{1}(M,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, hence we can define

\D_{A}u:=\D_{0}u-iAu.

Clearly,
\D_{A}u
belongs to
L^{1}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)
. Moreover, if
u\in(L^{\infty}\cap W^{1,2})(M,\,E)
, then
\D_{A}u\in L^{2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes E)
. Notice that, even in this context,
\D_{A}
is a local operator and behaves naturally with respect to restrictions.

For
A\in W^{1,2}(M,\,\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, we
define
the curvature 2-form of
\D_{A}
by Equation (
A.26
), i.e., we define
F_{A}:=F_{0}+\d A
. In such a way,
F_{A}\in L^{2}(M,\,\Lambda^{2}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
for any

.

Remark A.17
.

Although of no use in this work, we record the following fact which will be needed in the forthcoming work
[
22
]

\D_{A}

\D_{A}^{*}

∗

Given a point

Let

The Hodge dual of currents and forms.

Appendix C
A few technical results

C.1
An interpolation lemma

Let
k\in\{0,\,1,\,\ldots,n\}
and
p\in[1,\,+\infty]
.
We define the space of
W^{-1,p}
-forms of dimension
k

as a topological dual,

W^{-1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M):=(W^{1,q}(M,\,\Lambda_{k}\mathrm{T}M))^{\prime}

where
q\in[1,\,+\infty]
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
,
such that
1/p+1/q=1
.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
p\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
0\leq k\leq n
,

1<p<n/(n-1)
. For any bounded measure
\omega

with values in
k
-forms,
there holds

\left\|\omega\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(M)}\lesssim\left\|\omega\right\|_{W^{-1,1}(M)}^{\alpha}\left|\omega\right|\!(M)^{1-\alpha}

where
\alpha:=1+n/p-n\in(0,\,1)
.

Lemma
C.1
is a special case of the more general result below (which is of some independent interest).

Lemma C.2
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
1<p<\frac{n}{n-1}
. For any
Radon vector measure
\nu
on
M
, with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
there holds

\left\|\nu\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(M)}\lesssim\left\|\nu\right\|_{W^{-1,1}(M)}^{\alpha}\left|\nu\right|\!(M)^{1-\alpha}

where
\alpha:=1+n/p-n\in(0,\,1)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{(U_{i},\varphi_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a finite regular atlas for
M
, where the (bounded) open sets
U_{i}
are contractible, and let
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a partition of unity subordinate to the covering
\{U_{i}\}
. Write
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{k})
and, for each
k\in\{1,2,\dots,d\}
and
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, define
\mu_{k,i}:=(\rho_{i}\nu_{k})\circ\varphi_{i}^{-1}
. Then, for every
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and every
k\in\{1,2,\dots,d\}
,
\mu_{k,i}
is a Radon measure on
\Omega_{i}:=\varphi_{i}(U_{i})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Therefore, we can argue exactly as in
[
41
, Lemma 3.3]
to deduce that

\left\|\mu_{k,i}\right\|_{(C^{0,\alpha}_{0}(\Omega_{i}))^{\prime}}\lesssim\left\|\mu_{k,i}\right\|_{(C^{0,1}_{0}(\Omega_{i}))^{\prime}}^{\alpha}\left|\mu_{k,i}\right|(\Omega_{i})^{1-\alpha}.

On the other hand, since the bounded open sets
\Omega_{i}
have smooth boundary, we have
C^{0,1}_{0}(\Omega_{i})=W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{i})
as Banach spaces, for every
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. Consequently,
(C^{0,1}_{0}(\Omega_{i}))^{\prime}=W^{-1,1}(\Omega_{i})
as Banach spaces for every
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. Since for
1<p<\frac{n}{n-1}
and
\alpha=1+n/p-n
any functional in
(C^{0,\alpha}_{0}(\Omega))^{\prime}
restricts to a functional in
W^{-1,p}(\Omega)
and the restriction map is continuous (by Sobolev embedding, cf. Remark
2.6
), we infer

\left\|\mu_{k,i}\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(\Omega_{i})}\lesssim\left\|\mu_{k,i}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}(\Omega_{i})}^{\alpha}\left|\mu_{k,i}\right|(\Omega_{i})^{1-\alpha}\qquad(k=1,2,\dots d).

Thus, as each
\mu_{k,i}
is the push-forward of
\rho_{i}\nu_{k}
through
\varphi_{i}
,

\left\|\rho_{i}\nu_{k}\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(U_{i})}\lesssim\left\|\rho_{i}\nu_{k}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}(U_{i})}^{\alpha}\left|\rho_{i}\nu_{k}\right|(U_{i})^{1-\alpha}\qquad(k=1,2,\dots d),

and hence

\begin{split}\left\|\nu_{k}\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(M)}&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\rho_{i}\nu_{k}\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(M)}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\rho_{i}\nu_{k}\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(U_{i})}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\left\|\rho_{i}\nu_{k}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}(U_{i})}^{\alpha}\left|\rho_{i}\nu_{k}\right|(U_{i})^{1-\alpha}\right\}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\left\|\rho_{i}\nu_{k}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}(M)}^{\alpha}\left|\rho_{i}\nu_{k}\right|(M)^{1-\alpha}\right\}\\
&\leq\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\rho_{i}\nu_{k}\right\|_{W^{-1,1}(M)}^{\alpha}\right\}\left|\nu_{k}\right|(M)^{1-\alpha}\lesssim\left\|\nu_{k}\right\|^{\alpha}_{W^{-1,1}(M)}\left|\nu_{k}\right|(M)^{1-\alpha},\end{split}

for every
k=1,2,\dots,d
, and the claimed conclusion follows immediately.
∎

Proof of Lemma
C.1
.

Recalling the (easy but useful) observation at the end of
[
5
, p. 462]
, by Nash theorem,
M\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+r}
isometrically for some
r\in\mathbb{N}
, hence
\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M
can be seen as a subbundle of
\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n+r}
, and every bounded measure
\omega
with values in
k
-forms can be regarded as a (Radon) vector measure on
M
, with values in the Euclidean inner product space
\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n+r})^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{R}^{\binom{n+r}{k}}
(where the last isomorphism of Euclidean spaces is canonical). Calling
\mathcal{V}
the image of the inclusion
\mathcal{I}:\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\binom{n+r}{k}}
,
\mathcal{V}
is a finite dimensional Hilbert space space. Then, the conclusion follows from the fact that
W^{1,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\cong W^{1,p}(M,\mathcal{V})
for every
p\in[1,\infty]
(and hence their duals can be identified, too). Indeed, although the underlined isomorphism depends on the isometric embedding
\mathcal{J}:M\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+r}
, which is generally not unique, any two choices
\mathcal{J}_{1}
,
\mathcal{J}_{2}
for the embedding give rise to equivalent Banach spaces
W^{1,p}(M,\mathcal{V}_{1})
,
W^{1,p}(M,\mathcal{V}_{2})
because of the compactness of
M
. Thus, we can apply Lemma
C.2
with
d=\binom{n+r}{k}
and reach the conclusion.
∎

C.2
Elliptic regularity

We establish here two results concerning existence, uniqueness and estimates for solutions to London (Lemma
C.3
) and Poisson (Lemma
C.4
) equations for differential
k
-forms and data in
W^{-1,p}
. Although Lemma
C.3
and Lemma
C.4
are certainly known to experts, we have not found explicit proofs in the literature. Since they are crucial to our arguments, we provide detailed proofs.

Lemma C.3
.

Let
p\in(1,\,2)
. For any
k
-form
f\in W^{-1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
,
the equation

-\Delta v+v=f\qquad\textrm{in the sense of }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)

(C.1)

has a unique solution
v\in W^{1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
,
which satisfies

\left\|v\right\|_{W^{1,p}(M)}\leq C_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(M)}

(C.2)

for some constant
C_{p}
depending only on
M
,
k
,
p
.

The proof of Lemma
C.3
depends on
Gaffney’s inequality, see Proposition
A.8
.

Proof of Lemma
C.3
.

We split the proof into several steps.

Step 1
.

Let
q>2
. We claim the following: for
any
f\in L^{q}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
,
the equation (
C.1
) has a unique solution
v\in W^{2,q}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, which satisfies

\left\|v\right\|_{W^{2,q}(M)}\leq C_{q}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{q}(M)}

(C.3)

for some constant
C_{q}
that depends only on
M
,
k
and
q
.
For any
f\in L^{2}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, existence and uniqueness
of a solution
v\in W^{1,2}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
follow from
Lax-Milgram lemma combined with Gaffney’s inequality,
Proposition
A.8
. By elliptic regularity
(see, for instance,
[
48
, Theorem 10.3.11]
),
if
f\in L^{q}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

then
u\in W^{2,q}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
and

\left\|v\right\|_{W^{2,q}(M)}\leq C_{q}\left(\left\|f\right\|_{L^{q}(M)}+\left\|v\right\|_{L^{q}(M)}\right)

(C.4)

for some constant
C_{q}
that depends only on
M
,
k
and
q
.
Now, there exists a number
s>q
such that

W^{2,q}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
embeds continuously
in
L^{s}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
(see, e.g.,
[
56
, Theorem 1.3.6]
).
By interpolation, there exists a
number
\alpha=\alpha(q,\,s)\in(0,\,1)
such that

\left\|v\right\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\left\|v\right\|_{L^{s}(M)}^{\alpha}\left\|v\right\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{2}(M)}\leq C_{q}\left\|v\right\|_{W^{2,q}(M)}^{\alpha}\left\|v\right\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{2}(M)}

By applying Young’s inequality, for any
\delta>0

we find a constant
C_{\delta}
(depending on
\alpha
,
M
,
k
and
q
as well)
such that

\left\|v\right\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\delta\left\|v\right\|_{W^{2,q}(M)}+C_{\delta}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}

(C.5)

By comparing (
C.4
) and (
C.5
),
and choosing
\delta
small enough, we deduce

\left\|v\right\|_{W^{2,q}(M)}\leq C_{q}\left(\left\|f\right\|_{L^{q}(M)}+\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\right)

(C.6)

However, by testing the equation (
C.1
) against
v
,
we obtain

\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\leq C_{q}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{q}(M)}

and (
C.3
) follows.

Step 2
.

Now, take
p\in(1,\,2)
. We claim that, for
any
f\in W^{-2,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, the
equation (
C.1
) has exactly one
solution
v\in L^{p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, which satisfies

\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq C_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-2,p}(M)}

(C.7)

for some constant
C_{p}
that depends only on
M
,
k
and
p
.
To prove the claim, we will first show existence and uniqueness of a duality solution
v\in L^{p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
and then we will prove that every solution
v\in L^{p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
in the sense of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)
is a duality solution.

We notice in first place that
p\in(1,2)
implies
q:=p^{\prime}>2
. By Step 1, given
h\in L^{q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, there exists a uniquely determined
w_{h}\in W^{2,q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
such that
w_{h}=(-\Delta+{\rm Id})^{-1}h
(that it is to say, solving
-\Delta w_{h}+w_{h}=h
), which moreover satisfies
\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{W^{2,q}}\leq C_{p}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{q}}
, where
C_{p}>0
is constant depending only on
p
,
k
and
M
.

The map
V\colon L^{q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\forall h\in L^{q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M),\quad V(h):=\left\langle f,\,\star((-\Delta+{\rm Id})^{-1}h)\right\rangle_{W^{-2,p},W^{2,q}}

(C.8)

is a bounded linear functional over
L^{q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
. Indeed, by Step 1,

\begin{split}\forall h\in L^{q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M),\quad\left|V(h)\right|&\leq\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-2,p}}\left\|\star((-\Delta+{\rm Id})^{-1}h)\right\|_{W^{2,q}}\\
&=\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-2,p}}\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{W^{2,q}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-2,p}}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{q}},\end{split}

hence

\left\|V\right\|_{(L^{q})^{\prime}}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{W^{-2,p}},

(C.9)

where the constant
C_{p}
depends only on
p
,
k
and
M
. Consequently, Riesz’ theorem implies the existence of a unique
v\in L^{p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
such that

\forall h\in L^{q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M),\quad(v,h)=\left\langle f,\,\star((-\Delta+{\rm Id})^{-1}h)\right\rangle_{W^{-2,p},W^{2,q}},

i.e., replacing
h
by the corresponding
w=w_{h}\in W^{2,q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
,

\forall w\in W^{2,q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M),\quad(v,-\Delta w+w)=\left\langle f,\,\star w\right\rangle_{W^{-2,p},W^{2,q}}.

Hence,
v
is the unique duality solution of (
C.1
) for the given
f\in W^{-2,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
. Since Riesz’ theorem also implies
\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}}=\left\|V\right\|_{(L^{q})^{\prime}}
, by (
C.9
)
v
satisfies the estimate (
C.4
).

Conversely, every distributional solution to (
C.1
) is a duality solution. Indeed, suppose
v\in L^{p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
is a distributional solution to (
C.1
) for
f\in W^{-2,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
. Then, for every
w\in C^{\infty}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
,

\begin{split}(v,-\Delta w+w)&=\left\langle v,\,\star(-\Delta w+w)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime},\mathcal{D}}=\left\langle-\Delta v+v,\,\star w\right\rangle_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime},\mathcal{D}}\\
&=\left\langle f,\,\star w\right\rangle_{W^{-2,p},W^{2,q}}.\end{split}

(C.10)

Letting again
h=-\Delta w+w
, then
h\in L^{q}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
and, from (
C.8
) and (
C.10
), we have
(v,h)=V(h)
. By the density of
C^{\infty}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
into
W^{2,q}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, the equality
(v,h)=V(h)
actually holds for every
h\in L^{q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
. Thus,
v
is duality solution. Therefore, for every
f\in W^{-2,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, Eq. (
C.1
) has a unique distributional solution in
L^{p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
, which is the duality solution.

Step 3
.

Let
f\in W^{-1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
and let
v

be the unique solution of (
C.1
). By Step 2, we have

\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq C_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-2,p}(M)}\leq C_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(M)}

(C.11)

By differentiating the equation (
C.1
), we obtain

-\Delta(\d v)+\d v=\d f
. Therefore, Step 2 implies

\left\|\d v\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq C_{p}\left\|\d f\right\|_{W^{-2,p}(M)}\leq C_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(M)}

(C.12)

Similarly,
-\Delta(\d^{*}v)+\d^{*}v=\d^{*}f
and hence

\left\|\d^{*}v\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq C_{p}\left\|\d^{*}f\right\|_{W^{-2,p}(M)}\leq C_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(M)}

(C.13)

Combining (
C.11
), (
C.12
), (
C.13
)
with Gaffney’s inequality (Proposition
A.8
),
the lemma follows. ∎

Here is a variant of Lemma
C.3
,
which will also be useful.

Lemma C.4
.

Let
p\in(1,\,2)
. Let
f\in W^{-1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)

be a
k
-form such that

\left\langle f,\,\star\xi\right\rangle_{W^{-1,p},W^{1,p^{\prime}}}=0\qquad\textrm{for any }\xi\in\Harm^{k}(M)

Then, there exists a unique
v\in W^{1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
such that

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=f&\textrm{in the sense of }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)\\
\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\langle v,\,\xi\right\rangle\vol_{g}=0&\textrm{for any }\xi\in\Harm^{k}(M)\end{cases}

(C.14)

Moreover,
v
satisfies

\left\|v\right\|_{W^{1,p}(M)}\leq C_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(M)}

(C.15)

for some constant
C_{p}
depending only on
M
,
k
,
p
.

Proof.

We proceed in three steps.

Step 1

(Existence and uniqueness of a duality solution)
.

Set
q:=p^{\prime}
. We claim that for any
f\in W^{-2,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
such that
\left\langle f,\,\star\xi\right\rangle_{W^{-2,p},W^{2,q}}=0
for every
\xi\in\Harm^{k}(M)
there exists a unique distributional solution
v\in L^{p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
to (
C.14
), which satisfies the estimate

\|v\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{-2,p}}.

(C.16)

Define
V\colon L^{q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\to\mathbb{R}
setting

\forall\alpha\in L^{q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M),\quad V(\alpha):=\left\langle f,\,-(\star G(\alpha))\right\rangle_{W^{-2,p},W^{2,q}},

where
G:L^{q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\to W^{2,q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)\cap(\Harm^{k}(M))^{\perp}
is Green’s operator, which is linear and bounded (see Section
A.1
). Then
V
is linear and bounded, because

\forall\alpha\in L^{q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M),\quad\left|V(\alpha)\right|\leq\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-2,p}}\left\|G(\alpha)\right\|_{W^{2,q}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-2,p}}\left\|\alpha\right\|_{L^{q}},

whence

\left\|V\right\|_{(L^{q})^{\prime}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-2,p}}.

It then follows from Riesz’ Representation theorem that there exists a uniquely determined
v\in L^{p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
representing
V
, i.e., such that
V(\alpha)=(v,\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in L^{q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
. Thus,

\forall\alpha\in L^{q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M),\quad(v,\alpha)=\left\langle f,\,-(\star G(\alpha))\right\rangle_{W^{-2,p},W^{2,q}},

(C.17)

and moreover
\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}}=\left\|V\right\|_{(L^{q})^{\prime}}
, whence
\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{-2,p}}
, i.e., (
C.16
). In addition, (
C.17
) implies
(v,\xi)=0
for every
\xi\in\Harm^{k}(M)
.

We now show that
v
solves (
C.14
) in the sense of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)
. To this purpose, we first recall from Remark
A.12
that
\Delta G(\xi)=G(\Delta\xi)
for every form
\xi\in W^{2,q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
. Then, we notice that, upon writing
\xi=\Delta G(\xi)+H(\xi)
, where
H(\xi)
is the harmonic part of
\xi
, by assumption we have
\left\langle f,\,\star H(\xi)\right\rangle_{W^{-2,p},W^{2,q}}=0
. Hence, by (
C.17
) we deduce that

\left\langle f,\,\star\xi\right\rangle_{W^{-2,p},W^{2,q}}=\left\langle f,\,\star(\Delta G(\xi))\right\rangle_{W^{-2,p},W^{2,q}}=\left\langle f,\,\star(G(\Delta\xi))\right\rangle_{W^{-2,p},W^{2,q}}\overset{\eqref{eq:v-elliptic_eq_bis}}{=}(v,-\Delta\xi),

for all
\xi\in W^{2,q}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
. Thus,
v
solves (
C.14
) in the sense of distributions and satisfies estimate (
C.16
).

Step 2

(Every distributional solution is a duality solution)
.

We argue exactly as in (
C.10
), with the operator
-\Delta+{\rm Id}
replaced by
\Delta G
.

Step 3

(Estimate)
.

To conclude, let
f\in W^{-1,p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
and let
v\in L^{p}(M,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
be the corresponding unique solution to (
C.14
). By (
C.16
), we have

\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-2,p}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-1,p}}.

(C.18)

By differentiating Eq. (
C.14
) exactly as we did for (
C.1
) in Step 3 of the proof of Lemma
C.3
, we obtain

\left\|\d v\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(M)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left\|\d^{*}v\right\|_{L^{p}(M)}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{W^{-1,p}(M)}.

(C.19)

Then, both
v\in W^{1,p}(M,\,\Lambda^{k}\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
and estimate (
C.15
) follow by (
C.18
), (
C.19
), and Gaffney’s inequality (Proposition
A.8
).
∎

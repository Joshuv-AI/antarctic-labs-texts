The Boolean polynomial polytope with multiple choice constraints
Sihong Shao, Yishan Wu

Abstract
We consider a class of 0 0 - 1 1 polynomial programming termed multiple choice polynomial programming (MCPP) where the constraint requires exact one component per subset of the partition to be 1 1 after all the entries are partitioned. Compared to the unconstrained counterpart, there are few polyhedral studies of MCPP in general form. This paper serves as the first attempt to propose a polytope associated with a hypergraph to study MCPP, which is the convex hull of 0 0 - 1 1 vectors satisfying multiple choice constraints and production constraints. With the help of the decomposability property, we obtain an explicit half-space representation of the MCPP polytope when the underlying hypergraph is α \alpha -acyclic by induction on the number of hyperedges, which is an analogy of the acyclicity results on the multilinear polytope by Del Pia and Khajavirad (SIAM J Optim 28 (2018) 1049) when the hypergraph is γ \gamma -acyclic. We also present a necessary and sufficient condition for the inequalities lifted from the facet-inducing ones for the multilinear polytope to be still facet-inducing for the MCPP polytope. This result covers the particular cases by Bärmann, Martin and Schneider (SIAM J Optim 33 (2023) 2909). Keywords: Pseudo-Boolean optimization, Multiple choice constraint, Multilinear polytope, Hypergraph acyclicity, Lifted inequality Mathematics Subject Classification: 90C09, 52B12, 90C57, 05C65, 90C26

Theorem 1.1

(Half-space Representation)

Suppose

\displaystyle\mathrm{MC}^{H}=\bigg\{w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{\mathcal{J}^{H}}\bigg|

\displaystyle w(I)=1,\ \forall\,I\in V;

\displaystyle w_{i}-\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{e}:J\ni i}w_{J}=0,\ \forall\,e\in E,i\in I\in e;

\displaystyle\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{e}:J\supseteq J_{0}}w_{J}-\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{e^{\prime}}:J\supseteq J_{0}}w_{J}=0,\ \forall\,\{e,e^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{E},|e\cap e^{\prime}|>1,J_{0}\in\mathcal{J}^{e\cap e^{\prime}}\bigg\}.

1.1

We prove the above Theorem

The second contribution is a verifiable necessary and sufficient condition for a lifted inequality to be facet-inducing.
The so-called “multilinear polytope”,

[

\max_{x\in\{0,1\}^{V}}\sum_{I\in V}p_{I}x_{I}+\sum_{e\in E}p_{e}\prod_{I\in e}x_{I},

(1.7)

where

\mathcal{S}^{H}=\left\{z\in\{0,1\}^{L(V)\cup E}\middle|z_{e}=\prod_{I\in e}z_{I},\ \forall\,e\in E\right\},

L(V):=\{\{I\}|I\in V\}

It can be readily seen that compared to

in Eq. (

This can be seen as follows: for any

Since facets are important structure

Answering the above question faces some difficulties because

With these at our disposal, we give a positive answer in Theorem

Theorem 1.2

(Lifted Facet-inducing Inequalities)
.

Suppose the hypergraph

is downward-closed, i.e., for any

,

, we have

. Let

\mathrm{MP}^{H}
. Define

\displaystyle V_{0}

\displaystyle=\{I\in V|\forall\,z\in\mathrm{MP}^{H},z_{I}=0\ \text{implies}\ c\cdot z=\delta\},

(1.10)

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=\{I\in V|\forall\,z\in\mathrm{MP}^{H},z_{I}=1\ \text{implies}\ c\cdot z=\delta\}.

(1.11)

For any

Throughout the rest of this paper, we adopt the following conventions.

For any finite sets

•

For any set

2
Basic properties

In this section, we first delineate the subset-uniformity of
\mathcal{J}^{H}
and then present some basic properties associated with two kinds of projection of

2.1
Subset-uniformity of
\mathcal{J}^{H}

For each nonempty subset
e\subseteq[n]
, let

\mathcal{J}^{e}:=\{J\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathscr{P}}|E(J)=e\}

(2.1)

collect all the subsets in
\mathcal{J}_{\mathscr{P}}
associated with
e
, and then define

\mathcal{J}^{H}:=\bigcup_{e\in L(V)\cup E}\mathcal{J}^{e}.

(2.2)

Noting that
\bigcup_{e\in L(V)}\mathcal{J}^{e}=L([n])

in Eq. (

Proposition 2.3

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle\mathrm{MC}^{H^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{MC}^{H})

(2.11a)

\displaystyle=\left\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{J}^{H^{\prime}}}\middle|\proj_{\mathcal{J}^{H}}w\in\mathrm{MC}^{H};w_{J}=\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}^{e_{1}}:J^{\prime}\supseteq J}w_{J^{\prime}},\ \forall\,J\in\mathcal{J}^{e_{2}}\right\}.

(2.11b)

Example 3.2

Suppose

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose
H(V,E)
is downward-closed. Given any
D\in\mathcal{J}^{V}
,
\proj_{\leqslant}
in Eq. (
3.6
) defines a bijection between
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
and
\mathrm{MC}^{H}_{\leqslant}

Proof.

We define
\mathcal{T}:\mathrm{MC}^{H}_{\leqslant}\rightarrow\mathrm{MC}^{H}
that maps
v
to
w
as follows:

w_{J}=v_{J},\quad\forall\,J\in\mathcal{J}^{H}_{\leqslant},

(3.7)

and for other components,

w_{J}=\mathscr{L}\prod_{\bar{i}_{I}\in J\cap D}\left(1-\sum_{i\in I-\bar{i}_{I}}v_{i}\right)\prod_{i\in J\setminus D}v_{i},\quad J\in\mathcal{J}^{H}\setminus\mathcal{J}^{H}_{\leqslant}.

(3.8)

Note that any monomial
\prod_{i\in J^{\prime}}v_{i}
in the expansion of

\prod_{\bar{i}_{I}\in J\cap D}\left(1-\sum_{i\in I-\bar{i}_{I}}v_{i}\right)\prod_{i\in J\setminus D}v_{i}

. Therefore,
\mathscr{L}
is well-defined, and so is
\mathcal{T}
.

Now, we verify that
\mathcal{T}
is the inverse mapping of
\proj_{\leqslant}
.
It is clear from (

\proj_{\leqslant}\circ\mathcal{T}=id
. Next, we prove
\mathcal{T}\circ\proj_{\leqslant}=id

, suppose

, then

satisfies the production constraints. Hence, the values of any polynomial w.r.t.

remain unchanged under the linearization operator.

We prove that
\mathcal{T}_{J}(v)=w_{J}
for any
J\in\mathcal{J}^{H}
. Firstly, for any
J\in\mathcal{J}^{H}_{\leqslant}
, we have

\mathcal{T}_{J}(v)=v_{J}=w_{J}.

Especially, when
i\in[n]\setminus D
,
v_{i}=w_{i}
. Secondly, for any
J\in\mathcal{J}^{H}\setminus\mathcal{J}^{H}_{\leqslant}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{J}(v)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{L}\prod_{\bar{i}_{I}\in J\cap D}\left(1-\sum_{i\in I-\bar{i}_{I}}v_{i}\right)\prod_{i^{\prime}\in J\setminus D}v_{i^{\prime}}

(3.9a)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\bar{i}_{I}\in J\cap D}\left(1-\sum_{i\in I-\bar{i}_{I}}v_{i}\right)\prod_{i^{\prime}\in J\setminus D}v_{i^{\prime}}.

(3.9b)

Using the fact that
i\in I-\bar{i}_{I}\subseteq[n]\setminus D
and
i^{\prime}\in J\setminus D\subseteq[n]\setminus D
, we can replace
v_{i}
and
v_{i^{\prime}}
with
w_{i}
and
w_{i^{\prime}}

RHS of Eq. (
3.9

\displaystyle=\prod_{\bar{i}_{I}\in J\cap D}\left(1-\sum_{i\in I-\bar{i}_{I}}w_{i}\right)\prod_{i^{\prime}\in J\setminus D}w_{i^{\prime}}

(3.10a)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\bar{i}_{I}\in J\cap D}w_{\bar{i}_{I}}\prod_{i^{\prime}\in J\setminus D}w_{i^{\prime}}

(3.10b)

\displaystyle=w_{J}.

(3.10c)

Therefore,
\mathcal{T}(\proj_{\leqslant}(w))=\mathcal{T}(v)=w
. Combining the fact that
w
is any vertex of
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
, and that both
\mathcal{T}
and
\proj_{\leqslant}
are linear, we conclude that

also holds on
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
. That is,
\mathcal{T}=\proj_{\leqslant}^{-1}
.
∎

4
Decomposability of
\mathrm{MC}^{H}

In this section, we provide a sufficient condition for the decomposability of the MCPP polytope
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
. Let
H_{1}(V_{1},E_{1})
and
H_{2}(V_{2},E_{2})
be subhypergraphs of
H
such that
H_{1}\cup H_{2}=H
, where
H_{1}\cup H_{2}
is defined as
(V_{1}\cup V_{2},E_{1}\cup E_{2})
. We say that
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
is decomposable into
\mathrm{MC}^{H_{1}}
and
\mathrm{MC}^{H_{2}}
if

\mathrm{MC}^{H}=\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{1}}\cap\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{2}},

(4.1)

where the polyhedron
\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{k}}
collects all the points in
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{J}^{H}}
whose projections on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{J}^{H_{k}}}
belong to
\mathrm{MC}^{H_{k}}
,
k=1,2
.
Namely,

\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{k}}=\left\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{J}^{H}}\middle|\proj_{\mathcal{J}^{H_{k}}}w\in\mathrm{MC}^{H_{k}}\right\},\quad k=1,2.

(4.2)

The decomposability in Eq. (
4.1
) implies that the half-space representation of
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
is the union of the half-space representations of
\mathrm{MC}^{H_{1}}
and
\mathrm{MC}^{H_{2}}
. Therefore, the study of
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
can be transferred to that of smaller
\mathrm{MC}^{H_{k}}
. In the rest of this paper, for
w\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{J}^{H}}
and
H^{\prime}
being a subhypergraph of
H
, we shortly denote
\proj_{H^{\prime}}w=\proj_{\mathcal{J}^{H^{\prime}}}w
.

Example 4.1
.

A natural sufficient condition for the decomposability is
V_{1}\cap V_{2}=\varnothing
, in which any
J_{1}\in\mathcal{J}^{H_{1}}
and
J_{2}\in\mathcal{J}^{H_{2}}
are disjoint. In the definition of
\mathscr{S}^{H}
in Eq. (
1.5
), all production constraints
w_{J}=\prod_{i\in J}w_{i}
are partitioned into two unrelated groups. Hence

\mathscr{S}^{H}=\left\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{J}^{H}}\middle|\proj_{H_{k}}w\in\mathscr{S}^{H_{k}},\,k=1,2\right\}.

(4.3)

Then it is easy to see that
\mathrm{MC}^{H}=\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{1}}\cap\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{2}}
.

Due to the redundancy or non-full-dimensionality inside
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
discussed in the previous section,
the conditions for the decomposability may be weaker than that for
\mathrm{MP}^{H}

[
14
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose the hypergraphs
H(V,E)
,
H_{1}(V_{1},E_{1})
and
H_{2}(V_{2},E_{2})
satisfy
H=H_{1}\cup H_{2}
. If
V_{1}\cap V_{2}\in(L(V_{1})\cup E_{1})\cap(L(V_{2})\cup E_{2})
, then

\mathrm{MC}^{H}=\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{1}}\cap\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{2}}.

(4.4)

Proof.

Take any
w\in\mathscr{S}^{H}
. It is easily seen that

\proj_{H_{k}}w\in\mathscr{S}^{H_{k}}\subset\mathrm{MC}^{H_{k}},\ k=1,2,

implying
w\in\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{1}}\cap\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{2}}
. By the arbitrariness of
w
, we have

\mathscr{S}^{H}\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{1}}\cap\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{2}}.

(4.5)

As the RHS of the above equation is convex, we can take the convex hull on the LHS, yielding

\mathrm{MC}^{H}\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{1}}\cap\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{2}}.

(4.6)

Now it suffices to prove the inverse inclusion.
Since
\mathcal{J}^{H}=\mathcal{J}^{H_{1}}\cup\mathcal{J}^{H_{2}}
, it is easily shown that
\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{1}}\cap\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{2}}
is bounded and thus a polytope.
Suppose
w
is a vertex of
\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{1}}\cap\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{2}}
, and let
u^{k}=\proj_{H_{k}}w
,
k=1,2
. Since both
\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{1}}
and
\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{2}}
are rational polyhedra,
w
is also rational. Then, by the definition of
\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{k}}
, we know that
u^{k}\in\mathrm{MC}^{H_{k}}
, so it can be expressed as convex combination of points in
\mathscr{S}^{H_{k}}
. Since
u^{k}
and all points in
\mathscr{S}^{H_{k}}
are rational, the convex combination can be written as follows without loss of generality:

u^{k}=\frac{1}{s_{k}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{s_{k}}u^{k,\ell},

(4.7)

where
u^{k,i}\in\mathscr{S}^{H_{k}},i=1,2,\dots,s_{k}
. Furthermore, by repeating
u^{k,\ell}
, we can assume without loss of generality that
s_{1}=s_{2}=:s
. Let
p=V_{1}\cap V_{2}
, and due to the condition,
p\in(L(V_{1})\cup E_{1})\cap(L(V_{2})\cup E_{2})
. For any
J\in\mathcal{J}^{p}
, we have
u^{1}_{J}=u^{2}_{J}=w_{J}
, and combining that
u^{k,\ell}_{J}\in\{0,1\}
, we obtain

sw_{J}=su^{k}_{J}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{s}u^{k,\ell}_{J}=|\{\ell\in[s]|u^{k,\ell}_{J}=1\}|,\quad k=1,2.

(4.8)

Since
\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{p}}u^{k,\ell}_{J}=1
, the subsets of
[s]
,
\{\ell\in[s]|u^{k,\ell}_{J}=1\}
, form a disjoint partion of
[s]
over
J
in
\mathcal{J}^{p}
.
Note that both of
|\{\ell\in[s]|u^{k,\ell}_{J}=1\}|
are equal to
sw_{J}
. Thus, we can rearrange
\{u^{1,\ell}\}_{\ell\in[s]}
and assume that
u^{1,\ell}_{J}=u^{2,\ell}_{J},\forall\,J\in\mathcal{J}^{p},\ell=1,2,\dots,s
. Then, by the properties of
\mathscr{S}^{H_{k}}
, for any
i\in\bigcup_{I\in p}I
, we have

u^{k,\ell}_{i}=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{p}:J\ni i}u^{k,\ell}_{J},

(4.9)

so
u^{1,\ell}_{i}=u^{2,\ell}_{i}
. Moreover, for any
J\in\mathcal{J}^{H_{1}}\cap\mathcal{J}^{H_{2}}\subseteq\mathcal{J}^{p}
, since
J\subseteq\bigcup_{I\in p}I
, we have

u^{1,\ell}_{J}=\prod_{i\in J}u^{1,\ell}_{i}=\prod_{i\in J}u^{2,\ell}_{i}=u^{2,\ell}_{J}.

(4.10)

Therefore, we can naturally combine
u^{1,\ell}
and
u^{2,\ell}
to form
w^{\ell}\in\mathscr{S}^{H}
for
\ell\in[s]
, and

w=\frac{1}{s}\sum_{\ell=1}^{s}w^{\ell}.

(4.11)

This shows that
w
is a convex combination of points in
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
, and hence
w\in\mathrm{MC}^{H}
. By the arbitrariness of vertex
w
,
\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{1}}\cap\overline{\mathrm{MC}}^{H_{2}}\subseteq\mathrm{MC}^{H}
.
∎

Recall that the join tree is an important characterization for
\alpha
-acyclicity.

Theorem 5.1

(Part of Theorem 3.4 of
[
2
]
)
.

A hypergraph is
\alpha
-acyclic if and only if it has a join tree.

We repeat the definition of join tree to be more self-contained.

Definition 5.2

(Condition 3.9 of
[
2
]
)
.

Give a hypergraph
H(V,E)
, a join tree
T
of

is a tree (connected acyclic undirected graph) with vertex set
E
such that for any path, the intersection of the two endpoints, is contained in every vertex along the path. More precisely, let
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
be any distinct vertices of
T
, then each vertex
e
on the unique path connecting
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
satisfies
e_{1}\cap e_{2}\subseteq e
.

Note that each vertex of
T
is a hyperedge of
H
. To distinguish it from vertex of
H
, we sometimes refer to vertices of
T
as nodes when we need to stress their properties about
T
.

Before showing the proof, we quickly verify that the RHS of Eq. (
1.6
) is a relaxation of
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
.

Suppose
H
is an arbitrary graph. It is easily seen that for any
w\in\mathscr{S}^{H}
,
e\in E
, and
i_{0}\in I_{0}\in e
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{e}:J\ni i_{0}}w_{J}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{e}:J\ni i_{0}}\prod_{i\in J}w_{i}

(5.1a)

\displaystyle=w_{i_{0}}\prod_{I\in e-I_{0}}\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}

(5.1b)

\displaystyle=w_{i_{0}}.

(5.1c)

Similarly, for any
e,e^{\prime}\in E
with
|e\cap e^{\prime}|>1
and
J_{0}\in\mathcal{J}^{e\cap e^{\prime}}
, it holds that

\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{e}:J\supseteq J_{0}}w_{J}=\prod_{i\in J_{0}}w_{i}=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{e^{\prime}}:J\supseteq J_{0}}w_{J}.

(5.2)

Thus, we can define the following relaxation of
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{MC}^{H}_{\cap}:=\bigg\{w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{\mathcal{J}^{H}}\bigg|

\displaystyle w(I)=1,\ \forall\,I\in V;

(5.3a)

\displaystyle w_{i}-\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{e}:J\ni i}w_{J}=0,\ \forall\,e\in E,i\in I\in e;

(5.3b)

\displaystyle\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{e}:J\supseteq J_{0}}w_{J}-\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{e^{\prime}}:J\supseteq J_{0}}w_{J}=0,\ \forall\,e,e^{\prime}\in E,|e\cap e^{\prime}|>1,J_{0}\in\mathcal{J}^{e\cap e^{\prime}}\bigg\}.

(5.3c)

Since
\mathscr{S}^{H}\subseteq\mathrm{MC}^{H}
, we have
\mathrm{MC}^{H}\subseteq\mathrm{MC}^{H}_{\cap}
.

When

is
\alpha

,

be its join tree. By limiting

\displaystyle\mathrm{MC}^{H}_{T}:=\bigg\{w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{\mathcal{J}^{H}}\bigg|

\displaystyle w(I)=1,\ \forall\,I\in V;

(5.4a)

\displaystyle w_{i}-\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{e}:J\ni i}w_{J}=0,\ \forall\,e\in E,i\in I\in e;

(5.4b)

\displaystyle\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{e}:J\supseteq J_{0}}w_{J}-\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{e^{\prime}}:J\supseteq J_{0}}w_{J}=0,\ \forall\,ee^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E},|e\cap e^{\prime}|>1,J_{0}\in\mathcal{J}^{e\cap e^{\prime}}\bigg\},

(5.4c)

which is the same to the RHS of Eq. (

) of Theorem

.

\mathrm{MC}^{H}_{\cap}\subseteq\mathrm{MC}^{H}_{T}

Lemma 5.3

Suppose

\alpha
-acyclic, and

Proof.

Comparing Eq. (
5.3
) with Eq. (
5.4

To prove

Suppose

Suppose

\displaystyle=\{I\in V|\forall\,z\in\mathrm{MP}^{H},z_{I}=1\ \text{implies}\ z\in F\},

Lemma 6.1

If hypergraph

Suppose

The above dedution shows that

7
Conclusion and discussion

We proposed the Boolean polynomial polytope with multiple choice constraints,
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
, to study the feasible region of linearized MCPP (
1.4
). Two main theorems were proved. The first gives the explicit half-space representation of
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
when the hypergraph
H
is
\alpha
-acyclic. The second provides a necessary and sufficient condition for the inequalities lifted from the facet-inducing ones for the multilinear polytope
\mathrm{MP}^{H}
to be still facet-inducing for
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
. However, compared with the rich polyhedral study for the unconstrained case,
there is still very little research on
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
at current stage and many possible directions are left for the future.
We list some of them below.

•

It will be interesting to develop separation algorithms for the lifted facet-inducing inequalities.

•

It is mentioned that the description of
\aff\mathrm{MC}^{H}
for general hypergraph
H
is still unclear in Section
3
. A possible way to determine the implicit equalities for
\aff\mathrm{MC}^{H}
is adding hyperedges into
H
to achieve a downward-closed
H^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{MC}^{H^{\prime}}
. Then the known properties of
\aff\mathrm{MC}^{H^{\prime}}
may be useful for
\aff\mathrm{MC}^{H}
.

•

Since
\mathrm{MC}^{H}_{\leqslant}
is actually the same to
\mathrm{MP}^{H}
when
|I|=2
for all
I\in V
(see Example
3.1
), it is plausible to think that the results of
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
, e.g., Theorems
1.1
and
4.1
, may be specialized for
\mathrm{MP}^{H}
through the isomorphism between
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
and
\mathrm{MC}^{H}_{\leqslant}
(under a certain condition).

•

There is a less well-known acyclicity degree called “cycle-free” defined in
[
5
]
. The condition for this cycle-freedom is even weaker than that for the
\alpha
-acyclicity. It may be worth studying the properties of
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
when
H
is cycle-free.

•

For small hypergraph
H
, there are special facet-inducing inequalities for
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
other than those lifted from
\mathrm{MP}^{H}
found by symbolic computation. Similar inequalities for general
\mathrm{MC}^{H}
still need to be identified and classified.

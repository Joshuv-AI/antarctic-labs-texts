Dynamo and the Adiabatic Invariant
Alexander M. Balk

Abstract
The paper considers dynamo generated by a shallow fluid layer in a celestial body (planet or star). This dynamo is based on the extra invariant for interacting magnetic Rossby waves. The magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) is linearized on the background of strong toroidal magnetic field. The extra invariant is used to show that the background field is maintained.

Introduction

Magnetic field of a celestial body often crucially depends on the shallow fluid layer. In the case of Sun, this layer is tachocline

1
]

We linearize this dynamics on the background of strong toroidal magnetic field

\displaystyle{\sf r}=c_{g}/f\qquad\&\qquad\ell=\sqrt{|B_{0}/\beta|}.

The scale

B_{0}
;

We notice that in each of the two cases (Earth and Sun), these scales have the same order of magnitude. If

\displaystyle\omega_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-|B_{0}|p,

(1.2a)

\displaystyle\omega_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|B_{0}|p\,{\sf r}^{2}k^{2}}{1+{\sf r}^{2}k^{2}}

(1.2b)

We then see that the long magnetic Rossby waves, (

Some source (e.g. the arrival of light fluid bubbles from the inner core) excites relatively short waves (
{\sf r}k\gtrsim 1
). Interacting, they produce other waves. Most of the energy from the source is transferred towards large

We use the adiabatic invariant

4

5
]
to show that the energy mainly accumulates in zonal magnetic field, and so, the background field

is maintained. This extra invariant was found for the usual hydrodynamic Rossby waves and plasma drift waves. But since the dispersion law (

\displaystyle\eta=\arctan\frac{q+p\sqrt{3}}{{\sf r}k^{2}}-\arctan\frac{q-p\sqrt{3}}{{\sf r}k^{2}};

(1.3)

the invariant equals integrated over
{\bf k}
the product of this function with the wave action spectrum,

We then find the proportion of magnetic energy contained in different components of magnetic field. Finally, we consider the build-up of magnetic field in the process resembling sunspot activity.

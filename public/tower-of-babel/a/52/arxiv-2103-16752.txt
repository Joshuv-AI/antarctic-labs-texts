Abstract
In this paper, we consider a prototypical convex optimization problem with multi-block variables and separable structures. By adding the Logarithmic Quadratic Proximal (LQP) regularizer with suitable proximal parameter to each of the first grouped subproblems, we develop a partial LQP-based Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM-LQP). The dual variable is updated twice with relatively larger stepsizes than the classical region ( 0 , 1 + 5 2 ) (0,\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}) . Using a prediction-correction approach to analyze properties of the iterates generated by ADMM-LQP, we establish its global convergence and sublinear convergence rate of O ⁡ ( 1 / T ) O(1/T) in the new ergodic and nonergodic senses, where T T denotes the iteration index. We also extend the algorithm to a nonsmooth composite convex optimization and establish similar convergence results as our ADMM-LQP.

Convex optimization; Alternating direction method of multipliers; Proximal term; Larger stepsize; Convergence complexity

Introduction

Consider the following multi-block separable convex optimization problem

\begin{array}[]{lll}\min&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}f_{i}(x_{i})+g(y)\\
\textrm{s.t. }&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}A_{i}x_{i}+By=b,\\
&x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m_{i}}{,}~y\in{\cal{Y}}~,i=1,\cdots,p,\end{array}

(1)

where
f_{i}(x_{i}):\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m_{i}}\to\mathbb{R},\;g(y):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
are
closed proper convex functions (but not necessarily smooth/ strongly convex);
A_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m_{i}},B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are given data;
{\cal{Y}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a closed convex set and
p\geq 1
is an integer. Throughout our discussions, the solution set of the problem (
1
) is assumed to be nonempty and all of matrices
A_{i}(i=1,\ldots,p)
and
B
have full column ranks. For convenience of analysis, we denote
A=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{p}),\;{\bf{x}}=({x_{1}^{\sf T}},\ldots,{x_{p}^{\sf T}})^{\sf T},\;{\cal{X}}=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m_{p}}
and
{\cal{W}}={\cal{X}}\times{\cal{Y}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Problems in the form of (
1
) cover a large number of practical applications, for example, traffic assignment, network economics, game theoretic and cancer diagnostics
problems, see
[
10
,
15
,
22
,
24
,
30
]
. Here we take three concrete motivating examples:

•

Traffic network equilibrium problem
. Consider a directed traffic network including 25
nodes, 37 links, and 6 origin/destination (O/D) pairs. In order to avoid traffic
congestion, it is common to require the link flow to be bounded from above, that is, the path flow
x
should satisfy
x\in S:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{55}|A^{\sf T}x\leq b,x\geq{\bf{0}}\},

where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{55\times 37}
is the path-link incidence matrix and
b
is the given link capacity vector.
Then, the traffic network equilibrium problem can be described to seek a path flow
pattern
x^{*}
such that

x^{*}\geq{\bf{0}},\quad\left\langle x-x^{*},f(x^{*})\right\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall x\in S,

(2)

where
f(x^{*})=At(A^{\sf T}x)-E\eta(E^{\sf T}x)
,
E\in\mathbb{R}^{55\times 6}
is the path-O/D pair incidence
matrix,
t
represents a given link travel cost vector and
\eta
denotes the travel disutility determined by O/D pair. As discussed in
[
15
]
,
the traffic equilibrium problem (
2
) can be mathematically characterized as the form (
10
), i.e., an equivalent form of Problem (
1
) with
\theta_{2}(y)\equiv 0,B={\bf{I}},p=1
and
{\cal{Y}}=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{55}
. Performance of the two-block case of our proposed algorithm for solving this problem had been verified in the experiments
[
30
, Section 5]
.

•

Sparse signal processing problem
. As stated in
[
32
]
, many signal processing problem in medical imaging, computed tomography and so forth can be mathematically modeled as a large-scale linear equations
{\cal{A}}{\bf{x}}=b
. In order to find a sparse nonnegative solution to this equation with e.g.
{\cal{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{10000\times 5000}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{10000}
, an effective way is to split
{\cal{A}}=[A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{10}]
and
{\bf{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{10})^{\sf T}
where
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{500}
. Hence, the problem can be converted to the following small-scale minimization problem:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\min&\|x_{1}\|_{1}+\|x_{2}\|_{1}+\cdots+\|x_{10}\|_{1}\\
\textrm{s.t.}&A_{1}x_{1}+A_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+A_{10}x_{10}=b,~x_{i}\geq{\bf{0}},i=1,\cdots,10.\end{array}

Clearly, this reformulated problem is a multi-block case of Problem (
1
) with
p=9,f_{i}=\|x_{i}\|_{1},g=\|x_{10}\|_{1},B=A_{10}
and
{\cal{Y}}=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{500}
.

•

Linear programming with box constraints
. Linear programming can date back to the time of Fourier who published a method to solve problems with a system of linear inequalities, see e.g.
[
3
, page 257]
. It is a widely studied field in optimization and covers many practical problems, e.g. network flow problems and the model of trolley network
[
28
]
. Linear programming problems with box-type constraints can be expressed as

\begin{array}[]{lll}\min&c^{\sf T}z\\
\textrm{s.t. }&Bz=b,l\leq z\leq u,\end{array}

where
c,l,u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
B\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are given. Its dual problem reads

\begin{array}[]{lll}\min&u^{\sf T}x_{1}-l^{\sf T}x_{2}+b^{\sf T}y\\
\textrm{s.t. }&x_{1}-x_{2}+B^{\sf T}y=-c,\\
&x_{1}\geq{\bf{0}},x_{2}\geq{\bf{0}},y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\end{array}

which is clearly the form of Problem (
1
) with three blocks.

A prototype method for solving the equality constrained problem in the form of (
1
) is the Augmented Lagrangian
Method (ALM,
[
14
]
). For any
\beta>0
, by constructing the augmented Lagrange function associated with (
1
):

{\cal{L}}_{\beta}({\bf{x}},y,\lambda)={\cal{L}}({\bf{x}},y,\lambda)+\frac{\beta}{2}\left\|A{\bf{x}}+By-b\right\|^{2},

(3)

where

\qquad~{\cal{L}}({\bf{x}},y,\lambda)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}f_{i}(x_{i})+g(y)-\left\langle\lambda,A{\bf{x}}+By-b\right\rangle,

(4)

ALM firstly updates the primal variables by solving the jointed subproblem

\min_{({\bf{x}},y)\in{\cal{X}}\times{\cal{Y}}}{{\cal{L}}_{\beta}}({\bf{x}},y,\lambda^{k})

and then updates the Lagrange multipliers by
\lambda^{k+1}=\lambda^{k}-\beta(A{\bf{x}}^{k+1}+By^{k+1}-b)
. However, ALM does not make full use of the separable structure of the
objective functions and hence, could not take advantage of some special
properties of each component function; furthermore, in many real applications involving big-data, solving this jointed subproblem
would be very expensive or even difficult at each iteration.

An effective approach to overcome the above disadvantages is the Alternating
Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) which could be regarded as a
splitting version of ALM:

\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}x_{1}^{k+1}&\leftarrow&\arg\min\limits_{x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m_{1}}}{\cal{L}}_{\beta}(x_{1},x_{2}^{k},\cdots,x_{p}^{k},y^{k},\lambda^{k}),\\
~~\vdots&&\\
x_{p}^{k+1}&\leftarrow&\arg\min\limits_{x_{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m_{p}}}{\cal{L}}_{\beta}(x_{1}^{k+1},\cdots,x_{p-1}^{k+1},x_{p},y^{k},\lambda^{k}),\\
y^{k+1}&\leftarrow&\arg\min\limits_{y\in{\cal{Y}}}{\cal{L}}_{\beta}(x_{1}^{k+1},\cdots,x_{p}^{k+1},y,\lambda^{k}),\\
\lambda^{k+1}&\leftarrow&\lambda^{k}-\beta(A{\bf{x}}^{k+1}+By^{k+1}-b).\end{array}\right.

(5)

Compared ADMM (
5
) to ALM, each subproblem of ADMM annihilates the coupled term
\beta\langle A{\bf{x}},By\rangle
and makes its iteration independent on other variables, so ADMM can be quite effective for solving large-scale problems with preferred structures. As commented by
Boyd et al.
[
2
]
, “ADMM is at least comparable to very specialized algorithms (even in the
serial setting), and in most cases, the simple ADMM algorithm will be efficient enough to be
useful.”
Although the classical ADMM
[
12
,
13
]
was demonstrated convergent for the two-block separable convex optimization, its direct extension in the Gauss-Seidel scheme (
5
) is not necessarily convergent
[
8
]
without special assumptions on the coefficient matrix in the equality constraints. Besides, it was pointed out by
He et al.
[
17
]
that the following Jacobi-type extension of ADMM:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}x_{1}^{k+1}&\leftarrow&\arg\min\limits_{x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m_{1}}}{\cal{L}}_{\beta}(x_{1},x_{2}^{k},\cdots,x_{p}^{k},y^{k},\lambda^{k}),\\
~~\vdots&&\\
x_{p}^{k+1}&\leftarrow&\arg\min\limits_{x_{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m_{p}}}{\cal{L}}_{\beta}(x_{1}^{k},\cdots,x_{p-1}^{k},x_{p},y^{k},\lambda^{k}),\\
y^{k+1}&\leftarrow&\arg\min\limits_{y\in{\cal{Y}}}{\cal{L}}_{\beta}(x_{1}^{k+1},\cdots,x_{p}^{k+1},y,\lambda^{k}),\\
\lambda^{k+1}&\leftarrow&\lambda^{k}-\beta(A{\bf{x}}^{k+1}+By^{k+1}-b),\end{array}\right.

(6)

is not necessarily convergent either.

Since the development of ADMM in 1970s, a great majority of ADMM-type methods were proposed for solving the multi-block separable convex optimization problems. Particularly, many researchers focused on modifying the above scheme (
5
) or (
6
) by adding proper
proximal terms into the subproblems of ADMM, which aims to promote the algorithmic convergence and also to overcome the obstacle that each involved subproblem is not easily solvable. For instance, a proximal ADMM using the Gauss-Seidel approach (
5
) was developed in
[
18
]

to solve the
3
-block separable convex problem, in which proximal terms
\frac{\tau\beta}{2}\|A_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{k})\|^{2}
were
added for the second and third subproblems, and global convergence was guaranteed when the proximal parameter
\tau\in[1,+\infty)
.
To deal with the convex problem (
1
) with
N
-block variables (
N\geq 3
), a proximal ADMM using the Jacobian approach (
6
) was proposed in
[
19
]
,
where the terms in the form of
\frac{s\beta}{2}\|A_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{k})\|^{2}
were added to each generated subproblem and its global convergence
was established when the proximal parameter
s\in[N-1,+\infty)
.
In the recent work
[
20
,
21
]
, the proximal point techniques were further studied and
global convergence of the algorithms therein was established with the aid of a prediction-correction approach for the iterative sequence.
More recently, by designing a positive proximal term for each
x_{i}
-subproblem, Bai et al.
[
5
, the scheme (74)]
developed a generalized symmetric ADMM whose dual variable was updated twice with larger stepsizes than the region
(0,\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2})
. Under the assumptions that all subdifferential of each component objective function is piecewise linear multi-functions, Bai et al.
[
6
]
even established the linear convergence rate of this generalized symmetric ADMM.
We refer interested readers to
[
9
,
11
,
23
,
25
,
31
,
30
]
for other proximal ADMMs by exploiting general proximal terms
to solve the two block and multiple block cases.

It is a fact acknowledged that
the performance of ADMM-type methods depends significantly on the difficulty of solving each subproblem. For the problem (
1
), the involved subproblems usually do
not have closed-form solutions and need to be solved approximately by inexact methods or some inner iterative algorithms. Fortunately, for the nonnegative linearly constrained problem the Logarithmic Quadratic Proximal (LQP,
[
1
]
) regularizer can convert the splitting subproblem to a system of nonlinear
equations
, which is comparatively easier
than the original one. Another merit is that LQP regularization can
ensure that the solution of involved subproblem stays strictly within the interior of positive
orthant. Mainly motivated by the advantages of LQP regularization and previous researches
[
5
,
27
,
29
,
30
]
, in this paper we propose a partial LQP-based ADMM (
ADMM-LQP
), which is described formally in the following table, with larger stepsizes of dual variables to solve the general problem (
1
). In our proposed algorithm ADMM-LQP,
r_{i}>0
plays the role of proximal parameter,
d(\cdot,\cdot)
is a specified LQP regularizer given by Definition
1
and the dual stepsizes

(\alpha,\tau)\in{\cal{K}}:=\left\{(\alpha,\tau)\ |\ 1>\alpha>-1,~\alpha+\tau>0,\ 1+\alpha+\tau-\alpha\tau-\alpha^{2}-\tau^{2}>0\right\}.

(7)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\hskip-7.22743pt\textrm{\bf Initialize }(x_{1}^{0},\cdots,x_{p}^{0},y^{0},\lambda^{0})\textrm{ and choose }(\alpha,\tau)\in{\cal{K}},\beta>0;\\
\hskip-7.22743pt\textrm{\bf While }\textrm{stopping criteria is not satisfied }\textrm{\bf do}\\
\hskip 7.22743pt\textrm{\bf For}\ i=1,2,\cdots,p,~\textrm{\bf do}\\
\hskip 21.68121ptx_{i}^{k+1}\leftarrow\arg\min\limits_{x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m_{i}}}{\cal{L}}_{\beta}(x_{1}^{k},\cdots,x_{i-1}^{k},x_{i},x_{i+1}^{k}\cdots,x_{p}^{k},y^{k},\lambda^{k})+r_{i}d(x_{i},x_{i}^{k});\\
\hskip 7.22743pt\textrm{\bf End for}\\
\hskip 7.22743pt\lambda^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\leftarrow\lambda^{k}-\alpha\beta(A{\bf{x}}^{k+1}+By^{k}-b);\\
\hskip 7.22743pty^{k+1}\leftarrow\arg\min\limits_{y\in{\cal{Y}}}{\cal{L}}_{\beta}(x_{1}^{k+1},\cdots,x_{p}^{k+1},y,\lambda^{k+\frac{1}{2}});\\
\hskip 7.22743pt\lambda^{k+1}\leftarrow\lambda^{k+\frac{1}{2}}-\tau\beta(A{\bf{x}}^{k+1}+By^{k+1}-b);\\
\hskip 7.22743ptk\leftarrow k+1;\\
\hskip-7.22743pt\textrm{\bf End while}\end{array}

Definition 1

Let

d(v,z):=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(v_{j}-z_{j}\right)^{2}+\mu\left(z_{j}^{2}\log\frac{z_{j}}{v_{j}}+v_{j}z_{j}-z_{j}^{2}\right)\right],&\operatorname{if}\;v\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^{n},\\
{+\infty},&\operatorname{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Lemma 1

Let
P:=\operatorname{diag}(p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

Then, the following variational inequality

\theta(v)-\theta(z)+\left\langle v-z,q(z)+P\phi^{{}^{\prime}}(\bar{z},z)\right\rangle\geq 0,~~\forall v\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},

has a unique positive solution
z

\theta(v)-\theta(z)+\left\langle v-z,q(z)\right\rangle\geq(1+\mu)\left\langle\bar{z}-z,P(v-z)\right\rangle-\mu\left\|\bar{z}-z\right\|_{P}^{2},\quad\forall v\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}.

Lemma 3

(Prediction step)
The iterates generated by ADMM-LQP satisfy

\Theta({\bf{w}})-\Theta(\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k})+\left\langle{\bf{w}}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k},{\cal{J}}(\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k})+Q(\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}-{\bf{w}}^{k})\right\rangle+\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{N}^{2}\geq 0

(16)

for any
{\bf{w}}\in{\cal{W}}
where

N=\begin{bmatrix}N_{1}&{\bf{0}}\\
{\bf{0}}&{\bf{0}}\\
\end{bmatrix}

with
N_{1}=\mu\operatorname{diag}(r_{1}{\bf{I}},r_{2}{\bf{I}},\cdots,r_{p}{\bf{I}})
,
and

Q=\begin{bmatrix}Q_{1}&{\bf{0}}\\
{\bf{0}}&Q_{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}

(17)

with

Q_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}(1+\mu)r_{1}{\bf{I}}&-\beta A_{1}^{\sf T}A_{2}&\cdots&-\beta A_{1}^{\sf T}A_{p}\\
-\beta A_{2}^{\sf T}A_{1}&(1+\mu)r_{2}{\bf{I}}&\cdots&-\beta A_{2}^{\sf T}A_{p}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-\beta A_{p}^{\sf T}A_{1}&-\beta A_{p}^{\sf T}A_{2}&\cdots&(1+\mu)r_{p}{\bf{I}}\\
\end{bmatrix},~Q_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}\beta B^{\sf T}B&-\alpha B^{\sf T}\\
-B&\frac{1}{\beta}{\bf{I}}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

(18)

Proof
. Using Lemma
2
and previous notations, the first-order optimality condition of

x_{i}
-subproblem is

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{i}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m_{i}},\quad f_{i}(x_{i})-f_{i}(\tilde{x}_{i}^{k})+\left\langle x_{i}-\tilde{x}_{i}^{k},-A_{i}^{\sf T}\lambda^{k}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\beta A_{i}^{\sf T}\left(A_{i}\tilde{x}_{i}^{k}+\sum_{r=1,r\neq i}^{p}A_{r}x_{r}^{k}+By^{k}-b\right)+r_{i}\nabla_{x_{i}^{k}}d(x_{i}^{k},\tilde{x}_{i}^{k})\right\rangle\geq 0

(19)

for any

Applying Lemma
1

\theta(v)=f_{i}(x_{i}),v=x_{i},z=\tilde{x}_{i}^{k},\bar{z}=x_{i}^{k},P=r_{i}{\bf{I}},q(z)=-A_{i}^{\sf T}\lambda^{k}+\beta A_{i}^{\sf T}\left(A_{i}\tilde{x}_{i}^{k}+\sum_{r=1,r\neq i}^{p}A_{r}x_{r}^{k}+By^{k}-b\right)

to (

\displaystyle f_{i}(x_{i})-f_{i}(\tilde{x}_{i}^{k})+\left\langle x_{i}-\tilde{x}_{i}^{k},-A_{i}^{\sf T}\tilde{\lambda}^{k}+\beta A_{i}^{\sf T}\sum\limits_{r=1,r\neq i}^{p}A_{r}(x_{r}^{k}-\tilde{x}_{r}^{k})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq(1+\mu)r_{i}\left\langle x_{i}-\tilde{x}_{i}^{k},x_{i}^{k}-\tilde{x}_{i}^{k}\right\rangle-\mu r_{i}\left\|x_{i}^{k}-\tilde{x}_{i}^{k}\right\|^{2}.

(20)

Meanwhile, the first-order optimality condition of

{\tilde{y}^{k}}\in{\cal{Y}},\quad g(y)-g(\tilde{y}^{k})+\left\langle y-\tilde{y}^{k},-B^{\sf T}\lambda^{k+\frac{1}{2}}+\beta B^{\sf T}\left(A\tilde{{\bf{x}}}^{k}+B\tilde{y}^{k}-b\right)\right\rangle\geq 0

(21)

for any

. By the way of generating

and (

), it holds

\lambda^{k+\frac{1}{2}}=\lambda^{k}-\alpha(\lambda^{k}-\tilde{\lambda}^{k})=\tilde{\lambda}^{k}+(1-\alpha)(\lambda^{k}-\tilde{\lambda}^{k}).

(22)

Plugging it into (

) and using an equivalent reformulation of (

), i.e.,

\beta(A\tilde{{\bf{x}}}^{k}+B\tilde{y}^{k}-b)=\lambda^{k}-\tilde{\lambda}^{k}+\beta B(\tilde{y}^{k}-y^{k}),

(23)

are to achieve

g(y)-g(\tilde{y}^{k})+\left\langle y-\tilde{y}^{k},-B^{\sf T}\tilde{\lambda}^{k}+\alpha B^{\sf T}(\lambda^{k}-\tilde{\lambda}^{k})+\beta B^{\sf T}B(\tilde{y}^{k}-y^{k})\right\rangle\geq 0.

(24)

Notice that, the equation (

) is equivalent to

\left\langle\lambda-\tilde{\lambda}^{k},A\tilde{{\bf{x}}}^{k}+B\tilde{y}^{k}-b+\frac{1}{\beta}(\tilde{\lambda}^{k}-\lambda^{k})-B(\tilde{y}^{k}-y^{k})\right\rangle\geq 0

for any

. Combining these inequalities (

) and the definitions of matrices

\blacksquare

Lemma 4

(Correction step)
The iterates
\tilde{{\bf{w}}}
defined by (

{\bf{w}}^{k+1}

.

3

3.1
Basic properties of
\{{\bf{w}}^{k}-{\bf{w}}^{*}\}

In the following, we begin to analyze some properties of the sequences
\{{\bf{w}}^{k}-{\bf{w}}^{*}\}
under a special
H
-weighted norm.
Followed by (
13
), (
16
) can be rewritten as

\Theta({\bf{w}})-\Theta(\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k})+\langle{\bf{w}}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k},{\cal{J}}({\bf{w}})\rangle\geq\left\langle{\bf{w}}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k},Q({\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k})\right\rangle-\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{N}^{2}.

(27)

For any
\alpha+\tau>0
, the matrix
M
involved in Lemma
4
is nonsingular. So, by (
26
) and a direct calculation, the first term on the right-hand side of (
27
) becomes

\left\langle{\bf{w}}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k},Q({\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k})\right\rangle=\left\langle{\bf{w}}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k},H({\bf{w}}^{k}-{\bf{w}}^{k+1})\right\rangle

(28)

with

H=QM^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}Q_{1}&{\bf{0}}\\
{\bf{0}}&H_{2}\\
\end{bmatrix},

(29)

where
Q_{1}
is given by (
18
) and

H_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}(1-\frac{\alpha\tau}{\alpha+\tau})\beta B^{\sf T}B&-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\tau}B^{\sf T}\\
-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\tau}B&\frac{1}{(\alpha+\tau)\beta}{\bf{I}}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

Next, we provide a sufficient condition to ensure the positive definiteness of

.

Lemma 5

For any
0<\mu<1
and
\gamma>\frac{p-1}{1+\mu}
,
the matrix
H
defined in (
29
) is symmetric positive definite if

{r_{i}\geq\gamma\beta\left\|A_{i}^{\sf T}A_{i}\right\|,i=1,2,\cdots,p,~~\alpha<1~\textrm{and}~\alpha+\tau>0}

(30)

Proof
.
It follows from the known conditions that

H\succeq\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{Q}_{1}&{\bf{0}}\\
{\bf{0}}&H_{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}:=\tilde{H},

where

\begin{split}\tilde{Q}_{1}=\beta\begin{bmatrix}(1+\mu)\gamma A_{1}^{\sf T}A_{1}&-A_{1}^{\sf T}A_{2}&\cdots&-A_{1}^{\sf T}A_{p}\\
-A_{2}^{\sf T}A_{1}&(1+\mu)\gamma A_{2}^{\sf T}A_{2}&\cdots&-A_{2}^{\sf T}A_{p}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-A_{p}^{\sf T}A_{1}&-A_{p}^{\sf T}A_{2}&\cdots&(1+\mu)\gamma A_{p}^{\sf T}A_{p}\\
\end{bmatrix}.\end{split}

So,
H
is positive definite if
\tilde{H}
is positive definite. By the block structure of
\tilde{H}
, we only need to show that both
\tilde{Q}_{1}
and
H_{2}
are positive definite. Note that
\tilde{Q}_{1}
can be decomposed as

\tilde{Q}_{1}=\beta{\begin{bmatrix}A_{1}&&&\\
&A_{2}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&A_{p}\\
\end{bmatrix}}^{\sf T}\tilde{Q}_{1,0}\begin{bmatrix}A_{1}&&&\\
&A_{2}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&A_{p}\\
\end{bmatrix}

(31)

where

\tilde{Q}_{1,0}={\begin{bmatrix}\gamma(1+\mu){\bf{I}}&-{\bf{I}}&\cdots&-{\bf{I}}\\
-{\bf{I}}&\gamma(1+\mu){\bf{I}}&\cdots&-{\bf{I}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-{\bf{I}}&-{\bf{I}}&\cdots&\gamma(1+\mu){\bf{I}}\\
\end{bmatrix}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}.}

For any
\gamma>\frac{p-1}{1+\mu}
, then
\tilde{Q}_{1,0}
is strictly diagonally dominant and thus positive definite. So, by (
31
)
\tilde{Q}_{1}
is positive definite under the conditions that
\gamma>\frac{p-1}{1+\mu}
and all
A_{i}(i=1,\ldots,p)
have full column ranks. In addition, we have

H_{2}={\begin{bmatrix}\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}B&\\
&\beta^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf{I}}\\
\end{bmatrix}}^{\sf T}H_{2,0}\begin{bmatrix}\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}B&\\
&\beta^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf{I}}\\
\end{bmatrix}

where

H_{2,0}=\frac{1}{\alpha+\tau}\begin{bmatrix}(\alpha+\tau-\alpha\tau){\bf{I}}&-\alpha{\bf{I}}\\
-\alpha{\bf{I}}&{\bf{I}}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

H_{2,0}
is positive definite if
(\alpha,\tau)
satisfy (
30
).
So,
H_{2}
is positive definite if the last two conditions of (
30
) hold and the matrix
B
has full column rank.

Summarizing the above discussions, if the conditions in (
30
) hold, then the matrix
H
is positive definite. This completes the whole proof.
\blacksquare

Theorem 1

The iterates generated by ADMM-LQP satisfy

\begin{split}&\Theta({\bf{w}})-\Theta(\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k})+\left\langle{\bf{w}}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k},{\cal{J}}({\bf{w}})\right\rangle\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left\|{\bf{w}}-{\bf{w}}^{k+1}\right\|_{H}^{2}-\left\|{\bf{w}}-{\bf{w}}^{k}\right\|_{H}^{2}\right\}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{G}^{2},~~\forall{\bf{w}}\in{\cal{W}},\end{split}

(32)

where

G=Q^{\sf T}+Q-M^{\sf T}HM-2N.

(33)

Proof
.
Taking

(a,b,c,d)=({\bf{w}},\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k},{\bf{w}}^{k},{\bf{w}}^{k+1})

in the following identity

(a-b)^{\sf T}H(c-d)=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\|a-d\|_{H}^{2}-\|a-c\|_{H}^{2}\right\}+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\|c-b\|_{H}^{2}-\|d-b\|_{H}^{2}\right\}

gives

\begin{split}({\bf{w}}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k})^{\sf T}H({\bf{w}}^{k}-{\bf{w}}^{k+1})&=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left\|{\bf{w}}-{\bf{w}}^{k+1}\right\|_{H}^{2}-\left\|{\bf{w}}-{\bf{w}}^{k}\right\|_{H}^{2}\right\}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{H}^{2}-\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k+1}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{H}^{2}\right\}.\end{split}

(34)

By a simple deduction, we have

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{H}^{2}-\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k+1}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{H}^{2}\right\}-\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{N}^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{H}^{2}-\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k+1}-{\bf{w}}^{k}+{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{H}^{2}\right\}-\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{N}^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{H}^{2}-\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}-M({\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k})\right\|_{H}^{2}\right\}-\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{N}^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k},\left(HM+(HM)^{\sf T}-M^{\sf T}HM-2N\right)({\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k})\right\rangle\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{Q^{\sf T}+Q-M^{\sf T}HM-2N}^{2},\end{split}

(35)

where the second and the last
equalities
follow from (
26
) and (
29
), respectively. Finally, (
32
) follows from (
27
)-(
28
), (
34
)-(
35
) and the definition of
G
in (
33
).
\blacksquare

In fact, setting
{\bf{w}}={\bf{w}}^{*}\in{\cal{W}}^{*}
in (
32
) we could have

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left\|{\bf{w}}^{*}-{\bf{w}}^{k}\right\|_{H}^{2}-\left\|{\bf{w}}^{*}-{\bf{w}}^{k+1}\right\|_{H}^{2}-\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{G}^{2}\right\}\\
&\geq\Theta(\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k})-\Theta({\bf{w}}^{*})+\left\langle\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}-{\bf{w}}^{*},{\cal{J}}({\bf{w}}^{*})\right\rangle\geq 0,\end{split}

from which the following theorem holds immediately.

Theorem 2

The iterates generated by ADMM-LQP satisfy

\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k+1}-{\bf{w}}^{*}\right\|_{H}^{2}\leq\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-{\bf{w}}^{*}\right\|_{H}^{2}-\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{G}^{2},~~\forall{\bf{w}}^{*}\in{\cal{W}}^{*}.

(36)

Observing that if the matrix
G
in Theorem

is positive definite, then similar convergence results to
[

,
15
,

]
hold for our ADMM-LQP. However, the matrix

3.2
Lower bound of
\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{G}^{2}

In this subsection, we focus on estimating the lower bound of
\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{G}^{2}
by combining the structure of
G
as well as the first-order optimality condition of
y
-subproblem.
By a direct calculation we have

G=\begin{bmatrix}G_{1}&{\bf{0}}\\
{\bf{0}}&G_{2}\\
\end{bmatrix},

(37)

where

G_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}(1-\mu)r_{1}{\bf{I}}&-\beta A_{1}^{\sf T}A_{2}&\cdots&-\beta A_{1}^{\sf T}A_{p}\\
-\beta A_{2}^{\sf T}A_{1}&(1-\mu)r_{2}{\bf{I}}&\cdots&-\beta A_{2}^{\sf T}A_{p}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-\beta A_{p}^{\sf T}A_{1}&-\beta A_{p}^{\sf T}A_{2}&\cdots&(1-\mu)r_{p}{\bf{I}}\\
\end{bmatrix},G_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}(1-\tau)\beta B^{\sf T}B&(\tau-1)B^{\sf T}\\
(\tau-1)B&\frac{2-\alpha-\tau}{\beta}{\bf{I}}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

In the following, we define a set of auxiliary variables

{\bf{E}}^{k+1}=A{\bf{x}}^{k+1}+By^{k+1}-b,~~{\bf{E}}_{{\bf{x}}}^{k+1}={\bf{x}}^{k+1}-{\bf{x}}^{k},~~\textrm{and}~~{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}=y^{k+1}-y^{k}.

(38)

Lemma 6

For any
\gamma>\frac{p-1}{1-\mu}
, if
r_{i}\geq\gamma\beta\left\|A_{i}^{\sf T}A_{i}\right\|
for
i=1,2,\cdots,p
, then there exists a constant
\xi_{1}>0
such that

\begin{split}\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{G}^{2}\geq&\xi_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\left\|A_{i}{\bf{E}}_{x_{i}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\beta(1-\alpha)\left\|B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}\\
&+\beta(2-\alpha-\tau)\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}-2\beta(1-\alpha)\left\langle{\bf{E}}^{k+1},B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\rangle.\end{split}

(39)

Proof
.
Since
r_{i}\geq\gamma\beta\left\|A_{i}^{\sf T}A_{i}\right\|
, we deduce

\begin{split}G_{1}&=\begin{bmatrix}(1-\mu)r_{1}{\bf{I}}&-\beta A_{1}^{\sf T}A_{2}&\cdots&-\beta A_{1}^{\sf T}A_{p}\\
-\beta A_{2}^{\sf T}A_{1}&(1-\mu)r_{2}{\bf{I}}&\cdots&-\beta A_{2}^{\sf T}A_{p}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-\beta A_{p}^{\sf T}A_{1}&-\beta A_{p}^{\sf T}A_{2}&\cdots&(1-\mu)r_{p}{\bf{I}}\\
\end{bmatrix}\\
&\succeq{\beta\begin{bmatrix}(1-\mu)\gamma A_{1}^{\sf T}A_{1}&-A_{1}^{\sf T}A_{2}&\cdots&-A_{1}^{\sf T}A_{p}\\
-A_{2}^{\sf T}A_{1}&(1-\mu)\gamma A_{2}^{\sf T}A_{2}&\cdots&-A_{2}^{\sf T}A_{p}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-A_{p}^{\sf T}A_{1}&-A_{p}^{\sf T}A_{2}&\cdots&(1-\mu)\gamma A_{p}^{\sf T}A_{p}\\
\end{bmatrix}}=\tilde{G}_{1}.\end{split}

So, it is clear that

G\succeq\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{G}_{1}&{\bf{0}}\\
{\bf{0}}&G_{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}:=\tilde{G}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{G}^{2}\geq\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{\tilde{G}}^{2}.

Then, by the structure of
\tilde{G}
the following equality holds:

\begin{split}\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{\tilde{G}}^{2}=&\beta{\begin{Vmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}(x_{1}^{k}-x_{1}^{k+1})\\
A_{2}(x_{2}^{k}-x_{2}^{k+1})\\
\vdots\\
A_{p}(x_{p}^{k}-x_{p}^{k+1})\\
\end{pmatrix}\end{Vmatrix}}_{\bar{G}_{1,0}}^{2}+\beta(1-\tau)\left\|B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}\\
&+2(1-\tau)\left\langle\lambda^{k}-\tilde{\lambda}^{k},B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\rangle+\frac{2-\alpha-\tau}{\beta}\left\|\lambda^{k}-\tilde{\lambda}^{k}\right\|^{2},\end{split}

where

\bar{G}_{1,0}={\begin{bmatrix}\gamma(1-\mu){\bf{I}}&-{\bf{I}}&\cdots&-{\bf{I}}\\
-{\bf{I}}&\gamma(1-\mu){\bf{I}}&\cdots&-{\bf{I}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-{\bf{I}}&-{\bf{I}}&\cdots&\gamma(1-\mu){\bf{I}}\\
\end{bmatrix}}.

Since
0<\mu<1
,
\gamma>\frac{p-1}{1-\mu}
and
\bar{G}_{1,0}
is positive definite, there exists a
\xi_{1}>0
such that

\beta{\begin{Vmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}(x_{1}^{k}-x_{1}^{k+1})\\
A_{2}(x_{2}^{k}-x_{2}^{k+1})\\
\vdots\\
A_{p}(x_{p}^{k}-x_{p}^{k+1})\\
\end{pmatrix}\end{Vmatrix}}_{\bar{G}_{1,0}}^{2}\geq\xi_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\left\|A_{i}{\bf{E}}_{x_{i}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}.

The definition of
\tilde{\lambda}^{k}
in (
15
) shows

\lambda^{k}-\tilde{\lambda}^{k}=\beta\left[{\bf{E}}^{k+1}-B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right].

So, by (
38
), we see that

\begin{split}2(1-\tau)\left\langle\lambda^{k}-\tilde{\lambda}^{k},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}}\right\rangle=2\beta(1-\tau)\left\langle{\bf{E}}^{k+1},B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\rangle+2\beta(\tau-1)\left\|B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}&\frac{2-\alpha-\tau}{\beta}\left\|\lambda^{k}-\tilde{\lambda}^{k}\right\|^{2}=\beta(2-\alpha-\tau)\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}\\
&+\beta(2-\alpha-\tau)\left\|B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}-2\beta(2-\alpha-\tau)\left\langle{\bf{E}}^{k+1},B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\rangle.\end{split}

The above discussions illustrate that

\begin{split}\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{\tilde{G}}^{2}\geq&\xi_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\left\|A_{i}{\bf{E}}_{x_{i}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\beta(1-\alpha)\left\|B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}\\
&+\beta(2-\alpha-\tau)\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\|^{2}-2\beta(1-\alpha)\left\langle{\bf{E}}^{k+1},B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\rangle.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
\blacksquare

Lemma 7

Suppose
\alpha>-1
. Then the iterates generated by ADMM-LQP satisfy

\begin{split}\left\langle{\bf{E}}^{k+1},-B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\rangle\geq\frac{\tau-1}{1+\alpha}\left\langle{\bf{E}}^{k},B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\rangle-\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}\left\|B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}.\end{split}

(40)

Proof
.
The optimality condition of
y
-subproblem is

y^{k+1}\in{\cal{Y}},\quad g(y)-g(y^{k+1})+\left\langle y-y^{k+1},-B^{\sf T}\lambda^{k+\frac{1}{2}}+\beta B^{\sf T}{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\rangle\geq 0

for any
y\in{\cal{Y}}
. Setting
y=y^{k}
in the above inequality gives

g(y^{k})-g(y^{k+1})-\left\langle{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1},-B^{\sf T}\lambda^{k+\frac{1}{2}}+\beta B^{\sf T}{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\rangle\geq 0.

(41)

Similarly, the optimality condition of
y
-subproblem at the previous iteration is

g(y)-g(y^{k})+\left\langle y-y^{k},-B^{\sf T}\lambda^{k-\frac{1}{2}}+\beta B^{\sf T}{\bf{E}}^{k}\right\rangle\geq 0.

Letting
y=y^{k+1}
in the above inequality, we have

g(y^{k+1})-g(y^{k})+\left\langle{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1},-B^{\sf T}\lambda^{k-\frac{1}{2}}+\beta B^{\sf T}{\bf{E}}^{k}\right\rangle\geq 0.

(42)

Then, adding (
41
) and (
42
) together is to obtain

\begin{split}-\left\langle B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1},\lambda^{k-\frac{1}{2}}-\lambda^{k+\frac{1}{2}}+\beta({\bf{E}}^{k+1}-{\bf{E}}^{k})\right\rangle\geq 0.\end{split}

(43)

Notice by the updates of
\lambda^{k+\frac{1}{2}}
and
\lambda^{k}
, i.e.,

\lambda^{k+\frac{1}{2}}=\lambda^{k}-\alpha\beta(A{\bf{x}}^{k+1}+By^{k}-b)

and
\lambda^{k}=\lambda^{k-\frac{1}{2}}-\tau\beta{\bf{E}}^{k}
,
that

\displaystyle\lambda^{k-\frac{1}{2}}-\lambda^{k+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\beta{\bf{E}}^{k}+\alpha\beta{\bf{E}}^{k+1}-\alpha\beta B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}.

(44)

Substituting (
44
) into the
left-hand
side of (
43
), we get

\begin{split}&\left\langle-B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1},(\alpha+1){\bf{E}}^{k+1}+(\tau-1){\bf{E}}^{k}-\alpha B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\rangle\geq 0\end{split}

which is equivalent to (
40
).
\blacksquare

The following result provides a concrete form of the lower bound of
\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{G}^{2}
, which is the key technique to analyze convergence properties of the proposed algorithm.

Theorem 3

(Lower bound estimation)
Let the sequences
\{{\bf{w}}^{k}\}
be generated by ADMM-LQP and
\{\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\}
be defined by (

\gamma>\frac{p-1}{1-\mu}~~\textrm{and}~~(\alpha,\tau)\in\mathcal{K},

where

{\cal{K}}
is defined by (

there exist

\begin{split}\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{G}^{2}\geq&\xi_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\left\|A_{i}{\bf{E}}_{x_{i}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\xi_{2}\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\xi_{3}\left(\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right).\end{split}

Proof
. First of all, it follows from (
39
) and (
40
) that
there exists a
\xi_{1}>0
such that

\begin{split}&\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{G}^{2}\geq\xi_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\left\|A_{i}{\bf{E}}_{x_{i}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\beta(1-\alpha)^{2}}{1+\alpha}\left\|B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}\\
&+\beta(2-\alpha-\tau)\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{2\beta(1-\alpha)(\tau-1)}{1+\alpha}\left\langle{\bf{E}}^{k},B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\rangle.\end{split}

By the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, the following holds

\begin{split}2(1-\alpha)(\tau-1)\left\langle{\bf{E}}^{k},B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\rangle\geq-(1-\tau)^{2}\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k}\right\|^{2}-(1-\alpha)^{2}\left\|B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}.\end{split}

Substituting it into (

\begin{split}&\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{G}^{2}\geq\xi_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\left\|A_{i}{\bf{E}}_{x_{i}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}\\
&+\beta\left(2-\alpha-\tau-\frac{(1-\tau)^{2}}{1+\alpha}\right)\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\beta(1-\tau)^{2}}{1+\alpha}\left(\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right).\end{split}

Remark 2

In the following, we further investigate another variant of the sublinear convergence rate in terms of the objective function value

4
Extension of ADMM-LQP

In this part, we consider the following structured convex optimization problem

\begin{array}[]{lll}\min&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}f_{i}(x_{i})+g(y)+h(y)\\
\textrm{s.t. }&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}A_{i}x_{i}+By=b,\\
&x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m_{i}}{,}~y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}~,i=1,\cdots,p,\end{array}

(56)

where
f_{i}(i=1,\cdots,p)
are the same as before,
h(y)
is nonsmooth but
g(y)
is smooth. We assume the gradient of
g
satisfies the Lipschitz condition, i.e., for any
y,\bar{y}\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},
there exists a constant
L_{g}>0
such that

\|g(y)-g(\bar{y})\|\leq L_{g}\|y-\bar{y}\|.

This inequality, by a Taylor expansion, implies

g(y)\leq g(\bar{y})+\langle\nabla g(\bar{y}),y-\bar{y}\rangle+\frac{L_{g}}{2}\|y-\bar{y}\|^{2}.

(57)

The function
h(y)
is usually used to promote a data structure different from the structure promoted by
g(y)
at the solution. Particularly, constraints of the form
y\in{\cal{Y}}
where
{\cal{Y}}
is a closed convex set, can be incorporated in the optimization problem by letting
h(y)
be the indicator function of
{\cal{Y}}
, that is,
h(y)=\infty
when
y\notin{\cal{Y}}
and otherwise
h(y)=0.

Using the popular linearization technique, the
y
-subproblem in ADMM-LQP can be updated by the following

y^{k+1}\leftarrow\arg\min\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\begin{split}&h(y)+\left\langle y,\nabla g(y^{k})-B^{\sf T}\lambda^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle+\\
&\frac{\beta}{2}\left\|A{\bf{x}}^{k+1}+By-b\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|y-y^{k}\right\|_{D}^{2}\end{split}\right\},

(58)

where
D
is a determined proximal matrix. An obvious advantage of using the proximal term
\frac{1}{2}\left\|y-y^{k}\right\|_{D}^{2}
, where
D=\sigma{\bf{I}}-\beta B^{\sf T}B
and
\sigma\geq\beta\|B^{\sf T}B\|
, is that it could transform the
y
-subproblem into the proximal mapping:

\textrm{\bf prox}_{h,\sigma}(y_{c}^{k})=\arg\min h(y)+\frac{\sigma}{2}\left\|y-y_{c}^{k}\right\|^{2}.

Here
y_{c}^{k}=y^{k}-\frac{\nabla g(y^{k})-B^{\sf T}\lambda^{k+\frac{1}{2}}+\beta B^{\sf T}(A{\bf{x}}^{k+1}+By^{k}-b)}{\sigma}.

In the following, we briefly analyze the convergence of our extended algorithm named
eADMM-LQP
for solving problem (
56
). Since the
x_{i}
-subproblem updates in the same way as before, we just need to analyze the first-order optimality condition of the subproblem in (
58
).

By the convexity of
g(y)
and (
57
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}g(\tilde{y}^{k})&\leq g(y^{k})+\left\langle\nabla g(y^{k}),\tilde{y}^{k}-y^{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{g}}{2}\left\|\tilde{y}^{k}-y^{k}\right\|^{2}\\
&=g(y^{k})+\left\langle\nabla g(y^{k}),y-y^{k}+\tilde{y}^{k}-y\right\rangle+\frac{L_{g}}{2}\left\|\tilde{y}^{k}-y^{k}\right\|^{2}\\
&\leq g(y)+\left\langle\nabla g(y^{k}),\tilde{y}^{k}-y\right\rangle+\frac{L_{g}}{2}\left\|\tilde{y}^{k}-y^{k}\right\|^{2}.\end{split}

The first-order optimality condition of
y
-subproblem is

\begin{split}&h(y)-h(y^{k+1})+\left\langle y-y^{k+1},\nabla g(y^{k})-B^{\sf T}\lambda^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\right.\\
&\left.+\beta B^{\sf T}(A{\bf{x}}^{k+1}+By^{k+1}-b)+D(y^{k+1}-y^{k})\right\rangle\geq 0,~~\forall y\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\end{split}

which, by (
14
)-(
15
) and (
22
), can be rewritten as

\begin{split}&h(y)-h(\tilde{y}^{k})+\left\langle y-\tilde{y}^{k},\nabla g(y^{k})\right\rangle\\
&+\left\langle y-\tilde{y}^{k},-B^{\sf T}\tilde{\lambda}^{k}+\alpha B^{\sf T}(\lambda^{k}-\tilde{\lambda}^{k})+(\beta B^{\sf T}B+D)(\tilde{y}^{k}-y^{k})\right\rangle\geq 0.\end{split}

The above inequalities show

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\{h(y)+g(y)-h(\tilde{y}^{k})-g(\tilde{y}^{k})\right\}+\left\langle y-\tilde{y}^{k},-B^{\sf T}\tilde{\lambda}^{k}\right\rangle\\
&\geq\left\langle\tilde{y}^{k}-y,\alpha B^{\sf T}(\lambda^{k}-\tilde{\lambda}^{k})+(\beta B^{\sf T}B+D)(\tilde{y}^{k}-y^{k})\right\rangle-\frac{L_{g}}{2}\left\|\tilde{y}^{k}-y^{k}\right\|^{2},\end{split}

which, by combining the previous inequalities (
2
) and (
25
), gives

\begin{split}\Theta({\bf{w}})-\Theta(\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k})+\left\langle{\bf{w}}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k},{\cal{J}}(\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k})+Q(\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}-{\bf{w}}^{k})\right\rangle+\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{N}^{2}\geq 0\end{split}

(59)

for any
{\bf{w}}\in{\cal{W}}
, where the notations
\Theta,{\bf{w}},\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k},{\cal{J}},Q
are
the same
as that in Lemma
3
, but the right-lower block of
Q
is

Q_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}\beta B^{\sf T}B+D&-\alpha B^{\sf T}\\
-B&\frac{1}{\beta}{\bf{I}}\\
\end{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1},}

(60)

and

N=\begin{bmatrix}N_{1}&{\bf{0}}&{\bf{0}}\\
{\bf{0}}&\frac{L_{g}}{2}{\bf{I}}&{\bf{0}}\\
{\bf{0}}&{\bf{0}}&{\bf{0}}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

Note that if taking
D=\sigma{\bf{I}}-\beta B^{\sf T}B
and
\sigma\geq\beta\|B^{\sf T}B\|
, then the
Q_{2}
given by (
60
) is similar to that in (
18
). Next, we use this form to simplify convergence analysis of eADMM-LQP.

Theorem 7

Let
D=\sigma{\bf{I}}-\beta B^{\sf T}B

Proof
.
Similar to the proof of Theorem

, we can show

\begin{split}\Theta({\bf{w}})-\Theta(\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k})+\left\langle{\bf{w}}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k},{\cal{J}}({\bf{w}})\right\rangle&\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left\|{\bf{w}}-{\bf{w}}^{k+1}\right\|_{H}^{2}-\left\|{\bf{w}}-{\bf{w}}^{k}\right\|_{H}^{2}\right\}\\

(61)

where

with

and

Finally, setting

\blacksquare

&+\beta(2-\alpha-\tau)\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+{\left\|{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}_{D-L_{g}{\bf{I}}}}+2\beta(1-\alpha)\left\langle{\bf{E}}^{k+1},-B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\rangle.\end{split}

.

y
-subproblem together with the previous notations in (

) that

Theorem 8

(Lower bound estimation)
Let
D=\sigma{\bf{I}}-\beta B^{\sf T}B
with
\sigma\geq\beta\|B^{\sf T}B\|+\frac{3-\alpha}{1+\alpha}L_{g}
, and the parameters
(\gamma,\mu)
satisfy the conditions in Theorem
3
. Then, for any

(\alpha,\tau)\in\mathcal{K},

there exist
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}>0
and
\xi_{3}\geq 0
such that

\begin{split}\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{G}^{2}\geq&\xi_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\left\|A_{i}{\bf{E}}_{x_{i}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\xi_{2}\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\xi_{3}\left(\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right).\end{split}

Proof
.
Plugging (
8
) into (
62
) and using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we have

\begin{split}&\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{G}^{2}\\
\geq&\xi_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\left\|A_{i}{\bf{E}}^{k+1}_{x_{i}}\right\|^{2}+\beta(1-\alpha)\left\|B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\beta(2-\alpha-\tau)\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\left\|{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}_{D-L_{g}{\bf{I}}}\\
&-\frac{\beta}{1+\alpha}\left[(1-\tau)^{2}\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k}\right\|^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}\left\|B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}\right]-\frac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}\left[\left\|{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k}\right\|^{2}_{D}+\left\|{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}_{D}\right]\\
&+\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{1+\alpha}\left\|{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}_{D}-\frac{2\beta\alpha(1-\alpha)}{1+\alpha}\left\|B{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{(1-\alpha)L_{g}}{1+\alpha}\left[\left\|{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\left\|{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right]\\
=&\xi_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\left\|A_{i}{\bf{E}}^{k+1}_{x_{i}}\right\|^{2}+\beta\left(2-\alpha-\tau-\frac{(1-\tau)^{2}}{1+\alpha}\right)\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}\\
&+\frac{\beta(1-\tau)^{2}}{1+\alpha}\left[\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right]+R_{xy},\end{split}

where

\begin{split}R_{xy}&=\frac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}\left[\left\|{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}_{D}-\left\|{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k}\right\|^{2}_{D}\right]+\left\|{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}_{D-L_{g}{\bf{I}}}-\frac{(1-\alpha)L_{g}}{1+\alpha}\left[\left\|{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\left\|{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right]\\
&=\frac{2}{1+\alpha}\left\|{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}_{D-L_{g}{\bf{I}}}-\frac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}\left\|{\bf{E}}_{y}^{k}\right\|^{2}_{D+L_{g}{\bf{I}}}.\end{split}

Since
D=\sigma{\bf{I}}-\beta B^{\sf T}B
, then for any
\sigma\geq\beta\|B^{\sf T}B\|+\frac{3-\alpha}{1+\alpha}L_{g}
, we have

D\succeq\frac{3-\alpha}{1+\alpha}L_{g}{\bf{I}}
and thus
R_{xy}\geq 0
. So, it holds

\left\|{\bf{w}}^{k}-\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}\right\|_{G}^{2}\geq\xi_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\left\|A_{i}{\bf{E}}^{k+1}_{x_{i}}\right\|^{2}+\xi_{2}\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\xi_{3}\left[\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|{\bf{E}}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right],

where
\xi_{i}(i=1,2,3)
are the same as that in Theorem
3
.

\blacksquare

Finally, based on the above discussions and the similar analysis to Section
3.3
, the algorithm
eADMM-LQP converges globally with a sublinear convergence rate in the ergodic and nonergodic sense.

5
Concluding remarks

In this article, we have proposed a partial LQP-based ADMM with larger stepsizes of dual variables as in
[
5
]
to solve a family of separable convex minimization problems. The so-called LQP regularization term is added to the first grouped subproblems with proper proximal parameters but it is not added to the final subproblem. With the aid of prediction-correction technique, we establish the global convergence of the proposed algorithm and its sublinear convergence rate in terms of the objective residual and the primal residual. The proposed algorithm is also extended to solve a nonsmoooth composite convex optimization, where the second subproblem is updated by using the linearization technique and proximal technique. We analyze the global sublinear convergence properties of the extended method in a brief way.

Notice that our result about the ergodic convergence rate is established for a new average iterate
{\bf{w}}_{T}:=\frac{1}{1+T}\sum_{k=\kappa}^{\kappa+T}\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}
for any integer
\kappa\geq 0
, which is more general than the traditional iterate
{\bf{w}}_{T}:=\frac{1}{1+T}\sum_{k=0}^{T}\tilde{{\bf{w}}}^{k}
in the literature of ADMM. This makes it possible to use some accelerated techniques in practical programming, see the experiments in
[
7
]
for more details. Another observation is that the constrained set
{\cal{Y}}
is any closed convex set, so our problem and algorithm are more general than two existing researches in
[
27
,
30
]
and thus have a large number of potential applications. How to extend our algorithm to the nonseparable nonconvex optimization problem and how to solve the subproblem inexactly are very interesting topics. Finally, whether the proposed algorithm could use indefinite proximal term needs our further investigations in the future work.

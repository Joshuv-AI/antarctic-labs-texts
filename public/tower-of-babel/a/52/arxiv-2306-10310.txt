Regularity and Pohozaev identity for the Choquard equation involving the p p -Laplacian operator
Vincenzo Ambrosio

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the regularity of weak solutions for a class of nonlinear Choquard equations driven by the p p -Laplacian operator. We also establish a Pohozaev type identity.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
35J92, 35B65, 45K05

1.
Introduction

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
and
N>p
.
Let us consider the following nonlinear problem:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta_{p}u+|u|^{p-2}u=(I_{\alpha}*F(u))f(u)\,\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{array}\right.

where
\Delta_{p}u:=\dive(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
is the
p
-Laplacian operator,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
I_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the Riesz potential given by

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}2^{\alpha}|x|^{N-\alpha}},

the nonlinearity
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function satisfying the following conditions:

(f_{1})

\displaystyle{\lim_{|t|\rightarrow 0}\frac{|f(t)|}{|t|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}\frac{p}{2}-1}}=0}
,

(f_{2})

\displaystyle{\limsup_{|t|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|f(t)|}{|t|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}-1}}<\infty}
,

and
F(t):=\int_{0}^{t}f(\tau)\,d\tau
.
We recall that when
p=2
,
N=3
and
F(t)=\frac{t^{2}}{2}
in (
1.1
), then we obtain the following Choquard-Pekar equation

(1.2)

\displaystyle-\Delta u+u=(I_{2}*|u|^{2})u\,\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},

which seems to originate from Fröhlich and Pekar’s model of the quantum polaron and then used by Choquard
to describe an electron trapped in its own hole, in a certain approximation to Hartree-Fock theory of one component plasma.
Equation (
1.2
) also appears as a model of self-gravitating matter and it is usually called the nonlinear Schrödinger-Newton equation.
For more details on Choquard type equations and some of its variants and generalizations, we refer to
[
10
]
and the references therein.

We stress that the choice of the exponents
\frac{N+\alpha}{N}\frac{p}{2}
and
\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}
in
(f_{1})
and
(f_{2})
is motivated by the following Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
9
]

Let
r,t\in(1,\infty)
and
\mu\in(0,N)
with
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{\mu}{N}+\frac{1}{t}=2
. Let
f\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
h\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Then there exists a sharp constant
C(N,\mu,r)>0
, independent of
f
and
h
, such that

\left|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{f(x)h(y)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\,dxdy\right|\leq C(N,\mu,r)\|f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|h\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Indeed, if
F(\tau)=|\tau|^{q}
for some
q>0
,
\mu=N-\alpha
and
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then Theorem
1.1
implies that the term

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{\mu}}*F(u)\right)F(u)\,dx

is well-defined whenever
F(u)\in L^{\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\tau=\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}
. Since
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is continuously embedded into
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
r\in[p,p^{*}]
, where
p^{*}:=\frac{Np}{N-p}
is the critical Sobolev exponent, we have to require that
\tau q\in[p,p^{*}]
and so

\frac{N+\alpha}{N}\frac{p}{2}\leq q\leq\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}.

The endpoints in the above range of
q
are extremal values for the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality and sometimes called lower and upper Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev critical exponents.
We recall that if

\alpha\in(N-p,N)
and
f
satisfies
|f(t)|\leq C_{0}(|t|^{q_{1}-1}+|t|^{q_{2}-1})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
with
p<q_{1}\leq q_{2}<\frac{\alpha p}{N-p}
,
then it is easy to verify that every weak solution to (
1.1
) belongs to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{1,\sigma}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
\sigma\in(0,1)
(see
[
1
, Theorem 2.2]
).
In fact, the restrictions on
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
and Theorem
1.1
permit to consider the convolution
K(x):=(I_{\alpha}*F(u))(x)
as bounded term, and thus
[
8
, Theorem 1.11]
can be applied for
-\Delta_{p}u+|u|^{p-2}u=K(x)f(u)
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. In this paper, we prove that the same regularity holds for solutions of (
1.1
) by considering more general nonlinearities.
More precisely, the main result of this work is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
,
N>p
and
\alpha\in((N-2p)_{+},N)
. Assume that
f\in C(\mathbb{R})
fulfills
(f_{1})
-
(f_{2})
.
Let
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a weak solution to (
1.1
), that is

(1.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p-2}u\phi\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*F(u))f(u)\phi\,dx

for all
\phi\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Then,
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{1,\sigma}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
\sigma\in(0,1)
. Moreover,
u
satisfies the following Pohozaev identity:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\frac{N-p}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx+\frac{N}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\,dx=\frac{N+\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*F(u))F(u)\,dx.

To accomplish the boundedness of solutions to (
1.1
), we use the growth conditions on
f
, Theorem
1.1
and a Brezis-Kato argument
[
4
]
to reach the
L^{r}
-regularity for all
r\in[p,\infty)
, and finally we carry out a De Giorgi type iteration
[
5
]
. The restriction
\alpha\in((N-2p)_{+},N)
ensures that
\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}>p
, and that the term

\left(\int_{\{|u|>\mu\}}|u|^{p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{\alpha+2p-N}{2N}}

goes to zero as
\mu\rightarrow\infty
and this allows us to have a convenient estimate to achieve the
L^{q}
-regularity; see formula (
2.13
).
The
C^{1,\sigma}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-regularity result is thus a direct consequence of the classical theorems established in
[
7
,
13
]
.
We recall that in the case
p=2
, Moroz and Van Schaftingen
[
11
]
obtained a
W^{2,q}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-regularity result for weak solutions to (
1.1
) and involving a Berestycki-Lions type nonlinearity
[
3
]
, by proving a suitable nonlocal version of the Brézis-Kato regularity result
[
4
, Theorem 2.3]
and invoking the Calderon-Zygmund theory. The approach in
[
11
]
does not seem to be so simple to adopt when the Laplacian operator
\Delta
is replaced by the
p
-Laplacian operator
\Delta_{p}
with
p\neq 2
, due to the nonlinear character of
\Delta_{p}
, and so we follow a different strategy. Finally, we show that every weak solution to (
1.1
) fulfills the Pohozaev identity (
1.4
). For this purpose, we take advantage of a variational identity for locally Lipschitz continuous solutions of a general class of quasilinear equations demonstrated in
[
6
]
.
Note that the identity (
1.4
) implies that (
1.1
) does not have nontrivial weak solutions in
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
when
F(t)=\frac{1}{q}|t|^{q}
with
q\notin(\frac{N+\alpha}{N}\frac{p}{2},\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2})
.
Moreover, (
1.4
) is in clear accordance with the case
p=2
investigated in
[
11
]
.

To our knowledge, this is the first time that the regularity of solutions to (
1.1
) and the Pohozaev identity (
1.4
) are considered in literature. Finally, we believe that Theorem
1.2
will play a crucial role in studying existence, multiplicity and concentration of solutions for quasilinear Choquard equations with a general nonlinearity (see
[
2
]
).

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Without loss of generality, we assume that
u\geq 0
(otherwise, we can deal with
u^{+}:=\max\{u,0\}
and
u^{-}:=\max\{-u,0\}
separately).
Let
M>1
and
u_{M}:=\min\{u,M\}
. Taking
u_{M}^{kp+1}
(
k\geq 0
) as test function in (
1.3
), we get

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(u_{M}^{kp+1})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p-1}u_{M}^{kp+1}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*F(u))f(u)u_{M}^{kp+1}\,dx.

Now, using the Sobolev inequality, we see that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(u_{M}^{kp+1})\,dx&=\frac{kp+1}{(k+1)^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla(u_{M})^{k+1}|^{p}\,dx\\
&\geq S_{*}\frac{kp+1}{(k+1)^{p}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{M}^{k+1}|^{p^{*}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}},\end{split}

where
S_{*}=S_{*}(N,p)>0
denotes the best Sobolev constant.
Clearly, since
u_{M}\leq u
, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p-1}u_{M}^{kp+1}\,dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{M}^{k+1}|^{p}\,dx.

On the other hand, exploiting Theorem
1.1
and that
(f_{1})
-
(f_{2})
yield

(2.4)

\displaystyle|F(t)|\leq C(|t|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}\frac{p}{2}}+|t|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}})\quad\mbox{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R},

we obtain

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*F(u))f(u)u_{M}^{kp+1}\,dx\right|&\leq C\|F(u)\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|f(u)u_{M}^{kp+1}\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&\leq C\left(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}\|f(u)u_{M}^{kp+1}\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&=:\tilde{C}\,\|f(u)u_{M}^{kp+1}\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.\end{split}

By
(f_{1})
, fixed
\e>0
there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that

(2.6)

\displaystyle|f(t)|\leq\e|t|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}\frac{p}{2}-1}\quad\mbox{ for all }0<|t|<\delta_{0}.

Let
0<\delta<\min\{\delta_{0},1\}
.
Using
(f_{2})
, we can find
K_{0}>0
and
C_{1}>0
such that

(2.7)

\displaystyle|f(t)|\leq C_{1}|t|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}-1}\quad\mbox{ for all }|t|>K_{0}.

Let
K>\max\{K_{0},1\}
.
Fix
\mu>K
. Since
f
is continuous in
\mathbb{R}
, there exists
C_{\delta,\mu}>0
such that

(2.8)

\displaystyle|f(t)|\leq C_{\delta,\mu}|t|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}-1}\quad\mbox{ for all }\delta\leq|t|\leq\mu.

Note that, thanks to
u_{M}\leq u
, (
2.6
), (
2.7
), (
2.8
),
(x+y+z)^{a}\leq x^{a}+y^{a}+z^{a}
for all
x,y,z\geq 0
and
a\in(0,1)
, we find

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f(u)u_{M}^{kp+1}|^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{\{|u|<\delta\}}|f(u)u_{M}^{kp+1}|^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx+\int_{\{\delta<|u|<\mu\}}|f(u)u_{M}^{kp+1}|^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx+\int_{\{|u|>\mu\}}|f(u)u_{M}^{kp+1}|^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\e^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\int_{\{|u|<\delta\}}||u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}\frac{p}{2}+kp}|^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx+C_{\delta,\mu}^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\int_{\{\delta<|u|<\mu\}}||u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}+kp}|^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx+C_{1}^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\int_{\{|u|>\mu\}}||u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}+kp}|^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}

\displaystyle\leq\e\left(\int_{\{|u|<\delta\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N}\frac{p}{2}+kp)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}+C_{\delta,\mu}\left(\int_{\{\delta<|u|<\mu\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}+kp)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{1}\left(\int_{\{|u|>\mu\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}+kp)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}

\displaystyle\leq\e\left(\int_{\{|u|<\delta\}}|u|^{p}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}kp}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}+C_{\delta,\mu}\left(\int_{\{|u|<\mu\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}+kp)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{1}\left(\int_{\{|u|>\mu\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}+kp)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}.

Since for
|u|<\delta<1
we have
|u|^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}kp}\leq|u|^{kp}
(note that
\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}>1
), we see that

\displaystyle\e\left(\int_{\{|u|<\delta\}}|u|^{p}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}kp}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}\leq\e\left(\int_{\{|u|<\delta\}}|u|^{(k+1)p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}\leq\e\int_{\{|u|<\delta\}}|u|^{(k+1)p}\,dx+\e,

where we used
x^{a}\leq x+1
for all
x\geq 0
and
a\in(0,1)
. Therefore,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f(u)u_{M}^{kp+1}|^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}\\
&\leq\e\int_{\{|u|<\delta\}}|u|^{(k+1)p}\,dx+\e+C_{\delta,\mu}\left(\int_{\{|u|<\mu\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}+kp)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}\\
&\quad+C_{1}\left(\int_{\{|u|>\mu\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}+kp)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}.\end{split}

Note that, because
0<\delta<1<M
, it holds
u_{M}=u
, and so

\int_{\{|u|<\delta\}}|u|^{(k+1)p}\,dx=\int_{\{|u|<\delta\}}|u_{M}|^{(k+1)p}\,dx.

Combining (
2.1
), (
2.2
), (
2.3
), (
2.5
) and (
2.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle S_{*}\frac{kp+1}{(k+1)^{p}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{M}|^{(k+1)p^{*}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}}+(1-\tilde{C}\e)\int_{\{|u|<\delta\}}|u_{M}|^{(k+1)p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\e+\tilde{C}C_{\delta,\mu}\left(\int_{\{|u|<\mu\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}+kp)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}+\tilde{C}C_{1}\left(\int_{\{|u|>\mu\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}+kp)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}.

Letting first
\e\rightarrow 0
and then
M\rightarrow\infty
, we arrive at

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&S_{*}\frac{kp+1}{(k+1)^{p}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{(k+1)p^{*}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}}\\
&\leq\tilde{C}C_{\delta,\mu}\left(\int_{\{|u|<\mu\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}+kp)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}+\tilde{C}C_{1}\left(\int_{\{|u|>\mu\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}+kp)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}.\end{split}

Now, we observe that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\int_{\{|u|<\mu\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}+kp)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}&=\left(\int_{\{|u|<\mu\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}-p+(k+1)p)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}\\
&\leq\mu^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}-p}\left(\int_{\{|u|<\mu\}}|u|^{(k+1)\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}\\
&\leq\mu^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}-p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{(k+1)\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}.\end{split}

On the other hand, using Hölder’s inequality,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\int_{\{|u|>\mu\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}+kp)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}&=\left(\int_{\{|u|>\mu\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}-p+(k+1)p)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}\\
&=\left(\int_{\{|u|>\mu\}}|u|^{(\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}-p)\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}|u|^{(k+1)\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}\\
&\leq\left(\int_{\{|u|>\mu\}}|u|^{p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{\alpha+2p-N}{2N}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{(k+1)p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}}\\
&=:D(\mu)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{(k+1)p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}}.\end{split}

In view of (
2.10
), (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), we deduce that

\displaystyle S_{*}\frac{kp+1}{(k+1)^{p}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{(k+1)p^{*}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}C_{\delta,\mu}\,\mu^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}-p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{(k+1)\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}}+\tilde{C}C_{1}D(\mu)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{(k+1)p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}}.

Since
\alpha+2p-N>0
,
D(\mu)\rightarrow 0
as
\mu\rightarrow\infty
, and we can choose
\mu>K
sufficiently large such that

0\leq D(\mu)<\theta\frac{S_{*}}{\tilde{C}C_{1}}\frac{kp+1}{(k+1)^{p}}\quad\mbox{ with }\theta\in(0,1).

Hence,

\displaystyle(1-\theta)S_{*}\frac{kp+1}{(k+1)^{p}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{(k+1)p^{*}}\,dx\right]^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}}\leq\tilde{C}C_{\delta,\mu}\,\mu^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}-p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{(k+1)\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2N}},

and so

(2.13)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{(k+1)p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{*}^{\frac{1}{k+1}}\left[\frac{k+1}{(kp+1)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\right]^{\frac{1}{k+1}}\|u\|_{L^{(k+1)\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

for some
C_{*}>0
depending on
k
. Set
q:=\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}
and observe that
p^{*}>q
thanks to
\alpha>N-2p
. Now a bootstrap argument can start: because
u\in L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we can apply (
2.13
) with
k+1=\frac{p^{*}}{q}
to see that
u\in L^{(k+1)p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=L^{\frac{(p^{*})^{2}}{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We can then exploit again (
2.13
) and, after
m
iterations, we obtain that
u\in L^{p^{*}(\frac{p^{*}}{q})^{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and thus
u\in L^{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
\nu\in[p^{*},\infty)
. Consequently,
u\in L^{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
\nu\in[p,\infty)
. Combining this fact with (
2.4
), and applying Young’s inequality for convolutions, it is easy to verify that

K(x):=(I_{\alpha}*F(u))(x)\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\{v\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N}):|v(x)|\rightarrow 0\mbox{ as }|x|\rightarrow\infty\}
.
In particular,
K\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then
u
satisfies
-\Delta_{p}u=\psi(x,u):=-|u|^{p-2}u+K(x)f(u)
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
with
|\psi(x,u)|\leq C(1+|u|^{r-1})
for some
r\in(p,p^{*})
. Now we show that
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by means of a De Giorgi type iteration.
Let
\rho\geq\max\{1,\|u\|^{-1}_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\}
and
v:=(\rho\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})^{-1}u
. Set
w_{k}:=(v-1+2^{-k})^{+}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
w_{0}:=v^{+}
. Note that
w_{k}\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
0\leq w_{k+1}\leq w_{k}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Let
U_{k}:=\|w_{k}\|^{r}_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
. Put
\Omega_{k}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:w_{k}(x)>0\}
. Observe that
\Omega_{k+1}\subset\{w_{k}>2^{-(k+1)}\}
,
v(x)<2^{k+1}w_{k}(x)
for
x\in\Omega_{k+1}
and
|\Omega_{k+1}|\leq 2^{(k+1)r}U_{k}
. Testing
-\Delta_{p}u=\psi(x,u)
with
w_{k+1}
and using the growth assumption on
\psi
, we can see that

\displaystyle\|\nabla w_{k+1}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla w_{k+1}\,dx=(\rho\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})^{1-p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla w_{k+1}\,dx

\displaystyle=(\rho\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})^{1-p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi(x,u)w_{k+1}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})^{1-p}\left[\int_{\Omega_{k+1}\cap\{u\leq 1\}}w_{k+1}\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{k+1}\cap\{u>1\}}u^{r-1}w_{k}\,dx\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})^{1-p}\left[2^{-(k+1)}|\Omega_{k+1}|+(\rho\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})^{r-1}2^{(k+1)(r-1)}U_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2C2^{(k+1)(r-1)}(\rho\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})^{r-p}U_{k},

where we used
w_{k+1}(x)\leq 2^{-(k+1)}
for
x\in\Omega_{k+1}\cap\{u\leq 1\}
and
u(x)<(\rho\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})2^{k+1}w_{k}(x)
for
x\in\Omega_{k+1}
. In view of the above inequality and using the Hölder and Sobolev inequalities, we arrive at

\displaystyle U_{k+1}\leq\|w_{k+1}\|^{r}_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}|\Omega_{k+1}|^{1-\frac{r}{p^{*}}}\leq\bar{C}^{k}(\rho\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})^{\frac{r^{2}}{p}-r}U_{k}^{1+\frac{r}{N}},

for some
\bar{C}>1
independent of
k
. Let
\eta:=\bar{C}^{-\frac{N}{r}}\in(0,1)
. Choosing

\rho:=\max\{1,\|u\|^{-1}_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},(\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\frac{r^{2}}{p}-r}\eta^{-1})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\},

where
\gamma:=\frac{r^{2}}{N}+r-\frac{r^{2}}{p}>0
, by induction we can prove that
U_{k}\leq\frac{\eta^{k}}{\rho^{r}}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, from which
U_{k}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Since
w_{k}\rightarrow(v-1)^{+}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
and
w_{k}\leq v\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it follows from the dominated convergence theorem that
\|(v-1)^{+}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0
. As a result,
v\leq 1
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, that is,
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\rho\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.
Therefore,
u
is a bounded solution to
-\Delta_{p}u=\psi=-|u|^{p-2}u+K(x)f(u)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and according to
[
7
,
13
]
we can infer that
u\in C^{1,\sigma}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
\sigma\in(0,1)
.

Remark 2.1
.

If we assume that
u
is a positive solution to (
1.1
)
and
f
is an odd continuous function satisfying
f\geq 0
in
(0,\infty)
,

f(t)=o(|t|^{p-1})
as
|t|\rightarrow 0
instead of
(f_{1})
, and
(f_{2})
, then we can show that
u
decays exponentially at infinity. To prove this,
fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Since
|f(t)|\leq C^{\prime}(|t|^{p-1}+|t|^{p^{*}-1})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
(note that
\frac{N+\alpha}{N-p}\frac{p}{2}<p^{*}
due to
\alpha\in(0,N)
), where
C^{\prime}>0
is a constant,
u\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
r\in[p,\infty]
and
K=I_{\alpha}*F(u)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it follows that

|\psi|\leq(1+C^{\prime}\|K\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})|u|^{p-1}+f_{*}
with
f_{*}:=C^{\prime}\|K\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}|u|^{p^{*}-p}\in L^{\frac{N}{p-\e}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
\e\in(0,1]
.
From
[
12
, Theorem 1]
, we derive that there exists a constant
C^{\prime\prime}>0
, independent of
x
, such that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x))}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\left(\|u\|_{L^{p}(B_{2}(x))}+\|f_{*}\|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}_{L^{\frac{N}{p-\e}}(B_{2}(x))}\right).

Thus,
|u(x)|\rightarrow 0
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
. Since
I_{\alpha}*F(u)\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
f(t)=o(|t|^{p-1})
as
|t|\rightarrow 0
, we can see that

(I_{\alpha}*F(u))f(u)\leq\frac{1}{2}u^{p-1}
in
B_{R}^{c}(0)
, for some
R>0
. Hence,
u
satisfies
-\Delta_{p}u+\frac{1}{2}u^{p-1}\leq 0
in
B_{R}^{c}(0)
, and arguing as in the proof of
[
1
, Lemma 2.3]
we deduce the desired exponential decay of
u
.

Next we show that
u
satisfies (
1.4
). Firstly, we recall the following useful result.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
6
]

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set,
\mathcal{L}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a function of class
C^{1}
and
g\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega)
. Assume also that
\xi\mapsto\mathcal{L}(x,s,\xi)
is strictly convex for each
(x,s)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
. Let
u:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz solution of

\displaystyle-\dive\{\nabla_{\xi}\mathcal{L}(x,u,\nabla u)\}+D_{s}\mathcal{L}(x,u,\nabla u)=g\,\,\mbox{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega).

Then

(2.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}D_{i}h_{j}D_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}(x,u,\nabla u)D_{j}u\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\left[(\dive h)\mathcal{L}(x,u,\nabla u)+h\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(x,u,\nabla u)\right]\,dx\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega}(h\cdot\nabla u)g\,dx\end{split}

for every
h\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Take
\mathcal{L}(x,s,\xi)=\frac{1}{p}|\xi|^{p}
and
g=-|u|^{p-2}u+(I_{\alpha}*F(u))f(u)
in Lemma
2.1
.
Let
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\varphi(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 1
and
\varphi(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 2
. Put

h(x):=\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)x\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Note that if
h_{j}(x)=\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)x_{j}
, for
j=1,\dots,N
, then

\displaystyle D_{i}h_{j}(x)=D_{i}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\frac{x_{j}}{k}+\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\delta_{ij},\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,j=1,\dots,N,

\displaystyle\dive h(x)=D\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\cdot\frac{x}{k}+N\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\delta_{ij}
denotes the Kronecker delta symbol. We also observe that

(2.15)

\displaystyle\left|D_{i}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\frac{x_{j}}{k}\right|\leq C\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,i,j=1,\dots,N.

On account of (
2.14
), we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}D_{i}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\frac{x_{j}}{k}D_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}(x,u,\nabla u)D_{j}u\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)D_{\xi}\mathcal{L}(x,u,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u\,dx\\
&\quad-\int_{\Omega}\left[D\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\cdot\frac{x}{k}\mathcal{L}(x,u,\nabla u)+N\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\mathcal{L}(x,u,\nabla u)\right]\,dx\\
&\quad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)x\cdot\nabla u\right)g\,dx.\end{split}

In view of (
2.15
),
\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\rightarrow 1
and
D\varphi(\frac{x}{k})\cdot\frac{x}{k}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, and
|\nabla u|^{p}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it follows from the dominated convergence theorem that

(2.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}D_{i}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\frac{x_{j}}{k}D_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{L}(x,u,\nabla u)D_{j}u\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)D_{\xi}\mathcal{L}(x,u,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u\,dx\\
&\quad-\int_{\Omega}\left[D\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\cdot\frac{x}{k}\mathcal{L}(x,u,\nabla u)+N\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\mathcal{L}(x,u,\nabla u)\right]\,dx\\
&\quad\rightarrow\left(1-\frac{N}{p}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx,\end{split}

as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
Now, we note that an integration by parts and the dominated convergence theorem imply that

(2.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)x\cdot\nabla u\right)|u|^{p-2}u\,dx&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)x\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{|u|^{p}}{p}\right)\,dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[N\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)+\nabla\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\cdot\frac{x}{k}\right]\frac{|u|^{p}}{p}\,dx\rightarrow\frac{N}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\,dx,\end{split}

as
k\rightarrow\infty
. On the other hand, an integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)x\cdot\nabla u\right)(I_{\alpha}*F(u))f(u)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(F\circ u)(y)I_{\alpha}(x-y)\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)x\cdot\nabla(F\circ u)(x)\,dxdy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}I_{\alpha}(x-y)\left((F\circ u)(y)\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)x\cdot\nabla(F\circ u)(x)+(F\circ u)(x)\varphi\left(\frac{y}{k}\right)y\cdot\nabla(F\circ u)(y)\right)\,dxdy

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u(y))I_{\alpha}(x-y)\left[N\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)+x\cdot\nabla\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\right]F(u(x))\,dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{N-\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u(y))I_{\alpha}(x-y)\frac{(x-y)\cdot(x\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)-y\varphi\left(\frac{y}{k}\right))}{|x-y|^{2}}F(u(x))\,dxdy,

and applying the dominated convergence theorem we see that

(2.19)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\varphi\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)x\cdot\nabla u\right)(I_{\alpha}*F(u))f(u)\,dx\rightarrow-\frac{N+\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*F(u))F(u)\,dx,

as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Combining (
2.16
), (
2.17
), (
2.18
) and (
2.19
), we obtain (
1.4
).
The proof of Theorem
1.2
is now completed.

Abstract
We start by studying the subgroup structures underlying stabilizer circuits and we use our results to propose a new normal form for stabilizer circuits. This normal form is computed by induction using simple conjugation rules in the Clifford group. It has shape CX-CZ-P-H-CZ-P-H, where CX (resp. CZ) denotes a layer of 𝙲𝙽𝙾𝚃 \mathtt{CNOT} (resp. 𝙲𝚉 \mathtt{CZ} ) gates, P a layer of phase gates and H a layer of Hadamard gates. Then we consider a normal form for stabilizer states and we show how to reduce the two-qubit gate count in circuits implementing graph states. Finally we carry out a few numerical tests on classical and quantum computers in order to show the practical utility of our methods. All the algorithms described in the paper are implemented in the C language as a Linux command available on GitHub.



1
Introduction

In Quantum Computation, any unitary operation can be approximated to arbitrary accuracy using
\mathtt{CNOT}
gates together with Hadamard, Phase, and
\pi/8
gates (see Figure
2
for a definition of these gates and
[
11
, Section 4.5.3]
for a proof of this result). Therefore, this set of gates is often called the standard set of universal gates. When we restrict this set to Hadamard, Phase and
\mathtt{CNOT}
gates, we obtain the set of Clifford gates. The Pauli group
\mathcal{E}_{n}
is the group generated by the Pauli gates acting on
n
qubits (see Figure
1
) and the normalizer of the Pauli group in the unitary group
\mathcal{U}_{2^{n}}
is called the Clifford group.
In his PhD thesis
[
7
, Section 5.8]
, Gottesman gave a constructive proof of the fact that any element of the Clifford group can be expressed, up to a global phase factor, as a product of Clifford gates. He also introduced the stabilizer formalism
[
11
, Section 10.5.1]
, which turned out to be is a very efficient tool to analyze quantum error-correction codes
[
7
]
and, more generally, to describe unitary dynamics
[
11
, Section 10.5.2]
. Indeed, the Gottesman-Knill theorem asserts that a stabilizer circuit (
i.e.
a quantum circuit consisting only of Clifford gates) can be simulated efficiently on a classical computer (see
[
11
, Section 10.5.4]
and
[
7
, p. 52]
).

In the context of quantum stabilizer circuits, the usual denomination
normal form
or
canonical form
just means that any stabilizer circuit is equivalent to a circuit written in this form and that this equivalent circuit is composed of a bounded number of Clifford gates. Generally this equivalent circuit has the shape of a layered decomposition, each layer consisting in a subcircuit composed of a unique type of quantum gate (
e.g.
only
\mathtt{CNOT}
gates, only phase gates). Of course, one tries to find the shortest and simplest decomposition. For simplicity and consistency with the previous works on this topic, we continue using the habitual expression
normal form
, although a more meaningful term would be probably better suited.
Due to the importance of the Clifford gates in many fields of Quantum Computation, several normal forms for stabilizer circuits were proposed over the last two decades, with the aim of reducing the gate count in this type of circuits. Indeed, in the experimental quantum computers, the noise in the gate as well as the decoherence time are currently the main causes of their unreliability and it is therefore imperative to minimize the number of gates in quantum circuits.
The first normal form proposed by Aaronson and Gottesman
[
1
]
was successively improved by Maslov and Roetteler
[
10
]
, Bravyi and Maslov
[
4
]
and
Duncan
et al.

[
6
]
. These authors used decomposition methods in the symplectic group over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
in dimension
2n

[
1
,
10
,
4
]
or
ZX-calculus
[
6
]
in order to compute normal forms. In this paper we provide a new normal form for stabilizer circuits. This form is similar to the most recent ones
[
4
,
6
]
but it is slightly simpler and we compute it through an original induction process based on conjugation rules in the Clifford group.

Our result is applied to the case of stabilizer states and graph states : we propose a normal form for stabilizer states as well as a new proof of a result due to Van den Nest
et al.
that asserts the local Clifford equivalence of stabilizer states and graph states
[
13
, theorem 1]
.
Graph states form an important class of stabilizer states that plays a central role in Quantum Information Theory. They are of great use in many fields such as Quantum Computing based on measurements, Quantum Error Correction, or the study of multipartite entanglement (see the numerous references given in the rich introduction of
[
9
]
).
We show that it is possible to reduce the two-qubit gate count in a circuit implementing a graph state by using an algorithm proposed in 2004 by Patel
et al.

[
12
]
together with some conjugation rules in the Clifford group.

This article is structured as follows. Section
2
is a background section on quantum circuits and Clifford gates that will guide the non-specialist reader through the rest of the paper. In Section
3
, we investigate some remarkable subgroups of the Clifford Group and deduce thereby a first normal form for a particular case of stabilizer circuits. In Section
4
, we generalize this form to any stabilizer circuits.
Finally, in Section
5
, we apply this normal form to
stabilizer states and we propose an original implementation of graph states. We also provide a few simple statistics to evaluate the practical utility of our method
and we consider the case of an implementation of graph states in the publicly available IBM quantum computers.

the transvection matrix
\mathbf{I}\oplus\mathbf{E}_{ij}
. The general linear group
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
is equal to
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, the special linear group on
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, and is consequently generated by the matrices
[ij]
. The following simple property of the matrices
[ij]
will be frequently used in the rest of the article.

Proposition 1
.

Multiplying to the left (resp. the right) any matrix
M
by a
transvection matrix
[ij]
is equivalent to add the row
j
(resp. column
i
) to the row
i
(resp. column
j
) in
M
.

Applying Proposition
1
to the column vector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
corresponding to the binary label
x
of the basis vector
\ket{x}
, we can rewrite Relation (
9
) in a cleaner way as

X_{ij}\ket{x}=\ket{[ij]\mathbf{x}}.

(21)

The above considerations lead quite naturally to the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

The group
{\left\langle\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle}_{n}
generated by the
\mathtt{CNOT}
gates acting on
n
qubits is isomorphic to
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
.
The morphism
\Phi
sending each gate
X_{ij}
to the transvection matrix
[ij]
is an explicit isomorphism.
The order of
{\left\langle\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle}_{n}
is
2^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(2^{i}-1)
.

Proof.

As the matrices
[ij]
generate
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, it is clear that
\Phi
is surjective. Since Identity (
21
) holds, a preimage
U
under
\Phi
of any matrix
\mathbf{A}
in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
must satisfy the relations
U\ket{x}=\ket{\mathbf{Ax}}
for any basis vector
\ket{x}
. As these relations define a unique matrix
U
,
\Phi
is injective. The order of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
is classically obtained by counting the number of basis of the vector space
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.
∎

For any
\mathbf{A}
in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, let
X_{\mathbf{A}}=\Phi^{-1}(\mathbf{A})
, where
\Phi
is the morphism defined in Theorem
2
. The unitary operator
X_{\mathbf{A}}
thus corresponds to any circuit composed of the
\mathtt{CNOT}
gates
X_{i_{1}j_{1}}\dots X_{i_{\ell}j_{\ell}}
such that
\mathbf{A}=\prod_{k=1}^{\ell}[i_{k}j_{k}]
and the gate
X_{ij}
can also be denoted by
X_{[ij]}
. Pay attention to the fact that
X_{\mathbf{A}}
denotes a product of
\mathtt{CNOT}
gates while
X_{\mathbf{u}}
denotes the product of Pauli-
\mathtt{X}
gates defined by the vector
\mathbf{u}
. As
([ij][jk])^{2}=[ik]
, a straightforward consequence of the isomorphism between
{\left\langle\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle}_{n}
and
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
is the following conjugation rule between the
\mathtt{CNOT}
gates.

X_{[ij]}X_{[jk]}X_{[ij]}=X_{[ik]}X_{[jk]}=X_{[jk]}X_{[ik]}\quad(i,j,k\text{ distinct})

(22)

3.2
The PZX form for quantum circuits of phase,
\mathtt{CZ}
and
\mathtt{CNOT}
gates

Let
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ}\right\rangle_{n}
be the group generated by the set
\{P_{i},Z_{ij}\mid 0\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1\}
.
Any element of the group generated by the
P_{i}
gates can be written uniquely in the form
Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}
where
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.
This group is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n},+)
, one possible isomorphism associating
Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}
to
2\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{b}
.
As the generators of the group
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ}\right\rangle_{n}
commute between each other, the group
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ}\right\rangle_{n}
is isomorphic to the direct product
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}
. Any element in
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ}\right\rangle_{n}
can be written uniquely in the form

Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}
and

Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}Z_{\mathbf{v^{\prime}}}P_{\mathbf{b^{\prime}}}Z_{\mathbf{B^{\prime}}}=Z_{\mathbf{v}\oplus\mathbf{v^{\prime}}\oplus\mathbf{b}\odot\mathbf{b^{\prime}}}P_{\mathbf{b}\oplus\mathbf{b^{\prime}}}Z_{\mathbf{B}\oplus\mathbf{B^{\prime}}}.

(23)

The conjugation by the
X_{[ij]}
gates in
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ}\right\rangle_{n}
obey to the seven rules below.
Each equality can be proved by checking, thanks to Identities (
7
) to (
11
), that the actions of its left hand side and its right hand side on any basis vector
\ket{x}
are the same.

\displaystyle X_{[ij]}Z_{\{i,j\}}X_{[ij]}=Z_{\{i,j\}}Z_{j}

(24)

\displaystyle X_{[ij]}Z_{\{i,k\}}X_{[ij]}=Z_{\{i,k\}}Z_{\{j,k\}}\quad(i,j,k\ \text{distinct})

(25)

\displaystyle X_{[ij]}Z_{\{p,q\}}X_{[ij]}=Z_{\{p,q\}}\quad(p,q\neq i)

(26)

\displaystyle X_{[ij]}Z_{i}X_{[ij]}=Z_{i}Z_{j}

(27)

\displaystyle X_{[ij]}Z_{j}X_{[ij]}=Z_{j}

(28)

\displaystyle X_{[ij]}P_{i}X_{[ij]}=P_{i}P_{j}Z_{\{i,j\}}

(29)

\displaystyle X_{[ij]}P_{j}X_{[ij]}=P_{j}

(30)

Let us denote by
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ},\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle_{n}
the group generated by the set
\{P_{i},Z_{ij},X_{ij}\mid 0\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1\}
. As described in the following proposition, we can extend relations (
24
) to (
30
) to the unitary matrices
Z_{\mathbf{v}},P_{\mathbf{b}}
and
Z_{\mathbf{B}}
.

Proposition 3
.

The group
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ}\right\rangle_{n}
is a normal subgroup of
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ},\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle_{n}
. The conjugation of any element of
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ}\right\rangle_{n}
by a
\mathtt{CNOT}
gate is described by the relations

\displaystyle X_{[ij]}Z_{\mathbf{v}}X_{[ij]}=Z_{[ji]\mathbf{v}}\ ,

(31)

\displaystyle X_{[ij]}P_{\mathbf{b}}X_{[ij]}=Z_{b_{i}b_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}}P_{[ji]\mathbf{b}}Z_{\{i,j\}}^{b_{i}}=Z_{b_{i}b_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}}P_{[ji]\mathbf{b}}Z_{b_{i}\{\{i,j\}\}}\ ,

(32)

\displaystyle X_{[ij]}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{[ij]}=Z_{b_{ij}\mathbf{e}_{j}}Z_{[ji]\mathbf{B}[ij]}\ ,

(33)

Proof.

Identities (
31
) and (
32
) are direct consequences of the conjugation relations (
27
), (
28
), (
29
), (
30
) and Proposition (
1
) applied to the vectors
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{b}
.
Let us prove Identity (
33
).
Let
\mathbf{B}_{i}=\{\{p,q\}\in\mathbf{B}\mid i\in\{p,q\}\}
,
\mathbf{B}_{i}^{c}=\mathbf{B}_{i}\oplus\mathbf{B}
and
\mathbf{B}_{i}^{\prime}=\mathbf{B}_{i}\oplus b_{ij}\{\{i,j\}\}
, then
\mathbf{B}=b_{ij}\{\{i,j\}\}\oplus\mathbf{B}_{i}^{\prime}\oplus\mathbf{B}_{i}^{c}
.
On one hand,
[ji]\mathbf{B}[ij]=b_{ij}[ji]\{\{i,j\}\}[ij]\oplus[ji]\mathbf{B}_{i}^{\prime}[ij]\oplus[ji]\mathbf{B}_{i}^{c}[ij]
.
We check that
[ji]\{\{i,j\}\}[ij]=\{\{i,j\}\}
,

[ji]\{\{i,k\}\}[ij]=\{\{i,k\},\{j,k\}\}
when
k\neq j
and
[ji]\{\{p,q\}\}[ij]=\{\{p,q\}\}
when
p,q\neq i

(recall that
\{\{p,q\}\}
denotes the matrix in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
whose entries are 0 but entries
(p,q)
and
(q,p)
that are 1).
Hence

Z_{[ji]\mathbf{B}[ij]}=Z_{ij}^{b_{ij}}Z_{\mathbf{B}_{i}^{c}}\prod_{k\in\Lambda_{i}}Z_{ik}Z_{jk},

(34)

where
\Lambda_{i}=\{k\mid\{i,k\}\in\mathbf{B}_{i}^{\prime}\}
.
On the other hand,
X_{[ij]}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{[ij]}=X_{[ij]}Z_{ij}^{b_{ij}}Z_{\mathbf{B}_{i}^{\prime}}Z_{\mathbf{B}_{i}^{c}}X_{[ij]}
, so using (
24
), (
25
)
and (
26
), one has

X_{[ij]}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{[ij]}=Z_{ij}^{b_{ij}}Z_{j}^{b_{ij}}Z_{\mathbf{B}_{i}^{c}}\prod_{k\in\Lambda_{i}}Z_{ik}Z_{jk}.

(35)

As
Z_{j}^{b_{ij}}=Z_{b_{ij}\mathbf{e}_{j}}
, we conclude by comparing (
34
) and (
35
).
∎

We can extend Identity
33
to the case of any unitary matrix
X_{\mathbf{A}}
.

Proposition 4
.

For any matrix
\mathbf{B}
in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
and any matrix
\mathbf{A}
in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, one has

X_{\mathbf{A}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}=Z_{q_{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{A}^{-1})}Z_{\mathbf{A}^{-t}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}^{-1}}\ ,

(36)

where
q_{\mathbf{B}}
is the quadratic form defined by
\mathbf{B}
,

q_{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{A})
is a shorthand for the vector
[q_{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{c_{0}}),\dots,q_{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{c_{n-1}})]^{t}
,

\mathbf{c}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{c}_{n-1}
are the columns of matrix
\mathbf{A}

and
\mathbf{A}^{-t}
is a shorthand for
\left(\mathbf{A}^{t}\right)^{-1}
.

Proof.

Since Identities (
33
) and (
31
) hold, it is clear that
X_{\mathbf{A}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}
can be written in the form

Z_{\mathbf{v}}Z_{\mathbf{A}^{-t}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}^{-1}}
for some
\mathbf{v}
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. So we have to prove that
\mathbf{v}=q_{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{A}^{-1})
. We start from
Z_{\mathbf{v}}=X_{\mathbf{A}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}Z_{\mathbf{B^{\prime}}}
, where

\mathbf{B^{\prime}}=\mathbf{A}^{-t}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}^{-1}
. Let
\ket{\psi}=Z_{\mathbf{v}}\ket{\mathbf{e_{i}}}
, then
\ket{\psi}=(-1)^{v_{i}}\ket{\mathbf{e_{i}}}
.
On the other hand,
\ket{\psi}=X_{\mathbf{A}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}\ket{\mathbf{e_{i}}}
since
q_{\mathbf{B^{\prime}}}(\mathbf{e_{i}})=0
for any
\mathbf{B^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
. Besides,
X_{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}\ket{\mathbf{e_{i}}}=\ket{\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{e_{i}}}=\ket{\mathbf{c_{i}}}
where
\mathbf{c_{i}}
is the column
i
of
\mathbf{A}^{-1}
, hence

\ket{\psi}=X_{\mathbf{A}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}\ket{\mathbf{c}_{i}}=(-1)^{q_{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{c}_{i})}X_{\mathbf{A}}\ket{\mathbf{c}_{i}}=(-1)^{q_{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{c}_{i})}\ket{\mathbf{e}_{i}}
.
Finally we see that
v_{i}=q_{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{c}_{i})
, thus
\mathbf{v}=q_{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{A}^{-1})
.
∎

From Identity (
15
), the gate
S_{ij}
is in
{\left\langle\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle}_{n}
and is therefore a gate of type
X_{\mathbf{A}}
. Let
(ij)
be the permutation matrix of

associated to the transposition
\tau
of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
that swaps
i
and

, then
(ij)=[ij][ji][ij]=[ji][ij][ji]
, hence

S_{ij}=X_{[ij]}X_{[ji]}X_{[ij]}=X_{[ij][ji][ij]}=X_{(ij)}
.
The group generated by the
X_{(ij)}
gates is a subgroup of
{\left\langle\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle}_{n}
that is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and we denote by
X_{\mathbf{\sigma}}
the unitary matrix associated to the permutation matrix
\mathbf{\sigma}
in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
. The conjugation by

is given by
X_{\sigma}Z_{\{p,q\}}X_{\sigma}=Z_{\{\sigma(p),\sigma(q)\}}
and, in particular, one has
X_{(ij)}Z_{\{p,q\}}X_{(ij)}=Z_{\{\tau(p),\tau(q)\}}
(see
[
3
]
for further development on
\mathtt{CZ}
and
\mathtt{SWAP}
gates). As a consequence of Propositions
3
and

, the followings identities hold :

\displaystyle X_{(ij)}Z_{\mathbf{v}}X_{(ij)}=Z_{(ij)\mathbf{v}}\ ,

(37)

\displaystyle X_{(ij)}P_{\mathbf{b}}X_{(ij)}=P_{(ij)\mathbf{b}}\ ,

(38)

\displaystyle X_{(ij)}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{(ij)}=Z_{(ij)\mathbf{B}(ij)}\ ,

(39)

\displaystyle X_{\sigma}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\sigma}^{-1}=Z_{\sigma\mathbf{B}\sigma^{-1}}\text{\ (for any permutation matrix }\sigma).

(40)

Proposition
3
provides straightforwardly an algorithm to write in normal form any quantum circuit
C
composed of

\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ}
and
\mathtt{CNOT}
gates. This normal form is called the PZX form (Theorem

\mathtt{C\text{-}to\text{-}PZX}

\mathtt{ALGORITHM}
: Compute the PZX form for a stabilizer circuit of
\mathtt{P}
,
\mathtt{CZ}
and
\mathtt{CNOT}
gates.

\mathtt{INPUT}:(C,F_{\text{in}})
, where

C is a circuit given as a matrix product
C=\prod_{k=1}^{\ell}M_{k}
, of
\ell
quantum gates
in the set
\{P_{i},Z_{\{i,j\}},X_{[ij]}\mid 0\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1\}
,

F_{\text{in}}
is a circuit which is already in PZX form .

\mathtt{OUTPUT}:F_{\text{out}}
is a circuit equivalent to the product
CF_{\mathrm{in}}
,

written in PZX form
Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}
.

\mathtt{1}\quad\
/*
initialisation of the form

F_{\text{out}}
*/

\mathtt{2}\quad\ F_{\text{out}}\leftarrow F_{\text{in}};

\mathtt{3}\quad\ \mathtt{for}\ k=\ell\ \mathtt{to}\ 1\ \mathtt{do}

\mathtt{4}\quad\quad\
/*
Case a :
M_{k}
is a
\mathtt{CZ}
gate
*/

\mathtt{5}\quad\quad\ \mathtt{if}\ M_{k}=Z_{\{i,j\}}\ \mathtt{then}

\mathtt{6}\quad\quad\quad\ \mathbf{B}\leftarrow\mathbf{B}\oplus\{\{i,j\}\}\ ;

\mathtt{7}\quad\quad\
/*
Case b :
M_{k}
is a
\mathtt{P}
gate
*/

\mathtt{8}\quad\quad\ \mathtt{else\ if\ }M_{k}=P_{i}\ \mathtt{then}

\mathtt{9}\quad\quad\quad\ \mathbf{v}\leftarrow\mathbf{v}\oplus b_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}\ ;\ \mathbf{b}\leftarrow\mathbf{b}\oplus\mathbf{e}_{i}\ ;

\mathtt{10}\quad\quad
/*
Case c :
M_{k}
is a
\mathtt{CNOT}
gate
*/

\mathtt{11}\quad\quad\mathtt{else\ }\

\mathtt{12}\quad\quad\quad\mathbf{v}\leftarrow[ji]\mathbf{v}\oplus b_{i}b_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}\oplus b_{ij}\mathbf{e}_{j}\ ;

\mathtt{13}\quad\quad\quad\mathbf{B}\leftarrow[ji]\mathbf{B}[ij]\oplus b_{i}\{\{i,j\}\}\ ;

\mathtt{14}\quad\quad\quad\mathbf{b}\leftarrow[ji]\mathbf{b}\ ;\ \mathbf{A}\leftarrow[ij]\mathbf{A}\ ;

\mathtt{15}\quad\mathtt{return}\ F_{\text{out}};

Figure 4:
Algorithm
\mathtt{C\text{-}to\text{-}PZX}
: the time complexity of this algorithm is only
O(n\ell)
since we use row and column additions instead of matrix multiplication in Case c (thanks to Proposition
1
). At the end of the algorithm, the matrix
\mathbf{A}
is the product of all the transvections corresponding to the
\mathtt{CNOT}
gates that appear in the input circuit
C
, in the same order.

Theorem 5

(
The PZX form for a quantum circuit of
\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ}
and
\mathtt{CNOT}
gates
)
.

Any element of
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ},\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle_{n}
admits a unique decomposition in the form

Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}},

(41)

where
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
\mathbf{B}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
,
\mathbf{A}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
.

The group
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ},\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle_{n}
is the semidirect product of its normal subgroup
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ}\right\rangle_{n}
with
{\left\langle\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle}_{n}
, i.e.
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ},\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle_{n}=\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ}\right\rangle_{n}\rtimes{\left\langle\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle}_{n}
.
The order of
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ},\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle_{n}
is therefore
2^{n(n+1)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(2^{i}-1)
.

Proof.

The existence of the decomposition can be proved by using the
\mathtt{C\text{-}to\text{-}PZX}
algorithm described in Figure
4
:
let
\ell\geqslant 0
be an integer and
C=\prod_{k=1}^{\ell}M_{k}
be an element of
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ},\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle_{n}
, where
M_{k}
is a unitary in the gate set
\{P_{i},Z_{ij},X_{[ij]}\mid 0\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1\}
. Then the form
Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}
for
C
is the result of
Algorithm
\mathtt{C\text{-}to\text{-}PZX}
applied to
C
and
F_{\mathrm{in}}=I
,
that is :
Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}=\mathtt{C\text{-}to\text{-}PZX}(C,I)
.
Let us prove, by contradiction, the unicity of this decomposition.
Suppose that
Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}=Z_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}P_{\mathbf{b}^{\prime}}Z_{\mathbf{B}^{\prime}}X_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}
.
If
\mathbf{A}\neq\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
, there exists
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
such that
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}
. But this leads to a contradiction
because
Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}\ket{\mathbf{x}}=Z_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}P_{\mathbf{b}^{\prime}}Z_{\mathbf{B}^{\prime}}X_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\ket{\mathbf{x}}
, so
Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}\ket{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}}=Z_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}P_{\mathbf{b}^{\prime}}Z_{\mathbf{B}^{\prime}}\ket{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}}
, hence
\ket{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}}
and
\ket{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}}
are two different basis vector that are collinear, which is impossible. So
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
and
Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}=Z_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}P_{\mathbf{b}^{\prime}}Z_{\mathbf{B}^{\prime}}
.
If
\mathbf{b}\neq\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
, we can suppose, without loss of generality, that there exists
i
such that
b_{i}=1
and
b^{\prime}_{i}=0
. Then
P_{\mathbf{b}}\ket{\mathbf{e}_{i}}=\mathrm{i}\ket{\mathbf{e}_{i}}
and
P_{\mathbf{b}^{\prime}}\ket{\mathbf{e}_{i}}=\ket{\mathbf{e}_{i}}
, so
\mathrm{i}Z_{\mathbf{v}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}\ket{\mathbf{e}_{i}}=Z_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}Z_{\mathbf{B}^{\prime}}\ket{\mathbf{e}_{i}}
, hence
\mathrm{i}\ket{\mathbf{e}_{i}}=\pm\ket{\mathbf{e}_{i}}
, which is not possible. Thus
\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
. Finally, if
Z_{\mathbf{v}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}=Z_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}Z_{\mathbf{B}^{\prime}}
, we show that
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}^{\prime}
by comparing their action on
\ket{\mathbf{e}_{i}}
and
\ket{\mathbf{e}_{i}\oplus\mathbf{e}_{j}}
for any
i,j
.

Since
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ}\right\rangle_{n}
is a normal subgroup of
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ},\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle_{n}
, the semidirect product structure is a consequence of the existence and uniqueness of the decomposition. The order of
\left\langle\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ},\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle_{n}
is computed using Theorem
2
.
∎

3.3
Toolbox of conjugation rules

The
\mathtt{C\text{-}to\text{-}PZX}
algorithm computes a normal form for particular stabilizer circuits, consisting only of
\mathtt{P},\mathtt{CZ}
and
\mathtt{CNOT}
gates.
In the next section, we use the
\mathtt{C\text{-}to\text{-}PZX}
algorithm as a subroutine called by the main algorithm that computes a normal form for any stabilizer circuit.
In order to describe this algorithm, we need some more conjugation rules.
Let
\Omega=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}H_{i}
, let
U^{\Omega}=\Omega U\Omega^{-1}=\Omega U\Omega
for any
U\in\mathcal{U}_{2^{n}}
. We use
U^{-\Omega}
as a shorthand notation for
(U^{\Omega})^{-1}
. The following identities hold.

\displaystyle X_{\mathbf{A}}^{\Omega}=X_{\mathbf{A}^{-t}}

(42)

\displaystyle P_{i}^{\Omega}P_{i}P_{i}^{-\Omega}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{4}}H_{i}X_{i}

(43)

\displaystyle P_{i}^{\Omega}Z_{\{i,k\}}P_{i}^{-\Omega}=Z_{\{i,k\}}X_{[ik]}P_{k}

(44)

\displaystyle Z_{\{i,j\}}^{\Omega}P_{j}Z_{\{i,j\}}^{-\Omega}=P_{i}^{\Omega}X_{[ij]}P_{j}

(45)

\displaystyle Z_{\{i,j\}}^{\Omega}Z_{\{i,j\}}Z_{\{i,j\}}^{-\Omega}=Z_{\{i,j\}}Z_{\{i,j\}}^{\Omega}Z_{\{i,j\}}=H_{i}H_{j}X_{(ij)}=X_{(ij)}H_{i}H_{j}

(46)

\displaystyle Z_{\{i,j\}}^{\Omega}Z_{\{i,k\}}Z_{\{i,j\}}^{-\Omega}=X_{[jk]}Z_{\{i,k\}}=Z_{\{i,k\}}X_{[jk]}\quad(i,j,k\text{ distinct})

(47)

To write a stabilizer circuit in normal form, we also need the conjugation rules of a Pauli product of type
X_{\mathbf{u}}Z_{\mathbf{v}}

by the gates
P_{i}
,
X_{[ij]}
,
Z_{\{i,j\}}
and
\Omega
.

\displaystyle P_{i}X_{\mathbf{u}}Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{i}^{-1}=\mathrm{i}^{u_{i}}X_{\mathbf{u}}Z_{\mathbf{v}\oplus u_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}}

(48)

\displaystyle X_{[ij]}X_{\mathbf{u}}Z_{\mathbf{v}}X_{[ij]}=X_{[ij]\mathbf{u}}Z_{[ji]\mathbf{v}}

(49)

\displaystyle Z_{\{i,j\}}X_{\mathbf{u}}Z_{\mathbf{v}}Z_{\{i,j\}}=(-1)^{u_{i}u_{j}}X_{\mathbf{u}}Z_{\mathbf{v}\oplus u_{j}\mathbf{e}_{i}\oplus u_{i}\mathbf{e}_{j}}=(-1)^{u_{i}u_{j}}X_{\mathbf{u}}Z_{\mathbf{v}\oplus\{\{i,j\}\}\mathbf{u}}

(50)

\displaystyle\Omega X_{\mathbf{u}}Z_{\mathbf{v}}\Omega=X_{\mathbf{v}}Z_{\mathbf{u}}

(51)

The proofs of Identities (
42
) to (
51
) are short and simple. They are based on the different conjugation rules already seen in

4
The generalized PZX form for stabilizer circuits

In this section we provide an algorithm to compute a normal form for stabilizer circuits.
This form is a generalization of the PZX form (
Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}
) introduced in the previous section (
41
).

Definition 6
.

A unitary matrix
C
corresponding to a circuit of Clifford gates is in GenPZX form when it is written in the form

C=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}H_{\mathbf{r}}Z_{\mathbf{u}}P_{\mathbf{d}}Z_{\mathbf{D}}H_{\mathbf{s}}Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}},

(55)

where
\mathbf{r},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{d},\mathbf{s},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{b}
are vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
\mathbf{D}
and
\mathbf{B}
are matrices in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
,
\mathbf{A}
is an invertible matrix in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, and
\varphi\in\{k\frac{\pi}{4},k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.

We remark that, unlike the PZX form, the GenPZX form is not unique. Indeed, from Identity
46
, one has

H_{i}H_{j}Z_{\{i,j\}}H_{i}H_{j}Z_{\{i,j\}}=Z_{\{i,j\}}H_{i}H_{j}X_{(ij)}
.

4.1

We introduce a form for stabilizer circuits that we use as an intermediary technical step to compute the GenPZX form and we prove three lemmas concerning this intermediate form.

4.2
Computing the generalized PZX form

We show that a GenPZX form of a stabilizer circuit
C
can be obtained in polynomial time by applying an algorithm summarized in Figure
5
. This algorithm is called the
\mathtt{C\text{-}to\text{-}GenPZX}
algorithm.

Theorem 11

(
The GenPZX form for stabilizer circuits
)
.

Any
n
-qubit stabilizer circuit
C
given as a product of
\ell
Clifford gates, i.e.
C=\prod_{k=1}^{\ell}M_{k}
, where

M_{k}\in\{P_{i},H_{i},X_{[ij]}\mid 0\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1\}
, can be written in polynomial time
O(\ell n^{2})
in the form

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}H_{\mathbf{r}}Z_{\mathbf{u}}P_{\mathbf{d}}Z_{\mathbf{D}}H_{\mathbf{s}}Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}\ ,

(62)

where
\mathbf{r},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{d},\mathbf{s},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{b}
are vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
\mathbf{D}
and
\mathbf{B}
are matrices in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
,
\mathbf{A}
is an invertible matrix in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, and
\varphi\in\{k\frac{\pi}{4},k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.

Proof.

The computation of the GenPZX form is divided into three steps A, B, C. We describe each step and evaluate the number of operations needed to perform the step.

Step A
: We prove by induction on the length
\ell
of the input circuit that any stabilizer circuit
C=\prod_{k=1}^{\ell}M_{k}
, where

M_{k}\in\{P_{i},H_{i},X_{[ij]}\mid 0\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1\}
, can be written in the intermediate form
H_{\mathbf{a}}P_{\mathbf{d}}Z_{\mathbf{D}}\Omega\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}X_{\mathbf{u}}Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}
. The base case of the induction is clear : if
\ell=0
, then
C=I=H_{\mathbf{a}}\Omega
, where
\mathbf{a}
is the vector of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with all entries equal to 1.
To perform an induction step, we must prove that, for any
M\in\{P_{i},H_{i},X_{[ij]}\mid 0\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1\}
and any Clifford circuit
C
in intermediate form, the product
MC
can be written in intermediate form. Clearly, the induction step is achieved by using Lemmas
8
,
9
and
10
.

Taking in account the different cases in the proofs of these three Lemmas, it appears that the algorithmic cost of merging a Clifford gate into the intermediate form is bounded by the cost of the
\mathtt{C\text{-}to\text{-}PZX}
algorithm.
The complexity of the
\mathtt{C\text{-}to\text{-}PZX}
algorithm is
O(\ell^{\prime}n)
, where
\ell^{\prime}
is the number of gate in the input circuit (see Figure
4
). At each induction step, we apply this algorithm to subcircuits composed of
O(n)
gates, so the cost of using
\mathtt{C\text{-}to\text{-}PZX}
is
O(n^{2})
operations for each step.
Starting from a stabilizer circuit
C
of length
\ell
, one needs
\ell
induction steps to write
C
in intermediate form, so we see that the number of operations needed in Step A is
O(\ell n^{2})
.

Step B
: We write
C
in the form
C=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}H_{\mathbf{a}}P_{\mathbf{d}}Z_{\mathbf{D}}\cdot X_{\mathbf{u}}^{\Omega}\cdot\Omega Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}

and we use Identity (
51
) as well as the commutativity of the unitaries
P_{\mathbf{d}}
,
Z_{\mathbf{D}}
and

Z_{\mathbf{u}}
to obtain
C=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}H_{\mathbf{a}}Z_{\mathbf{u}}P_{\mathbf{d}}Z_{\mathbf{D}}\Omega Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}
.
The cost of this step is
O(1)
(we neglect operations such as initializing or copying which depend on the implementation of the
\mathtt{C\text{-}to\text{-}GenPZX}
algorithm).

Step C
: The unitaries
H_{\mathbf{r}}
and
H_{\mathbf{s}}
appear after a straightforward simplification of the Hadamard gates. One defines the vectors
\mathbf{r}
and
\mathbf{s}
as follows. Let
\Gamma
be the set of qubits involved in the subcircuit
Z_{\mathbf{u}}P_{\mathbf{d}}Z_{\mathbf{D}}
,
i.e

\Gamma=\{i\mid u_{i}=1\}\cup\{i\mid d_{i}=1\}\cup\{i\mid\exists j,d_{ij}=1\}
. If
a_{i}=1
and
i\notin\Gamma
then
r_{i}=s_{i}=0
,
otherwise
r_{i}=a_{i}
and
s_{i}=1
.
After this last step,

Remark 13
.

The global phase

during the computation process of the normal form, because knowing its exact value is, at least, of mathematical interest (for instance the group

4.3
Discussion

We discuss some questions related to the implementation of the GenPZX form as a quantum circuit and we compare the GenPZX form to other recent normal forms for stabilizer circuits.

In order to implement the unitary
X_{\mathbf{A}}
of the GenPZX form as a
\mathtt{CNOT}
subcircuit, we need to write the matrix
\mathbf{A}
as a product of transvections. To this end, one can apply an algorithm proposed in 2004 by Patel
et al.

[
12
]
. This algorithm is superior to the classical Gaussian elimination as it allows a decomposition in
O(n^{2}/\log n)
transvections
[
12
, Theorem 1]
whereas the number of transvections in the decomposition obtained by the Gauss-Jordan algorithm is bounded by
n^{2}

[
2
, Proposition 10]
. In the rest of this paper, we refer to the Patel
et al.
’s algorithm with parameter
m
equal to
\lceil\log_{2}(n)/2\rceil
as the
\mathtt{A\text{-}to\text{-}\mathtt{CNOT}}
algorithm (see
[
12
]
for the definition of
m
). It is important to remark that the
\mathtt{A\text{-}to\text{-}\mathtt{CNOT}}
algorithm does not return, in general, an optimized decomposition in transvections of the matrix
\mathbf{A}
.
Of course, it is possible to optimize a
\mathtt{CNOT}
circuit by a brute force algorithm but the cost is exponential and the algorithm can be used in practice only for small values of
n
. The method is as follows. First build
the Cayley graph of the group
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
by Breadth-first search, then find in this
graph the matrix
\mathbf{A}
corresponding to that
\mathtt{CNOT}
circuit. We implemented this algorithm in the C language and the
source file
cnot_opt.c
is available at

\mathtt{https://github.com/marcbataille/cnot\text{-}circuits/tree/master/optimization}
. Run on a basic laptop, the program allows to optimize in a few seconds any
\mathtt{CNOT}
circuit up to 5 qubits.

Note that the unitary operators
Z_{\mathbf{u}}P_{\mathbf{d}}
and
Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}
in the GenPZX form can be implemented as subcircuits of phase gates since
\mathtt{Z}=\mathtt{P}^{2}
. So the unitary operator described by the GenPZX form can be implemented as a quantum circuit of type

CX-CZ-P-H-CZ-P-H,

(63)

where
CX
(resp.
CZ
) is a subcircuit of
\mathtt{CNOT}
(resp.
\mathtt{CZ}
) gates,

P
(resp.
H
) is a subcircuit of Phase (resp. Hadamard) gates

\ket{\psi}

presented to its left but the associated operator acts to the left of
\ket{\psi}

is inverted comparing to the GenPZX form

.
The GenPZX form has some similarities with the normal forms proposed in 2020 by Duncan

[

(H-P-CZ-CX-H-CZ-P-H) or

[

(X-Z-P-CX-CZ-H-CZ-H-P). These two forms have, like the form

The Clifford Group (defined as the normalizer of the Pauli Group in the unitary group
\mathcal{U}_{2^{n}}
) is infinite. However the group generated by the gate set
\{H_{i},P_{i},X_{[ij]}\mid 0\leqslant i,j\leqslant n-1\}
is a finite subgroup of the Clifford Group and its order is

8\times 2^{2n}|\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})|=2^{n^{2}+2n+3}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(4^{j}-1)

, where
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
is the symplectic group over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
in dimension
2n
(see
e.g.

[
5
]
).
In
[
10
]
, the authors consider that the number of Boolean degrees of
freedom in that group is
2n^{2}+O(n)
, since its order is
2^{2n^{2}+O(n)}
.
They deduce thereby that a normal form for stabilizer circuits
must have at least
2n^{2}+O(n)
degrees of freedom. As a
\mathtt{CNOT}
layer adds
n^{2}
degrees of freedom, a
\mathtt{CZ}
layer adds
n(n-1)/2
degree of freedom
and the single qubit layers add a linear amount of degree of freedom (this is a direct consequence of the group orders of
{\left\langle\mathtt{CZ}\right\rangle}_{n}
and
{\left\langle\mathtt{CNOT}\right\rangle}_{n}
, see
[
10
, Section I]
and
[
6
, Section 6]
), it is easy to see that all three normal forms mentioned in this discussion have
2n^{2}+O(n)
degrees of freedom and are therefore asymptotically optimal in the sense defined in
[
10
]
.

5
Implementing stabilizer states and graph states

5.1
The connection between stabilizer states and graph states

A stabilizer state
\ket{S}
for a
n
-qubit register can be written in the form

\ket{S}=C\ket{0}^{\otimes n},

(64)

where
C
is a product of Clifford gates
[
1
, Theorem 1]
. A graph state
\ket{G}
is a special case of a stabilizer state that can be written in the form

\ket{G}=Z_{\mathbf{B}}\ket{+}^{\otimes n}=Z_{\mathbf{B}}\Omega\ket{0}^{\otimes n},

(65)

where
\ket{+}=\mathtt{H}\ket{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{0}+\ket{1})
is the eigenvector corresponding to the eigenvalue 1 of the Pauli-
\mathtt{X}
gate,
Z_{\mathbf{B}}
is a product of
\mathtt{CZ}
gates defined by a matrix
\mathbf{B}
in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
and
\Omega=\mathtt{H}^{\otimes n}
[
9
]
. The graph
G
associated to the graph state
\ket{G}
is the graph of order
n
whose vertices are labeled by the
n
qubits and whose set of edges is
\{\{i,j\}\mid b_{ij}=1\}
.

Let
\ket{S}=C\ket{0}^{\otimes n}
be a stabilizer state. Applying the
\mathtt{C\text{-}to\text{-}GenPZX}
algorithm up to Step B to the stabilizer circuit
C
yields

C=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}H_{\mathbf{a}}Z_{\mathbf{u}}P_{\mathbf{d}}Z_{\mathbf{D}}\Omega Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}
. Since the unitary
Z_{\mathbf{v}}P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}
has no effect on the ket
\ket{0}^{\otimes n}
, one has, neglecting the global phase
\varphi
:
\ket{S}=H_{\mathbf{a}}Z_{\mathbf{u}}P_{\mathbf{d}}Z_{\mathbf{D}}\Omega\ket{0}^{\otimes n}
. Hence

\ket{S}=H_{\mathbf{a}}Z_{\mathbf{u}}P_{\mathbf{d}}\ket{G}
,
where
\ket{G}
is the graph state
Z_{\mathbf{D}}\Omega\ket{0}^{\otimes n}
. So, using the
\mathtt{C\text{-}to\text{-}GenPZX}
algorithm, we obtain a new proof of a theorem from Van den Nest
et al.

[
13
, theorem 1]
that asserts the equivalence under local Clifford operations of any stabilizer state
\ket{S}
to a graph state
\ket{G}
: there exists a stabilizer circuit
C^{\prime}
consisting only of local Clifford gates (
i.e.
phase and Hadamard gates) and a graph state
\ket{G}
such that
\ket{S}=C^{\prime}\ket{G}
. Moreover, the
\mathtt{C\text{-}to\text{-}GenPZX}
algorithm provides a possible construction of the circuit
C^{\prime}
and the graph
G
.

Theorem 16

(
Normal form of a stabilizer state
)
.

For any stabilizer state
\ket{S}
, there exists a graph state
\ket{G}
and 3 vectors
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{d}
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
such that

\ket{S}=H_{\mathbf{a}}Z_{\mathbf{u}}P_{\mathbf{d}}\ket{G}.

(66)

Because of Theorem
16
, implementing a stabilizer state is equivalent to implementing a graph state, up to a circuit of local Clifford gates.
So, in the rest of this section, we focus on the implementation of a graph state as a circuit in a quantum machine.

5.2
Reducing the two-qubit gate count of a graph state

We address the following question : what kind of pretreatment can be done in the classical circuit of
\mathtt{CZ}
and Hadamard gates that implements a graph state
\ket{G}=Z_{\mathbf{B}}\ket{+}^{\otimes n}
in order to reduce the two-qubit gate count ? We propose an implementation based on the gate set
\{\mathtt{H},\mathtt{Z},\mathtt{CZ},\mathtt{CNOT}\}
obtained thanks to the use of Identity (
36
) (
X_{\mathbf{A}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}X_{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}=Z_{q_{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{A}^{-1})}Z_{\mathbf{A}^{-t}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}^{-1}})
together with the
\mathtt{A\text{-}to\text{-}\mathtt{CNOT}}
algorithm. The main idea is as follows. The two-qubit gate count in a
\mathtt{CZ}
circuit is at most
n(n-1)/2
gates, while the
\mathtt{A\text{-}to\text{-}\mathtt{CNOT}}
algorithm allows an implementation of
X_{\mathbf{A}}
in
O(n^{2}/\log n)

\mathtt{CNOT}
gates.
Hence, if we find an equivalent circuit to the
\mathtt{CZ}
circuit corresponding to the unitary
Z_{\mathbf{B}}
, in which the gate count is dominated by the
\mathtt{CNOT}
gates, we can expect a possible reduction of the initial circuit.

Definition 17
.

We say that a matrix
\mathbf{B}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}

\mathbf{B}
contains at most one non-zero entry, i.e.
Z_{\mathbf{B}}
corresponds to a

Lemma 18

For any

\mathbf{B}_{\text{red}}=\mathbf{A}^{t}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}

The matrix

\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}

is just the classical change of basis formula, where
\mathbf{A}
is the matrix of the new basis. A possible construction of
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}_{\text{red}}
is given by the algorithm
\mathtt{B\text{-}to\text{-}B_{\mathrm{red}}}

and
\mathbf{B}_{\text{red}}

5.4
Implementation of graph states in the IBM quantum computers

We deal with the case of a concrete implementation of graph states in a real-life quantum machine. Our intention is to show the practical usefulness that can have a pretreatment of the circuit based on Theorem
19
, in terms of reduction of the
native
gate count in the
compiled
circuit.
We implemented in the publicly available 5-qubit ibmq_belem device
(
https://quantum-computing.ibm.com/
) the complete graph state

\ket{K_{5}}=Z_{01}Z_{02}Z_{03}Z_{04}Z_{12}Z_{13}Z_{14}Z_{23}Z_{24}Z_{34}\ket{+}^{\otimes 5}.

(68)

This graph-state is of particular interest because it is LC (Local Clifford) equivalent, and thus SLOCC equivalent, to the entangled state

\ket{\mathtt{GHZ}}_{5}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{00000}+\ket{11111})
(see
[
8
]
for the first introduction of the
\ket{\mathtt{GHZ}}
state and
[
9
, Section 4.1]
for a proof of the equivalence). To write
\ket{K_{5}}
in the form (
67
), we simply use our command
./stabnf
in manual mode and obtain :

\ket{K_{5}}=Z_{2}Z_{3}X_{34}X_{23}X_{12}X_{02}X_{24}X_{23}Z_{01}Z_{23}\ket{+}^{\otimes 5}.

(69)

Observe that the form
69
contains only 8 two-qubit gates comparing to the 10
\mathtt{CZ}
gates of the form
68
, which is a substantial reduction of 20%.
But what about the reduction if we consider the circuit, consisting exclusively of native gates, that is actually implemented in the quantum computer ? Is it still significant ? In the IBM quantum devices, the
\mathtt{CZ}
gate
is not native and is simulated thanks to Identity (
14
). The Hadamard gate is implemented from the
R_{z}(\pi/2)
and
\sqrt{\mathtt{X}}
gates, since

\mathtt{H}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{4}}R_{z}(\pi/2)\sqrt{\mathtt{X}}R_{z}(\pi/2)
, where
\sqrt{\mathtt{X}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}1+\mathrm{i}&1-\mathrm{i}\\
1-\mathrm{i}&1+\mathrm{i}\end{bmatrix}
and

R_{z}(\theta)=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{\theta}{2}}&0\\
0&\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\theta}{2}}\end{bmatrix}
. Moreover full connectivity is not achieved and the direct connections allowed between two qubits are given by a graph. The graph of the 5-qubit ibmq_belem device is
\{\{1,0\},\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{3,4\}\}
.
So, to implement a
\mathtt{CNOT}
gate between qubits without direct connection (
e.g.
qubits 2 and 3), it is necessary to simulate it from the native
\mathtt{CNOT}
gates using methods we described in a previous work
[
2

. Due to its similarities to the compilation process in classical computing, the rewriting process that transforms an input circuit with measurements into a native gate circuit giving statistically the same measurement results, is called

6
Conclusion and future work

Gottesman proved in his PhD thesis that any unitary matrix in the Clifford group is uniquely defined, up to a global phase, by its action by conjugation on the Pauli gates
X_{i}
and
Z_{i}

[
7
, pp.41,42]
. This central statement of Gottesman stabilizer formalism can be used to compute normal forms for
n
-qubit stabilizer circuits via the symplectic group over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
in dimension
2n
(
e.g.

[
1
,
10
]
).
In this paper we showed that it is possible to compute normal forms in polynomial time without using this formalism. We proposed a new method based on induction and on simple conjugation rules in the Clifford group.
The reader who is used to work with the symplectic group will notice that our induction process can also be applied inside this group, giving rise to a decomposition of type
\mathbf{M}_{\sigma}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{D}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{I}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{B}&\mathbf{I}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{A}^{-t}\end{bmatrix}
for the symplectic matrix associated to the GenPZX form,
where
\mathbf{B}
(resp.
\mathbf{D}
) is a symmetric matrix corresponding to
P_{\mathbf{b}}Z_{\mathbf{B}}
(resp.
P_{\mathbf{d}}Z_{\mathbf{D}}
),
\mathbf{A}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
is the invertible matrix corresponding to the
\mathtt{CNOT}
sub-circuit
X_{\mathbf{A}}
, and
\mathbf{M}_{\sigma}
is a permutation matrix in dimension
2n
corresponding to a circuit of Hadamard gates.

In the NISQ era (Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum), noise in quantum gates strongly limits the reliability of quantum circuits and is currently a major technical concern. Developing optimization algorithms and heuristics to reduce the gate count in circuits is one of the solutions to improve reliability. In this article, we proposed algorithms to reduce circuits implementing an important class of quantum states, namely the graph states, which are local Clifford equivalent to stabilizer states.
We realised a few experimental tests on quantum computers that highlight the utility of a pretreatment based on our algorithms to reduce the gate count in the compiled circuit implementing a graph state. We believe that the field of quantum circuits compilation is yet in its infancy but it will play an increasing significant role due to the current quick development of experimental quantum computers. We will continue to investigate reduction techniques related to the compilation of quantum circuits in future works.

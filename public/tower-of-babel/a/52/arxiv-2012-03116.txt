Vortex lattices and the Bogoliubov-de Gennes Equations
Ilias (Li) Chenn and I. M. Sigal

Abstract
. We consider the Bogoliubov-de Gennes equations giving an equivalent formulation of the BCS theory of superconductivity. We are interested in static solutions with the magnetic field present. We carefully formulate the equations in the basis independent form, discuss their general features and isolate key physical classes of solutions (normal and vortex lattice states) which are the candidates for the ground state. We prove existence of the normal and vortex lattice states and stability of the normal states for large temperature or magnetic fields and their instability for small temperature and small magnetic fields. Mathematics Subject Classification (MSC): 81Q80, 81V70, 81V74, 35Q40, 35Q56, 35Q82

The Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) equations describe the remarkable quantum phenomenon of superconductivity.

They present an equivalent formulation of the BCS theory of superconductivity and are among the latest additions to the family of important effective equations of mathematical physics. Together with the Hartree, Hartree-Fock (-Bogoliubov), Ginzburg-Landau, Gross-Pitaevskii and Landau-Lifshitz equations, they are the quantum members of this illustrious family consisting of such luminaries as the heat, Euler, Navier-Stokes, Boltzmann and Vlasov equations.

The BdG equations describe the evolution of superconductors on nanoscopic and macroscopic scales. There are still many fundamental questions about these equations which are completely open, namely

•

Derivation;

•

Well-posedness;

•

Existence and stability of stationary magnetic solutions.

In this paper we address the third problem.

The key special solutions of the Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) equations are normal, superconducting and mixed or intermediate states. The superconducting (or Meissner) states assume, by the definition, that the magnetic field is zero, while mixed ones, to have non-vanishing magnetic fields. For type II superconductors, according to experiments, the latter are (magnetic) vortex lattices. In this paper, we prove the existence of the normal states for non-vanishing magnetic fields and partial results on their stability and the existence of the vortex lattices.

There is a considerable physics literature devoted to the BdG equations, but despite the role played by magnetic phenomena in superconductivity it deals mainly with the zero magnetic field case, with only few disjoint remarks about the case when the magnetic fields are present, the main subject of this work.

1.2.
Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) equations

In the Bogoliubov-de Gennes approach states of superconductors are described by the pair of bounded operators
\gamma
and
\alpha
, acting on the one-particle complex Hilbert space,
\mathfrak{h}
, with a complex conjugation (an anti-linear involution), and satisfying

(1.1)

0\leq\gamma=\gamma^{*}\leq 1,\ \quad\alpha^{*}=\overline{\alpha}\ \quad{\rm\ and\ }\ \quad\alpha\alpha^{*}\leq\gamma(1-\gamma)

where
\overline{\gamma}:=\mathcal{C}\gamma\mathcal{C}
, with
\mathcal{C}
, the operation of complex conjugation (see Appendix
E
for the origin of these operators).
\gamma
is a one-particle density operator and
\alpha
is a two-particle coherence operator, or diagonal and off-diagonal correlations.
\gamma(x,x)
is interpreted as the one-particle density.

The one-particle space
\mathfrak{h}
is determined by the many-body quantum problem. For zero density (or ‘finite’) systems, it is
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and for positive density ones,
L^{2}(\Omega)
, where
\Omega
is an arbitrary fundamental cell in a lattice
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with magnetic field dependent (twisted) boundary conditions (see Subsection
1.6
for more details). To fix ideas, we take
\mathfrak{h}
to be the latter, specifically,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}:=

\displaystyle\{f\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):u_{s}^{\mathcal{L}}f=f\text{ for all }s\in\mathcal{L}\},

where
u_{s}^{\mathcal{L}}
are the magnetic translations given in (
1.26
) below, with the scalar product given by
L^{2}(\Omega)
for an arbitrary fundamental cell
\Omega
of
\mathcal{L}
. Furthermore, we understand
\int
without specifying the domain of integration as taken over
\Omega
.

The BdG equations form a system of coupled, nonlinear equations for
\gamma
and
\alpha
. It is convenient to organize the operators
\gamma
and
\alpha
into the self-adjoint operator-matrix

\displaystyle\eta:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\gamma&\alpha\\
\alpha^{*}&\mathbf{1}-\bar{\gamma}\end{array}\right).

The definition of
\gamma
and
\alpha
in terms of the many-body theory (see (
E.2
) of Appendix
E
) implies that

\displaystyle 0\leq\eta=\eta^{*}\leq 1\ {\rm\ and\ }\ J^{*}\eta J=\mathbf{1}-\bar{\eta},\ J:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\mathbf{1}\\
-\mathbf{1}&0\end{array}\right).

These relations imply relations (
1.1
).

Since the BdG equations describe the phenomenon of superconductivity, they are naturally coupled to the electromagnetic field. We describe the latter by the vector potential
a
in the
gauge in which the electrostatic potential is zero
so that the equations have a slightly simpler form). In what follows, for an operator
A
, we denote by
A(x,y)
its integral kernel. Then the time-dependent BdG equations state (see e.g.
[

(1.6)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\eta=[\Lambda(\eta,a),\eta],

(1.7)

\displaystyle\Lambda(\eta,a)=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}h_{\gamma a}&v^{\sharp}\alpha\\
v^{\sharp}\bar{\alpha}&-\overline{h_{\gamma a}}\end{smallmatrix}\big),\ \quad h_{\gamma a}=-\Delta_{a}+v^{*}\gamma-v^{\sharp}\,\gamma\,,

where, for the pair potential
v(x,y)
, the operator
v^{\sharp}
(acting on operators on
\mathfrak{h}
) is defined through the integral kernels as
(v^{\sharp}\,\alpha)\,(x;y):=v(x,y)\alpha(x;y)
and

with

.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}a

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{curl}^{*}\operatorname{curl}a+j(\eta,a),

where

is the superconducting current. Here

is the anti-commutator of

and
B

[A,B]_{+}:=AB+BA
. (To see that (

.) In what follows, we assume that

(1.9)

\displaystyle v(x;y)=v(x-y)\ \text{ and }\ v(-x)=v(x),

so that

. We specify below additional assumptions on

1.8.
Results

Let the reflection operator
\tau^{\rm refl}
be given by conjugation by the reflections,
u^{\rm refl}:f(x)\rightarrow f(-x)
. We say that a state
(\eta,a)
is
even (or reflection symmetric)

(1.43)

\displaystyle\tau^{\rm refl}\gamma=\gamma,\ \tau^{\rm refl}\alpha=\alpha\ \text{ and }\ u^{\rm refl}a=-a.

The reflection symmetry of the BdG equations implies that if an initial condition is even then so is the solution at every moment of time.

In what follows, we use the notation

and

.

Stability/instability of normal solutions.

We address the question of the energetic stability of the mt-invariant states. To this end we define, in the standard way, the Hessian of the free energy in
\eta
as

(1.47)

\displaystyle F_{T}^{\prime\prime}(\eta_{*},a_{*}):=d_{\eta}\operatorname{grad}_{\eta}F_{T}(\eta_{*},a_{*}),

where
d_{\eta}
is the Gâteaux derivative w.r.to
\eta
and
\operatorname{grad}_{\eta}
is the gradient w.r.to
\eta
, defined by the equation

\mathrm{Tr}(\operatorname{grad}_{\eta}F(\eta_{*},a_{*})\eta^{\prime})=d_{\eta}F(\eta_{*},a_{*})\eta^{\prime}.

We consider

along physically relevant perturbations of the form
\eta^{\prime}=\phi(\alpha)
, where
\phi(\alpha)
is the off-diagonal operator-matrix, defined by

\displaystyle\phi(\alpha):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\alpha\\
\alpha^{*}&0\end{array}\right),

with
\alpha
a Hilbert-Schmidt operator on
\mathfrak{h}
.
Moreover, we require that perturbations
\eta^{\prime}=\phi(\alpha)
satisfy the condition

(1.50)

\displaystyle\alpha\alpha^{*}\lesssim[\gamma_{Tb}(1-\gamma_{Tb})]^{2}

(which is equivalent to (
2.11
) at
\eta=\eta_{Tb}
, c.f. also Lemma
5.4
(1)).

For an operator
h
, let

and

stand for the operators acting on other operators by multiplication from the left by
h
and from right by
\bar{h}
, respectively, and recall
v^{\sharp}
is defined after (
1.6
). We have the following

Proposition 1.5
.

For off-diagonal perturbations
\eta^{\prime}=\phi(\alpha)
,
F_{T}^{\prime\prime}(\eta_{Tb},a_{b})\phi(\alpha)=\phi(L_{Tb}\alpha)
, where the operator
L_{Tb}
is given by

(1.51)

\displaystyle L_{Tb}:=K_{Tb}+v^{\sharp},

(1.52)

\displaystyle K_{Tb}:=\frac{h_{Tb}^{L}+h_{Tb}^{R}}{\tanh(h_{Tb}^{L}/T)+\tanh(h_{Tb}^{R}/T)},

on the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators on
\mathfrak{h}
. Here
h_{Tb}:=h_{\gamma_{Tb}a_{b}\mu}
(with
h_{\gamma a\mu}:=h_{\gamma a}-\mu
, where
h_{\gamma a}
is given in (
1.7
)).

Let
\langle\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\rangle:=\mathrm{Tr}(\alpha^{*}\alpha^{\prime})
. We say that
\eta^{\prime}
is an
off-diagonal perturbation
if and only if
\eta^{\prime}=\phi(\alpha)
with
\alpha
a Hilbert-Schmidt operator satisfying (
1.50
). The next result generalizes that of
[
19
]
for
a=0
:

Proposition 1.6
.

For off-diagonal perturbations
\eta^{\prime}=\phi(\alpha)
, we have

(1.53)

\displaystyle F_{T}(\eta_{Tb}+\epsilon\eta^{\prime},a_{b})=

\displaystyle F_{T}(\eta_{Tb},a_{b})+\epsilon^{2}\langle\alpha,L_{Tb}\alpha\rangle+O(\epsilon^{3}).

Definition (
1.25
) of
E(\eta,a)
implies that
E(\eta_{Tb}+\epsilon\eta^{\prime},a_{b})=E(\eta_{Tb},a_{b})+\epsilon^{2}\mathrm{Tr}(\bar{\alpha}v^{\sharp}\alpha)
for
\eta^{\prime}=\phi(\alpha)
. This together with Corollary
A.3
and (

) of Appendix

dealing with the entropy yields Propositions
1.5
and
1.6
, respectively.

(The absence of the linear term is due to the fact that

is a critical point of the energy function.)

The next two propositions are proven in Section
4
.

Proposition 1.7
.

L_{Tb}\geq\frac{1}{2}T-\|v\|_{\infty}
and consequently, for
T
sufficiently large,
L_{Tb}>0
and the mt-invariant state
(\eta_{T,b},a_{b})
is energetically stable under off-diagonal perturbations.

On the other hand, for

T
and

sufficiently small, we have

Proposition 1.8
.

Drop the exchange term
-v^{\sharp}\gamma
.
Suppose that

in a fundamental cell
\Omega
centred at the origin. Then, for

and

acting on the set of Hilbert-Schmidt operators on

Note that

Let

We

At

A proof of energetic stability under general perturbations for either

In conclusion of this paragraph, we mention the proposition which follows from a simple computation:

The operator

2.
Stationary Bogoliubov-de Gennes equations

In this section we establish the connection between the time-dependent and stationary BdG equations and show that the latter are the Euler-Lagrange equations for the free energy, (

).

In terms of
\eta
, the gauge transformation,

, could be written as

The invariance under the gauge transformations follows from the relation

\Lambda(T_{\chi}^{\rm gauge}(\eta,a))=T_{\chi}^{\rm gauge}(\Lambda(\eta,a)),

We consider stationary solutions to (

\displaystyle\eta_{t}:=T^{\rm gauge}_{\chi}\eta=U_{\chi}^{\rm gauge}\eta(U_{\chi}^{\rm gauge})^{-1},

The operator-family (

[\Lambda_{\eta a},\eta]=0,

Proof.

We write

and use that

, where

S:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right),

and therefore
\partial_{t}(U_{\chi}\eta U_{\chi}^{-1})=i\dot{\chi}[S,U_{\chi}\eta U_{\chi}^{-1}]=i\dot{\chi}U_{\chi}[S,\eta]U_{\chi}^{-1}

2.3

1.6
), and using (

), the previous relation and that

(2.5)

\displaystyle-\dot{\chi}U_{\chi}[S,\eta]U_{\chi}^{-1}=

\displaystyle[\Lambda(U_{\chi}\eta U_{\chi}^{-1},a),U_{\chi}\eta U_{\chi}^{-1}]

(2.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\Lambda(U_{\chi}\eta U_{\chi}^{-1},a+\nabla\chi),U_{\chi}\eta U_{\chi}^{-1}]

(2.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{\chi}[\Lambda(\eta,a),\eta]U_{\chi}^{-1}.

Since

, it follows then that

[\Lambda(\eta,a)-\mu S,\eta]=0.
Since

is equal to (

), the last equation is exactly (

) which gives the statement of the proposition.
∎

In general, a many-body evolution can be defined on states (i.e. positive linear (‘expectation’) functionals) on the CAR or Weyl CCR algebra
{\mathfrak{W}}

To fix ideas we concentrate on spin

. Let

(E.1)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\omega_{t}(A)=\omega_{t}([A,H])\,,\ \forall A\in\mathfrak{W},

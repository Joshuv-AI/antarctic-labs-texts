Many-body Stark resonances by the complex absorbing potential method
Kentaro Kameoka

Abstract
The resonances of many-body Stark Hamiltonians are characterized by the complex absorbing potential method. Namely, the resonances are shown to be the limit points of complex discrete eigenvalues of many-body Stark Hamiltonians with quadratic complex potential when the coefficient of the complex potential tends to zero.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we discuss the complex absorbing potential method for resonances of
the following many-body Stark Hamiltonians

P=\sum_{j}(-\frac{1}{2m_{j}}\Delta_{j}+V_{j}(x_{j})+q_{j}x_{j}^{1})+\sum_{j<k}V_{jk}(x_{j}-x_{k})

on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
.
In this paper, the indices
j,k
run through
1
to
N
unless otherwise stated.
Here
d
and
N
are positive integers
and
m_{j}
and
q_{j}
are positive real numbers.
These are fixed in this paper.
We assume
\min_{j}q_{j}=1
for simplicity.
The coordinate is written as
x_{j}=(x_{j}^{1},x_{j}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and
\Delta_{j}
is the
Laplacian with respect to
x_{j}
. We also set
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}
.
The potentials
V_{j}
and
V_{jk}
are real-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which satisfy the following assumption.

Assumption A
.

There exist
R_{0}>0
,
\delta_{0}>0
and
c_{0}>0
such that the following hold.
(i) For any
1\leq j\leq N
,

V_{j}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
has an analytic continuation to the region

\{y\in\mathbb{C}^{d}|\,|\mathrm{Re}\,y|>R_{0},|\mathrm{Im}\,y|<\delta_{0}\}
and
\lim_{|y|\to\infty}\partial V_{j}(y)=0
there.
(ii) For any
1\leq j<k\leq N
,

V_{jk}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
is bounded and has an analytic continuation to the region

\{y\in\mathbb{C}^{d}|\,|\mathrm{Im}\,y|<\min\{\delta_{0},c_{0}|\mathrm{Re}\,y|\}\}
. Moreover,

\partial V_{jk}
is bounded and
\lim_{|y|\to\infty}\partial V_{jk}(y)=0
in that region.

Under this assumption,
P
is essentially self-adjoint on
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
by the Faris-Lavine theorem.
Moreover, we can define the resonances of
P
as follows (see Section 2 for details).
We set
R_{+}(z)=(z-P)^{-1}
for
\mathrm{Im}\,z>0
.
Then for any non-zero
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
, the cutoff resolvent
\chi_{1}R_{+}(z)\chi_{2}
has
a meromorphic continuation from the upper half plane to the region
\{z\in\mathbb{C}|\,\mathrm{Im}\,z>-\delta_{0}\}
.
We define the multiplicity
m_{z}
of resonance by

m_{z}=\mathrm{rank}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{z}\chi_{1}R_{+}(z)\chi_{2}dz,

which is independent of the choice of
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
. We call
z
a resonance of
P
if
m_{z}>0
and
the set of resonances is denoted by
\mathrm{Res}(P)
.

We set

P_{\varepsilon}=P-i\varepsilon x^{2}

for
\varepsilon\geq 0
, where
x^{2}=\sum_{j}x_{j}^{2}
.
Then
P_{\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon>0
with
D(P_{\varepsilon})=D(-\Delta)\cap D(x^{2})

has purely discrete spectrum.
Then our main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1
.

Under Assumption
A
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to+0}\sigma_{d}(P_{\varepsilon})=\mathrm{Res}(P)

in
\{z\in\mathbb{C}|\,\mathrm{Im}\,z>-\delta_{0}\}
including multiplicities.

More precisely, for any
z
with
\mathrm{Im}\,z>-\delta_{0}
,
there exists
\gamma_{0}>0
such that
for any
0<\gamma<\gamma_{0}
there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0

such that for any
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, the number of eigenvalues of
P_{\varepsilon}
in

\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}|\,|\zeta-z|\leq\gamma\}
counted with their algebraic multiplicities
coincides with
m_{z}
.

Remark 1.1
.

Our proof of Theorem
1
preserves the symmetry with respect to permutations of particles
if
V_{j}
,
V_{jk}
,
m_{j}
and
q_{j}
are independent of indices.
Thus, for instance, our result holds true for fermions with spins.

Complex distortion

In this section, we discuss the complex distortion outside a cone for many-body Stark Hamiltonians,
which was introduced in
[
4

in the one-body case.
We take
\kappa>1
,
\rho>1

C(\kappa,\rho)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}|\,|y^{\prime}|\leq\kappa(y_{1}+\rho)\}

Take a convex set
\widetilde{C}(\kappa,\rho)

\widetilde{C}(\kappa,\rho)

\widetilde{C}(\kappa,\rho)=C(\kappa,\rho)

We also take
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\phi_{\tau}(y)=\tau^{-d}\phi(y/\tau)

F=-(1+\kappa^{-2})^{1/2}\mathrm{dist}\bigl(\bullet,\widetilde{C}(\kappa,\rho)\bigr)\ast\phi_{\tau}

We also set
v(y)=(v_{1}(y),\dots,v_{d}(y))=\partial F(y)\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})

Note that the derivatives of

We next set

The many-body case is defined as
\Phi_{N,\theta}(x)=(\Phi_{\theta}(x_{1}),\dots,\Phi_{\theta}(x_{N}))

We also write
\Phi_{N,\theta}(x)=x_{\theta}=(x_{1,\theta},\dots,x_{N,\theta})

We set
U_{\theta}f(x)=\left(\mathrm{det}\Phi_{N,\theta}^{\prime}(x)\right)^{1/2}f(\Phi_{N,\theta}(x))

which is unitary on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})

We define the distorted operator
P_{\theta}=U_{\theta}PU_{\theta}^{-1}

Remark 2.1
.

The notation

Then we have

P_{\theta}=\sum_{j}P_{j,\theta}+\sum_{j<k}V_{jk}(x_{j,\theta}-x_{k,\theta}),

\displaystyle P_{j,\theta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2m_{j}}\partial_{j}\Phi_{\theta}^{\prime}(x_{j})^{-2}\hskip 2.84544pt{}^{t}\!\partial_{j}+r_{\theta}(x_{j})+q_{j}x_{j}^{1}+q_{j}\theta v_{1}(x_{j})+V_{j}(x_{j,\theta}).

\Phi_{\theta}^{\prime}

For

\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi_{\theta}^{\prime}(y)^{-2}\leq 0

\mathrm{Re}\,\Phi_{\theta}^{\prime}(y)^{-2}\geq 0

We have

everywhere and

The function

).

Remark 2.2
.

Obviously, it is enough to assume that
V_{j}
is analytic outside

\kappa>1

\rho>1

We wrote a stronger assumption for simplicity in Section 1.

We interpret
P_{\theta}
as the closure of the operator
P_{\theta}
defined on

We take
\tau>1

We also take
\kappa>1

\rho>1

We first prove the basic operator-theoretic properties of
P_{\theta}
using the theory of semigroup.

Proposition 2.3
.

The operator
P_{\theta}
is an analytic family of closed operators in the sense of Kato
for
\theta
with
|\mathrm{Im}\,\theta|<\delta_{0}(1+\kappa^{-2})^{-1/2}

|\mathrm{Re}\,\theta|
small
if
\kappa>1
,
\rho>1
and
\tau>1
in the definition of
P_{\theta}

We also have
P_{\theta}^{*}=P_{\bar{\theta}}

Proof.

This proposition was proved for the one-body case in
[
4
, Section 2]
.
Although we assumed that
V_{j}
is decaying in
[
4
, Section 2]
, the same argument applies
since
V_{j}(x_{j,\theta})-V_{j}(x_{j,\theta^{\prime}})
is bounded (recall that
\partial V_{j}
is bounded).
Since
\mathrm{Im}\,(u,(P_{j,\theta}-iC-i\mu)u)\leq-\mu\|u\|^{2}
for some
C>0
and any
\mu>0
with the
similar estimate for the adjoint,
we can consider the semigroup
e^{-itP_{j,\theta}}
,
t\geq 0
, by the Hille-Yosida theorem.
Then we can consider the semigroup
e^{-itP_{1,\theta}}\cdots e^{-itP_{N,\theta}}
.
Since this semigroup preserves
D(P_{1,\theta})\otimes\cdots\otimes D(P_{N,\theta})
,
it is a core for the generator
\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j,\theta}
of this semigroup.
Since
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a core for
P_{j,\theta}
, we conclude that
the generator
\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j,\theta}
coincides with the closure of its restriction to
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
.
By the formula
e^{-itP_{j,\theta}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(1+\frac{it}{n}P_{j,\theta})^{-n}
and the
analyticity of
(z-P_{j,\theta})^{-1}
with respect to
\theta
, we see that
e^{-itP_{j,\theta}}
is
analytic with respect to
\theta
.
Thus
(z-\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j,\theta})^{-1}=-i\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{itz}e^{-itP_{1,\theta}}\cdots e^{-itP_{N,\theta}}dt

is analytic with respect to
\theta
for
\mathrm{Im}\,z\gg 1
.
Since the generator of the adjoint semigroup is the adjoint of the original semigroup by the above relation between
the resolvent and semigroup, we see
(\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j,\theta})^{*}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j,\bar{\theta}}
.
We see that
\sum_{j<k}^{N}V_{jk}(x_{j,\theta}-x_{k,\theta})
is an analytic family of bounded operators which is
self-adjoint for real
\theta
under our assumption.
Note that
|v(x_{j})-v(x_{k})|\leq\max\{|v(x_{j})|,|v(x_{k})|\}\leq(1+\kappa^{-2})^{1/2}
since two vectors

v(x_{j})
and
v(x_{k})
have almost the same directions for
\kappa\gg 1
.
Thus
|\mathrm{Im}\,(x_{j,\theta}-x_{k,\theta})|<\delta_{0}
for our
\theta
.
Thus we see that
P_{\theta}^{*}=P_{\bar{\theta}}
.
The analyticity of
P_{\theta}
with respect to
\theta
follows from that of

\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j,\theta}
and the Kato’s definition
of the analyticity (see
[
7

) or the resolvent equation.

We next prove the discreteness of
\sigma(P_{\theta})
and the fact that
\mathrm{Res}(P)=\sigma(P_{\theta})

using Proposition

in the next section.

Proposition 2.4
.

For any
0<\delta_{1}<\delta<\delta_{0}
,
the operator
P_{\theta}
with
\theta=-i\delta
has purely discrete spectrum in

\{z|\,\mathrm{Im}\,z>-\delta_{1}\}
if
\kappa>1
,
\rho>1
and
\tau>1
are large enough.
Resonances of
P
coincide with discrete eigenvalues of
P_{\theta}
including multiplicities there.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
, there exists a bounded operator
K
such that

(P_{\theta}-z-iK)^{-1}
exists and
K(P_{\theta}-z-iK)^{-1}
is compact.
Thus
P_{\theta}-z=(1+iK(P_{\theta}-z-iK)^{-1})(P_{\theta}-z-iK)
is a Fredholm operator with index zero.
Since
-\mathrm{Im}\,(u,(P_{\theta}-z)u)\gtrsim\|u\|^{2}
for
\mathrm{Im}\,z\gg 1
, we conclude that
(P_{\theta}-z)^{-1}
is
meromorphic in
\{z|\,\mathrm{Im}\,z>\delta_{1}\}
with finite rank poles by the analytic Fredholm theory.
Thus
P_{\theta}
has purely discrete spectrum there.
Once we established this fact, standard arguments in resonance theory (see for instance
[
4
, Section 2]
) imply that
the cutoff resolvent
\chi_{1}R_{+}(z)\chi_{2}
has a meromorphic continuation to

\{z|\,\mathrm{Im}\,z>-\delta_{1}\}
,
the multiplicity
m_{z}
of resonance

is independent of
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
,
and resonances of
P
coincide with discrete eigenvalues of
P_{\theta}
there including multiplicities.
∎

In the proof of Theorem

The basic properties of

As in the one-body case (see

\Omega\Subset\{z|\,\mathrm{Im}\,z>-\delta_{0}\}

large enough in the definition of the complex distortion.

If

.

We fix

P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z=(1+iK(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK)^{-1})(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK).

By Proposition

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon,z}^{M}=[P_{\varepsilon,\theta},\chi^{M}]

\displaystyle(P_{0,\theta}-z-iK)^{-1}-[P_{\varepsilon,\theta},\chi^{M}](P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK)^{-1}

Remark 3.2
.

In one-body case

We recall the notation

Lemma 4.1

There exists

A^{-1}=\mathcal{O}(M^{-1+k/2})\,\,\,\,\mathrm{in}\,\,\,\mathrm{Op}S(\langle\xi\rangle^{-k})

Proof.

The following arguments are uniform with respect to

We set

We estimate

We first see that

\left|\frac{\langle\xi\rangle^{k}}{a(x,\xi;M)}\right|\lesssim M^{-1+k/2},

(1)

which is seen by estimating it separately
for
|\xi|/M^{1/2}\gg 1
and
|\xi|/M^{1/2}\lesssim 1
.
We used
x_{j}\cdot v(x_{j})\leq 0
(see
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
), which implies
\mathrm{Re}\,(-i\varepsilon x_{j,\theta}^{2})\geq 0
.
The inequality (
1
) implies that

\left|\frac{\partial_{\xi}a(x,\xi;M)}{a(x,\xi;M)}\right|\lesssim\left|\frac{\langle\xi\rangle}{a(x,\xi;M)}\right|\lesssim M^{-1/2}.

(2)

We next claim that

\left|\frac{\partial_{x}a(x,\xi;M)}{a(x,\xi;M)}\right|\lesssim\frac{1+\xi^{2}+\varepsilon x^{2}}{|a(x,\xi;M)|}\lesssim 1,

(3)

which is also seen by estimating it separately
for
|\xi|/M^{1/2}\gg 1
and
|\xi|/M^{1/2}\lesssim 1
.

The inequalities (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
) imply
that
a(x,\xi;M)^{-1}
is
\mathcal{O}(M^{-1+k/2})
in
S(\langle\xi\rangle^{k})
when
M\to\infty
.
This implies that
\partial_{x}a(x,\xi;M)^{-1}
is
\mathcal{O}(M^{-1/2})
in
S(\langle\xi\rangle^{-1})
.
We also see that
\partial_{\xi}a(x,\xi;M)^{-1}
is
\mathcal{O}(M^{-1/2})
in
S((1+\xi^{2}+\varepsilon x^{2})^{-1})
.
This can be seen from

\left|\frac{1+\xi^{2}+\varepsilon x^{2}}{a(x,\xi;M)}\cdot\frac{\langle\xi\rangle}{a(x,\xi;M)}\right|\lesssim M^{-1/2}

and
(\partial_{x}a)/a,(\partial_{\xi}a)/a\lesssim 1
.
Since
\partial_{x}a\in S(1+\xi^{2}+\varepsilon x^{2})
and
\partial_{\xi}a\in S(\langle\xi\rangle)
,
a standard argument for pseudodifferential operators (see
[
15
, Chapter 5]
)
implies that
the symbols of
a(x,D;M)a^{-1}(x,D;M)-1
and
a^{-1}(x,D;M)a(x,D;M)-1
are

\mathcal{O}(M^{-1/2})
in
S(1)
.
Thus the Neumann series argument and the Beals’s theorem complete the proof.
∎

Using this lemma, we prove the proposition in the previous section.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

The following arguments are uniform with respect to
z\in\Omega
and
0\leq\varepsilon\ll 1
,
which we do not write explicitly.
We first prove the existence of
(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})^{-1}
.
For that, we first take a many-body partition of unity.
We set
q=\max_{j}q_{j}
and take
0<c_{1}\ll 1
.
We take
\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{3}\in C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
such that
0\leq\chi_{l}\leq 1

for
l=1,2,3
and
\sum_{l=1}^{3}\chi_{l}=1
with the following properties.

(i) If
\chi_{1}(x)>0
, then

x_{j}\in\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}|y_{1}\leq-c_{1}/4qN\}\cup\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}|y_{1}\leq c_{1},|y^{\prime}|\geq 1/2\}
for some
1\leq j\leq N
.

(ii) If
\chi_{2}(x)>0
, then
x_{j}\in\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}|y_{1}\geq-c_{1}/2qN,|y^{\prime}|\leq 1\}\cup\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}|y_{1}\geq 3c_{1}/4\}

for any
1\leq j\leq N
.
Moreover,
x_{j}\in\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}|y_{1}\geq 3c_{1}/4\}
for some
1\leq j\leq N
.

(iii) If
\chi_{3}(x)>0
, then
x_{j}\in\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}|-c_{1}/2qN\leq y_{1}\leq c_{1},|y^{\prime}|\leq 1\}
for any
1\leq j\leq N
.

Such a partition of unity can be easily constructed by summing products of a one-body partition unity.
We define a rescaled partition of unity by
\chi_{l}^{R}(x)=\chi_{l}(x/R)
.

We next estimate
\|(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})\chi_{l}^{R}u\|
from below for
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
.
We first claim

-\mathrm{Im}\,(\chi_{1}^{R}u,(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})\chi_{1}^{R}u)\gtrsim\|\chi_{1}^{R}u\|^{2}

by our complex distortion.
Recall that three parameters
\kappa>1
,
\rho>1
and
\tau>1
appeared in the definition of the complex distortion.
We note that
|\mathrm{Im}\,V_{jk}(x_{j,\theta}-x_{k,\theta})|
is sufficiently small for

\tau\gg 1
.
In fact, it is small when
|x_{j}-x_{k}|\gg 1
by the decay of
\mathrm{Im}\,V_{jk}
.
It is small when
|x_{j}-x_{k}|\lesssim 1
by the smallness of
|\mathrm{Im}\,(x_{j}-x_{k}+\theta(v(x_{j})-v(x_{k})))|
, which follows
from the smallness of the Lipschitz constant of the vector field
v
.
Thus we can neglect the pair potential terms.
The contribution from
V_{j}(x_{j,\theta})
is also negligible if
\rho>1
is large enough depending on
\tau\gg 1
.
We recall from Section 2 that
r_{\theta}(x_{j})
is also negligible if we take
\tau\gg 1
.
We assumed that
0\leq\varepsilon\ll 1
to estimate the constant term of
-i\varepsilon x_{\theta}^{2}
.

We also have

\mathrm{Re}\,(\chi_{2}^{R}u,(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})\chi_{2}^{R}u)\gtrsim R\|\chi_{2}^{R}u\|^{2}

by the positive divergence of Stark potential
q_{j}x_{j}^{1}
for some
j
in the condition (ii) above
(the contribution from negative divergence of other Stark potentials is smaller).
We also have

-\mathrm{Im}\,(\chi_{3}^{R}u,(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})\chi_{3}^{R}u)\gtrsim R\|\chi_{3}^{R}u\|^{2}

by the
-iK^{R}
term.

We then have

\displaystyle\|u\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l}\|\chi_{l}^{R}u\|\lesssim\sum_{l}\|(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})\chi_{l}^{R}u\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})u\|+\sum_{l}\|[P_{\varepsilon,\theta},\chi_{l}^{R}]u\|.

To estimate the commutator terms,
we set
\chi_{4}^{R}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{\chi}(x_{j}/R),\chi_{5}^{R}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{\chi}(x_{j}/3R)
,
where
\tilde{\chi}
is the same as in the definition of
A
.
Since
\chi_{4}^{R}=1
on
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\partial\chi_{l}^{R}
for
l=1,2,3
, we have

\sum_{l}\|[P_{\varepsilon,\theta},\chi_{l}^{R}]u\|\lesssim R^{-1}\|\chi_{4}^{R}u\|_{H^{1}}.

We now take
k=1
and use Lemma
4.1
.
Here we take
M=3R
in the definition of
A
.
Since
A^{-1}:L^{2}\to H^{1}
is
\mathcal{O}(R^{-1/2})
,

\displaystyle R^{-1}\|\chi_{4}^{R}u\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-3/2}\|A\chi_{4}^{R}u\|

\displaystyle=R^{-3/2}\|(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R}-3iR)\chi_{4}^{R}u\|

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-3/2}\|(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})u\|+R^{-1/2}\|u\|+R^{-3/2}\|[P_{\varepsilon,\theta},\chi_{4}^{R}]u\|.

Here we used
(1-\tilde{\chi}^{3R}(x_{j}))\chi_{4}^{R}(x)=0
(see the definition of
A
).
We then use Lemma
4.1
with
k=2
and
M=7R
in the definition of
A
.
Since
A^{-1}:H^{-1}\to H^{1}
uniformly,
the last commutator term is bounded by

\displaystyle R^{-5/2}\|\chi_{5}^{R}u\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-5/2}\|A\chi_{5}^{R}u\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle=R^{-5/2}\|(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R}-7iR)\chi_{5}^{R}u\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-5/2}\|(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})u\|+R^{-3/2}\|u\|+R^{-5/2}\|[P_{\varepsilon,\theta},\chi_{5}^{R}]u\|_{H^{-1}}.

Here we used
(1-\tilde{\chi}^{7R}(x_{j}))\chi_{5}^{R}(x)=0
.
The last commutator term is bounded by
R^{-7/2}\|u\|
.

Summing up these and taking
R\gg 1
, we conclude that

\|u\|\leq C\|(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})u\|.

By approximation, this is valid for all
u
in the domain of
P_{\varepsilon,\theta}
.
Since we have the same estimate for the adjoint operator, we conclude that

\|(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})^{-1}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C
for
R\gg 1
.
We fix such
R\gg 1
.

We finally prove
\|(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})^{-1}\|_{L^{2}\to H^{k}(\{|x|<M\})}\leq CM^{k/2}
.
By the interpolation, it is enough to prove it for
k=2
since the case where
k=0
follows from the previous estimate.
We may assume that
M\gg 1
.
We take
M\gg 1
and we use Lemma
4.1
with
k=2
, where
M
is replaced with
7M
and

R
is the above fixed one.
We then have

\displaystyle\|(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})^{-1}\|_{L^{2}\to H^{2}(\{|x|<M\})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\chi_{4}^{M}(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})^{-1}\|_{L^{2}\to H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\chi_{4}^{M}A^{-1}\chi_{5}^{M}A(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})^{-1}\|_{L^{2}\to H^{2}}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\|\chi_{4}^{M}A^{-1}(1-\chi_{5}^{M})A(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})^{-1}\|_{L^{2}\to H^{2}}.

Since
\chi_{5}^{M}(x)(1-\tilde{\chi}^{7M}(x_{j}))=0
and
A^{-1}:L^{2}\to H^{2}
uniformly for
M\gg 1
,
the first term is bounded by

\displaystyle\|\chi_{5}^{M}(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R}-7iM)(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})^{-1}\|\lesssim M.

Since
\chi_{4}^{M}(1-\chi_{5}^{M})=0
, the second term is bounded by

\displaystyle\|\chi_{4}^{M}A^{-1}(1-\chi_{5}^{M})A(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})^{-1}\|_{L^{2}\to H^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|A^{-1}[\chi_{4}^{M},A]A^{-1}(1-\chi_{5}^{M})A(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})^{-1}\|_{L^{2}\to H^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|A^{-1}[\chi_{4}^{M},A]A^{-1}[\chi_{5}^{M},A](P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})^{-1}\|_{L^{2}\to H^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|A^{-1}\|_{L^{2}\to H^{2}}\cdot\|[\chi_{4}^{M},A]\|_{H^{1}\to L^{2}}\cdot\|A^{-1}\|_{H^{-1}\to H^{1}}\cdot\|[\chi_{5}^{M},A]\|_{L^{2}\to H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{-2}.

Summing up these, we have
\|(P_{\varepsilon,\theta}-z-iK^{R})^{-1}\|_{L^{2}\to H^{2}(\{|x|<M\})}\leq CM
and
finish the proof of the proposition.

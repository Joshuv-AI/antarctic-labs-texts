Wedge product matrices and orbits of principal congruence subgroups
Yao Ming Chan

Abstract
Following Bump and Hoffstein in [ BH86 ] , the orbits in Γ ∞ ​ ( 3 ) \ Γ ⁡ ( 3 ) \Gamma_{\infty}(3)\backslash\Gamma(3) are in bijection with sets of invariants satisfying certain relations. We explain how wedge product matrices give an alternative definition of the invariants of matrix orbits. This new method provides the possibility of performing similar computations with other congruence subgroups and arbitrary n × n n\times n matrices. Using Steinberg’s refined version of the Bruhat decomposition, we construct an explicit choice of coset representative for each orbit in the orbit space Γ ∞ ​ ( 3 ) \ Γ ⁡ ( 3 ) \Gamma_{\infty}(3)\backslash\Gamma(3) of 3 × 3 3\times 3 matrices over the PID of Eisenstein integers.



Keywords—
Bruhat decomposition, principal congruence subgroup, wedge product matrices

2.1
Definition and properties of

In this section, we define the matrix orbit space
\Gamma_{\infty}(3)\backslash\Gamma(3)

\Gamma_{\infty}(3)

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}
and
\mathbb{Z}[\omega]={\gothfamily o}
be the ring of Eisenstein integers. Define

\begin{matrix}\Gamma(3)&=&\{A\in SL_{3}({\gothfamily o})\;|\;A\equiv I_{3}\text{ mod }3{\gothfamily o}\},\\
\\
\Gamma_{\infty}(3)&=&\Gamma(3)\cap U,\\
\\
\Gamma_{\infty}(3)\backslash\Gamma(3)&=&\{\Gamma_{\infty}(3)\cdot A\;|\;A\in\Gamma(3)\}.\end{matrix}

where

is the

identity matrix, the congruence

mod

is computed entry by entry and

denotes the subgroup of upper triangular matrices in

\Gamma_{\infty}(3)
is the subgroup of upper triangular unipotent matrices in
\Gamma(3)

The question we will focus on is: If

Definition 2.2

Let

Define the

The matrix

-h&e&-b\\

(2.3)

We call

Theorem 2.3

Let

\implies

\impliedby

\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}a_{2}&b_{2}&c_{2}\\

\end{pmatrix}

(2.7)

Lemma 2.8

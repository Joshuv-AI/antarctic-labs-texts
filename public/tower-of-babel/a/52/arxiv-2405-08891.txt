Noncommutative resolution of 𝒮 ​ U C ​ ( 2 ) \mathcal{S}U_{C}(2)
Elias Sink and Jenia Tevelev

Abstract
. We study the derived category of the moduli space 𝒮 ​ U C ​ ( 2 ) \mathcal{S}U_{C}(2) of rank 2 2 vector bundles on a smooth projective curve C C of genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 with trivial determinant. This generalizes the recent work by Tevelev and Torres on the case with fixed odd determinant. Since 𝒮 ​ U C ​ ( 2 ) \mathcal{S}U_{C}(2) is singular, we work with its noncommutative resolution of singularities constructed by Pădurariu and Špenko–Van den Bergh (in the more general setting of symmetric stacks). We show that this noncommutative resolution admits a semiorthogonal decomposition into derived categories of symmetric powers Sym 2 ​ k ​ C \mathrm{Sym}^{2k}C for 2 ​ k ≤ g − 1 2k\leq g-1 . In the case of even genus, each block appears four times. This is also true in the case of odd genus, except that the top symmetric power Sym g − 1 ​ C \mathrm{Sym}^{g-1}C appears twice. In the case of even genus, the noncommutative resolution is strongly crepant in the sense of Kuznetsov and categorifies the intersection cohomology of 𝒮 ​ U C ​ ( 2 ) \mathcal{S}U_{C}(2) . Since all of its components are “geometric,” our semiorthogonal decomposition provides evidence for the expectation, which dates back to the work of Newstead and Tyurin, that 𝒮 ​ U C ​ ( 2 ) \mathcal{S}U_{C}(2) is a rational variety. Finally, we study mutations of semiorthogonal decompositions on the Hecke correspondence, answering a question of Pădurariu and Toda.

Introduction

Let

is a Gorenstein Fano variety of dimension

with rational singularities
[
7
]
. Various resolutions of singularitities of
\mathcal{S}U_{C}(2)
have been studied in
[
31
,
22
,
15
]
, and the relationships between these desingularizations have been worked out in
[

,
5

.
On the other hand, Kuznetsov
[
18
]

defines a
noncommutative resolution of singularities
of a variety

as a smooth triangulated category

with an adjoint pair of functors
f_{*}:\,{\mathcal{D}}\to D^{b}(X)
and

to be proper. In
[

]
*Theorem 1.4, it is shown that every variety

The main result of this paper is the following theorem, which provides an even-degree counterpart
of the main result in
[
33
,
34
]

(the proof of the BGMN conjecture).

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a noncommutative resolution of singularities

of
\mathcal{S}U_{C}(2)
with a semiorthogonal decomposition into blocks equivalent to
D^{b}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2k}C)
for
2k\leq g-1
. There are four copies of each block except when

is odd, in which case the block

appears twice. The category
\mathcal{D}
is an example of the noncommutative resolution of singularities constructed in
[
28
,
32
]
for symmetric stacks. In particular, it is an admissible subcategory of the derived category of the Kirwan resolution of
\mathcal{S}U_{C}(2)
.
If

\mathcal{D}
is a strongly crepant noncommutative resolution of
\mathcal{S}U_{C}(2)
in the sense of
[
18

We refer to Theorem
3.8

As in

,

the intersection cohomology of

in the even genus case. Indeed, our decomposition is compatible with its computation in

By

*Conjecture 4.10, we expect

In the odd degree case (

, the moduli spaces of stable bundles and stable pairs are birational.
In contrast, when

While

*Section 3.4.

from

There is a large body of conjectures on explicit semiorthogonal decompositions of Fano varieties, which are often obtained by analyzing Hodge diamonds and using the additivity of Hochschild homology in semiorthogonal decompositions
[
12

*Theorem 1.5; see

One general framework is to analyze a “two-ray game” given by two extremal contractions of Fano varieties

(1.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}

In our paper,

,

, and

. We start with a known semiorthogonal decomposition of
D^{b}(Y)
and “extend” it to a semiorthogonal decomposition of
D^{b}(X)
.
The difficult part is to mutate this decomposition of
D^{b}(X)
into a decomposition compatible with a semi-orthogonal decomposition of
D^{b}(Z)
:

Conjecture 1.2

(Two-Ray Game Conjecture (1))
.

Given extremal contractions (
1.1
)
of smooth Fano varieties, there exist semiorthogonal decompositions
“compatible” with pullbacks along maps

D^{b}(Y)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{s}\rangle,\ D^{b}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{s},\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{r}\rangle=\langle Q_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Q}_{u},\mathcal{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{t}\rangle,\ D^{b}(Z)=\langle\mathcal{B}_{1},\ldots,B_{t}\rangle.

The two semiorthogonal decompositions
of
D^{b}(X)
are related by a mutation.

Our approach uses weaving patterns of
[
34
]
, allowing for tight control of the Fourier–Mukai kernels for the various functors

D^{b}(\mathrm{Sym}^{k}C)\to D^{b}(M)

embedding the blocks on different stages of the mutation.
From the perspective of homological mirror symmetry for Fano manifolds
(see the survey
[
1

),
such mutations should exist in general, and we can even
predict a description of the hypothetical braid:

Conjecture 1.3

(Two-Ray Game Conjecture (2))

The braid in Conjecture

can be computed as the monodromy braid of eigenvalues of

acting on quantum cohomology

as the base

Y\dashleftarrow X\dashrightarrow Z
.

At the moment, the only way to verify this conjecture is to compute and compare the two braids; see

[
11
]
*Figure 16 for a worked-out example
of extremal contractions
\mathbb{P}^{4}\leftarrow\text{Bl}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathbb{P}^{4}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
or

[
35
]
for contractions
\mathbb{P}^{3}\leftarrow\text{Bl}_{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}=\text{Bl}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}Q_{4}\rightarrow Q_{4}

where

is a quintic curve of genus

and

is the intersection of two quadrics in

(this is the genus

[

It seems plausible that smoothness assumptions in Conjecture

can be weakened by considering noncommutative resolutions of singularities
as in our paper: the moduli space

It would be interesting to gather more evidence supporting this conjecture by connecting other Fano varieties

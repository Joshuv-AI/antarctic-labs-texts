On the supercritical Schrödinger equation on the exterior of a ball
Piero D’Ancona

Abstract
. We consider the mixed problem on the exterior of the unit ball in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , for a defocusing Schrödinger equation with a power nonlinearity | u | p − 1 ​ u |u|^{p-1}u , with zero boundary data. Assuming that the initial data are non radial, sufficiently small perturbations of large radial initial data, we prove that for all powers p > n + 6 p>n+6 the solution exists for all times, its Sobolev norms do not inflate, and the solution is unique in the energy class.



1.
Introduction

The literature on the
defocusing semilinear Schrödinger equation

(1.1)

iu_{t}+\Delta u=|u|^{p-1}u,\qquad u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)

is extensive and we
mention
[
4
]
,
[
16
]
and

[
8
]
for an
introduction and detailed bibliographies.
Restricting to large
H^{1}
(energy class) data,
Problem (
1.1
) is well posed for
large data below the critical value

p<p_{0}(n)
where

p_{0}(n)=\frac{n+2}{n-2}
for
n>2
and
p_{0}(n)=+\infty

if
n=1,2
(
[
9
]
).
The problem is well posed also in the critical case

p=p_{0}(n)
as proved in a series of important papers
(
[
1
]
,

[
2
]
,

[
10
]
,

[
15
]
,

[
5
]
,

[
13
]
,

[
18
]
).
Well or ill posedness
in the
supercritical
case
p>p_{0}(n)

has been for many years a completely open problem.
A recent breaktrhough was obtained in

[
12
]
, where finite time blow up
was established for a class of large, radially symmetric,
localized initial data and suitable ranges of
(n,p)
.

Here we consider the supercritical case
p>p_{0}(n)

from a different perspective. It is not difficult
to check that for radial data the first blow up must
occur at the origin, or, equivalently, that if the
solution remains bounded near the origin then no
blow up can occur. This is an immediate consequence
of the bound, valid for spherically symmetric functions,

(1.2)

|x|^{\frac{n}{2}-1}|u(x)|\lesssim\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}},

usually called
Strauss’ Lemma
.
Inequality (
1.2
)
is a special case of the family of inequalities

(1.3)

\textstyle|x|^{\frac{n}{p}-\sigma}|u(x)|\lesssim\||D|^{\sigma}u\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L^{r}_{\omega}},\qquad\frac{n-1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}<\sigma<\frac{n}{p}

(see
[
7
]
), where
the norm
L^{p}_{|x|}L^{r}_{\omega}
is an
L^{p}
norm
in the radial direction of the
L^{r}_{\omega}

norm on spheres centered at 0.

Exploiting the previous remark, one can remove the singularity,
by considering the mixed problem

(1.4)

iu_{t}+\Delta u=|u|^{p-1}u,\qquad u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\qquad u(t,\cdot)|_{\partial\Omega}=0

on the exterior of the unit ball

\Omega=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|>1\}.

One obtains that for radial initial data the solution
must exist for all times and all values of
p,n
.
The precise statement is the following:

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{B(0,1)}
,

n\geq 2
,
p>1
and let

u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}\cap H^{2}(\Omega)

be radially symmetric.
Then the mixed problem (
1.4
)
has a global solution

u\in C^{2}L^{2}\cap C^{1}H^{1}_{0}\cap CH^{2}
,
satisfying the conservation of mass

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
and of energy

(1.5)

\textstyle E(u(t)):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}u(t)|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\Omega}|u(t)|^{p+1}dx=E(u_{0})

and the uniform bound

(1.6)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}.

If
v\in C^{2}L^{2}\cap C^{1}H^{1}_{0}\cap CH^{2}
is
a second solution of (
1.4
)
with the same data, which is radially symmetric
or, more generally, bounded on any strip

0\leq t\leq T
, then
v\equiv u
.

Assume in addition
p>2N
for some integer
N\geq 1

and
(u_{0},f)
with
f(z)=|z|^{p-1}z

satisfy the nonlinear compatibility conditions of order
N
.
Then

u\in C^{N}L^{2}(\Omega)
and

u\in C^{k}(H^{2(N-k)}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))

for
0\leq k\leq N-1
.

The proof of Proposition
1.1
is given in
Section
4.1
;
see Definition
3.1
for the meaning of
the nonlinear compatibility conditions.
In principle, one can not expect that the
radial solution thus constructed is unique; there might
exist other non radial solutions with the same data,
since the problem is supercritical.
Uniqueness is discussed in detail below.

Once a global radial solution is available, a natural
question concerns the stability for non radial perturbations
of the initial data. In view of the blow up
result mentioned above, one may expect that for large
non radial data the solution blows up also for the
supercritical exterior problem. However,
using a pseudoconformal transform argument,
one verifies that radial solutions decay as
t\to+\infty
,
and the decay is good enough to work out a perturbative argument:

Proposition 1.2

(Decay of the radial solution)
.

Let
u
be the solution constructed in
Proposition
1.1
for
a radially symmetric

u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
.
Assume in addition that
xu_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Then, for all
t>0
,
|x|\geq 1
,

u
satisfies the decay estimate

(1.7)

|u(t,x)|\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}})\cdot\langle t\rangle^{-1}|x|^{1-\frac{n}{2}}.

Proposition
1.2
is proved in
Section
4.2
.
If
u_{0}
is smoother (and
xu_{0}\in L^{2}
)
then regularity propagates, and Sobolev norms do not
inflate but remain bounded for all times
(see Corollary
4.1
in
Section
4.3
).

Since the radial solution decays, a perturbative argument
is sufficient to obtain the following global existence
result, proved in Section
5
, which
is the main result of the paper.
Consider again the problem on

\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega

(1.8)

iv_{t}+\Delta v=f(v),\qquad v(0,x)=v_{0},\qquad v(t,\cdot)|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

Then we have:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
p>n+6
,
and
u_{0}\in H^{2m}(\Omega)

with
m=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1
a radial
function such that
(u_{0},f)
with
f(z)=|z|^{p-1}z

satisfy the compatibility conditions of order
m
.
Assume in addition that
xu_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Then there exists

\epsilon=\epsilon(u_{0})>0
such that the following holds.

If
v_{0}\in H^{2m}(\Omega)
, with
(v_{0},f)
satisfying
the nonlinear compatibility conditions of order
N
,
and
\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{H^{2m}}<\epsilon
,
then Problem (
1.8
) has a global
solution

v\in C^{m}L^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{k}(H^{2(m-k)}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))

for
0\leq k\leq m-1
.

In the proof of Theorem
1.3
we use
a Strichartz estimate for the exterior problem in
presence of an integrable potential. This is proved
in Section
2
as a consequence
of the Strichartz estimates on the exterior of convex
obstacles proved in
[
11
]
.

It remains to consider the problem of uniqueness.
A well known strategy allows to establish uniqueness
of energy class solutions to dispersive equations
under the assumption that a smooth solution exists
(weak–strong uniqueness).
This is particularly convenient in the present situation
since Theorem
1.3
yields a smooth solution
to (
1.8
). By adapting the arguments in

[
14
]
we get:

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose all the assumptions in Theorem
1.3

are satisfied and let
v
be the solution constructed
there.
Let
I
be an open interval containing

[0,T]
and

\underline{v}\in C(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap C^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))

a distributional solution for
0\leq t\leq T

to Problem (
1.8
) with the same initial data
as
v
,
which satisfies an energy inequality

E(\underline{v}(t))\leq E(\underline{v}(0))

(see (
1.5
)).
Then we have
\underline{v}(t)=v(t)
for
0\leq t\leq T
.

It is clear that a similar strategy can be applied to other
dispersive equations. Indeed, for the supercritical nonlinear
wave equation on the exterior of a ball,
we proved the global well posedness for quasi radial initial
data in the companion paper
[
6
]
.
For wave equations the pseudoconformal transform is not available,
however the decay of radial solutions can be proved using
the Penrose transform.

The plan of the paper is the following.
In Section
2
we recall the linear
theory for exterior problems and we prove energy and
Strichartz estimates for derivatives of solutions, also
for Schrödinger equations perturbed by a
well behaved potential
V(t,x)
.
Section
3
is devoted to the general
nonlinear theory and local existence results.
In Section
4
the global radial solution
is studied in detail. The main result,
Theorem
1.3
, is proved in Section
5
,
and the weak–strong uniqueness is proved in the
final Section
6
.

2.
The linear exterior problem

Consider the linear mixed problem
with
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega

(2.1)

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=F(t,x)\qquad u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\qquad u(t,\cdot)|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

Denote by
\Delta_{D}
or simply
\Delta

the selfadjoint Dirichlet Laplacian on
\Omega
with domain

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
,
by
\Lambda
its
(nonnegative selfadjoint) square root

\Lambda=(-\Delta_{D})^{1/2},\qquad D(\Lambda)=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

and by
e^{it\Delta}
the flow defined via
the spectral theorem.
Note that
\Lambda^{2k}=(-\Delta)^{k}
for integer
k\geq 0
,
and

D(\Lambda^{2k})=D(\Delta^{k})=\{f\in H^{2k}(\Omega):f,\Delta f,\dots,\Delta^{k-1}f\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\},

D(\Lambda^{2k+1})=\{f\in H^{2k+1}(\Omega):f,\Delta f,\dots,\Delta^{k}f\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\}

that is to say

\textstyle D(\Lambda^{k})=\{f\in H^{k}(\Omega)\colon\Delta^{j}f\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\ 0\leq 2j\leq k-1\}.

Then for all data
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,

F\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R};L^{2}(\Omega))

there exists a unique solution

u(t,x)\in C(\mathbb{R};L^{2}(\Omega))
, which can
be written in the form

\textstyle u(t,x)=e^{it\Delta}u_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}F(s,\cdot)dx.

To formulate estimates of
u
we need some notations.
Given an interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, a Banach
space
X
of functions on
\Omega
and
T>0
, we shall write

L^{p}_{I}X=L^{p}(I;X),\qquad L^{p}_{T}X=L_{[0,T]}X,\qquad L^{p}X=L_{[0,+\infty)}X

with the obvious norms. Moreover we shall write
for
N\in\mathbb{N}

\textstyle\|u\|_{X^{q,r;N}_{I}}=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\|\partial^{N-j}_{t}u\|_{L^{p}_{I}W^{2j,q}}=\sum_{2k+|\alpha|\leq 2N}\|\partial_{t}^{k}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}u\|_{L^{p}_{I}L^{q}}

where
W^{k,q}=W^{k,q}(\Omega)
is the usual Sobolev space
with norm

\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|\partial^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{q}}

and
H^{k}=W^{{2,k}}
.
Note that the order of spatial regularity of functions
in
X^{q,r;N}_{I}
is
2N
. When

I=[0,T]
or
I=[0,+\infty)
we use the notations

X_{T}^{q,r;N}=X_{[0,T]}^{q,r;N}\qquad X^{q,r;N}=X_{[0,+\infty)}^{q,r;N}.

From the integral representation of
u
and the unitarity of the group we have

(2.2)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{I}L^{2}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|F\|_{L^{1}_{I}L^{2}}

for any interval
I
containing 0.
Higher regularity estimates require compatibility conditions.
Given the data
(u_{0},F)
we define
recursively a sequence of functions
h_{j}
as follows:

(2.3)

h_{0}=u_{0},\qquad h_{j}(x)=\partial_{t}^{j}u(0,x)=i^{-1}(\partial^{j-1}_{t}F(0,x)-\Delta h_{j-1}(x))\qquad j\geq 1.

An explicit computation gives

h_{j}=(-i\Delta)^{j}u_{0}-i\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}(-i\Delta)^{j-\ell-1}\partial^{\ell}_{t}F(0,x)
.

Definition 2.1

(Linear compatibility conditions)
.

We say that the data
(u_{0},F)
satisfy the

linear compatibility conditions of order
N\geq 1
if

u_{0}\in H^{2N}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

F\in C^{k}H^{2(N-k-1)}(\Omega)\cap C^{N}L^{2}(\Omega)

for
0\leq k\leq N-1
, and

(2.4)

h_{j}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq j\leq N-1.

Then one has the following standard result.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume
(u_{0},F)
satisfy the
linear compatibility conditions of order
N
for some
N\geq 1
.
Then the global solution
u
to Problem (
2.1
)
satisfies
u\in C^{N}L^{2}(\Omega)
and

u\in C^{k}(H^{2(N-k)}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))

for
0\leq k<N
.
Moreover, for any interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}

containing 0 and of length
\gtrsim 1
we have the estimate

(2.5)

\|u\|_{X^{\infty,2;N}_{I}}\leq C(N)\left[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2N}}+\|F\|_{X^{1,2;N}_{I}}\right].

Proof.

The result is classical (see e.g.
[
17
]
)
and is proved by differentiating the equation w.r.to time
and estimating spatial derivatives inductively.
We depart from standard results only in the
formulation of the energy estimate
(
2.5
): the right
hand side is usually expressed in the form

\textstyle\|F\|_{X^{\infty,2;N-1}_{I}}+\|\partial_{t}^{N}F\|_{L^{1}_{I}L^{2}},

which can be estimated by the
X^{1,2;N}_{I}
norm
of
F
since

\|G\|_{L^{\infty}_{I}L^{2}}\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{1}_{I}L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}G\|_{L^{1}_{I}L^{2}}.\qed

Consider next the equation with a time dependent potential

V(t,x)

(2.6)

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=V(t,x)u+F(t,x),\qquad u(t_{0},x)=f(x),\qquad u(t,\cdot)|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

If we assume that
for some interval
I
containing 0 (possibly
I=\mathbb{R}
)

u_{0}\in L^{2},\qquad F\in C_{I}L^{2},\qquad V\in L^{1}_{I}L^{\infty},

then the existence of a unique solution

u\in C_{I}L^{2}
is proved by a simple
contraction argument
for the map
v\mapsto u
, where
u
is defined as the
solution to

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=V(t,x)v+F(t,x),\qquad u(t_{0},x)=f(x),\qquad u(t,\cdot)|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

followed by a continuation argument.
The solution satisfies the estimate

(2.7)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{I}L^{2}}\leq C(\|V\|_{L^{1}_{I}L^{\infty}})\left[\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|F\|_{L^{1}_{I}L^{2}}\right].

Note that it is not necessary to modify the compatibility
conditions in the higher regularity case. Indeed, the
conditions should be

\textstyle h_{j}=i^{-1}(\partial^{j-1}_{t}F(0,x)+\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\binom{j-1}{k}\partial_{t}^{j-1-k}V(0,x)h_{k}-\Delta h_{j-1}(x)),\qquad j\geq 1

but if the potential
V
is sufficiently smooth, the
term
\partial_{t}^{j-1-k}V(0,x)h_{k}
belongs to

H^{1}_{0}
by the recursive assumption and can be
omitted.

We denote the solution of (
2.6
),
with initial data at
t=t_{0}
, by

u(t,x)=S(t;t_{0})f.

Note that
S(t;t_{0})
fails to be a group since
the potential
V
depends on time.
Regarding the
term
Vu
as a forcing term and applying Duhamel’s formula
we can write
S(t;t_{0})
as a perturbation of the free flow:

(2.8)

\textstyle S(t;t_{0})=e^{i(t-t_{0})\Delta}-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}V(s,x)S(s,t_{0})ds.

We impose a rather restrictive condition on
V
in order
to obtain a uniform energy estimate for all times:

(2.9)

\textstyle\|V\|_{X^{1,\infty;N}_{I}}=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\|\partial_{t}^{N-j}V\|_{L^{1}_{I}W^{2j,\infty}}<\infty.

Note that by standard embeddings we have

\textstyle\|V\|_{X^{1,\infty;N}_{I}}\lesssim\|V\|_{X^{1,2;N+N_{2}}_{I}},\qquad N_{2}=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1.

By the usual recursive argument
(repeated differentiation with respect to time)
we obtain the regularity result:

Theorem 2.3

(Perturbed energy estimate)
.

Assume
(u_{0},F)
satisfy the compatibility
conditions of order
N
for some integer
N\geq 1
.
Then problem (
2.6
) has a unique global
solution, which
satisfies
u\in C^{N}L^{2}(\Omega)
and

u\in C^{k}(H^{2(N-k)}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))

for
0\leq k<N
.
Moreover, for any interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}

containing 0

(2.10)

\|u\|_{X^{\infty,2;N}_{I}}\leq C(N,\|V\|_{X^{1,\infty;N}_{I}})\left[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2N}}+\|F\|_{X^{1,2;N}_{I}}\right].

We next consider the decay properties of the linear solution.
Strichartz estimates for the exterior problem
are available from
[
11
]
.
Recall that a couple of indices
(q,r)

is
admissible
if
(q,r)\in[2,\infty]\times[2,\infty)

and it satisfies the scaling condition

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{r}=\frac{n}{2}.

The
endpoint
is the couple
(2,\frac{2n}{n-2})
;
the restriction
r<\infty

means that the endpoint is not admissible when
n=2
.
Note that in the following result

\Omega
could be more generally
the exterior of any smooth convex obstacle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
(q,r)
a non endpoint
admissible couple, and
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
an interval
containing 0.
Assume
(u_{0},F)
satisfy the
linear compatibility conditions of order
m
for some
m\geq 1
.
Then the solution
u
to Problem (
2.1
) satisfies
the Strichartz estimate

(2.11)

\|u\|_{X^{q,r;m}_{I}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2m}}+\|F\|_{X^{1,2;m}_{I}}.

Proof.

We can assume
I=\mathbb{R}
.
If
m=0
and
F=0
,
the result is Theorem 1.7 in
[
11
]
,
and if
F
is nonzero the result follows by a
standard Christ–Kiselev argument.
If
m=1
, we apply
\partial_{t}

and use the estimate just obtained; this gives

\|u_{t}\|_{L^{q}L^{r}}\lesssim\|\Delta u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|F_{t}\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}\leq\|\Delta u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|F\|_{X^{1,2;1}}.

Since
\Delta u=F-iu_{t}
this implies

(2.12)

\|\Delta u\|_{L^{q}L^{r}}\lesssim\|\Delta u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|F\|_{X^{1,2;1}}+\|F\|_{L^{q}L^{r}}.

Note that
\|F\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\lesssim\|F\|_{X^{1,2;1}}
;
moreover,
X^{1,2;1}
embeds into
L^{1}H^{2}
and into

L^{\infty}L^{2}
, hence by complex interpolation it
embeds into
L^{2}H^{1}
.
If
n\geq 3
, this embeds into the endpoint

L^{2}L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}
and hence in all admissible
spaces
L^{q}L^{r}
by interpolation with the
embedding into
L^{\infty}L^{2}
.
This proves the
L^{q}L^{r}
estimate for
\Delta u
,
and by
L^{q}
elliptic regularity
(see e.g.
[
3
]
) this gives (
2.11
)
for
m=1
,
n\geq 1
. The same argument works for
the case
m=1
,
n=2
using the embedding

H^{1}\hookrightarrow BMO
. Finally,
for larger values of
m>1
, the usual recursion argument
and the embedding

X^{1,2;m}\hookrightarrow X^{q,r;m-1}
just proved for
admissible
(q,r)
allows to conclude the proof.
∎

) with the perturbed energy

), we obtain a similar result for the

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u-V(t,x)u=F,\qquad u(t_{0},x)=f(x),\qquad u(t,\cdot)|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

(Perturbed Strichartz estimate)
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
(q,r)
a non endpoint
admissible couple, and
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
an interval
containing 0.
Assume
(u_{0},F,V)
satisfy the perturbed

for an

.
Then the solution
u
to Problem (

) satisfies

(2.14)

\|u\|_{X_{I}^{q,r;m}}\leq C(m,p,\|V\|_{X_{I}^{1,\infty;m}})\cdot\left[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2m}}+\|F\|_{X_{I}^{1,2;m}}\right].

Write

and apply (

) to get

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X_{I}^{q,r;m}}

Using (
2.5
) we obtain (
2.14
).

Remark 2.6
.

Note that the previous arguments

3.
The nonlinear theory

We consider now the nonlinear
mixed problem on
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega

(3.1)

iu_{t}+\Delta u=f(u),\qquad u(0,x)=u_{0},\qquad u(t,\cdot)|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

The compatibility conditions must be modified as follows.
Define recursively the sequence of functions

\psi_{j}(x)
for
j\geq 0
as

\psi_{0}=u(0,x)=u_{0},\qquad\psi_{j}=\partial^{j}_{t}u(0,x)=\iota^{-1}(\partial_{t}^{j-1}f(u)|_{t=0}-\Delta\psi_{j-1}(x))

where in the expansion of
\partial_{t}^{j-1}f(u)

we replace
\partial^{k}_{t}u(0,x)
with
\psi_{k}
for
k<j
.

Definition 3.1

(Nonlinear compatibility conditions)
.

We say that the data
(u_{0},f)
satisfy the

nonlinear compatibility conditions of order

N\geq 1
if
u_{0}\in H^{2N}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

f\in C^{N}
,
f(0)=0
and

(3.2)

\psi_{j}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq j\leq N-1.

We get a local smooth solution to (
3.1
)
by a standard contraction argument:

Proposition 3.2

(Local existence in
H^{\frac{n}{2}+}
)
.

Assume

Let

endowed with the metric

d(v,w)=\|v-w\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;N}}
.
Consider the linearized problem

(3.3)

iu_{t}+\Delta u=f(v(t,x)),\qquad u(0,x)=u_{0},\qquad u(t,\cdot)|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

If
(u_{0},f)
satisfy (

)
and

of order
N
(

) (note that

is bounded

).
Thus the solution of (

)
is uniquely defined on

and has the

.
Hence the map
\Phi:v\mapsto u
which takes

into the solution

of the linearized problem
(

) operates on the metric space

.
If

iw_{t}+\Delta w=f(v_{1})-f(v_{2}),\qquad w(0,x)=0,\qquad w(t,\cdot)|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

(0,G)

\|\Phi(v_{1})-\Phi(v_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,1;N}}\leq C(N)\|f(v_{1})-f(v_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{1,2;N}}\leq C(N)T\|f(v_{1})-f(v_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;N}}.

Moreover, for

\|f(v)\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;N}}\leq\phi_{N}(\|v\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;N}}),

\|f(v_{1})-f(v_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;N}}\leq\phi_{N}(\|v_{1}\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;N}}+\|v_{2}\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;N}})\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;N}}

4.
The radial solution

4.1.
Existence: Proof of Proposition
1.1

Consider the equation

(4.1)

iu_{t}+\Delta u=|u|^{p-1}u,\qquad u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\qquad u(t,\cdot)|_{\partial\Omega}=0

with
radial
initial data

u_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
The existence of a local solution for
t\in[0,T]
is standard
and similar to the proof of Proposition
3.2
.
We use the space
Y_{T}

of functions
v(t,x)
, radial in
x
, such that

v\in C^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))\cap C([0,T];H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)),\qquad v(0,x)=u_{0}.

We endow
Y_{T}
with the distance

d(v,w)=\|v-w\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;1}}
.
By radiality and (
1.2
), functions in
Y_{T}

are bounded. Hence the map
\Phi:v\mapsto u
,
defined as above via the linearization (
3.3
),
operates on
Y_{T}
and satisfies

\|u\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;1}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\||v|^{p-1}v\|_{X_{T}^{1,2;1}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}^{p-1}\|v\|_{X_{T}^{1,2;1}}.

Estimate (
1.2
) implies that

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|v\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;1}}

so that

\|\Phi(v)\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;1}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|v\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;1}}^{p-1}\|v\|_{X_{T}^{1,2;1}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+T\|v\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;1}}^{p}.

In a similar way,

\|\Phi(v_{1})-\Phi(v_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;1}}\lesssim(\|v_{1}\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;1}}+\|v_{2}\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;1}})^{p-1}\cdot T\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{X_{T}^{\infty,2;1}}^{p}.

Thus taking
T
sufficiently small with respect to

\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}
we obtain a local solution with
the required regularity.

The local solution satisfies the energy conservation
(
1.5
), which implies the uniform bound
(
1.6
) for
0\leq t\leq T
.
In particular,
\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}\cap L^{p+1}}

remains bounded on
[0,T]
by a constant depending
only on
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}\cap L^{p+1}}
.
A standard continuation argument allows to extend
u

to a global solution, which satisfies (
1.5
)
and hence (
1.6
) for all times.
The claim about uniqueness follows immediately from
energy estimates.

Assume now that the data satisfy the compatibility conditions
of order
N\geq 2
. Differentiating the equation w.r.to
t

we see that
v=\partial_{t}u

satisfies an equation of the form

iv_{t}+\Delta v=a(t,x)v+b(t,x)\overline{v}

with
|a|+|b|\lesssim|u|^{p-1}
bounded.
By the linear theory we get
v\in CL^{2}

i.e.
u\in C^{1}L^{2}
.
Further differentiating w.r.to
t
, by a recursive argument
we get
u\in C^{N}L^{2}
, and using the equation itself we
obtain that

u\in C^{k}(H^{2(N-k)}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))

for
0\leq k\leq N-1
.

4.2.
Decay: Proof of Proposition
1.2

Apply to
u
the pseudoconformal transform

\textstyle u(t,x)=t^{-\frac{n}{2}}U(-\frac{1}{t},\frac{x}{t})e^{\frac{i|x|^{2}}{4t}}.

If
u(t,x)
is defined on the domain
t\geq 1
,

|x|\geq 1
, then
U(T,X)
is defined for

-1\leq T<0
,
|X|\geq|T|
and we have

\textstyle iu_{t}+\Delta_{x}u-|u|^{p-1}u=t^{-\frac{n}{2}-2}(iU_{T}+\Delta_{X}U-(-T)^{\nu}|U|^{p-1}U)e^{\frac{i|x|^{2}}{4t}}

with
\nu=\frac{n}{2}(p-1)-2
.
The energy density

e_{0}(T,X)=\textstyle\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{X}U|^{2}+\frac{1}{p+1}|U|^{p+1}

satisfies the identity

\textstyle\partial_{T}e_{0}=\Re\nabla\cdot\{\overline{U}_{T}\nabla U\}-\frac{\nu(-T)^{\nu-1}}{p+1}|U|^{p+1}.

Since
U
is a radial function we have

R^{n-1}\nabla_{X}\cdot\{\overline{U}_{T}\nabla U\}=\partial_{R}\{R^{n-1}U_{R}\overline{U}_{T}\}

where
\partial_{R}U=U_{R}=\frac{X}{|X|}\cdot\nabla_{X}U

denotes the radial derivative of
U
and
R=|X|
.
Introduce the radial energy density

\textstyle e(T,R)=R^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}|U_{R}|^{2}+\frac{1}{p+1}|U|^{p+1}\right)

so that

(4.2)

\partial_{T}e(T,R)=\Re\partial_{R}\{R^{n-1}U_{R}\overline{U}_{T}\}-\textstyle\frac{\nu(-T)^{\nu-1}}{p+1}R^{n-1}|U(T)|^{p+1}.

We now integrate the identity
(
4.2
) on the
(T,R)
domain

T_{1}\leq T\leq T_{2}
,
R\geq-T

for some
-1\leq T_{1}<T_{2}<0
.
Note that the exterior normal on the line
R=-T

(for
T<0
) is given by

\mathbf{n}=(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})
.
Writing

\mathcal{E}(T)=\int_{-T}^{+\infty}e(T,R)dR

after integration of (
4.2
) we obtain

\begin{split}\textstyle\mathcal{E}(T_{2})-\mathcal{E}(T_{1})-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}&e(T,-T)dT=\\
=&\textstyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Re\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}U_{R}\overline{U}_{T}|T|^{n-1}dT-\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\int_{-T}^{+\infty}\frac{\nu(-T)^{\nu-1}|U|^{p+1}}{p+1}dRdT\end{split}

since
-T=|T|=R
at the points of the cone.
Writing (with a slight abuse)
U(T,|X|)
instead of

U(T,X)
, the Dirichlet condition implies

U(T,-T)=0
, so that

\textstyle e(T,-T)=|T|^{n-1}\frac{1}{2}|U_{R}|^{2}.

On the other hand, differentiating
U(T,-T)=0
we get

U_{T}(T,-T)=U_{R}(T,-T)
.
Hence the previous estimate reduces to

\textstyle\mathcal{E}(T_{2})-\mathcal{E}(T_{1})+\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\int_{-T}^{+\infty}\frac{\nu(-T)^{\nu-1}|U|^{p+1}}{p+1}dRdT\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\left(\frac{|U_{R}|^{2}}{2}-|U_{R}|^{2}\right)|T|^{n-1}dT\leq 0.

Thus
\mathcal{E}(T)
is nonincreasing as
T\uparrow 0
and in
particular
\mathcal{E}(T)\leq\mathcal{E}(-1)

for
-1<T<0
. This implies

\textstyle\int_{-T}^{+\infty}|U_{R}|^{2}R^{n-1}dR\leq 2\mathcal{E}(-1)\quad\text{for}\quad-1<T<0

or equivalently

\textstyle\int_{|X|>-T}|\nabla_{X}U|^{2}dX\leq 2\mathcal{E}(-1)\quad\text{for}\quad-1<T<0.

Note that, if we extend
U
as zero in the region
|X|<-T
,
the extended function
\widetilde{U}(T,\cdot)
is

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

and radial, hence we can apply (
1.2
)
and we obtain

(4.3)

\textstyle|U(T,X)|^{2}\lesssim|X|^{2-n}\int|\nabla_{X}\widetilde{U}|^{2}dX=|X|^{2-n}\int_{|X|>-T}|\nabla_{X}U|^{2}dX\leq 2|X|^{2-n}\mathcal{E}(-1).

We convert (
4.3
) into an estimate for
u(t,x)
. We have

\textstyle\mathcal{E}(-1)=\int_{|X|>1}\left(\frac{|\nabla U(-1,X)|^{2}}{2}+\frac{|U(-1,X)|^{p+1}}{p+1}\right)dX.

Since

\textstyle U(T,X)=(-T)^{-\frac{n}{2}}u(-\frac{1}{T},\frac{X}{T})e^{\frac{i|X|^{2}}{4T}}

we compute

\textstyle\nabla_{X}U(T,X)=(-T)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{\frac{i|X|^{2}}{4T}}\left[\nabla_{x}u(-\frac{1}{T},\frac{X}{T})\cdot\frac{1}{T}+u(-\frac{1}{T},\frac{X}{T})\cdot i\frac{X}{2T}\right]

so that

\textstyle|\nabla_{X}U(-1,X)|\leq|\nabla u(1,-X)|+|Xu(1,-X)|

and

(4.4)

\textstyle\mathcal{E}(-1)\leq\int_{|x|>1}\left[|\nabla u(1,x)|^{2}+|xu(1,x)|^{2}+|u(1,x)|^{p+1}\right]dx.

On the other hand, changing variables

(t,x)=(-\frac{1}{T},\frac{X}{T})
in
\mathcal{E}(T)

and writing

\textstyle\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)=\mathcal{E}(-\frac{1}{t})

we get, after a standard computation,

\textstyle\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)=\int_{|x|>1}\left[\frac{1}{8}|(x+2it\nabla)u(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{|t|^{\frac{n}{2}(p-1)}}{p+1}|u(t,x)|^{p+1}\right]dx

and
\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)
is nonincreasing in
t
by the
previous computation; note this is a proof of the
pseudoconformal energy conservation on an exterior domain.
Hence we have

\|xu(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)+(t+t^{\frac{n}{2}(p-1)})E(u(t))\leq\mathcal{E}_{1}(0)+\langle t\rangle^{\frac{n}{2}(p-1)}E(u_{0})

and in conclusion

\|xu(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C(t)\left[\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+E(u(0))\right].

Combined with the conservation of
E(u(t))
and (
4.4
)
this gives

\mathcal{E}(-1)\lesssim\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+E(u(0))

and, recalling (
4.3
), we have proved

|U(T,X)|\lesssim|X|^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\left[\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+E(u(0))^{1/2}\right].

Writing
|U(T,X)|=|T|^{-n/2}|u(-\frac{1}{T},\frac{X}{T})|
we finally
obtain

\textstyle t^{\frac{n}{2}}|u(t,x)|\leq C|x|^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\cdot t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\left[\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+E(u(0))^{1/2}\right]

that is to say

(4.5)

|u(t,x)|\leq C\left[\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+E(u(0))^{1/2}\right]\cdot|x|^{1-\frac{n}{2}}t^{-1}.

Note that, using (
1.2
), we have

\textstyle E(u(0))=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{p+1}\int|u_{0}|^{p+1}\leq\|\nabla_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-1}\int|u_{0}|^{2}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H_{1}}).

Using (
4.5
) for
t>1
, and the inequality

|u(t,x)|\leq C|x|^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}

for
t\leq 1
, we obtain as claimed

|u(t,x)|\leq C\cdot|x|^{1-\frac{n}{2}}\langle t\rangle^{-1}.

with a constant depending on

\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}
.

4.3.
Non inflation of Sobolev norms

We now consider the issue of regularity;
we prove that if the data are smoother
the solution remains smooth and Sobolev norms remain bounded,
for any order of regularity.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
p>2N-1
, and assume
(u_{0},f)

with
f(z)=|z|^{p-1}z

satisfy the compatibility conditions of order
N
.
Assume in addition that
xu_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Then the radial solution constructed in
Proposition
1.1
satisfies the
uniform bound on
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega

(4.6)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{X^{\infty,2;N}}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2N}},\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}}).

Recall that for all times
t
we have

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
.
We next prove
uniform bounds for the derivatives of
u
;
in the following proof we write for brevity

C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{M}})=C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{M}},\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}}).

leaving the dependence on
\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
implicit.

Differentiating the equation once with respect
to
t
we see that
v=u_{t}
solves

iv_{t}+\Delta v=\partial_{t}(|u|^{p-1}u)

and we have by (
1.7
)

\textstyle\|\partial_{t}(|u|^{p-1}u)\|_{L_{T}^{1}L^{2}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-1}\|v\|_{L^{2}}dt\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\langle t\rangle^{1-p}\|v\|_{L^{2}}dt.

Thus we can write

\textstyle\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}=\|u_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{t}(0)\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{T}\langle t\rangle^{1-p}\|v\|_{L^{2}}dt

and by Gronwall’s Lemma, if
p>2
we get

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}=\|u_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq C\|u_{t}(0)\|_{L^{2}}

with
C=C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}})
;
since
|u_{t}(0)|\leq|\Delta u_{0}|+|u_{0}|^{p}
we have

\|u_{t}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-1}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}})

which gives the estimate

\|u_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}).

Using the equation for
u
we can estimate also

\|\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq\|u_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\||u|^{p}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}})+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{p-1}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}).

By elliptic regularity we have then

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}})

and summing up we have proved

(4.7)

\|u\|_{X^{\infty,2;1}}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}).

For higher order derivatives we proceed in a similar
way by induction.
Applying
\partial_{t}^{j}
to the equation we have

i(\partial^{j}_{t}u)_{t}+\Delta(\partial^{j}_{t}u)=\partial^{j}_{t}(|u|^{p-1}u).

The right hand side satisfies

|\partial^{j}_{t}(|u|^{p-1}u)|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{1-p}|\partial^{j}_{t}u|+L.O.T.

where the lower order terms are products of derivatives

\partial^{h}_{t}u
with
h<j
, which are bounded by
the induction hypothesis (recall that
u
is radial in
x
,
hence
L^{\infty}
norms are bounded by
L^{2}
norms of
the gradient),
times a power of
u
of order at least
p-j
, which
decays like
\langle t\rangle^{j-p}
and is integrable provided

p>j+1
. This implies

\textstyle\|L.O.T.\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{2}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\langle t\rangle^{p-j}C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2j}})dt\lesssim C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2j}})

and hence

\textstyle\|\partial^{j}_{t}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}\leq\|\partial^{j}_{t}u(0)\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial^{j}_{t}(|u|^{p-1}u)\|_{L^{2}}dt

\textstyle\lesssim\|\partial^{j}_{t}u(0)\|_{L^{2}}+C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2j}})+\|\langle t\rangle^{1-p}\partial^{j}_{t}u\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{2}}.

Thus again by Gronwall’s Lemma, if
p>j+1
, we have

(4.8)

\|\partial^{j}_{t}u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2j}}).

Using the equation, this shows that if
p>N+1
one has

\|\partial^{N}_{t}u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|\partial^{N-1}_{t}\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2j}})

and by elliptic regularity we have also

\textstyle\|\partial^{N}_{t}u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|\partial^{N-1}_{t}u\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2j}})

provided
p>N+1
.
Derivatives
\partial^{j}_{t}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}u

can be estimated using the equation and elliptic regularity
by the usual recursive procedure;
this requires to estimate

\Delta^{j}(|u|^{p-1}u)
with
j=1,\dots,N-1
,
and in order to get an integrable factor
\langle t\rangle^{p-2j}

we must assume
p-2(N-1)>1
i.e.
p>2N-1
.
We finally arrive at the estimate

(4.9)

\|u\|_{X^{\infty,2;N}}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2N}})

provided
p>2N-1
.

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Denote by
u(t,x)
the global radial solution with

constructed in
Propositions

,
1.2

and Corollary

,
and let

We have

\textstyle\|V\|_{X_{T}^{1,\infty;m}}\lesssim\sum_{2j+|\alpha|\leq 2m}\|\partial^{j}_{t}\partial^{\alpha}|u|^{p-1}\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{\infty}}

where the sum is extended over

,

\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{\nu}=\alpha
and

\nu\leq j+|\alpha|
, so that
\nu\leq 2m
.
By the decay estimate (

)
we have
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1}

hence the last integral is convergent provided

. By Sobolev embedding and the bound (

)

\textstyle\|u\|_{X^{\infty,\infty;m}_{T}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{\infty,2;m+N_{2}}_{T}}\lesssim C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2(m+N_{2})}},\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}}),\qquad N_{2}=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1

\|V\|_{X^{1,\infty;m}}\lesssim C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2(m+N_{2})}},\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}})<\infty

provided
p>2m+2
.
Thus we are in position to apply Theorem
2.3
and
Proposition
2.5
,
and we get that the linear equation

iw_{t}+\Delta w-V(t,x)w=F(t,x)

satisfies for all admissible non endpoint
(q,r)

and all
m\geq 1
the perturbed energy–Strichartz estimates

(5.3)

\|w\|_{X^{\infty,2;m}_{T}}+\|w\|_{X^{q,r;m}_{T}}\lesssim\|w_{0}\|_{H^{2m}}+\|F\|_{X^{1,2;m}_{T}}

provided
p>2m+2
,
u_{0}\in H^{2(m+N_{2})}

and compatibility conditions of suitable order are satisfied.
The implicit constant in (
5.3
) depends on

\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
but not on
T
.

We now apply (
5.3
) to the equation
(
5.1
). For an admissible couple
(q,r)

and an integer
m
to be chosen we write

M_{m}(T)=\|w\|_{X^{\infty,2;m}_{T}}+\|w\|_{X^{q,r;m}_{T}}

and by (
5.3
) we have

(5.4)

M_{m}(T)\lesssim\|w_{0}\|_{H^{2m}}+\|w^{2}F[u,w]\|_{X^{1,2;m}_{T}}.

We must estimate

\textstyle\|w^{2}F[u,w]\|_{X^{1,2;m}_{T}}=\sum_{2j+|\alpha|\leq 2m}\|\partial^{j}_{t}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(w^{2}F[u,w])\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{2}}.

The derivative can be expanded as a finite sum

\textstyle\partial^{j}_{t}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(w^{2}F[u,w])=\sum\int_{0}^{1}G\cdot W_{1}W_{2}U_{1}\cdot\dots\cdot U_{\nu}d\sigma

where
0\leq\nu\leq 2m
and

•

W_{1}=\partial^{h_{1}}_{t}\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{x}w

and

W_{2}=\partial^{h_{2}}_{t}\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{x}w

•

U_{k}=\partial^{j_{k}}_{t}\partial^{\beta_{k}}_{x}(u+\sigma w)

•

h_{1}+h_{2}+j_{1}+\dots+j_{\nu}=j
,

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\beta_{1}+\dots+\beta_{\nu}=\alpha

•

it is not restrictive to assume that

2j_{\nu}+|\beta_{\nu}|\geq 2j_{k}+|\beta_{k}|
for
all
k
and that
2h_{2}+|\alpha_{2}|\geq 2h_{1}+|\alpha_{1}|

•

G
satisfies
|G|\lesssim|u+\sigma w|^{p-\nu-2}

so that

\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}})^{p-\nu-2}
.

We take the
L^{2}
norm in
x
of each product.
We consider two cases.

First case:

2j_{\nu}+|\beta_{\nu}|\geq 2h_{2}+|\alpha_{2}|
.
Then we write

\begin{split}\|GW_{1}W_{2}U_{1}\dots U_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}\leq&\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}\|W_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|U_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\dots\|U_{\nu-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|U_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}\\
\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}&\|W_{1}\|_{W^{N_{r},r}}\|W_{2}\|_{W^{N_{r},r}}\|U_{1}\|_{H^{N_{2}}}\dots\|U_{\nu-1}\|_{H^{N_{2}}}\|U_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

by Sobolev embedding, where

\textstyle N_{r}=\lfloor\frac{n}{r}\rfloor+1,\qquad N_{2}=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1;

note that since the maximal order of derivation is

2j_{\nu}+|\beta_{\nu}|\leq 2m
, we have

2j_{k}+|\beta_{k}|\leq m
for
k<\nu

and
2h_{i}+|\alpha_{i}|\leq m
,
i=1,2
,
so that, using (
4.6
), we have

\|U_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{N_{2}}}\leq\|u\|_{X^{\infty,2;(m+N_{2})/2}}+\|w\|_{X^{\infty,2;(m+N_{2})/2}}\leq\|u\|_{X^{\infty,2;m}}+\|w\|_{X^{\infty,2;m}}

\leq C_{m}+M_{m}(T)

provided
m\geq N_{2}
, where

C_{m}=C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2m}},\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}})
.
Moreover we can estimate

\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}})^{p-\nu-2}\lesssim(\langle t\rangle^{-1}+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}})^{p-\nu-2}.

This gives, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\|GW_{1}W_{2}U_{1}\dots U_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}\leq(C_{m}+M_{m}(T))^{\nu}(\langle t\rangle^{-1}+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}})^{p-\nu-2}\|W_{1}\|_{W^{N_{r},r}}\|W_{2}\|_{W^{N_{r},r}}.

We now take the
L^{1}
norm in
t\in[0,T]
.
Since
2h_{i}+|\alpha_{i}|\leq m
,
i=1,2
,
we can write

\textstyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle t\rangle^{2+\nu-p}\|W_{1}\|_{W^{N_{r},r}}\|W_{2}\|_{W^{N_{r},r}}dt\leq\|\langle t\rangle^{2+\nu-p}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-2}}}\|w\|_{X_{T}^{q,r;(m+N_{r})/2}}^{2}

and if
m\geq N_{r}
(which is implied by
m\geq N_{2}
)
this gives

\leq\|\langle t\rangle^{2+\nu-p}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-2}}}M_{m}(T)^{2}.

We choose
q\in(2,\infty)
such that
(q,r)
is admissible,
i.e.
q=\frac{4r}{n(r-2)}
. Since
\nu\leq 2m
,
we see that

\textstyle\|\langle t\rangle^{2+\nu-p}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-2}}}\leq\|\langle t\rangle^{2+2m-p}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-2}}}<\infty\quad\text{provided}\quad p>2m+3-n\frac{r-2}{2r}

and in this case

(5.5)

\textstyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle t\rangle^{2+\nu-p}\|W_{1}\|_{W^{N_{r},r}}\|W_{2}\|_{W^{N_{r},r}}dt\lesssim M_{m}(T)^{2}.

With a similar computation we can write

\textstyle\int_{0}^{T}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-\nu-2}\|W_{1}\|_{W^{N_{r},r}}\|W_{2}\|_{W^{N_{r},r}}dt\lesssim\left\|\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-\nu-2}\right\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{q}{q-2}}}M_{m}(T)^{2}

and

\left\|\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-\nu-2}\right\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{q}{q-2}}}\leq\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}^{p-q-\nu}\|w\|_{L^{q}_{T}L^{\infty}}^{q-2}\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{N_{2}}}^{p-q-\nu}\|w\|_{L^{q}_{T}W^{N_{r},r}}^{q-2}\lesssim M_{m}(T)^{p-q-\nu}

provided
p>2m+q
. In conclusion we have

(5.6)

\textstyle\int_{0}^{T}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-\nu-2}\|W_{1}\|_{W^{N_{r},r}}\|W_{2}\|_{W^{N_{r},r}}dt\lesssim M_{m}(T)^{p-q-\nu}.

Combining (
5.5
), (
5.6
) we conclude

(5.7)

\|GW_{1}W_{2}U_{1}\dots U_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim M_{m}(T)^{2}+M_{m}(T)^{p}

provided
p>2m+q+2
(so that
p-q-\nu>2
).

Second case:

2j_{\nu}+|\beta_{\nu}|<2h_{2}+|\alpha_{2}|
.
Then we estimate the product as follows

\begin{split}\|GW_{1}W_{2}U_{1}\dots U_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}\leq&\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}\|W_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|U_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\dots\|U_{\nu-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|U_{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
\lesssim&\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}\|W_{1}\|_{W^{N_{r},r}}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|U_{1}\|_{H^{N_{2}}}\dots\|U_{\nu-1}\|_{H^{N_{2}}}\|U_{\nu}\|_{H^{N_{2}}}\end{split}

and in this case the maximal order of derivation is

2h_{2}+|\alpha_{2}|
so that we have

2j_{k}+|\beta_{k}|\leq m
for all
k

and
2h_{1}+|\alpha_{1}|\leq m
.
Proceeding in a similar way as in the first case, we get
again (
5.7
).

Summing up, and recalling (
5.4
), we have
proved

(5.8)

M_{m}(T)\lesssim\|w_{0}\|_{H^{2m}}+M_{m}(T)^{2}+M_{m}(T)^{p}

with an implicit constant depending on

\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2m}}+\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
, provided

(5.9)

\textstyle m=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1,\qquad p>2m+q+2

and
(q,r)
is admissible with
q\in(2,\infty)
.
We can take
q>2
arbitrarily close to 2, so that
it is sufficient to assume

p>n+6

to achieve (
5.9
).
Finally, a standard continuation argument shows that
if
\|w_{0}\|_{H^{2m}}
is sufficiently small
with respect to
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2m}}+\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
,
the (maximal) local solution
w
to the equation
(
5.1
) can be continued for all times, and
this proves the claim.

6.
Weak–strong uniqueness

We recall the definition of the energy
E(u(t))

E(u(t))=E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p+1}dx

of a solution
u(t,x)
to the Cauchy problem

(6.1)

iu_{t}+\Delta u=|u|^{p-1}u,\qquad u(0,x)=u_{0},\qquad u(t,\cdot)|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

Following
[
14
]
, we prove a general stability result
for local solutions of (
6.1
), from which the
uniqueness Theorem
1.4
follows
immediately.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
I
be an open interval containing
[0,T]
,
T>0
.
Let
u,v
be two distributional solutions to (
6.1
)
on
I\times\Omega
such that

u\in C(I;H^{2}(\Omega))\cap C^{1}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap C^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(I\times\Omega),\quad\Delta u\in C(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)),

v\in C(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap C^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)).

Assume in addition that
v
satisfies an energy inequality

E(v(t))\leq E(v(0)).

Then the difference
w=v-u
satisfies the energy estimate

E(w(t))\leq Ce^{Ct}(E(w(0))+\|w(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}),\qquad t\in[0,T]

where
C
is a constant depending on

(6.2)

C=C(p,T,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}).

To prove Theorem
6.1
, consider the
difference
w=v-u
, which satisfies the equation

iw_{t}+\Delta w=|u+w|^{p-1}(u+w)-|u|^{p-1}u.

We prepare an estimate for the
L^{2}
norm of
w
.
Using the multiplier
i\overline{w}
we get

\begin{split}\textstyle\partial_{t}\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=&\textstyle 2\Im\int_{\omega}(|u+w|^{p-1}(u+w)-|u|^{p-1}u)\overline{w}dx\\
\leq&\textstyle C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{I}L^{\infty}})\cdot\int_{\Omega}(|w|^{2}+|w|^{p+1})dx\\
\leq&\textstyle C[\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+E(w(t))]\end{split}

and by Gronwall’s Lemma

(6.3)

\textstyle\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|w(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{C(t-s)}E(w(s))ds,\qquad t\in[0,T]

with
C=C(T,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}})
.

Next, we split

E(v)=E(u)+A(t)+B(t)

where

\textstyle A(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla_{x}w|^{2}dx+\int(\frac{|u+w|^{p+1}-|u|^{p+1}}{p+1}-|u|^{p-1}\Re(u\overline{w}))dx

\textstyle B(t)=\Re\int(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\overline{w}+|u|^{p-1}u\overline{w})dx.

Since
E(u)=E(u(0))
and
E(v)\leq E(v(0))
we have

(6.4)

0\leq E(v(0))-E(v(t))=A(0)-A(t)+B(0)-B(t).

Writing

\phi(t)=\frac{|t|^{\frac{p+1}{2}}}{p+1}\quad\text{so that}\quad\frac{|u|^{p+1}}{p+1}=\phi(|u|^{2}),

we see that

\textstyle\partial_{\sigma}\phi(|u+\sigma w|^{2})=\phi^{\prime}(|u+\sigma w|^{2})2\Re((u+\sigma w)\overline{w})=\Re(|u+\sigma w|^{p-1}(u+\sigma w)\overline{w}),

\begin{split}\partial^{2}_{\sigma}\phi(|u+\sigma w|^{2})=&(p-1)|u+\sigma w|^{p-3}\Re((u+\sigma w)\overline{w})^{2}+|u+\sigma w|^{p-1}|w|^{2}\\
\geq&|u+\sigma w|^{p-1}|w|^{2}\geq 2^{2-p}\sigma^{p-1}|w|^{p+1}-|u|^{p-1}|w|^{2}\end{split}

(since
p\geq 3
). We get easily

\textstyle\frac{|u+w|^{p+1}-|u|^{p+1}}{p+1}-|u|^{p-1}\Re(u\overline{w})=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\sigma}\partial^{2}_{\tau}\phi(|u+\tau w|^{2})d\tau d\sigma

\geq\textstyle\frac{2^{2-p}}{p(p+1)}|w|^{p+1}-\frac{1}{2}|u|^{p-1}|w|^{2}

which implies

\textstyle A(t)\geq\frac{1}{p2^{p}}E(w(t))-C\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\qquad C=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}^{p-1}.

Recalling (
6.3
), this gives
for
t\in[0,T]

\textstyle A(t)\geq\frac{1}{p2^{p}}E(w(t))-C\int_{0}^{t}E(w(s))ds-C\|w(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for some
C=C(T,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}})
.
On the other hand

\textstyle\frac{|u+w|^{p+1}-|u|^{p+1}}{p+1}-|u|^{p-1}\Re(u\overline{w})\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}})(|w|^{p+1}+|w|^{2})

which implies

A(0)\leq CE(w(0))+C\|w(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and in conclusion

(6.5)

\textstyle A(0)-A(t)\leq-\frac{1}{p2^{p}}E(w(t))+C\int_{0}^{t}E(w(s))ds+C\|w(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

with
C=C(T,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}})
.

In order to estimate
B(t)
, we first remark the following.
If
W(t,x)
,
U(t,x)
satisfy

iW_{t}+\Delta W=F,\qquad iU_{t}+\Delta U=G

with Dirichled boundary conditions,
then for any
\chi(t)\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T))
we have formally

(6.6)

\textstyle\iint_{\Omega}\chi^{\prime}(t)(\nabla U\cdot\nabla\overline{W})dxdt=\iint_{\Omega}\chi(t)(U_{t}\overline{F}+\overline{W}_{t}G)dxdt

Identity (
6.6
) is obvious for smooth

U,W
, by integration by parts.
By approximation, (
6.6
) holds also if

W
is a solution of
iW_{t}+\Delta W=F
in

\mathscr{D}^{\prime}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, with

W\in C^{1}([0,T];H^{-1}(\Omega))\cap C([0,T];H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)),

so that
F\in C([0,T];H^{-1}(\Omega))
, and

U\in C^{1}([0,T];H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap C([0,T];H^{2}(\Omega))\quad\text{with}\quad\Delta U\in C([0,T];H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))

so that
G\in C([0,T];H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
).
Consider now a sequence of test functions

\chi_{k}(t)\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T))
, non negative,
such that
\chi_{k}\uparrow\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}
pointwise,

t\in(0,T]
, and write

\textstyle B(t)-B(0)=\lim_{k\to\infty}I_{k},\qquad I_{k}:=\Re\iint_{\Omega}\chi_{k}^{\prime}(t)(\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla w}+|u|^{p-1}u\overline{w})dxdt.

Using (
6.6
) with the choices
W=w
,
U=u
,

F=|u+w|^{p-1}(u+w)-|u|^{p-1}u
and

G=|u|^{p-1}u
, we get

\textstyle I_{k}=\Re\iint_{\Omega}\chi_{k}(t)\left[u_{t}(|u+w|^{p-1}(\overline{u}+\overline{w})-|u|^{p-1}\overline{u})+\overline{w}_{t}|u|^{p-1}u-\partial_{t}(|u|^{p-1}u\overline{w})\right]

\textstyle=\Re\iint_{\Omega}\chi_{k}(t)\left[u_{t}(|u+w|^{p-1}(\overline{u}+\overline{w})-|u|^{p-1}\overline{u})-\overline{w}\partial_{t}(|u|^{p-1}u)\right]dxdt.

We compute

\textstyle\partial_{t}(|u|^{p-1}u)=|u|^{p-3}(|u|^{2}u_{t}+\frac{p-1}{2}u\overline{u}_{t}+\frac{p-1}{2}\overline{u}u_{t})

so that

\textstyle\Re[\overline{w}\partial_{t}(|u|^{p-1}u)]=\Re(Hu_{t}),\qquad H=\frac{1}{2}|u|^{p-3}((p+1)|u|^{2}\overline{w}+(p-1)\overline{u}^{2}w)

and

\textstyle I_{k}=\Re\iint_{\Omega}\chi_{k}(t)\left[|u+w|^{p-1}(\overline{u}+\overline{w})-|u|^{p-1}\overline{u}-H\right]u_{t}dxdt.

We have

\begin{split}|u+w&|^{p-1}(\overline{u}+\overline{w})-|u|^{p-1}\overline{u}-H\\
=&(|u+w|^{p-1}-|u|^{p-1})(\overline{u}+\overline{w})-\textstyle\frac{p-1}{2}|u|^{p-3}(|u|^{2}w+\overline{u}^{2}w)\\
=&\textstyle\frac{p-1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(|u+\sigma w|^{p-3}-|u|^{p-3})(|u|^{2}\overline{w}+\overline{u}^{2}w)d\sigma+R\end{split}

where

\textstyle R=\frac{p-1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}|u+\sigma w|^{p-3}[2\sigma|w|^{2}(\overline{u}+\overline{w})+u\overline{w}^{2}+\overline{u}|w|^{2}]d\sigma.

We have easily (if
p\geq 4
)

|R|\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}^{p-1})(|w|^{2}+|w|^{p}),\qquad||u+\sigma w|^{p-3}-|u|^{p-3}|\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}^{p-1})(|w|+|w|^{p-3})

and summing up

\textstyle|I_{k}|\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}}^{p-1})\iint_{\Omega}\chi_{k}(|w|^{2}+|w|^{p+1}).

Letting
k\to\infty
we deduce

\textstyle|B(t)-B(0)|\leq C\int_{0}^{t}[E(w(s))+\|w(s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}]ds,\qquad C=C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}})

and using (
6.3
) we have

\textstyle B(0)-B(t)\leq C\int_{0}^{t}E(w(s))ds+C\|w(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Recalling (
6.4
) and (
6.5
)
we obtain

\textstyle E(w(t))\leq C\int_{0}^{t}E(w(s))ds+CE(w(0))+C\|w(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

with
C=C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}})
as usual,
and by Gronwall’s Lemma we conclude the proof.

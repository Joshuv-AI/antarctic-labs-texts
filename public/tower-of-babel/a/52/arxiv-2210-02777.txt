A dynamical analogue of Ding-Iohara quantum algebras
Masamune Hattori, Shintarou Yanagida

Abstract
. We introduce a family of dynamical Hopf algebroids U q , p ​ ( g , X l ) U_{q,p}(g,X_{l}) depending on a complex parameter q q , a formal parameter p p , a set g g of structure functions satisfying the so-called Ding-Iohara condition, and a finite root system of type X l X_{l} . If g g is set to be certain theta functions, then our family recovers the elliptic algebras U q , p ​ ( 𝔤 ^ ) U_{q,p}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}) for untwisted affine Lie algebras 𝔤 ^ \widehat{\mathfrak{g}} studied by Konno (1998, 2009), Jimbo-Konno-Odake-Shiraishi (1999) and Farghly-Konno-Oshima (2014). Also, taking the limit p → 0 p\to 0 in the case X l = A l X_{l}=A_{l} , we recover the Hopf algebras U q ​ ( g ¯ , A l ) U_{q}(\overline{g},A_{l}) of type A l A_{l} with structure functions g ¯ := lim p → 0 g \overline{g}:=\lim_{p\to 0}g , introduced by Ding-Iohara (1998) as a generalization of Drinfeld quantum affine algebras. Thus, our Hopf algebroid U q , p ​ ( g , X l ) U_{q,p}(g,X_{l}) can be regarded as a dynamical analogue of the Ding-Iohara quantum algebras. As a byproduct, we obtain an extension of the Ding-Iohara quantum algebras to those of non-simply-laced type.

Introduction

0.1.
Motivation and backgrounds

The motivation of this note is to investigate the Hopf algebroid structure of elliptic quantum groups and its relation to the Hopf algebra structure of Ding-Iohara quantum algebras. Let us explain briefly these two objects, Ding-Iohara quantum algebras and elliptic quantum groups.

Ding-Iohara quantum algebras

Ding and Iohara
[
DI97b
]
introduced a family of (topological or formal) Hopf algebras which generalize the Drinfeld realization
[
D89
]
of the quantum affine algebras
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{l+1})
with the Drinfeld comultiplication
[
DI97a
]
. This Hopf algebra depends on a quantum parameter
q
and a set
g
of structure functions satisfying certain conditions. we denote it by
U_{q}(g,A_{l})
and call it
the Ding-Iohara quantum algebra
. A brief review will be given in
section

1.2
.

Let us briefly recall the algebra structure of the Ding-Iohara quantum algebra
U_{q}(g,A_{l})
. See
1.2.1
for the detail. The algebra
U_{q}(g,A_{l})
is compactly presented by generating currents

e_{i}(z)\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e_{i,n}z^{-n},\quad f_{i}(z)\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{i,n}z^{-n},\quad\psi^{+}_{i}(z)\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi^{+}_{i,n}z^{-n},\quad\psi^{-}_{i}(z)\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi^{-}_{i,n}z^{-n},

for
i=1,\dotsc,l
and current relations among them. The genuine generators of the algebra are the Fourier modes
e_{i,n},f_{i,n},\psi^{\pm}_{i,n}
, and the genuine relations are obtained by expanding the current relations. See
1.2.2
for the precise treatment. The current relations involve the structure functions
g=\{g_{ij}(z)\mid i,j=1,\dotsc,l\}
. If we set
g_{ij}(z)\coloneqq q^{-a_{ij}}(1-q^{a_{ij}}z)/(1-q^{-a_{ij}}z)
with
(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{l}
being the Cartan matrix of type
A_{l}
, then the algebra
U_{q}(g,A_{l})
recovers the Drinfeld realization
[
D89
]
of the quantum affine algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{l+1})
. See
1.2.5
for the detail.

The algebra structure of
U_{q}(g,A_{l})
is designed to admit the
Drinfeld-type comultiplication
, which involves infinite-summation of the genuine generators. In the case
g_{ij}(z)=q^{-a_{ij}}(1-q^{a_{ij}}z)/(1-q^{-a_{ij}}z)
, this comultiplication reduces to the Drinfeld comultiplication
[
DI97a
]
of the quantum affine algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{l+1})
. To admit such a comultiplication, the structure functions
g
must satisfy a certain condition, which we call the Ding-Iohara condition. See (
1.2.2
) and (
1.2.3
) for the detail.

Let us give one more comment on the algebra structure of

, which is the starting point of our study. Among the current relations, we have the
Serre-type relations
(
1.2.17
). For the generating currents
e_{i}(z)
and
e_{j}(z)
with
a_{ij}=-1
, the Serre-type relation is given by

\displaystyle e_{i}(z_{1})e_{i}(z_{2})e_{j}(w)-h_{ij}(\tfrac{z_{1}}{w},\tfrac{z_{2}}{w})e_{i}(z_{1})e_{j}(w)e_{i}(z_{2})+e_{j}(w)e_{i}(z_{1})e_{i}(z_{2})

\displaystyle+\,

\displaystyle e_{i}(z_{2})e_{i}(z_{1})e_{j}(w)-h_{ij}(\tfrac{z_{2}}{w},\tfrac{z_{1}}{w})e_{i}(z_{2})e_{j}(w)e_{i}(z_{1})+e_{j}(w)e_{i}(z_{2})e_{i}(z_{1})=0,

where

is given in terms of the structure functions

as (

(0.1.1)

\displaystyle h_{ij}(x,y)\coloneqq\frac{(g_{ii}(x/y)+1)(g_{ij}(x)g_{ij}(y)+1)}{g_{ij}(y)+g_{ii}(x/y)g_{ij}(x)}.

If the structure functions are set to be

As mentioned in the final paragraph of

]
, the argument carries over the simply-laced types

Elliptic quantum groups

Elliptic quantum groups are still under active investigation, and the meaning of the word “elliptic quantum group” is less definite than the word “quantum group”, as far as we understand. Roughly speaking, the notion of elliptic quantum groups refers to an “elliptic deformation” or a “dynamical analogue” of quantum groups. It originates in the classification of solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation: the rational, trigonometric, and elliptic solutions, and also in the (classical or quantum) dynamical Yang-Baxter equation. We refer to
[
Ko20
, §1.1]
for the history and backgrounds of elliptic quantum groups.

At present, there are several objects in literature which are called elliptic quantum groups. This note focuses on
the elliptic algebra
U_{q,p}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
for the untwisted affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
of a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, which was first introduced by Konno
[
Ko98
]
for the case
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, and by Jimbo-Konno-Odake-Shiraishi
[
J+99
, Appendix]
for general
\mathfrak{g}
. Here
q
denotes the quantum parameter, and
p
denotes the elliptic nome. We will give a review of
U_{q,p}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
in
section

1.3
. For a comprehensive review of the relation between the elliptic algebra
U_{q,p}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
and the other “elliptic quantum groups”, we refer to
[
Ko20
, §1.2]
.

As mentioned in

Although the algebra structure of the elliptic algebra

•

The paper

•

After a decade, another coalgebra structure

One of the motivations of this note is to investigate the Drinfeld-type coalgebra structure

-Hopf algebroid formulation.

The goal of this note

At this point, it is natural to ask the following questions.

•

Is there an extension
U_{q}(g,X_{l})
of the Ding-Iohara quantum algebra for a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
non-simply-laced type

X_{l}=B_{l},C_{l},F_{4}
or

so that the quantum affine algebra

of the same type

is a special case of
U_{q}(g,X_{l})
with structure functions

set to be some rational functions?

•

Is there an algebra
U_{q,p}(g,X_{l})
which extends the above
U_{q}(g,X_{l})
, has the additional elliptic parameter
p
, and gives the elliptic algebra
U_{q,p}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
of the same type

if the structure functions
g
set to be some theta functions?

•

Does the algebra

admit a Hopf algebroid structure with Drinfeld-type comultiplication?

The goal of this note is to introduce a family of Hopf algebroids
U_{q,p}(g,X_{l})
which gives a simultaneous answer to the above questions.

Recollections on Ding-Iohara quantum algebras and elliptic algebras

1.1.
Hopf algebroid

We begin with the definition of
\mathfrak{h}
-Hopf algebroid for a finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
in the sense of Etingof and Varchenko
[
EV98
, Chap. 4]
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a finite-dimensional commutative Lie algebra over the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
. We denote the field
\clM(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
of meromorphic functions on the linear dual
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, regarded as a complex affine space, by

\mathbb{F}\coloneqq\clM(\mathfrak{h}^{*}).

An algebra
A
over
\mathbb{C}
is called an
\mathfrak{h}
-algebra
if it is equipped with an
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
-bigrading
A=\bigoplus_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}}A_{\alpha,\beta}
, and with two algebra embeddings
\mu_{l},\mu_{r}\colon\mathbb{F}\to A_{0,0}
, called
the left
and
right moment maps
, such that

\displaystyle\mu_{l}(F)a=a\mu_{l}(T_{\alpha}F),\quad\mu_{r}(F)a=a\mu_{r}(T_{\beta}F)\quad(F\in\mathbb{F},\,a\in A_{\alpha,\beta}),

where
T_{\alpha}
is the shift operator

(T_{\alpha}F)(x)\coloneqq F(x+\alpha)\quad(x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}).

An
\mathfrak{h}
-algebra morphism
A\to B
is an algebra homomorphism consistent with the moment maps. Such a morphism automatically preserves the
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
-bigrading.

We define
the modified tensor product
of two
\mathfrak{h}
-algebras
A
and
B
to be the
\mathfrak{h}
-algebra

(1.1.1)

\displaystyle\bigl(A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}B,\bigoplus_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}}(A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}B)_{\alpha,\beta},\mu_{l}^{A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}B},\mu_{r}^{A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}B}\bigr)

as follows. We set

(1.1.2)

\displaystyle A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}B\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}}(A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}B)_{\alpha,\beta},\quad(A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}B)_{\alpha,\beta}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}}A_{\alpha,\gamma}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}B_{\gamma,\beta},

where
A_{\alpha,\gamma}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}B_{\gamma,\beta}
is the quotient of the standard tensor product
A_{\alpha,\gamma}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}B_{\gamma,\beta}
of linear spaces over
\mathbb{C}
by the linear subspace generated by the elements

(1.1.3)

\displaystyle\mu^{A}_{r}(F)a\otimes b-a\otimes\mu^{B}_{l}(F)b\quad(a\in A,\,b\in B,\,F\in\mathbb{F}).

We denote an element of
A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}B
by
a\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}b
for
a\in A
and
b\in B
. The modified tensor product
A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}B
is again an
\mathfrak{h}
-algebra by the multiplication

(a\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}b)(a^{\prime}\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}b^{\prime})\coloneqq(aa^{\prime})\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}(bb^{\prime})

and the moment maps

(1.1.4)

\displaystyle\mu^{A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}B}_{l}(F)\coloneqq\mu_{l}^{A}(F)\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}1_{B},\quad\mu^{A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}B}_{r}(F)\coloneqq 1_{A}\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}\mu_{r}^{B}(F)\quad(F\in\mathbb{F}).

We also have the natural notion of
the modified tensor product
f\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}g
of
\mathfrak{h}
-algebra homomorphisms
f
and
g
, whose detail is omitted.

The category of
\mathfrak{h}
-algebras has a monoidal structure whose tensor product is the above
\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}
and the unit object is
the
\mathfrak{h}
-algebra
D_{\mathfrak{h}}
of difference operators on the meromorphic function field
\mathbb{F}=\clM(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
. As a
\mathbb{C}
-linear space,
D_{\mathfrak{h}}
is defined to be the subspace of
\mathbb{C}
-linear endomorphisms
\End_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{F})
spanned by the operators of the form
fT_{\alpha}
with
f\in\mathbb{F}
and
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. It is a subalgebra of
\End_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{F})
, and has the
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
-bigrading

(1.1.5)

\displaystyle D_{\mathfrak{h}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}}(D_{\mathfrak{h}})_{\alpha,\beta},\quad(D_{\mathfrak{h}})_{\alpha,\beta}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\{fT_{-\alpha}\mid f\in\mathbb{F}\}&(\alpha=\beta)\\
0&(\alpha\neq\beta)\end{cases}.

Note that
(D_{\mathfrak{h}})_{0,0}=\mathbb{F}T_{0}=\mathbb{F}\id_{\mathbb{F}}
. The moment maps are given by

\mu_{l}=\mu_{r}\colon\mathbb{F}\longrightarrow(D_{\mathfrak{h}})_{0,0}=\mathbb{F}T_{0},\quad f\longmapsto fT_{0}.

These data give
D_{\mathfrak{h}}
a structure of an
\mathfrak{h}
-algebra, and it is a monoidal unit with respect to the modified tensor product
\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}

[
EV98
, Lemma 4.1]
.

An
\mathfrak{h}
-bialgebroid
is a triple
(A,\Delta,\varepsilon)
consisting of an
\mathfrak{h}
-algebra
A
and two
\mathfrak{h}
-algebra homomorphisms
\Delta\colon A\to A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}A
and
\varepsilon\colon A\to D_{\mathfrak{h}}
satisfying
the coassociativity and counit axioms

(1.1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\Delta\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}\id_{A})\Delta=(\id_{A}\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}\Delta)\Delta&:A\longrightarrow(A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}A)\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}A\cong A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}(A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}A),\\
(\varepsilon\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}\id_{A})\Delta=\id_{A}=(\id_{A}\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}\varepsilon)\Delta&:A\longrightarrow D_{\mathfrak{h}}\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}A\cong A\cong A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}D_{\mathfrak{h}},\end{split}

where
\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}
denotes the modified tensor product of
\mathfrak{h}
-algebra homomorphisms, and
\cong
’s denote the natural isomorphisms.
The map
\Delta
is called
the comultiplication
, and
\varepsilon
is called
the counit
of the
\mathfrak{h}
-bialgebroid
A
.

Let
(A,\Delta,\varepsilon)
be an
\mathfrak{h}
-bialgebroid. A linear map
S\in\End_{\mathbb{C}}A
is called an
antipode
if the following relations hold for
f\in\mathbb{F}
and
a\in A_{\alpha,\beta}
.

(1.1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S(\mu_{r}(f)a)=S(a)\mu_{l}(f),\quad&S(a\mu_{l}(f))=\mu_{r}(f)S(a),\\
m(\id_{A}\otimes S)\Delta(a)=\mu_{l}(\varepsilon(a)1),\quad&m(S\otimes\id_{A})\Delta(a)=\mu_{r}\bigl(T_{\alpha}(\varepsilon(a)1)\bigr),\end{split}

where
\varepsilon(a)1
denotes the action of the operator
\varepsilon(a)\in D_{\mathfrak{h}}
on the constant function
1
, and
\otimes
in the second line denotes the ordinary tensor product of linear maps over
\mathbb{C}
. An
\mathfrak{h}
-bialgebroid equipped with an antipode is called an
\mathfrak{h}
-Hopf algebroid
.

Note that for
\mathfrak{h}=0
, the zero-dimensional commutative Lie algebra, we have
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
and
\mathfrak{h}
-algebra is nothing but an algebra
A
over
\mathbb{C}
whose moment maps are the unit
\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to A
. Then the modified tensor product
\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}
is equal to the standard tensor product
\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}
and
D_{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathbb{C}
. Moreover, an
\mathfrak{h}
-bialgebroid
(A,\Delta,\varepsilon)
is a bialgebra over
\mathbb{C}
with comultiplication
\Delta\colon A\to A\otimes A
and counit
\varepsilon\colon A\to\mathbb{C}
, and an
\mathfrak{h}
-Hopf algebroid
(A,\Delta,\varepsilon,S)
is a Hopf algebra with antipode
S
. Thus, an ordinary Hopf algebra is a special case of
\mathfrak{h}
-Hopf algebroid.

To close this
section

1.1
, let us mention the relation of
\mathfrak{h}
-bialgebroids and Hopf algebroids in the sense of
[
L96
,
X01
]
. As far as we know, what is today commonly called a Hopf algebroid means the one introduced in loc. cit. Our presentation follows
[
X01
, §3]
.

A Hopf algebroid is a tuple
(A,R,\alpha,\beta,m,\Delta,\varepsilon)
consisting of:

1

An algebra
(A,m)
.

2

Another algebra
R
.

3

An algebra homomorphism
\alpha\colon R\to A
and an algebra anti-homomorphism
\beta\colon R\to A
such that the images of
\alpha
and
\beta
commute in
A
.

At this point, the algebra
A
has an
R
-
R
-bimodule structure with
r.a\coloneqq\alpha(r)a
and
a.r\coloneqq\beta(r)a
for
a\in A
and
r\in R
. In particular, we have the
R
-
R
-bimodule product
A\otimes_{R}A
, which admits a natural
R
-
R
-bimodule structure.

4

An
R
-
R
-bimodule homomorphism
\Delta\colon A\to A\otimes_{R}A
.

5

An
R
-
R
-bimodule homomorphism
\varepsilon\colon A\to R
.

These should satisfy the following conditions.

(1)

The map
\Delta
satisfies
\Delta(1_{A})=1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}
and
(\Delta\otimes_{R}\id_{A})=(\id_{A}\otimes_{R}\Delta)
under the natural isomorphism
(H\otimes_{R}H)\otimes_{R}H\cong H\otimes_{R}(H\otimes_{R}H)
.

(2)

The maps
m
and
\Delta
are compatible in the sense that
\Delta(a)\bigl(\beta(r)\otimes 1_{A}-1_{A}\otimes\alpha(r)\bigr)=0
for any
r\in R
and
a\in A
, and that
\Delta(ab)=\Delta(a)\Delta(b)
for any
a,b\in A
.

(3)

The map
\varepsilon
satisfies
\varepsilon(1_{A})=1_{R}
and
(\varepsilon\otimes_{R}\id_{A})\Delta=\id_{A}=(\id_{A}\otimes_{R}\varepsilon)\Delta
under the natural isomorphisms
R\otimes_{R}A\cong A\cong A\otimes_{R}R
.

An
\mathfrak{h}
-bialgebroid
(A,\Delta,\varepsilon)
is an Hopf algebroid in the above sense by setting

R\coloneqq\mathbb{F}=\clM(\mathfrak{h})
and
\alpha\coloneqq\mu_{l}
,
\beta\coloneqq\mu_{R}
. In fact,
\mathbb{F}
is commutative so that the commuting condition in (iii) holds. Then we find that
A\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}A\cong A\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}A
(see (
1.1.3
)). The conditions (1)–(3) are checked immediately.

1.2.
Ding-Iohara quantum algebras

Here we give an overview of the quantum algebras introduced by Ding and Iohara in
[
DI97b
]
.

For
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, let
(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{l}
be the Cartan matrix of type
A_{l}
, i.e.,

a_{ij}=\begin{cases}2&(i=j)\\
{-1}&(\left|i-j\right|=1)\\
0&(\text{otherwise})\end{cases}.

We will also denote it as
(a_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
with
I\coloneqq\{1,\dotsc,l\}
.

Also, we denote

(1.2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\{\text{analytic functions of $z$ without poles except $z=0$ or $\infty$}\},

regarded as a commutative algebra over
\mathbb{C}
. Let

g=\{g_{ij}(z)\mid i,j\in I\}

be a set of analytic functions satisfying the following condition.

1

There are some
G_{ij}^{\pm}(z)\in\mathcal{A}
such that

(1.2.2)

\displaystyle g_{ij}(z)=G_{ij}^{+}(z)/G_{ij}^{-}(z).

2

We have

(1.2.3)

\displaystyle g_{ij}(z)=g_{ji}(z^{-1})^{-1}.

We call the set
g

the structure functions
, and call the condition on
g

the Ding-Iohara condition
. Note that the condition
G^{\pm}_{ij}(z)\in\mathcal{A}
implies that we can expand
G^{\pm}_{ij}(z)
and
g_{ij}(z)=G_{ij}^{+}(z)/G_{ij}^{-}(z)
as

(1.2.4)

\displaystyle G^{\pm}_{ij}(z)=\sum_{n=-L^{\pm}}^{M^{\pm}}G^{\pm}_{ij,n}z^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[z^{\pm 1}],\quad g_{ij}(z)^{\pm 1}=\sum_{n=-N^{\pm}}^{\infty}g_{ij,n}z^{n}\in\mathbb{C}(\!(z)\!)

for some
L^{\pm},M^{\pm},N^{\pm}\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let also
q
be a complex number such that
\left|q\right|\neq 0,1
, called
the quantum parameter
.
Using these data
A,g
and
q
, we define the topological algebra
U_{q}(g,A_{l})
as follows.
See
1.2.2
for the meaning of the phrase “topological”.

Definition 1.2.1
.

Using the Cartan matrix
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
of type
A_{l}
, the structure functions
g
and the quantum parameter
q
, we define

U=U_{q}(g,A_{l}),

to be the topological algebra over
\mathbb{C}
generated by the elements

(1.2.5)

\displaystyle q^{\pm c/2},\ e_{i,n},\ f_{i,n},\ \psi^{+}_{i,n},\ \psi^{-}_{i,n}\quad(i\in I,\,n\in\mathbb{Z})

with the following defining relations.
We will use the
generating currents

(1.2.6)

\displaystyle e_{i}(z)\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e_{i,n}z^{-l},\quad f_{i}(z)\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{i,n}z^{-l},\quad\psi^{\pm}_{i}(z)\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi^{\pm}_{i,n}z^{-n},

where
z
is a formal variable.

•

The generators
q^{\pm c/2}
satisfy that

(1.2.7)

q^{\pm c/2}
are central and
q^{c/2}q^{-c/2}=1
.

Below we denote
q^{rc/2}\coloneqq(q^{c/2})^{r}
for
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

•

The generating currents
\psi^{\pm}(z)
satisfy that

(1.2.8)

\psi^{\pm}(z)
are invertible as series of
z
,

which means that
\psi^{\pm}(z)
are bounded above or bounded below, and the initial terms are invertible in
U
. Explicitly, in the case
\psi^{+}(z)
is bounded above, we have

there is some
N\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\psi^{+}_{i,n}=0
for
n>N
,
and
\psi^{+}_{i,N}
is invertible in
U
.

•

For any
i,j\in I
, we have in terms of the formal variables
z
and
w
that

(1.2.9)

\displaystyle\psi^{+}_{i}(z)\psi^{+}_{j}(w)=\psi^{+}_{j}(w)\psi^{+}_{i}(z),\quad

\displaystyle\psi^{-}_{i}(z)\psi^{-}_{j}(w)=\psi^{-}_{j}(w)\psi^{-}_{i}(z),

(1.2.10)

\displaystyle\psi^{+}_{i}(z)\psi^{-}_{j}(w)\psi^{+}_{i}(z)^{-1}\psi^{-}_{j}(w)^{-1}=\

\displaystyle g_{ij}(q^{-c}z/w)g_{ij}(q^{c}z/w)^{-1},

(1.2.11)

\displaystyle\psi^{+}_{i}(z)e_{j}(w)=g_{ij}(q^{-c/2}z/w)e_{j}(w)\psi^{+}_{i}(z),\quad

\displaystyle\psi^{+}_{i}(z)f_{j}(w)=g_{ij}(q^{c/2}z/w)^{-1}f_{j}(w)\psi^{+}_{i}(z),

(1.2.12)

\displaystyle\psi^{-}_{i}(z)e_{j}(w)=g_{ij}(q^{c/2}z/w)e_{j}(w)\psi^{-}_{i}(z),\quad

\displaystyle\psi^{-}_{i}(z)f_{j}(w)=g_{ij}(q^{-c/2}z/w)^{-1}f_{j}(w)\psi^{-}_{i}(z),

(1.2.13)

\displaystyle G_{ij}^{-}(z/w)e_{i}(z)e_{j}(w)=G_{ij}^{+}(z/w)e_{j}(w)e_{i}(z),\quad

\displaystyle G_{ij}^{+}(z/w)f_{i}(z)f_{j}(w)=G_{ij}^{-}(z/w)f_{j}(w)f_{i}(z),

(1.2.14)

\displaystyle[e_{i}(z),f_{j}(w)]=\frac{\delta_{i,j}}{q-q^{-1}}\bigl(\delta(q^{-c}z/w)

\displaystyle\psi^{-}_{i}(q^{c/2}w)-\delta(q^{c}z/w)\psi^{+}_{i}(q^{c/2}z)\bigr).

In each equality, we expand both sides as series of
z
and
w
using (

) and (

), and compare the coefficients of

for each

to get genuine relations.
Here we should consider an appropriate topology for general structure functions

, which will be explained in

. Also, in (

), we used the commutator
[X,Y]\coloneqq XY-YX
and the formal delta function

(1.2.15)

\displaystyle\delta(x)\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}x^{n}.

•

For

with

, we have in terms of the formal variables

and

that

(1.2.16)

\displaystyle[e_{i}(z),e_{j}(w)]=[f_{i}(z),f_{j}(w)]=0.

The meaning of these equalities are the same as the previous item.

•

For
i,j\in I
such that

and

the Serre-type
(or

(1.2.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}X_{i}(z_{1})X_{i}(z_{2})X_{j}(w)&-h_{ij}(\tfrac{z_{1}}{w},\tfrac{z_{2}}{w})X_{i}(z_{1})X_{j}(w)X_{i}(z_{2})+X_{j}(w)X_{i}(z_{1})X_{i}(z_{2})\\
&+(\text{three terms given by $z_{1}\leftrightarrow z_{2}$})=0\end{split}

for

or

. Here we set

(1.2.18)

\displaystyle h_{ij}(x,y)\coloneqq\frac{(g_{ii}(x/y)+1)(g_{ij}(x)g_{ij}(y)+1)}{g_{ij}(y)+g_{ii}(x/y)g_{ij}(x)},

regarded as a series belonging to

by expanding

and

as in (

).

Remark 1.2.2
.

Let us comment on the topology of
U
. As mentioned after (
1.2.14
), the genuine relations are obtained by expanding the current relations. For example, the first equality of (
1.2.13
) yields

\sum_{k=-L^{-}}^{M^{-}}G_{ij,k}^{-}e_{i,m}e_{j,n}=\sum_{k^{\prime}=-L^{+}}^{M^{+}}G_{ij,k}^{+}e_{j,n+k-k^{\prime}}e_{i,m-k+k^{\prime}}

for each
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, under the expansion (
1.2.4
).
This involves finite summations only, and we can apply the ordinary diamond lemma
[
Be78
]
.
On the other hand, the first equality in (
1.2.11
) yields

(1.2.19)

\displaystyle\psi_{i,m}^{+}e_{j,n}=\sum_{k=-N^{+}}^{\infty}g_{ij,k}q^{-kc/2}e_{j,n-k}\psi^{+}_{i,m+k}

for each
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, unless
\psi^{+}_{i}(z)
is bounded above or
e_{j}(z)
is bounded on both sides, we have an infinite summation in the defining relation, and cannot obtain a genuine algebra. Largely speaking, there are two solutions of this problem.

1

Require
\psi^{+}_{i}(z)
to be bounded above, and
\psi^{-}_{i}(z)
to be bounded below, so that the genuine relations contain finite summations only. This is the case of the Drinfeld realization of quantum affine algebras
[
D89
]
, recalled in
1.2.5
.

2

Equip the structure functions
g_{ij}(z)
with a parameter, say
p
, and require
g_{ij}(z)=g_{ij}(z;p)
to have a series expansion
g_{ij}(z;p)=\sum_{k=-N}^{\infty}g_{ij,k}(z)p^{k}\in\mathbb{C}[z^{\pm 1}](\!(p)\!)
. Now we equip
U
with the
p
-adic topology. Then, since
g_{ij,k}(z)\in\mathbb{C}[z^{\pm 1}]
, we see that each
p
-adic component of the relations like (
1.2.19
) contains finite summation only, Thus we can regard
U
as a complete algebra with respect to the
p
-adic topology. We only consider this situation in the following text.

In a general situation, where no parameters are available, we are not sure how to resolve the problem. One possible solution may be to use the diamond lemma adapted to the formal power series
[
H02
]
, which requires an appropriate choice of the norm on the formal power series ring.

Fact 1.2.4

\displaystyle\Delta(e_{i}(z))\coloneqq e_{i}(z)\otimes 1+\psi_{i}^{+}(q^{c_{1}/2}z)\otimes e_{i}(q^{c_{1}}z),\quad\Delta(f_{i}(z))\coloneqq 1\otimes f_{i}(z)+f_{i}(q^{c_{2}}z)\otimes\psi^{-}_{i}(q^{c_{2}/2}z),

(1.2.22)

\displaystyle\Delta(\psi^{+}_{i}(z))\coloneqq\psi^{+}_{i}(q^{-c_{2}/2}z)\otimes\psi^{+}_{i}(q^{c_{1}/2}z),\quad\Delta(\psi^{-}_{i}(z))\coloneqq\psi^{-}_{i}(q^{c_{2}/2}z)\otimes\psi^{-}_{i}(q^{-c_{1}/2}z),

where

and
q^{rc_{2}/2}\coloneqq 1\otimes q^{rc/2}
for

. In each equality, we expand both sides to get the value of
\Delta
on each generator. See
[
DI97a
,
DI97b
]
for the detail of such a calculation. Since infinite summations appear in this calculation, the obtained comultiplication

is called formal. The infinite sums make sense if we take into account the topology given by a good gradation on the generators (
1.2.5
).

•

Counit
\varepsilon
:

(1.2.23)

\displaystyle\varepsilon(q^{c})\coloneqq 1,\quad\varepsilon(\psi^{\pm}_{i}(z))\coloneqq 1,\quad\varepsilon(e_{i}(z))=\varepsilon(f_{i}(z))=0.

Again, we expand the left hand sides to get the value of
\varepsilon
on each generator.

•

Formal antipode
S
:

(1.2.24)

\displaystyle S(q^{c})\coloneqq q^{c}\otimes q^{c},\quad

\displaystyle S(\psi^{\pm}_{i}(z))\coloneqq\psi^{\pm}_{i}(z)^{-1}

(1.2.25)

\displaystyle S(e_{i}(z))\coloneqq-\psi^{+}_{i}(q^{-c/2}z)^{-1}e_{i}(q^{-c}z),\quad

\displaystyle S(f_{i}(z))\coloneqq-f_{i}(q^{-c}z)\psi_{i}^{-}(q^{-c/2}z)^{-1}.

We call this topological Hopf algebra
U_{q}(g,A_{l})

the Ding-Iohara quantum algebra of type
A_{l}
,
and
the Ding-Iohara algebra
for short.

Let us give an example of the Ding-Iohara algebra.

Let us mention that the quantum toroidal algebra

Let

We prove that

\displaystyle\mu_{l}^{\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}^{\dagger}}\coloneqq 1\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}\mu^{\dagger}_{r}=1\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}\mu_{l},\quad\mu_{r}^{\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}^{\dagger}}\coloneqq\mu^{\dagger}_{l}\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}1=\mu_{r}\mathbin{\widetilde{\otimes}}1.

Now, let

and

in

, we have

\displaystyle a\otimes b-a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime}=\sum_{i}(\mu_{r}(f_{i})a_{i}\otimes b_{i}-a_{i}\otimes\mu_{l}(f_{i})b_{i})

and

\displaystyle b\otimes a-b^{\prime}\otimes a^{\prime}=\sum_{i}(b_{i}\otimes\mu_{r}(f_{i})a_{i}-\mu_{l}(f_{i})b_{i}\otimes a_{i})=\sum_{i}(b_{i}\otimes\mu^{\prime}_{l}(f_{i})a_{i}-\mu^{\prime}_{r}(f_{i})b_{i}\otimes a_{i}),

4.
Concluding remarks

Let us give some possible directions of future works as concluding remarks.

•

As mentioned in the introduction
section

0
and
2.2.3
,
3.2.5
and
3.3.5
, unlike the other root systems, the Serre relations of type
G_{2}
are not determined uniquely in this note. Recall that to generalize the quantum affine algebras
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
(resp. the elliptic quantum algebras
U_{q,p}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
) to the Ding-Iohara quantum algebras
U_{q}(g,X_{l})
(resp. the dynamical Ding-Iohara algebroids
U_{q,p}(g,X_{l})
), certain
q
-binomials in the original Serre relations are replaced by the functions
h_{ij}
. We impose some principles to the functions (see
section

3.1
), and then, except for the type
G_{2}
, we can determine the functions
h_{ij}
and
h^{3}_{ij}
uniquely (
2.2.3
and
3.2.5
). However, as for the type
G_{2}
, the functions
h^{4,1}_{ij}
and
h^{4,2}_{ij}
can be determined only up to the ideal
J
in (
3.3.5
). We may resolve this ambiguity by building some additional principles on the functions
h
.

•

This note only considers elliptic quantum groups attached to finite root systems, or to finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras. However, there are important elliptic quantum groups attached to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. For example, the paper
[
Ko18
]
studies the elliptic algebra
U_{q,p}(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n})
whose algebra structure is akin to
U_{q,p}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
in
section

1.3
, and reveals its relation to Felder’s elliptic quantum group. Another example is the elliptic quantum toroidal algebra
U_{q,t,p}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1,\textup{tor}})
introduced in a recent paper
[
KO
]
, which can be regarded as a dynamical analogue of the quantum toroidal algebra
U_{q,t}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1,\textup{tor}})
mentioned in
section

1.2
, (
1.2.26
). It would be nice to extend our dynamical Ding-Iohara algebroid
U_{q,p}(g,X_{l})
to the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
case, and construct a class of Hopf algebroids
U_{q,p}(g,\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
including the algebras
U_{q,p}(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n})
and
U_{q,t,p}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1,\textup{tor}})
.

•

To the best of our knowledge, it is still unclear whether there is a sort of Drinfeld double construction for the Ding-Iohara quantum algebra for general structure functions
g
, which was already mentioned in the final paragraph of
[
DI97b
]
. We have a similar problem for our dynamical Ding-Iohara algebroids. The point is the construction of well-behaved Hopf pairing and its dynamical analogue. We guess that this problem relates to the elliptic weight functions
[
Ko20
, Chap. 6]
.

•

Finally, let us recall that there is a Hall-algebraic construction of quantum groups. It is known that the quantum affine algebras and the quantum toroidal algebras (both with Drinfeld comultiplication) can be constructed as Drinfeld double of Ringel-Hall algebras. Is there a dynamical analogue of this construction? If such a construction exists, does it relate to our dynamical Ding-Iohara algebroids?

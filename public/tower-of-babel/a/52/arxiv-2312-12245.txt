On generalized Sidon spaces
Chiara Castello

Abstract
. Sidon spaces have been introduced by Bachoc, Serra and Zémor as the q q -analogue of Sidon sets, classical combinatorial objects introduced by Simon Szidon. In 2018 Roth, Raviv and Tamo introduced the notion of r r -Sidon spaces, as an extension of Sidon spaces, which may be seen as the q q -analogue of B r B_{r} -sets, a generalization of classical Sidon sets. Thanks to their work, the interest on Sidon spaces has increased quickly because of their connection with cyclic subspace codes they pointed out. This class of codes turned out to be of interest since they can be used in random linear network coding. In this work we focus on a particular class of them, the one-orbit cyclic subspace codes, through the investigation of some properties of Sidon spaces and r r -Sidon spaces, providing some upper and lower bounds on the possible dimension of their r-span and showing explicit constructions in the case in which the upper bound is achieved. Moreover, we provide further constructions of r r -Sidon spaces, arising from algebraic and combinatorial objects, and we show examples of B r B_{r} -sets constructed by means of them.



Keywords
: Sidon space; cyclic subspace code; linearized polynomial; generalized Sidon space

: Primary 11T99; 11T06. Secondary 11T71; 94B05.

1.
Introduction

Sidon spaces have been originally introduced by Bachoc, Serra and Zémor in
[
1
]
, in relation with the linear analogue of Vosper’s Theorem (see also
[
2
,
20
]
) as the
q
-analogue of Sidon sets, classical combinatorial objects introduced by Simon Szidon.

An
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
V
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is called a
Sidon space
if the products of any two nonzero elements of
V
are uniquely determined, up to a multiplicative factor in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, i.e. for all nonzero
a,b,c,d\in V
such that
ab=cd
then

\{a{\mathbb{F}}_{q},b{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\}=\{c{\mathbb{F}}_{q},d{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\},

where
e\mathbb{F}_{q}=\{e\lambda\colon\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}
. This can be naturally seen as the
q
-analog of Sidon sets originally introduced by Erdős in
[
15
]
. A subset
S
of an abelian group
(G,+)
is a Sidon set if the sums of any pairs of elements (possibly identical) are distinct, i.e. if for all
a,b,c,d\in S
such that
a+b=c+d
then

\{a,b\}=\{c,d\}.

The interest on Sidon spaces has increased quickly especially after the work of Roth, Raviv and Tamo because in
[
38
]
they pointed out the connection of Sidon spaces with
cyclic subspace codes
.

Let
k
be a non-negative integer with
k\leq n
, the set of all
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
, viewed as
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector space, forms a
Grassmannian space
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, which is denoted by
\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
. A
constant dimension subspace code
is a subset
C
of
\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
endowed with the metric defined as follows

d(U,V)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V)-2\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap V),

where
U,V\in C
. This metric is also known as
subspace metric
.
As usual, we define the
minimum distance
of
C
as

d(C)=\min\{d(U,V)\colon U,V\in C,U\neq V\}.

Subspace codes have been recently used for the error correction in random
network coding, see
[
22
]
.
The first class of subspace codes studied was the one introduced in
[
16
]
, which is known as
cyclic subspace codes
.
A subspace code
C\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
is said to be
cyclic
if for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}
and every
V\in C
then
\alpha V\in C
. If we denote by

\mathrm{Orb}(V)=\{\alpha V\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\}.

and
C
coincides with
\mathrm{Orb}(V)
, for some subspace
V
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, then we say that
C
is a
one-orbit
cyclic subspace code and
V
is said to be a
representative
of the orbit. In this case, we define the
dimension
of
C
as the dimension of
V
, since all the subspaces in
C
have the same dimension.

There are constructions of cyclic subspace codes for several sets of parameters (close to the optimality, in a sense that we will explain soon), see e.g.
[
17
,
31
,
34
,
39
,
47
,
48
,
49
,
50
,
51
]
.
Let
V
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
of dimension
k
. Then the maximum field of linearity of
V
is the maximum subfield
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
V
is closed under multiplication by a scalar in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
. In particular it can be proved that
|\mathrm{Orb}(V)|=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q^{t}-1}
if and only if
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
is the maximum field of linearity of
V
; see
[
33
, Theorem 1]
.
Therefore, every orbit of a subspace
V\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
defines a cyclic subspace code of size
(q^{n}-1)/(q^{t}-1)
, for some
t\mid n
. Let
V
be a strictly
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace of dimension
k
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Then the cyclic subspace code defined by
V
has size
(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)
. Also, in such a case, the maximum value for the minimum distance is at most
2k
and it is exactly
2k
if and only if the orbit of
V
is a
k
-spread of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
, which happens if and only if
k=1
, since
V
is strictly
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear. So, when
k>1

d(\mathrm{Orb}(V))\leq 2k-2.

In
[
43
]
the authors conjectured the existence of a cyclic subspace code of size
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
and minimum distance
2k-2
for every positive integers
n,k
such that
1<k\leq n/2
.

In
[
6
]
the authors used subspace polynomials to construct cyclic subspace codes with size
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
and minimum distance
2k-2
, proving that the conjecture is true for any given
k
and infinitely many values of
n
and
q
. Such result was then improved in
[
33
]
.
Finally, the conjecture was solved in
[
38
]
for most of the cases, by making use of Sidon spaces.

Roth, Raviv and Tamo in
[
38
]
pointed out that the study of cyclic subspace codes with size
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
and minimum distance
2k-2
is equivalent to the study of Sidon spaces.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
38
, Lemma 34]

Let
U\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
.
Then
U
is a Sidon space if and only if
|\mathrm{Orb}(U)|=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
and
d(\mathrm{Orb}(U))=2k-2
, i.e. for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\alpha U)\leq 1.

Moreover, the condition
k\leq n/2
in the aforementioned conjecture may be obtained as a consequence of the following theorem. To this aim we need the following notation: if
V
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
then

V^{2}=\langle ab\colon a,b\in V\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}

and
V^{2}
is said to be the
square-span
of V.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
1
, Theorem 18]

Let
V\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
be a Sidon space of dimension
k\geqslant 3
, then

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{2})\geqslant 2k.

Also, Sidon spaces with the smallest dimension of its square span were used in designing a multivariate public-key cryptosystem (see
[
37
]
). This motivates the study of more general bounds on the dimension of the subspace generated by the
r
-products of
V
(the
r
-span of
V
).

In
[
38
]
, the notion of Sidon space was extended to the notion of
r
-Sidon space (see Section
2
), similarly to what has been done for the classical Sidon sets.
Indeed, this notion can be seen as the
q
-analog of the classical notion of
B_{r}
-sets defined in abelian groups; see e.g.
[
11
]
.
This property may be used to distinguish cyclic subspace codes, in the spirit of what happens for the square-span and more in general
r
-span of a subspace; see
[
10
]
, and also its investigation was strongly encouraged by Raviv, Langton and Tamo in
[
37
]
in order to extend the hardness of the problem on which their cryptosystem is based.

In this paper we exploit the theory of
r
-Sidon spaces, first by giving some bounds on the dimension of an
r
-Sidon space obtained by evaluating the possible dimension of the
r
-span of an
r
-Sidon space (cf. Section
3
). In particular, we prove a lower bound on the dimension of
V^{r}
for any
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
V
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
1\in V
, which involves the stabilizer of
V^{r}
, i.e. the subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
containing the elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
that fix
V^{r}
. Since in the prime degree extensions, the stabilizer of
V^{r}
is forced to be
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, except if the stabilizer coincided with
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, the lower bound on the dimension of
V^{r}
can be improved. Moreover, if
k=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)
, we observe that the dimension of
V^{r}
can be at most
\binom{k+r-1}{r}
and we prove that any
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
V
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{r})=\binom{k+r-1}{r}
is an
r
-Sidon space. This allows us, in Section
4
, to show some explicit constructions of
r
-Sidon spaces that reach this upper bound, that we define as
max-span r-Sidon spaces
, in analogy with the definition given by Roth, Raviv and Tamo in
[
38
]
in the case
r=2
. Then we also explore constructions of
r
-Sidon spaces arising from scattered polynomials (cf. Section
5
) and others with the aid of MAGMA computations
[
8
]
(cf. Section
6
).
We conclude the paper with Section
7
where we show some examples of
B_{r}
-sets that can be constructed from our examples of
r
-Sidon spaces.

2.
Preliminaries

In the Introduction we mentioned that the definition of Sidon space was originally introduced in
[
1
]
.
A generalization of this concept has been given in
[
38
]
, as the
q
-analogue of
B_{r}
-sets, where an
r
-Sidon space is defined as follows. In this section, we resume what is known about
r
-Sidon spaces, some results and examples of them and we will recall the definition of equivalence for Sidon spaces arising from the equivalence of subspace codes.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
k,n,r\in\mathbb{N}
with
k<n
and
r\geq 2
. A subspace
V\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
is said to be an
r
-Sidon space
if for every nonzero
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\in V
, if

\prod_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}b_{i},

then the multisets
\{\{a_{1}\mathbb{F}_{q},\ldots,a_{r}\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\}
and
\{\{b_{1}\mathbb{F}_{q},\ldots,b_{r}\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\}
coincide.

Notice that in an
r
-Sidon space the product of any
r
nonzero elements is uniquely determined, up to scalars over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Clearly, the following holds.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
V\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
is an
r
-Sidon space, then it is an
r^{\prime}
-Sidon space for any
r^{\prime}
such that
2\leq r^{\prime}\leq r
.

In particular, any
r
-Sidon space is a Sidon space as well.

In
[
38
, Lemma 41]
, the following bound on the dimension of an
r
-Sidon space
V
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
was proved:

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)<\frac{n}{r}+1+\log_{q}(r).

Also in
[
38
, Constructions 42 and 45]
, the following two constructions of
r
-Sidon spaces were shown:

•

Let
q
be a prime power and let
k
and
r
be two integers. Let
n=k(r+1)
and let
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
be a root of an irreducible polynomial of degree
r+1
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
. Then
V=\{u+u^{q}\gamma\colon u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}\}
is an
r
-Sidon space.

•

Let
q
be a prime power and let
k
and
r
be two integers. Let
n=kr
and let
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
be a root of an irreducible polynomial
p(x)
of degree
r
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
such that
p(0)
is not a
(q-1)
-th power. Then
V=\{u+u^{q}\gamma\colon u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}\}
is an
r
-Sidon space.

As already explained in the Introduction, Sidon spaces and cyclic subspace codes with a certain minimum distance are equivalent objects. Therefore, it is quite natural to give a definition of equivalence for Sidon spaces arising from the equivalence of subspace codes; see
[
9
]
and
[
51
]
.
The study of the equivalence for subspace codes was initiated by Trautmann in
[
42
]
and the case of cyclic subspace codes has been investigated in
[
18
]
by Gluesing-Luerssen and Lehmann.
Thus, motivated by
[
18
, Definition 3.5 and Theorem 6.2(a)]
, the authors of
[
9
]
defined two cyclic subspace codes as
semilinearly equivalent
if there exists
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
such that

\mathrm{Orb}(U)=\mathrm{Orb}(V^{\sigma}),

where
V^{\sigma}=\{v^{\sigma}\colon v\in V\}
and this happens if and only if
U=\alpha V^{\sigma}
, for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
.
As a consequence, they gave the following definition of equivalence among two Sidon spaces.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
U
and
V
be two
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Then we say that
U
and
V
are
semilinearly equivalent
if the associated codes
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
and
\mathrm{Orb}(V)
are semilinearly equivalent, that is there exist
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
such that
U=\alpha V^{\sigma}
.
In this case, we will also say that they are equivalent under the action of
(\alpha,\sigma)
.

In the following theorem, we will show that the property of
V
of being an
r
-Sidon space is invariant under semilinear equivalence.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
V
be an
r
-Sidon space of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
of dimension
k
. Then
\alpha V^{\sigma}
is an
r
-Sidon space of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
of dimension
k
, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
and
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
.

Proof.

Clearly
\dim(\alpha V^{\sigma})=k
. Assume that

\prod_{i=1}^{r}(\alpha a_{i}^{\sigma})=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(\alpha b_{i}^{\sigma}).

for some
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\in V
and
b_{1},\dots,b_{r}\in V
. Thus, since
\alpha\neq 0
and
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
then

(1)

\prod_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}b_{i}.

Since
V
is an
r
-Sidon space, Equation (
1
) implies

\{\{a_{1}\mathbb{F}_{q},\ldots,a_{r}\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\}=\{\{b_{1}\mathbb{F}_{q},\ldots,b_{r}\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\},

and so

\{\{\alpha a_{1}^{\sigma}\mathbb{F}_{q},\ldots,\alpha a_{r}^{\sigma}\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\}=\{\{\alpha b_{1}^{\sigma}\mathbb{F}_{q},\ldots,\alpha b_{r}^{\sigma}\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\}.

∎

3.
Bounds on Sidon spaces and max-span
r
-Sidon spaces

In this section we provide a bound on the dimension of a Sidon space
V
in the case in which we have some information on the
s
-span of
V
for some positive integer
s\leq 2
. Indeed, this bound follows from a lower bound on the dimension of the
s
-span of
V
which involves the stabilizer of
V^{s}
. Then, we also provide an upper bound on the dimension of
V^{s}
, and we show how to use it in order to get constructions of
r
-Sidon spaces.

To this aim, let start by recalling the following theorem, known as the linear analogue of Kneser’s Theorem. Consider a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and let
A,B
be two non-zero
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Then we denote by

AB=\langle ab\colon a\in A,b\in B\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}.

For the finite field case,
[
20
, Theorem 2.4]
reads as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A,B
be non-zero

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Then

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(AB)\geqslant\min\{n,\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(A)+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(B)-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(H(AB))\},

where
H(AB)=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\colon xAB=AB\}\cup\{0\}
is the stabilizer of
AB
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.

Since
H(AB)
is a subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, if
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is a prime extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
H(AB)\neq\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, then
H(AB)=\mathbb{F}_{q}
and so Theorem
3.1
represents the linear analogue of the well-known
Cauchy-Davenport inequality
. The following result characterizes the subspaces attaining the equality in the Cauchy-Davenport inequality, which can be seen as the linear analogue of Vosper’s Theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
1
, Theorem 3]

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
be a prime extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
S,T
be
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
2\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(S),\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(T)
and
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(ST)\leq n-2
. If

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(ST)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(S)+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(T)-1,

then there are bases of
S
and
T
respectively of the form

\{g,ga,\dots,ga^{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(S)-1}\}
and
\{g^{\prime},g^{\prime}a,\dots,g^{\prime}a^{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(T)-1}\}

for some
g,g^{\prime},a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Given an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
V
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, we will frequently use that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)=[H(V)\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}]\dim_{H(V)}(V).

Indeed, let notice that the stabilizer
H(V)
of
V
is a subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
containing
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
V
is linear over
H(V)
. Hence, if
1\in V
then
H(V)\subseteq V
and

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)=[H(V)\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}]\dim_{H(V)}(V).

If
1\notin V
then
1\in\gamma^{-1}V
for some
\gamma\in V
,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\gamma^{-1}V)
and
H(V)=H(\gamma^{-1}V)
. Therefore, also in this case

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\gamma^{-1}V)=[H(\gamma^{-1}V)\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}]\dim_{H(\gamma^{-1}V)}(\gamma^{-1}V)=[H(V)\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}]\dim_{H(V)}(V).

We will denote by
h(V)
the degree of the field extension
H(V)
, i.e.
h(V)=[H(V)\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}]
.

As an immediate consequence of Remark (
3.3
), the following lemma holds.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
U
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
if dimension
k
such that
\gcd(k,n)=1
. Then
H(V)=\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

The following bounds on the dimension of a Sidon space
V
can be useful to restrict the possible dimension of a Sidon space when we have some information, not only on
V^{2}
, but also on
V^{s}
for some
s>2
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
V
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

V^{s}=\langle v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{s}:v_{i}\in V,i\in[s]\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}

is said to be the
s
-span
of V over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Moreover, we provide a natural upper bound on the dimension of the
r
-span of an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
proving also that the subspaces attaining the equality in such a bound are
r
-Sidon spaces.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
V
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Then for any positive integer
s
it holds:

H(V^{s-1})\subseteq H(V^{s})\text{ and }h(V^{s-1})\leq h(V^{s}).

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in H(V^{s-1})
with
\lambda\neq 0
then

\lambda V^{s-1}=V^{s-1}.

Since
V^{s}=V^{s-1}V
, then

\lambda V^{s}=\lambda(V^{s-1}V)=(\lambda V^{s-1})V=V^{s-1}V=V^{s},

therefore
\lambda\in H(V^{s})
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Let notice that if
V
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
1\in V
then
H(V^{s})\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}(V)
. Indeed, let
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in H(V^{s})
then

\alpha V^{s}\subseteq V^{s}.

and, since
1\in V
then
\alpha\in V^{s}
. Therefore
H(V^{s})\subseteq V^{s}
and, since
V^{s}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}(V)
for any
s\in\mathbb{N}
, then
H(V^{s})\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}(V)
.

We can now prove a bound on the dimension of a Sidon space which involves the stabilizer of its
s
-span for some values of
s
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
be a Sidon space containing
1
of dimension
k\geq 3
.
Then

(1)

there exists
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
t=\min\{l\in\mathbb{N}:V^{l}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(V)\}
;

(2)

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s})\geqslant sk-(s-2)h(V^{s})
and
k\leq[\mathbb{F}_{q}(V)\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}]\left(1-\frac{1}{s}\right)
for any
2\leq s\leq t
.

In particular,

k\leq[\mathbb{F}_{q}(V)\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}]\left(1-\frac{1}{t}\right).

Proof.

We first prove the existence of such a
t
. Let notice that since
1\in V
, by Remark
3.7
we get

V\subseteq V^{2}\subseteq V^{3}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq V^{s}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}(V)

and so this chain is finite and we can consider

t=\min\{s\in\mathbb{N}\colon V^{s}=V^{s+1}\}.

Let
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}
be a basis of
V
, then by definition we have that

V^{t+1}=a_{1}V^{t}+\ldots+a_{k}V^{t},

and so

a_{1}V^{t}=a_{2}V^{t}=\ldots=a_{k}V^{t}=V^{t},

implying that
V^{t}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}(V)
-linear.
Since
V^{t}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}(V)
, this implies that
V^{t}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(V)
, hence the first item is proved.

For the second item, let us proceed by induction on
s
.

For
s=2
the statement corresponds to Theorem
1.2
.

Let
2<s\leq t
and suppose the assertion true for
s^{\prime}<s
. Thus, by induction we have that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s-1})\geqslant(s-1)k-(s-3)h(V^{s-1}).

Note that since
s\leq t
, then
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s-1})-h(V^{s})<n
. Therefore, by Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
3.6
we have that

(2)

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s})

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(VV^{s-1})\geqslant\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s-1})-h(V^{s})

\displaystyle\geqslant k+(s-1)k-(s-3)h(V^{s-1})-h(V^{s})

\displaystyle=sk-(s-2)h(V^{s}).

Moreover, by Remark
3.7
we have that
h(V^{s})\leq[\mathbb{F}_{q}(V):\mathbb{F}_{q}]
and since
1\in V
we have
V^{s}\subseteq V^{t}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(V)
for any
s\leq t
, thus
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s})\leq[\mathbb{F}_{q}(V)\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}]
. Then we get

k\leq[\mathbb{F}_{q}(V)\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}]\left(1-\frac{1}{s}\right).

∎

Remark 3.9
.

Let notice that if
1\notin V
it is not true in general that
H(V^{s})\subseteq V^{s}
. Indeed, let
V=\gamma\mathbb{F}_{q}
where
\gamma
is a generating element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since
\mathbb{F}_{q}\subseteq H(V)
we can consider
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and clearly
\alpha\in H(V)
but
\alpha\notin V^{s}
since
1\notin V
. Therefore
H(V^{s})\not\subseteq V^{s}
.

However, the assumption that
1\in V
is not restrictive since
\alpha V
is still a Sidon space for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
and the bound provided in the above theorem still holds by replacing
\mathbb{F}_{q}(V)
by
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\alpha^{-1}V)
, where
\alpha\in V\setminus\{0\}
.

Let notice that when
n
is prime
H(V^{s})=\mathbb{F}_{q}
whenever
V^{s}\neq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
, then the above result can be improved by using the linear analogue of Vosper’s Theorem, i.e. Theorem
3.2
as shown in the following theorem

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
n
be a prime number and let
V\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
be a Sidon space containing
1
of dimension
k\geq 3
and let
t
be the minimum integer such that
V^{t}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. If
t>2
then

(3)

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s})\geq sk

for any
2\leq s\leq t-1
. In particular,

(4)

k\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{t-1}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

Let us proceed by induction on
s
.

For
s=2
Equation (
3
) follows from Theorem
1.2
.

Let
s>2
and
s\leq t-1
and suppose Equation (
3
) holds for any
s^{\prime}<s
. Then by induction we have that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s-1})\geqslant(s-1)k.

Since
n
is prime
h(V^{i})=1
for any
i<t
. Thus, since
s\leq t-1
,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s})<n
and by Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
3.6
it follows that

(5)

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s})

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(VV^{s-1})\geqslant\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s-1})-1

\displaystyle\geqslant k+(s-1)k-1

\displaystyle=sk-1

Now we prove that it cannot happen that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s})=sk-1
.

If
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s})=n-1
then

n-1=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s})=sk-1

therefore
s\mid n
and this is not possible since
n
is prime.

If
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s})\leqslant n-2
then Theorem
3.2
, applied to
V
and
V^{s-1}
, implies that
V
has a basis in geometric progression, up to a multiplicative factor in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, that is

V=g\langle 1,a,\dots,a^{k-1}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}},

for some
a,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.

By hypothesis
V
is a Sidon space and by Theorem
1.1
we have that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V\cap\alpha V)\leqslant 1
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
. Therefore, the only possibility for
V
is that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)=k=2
. Since
k\geqslant 3
, a contradiction arises.

Therefore

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{s})\geqslant sk

and so by choosing
s=t-1
, since
(t-1)k\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{t-1})<n
, we get

k\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{t-1}\right\rfloor.

∎

Now we want to provide also an upper bound on the dimension of
V^{r}
. Let denote by
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{k}]_{r}
the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space of multivariate homogeneous polynomials of degree
r
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
and note that this
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space has dimension
\binom{k+r-1}{r}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then the following result holds.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
V
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and let
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)=k
. Then

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{r})\leqslant\min\left\{n,\binom{k+r-1}{r}\right\}.

Proof.

Let observe that if
V=\langle a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
then a generator system for
V^{r}
is given by

\Gamma=\{a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{r}}:i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\dots\leq i_{r},i_{j}\in[k],j\in[r]\}

and so

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{r})\leqslant|\Gamma|=\binom{k+r-1}{r}.

∎

The previous lemma gives us an upper bound on the possible dimension of an
r
-Sidon space. The following theorem gives us a sufficient condition in order to have an
r
-Sidon space, obtained as a generalization of Lemma 20 in
[
38
]
.

Theorem 3.12
.

If
V\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
is such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{r})=\binom{k+r-1}{r}
, then V is an
r
-Sidon space.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{k}
whose components form an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-basis of
V
and let
u_{1},\dots,u_{r},v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\in V\setminus\{0\}
.

Let denote by

u_{i}=\displaystyle\sum_{s\in[k]}\alpha_{i,s}a_{s}=p_{u_{i}}(\mathbf{a})

v_{j}=\displaystyle\sum_{t\in[k]}\beta_{j,t}a_{t}=p_{v_{j}}(\mathbf{a})

for
i,j\in[r]
, where
p_{u_{i}},p_{v_{j}}
,
i,j\in[r]
, are the following multivariate polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in the indeterminates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
:

p_{u_{i}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\displaystyle\sum_{s\in[k]}\alpha_{i,s}x_{s}

p_{v_{j}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\displaystyle\sum_{t\in[k]}\beta_{j,t}x_{t}

Let observe that
u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{r}=v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{r}
implies

(6)

\displaystyle p_{u_{1}}(\mathbf{a})p_{u_{2}}(\mathbf{a})\cdots p_{u_{r}}(\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\leqslant\dots\leqslant i_{r},\\
i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[k]\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{r}}a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\dots a_{i_{r}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}\leqslant\dots\leqslant j_{r},\\
j_{1},\dots,j_{r}\in[k]\end{subarray}}\beta_{j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{r}}a_{j_{1}}a_{j_{2}}\dots a_{j_{r}}

\displaystyle=p_{v_{1}}(\mathbf{a})p_{v_{2}}(\mathbf{a})\dots p_{v_{r}}(\mathbf{a}).

Let observe that since
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{r})=\binom{k+r-1}{r}
then both
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{k}]_{r}
and
V^{r}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector spaces of the same dimension. Once fixed the ordered basis
(x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\dots x_{i_{r}}:i_{1}\leqslant\dots\leqslant i_{r},i_{j}\in[k],j\in[r])
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{k}]_{r}
and the ordered basis
(a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\dots a_{i_{r}}:i_{1}\leqslant\dots\leqslant i_{r},i_{j}\in[k],j\in[r])
of
V^{r}
, there exists a unique isomorphism

\displaystyle\phi\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{k}]_{r}

\displaystyle\to V^{r}

such that if

L(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\leqslant\dots\leqslant i_{r},\\
i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[k]\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{r}}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\dots x_{i_{r}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{k}]_{r},

then

\phi(L(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}))=L(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\leqslant\dots\leqslant i_{r},\\
i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[k]\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{r}}a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\dots a_{i_{r}}\in V^{r}.

Therefore, Equation (
6
) implies a polynomial identity, that is

p_{u_{1}}(x)p_{u_{2}}(x)\cdots p_{u_{r}}(x)=p_{v_{1}}(x)p_{v_{2}}(x)\cdots p_{v_{r}}(x).

Also, the polynomials
p_{u_{1}},p_{u_{2}},\ldots,p_{u_{r}},p_{v_{1}},p_{v_{2}},\ldots,p_{v_{r}}
are irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
since they have degree 1 and
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
is a unique factorization domain, then for every
i\in[r]
there exists
j\in[r]
and
\rho_{i,j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that

p_{u_{i}}(x)=\rho_{i,j}p_{v_{j}}(x).

Without loss of generality we may suppose
p_{u_{i}}(x)=\rho_{i,i}p_{v_{i}}(x)=\rho_{i}p_{v_{i}}(x)
for every
i\in[r]
, then

p_{u_{i}}(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{s\in[k]}\alpha_{i,s}x_{s}=\displaystyle\sum_{s\in[k]}\rho_{i}\beta_{i,s}x_{s}=\rho_{i}p_{v_{i}}(x)

and thus

\alpha_{i,s}=\rho_{i}\beta_{i,s}

Therefore,
u_{i}=\rho_{i}v_{i}
for every
i\in[k]
then
\{\{u_{1}\mathbb{F}_{q},\dots,u_{k}\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\}=\{\{v_{1}\mathbb{F}_{q},\dots,v_{k}\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\}
.
∎

Similarly to the Sidon space case, we define
max-span

r
-Sidon spaces those
r
-Sidon spaces that satisfy the equality in Proposition
3.11
. In Theorem
3.12
, we have proved that every subspace that satisfies the equality in Proposition
3.11
is an
r
-Sidon space and hence is a max-span
r
-Sidon space.

4.
Constructions of max span r-Sidon spaces

In the last result of the above section, we have seen that when
V^{r}
has dimension
\binom{k+r-1}{r}
it turns out that
V
is an
r
-Sidon space.
In this section, we provide some explicit constructions of such spaces obtained by generalizing Construction 21 and Construction 23 in
[
38
]
, by making use of classical combinatorial objects and with a more algebraic approach.
We start with the former approach by introducing the notion of
B_{r}
-set.
A
B_{r}
-set in an abelian group can be defined as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

A subset
S
of an abelian group
(G,+)
is called a
B_{r}
-set
if the sums of the elements of all multi-sets of size
r
of
S
are distinct.

In particular, for
r=2
we get the definition of Sidon set.

Remark 4.2
.

Let remember that
\binom{h+r-1}{r}
counts all the possible multi-subsets of order
r
of a multi-set of elements of order
h
, such that every element can be repeated up to
r
times in the same multi-subset, without caring about the order of the elements in each multi-subsets. Therefore, in such a case, two multi-subsets are distinct if and only if they differ for at least one element (taking into account the multiplicity).

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{S}:=\{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{k}\}\subseteq[h]
be a
B_{r}
-set in
\mathbb{Z}
, where
r\geq 2
, and let consider an integer
n>rh
and an element
\gamma
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
which does not belong to any proper subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Let
V:=\langle\{\gamma^{n_{i}}\}_{i\in[k]}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, then
V
is a max-span
r
-Sidon space.

Proof.

Let observe that
V:=\langle\{\gamma^{n_{i}}\}_{i\in[k]}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of dimension
k
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Indeed, by hypothesis
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\gamma)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, hence the elements
1,\gamma,\dots,\gamma^{n-1}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent. Since
n>2h
, then
\gamma^{n_{i}}\neq\gamma^{n_{j}}
for all
i,j\in[k],i\neq j
. Therefore,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)=k
.

In order to prove that
V
is a max-span Sidon space, by Theorem
3.12
it is sufficient to show that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{r})=\binom{k+r-1}{r}
.

Clearly,

V^{r}=\langle\{\gamma^{n_{i_{1}}+\dots+n_{i_{r}}}:i_{j}\in[k],j\in[r]\}\rangle.

Also, since
\mathcal{S}=\{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{k}\}
is a
B_{r}
-set then all the sums of any
r
-elements of
S
are distinct. Therefore, by Remark
4.2
we have that the set

\bar{\Gamma}=\{n_{i_{1}}+\dots+n_{i_{r}}:i_{j}\in[k],j\in[r],i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{r}\}

contains
\binom{k+r-1}{r}
distinct elements. Thus the exponents of the elements in
\Gamma
are distinct and, since
n_{i_{1}}+\dots+n_{i_{r}}\leq hr<n
for any
i_{j}\in[k],j\in[r]
, then the generating elements of
V^{r}
, i.e.

\gamma^{n_{i_{1}}+\dots+n_{i_{r}}}:i_{j}\in[k],j\in[r]

are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Therefore,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{r})=\binom{k+r-1}{r}
and
V
is a max-span Sidon space by Theorem
3.12
.
∎

Example 4.4
.

For any prime power
q
, let consider
S=\{0,1,4,16\}\subseteq[16]\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, which is a
B_{3}
-set in
\mathbb{Z}
. By taking
n=49>3\cdot 16=48
and
\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{49}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{7}}
, then by Theorem
3.12
we have that

V=\langle 1,\gamma,\gamma^{4},\gamma^{16}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}

is a max-span
3
-Sidon space in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{49}}
of dimension
4
.

We will now explore another construction of max-span
r
-Sidon spaces via a polynomial setting. We start with the following auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x),\ldots,f_{t}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\setminus\{0\}
and let
d=\max\{\deg f_{i}(x)\colon i\in[t]\}
. Then for any
n>d
and for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\gamma)
, the elements
f_{1}(\gamma),\ldots,f_{t}(\gamma)
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent if and only if the polynomials
f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{t}(x)
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent.

Proof.

Suppose that the elements
f_{1}(\gamma),\ldots,f_{t}(\gamma)
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent and let

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[t]}\alpha_{i}f_{i}(x)=0

where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
for every
i\in[t]
. Then, by evaluating in
\gamma
we get

(8)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[t]}\alpha_{i}f_{i}(\gamma)=0

and since
f_{1}(\gamma),\ldots,f_{t}(\gamma)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent, then
\alpha_{i}=0
for all
i\in[t]
.

Conversely, suppose that the polynomials
f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{t}(x)
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent and let

(9)

p(\gamma)=\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[t]}\alpha_{i}f_{i}(\gamma)=0.

where
p(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[t]}\alpha_{i}f_{i}(x)
. If
p(x)
is not the zero polynomial,
\gamma
would be the root of a polynomial of degree
\deg p(x)\leq d<n
and this is not possible since
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\gamma)
. Therefore,
p(x)
is the zero polynomial and so
\alpha_{i}=0
for all
i\in[t]
.
∎

By using an enough large set of irreducible polynomials over the base field, we are able to construct max-span
r
-Sidon spaces.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
k,r
be integers such that
1<r<k
. Let
\mathcal{I}:=\{p_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}(x):i_{j}\in[k],j\in[r],i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{r}\}
be a set of
\binom{k+r-1}{r}
distinct monic irreducible polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
\Delta=\max\{\deg(p_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}(x)):p_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}(x)\in\mathcal{I}\}
. For any
j\in[k]
, let

(10)

f_{j}(x):=\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i_{1},\dots,i_{r})\in[k]\times\dots\times[k]:\\
i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{r},\\
i_{h}\neq j\,\,\,\forall h\in[r]\end{subarray}}p_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}(x),

and, for
n>r\Delta\cdot\binom{k+r-2}{r}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
\gamma
does not belong to any proper subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Then
V:=\langle\{f_{j}(\gamma)\}_{j\in[k]}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
is a max-span
r
-Sidon space.

Proof.

First, let notice that
V
has dimension
k
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Indeed, let observe that since the maximum degree of any polynomial in
\mathcal{I}
is
\Delta
, then by Remark
4.2
it follows that

(11)

\displaystyle\deg(f_{s}(x))

\displaystyle=\deg\left(\displaystyle\prod_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\neq s}p_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}(x)\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[k]\times\dots\times[k],\\
i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{r},\\
i_{h}\neq s\,\,\,\forall h\in[r]\end{subarray}}\deg(p_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}(x))

\displaystyle\leq\binom{k-1+r-1}{r}\Delta<\frac{n}{r}<n

for all
s\in[k]
.

Hence, since
\gamma
does not belong to any proper subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, Lemma
4.5
implies that the elements
f_{1}(\gamma),\ldots,f_{k}(\gamma)
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent if and only if the polynomials
f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{k}(x)
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent. Then, suppose that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in[k]}\alpha_{j}f_{j}(x)=0.

Notice that for any
s\in[k]
,
p_{s,\dots,s}(x)
is an irreducible factor of the right hand of the equation but not of the left hand because by definition
\gcd(f_{s}(x),p_{s,\dots,s}(x))=1
and
\gcd(f_{j}(x),p_{s,\dots,s}(x))=p_{s,\dots,s}(x)
for all
j,s\in[k],j\neq s
. Then we get that
\alpha_{s}=0
for any
s\in[k]
. Therefore the polynomials
\{f_{j}(x):j\in[k]\}
are linearly independent and so by Lemma
4.5

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)=k
.

In order to prove the statement, by Theorem
3.12
, it is sufficient to show that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V^{r})=\binom{k+r-1}{r}
. Let consider the elements

V^{r}=\langle f_{i_{1}}(\gamma)\cdots f_{i_{r}}(\gamma):i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[k],i_{1}\leq\ldots\leq i_{r}\rangle.

We only have to prove that these elements are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Let notice that, since
\frac{n}{r}>\max\{\deg(f_{j}(x)):j\in[k]\}
, then by Remark
4.2
we have that

(12)

\displaystyle\deg\left(\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[k],\\
i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{r}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}f_{i_{1}}(x)\cdots f_{i_{r}}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\deg(f_{i_{1}}(x)\cdots f_{i_{r}}(x))\right\}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[k],i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{r}}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\displaystyle\sum_{j\in[r]}{\deg(f_{i_{j}}(x))}\right\}_{i_{j}\in[k],j\in[r],i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq r\max\{\deg(f_{i_{1}}(x)),\dots,\deg(f_{i_{r}}(x))\}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{j}\in[k],j\in[r],\\
i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{r}\end{subarray},}

\displaystyle\leq r\Delta

\displaystyle<n.

Therefore, since
\gamma
does not belong to any proper subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, then by Lemma
4.5
, it follows that the chosen generating elements of
V^{r}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if the related polynomials of
f_{i_{1}}(x)\cdots f_{i_{r}}(x)
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Thus, suppose that

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[k],\\

5.
New constructions from scattered polynomials

In this section, we want to provide further and explicit constructions of
r
-Sidon spaces, by using combinatorial and algebraic objects such as scattered polynomials and scattered subspaces. In particular, we prove that in sufficiently large extensions, every scattered polynomial defines an
r
-Sidon space. This will allow us to construct many non-equivalent examples of them. Then we exactly determine the dimension over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of the
r
-span of a Sidon space defined by a monomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
where
n=kt
,
t\geq r+1
in order to compare this dimension with the bound of Theorem
3.8
. We can conclude the section by considering the subspace defined by the trace function. We prove that this subspace is not a Sidon space and we compute the dimension of its
r
-span.

Let start with the following definition.

A
linearized polynomial
(or
q
-
polynomial
) is a polynomial of the form

f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{t}a_{i}x^{q^{i}},\quad a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{k}}.

If
f
is nonzero, the
q
-
degree
of
f
will be the maximum
i
such that
a_{i}\neq 0
.

The set of linearized polynomials forms an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-algebra with the usual addition, the scalar multiplication by elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the composition of polynomials. We denote this
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-algebra by
\mathcal{L}_{k,q}
.
The elements of the quotient algebra
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{k,q}
obtained from
\mathcal{L}_{k,q}
modulo the two-sided ideal
(x^{q^{k}}-x)
are represented by linearized polynomials of
q
-degree less than
k
, i.e.

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{k,q}=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}f_{i}x^{q^{i}},\quad f_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{k}}\right\}.

It is well-known that the above
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-algebra is isomorphic to the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear endomorphisms of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.
This allows us to identify a linearized polynomial with the endomorphism it defines and so we will speak of
kernel
and
rank
of a
q
-polynomial
f
meaning by this the kernel and rank of the corresponding endomorphism, denoted by
\ker(f)
and
\mathrm{rk}(f)
, respectively.

We refer to Chapter 3 in
[
24
]
and
[
44
]
for some references on linearized polynomials.

Sheekey in
[
40
]
introduced the notion of scattered polynomials in order to find new examples of optimal codes in the rank metric.

Definition 5.1
.

A polynomial
f\in{\mathcal{L}}_{k,q}
is said to be

if for any
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}^{*}
,
f(a)/a=f(b)/b
implies that
a
and
b
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-proportional.

Let
f\in\mathcal{L}_{k,q}
and
U_{f}=\{(x,f(x))\colon x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{k}}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}^{2}
be the
k

-subspace defined by the graph of

is scattered if

is a

-subspace of

(17)

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(S_{a,f})=1,

Our aim now is to construct

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
q
be a prime power and let

and
r
be two positive integers. Let

for some

, and let

Proof.

Clearly,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V_{f,\gamma})=k
. Now, let

a_{1}=u_{1}+f(u_{1})\gamma,\ldots,a_{r}=u_{r}+f(u_{r})\gamma,

and

b_{1}=v_{1}+f(v_{1})\gamma,\ldots,b_{r}=v_{r}+f(v_{r})\gamma,

for any nonzero

The above equation may be read as

\prod_{i=1}^{r}(u_{i}+f(u_{i})\gamma)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(v_{i}+f(v_{i})\gamma),

and Lemma

implies that the following polynomials

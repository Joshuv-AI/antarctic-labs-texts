1.
Introduction

The Selberg trace formula
[
7
]
is one of the most celebrated mathematical results of the past century. In its original form, it relates the spectrum of the Laplacian on a hyperbolic surface to lengths of closed geodesics on the surface. It can be viewed as a non-abelian generalization of the well-known Poisson summation formula in Fourier analysis and has found numerous applications in many fields, for example in number theory and the theory of automorphic forms (see Sarnak
[
6
]
).

The Selberg trace formula deals with surfaces which can be represented as double cosets of the form
\Gamma\backslash\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})/\SO_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. It is a natural question to work out the trace formula for higher-dimensional matrix groups. To quote Dennis Hejhal: “Of course, we won’t really understand the trace formula until it is written down for
\SL_{4}(\mathbb{Z})
.” Dorothy Wallace
[
10
]
has worked out the explicit details of the trace formula for
\SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\SL_{3}(\mathbb{R})/\SO_{3}(\mathbb{R})
but the four-dimensional case still remains mysterious. A further generalization is the so-called Arthur-Selberg trace formula
[
3
]
, which plays an important role in the Langlands program.

Selberg type trace formulae have been derived for discrete spaces like graphs as well. By realizing a
k
-regular graph as a quotient of the infinite
k
-regular tree, Ahumada
[
1
]
found such a formula for
k
-regular graphs. Audrey Terras
[
9
]
introduced the concept of finite upper half-planes as a finite analogue of the Poincaré upper half-plane model where the complex number field is
replaced by a quadratic extension of a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
q=p^{r}
is a prime power. She constructed a family of Ramanujan graphs
[
5
]
using these upper-half planes and developed a representation-theoretic trace formula on
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\backslash\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})/K
, where
K
is the finite analogue of
\SO_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

The aim of this paper is to present an analogous trace formula for
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Our main result in this paper is as follows. Consider the pre-trace formula

\sum_{\pi\in\hat{G}}m(\pi,\rho)\Tr(\hat{f}(\pi))=\sum_{\{\gamma\}\in C_{\Gamma}}\frac{|G_{\gamma}|}{|\Gamma_{\gamma}|}I_{G}(f,\gamma),

(1.1)

where
G
is a finite group,
\Gamma
is a subgroup of
G
,

\rho=\Ind_{\Gamma}^{G}1
is the induced representation of the trivial representation of
\Gamma
, and
I_{G}(f,\gamma)
is the orbital sum of
f
at
\gamma
. These terms are defined in Section 3 and a proof of the pre-trace formula is recalled there as well. Note that (1.1) can be viewed as a representation-theoretic analogue of Selberg’s trace formula
[
7
,
9
]
: the left hand side can be thought of as the spectral side, summing over the irreducible representations of
G
and their multiplicities in
\rho
, while the right hand side can be interpreted as the geometric side, summing over conjugacy classes in
G
.
In this paper, to obtain a trace formula for the finite upper half-space we take
G=\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\Gamma=\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Also,
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
is chosen to be a
K-
bi-invariant function (
K
is the stabilizer of
p_{0}
as above), i.e.
\forall k,h\in K
and
\forall x\in G
we have
f(kxh)=f(x)
. One can also think of
f
as a function on
G/K\simeq\mathbb{H}_{q}
which is invariant under left multiplication by elements in
K
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we define an analogue, denoted
\mathbb{H}_{q},
of the upper half plane for the group
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
by considering a cubic extension of the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We define an action of
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
on
\mathbb{H}_{q}
and this sets the stage for our discrete trace formula. In Section 3, we recall a standard pre-trace formula for finite groups. In Sections 4-6, we compute each term on the right hand side of the pre-trace formula (
3.4
) (the so called geometric side) using the explicit description of conjugacy classes of
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
given in
[
2
]
. The conjugacy classes are separated into central, hyperbolic, parabolic, and elliptic terms. Each of these types of terms are explicitly identified. It would be helpful to have a more conceptual understanding of these computations. In Section 7, we give some simple examples of the application of our formula in which the left side of the pre-trace formula is known
a priori
. In Sections 8-9, we calculate the left side of the pre-trace formula by computing the character of the induced representation
\rho=\Ind_{\Gamma}^{G}1
and then using that to compute its decomposition into irreducible representations in the case that
2,3\nmid n
. The calculation of the decomposition in the other cases is similar but involve more cases and hence they are not incorporated in the arXiv version of this paper. They are available on demand.

2.
A finite upper half-space

To generalize Terras’s results to the
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
case, we must first discuss the generalization of the finite upper half-space. Let
p
be a prime number and
q=p^{n}
for a positive integer
n
. The definition of the upper half space
\mathbb{H}_{q}
comes from considering the cubic extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The construction was first given by Martinez in
[
4
]
. Here we describe it explicitly for our special case of

\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

\mathbb{F}_{q}
has a cubic nonresidue if and only if
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.

Proof.

Let
q=p^{n}
. Consider the cube map
x\mapsto x^{3}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. This map has as kernel the subgroup
\mu_{3}
consisting of the cube roots of unity in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Let
\alpha
be a generator of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Then if
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, we have
\mu_{3}=\{1,\alpha^{(q-1)/3},\alpha^{2(q-1)/3}\}
, in which case there are
(q-1)/3
cubic residues. Otherwise,
\mu_{3}=\{1\}
, and so each element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
is a cubic residue.
∎

From now on, we assume that
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
. Take a cubic nonresidue
\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and consider the cubic extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}\left(\sqrt[3]{\delta}\right)\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
. A basis for
\mathbb{F}_{q}\left(\sqrt[3]{\delta}\right)/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is
\{1,\delta^{1/3},\delta^{2/3}\}
. For an element
\alpha
, we will write
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
to denote its components, i.e.,

\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\delta^{1/3}+\alpha_{3}\delta^{2/3}.

We define

\mathbb{H}_{q}=\left\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(\sqrt[3]{\delta})\times\mathbb{F}_{q}(\sqrt[3]{\delta}):\begin{vmatrix}\alpha_{2}&\alpha_{3}\\
\beta_{2}&\beta_{3}\end{vmatrix}\neq 0\right\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

The following map defines an action of
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
on
\mathbb{H}_{q}:

\begin{bmatrix}a&b&c\\
d&e&f\\
r&s&t\end{bmatrix}(\alpha,\beta)=\left(\frac{a\alpha+b\beta+c}{r\alpha+s\beta+t},\frac{d\alpha+e\beta+f}{r\alpha+s\beta+t}\right).

Proof.

The compatibility of the action with multiplication in
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
follows from the fact that
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
has an action on the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}})
. So, we need to check that the action is closed in
\mathbb{H}_{q}
. This is a routine verification, which can be found in
Appendix A
.
∎

Our distinguished point will be
p_{0}=(\delta^{2/3},\delta^{1/3})\in\mathbb{H}_{q}
. The action of
\Aff_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\leq\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is transitive on
\mathbb{H}_{q}
, where

\Aff_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\coloneqq\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b&c\\
d&e&f\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\right\}.

With this action, we calculate that

K\coloneqq\stab(p_{0})=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&c\delta&b\delta\\
b&a&c\delta\\
c&b&a\end{bmatrix}\right\}.

Indeed, we find that

K\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{F}_{q}(\delta^{1/3})^{\times}:\begin{bmatrix}a&c\delta&b\delta\\
b&a&c\delta\\
c&b&a\end{bmatrix}\mapsto a+b\delta^{1/3}+c\delta^{2/3}.

Also, we have the homogeneous space
\mathbb{H}_{q}\simeq\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})/K
. As a quick check note that we have
|\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=(q^{3}-1)(q^{3}-q)(q^{3}-q^{2})
and
|K|=q^{3}-1
(from the canonical isomorphism), so we should have
|\mathbb{H}_{q}|
equal to

\frac{(q^{3}-1)(q^{3}-q)(q^{3}-q^{2})}{q^{3}-1}=(q^{2}-1)(q-1)q^{3}.

Indeed, from the definition we have

|\mathbb{H}_{q}|=|\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|\cdot q^{2}=(q^{2}-1)(q^{2}-q)q^{2}=(q^{2}-1)(q-1)q^{3}.

Remark 2.3
.

Observe that the above construction is a natural generalization of the finite upper half-plane defined by Terras
[
9
]
(here
\delta
is a nonsquare in the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
):

H_{q}=\left\{x+y\sqrt{\delta}:x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q},y\neq 0\right\}.

The condition
y\neq 0
is equivalent to
1
and
x+y\sqrt{\delta}
being linearly independent elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}\left(\sqrt{\delta}\right)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

3.
Trace formula

3.1.
The pre-trace formula

Our trace formula relies on interpretation of a known result, the pre-trace formula. We now derive the pre-trace formula and contextualize it in our setting of the group
G=\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. The idea behind the pre-trace formula is to compute the trace of a specific operator in two different bases. We may construct the relevant information for this operator as follows:

Suppose that
G
is a finite group and
\Gamma\leq G
is a subgroup. Let
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
be a complex-valued function on
G
. For a representation
\kappa:G\to\GL(V)
of
G
(where
V
is a finite-dimensional complex vector space endowed with a linear
G
-action), define the Fourier transform of
f
at
\kappa
by:

\hat{f}(\kappa)=\sum_{g\in G}f(g)\kappa\left(g^{-1}\right).

Observe that the Fourier transform
\hat{f}(\kappa)
is
\End V
-valued. The pre-trace formula is now obtained by computing the trace of
\hat{f}(\rho)
in two different bases with
\rho=\Ind_{\Gamma}^{G}1
. First, we can decompose
\rho
as a direct sum of irreducible representations of
G
to get:

\Tr\left(\hat{f}(\rho)\right)=\sum_{\pi\in\hat{G}}m(\pi,\rho)\Tr\left(\hat{f}(\pi)\right).

(3.1)

Here
\hat{G}
is the set of irreducible representations of
G
and
m(\pi,\rho)
is the multiplicity of the irreducible representation
\pi\in\hat{G}
in
\rho
. Let

V=\{f:G\to\mathbb{C}\,|\,f(\gamma g)=f(g)\quad\forall\gamma\in\Gamma,g\in G\}=L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G).

As
G
acts on linearly on
V
by right multiplication, the corresponding representation is precisely
\rho=\Ind_{\Gamma}^{G}1
. More concretely, for
\phi\in V
,
g,x\in G
we have

\left[\rho(g)\phi\right](x)=\phi(xg).

Thus:

\left[\hat{f}(\rho)\phi\right](x)=\sum_{y\in G}f(y)\phi\left(xy^{-1}\right)=\sum_{u\in G}f\left(u^{-1}x\right)\phi(u).

Further, as
\phi
is
\Gamma
-invariant,

\left[\hat{f}(\rho)\phi\right](x)=\sum_{y\in\Gamma\backslash G}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f\left(y^{-1}\gamma x\right)\phi(y).

Now we are ready to compute the trace of
\hat{f}(\rho)
in another way: consider the indicator basis
\left\{\delta_{y}\right\}_{y\in\Gamma\backslash G}
of
V
1
1

1

For
y\in\Gamma\backslash G
,
\delta_{y}
is the function on
\Gamma\backslash G
which outputs
1
if the input is
y
and
0
otherwise.
; with respect to this basis,
\hat{f}(\rho)
is represented by a matrix where the entry corresponding to
x,y\in\Gamma\backslash G
is
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f\left(y^{-1}\gamma x\right)
. Therefore,

\Tr\left[\hat{f}(\rho)\right]=\sum_{x\in\Gamma\backslash G}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f\left(x^{-1}\gamma x\right).

(3.2)

We can rewrite the right-hand side as a sum over conjugacy classes in
G
. Let:

\Gamma_{\gamma}=\left\{x\in\Gamma\,|\,x^{-1}\gamma x=\gamma\right\}=\text{centralizer of }\gamma\text{ in }\Gamma

G_{\gamma}=\left\{x\in G\,|\,x^{-1}\gamma x=\gamma\right\}=\text{centralizer of }\gamma\text{ in }G

\{\gamma\}=\left\{x^{-1}\gamma x\,|\,x\in G\right\}=\text{conjugacy class of }\gamma\text{ in }G

C_{\Gamma}=\text{set of conjugacy classes in }\Gamma.

Note that there is a 1-to-1 correspondence between right cosets
\Gamma_{\gamma}\backslash\Gamma
and elements of
\{\gamma\}
given by
\Gamma_{\gamma}x\mapsto x^{-1}\gamma x
. Since conjugacy classes partition
\Gamma
we can rewrite the inner sum as:

\sum_{x\in\Gamma\backslash G}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(x^{-1}\gamma x)=\sum_{x\in\Gamma\backslash G}\sum_{\{\gamma\}\in C_{\Gamma}}\sum_{u\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\backslash\Gamma}f\left(x^{-1}u^{-1}\gamma ux\right).

Set
y=ux
, then we can rewrite this as a sum over right cosets
y\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\backslash G
:

\sum_{x\in\Gamma\backslash G}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(x^{-1}\gamma x)=\sum_{y\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\backslash G}\sum_{\{\gamma\}\in C_{\Gamma}}f(y^{-1}\gamma y).

Observe that
\Gamma_{\gamma}\leq G_{\gamma}
hence we can write
y=ts
for
t\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\backslash G_{\gamma}
and
s\in G_{\gamma}\backslash G
:

\sum_{x\in\Gamma\backslash G}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(x^{-1}\gamma x)=\sum_{t\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\backslash G_{\gamma}}\sum_{s\in G_{\gamma}\backslash G}\sum_{\{\gamma\}\in C_{\Gamma}}f\left(s^{-1}t^{-1}\gamma ts\right)=\sum_{\{\gamma\}\in C_{\Gamma}}\frac{|G_{\gamma}|}{|\Gamma_{\gamma}|}\sum_{s\in G_{\gamma}\backslash G}f(s^{-1}\gamma s).

(3.3)

Define the orbital sum
I_{G}(f,\gamma)
of
f
at
\gamma
to be:

I_{G}(f,\gamma)\coloneqq\sum_{s\in G_{\gamma}\backslash G}f(s^{-1}\gamma s).

The pre-trace formula now follows from combining (
3.1
)
-
(
3.3
):

\sum_{\pi\in\hat{G}}m(\pi,\rho)\Tr(\hat{f}(\pi))=\sum_{\{\gamma\}\in C_{\Gamma}}\frac{|G_{\gamma}|}{|\Gamma_{\gamma}|}I_{G}(f,\gamma),

(3.4)

where
\rho=\Ind_{\Gamma}^{G}1
and
I_{G}(f,\gamma)
is the orbital sum of
f
at
\gamma
as defined above. Note that (
3.4
) can be viewed as a representation-theoretic analogue of Selberg’s trace formula
[
7
,
9
]
: the left hand side can be thought of as the spectral side, summing over the irreducible representations of
G
and their multiplicities in
\rho
, while the right hand side can be interpreted as the geometric side, summing over conjugacy classes in
G
. For further reference see
[
9
, Ch. 22]
.

To obtain the trace formula for the finite upper half-space we take
G=\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\Gamma=\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Also,
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
is chosen to be a
K-
bi-invariant function (
K
is the stabilizer of
p_{0}
as above), i.e.
\forall k,h\in K
and
\forall x\in G
we have
f(kxh)=f(x)
. One can also think of
f
as a function on
G/K\simeq\mathbb{H}_{q}
which is invariant under left multiplication by elements in
K
.

Remark 3.5
.

The left-hand side of (
3.4
) can be expressed solely in terms of the characters
\chi_{\pi}
of the group
G
:

\begin{split}\Tr\left[\hat{f}(\pi)\right]&=\Tr\left[\sum_{g\in G}f(g)\pi\left(g^{-1}\right)\right]=\sum_{g\in G}f(g)\Tr\left[\pi\left(g^{-1}\right)\right]=\\
&=\sum_{g\in G}f(g)\chi_{\pi}\left(g^{-1}\right)=\sum_{g\in G}f(g)\overline{\chi_{\pi}(g)}=\langle f,\chi_{\pi}\rangle.\end{split}

Here
\langle x,y\rangle
denotes the usual Hermitian inner product on the complex vector space
L^{2}(G)
:

\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{g\in G}x(g)\overline{y(g)}

We will, in Sections 4-6, compute each term in the sum on the right hand side of the pre-trace formula (
3.4
) using the explicit description of conjugacy classes of
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
given in
[
2
]
. The conjugacy classes are separated into the cases of central, hyperbolic, parabolic, and elliptic terms. It is helpful to introduce one more tool that will help us simplify these computations.

3.2.
Double cosets and fundamental domains

To calculate the orbital sums it will be convenient to identify
G_{\gamma}\backslash G
with
G_{\gamma}\backslash G/K\times K/Z
(when possible), where
Z
is the center of
G
, the subgroup of diagonal matrices
\{aI:a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\}
. Let
\{t_{i}\}_{i=1}^{l}
be representatives of double cosets in
G_{\gamma}\backslash G/K
and consider the map:

\begin{split}G_{\gamma}\backslash G/K\times K/Z&\to G_{\gamma}\backslash G\\
(G_{\gamma}t_{i}K,kZ)&\mapsto G_{\gamma}t_{i}k.\end{split}

We first show that this map is well-defined and onto. If
z\in Z
then
G_{\gamma}t_{i}kz=G_{\gamma}zt_{i}k=G_{\gamma}t_{i}k
because
z\in G_{\gamma}
as
z
commutes with every element of
G
. Now let
G_{\gamma}x\in G\backslash G_{\gamma}
, then since double cosets
G_{\gamma}t_{i}K
partition
G
and they are unions of right cosets
G_{\gamma}y
we can find a representative
t_{i}\in G
and
k\in K
such that
G_{\gamma}x=G_{\gamma}t_{i}k
.

As for injectivity, let
t_{i},t_{j}
be representatives of two double cosets in
G_{\gamma}\backslash G/K
and
k,k^{\prime}
be representatives of two left cosets in
K/Z
. Suppose that
G_{\gamma}t_{i}k=G_{\gamma}t_{j}k^{\prime}.
Since double cosets are either disjoint or identical this is only possible if
t_{i}=t_{j}
, let
t=t_{i}
. So, we have,

G_{\gamma}=G_{\gamma}tk^{\prime}k^{-1}t^{-1},

(3.6)

which happens if and only if
tk^{\prime}k^{-1}t^{-1}\in G_{\gamma}
, equivalently, if
k^{\prime}k^{-1}\in tG_{\gamma}t^{-1}\cap K
. This can also be stated as
kH=k^{\prime}H
where
H=tG_{\gamma}t^{-1}\cap K
. Note that
Z\leq K
and
Z\leq tG_{\gamma}t^{-1}=G_{t\gamma t^{-1}}
as
Z
is the intersection of all centralizers in
G
, hence we always have
Z\leq K\cap tG_{\gamma}t^{-1}=H
. If
Z=H
, then (
3.6
) is equivalent to
kZ=k^{\prime}Z
, which implies that the above map is injective. In our specific example, the centralizers
G_{\gamma}
contain no non-central elements conjugate to some matrix in
K
unless
\gamma
itself is conjugate to a member of
K
. (This can be seen e.g. by taking simple representatives
\gamma
of conjugacy classes in
G
and looking at the characteristic polynomials and eigenvalues of elements in
G_{\gamma}
, which are preserved by conjugation.)

Thus we have two cases:

Case 1:

\gamma
is not conjugate to an element of
K
. Then we have:

I_{G}(f,\gamma)=\sum_{s\in G_{\gamma}\backslash G}f(s^{-1}\gamma s)=\sum_{t\in G_{\gamma}\backslash G/K}\sum_{k\in K/Z}f((tk)^{-1}\gamma tk)=|K/Z|\sum_{t\in G_{\gamma}\backslash G/K}f(t^{-1}\gamma t).

(The last equality follows from the fact that
f
is
K-
bi-invariant.) We can identify
G_{\gamma}\backslash G/K
with
G_{\gamma}\backslash\mathbb{H}_{q}
. In order to calculate orbital sums we will find fundamental domains for the action of
G_{\gamma}
on
\mathbb{H}_{q}
, i.e. subsets of
\mathbb{H}_{q}
which contain exactly one element from each
G_{\gamma}
-orbit.

Case 2:

\gamma
is conjugate to an element of
K
.
In this case, the above map is not necessarily injective. However,
G_{\gamma}=G\text{ or }K
depending on whether
\gamma
is conjugate to a central element of
K
or not. Then either
G_{\gamma}\backslash G
is trivial or it can be identified with
\mathbb{H}_{q}
.

4.
Central and Hyperbolic Terms

4.1.
Central terms

Let
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&0&0\\
0&a&0\\
0&0&a\end{bmatrix}
for
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
. In this case,
G_{\gamma}=G
and
\Gamma_{\gamma}=\Gamma
. So,

I_{G}(f,\gamma)=f(p_{0})
and the total contribution from such
\gamma
is

\frac{|G|}{|\Gamma|}f(p_{0})\cdot(p-1)=\frac{(q^{3}-1)(q^{2}-1)(q-1)q^{3}}{(p^{3}-1)(p^{2}-1)p^{3}}f(p_{0}).

4.2.
Hyperbolic terms of the first kind

Let
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&0&0\\
0&a&0\\
0&0&b\end{bmatrix},a\neq b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}.
Here

G_{\gamma}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}x&y&0\\
w&z&0\\
0&0&t\end{bmatrix}\right\}\simeq\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}

and similarly for
\Gamma_{\gamma}
.

A fundamental domain for the action of
G_{\gamma}
is given by

\displaystyle G_{\gamma}\backslash\mathbb{H}_{q}=\{(u+\delta^{1/3},v+\delta^{2/3}):u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}.

(4.1)

We verify this in the next proposition for the sake of illustration. In the next sections, the verificiation of the fundamental domains is relegated to
Appendix A
.

Proposition 4.2
.

A fundamental domain for
G_{\gamma}
is given by (
4.1
).

Proof.

First to show the uniqueness of each representative, suppose there exists
m\in G_{\gamma}
such that

m\cdot(u_{1}+\delta^{1/3},v_{1}+\delta^{2/3})=(u_{2}+\delta^{1/3},v_{2}+\delta^{2/3}).

Then we deduce that
y=0=w
, and
x=t=z
. So,
u_{1}=u_{2}
and
v_{1}=v_{2}
.

Next, to show the completeness of this fundamental domain, take an arbitrary element
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{H}_{q}
. We wish to find
m\in G_{\gamma},u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
m\cdot(u+\delta^{1/3},v+\delta^{2/3})=(\alpha,\beta)
. Set
x=\alpha_{2},y=\alpha_{3},w=\beta_{2},z=\beta_{3}
, and
t=1
. Then we see
u,v
is a solution to

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{2}&\alpha_{3}\\
\beta_{2}&\beta_{3}\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}u\\
v\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{1}\\
\beta_{1}\end{bmatrix},

which has a solution since the left matrix is invertible.
∎

Thus,

\displaystyle I_{G}(f,\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in G_{\gamma}\backslash G/K}\sum_{u\in K/\{aI\}}f((tu)^{-1}\gamma(tu))

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in G_{\gamma}\backslash\mathbb{H}_{q}}\frac{|K|}{|\{aI\}|}f(t^{-1}\gamma t)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{3}-1}{q-1}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}f\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&1&-y\\
1&0&-x\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\gamma\begin{bmatrix}0&1&x\\
1&0&y\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}p_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=(q^{2}+q+1)\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}f\left(\begin{bmatrix}a&0&(a-b)y\\
0&a&(a-b)x\\
0&0&b\end{bmatrix}p_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=(q^{2}+q+1)\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}f\left((x+\frac{a}{b}\delta^{2/3},y+\frac{a}{b}\delta^{1/3})\right).

Let us define the
horocycle transform

Hf:\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{C}
of
f\in L^{2}(K\backslash G/K)
by

Hf(\kappa)\coloneqq\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\sum_{\xi\in\{\kappa\}}f\left(\begin{bmatrix}\xi_{11}&\xi_{12}&x\\
\xi_{21}&\xi_{22}&y\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}p_{0}\right),

where the inner sum runs over
\xi\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in the conjugacy class of
\kappa
.
So, this hyperbolic term is equal to

\displaystyle(q^{2}+q+1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}\frac{(q^{2}-1)(q^{2}-q)(q-1)}{(p^{2}-1)(p^{2}-p)(p-1)}\cdot Hf\left(\frac{a}{b}I\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{3}-1)(q^{2}-1)(q^{2}-q)}{(p^{2}-1)(p^{2}-p)(p-1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\\
a\neq 1\end{subarray}}(p-1)\cdot Hf(aI)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{3}-1)(q^{2}-1)(q^{2}-q)}{(p^{2}-1)(p^{2}-p)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\\
a\neq 1\end{subarray}}Hf(aI).

We have shown the full computation for the orbital sums for this case both to demonstrate the computational technique and to motivate and introduce the horocycle transform. For the next cases we omit detailed computations for orbital sums, but included some in
Appendix A
for the curious reader.

4.3.
Hyperbolic terms of the second kind

Let
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&0&0\\
0&b&0\\
0&0&c\end{bmatrix}
with
a,b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
distinct. Here
G_{\gamma}
is the set of diagonal matrices and:

\displaystyle G_{\gamma}\backslash\mathbb{H}_{q}

\displaystyle=\{(x+\delta^{1/3}+y\delta^{2/3},r+s\delta^{1/3}+\delta^{2/3}):ys\neq 1\}\sqcup\{(x+\delta^{2/3},r+\delta^{1/3}+s\delta^{2/3}):s\neq 0\}

\displaystyle\qquad\sqcup\{(x+y\delta^{1/3}+\delta^{2/3},r+\delta^{1/3}):y\neq 0\}\sqcup\{(x+\delta^{2/3},y+\delta^{1/3})\}

We have

I_{G}(f,\gamma)=(q^{2}+q+1)\cdot Hf\left(\begin{bmatrix}a/c&0\\
0&b/c\end{bmatrix}\right),

where
a,b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
comes from a change of variables outlined in
Appendix A
.

The total contribution from the second hyperbolic term is:

\frac{1}{6}(q^{2}+q+1)\frac{(q-1)^{3}}{(p-1)^{3}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\\
a\neq b\neq c\end{subarray}}Hf\left(\begin{bmatrix}a/c&0\\
0&b/c\end{bmatrix}\right)=\frac{1}{6}(q^{2}+q+1)\frac{(q-1)^{3}}{(p-1)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\backslash\{1\}\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}Hf\left(\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\

0&b\end{bmatrix}\right).

G_{\gamma}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}d&y&x\\

.

A fundamental domain for
G_{\gamma}
is

G_{\gamma}\backslash\mathbb{H}_{q}=\{(u\delta^{1/3},v\delta^{1/3}+\delta^{2/3}):u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q},u\neq 0\}\sqcup\{(\delta^{1/3}+u\delta^{2/3},\delta^{1/3}):u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\}.

Proof.

See

.

Following the computations outlined in

I_{G}(f,\gamma)=(q^{2}+q+1)\left(Hf(I)-f(p_{0})\right).

Parabolic terms of the second kind

.

A fundamental domain for
B

B\backslash\mathbb{H}_{q}=\{(\delta^{1/3},v\delta^{1/3}+\delta^{2/3}):v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\sqcup\{(\delta^{2/3},\delta^{1/3})\}.

We find the orbital sum (see

I_{G}(f,\gamma)=(q^{2}+q+1)\left(f\left(\delta^{2/3}+\delta^{1/3},\delta^{1/3}+1\right)+\sum_{v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}f\left((1-v)\delta^{2/3}-v^{2}\delta^{1/3}+1,\delta^{2/3}+(v+1)\delta^{1/3}\right)\right).

\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&0&0\\

I_{G}(f,\gamma)=(q^{2}+q+1)\cdot Hf\left(\begin{bmatrix}a/b&1\\

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\\

\displaystyle=\frac{q(q-1)(q^{3}-1)}{p(p-1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\\

6.
Elliptic Terms

6.1.
Elliptic terms of the first kind

Irreducible
\gamma
. The characteristic polynomial of such a
\gamma
is irreducible in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
. Recall that
q=p^{n}
. Depending on whether
n\equiv 0\pmod{3}
, there are two cases to consider.

•

n\equiv 0\pmod{3}
. In this case, we have the tower of field extensions
\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p^{3}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
. So,
\gamma
is similar to a diagonal matrix in
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. But that is not the case in
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, which is why this case is different from the second hyperbolic term. Therefore
G_{\gamma}
is the subgroup of diagonal matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
while
\Gamma_{\gamma}
is
K
with entries in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, i.e.
|\Gamma_{\gamma}|=p^{3}-1
. Since
G_{\gamma}
is the same, we can reuse the fundamental domain for
G_{\gamma}
from earlier, in the second hyperbolic case.

Suppose
\gamma
is similar to
\bigg(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi_{1}&0&0\\
0&\xi_{2}&0\\
0&0&\xi_{3}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\bigg)
in
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, where
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{3}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Similarly as for the second hyperbolic term the orbital sum will be:

I_{G}(f,\gamma)=|K/Z|\cdot Hf\left(\begin{bmatrix}\xi_{1}/\xi_{3}&0\\
0&\xi_{2}/\xi_{3}\end{bmatrix}\right).

Since
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}
are Galois conjugates without loss of generality
\xi_{1}=\xi_{3}^{p}
and
\xi_{2}=\xi_{3}^{p^{2}}
. The total contribution of the first elliptic terms is therefore:

\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{(q-1)^{3}}{p^{3}-1}\cdot\frac{q^{3}-1}{q-1}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{3}}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}Hf\left(\begin{bmatrix}\xi^{p-1}&0\\
0&\xi^{p^{2}-1}\end{bmatrix}\right).

(The factor of
\frac{1}{3}
comes from the fact that each
\gamma
is counted three times in the sum.)

•

n\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}
. In this case, there is no cubic extension intermediate to
\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and so
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\delta^{1/3})
is the minimal field (of course, in the sense of containment, not size) over which the characteristic polynomial of
\gamma
has a root. Suppose an eigenvalue of
\gamma
is
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\delta^{1/3}+\alpha_{3}\delta^{2/3}
with eigenvector
x_{1}+x_{2}\delta^{1/3}+x_{3}\delta^{2/3}
, where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
. Then,

\displaystyle\gamma x_{1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\alpha_{3}\delta x_{2}+\alpha_{2}\delta x_{3},

\displaystyle\gamma x_{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{2}x_{1}+\alpha_{1}x_{2}+\alpha_{3}\delta x_{3},

\displaystyle\gamma x_{3}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{3}x_{1}+\alpha_{2}x_{2}+\alpha_{1}x_{3}.

Thus, viewed in
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),

\gamma
is similar to a matrix in
K
. Conversely, non-diagonal matrices in
K
give us irreducible elements in
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Further, in this case
G_{\gamma}
is precisely
K
, and so
G_{\gamma}\backslash G=\mathbb{H}_{q}
. After identifying elements
y\in\mathbb{H}_{q}
with elements
y=\begin{bmatrix}a&b&c\\
d&e&f\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\in\Aff_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and writing
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&\varepsilon\delta&\beta\delta\\
\beta&\alpha&\varepsilon\delta\\
\varepsilon&\beta&\alpha\end{bmatrix}
, we can compute:

\displaystyle I_{G}(f,\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{H}_{q}}f(y^{-1}\gamma y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b,c,d,e,f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}f\left(\frac{1}{bd-ae}\begin{bmatrix}-e&b&ce-bf\\
d&-a&af-cd\\
0&0&bd-ae\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&\varepsilon\delta&\beta\delta\\
\beta&\alpha&\varepsilon\delta\\
\varepsilon&\beta&\alpha\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a&b&c\\
d&e&f\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}p_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b,c,d,e,f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}f\left(\frac{1}{bd-ae}\begin{bmatrix}x_{11}&x_{12}&x_{13}\\
x_{21}&x_{22}&x_{23}\\
x_{31}&x_{32}&x_{33}\end{bmatrix}p_{0}\right),

where the entries of the matrix, by column, are as follows:

\diamond

x_{11}=\alpha(bd-ae)+\beta(ab+cde-bdf)+\varepsilon(ace-abf-de\delta)

\diamond

x_{21}=\beta(adf-a^{2}-cd^{2})+\varepsilon(a^{2}f-acd+d^{2}\delta)

\diamond

x_{31}=\beta d+\varepsilon a

\diamond

x_{12}=\beta(ce^{2}-bef+b^{2})+\varepsilon(bce-b^{2}f-e^{2}\delta)

\diamond

x_{22}=\alpha(bd-ae)+\beta(aef-cde-ab)+\varepsilon(abf-bcd+de\delta)

\diamond

x_{32}=\beta e+\varepsilon b

\diamond

x_{13}=\beta(cef+bc-bf^{2}-e\delta)

\diamond

x_{23}=\beta(af^{2}-cdf-ac+d\delta)+\varepsilon(acf-ac^{2}+df\delta-a\delta)

\diamond

x_{33}=\alpha+\beta f+\varepsilon c

Unfortunately, there does not seem to be an obvious way to simplify this expression further. When computing this case for a specific field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, it seems that applying this formula may not be more helpful than directly computing.

6.2.
Elliptic terms of the second kind

\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&b&0\\
c&d&0\\
0&0&e\end{bmatrix}
, where
g=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}
is irreducible, and
e\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
. Also,

\Gamma_{\gamma}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\xi&0\\
b&a&0\\
0&0&c\end{bmatrix}:\,c(a^{2}-b^{2}\xi)\neq 0\right\},

where
\xi
is a nonsquare in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Once again, depending on whether
n\equiv 0\pmod{2}
, i.e. whether
n
is even or odd, there are two cases to consider.

•

n
is even. In this case,
\mathbb{F}_{p}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\gamma
is diagonalizable in
\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
G_{\gamma}
is a subgroup of diagonal matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, so the fundamental domain
G_{\gamma}\backslash\mathbb{H}_{q}
is the same as for the second hyperbolic case.

Suppose
\gamma
is similar to
\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}\eta_{1}&0&0\\
0&\eta_{2}&0\\
0&0&e\\
\end{smallmatrix}\Big)
in
G
, where
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
e\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
. The orbital sum will be (analogously to the second hyperbolic case):

I_{G}(f,\gamma)=|K/Z|\cdot Hf\left(\begin{bmatrix}\eta_{1}/e&0\\
0&\eta_{2}/e\end{bmatrix}\right).

As
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
are Galois conjugates without loss of generality we may assume that
\eta_{1}=\eta_{2}^{p}
. Therefore the total contribution of the second elliptic terms will be:

\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{(q-1)^{3}}{(p-1)(p^{2}-1)}\cdot\frac{q^{3}-1}{q-1}\sum_{e\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}Hf\left(\begin{bmatrix}\eta/e&0\\
0&\eta^{p}/e\end{bmatrix}\right)=

=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{(q-1)^{3}}{(p^{2}-1)}\cdot\frac{q^{3}-1}{q-1}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}Hf\left(\begin{bmatrix}\eta&0\\
0&\eta^{p}\end{bmatrix}\right).

•

n
is odd. In this case
\gamma
would not be diagonalizable in
\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. In general, it will be conjugate to an element of the form:

\begin{bmatrix}k&l\xi&0\\
l&k&0\\
0&0&m\end{bmatrix},

where
k\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
m,l,\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
such that
\xi
is a nonsquare in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. (This ensures that the determinant
m(k^{2}-l^{2}\xi)
is nonzero.) In this case we have:

G_{\gamma}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\xi&0\\
b&a&0\\
0&0&c\end{bmatrix}:\,a,b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{q},c(a^{2}-b^{2}\xi)\neq 0\right\}.

We seek a fundamental domain for this specific
G_{\gamma}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

A fundamental domain for
G_{\gamma}
is given by:

G_{\gamma}\backslash\mathbb{H}_{q}=\left\{\left(x+u\delta^{1/3}+v\delta^{2/3},y+\delta^{1/3}\right):v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times},x,y,u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}.

The corresponding orbital sum is

I_{G}(f,\gamma)=\frac{(q^{3}-1)(q^{2}-1)}{(p-1)(p^{2}-1)}Hf\left(\begin{bmatrix}k/m&l\xi/m\\
l/m&k/m\end{bmatrix}\right).

The total contribution from the second elliptic terms in this case is

\frac{(q^{3}-1)(q^{2}-1)}{(p^{2}-1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\\
t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\end{subarray}}Hf\left(\begin{bmatrix}s&t\xi\\
t&s\end{bmatrix}\right).

A detailed proof of both the proposition and the orbital sum
calculation can be found in
Appendix A
.

7.
Examples

Throughout this section, subscripts under multiplicities indicate which group is the domain of the corresponding representations. Set
\rho=\Ind_{\Gamma}^{G}1
and
\kappa=\Ind_{K}^{G}1
.

Example 7.1
.

Let
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
be the constant one function. The right-hand side of the trace formula will be
|G|
while the left-hand side becomes (by orthogonality of characters):

\sum_{\pi\in\hat{G}}m(\pi,\rho)_{G}\langle f,\chi_{\pi}\rangle=\sum_{\pi\in\hat{G}}m(\pi,\rho)_{G}\langle\chi_{1},\chi_{\pi}\rangle=|G|\cdot m(1,\rho)_{G}.

So the trace formula says
|G|\cdot m(1,\rho)=|G|
which is a manifestation of Frobenius reciprocity:

m(1,\rho)_{G}=\frac{1}{|G|}\langle 1,\Ind_{\Gamma}^{G}1\rangle_{G}=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\langle\Res^{G}_{\Gamma}1,1\rangle_{\Gamma}=m(1,1)_{\Gamma}=1.

Example 7.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{H}_{q}\to\mathbb{C}
be the indicator function
\delta_{p_{0}}
which is
1
on
p_{0}
and zero otherwise. It is
K-
bi-invariant because
\stab(p_{0})=K
. On the left-hand side of the trace formula we have:

\begin{split}\sum_{\pi\in\hat{G}}m(\pi,\rho)_{G}\langle f,\chi_{\pi}\rangle&=\sum_{\pi\in\hat{G}}m(\pi,\rho)_{G}\sum_{g\in G}f(g\cdot p_{0})\chi_{\pi}\left(g^{-1}\right)=\sum_{\pi\in\hat{G}}m(\pi,\rho)_{G}\sum_{k\in K}\chi_{\pi}\left(k^{-1}\right)=\\
&=\sum_{\pi\in\hat{G}}m(\pi,\rho)_{G}\langle 1,\chi_{\pi}\rangle_{K}=|K|\cdot\sum_{\pi\in\hat{G}}m(\pi,\rho)_{G}\cdot m(1,\Res^{G}_{K}\pi)_{K}=\\
&=|K|\cdot\sum_{\pi\in\hat{G}}m(\pi,\rho)_{G}\cdot m(\pi,\kappa)_{G}.\end{split}

On the right-hand side, the only nonzero terms are the central terms and the first elliptic terms when
n
is not divisible by
3
. If that is the case, we claim that for a non-central
\gamma
in
K
we have
I_{G}(f,\gamma)=3.

Suppose
\gamma
corresponds to multiplication by
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\left(\delta^{1/3}\right)^{\times}
under the isomorphism
K\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}\left(\delta^{1/3}\right)^{\times}
. When viewed as an element of
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, the eigenvalues of
\gamma
are the Galois conjugates of
\xi
, which are
\xi
,
\xi^{q}
and
\xi^{q^{2}}
in our setting. This means that the conjugates of
\gamma
lying in K are precisely
\gamma^{q}
and
\gamma^{q^{2}}
, hence there will be exactly three non-zero terms in each orbital sum
I_{G}(f,\gamma)
.

The total contribution of the first elliptic terms will be:

\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{q^{3}-1}{p^{3}-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in K\\
\gamma\not\in Z\end{subarray}}I_{G}(f,\gamma)=\frac{(q^{3}-1)(q^{3}-q)}{p^{3}-1}.

Thus we get:

Case 1:

n
is divisible by
3
. Then we have

\sum_{\pi\in\hat{G}}m(\pi,\rho)_{G}\cdot m(\pi,\kappa)_{G}=\frac{q^{3}(q^{2}-1)(q-1)}{p^{3}(p^{2}-1)(p^{3}-1)}.

Case 2:

n
is not divisible by
3
. Then we have

\sum_{\pi\in\hat{G}}m(\pi,\rho)_{G}\cdot m(\pi,\kappa)_{G}=\frac{q^{3}(q^{2}-1)(q-1)}{p^{3}(p^{2}-1)(p^{3}-1)}+\frac{q^{3}-q}{p^{3}-1}.

Remark 7.3
.

Note that the sum on the left-hand side can also be written as:

\sum_{\pi\in\hat{G}}m(\pi,\rho)_{G}\cdot m(\pi,\kappa)_{G}=\frac{1}{|G|}\langle\chi_{\rho},\chi_{\kappa}\rangle.

8.
Character of the representation
\rho=\Ind_{\Gamma}^{G}1

Having effectively computed the right-hand side of the pre-trace formula (
3.4
), we now turn to the left-hand side. For this, we require the character
\chi_{\rho}
of
\rho
, which we now calculate. We use the usual formula for the character of an induced representation (see
[
9
, Ch. 16]
):

\chi_{\rho}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\sum_{x\in G}\mathbbm{1}_{\Gamma}(x\gamma x^{-1}).

We treat each conjugacy class. By
H_{1,q},H_{2,q},P_{1,q},\dots,E_{2,q}
we denote the size of various types of conjugacy classes in
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Given a set
S
, we use
\mathbbm{1}_{S}
to denote the indicator function for
S
.

•

Central class:
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&0&0\\
0&a&0\\
0&0&a\end{bmatrix},a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}.
Since
\gamma\in Z(G)
, we have
x\gamma x^{-1}=\gamma
. So,

\chi_{\rho}(\gamma)=\frac{|G|}{|\Gamma|}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(a).

•

First hyperbolic class:
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&0&0\\
0&a&0\\
0&0&b\end{bmatrix}
with
a\neq b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}.
Now,
\gamma
is similar to an element of
\Gamma
only when
a,b
are roots of a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. This occurs only when
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
; for instance, this can be seen by considering the determinant
\Det(\gamma)=a^{2}b
. Being the constant coefficient of
\text{char}_{\gamma}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
, we have
\Det(\gamma)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, which implies
\Det(\gamma)
is invariant under the Frobenius automorphism (as
\Gal(\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p})
is generated by the Frobenius). Since
\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
is Galois, if
a,b\not\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, they must be Galois conjugates, i.e.,
a^{p}=b
and
b^{p}=a
. But this forces
a^{2}b=(a^{2}b)^{p}=b^{2}a,
i.e.,
a=b
, a contradiction. So, by a simple application of the orbit-stabilizer theorem, it follows that

\chi_{\rho}(\gamma)=\frac{|G|}{|\Gamma|}\frac{H_{1,p}}{H_{1,q}}\mathbbm{1}_{\Gamma}(\gamma).

•

Second hyperbolic class:
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&0&0\\
0&b&0\\
0&0&c\end{bmatrix}
with
a,b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
distinct. There are three cases which allow
\gamma
to be similar to an element of
\Gamma
: either all three are Galois conjugates in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{3}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
(only possible if
3\mid n
), or
a,b
are Galois conjugates in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
(only possible if
2\mid n
), or all three belong to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. These cases give rise to the three terms in the sum:

\chi_{\rho}(\gamma)=\frac{|G|}{|\Gamma|\cdot H_{2,q}}\left(E_{2,p}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{3}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}}(a)\delta_{b}(a^{p})\delta_{c}(a^{p^{2}})+E_{1,p}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}}(a)\delta_{b}(a^{p})\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(c)+H_{2,p}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\Gamma}(\gamma)\right).

•

First parabolic class:
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&1&0\\
0&a&0\\
0&0&a\end{bmatrix},a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. If
a\not\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
was the root of a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, its Galois conjugate
a^{p}\neq a
would also be a root, which is clearly not the case. So,

\chi_{\rho}(\gamma)=\frac{|G|}{|\Gamma|}\frac{P_{1,p}}{P_{1,q}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(a).

•

Second parabolic class:
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&1&0\\
0&a&1\\
0&0&a\end{bmatrix},a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}.
Similar to the previous class,

\chi_{\rho}(\gamma)=\frac{|G|}{|\Gamma|}\frac{P_{2,p}}{P_{2,q}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(a).

•

Third parabolic class:
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&1&0\\
0&a&0\\
0&0&b\end{bmatrix}
with
a\neq b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. By a similar argument as for the first hyperbolic class, we have

\chi_{\rho}(g)=\frac{|G|}{|\Gamma|}\frac{P_{3,p}}{P_{3,q}}\mathbbm{1}_{\Gamma}(g).

•

First elliptic class:
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}w&0&0\\
0&w^{q}&0\\
0&0&r\end{bmatrix}
with
w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Here
\gamma
is similar to an element of
\Gamma
if and only if
w,w^{q}
are roots of a degree-2 irreducible polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. However, if
2\mid n
, then
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
is an intermediate extension in
\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
and so roots of any degree-2 polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
are included in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus,
\chi_{\rho}(\gamma)=0
if
n
is divisible by 2, otherwise

\chi_{\rho}(\gamma)=\frac{|G|}{|\Gamma|}\frac{E_{1,p}}{E_{1,q}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}}(w)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(r).

•

Second elliptic class:
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}w&0&0\\
0&w^{q}&0\\
0&0&w^{q^{2}}\end{bmatrix},w\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(\delta^{1/3})\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
. As for the previous class,
\chi_{\rho}(\gamma)=0
if
n
is divisible by 3, otherwise

\chi_{\rho}(\gamma)=\frac{|G|}{|\Gamma|}\frac{E_{2,p}}{E_{2,q}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{3}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}}(w).

9.
Decomposition of
\rho
when
2,3\nmid n

In this section, we compute the decomposition of
\rho
when neither
2
nor
3
divide
n
; thus elliptic elements in
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
remain elliptic in
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

We use the character table for
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in Terras
[
9
, pp. 383-4]
and Steinberg
[
8
]
. As usual, to calculate multiplicity
m(\pi,\rho)
of an irreducible representation
\pi
present in
\rho
, we compute the inner product of their respective characters. By
H_{1},H_{2},P_{1}\dots,E_{2}
we denote the size of different types of conjugacy classes in
\GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. By
\Nm_{E/F}
, we denote the norm of the field extension
E/F
.

(1)

\alpha
, a character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Then,

\displaystyle m(\alpha,\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\Bigg(\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\alpha(a^{3})+H_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}\alpha(a^{2}b)+\frac{H_{2}}{6}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\\
a,b,c\text{ distinct}\end{subarray}}\alpha(abc)+P_{1}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\alpha(a^{3})+P_{2}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\alpha(a^{3})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+P_{3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}\alpha(a^{2}b)+\frac{E_{1}}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}\\
r\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\alpha(r\Nm_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(w))+\frac{E_{2}}{3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{3}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}\end{subarray}}\alpha(\Nm_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(w))\Bigg).

We can simplify these sums on the basis of three cases:

•

\alpha^{3}\mid_{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\neq 1
. Each of the sums is zero and so
m(\alpha,\rho)=0
.

•

\alpha^{3}\mid_{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}=1
but
\alpha\mid_{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\neq 1
. A short calculation yields

m(\alpha,\rho)=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\left(1-H_{1}+\frac{H_{2}}{3}+P_{1}+P_{2}-P_{3}-\frac{E_{2}}{3}\right)(p-1)=0.

•

\alpha\mid_{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}=1
. Then simply

m(\alpha,\rho)=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\sum_{\{g\}\in C_{\Gamma}}|\{g\}|=1.

(2)

\pi_{\alpha}
, where
\alpha
is a character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.

\displaystyle m(\pi_{\alpha},\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\Bigg((q^{2}+q)\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\alpha(a^{3})+(q+1)H_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}\alpha(a^{2}b)+\frac{H_{2}}{3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\\
a,b,c\text{ distinct}\end{subarray}}\alpha(abc)+qP_{1}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\alpha(a^{3})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+0+P_{3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}\alpha(a^{2}b)+0-\frac{E_{2}}{3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{3}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}\end{subarray}}\alpha(\Nm_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(w))\Bigg).

•

\alpha^{3}\mid_{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\neq 1
. Here again all the sums are zero, giving
m(\pi_{\alpha},\rho)=0
.

•

\alpha^{3}\mid_{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}=1
but
\alpha\mid_{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\neq 1
.

\displaystyle m(\pi_{\alpha},\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\left((q^{2}+q)-(q+1)H_{1}+\frac{2}{3}H_{2}+qP_{1}-P_{3}+\frac{E_{2}}{3}\right)(p-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q-p)(q-p^{2})}{p^{3}(p-1)^{2}(p+1)(p^{2}+p+1)}.

•

\alpha\mid_{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}=1
.

\displaystyle m(\pi_{\alpha},\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\Bigg((q^{2}+q)(p-1)+(q+1)H_{1}(p-1)(p-2)+(p-1)(p-2)(p-3)\frac{H_{2}}{3}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+qP_{1}(p-1)+(p-1)(p-2)P_{3}-\frac{E_{2}}{3}(p^{3}-p)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q-p)(q+p^{5}-2p^{2})}{p^{3}(p-1)^{2}(p+1)(p^{2}+p+1)}.

As a sanity check, showing this is actually an integer is not very hard.

(3)

\pi_{\alpha}^{\prime},
where
\alpha
is a character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.

\displaystyle m(\pi_{\alpha}^{\prime},\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\Bigg(q^{3}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\alpha(a^{3})+qH_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}\alpha(a^{2}b)+\frac{H_{2}}{6}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\\
a,b,c\text{ distinct}\end{subarray}}\alpha(abc)+0+0+0

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{E_{1}}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}\\
r\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\alpha(r\Nm_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(w))+\frac{E_{2}}{3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{3}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}\end{subarray}}\alpha(\Nm_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(w))\Bigg).

•

\alpha^{3}\mid_{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\neq 1
.
m(\pi_{\alpha},\rho)=0
.

•

\alpha^{3}\mid_{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}=1
but
\alpha\mid_{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\neq 1
.

\displaystyle m(\pi_{\alpha}^{\prime},\rho)=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\left(q^{3}-qH_{1}+\frac{1}{3}H_{2}-\frac{E_{2}}{3}\right)(p-1)=\frac{(q-p)(q-p^{2})(q+p^{2}+p)}{p^{3}(p-1)^{2}(p+1)(p^{2}+p+1)}.

•

\alpha\mid_{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}=1

\displaystyle m(\pi_{\alpha}^{\prime},\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\Bigg(q^{3}(p-1)+q(p-1)(p-2)H_{1}+(p-1)(p-2)(p-3)\frac{H_{2}}{6}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-(p^{2}-p)(p-1)\frac{E_{1}}{2}+(p^{3}-p)\frac{E_{2}}{3}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q-p)(q^{2}+pq+p^{5}-p^{4}-p^{3}-p^{2})}{p^{3}(p-1)^{2}(p+1)(p^{2}+p+1)}.

\displaystyle m(\rho_{\alpha,\nu},\rho)

Proof of Lemma 2.2

Proof.

Observe that if
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\left(\delta^{1/3}\right)
then
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{H}_{q}
if and only if
\{\alpha,\beta,1\}
form a basis of

over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Suppose
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{H}_{q}
and

are such that:

x\cdot\frac{a\alpha+b\beta+c}{r\alpha+s\beta+t}+y\cdot\frac{d\alpha+e\beta+f}{r\alpha+s\beta+t}+z\cdot 1=0.

(as
\alpha,\beta,1

d&e&f\\

d&e&f\\

\beta\\
1\end{bmatrix}=0.

d&e&f\\

\mathbb{F}_{q}

, proving the claim that

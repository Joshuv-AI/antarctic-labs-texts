The Quantum Kalman Decomposition: A Gramian Matrix Approach
Guofeng Zhang, Jinghao Li, Zhiyuan Dong, Ian R. Petersen

Abstract
The Kalman canonical form for quantum linear systems was derived in [ 66 ] . The purpose of this paper is to present an alternative derivation by means of a Gramian matrix approach. Controllability and observability Gramian matrices are defined for linear quantum systems, which are used to characterize various subspaces. Based on these characterizations, real orthogonal and block symplectic coordinate transformation matrices are constructed to transform a given quantum linear system to the Kalman canonical form. An example is used to illustrate the main results.

Introduction

In recent decades, significant advancements have been made in both theoretical understanding and experimental applications of quantum control. Quantum control plays a pivotal role in various quantum information technologies, such as quantum communication, quantum computation, quantum cryptography, quantum ultra-precision metrology, and nano-electronics. Similar to classical control systems theory, linear quantum systems hold great importance in the field of quantum control. Quantum linear systems are mathematical models that describe the behavior of quantum harmonic oscillators. In this context, “linear” refers to the linearity of the Heisenberg equations of motion for quadrature operators in the quantum systems. This linearity often leads to simplifications that facilitate analysis and control of these systems. Consequently, quantum linear systems can be effectively studied using powerful mathematical techniques derived from classical linear systems theory. Observability and controllability Gramian matrices

In this section, we define observability and controllability Gramian matrices for the quantum linear system (
2.4
), and then use them to characterize various subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

For the quantum linear system (
2.4
), define the observability and controllability matrices to be

\mathbb{O}_{G}\triangleq\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbb{C}\\
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{A}\\
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{A}^{2}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{A}^{2n-1}\end{array}\right],\ \ \ \mathbb{C}_{G}\triangleq\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathbb{B}&\mathbb{A}\mathbb{B}&\mathbb{A}^{2}\mathbb{B}&\cdots&\mathbb{A}^{2n-1}\mathbb{B}\end{array}\right],

(3.1)

respectively. We also define two more matrices

\mathbb{O}_{s}\triangleq\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbb{C}\\
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H}\\
\mathbb{C}(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})^{2}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbb{C}(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})^{2n-1}\end{array}\right],\ \ \ \mathbb{C}_{s}\triangleq\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathbb{B}&(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})\mathbb{B}&(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})^{2}\mathbb{B}&\cdots&(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})^{2n-1}\mathbb{B}\end{array}\right].

(3.2)

The matrices
\mathbb{O}_{s}
and
\mathbb{C}_{s}
are related by

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathbb{J}_{m}&&&&\\
&-\mathbb{J}_{m}&&&\\
&&\ddots&&\\
&&&\mathbb{J}_{m}&\\
&&&&-\mathbb{J}_{m}\end{array}\right]\mathbb{C}_{s}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}=\mathbb{O}_{s}.

(3.3)

Consequently,

\displaystyle{\rm Ker}(\mathbb{O}_{s})=

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle Ker}(\mathbb{C}_{s}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}),

(3.4)

\displaystyle{\rm Ker}(\mathbb{O}_{s}\mathbb{J}_{n})=

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle Ker}(\mathbb{C}_{s}^{\top})={\rm Im}(\mathbb{C}_{s})^{\perp}.

It can easily checked that

{\rm Im}(\mathbb{C}_{G})={\rm Im}(\mathbb{C}_{s}),\ {\rm Ker}(\mathbb{O}_{G})={\rm Ker}(\mathbb{O}_{s}).

(3.5)

Hence, we use
\mathbb{O}_{s}
and
\mathbb{C}_{s}
, instead of
\mathbb{O}_{G}
and
\mathbb{C}_{G}
, in the following discussions as they appear simpler.

Remark 3.1

Notice that the matrix
\mathbb{O}_{s}
defined in Eq. (
3.2
) is the real-domain counterpart of the matrix

defined in

Similar to the classical case, see e.g.,

[
49

W_{o}(t,s)
and controllability Gramian matrix
W_{c}(t,s)
to be

\displaystyle W_{o}(t,s)\triangleq\;\int_{t}^{s}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})^{\top}(\tau-t)}\mathbb{C}^{\top}\mathbb{C}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})(\tau-t)}d\tau,\ \ \forall-\infty<t<s<\infty,

(3.6a)

\displaystyle W_{c}(t,s)\triangleq\;\int_{t}^{s}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})(t-\tau)}\mathbb{B}\mathbb{B}^{\top}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})^{\top}(t-\tau)}d\tau,\ \ \forall-\infty<t<s<\infty,

(3.6b)

respectively. Clearly, both
W_{o}(t,s)
and

are real and positive semi-definite matrices.

The following result reveals a nice relation between the observability and controllability Gramians which is unique to quantum linear systems,

Lemma 3.1

The observability Gramian
W_{o}(t,s)
and controllability Gramian
W_{c}(t,s)
are related by

W_{o}(t,s)=W_{c}(t,s)^{\sharp}.

(3.7)

Proof.

Noticing that
\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}=-\mathbb{J}_{n}
and
\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}e^{\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H}}\mathbb{J}_{n}=e^{\mathbb{H}\mathbb{J}_{n}}
, we have

\displaystyle W_{c}(t,s)^{\sharp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}W_{c}(t,s)\mathbb{J}_{n}

(3.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{s}\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})(t-\tau)}\mathbb{B}\mathbb{B}^{\top}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})^{\top}(t-\tau)}\mathbb{J}_{n}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{s}\left(\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})(t-\tau)}\mathbb{J}_{n}\right)\left(\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}\mathbb{B}\mathbb{J}_{m}\right)\left(\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}\mathbb{B}\mathbb{J}_{m}\right)^{\top}\left(\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})(t-\tau)}\mathbb{J}_{n}\right)^{\top}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{s}\left(e^{(\mathbb{H}\mathbb{J}_{n})(t-\tau)}\right)\left(-\mathbb{C}^{\top}\right)\left(-\mathbb{C}\right)\left(e^{(\mathbb{H}\mathbb{J}_{n})(t-\tau)}\right)^{\top}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{s}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})^{\top}(\tau-t)}\mathbb{C}^{\top}\mathbb{C}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})(\tau-t)}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{o}(t,s).

This completes the proof.

In the following, We characterize various subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}

and controllability Gramian matrix

We start from the following result.

Lemma 3.2

We have

{\rm Ker}(\mathbb{O}_{s})={\rm Ker}(W_{o}(t,s)),\ \ \forall-\infty<t<s<\infty,

(3.9)

and

{\rm Im}(\mathbb{O}_{s}^{\top})={\rm Im}(W_{o}(t,s)),\ \ \forall-\infty<t<s<\infty.

(3.10)

Proof.

For each
x\in{\rm Ker}(\mathbb{O}_{s})
, by Eq. (
3.2
) we have
\mathbb{C}(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})^{k}x=0
for all

. Hence, according to the Cayley-Hamilton theorem,
\mathbb{C}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})t}x=0
for all

, which means that
x\in{\rm Ker}(W_{o}(t,s))
for all
-\infty<t<s<\infty
. Hence,
{\rm Ker}(\mathbb{O}_{s})\subset{\rm Ker}(W_{o}(t,s))
for all
-\infty<t<s<\infty
. On the other hand, suppose
x\in{\rm Ker}(W_{o}(t,s))
for all
-\infty<t<s<\infty
. Then
\int_{t}^{s}\|\mathbb{C}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})(\tau-t)}x\|^{2}d\tau=0
, which means that
\mathbb{C}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})(\tau-t)}x\equiv 0
for all
t\leq\tau\leq s
. In other words,
\mathbb{C}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})\tau}x\equiv 0
for all
\tau\in[0,s-t]
. As the derivative
\frac{{\rm d}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})\tau}}{{\rm d}\tau}=\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})\tau}
, we have
\mathbb{C}(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})^{k}e^{(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})\tau}x\equiv 0
for all
\tau\in[0,s-t]
and for all
k=0,1,\ldots
. Setting
\tau=0
yields
\mathbb{C}(\mathbb{J}_{n}\mathbb{H})^{k}x\equiv 0
for all
k=0,1,\ldots
, which means that
x\in{\rm Ker}(\mathbb{O}_{s})
. Thus,
{\rm Ker}(W_{o}(t,s))\subset{\rm Ker}(\mathbb{O}_{s})
. Consequently, Eq. (
3.9
) holds. Eq. (
3.10
) immediately follows Eq. (
3.9
).

A procedure for computing the coordinate transformation matrix

In the preceding section, we showed how to obtain the coordinate transformation matrix

We apply the singular value decomposition (SVD) to the observability Gramian matrix

. Since

W_{o}(t,s)=U\Sigma U^{\top}=U\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\Sigma_{1}&0\\

(5.1)

where
\Sigma_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
is a diagonal matrix with positive diagonal entries. Therefore,
\mathrm{rank}(U_{1})=\mathrm{rank}(W_{o}(t,s))=r=2n_{1}+n_{3}
and
\mathrm{rank}(U_{2})=2n-r
. As mentioned in Remark
3.2
,

is constant as long as

5.1
) above we implicitly assumed

and
s
are two fixed constant satisfying
t<s
. Thus the RHS of Eq. (
5.1
) does not depend on
t
and
s

We characterize the subspaces in Eq. (
3.14
) by means of orthogonal matrices

and

given above.

\displaystyle R_{c\bar{o}}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}(\mathbb{J}U_{1})\cap\mathrm{Im}(U_{2}),

According to Theorem
5.1
, Hamiltonian matrices such as
\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}
and skew-Hamiltonian matrices such as
(\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top})^{2}
can be employed to characterize various subspaces. In what follows, we present some of their properties.

Corollary 5.1

The eigenvalues of the matrices
\mathbb{J}U_{j}U_{j}^{\top}\mathbb{J}U_{k}U_{k}^{\top}
must be
0
or
-1
for all
j,k=1,2
.

Proof.

We give the proof of the case of
\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}
. The other cases can be proved similarly.
Notice that
\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}U_{2}=0
,
which means that
0
is the eigenvalue of the matrix
\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}
if
{\rm rank}(U_{1})<2n
. On the other hand,
\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}
and
U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}^{\top}U_{1}
share the same nonzero eigenvalues. Since the only nonzero eigenvalue of
U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}^{\top}U_{1}
is
1
, the nonzero eigenvalue of
\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}
must be
1
. Moreover, it follows from
\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}=-\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}
that only
-1
is the nonzero eigenvalue of
\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}
.

Corollary 5.2

We have

Take the matrix

(\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top})^{2}
is skew-Hamiltonian, there exists a real orthogonal matrix

or 0. Thus, one may find a set of orthonormal eigenvectors of the matrix on the RHS of Eq. (

-1

\mathbb{J}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}

Corollary 5.3

x\in R_{c\bar{o}}
if and only if

x=U_{2}U_{2}^{\top}x=\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}x.

(5.16)

Proof.

Noticing

\left(I+\mathbb{J}_{n}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}U_{2}U_{2}^{\top}\right)^{\sharp}=I+\mathbb{J}_{n}U_{2}U_{2}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top},

we have

\displaystyle R_{\bar{c}o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Ker}(I+\mathbb{J}_{n}U_{2}U_{2}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{J}_{n}{\rm Ker}(I+U_{2}U_{2}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}).

However, by Eq. (
3.19
) we have

R_{c\bar{o}}=\mathbb{J}_{n}R_{\bar{c}o}=\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}R_{\bar{c}o}
.
Hence,

{\rm Ker}\left(I-\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}U_{2}U_{2}^{\top}\right)={\rm Ker}\left(I-U_{2}U_{2}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}\right).

This means that for all
x\in R_{c\bar{o}}
,

x=U_{2}U_{2}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}x=\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}U_{2}U_{2}^{\top}x.

Consequently,

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{2}U_{2}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(U_{2}U_{2}^{\top})(\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n})(\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n})(U_{2}U_{2}^{\top})x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(U_{2}U_{2}^{\top})\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}U_{2}U_{2}^{\top}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{2}U_{2}^{\top}x

and

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}U_{2}U_{2}^{\top}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n})(U_{2}U_{2}^{\top})(U_{2}U_{2}^{\top})(\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n})x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n})U_{2}U_{2}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{J}_{n}^{\top}U_{1}U_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{J}_{n}x.

The proof is completed.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we have presented a Gramian matrix approach to deriving the quantum Kalman canonical form for linear quantum systems in their real quadrature-operator representation. A detailed numerical computational procedure has also been provided for the construction of the real orthogonal and block symplectic coordinate transformation matrices to transform a given quantum linear system to the Kalman canonical form.

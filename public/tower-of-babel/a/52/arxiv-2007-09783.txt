1.
Introduction

Comparison theory of projections plays an important role in the type classification of factors.
A C*-algebra might have few or no projections.
In this case comparison theory of projections may say
nothing about the structure of the C*-algebra.
The appropriate substitute for projections is positive elements.
This idea was first introduced by Cuntz in
[
8
]
for the purpose of studying dimension functions on simple C*-algebras.
Later, the radius of comparison of C*-algebras, based on the Cuntz semigroup,
was introduced by Andrew S. Toms
in Section 6 of
[
35
]
to study exotic examples of simple amenable
C*-algebras that are not
\mathcal{Z}
-absorbing.
In the commutative setting, it
is well known that the radius of comparison of
C(X)
is bounded above
by one half the covering dimension of
X

[
7
,
12
]
. Also, the comparison theory can be viewed as a non-commutative dimension
theory
[
3
]
.
Remarkable progress has been achieved on the comparison theory
in
[
7
,
20
,
25
,
26
,
29
]
.
In this paper, we consider the relation between comparison
theory in a simple C*-algebra
A
and in the crossed product of
A
by
a finite group, under a tracially strictly approximately inner action.

The Cuntz semigroup is a key ingredient in the Elliott program for the classification of C*-algebras
[
13
,
36
]
.
We refer to
[
2
,
34
]
for many aspects of the Cuntz semigroup.
It is generally large and complicated.
Among simple nuclear C*-algebras,
the classifiable ones are those whose Cuntz semigroups
are easily accessible (Section 5 of
[
2
]
).
With the near completion of the Elliott program,
nonclassifiable C*-algebras attract more attention (see
[
4
,
20
,
26
]
)
and the Cuntz semigroup is the main additional available invariant.
The goal of this paper
is to conduct an investigation beyond classifiable C*-algebras by
considering crossed products by finite groups of simple C*-algebras which may not have strict comparison of positive elements,
although there are still many pieces of the puzzle that have to be put together.

Section
2
is devoted to
providing a quick overview of the background we need here. In Section
3
and Section
4
, we give a standard definition for
a strictly approximately inner action of a finite group on a unital C*-algebra
and its tracial analog, tracial strict approximate innerness.
(See Definition
3.6
and Definition
4.2
.)
To provide these definitions, we basically give
a generalization of the Definition 1.3 of
[
27
]

and
a generalization of Definition 3.2 of
[
28
]

to nonabelian finite groups and not necessarily separable C*-algebras, and then omit some of their assumptions.
(See Definition
3.4
and Definition
4.1
.)
Clearly, in the setting of abelian groups and simple separable unital C*-algebras,
approximate representability in the sense of Phillips (Definition 3.2 of
[
28
]
),
approximate representability in the sense of Izumi (Definition 3.6(2) of
[
21
]
),
approximate representability in the sense of Osaka and Teruya (Definition 3.4 of
[
24
]
),
and
approximate representability in our sense (Definition
3.4
) are the same.
There is no apparent reason to
believe that strict approximate innerness implies approximate
representability.
No examples seem to be known.

Also, we prove that if
G
is a finite group,
A
is an infinite-dimensional simple unital stably finite C*-algebra,
and
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be a tracially strictly approximately inner action, then
the radii of comparison of
A
and
the crossed product are related by

{\operatorname{rc}}(A)\leq{\operatorname{rc}}\big(C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)\big).

Getting the reverse inequality is likely to be very difficult.
When we further assume that
C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)
is simple, then
the radii of comparison of
A
, the crossed product, and the fixed point algebra
are related by

{\operatorname{rc}}(A)\leq{\operatorname{rc}}\big(C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)\big)\leq{\operatorname{rc}}(A^{\alpha}).

In fact,
we prove a much stronger result,
relating the Cuntz semigroups
(see Theorem
3.18
and Theorem
4.10
).
We show that
the inclusion of
A
in
C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)

induces an isomorphism from
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
to its range in
{\operatorname{Cu}}(C^{*}(G,A,\alpha))

if
\alpha
is a strictly approximate inner action of a finite group
G
on a unital C*-algebra
A
.
It induces an isomorphism from
{\operatorname{Cu}}_{+}(A)\cup\{0\}
to its range
if
\alpha
is a tracially strictly approximately inner action of a finite group
G

on an infinite-dimensional simple unital stably finite C*-algebra
A
.

Suppose further that
G
is abelian,
\alpha
is tracially approximately representable, and
C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)
is simple.
Let
\widehat{\alpha}
be the dual action of
\widehat{G}
on
C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)
.
Then
\widehat{\alpha}
has the tracial Rokhlin property by Theorem 3.11 of
[
28
]
. Therefore our results are immediate from
Takai duality
[
32
]
and the main results in
[
4
]
.
But the hypothesis in our results is only that the action is tracially
strictly approximately inner.
Also, the group in our work need not to be abelian.
Thus, our results generalize the tracial Rokhlin property case
of
[
4
]
.
Note that the right version of the Rokhlin property for
actions of finite dimensional quantum groups was given in
[
16
]

under the name “spatial Rokhlin property”
and the tracial version of spatial Rokhlin property has not been defined yet.
Also, even if we had the result of
[
4
]
for duals of finite dimensional quantum groups,
Theorem 4.12 of
[
5
]
would only give the current result assuming approximate representability.
It is because of the fact that our actual hypotheses are weaker.
Namely, strict approximate innerness is weaker than approximate representability and
our results cover also the tracial version of approximate representability.

We also prove in Proposition
3.22
that actions of finite groups on
stably finite unital C*-algebras
A
with
0<{\operatorname{rc}}(A)<\infty

cannot simultaneously have the Rokhlin property and be strictly approximately inner.
We further prove in Proposition
4.13
that actions of finite groups on many nonclassifiable simple unital C*-algebras cannot simultaneously
have the weak tracial Rokhlin property and be tracially strictly approximately inner.
Proposition
3.22
and Proposition
4.13
fail when the C*-algebras have strict comparison of positive elements.
(See Example
3.23
.)

Finally, in Section
5
, for every finite group
G
and for every
\eta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{{\operatorname{card}}(G)}\right)
,
we construct a simple separable unital AH algebra
A
with stable rank one
and an action
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
such that:

(1)

\alpha
is pointwise outer, strictly approximately inner, and doesn’t have the weak tracial Rokhlin property.

(2)

{\operatorname{rc}}(A)={\operatorname{rc}}\left(C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)\right)=\eta
.

Notation 2.1

Throughout,

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
be a C*-algebra.

(1)

For any
a,b\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}
,
we say that
a
is
Cuntz subequivalent to
b
in
A
,
written
a\precsim_{A}b
,
if there is a sequence
(c_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
M_{\infty}(A)

such that

\lim_{{n}\to\infty}c_{n}bc_{n}^{*}=a.

If
a\precsim_{A}b
and
b\precsim_{A}a
,
we say that
a
and
b
are
Cuntz equivalent in
A
and write
a\sim_{A}b
.
This relation is an equivalence relation,
and we write
\langle a\rangle_{A}
for the equivalence class of
a
.
We define
{\operatorname{W}}(A)=M_{\infty}(A)_{+}/\sim_{A}
,
together with the commutative semigroup operation

\langle a\rangle_{A}+\langle b\rangle_{A}=\langle a\oplus b\rangle_{A}

and the partial order

\langle a\rangle_{A}\leq\langle b\rangle_{A}

if
a\precsim_{A}b
.
We write
0
for
\langle 0\rangle_{A}
.
Further,
we take
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)={\operatorname{W}}({\mathcal{K}}\otimes A)
.
We write the classes as
\langle a\rangle_{A}

for
a\in({\mathcal{K}}\otimes A)_{+}
.

(2)

Let
A
and
B
be C*-algebras
and let
\psi\colon A\to B
be a homomorphism.
We use the same letter for the induced maps

M_{n}(A)\to M_{n}(B)

for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
,

M_{\infty}(A)\to M_{\infty}(B)
, and

{\mathcal{K}}\otimes A\to{\mathcal{K}}\otimes B
.
We define

{\operatorname{W}}(\psi)\colon{\operatorname{W}}(A)\to{\operatorname{W}}(B)

and
{\operatorname{Cu}}(\psi)\colon{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)\to{\operatorname{Cu}}(B)

by
\langle a\rangle_{A}\mapsto\langle\psi(a)\rangle_{B}

for
a\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}

or
a\in({\mathcal{K}}\otimes A)_{+}
as appropriate.

The original version
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
of the Cuntz semigroup has some flaws
which are fixed by considering
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
, although both invariants are closely related.
Nevertheless, the radius of comparison is easier to deal with in terms
of
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
.
Also, the usual notation for Cuntz subequivalence is
a\precsim b
.
Since we need to use Cuntz subequivalence with respect to different C*-algebras,
we include
A
in the notation.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

(1)

Let

(b)

.

(2)

.

(b)

Definition 2.4
.

A

(2)

\rho(b+ic)=\rho(b)+i\rho(c)

(3)

\rho|_{B}

for every commutative C*-subalgebra

.

(4)

There is a function
\rho_{2}\colon M_{2}(A)\to\mathbb{C}

(

), (

), and (

)

,

\rho(a)=\rho_{2}(a\otimes e_{1,1}).

.
We refer to

3.
The radius of comparison and strict approximate innerness

In this section, we first prove that approximate representability in the sense of Izumi
and approximate representability in the sense of Phillips are the same in the setting of nonabelian finite groups.
We then give the definition of strictly approximately inner actions
of finite groups on unital C*-algebras.
Then we find a unital linear map from
C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)

to
A
which is an approximate homomorphism on finite sets and whose restriction to
A
is the identity map.
Also, we show that
the map
{\operatorname{W}}(\iota)\colon{\operatorname{W}}(A)\to{\operatorname{W}}(C^{*}(G,A,\alpha))

and
the map
{\operatorname{Cu}}(\iota)\colon{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)\to{\operatorname{Cu}}(C^{*}(G,A,\alpha))

which are induced by the inclusion map of
A
in
C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)
are injective.
We further find a lower bound and an upper bound for the radius of comparison of
C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A
be a C*-algebra. We denote the set of all bounded
sequences in A with the supremum norm and pointwise operations by
l^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},A)
.
Define

c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},A)=\left\{(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}}\in l^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},A)\colon\lim_{n\to\infty}\|a_{n}\|=0\right\}.

Clearly
c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},A)
is an ideal of
l^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},A)
. Then define

A_{\infty}=l^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},A)/c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},A).

We identify
A
with the C*-subalgebra of
A_{\infty}
consisting of the equivalence classes of
constant sequences.
Let
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be an action of a finite group
G
on a C*-algebra
A
.
We write
\alpha_{\infty}\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A_{\infty})
for the induced action.

The following definition
is a generalization of the Definition 3.6(1) of
[
21
]

to nonabelian finite groups in terms of the sequence algebra.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be an action of a finite group
G
on a unital C*-algebra
A
.
The action
\alpha
is said to be
strongly approximately inner
if
there exist unitaries
w(g)\in A_{\infty}
such that:

(1)

\alpha_{{\infty},g}(x)=w(g)xw(g)^{*}
for all
x\in A
and all
g\in G
.

(2)

\alpha_{{\infty},g}(w(h))=w(ghg^{-1})
for all
g,h\in G
.

The following definition is a generalization of the Definition 3.6(2) of
[
21
]

to nonabelian finite groups in terms of the sequence algebra.
It was also pointed out by Izumi in Remark 3.7 of
[
21
]
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be an action of a finite group
G
on a unital C*-algebra
A
.
The action
\alpha
is said to be
approximately representable
if
there exists a unitary representation
w
of
G
in
A_{\infty}
such that:

(1)

\alpha_{{\infty},g}(x)=w(g)xw(g)^{*}
for all
x\in A
and all
g\in G
.

(2)

\alpha_{{\infty},g}(w(h))=w(ghg^{-1})
for all
g,h\in G
.

The following is a generalization of the Definition 1.3 of
[
27
]
to nonabelian finite groups.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra,
let
G
be a finite group,
and let
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)

be an action of
G
on
A
.
We say that
\alpha
is

approximately representable

if for every finite set
F\subset A
and every
\varepsilon>0
,
there are
z_{g}\in{\operatorname{U}}(A)
for
g\in G
such that:

(1)

\|\alpha_{g}(a)-z_{g}az_{g}^{*}\|<\varepsilon
for all
g\in G
and all
a\in F
.

(2)

\|z_{g}z_{h}-z_{gh}\|<\varepsilon
for all
g,h\in G
.

(3)

\|\alpha_{g}(z_{h})-z_{ghg^{-1}}\|<\varepsilon
for all
g,h\in G
.

Obviously, in the abelian case,
Condition (
3
)
reduces to
\|\alpha_{g}(z_{h})-z_{h}\|<\varepsilon
.
The following lemma is only intended to show that Lemma 3.1 of
[
28
]
is still true in the setting of nonabelian groups.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
A
be a separable unital C*-algebra and let
G
be a finite group (not necessarily abelian).
Let
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be an action.
Then
\alpha
is approximately representable in the sense of Izumi (Definition
3.3
) if and only if

\alpha
is approximately representable in the sense of Phillips (Definition
3.4
).

Proof.

We first prove the backwards implication. Since
A
is separable,
we can choose finite sets
F_{n}\subset A
such that

F_{1}\subseteq F_{2}\subseteq\ldots
and
\overline{\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}}=A
.
Applying Definition
3.4
for each
F_{n}
, we get

z_{n}(g)\in{\operatorname{U}}(A)
for
g\in G
such that:

(1)

\|\alpha_{g}(a)-z_{n}(g)az_{n}(g)^{*}\|<\frac{1}{n}
for all
a\in F_{n}
and all
g\in G
.

(2)

\|z_{n}(g)z_{n}(h)-z_{n}(gh)\|<\frac{1}{n}
for all
g,h\in G
.

(3)

\|\alpha_{g}(z_{n}(h))-z_{n}(ghg^{-1})\|<\frac{1}{n}
for all
g,h\in G
.

Now, for
g\in G
, set
w^{(0)}_{g}=(z_{n}(g))_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}}
as an element in
l^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},A)

and let
w(g)
be its image in
A_{\infty}
.
Then, for all
a\in\cup_{m=1}^{\infty}F_{m}
, we have

(3.1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\alpha_{g}(a)-z_{n}(g)az_{n}(g)^{*}\right\|=0.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
x\in A
. Choose
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
and
a\in F_{m}
such that

(3.2)

\|x-a\|<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}.

By (
3.1
), we can choose
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
such that
N\geq m
and for all

(3.3)

\left\|\alpha_{g}(a)-z_{n}(g)az_{n}(g)^{*}\right\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

For all

, we use (

) and (
3.3
) to get

\displaystyle\left\|\alpha_{g}(x)-z_{n}(g)xz_{n}(g)^{*}\right\|

\displaystyle<\left\|\alpha_{g}(x)-\alpha_{g}(a)\right\|+\left\|\alpha_{g}(a)-z_{n}(g)az_{n}(g)^{*}\right\|

\displaystyle\qquad+\|z_{n}(g)\|\cdot\|a-x\|\cdot\|z_{n}(g)^{*}\|

\displaystyle<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}=\varepsilon.

Thus,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\alpha_{g}(x)-z_{n}(g)xz_{n}(g)^{*}\right\|=0.

Therefore, for all
x\in A
, and all
g\in G
,

\alpha_{\infty,g}(x)=w(g)xw(g)^{*}.

By (
2
) and (
3
), we further have, for all

,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|z_{n}(g)z_{n}(h)-z_{n}(gh)\right\|=0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\alpha_{g}(z_{n}(h))-z_{n}(ghg^{-1})\right\|=0.

Thus, the map

is a unitary representation from
G
to
A_{\infty}
and

\alpha_{\infty,g}(w(h))=w(ghg^{-1})
for all
g,h\in G
.

To prove the forwards implication, consider

in Definition

as being presented by a sequence

Then

Now using semiprojectivity of

.
Then we still have, for all

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\alpha_{g}(a)-u_{n}(g)au_{n}(g)\|=0\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\alpha_{g}(u_{n}(h))-u_{n}(ghg^{-1})\|=0.

Now take

for some fixed and sufficiently large

.
Therefore Condition (
1
) through Condition (

)
in Definition

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
let
\big((G,A_{j},\alpha^{(j)})_{j\in J},(\varphi_{j,k})_{j\leq k}\big)
be a direct system of
G
-algebras,
let
A
be the direct limit of the
A_{j}
,
and let
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be the direct limit of the
\alpha^{(j)}
.
If
\alpha^{(j)}
is inner for each
j
, then
\alpha
is strictly approximately inner.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
, let
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, and let
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}\in A
.
Choose
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}

and
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m}\in A_{n}

such that, for
j=1,2,\ldots,m
,

(3.4)

\|\varphi_{\infty,n}(b_{j})-a_{j}\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Since
\alpha^{(n)}
is inner,
there is a homomorphism
g\mapsto z_{g}
from
G
to
{\operatorname{U}}(A_{n})

such that, for all
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
and all
g\in G
,

(3.5)

\alpha^{(n)}_{g}(b_{j})=z_{g}b_{j}z^{*}_{g}.

Define
w_{g}=\varphi_{\infty,n}(z_{g})
. Therefore,
using
\varphi_{\infty,n}\circ\alpha^{(n)}_{g}=\alpha_{g}\circ\varphi_{\infty,n}

and (
3.5
) at the first step and using (
3.4
) at the second step,

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{g}(a_{j})-w_{g}a_{j}w^{*}_{g}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\alpha_{g}(a_{j})-\alpha_{g}(\varphi_{\infty,n}(b_{j}))\|

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\varphi_{\infty,n}(z_{g})\varphi_{\infty,n}(b_{j})\varphi_{\infty,n}(z^{*}_{g})-w_{g}a_{j}w^{*}_{g}\|

\displaystyle<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon.

This completes the proof.

Notation 3.14
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra and let
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be an action
of a finite group
G
on
A
.
For
g\in G
,
we let
u_{g}
be the element of
C_{\mathrm{c}}(G,A,\alpha)

which takes the value
1
at
g

and
0
at the other elements of
G
.
We use the same notation for its image in
C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)
.
Also, for each
g\in G
, we define the map

E_{g}\colon C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)\to A
by

E_{g}(a)=a_{g},

where
a=\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}u_{g}
.
We also denote by
A^{\alpha}
the fixed
point algebra, given by

A^{\alpha}=\big\{a\in A\colon\alpha_{g}(a)=a\mbox{ for all }g\in G\big\}.

It is known that the Rokhlin property allows an
approximate homomorphism from the C*-algebra into the fixed point algebra
and
approximate representability allows an approximate homomorphism from
the crossed product into the C*-algebra in
the more general setting of finite groups
(see
[
5
,
15
]
).
Now, in the following lemma, we show that strict approximate innerness, which is a weakening of approximate representability,
suffices to provide
an approximate homomorphism from
the crossed product into the C*-algebra in the setting of finite groups.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra and let
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)
be an action of a
finite group
G
on
A
which is strictly approximately inner.
Then for every finite
set
F\subset C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a unital surjective linear map

\psi\colon C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)\to A
such that:

(1)

\|\psi\|\leq{\operatorname{card}}(G)
.

(2)

\psi(a)=a
for all
a\in A
.

(3)

\|\psi(xyz^{*})-\psi(x)\psi(y)\psi(z)^{*}\|<\varepsilon
for all
x,y,z\in F
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
F\subset C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)
be a finite set.
Set

F_{0}=\Big\{E_{g}(v),E_{g}(v)^{*}\colon g\in G\mbox{ and }v\in F\Big\},

M=\max\Big(1,\ \max_{a\in F_{0}}\|a\|\Big),\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\varepsilon^{\prime}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2M^{2}{\operatorname{card}}(G)^{3}}.

Applying Lemma
3.8
with
\varepsilon^{\prime}
in place of

\varepsilon
and
F_{0}
as given, we get a homomorphism
g\mapsto z_{g}

from
G
to
U(A)
such that, for all
g\in G
and
a\in F_{0}
,

(3.6)

\|\alpha_{g}(a)-z_{g}az_{g}^{*}\|<\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Now define
\psi\colon C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)\to A
by

\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}u_{g}\mapsto\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}z_{g}.

Clearly
\psi
is unital, linear, and surjective.
To prove (
1
), let

\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}u_{g}
be an arbitrary element in
C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)
. Then

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\psi\Bigg(\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}u_{g}\Bigg)\Bigg\|=\Bigg\|\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}z_{g}\Bigg\|\leq\sum_{g\in G}\|a_{g}\|\leq{\operatorname{card}}(G)\Bigg\|\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}u_{g}\Bigg\|.

Part (
2
) is immediate from the fact that
z_{1}=1_{A}
.
To prove (
3
), let

x=\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}u_{g},\quad y=\sum_{h\in G}b_{h}u_{h},\quad\mbox{and}\quad z=\sum_{t\in G}c_{t}u_{t}

be arbitrary elements in
F
.
So

z^{*}=\sum_{t\in G}\alpha_{t^{-1}}(c^{*}_{t})u_{t^{-1}}
.
A short computation then shows that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\psi(xyz^{*})=\sum_{g,h,t\in G}a_{g}\alpha_{g}(b_{h})\alpha_{ght^{-1}}(c^{*}_{t})z_{ght^{-1}}

and

(3.8)

\psi(x)\psi(y)\psi(z)^{*}=\sum_{g,h,t\in G}a_{g}z_{g}b_{h}z_{h}z^{*}_{t}c^{*}_{t}.

Therefore, using (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) at the first step and
using (
3.6
) at the second step,

\displaystyle\|\psi(xyz^{*})-\psi(x)\psi(y)\psi(z)^{*}\|

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt{\mbox{}}\leq\sum_{g,h,t\in G}\|a_{g}\|\cdot\|\alpha_{g}(b_{h})-z_{g}b_{h}z^{*}_{g}\|\cdot\|\alpha_{ght^{-1}}(c^{*}_{t})z_{ght^{-1}}\|

\displaystyle\hskip 60.00009pt{\mbox{}}+\sum_{g,h,t\in G}\|a_{g}z_{g}b_{h}z^{*}_{g}\|\cdot\|\alpha_{ght^{-1}}(c^{*}_{t})-z_{ght^{-1}}c^{*}_{t}z^{*}_{ght^{-1}}\|\cdot\|z_{ght^{-1}}\|

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt{\mbox{}}<{\operatorname{card}}(G)^{3}M^{2}\varepsilon^{\prime}+{\operatorname{card}}(G)^{3}M^{2}\varepsilon^{\prime}=2{\operatorname{card}}(G)^{3}M^{2}\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra and let

be an action of a

on

if and only if

Proof.

We only need to prove the backwards implication. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Let
a,b\in A_{+}
.
By Lemma
2.3
(
3
), it suffices to show that

(a-\varepsilon)_{+}\precsim_{A}b
.
We use
a\precsim_{C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)}b
to find

such that

(3.9)

\left\|vbv^{*}-a\right\|<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2{\operatorname{card}}(G)}.

Set

F=\big\{b,v,v^{*}\big\}
.
Now, we apply Lemma
3.15
with
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and
F
as given to get a unital surjective linear map

\psi\colon C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)\to A
such that:

(1)

\|\psi\|\leq{\operatorname{card}}(G)
.

(2)

\psi(c)=c
for all

.

(3)

\left\|\psi(xyz^{*})-\psi(x)\psi(y)\psi(z)^{*}\right\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

.

Using (
2
) at the first step,
using (
3.9
) and (
3
) at the second step,
and using (
1
) at the last step, we get

\displaystyle\left\|a-\psi(v)b\psi(v)^{*}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\psi\left(a\right)-\psi\left(vbv^{*}\right)\right\|+\left\|\psi\left(vbv^{*}\right)-\psi(v)\psi(b)\psi(v)^{*}\right\|

Therefore, using (

To prove (

Question 6.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
let
A
be
an infinite-dimensional stably finite simple unital C*-algebra,
and let
\alpha\colon G\to{\operatorname{Aut}}(A)

Does it follow that

{\operatorname{rc}}(A)={\operatorname{rc}}\big(C^{*}(G,A,\alpha)\big)?

Question 6.2

Does there exist a strictly approximately inner action of a nontrivial nonabelian finite group
on a unital C*-algebra which is not approximately representable?

Question 6.3

Does there exist a tracially strictly approximately inner action of a nontrivial nonabelian finite group
on an infinite-dimensional simple unital C*-algebra which is not strictly approximately inner and not tracially approximately representable?

Question 6.4
.

Let
\alpha
be an action of a nontrivial finite group on a C*-algebra
which is strictly approximately inner and strongly approximately inner.
Does it follow that

Diophantine Tuples over \symAMSb ​ Z p {\symAMSb Z}_{p}
Nitya Mani, Simon Rubinstein-Salzedo

Abstract
. For an element r r of a ring R R , a D ⁡ ( r ) D(r) m m -tuple is an m m -tuple ( a 1 , a 2 , … , a m ) (a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}) of elements of R R such that for all i , j i,j with i ≠ j i\neq j , a i ​ a j + r a_{i}a_{j}+r is a perfect square in R R . In this article, we compute and estimate the measures of the sets of D ⁡ ( r ) D(r) m m -tuples in the ring \symAMSb ​ Z p {\symAMSb Z}_{p} of p p -adic integers, as well as its residue field \symAMSb ​ F p {\symAMSb F}_{p} .



1.
Introduction

The study of Diophantine
m
-tuples has a long history, attracting the attention of the some of the most prominent mathematicians. The story begins in the 3rd century with Diophantus, who first observed that the quadruple
(\frac{1}{16},\frac{33}{16},\frac{17}{4},\frac{105}{16})
has the curious property that the product of any two elements in the set was one less than a rational square. It was not until Fermat that the first quadruple of integers with this property was discovered, namely
(1,3,8,120)
:
1\cdot 3+1=2^{2}
,
3\cdot 8+1=5^{2}
,
\ldots
,
3\cdot 120+1=19^{2}
. Euler found infinitely many Diophantine quadruples in the positive integers and also found a way of extending Fermat’s set to a rational quintuple with this property, namely
(1,3,8,120,\frac{777480}{8288641})
. Such sets of numbers are now known as
Diophantine
m
-tuples
, precisely defined below:

Definition 1.1
.

For a commutative ring with unit
R
, an
m
-tuple
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in R^{m}
is said to be a
Diophantine
m
-tuple over
R
if for every
i,j
with
1\leq i<j\leq m
,
a_{i}a_{j}+1\in\square(R)
, where
\square(R)
denotes the set of square elements in ring
R
.

Here, we will leverage some notation that enables us to include a more general family of sets than just the Diophantine
m
-tuples, as investigated in a number of works, such as
[
Duj04
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

For
r\in R
, an
m
-tuple
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in R^{m}
is said to be a
\D(r)

m
-tuple over
R
if

a_{i}a_{j}+r\in\square(R),\quad\text{for all }1\leq i<j\leq m.

We denote by
\Diop_{m}^{r}(R)\subset R^{m}
the set of
\D(r)

m
-tuples over
R
. Note that
\Diop_{m}^{1}(R)
is the set of Diophantine
m
-tuples over
R
.

In this notation, Diophantus’s original example
(\frac{1}{16},\frac{33}{16},\frac{17}{4},\frac{105}{16})
is in
\Dioph_{4}^{1}({\symAMSb Q})
, and after clearing denominators, we see that
(1,33,68,105)\in\Dioph_{4}^{256}({\symAMSb Z})
. Fermat’s first Diophantine (i.e.
\D(1)
) quadruple in the positive integers is
(1,3,8,120)\in\Dioph_{4}^{1}({\symAMSb Z})
.

The problem of extending Fermat’s triple
\{1,3,8\}
to a quadruple was one of the early successes of Baker’s results on linear forms in logarithms; see
[
BD69
]
. More recently, Gibbs in
[
Gib06
]
found a rational Diophantine sextuple, namely
(\frac{11}{192},\frac{35}{192},\frac{155}{27},\frac{512}{27},\frac{1235}{48},\frac{180873}{16})\in\Dioph_{6}^{1}({\symAMSb Q})
. Recently, He, Togbé, and Ziegler in
[
HTZ16
]
showed that there is no Diophantine quintuple in positive integers, i.e. that
\Dioph_{5}^{1}({\symAMSb Z})=\varnothing
.

A curious property about Diophantine tuples is that any triple, over any ring (or even any semiring) can be extended to a Diophantine quadruple. This is special to the case of
\D(1)
(or more generally
\D(k^{2})
), as
\D(r)
triples do not generally extend to
\D(r)
quadruples in interesting ways. (Of course, one can always extend any
\D(r)

m
-tuple to a
\D(r)

(m+1)
-tuple by appending 0, but this is rather trivial.) An open question of considerable interest is whether or not every Diophantine triple
(a,b,c)
in the positive integers can be uniquely extended to a Diophantine quadruple
(a,b,c,d)
with
d\in{\symAMSb Z}
and
d>\max(a,b,c)
.

Most of the past work in Diophantine and
\D(r)
tuples has been in the case of
R={\symAMSb Z}
(or even
{\symAMSb N}
) and
R={\symAMSb Q}
. There have been a few papers studying the case where
R
is the ring of integers in a quadratic field (e.g.
[
Duj97
,
Fra04
]
) and a cubic field (e.g.
[
Fra13
]
). The question of Diophantine tuples over finite fields was considered in
[
DK16
]
.
In recent years, there has been a flurry of activity on Diophantine and
\D(r)
tuples. Instead of summarizing more of it, we instead refer the interested reader to Dujella’s extensive bibliography of the field, which can be found at
https://web.math.pmf.unizg.hr/~duje/ref.html
.

In the present paper, we investigate some questions similar in spirit to those of
[
DK16
]
. In
[
DK16
]
and frequently in this literature, it is assumed that elements of a Diophantine tuple must be distinct. However, our main goal is an understanding of the Haar measure of tuples over
{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
. To do so, it is often convenient to reduce modulo
p
, so we do not require our tuples to have distinct elements.

The main goal of this article is to evaluate or approximate the Haar measure of the set
\Diop_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
, for certain values of
m
,
r
, and
p
. Then our goal is to evaluate
\diop_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
defined below:

Definition 1.3
.

For
r\in R
, let
\diop_{m}^{r}(R)=\mu(\Diop_{m}^{r}(R))
be the measure of the set of
\D(r)

m
-tuples in
R^{m}
, where
\mu
is the product measure induced by a choice of finite measure
\mu
on
R
.

Throughout this article, we will assume that
R
is a commutative ring equipped with a probability measure
\mu
, i.e. one in which
\mu(R)=1
. The main case we will be focusing on is the case in which
R
is the ring
{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
of
p
-adic integers equipped with its Haar measure
\mu
, normalized so that
\mu({\symAMSb Z}_{p})=1
. We will also look at the case where
R=\mathcal{O}_{K}
is the valuation ring of a finite extension
K
of
{\symAMSb Q}_{p}
, again equipped with its normalized Haar measure. We will also consider the case where
R={\symAMSb F}_{q}
is a finite field, equipped with the normalized counting measure, where the normalization is such that
\mu({\symAMSb F}_{q})=1
.

Unless otherwise specified, we take
a,b,c,d\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
. All of our results over
{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
can be easily modified to obtain bounds over
{\symAMSb F}_{p}
with respect to the counting measure on
{\symAMSb F}_{p}
and vice versa, modulo lower order terms.

In the case where
m
is small, we can usually give an exact value for
\diop_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
, whereas when
m\geq 4
, we can only give an asymptotic as
p\to\infty
, with a lower-order error term. One of our main techniques is to study the reduction to
\diop_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
and then use results about character sums in
{\symAMSb F}_{p}
. More precisely, we note that
\diop_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
is quite close to

\frac{1}{2^{\binom{m}{2}}}\int_{{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{m}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\left(1+\left(\frac{a_{i}a_{j}+1}{p}\right)\right)\,d\mu,

which can be calculated exactly for
m\leq 3
and approximated up to an error term of the form
O(p^{-1/2})
as
p\to\infty
when
m\geq 4
. Here and elsewhere,
\left(\frac{\cdot}{p}\right)
denotes the Legendre symbol on
{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
.

We proceed as follows in this article. In Section
2
we collect some notation and observe that the Haar measure of Diophantine pairs over
{\symAMSb Z}_{2}
is
\frac{1}{3}
. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
3.1
and exactly compute the Haar measure of Diophantine
D(r)
pairs over
{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
for all odd primes
p
. We observe in Section
4
that when
p=3
, we can compute the Haar measure exactly of
D(r)

m
-tuples for all
\left(\frac{r}{3}\right)=1
and
m\geq 2
. We proceed in Section
5
to give asymptotic bounds in Theorem
5.2
on
\diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
, exactly computing
\diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
in Theorem
5.3
as an intermediate step. We extend our asymptotic analysis of
\Diop_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
to
m\geq 4
in Section
6
. Finally, we are able to carry over many of our methods when we replace
{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
by a finite extension thereof as we observe in Section
7
, for example exactly computing the Haar measure of Diophantine
D(r)
pairs in Theorem
7.1
when the residue characteristic is odd.

2.
\Diop_{2}^{1}({\symAMSb Z}_{2})

Before stating any results, let us recall some notation that will be used throughout the article.

Definition 2.1
.

For
a\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
, we let
v_{p}(a)
denote the
p
-adic valuation
of
a
, given by

v_{p}(a)=\sup\left\{k\in{\symAMSb Z}_{\geq 0}:a\in p^{k}{\symAMSb Z}_{p}\right\}.

Definition 2.2
.

More generally, if
R=\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a
valuation ring
, we let
v_{R}
denote the normalized valuation so that if
\pi\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a uniformizer, then
v_{R}(\pi)=1
.

Note that
(a,b)\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{2}
is a Diophantine pair over
{\symAMSb Z}_{2}
if
ab+1\in\square({\symAMSb Z}_{2})
. An element
\alpha=2^{v_{2}(\alpha)}\beta\in\square({\symAMSb Z}_{2})
if

v_{2}(\alpha)\equiv 0\pmod{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta\equiv 1\pmod{8}.

Proposition 2.3
.

The Haar measure of Diophantine pairs over
{\symAMSb Z}_{2}
is given by

\diop_{2}^{1}({\symAMSb Z}_{2})=\frac{1}{3}.

Proof.

We consider cases based on the residues of
ab
modulo
8
and the
2
-adic valuation of
ab+1
in
{\symAMSb Z}_{2}
. To that end, we will define the measures of disjoint subsets of
{\symAMSb Z}_{2}^{2}
. For
k\geq 0
, let

A_{2k}=\{(a,b)\in\Diop_{2}^{1}({\symAMSb Z}_{2}):v_{2}(ab+1)=2k\}.

If
ab+1\not\equiv 0\pmod{4}
,
ab+1=\square\in{\symAMSb Z}_{2}
exactly when
ab\equiv 0\pmod{8}
. This occurs when either one of
a,b
is
0\pmod{8}
or

(a,b)\pmod{8}\in\{(\pm 2,4),(4,\pm 2),(4,4)\}.

This yields

A_{0}=\frac{7+7+1+2+2+1}{64}=\frac{5}{16}.

If
v_{2}(ab+1)=2
, then
ab+1=\square\in{\symAMSb Z}_{2}
exactly when
ab+1\equiv 4\pmod{32}
. Considering residue classes of
a,b\pmod{32}
yields
A_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{32}
. Similarly we obtain for
v_{2}(ab+1)=2k
for
k>1
, that
ab+1=\square
exactly when

ab+1=2^{2k}\cdot\alpha,\quad\alpha\equiv 1\pmod{8}

or in other words when

ab+1\equiv 2^{2k}\mod 2^{2k+3}.

The above result implies that for
k\geq 1
, we have that

A_{2k}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{2k+3}}=\frac{1}{2^{2k+4}}.

We therefore obtain the desired result:

\diop_{2}^{1}({\symAMSb Z}_{2})=\mu\{(a,b)\in{\symAMSb Z}_{2}^{2}:ab+1\in\square({\symAMSb Z}_{2})\}=A_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{2k}=\frac{5}{16}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{2k+4}}=\frac{5}{16}+\frac{1}{48}=\frac{1}{3}.

∎

3.
\Diop_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and let
\alpha=v_{p}(r)
with
r=p^{\alpha}s
. Then, we have the following:

\diop_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p(p+1)}&\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)=1,\\
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}&\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)=-1,\\
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)^{2}}-\frac{(\alpha+2)}{2(p+1)^{2}p^{\alpha-1}}-\frac{1}{2(p+1)^{2}p^{\alpha}}+\frac{(\alpha-1)}{2(p+1)^{2}p^{\alpha+1}}&\alpha\equiv 1\pmod{2},\\
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(\alpha+1)(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{\alpha+2}}-\frac{2p-1}{2(p+1)^{2}}+\frac{1}{2(p+1)^{2}p}-\frac{(\alpha+1)}{2(p+1)^{2}p^{\alpha-2}}\\
\hskip 8.50012pt+\frac{(\alpha-1)}{2(p+1)^{2}p^{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{2(p+1)^{2}p^{\alpha+1}}-\frac{1}{2(p+1)^{2}p^{\alpha+2}}&\alpha>0,\,\alpha\equiv 0\pmod{2},\left(\frac{s}{p}\right)=1,\\
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha(p-1)^{2}+p^{2}+1}{2p^{\alpha+2}}-\frac{2p-1}{2(p+1)^{2}}+\frac{1}{2(p+1)^{2}p}-\frac{(\alpha+1)}{2(p+1)^{2}p^{\alpha-2}}\\
\hskip 8.50012pt+\frac{(\alpha-1)}{2(p+1)^{2}p^{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{2(p+1)^{2}p^{\alpha+1}}-\frac{1}{2(p+1)^{2}p^{\alpha+2}}&\alpha>0,\,\alpha\equiv 0\pmod{2},\left(\frac{s}{p}\right)=-1,\end{cases}

where
(\frac{a}{b})
denotes the Legendre symbol.
In particular, we have that the measure of the Diophantine pairs over
{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
is given by

\diop_{2}^{1}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p(p+1)}.

Remark 3.2
.

We will repeatedly use the following characterization of
\square({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
. For an odd prime
p
,
\alpha\in\square({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
exactly if
\alpha=p^{2k}\beta
for some nonnegative integer
k
and some
\beta\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
with
\left(\frac{\beta}{p}\right)=1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and take
r\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
so that
\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)=-1
. Then,

\dioph_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}.

Proof.

We compute
\dioph_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
by summing the measures of disjoint subsets of
\Diop_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
. For
k\geq 0
, let

A_{k}=\left\{(a,b)\in\Diop_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p}):v_{p}(ab+r)=2k\right\}.

First, we consider
A_{0}
. If
(a,b)\in A_{0}
, then
ab+r=\beta
where
\left(\frac{\beta}{p}\right)=1
. Thus

ab=\beta-r\not\equiv 0\pmod{p},

since
\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)=-1
. This implies that for each choice of nonzero residue class of
a\pmod{p}
and every nonzero square residue class choice of
\beta\pmod{p}
, there is exactly one choice of residue class
\pmod{p}
for
b
that results in
ab+r\in A_{0}
. Since there are
(p-1)/2
nonzero square residue classes
\pmod{p}
, we have that

\mu(A_{0})=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\cdot(p-1)\cdot\frac{(p-1)}{2}=\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{2}}.

Similarly, for
k\geq 1
, if
(a,b)\in A_{k}
, then
ab+r=p^{2k}\cdot\beta
where
\left(\frac{\beta}{p}\right)=1
. Here

ab=p^{2k}\beta-r\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}

since
r\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.
such that
a\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
and the resulting possibilities for
b\pmod{p^{2k+1}}
, we obtain

\mu(A_{2k})=\frac{1}{p^{4k+2}}\cdot(p^{2k+1}-p^{2k})\cdot\frac{p-1}{2}=\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{2k+2}}.

This yields the desired result:

\diop_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu(A_{k})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{2k+2}}=\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{2}}\cdot\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}-1}=\frac{(p-1)}{2(p+1)}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and take
r\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
so that
\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)=1
. Then,

\dioph_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p(p+1)}.

Proof.

As above, we compute
\dioph_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
by summing the measures of disjoint subsets of
\Diop_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
. For
k\geq 0
, let

A_{k}=\left\{(a,b)\in\Diop_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p}):v_{p}(ab+r)=2k\right\}.

We first consider
A_{0}
. If
(a,b)\in A_{0}
, then
ab+r=\beta
where
\left(\frac{\beta}{p}\right)=1
. Since
\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)=1
, if either
a,b\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, then
ab+r\equiv r\pmod{p}
is nonzero square in
{\symAMSb F}_{p}
and thus a square in
{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
. We note that

\mu(\{(a,b)\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{2}:ab\equiv 0\pmod{p}\})=\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}.

Else, we write

ab=\beta-r\pmod{p}

This expression is nonzero as long as
\beta\neq r
. This implies that for each choice of nonzero residue class of
a\pmod{p}
and every nonzero square residue class choice of
\beta\pmod{p}
except
r\pmod{p}
, there is exactly one choice of residue class
\pmod{p}
for
b
that results in
ab+r\in A_{0}
. Since there are
(p-1)/2
nonzero square residue classes
\pmod{p}
, we have that

\mu(A_{0})=\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}+\frac{1}{p^{2}}\cdot(p-1)\cdot\left(\frac{(p-1)}{2}-1\right)=\frac{p^{2}+1}{2p^{2}}.

Similar to Lemma
3.3
, for
k\geq 1
, if
(a,b)\in A_{k}
, then
ab+r=p^{2k}\cdot\beta
where
\left(\frac{\beta}{p}\right)=1
. Here

ab=p^{2k}\beta-r\not\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k+1}}.

since
r\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Considering all residue class of
a

\pmod{p}^{3}
such that
a\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
and the resulting possibilities for
b\pmod{p}^{3}
, we obtain

\mu(A_{2k})=\frac{1}{p^{4k+2}}\cdot(p^{2k+1}-p^{2k})\cdot\frac{p-1}{2}=\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{2k+2}}.

This yields the desired result:

\displaystyle\diop_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu(A_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{2}+1}{2p^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{2k+2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{2}+1}{2p^{2}}+\frac{p-1}{2p^{2}(p+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{2}+p+2}{2p(p+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p(p+1)}.

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Fix some
r\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
such that
p\mid r
, with
v_{p}(r)=\alpha>0
. Then for all
\beta\equiv 0\pmod{2}
such that
\beta>\alpha
, if

B_{\beta}=\{(a,b)\in\Diop_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p}):v_{p}(ab+r)=\beta\},

then

\mu(B_{\beta})=\frac{(p-1)(p^{\alpha+1}-1)}{2p^{\beta+2}}.

Proof.

We can write
r=p^{\alpha}s
with
s\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{\times}
, and note that
\alpha\geq 1
. Note that for any
(a,b)\in B_{\beta}
,
ab+r=p^{\beta}q^{2}
for some
q\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{\times}
. Thus,
(a,b)\in B_{\beta}
iff there exists some
q\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{\times}
such that

ab\equiv p^{\alpha}\left(p^{\beta-\alpha}q^{2}-s\right)\pmod{p^{\beta+1}}.

Thus, we can compute
\mu(B_{\beta})
using a probability over the residue classes of
a,b
in
{\symAMSb Z}/p^{\beta+1}{\symAMSb Z}
.
We can write
a=p^{e_{a}}a^{\prime},b=p^{e_{b}}b^{\prime}
where
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{\times}
, and
e_{a}+e_{b}=\alpha
. Then, there are
p^{\beta+1-e_{a}}-p^{\beta-e_{a}}
choices for the residue
a\pmod{p}^{\beta+1}
, such that there exists some
q
and choice(s) of
b\pmod{p}^{\beta+1}
to satisfy the above equivalence.

For every such choice of
a
, there are
\frac{p-1}{2}
choices for
b^{\prime}\pmod{p^{\beta-\alpha+1}}
, corresponding to the
\frac{p-1}{2}
choices for the residue
q^{2}\pmod{p}
. There are
p^{\alpha}
ways to lift this residue
\pmod{p^{\beta+1-\alpha}}
to a residue
\pmod{p^{\beta+1}}
, yielding
p^{\alpha}\cdot(p-1)/2
residue choices of
b\pmod{p^{\beta+1}}
for each choice of
a
, such that there exists some
q\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
where the above equivalence holds. Since
e_{a}
can be any integer from
0
to
\alpha
, inclusive, this gives the desired result:

\displaystyle\mu(B_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{e_{a}=0}^{\alpha}\frac{p^{\beta-e_{a}}(p-1)p^{\alpha}(p-1)}{2p^{2(\beta+1)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e_{a}=0}^{\alpha}\frac{p^{\alpha-e_{a}}(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{\beta+2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{\beta-\alpha+2}}\sum_{e_{a}=0}^{\alpha}\frac{1}{p^{e_{a}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{\beta-\alpha+2}}\cdot\frac{p^{\alpha+1}-1}{p^{\alpha}(p-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-1)(p^{\alpha+1}-1)}{2p^{\beta+2}}.

∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Fix some
r\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
such that
p\mid r
, with
v_{p}(r)=\alpha
. Let
\beta\equiv 0\pmod{2}
be such that
\beta<\alpha
, and set

B_{\beta}=\{(a,b)\in\Diop_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p}):v_{p}(ab+r)=\beta\}.

Then

\mu(B_{\beta})=\frac{(\beta+1)(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{\beta+2}}.

Proof.

As above, we write
r=p^{\alpha}s
with
s\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{\times}
, and note that
\alpha\geq 1
. Note that for any
(a,b)\in B_{\beta}
,
ab+r=p^{\beta}q^{2}
for some
q\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{\times}
. Thus,
(a,b)\in B_{\beta}
iff there exists some
q\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{\times}
such that

ab\equiv p^{\beta}\left(q^{2}-p^{\alpha-\beta}s\right)\pmod{p^{\alpha+1}}.

Thus, we can compute
\mu(B_{\beta})
using a probability over the residue classes of
a,b
in
{\symAMSb Z}/p^{\beta+1}{\symAMSb Z}

p^{\alpha+1-e_{a}}-p^{\alpha-e_{a}}

q
and choice(s) of

to satisfy the above equivalence.

For every such choice of
a

\frac{p-1}{2}

\frac{p-1}{2}
choices for the residue

p^{\alpha-e_{b}}

to a residue of
b\pmod{p^{\alpha+1}}

p^{\alpha-e_{b}}\cdot(p-1)/2
residue choices of
b\pmod{p^{\beta+1}}
for each choice of

q\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
where the above equivalence holds. Since
e_{a}

\alpha

\displaystyle\mu(B_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{e_{a}=0}^{\beta}\frac{p^{\alpha-e_{a}}(p-1)p^{\alpha-e_{b}}(p-1)/2}{p^{2(\alpha+1)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e_{a}=0}^{\beta}\frac{p^{2\alpha-e_{a}-e_{b}}(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{2(\alpha+1)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e_{a}=0}^{\beta}\frac{p^{-\beta}(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\beta+1)(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{\beta+2}}.

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Fix some
r\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
with
v_{p}(r)=\alpha>0
and let

. If

then

\mu(B_{\alpha})=\begin{cases}\frac{(\alpha+1)(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{\alpha+2}}&\alpha\equiv 0\pmod{2},\ \left(\frac{s}{p}\right)=1,\\
\frac{\alpha(p-1)^{2}+p^{2}+1}{2p^{\alpha+2}}&\alpha\equiv 0\pmod{2},\ \left(\frac{s}{p}\right)=-1,\\
0&\alpha\equiv 1\pmod{2}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that if
\alpha\equiv 1\pmod{2}
, then no element

with
v_{p}(x)=\alpha
is a square. So now assume that
\alpha\equiv 0\pmod{2}
. For any
(a,b)\in B_{\alpha}
, we have
ab+r=p^{\alpha}q^{2}
for some
q\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{\times}
. Rearranging, we find that any
(a,b)\in B_{\alpha}
satisfies
ab=p^{\alpha}(q^{2}-s)
for some
q\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{\times}
. Note that if
v_{p}(q^{2}-s)=\gamma
, we can check if
(a,b)\in\Diop_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
by seeing if
ab+r
is a square
\pmod{p^{\alpha+\gamma+1}}
.
Thus, we consider two cases for
\gamma
.

If
\gamma=0
, then
q^{2}-s\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{\times}
, and we can check if
ab+r\in\Diop_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
by computing the residue
\pmod{p^{\alpha+1}}
.
As above, we write
a=p^{e_{a}}a^{\prime},b=p^{e_{b}}b^{\prime}
where
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{\times}
and
e_{a}+e_{b}=\alpha
.
There are
p^{\alpha-e_{a}}(p-1)
choices for
a\pmod{p^{\alpha+1}}
such that there exists
b\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
with
ab+r\in\Diop_{2}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
. For each such fixed choice of a residue class for
a

\frac{p-1}{2}

if
\left(\frac{s}{p}\right)=-1
, and
\frac{p-3}{2}

b^{\prime}\pmod{p}

\left(\frac{s}{p}\right)=1
, and
p^{\alpha-e_{b}}
ways to lift each such choice of

to a residue

such that

for some

. In particular, this implies that if
\left(\frac{s}{p}\right)=-1

\mu(B_{\alpha})=\sum_{e_{a}=0}^{\alpha}\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{\alpha+2}}=\frac{(\alpha+1)(p-1)^{2}}{2p^{\alpha+2}}.

If
\gamma\geq 1
, then
\left(\frac{s}{p}\right)=1

q^{2}-s

\pmod{p}
, but not modulo higher powers of

q^{2}-s=p^{\gamma}z

in these cases reduces to a check of

. Using the same decomposition of
a,b
as above,
a=p^{e_{a}}a^{\prime},b=p^{e_{b}}b^{\prime}
with
e_{a}+e_{b}=\alpha+\gamma
, we have
p^{\alpha+\gamma-e_{a}}(p-1)
choices for
a\pmod{p^{\alpha+\gamma+1}}
such that there exists some

ab\equiv p^{\alpha+\gamma}z\pmod{p^{\alpha+\gamma+1}},\quad z\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{\times}.

For each such choice of
a
, there are
p^{\alpha+\gamma-e_{b}}(p-1)
choices for the residue
b\pmod{p^{\alpha+\gamma+1}}
such that there is some

satisfying the above equivalence. This yields a

\frac{p^{\alpha+\gamma-e_{a}}(p-1)p^{\alpha+\gamma-e_{b}}(p-1)}{p^{2(\alpha+\gamma+1)}}=\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{p^{\alpha+\gamma+2}}

fraction of the residue classes
(a,b)\pmod{p}^{\alpha+\gamma+1}
such that

with

such that

Combining these disjoint cases gives the desired equality when
\left(\frac{s}{p}\right)=1

\displaystyle\mu(B_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\sum_{e_{a}=0}^{\alpha}\frac{(p-3)(p-1)}{2p^{\alpha+2}}+\sum_{\gamma=1}^{\infty}\sum_{e_{a}=0}^{\alpha+\gamma}\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{p^{\alpha+\gamma+2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha+1)(p-3)(p-1)}{2p^{\alpha+2}}+\sum_{\gamma=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha+\gamma+1)(p-1)^{2}}{p^{\alpha+\gamma+2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha+1)(p-3)(p-1)}{2p^{\alpha+2}}+\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{p}\sum_{x=\alpha+2}^{\infty}\frac{x}{p^{x}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha+1)(p-3)(p-1)}{2p^{\alpha+2}}+\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{p}\cdot\frac{1+(\alpha+2)(p-1)}{(p-1)^{2}p^{\alpha+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha+1)(p-3)(p-1)}{2p^{\alpha+2}}+\frac{1+(\alpha+2)(p-1)}{p^{\alpha+2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha+1)(p-3)(p-1)+2+2(\alpha+2)(p-1)}{2p^{\alpha+2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha(p-1)^{2}+p^{2}+1}{2p^{\alpha+2}}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

The result follows immediately by applying Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
when
\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)\neq 0
.
Next, let

4.
\Diop_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{3})

We can exactly compute the Haar measure of
\D(r)

m
-tuples over
{\symAMSb Z}_{3}
for a broad class of values
r
and all
m\geq 2
. More precisely, we will show the following result:

Theorem 4.1
.

For any
r\in{\symAMSb Z}_{3}
with
\left(\frac{r}{3}\right)=1
, the Haar measure of
\D(r)

m
-tuples in
{\symAMSb Z}_{3}^{m}
for
m\geq 2
is

\diop_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{3})=\frac{m^{2}+71m+36}{36\cdot 3^{m}}.

Recall that
\alpha\in\square({\symAMSb Z}_{3})
iff
v_{3}(\alpha)
is even and

\frac{\alpha}{3^{v_{3}(\alpha)}}\equiv 1\pmod{3}.

This leads us to the following observation:

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix
r\in{\symAMSb Z}_{3}
so that
(\frac{r}{3})=1
. Then any
\D(r)
triple
(a,b,c)
over
{\symAMSb Z}_{3}
has at least one of
a,b,c\equiv 0\pmod{3}
.

Proof.

If
(\frac{r}{3})=1
, then
r\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.
Suppose to the contrary that
a,b,c\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}
. Then either two of
a,b,c
are
1\pmod{3}
or
-1\pmod{3}
. Suppose WLOG that
a\equiv b\pmod{3}
. Then

ab\equiv(1)(1)\equiv(-1)(-1)\equiv 1\pmod{3}

and thus

ab+r\equiv ab+1\equiv 2\pmod{3},

so
ab+r
is not a square over
{\symAMSb Z}_{3}
. Thus,
(a,b,c)
cannot be a
\D(r)
triple, a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Fix
r\in{\symAMSb Z}_{3}
such that
\left(\frac{r}{3}\right)=1
. Lemma
4.2
implies that for a
\D(r)

m
-tuple
(a_{1},\ldots a_{m})
with
(\frac{r}{3})=1
, all but at most two of the
a_{i}
’s are congruent to
0\pmod{3}
. We consider cases:

Case 1:

a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\equiv 0\pmod{3}
. Then for any
i\neq j
,

a_{i}a_{j}+r\equiv r\equiv 1\pmod{3}\implies a_{i}a_{j}+r\in\square({\symAMSb Z}_{3}).

Thus all of the above
m
-tuples are in
\Diop_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{3})
. Therefore,

\mu\left\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in\Diop_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{3}):a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\equiv 0\pmod{3}\right\}=\frac{1}{3^{m}}.

Case 2:

Exactly one of the
a_{i}
’s is not congruent to
0\pmod{3}
.
We have that whenever
i\neq j

a_{i}a_{j}+r\equiv r\equiv 1\pmod{3}\implies a_{i}a_{j}+r\in\square({\symAMSb Z}_{3}),

since for all such pairs
i,j
at least one of
a_{i},a_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{3}
.
Since there are
m
ways to choose which element of the triple is not
0\pmod{3}
and two choices of its nonzero residue mod
3
, we have that

\mu\left\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in\Diop_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{3}):\exists a_{i}\not\equiv 0\pmod{3},\,\forall j\neq i,\,a_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{3}\right\}=\frac{2m}{3^{m}}.

Case 3a:

Suppose exactly two of
a_{1},\ldots a_{m}
are not congruent to
0\pmod{3}
,
a_{i},a_{j}
, and those two are distinct modulo 3 with
a_{i}\equiv 1\pmod{3},a_{j}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
.
Then without loss of generality
a_{1}\equiv 1\pmod{3},a_{2}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. For
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
,
(k_{1},k_{2})\neq(i,j),(j,i)
, we have that

a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}}+r\equiv r\equiv 1\pmod{3}\implies a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}}+r\in\square({\symAMSb Z}_{3}).

Now, we compute the measure of pairs
(a_{i},a_{j})
in
{\symAMSb Z}_{3}^{2}
such that
a_{i}a_{j}+r\in\square({\symAMSb Z}_{3})
. For
k=1,2,\ldots
, let

A_{2k}=\{(a_{i},a_{j})\in\Diop_{2}^{1}({\symAMSb Z}_{3}):v_{3}(a_{i}a_{j}+r)=2k\}.

Following the method of Lemma
3.4
, we find that

\mu(A_{2k})=\frac{3^{2k}(3-1)\cdot\frac{3-1}{2}}{3^{2(2k+1)}}=\frac{2}{3^{2k+2}}.

The measure inside
{\symAMSb Z}_{3}^{2}
of the desired set is then

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu(A_{2k})=\frac{2}{9}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^{2k}}=\frac{2}{9}\cdot\frac{1}{9}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{9}}=\frac{2}{9}\cdot\frac{1}{8}=\frac{1}{36}.

Since there are
m(m-1)
ways to pick the pair of elements that are
1,-1\pmod{3}
in the
m
-tuple,

\mu\left\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in\Diop_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{3}):\exists i\neq j,\,a_{i}\equiv 1(3),a_{j}\equiv 2(3),\,\forall k\neq i,j,\,a_{k}\equiv 0(3)\right\}=\frac{m(m-1)}{36\cdot 3^{m}}.

Case 3b:

If for
(a_{1},\ldots a_{m})
there are exactly 2 elements
a_{i},a_{j}\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}
, but
a_{i}\equiv a_{j}\pmod{3}
, then

a_{i}a_{j}+r\equiv r+1\equiv 2\pmod{3}\not\in\square({\symAMSb Z}_{3})

meaning that
(a_{1},\ldots a_{m})
cannot be a
D(r)
-tuple over
{\symAMSb Z}_{3}
.

Case 4:

If at least
3

a_{i}
(WLOG
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
) are not
0\mod 3
, then via Lemma
4.2
, we can find
i\neq j
with
a_{i}a_{j}+1\not\in\square({\symAMSb Z}_{3})
, so

(a_{1},\ldots a_{m})
is not a Diophantine
m
-tuple.

The above allows us to compute the Haar measure of
\D(r)

m
-tuples in

for

\diop_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{3})=\frac{1}{3^{m}}+\frac{2m}{3^{m}}+\frac{m(m-1)}{36\cdot 3^{m}}=\frac{m^{2}+71m+36}{36\cdot 3^{m}}.

∎

5.
\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})

Throughout this section, we take

.

For fixed

Lemma 5.4

(Theorem 5.48

Lemma 5.5

Lemma 5.6
.

Lemma 5.7
.

For fixed
r\in{\symAMSb Z}
viewed in
{\symAMSb F}_{p}
such that
p\nmid r
, we have that

\displaystyle\#\left\{(a,b,c)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p}):(ab+r)(bc+r)(ac+r)\equiv 0\pmod{p},\,abc\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{3p^{2}+3p+4}{4}-\frac{3p+3}{4}\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)+\frac{13}{4}\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right).

Proof.

We consider cases:

(1)

Suppose
ab+r\equiv ac+r\equiv ab+r\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.
Then, since
a\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
,

ab+r\equiv ac+r\pmod{p}\implies ab\equiv ac\pmod{p}\implies b\equiv c\pmod{p}.

By symmetry, this yields,
a\equiv b\equiv c\pmod{p}
, yielding two points in
\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
if
\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)=1
, else no point. Thus, there are

1+\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)

points in
\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
with
ab+r\equiv ac+r\equiv ab+r\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.

(2)

Now without loss of generality suppose
ab+r\equiv bc+r\equiv 0\pmod{p}
but
ac+r\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. We consider values for
b
in a triple
(a,b,c)
and note that
(a,b,c)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
exactly if

ac+r=\frac{r^{2}}{b^{2}}+r\in\square({\symAMSb F}_{p})\backslash\{0\}.

Note that as
b
ranges over all nonzero (
ab+r\equiv 0\pmod{p}
implies
b\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
) elements of
{\symAMSb F}_{p}
,
r^{2}/b^{2}
ranges over all nonzero squares in
{\symAMSb F}_{p}
. Thus let
r/b=X
and consider the following quadratic function in
{\symAMSb F}_{p}[X]
:

f(X)=X^{2}+r.

We observe that for each
X\neq 0
with
\left(\frac{f(X)}{p}\right)=1
, we have a single choice of
b=r/X
that gives a point
(a,b,c)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}
. We proceed to count the number of such
X
as follows:

\displaystyle\#\left\{X\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}^{\times}:\left(\frac{f(X)}{p}\right)=1\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{X\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}^{\times}}\left[1+\left(\frac{f(X)}{p}\right)\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{X\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}}\left(1+\left(\frac{f(X)}{p}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}}\left(\frac{f(X)}{p}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(\ast)}{=}\frac{1}{2}\left(p-1-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)+\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)\right),

where to obtain (
\ast
), we apply Lemma
5.4
. Thus, we have
\frac{1}{2}\left(p-1-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)+\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)\right)
such points
(a,b,c)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
.

(3)

Finally, suppose that exactly one of
ab+r,bc+r,ac+r\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, WLOG
ab+r\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, so
a=-rb^{-1}
. Then, we wish to count the number of choices of
b,c\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}\backslash\{0\}
such that

bc+r,(ac+r)=r(1-cb^{-1})\in\square({\symAMSb F}_{p})\backslash\{0\}.

Since
r\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, it suffices for
bc+r\in\square({\symAMSb F}_{p})\backslash\{0\},\left(\frac{1-cb^{-1}}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)
.

Note that for any of the
(p-1)/2
nonzero values
t^{2}\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}
,
r(1-cb^{-1})=t^{2}
exactly if we take
c=b(1-r^{-1}t^{2})
. For any such fixed
t^{2}
,
(a,b,c)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
with
bc+r\neq 0
for all choices of
b
such that

b^{2}(1-r^{-1}t^{2})+r=u^{2}\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}^{\times}.

Since
c\neq 0
,
t^{2}\neq r
.
Therefore, we can find all such
b
by applying Lemma
5.4
to

f(X)=(1-r^{-1}t^{2})X^{2}+r,

finding that the number of such
b
is given by

\frac{p}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}}\left(\frac{f(X)}{p}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)=\frac{p}{2}-\left(\frac{1-r^{-1}t^{2}}{p}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right).

We can sum over the all the possibilities of
t^{2}\in\square({\symAMSb F}_{p}^{\times})
to count the number of
(a,b,c)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
with
ab+r\equiv 0\pmod{p}
and
(bc+r)(ac+r)abc\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
:

\displaystyle\#\{(a,b,c)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p}):ab+r\equiv 0\pmod{p},\,(bc+r)(ac+r)abc\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{(p-1)/2}\left[\frac{p}{2}-\left(\frac{1-r^{-1}t^{2}}{p}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{p(p-1)}{4}+\frac{p-1}{4}\left(\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)\right)-\sum_{t=1}^{(p-1)/2}\left(\frac{1-r^{-1}t^{2}}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{p(p-1)}{4}+\frac{p-1}{4}\left(\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}^{\times}}\left(\frac{1-r^{-1}t^{2}}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{p(p-1)}{4}+\frac{p-1}{4}\left(\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}}\left(\frac{1-r^{-1}t^{2}}{p}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{p(p-1)}{4}+\frac{p-1}{4}\left(\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}}\left(\frac{1-r^{-1}t^{2}}{p}\right)+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(\ast)}{=}\frac{p(p-1)}{4}+\frac{p-1}{4}\left(\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{-r^{-1}}{p}\right)+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{p(p-1)}{4}+\frac{p-1}{4}\left(\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)+\frac{1}{2},

where as above
(\ast)
follows by Lemma
5.4
applied to
g(t)=1-r^{-1}t^{2}
.

Using the symmetry between
a,b,c
, we can use the above three cases to obtain the desired result:

\displaystyle\#\left\{(a,b,c)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p}):(ab+r)(bc+r)(ac+r)\equiv 0\pmod{p},\,abc\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}\right\}

\displaystyle=1+\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)+\frac{3}{2}\left(p-1-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)+\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+3\left(\frac{p(p-1)}{4}+\frac{p-1}{4}\left(\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{3p^{2}+3p+4}{4}-\frac{3p+3}{4}\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)+\frac{13}{4}\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right).

∎

Lemma 5.8
.

For
r\in{\symAMSb Z}
with
p\nmid r
viewed as an element of
{\symAMSb F}_{p}
, we have that

\widetilde{\diop}_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})=\frac{1}{8}-\frac{1}{p}\cdot\frac{6+3\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)}{8}+\frac{1}{p^{2}}\cdot\frac{15+12\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)}{8}-\frac{1}{p^{3}}\cdot\frac{16+13\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)+2\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)}{8}.

Proof.

We first compute the count of triples in
(a,b,c)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
such that
abc(ab+r)(bc+r)(ac+r)\neq 0.

Let
\alpha=ab,\beta=bc
and let
\gamma=b^{-1}
, so that
\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}=ac
. This gives the following:

\displaystyle\#

\displaystyle\left\{(a,b,c)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p}):(ab+r)(bc+r)(ac+r)abc\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{c\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}^{\times}}\sum_{b\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}^{\times}}\sum_{a\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}^{\times}}\mathbb{1}_{\{(ab+r)(bc+r)(ac+r)\neq 0\}}\left(1+\left(\frac{ab+r}{p}\right)\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{bc+r}{p}\right)\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{ac+r}{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{\gamma\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}^{\times}}\sum_{\beta\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}^{\times}}\sum_{\alpha\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}^{\times}}\mathbb{1}_{\{(\alpha+r)(\beta+r)(\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}+r)\neq 0\}}\left(1+\left(\frac{\alpha+r}{p}\right)\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{\beta+r}{p}\right)\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}+r}{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{\gamma\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}^{\times}}\sum_{\beta\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}\setminus\{r\}}\sum_{\alpha\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}\setminus\{r\}}\mathbb{1}_{\{\alpha\beta((\alpha-r)(\beta-r)\gamma^{2}+r)\neq 0\}}\left(1+\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{\beta}{p}\right)\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{(\alpha-r)(\beta-r)\gamma^{2}+r}{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{\beta\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}\setminus\{r\}}\sum_{\alpha\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}\setminus\{r\}}\mathbb{1}_{\{\alpha\beta\neq 0\}}\left(1+\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{\beta}{p}\right)\right)\sum_{\gamma\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}^{\times}}\mathbb{1}_{\{(\alpha-r)(\beta-r)\gamma^{2}+r\neq 0\}}\left(1+\left(\frac{(\alpha-r)(\beta-r)\gamma^{2}+r}{p}\right)\right).

Recall that
p\nmid r
and first consider the two outer sums. As
\alpha,\beta
range over the summation, they range over all values in
{\symAMSb F}_{p}
except
r
, but contribute a nonzero value only when
\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{\beta}{p}\right)=1
.
We first consider the inner summation, recalling that
\alpha,\beta\neq r
:

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}^{\times}}\mathbb{1}_{\{(\alpha-r)(\beta-r)\gamma^{2}+r\neq 0\}}\left(1+\left(\frac{(\alpha-r)(\beta-r)\gamma^{2}+r}{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}}\left[1+\left(\frac{(\alpha-r)(\beta-r)\gamma^{2}+r}{p}\right)\right]-\left(1+\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)\right)-1

\displaystyle=\left(p-2-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)\right)+\sum_{\gamma\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}}\left(\frac{(\alpha-r)(\beta-r)\gamma^{2}+r}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(\ast)}{=}p-2-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)-\left(\frac{(\alpha-r)(\beta-r)}{p}\right)

where as earlier, we apply Lemma
5.4
in
(\ast)
.
This simplification gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta=1+r}^{p-1+r}\sum_{\alpha=1+r}^{p-1+r}\mathbb{1}_{\{\alpha\beta\neq 0\}}\left(1+\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{\beta}{p}\right)\right)\sum_{\gamma=1}^{p-1}\mathbb{1}_{\{(\alpha-r)(\beta-r)\gamma^{2}+r\neq 0\}}\left(1+\left(\frac{(\alpha-r)(\beta-r)\gamma^{2}+r}{p}\right)\right).

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{\beta=1+r}^{p-1+r}\sum_{\alpha=1+r}^{p-1+r}\mathbb{1}_{\{\alpha\beta\neq 0\}}\left(1+\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{\beta}{p}\right)\right)\left[p-2-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)-\left(\frac{(\alpha-r)(\beta-r)}{p}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{\beta\neq 0,r}\sum_{\alpha\neq 0,r}\left(1+\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{\beta}{p}\right)\right)\left(p-2-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{\beta\neq 0,r}\sum_{\alpha\neq 0,r}\left(1+\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{\beta}{p}\right)\right)\left(\frac{(\alpha-r)(\beta-r)}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\left(p-2-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)\right)\left(p-2-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{8}\left(\sum_{\alpha\neq 0,r}\left(1+\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\right)\left(\frac{\alpha-r}{p}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(\ast)}{=}\frac{1}{8}\left(p-2-\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)\right)^{3}-\frac{1}{8}\left(-1-\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\left[(p^{3}-6p^{2}+15p-16)+\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)(-3p^{2}+12p-13)-2\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)\right]

where we use Lemma
5.4
in
(\ast)
.
This implies that

\widetilde{\diop}_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})=\frac{1}{8}-\frac{1}{p}\cdot\frac{6+3\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)}{8}+\frac{1}{p^{2}}\cdot\frac{15+12\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)}{8}-\frac{1}{p^{3}}\cdot\frac{16+13\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)+2\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)}{8}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

We add the results of Lemmas
5.5
–
5.8
to give a unified expression for all
p\nmid r
. We thus obtain the desired Haar measure

\diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})=\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{p}\cdot\left(\frac{6+3\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)}{8}\right)+\frac{1}{p^{2}}\left(\frac{21+6\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)}{8}\right)+\frac{1}{p^{3}}\left(\frac{24\left(\frac{-r}{p}\right)-10-21\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)}{8}\right).

∎

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

The result follows by noting that

\widetilde{\diop}_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})=\widetilde{\diop}_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})

as if
abc(ab+r)(bc+r)(ac+r)\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, then
ab+r,bc+r,ac+r\in\square({\symAMSb F}_{p})
if and only if
ab+r,bc+r,ac+r\in\square({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
.
Further,

\mu\{(a,b,c)\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{3}\mid abc(ab+r)(bc+r)(ac+r)\equiv 0\pmod{p}\}\leq\frac{6}{p}.

Combining this bound with the result of Theorem
5.3
gives the asymptotic result:

\left|\diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})-\diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})\right|\leq\frac{6}{p}.

This implies that

\diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})=\frac{1}{8}+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right),

as desired.
∎

6.
\Diop_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
for
m\geq 4

In this section, we take
p
to be an odd prime. It appears to be difficult to compute
\dioph_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
or
\dioph_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
exactly, but it is possible to give asymptotics. In the case of
r=1
, this was done for
{\symAMSb F}_{p}
by Dujella and Kazalicki in
[
DK16
]
; they proved that

\dioph_{m}^{1}({\symAMSb F}_{p})=2^{-\binom{m}{2}}+O(p^{-1/2}).

Their technique is similar to ours in the previous section, involving character sums evaluated at the values of polynomials. However, the best analogue of Lemma
5.4
in this case is only an inequality. To do this in the case of
r=1
, Dujella and Kazalicki consider the sum

\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\left(\frac{a_{i}a_{j}+1}{p}\right)\right).

They note that the main contribution to this sum is

\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}}\frac{1}{2^{\binom{m}{2}}},

obtained by choosing the 1 from each factor of the form
1+\left(\frac{a_{i}a_{j}+1}{p}\right)
. The remaining terms can be bounded using the Weil bound on character sums (or, equivalently, the Hasse–Weil bound for the number of
{\symAMSb F}_{p}
-points on a genus-
g
curve): for fixed
m
, the remaining terms are
O(p^{-1/2})
by
[
LN97
, Theorem 5.41]
.

The arguments in
[
DK16
]
do not make any use of
r=1
; they only rely on
p\nmid r
. Thus we have the following:

Theorem 6.1
.

For all fixed
m\geq 3
and
r\in{\symAMSb Z}
, we have that

\dioph_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})=\frac{1}{2^{\binom{m}{2}}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right)

as
p\to\infty
.

If we instead work with
{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
instead of
{\symAMSb F}_{p}
, we have

\widetilde{\dioph}_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})=\widetilde{\dioph}_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})

as in the previous section, and that

|\dioph_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})-\dioph_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})|\leq\frac{m+\binom{m}{2}}{p}=O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right),

so that

\dioph_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})=\frac{1}{2^{\binom{m}{2}}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right)

as well.

We also note that there is a second technique for estimating
\dioph_{4}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
and
\dioph_{4}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
, which we now describe.

Proposition 6.2
.

For any fixed
r\in{\symAMSb Z}
viewed in
{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
, we have that as
p\rightarrow\infty

\diop_{4}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})=\frac{1}{64}+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right).

It is helpful to begin by showing the relevant result for tuples over
{\symAMSb F}_{p}
:

Proposition 6.3
.

For any fixed
r\in{\symAMSb Z}
viewed in
{\symAMSb F}_{p}
, we have that as
p\rightarrow\infty

\diop_{4}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})=\frac{1}{64}+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right).

Proof.

Let
(a,b,c,d)\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}^{4}
. Note that
(a,b,c,d)\in\Diop_{4}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
only if
(a,b,c)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
and if
ab+r,bc+r,ac+r\in\square({\symAMSb F}_{p})
.
Assume that
a,b,c
are all distinct and let

S_{0}=\{(a,b,c)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p}),\,a,b,c\text{ distinct}\}.

Fix
(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0})\in S_{0}
and consider the elliptic curve
E
defined as

(6.1)

E:Y^{2}=(a_{0}X+r)(b_{0}X+r)(c_{0}X+r).

The Hasse–Weil bound gives that

p+1-2\sqrt{p}\leq|E({\symAMSb F}_{p})|\leq p+1+2\sqrt{p}.

Excluding the point at infinity, we find that this gives between
\frac{p-2\sqrt{p}}{2}-2
and
\frac{p+2\sqrt{p}}{2}
choices of
d\neq 0\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}
, such that there is a point
(X,Y)\in E({\symAMSb F}_{p})
with
X=d
. Of course, not all of these choices
d\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}
make
a_{0}d+r,b_{0}d+r,c_{0}d+r\in\square({\symAMSb F}_{p})
; they merely guarantee that the product
(a_{0}d+r)(b_{0}d+r)(c_{0}d+r)\in\square({\symAMSb F}_{p})
.
For our fixed
(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0})\in S_{0}
, the values
d\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}
that make
(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0},d)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
correspond exactly to the choices of
X
for which there is a
Y\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}
with
(X,Y)\in 2E({\symAMSb F}_{p})
by
[
Kna92
, Theorem 4.2]
.

E({\symAMSb F}_{p})
has full 2-torsion as seen via the factored form of
E
in Equation (
6.1
). Thus
E({\symAMSb F}_{p})\cong{\symAMSb Z}/t_{1}{\symAMSb Z}\times{\symAMSb Z}/t_{2}{\symAMSb Z}
, where
t_{1},t_{2}\equiv 0\pmod{2}
. Thus,
\frac{1}{4}
of the points on
E({\symAMSb F}_{p})
are in
2E({\symAMSb F}_{p})
and
(X,Y)\in 2E({\symAMSb F}_{p})
if and only if
(X,-Y)\in 2E({\symAMSb F}_{p})
, which since
p>2
, gives the following bound (noting that the point at infinity is guaranteed to be a multiple of
2
):

(6.2)

\frac{1}{8}p-\frac{\sqrt{p}}{4}-1\leq\#\{d\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}:(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0},d)\in\Diop_{4}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})\}\leq\frac{1}{8}p+\frac{\sqrt{p}}{4}.

Notice that

\diop_{4}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})=\sum_{(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0})\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})}\frac{\#\{d\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}:(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0},d)\in\Diop_{4}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})\}}{p^{4}}.

Further, observe that

|\diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})-\mu(S_{0})|=O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right).

Consequently,

\mu\{(a,b,c,d)\in\Diop_{4}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p}):(a,b,c)\not\in S_{0}\}=O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right).

Using the bound of Equation (
6.2
) and recalling that via Theorem
5.3
,
\diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})=\frac{1}{8}+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)
in conjunction with the above, we find that

\displaystyle\diop_{4}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})

\displaystyle=\diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})\cdot\left(\frac{1}{8}+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{8}+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)\left(\frac{1}{8}+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{64}+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right).

∎

Proof of Proposition
6.2
.

Let
(a,b,c,d)\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}^{4}
. Note that
(a,b,c,d)\in\Diop_{4}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
if and only if both
(a,b,c)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
and
ab+r,bc+r,ac+r\in\square({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
. For every
(a,b,c)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
,
(a,b,c)\in\Diop_{3}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
where we view
a,b,c
under the natural map
{\symAMSb Z}_{p}\rightarrow{\symAMSb F}_{p}
. For
a_{1},a_{2}\in{\symAMSb Z}_{p}
unless
a_{1}a_{2}+r\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, then
a_{1}a_{2}+r\in\square({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
if and only if
a_{1}a_{2}+r\in\square({\symAMSb F}_{p})
. Thus there are at most
3
choices of
d\pmod{p}
, such that
(ad+r)(bd+r)(cd+r)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for
a,b,c,d\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}
. This computation implies that

|\diop_{4}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})-\diop_{4}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})|=O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right),

and consequently as desired,

\diop_{4}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})=\frac{1}{64}+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right).

∎

A similar technique can be used to show that
\dioph_{5}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
and
\dioph_{5}^{r}({\symAMSb Z}_{p})
are
\frac{1}{1024}+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right)
, since the curve
y^{2}=(ax+r)(bx+r)(cx+r)(dx+r)
is again an elliptic curve. There is an analogue of
[
Kna92
, Theorem 4.2]
for hyperelliptic curves (see
[
Zar16
]
). Let
C
be the hyperelliptic curve
y^{2}=(a_{1}x+r)\cdots(a_{m-1}x+r)
, where
m-1
is odd and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m-1}\in{\symAMSb F}_{p}
are distinct. Let
(x_{0},y_{0})\in C({\symAMSb F}_{p})
be a point. Then
a_{i}x_{0}+r
is a perfect square for all
i
with
1\leq i\leq m-1
if and only if the point
[(x_{0},y_{0})-\infty]\in 2\Jac(C)({\symAMSb F}_{p})
, where
\Jac(C)
is the Jacobian of
C
. So, in order to estimate
\dioph_{m}^{r}({\symAMSb F}_{p})
, it suffices to understand the proportion of points of the form
[(x_{0},y_{0})-\infty]
in
\Jac(C)({\symAMSb F}_{p})
that are multiples of 2 in
\Jac(C)
. While we know that the 2-torsion in
\Jac(C)({\symAMSb F}_{p})
is isomorphic to
({\symAMSb Z}/2{\symAMSb Z})^{2g}
, and so that the proportion of points in
\Jac(C)({\symAMSb F}_{p})
which are multiples of 2 is
\frac{1}{2^{2g}}
, what we need to know is that of the points of the more specific form
[(x_{0},y_{0})-\infty]\in\Jac(C)({\symAMSb F}_{p})
, the proportion which are multiples of 2 is
\frac{1}{2^{2g}}
, up to a small error term. This does not appear to be so easy, since we cannot guarantee that points of this specific form in the Jacobian are “representative” with respect to being multiples of 2.

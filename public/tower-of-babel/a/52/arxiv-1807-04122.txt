Introduction

In this paper we are interested in well-posedness of the nonlinear boundary problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta u=0&\text{in}\;\;\;\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}\\
\displaystyle{\partial_{\nu}u}=V(x)u+b|u|^{\rho-1}u+f\;&\text{on}\;\;\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+},\end{cases}

(1.1)

when the force
f
and potential
V
are taken in weak-Morrey spaces or Morrey spaces on boundary
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
of half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\,|\,t>0\}
, for all
n/(n-1)<\rho<\infty
with
n\geq 2
and
b\geq 0
. Here
{\partial_{\nu}u}=-\partial_{t}u
denotes the exterior normal derivative on boundary
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. The linear problem associated to (
1.1
) read as follows

\Delta u=0\;\;\text{in}\;\;\;\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\partial_{\nu}u=V(x)u+f\;\;\text{on}\;\;\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}.

(1.2)

This problem has been studied in many papers (see e.g.
[
5
,
18
]
) and can be rewritten as a single layer potential

u(x,t)=(\mathbf{S}\tilde{f})(x,t):=\frac{\Gamma((n+1)/2)}{\pi^{(n+1)/2}}\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\frac{1}{(|x-y|^{2}+t^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\tilde{f}(y)dy

(1.3)

for
\tilde{f}(y)=V(y)u+f(y)
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|y|^{2})^{-\frac{n}{2}}|\tilde{f}(y)|dy<\infty
. The last integral can be pointwise controled by fractional Hardy-Littlewood maximal function

(M_{\alpha}\tilde{f})(y_{0})\,=\sup_{r>0,\,y_{0}\in\,\mathbb{R}^{n}}r^{\alpha-n}\int_{B(y_{0},r)}|\tilde{f}(y)|dy,\quad 0<\alpha<n

since, in view of
(1+|y-y_{0}|)^{2}\leq(1+|y_{0}|)^{2}\,(1+|y|^{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\tilde{f}(y)|}{(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}}}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\tilde{f}(y)|}{1+|y-y_{0}|^{n}}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{B(y_{0},1)}\frac{|\tilde{f}(y)|}{1+|y-y_{0}|^{n}}dy+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\{y\;|\;2^{k-1}\leq|y-y_{0}|<2^{k}\}}\frac{|\tilde{f}(y)|}{1+|y-y_{0}|^{n}}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|B(y_{0},1)|}\int_{B(y_{0},1)}|\tilde{f}(y)|dy+2^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k\alpha}\,2^{k(\alpha-n)}\int_{B(y_{0},2^{k})}|\tilde{f}(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim(M_{0}\tilde{f})(y_{0})+(M_{\alpha}\tilde{f})(y_{0})\quad\text{ for }\quad\alpha>0.

The second motivation is that (

\displaystyle u(x,t)

Theorem 1.1

(Main theorem)
.

Let
1<p<\omega=n(\rho-1)/\rho
for
\frac{n}{n-1}<\rho<\infty
and
n\geq 2
. Let
b>0
and
V\in\mathcal{M}_{\ell\infty}^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for

.

(I)

(Existence and uniqueness)
There are
\varepsilon>0
and

such that if
\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}_{p\infty}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\varepsilon/C
, the problem (
1.1
) has a unique solution

\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\mu}_{r\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+})}\leq c\prime\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}_{p\infty}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\textnormal{tr}_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}u\|_{\mathcal{M}_{q\infty}^{\lambda}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+})}\leq c\prime\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}_{p\infty}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

with exactly constant
c\prime=C\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\omega}_{p\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}/\big(1-c\|V\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\ell\infty}^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big)>0
, for all
1<r<\mu<\lambda=n(\rho-1)
and
\rho<q<\lambda
such that
r/\mu\leq q/\lambda
and
\rho/(\rho-1)=n/\mu+1/r
.

(II)

(Stability of data)
The
solution

depends continuously of singular function

and potential
V\in\mathcal{M}_{\ell\infty}^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

2.1
Lorentz spaces

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a measure space endowed with Lebesgue measure
dx
, the Lorentz space
L^{pd}(\Omega)
is defined to be the set of
measurable functions

f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\|f\|_{L^{pd}(\Omega)}^{\ast}=\left(\frac{d}{p}\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}[t^{{1}/{p}}f^{\ast}(t)]^{d}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}}<\infty

(2.1)

with
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq d<\infty
. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
with
d=\infty
, the Lorentz space
L^{p\infty}(\Omega)
is defined by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p\infty}(\Omega)}^{\ast}=\sup_{0<t<|\Omega|}t^{1/p}f^{\ast}(t),

(2.2)

where
f^{\ast}(t)
denotes the decreasing rearrangement
\text{ }f^{\ast}(t)=\inf\{s>0:d_{f}(s)\leq t\}
for
d_{f}(s):=\mu(\{x\in\Omega:|f(x)|>s\})
.
The Lorentz space
L^{pd}(\Omega)
increase with index
d
in the sense of continuous inclusions

L^{p1}(\Omega)\subset L^{pd_{1}}(\Omega)\subset L^{p}(\Omega)\subset L^{pd_{2}}(\Omega)\subset L^{p\infty}(\Omega)

(2.3)

for all
1<d_{1}\leq p\leq d_{2}<\infty.

Consider the interpolation functor

(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta,d}
which can be constructed via the
K_{\theta,d}-
method (see e.g.
[
6
]
). Let

0<p_{1}<p<p_{2}\leq\infty
and
\theta\in(0,1)
be such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{1}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{2}}
, then we get the real interpolation property (see
[
20
]
or
[
6
, Theorems 5.3.1
and 5.3.2]
)

\left(L^{p_{1}d_{1}},L^{p_{2}d_{2}}\right)_{\theta,d}=L^{pd}

for all
1\leq d_{1},d,d_{2}\leq\infty.

Also, the multiplication operator
T_{f}(g)=fg\,
works well in Lorentz spaces.

Lemma 2.1

(Theorem 3.4 and 3.5 at
[
29
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}\leq\infty
,
1<r\leq\infty
and
1\leq z_{1},z_{2}\leq\infty
be such that

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\frac{1}{z_{1}}+\frac{1}{z_{2}}\geq\frac{1}{s},

where
s\geq 1
. If
f\in L^{p_{1}z_{1}}
and
g\in L^{p_{2}z_{2}}
, then

\|fg\|_{rs}\leq\frac{r}{r-1}\|f\|_{p_{1}z_{1}}\|g\|_{p_{2}z_{2}}.

(2.4)

If

and

\|fg\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|f\|_{p_{1}z_{1}}\|g\|_{p_{1}^{\prime}z_{2}}.

An extension of Minkowski inequality for integrals can be easily obtained from duality (see
[
19
]
) between

and
L^{p^{\prime}d^{\prime}}
, where

and

are exponential conjugate of

and

Lemma 2.2

(Minkowski inequality in

-spaces
[

]
)

Let

for each

\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(x,y)f(y)dy\right\|_{L^{pd}(\mathbb{R}^{n},dx)}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\|K(x,y)\right\|_{L^{pd}(\mathbb{R}^{n},dx)}|f(y)|dy,

(2.5)

for every

2.2
Morrey-Lorentz spaces

Let
B_{\ell}(x_{0})
be a ball in

The Morrey-Lorentz spaces is also denoted by

with

Lemma 2.3

(Hölder’s inequality

]
)

Let

(2.6)

for all

3.1

Consider the double layer potential

(respectively

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta z=0&\text{in}\;\;\;\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+},\\

As we known the Poisson kernel

is given by
\frac{\Gamma((n+1)/2)}{\pi^{(n+1)/2}}\frac{t}{(|y|^{2}+t^{2})^{(n+1)/2}}
for

. Our first result is this section is the following.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
1<p\leq\omega<\infty
and
1<r\leq\mu<\infty
. If
1<p<r<\infty
and
\omega/n<r\leq s

\displaystyle\frac{r}{\mu}\leq\frac{p}{\omega}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{n}{\omega}=\frac{n}{\mu}+\frac{1}{r}

C>0
(independent of
f
) such that

\|\mathbf{D}f\|_{\mathcal{M}_{rs}^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}_{pd}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(3.2)

for all
1\leq d<s\leq\infty
. In addition, if
\frac{n}{\omega}=\frac{n}{\mu}+1
then there exists a constant
C>0
(independent of
f
) such that

Proof.

and

\displaystyle|\mathbf{D}f(x_{0})|

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}}|\mathbf{D}f|dx

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{D}f\|_{L^{rs}(B_{\delta})}\leq C_{1}^{1-\theta}C_{2}^{\theta}\left\|\left\|\mathbf{D}f\right\|_{L^{rs}(I_{\delta})}\right\|_{L^{rs}(Q_{\delta})}.

According to Calderon-Zygmund theory, the singular integral operator

Consider the map

Based on the assumptions (

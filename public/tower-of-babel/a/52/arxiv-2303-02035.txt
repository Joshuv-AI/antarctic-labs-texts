Reentrant condensation transition in a model of driven scalar active matter with diffusivity edge
Jonas Berx, Aritra Bose, Ramin Golestanian, Benoît Mahault

Abstract
The effect of a diffusivity edge is studied in a system of scalar active matter confined by a periodic potential and driven by an externally applied force. We find that this system shows qualitatively distinct stationary regimes depending on the amplitude of the driving force with respect to the potential barrier. For small driving, the diffusivity edge induces a transition to a condensed phase analogous to the Bose–Einstein-like condensation reported for the nondriven case, which is characterized by a density-independent steady state current. Conversely, large external forces lead to a qualitatively different phase diagram since in this case condensation is only possible beyond a given density threshold, while the associated transition at higher densities is found to be reentrant.



Introduction

Systems for which detailed balance is broken at the microscopic scale are known as active matter
Gompper et al. 2020
.
This property endows them with the ability to exhibit collective behaviour impossible at equilibrium.
Notable examples are the possibility of active particles to phase separate without attractive interactions
Cates and Tailleur 2015
;
Cotton et al. 2022
;
Golestanian 2019
,
or to exhibit long-range orientational order in two dimensions
Toner and Tu 1995
;
Chaté 2020
;
Mahault and Chaté 2021
.

In recent years, intensive efforts have been made to study the rich phenomenology of active systems via minimal models that capture their key features.
Active particles are indeed routinely modelled as persistent walkers
Romanczuk et al. 2012
;
Howse et al. 2007
;
Bechinger et al. 2016
;
Zöttl and Stark 2016
, and in a lattice gas setting as agents with an internal polarity setting a preferred hopping direction
Thompson et al. 2011
;
Solon and Tailleur 2013
;
Soto and Golestanian 2014
;
Kourbane-Houssene et al. 2018
.
Without large-scale orientational order, the macroscopic dynamics of active systems is often captured by a nonlinear
drift-diffusion equation describing the evolution of the particle density field
Chavanis 2008
;
Cates and Tailleur 2015
;
Kourbane-Houssene et al. 2018
;
Chakraborty et al. 2020
.
Furthermore, because such equation describes a dynamics evolving far from equilibrium,
its effective diffusivity and mobility
a priori
do not satisfy a Fluctuation Dissipation Relation (FDR)
Kubo 1966
.

Due to the interplay between activity and particle interactions,
some mean field active dynamics may be formulated in terms of an effective diffusivity that vanishes or becomes negative beyond a certain density threshold
Cates and Tailleur 2015
;
Meng et al. 2021
.
The class corresponding to a diffusivity remaining identically zero at large enough densities, which hereafter we will refer to as
diffusivity edge
, was introduced phenomenologically in
Golestanian 2019
.
There, it was shown that the coupling of the diffusivity edge with harmonic confinement triggers the formation of a point-like condensate at the ground state of the potential,
while the corresponding transition exhibits remarkable similarities with Bose-Einstein Condensation (BEC)
Ziff et al. 1977
.
More recently, this BEC-like condensation transition was reported in numerical simulations of a model of magnetic microswimmers confined in a quasi-one-dimensional channel
Meng et al. 2021

The BEC-like transition observed in the diffusivity edge class is furthermore reminiscent of condensation phenomena arising in various mass transport models
Evans and Hanney 2005
;
Evans et al. 2006
;
Evans and Waclaw 2014
;
Majumdar et al. 1998
;
Majumdar et al. 2000
;
Chakraborti et al. 2021
.

In this work, we study the influence of an external driving on the behaviour of a scalar active matter system presenting a diffusivity edge.
Working in one dimension, we achieve a nonvanishing steady state current by confining the dynamics in a tilted periodic potential.
This setup plays a fundamental role in the modelling of a plethora of physical systems
Risken 1996
including molecular motors
Jülicher et al. 1997
, enzymatic cycles
Agudo-Canalejo et al. 2021
,
electrophoresis of polyelectrolytes
Ajdari and Prost 1991
;
Nixon and Slater 1996
, rotating dipoles in external fields
Reguera et al. 2000
, Josephson junctions
Barone and Paternò 1982

and active ratchets
Angelani et al. 2011
;
Reichhardt and Reichhardt 2017
.
Although for weak external driving forces such that the potential has local minima the phenomenology of condensation resembles that observed at zero drive
Mahault and Golestanian 2020
,
the strong force regime where the potential is monotonous leads to striking differences.
For strong forces our study indeed reveals the existence of a particle density threshold below which condensation cannot arise.
The condensation transition at large enough densities, moreover, leads to the formation of a condensate occupying a finite volume,
such that upon cooling the system may undergo a subsequent evaporation transition, leading to reentrance.
All the theoretical results presented below are systematically compared to direct numerical simulations of the continuous theory governing the dynamics (details about numerical methods are given in
SM
).

Scalar active matter with diffusivity edge

We consider the following one-dimensional phenomenological evolution equation for a scalar density field
\rho(x,t)
:

\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}{J}=0\,,\qquad{J}=-M(\rho)\rho\partial_{x}U-D(\rho)\partial_{x}\rho\,,

(1)

where
U({x})
is an externally applied potential, while
M(\rho)
, and
D(\rho)
respectively denote the
\rho
-dependent effective mobility and diffusivity.
When the microscopic dynamics satisfies detailed balance, the ratio between
M(\rho)
, and
D(\rho)
obeys a FDR and is thus equal to the temperature of the system.
With broken detailed balance, on the contrary,
D(\rho)/M(\rho)
is not constrained by any FDR.
A number of non-interacting active systems, however,
satisfy a generalised FDR accounting for the renormalization of their effective diffusivity by activity
Romanczuk et al. 2012
;
Cates and Tailleur 2015
;
Meng et al. 2021
;
Loi et al. 2008
.
Here, we therefore assume such a relation in the dilute limit, and define an effective temperature as

k_{\rm B}T_{\rm eff}\equiv\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{D(\rho)}{M(\rho)},

(2)

which we will moreover use as a control parameter for the dynamics.
For finite densities, due to the nonequilibrium character of active dynamics the ratio
D(\rho)/M(\rho)
has
a priori
no reason to remain equal to
k_{\rm B}T_{\rm eff}
.
In particular, here we impose a diffusivity edge beyond a threshold
\rho_{\rm c}
such that

D(\rho)/M(\rho)=0
for
\rho\geq\rho_{\rm c}
.

When
U(x)
is harmonic, such diffusivity edge induces the formation of a
singular condensate at the ground state for
T_{\rm eff}
below a transition temperature
T_{\rm c}

Golestanian 2019
.
Remarkably, although the dynamics described by eq. (
1
) is fully classical the corresponding condensation transition carries signatures of BEC.
The BEC-like condensation moreover holds for periodic potentials presenting degenerate minima
Mahault and Golestanian 2020
.
To investigate how the BEC-like condensation transition is modified by the presence of an external drive, we consider here the external potential

U(x)=V(x)-Fx
,
whose periodic part
V(x)
has a peak value
V_{\rm b}
at
x=0
and satisfies
V(x+L)=V(x)
with
L
denoting the period,
while
F
is a stationary uniform driving force.

The steady state solution

We now calculate the steady state solution of eq. (
1
), which satisfies
\partial_{x}J=0
.
For
F\neq 0
, it is thus associated with a non-vanishing uniform current
\bar{J}
.
We moreover assume a constant mobility
M(\rho)=M_{\rm s}
, and a step diffusivity profile such that

D(\rho)=M_{\rm s}k_{\rm B}T_{\rm eff}\,\Theta(\rho_{\rm c}-\rho)\,,

(3)

where
\Theta
is the Heaviside step function.
This simple way to implement the diffusivity edge, while it allows for analytical progress,
does not qualitatively modify the properties of the condensation transition at
F=0
as compared to more realistic mobility and diffusivity profiles
Golestanian 2019
;
Mahault and Golestanian 2020
.
As we discuss in the final section,
for
F\neq 0
the properties of the system are also largely insensitive to the specific shape of
D(\rho)
.
In what follows, we rescale space and time such that
L
and
M_{\rm s}V_{\rm b}
are set to unity.

Assuming that the dynamics evolves on a ring, due to the translational invariance of the problem
we express stationary density profiles over a single period of
V(x)
.
With the above choice of parameters, we derive the steady state solution of eq. (
1
) via usual techniques
Risken 1996
(see also the Supplementary Material
SM
for calculation details).
Defining
\beta\equiv(k_{\rm B}T_{\rm eff})^{-1}
as the inverse effective temperature and assuming the density to be lower than
\rho_{\rm c}
everywhere, we obtain

\rho(x)=\beta V_{\rm b}\bar{J}e^{-\beta U(x)}\left(\frac{I_{+}(1)}{1-e^{-\beta F}}-I_{+}(x)\right)\,,

(4)

with
I_{+}(x)\equiv\int_{0}^{x}\exp\left[\beta U(x^{\prime})\right]\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}
.
The expression of the steady state current is then obtained from the normalization condition
\bar{\rho}=\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\,\rho(x)
, with
\bar{\rho}
denoting the mean density in the system
SM
.

Although the integrals in the expression of the steady state profile (
4
) can be numerically determined for an arbitrary periodic potential
V(x)
,
it is instructive to consider the case of a sawtooth potential with anisotropy parameter
\alpha\in(0;1)
, such that

V(x)=V_{\rm b}\left(1-\frac{x}{\alpha}\right)\times\begin{cases}1&0\leq x\leq\alpha\\
\alpha/(\alpha-1)&\alpha\leq x\leq 1\end{cases},

(5)

and which is to be extended periodically with unit period. Note that with this choice the potential
U(x)
is symmetric under the combined transformations
F\rightarrow-F
and
\alpha\rightarrow 1-\alpha
,
such that we only consider positive values of
F
without loss of generality.
The piecewise linearity of (
5
) conveniently leads to an exact expression for the density profile (
4
).
Denoting
\rho_{0}
as the maximum value of the density, we find that for
\rho_{0}<\rho_{\rm c}
the expression for the full profile can be recast as
\rho(x)=\rho_{0}R(x)

with

R(x)\!=\!\frac{1}{\gamma}\!\begin{cases}\left(1-e^{\beta V_{\rm b}f}\right)z^{\tfrac{x}{\alpha}}\!-\!\alpha f\left(ze^{\beta V_{\rm b}f}-1\right)\!\!&0\leq x\leq\alpha\\
\left(z-1\right)e^{\beta V_{\rm b}f\tfrac{x-\alpha}{1-\alpha}}&\\
\qquad\qquad-(1+\alpha f)\left(ze^{\beta V_{\rm b}f}-1\right)&\alpha\leq x\leq 1\end{cases},

(6)

where we have defined the dimensionless force
f\equiv F/F_{\rm c}-1
with
F_{\rm c}\equiv V_{\rm b}/(1-\alpha)
, and

z\equiv e^{\tfrac{\beta V_{\rm b}(1+\alpha f)}{1-\alpha}},\quad\gamma\equiv z\left(1-e^{\beta V_{\rm b}f}\right)-\alpha f\left(ze^{\beta V_{\rm b}f}-1\right).

\rho_{0}
can moreover be expressed in terms of the steady state current
\bar{J}
, which yields

\rho_{0}=\frac{\bar{J}(1-\alpha)}{f(1+\alpha f)}\left(\alpha f+\frac{z(1-e^{\beta V_{\rm b}f})}{1-ze^{\beta V_{\rm b}f}}\right)\,.

(7)

Looking back at the expression of the potential (
5
), it is clear that
F_{\rm c}
corresponds to the value of the external forcing such that the full potential
U
becomes monotonous.
In the following, we thus distinguish between the dynamical regimes associated with
f<0
(
F<F_{\rm c}
) and
f>0
(
F>F_{\rm c}
), which we will respectively refer to as subcritical and supercritical.
As shown in fig.
1
, the solution (
6
) in the subcritical regime consists of two convex branches decaying from
\rho_{0}
at
x=\alpha
.
For
f=0
, on the contrary, due to the vanishing potential slope in the range
\alpha\leq x\leq 1
the corresponding branch decays linearly from
\rho_{0}
,
while in the supercritical regime it takes a concave shape.
The maximum value of the density,
\rho_{0}
, typically increases with
\beta V_{\rm b}
and
\bar{\rho}
, while it decreases with increasing
f
.
In the limit
\beta V_{\rm b}\gg 1
of small effective temperatures the expression of
\rho_{0}
moreover simplifies as

\rho_{0}\underset{\beta V_{\rm b}\gg 1}{\sim}\frac{\bar{\rho}}{1-\alpha}\begin{cases}-\beta V_{\rm b}f(1+\alpha f)&(f<0)\\
2&(f=0)\\
\tfrac{(1-\alpha)(1+\alpha f)}{1+\alpha(f-1)}&(f>0)\end{cases},

(8)

while for large effective temperatures fluctuations dominate over the confinement, such that the density profile is nearly uniform and
\rho_{0}\sim\bar{\rho}

for
\beta V_{\rm b}\ll 1
and for all
f
.

The solution (
6
), however, holds only so long as
\rho_{0}
remains below the diffusivity edge
\rho_{\rm c}
.
When the condition
\rho_{0}=\rho_{\rm c}
is satisfied, the stationary density profile indeed becomes singular at
x=\alpha
which reflects the
fact that the system undergoes a condensation transition
Golestanian 2019
.
We now analyse the properties of this transition and the nature of the condensate and discuss how they are affected by the presence of a nonzero driving force.
From now on we fix
\alpha=\tfrac{7}{10}
for all numerical evaluations.
In all figures theoretical results are displayed with solid lines while numerical data from simulations of eq. (
1
) are shown with symbols.

The subcritical regime

For
f<0
the potential
U(x)
exhibits a local minimum at
x=\alpha
. This case is therefore qualitatively similar to the nondriven case addressed in ref.
Mahault and Golestanian 2020
.
Setting
\rho_{0}=\rho_{\rm c}
in eq. (
7
) gives the formal condition on the parameters
\beta V_{\rm b}
,
f
and
\bar{\rho}
for the condensation to occur.
The resulting transition lines at fixed
f
and
\bar{\rho}
are shown respectively in fig.
2
(a,b) (see the blue and yellow curves).
They predict the existence of a transition for all
\bar{\rho}<\rho_{\rm c}
, while the transition temperature in the limit of vanishing densities reads

\frac{k_{\rm B}T_{\rm c}}{V_{\rm b}}\underset{\bar{\rho}\ll\rho_{\rm c}}{\sim}-\frac{\bar{\rho}}{\rho_{\rm c}}\frac{f(1+\alpha f)}{1-\alpha}.

(9)

For
T_{\rm eff}<T_{\rm c}
, the density profile in the condensed phase can moreover formally be written as

\rho(x)=\bar{\rho}\phi_{\rm c}\delta(x-\alpha)+\rho_{\rm c}R(x)\qquad(T_{\rm eff}<T_{\rm c}),

(10)

with the function
R(x)
defined in eq. (
6
).
This solution thus consists of a smooth part with maximum density given by
\rho_{\rm c}
and a singular condensate located at the local potential minimum
x=\alpha
.
The condensate fraction,
\phi_{\rm c}
, is then determined from the density normalization.
The expression of
\phi_{\rm c}
in the general case is rather cumbersome and does not yield much physical insight, but taking the limits of small and large effective temperatures it simplifies as

\!\!\phi_{\rm c}\underset{\beta V_{\rm b}\gg 1}{\sim}1-\frac{T_{\rm eff}}{T_{\rm c}},\quad\phi_{\rm c}\underset{\beta V_{\rm b}\ll 1}{\sim}\left(1-\frac{\rho_{\rm c}}{\bar{\rho}}\right)\left(1-\frac{T_{\rm c}}{T_{\rm eff}}\right).

(11)

These expressions correspond to those derived in
Mahault and Golestanian 2020
, and match well with the full solution in the low temperature regime (fig.
3
(a)).
The presence of a weak driving force thus does not qualitatively
modify the scaling of the condensate fraction with effective temperature but only affects the value of the transition temperature
T_{\rm c}
.

Since the condensate is located at a local minimum of the potential, it does not contribute to the global current
\bar{J}
.
Therefore, the latter is obtained from eq. (
7
) by replacing
\rho_{0}
with
\rho_{\rm c}
, namely

\bar{J}=\frac{\rho_{\rm c}f(1+\alpha f)}{(1-\alpha)}\left(\alpha f+\frac{z(1-e^{\beta V_{\rm b}f})}{1-ze^{\beta V_{\rm b}f}}\right)^{-1}\,(T_{\rm eff}<T_{\rm c}).

(12)

Remarkably,
\bar{J}
does not depend on the mean particle density
\bar{\rho}
,
which reflects the fact that increasing
\bar{\rho}
does not affect the smooth part of the distribution (
10
) (cfr. inset of fig.
3
(b)), but only leads to an increase of the condensate fraction
\phi_{\rm c}
.
This feature is analogous to the divergence of the isothermal compressibility at
T_{\rm eff}=T_{\rm c}
found in the nondriven case
Golestanian 2019
;
Mahault and Golestanian 2020
,
which implies that the confining pressure exerted by the potential is independent of
\bar{\rho}
.

The BEC-like condensation transition at
F=0
is moreover associated with a singular behaviour of several observables such as
the mean internal energy
\langle U\rangle\equiv\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\,\rho(x)U(x)
or the heat capacity
C\equiv{\rm d}\langle U\rangle/{\rm d}T_{\rm eff}

Golestanian 2019
.
For
F>0
, the explicit expressions of these thermodynamic functions, although they can straightforwardly be obtained from eq. (
6
), are generally quite lengthy and we do not report them here. Some asymptotic results are given in the Supplementary Material
SM
.
Figure
3
(c,d) shows that their behavior at the transition is qualitatively similar to the nondriven case, with
\langle U\rangle
exhibiting a discontinuous slope at
T_{\rm eff}=T_{\rm c}
, resulting in a discontinuous jump of
C
.
In addition, in the limit of strong confinement, the mean particle current can be expressed as

\bar{J}\!\underset{\beta V_{\rm b}\gg 1}{\sim}\!-\frac{\rho_{\rm c}f(1+\alpha f)}{1-\alpha}e^{-\tfrac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha f}\tfrac{\rho_{\rm c}}{\bar{\rho}}\tfrac{T_{\rm c}}{T_{\rm eff}}}\begin{cases}\tfrac{T_{\rm c}}{T_{\rm eff}}&(T_{\rm eff}\geq T_{\rm c})\\
1&(T_{\rm eff}<T_{\rm c})\end{cases},

(13)

which highlights that the condensation transition is associated with a cusp in the decay of
\bar{J}
with the effective temperature (fig.
3
(b)).
Finally, eq. (
13
) also indicates that the presence of a condensate leads to a faster decay of the current with the effective temperature.

The supercritical regime

We now address the case
f\geq 0
, which marks a qualitative change in the phase diagram of the system (see the green and red curves in fig.
2
).
Indeed, it appears from eq. (
8
) that for
f\geq 0
the condensed phase can be reached at zero effective temperature only for
\bar{\rho}\geq\lambda\rho_{\rm c}

with

\lambda\equiv\begin{cases}(1-\alpha)/2&(f=0)\\
(1-\alpha+\alpha f)/(1+\alpha f)&(f>0)\end{cases}.

(14)

Taking
f=0
, numerical simulations of eq. (
1
) reveal that the condensate remains point-like (fig.
4
(a)).
Therefore, density profiles in the condensed phase are described by eq. (
10
).
This case is thus analogous to the subcritical regime, with however some qualitative differences in the low effective temperature limit.
Taking
f=0
and
\beta V_{\rm b}\gg 1
in eq. (
12
), we indeed find that the current vanishes linearly with the effective temperature:

\bar{J}\underset{T_{\rm eff}\to 0}{\sim}\frac{\rho_{\rm c}}{1-\alpha}\frac{k_{\rm B}T_{\rm eff}}{V_{\rm b}}\qquad(T_{\rm eff}<T_{\rm c},\,f=0),

in contrast with the faster exponential decay found in the subcritical regime (see eq. (
13
)).
Correspondingly, we find that in the limit of zero effective temperature the condensate does not contain all the mass of the system but carries a finite fraction equal to

\phi_{\rm c}\underset{T_{\rm eff}\to 0}{\sim}1-\lambda\rho_{\rm c}/\bar{\rho}
.
This feature can be explained by the fact that, since for
f=0
the potential is no more confining,
it allows for spatial coexistence between a point-wise condensate at
x=\alpha
and a residual gas phase even for
T_{\rm eff}=0
.
Figure
4
(a) shows that this gas phase is indeed located in the region where the net force balances to zero,
and hence can persist even for
T_{\rm eff}=0
without inducing a global current.

Considering now the supercritical regime at
f>0
, we find from numerical simulations of eq. (
1
) that the condensate is only point-like at
T_{\rm eff}=T_{\rm c}
,
while for
T_{\rm eff}<T_{\rm c}
it consists of a uniform domain of finite density extending from
x=\alpha
with a width
w
(fig.
4
(b)).
Therefore,
f=0
marks a depinning transition from a point-wise condensate to an extended condensate occupying a finite volume.
Consequently, for
f>0
cooling down the system from
T_{\rm c}
may result in the condensate density becoming lower than
\rho_{\rm c}
at
T_{\rm eff}\equiv T_{\rm e}<T_{\rm c}
,
leading to a low-temperature evaporation transition.
We find that such a scenario typically occurs for
\bar{\rho}\lesssim\lambda\rho_{\rm c}
, and therefore that in the supercritical regime the transition to condensation is reentrant,
as shown in the phase diagrams of fig.
2
.
In
SM
we moreover provide a theoretical argument for the supercritical reentrant transition in the low effective temperature limit.
Reentrant condensation transitions have been observed in models of two-species zero-range processes
Daga 2017
, while in the context of active matter reentrance was also predicted for motility induced phase separation
Paoluzzi et al. 2020
.

To study the condensed phase at
f>0
, we describe the density profile as a piecewise function defined on the three intervals
[0;\alpha]
,
[\alpha;x^{*}]
and
[x^{*};1]
with
x^{*}\equiv\alpha+w
.
Solving eq. (
1
) for a steady state with constant current
\bar{J}
for
x\leq\alpha
and
x^{*}\leq x
, while assuming a uniform condensate of density
\bar{\rho}\phi_{\rm c}/w>\rho_{\rm c}
in between, we obtain

\rho(x)\underset{f>0}{=}\begin{cases}\frac{\bar{J}\alpha(1-\alpha)}{1+\alpha f}\left(1-z^{\tfrac{x}{\alpha}-1}\right)\!+\!\rho_{\rm c}z^{\tfrac{x}{\alpha}-1}&0\leq x\leq\alpha\\
\bar{\rho}\phi_{\rm c}/w&\alpha<x<x^{*}\\
\frac{\bar{J}(1-\alpha)}{f}\left(1-e^{\beta V_{\rm b}f\tfrac{x-x^{*}}{1-\alpha}}\right)&\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad+\rho_{\rm c}e^{\beta V_{\rm b}f\tfrac{x-x^{*}}{1-\alpha}}&x^{*}\leq x\leq 1\end{cases},

(15)

where we have used the fact that
\rho(\alpha)=\rho(x^{*})=\rho_{\rm c}
.
The periodicity condition
\rho(0)=\rho(1)
then allows to express the condensate width
w
as function of the current
\bar{J}
.
Finally, a closed expression for the current is obtained from
\bar{J}=-V_{\rm b}^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\,U^{\prime}(x)\rho(x)
which,
after eliminating
\phi_{\rm c}
,
leads to (details in
SM
)

\displaystyle\bar{J}\underset{f>0}{=}

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{\rm c}(1+\alpha f)}{\kappa}\left[1-\frac{1}{z}+\frac{\beta V_{\rm b}f(1+\alpha f)}{1-\alpha}\frac{\bar{\rho}}{\rho_{\rm c}}\right]\;(T_{\rm eff}<T_{\rm c}),

(16)

where
\kappa\equiv\alpha(1-\alpha)(1-z^{-1})+\lambda\beta V_{\rm b}(1+\alpha f)^{2}
.

Since for
f>0
the condensate occupies a finite volume, it contributes to the global current that now explicitly depends on the mean particle density,
in contrast with eq. (
12
) derived in the subcritical regime.
Equation (
16
) can then be used to calculate the condensate width
w
as well as the condensate fraction
\phi_{\rm c}

SM
.
Close to the transition, we find that both expressions simplify as

w\underset{T_{\rm eff}\lesssim T_{\rm c}}{\propto}\frac{T_{\rm c}}{T_{\rm eff}}-1,\qquad\phi_{\rm c}\underset{T_{\rm eff}\lesssim T_{\rm c}}{\propto}1-\frac{T_{\rm eff}}{T_{\rm c}}\qquad(f>0),

such that near the threshold the condensate density decays linearly with the effective temperature:
\bar{\rho}\phi_{\rm c}/w\propto T_{\rm eff}/T_{\rm c}
.

) (fig.
4
(b)).
Furthermore, the thermodynamics of condensation carries the BEC signatures similarly to the subcritical regime (fig.
5
).
The current and mean energy indeed exhibit a discontinuous slope at
T_{\rm eff}=T_{\rm c}
associated with a discontinuous jump of the heat capacity.
Remarkably, in the reentrant regime these features also hold for the evaporation transition at
T_{\rm eff}=T_{\rm e}
, despite the condensate not being point-like in this case.

In the limit of small effective temperatures, we find that the condensate width approaches
1-\alpha
from below as

1-\alpha-w\underset{T_{\rm eff}\to 0}{\propto}\frac{T_{\rm eff}}{T_{\rm c}}\qquad(f>0),

while the current and condensate fraction converge to

\bar{J}\underset{T_{\rm eff}\to 0}{\sim}\frac{\bar{\rho}f}{(1-\alpha)\lambda},\quad\phi_{\rm c}\underset{T_{\rm eff}\to 0}{\sim}1-\frac{\alpha^{2}f}{(1+\alpha f)\lambda},\quad(f>0).

As a consequence of the depinning transition, in the supercritical regime the current saturates to finite values for
T_{\rm eff}=0
.
Similarly to the
f=0
case, the condensate fraction moreover saturates to a finite value, which highlights a gas-condensate coexistence.
Indeed, the density distribution at
T_{\rm eff}=0
and
f>0
simply consists of two disconnected plateaus with respective values

\rho(x)\underset{T_{\rm eff}\to 0}{\sim}\frac{\bar{\rho}}{\lambda}\begin{cases}\alpha f/(1+\alpha f)&0\leq x\leq\alpha\\
1&\alpha<x\leq 1\end{cases}\qquad(f>0).

Generalization to the sinusoidal potential

All the results obtained so far correspond to the step diffusivity profile (
3
) and the sawtooth periodic potential (
5
), which allow for exact derivations.
To assess their generality, we now consider a different setting for which the diffusivity edge is reached as (keeping the previous notations)

D(\rho)=M_{\rm s}k_{\rm B}T_{\rm eff}(1-\rho/\rho_{\rm c})^{2}\,\Theta(\rho_{\rm c}-\rho)\,,

(17)

while the periodic potential is sinusoidal:
V(x)=\tfrac{V_{\rm b}}{2}\left[1-\cos(\pi x)\right]
.
As expected, this choice leads to density profiles smoother than obtained previously with the sawtooth potential (fig.
6
(a)).
Looking at the condition for the full potential to be monotonous, the threshold associated with the depinning transition is in this case
F_{\rm c}=\pi V_{\rm b}/2
.

Condensation transition in a sinusoidal potential with
V_{\rm b}=1
. (a) Density profiles for
T_{\rm eff}>T_{\rm c}
and
T_{\rm eff}<T_{\rm c}
with
f=-0.05
. The potential is also shown with its minimum (black dot).
(b) Scaling of
\bar{J}
with the mean density while crossing the transition at
\bar{\rho}/\rho_{\rm c}\approx 0.26
at fixed
T_{\rm eff}=0.5
.
(c) Phase diagram showing
T_{\rm c}
as a function of
\bar{\rho}
.
(d) Current and condensate fraction as a function of
T_{\rm eff}
at
\bar{\rho}/\rho_{\rm c}\approx 0.096
and
f\approx 0.02
.
Here lines are drawn as a guide for the eye.

In the subcritical regime for which
f=F/F_{\rm c}-1\leq 0
, numerical simulations of eq. (
1
)
reveal that the phenomenology of the transition is similar to that described previously.
Given a mean particle density
\bar{\rho}<\rho_{\rm c}
and a driving force
f<0
,
there always exists a value
T_{\rm c}>0
of
T_{\rm eff}
such that the maximum density reaches
\rho_{\rm c}
and the system condenses.
In this case, however, the maximum of the density profile (
4
) is distinct from the local minimum of the total potential
U(x)
(fig.
6
(a)).
Therefore, the condensate is generally not point-like but has a finite width.
Consequently, the condensate contributes to the current
\bar{J}
which increases sub-linearly with the mean particle density for
T_{\rm eff}<T_{\rm c}
(fig.
6
(b)).
The condensate width, however, is in general fairly small and converges to zero as
T_{\rm eff}\to 0
,
such that its effect on
\bar{J}
is hardly appreciable in numerical simulations.

Taking now
f>0
, similarly to what was described previously for the sawtooth potential there exists a finite density threshold below which no condensation occurs (fig.
6
(c)).
Noting that the supercritical regime is defined as when the potential becomes strictly monotonous,
the zero-temperature density profile indeed obeys in steady state (assuming that
U
is smooth)

\bar{J}=-\rho(x)U^{\prime}(x)
such that, after using the normalization condition

\rho(x)\underset{f>0}{=}\bar{\rho}\,\left[U^{\prime}(x)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{{\rm d}y}{U^{\prime}(y)}\right]^{-1}\qquad(T_{\rm eff}=0).

(18)

Hence, we deduce that in the limit of vanishing effective temperatures condensation only happens for
\bar{\rho}\geq\lambda\rho_{\rm c}
with

\lambda=\min_{x}[|U^{\prime}(x)|]\int_{0}^{1}\frac{{\rm d}y}{|U^{\prime}(y)|}.

(19)

For the sinusoidal potential, we find
\lambda=\sqrt{f/(2+f)}
which is in agreement with our numerical simulations (fig.
6
(c)).
More remarkably, our numerical results point towards a reentrant transition with the system undergoing an evaporation transition at
T_{\rm eff}=T_{\rm e}<T_{\rm c}
, highlighting the generality of this feature
(figs.
6
(c,d)).

Conclusion

We have studied the effect of nonzero steady state currents on the BEC-like condensation induced by a diffusivity edge.
In contrast with the nondriven case, the condensate at nonzero drive might occupy a finite volume
similar to what was observed in mass transport models with finite-range interactions
Evans et al. 2006
;
Evans and Waclaw 2014
.
Moreover, the stationary current is found to be essentially independent of the mean particle density in the condensed phase of subcritical regime,
a feature that has also been described for the externally driven zero-range process
Schütz and Harris 2007
.
The most prominent differences with respect to previous studies of the diffusivity edge class were observed in the supercritical regime.
There, we found that condensation can only occur beyond a minimum particle density,
while the corresponding transition is reentrant at moderate densities due to the presence of an evaporation transition at low effective temperatures.

The results presented here highlight the similarities between the diffusivity edge class and a variety of mass transport models.
Characterising further these similarities at the dynamical level,
e.g.
to study the coarsening of the condensate or
to investigate the presence of kinematic waves such as those observed in the zero-range process
Schütz and Harris 2007
, requires to go beyond the mean field level considered here.
Introducing noise in the dynamics would moreover allow to study the interplay between other features of externally driven Brownian motion,
such the enhanced effective diffusion
Reimann et al. 2001
;
Reimann et al. 2002
;
Reimann 2002
, with the BEC-like condensation.

\begin{split}\rho(x+k)&=e^{-\beta U(x)}\left[e^{\beta kF}\left(\rho^{*}e^{\beta V_{\rm b}}-\beta V_{\rm b}\bar{J}\frac{I_{+}(1)}{1-e^{-\beta F}}\right)-\beta V_{\rm b}\bar{J}\left(I_{+}(x)-\frac{I_{+}(1)}{1-e^{-\beta F}}\right)\right]\,.\\

(S6)

Condensation with a piecewise linear periodic potential

Here we provide additional calculation details about the condensation transition in presence of a sawtooth periodic potential

V(x)=V_{\rm b}\left(1-\frac{x}{\alpha}\right)\times\begin{cases}1&0\leq x\leq\alpha\\
\tfrac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}&\alpha\leq x\leq 1\end{cases},

(S7)

where
0<\alpha<1
denotes the anisotropy parameter.
As is done in the main text, we define
F_{\rm c}\equiv V_{\rm b}/(1-\alpha)
as the value of
F

U
is monotonous,
while
f\equiv F/F_{\rm c}-1

II.1
Transition to condensation and reentrance

For effective temperatures
T_{\rm eff}
above the transition threshold
T_{\rm c}
, we find after some calculation that the maximum density
\rho_{0}
is given by

\rho_{0}=\bar{\rho}\frac{z(e^{\beta V_{\rm b}f}-1)+\alpha f(ze^{\beta V_{\rm b}f}-1)}{(ze^{\beta V_{\rm b}f}-1)(1+\alpha(f-1))-\tfrac{(1-\alpha)(z-1)(e^{\beta V_{\rm b}f}-1)}{\beta V_{\rm b}f(1+\alpha f)}},

(S8)

where
z\equiv\exp[\beta V_{\rm b}(1+\alpha f)/(1-\alpha)]
.
As explained in the main text, the transition temperature
T_{\rm c}
is defined from the condition
\rho_{0}=\rho_{\rm c}
, with
\rho_{\rm c}
marking the diffusivity edge.
An analytical expression for
T_{\rm c}(\bar{\rho}/\rho_{\rm c},f)
cannot be obtained in general.
However, considering the limit
|\beta V_{\rm b}f|\gg 1
of small effective temperatures we find that the transition threshold condition can be approximated as

\frac{\bar{\rho}}{\rho_{\rm c}}=\begin{cases}-\tfrac{1-\alpha}{\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}f(1+\alpha f)}-(1-\alpha+\alpha f)e^{\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}f}+{\cal O}\left((\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}f)^{-1}e^{\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}f}\right)&f<0\\
\lambda-\tfrac{1-\alpha}{\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}f(1+\alpha f)^{2}}+\tfrac{\lambda}{(1+\alpha f)}e^{-\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}f}+{\cal O}\left((\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}f)^{-1}e^{-\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}f}\right)&f>0\end{cases},

(S9)

where
\lambda\equiv(1-\alpha+\alpha f)/(1+\alpha f)
and we have used the notation
\beta_{\rm c}\equiv(k_{\rm B}T_{\rm c})^{-1}
.
Keeping only the leading order contributions of the above equations, we recover the expressions given in Eq. (8) of the main text.
On the other hand, a sufficient condition for the existence of a reentrant transition is that there exists a finite value
\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}
such that
{\rm d}(\bar{\rho}/\rho_{\rm c})/{\rm d}\beta V_{\rm b}=0
.
After some algebra, we find that the corresponding value of the effective temperature solves

-\frac{\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}|f|}{2}e^{-\tfrac{\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}|f|}{2}}=-\frac{\sqrt{K}}{2},\quad{\rm with}\quad K\equiv\frac{1-\alpha}{1-\alpha+\alpha f}\begin{cases}(1+\alpha f)^{-1}&f<0\\
1&f>0\end{cases}.

(S10)

Moreover, Eq. (
S10
) admits real solutions if and only if
K\leq 4/e^{2}\approx 0.54
.
It is easily verified that
K(f<0)>1
, such that we do not expect any reentrance in that case.
On the other hand, since
K(f>0)
decays to zero with increasing
f
, the supercritical regime is reentrant, at least for
f
large enough, in agreement with our numerical simulations.
In this case, there are two solutions to Eq. (
S10
) which correspond to the two branches of the Lambert
W
function.
Considering the branch leading to lower effective temperatures, we finally find that at fixed
f
the tip of the reentrant region is located in the density effective temperature plane at

\frac{\bar{\rho}}{\lambda\rho_{\rm c}}=1-\frac{K}{(1+\alpha f)\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}f}\left(1-\frac{1}{\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}f}\right),\qquad\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}f=-2W_{-1}\left(-\frac{\sqrt{K}}{2}\right)\qquad(f>0).

(S11)

The derivation of Eq. (
S11
) only formally holds when
|\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}f|\gg 1
, such that we do not expect it to hold
too close to the depinning transition at
f=0
.
Indeed, taking
f\to 0
eq. (
S11
) predicts for instance that the tip of the reentrant region is found at mean particle densities larger than
\lambda\rho_{\rm c}
,
which corresponds to the condensation threshold in the limit of vanishing effective temperatures, see fig.
S1
.
The lower threshold for which the effective temperature and density (
S11
) take on real values can be found to correspond to solving
K=4/e^{2}
for
\alpha
, so,
\alpha=(e^{2}-4)/(e^{2}-4+4f)
and subsequently plugging this into (
S11
),

\frac{\bar{\rho}}{\rho_{\rm c}}=f\frac{4-5e^{2}-4f+(f+1)e^{4}}{\left((f+1)e^{2}-4\right)^{2}}\,,\qquad\beta_{\rm c}V_{\rm b}=\frac{2}{f}\,.

(S12)

These limiting values are shown as functions of
f
by dashed black lines in fig.
S1
.

II.2
Density profile in the supercritical condensation regime

As we discuss in the main text, for
f>0
and
T_{\rm eff}<T_{\rm c}
the system develops a condensate with a finite width
w

leading the piecewise density profile

\rho(x)\underset{f>0}{=}\begin{cases}\frac{\bar{J}\alpha(1-\alpha)}{1+\alpha f}\left(1-z^{\tfrac{x}{\alpha}-1}\right)\!+\!\rho_{\rm c}z^{\tfrac{x}{\alpha}-1}&0\leq x\leq\alpha\\
\bar{\rho}\phi_{\rm c}/w&\alpha<x<x^{*}\\
\frac{\bar{J}(1-\alpha)}{f}\left(1-e^{\beta V_{\rm b}f\tfrac{x-x^{*}}{1-\alpha}}\right)+\rho_{\rm c}e^{\beta V_{\rm b}f\tfrac{x-x^{*}}{1-\alpha}}&x^{*}\leq x\leq 1\end{cases}\quad(T_{\rm eff}<T_{\rm c}),

(S13)

where
x^{*}=w+\alpha
marks the upper limit of existence of the condensate and
\phi_{\rm c}
is the condensate fraction.
Since the potential is linear, the stationary current can simply be obtained from its integral definition as

\displaystyle\bar{J}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{V_{\rm b}}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\,U^{\prime}(x)\rho(x)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}+f\right)\int_{0}^{\alpha}{\rm d}x\,\rho(x)+f\bar{\rho}\phi_{\rm c}+f\int_{x{{}^{*}}}^{1}{\rm d}x\,\rho(x)\right],

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\left[\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\alpha}{\rm d}x\,\rho(x)+f\bar{\rho}\right],

(S14)

where the last equality was obtained substituting
\phi_{\rm c}
using the normalization condition on
\rho(x)
.
Replacing
\rho(x)
by its expression given in (
S13
) and solving for
\bar{J}
we finally obtain

\bar{J}\underset{f>0}{=}\frac{\rho_{\rm c}(1+\alpha f)}{1-\alpha}\frac{(1-\alpha)(z-1)+z\beta V_{\rm b}f(1+\alpha f)\bar{\rho}/\rho_{\rm c}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)(z-1)+z\beta V_{\rm b}\lambda(1+\alpha f)^{2}}\qquad(T_{\rm eff}<T_{\rm c}),

(S15)

Using the periodicity condition
\rho(0)=\rho(1)
then allows to express the condensate width as function of the stationary current
\bar{J}
.
Namely, we find after some algebra that

w=(1-\alpha)\left(1-\frac{\ln(\chi)}{\beta V_{\rm b}f}\right),\qquad\chi\equiv\frac{\bar{\rho}\beta V_{\rm b}f(1+\alpha f)(z+\alpha f)-\rho_{\rm c}\left[(1-\alpha)(z-1)+\beta V_{\rm b}\lambda(1+\alpha f)^{2}\right]}{\bar{\rho}z\beta V_{\rm b}(1+\alpha f)^{2}+\rho_{\rm c}\left[(1-\alpha)(z-1)-\beta V_{\rm b}z\lambda(1+\alpha f)^{2}\right]}.

(S16)

Although the expression of
w
is fairly complicated, it reduces close to the transition and for vanishing temperatures to

w\underset{T_{\rm eff}\lesssim T_{\rm c}}{\propto}\frac{T_{\rm c}}{T_{\rm eff}}-1,\qquad w\underset{T_{\rm eff}\ll T_{\rm c}}{\simeq}(1-\alpha)\left(1+\frac{k_{\rm B}T_{\rm eff}}{V_{\rm b}f}\ln\left[(1+\alpha f)(1-\lambda\rho_{\rm c}/\bar{\rho})\right]\right),

(S17)

where the first expression is given in terms of a complicated prefactor which is unimportant to specify here.
Similarly, the condensate fraction which can be calculated from

\phi_{\rm c}=1-\frac{1}{\bar{\rho}}\left(\int_{0}^{\alpha}{\rm d}x\,\rho(x)+\int_{x{{}^{*}}}^{1}{\rm d}x\,\rho(x)\right),

(S18)

is a complicated expression of limited practical use.
Nevertheless, it is relatively straightforward to derive the following limiting behaviours

\phi_{\rm c}\underset{T_{\rm eff}\lesssim T_{\rm c}}{\propto}1-\frac{T_{\rm eff}}{T_{\rm c}},\qquad\phi_{\rm c}\underset{T_{\rm eff}\to 0}{\sim}1-\frac{\alpha^{2}f}{(1+\alpha f)\lambda}\qquad(f>0).

(S19)

II.3
The Thermodynamics of condensation

The average internal energy is defined as
\langle U\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}U(x)\rho(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
. Above the transition threshold
T_{\rm c}
, the average energy per particle can be rewritten as

\frac{\langle U\rangle}{N}=\frac{\rho_{0}}{N}\int_{0}^{1}U(x)R(x)\mathrm{d}x\qquad(f<0,\,T_{\rm eff}>T_{\rm c})\,,

(S20)

where
N
is the total number of particles (
N=\bar{\rho}
in the chosen units).
In the subcritical condensed regime, the condensate energy depends only on the external driving force, not on the temperature. The energy per particle is then

\frac{\langle U\rangle}{N}=\frac{\rho_{\rm c}}{\bar{\rho}}\int_{0}^{1}U(x)R(x)\mathrm{d}x-\alpha F\phi_{\rm c}\,\qquad(f<0,\,T_{\rm eff}\leq T_{\rm c})\,.

(S21)

For supercritical condensed systems, however, the expression for the internal energy is more complicated. Splitting the integral in three parts, corresponding to the three intervals
[0;\alpha]
,
[\alpha;x^{*}]
and
[x^{*};1]
with
x^{*}\equiv\alpha+w
, yields

\frac{\langle U\rangle}{N}=\frac{\rho_{\rm c}}{N}\int_{0}^{\alpha}U(x)R(x)\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\phi_{\rm c}}{w}\int_{\alpha}^{x^{*}}U(x)\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\rho_{\rm c}}{N}\int_{x^{*}}^{1}U(x)R(x)\mathrm{d}x\,\qquad(f>0)\,.

(S22)

The second integral corresponds to the energy that is contained in the extended condensate, and it can be simplified as

\frac{\phi_{\rm c}}{w}\int_{\alpha}^{x^{*}}U(x)\mathrm{d}x=-\phi_{\rm c}F_{\rm c}\left[\alpha+f\left(\alpha+\frac{w}{2}\right)\right]\,,

(S23)

such that the average energy is

\frac{\langle U\rangle}{N}=\frac{\rho_{\rm c}}{N}\left[\int_{0}^{\alpha}U(x)R(x)\mathrm{d}x+\int_{x^{*}}^{1}U(x)R(x)\mathrm{d}x\right]-\phi_{\rm c}F_{\rm c}\left[\alpha+f\left(\alpha+\frac{w}{2}\right)\right]\,.

(S24)

The integrations occurring in equations (
S20
) to (
S24
) can be performed exactly, but they result in complicated expressions that are of limited practical value. However, for subcritical strong and weak confinement, respectively, the energy becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\langle U\rangle}{NV_{\rm b}}

\displaystyle\underset{\beta V_{\rm b}\gg 1}{\sim}-\frac{f}{2(1-\alpha)}\begin{cases}1&T_{\rm eff}>T_{\rm c}\\
2\alpha+(1-2\alpha)\frac{\rho_{\rm c}}{\bar{\rho}}&T_{\rm eff}\leq T_{\rm c}\end{cases}

(S25)

\displaystyle\frac{\langle U\rangle}{NV_{\rm b}}

\displaystyle\underset{\beta V_{\rm b}\ll 1}{\sim}\frac{1}{2}\begin{cases}1-\frac{1+f}{1-\alpha}&T_{\rm eff}>T_{\rm c}\\
\frac{\rho_{\rm c}}{\bar{\rho}}-\frac{1+f}{1-\alpha}\left[2\alpha+\frac{\rho_{\rm c}}{\bar{\rho}}(1-2\alpha)\right]\,.&T_{\rm eff}\leq T_{\rm c}\end{cases}

(S26)

For weak confinement, the internal energy corresponds exactly to the results found in ref.
Mahault and Golestanian 2020
if the rescaled force is set to
f=-1
. Driving the system hence results in a linear decrease in energy as a function of the driving force. For strong confinement, however, the energy shows a clear departure from the ideal Bose gas, since is independent of
T_{\rm eff}
.

The heat capacity
C=\mathrm{d}\langle U\rangle/\mathrm{d}T_{\rm eff}
can be rewritten as
C=-k_{\rm B}\beta^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\langle U\rangle/\mathrm{d}\beta
, and in the weak confinement limit it scales as

\displaystyle\frac{C}{Nk_{\rm B}}

\displaystyle\underset{\beta V_{\rm b}\ll 1}{\sim}\frac{(\beta V_{\rm b})^{2}}{12}\left[1-\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}(1+f)\right]\begin{cases}1&T_{\rm eff}>T_{\rm c}\\
4\,\frac{\rho_{\rm c}}{\bar{\rho}}&T_{\rm eff}\leq T_{\rm c}\,,\end{cases}

(S27)

which again reduces to the known result for the undriven
f=-1
case. In the strong confinement limit, the exact expressions become too complicated to study the asymptotic behaviour. However, numerical analysis reveals that
C/Nk_{\rm B}\sim{\rm const}
for
T_{\rm eff}>T_{\rm c}
and
C/Nk_{\rm B}\sim 1/\beta V_{\rm b}
for
T_{\rm eff}\leq T_{\rm c}
, similar to the ideal Bose gas results of ref.
Golestanian 2019
.

III
Details about numerical simulations

Our numerical simulations of equation (1) of the main text were performed using the finite volume scheme proposed in ref.
Bessemoulin-Chatard and Filbet 2012
, which is tailored to handle the steep solutions associated with the condensed phase.
The main idea of this scheme is to merge the convective and diffusive parts of the current, which we thus write as

J(x,t)=-g(\rho(x,t))\partial_{x}\left[U(x)+h(\rho(x,t))\right]\equiv g(\rho(x,t))\partial_{x}A(x,t),

(S28)

where we have defined

g(\rho)\equiv M(\rho)\rho
and assumed
M(\rho)>0
such that we can define a function
h(\rho)
satisfying

\partial_{x}h(\rho)\equiv\frac{D(\rho)}{M(\rho)}\partial_{x}\ln(\rho).

Following the finite volume approach in ref.
LeVeque 2002
, we discretize the spatial domain into cells of width
\Delta x
and define for a given function
\zeta(x,t)
its average over the
i^{\rm th}
cell as
\zeta_{i}(t)
.
The continuity equation is then expressed for the
i^{\rm th}
cell as

\frac{{\rm d}\rho_{i}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{J_{i+1/2}-J_{i-1/2}}{\Delta x}\,,

(S29)

where
J_{i\pm 1/2}
denote the numerical approximations of the current at the boundaries of the cell, which are defined as

\displaystyle J_{i+1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{i+1/2}}{2}\left(g(\rho_{i})+g(\rho_{i+1})\right)-\frac{|A_{i+1/2}|\alpha_{i+1/2}}{2}\left(\rho_{i+1}-\rho_{i}\right),

\displaystyle A_{i+1/2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{U(r_{i+1})-U(r_{i})}{\Delta x}-\frac{h(\rho_{i+1})-h(\rho_{i})}{\Delta x},

\displaystyle\alpha_{i+1/2}

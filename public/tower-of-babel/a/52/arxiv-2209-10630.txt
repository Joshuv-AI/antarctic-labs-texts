John Bridstrup, Jian-Min Yuan, John S. Schreck

Abstract
Two isoforms of β − \beta- amyloid peptides, A β \beta 40 and A β \beta 42, differ from each other only in the last two amino acids, IA, at the end of A β \beta 42. They, however, differ significantly in their ability in inducing Alzheimer’s disease (AD). The rate curves of fibril growth of A β \beta 40 and A β \beta 42 and the effects of molecular crowding have been measured in in vitro experiments. These experimental curves, on the other hand, have been fitted in terms of rate constants for elementary reaction steps using rate equation approaches. Several sets of such rate parameters have been reported in the literature. Employing a recently developed stochastic kinetic method, implemented in a browser-based simulator, popsim , we study to reveal the differences in the kinetic behaviors implied by these sets of rate parameters. In particular, the stochastic method is used to distinguish the kinetic behaviors between A β \beta 40 and A β \beta 42 isoforms. As a result, we make general comments on the usefulness of these sets of rate parameters.

III
Gillespie stochastic simulation algorithm

popsim
implements the Gillespie stochastic simulation algorithm (SSA), which is a method for generating statistically correct trajectories of a system of molecules undergoing discrete reaction steps. It is particularly useful for investigating the behavior of small numbers of reacting particles (
ie
. small system volumes), for which number fluctuations are comparable to the total number of reactants and continuous rate equations are inaccurate in describing the reaction processes. The algorithm is given in
J. Bridstrup, J. S. Schreck, and J. M.
Yuan 2021
and included here for completeness:

1.

Set the initial species populations and set
t=0
.

2.

Calculate the transition rate or the propensity function,
r_{i}
, for each of the
M
possible reactions.

3.

Set the total transition rate
Q=\sum_{i=1}^{M}r_{i}
.

4.

Generate two uniform random, (0,1), numbers,
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
.

5.

Set
\Delta t=\frac{1}{Q}ln(\frac{1}{u_{1}})
.

6.

Find
\mu\in[1,..,M]
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{\mu-1}r_{i}<u_{2}Q\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\mu}r_{i}
.

7.

Set
t=t+\Delta t
and update molecular species based on reaction
\mu
.

8.

Return to step 2 and repeat until an end condition is met.

III.1
Calculating stochastic rate constants

In the previous sections, we have discussed chemical species in terms of their bulk concentration in solution. Since
popsim
deals with discrete populations of species, it is necessary to relate the bulk rate constants, which are volume independent, to the volume-dependent stochastic rate constants. As an example, the bulk rate of change in the concentration of nuclei due to primary nucleation is given by

\frac{dc_{n_{c}}}{dt}=k_{n}c_{1}^{n_{c}},

where all
c
are in units of molar concentration. Using the relationship

c_{i}=\frac{N_{i}}{N_{A}V},

where
N_{i}
is the number of species of size
i
,
V
is the system volume and
N_{A}
is Avogadro’s number, the rate of change of the number of nuclei due to primary nucleation is found to be

\frac{dN_{n_{c}}}{dt}=\frac{k_{n}}{(N_{A}V)^{n_{c}-1}}N_{1}^{n_{c}}.

Again, making use of the relationship between concentration and volume, the stochastic rate constant is found to be

k_{n}^{\prime}=\bigg(\frac{c_{0}}{N_{0}}\bigg)^{n_{c}-1}k_{n},

where
N_{0}
and
c_{0}
are the initial number and bulk concentration of monomers, respectively. The other stochastic rate constants (’) are given, relative to their bulk counterparts, as follows:

\displaystyle a^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{c_{0}}{N_{0}}\bigg)a

\displaystyle k_{+}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{c_{0}}{N_{0}}\bigg)k_{+}

\displaystyle k_{f}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{c_{0}}{N_{0}}\bigg)k_{f}

\displaystyle k_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{c_{0}}{N_{0}}\bigg)^{n_{2}}k_{2}

\displaystyle b^{\prime}

\displaystyle=b

\displaystyle k_{-}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=k_{-}

\displaystyle k_{b}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=k_{b}

\displaystyle\bar{k_{2}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\bar{k_{2}}.

popsim

Comments

V.1
System volume

Stochastic simulations inherently deal with small numbers of particles and, thus, small volumes. It is relatively simple to calculate the volume of the system in question from initial conditions by rearranging the concentration relationship to find

V=\frac{N_{0}}{N_{A}c_{0}}.

Typically the smaller the simulation volume, the larger fluctuations will be relative to total number of particles. This is where
popsim
and stochastic simulation become increasingly useful.

V.2
Size distribution

In this article we have examined five sets of rate parameters for the kinetic behaviors of A
\beta
40 and A
\beta
42. It turns out quite diverse results are predicted for the two peptides. For instance, the steady-state average aggregate size predicted (for N = 3000) for A
\beta
42 are, respectively, 4.43, 45.3, 2970
based on the Ab42-JSS, Ab42-Cohen, and Ab42-Meisl sets of parameters. Because of such very different kinetic behaviors predicted, it is, in principle, easy to determine which set is more correct by carrying out one additional experimental measurement, for example, the aggregate size distributions. The tools for such measurements are already available, for example, atomic force microscopy
Ruggeri et al. 2019
or cryo-EM methods
Yang et al. 2022
. Measurements of the size distributions should be the best way in the present case to determine which sets of rate parameters are the proper ones to use.

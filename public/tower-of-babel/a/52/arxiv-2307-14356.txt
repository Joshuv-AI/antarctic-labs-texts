Introduction

This paper deals with the following system

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-\Delta)^{1/2}u=\lambda_{1}u+(I_{\mu}*F(u,v))F_{u}(u,v),\quad\mbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R},\\
(-\Delta)^{1/2}v=\lambda_{2}v+(I_{\mu}*F(u,v))F_{v}(u,v),\quad\mbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(1.1)

with the prescribed masses

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=a^{2},\quad\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|v|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=b^{2},\quad u,v\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}),

(1.2)

where
(-\Delta)^{1/2}
denotes the 1/2-Laplacian operator,
a,b>0
are prescribed,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
I_{\mu}(x)=\frac{{1}}{{|x|^{\mu}}}
with
\mu\in(0,1)
,
F_{u},F_{v}
are partial derivatives of
F
and

The interest in studying problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) originates from the study of the following nonlocal elliptic equation

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u=\lambda u+(I_{\mu}*F(u))f(u),\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},

(1.3)

where
s\in(0,1)
,
I_{\mu}=\frac{1}{|x|^{\mu}}
with
\mu\in(0,N)
,
F(u)
is the primitive function of
f(u)
, and
(-\Delta)^{s}
is the fractional Laplacian operator defined by

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u(x):=C({N,s})\ \mbox{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}y,\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},

for
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
where P.V. means the Cauchy principal value and
C({N,s})
is some positive normalization constant, we refer to
[
19
]

1.3
) arises in many interesting physical situations in quantum theory and plays an important role in describing the finite-range many-body interactions. Equation (
1.3
) is well known in the literature as the Choquard equation and was first introduced by Penrose in
[
41

to investigate the self-gravitational collapse of a
quantum mechanical wave function.

To get solutions of
(\ref{1.2})
, one way is to fix
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and look for solutions of
(\ref{1.2})

as critical points of the energy functional
\hat{J}:H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

[

\displaystyle\hat{J}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}-\lambda|u|^{2})\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))F(u)\mathrm{d}x

with

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,

where
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a Hilbert space with the inner product and norm respectively

\langle u,v\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s/2}u(-\Delta)^{s/2}v\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}uv\mathrm{d}x,

\|u\|=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{1/2}.

Another interesting way is to prescribe the
L^{2}
-norm of the unknown

appears as a Lagrange multiplier, that is to consider the following problem

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}u=\lambda u+(I_{\mu}*F(u))f(u),\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=a^{2},\quad u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{cases}

(1.4)

for any fixed
a>0
. This type of solutions is called normalized solution, and can be obtained by
looking for critical points of the following energy functional

\displaystyle\bar{J}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))F(u)\mathrm{d}x

In particular, we are interested in looking for ground state solutions, i.e., solutions minimizing
\bar{J}

3
The variational framework

For the fractional Laplacian operator, the special case when
s=1/2
is called the square of the Laplacian. We recall the definition of the fractional Sobolev space

H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})=\Big\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y<\infty\Big\},

endowed with the standard norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{1/2}=\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi}[u]_{1/2}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{1/2},

where the term

[u]_{1/2}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{1/2}

denotes the Gagliardo semi-norm of a function
u
. Moreover, by
[
19
, Proposition 3.6]
, we have

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}u\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,\ \ \mbox{for any}\ u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}).

Let
\mathcal{X}:=H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
with the norm

\|(u,v)\|:=\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi}[u]_{1/2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\pi}[v]_{1/2}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|v|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{1/2}.

Moreover, for any
c>0
, we set

S(c):=\Big\{u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}):\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=c^{2}\Big\},

and

\mathcal{S}:=S(a)\times S(b).

Problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) has a variational structure and its associated energy functional
\mathcal{J}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}v\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(u,v))F(u,v)\mathrm{d}x.

By using assumptions
(F_{1})
and
(F_{2})
, it follows that for any
\zeta>0
,
q>1
and
\alpha>\pi
, there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|F_{z_{1}}(z)|,|F_{z_{2}}(z)|\leq\zeta|z|^{\kappa}+C|z|^{q-1}(e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}-1),\quad\mbox{for any $z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}$},

and using
(F_{3})
, we have

\displaystyle|F(z)|\leq\zeta|z|^{\kappa+1}+C|z|^{q}(e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}-1),\quad\mbox{for any $z\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}$}.

(3.1)

By (
2.1
) and (
3.1
), we know
\mathcal{J}
is well defined in
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{J}\in C^{1}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{R})
with

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u,v),(\varphi,\psi)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{[u(x)-u(y)][\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)]}{|x-y|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{[v(x)-v(y)][\psi(x)-\psi(y)]}{|x-y|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(u,v))F_{u}(u,v)\varphi\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(u,v))F_{v}(u,v)\phi\mathrm{d}x,

for any
(u,v),(\varphi,\psi)\in\mathcal{X}
. Hence, a critical point of
\mathcal{J}
on
\mathcal{S}

corresponds to a solution of problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

To understand the geometry of
\mathcal{J}|_{\mathcal{S}}
, for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
, we define

\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)(x):=e^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u(e^{\beta}x),\quad\text{for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}$}.

One can easily check that
\|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{2}=\|u\|_{2}
for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. As a consequence, for any
(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}
, it holds that
\mathcal{H}((u,v),\beta):=(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta),\mathcal{H}(v,\beta))\in\mathcal{S}
for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\mathcal{H}((u,v),\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})=\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{H}((u,v),\beta_{1}),\beta_{2})=\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{H}((u,v),\beta_{2}),\beta_{1})
for any
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.
By Lemma
4.1
, we find that
\mathcal{J}
is unbounded from below on
\mathcal{S}
.
It is well known that all critical points of

belong to the Pohoz̆aev manifold (see

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(a,b)=\big\{(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}:P(u,v)=0\big\},

\displaystyle P(u,v)=\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}u\|_{2}^{2}+\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}v\|_{2}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(u,v))\widetilde{F}(u,v)\mathrm{d}x,

where
\widetilde{F}(z)=z\cdot\nabla F(z)-(2-\mu)F(z)
.
This enlightens us to consider the minimization of
\mathcal{J}
on

, i.e.,

\displaystyle m(a,b)=\inf_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{P}(a,b)}\mathcal{J}(u,v).

Our task is to show that

is a critical level of
\mathcal{J}|_{\mathcal{S}}
. As will be shown in Lemma

,

)-(

In order to estimate the upper bound of

(4.3)

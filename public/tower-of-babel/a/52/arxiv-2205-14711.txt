The derivative formula of p p -adic L L -functions for imaginary quadratic fields at trivial zeros Thanks: Hsieh was partially supported by a MOST grant MOST 108-2628-M-001-009-MY4 and 110-2628-M-001-004-. Chida was supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Number JP18K03202.
Masataka Chida, Ming-Lun Hsieh

Abstract
. The rank one Gross conjecture for Deligne-Ribet p p -adic L L -functions was solved in works of Darmon-Dasgupta-Pollack and Ventullo by the Eisenstein congruence among Hilbert modular forms. The purpose of this paper is to prove an analogue of the Gross conjecture for the Katz p p -adic L L -functions attached to imaginary quadratic fields via the congruences between CM forms and non-CM forms. The new ingredient is to apply the p p -adic Rankin-Selberg method to construct a non-CM Hida family which is congruent to a Hida family of CM forms at the 1 + ε 1+\varepsilon specialization. Résumé. Le conjecture de Gross en rang 1 pour les fonctions L L p p -adiques de Deligne-Ribet a été résolue par Darmon-Dasgupta-Pollack et Ventullo au moyen de congruences d’Eisenstein parmi les formes modulaires de Hilbert. Le but de cet article est de prouver un analogue de la conjecture de Gross pour les fonctions L L p p -adiques de Katz des corps quadratiques imaginaires, via les congruences entre formes CM et formes non-CM. Le nouvel ingrédient est l’application de la méthode de Rankin-Selberg p p -adique pour construire une famille de Hida non-CM qui est congruente à une famille de Hida de formes CM pour la spécialisation 1 + ε 1+\varepsilon .

Conjecture 1

For all primitive ray class characters

Theorem A

Let

The proof in
[

]

In the case of general CM fields, their method relies on the existence of Rubin-Stark units in ray class fields, which is one of the major open conjectures in algebraic number theory. In contrast, we adapt the ideas in
[

]

3.1.

We recall a general construction of Eisenstein series in the theory of automorphic forms. If

{\mathcal{A}}_{k}(\omega)
denote the space of automorphic forms
\varphi:\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbf{Q})\backslash\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbf{A})\to\mathbf{C}
such that

\varphi(zg\kappa_{\theta})=\omega(z)\varphi(g)e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}k\theta},\quad z\in\mathbf{A}^{\times},\,\kappa_{\theta}=\begin{pmatrix}{\cos\theta}&{\sin\theta}\\
{-\sin\theta}&{\cos\theta}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbf{R}).

Let
{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{k}(\omega)\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{k}(\omega)
be the subspace of cusp forms. For
a\in\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, put
\left<{a}\right>:=a\cdot(\iota_{p}\circ\boldsymbol{\omega})(a)^{-1}
.
For each place
v
, let
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{v}
be the local component of
\boldsymbol{\omega}
at
v
.
Let
\mathcal{D}
be the pair

\mathcal{D}=(k,C),\quad C\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}\text{ and }p\nmid C.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbf{A}^{2})
be the space of Bruhat-Schwartz functions on
\mathbf{A}^{2}
. Define

\Phi_{\mathcal{D}}=\Phi_{\mathcal{D},\infty}\otimes_{\ell}^{\prime}\Phi_{\mathcal{D},\ell}\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbf{A}^{2})
by

—

\Phi_{\mathcal{D},\infty}(x,y)=2^{-k}(x+\sqrt{-1}y)^{k}e^{-\pi(x^{2}+y^{2})}
,

—

\Phi_{\mathcal{D},\ell}(x,y)=\mathbb{I}_{C\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}}(x)\mathbb{I}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}}(y)
,

—

\Phi_{\mathcal{D},p}(x,y)=\boldsymbol{\omega}_{p}^{-k}(x)\mathbb{I}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}}(x)\mathbb{I}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}(y).

Recall that
f_{\mathcal{D},s}=\otimes_{v}f_{\mathcal{D},s,v}
, where
f_{\mathcal{D},s,v}=f_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{v}^{k},{\mathbf{1}},\Phi_{\mathcal{D},v},s}:\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{v})\to\mathbf{C}
is the Godement section associated with
\Phi_{\mathcal{D},v}
defined by

f_{\mathcal{D},s,v}(g_{v})=\boldsymbol{\omega}_{v}^{k}(\det g)\left|\det g_{v}\right|_{v}^{s+{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{\mathbf{Q}_{v}^{\times}}\Phi_{\mathcal{D},v}((0,t_{v})g_{v})\boldsymbol{\omega}^{k}(t_{v})\left|t_{v}\right|_{v}^{2s+1}\mathrm{d}^{\times}t_{v}

(
cf.

[
CH20
, (4.1)]
). Let
B(\mathbf{Q})
be the upper triangular matrices in
\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbf{Q})
. Then the Eisenstein series
E_{\mathbf{A}}(-,f_{\mathcal{D},s}):\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbf{A})\to\mathbf{C}
is the series defined by

E_{\mathbf{A}}(g,f_{\mathcal{D},s})=\sum_{\gamma\in B(\mathbf{Q})\backslash\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbf{Q})}f_{\mathcal{D},s}(\gamma g)\in{\mathcal{A}}_{k}({\mathbf{1}})

(
cf.

[
Bum97
, (7.8), page 351]
). The series
E_{\mathbf{A}}(g,f_{\mathcal{D},s})
is absolutely convergent for
\text{Re}\,(s)>1/2
and can be analytically continued to the whole complex plane except at
s=\pm\frac{1}{2}
. Suppose that
k\geq 2
. For

, put

E_{k}(C)(z)=y^{-\frac{k}{2}}E_{\mathbf{A}}(\begin{pmatrix}{y}&{x}\\
{0}&{1}\end{pmatrix},f_{\mathcal{D},s})|_{s=\frac{1-k}{2}}.

Then

defines a classical Eisenstein series of weight
k
and level
\Gamma_{0}(pC)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The Fourier expansion of
E_{k}(C)
is given by

E_{k}(C)=\frac{\left<{C}\right>^{k}}{2C}\zeta_{p}(1-k)+\sum_{n>0,C\mid n}{\bf a}(n,E_{k}(C))q^{n},

where

(3.1)

{\bf a}(n,E_{k}(C))=\sum_{C\mid d\mid n,\,p\nmid d}d^{k-1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\rm Dir}(d)^{-k}.

Proof.

For each positive integer
n
, the Fourier coefficient
{\bf a}(n,E_{k}(C))
is the product of local Whittaker functions

{\bf a}(n,E_{k}(C))=n^{\frac{k}{2}}\prod_{\ell}W(\begin{pmatrix}{n}&0\\
0&{1}\end{pmatrix},f_{\mathcal{D},s,\ell})|_{s=\frac{1-k}{2}},

where
W(-,f_{\mathcal{D},s,\ell}):\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})\to\mathbf{C}
is the local Whittaker function defined by

W(g,f_{\mathcal{D},s,\ell})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\ell^{-n}\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}}f_{\mathcal{D},s,\ell}(\begin{pmatrix}{0}&{-1}\\
{1}&{0}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{1}&{x}\\
{0}&{1}\end{pmatrix}g){\bf e}_{\ell}(-x)\mathrm{d}x_{\ell},

and the Haar measure
\mathrm{d}x_{\ell}
is normalized so that
\mathop{\rm vol}\nolimits(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell},\mathrm{d}x_{\ell})=1
(
cf.

[
CH20
, Corollary 4.7]
and
[
Bum97
, (7.14)]
). Hence we get (
3.1
) from the explicit formulae of these local Whittaker functions in
[
CH20
, Lemma 4.6]
.

On the other hand, the constant term
{\bf a}(0,E_{k}(C))
of
E_{k}(C)
at the infinity cusp is given by

{\bf a}(0,E_{k}(C))=f_{\mathcal{D},\frac{1-k}{2}}(1)+(Mf_{\mathcal{D},s})(1)\Big|_{s=\frac{1-k}{2}},

where
Mf_{\mathcal{D},s}(g)
is obtained by the analytic continuation of the intertwining integral

Mf_{\mathcal{D},s}(g)=\int_{\mathbf{A}}f_{\mathcal{D},s}(\begin{pmatrix}{0}&{-1}\\
{1}&{0}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{1}&{x}\\
{0}&{1}\end{pmatrix})\mathrm{d}x,\,g\in\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbf{A})

(
cf.

[
Bum97
, (7.15)]
). A direct computation shows that for
\text{Re}\,(s)\gg 0
,

\displaystyle Mf_{\mathcal{D},s}(1)=

\displaystyle\prod_{v}\int_{\mathbf{Q}_{v}}f_{\mathcal{D},s,v}(\begin{pmatrix}{0}&{-1}\\
{1}&{x}\end{pmatrix})\mathrm{d}x_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C^{2s}\prod_{\ell\mid C}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{k}_{\ell}(C)}{2}\cdot\frac{L(2s,\boldsymbol{\omega}^{k})}{L(2s,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{p}^{k})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C^{2s}\boldsymbol{\omega}((C))^{k}}{2}\cdot L(2s,\boldsymbol{\omega}^{k})\cdot\begin{cases}(1-p^{-2s}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{p}^{k}(p))&\text{ if $\boldsymbol{\omega}_{p}^{k}$ is unramified},\\
1&\text{ if $\boldsymbol{\omega}_{p}^{k}$ is ramified},\end{cases}

(
cf.

[
Bum97
, Proposition 2.6.3 and (7.27)]
). Since
f_{\mathcal{D},s,p}(1)=0
, we see that

\displaystyle{\bf a}(0,E_{k}(C))

\displaystyle=Mf_{\mathcal{D},s}(1)|_{s=\frac{1-k}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left<{C}\right>^{k}}{2C}(1-p^{k-1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\rm Dir}^{-k}(p))L(1-k,\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\rm Dir}^{-k})=\frac{\left<{C}\right>^{k}}{2C}\zeta_{p}(1-k).

This finishes the computation of the Fourier expansion of
E_{k}(C)
. ∎

as the unique maps making this diagram commute.
Note that
\rho_{W^{*}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}),c}
is an isomorphism.

We define the
\mathscr{L}
-invariant associated to
V^{*}(1)
and
D^{\perp}
by

\mathscr{L}(V^{*}(1),D^{\perp})=(-1)^{e}\operatorname{det}\left(\rho_{W^{*}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}),f}\circ\rho_{W^{*}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}),c}^{-1}\,\middle|\,\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(W^{*}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}))\right),

where
e=\operatorname{dim}_{E}\mathscr{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(W^{*}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}))
.

Proposition 5.2
.

\mathscr{L}(V^{*}(1),D^{\perp})=(-1)^{e}\mathscr{L}(V,D).

Proof.

See
[
Ben11
, Proposition 2.2.7]
and
[
BH20
, Proposition 2.3.8]
.
∎

Using this
\mathscr{L}
-invariant, Benois formulated the exceptional zero conjecture for general crystalline case including non-critical range.

5.2.
Comparison of
\mathscr{L}
-invariants

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field and
p
a prime such that
p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Let
\chi:\operatorname{Gal}(H/\penalty K)\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}
be a non-trivial ring class character.
Let
E
be a
p
-adic field containing all of the values of
\chi
.
Assume that
\chi
is unramified at places above
p
and
\chi(\overline{\mathfrak{p}})=1
.
Now we consider the case
V=(\operatorname{Ind}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{K}\chi)^{*}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\rm{cyc}})
.
In this case, we have
V^{*}(1)=\operatorname{Ind}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{K}\chi
and
it is known that

\mathit{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},V)=\mathit{H}^{1}_{f}(K,\chi^{-1}(1))=(\mathcal{O}_{H}^{\times}\otimes E)[\chi]
,

\mathit{H}^{1}_{f,\{p\}}(\mathbb{Q},V)=\mathit{H}^{1}_{\{\mathfrak{p},\overline{\mathfrak{p}}\}}(K,\chi^{-1}(1))=(\mathcal{O}_{H}[1/\penalty p]^{\times}\otimes E)[\chi]

and
\mathit{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},V^{*}(1))=\mathit{H}^{1}_{f}(K,\chi)=0
.
For
V=(\operatorname{Ind}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{K}\chi)^{*}(1)
, it is easy to see that
V
satisfies the conditions C1) – C5).

Denote

V^{+}=\{v\in V|_{G_{K}}\mid\sigma(v)=\chi^{-1}(\sigma)\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\rm{cyc}}(\sigma)v\textup{ for all }\sigma\in G_{K}\}

and

V^{-}=\{v\in V|_{G_{K}}\mid\sigma(v)=\chi^{-1}(c\sigma c)\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\rm{cyc}}(\sigma)v\textup{ for all }\sigma\in G_{K}\}.

Since
\chi\neq{\chi}^{c}
, we have a canonical decomposition
V|_{G_{K}}=V^{+}\oplus V^{-}
.
Put

V_{\mathfrak{p}}=V^{+}|_{G_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}}
and
V_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}=V^{-}|_{G_{K_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}}}
.
Then the natural map
\iota:V|_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to V_{\mathfrak{p}}\oplus V_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}
becomes an isomorphism.
Hence,
\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},V)=\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},(\operatorname{Ind}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{K}\chi)^{*}(1))
can be identified with

\mathit{H}^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{p}},\chi^{-1}(1))\oplus\mathit{H}^{1}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}},\chi^{-1}(1))
.

Definition 5.3
.

We choose a regular submodule
D
of
\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)
as

D=\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\iota^{-1}(V_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}))
.

Then
\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathit{F}_{0}\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{\dagger}(V))
is identified with
\mathit{H}^{1}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}},\chi^{-1}(1))

under the isomorphism
\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{\dagger}(V))\simeq\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},V)
.
Here we recall that
\mathit{F}_{0}\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{\dagger}(V)
is the
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-submodule of

\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{\dagger}(V)
associated to
D
.
This property also characterizes the choice of the regular submodule
D
.
Then the modified Euler factor associated to
(V,D)
is given by

\mathcal{E}(V,D)=\operatorname{det}(1-p^{-1}\varphi^{-1}|D)\operatorname{det}(1-\varphi|\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)/\penalty D)=(1-\chi(\overline{\mathfrak{p}}))(1-\chi^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})p^{-1})=0

and

\mathcal{E}^{+}(V,D)=\operatorname{det}(1-p^{-1}\varphi^{-1}|D_{-1})\operatorname{det}(1-p^{-1}\varphi^{-1}|D^{\perp})=(1-\chi(\mathfrak{p})p^{-1}),

where
\mathcal{E}^{+}(V,D)
is the modified Euler factor which is used in the formula of the exceptional zero conjecture (

, Conjecture 4]
).
Note that

coincides with the Euler factor appeared in Conjecture
1
.
Therefore Conjecture

is compatible with the exceptional zero conjecture formulated by Benois.

Proposition 5.4
.

We have
\mathscr{L}(V,D)=-\mathscr{L}(\chi)
, where
\mathscr{L}(\chi)
is the
\mathscr{L}
-invariant defined in (
1.4
).

Proof.

In this case,
\mathit{F}_{0}\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{\dagger}(V)\simeq\mathscr{R}_{E}(|x|x)
and
\mathit{F}_{-1}\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{\dagger}(V)=0
,
where we write
x
for the character given by the identity map and
|x|
for
|x|=p^{v_{p}(x)}
.
Hence we have
W=\operatorname{gr}_{0}\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{\dagger}(V)\simeq\mathscr{R}_{E}(|x|x)
and

\mathit{H}^{1}(W)\simeq\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathscr{R}_{E}(|x|x))\simeq\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathscr{R}_{E}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}))\simeq\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},E(1))
.

Define
\alpha_{W}=i_{W,f}(1)
and
\beta_{W}=i_{W,c}(1)
. Let
\kappa:\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}\otimes E\to\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},E(1))
be the
Kummer map.
Then we have

p_{W,f}(\kappa(u))=\log_{p}u\cdot\alpha_{W}
and
p_{W,c}(\kappa(u))=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(u)\cdot\beta_{W}

for
u\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}\otimes E
(see
[
Ben11
, 1.5.6 and 1.5.10]
for details).
Since

\mathit{H}^{1}_{f,\{p\}}(\mathbb{Q},V)=(\mathcal{O}_{H}[1/\penalty p]^{\times}\otimes E)[\chi]

and
\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathit{F}_{0}\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{\dagger}(V))=\mathit{H}^{1}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}},\chi^{*}(1))
,
one has

\displaystyle\mathit{H}^{1}(V,D)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{D}^{-1}(\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathit{F}_{0}\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{\dagger}(V))/\penalty\mathit{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathit{F}_{0}\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{\dagger}(V)))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ker}\left[\mathit{H}^{1}_{f,\{p\}}(\mathbb{Q},V)\to\mathit{H}^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{p}},\chi^{*}(1))\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ker}\left[(\mathcal{O}_{H}[1/\penalty p]^{\times}\otimes E)[\chi]\to H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}\otimes E\right].

In this case,
\mathit{H}^{1}(V,D)
is an one-dimensional
E
-vector space.

Let
\operatorname{ord}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}
and
\log_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}
be the elements in

\mathit{H}^{1}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}},\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\operatorname{Hom}(G_{K_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}},\mathbb{Q}_{p})

corresponding to
\operatorname{ord}_{p}
and
\log_{p}
under the identification

\operatorname{Hom}(G_{K_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}},\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\operatorname{Hom}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}},\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.
They can be viewed as maps
\operatorname{ord}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}},\log_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}:K_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\times}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}

via the geometrically normalized reciprocity law map
\operatorname{rec}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}:K_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\times}\to G_{K_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}}
.

We fix a non-zero element
u
in the one-dimensional
E
-vector space

\mathit{H}^{1}(V,D)
.
Then we have

\mathscr{L}(V,D)=\frac{\log_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}(u)}{\operatorname{ord}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}(u)}

by the definition.
This shows
\mathscr{L}(V,D)=-\mathscr{L}(\chi)
.
∎

Next we compute the dual construction.
In this case, it is easy to see

\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathit{F}_{1}\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{\dagger}(V^{*}(1)))=\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{\dagger}(V^{*}(1)))=\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},V^{*}(1))=\mathit{H}^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{p}},\chi)\oplus\mathit{H}^{1}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}},\chi),

\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathit{F}_{0}\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{\dagger}(V^{*}(1)))=\mathit{H}^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{p}},\chi)

and

\mathit{H}^{1}(V^{*}(1),D^{\perp})=\mathit{H}^{1}_{f,\{p\}}(\mathbb{Q},V^{*}(1))=\mathit{H}^{1}_{f,\{\mathfrak{p},\overline{\mathfrak{p}}\}}(K,\chi)=\mathit{H}^{1}(K,\chi),

which is an one-dimensional
E
-vector space.
Moreover

\mathit{H}^{1}(W^{*}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}))\simeq\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathit{F}_{1}\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{\dagger}(V^{*}(1)))/\penalty\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathit{F}_{0}\mathit{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{\dagger}(V^{*}(1)))\simeq\mathit{H}^{1}(K_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}},\chi)\simeq\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},E).

Define

\alpha_{W^{*}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}})}=i_{W^{*}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}),f}(1)
and
\beta_{W^{*}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}})}=i_{W^{*}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}),c}(1)

Under the identification

\mathit{H}^{1}(W^{*}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}))\simeq\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathscr{R}_{E})\simeq\mathit{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},E),

one has
\alpha_{W^{*}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}})}=-\operatorname{ord}_{p}
and
\beta_{W^{*}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}})}=\log_{p}
(see
[
Ben11
, 1.5.6 and 1.5.10]
).
Note that our normalization of the reciprocity law map is different from Benois
[
Ben11
,
Ben14
,
BH20
]
.
More precisely, we have
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\mathrm{Fr}_{p})=1
,
where
\mathrm{Fr}_{p}
is the geometric Frobenius. This gives the difference of the sign with Benois’ description.

Fix a non-zero element
\eta\in\mathit{H}^{1}(V^{*}(1),D^{\perp})=\mathit{H}^{1}(K,\chi)
.
Then we can write

\kappa_{D^{\perp}}(\eta)=x\cdot\operatorname{ord}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}+y\cdot\log_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}=(-x)\cdot(-\operatorname{ord}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}})+y\cdot\log_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}

where

By Proposition
5.2

\mathscr{L}(V^{*}(1),D^{\perp})=-\mathscr{L}(V,D)=\mathscr{L}(\chi)

Therefore this gives an alternative proof of Lemma

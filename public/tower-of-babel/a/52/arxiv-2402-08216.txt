An Improved Approximation Algorithm for Metric Triangle Packing
Jingyang Zhao, Mingyu Xiao

Abstract
Given an edge-weighted metric complete graph with n n vertices, the maximum weight metric triangle packing problem is to find a set of n / 3 n/3 vertex-disjoint triangles with the total weight of all triangles in the packing maximized. Several simple methods can lead to a 2/3-approximation ratio. However, this barrier is not easy to break. Chen et al. proposed a randomized approximation algorithm with an expected ratio of ( 0.66768 − ε ) (0.66768-\varepsilon) for any constant ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 . In this paper, we improve the approximation ratio to ( 0.66835 − ε ) (0.66835-\varepsilon) . Furthermore, we can derandomize our algorithm. Keywords: Approximation Algorithms, Metric, Triangle Packing, Cycle Packing



1
Introduction

In a graph with
n
vertices, a
triangle packing
is a set of vertex-disjoint triangles (i.e., a simple cycle on three different vertices).
The triangle packing is called
perfect
if its size is
n/3
(i.e., it can cover all vertices).
Given an unweighted graph, the Maximum Triangle Packing (MTP) problem is to find a triangle packing of maximum cardinality.
In an edge-weighted graph, every edge has a non-negative weight.
There are two natural variants.
If the graph is an edge-weighted complete graph, a perfect triangle packing will exist. The Maximum Weight Triangle Packing (MWTP) problem is to find a perfect triangle packing such that the total weight of all triangles is maximized. Furthermore, if the graph is an edge-weighted metric complete graph (i.e., the weight of edges satisfies the symmetric and triangle inequality properties), the problem is called the Maximum Weight Metric Triangle Packing (MWMTP) problem.

In this paper, we mainly study approximation algorithms of MWMTP.

1.2
Our results

In this paper, we propose a deterministic
(0.66835-\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm, which improves the deterministic
2/3
-approximation algorithm
[
17
]
and the randomized
(0.66768-\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm
[
4
]
. Our algorithm is based on the randomized algorithm in
[
4
]
, but the framework of our analysis is completely different. The main differences are shown as follows.

Firstly, the previous algorithm considers so-called balanced/unbalanced triangles in an optimal solution of MWMTP, while in our algorithm we use novel definitions to make tighter analysis and do not need to separate them. Secondly, we also consider orientations of cycles in a cycle packing, which can simplify the structure significantly and enable us to design better algorithms. For example, the previous decomposition algorithm could only lead to a probability of
1/27
(for one specific event) while our new decomposition algorithm enables us to obtain a probability of at least
97/1215
(see details in Section
4.3
). Lastly, our algorithm is deterministic, which is obtained by derandomizing our randomized algorithm: we first propose a new randomized algorithm such that if one specific matching is given our algorithm is deterministic, and then we further derandomize our algorithm by finding a desirable deterministic matching by the method of conditional exceptions (see details in Section
5
).

2
Preliminaries

We use
G=(V,E)
to denote a complete graph with
n
vertices, where
n\bmod 3=0
. There is a non-negative weight function
w:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
on the edges in
E
.
The weight function
w
is a semi-metric function, i.e., it satisfies the symmetric and triangle inequality properties.
For any weight function
w:X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we extend it to subsets of
X
, i.e., we define
w(Y)=\sum_{x\in Y}w(x)
for
Y\subseteq X
.

A triangle
t=xyz
is a simple cycle on three different vertices
\{x,y,z\}
. It contains exactly three edges
\{xy,xz,yz\}
. We may also use
\{a_{t},b_{t},c_{t}\}
to denote them such that
w(a_{t})\leq w(b_{t})\leq w(c_{t})
.

Two subgraphs or sets of edges are
vertex-disjoint
if they do not share a common vertex. As mentioned, a
perfect triangle packing
in graph
G
is a set of vertex-disjoint
n/3
triangles, and all vertices are covered.
It can be seen as the edges of
n/3
vertex-disjoint triangles.
In the following, we will always consider a triangle packing as a perfect triangle packing. We will use
\mathcal{B}^{*}
to denote the maximum weight triangle packing.

A
matching
is a set of vertex-disjoint edges. In this paper, we often consider a matching of size
n/3
and use
\mathcal{M}^{*}
to denote the maximum weight matching, which can be found in
O(n^{3})
time
[
10
,
21
]
. A
cycle packing
is a set of vertex-disjoint simple cycles, the length of each cycle is at least 3, and all vertices of the graph are covered. We use
\mathcal{C}^{*}
to denote the maximum weight cycle packing, which can be found in
O(n^{3})
time
[
14
]
. We can get
w(\mathcal{C}^{*})\geq w(\mathcal{B}^{*})
since a triangle packing is also a cycle packing. Given a set of edges
\mathcal{X}
and a vertex
x
, we may simply use
x\in\mathcal{X}
to denote that there is an edge in
\mathcal{X}
that contains the vertex
x
.

Let
\varepsilon
be a constant such that
0<\varepsilon\leq 2/5
. A cycle
C
is
short
if
|C|\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}
; otherwise, the cycle is
long
. For each long cycle
C\in\mathcal{C}^{*}
, it is easy to see that we can delete at most
\lceil\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|C|\rceil<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|C|+1<\varepsilon|C|
edges to get a set of paths such that the length of each path,
|P|
, satisfies that
3\leq|P|\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}
.
Moreover, the total weight of these paths is at least
(1-\varepsilon)w(C)
.
By connecting the endpoints of each path, we can get a set of short cycles. Hence, we can get a short cycle packing (i.e., a cycle packing containing only short cycles) in polynomial time, denoted by
\mathcal{C}
, such that
w(\mathcal{C})\geq(1-\varepsilon)w(\mathcal{C}^{*})
. Note that the constant
\varepsilon
needs to be at most
2/5
. Otherwise, even the cycle of length 5 is a long cycle, and then the short cycle packing may contain a cycle of length less than 3.

We first define several kinds of triangles in
\mathcal{B}^{*}
.
Fix the short cycle packing
\mathcal{C}
. An edge is an
internal edge
if both of its endpoints fall on the same cycle in
\mathcal{C}
; otherwise, it is an
external edge
. Consider a triangle
t
in the optimal triangle packing
\mathcal{B}^{*}
.
There are three cases:

•

t
is an
internal
triangle if it contains three internal edges;

•

t
is a
partial-external
triangle if it contains one internal edge and two external edges;

•

t
is an
external
triangle if it contains three external edges.

See Figure
1
for an illustration.

x\in t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}

x\in t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}

.

Next, we further define type-1 and type-2 triangles. Fix a constant
\tau
with
0\leq\tau\leq 1/3
. For each triangle
t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}
, it is a
type-1
triangle if it holds that
w(e)>\frac{1}{2}(1-\tau)w(t)
for every edge
e\in E_{t}
; otherwise, it is a
type-2
triangle (note that our definition is different from the definition in
[
4
]
).
For this definition, we only consider partial-external triangles and external triangles since internal triangles have no out-edges. Intuitively, the out-edges of type-1 triangles are all heavy edges.

In the following, we split
\mathcal{B}^{*}
into five disjoint sets
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{1}
,
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{2}
,
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{3}
,
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{4}
, and
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{5}
such that

\mathcal{B}^{*}_{1}
:

the set of all internal triangles in
\mathcal{B}^{*}
;

\mathcal{B}^{*}_{2}
:

the set of all type-1 partial-external triangles in
\mathcal{B}^{*}
;

\mathcal{B}^{*}_{3}
:

the set of all type-2 partial-external triangles in
\mathcal{B}^{*}
;

\mathcal{B}^{*}_{4}
:

the set of all type-1 external triangles in
\mathcal{B}^{*}
;

\mathcal{B}^{*}_{5}
:

the set of all type-2 external triangles in
\mathcal{B}^{*}
.

Note that
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{1}
is the set of all internal triangles in
\mathcal{B}^{*}
,
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{2}\cup\mathcal{B}^{*}_{3}
is the set of all partial-external triangles in
\mathcal{B}^{*}
, and
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{4}\cup\mathcal{B}^{*}_{5}
is the set of all external triangles in
\mathcal{B}^{*}
. Hence, we have
\mathcal{B}^{*}=\mathcal{B}^{*}_{1}\cup\mathcal{B}^{*}_{2}\cup\mathcal{B}^{*}_{3}\cup\mathcal{B}^{*}_{4}\cup\mathcal{B}^{*}_{5}
.

We also propose two new important definitions, which will be used frequently:

u_{i}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}}w(a_{t})/\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}}w(c_{t})\quad\mbox{and}\quad v_{i}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}}w(b_{t})/\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}}w(c_{t}).

So,
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}}w(a_{t})=\frac{u_{i}}{u_{i}+v_{i}+1}w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i})
,
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}}w(b_{t})=\frac{v_{i}}{u_{i}+v_{i}+1}w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i})
, and
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}}w(c_{t})=\frac{1}{u_{i}+v_{i}+1}w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i})
.

2.1
Paper Organization

In the remaining parts of the paper, we will describe the approximation algorithm. It will compute three triangle packings:
\mathcal{T}_{1}
,
T_{2}
, and
\mathcal{T}_{3}
. The computation of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, which can be seen in Section
3
, is based on the short cycle packing
\mathcal{C}
via a dynamic method. In Section
4
, we will use the maximum weight matching
\mathcal{M}^{*}
to compute
\mathcal{T}_{2}
. In Section
5
, we will first compute a randomized matching on the short cycle packing
\mathcal{C}
, and then use it to compute
\mathcal{T}_{3}
. The algorithm of
\mathcal{T}_{3}
is randomized but we show that it can be derandomized efficiently by the method of conditional expectations. The approximation algorithm will return the best one. Hence, the approximation ratio is

\frac{\max\{w(\mathcal{T}_{1}),\ w(T_{2}),\ w(\mathcal{T}_{3})\}}{w(\mathcal{B}^{*})}.

The trade-off among these three triangle packings will be shown in Section
6
.

3
The First Triangle Packing

3.1
The algorithm

A
partial triangle packing
, denoted by
\mathcal{P}
, is a set of triangle-components and edge-components such that the total number of components is at most
n/3
and the edges of each component are all internal edges.
The augmented weight of
\mathcal{P}
is defined as
\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{P}}w(t)+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{P}}2w(e)
.
Suppose there are
p_{1}
triangle-components and
p_{2}
edge-components in
\mathcal{P}
, we have
n-3p_{1}-2p_{2}\geq p_{2}
unused vertices (i.e., vertices not in
\mathcal{P}
).
Hence there are at least as many unused vertices as edge-components.
Given a partial triangle packing
\mathcal{P}
, one can construct a triangle packing
\mathcal{T}
as follows:

Step 1.
Pick all triangle-components into the packing;

Step 2.
Arbitrarily assign an unused vertex for each edge-component and complete them into a triangle;

Step 3.
Arbitrarily construct a set of triangles if there are still unused vertices.

Note that the weight of triangles in Step 1 is
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{P}}w(t)
and the weight of triangles in Step 2 is at least
\sum_{e\in\mathcal{P}}2w(e)
by the triangle inequality. Hence, we have that
w(\mathcal{T})\geq\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{P})
.

Since the length of each cycle in
\mathcal{C}
is bounded by a constant, the maximum augmented weight partial triangle packing, denoted by
\mathcal{P}^{*}
, can be found in polynomial time via a dynamic programming method
[
4
]
. Hence, we can get the following lemma.

Lemma 1

(
[
4
]
)
.

There is a polynomial-time algorithm that can compute a triangle packing
\mathcal{T}_{1}
such that
w(\mathcal{T}_{1})\geq\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{P}^{*})
.

3.2
The analysis

Let
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
be the number of internal triangles and partial-external triangles, respectively.
After deleting all external edges of all triangles in
\mathcal{B}^{*}
, we can get a set of components, denoted by
\mathcal{P}
, where there are exactly
p_{1}
triangle-components and
p_{2}
edge-components. We can get
p_{1}+p_{2}\leq n/3
since a triangle-component corresponds to an internal triangle and an edge-component corresponds to a partial-external triangle. Hence,
\mathcal{P}
is a partial triangle packing. We can get that

w(\mathcal{T}_{1})\geq\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{P}^{*})\geq\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{P}}w(t)+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{P}}2w(e).

It is easy to see that
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{P}}w(t)=w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{1})
since the triangle-components in
\mathcal{P}
contains all internal triangles and
\mathcal{B}_{1}
is the set of all internal triangles. However, for edge-components, it contains only one (internal) edge of each partial-external triangle. In the worst case, the contained edge in each partial-external triangle
t
is the least weighted edge
a_{t}
. So, we have
\sum_{e\in\mathcal{P}}2w(e)\geq\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{2}\cup\mathcal{B}^{*}_{3}}2w(a_{t})
.
Then, we can get that

w(\mathcal{T}_{1})\geq w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{1})+\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{2}\cup\mathcal{B}^{*}_{3}}2w(a_{t}).

Recall that
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}}w(a_{t})=\frac{u_{i}}{u_{i}+v_{i}+1}w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i})
. By Lemma
1
, we can get the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

There is a polynomial-time algorithm that can compute a triangle packing
\mathcal{T}_{1}
such that

w(\mathcal{T}_{1})\geq w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{1})+\sum_{i=2}^{3}\frac{2u_{i}}{u_{i}+v_{i}+1}w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}).

4
The Second Triangle Packing

4.1
The algorithm

The second triangle packing
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is generated using the maximum weight matching
\mathcal{M}^{*}
. The algorithm is simple and contains two following steps.

Step 1.
Find the maximum weight matching
\mathcal{M}^{*}
(of size
n/3
) in
O(n^{3})
time
[
10
,
21
]
;

Step 2.
Arbitrarily assign an unused vertex for each edge of
\mathcal{M}^{*}
and complete them into a triangle.

The matching
\mathcal{M}^{*}
uses
2n/3
vertices, and hence after Step 1, there are still
n/3
unused vertices. Then, we can get a triangle packing (i.e.,
\mathcal{T}_{2}
) after Step 2. Moreover, it is easy to see that the running time is
O(n^{3})
, dominated by computing
\mathcal{M}^{*}
.

Note that we can also construct a bipartite graph
B=(\mathcal{X},V\setminus V(\mathcal{X}))
such that there are
n/3
super-vertices on the left corresponding to the
n/3
edges in
\mathcal{X}
and
n/3
super-vertices on the right corresponding to the unused
n/3
vertices in
V\setminus V(\mathcal{X})
, where
V(\mathcal{X})
denotes the set of vertices used by
\mathcal{X}
. For each left super-vertex
xy\in\mathcal{X}
and each right super-vertex
z\in V\setminus V(\mathcal{X})
, the weight of the edge between them is defined as
w(xz)+w(yz)
. We could obtain a better triangle packing that contains the matching
\mathcal{M}^{*}
by finding a maximum weight matching in graph
B
, as did in
[
4
]
. But, it cannot improve the approximation ratio in our analysis. So, we only use the simpler algorithm.

4.2
The analysis

Consider a triangle
t=xyz\in\mathcal{T}_{2}
, and assume w.l.o.g. that
xy\in\mathcal{M}^{*}
. By the triangle inequality, we can get that
w(t)=w(xy)+w(xz)+w(yz)\geq 2w(xy)
. Hence, it is easy to get
w(\mathcal{T}_{2})\geq 2w(\mathcal{M}^{*})
.

Take the most weighted edge
c_{t}
for each triangle
t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}
. Then, the set of edges forms a matching of size
n/3
. Since
\mathcal{M}^{*}
is the maximum weight matching of size
n/3
, we have
w(\mathcal{M}^{*})\geq\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}}w(c_{t})
. Recall that
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}}w(c_{t})=\frac{1}{u_{i}+v_{i}+1}w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i})
and
w(\mathcal{T}_{2})\geq 2w(\mathcal{M}^{*})
. We can get the following lemma.

Lemma 3
.

There is a polynomial-time algorithm that can compute a triangle packing
\mathcal{T}_{2}
such that

w(\mathcal{T}_{2})\geq\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}}2w(c_{t})=\sum_{i=1}^{5}\frac{2}{u_{i}+v_{i}+1}w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}).

It is worth noting that that the algorithm of
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is a simple
2/3
-approximation algorithm. Since
u_{i}\leq v_{i}\leq 1
, we can get
w(\mathcal{T}_{2})\geq\frac{2}{3}w(\mathcal{B}^{*})
by Lemma
3
.
Next, we will construct a randomized matching
\mathcal{Z}
of size
n/3
. A good advantage of
\mathcal{Z}
is that if type-1 triangles in
\mathcal{B}^{*}
has a nonzero weight, the expected weight of
\mathcal{Z}
will have a strictly larger weight than
\frac{1}{3}w(\mathcal{B}^{*})
. In this case,
\mathcal{T}_{2}
will have a strictly larger weight than
\frac{2}{3}w(\mathcal{B}^{*})
since
\mathcal{M}^{*}
is the maximum weight matching and we have
w(\mathcal{T}_{2})\geq 2w(\mathcal{M}^{*})\geq 2\cdot\mathbb{E}[w(\mathcal{Z})]
.

4.3
The randomized matching algorithm

Our randomized matching algorithm is a refined version of the algorithm presented in
[
4
]
, which mainly contains four following steps.

Step 1.
For each triangle
t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}
, select an edge
e_{t}
uniformly at random. The set of selected edges is denoted by
\mathcal{X}
.

Step 2.
For each type-1 triangle
t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}
, if there is an out-edge that does not share a common vertex with any edge of
\mathcal{X}
, select it. The set of selected edges is denoted by
\mathcal{Y}
.

Step 3.
Initialize
\mathcal{Z}=\emptyset
. Consider the out-edges in
\mathcal{Y}
, which contains a set of edge-components, path-components, and cycle-components. We consider three following cases.

•

For each edge-component, select the edge into
\mathcal{Z}
.

•

For each path-component or even cycle-component (i.e., the number of vertices in it is even), partition it into two matchings, select one matching uniformly at random, and then select the edges in the chosen matching into
\mathcal{Z}
.

•

For each odd cycle-component (i.e., the number of vertices in it is odd), select one edge uniformly at random, delete it, partition it into two matchings, select one matching uniformly at random, and then select the edges in the chosen matching into
\mathcal{Z}
.

Note that
\mathcal{Z}
is a matching containing only out-edges. Moreover, the out-edges are vertex-disjoint with the edges in
\mathcal{X}
.

Step 4.
Consider each triangle
t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}
. If
\mathcal{Z}
contains no out-edges of
t
, select the edge
e_{t}
in
\mathcal{X}
into
\mathcal{Z}
.

Roughly speaking, the main idea of the randomized matching algorithm in
[
4
]
is to decompose the out-edges in
\mathcal{Y}
into three matchings and put one into
\mathcal{Z}
, while we can put more out-edges into
\mathcal{Z}
in Step 3 in our algorithm. Although our algorithm becomes more complicated, we can quickly analyze the expected weight of the randomized matching
\mathcal{Z}
because the cycles in
\mathcal{C}
are all oriented.

Lemma 4
.

The edge set
\mathcal{Z}
is a matching of size
n/3
.

Proof.

First, it is easy to see that the edge set
\mathcal{X}
in Step 1 is a matching of size
n/3
. Second, the edge set
\mathcal{Y}
in Step 2 is a set of out-edges, which can be seen as a subgraph of the cycle packing
\mathcal{C}
. Hence, it contains a set of edge-components, path-components, and cycle-components, and Step 3 is feasible. Then, since the out-edges in
\mathcal{Y}
do not share a common vertex with any edge in
\mathcal{X}
, and
\mathcal{Z}
after Step 3 is a subset of vertex-disjoint edges in
\mathcal{Y}
, hence
\mathcal{Z}
is also a matching. Moreover, it is easy to see that for each triangle
t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}
the edge set
\mathcal{Y}
contains at most one out-edge of
t
. Hence, according to Step 4, for each triangle
t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}
,
\mathcal{Z}
either contains exactly one out-edge of
t
or it will contain the edge
e_{t}
. Hence, there are exactly
n/3
edges in
\mathcal{Z}
.
∎

Next, we analyze the expected weight of the randomized matching
\mathcal{Z}
.

Lemma 5
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[w(\mathcal{Z})]>

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{3}w(\mathcal{B}^{*})+\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{2},e\in E_{t}}\left(\frac{1-3\tau}{6}w(t)\cdot\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}]\right)+\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{4}}\left(\frac{1-3\tau}{6}w(t)\cdot\sum_{e\in E_{t}}\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}]\right).

Proof.

Recall that for each triangle

. We will consider the following three cases.

Case 1: Non type-1 triangles.

For each triangle

(i.e., the set of non type-1 triangles), it does not have out-edges, and hence it is clear that

. Recall that

was selected uniformly at random. Hence, the expected contributed weight is
\mathbb{E}[w(e_{t})]=\frac{1}{3}w(t)
. The total expected contributed weight of all non type-1 triangles is

\frac{1}{3}w(\mathcal{B}^{*}\setminus(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{2}\cup\mathcal{B}^{*}_{4}))=\frac{1}{3}w(\mathcal{B}^{*})-\frac{1}{3}w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{2})-\frac{1}{3}w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{4}).

Case 2: Type-1 partial-external triangles.

For each type-1 partial-external triangle

for every edge

. Moreover, since

, we know that the expected contributed weight satisfies

\displaystyle w(e)\cdot\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}]+\mathbb{E}[w(e_{t})]\cdot(1-\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}])

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{2}(1-\tau)w(t)\cdot\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}]+\frac{1}{3}w(t)\cdot(1-\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}])

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}w(t)+\frac{1-3\tau}{6}w(t)\cdot\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}].

The total expected contributed weight of all type-1 partial-external triangles is at least

\frac{1}{3}w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{2})+\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{2},e\in E_{t}}\left(\frac{1-3\tau}{6}w(t)\cdot\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}]\right).

Case 3: Type-1 external triangles.

For each type-1 external triangle

, and hence the expected contributed weight is
\sum_{e\in E_{t}}(w(e)\cdot\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}])+\mathbb{E}[w(e_{t})]\cdot(1-\sum_{e\in E_{t}}\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}])

By a similar argument with Case 2, the expected contributed weight satisfies

\sum_{e\in E_{t}}(w(e)\cdot\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}])+\mathbb{E}[w(e_{t})]\cdot\left(1-\sum_{e\in E_{t}}\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}]\right)>\frac{1}{3}w(t)+\frac{1-3\tau}{6}w(t)\cdot\sum_{e\in E_{t}}\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}].

The total expected contributed weight of all type-1 external triangles is at least

\frac{1}{3}w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{4})+\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{4}}\left(\frac{1-3\tau}{6}w(t)\cdot\sum_{e\in E_{t}}\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}]\right).

Therefore, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[w(\mathcal{Z})]>

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{3}w(\mathcal{B}^{*})+\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{2},e\in E_{t}}\left(\frac{1-3\tau}{6}w(t)\cdot\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}]\right)+\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{4}}\left(\frac{1-3\tau}{6}w(t)\cdot\sum_{e\in E_{t}}\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{Z}]\right).

∎

5
The Third Triangle Packing

Recall that we split triangles into type-1 and type-2 triangles. Especially, the randomized matching in Section
4
is based on type-1 triangles. However, there may not exist type-1 triangles in the worst case. Then, it comes to the third triangle packing
\mathcal{T}_{3}
, which uses the property of type-2 triangles.
The ideas of
\mathcal{T}_{3}
are mainly from the randomized algorithm in
[
4
]
. We will first propose a new randomized algorithm and then derandomize it.

5.1
The randomized algorithm

Our new randomized algorithm of
\mathcal{T}_{3}
is based on two edge-disjoint matchings
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, where
\mathcal{X}
is a randomized matching of size
n/3
on the short cycle packing and
\mathcal{Y}
is a matching of size at most
n/3
determined by
\mathcal{X}
. The algorithm of
\mathcal{X}
in our algorithm is obtained directly from
[
4
]
but the algorithm of
\mathcal{Y}
is different: the triangle packing algorithm in
[
4
]
is randomized even if
\mathcal{X}
is determined, while our algorithm of
\mathcal{T}_{3}
would be deterministic. So, our new randomized algorithm can be derandomized simply by finding a desirable deterministic matching
\mathcal{X}
.

The algorithm of
\mathcal{X}
in
[
4
]
mainly contains four steps.

Step 1.
Initialize
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{R}=\emptyset
.

Step 2.
For each even cycle
C\in\mathcal{C}
, partition it into two matchings, select one matching uniformly at random, and select the edges in the chosen matching into
\mathcal{L}
.

Step 3.
For each odd cycle
C\in\mathcal{C}
, select one edge uniformly at random, delete it, partition the rest edges into two matchings, select one matching uniformly at random, and select the edges in the chosen matching into
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{R}
in the following way:
select one edge uniformly at random, put the edge into
\mathcal{R}
, and put the rest edges into
\mathcal{L}
.

Step 4.
Select
\frac{2}{3}|\mathcal{L}|
edges of
\mathcal{L}
uniformly at random into
\mathcal{X}
, and put all edges of
\mathcal{R}
into
\mathcal{X}
.

The running time is dominated by computing the short cycle packing
\mathcal{C}
, which is polynomial. The randomized matching
\mathcal{X}
has some good properties. We have the following lemma.

Lemma 9

(
[
4
]
)
.

The size of
\mathcal{X}
is
n/3
. For every edge
e\in C\in\mathcal{C}
and every vertex
v\notin C
, we have
\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{X}]=\frac{1}{3}
and
\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{X}\wedge v\notin\mathcal{X}]\geq\frac{1}{9}
.

We remark that if
\mathcal{C}
is a set of triangles the matching
\mathcal{X}
is exactly obtained by taking an edge of each triangle uniformly at random. In this case, for every edge
e\in C\in\mathcal{C}
and every vertex
v\notin C
, we have
\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{X}]=\frac{1}{3}
and
\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{X}\wedge v\notin\mathcal{X}]=\frac{1}{9}
. So, the result in Lemma
9
is essentially tight.

Next, we introduce the algorithm of the third triangle packing
\mathcal{T}_{3}
, where we will first compute the matching
\mathcal{Y}
and then obtain
\mathcal{T}_{3}
using
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
. We first give some definitions.

For some edge
e=xy\in C\in\mathcal{C}
and vertex
z\notin C
, we call
(x,y;z)
a

. Recall that
\tau
is a fixed constant defined before. A triplet
(x,y;z)
is
good
if it satisfies that
w(xy)\leq(1-\tau)(w(xz)+w(yz))
.

We construct a multi-graph
H
. Initially, graph
H
contains
n
vertices only and no edges. For each good triplet
(x,y;z)
, we add two edges
xz
and
yz
with an augmented weight
\widetilde{w}(xz)=\widetilde{w}(yz)=w(xz)+w(yz)
, and each of them has a label corresponding to the good triplet
(x,y;z)
. Hence, graph
H
contains only external edges. Two edges of
H
are called
conflicting
if their corresponding triplets share a common vertex.

The algorithm of
\mathcal{T}_{3}
is shown as follows.

Step 1.
Find a maximum augmented weight matching
\mathcal{Y}^{*}
in graph
H
in
O(n^{3})
time
[
10
,
21
]
. Note that there is no constraint on its size, and hence we have
0\leq|\mathcal{Y}^{*}|\leq n/2
.

Step 2.
Let
\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}}
denote the set of edges
\{zx\in\mathcal{Y}^{*}\mid xy\in\mathcal{X},z\notin\mathcal{X}\}
. We claim that for each edge in
\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}}
there is at most one different edge in
\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}}
that are conflicting with it (see Lemma
10
).

Step 3.
For each pair of conflicting edges of
\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}}
, delete the edge with the less augmented weight (if their augmented weights are the same we may simply delete one of them arbitrarily). The remained matching is denoted by
\mathcal{Y}
.

Step 4.
Note that
\mathcal{X}\cup\mathcal{Y}
is a set of components of size
n/3
, which contains
|\mathcal{Y}|
path-components (each contains 3 vertices) and
|\mathcal{X}|-|\mathcal{Y}|
edge-components. For each path-component
xyz
, complete it into a triangle. For each edge-component, arbitrarily assign an unused vertex, and complete it into a triangle.

We need to prove the claim in Step 2.

Lemma 10
.

For any edge
zx\in\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}}
, there is at most one different edge in
\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}}
that are conflicting with it.

Proof.

Consider an edge
zx\in\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}}
with its corresponding triplet
(x,y;z)
. Assume that

with its corresponding triplet
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime};z^{\prime})
is conflicting with

, i.e.,

. Since
\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}}
is a matching, we get that

and
z^{\prime}x^{\prime}
are vertex-disjoint. Moreover, we have

and

by the definition of
\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}}
. So, we have

Since
\mathcal{X}
is a matching, we must have

and
x=y^{\prime}
. So,
z^{\prime}x^{\prime}=z^{\prime}y
. Since
\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}}
is a matching, there is at most one such edge.
∎

For the triangles corresponding to the edge-components, the weight is at least

by the triangle inequality.
Hence, we have

w(\mathcal{T}_{3})\geq\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y})+w(\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{Y}})+2w(\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{Y}})=\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y})-w(\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{Y}})+2w(\mathcal{X})\geq\tau\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y})+2w(\mathcal{X}).

Recall that for each pair of conflicting edges we delete the edge with the less augmented weight. Hence, we have
\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y})\geq\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}})

w(\mathcal{T}_{3})\geq\frac{\tau}{2}\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}})+2w(\mathcal{X}).

Then, we consider
\mathbb{E}[w(\mathcal{X})]
and
\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}})]
.
By Lemma

, we have

\mathbb{E}[w(\mathcal{X})]=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{C}}w(e)\cdot\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{X}]=\frac{1}{3}w(\mathcal{C})

and

\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}})]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}zx\in\mathcal{Y}^{*}:\\
(x,y;z)\end{subarray}}\widetilde{w}(zx)\cdot\mbox{Pr}[xy\in\mathcal{X}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{X}]\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}zx\in\mathcal{Y}^{*}:\\
(x,y;z)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{9}\widetilde{w}(zx)=\frac{1}{9}\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*}).

Hence,
\mathbb{E}[w(\mathcal{T}_{3})]\geq\frac{\tau}{18}\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*})+\frac{2}{3}w(\mathcal{C})
.
∎

5.3
The derandomization

The third triangle packing
\mathcal{T}_{3}
is randomized due to the randomized matching
\mathcal{X}
on
\mathcal{C}
. Let
f(\mathcal{X})=\frac{\tau}{2}\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}})+2w(\mathcal{X})
. By the proof of Lemma
11
, we have
w(\mathcal{T}_{3})\geq f(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathbb{E}[f(\mathcal{X})]\geq\frac{\tau}{18}\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*})+\frac{2}{3}w(\mathcal{C})
. Therefore, if we can derandomize the matching
\mathcal{X}
such that
f(\mathcal{X})\geq\frac{\tau}{18}\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*})+\frac{2}{3}w(\mathcal{C})
, then we will obtain a deterministic algorithm of
\mathcal{T}_{3}
.

Recall that the randomized matching algorithm mainly contains two phases. In the first phase, the algorithm obtains two edge sets
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{R}
by making random decisions on each cycle in
\mathcal{C}
. After that, the algorithm obtains the matching
\mathcal{X}
by choosing
\frac{2}{3}|\mathcal{L}|
edges in
\mathcal{L}
uniformly at random and all edges in
\mathcal{R}
, which can be seen as making random decisions on each edge in
\mathcal{L}
.

Using the method of conditional expectations, these two phases can be derandomized efficiently. The idea is to consider random decisions in the algorithm sequentially. For each random decision, we can compute the expected weight of
f(\mathcal{X})
conditioned on each possible outcome of the random decision. Since at least one of these outcomes has an expected weight of at least
\mathbb{E}[f(\mathcal{X})]
, we fix this outcome and continue to the next random decision. By repeating this procedure, we can get a deterministic solution with a weight of at least
\mathbb{E}[f(\mathcal{X})]
.

We will show that there is a polynomial number of outcomes for each random decision in the algorithm. Moreover, if we can compute the conditional expected weight of
f(\mathcal{X})
in polynomial time, then the derandomization will take polynomial time. Since
f(\mathcal{X})=\frac{\tau}{2}\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}})+2w(\mathcal{X})
, by the linearity, it is sufficient to show that we can compute the conditional expected weight of
\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}})
and
w(\mathcal{X})
in polynomial time, respectively.

Next, we consider the derandomization of these two phases, respectively.

Lemma 14
.

The first phase of the algorithm can be derandomized in polynomial time.

Proof.

We consider random decisions on each cycle in
\mathcal{C}
sequentially.
Let
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{|\mathcal{C}|}\}
. For the decision on
C_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
, it contains
2
outcomes if
C_{i}
is an even cycle, and
|C_{i}|\cdot(|C_{i}|-1)=O_{\varepsilon}(1)
outcomes otherwise.
Next, we show that we can use polynomial time to compute the conditional expected weight of
w(\mathcal{X})
and
\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}})
. For the sake of presentation, for some variable
\mathcal{Z}
, we simply use
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]
to denote the expected weight of
\mathcal{Z}
conditioned on the
s
outcomes of the first
s
decisions on
C_{1},\dots,C_{s}
.

The conditional expected weight of
w(\mathcal{X})
.
Since
\mathcal{X}
is a matching on
\mathcal{C}
, we have

\mathbb{E}[w(\mathcal{X})\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]=\sum_{C_{i}\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{e\in C_{i}}\left(w(e)\cdot\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{X}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]\right).

Assume that
e\in C_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
.
Since the algorithm selects
\frac{2}{3}|\mathcal{L}|
edges in
\mathcal{L}
uniformly at random and all edges in
\mathcal{R}
into
\mathcal{X}
, we can get

\displaystyle\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{X}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]

\displaystyle\ =\frac{2}{3}\cdot\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{L}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]+1\cdot\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]+0\cdot\mbox{Pr}[e\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge e\notin\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}].

Hence, it is sufficient to compute the conditional probabilities of events
e\in\mathcal{L}
,
e\in\mathcal{R}
, and
e\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge e\notin\mathcal{R}
. If
i\leq s
, we can determine
\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{L}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]
,
\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]
, and
\mbox{Pr}[e\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge e\notin\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]
based on the outcome of the decision on
C_{i}
. Otherwise, let
k=|C_{i}|
, and by the algorithm, we can compute them as follows:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{L}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]=\frac{1}{2},\quad k\bmod 2=0,\\
&\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]=0,\quad k\bmod 2=0,\\
&\mbox{Pr}[e\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge e\notin\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]=\frac{1}{2},\quad k\bmod 2=0,\\
&\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{L}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]=\frac{k-3}{2k},\quad k\bmod 2=1,\\
&\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]=\frac{1}{k},\quad k\bmod 2=1,\\
&\mbox{Pr}[e\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge e\notin\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]=\frac{k+1}{2k},\quad k\bmod 2=1.\end{aligned}\right.

Hence, the conditional expected weight of
w(\mathcal{X})
can be computed in polynomial time.

The conditional expected weight of
\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}})
.
Since
\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}^{*}
, we have

\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}})\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}zx\in\mathcal{Y}^{*}:\\
(x,y;z)\end{subarray}}\left((w(zx)+w(zy))\cdot\mbox{Pr}[xy\in\mathcal{X}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{X}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]\right).

Assume that
xy\in C_{i}
and
z\in C_{j}
, where
i\neq j
by the definition of the good triplet
(x,y;z)
. There are nine events to consider based on whether the two disjoint sets
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{R}
contain
xy
or
z
. Since the algorithm selects
\frac{2}{3}|\mathcal{L}|
edges in
\mathcal{L}
uniformly at random and all edges in
\mathcal{R}
into
\mathcal{X}
, we can get

\displaystyle\mbox{Pr}[xy\in\mathcal{X}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{X}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]

\displaystyle=\ 0\cdot\mbox{Pr}[xy\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge xy\notin\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]+0\cdot\mbox{Pr}[z\in\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]

\displaystyle\ +\frac{\binom{|\mathcal{L}|-2}{\frac{2}{3}|\mathcal{L}|-1}}{\binom{|\mathcal{L}|}{\frac{2}{3}|\mathcal{L}|}}\cdot\mbox{Pr}[xy\in\mathcal{L}\wedge z\in\mathcal{L}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]+\frac{2}{3}\cdot\mbox{Pr}[xy\in\mathcal{L}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{3}\cdot\mbox{Pr}[xy\in\mathcal{R}\wedge z\in\mathcal{L}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]+1\cdot\mbox{Pr}[xy\in\mathcal{R}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}],

where

is a number related to the numbers of odd cycles in
\mathcal{C}
, which can be computed as follows.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{o}
and
\mathcal{C}_{e}
be the set of odd cycles and even cycles in

, respectively. By the algorithm, we can get
|\mathcal{L}|=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{o}}(\frac{1}{2}(|C|-1)-1)+\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{e}}\frac{1}{2}|C|=\frac{1}{2}(n-3|\mathcal{C}_{o}|)
. The event on
xy
is independent with the event on

since in the first phase the algorithm makes random decisions on each cycle independently.
Therefore, it is sufficient to compute the probabilities of events
xy\in\mathcal{L}
,
xy\in\mathcal{R}
,
xy\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge xy\notin R
,
z\in\mathcal{L}
,
z\in\mathcal{R}
, and
z\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge z\notin R
conditioned on
C_{1},\dots,C_{s}
. Using a similar argument as in the previous case, we can compute the conditional probabilities of events
xy\in\mathcal{L}
,
xy\in\mathcal{R}
, and
xy\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge xy\notin R
in polynomial time.
Next, we compute the conditional probabilities of events
z\in\mathcal{L}
,
z\in\mathcal{R}
, and
z\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge z\notin R
. Similarly, if
j\leq s
, we can determine
\mbox{Pr}[z\in\mathcal{L}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]
,
\mbox{Pr}[z\in\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]
, and
\mbox{Pr}[z\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]
based on the outcome of the decision on
C_{j}
. Otherwise, let
k=|C_{j}|
, and by the algorithm, we can compute them as follows:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mbox{Pr}[z\in\mathcal{L}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]=1,\quad k\bmod 2=0,\\
&\mbox{Pr}[z\in\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]=0,\quad k\bmod 2=0,\\
&\mbox{Pr}[z\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]=0,\quad k\bmod 2=0,\\
&\mbox{Pr}[z\in\mathcal{L}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]=\frac{k-3}{k},\quad k\bmod 2=1,\\
&\mbox{Pr}[z\in\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]=\frac{2}{k},\quad k\bmod 2=1,\\
&\mbox{Pr}[z\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{R}\mid C_{1},\dots,C_{s}]=\frac{1}{k},\quad k\bmod 2=1.\end{aligned}\right.

Therefore, the conditional expected weight of

can also be computed in polynomial time.

Lemma 15
.

The second phase of the algorithm can be derandomized in polynomial time.

Proof.

Currently, we have determined the edges in
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{R}

\frac{2}{3}|\mathcal{L}|-b_{1}-\dots-b_{s}

\mathcal{X}

The conditional expected weight of

.
We have

\mathbb{E}[w(\mathcal{X})\mid b_{1},\dots,b_{s}]=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{L}}\left(w(e)\cdot\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{X}\mid b_{1},\dots,b_{s}]\right)+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{R}}\left(w(e)\cdot\mbox{Pr}[e\in\mathcal{X}\mid b_{1},\dots,b_{s}]\right).

\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}})\mid b_{1},\dots,b_{s}]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}zx\in\mathcal{Y}^{*}:\\

Similar to the derandomization of the first phrase, there are nine cases (note that we use the term ‘cases’ rather than ‘events’ because in the second phrase the edges in

, we have
xy\in\mathcal{X}
. Moreover, since the algorithm selects
\frac{2}{3}|\mathcal{L}|-b_{1}-\dots-b_{s}
edges from
\mathcal{L}\setminus\{e_{1},\dots,e_{s}\}
uniformly at random into
\mathcal{X}
, we can get

\mbox{Pr}[xy\in\mathcal{X}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{X}\mid b_{1},\dots,b_{s}]=\frac{\binom{|\mathcal{L}|-s-1}{\frac{2}{3}|\mathcal{L}|-b_{1}-\dots-b_{s}}}{\binom{|\mathcal{L}|-s}{\frac{2}{3}|\mathcal{L}|-b_{1}-\dots-b_{s}}}.

Case 3.2:
z\in\{e_{1},\dots,e_{s}\}
.
Assume that
z\in e_{j}\in\{e_{1},\dots,e_{s}\}
. Since
xy\in\mathcal{X}
, we know that
\mbox{Pr}[xy\in\mathcal{X}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{X}\mid b_{1},\dots,b_{s}]=1
if and only if
b_{j}=0
.

Case 4:
xy\in\mathcal{R}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{R}
.
Since
xy\in\mathcal{R}
, we get
xy\in\mathcal{X}
. Moreover, since
z\notin\mathcal{L}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{R}
, we get
z\notin\mathcal{X}
. Therefore, we get
\mbox{Pr}[xy\in\mathcal{X}\wedge z\notin\mathcal{X}\mid b_{1},\dots,b_{s}]=1
.

Hence, the conditional expected weight of
\widetilde{w}(\mathcal{Y}^{*}_{\mathcal{X}})
can be computed in polynomial time.
∎

By Lemmas
14
and
15
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 16
.

There is a polynomial-time algorithm that can compute a triangle packing
\mathcal{T}_{3}
such that

w(\mathcal{T}_{3})\geq\frac{2}{3}(1-\varepsilon)w(\mathcal{B}^{*})+\sum_{i\in\{3,5\}}\frac{\tau}{36}w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}).

6
The Trade-off

We are ready to make a trade-off among the three triangle packings:
\mathcal{T}_{1}
,
\mathcal{T}_{2}
, and
\mathcal{T}_{3}
.

We first introduce some new parameters.
We define
\alpha_{i}=w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i})/w(\mathcal{B}^{*})
, which measures the weight proportion of the triangles in
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}
compared to the triangles in
\mathcal{B}^{*}
. Note that

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{4}+\alpha_{5}=1.

(1)

Then, we define
\rho_{i}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}}w(a_{t})/w(\mathcal{B}^{*})
,
\sigma_{i}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}}w(b_{t})/w(\mathcal{B}^{*})
, and
\theta_{i}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}}w(c_{t})/w(\mathcal{B}^{*})
. Recall that
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i}}w(a_{t})=\frac{u_{i}}{u_{i}+v_{i}+1}w(\mathcal{B}^{*}_{i})=\frac{u_{i}}{u_{i}+v_{i}+1}\alpha_{i}w(\mathcal{B}^{*})
.
Hence, we can get that
\rho_{i}=\frac{u_{i}}{u_{i}+v_{i}+1}\alpha_{i}
.
Analogously, we have
\sigma_{i}=\frac{v_{i}}{u_{i}+v_{i}+1}\alpha_{i}
and
\theta_{i}=\frac{1}{u_{i}+v_{i}+1}\alpha_{i}
. Hence, we have

\rho_{i}+\sigma_{i}+\theta_{i}=\alpha_{i},\quad i\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}.

(2)

By Lemmas
2
,
3
,
8
, and
16
, we can get that

w(\mathcal{T}_{1})/w(\mathcal{B}^{*})\geq\alpha_{1}+2\rho_{2}+2\rho_{3},

(3)

w(\mathcal{T}_{2})/w(\mathcal{B}^{*})\geq 2\theta_{1}+2\theta_{2}+2\theta_{3}+2\theta_{4}+2\theta_{5},

(4)

w(\mathcal{T}_{2})/w(\mathcal{B}^{*})\geq\frac{2}{3}+\frac{97(1-3\tau)}{3645}\alpha_{2}+\frac{97(1-3\tau)}{1215}\alpha_{4},

(5)

w(\mathcal{T}_{3})/w(\mathcal{B}^{*})\geq\frac{2}{3}(1-\varepsilon)+\frac{\tau}{36}\alpha_{3}+\frac{\tau}{36}\alpha_{5}.

(6)

For each triangle
t
, we have
w(c_{t})\geq w(b_{t})\geq w(a_{t})
by the definition and
w(a_{t})+w(b_{t})\geq w(c_{t})
by the triangle inequality.
Hence, we also have

\rho_{i}+\sigma_{i}\geq\theta_{i}\geq\sigma_{i}\geq\rho_{i}\geq 0,\quad i\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}.

(7)

If
\tau
is fixed, using (
1
)-(
7
) the approximation ratio
\frac{\max\{w(\mathcal{T}_{1}),\ w(T_{2}),\ w(\mathcal{T}_{3})\}}{w(\mathcal{B}^{*})}
can be obtained via solving a linear program (see Appendix
A
).

Setting
\tau=0.25
, we can get an approximation ratio of at least
(0.66835-\varepsilon)
. Hence, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 17
.

For MWMTP with any constant
\varepsilon>0
, there is a polynomial-time
(0.66835-\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we consider approximation algorithms for the maximum weight metric triangle packing problem. This problem admits an almost-trivial
2/3
-approximation algorithm
[
17
]
.
The first nontrivial result, given by Chen et al.
[
4
]
, is a randomized
(0.66768-\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm.
Based on novel modifications, deep analysis, and conditional expectations, we propose a deterministic
(0.66835-\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm. Whether it admits a simple algorithm with a better-than-
2/3
-approximation ratio is still unknown.

In our analysis of the first triangle packing, the internal edges of the partial-external triangles contained in the short cycle packing are all the least weighted edges. If we take care of this special case, we may also obtain some tiny improvements.

In the future, it would be interesting to study the well-related maximum weight metric 3-path packing problem, where we need to find a set of
n/3
vertex-disjoint
3
-paths with the total weight maximized. This problem admits a similar almost-trivial
3/4
-approximation algorithm (see
[
22
]
). However, it is still unknown to obtain a nontrivial approximation algorithm for this problem.

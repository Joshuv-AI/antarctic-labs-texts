On the regularity of Dirichlet problem for fully non-linear elliptic equations on Hermitian manifolds
Rirong Yuan

Abstract
We derive the solvability and regularity of the Dirichlet problem for fully non-linear elliptic equations possibly with degenerate right-hand side on Hermitian manifolds, through establishing a quantitative version of boundary estimate under a subsolution assumption. In addition, we construct the subsolution when the background manifold is a product of a closed Hermitian manifold with a compact Riemann surface with boundary.

Introduction

Let
(M,J,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian manifold of complex dimension
n\geq 2
with boundary
\partial M
, where

\omega=\sqrt{-1}g_{i\bar{j}}dz^{i}\wedge d\bar{z}^{j}

denotes the Kähler form compatible with the complex structure
J
. Suppose
\chi=\sqrt{-1}\chi_{i\bar{j}}dz^{i}\wedge d\bar{z}^{j}
is a smooth real
(1,1)
-form on
\bar{M}:=M\cup\partial M
. Given a
C^{2}
-function
u
on
\bar{M}
, one can obtain a new real
(1,1)
-form
\mathfrak{g}[u]:=\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u.

This paper is devoted to investigating the following Dirichlet problem

\displaystyle F(\mathfrak{g}[u])=\psi\text{ in }M,\quad u=\varphi\,\text{ on }\,\partial M.

(1.1)

We assume
the operator
F(\mathfrak{g}[u])
takes the form

F(\mathfrak{g}[u])=f(\lambda(\mathfrak{g}[u])),

where
\lambda(\mathfrak{g}[u])=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
denote the eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{g}[u]
with respect to
\omega
, and
f
is a smooth symmetric function defined in an open symmetric convex cone
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing the positive cone

\Gamma_{n}:=\left\{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mbox{ each }\lambda_{i}>0\right\}\subseteq\Gamma

with vertex at the origin and with the boundary
\partial\Gamma\neq\emptyset
.
In addition, we have the following hypotheses on
f
:

f_{i}(\lambda):=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(\lambda)>0\,\text{ in }\,\Gamma,\mbox{ }\forall 1\leqslant i\leqslant n,

(1.2)

\displaystyle f\text{ is concave in }\Gamma,

(1.3)

\text{ For any }\lambda\in\Gamma,\mbox{ }\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}f(t\lambda)>-\infty,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}f\left(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n-1},\lambda_{n}+t\right)=\sup_{\Gamma}f,\mbox{ }\forall\lambda=\left(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}\right)\in\Gamma.

(1.5)

In real variables, equations of this type
were investigated
by Caffarelli-Nirenberg-Spruck
[
5
]
,
which extended the work of Ivochkina
[
28
]

on equations of Monge-Ampère type.

Notice that equation (
1.1
) includes many equations as special cases. For instance, if we set
f(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\lambda_{i}
, it reads
the complex Monge-Ampère equation, which has played a significant role in Yau’s proof of Calabi’s conjecture
[
45
]
. In fact, over the past decades, there were many researches on complex Monge-Ampère equation.
Below we list parts of these works.
For
\chi=0
and
M=\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain, the Dirichlet problem for complex Monge-Ampère equation was solved by Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg-Spruck
[
4
]
in the class of plurisubharmonic functions.
Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg-Spruck’s work was extended by Guan
[
16
]
to general bounded domains, replacing

[
18

solved the Dirichlet problem for complex Monge-Ampère equation on compact Hermitian manifolds with boundary.
For

, the Dirichlet problem for complex Monge-Ampère equation becomes degenerate, which is much more complicated. In
[
7
]
, Chen solved the Dirichlet problem for homogeneous complex Monge-Ampère equation on
M=X\times A
and proved the existence of

-regularity (weak) geodesics in the space of Kähler potentials
[

,

The Dirichlet problem for degenerate complex Monge-Ampère equation was further complemented by Błocki
[

]
, Phong-Sturm
[

]
and Boucksom
[
3
]
.
We refer to

for complement and progress on understanding how geodesics and homogeneous complex Monge-Ampère equation are related to the geometry of

There are also several results on the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) for other equations. For
\chi=0
and
M=\Omega
a bounded domain in

,

For complex inverse

The above results mainly concentrate on special cases of (
1.1
) and the proof more or less relies on specific structures of equations
or underlying manifolds,
which
seems not adaptive
to more general cases. On the other hand, motivated by
increasing interests from complex geometry and analysis, we are interested in investigating the Dirichlet problem (

The primary problem left open is to derive the gradient estimate as described below. To this end, in this paper we
set up a quantitative version of boundary estimate of the form

\displaystyle\sup_{\partial M}\Delta u\leqslant C\left(1+\sup_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)

(1.6)

and then solve the Dirichlet problem.

First let us introduce some basic notions.
We say a function
w\in C^{2}(\bar{M})
is
admissible
if

\lambda(\mathfrak{g}[w])\in\Gamma\text{ in }\bar{M}.

For an admissible function
\underline{u}\in C^{2}(\bar{M})
, it is called an
admissible subsolution
of the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
), if

\displaystyle f(\lambda(\mathfrak{g}[\underline{u}]))\geqslant\psi\text{ in }\bar{M},\quad\underline{u}=\varphi\text{ on }\partial M.

(1.7)

Moreover,
\underline{u}
is called a

strictly admissible subsolution
, if

\displaystyle f(\lambda(\mathfrak{g}[\underline{u}]))>\psi\text{ in }\bar{M},\quad\underline{u}=\varphi\text{ on }\partial M.

(1.8)

Meanwhile, we say the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) is non-degenerate if

\inf_{M}\psi>\sup_{\partial\Gamma}f.

(1.9)

We say it is degenerate if

\displaystyle\inf_{M}\psi=\sup_{\partial\Gamma}f

(1.10)

and

\displaystyle f\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap C(\overline{\Gamma}).

(1.11)

Here

\sup_{\partial\Gamma}f=\sup_{\lambda_{0}\in\partial\Gamma}\limsup_{\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}}f(\lambda),\quad\overline{\Gamma}=\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma.

Throughout this paper, we always assume the boundary data
\varphi
can be extended to a
C^{2,1}
-admissible function on
\bar{M}
. (Such an assumption is necessary for the solvability). For simplicity, we still denote it by
\varphi

.

Let

Theorem 1.6

Remark 1.8

When

It is a remarkable fact that the Dirichlet problem is not always solvable without the subsolution assumption.

Unfortunately, except on certain domains in Euclidean spaces

We confirm the subsolution assumption when the manifold is a product.
Without specific clarification,
(X,J_{X},\omega_{X})
is a closed

, and

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}f\left(\lambda(\mathfrak{g}[\varphi]+t\pi_{2}^{*}\omega_{S})\right)>\psi\mbox{ }\mbox{ in }\bar{M}.

Remark 1.11
.

The degenerate fully nonlinear elliptic equations on the product

have many applications in geometry.
When

and

In conclusion, in this paper we first derive the quantitative boundary estimate and then
solve the Dirichlet problem for fully nonlinear elliptic equations, possibly with degenerate right-hand side.
No matter the equations are degenerate or not, the existence results for Dirichlet problem of general fully nonlinear elliptic equations are rarely known until this work.
In addition, we construct subsolutions on products, which are of numerous interests. Our results include the existence and regularity results of Chen
[

as a special case and extend extensively Székelyhidi’s work

to complex manifolds with boundary.

Sketch of the proof of main estimates

We first summarize the notations as follows.

•

For
\sigma
, we denote

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\sigma}=\{\lambda\in\Gamma:f(\lambda)>\sigma\},\quad\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}=\{\lambda\in\Gamma:f(\lambda)=\sigma\}.

(2.1)

•

\vec{\bf 1}=(1,\cdots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

•

For the solution
u
and subsolution
\underline{u}
, we denote
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}[u]
and

\underline{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}[\underline{u}]
, respectively.

Condition (
1.2
) ensures (
1.1
) to be elliptic at admissible functions,
while (

is concave with respect to

Consequently, according to Evans-Krylov theorem
[

,
29
]
,
adapted to complex setting (see e.g.
[

]
), together with
Schauder theory, higher order estimates for admissible solutions can be derived from
uniform bound of complex hessian

|\partial\overline{\partial}u|\leq C.

(2.2)

The existence results then follow from standard continuity method.

Outline of the proof of Theorem
1.4

Before stating it, we denote

\displaystyle\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n-1}

(2.3)

an orthonormal basis of

\displaystyle\xi_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\mathrm{{\bf\nu}}-\sqrt{-1}J\nu\right).

Double normal case

Under the assumption

satisfies
(

) or

satisfies

we derive the boundary estimate for double normal derivative

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(\xi_{n},J\bar{\xi}_{n})(p_{0})\leq C\left(1+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n-1}|\mathfrak{g}(\xi_{\alpha},J\bar{\xi}_{n})(p_{0})|^{2}\right),\mbox{ }\forall p_{0}\in\partial M.

(2.5)

Case 1:

This assumption is used to compare

Case 2:

Without imposing any restriction to shape of boundary, following a strategy of Caffarelli-Nirenberg-Spruck

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{g}(\xi_{\alpha},J\bar{\xi}_{n})(p_{0})|\leq C\left(1+\sup_{M}|\nabla u|\right),\mbox{ }\forall 1\leq\alpha\leq n-1,\mbox{ }\forall p_{0}\in\partial M.

Sketch the proof of gradient estimate

The main obstruction
to deriving (

Our strategy is blow-up argument.
According to Lemma
3.2
below, in the presence of (

) and (
1.3

is in effect equivalent to

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}f(t\lambda)>f(\mu),\mbox{ }\forall\lambda,\mbox{ }\mu\in\Gamma.

Such a condition allows one to apply the blow-up argument in
[

As shown in Theorem

, the admissible solutions satisfy

\displaystyle\sup_{\partial M}\Delta u\leq C\left(1+\sup_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\right).

On the other hand,
Székelyhidi’s second estimate

[

yields that any admissible solution to (

\displaystyle\sup_{M}\Delta u\leq C\left(1+\sup_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}+\sup_{\partial M}|\Delta u|\right).

Moreover, by

, one has
\Delta u>-\mathrm{tr}_{\omega}\chi.

With those at hand, we
obtain (

\displaystyle\sup_{M}\Delta u\leq C\left(1+\sup_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\right).

Consequently, the gradient estimate can be derived from (

[

3
Lemmas

To prove Theorem
1.4

3.1
Criteria for symmetric concave functions

The concavity of
f
implies

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)(\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i})\geq f(\mu)-f(\lambda),\mbox{ }\forall\lambda,\mbox{ }\mu\in\Gamma.

(3.1)

This yields

\displaystyle\mbox{For any }\lambda,\mbox{ }\mu\in\Gamma,\mbox{ }\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)\mu_{i}\geq\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}f(t\mu)/t.

Inspired by this observation,
we introduce the following condition

\displaystyle\mbox{For any }\lambda\in\Gamma,\mbox{ }\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}f(t\lambda)/t\geq 0.

(3.2)

This leads to

Lemma 3.1
.

For
f
satisfying (
1.3
), the condition (
3.2
) is equivalent to each one of the following three conditions

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)\mu_{i}\geq 0,\mbox{ }\forall\lambda,\mbox{ }\mu\in\Gamma,

(3.3)

\displaystyle f(\lambda+\mu)\geq f(\lambda),\mbox{ }\forall\lambda,\mbox{ }\mu\in\Gamma,

(3.4)

3.2
Quantitative lemmas

A key ingredient for quantitative boundary
for double normal derivative is how to follow the track of the behavior of the eigenvalues of the matrix
\left(\mathfrak{g}(\xi_{i},J\bar{\xi}_{j})\right)

as
\mathfrak{g}(\xi_{n},J\bar{\xi}_{n})

tends to infinity. To this end, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
A
be an
n\times n
Hermitian matrix

\left(\begin{matrix}d_{1}&&&&a_{1}\\
&d_{2}&&&a_{2}\\
&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
&&&d_{n-1}&a_{n-1}\\
\bar{a}_{1}&\bar{a}_{2}&\cdots&\bar{a}_{n-1}&\mathrm{{\bf a}}\end{matrix}\right)

with
d_{1},\cdots,d_{n-1},a_{1},\cdots,a_{n-1}
fixed, and with
\mathrm{{\bf a}}
variable.
Denote the eigenvalues of
A
by
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
.
Let
\epsilon>0
be a fixed constant.
Suppose that the parameter
\mathrm{{\bf a}}
satisfies the quadratic
growth condition

\displaystyle\mathrm{{\bf a}}\geq\frac{2n-3}{\epsilon}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|a_{i}|^{2}+(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|d_{i}|+\frac{(n-2)\epsilon}{2n-3}.

(3.13)

Then the eigenvalues (possibly with a proper permutation)
behave like

\displaystyle d_{\alpha}-\epsilon

\displaystyle<\lambda_{\alpha}<d_{\alpha}+\epsilon,\mbox{ }\forall 1\leq\alpha\leq n-1,

\displaystyle\mathrm{{\bf a}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}<\mathrm{{\bf a}}+(n-1)\epsilon.

This lemma asserts that
if
the parameter

\mathrm{{\bf a}}

satisfies the quadratic growth condition (
3.13
)
then the eigenvalues concentrate near diagonal elements
correspondingly. Consequently, it suggests an effective way to follow the track of the behavior of the eigenvalues as
|\mathrm{{\bf a}}|
tends to infinity.
In fact, Lemma
3.6
can be viewed as a quantitative version of
[
5
, Lemma 1.2]
.

Lemma 3.7

(
[
5
, Lemma 1.2]
)
.

Consider the
n\times n
symmetric matrix

A=\left(\begin{matrix}d_{1}&&&&a_{1}\\
&d_{2}&&&a_{2}\\
&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
&&&d_{n-1}&a_{n-1}\\
a_{1}&a_{2}&\cdots&a_{n-1}&\mathrm{{\bf a}}\end{matrix}\right)

with
d_{1},\cdots,d_{n-1}
fixed,
|\mathrm{{\bf a}}|
tends to infinity and

|a_{i}|\leq C,\mbox{ }i=1,\cdots,n.

Then the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}
behave like

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha}=d_{\alpha}+o(1),\mbox{ }1\leq\alpha\leq n-1,

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}=\mathrm{{\bf a}}\left(1+O\left(1/\mathrm{{\bf a}}\right)\right),

where the
o(1)
and
O(1/\mathrm{{\bf a}})
are uniform–depending only on
d_{1},\cdots,d_{n-1}
and
C
.

This lemma was first used by Caffarelli-Nirenberg-Spruck
[
5
]
and later by
[
42
,
30
]
to derive boundary
estimates for double normal derivative of
certain fully nonlinear elliptic equations on bounded domains
\Omega
in Euclidean spaces.
However, their boundary estimate is not a quantitative form. The reason is that Lemma
3.7
does not figure out
how the eigenvalues of matrix
A
concentrate explicitly near the corresponding
diagonal elements when
|\mathrm{{\bf a}}|
is sufficiently large.

In the rest of this subsection, we complete the proof of Lemma
3.6
.
We start with the case
n=2
.
For
n=2
, the eigenvalues of
A
are

\lambda_{1}=\frac{\mathrm{{\bf a}}+d_{1}-\sqrt{(\mathrm{{\bf a}}-d_{1})^{2}+4|a_{1}|^{2}}}{2},\quad\lambda_{2}=\frac{\mathrm{{\bf a}}+d_{1}+\sqrt{(\mathrm{{\bf a}}-d_{1})^{2}+4|a_{1}|^{2}}}{2}.

We assume
a_{1}\neq 0
; otherwise we are done.
If
\mathrm{{\bf a}}\geq\frac{|a_{1}|^{2}}{\epsilon}+d_{1}
then one has

\displaystyle 0\leq d_{1}-\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}-\mathrm{{\bf a}}=\frac{2|a_{1}|^{2}}{\sqrt{(\mathrm{{\bf a}}-d_{1})^{2}+4|a_{1}|^{2}}+(\mathrm{{\bf a}}-d_{1})}<\frac{|a_{1}|^{2}}{\mathrm{{\bf a}}-d_{1}}\leq\epsilon.

Here we use
a_{1}\neq 0
to confirm the strictly inequality in the above formula.

The following lemma enables us to count the eigenvalues near the diagonal elements
via a deformation argument. It is an essential ingredient in the proof of

for general

.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
A
be a Hermitian
n

n

\left(\begin{matrix}d_{1}&&&&a_{1}\\
&d_{2}&&&a_{2}\\

\mathrm{{\bf a}}

Suppose that the parameter

Without loss of generality, we assume
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|a_{i}|^{2}>0

(otherwise we are done). Note that the eigenvalues have
the order
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}

It is well known that,

We only need to prove (

3.15

It is easy to see that for any

\displaystyle|\lambda_{\alpha}|<\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|d_{i}|+\epsilon.

Fix

The eigenvalue

\displaystyle(\lambda_{\alpha}-\mathrm{{\bf a}})\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(\lambda_{\alpha}-d_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|a_{i}|^{2}\prod_{j\neq i}(\lambda_{\alpha}-d_{j}).

By the definition of
{\bf B}
, for
\alpha\in{\bf B}

|\lambda_{\alpha}-d_{i}|\geq\epsilon

We therefore derive

\displaystyle|\lambda_{\alpha}-\mathrm{{\bf a}}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{|a_{i}|^{2}}{|\lambda_{\alpha}-d_{i}|}\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|a_{i}|^{2},\quad\mbox{ if }\alpha\in{\bf B}.

Hence, for

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha}\geq\mathrm{{\bf a}}-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|a_{i}|^{2}.

For a set
{\bf S}
, we denote
|{\bf S}|

{\bf S}
.
We shall use proof by contradiction to prove

Assume

Then

We compute the trace of the matrix

\displaystyle\mbox{tr}(A)=

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}+\sum_{\alpha\in{\bf B}}\lambda_{\alpha}+\sum_{\alpha\in{\bf G}}\lambda_{\alpha}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}+|{\bf B}|(\mathrm{{\bf a}}-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|a_{i}|^{2})-|{\bf G}|(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|d_{i}|+\epsilon)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}d_{i}+\mathrm{{\bf a}}=\mbox{tr}(A),

7.1
Construction of subsolutions

According to the main theorems, the existence of subsolutions is a key ingredient to solve the Dirichlet problem.

Let

with sufficiently smooth boundary, and let

We construct strictly admissible subsolutions with

Let

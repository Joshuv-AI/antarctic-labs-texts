Finite-size scaling at the edge of disorder in a time-delay Vicsek model
Viktor Holubec, Daniel Geiss, Sarah A.M. Loos, Klaus Kroy, Frank Cichos

Abstract
Living many-body systems often exhibit scale-free collective behavior reminiscent of thermal critical phenomena. But their mutual interactions are inevitably retarded due to information processing and delayed actuation. We numerically investigate the consequences for the finite-size scaling in the Vicsek model of motile active matter. A growing delay time initially facilitates but ultimately impedes collective ordering and turns the dynamical scaling from diffusive to ballistic. It provides an alternative explanation of swarm traits previously attributed to inertia.



Interacting assemblies of active elements ranging from neural networks in the brain to forest fires and bird flocks can exhibit scale-free behavior
Mora and Bialek 2011
;
Barabási and Bonabeau 2003
;
Munoz 2018
;
Cavagna et al. 2018
. This might be indicative of an underlying powerful physical ordering principle overwriting their inherent complexity.
Finite-size scaling theory
Plischke and Bergersen 2006
associates such behavior with a correlation length exceeding the system size and conjectured to arise from a mechanism called self-organized critically
Bak et al. 1988
. It is indeed an appealing idea that simple interaction rules, such as an innate tendency of individual active elements to replicate the action of their neighbors, can drive a non-equilibrium ensemble towards criticality. Yet, it does not generally seem to apply to both natural systems
Clauset et al. 2009
;
Jhawar et al. 2020
and their models
Pruessner and Jeldtoft Jensen 2002
;
Palmieri and Jensen 2020
;
Martin et al. 2021
.

Studying the emergent finite-size scaling in natural assemblies is thus vital for their prospective modeling in the spirit of non-equilibrium many-body systems, as successful models should be required to reproduce the observed scaling
Palmieri and Jensen 2020
and correlations
Cavagna et al. 2019
. In this vein, the inertia spin model
Cavagna et al. 2019
was proposed to overcome known deficiencies of the classical Vicsek model (VM)
Vicsek et al. 1995
in comparison with empirical data for natural swarms and flocks. Inspired by observations of birds and insects
Cavagna et al. 2018
, which cannot turn instantaneously, it adds inertia to the navigation rules for the individual motile spins. The dynamical scaling and time correlation functions found in natural swarms
Cavagna et al. 2017
of moderate size (dynamical exponent
z\approx 1.1
) could thereby be reconciled with the classical VM prediction (
z=2
)
Cavagna et al. 2018
.

However, it seems worthwhile to point out that, for motile ensembles, physical inertia can have quite similar effects as delayed reactions due to finite speeds of information transfer and processing, and actuation
Nagy et al. 2010
;
Attanasi et al. 2014a
;
Geiss et al. 2019
. The latter are indeed ubiquitously found both in nature and in engineering practice, from insects to birds and various robotic systems
Vásárhelyi et al. 2014
;
Virágh et al. 2014
;
Vásárhelyi et al. 2018
, and also thought to cause traffic jams
Davis 2003
. Recent experiments
Khadka et al. 2018
;
Muiños-Landin et al. 2021
with feedback-driven artificial microswimmers
Fränzl et al. 2021
have moreover established the role of delayed interactions also in the microscopic world of active Brownian particles.
Beyond oscillatory behavior, which is also a common trait of inertial motion, time-delayed interactions can give rise to multistability, instabilities, and even chaos
Forgoston and Schwartz 2008
;
Piwowarczyk et al. 2019
;
Geiss et al. 2019
;
Loos and Klapp 2019
. Conversely, intermediate time delays may facilitate clustering compared to the classical VM
Piwowarczyk et al. 2019
and flocking in the Cucker–Smale model
Erban et al. 2016
. And recent indications that delay-dependent optimizations play a role in artificial microswimmer assemblies
Muiños-Landin et al. 2021
seem reminiscent of the optimum run-and-tumble times of bacteria
Romanczuk and Salbreux 2015
;
Diz-Muñoz et al. 2016
or the improved localisation achieved with feedback cooling
Bushev et al. 2006
;
Goldwater et al. 2019
or feedback-driving of robots
Mijalkov et al. 2016
.

In this Letter, we demonstrate that by introducing retarded reactions in the classical VM
Vicsek et al. 1995
, we can obtain the same finite-size scaling and time correlations near the ordering transition as previously obtained with the inertia spin model
Cavagna et al. 2018
. This suggests that similar scaling and correlations as observed for natural swarms can be expected for a wealth of systems with time-delayed interactions, including over-damped Brownian particle assemblies. We can also corroborate the observation that increasing delay times may have a non-monotonic effect on the stability of coherent collective motion
Piwowarczyk et al. 2019
.

Model:
The (classical) VM
Vicsek et al. 1995
arguably is the simplest model for motile active-particle assemblies, ranging from bacteria to birds, and a central paradigm in the field motile active matter
Bechinger et al. 2016
. In each discrete time step, all particles advance with the same constant speed
v_{0}
. And they instantaneously adapt their orientations to the previous average orientation of their neighbors within an interaction sphere of radius
R

Since the static critical exponents in the standard VM are known to depend strongly on the density, speed and interaction radius

Conclusion:

We analyzed the VM with retarded interactions, as they are expected from natural delays between sensing and reaction. It provides an alternative to the rotational inertia hypothesis for reconciling the discrepancies in the dynamical scaling and relaxation between the standard VM and natural swarms
Cavagna et al. 2016
;
Cavagna et al. 2018
. While the navigation of insects and other flying species is certainly influenced both by inertia and time delay, our focus on delay could help to better understand their relation to feedback-driven robotic
Mijalkov et al. 2016
or micro-particle
Khadka et al. 2018
;
Lavergne et al. 2019
;
Muiños-Landin et al. 2021
swarms. Especially in the latter, current experimental techniques
Fränzl et al. 2021
allow inertial effects to be suppressed so that only the unavoidable time delay remains.

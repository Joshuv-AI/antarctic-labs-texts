Abstract
. The mod- 2 2 arithmetic Milnor invariants, introduced by Morishita, provide a decomposition law for primes in canonical Galois extensions of ℚ \mathbb{Q} with unitriangular Galois groups, and contain the Legendre and Rédei symbols as special cases. Morishita further proposed a notion of mod- q q arithmetic Milnor invariants, where q q is a prime power, for number fields containing the q q th roots of unity and satisfying certain class field theory assumptions. We extend this theory from the number field context to general fields, by introducing a notion of a linking invariant for discrete valuations and orderings. We further express it as a Magnus homomorphism coefficient, and relate it to Massey product elements in Galois cohomology.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

The classical Legendre symbol
(p_{1}/p_{2})
for distinct prime numbers
p_{1},p_{2}
describes the decomposition behavior of
p_{2}
in the quadratic number field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_{1}})
.
On 1939 Rédei
[
28
]
introduced a triple symbol
[p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}]\in\{\pm 1\}
, for odd primes
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
, which describes the decomposition pattern of
p_{3}
in a certain Galois extension
K_{\{p_{1},p_{2}\}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
with a dihedral Galois group of order
8
.
In the early 2000s, Morishita introduced a far-reaching generalization of these symbols, the
mod-
2
arithmetic Milnor invariant
(
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
):
Given primes numbers
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},p_{n+1}
,
n\geq 1
, this symbol describes the decomposition law of
p_{n+1}
in a certain
\mathbb{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
-Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, associated with
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
.
Here
\mathbb{U}_{n}(R)
denotes the group of all unipotent upper-triangular
(n+1)\times(n+1)
-matrices over the unital ring
R
.
Thus the Legendre and Rédei symbols are the first and second symbols in this infinite sequence of arithmetic objects.
A candidate for the next step in this sequence, namely a 4-fold symbol with values in
\{\pm 1\}
, was constructed by Amano
[
2
]
.

More generally, for a prime power
q
, Morishita proposed to define
mod-
q
arithmetic Milnor invariants
over number fields
F
with values in
\mathbb{Z}/q
.
Here
F
is assumed to contain the
q
th roots of unity and to satisfy certain assumptions arising from class field theory; in particular, the class number
|\mathrm{Cl}(F)|
should be prime to
q
.
An explicit example was constructed by Amano, Mizusawa and Morishita
[
1
]
, who define a 3-fold mod-
3
symbol over the Eisenstein number field
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
.

Morishita’s construction was motivated by the analogy between prime numbers in
\mathbb{Q}
and knots in the
3
-sphere
S^{3}
.
This general philosophy, known as
arithmetic topology
, was suggested by Mumford, Manin and Mazur, and later developed by Kapranov, Morishita, Reznikov, and others.
We refer to
[
22
]
and
[
23
]
for more information on the development of these ideas.
Indeed, in knot theory Milnor defined similar symbols, called
higher linking invariants

[
20
]
, for links in
S^{3}
, and more generally,
over
q
-homology
3
-spheres
, that is, closed oriented
3
-manifolds
M
with
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z}/q)=0
.
Morishita’s invariants are their arithmetic analog over
\mathbb{Q}
, and over number fields as above, respectively.

1.2.
Contents of the present work

In this paper, we extend parts of Morishita’s theory from the context of number fields to the context of
general fields

F
containing the roots of unity of order
q=p^{s}
, with
p
prime.
In this generalization, finite and infinite primes are replaced by discrete valuations and orderings, respectively, where the latter are considered only when
q=2
.

Morishita’s invariants are closely related to the maximal pro-
p
Galois group of the number field
F
that is unramified outside a given finite list of primes.
In the general field context, we take a set
\widehat{I}
of discrete valuations or orderings on
F
(when
q=2
).
Factoring
G_{F}(p)
by its closed normal subgroup generated by the inertia subgroups of the discrete valuations not in
\widehat{I}
, and the
\mathbb{Z}/2
-subgroups corresponding to the orderings not in
\widehat{I}
, we obtain a Galois group
G_{F,{\widehat{I}}}(p)
.
We further assume that this group is generated by the images of the inertia groups and
\mathbb{Z}/2
-subgroups corresponding to the valuations and orderings, respectively, in a subset
I
of
\widehat{I}
consisting of
n
independent valuations and orderings (this is the analog of the condition
p\hbox{$\,\not|\,$}|\mathrm{Cl}(F)|
in the number field case).
For a discrete valuation
v=v_{j}
(resp., an ordering
P=P_{j}
), with
j\not\in{\widehat{I}}
, we construct a
linking invariant

[{\widehat{I}},I,j]
(see Section
8
for the indexing convention).
It is a pro-
p
group homomorphism

[{\widehat{I}},I,j]\colon Z_{j}\to\mathbb{Z}/q,

where
Z_{j}
is a decomposition group (resp., a Galois group of a relative real closure) in
G_{F}(p)
associated with
v
(resp.,
P
); see Section
8
.
In the main examples, where
F=\mathbb{Q}
, we take
I
to be a finite set of prime numbers and take
\widehat{I}
to be this set together with the unique ordering on
\mathbb{Q}
.
Then the linking invariant, when properly interpreted, contains the Legendre and Rédei symbols as special cases (Section
9
).
As in the works of Milnor and Morishita,
[{\widehat{I}},I,j]
is defined only under the assumption that certain linking invariants for proper subsets of
\widehat{I}
and
I
vanish.

The homomorphism
[{\widehat{I}},I,j]
is obtained from a canonical pro-
p
group epimorphism

\rho\colon G_{F,{\widehat{I}}}(p)\to\mathbb{U}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/q),

by restricting it to
Z_{j}
and projecting into the upper-right entry of
\mathbb{U}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/q)
.
This homomorphism corresponds via Galois theory to a
\mathbb{U}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/q)
-Galois extension
E_{\rho}
of
F
.
Among the main properties of
E_{\rho}
and the linking invariant
[{\widehat{I}},I,j]
we have (see Subsection
8.2
):

•

The discrete valuations in
I
ramify in
E_{\rho}
with ramification groups
\mathbb{Z}/q
, whereas discrete valuations on
F
outside
\widehat{I}
are unramified in
E_{\rho}
.

•

The orderings in
I
do not extend to
E_{\rho}
, whereas orderings on
F
outside
\widehat{I}
extend to
E_{\rho}
.

•

If
j
corresponds to a valuation
v
, then
[{\widehat{I}},I,j]=0
if and only if
v
is completely decomposed in
E_{\rho}
.

•

If
j
corresponds to an ordering, then
[{\widehat{I}},I,j]=0
.

We further define a notion of a
linking homomorphism
associated to two discrete valuations (Definition
8.8
).
It is the field-theoretic generalization of Morishita’s
linking numbers
of two finite primes in a number field and is, in turn, analogous to the linking number of two knots in the topological context.

As conjectured by Stalling and proved by Turaev
[
30
]
and Porter
[
27
]
, the Milnor invariants in knot theory can be interpreted cohomologically as Massey products, which are cohomological operations extending the cup product.
It was shown by Morishita
[
23
]
that his arithmetic Milnor invariants also have such an interpretation.
This may be viewed as a generalization of the well-known interpretation of Legendre symbols as cup products.
We similarly relate our generalized linking invariants to Massey product elements in Galois cohomology – see Section
11
.

1.3.
Strategy

Needless to say, our approach is generally inspired by the above-mentioned works of Morishita.
Further, it is based on an abstract algebraic notion, which we call a
pro-
p
linking structure
.
Loosely speaking, it consists of a pro-
p
group
G
, families of closed subgroups
Z_{i}\trianglerighteq T_{i}
of
G
, as well as information on the required image of the
T_{i}
’s in a certain target pro-
p
group
U
.
A
globalization
for the linking structure is a homomorphism
G\to U
which is consistent with this local information.
In our field-theoretic applications, we apply this to
G=G_{F,{\widehat{I}}}(p)
and the decomposition and inertia subgroups
Z_{i},T_{i}
,
i\in I
, respectively (in the valuation case), or to the Galois groups of relative real closures (in the case of orderings in
I
).
This gives rise to a linking structure
\mathcal{G}_{{\widehat{I}},I}
with target group
U=\mathbb{U}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/q)
, which we call the (
I
-indexed)
Kummer linking structure of
F
unramified outside
\widehat{I}
.
A globalization for this structure gives rise to the homomorphism
\rho
, whence to the Galois extension
E_{\rho}
and to the linking invariants
[{\widehat{I}},I,j]
.

The actual construction of our linking invariants is by induction on
n=|I|
:
For
n=1
the globalization is essentially a Kummer homomorphism of a uniformizer for the discrete valuation in
I
, or of a negative element in the case of orderings.
The induction step uses a fiber product construction for the groups
\mathbb{U}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/q)
.
Moreover, it is based on the assumption that the underlying group
G_{{\widehat{I}}}
of
\mathcal{G}_{{\widehat{I}},I}
has a certain presentation using generators and relations, which we call a group of
link type
.
This group structure extends results by Koch and Hoechsmann on the structure of
G_{F,{\widehat{I}}}(p)
in the number field case, and follows Morishita’s approach (see Theorem
9.3
).
It is analogous to link groups in the knot theory.

1.4.
The Structure of the paper

Section
2
provides some basic facts about the unitriangular group
\mathbb{U}_{n}(R)
, and quotients of it consisting of partial matrices.
Section
3
introduces linking structures and their globalizations in an abstract pro-
p
context, as well as the notion of a linking structure
\mathcal{G}
of
V
-link type, where
V
is a closed normal subgroup of the target group of
\mathcal{G}
.
We show that in this case, and under a certain local triviality condition, any globalization for
\mathcal{G}/V
lifts to a globalization for
\mathcal{G}
(Proposition
3.5
).
Section
4
introduces the linking structure
\mathcal{G}_{{\widehat{I}},I}
, which is an abstract pro-
p
analog of the maximal pro-
p
Galois group
G_{F,{\widehat{I}}}(p)
of a number field unramified outside a finite set
\widehat{I}
of primes.
We show how globalizations for such linking structures can be combined using a fiber product construction for unitriangular groups, to obtain globalizations for structures
\mathcal{G}_{{\widehat{I}},I}
with larger sets
{\widehat{I}},I
.
Globalizations for
\mathcal{G}_{{\widehat{I}},I}
give rise to our main notion of the
linking invariant

[{\widehat{I}},I,j]
.
Our approach here is different from that of
[
22
]
and
[
23
]
, where the arithmetic Milnor invariants are defined as coefficients of the Magnus homomorphism of certain Frobenius lifts.
However, in Section
5
we show that, assuming that the groups
T_{i}
are free pro-
p
-cyclic,
[{\widehat{I}},I,j]
can also be interpreted in the relevant cases in terms of Magnus homomorphisms.

Then we turn to the arithmetic situation and recall in Section
6
the needed notions and facts on valuations and orderings on fields.
Crucial to our approach is the connection between inertia groups of valuations and the Kummer pairing, which is discussed in Section
7
.
Then we introduce in Section
8
our main construction of
Kummer linking structures
of fields with respect to a sets
I\subseteq{\widehat{I}}
of valuations and orderings.
We explain the meaning of the linking invariant
[{\widehat{I}},I,j]
in this arithmetic context in terms of ramification and decomposition of valuations and orderings in the Galois extension
E_{\rho}
associated with a globalization
\rho
for
\mathcal{G}_{{\widehat{I}},I}
.
We also define the
linking homomorphism

{\rm lk}(v_{j},v_{i})
of two discrete valuations.

In Sections
9
and
10
we focus on number fields.
We interpret some of the main results of Morishita and Amano in the language of linking structures and globalizations, and in particular, interpret the Legendre and Rédei symbols as linking invariants arising from globalizations of Kummer structures.
Here
I
consists of discrete valuations corresponding to primes numbers
p_{i}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, and
\widehat{I}
consists of
I
together with the unique ordering on
\mathbb{Q}
.

Finally, in Section
11
we go back to the general pro-
p
setting of the linking structures
\mathcal{G}_{{\widehat{I}},I}
, and interpret the linking invariant
[{\widehat{I}},I,j]
cohomologically in terms of Massey product elements.

2.
Unitriangular Matrices

2.1.
Preliminaries

We first recall some notions and facts from
[
11
]
.
Fix a non-negative integer
n
and let

\mathbb{I}_{n}=\{(k,l)\ |\ 1\leq k\leq l\leq n+1\},\quad\Delta_{n}=\{(k,k)\ |\ 1\leq k\leq n+1\}.

We view
\mathbb{I}_{n}
and
\Delta_{n}
as the sets of upper-right entries, resp., the diagonal, of an
(n+1)\times(n+1)
-matrix.

A subset
S
of
\mathbb{I}_{n}
is called
convex
if:

(i)

\Delta_{n}\subseteq S
;

(ii)

For every
(k,l)\in S
and
(k^{\prime},l^{\prime})\in\mathbb{I}_{n}
such that
k\leq k^{\prime}
and
l^{\prime}\leq l
also
(k^{\prime},l^{\prime})\in S
.

Loosely speaking, (ii) means that
S
has no “holes” towards the diagonal.

We fix a profinite unital commutative ring
R
with additive group
R^{+}
.
For a convex subset
S
of
\mathbb{I}_{n}
, we write
\mathbb{U}_{S}
for the set of all partial matrices
(a_{kl})_{(k,l)\in S}
with
a_{kl}\in R
and
a_{kk}=1
for
1\leq k\leq n
.
It forms a group under the multiplication map

(a_{kl})_{(k,l)\in S}\cdot(b_{kl})_{(k,l)\in S}=\Bigl(\sum_{r=k}^{l}a_{kr}b_{rl}\Bigr)_{(k,l)\in S}.

We write
\Id_{\mathbb{U}_{S}}=(\delta_{kl})_{(k,l)\in S}
for the identity element in
\mathbb{U}_{S}
.
In particular,

\mathbb{U}_{n}=\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{I}_{n}}

may be identified with the group of all unitriangular (i.e., unipotent and upper-triangular)
(n+1)\times(n+1)
-matrices over
R
.

For
(k,l)\in S
let
\mathrm{pr}_{kl}\colon\mathbb{U}_{S}\to R
be the projection map on the
(k,l)
-entry.

Given
(k,l)\in S\setminus\Delta_{n}
and
r\in R
, we write
\Id_{S}+rE_{kl}
for the matrix in
\mathbb{U}_{S}
which is
1
on
\Delta_{n}
and
r
at entry
(k,l)
, and is
0
at all other entries.
We write
\Id_{S}+RE_{kl}
for the set of all such matrices with
r\in R
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(a)

For every
(k,l)\in S\setminus\Delta_{n}
the subset
\Id_{S}+RE_{kl}
forms a subgroup of
\mathbb{U}_{S}
which is isomorphic to
R^{+}
.

(b)

The partial matrices
\Id_{\mathbb{U}_{n}}+E_{k,k+1}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,n
, generate
\mathbb{U}_{n}
.

Proof.

(a) is straightforward.
For (b) see
[
32
]
*§1.
∎

2.2.
Projection homomorphisms

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1\leq m\leq n
and let
\iota\colon\{1,2,\ldots,m+1\}\to\{1,2,\ldots,n+1\}
be a strictly increasing map.
Let
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
be convex subsets of
\mathbb{I}_{n}
such that every
1\leq r\leq n
with
(r,r+1)\in S^{\prime}
is in the image of
\iota
.
Then:

(a)

The subset
\bar{S}=\{(k,l)\in\mathbb{I}_{m}\ |\ (\iota(k),\iota(l))\in S^{\prime}\}
is convex in
\mathbb{I}_{m}
;

(b)

The map

u\colon\mathbb{U}_{S}\to\mathbb{U}_{\bar{S}},\quad(a_{ij})_{(i,j)\in S}\mapsto\bigl(a_{\iota(k),\iota(l)}\bigr)_{(k,l)\in\bar{S}},

is a group homomorphism.

Proof.

(a)  Let
(k,l)\in\bar{S}
and
(k^{\prime},l^{\prime})\in\mathbb{I}_{m}
satisfy
k\leq k^{\prime}
and
l\geq l^{\prime}
.
Then
\iota(k)\leq\iota(k^{\prime})
and
\iota(l)\geq\iota(l^{\prime})
.
As
(\iota(k),\iota(l))\in S^{\prime}
, the convexity of
S^{\prime}
implies that
(\iota(k^{\prime}),\iota(l^{\prime}))\in S^{\prime}
, so
(k^{\prime},l^{\prime})\in\bar{S}
.

(b)  First we claim that if
(k,l)\in\bar{S}
, then every integer
\iota(k)\leq r\leq\iota(l)
is of the form
\iota(t)
for some
k\leq t\leq l
;

This is immediate if
r=\iota(l)
.

If
\iota(k)\leq r<\iota(l)
, then the inclusion
(\iota(k),\iota(l))\in S^{\prime}
and the convexity of
S^{\prime}
imply that
(r,r+1)\in S^{\prime}
.
By assumption, this implies that
r=\iota(t)
for some
1\leq t\leq m+1
.
Since
\iota
is strictly increasing,
k\leq t<l
, as required.

Now for
(a_{ij})_{(i,j)\in S},(b_{ij})_{(i,j)\in S}\in\mathbb{U}_{S}
we compute using the above claim:

\begin{split}u\Bigl(\bigl(a_{ij}\bigr)_{(i,j)\in S}\cdot\bigl(b_{ij}\bigr)_{(i,j)\in S}\Bigr)&=u\Bigl(\bigl(\sum_{i\leq r\leq j}a_{ir}b_{rj}\bigr)_{(i,j)\in S}\Bigr)\\
&=\Bigl(\sum_{\iota(k)\leq r\leq\iota(l)}a_{\iota(k),r}b_{r,\iota(l)}\Bigr)_{(k,l)\in\bar{S}}\\
&=\Bigl(\sum_{k\leq t\leq l}a_{\iota(k),\iota(t)}b_{\iota(t),\iota(l)}\Bigr)_{(k,l)\in\bar{S}}\\
&=(a_{\iota(k),\iota(l)})_{(k,l)\in\bar{S}}\cdot(b_{\iota(k),\iota(l)})_{(k,l)\in\bar{S}}\\
&=u\bigl((a_{ij})_{(i,j)\in S}\bigr)\cdot u\bigl((b_{ij})_{(i,j)\in S}\bigr),\end{split}

as desired.
∎

2.3 Examples
.

(1)  Take
m=n
and
\iota=\id
.
Then for every convex subsets
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
of
\mathbb{I}_{n}
, the projection map
\mathrm{pr}_{S,S^{\prime}}\colon\mathbb{U}_{S}\to\mathbb{U}_{S^{\prime}}
is a group homomorphism.

(2)  Let
1\leq m\leq n
,
S=\mathbb{I}_{n}
and
S^{\prime}=\Delta_{n}\cup\mathbb{I}_{m}
.
Define the map
\iota\colon\{1,2,\ldots,m+1\}\to\{1,2,\ldots,n+1\}
by
\iota(k)=k
.
Then
\bar{S}=\mathbb{I}_{m}
.
We obtain that the projection map on the upper-left
(m+1)\times(m+1)
-submatrix is a group homomorphism
p^{\prime}_{n,m}\colon\mathbb{U}_{n}\to\mathbb{U}_{m}
.

Similarly, the projection map on the lower-right
(m+1)\times(m+1)
-submatrix is a group homomorphism
p^{\prime\prime}_{n,m}\colon\mathbb{U}_{n}\to\mathbb{U}_{m}
.

(3)  By combining the two maps in (2) (or directly), we see that for every
1\leq l\leq n
the projection map
\mathrm{pr}_{l,l+1}\colon\mathbb{U}_{n}\to\mathbb{U}_{1}\cong R^{+}
on the
(l,l+1)
-entry is a group homomorphism.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
1\leq m_{1},m_{2}\leq n
satisfy
m_{1}+m_{2}\geq n
, and set
t=m_{1}+m_{2}-n
.
Also let
V=\Ker(p^{\prime}_{n,m_{1}})\cap\Ker(p^{\prime\prime}_{n,m_{2}})
.
Then the maps
p^{\prime}_{n,m_{1}}
and
p^{\prime\prime}_{n,m_{2}}
induce a group isomorphism

\mathbb{U}_{n}/V=\mathbb{U}_{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{U}_{t}}\mathbb{U}_{m_{2}},

where the fiber product is taken with respect to
p^{\prime\prime}_{m_{1},t}
and
p^{\prime}_{m_{2},t}
.

Proof.

We note that

p^{\prime\prime}_{m_{1},t}\circ p^{\prime}_{n,m_{1}}=p^{\prime}_{m_{2},t}\circ p^{\prime\prime}_{n,m_{2}}

is the projection to rows and columns
n-m_{2}+1,\ldots,m_{1}
.
This gives rise to a homomorphism

(p^{\prime}_{n,m_{1}},p^{\prime\prime}_{n,m_{2}})\colon\mathbb{U}_{n}\to\mathbb{U}_{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{U}_{t}}\mathbb{U}_{m_{2}}

with kernel
V
.

This homomorphism is surjective, since any pair
(M_{1},M_{2})\in\mathbb{U}_{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{U}_{t}}\mathbb{U}_{m_{2}}
has a preimage
M
in
\mathbb{U}_{n}
, which is
M_{1}
on the upper-left
(m_{1}+1)\times(m_{1}+1)
-submatrix, is
M_{2}
on the lower-right
(m_{2}+1)\times(m_{2}+1)
-submatrix, and is
0
elsewhere.
∎

.

3.
Linking Structures

3.1.
Globalizations of linking structures

We fix a prime number
p
.
A (pro-
p
)
linking structure

(3.1)

\mathcal{G}=(G,U,Z_{i},T_{i},\lambda_{i},\pi_{i})_{i\in I}.

consists of the following data:

•

Pro-
p
groups
G
and
U
;

•

An index set
I
;

•

For each
i\in I
a pro-
p
group
Z_{i}
and a closed normal subgroup
T_{i}
of
Z_{i}

(\rho^{*}\circ\lambda^{*}_{T^{*}})(\hat{\sigma})
has zero entries outside the main diagonal, the
(n+2)
-th column, and entry
(1,n+1)
.
Moreover, its
(1,n+1)
-entry is
[{\widehat{I}},I,j](\sigma)
.
A direct computation of matrix commutators gives

\begin{split}\hat{\pi}^{*}(r)&=(\rho^{*}\circ\hat{\lambda}^{*}_{T^{*}})((\tau^{\prime})^{q}[\tau,\hat{\sigma}])=[(\rho^{*}\circ\res^{*}_{j})(\tau),(\rho^{*}\circ\hat{\lambda}^{*}_{T^{*}})(\hat{\sigma})]\\
=&[\hat{\pi}^{*}(\tau),(\rho^{*}\circ\hat{\lambda}^{*}_{T^{*}})(\hat{\sigma})]\\
=&\begin{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
&1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
&&&\ddots&0&0\\
&&&&1&\bar{\pi}_{j}(\tau)\\
&&&&&1\end{bmatrix},&\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&\cdots&[{\widehat{I}},I,j](\sigma)&*\\
&1&0&\cdots&0&*\\
&&&\ddots&0&*\\
&&&&1&*\\
&&&&&1\end{bmatrix}\end{bmatrix}\\
=&\Id-\bar{\pi}_{j}(\tau)\cdot[{\widehat{I}},I,j](\sigma)\cdot E_{1,n+2},\end{split}

where
E_{1,n+2}
is as in Subsection
2.1
.
The assertion now follows by examining the
(1,n+2)
-th entries.
∎

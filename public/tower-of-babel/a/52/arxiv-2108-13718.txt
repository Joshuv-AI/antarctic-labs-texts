The two halves of disjunctive correctness
Cezary Cieśliński, Mateusz Łełyk, Bartosz Wcisło

Abstract
Ali Enayat had asked whether two halves of Disjunctive Correctness ( DC ) for the compositional truth predicate are conservative over Peano Arithmetic. In this article, we show that the principle “every true disjunction has a true disjunct” is equivalent to bounded induction for the compositional truth predicate and thus it is not conservative. On the other hand, the converse implication “any disjunction with a true disjunct is true” can be conservatively added to PA . The methods introduced here allow us to give a direct nonconservativeness proof for DC .



1
Introduction

The area of axiomatic truth theories analyses the concept of truth by studying first-order theories which try to capture various properties of this notion. These theories are formulated as follows: We choose a base theory strong enough to represent syntax (this is typically Peano Arithmetic,
PA
). To this theory, we add a fresh predicate
T(x)
whose intended reading is “
x
is (a code of) a true sentence” together with axioms governing the behaviour of that predicate. A notable example of such a theory is
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
which stipulates that the truth predicate satisfies Tarski’s compositional conditions for arithmetical sentences. For instance, a conjunction
\phi\wedge\psi
is true iff both
\phi
and
\psi
are true.

If we add to
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
full induction for the formulae containing the truth predicate, the resulting theory, called
CT
, is not conservative over
PA
, that is, it proves arithmetical theorems which cannot be demonstrated in
PA
itself. More specifically, we can show by induction on the lengths of proofs that every sentence provable in
PA
is true (a principle called global reflection over
PA
) and, consequently, that
PA
is consistent.
1
1

1

This is one of the basic results of truth theory. A comprehensive introduction to the area, including this result, can be found in
(
Halbach 2011
)
.

On the other hand, by a theorem of Kotlarski, Krajewski, and Lachlan (see
(
Kotlarski et al. 1981
)
)
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
itself does not prove any new arithmetical theorems.

In
(
Cieśliński 2010
)
, it was shown that already
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
extended with a principle of propositional reflection “sentences derived in propositional logic from true premises are true” suffices to prove global reflection over
PA
. In other words, an overtly nonconservative principle can be derived without explicitly assuming induction on a ground of a rather innocuous principle of overtly truth-theoretic nature. Subsequently, it turned out that a number of other truth-theoretic principles are not conservative over
PA
and are all equivalent to
\Delta_{0}
-induction for the compositional truth predicate (
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
with
\Delta_{0}
-induction is called
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
).
2
2

2

For the proof of nonconservativity of
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
, see
(
Łełyk and Wcisło 2017
)
.
The picture that emerged was that there seems to be a “minimal natural” nonconservative extension of
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
.

The “dividing line” between conservative and nonconservative extensions of
PA
has been named ‘‘Tarski boundary’’ by Ali Enayat.
3
3

3

One can find more information on the Tarski boundary in
(
Cieśliński 2017
)
. A more concise discussion is also contained in
(
Łełyk and Wcisło 2016
)
and
(
Łełyk 2019
)
.
One of the most striking results on Tarski boundary was obtained by
Enayat and Pakhomov 2019
. It was shown that among theories equivalent to
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
is
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
together with the principle of disjunctive correctness,
DC
, which states that any finite (but possibly nostandard) disjunction is true iff one of the disjuncts is true. This axiom appears to be a mild, natural, extension of the compositional clauses and yet turns out to carry the full strength of
\Delta_{0}
-induction.

It was not clear whether this result can be pushed further in the following manner: Disjunctive correctness can be naturally split into two halves. The first half is a principle
DC-in
saying “a disjunction with true disjunct is true” and the second is
DC-out
which says “a true disjunction has a true disjunct”. It has been asked in
(
Enayat and Pakhomov 2019
)
whether the second of these principles can be added conservatively to
PA
.
4
4

4

See Question 5.3 in
(
Enayat and Pakhomov 2019
)
. The conservativity of
DC-in
was settled in December 2018 and stated in the formulation of that question, but the proof was not published.

In this article, we analyse both of the above principles. We show that over
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
,
DC-in
gives rise to a conservative extension of
PA
while
DC-out
yields
\Delta_{0}
-induction. The methods used for the nonconservativeness result yield a new, direct proof that
DC
is yet another incarnation of
\Delta_{0}
-induction for the formulae containing the truth predicate.

2
Preliminaries

We consider truth theories over Peano arithmetic,
PA
, as our base theory.
5
5

5

The choice is motivated mostly by a certain tradition in the field. However, the results make sense and still hold true over much weaker theories, say
\textnormal{I}\Delta_{0}+\exp
.
It is an axiomatic theory in a language
\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}=\{S,+,\times,0\}
whose axioms consist of inductive definitions of addition and multiplication in terms of the successor function
S
together with the full induction scheme. Although this theory overtly speaks of natural numbers, in fact it is strong enough to capture objects such as finite sets, finite sequences, or finite graphs. Crucially,
PA
is capable of expressing syntactic notions such as “term”, “formula” or “proof” and proving basic facts about these notions such as “a conjunction of two formulae is a formula.” We assume that the reader is familiar with the coding of syntax and the basic metamathematics. This is discussed in many sources such as
(
Kaye 1991
)
, Chapter 9 or
(
Hájek and Pudlák 1993
)
, Chapter I, Section 1(d), pp.50–61. Throughout the article, we will explain those bits of notation that seem not to explain themselves. We provide a glossary of all formalised notions we are using throughout the paper in the appendix.

Convention 1
.

•

We will often conflate Gödel numbers of syntactic objects with those objects. We will also use formulae denoting syntactic expressions as if they were sets. For instance, we will write
\phi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}
rather than
\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(\phi)
, where
\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}
expresses that
\phi
is (a Gödel code of) an arithmetical sentence.

•

Provably functional formulae will be used as if denoting actual functions. For instance, we will write
\textnormal{FV}(\phi)
for the set of free variables of
\phi
.

•

In particular, we will often write the results of syntactic operations without explicitly mentioning the operations themselves. For instance, if
\phi,\psi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}
, we will freely speak of the conjunction
\phi\wedge\psi
rather than “the only
z
such that
z
is a conjunction of
\phi
,
\psi
.”

The research on Tarski boundary concerns extensions of the compositional truth theory. Let us define it. By writing
\underline{n}
, we mean (the Gödel code of) a
numeral
denoting the number
n
, that is,

\ulcorner\underbrace{S(S\ldots S(0)\ldots)}_{\textnormal{``$S$'' repeated $n$ times}}\urcorner.

If
t
is (a Gödel code of) a closed arithmetical term, then by
{t}^{\circ}
we mean the value of that term. Thus we have, for instance,

\mathbb{N}\models{\ulcorner S0+S(S(0))\urcorner}^{\circ}={\underline{3}}^{\circ}=3.

Note that a value of an arithmetical term can be computed in a primitive recursive way and the natural function computing it may be formalised in
PA
. Now we can proceed to the actual definition.

Definition 2
.

By
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
(Compositional Truth) we mean a theory in the arithmetical language with a fresh unary predicate
T
extending
PA
with the following axioms:

•

\forall x\ \Big(T(x)\rightarrow\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(x)\Big).

•

\forall s,t\in\textnormal{ClTerm}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\Big(T(s=t)\equiv{s}^{\circ}={t}^{\circ}\Big).

•

\forall\phi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\Big(T(\neg\phi)\equiv\neg T\phi\Big).

•

\forall\phi,\psi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\Big(T(\phi\vee\psi)\equiv T\phi\vee T\psi\Big).

•

\forall\phi,\psi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\Big(T(\phi\wedge\psi)\equiv T\phi\wedge T\psi\Big).

•

\forall\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\forall v\in\textnormal{Var}\Big(\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(\exists v\phi)\rightarrow T(\exists v\phi)\equiv\exists x\ T\phi(\underline{x})\Big).

•

\forall\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\forall v\in\textnormal{Var}\Big(\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(\forall v\phi)\rightarrow T(\forall v\phi)\equiv\forall x\ T\phi(\underline{x})\Big).

•

\forall\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\forall\bar{s},\bar{t}\in\textnormal{ClTermSeq}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\Big({\bar{s}}^{\circ}={\bar{t}}^{\circ}\rightarrow T\phi(\bar{s})=T\phi(\bar{t})\Big).

Above,
\textnormal{ClTermSeq}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(x)
is a formula expressing “
x
is a sequence of closed arithmetical terms.” The rest of the notation should be self-explanatory and, as we remarked earlier, it is discussed in the appendix.

By
CT
, we mean
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
with full induction for the extended language. By
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
, we mean
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
with induction for
\Delta_{0}
formulae containing the truth predicate. (Note that already
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
contains full arithmetical induction.)

The last clause in the above axioms for
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
is called
Regularity Axiom
,
REG
. It is not included among the basic axioms in the standard presentations of this theory, like
(
Halbach 2011
)
or
(
Cieśliński 2017
)
. The version of
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
with
REG
appears, e.g., in
(
Enayat et al. 2020
)
or
(
Łełyk and Wcisło 2021
)
. Admittedly, the regularity axiom has a clearly different status than the rest of the axioms of
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
. We add it mostly for two (admittedly technical) reasons which we will explain in Section
6
.

Although
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
seems to express the crucial properties of the truth predicate, it is not arithmetically stronger than
PA
. The result was essentially proved by
Kotlarski et al. 1981
.
6
6

6

The original result concerns satisfaction classes over a purely relational language. This work has been extended in
(
Kaye 1991
)
to languages with terms and in
(
Engström 2002
)
to truth classes over languages with terms. Subsequently,
Enayat and Visser 2015
introduced a new, elegant and flexible method of constructing satisfaction classes which allowed them to strengthen the previous results in a number of interesting ways. They worked again in purely relational languages. A version for functional languages can be found in
(
Cieśliński 2017
)
. A version of Enayat–Visser construction covering languages with functional symbols with the regularity axioms included is discussed in
(
Łełyk and Wcisło 2021
)
. A reader may find a proof of a stronger result also in this article in Section
4
.

Theorem 3

(Kotlarski–Krajewski–Lachlan)
.

\textnormal{CT}^{-}
is conservative over
PA
, i.e., for any sentence
\phi\in\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}
if
\textnormal{CT}^{-}\vdash\phi
, then
\textnormal{PA}\vdash\phi
.

The above result contrasts with the situation for
CT
. By induction on the length of proofs, we can show that every proof in
PA
has true conclusion. In other words, we can prove in
CT
the following principle of
Global Reflection
,
GRef
:

\forall\phi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\Big(\textnormal{Pr}_{\textnormal{PA}}(\phi)\rightarrow T\phi\Big).

Notice that
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{GRef}
is clearly not conservative over
PA
, since in particular
\neg T(0\neq 0)
is provable in the compositional theory
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
and, by contraposition
\neg\textnormal{Pr}_{\textnormal{PA}}(0\neq 0)
. The latter is not provable in
PA
by Gödel’s second theorem.

As we have already mentioned, a number of seemingly unrelated principles turned out to be equivalent to this canonical nonconservative axiom
GRef
. One of them is
\Delta_{0}
-induction for the compositional truth predicate. Another principle which will play an important role in this article is
Propositional Reflection
,
PropRef
, defined as follows:

\forall\phi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\Big(\textnormal{Pr}^{T}_{\textnormal{Prop}}(\phi)\rightarrow T\phi\Big),

where
\textnormal{Pr}_{\textnormal{Prop}}^{T}(x)
means that
x
is derivable in propositional logic from the set of premises
\Gamma
, such that
T(y)
holds for each
y\in\Gamma
. As we have already noted,
Cieśliński 2010
showed that over
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
,
PropRef
is equivalent to
\Delta_{0}
induction for the truth predicate. Subsequently,
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
was shown by
Łełyk and Wcisło 2017
to be arithmetically equivalent to
GRef
and then, in
Łełyk 2017
, to be exactly the same theory as
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{GRef}
. Another presentation of the last result can be also found in a recent preprint
(
Łełyk 2021
)
.

Related to propositional reflection is the following principle of
Propositional Soundness
,
PropSnd
:

\forall\phi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\Big(\textnormal{Pr}_{\textnormal{Prop}}(\phi)\rightarrow T\phi\Big).

In effect, this axiom expresses that any arithmetical sentence which is a propositional tautology is true. It is still unknown whether
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{PropSnd}
is conservative over
PA
. In the next section, we shall present a partial result towards this problem.

Now, let us turn to the main subject of our article. If
M\models\textnormal{PA}
and
\bar{\phi}\in\textnormal{SentSeq}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
is a coded sequence of sentences, we can form their disjunction
\bigvee\bar{\phi}
, which we will also denote by
\bigvee_{i\leq c}\phi_{i}
if the length of
\bar{\phi}
is
c
. We always assume that in “big disjunctions” parentheses are grouped to the left, so that the following equality holds in
M
:

\bigvee_{i\leq c+1}\phi_{i}=\bigvee_{i\leq c}\phi_{i}\vee\phi_{c+1}.

In effect,
\bigvee_{i\leq c}\phi
denotes the following formula:

(((\phi_{0}\vee\phi_{1})\vee\ldots)\vee\phi_{c-1})\vee\phi_{c}.

The precise definition of how disjunctions over multiple disjuncts are parenthesised can actually matter in some cases. In the presence of
\Delta_{0}
-induction for the extended language, one can show that any two disjunctions with the same disjuncts are equivalent, no matter how the disjuncts are ordered and grouped together. However, this is not the case in pure
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
. The precise parenthesising can become relevant, since it dictates the relations between disjunctions over arbitrarily many disjuncts and the usual binary disjunctions. We will return to this issue in Section
5
.

By
Disjunctive Correctness
,
DC
, we mean the following axiom:

\forall\bar{\phi}\in\textnormal{SentSeq}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\bigg(T\left(\bigvee\bar{\phi}\right)\equiv\exists i\leq\textnormal{lh}(\bar{\phi})\ T\phi_{i}\bigg).

Therefore,
DC
states that a finite (but possibly nonstandard) disjunction is true iff it has a true disjunct. Very surprisingly,
DC
is yet another incarnation of
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
as shown by
Enayat and Pakhomov 2019
.

Theorem 4

(Enayat–Pakhomov)
.

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC}
and
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
are equivalent.

As we mentioned,
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
is not conservative over
PA
. Hence the following fact easily follows:

Corollary 5

(Enayat–Pakhomov)
.

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC}
is not conservative over
PA
.

Disjunctive correctness can be naturally split into two principles. The first is
DC-out
(“a true disjunction has a true disjunct”):

\forall\bar{\phi}\in\textnormal{SentSeq}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\Big(T\bigvee\bar{\phi}\rightarrow\exists i\leq\textnormal{lh}(\bar{\phi})\ T\phi_{i}\Big).

The second is
DC-in
(“a disjunction with a true disjunct is true”):

\forall\bar{\phi}\in\textnormal{SentSeq}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\Big(\exists i\leq\textnormal{lh}(\bar{\phi})\ T\phi_{i}\rightarrow T\bigvee\bar{\phi}\Big).

As we have mentioned, the status of
DC-out
was not settled in
(
Enayat and Pakhomov 2019
)
and the claim that
DC-in
is conservative was stated without a proof. The subsequent parts of this article will be devoted to the analysis of both principles.

Let us finish this section by summing up the positive results on equivalences of theories of truth relevant for this work:

Theorem 6
.

The following theories are equivalent:

1.

\textnormal{CT}_{0}
.

2.

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{GRef}
.

3.

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{PropRef}
.

4.

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC}
.

3
Yablo sequences and disjunctive correctness

In this section, we prove that
DC-out
is equivalent to
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
. The argument is indirectly inspired by the classical Visser--Yablo paradox:
7
7

7

See
(
Yablo 1993
)
.

Consider the sequence of sentences
Y_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
Y_{n}
says: “some sentence
Y_{k}
for
k>n
is false.” If for some
k
,
Y_{k}
is false, then every sentence
Y_{l}
for
l>k
is true. However, if
Y_{l}
is true, then there is some
m>l
such that
Y_{m}
is false contradicting the assumption. Hence, every sentence in the Yablo sequence is true. However, if some sequence in the Yablo sequence is true, then some has to be false, so they cannot be all true. We reach a contradiction.

Using a construction inspired by the Yablo sequence, we will show that
DC
-out implies
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
. We will actually use two intermediate principles which are overtly related to
\Delta_{0}
-induction. By
sequential induction
,
SInd
, we mean the following axiom:
8
8

8

A related principle of
Modus Ponens correctness
was introduced earlier by Ali Enayat in an unpublished note
(
Enayat 2014
)
. The principle states that if a conjunction of the implications
\phi_{i}\rightarrow\phi_{i+1}
is true for
i=0,1,\ldots,c
and
\phi_{0}
is true, then
\phi_{c+1}
is true. However, as stated, this principle is conservative over
PA
. Namely, it holds in any model in which all conjunctions of nonstandard length are false and by a construction very similar to that presented in Section
4
, for any completion
U
of
PA
, we can find models with this property satisfying
U
.

\forall s\in\textnormal{FinSeq}\ \Big(Ts_{0}\wedge\forall i<\textnormal{lh}(s)-1\big(Ts_{i}\rightarrow Ts_{i+1}\big)\rightarrow\forall j<\textnormal{lh}(s)\ Ts_{j}\Big)

The
sequential order induction
,
SOInd
, is a natural variant of the above principle:

\forall s\in\textnormal{FinSeq}\ \Big(\forall j<\textnormal{lh}(s)\big((\forall i<jTs_{i})\rightarrow Ts_{j}\big)\rightarrow\forall l<\textnormal{lh}(s)\ Ts_{l}\Big).

As we already mentioned,
SInd
and
SOInd
are clearly related to
\Delta_{0}
-induction:

Proposition 7
.

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{SOInd}
and
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
are equivalent.

Proof.

\textnormal{CT}_{0}
clearly entails
SOInd
. On the other hand, observe that
SOInd
implies
PropRef
which by Theorem
6
is equivalent to
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
. Working in
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{SOInd}
, fix a proof
(\phi_{0},\ldots,\phi_{c})
in propositional logic from true premises. We can assume that the proof system is chosen so that for all
i
, either
\phi_{i}
is an assumption of the proof or
\phi_{i}
is obtained by modus ponens from two formulas appearing earlier in the proof. In particular, for every
j
, if every formula
\phi_{j}
for
j<i
is true, then
\phi_{i}
is true which, by
SOInd
, implies that the conclusion of the proof is true. Thus
PropRef
holds.
∎

Now, we get to the core argument of the article:

Theorem 8
.

Over
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
,
DC-out
implies
SInd
.

Proof.

Working in
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC-out}
, fix any sequence
(\phi_{0},\ldots,\phi_{c})\in\textnormal{SentSeq}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}
such that
T\phi_{0}
holds and for each
i
,
T\phi_{i}
entails
T\phi_{i+1}
. Let us define a sequence
\psi_{i},i\leq c
as follows:

\displaystyle\psi_{0}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\phi_{0}

\displaystyle\psi_{j+1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\neg\phi_{j+1}\rightarrow\bigvee_{i<j+1}\neg\psi_{i}.

We claim that for all
j\leq c
, the sentence
\psi_{j}
is true. Suppose that
\neg T\psi_{j}
holds for some
j
. Then
j>0
, so we have:

\neg T\phi_{j}\wedge\neg T\bigvee_{i<j}\neg\psi_{i}.

By compositional conditions, this is equivalent to:

\neg T\phi_{j}\wedge\neg T\left(\bigvee_{i<j-1}\neg\psi_{i}\right)\wedge T\psi_{j-1}.

Expanding the definition of
\psi_{j-1}
, we obtain:

\neg T\phi_{j}\wedge\neg T\left(\bigvee_{i<j-1}\neg\psi_{i}\right)\wedge\left(T\phi_{j-1}\vee T\left(\bigvee_{i<j-1}\neg\psi_{i}\right)\right).

However,
\neg T\phi_{j}
implies
\neg T\phi_{j-1}
. Therefore, we have the following:

\neg T\phi_{j}\wedge\neg T\left(\bigvee_{i<j-1}\neg\psi_{i}\right)\wedge T\left(\bigvee_{i<j-1}\neg\psi_{i}\right).

This contradiction concludes the proof of the claim. Notice that the proof of the claim only uses the fact that provably in
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
,

T\left(\bigvee_{i\leq j+1}\eta_{i}\right)\equiv T\left(\bigvee_{i\leq j}\eta_{i}\right)\vee T\eta_{j+1}.

Now, we show that for all
j<c
,
\phi_{j}
is true. Suppose otherwise and fix
j
such that
\neg T\phi_{j}
. Since
T\psi_{j}
holds, we have:

T\bigvee_{i<j}\neg\psi_{i}

By
DC-out
, we can fix
i<j
such that
\neg T\psi_{i}
holds. However, this contradicts the previous claim.
∎

As an application of the above result, we show that
DC-out
is the same theory as
DC
. In particular,
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC-out}
is not conservative over
PA
.

Theorem 9
.

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{SInd}
implies
DC-in
. Consequently,
DC-out
and
DC
are equivalent over
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
.

Proof.

Working in
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{SInd}
fix any sequence
(\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{c})
such that
\alpha_{i}\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}
for all
i\leq c
. Suppose that
\alpha_{j}
is true for some
j\leq c
and notice that

\bigvee_{i\leq j}\alpha_{i}=\bigvee_{i\leq j-1}\alpha_{i}\vee\alpha_{j},

hence the disjunction of
\alpha_{i}
up to and including
j
has to be true as well. Moreover, for any
k
, if
\bigvee_{i\leq k}\alpha_{i}
is true, then
\bigvee_{i\leq k+1}\alpha_{i}
is true. Hence by sequential induction (starting with
\bigvee_{i\leq j}\alpha_{i}
),
T\bigvee_{i\leq c}\alpha_{i}
holds.
∎

Another application is a more perspicuous proof of nonconservativity of
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC}
.

Theorem 10
.

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC}
implies
SOInd
. Hence,
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC}
is equivalent to
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
.

Proof.

Working in
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC}
, fix any sequence
(\phi_{0},\ldots,\phi_{c})
and suppose that for any
j
, if
T\phi_{i}
holds for all
i<j
, then
T\phi_{j}
holds. By
DC
, the following implication holds for all
j\leq c
:

T\neg\bigvee_{i\leq j}\neg\phi_{i}\rightarrow T\neg\bigvee_{i\leq j+1}\neg\phi_{i}.

(Of course, these are essentially big conjunctions, but
prima facie
, disjunctive correctness does not imply conjunctive correctness.) Then, by
SInd
, we conclude that

T\neg\bigvee_{i\leq j}\neg\phi_{i}

holds for each
i
which, again using
DC
implies that
T\phi_{j}
holds for each
j
.

By Proposition
7
it follows that
\textnormal{CT}_{0}\subseteq\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC}
. Since
DC
is easily provable in
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
, we conclude that
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC}
is equivalent to
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
.
∎

Remark 11
.

The main nonconservativeness proof for
DC
in
(
Enayat and Pakhomov 2019
)
consists of two parts: it is first shown that over
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
,
DC
implies the axiom of internal induction
INT
, to be defined in the next section, and then a much more direct proof that
\textnormal{DC}+\textnormal{INT}
implies
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
follows. It is easy to verify that
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{SInd}

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC}
implies

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC}
is equivalent to
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
.

We can present the above results in a slightly more abstract manner. This will allow us to obtain a significantly simpler proof of the result from
(

)
on the strength of certain extensions of Propositional Soundness.

Definition 12
.

Let
U
be a theory extending
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
. We say that
U
has
outer disjunctions
if there exists a provably functional formula
D(x,y)
such that for any
\bar{\phi}=\langle\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{c}\rangle\in\textnormal{SentSeq}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}
, we have
D(\bar{\phi})\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}
and the following two properties hold provably in
U
:

•

\forall\bar{\phi}\in\textnormal{SentSeq}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\forall\psi\in\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\ \Big(TD(\bar{\phi}\frown\langle\psi\rangle)\equiv TD(\bar{\phi})\vee T\psi\Big).

•

\forall\bar{\phi}\in\textnormal{SentSeq}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\Big(TD(\bar{\phi})\rightarrow\exists i\leq\textnormal{lh}(\bar{\phi})\ T\phi_{i}\Big).

We call
D
as above an outer disjunction of
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{c}
.

In other words, a theory of truth has outer disjunctions if it has some uniform construction that behaves like disjunctions in
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC-out}
.

Proposition 13
.

Suppose that
U
has outer disjunctions. Then it satisfies
SOInd
and in particular, it contains
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
.

Sketch of the proof.

This is literally the same argument as in Theorems

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC-out}
has outer disjunctions.

By
quantifier-free correctness
,
QFC
, we mean the following axiom:

\forall\phi\in\textnormal{qfSent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\Big(\textnormal{Tr}_{0}(\phi)\rightarrow T\phi\Big).

Let us pause for a moment and explain the notation. The formula
\textnormal{qfSent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(x)
expresses that

is a quantifier-free sentence of
\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}

\textnormal{Tr}_{0}
is such a predicate for
\Delta_{0}
formulae.

Proposition 14

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{QFC}

The above proposition follows by a routine application of the methods introduced by Enayat and Visser, see e.g.

Proposition 15
.

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{QFC}+\textnormal{PropSnd}
has outer disjunctions. In particular, it is equivalent to
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
.

Proof.

The “in particular” part follows by Proposition
13
and the fact that
QFC
and
PropSnd
are clearly implied by
\Delta_{0}
-induction. So it is enough to show that
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{QFC}+\textnormal{PropSnd}
has outer disjunctions.

For
\bar{\phi}=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{c})
, let

D(\bar{\phi})=\exists x\leq c\bigvee_{i\leq c}\underline{i}=x\wedge\phi_{i}.

Let us check that
D
is an outer disjunction. First, consider the formula
D(\bar{\phi}\frown\langle\phi_{c+1}\rangle)
, i.e.

\exists x\leq c+1\bigvee_{i\leq c+1}\underline{i}=x\wedge\phi_{i}.

We want to check that it is true iff either
D(\bar{\phi})
is true or
\phi_{c+1}
is true. Suppose that

T\exists x\leq c+1\ \bigvee_{i\leq c+1}\underline{i}=x\wedge\phi_{i}.

By the compositional clauses and the definition of big disjunctions, this implies:

\exists x\leq c+1\ T\left(\bigvee_{i\leq c}\underline{i}=\underline{x}\wedge\phi_{i}\right)\vee\exists x\leq c+1\ T\left(\underline{c+1}=\underline{x}\wedge\phi_{c+1}\right).

It can be easily checked by compositional clauses that if the second clause holds, then
T\phi_{c+1}
holds. So it is enough to check that the first clause in fact implies

\exists x\leq c\ T\left(\bigvee_{i\leq c}\underline{i}=\underline{x}\wedge\phi_{i}\right).

To this end, we have to check that

\neg T\left(\bigvee_{i\leq c}\underline{i}=\underline{c+1}\wedge\phi_{i}\right).

However, notice that the following implication is an instance of a propositional tautology:

\left(\bigwedge_{i\leq c}\underline{i}\neq\underline{c+1}\right)\rightarrow\neg\left(\bigvee_{i\leq c}\underline{i}=\underline{c+1}\wedge\phi_{i}\right).

Hence, by
PropRef
, the whole implication is true and by
QFC
the antecedent is true as well and the conclusion follows by compositionality. This ends the proof of the implication.

The verification that
TD(\bar{\phi})\vee T\phi_{c+1}
implies
TD(\bar{\phi}\frown\langle\phi_{c+1}\rangle)
is similar, but simpler, as it only uses compositional clauses of
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
.

Now, suppose that the following holds:

T\exists x\leq c\bigvee_{i\leq c}\underline{i}=x\wedge\phi_{i}.

We want to check that for some
i\leq c
,
T\phi_{i}
holds. By compositional clauses, we know that there exists
a\leq c
such that:

T\bigvee_{i\leq c}\underline{i}=\underline{a}\wedge\phi_{i}.

Again, using
QFC
and
PropSnd
, we check that the following holds:

T\left(\neg\phi_{a}\rightarrow\neg\bigvee_{i\leq c}\underline{i}=\underline{a}\wedge\phi_{i}\right).

Thus we can conclude that
T\phi_{a}
holds which ends the proof.
∎

Remark 16
.

In the above proof, we could actually show that the formula
D(\bar{\phi})
satisfies both directions of
DC
.

The principle

clearly looks very related to
\Delta_{0}
-induction for the truth predicate. Indeed, it turns out that over
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
the two principles are equivalent.

Theorem 17
.

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{SInd}
has outer disjunctions.

Proof.

For a coded sequence
(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{k})
of
\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}
-sentences, let
\bigwedge_{i\leq c}\phi_{i}
be their conjunction with parentheses grouped to the left so that we have:

\bigwedge_{i\leq c+1}\phi_{i}=\bigwedge_{i\leq c}\phi_{i}\wedge\phi_{c+1}.

Using
SInd
, we can show that if
T\phi_{i}
holds for every
i\leq c
, then
T\bigwedge_{i\leq c}\phi_{i}
. We show this by considering an auxiliary sequence

\bigwedge_{i\leq 1}\phi_{i},\bigwedge_{i\leq 2}\phi_{i},\ldots,\bigwedge_{i\leq c}\phi_{i}.

Now, we claim that the formula

D(\bar{\phi}):=\neg\bigvee_{i\leq c}\neg\phi_{i}

is an outer disjunction. It is easy to verify that over
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
:

TD(\bar{\phi}\frown\langle\phi_{c+1}\rangle)\equiv TD(\bar{\phi})\vee T\phi_{c+1}.

We show that
D
satisfies the second condition of outer disjunctions (
DC-out
) by contraposition. If there is no
i\leq c
such that
T\phi_{i}
, then
T\neg\phi_{i}
holds for every
i
. Consequently, the following conjunction is true:

\bigwedge_{i\leq c}\neg\phi_{i}

which implies that
D\bar{\phi}
cannot be true. This shows that
D
is indeed outer disjunction provably in
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{SInd}
.
∎

Corollary 18
.

The following theories are equivalent:

•

\textnormal{CT}_{0}

•

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{SInd}

•

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{SOInd}
.

•

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC-out}
.

We conclude this section with some results that clarify the status of
SInd
and
SOInd
. First of all, let us note that
SInd
and
SOInd
are really just some forms of induction axioms and as such they do not require us to assume
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
to make sense. They both clearly follow from
\textnormal{I}\Delta_{0}(T)
, the induction scheme for
\Delta_{0}
-formulae containing the predicate
T
(which is now treated just as some arbitrary predicate). It turns out that these principles form a strict hierarchy.

Theorem 19
.

Over
PA
,
\textnormal{I}\Delta_{0}(T)\rightarrow\textnormal{SOInd}\rightarrow\textnormal{SInd}
. Moreover, none of the implication reverses.

Proof.

Both implications are straightforward, so let us show that neither reverses.

(\textnormal{SOInd}\nvdash\textnormal{I}\Delta_{0}(T))
. Let
M
be a countable nonstandard model of
PA
. Let
s_{i},i<\omega
be an enumeration of all coded sequences in
M
. We will inductively construct a sequence of finite subsets of the standard cut
\omega^{M}
,
A_{i},B_{i},i<\omega
such that for all
i
,
A_{i}\cap B_{i}=\emptyset
. The sets
A
are approximations to
T
, the sets
B
are approximations to the complement.

Let
A_{0}=B_{0}=\emptyset
. For an arbitrary
i<\omega
, we consider two cases: if all the values of
s_{i}
are elements of
A_{i}
, i.e.:

\{a\in M\ \mid\ \exists j<\textnormal{lh}(s_{i})\ a=s_{i}(j)\}\subseteq A_{i},

then we set
A_{i+1}=A_{i},B_{i+1}=B_{i}
. Otherwise, let
j
be the least element such that
s_{i}(j)\notin A_{i}
(this element exists, since
|A_{i}|
is standard, and therefore,
A_{i}
is arithmetically definable). Let
b=s_{i}(j)
, let
a\neq b
be an arbitrary element in
\omega\setminus(A_{i}\cup B_{i})
and set:

\displaystyle A_{i+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{i}\cup\{a\}

\displaystyle B_{i+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{i}\cup\{b\}.

Finally, we set
T=\bigcup_{i\in\omega}A_{i}
. We claim that
(M,T)\models\textnormal{SOInd}
, but
(M,T)
does not satisfy
\textnormal{I}\Delta_{0}(T).
The latter claim holds, since by construction
T
is an infinite (hence cofinal) subset of the standard cut
\omega
. To check that the first claim holds, fix any coded sequence
s
. Pick
i<\omega
such that
s=s_{i}
. By construction, either all values of
s_{i}
are in
A_{i}
, hence in
T
or there exists an

such that for all

, but
s_{i}(l)\in B_{i+1}
(which means that it is not in
T
). This shows that
(M,T)\models\textnormal{SOInd}
.

(
\textnormal{SInd}\nvdash\textnormal{SOInd}
) As before, fix an arbitrary countable nonstandard model
M\models\textnormal{PA}
and let
s_{i},i<\omega
be an (external) enumeration of coded sequences from
M
. We inductively construct two sequences of sets
A_{i},B_{i}
such that for all
i
,
A_{i}\cap B_{i}=\emptyset
, the sets
B_{i}
are finite, and the sets
A_{i}
contain only finitely many nonstandard elements of
M
.

We construct
A_{i},B_{i}
as follows: let
A_{0}=\omega^{M}
(the standard initial cut of
M
) and
B_{0}=\emptyset
.

For an arbitrary
i
, if the set of values of
s_{i}
is (standardly) finite, we set
A_{i+1}=A_{i},B_{i+1}=B_{i}
. Otherwise, we consider two further subcases. Suppose that the set of
j
such that
s_{i}(j)\in B_{i}
is an initial segment in
M
(possibly empty). Let
j_{0}
be its supremum (it exists by arithmetical induction, since
B_{i}
is finite; if the considered set is empty, then by definition, its supremum is
0
). On the other hand, the set of values of
s_{i}
is by assumption infinite, so by overspill there exists
j>j_{0}
such that
s_{i}(j)\notin A_{i}
. Let
a:=s_{i}(j-1)
and let
b:=s_{i}(j)
.

If the set
\{j<\textnormal{lh}(s)\ \mid\ s_{i}(j)\in B_{i}\}
is not an initial segment of
M
, then (again by arithmetical induction and finiteness of
B_{i}
) there exists some
j
such that
s_{i}(j)\notin B_{i}
and
s_{i}(j+1)\in B
. Let
a:=s_{i}(j),b:=s_{i}(j+1)
.

In both cases, set:

\displaystyle A_{i+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{i}\cup\{a\}

\displaystyle B_{i+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{i}\cup\{b\}.

Finally, let
T=\bigcup_{i\in\omega}A_{i}
. We claim that
(M,T)\models\textnormal{SInd}
, but not

.

To see that
(M,T)\models\textnormal{SInd}
, fix any coded sequence
s\in M
. If the set of values of

has (standard) finite number of elements, then

cannot violate the sequential induction axiom for
s
. So suppose that the number of values of

is nonstandard. Fix

such that
s=s_{i}
in our enumeration. By construction, there exists

such that

and

and thus the sequential induction axiom for

is satisfied.

On the other hand,
(M,T)
does not satisfy

. This

4
Conservativeness of
DC-in

In the previous section, we have shown that
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC-out}
is not conservative over
PA
and that, in fact, it is another incarnation of
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
. Now we will use methods introduced by
Enayat and Visser 2015
to show that the related principle
DC-in
is actually conservative over
PA
. The Enayat–Visser technique typically allows us to combine various results, so we can show joint conservativity of several distinct principles. Here we will illustrate this point by requiring that the constructed truth predicate additionally satisfies internal induction. Let us recall that principle. If
(M,T)\models\textnormal{CT}^{-}
, then each formula
\phi\in\textnormal{Form}^{\leq 1}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
“defines” a set
\{x\in M\ \mid\ \phi(\underline{x})\in T\}
.
Internal induction

(\textnormal{INT})
expresses that each such set satisfies the induction principle:

\forall\phi\in\textnormal{Form}^{\leq 1}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\Big(T\phi(\underline{0})\wedge\forall x\big(T\phi(\underline{x})\rightarrow T\phi(\underline{x+1})\big)\rightarrow\forall x\ T\phi(\underline{x})\Big).

It was essentially observed already by
Kotlarski et al. 1981
that
\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{INT}
is conservative over
PA
. This result can be also proved using cut elimination as in
(
Leigh 2015
)
or the methods invented by Enayat and Visser which we will use in this section in order to show the following theorem:

Theorem 20
.

\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{DC-in}+\textnormal{INT}
is conservative over
PA
.

The theorem is a direct corollary to the following model-theoretic result:

Theorem 21
.

Let
M\models\textnormal{PA}
. Then there exists
M^{\prime}\succeq M
and a
T^{\prime}\subseteq M^{\prime}
such that
(M^{\prime},T^{\prime})\models\textnormal{CT}^{-}+\textnormal{INT}
and for all sequences
\bar{\phi}\in\textnormal{SentSeq}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
of nonstandard length, the disjunction
\bigvee\bar{\phi}
is in
T^{\prime}
.

In other words, we will construct a model in which every disjunction with infinitely many disjuncts is true. Such a model clearly satisfies
DC-in
: fix any sequence of sentences
\bar{\phi}
and suppose that there is
i\leq\textnormal{lh}(\bar{\phi})
such that
T^{\prime}\phi_{i}
. If
\bar{\phi}
has nonstandard length, then
\bigvee\bar{\phi}\in T^{\prime}
by assumption. On the other hand, if the length of
\bar{\phi}
is standard, then
\bigvee\bar{\phi}\in T^{\prime}
directly by the compositional axioms. So it is enough to prove Theorem
21
.

In the proof, we will construct an elementary sequence of models
M_{i}\models\textnormal{PA}
and a sequence of satisfaction classes
S_{i}\subset M_{i}^{2},i<\omega
such that
S_{i+1}
will satisfy compositional clauses for formulae in the model
M_{i}
. Let us first define what a satisfaction class actually is. Below, if
s
is (a code of) a term and
\alpha
is a function whose domain contains the free variables of
s
(an
s
-assignment), by
s^{\alpha}
we mean the formally computed value of
s
under the valuation
\alpha
. For instance, if
\alpha
ascribes the value
2
to
x
and
5
to
y
, then

\mathbb{N}\models\ulcorner SSx\times Sy\urcorner^{\alpha}=24.

If
\alpha,\beta
are assignments and
v
is a variable, then by
\beta\sim_{v}\alpha
we mean that
\textnormal{dom}(\beta)\supseteq\textnormal{dom}(\alpha)\cup\{v\}
and
\beta(w)=\alpha(w)
for every
w\in\textnormal{dom}(\alpha)\setminus\{v\}
. In other words,
\beta
is just like
\alpha
, possibly except for the value
\beta(v)
which is not even required to be defined for
\alpha
.

Definition 22
.

Let
M\models\textnormal{PA}
and let
\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
. By the
compositional clauses
for
\phi
,
\textnormal{Comp}(\phi)
, we mean the disjunction of the following sentences:

1.

\exists s,t\in\textnormal{Term}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\ \Big(\phi=(s=t)\wedge\forall\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)\ \Big(S(\phi,\alpha)\equiv s^{\alpha}=t^{\alpha}\Big)\Big).

2.

\exists\psi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\ \Big(\phi=(\neg\psi)\wedge\forall\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)\ \Big(S(\phi,\alpha)\equiv\neg S(\psi,\alpha)\Big)\Big).

3.

\exists\psi,\eta\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)\ \Big(\phi=(\psi\vee\eta)\wedge\forall\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)\Big(S(\phi,\alpha)\equiv S(\psi,\alpha)\vee S(\eta,\alpha)\Big)\Big).

4.

\exists\psi,\eta\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)\ \Big(\phi=(\psi\wedge\eta)\wedge\forall\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)\Big(S(\phi,\alpha)\equiv S(\psi,\alpha)\wedge S(\eta,\alpha)\Big)\Big).

5.

\exists\psi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)\exists v\in\textnormal{Var}\ \Big(\phi=(\exists v\psi)\wedge\forall\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)\Big(S(\phi,\alpha)\equiv\exists\beta\sim_{v}\alpha S(\psi,\beta)\Big)\Big).

6.

\exists\psi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)\exists v\in\textnormal{Var}\ \Big(\phi=(\forall v\psi)\wedge\forall\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)\Big(S(\phi,\alpha)\equiv\forall\beta\sim_{v}\alpha\ S(\psi,\beta)\Big)\Big).

We say that
S\subset M^{2}
is a
satisfaction class
if there exists a subset
D\subseteq\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
such that the following holds:

•

If
(\phi,\alpha)\in S
, then
\phi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
and
\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)
.

•

If
(\phi,\alpha)\in S
for some
\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)
, then
\textnormal{Comp}(\phi)
holds.

•

If
(\phi,\alpha)\in S
, then
\phi\in D
or
\phi=\neg\psi
for some
\psi\in D
.

•

If
\phi\in D
, then
\textnormal{Comp}(\phi)
holds.

•

If
\phi\in D
and
\psi
is a direct subformula of
\phi
, then
\psi\in D
.

•

If
\phi\in D
, then for all
\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)
,
(\phi,\alpha)\in S
or
(\neg\phi,\alpha)\in S.

By the
domain
of
S
,
\textnormal{dom}(S)
, we mean the maximal set
D
satisfying the above conditions (the maximality requirement is needed, as there is a slight ambiguity in how to count negations of formulae which are not satisfied by any assignment). A satisfaction class is
full
iff its domain is the whole set
\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
.

The definition of a satisfaction class presented above involves some technical requirements which might seem to be slightly too restrictive, so let us briefly explain our motivations. The definition of a satisfaction class completely agrees with the usual one in the case of full satisfaction classes. However, if
S
is not a full satisfaction class, i.e., if it does not satisfy compositional conditions for all formulae, it becomes ambiguous whether we should interpret the fact that
(\phi,\alpha)\notin S
as saying that
\phi
is not satisfied under the valuation
\alpha
or that
S
simply does not decide
\phi
which might be a technical nuisance in some proofs or statements of results. This includes results and arguments in this article, though the specific difficulties we overcome with this definition will not be really visible. Therefore, we essentially require that for every formula
\phi
we can unambiguously tell whether it is decided by
S
or not. This requirement is harmless: Define a pre-satisfaction class on
M
as a set
S\subset M^{2}
such that it satisfies compositional axioms on some set
D
of formulae closed under direct subformulae and does not contain any pair
(\phi,\alpha)
for
\phi\notin D
. Then for any pre-satisfaction class, we can canonically define a satisfaction class extending it. Simply take the maximal set
D
with the two mentioned properties and extend
S
with all pairs
(\neg\phi,\alpha)
such that
\phi\in D
and
(\phi,\alpha)\notin S.
One can check that after such a one-step extension, we obtain a satisfaction class in our sense.

Satisfaction classes and truth predicates satisfying
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
are very closely related objects. However, the link between them is not as direct as one could hope (see
(
Wcisło 2021
)
for a discussion of this connection). We have to introduce a certain technical condition in order to switch between them in a completely unproblematic manner.

Let
M\models\textnormal{PA}
,
\phi,\psi\in\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(M)
,
\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi),\beta\in\textnormal{Asn}(\psi)
. We say that the pairs
(\phi,\alpha),(\psi,\beta)
are
extensionally equivalent
,
(\phi,\alpha)\simeq(\psi,\beta)
if there exists a formula
\eta
and two sequences of closed terms
\bar{s},\bar{t}\in\textnormal{ClTermSeq}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}
of the same length such that the sequences of their values are equal and

\phi[\alpha]=\eta(\bar{s}),\psi[\beta]=\eta(\bar{t}),

where
\phi[\alpha]
is a sentence obtained by substituting in
\phi
the numeral
\underline{\alpha(v)}
for each variable
v
. For instance, let
\phi=\exists x(x+y=SS0)
,
\psi=\exists x(x+u\times v=w+S0)
, and let
\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi),\beta\in\textnormal{Asn}(\psi)
be such that
\alpha(y)=2,\beta(u)=2,\beta(v)=\beta(w)=1
. Then
(\phi,\alpha)\simeq(\psi,\beta)
as witnessed by the formula

and the terms

Remark 27
.

As we have already pointed out, Enayat–Visser methods of building satisfaction classes typically allow us to combine various conservativeness results. For instance, in Theorem
21

It might appear that since a class we build in the proof of this theorem is somewhat pathological, there are certain obvious limits to what truth-theoretic principles can be additionally satisfied. For instance, if the constructed truth predicate makes true all infinite disjunctions, then it cannot agree with
\Sigma_{n}
arithmetical truth predicates on sentences from the respective syntactic classes. However, as pointed out by Ali Enayat, we could still construct a model in which the truth predicate

agrees with all the usual partial arithmetical

-truth predicates and

is satisfied. It is enough that in the proof of Lemma

we change the definition of

so that for a minimal class

, where

is a
\Sigma_{n}
-formula for some standard

which is not in the domain of

,

agrees with the partial arithmetical satisfaction predicates. An alternative approach, mixing an Enayat–Visser approach and resplendence (by building a satisfaction class from recursively saturated partial classes) was proposed in an unpublished note by

.

Alternative versions of

In our paper, we defined

((\ldots(\phi_{0}\vee\phi_{1})\vee\ldots)\vee\phi_{c-1})\vee\phi_{c}

This point has been partially addressed in the definition of outer disjunctions: our proof of nonconservativity of

TD(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{c},\phi_{c+1})\equiv TD(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{c})\vee T\phi_{c+1}.

(*)

It turns out that this assumption cannot be weakened much further. Let us define

of a sequence of formulae
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{c}

by recursion in the following way:

\displaystyle B(\emptyset)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle 0\neq 0

\displaystyle B(\phi_{1})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\phi_{1}

\displaystyle B(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{c})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle B(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{\llcorner c/2\lrcorner})\vee B(\phi_{\llcorner c/2\lrcorner+1},\ldots,\phi_{c}).

In other words, we are grouping disjunctions of

so that the parentheses form a binary tree. Over

5

6
The role of the regularity axiom

In Section
2
, we briefly mentioned that we have certain technical reasons to include Regularity,
REG
among the axioms for the compositional truth. Let us now explain why we adopt this principle and how this choice affects our results.

First of all, without
REG
, we have to be careful about the exact formulation of the compositional axioms for quantifiers. Our basic choice is between two options. We can require that, say, an existential statement
\exists v\phi(v)
is true iff some sentence obtained by substituting a numeral in
\phi(v)
is true. This amounts to adopting quantifier axioms in the form which we have chosen in this article:

T\exists v\phi(v)\equiv\exists x\ T\phi(\underline{x}).

The second option is to require that an existential statement is true iff a sentence obtained by substituting some closed arithmetical term for the quantified variable is true. Then, the quantifier axioms would have a form:

T\exists v\phi(v)\equiv\exists t\in\textnormal{ClTerm}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\ T\phi(t).

Note that these two versions of the quantifier axiom are not immediately comparable with respect to their strength. The left-to-right implication is stronger in the numeral version:

T\exists v\phi(v)\rightarrow\exists xT\phi(\underline{x})

whereas the reverse implication is formally stronger for the term version:

\exists t\in\textnormal{ClTerm}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}\ T\phi(t)\rightarrow T\exists v\phi(v).

The term version was chosen, for instance, by
Halbach 2011
. The numeral version appears, e.g., in
(
Friedman and Sheard 1987
)
or
(
Horsten and Leigh 2017
)
. This choice, in turn, may have a significant bearing on our results. Most importantly, we do not know whether adding
\Delta_{0}
-induction to a version of
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
whose compositional axioms for quantifiers involve terms results in a non-conservative extension of
PA
. So, when regularity is missing, some of the main results in this article will depend on a technical choice in the formulation of compositional axioms which may lead to some further confusions.

Another issue is that without regularity, there is a mismatch between the notion of a truth predicate as discussed in the philosophical literature and the notion of satisfaction classes, as discussed in the literature on models of
PA
(e.g., as in
(
Kaye 1991
)
). There is a direct link between the compositional truth and satisfaction classes assuming that we include some form of extensionality conditions in both these cases. (A more comprehensive discussion of why these kind of assumptions are relevant can be found in
(
Wcisło 2021
)
.) This link is important, as some techniques used in our proofs are designed to work specifically in the context of satisfaction classes. Most importantly, the conservativity arguments using the Enayat–Visser technique which we employ in Section
4
do not really work when directly applied to truth classes. Therefore, we would most likely need to add regularity assumptions to statements of some technical lemmas which could make for a potentially awkward reading.

Finally, let us discuss the impact of
REG
on the results in this article. The main results in Section
4
, namely Theorems
20
and
21
are also true for
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
without the regularity axiom, as the latter versions are formally weaker. The same applies to Remark
27
, Proposition
14
, and Theorem
28
.

The situation of the results in Section
3
is slightly more complicated. As we have already mentioned, Theorem
6
is only known to hold if we consider a version of
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
in which the numeral variant of the compositional quantifier axioms is assumed. In this case, all results from Section
3
, still hold. Unfortunately, this is not necessarily true for the term variant. Let us discuss in some detail what can be salvaged in this scenario.

Theorem
8
does not depend at all on quantifier axioms and thus it holds without assuming regularity in either version of
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
; similarly for Theorem
9
. The first part of Theorem
10
and Theorem
17
also hold true. It is clear that
SOInd
implies
PropRef
for
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
in the term version as well. Moreover,
PropRef
clearly implies
DC
using just compositional axioms for boolean connectives. However, it is unclear whether
PropRef
and
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
are equivalent without assuming
REG
and with the quantifier axioms in the term version. Similarly, the first part of Proposition
13
does not depend of regularity or the quantifier axioms, but we do not know whether such theories contain
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
. Proposition
15
may be entirely false if we do not assume the numeral version of the quantifier axioms, as the proof relies significantly on that assumption.

To sum it up: by the results of Section
3
,
\textnormal{DC-out},\textnormal{DC},\textnormal{SInd}
,
SOInd
, and
PropRef
are pairwise equivalent over any version of
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
without assuming regularity. They are equivalent to
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
and not conservative over
PA
if we consider
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
with quantifier axioms for numerals. If we consider
\textnormal{CT}^{-}
with the term variant of the quantifier axioms, we do not know either whether
DC
or
PropRef
are equivalent to
\textnormal{CT}_{0}
, or whether the latter theory is arithmetically stronger than
PA
.

Appendix: a glossary of formalised notions

Throughout the article, we referred to a number of formalised notions and used some rather technical notation. Let us now gather it in a glossary for the convenience of the reader.

•

\alpha\sim_{v}\beta
means that
\textnormal{dom}(\beta)\supseteq\textnormal{dom}(\alpha)\cup\{v\}
and
\beta(w)=\alpha(w)
for every
w\in\textnormal{dom}(\alpha)\setminus\{v\}
.

•

{t}^{\circ}
. If
t
is (a Gödel code of) a closed arithmetical term, then
{t}^{\circ}
is the value of a term (whose Gödel code is)
t
. We use the same expression to denote the formalised version of this function.

•

t^{\alpha}
. If
t
is (a Gödel code of) an arithmetical term, and
\alpha
is a
t
-assignment, then
t^{\alpha}
is the value of term
t
under the assignment
\alpha
. We use the same expression to denote the formalised version of this function.

•

{\bar{t}}^{\circ}
. If
\bar{t}
is a sequence of (Gödel codes of) closed arithmetical terms, then
{\bar{t}}^{\circ}
is the sequence of their values. We also use this expression to denote the formalised version of this function.

•

\underline{x}
is (a Gödel code of) a canonical numeral denoting the number
x
. We also use this expression to denote the formalised version of this function.

•

\phi[\alpha]
. If
\phi
is a formula and
\alpha\in\textnormal{Asn}(\phi)
, then by
\phi[\alpha]
we mean a sentence obtained by substituting in
\phi
the numeral
\underline{\alpha(v)}
for each variable
v
. We also use this expression to denote the corresponding formalised notion.

•

\textnormal{Asn}(x)
is a set of
x
-assignments, that is, functions whose domain contains the set of free variables of
x
, where
x
is a term, a formula, or a sequence thereof. We also use this expression to denote the corresponding formalised notion.

•

\textnormal{ClTerm}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(x)
is a formula expressing “
x
is (a Gödel code of) a closed arithmetical term.” (That is, a term with no free variables.)

•

\textnormal{ClTermSeq}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(x)
is a formula expressing “
x
is a sequence of (Gödel codes of) closed arithmetical terms.”

•

\textnormal{FinSeq}(x)
is a formula expressing “
x
is a finite sequence of numbers.”

•

\textnormal{Form}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(x)
is a formula expressing “
x
is (a Gödel code of) an arithmetical formula.”

•

\textnormal{Form}^{\leq 1}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}
is a formula expressing “
x
is (a Gödel code of) an arithmetical formula with at most one free variable.”

•

\textnormal{FormSeq}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(x)
is a formula expressing “
x
is a sequence of (Gödel codes of) arithmetical formulae.”

•

\textnormal{lh}(s)=x
is a formula expressing “
s
is a sequence and its length is
x
.”

•

\textnormal{Pr}_{\textnormal{PA}}(x)
is a formula expressing “
x
is (a Gödel code of) an arithmetical sentence provable in
PA
.”

•

\textnormal{Pr}_{\textnormal{Prop}}(x)
is a formula expressing “
x
is (a Gödel code of) an arithmetical sentence which is provable in pure propositional logic.”

•

\textnormal{Pr}^{T}_{\textnormal{Prop}}(x)
is a formula expressing “
x
is (a Gödel code of) an arithmetical sentence which is provable in propositional logic from the set of premises
\Gamma
such that
T(y)
holds for all
y\in\Gamma
.”

•

\textnormal{qfSent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(x)
is a formula expressing “
x
is (a Gödel code of) a quantifier-free arithmetical sentence.”

•

\textnormal{Sent}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(x)
is a formula expressing “
x
is (a Gödel code of) an arithmetical sentence.”

•

\textnormal{SentSeq}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(x)
is a formula expressing “
x
is a sequence of (Gödel codes of) arithmetical sentences.”

•

\textnormal{SentSet}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(x)
is a formula expressing “
x
is a finite set of (Gödel codes of) arithmetical sentences.”

•

\textnormal{Term}_{\mathscr{L}_{\textnormal{PA}}}(x)
is a formula expressing “
x
is (a Gödel code of) an arithmetical term.”

•

\textnormal{Tr}_{0}
is the arithmetical truth predicate for
\Delta_{0}
-formulae.

•

\textnormal{Var}(x)
is a formula expressing “
x
is (a Gödel code of) a first-order variable.”

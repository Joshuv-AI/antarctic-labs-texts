1.
Introduction

Let
F_{d}
be a class of continuous functions on
D_{d}
, where
D_{d}
is a
compact subset of the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with a probability measure
\rho
. The integral of a continuous function
f:\,F_{d}\rightarrow\,{R}
denotes by

(1.1)

{\rm INT}_{d}(f)=\int_{D_{d}}f(x)d\rho(x).

We want to approximate this integral
{\rm INT}_{d}(f)
by
(deterministic) algorithms of the form

A_{n}(f):=\varphi_{n}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}),\dots,f(x_{n})),

where
x_{j}\in D_{d}
can be chosen adaptively and
\varphi_{n}:{R}^{n}\rightarrow{R}

is an arbitrary mapping. Adaption means that the selection of

x_{j}
may depend on the already computed values
f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}),\dots,f(x_{j-1})
. We denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\rm det}

the class of all algorithms of this form. If
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
are
fixed and
\varphi_{n}
is linear, i.e.,

A_{n}(f)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\lambda}_{j}f(x_{j}),\ \ {\lambda}_{j}\in{R},\ j=1,\dots,n,

then the algorithm
A_{n}
is called a linear
algorithm. Such linear algorithm
A_{n}
is also called a quadrature
formula. We say that a quadrature formula
A_{n}
is positive if

{\lambda}_{j}>0,\,j=1,\dots,n
.

The deterministic case error of
A_{n}
on
F_{d}
is given by

e^{\rm det}(F_{d},A_{n}):=\sup\limits_{f\in F_{d}}|{\rm INT}_{d}(f)-A_{n}(f)|,

and the minimal (optimal) deterministic case error on
F_{d}
given by

e_{n}^{\rm det}(F_{d}):=\inf\limits_{A_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\rm det}}e^{\rm det}(F_{d},A_{n}).

It was well known (see
[
3
]
) that if
F_{d}
is convex and
balanced, then
e_{n}^{\rm det}(F_{d})
can be achieved by linear
algorithms. Hence
e_{n}^{\rm det}(F_{d})
is also called the
optimal quadrature error.

Randomized algorithms, called also Monte-Carlo algorithms, are
understood as
\Sigma\otimes\mathcal{B}(F_{d})
measurable
functions

(A^{\omega})=(A^{\omega}(\cdot))_{{\omega}\in\Omega}:\Omega\times F_{d}\to{R},

where
\mathcal{B}(F_{d})
denotes Borel
\sigma
-algebra
of
F_{d}
,
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
is a suitable
probability space, and for any fixed
{\omega}\in\Omega
,
A^{\omega}
is a
deterministic method with cardinality
n(f,{\omega})
. The number

n(f,{\omega})
may be randomized and adaptively depend on the input,
and the cardinality of
(A^{\omega})
is then defined by

{\rm Card}(A^{\omega}):=\sup_{f\in F_{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\,n(f,{\omega}):=\sup\limits_{f\in F_{d}}\int_{\Omega}n(f,\omega)d\mathcal{P}(\omega).

We denote by
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\rm ran}
the class of
all randomized algorithms with cardinality not exceeding
n
.

The randomized case error of
(A^{\omega})
on
F_{d}
is defined by

e^{\rm ran}(F_{d},(A^{\omega})):=\sup\limits_{f\in F_{d}}{E}_{\omega}|{\rm INT}_{d}(f)-A^{\omega}(f)|,

and the minimal (optimal) randomized case error on
F_{d}
is defined by

e_{n}^{\rm ran}(F_{d}):=\inf\limits_{(A^{\omega})\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\rm ran}}e^{\rm ran}(F_{d},(A^{\omega})).

There are many papers devoted to investigating the integration
problem (
1.1
) in the deterministic and randomized case
settings. Compared to deterministic algorithms, randomized
algorithms may speed up the order of convergence in many cases,
especially for integration problem. We recall some known results.

Throughout the paper, the notation

a_{n}\asymp b_{n}
means
a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}
and
a_{n}\gtrsim b_{n}
. Here,
a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}\,(a_{n}\gtrsim b_{n})
means
that there exists a constant
c>0
independent of
n
such that

a_{n}\leq cb_{n}\,(b_{n}\leq ca_{n})
.

(1) Consider the classical Sobolev class
BW_{p}^{r}([0,1]^{d})
,

1\leq p\leq\infty
,
r\in{N}
, defined by

BW_{p}^{r}([0,1]^{d})=\big\{f\in L_{p}([0,1]^{d})\,\big|\,\sum_{|\alpha|_{1}\leq r}\|D^{\alpha}f\|_{p}\leq 1\big\},

and the Hölder class
C^{k,{\gamma}}_{d}
,
k\in{N}_{0}
,

0<{\gamma}\leq 1
, defined by

C^{k,\gamma}_{d}:=\big\{f\in C([0,1]^{d})\,\big|\,|D^{\alpha}f(x)-D^{\alpha}f(y)|\leq\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq d}|x_{i}-y_{i}|^{\gamma},\,|{\alpha}|_{1}=k\big\},

where
{\alpha}\in{N}_{0}^{d},\ |\alpha|_{1}:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}
, and
D^{\alpha}f
is the
partial derivative of order
{\alpha}
of
f
in the sense of
distribution. Bakhvalov in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
proved that

e_{n}^{\rm det}(C^{k,{\gamma}}_{d})\asymp n^{-\frac{k+{\gamma}}{d}}\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ e_{n}^{\rm det}(BW_{\infty}^{r}([0,1]^{d}))\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d}}.

Novak extended the second equivalence result in
[
24
]
and
[
25
]
, and proved that for
1\leq p<\infty
and
r>d/p
,

e_{n}^{\rm det}(BW_{p}^{r}([0,1]^{d}))\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d}}.

Meanwhile, Novak considered the randomized case errors of the above two classes in
[
24
]
and
[
25
]
, and proved that

e_{n}^{\rm ran}(C^{k,{\alpha}}_{d})\asymp n^{-\frac{k+{\alpha}}{d}-\frac{1}{2}},

and for
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
r>d/p
,

e_{n}^{\rm ran}(BW_{p}^{r}([0,1]^{d}))\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}},

where

a_{+}=\max(a,0)
.

(2) Consider the anisotropic Sobolev class

BW_{p}^{{\bf r}}([0,1]^{d}),\ 1\leq p\leq\infty,\ {\bf r}=(r_{1},\cdots,r_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, defined by

BW_{p}^{{\bf r}}([0,1]^{d})=\big\{f\in L_{p}([0,1]^{d})\,\big|\,\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big\|\frac{\partial^{r_{j}}f}{\partial x_{j}^{r_{j}}}\big\|_{p}\leq 1\big\}.

Fang and Ye in
[
13
]

obtained for
g({\bf r})>d/{p}
,

e_{n}^{\rm det}(BW_{p}^{{\bf r}}([0,1]^{d}))\asymp n^{-g({\bf r})},

and for
g({\bf r})>1/{p}
,

e_{n}^{\rm ran}(BW_{p}^{{\bf r}}([0,1]^{d}))\asymp n^{-g({\bf r})-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}},

where
g({\bf r})=\big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}r_{j}^{-1}\big)^{-1}
. For the anisotropic
Hölder-Nikolskii classes, Fang and Ye obtained the similar
results in
[
13
]
.

(3) Consider the Sobolev class with bounded mixed derivative

BW_{p}^{r,\rm mix}([0,1]^{d}),\ r\in\mathbb{N},\ 1\leq p\leq\infty
, defined by

BW_{p}^{r,\rm mix}([0,1]^{d})=\big\{f\in L_{p}([0,1]^{d})\,\big|\,\sum_{|\alpha|_{\infty}\leq r}\|D^{\alpha}f\|_{p}\leq 1\big\},

where

|\alpha|_{\infty}:=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq d}{\alpha}_{i}
. The authors
in
[
5
,
14
,
31
,
32
]
obtained for
r>1/{p}
and
1<p<\infty
,

e_{n}^{\rm det}(BW_{p}^{r,\rm mix}([0,1]^{d}))\asymp n^{-r}(\log n)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}.

It was shown in
[
19
,
27
,
35
]
that for
r>\max\{1/p,1/2\}
and

1<p<\infty
,

e_{n}^{\rm ran}(BW_{p}^{r,\rm mix}([0,1]^{d}))\asymp n^{-r-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}}.

(4) For the Sobolev class
BW_{p}^{r}({S}^{d-1})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
r>0
, on the sphere
{S}^{d-1}
, it was proved in

[
4
,
17
,
37
]
that for
r>(d-1)/p
,

e_{n}^{\rm det}(BW_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}))\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d-1}}.

Wang and Zhang in
[
39
]
obtained for
r>(d-1)/p
,

e_{n}^{\rm ran}(BW_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}))\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d-1}-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}}.

(5) For the generalized Besov class
BB_{p,{\theta}}^{\Omega}({S}^{d-1})
,
1\leq p,{\theta}\leq\infty,
with the smoothness index

\Omega
satisfying some conditions, Duan and Ye in
[
9
]

obtained

e_{n}^{\rm det}(BB_{p,{\theta}}^{\Omega}({S}^{d-1}))\asymp\Omega(n^{-\frac{1}{d-1}}),

and

e_{n}^{\rm ran}(BB_{p,{\theta}}^{\Omega}({S}^{d-1}))\asymp\Omega(n^{-\frac{1}{d-1}})n^{-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}}.

We remark that if
\Omega(t)=t^{r}
, then
BB_{p,{\theta}}^{\Omega}({S}^{d-1})
recedes to the usual Besov class
BB^{r}_{\theta}(L_{p}({S}^{d-1}))
.

(6) Dai and Wang in
[
6
]
investigated the weighted Besov
class
BB_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,w}({S}^{d-1}))
,
r>0,\,0<\tau\leq\infty,\,1\leq p\leq\infty,
with an
A_{\infty}
weight
w
on

{S}^{d-1}
. They obtained for
r>s_{w}/p
,

e_{n}^{\rm det}(BB_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,w}({S}^{d-1})))\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d-1}},

where
s_{w}
is a critical index for the
A_{\infty}
weight
w
.
This generalized the unweighted result of
[
18
]
. Meanwhile,
they also obtained the corresponding results for the weighted
Besov classes on the unit ball and on the standard simplex of the
Euclidean space
{R}^{d}
.

The above results indicate that randomized algorithms effectively
improve the optimal rate of convergence in many cases. There is a
vast literature of integration problems in the deterministic and
randomized case settings, see for example,
[
6
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
15
,
25
,

,

,

,

. However, as far as we know, there are
few results about integration problem on the unite ball
{B}^{d}

in the randomized case setting.

Let
\mathbb{B}^{d}=\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,|\,|x|\leq 1\big\}

be the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
x\cdot y
is the usual
inner product and
|x|=(x\cdot x)^{1/2}
is the usual Euclidean
norm. We denote by
L_{p,\mu}\equiv L_{p,\mu}({B}^{d}),\,0<p<\infty,
the space of all measurable functions with finite
quasi-norm

\|f\|_{p,\mu}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}w_{\mu}(x)dx\Big)^{1/p},

where
w_{\mu}(x)=b_{d}^{\mu}(1-|x|^{2})^{\mu-1/2},\ \mu\geq 0
is the
classical Jacobi weight on
{B}^{d}
, normalized by
\int_{{B}^{d}}w_{\mu}(x)dx=1
. When
p=\infty
we consider the space of
continuous functions
C({B}^{d})
with the uniform norm. Let

BW_{p,\mu}^{r}
and
BB_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
r>0
,
0<\tau\leq\infty
, denote the weighted Sobolev
class and the weighted Besov class on
\mathbb{B}^{d}
,
respectively (see the precise definitions in Section 2). We remark
that if
r>(d+2\mu)/p
, then the spaces
W_{p,\mu}^{r}
and

B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
are compactly embedded into the space of
continuous functions
C(\mathbb{B}^{d})
.

This paper is concerned with numerical integration on
{B}^{d}

(1.2)

{\rm INT}_{d}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}f(x)w_{\mu}(x)dx.

For the
weighted Besov class
BB_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

0<\tau\leq\infty
, and
r>(d+2\mu)/p
, it follows from
[
6
]

that

(1.3)

e_{n}^{\rm det}(BB_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu}))\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d}}.

For the weighted Sobolev class
BW_{p,\mu}^{r}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

r>(d+2\mu)/p
, we obtain the similar result as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and

r>(d+2\mu)/{p}
. Then we have

(1.4)

e_{n}^{\rm det}(BW_{p,\mu}^{r})\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d}}.

From (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), we know that the integration
problems (
1.2
) for the weighted Besov class

BB_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
and the weighted Sobolev class

BW_{p,\mu}^{r}
is “intractable” in the deterministic setting if

d
is much larger than
r
. So it is natural to ask whether
randomness improves the order of convergence. In this paper we
investigate randomized quadrature for
BW_{p,\mu}^{r}
and

BB_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
. We obtain their sharp asymptotic orders of
quadrature errors in the randomized case setting, and find that
randomized algorithms provide a faster rate than that of
deterministic ones for
p>1
. Our main results can formulated as
follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
0<\tau\leq\infty
, and
r>(d+2\mu)/{p}
.
Then we have

(1.5)

e_{n}^{\rm ran}(BX_{p}^{r})\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}},

where
X_{p}^{r}
denotes
W_{p,\mu}^{r}
or
B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
.

Remark 1.3
.

We compare the results in the deterministic and randomized case settings. For
p=1
, the order of convergence is the same,
which means that randomness does not help for
p=1
. Randomness
does help for
1<p\leq\infty.
Indeed, randomness improve the
order of convergence by a factor
n^{1-1/p}
for
1<p<2
and

n^{1/2}
for
2\leq p\leq\infty
.

The organization of the paper is the following. Section

presents some facts about harmonic analysis on the ball. In

or

, and show that the filtered

. Section

and Section

are devoted to proving the upper and lower estimates of
the quantities

as in Theorem

1.2
, respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

This section is devoted to give some basic knowledge about harmonic analysis on the unit ball
{B}^{d}
.

For the classical Jacobi weight

w_{\mu}(x)=b_{d}^{\mu}(1-|x|^{2})^{\mu-1/2},\ \mu\geq 0,\ b_{d}^{\mu}=\Big(\int_{{B}^{d}}(1-|x|^{2})^{\mu-1/2}dx\Big)^{-1},\

on
\mathbb{B}^{d}
, denote by
L_{p,\mu}\equiv L_{p,\mu}({B}^{d})\,(0<p<\infty)

the space of all Lebesgue measurable functions
f
on

\mathbb{B}^{d}
with the finite quasi-norm

\|f\|_{p,\mu}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}w_{\mu}(x)dx\Big)^{1/p}.

And when
p=\infty
we consider the space of
continuous functions
C({B}^{d})
with the uniform norm. In particular,
L_{2,\mu}
is a Hilbert space with inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mu}:=\int_{{B}^{d}}f(x)g(x)w_{\mu}(x)dx,\ {\rm for}\ f,g\in L_{2,\mu}.

Let
\Pi_{n}^{d}
be the space of all polynomials in
d
variables
of total degree at most
n
. We denote by

\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\mu})
the space of all polynomials of degree

n
which are orthogonal to lower degree polynomials in

L_{2,\mu}.
It is well known (see
[
7
, p.38 or p.229]
) that
the spaces
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\mu})
are just the eigenspaces
corresponding to the eigenvalues
-n(n+2\mu+d-1)
of the
second-order differential operator

D_{\mu}:=\triangle-(x\cdot\nabla)^{2}-(2\mu+d-1)x\cdot\nabla,

where
\triangle
and
\nabla
are the Laplace operator and gradient operator, respectively. More precisely,

D_{\mu}P=-n(n+2\mu+d-1)P,\ \ {\rm for\ all}\ P\in\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\mu}).

It is easy to see that the spaces
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\mu})
are
mutually orthogonal in
L_{2,\mu}
. Let

\{\phi_{nk}\equiv\phi_{nk}^{d}:\,k=1,2,\dots,a_{n}^{d}\}
be a fixed
orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\mu})
, where
a_{n}^{d}:={\rm dim}\,\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\mu})
. Then

\{\phi_{nk}:\,k=1,2,\dots,a_{n}^{d},\,n=0,1,2,\dots\}

is an orthonormal basis for

L_{2,\mu}
.
The orthogonal projector
{\rm Proj}_{n}:\,L_{2,\mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\mu})
can be written
as

\displaystyle({\rm Proj}_{n}f)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{a_{n}^{d}}\langle f,\phi_{nk}\rangle\phi_{nk}(x)=\langle f,P_{n}(w_{\mu};x,\cdot)\rangle_{\mu},

where
P_{n}(w_{\mu};x,y)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{a_{n}^{d}}\phi_{nk}(x)\phi_{nk}(y)
is the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}(w_{\mu})
. See
[
41
]
for more details about
P_{n}(w_{\mu};x,y)
.

For
r>0
, we define the fractional power
(-D_{\mu})^{r/2}
of the operator
-D_{\mu}
on
f
by

(-D_{\mu})^{r/2}f:=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}(k(k+2\mu+d-1))^{r/2}{\rm Proj}_{k}f,

in the sense of distribution.
By
[
42
]
, we have for any
P\in\Pi_{n}^{d}
,

(2.1)

\|(-D_{\mu})^{r/2}P\|_{p,\mu}\lesssim n^{r}\|P\|_{p,\mu}.

Given
r>0
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we define the weighted Sobolev space by

W_{p,\mu}^{r}\equiv W_{p,\mu}^{r}({B}^{d}):=\big\{f\in L_{p,\mu}\ \big|\ \|f\|_{W_{p,\mu}^{r}}:=\|f\|_{p,\mu}+\|(-D_{\mu})^{r/2}f\|_{p,\mu}<\infty\big\},

while the weighted Sobolev class
BW_{p,\mu}^{r}
is defined to be
the unit ball of the weighted Sobolev space
W_{p,\mu}^{r}
. We
remark that if
r>(d+2\mu)/p
, then
W_{p,\mu}^{r}
is compactly
embedded into
C({B}^{d})
.

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}[0,+\infty)
(a “
C^{\infty}
-filter”)
satisfy

\chi_{\left[0,1\right]}\leq\eta\leq\chi_{\left[0,2\right]}.

Here,
\chi_{A}
denotes the characteristic function of
A
for

A\subset{R}
. For
L\in{N}
, we define the filtered
polynomial operator by

(2.2)

V_{L}(f)(x)\equiv V_{L,\eta}(f)(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\eta(\frac{k}{L}){\rm Proj}_{k}(f)(x)=\langle f,K_{L,\eta}(x,\cdot)\rangle_{\mu},

where
f\in L_{1,\mu}
, and

(2.3)

K_{L,\eta}(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\eta(\frac{k}{L})P_{k}(w_{\mu};x,y),\ x,y\in{B}^{d}.

Then the following properties hold (see, for example,
[
28
]
)):

(a)
V_{L}(f)\in\Pi^{d}_{2L-1}
for any
f\in L_{1,\mu}
;

(b)
P=V_{L}(P)
for any
P\in\Pi_{L}^{d}
;

(c)

\|V_{L}\|:=\|V_{L}\|_{(\infty,\infty)}=\|V_{L}\|_{(1,1)}=\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{B}^{d}}\|K_{L,\eta}(x,\cdot)\|_{1,\mu}\lesssim 1
;

(d)
\|V_{L}\|_{(p,p)}\leq\|V_{L}\|\lesssim 1
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
;

(e)
\|f-V_{L}(f)\|_{p,\mu}\leq(1+\|V_{L}\|_{(p,p)})E_{L}(f)_{p,\mu}\lesssim E_{L}(f)_{p,\mu}
for

1\leq p\leq\infty
,

where

\|A\|_{(p,p)}:=\sup\limits_{\|f\|_{p,\mu}\leq 1}\|Af\|_{p,\mu}

is the operator norm of a linear operator
A

on
L_{p,\mu}
, and
E_{L}(f)_{p,\mu}
is the best approximation of

f\in L_{p,\mu}
from
\Pi_{L}^{d}
defined by

E_{L}(f)_{p,\mu}:=\inf\limits_{P\in\Pi_{L}^{d}}\|f-P\|_{p,\mu}.

We note that property (c) is essential.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\eta
be a filter, i.e.,
\eta
is a continuous function
satisfying
\chi_{\left[0,1\right]}\leq\eta\leq\chi_{\left[0,2\right]}.
We may weaken the
smoothness condition on
\eta
such that the operator norms

\|V_{L,\eta}\|
are uniformly bounded. Wang and Sloan
investigated the corresponding problem on the sphere, and gave
the compact condition on
\eta
for which the operator norms of
the filtered polynomial operators
V^{{S}}_{L,\eta}
on the
sphere are uniformly bounded. Following the way in
[
38
]
, Li
obtained in
[
22
]
that the operator norms
\|V_{L,\eta}\|

are uniformly bounded whenever
\eta\in W^{\lfloor\frac{d+2\mu+1}{2}\rfloor}BV
, where
W^{r}BV[a,b]
denotes the set
of all continuous functions
\eta
on
[a,b]
for which

\eta^{(r-1)}
is absolutely continuous and
\eta^{(r)}_{+}
and

\eta^{(r)}_{-}
exist and are of bounded variation on
[a,b]
for
r\in{N}
. Hence, if
\eta\in W^{\lfloor\frac{d+2\mu+1}{2}\rfloor}BV
, then properties (a)-(e) hold.
Note that

C^{r+1}[a,b]\subset W^{r}BV[a,b]\subset C[a,b],\ {\rm for\ any}\ r\in{N}.

The condition
\eta\in W^{\lfloor\frac{d+2\mu+1}{2}\rfloor}BV
is compact, since there exists an

\eta\in W^{\lfloor\frac{d+2\mu-1}{2}\rfloor}BV
such that

\|V_{L,\eta}\|
are not uniformly bounded.

Now we define weighted Besov spaces on the ball. Given
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
r>0
, and
0<\tau\leq\infty
, we define the weighted
Besov space
B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
to be the space of all real
functions
f
with quasi-norm

\|f\|_{B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})}:=\Bigg\{\begin{aligned} &\|f\|_{p,\mu}+\Big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{jr\tau}E_{2^{j}}(f)_{p,\mu}^{\tau}\Big)^{1/\tau},\ \ &0<\tau<\infty,\\
&\|f\|_{p,\mu}+\sup\limits_{j\geq 0}2^{jr}E_{2^{j}}(f)_{p,\mu},\ \ \ &\tau=\infty,\end{aligned}

while the weighted Besov class

BB_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
is defined to be the unit ball of the
weighted Besov space
B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
.

There are other definitions of the weighted Besov spaces which
are equivalent (see
[
20
, Proposition 5.7]
). We remark that
if
1\leq p,q\leq\infty,\ r>(d+2\mu)/p
, then
B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})

is compactly embedded into
C({B}^{d})
, and for
1\leq p\leq\infty,\ r>0
,
0<\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}\leq\infty
,

B_{\tau_{1}}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})\subset B_{\tau_{2}}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})\subset B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu}).

It follows from the Jackson inequality (see
[
42
]
) that for

f\in W_{p,\mu}^{r},\ 1\leq p\leq\infty
,
r>0
,

(2.4)

E_{n}(f)_{p,\mu}\lesssim n^{-r}\|f\|_{W_{p,\mu}^{r}}.

This means that

W_{p,\mu}^{r}\subset B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu}).

It can be seen that for
f\in B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
,
0<\tau\leq\infty
,

(2.5)

E_{n}(f)_{p,\mu}\leq 2^{r}n^{-r}\|f\|_{B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})}.

We introduce a metric
\rho
on
\mathbb{B}^{d}
:

\rho(x,y):=\arccos\Big((x,y)+\sqrt{1-|x|^{2}}\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}}\Big).

For
r>0,\ x\in\mathbb{B}^{d}
and a positive integer
n
, we set

{\bf B}_{\rho}(x,r):=\{y\in\mathbb{B}^{d}\ |\ \rho(x,y)\leq r\}.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we say that a finite subset

\Lambda\subset\mathbb{B}^{d}
is maximal
\varepsilon
-separated if

\mathbb{B}^{d}\subset\bigcup\limits_{{\omega}\in\Lambda}{\bf B}_{\rho}({\omega},\varepsilon)\ {\rm\ and}\ \min\limits_{{\omega}\neq{\omega}^{\prime}}\rho({\omega},{\omega}^{\prime})\geq\varepsilon.

Note that such a maximal
\varepsilon
-separated set
\Lambda

exists and
\#\Lambda\asymp\varepsilon^{-d}
, where
\#A
denotes the number
of elements of a set
A
(see
[
28
, Lemma 5.2]
).

Finally, we give the Nikolskii inequalities on
{B}^{d}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
20
, Proposition 2.4]
) Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
\mu\geq 0
. Then for any
P\in\Pi_{n}^{d}
we
have,

(2.6)

\|P\|_{q,\mu}\lesssim n^{(d+2\mu)(1/p-1/q)_{+}}\|P\|_{p,\mu}.

3.
Filtered hyperinterpolation on the ball

Let
\eta
be a filter such that properties (a)-(e) hold. We want
to approximate the inner product integral (
2.2
) of

V_{L,\eta}(f)(x)
by a positive quadrature rule of polynomial
degree
3L
. Following
[
30
]
, we shall call the resulting
operator “filtered hyperinterpolation”.

For this purpose, we need positive quadrature rules on
{B}^{d}
. For
L\in{N}
, we assume that
Q_{L}(f):=\sum_{{\omega}\in\Lambda_{L}}{\lambda}_{\omega}f({\omega})
is a positive quadrature rule
on
{B}^{d}
which is exact for
f\in\Pi_{3L}^{d}
, i.e.,
\Lambda_{L}

is a finite subset of
\mathbb{B}^{d}
with
\#\Lambda_{L}\asymp L^{d}
,
weights
{\lambda}_{\omega}>0,\ {\omega}\in\Lambda_{L}
, satisfy, for all

P\in\Pi_{3L}^{d}
,

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}P(x)w_{\mu}(x)dx=Q_{L}(P)=\sum_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}\lambda_{\omega}P(\omega).

Such positive quadrature rules exist. Indeed, for
L\in{N}
,
it follows from
[
7
, Theorem 11.6.5]
that for a given maximal

\delta/L
-separated subset
\Lambda_{L}
of
{B}^{d}
with

\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
for some
\delta_{0}>0
, there exists a
positive quadrature formula

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}f(x)w_{\mu}(x)dx\approx Q_{L}(f):=\sum_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}\lambda_{\omega}f(\omega),\ \lambda_{\omega}>0,

with
\lambda_{\omega}\asymp\int_{{\bf B}_{\rho}(y,\delta/L)}w_{\mu}(x)dx
and
\#\Lambda_{L}\asymp L^{d}
, which
is exact for
\Pi^{d}_{3L}
.

For the above positive quadrature formula
Q_{L}
, we can define the
discreted inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Q_{L}}
on

C({B}^{d})
by

\langle f,g\rangle_{Q_{L}}:=Q_{L}(fg)=\sum_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}\lambda_{\omega}f(\omega)g({\omega}).

The filtered hyperinterpolation operator is defined by

(3.1)

G_{L}(f)(x):=\langle f,K_{L,\eta}(x,\cdot)\rangle_{Q_{L}}=\sum_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}\lambda_{\omega}f({\omega})K_{L,\eta}(x,{\omega}).

From
[
40
]
we know that

(3.2)

\|G_{L}\|=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{B}^{d}}\sum_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}\lambda_{\omega}|K_{L,\eta}(x,{\omega})|\lesssim 1.

The following theorem plays an important role in the proof of
upper estimates.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,
r>(d+2\mu)/p
,
0<\tau\leq\infty
, and
G_{L}

be given as in (
3.1
). Then for all
f\in X_{p}^{r}
, we have

(3.3)

\|f-G_{L}(f)\|_{q,\mu}\lesssim L^{-r+(d+2\mu)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}}\|f\|_{X_{p}^{r}},

where
X_{p}^{r}
denotes
W_{p,\mu}^{r}
or
B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
.

Remark 3.2
.

When
1\leq p=q\leq\infty
and
X_{p}^{r}=W_{p,\mu}^{r}
, (
3.3
) was obtained by Li in
[
22
]
.

In order to prove Theorem
3.1
, we need the following two
lemmas.

Lemma 3.3
.

(
[
40
, Theorem 3.1]
and
[
21
, Lemma 2.2]
)
Suppose that
n\in{N}
,
\Omega_{n}
is a finite subset of

\mathbb{B}^{d}
, and
\{\mu_{\omega}:\,\omega\in\Omega_{n}\}
is a set of positive numbers. If there exists a
p_{0}\in(0,\infty)
such that
for any
f\in\Pi_{n}^{d}
,

(3.4)

\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{n}}\mu_{\omega}|f(\omega)|^{p_{0}}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p_{0}}w_{\mu}(x)dx,

then the following regularity
condition

(3.5)

\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{n}\cap{\bf B}_{\rho}(y,\frac{1}{n})}\mu_{\omega}\lesssim\int_{{\bf B}_{\rho}(y,\frac{1}{n})}w_{\mu}(x)dx,\ {\rm for\ any}\ y\in{B}^{d},

holds.

Conversely, if the regularity condition (
3.5
) holds,
then for any
1\leq p<\infty
,
m\in{N}
,
m\geq n
,
f\in\Pi_{m}^{d}
, we have

(3.6)

\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{n}}\mu_{\omega}\left|f(\omega)\right|^{p}\lesssim(\frac{m}{n})^{d+2\mu}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\left|f(x)\right|^{p}w_{\mu}(x)dx.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and
L\in{N}
. Then for any
N\geq L
and
P\in\Pi_{N}^{d}
, we have

(3.7)

\|G_{L}(P)\|_{p,\mu}\lesssim(\frac{N}{L})^{\frac{d+2\mu}{p}}\|P\|_{p,\mu}.

Proof.

Our proof will be divided into three cases.

Case 1:

p=\infty
.

In this case, by (
3.2
) we have for
N\geq L
and
P\in\Pi_{N}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\|G_{L}(P)\|_{\infty}\leq\|G_{L}\|\|P\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|P\|_{\infty}.

Case 2:

p=1
.

In this case, since
Q_{L}
is a positive quadrature rule which is
exact for
\Pi_{3L}^{d}
, then (
3.4
) is true for

\{\lambda_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}
with
p_{0}=2
. This leads
that the regular condition (
3.5
) holds. By property (c) and
Lemma
3.3
we obtain for
N\geq L
and
P\in\Pi_{N}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\|G_{L}(P)\|_{1,\mu}

\displaystyle=\|\sum\limits_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}\lambda_{\omega}P({\omega})K_{L,\eta}(\cdot,{\omega})\|_{1,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}\lambda_{\omega}|P({\omega})|\|K_{L,\eta}(\cdot,{\omega})\|_{1,\mu}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}\lambda_{\omega}|P({\omega})|\lesssim(\frac{N}{L})^{{d+2\mu}}\|P\|_{1,\mu}.

Case 3:

1<p<\infty
.

In this case, for
N\geq L
and
P\in\Pi_{N}^{d}
, by the Hölder inequality,
(
3.2
), property (c), and Lemma
3.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|G_{L}(P)\|_{p,\mu}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\Big|\sum\limits_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}\lambda_{\omega}P({\omega})K_{L,\eta}(x,{\omega})\Big|^{p}w_{\mu}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\Big(\sum\limits_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}{\lambda}_{{\omega}}|P(\omega)||K_{L,\eta}(x,{\omega})|\Big)^{p}w_{\mu}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\Big(\sum\limits_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}\lambda_{\omega}|P({\omega})|^{p}|K_{L,\eta}(x,{\omega})|\Big)\Big(\sum\limits_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}\lambda_{\omega}|K_{L,\eta}(x,{\omega})|\Big)^{p-1}w_{\mu}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum\limits_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}{\lambda}_{{\omega}}|P({\omega})|^{p}\|K_{L,\eta}(\cdot,{\omega})\|_{1,\mu}\Big)\Big(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{B}^{d}}\sum\limits_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}\lambda_{\omega}|K_{L,\eta}(x,{\omega})|\Big)^{{p-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{\omega\in\Lambda_{L}}\lambda_{\omega}|P({\omega})|^{p}\lesssim(\frac{N}{L})^{d+2\mu}\|P\|_{p,\mu}^{p},

which proves (
3.7
).

Lemma
3.4
is proved.∎

Now we turn to prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Since
X_{p}^{r}
is continuously embedded into

B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
, it suffices to show Theorem
3.1

for
B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
.

Suppose that
m
is an integer satisfying
2^{m}\leq L<2^{m+1}
.
Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty,\ r>(d+2\mu)/p
,
and
f\in B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
. Define

g_{2^{k}}\in\Pi_{2^{k}}^{d}
by

E_{2^{k}}(f)_{p,\mu}=\|f-g_{2^{k}}\|_{p,\mu},

and let
f_{k}=g_{2^{k}}-g_{2^{k-1}}
for
k\geq 0
, where we set
g_{2^{-1}}=0
. Note that since
\Pi_{2^{k}}^{d}

are the finite dimensional linear spaces, the best approximant
polynomials
g_{2^{k}}
always exist (see, for instance,

[
23
, p. 17, Theorem 1]
). It can be seen that

f_{k}\in\Pi_{2^{k}}^{d}
, and the series
\sum\limits_{k=j+1}^{\infty}f_{k}
converges to
f-g_{2^{j}}
in the uniform norm for each

j\geq-1
. We have

\displaystyle\|f-G_{L}(f)\|_{q,\mu}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}(f_{k}-G_{L}(f_{k}))\|_{q,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\|f_{k}-G_{L}(f_{k})\|_{q,\mu}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\|f_{k}\|_{q,\mu}+\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\|G_{L}(f_{k})\|_{q,\mu}.

It follows from the Nikolskii
inequality (
2.6
) and (
2.5
) we have

\displaystyle\|f_{k}\|_{q,\mu}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k(d+2\mu)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}}\|f_{k}\|_{p,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{k(d+2\mu)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}}(\|f-g_{2^{k}}\|_{p,\mu}+\|f-g_{2^{k-1}}\|_{p,\mu})

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k(d+2\mu)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}}E_{2^{k-1}}(f)_{p,\mu}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k(r-(d+2\mu)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+})}\|f\|_{B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})}.

This means that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\|f_{k}\|_{q,\mu}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}2^{-k(r-(d+2\mu)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+})}\|f\|_{B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})}

\displaystyle\asymp 2^{-m(r-(d+2\mu)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+})}\|f\|_{B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\asymp L^{-r+(d+2\mu)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}}\|f\|_{B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})}.

Applying (
3
) and Lemma
3.4
, we get for
k\geq m+1
,

\displaystyle\|G_{L}(f_{k})\|_{q,\mu}

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{(d+2\mu)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}}\|G_{L}(f_{k})\|_{p,\mu}

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{(d+2\mu)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}}(\frac{2^{k}}{L})^{\frac{d+2\mu}{p}}\|f_{k}\|_{p,\mu}

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{(d+2\mu)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}}(\frac{2^{k}}{L})^{\frac{d+2\mu}{p}}2^{-kr}\|f\|_{B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\|G_{L}(f_{k})\|_{q,\mu}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}L^{(d+2\mu)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}}(\frac{2^{k}}{L})^{\frac{d+2\mu}{p}}2^{-kr}\|f\|_{B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})}

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{(d+2\mu)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}-\frac{d+2\mu}{p}}\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}2^{-k(r-\frac{d+2\mu}{p})}\|f\|_{B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\asymp L^{-r+(d+2\mu)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}}\|f\|_{B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})}.

Hence, for
f\in B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
, by (
3
),
(
3
), and (
3
) we have

\displaystyle\|f-G_{L}(f)\|_{q,\mu}\lesssim L^{-r+(d+2\mu)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})_{+}}\|f\|_{B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
\hfill\Box

Next we show that the filtered hyperinterpolation operators
G_{L}

are order optimal in sense of the optimal recovery in some cases.
Let
F_{d}
be a class of continuous functions on
D_{d}
, and

(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a normed linear space of functions on
D_{d}
,
where
D_{d}
is a subset of
{R}^{d}
. For
n\in{N}
, the
sampling numbers (or the optimal recovery) of
F_{d}
in
X
are
defined by

g_{n}(F_{d},X)\ :=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\in D_{d}\\
\varphi:\ {R}^{n}\rightarrow X\end{subarray}}\sup_{f\in F_{d}}\|f-\varphi(f(\xi_{1}),\dots,f(\xi_{n}))\|_{X},

where the infimum is taken over all
n
points

\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
in
D_{d}
and all mappings
\varphi
from

{R}^{n}
to
X
. If in the above definition, the infimum is
taken over all
n
points
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
in
D_{d}
and all
linear mappings
\varphi
from
{R}^{n}
to
X
, we obtain the
definition of the linear sampling numbers
g_{n}^{\rm lin}(F_{d},X)
.

It is well known (see
[
33
]
) that for a balanced convex set

F_{d}
,

(3.12)

g_{n}^{\rm lin}(F_{d},X)\geq g_{n}(F_{d},X)\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\in D_{d}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in F_{d}\\
f(\xi_{1})=\dots=f(\xi_{n})=0\end{subarray}}\|f\|_{X}.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
,
0<\tau\leq\infty
, and

r>(d+2\mu)/p
. Then we have

(3.13)

g_{n}^{\rm lin}(BX_{p}^{r},L_{q,\mu})\asymp g_{n}(BX_{p}^{r},L_{q,\mu})\asymp n^{-r/d},

where
X_{p}^{r}
denotes
W_{p,\mu}^{r}
or
B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
.

In order to prove Theorem
3.5
we need the following
lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

(
[
6
, Proposition 4.8]
)
Let
X
be a linear subspace of
\Pi_{N}^{d}
with
{\rm dim}\,X\geq\varepsilon\,{\rm dim}\,\Pi_{N}^{d}
for some

\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then there exists a function
f\in X
such
that
\|f\|_{p,\mu}\asymp 1
for all
0<p\leq\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Without loss of generality we assume that
n
is sufficiently
large. We choose
L\in{N}
such that

\#\Lambda_{L}\leq n\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \#\Lambda_{L}\asymp n.

It follows from the definition of
G_{L}
and
g_{n}^{\rm lin}(BX_{p}^{r},L_{q,\mu})
that

g_{n}^{\rm lin}(BX_{p}^{r},L_{q,\mu})\leq\sup_{f\in BX_{p}^{r}}\|f-G_{L}(f)\|_{q,\mu}.

By Theorem
3.1
and the above inequlity we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle g_{n}(BX_{p}^{r},L_{q,\mu})\leq g_{n}^{\rm lin}(BX_{p}^{r},L_{q,\mu})\lesssim L^{-r}\asymp n^{-r/d}.

Now we show the lower bound. Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
be any
n

distinct points on
{B}^{d}
. Take a positive integer
N
such
that
2n\leq{\rm dim}\,\Pi_{N}^{d}\leq Cn
, and denote

X_{0}:=\big\{g\in\Pi_{N}^{d}\ \big|\ g(\xi_{j})=0\ {\rm for\ all}\ j=1,\dots,n\big\}.

Thus,
X_{0}
is a linear subspace of
\Pi_{N}^{d}
with

{\rm dim}\,X_{0}\geq{\rm dim}\,\Pi_{N}^{d}-n\geq\frac{1}{2}{\rm dim}\,\Pi_{N}^{d}.

It
follows from Lemma
3.6
that there exists a function

g_{0}\in X_{0}
such that

\|g_{0}\|_{p_{0},\mu}\asymp 1,\ {\rm}\ {\rm for\ all}\ 0<p_{0}\leq\infty.

Let
f_{0}(x)=N^{-r}(g_{0}(x))^{2}
and

m\in{N}
such that
2^{m-1}\leq N<2^{m}
. Then by the fact that

E_{2^{j}}(f_{0})_{p,\mu}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{p,\mu}
we have

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})}

\displaystyle=\|f_{0}\|_{p,\mu}+\Big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m+1}2^{jr\tau}E_{2^{j}}(f_{0})_{p,\mu}^{\tau}\Big)^{1/\tau}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m+1}2^{jr\tau}\Big)^{1/\tau}\|f_{0}\|_{p,\mu}\lesssim 2^{mr}N^{-r}\|g_{0}\|_{2p,\mu}^{2}\lesssim 1.

By the fact that
f_{0}\in\Pi_{2N}^{d}
and (
2.1
) we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{W_{p,\mu}^{r}}

\displaystyle=\|f_{0}\|_{p,\mu}+\|(-D_{\mu})^{r/2}f_{0}\|_{p,\mu}\lesssim N^{r}\|f_{0}\|_{p,\mu}=\|g_{0}\|_{2p,\mu}^{2}\lesssim 1.

Hence, there exists a positive constant
C
such that
f_{1}=Cf_{0}\in BX_{p}^{r}
, and
f_{1}(\xi_{1})=\dots=f_{1}(\xi_{n})=0
. It follows from
(
3.12
) that

g_{n}(BX_{p}^{r},L_{q,\mu})\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\in D_{d}\end{subarray}}\|f_{1}\|_{q,\mu}\gtrsim N^{-r}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\in D_{d}\end{subarray}}\|g_{0}\|_{2q,\mu}^{2}\asymp N^{-r}\asymp n^{-r/d},

which combining with (
3.14
), gives
(
3.13
).

The proof of Theorem
3.5
is finished.

.

.

n

N

4.
The upper estimates

This section is devoted to proving the upper
estimates of the quantities
e_{n}^{\rm ran}(X_{p}^{r})
given as in
(
1.5
). That is, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
r>(d+2\mu)/p
, and

0<\tau\leq\infty
,

(4.1)

e_{n}^{\rm ran}(BX_{p}^{r})\lesssim n^{-\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}},

where
X_{p}^{r}
denotes

W_{p,\mu}^{r}
or
B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
.

For this purpose, we will use the positive quadrature rule and the
filtered hyperinterpolation operator to construct an randomized
algorithm to attain the upper bounds. Due to Henrich
[
15
]
, we
need a concrete Monte Carlo method by virtue of the standard Monte
Carlo algorithm. It is defined as follows: let
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}

be independent,
{B}^{d}
-valued, distributed over
{B}^{d}

with respect to the measure
w_{\mu}(x)dx
random vectors on
probability space
(\Omega,\Sigma,\nu)
. For any
h\in C({B}^{d})
, we put

(4.2)

Q_{N}^{\omega}(h)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}h(\xi_{i}({\omega})),\ {\omega}\in\Omega.

The
following lemma can be drawn in a same way as in

[
15
, Proposition 5.4]
. Here we omit the proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
\mu\geq 0
, Then for any
h\in C({B}^{d})
, we have

(4.3)

{E}_{\omega}|{\rm INT}_{d}(h)-Q_{N}^{\omega}(h)|\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}}\|h\|_{p,\mu}.

Now we prove (
4.1
).

Proof of (
4.1
).

Let
n\in{N}
. Without loss of generality we assume that
n
is
sufficiently large. Then there exists a positive quadrature rule

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}f(x)w_{\mu}(x)dx\approx Q_{L}(f):=\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda_{L}}\lambda_{\xi}f(\xi),\ \lambda_{\xi}>0,

which is exact for
\Pi_{3L}^{d}
, where

\#\Lambda_{L}\leq n/2
and
n\asymp L^{d}
.

As in Section 3.1, we can construct the filtered
hyperinterpolation operator by

G_{L}(f)(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda_{L}}\lambda_{\xi}f(\xi)K_{L,\eta}(x,\xi),

where
K_{L,\eta}
is given as in (
2.3
).
Hence, according to Theorem
3.1
we have for any
f\in X_{p}^{r},\ 1\leq p\leq\infty,\ r>(d+2\mu)/{p}
,

(4.4)

\|f-G_{L}(f)\|_{p,\mu}\lesssim L^{-r}\|f\|_{X_{p}^{r}}.

Let
N=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, where
\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the largest integer not exceeding
x
.
We define the randomized algorithm
(A_{n}^{\omega})
by

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\omega}(f)=Q_{N}^{\omega}(f-G_{L}(f))+{\rm INT}_{d}(G_{L}(f)),

where
f\in C({B}^{d})
, and
Q_{N}^{\omega}
is the standard Monte Carlo algorithm
given as in (
4.2
). We also note that

{\rm INT}_{d}(G_{L}(f))=\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda_{\xi}}{\lambda}_{\xi}f(\xi).

Clearly, the algorithm
(A_{n}^{\omega})
use only at most

\#\Lambda_{L}+N\leq n
function values of
f
. This means that

(A_{n}^{\omega})\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\rm ran}
. Also the algorithm

(A_{n}^{\omega})
is a randomized linear algorithm. It is easy to
check that

|{\rm INT}_{d}(f)-A_{n}^{\rm{\omega}}(f)|=|{\rm INT}_{d}(g)-Q_{N}^{\omega}(g)|,

where
g=f-G_{L}(f)
.
Combining with (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we obtain for
f\in BX_{p}^{r}
,
r>(d+2\mu)/p
,

\displaystyle{E}_{\omega}|{\rm INT}_{d}(f)-A_{n}^{{\omega}}(f)|

\displaystyle={E}_{\omega}|{\rm INT}_{d}(g)-Q_{N}^{\omega}(g)|

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}}\|f-G_{L}(f)\|_{p,\mu}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}}L^{-r}\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}},

which leads to

e_{n}^{\rm ran}(BX_{p}^{r})\leq e^{\rm ran}(BX_{p}^{r},(A_{n}^{\omega}))\lesssim n^{-\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}}.

This completes the proof of (
4.1
).

\hfill\Box

5.
Lower estimates

This section is devoted to proving the lower estimates of the
quantities
e_{n}^{\rm ran}(X_{p}^{r})
given as in (
1.5
). That
is, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
r>(d+2\mu)/p
, and
0<\tau\leq\infty
,

(5.1)

e_{n}^{\rm ran}(BX_{p}^{r})\gtrsim n^{-\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})_{+}},

where
X_{p}^{r}
denotes

W_{p,\mu}^{r}
or
B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
. Theorem
1.2

follows from (
4.1
) and (
5.1
) immediately.

The proof of (
5.1
) is based on the idea of Bakhvalov in

[
1
]
and Novak in
[
24
,
25
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

(See
[
24
, Lemma 3]
.)

(a) Let
F\subset L_{1,\mu}
and
\psi_{j},\,j=1,\dots,4n
, with the
following conditions:

(i) the
\psi_{j}
have disjoint supports and satisfy

{\rm INT_{d}}(\psi_{j})=\int_{{B}^{d}}\psi_{j}(x)w_{\mu}(x)dx\geq{\delta},\ {\rm for}\ j=1,\dots,4n.

(ii)
F_{1}:=\big\{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{4n}{\alpha}_{j}\psi_{j}\,\big|\,\alpha_{j}\in\{-1,1\}\big\}\subset F
.

Then

e_{n}^{\rm ran}(F)\geq\frac{1}{2}{\delta}n^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(b) We assume that instead of (ii) in statement (a) the
property

(ii’)
F_{2}:=\big\{\pm\psi_{j}\,\big|\,j=1,\dots,4n\big\}\subset F
.

Then

e^{\rm ran}_{n}(F)\geq\frac{1}{4}{\delta}.

By this lemma, we proceed to construct a sequence of functions

\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{4n}
satisfying the conditions of Lemma

5.1
for
F=BX_{p}^{r}
, where
X_{p}^{r}
denotes
W_{p,\mu}^{r}

or
B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty,\ r>(d+2\mu)/p,\ 0<\tau\leq\infty
.

For a given
n\in{N}
, choose a positive integer
m

satisfying
n\asymp m^{d}
,
m\geq 6
, and

\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{4n}\subset{\bf B}({\bf 0},\frac{2}{3})
such that

{\bf B}(x_{i},\frac{2}{m})\bigcap{\bf B}(x_{j},\frac{2}{m})=\emptyset,\ \ {\rm if}\ \ i\neq j,

where
{\bf B}(\xi,r)=\big\{x\in{B}^{d}\,\big|\,|x-\xi|\leq r\big\}
for
\xi\in{B}^{d}
and
r>0
.

Let
\varphi
be a nonnegative

C^{\infty}
-function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
supported in
{B}^{d}

and being equal to 1 on
{\bf B}({\bf 0},\frac{1}{2})
. We define

\varphi_{j}(x)=\varphi(m(x-x_{j})),\ j=1,\dots,4n.

Clearly,

{\rm supp}\,\varphi_{j}\subset{\bf B}(x_{j},\frac{1}{m})\subset{\bf B}({\bf 0},\frac{5}{6}),\ j=1,\dots,4n,

and

(5.2)

{\rm supp}\,\varphi_{i}\cap{\rm supp}\,\varphi_{j}=\emptyset,\ {\rm for}\ i\neq j.

It is easy to verify that

(5.3)

\|\varphi_{j}\|_{p,\mu}\asymp\Big(\int_{{\bf B}(x_{j},\frac{1}{m})}|\varphi_{j}(x)|^{p}dx\Big)^{1/p}=\Big(\int_{{\bf B}({\bf 0},\frac{1}{m})}|\varphi(mx)|^{p}dx\Big)^{1/p}\asymp m^{-d/p}.

We set

F_{0}:=\big\{f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}:=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{4n}{\alpha}_{j}\varphi_{j}\,\big|\,{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}=({\alpha}_{1},\cdots,{\alpha}_{4n})\in\mathbb{R}^{4n}\big\}.

Then we have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\in F_{0}
, then for
r>0
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and
0<\tau\leq\infty
,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\|f_{{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\|_{X_{p}^{r}}\lesssim m^{r-d/p}\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{l_{p}^{4n}},

where
X_{p}^{r}
denotes
W_{p,\mu}^{r}
or
B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
,
and

\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\big(\sum_{j=1}^{4n}|{\alpha}_{j}|^{p}\big)^{1/p},\ \ &1\leq p<\infty,\\
&\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq 4n}|{\alpha}_{j}|,\ \ \ &p=\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

Indeed, for any
f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\in F_{0}
, it follows from (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) that

(5.5)

\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{p,\mu}\asymp m^{-d/p}\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}}.

For a positive integer
v>r
, by the definition of
-D_{\mu}
and (
5.2
), it is easy to verify that

{\rm supp}\,(-D_{\mu})^{v}\varphi_{i}\,\bigcap\,{\rm supp}\,(-D_{\mu})^{v}\varphi_{j}=\emptyset,\ {\rm for}\ i\neq j,

and

\|(-D_{\mu})^{v}\varphi_{j}\|_{p,\mu}\lesssim m^{2v-d/p},

which leads to

(5.6)

\|(-D_{\mu})^{v}f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{p,\mu}\lesssim m^{2v-d/p}\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}}.

It follows from the Kolmogorov type inequality (see
[
8
, Theorem 8.1]
) that

(5.7)

\|(-D_{\mu})^{r/2}f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{p,\mu}\lesssim\|(-D_{\mu})^{v}f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{p,\mu}^{\frac{r}{2v}}\,\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{p,\mu}^{\frac{2v-r}{2v}}\lesssim m^{r-d/p}\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}},

which combining with (
5.5
), we obtain (
5.4
) for
W_{p,\mu}^{r}
.

By the fact that

\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{B_{\infty}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})}\lesssim\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})},\ 0<\tau<\infty,

it
suffices to show (
5.4
) for
B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})
,

0<\tau<\infty
. Since
E_{2^{j}}(f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}})_{p,\mu}\leq\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{p,\mu}
for any
j\geq 0
, by (
5.4
)
we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{2^{j}<m}\Big(2^{jr}E_{2^{j}}(f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}})_{p,\mu}\Big)^{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{p,\mu}^{\tau}\sum\limits_{2^{j}<m}2^{jr\tau}

(5.8)

\displaystyle\asymp m^{r\tau}\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{p,\mu}^{\tau}\asymp m^{(r-d/p)\tau}\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}}^{\tau}.

Choose a positive number
\upsilon>r
, by (
2.4
) we obtain for any
j\geq 0
,

E_{2^{j}}(f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}})_{p,\mu}\lesssim 2^{-j\upsilon}\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{W_{p,\mu}^{\upsilon}},

which combining with (
5.4
) for
W_{p,\mu}^{r}
, we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{2^{j}\geq m}\Big(2^{jr}E_{2^{j}}(f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}})_{p,\mu}\Big)^{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{W_{p,\mu}^{\upsilon}}^{\tau}\sum\limits_{2^{j}\geq m}2^{j(r-\upsilon)\tau}

(5.9)

\displaystyle\asymp m^{(r-\upsilon)\tau}\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{W_{p,\mu}^{\upsilon}}^{\tau}\lesssim m^{(r-d/p)\tau}\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}}^{\tau}.

It follows from (
5.5
), (
5
), and (
5
) that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B_{\tau}^{r}(L_{p,\mu})}

\displaystyle:=\|f\|_{p,\mu}+\Big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big(2^{jr}E_{2^{j}}(f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}})_{p,\mu}\Big)^{\tau}\Big)^{1/\tau}\lesssim m^{r-d/p}\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{p}^{4n}}.

This completes the proof of Lemma 5.2.
∎

Finally we turn to prove (
5.1
).

Proof of (
5.1
).

First we consider the case
2\leq p\leq\infty
. By the fact that

X_{\infty}^{r}\subset X_{p}^{r},\ \ 2\leq p\leq\infty,

it suffices to consider the case
p=\infty
.
It follows from (
5.4
) that when
{\alpha}_{j}\in\{-1,1\},\,j=1,\dots,4n,

\|f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{X_{\infty}^{r}}\lesssim m^{r}\|{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\|_{\ell_{\infty}^{4n}}\lesssim m^{r}.

Hence, there exists a positive constant
C_{1}
such that

C_{1}m^{-r}f_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\in BX_{\infty}^{r}
. Set

\psi_{j}(x):=C_{1}m^{-r}\varphi_{j}(x),\ j=1,\dots,4n.

We have

F_{1}:=\Big\{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{4n}{\alpha}_{j}\psi_{j}\ \big|\ \alpha_{j}\in\{-1,1\},\ j=1,\dots,4n\Big\}\subset BX_{\infty}^{r}.

It follows from
(
5.3
) that

{\rm INT}_{d}(\psi_{j})=\int_{{B}^{d}}\psi_{j}(x)w_{\mu}(x)dx=C_{1}m^{-r}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{1,\mu}\asymp m^{-r-d},

Applying Lemma
5.1
(a) we obtain for
2\leq p\leq\infty
,

(5.10)

e_{n}^{\rm ran}(BX_{p}^{r})\geq e_{n}^{\rm ran}(BX_{\infty}^{r})\gtrsim m^{-r-d}n^{1/2}\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d}-\frac{1}{2}}.

Next we consider the case
1\leq p<2
. It follows from (
5.4
)
that

\|\pm\varphi_{j}\|_{X_{p}^{r}}\lesssim m^{r-d/p}.

Hence, there
exists a positive constant
C_{2}
such that

\psi_{j}(x):=C_{2}m^{-r+d/p}\varphi_{j}(x)\in BX_{p}^{r},\ j=1,\dots,4n.

We have

F_{2}:=\{\pm\psi_{j}:\ j=1,\dots,4n\}\subset BX_{p}^{r}.

It
follows from (
5.3
) that

{\rm INT}_{d}(\psi_{j})=\int_{{B}^{d}}\psi_{j}(x)w_{\mu}(x)dx=C_{2}m^{-r+d/p}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{1,\mu}\asymp m^{-r+d/p-d}.

Applying Lemma
5.1
(b), we obtain for
1\leq p<2
,

e_{n}^{\rm ran}(BX_{p}^{r})\gtrsim m^{-r+d/p-d}\asymp n^{-\frac{r}{d}+\frac{1}{p}-1},

which combining with
(
5.10
), gives the lower bounds of
e_{n}^{\rm ran}(BX_{p}^{r})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

This completes the proof of (
5.1
).

\hfill\Box

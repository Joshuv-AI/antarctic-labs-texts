Normality of smooth statistics for planar determinantal point processes Thanks: A. H. and J. L. R. gratefully acknowledge support from the Austrian Science Fund (FWF): Y 1199.
Antti Haimi, José Luis Romero

Abstract
. We consider smooth linear statistics of determinantal point processes on the complex plane, and their large scale asymptotics. We prove asymptotic normality in the finite variance case, where Soshnikov’s theorem is not applicable. The setting is similar to that of Rider and Virág [Electron. J. Probab., 12, no. 45, 1238–1257, (2007)] for the complex plane, but replaces analyticity conditions by the assumption that the correlation kernel is reproducing. Our proof is a streamlined version of that of Ameur, Hedenmalm and Makarov [Duke Math J., 159, 31–81, (2011)] for eigenvalues of normal random matrices. In our case, the reproducing property is brought to bear to compensate for the lack of analyticity and radial symmetries.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Determinantal point processes

Determinantal point processes (DPP’s) are certain interacting particle systems which exhibit repulsion between different points

[
24
,
6
,
16
,
15
]
. Their central feature is that all the statistical information is encoded in a single function, called correlation kernel. Since their introduction by Macchi in 1975
[
17
]
, DDP’s have found extensive applications in areas of knowledge as varied as random matrix theory, number theory, quantum mechanics and machine learning.

For a precise definition, we restrict attention to the Euclidean space and consider a random subset
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
encoded as a random integer-valued positive Radon measure
E\mapsto\mathcal{X}(E)
, which counts the number of points within a Borel set. We only consider
simple
processes, that is, almost surely
\mathcal{X}
assigns mass at most one to each singleton
\{x\}
. Such a process is said to admit
correlation functions
(with respect to the Lebesgue measure) if for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a function
\rho_{n}:(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\to[0,\infty)
such that
for all mutually disjoint Borel sets
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{n}X(E_{j})\Big]=\int_{E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{n}}\rho_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\,dx_{1}\ldots dx_{n}.

In addition,
\rho_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is required to vanish if
x_{j}=x_{k}
for some
j\not=k
. The correlation functions
\rho_{k}
, also called
joint intensities
, describe the essential statistical properties of
\mathcal{X}
. A simple point process
\mathcal{X}
is called
determinantal
if there exists a function
K:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
, called
correlation kernel
, such that

\rho_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\det K(x_{k},x_{j})_{k,j=1,\ldots,n},\qquad x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

One of the most famous DDP’s is the infinite
Ginibre ensemble

[
11
]
on
\mathbb{C}^{r}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2r}
, which has correlation kernel

(1.1)

\displaystyle K(z,w)=\frac{1}{\pi^{r}}\exp\Big[{\sum_{j=1}^{r}z_{j}\bar{w}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}|z_{j}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|w_{j}|^{2}}\Big],\qquad z,w\in\mathbb{C}^{r}.

The Macchi-Soshnikov theorem
[
17
]
provides a machinery to produce examples of DPP’s: any Hermitian symmetric kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
which induces a locally trace class integral operator with spectrum in the interval
[0,1]
is the correlation kernel of a uniquely determined DPP. A simple way to satisfy the spectral assumption is to let
\mathcal{H}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a

reproducing kernel subspace
- that is, a closed subspace where evaluations are continuous functionals - and let
K
be
its
reproducing kernel
, that is, the integral kernel representing the orthogonal projection
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{H}
. As such, the kernel satisfies the
reproducing formula

(1.2)

\displaystyle K(x,y)=\int K(x,u)K(u,y)\,du.

The Ginibre kernel (
1.1
) in one complex dimension
r=1
corresponds in this way to the weighted Bargmann-Fock space
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathbb{C})
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathbb{C})=\Big\{F(z)e^{-|z|^{2}/2}:F:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}\text{ entire, }\int_{\mathbb{C}}|F(z)|^{2}e^{-|z|^{2}}dA(z)<\infty\Big\},

where
dA
is the Lebesgue (area) measure. Further examples, important in high energy physics, are the polyanalytic Ginibre ensembles
[
13
,
22
]
associated with the weighted poly-Bargmann spaces
[
26
]

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{F}_{k}(\mathbb{C})
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{k}(\mathbb{C})=\Big\{F(z)e^{-|z|^{2}/2}:\bar{\partial}^{k}F=0,\int_{\mathbb{C}}|F(z)|^{2}e^{-|z|^{2}}dA(z)<\infty\Big\},

or with the pure poly-Bargmann spaces
\mathcal{F}_{k}(\mathbb{C})\ominus\mathcal{F}_{k-1}(\mathbb{C})

[
26
]
.

1.2.
Linear statistics and asymptotic normality

One of the main topics of interest around DPP’s has been the validity of central limit theorems for various statistical observations. In this article we will be interested in the
linear statistic

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(f):=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}f(x)

associated with a
smooth
function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
and a DPP
\mathcal{X}
. Specifically, we will consider large scale asymptotics of linear statistics

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{\rho}}f(x)

calculated under increasing contractions of the point process

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{\rho}=\big\{\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}x:x\in\mathcal{X}\big\},\qquad\rho\to\infty,

or, more generally, under qualitatively similar asymptotic deformations.
The corresponding expectations are given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)\big]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)K_{\rho}(x,x)\,dx,

where
K_{\rho}
is the correlation kernel of
\mathcal{X}_{\rho}
. In the model case (
1.6
),

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)\big]=\rho^{d/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)K(\sqrt{\rho}x,\sqrt{\rho}x)\,dx,

where
K
is the correlation kernel of
\mathcal{X}
.

Soshnikov’s celebrated central limit theorem
[
25
, Theorem 1]
, which builds on
[
7
]
, gives very general conditions under which the standardized linear statistic

(1.8)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}}(f):=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)-\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)\big]}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}\big[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)}\big]}

converges in distribution to a normal variable. The key assumption is that the expected value and variance of
\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)
be related by

(1.9)

\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}|f|\big]=\mathrm{O}\big(\mathrm{Var}[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)]^{\delta}\big),\mbox{ as }\rho\to\infty,

for some
\delta>0
, while
\mathrm{Var}[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)]\to\infty
.

Soshnikov’s theorem immediately implies, for example, that the standardized point counting statistics for the Ginibre ensemble (
1.1
), where
f
is the indicator function of a smooth domain, are asymptotically normal. For smooth statistics, the Ginibre ensemble (
1.1
) satisfies
the key relation (
1.9
) only in complex dimension
r>1
. While standardized smooth linear statistics of the Ginibre ensemble are also asymptotically normal in one complex dimension, the planar case requires ad hoc arguments
[
20
]
.

1.3.
Asymptotic normality for planar DPP’s

Rider and Virág introduced an abstract model to study the normality of standardized smooth statistics of DDP’s on certain Riemann surfaces
[
20
]
. The central assumption is an approximate form of analyticity of the correlation kernel, and applies to the planar Ginibre ensemble, as well as to its analogues on the sphere and unit disk.

A second technique was introduced by Ameur, Hedenmalm and Makarov to study the normality of fluctuations of eigenvalues of certain ensembles of random matrices under a confining potential of increasing strength
[
3
,
2
]
(see also Berman’s work in several complex variables
[
5
]
). While the corresponding correlation kernels are analytic, they do not have an explicit formula that would facilitate the approach of
[
20
,
21
]
.

Beyond the analytic setting, normality of fluctuations of smooth statistics was shown to hold for certain planar DDPs in
[
14
]
. Here, the correlation kernel is
polyanalytic
and the key insight is that the cumulants of (
1.5
) can be related by precise algebraic formulas to those of the analytic Ginibre ensemble, essentially reducing the problem to
[
21
]
.

The first goal of this article is to present a general result on asymptotic normality of smooth statistics for planar DPP’s with correlation kernels that satisfy the
reproducing property
(
1.2
).
The result we derive neither requires analyticity nor relies on algebraic relations and symmetries linking the correlation kernel to the Ginibre one. Our main insight is that the technique from
[
3
]
can be streamlined to
substitute many of the cancellations attributed to analyticity and radiality to instead rely on the reproducing property. The reproducing assumption is to a great extent optimal, since under mild assumptions non-reproducing kernels fall within the scope of Soshnikov’s theorem (see Sections
2
and
5
).

The result we present can be applied for example to infinite poly-analytic Ginibre ensembles considered with respect to general potentials, that is, infinite analogues of the finite ensembles introduced in
[
13
]
. These are related to Hilbert spaces analogous to (
1.4
) but with respect to general weights

Q:{\mathbb{C}}\to[0,\infty)
:

(1.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}=\Big\{F(z)e^{-Q(z)/2}:\bar{\partial}^{k}F=0,\int_{\mathbb{C}}|F(z)|^{2}e^{-Q(z)}\,dA(z)<\infty\Big\},

and cannot be related to their analytic counterparts by simple algebraic formulae, as done for standard potentials in
[
14
]
.

A second motivating application is to the kernels arising from the Schrödinger representation of the Weyl-Heisenberg group. Let
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be a smooth and fast decaying function satisfying
\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}|g|^{2}\,dA=1
. Then

(1.11)

\displaystyle K_{g}(z,w)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{g(t-x_{1})}g(t-x_{2})e^{2\pi it(y_{2}-y_{1})}\,dt,\qquad z=x_{1}+iy_{1},w=x_{2}+iy_{2}\in\mathbb{C},

is a locally trace class reproducing kernel acting on
L^{2}({\mathbb{C}},dA)

[
10
, Chapters 1 and 2]
. Non-smooth linear statistics for the associated DPP
have been considered in
[
1
,
18
]
, providing variance asymptotics. Under mild hypotheses, non-smooth statistics satisfy (
1.9
), and, as with the Ginibre ensemble, Soshnikov’s theorem readily shows that standardized statistics corresponding to the number of points within a growing disk are asymptotically normal (while the investigation of precise rates for such convergence is more challenging
[
9
]
). As an application of our main result, we shall conclude that Weyl-Heisenberg DPP’s (
1.11
) also enjoy asymptotically normal
smooth
linear statistics. Interestingly, the kernel (
1.11
) may exhibit no radial symmetries.

2.
Results

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}\subset L^{2}({\mathbb{C}},dA)
,
\rho>0
, be a family of reproducing Hilbert spaces, where
dA
is the Lebesgue area measure, and denote by
K_{\rho}:{\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{C}
the corresponding reproducing kernels. We will assume that the kernels
K_{\rho}
satisfy the following: there exist continuous
envelope functions

\phi_{\rho}:{\mathbb{C}}\to[0,\infty)
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle|K_{\rho}(z,w)|\leq\phi_{\rho}(z-w),\qquad z,w\in{\mathbb{C}}.

The envelops are further assumed to satisfy the following properties:

\bullet

(
Size
)

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{\rho>0}\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{C}}}\tfrac{1}{\rho}\phi_{\rho}(z)<\infty,

\bullet

(
Uniform integrability
)

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{\rho>0}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\phi_{\rho}\,dA<\infty,

\bullet

(
Interaction decay
)

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\rho\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}|z|^{3}\phi_{\rho}(z)dA(z)=0.

The above assumptions mean that the envelops behave qualitatively like dilations
\phi_{\rho}(z)=\rho\phi(\sqrt{\rho}z)
.
The model is essentially as in
[
20
]
, but, crucially, does not assume analyticity. On the other hand, here
K_{\rho}
is assumed to be reproducing, while

[
20
]
requires an asymptotic reproducing property for polynomials.

Remark 2.1
.

A positive kernel
K_{\rho}
with envelope
\phi_{\rho}
satisfying (
2.2
) is
locally trace class
, that is, for every compact set
B\subset\mathbb{C}
, the localized kernel
K^{B}_{\rho}(z,w)=1_{B}(z)K_{\rho}(z,w)1_{B}(w)
represents a trace-class integral operator. Indeed,
the operator with integral kernel
K^{B}_{\rho}
is positive because it is a compression of that with kernel
K_{\rho}
, while the corresponding trace is
\lesssim\rho|B|
by (
2.1
) — if
K
is continuous this follows from

\int_{\mathbb{C}}K^{B}_{\rho}(z,z)\,dA(z)=\int_{B}K_{\rho}(z,z)\,dA(z)\lesssim\rho|B|
,
see, e.g.,
[
23
, Theorem 2.1]
, while in general one can use an approximation argument. Hence,
if
K_{\rho}
is also reproducing, by the Soshnikov-Macchi theorem
[
17
]

[
24
, Theorem 3]
, it is the correlation kernel of a unique determinantal point process.

The following is our main result on asymptotic normality.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\{K_{\rho}:\rho>0\}
be a family of Hermitian symmetric reproducing kernels acting on
L^{2}({\mathbb{C}},dA)

with continuous envelopes
(
2.1
)
satisfying (
2.2
), (
2.3
), (
2.4
). Let
f
be a compactly supported, real-valued
C^{3}
test function. Then the cumulants of the order

of the variable
\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)
tend to zero as
\rho\rightarrow\infty
. As a consequence:

(i)

If
\mathrm{Var}\big[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)\big]\to\sigma^{2}

for some finite
\sigma\geq 0
, then
\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)-\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)]\to\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
in distribution;

(ii)

If
\liminf_{\rho\to\infty}\mathrm{Var}\big[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)\big]>0
, then
\widetilde{\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(}f)\to\mathcal{N}(0,1)
in distribution.

(In (i), the degenerate case

means that the limit variable is almost surely zero.)

While the setting of Theorem

resembles
the planar case of
[

]
, our proof builds mainly on
[

]
and is presented in Section

Theorem

is applicable for example to general

[

]
, where

is the reproducing kernel of the space (

) and

. Under mild assumptions on

, which concern finite-particle systems, shows that

. (While we are not aware of a citable reference for this fact, we expect to provide the particulars in a forthcoming article.)

Concerning translation invariant processes, we obtain the following application of Theorem
2.2
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
K:{\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a Hermitian symmetric reproducing kernel acting on

. Suppose that

(2.5)

\displaystyle|K(z,w)|=\phi(z-w),\qquad z,w\in{\mathbb{C}},

for a bounded continuous function

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}}(1+|z|^{3})\phi(z)\,dA(z)<\infty.

Define the dilated kernels

\displaystyle K_{\rho}(z,w)=\rho K(\sqrt{\rho}z,\sqrt{\rho}w),\qquad\rho>0.

For a compactly supported, real-valued

test function

set

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mu_{f}

\displaystyle:=\phi(0)\cdot\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}f(z)\,dA(z),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}_{f}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}(\nabla f(z)\cdot w)^{2}\phi(w)^{2}\,dA(z)dA(w).

Then

\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)-\rho\cdot\mu_{f}\to N(0,\sigma_{f}^{2})
in distribution.

Note that if
\phi(0)\not=0
Corollary
2.3
implies that (
1.9
) is not satisfied. A proof of Corollary
2.3
is presented in Section

. As an example, the Weyl-Heisenberg kernel (

, and a suitable envelope is provided by

\displaystyle\phi(z)=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}{g(t)}\overline{g(t-x)}e^{-2\pi ity}\,dt\Big|,\qquad z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}.

Concerning possible generalizations of Corollary
2.3
, we remark that an analogous setting in higher dimension leads to smooth observables with expectation and variance growing polynomially on
\rho
(see Remark

). Similarly, as we show in Proposition

,

also grow polynomially in

. Thus, in these cases, (

and Soshnikov’s asymptotic normality theorem can be invoked.

Before proceeding to the proofs, we make some final comments.
An abstract setting in which linear statistics can be shown to be asymptotically normal was recently introduced in
[
8
]
, where, moreover, quantitative convergence rates are derived. In the planar case, however,
[
8
, Model 1.2]
is not compatible with the envelope assumptions in Theorem
2.2
, and, as in Soshnikov’s result,
\mathrm{Var}[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)]
needs to diverge
as
\rho\to\infty

[
8
, Proposition 4.9]
.

Second, we remark that Theorem
2.2
can probably be refined to cover kernels which satisfy the assumptions only near the support of the test function, provided that (
1.2
) is replaced by a suitably local approximate formula
(while in analytic settings, reproducing formulas are exactly local). Such an extension would allow us to also treat finite particle systems in regimes where points congregate in a compact set (droplet) as long as the test function is supported away from its boundary. However, the expected gain in generality did not seem to merit the inclusion of additional technicalities. A more promising future direction is an extension of Theorem
2.2
to allow for droplets and test functions that interact with them, paralleling what is known in the model cases
[
21
,
4
,
14
]
.

4.
Asymptotic normality

The overall strategy is as follows. To show that smooth linear statistics are asymptotically normal we apply the cumulant method (see, e.g.,
[
19
, Appendix A.3]
). We use the exact expressions for cumulants derived in
[
25
]
to then obtain asymptotic expansions by Taylor expanding the test function to second order, as in
[
21
,
3
]
. Elaborating on
[
3
]
, we show that the reproducing property of the correlation kernel leads to certain significant cancellations, which allow us to conclude that higher order cumulants vanish asymptotically.

4.1.
Cumulants

Let us fix a family of reproducing kernels
K_{\rho}
satisfying (A1), (A2) and (A3) and a compactly supported
C^{3}
function
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that the kernel envelops are symmetric

\displaystyle\phi_{\rho}(z)=\phi_{\rho}(-z),\qquad z\in{\mathbb{C}},\rho>0.

The cyclic products of the correlation kernel
K_{\rho}
are denoted

\displaystyle R_{\rho,k}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}):=K_{\rho}(z_{1},z_{2})\cdots K_{\rho}(z_{k-1},z_{k})K_{\rho}(z_{k},z_{1}),\qquad z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\in\mathbb{C}.

These are instrumental to compute the cumulants of the linear statistics
\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)
. Indeed, let us associate with
f
the expression

(4.1)

\displaystyle G_{k}(z_{1},\ldots z_{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+\ldots+k_{j}=k\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{j}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{k!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{j}!}\prod_{l=1}^{j}f(z_{l})^{k_{l}},\qquad z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\in\mathbb{C}.

As shown in
[
25
, Lemma 1]
,
the cumulants
C_{\rho,k}(f)
of
\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)
are

(4.2)

\displaystyle C_{\rho,k}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{k}}G_{k}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})R_{\rho,k}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\,dA(z_{1})\cdots dA(z_{k}).

(This is a simplification of a more general formula in
[
25
, Lemma 1]
that concerns possibly non-reproducing kernels.)
By the reproducing property of the kernel
K_{\rho}
, we can extend integration in (
4.2
) to a new variable
z_{0}
and write

(4.3)

\displaystyle C_{\rho,k}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{k+1}}G_{k}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})R_{\rho,k+1}(z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\,dA(z_{0})dA(z_{1})\cdots dA(z_{k}).

This observation is a main tool in
[
3
]
and it is similarly fundamental for our proof.

4.2.
Taylor expansions

As in
[
21
,
3
]
we start by expanding
G_{k}
up to second order. We write

(4.4)

G_{k}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})=G_{k}(\vec{z_{0}})+{T^{1}_{k}}(z_{0},\ldots,z_{k})+{T^{2}_{k}}(z_{0},\ldots,z_{k})+{T^{*}_{k}}(z_{0},\ldots,z_{k})

where

(4.5)

\displaystyle\vec{z_{0}}

\displaystyle=(z_{0},\ldots,z_{0}),

(4.6)

\displaystyle{T^{1}_{k}}(z_{0},\ldots,z_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq r\leq k,1\leq j\leq 2}\nabla_{rj}G_{k}(\vec{z_{0}})\big(z_{r}^{(j)}-z_{0}^{(j)}\big),

(4.7)

\displaystyle{T^{2}_{k}}(z_{0},\ldots,z_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq r_{1},r_{2}\leq k,1\leq j_{1},j_{2}\leq 2}\nabla_{r_{1}j_{1}}\nabla_{r_{2}j_{2}}G_{k}(\vec{z_{0}})\big(z_{r_{1}}^{(j_{1})}-z_{0}^{(j_{1})}\big)\big(z_{r_{2}}^{(j_{2})}-z_{0}^{(j_{2})}\big)

because

(and hence

).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

\,R_{\rho,k+1}(z_{0},\hbox to7.66663pt{.\hss.\hss.},z_{k})\,dA(z_{0},\hbox to7.66663pt{.\hss.\hss.},z_{k})+\mathrm{o}(1),

B
, and

Proof.

Step 1
. Let
\varepsilon:=\mathrm{dist}(\operatorname{supp}(f),B^{c})>0

. We first show that

\displaystyle C_{\rho,k}(f)=\int_{B\times\mathbb{C}^{k}}G_{k}(z_{1},\hbox to7.5pt{.\hss.\hss.},z_{k})R_{\rho,k+1}(z_{0},\hbox to7.5pt{.\hss.\hss.},z_{k})\,dA(z_{0},\hbox to7.5pt{.\hss.\hss.},z_{k})+\mathrm{o}(1).

is a sum of terms of the form

is a power of

1
,
and not all
f_{j}
are
1
. Hence, it suffices to show that for each

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{\mathbb{C}^{k+1}}f_{1}(z_{1})\cdots f_{k}(z_{k})R_{\rho,k+1}(z_{0},\hbox to7.5pt{.\hss.\hss.},z_{k})\,dA(z_{0},\hbox to7.5pt{.\hss.\hss.},z_{k})\\

Moreover, we can assume that

is compactly supported; otherwise, it would be identically
1
and we would eliminate the variable

using the reproducing property of the kernel

are

).
We exploit the support of

and bound the cyclic product

2.2
) to the factor

and (

) to the others:

\displaystyle E_{k}:=\bigg|\int_{B^{c}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{k}}f_{1}(z_{1})\hbox to7.5pt{$\cdot$\hss$\cdot$\hss$\cdot$}f_{k}(z_{k})R_{\rho,k+1}(z_{0},\hbox to7.5pt{.\hss.\hss.},z_{k})\,dA(z_{0},\hbox to7.5pt{.\hss.\hss.},z_{k})\bigg|

Step 2
. By the Taylor expansion (
4.4
)
and (

), it suffices to prove that
for all

\int_{B\times{\mathbb{C}}^{k}}|z_{j}-z_{0}|^{3}R_{\rho,k+1}(z_{0},\hbox to7.5pt{.\hss.\hss.},z_{k})\,dA(z_{0},\hbox to7.5pt{.\hss.\hss.},z_{k})=\mathrm{o}(1).

∎

4.3.
Symmetry properties of cumulants

We now collect various symmetry properties of the functions
G_{k}
in the following lemma, which elaborates on
[
3
, Lemmas 3.1, 3.2, 3.3]
by recording certain additional symmetries implicit in the proof of those results.

Lemma 4.2
.

The following holds for all
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and

k\geq 3
:

(i)

G_{k}(\vec{z_{0}})=0
,

(ii)

\sum_{1\leq r\leq k}(\nabla_{rj}G_{k})(\vec{z_{0}})=0
for all
1\leq j\leq 2
,

(iii)

\sum_{1\leq r_{1},r_{2}\leq k}(\nabla_{r_{1}m}\nabla_{r_{2}l}G_{k})(\vec{z_{0}})=0
for all
1\leq m,l\leq 2
,

(iv)

\sum_{1\leq r\leq k}(\nabla_{rm}\nabla_{rl}G_{k})(\vec{z_{0}})=0
for all
1\leq l,m\leq 2
,

(v)

\sum_{1\leq r_{1}\neq r_{2}\leq k}(\nabla_{r_{1}m}\nabla_{r_{2}l}G_{k})(\vec{z_{0}})=0
, for all
1\leq m,l\leq 2
.

Proof.

The proof of (i) is exactly as in
[
3
, Lemma 3.1]
. Parts (ii) and (iii) follow by taking derivatives on (i). We now turn to (iv), that is a refined version of
[
3
, Lemma 3.3]
which shows that
\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{2k}}G_{k}(\vec{z_{0}})=0
.

Let
k_{1},\ldots,k_{j}
be positive integers such that
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{j}=k
. Then for
1\leq r\leq j
,
1\leq m,l\leq 2
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}
we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\nabla_{rm}\nabla_{rl}\bigg(\prod_{n=1}^{j}f(z_{n})^{k_{n}}\bigg)\\
&\quad=\prod_{1\leq n\leq j,n\neq r}f(z_{n})^{k_{n}}\bigg(k_{r}(k_{r}-1)f(z_{r})^{k_{r}-2}\nabla_{m}f(z_{r})\nabla_{l}f(z_{r})+k_{r}f(z_{r})^{k_{r}-1}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{l}f(z_{r})\bigg).\end{aligned}

This gives

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq r\leq k}(\nabla_{rm}\nabla_{rl}G_{k})(\vec{z_{0}})=f(z_{0})^{k-2}\nabla_{m}f(z_{0})\nabla_{l}f(z_{0})\cdot S_{k}+f(z_{0})^{k-1}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{l}f(z_{0})\cdot S^{\prime}_{k},

where

\displaystyle S_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+\ldots+k_{j}=k\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{j}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{k!(k_{1}(k_{1}-1)+\cdots+k_{j}(k_{j}-1))}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{j}!},

\displaystyle S^{\prime}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+\ldots+k_{j}=k\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{j}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{k\cdot k!}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{j}!}.

S_{k}
is shown to be zero in the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 3.3]
(see Eqs. (3.5) and (3.9)
in
[
3
]
), while
S^{\prime}_{k}
shown to be zero in
[
3
, Lemma 3.1 and Eq. (3.1)]
.

Finally, (v) follows by combining (iii) and (iv). ∎

be a compact set such that
\varepsilon:=\mathrm{dist}(\operatorname{supp}(f),B^{c})>0

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Var}[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)]&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\big[f(z)-f(w)\big]^{2}|K_{\rho}(z,w)|^{2}\,dA(w)dA(z)\\

f
to order 1 we see that

\displaystyle[f(w)-f(z)]^{2}=[\nabla f(z)\cdot(w-z)]^{2}+E(z,w),

where
|E(z,w)|\leq C|z-w|^{3}
since
f\in C^{3}
and compactly supported. Thus

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{B}\int_{\mathbb{C}}|E(z,w)||K_{\rho}(z,w)|^{2}\,dA(w)dA(z)\\
&\qquad\lesssim\rho^{2}\int_{B}\int_{\mathbb{C}}|z-w|^{3}\phi(\sqrt{\rho}(z-w))\,dA(w)dA(z)=O(\rho^{-1/2}).\end{aligned}

Combining (
5.1
), (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\big[\nabla f(z)\cdot(w-z)\big]^{2}|K_{\rho}(z,w)|^{2}\,dA(w)dA(z)+O(\rho^{-1/2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}\int_{B}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\big[\nabla f(z)\cdot(w-z)\big]^{2}\phi(\sqrt{\rho}(z-w))^{2}\,dA(w)dA(z)+O(\rho^{-1/2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}\int_{B}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\big[\nabla f(z)\cdot u\big]^{2}\phi(\sqrt{\rho}u)^{2}\,dA(u)dA(z)+O(\rho^{-1/2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}\rho\int_{\mathbb{C}}\big[\nabla f(z)\cdot(\sqrt{\rho}u)\big]^{2}\phi(\sqrt{\rho}u)^{2}\,dA(u)dA(z)+O(\rho^{-1/2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\big[\nabla f(z)\cdot u\big]^{2}\phi(u)^{2}\,dA(u)dA(z)+O(\rho^{-1/2}).

We now invoke Theorem
2.2
(part (i)). Suitable envelopes are provided by
\phi_{\rho}(z)=\rho\phi(\sqrt{\rho}z)
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

If we consider a reproducing kernel
K
acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, satisfying
|K(z,w)|=\phi(z-w)
, and dilate it by
K_{\rho}(z,w)=\rho^{d/2}K\big(\sqrt{\rho}(z-w)\big)
, then under similar assumptions as in Corollary
2.3
reinspection of the proof of Corollary
2.3
shows

(5.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)]=\frac{1}{2}\rho^{d/2-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[\nabla f(z)\cdot u\big]^{2}\phi(u)^{2}\,dA(u)dA(z)+O(\rho^{(d-3)/2}).

Thus, for
d>2
, whenever the integral in (
5.4
) does not vanish,
\mathrm{Var}[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)]
is of order
\rho^{d/2-1}
for large
\rho
. On the other hand,
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(|f|)]=\rho^{d/2}\cdot\phi(0)\cdot\int|f|
. Thus, (
1.9
) is satisfied for some
\delta>0
, and Soshnikov’s asymptotic normality theorem is applicable.

Similarly, we now note that if we relax the assumptions of Corollary
2.3
to allow for non-reproducing kernels, then each such kernel leads to smooth observables with variance of order at least
\rho
(and therefore falls within the scope of Soshnikov’s normality theorem).

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
K:{\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a continuous Hermitian symmetric kernel acting boundedly on
L^{2}({\mathbb{C}},dA)
and satisfying (
2.5
) with
\phi
continuous and bounded. Instead of assuming the reproducing property, suppose only that the spectrum of
T_{K}
, the integral operator with kernel
K
, satisfies

(5.5)

\displaystyle\sigma(T_{K})\subset[0,1],

so that there exists a unique DPP associated with
K
. Define the dilated kernels
K_{\rho}
by (
2.7
).

Suppose that
K
is not reproducing (i.e.,
T_{K}\not=T_{K}^{2}
). Then, for every non-zero compactly supported
C^{3}
test function
f
,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\rho\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho}\mathrm{Var}[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)]>0.

Proof.

First note that the operator
T_{K}
is locally trace class by assumption (
2.5
). This fact along with (
5.5
) guarantee the existence of a unique DPP associated with
K
(see Remark
2.1
).

Let
f
be a non-zero compactly supported
C^{3}
test function. Taking into account that
K
is not reproducing, we write
\mathrm{Var}[\mathrm{Tr}_{\rho}(f)]
as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}\big[f(z)-f(w)\big]^{2}|K_{\rho}(z,w)|^{2}\,dA(z,w)+\int_{\mathbb{C}}f(z)^{2}\Big[K_{\rho}(z,z)-\int_{\mathbb{C}}|K_{\rho}(z,w)|^{2}\,dA(w)\Big]\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\int_{\mathbb{C}}f(z/\sqrt{\rho})^{2}\Big[K(z,z)-\int_{\mathbb{C}}|K(z,w)|^{2}\,dA(w)\Big]\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\qquad=\rho\cdot\int_{\mathbb{C}}f^{2}\,dA\cdot\big[\phi(0)-\int_{\mathbb{C}}\phi^{2}\,dA\big],

where the first line is a general fact
[
25
, Equation 5]
, and the last equality follows from (
2.5
).

Since
f
is non-zero, to prove (
5.6
), we only need to observe that for non-reproducing
K
,
\phi(0)-\int_{\mathbb{C}}\phi^{2}\,dA\not=0
. Suppose on the contrary that
\phi(0)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\phi^{2}\,dA
. Then

K(z,z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}K(z,w)K(w,z)\,dA(w),\qquad z\in{\mathbb{C}}.

This means that the diagonals of the integral kernels of the operators
T_{K}
and
T_{K}^{2}
coincide. On the other hand, by (
5.5
),
T_{K}-T_{k}^{2}
is a positive operator. Thus
\mathrm{trace}(T_{K}-T_{K}^{2})=0
and
T_{K}=T_{K}^{2}
— see, e.g.,
[
23
, Theorem 2.1]
— which contradicts the assumption that
K
is not reproducing.
∎

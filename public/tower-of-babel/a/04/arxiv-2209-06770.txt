Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we extend the main results of a 2024
Advances in Applied Mathematics
paper
[
28
]
about Apéry-type series involving central binomial coefficients and the multiple (
t-
)harmonic sums to parametric Apéry-type series involving parametric binomial coefficients and Hurwitz-type multiple harmonic (star) sums. In particular, we will establish many explicit relations between parametric Apéry-type series involving one or two parametric binomial coefficients and Hurwitz-type multiple zeta values (with
r
-variables) by using the method of iterated integrals.

Keywords
: Parametric Apéry-type series; (colored) multiple zeta values; Hurwitz-type multiple zeta values; Hurwitz-type multiple harmonic (star) sums; Hurwitz-type multiple
T
-values.

1
Introduction

We begin with some basic notations. Let
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{N}^{-}
,
\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
be the set of positive integers, negative integers, rational numbers, real numbers and complex numbers, respectively, and
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{-}:=\mathbb{N}^{-}\cup\{0\}
.
A finite sequence
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}:=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
is called a
composition
. We put

|{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}|:=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r},\quad\dep({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=r,

and call them the weight and the depth of
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
, respectively. If
k_{1}>1
,
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
is called
admissible
.
As a convention, we denote by
\{m\}_{r}
the sequence of
m
’s with
r
repetitions.

For a composition
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and positive integer
n
, the
multiple harmonic sums
and
multiple harmonic star sums
are defined by

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}\in\mathbb{Q}\quad\text{and}\quad\zeta^{\star}_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}>0}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}\in\mathbb{Q},

(1.1)

respectively. If
n<r
then
{\zeta_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=0
and
{\zeta_{n}}(\emptyset)={\zeta^{\star}_{n}}(\emptyset):=1
. When
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
is admissible, by taking the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
in (
1.1
) we get the
multiple zeta values
(MZVs) and the
multiple zeta star values
, respectively

\displaystyle{\zeta}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\zeta_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})\in\mathbb{R}\quad\text{and}\quad{\zeta^{\star}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\zeta^{\star}_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})\in\mathbb{R}.

When the depth is 1, we recover the classical Riemann zeta values
\zeta(n)
.

As is well known, the transcendence of
\zeta(2n+1)
is still an unsolved problem. In 1979, Apéry
[
2
]
shocked the math world by showing that
\zeta(3)
is irrational. His key idea is to use the series expansion of the form

\zeta(3)=\frac{5}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k^{3}\binom{2k}{k}}.

(1.2)

We say a series is of
Apéry-type
if it is obtained from a (variant of the) MZV series by multiplying or dividing a factor of some binomial coefficient. In this paper, we will mainly study these series, but often with the binomial coefficient appearing in the numerator, contrary to what Apéry did in (
1.2
) or the series
considered in
[
6
,
7
]
.

The systematic study of MZVs began in the early 1990s with the works of Hoffman
[
11
]
and Zagier
[
31
]
. Due to their surprising and sometimes mysterious appearance in the study of many branches of mathematics and theoretical physics, these special values have attracted a lot of attention and interest in the past three decades (for example, see the book by the last author
[
33
]
).

In general, let
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
{\boldsymbol{\sl{z}}}=(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{r})
where
z_{1},\dotsc,z_{r}
are the
N
th roots of unity. We can define the
colored MZVs
of level
N
by

\Li_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{z}}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}\in\mathbb{C},

(1.3)

which converges if
({{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{z}}})
is
admissible
, that is,
(k_{1},z_{1})\neq(1,1)
(see
[
33
, Ch. 15]
). The level two colored MZVs are often called
Euler sums
or
alternating MZVs
. In this case, namely,
when
(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{r})\in\{\pm 1\}^{r}
and
(k_{1},z_{1})\neq(1,1)
, we denote

\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{z}}})=\Li_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{z}}})
. Further, we put a bar on top of

k_{j}
if
z_{j}=-1
. For example,

\zeta(\bar{2},3,\bar{1},4)=\zeta(2,3,1,4;-1,1,-1,1).

More generally, for any composition
(k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, the
multiple polylogarithm function
with
r
-variables is defined by

\displaystyle\Li_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}})\equiv\Li_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r}}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{r}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\frac{x_{1}^{n_{1}}\dotsm x_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\dotsm n_{r}^{k_{r}}}

(1.4)

which converges if
|x_{1}\cdots x_{j}|<1
for all
j=1,\dotsc,r
. They can be analytically continued to a multi-valued meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}^{r}
(see
[
32
]
). In particular, if
x_{1}=x,x_{2}=\cdots=x_{r}=1
, then
\Li_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(x,\{1\}_{r-1})
is the single-variable multiple polylogarithm function
\Li_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(x)
.

Kaneko and Tsumura
[
13
,
14
]
introduced the following new kind of multiple polylogarithm functions of level two

\displaystyle{\rm A}(k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r};x):

\displaystyle=2^{r}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0\atop n_{j}\equiv r+1-j\pmod{2}}{\frac{{{x^{{n_{1}}}}}}{{n_{1}^{{k_{1}}}n_{2}^{{k_{2}}}\cdots n_{r}^{{k_{r}}}}}}

\displaystyle=2^{r}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m_{1}>\cdots>m_{r}>0}{\frac{{{x^{{2m_{1}-r}}}}}{{(2m_{1}-r)^{{k_{1}}}(2m_{2}-r+1)^{{k_{2}}}\dotsm(2m_{r}-1)^{{k_{r}}}}}},

(1.5)

where
|x|\leq 1
,
k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r}
are positive integers and
(k_{1},x)\neq(1,1)
. We call them
Kaneko–Tsumura
A
-functions
. Note that
2^{-r}{\rm A}(k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r};x)
is denoted by
{\rm Ath}(k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r};x)
in
[
13
]
. Clearly, if
k_{1}>1
and
x=1
,
then the Kaneko–Tsumura A-functions in (
1
) reduce to the
multiple
T
-values
defined by

\displaystyle T(k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r}):

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0\atop n_{j}\equiv r+1-j\pmod{2}}\frac{2^{r}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}n_{2}^{k_{2}}\dotsm n_{r}^{k_{r}}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m_{1}>\cdots>m_{r}>0}{\frac{2^{r}}{{(2m_{1}-r)^{{k_{1}}}(2m_{2}-r+1)^{{k_{2}}}\dotsm(2m_{r}-1)^{{k_{r}}}}}}.

(1.6)

Similarly to the multiple harmonic sums and multiple harmonic star sums, for a composition
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and positive integer
n
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{N}_{0}^{-}
, we define the
Hurwitz-type multiple harmonic sums
and
Hurwitz-type multiple harmonic star sums
by

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};\alpha):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\frac{1}{(n_{1}+\alpha-1)^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}+\alpha-1)^{k_{r}}}

(1.7)

and

\displaystyle\zeta^{\star}_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};\alpha):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}>0}\frac{1}{(n_{1}+\alpha-1)^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}+\alpha-1)^{k_{r}}},

(1.8)

respectively. If
n<r
then
{\zeta_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};\alpha):=0
and
{\zeta_{n}}(\emptyset;\alpha)={\zeta^{\star}_{n}}(\emptyset;\alpha):=1
. Further, for
k_{1}>1
, we let

\displaystyle\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};\alpha):=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\zeta_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};\alpha)\quad\text{and}\quad{\zeta^{\star}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};\alpha):=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\zeta^{\star}_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};\alpha)

(1.9)

and call them
Hurwitz-type multiple zeta values

[
15
]
and
Hurwitz-type multiple zeta star values
, respectively. Similarly, for
k_{1}>1
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{N}_{0}^{-}
, we define the
Hurwitz-type multiple
T
-values
by

\displaystyle T({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};\alpha)

\displaystyle\equiv T(k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r};\alpha)

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m_{1}>\cdots>m_{r}>0}{\frac{2^{r}}{{(2m_{1}-r-1+\alpha)^{{k_{1}}}(2m_{2}-r+\alpha)^{{k_{2}}}\dotsm(2m_{r}-2+\alpha)^{{k_{r}}}}}}.

(1.10)

Clearly,
\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};1)=\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}),\zeta^{\star}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};1)=\zeta^{\star}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})
and
T({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};1)=T({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})
.

More generally, for a composition
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, positive integer
n
and
{\boldsymbol{\sl{a}}}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
with all
a_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{N}_{0}^{-}
, we define the Hurwitz-type multiple harmonic sums and Hurwitz-type multiple harmonic star sums with
r
-variables by

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{a}}})\equiv\zeta_{n}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r};a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\frac{1}{(n_{1}+a_{1}-1)^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}+a_{r}-1)^{k_{r}}},

(1.11)

and

\displaystyle\zeta^{\star}_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{a}}})\equiv\zeta_{n}^{\star}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r};a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}>0}\frac{1}{(n_{1}+a_{1}-1)^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}+a_{r}-1)^{k_{r}}},

(1.12)

respectively. If
n<r
then
{\zeta_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{a}}}):=0
and
{\zeta_{n}}(\emptyset;{\boldsymbol{\sl{a}}})={\zeta^{\star}_{n}}(\emptyset;{\boldsymbol{\sl{a}}}):=1
. Further, for
k_{1}>1
, let

\displaystyle\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{a}}}):=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\zeta_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{a}}})\quad\text{and}\quad{\zeta^{\star}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{a}}}):=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\zeta^{\star}_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{a}}}),

(1.13)

and call them Hurwitz-type multiple zeta values and Hurwitz-type multiple zeta star values with
r
-variables, respectively. For convenience, if
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{r}=a
in (
1.11
)-(
1.13
), let
\zeta_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};a):=\zeta_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};\{a\}_{r}),\zeta^{\star}_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};a):=\zeta^{\star}_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};\{a\}_{r}),\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};a):=\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};\{a\}_{r})
and
\zeta^{\star}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};a):=\zeta^{\star}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};\{a\}_{r})
.

In particular, if all
a_{j}=1/2
then (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) essentially reduce to the

multiple t-harmonic sum
and
multiple t-harmonic star sum
respectively defined by
[
28
]

\displaystyle t_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{(2n_{j}-1)^{k_{j}}}\quad\text{and}\quad t^{\star}_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{(2n_{j}-1)^{k_{j}}}.

If
n<r
then
{t_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=0
and
{t_{n}}(\emptyset)={t^{\star}_{n}}(\emptyset):=1
. When taking the limit

we get the so-called

t
-values
and
multiple
t
-star values
, respectively, see
[
12
]
. Clearly, the multiple
T
-values
[
14
]
and
multiple mixed values

[
26
]
are also special cases of the Hurwitz-type MZVs with
r
-variables. For example, letting
a_{j}=(j+1-r)/2\ (j=1,2,\ldots,r)
in the first formula of (

) gives the multiple
T
-value

\displaystyle\zeta\left(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{r};1-\frac{r}{2},1-\frac{r-1}{2},\ldots,1-\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{r}}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\frac{1}{(2n_{1}-r)^{k_{1}}(2n_{2}-r+1)^{k_{2}}\cdots(2n_{r}-1)^{k_{r}}}

\displaystyle=2^{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{r}-r}T(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{r}).

(1.14)

In this paper, by considering several integrals involving multiple polylogarithm, we will find some explicit relations between the Hurwitz-type multiple zeta values and the following two parametric Apéry-type series

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{k};1-\alpha)}{n^{k_{1}+1}\binom{n-\alpha}{n}},

(1.15)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{k};\alpha)\zeta^{\star}_{n}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})}{n^{k_{1}}}\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n},

(1.16)

where
(k,k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
,
\Re(\alpha)<1
with
\alpha\notin\mathbb{N}_{0}^{-}
, and the general binomial coefficient
\binom{a}{b}
is denoted by

\binom{a}{b}:=\frac{\Gamma(a+1)}{\Gamma(b+1)\Gamma(a-b+1)}\quad\text{for}\ a,b,a-b\notin\mathbb{N}^{-}.

In particular, if
a=n-1/2
and
b=n\ (n\in\mathbb{N})
, we have

\binom{n-1/2}{n}=\frac{1}{4^{n}}\binom{2n}{n}.

Furthermore, we also study some special cases of the following Apéry-type series involving two parametric binomial coefficients

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r};\alpha)\zeta_{n}^{\star}(l_{1},\ldots,l_{p};1-\beta)}{n^{m+2}}\frac{\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}}{\binom{n-\beta}{n}}\,,\quad m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(1.17)

Letting
(\alpha,\beta)\rightarrow(0,0)
in (
1.17
) and applying (

), we can recover the Kaneko–Yamamoto multiple zeta value
\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}\circledast{\boldsymbol{\sl{l}}}^{\star})

[
17
]
defined by

\displaystyle\zeta({{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}\circledast{{\boldsymbol{\sl{l}}}}^{\star}):

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{0<m_{r}<\cdots<m_{1}=n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{p}>0}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}n_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots n_{p}^{l_{p}}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})\zeta^{\star}_{n}(l_{2},\ldots,l_{p})}{n^{k_{1}+l_{1}}},

(1.18)

where
{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and
{{\boldsymbol{\sl{l}}}}=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{p})
are two compositions. It should be emphasized that Kaneko and Yamamoto presented a new “integral
=

(see

\displaystyle\xi(p;{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}})=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\{1\}_{p-1})}{n^{k_{1}+1}}=\zeta({{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}\circledast\{\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{p}\}^{\star})

are special cases of the Kaneko–Yamamoto MZVs (see
[

]

Remark 1.1
.

From the asymptotic expansion for the ratio of two gamma functions (see
[
19
, Sections 2.3 and 2.11]
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(z+a)}{\Gamma(z+b)}=z^{a-b}\left(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{z}\Big)\right)\quad\text{for }|{\rm arg}(z)|\leq\pi-\varepsilon,\ \varepsilon>0,\ |z|\rightarrow\infty.

Hence, for (
1.17
), by a direct calculation, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}}{\binom{n-\beta}{n}}=\frac{\Gamma(1-\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\frac{1}{n^{1-\alpha-\beta}}\left(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{n}\Big)\right),\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Thus, for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and complex numbers
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{N}_{0}^{-}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{N}
with

, the Apéry-type series involving two parametric binomial coefficients in (
1.17

2
Apéry-type series with one parametric binomial coefficient

In this section, we will use the iterated integrals to prove some interesting results involving Apéry-type series with one parametric binomial coefficient.

2.1
Iterated Integrals and Notations

The theory of iterated integrals was developed firstly by K.T. Chen in the 1960’s
[
8
,
9
]
. It has played important roles in the study of algebraic topology and algebraic geometry in the past half century. Its simplest form is

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f_{p}(t)dtf_{p-1}(t)dt\cdots f_{1}(t)dt:=\displaystyle\int_{a<t_{p}<\cdots<t_{1}<b}f_{p}(t_{p})f_{p-1}(t_{p-1})\cdots f_{1}(t_{1})dt_{1}dt_{2}\cdots dt_{p}.

In particular, according to the definitions, for any (admissible) composition
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}:=(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{N}^{-}
, we have

\displaystyle\Li_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}(x)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\left(\frac{dt}{1-t}\right)\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r}-1}\cdots\left(\frac{dt}{1-t}\right)\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1},

(2.19)

\displaystyle{\rm A}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};x)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\left(\frac{2dt}{1-t^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r}-1}\cdots\left(\frac{2dt}{1-t^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1},

(2.20)

\displaystyle\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};1+\alpha)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{\alpha}dt}{1-t}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r}-1}\left(\frac{dt}{1-t}\right)\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r-1}-1}\cdots\left(\frac{dt}{1-t}\right)\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1},

(2.21)

\displaystyle T({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};1+\alpha)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{2t^{\alpha}dt}{1-t^{2}}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r}-1}\left(\frac{2dt}{1-t^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r-1}-1}\cdots\left(\frac{2dt}{1-t^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1}.

(2.22)

More generally, by the definition of Hurwitz-type MZVs, we have the following iterated integral expressions

\displaystyle\zeta(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{r};a_{1}+1,a_{2}+1,\ldots,a_{r}+1)

\displaystyle\quad=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{a_{r}}dt}{1-t}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r}-1}\frac{t^{a_{r-1}-a_{r}}dt}{1-t}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r-1}-1}\cdots\frac{t^{a_{1}-a_{2}}dt}{1-t}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1}

(2.23)

and

\displaystyle\zeta(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{r};a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}+1,a_{2}+\cdots+a_{r}+1,\ldots,a_{r}+1)

\displaystyle\quad=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{a_{r}}dt}{1-t}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r}-1}\frac{t^{a_{r-1}}dt}{1-t}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r-1}-1}\cdots\frac{t^{a_{1}}dt}{1-t}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1}.

(2.24)

For convenience, for compositions
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}:=(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})
and
{\boldsymbol{\sl{j}}}:=(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{r})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}
, let
\overleftarrow{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}:=(k_{r},\ldots,k_{2},k_{1})
be the reversal of
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
and

B({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{j}}}):=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\binom{k_{i}+j_{i}-1}{j_{i}}.

Moreover, for two such
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
and
{\boldsymbol{\sl{j}}}
of the same depth, we denote by
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}+{\boldsymbol{\sl{j}}}
the composition obtained by adding the corresponding components.

The Hoffman dual of a composition
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
is
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}^{\vee}=(k^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,k^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}})
determined by

|{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}|:=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}=k^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+k^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}}
and

\{1,2,\ldots,|{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}|-1\}=\Big\{\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{j}k_{i}\Big\}_{j=1}^{r-1}\coprod\Big\{\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{j}k_{i}^{\prime}\Big\}_{j=1}^{r^{\prime}-1}.

Here
\coprod
denotes the union of two disjoint sets. Equivalently,
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}^{\vee}
can be obtained from
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
by swapping the commas “,” and the plus signs “+” in the expression

{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(\underbrace{1+\cdots+1}_{\text{$k_{1}$ times}},\dotsc,\underbrace{1+\cdots+1}_{\text{$k_{r}$ times}}).

(2.25)

For example, we have

({1,1,2,1})^{\vee}=(3,2)\quad\text{and}\quad({1,2,1,1})^{\vee}=(2,3).
More generally, one obtains

\displaystyle{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}^{\vee}=(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{k_{1}}+\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{k_{2}}+1,\ldots,1+\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{k_{r}}).

(2.26)

In particular, setting

Theorem 2.5
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{N}_{0}^{-}
with
x\in(-1,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\frac{\log^{k}(1-x)}{(1-x)^{\alpha}}=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{k};\alpha)x^{n}.

(2.30)

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x)^{\alpha}}=1+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha)_{n}}{n!}x^{n}=1+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}x^{n}\,,\quad x\in(-1,1),

(2.31)

where
(\alpha)_{n}
represents the
Pochhammer symbol
(or the
shifted factorial
) given
by

(\alpha)_{n}=\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n-1)\quad\text{and}\quad(\alpha)_{0}:=1.

(2.32)

From
[
22
]
, we have

\displaystyle\log^{k}(1-x)=(-1)^{k}k!\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(\{1\}_{k-1})}{n}x^{n}\,,\quad x\in[-1,1).

(2.33)

By the
Cauchy product
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\frac{\log^{k}(1-x)}{(1-x)^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\{\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(\{1\}_{k-1})}{n}+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i+j=n\atop i,j\geq 1}\frac{\zeta_{i-1}(\{1\}_{k-1})}{i}\cdot\frac{(\alpha)_{j}}{j!}\right\}\cdot x^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial\alpha^{k}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-x)^{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{k!}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial^{k}(\alpha)_{n}}{\partial\alpha^{k}}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

(2.34)

Then,

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(\{1\}_{k-1})}{n}+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i+j=n\atop i,j\geq 1}\frac{\zeta_{i-1}(\{1\}_{k-1})}{i}\cdot\frac{(\alpha)_{j}}{j!}=\frac{1}{k!n!}\frac{\partial^{k}(\alpha)_{n}}{\partial\alpha^{k}}=:a_{n}^{(\alpha)}(k).

(2.35)

By a simple calculation,
\frac{\partial^{k}(\alpha)_{n}}{\partial\alpha^{k}}
satisfy a recurrence relation in the form

\displaystyle\frac{{{\partial^{k}}{{\left(\alpha\right)}_{n}}}}{{\partial{\alpha^{k}}}}=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{i}\frac{\partial^{i}(\alpha)_{n}}{\partial\alpha^{i}}\left[\psi^{(k-i-1)}(\alpha+n)-\psi^{(k-i-1)}(\alpha)\right]\,,\quad\text{for }k\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.36)

Here,
\psi^{(m)}(x)
stands for the polygamma function of order
m
defined by

\psi^{(m)}(x):=\frac{d^{m}}{dx^{m}}\psi(x)=\frac{d^{m+1}}{dx^{m+1}}\log\Gamma(x).

Thus

{\psi^{\left(0\right)}}(x)=\psi(x)=\frac{{\Gamma^{\prime}(x)}}{{\Gamma(x)}}

holds, where
\psi(x)
is the digamma function. The functions
{\psi^{(m)}}(x)
satisfy the following relations:

\displaystyle\psi(x)

\displaystyle=-\gamma+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k+1}-\frac{1}{k+x}\right),

\displaystyle{\psi^{\left(n\right)}}(x)

\displaystyle={\left({-1}\right)^{n+1}}n!\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(x+k)^{n+1}},

\displaystyle\psi\left({x+n}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{{x+1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{{x+n-1}}+\psi(x),

for
x\notin\mathbb{N}^{-}_{0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Here,
\gamma
denotes the
Euler-Mascheroni constant
, defined by

\gamma:=\mathop{\displaystyle\lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}\left({\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{1}{k}}-\log n}\right)=-\psi\left(1\right)\approx{\rm{0}}{\rm{.577215664901532860606512}}....

From (
2.36
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}(\alpha)_{n}}{\partial\alpha^{k}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{i}\cdot\frac{\partial^{i}(\alpha)_{n}}{\partial\alpha^{i}}\cdot[\psi^{(k-i-1)}(\alpha+n)-\psi^{(k-i-1)}(\alpha)]

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{i}\cdot\frac{\partial^{i}(\alpha)_{n}}{\partial\alpha^{i}}\cdot(-1)^{k-i}(k-i-1)!\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\left\{\frac{1}{(m+\alpha+n)^{k-i}}-\frac{1}{(m+\alpha)^{k-i}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{(k-1)!}{i!(k-i-1)!}\cdot\frac{\partial^{i}(\alpha)_{n}}{\partial\alpha^{i}}\cdot(-1)^{k-i}(k-i-1)!(-1)\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{(m+\alpha)^{k-i}}

\displaystyle=(k-1)!(-1)^{k-1}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}\cdot\frac{1}{i!}\cdot\frac{\partial^{i}(\alpha)_{n}}{\partial\alpha^{i}}\cdot\zeta_{n}(k-i;\alpha)\,.

Hence, we obtain the recurrence relation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\cdot\frac{\partial^{k}(\alpha)_{n}}{\partial\alpha^{k}}=\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}\cdot\frac{1}{i!}\cdot\frac{\partial^{i}(\alpha)_{n}}{\partial\alpha^{i}}\cdot\zeta_{n}(k-i;\alpha).

(2.37)

In particular, if
k=0
then
(\alpha)_{n}=\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n-1)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+n)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}
.

In Lemma
2.4
, letting
x_{k}={1}/(k+\alpha-1)
yields

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{m};\alpha)=\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{m}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{i}\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{i};\alpha)\zeta_{n}(m-i;\alpha).

(2.38)

Multiplying (
2.38
) by
(\alpha)_{n}
, then comparing it with (
2.37
), we obtain
\frac{\partial^{k}(\alpha)_{n}}{\partial\alpha^{k}}=k!(\alpha)_{n}\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{k};\alpha)
. Thus, we get

\displaystyle a_{n}^{(\alpha)}(k)=\frac{k!}{k!n!}(\alpha)_{n}\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{k};\alpha)=\frac{(\alpha)_{n}}{n!}\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{k};\alpha)=\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{k};\alpha).

(2.39)

Combining (
2.2
), (
2.35
) and (
2.39
), we obtain the desired formula.
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(\alpha)<1
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}{x^{n-1}\frac{\log^{k}(1-x)}{(1-x)^{\alpha}}dx}=(-1)^{k}k!\frac{1}{n\binom{n-\alpha}{n}}\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{k};1-\alpha).

(2.40)

Proof.

We note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}{x^{n-1}\frac{\log^{k}(1-x)}{(1-x)^{\alpha}}dx}=\frac{\partial^{k}B(a,b)}{\partial b^{k}}\bigg|_{a=n\atop b=1-\alpha},

(2.41)

where
B(a,b)
is the
beta function
defined by

B\left({a,b}\right):=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}{{x^{a-1}}{{\left({1-x}\right)}^{b-1}}dx}=\frac{{\Gamma\left(a\right)\Gamma\left(b\right)}}{{\Gamma\left({a+b}\right)}}\,,\quad\text{for }{\mathop{\Re}\nolimits}\left(a\right)>0,{\mathop{\Re}\nolimits}\left(b\right)>0.

(2.42)

By using the definitions of the beta function
{B\left(a,b\right)}
and the digamma function
\psi(x)
, it is obvious that

\frac{{\partial B\left(a,b\right)}}{{\partial a}}=B\left(a,b\right)\left[{\psi\left(a\right)-\psi\left(a+b\right)}\right].

Therefore, differentiating this equality
k-1
times, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}B(a,b)}{\partial a^{k}}=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{i}\frac{\partial^{i}B(a,b)}{\partial a^{i}}\cdot\Big[\psi^{(k-i-1)}(a)-\psi^{(k-i-1)}(a+b)\Big].

(2.43)

Note that
B(a,b)=B(b,a)
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial b^{k}}B(a,b)\bigg|_{a=n\atop b=1-\alpha}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{i}\frac{\partial^{i}B(a,b)}{\partial b^{i}}\bigg|_{a=n\atop b=1-\alpha}(-1)^{k-i}(k-i-1)!

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left\{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\alpha+m)^{k-i}}-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\alpha+n+m)^{k-i}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{i}\frac{\partial^{i}B(a,b)}{\partial b^{i}}\bigg|_{a=n\atop b=1-\alpha}(-1)^{k-i}(k-i-1)!\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{(\alpha+m)^{k-i}}

\displaystyle=(k-1)!(-1)^{k}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{i!}\frac{\partial^{i}B(a,b)}{\partial b^{i}}\bigg|_{a=n\atop b=1-\alpha}\zeta_{n}(k-i;\alpha).

This yields the recurrence relation

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\cdot\frac{\partial^{k}B(a,b)}{\partial b^{k}}\right\}\bigg|_{a=n\atop b=1-\alpha}=\frac{1}{k}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\left\{\frac{(-1)^{i}}{i!}\cdot\frac{\partial^{i}B(a,b)}{\partial b^{i}}\right\}\bigg|_{a=n\atop b=1-\alpha}\zeta_{n}(k-i;1-\alpha).

(2.44)

In particular, if
k=0
then

B(n,1-\alpha)=\frac{1}{n\binom{n-\alpha}{n}}.

On the other hand, taking
x_{k}=\frac{1}{k-\alpha}
in Lemma
2.3
we see that

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{m};1-\alpha)=\frac{1}{m}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m-1}\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{i};1-\alpha)\zeta_{n}(m-i;1-\alpha).

(2.45)

Multiplying (
2.45
) by
\frac{1}{n\binom{n-\alpha}{n}}
and comparing it with (
2.44
) yield

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\frac{\partial^{k}B(a,b)}{\partial b^{k}}\bigg|_{a=n\atop b=1-\alpha}=\frac{1}{n\binom{n-\alpha}{n}}\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{k};1-\alpha).

(2.46)

This completes the proof of Theorem
2.6
by (
2.41
).
∎

\displaystyle\frac{d^{k}}{d\alpha^{k}}\frac{1}{\binom{n-\alpha}{n}}=\frac{k!}{\binom{n-\alpha}{n}}\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\{1\}_{k};1-\alpha).

(2.47)

Setting

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}{\left[\frac{1}{t}\left(\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\alpha}}-1\right)dt\right]}\cdot\underbrace{\frac{dt}{t}\cdots\frac{dt}{t}}_{m}=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}}{n^{m+1}}

and Lemma
3.1
together imply (
3.2
) for
m\geq 0
easily.
Applying (
3.68
)
we can also deduce (
3.2
) for
m=-1
by a similar argument as in the proof above.
∎

If setting
\alpha=\beta=\frac{1}{2}
in (
3.2
), we get the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

For any integer
m\geq-1
, we have

\left(1+(-1)^{m}\right)\zeta(m+2)=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m+1}(-1)^{i-1}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n^{i}4^{n}}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n^{m+2-i}4^{n}}+\left(1+(-1)^{m}\right)\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n^{m+2}4^{n}}.

Remark 3.4
.

In
[
20
, Thm. 3.4]
, Wang and Xu proved that for
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n^{m}4^{n}}\in\mathbb{Q}[\log(2),\zeta(2),\zeta(3),\zeta(4),\ldots].

Theorem 3.5
.

For any integers
m\geq-1,k,p\geq 0
and complex numbers
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{N}_{0}^{-}
with
\Re(\alpha),\Re(\beta)<1
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{k};\alpha)\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{p};1-\beta)}{n^{m+2}}\frac{\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}}{\binom{n-\beta}{n}}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{m}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{p};\beta)\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{k};1-\alpha)}{n^{m+2}}\frac{\binom{n+\beta-1}{n}}{\binom{n-\alpha}{n}}

\displaystyle-\delta_{0,p}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{k};\alpha)}{n^{m+2}}\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}-(-1)^{m}\delta_{0,k}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{p};\beta)}{n^{m+2}}\binom{n+\beta-1}{n}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m+1}(-1)^{i-1}\left\{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{p};\beta)}{n^{i}}\binom{n+\beta-1}{n}\right\}\left\{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{k};\alpha)}{n^{m+2-i}}\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}\right\}.

(3.71)

Proof.

Differentiating (
3.2
)
k
times with respect to
\alpha
and
p
times with respect to
\beta
yields the desired result with the help of (
2.47
) and (
2.52
).
∎

(\alpha,\beta)\rightarrow(0,0)
and
(0,1/2)

Corollary 3.6

For any integers

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(\{1\}_{k-1})\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{p})}{n^{m+3}}+(-1)^{m}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(\{1\}_{p-1})\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{k})}{n^{m+3}}

\displaystyle\quad=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m+1}(-1)^{i-1}\zeta(i+1;\{1\}_{p-1})\zeta(m+3-i;\{1\}_{k-1}).

(3.72)

Corollary 3.7
.

For any integers
m\geq-1,k\geq 1
and
p\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(\{1\}_{k-1})t_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{p})}{n^{m+3}}\frac{4^{n}}{\binom{2n}{n}}+(-1)^{m}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{t_{n}(\{1\}_{p})\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{k})}{n^{m+2}}\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^{n}}-\delta_{0,p}\zeta(m+3,\{1\}_{k-1})

\displaystyle\quad=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m+1}(-1)^{i-1}\left\{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{t_{n}(\{1\}_{p})}{n^{i}}\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^{n}}\right\}\zeta(m+3-i,\{1\}_{k-1}).

(3.73)

Theorem 3.8
.

For any integer
m\geq-1
and complex numbers
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{N}_{0}^{-},\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(\beta)<1
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}}{n^{m+2}\binom{n-\beta}{n}}=\alpha\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(\{1\}_{m+1};1-\beta)}{(n-\beta-\alpha)(n-\beta)}.

(3.74)

Proof.

We only prove that (
3.74
) holds for
m\geq 0
. Applying
t\rightarrow 1-t
in (
3.67
),

\displaystyle I_{m}(\alpha,\beta)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}{\left[\frac{1}{1-t}\left(\frac{1}{t^{\beta}}-1\right)dt\right]}\cdot\underbrace{\frac{dt}{1-t}\cdots\frac{dt}{1-t}}_{m}\cdot\left[\frac{1}{1-t}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}-1\right)dt\right]

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 1}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}{\left[\frac{1}{1-t}\left(\frac{1}{t^{\beta}}-1\right)dt\right]}\cdot\underbrace{\frac{dt}{1-t}\cdots\frac{dt}{1-t}}_{m}\cdot\left[\frac{1}{1-t}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}-1\right)dt\right]

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 1}\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}{\frac{dt}{(1-t)t^{\beta}}}\cdot\underbrace{\frac{dt}{1-t}\cdots\frac{dt}{1-t}}_{m}\cdot\frac{dt}{(1-t)t^{\alpha}}-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}{\frac{dt}{(1-t)t^{\beta}}}\cdot\underbrace{\frac{dt}{1-t}\cdots\frac{dt}{1-t}}_{m}\cdot\frac{dt}{1-t}\\
-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}{\frac{dt}{1-t}}\underbrace{\frac{dt}{1-t}\cdots\frac{dt}{1-t}}_{m}\cdot\frac{dt}{(1-t)t^{\alpha}}+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}{\frac{dt}{1-t}}\underbrace{\frac{dt}{1-t}\cdots\frac{dt}{1-t}}_{m}\cdot\frac{dt}{1-t}\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 1}\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{m+2}>0}\frac{x^{n_{1}-\beta-\alpha}}{(n_{1}-\beta-\alpha)\prod_{j=2}^{m+2}(n_{j}-\beta)}-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{m+2}>0}\frac{x^{n_{1}-\beta}}{\prod_{j=1}^{m+2}(n_{j}-\beta)}\\
-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{m+2}>0}\frac{x^{n_{1}-\alpha}}{(n_{1}-\alpha)n_{2}\cdots n_{m+2}}+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{m+2}>0}\frac{x^{n_{1}}}{n_{1}\cdots n_{m+2}}\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{n-1}(\{1\}_{m+1};1-\beta)\left(\frac{1}{n-\beta-\alpha}-\frac{1}{n-\beta}\right)-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{n-1}(\{1\}_{m+1})\left(\frac{1}{n-\alpha}-\frac{1}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(\{1\}_{m+1};1-\beta)}{(n-\beta-\alpha)(n-\beta)}-\alpha\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(\{1\}_{m+1})}{n(n-\alpha)}.

Hence, applying (
3
) yields

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}}{n^{m+2}\binom{n-\beta}{n}}-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}}{n^{m+2}}=\alpha\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(\{1\}_{m+1};1-\beta)}{(n-\beta-\alpha)(n-\beta)}-\alpha\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(\{1\}_{m+1})}{n(n-\alpha)}.

Further, (
2.50
) gives rise to the formula

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}}{n^{m+1}}=\alpha\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(\{1\}_{m})}{n(n-\alpha)}\,,

which leads to (
3.74
) immediately.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

For any integers
m\geq-1,k,p\geq 0
and complex numbers
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{N}_{0}^{-},\beta\in\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{k};\alpha)\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{p};1-\beta)}{n^{m+2}}\frac{\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}}{\binom{n-\beta}{n}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m+2}=p\atop i_{1},\ldots,i_{m+2}\geq 0}\binom{i_{1}+k}{k}\left\{\zeta(i_{1}+k+1,i_{2}+1,\ldots,i_{m+2}+1;1-\alpha-\beta,\{1-\beta\}_{m+1})\atop-\delta_{0,k}\zeta(i_{1}+k+1,i_{2}+1,\ldots,i_{m+2}+1;\{1-\beta\}_{m+2})\right\},

(3.75)

where if

then

\displaystyle\zeta(1,i_{2}+1,\ldots,i_{m+2}+1;1-\alpha-\beta,\{1-\beta\}_{m+1})-\zeta(1,i_{2}+1,\ldots,i_{m+2}+1;\{1-\beta\}_{m+2})

\displaystyle=\alpha\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(i_{2}+1,\ldots,i_{m+2}+1;1-\beta)}{(n-\beta)(n-\alpha-\beta)}.

(3.76)

Proof.

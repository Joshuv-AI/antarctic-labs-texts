Semigroup algorithmic problems in metabelian groups
Ruiwen Dong

Abstract
We consider semigroup algorithmic problems in finitely generated metabelian groups. Our paper focuses on three decision problems introduced by Choffrut and Karhumäki (2005): the Identity Problem (does a semigroup contain a neutral element?), the Group Problem (is a semigroup a group?) and the Inverse Problem (does a semigroup contain the inverse of a generator?). We show that all three problems are decidable for finitely generated sub-semigroups of finitely generated metabelian groups. In particular, we establish a correspondence between polynomial semirings and sub-semigroups of metabelian groups using an interaction of graph theory, convex polytopes, algebraic geometry and number theory. Since the Semigroup Membership problem (does a semigroup contain a given element?) is known to be undecidable in finitely generated metabelian groups, our result completes the decidability characterization of semigroup algorithmic problems in metabelian groups.

In this paper we study algorithmic problems in

Metabelian groups are groups whose

Developing a complete algorithmic theory for finitely generated metabelian groups has been the focus of intense research since the 1950s.

As the convention in computational group theory, a finitely generated metabelian group

Every finitely generated metabelian group admits a finite metabelian presentation, making them a natural target for algorithmic methods

Among the classic Max Dehn problems for finitely generated metabelian groups, decidability of the Word Problem is known since the 1950s following the seminal work of Hall

The Conjugacy Problem is shown to be decidable by Noskov

The Isomorphism Problem remains an outstanding open problem

We may note that in the hierarchy of solvable groups, metabelian groups (also known as 2-step solvable groups) are on the fringe of decidability. By a celebrated result of Kharlampovich, all three Max Dehn problems are undecidable in 3-step solvable groups (these are groups whose commutator is metabelian)

]
,
[

In finitely generated metabelian groups, decidability of Group Membership is a classic result of Romanovskii

whereas Semigroup Membership is undecidable for many instances such as large direct powers of the Heisenberg group

Decidability of the Identity Problem, the Group Problem and the Inverse Problem remained open.
A recent result by Dong

]
showed decidability of the Group Problem in an important example of metabelian groups, the

\mathbb{Z}\wr\mathbb{Z}
.
This hinted at the possibility of decidability results for other finitely generated metabelian groups.
Our main results solve these open problems:

Theorem 1.1
.

The Group Problem, the Identity Problem and the Inverse Problem are decidable in all finitely generated metabelian groups.

1.3 Related work

It has been noticed since the work of Hall that metabelian groups have natural connections with polynomials rings.
Indeed, this connection is the key to deciding many
group
algorithmic problems in metabelian groups.
However, a corresponding theory for
semigroups
has yet to be developed.
Recent work by Dong
[
16
]
suggested there are connections between certain semigroups, directed graphs and polynomial semirings.
However, such connections are highly sophisticated and a satisfactory characterization is yet to be obtained.
We build on Dong’s work to establish a full connection between sub-
semigroups
of metabelian groups and polynomial
semirings
.
Many of our ideas are inspired by
[
16
]
, notably the use of
\mathcal{G}
-graphs
to describe words over metabelian groups, as well as exploiting the interaction between semigroups, graphs and algebraic geometry.
Here are our main new contributions.

1.

We introduce a
systematic
way of translating from words over metabelian groups to elements in polynomial semirings. We introduce the notion of “position polynomials” to describe the associated
\mathcal{G}
-graph. These polynomials are powerful enough to describe interesting properties of the graph such as “full-image” and symmetry.
In Dong’s work this translation was done using an
ad hoc
method that decomposes a walk over
\mathbb{Z}
into “primitive circuits”.
As noted there, that method could not be generalized to walks over
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. The new method we develop here overcomes this difficulty.

2.

The drawback of using “position polynomials” instead of decomposition into primitive circuits is the inability to express the connectivity property of the graph.
Our second main idea is to introduce a new property of the graph called “face-accessibility”.
This property is weaker then connectivity but has the advantage of being describable by position polynomials.
We then show that together with symmetry, face-accessibility is enough to characterize Eulerian graphs up to taking unions of translations.
This is done using a series of manipulation in convex geometry and graph theory.

3.

Thanks to the two previous ideas we are able to reduce semigroup problems to decision problems over polynomial semirings.
Our third new idea is a simultaneous generalization of two deep results by Einsiedler, Mouat and Tuncel
[
17
, Theorem 1.3]
and by Dong
[
16
, Proposition 3.4]
.
This is mathematically the deepest part of our paper: it includes highly intricate applications of algebraic geometry tools such as Gröbner basis over modules, as well as various ideas from number theory.

j=0
then the edge starts at
0
and ends at
a_{i_{1}}
.)
The graph
\Gamma(w)
is then obtained by taking the connected component of the vertex
0
.
See Figure
2
for an illustration.

Fact 2.7
.

For a word
w
over the alphabet
\mathcal{G}
, the element of
\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
represented by its associated graph
\Gamma(w)
is equal to the element of
\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
represented by the word
w
.

By reading the letters in
w
one by one and tracing the corresponding edges of
\Gamma(w)
, we obtain an Euler path of
\Gamma(w)
.
Furthermore, if the word
w
represents the neutral element (or any element of the form
(y,0)
), then this Euler path is an Euler circuit.
Conversely, given an Eulerian
\mathcal{G}
-graph
\Gamma
containing the vertex 0, we can follow an Euler circuit starting from 0 and read a word
w
such that
\Gamma(w)=\Gamma
.

A
\mathcal{G}
-graph
\Gamma
is called
full-image
if it contains an edge with label
i
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
.
Note that for a word
w
over the alphabet
\mathcal{G}
, its associated graph
\Gamma(w)
is full-image if and only if the word
w
is full-image.
Combining Lemma
2.1
with the above correspondence between words and Eulerian graphs, we immediately obtain the following lemma.
Note that we can always translate a
\mathcal{G}
-graph so that it contains the vertex 0; translating a
\mathcal{G}
-graph by a vector
z
multiplies the first entry of its represented element by
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{z}
.

Lemma 2.8
.

The semigroup
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
is a group if and only if there exists a full-image Eulerian
\mathcal{G}
-graph that represents the neutral element.

3
Proof of main technical result

By Lemma
2.2
and Proposition
2.4
, our main result (Theorem
1.1
) boils down to proving the following technical theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{Y}
be a
\mathbb{Z}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
-module with a given finite presentation.
Suppose we are given a finite subset
\mathcal{G}
of the semidirect product
\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, such that the subgroup
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle_{grp}
of
\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
admits the image
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
under the canonical projection
\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
is a group.

3.1
.

In Subsection

-graphs to algorithmic problems over polynomial semirings.
In Subsection

In Subsection

Proofs of several stated theorems will be given in the later Sections

,
5
and

.

.

3.1 From semigroups to face-accessible graphs

We now fix the set of elements

\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\{(y_{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(y_{K},a_{K})\}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

By Lemma
2.8
, deciding the Group Problem boils down to finding an Eulerian
\mathcal{G}
-graph.
However, being Eulerian is hard to characterize as a graph theory property.
In fact, being Eulerian is equivalent to being symmetric and connected. While symmetry is easy to describe
locally
(i.e. at each vertex), connectivity of a graph is a
global
property and is hence hard to describe.
The key idea of this subsection is to introduce a
local
property called “face-accessibility” to replace connectivity.

Let
\Gamma
be a
\mathcal{G}
-graph.
Denote by
C
the convex hull of
V(\Gamma)
.
A strict face
F
of
C
is called
accessible
if there exists an edge starting inside the face and ending outside of it.
That is,
F
is accessible if there is an edge
e\in E(\Gamma)
starting from a vertex
s(e)\in F\cap V(\Gamma)
and ending at a vertex
d(e)\in(C\setminus F)\cap V(\Gamma)
.
See Figure
3
for an example.

The graph
\Gamma
is called
face-accessible
if every strict face of
C
is accessible.

Figure 3:
Accessible and inaccessible faces.

Observation 3.2
.

An Eulerian graph is face-accessible.

On the contrary, a symmetric face-accessible graph is not necessarily Eulerian, as it may not be connected.
Moreover, a symmetric graph need not be face-accessible.
See Figure
5
and
5
for counterexamples.

A symmetric face-accessible graph that is not Eulerian (due to connectivity).

A graph
\Gamma
with vertices in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is called
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-generating if the set of vectors
\{d(e)-s(e)\mid e\in E(\Gamma)\}
generates
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as a semigroup.
If
\Gamma
is symmetric, this semigroup is a group.
Hence, a full-image symmetric
\mathcal{G}
-graph is
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-generating if and only if
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{K}\}
generates
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as a group, which is true by the assumption in Theorem
3.1
.

The main theorem of this subsection is the following, it shows that face-accessibility can characterize connectivity up to taking a finite union of translations.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
\mathcal{G}
-graph that is symmetric, face-accessible and
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-generating.
Then there exist
z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, such that the union of translations
\widehat{\Gamma}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\left(\Gamma+z_{i}\right)
is an Eulerian graph.

See Figures
9
and
9
for an illustration of Theorem
3.3
.
The proof of Theorem
3.3
uses a sophisticated combination of convex geometry and graph theory, and will be given in Section
4
.
Note that the face-accessibility condition in Theorem
3.3
is necessary. Figures
9
and

show that Theorem
3.3
does not hold without face-accessibility.

-generating graph

.

, consisting of four translations of

If
\Gamma
represents the neutral element, then the union
\widehat{\Gamma}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\left(\Gamma+z_{i}\right)
represents the element
(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{z_{i}}\cdot 0,\sum_{i=1}^{m}0)=(0,0)
.
Therefore, from Lemma
2.8
and Theorem
3.3
we immediately obtain the following.

Proposition 3.4
.

The semigroup
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
is a group if and only if there exists a full-image symmetric face-accessible
\mathcal{G}

3.2 From face-accessible graphs to positive polynomials

In this subsection we introduce “position polynomials” to describe a
\mathcal{G}
-graph, and reduce our graph theory problem to a computational problem over polynomials.
Recall that for an edge
e
in a
\mathcal{G}
-graph,
\ell(e)
denotes the label of
e
, and
s(e)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
denotes the starting vertex of
e
.
Given a
\mathcal{G}
-graph
\Gamma
, define its tuple of
position polynomials

\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{K})
in the following way:

f_{i}\coloneqq\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma),\ell(e)=i}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{s(e)},\quad i=1,\ldots,K.

(4)

That is,
f_{i}
is the sum of monomials
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{s}
, where
s
ranges over the starting vertex of all label
i
edges in
\Gamma
.
These polynomials have only non-negative coefficients, hence are in
\mathbb{N}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
. See Figure
11
.

Figure 10:
In this example,
f_{1}=1+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{-1}
,
f_{2}=X_{1}^{-2}X_{2}^{3}
,
f_{3}=X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{3}+X_{1}^{3}X_{2}
.

Figure 11:
Let
v=(0,1)
, we have

M_{v}(\{1,2,3\},\boldsymbol{f})=\{2,3\}
,
O_{v}=\{1,3\}
.

Conversely, given any tuple of polynomials
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{K})\in\mathbb{N}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{K}
, one can construct a
\mathcal{G}
-graph
\Gamma
such that
\boldsymbol{f}
is exactly its position polynomials.
Indeed, for each monomial
cX^{b}
of
f_{i}
, we draw
b
edges of label
i
starting at vertex
b
.
Note that it is crucial for
f_{i}
to be an element of
\mathbb{N}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
instead of
\mathbb{Z}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
, so that all monomials have non-negative coefficients.

For
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
and
I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,K\}
, define

M_{v}(I,\boldsymbol{f})\coloneqq\left\{i\in I\;\middle|\;\deg_{v}(f_{i})=\max_{i^{\prime}\in I}\{\deg_{v}(f_{i^{\prime}})\}\right\}.

This is the set of indices
i\in I
such that
\deg_{v}(f_{i})
is maximal among
i\in I
.
Define

O_{v}\coloneqq\{i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\mid a_{i}\not\perp v\}.

This is the set of indices
i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
such that
a_{i}
is not orthogonal to
v
.
See Figure
11
.

The following proposition shows we can completely characterize the graph theoretic properties from Proposition
3.4
using position polynomials.
The key point is how we characterize face-accessibility in (iii).
As a comparison, no good characterization of
connectivity
can be obtained from position polynomials.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
\mathcal{G}
-graph with position polynomials
f_{i}\in\mathbb{N}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}],i=1,\ldots,K
.

(i)

\Gamma
is full-image if and only if

f_{i}\neq 0\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,K
.

(ii)

\Gamma
is symmetric if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{K}f_{i}\cdot(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{a_{i}}-1)=0
.

(iii)

\Gamma
is face-accessible if and only if

O_{v}\cap M_{v}(\{1,\ldots,K\},\boldsymbol{f})\neq\emptyset\quad\text{ for every }v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}.

(5)

(iv)

Suppose
\Gamma
is symmetric.
\Gamma
represents the neutral element if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{K}f_{i}\cdot y_{i}=0
.

Proof.

(i) and (iv) are easy and follow by direct computation using the definition (
4
) of position polynomials (see Appendix
C
for details).

(ii) We have

\sum_{i=1}^{K}f_{i}\cdot(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{a_{i}}-1)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma),\ell(e)=i}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{s(e)}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{a_{i}}-1)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma),\ell(e)=i}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{s(e)+a_{i}}-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{s(e)})\\
=\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma)}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{d(e)}-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{s(e)})=\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma)}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{d(e)}-\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma)}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{s(e)}.

This is equal to zero if and only if the in-degree equals the out-degree at every vertex.

(iii) Let
C
be the convex hull of
V(\Gamma)
.
For every strict face
F
of
C
there is a vector
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
such that
F
consists of all points
x
in
C
where
v\cdot x
is maximal.
Conversely, for every vector
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
, the set
F
of all points
x
in
C
such that
v\cdot x
is maximal forms a strict face of
C
.

Let
F
be a strict face, then
F
is accessible if and only if some edge starting in
F
does not end in
F
.
Let
e
be an edge starting in
F
, with label
\ell(e)
.
Then
v\cdot s(e)
is maximal among all
e\in E(\Gamma)
.
Since the monomial
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{s(e)}
is contained in
f_{\ell(e)}
, this means
\ell(e)\in M_{v}(\{1,\ldots,K\},\boldsymbol{f})
.

Observe that
d(e)\in F
if and only if
a_{\ell(e)}=d(e)-s(e)
is orthogonal to
v
, which is equivalent to
\ell(e)\not\in O_{v}
.
Therefore,
F
is accessible if and only if an edge
e
exists such that
\ell(e)\in M_{v}(\{1,\ldots,K\},\boldsymbol{f})
and
\ell(e)\in O_{v}
;
that is,
O_{v}\cap M_{v}(\{1,\ldots,K\},\boldsymbol{f})\neq\emptyset
.

By the definition of face-accessibility,
\Gamma
is face-accessible if and only if
O_{v}\cap M_{v}(\{1,\ldots,K\},\boldsymbol{f})\neq\emptyset
holds for every
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{Z}}
be the
\mathbb{Z}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-module consisting of all
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{K}
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{K}f_{i}\cdot(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{a_{i}}-1)=0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{K}f_{i}\cdot y_{i}=0
:

\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{Z}}\coloneqq\left\{\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{K}\;\middle|\;\sum_{i=1}^{K}f_{i}\cdot(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{a_{i}}-1)=0\text{ and }\sum_{i=1}^{K}f_{i}\cdot y_{i}=0\right\}.

(6)

Then Proposition
3.5
shows the following: there exists a full-image symmetric face-accessible
\mathcal{G}
-graph that represents the neutral element if and only if
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{Z}}
contains an element
\boldsymbol{f}\in\left(\mathbb{N}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{*}\right)^{K}
satisfying Property (
5
).
Using linear algebra over
\mathbb{Z}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
, generators of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{Z}}
can be effectively computed (a simple proof is given in Appendix
C
):

Lemma 3.6
.

A finite set of generators
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{K}
of the
\mathbb{Z}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-module
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{Z}}
can be computed from
y_{i},a_{i},i=1,\ldots,K
.

3.3 Local-global principle for positive polynomials

We now start to construct an algorithm that decides whether
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{Z}}
contains an element
\boldsymbol{f}\in\left(\mathbb{N}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{*}\right)^{K}
satisfying Property (
5
).
This problem is highly non-trivial due to the polynomials having coefficients in
\mathbb{N}
instead of
\mathbb{Z}
.
In fact, solving linear equations over the semiring
\mathbb{N}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
is known to be
undecidable

[
31
]
.
Our key to obtaining a decidability result is to exploit the
homogeneity
of our linear equations.

The first step is to generalize Property (
5
).
Given two sets
I,J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,K\}
, our new goal is to decide whether
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{Z}}
contains an element
\boldsymbol{f}\in\left(\mathbb{N}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{*}\right)^{K}
satisfying the following condition.

\left(O_{v}\cup J\right)\cap M_{v}(I,\boldsymbol{f})\neq\emptyset,\quad\text{ for every }v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}.

(7)

Note that Property (
5
) can be considered as a special case of Property (
7
) with
I=\{1,\ldots,K\},J=\emptyset
.
This generalization will be crucial to our subsequent decidability result.
Intuitively, edges with labels in
J
can be considered to be “going out into an
(n+1)
-th dimension”; and edges with labels outside of
I
can be considered to “exist in an
(n+1)
-th dimension”.

The second step is to pass from polynomial rings over
\mathbb{Z}
to polynomial rings over
\mathbb{R}
in order to facilitate subsequent usage of analytic methods.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be the
\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-submodule of
\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{K}
generated by
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{m}
from Lemma
3.6
, that is,

\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{m}h_{j}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{j}\;\middle|\;h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}\in\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]\right\}.

Lemma 3.7
.

There exists an element
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{f}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{Z}}\cap\left(\mathbb{N}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{*}\right)^{K}
satisfying Property (
7
), if and only if there exists
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathcal{M}\cap\left(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{*}\right)^{K}
satisfying Property (
7
).

Denote
\mathbb{A}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}],\mathbb{A}^{+}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]^{*}
.
Given
f\in\mathbb{A}
and
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
, the
initial polynomial
of
f
is defined as the sum of all monomials in
f
having the maximal degree
\deg_{v}(\cdot)
:

\operatorname{in}_{v}(f)\coloneqq\sum\nolimits_{\deg_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{b})=\deg_{v}(f)}c_{b}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{b},\quad\text{ where }f=\sum c_{b}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{b}.

For
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{K})\in\mathbb{A}^{K}
, we naturally denote
\operatorname{in}_{v}(\boldsymbol{f})\coloneqq(\operatorname{in}_{v}(f_{1}),\ldots,\operatorname{in}_{v}(f_{K}))\in\mathbb{A}^{K}
.
The key result of this subsection is the following local-global principle, which simultaneously generalizes two deep results of Einsiedler, Mouat and Tuncel
[
17
, Theorem 1.3]
and of Dong
[
16
, Proposition 3.4]
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a
\mathbb{A}
-submodule of
\mathbb{A}^{K}
and
I,J
be two subsets of
\{1,\ldots,K\}
.
There exists
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathcal{M}\cap\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
satisfying

\left(O_{v}\cup J\right)\cap M_{v}(I,\boldsymbol{f})\neq\emptyset,\quad\text{ for every }v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*},

(8)

if and only if the two following conditions are satisfied:

1.

(LocR):
For every
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
, there exists
\boldsymbol{f}_{r}\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\boldsymbol{f}_{r}(r)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{K}
.

2.

(LocInf):
For every
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
, there exists
\boldsymbol{f}_{v}\in\mathcal{M}
, such that

(a)

\operatorname{in}_{v}\left(\boldsymbol{f}_{v}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
.

(b)

Denote
I^{\prime}\coloneqq M_{v}(I,\boldsymbol{f}_{v}),J^{\prime}\coloneqq O_{v}\cup J
.
We have

\left(O_{w}\cup J^{\prime}\right)\cap M_{w}(I^{\prime},\operatorname{in}_{v}(\boldsymbol{f}_{v}))\neq\emptyset\quad\text{ for every $w\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}$}.

(9)

The full proof of Theorem
3.8
is highly non-trivial and is given in Section
5
.
Compared to the cited results
[
16
,
17
]
, the new element here is the inclusion of Property (
8
).
This property is crucial due to our characterization of face-accessibility.
The main difficulty in this generalization is the complex interaction between the sets
M_{v},O_{v}
and
\boldsymbol{f}
, which is absent from the two cited results.

3.4 Decidability

Theorem
3.8
is the key to an algorithm that finds
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathcal{M}\cap\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
satisfying Property (
7
).
Indeed, we have:

Theorem 3.9
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Suppose we are given as input a set of generators
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{m}\in\mathbb{A}^{K}
with integer coefficients, as well as the vectors
a_{1},\ldots,a_{K}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and two subsets
I,J
of
\{1,\ldots,K\}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{M}
be the
\mathbb{A}

Theorem

The difference in our paper is the additional constraint (

Recall that this property stems from the face-accessibility constraint in Proposition

Many components of the original proof in

In order to take into account this extra property, we need to introduce new arguments to rework many parts of the original proof.
The key new component will be the following Lemma

Before stating Lemma

Define the quotient

D_{n}\coloneqq\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}.

We equip

Note that

by any positive real number.

M_{v}(I,\boldsymbol{F}_{j^{\prime}})\subseteq M_{v}(I,\boldsymbol{f})
.
Take any

On one hand, since

, so
\operatorname{in}_{v}(\boldsymbol{F}_{j^{\prime}})\in\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
.
We have

\deg_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j^{\prime}}}F_{j^{\prime},i^{\prime}})=\max_{i\in I}\{\deg_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j^{\prime}}}F_{j^{\prime},i})\}=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\max_{i\in I}\{\deg_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j}}F_{j,i})\}\\
=\max_{i\in I}\max_{j:||v-v_{j}||<\lambda}\{\deg_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j}}F_{j,i})\}=\max_{i\in I}\deg_{v}\left(\sum_{j:||v-v_{j}||<\lambda}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j}}F_{j,i}\right)=\max_{i\in I}\deg_{v}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j}}F_{j,i}\right)\\
=\max_{i\in I}\deg_{v}(f_{i}),

(15)

since there can be no cancellation when summing
\operatorname{in}_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j}}F_{j,i})\in\mathbb{A}^{+}
for
j,||v-v_{j}||<\lambda
.

On the other hand,

\deg_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j^{\prime}}}F_{j^{\prime},i^{\prime}})=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\max_{i\in I}\{\deg_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j}}F_{j,i})\}\geq\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\{\deg_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j}}F_{j,i^{\prime}})\}.

So
\deg_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j^{\prime}}}F_{j^{\prime},i^{\prime}})=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\{\deg_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j}}F_{j,i^{\prime}})\}
.
Hence,

\deg_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j^{\prime}}}F_{j^{\prime},i^{\prime}})=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\{\deg_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j}}F_{j,i^{\prime}})\}=\max_{j:||v-v_{j}||<\lambda}\{\deg_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j}}F_{j,i^{\prime}})\}\\
=\deg_{v}\left(\sum_{j:||v-v_{j}||<\lambda}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j}}F_{j,i^{\prime}}\right)=\deg_{v}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j}}F_{j,i^{\prime}}\right)=\deg_{v}(f_{i^{\prime}})

since there can be no cancellation when summing
\operatorname{in}_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j}}F_{j,i^{\prime}})\in\mathbb{A}^{+}
for
j,||v-v_{j}||<\lambda
.

Hence
\deg_{v}(f_{i^{\prime}})=\deg_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{w_{j^{\prime}}}F_{j^{\prime},i^{\prime}})=\max_{i\in I}\deg_{v}(f_{i})

.
Since this holds for all

.
Thus,
(O_{v}\cup J)\cap M_{v}(I,\boldsymbol{f})\supseteq(O_{v}\cup J)\cap M_{v}(I,\boldsymbol{F}_{j^{\prime}})\neq\emptyset
.
Therefore
\boldsymbol{f}
satisfies condition (ii).
∎

Let

This section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem

and

6.1 Decidability of local condition at positive reals
(LocR)

In this subsection we show that the Condition
(LocR)
of Theorem
3.8
is decidable.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a
\mathbb{A}
-submodule of
\mathbb{A}^{K}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{m}
be the generators for
\mathcal{M}
.
Condition
(LocR)
of Theorem
3.8
is equivalent to the following:

1.

(LocRLin)
For every
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
, there exist
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\boldsymbol{g}_{i}(r)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{K}
.

Proof.

(LocR)
\implies
(LocRLin)
:
Suppose Condition
(LocR)
of Theorem
3.8
is true.
For
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
, Condition
(LocR)
shows there exist
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\in\mathbb{A}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}\boldsymbol{g}_{i}=\boldsymbol{f}_{r}
with
\boldsymbol{f}_{r}(r)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{K}
.
Then letting

we have
\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\boldsymbol{g}_{i}(r)=\boldsymbol{f}_{r}(r)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{K}
.

\implies
(LocR)
:
Suppose Condition
(LocRLin)
is true.
For any

, Condition
(LocRLin)
shows there exist

satisfies

.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Given the generators
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{m}
for

, it is decidable whether Condition
(LocR)
of Theorem

is satisfied.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.1
, it suffices to decide Condition

.
This is expressible in the first order theory of the reals:

[
37
]
, the truth of this sentence is decidable.
∎

6.2 Local condition at infinity: from
(LocInf)
to shifted initials
(LocInfShift)

In this subsection we introduce the
shifted initials
, in order to replace Condition
(LocInf)
of Theorem
3.8
with a new Condition
(LocInfShift)
.
Our definition follows that of
[
17
, Section 1]
.

Suppose we are given
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{A}^{K}
,
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{K})\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
.
Then the
shifted initials

\operatorname{in}_{v,\alpha}(\boldsymbol{f})=(\operatorname{in}_{v,\alpha}(\boldsymbol{f})_{1},\ldots,\operatorname{in}_{v,\alpha}(\boldsymbol{f})_{K})
is defined as

\displaystyle\operatorname{in}_{v,\alpha}(\boldsymbol{f})_{i}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\operatorname{in}_{v}(f_{i})&\text{ if }\deg_{v}(f_{i})+\alpha_{i}=\max_{1\leq i^{\prime}\leq K}\{\deg_{v}(f_{i^{\prime}})+\alpha_{i^{\prime}}\},\\
0&\text{ if }\deg_{v}(f_{i})+\alpha_{i}<\max_{1\leq i^{\prime}\leq K}\{\deg_{v}(f_{i^{\prime}})+\alpha_{i^{\prime}}\}.\\
\end{cases}

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{A}^{K}
and
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
.
We have
\operatorname{in}_{v}(\boldsymbol{f})\in\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
if and only if there exists
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
such that
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{f})\in\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
.
Furthermore, in this case,
\operatorname{in}_{v}(\boldsymbol{f})=\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{f})
and
\alpha_{1}+\deg_{v}(f_{1})=\cdots=\alpha_{K}+\deg_{v}(f_{K})
.

Proof.

If
\operatorname{in}_{v}\left(\boldsymbol{f}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
, then let

\alpha_{1}\coloneqq-\deg_{v}(f_{1}),\ldots,\alpha_{K}\coloneqq-\deg_{v}(f_{K})
.
We have
\deg_{v}(f_{1})+\alpha_{1}=\cdots=\deg_{v}(f_{K})+\alpha_{K}=\max_{1\leq i\leq K}\{\deg_{v}(f_{i})+\alpha_{i}\}
, so
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\left(\boldsymbol{f}\right)=\operatorname{in}_{v}\left(\boldsymbol{f}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
.

If
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\left(\boldsymbol{f}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
, then
\operatorname{in}_{v}\left(f_{i}\right)=\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{f})_{i}\in\mathbb{A}^{+}
for
i=1,\ldots,K
.
Therefore
\operatorname{in}_{v}\left(\boldsymbol{f}\right)=\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{f})\in\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
.

Furthermore, in this case, since
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\left(\boldsymbol{f}\right)_{i}\neq 0
for
i=1,\ldots,K
, we have
\deg_{v}(f_{i})+\alpha_{i}=\max_{1\leq i^{\prime}\leq K}\{\deg_{v}(f_{i^{\prime}})+\alpha_{i^{\prime}}\}
.
Hence
\alpha_{1}+\deg_{v}(f_{1})=\cdots=\alpha_{K}+\deg_{v}(f_{K})
.
∎

Given
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
, define the following set of real numbers:

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}v_{k}\coloneqq\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{n}z_{k}v_{k}\;\middle|\;z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

Then for every
f\in\mathbb{A}
, we have
\deg_{v}(f)\in\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}v_{k}
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Condition
(LocInf)
of Theorem
3.8
is equivalent to the following:

2.

(LocInfShift):
For every
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
, there exists
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathcal{M}
as well as
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}v_{k}\right)^{K}

(a)

\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\left(\boldsymbol{f}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}

(b)

Denote

We have

\left(O_{w}\cup J^{\prime}\right)\cap M_{w}(I^{\prime},\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{f}))\neq\emptyset\quad\text{ for every $w\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}$}.

Proof.

(LocInf)
\implies
(LocInfShift)
.
Suppose Condition
(LocInf)
of Theorem
3.8
is true.
Fix a vector
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
.
Then there exists
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathcal{M}

\operatorname{in}_{v}\left(\boldsymbol{f}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
satisfies Property
(LocInf)
(b).
As in Lemma
6.3
, we can let
\alpha_{i}\coloneqq-\deg_{v}(f_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,K
, then
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\left(\boldsymbol{f}\right)=\operatorname{in}_{v}\left(\boldsymbol{f}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
, satisfying
(LocInfShift)
(a).
Furthermore, we have
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}v_{k}\right)^{K}
by the definition of
\alpha_{i}=-\deg_{v}(f_{i})
.
Finally,
\{i\in I\mid\alpha_{i}=\min_{i^{\prime}\in I}\alpha_{i^{\prime}}\}=\{i\in I\mid\deg_{v}(f_{i})=\max_{i^{\prime}\in I}\deg_{v}(f_{i^{\prime}})\}=M_{v}(I,\boldsymbol{f})
, so
(LocInf)
(b) implies
(LocInfShift)
(b).

(LocInfShift)
\implies
(LocInf)
.
Suppose Condition
(LocInfShift)
is true.
Fix a vector
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
.
Then there exists
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathcal{M}
as well as
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
, such that
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\left(\boldsymbol{f}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
satisfies Property
(LocInfShift)
(b).
By Lemma
6.3
, we have
\operatorname{in}_{v}\left(\boldsymbol{f}\right)=\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\left(\boldsymbol{f}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
, and
\alpha_{1}+\deg_{v}(f_{1})=\cdots=\alpha_{K}+\deg_{v}(f_{K})
.
Therefore we have
\{i\in I\mid\alpha_{i}=\min_{i^{\prime}\in I}\alpha_{i^{\prime}}\}=\{i\in I\mid\deg_{v}(f_{i})=\min_{i^{\prime}\in I}\deg_{v}(f_{i^{\prime}})\}=M_{v}(I,\boldsymbol{f})
, so
(LocInfShift)
(b) implies
(LocInf)
(b).
∎

6.3 Dimension reduction: a special case

In this and the following subsections we will further reduce Condition
(LocInfShift)
to a Condition
(LocInfD)
(see Proposition
6.11
).
In this subsection we first consider the special case where the vector
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
in Condition
(LocInfShift)
is of the form
(0,\ldots,0,v_{d+1},\ldots,v_{n})
, where
v_{d+1},\ldots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent.

We now define the
super Gröbner basis
of an
\mathbb{A}
-module
\mathcal{M}
.
Note that our definition is different from
[
17
, Section 2]
, although the intuition is the same.
Let
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*},\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
.
Define
\operatorname{in}_{v,\alpha}(\mathcal{M})
to be the
\mathbb{A}
-module generated by the elements
\operatorname{in}_{v,\alpha}(\boldsymbol{f}),\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathcal{M}
:

\operatorname{in}_{v,\alpha}(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\sum_{\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathcal{M}}\mathbb{A}\cdot\operatorname{in}_{v,\alpha}(\boldsymbol{f})=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{q}p_{j}\cdot\operatorname{in}_{v,\alpha}(\boldsymbol{f}_{j})\;\middle|\;q\in\mathbb{N},p_{1},\ldots p_{q}\in\mathbb{A},\boldsymbol{f}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{f}_{q}\in\mathcal{M}\right\}.

Definition 6.5

(Super Gröbner basis)
.

A set of generators
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{m}
for the module
\mathcal{M}
is called a
super Gröbner basis
if for all
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*},\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
, the set
\{\operatorname{in}_{v,\alpha}(\boldsymbol{g}_{1}),\ldots,\operatorname{in}_{v,\alpha}(\boldsymbol{g}_{m})\}
generates
\operatorname{in}_{v,\alpha}(\mathcal{M})
as an
\mathbb{A}
-module.

Lemma 6.6

(Reformulation of
[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Suppose we are given an arbitrary set of generators for a module
\mathcal{M}
. Then a super Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{M}
is effectively computable.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]\coloneqq\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
be the regular polynomial ring over
n
variables (note that for the moment, we are not considering the Laurent polynomial ring
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
).
Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{K}
be the canonical
\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
-basis of
\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]^{K}
.
A
monomial
of
\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]^{K}
is an element of the form
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{u}e_{i}
for some
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n},i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
.
A
term order
on the monomials of
\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]^{K}
is a total order
\prec
satisfying

(i)

e_{i}\prec\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{u}e_{i}
,

(ii)

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{u}e_{i}\prec\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{u^{\prime}}e_{i^{\prime}}\implies\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{u+w}e_{i}\prec\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{u^{\prime}+w}e_{i^{\prime}}
,

for all
i,i^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
and
u,u^{\prime},w\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
.

Let
N
be an
\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
-submodule of
\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]^{K}
. An element
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]^{K}
can be written uniquely as a sum
\sum_{u,i}c_{u,i}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{u}e_{i}
with coefficients
c_{u,i}
in
\mathbb{R}
.
Among the finitely many monomials of
\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]^{K}
that have nonzero coefficients in this sum, the one that is maximal according to the term order
\prec
is denoted
\operatorname{in}_{\prec}(\boldsymbol{f})
.
Define
\operatorname{in}_{\prec}(N)
, the
initial module
of
N
with respect to
\prec
, to be the
\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
-module generated by all
\operatorname{in}_{\prec}(\boldsymbol{f}),\boldsymbol{f}\in N
.
We say that the elements
\boldsymbol{f}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{f}_{l}\in N
form a
Gröbner basis
for
N
with respect to
\prec
if
\operatorname{in}_{\prec}(N)
is generated as an
\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
-module by
\operatorname{in}_{\prec}(\boldsymbol{f}_{1}),\ldots,\operatorname{in}_{\prec}(\boldsymbol{f}_{l})
.

A
universal Gröbner basis
of
N
is given by elements
\boldsymbol{f}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{f}_{l}
that form a Gröbner basis of
N
with respect to
every
term order.
A universal Gröbner basis of
N
exists and can be effectively computed from a set of generators of
N
as described in
[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Fix an
\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-submodule
\mathcal{M}
of
\left(\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]\right)^{K}
.
Find
\boldsymbol{f}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{f}_{l}
that generate
\mathcal{M}
as an
\mathbb{R}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\pm}]
-module.
Let
\delta=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n})\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
.
Pick
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{u}\boldsymbol{f}_{1},\ldots,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{u}\boldsymbol{f}_{l}\in\left(\mathbb{R}[X_{1}^{\delta_{1}},\ldots,X_{n}^{\delta_{n}}]\right)^{K},

and let
\boldsymbol{f}_{\delta,1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{f}_{\delta,l_{\delta}}
be a universal Gröbner basis for the
\mathbb{R}[X_{1}^{\delta_{1}},\ldots,X_{n}^{\delta_{n}}]
-submodule generated by
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{u}\boldsymbol{f}_{1},\ldots,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{u}\boldsymbol{f}_{l}
.
List the union of
\{\boldsymbol{f}_{\delta,1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{f}_{\delta,l_{\delta}}\}
over
\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
as
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{m}
.
By
[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
, this is a super Gröbner basis for
\mathcal{M}
.
∎

It is easy to see the following from the proof: if the given generators for
\mathcal{M}
contain only polynomials with integer coefficients, then Lemma
6.6
computes a super Gröbner basis containing only polynomials with integer coefficients.

Let
0\leq d\leq n-1
be an integer.
From now on we denote

\mathbb{A}_{d}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{d}^{\pm}],\quad\mathbb{A}_{d}^{+}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{d}^{\pm}]^{*}.

In particular,
\mathbb{A}_{0}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{A}_{0}^{+}=\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

As stated in the beginning of this subsection, we now consider the vectors
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
with the special form
(0,\ldots,0,v_{d+1},\ldots,v_{n})
where
v_{d+1},\ldots,v_{n}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent.
The following lemma can be seens as a generalization of
[
17
, Lemma 6.2]
.

Lemma 6.7

(Generalization of
[
17
, Lemma 6.2]
)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{m}
be a super Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{M}
.
Let
v=(0,\ldots,0,v_{d+1},\ldots,v_{n})\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
be such that
0\leq d\leq n-1
and
v_{d+1},\ldots,v_{n}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent.
Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
.
Then there exists
b_{i}\in\{0\}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
and
c_{j}\in\{0\}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
such that
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{b_{i}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{c_{j}}\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i}\in\mathbb{A}_{d}
for
i=1,\ldots,K
and
j=1,\ldots,m
.

Proof.

Let
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\},i\in\{1,\ldots,K\},
be such that
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i}\neq 0
.
Since
v_{d+1},\ldots,v_{n}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent, there exists an open neighbourhood
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n-d}
of
(v_{d+1},\ldots,v_{n})
, such that every
v^{\prime}\in\{0\}^{d}\times U
satisfies
\operatorname{in}_{v^{\prime}}(\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i})=\operatorname{in}_{v}(\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i})
.
This shows that
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{z_{ij}}\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i}\in\mathbb{A}_{d}
for some
z_{ij}\in\{0\}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
.

Letting
F\coloneqq\{(i,j)\mid\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i}\neq 0\}
, this defines
z_{ij}
for all
(i,j)\in F
.
Note that for
(i,j)\in F
we have

\max_{1\leq i^{\prime}\leq K}\{\deg_{v}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j,i^{\prime}})+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i^{\prime}}\}=-v\cdot z_{ij}+\alpha_{i}.

Considering a sequence

(i_{0},j_{0}),(i_{1},j_{0}),(i_{1},j_{1}),\ldots,(i_{l},j_{l-1}),(i_{l},j_{l}),(i_{0},j_{l})

(18)

in
F
, and writing
i_{l+1}=i_{0}
, we find that

0=\sum_{s=0}^{l}\left(\max_{1\leq i^{\prime}\leq K}\{\deg_{v}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j,i^{\prime}})+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i^{\prime}}\}-\max_{1\leq i^{\prime}\leq K}\{\deg_{v}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j,i^{\prime}})+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i^{\prime}}\}\right)=\sum_{s=0}^{l}\left(v\cdot z_{i_{s+1}j_{s}}-v\cdot z_{i_{s}j_{s}}\right).

Since
v=(0,\ldots,0,v_{d+1},\ldots,v_{n})
with
v_{d+1},\ldots,v_{n}
being
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent and
z_{ij}\in\{0\}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
,
the above equation yields

\sum_{s=0}^{l}\left(z_{i_{s+1}j_{s}}-z_{i_{s}j_{s}}\right)=0

(19)

for every allowed sequence (
18
) in
F
.

We now extend
z_{ij}
and Equation (
19
) to all pairs
(i,j)\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\times\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
Assume
z_{ij}
is already defined on a set
E\supseteq F
and (
19
) is valid on
E
.
Pick
(i,j)\not\in E
.
If there exists a sequence

(i_{1},j),(i_{1},j_{1}),\ldots,(i_{l},j_{l-1}),(i_{l},j_{l}),(i,j_{l})\in E,

we put
i_{l+1}=i
and define

z_{ij}\coloneqq z_{i_{1}j}-\sum_{s=1}^{l}\left(z_{i_{s}j_{s}}-z_{i_{s+1}j_{s}}\right).

One easily verifies that Equation (
19
) then holds for every allowed sequence (
18
) in
E\cup\{(i,j)\}
.

If there is no sequence

(i_{1},j_{0}),(i_{1},j_{1}),\ldots,(i_{l},j_{l-1}),(i_{l},j_{l}),(i_{0},j_{l})

in
E
, we can take
z_{ij}
to be any element of
\{0\}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
and have Equation (
19
) hold for all sequences (
18
) in
E\cup\{(i,j)\}
.

Having thus extended
z_{ij}
to all pairs
(i,j)\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\times\{1,\ldots,m\}
, we define

b_{i}\coloneqq z_{i1},

and

c_{j}\coloneqq z_{ij}-z_{i1},

which is independent of
i
thanks to Equation (
19
). Indeed, using Equation (
19
) on the allowed sequence
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j),(i^{\prime},1),(i,1)
we get
z_{ij}-z_{i1}=z_{i^{\prime}j}-z_{i^{\prime}1}
.
Hence,
z_{ij}=b_{i}+c_{j}
and the lemma follows.
∎

Suppose
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
is such that
v=(0,\ldots,0,v_{d+1},\ldots,v_{n})
with
v_{d+1},\ldots,v_{n}
being
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent.
Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
.
For each
j=1,\ldots,m
, define
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})=(\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{1},\ldots,\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{K})
where

\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i}\coloneqq\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{b_{i}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{c_{j}}\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i}\in\mathbb{A}_{d},\quad i=1,\ldots,K.

Here,
b_{i}
and
c_{j}
are defined as in Lemma
6.7
.
Note that the vectors
b_{i},c_{j}\in\{0\}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
are not necessarily uniquely determined. However, when
d,v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}
are fixed, the polynomials
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i}
are uniquely determined by
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{m}
.
In fact, by Lemma
6.7
each
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i}
can be uniquely written as
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{s}\cdot p
for some
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{s}\in\mathbb{R}[X_{d+1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
and
p\in\mathbb{R}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{d}^{\pm}]
.
Therefore
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i}
is uniquely determined as the polynomial
p
in the decomposition.

Note that if
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i}\neq 0
then
\deg_{v}(g_{j,i})=-v\cdot(b_{i}+c_{j})
, otherwise
\deg_{v}(g_{j,i})<-v\cdot(b_{i}+c_{j})
.
In both cases,

\deg_{v}(g_{j,i})\leq-v\cdot(b_{i}+c_{j}),

(20)

where the equality holds if and only if
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i}\neq 0
.

Lemma 6.8
.

If there exists
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
such that
\operatorname{in}^{d}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i}\neq 0,\operatorname{in}^{d}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i^{\prime}}\neq 0
, then
\alpha_{i}-v\cdot b_{i}=\alpha_{i^{\prime}}-v\cdot b_{i^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

If
\operatorname{in}^{d}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i}\neq 0,\operatorname{in}^{d}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i^{\prime}}\neq 0
, then
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i}\neq 0,\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i^{\prime}}\neq 0
, so
\deg_{v}(\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i})+\alpha_{i}=\deg_{v}(\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i^{\prime}})+\alpha_{i^{\prime}}
.

But
\deg_{v}(\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i})=\deg_{v}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{-b_{i}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{-c_{j}}\cdot\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})_{i})=-v\cdot(b_{i}+c_{j})
.
Deriving the same equation for
i^{\prime}
we have
-v\cdot(b_{i}+c_{j})+\alpha_{i}=-v\cdot(b_{i^{\prime}}+c_{j})+\alpha_{i^{\prime}}
.
This yields
\alpha_{i}-v\cdot b_{i}=\alpha_{i^{\prime}}-v\cdot b_{i^{\prime}}
.
∎

Define by
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\mathcal{M})
the
\mathbb{A}_{d}
-module generated by
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\boldsymbol{g}_{1}),\ldots,\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\boldsymbol{g}_{m})\in\mathbb{A}_{d}^{K}
:

\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbb{A}_{d}\cdot\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{j}\cdot\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\boldsymbol{g}_{j})\;\middle|\;p_{1},\ldots p_{m}\in\mathbb{A}_{d}\right\}.

A key component of proving the original result of Einsiedler et al.
[
17
]
is
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
, which shows that
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\mathcal{M})\cap\left(\mathbb{A}_{d}^{+}\right)^{K}\neq\emptyset
implies
\mathcal{M}\cap\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}\neq\emptyset
.
However, this fails when we additionally impose Property (
8
): an element in
\mathcal{M}\cap\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
satisfying Property (
8
) might not be obtained from an element in
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{d}(\mathcal{M})\cap\left(\mathbb{A}_{d}^{+}\right)^{K}
satisfying a similar property.
Indeed, if we directly apply
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
to our situation, the main caveat would be in the last paragraph of the proof, where different “levels” of polynomials are combined together to create a positive element.
This no longer works if we add in degree constraints.
The following lemma shows that
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
can still be made partially compatible with Property (
8
), if we impose the additional constraint
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\left(\sum_{k=d+1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}v_{k}\right)^{K}
.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{m}
be a super Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{M}
.
Let
v=(0,\ldots,0,v_{d+1},\ldots,v_{n})
be such that
0\leq d\leq n-1
and
v_{d+1},\ldots,v_{n}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent.
Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\left(\sum_{k=d+1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}v_{k}\right)^{K}
.
Denote
I^{\prime}\coloneqq\{i\in I\mid\alpha_{i}=\min_{i^{\prime}\in I}\alpha_{i^{\prime}}\},J^{\prime}\coloneqq O_{v}\cup J
.
Denote by
\pi_{d}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}
the projection onto the first
d
coordinates.
For every
u\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{*}
, define
O^{\prime}_{u}\coloneqq\{i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\mid\pi_{d}(a_{i})\not\perp u\}
.
Then the two following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

(Condition in
(LocInfShift)
): There exists
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\left(\boldsymbol{f}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{K}
and

\left(O_{w}\cup J^{\prime}\right)\cap M_{w}(I^{\prime},\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{f}))\neq\emptyset\quad\text{ for every $w\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}$}.

(21)

(ii)

We have
J^{\prime}\cap I^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, and there exists

Our goal is to Condition

In the previous subsection we considered the special case where the vector

Given a matrix

(LocInfCell)

In this subsection we further reduce the Condition

to a Condition

which consists of verifying a

number of
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
for each coordinate-change matrix

.

Let
v\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*},\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
.
Denote by

the canonical basis of the
\mathbb{A}
-module
\mathbb{A}^{K}
.
We introduce the new variables

and define an
\mathbb{A}
-module homomorphism

\phi:\mathbb{A}^{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm},T_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,T_{K}^{\pm}],\quad\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{u}e_{i}\mapsto\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{u}T_{i}.

We have
\phi(\operatorname{in}_{v,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{f}))=\operatorname{in}_{(v,\boldsymbol{\alpha})}(\phi(\boldsymbol{f}))
for every
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{A}^{K}

As in the previous subsections let
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{m}
be a super Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{M}
.
Since
\phi(\boldsymbol{g}_{i})
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{R}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm},T_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,T_{K}^{\pm}]

\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{K}

are the same.
Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
be the common refinement of the partitions associated to the polynomials
\phi(\boldsymbol{g}_{1}),\ldots,\phi(\boldsymbol{g}_{m})

From now on we use the term “
cell
” to call elements of a given partition.
Fix
I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,K\}
.
There exists a partition
\mathcal{L}_{I}
of
\mathbb{R}^{K}
such that for any two vectors
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{K})
,
(\alpha^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\alpha^{\prime}_{K})
in the same cell, we have
\alpha_{i}>\alpha_{j}\iff\alpha^{\prime}_{i}>\alpha^{\prime}_{j}
and
\alpha_{i}<\alpha_{j}\iff\alpha^{\prime}_{i}<\alpha^{\prime}_{j}
for all

Define the partition
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{I}\coloneqq\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}\times\mathcal{L}_{I}
of
\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{K}
where each cell is of the form
\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}\times P,P\in\mathcal{L}_{I}

Finally, there exists a partition
\mathcal{L}_{O}
of
\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}
such that any two vectors
v,v^{\prime}
in the same cell satisfy
v\perp a_{i}\iff v^{\prime}\perp a_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}

By subdividing
\mathcal{L}_{O}
we can suppose that each cell is a convex polyhedron.
Similar to the definition of
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{I}
, we define the partition
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{O}\coloneqq\mathcal{L}_{O}\times\mathbb{R}^{K}
of
\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{K}
.

For any two partition
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
of the same set
S

\mathcal{A}\vee\mathcal{B}
to be the partition of

whose elements are of the form
A\cap B,A\in\mathcal{A},B\in\mathcal{B}
.
Consider the partition
\mathcal{L}
of
\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)^{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{K}

We point out that from the definition of the partitions

are invariant under scaling by a positive real, meaning
x\in Q\iff r\cdot x\in Q
for all cells
Q\in\mathcal{L}
and

.

Let

be the canonical projection.
For each
Q\in\mathcal{L}
, define the two-element partition

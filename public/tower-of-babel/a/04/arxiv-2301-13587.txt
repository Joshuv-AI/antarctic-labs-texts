Cofibration category structures on the category of graphs
Shuchita Goyal, Rekha Santhanam

Abstract
In this article, we show that there is no cofibration category structure on the category of finite graphs with × \times -homotopy equivalences as the class of weak equivalences. Further, we show that it is not possible to enlarge the class of weak equivalences to get cofibration category structure on the category of finite graphs without including morphisms where domain and codomain have non-isomorphic stiff subgraphs. Keywords: Cofibration category, Category of finite graphs, × \times -homotopy equivalences, stiff graphs. MSC 2020: 05C10; 18N40; 55P99



1
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{G}
denote the category of finite undirected graphs without multiple edges, with graph morphisms being functions on vertices which preserve edges.
The category
\mathcal{G}
with
\times
-homotopy equivalences forms a category with weak equivalences, and we denote it by
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{W}_{\times})
.
The notion of
\times
-homotopy equivalences was defined by Dochtermann in
[
3
]
, and Chih and Scull
[
2
]
, gave a construction of the homotopy category of graphs with respect to
\times
-homotopy equivalences.

Let
\mathcal{W}_{\times}
denote the class of
\times
-homotopy equivalences in
\mathcal{G}
. In
[
5
]
, we showed that there is no model structure on
\mathcal{G}
with
\mathcal{W}_{\times}
as the class of weak equivalences, irrespective of the choice for the class of cofibrations and fibrations. In the proof we used the fact that in a model category, acyclic cofibrations have left lifting property with respect to fibrations.

The advantage of defining a model structure on
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{W}_{\times})
is that we can construct homotopy pushouts in
\mathcal{G}
. This would potentially simplify computing the neighbourhood/hom complexes of graphs since they take double mapping cylinders of graphs to homotopy pushouts in spaces
[
6
]
.

A cofibration category is a category with distinguished classes of weak equivalences and cofibrations satisfying compatibility conditions (cf.
2.4
). This is a weaker notion than that of a model structure, but several of the homotopy theoretic constructions can still be defined. In particular, we can define homotopy pushouts in a cofibration category. In a recent article, Carranza, Doherty, Kapulkin, Opte, Saruzola and Wong
[
1
]
gave a construction of a cofibration category structure on the category of digraphs.

In this article, we prove that there is no choice of the class of cofibrations for which
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{W}_{\times})
is a cofibration category
(cf.
3.6
and
3.3
).
The class of
\times
-homotopy equivalences classify graphs up to their stiff subgraphs. We then enlarge
\mathcal{W}_{\times}
, the class of weak equivalences in
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{W}
(cf.
4.1
), a class of maps that preserve all the stiff subgraphs up to isomorphism. We show that this class
\mathcal{W}
does not satisfy the 2 out of 6 property (cf.
4.2
).
Moreover, extending this class of weak equivalences
\mathcal{W}
to
\mathcal{W}_{\text{new}}
that satisfies the
2
out of
6
property will include morphisms between graphs having non-isomorphic stiff subgraphs (cf.
4.5
).
This then implies that there is no cofibration structure on finite graphs with weak equivalences classifying stiff subgraphs.

2
Preliminaries

We consider the category of finite graphs
\mathcal{G}
, namely, finite undirected graphs without multiple edges with morphisms as vertex set maps preserving edges.
An edge in a graph with the same endpoints is called a
loop,
and the endpoint vertex is often referred to as a
looped vertex
.
A graph without multiple edges and loops is called a
simple graph
.

Definition 2.1
.

A vertex
v\in G
is said to
fold
to a vertex
v^{\prime}\in G
, if every neighbour of
v
is also a neighbour of
v^{\prime}
,
i.e.
, if the set of neighbours of
v
,
{\rm N}(v)\subseteq{\rm N}(v^{\prime})
.
We note that the map
f:G\to G-v
that maps each vertex (other than
v
) of
G
to itself, and
v
to
v^{\prime}
is a graph map.
In such a case, we call
f
a
fold map
that folds
G
to
G-v
.

A graph is
stiff
if there is no fold in that graph.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G,H\in\mathcal{G}
be two graphs. The
(categorical) product
of
G
and
H
is defined to be the graph
G\times H
whose vertex set is the Cartesian product,
V(G)\times V(H)
, and
(g,h)(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in E(G\times H)
whenever
gg^{\prime}\in E(G)
and
hh^{\prime}\in E(H)
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
I_{n}
be the graph with vertex set
\{0,1,\dots,n\}
and edge set
\{ij:|i-j|\leq 1\}
. We note that the graph
I_{n}
folds down to a single looped vertex for any,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and is said to be
contractible
.

Definition 2.3
.

[
3
]

Two graph maps
f,g:A\to B
are said to be
\times
-homotopic
, if for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a graph map
F:A\times I_{n}\to B
such that
F|_{A\times\{0\}}=f
and
F|_{A\times\{n\}}=g
. Two
\times
-homotopic maps are denoted as
f\simeq_{\times}g
. A graph map
f:A\to B
is a
\times
-homotopy equivalence
, if there exists a graph map
g:B\to A
such that
gf\simeq_{\times}1_{A}
and
fg\simeq_{\times}1_{B}
, where
1_{X}
denotes the identity map on
X
.

By a
stiff subgraph
of a graph
G
, we always mean a subgraph of
G
, which is stiff, that
G
folds down to and denote it by
G_{s}
. We note that a graph
G
, via different sequences of folds, may fold down to different subgraphs of it, but all of these subgraphs are isomorphic
[
3
, Proposition 6.6]
.

Definition 2.4
.

[
7
]

A category with weak equivalences and cofibrations as distinguished subclasses of morphisms is called a
cofibration category
if the following axioms are satisfied:

1.

Weak equivalences satisfy ‘2 out of 6’ property,
i.e.
, for any three composable morphisms
f:A\to B,\ g:B\to C,\ h:C\to D
, if
hg,gf
are weak equivalences, then so are
f,g,h
and
hgf.

2.

Every isomorphism of the category is an acyclic cofibration,
i.e.
a map which is a weak equivalence and a cofibration.

3.

An initial object exists in the category.

4.

Every object X of the category is cofibrant,
i.e.
, the map from the initial object to
X
is a cofibration.

5.

Cofibrations and acyclic cofibrations are preserved under cobase change.

6.

Every morphism in the category factors as a composite of a cofibration followed by a weak equivalence.

3
Non existence of cofibration category structure on
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{W}_{\times})

We now consider the category of graphs,
\mathcal{G}
, with
\times
-homotopy equivalences. An empty graph is a graph whose vertex set and edge set are empty. One can easily verify that an empty graph is an initial object in
\mathcal{G}
, and hence the axiom (3) of

\mathcal{W}_{\times}
denotes the class of
\times
-homotopy equivalences.

The class

Next, we note that the composition of two

We now prove that if there is a cofibration category structure on

, then the class of cofibrations is a subclass of inclusions.

Proposition 3.2

Let

Let

for two distinct vertices

If

is simple, then
B
is simple, as

is a
\times
-homotopy equivalence.
Note that

is not a looped vertex, therefore

Let

be the graph with

and

Let

be the graph inclusion.
Then

is simple, but the pushout of the diagram

is not simple and hence not
\times
-homotopy equivalent to

.

If

is not simple, but neither of

. Then the pushout has a

with a looped vertex as a stiff subgraph. Any other copy of such a graph in

is not

-homotopy equivalent to the pushout.

If either

or

or

in

Corollary 3.3
.

If there exists a cofibration structure on
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{W}_{\times})
then cofibrations have to be a subclass of induced inclusions.

Proof.

By
3.2
we know that cofibrations have to be a subclass of inclusions.
In
[
5

, we have shown that if for any
f:A\to B
which is a
\times
-homotopy equivalence and a non-induced graph inclusion, there is a graph

such that the cobase change of

\times
-homotopy equivalence.

Thus any class of cofibrations on

An induced inclusion

There is no cofibration category structure on

Investigation of two-particle contributions to nucleon matrix elements
Ferenc Pittler

Abstract
We investigate contributions of excited states to nucleon matrix elements by studying the two- and three-point functions using nucleon and pion-nucleon interpolating fields. This study is carried out using twisted mass fermion ensembles with pion masses 346 MeV and 131 MeV. We compare the results obtained using these two ensembles and show preliminary results for nucleon charges.

Introduction

Nucleon matrix elements
\braket{N|J|N}
are important quantities to understand the structure of the nucleon. They can be extracted from the ratio of three- to two-point functions in the asymptotic limit:

\displaystyle\frac{\braket{0|O_{N}(t_{\text{sink}})J(t_{\text{ins}})\bar{O}_{N}(0)|0}}{\braket{0|O_{N}(t_{\text{sink}})\bar{O}_{N}(0)|0}}\xrightarrow{\text{$t_{\rm ins},\,t_{\rm sink}\,\rightarrow\infty$ }}\braket{N|J|N}\,.

(1)

The time separations used in practice, however, are limited and the ratio shows clear time dependence even at the largest available time separations. Since the time dependence comes from contributions of excited states, and the lowest excited state with the quantum numbers of the nucleon is a nucleon-pion state, we investigate the contribution of nucleon-pion states to the matrix elements.

2
Generalized eigenvalue problem (GEVP) and its impact on three-point functions

For a set of interpolating operators
\{O_{i}\}
with fixed quantum numbers, one can compute the following matrix function:

\displaystyle C_{ij}(t)=\braket{0|O_{i}(t)O^{\dagger}_{j}(0)|0}\,.

(2)

The generalized eigenvalue problem (GEVP)

\displaystyle C_{ij}(t)\,v^{n}_{j}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{n}(t,t_{0})\,C_{ij}(t_{0})\,v^{n}_{j}

(3)

can determine the eigenvalue
\lambda^{n}(t,t_{0})
and eigenvector
v^{n}_{j}
of the
n
-th eigenmode. The eigenvectors should be independent of the reference time
t_{0}
.
Solving the GEVP one extracts excited state energies
E_{n}
from eigenvalues
\lambda^{n}(t,t_{0})
via

\displaystyle\lambda^{n}(t,t_{0})

\displaystyle=e^{-E_{n}(t-t_{0})}\,+\text{contributions from higher states}.

(4)

The GEVP also provides improved operators
O_{n}^{\prime}
that increase the overlap with the
n
-th eigenstate by finding the optimal linear combination

\displaystyle O_{n}^{\prime}\ket{0}\equiv v^{n}_{j}O^{\dagger}_{j}\ket{0}\propto\ket{n}\,.

(5)

Therefore, one can use the GEVP to construct an improved nucleon operator
O_{N}^{\prime}
to obtain a ratio of three- to two-point functions with reduced time dependence:

\displaystyle\frac{\braket{0|O_{N}J\bar{O}_{N}|0}}{\braket{0|O_{N}\bar{O}_{N}|0}}\xrightarrow{\text{GEVP improved}}\frac{\braket{0|O_{N}^{\prime}J\bar{O}_{N}^{\prime}|0}}{\braket{0|O_{N}^{\prime}\bar{O}_{N}^{\prime}|0}}\,.

(6)

Here we consider a two-state model with
\ket{N}
and
\ket{N\pi}
to demonstrate how the GEVP works for three-point functions.
We start with two interpolating operators
O_{N}
and
O_{N\pi}
. When acting on the vacuum they create states

\displaystyle\bar{O}_{N}\ket{0}

\displaystyle=\ket{N}+x\ket{N\pi}+\cdots\,,

\displaystyle\bar{O}_{N\pi}\ket{0}

\displaystyle=\ket{N\pi}+y\ket{N}+\cdots\,,

(7)

where without loss of generality we take one of the coefficients to by unity, and we also suppress the time-evolution factors to simplify the notations and the ellipsis denote higher excited states.
Then the improved nucleon operator
O_{N}^{\prime}
satisfies

\displaystyle\bar{O}_{N}^{\prime}\ket{0}\equiv(\bar{O}_{N}-x\,\bar{O}_{N\pi})\ket{0}

\displaystyle=(1-xy)\ket{N}\,,

(8)

and the improved three-point function
I
can be expanded as

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\equiv\braket{0|O_{N}^{\prime}J\bar{O}_{N}^{\prime}|0}=\braket{0|(O_{N}-x\,O_{N\pi})J(\bar{O}_{N}-x\,\bar{O}_{N\pi})|0}

\displaystyle=\braket{0|O_{N}J\bar{O}_{N}|0}-x\braket{0|O_{N}J\bar{O}_{N\pi}|0}-x\braket{0|O_{N\pi}J\bar{O}_{N}|0}+x^{2}\braket{0|O_{N\pi}J\bar{O}_{N\pi}|0}\,.

(9)

Since the unimproved operators can both create two states as in Eq. (
2
), one can expand their three-point functions as follows

\displaystyle\braket{0|O_{N}J\bar{O}_{N}|0}

\displaystyle=\braket{N|J|N}+x\braket{N|J|N\pi}+x\braket{N\pi|J|N}+x^{2}\braket{N\pi|J|N\pi}

\displaystyle\braket{0|O_{N}J\bar{O}_{N\pi}|0}

\displaystyle=y\braket{N|J|N}+\braket{N|J|N\pi}+xy\braket{N\pi|J|N}+x\braket{N\pi|J|N\pi}

\displaystyle\braket{0|O_{N\pi}J\bar{O}_{N}|0}

\displaystyle=y\braket{N|J|N}+xy\braket{N|J|N\pi}+\braket{N\pi|J|N}+x\braket{N\pi|J|N\pi}

\displaystyle\braket{0|O_{N\pi}J\bar{O}_{N\pi}|0}

\displaystyle=y^{2}\braket{N|J|N}+y\braket{N|J|N\pi}+y\braket{N\pi|J|N}+\braket{N\pi|J|N\pi}\,.

(10)

5
Conclusions

We use an improved nucleon interpolating field constructed from the GEVP to compute the three- to two-point function ratios for various nucleon form factors.
We find that excited state contamination in the scalar and axial charges matrix elements is unlikely to come from the lowest
\pi N
state, while for the pseudoscalar and induced pseudoscalar form factors a significant improvement is observed.

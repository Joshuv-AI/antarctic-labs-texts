Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, we study strongly coupled elliptic systems in non-variational form with negative exponents involving fractional Laplace operators. We investigate the existence, nonexistence, and uniqueness of the positive classical solution. The results obtained here are a natural extension of the results obtained by Ghergu, in
[
6
]
, for the fractional case.

1
Introduction and main results

The present paper deals with existence, nonexistence, and uniqueness of positive solutions for elliptic systems of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}(-\Delta)^{s}u=u^{-p}v^{-q}&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\\
(-\Delta)^{t}v=u^{-r}v^{-\theta}&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\\
u=v=0&{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
\Omega
is a smooth bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
,
0<s,t<1
,
r,q>0
,
p,\theta\geq 0
and the fractional Laplace operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
is defined as

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=C(n,s)\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\;dy\,,

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

C(n,s)=\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1-\cos(\zeta_{1})}{|\zeta|^{n+2s}}\;d\zeta\right)^{-1}

with
\zeta=(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A natural space for this operator is a weighted
L_{1}
-space:

L_{s}:=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|}{1+|x|^{n+2s}}dx<+\infty\right\}.

The norm in
L_{s}
is naturally given by

\|u\|_{L_{s}}=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|}{1+|x|^{n+2s}}dx.

The study of system (
1
) was mainly motivated from the well known fractional Lane-Emden problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrll}(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&u^{p}&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\\
u&=&0&{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{array}\right.,

(2)

where
\Omega
is a smooth bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
and
0<s<1
.

Recently, it has been proved in
[
16
]
that this problem admits at least one positive solution for
1<p<\frac{n+2s}{n-2s}
. The nonexistence has been established in
[
14
]
whenever
p\geq\frac{n+2s}{n-2s}
and
\Omega
is star-shaped. These results were known long before for
s=1
, see the classical references
[
4
,
5
,
10
]
.

For system of the type (
1
) with
p=0=\theta
and
r,q<0
, existence results of positive solutions have been established when
qr>1
in
[
7
]
for
s\neq t
and in
[
8
]
for
s=t
. The latter also proves existence and uniqueness of positive solution in the case that
qr<1
. Finally, when
qr=1
, the behavior of (
1
) is resonant and the related eigenvalue problem has been studied in
[
9
]
.

Nowadays, there has been some interest in systems of the type (
1
) with
p,\theta\geq 0
and
q,r>0
. In
[
6
]
, the author studied existence, nonexistence, uniqueness, and regularity of solutions for the system (
1
) with
s=1=t
.

In this paper, we are going to treat the system (
1
) in the case
p,\theta\geq 0
and
q,r>0
. In this structure, the system above corresponds to the prototype equation (
2
) in which the exponent
p
is negative and generalize the results obtained in
[
6
]
. It is well known that for such a range of exponents, the system (
1
) does not have a variational structure. To overcome this, we employ the sub-super method, which our approach relies on the boundary behavior of solutions to (
2
) (with
p<0
) or more generally, to singular elliptic problems of the type

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrll}(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&K(x)u^{-p},\ \ u>0&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\\
u&=&0&{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{array}\right.

(3)

where
K\in C^{\nu}_{loc}(\Omega),\nu\in(0,1)
, such that
\inf\limits_{\Omega}K>0
and satisfies for some
0\leq q<2s
and
C_{1},C_{2}>0

C_{1}d(x)^{-q}\leq K(x)\leq C_{2}d(x)^{-q}\text{ in }\Omega,

where
d(x)=dist(x,\partial\Omega)
, studied by Adimurthi, Giacomoni and Santra in
[
3
]
.

We say that a pair
(u,v)
of continuous function in
\Omega
and bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a positive classical
solution of system (
1
), if
(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)
and
(-\Delta)^{t}v(x)
are well defined for all
x\in\Omega
, further
u
and
v
are positive in
\Omega
and all equalities in (
1
) hold pointwise in each corresponding set. Positive classical super and subsolutions are defined similarly.

We will establish our first result concerning the system (
1
).

Theorem 1.1
.

(Nonexistence). Let
p,\theta\geq 0
,
r,q>0.
Then the system (
1
) has no positive classical solutions, provided
that one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

\frac{qt}{s}+p<1
and
r\geq\frac{2t}{s}
;

(ii)

\frac{qt}{s}+p>1
and
r(2s-qt)\geq 2s(1+p)
;

(iii)

\frac{rs}{t}+\theta<1
and
q\geq\frac{2s}{t}
;

(iv)

\frac{rs}{t}+\theta>1
and
q(2t-rs)\geq 2t(1+\theta)
;

(v)

p>\max\{1,\frac{rs}{t}-1\},\frac{2rs}{t}>(1-\theta)(1+p)
and
qt(1+p-\frac{rs}{t})>(1+p)(1+\theta)s
;

(vi)

\theta>\max\{1,\frac{qt}{s}-1\},\frac{2qt}{s}>(1-p)(1+\theta)
and
rs(1+\theta-\frac{qt}{s})>(1+p)(1+\theta)t
.

Remark 1.1
.

The conditions (i) and (ii) in Theorem
1.1
impose conditions on the exponent
q
to vary on the interval
(0,\frac{2s}{t})
, while in (v) the exponent
q
can take any value greater than
\frac{2s}{t}
, provided adjusting the other three exponents
p,r,\theta
conveniently. Finally, from the conditions (iii), (iv) and (vi), the exponent
r
is also restricted as above.

Define the following quantities

\alpha=p+\frac{qt}{s}\min\left\{1,\frac{2t-rs}{(1+\theta)t}\right\},\ \ \ \beta=\theta+\frac{rs}{t}\min\left\{1,\frac{2s-qt}{(1+p)s}\right\}.

These above quantities
\alpha
and
\beta
are related to the boundary behavior of the solution to the singular elliptic problem (
3
), as they will be explained in Proposition
2.3
below.

Next, we will state the existence of classical solutions to (
1
).

Theorem 1.2
.

(Existence). Let
p,\theta\geq 0
,
q,r>0
satisfying the inequality

(1+p)(1+\theta)-qr>0.

(4)

In addition, assume that one of the following conditions below holds:

(i)

\alpha\leq 1
and
r<\frac{2t}{s}
;

(ii)

\beta\leq 1
and
q<\frac{2s}{t}
;

(iii)

p,\theta\geq 1
,
r<\frac{2t}{s}
and
q<\frac{2s}{t}
.

Then, the system (
1
) has at least one positive classical solution
(u,v)\in(C^{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{2}
, for some
\eta\in(0,1)
.

The proof is made invoking the Schauder’s fixed point theorem in a suitable chosen closed convex subset of
(C^{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{2}
, for some
\eta\in(0,1)
, which contains all the functions having a certain rate of decay expressed in terms of the distance function
d(x)
up to the boundary of
\Omega
.

The following necessary and sufficient
conditions for the existence of classical solutions to (
1
) follows directly from Theorem
1.1
(i) and (iii) and Theorem
1.2
(i) and (ii).

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
p,\theta\geq 0
,
q,r>0
satisfy (
4
).

(i)

Assume
\frac{qt}{s}+p<1
. Then system (
1
) has positive classical solutions if and only if
r<\frac{2t}{s}
;

(ii)

Assume
\frac{rs}{t}+\theta<1
. Then system (
1
) has positive classical solutions if and only if
q<\frac{2s}{t}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

(Uniqueness). Let
p,\theta\geq 0
,
q,r>0
, satisfy (
4
) and one of the following conditions:

(i)

\frac{qt}{s}+p<1
and
r<\frac{2t}{s}
;

(ii)

\frac{rs}{t}+\theta<1
and
q<\frac{2s}{t}
.

Then, the system (
1
) has a unique positive classical solution.

Several methods have been employed in the proof of existence, nonexistence and uniqueness results of positive solutions of elliptic systems. Our approach is inspired by a method developed by Ghergu in
[
6
]
to treat systems involving Laplace operators based on boundary behavior of the solution to (
3
), when
s=1
. Particularly, the boundary behavior of the solution to (
3
), proved by Adimurthi, Giacomoni, and Santra
[
3
]
, as well as some fundamental results to be proved in the next section will play an important role in the proofs of Theorems of this work.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we obtain some preliminary properties related to the boundary behavior of the solution to (
3
). The rest of the Sections are devoted to the proofs of our results.

2
Notation and auxiliary results

Consider the nonlocal eigenvalue problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrll}(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&\lambda u&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\\
u&=&0&{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{array}\right..

(5)

Since the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
is self-adjoint, by using a weak formulation and a suitable variational framework, Servadei and Valdinoci
[
15
]
investigated in detail the discrete spectrum of
(-\Delta)^{s}
in
\Omega
for any
s\in(0,1)
. In particular, they proved that the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(s):=\lambda_{1}((-\Delta)^{s})
is positive, simple and characterized by

\lambda_{1}(s)=\inf_{u\in X(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}dx}{\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2}}dx,

where

X(\Omega):=\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u=0\text{ a.e in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\}.

Let
\varphi_{s}
be a nonnegative eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{1}(s)
in the weak sense. Results of Hölder regularity to the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
obtained by Ros-Oton and Serra
[
12
]
imply that
\varphi_{s}\in C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and moreover is a classical solution of (
5
) which is positive in
\Omega
. The last claim follows from Silvestre’s strong maximum principle
[
17
]
which holds for classical supersolutions (subsolutions).

By suitable normalization we may assume
|\varphi_{s}|_{\infty}=1
. In addition, it follows from the results in
[
13
]
that

cd(x)^{s}\leq\varphi_{s}(x)\leq\frac{1}{c}d(x)^{s},

(6)

for some positive constant
c
.

We denote by
G_{s}(\cdot,\cdot)
the Green’s function of the fractional Laplace operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
on
\Omega
. Let
w
be a weak solution of the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrll}(-\Delta)^{s}w&=&h&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\\
w&=&0&{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{array}\right..

(7)

If
h\in C^{\alpha}_{loc}(\Omega)
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, by Theorem 2.5 of
[
11
]
, there exists
\gamma>0
such that
w\in C^{2s+\gamma}_{loc}(\Omega)
is a classical solution of (
7
), i.e, both equalities hold pointwise in each corresponding set. Therefore,

w(x)=\int\limits_{\Omega}G_{s}(x,y)h(y)dy\text{ in }\Omega\text{ and }w(x)=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.

(8)

Reciprocally, if
h\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, by Theorem 1.2.3 of
[
2
]
the function defined by setting (
8
) belongs to
C^{2s+\varepsilon}_{loc}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})\cap L_{s}
, fulfills
d(x)^{1-s}w\in C(\overline{\Omega})
, and
w
is the only classical solution of problem (
7
).

Now, let
\phi_{s}
be the function that satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrll}(-\Delta)^{s}\phi_{s}&=&1&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\\
\phi_{s}&=&0&{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{array}\right..

By Silvestre’s strong maximum principle (see
[
17
]
), we get
\phi_{s}(x)>0
in
\Omega
. Therefore,

\varphi_{s}(x)=\lambda_{1}(s)\int\limits_{\Omega}G_{s}(x,y)\varphi_{s}(y)dy

and

\phi_{s}(x)=\int\limits_{\Omega}G_{s}(x,y)dy,

which, as a consequence of the normalization of
\varphi_{s}
, leads to

\varphi_{s}\leq\lambda_{1}(s)\phi_{s}.

(9)

An important tool for the uniqueness result of solutions of the system (
1
) is as follows:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
p\geq 0
and
\psi:\Omega\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be a continuous function. If
\underline{u}
is a positive classical subsolution and
\overline{u}
is a positive classical supersolution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrll}(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&\psi(x)u^{-p}&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\\
u&=&0&{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{array}\right.,

then
\underline{u}\leq\overline{u}
in
\Omega
.

Proof.
If
p=0
the result is a consequence of the Silvestre’s strong maximum principle. Suppose
p>0,
and assume by contradiction that the set
\omega:=\{x\in\Omega:\overline{u}(x)<\underline{u}(x)\}
is not empty and let
w:=\underline{u}-\overline{u}
. Then,
w
achieves its maximum on
\Omega
at a point
x_{0}\in\omega
. Then,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(n,s)\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})}\frac{w(x_{0})-w(y)}{|x_{0}-y|^{n+2s}}\;dy=(-\Delta)^{s}w(x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\psi(x_{0})[\underline{u}(x_{0})^{-p}-\overline{u}(x_{0})^{-p}]<0,

which is a contradiction. Therefore,
\omega=\emptyset
, that is,
\underline{u}\leq\overline{u}
in
\Omega
.

Now an important tool for the nonexistence and uniqueness results of solutions of the system (
1
) is as follows:

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(u,v)
be a positive classical solution of system (
1
). Then, there exists a constant
c>0
such
that

u(x)\geq cd(x)^{s}\text{ and }v(x)\geq cd(x)^{t}\text{ in }\Omega.

(10)

Proof.

Let
(u,v)
be a positive classical solution of (
1
). By inequalities (
6
) and (
9
), there is a constant
c_{0}>0
such that
\phi_{s}(x)\geq c_{0}d(x)^{s}
and
\phi_{t}(x)\geq c_{0}d(x)^{t}
in
\Omega
. Notice that
(-\Delta)^{s}u\geq C=(-\Delta)^{s}(C\phi_{s})
in
\Omega
, where
C=\min\limits_{\Omega}\{u^{-p}v^{-q}\}>0
. Then, by Silvestre’s strong maximum principle, we deduce
u(x)\geq C\phi_{s}(x)\geq cd(x)^{s}
in
\Omega
and similarly
v(x)\geq cd(x)^{t}
in
\Omega
, where
c>0
is a positive constant.

The following result is a direct consequence of Silvestre’s strong maximum principle, inequality (
6
) and Theorem 1.2 of
[
3
]
. This is the key tool for the existence, nonexistence and uniqueness results of solutions of the system (
1
).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
p\geq 0
and
\gamma>0
. There are constants
c,C>0
such that any positive classical subsolution
\underline{u}
and any positive classical supersolution
\overline{u}
of problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrll}(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&d(x)^{-\gamma}u^{-p}&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\\
u&=&0&{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{array}\right.,

(11)

satisfies:

(i)

\underline{u}(x)\leq Cd(x)^{s}
and
\overline{u}\geq cd(x)^{s}
in
\Omega
, if
\frac{\gamma}{s}+p<1
;

(ii)

\underline{u}(x)\leq Cd(x)^{s}\left(\ln\left(\frac{2}{\varphi_{s}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{1+p}}
and
\overline{u}\geq cd(x)^{s}\left(\ln\left(\frac{2}{\varphi_{s}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{1+p}}
in
\Omega
, if
\frac{\gamma}{s}+p=1
;

(iii)

\underline{u}(x)\leq Cd(x)^{\frac{2s-\gamma}{1+p}}
and
\overline{u}\geq cd(x)^{\frac{2s-\gamma}{1+p}}
in
\Omega
, if
\frac{\gamma}{s}+p>1
with
0<\gamma<2s
.

Finally, Theorem 1.2(iii) of
[
3
]
also guarantees that the problem (
11
) has no positive classical solution, if
\gamma\geq 2s
. Such claimed is important for the proof of nonexistence results of positive classical solutions of the system (
1
).

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Notice that the system (
1
) is invariant under the transform
(u,v,p,q,r,\theta,s)\rightarrow(v,u,\theta,r,q,p,t)
, so that, we need to prove only the cases
(i), (ii)
and
(v)
.

Suppose that there exists
(u,v)
a positive classical solution of system (
1
). By Proposition
2.2
, we can find
c>0
such that (
10
) holds.

(i)

\frac{qt}{s}+p<1
and
r\geq\frac{2t}{s}
. Using the estimate (
10
) in the first equation of the system (
1
) we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrll}(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&v^{-q}u^{-p}\leq c_{1}d(x)^{-qt}u^{-p},\ \ \ u>0&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\\
u&=&0&{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{array}\right.,

(12)

for some
c_{1}>0
. By Proposition
2.3
(i) we conclude
u(x)\leq c_{2}d(x)^{s}
in
\Omega
, for some
c_{2}>0
. From this and (
10
), we have there exists
c_{0},c_{3}>0
such that
c_{0}d(x)^{-rs}\leq u^{-r}\leq c_{3}d(x)^{-rs}
in
\Omega
. Using the second equation of (
1
) we find

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrll}(-\Delta)^{t}v&=&u^{-r}v^{-\theta},\ \ \ v>0&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\\
v&=&0&{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{array}\right..

(13)

According to Theorem 1.2(iii) of
[
3
]
, this is impossible, since
rs\geq 2t
.

(ii)

\frac{qt}{s}+p>1
and
r(2s-qt)\geq 2t(1+p)
. In the same manner as above,
u
satisfies the problem (
12
). From Proposition
2.3
(iii), we now deduce

u(x)\leq c_{1}d(x)^{\frac{2s-qt}{1+p}}

in
\Omega
, for some
c_{1}>0
. Since
\frac{qt}{s}+p>1
, we have
\frac{2s-qt}{1+p}>s
. From this and (

) we deduce

u(x)\geq cd(x)^{\frac{2s-qt}{1+p}}.

Then, there are
c_{2},c_{3}>0
such that

c_{2}d(x)^{-\frac{r(2s-qt)}{1+p}}\leq u^{-r}\leq c_{3}d(x)^{-\frac{r(2s-qt)}{1+p}}\text{ in }\Omega.

Now, using the second equation of (
1
) we have
v
is a classical solution of problem (
13
), which is impossible in view of Theorem 1.2(iii) of
[
3
]
, since
\frac{r(2s-qt)}{1+p}\geq 2t
.

(v)
Let
M=\sup\limits_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}v
. From the first equation of the system (
1
) we find

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrll}(-\Delta)^{s}u&\geq&M^{-q}u^{-p},\ \ \ u>0&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\\

From Proposition
2.3
(iii), we have
u(x)\geq c_{1}d(x)^{\frac{2s}{1+p}}
in
\Omega
, for some

. Combining this estimate with the second equation of (

) we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrll}(-\Delta)^{t}v&\leq&c_{2}d(x)^{-\frac{2rs}{1+p}}v^{-\theta},\ \ \ v>0&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\\

where

. Since

, again by Proposition

(iii) we obtain that the function

. Since

) we deduce

) has no positive classical solutions. This completes the proof of Theorem

.

4
Proof of Theorem
1.2

(i)
The proof is made in six cases according to bounded behavior of singular elliptic problems of the type (
3
), as it was pointed out in Proposition
2.3
.

Case 1:

\frac{rs}{t}+\theta>1
and
\alpha=\frac{q(2t-rs)}{s(1+\theta)}+p<1
. From Proposition
2.3
(i) and (iii) there exist
0<c_{1}<1<c_{2}
such that:

\bullet
Any positive classical subsolution
\underline{u}
and any positive classical supersolution
\overline{u}
of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrll}(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&d(x)^{-\frac{q(2t-rs)}{(1+\theta)}}u^{-p}&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\\
u&=&0&{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\end{array}\right.,

(14)

satisfy

\overline{u}(x)\geq c_{1}d(x)^{s}\text{ and }\underline{u}(x)\leq c_{2}d(x)^{s}\text{ in }\Omega.\\

(15)

\bullet
Any positive classical subsolution
\underline{v}

Abstract
We report a measurement of decay-time dependent charge-parity ( CP ) asymmetries in B 0 → K S 0 ​ K S 0 ​ K S 0 B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S} decays. We use 387 × 10 6 387\times 10^{6} B ​ B ¯ B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{} pairs collected at the Υ ⁡ ( 4 ​ S ) \Upsilon(4S) resonance with the Belle II detector at the SuperKEKB asymmetric-energy electron-positron collider. We reconstruct 220 signal events and extract the CP -violating parameters S S and C C from a fit to the distribution of the decay-time difference between the two B B mesons. The resulting confidence region is consistent with previous measurements in B 0 → K S 0 ​ K S 0 ​ K S 0 B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S} and B 0 → ( c ​ c ¯ ) ​ K 0 B^{0}\rightarrow(c\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muc\mkern-0.1mu}\mkern 0.1mu)K^{0} decays, and with predictions based on the standard model.

Introduction

In the standard model (SM), the charmless three-body decay
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
is mediated by the
b\rightarrow sq\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muq\mkern-0.1mu}\mkern 0.1mu
quark transition, which is dominated by a one-loop process, the so-called penguin amplitude.
Charge-conjugate decays are implied hereafter unless specified otherwise.
Penguin amplitudes are suppressed in the SM, e.g,
{\mathcal{B}}(B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{S}K^{0}_{S}K^{0}_{S})=(6.0\pm 0.5)\times 10^{-6}

[
1

[
2

CP

and

decay rates. These asymmetries arise from interference between amplitudes for direct decay and decay following flavor oscillations, due to the irreducible phase in the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) quark-mixing matrix
[
3

B^{0}\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
pairs are a primary goal of experiments in electron-positron collisions at the
\Upsilon(4S)
resonance. If one of the neutral

, decays into a
CP

at proper time

at proper time

with flavor

II
The Belle II detector and data sample

The Belle II experiment is located at SuperKEKB,
which collides electrons and positrons at and near the
\Upsilon(4S)

resonance
[
12
]
. The Belle II
detector
[
13
]
has a cylindrical geometry and includes a six-layer silicon detector (VXD) and a 56-layer central drift chamber (CDC). These detectors reconstruct trajectories of charged particles (tracks).
The VXD consists of two layers of silicon-pixel detectors (PXD) surrounded by four layers of double-sided silicon-strip detectors
[
14
]
.
Only the innermost PXD layer, and one sixth of the outermost layer are installed for the data analyzed here.
The symmetry axis of these detectors, defined as the
z
axis, is almost
coincident with the direction of the electron beam. Surrounding the
CDC, which also provides
dE/dx
energy-loss measurements, is a
time-of-propagation counter
[
15
]
in the
central region and an aerogel-based ring-imaging Cherenkov
counter in the forward region. These detectors provide
charged-particle identification. Surrounding them is an
electromagnetic calorimeter based on CsI(Tl) crystals that
primarily provides energy and timing measurements for photons and
electrons. Outside of the calorimeter is a superconducting solenoid
magnet. The magnet provides a 1.5 T magnetic field parallel to the
z
axis. Its flux return is instrumented with resistive-plate chambers
and plastic-scintillator modules to detect muons,
K^{0}_{L}
mesons, and
neutrons.

We use data collected at the
\Upsilon(4S)
resonance in 2019–2022, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of
(362\pm 2)\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
and containing
(387\pm 6)\times 10^{6}\ B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
pairs. We use simulated samples to train the multivariate classifiers and define fit models.
The
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Upsilon(4S)\rightarrow B\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muB\mkern-0.1mu}\mkern 0.1mu
sample is generated using
evtgen

[
16
]
and
pythia

[
17
]
. In the simulated signal sample, one of the
B
mesons decays to the
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
signal mode or the
B^{+}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{+}
control mode according to phase space. The simulated
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow q\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muq\mkern-0.1mu}\mkern 0.1mu
sample, where
q
indicates an
u
,
d
,
s
, or
c
quark, is generated using the
kkmc

[
18
]
generator interfaced with
pythia
. We also use
evtgen
to simulate the decay of short-lived particles.
The detector response is simulated by
geant4

[
19
]
.
Experimental and simulated data are analyzed with the Belle II software
[
20
,
21
]
.

III
Event reconstruction

The
\Upsilon(4S)
is produced at the
e^{+}e^{-}
collision point with a Lorentz boost (
{\beta\gamma}
) of 0.288
and subsequently decays to a
B
and a
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muB\mkern-0.1mu}\mkern 0.1mu
meson, which are both nearly at rest in the
e^{+}e^{-}
center-of-mass (c.m.) frame.
Therefore, the
B
-meson pairs propagate nearly along the boost direction with known velocity in the laboratory. This allows one to approximate the difference between their decay times as
\Delta t=(z_{{\it CP}}-z_{\rm tag})/\beta\gamma c
, where
z_{{\it CP}({\rm tag})}
is the decay position of
B_{{\it CP}({\rm tag})}
projected onto the boost axis.

Events are selected online based on the number of charged particles and total energy deposited in the calorimeter with nearly 100% efficiency.
Pairs of oppositely-charged particles are used to reconstruct
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
candidates.
The four-momentum and decay vertex of the
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
candidate are obtained from a kinematic fit of the
\pi^{+}
and
\pi^{-}
tracks. To reduce combinatorial background from incorrectly reconstructed
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
candidates, we use a boosted-decision-tree (BDT) classifier
{O}_{K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
with 15 input variables that include the
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
flight length, the impact parameters of the
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
candidate and the
\pi^{\pm}
,
and the number of measurement points (hits) in the VXD associated with the
\pi^{\pm}
.
The most discriminating variables are the angle between the
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
momentum and the displacement of the
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
decay vertex from the beam interaction point (IP) and the
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
flight length normalized by its uncertainty.
We select
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
candidates with invariant mass
M(\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
between 462.6
{\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and 532.6
{\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
, corresponding to about 35 units of the relevant resolution, and with an
{O}_{K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
requirement that accepts 91% of
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
mesons. The mass window is wide since the BDT efficiently suppresses the background. These criteria are optimized as described later.

We reconstruct
B_{{\it CP}}
candidates by combining three
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
candidates and treat the particles not belonging to
B_{{\it CP}}
as
B_{\rm tag}
decay products.
We select
B_{{\it CP}}
candidates using the invariant mass
M(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S})
and the beam energy constrained mass
M_{\rm bc}\equiv\sqrt{E_{\rm beam}^{2}-|\vec{p}_{B}|^{2}c^{2}}/c^{2}
, where
E_{\rm beam}
and
\vec{p}_{B}
are the beam energy and the momentum of the
B
meson in the
e^{+}e^{-}
c.m. frame.
We retain
B_{{\it CP}}
candidates satisfying
5.2<M_{\rm bc}<5.29{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and
5.08<M(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S})<5.48{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
, but exclude those satisfying
5.265<M_{\rm bc}<5.29{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and
5.08<M(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S})<5.2{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
to avoid contamination by
B^{0(+)}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{*0(+)}
decays.

The dominant source of background is the
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow q\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muq\mkern-0.1mu}\mkern 0.1mu
continuum.
We suppress this background by using another BDT classifier,
O_{\rm CS}
, with the following input variables that exploit event topology:
the cosine of the angle between the thrust axes of
B_{{\it CP}}
and
B_{\rm tag}
in the
e^{+}e^{-}
c.m. frame; the magnitude of the
B_{\rm tag}
thrust; the sum of the transverse momenta of the particles in the event; the squared four-momentum difference between the beams and the detected particles in the c.m. frame; and the modified Fox-Wolfram moments
[
22
]
.
The
B
thrust axis is a unit vector
\hat{t}
that maximizes the thrust magnitude
T\equiv\left(\sum_{i}\left|\hat{t}\cdot\vec{p}_{i}\right|\right)/\left(\sum_{i}\left|\vec{p}_{i}\right|\right)
, where
\vec{p}_{i}
is the momentum of the
B
meson’s
i
-th decay-product in the c.m. frame. The BDT classifier
O_{\rm CS}
ranges from zero for background-like events to one for signal-like events. We use simulated events to train the classifier. A requirement of
O_{\rm CS}>0.1
results in 51% background rejection with a signal efficiency of 98%. We then calculate a transformation of the classifier,
O_{\rm CS}^{\prime}=\log\left[(O_{\rm CS}-0.1)/(1-O_{\rm CS})\right]
, which yields a classifier distribution more convenient to parametrize.
The selection criteria on
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
candidate mass and
{O}_{K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
are determined by maximizing
N_{\rm sig}/\sqrt{N_{\rm sig}+N_{\rm bkg}}
, where
N_{\rm sig}
and
N_{\rm bkg}
are the expected yields of
B_{{\it CP}}
signal and background events determined from simulation, respectively, meeting the following signal-enhancement conditions:
5.27<M_{\rm bc}<5.29{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
,
5.18<M(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S})<5.38{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
, and
O_{\rm CS}>0.5
.

In addition to the nonresonant decay amplitude, quasi-two-body decays
B^{0}\rightarrow X(\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S})K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
via intermediate resonances
X
due to
b\rightarrow s
and
b\rightarrow c
transitions contribute to
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
decays. We consider
b\rightarrow s
decays to be signal, but we veto
b\rightarrow c
contributions to measure the
CP
asymmetries for the
b\rightarrow s
transition.
We expect a significant
b\rightarrow c
contribution only from
B^{0}\rightarrow\chi_{c0}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
decays based on the rates of
B^{0}\rightarrow X(\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S})K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
decays where
X
indicates a
\overline{D}{}^{0},\chi_{c0},\chi_{c1},\chi_{c2},\eta_{c},J/\psi,
or
\psi(2S)
meson
[
1
]
. The
B^{0}\rightarrow\chi_{c0}(\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S})K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
branching fraction is around 5% of the signal branching fraction.
We reject signal
B_{{\it CP}}
candidates if the invariant mass of any combination of two
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
candidates is in the range
3.379<M(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S})<3.447{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
. This requirement rejects 90% of the background from
B^{0}\rightarrow\chi_{c0}(\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S})K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
decays and 7.2% of signal.

The control channel
B^{+}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{+}
is reconstructed from two
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
mesons and a track and is similar to the signal decay. We require the particle identification information for the track to be consistent with a
K^{+}
.
We use the control channel to constrain the parameters of
B
-vertex-resolution model for signal, as well as those of the shapes of the
B_{{\it CP}}
mass and
O_{\rm CS}^{\prime}
background distributions.
We do not veto
\chi_{c0}K^{+}
decays for the control channel because their kinematic distributions are the same as those of the
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{+}
final state.

IV
Measurement of
B
-meson flavor and decay-time difference

We use a category-based BDT algorithm to identify the
B_{\rm tag}
flavor
[
23
]
. The algorithm uses 13 BDTs, each geared toward discriminating a specific signature of
b\rightarrow c\rightarrow s
cascade decays using particle identification and kinematic variables of the
B_{\rm tag}
charged decay products.
The outputs from these BDTs are combined by the top-level BDT to return the flavor value
q_{f}
and tagging quality
r\equiv 1-2w
, where
w
is the probability for wrong flavor assignment. The probability density of Eq. (
1
) is modified to include the parameter
w
, and its difference between
B^{0}
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muB\mkern-0.1mu}\mkern 0.1mu^{0}
,
\Delta w
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{P}^{\rm TD}_{\rm sig}&(\Delta t,q_{f})=\frac{e^{-|\Delta t|/\tau_{B^{0}}}}{4\tau_{B^{0}}}\Big(\mathstrut^{\mathstrut}_{\mathstrut}1-q_{f}\Delta w\\
&+q_{f}(1-2w)[S\sin(\Delta m_{d}\Delta t)-C\cos(\Delta m_{d}\Delta t)]\Big).\end{split}

(2)

The events are classified into seven independent
r
intervals (bins).
For each bin,
w
and
\Delta w
are determined using flavor-specific
B
meson decays with large branching fractions
[
24
]
.
Since the signal purity varies as a function of
r
, using the distribution of
r
improves the statistical sensitivity to the
CP
asymmetries.

To measure
\Delta t
, we reconstruct the
B_{{\it CP}}
and
B_{\rm tag}
decay vertices
using information about the IP. The spatial distribution of the IP is described by a three-dimensional Gaussian whose parameters are regularly measured in a calibration based on
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
events. The IP size is typically 250
{\,\mu\rm m}
in the boost direction, 10
{\,\mu\rm m}
in the horizontal direction, and 0.3
{\,\mu\rm m}
in the vertical direction
[
25
]
.
The
B_{{\it CP}}
vertex position is reconstructed from the six final-state pions using a decay-chain vertex fit, which constrains the
B_{{\it CP}}
to originate from the IP (IP constraint)
[
26
]
.
Due to their long lifetime, a fraction of
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
mesons decay outside of the VXD volume resulting in poorly measured decay positions. This causes the
B_{{\it CP}}
vertex resolution to depend strongly on the number of
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
mesons with associated VXD hits.
In simulation, the fractions of signal decays in which zero, one, two, or three
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
mesons have VXD hits are 0.4%, 7.9%, 37.9%, or 53.8%, respectively. When only one
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
meson has VXD hits, the IP constraint significantly improves the
B_{{\it CP}}
vertex resolution, reducing the average vertex-position uncertainty in the boost direction from around 270
{\,\mu\rm m}
to 120
{\,\mu\rm m}
.
The average uncertainty with the IP constraint is 49
{\,\mu\rm m}
when two
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
mesons have VXD hits and 35
{\,\mu\rm m}
when all three have such hits.

We use the
B_{\rm tag}
tracks to reconstruct the
B_{\rm tag}
vertex, excluding those having no associated PXD hits. We also exclude pairs of oppositely-charged pions consistent with a
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
decay because they are likely to be produced away from the
B_{\rm tag}
vertex.
Similarly to the
B_{{\it CP}}
vertex, we constrain the
B_{\rm tag}
to originate from the IP to improve the vertex resolution and reconstruction efficiency
[
27
]
.
In order to reduce the contamination from tracks from secondary and tertiary displaced vertices, which would bias the determination of the
B_{{\it CP}}
vertex position, the fit is repeated by iteratively removing the tracks contributing the largest increase to the vertex-fit
\chi^{2}
until a satisfactory fit quality is achieved. A selection on fit quality and vertex-position uncertainty is applied to ensure the quality of the
\Delta t
measurement.

We divide the remaining
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
candidates into two classes based on the quality of the
\Delta t
information to maximize the sensitivity of the measurement of
S
and
C
.
For the time-differential (TD) analysis that determines both
S
and
C
, we require candidates that satisfy the following criteria: both tracks from one or more signal
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
are associated with at least one VXD hit, the decay-time-difference satisfies
-30<\Delta t<30{\rm\,ps}
, satisfactory vertex-fit quality, and small vertex-position uncertainty.
The
\Delta t
resolution is around 0.9
{\rm\,ps}
in the TD events.
The
\Delta t
information of the other events is not used. They are included in the time-integrated (TI) analysis, which contributes only to
C
.
The probability density in Eq. (
2
) is integrated over
\Delta t
for TI events, yielding

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\rm sig}^{\rm TI}(q_{f})=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-q_{f}\Delta w-q_{f}(1-2w)C\frac{1}{1+\Delta m_{d}^{2}\tau_{B^{0}}^{2}}\right).

(3)

For 1.1% of simulated signal events, multiple (typically two)
B_{{\it CP}}
candidates are reconstructed. We choose the candidate with the best vertex-fit quality for such events, which retains the correctly reconstructed
B_{{\it CP}}
candidates in 82% of these events. This requirement has negligible impact on the
\Delta t
distribution and the
CP
asymmetry results. The reconstruction efficiency including the
B_{\rm tag}
selection is 28.3% in simulation.
For the control channel, we reconstruct the
B^{+}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{+}
vertex without using the
K^{+}
track to emulate the
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
vertex fit. We discard
B^{+}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{+}
candidates that fail the TD criteria. The reconstruction efficiency for the control channel is 24.7%.

V
Determination of signal yield

We extract the yields for TD, TI, and control channel events from a three-dimensional likelihood fit to the unbinned distributions of
M_{\rm bc}
;
M(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K)
, where
K
indicates a
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
or
K^{+}
meson; and
O_{\rm CS}^{\prime}
.
The likelihood function includes two sample components, signal and background.
We determine the shape of the signal component from fits to distributions of simulated signal and control samples.
The
M_{\rm bc}
distribution is modeled with a Gaussian function for the signal TD and control samples and with a Crystal Ball shape
[
28
,
29
]
for the signal TI sample. The signal and control-sample
M(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K)
distribution is modeled with the sum of a Gaussian function and an asymmetric Breit-Wigner function. The signal and control-sample
O_{\rm CS}^{\prime}
distribution is modeled with the sum of a symmetric and an asymmetric Gaussian function.
For the background, the
M_{\rm bc}
distribution is modeled with an ARGUS function
[
30
]
, the
M(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K)
distribution with a linear function, and the
O_{\rm CS}^{\prime}
distribution with the sum of a symmetric and an asymmetric Gaussian function.
The endpoint of the ARGUS function is set to
E_{\rm beam}
, which is calibrated using other
B
decays.
The parameter sets for the
O_{\rm CS}^{\prime}
shapes are shared between TD and TI events.
We use the same parameter set for the
M_{\rm bc}
and
M(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K)
background shapes across the three samples as the
B^{+}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{+}
kinematic properties are similar to those of the signal decay, as confirmed in simulation. The fit simultaneously determines the yield of each sample and 14 background shape parameters
[
31
]
.

Figures
1
,
2
, and
3
show the data distributions with fit results overlaid. The low-mass tails of the
M(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K)
distribution of the signal TI component is mainly due to
\pi\rightarrow\mu\nu_{\mu}
decays, which occur in 3% of the reconstructed signal
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
events.
Such events are mostly classified as TI events due to the poor vertex fit quality.
We define the signal region as
5.272<M_{\rm bc}<5.288{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
,
5.2<M(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K)<5.36{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
, and
-4.44<O_{\rm CS}^{\prime}<8.85
.
Each range for
M_{\rm bc}
and
O_{\rm CS}^{\prime}
retains 99.73% of signal TD events.
The signal yield and the purity in the signal region is
158^{+14}_{-13}
and 57% for TD events,
62\pm 9
and 40% for TI events, and
403^{+24}_{-23}
and 22% for the control channel events.

VI
Determination of
CP
asymmetries

We determine the
CP
asymmetries
S
and
C
from a maximum-likelihood fit to the unbinned
\Delta t
and binned
q_{f}
distributions combining TD, TI, and
B^{+}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{+}
events restricted to the signal region.
The contribution to the likelihood function from the
i
-th TD event is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\rm TD}&(S,C|\Delta t_{i},q_{f,i})=\\
&f^{\rm sig}_{i}\int d(\Delta t^{\prime})R(\Delta t_{i}-\Delta t^{\prime})\mathcal{P}^{\rm TD}_{\rm sig}(\Delta t^{\prime},q_{f,i})\\
&+(1-f^{\rm sig}_{i})\mathcal{P}_{\rm bkg}(\Delta t_{i}),\end{split}

(4)

where
R(\Delta t_{i}-\Delta t^{\prime})
is the response function of the
\Delta t
measurement (resolution function),
f_{i}^{\rm sig}
is the signal probability of the
i
-th event, and
\mathcal{P}_{\rm bkg}
is the
\Delta t
distribution of background events.
We use a resolution function developed by the Belle collaboration
[
32
]
.
The resolution function is the convolution of four components:
detector resolution for the
B_{{\it CP}}
vertex,
detector resolution for the
B_{\rm tag}
vertex,
bias due to secondary particles from charmed intermediate states for the
B_{\rm tag}
vertex,
and corrections to the boost factor due to the nonzero c.m. momentum of the
B
mesons.
The correction to the boost factor is calculated analytically using the cosine of the angle between the
B_{{\it CP}}
momentum and the boost direction in the
e^{+}e^{-}
c.m. frame,
\cos{\theta_{B}^{*}}
, on an event-by-event basis. The resolution-function parameters are fixed to those obtained from a fit to simulated signal events, but the width in simulation is scaled by a parameter
s_{\rm det}
that accounts for data-simulation differences and that is determined simultaneously with
S
and
C
.
The distribution
\mathcal{P}_{\rm bkg}
is the sum of two Gaussian functions that depend on vertex quality and vertex-position uncertainty. The
\mathcal{P}_{\rm bkg}
parameters are determined by a fit to the
M_{\rm bc}
sideband data.
We calculate the signal probability on an event-by-event basis using the five-dimensional PDF of
M_{\rm bc}
,
M(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K)
,
O_{\rm CS}^{\prime}
,
r
, and
\cos{\theta_{B}^{*}}
. The PDF contains signal and background components, whose fractions are determined by the signal and background yields. No correlation is assumed between the variables.
The last two variables are included to avoid fit biases (0.03 for
S
and 0.02 for
C
) due to implicitly assuming equal distributions that differ across sample components
[
33
]
.
The
r
distribution for background is obtained from the
M_{\rm bc}<5.265{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
sideband.
For
\cos{\theta_{B}^{*}}
, we assume a uniform distribution for background and
\frac{3}{4}(1-\cos^{2}{\theta_{B}^{*}})
for signal.
For TI events, we use the likelihood in Eq. (
4
) integrated over
\Delta t
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\rm TI}_{i}(C|q_{f,i})=f^{\rm sig}_{i}\mathcal{P}^{\rm TI}_{\rm sig}(q_{f,i})+\frac{1-f^{\rm sig}_{i}}{2}.

(5)

We include the
B^{+}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{+}
decays in the fit using the likelihood in Eq. (
4
) summed over
q_{f}
and using the
B^{+}
lifetime instead of the
B^{0}
lifetime. The control channel helps to constrain
s_{\rm det}
since its signal yield is 2.5 times larger than the TD signal.
The resolution-function parameters and
s_{\rm det}
are the same as those of the
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
events except for the parameters that model the effect of secondary particles.
They differ since, compared to
B^{0}
mesons,
B^{+}
mesons yield fewer
D^{-}
mesons and more
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD\mkern-0.1mu}\mkern 0.1mu^{0}
mesons, which have shorter lifetimes.
We define the background
\Delta t
distribution for
B^{+}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{+}
with an independent parameter set from
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
and with an additional Gaussian function.

Figure
4
shows the background-subtracted
\Delta t
distributions using the
{}_{s}\mathcal{P}lot
technique
[
34
]
and their asymmetry with fit projections overlaid.
We obtain
S=-1.37_{-0.26}^{+0.32}
,
C=-0.07\pm 0.17
, and
s_{\rm det}=1.16\pm 0.15
. Linear correlation coefficients are
-0.02
between
S
and
C
,
-0.16
between
S
and
s_{\rm det}
, and
-0.07
between
C
and
s_{\rm det}
. However, simulation studies show that the above point estimates are not reliable. While the likelihood has no secondary maxima, the small sample size leads to biases and non-Gaussian uncertainties.
For more reliable results, we construct confidence intervals for the
CP
-violating parameters as described in Sec.
VIII
.

We consider various sources of systematic uncertainties, which are listed in Table

.
To evaluate the systematic uncertainties in
S
and
C
related to assumptions made on parameters of the fit model,
we repeat the fit on data using alternative values of the parameters sampled from Gaussian distributions based on their uncertainties. The widths of the resulting distributions of
S
and
C
are taken as contributions to the systematic uncertainty.
This approach is used for
\tau_{B^{0}}
,
\tau_{B^{+}}
, and
\Delta m_{d}
; the parameters of the
M_{\rm bc}
,
M(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K)
, and
O_{\rm CS}^{\prime}
shapes (referred to as signal modeling in the table); the parameters describing the resolution function; the parameters for the background
\Delta t
shape; and the parameters related to flavor tagging.

We sample the world averages of the
B^{0}
and
B^{+}
lifetimes and
\Delta m_{d}
including their uncertainties
[
1
]
. The parameters of signal probability, resolution function, and background
\Delta t
shape have uncertainties from the fits used to determine them, which depend on the size of data and simulated samples.
The systematic uncertainty in the resolution function includes the uncertainty due to the choice of the model, which is determined by analyzing a simulated sample with alternative resolution models whose dependence on the vertex-fit quality is partly or entirely removed. The simulation assumes
S=-0.7
and
C=0
.
The systematic uncertainty due to flavor tagging includes the bias due to the flavor asymmetry in the tagging efficiency between
B^{0}
and
\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
.
Two sets of simplified simulated experiments are generated, with and without the asymmetry, and fits for
S
and
C
are performed in both ignoring the asymmetry. The difference between the mean values of
S
and
C
obtained in the two sets is the uncertainty.
We repeat the simplified simulation assuming various input
CP
asymmetries and take the maximum difference.
We observe correlations between
M(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K)
and vertex-fit quality for
B_{{\it CP}}
(
-0.06
for TD events), and between
O_{\rm CS}^{\prime}
and
r
(0.15), which are not included in the default model.
To evaluate the bias due to these correlations, and to a mismodeling of the
\cos{\theta_{B}^{*}}
distribution, we use simplified simulated samples generated with and without these effects in the same way as above.
The
CP
asymmetries are affected by the interference between a CKM-favored transition
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mub\mkern-0.1mu}\mkern 0.1mu\rightarrow\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muc\mkern-0.1mu}\mkern 0.1muu\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mud\mkern-0.1mu}\mkern 0.1mu

b\rightarrow u\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muc\mkern-0.1mu}\mkern 0.1mud
on the tag side
[
35
]
.
We assign as a systematic uncertainty the effect of the tag-side interference assuming the world average values
S=-0.83
and
C=-0.15
[
11
]
.
The systematic uncertainty due to the vertex reconstruction is determined by varying the parameters describing the IP profile and boost vector, the track requirements used in the
B_{\rm tag}
vertex reconstruction, and the criteria to select TD events, and repeating the fit on data.
To evaluate the effect from possible misalignment of the vertex detector, we use four simulated samples, each assuming a different misalignment configuration and
CP
asymmetries of
S=-1.0
and
C=0
. We compare the resulting
CP
asymmetries with those in the sample without misalignment and the maximum deviation is taken as the systematic uncertainty.

VIII
Results and summary

Since the point estimates from the fit are not reliable, we construct confidence intervals for our results based on likelihood-ratio ordering
[
36
]
.
For the construction, simplified simulated experiments are generated by sampling the likelihoods of the yield fit and asymmetry fit. The nuisance parameters in the models are fixed to the values fitted to the data and the systematic uncertainty is not taken into account as its size is negligible.
Figure
5
shows the resulting two-dimensional confidence intervals where
S
and
C
are constrained within their physical boundary,
S^{2}+C^{2}\leq 1
.
The two-dimensional intervals are
-1<S<-0.72
and
-0.29<C<0.14
at the 68.3% confidence level,
-1<S<-0.41
and
-0.45<C<0.32
at the 95.5% confidence level, and
-1<S<-0.09
and
-0.61<C<0.49
at the 99.7% confidence level.
The results are consistent with the SM predictions and current best determinations by the Belle and BaBar experiments
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
.

.

In summary, we report a measurement of decay-time dependent
CP
asymmetries in
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
decays using a data set of
387\times 10^{6}

B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
pairs reconstructed from electron-positron collisions at the
\Upsilon(4S)
and collected with Belle II experiment from 2019 to 2022. We reconstruct 220 signal events and extract the
CP
-violating parameters from a fit to the distribution of the decay-time difference of the two
B
mesons. We determine a two-dimensional confidence region for the relevant parameters
S
and
C
obtaining results that are consistent with the SM predictions and previous determinations.

Quasi-analytic L p L^{p} -functions on Riemannian symmetric spaces of noncompact type, a theorem of Chernoff
Rudra P. Sarkar

Abstract
. A result of Chernoff gives sufficient condition for an L 2 L^{2} -function on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} to be quasi-analytic. This is a generalization of the classical Denjoy-Carleman theorem on ℝ \mathbb{R} and of the subsequent work on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} by Bochner and Taylor. In this note we endeavour to obtain an exact analogue of the result of Chernoff for L p , p ∈ [ 1 , 2 ] L^{p},p\in[1,2] functions on the Riemannian symmetric spaces of noncompact type. No restriction on the rank of the symmetric spaces and no condition on the symmetry of the functions is assumed.

1.
Introduction

The following result, of the genre of Denjoy-Carleman theorem, is proved in
[
7
]
.

Theorem 1.0.1
.

Let
f
be a
C^{\infty}
function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that, for all integers
m\geq 0
,
\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{m}f
is in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and that
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\|\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{m}f\|_{2}^{-1/2m}=\infty
, where
\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that all partial derivatives of
f
vanish at
0
. Then
f
is identically zero.

We call a set
\mathcal{S}
of
C^{\infty}
-functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
a
quasi-analytic
class if whenever a function
f\in\mathcal{S}
and all its derivatives vanish at a point
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
then
f\equiv 0
(
[
20
]
). In this terminology the result above gives a sufficient condition for an
L^{2}
-function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to be quasi-analytic. A brief description of the background of this result and an exposition on quasi-analyticity is given at the end of this section.
Very recently following two theorems (
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
) endeavored to generalize Theorem
1.0.1
for Riemannian symmetric spaces of noncompact type. A prototypical example of such spaces is the real hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. We recall that a Riemannian symmetric space of noncompact type can be realized as a quotient space
G/K
, where
G
is a connected noncompact semisimple Lie group with finite centre and
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. The group
G
and its subgroup
K
acts naturally (from left) on
G/K
. Let
\Delta
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
G/K
and
\mathbf{D}(G/K)
be the set of left
G
-invariant differential operators on
G/K
.

Theorem 1.0.2

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(G/K)
be such that
\Delta^{m}f\in L^{2}(G/K)
, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\|\Delta^{m}f\|_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}=\infty.

If
f
vanishes on any nonempty open set in
G/K
then
f
is identically zero.

The hypothesis of this theorem differs from that of Theorem
1.0.1
in the vanishing condition. Indeed, the condition implies in particular that
all derivatives
of
f
are zero at every point of an open set. An important subclass of functions on
G/K
are those which are invariant under left
K
-action. When
G/K=\mathbb{H}^{n}
, then these
K
-invariant functions are
radial
, i.e. the value of the function at a point depends only on the distance of the point from the origin of
G/K
.
In the next theorem the vanishing condition is only at a point like Theorem
1.0.1
, but this is achieved at the cost of restricting to the class of
K
-invariant functions on
G/K
, denoted by
C^{\infty}(G//K)
.

Theorem 1.0.3

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(G//K)
and
p\in[1,2]
. Suppose
\Delta^{m}f\in L^{p}(G/K)
, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\|\Delta^{m}f\|_{p}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}=\infty.

If there exists
x_{0}\in G/K
, such that
Df(x_{0})=0
for all
D\in\mathbf{D}(G/K)
then
f
is identically zero.

Purpose of this note to prove the following analogue of Theorem
1.0.1
for
G/K
, where
\Delta
and
\mathbf{D}(G/K)
are as defined above and
d_{r}(u)
is the right
G
-invariant differential operator defined by elements
u
of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
of
G
. (See Section 2 for details.)

Theorem 1.0.4
.

Let
p\in[1,2]
be fixed. Suppose that a function
f\in C^{\infty}(G/K)
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)
d_{r}(u)f\in L^{p}(G/K)
for all
u\in U(\mathfrak{g})
,

(ii)
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\Delta^{n}f\|_{p}^{-1/2n}=\infty
and

(iii) for a fixed point
x_{0}\in G/K
,
d_{r}(u)f(x_{0})=0
for all
u\in U(\mathfrak{g})
.

Then
f=0
.

It follows from the Taylor’s theorem that a nonzero real analytic function on
G/K
cannot satisfy condition (iii) above. Thus the Theorem
1.0.4
asserts that a function
f
on
G/K
which satisfies condition (i) and (ii) of the hypothesis is
quasi-analytic
in the sense that like a nonzero real analytic function, this function
f
also cannot satisfy (iii).

In the hypothesis of the theorem above, precisely those differential operators of
G
are used, which in particular preserves the right
K
-invariance of a function
f
on
G
, and hence are relevant for functions on
G/K
. It is worth pointing out that
\{d_{r}(u)\mid u\in U(\mathfrak{g})\}
includes
\mathbf{D}(G/K)
. On the other hand the theorem is not true if we restrict only to the differential operators in
\mathbf{D}(G/K)
. A counter example to establish this is given in
[
2
]
.
Thus we may consider Theorem
1.0.4
an exact analogue of Theorem
1.0.1
for
G/K
. (See Section 4 for a discussion on this.) If we assume that
f
is
K
-biinvariant then this theorem reduces to Theorem
1.0.3
, as expected (see Remark
3.0.4
).
For convenience we shall call conditions (ii) and (iii) in the hypothesis respectively as
Carleman-type condition
and the
vanishing condition
. Section 2 contains all preliminaries required for this paper. Theorem
1.0.4
is proved in Section 3.

1.1.
Background

If
f
is a real analytic function on an interval
(a,b)\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, then
f
and all of its derivatives cannot vanish at a point
x_{0}\in(a,b)
. On the other hand, by definition, as the Taylor series of an analytic function has to converges to the function, the formula for the remainder term shows that the derivatives of
f
cannot grow too fast. These observations intrigue one to find an appropriate growth condition on
C^{\infty}
-functions to define a space which accommodates functions which are not necessarily analytic, yet their nonzero members cannot vanish at a point along with all of its derivatives. For this last property which they share with real analytic functions, members of this space are called the
quasi-analytic
functions. The seminal result of Denjoy and Carleman (
[
10
,
6
]
) provides the precise formulation of this on
\mathbb{R}
. See
[
25
,
8
]
for a simple proof and exposition. Subsequently Bochner and Taylor
[
4
]
extended this result to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and other spaces, in which various special differential operators were constructed to replace
d/dx
used in the Denjoy-Carleman’s theorem. In all these results
L^{\infty}
-norms of the derivatives were considered to restrict the growth of the sequence of derivatives.
Some important developments in the intervening period are the study of analytic vectors of elliptic operators on Lie groups by Nelson
[
22
]
and subsequent work on quasi-analytic vectors by Nussbaum
[
23
,
24
]
in the set up of operator theory on Hilbert spaces, which was furthered by the work of de Jeu
[
9
]
. Our final point of departure from these classical results is the result of Chernoff (Theorem
1.0.1
), who obtained this as a part of his study of the relation between these operator theoretic developments on quasi-analytic vectors and quasi-analytic functions. Theorem
1.0.1
differs from the result of Bochner and Taylor in a number of ways. Most importantly, Carleman-type condition involves only (powers of) Laplacian and the
L^{2}
-norm replaces
L^{\infty}
-norm. Theorem
1.0.2
and
1.0.3
are the first two attempts to obtain versions of this result for Riemannian symmetric spaces. A recent paper
[
13
]
obtains a different version for
L^{2}
-functions on rank one symmetric spaces, where the vanishing condition is taken on every
k\in K
. Our aim here is to offer a generalization of Theorem
1.0.1
to the symmetric spaces of noncompact type of any rank and without any restriction of
K
-invariance on the functions.

2.
Preliminaries

The prerequisites of semisimple Lie groups and Riemannian symmetric spaces are standard and widely available, e.g. in
[
11
,
27
,
18
,
19
]
. To make this note self contained we shall gather them here without elaboration, and in the process will establish the notation, most of which are also standard.

A Riemannian symmetric space of noncompact type can be realized as a quotient space
G/K
, where
G
is a connected noncompact semisimple Lie group with finite centre and
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. The Group
G
and hence
K
acts naturally on
G/K
. Let us denote the origin
\{K\}
of
G/K
by
o
.

A function
f
on
G
is called right (respectively left)
K
-invariant if
f(gk)=f(g)
(respectively
f(kg)=f(g)
) for all
g\in G
and
k\in K
. If
f
is both left and right
K
-invariant then it is called a
K
-biinvariant function. We shall frequently identify right
K
-invariant functions on
G
with functions on
G/K
. Through this identification a left
K
-invariant function on
G/K
is a
K
-biinvariant function on
G
. The subset of the left
K
-invariant functions in a function space
\mathcal{F}(G/K)
will be denoted by
\mathcal{F}(G//K)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{k}_{0}
be the Lie algebras of
G
and
K
respectively and
\mathfrak{p}
be the orthogonal complement of
\mathfrak{k}_{0}
in
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
with respect to the Cartan-Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. Let
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
be a maximal abelian subspace of
\mathfrak{p}
, whose dimension (which is independent of its choice) is the real rank of
G
and the rank of the symmetric space
G/K
.
We shall assume that the rank of
G/K
is
l
and
\dim G/K
is
n
. Let
\Sigma
be the restricted root system of the pair
(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{a}_{0})
, and
W
the associated Weyl group. For a root
\alpha\in\Sigma
, let
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
be the associated root space and let
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
be
m_{\alpha}
, which is called the multiplicity of the root
\alpha
.
Let
(\mathfrak{a}_{0})_{+}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{0}
be a positive Weyl chamber and
\Sigma^{+}
the corresponding set of positive roots. Let
\mathfrak{n}_{0}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
. Then
N=\exp\mathfrak{n}_{0}
is a simply connected nilpotent Lie group and
A=\exp\mathfrak{a}_{0}
is an abelian group. Let
M
be the centralizer of
A
in
K
. We have the analytic diffeomorphism
(k,a,n)\mapsto kan
from
K\times A\times N
to
G
. The corresponding decomposition of
G
is called the Iwasawa decomposition:
G=K\exp\mathfrak{a}_{0}N
. Using this decomposition an element
g\in G
can be uniquely written as
g=k\exp(H(g))n
where
k\in K,n\in N
and
H(g)\in\mathfrak{a}_{0}
. The group
G
also has the Cartan decomposition
G=K(\exp\overline{(\mathfrak{a}_{0})_{+}})K
.
Let
\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{\ast}
be the (real) dual space of
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
and let
\mathfrak{a}^{\ast}
be its complexification. The half-sum of the positive roots counted with multiplicities is denoted by
\rho
and is given by
\rho=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}m_{\alpha}\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{\ast}
.

The Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
restricts to a positive definite form on
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
, which induces a positive inner product on
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
and hence on
\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{\ast}
. Equipped with these inner products we identify them with
\mathbb{R}^{l}
. The norm corresponding to this inner product on
\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{\ast}
is denoted by
|\cdot|
.
The Killing form endows
G/K
with both a natural
G
-invariant Riemannian metric, hence a Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
and a corresponding
G
-invariant measure (denoted by
dx
).
In the Cartan decomposition
x=k_{1}\exp Hk_{2}
, the Haar measure
dx
of
G
is given by

(2.0.1)

dx=c\,\delta(H)\,dk_{1}dHdk_{2}

for a constant
c
and

\delta(H)=\Pi_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}(\sinh\alpha(H))^{m_{\alpha}}\sim O(e^{2\rho(H)}),

where
dH
is the Lebesgue measure on
\overline{(\mathfrak{a}_{0})_{+}}
and
dk
is the normalized Haar measure on
K
.

Let
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{k}
be the complexifications of
\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{a}_{0},\mathfrak{k}_{0}
respectively. Then
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{k}
are Lie algebras over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
U(\mathfrak{g})
and
U(\mathfrak{a})
be the universal enveloping algebras of
G
and
A
respectively.
Let
\mathrm{Ad}
denote the adjoint representation of
G
on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. Through its restriction to
K
, the group
K
also acts on
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
by adjoint action, hence on
\mathfrak{g}
, and extends to a representation of
U(\mathfrak{g})
. Let

U(\mathfrak{g})^{K}=\{u\in U(\mathfrak{g})\mid\mathrm{Ad}(k)u=u\text{ for all }k\in K\}.

Every element of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, hence of
U(\mathfrak{g})
defines a left-invariant and a right-invariant differential operator denoted by
d_{l}
and
d_{r}
respectively which are defined as follows. For a function
f\in C^{\infty}(G)
and
u=X_{1}X_{2}\ldots X_{r},\,X_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
x\in G
, we define (
[
11
, p. 84]
)

(i)\ \ \ \ d_{l}(u)=f(x;u)=(\partial^{r}/\partial t_{1}\partial t_{2}\ldots\partial t_{r})_{0}f(x\exp t_{1}X_{1}\cdots\exp t_{r}X_{r}),

(ii)\ \ \ \ \ d_{r}(u)=f(u;x)=(\partial^{r}/\partial t_{1}\partial t_{2}\ldots\partial t_{r})_{0}f(\exp t_{1}X_{1}\cdots\exp t_{r}X_{r}x),

(the suffix
0
in the right hand sides denote that the derivatives are taken at
t_{1}=\cdots=t_{r}=0
) and then extend this definition to all
u\in U(\mathfrak{g})
.
The left-invariant differential operators
d_{l}(u)
defined by the elements of
U(\mathfrak{g})^{K}
, naturally descends to left-invariant differential operators on
G/K
. Let
\mathfrak{k}U(\mathfrak{g})
denotes the ideal generated by
\mathfrak{k}
in
U(\mathfrak{g})
. If
u\in\mathfrak{k}U(\mathfrak{g})\cap U(\mathfrak{g})^{K}
, then
d_{l}(u)
acts trivially on
C^{\infty}(G/K)
, hence the set of left invariant differential operators on
G/K
, denoted by
\mathbf{D}(G/K)
can be identified with
U(\mathfrak{g})^{K}/\mathfrak{k}U(\mathfrak{g})\cap U(\mathfrak{g})^{K}
. We recall that
\mathbf{D}(G/K)
is a commutative algebra which contains the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
and if the rank of
G/K
is one, then
\mathbf{D}(G/K)
is generated by
\Delta
. For an element
u\in U(\mathfrak{g})^{K}
, the projection of
d_{l}(u)
on
\mathbf{D}(G/K)
will be denoted by
D(u)
.
For
f\in C^{\infty}(G/K)
and
u\in U(\mathfrak{g})^{K}
, we have
d_{r}(u)f=d_{l}(u)f=D(u)f
(
[
11
, p. 52-53]
). It is easy to verify that for any
f\in C^{\infty}(G/K)
and
u\in U(\mathfrak{g})^{K}
,

(d_{r}(u)f)_{0}=d_{r}(u)f_{0}
, where
f_{0}
denotes the
K
-biinvariant component of
f
, precisely
f_{0}(x)=\int_{K}f(kx)dk
.

Since
\exp\mathrm{Ad}(g)X=g\exp Xg^{-1}
we have
f(g\exp tX)=f(\exp t(Ad(g)X)g)
and
f(\exp tXg)=f(g\exp t(Ad(g^{-1})X))
, for any
f\in C^{\infty}(G)
,
X\in\mathfrak{g}
and
g\in G
. This defines a transition between right and left invariant derivatives:
d_{r}(u)f(g)=d_{l}(\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1})uf(g)
. See e.g.
[
11
, p. 84]
for details.

Since
\mathfrak{a}
is abelian,
U(\mathfrak{a})
is viewed as the set of complex polynomials on
\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{\ast}
.
The Weyl group
W
acts on
\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{\ast}
, hence on the polynomial algebra
U(\mathfrak{a})
. The set of
W
-fixed polynomials in
U(\mathfrak{a})
is denoted by
U(\mathfrak{a})^{W}
.
There is a surjective isomorphism
\Gamma:\mathbf{D}(G/K)\to U(\mathfrak{a})^{W}
. For
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{\ast},k\in K
, the function
e_{\lambda,k}(x)=e^{(i\lambda+\rho)H(x^{-1}k)}
on
G/K
is a joint eigenfunction of elements of
\mathbf{D}(G/K)
:
De_{\lambda,k}=\Gamma(D)(i\lambda)e_{\lambda,k}
and in particular
\Gamma(\Delta)(i\lambda)=-(|\lambda|^{2}+|\rho|^{2})
.

Let
(\pi,H)
be a unitary representation of
G
. A vector
v\in H
is said to be a
C^{\infty}
-vector for
\pi
if
g\mapsto\pi(g)v
from
G
to
H
is a
C^{\infty}
-function. The set of
C^{\infty}
-vectors in
H
, denoted by
H_{\infty}
is dense in
H
.
For
v\in H_{\infty}
and
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
we define

\pi(X)v=\frac{d}{dt}\pi(\exp tX)v|_{t=0}.

For
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
,
X=X_{1}+iX_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}
and
v\in H_{\infty}
, we set
\pi(X)v=\pi(X_{1})v+i\pi(X_{2})v
. Then
\pi(X)H_{\infty}\subseteq H_{\infty}
and
\pi([X_{1},X_{2}])=[\pi(X_{1}),\pi(X_{2})]
. That is

is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
.
For
X_{1},X_{2}
and
X
as above, we define
\overline{X}=X_{1}-iX_{2}
.
For an element

, its adjoint
u^{\ast}\in U(\mathfrak{g})
is defined through the following rules:

1^{\ast}=1,X^{\ast}=-\overline{X}\text{ for }X\in\mathfrak{g}\text{ and for }u_{1},u_{2}\in U(\mathfrak{g}),(u_{1}u_{2})^{\ast}=u_{2}^{\ast}u_{1}^{\ast}.

Then
\langle\pi(u)v,w\rangle=\langle v,\pi(u^{\ast})w\rangle
for

.

A vector

is called

-finite for the representation
\pi
if
\{\pi(k)v\mid k\in K\}
spans a finite dimensional vector subspace of
H_{\infty}
. The set of all

-finite vectors in

is denoted by

.
For
X\in\mathfrak{g}
,
\pi(X)H_{F}\subset H_{F}
. It is known (
[

, 5.3-5.5]
) that if an irreducible representation
(\pi,H)
of

is also an irreducible representation of the Lie algebra

.

Henceforth, we shall restrict our attention to the unitary class-1 principal series representations
\pi=\pi_{\lambda},\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{\ast}
, realized in the compact picture, i.e. the carrier space is

and the action of

on

is given by (

(\pi_{\lambda}(x)f)(k)=e^{-(i\lambda+\rho)H(x^{-1}k)}f(K(x^{-1}k),\ \ f\in L^{2}(K/M),x\in G,k\in K.

For almost every
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{\ast}
with respect to the Plancherel measure (described below),

is irreducible.

Let
\widehat{K}
be the set of equivalence classes of irreducible unitary representations of
K
. For a representation
(\delta,V_{\delta})\in\widehat{K}
, let

V_{\delta}^{M}=\{v\in V_{\delta}\mid\delta(m)v=v\text{ for all }m\in M\}.

Let
\widehat{K}_{M}
denote the set of representations
(\delta,V_{\delta})\in\widehat{K}
for which the subspace
V_{\delta}^{M}\neq\{0\}
. Let
d(\delta)=\dim V_{\delta},\ell(\delta)=\dim V_{\delta}^{M}
. We fix an orthonormal basis

of

such that

span

and consider the following matrix coefficients of

Y_{\delta}^{j,i}=\langle v_{j},\delta(k)v_{i}\rangle,1\leq j\leq d(\delta),1\leq i\leq\ell(\delta).

Then the set

forms a countable orthonormal basis of

. Any

-finite function in

is a finite linear combination of such matrix coefficients. We enumerate this orthonormal basis as

where

is the only matrix coefficient of the trivial representation

of

, hence is the constant function

on

.
The matrix coefficients of the principal series representations

with respect to this orthonormal basis, which are relevant for functions on

\phi^{i}_{\lambda}(x)=\langle\pi_{\lambda}(x)e_{0},e_{i}\rangle
.
In particular,

is the elementary spherical function denoted by

.
The following properties of

and

will be used
(see

,

,

,

(i)

is a

-biinvariant function on

,

(ii)
\varphi_{\lambda}=\varphi_{w\lambda}
for

and

,

(iii)
\varphi_{\lambda}(x^{-1})=\varphi_{-\lambda}(x)=\overline{\varphi_{\lambda}(x)}
for

,

(iv)
\varphi_{\lambda}(u^{\ast};x)=\overline{\varphi_{\lambda}(x^{-1};u)}

,

, for all

(vi)

for the vanishing condition, we use all differential operators of
G
which preserves the right
K
-invariance of the function and thereby are precisely the set of differential operators relevant for functions on
G/K
. A specific question could be that if the differential operators induced by an element of
\mathfrak{so}(n)
is used in Theorem
1.0.1
. Indeed, a straightforward computation shows that for any
A=(A_{ij})\in\mathfrak{so}(n)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

f(A;x)=\frac{d}{dt}f(\exp tAx)|_{t=0}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}\,x_{j}\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}(x).

Thus the vanishing condition in Theorem
1.0.1
(which uses all partial derivatives of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) implies that for any differential operator
D
of
M(n)
,
Df(x_{0})=0
. This establishes the analogy.

2. If in Theorem
1.0.1
, we consider the vanishing condition only for the
M(n)
-invariant differential operators, instead of all partial derivatives, the condition reduces to
\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{m}f(x_{0})=0
for any
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
where
\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is the usual Euclidean Laplacian. The conclusion will then be that the radial average of
f
around the point
x_{0}
is zero. Analogously, if our vanishing condition involves only the left-invariant differential operators, i.e. for all
D\in\mathbf{D}(G/K)
,
Df(x_{0})=0
, then we can conclude that
(\ell_{g}f)_{0}=0
, where
x_{0}=gK
,
f_{0}(x)=\int_{K}f(kx)dk
and
\ell_{g}f(x)=f(gx)
. The precise statement is given below, which is Theorem
1.0.3
without the restriction of
K
-biinvariance on the function
f
.

Theorem 4.0.1
.

Fix a
p\in[1,2]
.
Let
f\in C^{\infty}(G/K)
be such that
\Delta^{m}f\in L^{p}(G/K)
for all integers
m\geq 0
and

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\|\Delta^{m}f\|_{p}^{-1/2m}=\infty.

If
Df(x_{0})=0
at some point
x_{0}=gK\in G/K
for all
D\in\mathbf{D}(G/K)
, then
(\ell_{g}f)_{0}=0
.

Here is a sketch of the proof. Since
D\in\mathbf{D}(G/K)
commutes with translations
f
can be replaced by
h=\ell_{g}f
, so that the vanishing condition in the hypothesis is at the origin
o
, instead of at
x_{0}
. That is
Dh(o)=0
for all
D\in\mathbf{D}(G/K)
. But
Dh(o)=0
implies that
Dh_{0}(o)=0
.
Other two conditions are also satisfied by
h_{0}
, because by Minkowski’s inequality,
\|h_{0}\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}
and
\Delta
commutes with translations. Note that
h_{0}
is
K
-biinvariant.
Thus by Theorem
1.0.3
,
h_{0}=(\ell_{g}f)_{0}=0
. ∎

A counter example is given in
[
2
]
to show that the hypothesis of this theorem does not imply that
f=0
.
Crux is that for any function
f
on
G/K
whose

K
-biinvariant component is
0
,
Df(o)=0
for all
D\in\mathbf{D}(G/K)
. In other words, for a function
f\in C^{\infty}(G/K)
and for any
D\in\mathbf{D}(G/K)
, the condition
Df(o)=0
is equivalent to
Df_{0}(o)=0
, and therefore such a vanishing condition imposes nothing on the function
f-f_{0}
.

∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} Sobolev-type inequality and an improved L 2 L^{2} -estimate of ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} on bounded strictly pseudoconvex domains
Fusheng Deng, Weiwen Jiang, Xiangsen Qin

Abstract
. We prove several Sobolev-type inequalities related to the ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} -operator on bounded domains in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} , which can be viewed as a ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} -version of the classical Sobolev inequality and its various generalizations, and apply them to derive a generalization of the Sobolev Inequality with Trace in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . As applications to complex analysis, we get an integral form of Maximum Modulus Principle for holomorphic functions, and an improvement of Hörmander’s L 2 L^{2} -estimate for ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} on bounded strictly pseudoconvex domains.



1.
Introduction

For any Lebesgue measurable function
f
on an open subset
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
1\leq p\leq\infty
, let
\|f\|_{L^{p}(D)}

denote its
L^{p}
-norm. Similar for
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\partial D)}
if
D
has Lipschitz boundary.

The following Poincaré Inequality is well known. Itself and its various modifications are of great interest in partial differential equations
and isoperimetry theory (see e.g.
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
12
]
).

Theorem 1.1

(
L^{2}
Poincaré Inequality)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with Lispchitz boundary, and let
R
be the diameter of
D
, then
there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(R)>0
such that

\delta\|f\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(D)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial D)},\ \forall f\in C^{1}(\overline{D}).

In the above theorem,
\nabla f
is the gradient of
f
,
C^{1}(\overline{D})
represents the space of complex valued functions which are of class
C^{1}
in an open neighborhood of the closure
\overline{D}
of
D
.

Theorem
1.1
has a more general version as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary, then

(i)

If
p,q,r
satisfy

1\leq p<n,\ 1\leq q<\frac{np}{n-p},\ \frac{(n-1)q}{n}<r<\infty,

then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(|D|,n,p,q,r)>0
such that

\delta\|f\|_{L^{q}(D)}\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(D)}+\|f\|_{L^{r}(\partial D)},\ \forall f\in C^{1}(\overline{D}),

where
|D|
is the Lebesgue measure of
D
.

(ii)

If
p,q
satisfy

2\leq n<p<\infty,\ 1\leq q\leq\infty,

then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(n,p,q)>0
such that for any compact supported
f\in C^{1}(D)
,we have

\delta\|f\|_{L^{q}(D)}\leq|D|^{\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(D)}.

Part (i) (which is called Sobolev Inequality with Trace) is a slight modification (using Hölder’s Inequality) of
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
(see also
[
3
, Formula (2.1)]
, and part (ii)
(which is called Morrey’s Inequality) is a slight modification of
[
7
, Theorem 7.10]
.
However, the case
p=n
is more subtle, see
[
7
, Theorem 7.15]
. We do not find a version of part (ii) of Theorem
1.2
for functions without compact support in the literature. In the present paper, we derive a generalized form of Theorem
1.2
(without giving the explicit form of the constant in the estimates) from analogous estimates for the
\bar{\partial}
-operator, see Corollary
1.5
.

From the viewpoint of complex analysis, it is very natural to ask whether similar estimates still hold for the
\bar{\partial}
-operator,
which is the main topic of the present paper. Our initial motivation to this work is to give an improvement of Hörmander’s
L^{2}
-estimate of
\bar{\partial}
on bounded strictly pseudoconvex domains.

The first main result is the following

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary, and let
1\leq p<\infty
, then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(\Omega,n,p)>0
such that

\delta\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)},

for all
f\in C^{1}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\bar{\partial}f
has a continuous continuation to
\overline{\Omega}
.

In the above theorem,
C^{1}(\Omega)
denotes the space of complex valued functions which are of class
C^{1}
on
\Omega
,
C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
represents the space of complex valued continuous functions on
\overline{\Omega}
, and we identify
\bar{\partial}f
with
\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}_{n}}\right)
.

For the case
p=2
, Theorem
1.3
is a
\bar{\partial}
-version of the Poincaré inequality presented
as in Theorem
1.1
. If
p=2
and
f
is real valued, Theorem
1.3
is a consequence of Theorem
1.1

since in this case
|\nabla f|=2|\bar{\partial}f|.
If
p=2
and
f
has compact support, Theorem
1.3
is also a consequence of Theorem
1.1

as

\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{j}}\right|^{2}d\lambda=\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}_{j}}\right|^{2}d\lambda,\ \forall\ 1\leq j\leq n.

When
\Omega
is strictly pseudoconvex with
C^{2}
-boundary and
p=2
, the result of Theorem
1.3
is already known (e.g. see the proof of
[
2
, Proposition 2.2.2]
). The general case is beyond the scope of Theorem
1.1
since one cannot control the norm of
\bar{\partial}f
in term of that of

\nabla f
, and beyond the discussion in
[
2
]
since the strict pseudoconvexity condition is indispensable for the arguments there.

We don’t know whether Theorem
1.3
still holds or not if
p=\infty
.

Parallel to Theorem
1.2
, we also consider a generalization of Theorem
1.3
as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
and let
p,q,r
satisfy

1\leq p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\infty,\ 1\leq r<\infty,\ q(2n-p)<2np,\ q(2n-1)<2nr,

then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(\Omega,p,q,r)>0
such that

\delta\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\|\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{r}(\partial\Omega)},

for all
f\in C^{1}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\bar{\partial}f
has a continuous continuation to
\overline{\Omega}.

Compared to part (i) of Theorem
1.2
, we don’t need to assume that
p<2n
in Theorem

1.4
.

The constant
\delta
in the above theorem can be written explicitly by tracking our proof, and all constants in the following theorems and corollaries can be chosen similarly.
For the sake of simplicity, we will not repeat this argument.

Remark 1.1
.

Since
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
is dense in the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
for any
1\leq p<\infty
,
see
[
6
, Theorem 3, Subsection 5.3, Page 252]
, and the trace operator
\tr\colon W^{1,r}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{r}(\partial\Omega)

is continuous, see
[
6
, Theorem 1, Subsection 5.5, Page 258]
, then Theorem
1.4
can be easily
generalized to functions in the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,r}(\Omega)
by tracking our proof.

Now we discuss the main idea of the proof of Theorem
1.4
. Although Theorem
1.4

looks similar to Theorem
1.2
in form, it seems difficult to modify the idea in the proofs of Theorem
1.1

and Theorem
1.2
in the literature to give a proof of Theorem
1.4
. The key ingredients in our arguments for Theorem
1.4

are Bochner-Martinelli Formula and Hölder’s Inequality for three functions.

Based on a principle that connects convex analysis and complex analysis (proposed in
[
4
]
), we derive the following result from Theorem
1.4
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary and let
p,q,r
satisfy

1\leq p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\infty,\ 1\leq r<\infty,\ q(2n-p)<2np,\ q(2n-1)<2nr,

then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(D,p,q,r)>0
such that

\delta\|f\|_{L^{q}(D)}\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(D)}+\|f\|_{L^{r}(\partial D)},\ \forall f\in C^{1}(\overline{D}).

We now discuss the relationships between Theorem
1.4
, Corollary
1.5
and Theorem
1.2
.
Clearly, for domains
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, Theorem
1.4
can be seen as a generalization of Theorem
1.2

(overlooking the estimates of the constants in the inequalities). Corollary
1.5
is stronger than Theorem
1.2

in the sense that
p
can be larger than
n
and
f
can be taken to have noncompact support,
and a little bit weaker than Theorem
1.2
in the sense that the condition in Corollary
1.5
may lead to a narrower range of
p,q,r
.

In connection to Remark
1.1
, Corollary
1.5
can be generalized to Sobolev spaces
accordingly.

For any domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
d\lambda
be the Lebesgue measure on
D
, and let
dS
be the surface measure on the boundary
\partial D
of
D
if
D
has
Lipschitz boundary. For application to
L^{2}
-estimate of
\bar{\partial}
on strictly pseudoconvex domains, we need certain weighted version of Theorem
1.4
as follows.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded domain with a
C^{2}
-boundary defining function
\rho
.
Set

K:=\frac{\sup_{z\in\Omega}|\nabla\rho(z)|+\sup_{z\in\Omega}|\Delta\rho(z)|}{\inf_{x\in\partial\Omega}|\nabla\rho(z)|},

where
\Delta\rho
is the Laplacian of
\rho
.
Assume
\varphi\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}),
and set

m_{1}:=\inf_{z\in\Omega}e^{-\varphi(x)},\ m_{2}:=\sup_{z\in\Omega}e^{-\varphi(z)},

then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(|\Omega|,|\partial\Omega|,K,m_{1},m_{2},n)>0
such that

\delta\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}e^{-\varphi}d\lambda\leq\int_{\Omega}|\bar{\partial}f|^{2}e^{-\varphi}d\lambda+\int_{\partial\Omega}|f|^{2}e^{-\varphi}d\lambda,\ \forall f\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}).

Theorem
1.6
is a direct consequence of Theorem
1.3
as the constant can be tracked, so we omit it here.

We now consider applications of Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.6
to complex analysis.
Firstly, a direct interesting consequence of Theorem
1.4
is the following Corollary
1.7
, which
can be seen as an integral form of the Maximum Modulus Principle for holomorphic functions.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary and let
p,q
satisfy

1\leq p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\infty,\ q(2n-1)<2np,

then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(\Omega,p,q)>0
such that

\delta\int_{\Omega}|f|^{q}d\lambda\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}|f|^{p}dS,\ \forall f\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}),

where
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
denotes the space of holomorphic functions on
\Omega
.

Secondly, we derive an improvement of Hörmander’s
L^{2}
-estimate of
\bar{\partial}
(see
[
2
, Theorem 1.6.4]
for an appropriate formulation) when
\Omega
is strictly pseudoconvex from Theorem
1.6
. Note that in Theorem 1.6.4 of
[
2
]
, the right hand side of Inequality (
5.1
) is

\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\psi^{j\bar{k}}f_{j}\bar{f}_{k}e^{-\varphi-\psi}d\lambda.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain with a
C^{2}
-boundary defining function

\rho,
and let
c_{0}
be the smallest eigenvalue of the complex Hessian
\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial z_{j}\partial\bar{z}_{k}}\right)
of
\rho
on
\partial\Omega
.
Let
\varphi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
be a plurisubharmonic function, and let
\psi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
be a strictly plurisubharmonic function.
Let

K:=\frac{\sup_{z\in\Omega}|\nabla\rho(z)|+\sup_{z\in\Omega}|\Delta\rho(z)|}{\inf_{z\in\partial\Omega}|\nabla\rho(z)|},

and set

m_{1}:=\inf_{z\in\Omega}e^{-\varphi(z)-\psi(z)},\ m_{2}:=\sup_{z\in\Omega}e^{-\varphi(z)-\psi(z)},\ m_{3}:=\inf_{z\in\partial\Omega}\frac{c_{0}(z)}{|\nabla\rho(z)|}.

Then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(|\Omega|,|\partial\Omega|,m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},n)>0
such that for any nonzero
\bar{\partial}
-closed Lebesgue measurable
(0,1)
-form
f:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}d\bar{z}_{j}

satisfying

M_{f}:=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\psi^{j\bar{k}}f_{j}\bar{f}_{k}e^{-\varphi-\psi}d\lambda<\infty,

we can solve
\bar{\partial}u=f
with the estimate

(1.1)

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}e^{-\varphi-\psi}d\lambda\leq\frac{\|f\|_{2}}{\sqrt{\|f\|_{2}^{2}+\delta M_{f}}}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\psi^{j\bar{k}}f_{j}\bar{f}_{k}e^{-\varphi-\psi}d\lambda,

where
(\psi^{j\bar{k}})_{1\leq j,k\leq n}
is the inverse of the complex Hessian of
\psi
,

\|f\|_{2}^{2}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}|f_{j}|^{2}e^{-\varphi-\psi}d\lambda.

The main idea of the proof of Theorem
1.8
can be summarized as follows.
Based on Theorem
1.6
, we apply the last two terms on the right hand side of the Kohn-Morrey-Hörmander formula (see
Lemma
5.3
), which is thrown away in the classical method to
L^{2}
-estimate of
\bar{\partial}
, to deduce a better
estimate when
\Omega
is strictly pseudoconvex.

Theorem
1.8
can be easily generalized to any
(p,q)
-forms and any bounded
q
-pseudoconvex domains. But we don’t pursue this here.

If we take
\psi(z)=\frac{|z-z_{0}|^{2}}{R^{2}}
for some
z_{0}\in\Omega
, where
R
is the diameter of
\Omega
, then Theorem
1.8
gives the following

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain with a
C^{2}
-boundary defining function

\rho,
and let
c_{0}
be the smallest eigenvalue of the complex Hessian
\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial z_{j}\partial\bar{z}_{k}}\right)
of
\rho
on
\partial\Omega
.
Let
R
be the diameter of
\Omega
,

K:=\frac{\sup_{z\in\Omega}|\nabla\rho(z)|+\sup_{z\in\Omega}|\Delta\rho(z)|}{\inf_{z\in\partial\Omega}|\nabla\rho(z)|},

and let
\varphi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
be a plurisubharmonic function. Set

m_{1}:=\inf_{z\in\Omega}e^{-\varphi(z)},\ m_{2}:=\sup_{z\in\Omega}e^{-\varphi(z)},\ m_{3}:=\inf_{z\in\partial\Omega}\frac{c_{0}(z)}{|\nabla\rho(z)|}

Then there exists a constant
\delta:=\delta(|\Omega|,|\partial\Omega|,K,m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},n)>0
such that for any nonzero
\bar{\partial}
-closed Lebesgue measurable
(0,1)
-form
f
satisfying

\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}e^{-\varphi}d\lambda<\infty,

we can solve
\bar{\partial}u=f
with the estimate

(1.2)

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}e^{-\varphi}d\lambda\leq\frac{eR^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\delta}}\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}e^{-\varphi}d\lambda.

Corollary
1.9
is an improvement of
[
10
, Theorem 2.2.3]

in the sense that the constant
eR^{2}
is improved to
\frac{eR^{2}}{\sqrt{1+\delta}}
in Inequality (
1.2
) if
\Omega
is strictly pseudoconvex and
\varphi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
.
The constant
\delta
can be also explicitly computed. One may wonder whether the constant
\delta_{0}
in Corollary
1.9
can be chosen to be independent of
\varphi
.
But this is impossible by the main result in
[
5
]
.

It is of great importance and interest to consider optimal constants in the estimates presented in all above theorems and corollaries,
but we do not discuss this topic in depth in the present work.
It is possible to generalize Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
to the Laplace operator and other differential operators on
more general spaces, which will be systematically studied in forthcoming works.

2.
Notations and Conventions

In this section, we fix some notations and conventions.

Our convention for
\mathbb{N}
is that
\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,3,\cdots\}
, we also set
\mathbb{C}^{*}:=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}.
For any complex number
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
let
\operatorname{Re}(z)
denote its real part and
\operatorname{Im}(z)
denote its imaginary part. For any
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we always write
z=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
.
Similarly, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we always write
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
. Let
\omega_{n}
be the surface area of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\ |x|=1\}
.

We say
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a domain if
D
is a connected open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\overline{D}
denote the closure of
D
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
and let
\partial D:=\overline{D}\setminus D
be its boundary. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, let
C^{k}(D)
be the space of complex valued functions on

D
which are of class
C^{k}
, and let
C^{k}(\overline{D})
denote the space of complex valued functions which are of class
C^{k}
in an open neighborhood of
\overline{D}
.
We also set

C^{k}(\overline{D};\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{F=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n})\colon\overline{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}|\ f_{j}\in C^{k}(\overline{D}),\ \forall j=1,\cdots,n\}.

For any
f\in C^{1}(\overline{D})
, let
\nabla f
denote its gradient. For any
f\in C^{2}(D)
, let
\Delta f
denote its Laplacian.

Let
d\lambda
denote the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
|A|
denote the measure of a Lebesgue measurable subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If a domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
has Lipschitz boundary, let
dS
denote the (
(n-1)
-)dimensional Hausdorff measure defined on
\partial D
, and we also let
|\partial D|
be the Hausdorff measure of
\partial D
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a domain, let
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
denote the space of holomorphic functions on
\Omega
.
We recall a real valued function
\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
is plurisubharmonic (resp. strictly plurisubharmonic) if its complex Hessian
\left(\varphi_{j\bar{k}(z)}\right)_{1\leq j,k\leq n}
is positive semi-definite (resp. positive definite) for any
z\in\Omega
, where

\varphi_{j\bar{k}}(z):=\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial z_{j}\partial\bar{z}_{k}}(z),\ 1\leq j,k\leq n,

and we let
\left(\varphi^{j\bar{k}}\right)_{1\leq j,k\leq n}
be the inverse matrix of its complex Hessian if
\varphi
is strictly plurisubharmonic.
We also recall that a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is strictly pseudoconvex if it has a
C^{2}
-boundary defining function
\rho

such that
(\rho_{j\bar{k}}(p))_{1\leq j,k\leq n}
is positive definite when it restricts to the hyperplane defined by

\{(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}|\ \sum_{j=1}^{n}t_{j}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial z_{j}}(p)=0\}

for any
p\in\partial\Omega
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
let
L^{p}(D)
denote the space of
L^{p}
-integrable complex valued functions on
D
, and for any
f\in L^{p}(D)
, let
\|f\|_{L^{p}(D)}
denote the
L^{p}
-norm of
f
.
Similarly, we obtain
L^{p}(\partial D)
and
\|f\|_{L^{p}(\partial D)}
if
D
has Lipschitz boundary. Moreover, we set

W^{1,p}(D):=\{f\in L^{p}(D)|\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\in L^{p}(D)\text{ for any }1\leq j\leq n\},

and let
\tr\colon W^{1,p}(D)\rightarrow L^{p}(\partial D)
be the trace operator if
D
is bounded.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a domain, and let
\varphi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
. We denote by
H_{1}:=L^{2}(\Omega,\varphi)
the Hilbert space of functions on
\Omega
which are square integrable with respect to
e^{-\varphi}d\lambda
, and
H_{2}
(resp.
H_{3}
) the Hilbert space of forms of type
(0,1)
(resp. type
(0,2)
) with coefficients in
H_{1}
. It is clear that

T_{\varphi}\colon H_{1}\rightarrow H_{2},\ u\mapsto\bar{\partial}u

and

S_{\varphi}\colon H_{2}\rightarrow H_{3},\ u\mapsto\bar{\partial}u

are unbounded densely defined, closed operators. Let
T_{\varphi}^{*}
(resp.
S_{\varphi}^{*}
) denote the formal adjoint of
T_{\varphi}
(resp.
S_{\varphi}
), and let
\dom(T_{\varphi}^{*})

(resp.
\dom(S_{\varphi})
) denote the domain of
T_{\varphi}^{*}
(resp.
S_{\varphi}
). For any element
\alpha\in H_{j}
,
\|\alpha\|_{j}
denotes the norm of
\alpha

in
H_{j}
. Similarly,
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{j}
denotes the corresponding inner product of
H_{j}
for any
\alpha,\ \beta\in H_{j}
.
We also let
C_{(0,1)}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
denote the space of forms of type
(0,1)
with coefficients in
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
.
For any
\alpha\in\dom(T_{\varphi}^{*})\cap\dom(S_{\varphi})
, the graph norm of
\alpha
is defined by

\left(\|\alpha\|_{2}^{2}+\|T_{\varphi}^{*}\alpha\|_{1}^{2}+\|S_{\varphi}\alpha\|_{3}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

3.
The proof of Theorem
1.4

To prove Theorem
1.4
, we make some preparations.

The following lemma is well known, we also contain a proof of it for completeness.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain, and let
0<a\leq n
, then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\int_{D}|x-y|^{-n+a}d\lambda(y)\leq\int_{B(x,R)}|x-y|^{-n+a}d\lambda(y)=\frac{\omega_{n}}{a}\left(\frac{n|D|}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{a}{n}},

where
B(x,R)
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with center at
x
and radius
R
whose volume is equal to
|D|.

Proof..

We have

\displaystyle\quad\int_{D}|x-y|^{-n+a}d\lambda(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{D-B(x,R)}|x-y|^{-n+a}d\lambda(y)+\int_{D\cap B(x,R)}|x-y|^{-n+a}d\lambda(y)

\displaystyle\leq R^{-n+a}|D-B(x,R)|+\int_{D\cap B(x,R)}|x-y|^{-n+a}d\lambda(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(x,R)-D}|x-y|^{-n+a}d\lambda(y)+\int_{D\cap B(x,R)}|x-y|^{-n+a}d\lambda(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{B(x,R)}|x-y|^{-n+a}d\lambda(y)=\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{R}r^{-n+a}r^{n-1}dr=\frac{R^{a}}{a}\omega_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{n}}{a}\left(\frac{n|D|}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{a}{n}}.

For any

(i) Since

\displaystyle b:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\max\left\{-\frac{2}{q},-1\right\}+\min\left\{\frac{p-2}{p},0\right\}\right),

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\frac{1}{p}&\text{ if }q\leq 2,\\
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}&\text{ if }q\geq 2,\ p\leq 2,\\
-\frac{1}{q}&\text{ if }p\geq 2,\end{array}\right.

0<2+qb\leq 2,\ 0<p-2-pb\leq 2(p-1).

Choose

where if

.

\displaystyle\quad|B_{\Omega}f(z)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|f(\xi)||\xi-z|^{-1}d\lambda(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{a}|\xi-z|^{b}|f(\xi)|^{1-a}|\xi-z|^{-1-b}d\lambda(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{p}|\xi-z|^{qb}d\lambda(\xi)\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\left[\int_{\Omega}|f(\eta)|^{p}d\lambda(\eta)\right]^{\frac{q-p}{qp}}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left[\int_{\Omega}|w-z|^{(-1-b)\frac{p}{p-1}}d\lambda(w)\right]^{\frac{p-1}{p}},

\displaystyle\quad|B_{\Omega}f(z)|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\int_{\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{p}|\xi-z|^{qb}d\lambda(\xi)\left[\int_{\Omega}|\eta-z|^{(-1-b)\frac{p}{p-1}}d\lambda(\eta)\right]^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}

\displaystyle\quad\|B_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq M(2+qb,\Omega)M\left(\frac{p-2-pb}{p-1},\Omega\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}

\displaystyle=M(2+qb,\Omega)M\left(\frac{p-2-pb}{p-1},\Omega\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q}.

By Lemma
3.1
and a simple computation, we know

\displaystyle\quad M(2+qb,\Omega)M\left(\frac{p-2-pb}{p-1},\Omega\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{2}}{2+qb}\left(\frac{(p-1)\omega_{2}}{p-2-pb}\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}\left(\frac{2n|\Omega|}{\omega_{2}}\right)^{1+\frac{q}{2}-\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{2}}{2+qb}\left(\frac{(p-1)\omega_{2}}{p-2-pb}+1\right)^{q}\left(\frac{2n|\Omega|}{\omega_{2}}\right)^{1+\frac{q}{2}-\frac{q}{p}}.

(ii) Similar to (i), we may assume
q\geq r>1
.

\displaystyle b:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\max\left\{-\frac{2}{q},-1\right\}-\frac{1}{r}\right),

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2r}&\text{ if }q\leq 2,\\
-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2r}&\text{ if }q\geq 2,\\
\end{array}\right.

then we have

0<2+qb\leq 2,\ 0<-1-rb\leq r-1.

a,q_{0},r_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\quad|B_{\partial\Omega}f(z)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}|f(\xi)||\xi-z|^{-1}dS(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{a}|\xi-z|^{b}|f(\xi)|^{1-a}|\xi-z|^{-1-b}dS(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{\partial\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{qa}|\xi-z|^{qb}dS(\xi)\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\left[\int_{\partial\Omega}|f(\eta)|^{q_{0}(1-a)}dS(\eta)\right]^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left[\int_{\partial\Omega}|w-z|^{(-1-b)\frac{r}{r-1}}dS(w)\right]^{\frac{r-1}{r}}

4.
The proof of Corollary

In this section, we give the proof of Corollary
1.5
. For convenience, we restate it here.

Corollary 4.1

(= Corollary
1.5
)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary and let
p,q,r
satisfy

1\leq p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\infty,\ 1\leq r<\infty,\ q(2n-p)<2np,\ q(2n-1)<2nr,

then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(D,p,q,r)>0
such that

\delta\|f\|_{L^{q}(D)}\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(D)}+\|f\|_{L^{r}(\partial D)},\ \forall f\in C^{1}(\overline{D}).

Proof..

Let
\Omega_{z}:=D_{x}+i\mathbb{R}_{y}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}_{z}^{n}
. Consider the map

\phi\colon\mathbb{C}_{z}^{n}\rightarrow(\mathbb{C}_{w}^{*})^{n},\ (z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\mapsto(e^{z_{1}},\cdots,e^{z_{n}}),

and let
\Omega^{\prime}:=\phi(\Omega)
, then
\Omega^{\prime}
is a bounded domain of
(\mathbb{C}_{w}^{*})^{n}
with Lipschitz boundary. For any Lebesgue measurable subset
U\subset(\mathbb{C}_{w}^{*})^{n}
, define

\mu(U):=\left|\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}|\ \phi(z)\in U,\ \operatorname{Im}(z_{1}),\operatorname{Im}(z_{2}),\cdots,\operatorname{Im}(z_{n})\in[0,2\pi)\}\right|,

then
\mu
is a measure on
(\mathbb{C}_{w}^{*})^{n}
(such measure is usually called the push-forward measure). Similarly, we may define the push-forward measure
d\sigma
on
\partial\Omega^{\prime}
of
dS
on
\partial\Omega
.
Let
d\lambda^{\prime}(w)
is the Lebesgue measure of
(\mathbb{C}_{w}^{*})^{n}
and let
dS^{\prime}(w)
be the Hausdorff measure of
\partial\Omega^{\prime}
, then
there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(\Omega^{\prime})>0
such that

\delta d\mu\leq d\lambda^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{\delta}d\mu,\ dS^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{\delta}d\sigma,\ \inf_{w\in\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}}\inf_{1\leq j\leq n}|w_{j}|>\frac{\delta}{2}.

We extend
f
to a
C^{1}
-function on
\mathbb{R}_{x}^{n}
, and we may regard
f
as a function on
\mathbb{C}_{z}^{n}
which is independent of the imaginary part of
z
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}_{z}
.
It is clear that
f
induces a
C^{1}
-function
f^{\prime}
on
(\mathbb{C}_{w}^{*})^{n}
via
\phi
, i.e.

f(e^{z_{1}},\cdots,e^{z_{n}})=f(\operatorname{Re}(z_{1}),\cdots,\operatorname{Re}(z_{n})),\ \forall\ (z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}_{z}^{n}.

By Theorem
1.4
, we know there is a constant
\delta^{\prime}:=\delta^{\prime}(\Omega^{\prime},p,q,r)>0
such that

\delta^{\prime}\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\|\bar{\partial}f^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{r}(\partial\Omega^{\prime})}.

It is obvious that

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|f^{\prime}|^{q}d\lambda^{\prime}\geq\delta\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|f^{\prime}(w)|^{q}d\mu(w)=(2\pi)^{n}\delta\int_{D}|f|^{q}d\lambda,

and

\int_{\partial\Omega^{\prime}}|f^{\prime}|^{r}dS^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\partial\Omega^{\prime}}|f^{\prime}|^{r}d\sigma\leq\frac{(2\pi)^{n}}{\delta}\int_{\partial D}|f|^{r}d\lambda.

For any
w\in(\mathbb{C}_{w}^{*})^{n}
, we know

\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial\bar{w}_{j}}(w)=\frac{1}{2\bar{w}_{j}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}(\ln|w_{1}|,\cdots,\ln|w_{n}|),\ \forall 1\leq j\leq n,

so we get

|\bar{\partial}f^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{\delta}|\nabla f|.

Similar to the above, we have

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|\bar{\partial}f^{\prime}|^{p}d\lambda^{\prime}\leq\frac{(2\pi)^{n}}{\delta^{p+1}}\int_{D}|\nabla f|^{p}d\lambda.

Combing the above inequalities, we get what we wanted.
∎

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{3}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}e^{-\varphi-\psi}dS

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\alpha_{j}\bar{\alpha}_{k}\psi_{j\bar{k}}e^{-\varphi-\psi}d\lambda+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial\alpha_{j}}{\partial\bar{z}_{k}}\right|^{2}e^{-\varphi-\psi}d\lambda

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}\alpha_{j}\bar{\alpha}_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial z_{j}\partial\bar{z}_{k}}e^{-\varphi-\psi}\frac{dS}{|\nabla\rho|}

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{\varphi+\psi}^{*}\alpha\|_{1}^{2}+\|S_{\varphi+\psi}\alpha\|_{3}^{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

Appendix

In this appendix, we give the proof of Lemma
3.5
for general
n
.

Let us recall the Bochner-Martinelli formula, which is a generalization of Cauchy’s Integral Formula.

Lemma 5.5

(see
[
9
, Theorem 1.9.1]
or
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary, then for any

f\in C^{1}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\bar{\partial}f:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}_{j}}d\bar{z}_{j}

has a continuous continuation to
\overline{\Omega}
and any
z\in\Omega
, we have

f(z)=\frac{(n-1)!}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\left(\int_{\partial\Omega}f(\xi)\omega(\xi,z)-\int_{\Omega}\bar{\partial}f(\xi)\wedge\omega(\xi,z)\right),

where

\omega(\xi,z):=\frac{1}{|\xi-z|^{2n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}(\bar{\xi}_{j}-\bar{z}_{j})\mathop{\wedge}_{k\neq j}d\bar{\xi}_{k}\wedge\mathop{\wedge}_{l=1}^{n}d\xi_{l}.

It is clear that

\left|\mathop{\wedge}_{k=1}^{n}d\bar{\xi}_{k}\wedge\mathop{\wedge}_{l=1}^{n}d\xi_{l}\right|=2^{n}d\lambda(\xi),\ \sup_{1\leq j\leq n}\left|\mathop{\wedge}_{k\neq j}d\bar{\xi}_{k}\wedge\mathop{\wedge}_{l=1}^{n}d\xi_{l}\right|\leq 2^{n}dS(\xi).

By Lemma
5.5
, we only need to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary, and let
p,q,r
satisfy

1\leq p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\infty,\ 1\leq r<\infty,\ q(2n-p)<2np,\ q(2n-1)<2nr,

then

(i)

There exists a constant
\delta:=\delta(n,p,q)>0
such that

\delta\|B_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2n}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\ \forall f\in L^{p}(\Omega),

where

B_{\Omega}f(z):=\int_{\Omega}\frac{(\bar{\xi}_{1}-\bar{z}_{1})f(\xi)}{|\xi-z|^{2n}}d\lambda(\xi),\ \forall z\in\Omega.

(ii)

There exists a constant
\delta:=\delta(|\Omega|,|\partial\Omega|,K,n,q,r)>0
such that

\delta\|B_{\partial\Omega}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{r}(\partial\Omega)},\ \forall f\in L^{r}(\partial\Omega),

where

B_{\partial\Omega}f(z):=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{(\bar{\xi}_{1}-\bar{z}_{1})f(\xi)}{|\xi-z|^{2n}}dS(\xi),\ \forall z\in\Omega,

and

K:=\sup_{z\in\Omega}|F(z)|+\sup_{z\in\Omega}|\nabla\cdot F(z)|.

Proof..

For any
a>0,
set

M(a,\Omega):=\sup_{z\in\overline{\Omega}}\int_{\Omega}|\xi-z|^{-2n+a}d\lambda(\xi),

N(a,\partial\Omega):=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\xi-z|^{-2n+1+a}dS(\xi),

then
M(a,\Omega),\ N(a,\partial\Omega)<\infty
for any
a>0.

(i) We may assume
q\geq p>1
. Set

\displaystyle b:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\max\left\{-\frac{2n}{q},1-2n\right\}+\min\left\{\frac{p-2n}{p},0\right\}\right),

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1-n-\frac{n}{p}&\text{ if }q\leq\frac{2n}{2n-1},\\
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{p}-\frac{n}{q}&\text{ if }q\geq\frac{2n}{2n-1},\ p\leq 2n,\\
-\frac{n}{q}&\text{ if }p\geq 2n,\end{array}\right.

then by assumption, we have

0<2n+qb\leq 2n,\ 0<p-2n-pb\leq 2n(p-1).

Choose
a,q_{0},r_{0}
such that

qa=p,\ q_{0}(1-a)=p,\ \frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q_{0}}+\frac{1}{r_{0}}=1,

where if
p=q
, then
q_{0}=\infty
.
Fix any
f\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
, then for any
z\in\Omega,
we have (use Hölder’s Inequality for three functions)

\displaystyle\quad|B_{\Omega}f(z)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|f(\xi)||\xi-z|^{-2n+1}d\lambda(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{a}|\xi-z|^{b}|f(\xi)|^{1-a}|\xi-z|^{-2n+1-b}d\lambda(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{p}|\xi-z|^{qb}d\lambda(\xi)\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\left[\int_{\Omega}|f(\eta)|^{p}d\lambda(\eta)\right]^{\frac{q-p}{qp}}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left[\int_{\Omega}|w-z|^{(-2n+1-b)\frac{p}{p-1}}d\lambda(w)\right]^{\frac{p-1}{p}},

which implies

\displaystyle\quad|B_{\Omega}f(z)|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\int_{\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{p}|\xi-z|^{qb}d\lambda(\xi)\left[\int_{\Omega}|\eta-z|^{(-2n+1-b)\frac{p}{p-1}}d\lambda(\eta)\right]^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq M\left(\frac{p-2n-pb}{p-1},\Omega\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\int_{\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{p}|\xi-z|^{qb}d\lambda(\xi),

so (by Fubini’s Theorem)

\displaystyle\quad\|B_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq M(2n+qb,\Omega)M\left(\frac{p-2n-pb}{p-1},\Omega\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}

\displaystyle=M(2n+qb,\Omega)M\left(\frac{p-2n-pb}{p-1},\Omega\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q}.

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\quad M(2n+qb,\Omega)M\left(\frac{p-2n-pb}{p-1},\Omega\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{2n}}{2n+qb}\left(\frac{(p-1)\omega_{2n}}{p-2n-pb}\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}\left(\frac{2n|\Omega|}{\omega_{2n}}\right)^{1+\frac{q}{2n}-\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{2n}}{2n+qb}\left(\frac{(p-1)\omega_{2n}}{p-2n-pb}+1\right)^{q}\left(\frac{2n|\Omega|}{\omega_{2n}}\right)^{1+\frac{q}{2n}-\frac{q}{p}}.

(ii) Similar to (i), we may assume
q\geq r>1
. Set

\displaystyle b:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\max\left\{-\frac{2n}{q},1-2n\right\}+\frac{1-2n}{r}\right),

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2r}\right)(1-2n)&\text{ if }q<\frac{2n}{2n-1},\\
-\frac{n}{q}+\frac{1-2n}{2r}&\text{ if }q\geq\frac{2n}{2n-1},\\
\end{array}\right.

then we have

0<2n+qb\leq 2n,\ 0<1-2n-rb\leq(2n-1)(r-1).

Choose
a,q_{0},r_{0}
such that

qa=r,\ q_{0}(1-a)=r,\ \frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q_{0}}+\frac{1}{r_{0}}=1,

where if
q=r
, then
q_{0}=\infty
.
Fix
f\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
, then for any
z\in\Omega,
we have

\displaystyle\quad|B_{\partial\Omega}f(z)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}|f(\xi)||\xi-z|^{-2n+1}dS(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{a}|\xi-z|^{b}|f(\xi)|^{1-a}|\xi-z|^{-2n+1-b}dS(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{\partial\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{qa}|\xi-z|^{qb}dS(\xi)\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\left[\int_{\partial\Omega}|f(\eta)|^{q_{0}(1-a)}dS(\eta)\right]^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left[\int_{\partial\Omega}|w-z|^{(-2n+1-b)\frac{r}{r-1}}dS(w)\right]^{\frac{r-1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\frac{1-2n-rb}{r-1},\partial\Omega\right)^{\frac{r-1}{r}}\left[\int_{\partial\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{r}|\xi-z|^{qb}dS(\xi)\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left[\int_{\partial\Omega}|f(\eta)|^{r}dS(\eta)\right]^{\frac{q-r}{qr}},

so we get

\displaystyle\quad|B_{\partial\Omega}f(z)|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\frac{1-2n-rb}{r-1},\partial\Omega\right)^{\frac{q(r-1)}{r}}\int_{\partial\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{r}|\xi-z|^{qb}dS(\xi)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left[\int_{\partial\Omega}|f(\eta)|^{r}dS(\eta)\right]^{\frac{q-r}{r}},

then

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\Omega}|B_{\partial\Omega}f(z)|^{q}d\lambda(z)

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\frac{1-2n-rb}{r-1},\partial\Omega\right)^{\frac{q(r-1)}{r}}\int_{(z,\xi)\in\Omega\times\partial\Omega}|\xi-z|^{qb}d\lambda(z)dS(\xi)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\int_{\partial\Omega}|f(w)|^{r}dS(w)\cdot\left[\int_{\partial\Omega}|f(\eta)|^{r}dS(\eta)\right]^{\frac{q-r}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq M(2n+qb,\Omega)|\partial\Omega|N\left(\frac{1-2n-rb}{r-1},\partial\Omega\right)^{\frac{q(r-1)}{r}}\|f\|_{L^{r}(\partial\Omega)}^{q}.

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\quad\frac{2n+qb}{\omega_{2n}}M(2n+qb,\Omega)N\left(\frac{1-2n-rb}{r-1},\partial\Omega\right)^{\frac{q(r-1)}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{|\Omega|}{\omega_{2n}}\right)^{\frac{q(r-1)}{r}}\left(\frac{r-1}{1-2n-rb}\right)^{\frac{q(r-1)}{r}}\left(2nK\omega_{2n}\right)^{\frac{q(r-1)}{r}}\left(\frac{2n|\Omega|}{\omega_{2n}}\right)^{1+\frac{q}{2nr}-\frac{q}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{|\Omega|}{\omega_{2n}}\right)^{q}\left(\frac{r-1}{1-2n-rb}+1\right)^{q}\left(2nK\omega_{2n}+1\right)^{q}\left(\frac{2n|\Omega|}{\omega_{2n}}\right)^{1+\frac{q}{2nr}-\frac{q}{r}}.

The proof is completed.
∎

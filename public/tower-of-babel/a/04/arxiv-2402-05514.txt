The Neumann condition for the superposition of fractional Laplacians
Serena Dipierro, Edoardo Proietti Lippi, Caterina Sportelli, Enrico Valdinoci

Abstract
. We present a new functional setting for Neumann conditions related to the superposition of (possibly infinitely many) fractional Laplace operators. We will introduce some bespoke functional framework and present minimization properties, existence and uniqueness results, asymptotic formulas, spectral analyses, rigidity results, integration by parts formulas, superpositions of fractional perimeters, as well as a study of the associated heat equation.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Superposition of fractional Laplacians and Neumann conditions

Nonlocal operators have significantly impacted contemporary research, playing a crucial role in both theoretical studies and practical applications across various fields of applied sciences. In this trend, the analysis of models arising from
the superposition of operators of different orders is also receiving increasing attention also
to improve our understanding of complex systems with long-range interactions acting
on different scales,
to allow for a more accurate and realistic representation of several physical phenomena
and to possibly increase computational power in numerical simulations.

In this paper, a novel functional framework for Neumann conditions is introduced, specifically tailored to address the superposition of fractional Laplace operators, potentially involving an infinite (even uncountable) number of such operators. The results obtained include, among the others, minimization properties, existence and uniqueness theorems, asymptotic behavior, spectral analyses, continuity results, integration by parts formulas and divergence theorems, as well as a study of the associated heat equation.

The mathematical setting that we can consider with our methods is very broad but, for the sake of concreteness, we focus on the following specific framework.

Let
\mu
be a nonnegative and nontrivial finite (Borel) measure over
(0,1)
and
let
\alpha\geqslant 0
. The operator that we address here deals with the possible coexistence of a Laplacian and a possibly continuous superposition of fractional Laplacians, namely

(1.1)

L_{\alpha,\mu}(u):=-\alpha\Delta u+\int_{(0,1)}(-\Delta)^{s}u\,d\mu(s).

As customary, the notation
(-\Delta)^{s}
stands for the fractional Laplacian, defined, for all
s\in(0,1)
, as

(1.2)

(-\Delta)^{s}\,u(x)=c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{2u(x)-u(x+y)-u(x-y)}{|y|^{N+2s}}\,dy.

The positive normalizing constant
c_{N,s}
is chosen in such a way to provide
consistent limits as
s\nearrow 1
and as
s\searrow 0
, namely

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(-\Delta)^{s}u=(-\Delta)^{1}u=-\Delta u\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\lim_{s\searrow 0}(-\Delta)^{s}u=(-\Delta)^{0}u=u.

The explicit value of
c_{N,s}
does not play a major role in our paper and is given by

c_{N,s}:=-\frac{2^{2s-1}\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{\pi^{N/2}\Gamma(-s)}

see e.g.
[
1
, Definition 1.1]
.

In the nonlocal context, at least three notions of fractional normal derivatives have been
considered. One of them is of “geometric” type, and deals with the incremental quotient in the normal direction related to the half of the order of the operator: this notion is typically
very useful for boundary regularity, Pohozaev-type identities, Hopf-type results, moving plane methods, symmetry results, etc. (see in particular
[
36
,
37
,
31
,
26
,
38
]
).
This notion gives rise
to overdetermined problems when combined to Dirichlet external data,
but when one allows suitable larger function spaces, the problem with this type of Neumann condition becomes well-posed, see
[
28
,
29
]
.

A second notion of fractional boundary derivative arises from spectral considerations,
in which, roughly speaking, one considers, in lieu of (
1.2
), a different
operator of fractional order obtained by eigenfunction expansion and multiplication by powers of eigenvalues (the Neumann condition being thus encoded into the choice of eigenfunctions, see
[
39
,
33
,
40
]
).
The structure of this spectral operator is however quite different from that
of the integral fractional Laplacian in (
1.2
), see in particular
[
2
, Section 2.3]

for similarities and differences.

Another notion of fractional normal derivative has been put forth in
[
24
]

and it is nicely compatible with energy methods and variational techniques; also
it has a natural probabilistic interpretation which makes it suitable for applications in mathematical biology (see
[
25
,
23
]
). This latter notion of fractional derivative
related to
(-\Delta)^{s}
takes the form

(1.3)

\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x):=c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy.

While the first notion of fractional derivative is strictly linked to the order of the fractional operator (or of the induced boundary regularity)
and the second to the spectral structure of the classical Laplacian, the latter setting happens to be conveniently flexible and can deal with the case of operators of mixed order as well. In fact, as we aim to show in this paper, it can be advantageously exploited in a very general framework comprising also
the superposition of possibly infinitely many fractional Laplacians (and possibly a continuum of operators). The setting provided here is actually prone to further generalizations, but we present
the results in their simplest possible form. For this, we define the Neumann conditions associated to the operator
L_{\alpha,\mu}
in (
1.1
) in the following way:

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
u
satisfies the
(\alpha,\mu)
-
Neumann conditions
if

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x)\,d\mu(s)=g(x)\,\,\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega},&\mbox{ if }\alpha=0,\\
\\
\partial_{\nu}u(x)=h(x)\,\,\mbox{ for all }x\in\partial\Omega,&\mbox{ if }\mu\equiv 0,\\
\\
\partial_{\nu}u(x)=h(x)\,\,\mbox{ for all }x\in\partial\Omega\mbox{ and }\\
\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x)\,d\mu(s)=g(x)\,\,\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega},&\mbox{ if }\alpha\neq 0\mbox{ and }\mu\not\equiv 0.\end{cases}

Moreover, when
g\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}

and
h\equiv 0
on
\partial\Omega
, we call the conditions in (
1.4
) as
homogeneous
(\alpha,\mu)
-Neumann conditions
.

It is worth noting that continuous superpositions of operators of different fractional orders have been recently considered in
[
20
,
19
,
21
]

in the more general case in which
\mu
is a signed measure. We refer the interested reader also to
[
35
]
for the case of combinations of fractional Laplacians with the “wrong sign”.
However, the setting of
[
20
,
19
,
21
]
is quite different from the one presented here, since it was tailored for problems subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions, while the main focus here is about conditions of Neumann type.

1.2.
Main results

As a first result, we
show that the functions minimizing the integral of the Gagliardo seminorms
(which will be defined precisely in (
2.1
)), namely

\int_{(0,1)}[u]^{2}_{s}\,d\mu(s),

are the ones satisfying the homogeneous Neumann condition

(1.5)

\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x)\,d\mu(s)=0

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
(and this is interesting, because
it relates the homogeneous Neumann condition directly to a variational problem).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
with
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. For all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
, we define

E_{u}(x):=\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(z)}{|x-z|^{N+2s}}\,dz\,d\mu(s).

We set

(1.6)

\tilde{u}(x):=\begin{cases}u(x)&\mbox{ if }x\in\Omega,\\
\\
\dfrac{E_{u}(x)}{E_{1}(x)}&\mbox{ if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega},\end{cases}

where
E_{1}
stands for
E_{u}
when
u\equiv 1
.

Then,

(1.7)

\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\,[\widetilde{u}]_{s}^{2}\,d\mu(s)\leqslant\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\,[u]^{2}_{s}\,d\mu(s).

We also give an existence and uniqueness result for solutions of

(1.8)

\begin{cases}L_{\alpha,\mu}(u)=f\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\mbox{with }(\alpha,\mu)\mbox{-Neumann conditions}.\end{cases}

In the forthcoming Section
2
we will introduce
the precise functional setting for this problem,
see in particular formulas (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) for the definition
of the functional spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
, respectively.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. Assume that

f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
and
h\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
. Suppose that there exists
\psi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\partial_{\nu}\psi=h
on
\partial\Omega
and

\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}\psi\,d\mu(s)=g\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.

Then, problem (
1.8
) admits a solution in

\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
if and only if

(1.9)

\int_{\Omega}f\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}g\,dx-\alpha\int_{\partial\Omega}h\,d\mathscr{H}_{x}^{N-1}.

Moreover, if (
1.9
) holds,
the solution is unique up to an additive constant.

We observe that the statement of Theorem
1.3
is in analogy with the case
\mu\equiv 0
(see
[
32
, page 294]
) and with the fractional case in which
\alpha=0
and
\mu
is a Dirac delta centered at some
s\in(0,1)
(see
[
24
, Theorem 3.9]
). The proof of Theorem
1.3
follows the same strategy of
[
24
, Theorem 3.9]
and therefore it is postponed to Appendix
A
.

The following result deals with the behavior at infinity of
functions satisfying the homogeneous Neumann condition
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Let
u\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)
be a bounded function such that

\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}u\,d\mu(s)=0\,\,\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.

Then

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u(x)\,dx.

We also study the eigenvalue problem associated with our superposition operator under Neumann conditions:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain.

Then, there exists a sequence of eigenvalues

0=\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leqslant\lambda_{3}\dots

with a corresponding sequence of eigenfunctions
u_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
with
i\in\{1,2,\dots\}
, which provide a complete orthogonal system in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Interestingly, the Neumann condition forces a global continuity property, as stated in the following result:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a domain with
C^{1}
boundary. Let

u
be continuous in
\overline{\Omega}
, with

(1.10)

\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}u\,d\mu(s)=0\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.

Then,
u
is continuous in the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

These main results will be complemented by other auxiliary results
which have their own interest, such as
continuous and compact embeddings (Proposition
2.3
),
divergence theorems and integration by parts formulas
(Lemmata
3.1

and
3.2
),
maximum principles (Lemma
4.3
),
Poincaré inequalities (Lemma
5.1
),
mass conservation, energy decresingness and asymptotics for the heat equation
(Propositions
6.1
,
6.2
and
6.3
),
asymptotic properties of a normalized Neumann condition (Proposition
7.2
),
superpositions of fractional perimeters, etc.

2.
Functional setting

In this section, we introduce the functional setting that we work in.
To maintain the discussion as simple as possible, we follow the approach in
[
24
]
;
a different approach with a more delicate choice of test spaces would require the finer analysis put forth in
[
27
]
.

We start by considering the case in which only nonlocal interactions are present,
that is
\alpha=0
.
To do this, we introduce the Gagliardo seminorm, that is

(2.1)

[u]_{s}:=\left({c_{N,s}}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{1/2},

where
\mathcal{Q}:=\mathbb{R}^{2N}\setminus(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)^{2}
.

Given
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
, we also define the norm

(2.2)

\|u\|_{\mu}:=\left(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\||g|^{1/2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{(0,1)}[u]_{s}^{2}\,d\mu(s)\right)^{1/2}

as well as the space
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)
of all measurable functions such that the norm in (
2.2
) is finite, namely

(2.3)

\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega):=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}\text{ measurable }:\|u\|_{\mu}<+\infty\right\}.

To deal with the more general case,
we consider functions
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
and
h\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
and define the norm

(2.4)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{\alpha,\mu}:=&\Bigg(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\||h|^{1/2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\||g|^{1/2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\alpha\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{(0,1)}[u]_{s}^{2}\,d\mu(s)\Bigg)^{1/2}\end{split}

and the space

(2.5)

\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega):=\begin{cases}H^{1}(\Omega)&\hbox{ if }\mu\equiv 0,\\
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)&\hbox{ if }\alpha=0,\\
H^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)&\hbox{ if }\mu\not\equiv 0\text{ and }\alpha\neq 0,\end{cases}

endowed with the norm in (
2.4
).

It is easy to check that the norms defined in (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) are induced by the scalar products

(2.6)

(u,v)_{\mu}:=\int_{\Omega}uv\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}|g|uv\,dx+\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)

and

(2.7)

\displaystyle(u,v)_{\alpha,\mu}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}uv\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}|h|uv\,d\mathscr{H}_{x}^{N-1}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}|g|uv\,dx+\alpha\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s),

respectively.

Proposition 2.1
.

(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega),\|\cdot\|_{\alpha,\mu})
is a Hilbert space.

Proof.

We observe that

(2.8)

(\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega),\|\cdot\|_{\mu})\mbox{ is a Hilbert space.}

(see Appendix
B
).
Hence, the completeness of
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
follows from its
definition in (
2.5
) and
2.8
.

Accordingly, observing that (
2.7
) is a bilinear form and
\|u\|_{\alpha,\mu}=(u,u)_{\alpha,\mu}^{1/2}
, it only remains to show that if
\|u\|_{\alpha,\mu}=0
, then
u=0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(or, if
\mu\equiv 0
, in
\Omega
).

To this aim, we observe that, if
\|u\|_{\alpha,\mu}=0
, then
\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0
, and thus
u=0
a.e. in
\Omega
. Hence, if
\mu\equiv 0
, the proof is concluded. Otherwise, we also have

\int_{(0,1)}\left(c_{N,s}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\right)\,d\mu(s)=0,

which gives

\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy=0\quad\mbox{ for any }s\in\mbox{supp}(\mu).

Hence
|u(x)-u(y)|=0
for any
(x,y)\in\mathcal{Q}
. In particular, for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
and
y\in\Omega
, we get

u(x)=u(x)-u(y)=0,

which means that
u=0
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\underline{s}\in(0,1)
. Let also
\underline{s}\leqslant s_{1}\leqslant s_{2}<1
and
\Omega

\mathbb{R}^{N}

u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function. Then,

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s_{1}}}\,dx\,dy\leqslant c\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s_{2}}}\,dx\,dy\right),

where
c=c(N,\underline{s})\geqslant 1
.

Explicitly, one can take

c(N,\underline{s}):=1+\frac{2\,\omega_{N-1}}{\underline{s}},

where
\omega_{N-1}
denotes the measure of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

We write

\begin{split}&\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s_{1}}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad=\iint_{(\Omega\times\Omega)\cap\{|x-y|<1\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s_{1}}}\,dx\,dy+\iint_{(\Omega\times\Omega)\cap\{|x-y|\geqslant 1\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s_{1}}}\,dx\,dy.\end{split}

Notice that, since
s_{1}\leqslant s_{2}
, we have

(2.11)

\begin{split}\iint_{(\Omega\times\Omega)\cap\{|x-y|<1\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s_{1}}}\,dx\,dy&\leqslant\iint_{(\Omega\times\Omega)\cap\{|x-y|<1\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s_{2}}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\leqslant\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s_{2}}}\,dx\,dy.\end{split}

Moreover, since
N+2s_{1}>N
and
s_{1}\geqslant\underline{s}>0
, we have

(2.12)

\begin{split}&\iint_{(\Omega\times\Omega)\cap\{|x-y|\geqslant 1\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s_{1}}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\leqslant 2\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}dx\int_{|z|\geqslant 1}\frac{dz}{|z|^{N+2s_{1}}}+\int_{\Omega}|u(y)|^{2}dy\int_{|z|\geqslant 1}\frac{dz}{|z|^{N+2s_{1}}}\right)\leqslant\frac{2\,\omega_{N-1}}{\underline{s}}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

Thus, combining (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), we have

\begin{split}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s_{1}}}\,dx\,dy&\leqslant\left(1+\frac{2\,\omega_{N-1}}{\underline{s}}\right)\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s_{2}}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\leqslant c\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s_{2}}}\,dx\,dy\right),\end{split}

as desired.
∎

Thanks to Lemma
2.2
, we are able to prove an embedding result for the space
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)
defined in (
2.3
).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, open set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with Lipschitz boundary.
Let
s_{\sharp}
be as in (
2.9
). Then, the space
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
H^{s_{\sharp}}(\Omega)
.

In particular,
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{q}(\Omega)
for any
q\in[1,2^{*}_{s_{\sharp}})
.

Proof.

We observe that

(2.13)

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)\leqslant\|u\|_{\mu}^{2},

thanks to (
2.2
).
Then, we use Lemma
2.2
with
s_{1}:=s_{\sharp}
and
s_{2}:=s\in[s_{\sharp},1)
. By (
2.9
), without loss of generality we can assume that there exists
\delta>0
such that
\mu([s_{\sharp},1-\delta])>0
.
We set

m:=\min_{s\in[s_{\sharp},1-\delta]}\left\{\frac{1}{\mu([s_{\sharp},1-\delta])},\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\right\}.

From this, we have

(2.14)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle\geqslant\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{[s_{\sharp},1-\delta]}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{[s_{\sharp},1-\delta]}\left(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}{\mu([s_{\sharp},1-\delta])}+\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\right)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle\geqslant m\int_{[s_{\sharp},1-\delta])}\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\right)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{m}{C(N,s_{\sharp})}\int_{[s_{\sharp},1-\delta]}\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s_{\sharp}}}\,dx\,dy\right)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle\geqslant C_{1}\|u\|_{H^{s_{\sharp}}(\Omega)}^{2},

for some
C_{1}=C_{1}(N,s_{\sharp},\delta)>0
.

Now, combining inequalities (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) we arrive at

\|u\|_{H^{s_{\sharp}}(\Omega)}^{2}\leqslant C\|u\|_{\mu}^{2},

up to renaming the constant
C
.

This proves that
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
H^{s_{\sharp}}(\Omega)
, as claimed. Finally, the compact embedding follows from
[
18
, Corollary 7.2]
and this concludes the proof.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
2.3
, we derive a suitable compact embedding result for the space
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
as well.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, open set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with Lipschitz boundary.
Assume that
\alpha\neq 0
. Then, the space
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
H^{1}(\Omega)
.

In particular, we have that
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{q}(\Omega)
for any
q\in[1,2^{*})
, where

2^{*}:=\frac{2N}{N-2}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\neq 0
. We observe that, by the definition of the norm
in (
2.4
), if
u\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
, then

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|u\|_{\alpha,\mu},

for a suitable constant
C>0
.
∎

3.
Integration by parts formulas and minimization property

In this section, we will prove some auxiliary formulas which will be useful in the forthcoming results.

To start, we give the analogue of the divergence theorem. We point out that the assumptions required in the next two results are significantly more general than the ones required in
[
24
, Lemma 3.2 and Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}

be such that
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
and

(3.1)

(-\Delta)^{s}u\in L^{1}(\Omega\times(0,1)).

Assume that the function

(3.2)

\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,1)\ni(x,y,s)\mapsto c_{N,s}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mbox{ belongs to }L^{1}(\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,1)).

Then,

(3.3)

\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)\,dx\,d\mu(s)=\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x)\,dx\,d\mu(s).

Proof.

We first point out that the assumptions (
3.1
)
and (
3.2
) give that the integrals in
(
3.3
) are finite.

Now, we observe that

\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy=0,

since the role of
x
and
y
is symmetric.

This implies that

\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)=0.

Hence, recalling the definitions in (
1.2
) and
(
1.3
), we can use (
3.2
) to
exchange the order of integration and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)\,dx\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dydx\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dydx\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=-\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\mathscr{N}_{s}u(y)\,dy\,d\mu(s),

as desired.
∎

We have the following integration by parts formula.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u,v:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that

u,v\in C^{2}(\Omega)
and

(3.4)

\mathcal{Q}\times(0,1)\ni(x,y,s)\mapsto c_{N,s}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mbox{ belongs to }L^{1}(\mathcal{Q}\times(0,1)).

Assume that

(3.5)

(-\Delta)^{s}uv\in L^{1}(\Omega\times(0,1))

and that the function

(3.6)

\begin{split}&(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times\Omega\times(0,1)\ni(x,y,s)\mapsto c_{N,s}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))v(x)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\\
&\mbox{belongs to }L^{1}((\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times\Omega\times(0,1)).\end{split}

Then,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}v(x)(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)\,dx\,d\mu(s)+\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}v(x)\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x)\,dx\,d\mu(s)

where
c_{N,s}
is the constant in (
1.2
).

Proof.

First, we observe that the assumptions (
3.4
),
(
3.5
) and (
3.6
) guarantee
that the integrals in (
3.7
) are finite.

We notice that

\mathcal{Q}=(\Omega\times\Omega)\cup(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\cup(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\times\Omega)
.
Then, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}v(x)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy-\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}v(y)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=2\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}v(x)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}

\displaystyle\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}v(x)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy-\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}v(y)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}v(x)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\int_{\Omega}v(x)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy,

and in a similar way

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\int_{\Omega}v(x)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\int_{\Omega}v(y)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\int_{\Omega}v(x)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}v(x)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

Combining all the previous identities, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}

\displaystyle\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v(x)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\int_{\Omega}v(x)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

Thus, by using (
1.2
) and (
1.3
),

\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}v(x)c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,dx\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle+\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}v(x)c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,dx\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}v(x)(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)\,dx\,d\mu(s)+\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}v(x)\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x)\,dx\,d\mu(s).

This concludes the proof.
∎

Now we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Without loss of generality, we can suppose that

\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)<+\infty,

otherwise we are done.

Now, we observe that

(3.8)

\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)-\widetilde{u}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)=\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s),

so we only need to consider the integral over

(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times\Omega
, or equivalently over

\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
.
Setting
\varphi(x):=u(x)-\widetilde{u}(x)
, for every

y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,d\mu(s)=\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-\widetilde{u}(y)-\varphi(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-\widetilde{u}(y)|^{2}-2\varphi(y)(u(x)-\widetilde{u}(y))+|\varphi(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,d\mu(s).

We notice that, for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
,

\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x)-\widetilde{u}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,d\mu(s)=E_{u}(y)-\frac{E_{u}(y)}{E_{1}(y)}E_{1}(y)=0.

Therefore we deduce from the identity in (
3.9
) that

\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)-\widetilde{u}(y)|^{2}+|\varphi(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)-\widetilde{u}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,d\mu(s)

for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
, and the
equality holds if and only if
\varphi(y)=0
.

Integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
(or, equivalently, over

\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
), and using Fubini’s theorem,
we conclude that

\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)\geqslant\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\widetilde{u}(x)-\widetilde{u}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s),

and the equality holds if and only if
\varphi\equiv 0
in

\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
. This and (
3.8
) imply
(
1.7
), as desired.
∎

4.
Weak solutions with (
\alpha,\mu
)-Neumann conditions

In this section we consider the problem

(4.1)

\begin{cases}L_{\alpha,\mu}(u)=f\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\mbox{with }(\alpha,\mu)\mbox{-Neumann conditions}\end{cases}

for some suitable functions
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
,
g:\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
and
h:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
.

We now provide the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

Assume that
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega})

and
h\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
. We say that
u\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of the problem (
4.1
)
if, for any
v\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
,

(4.2)

\begin{split}&\alpha\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx+\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}gv\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}hv\,d\mathscr{H}_{x}^{N-1}.\end{split}

We state the following preliminary result, with the proof provided in Appendix
C
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)

and
h\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
. Let
I:\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
be the functional defined as

\begin{split}I(u):=\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx&+\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{4}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)\\
&-\int_{\Omega}fu\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}gu\,dx-\int_{\partial\Omega}hu\,d\mathscr{H}_{x}^{N-1}.\end{split}

Then, critical points of
I
are weak solutions of the problem (
4.1
).

Next result is a sort of maximum principle and is helpful to prove the existence and uniqueness result in Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
and
h\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
be nonnegative functions. Let
u\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of (
4.1
).

Then,
u
is constant.

Proof.

The argument is inspired by that of Lemma 3.8 in
[
24
]
, but some care is needed to address the presence of the measure
\mu
.
To this end, observing that the constant function
v\equiv 1
belongs
to
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
, we can use it as a test function in
(
4.2
) to obtain that

0=\int_{\Omega}f\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}g\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}h\,d\mathscr{H}_{x}^{N-1}.

This implies that
f=0
a.e. in
\Omega
,
g=0
a.e. in

\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
and
h=0
a.e. in
\partial\Omega
.

Thus, taking
v:=u
in (
4.2
), we get

\alpha\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)=0,

hence
u
is constant in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, as desired.
∎

Now we complete the proof of Theorem
1.4

(though inspired by Proposition 3.13
in
[
24
]
, the complication arising from the measure does require here some bespoke modifications).

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We observe that, since
\Omega
is bounded, there exists
R>0

such that
\Omega\subset B_{R}
.
Thus, if
y\in\Omega
and
|x|>R
, we have

|x|-R\leqslant|x-y|\leqslant|x|+R.

Since
u
is bounded, we set
\tilde{u}(x):=u(x)+c
for some

c>0
such that
\tilde{u}\geqslant 0
. We notice that, for any

x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
,

\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}\tilde{u}(x)\,d\mu(s)=\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x)\,d\mu(s)=0.

Thus, recalling the definition in (
1.3
), for any

x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
we can write

\tilde{u}(x)=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{u}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}.

Therefore, if
|x|>R
, from the previous inequality we obtain that

\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{u}(y)}{(|x|+R)^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{(|x|-R)^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}\leqslant\tilde{u}(x)\leqslant\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{u}(y)}{(|x|-R)^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{(|x|+R)^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)},

which becomes

(4.3)

\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{(|x|+R)^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{(|x|-R)^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}\fint_{\Omega}\tilde{u}(y)\,dy\leqslant\tilde{u}(x)\leqslant\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{(|x|-R)^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{(|x|+R)^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}\fint_{\Omega}\tilde{u}(y)\,dy.

Now, we claim that

(4.4)

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}\left(\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{(|x|+R)^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{(|x|-R)^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}\right)=1.

To check this, first we observe that

(4.5)

\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{(|x|+R)^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{(|x|-R)^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}=1-\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\left(\frac{1}{(|x|-R)^{N+2s}}-\frac{1}{(|x|+R)^{N+2s}}\right)\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{(|x|-R)^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s)},

where we can write

\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{(|x|-R)^{N+2s}}-\frac{1}{(|x|+R)^{N+2s}}\right)\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{(|x|-R)^{N+2s}}\left[1-\left(1-\frac{2R}{|x|+R}\right)^{N+2s}\right]\,d\mu(s).

Setting

t:=\frac{2R}{|x|+R}\leqslant 1,

we have

1-(1-t)^{N+2s}=(N+2s)\int_{0}^{t}(1-\tau)^{N+2s-1}\,d\tau\leqslant(N+2)\int_{0}^{t}\,d\tau=(N+2)t,

and so

\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{(|x|-R)^{N+2s}}\left[1-\left(1-\frac{2R}{|x|+R}\right)^{N+2s}\right]\,d\mu(s)\leqslant\frac{2R(N+2)}{|x|+R}\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{(|x|-R)^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s).

Accordingly,

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}\left(\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\left(\frac{1}{(|x|-R)^{N+2s}}-\frac{1}{(|x|+R)^{N+2s}}\right)\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{(|x|-R)^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(s)}\right)\leqslant\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}\frac{2R(N+2)}{|x|+R}=0.

From this and (
4.5
) we obtain (
4.4
).

Thus, from (
4.4
), passing to the limit in (
4.3
), we see that

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}\tilde{u}(x)=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{u}(x)\,dx,

which implies

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u(x)\,dx,

as desired.
∎

5.
Eigenvalues and eigenfunctions

In this section we examine the spectral properties of the problem (
4.1
). To this aim, for any measurable function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, we set

(5.1)

\|u\|_{H_{\mu}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)

and we define the space

H_{\mu}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)<+\infty\right\}.

Arguing as in Proposition
2.8
, it is easy to check that
H_{\mu}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space as well. Moreover, it is worth noting that in the proof of Proposition
2.3
, we have also proved that
H_{\mu}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
H^{s_{\sharp}}(\Omega)
.

In our setting, the following version of the Poincaré inequality holds:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be any bounded Lipschitz domain.

Then, for any function
u\in H_{\mu}(\Omega)
, we have

\int_{\Omega}\left|u(x)-\fint_{\Omega}u(x)dx\right|^{2}dx\leqslant c(\Omega,\mu)\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\,dy\,d\mu(s),

for a suitable constant
c(\Omega,\mu)>0
.

Proof.

The argument presented is a sharpening of
Lemma 3.10 in
[
24
]
.
Assume by contradiction that the desired inequality does not hold. Then, there exists a sequence
u_{n}\in H_{\mu}(\Omega)
such that

(5.2)

\fint_{\Omega}u_{n}(x)\,dx=0,\quad\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1

and

(5.3)

\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)<\frac{1}{n}.

Hence, by (
5.1
), (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) we gather that
u_{n}
is bounded in
H_{\mu}(\Omega)
. Besides, by Proposition
2.3
, we infer that the embeddings

\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(\Omega)\subset H_{\mu}(\Omega)\subset H^{s_{\sharp}}(\Omega)

are continuous. Moreover, by
[
18
, Corollary 7.1]
, we have that
H^{s_{\sharp}}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Hence, up to subsequences,
u_{n}
converges to some
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, namely

u_{n}\to u\quad\mbox{ in }L^{2}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad u_{n}\to u\quad\mbox{ a.e. in }\Omega.

Furthermore, by (
5.2
),

(5.4)

\fint_{\Omega}u(x)\,dx=0,\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1.

Thus, recalling that
u_{n}
converges to
u
a.e. in
\Omega
as
n\to+\infty
, by (
5.3
) and Fatou’s Lemma, we get

\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)\leqslant\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)\leqslant 0.

Since
\mu
is nontrivial, we infer by the previous identity that
u
is constant in
\Omega
, in contradiction with (
5.4
).
∎

We now provide the definition of eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the operator
L_{\alpha,\mu}
in (
1.1
) with homogeneous
(\alpha,\mu)
-Neumann conditions in Definition
1.1
.

Definition 5.2
.

We say that
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is an eigenvalue if the problem

(5.5)

\begin{cases}L_{\alpha,\mu}(u)=\lambda u\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\mbox{with homogeneous }(\alpha,\mu)\mbox{-Neumann conditions}\end{cases}

admits a solution
u\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)

which is not identically zero.

If
\lambda
is an eigenvalue, we call the solution
u
an eigenfunction associated to the eigenvalue
\lambda
.

With this setting, we can prove Theorem
1.5

(in this regard, some care is needed to deal with the presence of the measure
\mu
and the possible coexistence with a local operator):

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We set

L_{0}^{2}(\Omega):=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}f(x)dx=0\right\}.

Moreover, let
T_{0}
be the operator defined as

(5.6)

T_{0}:f\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\longmapsto T_{0}f=u\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega),

where
u
denotes the unique weak solution to the problem (
4.1
), according to the Definition
4.1
. We point out that, by Theorem
1.3
and since
f\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique solution to problem (
4.1
), up to an additive constant. We can choose such a constant to be e.g.
\fint u(x)dx
. In this way,

\int_{\Omega}\left(u(x)-\fint_{\Omega}u(x)dx\right)dx=0

and then
u\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
.

Also, we define the operator
T
as the restriction

(5.7)

T:f\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\longmapsto T_{0}f\big|_{\Omega}\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega).

Let us show that
T
is a compact and self-adjoint operator.

We start proving that
T
is compact. To this, we test (
4.2
) with
v=u=T_{0}f
, getting

(5.8)

\begin{split}\alpha\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)&=\int_{\Omega}fu\,dx\\
&\leqslant\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

We notice that two cases can occur:

•

if
\alpha\neq 0
, by (
5.8
), we infer that

(5.9)

\begin{split}\alpha\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx&\leqslant\alpha\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)\\
&\leqslant\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

Also, the classical Poincaré inequality gives

(5.10)

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant c_{1}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2},

for a suitable constant
c_{1}>0
depending only on
\Omega
. Thus, combining (
5.9
) and (
5.10
), we have

(5.11)

\alpha\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\leqslant c_{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

for a suitable
c_{2}>0
;

•

if
\alpha=0
, by (
5.8
) it follows that

(5.12)

\begin{split}\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)&\leqslant\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)\\
&\leqslant\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

Now, recalling that
\fint u(x)dx=0
since
u\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
, by Lemma
5.1
we get

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant\left(c(\Omega,\mu)\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)\right)^{1/2}.

Thus, (
5.12
) becomes

(5.13)

\left(\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)\right)^{1/2}\leqslant c_{3}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

with
c_{3}>0
depending only on
\Omega
and
\mu
.

Now, let
f_{n}
be any bounded sequence in
L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
and let
u_{n}:=Tf_{n}
. On the one hand, if
\alpha\neq 0
, by (
5.11
) we obtain that
u_{n}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\Omega)
, which is compactly embedded in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Hence,
u_{n}
converges strongly to some
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, up to subsequences.

On the other hand, if
\alpha=0
, by (
5.13
) we infer that
u_{n}
is bounded in
H_{\mu}(\Omega)
. By
[
18
, Corollary 7.2]
, the space
H_{\mu}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and again
u_{n}
converges strongly, up to subsequences, to some
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Consequently, we have that
T
is a compact operator.

Let us show that
T
is self-adjoint. To this aim, we take
f_{1},f_{2}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfying

\fint_{\Omega}f_{1}(x)dx=\fint_{\Omega}f_{2}(x)dx=0.

Hence, by (
5.6
) and (
4.2
) (recall that
g\equiv h\equiv 0
), we have

(5.14)

\begin{split}\alpha\int_{\Omega}\nabla(T_{0}f_{1})(x)\cdot\nabla v(x)\,dx+&\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{(T_{0}f_{1}(x)-T_{0}f_{1}(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)\\
&=\int_{\Omega}f_{1}v\,dx\qquad\mbox{ for any }v\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)\end{split}

and

(5.15)

\begin{split}\alpha\int_{\Omega}\nabla(T_{0}f_{2})(x)\cdot\nabla w(x)\,dx+&\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{(T_{0}f_{2}(x)-T_{0}f_{2}(y))(w(x)-w(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)\\
&=\int_{\Omega}f_{2}w\,dx\qquad\mbox{ for any }w\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega).\end{split}

Now, recalling the definition (
5.7
) and testing (
5.14
) and (
5.15
) with
v:=T_{0}f_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
and
w:=T_{0}f_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\mu}(\Omega)
respectively, we obtain that

(5.16)

\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(x)\,Tf_{2}(x)dx=\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(x)\,Tf_{1}(x)dx\quad\mbox{ for any }f_{1},f_{2}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

We employ a density argument to prove that the identity (
5.16
) holds for any
f_{1},f_{2}\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
. For this, we notice that if
f_{1},f_{2}\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
, one can find two sequences
f_{1,n},f_{2,n}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
f_{1,n}\to f_{1}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
f_{2,n}\to f_{2}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, as
n\to+\infty
.

Thus, by (
5.16
) we deduce that

(5.17)

\int_{\Omega}f_{1,n}(x)\,Tf_{2,n}(x)dx=\int_{\Omega}f_{2,n}(x)\,Tf_{1,n}(x)dx.

Moreover, recalling that (
5.11
) and (
5.13
) hold with
u=T_{0}f
and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, either by (
5.7
), (
5.10
), (
5.11
) if
\alpha\neq 0
or by Lemma
5.1
and (
5.13
) if
\alpha=0
, we have

\|Tf_{1,n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant c_{4}\|f_{1,n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|Tf_{2,n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant c_{5}\|f_{2,n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

for some constants
c_{4},c_{5}>0
.

Thus,
Tf_{1,n}\to Tf_{1}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
Tf_{2,n}\to Tf_{2}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
n\to+\infty
and by (
5.17
) we have

\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(x)\,Tf_{2}(x)dx=\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(x)\,Tf_{1}(x)dx,

which means that
T
is a self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Then, by the spectral theorem, there exists a sequence of eigenvalues
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i\geqslant 2}
of
T
with corresponding eigenfunctions
\{e_{i}\}_{i\geqslant 2}
which are a complete orthogonal system of
L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
.

We claim that

(5.18)

\mu_{i}\neq 0\quad\mbox{ for any }i\in\{2,\dots,n\}.

If not, there exists
i\in\{2,\dots,n\}
such that
\mu_{i}=0
. Hence,

(5.19)

0=\mu_{i}e_{i}=Te_{i}=T_{0}e_{i}\quad\text{ in }\Omega

and also

\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}e_{i}(x)\,d\mu(s)=0

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
, by construction.

This, along with (
5.19
), imply that

T_{0}e_{i}(x)=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{T_{0}e_{i}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy\,d\mu(s)}=0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.

Hence, by the previous identity and (
5.19
) we infer that

0=-\alpha\Delta(T_{0}e_{i})+\int_{(0,1)}(-\Delta)^{s}(T_{0}e_{i})\,d\mu(s)=e_{i}\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega

in the weak sense, which gives
e_{i}\equiv 0
in
\Omega
. This contradicts the fact that
e_{i}
is an egenfunction and then (
5.18
) holds.

Now, in light (
5.18
), we set

\lambda_{i}:=\mu_{i}^{-1}\quad\text{ for any }i\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

Moreover, we also define
u_{i}:=T_{0}e_{i}
and we claim that
u_{2},u_{3},\dots
is the desired systems of eigenfunctions with corresponding eigenvalues
\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\dots
.

Indeed, by definition

u_{i}=T_{0}e_{i}=Te_{i}=\mu_{i}e_{i}\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

which means that the desired properties of orthogonality and completeness properties of
u_{2},u_{3},\dots
simply follows by those of
e_{2},e_{3},\dots
and the previous identity provides that

-\alpha\Delta(u_{i})+\int_{(0,1)}(-\Delta)^{s}(u_{i})\,d\mu(s)=-\alpha\Delta(T_{0}e_{i})+\int_{(0,1)}(-\Delta)^{s}(T_{0}e_{i})\,d\mu(s)=e_{i}=\lambda_{i}u_{i}\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

which means that also the spectral property holds. Let us prove that

(5.20)

\lambda_{i}>0\quad\mbox{ for any }i\geqslant 2.

To this end, we notice that the corresponding eigenfunctions
u_{1},u_{2},\dots
are solutions of the problem

\begin{cases}L_{\alpha,\mu}(u_{i})=\lambda_{i}u_{i}\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
\mbox{with homogeneous }(\alpha,\mu)\mbox{-Neumann conditions},\end{cases}

Then, taking
u_{i}
as a test function in the weak formulation of this problem, we find

\alpha\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)=\lambda_{i}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}(x)^{2}dx,

which entails that
\lambda_{i}\geqslant 0
.

Let us prove that
\lambda_{i}>0
. To check this, suppose by contradiction that
\lambda_{i}=0
. Then, Lemma
4.3
provides that
u_{i}
is constant. As we also know that that
u_{i}\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, it follows that
u_{i}\equiv 0
, against the fact that
u_{i}
is an eigenvalue. This proves (
5.20
).

From (
5.20
), up to reordering them, we can suppose that
0<\lambda_{2}\leqslant\lambda_{3}\leqslant\dots
and we
notice that
\lambda_{1}=0
is an eigenvalue, with eigenfunction
u_{1}=1
, again by Lemma
4.3
.
Therefore, we have a sequence of eigenvalues
0=\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leqslant\lambda_{3}\leqslant\dots
and its corresponding eigenfunctions are a complete orthogonal system in
L^{2}(\Omega)
(see the proof of
[
24
, Theorem 3.11 ]
).
This concludes the proof.
∎

6.
The heat equation

In this section we study the heat equation with homogeneous
Neumann conditions, namely

(6.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(x,t)+L_{\alpha,\mu}(u(x,t))=0&\mbox{in }\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
\mbox{with homogeneous }(\alpha,\mu)\mbox{-Neumann conditions}&\mbox{in }(0,+\infty).\end{cases}

Here, we deal with classical solutions,
that is
u(\cdot,t)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{2}(\Omega)
for any
t\in[0,+\infty)
and
u(x,\cdot)\in C^{1}((0,+\infty))\cap C([0,+\infty))
,
with the homogeneous
(\alpha,\mu)
-Neumann conditions satisfied
pointwise in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
and
\partial\Omega

respectively.

The next two results give that classical solutions of problem
(
6.1
) preserve their mass and have
energy that decreases in time.

We point out that the assumptions required throughout this section are significantly more general than the ones required in
[
24
, Propositions 4.1, 4.2 and 4.3]
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
u(x,t)
be a classical solution of
(
6.1
) satisfying

(6.2)

\partial_{t}u\in L^{\infty}_{loc}\left((0,+\infty),L^{1}(\Omega)\right)

and

(6.3)

\alpha\Delta u(\cdot,t)\in L^{1}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad(-\Delta)^{s}u(\cdot,t)\in L^{1}\left(\Omega\times(0,1)\right)\quad\mbox{ for any }t\in(0,+\infty).

Assume that the following statements hold:

•

if
\alpha\neq 0
, then

(6.4)

u(\cdot,t)\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})\quad\mbox{ for any }t\in(0,+\infty);

•

if
\mu\not\equiv 0
, then, for any
t\in(0,+\infty)
, the function

(6.5)

\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,1)\ni(x,y,s)\mapsto\frac{u(x,t)-u(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mbox{ belongs to }L^{1}\left(\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(0,1)\right).

Then,

\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)\,dx\quad\mbox{ for any }t\in(0,+\infty),

namely the total mass is conserved.

Proof.

The argument presented here is a careful refinement of Proposition 4.1 in
[
24
]
.
In light of (
6.2
) and (
6.3
),
from the dominated convergence theorem we have

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u(x,t)\,dx=\alpha\int_{\Omega}\Delta u(x,t)\,dx-\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)\,dx\,d\mu(s).

Moreover, by (
6.4
) and (
6.5
), and using the homogeneous Neumann conditions in Definition
1.1
,
we have that divergence theorem and Lemma
3.1
apply and

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\,dx=\alpha\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{\nu}u(x)\,d\mathscr{H}_{x}^{N-1}+\int_{(0,1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x,t)\,dx\,d\mu(s)=0.

This implies that the quantity
\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\,dx
does
not depend on
t
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
u(x,t)
be a classical solution of
(
6.1
) such that

(6.6)

\alpha\nabla u(\cdot,t)\nabla\partial_{t}u(\cdot,t)\in L^{1}(\Omega)

and suppose that
the function

(6.7)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{Q}\times(0,1)\ni(x,y,s)\mapsto c_{N,s}\frac{(u(x,t)-u(y,t))(\partial_{t}u(x,t)-\partial_{t}u(y,t))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\\
&\mbox{belongs to }L^{1}(\mathcal{Q}\times(0,1)),\end{split}

and

(6.8)

\alpha\partial_{t}u(\cdot,t)\Delta u(\cdot,t)\in L^{1}(\Omega),\quad\partial_{t}u(\cdot,t)(-\Delta)^{s}u(\cdot,t)\in L^{1}(\Omega\times(0,1)),

for any
t\in(0,+\infty)
.

In addition, assume that:

•

if
\alpha\neq 0
, then the assumption in (
6.4
) is satisfied,

•

if
\mu\not\equiv 0
, then, for any
t\in(0,+\infty)
, the function

(6.9)

\begin{split}&(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times\Omega\times(0,1)\ni(x,y,s)\mapsto c_{N,s}\frac{(u(x,t)-u(y,t))\partial_{t}u(x,t)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\\
&\mbox{ belongs to }L^{1}\left((\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times\Omega\times(0,1)\right).\end{split}

Then, the energy

E(t):=\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{4}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)

is decreasing in time
t>0
(and strictly decreasing, unless

is independent of time).

Proof.

This proof is inspired to Proposition 4.2 in

and
\mu\not\equiv 0

We compute

Indeed, recalling (

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\alpha\int_{\Omega}\nabla u(x,t)\nabla\partial_{t}u(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{(u(x,t)-u(y,t))(\partial_{t}u(x,t)-\partial_{t}u(y,t))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s).

In light of (

),
(
6.7
), (
6.8
) and (

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\nabla u(x,t)\nabla\partial_{t}u(x,t)\,dx+\int_{(0,1)}\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{(u(x,t)-u(y,t))(\partial_{t}u(x,t)-\partial_{t}u(y,t))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

First, we fix
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
and we
prove that
u
is continuous at
x_{0}
. Since
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is an open set, there exists
\rho>0
such that
|x_{0}-y|\geqslant\rho
for any
y\in\Omega
.
So, if
x\in B_{\rho/2}(x_{0})
, we have that

|x-y|\geqslant|x_{0}-y|-|x_{0}-x|\geqslant\frac{\rho}{2}\quad\mbox{ for any }y\in\Omega.

As a concequence, if
\rho
is small enough,
for any
x\in B_{\rho/2}(x_{0})
and

we have that

\frac{|u(y)|+1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\leqslant\frac{2^{N+2s}}{\rho^{N+2s}}\Big(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})}+1\Big)\leqslant\frac{2^{N+2}}{\rho^{N+2}}\Big(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})}+1\Big).

From this, we deduce that

\begin{split}\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|+1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)&\leqslant\frac{2^{N+2}}{\rho^{N+2}}|\Omega|\Big(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})}+1\Big)\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\,d\mu(s)\\
&\leqslant\frac{2^{N+2}}{\rho^{N+2}}|\Omega|\Big(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})}+1\Big)\sup_{s\in(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{(0,1)}d\mu(s)\\
&<+\infty.\end{split}

Thus, by the Neumann condition in (
1.10
) and the dominated convergence theorem we obtain that

\begin{split}\lim_{x\to x_{0}}u(x)&=\lim_{x\to x_{0}}\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}\\
&=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{u(y)}{|x_{0}-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{1}{|x_{0}-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}\\
&=u(x_{0}).\end{split}

This proves that
u
is continuous at any point of

\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.

Now we show that
u
is continuous at any point

p\in\partial\Omega
.
To do so, we need a more accurate argument, since numerators and denominators
may create singularities which have to be attentively estimated to detect cancellations.
We take a sequence
\{p_{k}\}_{k}

converging to
p
as

goes to infinity.
Let
q_{k}
be the projection of
p_{k}
onto
\overline{\Omega}
.
Since
p\in\overline{\Omega}
, from the minimizing property of the

|p_{k}-q_{k}|=\inf_{\xi\in\overline{\Omega}}|p_{k}-\xi|\leqslant|p_{k}-p|,

and so

\lim_{k\to+\infty}|q_{k}-p|\leqslant\lim_{k\to+\infty}\big(|q_{k}-p_{k}|+|p_{k}-p|\big)\leqslant\lim_{k\to+\infty}2|p_{k}-p|=0.

Thus, since by assumption
u
is continuous in
\overline{\Omega}
,
we have

(7.1)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}u(q_{k})=u(p).

Now we claim that

(7.2)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\big(u(q_{k})-u(p_{k})\big)=0.

To prove this, it is enough to consider the points
p_{k}
that belong
to
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
. Indeed, if

p_{k}\in\overline{\Omega}
, we have
p_{k}=q_{k}
and so
(
7.2
) is trivially satisfied.

We define
\nu_{k}:=(p_{k}-q_{k})/|p_{k}-q_{k}|
, so that
\nu_{k}
is the
exterior normal of
\Omega
at
q_{k}\in\partial\Omega
.
We consider a rigid motion
\mathcal{R}_{k}
such that
\mathcal{R}_{k}q_{k}=0
and
\mathcal{R}_{k}\nu_{k}=e_{N}=(0,\cdots,0,1)
(see Figure
1
). Let
h_{k}:=|p_{k}-q_{k}|
. We notice that

(7.3)

h_{k}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{k}p_{k}=h_{k}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{k}(p_{k}-q_{k})=\mathcal{R}_{k}\nu_{k}=e_{N}.

Then, the domain

\Omega_{k}:=h_{k}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{k}\Omega

has a vertical exterior normal at 0 and approaches the half space

\Pi:=\{x_{N}<0\}
as
k\to\infty
.

The rigid motion
\mathcal{R}_{k}
of

Now, we use the homogeneous Neumann condition at

to obtain

\begin{split}u(p_{k})-u(q_{k})&=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{u(y)}{|p_{k}-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{1}{|p_{k}-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}-u(q_{k})\\

with

(7.4)

We observe that from the uniform continuity of

\Omega

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\sup_{\Omega\cap B_{\sqrt{h_{k}}}(q_{k})}|u(y)-u(q_{k})|=0.

Therefore,

(7.5)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}|I_{1}|\leqslant\lim_{k\to+\infty}\,\sup_{\Omega\cap B_{\sqrt{h_{k}}}(q_{k})}|u(y)-u(q_{k})|=0.

Moreover, using the change of variable

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\ \dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{\sqrt{h_{k}}}(q_{k})}\dfrac{|u(y)-u(q_{k})|}{|p_{k}-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{1}{|p_{k}-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\,d\mu(s)}

converges to the half space

\displaystyle c_{N,s}

8.
The superposition of fractional perimeters

In this section we discuss the relation between the superposition of nonlocal Neumann derivatives and the notion of superposition of fractional perimeters, which we introduce in here.

At first, we recall that, in the local case, taking
\partial_{\nu}u=1
, the perimeter of
\Omega

(often denoted by
\mbox{Per}(\Omega)
) can be obtained as

|\partial\Omega|=\int_{\partial\Omega}d\mathscr{H}^{N-1}_{x}=\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{\nu}u\,d\mathscr{H}^{N-1}_{x}.

In the nonlocal case, discussed in
[
24
, Remark 3.4]
, a similar result was established
by considering a notion of normalized nonlocal derivative. We now extend this idea to cover the case of superpositions of operators (and perimeters) of different order.
To this end, one considers

w_{s,\Omega}(x):=c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}

and the normalized nonlocal derivative given by

(8.1)

\widetilde{\mathscr{N}_{s}}u(x):=\dfrac{\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x)}{w_{s,\Omega}(x)}\quad\mbox{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.

In this setting, if
\widetilde{\mathscr{N}_{s}}u(x)=1
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
, then the fractional perimeter, introduced in
[
12
]
turns to be the integral of
\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x)
over
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
, namely

(8.2)

\mbox{Per}_{s}(\Omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\mathscr{N}_{s}u(x)dx,

see
[
24
, Remark 3.4]
.

In our setting, the normalized superposition of nonlocal derivatives is defined in (
7.12
) and can be written as

\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}u(x)=\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}\,u(x)\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}w_{s,\Omega}(x)\,d\mu(s)}\quad\mbox{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.

The reader can appreciate similarities and differences with respect to (
8.1
).

Also, if
\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}u(x)=1
, the superposition of fractional perimeters, provided that
\mbox{Per}_{s}(\Omega)\in L^{1}((0,1),d\mu)
, can be obtained as

(8.3)

\begin{split}\int_{(0,1)}\mbox{Per}_{s}(\Omega)\,d\mu(s)&:=\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\dfrac{1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy\right)d\mu(s)\\
&=\int_{(0,1)}\left(\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}w_{s,\Omega}(x)\,dx\right)d\mu(s)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\left(\,\int_{(0,1)}w_{s,\Omega}(x)\,d\mu(s)\right)dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\left(\,\int_{(0,1)}w_{s,\Omega}(x)\,d\mu(s)\right)\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}u(x)\,dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\left(\,\int_{(0,1)}\mathscr{N}_{s}\,u(x)\,d\mu(s)\right)\,dx\\
&=\int_{(0,1)}\left(\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\mathscr{N}_{s}\,u(x)\,d\mu(s)\right)\,dx.\end{split}

This identity is interesting, because it recovers the superposition of fractional perimeters
from the superposition of fractional Neumann derivatives.

We stress that (
8.3
) can not be obtained by directly integrating the identity in (
8.2
). This would be true if
\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}_{s}u(x)=1
for any
s\in\mbox{supp}(\mu)
which, in general, is not guaranteed by the condition
\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}u(x)=1
.

It is also worth noting that, if
\mu=\delta_{s}
for some fractional power
s\in(0,1)
, then (
8.3
) turns into (
8.2
).

Proof of Theorem

We now provide a complete proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem

.

We first deal with the case

Appendix D
Proof of Proposition
6.3

Here we prove Proposition
6.3
.

Proof of Proposition
6.3
.

Let

m:=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)\,dx

be the total mass of
u
, which does not depend on
t
thanks to
Proposition
6.1
.
Defining

A(t):=\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)-m|^{2}\,dx,

from Proposition
6.1
we have

A(t)=\int_{\Omega}(u^{2}(x,t)+2mu(x,t)+m^{2})\,dx=\int_{\Omega}u^{2}(x)\,dx-|\Omega|m^{2}.

In light of (
6.2
), (
6.3
),
(
6.4
) and (
6.5
), we can use
Lemma
3.2
, the classical integration by parts formula and the homogeneous Neumann conditions to obtain

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=2\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u(x,t)u(x,t)\,dx=2\alpha\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\Delta u(x,t)\,dx-2\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)(-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=-2\alpha\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx-\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle<0,

which gives that

is decreasing.

Moreover, if
\mu\not\equiv 0
, we can use the Poincaré

5.1

to get

(D.1)

\begin{split}&A^{\prime}(t)\leqslant-\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s)\\
&\qquad\qquad\leqslant-c\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)-m|^{2}\,dx=-cA(t),\end{split}

for some

. We notice that, in the case

Thus, from (

,

Appendix E
An alternative proof of (

Here we provide an alternative proof of (

Lemma E.1
.

Under the same assumptions of Theorem

, recalling the definition in (

\lim_{k\to+\infty}|I_{2}|=0.

Proof.

The same arguments as in Theorem

|I_{2}|\leqslant 2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})}\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\dfrac{h_{k}^{-\frac{7}{4}s}}{|e_{N}-\eta|^{N+2s}}\,d\eta\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\dfrac{h_{k}^{-2s}}{|e_{N}-\eta|^{N+2s}}\,d\eta\,d\mu(s)}.

Let us define

(E.1)

\displaystyle\alpha_{k,s}

\displaystyle:=h_{k}^{-\frac{7}{4}s}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\dfrac{d\eta}{|e_{N}-\eta|^{N+s}}

(E.2)

\displaystyle{\mbox{and }}\quad\beta_{k,s}

\displaystyle:=h_{k}^{-2s}c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\dfrac{d\eta}{|e_{N}-\eta|^{N+2s}}.

We claim that

(E.3)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\alpha_{k,s}\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\beta_{k,s}\,d\mu(s)}=0.

First, we notice that for
k
large enough, it holds that
\Omega_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1/2}(e_{N})
.
From this, we have

(E.4)

\begin{split}&\alpha_{k,s}\leqslant h_{k}^{-\frac{7}{4}s}c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1/2}(e_{N})}\dfrac{d\eta}{|e_{N}-\eta|^{N+s}}=h_{k}^{-\frac{7}{4}s}c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1/2}}\dfrac{dz}{|z|^{N+s}}\\
&\qquad\qquad=h_{k}^{-\frac{7}{4}s}c_{N,s}\omega_{N-1}\int_{1/2}^{+\infty}\rho^{-1-s}d\rho=\frac{2^{s}}{s}\omega_{N-1}\,c_{N,s}\,h_{k}^{-\frac{7}{4}s}.\end{split}

We define the set

\mathcal{C}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{N}\leqslant 1-\frac{9}{10}\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x^{2}_{N-1}}\,,\ 2\leqslant|x-1|\leqslant\frac{1}{2\sqrt{h_{k}}}\right\},

as shown in Figure
2
. Since
\Omega_{k}
converges to the halfplane
\Pi
, we get

\mathcal{C}\subset\left(\Omega_{k}\cap B_{1/2\sqrt{h_{k}}}\right)\subset\Omega_{k}\quad\mbox{ for sufficiently large }k.

The set
\mathcal{C}
(in blue).

Moreover, we can write

\mathcal{C}=\left\{(\rho,\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{N-1})\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,2\pi]^{N-2}\times[0,\pi]:\ \rho\in\left[2,\frac{1}{2\sqrt{h_{k}}}\right],\ \theta=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{N-1})\in\Sigma\right\},

with
\Sigma\subseteq[0,2\pi]^{N-2}\times[0,\pi]

From this and recalling the definition in (
E.2
), we have

(E.5)

\begin{split}\beta_{k,s}&\geqslant h_{k}^{-2s}c_{N,s}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\dfrac{d\eta}{|e_{N}-\eta|^{N+2s}}=h_{k}^{-2s}c_{N,s}\int_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\sqrt{h_{k}}}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}\dfrac{\rho^{N-1}}{\rho^{N+2s}}d\theta\right)d\rho\\
&\quad=h_{k}^{-2s}c_{N,s}\left(\int_{\Sigma}d\theta\right)\int_{2}^{\frac{1}{2\sqrt{h_{k}}}}\rho^{-1-2s}d\rho=h_{k}^{-2s}c_{N,s}\,\dfrac{\omega_{N-1}^{\mathcal{C}}}{2s}[2^{-2s}-2^{2s}h_{k}^{s}],\end{split}

where we introduced the notation

\omega_{N-1}^{\mathcal{C}}:=\int_{\Sigma}d\theta.

Now, by (
E.4
) and (
E.5
) we infer that

\dfrac{\alpha_{k,s}}{\beta_{k,s}}\leqslant\dfrac{\frac{2^{s}}{s}\omega_{N-1}\,c_{N,s}\,h_{k}^{-\frac{7}{4}s}}{h_{k}^{-2s}\,c_{N,s}\,\dfrac{\omega_{N-1}^{\mathcal{C}}}{2s}\,[2^{-2s}-2^{2s}h_{k}^{s}]}=\dfrac{2^{s+1}\,\omega_{N-1}\,h_{k}^{\frac{s}{4}}}{\omega_{N-1}^{\mathcal{C}}-h_{k}^{s}\,\omega_{N-1}^{\mathcal{C}}2^{2s}}.

.
Hence, we have

\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(\delta,1)}\alpha_{k,s}\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\beta_{k,s}\,d\mu(s)}\leqslant Ch_{k}^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\ \dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(\delta,1)}\beta_{k,s}\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\beta_{k,s}\,d\mu(s)}\leqslant Ch_{k}^{\frac{\delta}{4}}.

\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,\delta)}\alpha_{k,s}\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\beta_{k,s}\,d\mu(s)}\leqslant\dfrac{\omega_{N-1}\displaystyle\int_{(0,\delta)}\frac{2^{s}}{s}\,c_{N,s}\,h_{k}^{\frac{7}{4}(\delta-s)}\,d\mu(s)}{\displaystyle\int_{(0,\delta)}\beta_{k,s}\,d\mu(s)+\omega_{N-1}^{\mathcal{C}}\displaystyle\int_{(\delta,1)}\,\dfrac{c_{N,s}}{2s}\left[2^{-2s}h_{k}^{-2s+\frac{7}{4}\delta}-2^{2s}h_{k}^{-s+\frac{7}{4}\delta}\right]\,d\mu(s)}.

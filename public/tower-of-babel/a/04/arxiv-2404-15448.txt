Non-negative crystalline and Poisson measures in the Euclidean space
Sergii Yu. Favorov

Abstract




Abstract.

We study properties of temperate non-negative purely atomic measures in the Euclidean space such that the distributional Fourier transform of these measures are pure point ones.
A connection between these measures and almost periodicity is shown, several forms of the uniqueness theorem are proved. We also obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for a measure
with positive integer masses on the real line to correspond the zero set of an absolutely convergent Dirichlet series with bounded spectrum.

AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 30B50, 42A38, 52C23

Keywords: Poisson measure, crystalline measure, lighthouse, Fourier transform, almost periodic measure, almost periodic set, zero set, Dirichlet series

Introduction

Following
[
20
]
, we say that a pure point complex measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a Poisson measure if
\mu
is a temperate distribution and its Fourier transform
\hat{\mu}
is also a pure point measure. If
\mu

\hat{\mu}
have locally finite supports, then
\mu
is said to be a crystalline measure. If, in addition, the measures
|\mu|
and
|\hat{\mu}|
are temperate distributions, then

is called the Fourier quasicrystal.

Fourier quasicrystals and crystalline measures are currently being studied very actively. Many works are devoted to the study of the properties of Fourier quasicrystals.
For example, see the collections of papers
[
2
]
,
[
24
]
, in particular, the basic work
[
17
]
. Fourier quasicrystals have applications in modern physics,
where they are used as mathematical models of certain atomic structures.

Measures of the form

(1)

\mu_{A}=\sum_{n}\delta_{a_{n}},\quad A=\{a_{n}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

are the most important case of Fourier quasicrystals. A nontrivial example of such measures in the case
d=1
, where the sequence
A
is not a finite union of shifts
of an arithmetical progression, was found by P.Kurasov and P.Sarnak
[
15
]
.
Then A.Olevskii and A.Ulanovskii in
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
showed that a sequence

, which generate Fourier quasicrystals (
1
) is the zero set of some exponential polynomial

P(z)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}q_{n}e^{2\pi iz\omega_{j}},\qquad q_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}},\quad\omega_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}},

with real zeros and, conversely, every zero set

of each exponential polynomial (

A multidimensional version of the last result was obtained by W.Lawton and A.Tsikh

Here we suppose that

in (

However, the condition that the support of

is locally finite often too restrictive, so more general objects, Poisson measures, arise (cf.
[
21

Note that the class of Poisson measures is the widest class inducing the general Poisson formula: if

\mu=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}\delta_{\lambda}

and

\hat{\mu}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}\delta_{\gamma}

are measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with countable
\Lambda,\,\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we have for every Schwartz function
\varphi

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}\varphi(\lambda)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}\hat{\varphi}(\gamma).

Most effects with Fourier quasicrystals are associated with their almost periodicity, so our study of Poisson measures will begin with the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

For every non-negative Poisson measure
\mu

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
both convolutions
\mu\star\psi
with any continuous compactly supported function

\hat{\mu}\star\varphi

Using definitions of the next section, the measure

is almost periodic, and the measure

is an almost periodic distribution.

Note that every complex Fourier quasicrystal is an almost periodic distribution
[
8

[

Preliminary results

The Fourier transform in the sense of distributions

Denote by
S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
the Schwartz space of test functions
\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with the finite norms

N_{n}(\varphi)=\sup_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\max_{\|k\|\leq n}\,(\max\{1,|x|^{n}\})|D^{k}\varphi(x)|,\quad n=0,1,2,\dots,

where

|x|=(x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{d}^{2})^{1/2},\ k=(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})\in({\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\})^{d},\ \|k\|=k_{1}+\dots+k_{d},\ D^{k}=\partial^{k_{1}}_{x_{1}}\dots\partial^{k_{d}}_{x_{d}}.

These norms generate the topology on
S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Elements of the space
S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
of continuous linear functionals on
S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
are called
temperate distributions
.
If
\mu\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

is a measure, we will say that
\mu
is a
temperate measure
.
The Fourier transform of a temperate distribution
f
is defined by the equality

\hat{f}(\varphi)=f(\hat{\varphi})\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\varphi\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

where

\hat{\varphi}(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot t}dx

is the Fourier transform of the function
\varphi
. By
\check{\varphi}
we denote the inverse Fourier transform of
\varphi
.
The Fourier transform is a continuous bijection of
S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
on itself and a bijection of
S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
on itself.

Dirichlet series on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

Denote by
\mathcal{H}
the algebra of all Dirichlet series

Q(x)=\sum_{n}r_{n}e^{2\pi i\omega_{n}\cdot x},\,\omega_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

with finite Wiener’s norm
\|Q\|_{W}=\sum_{n}|r_{n}|
.

For any
Q\in\mathcal{H}
and any analytic function
h(z)
on a neighborhood of the set
\overline{\{Q(x):\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}}
we have
h(Q(x))\in\mathcal{H}
(see
[
26
]
, Ch.VI).
In particular,

for every
Q\in\mathcal{H}
we have
\exp Q\in\mathcal{H}
,

if
\inf_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|Q(x)|>0
, we have
1/Q\in\mathcal{H}
,

if
|Q(x)|\leq C<1
, we have
\log(1+Q(x))\in\mathcal{H}
.

Entire functions of exponential growth

Zeros
a_{n}
of any entire function
g(z),\,z\in{\mathbb{C}}
, of exponential growth satisfy the conditions

(10)

\#\{a_{n}:\,|a_{n}|\leq r\}=O(r)\quad\text{as}\quad r\to\infty,

(as usual,
\#E
means the number of points of a finite multiset
E
, taking into account a multiplicity of each point), and

(11)

\sum_{n:\,|a_{n}|\leq r,a_{n}\neq 0}\frac{1}{a_{n}}=O(1)\quad\text{as}\quad r\to\infty.

On the other hand, if a sequence
A=\{a_{n}\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
satisfies these conditions, then the function

(12)

F(z)=\prod_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(1-z/a_{n})e^{z/a_{n}}

is an entire function of exponential growth (Lindelöf’s Theorem, see
[
14
]
). Note that each
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
can be repeated in the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
any finite number of times.

If a function
g(z)
of the exponential type
\sigma
is bounded on the real axis, then for all
z\in{\mathbb{C}}

|g(z)|\leq e^{\sigma|\mbox{Im }z|}\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{R}}}|g(z)|\quad\text{ (Phragment--Lindel\"{o}f Principle, see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{K}{}{}]})}.

Almost periodic functions and measures

Definition 1

(cf.
[
1
]
,
[
19
]
)
.

A continuous function
g(x)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

is almost periodic if for any
\varepsilon>0
the set of
\varepsilon
-almost periods

E_{\varepsilon}=\{\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\,\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|g(x+\tau)-g(x)|<\varepsilon\}

is relatively dense, i.e.,
E_{\varepsilon}\cap B(x,L)\neq\emptyset
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and some
L
depending on
\varepsilon
.

For example every sum

(13)

Q(x)=\sum q_{n}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\omega_{n}},\quad\omega_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\quad q_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}},

with finite Wiener’s norm
\|Q\|_{W}=\sum_{n}|q_{n}|
is an almost periodic function.
Note that each almost periodic function is uniformly bounded and uniformly continuous. Also, a uniform in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
limit of a sequence of almost periodic functions is almost periodic.

Set
U(T)=\{x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\,-T/2<x_{j}\leq T/2,\,j=1,\dots,d\}
. We can prove the existence of the mean value of any almost periodic function

Mg=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T^{d}}\int_{U(T)}g(x)dx

the same method as in the dimension
1
in
[
1
]
, also see
[
10
]
.
The Fourier coefficient

b_{\omega}(g)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T^{d}}\int_{U(T)}g(x)e^{-2\pi i\omega\cdot x}dx.

is assigned to every almost periodic function
g
and every
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
; spectrum of
g
is the set

\spec g=\{\omega:\,b_{\omega}(g)\neq 0\}.

It is easy to see that
b_{\omega_{n}}(Q)=q_{n}
and
\spec Q(x)=\{\omega_{n}:\,q_{n}\neq 0\}
. Note that spectra of almost periodic functions are at most countable.

Definition 2

(cf.
[
25
]
)
.

A measure
\mu
is almost periodic, if for any continuous function
\varphi
with compact support the convolution of
\mu\star\varphi=\int\varphi(t-x)\mu(dx)
is almost periodic in
t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Definition 3

(cf.
[
25
]
)
.

A distribution
F
is almost periodic, if for any function
\varphi\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
the convolution of
\varphi\star F=(F(x),\varphi(t-x))
is almost periodic in
t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Note that there exist non-almost periodic measures that are almost periodic in sense of distributions (
[

]
,
[

Properties of temperate measures and proofs of theorems

Proposition 3
.

[
8
, Lemma 1]
.
Let
\mu
be a temperate measure and
\hat{\mu}
be measure such that
|\hat{\mu}|\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Then
\mu
is an almost periodic distribution.

The condition
|\hat{\mu}|\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is equivalent to the polynomially growth of masses
|\hat{\mu}|
in the balls
B(0,r)
as
r\to\infty
(cf.
[
5
]
).

We will say that a measure
\mu
is translation bounded, if
\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\mu|B(x,1)<\infty
. It is easy to see that any translation bounded measure is a temperate measure.
Every almost periodic measure is translation bounded (see
[
25
]
or
[
19
]
).

Proposition 4
.

[
20
, Lemma 5.8]
.
If
\mu
is a translation bounded measure and
\hat{\mu}
is a pure point measure, then
\mu
is almost periodic.

This Meyer’s proposition is of fundamental importance in our investigation. For convenience of readers, we give its complete proof here.

Proof
. Let
\hat{\mu}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}\delta_{\gamma}
and
\varphi
be any
C^{\infty}
-function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with compact support. We have

\mu\star\check{\varphi}(t)=(\hat{\mu}(x),\varphi(x)e^{2\pi it\cdot x})=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma\cap\supp\varphi}b_{\gamma}\varphi(\gamma)e^{2\pi it\cdot\gamma}

Since
\hat{\mu}
is a measure, we get

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma\cap\supp\varphi}|b_{\gamma}||\varphi(\gamma)|\leq\sup|\varphi(\gamma)|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma\cap\supp\varphi}|b_{\gamma}|<\infty.

Hence the function

\mu\star\check{\varphi}(t)
is almost periodic.

Now let
\phi
is an arbitrary function from
S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Since

\hat{\phi}\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we can approximate the function
\check{\phi}(x)=\hat{\phi}(-x)
with compactly supported
C^{\infty}
-functions
\varphi_{n}
in the metric of the space
S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Therefore the functions
\hat{\varphi}_{n}
approximate the function
\phi
in the same metric. Also,

|\phi(x)-\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)|\leq N_{d+1}(\phi-\hat{\varphi}_{n})\min\{1,|x|^{-d-1}\}.

Therefore,

|\mu\star\phi(t)-\mu\star\hat{\varphi}_{n}(t)|\leq N_{d+1}(\phi-\hat{\varphi}_{n})\int\min\{1,|x-t|^{-d-1}\}|\mu|(dx)=

N_{d+1}(\phi-\hat{\varphi}_{n})|\mu|B(t,1)+N_{d+1}(\phi-\hat{\varphi}_{n})\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-d-1}d|\mu|(B(t,r)).

Since the measure
\mu
is translation bounded, we see that
|\mu|B(t,1)\leq C_{0}
and
|\mu|B(t,r)\leq C_{d}r^{d}
as
r>1
uniformly with respect to
t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Therefore the both terms tend to
0
uniformly with respect to
t
as

\hat{\varphi}_{n}\to\phi
in the space
S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and the convolution
\phi\star\mu(t)
is almost periodic. For any continuous function

\psi
with compact support there are functions
\phi_{n}\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with compact supports, which uniformly converge to
\psi
and
\cap_{n}\supp\phi_{n}=\supp\psi
. Taking into account that
\mu
is translation bounded,
we get again that the functions
\phi_{n}\star\mu(t)
tend to
\psi\star\mu(t)
uniformly in
t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Hence the last function is almost periodic too,
and
\mu
is an almost periodic measure.

Proof of Theorem
1
. Let
\phi
is a
C^{\infty}
-function with compact support in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and non-negative Fourier transform
\Psi(x)
(to construct such
\phi

\phi(t)=\int\bar{\varphi}(s-t)\varphi(s)ds
for

-function
\varphi
with compact support). There exists a ball

such that
\inf_{x\in B(a,\rho)}\Psi(x)\geq\eta>0
. Therefore,

\eta\mu(B(a,\rho))\leq\int_{B(a,\rho)}\Psi(t)\mu(dt)\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Psi(t)\mu(dt)=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi(y)\hat{\mu}(dy)\right|\leq\int_{\supp\phi}|\phi(y)||\hat{\mu}|(dy).

Then for any

\eta\mu(B(x,\rho))\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Psi(t+a-x)\mu(dt)=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi(y)e^{2\pi iy(a-x)}\hat{\mu}(dy)\right|\leq\int_{\supp\phi}|\phi(y)||\hat{\mu}|(dy).

Hence, values
\mu(B(x,\rho)))
are uniformly bounded, and the measure
\mu
is translation bounded. Proposition

implies almost periodicity of

.
Since
\mu
is translation bounded, we see that
\mu
is a temperate distribution, hence by Proposition
3
, the convolution
\hat{\mu}\star\varphi
is almost periodic for every
\varphi\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proof of Theorem
2
. Assume that
\mu\not\equiv\nu
. Then there is a point
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and a continuous function
\varphi,\,0\leq\varphi\leq 1
, with
\supp\varphi\subset B(0,1)
such that
\mu\star\varphi(x)\neq\nu\star\varphi(x)
.
Let
2\varepsilon<|\mu\star\varphi(x)-\nu\star\varphi(x)|
. The function
g=\mu\star\varphi-\nu\star\varphi
is almost periodic, hence for
r_{\varepsilon}
large enough every ball
B(x^{\prime},r_{\varepsilon})
contains the ball
B(x+\tau,1)

for some
\varepsilon
-almost period
\tau
of the function
g
.
Therefore,
|g(x+\tau)|>\varepsilon
and

|\mu-\nu|B(x^{\prime},r_{\varepsilon})\geq|\mu-\nu|\star\varphi(x+\tau)\geq|\mu\star\varphi(x+\tau)-\nu\star\varphi(x+\tau)|\geq\varepsilon.

Note that every ball of radius
R
contains at least
\kappa_{d}(R/r_{\varepsilon})^{d}

disjointed balls of radius
r_{\varepsilon}
, where the constant
\kappa_{d}
depends only on the dimension
d
. Therefore,

|\mu-\nu|B(x_{n},R_{n})\geq\kappa_{d}r_{\varepsilon}^{-d}\varepsilon R_{n}^{d}.

We get a contradiction with conditions of the theorem.

The same arguments work in the case
\hat{\mu}\not\equiv\hat{\nu}
after replacing a continuous
\varphi
with
C^{\infty}
-function
\varphi
.

Proof of Theorem
3
. Assume
\mu\not\equiv\nu
. Let
x
,
\varphi
,
\varepsilon
,
r_{\varepsilon}
be the same as above. Take appropriate
\tau
such that
B(x+\tau,r_{\varepsilon})\subset B(x_{n},R_{n})
for some
n
and

(14)

|\mu\star\varphi(x+\tau)-\nu\star\varphi(x+\tau)|\geq\varepsilon.

Let
M_{\tau}
be a set of pairs
\{(a_{n},c_{n})\}
such that either
a_{n}\in\supp\mu\cap B(x+\tau,1)
or
c_{n}\in\supp\nu\cap B(x+\tau,1)
. By Theorem
1
, the measures
\mu
and
\nu
are translation bounded.
Therefore quantities
\#M_{\tau}
are bounded by a constant, which does not depend on
\tau
. We have

|\mu\star\varphi(x+\tau)-\nu\star\varphi(x+\tau)|\leq\sum_{(a_{n},c_{n})\in M_{\tau}}|\varphi(a_{n})-\varphi(c_{n})|\leq(\#M_{\tau})\kappa_{d}N_{1}(\varphi)\max_{(a_{n},c_{n})\in M_{\tau}}|a_{n}-c_{n}|.

It follows from conditions of the theorem that this inequality contradicts to (
14
) for
n
large enough.

Proof of Theorem
4
. Assume that there is a point
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
0<\mu(x)<c
. Set
\varepsilon<(1/2)\min\{(c-\mu(x)),\mu(x)\}
and
r<b/2

such that
\mu(B(x,2r))<\mu(x)+\varepsilon
. Let
\varphi(x),\ 0\leq\varphi(x)\leq 1
be a continuous function with support in
B(0,r)
such that
\varphi(0)=1
. We have

\mu(x)\leq\mu\star\varphi(x)\leq\mu(B(x,r))<\mu(x)+\varepsilon.

For
R_{n}
large enough the ball
B(x_{n},R_{n})\subset E
contains the ball
B(x+\tau,r)

for some
\varepsilon
-almost period
\tau
of the function
\mu\star\varphi
. Then
\mu\star\varphi(x+\tau)>0
, hence the ball
B(x+\tau,r)
contains exactly one point
x^{\prime}\in\supp\mu
such that

c\leq\mu(x^{\prime})\leq\mu\star\varphi(x^{\prime})
. Therefore,

c-\varepsilon<\mu\star\varphi(x^{\prime}-\tau)\leq\mu(B(x^{\prime}-\tau,r))\leq\mu(B(x,2r))<\mu(x)+\varepsilon,

which contradicts our choice of
\varepsilon
. Consequently, the estimate
\mu(x)\geq c
holds for all
x\in\supp\mu
.

Assume that there are points
x,\,x+h\in\supp\mu
such that
|h|<b
. Let
\varphi,\ 0\leq\varphi(x)\leq 1
be a continuous function with support in
B(0,\varepsilon)
with
\varepsilon<\min\{(b-|h|),c\}
such that
\varphi(0)=1
. Then

\mu\star\varphi(x)\geq c
and
\mu\star\varphi(x+h)\geq c
. For
R_{n}
large enough the ball
B(x_{n},R_{n})\subset E

contains the ball
B(x+\tau,|h|+\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon
-almost period
\tau
of the function
\mu\star\varphi
. Then

\mu\star\varphi(x+\tau)>0,\qquad\mu\star\varphi(x+h+\tau)>0,

hence there are points
x^{\prime}\in B(x+\tau,\varepsilon),\,x^{\prime\prime}\in B(x+h+\tau,\varepsilon)
such that
\mu(x^{\prime})>0,\,\mu(x^{\prime\prime})>0
. Since
|x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime}|<|h|+2\varepsilon<b
, we get a contradiction.

In the case of a complex measure
\mu
condition (
4
) and Proposition
3
yield that
\mu
is an almost periodic distribution. From here, repeating the previous arguments with replacing

\mu
by
|\mu|
, we obtain that inequalities
|\mu(\lambda)|\geq c
and
|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|\geq\beta>0,\,\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
hold for all points
\lambda,\,\lambda^{\prime}\in\supp\mu
. Under these conditions
the first equality in (
5
) was proved in
[
8
, Theorem 5]
, and the second one is contained in
[
7
, Theorem 4]
.

4.
Proof of sufficiency in Theorem
5

Let
A=\{a_{n}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a sequence such that the Fourier transform
\hat{\mu}_{A}
is measure (
7
). Our theorem does not depend on shifts along real axis, hence we can assume

a_{n}\neq 0
for all
n
. Since
\mu_{A}
is a non-negative Poisson measure, we see that
A
is an almost periodic set. By Proposition
2
, we can renumber
a_{n}
such that
a_{n}=n/D+\phi(n),\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}

with a bounded function
\phi(n)
and a density
D
, and we get condition (
10
).

Consider the sum

(15)

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{1}{x+iy-a_{n}}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{1}{x+iy-n/D-\phi(n)}.

Set
M=\sup_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi(n)|
. Since

(16)

\sum_{|n/D|<M+|x|+1}\left|\frac{1}{x+iy-n/D-\phi(n)}\right|+\sum_{|n/D|\geq M+|x|+1}\left|\frac{1}{x+iy-n/D-\phi(n)}+\frac{1}{x+iy+n/D-\phi(-n)|}\right|\leq R,

with
R
depending on
x
and
y
, we get that (
15
) is finite for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}},\,y>0
.

Furthermore, the sums

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}},|a_{n}|<N}\frac{1}{a_{n}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}},|n/D|<N}\frac{1}{a_{n}}=\frac{1}{a_{0}}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[\frac{1}{n/D+\phi(n)}+\frac{1}{-n/D+\phi(-n)}\right]

differ for a uniformly bounded (with respect to
N
) number of terms, and each of these terms tends to
0
as
N\to\infty
. Therefore these sums have the same finite limit
L
as
N\to\infty
,
and we get (
11
). By Lindelof’s Theorem, the function
F(z)
from (
12
) has exponential growth. Moreover, we obtain that the sum

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left|\frac{1}{a_{n}}+\frac{1}{a_{-n}}\right|

is also converges, hence

F(z)=(1-z/a_{0})e^{z/a_{0}}\prod_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}(1-z/a_{n})(1-z/a_{-n})e^{z(1/a_{n}-1/a_{-n})}=e^{Lz}f(z),

where

(17)

f(z)=(1-z/a_{0})\prod_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}(1-z/a_{n})(1-z/a_{-n})

is a well-defined entire function of exponential growth.

Set for
\mbox{Im }z>0

\xi_{z}(t)=\begin{cases}-2\pi ie^{2\pi itz}&\text{if }t>0,\\
0&\text{if }t\leq 0,\end{cases}

It is not hard to check that in the sense of distributions
\hat{\xi}_{z}(\lambda)=1/(z-\lambda)
.
Let
\varphi(t)
be any even nonnegative
C^{\infty}
-function such that

\supp\varphi\subset(-1,1)\quad\text{and}\quad\int\varphi(t)dt=1.

Set
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t)=\varepsilon^{-1}\varphi(t/\varepsilon)
for
\varepsilon>0
. The functions
\xi_{z}(t)\star\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t)
and
\hat{\xi}_{z}(\lambda)\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)
belong to
S({\mathbb{R}})
. Therefore,

(\mu_{A},\hat{\xi}_{z}(\lambda)\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(\lambda))=(\hat{\mu}_{A},\xi_{z}\star\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t)).

We have

(\mu_{A}(\lambda),\hat{\xi}_{z}(\lambda)\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(\lambda))=\frac{\hat{\varphi}(\varepsilon a_{0})}{z-a_{0}}+\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{\hat{\varphi}(\varepsilon a_{n})}{z-a_{n}}+\frac{\hat{\varphi}(\varepsilon a_{-n})}{z-a_{-n}}\right].

Clearly,
\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(t)\to 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Taking into account (
16
), we can apply Lebesgue’s Dominate Convergence Theorem. Hence,

(18)

(\mu_{A},\hat{\xi}_{z}(\lambda)\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(\lambda))\to\frac{1}{z-a_{0}}+\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{1}{z-a_{n}}+\frac{1}{z-a_{-n}}\right]\qquad(\varepsilon\to 0).

Set
n(s)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma:\,0<\gamma<s}|b_{\gamma}|=|\hat{\mu}_{A}|(0,s)
. If
|\hat{\mu}_{A}|(0,s)\leq Ce^{2\pi sK}
, we obtain for
y>K

(19)

\sum_{\gamma\geq r}|b_{\gamma}|e^{-2\pi\gamma y}=\int_{r}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi sy}n(ds)\leq\lim_{T\to\infty}n(T)e^{-2\pi Ty}+2\pi y\int_{r}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi sy}n(s)ds<\infty.

Therefore the series
\sum_{\gamma>0}b_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma z}
absolutely and uniformly converges for every fixed
y=\mbox{Im }z>K
.

Further, we have for
\varepsilon>0

\frac{i}{2\pi}(\hat{\mu}_{A}(t),\xi_{z}\star\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t))=\sum_{\gamma<0}b_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma z}\int_{s<\gamma}e^{-2\pi isz}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(s)ds+b_{0}\int_{s<0}e^{-2\pi isz}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(s)ds\\
+\sum_{\gamma>0}b_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma z}\int_{s<\gamma}e^{-2\pi isz}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(s)ds.

It is easy to see that all integrals are bounded and

\int_{s<\gamma<0}e^{-2\pi isz}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(s)ds\to 0,\quad\int_{s<0}e^{-2\pi isz}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(s)ds\to\frac{1}{2},\quad\int_{0<s<\gamma}e^{-2\pi isz}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(s)ds\to 1\qquad(\varepsilon\to 0).

Using Lebesgue’s Dominate Convergence Theorem, we obtain

(\hat{\mu}_{A},\xi_{z}\star\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t))\to-2\pi i\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma\cap(0,+\infty)}b_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma z}-\pi ib_{0}\qquad(\varepsilon\to 0),

and by (
18
),

(20)

f^{\prime}(z)/f(z)=\frac{1}{z-a_{0}}+\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{1}{z-a_{n}}+\frac{1}{z-a_{-n}}\right]=-2\pi i\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma\cap(0,+\infty)}b_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma z}-\pi iD,

where we replace
b_{0}
with the density
D
. Therefore,
f^{\prime}(z)/f(z)
is an absolutely convergent Dirichlet series on the line
x\in{\mathbb{R}},\,y=\const>K
, and its mean value equals
-\pi iD
.

Changing the order of summation and integration in (
20
), we get

(21)

\log f(x+iy)-\log f(iy)=\int_{iy}^{x+iy}\frac{f^{\prime}(\zeta)}{f(\zeta)}d\zeta=-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma\cap(0,+\infty)}b_{\gamma}\frac{(e^{2\pi i\gamma x}-1)e^{-2\pi\gamma y}}{\gamma}-iD\pi x.

Since
|e^{2\pi i\gamma x}-1|/|\gamma|\leq 2\pi|x|
, we see that (
21
) is well defined for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
y>K
. Then

\|\log f(x+iy)+iD\pi x\|_{W}\leq\sum_{0<\gamma<1}\frac{|b_{\gamma}|}{\gamma}2e^{-2\pi\gamma y}+\sum_{\gamma\geq 1}\frac{|b_{\gamma}|}{\gamma}2e^{-2\pi\gamma y}+|\log|f(iy)||<\infty.

Condition (
8
) implies that the first sum in the right-hand side converges, and (
19
) implies that the second one converges as well.

Redefine
y=y_{0}
. We obtain

f(x+iy_{0})e^{iD\pi x}=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\beta_{\omega}e^{2\pi i\omega x},\quad\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}|\beta_{\omega}|=\|f(x+iy_{0})e^{iD\pi x}\|_{W}\leq e^{\|\log f(x+iy_{0})+iD\pi x\|_{W}}<\infty,

with
\beta_{\omega}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and a countable spectrum
\Omega
. The entire function
f(z+iy_{0})e^{iD\pi z}
has the exponential growth with zeros in a horizontal strip of bounded width.
It follows from
[
16
, §1, Ch.VI]
, that
\Omega
is bounded. Hence the function

f(z)=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\beta_{\omega}e^{\pi(2\omega-D)y_{0}}e^{\pi i(2\omega-D)z}

is also a Dirichlet series and
\|f\|_{W}<\infty
.

5.
Proof of necessity in Theorem
5

Let
Q
be Dirichlet series of the form (
6
)
with the zero set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}
. Since zeros of
Q(z)
lie in a strip of finite width, we get that
\kappa:=\inf\Omega\in\Omega
and
\kappa^{\prime}:=\sup\Omega\in\Omega

(see
[
16
]
, Ch.VI, Cor.2). We obtain for
z=x+iy

\log Q(x+iy)=\log q_{\kappa}+2\pi i\kappa(x+iy)+\log(1+H(x+iy)),\quad H(x+iy)=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega\setminus\{\kappa\}}\frac{q_{\omega}}{q_{\kappa}e^{2\pi(\omega-\kappa)y}}e^{2\pi i(\omega-\kappa)x}.

Taking into account that
\sum_{\omega}|q_{\omega}|<\infty
, we can take a finite number of points
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{N}\in\Omega
and then
s>0
such that

\sum_{\omega\in\Omega\setminus\{\kappa,\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{N}\}}|q_{\omega}/q_{\kappa}|<1/3,\qquad\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-2\pi(\omega_{j}-\kappa)s}|q_{\omega_{j}}/q_{\kappa}|<1/3.

Also, we may suppose that for the same
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{N}
and
s

(22)

\sum_{\omega\in\Omega\setminus\{\kappa^{\prime},\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{N}\}}|q_{\omega}/q_{\kappa^{\prime}}|<1/3,\qquad\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{2\pi(\omega_{j}-\kappa^{\prime})s}|q_{\omega_{j}}/q_{\kappa^{\prime}}|<1/3.

So
\|H(x+iy)\|_{W}<2/3
for
y\geq s
, and the function

\log(1+H(x+iy))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}H^{n}(x+iy)}{n}

is well-defined and belongs to
\mathcal{H}
. Since
\spec H(x+iy)\subset(0,+\infty)
, we see that spectrum of the function

(23)

\log Q(x+iy)-2\pi i\kappa(x+iy)-\log q_{\kappa}=\log(1+H(x+iy))

lies in
(0,+\infty)
too.

From almost periodicity of
Q
it follows that for any
\varepsilon>0,\,s>0
there exists
m(\varepsilon,s)>0
such that

|Q(z)|\geq m(\varepsilon,s)\quad\mbox{for}\quad|\mbox{Im }z|\leq s\quad\mbox{and}\quad z\not\in A(\varepsilon):=\{z:\,\dist(z,A)<\varepsilon\}.

(
[
16
]
, Ch.6, Lemma 1). Hence we have for any fixed
y>0

(24)

\inf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}|Q(x\pm iy)|>0.

Further, the zero set
A
of the Dirichlet series
Q(z)
is almost periodic, and Proposition
1
implies that each connected component of
A(\varepsilon)
contains
no segment of length
1
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
.
Hence there are sequences
L_{k}\to+\infty,\,L^{\prime}_{k}\to-\infty
and
m>0
such that

|Q(x+iy)|>m\quad\text{for}\quad x=L_{k}\quad\text{or}\quad x=L^{\prime}_{k},\quad|y|\leq s.

Let
\varphi
be
C^{\infty}
-function with compact support on some interval
(-r,r)
. Set

\Phi(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi(t)e^{-2\pi itz}dt.

Clearly,
\Phi(x+iy)
is an entire function, which equals the Fourier transform of the function
\varphi(t)e^{2\pi ty}
for any fixed
y
.
Therefore,
\Phi(x+iy)
belongs to
S({\mathbb{R}})
for each fixed
y\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and we have for its converse Fourier transform

(25)

\check{\Phi}(t+iy)=\varphi(t)e^{2\pi ty},\qquad t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Hence the function
\Phi(x+iy)
tends to zero for
x=L_{k}
or
x=L^{\prime}_{k}
as
k\to\infty
uniformly with respect to
y\in[-s,s]
.
Consider integrals of the function
\Phi(z)Q^{\prime}(z)Q^{-1}(z)
over the boundaries of rectangles

\Pi_{k}=\{z=x+iy:\,L^{\prime}_{k}<x<L_{k},|y|<s\}
. These integrals tend to the difference

(26)

\int_{-is-\infty}^{-is+\infty}\Phi(z)Q^{\prime}(z)Q^{-1}(z)dz-\int_{is-\infty}^{is+\infty}\Phi(z)Q^{\prime}(z)Q^{-1}(z)dz=:I_{1}-I_{2}

as
k\to\infty
. On the other hand, they tend to the sum

(27)

2\pi i\sum_{\lambda:Q(\lambda)=0}\Res_{\lambda}\Phi(z)Q^{\prime}(z)Q^{-1}(z)=2\pi i\sum_{\lambda:Q(\lambda)=0}a(\lambda)\hat{\varphi}(\lambda)=2\pi i(\mu_{A},\hat{\varphi})=2\pi i(\hat{\mu}_{A},\varphi),

where
a(\lambda)
is the multiplicity of zero of
Q(z)
at the point
\lambda
.

We have
Q^{\prime}/Q=(\log Q)^{\prime}
, hence (
23
) implies

(28)

Q^{\prime}(x+is)/Q(x+is)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}}p_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma x},\quad\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}}|p_{\gamma}|<\infty,\quad p_{0}=2\pi i\kappa,\quad p_{\gamma}=0\quad\text{for}\quad\gamma<0.

It follows from (
22
) that the same arguments show

Q^{\prime}(x-is)/Q(x-is)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}}\tilde{p}_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma x},\quad\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}}|\tilde{p}_{\gamma}|<\infty,\quad\tilde{p}_{0}=2\pi i\kappa^{\prime},\quad\tilde{p}_{\gamma}=0\quad\text{for}\quad\gamma>0.

The functions
\Phi(x\pm is)
belong to
S({\mathbb{R}})
, hence
|x|^{2}\Phi(x\pm is)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
.
Changing the order of integration and summation and taking into account (
25
), we obtain for the difference of integrals (
26
)

(29)

I_{1}-I_{2}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}}\tilde{p}_{\gamma}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Phi(x-is)e^{2\pi i\gamma x}dx-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}}p_{\gamma}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Phi(x+is)e^{2\pi i\gamma x}dx\\
=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}}\tilde{p}_{\gamma}e^{-2\pi\gamma s}\varphi(\gamma)-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}}p_{\gamma}e^{2\pi\gamma s}\varphi(\gamma).

Since
\supp\varphi\subset(-r,r)
, we obtain

(30)

|(\hat{\mu}_{A},\varphi)|\leq(2\pi)^{-1}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}}|p_{\gamma}|+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}}|\tilde{p}_{\gamma}|\right)e^{2\pi rs}\sup_{|y|\leq r}|\varphi(y)|.

Hence the distribution
\hat{\mu}_{A}
has a unique expansion to a linear functional on the space of continuous functions
g
on
[-r,r]
such that
g(-r)=g(r)=0
with the same bound.
Since we can expand this functional to the space of all continuous functions on
[-r,r]
with the same bound too, we see that
\hat{\mu}_{A}
is a complex measure.
Then (
27
) and (
29
) implies

(31)

\hat{\mu}_{A}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}}\frac{\tilde{p}_{\gamma}e^{-2\pi\gamma s}}{2\pi i}\delta_{\gamma}-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}}\frac{p_{\gamma}e^{2\pi\gamma s}}{2\pi i}\delta_{\gamma}.

Therefore,
\hat{\mu}_{A}
is a purely point measure. Also, it follows from (
30
) that

(32)

|\hat{\mu}_{A}|(-r,r)\leq C(s)e^{2\pi sr}.

Furthermore, the functions
Q(z)
and
f(z)
from (
17
) have exponential growth and the same zeros. Therefore,

Q(z)=Ce^{\alpha z+i\beta z}f(z),\qquad C\in{\mathbb{C}},\quad\alpha,\,\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Hence,

(33)

\frac{Q^{\prime}(x+iy)}{Q(x+iy)}-\frac{f^{\prime}(x+iy)}{f(x+iy)}=\alpha+i\beta.

It follows from (
32
) that the function
f^{\prime}(x+iy)/f(x+iy)
holds (
20
) for
y>s
. Let

\log(1+H(x+iy))=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}c_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma x},\quad c_{\gamma}=c_{\gamma}(y),\quad\Gamma_{1}\subset(0,+\infty),\quad\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}|c_{\gamma}|<\infty.

By (
23
),

Q^{\prime}(x+iy)/Q(x+iy)=(\log Q(x+iy))^{\prime}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}}2\pi i\gamma c_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma x}+2\pi i\kappa.

Therefore, (
33
) and (
20
) yield

\alpha=0,\quad\beta=\pi i(D+2\kappa),\quad 2\pi i\gamma c_{\gamma}=-2\pi ie^{-2\pi\gamma y}b_{\gamma},\quad\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma\cap(0,+\infty),

and

\sum_{0<\gamma<1}|b_{\gamma}/\gamma|\leq e^{2\pi y}\sum_{0<\gamma<1}|c_{\gamma}|<\infty.

We obtain (
8
).

By (
24
),
1/Q(x\pm i\eta)\in\mathcal{H}
for every
\eta>0
. The spectrum
\Omega
is bounded, hence
Q^{\prime}(x\pm i\eta)\in\mathcal{H}
as well.
Therefore,
Q^{\prime}(x\pm i\eta)/Q(x\pm i\eta)\in\mathcal{H}
, and we get the representation

Q^{\prime}(x\pm i\eta)/Q(x\pm i\eta)=\sum_{\gamma\in S}h_{\gamma}^{\pm}e^{2\pi i\gamma x},\qquad\sum_{\gamma\in S}|h_{\gamma}^{\pm}|<\infty,

where
S
is a countable subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}
. Arguing as above, instead of (
31
) we obtain the representation

\hat{\mu}_{A}=\sum_{\gamma\in S}\frac{h_{\gamma}^{-}e^{-2\pi\gamma\eta}-h_{\gamma}^{+}e^{2\pi\gamma\eta}}{2\pi i}\delta_{\gamma}.

Therefore, (
32
) is valid for all
s=\eta>0
. This gives (
9
).

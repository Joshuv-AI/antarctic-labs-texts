Generalized Gapped-kmer Filters for Robust Frequency Estimation
M. Mohammad-Noori, N. Ghareghani, M. Ghandi

Abstract
In this paper, we study the generalized gapped k-mer filters and derive a closed form solution for their coefficients. We consider nonnegative integers ℓ \ell and k k , with k ≤ ℓ k\leq\ell , and an ℓ \ell -tuple B = ( b 1 , … , b ℓ ) B=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell}) of integers b i ≥ 2 b_{i}\geq 2 , i = 1 , … , ℓ i=1,\ldots,\ell . We introduce and study an incidence matrix A = A ℓ , k ; B A=A_{\ell,k;B} . We develop a Möbius-like function ν B \nu_{B} which helps us to obtain closed forms for a complete set of mutually orthogonal eigenvectors of A ⊤ ​ A A^{\top}A as well as a complete set of mutually orthogonal eigenvectors of A ​ A ⊤ AA^{\top} corresponding to nonzero eigenvalues. The reduced singular value decomposition of A A and combinatorial interpretations for the nullity and rank of A A , are among the consequences of this approach. We then combine the obtained formulas, some results from linear algebra, and combinatorial identities of elementary symmetric functions and ν B \nu_{B} , to provide the entries of the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse matrix A + A^{+} and the Gapped k-mer filter matrix A + ​ A A^{+}A .

Introduction

Sequences of length
k
, commonly referred to as
k
-mers, are used in many computational biology algorithms. We previously showed that robust frequency estimation of
k
-mers using gapped
k
-mer features could profoundly improve the performance of algorithms used for sequence classification in computational biology
[
7
,
6
]
. The method described in these previous publications was based on analytically deriving the coefficients of a gapped
k
-mer filter that could be used to find the robust frequency estimates of
k
-mers. Although this filter could be applied to datasets consisting of DNA or Protein sequences, it was not applicable to complex datasets that included sequences defined on more heterogeneous feature spaces. Here, we provide the closed-form solution for a generalized gapped
k
-mer filter matrix, by relaxing the constraint that all the features are defined on a fixed-size alphabet.

In order to introduce the main object of this introduction, we briefly mention few definitions and notations here; These are presented in more extent and details in the body of the paper. Given two integers
\ell
and
k

0\leq k\leq\ell

of integers

,

, we associate to them two sets of sequences,

and
V_{\ell,k;B}
, a match relation between the elements of these two sets and a corresponding
(0,1)
matrix

as below. The set
U_{\ell;B}
consists of all sequences
x_{1}\cdots x_{\ell}
of integers
x_{i}
satisfying
0\leq x_{i}<b_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,\ell
. The set
V_{\ell,k;B}
consists of all sequences
y_{1}\cdots y_{\ell}
, where each
y_{i}
is either an integer satisfying
0\leq y_{i}<b_{i}
or an additional gap symbol denoted as
g
; Furthermore, there are exactly
\ell-k
occurrences of the gap symbol in any
y_{1}\cdots y_{\ell}\in V_{\ell,k;B}
. Two sequences
x_{1}\cdots x_{\ell}\in U_{\ell;B}
and

are then matchable if for any

1\leq i\leq\ell

or

. In other words, the gap symbol

acts as a wildcard and can match to any symbol. The corresponding

U_{\ell;B}
and
V_{\ell,k;B}
and setting

if and only if

and

are matchable.

When

and we use the name

instead of

and

is the set of four DNA bases. Then the set of column and row indexes have special names: The set of column indexes,

-mers and the set of row indexes is gapped oligomers with

-mers (of length

\ell
. Apart from some previous studies of

in mathematics (see

14

[

,
6
]
. Specifically, the inherent symmetry in matrix

allowed finding simple closed-form solutions for two related matrices:

is the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse of

Beyond modeling enhancer sequences in mammalian genomes, this method has been widely applied to several problems in computational biology including prediction of the effect of non-coding variants

In all the above applications, the features were defined over a fixed alphabet length (

All matrices we concern in this paper are real matrices. The row space of a

The matrix

If

3
The function

and some of its properties

In this section, we consider an order on the set
\Delta_{B}
and based on this define a function
\nu_{B}
on the set
\Delta_{B}\times\Delta_{B}
and inspect some of its properties. For an integer

, the following linear order makes

a totally ordered set:

0\prec_{i}1\prec_{i}\ldots\prec_{i}b_{i}-1\prec_{i}g

\Delta_{B}=\Delta_{b_{1}}\times\cdots\times\Delta_{b_{\ell}}

with

,
(1\leq i\leq\ell)

if and only if

holds for

. Below is presented the definition of
a useful function on
\Delta_{B}\times\Delta_{B}

Definition 11

Consider the
\ell

v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell n}
. The following matrix identities hold.

(i)

A_{\ell k;B}^{\top}{\bf x}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell kn}=S_{\ell-k}(B(G_{v^{\prime}}))\,{\bf z}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell n}

(ii)

A_{\ell k;B}{\bf z}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell n}={\bf x}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell kn}
.

(iii)

A_{\ell k;B}A_{\ell k;B}^{\top}{\bf x}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell kn}=S_{\ell-k}(B(G_{v^{\prime}}))\,{\bf x}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell kn}

(iv)

A_{\ell k;B}^{\top}A_{\ell k;B}{\bf z}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell n}=S_{\ell-k}(B(G_{v^{\prime}}))\,{\bf z}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell n}

(v)

For any two distinct words
v\in V^{\prime}_{\ell n_{1}}
and
u\in V^{\prime}_{\ell n_{2}}
, the vectors

{\bf x}_{v}^{\ell kn_{1}}
and
{\bf x}_{u}^{\ell kn_{2}}
are orthogonal.

(vi)

For any two distinct words
v\in V^{\prime}_{\ell n_{1}}
and
u\in V^{\prime}_{\ell n_{2}}
, the vectors

{\bf z}_{v}^{\ell n_{1}}
and
{\bf z}_{u}^{\ell n_{2}}
are orthogonal.

Proof.

The proofs of (i) and (ii) are concluded from definitions of
{\bf x}_{v^{\prime}}
and
{\bf z}_{v^{\prime}}
and Proposition
2
. Combining (i) and (ii) yields (iii) and (iv). The proofs of (v) is concluded from Proposition
1
(ii). The same proposition yields part (vi) by setting
k=\ell
.

\Box

Theorem 1

.
Let
0\leq k\leq\ell
. Then

(i)

The set
\{S_{\ell-k}(B(G_{v^{\prime}})):v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,n;B},\,0\leq n\leq\ell\}\}
consists of all
eigenvalues of the matrix
A_{\ell,k;B}^{\top}A_{\ell,k;B}
and the set
\{{\bf z}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell n}:v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,n;B},\,0\leq n\leq\ell\}
is a complete set of eigenvectors corresponding to eigenvalues of
A_{\ell,k;B}^{\top}A_{\ell,k;B}
.
Moreover, these eigenvectors are pairwise orthogonal.

(ii)

The set
\{S_{\ell-k}(B(G_{v^{\prime}})):v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,\leq k;B}\}
consists of all non-zero
eigenvalues of the matrix
A_{\ell,k;B}A_{\ell,k;B}^{\top}
. The set

\{{\bf x}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell kn}:v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,\leq k;B}\}
is a complete set of eigenvectors corresponding to aforementioned nonzero eigenvalues.
Moreover, these eigenvectors are pairwise orthogonal.

Proof.

(i)

First we note that for any
v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,n;B},0\leq n\leq\ell
,
\,{\bf z}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell n}
is a nonzero vector. By Proposition
5
(iv), for any
v^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{B}
,
{\bf z}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell n}
is an eigenvector of
A_{\ell,k;B}^{\top}A_{\ell,k;B}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
S_{\ell-k}(B(G_{v^{\prime}}))
. By Proposition
5
(vi), these eigenvectors are pairwise orthogonal.
Since
|\{{\bf z}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell n}:v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,n;B},\,0\leq n\leq\ell\}|=|\Gamma_{B}|
and
|\Gamma_{B}|=|\Sigma_{B}|
equals the size of the matrix

A_{\ell,k;B}^{\top}A_{\ell,k;B}
, we conclude that
\{{\bf z}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell n}:v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,n;B},\,0\leq n\leq\ell\}
is a complete set of eigenvectors corresponding to eigenvalues of
A_{\ell,k;B}^{\top}A_{\ell,k;B}
, as required.

(ii)

The set of all non-zero eigenvalues of
A_{\ell,k;B}A_{\ell,k;B}^{\top}
is the same as the set of all non-zero eigenvalues of
A_{\ell,k;B}^{\top}A_{\ell,k;B}
. In part (i), we obtained the complete set of eigenvalues of
A_{\ell,k;B}^{\top}A_{\ell,k;B}
. On the other hand, if
0\leq n\leq\ell
and
v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,n;B}
, then
S_{\ell-k}(B(G_{v^{\prime}}))=0
holds if and only if
k<n\leq\ell
.
Hence, the set
\{S_{\ell-k}(B(G_{v^{\prime}})):v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,\leq k;B}\}
consists of all non-zero
eigenvalues of the matrix
A_{\ell,k;B}A_{\ell,k;B}^{\top}
as well as the matrix
A_{\ell,k;B}^{\top}A_{\ell,k;B}
.
Moreover, using Proposition
5
(iii), the
corresponding eigenvectors are
\{{\bf x}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell kn}:v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,\leq k;B}\}
and these vectors are pairwise orthogonal.

\Box

5
Bases for the null space and the row space of
A_{\ell,k;B}

In this section we give concrete bases for the null space and the row space of
A_{\ell,k;B}
.
The basis for the null space is obtained just as a corollary of the arguments of the Section
4
.
The basis for the row space is obtained by a proper selection of some rows of
A_{\ell,k;B}
;
This gives a combinatorial interpretation for the previous formula about the rank of
A_{\ell,k;B}
;
The process of finding a basis for the row space of the matrix
A_{\ell,k;B}

is similar to the one about incidence matrices presented in
[
16
]
.

Theorem 2

.
Let
0\leq k\leq\ell
. Then

(i)

The set
\{{\bf z}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell n}:v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,n;B},\,k<n\leq\ell\}
, is a basis for the null-space of the matrix
A_{\ell,k;B}
.

(ii)

The matrix
A_{\ell,\leq k;B}
has the same row space as
A_{\ell,k;B}
and
{\rm rank}\,(A_{\ell,k;B})=R_{k}(B)
.
For
w\in V_{\ell,\leq k;B}
denote by
{\bf r}_{w}
the row of
A_{\ell,\leq k;B}
indexed
w
. Then the set

\{{\bf r}_{w}:w\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,\leq k;B}\}
is a basis for the row space of
A_{\ell,k;B}
.

Proof.

(i)

First we observe that
the vector
{\bf x}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell kn}
is the zero vector if and only if
k<n\leq\ell
.
Therefore, we have
\{{\bf z}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell n}:v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,n;B},\,k<n\leq\ell\}\subseteq{\rm null}(A_{\ell,k;B})
. Now, considering

{\rm null}(A_{\ell,k;B})\subseteq{\rm null}(A_{\ell,k;B}^{\top}A_{\ell,k;B})
and using Corollary
1
, we conclude that

{\rm null}(A_{\ell,k;B})=\{{\bf z}_{v^{\prime}}^{\ell n}:v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,n;B},\,k<n\leq\ell\}
, as required.

(ii)

Let
1\leq i\leq k
and
v\in V_{\ell,i-1;B}
. Let
v_{1}=g
, say
v=gs
with
s\in V_{\ell,i-1;B^{\prime\prime}},
where
B^{\prime\prime}=(b_{2},\ldots,b_{\ell})
. If
v=gs
is matchable with some
u\in\Sigma_{B}
, then
u
is matchable with exactly one of the words
0\,s,1\,s,\ldots,(b_{1}-1)\,s
; Otherwise
u
is matchable with none of these words. Thus we have

{\bf r}_{gs}=\sum_{i=0}^{b_{1}-1}{\bf r}_{is}

(9)

which gives the row indexed
v\in V_{\ell,i-1;B}
as the summation of some rows indexed by elements
v^{\prime}\in V_{\ell,i;B}
. Similar argument is true if we consider any gapped position of a word
v\in V_{\ell,i-1;B}
(instead of its first position), thus we conclude
{\rm row}(A_{\ell,i-1;B})\subseteq{\rm row}(A_{\ell,i;B})
for each
i
,
1\leq i\leq k
. Hence,
A_{\ell,\leq k;B}
has the same row space as
A_{\ell,k;B}
.
In Theorem
1
(ii), we obtained a complete set of non-zero eigenvalues of
A_{\ell,k;B}A_{\ell,k;B}^{\top}
which yields
{\rm rank}(A_{\ell,k;B}A_{\ell,k;B}^{\top})=|V^{\prime}_{\ell,\leq k;B}|=R_{k}(B)
.
Since
{\rm rank}(A_{\ell,k;B}A_{\ell,k;B}^{\top})
equals
{\rm rank}(A_{\ell,k;B})
, we obtain
{\rm rank}(A_{\ell,k;B})=R_{k}(B)
. To provide a combinatorial interpretation of this, we first rewrite the equation (
9
) in the form
{\bf r}_{0s}={\bf r}_{gs}-\sum_{i=1}^{b_{1}-1}{\bf r}_{is}
and then generalize this as below

{\bf r}_{0^{m}s}=\sum_{t\in\Gamma_{B^{\prime}}}(-1)^{m-|t|_{g}}{\bf r}_{ts},

(10)

where
0<m\leq\ell
,
B^{\prime}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})
,
\,\,B^{\prime\prime}=(b_{m+1},\ldots,b_{\ell})
and
s\in\Delta_{B^{\prime\prime}}
. The proof of (
10
) is by induction on
m
and left to the reader. When
s\in\Gamma_{B^{\prime\prime}}
, equation (
10
) gives the row indexed by the word
v=0^{m}s\in V_{\ell,\leq k;B}\setminus V^{\prime}_{\ell,\leq k;B}
as a linear combination of rows indexed by some words
v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,\leq k;B}
. By a proper permutation of the
0
positions of
v
, it is observed that the last statement is true for any word
v\in V_{\ell,\leq k;B}\setminus V^{\prime}_{\ell,\leq k;B}
regardless of its positions of zeros; Hence, the set
\{{\bf r}_{w}:w\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,\leq k;B}\}
is a generator for the row space of
A_{\ell,k;B}
. Now, using
{\rm rank}(A_{\ell,k;B})=R_{k}(B)
, we conclude that

\{{\bf r}_{w}:w\in V^{\prime}_{\ell,\leq k;B}\}
is a basis for the row space of
A_{\ell,k;B}
.

\Box

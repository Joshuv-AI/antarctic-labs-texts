Holomorphic Floer theory and Donaldson–Thomas invariants
Pierrick Bousseau

Abstract
. We present several expected properties of the holomorphic Floer theory of a holomorphic symplectic manifold. In particular, we propose a conjecture relating holomorphic Floer theory of Hitchin integrable systems and Donaldson-Thomas invariants of non-compact Calabi-Yau 3-folds. More generally, we conjecture that the BPS spectrum of a 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 4-dimensional quantum field theory can be recovered from the holomorphic Floer theory of the corresponding Seiberg-Witten integrable system.



0.
Introduction

The goal of this paper is to present an overview of the expected formal aspects of holomorphic Floer theory of
holomorphic symplectic manifolds and to propose a conjectural relation between holomorphic Floer theory and Donaldson-Thomas (DT) theory.

0.1.
Holomorphic Floer theory

Holomorphic Floer theory is an example of infinite-dimensional complex Morse theory. We first review the more familiar case of Floer theory as an example of infinite-dimensional Morse theory.

0.1.1.
Floer theory as Morse theory

Floer theory of a symplectic manifold

is formally the result of applying Morse theory to the
infinite-dimensional manifold
\mathcal{P}
of paths between pairs of Lagrangian submanifolds
L_{1},L_{2}\subset M

\displaystyle f:\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}

\displaystyle\longmapsto-\int_{D_{\mathfrak{p}}}\omega\,,

\mathfrak{p}

[

]

Critical points of

Moreover, the choice of a

Hence, the Morse complex of

More generally, counting

0.1.2.
Complex Morse theory

Whereas Morse theory deals with a real-valued smooth function on a Riemannian manifold, complex Morse theory considers a holomorphic function on a Kähler manifold.
This includes the Picard-Lefschetz theory of critical points and vanishing cycles of holomorphic functions, and also its categorification given by the Fukaya-Seidel category

The original definition of the Fukaya-Seidel category is based on the geometry of Lefschetz thimbles, but we will use a conjecturally equivalent alternative definition due to Haydys

0.2.
DT theory and holomorphic Floer theory

0.2.1.
DT theory and complex Morse theory

DT invariants are counts of stable objects in a 3-dimensional Calabi–Yau triangulated category, such as for example the derived category of coherent sheaves or the Fukaya category of a Calabi-Yau 3-fold. As a function of the stability, DT invariants jump across real codimension one loci in a way determined by a universal wall-crossing formula
[
50
,
64
]
.

Many of the algebraic structures underlying DT invariants and their wall-crossing are formally similar to the ones appearing in complex Morse theory for a holomorphic Morse function on a Kähler manifold. For example, the wall-crossing formula can be viewed as an iso-Stokes condition
[
18
,
49
]
, and the DT invariants naturally define Riemann-Hilbert problems and geometric structures formally similar to Frobenius manifold structures
[
15
,
16
]
. In this analogy, DT invariants play the role of counts of gradient flow lines in complex Morse theory.

Whereas complex Morse theory of a holomorphic Morse function with
n
critical points leads to algebraic structures based on the finite-dimensional Lie algebra

\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
of
n\times n
matrices, the algebraic structures coming from DT theory are based on the infinite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{DT}
of vector fields preserving a holomorphic symplectic form on a complex torus.
Nevertheless, it is natural to ask if this relation between DT theory and complex Morse theory theory is deeper than a mere analogy:

Question 0.1
.

Is DT theory actually an example of complex Morse theory?

Answering Question
0.1
means finding a Kähler manifold
\mathcal{P}
with a holomorphic function

W\colon\mathcal{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that a formal application of complex Morse theory to
(\mathcal{P},W)
reproduces DT theory.
In this context, a natural puzzle is to understand the origin of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{DT}
: as
\mathfrak{g}_{DT}\neq\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, a necessary condition is that
W
has infinitely many critical points.

Question
0.1
admits a natural reformulation in terms of physics. DT invariants can often be interpreted as counts of BPS states in
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric 4-dimensional quantum field theories or string theory compactifications. On the other hand, a holomorphic Morse function on a Kähler manifold
defines a Landau-Ginzburg
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
2-dimensional quantum field theory. In this context, the analogy
[
34
]
is that the wall-crossing formula for the BPS states of
\mathcal{N}=2
4-dimensional theories is formally similar to the Cecotti-Vafa wall-crossing formula for the BPS states in

\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
2-dimensional theories
[
20
]
. Therefore, answering Question
0.1
seems to require the construction of an appropriate
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
2-dimensional theory from a

\mathcal{N}=2
4-dimensional theory.

0.2.2.
DT invariants and holomorphic Floer theory of complex integrable systems

We will argue that holomorphic Floer theory gives a positive answer to Question
0.1
in the particular case of DT invariants describing the BPS spectrum of a
\mathcal{N}=2

4
-dimensional field theory (without dynamical gravity). This includes for example string compactifications on non-compact Calabi-Yau 3-folds, but not string compactifications on compact Calabi–Yau 3-folds.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a
\mathcal{N}=2
4-dimensional field theory. The low energy physics of
\mathcal{T}
is controlled by its Seiberg-Witten complex integrable system

\pi\colon M\longrightarrow B\,.

Here

is the Coulomb branch of
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and

is the Coulomb branch of

\mathcal{T}
on

is hyperkähler and the holomorphic symplectic form

for the complex structure

in which

is holomorphic. The fibers

over points

in the complement of the discriminant locus

of

are abelian varieties and holomorphic Lagrangian submanifolds of
(M,\Omega_{I})
.
The lattice of charges for states in the vacuum

is

\Gamma_{b}:=\pi_{2}(M,F_{b})

and for every

, we have a space

of BPS states of charge

[
76
,
77
,
78
,
34
,
37
]
.

In many cases, there is an associated 3-dimensional Calabi-Yau (CY3) triangulated category
\mathcal{C}
, the base

maps to the space of Bridgeland stability conditions on
\mathcal{C}

[
14
]
, the central charge of the stability corresponding to a point

is given by

Z_{\gamma}(b)=\int_{\gamma}\Omega_{I}\,,

and the spaces of BPS states

are mathematically realized as a cohomological DT invariants of

counting

\gamma
. A concrete example is provided by class

A_{1}
defined by a Riemann surface

(possibly with punctures)
[

,
37

. In this case,

is a moduli space of

is a 3-dimensional Calabi-Yau triangulated category described by a quiver with potential defined in terms of ideal triangulations of

[

Our main claim is that the DT theory of

for the stability

is equivalent to the holomorphic Floer theory of

\pi

Physically,

In other words, our proposed answer to Question

As

The holomorphic action functional

Conjecture 0.2

0.3.
Related works

While thinking about Question
0.1
in 2017, the author stumbled upon the idea to consider the Fukaya-Seidel of the holomorphic action functional and did the exercise to derive the corresponding Fueter equation.
Recently, Doan-Rezchikov
[
23
]
independently derived the 2-categorical picture of holomorphic Floer theory. More importantly, they started the analysis of the compactness properties of the Fueter equation.
Any attempt to rigorously discuss the conjectures of our paper should probably start with their work.

In his thesis

[
60
, §1.1.1]
Khan also considered the holomorphic area functional and derived the Fueter equation as the
corresponding
\zeta
-instanton equation. Another important part of this work is a systematical study of the 2d categorical wall-crossing
[
61
]
] and of the 2d categorical wall-crossing with “twisted masses”
[
60
, §3]
, which applies in particular to multivalued Landau-Ginzburg potential. In particular, formal aspects of the 4d wall-crossing appear in this context. From the point of view of our Conjecture
0.2
, this is not surprizing because we claim that the 4d wall-crossing is an example of 2d wall-crossing for the multivalued holomorphic action functional.
In particular, applying
[
60
, §3]
to the holomorphic area functional should help to make more precise the algebraic structures involved in Conjecture

0.2
.

The 2-categories attached to holomorphic symplectic varieties conjecturally enter in 3d mirror symmetry. In this context, the B-side is the Rozansky-Witten 2-category, based on the category of matrix factorizations. The A-side should be a gauged version of the Fueter category, based on the Fukaya-Seidel category
[
39
,
40
]
: concrete examples of the A-side in the literature rely on explicit algebraic models of the 2-category and it is an interesting problem to clarify the relation with the geometric model given by the Fueter 2-category.

Finally, Kontsevich-Soibelman have an ongoing work on holomorphic Floer theory
[
66
]
, not focused on the 2-categorical aspects related to the Fueter equation, but rather on the exploration of the 1-categorical level in many directions that we do not touch upon, such as the relation with deformation quantization or a general theory of resurgence, see §
2.7
.

2.
Holomorphic Floer theory

In this section, we introduce holomorphic Floer theory as an example of infinite dimensional complex Morse theory applied to the holomorphic action functional on the space of paths connecting two holomorphic Lagrangian submanifolds in a holomorphic symplectic variety. We only describe a conjectural picture ignoring all analytic difficulties. We refer to the work of Doan-Rezchikov
[
23
]

for first steps towards solving these analytic problems.

2.1.
Holomorphic symplectic geometry

Let
M
be a holomorphic symplectic manifold, with complex structure
I
and holomorphic symplectic form

\Omega_{I}
. We fix a compatible hyperkähler structure on
M
, that is a Riemannian metric
g
and complex structures
J
and
K
such that:

(1)

I
,
J
,
K
form a quaternionic triple, that is
IJK=-1
.

(2)

g
is Kähler with respect to
I
,
J
,
K
. We denote by

\omega_{I}:=g(I-,-)\,,\,\,\omega_{J}:=g(J-,-)\,,\,\,\omega_{K}:=g(K-,-)

the corresponding Kähler forms.

(3)

\Omega_{I}=\omega_{J}+i\omega_{K}
.

Given a hyperkähler structure as above, the metric
g
is Kähler with respect to any complex structure in the
twistor sphere
spanned by
I
,
J
,
K
, that is any complex structure of the form
aI+bJ+cK
with
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=1
. For any phase
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
,
|\zeta|=1
, we denote

J_{\zeta}:=(\mathrm{Re}\,\zeta)J+(\mathrm{Im}\,\zeta)K\,.

If
I
and
-I
are viewed as respectively the North and South poles of the twistor sphere, then the complex structures of the form
J_{\zeta}
with
|\zeta|=1
are exactly the complex structures on the Equator of the twistor sphere.
The Kähler form of the metric
g
for the complex structure
J_{\zeta}
is

(2.1)

\omega_{\zeta}:=\mathrm{Re}(\zeta^{-1}\Omega_{I})=(\mathrm{Re}\,\zeta)\omega_{J}+(\mathrm{Im}\,\zeta)\omega_{K}\,.

2.2.
The holomorphic action functional

Let

and

and

Assume that the intersection

Let

(2.2)

For every intersection point

As

containing all these constant paths sitting at the intersection points of

and

to

We fix an intersection point

\displaystyle(x,y)\longmapsto w_{x}(y)\,.

and one defines

As

is closed and

are

\displaystyle W\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{0}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{p},[w])

2.3.
Infinite-dimensional complex Morse theory

The holomorphic action functional

is a holomorphic function on the
infinite-dimensional Kähler manifold
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
.
The critical points of

are of the form

is an

to

intersect transversally.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{0}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow M

\displaystyle\longmapsto u(x,y)

The

Applying formally §

-holomorphic curves of class

2.7.

As reviewed in §

]

4.
DT invariants from holomorphic Floer theory

After an overview of the geometry of Seiberg-Witten integrable systems in §

4.3.
A physics derivation

In this section, we give a heuristic derivation of Conjecture
4.4
by compactification on a cigar geometry.
We start with the
\mathcal{N}=2
4d theory
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times D
, where
D
is a cigar geometry reviewed at the beginning of §
4.2
. As boundary condition at the end of the cigar, one imposes to the theory to approach the vacuum
b\in B\setminus\Delta
. Finally, we restrict this system to the sector of charge
\gamma\in\Gamma_{b}
and we look for the space
V
of half-supersymmetric states of lowest energy. As we assume that the system is in the vacuum
u
at the end of the cigar, non-trivial excitations should be localized near the tip of the cigar. But near the tip of the cigar,
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times D
looks like
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. By definition, BPS states of charge
\gamma
are the lowest energy half-supersymmetric states of the theory on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
in the sector of charge
\gamma
, and so one concludes that
V\simeq BPS_{\gamma}^{b}
. In this picture, the BPS particles are just sitting at the tip of the cigar.

Now we take the limit where the circle
S^{1}
in the cigar becomes very small. In this limit, the cigar
D
reduces to the interval
[0,1]
. The low-energy effective description of
\mathcal{T}
compactified on a circle is the
\mathcal{N}=4
3-dimensional sigma-model with target
M
. Therefore, our previous system reduces to a
\mathcal{N}=4
3d sigma model of target
M
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,1]
with appropriate boundary conditions at the ends of
[0,1]
.
At
r=0
, the boundary condition defined by the tip of the cigar is the section
S
, as reviewed in
4.2
. On the other hand, at
r=1
, the condition to be in the vacuum
b
translates into the boundary condition imposed by the fiber
F_{b}
.

In the limit where the interval
[0,1]
also becomes very small, we obtain a
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
2d sigma model on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with target the infinite-dimensional Kähler manifold
\mathcal{P}
of paths
[0,1]\rightarrow M
stretched between
S
and
F_{b}
.
The central charge

Z_{\gamma}(b)=\int_{\gamma}\Omega_{I}

in the original
\mathcal{N}=2
4d supersymmetry algebra induces a holomorphic superpotential on
\mathcal{P}

which is exactly the holomorphic action functional
W
.
Therefore, we obtain as low energy effective theory on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
the
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
2d Landau-Ginzburg model
(\mathcal{P},W)
. Moreover, the sector of charge
\gamma
is obtained by considering only a pair of critical point of
W
differing by
\gamma
. By definition of holomorphic Floer theory, the space
V
of lowest energy supersymmetric states of this system is exactly the space
H((p,0),(p,\gamma))
.

In conclusion, Conjecture
4.4
follows from comparing the
\mathcal{N}=2
4d theory
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times D
with the
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
2d effective low energy on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
describing the theory in the limit where the cigar
D
is small, see Figure

4.7

Figure 4.7.
Dimensional reduction of the cigar to an interval.

Remark 4.8
.

It is clear from this argument that the assumption that
\mathcal{T}
is a theory without dynamical theory is necessary. Indeed, a
\mathcal{N}=2
4d supergravity theory, such as a Type II string compactification on a compact Calabi-Yau 3-fold, contains BPS black holes, and it is not possible to place a black hole at the tip of the cigar without destroying the local geometry.

Remark 4.9
.

In the literature, compactification on a cigar geometry is often done in combination with a
\frac{1}{2}\Omega
-deformation along the
S^{1}
-rotations of the cigar
[
71
]
. It is essential for our purposes that the
\frac{1}{2}\Omega
-deformation parameter
\epsilon_{1}
is set to

. For
\epsilon_{1}=0
, the possible supersymmetric boundary conditions at the boundary of the cigar are described by
I
-holomorphic Lagrangian submanifolds ((B,A,A)-branes) of
M
, and so we can take the fiber
F_{b}
as boundary condition. By contrast, for
\epsilon_{1}\neq 0
, possible supersymmetric boundary conditions are
J
-holomorphic Lagrangian submanifolds ((A,B,A)-brane)
and so the fiber
F_{b}
cannot be used (correspondingly, the boundary condition corresponding to the tip of the cigar deforms the (B,A,A)-brane given by the section
S
to a (A,B,A)-brane given by the locus of opers for class S theories
[
71
]
). On a related note,
it would be interesting to understand precisely the relation with the limit
\epsilon_{1}\rightarrow 0
of the twistorial topological string of Cecotti-Neitzke-Vafa which is defined using
\epsilon_{1}\neq 0

[
19
]
. Finally, Balasubramanian-Teschner consider in

[
7
, §7.3]
a family of (A,B,A)-branes which in the limit
\epsilon_{1}\rightarrow 0
reduce to the (B,A,A)-branes given by the fibers
F_{b}
and so it would also be interesting to see if this could be used to give a continuous deformation of our set-up.

In this section, we formulate conjectures about the categories attached by holomorphic Floer theory to the pairs of holomorphic Lagrangian submanifolds

and
(S,S)
in the Seiberg-Witten integrable system

.
It follows from the definition in §

The Hom spaces in

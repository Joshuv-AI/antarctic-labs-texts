1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the regularity of the almost minimizers of a one-phase (horizontal)
p
-Bernoulli-type functional in Carnot groups of step two. We are interested in the regularity properties of almost minimizers associated with degenerate functionals in noncommutative structures, endowed by a rich geometry, as in the case of the Carnot groups of step
2
, started in
[
34
]
when
p=2
.

The classical motivation to study this type of free boundary problem arises from flame propagation and jet flows models
[
1
,
6
,
5
]
, and it is also related to shape optimization problems, see
[
9
]
.

In the Euclidean setting, regularity issues concerning minimizers of such functionals were introduced in the pioneering work of Alt and Caffarelli, see
[
3
]
. Further contributions to this topic appeared in
[
13
,
26
,
57
]
and we refer to
[
14
,
78
]
and the references within for a comprehensive overview of the subject. More recently, regularity of almost minimizes and their free boundaries were also studied in
[
24
,
21
]
. A different approach is proposed in
[
28
]
, based on nonvariational techniques.

The research about the nonlinear Euclidean setting of
p
-Bernoulli-type functional, ruled by
p
-Laplace operator, appeared in
[
31
]
, where the authors generalize the contribution introduced in
[
28
]
without applying mean value properties to
p
-harmonic functions. However, the tools introduced in
[
31
]
have been useful in
[
34
]
, facing the linear framework given by (horizontal) Bernoulli-type functional in Carnot groups of step 2, where smooth vector fields, generating the first algebra stratum, are involved. In fact, in this case, the norm of the intrinsic gradient of an (intrinsic) harmonic function is not an (intrinsic) subharmonic function, contrary to what happens in the Euclidean case.

Hence, in this paper, combing the approach of
[
34
]
and the strategy of
[
31
]
, we manage to prove the (intrinsic) Lipschitz continuous behaviour of almost minimizers of
p
-Bernoulli-type functionals in Carnot groups of step 2. Dealing with the nonlinear framework, we pay particular attention to the noncommutative setting we are considering, since due to the linearization argument employed, we need more general regularity estimates with respect to the ones applied in
[
34
]
.

The mathematical setting we need will be presented later on. We refer to Section
2
for the main notation and definitions. Nevertheless, in order to introduce the main result of the paper, we recall here the definition of
almost minimizer
, we deal with.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot Group of step 2. Assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
is a measurable domain and
p>1
is fixed. We define the following energy functional

(1.1)

J_{p}(u,\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)|^{p}+\chi_{\{u>0\}}(x)\Big)\,dx

for all
u\in HW^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
u\geqslant 0
a.e. in
\Omega
, where
\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u
is the so-called
horizontal gradient
of
u
.

The condition
u\geqslant 0
a.e. corresponds, in the context of free boundary problems, to consider
one-phase
problems (solutions which may change sign are related to
two-phase
problems).

In this paper, we focus on regularity properties for almost minimizers of
J_{p}
defined in (
1.1
). The notion of almost minimizers is well-known in the Calculus of Variations. In particular, the notion of almost minimizers associated with energy functionals was introduced by Anzellotti in
[
2
]
. Concerning Bernoulli-type energy functionals, almost minimizers have been studied in
[
24
,
22
,
23
,
28
]
in the Euclidean setting and in
[
34
]
within Carnot groups. In the setting of this work, the precise notion of almost minimizers for
J_{p}
we are dealing with is the following one.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\kappa\geqslant 0
,
\beta>0
be constants and let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{G}
be an open set. We say that
u\in HW^{1,p}(\Omega)
is an almost minimizer for
J_{p}
in
\Omega
, with constant
\kappa
and exponent
\beta
, if
u\geqslant 0

\mathcal{L}^{n}
-almost everywhere in
\Omega
and

(1.2)

J_{p}(u,B_{\varrho}(x))\leqslant(1+\kappa\varrho^{\beta})J_{p}(v,B_{\varrho}(x)),

for every (intrinsic) ball
B_{\varrho}(x)
such that
\overline{B_{\varrho}(x)}\subset\Omega
and for every
v\in HW^{1,p}(B_{\varrho}(x))
such that
u-v\in HW^{1,p}_{0}(B_{\varrho}(x))
.

As well as in
[
34
]
, the rough idea of the definition of almost minimizes is that, locally, the energy is not necessarily minimal among all competitors but
almost minimal
, in the sense that it cannot decrease by a factor more than
1+\kappa\varrho^{\beta}
, where
\varrho
is the scale of the localization. Thus, almost minimizers can be considered as perturbations of minimizers and such perturbations have a smaller contribution at small scales. In general, almost minimizers only satisfy a variational inequality but they are not solutions to some partial differential equations. Thus, the main problem in facing their regularity properties is the lack of monotonicity formulas, contrary to what usually happens to minimizers.

Now, we are in position to state our main regularity result. Here the integer
Q
is the
homogeneous dimension
of
\mathbb{G}
, see Section
2
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
p>p^{\#}:=\frac{2Q}{Q+2}
. Let
u
be an almost minimizer for
J_{p}
in
B_{1}
with constant
\kappa
and exponent
\beta
. Then,

\left\|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\leqslant C\Big(\left\|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}+1\Big),

where
C>0
is a constant depending on
\kappa
,
\beta
,
Q
and
p
.

In addition,
u
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous in a neighborhood of
\left\{u=0\right\}
, namely if
u(0)=0
then

\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u\right|\leqslant C\quad\mbox{in }B_{r_{0}},

for some
C>0
, depending only on
Q
,
p
,
\kappa
and
\beta
, and
r_{0}\in(0,1)
, depending on
Q
,
p
,
\kappa
,
\beta
and
\|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}
.

Hence, the main contribution of our paper concerns the intrinsic Lipschitz regularity of almost minimizers to (
1.1
). Such a result extends the regularity result proved in
[
34
]
, only obtained in the case of
p=2
.

In particular, to prove the intrinsic Lipschitz regularity of the almost minimizer stated in Theorem
1.2
, we have to face a double difficulty: the noncommutative structure of the geometry and the nonlinear form of the studied functional. More precisely, the vector fields generating the first stratum of the Lie algebra
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m_{1}}
, in general, have no trivial commutator, i.e.

\left[X_{i},X_{j}\right]\not=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad X_{i}X_{j}\not=X_{j}X_{i},\quad\text{for every }i,j\in\{1,\ldots,m_{1}\},\text{ if }i\not=j.

As a consequence, denoting by
\mathcal{L}
the positive sub-Laplacian on
\mathbb{G}
, it results

(1.3)

X_{i}\mathcal{L}\not=\mathcal{L}X_{i},

for all
i=1,\ldots,m_{1}
. The lack of commutativity, encoded in (
1.3
), already faced in the linear case discussed in
[
34
]
, translates into a lack of
\mathbb{G}
-harmonicity of functions
X_{i}u
, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,m_{1}\}
. Consequently, the approach adopted in
[
28
]
cannot be straightforwardly generalized. To overcome this problem, we apply some regularity estimates proved in
[
17
]
. In addition, in this paper, we have to deal also with the nonlinear behaviour of the
p
-sub-Laplacian associated with
p
-Bernoulli functional (
1.1
). In particular, regularity estimates of
[
17
]
, are no longer sufficient. To apply the linearization argument performed in
[
31
]
, regularity estimates for subelliptic equations with variable coefficients, as ones studied in
[
71
]
in the case of the Heisenberg group, are needed. Therefore, to generalize the results of
[
31
]
to the framework of the Carnot groups of step two, such regularity estimates represent a key point and their treatment appears in Section
5
. We apply techniques, introduced in
[
67
,
32
]
in the Euclidean setting, and then employed in
[
71
]
in the case of the Heisenberg group, based on comparison estimates of corresponding equations with frozen coefficient. However, as well as in
[
71
]
, in our subelliptic setting, extra terms coming from nonvanishing commutators of the vector fields generating the first algebra stratum appear. Therefore, estimates are not always as strong as those in the Euclidean setting but they are still enough for our purposes.

As we already point out in Theorem
1.2
, we deduce that almost minimizes
u
for
J_{p}
, in an open domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
, are locally intrinsic Lipschitz functions, i.e. there exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
x,y\in B\subset\subset\Omega

|u(x)-u(y)|\leqslant Cd_{c}(x,y),

where
d_{c}
is the Carnot-Charathéodory distance, see Definition
2.4
in the Section 2. However, this Lipschitz intrinsic regularity only implies Hölder continuous regularity, from the Euclidean point of view, since the Carnot-Charathéodory distance is not equivalent to the Euclidean one, see for instance
[
7
]
*Proposition 5.15.1.. More precisely, denoting by
d_{E}
the Euclidean distance on
\mathbb{G}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{G}
, there exists a constant
c_{K}>0
such that

(c_{K})^{-1}\,d_{E}(x,y)\leqslant d_{c}(x,y)\leqslant c_{K}\,d_{E}^{1/k}(x,y),

for all
x,y\in K
, where
k
is the step of the Carnot group. In this paper, we always consider the case of
k=2
.

We also remark that the approach to the regularity theory of free boundary problems, based on monotonicity formulas, is well understood in the Euclidean setting. For example, it is well known that the so-called Alt-Caffarelli-Fredmann monotonicity formula is one of the key tools used to prove Lipschitz continuity of minimizer to two-phase problems, see
[
4
]
and also
[
14
]
. Nevertheless, these techniques appear difficult to extend to the noncommutative setting of Carnot groups. Indeed, as recently proved in
[
33
,
35
]
, an intrinsic Alt-Caffarelli-Friedman monotonicity formula, written as the natural counterpart to the classical Euclidean one, seems to fail. Furthermore, the viscosity approach developed in the nonlinear framework, see for instance
[
20
,
60
,
36
,
37
]
, appears more challenging due to the theoretical existence of characteristic points on the free boundary. Nevertheless, with our approach, we overcome these difficulties without relying on monotonicity formulas by dealing with almost minimizers inspired by the
[
28
,
31
,
34
]
, where Lipschitz regularity has been showed.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the main notation and tools we rely on all the paper. Then in Section
3
we deal with the
(\mathbb{G},p)
-harmonic proving some estimates you use in our argument. In Section
4
we prove some of the key tools of the proof associated with a dichotomy procedure of
[
28
]
. Next, in Section
5
we study some regularity results concerning subellipic equations with variable coefficient, this is a key step to perform a linearization argument as in
[
31
]
. Section
6
concludes the dichotomy argument, here we present an
improvement
of the result in Section
4
using the regularity estimated obtained in Section
5
. Finally, in Section
7
we conclude with the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Notation and preliminary results

2.1.
Carnot Groups

A connected and simply connected Lie group
(\mathbb{G},\cdot)
(in general non-abelian) is said to be a
Carnot group of step
k
if its Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits a
k
-step stratification
, i.e. there exist linear subspaces
V_{1},...,V_{k}
such that

(2.1)

{\mathfrak{g}}=V_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus V_{k},\quad[V_{1},V_{i}]=V_{i+1},\quad V_{k}\neq\{0\},\quad V_{i}=\{0\}{\textrm{ if }}i>k,

where
[V_{1},V_{i}]
is the subspace of
{\mathfrak{g}}
generated by the commutators
[X,Y]
with
X\in V_{1}
and
Y\in V_{i}
. The first layer
V_{1}
, the so-called
horizontal layer
, plays a key role in the theory since it generates all the algebra
\mathfrak{g}
by commutation. We point out that a stratified Lie algebra can admit more than one stratification, however, the stratification turns out to be unique up to isomorphisms, therefore, the related Carnot group structure is essentially unique (see
[
59
]
*Proposition 1.17). Note that when
k=1
the group
\mathbb{G}
is Abelian, we return to the Euclidean situation.

Setting
m_{i}=\dim(V_{i})
and
h_{i}=m_{1}+\dots+m_{i}
, with
h_{0}=0
, for
i=1,\dots,k
(so that
h_{k}=n=\dim\mathfrak{g}=\dim\mathbb{G}
), we choose a basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
of
\mathfrak{g}
adapted to the stratification , that is such that

e_{h_{j-1}+1},\ldots,e_{h_{j}}\;\text{is a basis of}\;V_{j}\;\text{for each}\;j=1,\ldots,k.

Let
X=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}
be the family of left-invariant vector fields such that
X_{i}(e)=e_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, where
e
is the identity of
(\mathbb{G},\cdot)
. Thanks to (
2.1
), the subfamily
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{m_{1}}\}
generates by commutations all the other vector fields, we will refer to
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m_{1}}
as
generating vector fields
of
(\mathbb{G},\cdot)
.

The map
X\mapsto X(e)
, that associated with a left-invariant vector field
X
its value in
e
, defines an isomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}
to
T\mathbb{G}_{e}
(in turn identified with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
). We systematically use these identifications. Furthermore, by the assumption that
\mathbb{G}
be simply connected, the exponential map is a global real-analytic diffeomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}
onto
\mathbb{G}
(see e.g.
[
77
,
18
]
), so any
x\in\mathbb{G}
can be written in a unique way as
x=\exp(x_{1}X_{1}+\dots+x_{n}X_{n})
. Using these
exponential coordinates
, we identify
x
with the
n
-tuple
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and we identify
\mathbb{G}
with
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdot)
where the explicit expression of the group operation
\cdot
is determined by the Campbell-Hausdorff formula (see e.g.
[
7
,
40
]
). In this coordinates
e=(0,\dots,0)
and
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})^{-1}=(-x_{1},\dots,-x_{n})
, and the adjoint operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
of
X_{j}
,
X_{j}^{*}
,
j=1,\dots,n
, turns out to be
-X_{j}
(see, for instance,
[
46
]
*Proposition 2.2). Moreover, if
x\in\mathbb{G}
and
i=1,\ldots,k
, we set
x^{(i)}:=(x_{h_{i-1}+1},\ldots,x_{h_{i}})\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
, so that we can also identify
x
with
[x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(k)}]\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{k}}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Two important families of automorphism of
\mathbb{G}
are the so-called
intrinsic translations
and the
intrinsic dilations
of
\mathbb{G}
. For any
x\in\mathbb{G}
, the
(left) translation

\tau_{x}:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
is defined as

z\mapsto\tau_{x}z:=x\cdot z.

For any
\lambda>0
, the
dilation

\delta_{\lambda}:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
, is defined as

(2.2)

\delta_{\lambda}(x_{1},...,x_{n})=(\lambda^{\alpha_{1}}x_{1},...,\lambda^{\alpha_{n}}x_{n}),

where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
is called
homogeneity of the variable

x_{i}
in
\mathbb{G}
(see
[
40
]
Chapter 1) and is defined as

(2.3)

\alpha_{j}=i\quad\text{whenever}\;h_{i-1}+1\leqslant j\leqslant h_{i},

hence
1=\alpha_{1}=...=\alpha_{m}<\alpha_{m+1}=2\leqslant...\leqslant\alpha_{n}=k.

From the definition (
2.2
), one easily verifies the following properties of intrinsic dilations.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
\lambda,\mu>0
one has:

(1)

\delta_{1}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{G}}
;

(2)

\delta^{-1}_{\lambda}=\delta_{\lambda^{-1}}
;

(3)

\delta_{\lambda}\circ\delta_{\mu}=\delta_{\lambda\mu}
;

(4)

for every
p,p^{\prime}\in\mathbb{G}
one has
\delta_{\lambda}(p)\circ\delta_{\lambda}(p^{\prime})=\delta_{\lambda}(p\cdot p^{\prime})
.

By left translation, the horizontal layer defines a subbundle of the tangent bundle
T\mathbb{G}
over
\mathbb{G}
: the subbundle of the tangent bundle spanned by the family of vector fields
X=(X_{1},\dots,X_{m_{1}})
plays a crucial role in the theory, it is called the
horizontal bundle

H\mathbb{G}
; the fibres of
H\mathbb{G}
are

H\mathbb{G}_{x}=\mbox{span }\{X_{1}(x),\dots,X_{m_{1}}(x)\},\qquad x\in\mathbb{G}.

A subriemannian structure is defined on
\mathbb{G}
, endowing each fibre of
H\mathbb{G}
with a scalar product
\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\rangle_{x}
and with a norm
|\cdot|_{x}
that make the basis
X_{1}(x),\dots,X_{m_{1}}(x)
an orthonormal basis. That is if
v=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}v_{i}X_{i}(x)=(v_{1},\dots,v_{m_{1}})
and
w=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}w_{i}X_{i}(x)=(w_{1},\dots,w_{m_{1}})
are in
H\mathbb{G}_{x}
, then
\langle{v},{w}\rangle_{x}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}v_{j}w_{j}
and
|v|_{x}^{2}:=\langle{v},{v}\rangle_{x}
.

The sections of
H\mathbb{G}
are called
horizontal sections
, and for any
x\in\mathbb{G}
, a vector of
H\mathbb{G}_{x}
is an
horizontal vector
while any vector in
T\mathbb{G}_{x}
that is not horizontal is a vertical vector. Each horizontal section is identified by its canonical coordinates with respect to this moving frame
X_{1}(x),\dots,X_{m_{1}}(x)
. This way, an horizontal section
\varphi
is identified with a function
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{m_{1}}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
. When dealing with two horizontal sections
\phi
and
\psi
, we drop the index
x
in the scalar product and in the norm.

We collect in the following results some properties of the group operation and of the canonical vector fields, see
[
7
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The group operation has the following form

x\cdot y=x+y+\mathcal{Q}(x,y),\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{G}

where
\mathcal{Q}=(\mathcal{Q}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Q}_{n}):\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}

and every
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
, is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\alpha_{i}
respecting the intrinsic dilations of
\mathbb{G}
, that is

\mathcal{Q}_{i}(\delta_{\lambda}x,\delta_{\lambda}y)=\lambda^{\alpha_{i}}\mathcal{Q}_{i}(x,y)\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{G}\quad\text{and}\quad\text{for }\lambda>0.

Moreover, for all
x,y\in\mathbb{G}
we have

(i)

\mathcal{Q}
is antisymmetric, that is

\mathcal{Q}_{i}(x,y)=-\mathcal{Q}_{i}(-y,-x),\quad\text{for }i=1,\ldots,n.

(ii)

\mathcal{Q}_{1}(x,y)=\ldots=\mathcal{Q}_{m_{1}}(y,x)=0

\mathcal{Q}_{i}(x,0)=\mathcal{Q}_{i}(0,y)=0\text{ and }\mathcal{Q}_{i}(x,x)=\mathcal{Q}_{i}(x,-x)=0,\quad\text{ for }m_{1}<i\leqslant n

\mathcal{Q}_{i}(x,y)=\mathcal{Q}_{i}(x_{1},,x_{{h_{j}}-1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{{h_{j}}-1}),\quad\text{ if }1<j\leqslant k\text{ and }i\leqslant h_{j}

(iii)

\mathcal{Q}_{i}(x,y)=\sum_{k,h}{\mathcal{R}_{h,k}^{i}(x,y)(x_{k}y_{h}-x_{h}y_{k})}

where the functions
\mathcal{R}_{h,k}^{i}
are homogeneous polynomials of degree
\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{h}
which respect the intrinsic dilations and the sum is extended to all
h
and
k
such that
\alpha_{h}+\alpha_{k}\leqslant\alpha_{i}
.

The following result is contained in
[
46
]
*Proposition 2.2.

Proposition 2.3
.

The vector fields
X_{j}
have polynomial coefficients and are of the form

(2.4)

X_{j}(x)=\partial_{j}+\sum_{i>h_{l}}^{n}{q_{i,j}(x)\partial_{i}},\quad\text{ for }\>j=1,\ldots,n\>\>\text{ and }\>\>j\leqslant h_{l}

where
q_{i,j}(x)=\frac{\partial\mathcal{Q}_{i}}{\partial y_{j}}(x,y)\rvert_{y=0}
(the
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
are those defined in the proposition
2.2
) for
h_{l-1}<j\leqslant h_{l}
and
1\leqslant l\leqslant k
; so if
h_{l-1}<j\leqslant h_{l}
then
q_{i,j}(x)=q_{i,j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{h_{l-1}})
and
q_{i,j}(0)=0
.

2.2.
Intrinsic distance and gauge pseudo-distance

An absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{G}
is called
sub-unitary
with respect to
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m_{1}}
if it is a
horizontal curve
, that is, if there exist real measurable functions
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m_{1}}:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\dot{\gamma}(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}{c_{j}(s)X_{j}(\gamma(s))},\quad\text{for $\mathcal{L}^{1}$ -a.e.}s\in[0,T]

with
\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}{c_{j}(s)^{2}}\leqslant 1
, for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.
s\in[0,T]
.

Definition 2.4

(Carnot-Carathéodory distance)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group. For
p,q\in\mathbb{G}
we define their
Carnot-Carathéodory distance

d_{c}(p,q)
as

d_{c}(p,q):=\mathrm{inf}\{T>0:\text{it exists an sub-unitary curve }\gamma\text{ with },\>\gamma(0)=p,\>\gamma(T)=q\}.

The last definition is well-placed: the set of sub-unitary curves connecting
p
and
q
is non-empty, by Chow’s theorem (
[
7
]
*Theorem 19.1.3), since by (
2.1
), the rank of the Lie algebra generated by
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m_{1}}
is
n
; hence,
d_{c}
is a distance on
\mathbb{G}
inducing the same topology as the standard Euclidean distance. We shall denote by
B_{r}(p)
the open balls, centred in
p
of radius
r>0
, associated with the distance
d_{c}
. For the sake of simplicity, if
p=e
we will use the notation
B_{r}(e)=B_{r}
.

The Carnot-Carathéodory metric
d_{c}
is equivalent to a more explicitly defined pseudo-distance, called the
gauge pseudo-distance
, defined as follows. Let
||\cdot||
denote the Euclidean distance to the origin in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. For
u=u_{1}+\cdots+u_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}
with
u_{i}\in V_{i}
, one defines

(2.5)

|u|_{\mathfrak{g}}:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}||u_{i}||^{2r!/i}\right)^{\frac{1}{2r!}}.

The
non-isotropic gauge
in
\mathbb{G}
is defined by letting

(2.6)

|p|_{\mathbb{G}}=|\exp^{-1}p|_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\quad\ \ \ \ \ p\in\mathbb{G},

see
[
39
]
and
[
40
]
. Since the exponential mapping
\exp:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{G}
is of class
C^{\infty}
(actually analitic) diffeomorphism, the maps
p\to|p|_{\mathbb{G}}
is
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}\setminus\{e\})
.
Notice that from (
2.5
) and (
2.2
) we have for any
\lambda>0

(2.7)

|\delta_{\lambda}(p)|_{\mathbb{G}}=\lambda|p|_{\mathbb{G}}.

The
gauge pseudo-distance
in
\mathbb{G}
is defined by

(2.8)

d(p,q):=|p^{-1}\cdot q|_{\mathbb{G}}.

The function
d
has all the properties of a distance, except symmetry and the triangle inequality, which is satisfied with a universal constant, usually different from one on the right-hand side, see
[
40
,
7
]
. Since the dilatations are group automorphisms, from (
2.8
) and (
2.7
), it follows that
d
id homogeneous of degree one with respect to the group dilatation, that is for any
\lambda>0

(2.9)

d(\delta_{\lambda}(p),\delta_{\lambda}(p^{\prime}))=\lambda d(p,p^{\prime}).

It is well known, see for instance Proposition 5.1.4 in
[
7
]
, that there exist universal constants
\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}
such that for
p\in\mathbb{G}
one has

(2.10)

\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}^{-1}|p|_{\mathbb{G}}\leqslant d_{c}(e,p)\leqslant\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}|p|_{\mathbb{G}}.

The integer

Q:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\alpha_{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{i\text{dim}(V_{i})}

is the
homogeneous dimension
of
\mathbb{G}
. It is the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbb{G}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to the distance
d_{c}
, see
[
68
]
.

The
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{n}
, is the Haar measure of the group
\mathbb{G}
that is, for every
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{G}
and for each
x\in\mathbb{G}
it results
\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(x\cdot E\right)=\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(E\right)
. Moreover, if
\lambda>0
then
\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(\delta_{\lambda}\left(E\right)\right)=\lambda^{Q}\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(E\right)
. In particular, for any
r>0
and any
p\in\mathbb{G}
, it holds

\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(B_{r}(p)\right)=r^{Q}\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(B_{1}(p)\right)=r^{Q}\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(B_{1}\right)

where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension.

All the spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{G})
that we will consider are defined with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{n}
. If
A\subset\mathbb{G}
is
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-measurable, we write
|A|=\mathcal{L}^{n}(A)
.

A map
L:\mathbb{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
\mathbb{G}
-linear
if it is a group homeomorphism from
\mathbb{G}\equiv(\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdot)
to
(\mathbb{R},+)
and if it is homogeneous of degree 1 with respect to the intrinsic dilations of
\mathbb{G}
, that is
L(\delta_{\lambda}x)=\lambda Lx
for
\lambda>0
and
x\in\mathbb{G}
. Similarly, we say that a map
\ell:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\mathbb{G}
-affine if there exists a linear map and
L
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\ell(x)=L(x)+c
, for every
x\in\mathbb{G}
.

Given a basis
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
, all
\mathbb{G}
-linear maps are represented as follows.

Proposition 2.5
.

A map
L:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\mathbb{G}
-linear if and only if there is
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m_{1}})\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
such that, if
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{G}
, then

L(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}a_{i}x_{i}.

Moreover, if
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{G}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{G}
are given, we set

\pi_{x_{0}}(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}x_{j}X_{j}(x_{0}).

2.3.
Folland-Stein and horizontal Sobolev classes

Fixed
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{G}
, the action of vector fields
X_{j}
, with
j=1,\ldots,m_{1}
on a function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is specified by the Lie derivative: we say that a function
f
is differentiable along direction
X_{j}
, for
j=1,\ldots,m_{1}
, in
x_{0}\in\mathbb{G}
if the map
\lambda\mapsto f(\tau_{x_{0}}(\delta_{\lambda}(e_{j}))
is differentiable in
\lambda=0
, where
e_{j}
is the
j
-th vector of the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
. In this case, we will write

X_{j}f(x_{0})=\left.\frac{d}{d\lambda}f(\tau_{x_{0}}(\delta_{\lambda}e_{j}))\right|_{\lambda=0}.

If instead
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
,
X_{j}f
exists in a weak sense, if

\int_{\Omega}{fX_{j}\varphi}\>d\mathcal{L}^{n}=-\int_{\Omega}{\varphi X_{j}f}\>d\mathcal{L}^{n}

for each
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Once a basis
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m_{1}}
of the horizontal layer is fixed we define, for any function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
for which the partial derivatives
X_{j}f
exist, the horizontal gradient of
f
, denoted by
\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}f
, is defined as the horizontal section

(2.11)

\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}f:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(X_{i}f)X_{i},

whose coordinates are
(X_{1}f,\ldots,X_{m_{1}}f)
. Moreover, if
\phi=(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{m_{1}})
is an horizontal section such that
X_{j}\phi_{j}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{G})
for
j=1,\dots,m_{1}
, we define
\mathrm{div}_{{\mathbb{G}}}\phi
as the real-valued function

(2.12)

\mathrm{div}_{{\mathbb{G}}}\phi:=-\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}X_{j}^{*}\phi_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}X_{j}\phi_{j}.

The positive
sub-Laplacian
operator on
\mathbb{G}
is the second-order differential operator, given by

\mathcal{L}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}X^{*}_{j}X_{j}=-\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}X_{j}^{2}.

It is easy to see that

\mathcal{L}u=-\mathrm{div}_{{\mathbb{G}}}(\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u).

The operator
\mathcal{L}
is left-invariant with respect to group translations and homogeneous of degree two with respect to group dilatations, i.e. for any
x\in\mathbb{G}
and
\lambda>0
we have

\mathcal{L}(u\circ\tau_{x})=(\mathcal{L}u)\circ\tau_{x},\qquad\mathcal{L}(\delta_{\lambda}u)=\lambda^{2}\delta_{\lambda}(\mathcal{L}u).

Furthermore, by the assumptions
2.1
, the system
\{X_{1},...,X_{m_{1}}\}
satisfies the finite rank condition

\text{rank Lie}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m_{1}}]=n,

therefore by Hörmander’s theorem
[
55
]
the operator
\mathcal{L}
is hypoelliptic. However, when the step
k
of
\mathbb{G}
is greater than one, the operator
\mathcal{L}
fails to be elliptic at every point of
\mathbb{G}
.

More in general, given
1<p<\infty
we consider the quasilinear operator, known as the
p
-sub-Laplacian
, defined by

(2.13)

\mathcal{L}_{p}u:=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}X_{j}(|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u|^{p-2}X_{j}u).

The Hölder classes
C^{k,\alpha}
has been introduced by Folland and Stein, see
[
39
,
40
]
. The functions in these classes are Hölder continuous with respect to the metric
d_{c}

Definition 2.6

(Folland-Stein classes)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{G}
an open set and
0<\alpha\leqslant 1
and
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
a function. We say that

u\in C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)
if there exists a constant
M>0
, such that

|u(x)-u(y)|\leqslant M\,d_{c}(x,y)^{\alpha},\qquad\text{for every }\ x,y\in\Omega.

Defining the Hölder seminorm of
u\in C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)
as

(2.14)

[u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)}:=\underset{\underset{x\neq y}{x,y\in\Omega}}{\sup}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{d_{c}(x,y)^{\alpha}},

the space
C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)
is a Banach space with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+[u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega).}

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the spaces
C^{k,\alpha}(\Omega)
are defined inductively as follows: we say that
u\in C^{k,\alpha}(\Omega)
if
X_{i}u\in C^{k-1,\alpha}(\Omega)
for every
i=1,\ldots,m_{1}
.

In the following, we will place with
C^{k,\lambda}(\Omega,H\mathbb{G})
the space of all the horizontal sections
\phi:\Omega\to H\mathbb{G}
with
\phi:=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{m_{1}})
, such that
\phi_{j}\in C^{k,\lambda}(\Omega)
, for
j=1,\ldots,m_{1}
. While if
u\in C^{k,\alpha}(\Omega^{\prime})
, for any
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
we will set
u\in C^{k,\alpha}_{loc}(\Omega)
.

Finally, note that the class
C^{0,1}(\Omega)
coincides with the class of Lipschitz continuous function on
\Omega
with respect to the metric
d_{c}
.

Definition 2.7

(Horizontal Sobolev spaces)
.

Given an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{G}
, a function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
and
1\leqslant p<\infty
, the Horizontal Sobolev spaces are defined as

HW^{1,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega):\ X_{j}u\in L^{p}(\Omega),\ \text{for all }\,j=1,\ldots,m_{1}\right\},

which is a Banach space with the norm

\|u\|_{HW^{1,p}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,H\mathbb{G})}.

The subspace
HW^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
of
HW^{1,p}(\Omega)
are defined as the closure with respect the norm
\|\cdot\|_{HW^{1,p}(\Omega)}
of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Definition 2.8

(Morrey-Campanato spaces)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{G}
. For every
1\leqslant p<+\infty
and
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)
, an horizontal section
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m_{1}})\in L^{p}_{loc}(\Omega,H\mathbb{G})
, is said to be in the Morrey-Campanato space
\mathcal{E}^{\lambda,\,p}(\Omega,H\mathbb{G})
if

[f]_{\mathcal{E}^{\lambda,\,p}(\Omega,H\mathbb{G})}:=\sup_{B\subset\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{|B|^{1+p\lambda}}\int_{B}|f(y)-\mathbf{f_{B}}(y)|^{p}\,dy\right)^{1/p}<+\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B\subset\Omega
and
\mathbf{f_{B}}
is the constant horizontal section given by

\mathbf{f_{B}}(y):=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}\left(\fint_{B}f_{i}(z)\,dz\right)X_{i}(y),\quad\text{for }y\in\Omega.

Remark 2.9
.

For every
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m_{1}})\in\mathcal{E}^{\lambda,p}(\Omega,H\mathbb{G})
, the quantity
[u]_{\mathcal{E}^{p,\lambda}(\Omega,H\mathbb{G})}
is a seminorm in
\mathcal{E}^{\lambda,p}(\Omega,H\mathbb{G})
and it is equivalent to the quantity

(2.15)

\sup_{B\subset\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{|B|^{1+p\lambda}}\inf_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\int_{B}\left|u(x)-\boldsymbol{\xi}(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p},

where the infimum is taken over all the constant horizontal sections such that

(2.16)

\boldsymbol{\xi}(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}\xi_{i}X_{i}(x),\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{G}\text{ and some }\xi:=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m_{1}})\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}.

For the proof we refer to Remark 3.6 in
[
34
]
.

Theorem 2.10
.

(
[
65
]
*Theorem 5)

For every
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{G}
,
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
one has

\mathcal{E}^{\lambda,p}(\Omega,H\mathbb{G})=C^{0,\lambda}(\Omega,H\mathbb{G}).

More precisely, it results that a function
\phi
belongs to
\mathcal{E}^{\lambda,p}(\Omega,H\mathbb{G})
if and only if
\phi
is equal
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-almost everywhere to a function
\psi
with belongs to the Hölder class
C^{0,\lambda}(\Omega,H\mathbb{G})
. Moreover the seminorms
[\phi]_{\mathcal{E}^{\lambda,p}(\Omega,H\mathbb{G})}
and
[\psi]_{C^{0,\lambda}(\Omega,H\mathbb{G})}
are equivalent.

3.
The
(\mathbb{G},p)
-harmonic replacement

In this section, we recall the notion of
(\mathbb{G},p)
-harmonic replacement.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{G}
an open set and
u\in HW^{1,p}(\Omega)
. We say that
v\in HW^{1,p}(\Omega)
is the
(\mathbb{G},p)
-harmonic replacement of
u
in
\Omega
if

(3.1)

\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v\right|^{p}\,dx=\min_{u-w\in HW^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}w\right|^{p}\,dx.

In particular, if
v
is the
(\mathbb{G},p)
-harmonic replacement of
u
in
\Omega
, it satisfies the critical condition

(3.2)

\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p-2}\left\langle\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x),\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}\varphi(x)\right\rangle\,dx=0

for all
\varphi\in HW^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, i.e.
v
is the unique weak solution for the Dirichlet problem

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{matrix}\mathcal{L}_{p}v=0\qquad\text{in }\Omega\vskip 2.84544pt\\
u-v\in HW^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\end{matrix}\right.

In the following Lemma, we provide some energy estimates for the
(\mathbb{G},p)
-harmonic replacement, which will be useful in the sequel.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{G}
open and
u\in HW^{1,p}(\Omega)
.
Let
v
be the
(\mathbb{G},p)
-harmonic replacement of
u
in
\Omega
.
Then,

(
i
)

if
1<p<2,
then

(3.4)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\right)\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|\big)^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}},\end{split}

for some positive constant
C
depending only on
\mathbb{G}
and
p
;

(
ii
)

if
p\geqslant 2,
then

(3.5)

\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant C\int_{\Omega}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\big)\,dx,

for some positive constant
C
depending only on
\mathbb{G}
and
p
.

Proof.

For all
s\in[0,1]
, we consider the family of functions
u_{s}(x):=su(x)+(1-s)v(x)
, so that
u_{0}=v
and
u_{1}=u
. As a consequence,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x)\right|^{p}\,ds\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{0}^{1}p\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x)\right|^{p-2}\left\langle\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x),\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}(u-v)(x)\right\rangle\,ds\right)\,dx.

Since
u-v\in HW^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, using it as test function in (
3.2
), by Fubini’s Theorem we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle=\;p\,\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x)\right|^{p-2}\left\langle\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x),\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}(u-v)(x)\right\rangle\,ds\right)\,dx\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.-\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p-2}\left\langle\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x),\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}(u-v)(x)\right\rangle\,dx\right)\,ds\right]

\displaystyle=\;p\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\int_{\Omega\cap A}\left\langle\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x)\right|^{p-2}\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x)-\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p-2}\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x),\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}(u-v)(x)\right\rangle\,dx\bigg)\,ds,

where
A=\left\{x\in\Omega:\;|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)|>0\right\}.

Then, recalling that,

(3.6)

u_{s}(x)-v(x)=s(u(x)-v(x))\qquad\text{for all }x\in\Omega,

it results

(3.7)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\Big(&\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\Big)\,dx\\
=\;&p\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{s}\bigg(\int_{\Omega\cap A}\left\langle\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x)\right|^{p-2}\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x)-\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p-2}\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x),\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}(u_{s}-v)(x)\right\rangle\,dx\bigg)\,ds.\end{split}

By apply some well-known inequalities (see e.g. Section 3 of
[
20
]
or Lemma 2.1 in
[
19
]
), for any horizontal sections
\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\zeta}:\mathbb{G}\to H\mathbb{G}
such that
|\boldsymbol{\xi}|+|\boldsymbol{\zeta}|>0
in
\mathbb{G}
any for any
1<p<\infty
we have

(3.8)

\left\langle\left|\boldsymbol{\xi}\right|^{p-2}\boldsymbol{\xi}-\left|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right|^{p-2}\boldsymbol{\zeta},\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right\rangle\geqslant\gamma\left|\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right|^{2}\left(\left|\boldsymbol{\xi}\right|+\left|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right|\right)^{p-2}.

for some positive constant
\gamma
depending only on
\mathbb{G}
and
p
. Moreover, for
p\geqslant 2
, for any horizontal sections
\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\zeta}:\mathbb{G}\to H\mathbb{G}

(3.9)

\left\langle\left|\boldsymbol{\xi}\right|^{p-2}\boldsymbol{\xi}-\left|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right|^{p-2}\boldsymbol{\zeta},\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right\rangle\geqslant\gamma\left|\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right|^{p}

for some positive constant
\gamma
of (
3.8
).

Step 1: the case
1<p<2
.
At first, we notice that we can apply inequalities (
3.8
) with the choices
\boldsymbol{\xi}:=\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u
and
\boldsymbol{\zeta}:=\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v
on
A
. Indeed, by (
3.6
), for any
x\in A
, it results

|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x)|+|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)|\geqslant|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)|=s|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)|>0.

Thus, by inequality (
3.8
) and (
3.7
), we obtain that

(3.10)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\Big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\Big)\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad\geqslant\;p\,\gamma\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{s}\left(\int_{\Omega\cap A}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{2}(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|)^{p-2}\,dx\right)\,ds\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad=\;p\,\gamma\int_{0}^{1}{s}\left(\int_{\Omega\cap A}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{2}(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|)^{p-2}\,dx\right)\,ds\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad=\;p\,\gamma\int_{0}^{1}{s}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{2}(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|)^{p-2}\,dx\right)\,ds,\end{split}

where in the first equality we used the identity in (
3.6
).

Furthermore, recalling that
s\in[0,1]
, for any
x\in\Omega
we have that

\begin{split}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|&\leqslant s\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|+(1-s)\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|\\
&=s\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|+(2-s)\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|\\
&\leqslant 2\,\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|\big),\end{split}

which together with (
3.10
), leads to

(3.11)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\Big(&\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\Big)\,dx\\
&\geqslant\;p\,\gamma\,2^{p-2}\int_{0}^{1}{s}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{2}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|\big)^{p-2}\,dx\right)\,ds\\
&=\;p\,\gamma\,2^{p-3}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{2}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|\big)^{p-2}\,dx.\end{split}

Moreover, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\;\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|\big)^{\frac{p(p-2)}{2}}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|\big)^{-\frac{p(p-2)}{2}}\,dx

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leqslant\;\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{2}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|\big)^{p-2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\cdot\left(\int_{\Omega}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|\big)^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}.

where in last inequality we apply the Hölder’s inequality with Hölder exponent
2/p
and conjugate exponent

\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{2/p}{2/p-1}=\frac{2}{2-p}.

Then using estimates (
3.11
) for the first term of the right hand side of (
3.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\;C\left(\int_{\Omega}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\big)\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|\big)^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}},

for some suitable constant
C>0
, depending only on
\mathbb{G}
and
p
. This proves concludes the proof of (
3.4
) when
1<p<2
.

Step 2: the case
p\geqslant 2
.
Applying inequalities (
3.9
) with the choices
\boldsymbol{\xi}:=\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u
and
\boldsymbol{\zeta}:=\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v
and using estimate (
3.7
), we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\Big)\,dx&\geqslant\;p\,\gamma\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{s}\left(\int_{\Omega\cap A}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{s}(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)\,ds\\
&=p\,\gamma\int_{0}^{1}{s}^{p-1}\left(\int_{\Omega\cap A}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)\,ds,\\
&=p\,\gamma\int_{0}^{1}{s}^{p-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)\,ds,\\
&=\gamma\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx,\end{split}

where in the first equality we used the identity in (
3.6
). This establishes (
3.5
) when
p\geqslant 2
and completes the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

4.
A Dichotomy result

As a first step, we a prove a
dichotomy
result. Roughly speaking, two situations can occur: either the average of the energy of an almost minimizer decreases in a smaller ball, or the distance of its horizontal gradient and a suitable constant horizontal section becomes as small as we wish. This implies that
\mathbb{G}
-affine functions are the only ones for which the average does not improve in small balls.

Our first result is the following:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
u\in HW^{1,p}(B_{1})
be such that

(4.1)

J_{p}(u,B_{1})\leqslant(1+\sigma)J_{p}(v,B_{1})

for all
v\in HW^{1,p}(B_{1})
such that
u-v\in HW^{1,p}_{0}(B_{1})
.
Denoting by

(4.2)

a:=\left(\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p},

there exists
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
there exist
\eta\in(0,1)
,
M\geqslant 1
and
\sigma_{0}\in(0,1)
, depending on
\varepsilon
,
Q
and
p
, such that, if
\sigma\in[0,\sigma_{0}]
and
a\geqslant M
, then the following dichotomy holds. Either

(4.3)

\left(\fint_{B_{\eta}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\frac{a}{2},

or

(4.4)

\left(\fint_{B_{\eta}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\varepsilon a,

where
\mathbf{q}:\mathbb{G}\to H\mathbb{G}
is a constant horizontal section, that is

(4.5)

\mathbf{q}(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}q_{j}X_{j}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{G}

for some suitable
q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m_{1}})\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
, with

(4.6)

\frac{a}{4}<|\mathbf{q}|\leqslant C_{0}a,

for some universal constant
C_{0}>0
.

Proof.

We split the proof into several steps.

Step 1: pointwise estimates.

Let
v\in HW^{1,p}(B_{1})
be the
(\mathbb{G},p)
-harmonic replacement of
u
in
B_{1}
. By Theorem 1.1 in
[
17
]
, it exists a constant
C_{0}>0
, depending only on
p
and
Q
, such that

(4.7)

\sup_{B_{1/2}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v\right|\leqslant C_{0}\left(\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}.

Then, recalling (
4.2
), by minimality condition of
v
in Dirichlet energy (
3.1
) together with (
4.7
), we conclude that for every
x\in B_{1/2}

(4.8)

\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|\leqslant C_{0}a.

Step 2: oscillation estimates.

By Theorem 2.1 in
[
17
]
, we have the following oscillation estimates: for all
\eta\in(0,1/2)

(4.9)

\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m_{1}}\mathrm{osc}_{B_{\eta}}X_{i}v\leqslant c\bigg(\frac{\eta}{1/2}\bigg)^{\alpha}\left(\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v|^{p}dy\right)^{1/p},

for some
\alpha=\alpha(Q,p)\in(0,1]
and some constant
c>0
depending only on
p
and
\mathbb{G}
.
Therefore, for every
x\in B_{\eta}
we have

(4.10)

\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m_{1}}|X_{i}v(x)-X_{i}v(0)|\leqslant\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m_{1}}\mathrm{osc}_{B_{\eta}}X_{i}v\leqslant c\bigg(\frac{\eta}{1/2}\bigg)^{\alpha}\left(\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v|^{p}dy\right)^{1/p},

thus, for any
i=1,\dots,m_{1}
denoting by
q_{i}=X_{i}v(0)
and by
\mathbf{q}:\mathbb{G}\to H\mathbb{G}
the constant horizontal section defined as in (
4.5
), it turns out

(4.11)

|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)-\mathbf{q}(x)|\leqslant c_{1}\bigg(\frac{\eta}{1/2}\bigg)^{\alpha}\left(\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v|^{p}dy\right)^{1/p},

for any
x\in B_{\eta}
and for some constant
c_{1}>0
depending only on
p
and
\mathbb{G}
. This gives that, for all
\eta\in(0,1/2]
,

(4.12)

\begin{split}\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)-\mathbf{q}(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant\;&\fint_{B_{\eta}}\bigg(c^{2}_{1}\bigg(\frac{\eta}{1/2}\bigg)^{p\alpha}\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(y)|^{p}\,dy\,\bigg)\,dx\\
=\;&C_{1}\,\eta^{p\alpha}\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v|^{p}dx\\
\leqslant\;&C_{1}\,\eta^{p\alpha}\,a^{p},\end{split}

for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
C_{1}>0
depending only on
p
and
\mathbb{G}
.

Step 3: proximity to the
(\mathbb{G},p)
-harmonic replacement.

By hypothesis (
4.1
) and the minimality of
v
in (
3.1
), for all
\sigma<|B_{1}|
, we obtain

(4.13)

\begin{split}\int_{B_{1}}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\big)\,dx&\leqslant J_{p}(u,B_{1})-\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\\
&\leqslant(1+\sigma)J_{p}(v,B_{1})-\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\\
&\leqslant C\left(\sigma\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+1\right)\\
&\leqslant C\left(\sigma\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+1\right)\end{split}

for some suitable positive constant
C
depending only on
Q
.

Now we distinguish two cases,
p\geqslant 2
and
1<p<2
.

Step 3.1: the case
p\geqslant 2
.
If
p\geqslant 2
, then from (
3.5
) and (
4.13
)
we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left(\sigma\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+1\right)

for some suitable constant
C>0
depending only on
p
and
\mathbb{G}
. Consequently taking the average over
B_{1}
, we thereby obtain that

\fint_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant C(\sigma a^{p}+1).

Using this and (
4.12
), we get that

(4.14)

\begin{split}\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}(x)\right|^{p}\,dx&\leqslant 2^{p-1}\fint_{B_{\eta}}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)-\mathbf{q}(x)\right|^{p}\big)\,dx\\
&\leqslant 2^{p-1}\left(\frac{\left|B_{1}\right|}{\left|B_{\eta}\right|}\fint_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+C_{1}\,\eta^{p\alpha}\,a^{p}\right)\\
&\leqslant 2^{p-1}\Big(C\eta^{-Q}(\sigma a^{p}+1)+C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{p\alpha}\Big),\\
&=2^{p-1}C\eta^{-Q}\sigma a^{p}+2^{p-1}C\eta^{-Q}+2^{p-1}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{p\alpha}.\end{split}

This yields that

(4.15)

\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant 2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-Q}\sigma a^{p}+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-Q}+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}+2^{p-1}\left|\mathbf{q}\right|^{p}.

Step 3.2: the case
1<p<2
.
If
1<p<2
, by virtue of (
3.4
)
and (
4.13
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\;C\left(\sigma\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+1\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{1}}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|\big)^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\;C\left(\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+1\right)\left(\int_{B_{1}}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\big)\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}.

Thus, since
v
is the
(\mathbb{G},p)
-harmonic replacement of
u
in
B_{1}
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\;C\left(\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+1\right)\left(\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\;C\left(\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+\left(\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\right).

Consequently, taking the average integral, we have that

\fint_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant C\left(\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}a^{p}+|B_{1}|^{-\frac{p}{2}}a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}\right)\leqslant C\left(\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}a^{p}+a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}\right).

From this and (
4.12
), we obtain that

(4.16)

\begin{split}\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant\;&2^{p-1}\fint_{B_{\eta}}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)-\mathbf{q}\right|^{p}\big)\,dx\\
\leqslant\;&2^{p-1}\left(\frac{\left|B_{1}\right|}{\left|B_{\eta}\right|}\fint_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+C_{1}\,\eta^{\alpha p}\,a^{p}\right)\\
\leqslant\;&2^{p-1}C\eta^{-Q}\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}a^{p}+2^{p-1}C\eta^{-Q}a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}+2^{p-1}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}.\end{split}

This gives that

(4.17)

\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant 2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-Q}\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}a^{p}+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-Q}a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}+2^{p-1}\left|\mathbf{q}\right|^{p}.

Step 4: perturbative estimates.
Now,
given
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1/4]
, we claim that
for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
there exists
\eta
small enough (depending on
\varepsilon
)
such that if
\sigma
is chosen sufficiently small and
a
sufficiently large (depending on
\eta
,
and thus on
\varepsilon
) then

(4.18)

2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-Q}\sigma a^{p}+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-Q}+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}\leqslant 2^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p}a^{p}\leqslant\frac{a^{p}}{2^{p+1}}\quad\mbox{if }p\geqslant 2,\\

while

(4.19)

2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-Q}\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}a^{p}+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-Q}a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}\leqslant 2^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p}a^{p}\leqslant\frac{a^{p}}{2^{p+1}}\quad\mbox{if }1<p<2.

To prove this we distinguish two cases.

Step 4.1: the case
p\geqslant 2
.
If
p\geqslant 2,
we pick
\eta>0
sufficiently small
such that

\varepsilon^{p}-2^{p-1}C\eta>2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}.

This allows us to define

M:=\left(\frac{2^{p-1}C\eta^{-Q}}{\varepsilon^{p}-2^{p-1}C\eta-2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}}\right)^{1/p}.

Note also that we can suppose
M\geqslant 1
by taking
\eta
small enough.
Let also

\sigma_{0}:=\min\{|B_{1}|,\eta^{Q+1}\}.

With this setting, we obtain that, for every
a\geqslant M
and for every
0<\sigma\leqslant\sigma_{0},

\displaystyle 2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-Q}\sigma a^{p}+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-Q}+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle a^{p}\Big(2^{2(p-1)}C\eta+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{p}\Big(2^{2(p-1)}C\eta+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)+2^{p-1}\,M^{p}\Big(\varepsilon^{p}-2^{p-1}C\eta-2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle a^{p}\Big(2^{2(p-1)}C\eta+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)+2^{p-1}\,a^{p}\Big(\varepsilon^{p}-2^{p-1}C\eta-2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p}a^{p}+a^{p}\Big(2^{2(p-1)}C\eta+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}-2^{2(p-1)}C\eta-2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p}a^{p},

which proves (
4.18
).

Step 4.2: the case
1<p<2
.
If instead
1<p<2,
we pick
\eta>0
small enough such that

\varepsilon^{p}>2^{p-1}C\eta+2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}.

In this way, we can define

M:=\left(\frac{C2^{p-1}\eta^{-Q}}{\varepsilon^{p}-2^{p-1}C\eta-2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}}\right)^{2/p^{2}}.

Let also
\sigma_{0}:=\eta^{(Q+1)\frac{2}{p}}.

Then, whenever
a\geqslant M
and
0<\sigma\leqslant\sigma_{0}
it follows that

\displaystyle\hskip-50.00008pt2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-Q}\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}a^{p}+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-Q}a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2^{2(p-1)}C\eta a^{p}+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-Q}a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}a^{p}\Big(2^{p-1}C\eta+2^{p-1}C\eta^{-Q}a^{-\frac{p^{2}}{2}}+2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}a^{p}\Big(2^{p-1}C\eta+2^{p-1}C\eta^{-Q}M^{-\frac{p^{2}}{2}}+2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}a^{p}\Big(2^{p-1}C\eta+\varepsilon^{p}-2^{p-1}C\eta-2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}+2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p}a^{p},

which establishes (
4.19
).

Step 5: conclusion of the proof.
In order to complete the proof of Proposition
4.1
,
we now distinguish two cases according to the size of
\left|\mathbf{q}\right|.
More precisely, we first suppose that

\left|\mathbf{q}\right|\leqslant\frac{a}{4}.

Then, using (
4.15
) and (
4.18
) if
p\geqslant 2
or (
4.17
) and (
4.18
) if
1<p<2
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant\frac{a^{p}}{2^{p+1}}+2^{p-1}\frac{a^{p}}{2^{2p}}=\frac{a^{p}}{2^{p+1}}+\frac{a^{p}}{2^{p+1}}=\frac{a^{p}}{2^{p}},

and thus

\left(\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\frac{a}{2},

which is the first alternative in (
4.3
).

Otherwise, it holds that

\frac{a}{4}<\left|\mathbf{q}\right|\leqslant C_{0}\,a,

and therefore, by either (
4.14
) and (
4.18
) if
p\geqslant 2
or (
4.16
) and (
4.19
) if
1<p<2
, we conclude that

\left(\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\varepsilon a,

which is the second alternative in (
4.4
).
The proof of Proposition
4.1
is thereby complete.
∎

5.
Regularity estimates for a subelliptic equation with variable coefficients

Now, our aim is to prove that the alternative in (
4.4
)
can be
improved
when
\varepsilon
and
\sigma
are sufficiently small.
On the other hand, differently from what happens when
p=2
(see Lemma 3.2 in
[
34
]
), here the main difficulty relies on the fact that the problem is not linear when
p\neq 2
. Therefore, for example, if
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are the
(\mathbb{G},p)
-harmonic replacements of
u_{1}

and
u_{2}
in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
, then it is not true that
v_{1}+v_{2}

is the
(\mathbb{G},p)
-harmonic replacement of
u_{1}+u_{2}
, unless
p=2
.

To overcome this difficulty, our goal is to show that affine perturbation of
(\mathbb{G},p)
-harmonic replacements satisfied a suitable subelliptic equation. With this purpose, in this section we prove some
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity estimate that will we useful to obtain the counterpart of Lemma 3.2 in
[
34
]
in our nonlinear setting.

More precisely, we will show the following result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group of step 2 and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
be an open set. Let
u\in HW^{1,2}(\Omega)
, be a weak solution of

(5.1)

\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{G}}\big(A(x)\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x))=0,\quad\text{for }x\in\Omega,

where
A:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}\times m_{1}}
satisfies the following structure condition, for any
x,y\in\Omega

(5.2)

\displaystyle\nu|\xi|^{2}\leqslant\left\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\leqslant L|\xi|^{2},\quad\text{for all }\xi\in H\mathbb{G}_{x}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\hskip-40.00006pt|A(x)-A(y)|\leqslant L^{\prime}d(x,y)^{\alpha}

for some
\nu>0
and
L,L^{\prime}\geqslant 1
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Then
\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u\in C^{0,\gamma}_{loc}(\Omega)
, for some
\gamma=\gamma(\mathbb{G},\nu,L,\alpha)\in(0,1)
. Moreover, there exists a constant
c=c(\mathbb{G},\nu,L)>0
such that for any
x_{0}\in\Omega
it exists
\bar{R}=\bar{R}(\mathbb{G},\nu,L,L^{\prime},\alpha,\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega))>0
such that for any
x,y\in B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
, with
0<R\leqslant\bar{R}
it follows

(5.4)

\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m_{1}}|X_{i}(x)-X_{i}(y)|\leqslant c\,d_{c}(x,y)^{\gamma}\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)|\,dx.

Remark 5.2
.

The regularity and apriori estimates of the homogeneous equation corresponding to (
5.1
) with
freezing of the coefficients is a key tool. Fixed
x_{0}\in\Omega
, we consider the equation

(5.5)

\mathrm{div}_{{\mathbb{G}}}(A(x_{0})\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u)=0\quad\text{in}\ \Omega.

The
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of sub-elliptic equation of the form (
5.5
) within Carnot Groups of step two, under assumption (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), has been dealt with in
[
17
]
(see also
[
72
]
in the case of Heisenberg group). In particular, in Theorem 1.1 in
[
17
]
, similar as the Euclidean case (see
[
30
,
66
]
), the following local estimate can be shown by using Sobolev’s inequality and
Moser’s iteration on the Caccioppoli type inequalities: for any
0<\sigma<1
and metric ball
B_{R}=B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
, it results

(5.6)

\sup_{B_{\sigma R}}\ |\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u|\leqslant c(1-\sigma)^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\bigg(\fint_{B_{R}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)|^{2}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for some
c=c(\mathbb{G},\nu,L)>0
. Furthermore, by another standard iteration argument proved in Lemma 3.38. in
[
54
]
, for any
0<q<2
,
0<\sigma<1
and metric ball
B_{R}=B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
, we obtain the following estimate

(5.7)

\sup_{B_{\sigma R}}\ |\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u|\leqslant c(1-\sigma)^{-\frac{Q}{q}}\bigg(\fint_{B_{R}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)|^{q}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for some
c=c(\mathbb{G},\nu,L,q)>0
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\mathbf{q}:\mathbb{G}\to H\mathbb{G}
a constant horizontal section and let
\eta:\mathbb{G}\to H\mathbb{G}
an horizontal section be such that
|\eta(x)|<\frac{|\mathbf{q}|}{2}
, for any
x\in\mathbb{G}

Let

the map defined by
F(z):=|z|^{p-2}z
. Let us consider

A(x):=\int_{0}^{1}D_{z}F\big(\mathbf{q}(x)+t\eta(x)\big)\,dt

where, for
z\in H\mathbb{G}_{x}
, the Euclidean vector fields on
H\mathbb{G}_{x}\cong\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
are denoted as
D_{z_{j}}
for
j=1,\ldots,m_{1}
and

for any
x\in\mathbb{G}
and
\xi\in H\mathbb{G}_{x}
, and some
\Lambda\geqslant\lambda>0
, depending on
p
.

Proof.

First of all, we notice that, for all
t\in(0,1)
and
x\in\mathbb{G}
, by triangular inequality, we have

(6.1)

\begin{split}&|\mathbf{q}(x)+t\eta(x)|\leqslant|\mathbf{q}|+|\eta(x)|<|\mathbf{q}|+\frac{|\mathbf{q}|}{2}=\frac{3|\mathbf{q}|}{2}\\
{\mbox{and }}\quad&|\mathbf{q}(x)+t\eta(x)|\geqslant|\mathbf{q}|-|\eta(x)|>|\mathbf{q}|-\frac{|\mathbf{q}|}{2}=\frac{|\mathbf{q}|}{2}.\end{split}

Furthermore, for
z\in H\mathbb{G}_{x}\cong\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
, it results

D_{z}F(z)=(p-2)|z|^{p-4}z\otimes z+|z|^{p-2}{\text{Id}},

where

denotes the identity map on
H\mathbb{G}_{x}\cong\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
. As a consequence, for all
\xi\in H\mathbb{G}_{x}
,

\displaystyle\left\langle D_{z}F(z)\xi,\xi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-2)|z|^{p-4}(\langle z,\xi\rangle)^{2}+|z|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle-(2-p)^{+}|z|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}+|z|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1-(2-p)^{+}\big)\,|z|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2},

that by integration leads to

\displaystyle\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\geqslant\big(1-(2-p)^{+}\big)|\xi|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|\mathbf{q}(x)+t\eta(x)|^{p-2}\,dt.

Then, using (
6.1
) we obtain

\left\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\geqslant\frac{1-(2-p)^{+}}{2^{p-2}}\,|\mathbf{q}|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}

if

, and

\left\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\geqslant\big(1-(2-p)^{+}\big)\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{p-2}|\mathbf{q}|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}

if
p\in(1,2)
, for any
\xi\in H\mathbb{G}_{x}
.

Similarly, for any
z,\xi\in H\mathbb{G}_{x}
, it results

\displaystyle\left\langle D_{z}F(z)\xi,\xi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-2)|z|^{p-4}(\langle z,\xi\rangle)^{2}+|z|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(p-2)^{+}|z|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}+|z|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1+(p-2)^{+}\big)\,|z|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2},

that by integration leads to

\displaystyle\left\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\leqslant\big(1+(p-2)^{+}\big)|\xi|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|\mathbf{q}(x)+t\eta(x)|^{p-2}\,dt.

Hence, making again use of (

if
p\geqslant 2
, and

\left\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\leqslant\frac{1+(p-2)^{+}}{2^{p-2}}\,|\mathbf{q}|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}

if
p\in(1,2)
, for any
\xi\in H\mathbb{G}_{x}
.
The proof of Lemma
6.1
is completed by choosing

\lambda:=\left\{\begin{matrix}\frac{1-(2-p)^{+}}{2^{p-2}},\ &\text{ if }p\geqslant 2\vskip 10.00002pt\\
\big(1-(2-p)^{+}\big)\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{p-2},\ \ &\text{ if }p\in(1,2)\end{matrix}\right.

and

\frac{1+(p-2)^{+}}{2^{p-2}},\ &\text{ if }p\in(1,2).\end{matrix}\right.

&\qquad\quad\leqslant\;C\left(\int_{B_{\tau}}\left(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|^{p}\right)\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{\tau}}\big(\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}v(x)\right|\big)^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\\
&\qquad\quad\leqslant\;C\Big(\left|\left\{u=0\right\}\cap B_{\tau}\right|+\sigma J_{p}(v,B_{1})\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{\tau}}2^{p}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\\
&\qquad\quad\leqslant\;C\Big(\left|\left\{u=0\right\}\cap B_{\tau}\right|+\sigma(a^{p}+1)\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}.\end{split}

Step 2: measure estimates for the zero level set.
Now, we claim that

(6.15)

\left|B_{\tau}\cap\left\{u=0\right\}\right|\leqslant C_{1}\varepsilon^{2+\delta},

for some
C_{1}>0
and
\delta>0
.

Step 2.1: comparison with a linear function.
To prove our claim (
6.15
), we consider function
\ell:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

(6.16)

\ell(x):=b+\left\langle\mathbf{q}(x),\pi_{x}(x)\right\rangle=b+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}q_{j}x_{j},\quad{\mbox{ with }}\quad x\in\mathbb{G}\quad{\mbox{ and }}\quad b:=\fint_{B_{1/10}}u(x)\,dx.

We remark that

(6.17)

\begin{split}(u-\ell)_{B_{1/10}}:=\fint_{B_{1/10}}\big(u(x)-\ell(x)\big)\,dx&=b-\left(b+\fint_{B_{1/10}}\left\langle\mathbf{q}(x),\pi_{x}(x)\right\rangle\,dx\right)\\
&=\fint_{B_{1/10}}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}q_{j}x_{j}\,dx=0\end{split}

where last equality is a consequence of the symmetry with respect to the identity element of the Carnot-Carathéodory ball
B_{1/10}
. Then by the Poincaré inequality (see e.g.
[
56
,
61
]
) we have that

(6.18)

\begin{split}\|u-\ell-(u-\ell)_{B_{1/10}}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1/10})}=\left\|u-\ell\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{1/10})}&\leqslant C\left\|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}(u-\ell)\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{1/10})}\\
&\leqslant C\left\|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}(u-\ell)\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})},\end{split}

for some
C>0
universal.

Since by Proposition
2.3

\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}\ell=\mathbf{q}
, (
6.18
) together with hypothesis (
6.4
) leads to

(6.19)

\fint_{B_{1/10}}\left|u(x)-\ell(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant C\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}(u-\ell)(x)|^{p}\,dx=C\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}(x)|^{p}\,dx\leqslant C\varepsilon^{p}a^{p}.

Finally we remark that, since by assumption
u\geqslant 0
, it holds that
\ell^{-}\leqslant\left|u-\ell\right|
, so by (
6.19
), we obtain that

(6.20)

\fint_{B_{1/10}}(\ell^{-}(x))^{p}\,dx\leqslant C\varepsilon^{p}a^{p},

for some
C>0
universal.

Step 2.2: lower bounds on the
\mathbb{G}
-affine function.
Now we claim that if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small,

(6.21)

\ell\geqslant c_{1}a\quad\mbox{in }B_{\tau},

for some
c_{1}>0
. To check this, we argue by contradiction assuming that

\min_{x\in\overline{B_{\tau}}}\ell(x)<ca

for any
c>0
.
We notice that, for every
x\in B_{\tau}
, recalling that
\tau<\frac{1}{10\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}
, we get

\left|\ell(x)-b\right|=\left|\left\langle\mathbf{q}(x),\pi_{x}(x)\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\left|\mathbf{q}\right|\left|x^{(1)}\right|\leqslant\left|\mathbf{q}\right|\left|x\right|_{\mathbb{G}}\leqslant\left|\mathbf{q}\right|\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}\,d_{c}(e,x)\leqslant\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{\left|\mathbf{q}\right|}{10\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}=\frac{\left|\mathbf{q}\right|}{10\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}},

where
\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}\geqslant 1
is the constant given by (
2.10
) which only depends on the group
\mathbb{G}
.
As a consequence

-\frac{\left|\mathbf{q}\right|}{10\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}}\leqslant\ell(x)-b\leqslant\frac{\left|\mathbf{q}\right|}{10\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}}\qquad\text{ for any }x\in B_{\tau}

and therefore

ca>\min_{x\in\overline{B_{\tau}}}\ell(x)\geqslant b-\frac{\left|\mathbf{q}\right|}{10\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}}

that leads to

(6.22)

b\leqslant ca+\frac{|\mathbf{q}|}{10\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}},

for any
c>0
.
Now, taking into account the usual identifications given by exponential coordinates, let us consider

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}:=\Big\{x=\left[x^{(1)},x^{(2)}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}}\equiv\mathbb{G}:\;

\displaystyle x^{(1)}_{j}=-\frac{tq_{j}}{|\mathbf{q}|}+\eta_{j},\;\text{for }j=1,\ldots,m_{1};

\displaystyle x^{(2)}_{i}=\xi_{i},\;\text{for }i=m_{1}+1,\ldots,m_{2}\;{\mbox{
for some }}(t,\eta,\mathbf{\xi})\in\mathcal{A}\Big\},

where we set

\mathcal{A}:=\left\{(t,\eta,\mathbf{\xi})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}}:\;t\in\left[\frac{1}{4\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}},\frac{3}{10\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}}\right],\;\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}\eta_{j}^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{100\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}{\mbox{ and }}\sum_{i=m_{1}+1}^{m_{2}}\xi_{i}^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{100\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}^{4}}\right\}.

We notice that if
x\in{\mathcal{B}}
then

\begin{split}d_{c}(e,x)^{4}&\leqslant\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}^{4}|x|_{\mathbb{G}}^{4}=\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}^{4}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}\big(-\frac{tq_{j}}{|\mathbf{q}|}+\eta_{j}\big)^{2}\Big)^{2}+\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}^{4}\sum_{i=m_{1}+1}^{m_{2}}\xi_{i}^{2}\leqslant 4\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}^{4}\Big(t^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}\eta_{j}^{2}\Big)^{2}+\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}^{4}\sum_{i=m_{1}+1}^{m_{2}}\xi_{i}^{2}\\
&\leqslant 4\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}^{4}\Big(\frac{9}{100\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}+\frac{1}{100\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}\Big)^{2}+\frac{\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}^{4}}{100\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}^{4}}=\frac{4}{100}+\frac{1}{100}=\frac{1}{20}<\frac{1}{10},\end{split}

so we have that

(6.23)

{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq B_{1/10}.

Furthermore, by (
6.5
) and (
6.22
), we have that, for
x\in{\mathcal{B}}
,

\displaystyle\ell(x)=b-t|\mathbf{q}|+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}q_{j}\eta_{j}\leqslant ca+\frac{|\mathbf{q}|}{10\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}}-\frac{|\mathbf{q}|}{4\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}}+\frac{|\mathbf{q}|}{10\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}}=ca-\frac{1}{20\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}}|\mathbf{q}|.

Now, using hypothesis (
6.5
), we get

\ell(x)\leqslant ca-\frac{1}{160\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}}a.

Then, taking

\ell(x)\leqslant-\frac{a}{320\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}}.

Accordingly, using this and (
6.23
) into (
6.20

\displaystyle C\,|B_{1}|\,\varepsilon^{p}a^{p}\geqslant C\,|B_{1/10}|\,\varepsilon^{p}a^{p}\geqslant\int_{B_{1/10}}(\ell^{-}(x))^{p}\,dx\int_{{\mathcal{B}}}(\ell^{-}(x))^{p}\,dx\geqslant\int_{{\mathcal{B}}}\left(\frac{a}{320\Lambda_{\mathbb{G}}}\right)^{p}\,dx\geqslant\overline{c}a^{p},

for some positive universal constant

. This establishes the desired contradiction if

is sufficiently small, and thus the proof of (

(by taking
\kappa_{0}
there less than or equal to
c_{0}
).
In this way, we deduce from (
6.6
) and (
6.8
)
that there exists a constant horizontal section
\mathbf{q}_{k+1}
such that

(6.69)

\begin{split}\left(\fint_{B_{\rho}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u_{r}(x)-\mathbf{q}_{k+1}(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\rho^{\alpha}\varepsilon_{k}a\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\left|\mathbf{q}_{k}-{\mathbf{q}}_{k+1}\right|\leqslant\widetilde{C}\varepsilon_{k}a.\end{split}

On the other hand, since
\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}\left(u(\delta_{r})\right)=r\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(\delta_{r}(x))
, scaling back, we find that

\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k+1}}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}_{k+1}(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\rho^{\alpha}\rho^{{k\alpha}}\varepsilon a=\rho^{{(k+1)\alpha}}\varepsilon a.

Moreover, using again inductive assumption and (
6.69
), we have

\displaystyle|\mathbf{q}_{k+1}|\leqslant|\mathbf{q}_{k}-\mathbf{q}_{k+1}|+|\mathbf{q}_{k}|\leqslant\widetilde{C}\varepsilon_{k}a+C_{0}a+\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{k\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\qquad=C_{0}a+\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{k\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}+\widetilde{C}\rho^{k\alpha}\varepsilon a=C_{0}a+\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{(k+1)\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}

and

\displaystyle|\mathbf{q}_{k+1}|\geqslant|\mathbf{q}_{k}|-|\mathbf{q}_{k}-\mathbf{q}_{k+1}|\geqslant\frac{a}{4}-\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{k\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}-\widetilde{C}\varepsilon_{k}a

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{a}{4}-\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{k\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}-\widetilde{C}\rho^{k\alpha}\varepsilon a=\frac{a}{4}-\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{(k+1)\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}.

In addition,

\displaystyle\left|\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k+1}}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}-|\mathbf{q}_{k+1}|\right|=\frac{1}{|B_{\rho^{k+1}}|^{1/p}}\left|\left(\int_{B_{\rho^{k+1}}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}-|\mathbf{q}_{k+1}||B_{\rho^{k+1}}|^{1/p}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{|B_{\rho^{k+1}}|^{1/p}}\left|\left\|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho^{k+1}})}-\left\|\mathbf{q}_{k+1}\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho^{k+1}})}\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{|B_{\rho^{k+1}}|^{1/p}}\left\|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u-\mathbf{q}_{k+1}\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho^{k+1}})}

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k+1}}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}_{k+1}(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\varepsilon a,

which yields that

\displaystyle\left|\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k+1}}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}-|\mathbf{q}|\right|=\left|\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k+1}}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}-|\mathbf{q}_{0}|\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\left|\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k+1}}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}-|\mathbf{q}_{k+1}|\right|+\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left||\mathbf{q}_{j+1}|-|\mathbf{q}_{j}|\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\varepsilon a+\widetilde{C}a\sum_{j=0}^{k}\varepsilon_{j}\leqslant\varepsilon a+\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\rho^{j\alpha}=\left(1+\frac{\widetilde{C}}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}\right)\varepsilon a

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\left(1+\frac{\widetilde{C}}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}\right)\varepsilon|\mathbf{q}|\leqslant\frac{|\mathbf{q}|}{p}.

These observations conclude the proof of the inductive step and establish (
6.68
).

Step 2: Morrey-Campanato estimates.

We now want to exploit the Morrey-Campanato estimate of Theorem
2.10

here applied to the function
\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u-\mathbf{q}
,
with the following choices

\Omega=B_{1/2},\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda=\alpha/Q.

In order to do this, we claim that, for every
B\subset B_{1/2}
,

(6.70)

\frac{1}{|B|^{1+2\lambda}}\inf_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\int_{B}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}(x)-\boldsymbol{\xi}(x)\right|^{2}\,dx\,\leqslant C\varepsilon^{2}a^{2},

up to renaming
C>0
, where the infimum is taken over all the constant horizontal sections
\boldsymbol{\xi}:\mathbb{G}\to H\mathbb{G}
as in (
2.16
).

To prove the claim (
6.70
), we distinguish two cases: either
|B|\geqslant 1
or
|B|\in(0,1)
.

If
|B|\geqslant 1
, we use (
6.4
)
and we get that

\displaystyle|B|^{-(1+p\lambda)}\inf_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\int_{B}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}(x)-\boldsymbol{\xi}(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant\int_{B_{1/p}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}(x)\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leqslant|B_{1}|\fint_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant|B_{1}|\,\varepsilon^{p}a^{p},

which gives (
6.70
) in the case of
|B|\geqslant 1
.

If instead
|B|\in(0,1)
, let be
k_{0}=k_{0}(B)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
B\subseteq B_{\rho^{k_{0}}}
and
B_{\rho^{k}}\subseteq B
for all
k>k_{0}
. By (
6.68
) we have that

\displaystyle|B|^{-(1+p\lambda)}\inf_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\int_{B}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}(x)-\boldsymbol{\xi}(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant|B_{\rho^{k_{0}+1}}|^{-(1+p\lambda)}\int_{B_{\rho^{k_{0}}}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}_{k_{0}}(x)\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=|B_{1}|\,\rho^{-Q(k_{0}+1)(1+p\lambda)}\,|B_{\rho^{k_{0}}}|\fint_{B_{\rho^{k_{0}}}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}_{k_{0}}(x)\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leqslant C|B_{1}|\,\rho^{-Q(k_{0}+1)(1+p\lambda)+Qk_{0}+pk_{0}\alpha}\,\varepsilon^{p}a^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=C|B_{1}|\,\rho^{(-Q-p\alpha)}\,\varepsilon^{p}a^{p},

which gives (
6.70
) up to renaming
C
. The proof of (
6.70
)
is thereby complete.

Step 4: conclusion of the proof.

Since, by (
6.4
)

\left\|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u-\mathbf{q}\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{1/2})}\leqslant C\varepsilon a,

and by (
2.15
) and (
6.70
), we have

[\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u-\mathbf{q}]_{\mathcal{E}^{p,\lambda}(B_{1/2},H\mathbb{G})}\leqslant C\varepsilon a,

up to renaming
C
, we can apply the Theorem
2.10
, from which it follows that

(6.71)

\left[\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u-\mathbf{q}\right]_{C^{0,\lambda}(B_{1/2},H\mathbb{G})}\leqslant C\varepsilon a,

up to renaming constant
C
with
\lambda=\alpha/Q\in(0,1)
.

Now we define the
\mathbb{G}
-affine function
\ell:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
as
\ell(x):=u(e)+\left\langle\mathbf{q}(x),\pi_{x}(x)\right\rangle
, for
x\in\mathbb{G}
. By Proposition
2.3
, we have
\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}\ell=\mathbf{q}
. For all
x\in B_{1/2}
, let
\delta_{x}:=d_{c}(0,x)
and
\gamma_{x}\colon[0,\delta_{x}]\to B_{1/2}
a sub-unitary curve

\gamma_{x}(0)=e,\quad\gamma_{x}(\delta_{x})=x\quad\text{ and }\quad\dot{\gamma}_{x}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}h_{i}(t)X_{i}(\gamma_{x}(t))\quad\text{ for a.e.\ $t\in[0,\delta_{x}]$},

with
\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}(h_{j}(t))^{2}\leqslant 1
, for a.e.
t\in[0,\delta_{x}]
, (such
\gamma
exists thanks to Chow’s Theorem,
[
7
]
*Theorem 19.1.3). By (
6.71
) we conclude

\displaystyle|u(x)-\ell(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|u(x)-u(e)-\left\langle\mathbf{q}(x),\pi_{x}(x)\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathbf{q}(e),\pi_{e}(e)\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{\delta_{x}}\frac{d}{dt}\left(u(\gamma_{x}(t))-\left\langle\mathbf{q}(\gamma_{x}(t)),\pi_{\gamma_{x}(t)}(\gamma_{x}(t))\right\rangle\right)\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C\int_{0}^{\delta_{x}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(\gamma_{x}(t))-\mathbf{q}(\gamma_{x}(t))|\,dt\leqslant C\varepsilon a,

up to renaming
C>0
a universal constant. By arbitrariness of
x\in B_{1/2}
, we conclude

\|u-\ell\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\leqslant C\varepsilon a.

This and (
6.71
) establish (
6.56
).
∎

7.
Lipschitz continuity of almost minimizers and proof of Theorem
1.2

We are now in position of establishing the Lipschitz regularity result in Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

In the light of Lemma
6.5
, up to a rescaling,
we can assume that
u
is an almost minimizer with constant

(7.1)

\widetilde{\kappa}:=\kappa s^{\beta},

which can be made arbitrarily small by an appropriate choice of
s>0
.

Let us set
P:=\max\{p,2\}
. Let also
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1]
be the structural constant given by Lemma
6.2
and define

\alpha:=\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{\alpha_{0},\frac{\beta}{P}\right\}.

We also consider
\varepsilon_{0}
as given by Proposition
4.1
and take
\eta\in(0,1)
and
M\geqslant 1
as in Proposition
4.1
(corresponding here to choice
\varepsilon:=\varepsilon_{0}/2
).

Let us define

(7.2)

a(\tau):=\bigg(\fint_{B_{\tau}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}.

We now divide the argument into separate steps.

Step 1: estimating the average.

We claim that, for every
r\in(0,\eta]
,

(7.3)

a(r)\leqslant C(M,\eta)(1+a(1)),

for some
C(M,\eta)>0
, possibly depending on
Q
and
p
as well.

To prove this, we consider the set
{\mathcal{K}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
containing all the integers
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(7.4)

a(\eta^{k})\leqslant C(\eta)M+2^{-k}a(1),

where

(7.5)

C(\eta):=2\eta^{-Q/p}.

We stress that for
k=0
formula (
7.4
) is clearly true, hence

(7.6)

0\in{\mathcal{K}}\neq\varnothing.

We then distinguish two cases, namely whether (
7.4
) holds for every
k
(i.e.
{\mathcal{K}}=\mathbb{N}
) or not (i.e.
{\mathcal{K}}\subsetneqq\mathbb{N}
).

Step 1.1: the case
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{N}
. For every
r\in(0,\eta],
we define
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}={\mathcal{K}}
such that
\eta^{k_{0}+1}<r\leqslant\eta^{k_{0}}.
Hence, according to (
7.2
) and (
7.4
), we get that

\begin{split}&a(r)\leqslant\bigg(\frac{1}{|B_{1}|\,\eta^{(k_{0}+1)Q}}\int_{B_{\eta^{k_{0}}}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}=\eta^{-Q/p}a(\eta^{k_{0}})\leqslant\eta^{-Q/p}(C(\eta)M+a(1))\\
&\qquad\qquad\leqslant\eta^{-Q/p}\max\left\{C(\eta)M,1\right\}(1+a(1))\leqslant C(M,\eta)(1+a(1)),\end{split}

provided that
C(M,\eta)\geqslant\eta^{-Q/p}\max\left\{C(\eta)M,1\right\}
.
The proof of (
7.3
) is thereby complete in this case.

Step 1.2: the case
{\mathcal{K}}\subsetneqq\mathbb{N}
By (
7.6
), there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\{0,\dots,k_{0}\}\in{\mathcal{K}}

and

(7.7)

k_{0}+1\not\in{\mathcal{K}}.

We notice that, by (
7.2
)
and (
7.5
),

\eta^{-Q/p}M<C(\eta)M\leqslant C(\eta)M+2^{-(k_{0}+1)}a(1)<a(\eta^{k_{0}+1})\leqslant\eta^{-Q/p}a(\eta^{k_{0}})

and therefore

(7.8)

a(\eta^{k_{0}})>M.

Furthermore, from (
7.7
),

(7.9)

a(\eta^{k_{0}+1})>C(\eta)M+2^{-(k_{0}+1)}a(1)\geqslant\frac{C(\eta)M+2^{-k_{0}}a(1)}{2}\geqslant\frac{a(\eta^{k_{0}})}{2}.

Now, we claim that

(7.10)

\left(\fint_{B_{\eta^{k_{0}+1}}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\varepsilon a(\eta^{k_{0}}),

for some constant horizontal section
\mathbf{q}:\mathbb{G}\to H\mathbb{G}
such that

\frac{a(\eta^{k_{0}})}{4}<\left|\mathbf{q}\right|\leqslant C_{0}a(\eta^{k_{0}})

and being
C_{0}
the constant given by Proposition
4.1
.

To prove (
7.10
), we apply the dichotomy result in Proposition
4.1
rescaled in the ball
B_{\eta^{k_{0}}}
.
In this way, we deduce from (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and (
4.6
) that (
7.10
) holds true, unless it occurs

a(\eta^{k_{0}+1})=\left(\fint_{B_{\eta^{k_{0}+1}}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\frac{a(\eta^{k_{0}})}{2},

but this contradicts (
7.9
).
This ends the proof of (
7.10
).

Now we apply Corollary
6.3
rescaled in the ball
B_{\eta^{k_{0}+1}}
: namely, we use here Corollary
6.3
with
B_{1}
replaced by
B_{\eta^{k_{0}+1}}
and
a
replaced by
a(\eta^{k_{0}+1})
.
To this end, we need to verify that the assumptions of Corollary
6.3
are fulfilled in this rescaled situation. Specifically, we note that, by (
7.8
) and (
7.9
),

a(\eta^{k_{0}+1})\geqslant\frac{M}{2}.

Also, since
k_{0}\in{\mathcal{K}}
,

a(\eta^{k_{0}+1})\leqslant\eta^{-Q/p}a(\eta^{k_{0}})\leqslant\eta^{-Q/p}\big(C(\eta)M+2^{-k_{0}}a(1)\big)\leqslant\eta^{-Q/p}\big(C(\eta)M+a(1)\big).

These observations give that (
6.55
) is satisfied in this rescaled setting with

(7.11)

a_{0}:=\frac{M}{2}\qquad{\mbox{ and }}\qquad a_{1}:=\eta^{-Q/p}\big(C(\eta)M+a(1)\big).

Moreover, by (
7.9
) and (
7.10
),

\left(\fint_{B_{\eta^{k_{0}+1}}}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)-\mathbf{q}(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant 2\varepsilon a(\eta^{k_{0}+1}),

showing that (
6.4
) is satisfied (here with
2\varepsilon
instead of
\varepsilon
).

We also deduce from (
7.9
) and (
7.10
) that

\frac{\eta^{Q/p}a(\eta^{k_{0}+1})}{4}\leqslant\frac{a(\eta^{k_{0}})}{4}<\left|\mathbf{q}\right|\leqslant C_{0}a(\eta^{k_{0}})\leqslant 2C_{0}a(\eta^{k_{0}+1}),

which gives that (
4.6
) is satisfied here (though with different structural constants).

We can thereby exploit Corollary
6.3

in a rescaled version
to obtain the existence of
\varepsilon_{0}
(possibly different from the one given by Proposition
4.1
)
and
\kappa_{0}
, depending on
Q
,
p
,
\beta
,
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
,
such that, if

(7.12)

\widetilde{\kappa}\in(0,\kappa_{0}\varepsilon_{0}^{P}],

we have that

(7.13)

\left\|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\eta^{k_{0}+1}/2})}\leqslant\bar{C}a(\eta^{k_{0}}),

for some structural constant
\bar{C}>0
.

We remark that condition (
7.12
) is satisfied by taking
s
in (
7.1
)
sufficiently small, namely taking
s:=\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}\varepsilon_{0}^{P}}{2\kappa}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}
.
Notice in particular that

(7.14)

s
depends on
Q
,
p
,
\kappa
,

\beta
and
\|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}
,

due to (
7.11
).

As a result of (
7.13
), for all
r\in\left(0,\frac{\eta^{k_{0}+1}}{2}\right)
,

(7.15)

\begin{split}&a(r)=\left(\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\bar{C}a(\eta^{k_{0}})\leqslant\bar{C}\big(C(\eta)M+2^{-k_{0}}a(1)\big)\\
&\qquad\qquad\leqslant\bar{C}\big(C(\eta)M+a(1)\big)\leqslant C(M,\eta)(1+a(1)),\end{split}

as long as
C(M,\eta)\geqslant\bar{C}(C(\eta)M+1)
.

Moreover, if
r\in\left[\frac{\eta^{k_{0}+1}}{2},\eta\right]
then we take
k_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\eta^{k_{r}+1}<r\leqslant\eta^{k_{r}}
.
Note that

\frac{1}{\eta^{k_{r}}}\leqslant\frac{1}{r}\leqslant\frac{2}{\eta^{k_{0}+1}},

whence

(7.16)

k_{r}\leqslant k_{0}+C_{\star},

where
C_{\star}:=1+\frac{\log 2}{\log(1/\eta)}
.

We now distinguish two cases: if
k_{r}\in\{0,\dots,k_{0}\}
then
k_{r}\in{\mathcal{K}}
and therefore

a(\eta^{k_{r}})\leqslant C(\eta)M+2^{-k_{r}}a(1).

From this, we obtain that

(7.17)

\begin{split}&a(r)\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\eta^{k_{r}+1}}|}\int_{B_{\eta^{k_{r}}}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}=\eta^{-Q/p}a(\eta^{k_{r}})\\
&\qquad\qquad\leqslant\eta^{-Q/p}\big(C(\eta)M+2^{-k_{r}}a(1)\big)\leqslant C(M,\eta)(1+a(1)).\end{split}

If instead
k_{r}>k_{0}
, we employ (
7.16
) to see that

\displaystyle a(r)\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\eta^{k_{0}+C_{\star}+1}}|}\int_{B_{\eta^{k_{0}}}}|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}=\eta^{-Q(C_{\star}+1)/p}a(\eta^{k_{0}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\leqslant\eta^{-Q(C_{\star}+1)/p}\big(C(\eta)M+2^{-k_{0}}a(1)\big)\leqslant\eta^{-Q(C_{\star}+1)/p}\big(C(\eta)M+a(1)\big)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad\leqslant C(M,\eta)(1+a(1)),

as long as
C(M,\eta)
is chosen large enough.

This and (
7.17
) give that for all
r\in\left[\frac{\eta^{k_{0}+1}}{2},\eta\right]
,
we have that
a(r)\leqslant C(M,\eta)(1+a(1))
. Combining this with (
7.15
),
we deduce that (
7.3
) holds true, as desired.

Step 2: conclusions.

Now, up to scaling and translations, we can extend (
7.3
) to all balls with centre
x_{0}
in
B_{1/2}

and sufficiently small radius. Namely, we have that, for all
r\in(0,\eta]
,

a(r,x_{0})\leqslant C(M,\eta)(1+a(1)),

where

a(r,x_{0}):=\left(\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}.

Therefore, according to the Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem (see e.g.
[
75
]
), recalling that
u\in HW^{1,p}(B_{1}),
we find that, for almost every
x_{0}\in B_{1/2}
,

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(x_{0})\right|=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}a(r,x_{0})\leqslant C(M,\eta)(1+a(1))=C\Big(1+\left\|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\Big),

for some
C>0
,
which yields

(7.18)

\left\|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\leqslant C\Big(1+\left\|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\Big).

So, the first claim in Theorem
1.2
is proved.

We now show that the second claim in Theorem
1.2

holds. For this, we can assume that

(7.19)

\{u=0\}\cap B_{s/100}\neq\varnothing

and we take a Lebesgue point
\bar{x}\in B_{s/100}
for
\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u
.
Up to a left-translation, we can suppose that
\bar{x}=0
and change our assumption (
7.19
) into

(7.20)

\{u=0\}\cap B_{s/50}\neq\varnothing.

We claim that, in this situation,

(7.21)

{\mathcal{K}}=\mathbb{N}.

Indeed, suppose not and let
k_{0}
as above (recall (
7.7
)).
Then, in light of (
7.10
),
we can apply Corollary
6.3
(rescaled as before)
and conclude, by (
6.59
), that
u>0

in
B_{s/2}
, in contradiction with our hypothesis in (
7.20
).
This establishes (
7.21
).

Therefore, in view of (
7.4
) and (
7.21
), for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

a(\eta^{k})\leqslant C(\eta)M+2^{-k}a(1),

and as a result

|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(\bar{x})|=|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}u(0)|=\lim_{k\to+\infty}a(\eta^{k})\leqslant\lim_{k\to+\infty}\big(C(\eta)M+2^{-k}a(1)\big)=C(\eta)M.

Recalling also (
7.14
),
the proof of the second claim in Theorem
1.2
is thereby complete.
∎

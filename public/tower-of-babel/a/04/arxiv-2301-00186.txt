Quantitative mean ergodic inequalities: power bounded operators acting on one single noncommutative L p L_{p} space Thanks: This work was partially supported by Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant: 12071355)
Guixiang Hong, Wei Liu, Bang Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish the quantitative mean ergodic theorems for two subclasses of power bounded operators on a fixed noncommutative L p L_{p} -space with 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty , which mainly concerns power bounded invertible operators and Lamperti contractions. Our approach to the quantitative ergodic theorems relies on noncommutative square function inequalities. The establishment of the latter involves several new ingredients such as the almost orthogonality and Calderón-Zygmund arguments for non-smooth kernels from semi-commutative harmonic analysis, the extension properties of the operators under consideration from operator theory, and a noncommutative version of the classical transference method due to Coifman and Weiss.

1.
Introduction

In classical ergodic theory, there are many works related to the convergence properties of certain averages along the orbits with respect to the transformations. Let
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space. The celebrated von Neumann mean ergodic theorem
[
50
]
states that when
T
is a unitary operator on
L_{2}(X,\mu)
induced by a
\mu
-preserving measurable transformation on
X
, the ergodic averages
M_{n}f
defined by

(1.1)

M_{n}f(x)=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T^{k}f(x)\ \ n\in{\mathbb{N}},

converges in
L_{2}(X)
for any
f\in L_{2}(X)
. Later on, Riesz
[
45
]
greatly generalized the von Neumann mean ergodic theorem; he proved that the convergence of ergodic averages is also valid for contractive operators defined simultaneously on all
L_{p}(X,\mu)
(
1\leq p<\infty
) spaces, where
(X,\mu)
is a probability space. Also, Riesz
[
46
]
gave a simple proof when
T
is a contraction operator on some Hilbert space. Furthermore, the mean ergodic theorem for contraction operators acting on general
L_{p}(X,\mu)
spaces for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
was established by Dunford and Schwartz
[
11
, VIII]
.

It is then natural to ask for the speed of the convergence of the ergodic averages. Unfortunately, Krengel
[
30
]
proved that the speed of the ergodic convergence can be arbitrarily slow. On the other hand, one can not capture any information on the rate of the convergence from the classical proofs of aforementioned works. With the aid of the spectral theorem and the dilation theorem developed in
[
49
]
, Jones, Ostrovskii and Rosenblatt
[
23
]
established the square function inequalities for ergodic averages (
1.1
) associated with a contraction on
L_{2}(X)
. More precisely, they proved that for a
L_{2}
-contraction
T
and any sequence of finite positive integers
n_{0}<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{m}
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\big\|M_{n_{i}}(T)f-M_{n_{i-1}}(T)f\big\|^{2}_{L_{2}(X)}\Big)^{1/2}\leq 25\|f\|_{L_{2}(X)}.

This result can be viewed as a quantitative and finer version of the mean ergodic theorem. Indeed, (
1.2
) implies that for any
\varepsilon>0
, the sequence
(M_{n}(f))_{n=1}^{\infty}
admits at most
625(\varepsilon^{-2}\|f\|_{2}^{2})
jumps of size at least
\varepsilon
in
L_{2}
norm, and as a consequence the sequence converges. Moreover, many variants of the inequality (
1.2
) have been obtained. Avigad and Rute
[
3
]
extended (
1.2
) with the power 2 of (
1.2
) replaced by
q
(
q\geq 2
) to
q
-uniformly convex Banach spaces and specific power bounded operators. Bourgain
[
5
]
considered the corresponding variational inequalities which deduces the pointwise convergence of ergodic averages immediately and also the quantitative ergodic inequality (
1.2
). For the latter, we refer the reader to
[
24
,
25
]
due to Jones
et al
for more details. We remark that Calderón’s transference principle plays an important role in the above works which reduces (
1.2
) to the study of the related operators in harmonic analysis where more tools are available.

Motivated by quantum physics, the convergence of the ergodic averages in von Neumann algebras has attracted much attention. For instance, Kovács and Szücs
[
29
]
considered the mean ergodic theorem for an automorphism
T
on von Neumann algebras equipped with a faithful
T
-invariant semifinite normal state, and Lance
[
32
]
gave a complete discussion of this subject. Later on, Yeadon
[
51
,
52
]
established the mean ergodic theorem for positive Dunford-Schwartz operators on noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces. For more results on the mean ergodic theorem in von Neumann algebras we refer the reader to
[
21
,
22
]
. On the other hand, on the study of pointwise ergodic theorems in noncommutative
L_{p}
, after the work in the case
p=\infty

[
32
,
31
,
9
]
and in the case
p=1

[
51
]
, a breakthrough was made by Junge and Xu
[
28
]
, where they established the noncommutative maximal ergodic inequalities for positive Dunford-Schwartz operators. This celebrated work motivated further research on noncommutative ergodic theory, such as
[
2
,
4
,
18
,
20
,
33
]
. In particular, the first author and his collaborators broke the framework of Junge and Xu by establishing the maximal and pointwise ergodic theorems for a large subclass of positive operators on one single noncommutative
L_{p}
space, see
[
14
,
17
]
for more details.

However, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, there is no quantitative estimate of noncommutative ergodic theorems in the literature.
This paper is devoted to the first study of quantitative mean ergodic theorem (
1.2
) under the noncommutative framework. To better state our results, we need to introduce some notions. Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra equipped with a normal semifinite faithful (abbreviated as
n.s.f
) trace
\tau
. Let
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})
be the associated noncommutative
L_{p}
space and
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell^{rc}_{2})
be one noncommutative analogue of Hilbert-valued
L_{p}
space (see Section
2
for the precise definition). Throughout the paper,
T
stands for a bounded linear operator on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
.
The one-sided ergodic averages
M_{n}(T)
is defined as

M_{n}(T)=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T^{k},\quad\ \forall\ n\in{\mathbb{N}};

and if
T
is invertible, one may define the two-sided ergodic averages
B_{n}(T)
by

B_{n}(T)=\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{k=-n}^{n}T^{k},\ \ \forall\ n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Suppose that
T
be an invertible power bounded operator,

(1.3)

\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|T^{k}:L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})\rightarrow L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})\|<\infty.

Then there exists a positive constant
C_{p}
such that

\sup\big\|\big(M_{n_{i}}(T)x-M_{n_{i+1}}(T)x\big)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell^{rc}_{2})}\leq C_{p}\|x\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})},\forall x\in L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})

where the supremum is taken over all the increasing subsequence
(n_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of positive integers. Similar inequality holds for two-sided ergodic averages
(B_{n}(T))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
is a noncommutative version of
[
23
, Theorem 1.2]
. Note also that Theorem
1.1
can be viewed as an ergodic theorem with respect to a bounded
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
-representation of the group
\mathbb{Z}
. This motivates us to further consider
the bounded noncommuative
L_{p}
-representations of other groups such as the ones of polynomial growth that appeared in
[
14
]
. Similar quantitative mean ergodic theorems will appear in the subsequent paper
[
15
]
.

The second main result concerns the quantitative mean ergodic theorem for Lamperti operators.

Definition 1.3
.

An operator
T
is called a Lamperti operator (or supports separating) if for any two
\tau
-finite projections
e,f\in\mathcal{M}
satisfies
ef=0
, then

(Te)^{*}Tf=Te(Tf)^{*}=0.

In
[
17
]
, the authors established the maximal inequalities for the convex combinations of positive Lamperti contractions on one single noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces, which can be viewed as the first Akcoglu’s maximal ergodic inequalities in the noncommutative setting.

In this paper, we establish the quantitative mean ergodic theorem for Lamperti operators where the positivity assumption can be relaxed.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Suppose that
T
belong to the family

(1.4)

\mathfrak{S}=\overline{\mbox{conv}}^{\mbox{sot}}\{S:L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow L_{p}(\mathcal{M})~\mbox{Lamperti contractions}\},

that is, the closed convex hull of all Lamperti contractions on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
with respect to strong operator topology. For
2\leq p<\infty
, there exists a positive constant
C_{p}
such that for any increasing subsequence of positive integers
(n_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,

\big\|\big(M_{n_{i}}(T)x-M_{n_{i+1}}(T)x\big)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell^{rc}_{2})}\leq C_{p}\|x\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})}\;\forall x\in L_{p}({\mathcal{M}}).

For
1<p<2
, if
T\in\mathfrak{S}
is positive, then the above conclusion holds too.

Remark 1.5
.

(i) Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.4
seem new even in the commutative case since the quantitative mean ergodic inequalities are deduced for a large class of operators acting on a fixed
L_{p}
spaces with
1<p\neq 2<\infty
. It should be pointed out that when
\mathcal{M}
is commutative, the class of operators in the two theorems should be able to be enlarged. We left this to the interested reader.

(ii) In the remarkable paper
[
3
]
, the authors obtained a form of quantitative mean ergodic theorems for power-bounded operators on a general Banach space
X
having martingalge cotype
p
property with
p\geq 2
,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\|M_{n_{i}}(T)x-M_{n_{i+1}}(T)x\|^{p}_{X}\leq C_{p}\|x\|^{p}_{X}\;\forall x\in X.

More interestingly, they exploited this quantitative estimate to provide lower bounds on the rate of metastability, which is related to Kohlenbach’s ‘proof mining’ program (see the end of
[
3
]
and the references therein). It is easy to observe that our Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
strengthen greatly (
1.5
) in the case
X
being the noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces, and thus one might expect further applications. We will take care of this elsewhere.

With a moment’s thought, there are many difficulties to apply the classical methods exploited in
[
1
,
3
,
23
,
24
]
to our setting. Our approach is mainly motivated by the study of maximal ergodic inequalities and semi-commutative harmonic analysis.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.4
rely on several auxiliary results. Our first key ingredient is to explore the noncommutative version of the classical transference principle due to Coifman and Weiss
[
8
]
. Before doing this, we introduce some notation. Let
(n_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be an increasing sequence in
{\mathbb{N}}
. A sequence of intervals
(A_{n_{i}})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
is called
nested
if it satisfies one of the following two cases:

(a) each
A_{n_{i}}
can be written as
[0,n_{i}]
;

(b) each
A_{n_{i}}
can be written as
[-n_{i},n_{i}]
.

Let
A\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
be an interval and
f:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow S_{{\mathcal{M}}}
be a bounded operator-valued function, where
S_{{\mathcal{M}}}
is the subset of
{\mathcal{M}}
with
\tau
-finite support. The averaging operator over
A
is defined by

M_{A}f(v)=\frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{y\in A}f(v+y),\quad v\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Given a nested sequence
(A_{n_{i}})_{i}
, set
T_{i}=M_{A_{n_{i}}}-M_{A_{n_{i+1}}}
.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
(A_{n_{i}})_{i}
be the case
(a)
.
If there exists a positive constant
C_{p}
such that

(1.6)

\|(T_{i}f)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}\ \forall f\in L_{p}(\mathcal{N}),

then for any invertible power bounded operator
T
, there exists an absolute constant
C
such that

\big\|\big(M_{n_{i}}(T)x-M_{n_{i+1}}(T)x\big)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell^{rc}_{2})}\leq CC_{p}\|x\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})}\ \forall x\in L_{p}({\mathcal{M}}).

A similar assertion holds for two-sided ergodic averages
(B_{n_{i}}(T))_{i}
.

The above noncommutative transference technique is partly motivated by noncommutative Calderón’s transference principle developed in
[
14
]
. According to the transference principle, to obtain (
1.6
), we need the operator-valued square function inequalities related to the Hardy-Littlewood averages on
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
(n_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be an increasing sequence of positive integers. Then the following assertions are true with a
positive constant
C_{p}
depending only on
p
:

(i)
for
p=1
,

\|(T_{i}f)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{1},\;\forall f\in L_{1}(\mathcal{N});

(ii)
for
p=\infty
,

\Big\|\sum_{i:i\in{\mathbb{N}}}T_{i}\hskip-1.0ptf\otimes e_{1i}\Big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{d}(\mathcal{R})}+\Big\|\sum_{i:i\in{\mathbb{N}}}T_{i}\hskip-1.0ptf\otimes e_{i1}\Big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{d}(\mathcal{R})}\leq C_{p}\,\|f\|_{\infty},\;\forall f\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N});

(iii)
for
1<p<\infty
,

\|(T_{i}f)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{p},\;\forall f\in L_{p}(\mathcal{N}).

Here
\mathcal{N}=L_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}})\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}}
is equipped with the tensor trace
\varphi=\sum_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\tau
and
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{N}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})
with the tensor trace
\varphi\otimes tr
, where

is the canonical trace on
\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})
.

Theorem
1.7
is an operator-valued version of the square function inequalities that were obtained in the fundamental works
[
23
,
24
]
for
p\leq 2
, and in
[
13
]
(see also
[
16
]
) for
p>2
. We refer to Section
5
for the definition of the dyadic
\mathrm{BMO}
space
\mathrm{BMO}_{d}(\mathcal{R})
.

To establish Proposition
1.6
, we need the following extension property of the bounded linear operators.

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Assume that
T
is a bounded linear operator on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
. Then
T
extends to a bounded operator on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell^{rc}_{2})
.

Lemma
1.8
follows easily from the noncommutative Khintchine inequalities, Proposition
2.2
, which should be known to experts. So we leave the proof to the interested reader. Then it is easy to see that Theorem
1.1
is a product of Theorem
1.7
and Proposition

.

However, in sharp contrast to showing Theorem
1.1
, to exploit the transference technique in order to prove Theorem

is much more complicated. To this end, we start with a reduction. More precisely, we first reduce Lamperti operators to isometries by applying the structural characterizations and dilation properties of Lamperti operators recently developed in

]
(see Section

). With this reduction, the effort will be devoted to the strong type
(p,p)
estimate of the square function for isometric operators. This will be achieved by using a similar noncommutative transference principle as Proposition
1.6
for isometries,
once
there holds the following property that concerns the isometric extension of isometries to

.

Proposition 1.9
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
T:L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
be an isometry. Then
T
extends to an isometry (resp. a contraction) on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})
if

(resp.

). If

is morevoer positive, then

extends also to an isometry on

for

.

When

is commutative, the above extension of Proposition

is almost plain. The truly noncommutative case is highly non-trivial. Our argument depends on the structural description of isometries, see e.g.
[

,

]
. Moreover, for

, some complicated duality argument are explored, and similar one has appeared in

in dealing with noncommutative maximal inequalities. For more details we refer to Section

.

Remark 1.10

What we need to point out here is that the estimates stated in all the aforementioned theorems for infinite summations should be understood as a consequence of the corresponding uniform boundedness for all finite summations by the standard approximation arguments (see e.g.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces

Throughout this paper,
{\mathcal{M}}
denotes a semifinite von Neumann algebra equipped with a
n.s.f
trace
\tau
. Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{+}
be the cone of positive elements in
{\mathcal{M}}
. Given
x\in{\mathcal{M}}_{+}
, the support projection of
x
, denoted by
\mathrm{supp}x
, is defined as the least projection
e
in
{\mathcal{M}}
such that
ex=xe=x
. Let
\mathcal{S_{{\mathcal{M}}+}}
be the set of all
x\in{\mathcal{M}}_{+}
such that
\tau(\mathrm{supp}x)<\infty
and
\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}
be the linear span of
\mathcal{S_{{\mathcal{M}}+}}
. Then
\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}
is a
w^{*}
-dense
\ast
-subalgebra of
{\mathcal{M}}
. Given
0<p<\infty
and
x\in\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}
, if we set

\|x\|_{p}=\big(\tau(|x|^{p})\big)^{1/p},

where
|x|=(x^{\ast}x)^{\frac{1}{2}}
is the modulus of
x
, then it turns out that
\|\cdot\|_{p}
is a norm in
\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}
for
1\leq p<\infty
, and a
p
-norm for
0<p<1
. The completion of
(\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}},\|\cdot\|_{p})
is the noncommutative
L_{p}
space associated to the pair
({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
, which is simply denoted by
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})
. As usual, we set
L_{\infty}({\mathcal{M}})={\mathcal{M}}
equipped with the operator norm.

We also work with noncommutative weak
L_{p}
spaces. Let
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
be the commutant of
{\mathcal{M}}
. A closed densely defined operator on
\mathcal{H}
(
\mathcal{H}
being the Hilbert space on which
{\mathcal{M}}
acts) is said to be affiliated with
{\mathcal{M}}
if it commutes with any unitary in
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
. Given a densely defined selfadjoint operator
x
, its spectral projection
\int_{\mathcal{I}}d\gamma_{x}(\lambda)
will be simply denoted by
\chi_{\mathcal{I}}(x)
, where
\mathcal{I}
is a measurable subset of
{\mathbb{R}}
.
A closed and densely defined operator

x
affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
is said to be
\tau
-measurable
if
there is
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that

\tau\big(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|x|)\big)<\infty.

Let
L_{0}({\mathcal{M}})
be the set of the
\ast
-algebra of
\tau
-measurable
operators. For
0<p<\infty
, the weak
L_{p}
space
L_{p,\infty}({\mathcal{M}})
is defined as the set of all
x
in
L_{0}({\mathcal{M}})
with the following finite quasi-norm

\|x\|_{p,\infty}=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\tau\big(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|x|)\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The following property has already been proved in
[
47
, Lemma 16]
that
for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in L_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}})
and any
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

(2.1)

\displaystyle\tau\big((\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|x_{1}+x_{2}|)\big)\leq\tau\big(\chi_{(\lambda/2,\infty)}(|x_{1}|)\big)+\tau\big(\chi_{(\lambda/2,\infty)}(|x_{2}|)\big).

The reader is referred to e.g.
[
12
,
42
]
for a comprehensive study of noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces.

2.2.
Noncommutative Hilbert-valued
L_{p}
spaces

In this subsection, we recall the noncommutative Hilbert-valued
L_{p}
spaces
[
42
]
. Let
(x_{n})
be a finite sequence in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
. Define

\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}=\|(\sum_{n}|x^{\ast}_{n}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{p},\ \|(x_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\|(\sum_{n}|x_{n}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{p}.

Then
{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}
(resp.
{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}
) is defined as the completion of all finite sequences in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}}
(resp.
\|\cdot\|_{{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}}
). The space
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})
is defined as follows.

•

If
2\leq p\leq\infty
,

L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})=L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\cap L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})

equipped with the intersection norm:

\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}=\Big(\|(x_{n})\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}+\|(x_{n})\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

•

If
1\leq p<2
,

L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})=L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})+L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})

equipped with the sum norm:

\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}=\inf\Big(\|(y_{n})\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}+\|(z_{n})\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the infimum runs over all possible decompositions
x_{n}=y_{n}+z_{n}
with
y_{n}
and
z_{n}
in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
.

This procedure is also used to define the spaces

L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})
(resp.

L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
) and
L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})
with the sum norm,

\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}=\inf_{x_{n}=y_{n}+z_{n}}\big\{\|(y_{n})\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}+\|(z_{n})\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}\big\}.

We remark that the definition of space
{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}
equals the classical one
[
42
]
, and one can easily see that the following basic properties related to space
{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}
are also valid.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
p^{\prime}
be its conjugate index. Then

(L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{c}))^{*}=L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{c}),\quad(L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{r}))^{*}=L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{r})

and

(L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc}))^{*}=L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc}).

The anti-duality bracket is given by

\langle(y_{n}),(x_{n})\rangle=\sum_{n}\tau(x_{n}y^{*}_{n}),\quad(x_{n})\subset L_{p}({\mathcal{M}}),(y_{n})\subset L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathcal{M}}).

The following noncommutative Khintchine inequalities will be frequently used. See e.g.
[

,

,
40
,
6
]
for the proof.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(\varepsilon_{n})
be a sequence of independent Rademarcher random variables on a probability space
(\Omega,P)

and
(x_{n})
be a sequence in

2.3.
Noncommutative Calderón-Zygmund decomposition

In this subsection, we introduce the noncommutative Calderón-Zygmund decomposition developed in
[
7
]
, whose construction is based on the noncommutative martingale theory. To this end, we first introduce the related notions. For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\mathcal{F}_{n}
be the set of dyadic intervals with length of
2^{n}
in
\mathbb{Z}
, that is each interval in
\mathcal{F}_{n}
can be written as
[s2^{n},(s+1)2^{n})
, where
s
is an integer.
Let
\sigma_{n}
be the
n
-th
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\mathcal{F}_{n}
and
\mathcal{N}_{n}=L_{\infty}(\mathbb{{\mathbb{Z}}},\sigma_{n})\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}}
. Recall that
\mathcal{N}=L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}}
. Then
(\mathcal{N}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence of decreasing von Neumann subalgebras of
\mathcal{N}
. Hence,
(\mathcal{N}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
forms a filtration and the resulting conditional expectations
(\mathsf{E}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
satisfy

\forall~m,n\in{\mathbb{N}},\quad\mathsf{E}_{m}\mathsf{E}_{n}=\mathsf{E}_{n}\mathsf{E}_{m}=\mathsf{E}_{\max(m,n)}

and for
f\in L_{p}(\mathcal{N})
with
1\leq p<\infty
,

(2.2)

f_{n}:=\mathsf{E}_{n}(f)=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{F}_{n}}^{\hbox{}}f_{I}\chi_{I},

where
\chi_{I}
is the characteristic function of
I
and

f_{I}=\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{y\in I}f(y).

It is easy to check that
(f_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a
L_{p}
-reverse martingale, namely
\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|f_{n}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}<\infty
.
The resulting martingale difference sequence
df=(df_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is defined by
df_{n}=f_{n-1}-f_{n}
for
n\geq 1
and
df_{0}=0
.

To give the content of noncommutative Calderón-Zygmund decomposition, consider

\mathcal{N}_{c,+}=\Big\{f:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathcal{M}}\cap L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})\,\big|\ f\geq 0,\ \overrightarrow{\mathrm{supp}}\hskip 1.0ptf\ \ \mathrm{is\ finite}\Big\},

which is dense in
L_{1}(\mathcal{N})_{+}
. Here

\overrightarrow{\mathrm{supp}}f=\mathrm{supp}\|f\|_{{}_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}}
. Observe that for any given
f\in\mathcal{N}_{c,+}
and
\lambda>0
, there exists
m_{\lambda}(f)\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
f_{n}\leq\lambda\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}}
for all
n\geq m_{\lambda}(f)
(see
[
39
, Lemma 3.1]
), where
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}}
denotes the unit
element in
\mathcal{N}
.

The following modified Cuculescu’s theorem
[
10
]
was obtained in
[
39
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{N}_{c,+}
and consider its related dyadic martingale
(f_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. Given
\lambda>0
, there exists an increasing sequence of projections
(q_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
defined by
q_{n}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}}
for
n\geq m_{\lambda}(f)
and recursively for
n<m_{\lambda}(f)

q_{n}=q_{n}(f,\lambda)=\chi_{(0,\lambda]}(q_{n+1}f_{n}q_{n+1})

such that the following conclusions hold:

(i)

q_{n}
commutes with
q_{n+1}f_{n}q_{n+1}
for each
n
;

(ii)

q_{n}
belongs to
\mathcal{N}_{n}
and
q_{n}f_{n}q_{n}\leq\lambda\hskip 1.0ptq_{n}
for each
n
;

(iii)
set
q_{0}:=q=\bigwedge_{n=0}^{m_{\lambda}(f)}q_{n}
, then
\lambda\varphi(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}}-q)\leq\|f\|_{1}.

In fact, for each
n
,
q_{n}
admits the following expression (see e.g.
[
39
]
)

q_{n}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{F}_{n}}q_{I}\chi_{I},

with
q_{I}
projections in
{\mathcal{M}}
defined by

q_{I}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{M}}&\mbox{if}\ n\geq m_{\lambda}(f),\\
\chi_{(0,\lambda]}\big(q_{\widehat{I}}f_{I}q_{\widehat{I}}\big)&\mbox{if}\ 0\leq n<m_{\lambda}(f),\end{cases}

where
\widehat{I}
is the dyadic father of
I
. Accordingly, these projections satisfy

(2.3)

\ q_{I}\leq q_{\widehat{I}},\ \ \ q_{I}\ \mbox{commutes\ with}\ q_{\widehat{I}}f_{I}q_{\widehat{I}},\ \ \ q_{I}f_{I}q_{I}\leq\lambda q_{I}.

If we define the sequence
(p_{n})_{n}
of pairwise disjoint projections by

(2.4)

p_{n}=q_{n+1}-q_{n}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{F}_{n}}(q_{\widehat{I}}-q_{I})\chi_{I}\triangleq\sum_{I\in\mathcal{F}_{n}}p_{I}\chi_{I}

for each
n
, then

(2.5)

\sum_{n}p_{n}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}}-q=q^{\perp}

and for each
n

(2.6)

\|p_{n}f_{n}p_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq 2\lambda.

Based on the previous notation, the noncommutative analogue for the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition was recently found by Caldilhac et al
[
7
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Fix
f\in\mathcal{N}_{c,+}
and
\lambda>0
. Let
(q_{n})_{n}
and
(p_{n})_{n}
be the two sequences of projections appeared in the above Cuculescu’s construction. Then there exist a projection
\zeta\in\mathcal{N}
defined by

(2.7)

\zeta=\big(\bigvee_{I\in\mathcal{F}}p_{I}\chi_{5I}\big)^{\bot},

where
5I
denotes the interval with the same center as
I
with length
|5I|=5|I|
,
and a decomposition of
f
,

(2.8)

f=g+b

such that the following assertions hold.

(i)

\lambda\varphi(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}}-\zeta)\leq 5\|f\|_{1}
.

(ii)

g=qfq+\sum_{n}p_{n}f_{n}p_{n}
satisfies

\|g\|_{1}\leq\|f\|_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|g\|_{\infty}\leq 2\lambda
.

(iii)

b=\sum_{n}b_{n}
, where

(2.9)

b_{n}=p_{n}(f-f_{n})q_{n}+q_{n+1}(f-f_{n})p_{n}.

Each
b_{n}
satisfies two cancellation conditions:

\mathsf{E}_{n}b_{n}=0
; and
for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
y\in 5I_{x,n}
,
\zeta(x)b_{n}(y)\zeta(x)=0
, where
I_{x,n}
is the unique interval in
\mathcal{F}_{n}
containing
x
.

3.
Proof of Theorem

: one reduction

To prove Theorem
1.7
, we give a reduction in the present section. Motivated by the study of the variational inequalities
[
5
]
, we split the square function into the ‘long one’ and the ‘short one’. To be more precise, fix an increasing sequence
(n_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and let
(A_{n_{i}})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be the associated nested sequence. For an interval
I_{i}=[n_{i},n_{i+1})
, one can see that there are two cases:

\bullet

Case 1:
I_{i}
contains no dyadic point, that is, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
2^{k}\notin I_{i}
;

\bullet

Case 2:
I_{i}
contains at least one dyadic point
2^{k}
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

According to the above classification, for each interval
I_{i}=[n_{i},n_{i+1})
, we split it into at most three disjoint parts

(3.1)

I_{i}:=[n_{i},\tilde{n}_{i})\cup[\tilde{n}_{i},\tilde{\tilde{n}}_{i})\cup[{\tilde{\tilde{n}}}_{i},n_{i+1})

by the law: if
I_{i}
belongs to Case 1, then set
\tilde{n}_{i}=\tilde{\tilde{n}}_{i}=n_{i+1}
; if
I_{i}
belongs to Case 2, we set

\tilde{n}_{i}=2^{k_{i}}:=\min\{2^{k}:2^{k}\in I_{i}\}

\tilde{\tilde{n}}_{i}=2^{l_{i}}:=\max\{2^{k}:2^{k}\in\bar{I_{i}}\}
where
\bar{I_{i}}
is the closure of

.

By above decomposition of intervals and using the quasi-triangle inequality for weak

norm
\|\cdot\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}
, we have

(3.2)

\begin{split}&\|(M_{A_{n_{i}}}f-M_{A_{n_{i+1}}}f)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\\
&\leq 3\|(M_{A_{n_{i}}}f-M_{A_{\tilde{n}_{i}}}f)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}+3\|(M_{A_{\tilde{n}_{i}}}f-M_{A_{\tilde{\tilde{n}}_{i}}}f)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\\
&\ \ \ \ +3\|(M_{A_{\tilde{\tilde{n}}_{i}}}f-M_{A_{n_{i+1}}}f)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}.\end{split}

On the other hand, by (
3.1
), we introduce two collections of intervals with respect to
\{[n_{i},n_{i+1})\}_{i}
:

\bullet

{\mathrm{S}}
consists of all intervals
I_{i}
belonging to Case 1, or
[{n}_{i},{\tilde{n}}_{i})
,
[{\tilde{\tilde{n}}}_{i},n_{i+1})
in (
3.1
).

\bullet

{\mathrm{L}}
consists of all intervals
[\tilde{n}_{i},\tilde{\tilde{n}}_{i})
in (
3.1
).

It is not difficult to check that

\begin{split}&\|(M_{A_{n_{i}}}f-M_{A_{n_{i+1}}}f)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\\
&\leq 3\|(M_{A_{\tilde{n}_{i}}}f-M_{A_{\tilde{\tilde{n}}_{i:}}}f)_{i:[\tilde{n}_{i},\tilde{\tilde{n}}_{i})\in L}\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\\
&\ \ +6\|(M_{A_{m_{i}}}f-M_{A_{\widetilde{m}_{i}}}f)_{i:[m_{i},\widetilde{m}_{i})\in\mathrm{S}}\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}.\end{split}

We now focus on the first term on the right hand side of the above inequality. Fix an interval
[\tilde{n}_{i},\tilde{\tilde{n}}_{i})
. By (
3.1
), we write
[\tilde{n}_{i},\tilde{\tilde{n}}_{i})=[2^{k_{i}},2^{l_{i}})
. Decompose

\displaystyle(M_{A_{2^{k_{i}}}}f-M_{A_{2^{l_{i}}}}f)=(M_{A_{2^{k_{i}}}}f-\mathsf{E}_{k_{i}}f)+(\mathsf{E}_{k_{i}}f-\mathsf{E}_{l_{i}}f)+(\mathsf{E}_{l_{i}}f-M_{A_{2^{l_{i}}}}f),

where

is the dyadic conditional expectations defined in the preliminary section. As a consequence, there exists a sequence of positive integers

such that

\begin{split}&\|(M_{A_{\tilde{n}_{i}}}f-M_{A_{\tilde{\tilde{n}}_{i}}}f)_{i:[\tilde{n}_{i},\tilde{\tilde{n}}_{i})\in\mathrm{L}}\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq 3\|(M_{A_{2^{k_{i}}}}f-\mathsf{E}_{k_{i}}f)_{{i}}\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\\
&+3\|(\mathsf{E}_{k_{i}}f-\mathsf{E}_{l_{i}}f)_{{i}}\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}+3\|(M_{A_{2^{l_{i}}}}f-\mathsf{E}_{l_{i}}f)_{{i}}\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}.\end{split}

Using the fact that

\begin{split}&\|(M_{A_{\tilde{n}_{i}}}f-M_{A_{\tilde{\tilde{n}}_{i}}}f)_{i:[\tilde{n}_{i},\tilde{\tilde{n}}_{i})\in\mathrm{L}}\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\\

) is equivalent to

(5.3)

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}T_{i}\hskip-1.0ptf\otimes e_{i1}\Big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{d}^{c}(\mathcal{R})}\lesssim\,\|f\|_{\infty}

and

(5.4)

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}T_{i}\hskip-1.0ptf\otimes e_{i1}\Big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{d}^{r}(\mathcal{R})}\lesssim\,\|f\|_{\infty}.

Now let us prove (
5.1
).

Proof of (
5.1
).

Let
f\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N})
and
I
be a dyadic cube in
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Decompose
f
as

f=f\chi_{3I}+f\chi_{{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus 3I}\triangleq f_{1}+f_{2}
, where
3I
denotes the interval with the same center as
I
such that
|3I|=3|I|
. If we set
\alpha_{I,i}=T_{i}f_{2}(c_{I})
where
c_{I}
is the center of
I
or the left neighborhood in
\mathbb{Z}
of the center if the center does not belong to
\mathbb{Z}
, and
\alpha_{I}=\sum\limits_{i}\alpha_{I,i}\otimes e_{i1}
, then

T_{i}f(x)-\alpha_{I,i}=T_{i}f_{1}(x)+(T_{i}f_{2}(x)-\alpha_{I,i})\triangleq B_{i1}f+B_{i2}f.

We first prove (
5.3
). By the operator convexity of square function
x\mapsto|x|^{2}
, we obtain

\big|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}(T_{i}f-\alpha_{I,i})\otimes e_{i1}\big|^{2}\leq 2\big|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}B_{i1}f\otimes e_{i1}\big|^{2}+2\big|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}B_{i2}f\otimes e_{i1}\big|^{2}.

The first term
B_{1}f=\sum\limits_{i}B_{i1}\hskip-1.0ptf\otimes e_{i1}
is easy to estimate. Indeed,

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{x\in I}(B_{1}f(x))^{\ast}(B_{1}f(x))\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{M}}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{x\in I}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}|T_{i}f_{1}(x)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|I|}\Big\|\sum_{x\in I}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}|T_{i}f_{1}(x)|^{2}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|I|}\sup_{\|a\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}\leq 1}\tau\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}|T_{i}f_{1}(x)a|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|I|}\sup_{\|a\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}\leq 1}\|(T_{i}(f\chi_{3I}a))_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\infty}^{2},

where in the third inequality, we considered elements in
{\mathcal{M}}
as bounded linear operators on
L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
via the left multiplication and the last inequality follows from the
L_{2}
-boundedness of
T
, namely Proposition
4.7
.

Now we turn to the second term
B_{2}f=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{S}}B_{i2}\hskip-1.0ptf\otimes e_{i1}
. Note that

\displaystyle B_{2}f(x)^{\ast}B_{2}f(x)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}|T_{i}f_{2}(x)-T_{i}f_{2}(c_{I})|^{2}

\displaystyle\ =\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}|(M_{A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(x)-M_{A_{n_{i+1}}}f_{2}(x))-(M_{A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(c_{I})-M_{A_{n_{i+1}}}f_{2}(c_{I}))|^{2}

\displaystyle\ =\sum_{k}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}|(M_{A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(x)-M_{A_{n_{i+1}}}f_{2}(x))-(M_{A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(c_{I})-M_{A_{n_{i+1}}}f_{2}(c_{I}))|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \triangleq\sum_{k}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}|F_{k,i}(x)|^{2}.

We claim that for any
k
satisfying
2^{k+1}<|I|
,
F_{k,i}(x)=0
for any
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
and
x\in I
. Indeed, fix
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
and
x\in I
. Since
2^{k+1}<|I|
and
f_{2}
is supported in
{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus 3I
, a simple geometric observation implies that both
M_{A_{n_{i+1}}}f_{2}
and
M_{A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}
are supported in
{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus I
. This is precisely the claim. Hence,

B_{2}f(x)^{\ast}B_{2}f(x)=\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}|F_{k,i}(x)|^{2}.

In the following, we further split the summation over
\mathcal{S}_{k}
into two parts by comparing
n_{i+1}-n_{i}
and
|I|
. More precisely,
we decompose
B_{2}f(x)^{\ast}B_{2}f(x)
as

\displaystyle\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\\
n_{i+1}-n_{i}<|I|\end{subarray}}}|F_{k,i}(x)|^{2}+\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\\
n_{i+1}-n_{i}\geq|I|\end{subarray}}}|F_{k,i}(x)|^{2}.

Let us estimate terms of the case
n_{i+1}-n_{i}<|I|
. By the operator-convexity of square function
x\mapsto|x|^{2}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\\
n_{i+1}-n_{i}<|I|\end{subarray}}}|F_{k,i}(x)|^{2}&\lesssim\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\\
n_{i+1}-n_{i}<|I|\end{subarray}}}|M_{A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(x)-M_{A_{n_{i+1}}}f_{2}(x)|^{2}\\
&\quad+\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\\
n_{i+1}-n_{i}<|I|\end{subarray}}}|M_{A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(c_{I})-M_{A_{n_{i+1}}}f_{2}(c_{I})|^{2}.\end{split}

Now we claim that to complete the argument of the case
n_{i+1}-n_{i}<|I|
, it is enough to show for any
z\in I

(5.5)

\displaystyle\|M_{A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(z)-M_{A_{n_{i+1}}}f_{2}(z)\|_{{\mathcal{M}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\infty}|I|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{|A_{n_{i}}|}^{|A_{n_{i+1}}|}\frac{1}{u^{2}}du\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Indeed, by (
5.5
) and the Minkowski inequality, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{x\in I}\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\\
n_{i+1}-n_{i}<|I|\end{subarray}}}|F_{k,i}(x)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\\
n_{i+1}-n_{i}<|I|\end{subarray}}}|F_{k,i}(x)|^{2}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\\
n_{i+1}-n_{i}<|I|\end{subarray}}}\|F_{k,i}(x)\|_{{\mathcal{M}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{\infty}\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\int_{|A_{n_{i}}|}^{|A_{n_{i+1}}|}\frac{1}{u^{2}}du

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|^{2}_{\infty}\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\int_{2^{k}}^{2^{k+1}}\frac{1}{u^{2}}du

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{\infty},

which is the desired estimate. It remains to show (
5.5
). To this end, fixing
z\in I
, and applying the Hölder inequality and the fact that
n_{i+1}-n_{i}<|I|
, we have

\displaystyle\|M_{A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(z)-M_{A_{n_{i+1}}}f_{2}(z)\|_{{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\frac{1}{|A_{n_{i}}|}-\frac{1}{|A_{n_{i+1}}|}\Big)\sum_{y\in z+A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(y)+\frac{1}{|A_{n_{i+1}}|}\sum_{y\in z+A_{n_{i+1}}\setminus A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(y)\Big\|_{{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{1}{|A_{n_{i}}|}-\frac{1}{|A_{n_{i+1}}|}\Big)|A_{n_{i}}|\|f_{2}\|_{\infty}+\frac{1}{|A_{n_{i+1}}|}(|A_{n_{i+1}}|-|A_{n_{i}}|)\|f_{2}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\infty}\int_{|A_{n_{i}}|}^{|A_{n_{i+1}}|}\frac{1}{u}du

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\infty}|I|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{|A_{n_{i}}|}^{|A_{n_{i+1}}|}\frac{1}{u^{2}}du\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Let us now turn to the case
n_{i+1}-n_{i}\geq|I|
. Likewise, we use the operator-convexity of square function
x\mapsto|x|^{2}
to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\\
n_{i+1}-n_{i}\geq|I|\end{subarray}}}|F_{k,i}(x)|^{2}&\lesssim\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\\
n_{i+1}-n_{i}\geq|I|\end{subarray}}}|M_{A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(x)-M_{A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(c_{I})|^{2}\\
&\quad+\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\\
n_{i+1}-n_{i}\geq|I|\end{subarray}}}|M_{A_{n_{i+1}}}f_{2}(x)-M_{A_{n_{i+1}}}f_{2}(c_{I})|^{2}.\end{split}

In this case, observe that for any
A_{n_{i}}
,

\displaystyle\|M_{A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(x)-M_{A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(c_{I})\|_{{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|A_{n_{i}}|}\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|f_{2}(y)(\chi_{x+A_{n_{i}}}(y)-\chi_{c_{I}+A_{n_{i}}}(y))\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|A_{n_{i}}|}\|f\|_{\infty}|(c_{I}+A_{n_{i}})\Delta(x+A_{n_{i}})|,

where
\Delta
denotes the usual symmetric difference of two sets. Note that
|(c_{I}+A_{n_{i}})\Delta(x+A_{n_{i}})|\lesssim|x-c_{I}|
. Then

\|M_{A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(x)-M_{A_{n_{i}}}f_{2}(c_{I})\|_{\infty}\lesssim\frac{1}{|A_{n_{i}}|}|x-c_{I}|\|f\|_{\infty}.

Moreover, it is not difficult to verify that the number of
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
such that
n_{i+1}-n_{i}\geq|I|
is smaller than
\frac{2^{k}}{|I|}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{x\in I}\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\\
n_{i+1}-n_{i}\geq|I|\end{subarray}}}|F_{k,i}(x)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|^{2}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{x\in I}\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\\
n_{i+1}-n_{i}\geq|I|\end{subarray}}}\|F_{k,i}(x)\|^{2}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{\infty}\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{x\in I}\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\frac{2^{k}}{|I|}\frac{1}{|A_{n_{i}}|^{2}}|x-c_{I}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|^{2}_{\infty}\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}\frac{2^{k}}{|I|}\frac{1}{|A_{n_{i}}|^{2}}|I|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|^{2}_{\infty}|I|\sum_{k:2^{k+1}\geq|I|}2^{-k-1}\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{\infty},

where we used the relations
|x-c_{I}|\leq|I|
and
|A_{n_{i}}|\thickapprox 2^{k+1}
. So we complete the proof of (
5.3
).

We now consider (
5.4
). However, we just deal with the term
B_{1}f=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{S}}B_{i1}\hskip-1.0ptf\otimes e_{i1}

We note that

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{x\in I}(B_{1}f(x))(B_{1}f(x))^{\ast}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{M}}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|I|}\Big\|\sum_{i_{1},i_{2}\in\mathcal{S}}\Big[\sum_{x\in I}T_{i_{1}}f_{1}(x)T_{i_{2}}f_{1}^{\ast}(x)\Big]\otimes e_{i_{1},i_{2}}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{M}}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})}

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{|I|}\|\Lambda\|_{{\mathcal{M}}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})}.

Note that

is a positive operator acting on

(=L_{2}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc}))
. Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|I|}\Big\|\sum_{x\in I}(B_{1}\hskip-1.0ptf(x))(B_{1}\hskip-1.0ptf(x))^{\ast}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{M}}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})}=\frac{1}{|I|}\sup_{\|a\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq 1}\big\langle\Lambda a,a\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|I|}\sup_{\|a\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq 1}\hskip 4.7pt\tau\Big(\big[\sum_{i_{1}\in\mathcal{S}}a_{i_{1}}^{\ast}\otimes e_{1i_{1}}\big]\,\Lambda\,\big[\sum_{i_{2}\in\mathcal{S}}a_{i_{2}}\otimes e_{i_{2}1}\big]\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|I|}\sup_{\|a\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq 1}\hskip 4.7pt\tau\sum_{x\in I}\Big|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}T_{i}f_{1}^{\ast}(x)a_{i}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|I|}\sup_{\|a\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq 1}\hskip 4.7pt\tau\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}|f_{1}^{\ast}(x)a_{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|I|}\sup_{\|a\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq 1}\hskip 4.7pt\tau(\sum_{i:i\in\mathcal{S}}|a_{i}|^{2})|3I|\,\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\ \lesssim\ \|f\|_{\infty}^{2},

where in the first inequality we applied Proposition
4.7
. This proves (
5.4
).
Finally, putting all the estimates obtained so far together with their row analogues, we get

\max\Big\{\Big\|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}T_{i}\hskip-1.0ptf\otimes e_{i1}\Big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{d}(\mathcal{R})},\Big\|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}T_{i}\hskip-1.0ptf\otimes e_{1i}\Big\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{d}(\mathcal{R})}\Big\}\lesssim\|f\|_{\infty}.

This completes the proof of the
(L_{\infty},\mathrm{BMO})

In this section, we prove Proposition

Definition 7.1

be a contraction. We call the operator
T
has a dilation if there exist a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{A}
equipped with a normal faithful semifinite trace
\tau_{\mathcal{A}}
, two contraction linear operators

,
J:L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau_{\mathcal{M}})\rightarrow L_{p}(\mathcal{A},\tau_{\mathcal{A}})
and an isometry
U:L_{p}(\mathcal{A},\tau_{\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow L_{p}(\mathcal{A},\tau_{\mathcal{A}})
such that

(7.1)

T^{n}=QU^{n}J,\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

We can use the following diagrams to represent the above decomposition

\textstyle{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau_{\mathcal{M}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{T^{n}}
\scriptstyle{J}
\textstyle{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau_{\mathcal{M}})}
\textstyle{L_{p}(\mathcal{A},\tau_{\mathcal{A}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{U^{n}}
\textstyle{L_{p}(\mathcal{A},\tau_{\mathcal{A}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Q}

for all
n\geq 0
.

We call the operator
T
has an
N
-dilation
if (
7.1
) holds for all
n\in\{0,1,\cdots,N\}
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
\mathbb{SS}(L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))
denote the set of all Lamperti contractions on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
. Moreover, for a given set
A
consisting of operators on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
, we denote by
\mbox{conv}\{A\}
the convex hull of
A
, namely

\mbox{conv}\{A\}=\bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}T_{i}:T_{i}\in A,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1,\lambda_{i}\geq 0,n\in\mathbb{N}\bigg\}.

The following result which can be seen as a
N
-dilation
theorem for
\mbox{conv}(\mathbb{SS}(L_{p}(\mathcal{M})))
was established in
[
17
, Corollary 4.7]
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Each operator
T\in\emph{conv}(\mathbb{SS}(L_{p}(\mathcal{M})))
has an
N
-dilation for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now we present a characterization theorem for isometric operators established in
[
53
,
27
]
.
Let
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{A}
be two von Neumann algebras. A complex linear map
J:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is called a Jordan
*
-homomorphism if
J(x)^{*}=J(x^{*})
and
J(x^{2})=J(x)^{2}
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
. Moreover,
J
is called the Jordan
*
-monomorphism if
J
is an injective Jordan
*
-homomorphism.

The following lemma was obtained by Størmer
[
48
]
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
J:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
be a normal (completely additive, ultraweakly continuous) Jordan
*
-homomorphism. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
denote the von Neumann subalgebra generated by
J(\mathcal{M})
in
\mathcal{A}
, and
\mathcal{Z}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}}
be the center of
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
. Then there are two projections
e,f\in\mathcal{Z}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}}
satisfying
e+f=1_{\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}}
, such that the map
x\rightarrow J(x)e
is a
*
-homomorphism and
x\rightarrow J(x)f
is a
*
-anti-homomorphism.

The following characterization of isometric operators will be frequently used.

Proposition 7.4
.

[
53
,
27
]

Let
1\leq p\neq 2<\infty
. Assume that
T:L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau_{\mathcal{M}})\rightarrow L_{p}(\mathcal{A},\tau_{\mathcal{A}})
is a bounded linear operator. Then
T
is
an isometry if and only if there exist uniquely a partial isometry
w\in\mathcal{A}
, a normal Jordan
*
-monomorphism
J:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
, and a positive self-adjoint operator
b
affiliated with
\mathcal{A}
, such that

(i)

w^{*}w=suppb=J(1)
;

(ii)
For all
x\in\mathcal{M}
,
J(x)
commutes with every spectral projection of
b
;

(iii)

T(x)=wbJ(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}
;

(iv)

\tau_{\mathcal{A}}(b^{p}J(x))=\tau_{\mathcal{M}}(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{+}
.

Now we are ready to prove Proposition
1.9
.

Proof of Proposition
1.9
.

Let
(x_{n})_{1\leq n\leq N}
be a finite sequence in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})
. By an approximation argument, without loss of generality, we may assume that
x_{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}
for all
1\leq n\leq N
. For
p=2
, the conclusion is trivially right. Indeed, in this case

\big\|\big(T(x_{n})\big)_{n}\big\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}=\sum_{n}\|Tx_{n}\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}=\|(x_{n})_{n}\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}.

We now focus on the case
2<p<\infty
. Since
T
is an isometric operator, there exist
w,b,J
such that
T=wbJ
satisfying properties (i)-(iii) in Proposition
7.4
. Moreover, by Lemma
7.3
, we may find projections
e,f
with the property that
e
and
f
commute with
b
such that

(7.2)

\begin{split}\big(T(x_{n})\big)^{*}\big(T(x_{n})\big)&=\big|\big(bJ(x_{n})e+bJ(x_{n})f\big)\big|^{2}=b^{2}J(x^{*}_{n}x_{n})e+b^{2}J(x_{n}x^{*}_{n})f.\end{split}

As a consequence,

\begin{split}\sum_{n}\big(T(x_{n})\big)^{*}\big(T(x_{n})\big)&=b^{2}J\big(\sum_{n}x^{*}_{n}x_{n}\big)e+b^{2}J\big(\sum_{n}x_{n}x^{*}_{n}\big)f.\end{split}

Set
y_{1}=\big(\sum_{n}x^{*}_{n}x_{n}\big)^{1/2}
and
y_{2}=\big(\sum_{n}x_{n}x^{*}_{n}\big)^{1/2}
. Then

\sum_{n}\big(T(x_{n})\big)^{*}\big(T(x_{n})\big)=b^{2}J(y_{1}^{2})e+b^{2}J(y_{2}^{2})f.

Moreover, applying the property that
e
and
f
commute with
b
again, we have

(7.3)

\begin{split}\big\|\big(T(x_{n})\big)_{n}\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}&=\tau\Big(\big(b^{2}J(y_{1}^{2})e+b^{2}J(y_{2}^{2})f\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big)\\
&=\tau(b^{p}J(y_{1}^{p})e)+\tau(b^{p}J(y_{2}^{p})f).\end{split}

On the other hand, by the fact that
T=wbJ
, we get

\displaystyle\big(T(x_{n})\big)\big(T(x_{n})\big)^{*}=(wbJ(x_{n}))(wbJ(x_{n}))^{*}

\displaystyle=wb^{2}J(x_{n})J(x_{n})^{*}w^{*}=wb^{2}J(x_{n}x_{n}^{*})ew^{*}+wb^{2}J(x_{n}^{*}x_{n})fw^{*},

which gives rise to

\sum_{n}\big(T(x_{n})\big)\big(T(x_{n})\big)^{*}=wb^{2}J(y_{2}^{2})ew^{*}+wb^{2}J(y_{1}^{2})fw^{*}.

Then by the above observations and Proposition
7.4
(i), we find

(7.4)

\begin{split}&\big\|\big(T(x_{n})\big)_{n}\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}=\|w(b^{2}J(y_{2}^{2})e+b^{2}J(y_{1}^{2})f)w^{*}\|_{L_{\frac{p}{2}}({\mathcal{M}})}^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
&=\|(b^{2}J(y_{2}^{2})e+b^{2}J(y_{1}^{2})f)^{\frac{1}{2}}w^{*}w(b^{2}J(y_{2}^{2})e+b^{2}J(y_{1}^{2})f)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L_{\frac{p}{2}}({\mathcal{M}})}^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
&=\|b^{2}J(y_{2}^{2})e+b^{2}J(y_{1}^{2})f\|_{L_{\frac{p}{2}}({\mathcal{M}})}^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
&=\tau(b^{p}J(y_{2}^{p})e)+\tau(b^{p}J(y_{1}^{p})f).\end{split}

Therefore, combining (
7.3
) with (
7.4
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\big\|\big(T(x_{n})\big)_{n}\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}+\big\|\big(T(x_{n})\big)_{n}\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}

\displaystyle=\tau(b^{p}J(y_{1}^{p})e)+\tau(b^{p}J(y_{2}^{p})f)+\tau(b^{p}J(y_{2}^{p})e)+\tau(b^{p}J(y_{1}^{p})f)

\displaystyle=\tau(b^{p}J(y_{1}^{p}))+\tau(b^{p}J(y_{2}^{p})).

Moreover, by Proposition
7.4
(iv), we have

\begin{split}\big\|\big(T(x_{n})\big)_{n}\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}+\big\|\big(T(x_{n})\big)_{n}\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}&=\tau(y_{1}^{p})+\tau(y_{2}^{p})\\
&=\|(x_{n})_{n}\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}+\|(x_{n})_{n}\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})},\end{split}

which yields
\|\big(T(x_{n})\big)_{n}\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}=\|(x_{n})_{n}\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}
. So
T
is an isometry on
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})
and we finish the proof of the case
2<p<\infty
.

We then turn to the case
1<p<2
. Since
(x_{n})_{n}\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})
, for any given
\varepsilon>0
, there are two finite sequences
(g_{n})_{n}
and
(h_{n})_{n}
such that
x_{n}=g_{n}+h_{n}
and

(7.5)

\|(g_{n})_{n}\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}+\|(h_{n})_{n}\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}\leq\|(x_{n})_{n}\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}+\varepsilon.

Then by Lemma
7.3
, we may decompose
T(x_{n})
as

T(x_{n})=T(g_{n})e+T(h_{n})f+(T(g_{n})f+T(h_{n})e).

By the definition of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}
, we have

(7.6)

\begin{split}&\big\|\big(T(x_{n})\big)_{n}\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\\
&\leq\big\|\big(T(g_{n})e+T(h_{n})f\big)_{n}\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}+\big\|\big(T(g_{n})f+T(h_{n})e\big)_{n}\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}.\end{split}

Set
y_{3}=\big(\sum_{n}g^{*}_{n}g_{n}\big)^{1/2}
and
y_{4}=\big(\sum_{n}h_{n}h^{*}_{n}\big)^{1/2}
. Similar to (
7.3
), one has

(7.7)

\big\|\big(T(g_{n})e+T(h_{n})f\big)_{n}\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\tau(b^{p}J(y_{3}^{p})e)+\tau(b^{p}J(y_{4}^{p})f);

and similar to (
7.4
), one gets

(7.8)

\big\|\big(T(g_{n})f+T(h_{n})e\big)_{n}\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}=\tau(b^{p}J(y_{3}^{p})f)+\tau(b^{p}J(y_{4}^{p})e).

Together (
7.7
), (
7.8
) and (
7.6
) with Proposition (
7.4
)(iv) and (
7.5
), we get

\big\|\big(T(x_{n})\big)_{n}\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}^{p}\leq\|(x_{n})_{n}\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}+\varepsilon.

By the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
, we proved that
T
extends to a contraction on
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})
.

Finally, we assume that
T
is positive. It is sufficient to consider the case
1<p<2
. The following facts are taken from
[
17
]
.
Note that
T
is a positive isometry. Then
T
is Lamperti and
T=bJ
, where
b,J
are defined in Lemma
7.3
(see
[
17
, Theorem 3.3 and Remark 3.9]
). Let
\mathcal{A}
be the von Neumann algebra generated by
J(\mathcal{M})
. By Lemma
7.3
, we may decompose
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
are two von Neumann subalgebras of
\mathcal{A}
, and write
J=J_{1}+J_{2}
such that
J_{1}:{\mathcal{M}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{1}
is a normal
*
-homomorphism and
J_{2}:{\mathcal{M}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{2}
is a normal
*
-anti-homomorphism. Let
\sigma:\mathcal{A}_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{2}^{op}
be the usual opposite map and define

\Sigma:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{2}^{op},~\quad\Sigma=\mbox{Id}_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\oplus\sigma.

Then
\Sigma\circ J
is a normal
*
-homomorphism and
\Sigma(J({\mathcal{M}}))
is a von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{2}^{op}
.
Define

\varphi:\Sigma(J({\mathcal{M}}))_{+}\rightarrow[0,\infty],\quad x\mapsto\tau(b^{p}\Sigma^{-1}x).

Then
\varphi
becomes a normal semifinite trace on
\Sigma(J({\mathcal{M}}))
. Let
L_{p}(\Sigma(J({\mathcal{M}})),\varphi)
be the associated noncommutative
L_{p}
space. Then
\Sigma\circ J
extends to a positive surjective isometry

\tilde{J}:L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)\rightarrow L_{p}(\Sigma(J({\mathcal{M}})),\varphi),\quad x\mapsto\Sigma(J(x)),

whence
\tilde{J}^{-1}
is well defined, positive and isometric on
L_{p}(\Sigma(J({\mathcal{M}})),\varphi)
. We refer to
[
17
, Proposition 5.3]
for the detailed description of above facts.

As being an extension of the normal
*
-homomorphism
\Sigma\circ J
, it is easy to check that
\tilde{J}
extends to an isometry from
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{rc})
onto
L_{p}(\Sigma(J({\mathcal{M}})),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{rc})
. And thus
\tilde{J}^{-1}
extends also to an isometry from
L_{p}(\Sigma(J({\mathcal{M}})),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{rc})
to
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{rc})
.

To complete the argument,
there remains to verify that the embedding

L_{p}(\Sigma(J({\mathcal{M}})),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{rc})\rightarrow L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{rc}),\quad(x_{n})_{n}\mapsto(b\Sigma^{-1}x_{n})_{n}

is an isometry. Let
p^{\prime}
be the conjugate index of
p
, that is
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. Then
2<p^{\prime}<\infty
. Let
(x_{n})
be a finite sequence in
L_{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{rc})
. Write
x_{n}=(x_{n,1},x_{n,2})\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{A}_{1})\oplus L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{A}_{2}^{op})
. Then

\displaystyle\|(b^{p/p^{\prime}}\Sigma^{-1}x_{n})_{n}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{c})}=\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n}|b^{p/p^{\prime}}\Sigma^{-1}x_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)}

\displaystyle=\tau\Big(b^{p}\Big(\sum_{n}\Sigma^{-1}(x_{n}^{*})\Sigma^{-1}(x_{n})\Big)^{p^{\prime}/2}\Big)=\tau\Big(b^{p}\Big(\sum_{n}\Big(x_{n,1}^{*}x_{n,1}+\sigma^{-1}(x_{n,2}x^{*}_{n,2})\Big)\Big)^{p^{\prime}/2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\tau\Big(b^{p}\Big(\sum_{n}x_{n,1}^{*}x_{n,1}+\sigma^{-1}\big(\sum_{n}x_{n,2}x^{*}_{n,2}\big)\Big)^{p^{\prime}/2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\tau\Big(b^{p}\Big(\sum_{n}x_{n,1}^{*}x_{n,1}\Big)^{p^{\prime}/2}\Big)+\tau\Big(b^{p}\Big(\sigma^{-1}\big(\sum_{n}x_{n,2}x^{*}_{n,2}\big)\Big)^{p^{\prime}/2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\|(x_{n,1})_{n}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{c})}+\|(x_{n,2})_{n}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{r})}.

Note that
(b^{p/p^{\prime}}\Sigma^{-1}x_{n})^{*}=b^{p/p^{\prime}}\Sigma^{-1}x_{n}^{*}
. Then replacing
b^{p/p^{\prime}}\Sigma^{-1}x_{n}
by
(b^{p/p^{\prime}}\Sigma^{-1}x_{n})^{*}
in the above identities, we deduce that

\|(b^{p/p^{\prime}}\Sigma^{-1}x_{n})_{n}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{r})}=\|(x_{n,1})_{n}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{r})}+\|(x_{n,2})_{n}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{c})}.

Combining the above two identities, we have

\displaystyle\|(b^{p/p^{\prime}}\Sigma^{-1}x_{n})_{n}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{c})}+\|(b^{p/p^{\prime}}\Sigma^{-1}x_{n})_{n}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{r})}

\displaystyle=\|(x_{n,1})_{n}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{r})}+\|(x_{n,2})_{n}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{r})}

\displaystyle+\|(x_{n,1})_{n}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{c})}+\|(x_{n,2})_{n}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{c})}

\displaystyle=\|(x_{n})_{n}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{rc})}.

Hence the map

\phi:L_{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{rc})\rightarrow L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{rc}),\quad(x_{n})_{n}\mapsto(b^{p/p^{\prime}}\Sigma^{-1}x_{n})_{n}

is an isometry.

By Proposition
2.1
, we know that
(L_{p}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{rc}))^{*}=L_{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{rc})
. Let
\phi^{*}
be the conjugate of
\phi
. Then we can check that
\phi^{*}((b\Sigma^{-1}x_{n})_{n})=(x_{n})_{n}
(see the proof of Proposition 5.3 in
[
17
]
).

Therefore, recalling that
T
is a contraction on
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{rc})
with
T=bJ
and together with above observations, we finally get

\displaystyle\|(x_{n})_{n}\|_{L_{p}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{rc})}=\|\phi^{*}((b\Sigma^{-1}x_{n})_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{rc})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(b\Sigma^{-1}x_{n})_{n}\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{rc})}=\|(T\tilde{J}^{-1}x_{n})_{n}\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{rc})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\tilde{J}^{-1}x_{n})_{n}\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{rc})}=\|(x_{n})_{n}\|_{L_{p}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{rc})},

which gives
\|(x_{n})_{n}\|_{L_{p}(\Sigma(J(M)),\varphi;\ell_{2}^{rc})}=\|(b\Sigma^{-1}x_{n})_{n}\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{rc})}
. So the proof of Proposition
1.9
is complete.
∎

Remark 7.5
.

For a positive isometry
T
on
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})
(
1<p<2
), once one gets that it extends to a contraction on
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})
, there is another way to conclude its isometry: For
T=bJ
, we consider another operator
T^{\prime}(x)=b^{p-1}J(x)
initially defined on
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}
, which extends to an isometry on
L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})
. Thus one may deduce that

\big|\sum_{n}\tau(b^{p}J(x_{n})J(y_{n}))\big|\leq\big\|(T(x_{n}))_{n}\|_{{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{rc})}}\|(y_{n})_{n}\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{rc})}.

But by commutation relations and the tracial property

\tau(b^{p}J(x_{n})J(y_{n}))=\tau(b^{p}J(y_{n})J(x_{n})),

and thus

2\tau(b^{p}J(x_{n})J(y_{n}))=\tau(b^{p}J(x_{n}y_{n}+y_{n}x_{n}))=2\tau(x_{n}y_{n}).

Therefore,

\big|\sum_{n}\tau(x_{n}y_{n})\big|\leq\big\|(T(x_{n}))_{n}\|_{{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{rc})}}\|(y_{n})_{n}\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{rc})},

which gives

\big\|(x_{n})_{n}\|_{{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{rc})}}\leq\big\|(T(x_{n}))_{n}\|_{{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau;\ell_{2}^{rc})}}.

Unfortunately, at the moment of writing, we still have no idea on how to remove the positivity assumption in the case
1<p<2
.

Based on Proposition
1.9
, a similar Coifman-Weiss’s transference principle as Proposition
1.6
holds also for isometries and we thus get the following quantitative mean ergodic theorem for isometries.

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
T:L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
be an isometry. Let
(n_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be any increasing sequence of positive integers. Then for
2\leq p<\infty
,

\big\|\big(M_{n_{i}}(T)x-M_{n_{i+1}}(T)x\big)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\big\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell^{rc}_{2})}\lesssim\|x\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})},\forall x\in L_{p}({\mathcal{M}}).

If
T
is moreover positive, then the above inequality holds also for
1<p<2
.

Lemma
7.6
can be proved in a similar way as Proposition
1.6
by observing that the “
\lesssim
” in (
6.2
) can be strengthened to “
=
” due to Proposition
1.9
. We omit the details.

By Lemma
7.6
, we now can conclude the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Recall that
M_{n}(T)=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T^{k}
. By the density argument, it is enough to prove the theorem for any finite positive sequence
(n_{i})_{0\leq i\leq N}
.

Fix an arbitrary
N\geq 1
. Note that
T\in\mathfrak{S}
. Then by definition, we can find
(T_{j})\subseteq\mbox{conv}(\mathbb{SS}(L_{p}(\mathcal{M})))
such that
T_{j}
converges to
T
in the sense of strong operator topology. Moreover, according to Lemma
7.2
, for each
T_{j}
, there exist two contractions
Q_{j,N}
,
J_{j,N}
and one isometry
U_{j,N}
such that
T_{j}^{n_{i}}=Q_{j,N}U_{j,N}^{n_{i}}J_{j,N}
for every
0\leq n_{i}\leq n_{N}
. As a consequence, by Proposition
1.8
,
Q_{j,N}
and
J_{j,N}
extend to two bounded operators on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})
.
Therefore, for any fixed
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
, together with Lemma
7.6
, we get

\displaystyle\|\big(M_{n_{i}}(T_{j})x-M_{n_{i+1}}(T_{j})x\big)_{0\leq i\leq N}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}

\displaystyle=\|(Q_{j,N}(M_{n_{i}}-M_{n_{i+1}})(U_{j,N})J_{j,N}x)_{0\leq i\leq N}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}

\displaystyle\leq\|((M_{n_{i}}-M_{n_{i+1}})(U_{j,N})J_{j,N}x)_{0\leq i\leq N}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|J_{j,N}x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}.

Consequently, combined with the noncommutative Khintchine inequality in
L_{p}
space-Proposition
2.2
,
we finally deduce that

\displaystyle\|\big(M_{n_{i}}(T)x-M_{n_{i+1}}(T)x\big)_{0\leq i\leq N}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\big(\big(M_{n_{i}}-M_{n_{i+1}}\big)T_{j}x)_{0\leq i\leq N}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}+\|\big(\big(M_{n_{i}}-M_{n_{i+1}}\big)(T-T_{j})x\big))_{0\leq i\leq N}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}+\|\big(\big(M_{n_{i}}-M_{n_{i+1}}\big)(T-T_{j})x\big))_{0\leq i\leq N}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}

\displaystyle\approx\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}+\bigg\|\sum_{i=0}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\big(M_{n_{i}}-M_{n_{i+1}}\big)(T-T_{j})x\bigg\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}+\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\big(M_{n_{i}}-M_{n_{i+1}}\big)(T-T_{j})x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})},

By letting
j\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain the desired conclusion.
∎

Remark 7.7
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space. Suppose that
T:L_{p}(\Omega)\rightarrow L_{p}(\Omega)
is a positive contraction. Define
\widetilde{T}=T\otimes I_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})}
. By the Akcoglu dilation theorem (cf.
[
1
]
) and Theorem
1.4
, we immediately obtain the quantitative mean ergodic theorem for
\widetilde{T}
, namely

\big\|\big(M_{n_{i}}(\widetilde{T})x-M_{n_{i+1}}(\widetilde{T})x\big)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\Omega)\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}};\ell^{rc}_{2})}\lesssim\|x\|_{p},~\forall\ x\in L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\Omega)\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc}),

where
(n_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is any increasing sequence of positive integers.

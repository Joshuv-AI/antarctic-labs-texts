τ \tau -Tilting modules over one-point extensions by a simple module at a source point Thanks: This work was partially supported by NSFC (Grant No. 11971225).
Hanpeng Gao

Abstract
Let B B be an one-point extension of a finite dimensional k k -algebra A A by a simple A A -module at a source point i i . In this paper, we classify the τ \tau -tilting modules over B B . Moreover, it is shown that there are equations | τ ​ - ​ tilt ⁡ B | = | τ ​ - ​ tilt ⁡ A | + | τ ​ - ​ tilt ⁡ A / ⟨ e i ⟩ | and | s ​ τ ​ - ​ tilt ⁡ B | = 2 | s ​ τ ​ - ​ tilt ⁡ A | + | s ​ τ ​ - ​ tilt ⁡ A / ⟨ e i ⟩ | . |\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits B|=|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A|+|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle|\quad\text{and}\quad|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits B|=2|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A|+|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle|. As a consequence, we can calculate the numbers of τ \tau -tilting modules and support τ \tau -tilting modules over linearly Dynkin type algebras whose square radical are zero. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : 16G20, 16G60. Key words : support τ \tau -tilting modules, one-point extensions, Dynkin type algebras.

Introduction

As a generalization of tilting module, the concept of support
\tau
-tilting modules is introduced by Adachi, Iyama and Reiten
[
2
]
. They are very important in representation theory of algebras because they are in bijection with some important objects including
functorially finite torsion classes,
2-term silting complexes, cluster-tilting objects. It is very interesting to calculate the number of support
\tau
-tilting modules over a given algebra.

For Dynkin type algebras
\Delta_{n}
, the numbers of tilting modules and support tilting modules were first calculated in
[
6
]
via cluster algebras, and later in
[
8
]
via representation theory.

Recall that a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra is said to be a
Nakayama~algebra
if every indecomposable projective module and every indecomposable injective module has a unique composition series. May authors calculate the numbers of
\tau
-tilting modules and support
\tau
-tilting modules over Nakayama algebras. In particular, for square radical zero Nakayama algebra
\Lambda^{2}_{n}
with
n
simple modules, there are the following recurrence relations (see,
[
1
,
3
,
5
]
),

|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda^{2}_{n}|=|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda^{2}_{n-1}|+|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda^{2}_{n-2}|\quad\text{and}\quad|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda^{2}_{n}|=2|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda^{2}_{n-}|+|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda^{2}_{n-2}|.

In this paper, we consider
\tau
-tilting modules and support
\tau
-tilting modules over the one-point extension
B
of
A
by a simple
A
-module at a source point
i
. We will show that there is a bijection

\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits B\mapsto\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A\coprod\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle.

We also get the following equations,

|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits B|=|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A|+|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle|\quad\text{and}\quad|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits B|=2|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A|+|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle|.

As an application , we can calculate the numbers of
\tau
-tilting modules and support
\tau
-tilting modules over linearly Dynkin type algebras whose
square radical are zero.

Throughout this paper, all algebras will be basic, connected, finite dimensional
k
-algebras over an algebraically closed field
k
. For an algebra
A
, we denote by
\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits A

the category of finitely generated left
A
-modules and by
\tau
the Auslander-Reiten
translation of
A
. Let
P_{i}
be the indecomposable projective module and
S_{i}
the simple module of
A
corresponding to the point
i
for
i=1,2,\cdots,n
. For
M\in\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits A
, we also denote by
|M|
the number of pairwise nonisomorphic indecomposable summands of
M
and by
\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits M
the full subcategory of
\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits A
consisting of direct summands of finite direct sums of copies of
M
. For a set
X
, we denote by
|X|
the cardinality of
X
. For two sets
X,Y
,
X\coprod Y
means the disjoint union.

2
Main results

Let
A
be an algebra. we recall the definition about support
\tau
-tilting modules.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
2
, Definition 0.1]
)

Let
M\in\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits A
.

(1)

M
is
\tau
-rigid if
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{A}(M,\tau M)=0
.

(2)

M
is
\tau
-tilting if it is
\tau
-rigid and
|M|=|A|
.

(3)

M
is support
\tau
-tilting if it is a
\tau
-tilting
A/AeA
-module
for some idempotent
e
of
A
.

We will denote by
\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A
(respectively,
\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A
) the set of isomorphism classes of basic
\tau
-tilting (respectively, support
\tau
-tilting)
A
-modules.

Let
X\in\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits A
. The
one-point extension
of
A
by
X
is defined as the following matrix algebra

B=\left(\begin{matrix}A&X\\
0&k\\
\end{matrix}\right)

with the ordinary matrix addition and the multiplication, and we write
B:=A[X]
with
a
the extension point.

Let
A
be an algebra with a source point
i
, in this paper, we always assume
B:=A[S_{i}]
. In this case,
P_{a}
is an indecomposable projective-injective
B
-module and
S_{a}
is a simple injective
B
-module by
[
4
, Proposition 2.5(c)]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
M\in\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits B
. If
M
is
\tau
-rigid, then
M\oplus P_{a}
is also. Moreover,
if
M
is
\tau
-tilting, then it have
P_{a}
as a direct summand.

Proof.

Since
S_{i}
is simple, there are only two indecomposable
B
-modules
P_{a}
,
S_{a}
which have
S_{a}
as a composition factor and they are injective, we have
\tau M
have no
S_{1}
as
a composition factor.Thus,
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{A}(P_{a},\tau M)=0
and, we get
M\oplus P_{a}
is
\tau
-rigid. If
M
is
\tau
-tilting, then it is maximal
\tau
-rigid by
[
2
, Theorem 2.12]
. Hence, it have
P_{a}
as a direct summand.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

There is a bijection

\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits B\longleftrightarrow\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A\coprod\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle.

Proof.

Let
M\in\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A\coprod\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle
. If
M\in\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A
, then
\tau M
has no
S_{a}
as a composition factor since the vertex
a
is a source in
B
, and hence
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{B}(P_{a},\tau M)=0
. Therefore,
M\oplus P_{a}
is a
\tau
-tilting
B
-module since it is
\tau
-rigid and
|M\oplus P_{a}|=|M|+1=|A|+1=|B|
. If
M\in\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle
, then
M
has no
S_{i}
as a composition factor and
\tau M
has no
S_{a}
as a composition factor.
Note that there is an almost split sequence
0\to S_{i}\to P_{a}\to S_{a}\to 0
, we have
\tau S_{a}=S_{i}
. Thus,

\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{B}(M\oplus P_{a}\oplus S_{a},\tau(M\oplus P_{1}\oplus S_{a}))=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{B}(M\oplus P_{a}\oplus S_{a},\tau M\oplus S_{i})=0.

So,
M\oplus P_{a}\oplus S_{a}
is a
\tau
-tilting
B
-module.

Conversely, Let
M\in\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits B
. Then we decompose
M
as
M=P_{a}\oplus N
by Lemma
2.2
. If
N
has no
S_{a}
as direct summand, the
N
is a
\tau
-tilting
B/\langle e_{a}\rangle(\cong A)
-module, that is,

N\in\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A
. If
N
has
S_{a}
as direct summand, then we decompose
N
as
N=S_{a}\oplus L
where
L
has no
S_{a}
as a composition factor. We claim that

L
has no
S_{i}
as a composition factor. Otherwise, there is a summand
K
of
L
such that the top of
K
is
S_{i}
since
i
is a source point. In particular,
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{B}(L,S_{i})\neq 0
. This implies

\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{B}(M,\tau M)=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{B}(L\oplus P_{a}\oplus S_{a},\tau L\oplus S_{i})\neq 0.

This is a contradiction. Hence,
L
is a
\tau
-tilting
A/\langle e_{i}\rangle
-module , that is,
L\in\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle
.

∎

Corollary 2.4
.

All
\tau
-tilting
B
-modules are exactly those forms
P_{a}\oplus M_{1}
and
P_{a}\oplus S_{a}\oplus M_{2}
where
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are
\tau
-tilting modules over
A
and
A/\langle e_{i}\rangle
respectively.

The above Corollary give a relation about
|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits B|
and
|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A|
.

Corollary 2.5
.

We have

|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits B|=|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A|+|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle|.

Let
A
be an algebra and
M\in\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits A
.
M
is called a (classical)
tilting
module if

(1)

The projective dimension of
M
is at most one.

(2)

\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits_{A}^{1}(M,M)=0
.

(3)

|M|=|A|
.

Hence, an
A
-module
M
is tilting if and only if it is a
\tau
-tilting and
its projective dimension is at most one by the Auslander-Reiten formula. The set of all tilting
A
-modules will be denoted by
\mathop{\rm tilt}\nolimits A
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
A
be an algebra with a source
i
. Assume that
i
is not a sink and
B:=A[S_{i}]
. All tilting
B
-modules are exactly those forms
P_{a}\oplus M_{1}
where
M_{1}
is a tilting module over
A
. In particular,
|\mathop{\rm tilt}\nolimits B|=|\mathop{\rm tilt}\nolimits A|
.

Proof.

By Corollary
2.4
, All
\tau
-tilting
B
-modules are exactly those forms
P_{a}\oplus M_{1}
and
P_{a}\oplus S_{a}\oplus M_{2}
where
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are
\tau
-tilting modules over
A
and
A/\langle e_{i}\rangle
respectively.
Note that the projective dimension of
M_{1}
as
A
-module is equal to the projective dimension of
M_{1}
as
B
-module since
a
is a source of
B
. Hence
P_{a}\oplus M_{1}
is a tilting
B
-module if and only if
M_{1}
is a tilting
A
-module.

Since there is an exact sequence
0\to S_{i}\to P_{a}\to S_{a}\to 0
in
\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits B
, we have the projective dimension of
S_{a}
is at least two since
S_{i}
is not projective when
i
is not a sink. Hence
P_{a}\oplus S_{a}\oplus M_{2}
is not tilting.
Thus,
|\mathop{\rm tilt}\nolimits B|=|\mathop{\rm tilt}\nolimits A|
.
∎

Suppose that
A
is an algebra with an indecomposable projective-injective module
Q
. Let
\overline{A}:=A/\mathop{\rm soc}\nolimits(Q)
and

\mathcal{N}:=\{N\in\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\overline{A}\mid Q/\mathop{\rm soc}\nolimits(Q)\in\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits N~\text{and}~\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{A}(N,Q)=0\}.

The following Lemma can be found in
[
1
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
A
be an algebra with an indecomposable projective-injective module
Q
. Then there is an isomorphism of posets

\mathop{\rm H}\nolimits(A)\longleftrightarrow\mathop{\rm H}\nolimits{(\overline{A})}^{\mathcal{N}}.

Applying this result to the algebra
B
, we have the following

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{N}:=\{S_{a}\oplus L\mid L\in\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle\}.
Then there is an isomorphism of posets

\mathop{\rm H}\nolimits(B)\longleftrightarrow\mathop{\rm H}\nolimits{(A\times k)}^{\mathcal{N}}.

Proof.

Take
Q=P_{a}
which is an indecomposable projective-injective
B
-module. Since
\mathop{\rm soc}\nolimits P_{a}\cong S_{i}
, we have
\overline{B}=B/S_{i}\cong A\times k
and
P_{a}/S_{i}\cong S_{a}
. We only need to show
\mathcal{N}=\{S_{a}\oplus L\mid L\in\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle\}
in Lemma
2.8
. Note that

\begin{split}\mathcal{N}&=\{N\in\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\overline{B}\mid Q/\mathop{\rm soc}\nolimits(Q)\in\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits N~\text{and}~\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{B}(N,Q)=0\}\\
&=\{N\in\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits(A\times k)\mid S_{a}\in\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits N~\text{and}~\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{B}(N,P_{a})=0\}\\
&=\{S_{a}\oplus L\mid L\in\mathop{\rm silt}\nolimits A~\text{and}~\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{B}(L,P_{a})=0\}\\
&=\{S_{a}\oplus L\mid L\in\mathop{\rm silt}\nolimits A~\text{and}~\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{A}(L,S_{i})=0\}.\end{split}

Since
i
is a source point of
A
, this implies
L
has no
S_{i}
as a composition factor and hence it is exactly a support
\tau
-tilting
A/\langle e_{i}\rangle
-module. Thus,

\mathcal{N}=\{S_{a}\oplus L\mid L\in\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle\}.

∎

Corollary 2.10
.

We have

|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits B|=2|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A|+|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle|.

Proof.

According to the definition of
\mathop{\rm H}\nolimits{(A\times k)}^{\mathcal{N}}
, we have

|\mathop{\rm H}\nolimits{(A\times k)}^{\mathcal{N}}|=|\mathop{\rm H}\nolimits{(A\times k)}|+|{\mathcal{N}}|=2|\mathop{\rm H}\nolimits(A)|+|{\mathcal{N}}|=2|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A|+|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle|.

Therefore,
|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits B|=|\mathop{\rm H}\nolimits(B)|=2|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A|+|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits A/\langle e_{i}\rangle|
by Proposition
2.9
.
∎

Now, it is easy to draw the quiver of
\mathop{\rm H}\nolimits(B)
from the quiver of
\mathop{\rm H}\nolimits(A)
as follows.

\mathop{\rm H}\nolimits(A)\to\mathop{\rm H}\nolimits(A\times k)\to\mathop{\rm H}\nolimits{(A\times k)}^{\mathcal{N}}\cong\mathop{\rm H}\nolimits(B).

New quermassintegral and Poincaré type inequalities for non-convex domains
Yingxiang Hu, Mohammad N. Ivaki

Abstract
. In the first part of this paper, we study the following non-homogeneous, locally constrained inverse curvature flow in Euclidean space ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} , (0.1) x ˙ = ( 1 E k ​ ( κ ^ ) E k − 1 ​ ( κ ^ ) − α − ⟨ x , ν ⟩ ) ν , k = 2 , 3 , … , n − 1 . \displaystyle\dot{x}=\left(\frac{1}{\frac{E_{k}(\hat{\kappa})}{E_{k-1}(\hat{\kappa})}-\alpha}-\langle x,\nu\rangle\right)\nu,\quad k=2,3,\ldots,n-1. Assuming that the initial hypersurface ℳ 0 ⊂ ℝ n + 1 \mathcal{M}_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1} is star-shaped and its shifted principal curvatures κ ^ = κ + α ⁡ ( 1 , … , 1 ) \hat{\kappa}=\kappa+\alpha(1,\ldots,1) lie in the convex set (0.2) Γ α , k := Γ k − 1 ∩ { λ ∈ ℝ n : E k ​ ( λ ) − α ​ E k − 1 ​ ( λ ) > 0 } , \displaystyle\Gamma_{\alpha,k}:=\Gamma_{k-1}\cap\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,E_{k}(\lambda)-\alpha E_{k-1}(\lambda)>0\}, we show that the flow admits a smooth solution that exists for all positive times, and it converges smoothly to a round sphere. 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Quermassintegral inequalities

The quermassintegrals of a bounded domain
\Omega
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
are defined by

(1.1)

\displaystyle W_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega),

\displaystyle W_{k}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\partial\Omega}E_{k-1}(\kappa)d\mu,\quad k=1,\ldots,n+1.

Here,
E_{i}(\kappa)
is the
i
-th elementary symmetric function of principal curvatures of
\partial\Omega
normalized so that
E_{i}(1,\ldots,1)=1
. It is also convenient to define a normalization of quermassintegrals:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{n+1-k}W_{k}(\Omega).

The
m
th Garding cone is defined by

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{m}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\penalty\ E_{i}(\lambda)>0,\,\forall i=1,\ldots,m\}.

For
\alpha>0
, define

(1.4)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\alpha,k}=\Gamma_{k-1}\cap\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,E_{k}(\lambda)-\alpha E_{k-1}(\lambda)>0\}.

The set
\Gamma_{\alpha,k}
is convex and contains the shifted cone
\alpha(1,\ldots,1)+\Gamma_{k}
.

Throughout the paper, all hypersurfaces are considered smooth, closed and embedded.
A hypersurface
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is said to be
(\alpha,k)
-convex, if its shifted principal curvatures
\hat{\kappa}=\kappa+\alpha(1,\ldots,1)
at any point belong to
\Gamma_{\alpha,k}
.
Moreover, we say
\mathcal{M}
is weakly
(\alpha,k)
-convex, if
\hat{\kappa}
lies in the closure of
\Gamma_{\alpha,k}
. A hypersurface that is
(0,k)
-convex is said to be
k
-convex.

Fix
k\in\{2,\ldots,n-1\}
.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{0}
be a
(\alpha,k)
-convex hypersurface. In the first part of this paper, we study the flow of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
under the locally constrained inverse curvature flow

(1.5)

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{F}-\langle x,\nu\rangle\right)\nu,

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle:=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}E_{k-i}(\kappa)}{\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}E_{k-1-i}(\kappa)},\quad\beta_{i}:=\binom{k-1}{i}\alpha^{i}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{0}
be a star-shaped,
(\alpha,k)
-convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then the flow (
1.5
) admits a star-shaped,
(\alpha,k)
-convex solution
\mathcal{M}_{t}
which exists for all times. Moreover, as
t\rightarrow\infty
,
\mathcal{M}_{t}
converges smoothly to a round sphere with the same value of
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\widetilde{W}_{k-i}
as
\mathcal{M}_{0}
.

Let
B_{r}
denote a ball of radius
r
. Define

(1.6)

\displaystyle g_{\alpha,k}(r)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\widetilde{W}_{k-i}(B_{r}),\quad h_{\alpha,k}(r)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\widetilde{W}_{k+1-i}(B_{r}),\quad f_{\alpha,k}=h_{\alpha,k}\circ g_{\alpha,k}^{-1}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with smooth boundary
\mathcal{M}=\partial\Omega
. Assume that
\mathcal{M}
is star-shaped and
(\alpha,k)
-convex. Then

(1.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\widetilde{W}_{k+1-i}(\Omega)\geq f_{\alpha,k}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\widetilde{W}_{k-i}(\Omega)\right),

and equality holds only for balls. Moreover, for
k=2
, the inequality holds for star-shaped, weakly
(\alpha,2)
-convex hypersurfaces:

(1.8)

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{3}(\Omega)+\alpha\widetilde{W}_{2}(\Omega)\geq f_{\alpha,2}\left(\widetilde{W}_{2}(\Omega)+\alpha\widetilde{W}_{1}(\Omega)\right).

Equality holds only for balls.

Remark 1.3
.

Let us define

(1.11)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\alpha,2}=\Gamma_{1}\cap\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,E_{2}+\alpha E_{1}>0\}.

Then it is clear that
\Gamma_{2}\subset\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\alpha,2}\subset\Gamma_{1}
for
\alpha>0
. Moreover,
\mathcal{M}
is
(\alpha,2)
-convex if and only if its principal curvatures lie in the set
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\alpha,2}
. To see this, note that if
\hat{\kappa}\in\Gamma_{\alpha,2}
, then

(1.12)

\displaystyle E_{1}(\hat{\kappa})

\displaystyle=E_{1}(\kappa)+\alpha>0,

\displaystyle E_{2}(\hat{\kappa})-\alpha E_{1}(\hat{\kappa})

\displaystyle=E_{2}(\kappa)+\alpha E_{1}(\kappa)>0.

As
E_{1}(\kappa)^{2}\geq E_{2}(\kappa)
, we have

(1.13)

\displaystyle E_{1}(\kappa)(E_{1}(\kappa)+\alpha)\geq E_{2}(\kappa)+\alpha E_{1}(\kappa)>0,

and hence
E_{1}(\kappa)>0
. This shows that
\kappa\in\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\alpha,2}
. Conversely, if
\kappa\in\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\alpha,2}
, it is clear that
\hat{\kappa}\in\Gamma_{\alpha,2}
. Therefore, the inequality (
1.8
) holds for hypersurfaces lying in a larger class than the class of 2-convex hypersurfaces.

We conjecture that the inequality

(1.14)

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{k+1}(\Omega)+\alpha\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)\geq h_{\alpha,k+1}\circ h_{\alpha,k}^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)+\alpha\widetilde{W}_{k-1}(\Omega)\right)

holds for all star-shaped hypersurfaces whose principal curvatures lie in the convex set

(1.15)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\alpha,k}:=\Gamma_{k-1}\cap\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,E_{k}+\alpha E_{k-1}>0\},

and equality holds only for balls. Here,

(1.16)

\displaystyle h_{\alpha,k}(r):=\widetilde{W}_{k}(B_{r})+\alpha\widetilde{W}_{k-1}(B_{r})=\frac{\omega_{n}}{n+1}\left(\frac{r^{n+1-k}}{n+1-k}+\alpha\frac{r^{n+2-k}}{n+2-k}\right).

For
k
-convex hypersurfaces, the following argument verifies (
1.14
): Suppose
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain with star-shaped and
k
-convex boundary (with
2\leq k\leq n-1
). Define

(1.17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{k}(\alpha):=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{k+1}(\Omega)+\alpha\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)-h_{\alpha,k+1}\circ h_{\alpha,k}^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)+\alpha\widetilde{W}_{k-1}(\Omega)\right).

Then inequality (
1.14
) is equivalent to
\mathcal{I}(\alpha)\geq 0
. First, we have

(1.18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{k}(0)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{W}_{k+1}(\Omega)-h_{0,k+1}\circ h_{0,k}^{-1}(\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega))

\displaystyle=\widetilde{W}_{k+1}(\Omega)-\widetilde{W}_{k+1}(B)\left(\frac{\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)}{\widetilde{W}_{k}(B)}\right)^{\frac{n-k}{n+1-k}}.

Note that
W_{i}(B)=\frac{\omega_{n}}{n+1}
for
i=0,1,\ldots,n+1
, where
B
is the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Thus, the inequality
\mathcal{I}_{k}(0)\geq 0
is equivalent to

(1.19)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{W_{k+1}(\Omega)}{W_{k+1}(B)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1-(k+1)}}\geq\left(\frac{W_{k}(\Omega)}{W_{k}(B)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1-k}},

which is known to hold for star-shaped, weakly
k
-convex hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by the work of Guan and Li
[
GL09
]
.

Next, we show that
\mathcal{I}_{k}^{\prime}(\alpha)\geq 0
for all
\alpha\geq 0
:

(1.20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{k}^{\prime}(\alpha)=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)-\left.\frac{\frac{d}{d\alpha}h_{\alpha,k+1}}{\frac{d}{d\alpha}h_{\alpha,k}}\right|_{r=h_{\alpha,k}^{-1}(\widetilde{W}_{k}+\alpha\widetilde{W}_{k-1})}\widetilde{W}_{k-1}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)-\frac{n+2-k}{(n+1-k)h_{\alpha,k}^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)+\alpha\widetilde{W}_{k-1}(\Omega)\right)}\widetilde{W}_{k-1}(\Omega).

Hence,
\mathcal{I}_{k}^{\prime}(\alpha)\geq 0
for
\alpha\geq 0
is equivalent to the inequality

(1.21)

\displaystyle h_{\alpha,k}^{-1}\left(\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)+\alpha\widetilde{W}_{k-1}(\Omega)\right)\geq\frac{n+2-k}{n+1-k}\frac{\widetilde{W}_{k-1}(\Omega)}{\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)}.

By the strict monotonicity of
h_{\alpha,k}
in
r
, we obtain

(1.22)

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)+\alpha\widetilde{W}_{k-1}(\Omega)\geq

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle h_{\alpha,k}\left(\frac{n+2-k}{n+1-k}\frac{\widetilde{W}_{k-1}(\Omega)}{\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{n}}{n+1}\frac{W_{k-1}(\Omega)^{n+1-k}}{W_{k}(\Omega)^{n+2-k}}\left(\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)+\alpha\widetilde{W}_{k-1}(\Omega)\right).

Since
\widetilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)+\alpha\widetilde{W}_{k-1}(\Omega)>0
, the above inequality is equivalent to

(1.23)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{W_{k}(\Omega)}{W_{k}(B)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1-k}}\geq\left(\frac{W_{k-1}(\Omega)}{W_{k-1}(B)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1-(k-1)}},

which holds for star-shaped and weakly
(k-1)
-convex hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Thus, we conclude that
\mathcal{I}_{k}(0)\geq 0
and
\mathcal{I}_{k}^{\prime}(\alpha)\geq 0
for all
\alpha\geq 0
. In particular, this implies that
\mathcal{I}_{k}(\alpha)\geq 0
for all
\alpha\geq 0
in the class of star-shaped, weakly
k
-convex hypersurfaces.

1.2.
A Poincaré type inequality

From the standard Reilly’s formula (see, for example,
[
KM18
]
) it follows that
if
(\mathcal{M},g,\nabla)
(with the induced structure from
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
) is a mean-convex hypersurface, then

(1.24)

\displaystyle\int\frac{(\Delta f)^{2}}{\mathcal{H}}d\mu\geq\int g(\mathcal{W}(\nabla f),\nabla f)d\mu\quad\forall f\in C^{2}(\mathcal{M}),

where
\mathcal{H}
denotes the mean curvature of
\mathcal{M}=\partial\Omega
.
Now note that the Heintze-Karcher inequality is a special case of this inequality, which follows by taking
f=\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}
. In fact, substituting
f
into (
1.24
) we obtain

(1.25)

\displaystyle\int h(x^{\top},x^{\top})\leq

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\int\frac{(n-\mathcal{H}\langle x,\nu\rangle)^{2}}{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\int\frac{n^{2}}{\mathcal{H}}-2n\langle x,\nu\rangle+\mathcal{H}\langle x,\nu\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\int\frac{n^{2}}{\mathcal{H}}+\mathcal{H}\langle x,\nu\rangle^{2}-2n(n+1)\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega),

where
x^{\top}
denotes the tangential component of
x
.
On the other hand, we have

(1.26)

\displaystyle\langle x,\nu\rangle\Delta|x|^{2}=2n\langle x,\nu\rangle-2\mathcal{H}\langle x,\nu\rangle^{2}.

By integrating by parts, we find

(1.27)

\displaystyle\int h(x^{\top},x^{\top})=\int\mathcal{H}\langle x,\nu\rangle^{2}-n(n+1)\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega).

Hence, the inequality (
1.25
) is equivalent to the Heintze-Karcher inequality:

(1.28)

\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}d\mu\geq\frac{n+1}{n}\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega).

In
section 6
, by establishing an integral identity and employing the concavity of
\sigma_{k}^{1/k}
in
\Gamma_{k}
we prove the
\sigma_{k}
counterpart of the inequality (
1.24
):

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
1<k\leq n
and
\mathcal{M}
be a
k
-convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then for all
f\in C^{2}(\mathcal{M})
we have

(1.29)

\displaystyle\int\frac{(\operatorname{tr}_{\dot{\sigma}_{k}}(\operatorname{Hess}f))^{2}}{\sigma_{k}}d\mu\geq k\int\langle\mathcal{W}(\nabla f),\nabla f\rangle_{\dot{\sigma}_{k}}d\mu.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation and basic definitions

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle,D)
be the
(n+1)
-dimensional Euclidean space equipped with its standard inner product and flat connection. The Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
can be expressed as a warped product manifold
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
with the metric

(2.1)

\displaystyle\bar{g}=dr^{2}+r^{2}\delta,

where
r
is the distance to a fixed point
o\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
\delta
is the standard metric of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with unit outward normal
\nu
. Write
g
for the (standard) induced metric on
\mathcal{M}
. The second fundamental form
A=(h_{ij})
of
\mathcal{M}
is defined by

(2.2)

\displaystyle h(X,Y)=\langle D_{X}\nu,Y\rangle

for any tangent vectors
X,Y
on
\mathcal{M}
.

Let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be a local orthonormal frame field on
\mathcal{M}
. Then we write
g_{ij}=g(e_{i},e_{j})
and
h_{ij}=h(e_{i},e_{j})
. The Weingarten map is defined via
\mathcal{W}=(h_{i}^{j})
, where
h_{i}^{j}=g^{kj}h_{ik}
and
(g^{ij})
is the inverse matrix of
(g_{ij})
. Moreover, the principal curvatures
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{n})
of
\mathcal{M}
are the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{W}
. It is convenient to introduce the function
\Phi=r^{2}/2
. Then, the position vector field of
\mathcal{M}
is given by
x=D\Phi|_{\mathcal{M}}=r\partial_{r}|_{\mathcal{M}}
. Finally, the support function of
\mathcal{M}
is defined by

(2.3)

\displaystyle u=\langle x,\nu\rangle.

The following formulas hold for smooth hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
; see, e.g.
[
GL15
, Lem. 2.2 & 2.6]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

(1)

The function

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\Phi

\displaystyle=\langle x,e_{i}\rangle,

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\Phi

\displaystyle=g_{ij}-uh_{ij}.

(2)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}u

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}u

where

.

Let

denote a symmetric, smooth function of the principal curvatures of a hypersurface:

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}(\kappa)=\mathcal{F}(\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{n})=\mathcal{F}(h_{i}^{j})=\mathcal{F}(g,h).

We write

\mathcal{F}_{i}^{j}:=\frac{\partial F}{\partial h_{j}^{i}},\quad\dot{\mathcal{F}}:=[\mathcal{F}_{i}^{j}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n},\quad\mathcal{F}^{ij}:=\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial h_{ij}},\quad\mathcal{F}^{ij,kl}:=\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{F}}{\partial h_{ij}\partial h_{kl}}.

For more details on curvature functions, refer to

2.2.

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}(\lambda)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{m}},\quad\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

\displaystyle E_{m}(\lambda)=\binom{n}{m}^{-1}\sigma_{m}(\lambda),\quad m=1,\ldots,n,

\displaystyle E_{m}(A)=E_{m}(\lambda(A))=\frac{(n-m)!}{n!}\delta_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{m}}A_{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{m}j_{m}},\quad m=1,\ldots,n.

Lemma 2.4

(Newton-Maclaurin inequalities II)

\displaystyle E_{k}(\hat{\lambda})=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\alpha^{i}E_{k-i}(\lambda).

\displaystyle\binom{k}{i}=\binom{k-1}{i}+\binom{k-1}{i-1},\quad 0\leq i\leq k,

where we used the convention that

By the Newton inequality (

\hat{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(2.16)

\displaystyle E_{k}^{2}(\hat{\lambda})\geq E_{k-1}(\hat{\lambda})E_{k+1}(\hat{\lambda}),\quad k=1,2,\ldots,n-1.

We have

(2.17)

\displaystyle\operatorname{LHS}\text{of \eqref{Newton-type-k}}=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\alpha^{i}E_{k-i}(\lambda)\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(\binom{k-1}{i}+\binom{k-1}{i-1}\right)\alpha^{i}E_{k-i}(\lambda)\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{i}\alpha^{i}E_{k-i}(\lambda)+\alpha\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{i}\alpha^{i}E_{k-1-i}(\lambda)\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}E_{k-i}(\lambda)\bigg)^{2}+\alpha^{2}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}E_{k-1-i}(\lambda)\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle+2\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}E_{k-i}(\lambda)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}E_{k-1-i}(\lambda)

and

(2.18)

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k+1}\binom{k+1}{i}\alpha^{i}E_{k+1-i}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\alpha^{i}E_{k+1-i}(\lambda)+\alpha\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\alpha^{i}E_{k-i}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{i}\alpha^{i}E_{k+1-i}(\lambda)+\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{k-1}{i-1}\alpha^{i-1}E_{k+1-i}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle+\alpha\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{i}\alpha^{i}E_{k-i}(\lambda)+\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{k-1}{i-1}\alpha^{i-1}E_{k-i}(\lambda)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}E_{k+1-i}(\lambda)+2\alpha\beta_{i}E_{k-i}(\lambda)+\alpha^{2}\beta_{i}E_{k-1-i}(\lambda).

Hence, we obtain

(2.19)

\displaystyle\operatorname{RHS}\text{of \eqref{Newton-type-k}}=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}E_{k-1-i}(\lambda)\sum_{i=0}^{k+1}\binom{k+1}{i}\alpha^{i}E_{k+1-i}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}E_{k+1-i}(\lambda)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}E_{k-1-i}(\lambda)+\alpha^{2}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}E_{k-1-i}(\lambda)\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle+2\alpha\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}E_{k-i}(\lambda)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}E_{k-1-i}(\lambda).

By subtracting the common terms, we obtain the inequality (
2.13
).

If equality holds in
(
2.13
)
, then equality also holds in (
2.16
). Then either

(2.20)

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{1}=\cdots=\hat{\lambda}_{n},

or both sides of (
2.16
) vanish. The former case implies that
\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{n}
, while the latter case implies that
E_{k}(\hat{\lambda})=0
(which does not happen due to our assumption).
∎

We need the following Minkowski integral identity; see, for example,
[
GL15
, Prop. 2.5]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then

(2.21)

\displaystyle\int E_{m}(\kappa)d\mu=\int uE_{m+1}(\kappa)d\mu,\quad m=0,1,\ldots,n-1,

where
E_{m}(\kappa)
is the normalized
m
th mean curvature of
\mathcal{M}
.

2.3.
Properties of a curvature function

Recall that

(2.22)

\displaystyle F(\lambda)=\frac{E_{k}(\hat{\lambda})}{E_{k-1}(\hat{\lambda})}-\alpha=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}E_{k-i}(\lambda)}{\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}E_{k-1-i}(\lambda)},\quad k=2,\ldots,n-1.

It is clear that in
\Gamma_{\alpha,k}
,
F>0
,
F^{ii}>0
and
F
is concave.

Lemma 2.6
.

We have

(2.23)

\displaystyle\partial\Gamma_{\alpha,k}=\bar{\Gamma}_{k-1}\cap\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,E_{k}(\lambda)=\alpha E_{k-1}(\lambda)\}.

In particular,
F|_{\partial\Gamma_{\alpha,k}}=0
.

Proof.

Take
\lambda\in\partial\Gamma_{\alpha,k}
. If
E_{k}(\lambda)>\alpha E_{k-1}(\lambda)
, then we would have
E_{k-1}(\lambda)=0
and
E_{k}(\lambda)>0
. Therefore,
\lambda\in\bar{\Gamma}_{k}
. Now, due to the Maclaurin inequality (
2.12
),
E_{k-1}(\lambda)\geq E_{k}^{\frac{k-1}{k}}(\lambda)>0
, which violates
E_{k-1}(\lambda)=0
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

We have

(2.24)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}F^{ii}\lambda_{i}\leq F,\quad\sum_{i}F^{ii}\lambda_{i}^{2}\geq F^{2},\quad\sum_{i}F^{ii}\geq 1.

Proof.

Recall that
F=\frac{E_{k}(\hat{\lambda})}{E_{k-1}(\hat{\lambda})}-\alpha
. We calculate

(2.25)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}F^{ii}=\sum_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\left(\frac{E_{k}(\hat{\lambda})}{E_{k-1}(\hat{\lambda})}\right)=\sum_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{\lambda}_{i}}\left(\frac{E_{k}(\hat{\lambda})}{E_{k-1}(\hat{\lambda})}\right)\geq 1,

and

(2.26)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}F^{ii}\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}F^{ii}\hat{\lambda}_{i}-\alpha\sum_{i}F^{ii}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}\hat{\lambda}_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{\lambda}_{i}}\left(\frac{E_{k}(\hat{\lambda})}{E_{k-1}(\hat{\lambda})}\right)-\alpha=F,

where we used the one-homogeneity of
E_{k}/E_{k-1}
.

Due to
2.2
, we have

(2.27)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}F^{ii}\hat{\lambda}^{2}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{\lambda}_{i}}\left(\frac{E_{k}(\hat{\lambda})}{E_{k-1}(\hat{\lambda})}\right)\hat{\lambda}^{2}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left(\frac{E_{k}^{ii}(\hat{\lambda})}{E_{k-1}(\hat{\lambda})}-\frac{E_{k}(\hat{\lambda})E_{k-1}^{ii}(\hat{\lambda})}{E_{k-1}^{2}(\hat{\lambda})}\right)\ \hat{\lambda}_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=(n+1-k)\frac{E_{k}^{2}(\hat{\lambda})}{E_{k-1}^{2}(\hat{\lambda})}-(n-k)\frac{E_{k+1}(\hat{\lambda})}{E_{k-1}(\hat{\lambda})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{E_{k}^{2}(\hat{\lambda})}{E_{k-1}^{2}(\hat{\lambda})}=(F+\alpha)^{2},

where the last inequality follows from the Newton inequality

(2.28)

\displaystyle E_{k-1}(\hat{\lambda})E_{k+1}(\hat{\lambda})\leq E_{k}(\hat{\lambda})^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad E_{k-1}(\hat{\lambda})>0.

Hence,

(2.29)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}F^{ii}\lambda_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}F^{ii}(\hat{\lambda}_{i}^{2}-2\alpha\hat{\lambda}_{i}+\alpha^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}F^{ii}\hat{\lambda}^{2}_{i}-2\alpha\sum_{i}F^{ii}\hat{\lambda}_{i}+\alpha^{2}\sum_{i}F^{ii}

\displaystyle\geq(F+\alpha)^{2}-2\alpha(F+\alpha)+\alpha^{2}=F^{2}.

∎

3.
Regularity estimates

We start by deriving a few standard evolution equations for the flow

(3.1)

\displaystyle\dot{x}=\left(\frac{1}{F}-u\right)\nu,\quad F=\frac{E_{k}(\hat{\kappa})}{E_{k-1}(\hat{\kappa})}-\alpha,\quad k=2,\ldots,n-1.

Lemma 3.1
.

Along the flow

\dot{x}=\mathcal{F}\nu,

we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\nu

\displaystyle=-\nabla\mathcal{F},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h_{i}^{j}

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{i}\nabla^{j}\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{F}(h^{2})_{i}^{j}.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have the following evolution equations along the flow (
3.1

(3.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h_{i}^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{F^{kl}}{F^{2}}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}h_{i}^{j}+\left(\frac{F^{kl}}{F^{2}}(h^{2})_{kl}+1\right)h_{i}^{j}+\frac{1}{F^{2}}F^{kl,pq}\nabla_{i}h_{pq}\nabla^{j}h_{kl}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{F^{3}}\nabla_{i}F\nabla^{j}F+\langle\nabla h_{i}^{j},x\rangle-\left(\frac{F^{kl}}{F^{2}}h_{kl}+\frac{1}{F}\right)(h^{2})_{i}^{j}.

(3.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F

\displaystyle=\frac{F^{ij}}{F^{2}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}F-\frac{2F^{ij}}{F^{3}}\nabla_{i}F\nabla_{j}F+\langle\nabla F,x\rangle

\displaystyle+F^{ij}h_{ij}-\frac{1}{F}F^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}.

(3.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=\frac{F^{kl}}{F^{2}}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}u+\langle x,\nabla u\rangle+\frac{1}{F}\left(1-\frac{F^{kl}h_{kl}}{F}\right)

\displaystyle+u\left(\frac{F^{kl}(h^{2})_{kl}}{F^{2}}-1\right).

Proof.

(i) Using (
3.3
), we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h_{i}^{j}

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{i}\nabla^{j}\left(\frac{1}{F}-u\right)-\left(\frac{1}{F}-u\right)(h^{2})_{i}^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{F^{2}}\nabla_{i}\nabla^{j}F-\frac{2}{F^{3}}\nabla_{i}F\nabla^{j}F+\nabla_{i}\nabla^{j}u-\frac{1}{F}(h^{2})_{i}^{j}+u(h^{2})_{i}^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{F^{kl}}{F^{2}}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}h_{i}^{j}+\left(\frac{F^{kl}}{F^{2}}(h^{2})_{kl}+1\right)h_{i}^{j}+\frac{1}{F^{2}}F^{kl,pq}\nabla_{i}h_{pq}\nabla^{j}h_{kl}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{F^{3}}\nabla_{i}F\nabla^{j}F+\langle\nabla h_{i}^{j},x\rangle-\left(\frac{F^{kl}}{F^{2}}h_{kl}+\frac{1}{F}\right)(h^{2})_{i}^{j},

where we used

(3.8)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}F

\displaystyle=F^{kl}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}h_{kl}+F^{kl,pq}\nabla_{i}h_{pq}\nabla_{j}h_{kl}

\displaystyle=F^{kl}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}h_{ij}+F^{kl}(h^{2})_{kl}h_{ij}-F^{kl}h_{kl}(h^{2})_{ij}+F^{kl,pq}\nabla_{i}h_{pq}\nabla_{j}h_{kl}

and

\nabla_{i}\nabla^{j}u=\langle\nabla h_{i}^{j},x\rangle+h_{i}^{j}-u(h^{2})_{i}^{j}
due to (
2.5
).

(ii) To deduce (
3.5
), by using (
3.3
) and (
2.5
) we compute

(3.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F

\displaystyle=F^{i}_{j}\partial_{t}h_{i}^{j}

\displaystyle=-F^{i}_{j}\nabla_{i}\nabla^{j}\left(\frac{1}{F}-u\right)-\left(\frac{1}{F}-u\right)F^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}

\displaystyle=F^{i}_{j}\left(\frac{1}{F^{2}}\nabla_{i}\nabla^{j}F-\frac{2}{F^{3}}\nabla_{i}F\nabla^{j}F\right)+F^{i}_{j}(\langle\nabla h_{i}^{j},x\rangle+h_{i}^{j}-u(h^{2})_{i}^{j})

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{F}-u\right)F^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{F^{ij}}{F^{2}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}F-\frac{2F^{ij}}{F^{3}}\nabla_{i}F\nabla_{j}F+\langle\nabla F,x\rangle+F^{ij}h_{ij}-\frac{F^{ij}}{F}(h^{2})_{ij}.

(iii) Owing to
\partial_{t}\nu=-\nabla(\frac{1}{F}-u)
(see (
3.2

(3.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{F}-u-\langle x,\nabla(\frac{1}{F}-u)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{F}-u+\frac{1}{F^{2}}\langle x,\nabla F\rangle+\langle x,\nabla u\rangle.

On the other hand, by (
2.5

(3.11)

\displaystyle\frac{F^{ij}}{F^{2}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}u

\displaystyle=\frac{F^{ij}}{F^{2}}(\langle\nabla h_{ij},x\rangle+h_{ij}-u(h^{2})_{ij})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{F^{2}}(\langle\nabla F,x\rangle+F^{ij}h_{ij}-uF^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}).

Now (
3.6
) follows immediately from (
3.10
) and (
3.11
).
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Along the flow (
3.1
), we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{F^{kl}}{F^{2}}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u}+\langle\nabla\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u},x\rangle+\frac{2}{uF^{2}}F^{kl}\nabla_{k}\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u}\nabla_{l}u

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{uF^{2}}F^{kl,pq}\nabla_{i}h_{pq}\nabla^{j}h_{kl}-\frac{2}{uF^{3}}\nabla_{i}F\nabla^{j}F

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{F^{kl}h_{kl}}{F}+1\right)\frac{(h^{2})_{i}^{j}}{uF}-\left(\frac{1}{uF}\left(1-\frac{F^{kl}h_{kl}}{F}\right)-2\right)\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u}.

Proof.

By (
3.4
) and (
3.6
), we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{u}\partial_{t}h_{i}^{j}-\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u^{2}}\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=\frac{F^{kl}}{uF^{2}}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}h_{i}^{j}+\left(\frac{F^{kl}(h^{2})_{kl}}{F^{2}}+1\right)\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u}+\frac{1}{uF^{2}}F^{kl,pq}\nabla_{i}h_{pq}\nabla^{j}h_{kl}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{uF^{3}}\nabla_{i}F\nabla^{j}F+\langle\nabla\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u},x\rangle-\left(\frac{F^{kl}h_{kl}}{F}+1\right)\frac{(h^{2})_{i}^{j}}{uF}

\displaystyle-\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u^{2}F^{2}}F^{kl}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}u-\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u^{2}F}\left(1-\frac{F^{kl}h_{kl}}{F}\right)-\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u}\left(\frac{F^{kl}(h^{2})_{kl}}{F^{2}}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{F^{kl}}{F^{2}}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u}+\langle\nabla\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u},x\rangle+\frac{2}{F^{2}}F^{kl}\nabla_{k}\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u}\nabla_{l}\log u

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{uF^{2}}F^{kl,pq}\nabla_{i}h_{pq}\nabla^{j}h_{kl}-\frac{2}{uF^{3}}\nabla_{i}F\nabla^{j}F

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{F^{kl}h_{kl}}{F}+1\right)\frac{(h^{2})_{i}^{j}}{uF}-\left(\frac{1}{uF}\left(1-\frac{F^{kl}h_{kl}}{F}\right)-2\right)\frac{h_{i}^{j}}{u}.

∎

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with the induced metric
g
. We say
\mathcal{M}
is star-shaped if the support function is positive everywhere on
\mathcal{M}
. In this case,
\mathcal{M}
can be expressed as a radial graph in spherical coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
:

(3.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\{(r(z),z)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}\penalty\ |\penalty\ z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\},

where
r=r(z)
is a smooth and positive function over
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and
z=(z^{1},\ldots,z^{n})
are local coordinates of
(\mathbb{S}^{n},\delta,\bar{\nabla})
.

We write
\partial_{i}=\partial_{z^{i}}
and
r_{i}=\bar{\nabla}_{i}r
. The tangent space of
\mathcal{M}
is spanned by

(3.15)

\displaystyle\{e_{i}:=\partial_{i}+r_{i}\partial_{r},\penalty\ i=1,\ldots,n\},

and we have

(3.16)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{v},\quad v=\sqrt{1+r^{-2}|\bar{\nabla}r|^{2}},

\displaystyle g_{ij}

\displaystyle=r^{2}\delta_{ij}+\bar{\nabla}_{i}r\bar{\nabla}_{j}r,\quad g^{ij}=r^{-2}\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{\bar{\nabla}^{i}r\bar{\nabla}^{j}r}{r^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}r|^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle h_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{rv}(-r\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}r+2\bar{\nabla}_{i}r\bar{\nabla}_{j}r+r^{2}\delta_{ij}),

\displaystyle h_{i}^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{3}v}\left(\delta^{jk}-\frac{\bar{\nabla}^{j}r\bar{\nabla}^{k}r}{r^{2}v^{2}}\right)\left(-r\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{i}r+2\bar{\nabla}_{i}r\bar{\nabla}_{k}r+r^{2}\delta_{ki}\right).

Let us introduce a new variable
\gamma=\log r
. Then

(3.17)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\gamma}}{v},\quad v=\sqrt{1+|\bar{\nabla}\gamma|^{2}},

\displaystyle g_{ij}

\displaystyle=e^{2\gamma}(\delta_{ij}+\bar{\nabla}_{i}\gamma\bar{\nabla}_{j}\gamma),\quad g^{ij}=e^{-2\gamma}\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{\bar{\nabla}^{i}\gamma\bar{\nabla}^{j}\gamma}{v^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle h_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\gamma}}{v}(-\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\gamma+\bar{\nabla}_{i}\gamma\bar{\nabla}_{j}\gamma+\delta_{ij}),

\displaystyle h_{i}^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{\gamma}v}\left(\delta^{jk}-\frac{\bar{\nabla}^{j}\gamma\bar{\nabla}^{k}\gamma}{v^{2}}\right)\left(-\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\gamma+\bar{\nabla}_{k}\gamma\bar{\nabla}_{i}\gamma+\delta_{ki}\right).

Suppose
\mathcal{M}_{t}
,
t\in[0,T)
, is a family of star-shaped,
(\alpha,k)
-convex hypersurfaces solving (
3.1
). The corresponding
\gamma=\gamma(x(z,t),t)
satisfies the following initial value problem:

(3.18)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\gamma&=\frac{v}{e^{\gamma}F}-1,\quad(z,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,T),\\
\gamma(\cdot,0)&=\gamma_{0}(\cdot),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\gamma_{0}=\log r_{0}
and
r_{0}
is the radial function of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

We have

(3.19)

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\gamma(x(\cdot,0),0)\leq\gamma(x(z,t),t)\leq\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\gamma(x(\cdot,0),0).

Proof.

Assume that
\gamma
attains its maximum at some point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Then, at
z_{0}
, we have
\bar{\nabla}\gamma=0
,
v=1
and
\bar{\nabla}^{2}\gamma\leq 0
. Therefore,

(3.20)

\displaystyle h_{i}^{j}\geq e^{-\gamma}\delta_{i}^{j}.

Since
F
is strictly increasing in
\kappa
, we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle F(\kappa)\geq F(e^{-\gamma}I)=\frac{E_{k}((e^{-\gamma}+\alpha)I)}{E_{k-1}((e^{-\gamma}+\alpha)I)}-\alpha=e^{-\gamma}.

Due to (
3.18
), the upper bound of
\gamma(x(z,t),t)
follows from

(3.22)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\gamma(x(\cdot,t),t)\leq 0,

at differentiable point of
\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\gamma(x(\cdot,t),t)
.
The lower bound of
\gamma(x(z,t),t)
can be deduced similarly.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

We have

u\geq\min_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}u.

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C_{1}
depending only on
\mathcal{M}_{0}
such that

(3.23)

\displaystyle|\bar{\nabla}\gamma|(z,t)\leq C_{1}.

Proof.

Recall the evolution equation (
3.6
):

(3.24)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=\frac{F^{kl}}{F^{2}}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}u+\langle x,\nabla u\rangle+\frac{1}{F}\left(1-\frac{F^{kl}h_{kl}}{F}\right)+u\left(\frac{F^{kl}(h^{2})_{kl}}{F^{2}}-1\right).

Due to
2.7
, we have

(3.25)

\displaystyle 1-\frac{F^{kl}h_{kl}}{F}\geq 0,\quad\frac{F^{kl}(h^{2})_{kl}}{F^{2}}-1\geq 0.

Hence, we obtain

(3.26)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\min_{\mathcal{M}_{t}}u(\cdot,t)\geq 0.

u
is bounded below away from zero and

(3.27)

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\gamma}}{\sqrt{1+|\bar{\nabla}\gamma|^{2}}}=u\geq C.

Now the upper bound on

follows from (
3.19
).
∎

.
∎

Lemma 3.7

Lemma 3.8
.

There exists
C_{4}>0
depending only on
\mathcal{M}_{0}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=2\psi h^{ij},

\displaystyle=-\psi\mathcal{H}d\mu,

(6.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sigma_{k}^{ij}=\partial_{t}(g^{im}(\sigma_{k})_{m}^{j})=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle g^{im}\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma_{k}}{\partial h_{j}^{m}\partial h_{r}^{s}}\partial_{t}h_{r}^{s}+(\partial_{t}g^{im})(\sigma_{k})_{m}^{j}

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\sigma_{k}^{ij,pq}(\psi_{;pq}+\psi(h^{2})_{pq})+2\psi\sigma_{k}^{jm}h_{m}^{i}.

Therefore,

(6.7)

\displaystyle 0=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{t}}\sigma_{k}^{ij}\bar{f}_{;ij}d\mu=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}\sigma_{k}^{ij,pq}f_{;ij}\psi_{;pq}+\psi\sigma_{k}^{ij,pq}(h^{2})_{pq}f_{;ij}d\mu

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle+\int_{\mathcal{M}}2\psi\sigma_{k}^{jm}h_{m}^{i}f_{;ij}-\sigma_{k}^{ij}(\partial_{t}\Gamma_{ij}^{m})\partial_{m}f-\psi\mathcal{H}\sigma_{k}^{ij}f_{;ij}d\mu.

In an orthonormal basis that diagonalizes
h_{ij}
, using
[
GM03
, (4.17)]
, we calculate

(6.8)

\displaystyle\psi\sigma_{k}^{ij,pq}(h^{2})_{pq}f_{;ij}=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\psi\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{p=1,p\neq i}^{n}\frac{\partial\sigma_{k-1}(\kappa|i)}{\partial\kappa_{p}}\kappa_{p}^{2}f_{;ii}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\psi\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\sigma_{1}(\kappa|i)\sigma_{k-1}(\kappa|i)-k\sigma_{k}(\kappa|i))f_{;ii}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\psi(\sigma_{2}^{im}(\sigma_{k})_{m}^{i}-k\sigma_{k+1}^{ij})f_{;ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\psi(\mathcal{H}\sigma_{k}^{ij}-(\sigma_{k})^{jm}h_{m}^{i}-k\sigma_{k+1}^{ij})f_{;ij},

where we used
\sigma_{2}^{ij}=\mathcal{H}g^{ij}-h^{ij}
by the definition of the Newton operator

(6.9)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k+1}^{ij}=\sigma_{k}g^{ij}-\sigma_{k}^{jm}h_{m}^{i},\quad 1\leq k<n,

and
\sigma_{1}^{ij}=g^{ij}
, and we also take
\sigma_{n+1}^{ij}=0
by convention.
We also have

(6.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}-\sigma_{k}^{ij}(\partial_{t}\Gamma_{ij}^{m})\partial_{m}fd\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\sigma_{k}^{ij}(2\partial_{i}\psi h_{j}^{m}-\partial_{l}\psi g^{ml}h_{ij}+\psi h_{ij;}{}^{m})\partial_{m}fd\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{M}}2\sigma_{k}^{ij}h_{j}^{m}\partial_{i}\psi\partial_{m}f-k\sigma_{k}\langle\nabla\psi,\nabla f\rangle+\psi\langle\nabla\sigma_{k},\nabla f\rangle d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{M}}2\sigma_{k}^{ij}h_{j}^{m}\partial_{i}\psi\partial_{m}f-(k+1)\sigma_{k}\langle\nabla\psi,\nabla f\rangle-\sigma_{k}\psi\Delta fd\mu.

Hence,

(6.11)

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}\sigma_{k}^{ij,pq}f_{;ij}\psi_{;pq}+(2\sigma_{k}^{im}h_{m}^{j}-(k+1)\sigma_{k}g^{ij})\partial_{i}\psi\partial_{j}fd\mu

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle+\int_{\mathcal{M}}\psi(-k\sigma_{k+1}^{ij}+\sigma_{k}^{jm}h_{m}^{i}-\sigma_{k}g^{ij})f_{;ij}d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}\sigma_{k}^{ij,pq}f_{;ij}\psi_{;pq}-(k-1)\sigma_{k}^{im}h_{m}^{j}\partial_{i}\psi\partial_{j}fd\mu,

where we used (
6.9
) in the last equality.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
1<k\leq n
and
\mathcal{M}
be a convex hypersurface. Then

(6.15)

\displaystyle\int\frac{|\nabla\sigma_{k}|^{2}}{\sigma_{k}}d\mu\geq\int\sum_{i<j}\frac{\sigma_{k}^{ii}\sigma_{k}^{jj}}{\sigma_{k}}\kappa_{i}\kappa_{j}(\kappa_{i}-\kappa_{j})^{2}d\mu.

Proof.

Note that

(6.16)

\displaystyle\nu_{;i}

\displaystyle=h_{i}^{m}e_{m}

\displaystyle\nu_{;ij}

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{m}h_{ij})e_{m}-(h^{2})_{ij}\nu.

Let
\{v_{\ell}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Applying (
6.1
) to each
f_{\ell}=\langle\nu,v_{\ell}\rangle
for
\ell=1,\ldots,n+1
and then summing over
\ell
we obtain

(6.17)

\displaystyle\int\frac{|\nabla\sigma_{k}|^{2}+(\sum\sigma_{k}^{ii}\kappa_{i}^{2})^{2}}{\sigma_{k}}d\mu\geq k\int\sum\sigma_{k}^{ii}\kappa_{i}^{3}d\mu.

Now recall that for any constants
a_{i},b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
, there holds

(6.18)

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(a_{i}b_{j}-a_{j}b_{i})^{2}=\sum_{i}a_{i}^{2}\sum_{j}b_{j}^{2}-\big(\sum_{i}a_{i}b_{i}\big)^{2}.

Let
a_{i}=\sqrt{\sigma_{k}^{ii}}\kappa_{i}^{\frac{3}{2}}
and
b_{j}=\sqrt{\sigma_{k}^{jj}}\kappa_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then we have

(6.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\sigma_{k}^{ii}\kappa_{i}^{3}\sum_{i}\sigma_{k}^{ii}\kappa_{i}-\big(\sum_{i}\sigma_{k}^{ii}\kappa_{i}^{2}\big)^{2}=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\sigma_{k}^{ii}\sigma_{k}^{jj}(\kappa_{i}^{\frac{3}{2}}\kappa_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\kappa_{j}^{\frac{3}{2}}\kappa_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\sigma_{k}^{ii}\sigma_{k}^{jj}\kappa_{i}\kappa_{j}(\kappa_{i}-\kappa_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{k}^{ii}\sigma_{k}^{jj}\kappa_{i}\kappa_{j}(\kappa_{i}-\kappa_{j})^{2}.

Hence, for
1<k\leq n
:

(6.20)

\displaystyle\int\frac{|\nabla\sigma_{k}|^{2}}{\sigma_{k}}d\mu\geq\int\sum_{i<j}\frac{\sigma_{k}^{ii}\sigma_{k}^{jj}}{\sigma_{k}}\kappa_{i}\kappa_{j}(\kappa_{i}-\kappa_{j})^{2}d\mu.

∎

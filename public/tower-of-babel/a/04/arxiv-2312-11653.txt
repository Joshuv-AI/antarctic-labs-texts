Self-dual toric varieties Thanks: Corresponding author: Apostolos Thoma
Apostolos Thoma, Marius Vladoiu

Abstract
. We describe explicitly all multisets of weights whose defining projective toric varieties are self-dual. In addition, we describe a remarkable and unexpected combinatorial behaviour of the defining ideals of these varieties. The toric ideal of a self-dual projective variety is weakly robust, that means the Graver basis is the union of all minimal binomial generating sets. When, in addition, the self-dual projective variety has a non-pyramidal configuration, then the toric ideal is strongly robust, namely the Graver basis is a minimal generating set, therefore there is only one minimal binomial generating set which is also a reduced Gröbner basis with respect to every monomial order and thus, equals the universal Gröbner basis.

1.
Introduction

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and
A
be an
m\times n
integer matrix such
that
(1,1,\cdots,1,1)
belongs in the row span of the matrix
A
. For a vector

{\bf u}\in\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
, we denote
by
{\bf u}^{+},{\bf u}^{-}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
, the unique
vectors of disjoint support such that
{\bf u}={\bf u}^{+}-{\bf u}^{-}
. The support of
{\bf u}
is the set
\{i|u_{i}\neq 0\}
. The toric ideal of
A
is the
homogeneous ideal
I_{A}\subset K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
generated by the binomials
{x}^{{{\mathbb{u}}}^{+}}-{x}^{{{\mathbb{u}}}^{-}}

where
{{\mathbb{u}}}\in\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A),
see
[
26
, Chapter 4]
. Let
T=(K^{*})^{m}
be the algebraic torus of rank
m
over
K
and
\chi(T)
be the lattice of characters of
T
; characters are of the form
\lambda({\bf t})={\bf t}^{\bf b}=t_{1}^{b_{1}}t_{2}^{b_{2}}\cdots t_{m}^{b_{m}}
, where
{\bf b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
, thus we can identify
\chi(T)
with
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
.
Then
T
acts on
K^{n}
by
{\bf t}\cdot(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})=({\bf t}^{{\bf a}_{1}}x_{1},{\bf t}^{{\bf a}_{2}}x_{2},\ldots,{\bf t}^{{\bf a}_{n}}x_{n})
, where the set of weights
{\bf a}_{1},{\bf a}_{2},\ldots,{\bf a}_{n}
are the columns of the matrix
A
. The projective toric variety
X_{A}=V(I_{A})\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{K}^{n-1}
is the Zariski closure of the orbit of the point
(1,1,\ldots,1,1)
.
By
X_{A}^{*}
we denote the dual variety of
X_{A}
, which is the closure in the dual projective space of the hyperplanes intersecting the regular points of
X_{A}
non transversally. The variety
X_{A}
is called self-dual if it is isomorphic to its dual
X_{A}^{*}
as embedded projective varieties.
In
[
5
]
, Bourel, Dickenstein and Rittatore prove that the variety
X_{A}
is a self-dual projective toric variety
if and only if
\dim(X_{A})=\dim(X_{A}^{*})
and the smallest linear subspaces containing
X_{A}
and
X_{A}^{*}
have the same dimension. The variety

X_{A}
is called defective if the codimension of its dual variety is not one. Thus self-dual toric varieties which are not hypersurfaces are defective. The classification of defective projective toric varieties is a difficult problem with a lot of recent interest, see
[
13
,
12
,
16
,
17
,
6
]
.

Also, in
[
5
]
, Bourel et al. produce the full list of self-dual smooth projective toric varieties and several examples of non-smooth self dual toric vatieties. They also provide combinatorial characterizations of self-duality for toric varieties.
In this article, we combine the combinatorial characterizations of
[
5
]
and the full bouquet theory, including the generalized Lawrence matrices
[
24
]
, which permit us to describe explicitly all multisets of weights whose defining projective toric varieties are self-dual and we prove their interesting algebro-combinatorial
properties.
Toric ideals of self-dual projective toric varieties associated to non-pyramidal configurations posess
the same combinatorial properties
[
26
, Proposition 7.1]
as the toric ideals of the second Lawrence liftings: they are
strongly robust
, see
[
28
]
. A toric ideal is called robust if it is minimally generated by its universal Gröbner basis, where the universal Gröbner basis is the union of all reduced Gröbner bases, see
[
3
]
. Robust related properties of ideals are studied in several recent articles; see
[
1
,
2
,
10
,
11
,
27
]
for robust ideals,
[
3
,
4
]
for robust toric ideals,

[
19
,
21
,
22
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
28
]
for strongly robust toric ideals and
[
18
,
29
]
for generalized robust toric ideals.

A
strongly robust
toric ideal is a toric ideal
I_{A}
for which the Graver basis
\operatorname{Gr}(I_{A})

is a minimal system of generators, see
[
28
]
. Since
(1,1,\cdots,1,1)
belongs to the row span of the matrix
A
, then
\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)\cap{\mathbb{N}}^{n}=\{{\mathbf{0}}\}
, which implies that any minimal binomial generating set is contained in the Graver basis, see
[
9
, Theorem 2.3]
. Then, for strongly robust ideals, the Graver basis is the unique minimal system of generators and, thus, any reduced Gröbner basis as well as the universal Gröbner basis are identical with the Graver basis, since all of them contain a minimal system of generators and they are subsets of the Graver basis (see
[
26
, Chapter 4]
). We conclude that for a strongly robust toric ideal
I_{A}
the
following sets are identical: the set of indispensable elements, any minimal system of binomial generators, any reduced Gröbner basis, the universal Gröbner basis and the Graver basis. Therefore, strongly robust toric ideals are robust. The set of indispensable elements is the intersection of all minimal binomial sets of generators, while their union is the universal Markov basis, up to the identification of opposite binomials.
Weakly robust
are the toric ideals for which the universal Markov basis and the Graver basis of
I_{A}
coincide. The ideals of self-dual toric varieties, pyramidal or non-pyramidal, are weakly robust.

In Section 2, we present the basic theory of bouquets. In Section 3, we make an introduction to the generalized Lawrence matrices and we use them to
describe explicitly all multisets of weights whose defining projective toric varieties are self-dual. Finally, in Section 4, we present the necessary notions from the different toric bases and
we prove that the toric ideal
of a self-dual projective variety is weakly robust. When, in addition, the self-dual projective variety is defined by a non-pyramidal configuration, then the toric ideal is strongly robust.

2.
Preliminaries on bouquets

Let
\{{\bf a}_{1},\dots,{\bf a}_{n}\}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
. Throughout the article we will use the same symbol,
A
, for the set
\{{\bf a}_{1},\dots,{\bf a}_{n}\}
and for the integer matrix
[{\bf a}_{1},\dots,{\bf a}_{n}]\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\times n}
with columns
{\bf a}_{1},\dots,{\bf a}_{n}
.

To any integer matrix
A\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\times n}

one can associate an oriented matroid structure consisting of a graph
G_{A}
and another matrix
A_{B}
, called the “bouquet graph” and the “bouquet matrix”, respectively. The name bouquet was inspired by the article
[
23
]
and the theory of bouquets was developed in a series of articles
[
24
,
25
,
21
]
. But a lot of the ideas behind the bouquet structure were used extensively before the definition to study the self-dual projective toric varieties in
[
5
]
.

The
bouquet graph

G_{A}
of
I_{A}
is the graph on the set of vertices
\{{\bf a}_{1},\dots,{\bf a}_{n}\}
and edges
\{{\mathbb{a}}_{i},{\mathbb{a}}_{j}\}
if there exists a vector
{\mathbb{v}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
such that the dot products
{\mathbb{v}}\cdot{\mathbb{a}}_{k}=0
for all
k\neq i,j
and
{\mathbb{v}}\cdot{\mathbb{a}}_{i}\neq 0
,
{\mathbb{v}}\cdot{\mathbb{a}}_{j}\neq 0
or equivalently that there is a covector with support
i,j
in the vector matroid defined by the columns of
A
, see
[
24
, Definition 1.1]
. The
bouquets
are the connected components of the graph
G_{A}
.

The bouquet graph can also be defined in terms of the Gale transform of the matrix
A
. To any matrix
A
one can associate its Gale transform, which is the
n\times(n-r)
matrix whose columns span the lattice
\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
, where
r
is the rank of
A
. We will denote the set of ordered row vectors of the Gale transform of
A
by
\{G({\mathbb{a}}_{1}),\dots,G({\mathbb{a}}_{n})\}
and we will call
G({\mathbb{a}}_{i})
the Gale transform of
{\mathbb{a}}_{i}
. There is an edge between the vertices
{\mathbb{a}}_{i}
and
{\mathbb{a}}_{j}
if and only if
{\mathbb{a}}_{i}
,
{\mathbb{a}}_{j}
are coparallel, that means
G({\mathbb{a}}_{i}),G({\mathbb{a}}_{j})
are parallel i.e. there exists a nonzero
\lambda\in{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
G({\mathbb{a}}_{i})=\lambda G({\mathbb{a}}_{j})
.

The vector
{\bf a}_{i}
is called
free
if
G({\mathbb{a}}_{i})
is equal to the zero vector, which means that
i
is not contained in the support of any element in
\operatorname{Ker}_{\mathbb{Z}}(A)
. It follows from the definition that the free vectors of
A
form one bouquet, which we call the
free bouquet
of
G_{A}
. If the configuration of weights
{\bf a}_{1},{\bf a}_{2},\cdots,{\bf a}_{n}
has no free vectors then it is called non-pyramidal.
If the configuration has
k
free vectors is called
k
-pyramidal.
The non-free bouquets are of two types:
mixed
and
non-mixed
. A non-free bouquet is mixed if contains an edge
\{{\mathbb{a}}_{i},{\mathbb{a}}_{j}\}
such that
G({\mathbb{a}}_{i})=\lambda G({\mathbb{a}}_{j})
for some
\lambda<0
, and is non-mixed if it is either an isolated vertex or for all of its edges
\{{\mathbb{a}}_{i},{\mathbb{a}}_{j}\}
we have
G({\mathbb{a}}_{i})=\lambda G({\mathbb{a}}_{j})
with
\lambda>0
, see
[
24
, Lemma 1.2]
.

Let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}
be the bouquets of
A
. Then we may rearrange the column vectors in such a way that there exist positive integers
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{s}
such that the first
i_{1}
column vectors
{\mathbb{a}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{a}}_{i_{1}}
belong to the first bouquet
B_{1}
, the next
i_{2}
column vectors belong to the second bouquet
B_{2}
, and so on the last
i_{s}
vectors belong to the
s
-th bouquet
B_{s}
, thus in particular
i_{1}+\cdots+i_{s}=n
.

Next, we recall from
[
24
]
, the definition of the
bouquet-index-encoding vector

{\mathbb{c}}_{B}
.
If the bouquet
B
is free then we set
{\mathbb{c}}_{B}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
to be any nonzero vector such that

\operatorname{supp}({\mathbb{c}}_{B})=\{i|{\mathbb{a}}_{i}\in B\}
and with the property that the first nonzero component is positive.
For a non-free bouquet
B
of
A
, consider the Gale transforms of the elements in
B
.
All the elements are nonzero and pairwise linearly dependent, therefore there exists at least one nonzero component
j
in all of them. Let
g_{j}=\gcd(G({\bf a}_{i})_{j}|\ {\bf a}_{i}\in B)
and fix the smallest
integer
\ell
such that
{\bf a}_{\ell}\in B
. Let
{\bf c}_{B}
be the vector in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
whose
i
-th component
is
0
if
{\bf a}_{i}\notin B
, and is
\varepsilon_{\ell j}G({\bf a}_{i})_{j}/g_{j}
if
{\bf a}_{i}\in B
, where
G({\mathbb{a}}_{i})_{j}
represents the
j
-th component of the vector
G({\mathbb{a}}_{i})
and
\varepsilon_{\ell j}
the sign of the integer
G({\bf a}_{\ell})_{j}
. Note that the choice of
\ell
implies that the first nonzero component of
{\bf c}_{B}
is positive. Division by
g_{j}
makes the gcd of the components of
{\bf c}_{B}
to be one.
Since each
{\bf a}_{i}
belongs to exactly one bouquet the supports of the vectors
{\bf c}_{B_{i}}
are pairwise disjoint. In addition,
\cup_{i}\operatorname{supp}({\mathbb{c}}_{B_{i}})=[n]
, and taking into account that the first
i_{1}
vectors are in
B_{1}
, the next
i_{2}
in
B_{2}
, and so forth, then
{\mathbb{c}}_{B_{1}}
has only the first
i_{1}
components nonzero,
{\mathbb{c}}_{B_{2}}
has only the following
i_{2}
components nonzero, and so forth. Therefore, we may label the nonzero components of
{\mathbb{c}}_{B_{1}},\ldots,{\mathbb{c}}_{B_{s}}
as follows: the nonzero components of
{\mathbb{c}}_{B_{q}}
are
c_{q1},\ldots,c_{qi_{q}}
, for all
1\leq q\leq s
.

The structure of a non-free bouquet
B
of
A
, that is mixed or non-mixed, can be detected from the vector
{\mathbb{c}}_{B}
. If
B_{i}
is a non-free bouquet of
A
, then
B_{i}
is a mixed bouquet if and only if the vector
{\bf c}_{B_{i}}
has a negative and a positive component, and
B_{i}
is non-mixed if and only if the vector
{\mathbb{c}}_{B_{i}}
has all nonzero components positive, see
[
24
, Lemma 1.6]
.

For each bouquet
B
we define the following vector:

{\mathbb{a}}_{B}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(c_{B})_{j}{\mathbb{a}}_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m},

where
(c_{B})_{j}
is the
j
-th component of the vector
{\mathbb{c}}_{B}
. Note that the
{\bf a}_{j}
’s that contribute in fact to the vector
{\bf a}_{B}
are those belonging to the bouquet
B
. The matrix
A_{B}
whose column vectors are the vectors
{\mathbb{a}}_{B_{1}},{\mathbb{a}}_{B_{2}},\ldots,{\mathbb{a}}_{B_{s}}
,
corresponding to the bouquets of
A
, is called the
bouquet matrix
of
A
.

Finally, we recall (see
[
24
, Theorem 1.9]
) that the matrix
A_{B}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\times s}
has its kernel
\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A_{B})
isomorphic to
\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
via the isomorphism
D:\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A_{B})\mapsto\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
given by

D(u_{1},\ldots,u_{q})=(c_{11}u_{1},\ldots,c_{1i_{1}}u_{1},c_{21}u_{2},\ldots,c_{2i_{2}}u_{2},\ldots,c_{s1}u_{s},\ldots,c_{si_{s}}u_{s}).

Note that the vector
D({\bf u})\in\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
has
n
components, which we will denote by
u_{jt}
in the rest of the paper, where
1\leq j\leq s
,
1\leq t\leq i_{j}
.

The following theorem describes the connection between the toric ideals of
A
and
A_{B}
. By
\mathcal{C}(A)

we denote the
circuits
of
A
, which consists of all nonzero vectors
{\bf{u}}\in\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
with minimal support and the gcd of their components equal to one. While by
\operatorname{Gr}(A)
we denote the
Graver basis
of
A
, which consists of all nonzero vectors
{\bf{u}}\in\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
with no proper conformal decomposition, see
[
26
, Section 4]
. We say that
{\bf u}={\bf v}+_{c}{\bf w}
is a conformal decomposition of the vector
{\bf u}
in two vectors
v
,
w
, if
{\bf u}={\bf v}+{\bf w}
and
{{\bf u}}^{+}={{\bf v}}^{+}+{{\bf w}}^{+},{{\bf u}}^{-}={{\bf v}}^{-}+{{\bf w}}^{-}
. We call the decomposition proper if both
{\bf{v}}
and
{\bf{w}}
are nonzero.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
24
, Theorems 1.9 and 1.11]
)
.

Let
A=[{\bf a}_{1},\dots,{\bf a}_{n}]\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d\times n}
and its bouquet matrix
A_{B}=[{\mathbb{a}}_{B_{1}},\dots,{\mathbb{a}}_{B_{s}}]
. There is a bijective correspondence between the elements of
\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A_{B})

(\operatorname{Gr}(A_{B})
and
\mathcal{C}(A_{B})
, respectively) and the elements of
\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)

(\operatorname{Gr}(A)
and
\mathcal{C}(A)
, respectively). More precisely, this correspondence is defined as follows: if
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{s})\in\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A_{B})
, then
D({\bf u})={\bf c}_{B_{1}}u_{1}+\cdots+{\bf c}_{B_{s}}u_{s}\in\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A=\{{\mathbb{a}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{a}}_{n}\}
be a configuration and
A_{B}=\{{\mathbb{a}}_{B_{1}},\ldots,{\mathbb{a}}_{B_{s}}\}
be its bouquet configuration. Then
A
is non-pyramidal if and only if
A_{B}
is non-pyramidal.

Proof.

Suppose that
A
is a non-pyramidal configuration and
A_{B}
is pyramidal. Then there exists an
{{\bf a}_{B}}_{j}
which is a free vector, thus
G({{\bf a}_{B}}_{j})={\bf 0}
. From the definition of the Gale transform this means that every
element
{\bf u}
in the
\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A_{B})
has the
j
-th component equal to
0
. Then, according to
Theorem
2.1
, all elements
D({\bf u})
in the
\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
have their components
u_{j1},\cdots,u_{ji_{j}}
corresponding to the
B_{j}
bouquet zero, since
u_{jt}=c_{jt}u_{j}=0
. Therefore all the Gale transforms
of the vectors of the bouquet
B_{j}
are zero and thus the vectors are free, contradicting the fact that
A
is non-pyramidal configuration.

For the other direction, suppose that
A_{B}
is a non-pyramidal configuration and
A
is a pyramidal. Then there exists a free vector in
A
, thus its Gale transform is zero. Suppose that this free vector is the
l
-th one in the bouquet
B_{j}
. Then all
elements
D({\bf u})
in the
\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
have their
jl
-th component equal to
0
. According to
Theorem
2.1
, the
jl
-th component is
c_{jl}u_{j}
. But
c_{jl}\not=0
thus
u_{j}=0
, for all
{\bf u}\in\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A_{B})
. Therefore, the Gale transform
of the vector
{{\bf a}_{B}}_{j}
is zero and thus the vector
{{\bf a}_{B}}_{j}
is free, contradicting the fact that
A_{B}
is a non-pyramidal configuration. ∎

3.
Generalized Lawrence matrices and Classification results

Next we recall the definition of a generalized Lawrence matrix from
[
24
, Section 2]
.
Let
\{{\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{s}\}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
be a configuration and
{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{s}
be any set of primitive vectors, a
primitive
vector is an integer vector such that the gcd of
all its components is
1
, with
{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m_{i}}
for some
m_{i}\geq 1
, each having full support and a positive first component. We denote a generalized Lawrence matrix by
\operatorname{GLM}({\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{s}|{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{s})
and is defined as follows: for each
i=1,\dots,s
, let
{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{i}=(c^{\prime}_{i1},\ldots,c^{\prime}_{im_{i}})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m_{i}}
and define

C_{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}-c^{\prime}_{i2}&c^{\prime}_{i1}&0&\ldots&0\\
-c^{\prime}_{i3}&0&c^{\prime}_{i1}&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-c^{\prime}_{im_{i}}&0&0&\ldots&c^{\prime}_{i1}\end{array}\right)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{(m_{i}-1)\times m_{i}}.

Primitivity of each
{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{i}
implies that there exist integers
\lambda_{i1},\ldots,\lambda_{im_{i}}

such that
1=\lambda_{i1}c^{\prime}_{i1}+\cdots+\lambda_{im_{i}}c^{\prime}_{im_{i}}
. Fix a choice of
\lambda_{i1},\ldots,\lambda_{im_{i}}
,
and define the matrices
A_{i}=[\lambda_{i1}{\bf a}^{\prime}_{i},\ldots,\lambda_{im_{i}}{\bf a}^{\prime}_{i}]\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d\times m_{i}}
.
The generalized Lawrence matrix is then the following block matrix:

\operatorname{GLM}({\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{s}|{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{s})=\ \left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}A_{1}&\ A_{2}&\cdots&\ A_{s}\\
C_{1}&\ 0&\cdots&0\\
0&\ C_{2}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\ 0&\cdots&\ C_{s}\end{array}\right)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{p\times n},

where
p=d+(m_{1}-1)+\cdots+(m_{s}-1)
and
n=m_{1}+\cdots+m_{s}
, see
3.6
for an example of a generalized Lawrence matrix.
Note that the definition of a generalized Lawrence matrix depends also on the choice of
\lambda_{ij}
, and thus there are infinitely many choices for
\operatorname{GLM}({\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{s}|{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{s})
. However, all of them have the same kernel and thus define the same toric variety,
see
[
24
, Section 2]
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
24
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
\{{\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{s}\}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
be an arbitrary set of vectors.
Let
{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{s}
be any set of primitive vectors, with
{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m_{i}}
for some
m_{i}\geq 1
, each having full support
and a positive first component. Then, any generalized Lawrence matrix
A=\operatorname{GLM}({\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{s}|{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{p\times n}

has a subbouquet decomposition,
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}
, such that the
i^{th}
subbouquet is encoded by the following vectors:

{\mathbb{a}}_{B_{i}}=({\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{i},{\bf 0},\ldots,{\bf 0})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
and
{\mathbb{c}}_{B_{i}}=({\bf 0},\ldots,{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{i},\ldots,{\bf 0})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{q}
, where
n=m_{1}+\cdots+m_{s}
,
p=d+q-s
and
the support of
{\mathbb{c}}_{B_{i}}
is precisely the
i^{th}
block of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
of size
m_{i}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A=\operatorname{GLM}({\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{s}|{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{s})
be such that
\sum_{j}c^{\prime}_{ij}=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,s
. Then
X_{A}
is a projective toric variety.

Proof.

Adding up the rows of
C_{i}
we obtain the row vector
(-c^{\prime}_{i2}-\cdots-c^{\prime}_{im_{i}},c^{\prime}_{i1},\ldots,c^{\prime}_{i1})
which is equal by the hypothesis with
(c^{\prime}_{i1},c^{\prime}_{i1},\ldots,c^{\prime}_{i1})
,
and since
c^{\prime}_{i1}\neq 0
we conclude that
{\bf 1}_{i}=(1,\ldots,1)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m_{i}}
lies in the rowspan of
C_{i}
. Since this can be done for every
i
by adding the corresponding row vectors
({\bf 0},\ldots,{\bf 1}_{i},\ldots,{\bf 0})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
of
A
we obtain
that
(1,\ldots,1)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
lies in the rowspan of
A
.
∎

Therefore the projectiveness of
X_{A}
, when
A=\operatorname{GLM}({\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{s}|{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{c}}^{\prime}_{s})
, does not depend on the vectors
{\mathbb{a}}^{\prime}_{i}
, in other words on the bouquet ideal, but only on the property of the bouquet-index-encoding vectors that
\sum_{j}c^{\prime}_{ij}=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,s
. For example in
3.6
the bouquet ideal is the ideal of an affine monomial curve.

The next theorem is a reformulation of
[
5
, Theorem 4.4]
since the vectors that their Gale transforms are parallel form a bouquet.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
A=[{\mathbb{a}}_{1}\cdots{\mathbb{a}}_{n}]
be a non pyramidal configuration. Then
X_{A}
is self-dual if and only if

k_{i}=0
for every free vector of
C
.

Suppose now that
A
is
non-pyramidal configuration therefore
k_{i}\geq 1
for all free vectors
{\bf a}_{i}
. Thus

k=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}\geq\sum_{{\bf a}_{i}\text{free}}k_{i}\geq k.

Therefore
k=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}=\sum_{{\bf a}_{i}\text{free}}k_{i}=k
which means that
k_{i}=0
if
{\bf a}_{i}
is a non-free vector and
k_{i}=1
if
{\bf a}_{i}
is a free vector of the
C
configuration. For the converse,
suppose that
k_{i}=1
if
{\bf a}_{i}
is a free vector of
C
then there is no free vector in
A
and thus the
A
configuration is non-pyramidal.
∎

4.
Toric bases and combinatorial properties of self-dual toric varieties

A
Markov basis
of an
m\times n
matrix
A
is a finite subset
M
of
\operatorname{Ker}_{\mathbb{Z}}(A)
such that whenever
\textbf{w},\textbf{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}

and
\textbf{w}-\textbf{v}\in\operatorname{Ker}_{\mathbb{Z}}(A)
there exists a set
\{\textbf{u}_{i}|i=1,\ldots,l\}
that
connects

w

to
v
, where either
\textbf{u}_{i}\in M
or the opposite
-\textbf{u}_{i}\in M
. To connect
w

to
v
means that
\textbf{w}-\textbf{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\textbf{u}_{i}
, and
(\textbf{w}-\sum_{i=1}^{p}\textbf{u}_{i})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
for all
1\leq p\leq l
. A Markov basis of
A
is
minimal
if no proper subset of it is a Markov basis of
A
. A renowned result of Diaconis and Sturmfels

[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
says that
\mathcal{M}
is a minimal Markov basis of
A
if and only if the set
\{\textbf{x}^{u^{+}}-\textbf{x}^{u^{-}}|\textbf{u}\in\mathcal{M}\}
forms a minimal system of binomial generators for the toric ideal
I_{A}
.
The universal Markov basis
\mathcal{M}(A)
of
A
is the union of all minimal Markov bases of
A
, up to identification of opposite vectors. The set of indispensable elements
\mathcal{S}(A)
is the intersection of all different minimal Markov bases, via the same identification. The set of indispensable binomials
\mathcal{S}(I_{A})
of the toric ideal is the set
\{\textbf{x}^{u^{+}}-\textbf{x}^{u^{-}}|\textbf{u}\in\mathcal{S}(A)\}
, that is the intersection of all minimal systems of binomial generators of
I_{A}
, up to identification of opposite binomials.

We say that
{\bf{u}}={\bf{v}}+_{sc}{\bf{w}}
is a semiconformal decomposition of the vector
u
in two vectors
v
,
w
, if
u
=
v
+
w
and
v_{i}>0
implies
w_{i}\geq 0
and
w_{i}<0
implies that
v_{i}\leq 0
for every
1\leq i\leq n
, a concept introduced first in
[
20
]
, where by
v_{i},w_{i}
we denote the
i
-th component of the vectors
{\bf v}
and
{\bf w}
, respectively. We call the decomposition proper if both
{\bf v},{\bf w}
are nonzero. From
[
8
, Proposition 1.1]
, we know that the set of the indispensable elements
\mathcal{S}(A)
of
A
consists of all nonzero vectors in
\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
with no proper semiconformal decomposition.

We say that
{\bf u}={\bf v}+_{ssc}{\bf w}
is a strongly semiconformal decomposition of the vector
{\mathbb{u}}
in two vectors
v
,
w
, if
{\bf u}={\bf v}+{\bf w}
,
{\bf u}^{+}>{\bf v}^{+}
and
{\bf u}^{-}>{\bf w}^{-}
. Recall that for two vectors
{\bf{u}},{\bf{v}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
we say that
{\bf{u}}\geq{\bf{v}}
if
{\bf{u}}-{\bf{v}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, where the inequality is strict if
{\bf{u}}\neq{\bf{v}}
. Note that strongly semiconformal decomposition can be of more than two vectors, see
[
8
, Section 1]
. The universal Markov basis of
A
,
\mathcal{M}(A)
, consists of all nonzero vectors in
\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
with no proper strongly semiconformal decomposition,

[
8
, Proposition 1.4]
.

Definition 4.1
.

A toric ideal
I_{A}
is called strongly robust
if the Graver basis of
I_{A}
is equal to a minimal Markov basis of
I_{A}
.

It follows from the definition, as we have seen in the Introduction, that for strongly robust ideals
the following sets are identical: the set of indispensable elements, any minimal Markov basis,
the universal Markov basis, any reduced Gröbner basis,
the universal Gröbner basis and the Graver basis of
A
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A=\{{\mathbb{a}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{a}}_{n}\}
be a non-pyramidal configuration such that
X_{A}
is a self-dual projective toric variety. Then the toric ideal is strongly robust.

Proof.

Since
A
is non-pyramidal configuration then by Theorem
3.4
we obtain
\sum_{i}(c_{B})_{i}=0
for every bouquet
B
of
A
.
The latter condition implies that each vector
{\mathbb{c}}_{B}
has at least one positive and one negative component,
since by definition
{\mathbb{c}}_{B}
has the first nonzero component positive. Thus each bouquet
B
is mixed by Theorem
3.4
,
and applying
[
24
, Corollary 4.4]
we obtain that the toric ideal is strongly robust.
∎

We examine the relation between the Graver basis of a multiset configuration

and the Graver basis of

the nonnegative integer

\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}
. If

I_{C}\subset K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

Let

{\bf u^{\prime}}

For

in
\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
such that

and all nonzero components in the i-block of

, for every
i

Theorem 4.5
.

The Graver basis of

is

(\bigcup_{{\bf u}\in Gr(C)}\pi^{-1}({\bf u})_{+})\bigcup Q_{A}.

Proof.

The elements in
Q_{A}
are circuits, since they are homogeneous of degree one and have support of cardinality two. Therefore they are
in the Graver basis, see
[
26
, Proposition 4.11]
.

Let
{\bf u}^{\prime}\in\pi^{-1}({\bf u})_{+}
for some
{\bf u}\in Gr(C)
. Suppose that
{\bf u}^{\prime}={\bf v}^{\prime}+_{c}{\bf w}^{\prime}
,
\pi
is linear therefore

\pi({\bf u}^{\prime})=\pi({\bf v}^{\prime})+\pi({\bf w}^{\prime})
.
Note that all non-zero components in the i-block of
{\bf u}
have the same sign, since
{\bf u}^{\prime}\in\pi^{-1}({\bf u})_{+}
. Then the conformality
of the sum
{\bf u}^{\prime}={\bf v}^{\prime}+_{c}{\bf w}^{\prime}

imply that the sum

{\bf u}=\pi({\bf u}^{\prime})

therefore either
\pi({\bf v}^{\prime})
or

\pi({\bf w}^{\prime})

is zero. But all the non-zero components in the i-th block have the same sign, for every

, therefore if
\pi({\bf v}^{\prime})={\bf 0}
then
{\bf v}^{\prime}={\bf 0}

and if
\pi({\bf w}^{\prime})={\bf 0}
then
{\bf w}^{\prime}={\bf 0}
.
Therefore the sum
{\bf u}^{\prime}={\bf v}^{\prime}+_{c}{\bf w}^{\prime}

.
Thus
(\bigcup_{{\bf u}\in Gr(C)}\pi^{-1}({\bf u})_{+})\bigcup Q_{A}\subset Gr(A)
.

Let
{\bf u}^{\prime}\in Gr(A)
which is not in

. First we claim that all non-zero components in any i-th block of
{\bf u}^{\prime}
are having the same sign.
Suppose not, then let

and

for some

except

and

. Then the sum

The

A contradiction, since

. Therefore all non-zero elements in the i-th block of

are having the same sign, for every

.

Next we claim that

. Then look at

{\bf u}^{\prime}

Suppose first that

and

for

In the case that

from the conformality. We set all

In the last case
u_{i}<0
. In the case
v_{i}=u_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k_{i}+1}u^{\prime}_{ij}

v_{ij}=u^{\prime}_{ij}
and

. Otherwise
there exist a

\sum_{j=1}^{t}u^{\prime}_{ij}\geq v_{i}>\sum_{j=1}^{t+1}u^{\prime}_{ij}

. Set

{\bf v}^{\prime}
the vector with components

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
A
be any configuration such that
X_{A}
is a self-dual projective toric variety. Then the toric ideal

is weakly robust.

Proof.

If

is non-pyramidal then we are done by theorem

, since strongly robust implies weakly robust.

Otherwise, by applying
[
5
, Theorem 3.8]
since

is self-dual, we obtain that the ground set of

,

, is

is non-pyramidal with the corresponding variety

being self-dual.

Since

belongs in the row span of the matrix

which implies that

, see

.

Next we prove that

Note that an element

belongs in the universal Markov basis,
\mathcal{M}(A)

if and only if the corresponding binomial
y^{{\bf u}^{\prime}_{+}}-y^{{\bf u}^{\prime}_{-}}

cannot be written as a combination
of binomials of

[

or

[

, Theorem 1.3.2]
or
[

Let
{\bf u}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Gr}(A)
such that
{\bf u}^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{M}(A)
. Then we
have

y^{{\bf u}^{\prime}_{+}}-y^{{\bf u}^{\prime}_{-}}=\sum y^{{\bf m}_{i}}(y^{{{\bf u}_{i}}_{+}}-y^{{{\bf u}_{i}}_{-}})\in K[y_{11},\ldots,y_{1k_{1}+1},\ldots,y_{n1},\ldots,y_{nk_{n}+1}],

where

, for some
{\bf u}_{i}\in\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
.
Certainly no element in
Q_{A}
can be written in this form, since all are of degree 1. Therefore
{\bf u}^{\prime}\in\pi^{-1}({\bf u})_{+}
for some
{\bf u}\in Gr(C)
, by Theorem

.
Note that the vectors
{\mathbb{a}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbb{a}}_{k}
are free therefore the first

components are zero for every element

{\bf v}
in
\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(C)
, thus

. Let

be the projection
from

to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n-k}

for every

and for the
{\bf u}\in Gr(C)
we have

,
since conformal decompositions in

\operatorname{Ker}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(C)
.

Then setting in the above equation
y_{ij}=x_{i}
for

,

, and
y_{ij}=0
for
1\leq i\leq k
,

, we have

in the ring

.
Therefore

The

is a non-pyramidal self-dual variety thus from Theorem

the ideal

.
But

and

We conclude that

.
∎

Next Theorem proves that for self-dual toric varieties the strongly robust property is equivalent with the property of being non-pyramidal.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
A
be any configuration such that

is a self-dual projective toric variety. The toric ideal

Proof.

By Theorem

Suppose that there exist

is not

Therefore all

is non free in the

Consistent Discretization of a Class of Predefined-Time Stable Systems This work has been submitted to IFAC for possible publication
Esteban Jiménez-Rodríguez, Rodrigo Aldana-López, Juan D. Sánchez-Torres, David Gómez-Gutiérrez, Alexander G. Loukianov

Abstract
As the main contribution, this document provides a consistent discretization of a class of fixed-time stable systems, namely predefined-time stable systems. In the unperturbed case, the proposed approach allows obtaining not only a consistent but exact discretization of the considered class of predefined-time stable systems, whereas in the perturbed case, the consistent discretization preserves the predefined-time stability property. All the results are validated through simulations and compared with the conventional explicit Euler scheme, highlighting the advantages of this proposal.



keywords

Predefined-time stability, Discrete-time systems, Digital implementation, Stability of nonlinear systems, Fixed-time stability

1
Introduction

Given a system with tunable parameters whose origin is fixed-time stable, generally, it is not straightforward, and sometimes it is even impossible to achieve any desired upper bound of the settling time through the selection of the parameters of the system
(
Jiménez-Rodríguez et al. 2019
, Example 1)
. To overcome this drawback, a class of dynamical systems that exhibit the property of
predefined-time stability
has been studied within the last six years
(
Sánchez-Torres et al. 2018
)
.

Predefined-time stability refers to the property that exhibits a particular class of fixed-time stable systems whose solutions converge to the origin before an arbitrary user-prescribed time, which is assigned through an appropriate selection of the tunable parameters of the system
(
Sánchez-Torres et al. 2018
)
. Due to its remarkable features, the study of the properties and applications of the predefined-time stability notion has attracted much attention, mainly in continuous time
(
Aldana-López et al. 2019
;
Aldana-López et al. 2019
;
Muñoz-Vázquez et al. 2019
;
Sánchez-Torres et al. 2019
)
.

The numerical simulation examples conducted in the above works were done applying the conventional explicit (forward) Euler discretization, with a tiny step size (
1\times 10^{-4}
or
1\times 10^{-5}
time units). However, the right side of the ordinary differential equations exhibiting predefined-time stability does not satisfy the Lipschitz condition at the origin. In this case, the explicit Euler discretization scheme does not guarantee that predefined-time stability property will be preserved
(
Levant 2013
;
Huber et al. 2016
;
Polyakov et al. 2019
)
. In other words, the discrete-time equation solutions may be inconsistent with the solutions of the continuous-time one.

In this sense, this work concerns with the discretization of a class of predefined-time stable systems. The discretization process uses the idea of
(
Polyakov et al. 2019
, Figure 1.1)
, but different from the contribution of the mentioned paper, which achieves consistent discretizations of
homogeneous finite-time and practically fixed-time stable systems
, the proposed approach allows the consistent discretization of the class of predefined-time stable systems proposed by
Aldana-López et al. 2019
;
Jiménez-Rodríguez et al. 2019
. Hence, this document provides a consistent discretization of a class of fixed-time stable systems.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows:
\mathcal{K}^{1}
functions and the considered class of predefined-time stable systems are introduced in Section
2
. Then, the exact discretization of the considered class of predefined-time stable systems is developed in Section
3
, in two different ways. Finally, Section
4
considers a consistent discretization of perturbed systems with discontinuous predefined-time control.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Class
\mathcal{K}^{1}
functions

The following definition was inspired on the comparison class
\mathcal{K}
functions
(
Kellett 2014
, Definition 1)

Definition 1

(
\mathcal{K}^{1}
functions)

A scalar continuous function
\kappa:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\left[0,1\right)
is said to belong to class
\mathcal{K}^{1}
, denoted as
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}^{1}
, if it is strictly increasing,
\kappa(0)=0
and
\lim_{r\to\infty}\kappa(r)=1
.

Remark 2

(Invertibility of class
\mathcal{K}^{1}
functions)

Let
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}^{1}
.

(i)

\kappa
is injective since it is strictly increasing;

(ii)

\kappa
is onto since it is continuous,
\kappa(0)=0
and
\lim_{r\to\infty}\kappa(r)=1
.

Hence,
\kappa
is bijective and its inverse exist.

Remark 3

(A connection with probability theory)

Let
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}^{1}
be continuously differentiable on its domain. In this case, there exists a continuous function
\Phi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\kappa(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\Phi(z)\text{d}z
. Moreover, since
\lim_{r\to\infty}\kappa(r)=1
, the function
\Phi
is required to satisfy
\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(z)\text{d}z=1
, i.e., functions
\Phi
and
\kappa
can be viewed as probability density functions and cumulative distribution functions, respectively, of positive random variables.

Example 4

Examples of class
\mathcal{K}^{1}
functions are:

•

\kappa(r)=\frac{2}{\pi}\arctan(ar)
, with
a>0
;

•

\kappa(r)=\frac{r}{r+a}
, with
a>0
;

•

\kappa(r)=1-a^{-r}
, with
a>1
;

•

\kappa(r)=P(a,r)
, with
a>0
, where
P(a,r)=\frac{\gamma(a,r)}{\Gamma(a)}
is the regularized Incomplete Gamma Function,
\gamma(a,r)=\int_{0}^{r}t^{a-1}e^{-t}\text{d}t
is the Incomplete Gamma Function and
\Gamma(a)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{a-1}e^{-t}\text{d}t
is the Gamma Function
(
Abramowitz and Stegun 1965
)
;

•

\kappa(r)=I(a_{1},a_{2},\frac{r}{r+1})
, with
a_{1},a_{2}>0
, where
I(a_{1},a_{2},r)=\frac{b(a_{1},a_{2},r)}{\mathcal{B}(a_{1},a_{2})}
is the regularized Incomplete Beta Function,
b(a_{1},a_{2},r)=\int_{0}^{r}t^{a_{1}-1}(1-t)^{a_{2}-1}\text{d}t
is the Incomplete Beta Function and
\mathcal{B}(a_{1},a_{2})=\int_{0}^{1}t^{a_{1}-1}(1-t)^{a_{2}-1}\text{d}t
is the Beta Function
(
Abramowitz and Stegun 1965
)
.

2.2
A class of predefined-time stable systems

Consider (the scalar form of) the class of systems introduced by
Aldana-López et al. 2019
and
Jiménez-Rodríguez et al. 2019

\dot{x}=-\frac{1}{\rho_{1}(1-\rho_{2})}\frac{\kappa(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)^{\rho_{2}}}{\kappa^{\prime}(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)}\text{sign}\left(x\right),\ \ x(0)=x^{0},

(1)

where
x:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
is the state of the system,
\rho_{1}>0
and
0\leq\rho_{2}<1
are tunable parameters,
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}^{1}
is continuously differentiable, and
\text{sign}\left(\cdot\right)
stands for the signum function given by

\text{sign}\left(x\right)\in\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\{1\}&\text{if}&x>0\\
\{-1\}&\text{if}&x<0\\
{[-1,1]}&\text{if}&x=0.\end{array}\right.

From the theory of ordinary differential equations, (
1
) is a separable first-order equation, whose solution
x(t)
satisfies

\int_{x^{0}}^{x(t)}(1-\rho_{2})\frac{\kappa^{\prime}(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert)}{\kappa(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert)^{\rho_{2}}}\text{sign}\left(\xi\right)\mathrm{d}\xi=-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Applying the change of variable
\xi\to\kappa(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert)^{1-\rho_{2}}\text{sign}\left(\xi\right)
in the left side integral and integrating both sides of the above equation, it yields

\kappa(\left\lvert x(t)\right\rvert)^{1-\rho_{2}}=\kappa(\left\lvert x^{0}\right\rvert)^{1-\rho_{2}}-\frac{t}{\rho_{1}},

for
\kappa(\left\lvert x^{0}\right\rvert)^{1-\rho_{2}}-\frac{t}{\rho_{1}}\geq 0
. From the above, the solution of (
1
) is

x(t)=\\
\small{\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\kappa^{-1}\left(\left[\kappa\left(\left\lvert x^{0}\right\rvert\right)^{1-\rho_{2}}-\frac{t}{\rho_{1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-\rho_{2}}}\right)\text{sign}\left(x^{0}\right)\\
\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\text{ if }0\leq t\leq\rho_{1}\kappa\left(\left\lvert x^{0}\right\rvert\right)^{1-\rho_{2}}\\
0\\
\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\text{ if }t>\rho_{1}\kappa\left(\left\lvert x^{0}\right\rvert\right)^{1-\rho_{2}},\end{array}\right.}

(2)

where
\kappa^{-1}
stands for the inverse of the function
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}
.

Hence, one can conclude that the origin
x=0
of (
1
) is finite-time stable and the settling-time function is
T(x^{0})=\rho_{1}\kappa\left(\left\lvert x^{0}\right\rvert\right)^{1-\rho_{2}}
. But the settling-time function
T(x^{0})=\rho_{1}\kappa\left(\left\lvert x^{0}\right\rvert\right)^{1-\rho_{2}}<\rho_{1}
is bounded, then the origin
x=0
of (
1
) is, in fact, fixed-time stable.

Moreover, for any desired upper bound of the settling-time function
T_{c}
, there exists an appropriate selection of the parameter
\rho_{1}=T_{c}
such that the convergence time will always be less than
T_{c}
for any initial condition
x^{0}
. This property is known as
predefined-time stability
(see Appendix
A
).

Remark 5

It is worth noticing that the right-hand side of (
1
) is continuous and non-Lipschitz if
0<\rho_{2}<1
, and discontinuous if
\rho_{2}=0
. In any case, all the above derivation remains true.

3
Exact discretization of the considered class of predefined-time stable systems

In this section, we develop a discrete-time system whose solutions are equal to the solution of (
1
) in the sampling instants. Hence, we provide the exact form of simulating the class of predefined-time stable systems represented by (
1
).

3.1
Direct construction

Consider the continuous-time system (
1
) and let
x_{k}=x(kh)
, where
h>0
is the sampling step size, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

To obtain the exact discretization of system (
1
), one can apply the same procedure as in Subsection
2.2
, integrating the left side between the samples
x_{k}
and
x_{k+1}
, and the right side between
kh
and
(k+1)h
, respectively, obtaining

\int_{x_{k}}^{x_{k+1}}(1-\rho_{2})\frac{\kappa^{\prime}(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert)}{\kappa(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert)^{\rho_{2}}}\text{sign}\left(\xi\right)\mathrm{d}\xi=-\int_{kh}^{(k+1)h}\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3)

Recall the transformation

y_{\rho_{2}}\colon\begin{array}[]{>{\displaystyle}r @{} >{{}}c<{{}} @{} >{\displaystyle}l}\mathbb{R}&\rightarrow&(-1,1)\\
x&\mapsto&y_{\rho_{2}}(x)=\kappa(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)^{1-\rho_{2}}\text{sign}\left(x\right)\end{array}

(4)

used in Subsection
2.2
to find the solutions of (
1
). This transformation is invertible, and its inverse
y_{\rho_{2}}^{-1}:(-1,1)\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

y_{\rho_{2}}^{-1}(x)=\kappa^{-1}\left(\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{1-\rho_{2}}}\right)\text{sign}\left(x\right).

Using (
4
) in the left side integral of (
3
), one obtains

\int_{\kappa(\left\lvert x_{k}\right\rvert)^{1-\rho_{2}}\text{sign}\left(x_{k}\right)}^{\kappa(\left\lvert x_{k+1}\right\rvert)^{1-\rho_{2}}\text{sign}\left(x_{k+1}\right)}\text{sign}\left(y\right)\mathrm{d}y=-\int_{kh}^{(k+1)h}\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Hence, integrating both sides of the above expression and having in mind that
\text{sign}\left(x_{k+1}\right)=\text{sign}\left(x_{k}\right)
for the first-order system (
1
), the above equation turns into

\kappa(\left\lvert x_{k+1}\right\rvert)^{1-\rho_{2}}\text{sign}\left(x_{k+1}\right)\\
=\kappa(\left\lvert x_{k}\right\rvert)^{1-\rho_{2}}\text{sign}\left(x_{k}\right)-\frac{h}{\rho_{1}}\text{sign}\left(x_{k+1}\right).

(5)

Finally, let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and consider the discontinuous function
\theta_{\rho_{2},\alpha}:\mathbb{R}\to(-\alpha-1,\alpha+1)
defined by
\theta_{\rho_{2},\alpha}(x)=\left(\kappa(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)^{1-\rho_{2}}+\alpha\right)\text{sign}\left(x\right)
. The inverse of
\theta_{\rho_{2},\alpha}
is the continuous function
\theta_{\rho_{2},\alpha}^{-1}:(-\alpha-1,\alpha+1)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\theta_{\rho_{2},\alpha}^{-1}(x)=\kappa^{-1}\left(\left(\max\{\left\lvert x\right\rvert-\alpha,0\}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\rho_{2}}}\right)\text{sign}\left(x\right)

(6)

Thus, replacing (
6
) in (
5
), it yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}x_{k+1}&=\theta_{\rho_{2},h/\rho_{1}}^{-1}\left(\kappa(\left\lvert x_{k}\right\rvert)^{1-\rho_{2}}\text{sign}\left(x_{k}\right)\right),\\
&=\kappa^{-1}\left(\max\left\{\kappa(\left\lvert x_{k}\right\rvert)^{1-\rho_{2}}-\frac{h}{\rho_{1}},0\right\}^{\frac{1}{1-\rho_{2}}}\right)\text{sign}\left(x_{k}\right),\end{split}

(7)

with
x_{0}=x^{0}
.

This following theorem summarizes the previous constructive result:

Theorem 6

The solution of the discrete-time system (
7
) is equivalent to the solution (
2
) of the continuous-time system (
1
) at the sample instants. This is,
x_{k}=x(kh)
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem
6
has the following attractive corollary:

Corollary 7

For any
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, the solution of the discrete-time system (
7
) satisfies
x_{k}=0,\text{ for }k\geq\left\lceil\frac{\rho_{1}}{h}\right\rceil,
where
\left\lceil\cdot\right\rceil
stands for the ceiling function.

{pf}

The solution of the continuous-time system (
1
) satisfies

x(t)=0,\text{ for }t\geq\rho_{1}\kappa\left(\left\lvert x^{0}\right\rvert\right)^{1-\rho_{2}}.

Moreover, from Theorem
6
, we can establish for the solution of the discrete-time system (
7
) that

x_{k}=x(kh)=0,\text{ for }kh\geq\rho_{1}\kappa\left(\left\lvert x^{0}\right\rvert\right)^{1-\rho_{2}}.

which also holds for
kh\geq\rho_{1}
, given that
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}
.

Hence, the result follows.
\blacksquare

3.2
Parallel with implicit Euler discretization

Consider the nonlinear transformation given by (
4
), and define
w(t)=y_{\rho_{2}}(x(t))
. Its time-derivative along the trajectories of system (
1
) is

\dot{w}=-\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}\text{sign}\left(w\right),\ \ w(0)=y_{\rho_{2}}(x^{0}),

(8)

since
\text{sign}\left(w\right)=\text{sign}\left(x\right)
.

Remark 9

If the explicit or forward Euler procedure with time step
h>0
were used to simulate system (
8
), it would lead to oscillations with amplitude
\mathcal{O}(h)
in the neighborhood of the origin
w=0

(
Drakunov and Utkin 1989
;
Utkin 1994
)
, and these oscillations would also be translated into oscillations in the original variable
x
. Those mentioned oscillations, known as numerical chattering, are an undesired effect of an inadequate discretization, since the solution (
2
) does not oscillate.

Using the
implicit or backward Euler method
(as in
(
Drakunov and Utkin 1989
;
Utkin 1994
)
) with time step
h>0
for the discretization of the transformed system (
8
), it yields

w_{k+1}=w_{k}-\frac{h}{\rho_{1}}\text{sign}\left(w_{k+1}\right),

where
w_{k}=y_{\rho_{2}}(x_{k})
and
x_{k}=x(kh)
. Rewriting the above in terms of
x_{k}
, one obtains (
3
), as before.

4
Consistent discretization of predefined-time control of perturbed systems

4.1
Continuous-time predefined-time stabilization of first order perturbed systems

Consider the perturbed control system

\dot{x}=u+\Delta(t,x),\,\,x(0)=x^{0},

(9)

where
x:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
is the state of the system,
u\in\mathbb{R}
is the control input signal, and
\Delta:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is an unknown perturbation term which is assumed to be bounded of the form
\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\Delta(t,x)\right\rvert\leq\delta
, with
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
known.

It is well known that, in continuous time, the perturbation term
\Delta(t,x)
can only be entirely rejected by a discontinuous control term, since no conditions of smoothness, Lipschitz continuity neither continuity are assumed
(
Jiménez-Rodríguez et al. 2019
)
.

According to the above, a suitable feedback predefined-time controller design is

u=-\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\rho_{3}\kappa^{\prime}(0)\right)\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)}\text{sign}\left(x\right),

(10)

where
\rho_{1}>0
,
\rho_{3}\geq\delta
, and
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}^{1}
is continuously differentiable and such that
\kappa^{\prime}(0):=\kappa^{\prime}(0^{+})\geq\kappa^{\prime}(r)
for all
r\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Note that (
10
) is an augmented gain version of the right side of (
1
) with
\rho_{2}=0
.

To verify the predefined-time convergence of the closed-loop system (
9
)-(
10
), note that

\displaystyle\frac{d\left\lvert x\right\rvert}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\rho_{3}\kappa^{\prime}(0)\right)\frac{1}{\kappa^{\prime}(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)}+\Delta(t,x)\text{sign}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{\rho_{1}\kappa^{\prime}(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)}-\rho_{3}\frac{\kappa^{\prime}(0)}{\kappa^{\prime}(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)}+\left\lvert\Delta(t,x)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{\rho_{1}\kappa^{\prime}(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)}-(\rho_{3}-\delta)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{\rho_{1}\kappa^{\prime}(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)},

for
x\neq 0
. Reasoning as in Subsection
2.2
, and using the comparison lemma in the above differential inequality, one can easily conclude that
\left\lvert x(t)\right\rvert=x(t)=0
for
t\geq\rho_{1}
.

4.2
Consistent discretization/simulation

Consider transformation (
4
), with
\rho_{2}=0
, given by
z(t)=y_{0}(x(t))=\kappa(\left\lvert x(t)\right\rvert)\text{sign}\left(x(t)\right)
. Its derivative along the trajectories of the closed-loop system (
9
)-(
10
) is

\dot{z}=-\beta\text{sign}\left(z\right)+f(t,x),\,\,z(0)=y_{0}(x^{0}),

(11)

where
\beta:=\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\rho_{3}\kappa^{\prime}(0)
, and
f(t,x)=\kappa^{\prime}(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)\Delta(t,x)
is the perturbation term in the transformed coordinate
z
, which complies to
\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert f(t,x)\right\rvert\leq\kappa^{\prime}(0)\delta
.

Again, using the
implicit Euler method
with time step
h>0
for the discretization of the system (
11
), it yields

z_{k+1}=z_{k}-h\beta\text{sign}\left(z_{k+1}\right)+hf_{k},

(12)

where
f_{k}=f(kh,x_{k})
is the perturbation term at the sample instants.

Hence, replacing the function
\theta_{0,h\beta}^{-1}
(
6
) into (
12
), one obtains

x_{k+1}=\\
\kappa^{-1}\left(\max\left\{\left\lvert\kappa(\left\lvert x_{k}\right\rvert)\text{sign}\left(x_{k}\right)+hf_{k}\right\rvert-h\beta,0\right\}\right)\times\\
\text{sign}\left(\kappa(\left\lvert x_{k}\right\rvert)\text{sign}\left(x_{k}\right)+hf_{k}\right),

(13)

with
x_{0}=x^{0}
.

The discrete-time system (
13
) provides a way of simulating the closed-loop continuous-time system (
9
)-(
10
). Moreover, it preserves the predefined-time stability behavior, as stated in the following proposition:

Proposition 10

For any
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, the solution of the discrete-time system (
13
) satisfies
x_{k}=0,\text{ for }k\geq\left\lceil\frac{\rho_{1}}{h}\right\rceil
.

{pf}

Taking absolute value in both sides of (
13
) and using the triangle inequality one gets,

\displaystyle\left\lvert x_{k+1}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\kappa^{-1}\left(\max\left\{\left\lvert\kappa(\left\lvert x_{k}\right\rvert)\text{sign}\left(x_{k}\right)+hf_{k}\right\rvert-h\beta,0\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{-1}\left(\max\left\{\kappa(\left\lvert x_{k}\right\rvert)+h\left\lvert f_{k}\right\rvert-h\beta,0\right\}\right).

On the other hand, from
\beta:=\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\rho_{3}\kappa^{\prime}(0)
,
\rho_{3}\geq\delta
,
\left\lvert f_{k}\right\rvert\leq\kappa^{\prime}(0)\delta
and the above, the inequality

\left\lvert x_{k+1}\right\rvert\leq\kappa^{-1}\left(\max\left\{\kappa(\left\lvert x_{k}\right\rvert)-\frac{h}{\rho_{1}},0\right\}\right)

is obtained.

Finally, noticing that the right side of the above inequality is equal to the absolute value of the right side of (
7
) with
\rho_{2}=0
, and that it is a non-decreasing function of
x_{k}
, we get

\left\lvert x_{k}\right\rvert\leq 0,\,\,\forall k\geq\left\lceil\frac{\rho_{1}}{h}\right\rceil

applying Lemma
13
.
\blacksquare

5
Conclusion

This paper presented the development of a consistent discretization for the class of predefined-time stable systems introduced in
Aldana-López et al. 2019
;
Jiménez-Rodríguez et al. 2019
.

The proposed approach allowed the exact discretization of the considered class of systems when no perturbations were assumed. In the perturbed case, the developed consistent discretization preserved the predefined-time stability property.

All the results were confirmed through numerical simulations and compared with the conventional explicit Euler scheme. Even with relatively large time steps, the proposed discretization worked well, as expected, whereas the explicit Euler discretization produced unstable oscillations.

Appendix A
Predefined-time stability

Predefined-time stability refers to the property that exhibits a particular class of fixed-time stable systems with tunable parameters, for which an upper bound of the settling-time function can be arbitrarily chosen through a suitable selection of the parameters
(
Jiménez-Rodríguez et al. 2019
, Defintion 2)
.

This notion is formally defined considering an autonomous system of the form

\dot{\bm{x}}=\bm{f}(\bm{x};\bm{\rho}),\ \ \bm{x}(0)=\bm{x}_{0},

(14)

with
\bm{x}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
the system state,
\bm{\rho}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
the
tunable
parameters of (

Appendix B
A comparison lemma for discrete-time systems

The following is a particular case of
(
Bitsoris and Gravalou 1995
, Proposition 1)
.

Lemma 13

Let
u_{k}
be the solution of

u_{k+1}=f(u_{k}),\,\,u_{0}=u^{0},\,\,u\in\mathbb{R},

where
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and non-decreasing, and let
v_{k}
be such that

v_{k+1}\leq f(v_{k}),\,\,v_{0}\leq u^{0},\,\,v\in\mathbb{R}.

Then,
v_{k}\leq u_{k}
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

{pf}

We proceed by induction. The base case follows from the hypothesis

v_{0}\leq u^{0}=u_{0}.

Now, assume that
v_{k}\leq u_{k}
. Then,

v_{k+1}\leq f(v_{k})\leq f(u_{k})=u_{k+1},

since
f
is non-decreasing. The result follows.
\blacksquare

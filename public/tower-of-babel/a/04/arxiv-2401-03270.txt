Homogenization of Smoluchowski-type equations with transmission boundary conditions
Bruno Franchi, Silvia Lorenzani

Abstract
In this work, we prove a two-scale homogenization result for a set of diffusion-coagulation Smoluchowski-type equations with transmission boundary conditions. This system is meant to describe the aggregation and diffusion of pathological tau proteins in the cerebral tissue, a process associated with the onset and evolution of a large variety of tauopathies (such as Alzheimer’s disease). We prove the existence, positivity and boundedness of solutions to the model equations derived at the microscale (that is the scale of single neurons). Then, we study the convergence of the homogenization process to the solution of a macro-model asymptotically consistent with the microscopic one.



Keywords
:
Smoluchowski equation, transmission boundary conditions,
two-scale homogenization, tauopathies

1.1
Motivation

The system of equations in (
1.1
), known under the generic name
of
discrete Smoluchowski equations with diffusion
, is meant to model the
aggregation and diffusion of the pathological tau protein in the
brain, a process associated with the development of a large
variety of cerebral diseases called tauopathies.
Indeed, pathological accumulations of hyperphosphorylated tau protein
aggregates,
known as neurofibrillary tangles (NFTs), are detected in several
neurodegenerative tauopathies, including Alzheimer’s disease (AD)

[
21
,
22
,
25
,
36
]
.
Tau is a highly soluble, natively unfolded protein which is predominantly
located in the axons of neurons of the central nervous system.
Here, its physiological function is to support assembly and stabilization
of axonal microtubules.
Under pathological conditions, tau can assume abnormal conformations,
due to two transformations: hyperphosphorylation and misfolding.
In particular, hyperphosphorylation has a negative impact on the
biological function of tau proteins since it inhibits the binding to
microtubules, compromising their stabilization and axonal transport and
promotes self-aggregation.
Thus, misfolded tau monomers constitute the building unit for the formation of
oligomers which
in turn lead to highly structured and insoluble fibrils.
For many years, cell autonomous mechanisms were believed to be responsible
for the evolution of neurodegenerative diseases, implying that the same
aggregation events occur independently in different brain cells.
However, accumulating evidence now demonstrates that the progression of tau
pathology reflects cell-to-cell propagation of the disease, achieved through
the release of tau into the extracellular space and the uptake by surrounding
healthy neurons
[
15
,
42
]
.
Extracellular tau then seeds physiological tau in the recipient cells to
propagate the pathological process through neural pathways made by
bundles of axons called tracts.
This mechanism, often referred to as ’prion-like’ propagation of tau
pathology, has led to the idea that extracellular tau could be a novel
therapeutic target to halt the spread of the disease
[
37
]
.
For this reason, in the present work, we focus on a model which describes
in a simplified way the tau diffusion along tracts starting from the
microscopic release and uptake mechanism.

In this context, the set
\Pi_{\epsilon}
represents a bundle of axons
in the white matter (a tract), while the
domain
\Omega_{\epsilon}
indicates the extracellular region filled by
cerebrospinal fluid.
The variables
u_{m}^{\epsilon}\geq 0
and

v_{m}^{\epsilon}\geq 0
refer to the concentration of
m
-clusters of
hyperphosphorylated tau spreading within the neuronal axons (represented
by the long cylindrical cavities) and in extracellular space, respectively.
Concerning the diffusion process, the choice made in our model to define
two coefficients reflects the biological properties of the tau protein
that diffuses differently in extracellular space and in the axon
[
21
]
.
Moreover, within the neuronal axon, we further distinguish between two
different diffusion coefficients, since tau propagates preferentially along
the
z
-axis of the cylinder that represents the axon.
The initial condition
U_{1}^{\epsilon}(x,z)
, given at
t=0
by the
concentration of monomers diffusing within the axons, represents the amount
of endogenous misfolded tau protein,
while the source term
f^{\epsilon}(t,x,z)
, in the system
(
1.1
) for
m=1
, indicates the production of
hyperphosphorylated tau monomers.

1.2
Background of this work

There is a large literature related to the use of the Smoluchowski equation
in various physical contexts (e.g.,

[
41
,
30
,
31
,
14
,
39
,
2
]
),
but only a few works
concerning its application in the biomedical field
[
9
,
5
]
.
Recently, the important role of the Smoluchowski equations in modelling
at different scales the evolution of neurodegenerative diseases, such as
AD, has been investigated in

[
32
,
1
,
4
,
5
,
38
,
18
,
16
,
17
]
.
In fact, the present work is part of a broader research effort carried
on by various
groups of researchers with diverse collaborations on mathematical models of
the progression of AD, and represents an initial bridge
between microscopic models of tau diffusion developed in biology
and macroscopic mathematical
models based on graph theory.
To this end, the homogenization technique, introduced by the mathematicians
in the seventies to carry out a sort of averaging process on the solutions
of partial differential equations with rapidly varying coefficients or
describing media with microstructures, has been applied

[
12
,
11
,
13
,
20
,
27
,
19
,
10
]
.

It is nowadays generally accepted that tau protein, in synergetic
combination with another protein, the so-called
\beta
-amyloid peptide,
plays a key role in the
development of AD (see
[
8
]
).
We refer for instance to
[
5
]
for a discussion on macroscopic
mathematical modeling of this interaction. Here,
our interest is focused on the tau protein
that diffuses through the neural pathway, whereas we ignore
deliberately the action of the
\beta
-amyloid.

Unlike the approach proposed for instance in
[
38
]
,
[
5
]
,
where the modeling of
tau coagulation-diffusion processes has been carried out
on a large scale, that is the scale of the neural network characterized
by the connectivity of different regions through bundles of axons (tracts),
in this paper only the mesoscopic dynamics within a portion of tract has
been investigated.
Starting from the derivation of model equations valid at the microscale,
by using the so-called
two-scale homogenization
technique
we have proved that the solution
two-scale converges to the solution of a macromodel asymptotically consistent
with the original one.
The notion of two-scale homogenization has been first introduced by
Nguetseng
[
35
]
and
Allaire
[
3
]
in the deterministic periodic setting, and later
generalized to the stochastic framework by Zhikov and Piatnitsky
[
43
]
.
Unlike other homogenization techniques (see
[
13
]
,
[
12
]
for a
review),
the two-scale convergence method is self-contained in that, in a single
step, one can derive the homogenized equations and prove the convergence
of the sequence of solutions to the problem at hand.

To stress the novelty of the present paper,
it is worth noting that two-scale homogenization techniques have been
already used by the authors to pass from microscopic to macroscopic
model of the diffusion of toxic proteins in the cerebral parenchyma affected
by AD, but in utterly different biological perspectives and
geometries.
Indeed, in
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
, the authors aimed to describe
production, aggregation and diffusion of
\beta
-amyloid peptide
in the cerebral
tissue (macroscopic scale), a process associated with the development of
AD, starting from the derivation of a model at the single
neuron level (microscopic scale). Thus, the present paper differs from
our previous works both for the biological meaning
and, consequently,
for the geometry of the problem and the
boundary conditions introduced in order to take into account the
peculiarities of tau protein propagation.

There is a large literature devoted to the study of transmission boundary
conditions somehow akin to those of (
1.1
), especially in the
framework of porous media
[
26
]
.
The main results in this respect, which are relevant to our work, can be
found in
[
24
,
34
,
28
]
.
In particular, in
[
24
]
, it is assumed that the porous medium is
composed of periodically arranged cubic cells of size
\epsilon
, split up
into a solid part (a ball surrounded by semi-permeable membranes) and a
fluid part.
In this setting, the diffusion and reactions of chemical species in the
fluid and in the solid part are studied, while transmission boundary
conditions are imposed on the interface.
The same geometry is considered also in
[
28
]
, where deposition effects
under the influence of thermal gradients are analyzed.
The model takes into account the motion of populations of colloidal particles
dissolved in the water interacting together via Smoluchowski coagulation
terms.
The colloidal matter cannot penetrate the solid grain boundary, but it
deposits there. This process is again described by transmission boundary
conditions.
A domain decomposed into long cylindrical cavities periodically distributed
has been defined in
[
34
]
to model a porous medium consisting of a
fluid part and solid bars.
Chemical substances, dissolved in the fluid, are transported by diffusion
and adsorbed on the surface of the bars (through transmission boundary
conditions) where chemical reactions take place.

1.3
Outline of the paper

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section

we prove the existence of weak solutions to the
system of Smoluchowski-type equations (

), while
Section

A priori estimates on the derivatives of the solutions are also obtained and

In Section

and prove our main result concerning the two-scale limit of the solutions

Lemma 2.1
.

For a given small

(

Proof.

Let {
\xi_{j}
} be an orthonormal basis of

H^{1}(\Pi_{\epsilon})
and {
\eta_{j}
} of
H^{1}(\Omega_{\epsilon})
.
We denote by
u_{i,n}^{\epsilon}
and
v_{i,n}^{\epsilon}
(

)
the Galerkin
approximations of
u_{i}^{\epsilon}
and

, respectively, that is

u_{i,n}^{\epsilon}(t,x,z):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{i,j}^{n}(t)\,\xi_{j}(x,z)

(2.6)

for all

,
(x,z)\in\Pi_{\epsilon}
and

v_{i,n}^{\epsilon}(t,x,z):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{i,j}^{n}(t)\,\eta_{j}(x,z)

(2.7)

for all
t\in[0,T]
,
(x,z)\in\Omega_{\epsilon}
.
Let now
i=1
.

Since {
\xi_{j}
} is an orthonormal basis of
H^{1}(\Pi_{\epsilon})
,
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists

u_{1,n}^{\epsilon,0}(x,z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{1,j}^{0,n}\,\xi_{j}(x,z)

(2.8)

so that
u_{1,n}^{\epsilon,0}\rightarrow U_{1}^{\epsilon}(x,z)
in

as

.

Likewise, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists

v_{1,n}^{\epsilon,0}(x,z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{1,j}^{0,n}\,\eta_{j}(x,z)

(2.9)

so that
v_{1,n}^{\epsilon,0}\rightarrow 0
in
H^{1}(\Omega_{\epsilon})

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

To derive the coefficients of the Galerkin approximations, we impose that
the functions
u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}
and
v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}

satisfy Eqs. (
2.1
) and (
2.1
).
Therefore, for all
\psi_{1}\in{\mathrm{s}pan}\{\xi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
,
Eq. (
2.1
) can be written as:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}\partial_{t}u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\,\psi_{1}\,dx\,dz+\epsilon^{2}\,\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}D_{1}\,\nabla_{x}u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi_{1}\,dx\,dz+\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}\tilde{D}_{1}\,\partial_{z}u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\cdot\partial_{z}\psi_{1}\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle+\epsilon\,\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)\,(u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-v_{1,n}^{\epsilon})_{+}\,\psi_{1}\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}=\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}L_{1}^{\tilde{M}}(u_{i,n}^{\epsilon})\,\psi_{1}\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}f^{\epsilon}(t,x,z)\,\psi_{1}\,dx\,dz

(2.10)

By testing Eq. (
2
) with
\psi_{1}=\xi_{k}
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}\partial_{t}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{1,j}^{n}(t)\,\xi_{j}(x,z)\bigg]\,\xi_{k}\,dx\,dz+\epsilon^{2}\,\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}D_{1}\,\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{1,j}^{n}(t)\,\nabla_{x}\xi_{j}(x,z)\bigg]\cdot\nabla_{x}\xi_{k}\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}\tilde{D}_{1}\,\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{1,j}^{n}(t)\,\partial_{z}\xi_{j}(x,z)\bigg]\cdot\partial_{z}\xi_{k}\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle+\epsilon\,\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)\,\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{1,j}^{n}(t)\,\xi_{j}(x,z)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{1,j}^{n}(t)\,\eta_{j}(x,z)\bigg]_{+}\,\xi_{k}\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=-\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}\xi_{k}\,\sum_{a=1}^{M}a_{1,a}\,\sigma_{\tilde{M}}\bigg[\sum_{b=1}^{n}\alpha_{1,b}^{n}(t)\,\xi_{b}(x,z)\bigg]\,\sigma_{\tilde{M}}\bigg[\sum_{c=1}^{n}\alpha_{a,c}^{n}(t)\,\xi_{c}(x,z)\bigg]\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}f^{\epsilon}(t,x,z)\,\xi_{k}\,dx\,dz.

(2.11)

Hence, for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\alpha_{1,k}^{n}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{1jk}\,\alpha_{1,j}^{n}(t)=-F_{1,k}(\alpha_{1}^{n}(t),\beta_{1}^{n}(t))

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}\xi_{k}\,\sum_{a=1}^{M}a_{1,a}\,\sigma_{\tilde{M}}\bigg[\sum_{b=1}^{n}\alpha_{1,b}^{n}(t)\,\xi_{b}(x,z)\bigg]\,\sigma_{\tilde{M}}\bigg[\sum_{c=1}^{n}\alpha_{a,c}^{n}(t)\,\xi_{c}(x,z)\bigg]\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}f^{\epsilon}(t,x,z)\,\xi_{k}\,dx\,dz

(2.12)

where the coefficients
A_{1jk}
are defined by

A_{1jk}:=\epsilon^{2}\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}D_{1}\,\nabla_{x}\xi_{j}\cdot\nabla_{x}\xi_{k}\,dx\,dz+\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}\tilde{D}_{1}\,\partial_{z}\xi_{j}\cdot\partial_{z}\xi_{k}\,dx\,dz

(2.13)

and

F_{1,k}(\alpha_{1}^{n}(t),\beta_{1}^{n}(t)):=\epsilon\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)\,(u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-v_{1,n}^{\epsilon})_{+}\,\xi_{k}\,\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

(2.14)

We choose now
v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}
satisfying Eq. (
2.1
) for all

\phi_{1}\in{\mathrm{s}pan}\{\eta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
, i.e.:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\partial_{t}v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\,\phi_{1}\,dx\,dz+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}d_{1}\,\nabla v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla\phi_{1}\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle-\epsilon\,\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)\,(u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-v_{1,n}^{\epsilon})_{+}\,\phi_{1}\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}N_{1}^{\tilde{M}}(v_{i,n}^{\epsilon})\,\phi_{1}\,dx\,dz

(2.15)

By testing Eq. (
2
) with
\phi_{1}=\eta_{k}
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\partial_{t}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{1,j}^{n}(t)\,\eta_{j}(x,z)\bigg]\,\eta_{k}\,dx\,dz+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}d_{1}\,\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{1,j}^{n}(t)\,\nabla\eta_{j}(x,z)\bigg]\cdot\nabla\eta_{k}\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle-\epsilon\,\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)\,\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{1,j}^{n}(t)\,\xi_{j}(x,z)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{1,j}^{n}(t)\,\eta_{j}(x,z)\bigg]_{+}\,\eta_{k}\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\eta_{k}\,\sum_{a=1}^{M}b_{1,a}\,\sigma_{\tilde{M}}\bigg[\sum_{b=1}^{n}\beta_{1,b}^{n}(t)\,\eta_{b}(x,z)\bigg]\,\sigma_{\tilde{M}}\bigg[\sum_{c=1}^{n}\beta_{a,c}^{n}(t)\,\eta_{c}(x,z)\bigg]\,dx\,dz

(2.16)

Hence, for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\beta_{1,k}^{n}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}B_{1jk}\,\beta_{1,j}^{n}(t)=G_{1,k}(\alpha_{1}^{n}(t),\beta_{1}^{n}(t))

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\eta_{k}\,\sum_{a=1}^{M}b_{1,a}\,\sigma_{\tilde{M}}\bigg[\sum_{b=1}^{n}\beta_{1,b}^{n}(t)\,\eta_{b}(x,z)\bigg]\,\sigma_{\tilde{M}}\bigg[\sum_{c=1}^{n}\beta_{a,c}^{n}(t)\,\eta_{c}(x,z)\bigg]\,dx\,dz

(2.17)

where the coefficients
B_{1jk}
are defined by

B_{1jk}:=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}d_{1}\,\nabla\eta_{j}\cdot\nabla\eta_{k}\,dx\,dz

(2.18)

and

G_{1,k}(\alpha_{1}^{n}(t),\beta_{1}^{n}(t)):=\epsilon\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)\,(u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-v_{1,n}^{\epsilon})_{+}\,\eta_{k}\,\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

(2.19)

Eqs.(
2
) and (
2
) represent a system of
2n
ordinary
differential
equations for the coefficients
\alpha_{1}^{n}=(\alpha_{1,k}^{n})_{k=1,\ldots,n}
,

\beta_{1}^{n}=(\beta_{1,k}^{n})_{k=1,\ldots,n}
.
By taking into account formulas (

) and (
2.9
), we assume as

\alpha_{1,k}^{n}(0)=\alpha_{1,k}^{0,n};\;\;\;\;\beta_{1,k}^{n}(0)=\beta_{1,k}^{0,n}.

(2.20)

Since the left-hand side of this system of ordinary differential equations
is linear and the right-hand side has a sublinear growth (thanks to the
Lipschitz property of the functions),
one can conclude that the
Cauchy problem (
2
), (
2
), (
2.20
) has a unique solution
extended to the whole interval
[0,T]

(see the generalization of the Picard-Lindelöf theorem in
[
23
]
,
Theorem 5.1, p. 156).
Moreover,
\alpha_{1}^{n}=(\alpha_{1,k}^{n})_{k=1,\ldots,n}(t)
,

\beta_{1}^{n}=(\beta_{1,k}^{n})_{k=1,\ldots,n}(t)\in H^{1}([0,T])

for
t\in[0,T]
.
We prove in the following the global Lipschitz property of
F_{1,k}
.
The proof of the Lipschitz continuity of
G_{1,k}
is similar.

Let
(u_{1,n}^{\epsilon},v_{1,n}^{\epsilon})
and

(\tilde{u}_{1,n}^{\epsilon},\tilde{v}_{1,n}^{\epsilon})
be of the form
(
2.6
) and (
2.7
), with coefficients
(
\alpha_{1}^{n}(t)=(\alpha_{1,k}^{n})_{k=1,\ldots,n}
,

\beta_{1}^{n}(t)=(\beta_{1,k}^{n})_{k=1,\ldots,n}
) and
(
\tilde{\alpha}_{1}^{n}(t)=(\tilde{\alpha}_{1,k}^{n})_{k=1,\ldots,n}
,

\tilde{\beta}_{1}^{n}(t)=(\tilde{\beta}_{1,k}^{n})_{k=1,\ldots,n}
),
respectively.

One has:

\displaystyle F_{1,k}(\alpha_{1}^{n}(t),\beta_{1}^{n}(t))-F_{1,k}(\tilde{\alpha}_{1}^{n}(t),\tilde{\beta}_{1}^{n}(t))

\displaystyle=\epsilon\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)\,\bigg[(u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-v_{1,n}^{\epsilon})_{+}-(\tilde{u}_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-\tilde{v}_{1,n}^{\epsilon})_{+}\bigg]\xi_{k}(x,z)\,\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\,C_{1}\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)\,|(u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-\tilde{u}_{1,n}^{\epsilon})-(v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-\tilde{v}_{1,n}^{\epsilon})|\,|\xi_{k}(x,z)|\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\,C_{1}\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)\bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\alpha_{1,j}^{n}(t)-\tilde{\alpha}_{1,j}^{n}(t))\xi_{j}(x,z)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\beta_{1,j}^{n}(t)-\tilde{\beta}_{1,j}^{n}(t))\eta_{j}(x,z)\bigg||\xi_{k}(x,z)|\,\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\,C_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\alpha_{1,j}^{n}(t)-\tilde{\alpha}_{1,j}^{n}(t)|\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)\,|\xi_{j}(x,z)|\,|\xi_{k}(x,z)|\,\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle+\epsilon\,C_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\beta_{1,j}^{n}(t)-\tilde{\beta}_{1,j}^{n}(t)|\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)\,|\eta_{j}(x,z)|\,|\xi_{k}(x,z)|\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\,C_{1}\max\{c_{jk}\}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\alpha_{1,j}^{n}(t)-\tilde{\alpha}_{1,j}^{n}(t)|

\displaystyle+\epsilon\,C_{1}\max\{d_{jk}\}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\beta_{1,j}^{n}(t)-\tilde{\beta}_{1,j}^{n}(t)|

(2.21)

where the coefficients (
c_{jk}
) and (
d_{jk}
) are given by

c_{jk}:=\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)|\xi_{j}(x,z)|\,|\xi_{k}(x,z)|\,\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

(2.22)

d_{jk}:=\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)|\eta_{j}(x,z)|\,|\xi_{k}(x,z)|\,\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

(2.23)

for
j,k=1,\ldots,n
.
Hence, we get

|F_{1,k}(\alpha_{1}^{n},\beta_{1}^{n})-F_{1,k}(\tilde{\alpha}_{1}^{n},\tilde{\beta}_{1}^{n})|\leq\epsilon\,C_{2}(n)\bigg[|\alpha_{1}^{n}-\tilde{\alpha}_{1}^{n}|+|\beta_{1}^{n}-\tilde{\beta}_{1}^{n}|\bigg].

(2.24)

The same conclusions can be drawn also when
1<i\leq M
by applying
exactly the arguments considered above.

Uniform estimates

Let us now prove uniform estimates in

for

and

(

).
In the case

, we take in Eq. (
2
)

as test

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}\partial_{t}(u_{1,n}^{\epsilon})^{2}\,dx\,dz+\epsilon^{2}\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}D_{1}|\nabla_{x}u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}|^{2}\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}\tilde{D}_{1}|\partial_{z}u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}|^{2}\,dx\,dz+\epsilon\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)(u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-v_{1,n}^{\epsilon})_{+}\,u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=-\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{M}a_{1,j}\,\sigma_{\tilde{M}}(u_{1,n}^{\epsilon})\sigma_{\tilde{M}}(u_{j,n}^{\epsilon})\bigg]\,u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\,dx\,dz+\displaystyle\int_{\Pi_{\epsilon}}f^{\epsilon}(t,x,z)\,u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\,dx\,dz

Since the first term on the right-hand side is always negative due to the

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\partial_{t}\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\epsilon^{2}\,D_{1}\|\nabla_{x}u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\tilde{D}_{1}\|\partial_{z}u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq-\epsilon\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)(u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-v_{1,n}^{\epsilon})_{+}\,u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\|f^{\epsilon}(t,x,z)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}

(2.26)

where we have applied the Hölder inequality to the last term on the
right-hand side of Eq. (
2
).
Since the function
f^{\epsilon}(t,x,z)
is bounded in

L^{2}([0,T]\times\Pi_{\epsilon})
,
Eq. (
2
) reads:

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\partial_{t}\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\epsilon^{2}\,D_{1}\|\nabla_{x}u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\tilde{D}_{1}\|\partial_{z}u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq-\epsilon\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)(u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-v_{1,n}^{\epsilon})_{+}\,u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}+C_{f}+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}

(2.27)

where

is a positive constant.
By testing now Eq. (
2
) with
\phi_{1}=v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)
,
we get

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\partial_{t}(v_{1,n}^{\epsilon})^{2}\,dx\,dz+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}d_{1}|\nabla v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}|^{2}\,dx\,dz-\epsilon\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)(u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-v_{1,n}^{\epsilon})_{+}\,v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{M}b_{1,j}\,\sigma_{\tilde{M}}(v_{1,n}^{\epsilon})\,\sigma_{\tilde{M}}(v_{j,n}^{\epsilon})\bigg]\,v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\,dx\,dz

(2.28)

Since again the term on the right-hand side is negative, we conclude:

\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\partial_{t}\|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}+d_{1}\|\nabla v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\leq\epsilon\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)(u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-v_{1,n}^{\epsilon})_{+}\,v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

(2.29)

Adding the inequalities (
2
) and (
2.29
), it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\partial_{t}\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\partial_{t}\|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}+\epsilon^{2}\,D_{1}\|\nabla_{x}u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\tilde{D}_{1}\|\partial_{z}u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}

Let us estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (
2
):

\displaystyle I=\epsilon\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)(u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-v_{1,n}^{\epsilon})_{+}\,(v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-u_{1,n}^{\epsilon})\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}\leq\epsilon\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}c_{1}(x,z)|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}-v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}|^{2}\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\|c_{1}(x,z)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})}\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}(|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}|^{2}+|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}|^{2})\,d\sigma_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\|c_{1}(x,z)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})}\bigg(\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})}+\|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})}\bigg)

(2.31)

Applying the generalized interpolation-trace inequality (
A.7
)
in Appendix A to each
term inside the round brackets, one has:

I\leq C_{1}\,\epsilon\,\eta\bigg(\|\nabla u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\|\nabla v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\bigg)+C_{2}\,\epsilon\,{\eta}^{-1}\bigg(\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\bigg)

(2.32)

where
\eta
is a small positive constant.
If we take into account the estimate (
2.32
), the inequality (
2
)
reads:

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\partial_{t}\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\partial_{t}\|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}+(\epsilon^{2}\,D_{1}-C_{1}\,\epsilon\,\eta)\|\nabla_{x}u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle+(\tilde{D}_{1}-C_{1}\,\epsilon\,\eta)\|\partial_{z}u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+(d_{1}-C_{1}\,\epsilon\,\eta)\|\nabla v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\leq C_{f}

\displaystyle+\bigg(C_{2}\,\epsilon\,{\eta}^{-1}+\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 2}\bigg)\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+C_{2}\,\epsilon\,{\eta}^{-1}\|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}

If one chooses

\partial_{t}\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\partial_{t}\|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\leq 2\,C_{f}+2\,C_{2}\,\epsilon\,{\eta}^{-1}\bigg(\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\bigg)

(2.34)

Integrating Eq. (

) over
[0,t]
with

,
we get

\displaystyle\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\leq\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon,0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon,0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle+2\,C_{f}\,T+2\,C_{2}\,\epsilon\,{\eta}^{-1}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\bigg(\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\bigg)

Since the sequences

and

converge in

.
Therefore, Eq. (

) reads

\displaystyle\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\leq C

\displaystyle+2\,C_{2}\,\epsilon\,{\eta}^{-1}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\bigg(\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\bigg)

Applying Gronwalls’s inequality, we obtain

\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\leq C+2\,C\,C_{2}\,\epsilon\,{\eta}^{-1}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{2\,C_{2}\,\epsilon\,{\eta}^{-1}(t-s)}\,ds

Therefore, given

,
\eta
is fixed
and for

we get:

\|u_{1,n}^{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi_{\epsilon})}+\|v_{1,n}^{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\epsilon})}\leq C_{3}

(2.38)

where

is a positive constant independent of
n
and
\epsilon
.

By testing Eqs. (
2.1
) and (
2.1
),
in the case
1<i\leq M
,
with
\psi_{i}=u_{i,n}^{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)

and

\displaystyle+\frac{\displaystyle p}{\displaystyle 2}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}b_{j,i-j}\,K_{j}\,K_{i-j}\bigg]\,[(v_{i}^{\epsilon}-\overline{m}_{i})^{+}]^{p-1}\,dx\,dz

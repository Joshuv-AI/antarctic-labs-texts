Repeated Angles in the Plane for Angles with Algebraic Tangents
Max Aires

Abstract
We construct a set of points with Ω ⁡ ( n 2 ​ log ⁡ n ) \Omega(n^{2}\log n) triples determining an angle θ \theta whenever tan ⁡ ( θ ) \tan(\theta) is algebraic over ℚ \mathbb{Q} , matching the upper bound of Pach and Sharir. This improves upon the original construction, which was optimal only for tan ⁡ ( θ ) = a ​ m / b \tan(\theta)=a\sqrt{m}/b with a , b , m a,b,m positive integers.

Introduction

Pach and Sharir
[
4
]
proved that, for all
0<\theta<\pi
, any set of
n
points in the Euclidean plane contains at most
O(n^{2}\log n)
triples which determine an angle with measure
\theta
. They also showed that, for
\tan(\theta)=\frac{a\sqrt{m}}{b}
with
a,b,m
positive integers, this upper bound is tight, and that there exists a configuration of
n
points in the plane with
\Omega_{\theta}(n^{2}\log n)
triples determining angle
\theta
. However, for other
\theta
, it remains open whether the upper bound is asymptotically tight (see problem 6, Chapter 6.2 in
[
1
]
).

We shall construct a configuration of
n
points with
\Omega_{\theta}(n^{2}\log n)
triples determining angle
\theta
for any
0<\theta<\pi
where
\tan(\theta)
is algebraic over
\mathbb{Q}
. This class contains many angles of geometric interest; in particular, this gives a construction with
\Omega(n^{2}\log n)
triples if
\theta
is any rational multiple of
\pi
, as well as for any constructible angle
\theta
.

The question remains open in the case that
\tan(\theta)
is transcendental. We note the similar problem of determining the maximum number of subsets similar to a desired set
S
; Laczkovich and Ruzsa
[
3
]
showed that there exist configurations with
\Omega(n^{2})
copies, matching the trivial upper bound, if and only if the cross-ratio of every quadruple in
S
is algebraic. Their construction builds upon that of Elekes and Erdős
[
2
]
, which finds similar copies within “pseudo-grids,” which are grid-like configurations of points made from generalized arithemetic progressions. Our construction makes use of a similar idea, generalizing the original approach of Pach and Sharir
[
4
]
from rectangle lattice grids to similar grid-like sets.

Proof

Theorem 2.1
.

If
\tan(\theta)
is algebraic over
\mathbb{Q}
, then for
n\geq 3
, there exist arrangements of
n
points in the Euclidean plane with
\Omega_{\theta}(n^{2}\ln n)
triples determining angle
\theta
.

Proof.

Let
\tan(\theta)
be a algebraic. Then we can write
\tan(\theta)=\frac{\alpha}{b}
, where
b\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha
is a positive real algebraic integer. Then we can write
\alpha
as the root of a monic, irreducible polynomial of degree
d
in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
. So
\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-module with basis
1,\alpha,\dots,\alpha^{d-1}
.

We shall represent the Euclidean plane as
\mathbb{C}
. Note that
b+i\alpha
will have argument
\theta
, so we seek triples of the form
z,z+\lambda_{1}v,z+(b+i\alpha)\lambda_{2}v
, with
z,v\in\mathbb{C}
and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Fix some positive integer
t
(to be determined later), and define
\mathcal{K}_{t}
and
\mathcal{G}_{t}
as

\mathcal{K}_{t}:=\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}a_{k}\alpha^{k}:a_{k}\in\mathbb{Z},|a_{k}|\leq t\right\}

\mathcal{G}_{t}:=\mathcal{K}_{t}+i\mathcal{K}_{t}=\{u_{1}+iu_{2}:u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{t}\}

Note that
\mathcal{G}_{t}
is the cartesian product of two real generalized arithmetic progression, while the pseduo-grids of Elekes and Erdős
[
2
]
are one complex generalized arithmetic progression. Clearly,
|\mathcal{K}_{t}|=(2t+1)^{d}
and
|\mathcal{G}_{t}|=(2t+1)^{2d}
.

First, suppose
\mu\in\mathcal{K}_{s}
and
\lambda\in\mathcal{K}_{t}
; clearly,
\mu+\lambda\in\mathcal{K}_{s+t}
. We shall now consider
\mu\lambda
. Since
\alpha
is an algebraic integer with minimal polynomial of degree
d
, we can write
\alpha^{d}=c_{0}+c_{1}\alpha+\dots+c_{d-1}\alpha^{d-1}
for
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
; let
C_{1}=1+\max(|c_{0}|,|c_{1}|,\dots,|c_{d-1}|)
. By writing
\lambda=a_{0}+a_{1}\alpha+\dots+a_{d-1}\alpha^{d-1}
with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
|a_{i}|\leq t
, we see that
\alpha\lambda=(a_{0}\alpha+\dots+a_{d-2}\alpha^{d-1})+a_{d-1}(c_{0}+\dots+c_{d-1}\alpha^{d-1})\in\mathcal{K}_{C_{1}t}
. Applying this observation repeatedly, we see that
\alpha^{k}\lambda\in\mathcal{K}_{C_{1}^{k}t}
. Hence, if we write
\mu=b_{0}+b_{1}\alpha+\dots+b_{d-1}\alpha^{d-1}
, we see that
\mu\lambda=b_{0}\lambda+b_{1}\alpha\lambda+\dots+b_{d-1}\alpha^{d-1}\lambda\in\mathcal{K}_{dC_{1}^{d-1}st}
.

Now suppose
u=(u_{1}+iu_{2})\in\mathcal{G}_{s}
and
v=(v_{1}+iv_{2})\in\mathcal{G}_{t}
. Then
u+v=(u_{1}+u_{2})+i(v_{1}+v_{2})\in\mathcal{G}_{s+t}
, and
uv=(u_{1}+iu_{2})(v_{1}+iv_{2})=(u_{1}v_{1}-u_{2}v_{2})+i(u_{1}v_{2}+u_{2}v_{1})\in\mathcal{G}_{2dC_{1}^{d-1}st}
. Letting
C_{2}=2dC_{1}^{d-1}
, we have that
uv\in\mathcal{G}_{C_{2}st}
.

Ungar
[
5
]
proved that a set of
N
points forms has at least
N-1
pairs of points each forming a distinct direction. By applying this to the set
\mathcal{G}_{t}
, we see that
\mathcal{G}_{t}
contains at least
(2t+1)^{2d}-1\geq(2t)^{2d}
pairs giving distinct directions. Since
\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{t}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{2t}
, this means that the nonzero elements of
\mathcal{G}_{2t}
determine at least
(2t)^{2d}
distinct arguments (directions) from
[0,\pi)
.

Now, for each
1\leq k\leq t
, pick
T_{k}
as
(2k)^{2d}-(2(k-1))^{2d}\geq(2k)^{2d-1}
elements from
\mathcal{G}_{2k}
whose angles are all distinct from each other and those of
T_{1},\dots,T_{k-1}
.

For each choice of
v\in\bigcup_{k=1}^{t}T_{k}
and each
0<\lambda\in\mathcal{K}_{\left\lfloor t/k\right\rfloor}
, the element
\lambda v
is distinct, since each
v\in T_{k}
has a distinct angle and each
\lambda
is a unique positive real number. Moreover, if
v\in T_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{2k}
and
\lambda\in\mathcal{K}_{\left\lfloor t/k\right\rfloor}
,
\lambda v\in\mathcal{G}_{C_{2}(2k)\left\lfloor t/k\right\rfloor}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{2C_{2}t}
. Therefore, for any choice of
1\leq k\leq t
and any
z\in\mathcal{G}_{t}
,
v\in T_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{2k}
, and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{\left\lfloor t/k\right\rfloor}
with
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
, the triple

(z,z+\lambda_{1}v,z+(b+i\alpha)\lambda_{2}v)

is a unique triple determining angle
\theta
. Observe that
z+\lambda_{1}v\in\mathcal{G}_{t+2C_{2}t}
and (since
b+i\alpha\in\mathcal{G}_{|b|}
)
z+(b+\alpha i)\lambda_{2}v\in\mathcal{G}_{t+C_{2}|b|(2C_{2}t)}=\mathcal{G}_{(1+2|b|C_{2}^{2})t}
, so for all choices of
z,v,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
, we have
z,z+\lambda_{1}v,z+(b+i\alpha)v\in\mathcal{G}_{(1+2|b|C_{2}^{2})t}
. Since the elements of
\mathcal{K}_{\left\lfloor t/k\right\rfloor}
are equally distributed between positive and negative, with one element
0
, the number of choices for
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
is
\left(\frac{|\mathcal{K}_{\left\lfloor t/k\right\rfloor}|-1}{2}\right)=\frac{(2\left\lfloor\frac{t}{k}\right\rfloor+1)^{2d}-1}{2}\geq\left\lfloor\frac{t}{k}\right\rfloor^{2d}
. Hence the total number of triples chosen in this way is

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{t}|\sum_{k=1}^{t}|T_{k}|\left\lfloor\frac{t}{k}\right\rfloor^{2d}

\displaystyle\geq(2t+1)^{2d}\sum_{k=1}^{t}(2k)^{d-1}\left(\frac{t}{2k}\right)^{2d}

\displaystyle\geq t^{2d}\sum_{k=1}^{t}k^{2d-1}\left(\frac{t}{k}\right)^{2d}

\displaystyle=t^{4d}\sum_{k=1}^{t}\frac{1}{k}

\displaystyle\geq t^{4d}\ln t

Hence, there are at least
t^{4d}\ln t
triples which determine angle
\theta
with elements chosen from
\mathcal{G}_{(1+2|b|C_{2}^{2})t}
. This set contains
(2(1+2|b|C_{2}^{2})t+1)^{2d}\leq(4|b|C_{2}^{2}+3)^{2d}t^{2d}
, so if we let
C_{3}=(4|b|C_{2}^{2}+3)^{2d}
, we have a set of at most
C_{3}t^{2d}
points whose elements form at least
t^{4d}\ln t
triples.

Now, for an arbitrary
n
, pick
t
so that
C_{3}t^{2d}<n\leq C_{3}(t+1)^{2d}
. If
n\geq C_{3}
, then we have a set with
C_{3}t^{2d}\leq n
points. Moreover, if
t\geq 1
, then
n\leq C_{3}(2t)^{2d}
, so
t\geq\frac{(n/C_{3})^{1/2d}}{2}
, so the number of triples determining angle
\theta
is at least
t^{4d}\ln t\geq\frac{(n/C_{3})^{2}}{2^{4d}}\ln\left(\frac{(n/C_{3})^{1/2d}}{2}\right)=\frac{n^{2}}{2dC_{3}^{2}2^{4d}}\ln\left(\frac{n}{2^{2d}C_{3}}\right)
. Since
d
and
C_{3}
depend only on our angle
\theta
and not on
n
, we have a construction of at most
n
points with
\Omega_{\theta}(n^{2}\log n)
triples determining angle
\theta
, as desired.
∎

A Solution Operator for the ∂ ¯ \overline{\partial} Equation in Sobolev Spaces of Negative Index
Ziming Shi, Liding Yao

Abstract
. Let Ω \Omega be a strictly pseudoconvex domain in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} with C k + 2 C^{k+2} boundary, k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 . We construct a ∂ ¯ \overline{\partial} solution operator (depending on k k ) that gains 1 2 \frac{1}{2} derivative in the Sobolev space H s , p ​ ( Ω ) H^{s,p}(\Omega) for any 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty and s > 1 p − k s>\frac{1}{p}-k . If the domain is C ∞ C^{\infty} , then there exists a ∂ ¯ \overline{\partial} solution operator that gains 1 2 \frac{1}{2} derivative in H s , p ​ ( Ω ) H^{s,p}(\Omega) for all s ∈ ℝ s\in\mathbb{R} . We obtain our solution operators via the method of homotopy formula. A novel technique is the construction of “anti-derivative operators” for distributions defined on bounded Lipschitz domains.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of the paper is to prove the following results:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
C^{k+2}
boundary, where
n\geq 2
and
k
is a positive integer. For
1\leq q\leq n
, there is a linear operator
\mathcal{H}_{q}
(depending on
k
) such that for all
1<p<\infty
and
s>\frac{1}{p}-k
,

(1)

\mathcal{H}_{q}:H^{s,p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)\to H^{s+\frac{1}{2},p}_{(0,q-1)}(\Omega)
is bounded.

(2)

u=\mathcal{H}_{q}\varphi
is a solution to the equation
\overline{\partial}u=\varphi
for any
\varphi\in H^{s,p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
which is
\overline{\partial}
-closed.

Here
H^{s,p}_{(0,q)}
denotes the space of
(0,q)
forms with
H^{s,p}(\Omega)
coefficients, and
H^{s,p}
is the fractional Sobolev space (see Definition
2.3
).

We also have the following result when the boundary is smooth.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
C^{\infty}
boundary, where
n\geq 2
. For
1\leq q\leq n
there is a linear operator
\mathcal{H}_{q}
such that for all
1<p<\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

(1)

\mathcal{H}_{q}:H^{s,p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)\to H^{s+\frac{1}{2},p}_{(0,q-1)}(\Omega)
is bounded.

(2)

u=\mathcal{H}_{q}\varphi
is a solution to the equation
\overline{\partial}u=\varphi
for any
\varphi\in H^{s,p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
which is
\overline{\partial}
-closed.

The main novelty of our results is the existence of solutions when the given forms are in
H^{s,p}
with negative (smoothness) index
s
, which can be viewed as the dual of certain positive indexed function space in the sense of distributions.
This is new in
\overline{\partial}
theory. In the
L^{2}
and
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann theory for the
\overline{\partial}
equation, the Hilbert space
L^{2}
, which is the
H^{0,2}
space, plays a fundamental role in the methods of approach as well as the formulation of regularity results; see for example Kohn
[
Koh63
]
, Hörmander
[
Hör65
]
and also the monographs
[
Str10
]
and
[
CS01
]
.

We now describe our approach when the given form
\varphi
has negative index. We shall call the operator
\mathcal{H}_{q}
in Theorem
1.1
a homotopy operator, since it satisfies the following
\overline{\partial}
homotopy formula

\varphi=\overline{\partial}\mathcal{H}_{q}\varphi+\mathcal{H}_{q+1}\overline{\partial}\varphi.

In particular when
\varphi
is a
\overline{\partial}
-closed form in
\Omega
,
\mathcal{H}_{q}\varphi
is a solution to the
\overline{\partial}
equation. To construct our solution operator, we start with a
\overline{\partial}
homotopy operator of the form

(1.1)

\mathcal{H}\varphi(z)=\int_{\mathcal{U}}K_{0}(z,\zeta)\wedge\mathcal{E}\varphi(\zeta)+\int_{\mathcal{U}\setminus\Omega}K_{1}(z,\zeta)\wedge[\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}]\varphi,\quad[\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}]\varphi:=\overline{\partial}\mathcal{E}\varphi-\mathcal{E}\overline{\partial}\varphi,

where
\mathcal{U}
is a neighborhood of
\overline{\Omega}
and

\mathcal{E}
denotes some extension operator that extends a function or distribution class defined on
\Omega
to the same class in the whole space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. A homotopy formula of this form was first constructed in
[
Gon19
]
to prove the estimates for Hölder-Zygmund space on strictly pseudoconvex domains with
C^{2}
boundary, and a similar modified form was introduced in
[
SY21b
]
to prove the estimates in Sobolev space
H^{s,p}
of positive index
s
.

The kernel
K_{0}
in (
1.1
) is a linear combination of the first derivative of the Newtonian potential, and consequently one can use standard theory of singular integrals to show that the first integral in (
1.1
) is in
H^{s+1,p}(\Omega)
for any
\varphi\in H^{s,p}(\Omega)
with
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
For the second integral, we note that the kernel and its derivatives in
z
will blow up as
\zeta\in\mathcal{U}\setminus\Omega
approaches to the boundary
b\Omega
.
The important fact here is that the commutator
[\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}]\varphi
vanishes identically inside
\Omega
, and thus have a certain vanishing order as
\zeta\to b\Omega
. Accordingly, one would expect to have some cancellations between the blow-up of the kernel and the vanishing of the commutator near
b\Omega
, so that the integrals can be estimated.
This is indeed the key idea used earlier in
[
Gon19
]
and
[
SY21b
]
. However, in the present case, the commutator
[\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}]\varphi
belongs to a Sobolev space with negative index, thus it exists only in the sense of distribution. In other words, the regularity is too low to even speak of the vanishing order. To remedy this, our idea is to express
[\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}]\varphi
as the derivatives of more regular functions
that lie in some positive index space. For this to work, we need to make sure these “more regular” functions still have the important property that it vanishes identically in
\Omega
. More specifically we construct a class of “anti-derivative” operators on bounded Lipschitz domains.

Proposition 1.3

(Anti-derivative operator with support condition)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain, and let
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denote the space of tempered distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For any
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, there exist linear operators
\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}^{m,\gamma}:\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, with
|\gamma|\leq m
such that

(1)

\mathcal{S}^{m,\gamma}_{\Omega}:H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to H^{s+m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
1<p<\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
;

(2)

f=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq m}D^{\gamma}\mathcal{S}^{m,\gamma}_{\Omega}f,\quad f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N});

(3)

If
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfies
f|_{\Omega}\equiv 0
, then
(\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}^{m,\gamma}f)|_{\Omega}\equiv 0
for
|\gamma|\leq m
.

In other words, the operator
S^{m,\gamma}_{\Omega}
gains
m
derivatives.
We can now apply the proposition with
f
being the commutator
[\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}]\varphi
, and write the second integral in (
1.1
) as

\int_{\mathcal{U}\setminus\Omega}K_{1}(z,\zeta)\wedge\sum_{|\gamma|\leq m}D^{\gamma}\mathcal{S}^{m,\gamma}_{\Omega}[\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}]\varphi(\zeta)\,dV(\zeta),

where
\mathcal{S}^{m,\gamma}_{\Omega}[\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}]\varphi
is identically
0
inside
\Omega
and is in
H^{s,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
for
s>0
(by choosing
m
sufficiently large).
We still need to transport the extra derivatives
D^{\gamma}
to the kernel
K_{1}
, a procedure we call “reverse integration by parts”, since it moves derivatives to the kernel rather than away from it as is usually done with integration by parts. Finally the blow up of
D^{\gamma}K_{1}
cancels nicely with the vanishing order of
\mathcal{S}^{m,\gamma}_{\Omega}[\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}]\varphi
near
b\Omega
, and thus we can show that the integral is in
H^{s+\frac{1}{2},p}(\Omega)
for any
\varphi\in H^{s,p}(\Omega)
with
s>\frac{1}{p}-k
.

To control the blow-up order of derivatives at the singularities of the kernel
K_{1}
, we use the regularized Henkin-Ramirez functions introduced in (
[
Gon19
, Section 5]
) when the boundary is
C^{k+2}
,
k\geq 1
. For the case of
C^{\infty}
boundary, the classical Henkin-Ramirez function is sufficient.

Proposition
1.3
is interesting in its own right and has other applications as well. In a recent preprint
[
SY21a
]
, the authors generalize Proposition
1.3
to Triebel-Lizorkin and Besov spaces. The results are then used to derive several important properties of the function spaces and the universal extension operator of Rychkov (see
[
Ryc99
]
). For the proof of Proposition
1.3
, we remark that
Condition
3
is the most non-trivial statement. To satisfy solely
1
and
2
, one can simply take
f=(I-\Delta)^{m}((I-\Delta)^{-m}f)=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2m}c_{\gamma}D^{\gamma}((I-\Delta)^{-m}f)
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
(I-\Delta)^{-m}
is the Bessel potential operator (see (
2.1
)). Our construction of
\mathcal{S}^{m,\gamma}_{\Omega}
is inspired by Rychkov’s construction of the universal extension operator
[
Ryc99
]
.
The idea is that to break up
\widehat{f}
using a special Littlewood-Paley family
\{\lambda_{j}\}
, and then reduce it to a division problem in the frequency space.

We now mention some brief history of the
1/2
estimate for
\overline{\partial}
equation in space of positive index, for which the theory has been well-studied and is quite satisfactory now. For
\Omega
with
C^{\infty}
boundary, Kohn
[
Koh63
]
showed that the
L^{2}
canonical solution
\overline{\partial}^{\ast}\mathcal{N}\varphi
is in
H^{s+\frac{1}{2},2}(\Omega)
if
\varphi\in H^{s,2}(\Omega)
for any
s\geq 0
. Here
\mathcal{N}
is the
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann operator. Later, Greiner-Stein
[
GS77
]
proved the
1/2
estimate for
\overline{\partial}^{\ast}\mathcal{N}\varphi
when
\varphi
is a
(0,1)
form of class
H^{k,p}(\Omega)
, where
k
is a non-negative integer and
1<p<\infty
. Their results were subsequently extended by Chang
[
Cha89
]
to any
(0,q)
form
\varphi
.
Also in
[
GS77
]
, it was proved that
\overline{\partial}^{\ast}\mathcal{N}\varphi\in\Lambda^{r+\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{\Omega})
for any
\varphi\in\Lambda^{r}(\overline{\Omega})
,
r>0
, where
\Lambda^{r}
is the Hölder-Zygmund space.
In both
[
GS77
]
and
[
Cha89
]
, the boundary is required to be
C^{\infty}
.

In parallel with the
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann method, another theory has been developed to solve the
\overline{\partial}
equation using integral kernels, in a way that
generalize the one dimensional Cauchy integral to higher dimension. Compared to the PDE approach used by the school of Kohn and Stein, the kernel method is more geometric in nature, and often times simpler to use. The solutions are not canonical in any sense, however they come in with a great variety of estimates, including some like the sup norm estimate which are not accessible by other methods. The theory began with the pioneering work of Grauert-Lieb
[
GL70
]
and Kerzman
[
Ker71
]
. Soon afterwards, Henkin-Ramanov
[
RH71
]
constructed a solution operator on domains with
C^{2}
boundary with the sharp
1/2
gain in smoothness in Hölder space. The operator of Henkin-Romanov works for a
(0,1)
form
\varphi\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
.
Later on, Siu
[
Siu74
]
(for
(0,1)
form
\varphi
) and Lieb-Range
[
LR80
]
(for
(0,q)
forms
\varphi
) constructed solutions in
C^{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{\Omega})
when
\varphi
in
C^{k}(\overline{\Omega})
, under the assumption that boundary is
C^{k+2}
for positive integer
k
. For a detailed history of the method of integral kernels in
\overline{\partial}
problems, we refer the reader to the book by Range
[
Ran86
]
.

More recently, using the kernel method Gong
[
Gon19
]
constructed a
\overline{\partial}
solution operator on strictly pseudoconvex domains with
C^{2}
boundary which gains
1/2
derivative for any
\varphi\in\Lambda^{r}(\overline{\Omega})
,
r>1
. Assuming
C^{2}
boundary still, the authors in a recent preprint
[
SY21b
]
found a
\overline{\partial}
solution operator which gains
1/2
derivative for any
\varphi\in H^{s,p}(\overline{\Omega})
, with
1<p<\infty
and
s>\frac{1}{p}
; it was further shown in
[
SY21b
]
that the same solution operator gains
1/2
derivative for any
\varphi\in\Lambda^{r}(\overline{\Omega})
,
r>0
.

Historically, the problem for constructing a
\overline{\partial}
solution operator with
derivative estimates has been a quite difficult one. In fact, it is still an open problem whether the solution operator of Henkin-Ramanov gains
1/2
derivative for
\varphi\in C^{k}
(or
\Lambda^{k}
),
k\geq 1
. In this regard, the use of extension operator and the commutator to prove derivative estimates turns out to be extremely fruitful and arguably essential. We remark that the construction of
the homotopy operator (
1.1
) was inspired by the earlier work of Lieb-Range
[
LR80
]
, Peters
[
Pet91
]
and Michel-Shaw
[
MS99
]
. Similar techniques have yielded sharp estimates for
\overline{\partial}
equation on other types of domains such as finite type convex domains
[
Ale06
]
, and strongly
\mathbb{C}
-linearly convex domains of class
C^{1,1}

[
GL21
]
.

2.
Function Spaces

In this section we review the definition and properties of Sobolev spaces. We also recall the Littlewood-Paley characterization, which plays a central role in later proofs.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set, and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq p<\infty
. We denote by
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
the space of (complex-valued) functions
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
such that
D^{\alpha}f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
|\alpha|\leq k
, and equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|D^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\left(\int_{\Omega}|D^{\alpha}f(x)|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dV(x),\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

We denote by
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the space of Schwartz functions, and by
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the space of tempered distributions. For
g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we set the Fourier transform
\widehat{g}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}dx
, and the definition extends naturally to tempered distributions.

Definition 2.2
.

We let
\mathscr{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denote the space
1
1

1

In some literature like
[
Tri83
, Section 5.1.2]
, the notation is
Z(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
of all infinite order moment vanishing Schwartz functions. That is, all
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\int x^{\alpha}f(x)dx=0
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
, or equivalently, all
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\widehat{f}(\xi)=O(|\xi|^{\infty})
as
\xi\to 0
.

Definition 2.3

(Fractional Sobolev Space)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
1<p<\infty
. We define
H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to be the fractional Sobolev space consisting of all (complex-valued) tempered distribution
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
(I-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and equipped with norm

\|f\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\|(I-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Here
(I-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}
is the Bessel potential operator given by

(2.1)

(I-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f=((1+4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\widehat{f}(\xi))^{\vee}.

Remark 2.4
.

There is another type of commonly-used fractional Sobolev spaces called
Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces
, which is in fact the Besov
\mathscr{B}_{pp}^{s}
-spaces. We do not use the Sobolev-Slobodeckij type space in our paper.

Definition 2.5
.

A
special Lipschitz domain

\omega
is an open set of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with Lipschitz boundary that has the following form

\omega=\{(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{N}>\rho(x^{\prime})\},\quad x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N-1}).

where
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz and satisfies
\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}<1
.

A
bounded Lipschitz domain

\Omega
is a bounded open set of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that for any
p\in b\Omega
there is an affine linear transformation
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
and a special Lipschitz domain
\omega
such that

\Omega\cap\Phi(\mathbb{B}^{N})=\Phi(\omega\cap\mathbb{B}^{N}).

We denote the positive cone
\mathbb{K}
as

\mathbb{K}=\{(x^{\prime},x_{N}):x_{N}>|x^{\prime}|\}.

By the assumption
|\nabla\rho|\leq 1
, we have
\omega+\mathbb{K}\subseteq\omega
.

Remark 2.6
.

In many literature like
[
Tri06
, Section 1.11]
, the definition for a special Lipschitz domain only requires
\rho
to be a Lipschitz function. In other words,
\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}
is finite but can be arbitrary large.
By taking invertible a linear transformation we can make
\nabla\rho
small in the new coordinates.

For functions and distributions defined on domains of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have the following definition:

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set.

(1)

Define
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\Omega):=\{\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}:\tilde{f}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\}
.

(2)

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1<p<\infty
, define
H^{s,p}(\Omega):=\{\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}:\tilde{f}\in H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\}
with norm

\|f\|_{H^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\inf_{\widetilde{f}|_{\Omega}=f}\|\tilde{f}\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

(3)

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1<p<\infty
, define
H^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
to be the subspace of
H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
which is the completion of

under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.

Remark 2.8
.

When
k
is a non-negative integer we have
H^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with equivalent norms. If
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, we have
H^{k,p}(\Omega)=W^{k,p}(\Omega)
which is intrinsically defined. (See
[
Tri83
, Theorem 2.5.6(ii)]
and
[
Tri06
, Theorem 1.222(i)]
.) For non-integer
s
, there is no such simple intrinsic characterization for
H^{s,p}(\Omega)
.
For
1<p<\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the Sobolev space
H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the same as the Triebel-Lizorkin space
\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with equivalent norm. See
[
Tri83
, Definition 2.3.1/2 and Theorem 2.5.6(i)]
. More specifically, we have the following result.

Proposition 2.9

(Littlewood-Paley Theorem)
.

Let
\lambda_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a Schwartz function whose Fourier transform satisfies

\operatorname{supp}\widehat{\lambda}_{0}\subseteq\{|\xi|<2\},\qquad\widehat{\lambda}_{0}|_{\{|\xi|\leq 1\}}\equiv 1.

For
j\geq 1
, let
\lambda_{j}
be the Schwartz function whose Fourier transform is

\widehat{\lambda}_{0}(2^{-j}\xi)-\widehat{\lambda}_{0}(2^{-(j-1)}\xi)
.
Then for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1<p<\infty
, there exists a
C=C_{\lambda_{0},p,s}>0
such that

(2.2)

C^{-1}\|f\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{2js}|\lambda_{j}\ast f(x)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\quad\forall f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

provided that either term in the inequality is finite.

The Triebel-Lizorkin norm
\mathscr{F}^{s}_{p2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is given by

\|f\|_{\mathscr{F}^{s}_{p2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{2js}|\lambda_{j}\ast f(x)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where different choices of
\lambda
satisfying the aforementioned properties give equivalent norms (see
[
Tri83
, Section 2.3.2]
). Thus (
2.2
) implies
H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\mathscr{F}^{s}_{p2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

By way of Definition
2.7
, one can also define for an arbitrary open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{s}(\Omega)=\{\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}:\tilde{f}\in\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\}
with norm
\|f\|_{\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{s}(\Omega)}:=\inf\limits_{\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}=f}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
(see
[
Tri06
, Definition 1.95(i)]
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}

(2)

(3)

and

s>\frac{1}{p}-1
.

[

Definition 2.11
.

A

\phi=(\phi_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}

•

3.
The Anti-derivative Operator

In this section we construct the “anti-derivative” operators that make a distribution more regular in the Sobolev space, while preserving the vanishing condition on bounded Lipschitz domains. The proof uses an estimate for the Peetre’s maximal operators.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
M>0
and let
\eta=(\eta_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of Schwartz functions. The associated
Peetre maximal operators

\mathcal{P}_{j}^{\eta,M}
to
\eta
is given by

\mathcal{P}^{\eta,M}_{j}f(x):=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|(\eta_{j}\ast f)(x-y)|}{(1+2^{j}|y|)^{M}},\quad f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Proposition 3.2

(Peetre’s maximal estimate)
.

Let
0<p<\infty
. Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
M>N/\min(p,2)
, and
\eta=(\eta_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}\in\mathfrak{G}
. Then there is a
C=C_{p,q,s,M,\eta}>0
such that

(3.1)

\|(2^{js}\mathcal{P}_{j}^{\eta,M}f)_{j=0}^{\infty}\|_{L^{p}(\ell^{2})}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Proof.

See
[
Pee75
]
(and
[
Pee76
]
for a correction) when
\eta
is taken to be a “homogeneous Littlewood-Paley family”. In our case we consider the “non-homogeneous case” and the results can be found in, for example
[
BPT96a
, Theorem 5.1(i)]
.
∎

Lemma 3.3

(Heideman type estimate)
.

Let
\phi,\psi\in\mathfrak{G}
be two generalized dyadic resolutions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then for any
M,J>0
there is a
C=C_{\phi,\psi,M,J}>0
such that

\int|\phi_{j}\ast\psi_{k}(x)|(1+2^{\max(j,k)}|x|)^{J}dx\leq C2^{-M|j-k|},\quad\forall\,j,k\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

This is a special case of
[
BPT96b
, Lemma 2.1]
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{R}
. Fix
\phi,\psi\in\mathfrak{G}
. We define the operator
T^{r}
by

(3.2)

T^{r}f:=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{jr}\psi_{j}\ast\phi_{j}\ast f,\quad f\in\mathscr{S}.

Then for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, there is a
C=C(\phi,\psi,p,r,s)
such that

(3.3)

\|T^{r}f\|_{H^{s-r,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Proof.

Using the Littlewood-Paley characterization of Sobolev norm, we can write (
3.3
) as

(3.4)

\left\|\left(2^{j(s-r)}\lambda_{j}\ast\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{kr}\psi_{k}\ast\phi_{k}\ast f\right)_{j=0}^{\infty}\right\|_{L^{p}(\ell^{2})}\leq C\|(2^{js}\lambda_{j}\ast f)_{j=0}^{\infty}\|_{L^{p}(\ell^{2})}.

Fixing
j
on the left-hand side of (
3.4
), we apply Lemma
3.3
to get

\displaystyle 2^{j(s-r)}2^{kr}|\lambda_{j}\ast\psi_{k}\ast\phi_{k}\ast f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{j(s-r)}2^{kr}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\lambda_{j}\ast\psi_{k}(x-y)||\phi_{k}\ast f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq 2^{j(s-r)}2^{kr}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\lambda_{j}\ast\psi_{k}(x-y)|(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{M}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\phi_{k}\ast f(t)|}{(1+2^{k}|x-t|)^{M}}\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j(s-r)}2^{kr}2^{-M|j-k|}(\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\phi,M}f)(x)

\displaystyle=2^{k(s-r)}2^{(j-k)(s-r)}2^{kr}2^{-M|j-k|}(\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\phi,M}f)(x)

\displaystyle=2^{ks}2^{(j-k)(s-r)}2^{-M|j-k|}(\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\phi,M}f)(x)\leq 2^{-|j-k|}2^{ks}(\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\phi,M}f)(x),

where in the last inequality we choose
M=|s-r|+1
.
It follows that

(3.5)

\displaystyle 2^{j(s-r)}|\lambda_{j}\ast T^{r}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{j(s-r)}2^{kr}|\lambda_{j}\ast\psi_{k}\ast\phi_{k}\ast f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-|j-k|}2^{ks}(\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\phi,M}f)(x).

Applying Young’s inequality and Proposition
3.2
with
M>N
we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-|j-k|}2^{ks}(\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\phi,M}f)\right)_{j=0}^{\infty}\right\|_{L^{p}(\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left(2^{ks}\mathcal{P}_{k}^{\phi,M}f\right)_{k=0}^{\infty}\right\|_{L^{p}(\ell^{2})}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Combinining (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) we obtain (
3.4
).
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose
u\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
is supported in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}u=0
. Then
(2\pi i\xi)^{-1}\widehat{u}(\xi)
is the Fourier transform of the function
v(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}u(s)ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}u(x-s)ds
. In addition,
v\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
and
v
is also supported in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Proof.

By the assumption,
\widehat{u}
has infinite order vanishing at
0
and rapid decay at
\infty
, thus
(2\pi i\xi)^{-1}\widehat{u}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

It suffices to show that
\widehat{v}(\xi)=(2\pi i\xi)^{-1}\widehat{u}(\xi)
. Indeed since
\big(\operatorname{p.v.}(2\pi i\xi)^{-1}\big)^{\vee}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sgn}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle\big((2\pi i\xi)^{-1}\widehat{u}(\xi)\big)^{\vee}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sgn}\ast f(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}u(t-s)ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{-}}u(t-s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}u(t-s)ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t-s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}u(t-s)ds=v(t),

where we use the fact that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t-s)ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(s)ds=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Finally since
\operatorname{supp}u\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we have
\operatorname{supp}u\subseteq[\varepsilon_{0},\infty)
for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. Therefore
v(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}u(t-s)\,ds=0
when
t<\varepsilon_{0}
, in particular
\operatorname{supp}v\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
∎

For
u,v
given in Lemma
3.5
, we shall use the notation:

v=D_{t}^{-1}u
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open convex cone centered at
0
, and suppose
f\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is supported in
K
. Then there exist
\sigma_{1},\dots\sigma_{N}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
which satisfy the following:

(1)

For each
l=1,\cdots,N
,
\operatorname{supp}\sigma_{l}
is supported in
K
.

(2)

f=\sum_{l=1}^{N}D_{x_{l}}\sigma_{l}
.

Proof.

By passing to an invertible linear transformation and taking suitable linear combinations, we can assume that
K\supseteq(0,\infty)^{N}
. Thus by convexity we have
K+[0,\infty)^{N}\subseteq K
.

By taking Fourier transform, it suffices to find
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{N})
such that

(3.7)

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\sum_{l=1}^{N}(2\pi i\xi_{l})\widehat{\sigma}_{l}(\xi).

Let
g(t)
be the function as in Lemma
2.12
, namely that
g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
,
\operatorname{supp}g\subseteq[1,\infty)
,
\int g=1
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{k}g(t)=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Define

g_{j}(t):=2^{j}g(2^{j}t)-2^{j-1}g(2^{j-1}t),\quad j\in\mathbb{Z},

so that
g_{j}
are Schwartz functions supported in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. By the moment condition on
g
, we have
g_{j}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
. It follows from Lemma
3.5
that
D_{t}^{-1}g_{j}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
and
\operatorname{supp}D_{t}^{-1}g_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

For
l=1,\dots,N
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define
\lambda_{l,j}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by

(3.8)

\lambda_{l,j}(x):=\left(\prod_{1\leq i\leq l-1}2^{j}g(2^{j}x_{i})\right)(D_{t}^{-1}g_{j})(x_{l})\left(\prod_{l+1\leq i\leq N}2^{j-1}g(2^{j-1}x_{i})\right).

The corresponding Fourier transform satisfies

(3.9)

\displaystyle\widehat{\lambda}_{l,j}(\xi)=\left(\prod_{1\leq i\leq l-1}\widehat{g}(2^{-j}\xi_{i})\right)\left[\frac{\widehat{g}(2^{-j}\xi_{l})-\widehat{g}(2^{1-j}\xi_{l})}{2\pi i\xi_{l}}\right]\left(\prod_{l+1\leq i\leq N}\widehat{g}(2^{1-j}\xi_{i})\right).

By the support conditions for
g
and
D_{t}^{-1}g_{j}
, we have
\operatorname{supp}\lambda_{l,j}\subseteq[2^{-j},\infty)^{N}
for all
1\leq l\leq N
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Also, we have the scaling relation:

(3.10)

\widehat{\lambda}_{l,j}(\xi)=2^{-j}\widehat{\lambda}_{l,0}(2^{-j}\xi),\quad\lambda_{l,j}(x)=2^{-j}2^{jN}\lambda_{l,0}(2^{j}x).

We now define formally

(3.11)

\sigma_{l}(x):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{l,j}\ast f(x),\quad 1\leq j\leq N,

and accordingly

(3.12)

\widehat{\sigma}_{l}(\xi)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\lambda}_{l,j}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),\quad 1\leq l\leq N.

Since
\operatorname{supp}\lambda_{l,j}\subseteq[0,\infty)^{N}
, we have
\operatorname{supp}\sigma_{l}\subseteq\overline{\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{supp}\lambda_{l,j}}+K\subseteq K
.
This proves
1
.

To show that
\sigma_{l}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it suffices to show that the sum

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\lambda}_{l,j}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),\quad 1\leq l\leq N

converges in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and has infinite order vanishing at
\xi=0
. To this end, we show that for any
M\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
, the following series converges to a bounded function uniformly for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

(3.13)

|\xi|^{M}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\partial{}^{\alpha}\widehat{\lambda}_{l,j}(\xi)||\partial{}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(\xi)|,\quad 1\leq l\leq N.

We separate the sum into
\sum_{j>0}
and
\sum_{j\leq 0}
. By (
3.10
) we have

(3.14)

\partial{}^{\alpha}\widehat{\lambda}_{l,j}(\xi)=2^{-(1+|\alpha|)j}\partial{}^{\alpha}(\widehat{\lambda}_{l,0})(2^{-j}\xi).

Since
\widehat{f}
has rapid decay as
\xi\to\infty
and is flat at
\xi=0
, we have

\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}

|\xi|^{M}|\partial{}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(\xi)|<\infty
. Thus

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\xi|^{M}|\partial{}^{\alpha}\widehat{\lambda}_{l,j}(\xi)||\partial{}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(\xi)|\leq\|\partial{}^{\alpha}\widehat{\lambda}_{l,0}\|_{C^{0}}\||\xi|^{M}\partial{}^{\beta}\widehat{f}\|_{C^{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-(1+|\alpha|)j}<\infty.

On the other hand, by (
3.14
) and the fact that
\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{|\alpha|+2}|\partial{}^{\alpha}\widehat{\lambda}_{l,0}(\xi)|=C_{0}<\infty
we have

\displaystyle|\partial{}^{\alpha}\widehat{\lambda}_{l,j}(\xi)|

\displaystyle=2^{-(1+|\alpha|)j}|\partial{}^{\alpha}\widehat{\lambda}_{l,0}(2^{-j}\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}2^{-(1+|\alpha|)j}|2^{-j}\xi|^{-|\alpha|-2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}2^{j}|\xi|^{-|\alpha|-2}.

Setting
\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{M-|\alpha|-2}|\partial{}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(\xi)|=C_{1}
, we get

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{0}|\xi|^{M}|\partial{}^{\alpha}\widehat{\lambda}_{l,j}(\xi)|\partial{}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(\xi)|\leq C_{0}C_{1}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{0}2^{j}<\infty.

Thus
(\ref{S0_cvg})
converges uniformly for all
M
, and multi-index
\alpha
and
\beta
, and we conclude that
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{l,j}\ast f\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
l=1,\dots,N
.

Finally we prove
2
. It suffices to show that for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

(3.15)

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(2\pi i\xi_{l})\widehat{\lambda_{l,j}}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi).

Indeed, using (
3.8
) we have for
\xi\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(2\pi i\xi_{l})\widehat{\lambda}_{l,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\widehat{g}(2^{-j}\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{g}(2^{-j}\xi_{l-1})\big(\widehat{g}(2^{-j}\xi_{l})-\widehat{g}(2^{1-j}\xi_{l})\big)\widehat{g}(2^{1-j}\xi_{l+1})\cdots\widehat{g}(2^{1-j}\xi_{N})

\displaystyle=\lim_{J\to\infty}\sum_{j=-J}^{J}\widehat{g}(2^{-j}\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{g}(2^{-j}\xi_{N})-\widehat{g}(2^{1-j}\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{g}(2^{1-j}\xi_{N})

\displaystyle=\lim_{J\to\infty}\widehat{g}(2^{-J}\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{g}(2^{-J}\xi_{N})-\widehat{g}(2^{J}\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{g}(2^{J}\xi_{N})=1,

where we used the fact
\widehat{g}(0)=1
and
\lim_{\xi\to\pm\infty}\widehat{g}(\xi)=0
.
This proves (
3.15
) and
2
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a cone centered at

and let
g\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be supported in

. Then for each

there exists

with Proposition

Proposition 3.8
.

For each
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, there exist convolution operators
\mathcal{F}_{0}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\beta}
with
|\beta|=m
, such that

(1)

\mathcal{F}_{0}:H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to H^{s+m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\mathcal{F}_{\beta}:H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to H^{s+m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

(2)

For any
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
f=\mathcal{F}_{0}f+\sum_{|\beta|=m}D^{\beta}\mathcal{F}_{\beta}f
.

(3)

Let
\omega
be a special Lipschitz domain and suppose
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfies

. Then
\mathcal{F}_{\beta}f|_{\omega}\equiv 0

Proof.

Let
(\phi,\psi)
be a
\mathbb{K}
-dyadic pair. Since
\psi_{1}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is supported
-\mathbb{K}
, in view of Proposition

with

, there exist
\mu_{\beta}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
supported in
-\mathbb{K}
such that

(3.16)

\psi_{1}=\sum_{|\beta|=m}D^{\beta}\mu_{\beta}.

Define

\eta_{0}^{\beta}:=0,\quad\eta^{\beta}_{j}(x):=2^{N(j-1)}\mu_{\beta}(2^{j-1}x),\quad j\geq 1.

In particular, we have
\eta_{1}^{\beta}(x)=\mu_{\beta}(x)
and
\eta_{j}^{\beta}(x)=2^{N(j-1)}\eta_{1}^{\beta}(2^{j-1}x)
.
Hence
\eta^{\beta}\in\mathfrak{G}
, and
\eta_{j}^{\beta}\subset-\mathbb{K}
. Setting

in Proposition

, we can write for

\displaystyle\psi_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=2^{N(j-1)}\psi_{1}(2^{j-1}x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\beta|=m}2^{N(j-1)}(D^{\beta}\mu_{\beta})(2^{j-1}x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\beta|=m}2^{m(1-j)}D^{\beta}\eta_{j}^{\beta}(x).

Therefore

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_{j}\ast\phi_{j}\ast f

\displaystyle=\psi_{0}\ast\phi_{0}\ast f+\sum_{|\beta|=m}D^{\beta}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{m(1-j)}\eta_{j}^{\beta}\ast\phi_{j}\ast f

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{F}_{0}f+\sum_{|\beta|=m}D^{\beta}(\mathcal{F}_{\beta}f),

where we set

(3.17)

\mathcal{F}_{0}f:=\psi_{0}\ast\phi_{0}\ast f,\quad\mathcal{F}_{\beta}f:=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{m(1-j)}\eta_{j}^{\beta}\ast\phi_{j}\ast f,\quad|\beta|=m.

Clearly
\mathcal{F}_{0}:H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to H^{s+l,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for any

, (since

. By Proposition

,
\mathcal{F}_{\beta}:H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to H^{s+m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Finally if

, then

\operatorname{supp}(\eta_{j}\ast\phi_{j}\ast f)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\eta_{j}+\operatorname{supp}\phi_{j}+\operatorname{supp}f=-\mathbb{K}-\mathbb{K}+\omega^{c}\subseteq\omega^{c}
, for each

. By (

) we have

.

We are ready to prove the main result of the section, namely constructing anti-derivative operators on bounded Lipschitz domains. This is done by using partition of unity and Proposition

We now introduce the following notation for partition of unity:

Notation 3.9
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain, and let
\mathcal{U}
be a bounded open set containing
\overline{\Omega}
. We shall use the following construction, which can be obtained by standard partition of unity argument:

•

(U_{\nu})_{\nu=1}^{M}
are finitely many bounded open sets on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

•

(\Phi_{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N})_{\nu=1}^{M}
are invertible affine linear transforms.

•

(\chi_{\nu})_{\nu=0}^{M}
as

-functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
0\leq\chi_{\nu}\leq 1
.

•

(\omega_{\nu})_{\nu=1}^{M}
as special Lipschitz domains on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. That is
\omega_{\nu}=\{x_{N}>\rho_{\nu}(x^{\prime})\}
for some
\|\nabla\rho_{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}}<1

They have the following properties:

•

b\Omega\subset\bigcup_{\nu=1}^{M}U_{\nu}\subset\subset\mathcal{U}
.

•

\chi_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\chi_{\nu}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U_{\nu})
for

, and
\chi_{0}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}^{2}\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
\overline{\Omega}
.

•

For each

,
U_{\nu}=\Phi_{\nu}(\mathbb{B}^{N})
and
U_{\nu}\cap\Omega=U_{\nu}\cap\Phi_{\nu}(\omega_{\nu})
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. For any
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, there exist operators
\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}^{m,\gamma}:\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, with
|\gamma|\leq m

\displaystyle\qquad\wedge\sum_{i+j=n-2}\left[\frac{\left<d\overline{\zeta}-d\overline{z}\,,\,d\zeta\right>}{|\zeta-z|^{2}}\right]^{i}\wedge\left[\overline{\partial}_{\zeta,z}\frac{\left<W(z,\zeta),d\zeta\right>}{\left<W(z,\zeta),\zeta-z\right>}\right]^{j}.

We set

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1<p<\infty
.
Suppose
\varphi\in H^{s,p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
. Then
\mathcal{H}^{0}_{q}\varphi\in H^{s+1,p}_{(0,q-1)}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. More precisely, there exists a constant
C=C(\Omega,s,p)
such that

(4.13)

\|\mathcal{H}^{0}_{q}\varphi\|_{H^{s+1,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}\leq C\|\varphi\|_{H^{s,p}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

The proof for positive integer
s
can be found in
[
Shi21
, Proposition 3.2]
. We now prove the case
s\leq-1
. The rest is done by interpolation.

\mathcal{H}^{0}_{q}\varphi
can be written as a linear combination of

\int_{\mathcal{U}}\frac{\overline{\zeta^{i}-z^{i}}}{|\zeta-z|^{2n}}f(\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)=\partial{}_{z_{i}}\mathcal{N}\ast f(z),

where
f
is a coefficient function of
\mathcal{E}\varphi
and
\mathcal{N}(x):=c_{n}\frac{1}{|x|^{2n-2}}
is the Newtonian potential. In particular
f
has compact support in
\mathcal{U}
. Let
g\in H^{-s-1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(\mathcal{U})
, where
-s-1\geq 0
(
s\leq-1
) and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. Then

\displaystyle\left<D\mathcal{N}\ast f,g\right>

\displaystyle=-\left<f,D\mathcal{N}\ast g\right>.

Now
D\mathcal{N}\ast g
is in
H^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
by the result for positive index. Since
f\in H^{s,p}_{c}(\mathcal{U})
has compact support, we have

|\left<f,D\mathcal{N}\ast g\right>|\leq\|f\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathcal{U})}\|D\mathcal{N}\ast g\|_{H^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{U})}\leq\|f\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathcal{U})}\|g\|_{H^{-s-1,p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{U})}.

Hence
|\left<D\mathcal{N}\ast f,g\right>|\leq\|f\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathcal{U})}\|g\|_{H^{-s-1,p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{U})}
. Since
H^{s+1,p}(\mathcal{U})=\left(H^{-s-1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(\mathcal{U})\right)^{\prime}
(see Proposition
2.10

3
), we see that
D\mathcal{N}\ast f\in H^{s+1,p}(\mathcal{U})
and (
4.13
) holds.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\beta\geq 0
,
\alpha>-1
,
\alpha<\beta-\frac{1}{2}
, and let
0<\delta<\frac{1}{2}
.
Define
\delta(z):=\operatorname{dist}(z,b\Omega)
. Then for any
z,\zeta\in\mathcal{U}
:

\int_{\mathcal{U}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\delta(\zeta)^{\alpha}\,dV(\zeta)}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{2+\beta}|\zeta-z|^{2n-3}}\leq C\delta(z)^{\alpha-\beta+\frac{1}{2}};\quad\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta(z)^{\alpha}\,dV(z)}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{2+\beta}|\zeta-z|^{2n-3}}\leq C\delta(\zeta)^{\alpha-\beta+\frac{1}{2}}.

where the constants depends only on
\alpha,\beta
,
\Omega
and
\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

By the remark after Proposition
4.6
, near each
\zeta_{0}\in b\Omega
there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{V}_{\zeta_{0}}
of
\zeta_{0}
and a coordinate system
\zeta\mapsto(s=(s_{1},s_{2}),t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2n-2}
such that

(4.14)

\displaystyle|\Phi(z,\zeta)|\gtrsim\delta(z)+|s_{1}|+|s_{2}|+|t|^{2},\quad|\zeta-z|\gtrsim|(s_{1},s_{2},t)|.

for
z\in\mathcal{V}_{\zeta_{0}}\cap\Omega
and
\zeta\in\mathcal{V}_{\zeta_{0}}\setminus\Omega
, with
|s_{1}(\zeta)|\approx\delta(\zeta)
. By switching the roles of
z
and
\zeta
, we can also find a coordinate system
z\mapsto(\tilde{s}=(\tilde{s}_{1},\tilde{s}_{2}),\tilde{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2n-2}
such that

(4.15)

\displaystyle|\Phi(z,\zeta)|\gtrsim\delta(\zeta)+|\tilde{s}_{1}|+|\tilde{s}_{2}|+|\tilde{t}|^{2},\quad|\zeta-z|\gtrsim|(\tilde{s}_{1},\tilde{s}_{2},t)|

for
z\in\mathcal{V}_{\zeta_{0}}\cap\Omega
and
\zeta\in\mathcal{V}_{\zeta_{0}}\setminus\Omega
, with
|\tilde{s}_{1}(z)|\approx\delta(z)
.
By partition of unity in both
z
and
\zeta
variables and (
4.14
), we have up to a constant depending only
\Omega
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\delta(\zeta)^{\alpha}\,dV(\zeta)}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{2+\beta}|\zeta-z|^{2n-3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{s_{1}^{\alpha}t^{2n-3}\,ds_{1}\,ds_{2}\,dt}{(\delta(\zeta)+s_{1}+s_{2}+t^{2})^{2+\beta}(\delta+s_{1}+s_{2}+t)^{2n-3}}.

By
[
SY21b
, Lemma 6.5]
, the integral is bounded by a constant multiple of
\delta(z)^{\alpha-\beta+\frac{1}{2}}
, which proves the first inequality. The second inequality follows by the same way.
∎

Proposition 4.9

We estimate the integral

be a coefficient function of

we mean each component of

, or
s+l-1>0
. Since

in
\Omega

2.10

in

and

Since

, it suffices to consider the case when

, namely,

(4.18)

\int_{\mathcal{U}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}\mathcal{S}^{l}f(\zeta)\frac{P_{\mu_{0},\mu_{1}}(\zeta,z)}{\Phi^{\mu_{2}+1}(z,\zeta)|\zeta-z|^{2n-3}}\,dV(\zeta),\quad\mu_{0}+\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}\leq m+l.

\zeta

Proposition 4.10

(4.41)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\delta(z)^{(m-s-\frac{1}{2})p}|\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{0}f(z)|^{p}\,dV(z)\lesssim\|\mathcal{S}^{l}f\|_{H^{s-1+l,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}^{p},\quad f=[\overline{\partial},E]\varphi

if
m
and
l
are chosen to be sufficiently large. And also for any
s>-k+\frac{1}{p}
,

(4.42)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\delta(z)^{(m-s-k-\frac{1}{2})p}|\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{1}f(z)|^{p}\,dV(z)\leq\|\mathcal{S}^{l}f\|_{H^{s-1+l,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})},

if
m
is sufficiently large. Here

(4.43)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{0}f(z):=\int_{\mathcal{U}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}\mathcal{S}^{l}f(\zeta)\frac{P_{0,0}(\zeta,z)}{\Phi^{r+l+1}(z,\zeta)|\zeta-z|^{2n-3}}\,dV(\zeta),\quad 1\leq r\leq m;

(4.44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{1}f(z):=\int_{\mathcal{U}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}\mathcal{S}^{l}f(\zeta)\frac{P_{0,l}(\zeta,z)}{\Phi^{r+1}(z,\zeta)|\zeta-z|^{2n-3}}\,dV(\zeta),\quad 1\leq r\leq m,

with
P_{0,0},P_{0,l}
given by formula (
4.17
).
Now
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{0}f
and
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{1}f
are both
C^{\infty}
for
z
in the interior of
\Omega
. By a partition of unity we can assume that
z\in\mathcal{V}\cap\Omega
and
\zeta\in\mathcal{V}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
, where
\mathcal{V}
is the neighborhood in the remark right after Proposition
4.6
. Since
|\Phi(z,\zeta)|\leq C|z-\zeta|
, we can assume that
|\Phi(z,\zeta)|<1
for any
z,\zeta\in V
. Therefore for any
r\leq m
,

(4.45)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{0}^{\prime}f(z)|\leq\int_{\mathcal{U}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}\frac{|\mathcal{S}^{l}f(\zeta)||P_{0,0}(\zeta,z)|}{|\Phi^{r+l+1}(z,\zeta)||\zeta-z|^{2n-3}}\,dV(\zeta)\leq\int_{\mathcal{U}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}\frac{|\mathcal{S}^{l}f(\zeta)||P_{0,0}(\zeta,z)|}{|\Phi^{m+l+1}(z,\zeta)||\zeta-z|^{2n-3}}\,dV(\zeta)

and

(4.46)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime}f(z)|\leq\int_{\mathcal{U}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}\frac{|\mathcal{S}^{l}f(\zeta)||P_{0,l}(\zeta,z)|}{|\Phi^{r+1}(z,\zeta)||\zeta-z|^{2n-3}}\,dV(\zeta)\leq\int_{\mathcal{U}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}\frac{|\mathcal{S}^{l}f(\zeta)||P_{0,l}(\zeta,z)|}{|\Phi^{m+1}(z,\zeta)||\zeta-z|^{2n-3}}\,dV(\zeta).

Inequalities (
4.41
) and (
4.42
) then follow by the proof of Proposition
4.9
, if
s>-k+\frac{1}{p}
, and
m,l
are sufficiently large.
∎

We can now prove Theorem

using Proposition

and Proposition

.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

We have for any

and

sufficiently large,

(4.47)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}_{q}^{1}\varphi\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2},p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\sum_{|\gamma|\leq m}\|\delta^{m-s-\frac{1}{2}}D^{\gamma}\mathcal{H}^{1}_{q}\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{H^{s,p}(\Omega)}.

Together with Proposition

for the operator

We now state and prove the version of Theorem

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain with

boundary. Let

and let
\mathcal{H}_{q}
be defined by (

)-(

), where

is any positive integer greater or equal to

and

defines a bounded linear operator

Proof.

The proof follows the same strategy as for Theorem

Using Theorem

Corollary 4.12

(Homotopy formula)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem

, let

,

and

. Suppose

\overline{\partial}\varphi\in H^{s,p}_{(0,q+1)}(\Omega)

Appendix A
Some Results on Sobolev Spaces

Proposition A.1

(Equivalent norms)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
1<p<\infty
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. Then for
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
H^{s,p}(\Omega)
have the following equivalent norm:

\|f\|_{H^{s,p}(\Omega)}\approx\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{H^{s-m,p}(\Omega)}.

This is
[
SY21b
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
with
r<m
. Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and define
\delta(x)
be the distance function to the boundary
b\Omega
. If
u\in W^{m,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, and
\|\delta^{m-r}D^{m}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}<\infty
, then
u\in H^{r,p}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, there exists a constant
C
that does not depend on
u
such that

\|u\|_{H^{r,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\sum_{|\beta|\leq m}\|\delta^{m-r}D^{\beta}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

For
m=0
, the statement is proved in
[
SY21b
, Proposition 5.4]
, namely for
u\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
,

\|u\|_{H^{-s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\delta^{s}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\quad s\geq 0.

The general case follows by applying Proposition
A.1
:

\|f\|_{H^{r,p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{H^{r-m,p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\delta^{m-r}\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\quad m>r.\qed

Proposition A.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and let
f\in H^{s,p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
with
\overline{\partial}f\in H^{s,p}_{(0,q+1)}(\Omega)
, then there exists a sequence
f_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}_{(0,q)}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}f\quad\text{in $H^{s,p}(\Omega)$;}

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}f_{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}\overline{\partial}f\quad\text{in $H^{s,p}(\Omega)$.}

Proof.

First, we show that for a function
g\in H^{s,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
,
there exist
g_{\varepsilon}
such that

\|g_{\varepsilon}-g\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}\to 0
. Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(-K)
, where
K
is some cone centered at
0
; we set
\psi_{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon^{-N}\psi(\varepsilon^{-1}x)
and
g_{\varepsilon}:=\psi_{\varepsilon}\ast g
. We have

\displaystyle\|g_{\varepsilon}-g\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\ast g-g\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|(I-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}(\psi_{\varepsilon}\ast g)-(I-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}

We claim that
(I-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}(\psi_{\varepsilon}\ast g)=\psi_{\varepsilon}\ast(I-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}g
. Indeed, this can be seen by taking Fourier transform:
(1+4\pi|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\widehat{\psi_{\varepsilon}}\widehat{g}=\widehat{\psi_{\varepsilon}}(1+4\pi|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\widehat{g}
. Hence

\|g_{\varepsilon}-g\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}=\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\ast(I-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}g-(I-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}\to 0,\quad\text{as $\varepsilon\to 0$}.

Now let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain.
Take an open covering
\{U_{\nu}\}_{\nu=0}^{M}
of
\Omega
such that

U_{0}\subset\subset\Omega,\quad b\Omega\subseteq\bigcup_{\nu=1}^{M}U_{\nu},\quad U_{\nu}\cap\Omega=U_{\nu}\cap\Phi_{\nu}(\omega_{\nu}),\quad\nu=1,\dots,M.

Here each
\omega_{\nu}
is special Lipschitz domain of the form
\omega_{\nu}=\{x_{2n}>\rho_{\nu}(x^{\prime})\}
, and
\Phi_{\nu}
,
1\leq\nu\leq M
are invertible affine linear transformations.

Let
(\chi_{\nu})_{\nu=0}^{M}
be a partition of unity associated with
\{U_{\nu}\}
, i.e.
\chi_{\nu}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U_{\nu})
and
\sum_{\nu=0}^{M}\chi_{\nu}=1
. We have the property
\omega_{\nu}+\mathbb{K}=\omega_{\nu}
, where
\mathbb{K}:=\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:x_{2n}>|x^{\prime}|\}
. We denote
K_{\nu}=\{\nabla\Phi_{\nu}\cdot v:v\in\mathbb{K}\}
so that
\Phi_{\nu}(\omega_{\nu})+K_{\nu}=\Phi_{\nu}(\omega_{\nu})
.

Take
\psi_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B}^{2n})
with
\psi_{0}\geq 0
and
\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\psi_{0}=1
. For
1\leq\nu\leq{2n}
, take
\psi_{\nu}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(-K_{\nu})
with
\psi_{\nu}\geq 0
and
\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\psi_{\nu}=1
.
Let
\psi_{\nu,\varepsilon}(x):=\varepsilon^{-{2n}}\psi_{\nu}(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})
.
For
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, the convolutions

(\chi_{\nu}f)\ast\psi_{\nu,\varepsilon}(z)=\int_{-K_{\nu}}(\chi_{\nu}f)(z-\varepsilon\zeta)\psi_{\nu}(\zeta)\,dV(\zeta),

is defined for
z\in\Omega
.

Let
\widetilde{f}
be an extension of
f
and set

\widetilde{f}_{\varepsilon}:=\sum_{\nu=0}^{M}(\chi_{\nu}\widetilde{f})\ast\psi_{\nu,\varepsilon}.

Clearly
\widetilde{f}_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. By
[
Tri83
, Theorem 2.8.2(ii)]
(recall from Proposition
2.9
that
H^{s,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{s}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
), we have
\chi_{\nu}\widetilde{f}\in H^{s,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
for all
\nu
.

Now by the first part of the proof applied to
g:=\chi_{\nu}\widetilde{f}
,
\nu=0,\dots,M
, we obtain

\|\widetilde{f}_{\varepsilon}-\sum_{\nu=0}^{M}\chi_{\nu}\widetilde{f}\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}=\|\widetilde{f}_{\varepsilon}-\widetilde{f}\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
By taking restriction
f_{\varepsilon}:=\widetilde{f}_{\varepsilon}|_{\Omega}
, we obtain
\|f_{\varepsilon}-f\|_{H^{s,p}(\Omega)}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Finally, to show that
\|\overline{\partial}f_{\varepsilon}-\overline{\partial}f\|_{H^{s,p}(\Omega)}\to 0
, it suffices to show that
\|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{f}_{\varepsilon}-\overline{\partial}\widetilde{f}\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}\to 0
since
\overline{\partial}\widetilde{f_{\varepsilon}}|_{\Omega}=\overline{\partial}f_{\varepsilon}
and
\overline{\partial}\widetilde{f}|_{\Omega}=\overline{\partial}f
. We have

(A.1)

\overline{\partial}\widetilde{f}_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{\nu=0}^{M}(\chi_{\nu}\overline{\partial}\widetilde{f})\ast\psi_{\nu,\varepsilon}+\sum_{\nu=0}^{M}(\overline{\partial}\chi_{\nu}\wedge\widetilde{f})\ast\psi_{\nu,\varepsilon}

By the first part of the proof applied to
g=\chi_{\nu}\overline{\partial}\widetilde{f}
and
\overline{\partial}\chi_{\nu}\wedge f
, the first sum in (
A.1
) converges to

\overline{\partial}\widetilde{f}
, while the sum of last two terms converges to
\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}(\overline{\partial}\chi_{\nu})\wedge\widetilde{f}=0
in
H^{s,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. This shows the claim
\|\overline{\partial}f_{\varepsilon}-\overline{\partial}f\|_{H^{s,p}(\Omega)}\to 0
and concludes the whole proof.
∎

Rational 2 2 -Functions are abelian
L. Felipe Müller

Abstract
. We show that the coefficients of rational 2 2 -functions are contained in an abelian number field. More precisely, we show that the poles of such functions are poles of order one and given by roots of unity and rational residue.

Primary: 81Q60, 30C10; Secondary: 33B30.

1.
Introduction

Fermat’s and Euler’s congruences are well-known in number theory and are rich of remarkable consequences.
In the following we will give a short survey of these congruences.
We start with the famous

Theorem 1.1

(Euler)
.

The congruence

(1.1)

\displaystyle a^{p^{r}}\equiv a^{p^{r-1}}\mod p^{r}

holds for all integers
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, all primes
p
, and all natural numbers
r\in\mathbb{N}
.

A sequence
(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of rational numbers is called an
Euler sequence
(or
Gauss sequence
as in
[
4
]
) for the prime
p
, if
a_{k}
is a
p
-adic integer for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

(1.2)

\displaystyle a_{mp^{r}}\equiv a_{mp^{r-1}}\mod p^{r}

for all integers
r\geq 1
and
m\geq 1
.
A survey of these congruences has been given in
[
14
]
and
[
19
]
.

Beukers coined the term
supercongruence
:
A supercongruence (with respect to a prime
p
) refers to a sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}_{p}
that satisfies congruences of the type

(1.3)

\displaystyle a_{mp^{r}}\equiv a_{mp^{r-1}}\mod p^{sr},

for all
m,r\in\mathbb{N}
and a fixed
s\in\mathbb{N}
,
s>1
(cf.
[
6
]
).
Such supercongruences are given by the Jacobsthal-Kazandzidis congruence (cf.
[
5
]
), Apéry numbers (cf.
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
), generalized Domb numbers (cf.
[
15
]
)
and Almkvist-Zudilin numbers (cf.
[
1
]
,
[
8
]
)
to name a few.
Note that all the above mentioned supercongruences are valid for
s=3
with respect to
p\geq 5
.

Let
K
be an algebraic number field and
\mathcal{O}
its ring of algebraic integers.
We consider a generalization of supercongruences to sequences of algebraic integers in
K
.
More precisely, for
s\in\mathbb{N}
, an
s
-sequence is a sequence
(a_{n})\in K^{\mathbb{N}}
, such that for any unramified prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{O}
lying above the prime
p\in\mathbb{Z}
,
a_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and for all
m,r\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\frob_{\mathfrak{p}}(a_{p^{r-1}m})-a_{p^{r}m}\equiv 0\mod\mathfrak{p}^{sr}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}},

where
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the ring of
\mathfrak{p}
-adic integers and
\frob_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the canonical lift of the standard Frobenius element of
\mathfrak{p}
in the Galois group of the local field extension
(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p})|(\mathbb{Z}/p)
.
The generating function
V(z)
of an
s
-sequence then integrates to what is referred to as an
s
-function in
[
16
]
.
More precisely, the
s
-sequence
a\in K^{\mathbb{N}}
corresponds to the
s
-function
\smallint\hskip-2.5pt^{s}V(z)
(see
Proposition

2.1
in Section 2) given by the (formal) power series

\displaystyle\smallint\hskip-2.5pt^{s}V(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{s}}z^{n}\in zK\llbracket z\rrbracket,

Interestingly,
2
-functions (where
s=2
) have their geometric origin in super symmetry.
As stated in
[
16
]
, see Thm. 22 therein,
2
-functions appear as the non-singular part of the superpotential function (without the constant term) with algebraic coefficients.
In other words, algebraic cycles on Calabi-Yau three-folds provide a source of
2
-functions that are analytic and furthermore satisfy a differential equation with algebraic coefficients.
It is therefore expected that understanding the numerical interpretation of open Gromov-Witten/BPS theory relative to Lagrangian submanifolds mirror to algebraic cycles highly depend on delivering some (natural) basis of the class of
2
-functions with algebraic coefficients.
It is therefore of main interest to characterize a submodule of
s
-functions by suitable algebraic or analytic properties, and a class of distinguished generators for this submodule.
The contribution of the present work to this problem is to give a characterization of a
2
-function
\smallint^{2}V(z)
, where
V
represents a rational function.
We have

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
V\in zK\llbracket z\rrbracket
,
V(z)\neq 0
, be the generating function of a
2
-sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in K^{\mathbb{N}}
, representing the rational function
F(z)\in K(z)
as its Maclaurin expansion.
Then, there are rational coefficients
A_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
for
i=1,...,N
and an appropriate primitive
N
-th root of unity
\zeta
, such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle F(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{A_{i}\zeta^{i}z}{1-\zeta^{i}z}.

In particular, the coefficients
a_{n}
of
V(z)
have the form

(1.5)

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{i}\zeta^{in}.

The first reduction in the proof of
Theorem

1.2
is given by
Theorem

4.1
, a statement due to Minton (cf.
[
14
]
).
It states, that the generating functions of Euler sequences are given by sums of logarithmic derivatives of polynomials with integral coefficients.

Notation

Throughout this paper, the natural numbers will be meant to be the set of all positive integers,
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,...\}
, while
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
If

is a set, then

denotes the set of all sequences indexed by the natural numbers,
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in X^{\mathbb{N}}

For a ring

denote the ring of formal power series in the variable

4.
A Theorem by Minton

The next
Theorem

4.1
is a modified version of Theorem 7.1 in
[
4
]
, which on the other hand is a re-proven statement from
[
14
]
.
It is the starting point for the proof of
Theorem

1.2
.
The crucial point is, that a rational 1-function only admits poles of order 1.
We give a proof for the sake of completeness.
In the course of this, we follow the ideas given in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 4.1

(compare with
[
4
]
,
[
14
]
)
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rat}}^{1}(K|\mathbb{Q})
representing the rational function
F(z)\in K(z)
as its Maclaurin expansion.
Then there is a integer
r\in\mathbb{N}
, distinct algebraic numbers
\alpha_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
, and
A_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, for
i=1,...,r
, such that
F
can be written as

\displaystyle F(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{A_{i}\alpha_{i}z}{1-\alpha_{i}z}.

Proof.

Let
F
be given by the fraction of
P,Q\in K[z]
,
Q\not\equiv 0
, i.e.
F=\frac{P}{Q}
.
We may assume that
Q(0)\neq 0
and
P(0)=0
.
By
[
4
, Prop. 3.5]
, we have
\mathrm{deg}(P)\leq\mathrm{deg}(Q)
.
By adding a constant
C\in K
to
F
it does not affect the
1
-function condition but we may assume
\deg(P)<\deg(Q)
.
Then, by the
Partial Fraction Decomposition

\widetilde{F}=\frac{P}{Q}+C
has the form

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{i}}\frac{A_{i,j}}{(1-\alpha_{i}z)^{j}},

where the
\alpha_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
,
i\in\{1,...,r\}
are distinct algebraic numbers,
m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
A_{i,j}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
for all
(i,j)\in\{1,...,r\}\times\{1,...,m_{i}\}
.
Now, let
p
be a sufficiently large prime, unramified in
K|\mathbb{Q}
, such that the following conditions are simultaneously satisfied:

(1)

\alpha_{i}
is a
p
-adic unit for all

,

(2)

\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}
is a
p
-adic unit for all
i,j\in\{1,...,r\}
,

, and

(3)

p>m_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,...,r\}
.

What we need to show is

for all
i\in\{1,...,r\}
.
We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\alpha_{i}z)^{j}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{k+j-1}{j-1}\alpha_{i}^{k}z^{k}.

Therefore, if
\widetilde{V}(z)=V(z)+C
is the Maclaurin series expansion of
\widetilde{F}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{p}\widetilde{V}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{i}}A_{i,j}\binom{pk+j-1}{j-1}\alpha_{i}^{pk}\right]z^{k}.

Since

, we find
\binom{pk+\nu}{\nu}\equiv 1\mod p

(in particular,

) by the following calculation

\displaystyle\binom{pk+\nu}{\nu}=\prod_{\ell=1}^{\nu}\left(1+\frac{pk}{\ell}\right)\equiv 1\mod p.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{p}\widetilde{V}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{i}}A_{i,j}\alpha^{pk}\right]z^{k}\mod p

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{i}}\frac{A_{i,j}}{1-\alpha_{i}^{p}z}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{A_{i}}{1-\alpha_{i}^{p}z},

where
A_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{i}}A_{i,j}
.
Hence,
\mathscr{C}_{p}\widetilde{V}
represents a rational function with exclusively simple poles modulo
p
.
Thus, the
1
-function property
\mathscr{C}_{p}\widetilde{V}-\widetilde{V}\equiv 0\mod p\mathcal{O}_{p}\llbracket z\rrbracket
ensures that

has only simple poles as well.
Therefore, we write from now on

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{A_{i}}{1-\alpha_{i}z},

where
A_{i},\alpha_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
and
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}
for
i\neq j
.
Evaluating
\tilde{F}
at

C=\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle F=\widetilde{F}-C=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{A_{i}}{1-\alpha_{i}z}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{A_{i}\alpha_{i}z}{1-\alpha_{i}z}.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n}\qquad\text{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

The local
1
-function property for

\displaystyle 0\equiv\mathrm{Frob}_{p}(a_{m})-a_{mp}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\mathrm{Frob}_{p}(A_{i})\mathrm{Frob}_{p}(\alpha_{i}^{m})-A_{i}\alpha_{i}^{mp}\right)\mod p\mathcal{O}_{p},

for all

.
Since
\mathrm{Frob}_{p}
is given by taking component-wise the

\mathfrak{p}
for all

\displaystyle 0\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(A_{i}^{p}-A_{i}\right)\alpha_{i}^{mp}\mod p\mathcal{O}_{p},

for all

The Vandermonde type matrix

\displaystyle M=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1}^{p}&\alpha_{1}^{2p}&\dots&\alpha_{1}^{rp}\\
\alpha_{2}^{p}&\alpha_{2}^{2p}&\dots&\alpha_{2}^{rp}\\

\displaystyle\det(M)

\displaystyle=\big(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}^{p}\big)\times\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\left(\alpha_{j}^{p}-\alpha_{i}^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\big(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}^{p}\big)\times\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\left(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i}\right)^{p}\mod p\mathcal{O}_{p}.

Hence,

Proposition 6.4

\displaystyle x^{p^{n}}

\displaystyle=\left(x^{p^{n-1}}\right)^{p}=\left(x^{p^{n}}-p^{n}\rho_{n}\right)^{p}=\sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{p}{k}x^{kp^{n}}(-1)^{p-k}p^{n(p-k)}\rho_{n}^{p-k}

\displaystyle p^{n+1}\rho_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\binom{p-1}{k}x^{kp^{n}}\frac{(-1)^{p-k+1}}{p-k}\rho_{n}^{p-k}p^{n(p-k-1)}-(-1)^{p}\rho_{n}^{p}p^{n(p-1)-1}.

\displaystyle\rho_{n+1}

\displaystyle\equiv x^{(p-1)p^{n}}\rho_{n}\mod p^{n+2\kappa_{n}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

.
We therefore write

.
Furthermore, using

\displaystyle\rho_{n+1}

\displaystyle\equiv x^{(p-1)p^{n}}\rho_{n}\mod p^{n+2\kappa}\mathbb{Z}_{p}

\displaystyle=\left(1+p^{n+1}x^{-p^{n}}\rho_{n+1}\right)\rho_{n}

Proposition 6.5

Let

\kappa(m)=\infty

\displaystyle x^{-(m-1)p^{n-1}}\frac{\rho_{n}(m)}{\rho_{n}(1)}

Theorem 6.6

Suppose the validity of the following family of congruences

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}B_{i}\left(x_{i}^{mp^{n}}-x_{i}^{mp^{n-1}}\right)\equiv 0\mod p^{2n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\quad\text{for all $m,n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Suppose there is a

In the following, we will write

\displaystyle\rho_{i,n}(m):=\frac{1}{p^{n}}\left(x_{i}^{mp^{n}}-x_{i}^{mp^{n-1}}\right),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sigma_{n}(m):=\frac{1}{p^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}B_{i}\rho_{i,n}(m)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}B_{i}\rho_{i,n}(m)\equiv 0\mod p^{n+2\kappa}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\qquad\text{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

\vdots\\
\rho_{r,1}(1)\end{pmatrix}\equiv 0\mod p^{1+2\kappa}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}.

The determinant of the above Vandermonde matrix is given by

\displaystyle\det\begin{pmatrix}B_{1}&B_{2}&\cdots&B_{r}\\
B_{1}x_{1}&B_{2}x_{2}&\cdots&B_{r}x_{r}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
B_{1}x_{1}^{r-1}&B_{2}x_{2}^{r-1}&\cdots&B_{r}x_{r}^{r-1}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\big(\prod_{i=1}^{r}B_{i}\big)\times\prod_{1\leq k<\ell\leq r}(x_{k}-x_{\ell})

\displaystyle\not\equiv 0\mod p\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Hence, the determinant is invertible mod
p
, since
x_{k}-x_{\ell}
is a
p
-adic unit for all
k\neq\ell
.
Consequently,
(\rho_{i,1}(1))_{i=1,...,r}\equiv 0\mod p^{1+2\kappa}\mathbb{Z}^{r}_{p}
.
In other words,
\kappa\geq 1+2\kappa
, which is the desired contradiction.
We conclude that all
x_{1},...,x_{r}
are roots of unity in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rat}}^{2}(K|\mathbb{Q})
,
V(z)\neq 0
, be the generating series of the underlying
2
-sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=([V(z)]_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, representing a rational function
F\in K(z)
.
Then there is an integer
N\in\mathbb{N}
and coefficients
A_{i}\in\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}\left[D^{-1}\right]
for
i=1,...,N
such that

\displaystyle F(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{A_{i}\zeta^{i}z}{1-\zeta^{i}z},

where
\zeta
is a appropriate primitive
N
-th root of unity.

Proof.

By
Theorem

4.1
the coefficients
a_{n}=[V(z)]_{n}
of
V
are given by the power sums
a_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}\alpha_{i}^{n}
, for fixed
r\in\mathbb{N}
, where
A_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
and where the
\alpha_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
are distinct algebraic numbers.
As mentioned at the beginning of this section, we may assume
\alpha_{i}\in K
for all
i
.
Now, choose a prime
p\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(1)

p
is unramified in
K|\mathbb{Q}
and splits completely,

(2)

\alpha_{i}
,
A_{i}
and
\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{\ell}
are
p
-adic units for all
i=1,...,r
and
k\neq\ell
,

(3)

\max\{r,2\}<p
.

This choice of
p
is possible by
Theorem

6.2
.
Therefore, we have
K_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}
dividing
(p)
.
Hence,
\mathcal{O}_{p}
may be identified with
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|(p)}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Since
\mathrm{Fr}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathrm{id}_{K}
, the local 2-function condition for
p
then reads

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}\left(\alpha_{i}^{mp^{n}}-\alpha_{i}^{mp^{n-1}}\right)\equiv 0\mod p^{2n}\mathcal{O}_{p},

for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
B_{i}=\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}(A_{i})
and
x_{i}=\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha_{i})
for
\mathfrak{p}\mid(p)
,
Theorem

6.6
states that
\alpha_{i}
are all roots of unity.
In particular, the coefficients lie in a Galois subfield of
K
, which is abelian over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Choose an appropriate primitive
N
-th root of unity
\zeta
and a bijection
\nu\colon\{1,...,N\}\rightarrow\{1,...,N\}
, such that
\alpha_{i}=\zeta^{\nu(i)}
for all
i\in\{1,..,N\}
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume
A_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
(zeros are allowed) and

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{i}\zeta^{in}.

We observe that the coefficients
a_{n}
are in
\mathcal{O}\left[D^{-1}\right]\cap\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
.
By assumption, we have

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\zeta&\zeta^{2}&\cdots&\zeta^{N-1}&1\\
\zeta^{2}&\zeta^{4}&\cdots&\zeta^{2(N-1)}&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\zeta^{N-1}&\zeta^{(N-1)2}&\cdots&\zeta^{(N-1)^{2}}&1\\
1&1&\cdots&1&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}\\
A_{2}\\
\vdots\\
A_{N-1}\\
A_{N}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{O}\left[D^{-1}\right]^{N}.

The above matrix is invertible in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
with inverse

\displaystyle\left(\zeta^{ij}\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,...,N\\
j=1,...,N\end{subarray}}^{-1}=\frac{1}{N}\cdot\left(\zeta^{-ij}\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,...,N\\
j=1,...,N\end{subarray}}.

Therefore,
A_{i}\in\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{O}\left[D^{-1},\zeta\right]\cap\mathbb{Q}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}\left[D^{-1}\right]
for all
i=1,...,N
.
∎

An obvious consequence of
Corollary

6.7
is that the coefficients of a given
V
are
periodic
(as defined below) with the periodicity being a positive integer
P_{V}
dividing the number
N
.
What remains to show is the simple fact, that a minimal such
N
is given by
P_{V}
.
This is the statement of
Proposition

6.9
.

Definition 6.8

(Periodicity)
.

Let
V\in\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{rat}}^{2}(K|\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{fin}}\setminus\{0\}
.
The
periodicity

P_{V}
of
V
is given by the periodicity of the coefficients of its Maclaurin series.
More precisely,
P_{V}\in\mathbb{N}
is given by

\displaystyle P_{V}=\min\{N\in\mathbb{N}\,|\,[V(z)]_{n}=[V(z)]_{n+N}\text{ for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$}\}.

Note, that the existence of
P_{V}
is ensured by
Corollary

6.7
.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\pi\colon\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\mathrm{rat}}(K|\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{fin}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}

denotes the map given by
\pi(V)=P_{V}
, if
V\neq 0
, and
\pi(0)=0
.
For
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we denote by
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{N}
(
\mathcal{S}_{N}
, resp.) the the preimage of
N
under
\pi
(the intersection of the preimage of
N
under
\pi
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rat}}^{2}(K|\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{fin}}
, resp.), i.e.

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{N}:=\pi^{-1}(N)\subset\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{rat}}^{2}(K|\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{fin}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{S}_{N}:=\pi^{-1}(N)\cap\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{rat}}^{2}(K|\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{fin}}.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
V\in\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{rat}}^{2}(K|\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{fin}}\setminus\{0\}
and let
P_{V}=\pi(V)
be the periodicity of
V
, i.e.
V\in\overline{S}_{N}
.
Then
[V(z)]_{n}\in K\cap\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{P_{V}})
and, for an appropriate
P_{V}
-th primitive root of unity
\zeta_{P_{V}}
, there are
A_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}
,
1\leq j\leq P_{V}
, such that

\displaystyle A_{1}\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad V(z)=\frac{z}{1-z^{P_{V}}}\sum_{i=0}^{P_{V}-1}a_{i+1}z^{i},\quad\text{and}\quad a_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{P_{V}}A_{j}\zeta^{ij}.

Furthermore, the map
\pi\colon\mathcal{S}^{2}_{\mathrm{rat}}(K|\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}
is surjective.

Proof.

By
Theorem

1.2
, there is an
N\in\mathbb{N}
and a primitive
N
-th root of unity and suitable coefficients
A_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq N
such that

(6.9)

\displaystyle V(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{A_{i}\zeta^{i}z}{1-\zeta^{i}z}.

From this representation of
V
, one immediately sees
P_{V}\leq N
.
Let
\hat{N}
denote the minimum of the set of all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
V
permits a representation given by
eq.

6.9
.
In particular,
\hat{N}\leq P_{V}
.
Assume that all
A_{i}
(which depend on
\hat{N}
) with
\gcd(i,\hat{N})=1
are vanishing.
In that case,
V
has an representation given by
eq.

6.9
with
N\leq\hat{N}
, violating the minimality of
\hat{N}
.
Therefore, we may assume, that at least one
A_{i}
does not vanish for
\gcd(i,\hat{N})=1
.
This implies by the representation given in
eq.

6.9
, that
V
has a pole of order
1
at an
\hat{N}
-th primitive root of unity, say
\zeta_{\hat{N}}
.
Therefore, we may assume
A_{1}\neq 0
.
Since
V
has periodicity
P_{V}
, we can write

\displaystyle V(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{P_{V}}a_{i}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{i+P_{V}k}=\sum_{i=1}^{P_{V}}a_{i}z^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{P_{V}k}=\frac{z}{1-z^{P_{V}}}\sum_{i=0}^{P_{V}-1}a_{i+1}z^{i}.

This shows, that all singularities of
V
are roots of the polynomial
1-z^{P_{V}}
.
Therefore,
\zeta_{\hat{N}}
is a
P_{V}
-th root of unity and hence,
\hat{N}\leq P_{V}
.
We conclude
\hat{N}=P_{V}
.
For the surjectivity of
\pi
recall the map
\varepsilon^{(2)}_{k}\colon\mathcal{S}^{2}(K|\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{2}(K|\mathbb{Q})
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
from
eq.

5.1
.
Now, take
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
V(z)=\frac{z}{1-z}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}z^{k}\in\mathcal{S}^{2}{\mathrm{rat}}(K|\mathbb{Q})
.
Then, in particular,
\varepsilon^{(2)}_{N}V\in\mathcal{S}^{2}_{\mathrm{rat}}(K|\mathbb{Q})
, and

\displaystyle\pi\left(\varepsilon_{N}^{(2)}\left(V(z)\right)\right)=\pi\left(\frac{N^{2}z^{N}}{1-z^{N}}\right)=N.

Hence,
\pi
is surjective.
∎

Theorem

6.1
now follows from
Corollary

6.7
and
Proposition

6.9
.

Cross-Domain AI for Early Attack Detection and Defense Against Malicious Flows in O-RAN
Bruno Missi Xavier 2 3 , Merim Dzaferagic 3 , Irene Vilà 3 4 , Magnos Martinello 2 , and Marco Ruffini 3

Abstract
In the fight against cyber attacks, ns ( ns ) is a flexible and adaptable shield, using advanced software to spot malicious activity in regular network traffic. However, the availability of comprehensive datasets for mobile networks, which are fundamental for the development of ml ( ml ) solutions for attack detection near their source, is still limited. Cross-Domain ai ( ai ) can be the key to address this, although its application in o-ran ( o-ran ) is still at its infancy. To address these challenges, we deployed an end-to-end o-ran network, that was used to collect data from the ran and the transport network. These datasets allow us to combine the knowledge from an in-network ml traffic classifier for attack detection to bolster the training of an ml -based traffic classifier specifically tailored for the ran . Our results demonstrate the potential of the proposed approach, achieving an accuracy rate of 93%. This approach not only bridges critical gaps in mobile network security but also showcases the potential of cross-domain ai in enhancing the efficacy of network security measures.

In terms of traffic classification, a fundamental

The features are derived either intrinsically from data, such as headers and payloads, or extrinsically, involving factors such as packet arrival time. The selection of the appropriate granularity and features is pivotal and varies significantly depending on the specific application at hand. This decision-making process is widely explored in

To assess the accuracy of the proposed Cross-Domain

in Table

IV
Conclusion and Future Work

This paper has presented a Cross-Domain
ai
training methodology for
ml
traffic classification solutions for attack detection and mitigation, encompassing the
ran
and the transport network domains. Specifically, the proposed methodology leverages the knowledge of an in-network
ml
traffic classifier for
sdn
switches in the transport network to support the training of an ML traffic classifier for the
ran
, which can be deployed as an
xApp
in the
bs
.

The methodology has been assessed in a realistic experimental setup deployed in OpenIreland testbed facilities. Results showed that the
RAN Classifier
achieves lower accuracy than the
In-Network Classifier
. Despite this, the ability to identify the malicious flow near the source of the attack holds paramount importance. This proximity allows for the prevention of widespread network dissemination of attacks by enabling the timely interruption of the traffic, thus containing potential threats effectively. Likewise, the
Benchmark Classifier
presented higher accuracy than the
RAN Classifier
. However, as previously mentioned, the
Benchmark Classifier
has unrealistic assumptions, like access to real traffic labels. This makes it static and does not allow for new traffic classes to be introduced in a fully automated way. The approach taken with the
RAN Classifier
, on the other hand, is extremely useful for many network scenarios where datasets for proper training are not readily available.

The decoupling between our
ns
components (i.e.,
sdn
and
ran
virtualization) empowers us to extend its applicability. Future work could explore transitioning from the knowledge encapsulated within the transport network and leverage insights from other systems such as firewalls,
ids
(
ids
)), or statistical models. This flexibility highlights the adaptability and interconnectivity of our approach, enabling the use of diverse feedback mechanisms for comprehensive threat analysis.

Weight 2 2 CM newforms as p-adic limits
Robert Dicks

Abstract
. Previous works have shown that certain weight 2 2 newforms are p p -adic limits of weakly holomorphic modular forms under repeated application of the U U -operator. The proofs of these theorems originally relied on the theory of harmonic Maass forms. Ahlgren and Samart obtained strengthened versions of these results using the theory of holomorphic modular forms. Here, we use such techniques to express all weight 2 2 CM newforms which are eta quotients as p p -adic limits. In particular, we show that these forms are p p -adic limits of the derivatives of the Weierstrass mock modular forms associated to their elliptic curves.

2.
Background

For the next several paragraphs, we follow the exposition in
[
AS16
]
.
Suppose that
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and that
N
is a positive integer. For a function
f(z)
on the upper half plane and

\gamma=\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right)\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q}),

we have the weight
k
slash operator

f(z)\big|_{k}\gamma:=\det(\gamma)^{\frac{k}{2}}(cz+d)^{-k}f\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right).

We denote by
M^{!}_{k}(N)
the space of forms
f
which satisfy the transformation law

f\big|_{k}\gamma=f\ \ \ \text{ for }\ \ \ \gamma=\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(N)

and which are holomorphic on the upper half plane and meromorphic at the cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
.
We identify each
f\in M_{k}^{!}(N)
with its
q
-expansion.
That is, if
q:=\exp(2\pi iz)
, we can write
f(z)=\sum a(n)q^{n}
for some coefficients
a(n)
.
Let
M_{k}(N)\subseteq M_{k}^{!}(N)
be the subspace of forms which are holomorphic at the cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
.
Let
M_{k}^{\infty}(N)\subseteq M_{k}^{!}(N)
be the subspace of forms which vanish at all cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
other than
\infty
and
S_{k}(N)\subseteq M_{k}^{\infty}(N)
be the subspace of forms which vanish at all of the cusps.

We next recall the
U
and
V
operators. For a positive integer
m
, we define them on Fourier expansions by

\left(\sum a(n)q^{n}\right)\big|U_{m}:=\sum a(mn)q^{n},

\left(\sum a(n)q^{n}\right)\big|V_{m}:=\sum a(n)q^{mn}.

For a positive integer
m
, let
T_{k}(m)
be the usual Hecke operator on
M^{!}_{k}(N)
. If
p
is a prime with
p\nmid N
,
n\geq 1
is an integer, and
f\in M_{k}^{!}(N)
, then

(2.1)

F\big|T_{k}(p^{n})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}p^{(k-1)j}f\big|U(p^{n-j})\big|V(p^{j}).

Define

(2.2)

\Theta:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{d}{dz}=q\frac{d}{dq}.

We have the following result (see e.g.
[
AS16
, Lemma
2.1
]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

If
(m,N)=1
, then

(2.3)

T_{k}(m):M^{\infty}_{k}(N)\rightarrow M^{\infty}_{k}(N).

If
k\geq 2
, then

(2.4)

\Theta^{k-1}:M^{\infty}_{2-k}(N)\rightarrow M^{\infty}_{k}(N).

Finally, we review some facts about filtrations. If
p
is a prime such that
p\nmid 6N
and
k
is a nonnegative integer, let
M^{(p)}_{k}(N)
be the subset of forms in
M_{k}(N)
which have coefficients which are
p
-integral rational numbers.
if
f=\sum a(n)q^{n}\in M^{(p)}_{k}(N)
,
we define

\overline{f}:=\sum\overline{a(n)}q^{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[[q]],

and we define

\overline{M^{(p)}_{k}(N)}:=\{\overline{f}:\ \ f\in M^{(p)}_{k}(N)\}.

If

f\in M^{(p)}_{k}(N)
, then we define the filtration of
\overline{f}
as

w_{p}(\overline{f})=\inf\{k^{\prime}:\ \text{there exists }g\in M_{k^{\prime}}(N)\text{ with }\overline{f}=\overline{g}\}.

We make use of the following facts from
[
Joc82
,
\mathsection
7
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that
f\in M^{(p)}_{k}(N)
and that
w_{p}(\overline{f})\neq-\infty
. Then we have

(1)

w_{p}(\overline{f})\equiv k\pmod{p-1}
.

(2)

w_{p}\left(\overline{f\big|V(p)}\right)=pw_{p}(\overline{f}).

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
for
N_{E}=27

The proof of Theorem
1.1
for
N_{E}=27
requires proving some preliminary results. Recalling our notation
g_{27}=\displaystyle\sum a_{27}(n)q^{n}
and
G_{27}=\displaystyle\sum C_{27}(n)q^{n}
, we have the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

For every integer
m\geq-1
except for
m=0
, there exists a unique form
H_{m}\in M^{\infty}_{2}(27)\cap\mathbb{Z}((q))
of the form

H_{m}=q^{-m}+O(q^{2}).

Moreover, if
p\neq 3
is prime and
n
is a nonnegative integer, we have

G_{27}\big|T_{2}(p^{n})=p^{n}H_{p^{n}}+C_{27}(p^{n})g_{27}.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle L_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta^{4}(9z)}{\eta(3z)\eta^{3}(27z)}=q^{-2}+q+O(q^{4}),

\displaystyle L_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta^{3}(3z)}{\eta^{3}(27z)}=q^{-3}-3+O(q^{3}).

For integers
d\geq 0
, consider the forms

(3.1)

g_{27}(z)L_{1}^{d}(z)=q^{-2d+1}+O(q^{-2d+4}),

(3.2)

g_{27}(z)L^{d}_{1}(z)L_{2}(z)=q^{-2d}+O(q^{-2d+3}).

The fact that the forms
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are holomorphic at all of the cusps other than
\infty
(see e.g.
[
Ono04
, Thm
1.64
, Thm
1.65
]
) implies that the forms in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) are in
M^{\infty}_{2}(27)
. By taking linear combinations of these forms, we obtain the forms
H_{m}
.
Since the space
S_{2}(27)
is one-dimensional, the forms
H_{m}
are unique. Some examples of these forms are given below.

\displaystyle H_{-1}=g_{27}=q-2q^{4}+\cdots,

\displaystyle H_{1}=G_{27}=q^{-1}-q^{2}+\cdots,

\displaystyle H_{2}=q^{-2}-5q^{4}+\cdots.

To prove the last part of the proposition, note that

G_{27}\big|T_{2}(p^{n})=G_{27}\big|U(p^{n})+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}p^{j}G_{27}\big|U(p^{n-j})\big|V(p^{j})+p^{n}G_{27}\big|V(p^{n})

by (
2.1
). By (
2.3
), we have
G_{27}\big|T_{2}(p^{n})\in M_{2}^{\infty}(27)
. Since

G_{27}=q^{-1}-q^{2}+\cdots,

we have

G_{27}\big|U(p^{n})=C_{27}(p^{n})g_{27}+O(q^{2})

and

\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}p^{j}G_{27}\big|U(p^{n-j})\big|V(p^{j})+p^{n}G_{27}\big|V(p^{n})=p^{n}q^{-p^{n}}+O(q^{2}).

This implies that

G_{27}\big|T_{2}(p^{n})-p^{n}H_{p^{n}{}}\in S_{2}(27).

Since
S_{2}(27)
is one-dimensional, we have
G_{27}\big|T_{2}(p^{n})-p^{n}H_{p^{n}}=C_{27}(p^{n})g_{27}
.
∎

Before we prove Theorem
1.1
for
N_{E}=27
, we require the following congruence.

Lemma 3.2
.

For each prime
p
which is inert in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
and each integer
m\geq 0
, we have the congruence

C_{27}(p^{2m+1})\equiv(-1)^{m}p^{m}C_{27}(p)\pmod{p^{m+1}}.

Proof.

By definition,
L_{1}
has an expansion of the form

L_{1}(z)=\sum b(n)q^{3n+1}\in\mathbb{Z}((q)),

and
L_{2}
has an expansion of the form

L_{2}(z)=\sum c(n)q^{3n}\in\mathbb{Z}((q)).

Suppose that
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
.
By taking polynomials in
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
with integer coefficients, we can construct a modular function
\psi_{p}\in M^{\!}_{0}(27)
which is holomorphic at all of the cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(27)
other than
\infty
of the form

(3.3)

\psi_{p}(z)=q^{-p}+C_{p}q+O(q^{4}).

Since
G_{27}\in M^{\infty}_{2}(27)
, we see that the meromorphic differential
G_{27}(z)\psi_{p}(z)dz
on the modular curve
X_{0}(27)
is holomorphic at all of the cusps of
X_{0}(27)
other than
\infty
. The sum of the residues of a meromorphic differential is zero, and the residue at
\infty
is a multiple of the constant term. Since the constant term of
G(z)\psi_{p}(z)
is
C_{p}+C_{27}(p)
, we have
C_{p}=-C_{27}(p)
.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have

(3.4)

\Theta(\psi_{p})=-pq^{-p}-C_{27}(p)q+O(q^{4})\in M^{\infty}_{2}(27).

By Proposition
3.1
, we also have

-G_{27}\big|T_{2}(p)=-pq^{-p}-C_{27}(p)q+O(q^{4}).

Thus,

(3.5)

G_{27}\big|T_{2}(p)=-\Theta(\psi_{p}),

which means that

(3.6)

G_{27}\big|U(p)=-\Theta(\psi_{p})-pG_{27}\big|V(p).

By applying
U(p^{2})
to both sides of (
3.6
), an induction argument implies for each
m\geq 0
that

G_{27}\big|U(p^{2m+1})=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k+1}p^{m-k}\Theta(\psi_{p})\big|U(p^{2k})+(-1)^{m+1}p^{m+1}G_{27}\big|V(p).

The fact that
\Theta(\psi_{p})\big|U(p^{2k})\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2k}}
implies for each integer
m\geq 0
that

(3.7)

G_{27}\big|U(p^{2m+1})\equiv(-1)^{m+1}p^{m}\Theta(\psi_{p})\pmod{p^{m+1}}.

The result follows by comparing the coefficients of
q
in (
3.7
) using (
3.4
).
∎

The next lemma helps us to establish (
1.2
).

Lemma 3.3
.

For each prime
p
which is inert in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
, we have
p\nmid C_{27}(p)
.

Proof.

Assume for the sake of a contradiction that
p\mid C_{27}(p)
. From Proposition
3.1
and (
3.5
), we see that

\Theta(\psi_{p})=-G_{27}\big|T_{2}(p)=-pH_{p}-C_{27}(p)g_{27}\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

This implies that
\psi_{p}
is of the form

\psi_{p}\equiv q^{-p}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A(pn)q^{pn}\pmod{p}

for some integral coefficients
A(pn)
.
A computation in Magma of an integral basis for
M_{2}(27)
gives

(3.8)

f_{1}=q+\cdots,\ \ f_{2}=q^{2}+\cdots,\ \ f_{3}=q^{3}+\cdots,\ \ f_{4}=q^{4}+\cdots,\ \ f_{5}=q^{6}+\cdots.

Set

h_{p}=\psi_{p}f_{5}^{p}\in M_{2p}(27).

For some integral coefficients
D(pn)
, we have

h_{p}\equiv\sum_{n=5}^{\infty}D(pn)q^{pn}\equiv q^{5p}+\cdots\pmod{p},

which means that

h_{p}\equiv h_{p}\big|U(p)\big|V(p)\pmod{p}.

Since

h_{p}\big|U(p)\equiv h_{p}\big|T(p)\pmod{p},

we have
\overline{h_{p}\big|U(p)}\in\overline{M^{(p)}_{2}(27)}
.
By
(2)
of Proposition
2.2
, we have

w_{p}(\overline{h_{p}})=pw_{p}(\overline{h_{p}\big|U(p)})

and
w_{p}(\overline{h_{p}})\equiv 2p\pmod{p-1}
. The fact that
p\mid w_{p}(\overline{h_{p}})
implies that
w_{p}(\overline{h_{p}})=2p
, so we have
w_{p}(\overline{h_{p}\big|U(p)})=2
. Thus, there exists a form
h_{0}\in M_{2}^{(p)}(27)
with

h_{0}\equiv h_{p}\big|U(p)\equiv q^{5}+O(q^{6})\pmod{p}.

However, an examination of the above basis for
M_{2}(27)
shows that no such form
h_{0}
exists. The result follows.
∎

for

Lemmas

and

imply (

Proofs of Theorem
1.1
for

N_{E}=144

Theorem
1.1
for
N_{E}=64
and
N_{E}=144
follow from
the cases
N_{E}=32
and
N_{E}=36
.
In particular,if
\chi_{8}
and
\chi_{12}
are the Kronecker characters with discriminants
8
and
12
, we have

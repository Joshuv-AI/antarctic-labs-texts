Abstract
This paper studies k k -claw-free graphs, exploring the connection between an extremal combinatorics question and the power of a convex program in approximating the maximum-weight independent set in this graph class. For the extremal question, we consider the notion, that we call conditional χ \chi -boundedness of a graph: Given a graph G G that is assumed to contain an independent set of a certain (constant) size, we are interested in upper bounding the chromatic number in terms of the clique number of G G . This question, besides being interesting on its own, has algorithmic implications (which have been relatively neglected in the literature) on the performance of SDP relaxations in estimating the value of maximum-weight independent set. For k = 3 k=3 , Chudnovsky and Seymour (JCTB 2010) prove that any 3 3 -claw-free graph G G with an independent set of size three must satisfy χ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 2 ​ ω ​ ( G ) \chi(G)\leq 2\omega(G) . Their result implies a factor 2 2 -estimation algorithm for the maximum weight independent set via an SDP relaxation (providing the first non-trivial result for maximum-weight independent set in such graphs via a convex relaxation). An obvious open question is whether a similar conditional χ \chi -boundedness phenomenon holds for any k k -claw-free graph. Our main result answers this question negatively. We further present some evidence that our construction could be useful in studying more broadly the power of convex relaxations in the context of approximating maximum weight independent set in k k -claw free graphs. In particular, we prove a lower bound on families of convex programs that are stronger than known convex relaxations used algorithmically in this context.

For

This paper focuses on an extremal question in

Most prior works that extend perfect graphs rely on the notion of the clique constrained stable set polytope (QSTAB)---a convex relaxation that can be optimized via semi-definite programs.

Overview of Techniques:

We give a high-level overview for the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
For simplicity, let us focus on the case of
k=5
and a slightly weaker bound. The first component of our construction is a Ramsey graph. It is known that there exists an
n
-vertex graph
H
such that
\alpha(H)=2
and there is no clique of size
c\sqrt{n\log n}
for some constant
c

[
18
]
. Therefore this graph is
3
-claw-free (since there is no independent set of size
3
) and
\chi(G)\geq n/\alpha(G)=n/2\geq\widetilde{\Omega}(\omega(G)^{2})
.
So, this graph has almost all our desired properties except that
\alpha(H)
is very small while we need large independent sets. We will “compose” several copies of
H
together to obtain a final graph
G
, ensuring that (i)
\alpha(G)
can be made arbitrarily large, (ii)
\omega(G)
is roughly the same as
\omega(H)
, and (iii) the graph remains
5
-claw-free.

The key component of our composition step is a special graph operation that we call
bi-conflict composition
.
In particular, given any two graphs
H_{1},H_{2}
having
n
vertices each, the graph
D=bcc(H_{1},H_{2})
is obtained as follows. First, construct a graph
D^{\prime}
by connecting
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
by an arbitrary matching
M\colon|M|=n
. Next, define

where
V(D)=M
and for each

, we have
(e,e^{\prime})\in E(D)
if and only if they are not induced matching edges in
D^{\prime}
.
There are two simple properties of graph

that are crucial to our analysis.

•

(P1) the maximum claw in

is at most
\min\{\alpha(H_{1}),\alpha(H_{2})\}
.

•

(P2) the maximum clique in
D
is
\omega(D)\leq\omega(H_{1})+\omega(H_{2})+1
.

The bi-conflict composition will be used as an analytical tool in our construction. Take
q

H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{q}
on
n
vertices. For each

to

by a matching

. Call this graph

. Our final graph

has vertices

and the edges are defined such that
(e,e^{\prime})\in E(G)
if

and
e^{\prime}
form an induced matching.
Notice that the structure of graph
G
for vertices that correspond to

-edges is roughly the same as

Notice that
\alpha(G)=\Omega(q)
(Pick one matching edge from each

when

(therefore

is
5
-claw-free) and property (P2) guarantees that the value of maximum clique is at most

.
By choosing

We remark that this construction gives a non-trivial lower bound (albeit weaker than Theorem
1.3
) for

. To make the result work for

1.2
Conclusion and open problems

Our main contribution is to initiate the study of convex relaxation aspects of independent set in
k
-claw-free graphs. The main open question is whether there is any convex relaxation approach that gives a reasonable approximation guarantee for MWIS in
k
-claw-free graphs (for
k\geq 4
).
Conceptually, we made explicit the implication of Chudnovsky and Seymour’s theorem (which can be seen as conditional
\chi
-boundedness), that SoS gives a reasonable approximation guarantee for claw-free graphs.
We refute the possibility of generalizing such a result to
k
-claw-free graphs for all
k\geq 4
and present evidence that this graph family might be a bad instance for SoS.

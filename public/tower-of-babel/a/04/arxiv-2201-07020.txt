Effect of electron spin polarization in laser-assisted electron-proton scattering
I. Dahiri, M. Baouahi, M. Jakha, S. Mouslih B. Manaut, S. Taj

Abstract
This work aims to study theoretically the electron-proton scattering for initially spin-polarized electrons in the presence of a circularly polarized electromagnetic field. Using the first Born approximation and the Dirac-Volkov states for dressed electrons, we derive an analytic expression for the differential cross-section with the help of the spin-helicity formalism. Meanwhile, a well-known concept of spin-flip and spin non-flip differential cross sections is applied. The influence of the electromagnetic field on the degree of polarization of the scattered electron is investigated, and the effect of electron spin polarization is examined. We found that the initial spin of the electron can be affected depending on its kinetic energy during the scattering process. This work is a continuation of a recent paper in which we studied the unpolarized electron-proton scattering in the presence of a laser field (Dahiri, et al., Laser Phys. Lett. 18 (2021) 096001).

2.1.
Laser-free unpolarized differential cross section

The elastic scattering of a proton by electron impact, shown in Fig.
1
, is studied by considering the proton as a structureless Dirac particle.

Figure 1:
Electron-proton scattering in the laboratory system, with
A^{\mu}
is the four-potential produced by the proton.

In Fig.
1
, the
p_{n}
(for
n=\{1,3\}
) represent the 4-momentum of the incident (
p_{1}
) and scattered (
p_{3}
) electrons, while
p_{m}
(for
m=\{2,4\}
) represent the 4-momentum of the proton in its initial (
p_{2}
) and final (
p_{4}
) states. In the absence of the laser field, the particles studied are described by Dirac wave functions, normalized by the quantum volume
V
, as follows:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{p_{n}}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{n}V}}u(p_{n},s_{n})e^{-ip_{n}.x},\\
\\
~~\psi_{p_{m}}(y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{m}V}}u(p_{m},s_{m})e^{-ip_{m}.y},\end{array}\right.

E_{n,m}
and
s_{n,m}
represent the energy and the spin of the particles respectively. At the leading order, the matrix element
S_{fi}
representing our scattering process is:

S_{fi}=-i\int d^{4}x~\overline{\psi}_{p_{3}}(x)\not{A}(x)\psi_{p_{1}}(x),

(5)

with
\not{A}(x)=A_{\mu}(x)\gamma^{\mu}
, where
A_{\mu}
is the electromagnetic 4-potential of the scattering given by:

\displaystyle A_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int d^{4}y~D_{F}(x-y)\bar{\psi}_{p_{4}}(y)\gamma_{\mu}\psi_{p_{2}}(y),

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}y~\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{e^{-iq(x-y)}}{q^{2}+i\epsilon}\big[\bar{\psi}_{p_{4}}(y)\gamma_{\mu}\psi_{p_{2}}(y)\big],

such as the element
D_{F}(x-y)=-\int d^{4}qe^{-iq(x-y)}/\big[(2\pi)^{4}(q^{2}+i\epsilon)\big]
is the Feynman electromagnetic propagator. By replacing Eqs. (
2.1.
) and (
6
) in the expression of
S_{fi}
(
5
), the matrix element becomes:

\displaystyle S_{fi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-i}{\sqrt{16E_{1}E_{2}E_{3}E_{4}V^{4}}}\int d^{4}x~d^{4}y~\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{e^{i(p_{3}-p_{1}-q).x}e^{i(p_{4}-p_{2}+q).y}}{q^{2}+i\epsilon}\mathcal{M}_{fi},

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-i}{\sqrt{16E_{1}E_{2}E_{3}E_{4}V^{4}}}\frac{(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(p_{4}-p_{2}+p_{3}-p_{1})}{(p_{3}-p_{1})^{2}+i\epsilon}\mathcal{M}_{fi},

where the element
\mathcal{M}_{fi}
represents the scattering amplitude such that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{fi}=\big[\bar{u}(p_{3},s_{3})\gamma^{\mu}u(p_{1},s_{1})\big]\big[\bar{u}(p_{4},s_{4})\gamma_{\mu}u(p_{2},s_{2})\big].

(8)

To express the unpolarized DCS, the squared matrix element
|S_{fi}|^{2}
is weighted by the flux of the incident electrons, the phase space of the particles in the final state and by unit of time
T
. Thus, we get:

\displaystyle d\bar{\sigma}=\frac{Vd^{3}\vec{p}_{3}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{Vd^{3}\vec{p}_{4}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{|J_{\text{inc.}}|T}\frac{(2\pi)^{4}VT\delta^{4}(p_{4}-p_{2}+p_{3}-p_{1})}{16E_{1}E_{2}E_{3}E_{4}V^{4}q^{4}}|\overline{\mathcal{M}_{fi}}|^{2},

(9)

where we average over the initial spins and sum over the final ones as follows:

|\overline{\mathcal{M}_{fi}}|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{s_{n,m}}|\mathcal{M}_{fi}|^{2}.

(10)

The incident particle flux is
|J_{\text{inc.}}|=|\vec{p}_{1}|c^{2}/(E_{1}V)
. To integrate with respect to
d^{3}\vec{p}_{4}
, we will use the following formula:

\displaystyle\frac{d^{3}\vec{p}_{4}}{E_{4}}=2\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d^{4}p_{4}\delta(p_{4}^{2}-M^{2}c^{2})\Theta(p_{4}^{0});\quad\,\,\Theta(p_{4}^{0})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&1&~~\text{for}~~p_{4}^{0}>0\\
&0&~~\text{for}~~p_{4}^{0}<0,\end{array}\right.

where
M
is the rest mass of the proton.
Using
d^{3}\vec{p}_{3}=|\vec{p}_{3}|^{2}d|\vec{p}_{3}|d\Omega_{f}
and
E_{3}dE_{3}=c^{2}|\vec{p}_{3}|d|\vec{p}_{3}|
, we find that the unpolarized DCS is expressed as :

\displaystyle\frac{d\overline{\sigma}}{d\Omega_{f}}=\frac{1}{8(2\pi)^{2}c^{6}Mq^{4}}\frac{|\vec{p}_{3}|}{|\vec{p}_{1}|}\int\delta(p_{4}^{2}-M^{2}c^{2})dE_{3}|\overline{\mathcal{M}_{fi}}|^{2}.

(14)

The integration over
dE_{3}
is performed using the following property
[
42
]
:

\displaystyle\int dx~f(x)\delta(g(x))=\frac{f(x)}{|g^{\prime}(x)|}\bigg|_{g(x)=0}.

(15)

In our case:
g^{\prime}(E_{3})=M+\frac{E_{1}}{c^{2}}-\frac{E_{3}}{c^{2}}\frac{|\vec{p}_{1}|}{|\vec{p}_{3}|}F(\theta_{i},\theta_{f},\varphi_{i},\varphi_{f})
, where

\displaystyle F(\theta_{i},\theta_{f},\varphi_{i},\varphi_{f})=\sin(\theta_{i})\sin(\theta_{f})\big[\cos(\varphi_{i})\cos(\varphi_{f})+\sin(\varphi_{i})\sin(\varphi_{f})\big]+\cos(\theta_{i})\cos(\theta_{f}).

(16)

Finally, we give the expression of the unpolarized DCS as follows:

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{d\overline{\sigma}}{d\Omega_{f}}\bigg)^{\text{laser-free}}_{\text{unpolarized}}=\frac{1}{16(2\pi)^{2}Mc^{6}q^{4}}\frac{|\vec{p}_{3}|}{|\vec{p}_{1}|}\frac{|\overline{\mathcal{M}_{fi}}|^{2}}{|g^{\prime}(E_{3})|},

(17)

where

\displaystyle|\overline{\mathcal{M}_{fi}}|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\text{Tr}\big[(c\not{p}_{3}+c^{2})\gamma^{\mu}(c\not{p}_{1}+c^{2})\gamma^{\nu}\big]\text{Tr}\big[(c\not{p}_{4}+Mc^{2})\gamma_{\mu}(c\not{p}_{2}+Mc^{2})\gamma_{\nu}\big].

(18)

2.2.
Laser-free differential cross section for polarized electrons

Until date, all calculations have been done assuming that the electron’s spin has not been detected. Now, we introduce the calculation of the DCS for polarised electrons. As a first step, we define the spin 4-vector
S_{\lambda}^{\mu}
as:

\displaystyle S_{\lambda}^{\mu}=\frac{\lambda}{c}\Big(|\vec{p}|,\frac{E}{c}\hat{p}\Big),~~~~\text{where}~~~\,\,\hat{p}=\frac{\vec{p}}{|\vec{p}|},

(19)

which verifies the normalization relation
S^{\mu}S_{\mu}=-1
, and the orthogonality relation with the 4-momentum
p
such that:
(p.S)=p^{\mu}.S_{\mu}=0
. The parameter
\lambda
defines the spin orientation (spin Up for
\lambda=+1
and Down for
\lambda=-1
).

Subsequently, the spin projection operator is introduced:

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}(S)=\frac{1}{2}(1+\gamma_{5}\not{S}),~~~\quad\text{with}~~~\gamma_{5}=i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}=-i\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}.

(20)

The spin projection operator
\hat{\Sigma}(S)
verifies the following property:

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}(S)u(p,S^{\prime})=\delta_{SS^{\prime}}u(p,S^{\prime}).

(21)

If we consider the spin polarization of electrons, the only quantity that changes in the expression of the laser-free unpolarized DCS (
17
) is
|\overline{\mathcal{M}_{fi}}|^{2}
because it is the only part that depends on the spin. It becomes

|\overline{\mathcal{M}_{fi}}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s_{2},s_{4}}|\mathcal{M}_{fi}|^{2}.

(22)

In Eq. (
22
), one has to be careful since there are no
summations on electron spin (either initial or final) polarizations. The factor
\tfrac{1}{2}
is due to the average over the initial polarizations of the unpolarized proton. Introducing the two spin projection operators for the initial and final electrons, we find

\begin{split}|\overline{\mathcal{M}_{fi}}|^{2}=&\text{Tr}\Big[\big(c\not{p}_{3}+c^{2}\big)\gamma^{\mu}\Big(\frac{1+\lambda_{i}\gamma_{5}\not{S}_{i}}{2}\Big)\big(c\not{p}_{1}+c^{2}\big)\gamma^{\nu}\Big(\frac{1+\lambda_{f}\gamma_{5}\not{S}_{f}}{2}\Big)\Big]\\
&\times\dfrac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\big[(c\not{p}_{4}+Mc^{2})\gamma_{\mu}(c\not{p}_{2}+Mc^{2})\gamma_{\nu}\big],\end{split}

(23)

where
\lambda_{i}
and
\lambda_{f}
are the helicity states respectively of initial and final electrons, and
S_{i}
and
S_{f}
are their 4-vectors spin. Now, the DCS for polarized electrons depends, in addition to other parameters, on
\lambda_{i}
and
\lambda_{f}
. Therefore, if a flip of the electron spin occurs during the process, which means that:

\lambda_{f}=-\lambda_{i}=\pm 1,~~~\Longrightarrow~~~\lambda_{i}\lambda_{f}=-1,

(24)

the obtained DCS is called spin-flip DCS. In the opposite case where no spin flip occurs (
\lambda_{f}=\lambda_{i}=\pm 1,~\Rightarrow~\lambda_{i}\lambda_{f}=1
), it is called spin non-flip DCS. One of the most important consistency tests to check is that the sum of the spin-flip and the spin non-flip DCSs must give the unpolarized DCS given in Eq. (
17
)

\bigg(\frac{d\overline{\sigma}}{d\Omega_{f}}\bigg)_{\text{unpolarized}}^{\text{laser-free}}=\bigg(\frac{d\overline{\sigma}}{d\Omega_{f}}\bigg)_{\text{flip}}+\bigg(\frac{d\overline{\sigma}}{d\Omega_{f}}\bigg)_{\text{non-flip}}.

(25)

Another important quantity to be introduced here is the degree of polarization (DP), which is defined as:

\displaystyle\text{DP}=\frac{\big(d\overline{\sigma}/d\Omega_{f}\big)_{\text{non-flip}}-\big(d\overline{\sigma}/d\Omega_{f}\big)_{\text{flip}}}{\big(d\overline{\sigma}/d\Omega_{f}\big)_{\text{non-flip}}+\big(d\overline{\sigma}/d\Omega_{f}\big)_{\text{flip}}}.

(26)

2.3.
Laser-assisted differential cross section for polarized electrons

The monochromatic laser field of circular polarization is described by the following classical 4-potential:

A_{\text{laser}}^{\mu}(\phi)=a^{\mu}_{1}\cos(\phi)+a^{\mu}_{2}\sin(\phi),

(27)

where
a_{1}^{\mu}=(0,|\vec{a}|,0,0)
and
a_{2}^{\mu}=(0,0,|\vec{a}|,0)
are the orthogonal polarization 4-vectors satisfying the following relation:
a_{1}^{2}=a_{2}^{2}=a^{2}=-|\vec{a}|^{2}=(c\varepsilon_{0}/\omega)^{2}
, where
\varepsilon_{0}
is the electric field strength and
\omega
is the electromagnetic field frequency.
\phi=k.x
is the phase of the electromagnetic field of wave 4-vector
k=(\omega/c,\vec{k})
(with
k^{2}=0
).
To describe an electron dressed by an electromagnetic field, we use the solution of the following Dirac equation in the presence of the laser field:

\displaystyle\Big[\Big(\hat{p}-\frac{A}{c}\Big)^{2}-c^{2}-\frac{i}{2c}F_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\Big]\psi(x)=0,

(28)

where
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
represents the electromagnetic tensor and
\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\tfrac{1}{2}[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}]
. The general solution of Eq. (
28
) is an exact Dirac-Volkov plane wave function expressed as follows
[
41
]
:

\displaystyle\psi_{p_{n}}(x)=\bigg(1+\frac{\not{k}\not{A}}{2c(k.p_{n})}\bigg)\frac{u(p_{n},s_{n})}{\sqrt{2Q_{n}V}}e^{iS(q_{n},x)},

(29)

where
u(p_{n},s_{n})
is the free Dirac bispinor of the electron, which verifies the following relation:

\displaystyle\bar{u}(p_{n},s_{n})u(p_{n},s_{n})=2c^{2}.

(30)

The dressed electron is of 4-momentum
q_{n}=(Q_{n}/c,\vec{q}_{n})
given by:

\displaystyle q_{n}=p_{n}-\frac{a^{2}}{2c^{2}(k.p_{n})}k,\quad\text{with}~~\,q_{n}^{2}={m^{*}}^{2}c^{2},

(31)

where
m^{*}=\sqrt{1-a^{2}/c^{4}}
is the effective mass of the electron in the presence of the laser field. The classical action
S(q_{n},x)
in Eq. (
29
) has the following expression:

\displaystyle S(q_{n},x)=-q_{n}.x-\frac{a_{1}.p_{n}}{c(k.p_{n})}\sin(\phi)+\frac{a_{2}.p_{n}}{c(k.p_{n})}\cos(\phi).

(32)

In the first Born approximation, using Feynman’s rules and after a simple treatment, we express
S_{fi}
in the presence of an electromagnetic field by:

\displaystyle S_{fi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-i}{\sqrt{16Q_{1}Q_{3}E_{2}E_{4}V^{4}}}\int d^{4}x~d^{4}y~\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{e^{i(q_{3}-q_{1}-q).x}e^{i(p_{4}-p_{2}+q).y}e^{-iz\sin(\phi-\phi_{0})}}{q^{2}+i\varepsilon}

(33)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\bigg[\overline{u}(p_{3},s_{3})\bigg(C_{\mu}^{0}+C_{\mu}^{1}\cos(\phi)+C_{\mu}^{2}\sin(\phi)\bigg)u(p_{1},s_{1})\bigg]\bigg[\overline{u}(p_{4},s_{4})\gamma^{\mu}u(p_{2},s_{2})\bigg],

where

\displaystyle z=\sqrt{\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}}~~~\text{and}~~~\phi_{0}=\arctan(\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1}),

(34)

with

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=\frac{a_{1}.p_{1}}{c(k.p_{1})}-\frac{a_{1}.p_{3}}{c(k.p_{3})}\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{2}=\frac{a_{2}.p_{1}}{c(k.p_{1})}-\frac{a_{2}.p_{3}}{c(k.p_{3})}.

(35)

The terms
C^{0}_{\mu}
,
C^{1}_{\mu}
and
C^{2}_{\mu}
are given by:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}C^{0}_{\mu}&=&\gamma_{\mu}-2k_{\mu}a^{2}\not{k}C(p_{1})C(p_{3}),\\
C^{1}_{\mu}&=&C(p_{1})\gamma_{\mu}\not{k}\not{a}_{1}+C(p_{3})\not{a}_{1}\not{k}\gamma_{\mu},\\
C^{2}_{\mu}&=&C(p_{1})\gamma_{\mu}\not{k}\not{a}_{2}+C(p_{3})\not{a}_{2}\not{k}\gamma_{\mu}.\end{array}\right.\qquad\text{where}\quad C(p_{i})=\frac{1}{2c(k.p_{i})}.

Then, by integrating with respect to
d^{4}x
,
d^{4}y
and
d^{4}q
, we obtain:

\displaystyle S_{fi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-i}{\sqrt{16Q_{1}Q_{3}E_{2}E_{4}V^{4}}}\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(p_{4}-p_{2}+q_{3}-q_{1}-\ell k)}{q^{2}+i\varepsilon}

(40)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\bigg[\overline{u}(p_{3},s_{3})\bigg(C_{\mu}^{0}B_{\ell}(z)+C_{\mu}^{1}B_{1\ell}(z)+C_{\mu}^{2}B_{2\ell}(z)\bigg)u(p_{1},s_{1})\bigg]\bigg[\overline{u}(p_{4},s_{4})\gamma^{\mu}u(p_{2},s_{2})\bigg],

where
\ell
is the number of photons exchanged and
q=q_{3}-q_{1}-\ell k
is the 4-momentum transfer. The functions
B_{\ell}
,
B_{1\ell}
and
B_{2\ell}
are obtained using Fourier transforms into Bessel functions
[
43
]
, such that:

\displaystyle\Big(1,\cos(\phi),\sin(\phi)\Big)e^{-iz\sin(\phi-\phi_{0})}=\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{+\infty}\Big(B_{\ell}(z),B_{1\ell}(z),B_{2\ell}(z)\Big),

(41)

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix}B_{\ell}(z)\\
B_{1\ell}(z)\\
B_{2\ell}(z)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}J_{\ell}(z)e^{i\ell\phi_{0}}\\
\big(J_{\ell+1}(z)e^{i(\ell+1)\phi_{0}}+J_{\ell-1}(z)e^{i(\ell-1)\phi_{0}}\big)/2\\
\big(J_{\ell+1}(z)e^{i(\ell+1)\phi_{0}}-J_{\ell-1}(z)e^{i(\ell-1)\phi_{0}}\big)/2i\end{bmatrix}.\end{split}

(42)

As in the laser-free case of the DCS (
9
), we give the DCS in the presence of an external laser field by the following formula:

\displaystyle d\bar{\sigma}=\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{Vd^{3}\vec{q}_{3}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{Vd^{3}\vec{p}_{4}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{|J_{\text{inc.}}|T}\frac{(2\pi)^{4}VT\delta^{4}(p_{4}-p_{2}+q_{3}-q_{1}-\ell k)}{16Q_{1}E_{2}Q_{3}E_{4}V^{4}q^{4}}|\overline{\mathcal{M}_{fi}^{\ell}}|^{2},

(43)

where
|J_{\text{inc.}}|=[|\vec{q}_{1}|c^{2}]/[VQ_{1}]
is the flux of incident electrons dressed by the laser field. The associated scattering amplitude
|\overline{\mathcal{M}_{fi}^{\ell}}|
, in case of well defined spin electrons, will take the following form:

\begin{split}|\overline{\mathcal{M}_{fi}^{\ell}}|^{2}=&\text{Tr}\big[(c\not{p}_{3}+c^{2})\Gamma_{\mu}^{\ell}(\frac{1+\lambda_{1}\gamma_{5}\not{S}_{1}}{2})(c\not{p}_{1}+c^{2})\Gamma_{\nu}^{\ell\dagger}(\frac{1+\lambda_{3}\gamma_{5}\not{S}_{3}}{2})\big]\\
&\times\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\big[(c\not{p}_{4}+Mc^{2})\gamma^{\mu}(c\not{p}_{2}+Mc^{2})\gamma^{\nu}\big],\end{split}

(44)

with
\Gamma_{\nu}^{\ell\dagger}
is the conjugate of
\Gamma_{\mu}^{\ell}
defined by:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu}^{\ell}=C_{\mu}^{0}B_{\ell}(z)+C_{\mu}^{1}B_{1\ell}(z)+C_{\mu}^{2}B_{2\ell}(z).

(45)

Finally, the DCS expression in the presence of an electromagnetic wave and for well-defined spin states of the electron is given by:

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{d\overline{\sigma}}{d\Omega_{f}}\bigg)^{\text{laser-assisted}}_{\text{$e$-polarized}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{3})=\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{d\overline{\sigma}^{\ell}}{d\Omega_{f}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{3})=\frac{1}{16(2\pi)^{2}Mc^{6}q^{4}}\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{|\vec{q}_{3}|}{|\vec{q}_{1}|}\frac{|\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\ell}_{fi}}|^{2}}{|g^{\prime}(Q_{3})|},

(46)

where the function
g^{\prime}(Q_{3})
is obtained by using the property in Eq. (
15
):

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(Q_{3})=\bigg[\frac{Q_{1}}{c^{2}}+M+\frac{\ell\omega}{c^{2}}\bigg]-\frac{Q_{3}}{c^{2}|\vec{q_{3}}|}\bigg[|\vec{q}_{1}|F(\theta_{i},\theta_{f},\varphi_{i},\varphi_{f})+\frac{\ell\omega}{c^{2}}\cos(\theta_{f})\bigg].

(47)

For the spin-flip and spin non-flip laser-assisted DCSs, we have:

\begin{split}\bigg(\frac{d\overline{\sigma}}{d\Omega_{f}}\bigg)_{\text{flip}}&=\bigg(\frac{d\overline{\sigma}}{d\Omega_{f}}\bigg)^{\text{laser-assisted}}_{\text{$e$-polarized}}(\lambda_{1}=-\lambda_{3}=\pm 1),\\
\bigg(\frac{d\overline{\sigma}}{d\Omega_{f}}\bigg)_{\text{non-flip}}&=\bigg(\frac{d\overline{\sigma}}{d\Omega_{f}}\bigg)^{\text{laser-assisted}}_{\text{$e$-polarized}}(\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{3}=\pm 1).\end{split}

(48)

The sum of the two (spin-flip and non-flip DCSs) should again yield the unpolarized laser-assisted DCS that was previously calculated in a recent work
[
11
]
.

The calculation of the traces appearing in Eqs. (
18
), (
23
) and (
44
) is performed with the help of the symbolic-algebra program Feyncalc
[
44
]
. We mention here that the result obtained is too long and cumbersome to be included here due to the complexity of the calculations. For example, the result of the trace calculation in the presence of the laser (given in eq. (
44
)) yielded about 63 different antisymmetric tensors,
\epsilon(a,b,c,d)=\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}a^{\mu}b^{\nu}c^{\rho}d^{\sigma}

4.
Conclusion

In this study, we have treated the process of laser-assisted electron-proton scattering considering the effect of electron spin polarization. By applying the concept of polarized electrons, the expressions of the DCS and
the degree of polarization are derived and their behavior in the presence of the laser field is studied. We found that the effect of high laser field strengths is important in each well defined polarization case (spin-flip and non-flip) for an exchanged number pf photons
\ell=\pm 1
. The comparison of the different spin polarizations, made by introducing the degree of polarization, shows that the laser field can modify both spin-flip and non-flip DCSs with a continuous dominance of the non-flip DCS. It is concluded that at high kinetic energy of the incident electron, the electron’s probability for changing its spin is zero. A consistency check that is carried out successfully is that the sum of the two spin-flip and spin non-flip DCSs always gives the unpolarized DCS.
This approach, developed to describe the electrons by Dirac-Volkov functions, is based on the fact that the involved proton is unpolarized and not dressed by the laser field. In order to provide an in-depth and comprehensive study, we are planning to also consider, in addition to the electron, the effect of proton dressing and its spin polarization in a future work.

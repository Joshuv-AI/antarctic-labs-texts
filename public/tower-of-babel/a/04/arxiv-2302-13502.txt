Spiked multiplicative random matrices and principal components
Xiucai Ding, Hong Chang Ji

Abstract
In this paper, we study the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the spiked invariant multiplicative models when the randomness is from Haar matrices. We establish the limits of the outlier eigenvalues λ ^ i \widehat{\lambda}_{i} and the generalized components ( ⟨ 𝐯 , 𝐮 ^ i ⟩ \langle\mathbf{v},\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{i}\rangle for any deterministic vector 𝐯 \mathbf{v} ) of the outlier eigenvectors 𝐮 ^ i \widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{i} with optimal convergence rates. Moreover, we prove that the non-outlier eigenvalues stick with those of the unspiked matrices and the non-outlier eigenvectors are delocalized. The results also hold near the so-called BBP transition and for degenerate spikes. On one hand, our results can be regarded as a refinement of the counterparts of [ 12 ] under additional regularity conditions. On the other hand, they can be viewed as an analog of [ 34 ] by replacing the random matrix with i.i.d. entries with Haar random matrix.

Introduction

Finite rank deformed random matrices have found applications in many scientific endeavors. In these contexts, the low-rank part is usually regarded as the signal whereas the random matrix part can be viewed as the high dimensional noise. From an application viewpoint, researchers are interested in understanding the signal part from the noisy matrix especially from the first few largest eigenvalues and eigenvectors, which are closely related to the principal component analysis (PCA)
[
42
]
.

In the literature, a popular and sophisticated model is the spiked covariance matrix model proposed by Johnstone
[
41
]
. In such a model, a finite number of spikes (eigenvalues detached from the bulk of the spectrum) are added to the spectrum of the population covariance matrix. Specifically, consider that

\widehat{Y}=\widehat{A}^{1/2}X.

Here
\widehat{A}
is the spiked covaraince matrix constructed by adding a finite rank perturbation to some non-spiked positive definite matrix
A,
and
X=(x_{ij})
is the main random source where
x_{ij}
’s are i.i.d. centered random variables. An extension is the spiked separable covariance matrix
[
34
]
, where the data matrix is

Y=\widehat{A}^{1/2}X\widehat{B}^{1/2},

with another spiked matrix
\widehat{B}
. In spatiotemporal data analysis,
\widehat{A}
and
\widehat{B}
are respectively the spatial and
temporal covariance matrices
[
52
]
.

While the assumption that
X
has i.i.d. entries has been useful in many instances, other types of random matrices also appear naturally in certain applications. An important example is the Haar distributed random matrices which have been used in statistical learning theory, for instance, see
[
36
,
37
,
46
,
48
,
58
]
. In the current paper, we aim to study the spiked random matrices where the main randomness is Haar random matrices. Especially, we consider that
X=U
is either an
N\times N
random Haar unitary or orthogonal matrix, so that

\widehat{Y}=\widehat{A}^{1/2}U\widehat{B}^{1/2}.

(1.1)

We point out that the data matrix (
1.1

,

,

]
.

1.1
Some related results on finite rank deformation of random matrices

In this section, we first pause to give a brief review of
the literature on the spectra of fixed-rank deformation of random matrices, a category of random matrix models including signal-plus-noise and spiked covariance matrices as typical examples. There exists rich literature in understanding the limiting behavior of the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of such deformed models. Since the seminal work of Baik, Ben Arous, and Péché
[
3
]
, it is now well-understood that the extreme eigenvalues undergo the so-called BBP transition as the magnitude of the deformation changes. Roughly speaking, the extreme eigenvalues of the deformed matrix detaches from spectrum of the undeformed random matrix if and only if the strength of the deformation exceeds a certain threshold. In this case, we call the extreme eigenvalue as an outlier, and the associated eigenvector as an outlier eigenvector. In parallel to the outlier eigenvalues, an outlier eigenvector is concentrated on a cone with the axis parallel to the true eigenvectors (of the deformation) and the aperture explicitly determined by the deformations. Moreover, the remaining eigenvalues are close to those of the undeformed random matrices and the associated eigenvectors are delocalized.

The results in the same spirit of the aforementioned arguments have been established for various deformed random matrix models under different settings when the random matrix part contains i.i.d. entries. On one hand, when the deformation is additive, the eigenvalues and eigenvectors have been studied for deformed Wigner matrices in
[
9
,
14
,
26
,
27
,
43
,
44
]
, for signal-plus-noise matrices in
[
6
,
15
,
23
,
24
,
30
]
and for deformed non-Hermtian matrices in
[
10
,
16
,
20
,
29
,
54
]
. On the other hand, when the deformation is multiplicative, the eigenvalues and eigenvectors have been investigated for spiked covariance matrices in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
14
,
18
,
19
,
31
,
51
]
, for spiked separable covariance matrices in
[
34
]
, for spiked CCA matrices in
[
8
,
49
]
, for spiked MANOVA matrices in
[
39
,
57
]
and for spiked correlation matrices in
[
50
]
.

When the randomness comes from Haar invariant random matrices, there are relatively fewer related works
[
12
,
14
,
25
]
. All the existing works focus on finding the limits of the outlier eigenvalues and eigenvectors under stronger assumptions that the spikes are far away from the critical values by a distance of constant order. Consequently, they leave the convergent rates and the non-outlier eigenvalues and eigenvectors undiscussed. The aim of this paper is to fill this gap by establishing the first order limits and precise rates of convergence for the outlier eigenvalues and eigenvectors and concentration bounds for the non-outlier eigenvalues and eigenvectors for the model (
1.1
).

A^{1/2}UBU^{*}A^{1/2}

3.14

and
3.14
are consistent with
[

That is, under some mild assumptions, the outlier eigenvalues are asymptotically Gaussian whereas the extremal non-outlier eigenvalues follow Tracy-Widom distribution. We will pursue this direction in future works.

Next for the sample eigenvector of
\widehat{Y}\widehat{Y}^{*}
associated with the outlier caused by a supercritical spike
\widehat{a}_{i}
, we show that it is concentrated on a cone with axis parallel to the true eigenvector with an explicit aperture determined by
\widehat{a}_{i}
and
\Omega_{B}
; see Theorem

that is associated with a subcritical spike

by switching the role of

and

We remark that in
[
12
]
, the authors have studied the convergent limits of the outlying eigenvalues and eigenvectors on a macroscopic scale; see Remarks

3.16

[

Our proof adapts the same proof strategy as in

,
43

Remark 2.7

3
Main results

In this section, we state the main results. Throughout the paper, we will consistently use the notion of
stochastic domination
, which was firstly introduced in
[
38
]
. It simplifies the presentation by systematizing the statements of the form "
X_{N}
is bounded by
Y_{N}
with high probability up to a small power of
N
."

Definition 3.9
.

For two sequences of random variables
\{X_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{Y_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}},
we say that
X_{N}
is stochastically dominated by
Y_{N},
written as
X_{N}\prec Y_{N}
or
X_{N}=\mathrm{O}_{\prec}(Y_{N}),
if for all (small)
\epsilon>0
and (large)
D>0,
we have

\mathbb{P}\left(|X_{N}|\geq N^{\epsilon}|Y_{N}|\right)\leq N^{-D},

for sufficiently large
N\geq N_{0}(\epsilon,D).
If
X_{N}(\upsilon)
and
Y_{N}(\upsilon)
depend on a common parameter
\upsilon,
we say
X_{N}\prec Y_{N}
uniformly in
\upsilon
if the threshold
N_{0}(\epsilon,D)
can be chosen independently of the parameter
\upsilon.
Moreover, we say an event
\Xi
holds with high probability if for any constant
D>0,

\mathbb{P}(\Xi)\geq 1-N^{-D}
for large enough
N.

Eigenvalue statistics

Remark 3.12
.

We remark that the locations, in other words, convergence limits of the outlier eigenvalues have been obtained without Assumption

Remark 3.13
.

On macroscopic level,
[
12
]
covers more general settings assuming (
3.2
) and (

). Firstly, the limiting measures
\mu_{\alpha}
and
\mu_{\beta}
are only assumed to be non-trivial and compactly supported. In this general setting the resulting set of outliers is (see

\left(\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq r}\Omega_{B}^{-1}(\{\widehat{a}_{i}\})\cup\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq s}\Omega_{A}^{-1}(\{\widehat{b}_{j}\})\right)\setminus\supp(\mu_{\alpha}\boxtimes\mu_{\beta}),

(3.4)

which is consistent with our Theorem
3.11
. It should be noted that, under Assumption

necessarily has at most one element. In other words, each

or
\widehat{b}_{j}
gives rise to at most one outlier. To see this, recall the following three facts from Lemma

; (i)

is an increasing real analytic function on

with

, (ii)
\im\Omega_{\beta}>0
in the interior of

\Omega_{\beta}(E_{+})>E_{+}^{\beta}
and
\Omega_{\beta}(E_{-})<E_{-}^{\beta}

\mu_{\alpha}\boxtimes\mu_{\beta}
is supported on a single interval, we easily find that each of
\Omega_{\beta}^{-1}(\widehat{a}_{i})
has at most one element.

However, for general
\mu_{\alpha}
and
\mu_{\beta}
, a single spike
\widehat{a}_{i}
or
\widehat{b}_{j}
may result in several outliers; see
[

for concrete examples. We believe that the three facts above extend to fairly general
\mu_{\alpha}
and
\mu_{\beta}
, but their free convolution may have disconnected support, in particular if either
\mu_{\alpha}
or
\mu_{\beta}
does. In this case, each interval between connected components of

may contain a solution

of the equation
\Omega_{B}(x)=\widehat{a}_{i}
, in contrast to our case where the support is connected.

Finally, we point out that
[
12
]
also studied the additive and circular models, namely

and

for Hermitian and unitary matrices

, respectively. We believe the counterpart of our results for the additive model can be proved analogously, since the two major inputs, the square-root decay of

near the edges and the optimal edge local laws, have already been proved in
[

[

Second, we study the non-outlier eigenvalues of

Theorem 3.14

(Eigenvalue sticking)
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.2

2.6

Fix any small constant

We have that

\left|\widehat{\lambda}_{i+r^{+}+s^{+}}-\lambda_{i}\right|\prec\frac{1}{N\gamma},\ 1\leq i\leq\tau N.

(3.6)

Theorem

provides the convergence limits and rates for the non-outlier eigenvalues of

in terms of those of
\mathcal{Q}_{1}.
On one hand, if

together with the rigidity of

established in

]

\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}
in (

(3.7)

For convenience, we use the following notations. For

\delta_{\pi_{a}(i_{1}),\pi_{a}(i_{2})}^{a}:=|\widehat{a}_{i_{1}}-\widehat{a}_{i_{2}}|,\quad\delta_{\pi_{a}(i_{1}),\pi_{b}(j)}^{a}:=\left|\widehat{b}_{j}-\Omega_{A}(\Omega_{B}^{-1}(\widehat{a}_{i_{1}}))\right|.

(3.8)

Similarly, for
1\leq j_{1}\leq s^{+}

and

\delta_{\pi_{b}(j_{1}),\pi_{a}(i)}^{b}:=|\widehat{a}_{i}-\Omega_{B}(\Omega_{A}^{-1}(\widehat{b}_{j_{1}}))|,\quad\delta_{\pi_{b}(j_{1}),\pi_{b}(j_{2})}^{b}:=|\widehat{b}_{j_{1}}-\widehat{b}_{j_{2}}|.

Further if
\mathfrak{a}\in S
, we define

\delta_{\mathfrak{a}}(S):=\begin{cases}\left(\min_{k:\pi_{a}(k)\notin S}\delta^{a}_{\mathfrak{a},\pi_{a}(k)}\right)\wedge\left(\min_{j:\pi_{b}(j)\notin S}\delta^{a}_{\mathfrak{a},\pi_{b}(j)}\right),&\ \text{if }\mathfrak{a}=\pi_{a}(i)\in S\\
\left(\min_{k:\pi_{a}(k)\notin S}\delta^{b}_{\mathfrak{a},\pi_{a}(k)}\right)\wedge\left(\min_{j:\pi_{b}(j)\notin S}\delta^{b}_{\mathfrak{a},\pi_{b}(j)}\right),&\ \text{if }\mathfrak{a}=\pi_{b}(j)\in S\end{cases};

(3.9)

if
\mathfrak{a}\notin S
, then we define

\delta_{\mathfrak{a}}(S):=\left(\min_{k:\pi_{a}(k)\in S}\delta^{a}_{\pi_{a}(k),\mathfrak{a}}\right)\wedge\left(\min_{j:\pi_{b}(j)\in S}\delta^{b}_{\pi_{b}(j),\mathfrak{a}}\right).

(3.10)

With the above preparation, we proceed to state our main results on the outlier singular vectors. Denote the projections

\mathcal{P}_{S}=\sum_{k\in S}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{*},\ \text{and}\ \mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{S}=\sum_{k\in S}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{k}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{k}^{*},

where
\{\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}\}
and
\{\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{k}\}
are the eigenvectors of
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{2}
in (
2.13
), respectively.

Theorem 3.15

(Outlier eigenvectors)
.

Suppose that Assumptions
2.2
and
2.6
hold. For the set
S
in (
3.7
) and any given deterministic vector
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{N})^{*}\in\mathbb{C}^{N},
we have that for the left singular vectors,

\left|\langle\mathbf{v},\mathcal{P}_{S}\mathbf{v}\rangle-g_{a}(\mathbf{v},S)\right|\prec\sum_{i:\pi_{a}(i)\in S}\frac{|v_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{N(\widehat{a}_{i}-\Omega_{\beta}(E_{+}))}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{|v_{i}|^{2}}{N\delta_{\pi_{a}(i)}(S)}+g_{a}(\mathbf{v},S)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\pi_{a}(i)\notin S}\frac{|v_{i}|^{2}}{N\delta_{\pi_{a}(i)}(S)}\right)^{1/2},

where
g_{a}(\mathbf{v},S)
is defined as

g_{a}(\mathbf{v},S):=\sum_{i:\pi_{a}(i)\in S}\widehat{a}_{i}\frac{(\Omega_{B}^{-1})^{\prime}(\widehat{a}_{i})}{\Omega_{B}^{-1}(\widehat{a}_{i})}|v_{i}|^{2}.

(3.11)

Similarly, for the right singular vectors, we have

\left|\langle\mathbf{v},\mathcal{P}_{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{v}\rangle-g_{b}(\mathbf{v},S)\right|\prec\sum_{i:\pi_{b}(j)\in S}\frac{|v_{i}|^{2}}{\sqrt{N(\widehat{b}_{j}-\Omega_{\alpha}(E_{+}))}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{|v_{j}|^{2}}{N\delta_{\pi_{b}(j)}(S)}+g_{b}(\mathbf{v},S)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\pi_{b}(j)\notin S}\frac{|v_{j}|^{2}}{N\delta_{\pi_{b}(j)}(S)}\right)^{1/2},

where
g_{b}(\mathbf{v},S)
is defined as

g_{b}(\mathbf{v},S):=\sum_{j:\pi_{b}(j)\in S}\widehat{b}_{j}\frac{(\Omega_{A}^{-1})^{\prime}(\widehat{b}_{j})}{\Omega_{A}^{-1}(\widehat{b}_{j})}|v_{j}|^{2}.

Theorem
3.15
establishes the concentration bounds for the generalized components of the outlier singular vectors. It demonstrates that the singular vectors are concentrated on a cone with axis parallel to the true singular vectors with an explicit aperture depending on the spikes and subordination functions. We consider an example for illustration. For simplicity, we consider the non-degenerate case such that all the outliers are well-separated in the sense that we can simply choose
S=\{\pi_{a}(i)\}
or
S=\{\pi_{b}(j)\}.
Let
S=\{\pi_{a}(i)\}
and
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{e}_{i}.
Then we obtain from Theorem
3.15
that

\left|\langle\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{i}\rangle\right|^{2}=\widehat{a}_{i}\frac{(\Omega_{B}^{-1})^{\prime}(\widehat{a}_{i})}{\Omega_{B}^{-1}(\widehat{a}_{i})}+\mathrm{O}_{\prec}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}(\widehat{a}_{i}-\Omega_{\beta}(E_{+}))^{1/2}}+\frac{1}{N\delta^{2}_{i}}\right),\ \delta_{i}=\delta_{\pi_{a}(i)}(\pi_{a}(i)).

(3.12)

It is easy to see from (
6.2
), (

) and (

) that the error term is much smaller than the first term of the right-hand side of (

). In this sense,
\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{i}
is concentrated on a cone with axis parallel to
\mathbf{e}_{i}.

.

We mention that some partial results of Theorem

have been obtained in

Finally, we state the main results regarding the non-outlier singular vectors. Denote

Theorem 3.17

(Non-outlier eigenvectors)
.

Suppose that Assumptions
2.2
and
2.6
hold. Fix any small constant

For
\pi_{a}(i)\notin\mathcal{O}_{+},\ i\leq\tau N
and any deterministic vector

we have that

\left|\langle\mathbf{v},\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{\pi_{a}(i)}\rangle\right|^{2}\prec\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{|v_{j}|^{2}}{N(\kappa_{i}+|\widehat{a}_{j}-\Omega_{\beta}(E_{+})|^{2})}.

(3.13)

Similarly, for the right singular vectors, we have

\left|\langle\mathbf{v},\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{\pi_{b}(j)}\rangle\right|^{2}\prec\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{|v_{i}|^{2}}{N(\kappa_{j}+|\widehat{b}_{i}-\Omega_{\alpha}(E_{+})|^{2})}.

Theorem

establishes the results for the non-outlier eigenvectors. Especially, if the spikes are well separated from the critical values at a distance of constant order, (

) implies that the non-outlier eigenvectors are completely delocalized in the direction of the associated spiked eigenvectors. More specifically, assuming that for the spike

for some fixed constant

and

we consider the direction of the associated spiked eigenvector, i.e.,

In such a setting, for

the result of (

) reads

\left|\langle\mathbf{e}_{l},\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{\pi_{a}(i)}\rangle\right|^{2}\prec N^{-1},

which indicates that the eigenvector
\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{\pi_{a}(i)}
is completely delocalized when projected on the eigenvector associated with
\widehat{a}_{l}.
Similar results hold for the right singular vectors.

Similarly, we have that

\ \sup_{\mu\notin\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{(b)}}\Omega_{A}^{-1}(\widehat{b}_{\mu})\leq\inf_{i\in\mathcal{O}_{\epsilon}^{(a)}}\Omega_{B}^{-1}(\widehat{a}_{i})+N^{-2/3+2\epsilon}.

(5.20)

For any

we denote

a^{k}:=\min_{i\in A_{k}}\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{m_{k}},\quad b^{k}:=\max_{i\in A_{k}}\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{l_{k}}.

With the above notations, we introduce the continuous path as

Note that

and

. The interval

\mathcal{Q}_{1}
that are in

, and the endpoint
x_{0}^{k}
(or
x_{1}^{k}
) is at a distance at least of the orders

from any eigenvalue of
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
.

Moreover, on one hand, using a standard perturbation approach as in Proposition D.6 of
[

]
, we can show that

has at least

eigenvalues in

. On the other hand, by (

), (

) and Lemma

, we conclude that
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}
has at most
|A_{k}|
eigenvalues in

This concludes the proof.
∎

In the third step, we generalize Lemma

using a continuity argument as in the proof of Theorem

. We omit the details and conclude the proof of Theorem

.

Step 1:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bm{\mathcal{D}}^{-1}+z\mathbf{U}^{*}\mathbf{G}(z)\mathbf{U}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bm{\mathcal{D}}^{-1}+z\mathbf{U}^{*}\Theta(z)\mathbf{U}}+\frac{1}{\bm{\mathcal{D}}^{-1}+z\mathbf{U}^{*}\Theta(z)\mathbf{U}}\mathcal{E}\frac{1}{\bm{\mathcal{D}}^{-1}+z\mathbf{U}^{*}\Theta(z)\mathbf{U}}

Together with (

) and (

Lemma 6.32
.

For any

\displaystyle\leq C\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{N^{-1/2+\epsilon}}{|\zeta-\widehat{a}_{i}||\zeta-\widehat{a}_{j}|}\frac{(\Omega_{B}^{-1})^{\prime}(\zeta)}{|\zeta-\Omega_{\beta}(E_{+})|}\left\|\bm{\mathcal{D}}^{-1}+\Omega_{B}^{-1}(\zeta)\mathbf{U}^{*}\mathbf{G}(\Omega_{B}^{-1}(\zeta))\mathbf{U}\right\||\mathrm{d}\zeta|,

Finally, the general case can be dealt with easily. For general

where for some

\quad d_{k}^{a}:=\begin{cases}d_{k}^{a},\ &\text{if }1\leq k\leq r\\
\widetilde{\epsilon},\ &\text{if }k\in\mathcal{R}\text{ and }k>r\end{cases}.

Then all the previous proof goes through for the perturbed model as long as we replace the
\mathbf{U}
and

in (

) with

\widehat{\mathbf{U}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{E}_{r+2}&0\\
0&\mathbf{E}_{s}\end{pmatrix},\quad\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{D}}}=\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{D}^{a}(\widehat{D}^{a}+1)^{-1}&0\\
0&D^{b}(D^{b}+1)^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

(6.23)

Note that in the proof, only the upper bound on the
d_{k}^{a}
’s were used. Moreover, the proof does not depend on the fact that
\widehat{a}_{i}
or
\widehat{a}_{j}
satisfy (
2.10
) (we only need the indices in

to satisfy Assumption
6.30
). By taking
\widetilde{\epsilon}\downarrow 0
and using continuity, we get that Proposition
6.29
holds for general
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
.

Step 2:
In the second step, we complete our proof by removing Assumption

. In fact, once we finish the proof of Step one, the second step is relatively standard and follows the same argument as in

, Section E.2]

; see Definition E.8 of

]
. We omit the details since our proof follows verbatim as

6.2
Proof of Theorem
3.17

In this subsection, we prove the results for the non-outlier eigenvectors. Our goal is to prove the following proposition, from which Theorem
3.17
immediately follows.

Proposition 6.33
.

Fix a small constant
\widetilde{\tau}\in(0,1/3).
For
\pi_{a}(i)\notin\mathcal{O}^{+}
and
i\leq\tau N,
where
\tau>0

\left|\langle\mathbf{e}_{j},\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{\pi_{a}(i)}\rangle\right|^{2}\prec\frac{1}{N(\kappa_{i}+|\widehat{a}_{j}-\Omega_{\beta}(E_{+})|^{2})}.

\left|\langle\mathbf{e}_{j},\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{\pi_{a}(i)}\rangle\right|^{2}\prec\frac{N^{4\widetilde{\tau}}}{N(\kappa_{i}+|\widehat{a}_{j}-\Omega_{\beta}(E_{+})|^{2})}.

(6.24)

Proof of Proposition
6.33
.

Due to similarity, we only prove the first statement. As mentioned earlier, the control relies on the deterministic inequality (
4.30
). By Theorems
4.19
,
3.11
,
3.15
,
3.14
and Lemmas

, for any fixed
\epsilon>0,
we can choose a high-probability event
\Xi_{3}
where (

)–(

5.16
)–(

) and the following hold:

\mathbf{1}(\Xi_{3})|\widehat{\lambda}_{i}-\gamma_{i}|\leq Ci^{-1/3}n^{-2/3+\epsilon/2},\ \text{for}\ \pi_{a}(i)\notin\mathcal{O}^{+}\ \text{and}\ i\leq\tau p.

where we used the interlacing result Lemma
5.26

In what follows, we again focus on
\Xi_{2}
and the discussion will be purely deterministic. Recall
\bm{e}_{i}
is the natural embedding of
\mathbf{e}_{i}
in

As discussed in (

\{1,2,3,\cdots,r\}\cup\{j\}
to handle the general case. For simplicity, we assume that

\eta_{i}>0
be the unique solution of

\im\Omega_{B}(\widehat{\lambda}_{i}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{i})=\frac{N^{6\epsilon}}{\eta_{i}N},

(6.25)

which follows from the facts that
N^{-1+\epsilon}\im\Omega_{B}(\widehat{\lambda}_{i}+\mathrm{i}N^{-1+\epsilon})\lesssim N^{-1+\epsilon}

is monotone increasing (see
[
32

). According to (
4.30
) and (
4.24
), fixing an
\pi_{a}(i)\notin\mathcal{O}^{+},
we can write

\left|\langle\mathbf{e}_{j},\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{\pi_{a}(i)}\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\eta_{i}\im\langle\bm{e}_{j},\widehat{\mathbf{G}}(z_{i})\bm{e}_{j}\rangle,\quad z_{i}=\widehat{\lambda}_{i}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{i}.

(6.26)

Recall (
6.9
) and (
6.1
). By (
4.3
), using the resolvent expansion (
6.1
), we find that

\displaystyle z\langle\bm{e}_{j},\widehat{\mathbf{G}}(z)\bm{e}_{j}\rangle=\frac{1}{d_{j}^{a}}-\frac{1+d_{j}^{a}}{(d_{j}^{a})^{2}}\left[\Phi_{j}(z)+\Phi_{j}^{2}(z)\left(\mathcal{E}(z)+\mathcal{E}(z)\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}^{-1}+z\mathbf{U}^{*}\mathbf{G}(z)\mathbf{U}}\mathcal{E}(z)\right)_{jj}\right],

(6.27)

and used the abbreviated notation that

\Phi_{j}(z):=\left(\frac{1}{d_{j}^{a}}-\frac{a_{j}}{\Omega_{B}(z)-a_{j}}\right)^{-1}.

Similar to the discussion between (F.7) and (F.15) of
[

]
, by (

), (

), Lemma
4.18
and the definition (
6.25

\min_{j}|\Phi_{j}(z)|\gg\|\mathcal{E}(z_{i})\|,

z_{i}\langle\bm{e}_{j},{\widehat{\mathbf{G}}(z_{i})}\bm{e}_{j}\rangle=\frac{\Omega_{B}(z_{i})}{\widehat{a}_{j}-\Omega_{B}(z_{i})}+\mathrm{O}\left(\frac{\|\mathcal{E}(z_{i})\|}{|\widehat{a}_{j}-\Omega_{B}(z_{i})|^{2}}\right).

(6.28)

In order to bound the right-hand side of the above equation, we will need the following estimate. Its proof is the same as (6.10) of
[
18
]
or Lemma F.2 of

]
and we omit the details.

Lemma 6.34
.

For any fixed
\delta\in[0,1/3-\epsilon),
there exists a constant
c>0
such that

|\widehat{a}_{j}-{\Omega_{B}(z_{i})}|\geq c\left[N^{-2\delta}|\widehat{a}_{j}-\Omega_{\beta}(E_{+})|+\im\Omega_{B}(z_{i})\right],

holds whenever
\widehat{\lambda}_{i}\in[0,\Omega_{B}^{-1}(\Omega_{\beta}(E_{+})+N^{-1/3+\delta+\epsilon})).

By (
6.26
), (
6.28
), and fixing a suitable
\delta>0

\left|\langle\mathbf{e}_{j},\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{\pi_{a}(i)}\rangle\right|^{2}\leq-\frac{\eta_{i}^{2}}{|z_{i}|^{2}}\re(\widehat{a}_{j}-\Omega_{B}(z_{i}))^{-1}-\frac{\eta_{i}\widehat{\lambda}_{i}}{|z_{i}|^{2}}\im(\widehat{a}_{j}-\Omega_{B}(z_{i}))^{-1}+\frac{C\eta_{i}\|\mathcal{E}(z_{i})\|}{|\widehat{a}_{j}-\Omega_{B}(z_{i})|^{2}}.

(6.29)

It only remains to control the terms on the right-hand side of (

\left|\frac{\eta_{i}^{2}}{|z_{i}|^{2}}\re(\widehat{a}_{j}-\Omega_{\beta}(E_{+}))^{-1}\right|\leq C\frac{\eta_{i}^{2}}{\im\Omega_{B}(z_{i})}\leq C\eta_{i}^{2}N^{1-6\epsilon}\leq CN^{-1+6\epsilon+3\delta},

where we used the definition of
\eta_{i}

2.8

The other two terms can be analyzed similarly and we refer the readers to Section F of

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{i}\widehat{\lambda_{i}}}{|z_{i}|^{2}}\operatorname{Im}\left(\widehat{a}_{j}-\Omega_{B}(z_{i})\right)^{-1}\sim\eta_{i}\frac{\operatorname{Im}\Omega_{B}(z_{i})}{|\widehat{a}_{j}-\Omega_{B}(z_{i})|^{2}}.

Consequently, we see that for some constant

(6.30)

In this subsection, we complete the proof of Theorem

As discussed earlier, it suffices to prove Proposition

S_{0},

S_{1}

S^{c}

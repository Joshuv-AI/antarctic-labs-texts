Abstract
The Grover search algorithm performs an unstructured search of a marked item in a database quadratically faster than classical algorithms and is shown to be optimal. Here, we show that if the search space is divided into two blocks with the local query operators and the global operators satisfy certain condition, then it is possible to achieve an improvement of bi-quadratic speed-up. Furthermore, we investigate the bi-quadratic speed-up in the presence of noise and show that it can tolerate noisy scenario. This may have potential applications for diverse fields, including database searching, and optimization, where efficient search algorithms play a pivotal role in solving complex computational problems.

II
Bi-Quadratic Improvement in Quantum Search

In search algorithm, we have a function

Our goal is to find

The Grover algorithm fundamentally entails the execution of a series of Grover operators, ultimately finding the desired target state in the requisite number of iterations. Each Grover operator corresponds to a subtle rotation within the two-dimensional subspace.
For

(1)

where

= 2

is the Hadamard transformation. After applying the Hadamard transformation, we apply the Grover Operator

G=-I_{0}I_{y},

(2)

where

and
I_{0}=(I-2|\Psi_{0}\rangle\langle\Psi_{0}|)
.
Geometrically, the Grover operator,

rotates the initial state towards the target state
|y\rangle
. As we proceed through the
k

O
(
\sqrt{N}
) steps. This outcome is particularly remarkable when contrasted with classical search algorithms. The ensuing state is depicted as

|\Psi_{k}\rangle=\frac{cos\theta_{k}}{\sqrt{N-1}}\sum_{x\neq y}^{N-1}|x\rangle+sin\theta_{k}|y\rangle,

(3)

where

=

II.1
Conditional Quantum Search for Pure State

Here, we will introduce the Conditional Quantum Search (CQS) to enhance the speed up of our search within an unsorted database. This constraint imparts a notable improvement, significantly amplifying the speed-up achieved in our search endeavors. This augmentation assumes a heightened significance, especially when our quest aligns with the prescribed criteria, as it empowers us to harness the capabilities of the conditional quantum search algorithm.

Imagine that the marked item has an inherent structure and we are able to partition our target state into two discernible components.
For example, the telephone directory has a person’s name which has the first name and the surname. One can directly search the full name and also search the first name and the surname separately. In the later case, the search space is divided into two blocks and the combination of marked items from both the database give the marked item in the larger database. Under such scenario, we can then effectively apply our tailored searching algorithm. We have provided one example here, but practical instances are abound where this condition can be met.

II.2
Conditional Quantum Search For Mixed State

Here, we will discuss how the conditional quantum search is affected if the initial state is not pure but a mixed state. For the sake of illustration, we consider a pseudo-pure state which is an admixture of pure state and a random state. This kind of state arises naturally in many noisy scenarios.
Suppose the noise parameter, denoted as

\displaystyle I_{Y}(G\otimes G)\rho_{0}(G\otimes G)I_{Y}

\displaystyle=\epsilon|\Psi_{k}\rangle\langle\Psi_{k}|+(1-\epsilon)\frac{I}{N^{2}}.

Bi-Quadratic Speed-up in Noise Scenario

In this section, we delve into the behavior of the final state
|\Psi_{k}\rangle
in the presence of a noisy channel, such as the amplitude damping channel (ADC). Given that our system is inherently multi-level, we employ the multi-level amplitude damping (MAD) channel (referenced as
19

\{|i\rangle\}

i
= 0, 1, 2…,
d-1

is a completely positive trace-preserving mapping that operates on the set

{\cal H}_{A}

\mathcal{D}(\rho)=K_{0}\rho K_{0}^{\dagger}+\sum_{0\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\eta_{ji}|i\rangle\langle i|\langle j|\rho|j\rangle,

(12)

where
K_{0}=|0\rangle\langle 0|+\sum_{1\leq j\leq d-1}\sqrt{1-\kappa_{j}}|j\rangle\langle j|

and
K_{ij}=\sqrt{\eta_{ji}}|i\rangle\langle j|
.

Here,
\rho
is the density matrix and
\eta_{ji}
describes about the decay rate from the j-th to i-th level which follows the conditions: 0
\leq

\eta_{ji}

\leq
1,
\forall

\leq

i

<

j

\leq

d-1

\kappa_{j}
=
\sum_{0\leq j<j}\eta_{ji}

\leq
1,
\forall j
= 1, 2, …,
d-1

If this noisy channel acts on the density matrix

=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\rho_{k})=

\displaystyle\left|\langle 0|\Psi_{k}\rangle\right|^{2}|0\rangle\langle 0|+\sum_{1\leq J\leq N^{2}-1}\left|\langle J|\Psi_{k}\rangle\right|^{2}|J\rangle\langle J|+

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\overline{Y}<J\leq N^{2}-1}\eta_{J\overline{Y}}\left|\langle J|\Psi_{k}\rangle\right|^{2}|\overline{Y}\rangle\langle\overline{Y}|.

Now, measuring the output state of MAD channel

By performing the calculations for all the terms, we ultimately obtain the transformed expression of

\displaystyle\langle Y|\mathcal{D}(\rho_{k})|Y\rangle=

\displaystyle(1-\kappa_{Y})sin^{4}\theta_{k}+

Finally, invoking the definition of the Grover algorithm, for

IV
Conclusion

The Grover search algorithm has undeniably been a cornerstone in the evolution of quantum algorithms, providing a quadratic speed-up. This paper has contributed to the discourse by demonstrating that under specific conditions, leveraging the intrinsic structure of marked items and employing local query operators, the algorithm’s prowess can be elevated to a bi-quadratic speed-up. Our exploration into the algorithm’s resilience in the face of noise underscores its ability to tolerate a noisy scenario. The implications of our findings reverberate across various domains, particularly in conditional database searching and optimization, reaffirming the algorithm’s crucial role in unraveling intricate computational challenges. As quantum computing advances, our study propels the Grover search algorithm from a catalyst of innovation to a versatile tool poised to shape the landscape of future quantum applications.

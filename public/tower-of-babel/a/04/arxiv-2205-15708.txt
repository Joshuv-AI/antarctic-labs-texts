Bifurcation results for quasi-linear operators from the Fučik spectrum of the Laplacian
Emmanuel Wend-Benedo Zongo

Abstract
In this paper, we analyze an eigenvalue problem for a quasi-linear elliptic operators involving Dirichlet boundary condition in an open smooth bounded set of ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} . We investigate a bifurcation results (from trivial solution and from infinity) of an eigenvalue problem involving the ( p , 2 ) (p,2) -Laplace operator, from the Fučik spectrum of the Laplacian. Keywords: quasi-linear operators, Fučik spectrum, bifurcation from trivial solution, bifurcation from infinity, half-eigenvalues. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35J20, 35J92, 35J25.

Introduction

Assume
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
) is an open bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega.
A classical result in the theory of eigenvalue problems guarantees that the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta u=\displaystyle\lambda u~~\text{in $\Omega$},\\
u=\displaystyle 0~~~~~~~~\text{on $\partial\Omega$}\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

possesses a nondecreasing sequence of eigenvalues
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\dots
and a sequence of corresponding eigenfunctions which define a Hilbert basis in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, (see
[
3
]
). Moreover, it is known that the first eigenvalue of problem (
1.1
) is characterized in the variational point of view by,

\lambda_{1}:=\inf_{u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}}\left\{\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}~dx}{\int_{\Omega}u^{2}~dx}\right\}.

In
[
7
]
, the authors investigated the asymptotic behavior of the spectrum and the existence of multiple solutions of the following nonlinear eigenvalue problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u-\Delta u=\lambda u~~~\text{on}~~\Omega,\\
u=0~~~~~\text{on}~~\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
-\Delta_{p}u=-\text{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
denotes the
p
-Laplace operator. In
[
7
]
it was shown that for
p>2
there exist eigenvalue branches emanating from
(\lambda_{k},0),
and for
1<p<2
emanating from
(\lambda_{k},\infty)
. A nonlinear generalization of the spectrum of problem (
1.1
) is the Fučik spectrum, given by the values
(\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
for which the problem
-\Delta u=\lambda_{+}u^{+}-\lambda_{-}u^{-}
with Dirichlet boundary condition has nontrivial solution. The values
\lambda_{+}
and
\lambda_{-}
which are such that
-\Delta u=\lambda_{+}u^{+}-\lambda_{-}u^{-}
has nontrivial solution
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
will be called half-eigenvalues, while the corresponding solutions
u
will be called half-eigenfunctions.
The Fučik spectrum was introduced by S. Fučik
[
13
]
and N. Dancer
[
10
]
in the 70’s, mainly motivated by the works of A. Ambrosetti and G. Prodi
[
2
]
in connection with what is known in the literature as “asymmetric problems".

In this paper we consider the following nonlinear eigenvalue problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u-\Delta u=\lambda_{+}u^{+}-\lambda_{-}u^{-}~~~\text{on}~~\Omega,\\
u=0~~~~~\text{on}~~\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.3)

The operator
-\Delta_{p}-\Delta
appears in quantum field theory (see,
[
4
]
), where it arises in the mathematical description of propagation phenomena of solitary waves. The main purpose of this paper is to study the asymptotic behavior of the spectrum of problem (
1.3
) for all
p>2
and
1<p<2.
We intend to study problem (
1.3
) as a bifurcation in the Fučik eigenvalues from trivial solution if
p>2
and a bifurcation from infinity if
1<p<2
.

In
[
6
]
, the author has proved that the equation

Au-\gamma u^{-}=\lambda u+N(\lambda,u)

(1.4)

has global bifurcation branches emanating from the points
(\lambda^{1}_{k},0)
and
(\lambda^{2}_{k},0)
in
\mathbb{R}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
for any
k\geq 1.
In equation (
1.4
), the operator
A:D(A)\subset L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
is an unbounded self-adjoint operator with compact resolvent and with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\dots,
repeated according to their multiplicity, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfies
0<\gamma<\min\{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k-1},\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}\}
(
k\geq 2
) and
N(\lambda,u)=o(\|u\|)
for
u
near zero uniformly on bounded
\lambda
intervals. The points
\lambda^{1}_{k}
and
\lambda^{2}_{k}
are called split eigenvalues of the operator
A-\gamma(\cdot)^{-}
and their existence was proved by B. Ruf in
[
5
]
. Our work is inspired by the work of
[
6
]
. In the present article, the point
(\lambda^{1}_{k},0)
will be a bifurcation point for positive solutions and the point
(\lambda^{2}_{k},0)
will be a bifurcation point for negative solutions of problem (
1.3
).

A similar structure as (
1.4
) was proved in
[
8
]
concerning bifurcation problems for Pucci’s operators. More precisely in
[
8
]
, the authors studied the following problem as a bifurcation problem from the trivial solution

\begin{cases}-\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2})u=\mu u+f(u)~~~\text{on}~~\Omega,\\
u=0~~~~~\text{on}~~\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.5)

(respectively
\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}
) where
\Omega
is a bounded regular domain, and
\mathcal{M}^{\pm}_{\lambda,\Lambda}
are the extremal Pucci’s operators with parameters
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda.
It is shown that bifurcation occurs form the “half-eigenvalues"
\mu_{k}^{+}
and
\mu_{k}^{-}
.

Our main results are: let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
that satisfies
0<\gamma<\min\{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k-1},\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}\},

k\geq 2

For
p>2,
we obtain bifurcation branches emanating from the points

in

for the operator

•

2
Fučik spectrum and nonlinear spectrum

An eigenvalue
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
of the quasi-linear elliptic operator
-\Delta_{p}-\Delta
on
\Omega
is such that the following problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u-\Delta u=\lambda u~~~\text{on}~~\Omega,\\
u=0~~~~~\text{on}~~\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(2.1)

has a nontrivial solution for
p\in(1,\infty)\backslash\{2\}
. We denote by
\Sigma
the spectrum of problem (
2.1
).
The Fučik spectrum of
-\Delta
on
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary conditions is defined as the set
\mathcal{S}_{(\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-})}
of pairs
(\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda_{+}u^{+}-\lambda_{-}u^{-}~~~\text{on}~~\Omega,\\
u=0~~~~~\text{on}~~\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(2.2)

has a nontrivial solution. Then,
the Fučik spectrum of
-\Delta_{p}-\Delta
on
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary conditions is defined as the set
\Sigma_{(\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-})}
of pairs
(\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u-\Delta u=\lambda_{+}u^{+}-\lambda_{-}u^{-}~~~\text{on}~~\Omega,\\
u=0~~~~~\text{on}~~\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(2.3)

has a nontrivial solution for
p\in(1,\infty)\backslash\{2\}.
Clearly
\Sigma_{(\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-})}
generalizes the notion of the spectrum of problem (
2.1
), since
\Sigma
consists of all values
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that problem (
2.3
) for
\lambda_{+}=\lambda_{-}=\lambda
has a nontrivial solution.
Here we write
u^{\pm}=\max\{\pm u,0\}
in
\Omega,
where
u^{+}
and
u^{-}
denote respectively the positive and the negative part of
u.
We also recall that
u=u^{+}-u^{-}.

2.1
Spectrum of problem (
2.3
)

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)

(
if
p>2

)
or
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)

(
if
1<p<2

)
with
u\not\equiv 0,\lambda_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
, is a weak solution of (
2.3
) if and only if

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi~dx+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi~dx=\lambda_{+}\int_{\Omega}u^{+}\varphi~dx-\lambda_{-}\int_{\Omega}u^{-}\varphi~dx

(2.4)

for all
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)

(
if
p>2

)
,
\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)

(
if
1<p<2

).

The function
u
is called an half-eigenfunction associated with the pair
(\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Remark 2.2
.

For
\lambda_{+}\leq\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{-}\leq\lambda_{1}
, equation (
2.3
) has only zero solution.

2.2
Invertibility property

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p>2.
Then operator
-\Delta_{p}-\Delta-\gamma(\cdot)^{-}
is a global homeomorphism between
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and its dual space for a fixed
\gamma\geq 0.

For the proof of this lemma, we need the following lemma (see
[
12
]
for a proof).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
p>2
. Then there exist two positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
such that, for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
we have :

(a)

(x_{2}-x_{1})\cdot(|x_{2}|^{p-2}x_{2}-|x_{1}|^{p-2}x_{1})\geq c_{1}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{p}

(b)

\left||x_{2}|^{p-2}x_{2}-|x_{1}|^{p-2}x_{1}\right|\leq c_{2}(|x_{2}|+|x_{1}|)^{p-2}|x_{2}-x_{1}|

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Define the quasi-linear operator
\mathcal{M}:W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
(with
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
) by

\langle\mathcal{M}u,v\rangle=\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v~dx+\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v~dx-\gamma\int_{\Omega}u^{-}vdx
for all
u,v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega).

To show that
-\Delta_{p}-\Delta-\gamma(\cdot)^{-}
is a homeomorphism, it is enough to show that
\mathcal{M}
is a continuous strongly monotone operator, (see
[
9
, Corollary 2.5.10]
).

For
p>2,
for all
u,v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, by
(a)
, we get

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{M}u-\mathcal{M}v,u-v\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-v)|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\right)\cdot\nabla(u-v)~dx

\displaystyle-\gamma\int_{\Omega}(u^{-}-v^{-})(u-v)dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-v)|^{2}dx+c_{1}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-v)|^{p}dx-\gamma\int_{\Omega}(u^{-}-v^{-})(u-v)dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-v)|^{2}dx+c_{1}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-v)|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}\|u-v\|^{p}_{1,p}.

Thus
\mathcal{M}
is a strongly monotone operator. We claim that
\mathcal{M}
is a continuous operator from
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
to
W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega).
Indeed, assume that
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega).
We have to show that
\|\mathcal{M}u_{n}-\mathcal{M}u\|_{W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty.
Using
(b)
, Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding theorem, one has

\displaystyle\left|\langle\mathcal{M}u_{n}-\mathcal{M}u,v\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left||\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right||\nabla v|dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u_{n}-u)||\nabla v|dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\gamma|u_{n}^{-}-u^{-}||v|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|+|\nabla u|\right)^{p-2}|\nabla(u_{n}-u)||\nabla v|dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u_{n}-u)||\nabla v|dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\gamma|u_{n}-u||v|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|+|\nabla u|\right)^{p}dx\right)^{(p-2)/p}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u_{n}-u)|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle c_{3}\|u_{n}-u\|_{1,2}\|v\|_{1,2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{4}(\|u_{n}\|_{1,p}+\|u\|_{1,p})^{p-2}\|u_{n}-u\|_{1,p}\|v\|_{1,p}+c_{5}\|u_{n}-u\|_{1,p}\|v\|_{1,p}.

Thus
\|\mathcal{M}u_{n}-\mathcal{M}u\|_{W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0
, as
n\rightarrow+\infty,
and hence
\mathcal{M}
is a homeomorphism.
∎

3
Bifurcation results

Here we show bifurcation results from trivial solutions and from infinity of problem (
2.1
) for
p>2
and
1<p<2
respectively.

3.1
Preliminary results toward the bifurcation results

Let
p>2
, then for
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega),
we have

-\Delta_{p}u-\Delta u=\lambda_{+}u^{+}-\lambda_{-}u^{-}\Leftrightarrow-\Delta_{p}u-\Delta u-(\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-})u^{-}=\lambda_{+}u.

We fix
\gamma=\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}>0
and we consider
\lambda:=\lambda_{+}
as a parameter of the equation

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u-\Delta u-\gamma u^{-}=\lambda u~~~\text{on}~~\Omega,\\
u=0~~~~~~~~~~~\text{on}~~\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(3.1)

Using Lemma
2.3
, we write equation (
3.1
) in the following form:

u=\lambda(-\Delta)^{-1}u+\left\{(-\Delta_{p}-\Delta-\gamma(\cdot)^{-})^{-1}-(-\Delta)^{-1}\right\}(\lambda u),

(3.2)

where we consider

(-\Delta_{p}-\Delta-\gamma(\cdot)^{-})^{-1}:L^{2}(\Omega)\subset W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\subset L^{2}(\Omega),

and

(-\Delta)^{-1}:L^{2}(\Omega)\subset W^{-1,2}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\subset L^{2}(\Omega).

So, for
p>2
the mapping

(-\Delta_{p}-\Delta-\gamma(\cdot)^{-})^{-1}-(-\Delta)^{-1}:L^{2}(\Omega)\subset W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\subset L^{2}(\Omega)

is a compact mapping thanks to the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem. For simplicity, we write equation (
3.2
) as follows

u=\lambda Au+T_{\lambda}(u),

(3.3)

with
Au=(-\Delta)^{-1}
and
T_{\lambda}(u)=\left\{(-\Delta_{p}-\Delta-\gamma(\cdot)^{-})^{-1}-(-\Delta)^{-1}\right\}(\lambda u).

The
L^{2}(\Omega)
space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{2}
admits the orthogonal decomposition

L^{2}(\Omega)=[e_{k}]\oplus[e_{k}]^{\perp},

(3.4)

where
e_{k}
is the normalized
k
-th eigenfunction associated to a simple eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
of the Dirichlet Laplace operator (see
[
5
]
), and
[e_{k}]
denotes the eigenspace of
\lambda_{k}.
In view of (
3.4
), we set
H_{k}=[e_{k}]^{\perp}
and
E_{k}=[e_{k}]
for simplicity. An element of
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
in (
3.4
) will be seen as
u=\alpha e_{k}+v,
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
v\in H_{k}.

The following result was obtained by B. Ruf in
[
5
]
. If
\gamma\geq 0
satisfies

0<\gamma<\min\{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k-1},\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}\},

(3.5)

then the following nonlinear eigenvalue problem

-\Delta u-\gamma u^{-}=\lambda u,

has a unique split eigenvalue
(\lambda^{1}_{k},\lambda^{2}_{k})\in[\lambda_{k},\lambda_{k+1}]\times[\lambda_{k},\lambda_{k+1}]
of
-\Delta-\gamma(\cdot)^{-}
and uniquely determined eigenfunctions
v^{1}_{k},v^{2}_{k}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

-\Delta v^{i}_{k}-\gamma(v^{i}_{k})^{-}=\lambda^{i}_{k}v^{i}_{k},~~~~i=1,2.

For
\gamma=0
the
k
-th split eigenvalue reduces to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
and
v^{1}_{k},v^{2}_{k}
to the eigenfunctions
e_{k}
,
-e_{k}
respectively.

Consider

\begin{cases}-\Delta u-\gamma u^{-}=\lambda u~~~\text{on}~~\Omega,\\
u=0~~~~~~~~~~~\text{on}~~\partial\Omega,~~~~\text{with}~~\gamma>0.\end{cases}

(3.6)

We say that
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
with
u\not\equiv 0
is a weak solution of (
3.6
) if and only if

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v~dx-\gamma\int_{\Omega}u^{-}v~dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}uv~dx,

(3.7)

for all
v\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega).

Remark 3.1
.

Suppose that
u
is an eigenfunction associated to
\lambda
in (
3.7
). Then
\alpha u
is also an eigenfunction associated to
\lambda
for
\alpha>0.

From this remark, we aim to show that problem (
3.1
) has bifurcation branches emanating from the points
(\lambda^{1}_{k},0)
and
(\lambda^{2}_{k},0)
in
\mathbb{R}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
in the direction
\alpha>0
when
p>2.

In what follows, we assume that (
3.5
) holds. Let
\eta:=\frac{1}{2}\min\{|(e_{k},v^{1}_{k}(\gamma))_{2}|,|(e_{k},v^{2}_{k}(\gamma))_{2}|\}>0,
and define for
\rho\in\mathbb{R},

\rho>0,

\boldsymbol{K^{+}_{\rho,\eta}}(\gamma):=\{(\lambda,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times L^{2}(\Omega):~|\lambda-\lambda^{1}_{k}(\gamma)|<\rho,~~(e_{k},u)_{2}>\eta\|u\|_{2}\},

\boldsymbol{K^{-}_{\rho,\eta}}(\gamma):=\{(\lambda,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times L^{2}(\Omega):~|\lambda-\lambda^{2}_{k}(\gamma)|<\rho,~~(e_{k},u)_{2}<-\eta\|u\|_{2}\},

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{2}
denotes the
L^{2}(\Omega)
inner product. Let

B_{\rho}:=\{(\lambda,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times L^{2}(\Omega):~(|\lambda|^{2}+\|u\|^{2}_{2})^{1/2}<\rho\}.

We see that both
\boldsymbol{K^{+}_{\rho,\eta}}(\gamma)
and
\boldsymbol{K}^{-}_{\rho,\eta}(\gamma)
are two disjoints convex cones.

For
\gamma>0
fixed, let
\Sigma_{\gamma}
denote the closure of the set of nontrivial solutions of problem (
3.1
). The following lemma localizes the possible solutions of equation (
3.1
). We refer to
[
11
, Lemma 1.24]
for similar results.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists
\rho_{0}>0
such that for all
0<\rho<\rho_{0}:

\left(\Sigma_{\gamma}\backslash\{(\lambda^{1}_{k},0)\}\cap B_{\rho}\right)\subset\boldsymbol{K^{+}_{\rho,\eta}}(\gamma).

If
(\lambda,u)\in\left(\Sigma_{\gamma}\backslash\{(\lambda^{1}_{k},0)\}\cap B_{\rho}\right),
then
u=\alpha e_{k}+v,
where
|\alpha|>\eta\|u\|_{2},
and
|\lambda-\lambda^{1}_{k}|=o(1),v=o(\alpha)
for
\alpha
near zero. Moreover
\alpha>0.
The same result holds for

\boldsymbol{K^{-}_{\rho,\eta}}(\gamma).

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that there is no
\rho_{0}
as in Lemma
3.2
. Then there exist two sequences
\rho_{n}
and
(\lambda_{n},u_{n})\in\left(\Sigma_{\gamma}\backslash\{(\lambda^{1}_{k},0)\}\cap B_{\rho_{n}}\right)
such that
\rho_{n}\rightarrow 0,

|\lambda_{n}-\lambda^{1}_{k}|\leq\rho_{n}
,
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
and
|(e_{k},u_{n})_{2}|\leq\eta\|u_{n}\|_{2}
as
n\rightarrow\infty.
Since
u_{n}\not\equiv 0,
we introduce the following change of variable
\bar{u}_{n}=u_{n}/\|u_{n}\|_{2}
in the following equation

-\Delta_{p}u_{n}-\Delta u_{n}-\gamma(u_{n})^{-}=\lambda_{n}u_{n}.

Then it follows that
-\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}_{2}\Delta_{p}\bar{u}_{n}-\Delta\bar{u}_{n}-\gamma(\bar{u}_{n})^{-}=\lambda_{n}\bar{u}_{n},
for
p>2.
Let
\mu\in\varrho(-\Delta)
(the resolvent set of
-\Delta
), hence

\bar{u}_{n}=(-\Delta-\mu)^{-1}[(\lambda_{n}-\mu)\bar{u}_{n}+\gamma(\bar{u}_{n})^{-}+\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}_{2}\Delta_{p}\bar{u}_{n}].

Using the fact that
(-\Delta-\mu)^{-}
is compact, then for a subsequence,
\bar{u}_{n}\rightarrow\bar{u}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
n\rightarrow\infty,
and the with the continuity of
\Delta_{p}
we find that

\bar{u}=(-\Delta-\mu)^{-1}[(\lambda^{1}_{k}-\mu)\bar{u}+\gamma(\bar{u})^{-}],

since
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\lambda^{1}_{k}
and
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty.
This implies that
\bar{u}=v^{1}_{k}
(or possibly
\bar{u}=v^{2}_{k}
if
\lambda^{1}_{k}=\lambda^{2}_{k}
). Thus we get
2\eta\leq|(e_{k},\bar{u})|\leq\eta
since
|(e_{k},u_{n})_{2}|\leq\eta\|u_{n}\|_{2}.
This is a contradiction. Hence there exists
\rho_{0}
as above and for
0<\rho<\rho_{0},
if
(\lambda,u)\in\left(\Sigma_{\gamma}\backslash\{(\lambda^{1}_{k},0)\}\cap B_{\rho}\right),
then
u=\alpha e_{k}+v
with
|\alpha|>\eta\|u\|_{2}.
We have
\|v\|_{2}\leq|\alpha|+\|u\|_{2}\leq|\alpha|+|\alpha|/\eta
and
|\lambda-\lambda^{1}_{k}|<\rho\rightarrow 0
. So, for
\alpha
near zero, we have
|\lambda-\lambda^{1}_{k}|=o(1)
and
v=o(\alpha).

∎

Remark 3.3
.

The
\rho_{0}
in Lemma
3.2
can be chosen indepently of
\gamma\in[0,\bar{\gamma}]
, where
\bar{\gamma}
satisfies (

\eta:=\frac{1}{2}\inf\limits_{\gamma\in[0,\bar{\gamma}]}\{|(e_{k},v^{1}_{k}(\gamma))_{2}|,|(e_{k},v^{2}_{k}(\gamma))_{2}|\}.

We employ a Lyapunov-Schmidt method, that is, we write equation (
3.3
) as equivalent system in
\mathbb{R}\times H_{k}
and
\mathbb{R}\times E_{k}.
We denote by
P_{k}
and
Q_{k}
the orthogonal projections onto
H_{k}
and
E_{k}
respectively, that is

and

We write equation (

) in

\alpha e_{k}=\lambda\alpha Ae_{k}+Q_{k}(T_{\lambda}(\alpha e_{k}+v)).\end{cases}

We set now,

Then the solutions of equation (

) are the zeros of

and conversely. The trivial solutions of (

) and (

for fixed

3.3
Bifurcation from infinity

Here, we prove a bifurcation from infinity result. As in
[
7
]
, we introduce the following change of variable:
for
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega),~u\neq 0,
we set
v=u/\|u\|_{1,2}^{2-\frac{1}{2}p}
for all
1<p<2.
We have
\|v\|_{1,2}=\frac{1}{\|u\|_{1,2}^{1-\frac{1}{2}p}}
and

|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v=\frac{1}{\|u\|_{1,2}^{(2-\frac{1}{2}p)(p-1)}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u.

Introducing this change of variable in (
2.4
), we find that,

\|u\|_{1,2}^{(2-\frac{1}{2}p)(p-2)}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi~dx+\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi~dx=\lambda_{+}\int_{\Omega}v^{+}\varphi~dx-\lambda_{-}\int_{\Omega}v^{-}\varphi~dx

(3.15)

for every
\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
But, on the other hand, we have

\|v\|^{p-4}_{1,2}=\frac{1}{\|u\|_{1,2}^{(1-\frac{1}{2}p)(p-4)}}=\frac{1}{\|u\|_{1,2}^{(2-\frac{1}{2}p)(p-2)}}.

Consequently it follows that equation (
3.15
) is equivalent to

\|v\|_{1,2}^{4-p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi~dx+\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi~dx=\lambda_{+}\int_{\Omega}v^{+}\varphi~dx-\lambda_{-}\int_{\Omega}v^{-}\varphi~dx,

(3.16)

for every
\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
This leads to the following nonlinear eigenvalue problem (
1<p<2
)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\|v\|_{1,2}^{4-p}\Delta_{p}v-\Delta v=\lambda_{+}v^{+}-\lambda_{-}v^{-}~~\text{in $\Omega$},\\
v=\displaystyle 0~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{on $\partial\Omega$}.\end{array}\right.

(3.17)

Remark 3.5
.

With this transformation, we have that the pair

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\gamma(u^{-}-v^{-},u-v)_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|u-v\|^{2}_{1,2}+\|u\|_{1,2}^{4-p}\left((-\Delta_{p}u)-(-\Delta_{p}v),u-v\right)_{2}

small enough. Hence, the operator

Irreducibility of random polynomials of ℤ ⁡ [ X ] \mathds{Z}[X]
Pierre-Alexandre Bazin

Abstract
In [ 1 ] , Bary-Soroker, Koukoulopoulos and Kozma proved that when A A is a random monic polynomial of ℤ ⁡ [ X ] \mathds{Z}[X] of deterministic degree n n with coefficients a j a_{j} drawn independently according to measures μ j , \mu_{j}, then A A is irreducible with probability tending to 1 1 as n → ∞ n\to\infty under a condition of near-uniformity of the μ j \mu_{j} modulo four primes (which notably happens when the μ j \mu_{j} are uniform over a segment of ℤ \mathds{Z} of length N ≥ 35 . N\geq 35. ) We improve here the speed of convergence when we have the near-uniformity condition modulo more primes. Notably, we obtain the optimal bound ℙ ⁡ ( A ​ irreducible ) = 1 − O ⁡ ( 1 / n ) \mathds{P}(A\text{ irreducible})=1-O(1/\sqrt{n}) when we have near-uniformity modulo twelve primes, which notably happens when the μ j \mu_{j} are uniform over a segment of length N ≥ 10 8 . N\geq 10^{8}.

Introduction

1.1
Main results

We consider
A
a random monic polynomial in
\mathds{Z}[X]
of determinisitic degree
n
with independently chosen coefficients. Formally, we introduce measures
(\mu_{j})_{0\leq j<n}
on
\mathds{Z}
(generally taken all equal to the same measure
\mu
), and we define our random monic polynomial

A(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}X^{j}\in\mathds{Z}[X],

with
a_{n}=1,a_{j}\sim\mu_{j}
for all
j<n
and the
a_{j}
are independent.

When the
\mu_{j}
are all taken equal to the same
\mu
uniform over
\{0,1\},
the problem of the irreduciblity of
A
when
n\to\infty
has been first studied by Konyagin
[
5
]
. The latest results have been achieved by Bary-Soroker, Koukoulopoulos and Kozma
[
1
]
:

Theorem 1

(Bary-Soroker, Koukoulopoulos and Kozma
[
1
]
)
.

Assume the
\mu_{j}
are all the same and uniform over a segment
\llbracket a,a+N-1\rrbracket
of length
N\geq 2.
Then there are absolute constants
c,K,\delta>0
such that when
n\geq(\log(a+N))^{3},

\overline{\mathds{P}}(A\text{ has a factor of degree }\leq cn)\leq\mathds{P}(a_{0}=0)+Kn^{-\delta},

where
\overline{\mathds{P}}(\mathcal{E}):=1-\mathds{P}(\mathcal{E})
for an event
\mathcal{E}.

When
N\geq 35,
we further get

\overline{\mathds{P}}(A\text{ irreducible})\leq\mathds{P}(a_{0}=0)+Kn^{-\delta}.

Remark
.

The
\mathds{P}(a_{0}=0)
term corresponds to the probability that
X
divides
A,
which does not depend on
n
(and is equal to
0
or
1/N
depending on
a
).

Building on the ideas from this paper, we achieve a better convergence speed, namely :

Theorem 2
.

Assume the
\mu_{j}
are all the same and uniform over a segment
\llbracket a,a+N-1\rrbracket
of length
N\geq 2.
Then there are absolute constants
c,K>0
such that when
n\geq(\log(|a|+N))^{3},

\overline{\mathds{P}}(A\text{ has a factor of degree }\leq cn)\leq\mathds{P}(a_{0}=0)+K/\sqrt{n}.

When
N\geq 10^{8},
we further get

\overline{\mathds{P}}(A\text{ irreducible})\leq\mathds{P}(a_{0}=0)+K/\sqrt{n}.

Remark
.

The
O(1/\sqrt{n})
bound here is optimal : indeed,
X+1
divides
A
with probability
\mathds{P}(A(-1)=0),
which decreases as
1/\sqrt{n}
when
N
is fixed. The definition below will allow us to write a stronger version of Theorem
2

Definition 1
.

A polynomial

can be decomposed as
\Phi\cdot P
where
P
is irreducible,
\Phi
has degree
\leq d

When we have such a decomposition but

is only known to have no factor of degree
\leq c,
we say

has
d
-nearly no factor of degree
\leq c.

In fact, Breuillard and Varjù
[

]
proved the irreducibility result from Theorem
2
for all

under the generalised Riemann Hypothesis (GRH). Ignoring small cyclotomic polynomials using Definition
1
, they achieved the much stronger convergence speed (under GRH) :

\overline{\mathds{P}}(A\text{ is $C\sqrt{n}$-near-irreducible})\leq K\mathrm{e}^{-c\sqrt{n}/\log n}

with absolute constants
c,C,K
when
A
is drawn as in theorem
2
with
N\geq 2
and degree
n\geq(\log(|a|+N))^{10}.

Without the Riemann hypothesis, we achieve here the following weaker results :

1.2
A master theorem

We give the main result with general measures
\mu_{j},
from which we will derive Theorem
3
. We first recall a definition :

Definition 2
.

For a measure
\mu
on
\mathds{Z},
we note

\widehat{\mu}:\theta\in\mathds{R}/\mathds{Z}\mapsto\mathds{E}_{\mu}(\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\pi\theta Y})=\sum_{j\in\mathds{Z}}\mu(j)\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\pi\theta j}

its Fourier transform.

Theorem 4
.

Let
A
a random monic polynomial of degree
n
with independent coefficients following probability measures
\mu_{j}
and
P=p_{1}\ldots p_{r}
a product of
r\geq 4
distinct primes.

Assume we can find an integer
s\geq 1
(independent of
n
) such that for all
j
and
Q,R,\ell
with
QR=P,\ Q>1
and
\ell\in\mathds{Z}/R\mathds{Z},
we have

\sum_{k\in\mathds{Z}/Q\mathds{Z}}|\widehat{\mu_{j}}(k/Q+\ell/R)|^{s}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{\log n}\right)\sqrt{Q}.

Then when
n\geq P^{4}
and
n_{1}\leq n/2s,
we have

\mathds{P}(\exists D|A\text{ of degree }\in[n_{1},n/2s])=O_{r,s}\left(n_{1}^{-rQ\left(\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}\right)+o(1)}\right),

with
Q(t):=t\log t-t+1=\int_{1}^{t}\log u\,\,\mathrm{d}u.

Here, the condition on the
\widehat{\mu_{j}}
translates to a kind of near-uniformity modulo
P
: indeed, the sum is minimised when
\mu
is uniform mod
P,
in which case we have
|\widehat{\mu_{j}}(a/P)|=0
for all
a\neq 0\mod P.
We show below the condition of Theorem
4
is verified with
s=1
when the
\mu_{j}
are uniform over a segment of large enough length
N.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\mu
an uniform measure over a segment
\llbracket a,a+N-1\rrbracket
of
\mathds{Z}
of length
N,
and
Q
a positive integer. Then for any
\theta_{0}\in\mathds{R}/\mathds{Z},
we have

\sum_{k\in\mathds{Z}/Q\mathds{Z}}|\widehat{\mu}(k/Q+\theta_{0})|\leq 1+\frac{Q(\log(Q-1)+2)}{N}.

Proof.

Here, for
\theta\neq 0\mod 1,
we have

\widehat{\mu}(\theta)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=a}^{a+N-1}\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\pi j\theta}=\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\pi a\theta}\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\pi N\theta}}{1-\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\pi\theta}},

so that

|\widehat{\mu}(\theta)|\leq\frac{2}{N|1-\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\pi\theta}|}=\frac{1}{N|\sin\pi\theta|}.

We also always have
|\widehat{\mu}(\theta)|\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=a}^{a+N-1}|\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\pi j\theta}|=1
for all
\theta.

Now, notice that the sum we want to bound does not change when
\theta_{0}
is translated by a multiple of
1/Q.
Thus, we can suppose without loss of generality that
\theta_{0}\in]-1/2Q,1/2Q],
so that when
k\in[-Q/2,Q/2]
with
|k|\geq 1,
we have

|\sin\pi(k/Q+\theta_{0})|\geq\min_{(k-1/2)/Q\leq\theta\leq(k+1/2)/Q}|\sin\pi\theta|=\sin\pi\frac{|k|-1/2}{Q}\geq\frac{2|k|-1}{Q}

(using
\sin x\geq\frac{2}{\pi}x
in
[0,\pi/2]
).

Then, rewriting the sum over
\mathds{Z}/Q\mathds{Z}
as a sum over integers in
]-Q/2,Q/2],
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathds{Z}/Q\mathds{Z}}|\widehat{\mu}(k/Q+\theta_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq|\widehat{\mu}(\theta_{0})|+\sum_{1\leq|k|\leq Q/2}\frac{1}{N\sin\pi(k/Q+\theta_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{1\leq|k|\leq Q/2}\frac{Q}{N(2|k|-1)}

\displaystyle=1+2\frac{Q}{2N}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor Q/2\rfloor}\frac{1}{k-1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{Q}{N}\left(2+\int_{1/2}^{Q/2-1/2}\frac{1}{t}\,\,\mathrm{d}t\right)=1+\frac{Q(\log(Q-1)+2)}{N}.

∎

In the more general setting of Theorem

]

Proposition 6
.

Suppose all the
\mu_{j}

Remark
.

This result is not directly stated anywhere in
[
1
]
, but appears in the proof of
[
1
]
[Proposition 2.1], which combines it with a weaker result on cyclotomic divisors. Here, we excluded cyclotomic divisors with our definition of near-irreducibility and will only bring them back for the proof of Theorem
2
.

and
3
using Theorem

. We first prove a weaker version of the second part of Theorem

, where

Lemma 7
.

Let
A
a random monic polynomial of
\mathds{Z}[X]
whose coefficients are drawn independently and uniformly over a fixed segment
\llbracket a,a+N-1\rrbracket
of length
N\geq 2
(as in Theorems
2
and
3
). We further fix constants
C,\varepsilon>0.
Then for some
c
depending on
C
and
\varepsilon,
we have for
n\geq(\log(|a|+N))^{3},

\overline{\mathds{P}}(A\text{ has $n^{\varepsilon}$-nearly no factor of degree }\leq cn)=O_{C,\varepsilon}(n^{-C}),

where "
n^{\varepsilon}
-nearly" has been defined in Definition
1
.

Proof.

We can assume
\varepsilon<1/10,
as reducing
\varepsilon
only strengthens the "
n^{\varepsilon}
-nearly" condition. Fix
r\geq 4
such that
rQ\left(\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}\right)>C/\varepsilon,
and
P=p_{1}\ldots p_{r}
the product of the
r
smallest prime numbers.

Now, let
\mu
the uniform measure on
\llbracket a,a+N-1\rrbracket
(which depends on
N
and
a
), and note that for all
\theta\neq 0\mod 1,
we have
\max_{N\geq 2}|\hat{\mu}(\theta)|<1
(this is a
\max
because
|\hat{\mu}(\theta)|
does not depend on
a
and tends to
0
when
N\to\infty
), so that
|\hat{\mu}(\theta)|^{s}\to 0
when
s\to\infty,
uniformly in
N
and
a.
Thus, for any fixed
Q,R=P/Q,\ell\in\mathds{Z}/R\mathds{Z},
for
s
large enough (depending only on
Q,R,\ell
), we have

\sum_{k\in\mathds{Z}/Q\mathds{Z}}|\hat{\mu_{j}}(k/Q+\ell/R)|^{s}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{4\log P}\right)\sqrt{Q}.

As
P
has a finite number of divisors, we can then fix
s
(depending only on
P,
which depends on
C
and
\varepsilon
) which verifies the condition above for all
Q
dividing
P
and
\ell\in\mathds{Z}/R\mathds{Z}.
Then with
c:=1/2s,
Theorem
4
with
n_{1}=n^{\varepsilon}
gives us for
n\geq P^{4},

\mathds{P}(\exists D|A\text{ of degree }\in[n^{\varepsilon},cn])=O_{r,s}\left(n^{-\varepsilon rQ\left(\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}\right)+o(1)}\right)=O_{C,\varepsilon}(n^{-C}).

As
P
depends only on
C
and
\varepsilon,
and probabilities are always bounded by

we can adjust the implicit constant of the big-Oh term to extend it to the values of

as well.

Furthermore, Proposition
6
, which applies with
c=1/2
as
\mu
doesn’t take any value with probability higher than
1/N\leq 1/2,
gives us (as
\varepsilon\leq 1/10
) when
n\geq(\log(|a|+N))^{3},

\mathds{P}(A\text{ has a non-$X,$ non-cyclotomic irreducible factor of degree }\leq n^{\varepsilon})=O(\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{n}})=O_{C}(n^{-C}).

Finally, when the two conditions above don’t hold,
A
has
n^{\varepsilon}
-nearly no factor of degree
\leq cn.
Indeed, writing
A=\Phi\cdot P
with
\Phi
being the product of all irreducible factors of
A
that are either
X
or cyclotomic of degree
\leq n^{\varepsilon},
the absence of non-
X
and non-cyclotomic irreducible factor of degree
\leq n^{\varepsilon}
tells us
P
has no factor in this degree range (any factor would be in
\Phi
), and the absence of factor of degree in
[n^{\varepsilon},cn]
tells us that
P
has no factor in this degree range either. Moreover, if we had
\deg\Phi\geq n^{\varepsilon},
as
\Phi
is a product of irreducibles of degree
\leq n^{\varepsilon},

\Phi
(and thus
A
) would have a factor of degree in
[n^{\varepsilon},2n^{\varepsilon}]\subset[n^{\varepsilon},cn]
(as soon as
n
is large enough).

Thus, combining the results we got from Theorem
4
and Proposition
6
, we get for
n\geq(\log(|a|+N))^{3},

\displaystyle\overline{\mathds{P}}

\displaystyle(A\text{ has $n^{\varepsilon}$-nearly no factor of degree }\leq cn)

\displaystyle\leq\mathds{P}(\exists D|A\text{ of degree }\in[n^{\varepsilon},cn])+\mathds{P}(A\text{ has a non-$X,$ non-cyclotomic irreducible factor of degree }\leq n^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=O_{C,\varepsilon}(n^{-C}).

∎

.

. For the first part, fix

(1)

To derive Theorem
2
, we have to bound the probability of small cyclotomic divisors.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Here, Theorem
3
applies with
C=1/2
(with
r=12,

P=2\cdot 3\cdot\ldots\cdot 37\simeq 7.4\cdot 10^{12}
and
N_{0}=10^{8},
we indeed have
rQ\left(\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}\right)=0.56\ldots\geq 1/2
and
N\geq f(P).
) We apply it with an arbitrary
\varepsilon:=1/10
to get

\overline{\mathds{P}}(A\text{ is $n^{1/10}$-near-irreducible})=O(1/\sqrt{n})

if
N\geq N_{0},
or

\overline{\mathds{P}}(A\text{ has $n^{1/10}$-nearly no factor of degree }\leq cn)=O(1/\sqrt{n})

for some absolute constant
c
otherwise.

To conclude, it is then sufficient to prove

\mathds{P}(A\text{ has a cyclotomic factor of degree }\leq n^{\varepsilon})=O(1/\sqrt{n}).

For any fixed cyclotomic polynomial
\Phi_{d}
of degree
m=\varphi(d),
as
\Phi_{d}|X^{d}-1,
we have

A\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}a^{\prime}_{i}X^{i}\mod\Phi_{d},

where

a^{\prime}_{i}=\sum_{j=i\mod d}a_{j}.

Now, as
\Phi_{d}
has degree
m,
for any choice of the
a^{\prime}_{i},i\geq m,
there is only one choice of the
a^{\prime}_{i},i<m
such that
\Phi_{d}|A.
As the
a^{\prime}_{i}
are independent, we thus get

\mathds{P}(\Phi_{d}|A)\leq\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}\sup_{x}\mathds{P}(a^{\prime}_{i}=x).

But with
Q(Y;\delta):=\sup_{x}\mathds{P}(|Y-x|\leq\delta)
(for a random variable
Y
), as the
a_{j}
take values in
\mathds{Z}
for all
j,
we get
Q(a_{j},1/2)=\sup_{x}\mathds{P}(a_{j}=x)\leq 1/2.
The Kolmogorov-Rogozin inequality
[
6
]
then gives for all
i,
as
a^{\prime}_{i}
is a sum of
n/d+O(1)
variables
a_{j},

\sup_{x}\mathds{P}(a^{\prime}_{i}=x)=Q(a^{\prime}_{i},1/2)\leq\sqrt{\frac{Kd}{n}}

for a constant
K,
so that (as probabilities are always smaller than
1
)

\mathds{P}(\Phi_{d}|A)\leq\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}\sup_{x}\mathds{P}(a^{\prime}_{i}=x)\leq\left(\min\left\{1;\frac{Kd}{n}\right\}\right)^{m/2}.

Now summing on
d,
we get (as
m=\varphi(d)\geq 2
except for
d=1,2
) :

\displaystyle\mathds{P}(A\text{ has a cyclotomic factor of degree }\leq n^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\varphi(d)\leq n^{\varepsilon}}\mathds{P}(\Phi_{d}|A)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\varphi(d)\leq n^{\varepsilon}}\left(\min\left\{1;\frac{Kd}{n}\right\}\right)^{\varphi(d)/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}(1+\sqrt{2})+\sum_{2\leq\varphi(d)\leq n^{\varepsilon}}\frac{Kd}{n}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}(1+\sqrt{2})+O\left(n^{2\varepsilon-1}\right)

\displaystyle=O(1/\sqrt{n}),

as we fixed
\varepsilon=1/10.

∎

1.3
Definitions and notations

We now give definitions that will be useful for the proof of Theorem
4
. When
p
is a prime, we note
\mathds{F}_{p}[X]^{u}
the set of monic polynomials of
\mathds{F}_{p}[X],
and
\mathds{F}_{p}[X]_{n}^{u}
the set of monic polynomials of
\mathds{F}_{p}[X]
of degree
n.
We now fix
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
distinct primes and define

\mathcal{P}:=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathds{F}_{p_{i}}[X]^{u}

the set of
r
-uples of monic polynomials each in
\mathds{F}_{p_{i}}[X].
We then define multiplication and divisibility in
\mathcal{P}
pointwise (that is,
\mathbf{D}=(D_{1},\ldots,D_{r})
divides
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{r})
if and only if each
D_{i}
divides
A_{i}
in
\mathds{F}_{p_{i}}[X]
). As usual, we use
\mathbf{bold\ characters}
to denote
r
-uples.

Remark
.

Here, we restricted ourselves to monic polynomials, as we are only interested in the multiplicative structure of
\mathcal{P}.

For
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{r})\in\mathcal{P},
we then define its degree
\deg\mathbf{A}:=(\deg A_{1},\ldots,\deg A_{r}),
and its total degree

\Deg\mathbf{A}:=\sum_{i}\deg A_{i}

as well as its norm

\|\mathbf{A}\|:=\prod_{i}p_{i}^{\deg A_{i}}.

For a fixed degree
\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}),
we then define

\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{d}}:=\prod_{i}\mathds{F}_{p_{i}}[X]_{d_{i}}^{u}

the set of degree-
\mathbf{d}
elements in
\mathcal{P}.
Notice all elements in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{d}}
have the same norm
\prod_{i}p_{i}^{d_{i}},
which is also the cardinality of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{d}}.

Here, any element of
(\mathcal{P},\cdot)
can be uniquely factorised as a product of irreducibles, where an irreducible of
\mathcal{P}
is an element of the form
(1,\ldots,1,I_{i},1,\ldots,1)
with
I_{i}
irreducible in
\mathds{F}_{p_{i}}[X]
(indeed, the factorisation is obtained as the product of factorisations of each component). For
\mathbf{I}
an irreducible of
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{P},
we note
\nu_{\mathbf{I}}(\mathbf{A})
the number of times
\mathbf{I}
appears as a factor in
\mathbf{A}.
We also note

\tau(\mathbf{A}):=\prod_{\mathbf{I}\text{ irreducible}}(\nu_{\mathbf{I}}(\mathbf{A})+1)

the number of divisors of
\mathbf{A}
in
\mathcal{P}.

We denote by

\mathbf{X}_{i}=(1,\ldots,1,X\in\mathds{F}_{p_{i}}[X],1,\ldots,1)

the irreducible obtained for
I_{i}=X
above. In particular, for any
i,
we have
\Deg\mathbf{X}_{i}=1,\|\mathbf{X}_{i}\|=p_{i},
and
\mathbf{X}_{i}
divides
\mathbf{B}\in\mathcal{P}
if and only if
X
divides
\mathbf{B}_{i}
in
\mathds{F}_{p_{i}}[X].
We note
\mathcal{P}^{X}
(resp.
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{d}}^{X}
) the set of elements of
\mathcal{P}
(resp.
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{d}}
) that are not divisible by any
\mathbf{X}_{i}.

We give now a few more definitions :

Definition 3
.

Given
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{r})
a random variable in
\mathcal{P},
we define the spread to uniformity modulo polynomials of degree
\leq m
of
\mathbf{A}
by

\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(m):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{B}\in\mathcal{P}^{X}\\
\max\deg\mathbf{B}\leq m\end{subarray}}\left|\mathds{P}(\mathbf{B}|\mathbf{A})-\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{B}\|}\right|.

Remark
.

We needed to exclude here the factors
\mathbf{B}
multiples of some
\mathbf{X}_{i}
from the sum, as they would otherwise contribute too much when the
A_{i}
are taken equal to
A\,\mathrm{mod}\,p_{i}
where
A
is the random polynomial from Theorem
4
.

Definition 4
.

For a fixed degree
m\in\mathds{N},
we define
\mathcal{I}_{m}
the set of irreducibles of
\mathcal{P}
of degree
\leq m
and not of the form
\mathbf{X}_{i},
and

\Sigma_{m}:=\sum_{\mathbf{I}\in\mathcal{I}_{m}}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{I}\|}\,,

\Pi_{m}:=\prod_{\mathbf{I}\in\mathcal{I}_{m}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{I}\|}\right).

For
\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{P},
we then define its
m
-friable part

\mathbf{A}_{\leq m}=\prod_{\mathbf{I}\in\mathcal{I}_{m}}\mathbf{I}^{\nu_{\mathbf{I}}(\mathbf{A})},

and its
m
-non-friable part

\mathbf{A}_{>m}=\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_{\leq m}=\prod_{\mathbf{I}\not\in\mathcal{I}_{m}}\mathbf{I}^{\nu_{\mathbf{I}}(\mathbf{A})}.

We say
\mathbf{A}
is
m
-friable if
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{A}_{\leq m}
(that is, all its irreducible factors are in
\mathcal{I}_{m}
).

Finally, when
\mathbf{A}
is a random variable in
\mathcal{P},
we define the event
\mathcal{E}_{m}
"
\mathbf{A}
does not have many factors of low degree" by

\mathcal{E}_{m}:=\{\Deg\mathbf{A}_{\leq m}\leq m(\Sigma_{m}-2),\tau(\mathbf{A}_{\leq m})\leq\mathrm{e}^{(1-1/r)\Sigma_{m}}\}.

Remark
.

Here, we needed to exclude the
\mathbf{X}_{i}
factors from the
m
-friable part as our bound on
\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(m)
gives no control over these.

We can estimate
\Sigma_{m}
and
\Pi_{m}
using the Prime Polynomial Theorem :

Lemma 8
.

We have
r(\log m-2)\leq\Sigma_{m}\leq r(\log m+1),
and
\Pi_{m}\leq\mathrm{e}^{2r}m^{-r}.

Proof.

For any prime
p,
the Prime Polynomial Theorem gives the estimate

p^{k}/k-2p^{k/2}/k\leq\#\{I\in\mathds{F}_{p}[X]\text{ monic irreducible of degree }k\}\leq p^{k}/k

on the number of irreducibles of degree
k.
We thus have

\displaystyle\Sigma_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{i}^{k}}\#\{I\in\mathds{F}_{p_{i}}[X]\text{ monic irreducible of degree }k,I\neq X\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(\frac{1}{k}-\varepsilon_{k,i}\right),

with
0\leq\varepsilon_{k,i}\leq 2p_{i}^{-k/2}/k\leq p_{i}^{-k/2}
when
k\geq 2
and
\varepsilon_{1,i}=1/p_{i}\leq p_{i}^{-1/2}
when
k=1
(here we treated separately
k=1
in order to exclude the polynomial
X
), so that

0\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k,i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{i}}-1}\leq\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}-1}.

As we always have
r(\log m+\gamma)\leq\sum_{i}\sum_{k}\frac{1}{k}\leq r(\log m+1),
we get

r(\log m-2)\leq\Sigma_{m}\leq r(\log m+1).

Then we have

\Pi_{m}\leq\prod_{\mathbf{I}\in\mathcal{I}_{m}}\mathrm{e}^{-1/\|\mathbf{I}\|}=\mathrm{e}^{-\Sigma_{m}}\leq\mathrm{e}^{2r}m^{-r}.

∎

1.4
Outline of the proof of Theorem
4

Our proof follows the ideas of Bary-Soroker, Koukoulopoulos and Kozma
[
1
]
. If
D
is a factor of
A
of degree
k,
this will also be true modulo any prime
p.
Thus, when we fix
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
distinct primes and define

\mathbf{A}=A_{P}:=(A\,\mathrm{mod}\,p_{i})_{1\leq i\leq r}\in\mathcal{P}_{(n,\ldots,n)},

if
A
has a factor
D
of degree in
[n_{1},n_{2}],
then
\mathbf{A}
has a factor
\mathbf{D}=D_{P}
of degree
(k,\ldots,k)
for some
k\in[n_{1},n_{2}].

We are thus interested in the presence of factors of degree
(k,\ldots,k)
of a random variable in
\mathcal{P}.
It is well known the multiplicative structure of
\mathds{F}_{p}[X]^{u}
is analogous to that of integers, and
\mathcal{P}
is a concatenation of
r
spaces
\mathds{F}_{p_{i}}[X]^{u}.
In the case of integers, Ford
[
4
]
achieved a precise estimate on the presence of factors in an interval
[2^{k},2^{k+1}]
(which corresponds to degree
k
in the polynomial-integer analogy) :

Theorem 9

(Ford,
[
4
]
)
.

If
a
is a random integer taken uniformly in
[1,x]
with
x\geq 2^{2k},
then

\mathds{P}(a\text{ has a factor in }[2^{k},2^{k+1}])\asymp k^{-Q(1/\log 2)}(\log k)^{-3/2}.

As we are only interested in a precision up to a
k^{o(1)}
term in Theorem
4
, we will not need such a precise result in
\mathcal{P}.
Still, this tells us we can expect a
O(k^{-rQ(1/\log 2)+o(1)})
bound on
\mathds{P}(\mathbf{A}\text{ has a factor of degree }(k,\ldots,k))
when
\mathbf{A}
is uniform in
\mathcal{P}_{(n,\ldots,n)}
with
n\geq 2k,
which happens for
\mathbf{A}=A_{P}
when the
\mu_{j}
are uniform modulo
P=p_{1}\ldots p_{r}.
Here, we got an extra
r
in the exponent of the bound as we want a factor modulo
r
different primes. When
\mathbf{A}
is not exactly uniform in
\mathcal{P}_{(n,\ldots,n)},
we will get an extra error term controlled by some
\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(k+o(k)).

Summing on
k
, we can then expect a
O\left(n_{1}^{1-rQ(1/\log 2)}\right)
bound on Theorem
4
when
r\geq\left\lceil\frac{1}{Q(1/\log 2)}\right\rceil.
However, it turns out we can improve the convergence speed. Indeed,
[
2
,
1
]
achieved a result with only
r=4
primes. The key for that is to note the "
\mathbf{A}
has a factor of degree
(k,\ldots,k)
" events are strongly correlated for close
k,
which we explain below. Using and improving on this idea, we achieve the better
O\left(n_{1}^{-rQ\left(\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}\right)+o(1)}\right)
bound announced in Theorem
4
when
r\geq 4.

Indeed, heuristically, when
m
is smaller than
k,
an element
\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{P}
verifying
\mathbf{A}_{\leq m}=\mathbf{B}
has roughly
\Pi_{m}\tau(\mathbf{B})
factors of degree
(k,\ldots,k)
on average. The proportionality with
\tau(\mathbf{B})
is because when
\mathbf{D}
is a factor of
\mathbf{A},
there are
\tau(\mathbf{B})
choices for
\mathbf{D}_{\leq m}
which is a factor of
\mathbf{B}.
Thus, when
v\Pi_{m}\tau(\mathbf{A}_{\leq m})\ll 1
(with
v\geq m
),
\mathbf{A}
is unlikely to have any factor of degree
(k,\ldots,k)
for some
k\in[v,2v],
whereas when
v\Pi_{m}\tau(\mathbf{A}_{\leq m})\gg 1,

\mathbf{A}
will have factors of degree
(k,\ldots,k)
for many
k\in[v,2v].
This cutoff point on
\tau(\mathbf{A}_{\leq m})
corresponds exactly to the cutoff point defined in
\mathcal{E}_{m}
when
v=m^{1+o(1)}
(as
\Pi_{m}\asymp\mathrm{e}^{-\Sigma_{m}}\asymp m^{-r}
). Our result on the likelihood of factors of degree
(k,\ldots,k)
in
\mathcal{P}
is given below.

Proposition 10
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
a random variable in
\mathcal{P}
such that
\mathds{P}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\nu}|\mathbf{A})\leq(1-1/L)^{\nu}
for some
L
and for all
i,\nu.
Then for
n_{1}\leq n_{2},

\mathds{P}\left(\exists\mathbf{D}\in\bigcup_{n_{1}\leq k\leq n_{2}}\mathcal{P}_{(k,\ldots,k)},\,\mathbf{D}|\mathbf{A}\right)=O_{r}\left(L^{r}n_{1}^{-rQ\left(\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}\right)+o(1)}+n_{2}^{5r}\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}\left(n_{2}+n_{2}/(\log n_{2})^{3}\right)\right).

Remark
.

Here, we work with a global condition on
\tau(\mathbf{A}_{\leq m})
in
\mathcal{E}_{m},
whereas
[
1
]
worked with a condition on each of the
\tau((A_{i})_{\leq m}).
This key difference is what allows us to achieve a bound that increases with
r
in Proposition
10
rather than the weaker
[
1
]
[Proposition 2.2].

In Proposition
10
, we have an error term controlled by some
\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(n_{2}+o(n_{2}))
(which does not control the factors that are multiples of the
\mathbf{X}_{i}
), and a separate condition on the
\mathds{P}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\nu}|\mathbf{A}).
This is so that Proposition
10
can be applied when
\mathbf{A}=A_{P}=(A\,\mathrm{mod}\,p_{i})_{i}
with
A
the random polynomial from Theorem
4
. To bound the
\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}
error term, we use the results obtained by Bary-Soroker, Koukoulopoulos and Kozma in
[
1
]
[Proposition 2.3], given below.

Proposition 11

(
[
1
]
, Proposition 2.3)
.

Let
A
a random monic polynomial of degree
n
with independent coefficients following probability measures
\mu_{j}
and
P=p_{1}\ldots p_{r}
a product of
r
distinct primes, with
P\leq n^{1/4}.

If
s\leq\log n
and
\gamma\geq 1/2
are such that for all
j
and
Q,R,\ell
with
QR=P,\ Q>1
and
\ell\in\mathds{Z}/R\mathds{Z},
we have

\sum_{k\in\mathds{Z}/Q\mathds{Z}}|\hat{\mu_{j}}(k/Q+\ell/R)|^{s}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{\log n}\right)Q^{1-\gamma},

then with
\mathbf{A}:=(A\mod p_{i})_{1\leq i\leq r},
we have

\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}\left(\gamma n/s+n/(\log n)^{3}\right)\leq\mathrm{e}^{-n^{1/10}}.

Remark
.

The proposition 2.3 as written in
[
1
]
only bounds this error term for degree up to
\gamma n/s+n^{0.88};
however, to obtain the constant
rQ\left(\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}\right)
in Theorem
4
, we need to go up to
\gamma n/s+n^{1-o(1)}.
To cover these higher degrees, notice the relevant bound at the end of the proof of
[
1
]
[Proposition 2.3] to cover degree
L=\gamma n/s+\varepsilon(n)>\gamma n/s
is to have
P^{\varepsilon(n)}\alpha^{n/s}
small enough, where
\alpha:=1-1/\log n
is the term in front of
Q^{1-\gamma}
in the condition on the
\hat{\mu}_{j}
(which was taken equal to
1-n^{-1/10}
in
[
1
]
).

Here when
\varepsilon(n)\leq n/(\log n)^{3}
and
\alpha=1-1/\log n,
we indeed have as
P\leq n^{1/4}
and
s\leq\log n,

P^{\varepsilon(n)}\alpha^{n/s}\leq\mathrm{e}^{\frac{n\log P}{(\log n)^{3}}-\frac{n}{s\log n}}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{3}{4}\frac{n}{(\log n)^{2}}},

hence our version of the proposition holds.

We can now prove Theorem
4
combining Propositions
10
and
11
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Here, we can assume
n
is large enough compared to
r
and
s,
as the result for the smaller values of
n
can be made trivial adjusting the implicit constant of the big-Oh term. As we also assumed
n\geq P^{4},
Proposition
11
applies with
\gamma=1/2
and gives us with
n_{2}:=n/2s,

\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}\left(n_{2}+n_{2}/(\log n_{2})^{3}\right)\leq\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}\left(n/2s+n/(\log n)^{3}\right)\leq\mathrm{e}^{-n^{1/10}}\leq n^{-5r},

where
\mathbf{A}=A_{P}=(A\,\mathrm{mod}\,p_{i})_{1\leq i\leq r}.

Moreover, we have for all
i,\nu,
if the
a_{j}
are the coefficients of
A
(drawn independently according to the
\mu_{j}
),

\mathds{P}\left(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\nu}|\mathbf{A}\right)=\prod_{0\leq j<\nu}\mathds{P}(a_{j}=0\,\mathrm{mod}\,p_{i})\leq(1-1/4s)^{\nu}

(see
[
1
]
, relation (2.8) for this bound on the
\mathds{P}(a_{j}=0\,\mathrm{mod}\,p_{i})
under the condition of Theorem
4
on the
\widehat{\mu_{i}}
). Thus, we can apply Proposition
10
with
L=4s
to get

\displaystyle\mathds{P}\left(\exists D|A\text{ of degree }\in[n_{1},n_{2}]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathds{P}(\exists\mathbf{D}|\mathbf{A}\text{ of degree }(k,\ldots,k)\text{ for some }k\in[n_{1},n_{2}])

\displaystyle=O_{r}\left(s^{r}n_{1}^{-rQ\left(\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}\right)+o(1)}+n_{2}^{5r}\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(n_{2}+n_{2}/(\log n_{2})^{3})\right)

\displaystyle=O_{r,s}\left(n_{1}^{-rQ\left(\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}\right)+o(1)}\right).

∎

Remark
.

Given Ford’s result, we expect the estimate in Proposition
10
to be only an overestimate by a
n_{1}^{o(1)}
factor when it comes to the main term. This means we cannot further reduce the number of primes
r
(depending on
C
) required to obtain Theorem
3
. Moving on to the dependency between
N_{0}
and
P=p_{1}\ldots p_{r}
given by equation (
1
), it likely cannot be improved by a lot either. Indeed, heuristically, when the coefficients of
A
are drawn in a segment of length
N\leq\sqrt{P}
and
\mathbf{B}
has degree
n/2
in each component,
\mathbf{A}=A_{P}
has only
N^{n}\leq P^{n/2}=\|\mathbf{B}\|
possible values which means we cannot estimate
\mathds{P}(\mathbf{B}|\mathbf{A})
by
1/\|\mathbf{B}\|
and thus cannot control our error term
\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(n/2+o(n)).

2
Ruling out factors of degree
(k,\ldots,k)
in
\mathcal{P}

We show
\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{P}
is unlikely to have a factor of degree
(k,\ldots,k)
for some
k
when the number of divisors of
\mathbf{A}_{\leq m}
is low for some
m
small compared to
k.

Proposition 12
.

Let
m>0
and
v\geq 6m\Sigma_{m}.
Let
\mathbf{A}
a random variable in
\mathcal{P},
and assume there is an
L>0
such that for all
i,\nu,

\mathds{P}(\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\nu}|\mathbf{A})\leq(1-1/L)^{\nu}.
Then

\mathds{P}\left(\left(\exists\mathbf{D}\in\bigcup_{v\leq k\leq 2v}\mathcal{P}_{(k,\ldots,k)},\,\mathbf{D}|\mathbf{A}\right)\cap\mathcal{E}_{m}\right)=O_{r}\left(\frac{v}{m}(L\log m)^{r}m^{-rQ\left(\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}\right)}+m^{5r}\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(2v+5m\Sigma_{m})\right).

As the typical values of
\tau(\mathbf{A}_{\leq m})
are concentrated around
2^{\Sigma_{m}},
the
\mathcal{E}_{m}
(which have been defined in Definition
4
along with
\Sigma_{m},\Pi_{m}
and
\mathbf{A}_{\leq m}
) will be unlikely when
r\geq 4>\frac{1}{1-\log 2}
and this will allow us to prove Proposition
10
.

2.1
Preliminary results

We will need a sieve lemma (similar to
[
1
]
[Lemma 8.2]) to estimate

\mathds{P}\big(\mathbf{D}|\mathbf{A};(\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{D})_{\leq m}=1\big)=\mathds{P}\big(\mathbf{D}_{>m}|\mathbf{A};\mathbf{A}_{\leq m}=\mathbf{D}_{\leq m}\big)
with an error term depending on some
\left|\mathds{P}(\mathbf{B}|\mathbf{A})-\|\mathbf{B}\|^{-1}\right|
with
\mathbf{B}
of small enough degree (so that it can be bounded by some
\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}
term).

Lemma 13
.

For any fixed
\mathbf{D}\in\mathcal{P},
we have

\mathds{P}\big(\mathbf{D}|\mathbf{A};(\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{D})_{\leq m}=1\big)\leq 2\cdot\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{D}\|}\Pi_{m}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{\ell}\\
\ell\leq 4\Sigma_{m}+2\end{subarray}}\left|\mathds{P}(\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}|\mathbf{A})-\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}\|}\right|,

where the sum is on all products
\mathbf{G}
of
\ell\leq 4\Sigma_{m}+2
distinct irreducible factors of
\mathcal{I}_{m}
(notably,
\mathbf{G}
will always have total degree
\leq m(4\Sigma_{m}+2).
)

Proof.

By the inclusion-exclusion principle, we get

\mathbb{1}_{\mathbf{D}|\mathbf{A};(\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{D})_{\leq m}=1}=\sum_{J\subset\mathcal{I}_{m}}(-1)^{\#J}\mathbb{1}_{\mathbf{D}\prod_{\mathbf{I}\in J}\mathbf{I}|\mathbf{A}}=\sum_{\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{\ell}}(-1)^{\ell}\mathbb{1}_{\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}|\mathbf{A}}.

This sum however includes some terms with
\mathbf{G}
of very large degree (up to
m|\mathcal{I}_{m}|
). We thus need to cut the sum, using the fact that an inclusion-exclusion always oscillates between both sides of its final value as we add and substract term : thus, for any cutting point
\ell_{0}\in\mathds{N},
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{\ell}\\
\ell\leq 2\ell_{0}-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\ell}\mathbb{1}_{\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}|\mathbf{A}}\leq\mathbb{1}_{\mathbf{D}|\mathbf{A};(\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{D})_{\leq m}=1}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{\ell}\\
\ell\leq 2\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\ell}\mathbb{1}_{\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}|\mathbf{A}}.

We thus get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{\ell}\\
\ell\leq 2\ell_{0}-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\ell}\mathds{P}(\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}|\mathbf{A})\leq\mathds{P}(\mathbf{D}|\mathbf{A};(\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{D})_{\leq m}=1)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{\ell}\\
\ell\leq 2\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\ell}\mathds{P}(\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}|\mathbf{A}),

and similarly

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{\ell}\\
\ell\leq 2\ell_{0}-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\ell}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}\|}\leq\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{D}\|}\Pi_{m}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{\ell}\\
\ell\leq 2\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\ell}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}\|}.

We then get

\displaystyle\mathds{P}(\mathbf{D}|\mathbf{A};(\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{D})_{\leq m}=1)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{\ell}\\
\ell\leq 2\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\ell}\mathds{P}(\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}|\mathbf{A})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{\ell}\\
\ell\leq 2\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\ell}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}\|}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{\ell}\\
\ell\leq 2\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}\left|\mathds{P}(\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}|\mathbf{A})-\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}\|}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{D}\|}\Pi_{m}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{\ell}\\
\ell=2\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}\|}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{\ell}\\
\ell\leq 2\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}\left|\mathds{P}(\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}|\mathbf{A})-\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}\|}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{D}\|}\Pi_{m}+\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{D}\|}\sum_{\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{2\ell_{0}}}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{G}\|}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{\ell}\\
\ell\leq 2\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}\left|\mathds{P}(\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}|\mathbf{A})-\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{D}\mathbf{G}\|}\right|.

To conclude, we then need to bound
S_{2\ell_{0}}:=\sum_{\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{2\ell_{0}}}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{G}\|}
by
\Pi_{m}
(where the sum is on products of
2\ell_{0}
distinct irreducibles of
\mathcal{I}_{m}
) for
\ell_{0}:=\lceil 2\Sigma_{m}\rceil.
To do this, notice we have

\Sigma_{m}^{2\ell_{0}}\geq(2\ell_{0})!S_{2\ell_{0}}

(by developing the sum
\Sigma_{m}
and keeping only the terms obtained by multiplying
2\ell_{0}
distinct terms of the sum), so that

Lemma 14
.

We have for all
t\in\mathds{R},

S_{t}:=\sum_{\mathbf{I}\in\mathcal{P}\text{ irreducible}}\sum_{\nu\geq 2}\frac{(\nu+1)^{t}}{\|\mathbf{I}\|^{\nu}}<\infty.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{I}\in\mathcal{P}\text{ irreducible}}\sum_{\nu\geq 2}\frac{(\nu+1)^{t}}{\|\mathbf{I}\|^{\nu}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{I\in\mathds{F}_{p_{i}}[X]^{u}_{j}\text{ irreducible}}\sum_{\nu\geq 2}\frac{(\nu+1)^{t}}{p_{i}^{j\nu}}

\displaystyle\leq r\sum_{\nu=2}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\nu+1)^{t}}{2^{j(\nu-1)}}

Lemma 16

We have

\overline{\mathds{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{m})\leq\mathds{P}\big(\Deg\mathbf{A}_{\leq m}\geq m(\Sigma_{m}-2)\big)+\mathds{P}\big(\omega(\mathbf{A}_{\leq m})\geq 3\Sigma_{m}\big)\\
+\mathds{P}\big(\tau(\mathbf{A}_{\leq m})\geq\mathrm{e}^{(1-1/r)\Sigma_{m}};\Deg\mathbf{A}_{\leq m}\leq m(\Sigma_{m}-2),\omega(\mathbf{A}_{\leq m})\leq 3\Sigma_{m}\big)

(2)

with by Lemma
16
,

\mathds{P}\big(\Deg\mathbf{A}_{\leq m}\geq m(\Sigma_{m}-2)\big)\leq O_{r}(m^{-r})+\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(m\Sigma_{m}),

and by Lemma
17
,

\mathds{P}\big(\omega(\mathbf{A}_{\leq m})\geq 3\Sigma_{m}\big)\leq O_{r}(\mathrm{e}^{(1-Q(3))\Sigma_{m}})+\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(3m\Sigma_{m}).

We can now estimate the main term
\mathds{P}\big(\tau(\mathbf{A}_{\leq m})\geq\mathrm{e}^{(1-1/r)\Sigma_{m}};\Deg\mathbf{A}_{\leq m}\leq m(\Sigma_{m}-2),\omega(\mathbf{A}_{\leq m})\leq 3\Sigma_{m}\big)
using our sieve lemma
13
:

\displaystyle\mathds{P}\big(

\displaystyle\tau(\mathbf{A}_{\leq m})\geq\mathrm{e}^{(1-1/r)\Sigma_{m}};\Deg\mathbf{A}_{\leq m}\leq m(\Sigma_{m}-2),\omega(\mathbf{A}_{\leq m})\leq 3\Sigma_{m}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}_{\leq m}\\
\tau(\mathbf{B})\geq\mathrm{e}^{(1-1/r)\Sigma_{m}}\\
\Deg\mathbf{B}\leq m(\Sigma_{m}-2)\\
\omega(\mathbf{B})\leq 3\Sigma_{m}\end{subarray}}\mathds{P}(\mathbf{A}_{\leq m}=\mathbf{B})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}_{\leq m}\\
\tau(\mathbf{B})\geq\mathrm{e}^{(1-1/r)\Sigma_{m}}\end{subarray}}2\frac{\Pi_{m}}{\|\mathbf{B}\|}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}_{\leq m}\\
\Deg\mathbf{B}\leq m(\Sigma_{m}-2)\\
\omega(\mathbf{B})\leq 3\Sigma_{m}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{l}\\
I_{j}\in\mathcal{I}_{m}\text{ distinct}\\
l\leq 4\Sigma_{m}+2\end{subarray}}\left|\mathds{P}(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{G}|\mathbf{A})-\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{G}\|}\right|.

To bound the main term, we use Rankin’s trick again. We have for all
t>0,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}_{\leq m}\\
\tau(\mathbf{B})\geq\mathrm{e}^{(1-1/r)\Sigma_{m}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{B}\|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}_{\leq m}}\frac{\tau(\mathbf{B})^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-t(1-1/r)\Sigma_{m}}}{\|\mathbf{B}\|}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-t(1-1/r)\Sigma_{m}}\prod_{\mathbf{I}\in\mathcal{I}_{m}}\sum_{\nu\geq 0}\frac{(\nu+1)^{t}}{\|\mathbf{I}\|^{\nu}}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-t(1-1/r)\Sigma_{m}+\sum_{\mathbf{I}\in\mathcal{I}_{m}}\sum_{\nu\geq 1}\frac{(\nu+1)^{t}}{\|\mathbf{I}\|^{\nu}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-t(1-1/r)\Sigma_{m}+2^{t}\Sigma_{m}+S_{t}}=O_{r,t}(\mathrm{e}^{\left((1-1/r)t+2^{t}\right)\Sigma_{m}}),

where
S_{t}
comes from Lemma
14
. Taking the optimal value
t=\frac{\log\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}}{\log 2}>0
(as
r\geq 4>\frac{1}{1-\log 2}
), we get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}_{\leq m}\\
\tau(\mathbf{B})\geq\mathrm{e}^{(1-1/r)\Sigma_{m}}\end{subarray}}2\frac{\Pi_{m}}{\|\mathbf{B}\|}=O_{r}\left(2\Pi_{m}\mathrm{e}^{\left[1-Q\left(\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}\right)\right]\Sigma_{m}}\right)=O_{r}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-Q\left(\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}\right)\Sigma_{m}}\right).

To bound the error term, we regroup based on the value of
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{B}\mathbf{G}.
We then have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}_{\leq m}\\
\Deg\mathbf{B}\leq m(\Sigma_{m}-2)\\
\omega(\mathbf{B})\leq 3\Sigma_{m}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{1}\ldots\mathbf{I}_{l}\\
I_{j}\in\mathcal{I}_{m}\text{ distinct}\\
l\leq 4\Sigma_{m}+2\end{subarray}}\left|\mathds{P}(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{G}|\mathbf{A})-\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{G}\|}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{C}_{\leq m}\\
\Deg\mathbf{C}\leq 5m\Sigma_{m}\\
\omega(\mathbf{C})\leq 7\Sigma_{m}+2\end{subarray}}\#\{\mathbf{G}|\mathbf{C}\text{ square-free}\}\left|\mathds{P}(\mathbf{C}|\mathbf{A})-\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{C}\|}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{7\Sigma_{m}+2}\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(5m\Sigma_{m}),

remembering
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{C}_{\leq m}
implies
\mathbf{C}
has no
\mathbf{X}_{i}
factor (given Definition
4
) and as such the sum on
\mathbf{C}
can be bounded by a
\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}
term.

Combining this and the results from Lemmas
16
and
17
in (
2
), we get

\displaystyle\overline{\mathds{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{m})

\displaystyle\leq O_{r}(m^{-r})+\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(m\Sigma_{m})+O_{r}(e^{(1-Q(3))\Sigma_{m}})+\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(3m\Sigma_{m})+O_{r}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-Q\left(\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}\right)\Sigma_{m}}\right)+2^{7\Sigma_{m}+2}\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(5m\Sigma_{m})

\displaystyle=O_{r}\left(m^{-rQ\left(\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}\right)}+m^{5r}\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(5m\Sigma_{m})\right),

as
\Sigma_{m}=r(\log m+O(1))
(lemma
8
),
Q(3)-1\geq Q(1/\log 2)\geq Q\left(\frac{1-1/r}{\log 2}\right)
and
\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(m\Sigma_{m})\leq\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(3m\Sigma_{m})\leq\Delta_{\mathbf{A}}(5m\Sigma_{m}).

∎

.

Proof of Proposition

.

For
v>0,
fix

When
n_{1}
is large enough compared to
r

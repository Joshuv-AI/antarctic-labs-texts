A review of the Elliott program of classification of simple amenable C ∗ C^{*} -algebras
Guihua Gong, Huaxin Lin, Zhuang Niu

Abstract
We give a brief survey of the development of the Elliott program of classification of separable simple amenable C ∗ C^{*} -algebras.



1
Introduction

By a
C^{*}
-algebra, we mean a norm closed and adjoint closed subalgebra
of the algebra
B(H)
of all bounded linear operators
on some Hilbert space
H.

There is an abstract definition of Gelfand and Naimark (
[
32
]
) for
C^{*}
-algebras, namely,
a Banach algebra with an involutive
*
-operation such that
\|x^{*}x\|=\|x\|^{2}

for all
x\in A.

Let
X
be a compact metric space,
G
be a group,
{\alpha}
be a homomorphism
from
G
to the group of homeomorphisms of
X
and
\mu
be a faithful

{\alpha}
-invariant
Borel probability measure. We have a representation

\pi:G\to U(B(H)),
the unitary group of
B(H),
where
H=L^{2}(X,\mu).

For each
f\in C(X),
we have a bounded operator
M_{f}
defined by
M_{f}(g)=fg

for all
g\in L^{2}(X,\mu).
Then we obtain a
C^{*}
-algebra
C(X)\rtimes_{\alpha}G,

the
C^{*}
-algebra  generated by
\pi(G)
and
\{M_{f}:f\in C(X)\}
as a
C^{*}
-subalgebra  of
B(H).
This
C^{*}
-algebra  is often used to
study the dynamical system
(X,G,{\alpha}).

C^{*}
-algebras may also arise from non-commutative geometry, number theory and group representations,
to name a few related fields. Needless to say, it is of paramount importance to determine the structure of a given
C^{*}
-algebra  from a few known and possibly computable data and
to recognize that some
C^{*}
-algebras from different subjects may be isomorphic as
C^{*}
-algebras.
This leads to the question of classification of
C^{*}
-algebras.

In the First International Congress of Basic Science, David Mumford
gave the opening plenary lecture on Consciousness, robots and DNA (
[
83
]
).
At the very beginning, he cautioned the audience that there would be no mathematics in his talk.
Nevertheless, by the very end of this magnificent lecture, when he ended his talk
on DNA being a measuring instrument opens a Pandora’s box, he proposed a problem in

C^{*}
-algebras about non-commuting self-adjoint operators to be approximated by commuting ones,
which is closely related to the Elliott program of classification of amenable
C^{*}
-algebras (see
[
60
]
).

Classification of amenable
C^{*}
-algebras might begin with Glimm’s classification of UHF-algebras (by supernatural numbers) in the late 1950’s. This was followed by Dixmier’s
classification of matroid
C^{*}
-algebras in the 1960’s. Ola Bratteli used the Bratteli diagram to classify AF-algebras,
i.e.,
C^{*}
-algebras which are inductive limits of finite dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras. George A. Elliott then (1976)
used dimension groups to classify AF-algebras (
[
14
]
).
It was later realized that
the dimension groups are in fact the
K_{0}
-groups. Even though
K_{1}
-groups of AF-algebras
are trivial, realizing dimension groups are
K_{0}
-groups is very important since, immediately,

K_{1}
-groups enter the picture.

By early 1990, Elliott proposed
to classify simple
C^{*}
-algebras by their K-theory.

As suggested by Elliott (
[
18
]
),
separable simple

C^{*}
-algebras
A
are
naturally divided into three cases
according to their

K_{0}
-groups:

Case 1:
K_{0}(A)\not=\{0\}
is a (directed) ordered group,

Case 2.

K_{0}(A)_{+}=\{0\}=V(A)
,

Case 3:
K_{0}(A)=K_{0}(A)_{+}
, and
V(A)\not=\{0\}
,

where
V(A)
is the set of Murry-von Neumann equivalence classes of projections in the stabilization
A\otimes{\cal K}.

For Case 3, i.e., the case of purely infinite simple
C^{*}
-algebras, the classification by the Elliott invariant
was accomplished in the last century by the work of Kirchberg and Phillips (see
[
51
]
and
[
93
]
)
with some earlier works of the M. Rørdam and others (cf.
[
98
]
,
[
99
]
,
[
79
]
,
[
30
]
and

[
100
]
).
This report will only discuss the finite case.

To clarify what classification of a class of
C^{*}
-algebras by the Elliott invariant is
about, let us end this section with the following statement.

For a complete classification of a class of
C^{*}
-algebras by the Elliott invariant, there must be three ingredients:

(1) A definition of invariants for
C^{*}
-algebras in the class; (2) A (range) theorem that says exactly what values of
the invariants defined in (1) occur for
C^{*}
-algebras in the class; (3) the isomorphism theorem, i.e.,
the statement that any two
C^{*}
-algebras in the class with the same, i.e., isomorphic invariants
are isomorphic.

2
The beginning

At the beginning of the 1990’s, Elliott presented the following
classification results:

Theorem 2.1

(G. A. Elliott
[
16

)
.

Let
A=\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n}
and
B=\lim_{n\to\infty}B_{n}
be unital simple

-algebras with real rank zero (see

and

-algebras of the form

Then
A\cong B
if and only if

(K_{0}(A),K_{0}(A)_{+},[1_{A}],K_{1}(A))\cong(K_{0}(B),K_{0}(B)_{+},[1_{B}],K_{1}(B)).

Theorem 2.2

(G. A. Elliott
[
16
]
)
.

Let
G_{0}
be a simple countable (ordered) dimension group, not equal to
\mathbb{Z},

with an order unit

and let

be a countable abelian group.
It follows that there is a unital simple

-algebra
of real rank zero which is the inductive limit of a sequence of finite direct sums of

This is the beginning of an age that classes of simple
C^{*}
-algebras became classifiable.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
A
be a
C^{*}
-algebra,

Let

We write

Denote by

the space of all tracial states of

Denote by
\operatorname{Aff}(T(A))
the space of all real affine continuous functions on
T(A).

A unital
C^{*}
-algebra

is said to have stable rank one,
if the set of invertible elements of
A
is dense in
A.

A non-unital
C^{*}
-algebra  has stable rank one if
\tilde{A},
the minimum
unitization of
A,
has stable rank one.

A unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
is said to have real rank zero,
if the set of invertible self-adjoint elements of
A
is dense in
A_{s.a.},

the self-adjoint part of
A.

A non-unital
C^{*}
-algebra  has real rank zero, if
\tilde{A}

has real rank zero.

A unital commutative
C^{*}
-algebra

where

is a compact Hausdorff space,
has stable rank one if and only if
{\rm dim}X\leq 1,
and
A
has real rank zero,
if and only if
{\rm dim}X=0.
Every von Neumann algebra has real rank zero.
Every AF-algebra has real rank zero. It was proved by S. Zhang
[
116

that
every purely infinite simple
C^{*}
-algebra  has real rank zero. It is then natural to begin
the classification program with
simple
C^{*}
-algebras of real rank zero in mind such as Theorem

.

Definition 2.4
.

Denote by
{\cal I}_{0}
the class of all finite dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras.
For

denote by

the class of

-algebras which are finite direct sums of

-algebras

A

A=\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n},

If there is
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

we say that the AH-algebra
A
has no dimension growth.

A unital simple
C^{*}
-algebra is said to be of

slow dimension growth
, if

A=\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n},

where
A_{n}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k(n)}P_{n,j}M_{r(n,j)}(C(X_{n,j})P_{n,j}

P_{n,j}M_{r(n,j)}(C(X_{n,j})P_{n,j}\in\bigcup_{k}{\cal I}_{k}

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max_{1\leq j\leq k(n)}{{\rm dim}(X_{n,j})\over{{\rm rank}(P_{n,j})+1}}=0.

(e 2.1)

Let
I_{k}=\{f\in M_{k}(C[0,1]):~f(0)=\lambda_{0}\cdot 1_{k},f(1)=\lambda_{1}\cdot 1_{k},\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}\in\mathbb{C}\}
.
A
C^{*}
-algebra  with the form

-algebras.

A

-algebra  is said to be sub-homogeneous, if it is a

-subalgebra  of a
C^{*}
-algebra  in

for some

A
C^{*}
-algebra  is said to be an ASH-algebra if it is an inductive limit of sub-homogeneous
C^{*}
-algebras.

Definition 2.8
.

By an ordered group, we mean an abelian group which is (partially) ordered.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
A

3
Abstract approach

Finite
C^{*}
-algebras discussed in the previous section are presented as inductive limits of
some basic building blocks such as finite dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras, or some (sub)-homogeneous

C^{*}
-algebras. However, some naturally arising simple

-algebras, such as crossed product C*-algebras

for minimal homeomorphisms

of inductive limits of some simpler forms.

\displaystyle K_{0}(A)_{+}=\{x\in K_{0}(A):\rho(A)>0\}\cup\{0\}\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\Sigma(K_{0}(A))=\Sigma_{\rho}(K_{0}(A)).

(e 5.25)

In other words,

(K_{0}(A),\Sigma(K_{0}(A)),\widetilde{T}(A),\widetilde{\langle e_{A}\rangle},\rho_{A})
is
a scaled simple ordered group pairing
(see Definition

above and Definitions 2.7 and 2.15 in

]
)
(and
K_{1}(A)
is a countable abelian group).

Let

(G_{0},\Sigma_{\rho}(G_{0}),T,s,\rho)

be a simple ordered group pairing with distinguished set
\Sigma(G_{0})
and
G_{1}
be a countable abelian group. Then there is a simple separable amenable

-stable

-algebra
A

\displaystyle((K_{0}(A),\Sigma_{\rho}(K_{0}(A)),\widetilde{T}(A),\widetilde{\langle e_{A}\rangle},\rho_{A}),K_{1}(A))=((G_{0},\Sigma_{\rho}(G_{0}),T,s,\rho),G_{1}).

(e 5.26)

(i) In the case that
\widetilde{T}(A)\not=\{0\},

A is unital if and only if
G_{0}
has a unit
u,
i.e.,
u

(ii) In the case that

-stable

\displaystyle((K_{0}(A),\Sigma(K_{0}(A)),{\widetilde{T}}(A),{{\widehat{\langle e_{A}\rangle}}},\rho_{A}),K_{1}(A))

(e 5.27)

\displaystyle\hskip 21.68121pt\cong((K_{0}(B),\Sigma(K_{0}(B)),{\widetilde{T}}(B),{{\widehat{\langle e_{B}\rangle}}},\rho_{B}),{{K_{1}(B)}}).

(e 5.28)

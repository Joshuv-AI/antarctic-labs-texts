A posteriori error estimates based on multilevel decompositions with large problems on the coarsest level
Petr Vacek, Jan Papež, Zdeněk Strakoš

Abstract
Multilevel methods represent a powerful approach in numerical solution of partial differential equations. The multilevel structure can also be used to construct estimates for total and algebraic errors of computed approximations. This paper deals with residual-based error estimates that are based on properties of quasi-interpolation operators, stable-splittings, or frames. We focus on the settings where the system matrix on the coarsest level is still large and the associated terms in the estimates can only be approximated. We show that the way in which the error term associated with the coarsest level is approximated is substantial. It can significantly affect both the efficiency (accuracy) of the overall error estimates and their robustness with respect to the size of the coarsest problem. The newly proposed approximation of the coarsest-level term is based on using the conjugate gradient method with an appropriate stopping criterion. We prove that the resulting estimates are efficient and robust with respect to the size of the coarsest-level problem. Numerical experiments illustrate the theoretical findings.

Introduction

Multilevel methods
Brandt 2011
;
Hackbusch 2016
;
Briggs et al. 2000
;
Trottenberg et al. 2001
are frequently used for solving systems of linear equations obtained from the discretization of partial differential equations (PDEs). They are applied either as standalone iterative solvers or as preconditioners.
In
geometric
multigrid methods the hierarchy of systems is obtained by discretizations of an infinite dimensional problem on a sequence of nested meshes. In
algebraic
multigrid methods the coarse systems are constructed using algebraic properties of the matrix.
Each multigrid cycle contains smoothing on fine levels, prolongation, and solving a system of linear equations on the coarsest level.
Smoothing is typically done by a few iterations of a stationary iterative method.
If the size permits, it is typical to solve the coarsest-level problem using a direct method based on LU or Cholesky decomposition. Although this does not provide a computed result with a zero error, many theoretical results on multigrid methods are proved under the assumption that the coarsest-level problem is solved exactly; see, e.g.,
Xu 1992
;
Yserentant 1993
.

Multilevel methods can in practice also use hierarchies where the problem on the coarsest level is large and can only be solved approximately to a properly chosen accuracy, e.g., by Krylov subspace methods, or direct methods can be used with low-rank matrix approximations. This arises for problems on complicated domains or for large-scale problems solved on modern parallel computers; see, e.g.,
Buttari et al. 2022
. Effects of approximate coarsest-level solves on convergence of multigrid method were analysed, e.g., in
Notay 2007
;
Xu and Zhang 2022
;
Vacek et al. 2023
.

The multilevel structure can also be used to construct estimates of total and algebraic errors; see, e.g.,
Becker et al. 1995
;
Rüde 1993
;
Harbrecht and Schneider 2016
;
Huber 2019
;
Papež et al. 2020
;
Miraçi et al. 2021
.
The estimates of
Becker et al. 1995
;
Rüde 1993
;
Harbrecht and Schneider 2016
;
Huber 2019
;
Papež et al. 2020
;
Miraçi et al. 2021
are, however, not suited for multilevel hierarchies with large coarsest-level problems, which are being used for complicated domains and/or in parallel implementations.
They either assume that the coarsest-level problem is solved exactly
Becker et al. 1995
;
Papež et al. 2020
;
Miraçi et al. 2021
, or they require computation of the term
\mathbf{r}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{A}^{-1}_{0}\mathbf{r}_{0}
associated with the coarsest level, where
\mathbf{A}_{0}
is the coarsest-level system matrix and
\mathbf{r}_{0}
a projection of a finest-level residual to the coarsest level,
Rüde 1993
;
Harbrecht and Schneider 2016
;
Huber 2019
.
The term
\mathbf{r}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{A}^{-1}_{0}\mathbf{r}_{0}
can be approximated, e.g., using the conjugate gradient method (CG) as in
Huber 2019
, or by replacing the system matrix with a diagonal matrix as in
Harbrecht and Schneider 2016
.
Then proving efficiency and robustness of estimates becomes an important challenge.

In this text, we discuss properties of the error estimates in multilevel settings where the system matrix on the coarsest level is large and the associated terms are only approximated.
We consider several a posteriori estimates on total and algebraic errors based on decomposing the error into a sequence of finite element subspaces and using either approximation properties of quasi-interpolation operators
Becker et al. 1995
, stable splittings
Rüde 1993
;
Huber 2019
, or so-called frames
Harbrecht and Schneider 2016
.
The main contribution of this paper is a new procedure for approximating the term associated with the coarsest level that is based on using the conjugate gradient method with an appropriate stopping criterion. We prove that the resulting estimates are
efficient

and robust
with respect to the size of the coarsest-level problem.

The text is organized as follows. First, we present a model problem, its discretization, and the notation used in the text. Derivations of error estimates for total and algebraic errors are presented in
Section

3
. In
Section

4
, we comment on the efficiency of the bounds. Main results are presented in
Section

5
where we describe how to replace the (uncomputable) terms in the estimates by a computable approximation and present an adaptive procedure for approximating the coarsest-level term
\mathbf{r}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{A}^{-1}_{0}\mathbf{r}_{0}
.
Numerical illustrations are given in
Section

6
and conclusions in
Section

7
.
Not to interrupt the presentation, we present detailed theoretical results, which are used in the derivation of the estimates, in Appendices.
Appendix
8
recalls some standard results from PDE and finite element method (FEM) analyses. Appendix
9
presents properties of the quasi-interpolation operator, and Appendix
10
recalls results on stable-splittings and frames.
This enables an easy comparison of different results that are presented separately in literature.

2
Model problem, setting, and notation

The estimates will be studied for a standard model problem, a prototype for elliptic equations, the Poisson’s problem with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, be an open bounded polytope with a Lipschitz-continuous boundary. Given
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, the weak form reads: find
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v=\int_{\Omega}fv\qquad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(1)

In this section, we introduce notation for meshes and finite element spaces, and the multilevel framework. Further, we present the Galerkin finite element discretization of the model problem on a particular level, define its approximate solution, the error, and (scaled) residuals associated with individual levels of the multilevel hierarchy.

Similarly to a standard literature, we introduce some simplifying assumptions, e.g., on the model problem or mesh hierarchies. This is done in order to reduce the complexity of proofs (that are already quite technical) and to allow us to refer to particular results in the literature. We use a standard notation for Lebesgue and Sobolev (Hilbert) spaces, norms, and seminorms; see, e.g.,
Brezis 2011
.

2.1
Notation for a single level

Throughout the paper, we consider simplicial meshes of
\Omega
, matching in the sense that for two distinct elements of a mesh
\mathcal{T}
(triangles in 2D, or tetrahedra in 3D), their intersection is either an empty set or a common node (vertex), edge, or face.
By
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{T}}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}
we denote the set of
(d-1)
-dimensional faces and set of nodes in the mesh
\mathcal{T}
, respectively.
By
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{T},\mathrm{int}}
we denote the set of all faces that are not on the boundary
\partial\Omega
.
By
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{T}}\subset\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}
we denote the set of all nodes in the mesh
\mathcal{T}
, which are not on the boundary, i.e.,
free nodes
.
For any element (simplex)
K\in\mathcal{T}
,
\mathcal{E}_{K}\subset\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{T}}
denotes the set of faces of the element
K
,
\mathcal{N}_{K}\subset\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}
denotes the set of nodes of the element
K
,
\mathcal{E}_{K,\mathrm{int}}=\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{T},\mathrm{int}}
, and
\mathcal{K}_{K}=\mathcal{N}_{K}\cap\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{T}}
.
We use hash to denote the cardinality of a set, for example
\#\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{T}}
denotes the number of free nodes in the mesh
\mathcal{T}
. For the ease of presentation, we will assume that the nodes in
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}
are ordered such that nodes
1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{T}}
belong to
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{T}}
, i.e., we first have the free nodes and then the nodes on the boundary.

By
h_{K}
we denote the diameter of
K\in\mathcal{T}

and define a mesh-size
h_{\mathcal{T}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
as

\displaystyle h_{\mathcal{T}}(x)

\displaystyle=h_{K},\quad x\in K,\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}.

Similarly
h_{\omega}
denotes the diameter of a domain
\omega
. We in particular use
h_{\Omega}
, the diameter of the domain
\Omega
. By
|\omega|
we denote the Lebesgue measure of a domain
\omega
.

For any element
K\in\mathcal{T}
,
\omega_{K}
denotes the patch of elements that share at least one common vertex with
K
, i.e.,

\omega_{K}=\bigcup_{K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T};K^{\prime}\cap K\neq\emptyset}K^{\prime}.

By
\rho_{K}
we denote the diameter of the largest ball inscribed in the element
K
.

For every node
z\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}
, let
\phi_{z}
be the continuous piecewise linear function (
hat function
) that has a value one at node
z
and vanishes at all the other nodes in
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}
.
Let
S_{\mathcal{T}}
denote the space of continuous, piecewise linear functions,

S_{\mathcal{T}}=\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega),v|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K),\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}\}=\mbox{span}\{\phi_{z},\ z\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}\}

and
V_{\mathcal{T}}\subset S_{\mathcal{T}}
the subspace of functions vanishing on the boundary
\partial\Omega
,

V_{\mathcal{T}}=\{v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),v|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K),\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}\}=\mbox{span}\{\phi_{z},\ z\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{T}}\}.

We write the basis of
V_{\mathcal{T}}
as
\Phi_{\mathcal{T}}=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{\#\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{T}}})
.

One of the key properties of a mesh that affects the size of the constants in the estimates derived below in this text is the so-called
shape regularity
of the mesh. This can be quantified by the shape-regularity constant, i.e., the smallest
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}>0
satisfying

\frac{h_{K}}{\rho_{K}}\leq\gamma_{\mathcal{T}},\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T};

(2)

see, e.g.,
(
Scott and Zhang 1990
, p. 484)
.

2.2
Multilevel framework

As the title of the paper suggests, we will work with a sequence of levels
j=0,1,\ldots,J
. For some parts of the theory, we will consider also infinite sequences of levels
j=0,1,\ldots,J,\ldots
. To simplify the previously introduced notation, we will replace in the subscripts
\mathcal{T}_{j}
by
j
to denote objects associated with the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{j}
on the
j
th level.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{0}
be an initial mesh of
\Omega
. We consider a sequence of meshes
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2},\ldots
obtained by successive uniform dyadic refinements of
\mathcal{T}_{0}
, i.e., each element is refined into
2^{d}
elements (congruent triangles in 2D, for a proper nondegenerating 3D mesh refinement; see, e.g.,
Zhang 1995
). We recall that
S_{j}
and
V_{j}
,
j=0,1,\ldots
, are the finite element spaces of continuous piecewise linear functions on
\mathcal{T}_{j}
, respectively spaces of continuous piecewise linear functions on
\mathcal{T}_{j}
that vanish on the boundary
\partial\Omega
. These spaces are nested, i.e.,

\displaystyle S_{0}\subset S_{1}\subset\cdots\subset H^{1}(\Omega),

\displaystyle V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset\cdots\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

On each level
j
, we consider a quasi-interpolation operator

I_{V_{j}}:L^{1}(\Omega)\to V_{j}

with the definition and properties described in detail in Appendix
9
.

Due to the uniform refinement, the mesh sizes
h_{j}
of
\mathcal{T}_{j}
,
j\geq 0
, satisfy
h_{j}=2^{-j}h_{0}
.
Moreover, the uniform refinement assures that the shape-regularity constants
\gamma_{j}
of the meshes are the same on all levels in 2D, i.e.,
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and that in 3D there exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{3D}}>0
such that
\gamma_{j}\leq C_{\mathrm{3D}}\gamma_{0}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
; see
Zhang 1995
.

2.3
Discretization, approximate solution, and residuals

Discretizing the model problem (
1
) on the subspace
V_{J}
, for some
J\geq 0
, using the Galerkin method reads as: find
u_{J}\in V_{J}
such that

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{J}\cdot\nabla w_{J}=\int_{\Omega}fw_{J},\quad\forall w_{J}\in V_{J}.

(3)

Let
v_{J}\in V_{J}
be a (computed) approximation of the discrete solution
u_{J}
. Our goal is to bound the energy norm of the total error
e=u-v_{J}
using computable quantities involving
v_{J}
and
f
.
The squared energy norm of the error
\|\nabla e\|^{2}
can be expressed as

\|\nabla e\|^{2}=\|\nabla(u-v_{J})\|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla(u-v_{J})\cdot\nabla(u-v_{J})=\int_{\Omega}f(u-v_{J})-\nabla v_{J}\cdot\nabla(u-v_{J}).

Denote by
\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\#}
the dual space to
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and define the residual
r\in\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\#}
as

\langle r,w\rangle=\int_{\Omega}fw-\nabla v_{J}\cdot\nabla w,\quad\forall w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(4)

Then (
4
)
yields the so-called residual equation

\|\nabla e\|^{2}=\langle r,e\rangle,

(5)

which is the key formula for the development of error bounds presented below.
Moreover, it can be shown (see, e.g.,
(
Verfürth 2013
, Section 1.4.1)
) that

\|\nabla e\|=\|r\|_{\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\#}}.

In order to derive computable estimates we consider Riesz representations of the infinite-dimensional residual
r
in the finite-dimensional spaces
V_{j}
,
j=0,1,\ldots
.
In particular, let
r_{j}\in V_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots
, be the Riesz representation of
r
in the space
V_{j}
with the scaled
L^{2}
-inner product, i.e.,

\langle r,w_{j}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}h^{-2}_{j}r_{j}w_{j},\quad\forall w_{j}\in V_{j},

(6)

and let
r_{0}\in V_{0}
be the Riesz representation of the residual
r
in the space
V_{0}
with the
H^{1}_{0}
-inner product, i.e.,

\langle r,w_{0}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla r_{0}\cdot\nabla w_{0},\quad\forall w_{0}\in V_{0}.

(7)

These definitions are used in
(
Rüde 1993
, Section 2.6)
where
r_{j}
are called
scaled residuals
.
In
(
Becker et al. 1995
, Section 5)
the authors use Riesz representations of
r
in the spaces
V_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,J
, with the classical
L^{2}
-inner products and call them discrete residuals. The different definition we use results in a slightly different form of the estimates below in comparison to
(
Becker et al. 1995
, Section 5)
.

3
Residual-based error estimates

In this section we recall several published error estimates with their derivation.
We first recall the standard residual-based error estimator for the discretization error in a single-level setting assuming exact algebraic computations or to steer an adaptive mesh refinement.

Consider the model problem (
1
) discretized on a level
J\geq 0
of a multilevel hierarchy as in
Section

2.2
.
The classical residual-based estimator (see, e.g.,
(
Ainsworth and Oden 1997
, Section 3)
,
(
Verfürth 2013
, Section 1.4)
) is for a (computed) approximation
v_{J}\in V_{J}
defined as

\displaystyle\eta^{2}_{J}

\displaystyle=\left(\eta^{\mathrm{RHS}}_{J}\right)^{2}+\left(\eta^{\mathrm{JUMP}}_{J}\right)^{2}+\left(\mathrm{osc}_{J}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\left(\eta^{\mathrm{RHS}}_{J}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{J}}h^{2}_{K}\|f_{K}\|^{2}_{K},

\displaystyle\left(\eta^{\mathrm{JUMP}}_{J}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{J}}h_{K}\!\!\!\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{K,\mathrm{int}}}\!\!\!\|\left[\nabla v_{J}\right]\|^{2}_{E},

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{osc}_{J}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{J}}h^{2}_{K}\|f-f_{K}\|^{2}_{K},

where
\left[\cdot\right]
denotes the jump of a piecewise constant function over the
(d-1)
-dimensional faces (faces in 3D and edges in 2D) and
f_{K}
is the mean value of
f
on
K
. Other choices of
f_{K}
are also possible; see, e.g.,
Harbrecht and Schneider 2016
.

The following result (see, e.g.,
(
Becker et al. 1995
, Lemma 3)
,
(
Stevenson 2007
, Section 4)
, or
(
Verfürth 2013
, Section 1.4)
) will be useful below. There exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{cls}}>0
depending only on the dimension
d
and the shape-regularity parameter
\gamma_{0}
such that

\langle r,w-I_{V_{J}}w\rangle\leq C_{\mathrm{cls}}\eta_{J}\|\nabla w\|,\quad\forall w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(8)

Note that if
v_{J}
is equal to the Galerkin solution
u_{J}
, the associated residual
r=r(u_{J})
satisfies the Galerkin orthogonality on the finest level, i.e.,

\langle r,w_{J}\rangle=0,\quad\forall w_{J}\in V_{J}.

(9)

Then

\|\nabla(u-u_{J})\|^{2}=\langle r,(u-u_{J})-I_{V_{J}}(u-u_{J})\rangle,

and using (
8
) for
w=u-u_{J}
yields the standard bound on the discretization error

\|\nabla(u-u_{J})\|\leq C_{\mathrm{cls}}\eta_{J}(u_{J}).

The following derivation is motivated by

3.2
Estimates of Rüde & Huber

The following derivation is motivated by
(
Rüde 1993
, Section 2.6)
and
(
Huber 2019
, Sections 4.1–4.3)
.
Considering the residual equation (
5
) and
decomposing the error using the quasi-interpolation operator on the finest level
I_{V_{J}}
yields

\|\nabla e\|^{2}=\langle r,e-I_{V_{J}}e\rangle+\langle r,I_{V_{J}}e\rangle.

(20)

The first term can be bounded as in (
12
). Rewriting the second term using the exact solution of the discrete problem
u_{J}
gives

\displaystyle\langle r,I_{V_{J}}e\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla(u-v_{J})\nabla I_{V_{J}}e

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla(u-u_{J})\nabla I_{V_{J}}e+\int_{\Omega}\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\nabla I_{V_{J}}e.

The Galerkin orthogonality on the finest level yields that
\int_{\Omega}\nabla(u-u_{J})\nabla I_{V_{J}}e
vanishes and thus

\langle r,I_{V_{J}}e\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\nabla I_{V_{J}}e\leq\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|\,\|\nabla I_{V_{J}}e\|.

(21)

After bounding the term
\|\nabla I_{V_{J}}e\|
using the stability property of the quasi-interpolation operator (Appendix
9
,
5
, inequality (
88
)) as

\|\nabla I_{V_{J}}e\|\leq C_{I_{V_{J}},4}\|\nabla e\|,

(22)

it remains to bound the energy norm of the algebraic error
\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|
. This can be done using stable splitting of piecewise linear function space, see
Appendix
10
,
12
or
(
Rüde 1993
, Theorem 2.6.2)
.
Consider an arbitrary decomposition of the algebraic error
u_{J}-v_{J}
into the subspaces
V_{j}
, i.e.,

u_{J}-v_{J}=\sum^{J}_{j=0}e_{j},\quad e_{j}\in V_{j},\quad j=0,1,\ldots,J.

(23)

Then

\displaystyle\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle r,u_{J}-v_{J}\rangle=\sum^{J}_{j=0}\langle r,e_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla r_{0}\|\cdot\|\nabla e_{0}\|+\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|\cdot\|h^{-1}_{j}e_{j}\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\|\nabla e_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}e_{j}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Taking the infimum over all possible decompositions (
23
) and using Appendix
10
,
12
yields

Estimate on algebraic error 3
.

\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|\leq C_{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(24)

Combining (
20
)–(
22
), the estimate (
24
) on the algebraic error, and using the inequality
\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}\leq\sqrt{2}\sqrt{a+b}
, we have

Estimate on total error 3
.

\|\nabla e\|\leq\sqrt{2}\left(C^{2}_{\mathrm{cls}}\eta^{2}_{J}+C^{2}_{I_{V_{J}},4}C_{S}\left(\sum^{J}_{j=0}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(25)

3.3
Estimates of Harbrecht & Schneider

In this section we present a derivation motivated by
Harbrecht and Schneider 2016
,
which is based on the fact that the basis functions provide a frame in
\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\#}
; see Appendix
10
,
14
. Recall that
\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\#}
is the dual space to
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Using the upper bound for the residual yields

\|\nabla e\|=\|r\|_{\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\#}}\leq C_{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{C}_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle r,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(26)

Following the derivation in
(
Harbrecht and Schneider 2016
, Proof of Theorem 5.1)
, it can be shown that the sum of the terms corresponding to levels
j>J
, i.e.,

\sum^{+\infty}_{j=J+1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle r,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}},

can be bounded by the classic residual based estimator on the
J
th level up to a constant
C_{\mathrm{HS}}>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{0}
, i.e.,

\sum^{+\infty}_{j=J+1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle r,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}\leq C_{\mathrm{HS}}\eta^{2}_{J}.

(27)

Combining (
26
) and (
27
) yields

Estimate on total error 4
.

\|\nabla e\|\leq C_{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{C}_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(C_{\mathrm{HS}}\eta^{2}_{J}+\sum^{J}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle r,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(28)

Considering the residual
r
as a functional on
V_{J}
, which is possible since
\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\#}\subset V^{\#}_{J}
, one can show that

\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|=\|r\|_{V^{\#}_{J}}.

From Appendix
10
,
15
, it yields that a part of the total error estimator (
28
) is an upper bound on the algebraic error,

Estimate on algebraic error 4
.

\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|\leq C_{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{C}_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum^{J}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle r,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(29)

3.4
New estimate derived using stable splitting

The approach from
Harbrecht and Schneider 2016
can also be modified in the following way. Consider the residual equation (
5
) and an arbitrary decomposition of the error
e=\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}e_{j}
,
e_{j}\in V_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Using the definition of scaled residuals (
6
) and (
7
), and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\|\nabla e\|^{2}=\langle r,e\rangle=\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}\langle r,{e}_{j}\rangle\leq\|\nabla r_{0}\|\cdot\|\nabla{e}_{0}\|+\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|\cdot\|h^{-1}_{j}{e}_{j}\|+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=J+1}\langle r,{e}_{j}\rangle.

(30)

Consider first the terms
\langle r,{e}_{j}\rangle
, for
j>J
.
Using the definition of
r
, Green’s theorem on elements
K\in\mathcal{T}_{J}
, and the definition of jump leads to

\displaystyle\langle r,e_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}fe_{j}-\int_{\Omega}\nabla v_{J}\cdot\nabla e_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{J}}\left(\int_{K}(f+\Delta v_{J})e_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{K,\mathrm{int}}}\int_{E}\left[\nabla v_{J}\right]e_{j}\right).

Since
v_{J}
is a piecewise affine function, the term
\Delta v_{J}
vanishes.
Adding and subtracting
f_{J}
yields

\langle r,e_{j}\rangle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{J}}\left(\int_{K}f_{J}e_{j}+\int_{K}(f-f_{J})e_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{K,\mathrm{int}}}\int_{E}\left[\nabla v_{J}\right]e_{j}\right).

Inserting
h_{K}h_{K}^{-1}
and
h^{1/2}_{K}h_{K}^{-1/2}
, respectively, and using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for integrals and subsequently for sums leads to

\displaystyle\langle r,e_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{K_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}h^{2}_{K_{j}}\|f_{J}\|^{2}_{K_{j}}+\sum_{K_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}h^{2}_{K_{j}}\|f-f_{J}\|^{2}_{K_{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{K_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}h_{K_{j}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{K_{j},\mathrm{int}}}\|\left[\nabla v_{J}\right]\|^{2}_{E}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\left(2\sum_{K_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}h^{-2}_{K_{j}}\|{e}_{j}\|^{2}_{K_{j}}+\sum_{K_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}h^{-1}_{K_{j}}\|e_{j}\|^{2}_{\partial K_{j}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
h_{j}=2^{J-j}h_{J}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{K_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}h^{2}_{K_{j}}\|f_{J}\|^{2}_{K_{j}}

\displaystyle=2^{2(J-j)}\sum_{K_{J}\in\mathcal{T}_{J}}h^{2}_{K_{J}}\|f_{J}\|^{2}_{K_{J}}=2^{2(J-j)}(\eta^{\mathrm{RHS}}_{J})^{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{K_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}h^{2}_{K_{j}}\|f-f_{J}\|^{2}_{K_{j}}

\displaystyle=2^{2(J-j)}\sum_{K_{J}\in\mathcal{T}_{J}}h^{2}_{K_{J}}\|f-f_{J}\|^{2}_{K_{J}}=2^{2(J-j)}(\mathrm{osc}_{J})^{2}.

Using that
\nabla v_{J}
is constant on elements in
\mathcal{T}_{J}
gives

\sum_{K_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{K_{j},\mathrm{int}}}\frac{h_{K_{j}}}{2}\|\left[\nabla v\right]\|^{2}_{E}=2^{J-j}\sum_{K_{J}\in\mathcal{T}_{J}}\frac{h_{K_{J}}}{2}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{K_{J},\mathrm{int}}}\|\left[\nabla v_{J}\right]\|^{2}_{E}=2^{J-j}(\eta^{\mathrm{JUMP}}_{J})^{2}.

The sum
\sum_{K_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}h^{-1}_{K_{j}}\|e_{j}\|^{2}_{\partial K_{j}}
can be bounded using Appendix
8
,
Lemma

5
as

\sum_{K_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}h^{-1}_{K_{j}}\|e_{j}\|^{2}_{\partial K_{j}}\leq C_{\mathrm{TI}}\sum_{K_{j}\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}h^{-2}_{K_{j}}\|{e}_{j}\|^{2}_{K_{j}}.

Thus we get

\langle r,e_{j}\rangle\leq\underbrace{\left(2+C_{\mathrm{TI}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(2^{2(J-j)}\left(\left(\eta^{\mathrm{RHS}}_{J}\right)^{2}+(\mathrm{osc}_{J})^{2}\right)+2^{J-j}\left(\eta^{\mathrm{JUMP}}_{J}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{\theta_{j}}\|h^{-1}_{j}e_{j}\|.

(31)

Combining (
30
) and (
31
) yields

\displaystyle\|\nabla e\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla r_{0}\|\cdot\|\nabla e_{0}\|+\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|\cdot\|h^{-1}_{j}e_{j}\|+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=J+1}\theta_{j}\|h^{-1}_{j}e_{j}\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=J+1}\theta^{2}_{j}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\left(\|\nabla e_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}e_{j}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=J+1}\|h^{-1}_{j}e_{j}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since the decomposition of
e=\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}e_{j}
,
e_{j}\in V_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is arbitrary, the stability of the splitting (Appendix
10
,
10
) gives

\|\nabla e\|\leq\left(\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=J+1}\theta^{2}_{j}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using

\sum^{+\infty}_{j=J+1}2^{2(J-j)}=\frac{1}{3}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum^{+\infty}_{j=J+1}2^{J-j}=1,

the infinite sum can be bounded as

\sum^{+\infty}_{j=J+1}\theta^{2}_{j}\leq C_{\theta}\eta^{2}_{J},

where
C_{\theta}>0
is a constant depending only on the dimension
d
and the shape-regularity parameter
\gamma_{0}
.
This results in the following estimate.

Estimate on total error 5
.

\|\nabla e\|\leq C_{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(C_{\theta}\eta^{2}_{J}+\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(32)

The fact that

C_{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

provides an upper bound on the algebraic error has already been shown in
Section

3.2
; see (
24
).

4
Efficiency of the estimates

Efficiency of the estimates is described by the constant
C_{\mathrm{eff}}
, such that

\mathrm{estimate}\leq C_{\mathrm{eff}}\cdot\|\mathrm{error}\|.

Here we in particular focus on whether
C_{\mathrm{eff}}
depends on the number of levels
J
, quasi-uniformity of the coarsest mesh, and/or on the ratio
h_{\Omega}/\min_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}h_{K}
, which is related to the size of the coarsest-level problem.

4.1
Efficiency of the estimates on the algebraic error

We will first discuss the estimates in the form

\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|\leq C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(33)

where either
C={C_{S,I_{V}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, or
C={C_{S}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
is a constant depending only on the dimension
d
and the shape-regularity parameter
\gamma_{0}
; see (
19
) and (
24
).
Using the definition of scaled residuals (
6
)–(
7
), the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the lower bound from Appendix
10
,
12
we have (see also the proof of Theorem 2.6.2 in
Rüde 1993
)

\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}=\sum^{J}_{j=0}\langle r,r_{j}\rangle=\\
=\int_{\Omega}\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\cdot\nabla\left(\sum^{J}_{j=0}r_{j}\right)\leq\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|\cdot\left\lVert\nabla\left(\sum^{J}_{j=0}r_{j}\right)\right\rVert\leq\\
\leq\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|\cdot{{c_{S}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-2}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\left(\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq{{c_{S}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|,

(34)

i.e., the efficiency constant depends only on the dimension
d
and the shape-regularity parameter
\gamma_{0}
.

The efficiency of the estimate (
29
)

\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|\leq C_{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{C}_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum^{J}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle r,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

can be shown by using
\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|=\|r\|_{\left(V_{J}\right)^{\#}}
and the lower bound from Appendix
10
,
15
, giving

\left(\sum^{J}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle r,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq c_{S}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\overline{c}_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|.

The efficiency constant depends only on the dimension
d
and the shape-regularity parameter
\gamma_{0}
.

Finally, for the estimate (
18
)

\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|\leq C_{I,\mathrm{2lvl}}\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|+C_{I_{0},3}\|\nabla r_{0}\|,

the equivalence of the Euclidean and
\ell^{1}
-norm,

\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|+\|\nabla r_{0}\|\leq\sqrt{J}\left(\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

and (
34
) yield

C_{I,\mathrm{2lvl}}\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|+C_{I_{0},3}\|\nabla r_{0}\|\leq\sqrt{J}\max\{C_{I,\mathrm{2lvl}},C_{I_{0},3}\}\,c^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{S}\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|.

This shows the efficiency of (
18
) with
C_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sqrt{J}\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{eff}}
, where
\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{eff}}
depends only on
d
and
\gamma_{0}
.
This result does not necessarily imply a dependence of (
18
) on the number of levels
J
. However, we present below in
Section

6.1
a numerical experiment indicating such behaviour.

4.2
Efficiency of estimates on total error

The efficiency of the total error estimates follows from the standard result on the efficiency of the classical (one-level) residual-based error estimator. There exists a positive constant
\overline{C}_{\mathrm{eff}}
depending on the shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{J}
such that

\left(\left(\eta^{\mathrm{RHS}}_{J}\right)^{2}+\left(\eta^{\mathrm{JUMP}}_{J}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\overline{C}_{\mathrm{eff}}\left(\|\nabla e\|+\mathrm{osc}_{J}\right);

(35)

see, e.g.,
(
Verfürth 2013
, Section 1.4)
.
Since
\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|\leq\|\nabla e\|
,
we can use the efficiency of the algebraic error estimates together with (
35
) to show the efficiency of the estimates on the total error (up to the oscillation term). The resulting efficiency constants depend on the same quantities as the efficiency constants for the algebraic error estimates.

For example, for the estimate (
25
) associated with the algebraic error estimate (
24
),

\sqrt{2}\left(C^{2}_{\mathrm{cls}}\eta^{2}_{J}+C^{2}_{I_{V_{J}},4}C_{S}\left(\sum^{J}_{j=0}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\left(\|\nabla e\|+\mathrm{osc}_{J}\right),

with
C^{2}=2(C^{2}_{\mathrm{cls}}(\overline{C}^{2}_{\mathrm{eff}}+1)+C^{2}_{I_{V_{J}},4}C_{S}c^{-1}_{S})
.

5
Computability of the error estimates

In this section we address several ways in which the scaled residual norms from the estimates presented in
Section

3
can be evaluated or bounded. When the scaled residual norms are replaced by their bounds, proving the efficiency of the estimates from
Section

3
becomes a nontrivial task.

We first state an algebraic formulation of the problem (
3
). Then we present the algebraic representation of the scaled residual norms and some of their bounds from the literature. As the main contribution of this paper, we present in
Section

5.4
a new approach for approximating the scaled residual norm on the coarsest level using adaptive number of conjugate gradient iterations. This yields total and algebraic error estimates which are provably efficient and robust with respect to the size of the coarsest-level problem.

5.1
Algebraic formulation of the problem, residual vectors

Given a basis
\Phi_{J}
of
V_{J}
, the problem (
3
) can be algebraically formulated as finding the vector of coefficients
\mathbf{u}_{{J}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\#\mathcal{K}_{J}}
of the function
u_{J}
in the basis
\Phi_{J}
such that

\mathbf{A}_{J}\mathbf{u}_{{J}}=\mathbf{f}_{J},

(36)

where
\mathbf{A}_{J}
is the
stiffness matrix
on the finest level
J
,

[\mathbf{A}_{J}]_{mn}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi^{(J)}_{n}\cdot\nabla\phi^{(J)}_{m},

and

[\mathbf{f}_{J}]_{m}=\int_{\Omega}f\phi^{(J)}_{m},\qquad m,n=1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{K}_{J}.

Recall that
\#\mathcal{K}_{J}
is the cardinality of the basis
\Phi_{J}
. We use the standard assumption that the right-hand side vector
\mathbf{f}_{J}
can be computed exactly using a numerical quadrature. If
\mathbf{f}_{J}
is only known approximately, an additional term must be added to the error bounds presented above; see e.g., the discussion in
(
Stevenson 2007
, Section 6)
.

Let
v_{J}
be an approximation of the solution
u_{J}
of (
3
) and
\mathbf{v}_{J}
be the vector of coefficients of
v_{J}
in the basis
\Phi_{J}
. Let
r
be the residual (
4
) associated with
v_{J}
.
Consider the residual vectors
\mathbf{r}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}
,
j=0,\ldots,J
,

\left[\mathbf{r}_{j}\right]_{m}=\langle r,\phi^{(j)}_{m}\rangle,\quad m=1,\ldots,{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}.

(37)

The vector
\mathbf{r}_{J}
corresponding to the finest level can be computed as

\mathbf{r}_{{J}}=\mathbf{f}_{J}-\mathbf{A}_{J}\mathbf{v}_{{J}}.

(38)

The residual vectors corresponding to coarser levels can be computed from
\mathbf{r}_{J}
by restriction. For the prolongation matrices
\mathbf{P}^{J}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{\#\mathcal{K}_{J}\times\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}
associated with the (nested) finite element spaces
V_{j}
,
V_{J}
and the bases
\Phi_{j}
,
\Phi_{J}
,

\mathbf{r}_{j}=(\mathbf{P}^{J}_{j})^{\top}\mathbf{r}_{J};

(39)

see, e.g.,
(
Rüde 1993
, Section 3.2)
, or
(
Vacek 2020
, Section 2.4)
for a more detailed explanation.

5.2
The terms associated with fine levels

In this section we present an algebraic form of the term
\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}
,
j=1,\ldots,J
, and several ways of bounding it by computable quantities presented in literature.

Let
\mathbf{c}_{j}
be the vector of coefficients of
r_{j}
in the basis
\Phi_{j}
. The definitions (
37
) of
\mathbf{r}_{j}
and (
6
) of
r_{j}
give

\left[\mathbf{r}_{j}\right]_{m}=\langle r,\phi^{(j)}_{m}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}h^{-2}_{j}r_{j}\phi^{(j)}_{m}=\sum_{n}\int_{\Omega}h^{-2}_{j}\left[\mathbf{c}_{j}\right]_{n}\phi^{(j)}_{n}\phi^{(j)}_{m},\qquad\forall m=1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{K}_{j}.

(40)

Let
\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j}
be a
scaled mass matrix
defined as

\left[\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j}\right]_{m,n}=\int_{\Omega}h^{-2}_{j}\phi^{(j)}_{n}\phi^{(j)}_{m},\qquad\forall m,n=1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{K}_{j}.

The equation (
40
) can then be expressed as
\mathbf{r}_{j}=\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j}\mathbf{c}_{j}
and therefore

\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}h_{j}^{-2}\Phi_{j}\mathbf{c}_{j}\cdot\Phi_{j}\mathbf{c}_{j}=\mathbf{c}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j}\mathbf{c}_{j}=\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}(\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j})^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}.

(41)

The evaluation of the term (
41
) thus involves the solution of a system with a possibly large
matrix
\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j}
. Instead of computing this quantity, one can seek a computable upper bound.

Let
\mathbf{D}_{j}
be a diagonal matrix
\left[\mathbf{D}_{j}\right]_{m,m}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{m}\cdot\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{m}
,
m=1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{K}_{j}
.
The stability of basis functions (Appendix
10
,
Lemma

12
) and (
123
) give

\displaystyle c_{B}\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}\leq\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}(\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j})^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}\leq C_{B}\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}.

(42)

The upper bound in (
42
) is used in
Rüde 1993
;
Huber 2019
to bound the algebraic error as

\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|\leq C_{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(C_{B}\sum^{J}_{j=1}\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{C}_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum^{J}_{j=1}\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(43)

where
\overline{C}_{B}=\max\{1,C_{B}\}
. For
\overline{c}_{B}=\min\{1,c_{B}\}
, using the lower bound in (
42
) and (
34
)

\Big(\sum^{J}_{j=1}\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\Big(c^{-1}_{B}\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}r_{j}\|^{2}+\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\overline{c}_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}c_{S}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|,

(44)

which proves the efficiency of the bound (
43
). Recall that
c_{B}
,
C_{B}
,
c_{S}
, and
C_{S}
only depend on
d
and
\gamma_{0}
.

Noting that

\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}=\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle r,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}},

(45)

we see that the algebraic error bounds (
29
) and (
43
) are identical.

The term (
41
) can also be bounded using other techniques, e.g., using the so-called mass lumping (suggested in
(
Becker et al. 1995
, Section 4)
) or the multigrid smoothing routines (see the discussion in
(
Huber 2019
, Section 4.5.2)
). By using these techniques, however, we introduce another unknown constant into the overall estimate and possibly weaken its efficiency.

In order to get a fully computable bound on (
41
) (i.e., a bound without any unknown constant) and to avoid solving an algebraic problem with a large matrix, we can proceed similarly to
Papež et al. 2018
. Define
\bar{r}_{j}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
to be (discontinuous) piecewise affine functions on
\mathcal{T}_{j}
such that for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{j}
,

\int_{K}h^{-2}_{j}\bar{r}_{j}\phi^{(j)}_{m}=\left[\mathbf{r}_{{j}}\right]_{m}\cdot\frac{1}{\#\left\{\bar{K}\in\mathcal{T}_{j};m\mbox{ is vertex of }\bar{K}\right\}}=:\left[\mathbf{r}_{{j},K}\right]_{m}\quad\forall\phi^{(j)}_{m}.

(46)

This ensures that

\int_{\Omega}h^{-2}_{j}\bar{r}_{j}\phi^{(j)}_{m}=\left[\mathbf{r}_{{j}}\right]_{m}=\langle r,\phi^{(j)}_{m}\rangle.

(47)

Since
\bar{r}_{j}
is piecewise affine on elements, the norms
\|h^{-1}_{j}\bar{r}_{j}\|^{2}_{K}
can be computed using the solutions of systems with local scaled mass matrices, i.e.,
\|h^{-1}_{j}\bar{r}_{j}\|^{2}_{K}=\mathbf{r}^{*}_{{j},K}(\mathbf{M}_{j,K}^{\mathrm{S}})^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{{j},K}
, where

\left[\mathbf{M}^{S}_{j,K}\right]_{m,n}=\int_{K}h^{-2}_{j}\phi^{(j)}_{n}\phi^{(j)}_{m},\qquad\forall m,n\in\mathcal{N}_{K}.

For the whole term
\|h^{-1}_{j}{r}_{j}\|
we have

\|h^{-1}_{j}{r}_{j}\|^{2}\leq\|h^{-1}_{j}\bar{r}_{j}\|^{2}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}\mathbf{r}^{*}_{{j},K}(\mathbf{M}_{j,K}^{\mathrm{S}})^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{{j},K};

(48)

cf.
(
Papež et al. 2018
, Eq. (5.9))
.

5.3
The term associated with the coarsest level

In this section we present the algebraic form of the term
\|\nabla{r}_{0}\|
and several ways of bounding it adapted from literature.

Let
\mathbf{c}_{0}
be the vector of coefficients of
r_{0}
in the basis
\Phi_{0}
.
Analogously to (
41
), using the definitions (
37
) of
\mathbf{r}_{0}
and (
7
) of
r_{0}
, we have

\left[\mathbf{r}_{0}\right]_{m}=\langle r,\phi^{(0)}_{m}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla r_{0}\cdot\nabla\phi^{(0)}_{m}=\sum_{n}\int_{\Omega}\left[\mathbf{c}_{0}\right]_{n}\nabla\phi^{(0)}_{n}\cdot\nabla\phi^{(0)}_{m},\qquad\forall m=1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{K}_{0}.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{0}
be the stiffness matrix associated with the coarsest level

[\mathbf{A}_{0}]_{mn}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi^{(0)}_{n}\cdot\nabla\phi^{(0)}_{m},\qquad m,n=1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{K}_{0}.

The vector of coefficients
\mathbf{c}_{0}
then satisfies
\mathbf{A}_{0}\mathbf{c}_{0}=\mathbf{r}_{0}
.
This leads to

\|\nabla{r}_{0}\|^{2}=\mathbf{c}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{A}_{0}\mathbf{c}_{0}=\mathbf{r}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{A}^{-1}_{0}\mathbf{r}_{0}.

(49)

The evaluation of the term
\|\nabla{r}_{0}\|^{2}
thus requires solution of the system with the stiffness matrix associated with the coarsest level.
For problems where the stiffness matrix is large, this can be too costly and in some settings even unfeasible.

An approximate solution
\widetilde{\mathbf{c}}_{0}
of
\mathbf{A}_{0}\mathbf{c}_{0}=\mathbf{r}_{0}
computed by the (preconditioned) conjugate gradient method with a fixed number of iterations was used in
(
Huber 2019
, Section 4.5.2)
.
The resulting term
\widetilde{\mathbf{c}}_{0}^{*}\mathbf{r}_{0}
might not be, however, an upper bound on
\|\nabla{r}_{0}\|^{2}
. Therefore, the resulting value may not led to an upper bound on the algebraic nor the total error.

The term (
49
) can also be bounded using a quantity involving only the inverse of a diagonal matrix.
Friedrich’s inequality (Appendix
8
,
Lemma

2
) imply that

\|w_{0}\|^{2}\leq C_{F}^{2}h_{\Omega}^{2}\|\nabla w_{0}\|^{2},\quad\forall w_{0}\in V_{0}.

(50)

Let
\mathbf{M}_{0}
be the mass matrix associated with the coarsest level, i.e.,

[\mathbf{M}_{0}]_{mn}=\int_{\Omega}\phi^{(0)}_{n}\phi^{(0)}_{m}
,
m,n=1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{K}_{0}.

The inequality (
50
) can be equivalently expressed algebraically as

\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{M}_{0}\mathbf{w}\leq C_{F}^{2}h_{\Omega}^{2}\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{A}_{0}\mathbf{w},\quad\forall\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\#\mathcal{K}_{0}}.

Since
\mathbf{A}_{0}
and
\mathbf{M}_{0}
are symmetric positive definite matrices we have

\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{A}^{-1}_{0}\mathbf{w}\leq C_{F}^{2}h_{\Omega}^{2}\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{M}^{-1}_{0}\mathbf{w},\quad\forall\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\#\mathcal{K}_{0}}.

This bound can be a possibly large overestimation; see the discussion in
(
Papež et al. 2018
, Sects. 3.1 and 5.2)
.
Define the diagonal matrix
\mathbf{D}_{0}
as
\left[\mathbf{D}_{0}\right]_{m,m}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi^{(0)}_{m}\cdot\nabla\phi^{(0)}_{m}
,
m=1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{K}_{0}
.
The term on the right-hand side can be further simplified using
the spectral equivalence of the mass matrix
\mathbf{M}_{0}
with
\mathbf{D}_{0}
; see inequality (
124
) in Appendix
10
.
Altogether we have

\|\nabla{r}_{0}\|^{2}=\mathbf{r}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{A}^{{-1}}_{0}\mathbf{r}_{0}\leq C_{F}^{2}h_{\Omega}^{2}\mathbf{r}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{M}^{{-1}}_{0}\mathbf{r}_{0}\leq C_{M}C_{F}^{2}\frac{h_{\Omega}^{2}}{\min_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}h^{2}_{K}}\mathbf{r}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{D}^{-1}_{0}\mathbf{r}_{0}.

(51)

As for the efficiency, this allows to prove, using the Inverse inequality (Appendix
8
,
Lemma

4
) and (
124
),

\mathbf{r}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{D}^{-1}_{0}\mathbf{r}_{0}\leq\frac{C^{2}_{\mathrm{INV}}}{c_{M}}\frac{\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}h^{2}_{K}}{\min_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}h^{2}_{K}}\|\nabla{r}_{0}\|^{2},

which indicates that bound (
51
) may not be robust with respect to
{h_{\Omega}^{2}}/{\min_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}h^{2}_{K}}
. Numerical experiments in
Section

6
illustrate this deficiency.

5.4
Adaptive approximation of the coarsest-level term

In order to overcome the deficiencies described above, we now present a new approach for approximating the term (
49
).
It consists of applying the preconditioned conjugate gradient method (PCG) to
\mathbf{A}_{0}\mathbf{c}_{0}=\mathbf{r}_{0}
and using lower and upper bounds on the error in PCG.
A number of PCG iterations is determined adaptively in order to ensure the efficiency of the resulting bounds on total and algebraic errors.

Let
\mathbf{c}^{(i)}_{0}
be the approximation of
\mathbf{c}_{0}=\mathbf{A}^{{-1}}_{0}\mathbf{r}_{0}
computed at the
i
-th iteration of PCG a with zero initial guess. Let
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
be the norm generated by the matrix
\mathbf{A}_{0}
, i.e.,
\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{A}_{0}}=\mathbf{v}^{*}\mathbf{A}_{0}\mathbf{v}
, for all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{\#\mathcal{K}_{0}}
.
The term (
49
) can be expressed using the following decomposition

\mathbf{c}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{A}_{0}\mathbf{c}_{0}=\underbrace{\sum^{i-1}_{m=0}\|\mathbf{c}^{(m+1)}_{0}-\mathbf{c}^{(m)}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{A}_{0}}}_{\normalsize{=:\mu^{2}_{i}}}+\|\mathbf{c}_{0}-\mathbf{c}^{(i)}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{A}_{0}},

(52)

which is a consequence of the
local orthogonality
in PCG. This formula was already shown for CG in the seminal paper
(
Hestenes et al. 1952
, Theorem 6:1, Eq. (6:2))
. The terms
\|\mathbf{c}^{(m)}_{0}-\mathbf{c}^{(m+1)}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
can be computed at a minimal cost from the scalars available during the computations.

It is crucial to note that the local orthogonality is in CG computations preserved proportionally to the machine precision. Therefore, (
52
) is valid, up to a negligible error, also in finite-precision computations; see the derivation and proofs in
Strakoš and Tichý 2002
(resp. in
Strakoš and Tichý 2005
for the preconditioned variant).

Let
\zeta_{i}^{2}
be an upper bound on the squared
\mathbf{A}_{0}
-norm of the error in the PCG computation, i.e., on
\|\mathbf{c}_{0}-\mathbf{c}^{(i)}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
; see, e.g.,
Golub and Strakoš 1994
,
Meurant and Tichý 2023
and the references therein
1
1

1

Strictly speaking, numerical stability of the upper bounds to the A-norm of the error in CG computations has not been rigorously proved. Well-justified heuristics supported by numerical experiments however suggest their validity also in finite-precision computations; see
Golub and Strakoš 1994
,
Meurant and Tichý 2023
.
.
The derivation of such a bound is based on the interpretation of CG as a procedure for computing the Gauss quadrature approximation to a Riemann–Stieltjes integral and typically requires a lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue of
\mathbf{A}_{0}
.
A simple lower bound can be derived using
(
Fried 1972
, Theorem 3)
, as

\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{A}_{0})\geq C_{F}^{-2}h^{-2}_{\Omega}\min_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{M}_{0,K}),

where
C_{F}
is the constant from Friedrich’s inequality (Appendix
8
,
Lemma

2
) and
\mathbf{M}_{0,K}
is the local mass matrix corresponding to
K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}
. If an upper bound
\zeta_{i}
is not available, the
\mathbf{A}_{0}
-norm of the error
\|\mathbf{c}_{0}-\mathbf{c}^{(i)}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
can be bounded using the ideas presented in
Section

5.3
; see also
(
Papež et al. 2018
, Section 3.2)
.

The approach then consists of running PCG for the coarsest problem until

\zeta_{i}^{2}\leq\theta\left(\sum^{J}_{j=1}\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}+\mu^{2}_{i}\right),

(53)

where
\theta>0
is a chosen parameter. Then we consider the bound

\mathbf{r}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{A}^{{-1}}_{0}\mathbf{r}_{0}\leq\mu^{2}_{i}+\zeta_{i}^{2},

(54)

which can be combined, e.g., with (
43
) to get an upper bound on the algebraic error

\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|\leq C_{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{C}_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum^{J}_{j=1}\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}+\mu^{2}_{i}+\zeta_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(55)

The criterion (
53
) guarantees that

\mathbf{r}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{A}^{{-1}}_{0}\mathbf{r}_{0}\leq\mu^{2}_{i}+\zeta_{i}^{2}\leq\theta\sum^{J}_{j=1}\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}+(1+\theta)\mu^{2}_{i},

(56)

which allows us to prove the efficiency of (
55
).
Indeed, using (
56
),
\mu^{2}_{i}\leq\|\nabla{r}_{0}\|^{2}
(see (
52
)), and (
44
)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}+\mu^{2}_{i}+\zeta_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\theta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}+\|\nabla{r}_{0}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\theta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,c_{S}^{-\frac{1}{2}}c_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(u_{J}-v_{J})\|\,.

The proposed strategy follows the ideas of
(
Papež et al. 2018
, Section 3.2)
.
In principle, the possible overestimation in
\|\mathbf{c}_{0}-\mathbf{c}^{(i)}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{A}_{0}}\leq\zeta_{i}^{2}
is controlled by (
53
) and it is compensated for within the procedure by performing extra iterations. This allows us to prove the efficiency even if the estimate
\zeta_{i}
is not very tight. However, in such case the number of extra iterations might be quite large; see
(
Papež et al. 2018
, Section 7.1)
.

Finally, we note that
\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}
in (
53
) can be replaced by any (efficient) bound on
\|h_{j}^{-1}r_{j}\|^{2}
. Then the algebraic error bound (
55
) should be changed accordingly, replacing
\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}
and
\overline{C}_{B}
.

6
Numerical experiments

The experiments focus on the efficiency of the error estimates on the algebraic error. In particular, we consider the estimate

C\Big(\sum^{J}_{j=1}\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}+\mathbf{r}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{A}^{-1}_{0}\mathbf{r}_{0}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(57)

and variants where
\mathbf{r}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{A}^{-1}_{0}\mathbf{r}_{0}=\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}
is replaced by computable approximations. This prototype covers most of the algebraic error estimates from
Section

3
, where the scaled residual norms
\|h_{j}^{-1}r_{j}\|^{2}
on the fine levels are efficiently approximated by
\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}
using (
42
).
As shown in the previous sections, approximating the coarsest-level term
\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}
while preserving the efficiency is more subtle.

For the experiments, we consider a 3D Poisson problem on a unit cube,
\Omega=(0,1)^{3}
, with the exact solution

u(x,y,z)=x(x-1)y(y-1)z(z-1)e^{-100((x-\frac{1}{2})^{2}+(y-\frac{1}{2})^{2}+(z-\frac{1}{2})^{2})}.

The problem is discretized by the standard Galerkin finite element method with piecewise affine polynomials on a sequence of six uniformly refined meshes with the same shape regularity (
2
). The associated matrices are generated in the FE software FEniCS
Alnaes et al. 2015
;
Logg et al. 2012
, and the computations are done in MATLAB 2023a.
The codes for the experiments are available from
https://github.com/vacek-petr/inMLEstimate
.

Given the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{J}
(the finest mesh varies in the experiments), the associated Galerkin solution
u_{J}
of (
3
) is for the purpose of the evaluation of the efficiency of the estimates considered (with a negligible inaccuracy) as a result of using the MATLAB backslash, or, for very large problems, using the multigrid V-cycle with an excessive number (30) of V-cycle repetitions.
The approximation
v_{J}
to
u_{J}
is given by a multigrid solver starting with a zero approximation and repeating V-cycles until the relative energy norm of the (algebraic) error
u_{J}-v_{J}
drops below
10^{-11}
. Each multigrid V-cycle uses 3 pre and 3 post Gauss–Seidel smoothing iterations. The problem on the coarsest level is solved using CG where the stopping criterion is based on the relative residual with the tolerance
10^{-1}
.
In order to monitor the efficiency for varying algebraic error, we will also plot below intermediate results after completing each multigrid V-cycle.

We observed very similar results also for a set of two-dimensional problems and a 3D problem with a more complicated geometry. These experiments can be found in the repository
https://github.com/vacek-petr/inMLEstimate
where also the data and codes are available.

7
Conclusions

This paper presents residual-based a posteriori error estimates on total and algebraic errors in multilevel frameworks inspired by several derivations from the literature.
It starts with algebraic error estimates containing sum of the (scaled) residual norms over the levels, including the coarsest one. Total error estimates incorporate additionally the standard residual-based estimator evaluated on the finest level.
Efficiency and robustness with respect to the number of levels and the size of the algebraic problem on the coarsest level were for several estimates of this type proved in literature. However, the estimates containing residual norms are not easily computable and applicable in practice.

Approximation of the scaled residual norms, i.e., the terms
\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{X}_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}
, where
\mathbf{r}_{j}
is the algebraic residual associated with the level
j
on all but the coarsest level does not represent a significant difficulty. Except for the coarsest level,
\mathbf{X}_{j}
is the scaled mass matrix denoted in the paper as
\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j}
and the term
\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}(\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j})^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}
can be bounded from above by the simpler term
\mathbf{r}^{*}_{j}(\mathbf{D}_{j})^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{j}
, where
\mathbf{D}_{j}
is an appropriate diagonal matrix, without affecting the efficiency and robustness.

Evaluating the residual norm
\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}=\mathbf{r}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{A}^{-1}_{0}\mathbf{r}_{0}
associated with the coarsest level, where
\mathbf{A}_{0}
is the stiffness matrix, is more subtle.
When using bounds or techniques to approximate
\mathbf{r}^{*}_{0}\mathbf{A}^{-1}_{0}\mathbf{r}_{0}
presented in the literature, the resulting (multilevel) estimates on the total and algebraic errors are no longer guaranteed to be independent of the size of the coarsest-level problem. This behaviour is illustrated by numerical experiments.

The approach proposed in this paper approximates the coarsest-level term
\|\nabla r_{0}\|^{2}
using the preconditioned conjugate gradient iterates.
A number of PCG iterations is determined adaptively such that the efficiency of the bound does not deteriorate with increasing size of the coarsest-level problem, and the efficiency and robustness of the multilevel error estimates is preserved. Numerical results support the theoretical findings.

The estimates for total and algebraic errors involve some constants that
must be approximately determined, which involves heuristics.
For residual-based error estimates, the constants can be determined for smaller problems with the same or analogous geometry where an approximation with very small algebraic error can be computed; see, e.g., the discussion in
(
Becker et al. 1995
, Section 7)
.
Since the new result in
Section

5.4
proves the robustness of the adaptive estimate with respect to the size of the coarsest-level problem, it provides a justification for extrapolating the estimated values of the constants from smaller to larger problems.

In view of a recent trend on using multiple precision in multigrid algorithms (see, e.g.,
McCormick et al. 2021
;
Tamstorf et al. 2021
), it is worth considering extension of the presented results to include effects of inexact (limited-precision) operations. This will require substantial further analysis. We plan to address this topic in the future.

Auxiliary results from the theory of PDEs and FEM

The following results are standard in PDE and FEM analysis. They are presented in various forms and sometimes with different names. We provide them in forms suitable for our development, with some standard references where the proofs can be found.

Lemma 1

(Bramble–Hilbert lemma)
.

There exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{BH}}(\mathcal{T})>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
such that for all
K\in\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\|w-c\|_{\omega_{K}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{BH}}(\mathcal{T})h_{K}\|\nabla w\|_{\omega_{K}}

\displaystyle\forall w\in H^{1}(\omega_{K}),

(61)

\displaystyle\inf_{p\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\omega_{K})}\|w-p\|_{\omega_{K}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{BH}}(\mathcal{T})h^{2}_{K}|w|_{H^{2}(\omega_{K})}

\displaystyle\forall w\in H^{2}(\omega_{K}).

(62)

For the proof, see, e.g.,
(
Scott and Zhang 1990
, p. 490)
and references therein.

Lemma 2

(Friedrich’s inequality)
.

Let
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain. There exists a constant
C_{F}(\omega)>0
such that for all
w\in H^{1}(\omega)
which have a zero trace on a part of the boundary
\partial\omega
of nonzero measure

\|w\|_{\omega}\leq C_{F}(\omega)h_{\omega}\|\nabla w\|_{\omega}.

(63)

When using Friedrich’s inequality on patches associated with the elements of the triangulation
\mathcal{T}
, there exists a constant
C_{F}(\mathcal{T})
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
such that for all
K\in\mathcal{T}

C_{F}(\omega_{K})\leq C_{F}(\mathcal{T});

see, e.g.,
(
Rektorys 1980
, Chapter 18)
.

Lemma 3

(Trace inequality)
.

There exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{TR}}(\mathcal{T})>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
such that for all
K\in\mathcal{T}
and all
w\in H^{1}(K)

\|w\|^{2}_{\partial K}\leq C_{\mathrm{TR}}(\mathcal{T})\left(h_{K}^{-1}\|w\|^{2}_{K}+h_{K}\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{K}\right).

(64)

For the proof, see, e.g.,
(
Carstensen 1999
, Proposition 4.1)
.

Lemma 4

(Inverse inequality)
.

There exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{INV}}(\mathcal{T})>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
such that for all
K\in\mathcal{T}
and all
w_{\mathcal{T}}\in S_{\mathcal{T}}

\|\nabla w_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{K}\leq C_{\mathrm{INV}}(\mathcal{T})h^{-1}_{K}\|w_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{K}.

(65)

For the proof, see, e.g.,
(
Elman et al. 2005
, Lemma 1.27)
.

The following lemma is a consequence of
Lemma

3
and
Lemma

4
.

Lemma 5
.

There exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{TI}}(\mathcal{T})>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
such that for all
K\in\mathcal{T}
and all
w_{\mathcal{T}}\in S_{\mathcal{T}}

\|w_{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{\partial K}\leq C_{\mathrm{TI}}(\mathcal{T})h^{-1}_{K}\|w_{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{K}.

(66)

Proof.

Bounding
\|w_{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{\partial K}
using the trace inequality yields

\|w_{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{\partial K}\leq C_{\mathrm{TR}}(\mathcal{T})\left(h^{-1}_{K}\|w_{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{K}+h_{K}\|\nabla w_{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{K}\right).

Applying the inverse inequality gives

\displaystyle\|w_{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{\partial K}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{TR}}(\mathcal{T})\left(h^{-1}_{K}\|w_{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{K}+h^{-1}_{K}C^{2}_{\mathrm{INV}}(\mathcal{T})\|w_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{K}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{\mathrm{TR}}(\mathcal{T})(1+C^{2}_{\mathrm{INV}}(\mathcal{T}))h^{-1}_{K}\|w_{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{K}.

∎

9
Quasi-interpolation operators

A quasi-interpolation operator is not explicitly used in the construction of the estimators but it is a crucial tool for proving the bounds.
In this section we present a quasi-interpolation operator as a generalization of nodal interpolation to integrable functions. We consider the quasi-interpolation operator used in
Oswald 1994
, which is closely related to the operator from
Scott and Zhang 1990
. Other, slightly different quasi-interpolation operators can be found, e.g., in
Clément 1975
;
Verfürth 2013
;
Carstensen 1999
. We list and prove some of the properties of the operator to be used later. The proofs of the properties are based on standard techniques. To keep the text self-contained and formally accurate we provide most of the proofs below.

The results in this section are mostly derived for a single mesh
\mathcal{T}
. We show that the constants only depend on the dimension
d
and the shape-regularity
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
and therefore we can again use them in the mesh hierarchy with the dependence on
d
and
\gamma_{0}
.

9.1
Nodal interpolation and its generalization

For a node
z\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}
, let
\Psi_{z}:C(\overline{\Omega})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denote the linear functional evaluation at point
z
, i.e.,

\Psi_{z}(w)=w(z)\quad\forall w\in C(\overline{\Omega}).

The standard nodal interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}:C(\overline{\Omega})\to S_{\mathcal{T}}
for continuous functions is defined as (see, e.g.,
Ciarlet 1978
;
Brenner and Scott 2007
)

\mathcal{I}w=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}}\Psi_{z}(w)\phi_{z}\quad\forall w\in C(\overline{\Omega}).

In order to construct an analogy of the operator
\mathcal{I}
for functions from
L^{1}(\Omega)
, the point evaluation is replaced by an appropriate average of the approximated function.
We will consider the quasi-interpolation operator defined in
Oswald 1994
and
Stevenson 2007
.

For a node
z\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}
, let
K_{z}
be a fixed element having
z
as its vertex, i.e.,
z\in K_{z}
. Let
\mathbb{P}^{1}(K_{z})
denote the space of linear polynomials on
K_{z}
and denote by
\widetilde{\Psi}_{z}
the restriction of the linear functional
\Psi_{z}
to functions from
\mathbb{P}^{1}(K_{z})
. Since
\mathbb{P}^{1}(K_{z})
is a finite-dimensional space, the linear functional
\widetilde{\Psi}_{z}
is bounded and it therefore belongs to the dual space
(\mathbb{P}^{1}(K_{z}))^{\#}
. Considering the space
\mathbb{P}^{1}(K_{z})
equipped with the
L^{2}
-inner product, the Riesz representation theorem (see, e.g.,
(
Brenner and Scott 2007
, Sect. 2.4)
) yields the existence of a function
\psi_{z}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K_{z})
such that

\widetilde{\Psi}_{z}(w)=w(z)=\int_{K_{z}}w\psi_{z},\quad\forall w\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K_{z}).

Since
\psi_{z}
is the Riesz representation of the point evaluation at
z
, it holds for all
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}
(recall that
\phi_{z_{2}}
is the hat function associated with
z_{2}
) that

\int_{K_{z_{1}}}\phi_{z_{2}}\psi_{z_{1}}=\phi_{z_{2}}(z_{1})=\begin{cases}1&z_{1}=z_{2},\\
0&z_{1}\neq z_{2}.\end{cases}

(67)

We will consider the quasi-interpolation operators defined as follows

\displaystyle I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}:L^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow S_{\mathcal{T}},\quad I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}}\left(\int_{K_{z}}w\psi_{z}\right)\phi_{z},

(68)

\displaystyle I_{V_{\mathcal{T}}}:L^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow V_{\mathcal{T}},\quad I_{V_{\mathcal{T}}}w=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{T}}}\left(\int_{K_{z}}w\psi_{z}\right)\phi_{z}.

(69)

These definitions and relation (
67
) imply that
I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}
and
I_{V_{\mathcal{T}}}
are projections onto
S_{\mathcal{T}}
and
V_{\mathcal{T}}
, respectively. Further,
I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}
preserves linear polynomials on
\Omega
and
I_{V_{\mathcal{T}}}
preserves linear polynomials on
\omega_{K}
for any element
K\in\mathcal{T}
whose patch
\omega_{K}
does not intersect with the boundary of
\Omega
, i.e.,
\overline{\omega_{K}}\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset
.

9.2
Local estimates

We now present local (elementwise) bounds on an interpolant
I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w
and the interpolation error
w-I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w
.

Theorem 1
.

There exist positive constants
\widehat{C}_{I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}},\ell}
,
\ell=1,2,3,4
, depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
such that for all elements
K\in\mathcal{T}
,

\displaystyle\|I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|_{K}

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{C}_{I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}},1}\|w\|_{\omega_{K}}

\displaystyle\forall w\in L^{2}(\omega_{K}),

(70)

\displaystyle\|w-I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|_{K}

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{C}_{I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}},2}h_{K}\|\nabla w\|_{\omega_{K}}

\displaystyle\forall w\in H^{1}(\omega_{K}),

(71)

\displaystyle\|w-I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|_{K}

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{C}_{I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}},3}h^{2}_{K}|w|_{H^{2}(\omega_{K})}

\displaystyle\forall w\in H^{2}(\omega_{K}),

(72)

\displaystyle\|\nabla I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|_{K}

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{C}_{I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}},4}\|\nabla w\|_{\omega_{K}}

\displaystyle\forall w\in H^{1}(\omega_{K}).

(73)

Proof.

The steps in the proof are inspired by
(
Oswald 1994
, pp. 17–18)
and
(
Scott and Zhang 1990
, Sections 3–4)
.

Using standard affine transformation to a reference element it can be shown that there exists a constant
C_{\psi}>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
such that for all
z\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}
,

\|\psi_{z}\|_{L^{\infty}(K_{z})}\leq C_{\psi}|K_{z}|^{-1},

(74)

and that there exists a constant
C_{\phi}>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
such that for all
K\in\mathcal{T}
and all
z\in\mathcal{K}_{K}
,

\|\nabla\phi_{z}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\leq C_{\phi}\rho^{-1}_{K};

(75)

see, e.g.,
(
Scott and Zhang 1990
, pp. 487–488)
.

Using Hölder’s inequality and (
74
) we can show that for all
z\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}
and all
w\in L^{2}(K_{z})

\left\lvert\int_{K_{z}}w\psi_{z}\right\rvert^{2}\leq\|\psi_{z}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(K_{z})}\left(\int_{K_{z}}\left\lvert w\right\rvert\right)^{2}\leq C^{2}_{\psi}|K_{z}|^{-2}|K_{z}|\|w\|^{2}_{K_{z}}=C^{2}_{\psi}|K_{z}|^{-1}\|w\|^{2}_{K_{z}}.

(76)

We now proceed to prove the inequality (
70
). Using that
0\leq\phi_{z}\leq 1
gives

\displaystyle\left\lVert I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\right\rVert^{2}_{K}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}\left(\int_{K_{z}}w\psi_{z}\right)\phi_{z}\right\rVert^{2}_{K}\leq\left\lvert\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}\int_{K_{z}}w\psi_{z}\right\rvert^{2}|K|

\displaystyle\leq(\#\mathcal{N}_{K})|K|\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}\left\lvert\int_{K_{z}}w\psi_{z}\right\rvert^{2}.

The inequality (
76
) and the fact that
\#\mathcal{N}_{K}\leq d+1
yields

\displaystyle\|I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|^{2}_{K}

\displaystyle\leq(d+1)|K|\sum_{z\in\mathcal{K}_{K}}C^{2}_{\psi}|K_{z}|^{-1}\|w\|^{2}_{K_{z}}

\displaystyle\leq(d+1)|K|C^{2}_{\psi}\max_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}|K_{z}|^{-1}\|w\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}

(77)

\displaystyle\leq(d+1)C^{2}_{\psi}\frac{|K|}{\min_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}|K_{z}|}\|w\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}.

(78)

Since
|K|
and
|K_{z}|
,
z\in\mathcal{N}_{K}
, are comparable up to a constant depending on
d
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
(in a shape-regular mesh, we can compare the size of any neighboring elements), inequality (
70
) follows.

To prove the inequalities (
71
) and (
72
), let
p
be a constant or linear polynomial on
\omega_{K}
. Using the fact that
I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}
reproduces linear polynomials and (
70
) we get

\displaystyle\|w-I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|_{K}

\displaystyle=\|w-p-I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}(w-p)\|_{K}

\displaystyle\leq\|w-p\|_{K}+\widehat{C}_{I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}},1}\|w-p\|_{\omega_{K}}

\displaystyle\leq(\widehat{C}_{I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}},1}+1)\|w-p\|_{\omega_{K}}.

Using the Bramble–Hilbert lemma (
Lemma

1
) gives

\|w-I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|_{K}\leq(\widehat{C}_{I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}},1}+1)C_{\mathrm{BH}}(\mathcal{T})h_{K}\|\nabla w\|_{\omega_{K}}

or

\|w-I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|_{K}\leq(\widehat{C}_{I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}},1}+1)C_{\mathrm{BH}}(\mathcal{T})h_{K}^{2}|w|_{H^{2}(\omega_{K})}.

It remains to verify the inequality (
73
).
Using the fact that
I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}
reproduces constants, we have, for arbitrary
c\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|^{2}_{K}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}(w-c)\|^{2}_{K}=\int_{K}\left\lvert\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}\left(\int_{K_{z}}(w-c)\psi_{z}\right)\nabla\phi_{z}\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\#\mathcal{N}_{K})\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}\|\nabla\phi_{z}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(K)}\int_{K}\left\lvert\int_{K_{z}}(w-c)\psi_{z}\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(d+1)\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}\|\nabla\phi_{z}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(K)}\left\lvert\int_{K_{z}}(w-c)\psi_{z}\right\rvert^{2}|K|

\displaystyle\leq(d+1)C^{2}_{\phi}\rho^{-2}_{K}|K|\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}\left\lvert\int_{K_{z}}(w-c)\psi_{z}\right\rvert^{2},

where we also used (
75
). Then, from (
76
), we get

\displaystyle\|\nabla I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|^{2}_{K}

\displaystyle\leq(d+1)C^{2}_{\phi}\rho^{-2}_{K}|K|C^{2}_{\psi}\max_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}|K_{z}|^{-1}\|w-c\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}.

Using the Bramble–Hilbert lemma (
Lemma

1
) and rearranging yields

\displaystyle\|\nabla I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|^{2}_{K}

\displaystyle\leq(d+1)C^{2}_{\phi}C^{2}_{\psi}\big(C_{\mathrm{BH}}(\mathcal{T})\big)^{2}\frac{|K|}{\min_{z\in\mathcal{K}_{K}}|K_{z}|}\cdot\frac{h^{2}_{K}}{\rho^{2}_{K}}\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}.

∎

For the interpolation operator
I_{V_{\mathcal{T}}}
, we can derive bounds analogous to those of

For the “inner” elements, i.e., the elements

such that patch

For the local interpolation error over the faces, we have the following bound.

Theorem 3

There exists a positive constant
\widehat{C}_{I_{V_{\mathcal{T}}},5}

9.3
Global estimates

We now state global variants of estimates for quasi-interpolants and interpolation errors.

For any
K\in\mathcal{T}
, let
C_{\mathrm{ovrlp}}(K)
denote the number of patches this element is contained in, i.e.,

C_{\mathrm{ovrlp}}(K)=\#\left\{K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T};K\subset\omega_{K^{\prime}}\right\}.

The constant
C_{\mathrm{ovrlp}}(K)
depends only on the geometry of the mesh
\mathcal{T}
, i.e.,
d
and the shape regularity
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
.

Theorem 4
.

There exist positive constants
C_{I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}},\ell}
,
\ell=1,2,4
, depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
such that

\displaystyle\|I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}},1}\|w\|

\displaystyle\forall w\in L^{2}(\Omega),

(83)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}h^{-2}_{K}\|w-I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|^{2}_{K}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\|h^{-1}_{\mathcal{T}}(w-I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w)\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}},2}\|\nabla w\|

\displaystyle\forall w\in H^{1}(\Omega),

(84)

\displaystyle\|\nabla(I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w)\|

\displaystyle\forall w\in H^{1}(\Omega).

Proof.

Using
1
,

\displaystyle\|I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|^{2}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\|I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|^{2}_{K}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\left(\widehat{C}_{I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}},1}\right)^{2}\|w\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}\leq\left(\widehat{C}_{I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}},1}\right)^{2}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}C_{\mathrm{ovrlp}}(K)\|w\|^{2}_{K}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\widehat{C}_{I_{S_{\mathcal{T}}},1}\right)^{2}\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}}C_{\mathrm{ovrlp}}(K)\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\|w\|^{2}_{K}.

The proofs of the other three inequalities are analogous.
∎

\displaystyle\forall w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

(88)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}h^{-1}_{K}\|w-I_{V_{\mathcal{T}}}w\|^{2}_{\partial K}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{I_{V_{\mathcal{T}}},5}\|\nabla w\|

\displaystyle\forall w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(89)

Let us now consider the mesh hierarchy as in
Section

2.2
. Since the constants
C_{I_{S_{j}},\ell}
and

depend only on

C_{I_{S},\ell}
and
C_{I_{V},\ell}
depending only on
d
and the shape regularity
\gamma_{0}
of the initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
.

Finally, we bound the difference of quasi-interpolates on two consecutive levels.

Theorem 6
.

There exists a constant
C_{I,\mathrm{2lvl}}>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{0}
such that for all
j\geq 1
and all
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\|h^{-1}_{j}(I_{V_{j}}w-I_{V_{j-1}}w)\|\leq C_{I,\mathrm{2lvl}}\|\nabla w\|.

(90)

Proof.

Using the fact that
h^{-1}_{j}=2h^{-1}_{j-1}
and the estimate (
87
) from
5
,

\displaystyle\|h^{-1}_{j}(I_{V_{j}}w-I_{V_{j-1}}w)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|h^{-1}_{j}(w-I_{V_{j}}w)\|+\|h^{-1}_{j}(w-I_{V_{j-1}}w)\|

\displaystyle=\|h^{-1}_{j}(w-I_{V_{j}}w)\|+2\|h^{-1}_{j-1}(w-I_{V_{j-1}}w)\|

\displaystyle\leq(C_{I_{V},2}+2C_{I_{V},2})\|\nabla w\|.

Taking
C_{I,\mathrm{2lvl}}
as
C_{I,\mathrm{2lvl}}=C_{I_{V},2}+2C_{I_{V},2}
finishes the proof.
∎

10
Stable splitting

This section presents several results on splitting (decomposing) a
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
-function or a piecewise polynomial function into a sum of piecewise polynomial functions.
Let a sequence of uniformly refined meshes
\mathcal{T}_{j}
,
j=0,1,\ldots
as in
Section

2.2
be given.

10.1
Splitting of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
into subspaces of piecewise linear functions

To make the text easier to follow we first state the main result of this section and subsequently provide auxiliary results and proofs. We will show that any function
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
can be uniquely decomposed using the quasi-interpolation operators
I_{V_{j}}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, as

w=I_{V_{0}}w+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w;

the convergence of the sum is understood in the space
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with the norm
\|\nabla\cdot\|
. This decomposition is stable, meaning that there exist positive constants
c_{S,I_{V}},C_{S,I_{V}}
such that for all
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

c_{S,I_{V}}\|\nabla w\|^{2}\leq\|\nabla I_{V_{0}}w\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}(I_{V_{j}}w-I_{V_{j-1}}w)\|^{2}\leq C_{S,I_{V}}\|\nabla w\|^{2}.

(91)

We will also show that the splitting of the space
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

into subspaces
V_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, is stable in the sense that there exist positive constants
c_{S},C_{S}
such that for all
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

c_{S}\|\nabla w\|^{2}\leq\inf_{w_{j}\in V_{j};\ w=\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}w_{j}}\|\nabla w_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}w_{j}\|^{2}\leq C_{S}\|\nabla w\|^{2};

(92)

the infimum is taken over all
(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\|\nabla\cdot\|)
-convergent decompositions.

We will show that the stability constants
c_{S,I_{V}},C_{S,I_{V}}
and
c_{S},C_{S}
depend
only
on
d
and the shape regularity
\gamma_{0}
of the initial mesh. In particular, the constants do not depend on the quasi-uniformity of the initial mesh or the ratio
h_{\Omega}/\min_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}h_{K}
. This result is important when considering settings where the problem associated with the coarsest level is difficult to solve and in practice can only be solved approximately.

Variants of these results can be found, e.g., in
Oswald 1994
;
Rüde 1993
;
Dahmen and Kunoth 1992
;
Dahmen 1997
;
Bornemann and Yserentant 1993
and references therein. Our form is, however, to the best of our knowledge, not presented in the literature.
The results in
Oswald 1994
;
Rüde 1993
;
Dahmen and Kunoth 1992
;
Dahmen 1997
are derived under the assumption that the initial mesh is quasi-uniform, and the authors do not track the dependence of the constants on
h_{\Omega}/\min_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}h_{K}
.
The results of
Bornemann and Yserentant 1993
are derived without the assumption on the quasi-uniformity of the initial mesh. The authors however consider only the splitting of piecewise linear functions.

We combine the approaches from
Oswald 1994
and
Bornemann and Yserentant 1993
. We first focus on showing the upper bound from (
91
), then continue with the lower bound, and later generalize it to show (
92
).

First, consider the
K
-functional in analogy to
(
Bornemann and Yserentant 1993
, Section 7, eq. (7.4))
. For
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
w\in L^{2}(\omega)
, it is defined as

K(t,w,\omega)=\inf_{g\in H^{2}(\omega)}\left\{\|w-g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}+t^{2}|g|^{2}_{H^{2}(\omega)}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad t>0.

(93)

Lemma 6
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
that have compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds that

\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}2^{2j}K(2^{-2j},w,\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}\leq C\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(94)

Proof.

A brief proof for
d=2
is given in
(
Bornemann and Yserentant 1993
, Lemma 7.3)
. We present its key part in more detail and for
d=2,3
.
We will show that the
K
-functional can be expressed in terms of the Fourier transform (here denoted by
F[\cdot]
) as

K(t,w,\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{t^{2}|\xi|^{4}}{1+t^{2}|\xi|^{4}}\big|F[w](\xi)\big|^{2}d\xi.

(95)

For
w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, using the properties of the Fourier transform,

\|w-g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+t^{2}|g|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{t^{2}|\xi|^{4}}{1+t^{2}|\xi|^{4}}\big|F[w](\xi)\big|^{2}+(1+t^{2}|\xi|^{4})\left(F[g](\xi)-\frac{F[w](\xi)}{1+t^{2}|\xi|^{4}}\right)^{2}d\xi.

(96)

By simple manipulations, one can show that the minimum is attained for

\widetilde{g}(x)=w(x)\ast F^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{1+t^{2}|\xi|^{4}}\right](x),

(97)

and it remains to show that
\widetilde{g}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
First, note that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|\xi|)^{2}\big(\frac{1}{1+t^{2}|\xi|^{4}}\big)^{2}<\infty,

and therefore, due to the characterization of Sobolev spaces using Fourier transformations (see, e.g.,
(
Oswald 1994
, Section 3.1.1)
),
F^{-1}[(1+t^{2}|\xi|^{4})^{-1}](x)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then use Young’s inequality for convolution (recall that by assumption,
w
is compactly supported and therefore
w\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
) and the fact that
\partial/\partial\xi_{i}(f*h)=(\partial f/\partial\xi_{i}*h)
to show that the
H^{2}
-norm of
\widetilde{g}
is bounded.

The equality (
95
) then follows by plugging in the expression for
\widetilde{g}
into (
96
) and performing algebraic manipulations. The rest of the proof of the lemma follows as in
(
Bornemann and Yserentant 1993
, Lemma 7.3)
.
∎

Lemma 7
.

Let
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a domain with a Lipschitz-continuous boundary. There exists a constant
C_{\alpha}(\omega)>0
depending on the shape of
\omega
such that for all
w\in H^{1}(\omega)
,

\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}2^{2j}K(2^{-2j},w,\omega)^{2}\leq C_{\alpha}(\omega)\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

(98)

Proof.

The proof for
d=2
is given in
(
Bornemann and Yserentant 1993
, Lemma 7.4)
. It is based on the use of an extension operator and
Lemma

6
. For the three-dimensional case, the proof is analogous as
Lemma

6
is valid also for
d=3
.
∎

Lemma 8
.

There exists a constant
C_{\beta}>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{0}
such that for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}
and all
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

h^{-2}_{K}\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}2^{2j}\|w-I_{S_{j}}w\|^{2}_{K}\leq C_{\beta}\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}.

(99)

Proof.

The steps in the proof are inspired by the development in
(
Oswald 1994
, Section 2.3)
and
(
Bornemann and Yserentant 1993
, Section 7)
.

We will use a scaling argument to consider an element
\widetilde{K}
with
h_{\widetilde{K}}=1
. This is done by a transformation
x=h_{K}\widetilde{x}
, where
x\in K
,
\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{K}
.
We denote
\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{x}):=f(x)
for any function
f
defined on
\omega_{K}
. Then

\|w-I_{S_{j}}w\|^{2}_{K}=h^{d}_{K}\|\widetilde{w}-\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}w}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{K}}.

(100)

From the definition of the interpolation operator one can write
\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}w}=\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}}\widetilde{w}
. In words, one can either consider the transformation of the interpolant
I_{S_{j}}w
or transform the function
w
to the element
\widetilde{K}
first and then consider the quasi-interpolation
\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}}
associated with the transformed mesh.

We will show that there exists a constant
C_{\delta}>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{0}
such that

\|\widetilde{w}-\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}}\widetilde{w}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{K}}\leq C_{\delta}\cdot\big(K(2^{-2j},\widetilde{w},\omega_{\widetilde{K}})\big)^{2}.

(101)

Let
\widetilde{g}\in H^{2}(\omega_{\widetilde{K}})
. Then

\|\widetilde{w}-\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}}\widetilde{w}\|_{\widetilde{K}}\leq\|\widetilde{w}-\widetilde{g}\|_{\widetilde{K}}+\|\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}}\widetilde{g}\|_{\widetilde{K}}+\|\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}}(\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{w})\|_{\widetilde{K}}.

(102)

Let
\widetilde{K}_{j}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j}
such that
\widetilde{K}_{j}\subset\widetilde{K}
. Then (thanks to the uniform refinement and
h_{\widetilde{K}}=1
,
h_{\widetilde{K}_{j}}=2^{-j}
) from
1
(inequalities (
70
) and (
72
)),

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}}(\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{w})\|_{\widetilde{K}_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{C}_{\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}},1}\|\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{w}\|_{\omega_{\widetilde{K}_{j}}},

(103)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}}\widetilde{g}\|_{\widetilde{K}_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{C}_{\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}},3}2^{-2j}|\widetilde{g}|_{H^{2}(\omega_{\widetilde{K}_{j}})}.

(104)

Define

U(\widetilde{K},j)=\left\{\cup\omega_{\widetilde{K}_{j}};\widetilde{K}_{j}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j},\widetilde{K}_{j}\subset\widetilde{K}\right\}.

The term on the right hand side of (
103
) can be bounded as

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}}(\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{w})\|_{\widetilde{K}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\widetilde{K}_{j}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j},\widetilde{K}_{j}\subset\widetilde{K}}\|\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}}(\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{w})\|_{\widetilde{K}_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\widetilde{K}_{j}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j},\widetilde{K}_{j}\subset\widetilde{K}}\widehat{C}_{\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}},1}\|\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{w}\|_{\omega_{\widetilde{K}_{j}}}

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{C}_{\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}},1}\max_{\widetilde{K}_{j}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j};\widetilde{K}_{j}\in U(\widetilde{K},j)}C_{\mathrm{ovrlp}}(\widetilde{K}_{j})\|\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{w}\|_{U(\widetilde{K},j)}

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{C}_{\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}},1}\max_{\widetilde{K}_{j}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j};\widetilde{K}_{j}\in U(\widetilde{K},j)}C_{\mathrm{ovrlp}}(\widetilde{K}_{j})\|\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{w}\|_{\omega_{\widetilde{K}}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{I_{S},1}\|\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{w}\|_{\omega_{\widetilde{K}}},

(105)

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
\widehat{C}_{\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}},1}=\widehat{C}_{{I_{S_{j}}},1}
(scaling does not change the geometry and shape regularity) and from the definition of
C_{I_{S},1}
. The term on the right hand side of (
104
) can be bounded as

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}}\widetilde{g}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{K}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\widetilde{K}_{j}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j},\widetilde{K}_{j}\subset\widetilde{K}}\|\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}}\widetilde{g}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{K}_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\widetilde{K}_{j}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j},\widetilde{K}_{j}\subset\widetilde{K}}\big(\widehat{C}_{\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}},3}2^{-2j}|\widetilde{g}|_{\omega_{\widetilde{K}_{j}}}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\widehat{C}_{\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}},3}\big)^{2}\max_{\widetilde{K}_{j}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j},\widetilde{K}_{j}\in U(\widetilde{K},j)}C_{\mathrm{ovrlp}}(\widetilde{K}_{j})2^{-4j}|\widetilde{g}|^{2}_{H^{2}(U(\widetilde{K},j))}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\widehat{C}_{\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}},3}\big)^{2}\max_{\widetilde{K}_{j}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j},\widetilde{K}_{j}\subset\widetilde{K}}C_{\mathrm{ovrlp}}(\widetilde{K}_{j})2^{-4j}|\widetilde{g}|^{2}_{H^{2}(\omega_{\widetilde{K}})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(C_{I_{S},3}\cdot 2^{-2j}|\widetilde{g}|_{H^{2}(\omega_{\widetilde{K}})}\right)^{2}.

(106)

Combining (
102
) - (
106
) yields

\|\widetilde{w}-\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}}\widetilde{w}\|_{\widetilde{K}}\leq\max_{\ell=1,3}C_{I_{S},\ell}\left(\|\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{w}\|_{\omega_{\widetilde{K}}}+2^{-2j}|\widetilde{g}|_{H^{2}(\omega_{\widetilde{K}})}\right).

From the definition of the
K
-functional,

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{w}-\widetilde{I_{S_{j}}}\widetilde{w}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{K}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\ell=1,3}C_{I_{S},\ell}^{2}\left(\|\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{w}\|_{\omega_{\widetilde{K}}}+2^{-2j}|\widetilde{g}|_{H^{2}(\omega_{\widetilde{K}})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\max_{\ell=1,3}C_{I_{S},\ell}^{2}\left(\|\widetilde{g}-\widetilde{w}\|^{2}_{\omega_{\widetilde{K}}}+\left(2^{-2j}\right)^{2}|\widetilde{g}|^{2}_{H^{2}(\omega_{\widetilde{K}})}\right)

\displaystyle=2\cdot\max_{\ell=1,3}C_{I_{S},\ell}^{2}\cdot\big(K(2^{-2j},\widetilde{w},\omega_{\widetilde{K}})\big)^{2}.

In the notation introduced above,
C_{\delta}=2\cdot\max_{\ell=1,3}C_{I_{S},\ell}^{2}
.

Using (
100
), (
101
) and
Lemma

7
yields

\displaystyle\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}2^{2j}\|w-I_{S_{j}}w\|^{2}_{K}

\displaystyle\leq h^{d}_{K}C_{\delta}\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}2^{2j}\big(K(2^{-2j},\widetilde{w},\omega_{\widetilde{K}})\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq h^{d}_{K}C_{\delta}C_{\alpha}(\omega_{\widetilde{K}})\|\nabla\widetilde{w}\|^{2}_{\omega_{\widetilde{K}}}.

Re-scaling back to
K
,

\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}2^{2j}\|w-I_{S_{j}}w\|^{2}_{K}\leq h^{d}_{K}C_{\delta}C_{\alpha}(\omega_{\widetilde{K}})h^{2}_{K}h^{-d}_{K}\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}.

Finally, note that the shape of
\omega_{\widetilde{K}}
depends on the shape regularity of the initial mesh and therefore
C_{\alpha}(\omega_{\widetilde{K}})
can be bounded, for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}
, by a constant
C_{\alpha}
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{0}
.
∎

Theorem 7
.

There exists a constant
C_{S,I_{S}}>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{0}
, such that for all
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\|\nabla I_{S_{0}}w\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}(I_{S_{j}}w-I_{S_{j-1}}w)\|^{2}\leq C_{S,I_{S}}\|\nabla w\|^{2}.

(107)

Proof.

From
4
,

\|\nabla I_{S_{0}}w\|^{2}\leq C_{I_{S_{0}},4}^{2}\|\nabla w\|^{2}.

For the rest of the sum,

\displaystyle\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}(I_{S_{j}}-I_{S_{j-1}})w\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}(I_{S_{j}}w-w+w-I_{S_{j-1}}w)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\left(\|h^{-1}_{j}(w-I_{S_{j}}w)\|^{2}+\|h^{-1}_{j}(w-I_{S_{j-1}}w)\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\left(\|h^{-1}_{j}(w-I_{S_{j}}w)\|^{2}+4\|h^{-1}_{j-1}(w-I_{S_{j-1}}w)\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}(w-I_{S_{j}}w)\|^{2}+4\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}\|h^{-1}_{j}(w-I_{S_{j}}w)\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot 5\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}\|h^{-1}_{j}(w-I_{S_{j}}w)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=10\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}2^{2j}h^{-2}_{K}\|w-I_{S_{j}}w\|^{2}_{K}

\displaystyle\leq 10\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}C_{\beta}\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}\leq 10\cdot C_{\beta}\cdot\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}C_{\mathrm{ovrlp}}(K)\|\nabla w\|^{2},

where we have used
Lemma

8
in the second to last inequality.
∎

Theorem 8
.

There exists a constant
C_{S,I_{V}}>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{0}
such that for all
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\|\nabla I_{V_{0}}w\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}(I_{V_{j}}w-I_{V_{j-1}}w)\|^{2}\leq C_{S,I_{V}}\|\nabla w\|^{2}.

(108)

Proof.

The key steps of the following proof of the upper bound were provided to us by professor P. Oswald in personal communications.
From
5
,

\|\nabla I_{V_{0}}w\|^{2}\leq C_{I_{V_{0}},4}^{2}\|\nabla w\|^{2}.

To bound
\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\left\lVert h^{-1}_{j}(I_{V_{j}}w-I_{V_{j-1}}w)\right\rVert^{2}
, consider the sequence
w_{j}=(I_{S_{j}}-I_{S_{j-1}})w
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}
. We will first show that there exists a constant
C_{\epsilon}>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{0}
such that

\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}2^{2j}\|(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w\|^{2}_{K}\leq C_{\epsilon}\sum^{+\infty}_{i=1}2^{2i}\|w_{i}\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}.

(109)

Since
I_{V_{j}}
are projections onto
V_{j}
, it holds that

(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w_{i}=0,\quad j>i.

(110)

Then for all
j\geq 1
, using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for sums and
2
,

\displaystyle\|(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w\|^{2}_{K}

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\left(\sum^{+\infty}_{i=j}(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w_{i}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{K}\left(\sum^{+\infty}_{i=j}2^{-i}\right)\left(\sum^{+\infty}_{i=j}2^{i}\left((I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w_{i}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot 2^{-j}\sum^{+\infty}_{i=j}2^{i}\|(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w_{i}\|^{2}_{K}

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot 2^{-j}\sum^{+\infty}_{i=j}2^{i}\cdot 2\left(\|I_{V_{j}}w_{i}\|^{2}_{K}+\|I_{V_{j-1}}w_{i}\|^{2}_{K}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot 2^{-j}\sum^{+\infty}_{i=j}2^{i}\cdot 2\cdot 2\cdot\left(\widehat{C}_{I_{V},1}\right)^{2}\|w_{i}\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}.

Consequently,

\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}2^{2j}\|(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w\|^{2}_{K}\leq\underbrace{8\cdot\left(\widehat{C}_{I_{V_{\mathcal{T}}},1}\right)^{2}}_{C_{\epsilon}}\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}2^{j}\sum^{+\infty}_{i=j}2^{i}\|w_{i}\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}.

Changing the order of summation,

\displaystyle\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}2^{j}\sum^{+\infty}_{i=j}2^{i}\|w_{i}\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}

\displaystyle=\sum^{+\infty}_{i=1}2^{i}\|w_{i}\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}\sum^{i-1}_{j=1}2^{j}\leq\sum^{+\infty}_{i=1}2^{2i}\|w_{i}\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}.

For the sum
\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w\|^{2}
, using (
109
),

\displaystyle\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}h^{-2}_{K}\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}2^{2j}\|(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w\|^{2}_{K}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}h^{-2}_{K}\sum^{+\infty}_{i=1}2^{2i}\|w_{i}\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon}\sum^{+\infty}_{i=1}2^{2i}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}h^{-2}_{K}\|w_{i}\|^{2}_{\omega_{K}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon}\sum^{+\infty}_{i=1}2^{2i}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}C_{\mathrm{ovrlp}}(K)\max_{\bar{K}\subset\omega_{K}}h^{-2}_{\bar{K}}\|w_{i}\|^{2}_{K}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{C_{\epsilon}\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}\left[C_{\mathrm{ovrlp}}(K)\frac{\max_{\bar{K}\subset\omega_{K}}h^{-2}_{\bar{K}}}{h^{-2}_{K}}\right]}_{C_{\zeta}}\sum^{+\infty}_{i=1}2^{2i}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}h^{-2}_{K}\|w_{i}\|^{2}_{K}.

Finally,
7
gives

\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w\|^{2}\leq C_{\zeta}C_{S,I_{S}}\|\nabla w\|^{2}.

∎

Now proceed with bounding the norm of the splittings from below by a
H^{1}
-seminorm. We start with some auxiliary lemmas.

Lemma 9
.

There exists a constant
c_{S}>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{0}
such that for any
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and any sequence
(w_{j})^{N}_{j=0},w_{j}\in S_{j}
,
j=0,\ldots,N
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\left(\sum^{N}_{j=0}w_{j}\right)\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{c_{S}}\left(\|\nabla w_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}w_{j}\|^{2}\right).

(111)

Proof.

The proof for
d=2
is given in
(
Bornemann and Yserentant 1993
, Lemma 3.4)
. It is based on the so-called Strengthened Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.
As the Strengthened Cauchy–Schwarz inequality is valid also for
d=3
(see, e.g.,
(
Xu 1992
, Lemma 6.1)
), the proof of the theorem in the three-dimensional case is analogous to the two-dimensional one.
∎

Lemma 10
.

Let
(w_{j})^{+\infty}_{j=0}
,
w_{j}\in V_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, be a sequence which satisfies

\|\nabla w_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}w_{j}\|^{2}<+\infty.

Then
\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}w_{j}
converges in
\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\|\nabla\cdot\|\right)
.

Proof.

We will use Lemma
9
to show that
(\sum^{N}_{j=0}w_{j})^{+\infty}_{N=0}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\|\nabla\cdot\|\right)
. Let
\epsilon>0
. Since
\|\nabla w_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}w_{j}\|^{2}
converges in
\mathbb{R}
, there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
m>n>M
, it holds that

\sum^{m}_{j=n}\|h^{-1}_{j}w_{j}\|^{2}<c_{S}{\epsilon^{2}}.

Using Lemma
9
for
w_{j}
,
j=n,\ldots,m
, and the previous inequality,

\left\lVert\nabla\left(\sum^{m}_{j=n}w_{j}\right)\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{c_{S}}\sum^{m}_{j=n}\|h^{-1}_{j}w_{j}\|^{2}<\epsilon^{2},

i.e.,
(\sum^{N}_{j=0}w_{j})^{+\infty}_{N=0}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\|\nabla\cdot\|\right)
and thus
\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}w_{j}
converges in
\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\|\nabla\cdot\|\right)
.
∎

Lemma 11
.

Let
c_{S}
be the constant from
Lemma

9
. Let
(w_{j})^{+\infty}_{j=0}
,
w_{j}\in V_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, be a sequence such that
\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}w_{j}
converges in
\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\|\nabla\cdot\|\right)
. Then

\left\lVert\nabla\left(\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}w_{j}\right)\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{c_{S}}\left(\|\nabla w_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}w_{j}\|^{2}\right).

Proof.

For any
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
Lemma

9
gives

\left\lVert\nabla\left(\sum^{N}_{j=0}w_{j}\right)\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{c_{S}}\left(\left\lVert\nabla w_{0}\right\rVert^{2}+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}w_{j}\|^{2}\right).

Since
\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}w_{j}
converges in
\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\left\lVert\nabla\cdot\right\lVert\right)
, we may switch the following limit and norm, giving

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\left(\lim_{N\to+\infty}\sum^{N}_{j=0}w_{j}\right)\right\rVert^{2}=\lim_{N\to+\infty}\left\lVert\nabla\left(\sum^{N}_{j=0}w_{j}\right)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{1}{c_{S}}\left(\left\lVert\nabla w_{0}\right\rVert^{2}+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}w_{j}\|^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{c_{S}}\left(\|\nabla w_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}w_{j}\|^{2}\right).

∎

Theorem 9
.

Any function
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
can be uniquely decomposed as

w=I_{V_{0}}w+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w;

(the convergence of the sum is understood in the space
(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\|\nabla\cdot\|)
).
Let
c_{S}
be the constant from Lemma
9
and
C_{S,I_{V}}
the constant from
8
. Then for all
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

c_{S}\|\nabla w\|^{2}\leq\left\lVert\nabla I_{V_{0}}w\right\rVert^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}(I_{V_{j}}w-I_{V_{j-1}}w)\|^{2}\leq C_{S,I_{V}}\|\nabla w\|^{2}.

(112)

Proof.

The upper bound is proven in
8
. Now we will prove the lower bound. Having the upper bound, we can use
Lemma

10
to show that the sum
I_{V_{0}}w+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w
converges in
\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\|\nabla\cdot\|\right)
and consequently, from
Lemma

11
with
w_{0}:=I_{V_{0}}w
and
w_{j}:=(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w
,

c_{S}\left\lVert\nabla\left(I_{V_{0}}w+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w\right)\right\rVert^{2}\leq\|\nabla I_{V_{0}}w\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}(I_{V_{j}}w-I_{V_{j-1}}w)\|^{2}.

It remains to show that
I_{V_{0}}w+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w=w
in
\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\|\nabla\cdot\|\right)
.
Since, for arbitrary
N\in\mathbb{N}
(see
5
),

\left\lVert w-\left(I_{V_{0}}w+\sum^{N}_{j=1}(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w\right)\right\rVert=\|w-I_{V_{N}}w\|\leq C_{I_{V},2}\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{K}\|\nabla w\|,

and
\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{K}\to 0
,

I_{V_{0}}w+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w=w
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. We will show by contradiction that
I_{V_{0}}w+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w=w
also in
\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\|\nabla\cdot\|\right)
. Let the sequence
I_{V_{0}}w+\sum^{N}_{j=1}(I_{V_{j}}-I_{V_{j-1}})w
converge in
\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\|\nabla\cdot\|\right)
to
\bar{w}\neq w
. Then, thanks to Friedrich’s inequality (
Lemma

2
) the sequence converges to
\bar{w}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, which is a contradiction with the uniqueness of the limit.
∎

Theorem 10
.

Let
c_{S}
be the constant from

Lemma

11
.
There exists a constant
C_{S}>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{0}
such that for all
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

c_{S}\|\nabla w\|^{2}\leq\inf_{w_{j}\in V_{j};\ w=\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}w_{j}}\|\nabla w_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}w_{j}\|^{2}\leq C_{S}\|\nabla w\|^{2}.

(113)

Proof.

From
9
we know that for any
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
there exists a decomposition
w=\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}w_{j}
,
w_{j}\in V_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, for which the upper bound holds with the constant
C_{S,I_{V}}
, so that we can take an infimum over all possible decompositions giving
C_{S}\leq C_{S,I_{V}}
.
The lower bound in (
113
) follows from
Lemma

11
.
∎

10.2
Splitting of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
into basis function spaces

This section presents a result on splitting a
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
-function into basis function spaces.
Denote by
V_{j,i}
,
j=1,2\ldots
,
i=1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{K}_{j}
, the space spanned by the basis function
\phi^{(j)}_{i}
,
V_{j,i}\subset V_{j}
.

First we will show that splitting a function
w_{j}\in V_{j}
into the basis function spaces
V_{j,i}
,
i=1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{K}_{j}
is stable.
This property is called stability of basis functions in the literature; see, e.g.,
(
Rüde 1993
, Definition 2.5.5)
and
(
Oswald 1994
, Assumption (A1), p. 17)
. We present this property in a form which suits our further development.

Lemma 12

(Stability of basis functions)
.

There exist positive constants
c_{B}
and
C_{B}

depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{0}
such that for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and all

w_{j}=\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}w_{j,i}\in V_{j},\quad w_{j,i}\in V_{j,i},\quad i=1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{K}_{j},

it holds that

\displaystyle c_{B}\|h^{-1}_{j}w_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\|\nabla w_{j,i}\|^{2}\leq C_{B}\|h^{-1}_{j}w_{j}\|^{2}.

(114)

Let
\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j}
be the so-called scaled mass matrix and
\mathbf{D}_{j}
the diagonal matrix defined as

\left[\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j}\right]_{m,n}=\int_{\Omega}h^{-2}_{j}\phi^{(j)}_{n}\phi^{(j)}_{m},\qquad\left[\mathbf{D}_{j}\right]_{m,m}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{m}\cdot\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{m},\qquad\forall m,n=1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{K}_{j}.

Let
\mathbf{w}_{j}
be the vector of coefficients of a function
w_{j}\in V_{j}
in the basis
\Phi_{j}
. Then
w_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}w_{j,i}
,
w_{j,i}=[\mathbf{w}_{j}]_{i}\phi^{(j)}_{i}
and (
114
) is equivalent to

c_{B}\mathbf{w}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{M}^{S}_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\leq\mathbf{w}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{D}_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\leq C_{B}\mathbf{w}^{*}_{j}\mathbf{M}^{S}_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}.

(115)

That is, the matrices
\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j}
and
\mathbf{D}_{j}
are spectrally equivalent with constants
c_{B}
and
C_{B}
.

Proof.

The proof is inspired by
(
Elman et al. 2005
, Proposition 1.30, Problem 1.35)
; see also
Fried 1972
.
We prove the spectral equivalence of local matrices associated with a mesh element. The assertion of the theorem for global matrices then follows by summing the local inequalities over the elements and taking the overlap into account.

Let
\mathbf{M}_{j,K}^{\mathrm{S}}
be a local scaled mass matrix corresponding to an element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{j}
defined as

\left[\mathbf{M}_{j,K}^{\mathrm{S}}\right]_{m,n}=\int_{K}h^{-2}_{K}\phi^{(j)}_{n}\phi^{(j)}_{m},\qquad\forall m,n\in\mathcal{N}_{K},

(116)

and let
\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{\hat{K}}
be the local scaled mass matrix on a reference element
\hat{K}
, which does not depend on
j
,
K
, or
\mathcal{T}_{j}
.
Using standard arguments of affine transformation to a reference element, it holds that

\mathbf{M}_{j,K}^{\mathrm{S}}=\frac{|K|}{h^{2}_{K}}\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{\hat{K}}.

(117)

If we denote by
c_{\hat{K}}
and

C_{\hat{K}}
the smallest and the largest eigenvalues of
\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{\hat{K}}
, respectively,
the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{M}_{j,K}^{\mathrm{S}}
can be bounded by
c_{\hat{K}}{|K|}/{h^{2}_{K}}
and
C_{\hat{K}}{|K|}/{h^{2}_{K}}
. Consequently,

\frac{c_{\hat{K}}|K|}{h^{2}_{K}}\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{M}_{j,K}^{\mathrm{S}}\mathbf{x}\leq\frac{C_{\hat{K}}|K|}{h^{2}_{K}}\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{x},\quad\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{(d+1)}.

(118)

By choosing
\mathbf{x}
as the
m
th column of the identity matrix of size
d+1
,

\frac{c_{\hat{K}}|K|}{h^{2}_{K}}\leq\left[\mathbf{M}_{j,K}^{\mathrm{S}}\right]_{m,m}=\frac{\|\phi^{(j)}_{m}\|^{2}_{K}}{h^{2}_{K}}\leq\frac{C_{\hat{K}}|K|}{h^{2}_{K}}.

(119)

Let
\mathbf{D}_{j,K}
be the local variant of
\mathbf{D}_{j}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{D}_{j,K}\right]_{m,m}

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{m}\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{m}=\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{m}\|^{2}_{K}.

(120)

Using the inverse inequality (
Lemma

4
) and (
119
),

\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{m}\|^{2}_{K}\leq C^{2}_{\mathrm{INV}}h^{-2}_{K}\|\phi^{(j)}_{m}\|^{2}_{K}\leq C^{2}_{\mathrm{INV}}C_{\hat{K}}\frac{|K|}{h^{2}_{K}}.

(121)

Similarly, using Friedrich’s inequality (
Lemma

2
),

\frac{c_{\hat{K}}|K|}{C^{2}_{F}h^{2}_{K}}\leq\frac{1}{C^{2}_{F}h^{2}_{K}}\|\phi^{(j)}_{m}\|^{2}_{K}\leq\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{m}\|^{2}_{K}.

(122)

Thus the matrix
\mathbf{D}_{j,K}
is spectrally equivalent to the identity matrix times
\frac{|K|}{h^{2}_{K}}
. From (
118
), we conclude that
\mathbf{D}_{j,K}
is also spectrally equivalent to
\mathbf{M}_{j,K}^{\mathrm{S}}
with the equivalency constants involving
C_{\mathrm{INV}}
,
C_{F}
,
c_{\hat{K}}
, and
C_{\hat{K}}
, i.e., depending only on
d
and the shape regularity
\gamma_{j}
.
∎

Since
\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j}
and
\mathbf{D}_{j}
are spectrally equivalent matrices and they are symmetric positive definite, we can use the generalized Hermitian eigenvalue decomposition (see, e.g.,
(
Bai et al. 2000
, Eq. (5.3))
) and algebraic manipulations to show that
\left(\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j}\right)^{-1}
and
\mathbf{D}^{-1}_{j}
are also spectrally equivalent, i.e.,

\frac{1}{C_{B}}\mathbf{w}^{*}\left(\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{w}\leq\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{D}^{-1}_{j}\mathbf{w}\leq\frac{1}{c_{B}}\mathbf{w}^{*}\left(\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{S}}_{j}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{w},\qquad\forall\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}.

(123)

Let
\mathbf{M}_{j}
denote the mass matrix associated with the
j
th level, i.e.,
\left[\mathbf{M}_{j}\right]_{mn}=\int_{\Omega}\phi^{(j)}_{n}\phi^{(j)}_{m}
,
m,n=1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{K}_{j}
.
Analogously to (
115
) we can show the spectral equivalence of the mass matrix
\mathbf{M}_{j}
with the diagonal matrix
\mathbf{D}_{j}
in the following form. There exist positive constants
c_{M},C_{M}
depending only on
d
and
\gamma_{0}
such that

c_{M}\min_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}h^{-2}_{K}\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{M}_{j}\mathbf{w}\leq\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{j}\mathbf{w}\leq C_{M}\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}h^{-2}_{K}\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{M}_{j}\mathbf{w},\quad\forall\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}.

(124)

Combining
10
and
Lemma

12
yields the following theorem on splitting a
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
-function into basis function spaces. It can be proven by the same technique as in
(
Rüde 1993
, Theorem 2.3.1)
.

Theorem 11
.

Let
c_{S},C_{S}
be the constants from
10
,
c_{B},C_{B}
the constants from
Lemma

12
, and let
\overline{c}_{B}=\min\{1,c_{B}\}
and
\overline{C}_{B}=\max\{1,C_{B}\}
.
Then for all
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle c_{S}\overline{c}_{B}\|\nabla w\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}w_{0}\in V_{0},w_{j,i}\in V_{j,i}\\
w=w_{0}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}w_{j,i}\end{subarray}}\|\nabla w_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\|\nabla w_{j,i}\|^{2}\leq C_{S}\overline{C}_{B}\|\nabla w\|^{2}.

(125)

V_{J}
,

(

.

Theorem 12
.

Let
c_{S}
and
C_{S}
be the constants from Theorem
10
. Let
J\geq 0
. For all
w_{J}\in V_{J}
,

\displaystyle c_{S}\|\nabla w_{J}\|^{2}\leq\inf_{w_{j}\in V_{j};\ w_{J}=\sum^{J}_{j=0}w_{j}}\|\nabla w_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{J}_{j=1}\|h^{-1}_{j}w_{j}\|^{2}\leq C_{S}\|\nabla w_{J}\|^{2}.

(126)

Theorem 13
.

Let
c_{S}
and

be the constants from Theorem

and
\overline{c}_{B},\overline{C}_{B}
the constants from

. Let
J\geq 0
. For all

,

\displaystyle c_{S}\overline{c}_{B}\|\nabla w_{J}\|^{2}\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}w_{0}\in V_{0},w_{j,i}\in V_{j,i}\\
w_{J}=w_{0}+\sum^{J}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}w_{j,i}\end{subarray}}\|\nabla w_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{J}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\|\nabla w_{j,i}\|^{2}\leq C_{S}\overline{C}_{B}\|\nabla w_{J}\|^{2}.

(127)

10.4
Frame

Finally, we present a consequence of the stability of the splittings presented in
11
, which is closely related to the fact that the normalized basis functions form a so-called
frame in

\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\#}
; see, e.g.,
(
Harbrecht and Schneider 2016
, Section 3)
,
Harbrecht et al. 2008
.

Theorem 14
.

Let
c_{S}
and
C_{S}
be the constants from Theorem
10
and
\overline{c}_{B},\overline{C}_{B}
the constants from
11
.
For all
g\in\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\#}
,

\displaystyle c_{S}\overline{c}_{B}\!\left(\|\nabla g_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle g,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|^{2}_{\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\#}}\leq C_{S}\overline{C}_{B}\!\left(\|\nabla g_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle g,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}\right)\!,

(128)

where
g_{0}\in V_{0}
is the Riesz representation of the functional
g
in the space
V_{0}
with respect to the inner product
(u_{0},v_{0})_{0}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla v_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0}
,
\forall u_{0},v_{0}\in V_{0}
.

Proof.

The proof is inspired by the proof of
(
Rüde 1993
, Theorem 2.6.2)
.

We will start with the upper bound. Let
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and consider an arbitrary decomposition
w=w_{0}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}w_{j,i}
,
w_{0}\in V_{0},w_{j,i}\in V_{j,i}
. Using the fact that

w_{i,j}=\frac{\text{sign}(w_{i,j})\left\lVert\nabla w_{i,j}\right\rVert}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|}\ \phi^{(j)}_{i},

we have

\displaystyle|\langle g,w\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq|\langle g,w_{0}\rangle|+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}|\langle g,w_{i,j}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\|g_{0}\|\cdot\|\nabla w_{0}\|+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\left|\left\langle g,\frac{\phi^{(j)}_{i}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|}\right\rangle\right|\cdot\|\nabla w_{i,j}\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|\nabla g_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle g,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\|\nabla w_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\|\nabla w_{j,i}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Taking the infimum over all decompositions
w=w_{0}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}w_{j,i}
,
w_{0}\in V_{0},w_{j,i}\in V_{j,i}
and using the stability of the decomposition into spaces defined by basis functions (
11
) yields

|\langle g,w\rangle|\leq\left(\|\nabla g_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{+\infty}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle g,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot C_{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{C}_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla w\|.

Taking the supremum over all
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\|\nabla w\|=1
gives the upper bound.

Proving the lower bound is more subtle. We will first show that for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\|\nabla g_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle g,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{c_{S}\overline{c}_{B}}\|g\|^{2}_{\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\#}}.

(129)

First, it holds that

\|\nabla g_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle g,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}=\left\langle g,g_{0}\right\rangle+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\left\langle g,\frac{\langle g,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}\phi^{(j)}_{i}\right\rangle\\
=\left\langle g,g_{0}+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle g,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}\phi^{(j)}_{i}\right\rangle.

Let
g_{j,i}=\frac{\langle g,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}\phi^{(j)}_{i}\in V_{j,i}
. Then using
13
,

\displaystyle\left\langle g,g_{0}+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}g_{j,i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\|g\|_{\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\#}}\left\lVert\nabla\left(g_{0}+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}g_{j,i}\right)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\#}}\frac{1}{c_{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{c}_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\|\nabla g_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\|\nabla g_{j,i}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\|g\|_{\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\#}}\frac{1}{c_{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{c}_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\|\nabla g_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle g,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This yields (
129
). Taking
N
to infinity in (
129
) finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem 15
.

Let
c_{S}
and
C_{S}
be the constants from Theorem
10
and
\overline{c}_{B},\overline{C}_{B}
the constants from
11
. Let
J\geq 0
and consider the space
V_{J}
with the norm
\|\nabla\cdot\|
. For all
g_{J}\in V_{J}^{\#}
,

c_{S}\overline{c}_{B}\left(\|\nabla g_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{J}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle g,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}\right)\leq\|g_{J}\|^{2}_{V_{J}^{\#}}\leq C_{S}\overline{C}_{B}\left(\|\nabla g_{0}\|^{2}+\sum^{J}_{j=1}\sum^{\#\mathcal{K}_{j}}_{i=1}\frac{\langle g,\phi^{(j)}_{i}\rangle^{2}}{\|\nabla\phi^{(j)}_{i}\|^{2}}\right),

where
g_{0}\in V_{0}
is the Riesz representation function of the functional
g
in the space
V_{0}

with respect to the inner product

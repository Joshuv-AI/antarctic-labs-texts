The third homotopy module of a 2-complex
W.H.Mannan

Abstract




A
BSTRACT
:
Given a connected 2- complex

X
with fundamental group
G
, we show how
\pi_{3}(X)
may be

\mathbb{Z}[G]

\mathbb{Q}[G]
.

§1 Introduction

Let
X
be a connected 2- complex with fundamental group
G
. Let

C_{*}(\tilde{X})
denote the algebraic complex over
\mathbb{Z}[G]

associated to the universal cover of
X
. We may write this
algebraic complex:

F_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial_{2}}}{{\to}}F_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial_{1}}}{{\to}}F_{0}

From the Hurewicz isomorphism theorem, we know that

\pi_{2}(X)\cong H_{2}(C_{*}(\tilde{X});\mathbb{Z}[G])\cong{\rm Ker}\partial_{2}

as (right) modules over
\mathbb{Z}[G]
. Hence
\pi_{2}(X)

is easily attained from
C_{*}(\tilde{X})
.

Now let
J
be a module over
\mathbb{Z}[G]
, which is freely generated over

\mathbb{Z}
. There is a
G
- action on
J\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}J
, given by
(a\otimes b)g=ag\otimes bg
, making it a
\mathbb{Z}[G]
module. Let

\sigma
be the
\mathbb{Z}[G]
- linear automorphism of
J\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}J

defined by
\sigma(a\otimes b)=b\otimes a
.

Definition 1.1

S^{2}(J)=\{x\in J\otimes J|\,\,\sigma x=x\}

We now state our main result:

Theorem A
We have an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}[G]
modules:

\pi_{3}(X)\cong S^{2}(\pi_{2}(X))

When
X
is a finite connected 2- complex, Schanuel’s lemma tells
us that the stable class of
\pi_{2}(X)
is determined by
G
. We
prove an analogous result for
\pi_{3}(X)
, under the added
assumption that
G
is finite, of odd order:

Theorem B

Let
X
and
X^{\prime}
be finite
connected 2- complexes, with finite fundamental group
G
. If the
order of
G
is odd, then
\pi_{3}(X)
and
\pi_{3}(X^{\prime})
are stably
equivalent.

Also if
G
is finite, of odd order we have the following result:

Theorem C

Let
X
be a finite connected
2
-
complex with finite fundamental group
G
. If the order of
G
is
odd, then
\pi_{3}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is free over
\mathbb{Q}[G]
.

Definition 1.2

G
- quadratic
A map

f:A\to B
between
\mathbb{Z}[G]
modules
A
,
B
, is
G
- quadratic if
given
a,b,c\in A
and
g\in G
we have:

i)
f(-a)=f(a)

ii)
f(a+b+c)=f(a+b)+f(a+c)+f(b+c)-f(a)-f(b)-f(c)

iii)
f(ag)=f(a)g

Lemma 1.3

Let
f:A\to B
be
G
- quadratic;

i)
f(0)=0

ii)
f(a+b)+f(a-b)=2f(a)+2f(b)

Proof:    i)
f(0)=f(0+0+0)=0

ii)
f(a+b)+f(a-b)=f(a+b)+f(a-b)+f(b-b)

=f(a+b-b)+f(a)+f(b)+f(-b)=2f(a)+2f(b)

\Box

Take
J
as before. We have a
G
- quadratic map
q:J\to S^{2}(J)

which maps
\alpha\mapsto\alpha\otimes\alpha
, for any
\alpha\in J
. The main argument of this paper will depend on a certain
universal property of
q
, which we mention as a preliminary.

Let
\{\alpha_{i}|\,\,i\in I\}
be a basis for
J
over
\mathbb{Z}

for some set
I
. Given
\alpha\in J
we may write
\alpha=\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\lambda_{i}
with each
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
only finitely many
\lambda_{i}
non-zero. We define the norm of

\alpha
to be
\sum_{i\in I}|\lambda_{i}|
.

The
\{\alpha_{i}\otimes\alpha_{j}|\,i,j\in I\}
are a basis
for
J\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}J
over
\mathbb{Z}
. So given
\omega\in S^{2}(J)
, we
may write
\omega=\sum_{i,j\in I}\lambda_{ij}\alpha_{i}\otimes\alpha_{j}
. Clearly for each
i,j\in I
we must have

\lambda_{ij}=\lambda_{ji}
. Hence
S^{2}(J)
is generated freely
over
\mathbb{Z}
by the
\alpha_{i}\otimes\alpha_{i}
and the
\alpha_{i}\otimes\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{j}\otimes\alpha_{i}
,
i\neq j
.

For each
i,j\in I
we have
\alpha_{i}\otimes\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{j}\otimes\alpha_{i}=q(\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j})-q(\alpha_{i})+q(\alpha_{j})
.
Hence another basis for
S^{2}(J)
over
\mathbb{Z}
is given by the

q(\alpha_{i})
and the
q(\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j})
,
i\neq j
.

Lemma 1.4

The map
q:J\to S^{2}(J)
is universal: Given any
G
-
quadratic map
f:J\to M
, there exists a unique
\mathbb{Z}[G]
- linear map

\phi:S^{2}(J)\to M
making the following diagram commute:

\textstyle{J\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{S^{2}(J)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{M}

Proof:    We define
\phi
linearly on a basis by:

\phi q(\alpha_{i})=f(\alpha_{i})

\phi q(\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j})=f(\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}),\,\,i\neq j

Clearly no other map can make the diagram commute. It remains to
show that
\phi q=f
and that
\phi
is
\mathbb{Z}[G]
- linear.

By lemma 1.3 ii) we have
\phi q(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})=f(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})
. Hence, by
construction we have
\phi q(\alpha)=f(\alpha)
for any
\alpha\in J
with norm less than or equal to
2
.

We proceed by induction. Suppose we have
verified that
\phi q(\alpha)=f(\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in J

with norm less than
r
, where
r\geq 3
. Then consider any

\beta\in J
with norm equal to
r
. We may write

\beta=\alpha+x+y
with
x=\pm\alpha_{i}
and
y=\pm\alpha_{j}
for
some
i,j\in I
, such that the norms of
\alpha
,
\alpha+x
,

\alpha+y
are all less than
r
.

Then property ii) of a
G
- quadratic map implies
that

\phi q(\beta)=\phi q(\alpha+x+y)=f(\alpha+x+y)=f(\beta)

So we have verified that
\phi q(\alpha)=f(\alpha)
for all

\alpha\in J
with norm less than
r+1
. Hence by induction

\phi q=f
. It remains to show that
\phi
is
\mathbb{Z}[G]
- linear.

By construction
\phi
is
\mathbb{Z}
- linear. Given

\alpha\in J
we have
\phi(q(\alpha)g)=\phi q(\alpha g)=f(\alpha g)=f(\alpha)g=(\phi q(\alpha))g
. Any element of

S^{2}(J)
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of elements of the form

q(\alpha)
,
\alpha\in J
. Hence
\phi
is
\mathbb{Z}[G]
- linear on the
whole of
S^{2}(J)
.
\Box

In §2 we consider a general topological space
X
with
\pi_{1}(X)=G
. We give a construction for a
G
- quadratic map
\pi_{2}(X)\to\pi_{3}(X)
. Whenever
\pi_{2}(X)
is freely generated over
\mathbb{Z}

lemma 1.4 will therefore give us a
\mathbb{Z}[G]
- linear map

\phi:S^{2}(\pi_{2}(X))\to\pi_{3}(X)
. In §3 we show that when
X

is a connected 2- complex, this map is an isomorphism.

§2 The Hopf fibration

Let
X
be an arbitrary topological space with basepoint
*
. Let

G
denote
\pi_{1}(X)
. We will consider the map which the Hopf
fibration
h:S^{3}\to S^{2}
induces on homotopy:
h^{*}:\pi_{2}(X)\to\pi_{3}(X)
. In particular we will show that it is
G
- quadratic.

We may regard
S^{3}
as
\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}|\,z\bar{z}+w\bar{w}=1\}
. Define a relation
\sim
, by setting
(z,w)\sim(z\lambda,w\lambda)
whenever
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
satisfies
\lambda\bar{\lambda}=1
.

Definition 2.1

Hopf fibration
The Hopf fibration,
h:S^{3}\to S^{2}
is the natural map

S^{3}\to S^{3}/\sim
composed with the identifications:
S^{3}/\sim

\cong\mathbb{C}P^{1}\cong S^{2}
.

We take
(0,1)
as basepoint of
S^{3}
and let
{\tiny+}=h(0,1)\in S^{2}
be the basepoint of
S^{2}
. We define
h^{*}:\pi_{2}(X)\to\pi_{3}(X)
to be the map given by precomposition with
h
. As
composition of maps is associative we know that
h^{*}
is natural:

Lemma 2.2

Let
f:X\to Y
be a basepoint preserving continuous map of
based topological spaces. Then
h^{*}f_{*}=f_{*}h^{*}
.

We first show that
h^{*}
satisfies property i) of a
G
- quadratic
map:

Lemma 2.3

Let
\alpha\in\pi_{2}(X)
. Then
h^{*}(-\alpha)=h^{*}(\alpha)
.

Proof:    Let
R:S^{3}\to S^{3}
be the rotation given by
(z,w)\mapsto(\bar{z},\bar{w})
. Let
\hat{R}:S^{2}\to S^{2}
be the reflection
induced on
S^{3}/\sim
. Then
R
is homotopic to the identity
(keeping the basepoint
(0,1)
fixed). So as elements of

\pi_{3}(X)
we have:

h^{*}(-\alpha)=\alpha\circ\hat{R}\circ h=\alpha\circ h\circ R=\alpha\circ h=h^{*}(\alpha)

\Box

Given
\alpha,\beta\in\pi_{2}(X)
, we follow
[
5
]
, Chap.
X, §7 by denoting their Whitehead product
[\alpha,\beta]
. We
also recall the following results from that section:

Proposition 2.4

The Whitehead product satisfies:

i) (Naturality, see (7.2)) Given
\alpha,\beta\in\pi_{2}(X)
we
have
f_{*}[\alpha,\beta]=[f_{*}\alpha,f_{*}\beta]
for any continuous
basepoint preserving map
f:X\to Y
.

ii) (Commutativity, see (7.5)) Given
\alpha,\beta\in\pi_{2}(X)

we have
[\alpha,\beta]=[\beta,\alpha]
.

iii) (Biadditivity, see (7.12)) Given
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta\in\pi_{2}(X)
we have:

[\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},\beta]=[\alpha_{1},\beta]+[\alpha_{2},\beta]
and
[\beta,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}]=[\beta,\alpha_{1}]+[\beta,\alpha_{2}]
.

Whitehead products are related to
h^{*}
by the following result:

Lemma 2.5

h^{*}(\alpha+\beta)=h^{*}(\alpha)+h^{*}(\beta)+[\alpha,\beta]

Proof:    See
[
5
]
, Chap. XI, (1.16) and from (4.4) note that
we may choose conventions so that the Hopf invariant of
h
is

1
.
\Box

We may now prove that
h^{*}
satisfies property ii) of a
G
-
quadratic map:

Lemma 2.6

h^{*}(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)=h^{*}(\alpha+\beta)+h^{*}(\alpha+\gamma)+h^{*}(\beta+\gamma)

-h^{*}(\alpha)-h^{*}(\beta)-h^{*}(\gamma)

Proof:
h^{*}(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)=h^{*}(\alpha+\beta)+h^{*}(\gamma)+[\alpha+\beta,\gamma]

=h^{*}(\alpha+\beta)+h^{*}(\alpha+\gamma)+h^{*}(\beta+\gamma)-h^{*}(\alpha)-h^{*}(\beta)-h^{*}(\gamma)

\Box

Finally it remains to show that
h^{*}
respects the action of
G
.
Let
\alpha:S^{2}\to X
represent an element of
\pi_{2}(X)
. We may
identify the complement of
+\in S^{2}
with the interior of the
closed unit disk
D^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
\alpha
extends naturally
to a map
\alpha^{\prime}:D^{2}\to X
which maps the boundary
\partial D^{2}
to the basepoint
*\in X
. Let
R_{\theta}
denote a positive
rotation of
D^{2}
through the angle
\theta
.

We consider
h^{*}(\alpha):S^{3}\to X
. For each

we
have a fibre
\{(\lambda z,\lambda w)|\,\lambda\bar{\lambda}=1\}
. If

- plane as it passes round the

z
- plane. The fibre
\{(0,\lambda)|\,\lambda\bar{\lambda}=1\}
gets mapped to
*\in X
by
h^{*}(\alpha)
. We
may deform
h^{*}(\alpha)
to thicken this fibre. The remaining

h^{*}(\alpha)\in\pi_{3}(X)
by the map
\alpha^{T}:S^{3}\to X
defined
as follows:

Consider an unknotted solid torus
N\subset S^{3}
, not containing
the basepoint. We make an untwisted topological identification of

N
with
S^{1}\times D^{2}
. Then:

\alpha^{T}(\theta,d)=\alpha^{\prime}(R_{\theta}(d))\,\qquad\qquad(\theta,d)\in N

\alpha^{T}(p)\quad=\,*\quad\quad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\,p\notin N

Let
I
denote the closed unit interval and let

represent an element of
\pi_{1}(X)
, so
g(0)=g(1)=*
. Then
g

acts on
\alpha
by stretching it round the loop

. Formally we
have
\alpha g:D^{2}\to X

\alpha g(d)=\alpha^{\prime}(2d)\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad|d|\leq\frac{1}{2}

\alpha g(d)=g(2|d|-1)\,\quad\qquad\qquad|d|\geq\frac{1}{2}

Let
K\subset S^{3}
be a thickened torus (parameterized

), bounding
N
and not enclosing the basepoint. Then

h^{*}(\alpha g)
is represented by the map

(\alpha g)^{T}(\theta,d)=\alpha^{\prime}(R_{\theta}(d))\,\qquad\qquad(\theta,d)\in N

(\alpha g)^{T}(t,\,i)\,=g(i)\,\qquad\qquad\,\,\,\qquad(t,\,i)\,\in K

(\alpha g)^{T}(p)\quad=\,*\,\,\,\,\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\,p\,\notin N,K

Let
L\subset S^{3}
be a thickened sphere (parameterized
S^{2}\times I
), enclosing
N
and not enclosing the basepoint. The

g

\pi_{3}(X)
by stretching
it round the loop
g

h^{*}(\alpha)g

the map
\alpha^{T}g:S^{3}\to X
, given by:

\alpha^{T}g(\theta,d)=\alpha^{\prime}(R_{\theta}(d))\,\qquad\qquad(\theta,d)\in N

\alpha^{T}g(s,\,i)\,=g(i)\,\qquad\qquad\,\,\,\qquad(s,\,i)\in L

\alpha^{T}g(p)\quad=\,*\,\,\,\,\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\,p\,\notin N,L

Lemma 2.7

Let
g
be an element of
\pi_{1}(X)
and let
\alpha
be an

\pi_{2}(X)
. Then

h^{*}(\alpha)g=h^{*}(\alpha g)
.

Proof: We must show that
\alpha^{T}g
is homotopic to
(\alpha g)^{T}
. Let

be a solid cylinder which passes
through the hole in the solid torus

and passes through the

We may parameterize
C
as

so that for

,

m_{t}(i)=g(5r-.5)\qquad\qquad\quad r\in[.1,.3]

- quadratic map.

Proof: We get property i) from lemma 2.3, property ii) from lemma

is an

\pi_{2}(X)

\mathbb{Z}

We have a
\mathbb{Z}
- basis for
\pi_{3}(X)

,

Proof:    As
m_{*}
is an isomorphism

\phi

Proof:    Recall that the

\Box

Theorem A

\mathbb{Z}[G]

\pi_{3}(X)\cong S^{2}(\pi_{2}(X))

§4 The effect of stabilizing
\pi_{2}
.

Let
X
be a finite connected
2
- complex with finite fundamental
group
G
. Let
X^{\prime}
be another finite connected
2
- complex with
fundamental group
G
. By Schanuel’s lemma we know that there
exist integers
a,b
such that

\pi_{2}(X)\oplus\mathbb{Z}[G]^{a}\cong\pi_{2}(X^{\prime})\oplus\mathbb{Z}[G]^{b}

In this section we investigate the corresponding relationship
between
\pi_{3}(X)
and
\pi_{3}(X^{\prime})

If we let
J=\pi_{2}(X)
and
J^{\prime}=\pi_{2}(X^{\prime})
, then from Theorem A we have

\pi_{3}(X)\cong S^{2}(J)
and
\pi_{3}(X^{\prime})\cong S^{2}(J^{\prime})

S^{2}(J\oplus\mathbb{Z}[G]^{a})\cong S^{2}(J^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{Z}[G]^{b})

The next few lemmas give us an expansion of this.

, let the

be a basis over

\mathbb{Z}
for

. Then
S^{2}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}A_{i})
is freely
generated over
\mathbb{Z}
, by elements of the form:

\boldsymbol{e_{i,r}}\otimes\boldsymbol{e_{i,r}}
,

\boldsymbol{e_{i,r}}\otimes\boldsymbol{e_{i,s}}+\boldsymbol{e_{i,s}}\otimes\boldsymbol{e_{i,r}}\qquad\qquad\qquad r<s
,

\boldsymbol{e_{i,r}}\otimes\boldsymbol{e_{j,s}}+\boldsymbol{e_{j,s}}\otimes\boldsymbol{e_{i,r}}\qquad\qquad\qquad i<j
,

For each
i
, the
\mathbb{Z}
- linear span of the

\boldsymbol{e_{i,r}}\otimes\boldsymbol{e_{i,r}}
,

\boldsymbol{e_{i,r}}\otimes\boldsymbol{e_{i,s}}+\boldsymbol{e_{i,s}}\otimes\boldsymbol{e_{i,r}}\qquad\qquad\qquad r<s
,

is closed under the group action and is isomorphic (over

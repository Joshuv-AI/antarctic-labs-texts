Abstract
. We consider a fully discrete scheme for nonlinear stochastic PDEs with non-globally Lipschitz coefficients driven by multiplicative noise in a multi-dimensional setting. Our method uses a polynomial based spectral method in space, so it does not require the elliptic operator A A and the covariance operator Q Q of noise in the equation commute, and thus successfully alleviates a restriction of Fourier spectral method for SPDEs pointed out by Jentzen, Kloeden and Winkel in [ 17 ] . The discretization in time is a tamed semi-implicit scheme which treats the nonlinear term explicitly while being unconditionally stable. Under regular assumptions which are usually made for SPDEs with additive noise, we establish optimal strong convergence rates in both space and time for our fully discrete scheme. We also present numerical experiments which are consistent with our theoretical results.

1.
Introduction

We consider numerical approximation of the following nonlinear stochastic PDE perturbed by multiplicative
noise:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{du=Audt+F(u)dt+G(u)dW^{Q}(t),\;\;x\in\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ }(=1,2)},\\
u(t,x)=0,x\in\partial\mathcal{O},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\;\;x\in\mathcal{O},\end{cases}

where
A
is the Laplacian operator on
\mathcal{O}
,

F
is the Nemytskii operator defined by
F(u)(\xi)=f(u(\xi)),\ \xi\in\mathcal{O}
, where

f
is an odd-degree polynomial with negative leading coefficient satisfying Assumption
2.2
. In particular, if
f(u)=u-u^{3}
, the equation becomes the well-known stochastic Allen-Cahn equation.
G(u)(\xi)=g(u(\xi))
is another Nemytskii operator, where
g(u)
is a Lipschitz continuous function with linear growth satisfying Assumption
2.3
, and
W^{Q}(t)
is a Q-Wiener process on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbf{P})
defined by (cf.
[
32
]
)

\displaystyle W^{Q}(t)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{q}_{j}e_{j}\beta_{j}(t),

where
\beta_{j}(t)
are independent standard Wiener processes, and
\{(q_{j},e_{j})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
are eigen-pairs of a symmetric non-negative operator
Q
. We emphasize that
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
are not necessarily eigenfunctions of
A
in
\mathcal{O}
.

It is well known that for
u_{0}\in C(\mathcal{O})
, (
1.1
) admits a unique mild solution in
L^{p}(\Omega;C((0,T);H)\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H))
for arbitrary
p\geq 1
, that satisfy (cf.
[
8
]
)

(1.2)

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle=e^{tA}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}f(u(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}g(u(s))dW^{Q}(s).

Moreover, under certain conditions to be specified later,
\mathbf{E}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|(-A)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}u(t)\|^{2}<\infty
for some
\gamma>1
(cf. Theorem
2.1
below).

[

, the authors considered a finite element method (FEM) for stochastic Allen-Cahn equation driven by the gradient type multiplicative noise under sufficient spatial regularity assumptions, and in
[

, where
\beta(t)
is a Brownian motion. In both cases, fully implicit time discretization schemes are used so that a nonlinear system has to be solved at each time step.

The main goal of this paper is to design and analyze a strongly convergent, linear and fully decoupled numerical method for SPDEs with local Lipschitz condition and driven by multiplicative noise in a multi-dimensional framework.
To avoid using a fully implicit scheme for SPDEs with local Lipschitz condition, we construct a tamed semi-implicit scheme in time (cf.
[

,
38
,
15
,
40

) and show (cf. Theorem

On the other hand, we adopt as spatial discretization a spectral-Galerkin method.
Distinguished for their high resolution and relative low computational cost for a given accuracy threshold, spectral methods have become a major computational tool for solving PDEs. However, only limited attempts have been made for using spectral methods
for SPDEs (cf.
[
4
,
17
,
18
]
), and most of these attempts are confined to Fourier spectral methods. Note that the use of Fourier-spectral methods in these work is essential as Fourier basis functions are eigenfunctions of the elliptic operator
-A

Since in our tamed semi-implicit scheme, the nonlinear term and the noise terms are treated explicitly, we shall employ a polynomial-based spectral method for spatial approximation to overcome the restriction mentioned above.
A key ingredient is to use a set of specially constructed Fourier-like it discrete eigenfunctions of
A
(cf.
[
36

), which are mutually orthogonal in both
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
and
H^{1}(\mathcal{O})
.

Combining the above ingredients together, we develop a fully discretized scheme that can be fully decoupled, making it very efficient.
Moreover, our method yields the following convergence rate under regular assumptions (Assumption
2.1
-Assumption

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}\|u(t_{k})-u_{N}^{k}\|\leq C(N^{-\gamma}+\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}),}

where

is the full-discretization of

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we first describe some notations and a few lemmas which will be used in our analysis, and then we present several general assumptions for the problem under consideration.

2.1.
Notations

We begin with notations. Let
U
and
V
be separable Hilbert spaces. We denote the norm in
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbf{P};U)
by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;U)}
, that is,

\|Y\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;U)}=\big(\mathbf{E}\big[\|Y\|_{U}^{p}\big]\big)^{\frac{1}{p}},\;\;Y\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbf{P};U).

Denote by
L_{1}(U,V)
the nuclear operator space from
U
to
V
and for
T\in L_{1}(U,V)
, its norm is given by

\|T\|_{L_{1}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}|(Te_{i},e_{i})_{U}|\ \;\text{and}\;\;Tr(T)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}(Te_{i},e_{i})_{U}

for any orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}
of
U
. In particular, if
T>0
, then
\|T\|_{L^{1}}=Tr(T)
. In this work, we assume that
W^{Q}(t)
is of trace class, i.e.

Tr(Q)<\infty
. Let
L_{2}(U,V)
be the Hilbert-Schmidt space such that for any
T\in L_{2}(U,V)

\|T\|_{L_{2}}=\bigg(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\|Te_{i}\|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}<\infty.

Moreover, if
Q
is of trace class, we introduce
L_{2}^{0}=L_{2}(U_{0},V)
with norm

\|T\|_{L_{2}^{0}}=\|TQ^{1/2}\|_{L_{2}(U,V)},

where
U_{0}=Q^{1/2}(U)
.

The following properties are frequently used hereafter

\|ST\|_{L_{2}}\leq\|S\|_{L_{1}}\|T\|_{L_{2}},\;\|TS\|_{L_{2}}\leq\|T\|_{L_{2}}\|S\|_{L_{1}}\ \;S\in L_{1}(U,V),\ T\in L_{2}(U,V).

Finally, when no confusion arises, we will drop the spatial dependency from the notations, i.e.,
u(t)=u(t,x)
.

2.2.
Some useful lemmas

We start with the Burkhold-Davis-Gundy-type inequality, which is a generalization of Ito isometry.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
21
, Theorem 6.1.2]

For any
p\geq 2,0\leq t\leq T
, and for any predictable stochastic process
\Phi(\sigma)
which satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(\mathbf{E}\|\Phi(\sigma)\|_{L_{2}^{0}}^{p}\bigg)^{2/p}d\sigma<\infty,

we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\mathbf{E}\bigg(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}\Phi(\sigma)dW(\sigma)\bigg\|^{p}\bigg)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C(p)\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(\mathbf{E}\|\Phi(\sigma)\|_{L_{2}^{0}}^{p}\bigg)^{2/p}d\sigma\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\displaystyle{C(p)=p\bigg(\frac{p}{2(p-1)}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

We recall the following generalized Gronwall’s inequality and its discretized version:

Lemma 2.2
.

(Generalized Gronwall’s lemma
[
13
]
)
Let
T>0
and
C_{1},C_{2}\geq 0
and let
\phi
be a nonnegative and consitnuous function. Let
\beta>0
. If we have

\phi(t)\leq C_{1}+C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\phi(s)ds,

\phi(t)\leq CC_{1}.

Let

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\ \text{For\ any}\ \mu\geq 0,\ \text{it\ holds\ that}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad(-A)^{\mu}E(t)v=E(t)(-A)^{\mu}v,\ \ \text{for}\ v\in{H}^{2\mu},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\|(-A)^{\mu}E(t)\|\leq Ct^{-\mu},\ \ t>0;

\displaystyle(ii)

(Nonlinearity) Let

2.4.
Spatial regularity of

We proceed to exploit the regularity of the solution (
1.2
) under these assumptions.
We note that an optimal spatial regularity has been established for additive noise under the conditions
\|(-A)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\|<\infty
(cf.
[

). To simplify the notation, we shall omit the dependence on

Theorem 2.1
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
-Assumption
2.4
, the unique mild solution
u(t)
of (
1.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup\limits_{t}\|u(t)\|_{\gamma}^{p},\;\mathbf{E}\sup\limits_{t}\|f(u(t))\|_{\gamma}^{p}<\infty\quad\forall p\geq 2.

Proof.

We start with (
1.2
). For any
t>0

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H^{\gamma})}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|(-A)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}E(t)u_{0}\big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}+\bigg\|(-A)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}E(t-\sigma)f(u(\sigma))d\sigma\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\quad+\bigg\|(-A)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}E(t-\sigma)g(u)dW^{Q}(\sigma)\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}.

The assumption on
u_{0}:\Omega\to{H}^{\gamma}
implies the bound for the first term

(2.7)

\displaystyle\big\|(-A)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}E(t)u_{0}\big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{H}^{\gamma})}<C.

For the last term in (
2.4
), we use the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, Assumption
2.3
and generalized Gronwall inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}(-A)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}E(t-\sigma)g(u(\sigma))dW^{Q}(\sigma)\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}

\displaystyle\leq C(p)\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\big(\mathbf{E}\big\|(-A)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}E(t-\sigma)g(u(\sigma))\big\|_{L_{2}^{0}}^{p}\big)^{\frac{2}{p}}d\sigma\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(p)\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\big(\mathbf{E}\big\|(-A)^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}}E(t-\sigma)(-A)^{\frac{\gamma-1+\epsilon}{2}}g(u(\sigma))\big\|_{L_{2}^{0}}^{p}\big)^{\frac{2}{p}}d\sigma\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}(t-\sigma)^{\epsilon-1}\big(\mathbf{E}\|g(u(\sigma))\|_{L^{0}_{2,\gamma-1+\epsilon}}^{p}\big)^{\frac{2}{p}}d\sigma\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}(t-\sigma)^{\epsilon-1}\big(\mathbf{E}\|(u(\sigma))\|_{L^{0}_{2,\gamma-1+\epsilon}}^{p}\big)^{\frac{2}{p}}d\sigma\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}(t-\sigma)^{\epsilon-1}\|u(\sigma)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H^{\gamma})}^{2}d\sigma\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

It remains to bound the second term in (
2.4
). Towards this end, we consider
\gamma
in differently intervals separately as follows.
(i) Case
\gamma\in(1,2)
:

(2.9)

\displaystyle\bigg\|(-A)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}E(t-\sigma)f(u(\sigma))d\sigma\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}\leq\int_{0}^{t}(t-\sigma)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|f(u(\sigma))\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}d\sigma<C.

Therefore,
u(t)\in L^{p}(\Omega;H^{\gamma})
by the Gronwall’s inequality using (
2.7
),(
2.9
) and (
2.4
).

Since
\gamma>1
, we have
H^{\gamma}(\mathcal{O})
is a Banach algebra for
d=1,2
(cf.
[
1
, Page 106]
). Hence,

\mathbf{E}\sup\limits_{t}\|f(u(t))\|_{\gamma}^{p}<\infty
.

(ii) Case
\gamma=2
:

From the previous case, one has
u(t)\in L^{p}(\Omega;H^{\mu})
for some
\mu\in(1,2)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}E(t-\sigma)Af(u(\sigma))d\sigma\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}=\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}E(t-\sigma)(-A)^{1-\frac{\mu}{2}}(-A)^{\frac{\mu}{2}}f(u(\sigma))d\sigma\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}(t-\sigma)^{\frac{\mu}{2}-1}\|f(u(\sigma))\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H^{\mu})}d\sigma<C.

Therefore,
u(t)\in L^{p}(\Omega;H^{2})
by the generalized Gronwall’s inequality using (
2.7
),(
2.4
) and (
2.4
), and by the same reason as in the previous case,

\mathbf{E}\sup\limits_{t}\|f(u(t))\|_{2}^{p}<\infty
.

(iii) Case
\gamma\in(2,4)
:

By virtue of the results of the previous case,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}(-A)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}E(t-\sigma)f(u(\sigma))d\sigma\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}\leq\int_{0}^{t}(t-\sigma)^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|f(u(\sigma)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H^{2})}d\sigma<C.

We repeat the above process for arbitrarily large

) and (

and

The next lemma establishes a local Lipschitz continuity for the nonlinear

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

. Then, under the assumption 2.2, we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle\|f(u)-f(v)\|\leq C(1+\|u\|_{\gamma}^{P-1}+\|v\|_{\gamma}^{P-1})\|u-v\|,\ \ u,v\in H^{\gamma}(\mathcal{O}),

where

is independent of

Proof.

Under the assumption on

. Hence,

\displaystyle\|f(u)-f(v)\|

\displaystyle=\bigg\|\sum\limits_{j=0}^{P}a_{j}(u^{j}-v^{j})\bigg\|

\displaystyle=\bigg\|(u-v)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{P}a_{j}(u^{j-1}+u^{j-2}v+\cdots+v^{j-1})\bigg\|

\displaystyle\leq\|u-v\|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{P}|a_{j}|\bigg(\|u\|^{j-1}_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|^{j-2}_{L^{\infty}}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}+\cdots+\|v\|^{j-1}_{L^{\infty}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{P-1}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{P-1})\|u-v\|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|u\|_{\gamma}^{P-1}+\|v\|_{\gamma}^{P-1})\|u-v\|.

∎

3.1.
Spatial semi-discretization

Let
\mathcal{P}_{N}

We define

(3.1)

\displaystyle(P_{N}v,y_{N})=(\nabla A^{-1}v,\nabla y_{N}),\ \forall v\in H^{-1},\ y_{N}\in V_{N}.

It is clear that for
v\in L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
, we have

\displaystyle(P_{N}v,y_{N})=(v,y_{N}),\ \forall y_{N}\in V_{N},

from which we derive
[
6

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|P_{N}v-v\|\leq\inf_{v_{N}\in V_{N}}\|v_{N}-v\|\leq CN^{-r}\|u\|_{r},\quad\forall r>0.

We introduce a discrete operator

Then the spectral Galerkin approximation of (

(3.3)

\displaystyle du_{N}=A_{N}u_{N}dt+P_{N}f(u_{N})dt+P_{N}g(u_{N})dW^{Q}(t),\;\;u_{N}(0)=P_{N}u_{0}.

Similar as the continuous case, there exists a unique mild solution

(3.4)

\displaystyle u_{N}(t)=E_{N}(t)P_{N}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}E_{N}(t-s)P_{N}f(u_{N}(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}E_{N}(t-s)P_{N}\big[g(u_{N})dW^{Q}(s)\big],

Similar to

\displaystyle\|(-A_{N})^{\mu}E_{N}v_{N}\|\leq Ct^{-\mu}\|v_{N}\|\ \ \text{for\ all}\ t>0,\ v_{N}\in\mathcal{P}_{N},

and defines the operator

\displaystyle F_{N}(t):=E_{N}(t)P_{N}-E(t).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

\displaystyle\|F_{N}(t)u\|\leq CN^{-\mu}t^{-\frac{\mu-\nu}{2}}\|u\|_{\nu},\ \forall u\in{H}^{\nu}.

Proof.

Thanks to (

[

), so we omit the detail here.
∎

Lemma 3.5

Let Assumptions

-
2.4

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup\limits_{t}\|u_{N}(t)\|^{p}<C,

Proof.

This can be done by following the arguments in

By (

\displaystyle\|u_{N}(t)\|^{p}

\displaystyle=\|P_{N}u_{0}\|^{p}+p(p-2)\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{N}(s)\|^{q-4}\|(P_{N}g(u_{N}(s)P_{N}))^{\ast}u_{N}(s)\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\quad+p\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{N}(s)\|^{p-2}\langle A_{N}u_{N}(s)+P_{N}f(u_{N}(s)),u_{N}(s)\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|P_{N}g(u_{N}(s))P_{N}\|_{L_{2}(H)}^{2}ds

\displaystyle\quad+p\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{N}(s)\|^{p-2}\langle u_{N}(s),P_{N}g(u_{N}(s))dW^{Q}(s)\rangle

For any given

It is obvious that

Then by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality and the Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup\limits_{r\in[0,t]}\bigg|\int_{0}^{r}\|u_{N}(s)\|^{p-2}\langle u_{N}(s),P_{N}g(u_{N}(s))dW^{Q}(s)\bigg|

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,\tau_{R}^{N}]}\|u_{N}(t)\|^{p}\leq C\mathbf{E}\|u_{0}\|^{p},\;\;n\geq 1.

Theorem 3.2

3.2.
Efficient implementation with spectral-Galerkin method

We present below an efficient implementation by using the spectral-Galerkin method
[
35
]
which will greatly simply the implementation and increase the efficiency.
To fix the idea, we take
\mathcal{O}=(0,1)^{2}
and
A=\Delta
as an example.

Our spectral semi-discretization (
3.3
) is to equivalent to finding
u_{N}\in V_{N}
such that

(3.14)

\displaystyle(du_{N},\chi_{N})=(A_{N}u_{N},\chi_{N})dt+(f(u_{N}),\chi_{N})dt+(g(u_{N})dW^{Q}(t),\chi_{N}),\ \ \chi_{N}\in V_{N},

where
W^{Q}(t)\approx\sum\limits_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{J}\sqrt{q_{j_{1}j_{2}}}e_{j_{1}j_{2}}(x,y)\beta_{j_{1}j_{2}}(t)
.

Let
\{\phi_{m}(\cdot)\}_{m=1}^{N}
be the basis functions of
V_{N}
in 1-D so that

\{\phi_{m}(x)\phi_{j}(y)\}_{m,j=1}^{N}
forms a basic for
V_{N}
in 2-D.

\displaystyle u_{N}(t)=\sum\limits_{m,n=0}^{N-2}c_{mn}(t)\phi_{m}(x)\phi_{n}(y),\;\mathbf{C}(t)=(c_{mn}(t))_{m,n=0,1,\cdots,N-2};

\displaystyle a_{mn}=\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{\prime}_{m}(x)\phi^{\prime}_{n}(x)dx,\;\mathbf{A}=(a_{mn})_{m,n=0,1,\cdots,N-2};

\displaystyle b_{mn}=\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{m}(x)\phi_{n}(x)dx,\;\mathbf{B}=(b_{mn})_{m,n=0,1,\cdots,N-2};

\displaystyle f_{mn}=\int_{\mathcal{O}}f(u_{N}(t))\phi_{m}(x)\phi_{n}(y)dxdy,\;\mathbf{F}(t)=(f_{mn})_{m,n=0,1,\cdots,N-2};

\displaystyle g^{j_{1}j_{2}}_{mn}=\int_{\mathcal{O}}g(u_{N}(t))e_{j_{1}j_{2}}(x,y)\phi_{m}(x)\phi_{n}(y)dxdy,\;\mathbf{G}_{j_{1}j_{2}}(t)=(g^{j_{1}j_{2}}_{m,n})_{m,n=0,1,\cdots,N-2}.

Then, (
3.14
) can be transformed into

(3.15)

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}(d{\mathbf{C}}(t))\mathbf{B}=-[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}(t)\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{C}(t)\mathbf{A}]dt+\mathbf{F}(t)dt+\sum\limits_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{J}\sqrt{q_{j_{1}j_{2}}}\mathbf{G}_{j_{1}j_{2}}(t)d\beta_{j_{1}j_{2}}(t).

We now perform a matrix diagonalization technique (cf.
[
36
, Chap 8]
) to the above system. Let
(\lambda_{i},\bar{h}_{i})

(i=0,1,\cdots,N-2)
be the generalized eigenpairs such that
\mathbf{B}\bar{h}_{i}=\lambda_{i}\mathbf{A}\bar{h}_{i}
, and set

(3.16)

\mathbf{\Lambda}=\text{diag}(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{N-2}),\;\mathbf{H}=(\bar{h}_{0},\bar{h}_{1},\cdots,\bar{h}_{N-2}).

Then, we have
\mathbf{B}\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H\Lambda}
. Note that since
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
are symmetric, we have
\mathbf{H}^{-1}=\mathbf{H}^{T}
.

Writing
\mathbf{C}(t)=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{V}(t)\mathbf{H}^{T}
in (
3.15
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}\mathbf{\Lambda}d\mathbf{V}(t)\mathbf{\Lambda}\mathbf{H}^{T}=-[\mathbf{HV}(t)\mathbf{\Lambda H}^{T}+\mathbf{H\Lambda V}(t)\mathbf{H}^{T}]dt+\mathbf{A}^{-1}(\mathbf{F}(t)dt+\sum\limits_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{J}\sqrt{q_{j_{1}j_{2}}}\mathbf{G}_{j_{1}j_{2}}(t)d\beta_{j_{1}j_{2}}(t))\mathbf{A}^{-1}.

Multiplying the left (resp. right) of the above equation by
\mathbf{H}^{T}
(resp.
\mathbf{H}
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Lambda}d\mathbf{V}(t)\mathbf{\Lambda}=-[\mathbf{V}(t)\mathbf{\Lambda}+\mathbf{\Lambda V}(t)]dt+\mathbf{H}^{T}\mathbf{A}^{-1}(\mathbf{F}(t)dt+\sum\limits_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{J}\sqrt{q_{j_{1}j_{2}}}\mathbf{G}_{j_{1}j_{2}}(t)d\beta_{j_{1}j_{2}}(t))\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{H},

which can be rewritten componentwise as a system of nonlinear SDEs with decoupled linear parts:

\displaystyle\lambda_{m}\lambda_{n}d{V}_{mn}(t)

\displaystyle=-[\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{n}]{V}_{mn}(t)dt+(\mathbf{H}^{T}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{F}(t)\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{H})_{mn}dt

(3.17)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum\limits_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{J}\sqrt{q_{j_{1}j_{2}}}(\mathbf{H}^{T}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{G}_{j_{1}j_{2}}(t)\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{H})_{mn}d\beta_{j_{1}j_{2}}(t),\;\;0\leq m,n\leq N-2.

Several remarks are in order:

•

In principle, one can solve the above system of nonlinear SDEs using any standard SDE solver. We shall construct a special tamed semi-implicit scheme in the next section which is unconditionally stable as well as extremely easy to implement.

•

The above procedure is also applicable to a separable operator
A
in the form
Au=\partial_{x}(a(x)\partial_{x}u)+\partial_{y}(b(y)\partial_{y}u)
, and can be extended in a straightforward fashion to three dimensions.

•

In the special case of
A=\Delta
considered above, we can use

(3.18)

\displaystyle\phi_{m}(x)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{4m+6}}(L_{m}(x)-L_{m+2}(x)),\ \ m\geq 0,

where
L_{m}(x)
is the shifted Legendre polynomials on
[0,1]
such that

\phi_{m}(0)=\phi_{m}(1)=0
and
(\phi^{\prime}_{m},\phi^{\prime}_{n})=\delta_{mn}

[
35
]
.
Hence,
\mathbf{A}
is the identity matrix, and the entries of
\mathbf{B}
has the explicit form
[
35
]

(3.19)

\displaystyle b_{mn}=b_{nm}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{1}{4(4m+6)}\big(\frac{1}{2m+1}+\frac{1}{2m+5}\big),\ \ m=n}\\
\displaystyle{-\frac{1}{4\sqrt{(4m+6)(4m-2)}}\frac{1}{2m+1},\ \;m=n+2}\\
\displaystyle{0},\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\;\;\;\;\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Therefore, the eigenpairs of
\mathbf{B}
can be efficiently and accurately computed.

4.
Full discretization and its error analysis

In this section, we present our full discretized scheme, establish its stability and carry out its convergence analysis.

4.1.
A tamed semi-implicit scheme

Let
\tau
be the time step size and
M=T/\tau
. We start with a first-order semi-discrete tamed time discretization scheme for (
1.1
):

(4.1)

\displaystyle u^{k+1}-u^{k}=\tau\Delta u^{k+1}+\frac{\tau f(u^{k})}{1+\tau\|f(u^{k})\|^{2}}+g(u^{k})\Delta W^{Q}(t_{k}),\;0\leq k\leq M-1.

Combining with (
3.14
), we have its fully discretized version:

\displaystyle(u_{N}^{k+1}-u_{N}^{k},\psi)=\tau(\Delta u_{N}^{k+1},\psi)+\frac{\tau(f(u_{N}^{k}),\psi)}{1+\tau\|f(u_{N}^{k})\|^{2}}+(g(u_{N}^{k})\Delta W^{Q}(t_{k}),\psi),\ \ \psi\in V_{N},

or

(4.2)

\displaystyle u_{N}^{k+1}-u_{N}^{k}=\tau A_{N}u_{N}^{k+1}+\frac{\tau P_{N}f(u_{N}^{k})}{1+\tau\|f(u_{N}^{k})\|^{2}}+P_{N}[g(u_{N}^{k})\Delta W^{Q}(t_{k})],\;0\leq k\leq M-1.

A remarkable property of the semi-discrete tamed schemes is that, despite treating the nonlinear term and noise term explicitly, there are still unconditionally stable as we show below.

Theorem 4.3
.

The schemes (
4.1
) and (
4.1
) admit a unique solution
u^{k+1}
and
u_{N}^{k+1}
, and are unconditionally stable in the sense that for
1\leq k\leq M-1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|u^{k+1}\|^{2}+2\tau\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}\mathbf{E}\|\nabla u^{j+1}\|^{2}\leq\exp((2K+2c^{2}Tr(Q))T)\mathbf{E}\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\frac{\exp((2K+2c^{2}Tr(Q))T)}{K+c^{2}Tr(Q)},

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{k+1}\|^{2}+2\tau\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}\mathbf{E}\|\nabla u_{N}^{j+1}\|^{2}\leq\exp((2K+2c^{2}Tr(Q))T)\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{0}\|^{2}+\frac{\exp((2K+2c^{2}Tr(Q))T)}{K+c^{2}Tr(Q)},

where
c
and
K
are constants from our Assumptions
2.1
-
2.4
.

Proof.

The proof for the semi-discrete and full-discrete cases are essentially the same so we shall only prove the result for the full-discrete case.

It is clear that the scheme (
4.1
) admits a unique solution.

Choosing
\psi=u_{N}^{k+1}
in (
4.1
) and using the identity

(a-b,2a)=|a|^{2}-|b|^{2}+|a-b|^{2},

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\|u_{N}^{k+1}\|^{2}-\|u_{N}^{k}\|^{2}+\|(u_{N}^{k+1}-u_{N}^{k})\|^{2}]+\tau\|\nabla u_{N}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau}{1+\tau\|f(u_{N}^{k})\|^{2}}(f(u_{N}^{k}),u_{N}^{k+1})+(g(u_{N}^{k})\Delta W^{Q}(t_{k}),u_{N}^{k+1}).

Then, the Young’s inequality, Assumption
2.2
and
2.3
, and
\mathbf{E}[(g(u_{N}^{k})\Delta W^{Q}(t_{k}),u_{N}^{k})\big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}]=0
imply

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{E}[\|u_{N}^{k+1}\|^{2}-\|u_{N}^{k}\|^{2}+\|(u_{N}^{k+1}-u_{N}^{k})\|^{2}]+\tau\mathbf{E}\|\nabla u_{N}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathbf{E}\bigg[\frac{\tau}{1+\tau\|f(u_{N}^{k})\|^{2}}(f(u_{N}^{k}),u_{N}^{k+1}-u_{N}^{k})\bigg)\bigg]+\tau\mathbf{E}(f(u_{N}^{k}),u_{N}^{k})+\mathbf{E}(g(u_{N}^{k})\Delta W^{Q}(t_{k}),u_{N}^{k+1}-u_{N}^{k})

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathbf{E}\bigg[\frac{\tau}{1+\tau\|f(u_{N}^{k})\|^{2}}\bigg(\tau\|f(u_{N}^{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\tau}\|u_{N}^{k+1}-u_{N}^{k}\|^{2}\bigg)\bigg]+K\tau\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\mathbf{E}\|g(u_{N}^{k})\Delta W^{Q}(t_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{k+1}-u_{N}^{k}\|^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\tau+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{k+1}-u_{N}^{k}\|^{2}+K\tau\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{k}\|^{2}+c^{2}\tau Tr(Q)\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{k}\|^{2},

where we have used
\Delta W^{Q}(t_{k})\sim N(0,Q\tau)
in the last line.

So, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{k+1}\|^{2}-\big(1+(2K+2c^{2}Tr(Q))\tau\big)\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{k}\|^{2}+2\tau\mathbf{E}\|\nabla u_{N}^{k+1}\|^{2}\leq 2\tau.

Denote

A_{0}=\big(1+(2K+2c^{2}Tr(Q))\tau\big),\ \ \text{and}\ \ k+1=M\gamma_{0},\ 0\leq\gamma_{0}\leq 1.

We have

\displaystyle\ \mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{k+1}\|^{2}-A_{0}\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{k}\|^{2}+2\tau\mathbf{E}\|\nabla u_{N}^{k+1}\|^{2}\leq 2\tau,

\displaystyle A_{0}\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{k}\|^{2}-A_{0}^{2}\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{k-1}\|^{2}+2A_{0}\tau\mathbf{E}\|\nabla u_{N}^{k}\|^{2}\leq 2A_{0}\tau,

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle A_{0}^{k}\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{1}\|^{2}-A_{0}^{k+1}\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{0}\|^{2}+2A_{0}^{k}\tau\mathbf{E}\|\nabla u_{N}^{1}\|^{2}\leq 2A_{0}^{k}\tau.

Summing up the above inequalities yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{k+1}\|^{2}-A_{0}^{k+1}\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{0}\|^{2}+2\tau\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}A_{0}^{k-j}\mathbf{E}\|\nabla u_{N}^{j+1}\|^{2}\leq\frac{2\tau(1-A_{0}^{k+1})}{1-A_{0}}.

Moreover, a simple computation shows that

\lim\limits_{M\to\infty}A_{0}^{k+1}=\lim\limits_{M\to\infty}\big(1+(2K+2c^{2}Tr(Q))\tau\big)^{k+1}=\exp((2K+2c^{2}Tr(Q))T\gamma_{0}).

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{k+1}\|^{2}+2\tau\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}A_{0}^{k-j}\mathbf{E}\|\nabla u_{N}^{j+1}\|^{2}\leq\exp((2K+2c^{2}Tr(Q))T)\mathbf{E}\|u_{N}^{0}\|^{2}+\frac{\exp((2K+2c^{2}Tr(Q))T)}{K+c^{2}Tr(Q)}.

The desired result follows since
A_{0}>1
.
∎

4.2.
Convergence analysis

Now, we carry out a convergence analysis for (

Lemma 4.6

Under Assumption

\displaystyle\|(-A)^{\frac{\rho}{2}}(E(t_{n})-E^{n})v\|\leq C\tau^{\frac{\beta}{2}}t_{n}^{-\frac{\beta-\gamma+\rho}{2}}\|(-A)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}v\|,\ 0\leq\gamma\leq\beta+\rho,\gamma\geq 0,\beta\in[0,2];

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|(E(t)-E^{n}P_{N})v\|\leq C(N^{-\mu}+\tau^{\min\{\frac{\mu}{2},1\}})\|v\|_{\mu},\ \ v\in H^{\mu}.

Proof.

The first inequality can be found in
[
25

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}^{k-1}=(f_{mn}^{k-1}),\;\;f_{mn}^{k-1}=\int_{\mathcal{O}}f(u_{N}^{k-1})\phi_{m}(x)\phi_{n}(y)dxdy;

(4.35)

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{j_{1}j_{2}}^{k-1}=(g^{j_{1}j_{2},k-1}_{mn}),\;\;g^{j_{1}j_{2},k-1}_{mn}=\int_{\mathcal{O}}g(u_{N}^{k-1})e_{j_{1}j_{2}}(x,y)\phi_{m}(x)\phi_{n}(y)dxdy.

Note that in general

\mathbf{F}^{k-1}
and
\mathbf{G}_{j_{1}j_{2}}^{k-1}

Let
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}_{i=0,N}

\displaystyle f_{mn}^{k-1}\approx\int_{\mathcal{O}}I_{N}(f(u_{N}^{k-1}))\phi_{m}(x)\phi_{n}(y)dxdy;

(4.36)

\displaystyle g^{j_{1}j_{2},k-1}_{mn}\approx\int_{\mathcal{O}}I_{N}(g(u_{N}^{k-1})e_{j_{1}j_{2}}(x,y))\phi_{m}(x)\phi_{n}(y)dxdy.

for the
d

One can compute

is

d

(1)

(2)

At time step

6.
Concluding remarks

We developed a fully discrete scheme for nonlinear stochastic partial differential equations with non-globally Lipschitz coefficients driven by multiplicative noise in a multi-dimensional setting. The space discretization is a Legendre spectral method, so it does not require the elliptic operator
A
and the covariance operator
Q
of noise in the equation commute, while can still be efficiently implemented as with a Fourier method.
The time discretization is a tamed semi-implicit scheme which treats the nonlinear term explicitly while being unconditionally stable, and it avoids solving nonlinear systems at each time step.
Under reasonable regularity assumptions, we established optimal strong convergence rates in both space and time for our fully discrete scheme. We also presented several numerical experiments to validate our theoretical results.

We note that the fully discrete scheme constructed in this paper can also be used for the three-dimensional case, but our error analysis is only valid for one- and two-dimensional cases.

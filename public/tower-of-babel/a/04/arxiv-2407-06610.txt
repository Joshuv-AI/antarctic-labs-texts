A converse theorem for Borcherds products in signature ( 2 , 2 ) (2,2) Thanks: The authors are partially funded by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) – SFB-TRR 358/1 2023 – 491392403. P.B. is supported by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft, project number 520675682.
Patrick Bieker, Paul Kiefer

Abstract
. We show that a modular unit on two copies of the upper half-plane is a Borcherds product if and only if its boundary divisor is a special boundary divisor. Therefore, we define a subspace of the space of invariant vectors for the Weil representation which maps surjectively onto the space of modular units that are Borcherds products. Moreover, we show that every boundary divisor of a Borcherds product can be obtained in this way. As a byproduct we obtain new identities of eta products.



1.
Introduction

In his famous paper
[
Bor98
]
, Borcherds constructed a multiplicative map from the space of weakly holomorphic modular forms of weight
1-\frac{n}{2}
for the Weil representation associated to an even lattice
L
of signature
(2,n)
to meromorphic orthogonal modular forms for a certain subgroup
\Gamma(L)
of the orthogonal group of
L
. These orthogonal modular forms, also called Borcherds products, have infinite product expansions, from which one can see that their divisors are supported on so called special divisors. Borcherds posed the question, when a meromorphic orthogonal modular form is a Borcherds product, see
[
Bor98
, Problem 16.10]
.

A first step into this direction was done in
[
Bru02
]
, where it was shown that if
n\geq 3
and
L
splits two hyperbolic planes over
\mathbb{Z}
, then every meromorphic orthogonal modular form whose divisor is supported on special divisors is a Borcherds product. The result has been generalized to the case that
L
splits a hyperbolic plane and a scaled hyperbolic plane over
\mathbb{Z}
, see
[
Bru14
]
. Both results construct a Borcherds product that has the same divisor as the meromorphic orthogonal form using the weak converse theorem
[
Bru02
, Theorem 4.23]
and then use the Koecher principle to show that they must be equal up to a constant factor. For lattices of signature
(2,1)
or
(2,2)
and Witt rank 2, neither the weak converse theorem nor the Koecher principle are available. The case
n=1
was considered in
[
BS20
]
, where a converse theorem using generalized Borcherds products was proven. The aim of the present paper is to tackle the problem for lattices of signature
(2,2)
with Witt rank
2
. We will define a space of
special boundary divisors
and show the following converse theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(see ?THM?
)
.

Let
F:\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
be a modular form of some weight and some character of finite order whose divisor is concentrated on the boundary, i.e. it is a modular unit. Then
F
is a Borcherds product if and only if it has a special boundary divisor.

We will now describe our results in more detail. Let
L=U(N^{\prime})\oplus U(N)
be the sum of two scaled hyperbolic planes and let
V=L\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.
Then
L
has signature
(2,2)
and Witt rank
2
.
For an easier exposition we will assume that
N=p^{r},N^{\prime}=p^{r^{\prime}}
are prime powers in the introduction.
Let
\rho_{L}
be the Weil representation of the metaplectic group
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
.
The hermitian symmetric domain associated to the orthogonal group
O(V)
can be identified with
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
. Under this identification the discriminant kernel
\Gamma(L)=\ker(O(L)\to O(L^{\prime}/L))
is identified with a certain discrete subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
in this setting. The Borcherds lift becomes a multiplicative map from weakly holomorphic modular forms
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
of weight
0
with respect to the Weil representation
\rho_{L}
to meromorphic modular forms
F:\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
with respect to
\Gamma(L)
. An important subspace of the space of weakly holomorphic modular forms of weight
0
is the space of invariant vectors
\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]^{\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
which has already been studied in
[
ES17
,
Bie23
,
Zem23
,
MS23
]
. Under the Borcherds lift, these map to modular units, i.e. holomorphic modular forms whose divisor is concentrated on the boundary
of the Bailey-Borel compactification
X(L)
of the quotient
Y(L)=\Gamma(L)\setminus\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
.
In fact the boundary divisor of a Borcherds product is completely determined by an invariant vector, see ?THM?
. In particular, to study the boundary divisors of Borcherds lifts, it is sufficient to study the Borcherds lifts of invariant vectors.

In our setting the boundary of the Bailey-Borel compactification can be described explicitly as follows.
The boundary of
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
consists of
0
-dimensional cusps given by elements in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})\times\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
and
1
-dimensional cusps isomorphic to
\{a/c\}\times\mathbb{H},\ \mathbb{H}\times\{a/c\}
for
a/c\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
where
a,c
are integers with
\gcd(a,c)=1
and
c\geq 0
. These
1
-dimensional boundary components are in
1
-
1
-correspondence with rational
2
-dimensional isotropic subspaces
I_{a,c}^{1},I_{a,c}^{2}\subseteq V
. We show that the image of
I_{a,c}^{*}\cap L^{\prime},*=1,2
in
L^{\prime}/L
is a self-dual isotropic subgroup
H_{a,c}^{*}=\operatorname{type}_{L}(I_{a,c})
and call this the type of
I_{a,c}^{*}
following
[
DSSW23
]
. This yields a subspace
\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]_{\operatorname{Types}(L)}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]^{\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
generated by the characteristic functions of the types
H_{a,c}^{*}
of rational
2
-dimensional isotropic subspaces
I_{a,c}^{*}
. We obtain the following

Proposition 1.2

(see ?THM?
and ?THM?
)
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in p\mathbb{Z}/p^{r^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}}v^{H^{1}_{a,1}}+\sum_{c\in p^{r-r^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z}}v^{H^{1}_{1,c}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\\
0\leq s\leq r-r^{\prime}\end{subarray}}v^{H^{1}_{1,up^{s}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in p\mathbb{Z}/p^{r^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}}v^{H^{2}_{a,1}}+\sum_{c\in p^{r-r^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z}}v^{H^{2}_{1,c}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\\
0\leq s\leq r-r^{\prime}\end{subarray}}v^{H^{2}_{1,up^{s}}}

and this is up to multiplication by scalars the only non-trivial linear relation among the characteristic functions of types of one-dimensional cusps.

As a corollary one can calculate the dimension of the space
\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]_{\operatorname{Types}(L)}
, see ?THM?
. Moreover, since according to
[
Bie23
, Proposition 5.1]
the Borcherds products of invariant vectors are eta products, one obtains an identity of eta products which generalizes the identity found in
[
Bie23

, see ?THM?
.

For a self-dual isotropic subgroup
H\subseteq L^{\prime}/L
let the special boundary divisor corresponding to
H
be defined by

Z(H)=\sum_{S}\#(H\cap\operatorname{type}_{L}(S))[S],

where the sum runs over all one-dimensional cusps of the compactified modular surface
X(L)
. We denote the space generated by these special boundary divisors by
\spbdiv(X(L))
. From the explicit description of the Borcherds lift of an invariant vector we obtain

Proposition 1.3

(see ?THM?
, ?THM?
)
.

The map

\mathbb{Q}[L^{\prime}/L]^{\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\to\spbdiv(X(L))_{\mathbb{Q}}

given by sending an invariant vector to the boundary divisor of its Borcherds product restricts to an isomorphism

\mathbb{Q}[L^{\prime}/L]_{\operatorname{Types}(L)}\to\spbdiv(X(L))_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Under this map, the characteristic function of a self-dual isotropic subgroup
H
is mapped to
Z(H)
.

The proposition makes it possible to explicitly determine the boundary divisors of Borcherds products.

Theorem 1.4

(see ?THM?
)
.

Let
f\in M_{0,L}^{!}
and denote by
\Psi_{f}
the corresponding Borcherds product. Then the boundary divisor of
\Psi_{f}(Z)
on
X(L)
is special and every special boundary divisor is the boundary divisor of
\Psi_{\mathfrak{v}}(Z)
for some
\mathfrak{v}\in\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]_{\operatorname{Types}(L)}
.
The space of special boundary divisors has dimension

\dim\spbdiv(X(L))_{\mathbb{Q}}=\left(2((r-r^{\prime}+1)p^{r^{\prime}}-(r-r^{\prime}-1)p^{r^{\prime}-1})-1\right)(r+1).

In fact, we show in ?THM?
that the boundary divisor of a Borcherds product only depends on a certain invariant vector whose corresponding Borcherds product has the same boundary divisor. This yields the previously mentioned converse theorem.

We remark that there remain two obstacles for a full converse theorem. The first one is the weak converse theorem as in
[
Bru02
, Theorem 4.23]
, which is only proven for signature
(2,n),n\geq 3
. We expect that such a theorem is still true also in our case. Then the proof of
[
Bru14
, Theorem 4.2]
carries over to our situation so that the full converse theorem is equivalent to the injectivity of the Kudla–Millson lift. The second obstacle is that this injectivity is only known for
L=U\oplus U(N)
so that even with the weak converse theorem, one only obtains the full converse theorem in these cases.

2.
Preliminaries

We briefly recall some facts on lattices, the Weil representation and associated modular forms. For more details also compare
[
Bru02
,
Sch09
]
.

2.1.
Vector valued modular forms for the Weil representation

Let
L
be an even lattice, i.e. a finitely generated free
\mathbb{Z}
-module with a non-degenerate quadratic form
q:L\to\mathbb{Z}
. Then its discriminant group
L^{\prime}/L
is a so called
discriminant form
, i.e. it is a finite abeleian group with a non-degenerate quadratic form
q\colon L^{\prime}/L\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
. We denote by
L^{-}
the lattice with the same underlying
\mathbb{Z}
-module and quadratic form given by
-q
. Then the discriminant form
(L^{\prime}/L)^{-}
of
L^{-}
is given by the same underlying abelian group as
L^{\prime}/L
together with quadratic form
-q
.
Recall that the Weil representation
\rho_{L}
is a representation of the metaplectic double cover
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
, see for example
[
Bru02
, Section 1.1]
. We denote the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
by
\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}
for
\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L
.
We denote by
\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]^{\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
the space of invariants under
\rho_{L}
.

Let
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. A holomorphic function
f\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
is a
weakly holomorphic modular form of weight
k
for
\rho_{L}
if
f(M\tau)=\phi^{2k}(\tau)\rho_{L}(M,\phi)f(\tau)

for all
(M,\phi)\in\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and
f
is meromorphic at
\infty
. If
f
is holomorphic at
\infty
(respectively vanishes at
\infty
),
f
is called a
holomorphic modular form
(respectively
cusp form
) of weight
k
for
\rho_{L}
.
We denote by
M_{k,L}^{!}
(respectively
M_{k,L}
and
S_{k,L}
) the space of weakly holomorphic modular forms (respectively holomorpic modular forms and cusp forms) of weight
k
for
\rho_{L}
.

Let

\Delta_{k}=-v^{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial v^{2}}\right)+ikv\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\right),\quad\tau=u+iv\in\mathbb{H}

be the weight
k
Laplace operator on
\mathbb{H}
. Following
[
BF04
]
a
harmonic weak Maass form
of weight
k
for
\rho_{L}
is a real analytic function
f\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
that has the transformation property of modular forms, satisfies
\Delta_{k}f=0
and has at most linear exponential growth at
\infty
.
Let
\xi_{k}
be the linear differential operator on harmonic Maass forms given by
\xi_{k}(f)=2iv\overline{\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\tau}}f}
. Then, by the linear exponential growth,
\xi_{k}(f)
is a weakly holomorphic modular form of weight
2-k
for the dual Weil representation
\rho_{L}^{*}=\rho_{L^{-}}
of
\rho_{L}
.
We denote by
H_{k,L}
the space of harmonic weak Maass forms of weight
k
for
\rho_{L}
such that
\xi_{k}(f)
is a cusp form.
Note that the map
\xi_{k}\colon H_{k,L}\to S_{2-k,L^{-}}
is surjective with kernel
M_{k,L}^{!}
, see
[
BF04
, Corollary 3.8]
. Every harmonic weak Maass form
f\in H_{k,L}
has a Fourier expansion of the form

\displaystyle f(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Q}\\
n\gg-\infty\end{subarray}}c^{+}(\gamma,n)e(n\tau)+\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Q}\\
n<0\end{subarray}}c^{-}(\gamma,n)\Gamma(1-k,-4\pi nv)e(n\tau),

where
e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
. The finite sum

P(f)=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{n\leq 0}c^{+}(\gamma,n)e(n\tau)

is called the principal part. Examples of harmonic weak Maass forms are given by the Hejhal Poincaré series
F_{\beta,m}(\tau,1-k/2)
, see
[
Bru02
, Section 1.3]
. For
k=0
these have to be defined using Hecke summation and we have the following

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\in H_{0,L}
with principal part

P(f)=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{n\leq 0}c^{+}(\gamma,n)e(n\tau).

Then there exists an invariant vector
\mathfrak{v}_{f}\in\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]^{\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
such that

f(\tau)=\mathfrak{v}_{f}+\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{n<0}c^{+}(\gamma,n)F_{\beta,m}(\tau,1).

Proof.

The difference

f(\tau)-\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{n<0}c^{+}(\gamma,n)F_{\beta,m}(\tau,1)

is a harmonic weak Maass form with constant principal part, hence is a holomorphic modular form of weight
0
according to
[
BY09
, Lemma 3.3]
, i.e. an invariant vector
\mathfrak{v}_{f}\in\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]^{\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
.
∎

For an isotropic subgroup
H
of
L^{\prime}/L
the lattice
L_{H}=L+H
is also an even lattice with dual lattice
L_{H}^{\prime}=L+H^{\perp}
. The corresponding discriminant form is
L_{H}^{\prime}/L_{H}=H^{\perp}/H
. Conversely, when
\tilde{L}\subset L
is a sublattice of finite index the quotient
H=L/\tilde{L}
is an isotropic subgroup of
\tilde{L}^{\prime}/\tilde{L}
with orthogonal complement
H^{\bot}=L^{\prime}/\tilde{L}
and
H^{\bot}/H\cong L^{\prime}/L
.

Recall that for an isotropic subgroup
H\subseteq L^{\prime}/L
we have a pair of operators, compare
[
Bru02
, Section 5.1]
and
[
Sch15
, Section 4]
, the
isotropic induction

\uparrow_{H}^{L}\colon\mathbb{C}[H^{\perp}/H]\to\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L],\qquad\uparrow_{H}^{L}(\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma+H})=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in H}\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}

for
\gamma\in H^{\perp}
and
isotropic descent

\downarrow_{H}^{L}\colon\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]\to\mathbb{C}[H^{\perp}/H],\qquad\downarrow_{H}^{L}(\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma})=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma+H}&\text{if $\gamma\in H^{\perp}$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The two operators commute with the Weil representation and therefore extend to maps
\uparrow_{H}^{L}\colon H_{k,L_{H}}\to H_{k,L}
and
\downarrow^{L}_{H}\colon H_{k,L}\to H_{k,L_{H}}
. In particular, the two operators map invariant vectors to invariant vectors.
Moreover, they are adjoint for the Petterson inner product (which restricts to the standard inner product on invariants up to a constant). If
\tilde{L}\subseteq L
is a sublattice of finite index with corresponding isotropic subgroup
H
, we also write
\uparrow_{L}^{\tilde{L}}
and
\downarrow_{L}^{\tilde{L}}
.

2.2.
The lattice
L=U(N)\oplus U(N^{\prime})

For a lattice
(L,q)
and positive integer
N
we denote by
L(N)
the lattice with rescaled quadratic form
Nq
. Let
U=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
q(x,y)=xy
be the hyperbolic plane.
The main example we consider below is the orthogonal sum of two rescaled hyperbolic planes
L=L_{N,N^{\prime}}=U(N)\oplus U(N^{\prime})
for two positive integers
N,N^{\prime}
such that
N^{\prime}\mid N
.
A model for this lattice is given by

\displaystyle L=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{M}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\bigg|\frac{N}{N^{\prime}}\mid c\right\}

with quadratic form
q(X)=-N^{\prime}\det(X)
. Let
V=L\otimes\mathbb{Q}
be its associated rational quadratic space and
V_{\mathbb{R}}=L\otimes\mathbb{R}
the associated real quadratic space given by real
2\times 2
matrices with quadratic form
-N^{\prime}\det
. The hermitian symmetric domain associated to the orthogonal group
O(V)
can be realised inside
\mathbb{P}(V\otimes\mathbb{C})
as the open subset in the zero quadric
\mathcal{N}
given by
\mathcal{K}=\{[Z]\in\mathcal{N}\colon(Z,\bar{Z})>0\}
. More explicitly, the map

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle Z=(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}z_{1}&-z_{1}z_{2}\\
\frac{1}{N^{\prime}}&-z_{2}\end{bmatrix}=:Z_{L}

biholomorphically identifies
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
with one of the two connected components of
\mathcal{K}
that we denote by
\mathcal{H}
. Note that the action of
O(V_{\mathbb{R}})
on
\mathcal{K}
restricts to an action of the identity component
\operatorname{SO}^{+}(V_{\mathbb{R}})\subseteq O(V_{\mathbb{R}})
on the connected component. Moreover, in our setting the double cover
\operatorname{Spin}(V_{\mathbb{R}})\to\operatorname{SO}^{+}(V_{\mathbb{R}})
can be identified with
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that the induced action on
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
is given by componentwise Möbius transformations.

The dual lattice of
L
is given by

\displaystyle L^{\prime}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{M}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\bigg|N^{\prime}a^{\prime},Nb^{\prime},N^{\prime}c^{\prime},N^{\prime}d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

The discriminant group
L^{\prime}/L
is identified with
(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{2}\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/N^{\prime}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
using the map

\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\left(Nb^{\prime},\frac{N}{N^{\prime}}c^{\prime},N^{\prime}a^{\prime},-N^{\prime}d^{\prime}\right),

where the quadratic form on
(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{2}\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/N^{\prime}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
is given by
q(w,x,y,z)=\frac{wx}{N}+\frac{yz}{N^{\prime}}
.

Under the double cover
\psi:\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\operatorname{SO}^{+}(V_{\mathbb{R}})
the discriminant kernel
\Gamma(L)
is identified as

\displaystyle\Gamma(L)\simeq

\displaystyle\bigg\{\left(\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&N^{\prime}b_{1}\\
Nc_{1}&d_{1}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}a_{2}&N^{\prime}b_{2}\\
Nc_{2}&d_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right)\bigg|\ a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for }i=1,2,

\displaystyle\quad a_{1}a_{2}\equiv d_{1}d_{2}\equiv 1\!\pmod{N},\ a_{1}d_{2}\equiv a_{2}d_{1}\equiv 1\!\pmod{N^{\prime}}\bigg\}/\{\pm 1\},

compare
[
Bie23
, Proposition 2.4]
(note that we chose a slightly different model of
L_{N,N^{\prime}}
here).

Let
\Gamma\subseteq O(V)
be a subgroup that is commensurable with
\Gamma(L)
. The Baily-Borel compactification of
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
is given as follows.
For an isotropic vector
v\in V
the point
[v]\in\mathbb{P}(V_{\mathbb{C}})
is in the boundary of
\mathcal{H}
and we call
\{[v]\}
a
zero-dimensional (rational) boundary component
,
special boundary point
or
cusp
. For a two-dimensional isotropic subspace
F
of
V
, we call the set of all non-special boundary points which can be represented by elements of
F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
the
one-dimensional (rational) boundary component
or the
one-dimensional (rational) cusp associated to

.
The cusps of
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
are in bijection with
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})\times\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
via
\begin{bmatrix}t&-st\\
1&-s\end{bmatrix}\mapsto(t,s)
, e.g. the isotropic vector
e_{1}
represents the cusp
(\infty,\infty)
.
The one-dimensional rational boundary components are via (
2.1
) of the form
\{a/c\}\times\mathbb{H}
or
\mathbb{H}\times\{a/c\}
for
a/c\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
, where
\{a/c\}\times\mathbb{H}
corresponds to the isotropic plane
I^{1}_{a,c}=\langle\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
c&0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\

while

corresponds to the isotropic plane
I^{2}_{a,c}=\langle\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\

0&0\end{pmatrix}\rangle\subset V
for
a/c\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
with
a,c\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\gcd(a,c)=1
and
c\geq 0
.
We denote the union of
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
with all rational boundary components by
(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H})^{*}
and write
X_{\Gamma}=\Gamma\backslash(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H})^{*}
.
Then
X_{\Gamma}
admits a structure of a normal projective variety and
Y_{\Gamma}=\Gamma\backslash(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H})\subset X_{\Gamma}
is an open subvariety.
Note that the boundary

has codimension one. For
\Gamma=\Gamma(L)
we will use the notation
X(L)=X_{\Gamma(L)}
and
Y(L)=Y_{\Gamma(L)}
.

A
(meromorphic) modular form
for
\Gamma
of weight
r\in\mathbb{Q}
and multiplier system
\chi
is a (meromorphic) function
f:\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
with the transformation property

f(M_{1}z_{1},M_{2},z_{2})=(c_{1}z_{1}+d_{1})^{r}(c_{2}z_{2}+d_{2})^{r}\chi(M_{1},M_{2})f(z_{1},z_{2})

for
M_{1},M_{2}\in\Gamma
, where we identify
\Gamma
with its preimage in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
M_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{i}&b_{i}\\

, and such that

is meromorphic along the boundary.
Note that since the Witt rank of
V
is
2
(or equivalently since the boundary has codimension one), the Koecher principle is not available. We call a meromorphic modular form
f:\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
a

, if its zeros and poles are concentrated on the boundary.

Let
L

, Witt rank 2 and level

contains a finite index sublattice of the form
L_{N,N^{\prime}}
for some divisor

of

.

Assume moreover that

Then

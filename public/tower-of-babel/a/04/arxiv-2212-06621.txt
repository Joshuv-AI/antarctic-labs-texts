Asymptotic regularity of invariant chains of edge ideals
Do Trong Hoang, Hop D. Nguyen, Quang Hoa Tran

Abstract
. We study chains of nonzero edge ideals that are invariant under the action of the monoid Inc \Inc of increasing functions on the positive integers. We prove that the sequence of Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity of ideals in such a chain is eventually constant with limit either 2 or 3, and we determine explicitly when the constancy behaviour sets in. This provides further evidence to a conjecture on the asymptotic linearity of the regularity of Inc \Inc -invariant chains of homogeneous ideals. The proofs reveal unexpected combinatorial properties of Inc \Inc -invariant chains of edge ideals.

Introduction

Let
\Bbbk
be a field,
R_{n}=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
a polynomial ring in
n
variables, and
R=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}R_{n}=\Bbbk[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]
be the polynomial ring in infinitely many variables. Denote by
{\mathbb{N}}
the set of positive integers, and
\Inc
the monoid of strictly increasing functions
\pi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
, with the operation being composition of maps. Then
\Inc
acts on
R
by endomorphisms via the rule

\pi(x_{i})=x_{\pi(i)},\quad\text{for each $i\in{\mathbb{N}}$}.

A family of ideals
{\mathcal{I}}=(I_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, where
I_{n}\subseteq R_{n}
, is called an
\Inc
-invariant chain
if for all
1\leq m<n
and for all
\pi\in\Inc
such that
\pi(m)\leq n
, we have
\pi(I_{m})\subseteq I_{n}
. A related notion is that of
\Inc
-invariant ideals: An ideal
I\subseteq R
is called
\Inc
-invariant if
\pi(I)\subseteq I
for any
\pi\in\Inc
. Recently, there was considerable interest in
\Inc
-invariant chains and
\Inc
-invariant ideals, due to several reasons. First,
\Inc
-invariant chains and ideals appear in various places. For instance, let
\Sym(n)
be the symmetric group on
n
objects and
\Sym=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\Sym(n)
the direct limit of these groups. Then any
\Sym
-invariant ideal
I\subseteq R
is also
\Inc
-invariant
[
18
]
. Let
{\mathcal{I}}=(I_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be any
\Sym
-invariant chain, i.e. each
I_{n}
is
\Sym(n)
-invariant and
\pi(I_{m})\subseteq I_{n}
whenever
1\leq m<n,\pi\in\Sym(n)
. Then any such
\Sym
-invariant chain is also
\Inc
-invariant. Special instances of
\Sym
-invariant ideals are abound in representation theory
[
3
,
22
]
and algebraic statistics
[
1
,
10
]
. Second,
\Inc
-invariant chains and ideals enjoy remarkable finiteness properties due to their highly symmetrical nature. By work of Cohen
[
4
]
, Aschenbrenner–Hillar
[
1
]
, Hillar–Sullivant
[
10
]
, Nagel–Römer
[
18
]
, it is known that
R
is
\Inc
-noetherian, in particular any
\Inc
-invariant ideal
I\subseteq R
is generated by finitely many
\Inc
-orbits. Moreover, any
\Inc
-invariant chain of ideals stabilizes, namely for integers
1\leq m\leq n
, let

\Inc_{m,n}=\{\pi\in\Inc\mid\pi(m)\leq n\},

then for any
\Inc
-invariant chain
{\mathcal{I}}=(I_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, there exists some finite
r\geq 1
such that
I_{n}=\Inc_{r,n}(I_{r})R_{n}
for all
n\geq r
. We call the smallest such
r
the
stability index
of
{\mathcal{I}}
, denoted
\ind({\mathcal{I}})
.

The present work can be seen as the continuation of an active program aiming at studying asymptotic properties of
\Inc
- and
\Sym
-invariant chains of ideals; see, for example
[
6
,
9
,
11
,
13
,
17
,
18
]
for various aspects of this program. In
[
13
, Conjecture 4.12]
, Le, Nagel, the second author, and Römer proposed

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}=(I_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an
\Inc
-invariant chain of homogeneous ideals. Then for all
n\gg 0
,
\reg I_{n}=Cn+D
for certain constants

and
D
depending only on
{\mathcal{I}}
. Here
\reg(-)
denotes the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity.

We say that
{\mathcal{I}}=(I_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a
saturated
chain if
I_{n}=I_{n+1}\cap R_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. In
[
13
]
, the conjecture was partially solved in some cases, notably when (the quotient ring defined by)
I_{n}
is artinian for all
n
, or when
{\mathcal{I}}
is a saturated chain, and
I_{n}
is a squarefree monomial ideal for all
n
. The main result of
[
14

implies that the limit
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}(\reg I_{n}/n)
always exists for an arbitrary invariant chain of monomial ideals, supporting the validity of
1.1

[
16
]
and Raicu
[
19
]
(using different approaches) if
{\mathcal{I}}=(I_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is an
\Sym
-invariant chain of monomial ideals. On the other hand,
1.1
remains open when the chain
{\mathcal{I}}
is not saturated, even if each
I_{n}
is a squarefree monomial ideal.

As usual, an ideal
I_{n}\subseteq R_{n}
is called an
edge ideal
if it is generated by squarefree quadratic monomials. Related to the theory of invariant chains of edge ideals, algebraic and combinatorial aspects of FI-graphs were studied in
[
20
,
21
]
. The goal of this paper is to prove the following results.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
7.1
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}=(I_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an
\Inc
-invariant
(
not necessarily saturated
)
chain of eventually nonzero edge ideals. Let

. Then for all

, there is an equality
\reg I_{n}=\reg I_{n+1}
, and moreover
\reg I_{n}\in\{2,3\}
.

It follows from
[
13
, Corollary 4.8]
that if

is an
\Inc
-invariant chain of eventually nonzero squarefree monomial ideals, then the sequence

is bounded by a constant. On the other hand, there was no information about this constant.

provides the surprising information that for
\Inc
-invariant chain of nonzero edge ideals, not only does the limit

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem

)
.

Keep using notations of

. Let

be the graph on the vertex set

The proofs of Theorems

,

There was a lot of work on the algebraic and combinatorial properties of edge ideals; see, for example,
[
15
]
and the references therein. The proofs of our main results also use some known results on this topic, e.g. the celebrated Fröberg theorem characterizing edge ideals with a 2-linear resolution

. Nevertheless, our work is more than just routine applications of the existing literature. For example, besides Lemmas

-invariant chains of monomial ideals in general. Based on experiments with Macaulay2

Let

2.1.
Equivariant chains of ideals

Let
R_{n}=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard graded noetherian polynomial ring where
n\geq 1
, and let
R_{0}=\Bbbk
. Denote by
G(J)
the set of minimal monomial generators of a monomial ideal
J
of
R_{n}
.

Definition 2.1

(Maximal index in the support)
.

For a monomial
m\in R_{n}
, denote by
\Msupp(m)
the maximal index
1\leq i\leq n
such that
x_{i}
divides
m
. If
m=1
, we set
\Msupp(1)=0
. For a monomial ideal
J\subseteq R_{n}
, define
\Msupp(J)=\max\{\Msupp(m):m\in G(J)\}
.

The following slight generalization of saturated chains was introduced in
[
14
]
.

Definition 2.2

(Quasi-saturated chains)
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}=(I_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an
\Inc
-invariant chain of ideals with
r=\ind({\mathcal{I}})
. Set
p_{n}=\Msupp(I_{n})
. We say that
{\mathcal{I}}
is
quasi-saturated
if

I_{n+1}\cap R_{p_{n}}=I_{n}\cap R_{p_{n}}
for all
n\geq r
.

Any saturated chain is also a quasi-saturated chain. We will invoke the following simple lemma several times. Below, for each
i\geq 0
, let
\sigma_{i}:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
be the function given by

\sigma_{i}(j)=\begin{cases}j,&\text{if $1\leq j\leq i$},\\
j+1,&\text{if $j\geq i+1$}.\end{cases}

Both assertions of the next lemma are immediate from the fact that for
n\geq r=\ind({\mathcal{I}})
, it holds that
\langle\Inc_{n,n+1}(I_{n})\rangle_{R_{n+1}}=I_{n+1}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}=(I_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an
\Inc
-invariant chain of nonzero proper monomial ideals with
r=\ind({\mathcal{I}})
. Denote
p=\Msupp I_{r}
, so
1\leq p\leq r
. Then we have
\Msupp(I_{n+1})\leq\Msupp(I_{n})+1
, and in particular
\Msupp I_{n}\leq n-r+p
for all
n\geq r
.

The following is a consequence of
[
14
, Lemma 4.7 and its proof]
, by setting
e=0
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}=(I_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an
\Inc
-invariant chain of eventually nonzero proper monomial ideals with
r=\ind({\mathcal{I}})
. Let
p=\Msupp(I_{r})
. Consider collection
{\mathcal{J}}=(J_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of ideals given by

J_{n}=\begin{cases}\langle I_{n}\cap R_{n-r+p-1}\rangle_{R_{n}},&\text{if $n\geq r+1$},\\
0,&\text{if $n\leq r$}.\end{cases}

The following statements hold.

(i)

{\mathcal{J}}
is an
\Inc
-invariant chain of eventually nonzero monomial ideals and
I_{n}\subseteq J_{n+1}
for all
n\geq r
.

(ii)

There is an equality
\ind{\mathcal{J}}=r+1
.

(iii)

The chain
{\mathcal{I}}
is quasi-saturated if and only if
J_{r+1}=\langle I_{r}\rangle_{R_{r+1}}
.

(iv)

There is an equality
\reg J_{n}=\reg(I_{n},x_{n-r+p})
for all
n\geq r+1
.

The following invariant is useful for inductive arguments.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
J\subseteq R_{n}
be a nonzero proper monomial ideal such that

\Msupp(J)=p\geq 1
. Let
\delta(J)
denote the maximal degree of an element of
G(J)
. Then the
q
-invariant
of
J
is defined to be the number

{q}(J)=\sum_{j=0}^{\delta(J)}\dim_{\Bbbk}\Big(\frac{R_{p}}{J\cap R_{p}}\Big)_{j}.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}=(I_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an
\Inc
-invariant chain with
r=\ind({\mathcal{I}})
. We call
q({\mathcal{I}}):=q(I_{r})
the
q
-invariant
of the chain
{\mathcal{I}}
.

If
J=R_{n}
, we use the convention that
q(J)=0
. Hence
q(J)=0
if and only if
J=R_{n}
.

Example 2.6
.

Let
J=(x_{1}x_{3},x_{2}x_{4})\subseteq R_{5}=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{5}]
. Then
\delta(J)=2
,
\Msupp(J)=4
, so

q(J)=\sum_{j=0}^{2}\dim_{\Bbbk}\Big(\frac{R_{4}}{J\cap R_{4}}\Big)_{j}=1+4+8=13.

The next lemma is immediate.

Lemma 2.7
.

The following statements hold.

(1)

For any monomial ideal
I\subseteq R_{n}
, there is an equality
q(I)=q(\langle I\rangle_{R_{n+1}})
.

(2)

Let
I\subseteq J
be nonzero monomial ideals of
R_{n}
such that
\delta(I)\geq\delta(J)
and
\Msupp(I)\geq\Msupp(J)
. Then there is an inequality

q(I)\geq q(J).

The equality holds if and only if

.

2.2.
Casteluovo–Mumford regularity

Let
S=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring where
n\geq 1
. Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module. Then (
Castelnuovo–Mumford
)
regularity
of

is

\reg M=\sup\{j-i:\Tor^{S}_{i}(\Bbbk,M)_{j}\neq 0\}.

The following useful lemma on regularity of monomial ideals is from
[
5
, Lemma 2.10]
and
[
2
, Corollary 3.3 and Theorem 4.7]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
J
be a monomial ideal of
S
and
x
any variable of
S
. Then there is a chain

\max\{\reg(J:x),\reg(J,x)\}\leq\reg J\in\{\reg(J:x)+1,\reg(J,x)\}.

Here
(J,x)
denotes the ideal
J+(x)
.

2.3.
Edge ideals and graphs

Let
G
be a simple graph on the vertex set
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and let
E(G)
be its edge set. The
edge ideal
of
G
is the following ideal of
R_{n}
:

I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}\mid\{i,j\}\in E(G)).

An
induced subgraph

H
of
G
is a graph with vertex set
V(H)\subseteq V(G)
and edge set
E(H)\subseteq E(G)
, such that two vertices
i,j\in V(H)
are adjacent in
H
if and only if they are adjacent in
G
.

The complementary graph of
G
is denoted by
G^{c}
. The
cycle

C_{m}
is the graph on
m\geq 3
vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}
with the edge set

E(C_{m})=\left\{\{v_{1},v_{2}\},\{v_{2},v_{3}\},\ldots,\{v_{m},v_{1}\}\right\}.

We call
m
the
length
of the cycle
C_{m}
. An
anticycle
is the complementary graph
C_{m}^{c}
of the cycle
C_{m}
for some
m\geq 4
. By abuse of terminology, we call
m
the
length
of the anticycle
C_{m}^{c}
, and say that an anticycle has
consecutive vertices

v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{m}
if its complementary graph is the cycle with edges
v_{1}v_{2},\ldots,v_{m-1}v_{m},v_{m}v_{1}
.

We say that
G
is
chordal
if it has no induced cycle of length at least 4. Dually, we say
G
is
cochordal
if
G^{c}
is chordal.

For each
g\geq 1
, the graph
gK_{2}
is a union of
g
disjoint edges. The
induced matching number
of
G
is

\indmatch(G)=\max\{g\mid\text{$G$ has an induced $gK_{2}$ subgraph}\}.

Clearly
C_{4}^{c}
is isomorphic to
2K_{2}
, therefore if
G
is a cochordal graph with at least one edge, then as
G
does not contain any induced

,
\indmatch(G)=1
.

The first part of the following lemma is contained in
[
12
, Lemma 2.2]
and
[
23
, Lemma 7]
. The second part is contained in
[
7
, Theorem 1]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
G
be a graph with at least one edge. The following statements hold.

(1)

There is an inequality
\reg I(G)\geq 1+\indmatch(G)
.

(2)

(Fröberg’s theorem)
We have
\reg I(G)=2

For a subset

of

of

E(G\setminus U):=\{\{x,y\}\in E(G)\mid x,y\in V(G)\setminus U\}.

For a vertex

Let

be a graph on

, and

(
[

There is an inequality

\reg I(G)\leq\max\{\reg I(G\setminus N_{G}[v])+1,\reg I(G\setminus v)\}.

2.4.
Regularity of saturated chains

The following result together with its simple proof serve as a motivation for Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}=(I_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a quasi-saturated chain of eventually nonzero edge ideals with
\ind({\mathcal{I}})=r
. Then we have an equality
\reg I_{n}=2
for all
n\geq r
, i.e.
I_{n}
has a
2
-linear resolution for all such
n
.

Proof.

By Fröberg’s theorem (
2.9
(2)), we have to show that the graph
G_{n}
corresponding to
I_{n}
is cochordal for all
n\geq r
.

Assume the contrary, that
i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}
is an induced cycle of length
m\geq 4
in
G_{n}^{c}
for some
n\geq r
. By reindexing, we may assume that
i_{1}=\min\{i_{j}:1\leq j\leq m\}
. Note that
i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}
are pairwise distinct.

We claim that
i_{2}<\min\{i_{3},\ldots,i_{m-1}\}
. If this is not the case, then
i_{2}>i_{j}
for some
3\leq j\leq m-1
. Since
\{i_{1},i_{j}\}\notin G_{n}^{c}
,
x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{j}}\in I_{n}
, and the
\Inc
-invariance of
{\mathcal{I}}
implies that
x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\in I_{n+i_{2}-i_{j}}
. Let
p=\Msupp(I_{n})
. The quasi-saturatedness of
{\mathcal{I}}
implies that
I_{s}\cap R_{p}\subseteq I_{n}
for all
s\geq n
. Now
x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\in I_{n+i_{2}-i_{j}}\cap R_{p}\subseteq I_{n}
, hence
\{i_{1},i_{2}\}\in G_{n}
, a contradiction. Similarly
i_{m}<\min\{i_{3},\ldots,i_{m-1}\}
.

Hence
\min\{i_{2},i_{3},\ldots,i_{m}\}=\min\{i_{2},i_{m}\}
. We assume that
\min\{i_{2},i_{3},\ldots,i_{m}\}=i_{2}
, the remaining case being similar. Now

a_{z-1}\leq a_{m-2}\leq n+i_{B}=n+i_{v_{\gamma}}\quad\text{(per \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{constr_inducedanticycle} and Equation \eqref{eq_vgammaB})},

we may let

\mu=\min\{\alpha\in\{1,\ldots,\gamma\}\mid a_{z-1}\leq i_{v_{\alpha}}+n\}.

Then
a_{z-1}\leq i_{v_{\mu}}+n
and by Algorithm
4
,
a_{z}-a_{z-1}=j_{v_{\mu}}-i_{v_{\mu}}-1
. Therefore

a_{z}-a_{p}=a_{z}-a_{z-1}+a_{z-1}-a_{p}\geq j_{v_{\mu}}-i_{v_{\mu}}

and

a_{z}=a_{z-1}+j_{v_{\mu}}-i_{v_{\mu}}-1\leq j_{v_{\mu}}+n.

It follows that if
a_{p}\geq i_{v_{\mu}}
, then
(a_{p},a_{z})\in\Delta_{v_{\mu}}
, since the following chain holds

0\leq a_{p}-i_{v_{\mu}}\leq a_{z}-j_{v_{\mu}}\leq n.

Finally, it remains to consider the case
i_{b}\leq a_{p}<i_{v_{\mu}}
. If
a_{z}\geq j_{b}-i_{b}+i_{v_{\mu}}
, then

a_{z}-a_{p}>a_{z}-i_{v_{\mu}}\geq j_{b}-i_{b},

which yields

0\leq a_{p}-i_{b}\leq a_{z}-j_{b}\leq n.

In other words,
(a_{p},a_{z})\in\Delta_{b}
.

Assume that
a_{z}<j_{b}-i_{b}+i_{v_{\mu}}
. In this case, the definition of
\mu
implies
\mu=1
, hence

a_{z}-a_{p}=a_{z}-a_{z-1}+a_{z-1}-a_{p}\geq j_{H}-i_{H}=j_{h}-i_{h}.

Since
n\geq 2r\geq j_{b}-i_{b}+i_{v_{\mu}}-j_{h}>a_{z}-j_{h},
the following chain holds

0\leq a_{p}-i_{h}\leq a_{z}-j_{h}\leq n,\;\text{i.e,}\;(a_{p},a_{z})\in\Delta_{h}.

This also finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

6.
A sharp regularity bound

The next theorem gives our first major result on the regularity of invariant chains of edge ideals.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}=(I_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an
\Inc
-invariant chain of eventually nonzero edge ideals. Let
r=\ind({\mathcal{I}})\geq 1
. Then for all
n\geq 4r
, there is an inequality
\reg I_{n}\leq 3
.

The key to this theorem is the following

Lemma 6.2
.

Keep using notations of
6.1
. Let
G_{n}
be the graph corresponding to
I_{n}
. Let
H
be an induced subgraph of
G_{n}
with at least one edge. Set

u_{1}=\max\{u\in V(H)\mid\text{for some $u<v,v\in V(H)$, we have}~\{u,v\}\in E(H)\}.

Let
v_{1}\in V(H)
be any vertex such that
u_{1}<v_{1}
and
\{u_{1},v_{1}\}\in E(H).
Then for every
n\geq 4r
and every choice of
H
and
v_{1}
as above, the graph
H\setminus N_{H}[v_{1}]
is cochordal.

Proof.

Assume the contrary that for some
n\geq 3r
and some induced subgraph
H
of
G_{n+r}
with
E(H)\neq\emptyset
, the graph
H\setminus N_{H}[v_{1}]
is not cochordal.

Assume that
E(G_{r})=\{\{i_{1},j_{1}\},\ldots,\{i_{s},j_{s}\}\}
where
s\geq 1
,
1\leq i_{p}<j_{p}\leq r
for all
p=1,\ldots,s
. For each
1\leq t\leq s
, denote
\Delta_{t}=\Delta((i_{t},j_{t}),n)
for simplicity. Therefore,

E(H)\subseteq E(G_{n+r})=\{\{u,v\}:(u,v)^{\leq}\in\cup_{t=1}^{s}\Delta_{t}\}.

Hence there exists an integer
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
such that
(u_{1},v_{1})\in\Delta_{\ell}
. Observe that
(i_{\ell}+j,v_{1})\in\Delta_{\ell}
for all
0\leq j\leq u_{1}-i_{\ell}
. Hence

\{v_{1},i_{\ell},i_{\ell}+1,\ldots,u_{1}\}\subseteq N_{G_{n+r}}[v_{1}].

Since
H
is an induced subgraph of
G_{n+r}
, it holds that

N_{H}[v_{1}]=V(H)\cap N_{G_{n+r}}[v_{1}].

Therefore
V(H\setminus N_{H}[v_{1}])\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,i_{\ell}-1\}\cup V_{1}
, where
i_{\ell}+j\notin V_{1}
for all
0\leq j\leq u_{1}-i_{\ell}
, in particular, if
u\in V_{1}
then
u>u_{1}
. If follows from the maximality of
u_{1}
in the hypothesis that:

(6.1)

\text{If $u<v$ and $\{u,v\}\in E(H\setminus N_{H}[v_{1}])$ then $u\leq i_{\ell}-1$}.

Step 1:
We claim that
H\setminus N_{H}[v_{1}]
has no induced
2K_{2}
.

Note that
H\setminus N_{H}[v_{1}]
is also an induced subgraph of
G_{n+r}
. By Lemma
3.6
, the graph
H\setminus N_{H}[v_{1}]
has no induced
2K_{2}
. Indeed, assume that
\{u_{2},v_{2}\},\{u_{3},v_{3}\}
is an induced
2K_{2}
of
H\setminus N_{H}[v_{1}]
where
u_{p}<v_{p}
. Then
3.6
implies that for some non-negative number
j_{2s}
,
u_{3}>n+j_{2s}>n
. This contradicts (
6.1
) and the fact that
n\geq 3r>i_{\ell}
.

Step 2:
Since
H\setminus N_{H}[v_{1}]
is not cochordal, and yet does not contained any induced
2K_{2}=C_{4}^{c}
, it necessarily contains an induced anticycle
C_{m}^{c}
, where
m\geq 5
. Label the consecutive vertices of
C_{m}^{c}
as
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
(following the terminology of
Section

2.3
). By reindexing, we may assume that
a_{1}=\min\{a_{p}:1\leq p\leq m\}
. Let
a_{m+1}=a_{1}
.

For each
1\leq p<q\leq m
such that
q-p\geq 2,(p,q)\neq(1,m)
, it holds that
\{a_{p},a_{q}\}\in E(H\setminus N_{H}[v_{1}])
, so
(a_{p},a_{q})^{\leq}\in\Delta_{pq}
, where
\Delta_{pq}=\Delta((i_{pq},j_{pq}),n)
is one of the sets
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{s}
. As in the proof of
4.2
, we have

Claim 1:
For all
1\leq t\leq m
, none of the points
(a_{t},a_{t+1}),(a_{t+1},a_{t})
belongs to

\Gamma=\mathop{\bigcup_{1\leq p<q\leq s}}_{q-p\geq 2,(p,q)\neq(1,m)}\Delta_{pq}.

Consider two cases according to whether
a_{2}\leq a_{m}
or
a_{m}<a_{2}
.

Case I:

a_{2}\leq a_{m}
. Thanks to
4.2
, we get
a_{m}=\max\{a_{p}:1\leq p\leq m\}
.

Apply
3.5
for the points
(a_{1},a_{3})\in\Delta_{13}
and
(a_{2},a_{m})\in\Delta_{2m}
. The conclusion is that
a_{1}<i_{2m}
and

(6.2)

a_{3}\leq j_{13}+n<a_{m}.

Case I.1:

a_{2}\leq a_{m-1}
. By Claim 1,
(a_{m-1},a_{m})\notin\Delta_{2m}
, hence the following chain is false

0\leq a_{m-1}-i_{2m}\leq a_{m}-j_{2m}\leq n.

The first inequality in the chain holds since
a_{m-1}\geq a_{2}\geq i_{2m}
, and the last inequality also holds since
a_{m}\leq j_{2m}+n
. Hence the middle inequality is false, namely

(6.3)

a_{m-1}>a_{m}+i_{2m}-j_{2m}.

By Claim 1,
(a_{m-2},a_{m-1})\notin\Delta_{1(m-1)}
, hence the following chain is false

0\leq a_{m-2}-i_{1(m-1)}\leq a_{m-1}-j_{1(m-1)}\leq n.

The first inequality in the chain holds since
a_{m-2}\geq a_{1}\geq i_{1(m-1)}
, and the last inequality also holds since
a_{m-1}\leq j_{1(m-1)}+n
. Hence the middle inequality is false, namely

(6.4)

a_{m-2}>a_{m-1}+i_{1(m-1)}-j_{1(m-1)}.

Combining (
6.2
), (
6.3
), and (
6.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle a_{m-2}>n+j_{13}+i_{2m}-j_{2m}+i_{1(m-1)}-j_{1(m-1)}.

Since
a_{m-2}\leq a_{m}
,
a_{m-2}\neq a_{m}
, and
\{a_{m-2},a_{m}\}\in E(H\setminus N_{H}[v_{1}])
, we obtain
a_{m-2}\leq i_{\ell}-1
by (
6.1
). Thus

i_{\ell}-1\geq a_{m-2}>n+j_{13}+i_{2m}-j_{2m}+i_{1(m-1)}-j_{1(m-1)},

so that

n<i_{\ell}-1-j_{13}-i_{2m}+j_{2m}-i_{1(m-1)}+j_{1(m-1)}\leq 3r,

where the last inequality follows from
i_{\ell}<j_{\ell}\leq\max\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{s}\}\leq r
. This contradicts the hypothesis
n\geq 3r.

Case I.2:

a_{2}>a_{m-1}
. Looking at the points
(a_{1},a_{m-2})=(a_{1},a_{m-2})^{\leq}\in\Delta_{1(m-2)}
and
(a_{m-1},a_{2})=(a_{2},a_{m-1})^{\leq}\in\Delta_{2(m-1)}
. Note that
a_{1}\leq a_{m-1}
, and
(a_{1},a_{2})
,
(a_{m-1},a_{m-2})\notin\Delta_{1(m-2)}\cup\Delta_{2(m-1)}
thanks to Claim 1, so arguing as above using
3.5
, we deduce
i_{1(m-2)}<i_{2(m-1)}.
Moreover, the same result yields

(6.5)

a_{2}>n+j_{1(m-2)}.

Since
\{a_{2},a_{m}\}\in E(H\setminus N_{H}[v_{1}])
,
a_{2}\leq a_{m}
and
a_{2}\neq a_{m}
, we get from (
6.1
) that
a_{2}\leq i_{\ell}-1
. Combining with (
6.5
), we obtain

i_{\ell}-1\geq a_{2}>n+j_{1(m-2)},

hence

n<i_{\ell}-1-j_{1(m-2)}<r.

This contradicts the hypothesis
n\geq 3r.

Case II:

a_{2}>a_{m}
. Relabel the vertices as follows:
b_{1}=a_{1},b_{i}=a_{m+2-i}
for
2\leq i\leq m
. Then
b_{1}=\min\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\}
and
b_{2}<b_{m}
. Arguing as for Case I, we finish Case II and conclude that
H\setminus N_{H}[v_{1}]
is cochordal. The proof is completed.
∎

Proof of
6.1
.

For a graph
H
, by abuse of notation, write
\reg H
for the regularity of the ideal
I(H)
. Let
n\geq 3r
be an integer. We prove that if
H
is an induced subgraph of
G_{n+r}
, then
\reg H\leq 3
. Induction on the cardinality of
E(H)
.

If
E(H)=\emptyset
then
\reg H=\reg(0)=-\infty
. Assume that
E(H)\neq\emptyset
. Let
u_{1}
be the maximal value of
u\in V(H)
such that for some
u<v,v\in V(H)
,
\{u,v\}
belongs to
E(H)
. Let
v_{1}\in V(H)
be a neighbor of
u_{1}
in
H
such that
v_{1}>u_{1}
. By
6.2
and Fröberg’s theorem,
\reg(H\setminus N_{H}[v_{1}])\leq 2
. This together with
2.10
, yields the chain

\reg(H)\leq\max\{\reg(H\setminus N_{H}[v_{1}])+1,\reg(H\setminus v_{1})\}\leq\max\{3,\reg(H\setminus v_{1})\}.

Clearly
\{u_{1},v_{1}\}\in E(H)\setminus E(H\setminus v_{1})
. Also

is an induced subgraph of

, hence by the induction hypothesis

. Therefore

. This finishes the induction and choosing

7.
Convergence

Now we state our main accomplishment in this paper. In the following statement, we employ the
q
-invariant introduced in
2.5
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}=(I_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an
\Inc
-invariant chain of eventually nonzero edge ideals. Keep using
5.1
. Then for all
n\geq N=\max\{5r,2r(r-2),4(r+q(I_{r}))\}
, there is an equality

\reg I_{n}=\reg I_{n+1},

and moreover
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\reg I_{n}\in\{2,3\}
.

In addition, the following statements are equivalent

(1)

\reg I_{n}=2
for all
n\gg 0
;

(2)

\reg I_{N}=2
;

(3)

Either
j_{q}=\max\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{s}\}
, or it holds that
\min\{j_{t}-i_{t}:t=1,\ldots,s\}=1
and
\indmatch(G_{3r})=1
.

Remark 7.2
.

We note that the number
N=\max\{5r,2r(r-2),4(r+q(I_{r}))\}
in
7.1
can be replaced by a constant depending only on
r
. Indeed, let
p=\Msupp I_{r}\leq r
, since
I_{r}
is quadratic and nonzero, by
2.5
,

q(I_{r})=\sum_{i=0}^{2}\dim_{\Bbbk}\left(\frac{R_{p}}{I_{r}\cap R_{p}}\right)_{i}\leq 1+p+\binom{p+1}{2}-1\leq\dfrac{r^{2}+3r}{2}.

Therefore
4(r+q(I_{r}))\leq 2(r^{2}+5r)
, and thus
N\leq 2(r^{2}+5r)
. Hence we can replace
N
by
2(r^{2}+5r)
in the statement of
7.1
. While we do not know the optimal (minimal) value for the number
N
such that
\reg I_{n}=\reg I_{n+1}
for all
n\geq N
,
7.5
below shows that this value has to be at least
3r-9
.

For the proof of
7.1
, we start in Propositions
7.3
and
7.4
with the sufficient conditions for the equality
\reg I_{n}=2
to hold for all
n\gg 0
.

Proposition 7.3
.

Keep using
5.1
. If
\indmatch(G_{3r})=1
and
\min\{j_{p}-i_{p}:p=1,\ldots,s\}=1
, then
\reg I_{n}=2
for all
n\geq\max\{5r,2r(r-2)\}
.

Proof.

By Fröberg’s theorem, we have to show that
G_{n}
is cochordal for all
n\geq\max\{5r,2r(r-2)\}
.

By the hypothesis,
I_{n}
is a nonzero edge ideal for all
n\geq r
. Thanks to
3.1
,
\indmatch(G_{n})=1
for all
n\geq 3r
. And hence
G_{n}
does not contain any induced
2K_{2}
for all
n\geq 3r
. Take
n\geq\max\{5r,2r(r-2)\}
. We show that
G_{n}
is cochordal.

It suffices to prove that
G_{n}
does not contain any induced
C_{m}^{c}
for
m\geq 5
. Assume the contrary, that for some
m\geq 5
and some
n\geq\max\{5r,2r(r-2)\}
,
G_{n}
contain an induced
C_{m}^{c}
. By
4.1
,

(7.1)

m>\dfrac{n}{r}\geq\max\{5,2(r-2)\}.

By the hypothesis, for some
1\leq p\leq s
, we have
j_{p}=i_{p}+1
. Since

\Delta((i_{p},i_{p}+1),n-r)=\{(u,v):i_{p}\leq u<v\leq i_{p}+1+n-r\},

we deduce that
A=\{i_{p},i_{p}+1,\ldots,i_{p}+n-r+1\}
is a clique in
G_{n}
. Note that
|V(G_{n}\setminus A)|\leq n-(n-r+2)=r-2
.

Denote the consecutive vertices of the induced
C_{m}^{c}
as a subgraph of
G_{n}
by
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}
. Denote
a_{m+1}=a_{1}
. So
a_{i},a_{i+1}
are not adjacent for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Since
A
is a clique and
C_{m}^{c}
is an induced subgraph of
G_{n}
, this implies
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}\cap A
has at most
m/2
elements. Hence

m=|\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}\cap A|+|\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}\setminus A|\leq\dfrac{m}{2}+|V(G_{n}\setminus A)|\leq\dfrac{m}{2}+(r-2).

In particular,
m\leq 2(r-2)
, contradicting (
7.1
). Therefore the above assumption is wrong, and
G_{n}
is cochordal for all
n\geq\max\{5r,2r(r-2)\}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 7.4
.

Keep using

. If

Label the consecutive vertices of the induced

Remark 7.5
.

The lower bound
3r
in the statement of

is nearly sharp. Let

be an integer,

be the invariant chain with stability index

and

.

Using the results in

, we complement Propositions

for

Theorem 7.6
.

5.1
. Assume

and
\min\{j_{p}-i_{p}:p=1,\ldots,s\}\geq 2

.

The hypothesis implies that

. We proceed by induction on

. The case

is vacuous: in that case

and the hypothesis is not satisfied.

Assume that

. Let

. If

, then by

, we deduce that
\reg I_{n}\geq 3
for all

. Together with

, we get
\reg I_{n}=3
for all

.

Now assume that

.

Let
{\mathcal{J}}=(J_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be the chain defined in
2.4
. By the last result,
\ind({\mathcal{J}})=r+1
and since

implies that

. By

, we get

is a quasi-saturated chain. But now
x_{i_{1}}x_{j_{q}}\in I_{r}
, so

Thus
x_{i_{1}}x_{j_{q}+1}\in I_{r}
, namely
\{i_{1},j_{q}+1\}\in E(G_{r})
. This contradicts the fact that
j_{q}=\max\{j_{t}\mid i_{t}=i_{1}\}
. Therefore
q(J_{r+1})<q(I_{r})
.

Let
H_{n}
be the graph corresponding to

for each

.

Observation:
There is an equality

\displaystyle E(H_{r+1})=\left\{\{i_{t},j_{t}\},\{i_{t},j_{t}+1\},\{i_{t}+1,j_{t}+1\}\mid 1\leq t\leq s,j_{t}<p\right\}\quad

\displaystyle\bigcup\quad\left\{\{i_{t},j_{t}\}\mid 1\leq t\leq s,j_{t}=p\right\}.

This follows from inspecting the definition

J_{r+1}=\langle I_{r+1}\cap R_{p}\rangle_{R_{r+1}}

and the fact that
I_{r+1}=\langle\Inc_{r,r+1}(I_{r})\rangle_{R_{r+1}}
.

From the Observation and the assumption that
p\geq j_{q}+2
, we see that the chain
{\mathcal{J}}
also satisfies the hypothesis of
7.6
. In details,

\displaystyle\min\{v-u\mid u<v,\{u,v\}\in E(H_{r+1})\}=\min\{v-u\mid u<v,\{u,v\}\in E(G_{r})\}\geq 2,

\displaystyle\max\{v\mid\{i_{1},v\}\in E(H_{r+1})\}=j_{q}+1,

\displaystyle\max\{v\mid\text{for some $u<v$ we have $\{u,v\}\in E(H_{r+1})$}\}=p.

By the induction hypothesis for the chain
{\mathcal{J}}
, we get for all
n\geq 4(r+1+q(J_{r+1}))
that

\reg J_{n}=3.

Since
q(J_{r+1})<q(I_{r})
, we have
4(r+q(I_{r}))\geq 4(r+1+q(J_{r+1}))
. For each
n\geq 4(r+q(I_{r}))\geq\max\{4(r+1+q(J_{r+1})),r+1\}
, we get

\reg I_{n}\geq\reg(I_{n}+(x_{n-r+p}))=\reg J_{n}=3.

The inequality in the chain follows from
2.8
. The first equality in the last chain is due to
2.4
. Together with
6.1
, we get

\reg I_{n}=3\quad\text{for each $n\geq 4(r+q(I_{r}))$}.

This finishes the induction and the proof.
∎

.

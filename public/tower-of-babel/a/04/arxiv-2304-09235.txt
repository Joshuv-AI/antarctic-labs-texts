Diagonalization-based preconditioners and generalized convergence bounds for ParaOpt
Arne Bouillon, Giovanni Samaey, Karl Meerbergen

Abstract
The ParaOpt algorithm was recently introduced as a time-parallel solver for optimal-control problems with a terminal-cost objective, and convergence results have been presented for the linear diffusive case with implicit-Euler time integrators. We reformulate ParaOpt for tracking problems and provide generalized convergence analyses for both objectives. We focus on linear diffusive equations and prove convergence bounds that are generic in the time integrators used. For large problem dimensions, ParaOpt’s performance depends crucially on having a good preconditioner to solve the arising linear systems. For the case where ParaOpt’s cheap, coarse-grained propagator is linear, we introduce diagonalization-based preconditioners inspired by recent advances in the ParaDiag family of methods. These preconditioners not only lead to a weakly-scalable ParaOpt version, but are themselves invertible in parallel, making maximal use of available concurrency. They have proven convergence properties in the linear diffusive case that are generic in the time discretization used, similarly to our ParaOpt results. Numerical results confirm that the iteration count of the iterative solvers used for ParaOpt’s linear systems becomes constant in the limit of an increasing processor count. The paper is accompanied by a sequential Matlab implementation.

These derivative terms are expensive to compute; this sparks interest in an \emph{inexact Newton iteration}, which only requires an approximate Jacobian. To this end, the authors in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{ganderPARAOPTPararealAlgorithm2020a}{}{}]} replace $\mathcal{P}_{y}$, $\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}$, $\mathcal{Q}_{y}$, and $\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda}$ by derivatives of coarse, approximate propagators $\tilde{\mathcal{P}}$ and $\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}$. This results in an approximate Jacobian
\begin{equation}{\bm{{\tilde{f}}}}^{\prime}\approx{\bm{{f}}}^{\prime}{.}\end{equation}
The ParaOpt algorithm, then, is to approximate \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:paraopt:tc:newton} -- in each iteration of the Newton procedure -- by using an inner iterative solver such as {gmres} that uses the coarse propagators $\tilde{\mathcal{P}}$ and $\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}$ to evaluate multiplications by an approximate Jacobian ${\bm{{\tilde{f}}}}^{\prime}$. Such multiplication is embarrassingly paralleli{z}{}able, since each element in the resulting vector can be calculated independently.

\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Convergence results}
Terminal-cost ParaOpt was proposed in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{ganderPARAOPTPararealAlgorithm2020a}{}{}]}, which includes a convergence bound for the case where both the fine and the coarse propagators use {fdto} implicit Euler. We propose alternative bounds $\rho^{*}$ on ParaOpt's convergence factor $\rho$ that are generic in the propagators used. \lx@cref{creftypepluralcap\lx@tilde refnum}{thm:conv:conv:tr-gen} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{thm:conv:conv:tc-gen} treat tracking and terminal cost, respectively.
\par\begin{theorem}When tracking ParaOpt is applied to a linear diffusive equation and \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{ass:conv:setting:1}, \lx@cref{refnum}{ass:conv:setting:2}, and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{ass:conv:setting:3} hold, the convergence factor $\rho$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\rho<\rho^{*}\coloneqq\max_{\sigma\in\mathrm{eig}(K)}\sqrt{\frac{(\tilde{\varphi}-\varphi)^{2}+(\tilde{\psi}-\psi)^{2}}{(1-\tilde{\varphi})^{2}+\tilde{\psi}^{2}}}{.}\end{equation}\@qedbox{}\end{theorem}
\@proof\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:proof-tr} is dedicated to proving this result.
\@qedbox{}\end@proof
\par\lx@newpage\par The $\varphi$, $\psi$, $\tilde{\varphi}$, and $\tilde{\psi}$ values for a specific set of propagators can be filled in to the general bound given in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{thm:conv:conv:tr-gen}. We give an example.
\begin{example}Consider tracking ParaOpt applied to a linear diffusive equation with an exact fine propagator and a one-step implicit-Euler coarse one. Then we can combine the results from \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{lmm:conv:setting:prop-tr-ex} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{lmm:conv:setting:prop-tr-ie} with \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:thm:conv:conv:tr-gen:bound} to bound $\rho$. In fact, in this specific case, we can prove that
\begin{equation}\rho<\rho^{*}<1\end{equation}for any $\sigma$, $\gamma$, and ${\Delta T}$, meaning that ParaOpt does not diverge. The bound \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:conv:conv:special} is proven in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:apdx-po-prop:special}.
\@qedbox{}\end{example}
\par\begin{theorem}When terminal-cost ParaOpt is applied to a linear diffusive equation and \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{ass:conv:setting:1}, \lx@cref{refnum}{ass:conv:setting:2}, and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{ass:conv:setting:3} hold, the convergence factor $\rho$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\rho\leq\rho^{*}\coloneqq\max_{\sigma\in\mathrm{eig}(K)}\max\left(\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\varphi-\tilde{\varphi}\right\rvert}}{1-\tilde{\varphi}},x^{*}\right){,}\end{equation}where $x^{*}$ is the root of $f_{\infty}(x)\coloneqq\frac{\tilde{\psi}-\psi}{\tilde{\psi}+1/\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(\tilde{\varphi}+\frac{\varphi-\tilde{\varphi}}{x})^{2l}}-x$ with the largest magnitude.
\@qedbox{}\end{theorem}
\@proof\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:proof-tc} is dedicated to proving this result.
\@qedbox{}\end@proof
Note that, while its definition is implicit, $x^{*}$ in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{thm:conv:conv:tc-gen} is efficiently computable. This is elaborated upon at the end of \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:proof-tc}.

The latter property not only results in efficiently computable bounds (as opposed to calculating eigenvalues of potentially large matrices), but also ensures that the number of ParaOpt iterations stays constant when increasing $T$ together with $\widehat{L}$. \par To investigate specific propagators, their $\varphi$ and $\psi$ can be calculated as a function of the problem parameters $\sigma$, $\gamma$, and $\tau$. \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:apdx-po-prop:phipsi} goes into more detail about this procedure. Let us do so for an exact fine propagator (or, equivalently, a numerical propagator in the limit for infinitely small time steps) and an implicit-Euler coarse propagator. Recall from \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{rem:conv:setting:optdisc} that, in the case of terminal cost, two implicit-Euler discreti{z}{}ations are possible: either first-discreti{z}{}e-then-optimi{z}{}e (as \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{ganderPARAOPTPararealAlgorithm2020a}{}{}]} uses) or the other way around. We will compare both techniques.

In the tracking case, \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{fig:conv:interp:rhostar:tr-1} confirms the result \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:conv:conv:special}, which guarantees $\rho$ never exceeds $1$. With an exact fine propagator, it is clear that our {fotd} coarse propagators never cause divergence for linear diffusive problems, while those based on {fdto} might. \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{fig:conv:interp:rhostar:fdtovsfotd} shows the ratio of the bounds $\rho^{*}$ from \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{fig:conv:interp:rhostar:tc-fotd-1} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{fig:conv:interp:rhostar:tc-fdto-1}, comparing the {fotd} and {fdto} strategies. This shows the novel bound to be at least as tight as the existing one.
\par\lx@newpage\par\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Diagonali\sz{}ation-based preconditioners}
Having presented the ParaOpt algorithm and studied its convergence, we will now consider its scaling in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:diag:scale}, establishing the need for efficient preconditioners in the inexact-Newton step. For affine coarse propagators, we propose in \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:diag:linear} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{sec:diag:prec} to construct a preconditioner that uses \emph{alpha-circulant} approximations of the system matrix, which can be inverted efficiently by a diagonali{z}{}ation procedure. \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:diag:small} then details how to solve the arising smaller linear systems, after which \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:diag:conv} mentions some properties of the preconditioners that influence the convergence of iterative solvers.
\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Scaling ParaOpt and the need for preconditioners}
When ParaOpt uses time integrators that satisfy \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{ass:conv:setting:1}, \lx@cref{refnum}{ass:conv:setting:2}, and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{ass:conv:setting:3}, $\rho^{*}$ in \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{thm:conv:conv:tc-gen} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{thm:conv:conv:tr-gen} is an upper bound on the rate of the algorithm's exponential convergence. When the number of intervals $L$ is increased -- which scales both the problem size and the available parallelism -- we study the evolution of $\rho^{*}$ in two regimes of \emph{weak scaling} \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{ganderPARAOPTPararealAlgorithm2020a}{}{}]}.
\begin{itemize}\itemize@item@When $T$ and $L$ are increased together, $\widehat{\sigma}$ and $\widehat{\gamma}$ do not change. Given that our bounds are $L$-independent, $\rho^{*}$ stays constant and the upper bound on the number of ParaOpt iterations is independent of the available parallelism.
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@When $T$ is kept constant, ${\Delta T}$ -- and with it, $\widehat{\sigma}$ and $\widehat{\gamma}$ -- decreases instead. Scaling $L$ then corresponds to traversing the graphs in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{fig:conv:interp:rhostar} in a diagonal line towards the region with small $\widehat{\sigma}$ and $\widehat{\gamma}$. In, \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{ganderPARAOPTPararealAlgorithm2020a}{}{}]}, it already noted that $\rho^{*}$ is bounded in this scaling regime for {fdto} implicit-Euler propagators on a linear diffusive terminal-cost problem. Our graphs allow similar conclusions and can be drawn for any propagators satisfying \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{ass:conv:setting:1}, \lx@cref{refnum}{ass:conv:setting:2}, and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{ass:conv:setting:3}. In \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{fig:conv:interp:rhostar}'s examples, $\rho^{*}$ even goes to zero in the scaling limit, surpassing the usual concept of weak scalability. In the non-asymptotic regime, weak scalability may be absent until the maximum at $\widehat{\sigma}\approx 1$ has been surmounted.
\end{itemize}
It follows from this discussion that, if the amount of work in each ParaOpt iteration scales linearly with $L$, the algorithm is weakly scalable for linear diffusive problems (and, experimentally, more broadly \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{ganderPARAOPTPararealAlgorithm2020a}{}{}]}). This condition, however, is not yet fulfilled. The inexact-Newton procedure solves, in each iteration, a linear system with a matrix of size $(2M\widehat{L}\times 2M\widehat{L})$. With a direct or non-preconditioned iterative method, the cost of this scales superlinearly with $\widehat{L}$ (and thus with $L$), which -- especially if $K\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}$ is large but sparse -- starts to dominate the algorithm's execution time.
\par Hence our mission in the rest of this section is to precondition these systems such that the cost of solving them with iterative methods is linear in $L$. Each iteration of the iterative solver uses a matrix multiplication and an inversion, which will both scale (log-)linearly with $L$. The number of iterations should then be constant.
\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Linear coarse-grid correction}
Our preconditioners will apply to affine coarse propagators (that is, $\tilde{\mathcal{P}}$ and $\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}$ are of the forms \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:conv:setting:lin:Ptilde} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{eq:conv:setting:lin:Qtilde}). Then the inexact-Newton step -- \emph{coarse-grid correction}, in Parareal vernacular -- looks like
\begin{equation}\tilde{A}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}{\bm{{y}}}^{k}-{\bm{{y}}}^{k-1}\\
{\bm{{\widehat{\lambda}}}}^{k}-{\bm{{\widehat{\lambda}}}}^{k-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]=-{\bm{{f}}}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}{\bm{{y}}}^{k-1}\\
{\bm{{\widehat{\lambda}}}}^{k-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)\end{equation}
where the matrix $\tilde{A}$ can be written as (using $\otimes$ to denote a Kronecker product)
\begin{equation}\tilde{A}=\begin{bmatrix}I\otimes I+B\otimes\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{P}}&I\otimes\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathcal{P}}\\
-I\otimes\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathcal{Q}}+E\otimes(\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathcal{Q}}-\partial\widehat{Q}/\partial{\bm{{y}}}_{\widehat{L}})&I\otimes I+B^{\mathstrut\scriptscriptstyle{\top}}\otimes\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{Q}}\end{bmatrix}{.}\end{equation}
Here, $\widehat{Q}$ is as defined in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:paraopt:paraopt:Qhat}, $E$ is a matrix with as only non-zero a one in its bottom-right corner, and $B$ only has $(-1)$s on its first sub-diagonal.
\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Formulating the preconditioners}
Inspired by work on paralleli{z}{}ing the coarse-grid correction of Parareal \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{wuParallelCoarseGrid2018a}{}{}]}, we propose a low-rank perturbation of $\tilde{A}$ in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:diag:linear:Atilde} as a preconditioner. Introduce a parameter $\alpha\in\mathbb{C}$ and define
\begin{equation}P(\alpha)\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}I\otimes I+C(\alpha)\otimes\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{P}}&I\otimes\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathcal{P}}\\
-I\otimes\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathcal{Q}}&I\otimes I+C^{*}(\alpha)\otimes\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{Q}}\end{bmatrix}{,}\end{equation}
where $C(\alpha)$ differs from $B$ only by an additional non-zero value of $-\alpha$ in the top-right corner. This preconditioner contains two modifications compared to $\tilde{A}$: \begin{itemize}\itemize@item@Replacing $B$ by $C(\alpha)$ causes a rank-$(2M)$ perturbation, which is scaled by $\alpha$.
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@The term $E\otimes(\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathcal{Q}}-\partial\widehat{Q}/\partial{\bm{{y}}}_{\widehat{L}})$ has been left out. This has no effect in the tracking case, but is a rank-$M$ perturbation for terminal cost (although it combines with the perturbation above to another rank-$(2M)$ perturbation).
\end{itemize}
The preconditioner is itself invertible by a parallel process, as will be explained next. We outline two methods to perform this inversion: a general one, which requires us to choose $\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert}=1$, and one for the case $\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathcal{Q}}=O$, which can use any $\alpha\neq 0$.
\par\par\@@unnumbered@section{paragraph}{toc}{General method}
The matrix $C(\alpha)$ used in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:diag:prec:Palpha} is an \emph{alpha-circulant} matrix -- that is, it is Toeplitz and each super-diagonal is equal to a value $\alpha$ times its complementing sub-diagonal. It is well-known (see{,} e.g.{,}\ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{biniNumericalMethodsStructured2005a}{}{}]}) that alpha-circulants diagonali{z}{}e as
\begin{equation}C(\alpha)=VD(\alpha)V^{-1}\hskip 8.19447pt\text{with}\hskip 8.19447ptV=\Gamma_{\alpha}^{-1}\mathbb{F}^{*}\hskip 8.19447pt\text{and}\hskip 8.19447ptD(\alpha)=\mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{\widehat{L}}\mathbb{F}\Gamma_{\alpha}{\bm{{c}}}_{1}(\alpha))\end{equation}
where ${\bm{{c}}}_{1}(\alpha)$ is $C(\alpha)$'s first column, $\mathbb{F}=\{{\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}jk/\widehat{L}}/\sqrt{\widehat{L}}\}_{j,k=0}^{\widehat{L}-1}$ is the discrete Fourier matrix, and we define $\Gamma_{\alpha}=\diag(1,\alpha^{1/\widehat{L}},\ldots,\alpha^{(\widehat{L}-1)/\widehat{L}})$. When $\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert}=1$, it holds that $\Gamma_{\alpha}^{-1}=\Gamma_{\alpha}^{*}$ (where $\Gamma_{\alpha}^{*}$ is the Hermitian transpose of $\Gamma_{\alpha}$) and, therefore, $C(\alpha)$ and $C^{*}(\alpha)$ are simultaneously diagonali{z}{}able \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{mezelfparadiag}{}{}]}. Then
\begin{equation*}P^{-1}(\alpha)=\Bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\Gamma_{\alpha}^{*}\mathbb{F}^{*}\\
&\Gamma_{\alpha}^{*}\mathbb{F}^{*}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\otimes I\end{smallmatrix}\Bigr)\left[\begin{smallmatrix}I\otimes I+D(\alpha)\otimes\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{P}}&I\otimes\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathcal{P}}\\
-I\otimes\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathcal{Q}}&I\otimes I+D^{*}(\alpha)\otimes\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{Q}}\end{smallmatrix}\right]^{-1}\Bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbb{F}\Gamma_{\alpha}\\
&\mathbb{F}\Gamma_{\alpha}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\otimes I\end{smallmatrix}\Bigr){,}\end{equation*}
where the inverted matrix on the right-hand side has only diagonal matrices as left operands in the Kronecker products. Thus inversion of $P(\alpha)$ can be decomposed into $\widehat{L}$ different inversions that can be solved in parallel, as implemented in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{alg:diag:prec:gen}. The Fourier matrix $\mathbb{F}$ can be applied efficiently (log-linearly in $L$) with the fast Fourier transform and can be paralleli{z}{}ed over the spatial dimensions of the problem.

\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Solving the smaller systems}
In \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{alg:diag:prec:gen} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{alg:diag:prec:triangle}, it is needed to solve $\widehat{L}$ linear systems in parallel to each other. In the general method (\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{alg:diag:prec:gen}), these systems \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:alg:diag:prec:gen:sys} use matrices of the form
\begin{equation}H_{l}\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}I+d_{l}(\alpha)\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{P}}&\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathcal{P}}\\
-\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathcal{Q}}&I+d_{l}^{*}(\alpha)\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{Q}}\end{bmatrix}\end{equation}
and this subsection outlines how $H_{l}$ can be inverted. This is non-trivial due to the matrices $\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{P}}$, $\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{Q}}$, $\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathcal{P}}$, and $\tilde{\Psi}_{\mathcal{Q}}$, which are defined through the coarse propagators \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:conv:setting:lin:Ptilde} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{eq:conv:setting:lin:Qtilde}. We focus on the general method; the speciali{z}{}ed case of \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{alg:diag:prec:triangle} is simpler and can be treated analogously. We propose two methods for solving systems with $H_{l}$, differing both in generality and in performance.
\par\lx@newpage\par\@@unnumbered@section{paragraph}{toc}{Method 1: Black-box approach}
One strength of the ParaOpt algorithm is that the propagators can be given as black boxes (although for our preconditioners, we do require the coarse ones to be affine). In that case we can only access the $\tilde{\Phi}$ and $\tilde{\Psi}$ matrices through the propagators $\tilde{\mathcal{P}}$ and $\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}$ by \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:conv:setting:lin:Ptilde} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{eq:conv:setting:lin:Qtilde}. Without giving up on the black-box character of the coarse solvers, the smaller systems can be tackled with an iterative solver, which only needs $H_{l}$ as a multiplication routine. It can be seen that, for any ${\bm{{x}}}$ and ${\bm{{z}}}$,
\begin{equation}H_{l}\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{{x}}}\\
{\bm{{z}}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{{x}}}+\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(d_{l}(\alpha){\bm{{x}}},-{\bm{{z}}})-\tilde{\mathcal{P}}({\bm{{0}}},{\bm{{0}}})\\
{\bm{{z}}}+\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}(-{\bm{{x}}},d_{l}^{*}(\alpha){\bm{{z}}})-\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}({\bm{{0}}},{\bm{{0}}})\end{bmatrix}\end{equation}
where the quantities $\tilde{\mathcal{P}}({\bm{{0}}},{\bm{{0}}})$ and $\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}({\bm{{0}}},{\bm{{0}}})$ can be precomputed.
\par\par\@@unnumbered@section{paragraph}{toc}{Method 2: Using the coarse propagators' explicit form}
In many cases, the coarse propagators will be simple and their explicit form known. Then it is often possible to solve the linear system more cheaply than with the black-box approach above. \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{ex:diag:small:tr-ie1} illustrates this for the case of a simple coarse propagator.
\begin{example}ParaOpt with a tracking objective and a one-step implicit-Euler coarse propagator for linear problems has that
\begin{equation}\tilde{\Phi}=(I+{\Delta T}K)^{-1}\hskip 8.19447pt\text{and}\hskip 8.19447pt\tilde{\Psi}=\widehat{\gamma}(I+{\Delta T}K)^{-1}{,}\end{equation}as can be derived using \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:apdx-po-prop:phipsi}'s arguments. Write $Z\coloneqq I+{\Delta T}K$. Then
\begin{equation}\Bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}I+d_{l}(\alpha)Z^{-1}&\widehat{\gamma}Z^{-1}\\
-\widehat{\gamma}Z^{-1}&I+d_{l}^{*}(\alpha)Z^{-1}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\Bigr]\Bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}{\bm{{r_{2}}}}\\
{\bm{{s_{2}}}}\end{smallmatrix}\Bigr]=\Bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}{\bm{{r_{1}}}}\\
{\bm{{s_{1}}}}\end{smallmatrix}\Bigr]\Leftrightarrow\Bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}Z+d_{l}(\alpha)I&\widehat{\gamma}I\\
-\widehat{\gamma}I&Z+d_{l}^{*}(\alpha)I\\
\end{smallmatrix}\Bigr]\Bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}{\bm{{r_{2}}}}\\

\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Convergence results}
To assess the convergence properties of solving systems with $\tilde{A}$ using the proposed preconditioners $P(\alpha)$, we take the usual approach of studying the eigenvalues of the preconditioned system matrix $P^{-1}(\alpha)\tilde{A}$. If those are clustered together and lie far enough away from zero, rapid convergence is expected for most iterative linear-system solvers such as {gmres} \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{saadGMRESGeneralizedMinimal1986a}{}{}]}.
\par An advantage of using ParaDiag-inspired preconditioners is that, in certain cases, eigenvalue results from ParaDiag apply directly. In \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{mezelfparadiag}{}{}]}, analytic expressions are provided for the preconditioned eigenvalues of optimi{z}{}ation ParaDiag methods applied to linear diffusive problems, for both objective functions we study. When \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{ass:conv:setting:1} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{ass:conv:setting:2} are satisfied \emph{for the coarse propagator}, those eigenvalue expressions also apply to the proposed ParaOpt preconditioners. In particular, for tracking and when choosing $\alpha=-1$ in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:diag:prec:Palpha}, it is shown in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{mezelfparadiag}{}{}]} that {gmres} converges exponentially with a problem-independent convergence rate under \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{ass:conv:setting:1}, \lx@cref{refnum}{ass:conv:setting:2}, and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{ass:conv:setting:3}.

\begin{equation}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\theta\right\rvert}^{2}=1+\frac{c}{a}-\frac{b}{a}=\frac{{\bm{{v}}}^{*}((B-\tilde{B})^{\mathstrut\scriptscriptstyle{\top}}(B-\tilde{B})){\bm{{v}}}}{{\bm{{v}}}^{*}(\tilde{B}^{\mathstrut\scriptscriptstyle{\top}}\tilde{B}+\tilde{\psi}E){\bm{{v}}}}{.}\end{equation}
\@qedbox{}\end@proof
\par We utili{z}{}e this lemma in the same way as before, but now must keep in mind that its result is only guaranteed for non-real eigenvalues. In that case, we can state
\begin{equation}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\theta\right\rvert}=\sqrt{\frac{{\bm{{v}}}^{*}M_{1}{\bm{{v}}}}{{\bm{{v}}}^{*}M_{2}{\bm{{v}}}+\tilde{\psi}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v_{\widehat{L}}\right\rvert}^{2}}}\leq\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\varphi-\tilde{\varphi}\right\rvert}}{1-\tilde{\varphi}}\end{equation}
where we recall $M_{1}$ and $M_{2}$ from \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:proof-tr:proof-tr:Ms}.
\par\par\@@unnumbered@section{paragraph}{toc}{Real eigenvalues}
When the contents of the square root in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:proof-tc:proof-tc:eig}'s solution are non-negative, the reasoning breaks down. This case can be treated in a different way. By left-multiplying \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:proof-tc:proof-tc:eig:1} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{eq:proof-tc:proof-tc:eig:2} by ${\bm{{w}}}^{*}$ and ${\bm{{v}}}^{*}$, respectively, and then subtracting the complex adjoint of the latter from the former, we find that
\begin{equation}{\bm{{w}}}^{*}\psi{\bm{{w}}}+{\bm{{v}}}^{*}E{\bm{{v}}}=\theta({\bm{{w}}}^{*}\tilde{\psi}{\bm{{w}}}+{\bm{{v}}}^{*}E{\bm{{v}}})\Leftrightarrow\theta=\frac{\psi\mathinner{\!\left\lVert{\bm{{w}}}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v_{\widehat{L}}\right\rvert}^{2}}{\tilde{\psi}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert{\bm{{w}}}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v_{\widehat{L}}\right\rvert}^{2}}{,}\end{equation}
such that, shifting our focus to $(1-\theta)$, we obtain
\begin{equation}(1-\theta)=\frac{(\tilde{\psi}-\psi)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert{\bm{{w}}}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}}{\tilde{\psi}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert{\bm{{w}}}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v_{\widehat{L}}\right\rvert}^{2}}{.}\end{equation}
As shown in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{ganderPARAOPTPararealAlgorithm2020a}{}{}, (3.24) and (3.28)]}, it holds that $v_{\widehat{L}}=w_{\widehat{L}}$ and $w_{l}=w_{\widehat{L}}(\tilde{\varphi}+\frac{\varphi-\tilde{\varphi}}{1-\theta})^{\widehat{L}-l}$. A normali{z}{}ation such that $w_{\widehat{L}}=1$ and the definition $x\coloneqq(1-\theta)$ then yield
\begin{equation}x=\frac{\tilde{\psi}-\psi}{\tilde{\psi}+1/\sum_{l=0}^{\widehat{L}-1}(\tilde{\varphi}+\frac{\varphi-\tilde{\varphi}}{x})^{2l}}\Leftrightarrow f_{\widehat{L}}(x)\coloneqq\frac{\tilde{\psi}-\psi}{\tilde{\psi}+1/\sum_{l=0}^{\widehat{L}-1}(\tilde{\varphi}+\frac{\varphi-\tilde{\varphi}}{x})^{2l}}-x=0{.}\end{equation}
Define $g(x)\coloneqq(\tilde{\psi}-\psi)/\tilde{\psi}-x$. We have, depending on $\mathrm{sign}(\tilde{\psi}-\psi)$,
\begin{equation}\forall x\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}f_{\widehat{L}}(x)\leq f_{\infty}(x)\leq g(x)\hskip 8.19447pt\text{or}\hskip 8.19447pt\forall x\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}f_{\widehat{L}}(x)\geq f_{\infty}(x)\geq g(x){.}\end{equation}
Now denote by $x_{1}$ a root of $f_{\widehat{L}}$, and by $x_{2}$ the root of $g$. Clearly,
\begin{equation}\mathrm{sign}(x_{1})=\mathrm{sign}(x_{2})\hskip 8.19447pt\text{and}\hskip 8.19447pt\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x_{1}\right\rvert}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x_{2}\right\rvert}{.}\end{equation}
This means that $\min\{f_{\infty}(x_{1}),f_{\infty}(x_{2})\}\leq 0\leq\max\{f_{\infty}(x_{1}),f_{\infty}(x_{2})\}$. Since $f_{\widehat{L}}$, $f_{\infty}$, and $g$ are continuous, Bolzano's theorem asserts that
\begin{equation}f_{\infty}(x^{*})=0\hskip 8.19447pt\text{for some}\hskip 8.19447pt\min\{x_{1},x_{2}\}\leq x^{*}\leq\max\{x_{1},x_{2}\}{,}\end{equation}
which, by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:proof-tc:proof-tc:x12}, means that $\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x^{*}\right\rvert}\geq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x_{1}\right\rvert}$. Recall that any \emph{real} eigenvalue $x=(1-\theta)$ of the ParaOpt iteration matrix must be a root of $f_{\widehat{L}}$; by the above argument, then, $\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 1-\theta\right\rvert}$ is bounded from above by the absolute value of at least one root of
\begin{equation}f_{\infty}(x)=\frac{\tilde{\psi}-\psi}{\tilde{\psi}+1/\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(\tilde{\varphi}+\frac{\varphi-\tilde{\varphi}}{x})^{2l}}-x{.}\end{equation}
This root is efficiently computable: if $x=(\tilde{\psi}-\psi)/\tilde{\psi}$ makes the infinite sum diverge, it is a root (and the one with the largest absolute value). Otherwise, all roots must have the sum converge, which can then be replaced by $(1-(\tilde{\varphi}+\frac{\varphi-\tilde{\varphi}}{x})^{2})^{-1}$. Then finding $f_{\infty}$'s roots amounts to solving a quadratic equation and checking when the sum converges. Our numerical tests suggest that $f_{\infty}$ always has exactly one root.

\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:conv} presents upper bounds on the ParaOpt convergence factor for linear diffusive problems. \lx@cref{creftypepluralcap\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:num:conv-tr} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{sec:num:conv-tc} will study the accuracy and sharpness of these bounds through numerical tests. Later, \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:num:prec} looks at the performance of the proposed preconditioners.
\par Our tests confirm the accuracy of our bounds and the scalability of preconditioned ParaOpt. To perform them, we have extended the {pintopt} package designed in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{mezelfparadiag}{}{}]} to include a sequential implementation of ParaOpt and its preconditioners. Though unoptimi{z}{}ed, it is a useful reference solver and can be used to study iteration counts.

\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Preconditioning}
As discussed in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:diag:scale}, the two main processes to study in ParaOpt are the outer inexact-Newton iterations and the inner linear-system solves that constitute the inexact-Newton corrections. The past two subsections have confirmed the scalability of the former -- now, we verify whether the proposed preconditioners succeed in completing the picture by keeping inner iterations constant when the number of parallel time intervals is scaled.
\par\par\@@unnumbered@section{paragraph}{toc}{Heat problem}
We study the same heat problem considered in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{mezelfparadiag}{}{}]} (and loosely adapted from \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{emmettEfficientParallelTime2012}{}{}]}), defined on a periodic spatial domain $\Omega=[0,1]^{2}$ and given by
\begin{equation}\partial_{t}y=\Delta y+u{,}\end{equation}
where the spatial derivative is discreti{z}{}ed using central differences. We also use \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{mezelfparadiag}{}{}]}'s initial value, target trajectory and target state
\@@amsalign
A non-smooth $y_{\mathrm{init}}$ is important to make the preconditioning problem sufficiently challenging, as also noticed in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{goddardNoteParallelPreconditioning2019,wuParallelInTimeBlockCirculantPreconditioner2020a}{}{}]}. We use the same parameters $\gamma=0.05$ and $T=2$ as \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{gotschelEfficientParallelinTimeMethod2019a}{}{}]}, and a spatial grid of $M=8\times 8$ points (a small value, to keep the non-preconditioned computation tractable). The fine propagator uses $10$ implicit-Euler steps; the coarse one just $1$. All further results will use a tolerance of $10^{-6}$ for ParaOpt, and will employ a {gmres} inner solver with a tolerance of $10^{-4}$.
\begin{remark}A significant difference between ParaDiag's use as a stand-alone method and its adaptation as a ParaOpt preconditioner is the tolerance to which the system should be solved. Since ParaOpt solves a system with a Jacobian that is already approximate, there is no need to solve the system to full precision. Figure \ref{fig:po-num:prec:gmrestol} shows that, for the heat problem we are about to study with $\widehat{L}=10$, $T=2$, $\gamma=0.05$, $M=8\times 8$, a ten-step fine and a one-step coarse implicit-Euler propagator, a tolerance as high as $10^{-3}$ already performs well.
\@qedbox{}\end{remark}

\par\par\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Conclusions}
The main focus of this paper is on linear diffusive problems. In that setting, and when \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{ass:conv:setting:1}, \lx@cref{refnum}{ass:conv:setting:2}, and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{ass:conv:setting:3} are satisfied, we adapted ParaOpt to work for both tracking and terminal-cost objectives, additionally proposing generic convergence bounds and a preconditioner that ensures good weak scalability. We summari{z}{}e our main contributions in the linear diffusive setting. \begin{itemize}\itemize@item@We formulated an extension of ParaOpt to the setting of tracking problems.
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@We proved a generali{z}{}ed convergence bound for terminal-cost ParaOpt that is generic in the propagators used (as long as they satisfy \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{ass:conv:setting:1}, \lx@cref{refnum}{ass:conv:setting:2}, and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{ass:conv:setting:3}). Numerical results confirmed the bound's validity and showed that it is even sharper than the state-of-the-art result \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{ganderPARAOPTPararealAlgorithm2020a}{}{}]}.
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@We proved a similar bound for the tracking setting and proved that the case of an exact fine and a one-step implicit-Euler coarse propagator guarantees ParaOpt does not diverge.
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@Thanks to the generic convergence bound, we were able to study a different type of implicit-Euler coarse propagator for terminal-cost problems and found that it improves ParaOpt's convergence.
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@We proposed diagonali{z}{}ation-based preconditioners to improve the scaling of ParaOpt. Analytic results from the ParaDiag literature and numerical tests confirm that ParaOpt is now a weakly scalable algorithm.
\end{itemize}
\par For problems outside the linear diffusive category, ParaOpt is still a very promising method \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{ganderPARAOPTPararealAlgorithm2020a}{}{}]}, although poorly understood. Our preconditioners apply as long as the coarse propagator is affine in its arguments, even for non-diffusive and non-symmetric problems. In addition, we are confident that these preconditioners can be extended to non-linear coarse propagators, although we leave this for future work. Recall that our application of diagonali{z}{}ation-based preconditioners to ParaOpt was inspired by proceedings in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{wuParallelCoarseGrid2018a}{}{}]} for the {ivp} Parareal algorithm. There, non-linear Parareal is supported by using non-linear variants of ParaDiag. For {ivp}s, these non-linear ParaDiag methods exist \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{ganderTimeParallelizationNonlinear2017a,liuFastBlockAcirculant2020a}{}{}]}. Developing similar techniques for optimi{z}{}ation ParaDiag would open the way for preconditioning non-linear ParaOpt similarly to what we proposed.
\par Other interesting future work could consist of applying ParaOpt to realistic applications, testing the proposed preconditioners on more complex (but still linear) problems. In addition, with the generic bounds in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:conv:conv}, the search for coarse propagators with better convergence properties than implicit Euler is wide open: one simply needs to calculate a propagator's $\tilde{\varphi}$ and $\tilde{\psi}$ (see \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:apdx-po-prop:phipsi}) to evaluate its performance.
\par We conclude by noting that ParaOpt does not necessarily need a parallel preconditioner; the fine propagations can already be paralleli{z}{}ed, so as long as the coarse-grid correction is cheap, it need not be parallel for the method itself to achieve speed-ups (in fact, the base version of Parareal has sequential coarse-grid correction). It would therefore be feasible for sequential preconditioners to match or surpass the performance of the ones proposed here, if they cause the system to be solved in fewer iterations.

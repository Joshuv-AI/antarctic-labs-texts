Mode analysis of cosmological perturbations with the E E -model α \alpha -attractor inflaton potentials
Arunoday Sarkar, Chitrak Sarkar, Buddhadeb Ghosh

Abstract
We have carried out detailed k k -mode analysis of single-inflaton slow-roll inflationary perturbations including quantum fluctuations by setting up non-linear coupled differential equations of inflaton field ( ϕ \phi ), its perturbation ( δ ​ ϕ \delta\phi ) and the metric perturbation (the Bardeen potential, Φ B \Phi_{B} ), and calculated the number of e-folds ( N N ), scalar spectral index ( n s n_{s} ), tensor spectral index ( n h n_{h} ), scalar power spectrum ( Δ s \Delta_{s} ), tensor power spectrum ( Δ h \Delta_{h} ), tensor-to-scalar ratio ( r r ) and the Hubble parameter ( H H ) for different k k values at the horizon crossing. In these calculations we have employed the E E -model α \alpha -attractor potentials which are found to display slow-roll behaviour. The values of n s n_{s} and r r obtained by us are consistent with those given by the well-known universal α \alpha -attractor formulae. We got n s = 0.956908 n_{s}=0.956908 and r = 0.005571 r=0.005571 at k = 10 6 k=10^{6} Planck unit for the value of the potential parameter n = 1 n=1 . These can be compared with the Planck-2018 data viz. , n s = 0.9649 ± 0.0042 n_{s}=0.9649\pm 0.0042 at 68 % 68\% CL, r < 0.064 r<0.064 at 95 % 95\% CL and ACT-2020 data viz. , n s = 0.9691 ± 0.0041 n_{s}=0.9691\pm 0.0041 at 68 % 68\% CL.



I
Introduction

The slow-roll inflationary scenario in the early universe was first introduced by Linde
Linde 1987
and Albrecht and Steinhardt
Albrecht and Steinhardt 1987
in order to rectify the shortcomings associated with the originally-proposed inflationary picture of Guth
Guth 1987
and to provide better solutions for the cosmological problems such as the flatness, the horizon, the homogeneity and isotropy and the magnetic monopole problems.

The main ingredient in the improved inflationary scenario of
Linde 1987
and
Albrecht and Steinhardt 1987
was a finite-temperature version of the Coleman-Weinberg (CW) potential
Coleman and Weinberg 1973
at the one-loop order for the scalar field (here, the
\it{inflaton}
field) which arises from the quantum correction of the classical potential and which can induce symmetry breaking in a gauge field theory. Linde, in
Linde 1987
, made use of the CW mechanism in the Grand Unified Theory (GUT) scale and demonstrated its efficacy in solving the cosmological problems.

The new (slow-roll) inflationary scenario
Linde 1987
;
Albrecht and Steinhardt 1987
was later employed by Dolgov and Linde
Dolgov and Linde 1982
to study the baryon asymmetry generation, and by Linde to investigate the scalar field fluctuations
Linde 1982a
and the temperature dependence of the Higgs boson coupling constant
Linde 1982b
during inflation.

Later on, quite a number of inadequacies of the CW potential in the description of the inflationary scenario of the early universe have been pointed out: (i) The potential gives a large density fluctuations
Shafi and Vilenkin 1984
which is about
10^{5}
times too large
Pi 1984
in comparison with the predicted value. This large density fluctuation arises from large gauge coupling constant in the CW potential
Pi 1984
, (ii) The effective coupling constant
\lambda(\phi)
due to the quantum fluctuations in the scalar field
\phi
in the Coleman-Weinberg type GUT is large enough
Linde 1982a
to spoil the slow-roll condition by making the journey across the flat region of the potential too fast
Steinhardt and Turner 1984
, (iii) The flatness in the CW potential during the spontaneous breaking of the SU(5) symmetry may be affected for another reason
Steinhardt and Turner 1984
;
Breit et al. 1983
. Instead of just two vacua corresponding to SU(5) and SU(3)
\times
SU(2)
\times
U(1) symmetries, there may be intermediate metastable vacua due to other symmetries, such as SU(4)
\times
U(1).

The CW potential, having the above difficulties, belongs to the category of small-field models, characterized by
\Delta\phi<M_{Pl}

Baumann 2011
.

With the shortcomings of the CW slow-roll potential in the perspective, various other inflationary models have been proposed
Albrecht and Brandenberger 1985
;
Albrecht et al. 1985
;
Holman et al. 1984
.

By considering a model of one-loop-order CW potential, of a singlet complex scalar field, weakly coupled to Abelian gauge fields, Albrecht and Brandenberger
Albrecht and Brandenberger 1985
have shown that an inflation with sufficient length is realizable with a small value of the gauge coupling constant and a very large value of the symmetry breaking scale. A numerical study later on by Albrecht, Brandenberger and Matzner
Albrecht et al. 1985
confirmed the results of
Albrecht and Brandenberger 1985
.

In
Holman et al. 1984
, a substantially different cosmological inflationary scenario based on supersymmetry (SUSY) was proposed, in which inflation takes place after the GUT symmetry breaking and before the SUSY symmetry breaking. In this formalism, a satisfactory inflaton potential based on
\mathcal{N}=1
supergravity can be obtained which yields the desired density fluctuation of
\mathcal{O}(10^{-4})
despite a low reheat temperature.

In recent times, a new type of inflationary models has been proposed based on the concept of
‘cosmological attractors’

Remmen and Carroll 2013
;
Remmen and Carroll 2014
;
Linde 2017
;
Fumagalli 2017
;
Dubinin et al. 2018
. Conventionally, an attractor is a solution, a dynamical system shows in time starting from wide range of initial conditions. In the cosmological context, it has been shown
Remmen and Carroll 2013
that it is possible to obtain an (apparent) attractor behaviour in the inflationary scenario in an effective phase space of ‘non-canonical’ variables. On a broader note, the concept of ‘attractor’ and its universality were indicated by S.Ferrara and R.Kallosh in
Ferrara and
Kallosh 1996a
and
Ferrara and
Kallosh 1996b
, in the context of supersymmetric blackholes. Subsequently, the term ‘cosmological attractors’ was employed to denote a group or class of potentials to paraphrase their universal properties (detailed discussions in Sec.
II
)
Kallosh and
Linde 2013a
;
Kallosh and
Linde 2013b
;
Kallosh and
Linde 2013c
;
Kallosh
et al. 2014a
;
Ellis et al. 2013a
;
Buchmuller et al. 2013
;
Linde 2015
;
Carrasco et al. 2015
and they were studied to describe the early universe physics namely, initial conditions for inflation
Carrasco et al. 2015
and asymptotic properties of inflaton field
Kallosh and Linde 2016
etc. Cosmological attractors added a new dimension in the field of slow-roll inflationary models, which in turn opened a doorway to comprehend the genesis of our universe. Understandably, these attractor potentials have removed many of the drawbacks of the early slow-roll potentials.

The purpose of this paper is to present a detailed mode analysis of inflationary perturbations based on the
\alpha
-attractors vis-
\grave{a}
-vis the recent experimental data.

Our goal here is to study the microphysical aspects of quantum fluctuations during inflation as well as to discover the scale-independence of relevant cosmological parameters (such as
n_{s}
and
r
) in the evolution of the expanding universe.

In Sec.
II
, we highlight the main aspects of the
\alpha
-attractor models, relevent for our study.
In Sec.
III

In Sec.
IV

In Sec.
V

\displaystyle\delta\ddot{\phi}+3H\delta\dot{\phi}+\frac{{\partial}^{2}V(\phi^{(0)})}{\partial{\phi^{(0)}}^{2}}\delta\phi+\frac{{\vec{k}}^{2}}{a^{2}}\delta\phi

\displaystyle=\dot{\phi}^{(0)}\left[\dot{\Phi}_{B}+\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\left(\frac{\Phi_{B}}{H}\right)+\frac{{\vec{k}}^{2}}{a^{2}}\left(\frac{\Phi_{B}}{H}\right)\right]

\displaystyle-2\left[\Phi_{B}+\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\Phi_{B}}{H}\right)\right]\frac{\partial V(\phi^{(0)})}{\partial\phi^{(0)}}.

(69)

Eqs.(
68
) and (
69
) are respectively the evolution equations of the unperturbed inflaton field and the perturbation part of that field, both at the classical level. However, we should quantize the inflaton field with the idea that the spatially distributed quantum condensates are the seeds for the observed large scale structure of the universe which has developed through gravitational instabilities. Since we shall be working in the
\vec{k}
space of the quantum inflaton field in an expanding background, we may write Eqs. (
68
) and (
69
) in
\vec{k}
space, considering the linear independence of the modes in this space:

\ddot{\phi}^{(0)}(k,t)+3H\dot{\phi}^{(0)}(k,t)+\frac{\partial V(\phi^{(0)}(k,t))}{\partial\phi^{(0)}(k,t)}=0,

(70)

\displaystyle\delta\ddot{\phi}(k,t)+3H\delta\dot{\phi}(k,t)+\frac{{\partial}^{2}V(\phi^{(0)}(k,t))}{\partial{\phi^{(0)}(k,t)}^{2}}\delta\phi(k,t)+\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\delta\phi(k,t)

(71)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}^{(0)}(k,t)\left[(\dot{\Phi}_{B})(k,t)+\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\left(\frac{(\Phi_{B})(k,t)}{H}\right)+\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\left(\frac{(\Phi_{B})(k,t)}{H}\right)\right]

\displaystyle-2\left[(\Phi_{B})(k,t)+\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{(\Phi_{B})(k,t)}{H}\right)\right]\frac{\partial V(\phi^{(0)}(k,t))}{\partial\phi^{(0)}(k,t)}.

It should be noted that in Eqs.(
70
) and (
71
) we have dropped the three-vector notation on
k
as these two equations depend only on the magnitude of
\vec{k}
, as in the Mukhanov equation.
Baumann 2011
;
Mukhanov 2005

Quantization of the inflaton field:
Eqs. (
70
) and (
71
) are valid for the mode functions
\phi(k,t)
at the classical level.
They may belong to a quantum field when we quantize the inflaton field cannonically as,

\hat{\phi}(t,\vec{X})=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}[\phi(k,t)\hat{a}(\vec{k})e^{i\vec{k}.\vec{x}}+{{\phi}^{*}}(k,t){\hat{a}^{\dagger}}(\vec{k})e^{-i\vec{k}.\vec{x}}],

(72)

with the commutation of the annihilation and creation operators,

[\hat{a}(\vec{k}),{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}(\vec{k^{\prime}})]=(2\pi)^{3}\delta(\vec{k}-\vec{k^{\prime}}).

(73)

The canonical momentum field can easily be calculated upto the first-order in perturbation to have the form,

\pi_{\phi}=\dot{\phi}^{(0)}+\delta\dot{\phi}-2\Phi\dot{\phi}^{(0)}.

(74)

Here,
\phi(k,t)
are the mode functions and the Eqs.(
70
) and (
71
), describing their time evolutions are valid both in the classical and the quantum formulations. An operator mode function can be written as,

\hat{\phi}(\vec{k},t)=\phi(k,t)\hat{a}_{\vec{k}}+\phi^{*}(-k,t){\hat{a}}^{\dagger}_{-{\vec{k}}}.

(75)

The power spectrum:

A two-point correlation function in a quasi de-Sitter universe is introduced as the vacuum expectation value of a product of first-order perturbations in the quantum mode functions (Eq.(
75
)) and can be expressed as,

\left\langle\delta\phi(\vec{k},t)\delta\phi(\vec{k^{\prime}},t)\right\rangle=\frac{4\pi^{3}}{k^{3}}\delta(\vec{k}+\vec{k^{\prime}})H^{2}

(76)

where, on the right-hand-side the time dependence comes through the Hubble rate
H
.

In the spatially flat gauge, we can relate the curvature perturbation
\mathcal{R}
to the inflaton perturbation
\delta\phi
as,

\mathcal{R}=H\frac{\delta\phi}{\dot{\phi}}.

(77)

The quantum fluctuations in the inflaton field are microscopically induced in the curvature and one can write a curvature correlation function in terms of the inflaton field as,

\left\langle\mathcal{R}(\vec{k},t)\mathcal{R}(\vec{k^{\prime}},t)\right\rangle=\frac{H^{2}}{\dot{\phi}^{2}}\left\langle\delta\phi(\vec{k},t)\delta\phi(\vec{k^{\prime}},t)\right\rangle.

(78)

A dimensionless curvature power spectrum may be defined as,

\Delta_{\mathcal{R}}(t)=\frac{H^{4}}{(2\pi)^{2}{\dot{\phi}^{2}}},

(79)

with its relation to the curvature correlation function as,

\left\langle\mathcal{R}(\vec{k},t)\mathcal{R}(\vec{k^{\prime}},t)\right\rangle=\frac{16\pi^{5}}{k^{3}}\delta(\vec{k}+\vec{k^{\prime}})\Delta_{\mathcal{R}}(t).

(80)

Similarly, for tensor perturbation we can write a dimensionless power spectrum for two polarizations as,

\Delta_{h}(t)=\frac{2}{{M_{Pl}}^{2}}\left(\frac{H}{\pi}\right)^{2}.

(81)

Now, the time-dependence of the power spectra can be converted to
k
-dependence if we use the horizon-crossing condition,
k=aH
, whereby we include all the
k
modes crossing the shrinking horizon during the inflationary period at different time.

With this understanding, we write the two power spectra (scalar and tensor) (Eq.(
79
) and(
81
)) as,

\Delta_{s}(k)=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}{M_{Pl}}^{2}}{\left(\frac{H^{2}}{\epsilon}\right)}_{k=aH}

(82)

and

\Delta_{h}(k)=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}{M_{Pl}}^{2}}{\left(H^{2}\right)}_{k=aH}.

(83)

where, in Eq.(
82
), we have identified the curvature perturbation with scalar perturbation and used the relation,

{\dot{\phi}}^{2}=2{M^{2}_{Pl}}\epsilon H^{2},

(84)

where
\epsilon
is the slow-roll parameter and
M_{Pl}(=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi G}})
is the reduced Planck mass.

Henceforth, we shall do our calculations with
M_{Pl}=1
.

Mode equations in k-space:

-space. With this aim, we shall first change the

-derivatives in Eqs.(

-derivatives.

It may be noted here that the horizon-crossing condition,

k=aH=a\frac{\dot{a}}{a}=\dot{a}

(85)

will imply that each point in

-space will correspond to a different expansion rate. Likewise, each value of

\dot{k}=\ddot{a}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(p+\frac{\rho}{3}\right)a

(86)

will correspond to a different accleration rate.

Now, applying the slow-roll condition,

\dot{\phi}^{2}<<V(\phi),

(87)

we obtain from Eqs.(

\rho\approx V(\phi)

(88)

and

p\approx-V(\phi).

(89)

Thus from Eq.(

), we get,

\dot{k}=\frac{1}{3}aV(\phi)

(90)

Also, retaining upto the first-order term in the Taylor series expansion of

V(\phi)=V(\phi^{(0)})\left(1+\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi^{(0)}}\ln({V(\phi^{(0)}})\delta\phi\right).

(91)

The scale factor can be written in terms of
V(\phi)
as,

a=\frac{k}{H}=k\sqrt{\frac{3}{\rho}}=k\sqrt{3}{(V(\phi))}^{-\frac{1}{2}},

(92)

where, we have used,

H^{2}=\frac{\rho}{3}.

(93)

We, then, get from Eqs.(
87
-
93
),

\dot{k}=k\sqrt{\frac{V(\phi^{(0)})}{3}}\left(1+\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi^{(0)}}\ln\sqrt{V(\phi^{(0)})}\delta\phi\right)

(94)

and

H=\sqrt{\frac{V(\phi^{(0)})}{3}}\left(1+\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi^{(0)}}\ln\sqrt{V(\phi^{(0)})}\delta\phi\right).

(95)

Then, converting the
t
-derivatives into
k
-derivatives, denotiong the latter by primes and retaining terms linear in perturbation we get respectively from Eqs. (

) and (

):

\displaystyle k^{2}{\phi}^{\prime\prime(0)}+k^{2}G_{1}{{\phi}^{\prime(0)}}^{2}+4k{\phi}^{\prime(0)}

\displaystyle+k^{2}(G_{1}\delta\phi^{\prime}+G_{2}{\phi}^{\prime(0)}\delta\phi){\phi}^{\prime(0)}+6G_{1}(1-2G_{1}\delta\phi)=0

(96)

and

\displaystyle k^{2}\delta\phi^{\prime\prime}+(k^{2}G_{1}{\phi}^{\prime(0)}+4k)\delta\phi^{\prime}+[1+6(G_{2}+2G_{1}^{2})]\delta\phi

\displaystyle=\Phi_{B}^{\prime\prime}[k^{3}{\phi}^{\prime(0)}]+\Phi_{B}^{\prime}[2k^{2}{\phi}^{\prime(0)}-k^{3}G_{1}{{\phi}^{\prime(0)}}^{2}-12kG_{1}]

\displaystyle+\Phi_{B}[k{\phi}^{\prime(0)}-k^{2}{{\phi}^{\prime(0)}}^{2}G_{1}-k^{3}{\phi}^{\prime(0)}(G_{2}{{\phi}^{\prime(0)}}^{2}+G_{1}{\phi}^{\prime\prime(0)})

\displaystyle-12G_{1}+12k{G_{1}}^{2}{\phi}^{\prime(0)}]

(97)

where,

G_{n}=\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial\phi^{(0)n)}}\ln\sqrt{V(\phi^{(0)})},n=1,2,3

(98)

An evolution equation for the Bardeen potential can be derived from the perturbed Einstein’s equations under the spatially flat gauge and with no anistropic stress,

3H^{2}\Phi-k^{2}H\frac{B}{a}=-4\pi G\delta\rho,

(99)

H\Phi=-4\pi G\delta q,

(100)

H\dot{\Phi}+(3H^{2}+2\dot{H})\Phi=4\pi G\delta p,

(101)

(\partial_{t}+3H)\left(\frac{B}{a}\right)+\frac{\Phi}{a^{2}}=0.

Trading
\Phi
and
B
for the Bardeen potential

H\partial_{t}\left(\Phi_{B}+\frac{\delta\rho}{2H^{2}}\right)-3H^{2}\left(\Phi_{B}+\frac{\delta\rho}{2H^{2}}\right)=3\left(\delta\dot{q}+\frac{1}{2}\delta p\right).

Converting the

\displaystyle k^{4}{{\phi}^{\prime(0)}}^{2}{\Phi^{\prime\prime}}_{B}+(2k^{4}{\phi}^{\prime(0)}{\phi}^{\prime\prime(0)}-2k^{3}{{\phi}^{\prime(0)}}^{2}-k^{4}G_{1}{{\phi}^{\prime(0)}}^{3}-2k){\Phi^{\prime}}_{B}+(6+k^{3}(2-3kG_{1}{\phi^{\prime}}^{(0)}){\phi^{\prime\prime}}^{(0)}{\phi^{\prime}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle-k^{2}(k^{2}G_{2}{{\phi^{\prime}}^{(0)}}^{2}-3kG_{1}{\phi^{\prime}}^{(0)}+4){{\phi^{\prime}}^{(0)}}^{2})\Phi_{B}

\displaystyle=6k(k{\phi^{\prime\prime}}^{(0)}+kG_{1}{{\phi^{\prime}}^{(0)}}^{2}+{\phi^{\prime}}^{(0)})\delta\phi+k^{3}{\phi^{\prime}}^{(0)}\delta\phi^{\prime\prime}+k(k^{2}{\phi^{\prime\prime}}^{(0)}+2k{\phi^{\prime}}^{(0)}+6G_{1})\delta\phi^{\prime}+6kG_{2}{\phi^{\prime}}^{(0)}\delta\phi

(104)

N(\phi)=\int_{\phi_{\text{end}}}^{\phi}\frac{V(\phi)}{\frac{dV(\phi)}{d\phi}}d\phi.

(114)

) and (

V
Conclusions

In conclusion, we have studied inflationary perturbations with quantum fluctuations in the early universe within a single-inflaton model. Agreements of the obtained values of cosmological parameters viz.,
n_{s}
,

etc. with those of the experimental values reflect the fact that, the perturbations and fluctuations during inflation acted as seed in the evolution and formation of present universe whose signatures have remained in the background as cosmic gene. The potentials for

V(\phi)=V_{0}(1-e^{-\sqrt{2/3}\phi})^{n}.

(119)

\frac{dV(\phi)}{d\phi}=nV_{0}\sqrt{2/3}(e^{-\sqrt{2/3}\phi})(1-e^{-\sqrt{2/3}\phi})^{n-1}.

(120)

\frac{d^{2}V(\phi)}{d\phi^{2}}=\frac{2nV_{0}}{3}(1-e^{-\sqrt{2/3}\phi})^{n}(e^{\sqrt{2/3}\phi}-1)^{-2}(n-e^{\sqrt{2/3}\phi}).

(121)

The number of e-folds, then is

N(\phi)=\int_{\phi_{\text{end}}}^{\phi}\frac{V(\phi)}{(dV(\phi)/d\phi)}d\phi

=\frac{3}{2n}[e^{\sqrt{2/3}\phi}-e^{\sqrt{2/3}\phi_{\text{end}}}-\sqrt{2/3}(\phi-\phi_{\text{end}})].

(122)

First potential slow-roll parameter is

\epsilon_{V}(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{dV/d\phi}{V}\right)^{2}

=\frac{n^{2}}{3}(e^{\sqrt{2/3}\phi}-1)^{-2}.

(123)

Inflation ends when
\epsilon_{V}(\phi_{\text{end}})\approx 1
.

\sqrt{2/3}\phi_{\text{end}}\approx\ln(1+\frac{n}{\sqrt{3}}).

(124)

Appendix B
First and second potential slow-roll parameters

Using Eqs. (

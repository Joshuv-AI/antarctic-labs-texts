A note about charts built by Eriksson-Bique and Soultanis on metric measure spaces
Luca Gennaioli, Nicola Gigli

Abstract
. This note is motivated by recent studies by Eriksson-Bique and Soultanis about the construction of charts in general metric measure spaces. We analyze their construction and provide an alternative and simpler proof of the fact that these charts exist on sets of finite Hausdorff dimension. The observation made here offers also some simplification about the study of the relation between the reference measure and the charts in the setting of RCD spaces.



1.
Introduction

In the recent, very interesting, paper
[
EBS21
]
the authors provided a general construction of charts on metric measure spaces, key features of their notion being: the compatibility with Sobolev calculus (and thus in particular with the differential calculus as developed by Cheeger in
[
Cheeger00
]
and the second author
[
Gigli12
]
), a very general existence result, notable consequences in terms of the structure of the Sobolev spaces (see also
[
EBRS22
]
and
[
EBRS222
]
). An example in this latter direction is the proof that the space
W^{1,p}(\mathsf{X})
,
p\in(1,\infty)
, is reflexive as soon as the space
\mathsf{X}
can be covered by a countable number of sets with finite Hausdorff measure (the ‘previous best’ result appeared in
[
ACM14
]
and required the metric to be locally doubling).

A crucial step in
[
EBS21
]
is the proof that if
\varphi:E\subset\mathsf{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a ‘
p
-independent weak chart’, then
n
is bounded from above by the Hausdorff dimension of
E
. For the precise meaning of ‘
p
-independent weak chart’ we refer to
2.24
; for the purpose of this introduction we shall limit ourselves to point out that in the smooth setting this would be equivalent to requiring the image of the differential of
\varphi
at every point to span the whole tangent space of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Starting from this result, existence of actual charts is obtained via a suitable maximality argument.

Interestingly, this upper bound is proved via means that have, in principle, little to do with analysis in non-smooth setting: key ingredients are indeed the elliptic regularity result in
[
DPR
]
and the study of the structure of the set of non-differentiability points of Lipschitz functions in
[
AM16
]
.

This sort of procedure has a recent analogue in the theory of
RCD
spaces. Let us recall indeed that in
[
Mondino-Naber14
]
it has been proved that finite dimensional
RCD
spaces admit bi-Lipschitz charts covering almost all the space. In
[
Mondino-Naber14
]
no information about the behaviour of the reference measure w.r.t. these charts has been provided: this topic has been later studied in
[
MK16
]
,
[
DPMR16
]
,
[
GP162
]
where, relying in a way or another on
[
DPR
]
and
[
AM16
]
, it has been proved that
\varphi_{*}(\mathfrak{m}_{|E})\ll\mathcal{\mathscr{L}}^{n}
for a Mondino-Naber chart
\varphi:E\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Of particular interest for the kind of discussion we want to make here is the fact that in
[
GP162
]
only the results in
[
DPR
]
have been used, while in
[
MK16
]
also those in
[
AM16
]
were necessary. Comparing this with the results in
[
EBS21
]
it is natural to wonder whether the use of
[
AM16
]
is really crucial or can be avoided: this is the question motivating the present note. Of course, there is nothing wrong in using a well-established result in doing research, our study is simply motivated by the desire of better understanding the interesting construction done in
[
EBS21
]
. The result of our investigation is that
[
AM16
]
is not really needed and the line of thought presented here simplifies not only some of the steps done in
[
EBS21
]
, but also some of those in
[
GP162
]
: see Section
3
.

Another remark that we make, consequence of the studies in
[
EBS21
]
, is that the dimension of the (co)tangent module (in the sense of
[
Gigli12
]
) on a subset
E\subset\mathsf{X}
is bounded from above from the Hausdorff dimension of
E
, see Remark
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we shall recall the definition of Sobolev space following the approach in
[
AmbrosioGigliSavare11
]
.
We say that a triple
(\mathsf{X},\text{d},\mathfrak{m})
is a metric measure space if
(\mathsf{X},\text{d})
is a complete and separable metric measure space and
\mathfrak{m}
is a Radon measure which is finite on balls. For the rest of the paper
p,q
will be conjugate exponents, namely
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a probability measure
\pi
on
C([0,1];\mathsf{X})
is a
\emph{q-testplan}
if it is concentrated on
AC([0,1];\mathsf{X})
and the following two conditions are met:

(1)

\exists

C=C(\pi)>0
such that
e_{t\sharp}\pi\leq C\mathfrak{m}
, where
\mathfrak{m}
is the reference measure on
\mathsf{X}
and
e_{t}:C([0,1];\mathsf{X})\to\mathsf{X}
is the evaluation map
e_{t}(\gamma)=\gamma_{t}
.

(2)

The following quantity, called
\emph{kineticenergy}
, is finite

\text{K.E.}(\pi)=\int\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\pi(\gamma),

with
|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}(\gamma_{t+h},\gamma_{t})}{h}
is the metric derivative of the curve
\gamma
.

With this notion at hand we can introduce the Sobolev space
\text{W}^{1,p}(\mathsf{X},\,\mathrm{d},\mathfrak{m})
:

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a function
f:\mathsf{X}\to\mathbb{R}
belongs to the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\mathsf{X},\,\mathrm{d},\mathfrak{m})
if
f\in{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
and if

(2.1)

\int|f(\gamma_{1})-f(\gamma_{0})|\,\mathrm{d}\pi(\gamma)\leq\int\int_{0}^{1}G(\gamma_{t})|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\pi(\gamma)\quad\forall\pi\;\text{q-test\;plan},

with
G:\mathsf{X}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
being a Borel function belonging to
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
.

Remark 2.3
.

It is easy to see that the set of functions
G
satisfying
2.1
is a closed convex set, hence it admits an element of minimal norm: we will call such an element
\emph{p-weakuppergradient}
and we will denote it by
|Df|_{p}
. With a little bit of work it is possible to prove that the function
|Df|_{p}
is such that
|Df|_{p}\leq G

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. for every other
G
satisfying
2.1
.

We now switch our attention to the theory of
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
-normed
\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
-modules that the second author built in
[
Gigli14
]
: the following material can be found there, unless otherwise stated.

Definition 2.4

(
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
-normed module)
.

We say that a Banach space
(\mathcal{M},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathcal{M}})
is an
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
-normed
\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
-module if there exists a bilinear continuous map
\cdot:\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
which makes
\mathcal{M}
a module with unity over the ring of
\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
functions and another map
|\cdot|:\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
with nonnegative values such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\lVert|v|\rVert_{{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}

\displaystyle=\lVert v\rVert_{\mathcal{M}},

(2.3)

\displaystyle|f\cdot v|

\displaystyle=|f||v|\qquad\mathfrak{m}-\text{a.e.}

for all
v\in\mathcal{M}
,
f\in\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
. We call
\cdot
the multiplication and
|\cdot|
the
\emph{pointwisenorm}
.

Remark 2.5
.

Note that the pointwise norm is continuos thanks to the triangular inequality, in fact

\lVert|v|-|w|\rVert_{{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}\leq\lVert|v-w|\rVert_{{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}=\lVert v-w\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}.

Moreover with a little bit of abuse of notation we will write
fv
instead of
f\cdot v
and write
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
-normed module instead of
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
-normed
\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
-module.

A related interesting concept is the one of
\emph{localization}
of a module, indeed it is easy to see that the following object

\mathcal{M}_{|E}:=\{\chi_{E}v:\;v\in\mathcal{M}\}

is a submodule of
\mathcal{M}
and it clearly inherits the normed structure from
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 2.6

(
Local independence
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
-normed
\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
-module and
A\in\mathscr{B}(X)
with
\mathfrak{m}(A)>0
, we say that a family
v_{1},...,v_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
is independent on
A
if for every
f_{1},...,f_{n}\in\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})

(2.4)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}v_{i}=0\quad\mathfrak{m}-\text{a.e.}\;\text{on}\;A\implies f_{i}=0\quad\mathfrak{m}-\text{a.e.}\;\text{on}\;A\quad\forall i=1,...,n.

In the spirit of linear algebra we shall also define what is the
\emph{span}
of a set of vectors

Definition 2.7

(
Span
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
-normed
\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
-module,
V\subset\mathcal{M}
a subset and
A\in\mathscr{B}(X)
. We denote with
\text{Span}_{A}(V)
the closure in
\mathcal{M}
of the
\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
-linear combinations of elements of
V
.
Moreover we say that
\text{Span}_{A}(V)
is the space generated by
V
on
A
.

After this natural definition, the one of basis and of dimension for an
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
-normed
\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
arise naturally:

Definition 2.8
.

We say that a finite family
v_{1},...,v_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
is a basis on
A\in\mathscr{B}(X)
if it is independent on
A
and
\text{Span}_{A}\{v_{1},...,v_{n}\}=\mathcal{M}_{|A}
. If the above happens we say that the
\emph{localdimension}
of
\mathcal{M}
on
A
is
n
and in case
\mathcal{M}
has not dimension
k
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
we say that it has infinite dimension.

It can be proved that the notion of dimension is well-posed, namely if we have
v_{1},...,v_{n}
generating
\mathcal{M}
on a set
A
and
w_{1},...,w_{m}
are independent on
A
, then
n\geq m
. Ultimately this means that two different basis must have the same cardinality.

Building over these tools we have the following proposition:

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
-normed
\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
-module. Then there is a unique partition
\{E_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}}
of
\mathsf{X}
, up to
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. equality, such that:

(1)

for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{m}(E_{i})>0
,
\mathcal{M}
has dimension
i
on
E_{i}
,

(2)

for every
E\subset E_{\infty}
with
\mathfrak{m}(E)>0
,
\mathcal{M}
has infinite dimension on
E
.

We now introduce the notion of
\emph{pullbackmodule}
which, roughly speaking, is nothing but a module over a space
\mathsf{X}
obtained by pulling back
a module on another space
\mathsf{Y}
via a certain map.

Definition 2.10

(Pullback)
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\text{d}_{\mathsf{X}},\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}})
and
(\mathsf{Y},\text{d}_{\mathsf{Y}},\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}})
be metric measure spaces,
\varphi:\mathsf{X}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Y}
a map of bounded compression and
\mathcal{M}
and
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}})
-normed module. Then there exists a unique, up to unique isomorphism, couple
(\varphi^{*}\mathcal{M},\varphi^{*})
with
\varphi^{*}\mathcal{M}
being an
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}})
-normed module and
\varphi^{*}:\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\varphi^{*}\mathcal{M}
being a linear and continuous operator such that:

(1)

|\varphi^{*}v|=|v|\circ\varphi
holds
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}}
-a.e., for every
v\in\mathcal{M}
,

(2)

the set
\{\varphi^{*}v\ :\ v\in\mathcal{M}\}
generates
\varphi^{*}\mathcal{M}
as a module.

At this point one can try to understand what is the relation between the dimension of a module and the one of its pullback via the map
\varphi
and in order to do so we need to introduce a sort of
\emph{leftinverse}
of the pullback operator
\varphi^{*}
. To do so let us assume
\varphi_{\sharp}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}}=\mathfrak{m}_{Y}
to simplify the exposition.

For
f\in{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}})
nonnegative we put

(2.5)

{\rm Pr}_{\varphi}(f):=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\varphi_{\sharp}(f\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}})}{\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}}}

and in a natural way we set
{\rm Pr}_{\varphi}(f):={\rm Pr}_{\varphi}(f^{+})-{\rm Pr}_{\varphi}(f^{-})
for general
f\in{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}})
.

For the next proposition we need to recall the classical Disintegration theorem. The statement below is taken from
[
AmbrosioGigliSavare08
, Theorem 5.3.1]
, see also
[
Fremlin4
, Chapter 452]
and
[
Bog07
, Chapter 10.6]

Theorem 2.11

(Disintegration)
.

Let
\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y}
be complete and separable metric spaces,
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{X})
, let
\pi:\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{Y}
be a Borel map and let
\nu=\pi_{\sharp}\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{Y})
. Then there
exists a
\nu
-a.e. uniquely determined Borel family of probability measures
\{\mu_{y}\}_{y\in\mathsf{Y}}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{X})
such that

\mu_{x}(\mathsf{X}\setminus\pi^{-1}(\{y\}))=0
for
\nu
-a.e.
y\in\mathsf{X}
and

(2.6)

\int_{\mathsf{X}}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{\mathsf{Y}}\biggl(\int_{\pi^{-1}(\{y\})}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{y}\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y)

for every Borel map
f:\mathsf{X}\to[0,+\infty]
.

Remark 2.12
.

Two remarks are in order here: the first one is that the above theorem in
[
AmbrosioGigliSavare08
]
is stated for Radon separable metric space but in our setting it suffices to state it for complete and separable ones (which in particular are Radon), the second is that the result easily extends to any
f:\mathsf{X}\to\mathbb{R}
Borel provided for example that
f\in L^{1}(\mu)
.

We now recall some properties of the map
\Pr_{\varphi}
.

Proposition 2.13
.

The operator
{\rm Pr}_{\varphi}:{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}})\longrightarrow{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m}_{Y})
is linear, continuous and

(2.7)

{\rm Pr}_{\varphi}(f)(y)=\int_{\mathsf{X}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{y}(x)\quad\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}}-\text{a.e.},\quad\forall f\in{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}}),

where
y\mapsto m_{y}
denotes the disintegration of
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}}
with respect to the map
\varphi
. Finally it holds

(2.8)

|{\rm Pr}_{\varphi}(f)|\leq{\rm Pr}_{\varphi}(|f|)\quad\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}}-\text{a.e.}

Proof.

Linearity is a consequence of the linearity of the integral. Formula (
2.8
) is also trivial while for (
2.7
) we have for any
A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathsf{Y})

\int_{A}{\rm Pr}_{\varphi}(f)(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}}=\int_{A}\,\mathrm{d}\varphi_{\sharp}(f\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}})=\int_{\varphi^{-1}(A)}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}},

and by the properties of the disintegration we have

\int_{\varphi^{-1}(A)}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}}=\int_{\mathsf{Y}}\int_{\varphi^{-1}(A)}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{y}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)=\int_{A}\int_{\mathsf{X}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{y}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}}(y),

therefore proving (
2.7
).

To prove continuity note that the case
p=\infty
is due to formula (
2.8
) while continuity in
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
follows from the following

\int_{\mathsf{Y}}|{\rm Pr}_{\varphi}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}}=\int_{\mathsf{Y}}\biggl|\int_{\mathsf{X}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{y}(x)\biggr|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)\leq\int_{\mathsf{Y}}\int_{\mathsf{X}}|f(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{y}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}}(y)=\lVert f\rVert^{p}_{{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})},

where we used Jensen’s inequality and the properties of the disintegration.
∎

In the case of a general
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}})
-normed module the continuous operator
{\rm Pr}_{\varphi}:\varphi^{*}\mathcal{M}:\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}
can be characterized by the following properties:

(2.9)

\displaystyle g{\rm Pr}_{\varphi}(v)={\rm Pr}_{\varphi}(g\circ\varphi v),\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{M}\quad\forall g\in\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}})

(2.10)

\displaystyle{\rm Pr}_{\varphi}(g\varphi^{*}v)={\rm Pr}_{\varphi}(g)v\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{M}\quad\forall g\in\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}}),

with the bound
|{\rm Pr}_{\varphi}(V)|\leq{\rm Pr}_{\varphi}(|V|)
still holding
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}}
-a.e. for every
V\in\varphi^{*}\mathcal{M}
.

With these objects we are now able to describe the structure of the pullback module, in particular (as one can expect by reasoning via pre-composition) the pullback of an
n
-dimensional module
\mathcal{M}
over

is an
n
-dimensional module over
\varphi^{-1}(E)
(see also
[

]
).

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}})
-normed module over the m.m.s.
(\mathsf{Y},d_{\mathsf{Y}},\mu)
and let
E\in\mathcal{B}(Y)
be a Borel set where
\mathcal{M}
has dimension
n
, with
\{v_{1},...,v_{n}\}
being a basis. Let
(\mathsf{X},d_{\mathsf{X}},\mathfrak{m})
be another m.m.s. and
\varphi:\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{Y}
be a map of bounded compression such that
\varphi_{\sharp}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}}=\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}}
, then
\{\varphi^{*}v_{1},...,\varphi^{*}v_{n}\}
is a basis of
\varphi^{*}\mathcal{M}
over
\varphi^{-1}(E)
.

Proof.

We first prove that
\{\varphi^{*}v_{1},...,\varphi^{*}v_{n}\}
generate
\varphi^{*}\mathcal{M}
over
\varphi^{-1}(E)
.

First recall that
\varphi^{*}\mathcal{M}
is generated (as module) by
\{\varphi^{*}v:v\in\mathcal{M}\}=:V
. Let us show that
V\subseteq\text{Span}_{\varphi^{-1}(E)}\{\varphi^{*}v_{1},...,\varphi^{*}v_{n}\}
: pick
w\in V
, then there exists
v\in\mathcal{M}
such that
w=\varphi^{*}v
so that there exists
(A_{j})_{j}\subseteq\mathscr{B}(\mathsf{X})
partition of
E
and
(g_{i}^{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}})

\forall i=1,...,n
such that

\chi_{A_{j}}v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}^{j}v_{i}\qquad\forall j\in\mathbb{N}

Using the linearity of the pullback map and the fact that
\varphi^{*}(gv)=g\circ\varphi\varphi^{*}v
for all
v\in\mathcal{M}
,
g\in\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}})
we get

\chi_{\varphi^{-1}(A_{j})}w=\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}^{j}\circ\varphi\varphi^{*}v_{i}.

Finally, since the pullback module has a natural structure of
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
-normed
\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
-module, we get that
\text{Span}_{\varphi^{-1}(E)}\{\varphi^{*}v_{1},...,\varphi^{*}v_{n}\}
is closed, proving the first result.

We now turn to local independence: assume by contradiction
\{\varphi^{*}v_{1},...,\varphi^{*}v_{n}\}
are not independent on
\varphi^{-1}(E)
then there exist
f_{1},...,f_{n}\in\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}})
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\varphi^{*}v_{i}=0

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. with (upon relabeling indexes)
|f_{1}|>0

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. on some subset
\tilde{E}
of positive measure. Without loss of generality, possibly considering a smaller set, we shall assume
f_{1}>0

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. so that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\varphi^{*}v_{i}=0\quad\mathfrak{m}-\text{a.e. on}\;\tilde{E}\implies\sum_{i=1}^{n}\text{Pr}_{\varphi}(f_{i})v_{i}=0\quad\mathfrak{m}-\text{a.e. on}\;\tilde{E}.

However note that
\text{Pr}_{\varphi}(f_{1})>0
on some set of positive
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}}
measure, contradicting the independence of the
v_{i}
s.

∎

Remark 2.15
.

We stress again that the assumption
\varphi_{\sharp}\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{X}}=\mathfrak{m}_{\mathsf{Y}}
is purely for the sake of exposition.

Definition 2.16
.

We say that the space of
\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
-linear and continuous maps

Remark 2.17
.

Being
\mathcal{M}

{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
-normed, we can endow

We are now in position to speak about the differential of a Sobolev function as the following proposition shows.

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\,\mathrm{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space, then there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) couple
({\rm L}^{p}({\rm T}^{*}\mathsf{X}),\,\mathrm{d}_{p})
where
{\rm L}^{p}({\rm T}^{*}\mathsf{X})
is an
{\rm L}^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
-normed
\rm{L}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
-module and
\,\mathrm{d}_{p}:W^{1,p}(\mathsf{X})\to{\rm L}^{p}({\rm T}^{*}\mathsf{X})
is a linear and continuous operator such that:

(1)

|\,\mathrm{d}_{p}f|=|Df|_{p}

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. for every
f\in W^{1,p}(\mathsf{X})
,

(2)

The set
\{\,\mathrm{d}f:\;f\in W^{1,p}(\mathsf{X})\}
generates
{\rm L}^{p}({\rm T}^{*}\mathsf{X})
.

Remark 2.19
.

We will call
1-forms
the elements of
{\rm L}^{p}({\rm T}^{*}\mathsf{X})
, in analogy with the section of the cotangent bundle on a Riemannian manifold.

Definition 2.20
.

We denote with
{\rm L}^{q}({\rm T}\mathsf{X})
the dual module of
{\rm L}^{p}({\rm T}^{*}\mathsf{X})
and we call its elements vector fields or vectors.

Besides the differential of a Sobolev function introduced in
2.18
, one can give another definition which exploits the fact that the map is Lipschitz and of bounded compression: this class of maps is that of
\emph{boundeddeformation}
. In this direction we need to recall the notion of
\emph{pullbackofforms}
: in order to distinguish it from the pullback of a module we shall proceed denoting with
\omega\mapsto[\varphi^{*}\omega]
the pullback map and with
\varphi^{*}
the pullback of 1-forms which is the following:

Definition 2.21
.

Let
\varphi:\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{Y}
be a map of bounded deformation, then we define
\varphi^{*}:{\rm L}^{p}({\rm T}^{*}\mathsf{Y})\to{\rm L}^{p}({\rm T}^{*}\mathsf{X})
to be the linear map such that

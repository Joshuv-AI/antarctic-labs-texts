Rigorous thin film approximations of the one-phase unstable Muskat problem
Edoardo Bocchi and Francisco Gancedo

Abstract
. This paper studies the one-phase Muskat problem driven by gravity and surface tension. The regime considered here is unstable with the fluid on top of a dry region. By a novel approach using a depth-averaged formulation, we derive two asymptotic approximations for this scenario. The lower order approximation is the classical thin film equation, while the higher order approximation provides a new refined thin film equation. We prove the optimal order of convergence in the shallowness parameter to the original Muskat solutions for both models with low-regular initial data.



1.
introduction

We study in this paper the one-phase Muskat problem, which describes the motion of an incompressible viscous fluid in a porous medium moving by gravity and surface tension.
This common physical phenomenon is classically modeled through the Darcy’s law
[
19
]
which states that forces acting on the fluid equal the flow velocity:

(1.1)

\frac{\nu}{k}u=-\nabla p-g\rho\mathbf{(}0,1).

Here
u
is the fluid velocity,

is the fluid pressure,
\nu>0
is the dynamic viscosity and
\rho>0
is the density. The constants

and

are the permeability of medium and gravity, respectively. This law was first used by Muskat in
[
42
]
two deal with the dynamics of immiscible fluids in porous media. It arises in hydrogeology, in particular in the study of groundwater flows through aquifers.
This scenario has been widely studied, in particular for the mathematical analogies with the free boundary system given by a fluid in a vertical Hele-Shaw cell
[
45
]
.
It governs the motion of a viscous fluid confined between two vertical parallel flat plates in proximity to each other. The equivalence between the two problems holds by identifying the fixed distance between the plates in a Hele-Shaw cell to the the constant permeability of the homogeneous medium in the one-phase Muskat problem.

In this paper, we consider the unstable configuration in which an incompressible, homogeneous and viscous fluid lies over the dry region.

Physical setting

The one-phase unstable Muskat problem is described by the following system:

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\frac{\nu}{k}u=-\nabla p-g\rho\mathbf{e}_{z}\qquad&\mbox{in}\quad\widetilde{\Omega}(t)\\
&\nabla\cdot u=0\qquad&\mbox{in}\quad\widetilde{\Omega}(t)\\
&\partial_{t}(-\zeta)=u\cdot N_{-\zeta}\qquad&\mbox{on}\quad\widetilde{\Gamma}(t)\\
&p=\gamma\kappa_{-\zeta}\qquad&\mbox{on}\quad\widetilde{\Gamma}(t)\\
&u\cdot\mathbf{e}_{z}=0\qquad&\mbox{on}\quad\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\rm top}\end{aligned}\end{cases}

where
\mathbf{e}_{z}=(0,1)
is the unit vector parallel to the vertical axis,
N_{-\zeta}=(\partial_{x}(-\zeta),-1)^{T}
is the outgoing normal vector to
\widetilde{\Gamma}(t)
,
\gamma
is the surface tension coefficient and
\kappa_{-\zeta}
is the mean surface curvature defined by

\kappa_{-\zeta}=\frac{\partial_{xx}(-\zeta)}{(1+(\partial_{x}(-\zeta))^{2})^{3/2}}.

The boundary condition for the pressure at the surface in (
1.2
) is given by the Young-Laplace law for the pressure drop at the interface between two immiscible fluids considering the exterior atmospheric pressure equals to zero for simplicity.

The configuration of the one-phase unstable Muskat problem naturally appears in hydrology, in particular when dealing with groundwater. Indeed, below the soil there are additional layers that contain water plus air in the pores. At sufficient depth, one can find a capillarity zone, where water is drawn upward by surface forces and this is in the form of a film around the soil grains. Hence, capillarity effects play a key role in the stabilization of the Rayleigh-Taylor instability (see below) due to the unstable configuration of a thin film fluid layer in the capillarity zone of the soil. This motivates our goals in this paper: derivation of lubrication approximations in the unstable case and the study of the influence of the surface tension on the thin film asymptotic models.

In the mathematical literature, this scenario has attracted a lot of attention, in particular because it is a classical free boundary problem with wide applicability

Viscous thin fluid films and lubrication approximations have been widely studied in fluid dynamics in the last three decades. The most studied asymptotic model is the classical thin film equation, a fourth-order degenerate parabolic equation. Both the existence of global weak solutions

The equations for the motion of two thin fluid films in a porous medium were derived from the three-phase Muskat problem

In the case of purely gravity-driven motion, the local existence of classical solutions in the Sobolev space

While the literature of the existence theory is wide and well-known, the rigorous justification of the lubrication approximations has not been deeply investigated. Indeed, to the best of our knowledge, the unique study dealing with this aspect is
[

1.2.
Main results

The aim of this work is twofold. First, we derive lubrication approximations for the one-phase Muskat problem in a thin film regime in the unstable case, where the fluid lies over a dry region, taking into account the gravitational effects. To do that, exploiting the incompressibility condition, we employ a depth-averaged formulation used in the derivation of asymptotic models of water waves
[
37
,
10
]
to write the evolution equation for the free surface. This formulation permits to derive different explicit thin film asymptotic models with respect to the shallowness parameter
\mu
in a direct manner after having introduced only one ansatz for the velocity potential. In the regime
\tfrac{1}{\rm Bo}=O(1)
and at approximation
O(\mu)
, denoting by
\zeta
the free surface, we recover the gravity-driven thin film equation

(1.3)

\partial_{t}\zeta+\partial_{x}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)(\partial_{x}\zeta+\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{Bo}}\partial_{xxx}\zeta)\right)=0\\

where
\varepsilon
is the nonlinearity parameter and
\rm Bo
is the Bond number describing the ratio between gravitational and surface forces (see Section
2.1
for the detailed definitions). Furthermore, in the different regime
\tfrac{1}{\rm Bo}=\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}=O(\sqrt{\mu})
and at approximation
O(\mu^{3/2})
we obtain a refined gravity-driven thin film equation

(1.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\zeta+\partial_{x}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)(\partial_{x}\zeta+\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}\partial_{xxx}\zeta)\right)+\tfrac{\mu}{3}\partial_{xx}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)^{3}\partial_{xx}\zeta\right)=0,

which, to the best of our knowledge, appears for the first time in the thin film literature.
Secondly, our goal is to rigorously justify the derived thin film asymptotic models, showing that their solutions are good approximations of the solution to the full one-phase unstable Muskat problem, whose existence has been recently obtained
[
31
]
. To do that, we derive elliptic estimates for the velocity potential adapting the ones used in
[
31
]
. We use them to get, respectively, in Theorem
4
and Theorem
5
, error estimates for both the gravity-driven thin film and the refined gravity-driven thin film equations. Namely, we obtain for
\mathrm{Bo}<1
and
\mu\in(0,1)

\|\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}-\zeta^{\rm app}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];\ \mathbb{A}^{0}_{\nu t})}+\tfrac{1}{64}\left(\tfrac{1}{\rm Bo}-1\right)\|\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}-\zeta^{\rm app}\|_{L^{1}([0,T];\ \mathbb{A}^{4}_{\nu t})}\leq C\mu\\

and for
\mathrm{bo}
small enough and
\mu\in[\mu_{0},1)

\|\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}-\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];\ \mathbb{A}^{0}_{\nu t})}+\ \tfrac{1}{64}\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1\right)\|\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}-\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}\|_{L^{1}([0,T];\ \mathbb{A}^{4}_{\nu t})}\leq C\mu^{3/2},\\

where
\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}
is solution to the full one-phase unstable Muskat problem obtained in
[
31
]
and
\zeta^{\rm app}
,
\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}
are solutions to (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) respectively.
One of the novelties with respect to
[
41
]
is that we obtain asymptotic stability in low regularity Wiener space
\mathbb{A}^{0}_{\nu t}
(see Section
3
for the definition). Moreover, no assumptions are made on the approximated surface profiles
\zeta^{\rm app}
and
\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}.
The unstable configuration is responsible for the lower bound
\mu_{0}
of the parameter
\mu
in the convergence result. Contrarily, in the stable case the sign of the gravity term permits to remove that condition and to take arbitrarily small values of
\mu
. More precisely, we derive the refined thin film equation in the stable case

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\zeta+\partial_{x}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)(-\partial_{x}\zeta+\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}\partial_{xxx}\zeta)\right)-\tfrac{\mu}{3}\partial_{xx}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)^{3}\partial_{xx}\zeta\right)=0

and, denoting now by
\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}
a solution to (
1.5
), we obtain for
\mathrm{bo}>0
and
\mu\in(0,\mu_{1})
the error estimate

\|\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}-\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];\ \mathbb{A}^{0}_{\nu t})}+\ \tfrac{1}{64}\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}-\tfrac{\mu}{3}\right)\|\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}-\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}\|_{L^{1}([0,T];\ \mathbb{A}^{4}_{\nu t})}\leq C\mu^{3/2}.\\

Finally, we point out that we investigate the most difficult unstable case and the most complete gravity-capillarity driven configuration. However, the analysis carried out in this paper, used for the derivation of asymptotic models and their rigorous justification, holds also in the purely capillarity-driven motion (
g=0
).
Indeed, in the purely capillarity-driven scenario, one only needs to change the definition of the Bond number
\rm{Bo}
and repeat the computations to obtain the same results (see Remark
2.2
). This configuration in the absence of gravity is the same of the well-known Hele-Shaw interface problem
[
45
]
. Since the new term in the refined model is due to the presence of gravity, it turns out that under both orders of approximation
O(\mu)
and
O(\mu^{3/2})
we obtain the standard thin film equation

\partial_{t}\zeta+\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{Bo}}\partial_{x}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)\partial_{xxx}\zeta\right)=0

whether
\tfrac{1}{\rm Bo}=O(1)
or
O(\sqrt{\mu})
.

2.1.
Dimensionless one-phase unstable Muskat problem

Let us introduce now three physical parameters intrinsic to the problem:

-

The characteristic horizontal scale
L

-

The characteristic fluid height
H

-

The characteristic free surface amplitude
a

These three parameters permit to define other three quantities whose size describes the properties of the fluid motion. They are the
shallowness parameter

\mu
, the
nonlinearity parameter

\varepsilon
and the
Bond number

\rm Bo
, defined respectively by

(2.4)

\mu=\frac{H^{2}}{L^{2}},\qquad\varepsilon=\frac{a}{H},\qquad\mathrm{Bo}=\frac{g\rho L^{2}}{\gamma}.

Notice that the Bond number describes the ratio of gravitational to capillarity forces. We have that
\mu>0
,
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
\rm Bo>0.

Remark 2.1
.

As a first approach we consider the case when
the typical horizontal and vertical characteristic scales of the problem are such that

\frac{1}{\rm Bo}=O(1).

This ceases to hold if the configuration studied is different and involves larger or smaller scales. For instance, the scenario changes in the study of water waves with surface tension, where it is reasonable to assume that (see
[
36
]
)

\frac{1}{\rm Bo}=O(\mu).

The different modeling with respect to water waves is relevant because it will allow us to keep the influence of the surface tension when we derive the asymptotic model at order
O(\mu)
. The presence of the surface tension will be crucial to the existence of the solution to the derived thin-film equation, which would be ill-posed in the presence of only gravity due to the unstable configuration we are dealing with.

We then introduce new dimensionless variables

(2.5)

x^{\prime}=\frac{x}{L},\quad z^{\prime}=\frac{z}{H},\quad t^{\prime}=\frac{t}{t_{0}},\quad\mbox{with}\mbox{\quad}t_{0}=\frac{\nu L^{2}}{kg\rho H},

and dimensionless unknowns

(2.6)

\zeta^{\prime}=\frac{\zeta}{a},\qquad\Phi^{\prime}=\frac{\Phi}{\Phi_{0}},\quad\mbox{with}\mbox{\quad}\Phi_{0}=\frac{kg\rho H}{\nu}.

After omitting the primes and defining the dimensionless domains

\displaystyle\Omega^{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle=\{(x,z)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\ |\ -1<z<\varepsilon\zeta(t,x)\},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle=\{(x,\varepsilon\zeta(t,x))\ |\ x\in\mathbb{T}\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm bot}

\displaystyle=\{(x,-1)\ |\ x\in\mathbb{T}\},

we write the dimensionless one-dimensional one-phase unstable Muskat problem :

(2.7)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\zeta+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}\left(\int_{-1}^{\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{x}\Phi\right)=0\quad&\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{T}\ \ \ \ \ \\
&\Delta^{\mu}\Phi=0&\mbox{in}\quad\Omega^{\varepsilon}(t)\\

and the dimensionless surface curvature

\kappa^{\varepsilon,\mu}_{\zeta}=\frac{\partial_{xx}\zeta}{(1+(\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2})^{3/2}}.

3.
Functional framework

In this section we present the functional framework in which we derive our results for both the thin film equations and the asymptotic stability. Moreover, we show basic properties that we will repeatedly use later on.

Before introducing the functional spaces we are working with, we list some notations that are used throughout the paper.
We consider the torus
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
that can be thought as the interval
[-\pi,\pi]
with periodic boundary conditions. Given a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of
f
is given by

\widehat{f}(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x)e^{-inx}dx,

and if
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
it can be written as a Fourier series, that it

f(x)=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{f}(n)e^{inx}.

Finally, we denote by
\mathcal{S}
the horizontally periodic strip
\mathbb{T}\times(-1,0).

3.1.
Wiener spaces on the torus

Let us introduce the Wiener space
\mathbb{A}^{s}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{T})
with
s,\lambda\geq 0
. It is defined as the space of functions
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
such that the following norm is finite:

|f|_{s,\lambda}:=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|n|)^{s}e^{\lambda|n|}|\widehat{f}(n)|.

Notice that the presence of the exponential weight in the norm
|f|_{s,\lambda}
implies that, for
\lambda>0
, the Wiener space
\mathbb{A}^{s}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{T})
contains real analytic functions. It is immediate that

\mathbb{A}^{s_{1}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{T})\subset\mathbb{A}^{s_{2}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{T})\quad\mbox{for}\quad s_{1}\geq s_{2}\geq 0.

Furthermore, for zero-mean functions the inhomogeneous norm
|f|_{s,\lambda}
is equivalent to the homogeneous norm. Indeed, since
\widehat{f}(0)=0
, one has

(3.1)

\tfrac{1}{2^{s}}|f|_{s,\lambda}\leq\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|n|^{s}e^{\lambda|n|}|\widehat{f}(n)|\leq|f|_{s,\lambda}.

For the sake of simplicity, we write
\mathbb{A}^{s}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{A}^{s}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{T}).

We show in the next proposition that Wiener spaces are algebras for
s\geq 0
. Although it has been already presented in
[
11
,
31
]
, we give the detailed proof since we obtain a sharper constant in the product estimate.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f,g\in\mathbb{A}^{[}s]_{\lambda}
with
s,\lambda\geq 0.
Then,

(3.2)

|fg|_{s,\lambda}\leq K_{s}\left(|f|_{0,\lambda}|g|_{s,\lambda}+|f|_{s,\lambda}|g|_{0,\lambda}\right)

with

K_{s}=\begin{cases}1\quad\mbox{ for}\quad 0<s\leq 1,\\
2^{s-1}\quad\mbox{for}\quad s=0,s>1.\end{cases}

Proof.

From the discrete version of the convolution theorem for the Fourier transform we have that

\displaystyle|fg|_{s,\lambda}=\sum\limits_{n}(1+|n|)^{s}e^{\lambda|n|}|\widehat{fg}(n)|=\sum\limits_{n}(1+|n|)^{s}e^{\lambda|n|}|\widehat{f}\ast\widehat{g}(n)|

and (
3.2
) directly follows for
s=0
using Young’s convolution inequality.

Let us now consider the case
s\neq 0
.
On the one hand, taking advantage of the convexity of the function
(1+x)^{s}
for
x\geq 0
and
s>1
, it yields

\displaystyle(1+|n|)^{s}\leq(1+\frac{2|n-m|}{2}+\frac{2|m|}{2})^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left[(1+2|n-m|)^{s}+(1+2|m|)^{s}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{s-1}\left[(1+|n-m|)^{s}+(1+|m|)^{s}\right].

On the other hand, for
s\in(0,1]
we use the following inequality

(1+\alpha)^{s}\leq 1+\alpha^{s}\qquad\forall\alpha\geq 0

to derive that

(3.3)

(x+y)^{s}\leq x^{s}+y^{s}\qquad\forall x,y\geq 0.

Then, from the monotonicity of the function
(1+x)^{s}
for positive
s
and applying (
3.3
) with
x=1+|n-m|
with

, we obtain for
s\in(0,1]

(1+|n|)^{s}\leq(1+|n-m|+1+|m|)^{s}\leq(1+|n-m|)^{s}+(1+|m|)^{s}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n}(1+|n|)^{s}e^{\lambda|n|}|\widehat{f}\ast\widehat{g}(n)|\leq\sum\limits_{n,m}(1+|n|)^{s}e^{\lambda|n|}|\widehat{f}(n-m)||\widehat{g}(m)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{n,m}K_{s}\left[(1+|n-m|)^{s}+(1+|m|)^{s}\right]e^{\lambda|n-m|}|\widehat{f}(n-m)|e^{\lambda|m|}|\widehat{g}(m)|

and (
3.2
) follows after using Young’s convolution inequality.
∎

4.
Lubrication approximations

In this section we consider lubrication approximations of the dimensionless one-phase Muskat problem, which are widely used in applications when a particular regime occurs. This is the so-called
thin film
regime, where the fluid height is small compared to the horizontal length scale of the problem. With our notation, this means that

\sqrt{\mu}=\frac{H}{L}\ll 1.

We will consider different approximations of the problem in the thin-film regime, truncating the equations at different orders.

Let us write the flattened one-phase Muskat problem (
2.8
)-(
2.9
) in a different way.
From
[
36
]
, we know that the elliptic operator
\nabla^{\mu}\cdot P(\Sigma)\nabla^{\mu}
can be written as the sum of two differential operators, the first one independent of
\mu
and the second one dependent of
\mu
, namely

\nabla^{\mu}\cdot P(\Sigma)\nabla^{\mu}=\tfrac{1}{1+\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{zz}+\mu A_{\sigma}(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})

with

A_{\sigma}(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})\cdot=\partial_{x}((1+\varepsilon\zeta)\partial_{x}\cdot)-\partial_{z}(\partial_{x}\sigma\partial_{x}\cdot)+\partial_{z}\left(\frac{(\partial_{x}\sigma)^{2}}{1+\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{z}\cdot\right)-\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}\sigma\partial_{z}\cdot).

Secondly, we write the dimensionless surface curvature as the sum of its linearized part and the remainder, that is

(4.1)

\kappa^{\varepsilon,\mu}_{\zeta}=\partial_{xx}\zeta+F_{\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}}(\partial_{x}\zeta)\partial_{xx}\zeta,

where
F_{\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}}(\partial_{x}\zeta)=F(\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}\partial_{x}\zeta)
with

F(x)=\dfrac{1}{(1+x^{2})^{3/2}}-1.

From the Taylor development of
F
at
x=0
, we observe that
F_{\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}}(\partial_{x}\zeta)
is
O(\mu)
.

We introduce now the following ansatz: we look for the potential
\phi
as an asymptotic expansion in terms of
\sqrt{\mu}
,
i.e.

(4.2)

\phi^{\mu}=\phi^{0}+\sqrt{\mu}\phi^{1/2}+\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}

with
\phi^{0}
satisfying

(4.3)

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{zz}\phi^{0}=0&\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{S},\\[5.0pt]
\phi^{0}=\varepsilon(\zeta+\tfrac{1}{\rm Bo}\partial_{xx}\zeta)&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{0\},\\[5.0pt]
\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{z}\phi^{0}=0&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{-1\},\end{cases}

\phi^{1/2}
satisfying

(4.4)

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{zz}\phi^{1/2}=0&\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{S},\\[5.0pt]
\phi^{1/2}=0&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{0\},\\[5.0pt]
\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{z}\phi^{1/2}=0&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{-1\}.\end{cases}

and the remainder potential
\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}
satisfying

(4.5)

\begin{cases}\nabla^{\mu}\cdot P(\Sigma)\nabla^{\mu}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}=-\mu A_{\sigma}(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})(\phi^{0}+\sqrt{\mu}\phi^{1/2})&\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{S},\\[5.0pt]
\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}=\frac{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{Bo}}F_{\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}}(\partial_{x}\zeta)\partial_{xx}\zeta&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{0\},\\[5.0pt]
\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{z}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}=0&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{-1\}.\end{cases}

It is straightforward that systems (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) admit respectively the unique explicit solutions

(4.6)

\displaystyle\phi^{0}(x,z)=\phi^{0}(x)=\varepsilon(\zeta+\tfrac{1}{\rm{Bo}}\partial_{xx}\zeta)

and

(4.7)

\displaystyle\phi^{1/2}(x,z)\equiv 0.

Let us now choose the diffeomorphism
\Sigma
such that
\Sigma^{-1}
flattens the fluid domain
\Omega^{\varepsilon}(t)
into the horizontal strip
\mathcal{S}
. We consider
\Sigma(x,z)=(x,z+\sigma(x,z))
with

(4.8)

\sigma(x,z)=\varepsilon\zeta(x)(z+1).

Notice that (
4.8
) is not the unique possible choice for
\sigma
. For instance, one can define
\sigma
as the harmonic extension of the free surface
\zeta
(as done in
[
31
]
for small perturbations of flat interfaces) or as a pseudo-differential vertical localization (as presented in
[
36
]
). We will see that, although with our simple choice of
\sigma
the diffeomorphism
\Sigma
is not
regularizing
(in the sense of
[
36
]
), we get the same control for
\nabla\sigma
in the Wiener-Sobolev space
\mathcal{A}^{s,0}_{\lambda}
as in
[
31
]
in terms of regularity of the free surface
\zeta.
With this choice,
A_{\sigma}(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})
reduces to

\displaystyle A_{\zeta}(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})\cdot=

\displaystyle\partial_{x}((1+\varepsilon\zeta)\partial_{x}\cdot)-\partial_{z}(\varepsilon(z+1)\partial_{x}\zeta\partial_{x}\cdot)

\displaystyle+\partial_{z}\left(\frac{(\varepsilon(z+1)\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2}}{1+\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{z}\cdot\right)-\partial_{x}(\varepsilon(z+1)\partial_{x}\zeta\partial_{z}\cdot).

In particular, when
A_{\zeta}(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})
is applied to a function independent of the vertical variable
z
it reads
A_{\zeta}(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})=(1+\varepsilon\zeta)\partial_{xx}
.

Therefore, after injecting (
4.8
), (
4.2
), (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) into (
2.8
), the one-dimensional one-phase unstable Muskat problem reduces to the evolution equation

(4.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\zeta+

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)\left(\partial_{x}\zeta+\tfrac{1}{\rm{Bo}}\partial_{xxx}\zeta\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}\left(\int_{-1}^{0}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)\partial_{x}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}-\varepsilon(z+1)\partial_{x}\zeta\partial_{z}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}\right)dz\right)

with
\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}
satisfying the elliptic problem (
4.5
).

4.1.
Approximation at order
O(\mu)

Let us truncate (
4.9
) by dropping the terms of order
O(\mu)
. Therefore, we obtain the following non-degenerate fourth-order parabolic equation

(4.10)

\partial_{t}\zeta+\partial_{x}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)(\partial_{x}\zeta+\tfrac{1}{\rm{Bo}}\partial_{xxx}\zeta)\right)=0.\vskip 10.00002pt

This equation is a generalization of the well-known thin-film equation that takes into account the influence of both gravity and surface tension, the latter appearing in the Bond number
Bo
(see definition in (
2.4
)). In some sense, the capillary effects balance the gravitational effects and allow to obtain existence of the solution to the approximated equation also in the unstable case.
Let us now introduce the definition of weak solution to (
4.10
).

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
\zeta\in L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))\cap L^{1}([0,T],W^{3,\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
is a weak solution to (
4.10
) with initial data
\zeta_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
if for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
and
t\in[0,T]
the following equality holds:

(4.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\zeta(t,x)\varphi(t,x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\zeta_{0}(x)\varphi(0,x)dx-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\zeta(t^{\prime})\partial_{t}\varphi(t^{\prime},x)dxdt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(1+\varepsilon\zeta(t,x))\partial_{x}\zeta(t^{\prime},x)\partial_{x}\varphi(t^{\prime},x)dxdt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\rm Bo}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(1+\varepsilon\zeta(t,x))\partial_{xxx}\zeta(t^{\prime},x)\partial_{x}\varphi(t^{\prime},x)dxdt^{\prime}.

We are now able to show the existence of the solution to (
4.10
) in Wiener spaces.

Theorem 1
.

Let
0<\rm Bo<1
and
\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{A}^{[}0]_{0}(\mathbb{T})
a zero-mean function such that

(4.12)

|\zeta_{0}|_{0,0}<\tfrac{\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{Bo}}-1}{8\varepsilon(\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{Bo}}+1)}.

Then there exists a global weak solution to (
4.10
) in the sense of Definition
4.1
with initial data
\zeta_{0}
which becomes instantaneously analytic in a growing strip in the complex plane. In particular, for

\nu\in\left[0,\tfrac{1}{2}(\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{Bo}}-1)\right),

we have

\zeta\in L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{A}^{[}0]_{\nu t})\cap L^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{A}^{[}4]_{\nu t})

for all
T>0
and the solution
\zeta
satisfies for any
t\in[0,T]
the energy inequality

(4.13)

|\zeta(t)|_{0,\nu t}+\tfrac{1}{64}\left(\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{Bo}}-1\right)\int_{0}^{t}|\zeta(t^{\prime})|_{4,\nu t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime}\leq|\zeta_{0}|_{0,0}

together with the decay

(4.14)

|\zeta(t)|_{0,\nu t}\leq|\zeta_{0}|_{0,0}\ e^{-\frac{t}{64}\left(\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{Bo}}-1\right)}.

Proof.

In order to get the existence of the solution to (
4.10
), first we need to derive a priori parabolic estimates. We rewrite (
4.10
) as

(4.15)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\zeta+

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\rm Bo}\partial_{xxxx}\zeta+\partial_{xx}\zeta

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{Bo}}(\partial_{x}\zeta\partial_{xxx}\zeta+\zeta\partial_{xxxx}\zeta)-\varepsilon(\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2}-\varepsilon\zeta\partial_{xx}\zeta=\rm NL_{2}(\zeta)

where we have denoted by
\rm NL_{2}(\zeta)
the quadratic nonlinear terms of the equation.
Considering the Fourier series of (
4.10
) we obtain

\partial_{t}\widehat{\zeta}+\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{Bo}}|n|^{4}\widehat{\zeta}-|n|^{2}\widehat{\zeta}=\widehat{\rm NL_{2}(\zeta)},

where
\widehat{\zeta}
denotes the Fourier coefficient of
\zeta
.
Multiplying by the complex conjugate
\overline{\widehat{\zeta}}
, dividing by
|\widehat{\zeta}|
and taking the real part it yields

(4.16)

\partial_{t}|\widehat{\zeta}|+\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{Bo}}|n|^{4}|\widehat{\zeta}|-|n|^{2}|\widehat{\zeta}|=\tfrac{\rm Re(\widehat{NL_{2}(\zeta)}\ \overline{\widehat{\zeta}})}{|\widehat{\zeta}|}\leq|\widehat{\rm NL_{2}(\zeta)}|,

Recalling that

\frac{d}{dt}|\zeta|_{s,\nu t}=\sum_{n}(1+|n|)^{s}e^{\nu t|n|}\partial_{t}|\widehat{\zeta}|+\nu|\Lambda\zeta|_{s,\nu t},

we multiply (
4.16

and taking the sum for

we obtain for
s=0

\frac{d}{dt}|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}+(\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{Bo}}-1-\nu)\sum_{n}|n|^{4}e^{\nu t|n|}|\widehat{\zeta}|\leq|\rm NL_{2}(\zeta)|_{0,\nu t}.

Choosing
\nu\in\left[0,\tfrac{1}{2}(\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{Bo}}-1)\right)
and due to (
3.1
) it yields

(4.17)

\frac{d}{dt}|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}+\tfrac{1}{32}(\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{Bo}}-1)|\zeta|_{4,\nu t}\leq|\rm NL_{2}(\zeta)|_{0,\nu t}.

Let us now estimate each term in the right-hand side of (
4.17

3.1
and
3.3

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}\zeta\partial_{xxx}\zeta|_{0,\nu t}

\displaystyle\leq|\partial_{x}\zeta|_{0,\nu t}|\partial_{xxx}\zeta|_{0,\nu t}\leq|\zeta|_{1,\nu t}|\zeta|_{3,\nu t}\leq|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}|\zeta|_{4,\nu t},

\displaystyle|\zeta\partial_{xxxx}\zeta|_{0,\nu t}\leq|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}|\partial_{xxxx}\zeta|_{0,\nu t}\leq|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}|\zeta|_{4,\nu t},

\displaystyle|(\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2}|_{0,\nu t}\leq|\partial_{x}\zeta|^{2}_{0,\nu t}\leq|\zeta|^{2}_{1,\nu t}\leq|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}|\zeta|_{2,\nu t},

\displaystyle|\zeta\partial_{xx}\zeta|_{0,\nu t}

\displaystyle\leq|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}|\partial_{xx}\zeta|_{0,\nu t}\leq|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}|\zeta|_{2,\nu t}.

Gathering all the previous estimates together, from (

we derive the following inequality:

(4.18)

\frac{d}{dt}|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}+\tfrac{1}{64}(\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{Bo}}-1)|\zeta|_{4,\nu t}\leq 0.

From the smallness assumption (

which implies

for all

Using a standard approximation argument together with the a priori estimate (

the interested reader. Moreover, since

Approximation at order

Motivated by the aim to obtain new thin-film models that can handle the unstable configuration of the one-phase Muskat problem, we want to go further in the expansion of the potential

with respect to powers of the parameter

(4.19)

with
\phi^{0}=\varepsilon(\zeta+\tfrac{1}{\rm{Bo}}\partial_{xx}\zeta)

\phi^{1}
satisfying

(4.20)

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{zz}\phi^{1}=-A_{\zeta}(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})\phi^{0}&\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{S},\\[5.0pt]

\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{z}\phi^{1}=0&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{-1\},\end{cases}

and now the refined remainder potential

\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}_{\rm ref}
satisfies

(4.21)

\begin{cases}\nabla^{\mu}\cdot P(\Sigma)\nabla^{\mu}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}_{\rm ref}=-\mu^{2}A_{\zeta}(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})\phi^{1}&\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{S},\\[5.0pt]
\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}_{\rm ref}=\tfrac{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{Bo}}(F_{\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}}(\partial_{x}\zeta)+\tfrac{3}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\mu(\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2})\partial_{xx}\zeta&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{0\},\\[5.0pt]

Notice that in (
4.19

\phi^{1/2}
identically vanishes. Recalling the definition of
A_{\zeta}(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})

4.20

(4.22)

\displaystyle A_{\zeta}(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})\phi^{0}=A_{\zeta}(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})(\varepsilon(\zeta+\tfrac{1}{\rm Bo}\partial_{xx}\zeta))=\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon\zeta)(\partial_{xx}\zeta+\tfrac{1}{\rm Bo}\partial_{xxxx}\zeta)

after having used (
4.6

Therefore, the potential
\phi^{1}
can be explicitly determined and it reads

(4.23)

\displaystyle\phi^{1}(x,z)=-\varepsilon\left(\tfrac{z^{2}}{2}+z\right)(1+\varepsilon\zeta)^{2}(\partial_{xx}\zeta+\tfrac{1}{\rm Bo}\partial_{xxxx}\zeta)-\tfrac{3\varepsilon^{3}}{2\rm Bo}(\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2}\partial_{xx}\zeta.

We inject the refined ansatz (
4.19
) into (
2.8
) together with (
4.6
), (
4.7
) and (
4.23
) to recast the one-phase unstable Muskat problem as

(4.24)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\zeta+\partial_{x}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)(\partial_{x}\zeta+\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{Bo}}\partial_{xxx}\zeta)\right)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{\mu}{3}\partial_{xx}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)^{3}(\partial_{xx}\zeta+\tfrac{1}{\rm Bo}\partial_{xxxx}\zeta)\right)-\tfrac{3\varepsilon^{2}\mu}{2\rm Bo}\partial_{x}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)\partial_{x}((\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2}\partial_{xx}\zeta)\right)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}\left(\int_{-1}^{0}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)\partial_{x}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}_{\rm ref}-\varepsilon(z+1)\partial_{x}\zeta\partial_{z}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}_{\rm ref}\right)dz\right)

with
\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}
satisfying the elliptic problem (
4.21
). From the definition of the remainder potential, we know that
\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}
is of order
O(\mu^{3/2})
. Therefore, taking into account the terms of order
O(\mu)
and dropping the terms of order
O(\mu^{3/2})
, we obtain the following sixth-order thin film approximation:

(4.25)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\zeta+\partial_{x}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)(\partial_{x}\zeta+\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{Bo}}\partial_{xxx}\zeta)\right)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{\mu}{3}\partial_{xx}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)^{3}(\partial_{xx}\zeta+\tfrac{1}{\rm Bo}\partial_{xxxx}\zeta)\right)-\tfrac{3\varepsilon^{2}\mu}{2\rm Bo}\partial_{x}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)\partial_{x}((\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2}\partial_{xx}\zeta)\right)=0.

It is quite surprising that the thin-film approximation at this order leads to an ill-posed sixth-order equation. Indeed, one can notice that at the linear level (
4.25
) reads

\partial_{t}\zeta+\tfrac{\mu}{3\rm Bo}\partial_{xxxxxx}\zeta+\mbox{low order terms}=0

whose leading order term has a bad sign. Indeed, in the Fourier formulation it gives

\partial_{t}\widehat{\zeta}=\tfrac{\mu}{3\rm Bo}|n|^{6}\widehat{\zeta}+\mbox{low order terms}

and the solution does not belong to any Sobolev or Wiener space.

Remark 4.2
.

The leading order term in (
4.25
) is due to the surface tension term in the boundary condition of the Hele-Shaw problem and not from the gravity term. This means that one faces the same bad sign either in the stable or in the unstable case using this second-order thin-film approximation.

At a first look, the bad sign comes surprisingly since one would expect the presence of parabolicity in the equation after a second-order approximation as it occurs in the full unstable Hele-Shaw problem studied in
[
31
]
. However it is known that, depending on the type of approximation one considers, high order truncations can lead to ill-posed asymptotic models. For instance, this is the case of some truncated series models for the water waves problem discussed in
[
5
]
.

The obstacle represented by the surface tension term motivates us to change the physical regime of the problem we are studying. More precisely, we consider now the case when

\frac{1}{\rm Bo}=\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}=O(\sqrt{\mu})

with the rescaled Bond number

\mathrm{bo}=\frac{\rho gHL}{\gamma}.

Remark 4.3
.

We highlight that this configuration is still physically reasonable when dealing with groundwater filtration. For instance, considering the characteristic scales
H\sim 0.1-1
mm and
L\sim 5
mm, the situation studied would belong to this regime.

In this new regime we write the potential
\phi^{\mu}
as

(4.26)

\phi^{\mu}=\phi^{0}+\sqrt{\mu}\phi^{1/2}+\mu\phi^{1}+\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}_{\rm ref}

where now
\phi^{0}=\varepsilon\zeta
,
\phi^{1/2}=\tfrac{\varepsilon}{\rm bo}\partial_{xx}\zeta,

\phi^{1}
satisfies

(4.27)

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{zz}\phi^{1}=-A_{\zeta}(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})\phi^{0}&\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{S},\\[5.0pt]
\phi^{1}=0&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{0\},\\[5.0pt]
\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{z}\phi^{1}=0&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{-1\},\end{cases}

and the refined remainder potential

\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}_{\rm ref}
satisfies

(4.28)

\begin{cases}\nabla^{\mu}\cdot P(\Sigma)\nabla^{\mu}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}_{\rm ref}=-\mu^{3/2}A_{\zeta}(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})\phi^{1/2}-\mu^{2}A_{\zeta}(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})\phi^{1}&\mbox{in}\,\,\mathcal{S},\\[5.0pt]
\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}_{\rm ref}=\tfrac{\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}F_{\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}}(\partial_{x}\zeta)\partial_{xx}\zeta&\mbox{on}\,\,\mathbb{T}\!\times\!\{0\},\\[5.0pt]
\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{z}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}_{\rm ref}=0&\mbox{on}\,\,\mathbb{T}\!\times\!\{-1\}.\end{cases}

The explicit expressions of
\phi^{0}
and
\phi^{1/2}
are obtained by identifying the terms of the same order in the expansion of the original elliptic problem on
\phi^{\mu}
. This leads to systems analogous to (
4.3
)-(
4.4
) that admit explicit solutions. Moreover, it is straightforward that
(
4.27
) admits the unique explicit solution

\phi_{1}(x,z)=-\varepsilon\left(\tfrac{z^{2}}{2}+z\right)(1+\varepsilon\zeta)^{2}\partial_{xx}\zeta.

Injecting the ansatz (
4.26
) and the new expressions of
\phi^{0}
,
\phi^{1/2}
and
\phi^{1}
into (
2.8
), we are able to rewrite the one-phase Muskat problem as

(4.29)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\zeta+\partial_{x}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)(\partial_{x}\zeta+\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}\partial_{xxx}\zeta)\right)+\tfrac{\mu}{3}\partial_{xx}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)^{3}\partial_{xx}\zeta\right)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}\left(\int_{-1}^{0}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)\partial_{x}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}_{\rm ref}-\varepsilon(z+1)\partial_{x}\zeta\partial_{z}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}_{\rm ref}\right)dz\right)

where
\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}_{\rm ref}
satisfies (
4.28
). Dropping the terms of order
O(\mu^{3/2})
, we obtain the following fourth-order refined thin film equation

(4.30)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\zeta+\partial_{x}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)(\partial_{x}\zeta+\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}\partial_{xxx}\zeta)\right)+\tfrac{\mu}{3}\partial_{xx}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)^{3}\partial_{xx}\zeta\right)=0.

Notice that in the new regime the approximation of order
O(\mu^{3/2})
does not capture the critical sixth-order term of (
4.25
). However, there is an additional term that refines the standard thin film equation (
4.10
). Let us now investigate the existence of the solution to (
4.30
). First we introduce the definition of its weak solution.

Definition 4.4
.

We say that
\zeta\in L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))\cap L^{1}([0,T],W^{3,\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
is a weak solution to (
4.30
) with initial data
\zeta_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
if for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
and
t\in[0,T]
the following equality holds:

(4.31)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\zeta(t,x)\varphi(t,x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\zeta_{0}(x)\varphi(0,x)dx-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\zeta(t^{\prime})\partial_{t}\varphi(t^{\prime},x)dxdt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(1+\varepsilon\zeta(t,x))\partial_{x}\zeta(t^{\prime},x)\partial_{x}\varphi(t^{\prime},x)dxdt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(1+\varepsilon\zeta(t,x))\partial_{xxx}\zeta(t^{\prime},x)\partial_{x}\varphi(t^{\prime},x)dxdt^{\prime}

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu}{3}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(1+\varepsilon\zeta(t,x))^{3}\partial_{xx}\zeta(t^{\prime},x)\partial_{xx}\varphi(t^{\prime},x)dxdt^{\prime}

We are now able to show the existence of the solution to (
4.30
) in Wiener spaces.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathrm{bo}>0
small enough and
0<\mu<1
such that
\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}>1
and
\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{A}^{[}0]_{0}(\mathbb{T})
a zero-mean function such that

(4.32)

|\zeta_{0}|_{0,0}<\tfrac{\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1}{128\varepsilon\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\tfrac{55}{6}\mu+1\right)}.

Then there exists a global weak solution to (
4.30
) in the sense of Definition
4.4
with initial data
\zeta_{0}
which becomes instantaneously analytic in a growing strip in the complex plane. In particular, for

\nu\in\left[0,\tfrac{1}{2}(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1)\right),

we have

\zeta\in L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{A}^{[}0]_{\nu t})\cap L^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{A}^{[}4]_{\nu t})

for all
T>0
and the solution
\zeta
satisfies for any
t\in[0,T]
the energy inequality

(4.33)

|\zeta(t)|_{0,\nu t}+\tfrac{1}{64}\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1\right)\int_{0}^{t}|\zeta(t^{\prime})|_{4,\nu t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime}\leq|\zeta_{0}|_{0,0}

together with the decay

(4.34)

|\zeta(t)|_{0,\nu t}\leq|\zeta_{0}|_{0,0}\ e^{-\frac{t}{64}\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1\right)}.

Proof.

Analogously to the proof of Theorem
1
, we derive a priori energy estimate and the existence is obtained by using a standard approximation argument that we omit. Considering the Fourier series of (
4.30
), we get

(4.35)

\partial_{t}\widehat{\zeta}+\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}\right)|n|^{4}\widehat{\zeta}-|n|^{2}\widehat{\zeta}=\widehat{\rm NL_{2}(\zeta)}+\widehat{\rm NL_{3}(\zeta)}+\widehat{\rm NL_{4}(\zeta)},

where
\widehat{\zeta}
denotes the Fourier coefficient of
\zeta
and
\rm NL_{2}(\zeta)
,
\rm NL_{3}(\zeta)
,
\rm NL_{4}(\zeta)
denote respectively the quadratic, the cubic and quartic nonlinear terms, namely

\displaystyle{\rm NL_{2}(\zeta)}=-\varepsilon\bigg[

\displaystyle\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+2\mu\right)\partial_{x}\zeta\partial_{xxx}\zeta+\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\mu\right)\zeta\partial_{xxxx}\zeta

\displaystyle+\mu(\partial_{xx}\zeta)^{2}+(\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2}+\zeta\partial_{xx}\zeta\bigg],

\displaystyle{\rm NL_{3}(\zeta)}=-\mu\varepsilon^{2}\left(2(\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2}\partial_{xx}\zeta+2\zeta(\partial_{xx}\zeta)^{2}+4\zeta\partial_{x}\zeta\partial_{xxx}\zeta+\zeta^{2}\partial_{xxxx}\zeta\right)

and

\displaystyle{\rm NL_{4}(\zeta)}=-\mu\varepsilon^{3}\left(2\zeta(\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2}\partial_{xx}\zeta+\zeta^{2}(\partial_{xx}\zeta)^{2}+2\zeta^{2}\partial_{x}\zeta\partial_{xxx}\zeta+\tfrac{1}{3}\zeta^{3}\partial_{xxxx}\zeta\right)

Choosing
\nu\in\left[0,\tfrac{1}{2}(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1)\right)
, it yields

(4.36)

\frac{d}{dt}|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}+\tfrac{1}{32}(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1)|\zeta|_{4,\nu t}\leq|\rm NL_{2}(\zeta)|_{0,\nu t}+|\rm NL_{3}(\zeta)|_{0,\nu t}+|\rm NL_{4}(\zeta)|_{0,\nu t}.\\

Using Proposition
3.1
for
s=0
,
\lambda=\nu t
and Proposition
3.3
, we have

(4.37)

|\rm{NL_{2}(\zeta)}|_{0,\nu t}\leq 2\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}+2\mu+1\right)\varepsilon|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}|\zeta|_{4,\nu t},

(4.38)

|\rm{NL_{3}(\zeta)}|_{0,\nu t}\leq 9\mu\varepsilon^{2}|\zeta|^{2}_{0,\nu t}|\zeta|_{4,\nu t},

and

(4.39)

|\rm{NL_{4}(\zeta)}|_{0,\nu t}\leq\tfrac{16}{3}\mu\varepsilon^{3}|\zeta|^{3}_{0,\nu t}|\zeta|_{4,\nu t}.

Injecting (
4.37
)-(
4.39
) into (
4.36
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{32}(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1)|\zeta|_{4,\nu t}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\left(2\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}+2\mu+1\right)+9\mu\varepsilon|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}+\tfrac{16}{3}\mu\varepsilon^{2}|\zeta|^{2}_{0,\nu t}\right)|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}|\zeta|_{4,\nu t}

and, if we consider
\varepsilon|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}<1
, it implies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{32}(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1)|\zeta|_{4,\nu t}\leq 2\varepsilon\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}+\tfrac{55}{6}\mu+1\right)|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}|\zeta|_{4,\nu t}.

Therefore, assuming
|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}<\tfrac{\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1}{128\varepsilon\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\tfrac{55}{6}\mu+1\right)},
we are able to close the estimate and obtain

(4.40)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{64}(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1)|\zeta|_{4,\nu t}\leq 0

Following the argument used in the proof of Theorem
1
, one has that (
4.40
) is global in time and implies (
4.33
) and (
4.34
).
∎

5.
Elliptic estimate for the remainder potential

In this section we focus on the remainder potential
\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}
in the ansatz (
4.2
)-(
4.19
) we have previously introduced in (
2.8
)-(
2.9
) to obtain the thin film approximations (
4.10
)-(
4.30
). Our aim is to derive an elliptic estimate that we will use in the next section when we will rigorously justify the thin-film approximations derived in this paper.

On one hand, using (
4.2
) the remainder potential satisfies the elliptic problem (
4.5
). On the other hand, using (
4.19
) the remainder potential satisfies the elliptic problem (
4.28
). One can notice that both elliptic problems have the same structure

(5.1)

\begin{cases}\nabla^{\mu}\cdot P(\Sigma)\nabla^{\mu}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}=f&\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{S},\\[5.0pt]
\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}=h&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{0\},\\[5.0pt]
\partial_{z}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}=0&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{-1\}.\end{cases}

for different source terms
f
. In order to derive an elliptic estimate for
\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}
, let us introduce the following proposition stating the existence in Wiener-Sobolev spaces of the solution to a Poisson problem with a source term that is a sum of a divergence form term and a function.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{s+1,1}
,
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{s,0}
and
h\in\mathbb{A}^{s}_{\lambda}
with
s\geq 0
and
\lambda\geq 0.
There exists a unique
\varphi
solution to

(5.2)

\begin{cases}\Delta^{\mu}\varphi=\nabla^{\mu}\cdot g+f&\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{S},\\
\varphi=h&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{0\},\\
\partial_{z}\varphi=0&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{-1\}.\end{cases}

Moreover, such solution satisfies the estimate

(5.3)

\|\nabla^{\mu}\varphi\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{s,0}}\leq C\left(\|g\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{s,0}}+\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{s,0}}+|h|_{s,\lambda}\right)

with
C=10.

Proof.

The proof of this proposition is an adaptation of the proof of Theorem 3.1 of
[
31
]
. Considering the Fourier series of the elliptic equation of (
5.2
), we obtain the sequence of ODEs

(5.4)

-\mu k^{2}\widehat{\varphi}(k,z)+\partial_{z}^{2}\widehat{\varphi}(k,z)=i\sqrt{\mu}k\ \widehat{g_{1}}(k,z)+\partial_{z}\widehat{g_{2}}(k,z)+\widehat{f}(k,z),\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Using the Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions of the elliptic problem, we can obtain the explicit solution to (
5.2
)

(5.5)

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(k,z)=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}|k|}\int_{-1}^{z}\Pi_{1}(\mu|k|,r,z)\left(i\sqrt{\mu}k\hat{g_{1}}(k,r)+\partial_{r}\hat{g_{2}}(k,r)+\hat{f}(k,r)\right)dr

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}|k|}\int_{z}^{0}\Pi_{2}(\sqrt{\mu}|k|,r,z)\left(i\sqrt{\mu}k\hat{g_{1}}(k,r)+\partial_{r}\hat{g_{2}}(k,r)+\hat{f}(k,r)\right)ds

\displaystyle+\widehat{h}(k)\frac{e^{\sqrt{\mu}|k|(1+z)}+e^{-\sqrt{\mu}|k|(1+z)}}{e^{\sqrt{\mu}|k|}+e^{-\sqrt{\mu}|k|}}

with

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}(\sqrt{\mu}|k|,s,z)=\frac{e^{\sqrt{\mu}|k|(1+s)}+e^{-\sqrt{\mu}|k|(1+s)}}{e^{\sqrt{\mu}|k|}+e^{-\sqrt{\mu}|k|}}\left(e^{\sqrt{\mu}|k|z}-e^{-\sqrt{\mu}|k|z}\right)

\displaystyle\Pi_{2}(\sqrt{\mu}|k|,s,z)=e^{\sqrt{\mu}|k|(s-z)}-e^{-\sqrt{\mu}|k|(s-z)}

Notice that
\Pi_{1,2}
are the same functions as in
[
31
]
. On the one hand, we can use the following estimates on
\Pi_{1}
and
\Pi_{2}
therein. For
(j,l)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
it holds

(5.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\|\mathbf{1}_{[-1,z]}(\cdot)\partial_{z}^{j}\partial_{r}^{l}\Pi_{1}(\sqrt{\mu}|k|,\cdot,z)\|_{L^{\infty}_{r}}dz\leq 2(\sqrt{\mu}|k|)^{j+l-1}

(5.7)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\|\mathbf{1}_{[z,0]}(\cdot)\partial_{z}^{j}\partial_{r}^{l}\Pi_{2}(\sqrt{\mu}|k|,\cdot,z)\|_{L^{\infty}_{r}}dz\leq\tfrac{5}{2}(\sqrt{\mu}|k|)^{j+l-1}

to obtain the bound for
\|\nabla^{\mu}\varphi\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s,0}_{\lambda}}
with
\|g\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s,0}_{\lambda}}
following the same argument as in
[
31
]
. On the other hand, using the definition of
\Pi_{1,2}
we know that
|\Pi_{1}(\sqrt{\mu}|k|,r,z)|\leq 1
for
-1\leq r\leq z
and
|\Pi_{2}(\sqrt{\mu}|k|,r,z)|\leq C
for
z\leq r\leq 0
and some constant
C>0.
Using these two inequalities we get the bound for
\|\nabla^{\mu}\varphi\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s,0}_{\lambda}}
with
\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s,0}_{\lambda}}
.
Let us detail only the bound coming from the non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition. We have

(5.8)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mu}|k|\int_{-1}^{0}|\widehat{h}(k)|\frac{e^{\sqrt{\mu}|k|(1+z)}+e^{-\sqrt{\mu}|k|(1+z)}}{e^{\sqrt{\mu}|k|}+e^{-\sqrt{\mu}|k|}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mu}|k|\int_{-1}^{0}|\widehat{h}(k)|\frac{\cosh((1+z)\sqrt{\mu}|k|)}{\cosh(\sqrt{\mu}|k|)}dz

\displaystyle=|\hat{h}(k)|\tanh(\sqrt{\mu}|k|)\leq|\hat{h}(k)|

and

(5.9)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{0}|\widehat{h}(k)|\frac{\left|\partial_{z}\left(e^{\sqrt{\mu}|k|(1+z)}+e^{-\sqrt{\mu}|k|(1+z)}\right)\right|}{e^{\sqrt{\mu}|k|}+e^{-\sqrt{\mu}|k|}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mu}|k|\int_{-1}^{0}|\widehat{h}(k)|\frac{\sinh((1+z)\sqrt{\mu}|k|)}{\cosh(\sqrt{\mu}|k|)}dz=|\hat{h}(k)|\left(1-\frac{1}{\cosh(\sqrt{\mu}|k|)}\right)\leq|\hat{h}(k)|.

These two inequalities give the bound for
\|\nabla^{\mu}\varphi\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s,0}_{\lambda}}
with
|h|_{s,\lambda}
. By linearity of the elliptic equation in (
5.2
), the estimate (
5.3
) follows putting together the three bounds obtained.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Let us point out that the elliptic estimate (
5.3
) is not sharp. Indeed using (
5.6
)-(
5.7
) also for the bound of the part of
\widehat{\phi}
containing
\widehat{f}
together with the inequality

\frac{(1+|k|)^{s}}{\sqrt{\mu}|k|}\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{\mu}}(1+|k|)^{s-1}\qquad\mbox{for}\quad|k|\geq 1,

one would obtain
\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{s-1,0}}
instead of
\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{s,0}}
in (
5.3
). However, since we will use this elliptic estimate to prove a quantitative convergence result in Section (
6
), the optimal order of convergence will be obtained considering the estimate without the singularity
1/\sqrt{\mu}
. Therefore, the price one has to pay for the optimal order is that the source term of the corresponding elliptic system needs to be more regular.

In order to apply Proposition
5.1
to the elliptic problem (
5.1
), using the fact that
P(\Sigma)=\mathrm{Id}+Q(\Sigma)
, we write it as the following Poisson problem

(5.10)

\begin{cases}\Delta^{\mu}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}=-\nabla^{\mu}\cdot Q(\Sigma)\nabla^{\mu}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}+f&\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{S},\\[5.0pt]
\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}=h&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{0\},\\[5.0pt]
\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{z}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}=0&\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{T}\times\{-1\},\\
\end{cases}

Following the approach in
[
31
]
, we aim to derive an estimate on the
\mathcal{A}^{s,0}_{\lambda}
-norm of
\nabla^{\mu}\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}
only in terms of Wiener norms of the free surface
\zeta
. To do that, we need to introduce the next two proposition that give regularity estimates in terms of
\zeta
of the matrix
Q(\Sigma)
and the function
F_{\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}}(\partial_{x}\zeta)
appearing respectively in the divergence term and in the Dirichlet boundary condition of (
5.10
).

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
Q(\Sigma)
be as in (
2.10
). Assume that
\mu<1
and
|\zeta|_{1,\lambda}\leq\frac{1}{8K_{s}\varepsilon}
with
s,\lambda\geq 0
. Then, choosing
\sigma(x,z)=\varepsilon\zeta(x)(z+1)
, we have

(5.11)

\|Q(\Sigma)\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{s,1}}\leq 3\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}|\zeta|_{s+1,\lambda}.

Proof.

With the particular choice
\sigma(x,z)=\varepsilon\zeta(x)(z+1)
,
Q(\Sigma)
reads

Q(\Sigma)=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon\zeta&-\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}\partial_{x}\zeta(z+1)\\[10.0pt]
-\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}\partial_{x}\zeta(z+1)&\dfrac{-\varepsilon\zeta+\varepsilon^{2}\mu(\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2}(z+1)^{2}}{1+\varepsilon\zeta}\end{pmatrix}.

Following
[
31
]
, we use the expansion of
\frac{1}{1+x}
for
|x|<1
to write
Q(\Sigma)
as the infinite sum

Q(\Sigma)=\varepsilon\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\varepsilon^{n}Q_{(n)}(\zeta)

with

Q_{(0)}(\zeta)=\begin{pmatrix}\zeta&-\sqrt{\mu}\partial_{x}\zeta(z+1)\\[10.0pt]
-\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}\partial_{x}\zeta(z+1)&-\zeta+\varepsilon\mu(\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2}(z+1)^{2}\end{pmatrix}

and

Q_{(n)}(\zeta)=\begin{pmatrix}\quad 0&0\\[10.0pt]
\quad 0&(-\zeta)^{n}\left(-\zeta+\varepsilon\mu(\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2}(z+1)^{2}\right)\end{pmatrix}.

We have

(5.12)

\displaystyle\|Q_{(0)}(\zeta)\|_{A^{s,1}_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\mu}\|\partial_{x}\zeta(z+1)\|_{A^{s,1}_{\lambda}}+\varepsilon\mu\|(\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2}(z+1)^{2}\|_{A^{s,1}_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\mu}|\zeta|_{s+1,\lambda}+2\varepsilon\mu|(\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2}|_{s,\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\mu}|\zeta|_{s+1,\lambda}+2\varepsilon\mu K_{s,2}|\zeta|_{1,\lambda}|\zeta|_{s+1,\lambda}

\displaystyle=2\left(\sqrt{\mu}+\varepsilon\mu K_{s}|\zeta|_{1,\lambda}\right)|\zeta|_{s+1,\lambda}

and

(5.13)

\displaystyle\|Q_{(n)}(\zeta)\|_{A^{s,1}_{\lambda}}

Lemma 6.2

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}N_{1}(f,\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref})-\varepsilon N_{2}(f,\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref})-\varepsilon\mu N_{3}(f,\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref})-\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}N(\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu},\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}_{\rm ref})

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\left(f^{2}\partial_{xx}f+(\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref})^{2}\partial_{xx}f+f^{2}\partial_{xx}\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}+2f\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}\partial_{xx}f+2f\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}\partial_{xx}\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}\right)

\displaystyle F_{1}(\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref},\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu})=C_{1}\varepsilon(|\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}|_{0,\nu t}

\displaystyle+|\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}|_{0,\nu t})

\displaystyle\times\left(1+\varepsilon|\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}|_{0,\nu t}+\varepsilon^{2}(|\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}|^{2}_{0,\nu t}+|\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}|^{2}_{0,\nu t})\right)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon(|\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}|_{0,\nu t}+|\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}|_{0,\nu t})

The proof follows exactly the same argument used in the proof of Proposition

\displaystyle|\partial_{xx}(f\partial_{xx}f)|_{0,\nu t}

L^{1}([0,T])
with respect to
\mu
for any
T>0
provided the initial data are in
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{0}
.
∎

We are now able to state the following asymptotic stability theorem for the refined thin-film equation (
4.30
):

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathrm{bo>0}
small enough and
\zeta_{\mathrm{in},\mu}
be a zero-mean function in
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{0}
satisfying the smallness assumption (
6.1
). Let
\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}
and
\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}
be the global weak solutions respectively to (
2.7
) and to (
4.30
) both with initial data
\zeta_{\mathrm{in},\mu}.
Then, for any
T>0
there exist some constant
C>0
independent of
\mu
and
\mu_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for
\mu\in[\mu_{0},1)

(6.16)

\|\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}-\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];\ \mathbb{A}^{0}_{\nu t})}+\tfrac{1}{64}(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1)\|\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}-\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}\|_{L^{1}([0,T];\ \mathbb{A}^{4}_{\nu t})}\leq C\mu^{3/2}.

Remark 6.7
.

Here we consider the adapted version of the smallness condition (
6.1
) and of the entire Theorem
3
according to the new regime considered, where
\frac{1}{\rm Bo}=\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}.

Proof.

Analogously to the proof of Theorem
4
, we implement an energy estimate for (
6.12
) and we get for
f=\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}-\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}
the following inequality:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|f|_{0,\nu t}+\tfrac{1}{32}(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1)|f|_{4,\nu t}\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}|N_{1}(f,\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref})|_{0,\nu t}+\varepsilon|N_{2}(f,\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref})|_{0,\nu t}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\varepsilon\mu|N_{3}(f,\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref})|_{0,\nu t}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}|N(\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu},\widetilde{\phi}^{\mu}_{\rm ref})|_{0,\nu t}.

From (
6.4
), (
6.13
) and (
6.14
), it yields

(6.17)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|f|_{0,\nu t}+

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{32}(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1)|f|_{4,\nu t}

\displaystyle\leq R_{1}(\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref},\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu})|f|_{4,\nu t}+R_{2}(\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref},\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu})|f|_{0,\nu t},

with

\displaystyle R_{1}(\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref},\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu})=2\varepsilon\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}+1\right)|\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}|_{0,\nu t}+\mu F_{1}(\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref},\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu})

and

\displaystyle R_{2}(\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref},\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu})=

\displaystyle\left(4\varepsilon\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}+1\right)+\mu F_{2}(\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref},\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu})\right)|\zeta^{\rm app}|_{4,\nu t}

\displaystyle+2\varepsilon\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}+1\right)|\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}|_{4,\nu t}.

One can observe that the required smallness assumption (
6.1
) for the initial data implies the smallness assumption (
4.32
). Therefore we can use
both estimates (
4.33
) for
\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}
and (
6.2
) for
\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}
, the fact that
\mu<1
and
\rm bo<1
in order to obtain that

\displaystyle R_{1}(\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref},\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu})\leq\tfrac{1}{64}(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1),

which gives

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|f|_{0,\nu t}+\tfrac{1}{64}(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1)|f|_{4,\nu t}\leq R_{2}(\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref},\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu})|f|_{0,\nu t}+\mu^{3/2}R^{\mu}_{\rm ref}

From Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
we know that both
\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}
and
(\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu})_{0<\mu<1}
are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{A}^{0}_{\nu t})\cap L^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\nu t})
, which implies that
R_{2}(\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref},\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu})
is uniformly bounded in
L^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\nu t})
. Moreover, from Proposition
6.6
we know that if
\zeta_{\mathrm{in},\mu}\in\mathbb{A}^{3}_{0}
then
R^{\mu}_{\rm ref}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{1}([0,T])
. We look for a convergence result for small values of the parameter
\mu
but at the same time we need to guarantee that the condition
\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1>0
holds true. This implies that the values of
\mu
cannot be taken arbitrarily small. Indeed, denoting
y=\sqrt{\mu}>0
, the previous condition reads

y^{2}+\tfrac{3}{\rm bo}y-3>0,

whose solution is the interval
(y^{\ast},+\infty)
for some
y^{\ast}\in(0,1)
if
\mathrm{bo}
is small enough. Let us hence define
\mu_{0}:=(y^{\ast}+\varepsilon)^{2}
for
\varepsilon>0
arbitrarily small. Therefore, using Gronwall’s inequality we obtain for
\mu\in[\mu_{0},1)

\displaystyle|f(t)|_{0,\nu t}\ +

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{64}\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\rm bo}+\tfrac{\mu}{3}-1\right)\int_{0}^{t}|f(t^{\prime})|_{4,\nu t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\mu^{3/2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}R^{\mu}_{\rm ref}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right)e^{\int_{0}^{t}R_{2}(\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref},\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu})(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\leq C\mu^{3/2}

for all
t\in[0,T]
and some constant
C>0
independent of
\mu
. Notice that in the previous inequality we have used that
f(0)=0
. The error estimate (
6.16
) easily follows after taking the supremum over
[0,T]
.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

Since
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq|f|_{0,\nu t}
, it follows from (
6.10
) that for all
T>0
and
\mu\in[\mu_{0},1)
we have

\|\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}-\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})}\leq C\mu^{3/2}.

6.2.1.
The stable case

As we have previously seen, the rigorous justification of the thin film approximation at order
O(\mu^{3/2})
in the unstable case is possible for small values of
\mu
that are bounded from below and cannot be taken arbitrarily small as one would expect. This impossibility is due to the unstable configuration we are considering. More precisely, it is due to the sign of the gravity term in (
4.30
) that is responsible for the appearance of
-1
in the coefficient of the fourth-order norm in the left hand side of (
4.36
). Then, we ask for the coefficient to be strictly positive in order to use the parabolic structure of the equation. This condition forces to impose a lower bound on the parameter
\mu
. We now consider the refined thin film approximation in the stable configuration where the sign of the gravity terms in (
4.30
) changes, namely

(6.18)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\zeta+\partial_{x}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)(-\partial_{x}\zeta+\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}\partial_{xxx}\zeta)\right)-\tfrac{\mu}{3}\partial_{xx}\left((1+\varepsilon\zeta)^{3}\partial_{xx}\zeta\right)=0.

Analogously to the proof of Theorem
2
, we can write (
6.18
) in Fourier as

\partial_{t}\widehat{\zeta}+\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}-\tfrac{\mu}{3}\right)|n|^{4}\widehat{\zeta}+|n|^{2}\widehat{\zeta}=\widehat{\rm NL_{2}(\zeta)}+\widehat{\rm NL_{3}(\zeta)}+\widehat{\rm NL_{4}(\zeta)}

for slightly different nonlinear terms
\mathrm{NL}_{2}(\zeta)
,
\mathrm{NL}_{3}(\zeta)
,
\mathrm{NL}_{4}(\zeta)
and it yields

\partial_{t}|\widehat{\zeta}|+\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}-\tfrac{\mu}{3}\right)|n|^{4}|\widehat{\zeta}|+|n|^{2}|\widehat{\zeta}|\leq|\widehat{\rm NL_{2}(\zeta)}|+|\widehat{\rm NL_{3}(\zeta)}|+|\widehat{\rm NL_{4}(\zeta)}|.\\

Taking advantage of the positive sign of the gravity term

|n|^{2}|\widehat{\zeta}|
and
choosing

\nu\in\left[0,\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}-\tfrac{\mu}{3}\right)\right)

we get

\frac{d}{dt}|\zeta|_{0,\nu t}+\tfrac{1}{32}\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}-\tfrac{\mu}{3}\right)|\zeta|_{4,\nu t}\leq|\rm NL_{2}(\zeta)|_{0,\nu t}+|\rm NL_{3}(\zeta)|_{0,\nu t}+|\rm NL_{4}(\zeta)|_{0,\nu t}.

Then, by using the same argument of the proof of Theorem
5
, we get the following existence result:

Theorem 6
.

Let
\mathrm{bo}>0
,
0<\mu<1
such that
\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}-\tfrac{\mu}{3}>0
and
\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{A}^{[}0]_{0}(\mathbb{T})
be a zero-mean function such that

(6.19)

|\zeta_{0}|_{0,0}<\tfrac{\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}-\tfrac{\mu}{3}}{128\varepsilon\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}+\tfrac{55}{6}\mu+1\right)}.

Then there exists a global weak solution to (
6.18
) in the sense of Definition
4.4
with initial data
\zeta_{0}
which becomes instantaneously analytic in a growing strip in the complex plane. In particular, for

\nu\in\left[0,\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}-\tfrac{\mu}{3}\right)\right),

we have

\zeta\in L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{A}^{[}0]_{\nu t})\cap L^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{A}^{[}4]_{\nu t})

for all
T>0
and the solution
\zeta
satisfies for any
t\in[0,T]
the energy inequality

(6.20)

|\zeta(t)|_{0,\nu t}+\tfrac{1}{64}\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}-\tfrac{\mu}{3}\right)\int_{0}^{t}|\zeta(t^{\prime})|_{4,\nu t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime}\leq|\zeta_{0}|_{0,0}

together with the decay

(6.21)

|\zeta(t)|_{0,\nu t}\leq|\zeta_{0}|_{0,0}\ e^{-\frac{t}{64}\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}-\tfrac{\mu}{3}\right)}.

Finally, we obtain a rigorous justification of the refined thin film approximation in the stable case without any lower bound for
\mu
:

Theorem 7
.

Let
\mathrm{bo>0}
and
\zeta_{\mathrm{in},\mu}
be a zero-mean function in
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{0}
satisfying the smallness assumption (
6.1
). Let
\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}
and
\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}
be the global weak solutions respectively to (
2.7
) and to (
6.18
) both with initial data
\zeta_{\mathrm{in},\mu}.
Then, for any
T>0
there exist
C>0
independent of
\mu
and
\mu_{1}\in(0,1)
such that for
\mu\in(0,\mu_{1})

(6.22)

\|\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}-\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];\ \mathbb{A}^{0}_{\nu t})}+\tfrac{1}{64}\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\mathrm{bo}}-\tfrac{\mu}{3}\right)\|\zeta^{\rm MU}_{\mu}-\zeta^{\rm app}_{\rm ref}\|_{L^{1}([0,T];\ \mathbb{A}^{4}_{\nu t})}\leq C\mu^{3/2}.

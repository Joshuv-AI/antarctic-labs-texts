Convergence of Nörlund means with respect to Vilenkin systems of integrable functions Thanks: The research was supported by Shota Rustaveli National Science Foundation grant no. FR-19-676.
Davit Baramidze, Zura dvalashvili and Giorgi Tutberidze

Abstract
. In this paper we derive converge of Nörlund means of Vilenkin-Fourier series with monotone coefficients of integrable functions in Lebesgue and Vilinkin-Lebesgue points. Moreover, we discuss pointwise convergence and convergence in L p L_{p} norms of such Nörlund means.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification.
42C10, 42B25.

Key words and phrases:
Vilenkin systems, Vilenkin groups, Nörlund means, a.e. convergence, Lebesgue points, Vilenkin-Lebesgue points.

2.
Preliminaries

Denote by
\mathbb{N}_{+}
the set of the positive integers,
\mathbb{N}:=\mathbb{N}_{+}\cup\{0\}.
Let
m:=(m_{0,}

m_{1},...)
be a sequence of
the positive integers not less than 2. Denote by

Z_{m_{k}}:=\{0,1,...,m_{k}-1\}

the additive group of integers modulo
m_{k}
.

Define the group
G_{m}
as the complete direct product of the groups
Z_{m_{i}}
with the product of the discrete topologies of
Z_{m_{j}}`
s. In this paper we discuss bounded Vilenkin groups, i.e. the case
when
\sup_{n}m_{n}<\infty.
The direct product
\mu
of the measures

\mu_{k}\left(\{j\}\right):=1/m_{k}\text{ \ \ \ }(j\in Z_{m_{k}})

is the Haar measure on
G_{m_{\text{ }}}
with
\mu\left(G_{m}\right)=1.

The elements of
G_{m}
are represented by sequences

x:=\left(x_{0},x_{1},...,x_{j},...\right),\ \left(x_{j}\in Z_{m_{j}}\right).

It is easy to give a base for the neighborhood of
G_{m}:

I_{0}\left(x\right):=G_{m},\text{ \ }I_{n}(x):=\{y\in G_{m}\mid y_{0}=x_{0},...,y_{n-1}=x_{n-1}\},

where
x\in G_{m},

n\in\mathbb{N}.
Denote
I_{n}:=I_{n}\left(0\right)

for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+},
and
\overline{I_{n}}:=G_{m}

\backslash

I_{n}
.

If we define the so-called generalized number system based on
m

in the following way
M_{0}:=1,\ M_{k+1}:=m_{k}M_{k}\,\,\,\ \ (k\in\mathbb{N}),
then every
n\in\mathbb{N}
can be uniquely expressed as
n=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}n_{j}M_{j},
where
n_{j}\in Z_{m_{j}}

(j\in\mathbb{N}_{+})
and only a finite number of
n_{j}`
s differ from zero.

Next, we introduce on
G_{m}
an orthonormal system which is called the
Vilenkin system. First, we define the complex-valued function
r_{k}\left(x\right):G_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},
the generalized
Rademacher functions, by

r_{k}\left(x\right):=\exp\left(2\pi ix_{k}/m_{k}\right),\text{ }\left(i^{2}=-1,x\in G_{m},\text{ \ }k\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Now, define the Vilenkin system
\,\,\,\psi:=(\psi_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
on
G_{m}
as:

\psi_{n}(x):=\prod\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}r_{k}^{n_{k}}\left(x\right),\,\,\ \ \,\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Specifically, we call this system the Walsh-Paley system when
m\equiv 2.

The norms (or quasi-norms) of the spaces
L_{p}(G_{m})
and
weak-L_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)

\left(0<p<\infty\right)
are
respectively defined by

\left\|f\right\|_{p}^{p}:=\int_{G_{m}}\left|f\right|^{p}d\mu,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left\|f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}^{p}:=\underset{\lambda>0}{\sup}\lambda^{p}\mu\left(f>\lambda\right)<+\infty.

The Vilenkin system is orthonormal and complete in
L_{2}\left(G_{m}\right)
(see
[
1
]
).

If
f\in L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
we can define Fourier coefficients,
partial sums of the Fourier series, Dirichlet kernels in the usual manner:

\widehat{f}\left(n\right):=\int_{G_{m}}f\overline{\psi}_{n}d\mu\ \ \ \ \ \,S_{n}f:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{f}\left(k\right)\psi_{k},\text{ \ \ \ }D_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\psi_{k\text{ }},\text{ \ \ }\left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right)

respectively. Recall that

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{G_{m}}D_{n}(x)dx=1,

(3)

\displaystyle D_{M_{n}-j}(x)=D_{M_{n}}(x)-\psi_{M_{n}-1}(x)\overline{D}_{j}(x),\ j<M_{n}.

The convolution of two functions
f,g\in L_{1}(G_{m})
is defined by

\left(f\ast g\right)\left(x\right):=\int_{G_{m}}f\left(x-t\right)g\left(t\right)dt\text{ \ \ }\left(x\in G_{m}\right).

It is easy to see that if
f\in L_{p}\left(G_{m}\right),

g\in L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
and
1\leq p<\infty.
Then
f\ast g\in L_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)
and

(4)

\left\|f\ast g\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{p}\left\|g\right\|_{1}.

Let
\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
be a sequence of non-negative numbers. Nörlund and
T
means for a Fourier series of
f
are respectively defined
by

t_{n}f=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}q_{n-k}S_{k}f\ \ \text{and}\ \ T_{n}f:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}q_{k}S_{k}f,\ \ \text{where}\ \ Q_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q_{k}.

It is obvious that

t_{n}f\left(x\right)=\underset{G_{m}}{\int}f\left(t\right)F_{n}\left(x-t\right)d\mu\left(t\right)\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ T_{n}f\left(x\right)=\underset{G_{m}}{\int}f\left(t\right)F^{-1}_{n}\left(x-t\right)d\mu\left(t\right)

where
F_{n}
and
F^{-1}_{n}
are kernels of Nörlund and
T
kernels
respectively

F_{n}:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}q_{n-k}D_{k}\ \ \text{
and }\ \ F^{-1}_{n}:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}q_{k}D_{k}.

We always assume that
\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
be a sequence of non-negative
numbers and
q_{0}>0.
Then Nörlund means generated by
\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
is
regular if and only if

\frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}\to 0,\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty.

Analogical regularity condition for
T
means is condition
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}Q_{n}=\infty.

If we invoke Abel transformation we get the following identities, which are
very important for the investigations of Nörlund summability:

(5)

\displaystyle Q_{n}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=0}{\sum}}q_{j}=\overset{n}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}q_{n-j}\cdot 1=\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j+q_{0}n,

(6)

F_{n}=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\left(\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)jK_{j}+q_{0}nK_{n}\right)

and

(7)

t_{n}=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\left(\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j\sigma_{j}+q_{0}n\sigma_{n}\right).

Let consider some class of Nörlund means with monotone and bounded sequence
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
, such that
q:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}q_{n}>c>0.

It easy to check that

\frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),\text{ \ as \ }n\rightarrow\infty.

The well-known example of Nörlund summability is
\left(C,\alpha\right)

-means (Cesàro means), where
0<\alpha<1
which are defined by

\sigma_{n}^{\alpha}f:=\frac{1}{A_{n}^{\alpha}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}A_{n-k}^{\alpha-1}S_{k}f,\ \text{where }\ A_{0}^{\alpha}:=0,\ A_{n}^{\alpha}:=\frac{\left(\alpha+1\right)...\left(\alpha+n\right)}{n!}.

Let
V_{n}^{\alpha}
denote the Nörlund mean, where

\left\{q_{0}=1,\ q_{k}=k^{\alpha-1}:k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right\},

that is

V_{n}^{\alpha}f:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}(n-k)^{\alpha-1}S_{k}f,\qquad 0<\alpha<1.

It is easy to show that

\frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\rightarrow 0,\text{ \ as \ }n\rightarrow\infty.

The
n
-th Riesz‘s logarithmic mean
R_{n}
and Nörlund logarithmic mean

L_{n}
are defined by

R_{n}f:=\frac{1}{l_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{S_{k}f}{k},\text{ \ \ \ }L_{n}f:=\frac{1}{l_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{S_{k}f}{n-k},\ \ \text{where}\ \ l_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}1/k.

Up to now we have considered
T
mean in the case when the sequence
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded but now we consider
T
summabilities
with unbounded sequence
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

Let us define the class of Nörlund means with non-decreasing coefficients:

\beta_{n}^{\alpha}f:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log^{\alpha}\left(n-k-1\right)S_{k}f.

It is obvious that

\frac{n}{2}\log^{\alpha}\left(n/2\right)\leq Q_{n}\leq n\log^{\alpha}n.

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\rightarrow 0,\text{ \ as \ }n\rightarrow\infty\ \ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \ \frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\rightarrow 0,\text{ \ as \ }n\rightarrow\infty.

A point
x\in G_{m}
is called a Lebesgue point of integrable function
f
if

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to 0}\frac{1}{|I_{n}(x)|}\int_{I_{n}(x)}\left|f(t)-f\left(x\right)\right|d\mu(t)=0.

It is known that a.e. point
x\in G_{m}
is a Lebesgue point of function
f
and Fejér means
\sigma_{n}f
of trigonometric Fourier series of
f\in L_{1}(G_{m})
converge to
f
at each Lebesgue point. It is also known that if
x\in G_{m}
is point of continuity of function
f
then it is Lebesgue point.

Let introduced the operator

\displaystyle W_{n}f(x):=\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}M_{s}\sum_{r_{s}=1}^{m_{s}-1}\int_{I_{n}(x-r_{s}e_{s})}\left|f(t)-f\left(x\right)\right|d\mu(t)

A point
x\in G_{m}
is a Vilenkin-Lebesgue point of
f\in L_{1}(G_{m}),
if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}W_{n}f(x)=0.

First we consider kernels of Nörlund kernels with non-decreasing sequences:

Lemma 3
.

Let

be a sequence of non-decreasing numbers, satisfying the condition

Let the sequence

be non-decreasing. Then, by using (

), we get that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\left(\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left|q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right|+q_{0}\right)\leq\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\left(\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)+q_{0}\right)\leq\frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}\leq\frac{c}{n}.

Hence, in view of (
12
) if we apply Lemma
1
and use the equalities (
5
) and (
6
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|F_{n}\right|\leq\left(\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\left(\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left|q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right|+q_{0}\right)\right)\sum_{i=0}^{\left|n\right|}M_{i}\left|K_{M_{i}}\right|\leq\frac{c}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{\left|n\right|}M_{i}\left|K_{M_{i}}\right|.

The proof is complete.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of non-decreasing numbers. Then, for any
n,N\in\mathbb{N_{+}},

(13)

\displaystyle\int_{G_{m}}F_{n}(x)d\mu(x)=1,

(14)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{G_{m}}\left|F_{n}(x)\right|d\mu(x)<\infty,

(15)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{G_{m}\backslash I_{N}}\left|F_{n}(x)\right|d\mu(x)\rightarrow 0,\ \ \text{as}\ \ n\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

According to (
2
) we readily obtain (
13
). By using (
10
) in Lemma
2
, combined with (
5
) and (
6
) we get that

\displaystyle\int_{G_{m}}\left|F_{n}(x)\right|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j\int_{G_{m}}\left|K_{j}\right|d\mu+\frac{q_{0}n}{Q_{n}}\int_{G_{m}}|K_{n}|d\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j+\frac{cq_{0}n}{Q_{n}}<c<\infty.

By using (
11
) in Lemma
2
and inequalities (
5
) and (
6
) we can conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{G_{m}\backslash I_{N}}\left|F_{n}\right|d\mu\leq\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=0}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j\int_{G_{m}\backslash I_{N}}\left|K_{j}\right|d\mu+\frac{q_{0}n}{Q_{n}}\int_{G_{m}\backslash I_{N}}|K_{n}|d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=0}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j\alpha_{j}+\frac{q_{0}n\alpha_{n}}{Q_{n}}:=I+II,\ \text{ where }\ \alpha_{n}\to 0,\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty.

Since the sequence is non-decreasing, we can conclude that
II\leq\alpha_{n}\to 0,\ \ \text{as}\ \ n\to\infty.

On the other hand, since

converges to

we get that there exists an absolute constant

such that

Hence,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{N_{0}}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j\alpha_{j}+\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=N_{0}+1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j\alpha_{j}:=I_{1}+I_{2}

Since

and

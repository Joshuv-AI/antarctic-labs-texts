A Duflo Star Product for Poisson Groups
Adrien BROCHIER

Abstract
Let G G be a finite-dimensional Poisson algebraic, Lie or formal group. We show that the center of the quantization of G G provided by an Etingof–Kazhdan functor is isomorphic as an algebra to the Poisson center of the algebra of functions on G G . This recovers and generalizes Duflo’s theorem which gives an isomorphism between the center of the enveloping algebra of a finite-dimensional Lie algebra 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} and the subalgebra of ad-invariant in the symmetric algebra of 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} . As our proof relies on Etingof–Kazhdan construction it ultimately depends on the existence of Drinfeld associators, but otherwise it is a fairly simple application of graphical calculus. This shed some lights on Alekseev–Torossian proof of the Kashiwara–Vergne conjecture, and on the relation observed by Bar-Natan–Le–Thurston between the Duflo isomorphism and the Kontsevich integral of the unknot.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic 0 and
G
be a Lie, algebraic or formal group over
\mathbb{K}
. A multiplicative Poisson structure on
G
is a Poisson structure such that the multiplication map
G\times G\rightarrow G
is a Poisson map. This leads to the notion of Poisson Lie, Poisson algebraic or Poisson formal group depending on the context. If
\mathcal{O}(G)
is the Hopf algebra of
C^{\infty}
, regular or formal functions on
G
then a multiplicative Poisson structure on
G
turns
\mathcal{O}(G)
into a Poisson Hopf algebra.

A quantization of
G
is a Hopf algebra which is a quantization of
\mathcal{O}(G)
as a Poisson algebra and whose coproduct reduces to the one of
\mathcal{O}(G)
at
\hslash=0
.

In
[
11
]
Etingof–Kazhdan associate to any Drinfeld associator a functorial way to quantize Lie bialgebras and Poisson formal groups. Their construction can be applied to Poisson Lie and algebraic groups as well. Our main result is the following (Theorem
4.2
below):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{\hslash}(G)
be the quantization of a finite-dimensional Poisson group
G
obtained from an Etingof–Kazhdan functor. Then the center of
\mathcal{O}_{\hslash}(G)
is isomorphic as an algebra to the trivial
\mathbb{K}[[\hslash]]
extension of the Poisson center of
\mathcal{O}(G)
.

If
\mathfrak{a}
is a finite-dimensional Lie algebra, then
G=\mathfrak{a}^{*}
as an abelian group is a Poisson algebraic group, the Poisson structure on
\mathcal{O}(G)=S(\mathfrak{a})
being induced by the Lie bracket of
\mathfrak{a}
. The Poisson center is identified with the sub-algebra of invariant
S(\mathfrak{a})^{\mathfrak{a}}
. On the other hand one can show that the quantization of
G=\mathfrak{a}^{*}
can be specialized at
\hslash=1
, and becomes isomorphic to the enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{a}
. Hence we get Duflo’s theorem
[
9
]
as a corollary:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra, then there is an isomorphism of algebras

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{a})^{\mathfrak{a}}\cong U(\mathfrak{a})^{\mathfrak{a}}.

In fact there are two constructions of a quantization of
G
in
[
11
]
: the first one comes from a natural construction of a certain fiber functor out of a coalgebra in a braided monoidal category constructed from a Drinfeld associator. This construction is not, however, functorial, and the authors then modify it in an appropriate way to make it so.

Our first result (Theorem
4.1
) is a fairly simple graphical proof that the restriction of the star product of the first construction to the Poisson center of
\mathcal{O}(G)
is undeformed, i.e., the same as the original product. This result alone guarantee the existence of a quantization of
G
fulfilling the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
.

The second step is to show that the two constructions yields isomorphic algebras (and in fact, isomorphic Hopf algebras). This isomorphism is higly non-trivial and depends on the underlying associator. Its existence relies on the fact that the two fiber functors at hand are isomorphic as monoidal functors. However, the proof of this fact given in
[
11
]
appears to be incorrect: the isomorphism between those functors given there is not monoidal due to the contribution of certain non-trivial associativity constraints. Taking the associativity constraints into account and correcting this construction requires a detailed discussion of dualities in categories constructed from Drinfeld associators and a generalization of a result by Le–Murakami on the behaviour of the Kontsevich integral under taking parallels of tangles (Proposition
3.4
). In particular, one has to modify the ordinary coevaluation map in the Drinfeld category using a very specific element closely related to the Kontsevich integral of the unknot, which explains its relation with the diagrammatic Duflo isomorphism of
[
3
]
.

For the sake of concreteness we work with Lie bialgebras in the category of vector spaces, but our proof also applies in the following situation: if
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie bialgebra in a linear symmetric monoidal category
\mathcal{S}
which is dualizable as an object of
\mathcal{S}
, then one can define the algebra of functions over the Poisson formal group of
\mathfrak{g}
as the object
\widehat{S}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
equipped with its standard multiplication and the coproduct induced by the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula. Then Theorem
1.1
is also valid in this setting. In particular we get a version of the Duflo Theorem for dualizable Lie algebras in arbitrary symmetric monoidal categories.

We note that an analog of Theorem
1.1
holds for Kontsevich’s deformation quantization of an arbitrary Poisson manifold
M
, which also implies Duflo’s theorem
[
5
,
13
,
17
]
. While it is true for duals of finite-dimensional Lie algebras, to the best of the author’s knowledge it is not known whether Kontsevich’s star product on a Poisson group seen as a Poisson manifold is isomorphic to the one coming from Etingof–Kazhdan construction (if for the former one uses Tamarkin’s construction
[
20
,
21
]
, and if one chooses the same associator in both cases). An affirmative answer to this question would thus give another proof of our result. Another motivation for this paper is Alekseev–Torrossian proof of the Kashiwara–Vergne conjecture
[
2
]
. Roughly speaking they show that solutions of this conjecture are essentially the same as universal twist-quantization
à la
Drinfeld of duals of finite-dimensional Lie algebras. The Kashiwara–Vergne conjecture implies (but is much stronger than) Duflo’s theorem. This raises the question of whether there is a direct proof of Duflo’s theorem in Etingof–Kazhdan formalism and whether it can be generalized to other Poisson groups. This paper is thus an affirmative answer to this question.

2
Poisson groups

To any finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over
\mathbb{K}
is associated a formal group, whose algebra of functions is by definition the dual
U(\mathfrak{g})^{*}
of the enveloping algebra. This is a topological Hopf algebra, which as an algebra is identified with the degree completion
\widehat{S}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
of the symmetric algebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, and whose coproduct

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}\colon\ \widehat{S}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\longrightarrow\widehat{S}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\hat{\otimes}\widehat{S}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}):=\widehat{S}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*})

is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}(f)(x,y):=f(\operatorname{BCH}(x,y)),

where
\operatorname{BCH}
is the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff series.

If
G
is a Lie or algebraic group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
then the algebra
\mathcal{O}(G)
of
C^{\infty}
or regular functions on

is a Hopf algebra with coproduct
1

1

In the Lie case, the tensor product is the completed one:
\mathcal{O}(G)\hat{\otimes}\mathcal{O}(G):=\mathcal{O}(G\times G)
.

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}(f)(X,Y):=f(XY).

Let

be the augmentation ideal of
\mathcal{O}(G)
, i.e., the ideal of functions vanishing at the identity. Then the Hopf algebra of functions on the formal group associated with
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to the
I

\mathcal{O}(G)
.

A multiplicative Poisson structure on a formal, Lie or algebraic group
G
is a Poisson bracket on
\mathcal{O}(G)
for which the coproduct is a map of Poisson algebra. In that case we say that
\mathcal{O}(G)
is a Poisson-Hopf algebra.
A quantization of
G
is an Hopf algebra
(\mathcal{O}_{\hslash}(G),\star_{\hslash},\Delta_{\hslash})
over the ring of formal power series
\mathbb{K}[[\hslash]]
such that:

•

\mathcal{O}_{\hslash}(G)\cong\mathcal{O}(G)[[\hslash]]
as a
\mathbb{K}[[\hslash]]
-module,

•

\forall\,f,g\in\mathcal{O}_{\hslash}(G)
,
f\star_{\hslash}g-g\star_{\hslash}f=\hslash\{f,g\}+O(\hslash^{2})
,

•

\Delta_{\hslash}=\Delta_{0}+O(\hslash)
.

A Lie bialgebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
is a linear map
\delta\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
such that the dual map is a Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and

\displaystyle\delta([a,b])=[a\otimes 1+1\otimes a,\delta(b)]+[\delta(a),b\otimes 1+1\otimes b].

A multiplicative Poisson structure on
G
induces a Lie bialgebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
, and conversely if
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie bialgebra then the cobracket induces a multiplicative Poisson structure on
G

[
6
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{d}
be the double of
\mathfrak{g}
. As a vector space this is
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and its Lie bracket is determined by the following conditions:

•

the inclusions
\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{d}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{d}
are Lie algebra maps,

•

the canonical pairing on
\mathfrak{d}
is ad-invariant.

By the PBW theorem, there is a vector space isomorphism

\displaystyle U(\mathfrak{g})\cong U(\mathfrak{d})/U(\mathfrak{d})\mathfrak{g}^{*},

which turns
U(\mathfrak{g})
into a
\mathfrak{d}
-module (this coincides with the universal Verma module
M_{-}
of the next section) hence
\mathfrak{d}
acts on the algebra of function on the formal group of
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie algebra of a Lie or algebraic group
G
, then this action is by vector fields on
G
, hence it can be globalised to an action of
\mathfrak{d}
on
\mathcal{O}(G)
is the Lie and algebraic setting as well (this is the so-called dressing action
[
18
]
). A key fact for the constructions in the next sections is that the Poisson bracket on
\mathcal{O}(G)
can be expressed as follows: let
x_{i}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
and
x^{i}
be the dual basis of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, then

\displaystyle\forall\,f,g\in\mathcal{O}(G),\qquad\{f,g\}=\sum_{i}\big(x^{i}\cdot f\big)(x_{i}\cdot g).

In particular, the following holds
[
18
]
:

Proposition 2.1
.

The Poisson center of
\mathcal{O}(G)
coincides with the subalgebra
\mathcal{O}(G)^{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}
of invariant under the dressing action of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\subset\mathfrak{d}
.

coincides with its subspace of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
-invariants. Therefore, by Frobenius reciprocity the
\mathbb{K}[[\hslash]]
-extension of the Poisson center of
\mathcal{O}(G)
coincides with
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(M_{-},\mathcal{O}(G))
. Since
M_{-}
is a coalgebra, the functor
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(M_{-},-)
is monoidal, and it is easily checked that this monoidal structure coincides with the canonical inclusion
V^{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\otimes W^{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\subset(V\otimes W)^{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}
. In the same way the map

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(M_{-},\mathcal{O}(G))\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(M_{-}\otimes M_{+},\mathcal{O}(G))

induced by the counit of
M_{+}
coincides with the inclusion
\mathcal{O}(G)^{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\subset\mathcal{O}(G)
. By Lemmas
4.3
and
4.4
, this is an algebra morphism whose image lies in the center of
\mathcal{O}_{\hslash}^{\circlearrowleft}(G)
.

On the other hand, observe that any central element of
\mathcal{O}_{\hslash}^{\circlearrowleft}(G)
belongs to the Poisson center of
\mathcal{O}(G)[[\hslash]]
: if
f
is central then for any function
g
one has

\displaystyle[f,g]=0=\hslash\{f,g\}+O\big(\hslash^{2}\big),

where
[f,g]
is the commutator in the deformed algebra
\mathcal{O}_{\hslash}^{\circlearrowleft}(G)
. This completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Remark 4.5
.

It is worth emphasizing that the map from the Poisson center of
\mathcal{O}(G)
to
\mathcal{O}_{\hslash}(G)
is not the standard inclusion. Indeed,, it relies on the isomorphism of Proposition
3.10
, which itself relies on the element
\beta
chosen in Proposition
3.4
, which thus plays the role of the Duflo element. Hence we expect
\beta
to be closely related to the Duflo function associated with
\Phi
in the sense of
[
1
]
, also known as the Gamma function of
\Phi
.

5
The Duflo isomorphism

In this section we show that applying Theorem
4.2
to the case where
G=\mathfrak{a}^{*}
for a finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
, one recovers the Duflo isomorphism. Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\hslash}
be the Lie algebra over
\mathbb{K}[[\hslash]]
which is
\mathfrak{a}[[\hslash]]
as a module, and whose bracket is
\hslash
times the bracket of
\mathfrak{a}
. Let
U(\mathfrak{a}_{\hslash})
be its enveloping algebra. By the PBW theorem, there is a coalgebra isomorphism

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{a})[[\hslash]]\cong U(\mathfrak{a}_{\hslash}).

Pulling back the product of
U(\mathfrak{a}_{\hslash})
through this isomorphism, one gets an algebra structure
\star_{\rm PBW}
on
S(\mathfrak{a})[[\hslash]]
compatible with the standard coproduct. It is easily seen that this is a quantization of the Poisson algebraic group
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
. By
[
10
, Lemma 3.2]
this is the only functorial quantization of this Poisson group. This implies

Proposition 5.1
.

The Hopf algebra
\mathcal{O}_{\hslash}(\mathfrak{a}^{*})
is canonically, naturally isomorphic to

\displaystyle(S(\mathfrak{a})[[\hslash]],\star_{\rm PBW},\Delta_{0}).

By construction the product
\star_{\rm PBW}
can be restricted to the subspace
S(\mathfrak{a})[\hslash]
of polynomials in
\hslash
. This restriction can then be specialized at
\hslash=1
and the resulting algebra is clearly the enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{a}
. On the other hand, the Poisson center of
\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{a}^{*})=S(\mathfrak{a})
coincide with the invariant under the adjoint action
S(\mathfrak{a})^{\mathfrak{a}}
. This is well-known, and consistent with Proposition
2.1
since in that case the dressing action of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}=\mathfrak{a}
coincides with the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{a}
on
S(\mathfrak{a})
. One check that the isomorphism

\displaystyle\rho\colon\ \mathcal{O}_{\hslash}(\mathfrak{a}^{*})^{\circlearrowleft}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\hslash}(\mathfrak{a}^{*})

coming from Theorem
3.12
also preserves the sub-space of polynomials in
\hslash
. Hence we get:

Corollary 5.2

(Duflo isomorphism)
.

The isomorphism

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{a})\cong U(\mathfrak{a})

given as the composition of
\rho
with the PBW isomorphism, restricts to an algebra isomorphism

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{a})^{\mathfrak{a}}\cong U(\mathfrak{a})^{\mathfrak{a}}.

Remark 5.3
.

It is known that the even part of a universal Duflo isomorphism for Lie algebras is uniquely determined and coincide with the original Duflo element introduced in
[
9
]
. Hence, in the case of an even associator one recovers the original Duflo isomorphism.

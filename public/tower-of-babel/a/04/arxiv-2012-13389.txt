Geometric Models and Variation of Weights on Moduli of Parabolic Higgs Bundles over the Riemann Sphere: a Case Study This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Mathematics of Integrable Systems: Classical and Quantum in honor of Leon Takhtajan. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/Takhtajan.html
Claudio MENESES

Abstract
We construct explicit geometric models for moduli spaces of semi-stable strongly parabolic Higgs bundles over the Riemann sphere, in the case of rank two, four marked points, arbitrary degree, and arbitrary weights. The mechanism of construction relies on elementary geometric and combinatorial techniques, based on a detailed study of orbit stability of (in general non-reductive) bundle automorphism groups on certain carefully crafted spaces. The aforementioned techniques are not exclusive to the case we examine, and this work elucidates a general approach to construct arbitrary moduli spaces of semi-stable parabolic Higgs bundles in genus 0, which is encoded into the combinatorics of weight polytopes. We also present a comprehensive analysis of the geometric models’ behavior under variation of parabolic weights and wall-crossing, which is concentrated on their nilpotent cones.

Introduction

The moduli spaces of parabolic Higgs bundles on a Riemann surface of lowest possible dimension are elliptic surfaces in virtue of their Hitchin fibrations
[
16
]
. These examples occur in low genus and encode at once, in its simplest possible form, the characteristic features of the rich and intricate geometry occuring in arbitrary dimensions. From the general theory of elliptic surfaces
[
25
]
, they are necessarily biholomorphic to one of the fibrations in Kodaira’s list. Following the seminal work of Gaiotto–Moore–Neitzke
[
8
]
, their hyperkähler geometry has recently attracted renewed interest in relation to the study of gravitational instantons of ALG type
[
7
]
.

The

There are some caveats when trying to carry out the previous biholomorphisms, though. First of all, they are not expected to hold for other ranks and number of marked points.
A nontrivial feature of parabolic Higgs bundles is the dependence of their moduli problem on a choice of parabolic weights.
While several classical results on this dependence
[
27

32

are linked to the study of the birational geometry of the moduli problem, not only they are trivial when the moduli space is a complex surface, but also they shed no light on other subtle phenomena. For instance, it is unclear how holomorphic invariants such as Harder–Narasimhan stratifications may depend on parabolic weights, or what happens to the moduli space when semi-stable parabolic bundles don’t exist
[

,

]

We propose a construction of moduli spaces of rank 2 stable parabolic Higgs bundles on
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
with four marked points, on which the dependence on parabolic weights and its wall-crossing phenomena becomes as transparent as possible. The construction is comprised of two steps. The first step is based on the observation that the peculiarities of genus 0 (namely, the infinitesimal rigidity of holomorphic bundles dictated by the Birkhoff–Grothendieck theorem) lead to the construction of Harder–Narasimhan strata for underlying holomorphic bundles
E\cong\mathcal{O}(m_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(m_{2})
as semi-stable orbit spaces under the action of bundle automorphisms, since any isomorphism class of quasi-parabolic Higgs bundles on
E
can be modeled as an orbit in an explicit complex manifold
\mathrm{QPH}(E)
with respect to an action of the group
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
of projective automorphisms of
E
.

Relation to other work

The literature on moduli spaces emerging from parabolic structures on

.

Geometry of
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))}
-actions and stratifications in
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{QP}(E)}

Let
E\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank 2 and degree
d
. A basic holomorphic invariant associated to
E
is its
Harder–Narasimhan filtration
. In genus 0, the Birkhoff–Grothendieck theorem postulates the existence of an isomorphism of vector bundles

E\cong\mathcal{O}(m_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(m_{2}),\qquad m_{1}\geq m_{2},

in such a way that
d=m_{1}+m_{2}
. Every rank 2 bundle
E
is either semi-stable (i.e., it splitting coefficients satisfy
m_{1}=m_{2}
) or otherwise admits a unique filtration of the form

E_{1}\subset E_{2}=E,\qquad\deg(E_{1})>\deg(E_{2})/2.

In other words, every isomorphism
E\cong\mathcal{O}(m_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(m_{2})
is a refinement of the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of
E
. When
E
is not semi-stable, we have that
E_{1}\cong\mathcal{O}(m_{1})
, and a choice of Birkhoff–Grothendieck splitting for
E
is equivalent to a choice of complementary sub-bundle
\mathcal{O}(m_{2})\subset E
. The integers
m_{1}\leq m_{2}
are holomorphic invariants for
E
.
E
is called
evenly-spit
if
m_{1}-m_{2}\leq 1
, or equivalently if
H^{1}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1},\End(E))=0
, where
\End(E):=E\otimes E^{\vee}
. Consequently, on any holomorphic family
\mathscr{F}\rightarrow\mathrm{B}\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
of rank 2 and degree
d
holomorphic vector bundles
E\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the existence of evenly-split bundles is an open condition.

Pointwise multiplication endows the space
H^{0}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1},\End(E))
with an associative algebra structure. The group of global bundle automorphisms of
E
is the Lie group of invertible elements

\Aut(E)\subset H^{0}\bigl(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1},\End(E)\bigr).

Its projectivization is defined as
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E)):=\Aut(E)/\mathrm{Z}(\Aut(E))
, where its center
\mathrm{Z}(\Aut(E))
consists of all nonzero multiples of the identity.
\Aut(E)\cong\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
in the special case when
E
is semi-stable. Otherwise, when
m_{1}>m_{2}
,
\Aut(E)
is
(m_{1}-m_{2}+3)
-dimensional and preserves the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of
E
. Its
unipotent radical
is the normal subgroup of unipotent automorphisms of
E
, and will be denoted by
\mathrm{R}(\Aut(E))
.
\mathrm{R}(\Aut(E))
is maximally abelian, has codimension 2 in
\Aut(E)
, and acts freely and transitively on the space
\mathrm{L}_{m_{2}}(E)
. In particular, the subgroup
\mathrm{R}(\Aut(E))\cap\mathrm{Z}(\Aut(E))
is trivial.

Consider a point
z\in\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
. For any
F\in\mathbb{P}(E|_{z})
, let
\mathrm{P}(F)\subset\mathrm{GL}(E|_{z})
be the parabolic subgroup stabilizing the flag
F\subset E|_{z}
, and
\mathrm{R}(F)
its unipotent radical. The evaluation maps

\Aut(E)\mapsto\Aut(E)|_{z}\subset\mathfrak{gl}(E|_{z})

determine a subgroup of
\GL(E|_{z})
. This subgroup coincides with
\GL(E|_{z})
when
m_{1}=m_{2}
, in which case the evaluation map is an isomorphism. Otherwise, when
m_{1}>m_{2}
,
\Aut(E)|_{z}
coincides with the parabolic subgroup
\mathrm{P}(E_{1}|_{z})
that is induced by the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of
E
.
In this case we define the affine line

\mathscr{V}_{z}(E):=\mathbb{P}(E|_{z})\backslash\{E_{1}|_{z}\}.

Since there is an isomorphism

\Aut(E)|_{z}\cong\mathrm{P}(E_{1}|_{z})
,
and
\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{R}(E_{1}|_{z}))\cong\mathbb{C}
acts freely and transitively on
\mathscr{V}_{z}(E)
, the cell decomposition

\mathbb{P}(E|_{z})=\{E_{1}|_{z}\}\sqcup\mathscr{V}_{z}(E)

is the partition into two orbits determined by the action of
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E)|_{z})
on
\mathbb{P}(E|_{z})
.

3.1
Line sub-bundles and interpolation of quasi-parabolic structures

For any
E
, we will denote the space of all line sub-bundles
L\subset E
of degree
j
by
\mathrm{L}_{j}(E)
. Given an isomorphism
E\cong\mathcal{O}(m_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(m_{2})
,
\mathrm{L}_{j}(E)
is modeled by the Zariski open set

\mathrm{L}_{j}(\mathcal{O}(m_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(m_{2}))\subset\mathbb{P}\bigl(H^{0}\big(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(m_{1}-j)\big)\oplus H^{0}\big(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(m_{2}-j)\big)\bigr)

generated by pairs of holomorphic sections
(s_{1},s_{2})
that are not simultaneously zero and have disjoint zero sets. In the particular case when
m_{1}=m_{2}=m
, we have the isomorphisms

\mathrm{L}_{m}(E)\cong\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1},\qquad\mathrm{L}_{m-1}(E)\cong\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}\backslash\mathrm{Segre}\big(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}\big).

On the other hand, when
m_{1}>m_{2}
there are no line sub-bundles
L\subset E
of degree
j
for
m_{1}>j>m_{2}
, and

\mathrm{L}_{m_{2}}(E)\cong\mathbb{A}^{m_{1}-m_{2}+1}\subset\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{m_{1}-m_{2}+1}.

Given
I\subset\{1,2,3,4\}
and
j\leq m_{2}
, we define the
\mathcal{O}(j)
-interpolation locus on
\mathrm{QP}(E)
, relative to
I
, as

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{I,j}(E):=\bigl\{(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4})\in\mathrm{QP}(E)\colon\exists L\in\mathrm{L}_{j}(E),\;F_{k}=L|_{z_{k}}\;\forall k\in I\bigr\},

(3.1)

which satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{I^{\prime},j}(E)\subset\mathrm{B}_{I,j}(E)\qquad\text{whenever}\quad I\subset I^{\prime}.

(3.2)

For any vector bundle
E
, there is a natural action of
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
on its spaces of line sub-bundles
\mathrm{L}_{j}(E)
. In the particular case when
m_{1}=m_{2}=m
, this reduces to the standard action on the projective line
\mathrm{L}_{m}(E)
by Möbius transformations, and moreover,
\mathrm{L}_{m-1}(E)
is a principal homogeneous space for
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
. Otherwise, in the case when
m_{1}>m_{2}
,
\mathrm{L}_{m_{2}}(E)
is a principal homogeneous space for
\mathrm{R}(\Aut(E))
.
Since the action of
\Aut(E)
on the spaces
\mathrm{L}_{j}(E)
commutes with the operations of evaluation, every
\mathrm{B}_{I,j}(E)
is invariant under the induced
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
-action. In the particular case when
I=\{1,2,3,4\}
, it follows from the general properties of polynomial interpolation under an arbitrary choice of bundle isomorphism
E\cong\mathcal{O}(m_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(m_{2})
that if
\dim\mathrm{L}_{j}(E)<4
, then the induced line bundle evaluation map

\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{L}_{j}(E)}\colon\quad\mathrm{L}_{j}(E)\rightarrow\mathrm{QP}(E),\qquad L\subset E\mapsto\bigl(L|_{z_{1}},L|_{z_{2}},L|_{z_{3}},L|_{z_{4}}\bigr)

is a holomorphic embedding, and

\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{L}_{j}(E)}(\mathrm{L}_{j}(E))=\mathrm{B}_{\{1,2,3,4\},j}(E).

In addition, when
m_{1}>m_{2}
,
\mathcal{O}(m_{1})
-interpolation relative to a subset
I
endows
\mathrm{QP}(E)
with the following
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
-invariant stratification

\mathrm{QP}(E)=\bigsqcup_{I\subset\{1,2,3,4\}}\mathrm{B}_{I,m_{1}}(E),\qquad\mathrm{B}_{I,m_{1}}(E):=\bigg\{\prod_{i\in I}\{E_{1}|_{z_{i}}\}\bigg\}\times\bigg\{\prod_{j\in I^{\mathsf{c}}}\mathscr{V}_{z_{i}}(E)\bigg\}.

3.2
Combinatorial stratification of orbit spaces in
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{QP}(E)}

We will now describe the action of
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
on the spaces
\mathrm{QP}(E)
. Due to the structural differences of the groups
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
depending on whether
E
is semi-simple or not, the treatment is split into the two cases
m_{1}=m_{2}
and
m_{1}>m_{2}
. In the first case, we define the
configuration space locus
to be the following Zariski open subset of
\mathrm{QP}(E)

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}(E):=\bigcap_{|I|=2}\mathrm{B}^{\mathsf{c}}_{I,m}(E)\cong\mathrm{Conf}_{4}\big(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}\big).

Proposition 3.1
.

If
m_{1}-m_{2}\leq 2
, then
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
acts freely on the Zariski open subset

\mathrm{U}(E):=\displaystyle\mathrm{QP}(E)\Big\backslash\bigcup_{I\sqcup I^{\prime}=\{1,2,3,4\}}\bigl\{\mathrm{B}_{I,m_{1}}(E)\cap\mathrm{B}_{I^{\prime},m_{2}}(E)\bigr\},

which is a
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
-principal homogeneous space if
m_{1}-m_{2}=2
, and empty if
m_{1}-m_{2}>2
. There is a
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
-invariant “cross-ratio” map
\operatorname{cr}\colon\mathrm{U}(E)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
identifying the
\mathrm{U}(E)
-orbit space with a projective line with three
(
resp. four
)
double points if
m_{1}=m_{2}

(
resp.
m_{1}-m_{2}=1)
, and such that
\operatorname{cr}(\mathrm{U}^{\prime}(E))=\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}\backslash\{z_{1},0,1,\infty\}
for

\mathrm{U}^{\prime}(E):=\begin{cases}\mathrm{C}(E)\big\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\{1,2,3,4\},m-1}(E),&m_{1}=m_{2}=m,\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{\varnothing,m_{1}}(E)\Big\backslash\bigsqcup_{|I^{\prime}|\geq 3}\mathrm{B}_{I^{\prime},m_{2}}(E),&m_{1}-m_{2}=1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Case

m_{1}=m_{2}=m
. All nonempty loci
\mathrm{B}_{I,m}(E)
provide an invariant characterization of the trivialization-dependent identities

F_{i}=F_{j},\qquad\forall\;i,j\in I,

since for any
|I|\geq 2

\mathrm{B}_{I,m}(E)=\bigcap_{I^{\prime}\subset I,\;|I^{\prime}|=2}\mathrm{B}_{I^{\prime},m}(E).

Therefore,
\mathrm{C}(E)
is the locus in
\mathrm{U}(E)
, where
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
acts properly, in such a way that
\mathrm{C}(E)/\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))\cong\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}\backslash\{0,1,\infty\}
. Moreover, the six connected components in

\mathrm{U}(E)\backslash\mathrm{C}(E)=\bigsqcup_{|I|=2}\bigl\{\mathrm{B}_{I,m}(E)\cap\mathrm{U}(E)\bigr\}

are principal homogeneous spaces for
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
, and it follows that there is a unique
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
-invariant map
\operatorname{cr}\colon\mathrm{U}(E)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
normalized in the following manner

\displaystyle\operatorname{cr}\bigl(\mathrm{B}_{\{1,2\},m}(E)\cap\mathrm{U}(E)\bigr)=\operatorname{cr}\bigl(\mathrm{B}_{\{3,4\},m}(E)\cap\mathrm{U}(E)\bigr)=0,

\displaystyle\operatorname{cr}\bigl(\mathrm{B}_{\{1,3\},m}(E)\cap\mathrm{U}(E)\bigr)=\operatorname{cr}\bigl(\mathrm{B}_{\{2,4\},m}(E)\cap\mathrm{U}(E)\bigr)=1,

\displaystyle\operatorname{cr}\bigl(\mathrm{B}_{\{1,4\},m}(E)\cap\mathrm{U}(E)\bigr)=\operatorname{cr}\bigl(\mathrm{B}_{\{2,3\},m}(E)\cap\mathrm{U}(E)\bigr)=\infty.

In turn, since
\mathrm{B}_{\{1,2,3,4\},m-1}(E)
is also a principal homogeneous space for
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
, we have that
\operatorname{cr}(\mathrm{U}^{\prime}(E))=\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}\backslash\{z_{1},0,1,\infty\}
, since under the previous
normalization

\operatorname{cr}\bigl(\mathrm{B}_{\{1,2,3,4\},m-1}(E)\bigr)=(z_{1},z_{2};z_{3},z_{4})=z_{1}.

Case
m_{1}>m_{2}
.

In general, we have that for any partition
I\sqcup I^{\prime}=\{1,2,3,4\}
and
|I|>2-m_{1}+m_{2}
,

\mathrm{B}_{I,m_{1}}(E)\cap\mathrm{B}_{I^{\prime},m_{2}}(E)=\mathrm{B}_{I,m_{1}}(E).

Moreover, when
|I|\leq 2-m_{1}+m_{2}
, the
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
-action on
\mathrm{B}_{I,m_{1}}(E)\backslash\mathrm{B}_{I^{\mathsf{c}},m_{2}}(E)
is free and proper, as it can be factored through the following intermediate affine quotients

\bigl\{\mathrm{B}_{I,m_{1}}(E)\backslash\mathrm{B}_{I^{\mathsf{c}},m_{2}}(E)\bigr\}/\mathrm{R}(\Aut(E))\cong\mathbb{C}^{3-m_{1}+m_{2}-|I|}\backslash\{0\},

on which
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))/\mathrm{R}(\Aut(E))\cong\mathbb{C}^{*}
acts in the standard way. Since we can re-express

\mathrm{U}(E)=\bigsqcup_{|I|\leq 2-m_{1}+m_{2}}\bigl\{\mathrm{B}_{I,m_{1}}(E)\backslash\mathrm{B}_{I^{\mathsf{c}},m_{2}}(E)\bigr\},

it follows that
\mathrm{U}(E)
is empty if
m_{1}-m_{2}>2
, a principal homogeneous space for
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
if
m_{1}-m_{2}=2
, and moreover, that there exists a
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
-invariant map
\mathrm{U}(E)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
when
m_{1}-m_{2}=1
, which determines an isomorphism

\mathrm{U}^{\prime}(E)/\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))\cong\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}\backslash\{z_{1},0,1,\infty\},

and the complement
\mathrm{U}(E)\backslash\mathrm{U}^{\prime}(E)
consists of eight connected components, namely

\mathrm{B}_{\varnothing,m_{1}}(E)\cap\mathrm{B}_{\{j,k,l\},m_{2}}(E)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{B}_{\{i\},m_{1}}(E)\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\{j,k,l\},m_{2}}(E),

each of which is a principal homogeneous space for
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
. These eight orbits determine four different pairs of compactification points for
\mathrm{U}^{\prime}(E)/\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

(i)

The elements of the sets

\displaystyle\mathrm{X}(E):=\displaystyle\bigcup_{|I|=2-m_{1}+m_{2}}\bigl\{\mathrm{B}_{I,m_{1}}(E)\cap\mathrm{B}_{I^{\mathsf{c}},m_{2}}(E)\bigr\}\big\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\{1,2,3,4\},m_{1}}(E),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Y}(E):=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I|=3-m_{1}+m_{2},\\
|I|=1-m_{1}+m_{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl\{\mathrm{B}_{I,m_{1}}(E)\cap\mathrm{B}_{I^{\mathsf{c}},m_{2}}(E)\bigr\}\big\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\{1,2,3,4\},m_{1}}(E)

have pointwise stabilizer subgroups
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))_{(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4})}\cong\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

(ii)

If
m_{1}-m_{2}\leq 2
then
\mathrm{X}(E)
and
\mathrm{Y}(E)
are degeneration loci for the orbits in
\mathrm{U}(E)\backslash\mathrm{U}^{\prime}(E)
, where
\mathrm{U}^{\prime}(E)=\varnothing
if
m_{1}-m_{2}=2
, i.e.,

\mathrm{X}(E)\cup\mathrm{Y}(E)\subset\overline{\mathrm{U}(E)\backslash\mathrm{U}^{\prime}(E)}.

Proof.

The first claim is verified in analogy to the proof of Proposition
3.1
. Notice that when
m_{1}>m_{2}
,
\{\mathrm{B}_{I,m_{1}}(E)\cap\mathrm{B}_{I^{\mathsf{c}},m_{2}}(E)\}\cap\mathrm{B}_{\{1,2,3,4\},m_{1}}(E)=\varnothing
,
\mathrm{X}(E)=\varnothing
if
m_{1}-m_{2}>2
, and the stabilizer of any point in
\mathrm{X}(E)
or
\mathrm{Y}(E)
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))/\mathrm{R}(\Aut(E))\cong\mathbb{C}^{*}
. The second claim is trivial when
m_{1}-m_{2}=2
. When
m_{1}=m_{2}=m
, the result follows since for any partition
I\sqcup I^{\prime}=\{1,2,3,4\}
,
|I|=2

\bigl\{\mathrm{B}_{I,m}(E)\cap\mathrm{B}_{I^{\prime},m}(E)\bigr\}\subset\overline{\mathrm{B}_{I,m}(E)\cap\mathrm{U}(E)}\cap\overline{\mathrm{B}_{I^{\prime},m}(E)\cap\mathrm{U}(E)},

while when
|I|=3

\mathrm{B}_{I,m}(E)\subset\bigcap_{I^{\prime}\subset I,\,|I^{\prime}|=2}\overline{\mathrm{B}_{I^{\prime},m}(E)\cap\mathrm{U}(E)}.

The case
m_{1}-m_{2}=1
,
|I|=1
, follows since for each partition
\{i\}\sqcup\{j,k,l\}=\{1,2,3,4\}

\mathrm{B}_{\{i\},m_{1}}(E)\cap\mathrm{B}_{\{j,k,l\},m_{2}}(E)\subset\overline{\mathrm{B}_{\varnothing,m_{1}}(E)\cap\mathrm{B}_{\{j,k,l\},m_{2}}(E)}\cap\overline{\mathrm{B}_{\{i\},m_{1}}(E)\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\{j,k,l\},m_{2}}(E)},

and is trivial when
|I|=2
or
|I|=0
from the definition of
\mathrm{U}^{\prime}(E)
.
∎

The stratification of
\mathrm{QP}(E)
can be completed by also grouping quasi-parabolic structures with stabilizer subgroups of higher dimension. We don’t describe these additional strata explicitly since they won’t appear in subsequent constructions.

4
Structural results and stratification of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{QPH}(E)}

The construction of geometric models for Harder–Narasimhan strata will be based on the following series of results on the structure of spaces of quasi-parabolic Higgs bundles. For any fixed holomorphic rank 2 bundle
E\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, let
\mathrm{QPH}(E)
denote the space of all quasi-parabolic Higgs bundles
(E_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}},\Phi)
with quasi-parabolic structure supported on
D
. We can identify
\mathrm{QPH}(E)
with an algebraic subvariety of the product

\mathrm{QP}(E)\times H^{0}\bigl(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1},\End(E)\otimes K_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}}(D)\bigr)

characterized as the locus of all points
(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4},\Phi)
satisfying the incidence constraints

\displaystyle\Res_{z_{i}}\Phi\in\mathfrak{n}(E|_{z_{i}}),\qquad F_{i}\subset\ker(\Res_{z_{i}}\Phi),\qquad i=1,2,3,4.

Equivalently, if we denote by
\mathrm{Int}(E)
the intersection of the four quadrics in the space

\bigl\{\Phi\in H^{0}\big(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1},\End(E)\otimes K_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}}(D)\big)\colon\tr(\Phi)|_{z_{i}}=0,\;i=1,2,3,4\bigr\}

defined by the equations

\det(\Phi)|_{z_{i}}=0,\qquad i=1,2,3,4,

then
\mathrm{QPH}(E)
is modeled by the blow-up of
\mathrm{Int}(E)
along each of the codimension 2 loci

\displaystyle\Res_{z_{i}}\Phi=0,\qquad i=1,2,3,4.

(4.1)

There is a natural action of
\Aut(E)
on
\mathrm{QPH}(E)
, whose point stabilizers contain
\mathrm{Z}(\Aut(E))
, descending to an action of the quotient group
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
. The proof of the following lemma is obvious.

Lemma 4.1
.

There is a bijective correspondence between isomorphism classes of quasi-parabolic Higgs bundles
\{(E_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}},\Phi)\}
and
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
-orbits in
\mathrm{QPH}(E)
.

Since in the present case
\dim H^{0}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1},K^{2}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}}(D))=1
, we will define the
bulk
as

\mathrm{QPH}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(E):=\det{}^{-1}\bigl(H^{0}\big(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1},K^{2}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}}(D)\big)\backslash\{0\}\bigr),

and the
nilpotent locus
as

\mathrm{QPH}_{0}(E):=\det{}^{-1}(0).

For the embedding
\iota\colon\mathrm{QP}(E)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{QPH}_{0}(E)
defined as
\iota:=\{0\}\times\textrm{id}
, we will denote

\mathrm{Q}(E):=\iota(\mathrm{QP}(E)),

which is also characterized as the fixed-point locus of the holomorphic involution defined as

\displaystyle\tau(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4},\Phi):=(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4},-\Phi).

(4.2)

More generally,
\mathrm{QPH}(E)
is equipped with a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action,
defined for any
c\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
as

\displaystyle c\cdot(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4},\Phi)=(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4},c\Phi),\qquad c\in\mathbb{C}^{*}.

(4.3)

Since both
\tau
and this
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action are
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
-equivariant, they descend to an involution and a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on the space of
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
-orbits of
\mathrm{QPH}(E)
. While they are only trivial on
\mathrm{Q}(E)
, we will verify that their descents
possess additional fixed loci.
The next results characterize the structure of
\mathrm{QPH}(E)
in terms of the stratification

\mathrm{QPH}(E)=\mathrm{QPH}_{0}(E)\sqcup\mathrm{QPH}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(E).

Lemma 4.2
.

A nonzero parabolic Higgs field
\Phi
preserves a line sub-bundle
L(\Phi)\subset E
if and only if it is nilpotent, in which case
L(\Phi)
is unique and determined by
\ker(\Phi)
.

Proof.

Given
\Phi\neq 0
, the equation
\Phi\cdot L=sL
with
s\in H^{0}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1},K_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}}(D))
requires that
s^{2}=-\det\Phi\in H^{0}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1},K^{2}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}}(D))
, from which
s\equiv 0
, i.e.,
\Phi
is nilpotent. Conversely, if
\Phi
is nilpotent, we can reconstruct
L(\Phi)
in a unique way. Since the zero set of
\Phi
as a holomorphic section of
\End(E)\otimes K_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}}(D)
is finite, we can associate an effective divisor
(\Phi)
to
\Phi
. The complement of such zero set is the Zariski open set
\mathscr{U}
of points
z\in\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, where
\ker(\Phi)(z)\subset E|_{z}
(or
\ker(\Res_{z_{i}}\Phi)
if
z=z_{i}
,
i=1,2,3,4
) is 1-dimensional. Hence
\ker(\Phi)|_{\mathscr{U}}
is a line sub-bundle of
E|_{\mathscr{U}}
. Let
\sigma
be a holomorphic section of the line bundle
[(\Phi)]
such that
(\sigma)=(\Phi)
. Then by construction
\sigma^{-1}\Phi
is a holomorphic section of
\End E\otimes K_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}}(D-(\Phi))
such that
\ker(\sigma^{-1}\Phi)(z)
is 1-dimensional
\forall\;z\in\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Therefore
L(\Phi):=\ker(\sigma^{-1}\Phi)
is a line sub-bundle of
E
such that
L|_{\mathscr{U}}=\ker(\Phi)|_{\mathscr{U}}
and invariant under
\Phi
over all
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
∎

Two important conclusions follow from Lemma
4.2
: when it exists, an element of
\mathrm{QPH}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(E)
can never be unstable, and moreover, the elements in
\mathrm{QPH}_{0}(E)\backslash\mathrm{Q}(E)
can be classified according to their unique invariant line sub-bundles, inducing a finer stratification of
\mathrm{QPH}_{0}(E)
that we will describe in detail.

Lemma 4.3
.

\mathrm{QPH}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(E)
is nonempty if and only if
m_{1}-m_{2}\leq 2
. Given a line sub-bundle
L\subset E
, there is a nonzero nilpotent parabolic Higgs field
\Phi
such that
L(\Phi)=L
if and only if

\displaystyle 2(\deg(L)+1)\geq\deg(E).

(4.4)

Proof.

Any given choice of bundle isomorphism
E\cong\mathcal{O}(m_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(m_{2})
induces an isomorphism

\displaystyle\End(E)\otimes K_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}}(D)\cong\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{O}(2)&\mathcal{O}(m_{1}-m_{2}+2)\\
\mathcal{O}(m_{2}-m_{1}+2)&\mathcal{O}(2)\end{pmatrix}\!,

from which it follows that a parabolic Higgs field on
E
gets characterized as a holomorphic section

\displaystyle\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}u_{2}&-v_{m_{1}-m_{2}+2}\\
w_{m_{2}-m_{1}+2}&-u_{2}\end{pmatrix}

(4.5)

whose pointwise evaluation on
D
moreover satisfies the set of nilpotency constraints

\det(\Phi)|_{z_{i}}=\bigl({-}u_{2}^{2}+v_{m_{1}-m_{2}+2}w_{m_{2}-m_{1}+2}\bigr)\big|_{z_{i}}=0,\qquad i=1,2,3,4.

It follows that the map
\det
is surjective if and only if
m_{1}-m_{2}\leq 2
, from which the first claim follows. To prove the second claim, first assume that
m_{1}>m_{2}
and
L=E_{1}
. A nonzero nilpotent parabolic Higgs field
\Phi
preserving
E_{1}
is characterized in terms of the identity
w_{m_{2}-m_{1}+2}\equiv 0
(which is independent of the choice of isomorphism
E\cong\mathcal{O}(m_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(m_{2})
) since the nilpotency constraints imply that
u_{2}\equiv 0
necessarily. Since
\Phi
is identified with a nonzero holomorphic section of
\mathcal{O}(m_{1}-m_{2}+2)
, it follows that there is always some
\Phi
such that
L(\Phi)=E_{1}
.

Otherwise, assume that
L\neq E_{1}
if
m_{1}>m_{2}
, or equivalently, that
\deg(L)\leq m_{2}
. Given a nonzero nilpotent parabolic Higgs field
\Phi
such that
L(\Phi)=L
, we can choose
\sigma_{k}\neq 0\in H^{0}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1},[(\Phi)])
for some
0\leq k\leq 2-m_{1}+m_{2}
, such that
(\sigma_{k})=(\Phi)
and express

\displaystyle\Phi=\sigma_{k}\begin{pmatrix}u_{2-k}&-v_{2-m_{2}+m_{1}-k}\\
w_{2-m_{1}+m_{2}-k}&-u_{2-k}\end{pmatrix}\!,

(4.6)

such that

6
Conditional stability and basic building blocks

The construction of geometric models for all possible Harder–Narasimhan strata as spaces of stable orbits under
\mathbb{P}(\Aut(E))
-actions, as well as the characterization of their wall-crossing under variation of parabolic weights, will be achieved by linking the classification of
conditionally stable strata
(Definition
6.3
) to the combinatorics of weight polytopes. This problem depends on a choice of degree parity for a rank 2 vector bundle. We will consider the two possibilities simultaneously.

In order to classify the geometry and topology of all orbit spaces of conditionally stable strata, which in general contain non-Hausdorff loci even after restricting the type of invariant sub-bundles, their “taming” is achieved by dissecting them into elementary Hausdorff irreducible components. The details are summarized in Definition
6.6
and Propositions
6.5
,
6.7
and
6.9
. We begin with the following corollary.

Corollary 6.1
.

If
m_{1}-m_{2}\leq 2
, then for any
\boldsymbol{\beta}:=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4})\in(0,1)^{4}
, the set
\mathrm{QPH}^{s}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(E)
of stable elements in
\mathrm{QPH}(E)
is nonempty, Zariski open, and contains
\mathrm{QPH}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(E)
. If
m_{1}-m_{2}\geq 4
, then
\mathrm{QPH}^{s}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(E)=\varnothing
with respect to any
\boldsymbol{\beta}\in[0,1)^{4}
.

Proof.

The first claim follows from Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
, since
\mathrm{QPH}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(E)\neq\varnothing
if and only if
m_{1}-m_{2}\leq 2
. If
m_{1}-m_{2}\geq 4
, Lemma
4.3
implies that
\mathrm{QPH}(E)=\mathrm{QPH}_{0}(E)=\mathrm{Q}(E)\sqcup\mathrm{R}(E)
, and every element in
\mathrm{QPH}(E)
would be destabilized by

∎

Remark 6.2
.

The absence of weight constraints for the existence of moduli spaces of semi-stable parabolic Higgs bundles in genus 0, following from Corollary
6.1
, is already implicit, in terms of the general non-abelian Hodge correspondence, in Simpson’s solution of the Deligne–Simpson problem
[
31
]
. This is a sharp contrast with moduli spaces of semi-stable parabolic bundles
[
1
,
5
]
, for which
\Phi\equiv 0
.

Corollary
6.1
states that for even degree
d=2m
, the only Birkhoff–Grothendieck splitting types that can support stable parabolic Higgs bundles are

\mathcal{O}(m)\oplus\mathcal{O}(m)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{O}(m+1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(m-1),

while for odd degree
d=2m+1
, these are

\mathcal{O}(m+1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(m)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{O}(m+2)\oplus\mathcal{O}(m-1).

It follows from general principles that stability of quasi-parabolic Higgs bundles is an invariant along a given open chamber, and moreover, that the subset of points in
\mathrm{QPH}(E)
that are stable with respect to every choice of open chamber in
\mathcal{W}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\bullet$}}}}}
is Zariski open. Nevertheless, we will verify that the intersection of the former Zariski open subset with
\mathrm{QPH}_{0}(E)
is always empty with respect to any choice of degree parity, i.e., it either coincides with
\mathrm{QPH}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(E)
when
m_{1}-m_{2}\leq 2
, or is empty when
m_{1}-m_{2}\geq 3
. For any choice of open chamber
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{W}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\bullet$}}}}}
, we will denote the stable locus associated to any
\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathcal{C}
by
\mathrm{QPH}^{s}_{\mathcal{C}}(E)
.

.

A point in
\mathrm{QPH}(E)

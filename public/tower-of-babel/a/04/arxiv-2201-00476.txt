ON INVARIANT OF THE REGULARITY INDEX OF FAT POINTS
Phan Van Thien

Abstract
We prove invariant of the regularity index of fat points under changes of the linear subspace containing the support of the fat points (Theorem 3.5 ). Then we show that Segre’s bound is attained by any set of s s non-degenerate equimultiple fat points in ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} , s ≤ n + 3 s\leq n+3 (Theorem 4.3 ). We also give an example showing that there always exists a set of n + 4 n+4 non-degenerate equimultiple fat points in ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} such that Segre’s bound is not attained (Example 4.4 ).



Key words and phrases.
Regularity index; Fat points.

In this paper, we denote by
\mathbb{P}^{n}:=\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}

\mathbb{P}^{n}

to be fat points (or a set of fat points) in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and we denote it by

The integer

is called the multiplicity of
P_{i}
;

. If

The homogeneous coordinate ring

of

R/I=\underset{t\geq 0}{\oplus}(R/I)_{t}

We call the function

to be the Hilbert function of

\mathbb{P}^{n}

Since

\dim_{K}I_{t}=\dim_{K}R_{t}-\dim_{K}(R/I)_{t}=\binom{t+n}{n}-H_{R/I}(t),

we know the dimension of linear space of all homogeneous polynomials of degree

;

The ring

The Hilbert function

, the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of the ring

If

\dim_{K}I_{t}=\binom{t+n}{n}-\underset{i=1}{\overset{s}{\sum}}\binom{m_{i}+n-1}{n}.

So, it is interesting to determine
\operatorname{reg}(Z)

From now on if

3
Invariant of the regularity index under changes of linear subspace containing the support of fat points

Let
Z=m_{1}P_{1}+\cdots+m_{s}P_{s}
be a set of fat points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and
I
be the ideal consists all homogeneous polynomials in
R
vanishing at
P_{i}\in\mathbb{P}^{n}
to order
m_{i}
;
i=1,\ldots,s
. The regularity index
\operatorname{reg}(Z)
is the least integer such that the Hilbert function

H_{R/I}(t)=\dim_{K}(R/I)_{t}=\binom{t+n}{n}-\dim_{K}I_{t}

reaches the multiplicity
e(R/I)=\underset{i=1}{\overset{s}{\sum}}\binom{m_{i}+n-1}{n}.

Note that the Hilbert function of an ideal is invariant under projective automorphisms. So, the regularity index of a set of fat points is also invariant under projective automorphisms.

Assume that
\alpha
is a
r
-dimensional linear subspace of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
containing the points
P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}
. We consider the
r
-dimensional linear subspace
\alpha
as a projective space
\mathbb{P}^{r}
containing the points
P_{1\alpha}:=P_{1},\ldots,P_{s\alpha}:=P_{s}
, and consider
Z_{\alpha}:=m_{1}P_{1\alpha}+\cdots+m_{s}P_{s\alpha}
as a set of fat points in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. We denote by
I_{\alpha}
the ideal consists all homogeneous polynomials in
R_{\alpha}:=K[X_{0},\ldots,X_{r}]
that vanish at
P_{i\alpha}
to order
m_{i}
;
i=1,\ldots,s
. The Hilbert function of
Z_{\alpha}
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
is

H_{Z_{\alpha}}(t):=H_{R_{\alpha}/I_{\alpha}}(t)=\dim_{K}(K[X_{0},\ldots,X_{r}]/I_{\alpha})_{t}=\binom{t+r}{r}-\dim_{K}(I_{\alpha})_{t}.

The multiplicity number of
Z_{\alpha}
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
is

e(R_{\alpha}/I_{\alpha}):=\underset{i=1}{\overset{s}{\sum}}\binom{m_{i}+r-1}{r}.

The regularity index of
Z_{\alpha}
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
is the least integer
t
such that

H_{R/I_{\alpha}}(t)=e(R_{\alpha}/I_{\alpha})
, and we denote it by
\operatorname{reg}(Z_{\alpha})
.

We have
e(R_{\alpha}/I_{\alpha})\leq e(R/I)
,
{\displaystyle\binom{t+r}{r}\leq\binom{t+n}{n}}
and
\dim_{K}(I_{\alpha})_{t}\leq\dim_{K}I_{t}
.
Here there are two questions:

1) Question 1:
H_{Z_{\alpha}}(t)=H_{Z}(t)
?

2) Question 2:
\operatorname{reg}(Z_{\alpha})=\operatorname{reg}(Z)
?

For Question 1, we now do not know about the relation between

and

. But if

, then

\operatorname{reg}(Z)\leq t.

\wp_{s\alpha}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1})
. If

\operatorname{reg}(R_{\alpha}/(J_{\alpha}+\wp_{s\alpha}^{m_{s}}))>b,

then by Lemma

there exists a monomial

,

,

, such that

Now we consider

in
K[X_{0},\ldots,X_{n}]
. We claim that

X_{0}^{b-i}M\notin J+\wp_{s}^{i+1}.

In fact, if
X_{0}^{b-i}M\in J+\wp_{s}^{i+1}
, then
X_{0}^{b-i}M\in\left[J+\wp_{s}^{i+1}\right]_{b}
. Consider the finite dimensional vector space
[J+\wp_{s}^{i+1}]_{b}
over the field
K
with the monomial
X_{0}^{b-i}M
being an element of it’s basis, we get

X_{0}^{b-i}M\in J\text{ or }X_{0}^{b-i}M\in\wp_{s}^{i+1}.

In case of
X_{0}^{b-i}M\in J
, then
X_{0}^{b-i}M=X_{0}^{b-i}X_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots X_{n-1}^{c_{n-1}}\in K[X_{0},\ldots,X_{n-1}]
and
X_{0}^{b-i}M
vanishing at the points
P_{j}\in\alpha
to order
m_{j}
;
j=1,\ldots,s
. Thus
X_{0}^{b-i}M\in J_{\alpha}
, this is a contradiction with
X_{0}^{b-i}M\notin J_{\alpha}+\wp_{s\alpha}^{i+1}
. In case of
X_{0}^{b-i}M\in\wp_{s}^{i+1}
, then
X_{0}^{b-i}M=X_{0}^{b-i}X_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots X_{n-1}^{c_{n-1}}\in K[X_{0},\ldots,X_{n-1}]
and
X_{0}^{b-i}M
vanishing at the points
P_{s}\in\alpha
to order

. Thus

, this is a contradiction with
X_{0}^{b-i}M\notin J_{\alpha}+\wp_{s\alpha}^{i+1}
. Thus, we have the claim

X_{0}^{b-i}M\notin J+\wp_{s}^{i+1}.

Then, by Lemma

we get

\operatorname{reg}(R/(J+\wp_{s\alpha}^{m_{s}}))>b.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

Since

T(Z)=T_{p}(Z)=T_{3}(Z)=4>T_{j}(Z);j=1,2
. So,
P_{1},\ldots,P_{6}
are in general position in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. By using the Lemma
2.8
we get

\operatorname{reg}(Z)=T(Z).

For
n=4
, then
T_{4}(Z)=4=T(Z)
. Consider the set of seven non-degenerate double points

Z=2P_{1}+\cdots+2P_{7}

in
\mathbb{P}^{4}.
By Lemma
4.1
we get

\operatorname{reg}(Z)\geq 4=T(Z).

The proof of Proposition
4.2
is completed.
∎

The Proposition
4.2
help us to prove the following theorem which shows that the Segre’s bound is attained for the regularity index of any
s+3
non-degenerate equimultiple fat points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
s\leq n+3
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
m
be a positive integer and
Z=mP_{1}+\cdots+mP_{s}
be a set of
s
non-degenerate equimultiple points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. If
s\leq n+3
, then

\operatorname{reg}(Z)=T(Z),

where

T(Z)=\max\{T_{j}(Z)|\ j=1,\ldots,n\},

T_{j}(Z):=\max\left\{\left[\frac{mq+j-2}{j}\right]|\ P_{i_{1}},\ldots,P_{i_{q}}\text{ on a }j\text{-dimensional linear subspace}\right\}.

Proof.

Since
P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}
are not on any
(n-1)
-dimensional linear subspace, we have
s\geq n+1
. We consider the three following cases for
s
.

Case
s=n+1
:
Then
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n+1}
are in general position in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. By Lemma
2.4
we get

\operatorname{reg}(Z)=2m-1=T_{1}(Z)=T(Z).

Case
s=n+2
:
Then
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n+2}
are not on a
(n-1)
-dimensional linear subspace in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. By Lemma
2.7
we get

\operatorname{reg}(Z)=T(Z).

Case
s=n+3
:
If
m\neq 2
, then by using Lemma
2.10
we get

\operatorname{reg}(mP_{1}+\cdots+mP_{n+3})=T(Z).

If
m=2
, then by Proposition
4.2
we get

\operatorname{reg}(2P_{1}+\cdots+2P_{n+3})=T(Z).

∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let

be positive integers,

. There always exists a set of

non-degenerate equimultiple fat points

T(Z)=\max\{T_{j}(Z)|\ j=1,\ldots,n\},

T_{j}(Z):=\max\left\{\left[\frac{mq+j-2}{j}\right]|\ P_{i_{1}},\ldots,P_{i_{q}}\text{ on a }j\text{-dimensional linear subspace}\right\}.

Proof.

We consider the set of fat points

Since

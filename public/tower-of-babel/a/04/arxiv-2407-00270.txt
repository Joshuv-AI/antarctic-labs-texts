Regularity of integral closures of edge ideals of weighted oriented graphs
Nguyen Cong Minh, Thanh Vu, Guangjun Zhu

Abstract
. We prove that the regularity cannot increase when taking the integral closure for edge ideals of arbitrary weighted oriented graphs.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
13B22, 13D02, 13F55

1.
Introduction

Küronya and Pintye
[
KP
]
studied the relationship between the log-canonical thresholds and the Castelnouvo-Mumford regularity of coherent sheaves of ideals in projective spaces and made the following striking conjecture

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal in a polynomial ring
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
over a field
\mathrm{k}
. Denote by
\overline{I}
the integral closure of
I
. Then

\reg(\overline{I})\leq\reg(I),

where
\reg
denotes the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity.

Integral closures of ideals also play an important role in the multiplicity and singularity theory
[
PTUV
,
R
,
T
,
UV
]
. To our knowledge, very little is known about this conjecture even for monomial ideals, partly due to the fact that computing the integral closure of an ideal is a difficult problem. In
[
MV1
]
, Minh and Vu proved that
\reg(\overline{I^{s}})=\reg(I^{s})
for natural exponents
s\leq 4
for arbitrary edge ideals of simple graphs and arbitrary natural exponents
s
when
I
is the Stanley-Reisner ideal of a one-dimensional simplicial complex. In this paper, we prove Conjecture
1.1
for edge ideals of arbitrary weighted oriented graphs. Let us recall the notion of weighted oriented graphs and their edge ideals.

Let
(D,E,\mathbf{w})
be a weighted oriented graph without isolated vertices, where
\mathbf{w}:V(D)\to\ZZ_{+}
is a weight function on the vertex set of
D
. Assume that
D
has
|V(D)|=n
vertices. We then identify the set of vertices of
D
with the set
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. The edge ideal of
(D,E,\mathbf{w})
is defined by

I(D,\mathbf{w})=\left(x_{i}x_{j}^{\mathbf{w}(j)}\mid(i,j)\in E(D)\right)\subseteq S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}].

The edge ideal of a weighted oriented graph was introduced in
[
GMSVV
,
PRT
]
, where the authors described the primary decomposition of
I(D,\mathbf{w})
and studied the unmixed and Cohen-Macaulay properties of
I(D,\mathbf{w})
. When
\mathbf{w}(j)=1
for all
j\in[n]
, then
I(D,\mathbf{w})=I(G)
is the usual edge ideal of the underlying undirected graph
G
associated with
D
. Hence, we assume that
\mathbf{w}
is nontrivial, i.e.,
\mathbf{w}(j)>1
for some vertex
j\in[n]
, which is not a source vertex of
D
.

In
[
MVZ
]
, we classified all integrally closed edge ideals of weighted oriented graphs. Indeed, when the weights are nontrivial, most of them are not integrally closed. In this paper, we prove

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(D,E,\mathbf{w})
be an arbitrary weighted oriented graph. Then

\reg\left(\overline{I(D,\mathbf{w})}\right)\leq\reg(I(D,\mathbf{w})).

Our proof relies on a careful analysis of the degree complexes associated with the integral closure of
I(D,\mathbf{w})
and a framework for comparing the regularity of monomial ideals laid out in
[
MNPTV
]
. We further compute the regularity of integral closures of arbitrary weighted oriented complete graphs.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(D,E,\mathbf{w})
be a weighted oriented complete graph on
n\geq 2
vertices. Then

\reg\left(\overline{I(D,\mathbf{w})}\right)=\max\{\mathbf{w}(j)\mid j=1,\ldots,n\}+1.

2.
Degree complexes and integral closures of monomial ideals

In this section, we discuss the Stanley-Reisner correspondence, degree complexes of monomial ideals, Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, and integral closures of monomial ideals. We refer to
[
MNPTV
,
MV1
]
for more information.

Throughout the paper, we denote by
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
a standard graded polynomial ring over an arbitrary field
\mathrm{k}
.

2.1.
Stanley-Reisner correspondence

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. For a face
F\in\Delta
, the link of
F
in
\Delta
is defined by

\lk_{\Delta}F=\{G\in\Delta\mid F\cup G\in\Delta,F\cap G=\emptyset\}.

For each subset
F
of
[n]
, let
x_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
be a squarefree monomial of
S
. We now recall the Stanley-Reisner correspondence.

(1)

For a squarefree monomial ideal
I\subseteq S
, the Stanley-Reinser complex of
I
is defined by
\Delta(I)=\{F\subseteq[n]\mid x_{F}\notin I\}
.

(2)

For a simplicial complex
\Delta
on the vertex set
[n]
, the Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\Delta
is defined by
I_{\Delta}=(x_{F}\mid F\notin\Delta)
.

(3)

The Stanley-Reisner ring of
\Delta
is
\mathrm{k}[\Delta]=S/I_{\Delta}
.

From the definition, we have the following simple properties, see e.g.,
[
S
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
I,J
be squarefree monomial ideals of
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Then

(1)

\Delta(I)
is a cone over
t\in[n]
if and only if
x_{t}
does not divide any minimal generator of
I
.

(2)

\Delta(I+J)=\Delta(I)\cap\Delta(J)
.

(3)

\Delta(I\cap J)=\Delta(I)\cup\Delta(J)
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
q
-th reduced homology group of
\Delta
with coefficients
\mathrm{k}
, denoted by
\widetilde{H}_{q}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})
, is the
q
-th homology group of the augmented oriented chain complex of
\Delta
over
\mathrm{k}
.

A simplicial complex
\Delta
is called
acyclic
if
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})=0
for all
i
.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex. Then

(1)

\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
if and only if
\Delta
is the empty complex (i.e.,
\Delta=\{\emptyset\}
).

(2)

If
\Delta
is a cone over some
t\in[n]
or
\Delta
is the void complex (i.e.,
\Delta=\emptyset
), then it is acyclic.

2.2.
Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity and degree complexes of monomial ideals

Let
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be the maximal homogeneous ideal of
S
. For a finitely generated graded
S
-module
L
, the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity (or regularity for short) of
L
is

\reg(L)=\max\{i+j\mid H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(L)_{j}\neq 0\},

where
H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(L)
denotes the
i
-th local cohomology module of
L
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
. For a non-zero proper homogeneous ideal
J
of
S
, we have
\reg(J)=\reg(S/J)+1
. When
I
is a monomial ideal, we have

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal and
x
be a variable of
S
. Then

\reg(I+(x))\leq\reg(I).

Proof.

See
[
CHHKTT
, Corollary 4.8]
or
[
MNPTV
, Corollary 2.20]
.
∎

To proceed further, we introduce some notation. For an exponent
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\NN^{n}
, we denote by
x^{\mathbf{a}}
the monomial
x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
in
S
and
|\mathbf{a}|=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}
. The support of
\mathbf{a}
is
\supp\mathbf{a}=\{i\in[n]\mid a_{i}\neq 0\}.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
S
and
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\NN^{n}
be an exponent. The degree complex of
I
in degree
\mathbf{a}
is defined by

\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)=\Delta(\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbf{a}}}).

An ideal of the form
\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
is called an associated radical of
I
. Associated radicals of monomial ideals play a fundamental role in studying the depth of monomial ideals
[
Hoc
]
. They also appear in the following result of Minh, Nam, Phong, Thuy, and Vu
[
MNPTV
]
for computing the regularity of monomial ideals.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal in
S
. Then

\reg(S/I)=\max\{|\mathbf{a}|+i\mid\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n},i\geq 0,\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}F;\mathrm{k})\neq 0\\
\text{ for some $F\in\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)$ with $F\cap\supp\mathbf{a}=\emptyset$}\}.

Proof.

Follows from Lemma 2.12 and Lemma 2.19 of
[
MNPTV
]
.
∎

Definition 2.7
.

A pair
(\mathbf{a},i)\in\NN^{n}\times\NN
is called a
critical pair
of
I
if there exists a face
F
of
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
with
F\cap\supp\mathbf{a}=\emptyset
and an index
i
such that
\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}F;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
. It is called an
extremal pair
of
I
if furthermore,
\reg(S/I)=|\mathbf{a}|+i
.

An exponent
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
is called a
critical exponent
(respectively an
extremal exponent
) of
I
if
(\mathbf{a},i)
is a critical pair (respectively an extremal pair) of
I
for some
i\geq 0
.

For a monomial
f
in
S
and
j\in[n]
,
\deg_{j}(f)
denotes the degree of
x_{j}
in
f
. For a monomial ideal
I
,
\rho_{j}(I)
is defined by

\rho_{j}(I)=\max\{\deg_{j}(u)\mid u\text{ is a minimal monomial generator of }I\}.

By
[
MNPTV
, Remark 2.13]
, we have

Remark 2.8
.

A critical exponent
\mathbf{a}
of
I
belongs to the finite set

\Gamma(I)=\{\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}\mid a_{j}<\rho_{j}(I)\text{ for all }j=1,\ldots,n\}.

The following lemma is
[
HV
, Lemma 2.9]
; we provide an argument here for completeness.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal. Assume that
(\mathbf{a},i)
is an extremal pair of
I
and
F
is a face of
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
such that
F\cap\supp\mathbf{a}=\emptyset
and
\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}F;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
. Assume that
j\in F
. Then
\reg(S/I)=\reg(S/(I,x_{j}))
.

Proof.

Let
J=I+(x_{j})
. Since
j\in F
,
j\notin\supp\mathbf{a}
. By
[
MNPTV
, Lemma 2.24]
,
\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}=\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbf{a}}}+(x_{j})
. In other words,
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
is the restriction of
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
to
[n]\setminus\{j\}
. Let
F^{\prime}=F\setminus\{j\}
. For simplicity of notation, we set
\Delta=\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}F^{\prime}
and
\Gamma=\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)}F^{\prime}
. Then we have
\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}F=\lk_{\Delta}\{j\}
and
\Delta=\Gamma\cup\{j\}*\lk_{\Delta}\{j\}.
Note that
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Delta)=0
, otherwise,
(\mathbf{a},i+1)
is a critical pair of
I
, which is a contradiction to Lemma
2.6
. From the Mayer-Vietoris sequence, we deduce a long exact sequence

0\to\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\lk_{\Delta}\{j\};\mathrm{k})\to\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\Gamma;\mathrm{k})\to\cdots.

Hence,
\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\Gamma;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
. In other words,
(\mathbf{a},i)
is a critical pair of
J
. By Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.6
, we have

\reg(S/J)\geq|\mathbf{a}|+i=\reg(S/I)\geq\reg(S/J).

The conclusion follows.
∎

2.3.
Integral closures of monomial ideals

We recall the definition and some properties of integral closures of monomial ideals; see
[
E
, Section 4]
and
[
SH
]
for more details. We refer to
[
Hoa
]
and
[
Tr
]
for beautiful expositions on the regularity and depth of integral closures of powers of monomial ideals.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
S
. The exponent set of
I
is
E(I)=\{\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}\mid x^{\mathbf{a}}\in I\}
. The Newton polyhedron of
I
, denoted by
\NP(I)
, is the convex hull of the exponent set of
I
in
\RR^{n}
.

The following result is standard, see e.g.
[
E
, Exercise 4.23]
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
S
. The integral closure of
I
is a monomial ideal with exponent set
E(\overline{I})=\NP(I)\cap\ZZ^{n}
.

For
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}),\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})\in\RR^{n}
, the notation
\mathbf{a}\geq\mathbf{b}
means that
a_{j}\geq b_{j}
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal and
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
be an exponent. Then
x^{\mathbf{a}}\in\overline{I}
if and only if there exist non-negative rational numbers
c_{j}
and exponents
\mathbf{b}_{j}
of
E(I)
for
j=1,\ldots,s
such that
\sum_{j=1}^{s}c_{j}\geq 1
and
\mathbf{a}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{s}c_{j}\mathbf{b}_{j}
.

Proof.

The conclusion follows from Lemma
2.11
.
∎

For a monomial
f
in
S
, the support of
f
, denoted by
\supp(f)
, is the set of all indices
i\in[n]
such that
x_{i}\mid f
. For a monomial ideal
J
of
S
and a subset
V
of
[n]
, the restriction of
J
to
V
, denoted by
J_{V}
, is

J_{V}=(f\mid f\text{ is a minimal generator of }J\text{ such that }\supp f\subseteq V).

We have

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal. Then

\overline{I_{V}}=(\overline{I})_{V}.

3.
Edge ideals of weighted oriented graphs and their integral closures

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
and compute the regularity of integral closures of edge ideals of weighted oriented complete graphs.

3.1.
Edge ideals of weighted oriented graphs and their associated radicals

Let
D
denote a simple oriented graph without isolated vertices over the vertex set
V(D)=[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and the edge set
E(D)
. We denote by
G
the underlying undirected graph associated with
D
. In other words,
V(G)=V(D)=[n]
and
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
if and only if either
(i,j)
or
(j,i)
is an edge of
D
. A subset
U\subseteq V(D)
is called an independent set of
D
if it is an independent set of
G
. For a vertex
i\in V(D)
, the in and out neighborhoods of
i
are defined by

N_{D}^{-}(i)=\{j\in V(D)\mid(j,i)\in E(D)\},~N_{D}^{+}(i)=\{j\in V(D)\mid(i,j)\in E(D)\}.

When
N_{D}^{-}(i)=\emptyset
(respectively
N_{D}^{+}(i)=\emptyset
), we call
i
a source vertex (respectively a sink vertex) of
D
. For a subset
U
of
V(D)
, we denote by
N_{D}^{-}(U)
and
N_{D}^{+}(U)
the in and out neighborhoods of
U
. We refer to
[
D
]
for more information on graph theory.

Let
\mathbf{w}:V(D)\to\ZZ_{+}
be a weight function on the vertices of
D
such that
\mathbf{w}(j)=1
when
j
is a source vertex. Let
V^{+}(D)
be the set of vertices of
D
with nontrivial weights, i.e.,
\mathbf{w}(j)>1
. The edge ideal of
(D,E,\mathbf{w})
is defined by

I(D,\mathbf{w})=(x_{i}x_{j}^{\mathbf{w}(j)}\mid(i,j)\in E(D))\subseteq S.

When
\mathbf{w}(j)=1
for all
j
, we have
I(D,\mathbf{w})=I(G)
is the usual edge ideal of
G
. We also denote by
I(D)
the edge ideal of
D
, i.e.,
I(D)=I(G)
. By definition, we have
\rho_{j}(I(D,\mathbf{w}))=\mathbf{w}(j)
for all
j\in V(D)
. By Remark
2.8
and
[
MV1
, Lemma 2.9]
, the critical exponents
\mathbf{a}
of
I(D,\mathbf{w})
and
\overline{I(D,\mathbf{w})}
satisfy
a_{j}<\mathbf{w}(j)
for all
j\in V(D)
. We start by analyzing associated radicals of edge ideals of weighted oriented graphs.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(D,E,\mathbf{w})
be a weighted oriented graph. Let
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
be an exponent such that
a_{j}<\mathbf{w}(j)
for all
j\in V(D)
. We denote by
U=N_{D}^{+}(\supp\mathbf{a})
and
G\backslash U
the induced subgraph of
G
on
V(G)\setminus U
. Then

\sqrt{I(D,\mathbf{w}):x^{\mathbf{a}}}=I(G\backslash U)+(x_{i}\mid i\in U).

Proof.

Let
J=\sqrt{I(D,{\mathbf{w}}):x^{\mathbf{a}}}
. By
[
MNPTV
, Lemma 2.24]
, the generators of
J
are
x_{k}x_{l}
with
\{k,l\}\in E(G)
and
x_{i}
for some
i\in[n]
. Since
a_{i}<\mathbf{w}(i)
, we deduce that
x_{i}\in J
if and only if there exists an index
j
such that

x_{i}=\sqrt{x_{j}x_{i}^{\mathbf{w}(i)}/\gcd(x_{j}x_{i}^{\mathbf{w}(i)},x^{\mathbf{a}})}.

In particular, we must have
a_{j}>0
. The conclusion follows.
∎

We now define induced weight functions on induced oriented subgraphs of
D
. Let
\mathbf{w}:V(D)\to\ZZ_{+}
be a weight function on the vertices of
D
and
D_{U}
be the induced oriented subgraph of
D
on
U\subseteq V(D)
. The induced weight function of
\mathbf{w}
on
V(D_{U})
, denoted by
\mathbf{w}_{U}
, is defined by

\mathbf{w}_{U}(j)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{w}(j)&\text{ if }j\text{ is not a source vertex in }D_{U},\\
1&\text{ if }j\text{ is a source vertex in }D_{U}.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
U
be a subset of
V(D)
. Let
D_{U}
and
\mathbf{w}_{U}
be the induced subgraph of
D
and the induced weight function of
\mathbf{w}
on
U
. Then

I(D,\mathbf{w})+(x_{i}\mid i\notin U)=I(D_{U},\mathbf{w}_{U})+(x_{i}\mid i\notin U).

Proof.

It suffices to note that for any
j,k\in U
,
x_{j}x_{k}^{\mathbf{w}(k)}
is a generator of
I(D,\mathbf{w})
if and only if
x_{j}x_{k}^{\mathbf{w}(k)}
is a generator of
I(D_{U},\mathbf{w}_{U})
.
∎

3.2.
Integral closures of edge ideals of weighted oriented graphs

In this subsection, we first prove a technical result about associated radicals of integral closures of edge ideals of weighted oriented graphs. We then prove Theorem
1.2
. Let
(D,E,\mathbf{w})
be a weighted oriented graph and
\mathbf{a}
be an exponent such that
U=\supp\mathbf{a}
is an independent set of
D
consisting of sink vertices only. For each subset
W
of
U
, we define the
\mathbf{a}
-capacity of
W
by

c(W)=\sum_{j\in W}\frac{a_{j}}{\mathbf{w}(j)}.

For any subset
Z\subseteq V(D)
, we denote by
(Z)
the ideal
(Z)=(x_{j}\mid j\in Z)
. Then, we define the ideal
n(W)
by

n(W)=\bigcap_{j\in W}(N_{D}^{-}(j))=\bigcap_{j\in W}(x_{k}\mid k\in N_{D}^{-}(j)).

Note that
j\in W
is a sink vertex, so
N_{D}(j)=N_{D}^{-}(j)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
U=\supp\mathbf{a}
is an independent set consisting of sink vertices of
D
only. We denote by
\mathcal{U}
the set of minimal subsets
W\subseteq U
such that
c(W)\geq 1
. Then

\sqrt{\overline{I(D,\mathbf{w})}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}=I(G)+\sum_{W\in\mathcal{U}}n(W).

Let us consider an example before proving this technical result.

Example 3.4
.

Let
(D,E,\mathbf{w})
be a weighted oriented graph whose edge ideal is

I=(x_{1}x_{2},x_{1}x_{3},x_{1}x_{4},x_{2}x_{5},x_{2}x_{6},x_{2}x_{7}^{4},x_{2}x_{10}^{6},x_{3}x_{7}^{4},x_{4}x_{7}^{4},x_{4}x_{8}^{7},x_{5}x_{8}^{7},x_{5}x_{9}^{4},x_{6}x_{10}^{6}).

The nontrivial weights of
D
are
\mathbf{w}(7)=4,\mathbf{w}(8)=7,\mathbf{w}(9)=4
and
\mathbf{w}(10)=6
. Let
x^{\mathbf{a}}=x_{7}^{2}x_{8}^{3}x_{9}x_{10}^{3}
. In particular,
U=\{7,8,9,10\}
is an independent set consisting of sink vertices of
D
only. Then, we can check that the minimal subsets
W\subseteq U
for which
c(W)\geq 1
are
\{7,8,9\}
,
\{7,10\}
and
\{8,9,10\}
. Furthermore, we have

N_{D}^{-}(7)=\{2,3,4\},N_{D}^{-}(8)=\{4,5\},N_{D}^{-}(9)=\{5\},\text{ and }N_{D}^{-}(10)=\{2,6\}.

Using Macaulay2
[
M2
]
, we can check that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\overline{I}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{I}+(x_{2},x_{3}x_{5},x_{3}x_{6},x_{4}x_{5},x_{4}x_{6},x_{5}x_{6})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{I}+n(\{7,8,9\})+n(\{7,10\})+n(\{8,9,10\}).

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

We denote by
\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{n}
the canonical basis of
\RR^{n}
. First, we prove that
n(W)\subseteq\sqrt{\overline{I(D,\mathbf{w})}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
for all subset
W\subseteq U
such that
c(W)\geq 1
. Let
f=x^{\mathbf{b}}
be a minimal generator of
n(W)
. We may assume that
\supp\mathbf{b}
is an independent set. By Corollary
2.13
, we may assume that
V(D)=W\cup\supp\mathbf{b}
. Since
W
is an independent set, the underlying graph
G
is bipartite. For simplicity of notation, we assume that
\supp\mathbf{a}=W=\{1,\ldots,s\}
and
\supp\mathbf{b}=\{s+1,\ldots,n\}
. By assumption, we have

c(W)=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\frac{a_{j}}{\mathbf{w}(j)}\geq 1.

In particular, we may choose positive rational numbers
\gamma_{j}\leq a_{j}/\mathbf{w}(j)
for all
j=1,\ldots,s
such that
\sum_{j=1}^{s}\gamma_{j}=1
. For each edge
(k,j)
in
D
with
k\in\supp\mathbf{b}
and
j\in\supp\mathbf{a}
, we set
\gamma_{kj}=\gamma_{j}/|N^{-}_{D}(j)|
. With this definition, we have

\sum_{k\in\supp\mathbf{b},j\in N^{+}_{D}(k)}\gamma_{kj}=1.

Furthermore, each edge
(k,j)
gives rise to a vector
\mathbf{c}_{kj}=\mathbf{e}_{k}+\mathbf{w}(j)\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\NP(I(D,\mathbf{w}))
. Let
\mathbf{c}=\sum_{kj}\gamma_{kj}\mathbf{c}_{kj}
. For all
\ell=1,\ldots,s
, we have

a_{\ell}\geq\left(\sum_{k\in N^{-}_{D}(\ell)}\gamma_{k\ell}\right)\mathbf{w}(\ell)=c_{\ell}.

By Lemma
2.12
, we deduce that
x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{s}^{a_{s}}x_{s+1}\cdots x_{n}\in\overline{I(D,\mathbf{w})}
.

Conversely, let
f=x^{\mathbf{b}}
be a minimal generator of
\sqrt{\overline{I(D,\mathbf{w})}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
. We need to prove that
f\in I(G)+\sum_{W\in\mathcal{U}}n(W)
. Again, we may assume that
\supp\mathbf{b}
is an independent set. By Lemma
2.12
, there exist positive rational numbers
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{t}
and vectors
\mathbf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{t}
corresponding to minimal generators of
I(D,\mathbf{w})
such that

(3.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\beta_{i}=1\text{ and }a_{j}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{t}\beta_{i}c_{ij}\text{ for all }j\in[n]\backslash\supp\mathbf{b},

where
a_{j}=0
if
j\notin\supp\mathbf{a}\cup\supp\mathbf{b}
. In particular,
\supp\mathbf{c}_{i}\subseteq\supp\mathbf{a}\cup\supp\mathbf{b}
. Since
\supp\mathbf{a}
and
\supp\mathbf{b}
are independent sets, we deduce that
\mathbf{c}_{i}=\mathbf{e}_{k}+\mathbf{w}(j)\mathbf{e}_{j}
for some
k\in\supp\mathbf{b}
and
j\in\supp\mathbf{a}
. Let
W=\cup_{i=1}^{t}\supp\mathbf{c}_{i}\setminus\supp\mathbf{b}
. Then, we have
W\subseteq\supp\mathbf{a}
. We may replace
\mathbf{c}_{i}
by
\mathbf{c}_{kj}
and
\beta_{i}
by
\beta_{kj}
for
k\in\supp\mathbf{b}\backslash\supp\mathbf{a}
and
j\in N_{D}^{+}(k)
. Eq. (
3.1
) can be rewritten as

\sum_{kj}\beta_{kj}=1\text{ and }a_{j}\geq\sum_{kj}\beta_{kj}\mathbf{w}(j).

Equivalently,

c(W)=\sum_{j\in W}\frac{a_{j}}{\mathbf{w}(j)}\geq 1.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

. Assume that

only. Let
\mathcal{T}
be any non-empty collection of non-empty subsets

\Delta(I+\sum_{W\in\mathcal{T}}n(W))

Proof.

We prove by induction on
|U|
. Since
U
consists of sink vertices of
D
only, we have
N_{D}^{-}(j)=N_{D}(j)=N_{G}(j)
for all
j\in U
. For simplicity of notation, we denote by

the neighborhood of
j
in this proof. When
U
consists of a single vertex, say
U=\{u\}
. By assumption,
\mathcal{T}
is non-empty, hence
\mathcal{T}
consists of the single set

. In this case, we have
n(W)=(w\mid w\in N(u))
. By Lemma
2.1
,
\Delta(I+n(W))

Now, assume that
|U|\geq 2
. By induction, we may assume that every element of

belongs to some subset

in
\mathcal{T}
. For simplicity of notation, assume that

\mathcal{U}=\{W\in\mathcal{T}\mid 1\notin W\}\text{ and }\mathcal{W}=\{W\subseteq U\setminus\{1\}\mid\{1\}\cup W\in\mathcal{T}\}.

\mathcal{U}

In this subsection, we assume that

. Thus, we assume that

in the sequel. Since

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(D,E,\mathbf{w})
be a weighted oriented complete graph on

vertices. Then

\reg(I(D,\mathbf{w}))=\begin{cases}|\mathbf{w}|-n+2&\text{ if }D\text{ is of type }1,\\
|\mathbf{w}|-n+1&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

For simplicity of notation, we denote by
I=I(D,\mathbf{w})
. Let
(\mathbf{a},i)
be an extremal exponent of
I
. Since
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
is a subcomplex of
\Delta(I)
, which is the disjoint union of
n
points, we deduce that
i=0
or
i=1
. Furthermore, by Remark
2.8
, we have that
a_{j}\leq\mathbf{w}(j)-1
for all
j
. Hence,

\reg(I)=|\mathbf{a}|+i+1\leq|\mathbf{w}|-n+2.

First, assume that
D
is of type
1
. We may assume that
1
is a source vertex of
D
and
2
is a source vertex of
D\setminus 1
. Let
a_{j}=\mathbf{w}(j)-1
for all
j
. Then
1,2\notin\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
. In other words, we have
\widetilde{H}_{0}(\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I);\mathrm{k})\neq 0
. By Lemma
2.6
, we deduce that
\reg(I)\geq|\mathbf{w}|-n+2
.

Now, assume that
D
is not of type 1. There are two cases.

Case 1.

D
has no source vertices. Let
a_{j}=\mathbf{w}(j)-1
for all

3.1
,
x_{j}\in\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
for all

. In other words,

is the empty complex. Hence,

is a critical pair of

.

Case 2.

has a source vertex. Assume that

is a source vertex of

. By assumption,

has no source vertices. Let
a_{j}=\mathbf{w}(j)-1
for all

is a source vertex of

. Hence,

is a critical pair of

with

.

In both cases, by Lemma
2.6

. Furthermore, for any other exponent

Let

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
(D,E,\mathbf{w})
be a weighted oriented complete graph on
n\geq 3
vertices. Assume that
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
is an exponent such that

for all

,
|\mathbf{a}|\geq\max\{\mathbf{w}(j)\mid j=1,\ldots,n\}+1
and

. Then

.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
n\geq 3
. Let
J=\overline{I(D,\mathbf{w})}
and
\omega=\max\{\mathbf{w}(j)\mid j=1,\ldots,n\}
. By Corollary
2.13
, we may assume that
\supp\mathbf{a}=[n]
. First, assume that
D
has a source vertex, say
1
. Then
\mathbf{w}(1)=1
and
a_{1}=1
. In other words, we have
\sum_{j=2}^{n}a_{j}\geq\omega
. In particular, we can choose positive rational numbers

for

such that

and
\sum_{j=2}^{n}c_{j}=1
. By Lemma

, we deduce that

. Thus, we assume that
D
has no source vertex.

We denote by

the canonical basis of

and define

By Lemma
2.11
and the fact that
Q
and
\NP(I(D,\mathbf{w}))
are convex sets, it suffices to prove that the vertices of
Q
belong to
\NP(I(D,\mathbf{w}))
. If
a_{j}=\mathbf{w}(j)
for some
j
, then
\mathbf{a}\in\NP(I(D,\mathbf{w}))
as
\supp\mathbf{a}=[n]
and
D
has no source vertex. Hence, we may assume that
\mathbf{w}(j)>a_{j}\geq 1
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
.

First, consider the case where
n=3
. Since
D
has no source vertex, we may assume that
I(D,\mathbf{w})=(x_{1}x_{2}^{\mathbf{w}(2)},x_{2}x_{3}^{\mathbf{w}(3)},x_{3}x_{1}^{\mathbf{w}(1)})
and
\mathbf{w}(3)=\omega
. Let
\mathbf{a}
be a vertex of
Q
. Since
a_{j}<\mathbf{w}(j)
for all
j
, we deduce that
\mathbf{a}=(1,1,\omega-1)
. The other possible vertices
(1,\omega-1,1)
and
(\omega-1,1,1)
are of the same form. By Lemma
2.12
, we need to prove that there exist nonnegative numbers
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
such that
c_{1}+c_{2}+c_{3}=1
and

\mathbf{a}\geq c_{1}(\mathbf{e}_{1}+\mathbf{w}(2)\mathbf{e}_{2})+c_{2}(\mathbf{e}_{2}+\mathbf{w}(3)\mathbf{e}_{3})+c_{3}(\mathbf{e}_{3}+\mathbf{w}(1)\mathbf{e}_{1}).

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle+\mathbf{w}(1)c_{3}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}(2)c_{1}

\displaystyle+c_{2}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle+c_{2}

\displaystyle+c_{3}

\displaystyle=1.

Then

c_{3}=\frac{\mathbf{w}(2)-1}{\mathbf{w}(2)\mathbf{w}(1)-\mathbf{w}(1)+1}\text{ and }c_{2}=(\mathbf{w}(1)-1)c_{3}.

We need to prove that

Now, assume that

\mathbf{a}

3.8

D

Remark 3.10
.

(1)

The inequality in Theorem
1.2
is in general strict, even for complete graphs, as shown by Theorem

and Lemma

. For example, consider the ideal

and
\reg(I)=12
.

(2)

Duan, Zhu, Cui, and Li
[
DZCL
]
classified all integrally closed edge ideals of edge-weighted graphs. Most of them are also not integrally closed when the weights are nontrivial. Note that the integral closure of

when
\mathbf{w}(e)=t
for all edges
e\in E(G)
is the same as the integral closure of
I(G)^{t}
. Hence, proving Conjecture

for edge ideals of edge-weighted graphs is an interesting problem.

(3)

By the results of
[
MV1
,
MV2
]
, it is expected that the regularity of integral closures of weighted hypergraphs associated with one-dimensional simplicial complexes are nice. We will carry this analysis in subsequent work.

Consecutive Piatetski-Shapiro primes based on the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture
Victor Zhenyu Guo, Yuan Yi

Abstract
. The Piatetski-Shapiro sequences are of the form 𝒩 ( c ) := ( ⌊ n c ⌋ ) n = 1 ∞ \mathcal{N}^{(c)}:=(\lfloor n^{c}\rfloor)_{n=1}^{\infty} with c > 1 , c ∉ ℕ c>1,c\not\in\mathbb{N} . Let p n p_{n} be the sequence of primes in ascending order. In this paper, we study the distribution of pairs ( p n , p n + 1 ) (p_{n},p_{n+1}) of consecutive primes such that p n ∈ 𝒩 ( c 1 ) p_{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{(c_{1})} and p n + 1 ∈ 𝒩 ( c 2 ) p_{n+1}\in\mathcal{N}^{(c_{2})} for c 1 , c 2 ∈ ( 1 , 2 ) c_{1},c_{2}\in(1,2) , c 1 ≠ c 2 c_{1}\neq c_{2} and give a conjecture with the prime counting functions of the pairs ( p n , p n + 1 ) (p_{n},p_{n+1}) . We give a heuristic argument to support this prediction based on a model by Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan which relies on a strong form of the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture. Moreover, we prove a proposition related to the average of singular series with a weight of a complex exponential function.



MSC Numbers:
11N05, 11B83.

Keywords:
Piatetski-Shapiro sequences, consecutive primes, Hardy-Littlewood conjectures, singular series.

1.
Introduction

The Piatetski-Shapiro sequences are sequences of the form

\mathcal{N}^{(c)}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\left\lfloor n^{c}\right\rfloor)_{n=1}^{\infty}\qquad(c>1,~c\not\in\mathbb{N}).

Piatetski-Shapiro
[
6
]
proved that if
c\in(1,\frac{12}{11})
the counting function

\pi^{(c)}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|\big\{\text{\rm prime~}p\leqslant x:p\in\mathcal{N}^{(c)}\big\}|

satisfies the asymptotic relation

\pi^{(c)}(x)\sim\frac{x^{1/c}}{\log x}\qquad\text{~\rm as }x\to\infty.

The admissible range for
c
of the above formula has been extended many times and is currently known to hold for all
c\in(1,\frac{2817}{2426})
thanks to Rivat and Sargos
[
7
]
. Rivat and Wu
[
8
]
also showed that there are infinitely many Piatetski-Shapiro primes for
c\in(1,\frac{243}{205})
. We refer the readers to see
[
3
]
for more details of the improvements of
c
. The asymptotic relation is expected to hold for all values of
c\in(1,2)
. The estimation of Piatetski-Shapiro primes is an approximation of the well-known conjecture that there exist infinitely many primes of the form
n^{2}+1
.

For a better understanding of the distribution of primes, it is natural to study consecutive primes, for example the twin prime conjecture. In this article, to understand the distribution of Piatetski-Shapiro primes, we are interested in the counting function of consecutive primes in Piatetski-Shapiro sequences. Let
p_{n}
be the sequence of primes in ascending order. Let
\mathbf{c}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(c_{1},c_{2},\cdots,c_{r})
with real numbers
c_{i}\in(1,2)
for all integers
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
. We define that

(1.1)

\pi(x;\mathbf{c})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|\{p_{n}\leqslant x:p_{n+i-1}\in\mathcal{N}^{(c_{i})}\text{~for~}1\leqslant i\leqslant r\}|

and aim at the behaviour of this counting function.

Our idea is inspired by a breakthrough in 2016 by Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan
[
4
]
. Let
q\geqslant 3
and
\mathbf{a}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{r})
with
(a_{i},q)=1
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
. Applying a model based on a modified version of the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture, Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan
[
4
]
investigated the biases in the occurrence of the pattern
\mathbf{a}
in strings of
r
consecutive primes reduced modulo
q
. In fact, they analyzed the counting function

\pi(x;q,\mathbf{a})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|\{p_{n}\leqslant x:p_{n+i-1}\equiv a_{i}\bmod q\text{ for }1\leqslant i\leqslant r\}|.

The method has been applied to analyze other consecutive sequences. David, Devin, Nam and Schlitt
[
2
]
applied Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan’s method to study consecutive sums of two squares. Let

\mathbb{E}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{a^{2}+b^{2}:a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{E_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

By the Hardy-Littlewood conjectures in arithmetic progressions for sum of two squares, they gave a heuristic argument of a conjecture of the counting function

|\{E_{n}\leqslant x:E_{n}\equiv a\pmod{q},E_{n+1}\equiv b\pmod{q}\}|.

For any given real numbers
\alpha>0
and
\beta\geqslant 0
, the associated (generalized) Beatty sequence is defined by

\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,\beta}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\big(\left\lfloor\alpha m+\beta\right\rfloor\big)_{m\in\mathbb{N}},

which is also called the generalized arithmetic progression. Banks and Guo
[
1
]
gave a conjecture of the estimation of the counting function

\big|\{p_{n}\leqslant x:p_{n}\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,\beta}\text{~and~}p_{n+1}\in\mathcal{B}_{\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta}}\}\big|

by a heuristic argument based on the method of Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan.

In this article, we apply a similar model to give a conjecture to the counting function (
1.1
). In particular, the case for
r=2
is a simple and interesting case. Fix real numbers
c_{1},c_{2}\in(1,2)
. We write that

(1.2)

\pi\big(x;(c_{1},c_{2})\big)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\big|\{p_{n}\leqslant x:p_{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{(c_{1})}\text{~and~}p_{n+1}\in\mathcal{N}^{(c_{2})}\}\big|

We shall give a deatiled heuristic argument of the following conjecture in Section
3
with a data analysis in Section
6
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

For any fixed positive number
\varepsilon>0
and
c_{1}\neq c_{2}
, the counting function
(
1.2
)
satisfies that

\pi\big(x;(c_{1},c_{2})\big)=\frac{x^{1/c_{1}+1/c_{2}-1}}{c_{1}c_{2}\log x}+O\left(\frac{x^{1/c_{1}+1/c_{2}-1}}{(\log x)^{3/2-\varepsilon}}\right),

where the implied constant depends only on
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
and
\varepsilon
.

Remark.

The case that
c_{1}=c_{2}
is easy to be excluded. By the well-believed Cramér’s model, it follows that
p_{n+1}-p_{n}\ll\log^{2}p_{n}
. If Conjecture
1.1
holds for the case when
c_{1}=c_{2}=c
, let
p_{n+1}=\left\lfloor n_{1}^{c}\right\rfloor,p_{n}=\left\lfloor n_{2}^{c}\right\rfloor,c>1.
We derive that

p_{n+1}-p_{n}=\left\lfloor n_{1}^{c}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor n_{2}^{c}\right\rfloor>n_{1}^{c}-1-n_{2}^{c}\geqslant(n_{2}+1)^{c}-1-n_{2}^{c}\gg n_{2}^{c-1}.

Since
n_{2}\sim{p_{n}}^{1/c}
, it gives that
p_{n+1}-p_{n}\gg{p_{n}}^{\frac{c-1}{c}}
, which conflicts the Cramér’s model.

In Section
5
, we also give a sketch of a more general conjecture.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Given any fixed positive number
\varepsilon>0
and
c_{i}\neq c_{j}
for
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant r
, the counting function (
1.1
) satisfies that

\pi(x;\mathbf{c})=\frac{x^{1/c_{1}+\dots+1/c_{r}-r+1}}{c_{1}\cdots c_{r}\log x}+O\bigg(\frac{x^{1/c_{1}+\dots+1/c_{r}-r+1}}{(\log x)^{3/2-\varepsilon}}\bigg),

where the implied constant depends only on
c_{1}
, …,
c_{r}
and
\varepsilon
.

In what follows we give a short survey of the breakthrough of Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan’s biases. We will end the introduction by the main proposition and key improvement to this topic.

1.1.
The Hardy-Littlewood conjecture

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}
, and let
\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}
denote the indicator function of the primes. A strong form of the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture for
\mathcal{H}
asserts that the estimate

(1.3)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h)=\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})\intop\nolimits_{2}^{x}\frac{du}{(\log u)^{|\mathcal{H}|}}+O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})

holds for every fixed
\varepsilon>0
, where
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})
is the singular series given by

\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\prod_{p}\bigg(1-\frac{|(\mathcal{H}\bmod p)|}{p}\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{1}{p}\bigg)^{-|\mathcal{H}|}.

For their work on primes in short intervals, Montgomery and Soundararajan
[
5
]
have introduced the modified singular series

\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{H})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{H}}(-1)^{|\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{T}|}\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{T}),

for which one has the relation

\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})=\sum_{\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{H}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{T}).

Note that
\mathfrak{S}(\varnothing)=\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\varnothing)=1
. The Hardy-Littlewood conjecture (
1.3
) can be reformulated in terms of the modified singular series as follows:

(1.4)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\bigg(\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h)-\frac{1}{\log n}\bigg)=\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{H})\intop\nolimits_{2}^{x}\frac{du}{(\log u)^{|\mathcal{H}|}}+O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon}).

1.2.
A modified Hardy-Littlewood conjecture with congruence conditions

To investigate the distribution of primes in arithmetic progressions, we introduce a modification of the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture with congruence conditions
(\bmod\,q)
from Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan’s model
[
4
]
. For any integer
q\geqslant 1
and a finite subset
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathbb{Z}
, define the singular series away from
q
by

\mathfrak{S}_{q}(\mathcal{H})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\prod_{p\nmid q}\bigg(1-\frac{|(\mathcal{H}\bmod p)|}{p}\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{1}{p}\bigg)^{-|\mathcal{H}|}.

We require that
a\pmod{q}
is such that
(h+a,q)=1
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, then it asserts that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n<x\\
n\equiv a\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h)\sim\mathfrak{S}_{q}(\mathcal{H})\bigg(\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\bigg)^{|\mathcal{H}|}\frac{1}{q}\intop\nolimits_{2}^{x}\frac{dy}{(\log y)^{|\mathcal{H}|}}.

Now similar to
\mathfrak{S}_{0}
, define

\mathfrak{S}_{q,0}(\mathcal{H})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{H}}(-1)^{|\mathcal{H}\backslash\mathcal{T}|}\mathfrak{S}_{q}(\mathcal{T}),

which gives that

\mathfrak{S}_{q}(\mathcal{H})=\sum_{\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{H}}\mathfrak{S}_{q,0}(\mathcal{T}).

Conditioning
(h+a,q)=1
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, we expect that

(1.5)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n<x\\
n\equiv a\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\bigg(\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h)-\frac{q}{\varphi(q)\log n}\bigg)\sim\mathfrak{S}_{q,0}(\mathcal{H})\bigg(\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\bigg)^{|\mathcal{H}|}\frac{1}{q}\intop\nolimits_{2}^{x}\frac{dy}{(\log y)^{|\mathcal{H}|}}.

The term
q/(\varphi(q)\log n)
is expected to be the probability that
n+h
is prime, given that
(n+h,q)=1
.

1.3.
Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan’s method

Based on a model by assuming a modified version of the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture (
1.5
), Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan
[
4
]
conjectured that

\pi(x;q,\mathbf{a})=\frac{\text{li}(x)}{\varphi(q)^{r}}\left(1+c_{1}(q;\mathbf{a})\frac{\log\log x}{\log x}+c_{2}(q;\mathbf{a})\frac{1}{\log x}+O((\log x)^{-7/4})\right),

where
c_{1}(q;\mathbf{a})
and
c_{2}(q;\mathbf{a})
are explicit constants.

They rewrited the sum of the characteristic function

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
n\equiv a\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n)\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<t<h\\
(t+a,q)=1\end{subarray}}(1-\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+t))

into a sum related to singular series and achieved that

\pi(x;q,(a,b))\sim\frac{1}{q}\intop\nolimits_{2}^{x}{\alpha(y)}^{\epsilon_{q}(a,b)}\bigg(\frac{q}{\varphi(q)\log y}\bigg)^{2}\mathcal{D}(a,b;y)dy

where
\mathcal{D}(a,b;y)
is a sum depending on the average of singular series. The key point to analyze
\mathcal{D}(a,b;y)
is a detailed estimation of

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h>0\\
h\equiv v\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{q,0}(\{0,h\})e^{-h/H},

which was calculated as the main proposition in
[
4
]
.

1.4.
Key proposition of this article

To complete the heuristic of Conjecture
1.1
, the key point is to analyze the average of singular series with a weight of exponential functions which differs from the case in
[
4
]
. Let
\nu(u)=1-1/\log u
. By Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan’s idea
[
4
]
, one can estimate the following expression

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\geqslant 1\\
2|h\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{0,h\})\nu(u)^{h}.

Since the counting function of the Piatetski-Shapiro sequence requires us to express the fractional part of a function into a sum of exponential sums, we need to estimate

(1.6)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\geqslant 1\\
2|h\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{0,h\})\nu(u)^{h}\mathbf{e}(f(h,u)),

where the function
f(h,u)
is “smooth”. The case when the function
f(h,u)
is linear was estimated in
[
1
]
, but the method has to be revised to adapt the smooth case (
1.6
). One can compare the following proposition to Lemma 2.4 in
[
1
]
. A detailed proof is in Section
4
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Fix
\theta\in[0,1]
and
\vartheta=0
or
1
. Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in(0,1)
be two real numbers. For all
j,k\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\geqslant 3
, let
c(j,k,u,h)
be a complex number with
|c(j,k,u,h)|=1
and
c(j,k,u,h)=1
if
j=k=0
. We define

\displaystyle R_{\theta,\vartheta;j,k}(u)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\geqslant 1\\
2\,\mid\,h\end{subarray}}h^{\theta}(\log h)^{\vartheta}\nu(u)^{h}c(j,k,u,h)\mathbf{e}(ju^{\gamma_{1}}+k(u+h)^{\gamma_{2}}),

\displaystyle S_{j,k}(u)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\geqslant 1\\
2\,\mid\,h\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{0,h\})\nu(u)^{h}c(j,k,u,h)\mathbf{e}(ju^{\gamma_{1}}+k(u+h)^{\gamma_{2}}).

When
j=k=0
we have the estimates

\displaystyle R_{\theta,0;0,0}(u)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\Gamma(1+\theta)(\log u)^{1+\theta}+O(1),

\displaystyle R_{\theta,1;0,0}(u)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(\log 2)\Gamma(1+\theta)(\log u)^{1+\theta}+O(1),

\displaystyle S_{0,0}(u)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\log u-\tfrac{1}{2}\log\log u+O(1).

On the other hand, if
k
is such that
|k|\geqslant(\log u)^{-1}
, then

\max\big\{|R_{\theta,\vartheta;j,k}(u)|,|S_{j,k}(u)|\big\}\ll|k|^{-4}.

If
j,k
are such that
|jk|\geqslant(\log u)^{-1}
, then

(1.7)

\max\big\{|R_{\theta,\vartheta;j,k}(u)|,|S_{j,k}(u)|\big\}\ll|jk|^{-4}.

1.5.
Remark to the main term of Conjecture
1.1

The main conjectures by Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan
[
4
]
provide a secondary main term, which makes the prediction more accurate and also show the unexpected biases. However, our conjectures only prove a main term although we apply a similar model. This is because of the construction of our key Proposition
1.3
.

Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan only need to consider
R_{\theta,\vartheta;j,k}(u)
and
S_{j,k}(u)
when
j=k=0
, which can be estimated by a careful calculation by L-functions and so on. In our problems, the way to construct Piatetski-Shapiro sequence is by the method of exponential sums. Whenever the sum is twisted by the term

c(j,k,u,h)\mathbf{e}(ju^{\gamma_{1}}+k(u+h)^{\gamma_{2}}),

we can hardly give an asymptotic formula instead of an upper bound. Therefore, unfortunately by the method in this article, we are not able to provide a secondary main term in our conjectures as the previous result.

We mention that David, Devin, Nam and Schlitt
[
2
]
also gave a conjecture with a secondary main term, since they formulated a new Hardy-Littlewood conjecture in arithmetic progressions for sum of two squares. If we also assume a Hardy-Littlewood conjecture for Piatetski-Shapiro primes, it may be possible to address a more accurate conjecture for consecutive Piatetski-Shapiro primes, which could be considered as a future topic.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

\left\lfloor t\right\rfloor
and
\{t\}
the greatest integer
\leqslant t
and the fractional part of
t
, respectively. We also write
\mathbf{e}(t)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=e^{2\pi it}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, as usual. We make considerable use of the sawtooth function defined by

\psi(t)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=t-\left\lfloor t\right\rfloor-\tfrac{1}{2}=\{t\}-\tfrac{1}{2}\qquad(t\in\mathbb{R}).

Let
\mathbb{P}
denote the set of primes in
\mathbb{N}
. In what follows, the letter
p
always denotes a prime number. Let
\gamma\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=c^{-1},\gamma_{1}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}={c_{1}}^{-1}

\gamma_{2}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}={c_{2}}^{-1}
. Throughout the paper,
\varepsilon
is always a sufficiently small positive number.

For an arbitrary set
\mathcal{S}
, we use
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}}
to denote its indicator function:

\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}}(n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\begin{cases}1&\quad\hbox{if $n\in\mathcal{S}$,}\\
0&\quad\hbox{if $n\not\in\mathcal{S}$,}\\
\end{cases}

and let
\mathbf{1}^{(c)}(n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}^{(c)}}(n)
.

Throughout the paper, implied constants in symbols
O
,
\ll
and
\gg
may depend (where obvious) on the parameters
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\varepsilon
but are absolute otherwise. For given functions
F
and
G
, the notations
F\ll G
,
G\gg F
and

F=O(G)
are all equivalent to the statement that the inequality
|F|\leqslant c|G|
holds with some constant
c>0
.

2.2.
Technical lemmas

We start by a simple average estimation of singular series.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
h
be a positive integer. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant t\leqslant h-1\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{0,t\})

\displaystyle\ll h^{1/2+\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant t\leqslant h-1\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{t,h\})

\displaystyle\ll h^{1/2+\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant t_{1}<t_{2}\leqslant h-1\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{t_{1},t_{2}\})

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}h\log h+\tfrac{1}{2}Ah+O(h^{1/2+\varepsilon}),

where
A\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=2-C_{0}-\log 2\pi
and
C_{0}
denotes the Euler-Mascheroni constant.

Proof.

See
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
.
∎

Recall that

\nu(u)=1-\frac{1}{\log u}\qquad(u>1).

Note that
\nu(u)
is the same as
\alpha(u)
in the notation of
[
4
]
and
\nu(u)\asymp 1
if
u\geqslant 3
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
c>0
be a constant, and suppose that
f
is a function such that
|f(h)|\leqslant h^{c}
for all
h\geqslant 1
. Then, uniformly for
3\leqslant u\leqslant x
and a real function
g(u,h)\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\leqslant\log x\\
2\,\mid\,h\end{subarray}}f(h)\nu(u)^{h}\mathbf{e}(g(u,h))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\geqslant 1\\
2\,\mid\,h\end{subarray}}f(h)\nu(u)^{h}\mathbf{e}(g(u,h))+O_{c}(x^{-1}).

Proof.

Let
H\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-(\log\nu(n))^{-1}
. We write
\nu(u)^{h}=e^{-h/H}
. By
H\leqslant\log u
for
u\geqslant 3
,
h>\log x
, we conclude that
h/H\geqslant h^{2/3}
with
u\leqslant x
. Hence

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h>\log x\\
2\,\mid\,h\end{subarray}}f(h)\nu(u)^{h}\mathbf{e}(g(u,h))\bigg|

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\sum_{h>\log x}h^{c}e^{-h^{2/3}}\leqslant x^{-1}\sum_{h>\log x}h^{c}e^{h^{1/3}-h^{2/3}}\ll_{c}x^{-1}.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Assuming the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture (
1.4
), let
h\leqslant\log x
and write

f_{h}(n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n)\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h)\prod_{0<t<h}\big(1-\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+t)\big).

Let

(2.1)

S_{h}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n\leqslant x}f_{h}(n).

For every integer
L\geqslant 0
we denote

(2.2)

\mathcal{D}_{h,L}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathop{\sum_{\mathcal{A}\subseteq\{0,h\}}\sum_{\mathcal{T}\subseteq[1,h-1]}}\limits_{(|\mathcal{A}|+|\mathcal{T}|=L)}(-1)^{|\mathcal{T}|}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{T})(\nu(u)\log u)^{-|\mathcal{T}|}\nu(u)^{h}.

Then

S_{h}(x)=\sum_{L=0}^{h+1}\intop\nolimits_{3}^{x}\nu(u)^{-1}(\log u)^{-2}\mathcal{D}_{h,L}(u)\,du+O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon}).

Proof.

First, write
\widetilde{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbb{P}}(n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n)-1/\log n
, and put

\widetilde{f}_{h}(n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbb{P}}(n)+\frac{1}{\log n}\right)\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h)+\frac{1}{\log n}\right)\prod_{0<t<h}\left(1-\frac{1}{\log n}-\widetilde{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+t)\right).

Recalling that
h\leqslant\log x
, we have the uniform estimate

f_{h}(n)=\widetilde{f}_{h}(n)\left(1+O\left(\frac{(\log x)^{6}}{x^{1/2}}\right)\right)

for all
n>x^{1/2}
. Since
f_{h}(n)
and
\widetilde{f}_{h}(n)
are bounded, it follows that

S_{h}(x)=\sum_{n\leqslant x}\widetilde{f}_{h}(n)+O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon}).

It follows that, up to an error term of size
O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})
, the quantity
S_{h}(x)
equals

\sum_{\mathcal{A}\subseteq\{0,h\}}\sum_{\mathcal{T}\subseteq[1,h-1]}(-1)^{|\mathcal{T}|}\sum_{n\leqslant x}\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)^{2-|\mathcal{A}|}\left(1-\frac{1}{\log n}\right)^{h-1-|\mathcal{T}|}\prod_{t\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{T}}\widetilde{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+t)

(compare to
[
4
, Equations (2.5) and (2.6)]
).

By the modified Hardy-Littlewood conjecture (
1.4
) the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant x}(\log n)^{-c}\prod_{t\in\mathcal{H}}\widetilde{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+t)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{3^{-}}^{x}(\log u)^{-c}\,d\left(\,\sum_{n\leqslant u}\prod_{t\in\mathcal{H}}\widetilde{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+t)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{H})\intop\nolimits_{3}^{x}(\log u)^{-c-|\mathcal{H}|}\,du+O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})

holds uniformly for any constant
c>0
; consequently, up to an error term of size
O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})
the quantity
S_{h}(x)
is equal to

\sum_{\mathcal{A}\subseteq\{0,h\}}\sum_{\mathcal{T}\subseteq[1,h-1]}(-1)^{|\mathcal{T}|}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{T})\intop\nolimits_{3}^{x}(\log u)^{-2-|\mathcal{T}|}\nu(u)^{h-1-|\mathcal{T}|}\,du.

By the definition (
2.2
), we have

S_{h}(x)=\sum_{L=0}^{h+1}\intop\nolimits_{3}^{x}\nu(u)^{-1}(\log u)^{-2}\mathcal{D}_{h,L}(u)\,du+O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon}).

∎

Lemma 2.4

For any

there are numbers

\bigg|\psi(t)-\sum_{0<|h|\leqslant H}a_{h}\,\mathbf{e}(th)\bigg|\leqslant\sum_{|h|\leqslant H}b_{h}\,\mathbf{e}(th),\qquad a_{h}\ll\frac{1}{|h|}\,,\qquad b_{h}\ll\frac{1}{H}\,.

Proof.

See
[

]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
g(n)
be bounded,
|a_{j}|\ll 1/|j|
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{3<n\leqslant x}\sum_{1\leqslant|j|\leqslant J}g(n)a_{j}\left(\mathbf{e}(j(n+h+1)^{\gamma})-\mathbf{e}(j(n+h)^{\gamma})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\ll x^{\gamma-1}\max_{N\leqslant x}\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\Bigg|\sum_{3<n\leqslant N}g(n)\mathbf{e}(j(n+h)^{\gamma})\Bigg|.

Proof.

Let

\phi_{j,h}(t):=\mathbf{e}\left(j((t+h+1)^{\gamma}-(t+h)^{\gamma})\right)-1.

Then

\phi_{j,h}(t)\ll|j|t^{\gamma-1}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\frac{\partial\phi_{j,h}(t)}{\partial t}\ll|j|t^{\gamma-2},

so we have

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{3<n\leqslant x}\sum_{1\leqslant|j|\leqslant J}g(n)a_{j}\left(\mathbf{e}(j(n+h+1)^{\gamma})-\mathbf{e}(j(n+h)^{\gamma})\right)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}j^{-1}\Bigg|\sum_{3<n\leqslant x}g(n)\phi_{j,h}(n)\mathbf{e}(j(n+h)^{\gamma})\Bigg|

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}j^{-1}\Bigg|\phi_{j,h}(x)\sum_{3<n\leqslant x}g(n)\mathbf{e}(j(n+h)^{\gamma})\Bigg|

\displaystyle\qquad+\intop\nolimits_{3}^{x}\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}j^{-1}\Bigg|\frac{\partial\phi_{j,h}(u)}{\partial u}\sum_{3<n\leqslant u}g(n)\mathbf{e}(j(n+h)^{\gamma})\Bigg|du

\displaystyle\ll x^{\gamma-1}\max_{N\leqslant x}\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\Bigg|\sum_{3<n\leqslant N}g(n)\mathbf{e}(j(n+h)^{\gamma})\Bigg|.

Finally, we use the following well-known lemma, which provides a characterization of the numbers that occur in the Piatetski-Shapiro sequence
\mathcal{N}^{(c)}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

A natural number
m
has the form
\left\lfloor n^{c}\right\rfloor
if and only if
\mathbf{1}^{(c)}(m)=1
, where

\mathbf{1}^{(c)}(m)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\left\lfloor-m^{\gamma}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor-(m+1)^{\gamma}\right\rfloor
. Moreover,

\mathbf{1}^{(c)}(m)=\gamma m^{\gamma-1}+\psi(-(m+1)^{\gamma})-\psi(-m^{\gamma})+O(m^{\gamma-2}).

3.

We assume that

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{3}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n\leqslant x}\gamma_{2}n^{\gamma_{2}-1}\sum_{1\leqslant|j|\leqslant J}f_{h}(n)a_{j}\left(\mathbf{e}(j(n+1)^{\gamma_{1}})-\mathbf{e}(jn^{\gamma_{1}})\right)

and

\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}_{3}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n\leqslant x}hn^{\gamma_{2}-2}\sum_{1\leqslant|j|\leqslant J}f_{h}(n)a_{j}\left(\mathbf{e}(j(n+1)^{\gamma_{1}})-\mathbf{e}(jn^{\gamma_{1}})\right).

We apply the partial summation as Lemma
2.5
, then

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{3}\ll x^{\gamma_{1}-1}\max_{N\leqslant x}\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\Bigg|\sum_{3<n\leqslant N}n^{\gamma_{2}-1}f_{h}(n)\mathbf{e}(jn^{\gamma_{1}})\Bigg|.

We define a complex function
c(j,h)
such that

\Bigg|\sum_{3<n\leqslant N}n^{\gamma_{2}-1}f_{h}(n)\mathbf{e}(jn^{\gamma_{1}})\Bigg|=c(j,h)\sum_{3<n\leqslant N}n^{\gamma_{2}-1}f_{h}(n)\mathbf{e}(jn^{\gamma_{1}}).

By the same construction of
\mathcal{S}_{2}
, we conclude that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\leqslant\log x\\
2\,\mid\,h\end{subarray}}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{3}\ll x^{\gamma_{1}-1}\max_{N\leqslant x}\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\sum_{l=1}^{5}\intop\nolimits_{3}^{N}\frac{u^{\gamma_{2}-1}}{\nu(u)\log^{2}u}\mathcal{F}_{3,l}(u)\,du,

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3,1}(u)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\geqslant 1\\
2\,\mid\,h\end{subarray}}\nu(u)^{h}c(j,h)\mathbf{e}(ju^{\gamma_{1}});

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3,2}(u)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\geqslant 1\\
2\,\mid\,h\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{0,h\})\nu(u)^{h}c(j,h)\mathbf{e}(ju^{\gamma_{1}});

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3,3}(u)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{(-1)}{\nu(u)\log u}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\geqslant 1\\
2\,\mid\,h\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant t\leqslant h-1\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{t,h\})\nu(u)^{h}c(j,h)\mathbf{e}(ju^{\gamma_{1}});

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3,4}(u)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{(-1)}{\nu(u)\log u}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\geqslant 1\\
2\,\mid\,h\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant t\leqslant h-1\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{t,h\})\nu(u)^{h}c(j,h)\mathbf{e}(ju^{\gamma_{1}});

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3,5}(u)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{1}{(\nu(u)\log u)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\geqslant 1\\
2\,\mid\,h\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant t_{1}<t_{2}\leqslant h-1\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{t_{1},t_{2}\})\nu(u)^{h}c(j,h)\mathbf{e}(ju^{\gamma_{1}}).

Therefore, it follows that

2\,\mid\,h\end{subarray}}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{3}\ll x^{\gamma_{2}-1}\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant K}(1+x^{\gamma_{2}}(\log x)^{-2}k^{-4})\ll x^{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}-1}(\log x)^{-2}.

4.
Proof of the key proposition

The proof of Proposition
1.7
starts by a similar construction of the proof of
[
1
, Lemma 2.4]
. Note that
\nu(u)\asymp 1
for
u\geqslant 3
. Let
H\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-(\log\nu(n))^{-1}
, which gives that
\nu(u)^{h}=e^{-h/H}
. Write that

\nu(u)^{h}\mathbf{e}(kh)=e^{-h/H_{k}}\qquad\text{with}\quad H_{k}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{H}{1-2\pi ikH}.

Since
\Re(h/H_{k})=h/H>0
for any positive integer
h
, by the Cahen-Mellin integral it gives that

\displaystyle R_{\theta,\vartheta;j,k}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\geqslant 1\\
2\,\mid\,h\end{subarray}}h^{\theta}(\log h)^{\vartheta}f(j,k,u,h)e^{-h/H_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\intop\nolimits_{4-i\infty}^{4+\infty}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\geqslant 1\\
2\,\mid\,h\end{subarray}}\frac{h^{\theta}(\log h)^{\vartheta}}{h^{s}}f(j,k,u,h)\bigg)\Gamma(s)H_{k}^{s}\,ds,

where

f(j,k,u,h)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=c(j,k,u,h)\mathbf{e}(ju^{\gamma_{1}}+k(u+h)^{\gamma_{2}}-kh).

The case that
j=k=0
is the same as
[
1
, Lemma 2.4]
. When
k\neq 0
we have

\displaystyle|R_{\theta,0;j,k}(u)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2\pi}\intop\nolimits_{4-i\infty}^{4+\infty}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\geqslant 1\\
2\,\mid\,h\end{subarray}}\big|\frac{h^{\theta}(\log h)^{\vartheta}}{h^{s}}\big|\bigg)\Gamma(s)H_{k}^{s}\,ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2^{\theta}}{2\pi}\intop\nolimits_{4-i\infty}^{4+i\infty}|2^{-4}\zeta(4-\theta)||\Gamma(s)H_{k}^{s}|\,ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2^{\theta-4}|H_{k}|^{4}}{2\pi}\intop\nolimits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big|\zeta(4-\theta)\Gamma(4+it)\big|\,dt

\displaystyle\ll|H_{k}|^{4}=\bigg(\frac{H^{2}}{1+4\pi^{2}k^{2}H^{2}}\bigg)^{2},

which gives that
R_{\theta,0;j,k}(u)\ll k^{-4}
if
|k|\geqslant(\log u)^{-1}
since
H\asymp\log u
for
u\geqslant 3
. The bound for
R_{\theta,1;j,k}
is proved similarly by considering
\zeta^{\prime}(4-\theta)
.

Secondly, we define

T_{j,k}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{h\geqslant 1}\mathfrak{S}(\{0,h\})f(j,k,u,h)e^{-h/H_{k}},

for
j,k\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\geqslant 3
. Since
\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{0,h\})=\mathfrak{S}(\{0,h\})-1
for all integers
h
,
and
\mathfrak{S}(\{0,h\})=0
if
h
is odd, it follows that

S_{j,k}(u)=T_{j,k}(u)-R_{0,0;j,k}(u)=T_{j,k}(u)+O(\log u).

Hence, to complete the proof of the lemma, it suffices to show that

T_{j,k}(u)\ll k^{-4}\text{~if~}|k|\geqslant(\log u)^{-1},

since the case that
j=k=0
is the same as
[
1
, Lemma 2.4]
. As in the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 2.1]
, we consider the Dirichlet series

F(s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{h\geqslant 1}\frac{\mathfrak{S}(\{0,h\})}{h^{s}},

which can be expressed in the form

F(s)=\frac{\zeta(s)\zeta(s+1)}{\zeta(2s+2)}\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2}}+\frac{2p}{(p-1)^{2}(p^{s+1}+1)}\right),

and the final product is analytic for
\Re(s)>-1
. Similar to the proof of the first part of the lemma, using the Cahen-Mellin integral with
k\neq 0
we have that

\big|T_{j,k}(u)\big|\leqslant\frac{|H_{k}|^{4}}{2\pi}\intop\nolimits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big|F(4)\Gamma(4+it)\big|\,dt\ll|H_{k}|^{4}=\bigg(\frac{H^{2}}{1+4\pi^{2}k^{2}H^{2}}\bigg)^{2}.

Hence
T_{j,k}(u)\ll k^{-4}
by a similar argument.

To prove (
1.7
), we choose

\nu(u)^{h}\mathbf{e}(jkh)=e^{-h/H_{j,k}}\qquad\text{with}\quad H_{j,k}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{H}{1-2\pi ijkH},

and

f(j,k,u,h)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=c(j,k,u,h)\mathbf{e}(ju^{\gamma_{1}}+k(u+h)^{\gamma_{2}}-jkh).

Everything else follows the same.

5.
A sketch of the heuristic argument for
r\geqslant 3

Let
\mathcal{H}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(h_{1},h_{2},\cdots,h_{r-1})
with
h_{i}
positive even integers for
1\leqslant i\leqslant r-1
. Writing that
h_{0}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=0
and

\mathfrak{H}_{i}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=h_{0}+\cdots+h_{i},

we give a brief sketch by writing
\pi(x;\mathbf{c})
as

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\sum_{h_{1},\dots,h_{r-1}>0}f_{\mathcal{H}}(n)\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathbf{1}^{(c_{i})}(n+\mathfrak{H}_{i-1}),

where

f_{\mathcal{H}}(n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n)\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\bigg[\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+\mathfrak{H}_{i})\prod_{0<t<h_{i}}\big(1-\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(n+t+\mathfrak{H}_{i-1})\big)\bigg].

Based on the arguments for
r=2
we mainly need to consider the following two sums, which are

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1},\dots,h_{r-1}\leqslant\log x\\
2|h_{i}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1},\dots,h_{r-1}\leqslant\log x\\
2|h_{i}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{2}

where

\mathcal{T}_{1}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n\leqslant x}f_{\mathcal{H}}(n)\prod_{i=1}^{r}\gamma_{i}(n+h_{i-1})^{\gamma_{i}-1}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{2}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n\leqslant x}f_{\mathcal{H}}(n)\prod_{i=1}^{r}\bigg(\sum_{1\leqslant|k_{i}|\leqslant K_{i}}a_{k_{i}}\Big(\mathbf{e}\big(k_{i}(n+1+\mathfrak{H}_{i-1})^{\gamma_{i}}\big)-\mathbf{e}\big(k_{i}(n+\mathfrak{H}_{i-1})^{\gamma_{i}}\big)\Big)\bigg),

where
K_{i}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=x^{1-\gamma_{i}+\varepsilon}
, since
\mathcal{T}_{1}
provides the main term and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is the most complicated error term.

5.1.
Estimation of
\mathcal{T}_{1}

Based on the estimation of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, it is deduced that

\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n\leqslant x}\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{r}n^{\gamma_{1}+\dots+\gamma_{r}-r}f_{\mathcal{H}}(n)

is the main term and all the rest are covered by the error term. Following a similar argument in
[
4
, Section 4]
, with an error term up to
O((x^{\gamma_{1}+\dots+\gamma_{r}-r+1})/\log^{3/2-\epsilon}x)
, we deduce that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1},\dots,h_{r-1}\leqslant\log x\\
2|h_{i}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}=\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{r}\intop\nolimits_{3}^{x}\frac{u^{\gamma_{1}+\dots+\gamma_{r}-r}}{\nu(u)\log^{r}u}\big(\mathcal{F}^{r}_{1,1}(u)+\mathcal{F}^{r}_{1,2}(u)\big)~du

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{r}_{1,1}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1},\dots,h_{r-1}\leqslant\log u\\
2|h_{i}\end{subarray}}\nu(u)^{h_{1}+\cdots+h_{r-1}};

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{r}_{1,2}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1},\dots,h_{r-1}\leqslant\log u\\
2|h_{i}\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leqslant s<t\leqslant r-1}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{0,h_{s+1}+\cdots+h_{t}\})\nu(u)^{h_{s+1}+\cdots+h_{t}}.

By a similar argument to Proposition
1.3
we deduce that
\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}
gives the main term which is

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1},\dots,h_{r-1}\leqslant\log x\\
2|h_{i}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}=\frac{x^{1/c_{1}+\dots+1/c_{r}-r+1}}{c_{1}\cdots c_{r}\log x}+O\bigg(\frac{x^{1/c_{1}+\dots+1/c_{r}-r+1}}{(\log x)^{3/2-\varepsilon}}\bigg).

5.2.
Estimation of
\mathcal{T}_{2}

By a partial summation similar to the estimation of
\mathcal{S}_{4}
, it follows that

\mathcal{T}_{2}\ll x^{\gamma_{1}+\dots+\gamma_{r}-r}\max_{N\leqslant x}\sum_{1\leqslant k_{i}\leqslant K_{i},\forall i\in\{1,\dots,r\}}\bigg|\sum_{3<n\leqslant N}f_{\mathcal{H}}(n)\mathbf{e}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i}(n+\mathfrak{H}_{i-1})^{\gamma_{i}}\Big)\bigg|.

We define a complex function
c(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{H})
with
\mathcal{K}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r})
for
1\leqslant k_{i}\leqslant K_{i},\forall i=1,\cdots,r
and
\mathcal{H}
as before such that

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{3<n\leqslant N}f_{\mathcal{H}}(n)\mathbf{e}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i}(n+\mathfrak{H}_{i-1})^{\gamma_{i}}\Big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\qquad=c(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{H})\sum_{3<n\leqslant N}f_{\mathcal{H}}(n)\mathbf{e}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i}(n+\mathfrak{H}_{i-1})^{\gamma_{i}}\Big).

Similar with above we achieve that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1},\dots,h_{r-1}\leqslant\log x\\
2|h_{i}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{2}\ll x^{\gamma_{1}+\dots+\gamma_{r}-r}\max_{N\leqslant x}\sum_{1\leqslant k_{i}\leqslant K_{i},\forall i\in\{1,\dots,r\}}\intop\nolimits_{3}^{N}\frac{u^{\gamma_{1}+\dots+\gamma_{r}-r}}{\nu(u)\log^{r}u}\big(\mathcal{F}^{r}_{2,1}(u)+\mathcal{F}^{r}_{2,2}(u)\big)~du

with an error term up to
O((x^{\gamma_{1}+\dots+\gamma_{r}-r+1})/\log^{3/2-\epsilon}x)
where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{r}_{2,1}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1},\dots,h_{r-1}\leqslant\log u\\
2|h_{i}\end{subarray}}\nu(u)^{h_{1}+\cdots+h_{r-1}}c(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{H})\mathbf{e}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i}(n+\mathfrak{H}_{i-1})^{\gamma_{i}}\Big)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{r}_{2,2}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1},\dots,h_{r-1}\leqslant\log u\\
2|h_{i}\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leqslant s<t\leqslant r-1}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{0,h_{s+1}+\cdots+h_{t}\})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\nu(u)^{h_{s+1}+\cdots+h_{t}}\mathbf{e}\Big(\sum_{i=s+1}^{t}k_{i}(u+\mathfrak{H}_{i-1})^{\gamma_{i}}\Big).

By a similar argument to Proposition
1.3
we deduce that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1},\dots,h_{r-1}\leqslant\log x\\
2|h_{i}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{2}\ll\frac{x^{1/c_{1}+\dots+1/c_{r}-r+1}}{(\log x)^{3/2-\varepsilon}}.

Assembling these contributions yields our Conjecture
1.2
.

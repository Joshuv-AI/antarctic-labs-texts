F. Eshmatov, X. García-Martínez, Z. Normatov, R. Turdibaev

Abstract
. This paper presents a comprehensive description of the coordinate rings and Poisson brackets associated with the fourth Calogero-Moser space and invariant commuting pairs of matrices of size four. As an application, we compute their respective classes in the Grothendieck ring of the category of complex varieties and we offer some novel insights about the geometry of the Hilbert scheme of points on the affine plane.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
16R30, 13A50, 14R20, 14L30, 17B63

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{M}_{n}
be the set of all
n\times n
complex matrices. The general linear group of degree
n
acts on the set of
d
-copies of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
by the simultaneous conjugation, i.e., for any
g\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,

g\cdot(X_{1},\dots,X_{d})=(gX_{1}g^{-1},\dots,gX_{d}g^{-1}).

(1.1)

It consequently induces an action of
\GL_{n}
on the algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}_{n}^{d}]
of polynomial functions on
\mathcal{M}_{n}^{d}
. A celebrated result of Procesi
[
17
]
and Razmyslov
[
18
]
states that the algebra of
\GL_{n}
-invariant polynomials
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}_{n}^{d}]^{\GL_{n}}
is generated by the trace functions
\Tr(A_{1}\cdots A_{k})
, where
A_{1},\dots,A_{k}\in\{X_{1},\dots,X_{d}\}

and every relation between them follows from the Cayley-Hamilton theorem.

For an integer
n\geq 0
, we introduce the following two subspaces of
\mathcal{M}_{n}\times\mathcal{M}_{n}

\mathrm{C}_{n}\coloneqq\{(X,Y)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\times\mathcal{M}_{n}\mid\mathrm{rank}([X,Y]+I_{n})=1\}\,.

(1.2)

and

\mathrm{Com}_{n}\coloneqq\{(X,Y)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\times\mathcal{M}_{n}\mid[X,Y]=0\}\,,

(1.3)

The action (
1.1
) restricts to these subspaces so we define the
n
-th

Calogero-Moser space

\mathcal{C}_{n}
and the
n
-th

invariant commuting variety

{\mathcal{C}om}_{n}
to be the respective quotient varieties

\mathcal{C}_{n}\coloneqq\mathrm{C}_{n}\mathord{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\GL_{n},\qquad{\mathcal{C}om}_{n}\coloneqq\mathrm{Com}_{n}\mathord{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\GL_{n}

These spaces hold significant importance in various branches of mathematics, including algebraic geometry (Hilbert schemes), representation theory (double affine Hecke algebras), deformation theory (symplectic reflection algebras), and Poisson geometry. As such, it is of great interest to have an explicit description of their coordinate rings,
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{n}]
and
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{C}om}_{n}]
. The primary objective of the paper is to provide such a description for the case when
n=4
.

Let us commence by reviewing what is currently known about these rings for a general
n
and their explicit descriptions for
n=2,3
.
The natural embeddings of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
and
{\mathcal{C}om}_{n}
into
(\mathcal{M}_{n}\times\mathcal{M}_{n})\mathord{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\mathrm{GL}_{n}
indicate that the rings

\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{n}]
and
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{C}om}_{n}]

are quotients of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}_{n}\times\mathcal{M}_{n}]^{\GL_{n}}
and therefore, by the Procesi-Razmyslov result, they are generated by trace functions
\Tr(A_{1}\cdots A_{k})
, where each
A_{i}
can either be
X
or
Y
. Due to the commutativity, the ring
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{C}om}_{n}]
is generated by traces of the form
\Tr(X^{i}Y^{j})
,
1~\leq~i,j\leq n
. In fact, the same holds for
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{n}]
.
Indeed, in Wilson’s landmark work
[
20
]
, he proved that the space
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is a smooth, irreducible, and complex symplectic variety with dimension of
2n
. To this end, he gave the following description of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
. Let us consider the space of all quadruples
(X,Y,v,w)
where
X,Y\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
and

v
and
w
are column and row vectors of length
n
, respectively.
Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
be its subspace
consisting of all
(X,Y,v,w)
satisfying

XY-YX+I_{n}=vw.

(1.4)

Then the well-defined action of
\GL_{n}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
given by

g\cdot(X,Y,v,w)=(gXg^{-1},gYg^{-1},gv,wg^{-1})

is free and it yields a natural identification:

\mathcal{C}_{n}\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{n}/\GL_{n}
.
Now, using relation (
1.4
),
one can easily show (see e.g.
[
3
]
) that the traces

\Tr(X^{i}Y^{j}),1\leq i,j\leq n
generate
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{n}]
.
However, as in the description of the ring of invariants of pairs of matrices, it is more convenient to consider the correspondent traceless versions of the matrices
(X,Y)
:

A\coloneqq X-\frac{1}{n}\Tr(X)I_{n},\qquad B\coloneqq Y-\frac{1}{n}\Tr(B)I_{n}.

Procesi’s results
[
17
, Section 5]
provide a compelling motivation for considering traceless matrices, which considerably helps to ease the complexity of the computations. Specifically, there exists an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-graded algebras:

\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}_{n}^{d}]^{\GL_{n}}\cong\mathbb{C}[u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{d}]\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}_{n}(0)^{d}]^{\GL_{n}},

where
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}_{n}(0)^{d}]^{\GL_{n}}
denotes the algebra of
\GL_{n}
-invariant polynomial functions on the space of
d
-tuples of traceless
n\times n
matrices. Notably, the elements
u_{i}
correspond to the traces of generic matrices. Applied to our case, this corresponds to the factorizations into products

\mathcal{C}_{n}=\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathcal{C}_{n}/\mathbb{C}^{2}\ ,\quad{\mathcal{C}om}_{n}=\mathbb{C}^{2}\times{\mathcal{C}om}_{n}/\mathbb{C}^{2}\,.

(1.5)

In
[
7
, Proposition 11.35]
and
[
11
]
, it is established that the generators of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{2}]
and
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{C}om}_{2}]
are

a_{1}\coloneqq\Tr(X),\,a_{2}\coloneqq\Tr(Y),\,a_{3}\coloneqq\Tr(A^{2}),\,a_{4}\coloneqq\Tr(AB),\,a_{5}\coloneqq\Tr(B^{2}),

and their presentations are

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{2}]

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[a_{1},a_{2}]\otimes\mathbb{C}[a_{3},a_{4},a_{5}]/(a_{4}^{2}-a_{3}a_{5}-1),

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{C}om}_{2}]

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[a_{1},a_{2}]\otimes\mathbb{C}[a_{3},a_{4},a_{5}]/(a_{4}^{2}-a_{3}a_{5}),

where
\mathbb{C}[a_{1},a_{2}]
is the coordinate ring of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
in (
1.5
).

As for the case
n=3
, the problem is fully solved in
[
16
]
. Explicitly, they have proved that both
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{3}]
and
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{C}om}_{3}]
are generated by
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{9}
, where

a_{6}\coloneqq\Tr(A^{3}),a_{7}\coloneqq\Tr(A^{2}B),a_{8}\coloneqq\Tr(AB^{2}),a_{9}\coloneqq\Tr(B^{3})\,,

subject to the following relations. For any,
v\in\mathbb{C}
, let
I_{v}
be the ideal of
\mathbb{C}[a_{3},\dots,a_{9}]
generated

\displaystyle r_{1}

\displaystyle=a_{3}a_{9}-2a_{4}a_{8}+a_{5}a_{7},

\displaystyle r_{2}

\displaystyle=a_{5}a_{6}-2a_{4}a_{7}+a_{3}a_{8},

\displaystyle r_{3,v}

\displaystyle=9va_{3}-a_{3}a_{4}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}a_{5}+6a_{6}a_{8}-6a_{7}^{2},

\displaystyle r_{4,v}

\displaystyle=9va_{4}-a_{4}^{3}+a_{3}a_{4}a_{5}+3a_{6}a_{9}-3a_{7}a_{8},

\displaystyle r_{5,v}

\displaystyle=9va_{5}-a_{4}^{2}a_{5}+a_{3}a_{5}^{2}+6a_{7}a_{9}-6a_{8}^{2}.

It was shown that
I_{v}\cong I_{1}
if
v\neq 0
, and

\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{3}]\cong\mathbb{C}[a_{1},a_{2}]\otimes\mathbb{C}[a_{3},\dots,a_{9}]/I_{1}\ ,\qquad\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{C}om}_{3}]\cong\mathbb{C}[a_{1},a_{2}]\otimes\mathbb{C}[a_{3},\dots,a_{9}]/I_{0}.

An alternative presentation of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{3}]
is implicitly found by setting
d=3
in the work of Bonnafé, who derived it using a different set of generators in
[
1
, Theorem 3.5]
.

We shall focus now in the case of
n=4
.
It is known that the large collection of generators of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}_{4}\times\mathcal{M}_{4}]^{\GL_{4}}
can be narrowed down to a minimal set formed by only
32
, catalogued in
[
5
]
. However, the full description of the ideal of relations is yet to be found (see
[
5
,
6
]
). Most of these
32
generators contain traces of expressions involving the commutators. Hence, if we confine our pairs of matrices to the invariant commuting variety, the set of generators becomes constrained to
a_{1},\dots a_{14}
, where
a_{1},\dots,a_{9}
as above and the rest are given by

a_{10}\coloneqq\Tr(A^{4}),a_{11}\coloneqq\Tr(A^{3}B),a_{12}\coloneqq\Tr(A^{2}B^{2}),a_{13}\coloneqq\Tr(AB^{3}),a_{14}\coloneqq\Tr(B^{4}).

In
[
15
]
, the author showed that the same
fourteen trace functions generate
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{4}]
. Therefore, both
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{4}]
and
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{C}om}_{4}]
can be expressed as quotients of the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[a_{1},\dots,a_{14}]
. Our attention will now turn to capturing and identifying the ideals of relations
I(\mathcal{C}_{4})
and
I({\mathcal{C}om}_{4})
in
\mathbb{C}[a_{1},\dots,a_{14}]
.

The main result in our paper is to find explicit descriptions of these ideals.
To this end, we use the Poisson brackets on the rings
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{4}]
and
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{C}om}_{4}]
, which arise from the symplectic structures on

\mathcal{C}_{4}
and on
{\mathcal{C}om}_{4}
respectively. Let us briefly describe this symplectic structure. First, the space
\mathcal{M}_{n}^{2}
of the pair of matrices
can be naturally identified with the cotangent bundle of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, and the symplectic form defined by:

\omega((X_{1},X_{2}),(Y_{1},Y_{2}))\coloneqq\Tr(X_{1}Y_{2})-\Tr(X_{2}Y_{1})\,.

(1.6)

Moreover, the moment map
\mu\colon\mathcal{M}_{n}^{2}\to\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
is given by
(X,Y)\mapsto XY-YX
, where
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
is the subspace of traceless matrices. Let
O\subset\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
be a closed
\GL_{n}
-orbit with respect to the adjoint action. Then the orbit space
\mathfrak{M}_{O}\coloneqq\mu^{-1}(O)\mathord{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\GL_{n}
is called an
affine quiver variety
(see
[
14
]
). The symplectic structure on
\mathcal{M}_{n}^{2}
induces, via the Marsden-Weinstein symplectic reduction construction
[
13
]
, a canonical symplectic structure on
\mathfrak{M}_{O}
. Note that both
\mathcal{C}_{n}\cong\mathfrak{M}_{O_{2}}
and
{\mathcal{C}om}_{n}\cong\mathfrak{M}_{O_{1}}
can be realized as quiver varieties. Here
O_{1}=\{0\}

and
O_{2}
is the set consisting of
n\times n

matrices of the from
S-I_{n}
, where
S
is a rank
1
semi-simple matrix such that
\Tr(S)=n
.
Symplectic structures on
\mathcal{C}_{n}
and
{\mathcal{C}om}_{n}
yield a Poisson algebra structure on their corresponding coordinate rings. Note that these rings are quotients of the polynomial algebra

B=\mathbb{C}[a_{1},\dots,a_{n^{2}+2n}]
, where the generators are exactly
{\Tr(A^{i}B^{j})}
, with
{0\leq i,j\leq n}
.
Hence using the Poisson structure on
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{n}]
(or/and
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{C}om}_{n}]
), we can define on
B
nonassociative brackets
\{-,-\}
, which is a derivation on the first and second components. This bracket will play an essential role in our construction.

For
n=4
, as we discussed above, the number of minimal generators is
14
, and
the bracket
\{-,-\}
is defined on
\mathbb{C}[a_{1},\dots,a_{14}]
. Using simple linear algebra tools, we obtain a defining relation in
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{4}]
:

r_{1}=8a_{3}+a_{3}(a_{4}^{2}-a_{3}a_{5})-2(a_{7}^{2}-a_{6}a_{8})+2(a_{3}a_{12}-2a_{4}a_{11}+a_{5}a_{10}).

Let
I
be the subideal of
I(\mathcal{C}_{4})
in
\mathbb{C}[a_{3},...,a_{14}]
generated by
r_{1}
via
\{-,-\}
and the multiplication in the ring. Similarly, using as defining relation
r_{1}-8a_{3}
in
{\mathcal{C}om}_{4}
, we let
J
to be the subideal of
I({\mathcal{C}om}_{4})
generated by
r_{1}-8a_{3}
. In this way, we obtain our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

We have
I=I(\mathcal{C}_{4})
and
J=I({\mathcal{C}om}_{4})
. In particular, there are Poisson algebra isomorphisms

\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{4}]\cong\mathbb{C}[a_{1},a_{2}]\otimes\dfrac{\mathbb{C}[a_{3},\dots,a_{14}]}{I}\,,

\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{C}om}_{4}]\cong\mathbb{C}[a_{1},a_{2}]\otimes\dfrac{\mathbb{C}[a_{3},\dots,a_{14}]}{J}\,.

For
n=1,\dots,4
, both
I(\mathcal{C}_{n})
and
I({\mathcal{C}om}_{n})
are generated by a single relation obtained through the Cayley-Hamilton theorem or the Jacobi identity. We propose the following conjecture for any
n\geq 1
.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
k=\frac{n(n+3)}{2}
. Then

\mathcal{C}[\mathcal{C}_{n}]\cong\mathbb{C}[a_{1},a_{2}]\otimes\dfrac{\mathbb{C}[a_{3},\dots,a_{k}]}{I}\,,

\mathcal{C}[{\mathcal{C}om}_{n}]\cong\mathbb{C}[a_{1},a_{2}]\otimes\dfrac{\mathbb{C}[a_{3},\dots,a_{k}]}{J}\,,

where
I
and
J
are generated via the bracket
\{-,-\}
by a single relation which can naturally be obtained using the Cayley-Hamilton theorem on corresponding varieties.

Recently, Bonnafé and Thiel proposed an elegant algorithm for computing a presentation of the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{n}]
(see
[
2
, Theorem 3.15]
). They use a different set of generators, which may yield a presentation that differs from the one in the above conjecture. However, Procesi pointed out to the fourth author, in private communication, that the Poisson bracket in
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{C}om}_{n}]
can be obtained via a type of Chevalley restriction theorem (see (
1.7
)) from the canonical Poisson bracket on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
. We hope that this fact may lead us to a proof of the above conjecture.

Let
{\mathcal{V}ar}_{\mathcal{C}}
be the category of quasiprojective complex varieties, and let
K_{0}({\mathcal{V}ar}_{\mathcal{C}})
be its Grothendieck ring. Using the presentation in Theorem
1.1
, we can stratify both
\mathcal{C}_{4}
and
{\mathcal{C}om}_{4}
to compute their classes in
K_{0}({\mathcal{V}ar}_{\mathcal{C}})
. Explicitly, we obtain:

[{\mathcal{C}om}_{4}]=\mathbb{L}^{8},\quad[\mathcal{C}_{4}]=\mathbb{L}^{8}-\mathbb{L}^{7}+2\mathbb{L}^{6}-\mathbb{L}^{5}.

Now we would like to discuss how our result can be applied to the geometry of Hilbert schemes. Recall that the
n
-th Hilbert scheme of points on the plane, denoted as
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, is defined as
\widetilde{H}\mathord{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, where

\widetilde{H}\coloneqq\{(X,Y,i)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\times\mathcal{M}_{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid XY=YX,\quad\mathbb{C}[X,Y]i=\mathbb{C}^{n}\}\,,

and this is acted upon by
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
as
g\cdot(X,Y,i)=(gXg^{-1},gYg^{-1},gi)
. Moreover, in
[
14
, Theorem 8.13]
an irreducible highest weight representation of a product of the Heisenberg and Clifford algebras on the direct sum of cohomologies
\oplus_{n}H^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2}))
has been constructed. Although the construction is clear, its complexity calls for a desire to find a simpler proof. Additionally, in
[
14
]
it is indicated that the characters of representations of affine Lie algebras have certain modularity properties. These features of affine Lie algebras and modularity suggest connections to loops and elliptic curves. It is natural to expect that these objects would appear in
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, and that the above construction of the highest weight representation would involve them. However, no elliptic curves or loops have been found in
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, and the problem remains unsolved.

Wilson and Nakajima independently demonstrated in their respective works
[
20
]
and
[
14
]
that
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
\mathcal{C}_{n}
are naturally diffeomorphic. This diffeomorphism allows us to establish an equivalence between their cohomology rings:

\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}H^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2}))\cong\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C}_{n})

Consequently, we can investigate Nakajima’s construction on the Calogero-Moser spaces. Since
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is an irreducible affine algebraic variety, its singular cohomology coincides with its de Rham cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{dR}(\mathcal{C}_{n})
. Therefore, the action of the Heisenberg Lie algebra is defined on the classes of differential forms on
\mathcal{C}_{n}
. We even propose extending this construction on forms by utilizing the Hochschild-Kostant-Rosenberg theorem. By identifying the graded rings
\Omega^{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{n})
, representing Kähler’s differential forms on
\mathcal{C}_{n}
, and the Hochschild homology
HH_{\bullet}(\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{n}))
, associated with the coordinate ring
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{n})
, we aim to define an action of the Heisenberg Lie algebra (or potentially its loop Lie algebra) on the direct sum of Hochschild chain complexes of
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{n})
. Additionally, we intend to ensure that this action commutes with Hochschild differentials.
To provide a description of the ring
\Omega^{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{n})
or the Hochschild homology
HH_{\bullet}(\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{n}))
, it is necessary to establish an explicit presentation of the algebra
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{n})
as in Theorem
1.1
(or Conjecture
1
, in general).
Having an explicit presentation of
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{n})
also allows for the straightforward generation of elliptic curves and algebraic loops within
\mathcal{C}_{n}
. Our proposed representation suggests that
\mathcal{C}_{n}
can be viewed as the product of the affine plane
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
and
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{0}
, which is isomorphic to a subvariety of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
comprising traceless matrix pairs. As a result, elliptic curves and loops can be realized on the affine plane
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.

Let us now examine how the presentation of the commuting variety
{\mathcal{C}om}_{n}
can be used to uncover elliptic curves and algebraic loops on
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
.
To this end, we need another description of
\mathcal{O}({\mathcal{C}om}_{n})
due to Gan and Ginzburg
[
8
]
, Domokos
[
4
]
and Vaccarino
[
19
]
.
Let
U_{n}
be the variety of pairs of diagonal
n\times n
matrices.
Since
U_{n}
can be identified with
the
n
-th product
(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{n}
,
its coordinate ring is isomorphic to the ring
B_{n}\coloneqq\mathbb{C}[x,y]^{\otimes n}
. The symmetric group
S_{n}
acts on
U_{n}
by permutation of diagonal entries and the corresponding action on
B_{n}
is given by permutation of factors. On the other hand, there is a group monomorphism
\phi\colon S_{n}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}
given by the permutation representation, which induces
the action of
S_{n}
on
\mathrm{Com}_{n}
. Clearly, the embedding of
U_{n}
into
\mathrm{Com}_{n}
is
S_{n}
-equivariant and we have a natural embedding
i\colon U_{n}\mathord{/\mkern-6.0mu/}S_{n}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Com}_{n}\mathord{/\mkern-6.0mu/}S_{n}
. Composing
i
with the projection

\mathrm{Com}_{n}\mathord{/\mkern-6.0mu/}S_{n}\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{Com}_{n}\mathord{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\mathrm{GL}_{n}
yields a morphism of algebraic varieties
\pi\colon U_{n}\mathord{/\mkern-6.0mu/}S_{n}\to{\mathcal{C}om_{n}}
. It has been proved in
[
4
,
8
,
19
]
that
\pi
is an isomorphism, or equivalently, the restriction homomorphism

\pi_{\ast}\colon\mathcal{O}({\mathcal{C}om}_{n})\to B_{n}^{S_{n}}\,

(1.7)

is an algebra isomorphism.
Note that, since the space
U_{n}\mathord{/\mkern-6.0mu/}S_{n}
can be identified with the
n
-th symmetric power
S^{n}\mathbb{C}^{2}\coloneqq(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{n}\mathord{/\mkern-6.0mu/}S_{n}
, we get
the isomorphism

S^{n}\mathbb{C}^{2}\cong{\mathcal{C}om_{n}}

defined by

((\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}),\dots,(\lambda_{n},\mu_{n})\big)\mapsto(\mathrm{Diag}[\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}],\mathrm{Diag}[\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}]).

Recall that the
Hilbert-Chow morphism
is the map
\rho\colon\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\to S^{n}\mathbb{C}^{2}
defined as follows. Let
(X,Y,i)\in\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Since
[X,Y]=0
, both matrices can be simultaneously transformed into upper triangular matrices with diagonal entries
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
and
(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})
respectively. In this way, there is a well-defined morphism
\rho(X,Y,i)\coloneqq\big((\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}),\dots,(\lambda_{n},\mu_{n})\big)
. Thus, the Hilbert-Chow morphism can be defined as the map

\rho\colon\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\to{\mathcal{C}om_{n}}\,,\quad(X,Y,i)\mapsto(X,Y)\,.

(1.8)

Now, by selecting an elliptic curve or a loop on the
\mathbb{C}^{2}
component of
{\mathcal{C}om_{n}}
in (
1.5
), we can easily pull back the corresponding curve or loop through the mapping
\rho
. Indeed, let us consider the elliptic curve
y^{2}=x^{3}+ax+b
on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. We define

X=\mathrm{Diag}[\lambda_{1}x,\lambda_{2}x,\dots,\lambda_{n}x],\quad Y=\mathrm{Diag}[0,\dots,0,y],

where
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
are distinct and
\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}=1
. Clearly, this choice of
(X,Y)
gives us an elliptic curve on
{\mathcal{C}om_{n}}
. For the vector
i=(1,1,\dots,1)^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, it is evident that
i,Xi,X^{2}i,\dots,X^{n-1}i
form a basis for
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Therefore, for every point
(x,y)
on the elliptic curve, the triple
(X,Y,i)
represents a point on
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Similarly, by selecting a loop
xy=1
, we can generate the corresponding loop on
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Moreover, let
G
be the unimodular affine Cremona group of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, which is the normal subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}[x,y])
preserving the symplectic form
dx\wedge dy
. The group
G
acts on

Invariant commuting variety

The full description of

When we obtained the equations in Section

.

The coordinate ring of the commuting variety of

5.
On the Grothendieck Ring

In this section we compute the class in the Grothendieck ring of both the Calogero-Moser space and the invariant commuting variety. To do so, we will outline the computations done for the invariant commuting variety, which can be done in a similar fashion for the Calogero-Moser space.

Recall that the Grothendieck ring in the category of quasi-projective complex varieties is the abelian group generated by isomorphism classes of quasi-projective varieties

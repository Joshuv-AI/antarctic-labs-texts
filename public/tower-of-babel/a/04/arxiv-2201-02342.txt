Existence and multiplicity of solutions to a Kirchhoff type elliptic system with Trudinger-Moser growth
Shengbing Deng, Xingliang Tian

Abstract




Abstract
:
This paper deals with the existence and multiplicity of solutions for a class of Kirchhoff type elliptic systems involving nonlinearities with Trudinger-Moser exponential growth. We first study the existence of solutions for the following system:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\|u\|^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\Delta u=\lambda H_{u}(x,u,v)&\ \mbox{in}\ \ \ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
-\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\|v\|^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\Delta v=\lambda H_{v}(x,u,v)&\ \mbox{in}\ \ \ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u=0,v=0&\ \mbox{on}\ \ \ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with smooth boundary,
\|w\|=\big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}dx\big)^{1/2}
,
H_{u}(x,u,v)
and
H_{v}(x,u,v)
behave like
e^{\beta|(u,v)|^{2}}
when
|(u,v)|\rightarrow\infty
for some
\beta>0
,
a_{1},a_{2}>0
,
b_{1},b_{2}>0
,
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>1
and
\lambda
is a positive parameter.
In the later part of the paper, we also discuss a new multiplicity result for the above system with a positive parameter induced by the nonlocal dependence. The Kirchhoff term and the lack of compactness of the associated energy functional due to the Trudinger-Moser embedding have to be overcome via some new techniques.

Keywords:
Kirchhoff type elliptic systems; existence of solutions; multiplicity of solutions; Trudinger-Moser inequality.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider a class of systems of the form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\|u\|^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\Delta u=\lambda H_{u}(x,u,v)&\ \mbox{in}\ \ \ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
-\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\|v\|^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\Delta v=\lambda H_{v}(x,u,v)&\ \mbox{in}\ \ \ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u=0,v=0&\ \mbox{on}\ \ \ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with smooth boundary,
\|w\|=\big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}dx\big)^{1/2}
,
a_{1},a_{2}>0
,
b_{1},b_{2}>0
,
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>1
and
\lambda
is a positive parameter.
H_{u}
and
H_{v}
have the maximal growth which allows treating (
1
) variationally in the Sobolev space
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Here
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
denotes the Sobolev space modeled in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
with the scalar product

\ \langle W,\Phi\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi dx+\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\psi dx,

where
W=(u,v)
and
\Phi=(\varphi,\psi)
, to which corresponds the norm
\|W\|=\langle W,W\rangle^{1/2}
. We shall consider the variational situation in which

\ \big(H_{u}(x,W),H_{v}(x,W)\big)=\nabla H(x,W)

for a function
H:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of class
C^{1}
, where
\nabla H
stands for the gradient of
H
in the variables
W=(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

We mention that a great attention has been focused on the study of problems involving exponential growth nonlinearities, which are related to the famous Trudinger-Moser inequality. Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and denote by
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
the standard first order Sobolev space given by

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)=cl\Big\{u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\ :\ \int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx<\infty\Big\},

with the inner product
\langle u,v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla vdx
and the norm
\|u\|=\langle u,u\rangle^{1/2}
. This space is a limiting case for the Sobolev embedding theorem, which yields
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
1\leq p<\infty
, but one knows by easy examples that
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\not\subseteq L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Hence, one is led to look for a function
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with maximal growth such that

\sup\limits_{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\|u\|\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}g(u)dx<\infty.

It was shown that by Trudinger
[
35
]
and Moser
[
31
]
that the maximal growth is of exponential type. More precisely,
\exp(\beta u^{2})\in L^{1}(\Omega),\ \forall\ u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\ \forall\ \beta>0
, and

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\|u\|\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}\exp(\beta u^{2})dx<\infty,\quad\mbox{if}\ \beta\leq 4\pi,

where
4\pi
is the sharp constant in the sense that the supreme in the left is
+\infty
if
\beta>4\pi
.
Therefore, from this result they have naturally associated notions of criticality and
subcriticality, namely, they say that a function
g:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
has subcritical growth if

\lim_{|u|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|g(x,u)|}{\exp\left(\beta u^{2}\right)}=0,\text{uniformly on }x\in\Omega,\ \forall\beta>0,

and
g
has critical growth on
\Omega
if there exists
\beta_{0}>0
such that

\lim_{|u|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|g(x,u)|}{\exp\left(\beta u^{2}\right)}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{uniformly on }x\in\Omega,\ \forall\beta>\beta_{0},\\[3.0pt]
\infty,&\text{uniformly on }x\in\Omega,\ \forall\beta<\beta_{0}.\end{cases}

We should stress that some results about the existence of solutions for elliptic problems involving exponential nonlinearities has been obtained by Adimurthi, de Figueiredo-do Ó-Ruf, de Figueiredo-Miyagaki-Ruf, do Ó, Lam-Lu, etc
[

and the references therein.
Moreover, de Souza
[

has studied the existence of solutions for a singular class of elliptic systems involving critical exponential growth in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

On the other hand, system (

\displaystyle u_{tt}-\left(a+b\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)\triangle u=g(x,u)

proposed by Kirchhoff

as an extension of the classical D’Alembert’s wave equation for free
vibrations of elastic strings. Kirchhoff’s model takes into account the changes in length of the

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-m\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)\Delta u=f(x,u),&\mbox{in}\ \ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]

where

Motivated by the above results, in the present paper, we will first prove the existence of solutions for a Kirchhoff type elliptic system (
1
), and we will treat both the subcritical case and the critical case, which
we define next. Let
h(x,W)
be a function in
\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We say that

(TM)^{sc}
:

h
has subcritical growth at
\infty
if

\lim_{|W|\to\infty}\frac{|h(x,W)|}{\exp(\beta|W|^{2})}=0,\ \ \text{uniformly on }x\in\Omega,\ \forall\beta>0.

(TM)^{c}
:

h
has critical growth at
\infty
if there exists
\beta_{0}>0
such that

\lim_{|W|\to\infty}\frac{|h(x,W)|}{\exp(\beta|W|^{2})}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{uniformly on }x\in\Omega,\ \forall\beta>\beta_{0},\\[3.0pt]
\infty,&\text{uniformly on }x\in\Omega,\ \forall\beta<\beta_{0}.\end{cases}

Let us introduce the precise assumptions under which our problem is studied. Set

\displaystyle\theta_{max}=

\displaystyle\max\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\},\ \theta_{min}=\min\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}>1,

\displaystyle a_{max}=

\displaystyle\max\{a_{1},a_{2}\},\ a_{min}=\min\{a_{1},a_{2}\}>0,

\displaystyle b_{max}=

\displaystyle\max\{b_{1},b_{2}\},\ b_{min}=\min\{b_{1},b_{2}\}>0.

We give some assumptions on
H
as follows:

(
H_{1}
):

H(x,\cdot,\cdot)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
H(x,W)\geq 0
,
uH_{u}(x,W)\geq 0
,
vH_{v}(x,W)\geq 0
for all
W=(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, uniformly on
x\in\Omega
.

(
H_{2}
):

there exist constants
S_{0},\ M_{0}>0
such that

0<H(x,W)\leq M_{0}|\nabla H(x,W)|
, for all
|W|\geq S_{0}
uniformly on
x\in\Omega
.

(
\overline{H_{2}}
):

there exist
\mu\geq 2\theta_{max}
and
R>0
such that

0<\mu H(x,W)\leq W\cdot\nabla H(x,W)
, for all
(x,|W|)\in\Omega\times[R,+\infty)
.

(
SL
):

\limsup_{|W|\rightarrow 0}\frac{H(x,W)}{|W|^{2}}=0
uniformly on
x\in\Omega
; superlinearity at 0.

(
AL
):

\limsup_{|W|\rightarrow 0}\frac{H(x,W)}{|W|^{2}}=\frac{a_{min}}{2}
uniformly on
x\in\Omega
; asymptotically linearity at 0.

Remark 1.1
.

We observe that condition
(H_{2})
implies that for each
\xi>0
, there exists
R_{\xi}>0
such that

(1.9)

0<\xi H(x,W)\leq|W||\nabla H(x,W)|,\ \ \forall(x,|W|)\in\Omega\times[R_{\xi},+\infty),

then for some

, it holds that

(1.10)

H(x,W)\geq C_{1}|W|^{\xi}-C_{2},\ \ \forall(x,W)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Furthermore, condition

or
(AL)
implies

uniformly on

.
It is worth to mention that condition

is slightly stronger than (

).

We say that

\ \big(a_{1}+b_{1}\|u\|^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi dx+\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\|v\|^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\psi dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}\Phi\cdot\nabla H(x,W)dx,

for all

. We first give the results about the existence of solutions for both subcritical case and critical case:

Suppose that

,

Theorem 1.3

Suppose that

have critical growth

(H_{1})
,
(H_{2}),(SL)
are satisfied. Moreover, if

(
H_{3}

Next, we consider the multiplicity of solutions for problem (

Some preliminary results

We first introduce some famous inequalities as follows, and inspired by those inequalities, we conclude some similar forms of inequalities.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
31
]
Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and

. Then for every
\beta>0
,

\displaystyle\ \int_{\Omega}\exp(\beta|u|^{2})dx<\infty.

Moreover, there exists a constant
C(\Omega)>0
such that

\sup_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\|u\|\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}\exp(\beta|u|^{2})dx\leq C(\Omega),\ \ \forall\beta\leq 4\pi.

Furthermore,
4\pi
is the best constant, that is, the supreme in the left is
\infty
if
\beta>4\pi
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
29
]
Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a sequence of functions in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}=1
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\neq 0
weakly in

. Then for any

\sup_{n}\int_{\Omega}\exp(p|u_{n}|^{2})dx<\infty.

Now, by the above results, we begin to establish the Trudinger-Moser type inequalities in

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
(u,v)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Then for every
\beta>0
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\exp(\beta|(u,v)|^{2})dx<\infty.

Moreover, it holds that

(2.2)

\sup_{(u,v)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2}),\|(u,v)\|\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(\beta|(u,v)|^{2}\right)dx\leq C(\Omega),\ \ \forall\beta\leq 4\pi,

where

is given as in Lemma

.

Proof.

For each
W=(u,v)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have that
|W|^{2}=|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}
. Then by using Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\exp(\beta|W|^{2})dx=&\int_{\Omega}\exp(\beta|u|^{2})\exp(\beta|v|^{2})dx\\
\leq&\left(\int_{\Omega}\exp(2\beta|u|^{2})dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\exp(2\beta|v|^{2})dx\right)^{1/2}<\infty.\end{split}

Thus (
2.1
) holds.

Then we begin to prove (
2.2
). It is sufficiently enough to prove that

(2.3)

\sup_{(u,v)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2}),\|(u,v)\|=1}\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(4\pi|(u,v)|^{2}\right)dx\leq C(\Omega).

Indeed, for each
(u,v)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\|(u,v)\|=1
, then
\|(u,v)\|^{2}=\|u\|^{2}+\|v\|^{2}=1
. If
\|u\|=0
or
\|v\|=0
, then from (
2.1
), we know (
2.3
) holds also. Otherwise,
\|u\|\neq 0
and
\|v\|\neq 0
, then we take
\frac{1}{p}=\|u\|^{2}
,
\frac{1}{q}=\|v\|^{2}
and by the Young’s inequality, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(4\pi|(u,v)|^{2}\right)dx=&\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(4\pi u^{2}\right)\exp\left(4\pi v^{2}\right)dx\\
\leq&\int_{\Omega}\frac{\exp\left(4\pi pu^{2}\right)}{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\exp\left(4\pi qv^{2}\right)}{q}dx\\
=&\|u\|^{2}\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(4\pi\left(\frac{u}{\|u\|}\right)^{2}\right)dx+\|v\|^{2}\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(4\pi\left(\frac{v}{\|v\|}\right)^{2}\right)dx\\
\leq&\|u\|^{2}C(\Omega)+\|v\|^{2}C(\Omega)=C(\Omega),\end{split}

thus (
2.3
) holds and the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\{W_{n}\}
be a sequence of functions in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\|W_{n}\|=1
such that
W_{n}\rightharpoonup W_{0}\neq(0,0)
weakly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Then for any
0<p<4\pi/(1-\|W_{0}\|^{2})
, we have

\sup_{n}\int_{\Omega}\exp(p|W_{n}|^{2})dx<\infty.

Proof.

Since
W_{n}\rightharpoonup W_{0}
and
\|W_{n}\|=1
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|W_{n}-W_{0}\|^{2}=1-2\langle W_{n},W_{0}\rangle+\|W_{0}\|^{2}\rightarrow 1-\|W_{0}\|^{2}<\frac{4\pi}{p}

Thus, for large
n
we have

\displaystyle p\|W_{n}-W_{0}\|^{2}<4\pi.

Now choosing
q>1
close to 1 and
\epsilon>0
satisfying

\displaystyle qp(1+\epsilon^{2})\|W_{n}-W_{0}\|^{2}\leq 4\pi.

Then Lemma
2.3
indicates that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(qp(1+\epsilon^{2})|W_{n}-W_{0}|^{2}\right)dx\leq C(\Omega).

Moreover, since

\displaystyle p|W_{n}|^{2}\leq p(1+\epsilon^{2})|W_{n}-W_{0}|^{2}+p(1+1/\epsilon^{2})|W_{0}|^{2}

which can be proved by Young’s inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\exp(p|W_{n}|^{2})\leq\exp(p(1+\epsilon^{2})|W_{n}-W_{0}|^{2})\exp(p(1+1/\epsilon^{2})|W_{0}|^{2}).

Consequently, by Hölder’s inequality and (
2.4
), it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\exp(p|W_{n}|^{2})dx\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\exp(qp(1+\epsilon^{2})|W_{n}-W_{0}|^{2})dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\exp(rp(1+1/\epsilon^{2})|W_{0}|^{2})dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\Omega}\exp(rp(1+1/\epsilon^{2})|W_{0}|^{2})dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},

for large
n
, where
r=\frac{q}{q-1}
. By Lemma
2.3
, we know the second term in the last inequality is bounded, thus the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

If
W=(u,v)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2}),\beta>0,q>0
and
\|W\|\leq N
with
\beta N^{2}<2\pi
, there exists
C=C(\beta,N,q)>0
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|W|^{q}\exp(\beta|W|^{2})dx\leq C\|W\|^{q}.

Proof.

We consider
r>1
close to 1 such that
r\beta N^{2}\leq 2\pi
and
sq\geq 1
, where
s=\frac{r}{r-1}
. Using Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|W|^{q}\exp(\beta|W|^{2})dx\leq&\left(\int_{\Omega}\exp(r\beta|W|^{2})dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\|W\|^{q}_{qs}\\
\leq&\left(\int_{\Omega}\exp(2r\beta N^{2}(\frac{u}{\|W\|})^{2})dx+\int_{\Omega}\exp(2r\beta N^{2}(\frac{v}{\|W\|})^{2})dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\|W\|^{q}_{qs}\end{split}

Finally, using the continuous embedding
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{sq}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
and Lemma
2.1
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|W|^{q}\exp(\beta|W|^{2})dx\leq C\|W\|^{q}.

∎

Finally, in order to deal with the dual Kirchhoff functions, in Section
6
we need the following algebraic inequality:

Lemma 2.6
.

If
x,y\geq 0,\ t\geq 1
, then

(2.6)

\displaystyle(x+y)^{t}\leq(2^{t}-1)(x^{t}+y^{t})\leq(2^{t}-1)(x+y)^{t}.

Proof.

It is obvious that
x^{t}+y^{t}\leq(x+y)^{t}
. If
0\leq y\leq x
, then we fix
y
and define
g(x)=(x^{t}+y^{t})-\frac{(x+y)^{t}}{2^{t}-1}
. It holds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}g(y)=&2y^{t}-\frac{2^{t}y^{t}}{2^{t}-1}=\frac{2^{t}-2}{2^{t}-1}y^{t}\geq 0,\\
g^{\prime}(x)=&tx^{t-1}-\frac{t(x+y)^{t-1}}{2^{t}-1}\geq\frac{2^{t-1}-1}{2^{t}-1}(tx^{t-1})\geq 0,\end{split}

thus
g(x)\geq 0
for all
x\geq y
, i.e.
(x+y)^{t}\leq(2^{t}-1)(x^{t}+y^{t})
. On the other hand, if
x\leq y
, the result can be obtained by the same way.
The proof is complete.
∎

3.
The variational framework

We now consider the functional

given by

where

.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under our assumptions, we have that
J_{\lambda}
is well defined and
C^{1}
on
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
for all
\lambda>0
. Moreover,

\begin{split}\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(W),\Phi\rangle=&\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\|u\|^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi dx+\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\|v\|^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\psi dx\\
&-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\Phi\cdot\nabla H(x,W)dx,\end{split}

where
\Phi=(\varphi,\psi)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Proof.

We have that
H_{u}
and
H_{v}
are continuous and have subcritical (or critical) growth
(TM)^{sc}
(or
(TM)^{c}
). Thus, giving
\beta>0
(or
\beta>\beta_{0}
), there exists
C>0
such that
|H_{u}(x,W)|,\ |H_{v}(x,W)|\leq Ce^{\beta|W|^{2}}
for all

. Then,

(3.1)

By
(SL)
(or
(AL)
), given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

(3.2)

|H(x,W)|\leq\frac{a_{min}+\epsilon}{2}|W|^{2}\ \ \text{always that }|W|<\delta.

Thus, using (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and
(H_{3})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|H(x,W)|dx\leq\frac{a_{min}+\epsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}|W|^{2}dx+C_{1}\int_{\Omega}|W|\exp(\beta|W|^{2})dx.

Considering the continuous imbedding
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{s}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
for

and using Lemma
2.5
, it follows that

which implies that
J_{\lambda}
is well defined. Using standard arguments together with Lemma
4.3
underlying (see
[
4
, Theorem A.VI]
and

]
), we can see that

with

\begin{split}\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(W),\Phi\rangle=&\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\|u\|^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi dx+\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\|v\|^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\psi dx\\
&-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\Phi\cdot\nabla H(x,W)dx,\end{split}

where
\Phi=(\varphi,\psi)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
∎

From Lemma
3.1
, we have that the critical points of the functional

are precisely the weak solutions of problem (
1
).
In the next two lemmas we check that the functional

satisfies the geometric conditions of the Mountain-pass theorem.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
(H_{2}),\ (SL)
hold and the functions
H_{u},\ H_{v}
have subcritical (or critical) growth
(TM)^{sc}
(or
(TM)^{c}
). Then, for each
\lambda>0
, there exist positive number
\rho_{0}
and
\tau_{0}
such that

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(W)\geq\tau_{0},\ \forall W\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ \|W\|=\rho_{0}.

Proof.

By
(SL)
, given
0<\epsilon<\frac{\lambda_{1}a_{min}}{2\lambda}
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle H(x,W)\leq\epsilon|W|^{2},

always that
|W|<\delta
. On the other hand, for
\beta>0
(subcritical case) or
\beta>\beta_{0}
(critical case), we have that there exists
C_{1}>0
such that
|H_{u}(x,W)|,\ |H_{v}(x,W)|\leq C_{1}|W|^{q-1}\exp(\beta|W|^{2})
for all
|W|\geq\delta
with
q>2
. Thus, by
(H_{2})
, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle H(x,W)\leq\epsilon|W|^{2}+C_{2}|W|^{q}\exp(\beta|W|^{2}),\ \forall(x,W)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\begin{split}J_{\lambda}(W)=&\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{1}\|u\|^{2}+\frac{b_{1}}{\theta_{1}}\|u\|^{2\theta_{1}}\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{2}\|v\|^{2}+\frac{b_{2}}{\theta_{2}}\|v\|^{2\theta_{2}}\Big)-\lambda\int_{\Omega}H(x,W)dx\\
\geq&\frac{1}{2}a_{min}\|W\|^{2}-\lambda\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|W|^{2}dx-\lambda C_{2}\int_{\Omega}|W|^{q}\exp(\beta|W|^{2})dx\\
\geq&\Big(\frac{a_{min}}{2}-\frac{\lambda\epsilon}{\lambda_{1}}\Big)\|W\|^{2}-\lambda C_{2}\int_{\Omega}|W|^{q}\exp(\beta|W|^{2})dx.\end{split}

By Lemma
2.5
, there exists
N>0
such that
\beta N^{2}<2\pi
and we take
\|W\|\leq N
, there exists
C>0
such that
\int_{\Omega}|W|^{q}\exp(\beta|W|^{2})dx\leq C\|W\|^{q}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(W)\geq\Big(\frac{a_{min}}{2}-\frac{\lambda\epsilon}{\lambda_{1}}\Big)\|W\|^{2}-\lambda C_{3}\|W\|^{q},

Since
q>2
, there exists
\kappa_{1}>0
such that
\Big(\frac{a_{min}}{2}-\frac{\lambda\epsilon}{\lambda_{1}}\Big)\kappa^{2}_{1}-\lambda C_{3}\kappa^{q}_{1}>0
.
Consequently, taking
\rho_{0}=\min\{N,\kappa_{1}\}>0
, we can get that
J_{\lambda}(W)\geq\tau_{0}
whenever
\|W\|=\rho_{0}
, where
\tau_{0}=\Big(\frac{a_{min}}{2}-\frac{\lambda\epsilon}{\lambda_{1}}\Big)\rho^{2}_{0}-\lambda C_{3}\rho^{q}_{0}>0
. Thus, the proof is finished.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
(H_{1})
,
(H_{2})
hold and
H_{u},\ H_{v}
have subcritical (or critical) growth

(or

(

for all

, and here we choose

.
Thus,

\displaystyle\begin{split}J_{\lambda}(tW)=&\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{1}\|u\|^{2}t^{2}+\frac{b_{1}}{\theta_{1}}\|u\|^{2\theta_{1}}t^{2\theta_{1}}\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{2}\|v\|^{2}t^{2}+\frac{b_{2}}{\theta_{2}}\|v\|^{2\theta_{2}}t^{2\theta_{2}}\Big)-\lambda\int_{\Omega}H(x,W)dx\\

for all

,

, and

is the Lebesgue measure of

. This yields that

. Setting

with

In order to prove that a Palais-Smale sequence converges to a weak solution of problem (

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose

such that

\displaystyle\|W_{n}\|\leq C,\ \ \ \ \int_{\Omega}u_{n}H_{u}(x,W_{n})dx\leq C,\ \ \ \ \int_{\Omega}v_{n}H_{v}(x,W_{n})dx\leq C.

Proof.

Let

and

\begin{split}\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(W_{n}),W_{n}\rangle=&\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\|u_{n}\|^{2}+\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\|v_{n}\|^{2}\\

Thus for

for some

, where

is bounded since

and

. From (
4.2

4.3

4.1

holds. ∎

Lemma 5.1

(5.3)

\begin{split}\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(W_{n}),W_{n}-W\rangle=&\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\langle u_{n},u_{n}-u\rangle+\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\langle v_{n},v_{n}-v\rangle\\
&-\lambda\int_{\Omega}(W_{n}-W)\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})dx.\end{split}

From
J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(W_{n})\rightarrow 0
in
\big(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\big)_{*}
, we have
\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(W_{n}),W_{n}-W\rangle\rightarrow 0
.
Meanwhile, by subscritical growth condition
(TM)^{sc}
and the Hölder’s inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|W_{n}-W|\cdot|\nabla H(x,W_{n})|dx&\leq C\int_{\Omega}|W_{n}-W|\exp(\beta|W_{n}|^{2})dx\\
&\leq C\|W_{n}-W\|_{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\exp(2\beta|W_{n}|^{2})dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

for all
\beta>0
,
r>1
, and
s=\frac{r}{r-1}
. Choosing
\beta=\frac{2\pi}{C^{2}_{0}}
, we have
2\beta\|W_{n}\|^{2}\leq 4\pi
, then by Lemma
2.3
and the embedding
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{s}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
is compact for
s\geq 2
, the second term in the last inequality converges to zero.
Thus, in (
5.3
), it must be that

\displaystyle\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\langle u_{n},u_{n}-u\rangle+\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\langle v_{n},v_{n}-v\rangle\rightarrow 0

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Since
a_{1},\ a_{2}>0
,
b_{1},\ b_{2}>0
, and
W_{n}\rightharpoonup W
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
, it must be that

\displaystyle\langle u_{n},u_{n}-u\rangle\rightarrow 0\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \langle v_{n},v_{n}-v\rangle\rightarrow 0,

by the semicontinuity of norm, which means
\|u_{n}\|^{2}\rightarrow\|u\|^{2}
and
\|v_{n}\|^{2}\rightarrow\|v\|^{2}
. Then by Brézis-Lieb Lemma
[
5
]
, we have
u_{n}\rightarrow u
and
v_{n}\rightarrow v
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, i.e.
W_{n}\rightarrow W
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
. This proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:
By (
5.2
) and Lemma
5.1
, using Minimax principle, there exists a critical point
W_{0}
for
J_{\lambda}
at level
c_{M,\lambda}
.
We affirm that
W_{0}\neq(0,0)
. In fact, suppose by contradiction that
W_{0}\equiv(0,0)
. Because
J_{\lambda}
is continuous,
J_{\lambda}(W_{n})\rightarrow c_{M,\lambda}
and
W_{n}\rightarrow W_{0}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we can know that
0<c_{M,\lambda}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J_{\lambda}(W_{n})=J_{\lambda}(W_{0})=J_{\lambda}((0,0))=0
, what is absurd. Thus, the proof of Theorem
1.2
is complete.
∎

5.2.
Critical case: Proof of Theorem
1.3

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that
H_{u},\ H_{v}
have critical growth
(TM)^{c}
, and
(H_{1})
,
(H_{2})
are satisfied. If
\{W_{n}\}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
is a bounded Palais-Smale sequence for
J_{\lambda}
at any finite level with

(5.4)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|W_{n}\|^{2}<\frac{4\pi}{\beta_{0}},

then
\{W_{n}\}
possesses a strongly subsequence, i.e. the functional
J_{\lambda}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition.

Proof.

Let
\{W_{n}\}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
is a bounded Palais-Smale sequence for
J_{\lambda}
satisfying (
5.4
), then up to a subsequence, there is
W=(u,v)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&W_{n}\rightharpoonup W\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2}).\end{split}

By
J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(W_{n})\rightarrow 0
in
\big(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\big)^{*}
, we can get

\displaystyle\begin{split}o_{n}(1)=&\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\langle u_{n},u_{n}-u\rangle+\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\langle v_{n},v_{n}-v\rangle\\
&-\lambda\int_{\Omega}(W_{n}-W)\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})dx,\end{split}

that is,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)(\|u_{n}\|^{2}-\|u\|^{2})+\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)(\|v_{n}\|^{2}-\|v\|^{2})\\
=&\lambda\int_{\Omega}(W_{n}-W)\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})dx+o_{n}(1).\end{split}

From (
5.4
), there exists
\zeta>0
such that
\beta_{0}\|W_{n}\|^{2}<\zeta<4\pi
for
n
sufficiently large and also, there exist
\beta>\beta_{0}
close to
\beta_{0}
and
q>1
close to
1
such that
q\beta\|W_{n}\|^{2}\leq\zeta<4\pi
for
n
sufficiently large. Then by conditions
(TM)^{c}
and
(H_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}(W_{n}-W)\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})dx\right|\leq C_{1}\int_{\Omega}|W_{n}-W|\exp(\beta|W_{n}|^{2})dx,\end{split}

and by the Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
2.3
and Sobolev embedding theorem, we can get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}(W_{n}-W)\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})dx\right|\leq&C_{1}\|W_{n}-W\|_{\frac{q}{q-1}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|W_{n}-W|\exp(\beta|W_{n}|^{2})dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
\leq&C_{2}\|W_{n}-W\|_{\frac{q}{q-1}}\to 0,\end{split}

as
n\to\infty
. Then combining with (
5.5
), we have
\|u_{n}\|\to\|u\|
and
\|v_{n}\|\to\|v\|
. Then by Brézis-Lieb Lemma
[
5
]
, we have
u_{n}\rightarrow u,\ v_{n}\rightarrow v
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, i.e.
W_{n}\rightarrow W
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
and the result follows.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
H_{u},\ H_{v}
have critical growth
(TM)^{c}
, and
(H_{1})
,
(H_{2})
,
(H_{3})
,
(SL)
are satisfied. Moreover, if

(5.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}c_{M,\lambda}<\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{1}\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}+\frac{b_{1}}{\theta_{1}}\big(\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}\big)^{\theta_{1}}\Big),\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{2}\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}+\frac{b_{2}}{\theta_{2}}\big(\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}\big)^{\theta_{2}}\Big)\bigg\}.\end{split}

for some
\lambda>0
, then the functional
J_{\lambda}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition at level
c_{M,\lambda}
.

Proof.

From (
5.2
) and Lemma
4.1
, there exists a bounded Palais-Smale consequence
\{W_{n}\}
for
J_{\lambda}
at level
c_{M,\lambda}
. Then up to a subsequence, still denoted by
\{W_{n}\}
, for some
W_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
, one has

(5.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}W_{n}&\rightharpoonup W_{0}\ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \ H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2}),\\
W_{n}&\rightarrow W_{0}\ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \ L^{s}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2}),\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ s\geq 1\\
W_{n}(x)&\rightarrow W_{0}(x)\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{2},\ \ \mbox{for a.e.}\ \ x\in\Omega.\end{split}

Next, we will make some claims as follows:

Claim 1.

W_{0}\neq(0,0)
.

Proof.

Supposing by contradiction that
W_{0}\equiv(0,0)
. Hence, by Lemma
4.5
and
(SL)
,
\int_{\Omega}H(x,W_{n})\rightarrow 0
and so

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{b_{1}}{\theta_{1}}\|u_{n}\|^{2\theta_{1}}\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{b_{2}}{\theta_{2}}\|v_{n}\|^{2\theta_{2}}\Big)\rightarrow c_{M,\lambda}\\
&<\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{1}\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}+\frac{b_{1}}{\theta_{1}}\big(\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}\big)^{\theta_{1}}\Big),\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{2}\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}+\frac{b_{2}}{\theta_{2}}\big(\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}\big)^{\theta_{2}}\Big)\bigg\}.\end{split}

We can get that for
n
large enough
\|u_{n}\|^{2},\ \|v_{n}\|^{2}<\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}
, so
\|W_{n}\|^{2}=\|u_{n}\|^{2}+\|v_{n}\|^{2}<\frac{4\pi}{\beta_{0}}
. Then by Lemma
5.2
, we have
\|W_{n}\|^{2}\rightarrow 0
. Therefore
J_{\lambda}(W_{n})\rightarrow 0
, what is absurd for
c_{M,\lambda}
and we must have
W_{0}\neq(0,0)
.
∎

Claim 2.
Let
\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{n}\|^{2}=\sigma^{2},\ \lim\inf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|v_{n}\|^{2}=\tau^{2}
. Then
W_{0}=(u_{0},v_{0})
is a weak solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\sigma^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\Delta u=\lambda H_{u}(x,u,v)&\ \mbox{in}\ \ \ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
-\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\tau^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\Delta v=\lambda H_{v}(x,u,v)&\ \mbox{in}\ \ \ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u=0,v=0&\ \mbox{on}\ \ \ \partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

By
J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(W_{n})\rightarrow 0
and Remark
4.4
, we get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\sigma^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{0}\nabla\varphi+\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\tau^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\int_{\Omega}\nabla v_{0}\nabla\psi-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\Phi\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{0})=0,\end{split}

\mbox{for all}\ \Phi=(\varphi,\psi)\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Since
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
is dense in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we conclude this claim.
∎

Claim 3.

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|W_{n}\|^{2}-\|W_{0}\|^{2}<\frac{4\pi}{\beta_{0}}
.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|W_{n}\|^{2}-\|W_{0}\|^{2}=\sigma^{2}-\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\tau^{2}-\|v_{0}\|^{2}\geq\frac{4\pi}{\beta_{0}}
where
\sigma
and
\tau
are given in Claim 2. Thus, at least one between
\sigma^{2}-\|u_{0}\|^{2}
and
\tau^{2}-\|v_{0}\|^{2}
is not less then
\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}
, let
\sigma^{2}-\|u_{0}\|^{2}\geq\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}
. Therefore, from (
5.2
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}c_{M,\lambda}=&\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big[J_{\lambda}(W_{n})-\frac{1}{2\theta_{max}}\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(W_{n}),W_{n}\rangle\big]\\
=&\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Bigg[\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{b_{1}}{\theta_{1}}\|u_{n}\|^{2\theta_{1}}\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{b_{2}}{\theta_{2}}\|v_{n}\|^{2\theta_{2}}\Big)\\
&-\frac{1}{2\theta_{max}}\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\|u_{n}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\theta_{max}}\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\|v_{n}\|^{2}\\
&+\lambda\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2\theta_{max}}W_{n}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})-H(x,W_{n})\right)dx\Bigg]\\
\geq&\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big[\big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\theta_{max}}\big)a_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2}+\big(\frac{1}{2\theta_{1}}-\frac{1}{2\theta_{max}}\big)b_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2\theta_{1}}\Big]\\
&+\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big[\big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\theta_{max}}\big)a_{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2}+\big(\frac{1}{2\theta_{1}}-\frac{1}{2\theta_{max}}\big)b_{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2\theta_{2}}\Big]\\
&+\lambda\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2\theta_{max}}W_{n}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})-H(x,W_{n})\right)dx\\
\geq&\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big[\big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\theta_{max}}\big)a_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2}+\big(\frac{1}{2\theta_{1}}-\frac{1}{2\theta_{max}}\big)b_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2\theta_{1}}\Big]\\
&+\frac{\lambda}{2\theta_{max}}\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\big(W_{n}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})-2\theta_{max}H(x,W_{n})\big)dx.\\
\end{split}

Then by
(H_{3})
, Remark
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}c_{M,\lambda}\geq&\Big[\big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\theta_{max}}\big)a_{1}\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}+\big(\frac{1}{2\theta_{1}}-\frac{1}{2\theta_{max}}\big)b_{1}(\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}})^{\theta_{1}}\Big]\\
&+\frac{\lambda}{2\theta_{max}}\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\big(W_{n}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})-W_{0}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{0})\big)dx.\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\Big[a_{1}\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}+\frac{b_{1}}{\theta_{1}}(\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}})^{\theta_{1}}\Big]\\
&-\frac{1}{2\theta_{max}}\Big[a_{1}\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}+b_{1}(\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}})^{\theta_{1}}-\lambda\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\big(W_{n}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})-W_{0}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{0})\big)dx\Big].\end{split}

Here, we assert

\displaystyle 0\geq a_{1}\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}+b_{1}(\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}})^{\theta_{1}}-\lambda\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\big(W_{n}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})-W_{0}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{0})\big)dx.

Indeed,
\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(W_{n}),W_{n}\rangle\rightarrow 0
and Claim 2 indicate that

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&=\Big(a_{1}+b_{1}\sigma^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\Big)\sigma^{2}+\Big(a_{2}+b_{2}\tau^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\Big)\tau^{2}-\lambda\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}W_{n}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})dx.\\
0&=\Big(a_{1}+b_{1}\sigma^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\Big)\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\Big(a_{2}+b_{2}\tau^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\Big)\|v_{0}\|^{2}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}W_{0}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{0})dx.\end{split}

Subtracting the second equality from the first one, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}0=&\Big(a_{1}+b_{1}\sigma^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\Big)(\sigma^{2}-\|u_{0}\|^{2})+\Big(a_{2}+b_{2}\tau^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\Big)(\tau^{2}-\|v_{0}\|^{2})\\
&-\lambda\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\big(W_{n}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})-W_{0}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{0})\big)dx\\
\geq&\Big(a_{1}+b_{1}\sigma^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\Big)\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}-\lambda\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\big(W_{n}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})-W_{0}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{0})\big)dx\\
\geq&a_{1}\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}+b_{1}(\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}})^{\theta_{1}}-\lambda\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\big(W_{n}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})-W_{0}\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{0})\big)dx.\end{split}

This shows the assertion. Then we can conclude that

\displaystyle c_{M,\lambda}\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big[a_{1}\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}+\frac{b_{1}}{\theta_{1}}(\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}})^{\theta_{1}}\Big],

which contradicts the assumption. Thus this claim is proved.
∎

Claim 4.

J_{\lambda}(W_{0})=c_{M,\lambda}
.

Proof.

By using Lemma
4.5
, and semicontinuity of norm, we have that
J_{\lambda}(W_{0})\leq c_{M,\lambda}
. We will show that the case
J_{\lambda}(W_{0})<c_{M,\lambda}
can not occur. We suppose
J_{\lambda}(W_{0})<c_{M,\lambda}
, and define
\sigma
and
\tau
as in Claim 2, then we get
\sigma>\|u_{0}\|
or
\tau>\|v_{0}\|
. Next, defining
Z_{n}=\frac{W_{n}}{\|W_{n}\|}
and
Z_{0}=\frac{W_{0}}{\sqrt{\sigma^{2}+\tau^{2}}}=\frac{W_{0}}{\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|W_{n}\|}
, we have
Z_{n}\rightharpoonup Z_{0}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\|Z_{0}\|<1
, then Lemma
2.4
which shows that

(5.12)

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\int_{\Omega}\exp(p|Z_{n}|^{2})dx<\infty,\ \ \forall p<\frac{4\pi}{1-\|Z_{0}\|^{2}}.

Since
\sigma^{2}+\tau^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{2}+\tau^{2}-\|W_{0}\|^{2}}{1-\|Z_{0}\|^{2}}
, by Claim 3, it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|W_{n}\|^{2}<\frac{4\pi/\beta_{0}}{1-\|Z_{0}\|^{2}}.

Thus, there exists
\alpha>0
such that
\beta_{0}\|W_{n}\|^{2}<\alpha<\frac{4\pi}{1-\|Z_{0}\|^{2}}
for
n
sufficiently large. For
q>1
close to 1 and
\beta>\beta_{0}
close to
\beta_{0}
we still have
q\beta\|W_{n}\|^{2}\leq\alpha<\frac{4\pi}{1-\|Z_{0}\|^{2}}
, and provoking (
5.12
), for some
C>0
and
n
large enough, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\exp(q\beta|W_{n}|^{2})dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\exp(\alpha|Z_{n}|^{2})dx\leq C.

Then, by using
(H_{2})
and critical growth condition
(TM)^{c}
, (
5.7
), Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev imbedding, we can obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}(W_{n}-W_{0})\cdot\nabla H(x,W_{n})dx\right|\leq&C_{1}\int_{\Omega}|W_{n}|\cdot|W_{n}-W_{0}|dx\\
&+C_{2}\int_{\Omega}|W_{n}-W_{0}|\exp(\beta|W_{n}|^{2})dx\\
\leq&C_{1}\|W_{n}-W_{0}\|_{2}\|W_{n}\|_{2}+C_{3}\|W_{n}-W_{0}\|_{\frac{q}{q-1}}\rightarrow 0.\end{split}

Since
\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(W_{n}),W_{n}-W_{0}\rangle\rightarrow 0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\langle u_{n},u_{n}-u_{0}\rangle+\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\langle v_{n},v_{n}-v_{0}\rangle\rightarrow 0.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\langle u_{n},u_{n}-u_{0}\rangle&=\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\|u_{n}\|^{2}-\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\|u_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\langle u_{n},u_{0}\rangle\\
&\rightarrow\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\sigma^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\sigma^{2}-\big(a_{1}+b_{1}\sigma^{2(\theta_{1}-1)}\big)\|u_{0}\|^{2},\\
\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\langle v_{n},v_{n}-v_{0}\rangle&=\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\|v_{n}\|^{2}-\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\langle v_{n},v_{0}\rangle\\
&\rightarrow\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\tau^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\tau^{2}-\big(a_{2}+b_{2}\tau^{2(\theta_{2}-1)}\big)\|v_{0}\|^{2},\end{split}

which implies that
\sigma=\|u_{0}\|
and
\tau=\|v_{0}\|
, i.e.
\|W_{n}\|\rightarrow\|W_{0}\|
. Then, we must have
J_{\lambda}(W_{0})=c_{M,\lambda}
, what is an absurd. Thus, this claim is proved.
∎

Finalizing the proof of Lemma
5.3
: By Lemma
4.5
and Claim 4, we can know that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big(a_{1}\sigma^{2}+\frac{b_{1}}{\theta_{1}}\sigma^{2\theta_{1}}\Big)+&\Big(a_{2}\tau^{2}+\frac{b_{2}}{\theta_{2}}\tau^{2\theta_{2}}\Big)=\Big(a_{1}\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\frac{b_{1}}{\theta_{1}}\|u_{0}\|^{2\theta_{1}}\Big)+\Big(a_{2}\|v_{0}\|^{2}+\frac{b_{2}}{\theta_{2}}\|v_{0}\|^{2\theta_{2}}\Big).\end{split}

By the semicontinuity of norm, we have
\sigma=\|u_{0}\|,\ \tau=\|v_{0}\|
. Then by Brézis-Lieb Lemma
[
5
]
, we have
u_{n}\rightarrow u_{0},\ v_{n}\rightarrow v_{0}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, i.e.
W_{n}\rightarrow W_{0}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose that
H_{u},\ H_{v}
have the critical growth
(TM)^{c}
and
(H_{1})
,
(H_{2})
,
(H_{3})
,
(SL)
are satisfied. Then, we have
0<c_{M,\lambda}\rightarrow 0
as
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
.

Proof.

Previous lemmas and the definition of
c_{M,\lambda}
(see (
5.1
)) imply
c_{M,\lambda}>0
for each
\lambda>0
. Fixing any nonnegative functions
\Phi=(\phi,\varphi)\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\backslash\{(0,0)\}
, then there exists
t_{\lambda}>0
such that
J_{\lambda}(t_{\lambda}\Phi)=\max_{t>0}J_{\lambda}(t\Phi)
. Since
\frac{dJ_{\lambda}(t\Phi)}{dt}\big|_{t=t_{\lambda}}=0
, then by
(H_{3})
and (
1.10
) we have

(5.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{1}t_{\lambda}^{2}\|\phi\|^{2}+a_{2}t_{\lambda}^{2}\|\varphi\|^{2}+b_{1}t_{\lambda}^{2\theta_{1}}\|\phi\|^{2\theta_{1}}+b_{2}t_{\lambda}^{2\theta_{2}}\|\varphi\|^{2\theta_{2}}=&\lambda\int_{\Omega}t_{\lambda}\Phi\cdot\nabla H(x,t_{\lambda}\Phi)dx\\
\geq&\lambda\left(C_{1}t_{\lambda}^{\xi}\int_{\Omega}|\Phi|^{\xi}dx-C_{2}\right)\end{split}

for some
C_{1},\ C_{2}>0
with
\xi>2\theta_{max}
. This implies that
t_{\lambda}
is bounded from above uniformly in
\lambda
. In addition, we claim
t_{\lambda}\rightarrow 0
as
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
. If not, we can choose a sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
and a constant
t_{0}>0
such that
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
and
t_{\lambda_{n}}\rightarrow t_{0}>0
. Since
\Phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have
\nabla H(x,t_{\lambda_{n}}\Phi)\rightarrow\nabla H(x,t_{0}\Phi)
in
L^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
by
[
12
, Lemma 2.1]
. Then we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\Phi\cdot\nabla H(x,t_{\lambda_{n}}\Phi)dx\rightarrow\int_{\Omega}\Phi\cdot\nabla H(x,t_{0}\Phi)dx.\end{split}

Noting that the right side is strictly positive by
(H_{3})
. Then it follows from (
5.13
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{1}t_{\lambda_{n}}^{2}\|\phi\|^{2}+a_{2}t_{\lambda_{n}}^{2}\|\varphi\|^{2}+b_{1}t_{\lambda_{n}}^{2\theta_{1}}\|\phi\|^{2\theta_{1}}+b_{2}t_{\lambda_{n}}^{2\theta_{2}}\|\varphi\|^{2\theta_{2}}\rightarrow+\infty\end{split}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, this is impossible. Therefore by
(H_{1})
, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\begin{split}c_{M,\lambda}\leq J_{\lambda}(t_{\lambda}\Phi)\leq t^{2}_{\lambda}\frac{a_{1}\|\phi\|^{2}}{2}+t^{2}_{\lambda}\frac{a_{2}\|\varphi\|^{2}}{2}+t_{\lambda}^{2\theta_{1}}\frac{b_{1}\|\phi\|^{2\theta_{1}}}{2\theta_{1}}+t_{\lambda}^{2\theta_{2}}\frac{b_{2}\|\varphi\|^{2\theta_{2}}}{2\theta_{2}}\rightarrow 0\end{split}

as
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
. Thus, this proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
:
By Lemma
5.4
:
0<c_{M,\lambda}\rightarrow 0
as
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
, thus, there exists a constant
\lambda_{*}>0
large enough such that for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{*}
, it holds that

\displaystyle c_{M,\lambda}<\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{1}\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}+\frac{b_{1}}{\theta_{1}}\big(\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}\big)^{\theta_{1}}\Big),\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{2}\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}+\frac{b_{2}}{\theta_{2}}\big(\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{0}}\big)^{\theta_{2}}\Big)\bigg\}.

Lemma
5.3
indicts that the functional
J_{\lambda}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition at level
c_{M,\lambda}

for each
\lambda\geq\lambda_{*}
.

Then by using Minimax principle, there exists a critical point
W_{*}
for
J_{\lambda}
at level
c_{M,\lambda}
for each
\lambda\geq\lambda_{*}
.
We affirm that
W_{*}\neq(0,0)
. In fact, suppose by contradiction that
W_{*}\equiv(0,0)
. Because
J_{\lambda}
is continuous,
J_{\lambda}(W_{n})\rightarrow c_{M,\lambda}
and
W_{n}\rightarrow W_{*}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we can know that
0<c_{M,\lambda}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J_{\lambda}(W_{n})=J_{\lambda}(W_{*})=J_{\lambda}((0,0))=0
, what is absurd. Thus, the proof of Theorem
1.3
is complete.
∎

6.
Multiplicity results

The next lemma motivates us to investigate the multiplicity of solutions in the asymptotically case.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose that
(H_{1})
,
(H_{2})
,
(AL)
hold and
H_{u},\ H_{v}
have subcritical growth
(TM)^{sc}
(or critical growth
(TM)^{c}
). Then, there exists a constant
b_{0}>0
such that for every
b_{min}>b_{0}
, we have a constant
\Lambda_{0}(b_{min})>\frac{a_{max}}{a_{min}}\lambda_{1}
such that there exist constants
\tau_{1}>0
and
0<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}<1
such that

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(W)\geq\tau_{1},\ \ \forall\ W\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\ \mbox{with}\ \rho_{1}\leq\|W\|\leq\rho_{2},

and

\displaystyle-\infty<\inf_{\|W\|\leq\rho_{1}}J_{\lambda}(W)<0,

for all
\frac{a_{max}}{a_{min}}\lambda_{1}<\lambda<\Lambda_{0}(b_{min})
, where
b_{min}>b_{0}
. Furthermore, we can take the constant
\Lambda_{0}(b_{min})
depending only on
b_{min}
such that
\Lambda_{0}(b_{min})\rightarrow\infty
as
b_{min}\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

By assumptions
(AL)
,
(TM)^{sc}
(or
(TM)^{c}
) and
(H_{2})
, we can choose
\beta=\max\{1,2\beta_{0}\}
(that is, for subcritical case we take
\beta=1
and for critical case we take
\beta=2\beta_{0}
) such that

\displaystyle H(x,W)\leq\frac{(a_{min}+1)|W|^{2}}{2}+C|W|^{2}e^{\beta|W|^{2}},\ \forall(x,W)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Thus by Lemmas
2.4
,
2.6
and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

(6.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}J_{\lambda}(W)=&\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{1}\|u\|^{2}+\frac{b_{1}}{\theta_{1}}\|u\|^{2\theta_{1}}\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\Big(a_{2}\|v\|^{2}+\frac{b_{2}}{\theta_{2}}\|v\|^{2\theta_{2}}\Big)-\lambda\int_{\Omega}H(x,W)dx\\
\geq&\frac{b_{min}}{2\theta_{max}}\big(\|u\|^{2\theta_{max}}+\|v\|^{2\theta_{max}}\big)+\frac{a_{min}}{2}\big(\|u\|^{2}+\|v\|^{2}\big)\\
&-\frac{\lambda(a_{min}+1)}{2}\int_{\Omega}|W|^{2}dx-\lambda C\int_{\Omega}|W|^{2}\exp(\beta|W|^{2})dx\\
\geq&\frac{b_{min}}{2\theta_{max}(2^{\theta_{max}}-1)}\|W\|^{2\theta_{max}}-\left[\frac{\lambda(a_{min}+1)}{2\lambda_{1}}-\frac{a_{min}}{2}\right]\|W\|^{2}\\
&-\lambda C\Big(\int_{\Omega}|W|^{4}dx\Big)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(2\beta\|W\|^{2}(W/\|W\|)^{2}\right)dx\right)^{1/2},\end{split}

for all
W\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\|W\|\leq 1
.
If
\|W\|^{2}\leq\min\{1,\frac{\pi}{\beta_{0}}\}
, then from Lemma
2.3
, it holds that

(6.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}J_{\lambda}(W)\geq&\frac{b_{min}}{2\theta_{max}(2^{\theta_{max}}-1)}\|W\|^{2\theta_{max}}-\Big[\frac{\lambda(a_{min}+1)}{2\lambda_{1}}-\frac{a_{min}}{2}\Big]\|W\|^{2}-\lambda C^{\prime}\|W\|^{2}\\
\geq&\frac{b_{min}}{2\theta_{max}(2^{\theta_{max}}-1)}\|W\|^{2\theta_{max}}-\lambda C_{*}\|W\|^{2}\\
=&\frac{b_{min}}{2\theta_{max}(2^{\theta_{max}}-1)}\|W\|^{2}\left[\|W\|^{2\theta_{max}-2}-\frac{2\theta_{max}(2^{\theta_{max}}-1)\lambda C_{*}}{b_{min}}\right],\end{split}

for some constant
C_{*}>0
. Then we choose

(6.3)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}(b_{min}):=\frac{b_{min}}{2\theta_{max}(2^{\theta_{max}}-1)C_{*}}\left(\min\{1,\frac{\pi}{\beta_{0}}\}\right)^{\theta_{max}-1}.

It follows that there exists a constant

(6.4)

b_{0}:=2\theta_{max}(2^{\theta_{max}}-1)C_{*}\left(\min\{1,\frac{\pi}{\beta_{0}}\}\right)^{1-\theta_{max}}\frac{a_{max}}{a_{min}}\lambda_{1}>0

such that

\Lambda_{0}(b_{min})>\frac{a_{max}}{a_{min}}\lambda_{1}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad b_{min}>b_{0}.

Taking
b_{min}>b_{0}
and
\frac{a_{max}}{a_{min}}\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda<\Lambda_{0}(b_{min})
we have

\frac{2\theta_{max}(2^{\theta_{max}}-1)C_{*}\lambda}{b_{min}}<\left(\min\{1,\frac{\pi}{\beta_{0}}\}\right)^{\theta_{max}-1}.

Then from (
6.2
), we can choose
0<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}<1
satisfying

\left[\frac{2\theta_{max}(2^{\theta_{max}}-1)C_{*}\lambda}{b_{min}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2(\theta_{max}-1)}}<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}<\left(\min\{1,\frac{\pi}{\beta_{0}}\}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

such that

(6.5)

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(W)\geq\tau_{1},\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ W\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\ \mbox{with}\ \rho_{1}\leq\|W\|\leq\rho_{2},

for some constant
\tau_{1}>0
.
On the other hand, we claim

(6.6)

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(t\Phi_{1})<0,

if
\frac{a_{max}}{a_{min}}\lambda_{1}<\lambda
and
t>0
is sufficiently small, where
\Phi_{1}=(\phi_{1},\varphi_{1})
is the eigenfunction of
\lambda_{1}
, i.e.
\lambda_{1}=\frac{\|\Phi_{1}\|^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}|\Phi_{1}|^{2}dx}
. Actually, fix
\epsilon>0
such that
\lambda(a_{min}-\epsilon)>\lambda_{1}a_{max}
.
(AL)
implies that there exists a constant
S_{\epsilon}
such that

\displaystyle H(x,W)\geq\frac{a_{min}-\epsilon}{2}|W|^{2},\ \mbox{if}\ 0\leq|W|\leq S_{\epsilon}\ \mbox{ uniformly for}\ x\in\Omega.

Set
\kappa_{\epsilon}>0
such that
\kappa_{\epsilon}\sup_{x\in\Omega}|\Phi_{1}|\leq S_{\epsilon}
. Consequently, by
(H_{1})
we can get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}J_{\lambda}(t\Phi_{1})\leq&\frac{t^{2}a_{max}}{2}\|\Phi_{1}\|^{2}-\frac{t^{2}\lambda(a_{min}-\epsilon)}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Phi_{1}|^{2}dx+t^{2\theta_{1}}\frac{b_{1}\|\phi_{1}\|^{2\theta_{1}}}{2\theta_{1}}+t^{2\theta_{2}}\frac{b_{2}\|\varphi_{1}\|^{2\theta_{2}}}{2\theta_{2}}\\
=&-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\left[\frac{\lambda(a_{min}-\epsilon)}{\lambda_{1}a_{max}}-1\right]a_{max}\|\Phi_{1}\|^{2}+t^{2\theta_{1}}\frac{b_{1}\|\phi_{1}\|^{2\theta_{1}}}{2\theta_{1}}+t^{2\theta_{2}}\frac{b_{2}\|\varphi_{1}\|^{2\theta_{2}}}{2\theta_{2}},\end{split}

for all
0<t<\kappa_{\epsilon}
. Since
2\theta_{1},2\theta_{2}>2
, this concludes the claim. Thus, (
6.5
) and (
6.6
) end the proof.
∎

In the rest of this section, we always suppose that
\frac{a_{max}}{a_{min}}\lambda_{1}<\lambda<\Lambda_{0}(b_{min})
, where
\Lambda_{0}(b_{min})
is obtained in Lemma
6.1
and
b_{min}>b_{0}
.

In order to obtain a weak solution with negative energy, by Lemma
6.1
, we define that

-\infty<c_{0,\lambda}:=\inf_{\|W\|\leq\rho_{1}}J_{\lambda}(W)<0,

where
\rho_{1}
is given as in Lemma
6.1
. Since
\overline{B}_{\rho_{1}}
is a complete metric space with the metric given by the norm of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
, convex and the functional
J_{\lambda}
is of class
C^{1}
and bounded below on
\overline{B}_{\rho_{1}}
, by the Ekeland variational principle, there exists a sequence
\{W^{0}_{n}\}
in
\overline{B}_{\rho_{1}}
such that

(6.7)

J_{\lambda}(W^{0}_{n})\rightarrow c_{0,\lambda}=\inf_{\|W\|\leq\rho_{1}}J_{\lambda}(W)<0\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(W^{0}_{n})\rightarrow 0.

And in order to obtain another weak solution with positive energy, according to Lemmas
3.3
and
6.1
, let

c_{*,\lambda}=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\lambda}}\max_{t\in[0,1]}J_{\lambda}(\gamma(t))>0

be the minimax level of
J_{\lambda}
, where
\Gamma_{\lambda}=\{\gamma\in C\big([0,1],H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\big):\gamma(0)=0,\gamma(1)=E_{0}\}
, where
E_{0}
is given in Lemma
3.3
and here we choose
\|E_{0}\|>\max\{\rho_{0},\rho_{2}\}
. Therefore, by using the Mountain-pass theorem, there exists a sequence
\{W^{*}_{n}\}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfying

(6.8)

J_{\lambda}(W^{*}_{n})\rightarrow c_{*,\lambda}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(W^{*}_{n})\rightarrow 0.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
:
Similarly to the proof of Lemma
5.1
, by using the subcritical growth condition
(TM)^{sc}
, we can get that the functional
J_{\lambda}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition. Then, similarly to the proof of Theorem
1.2
, by (
6.7
), (
6.8
), using Minimax principle, there exist two nontrivial critical points
W_{0,\lambda},\ W_{*,\lambda}
for
J_{\lambda}
at the levels of
c_{0,\lambda}
and
c_{*,\lambda}
, respectively. By compactness condition and the definitions of
c_{0,\lambda}
and
c_{*,\lambda}
, it is easy to verify that
W_{0,\lambda},\ W_{*,\lambda}
are different. Thus, the proof of Theorem
1.4
is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
:
From (
6.7
) and Lemma
6.1
, there exists a sequence
\{W^{0}_{n}\}
in
\overline{B}_{\rho_{1}}
such that

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(W^{0}_{n})\rightarrow c_{0,\lambda}=\inf_{\|W\|\leq\rho_{1}}J_{\lambda}(W)<0\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(W^{0}_{n})\rightarrow 0,

where
\rho^{2}_{1}\leq\min\{1,\frac{\pi}{\beta_{0}}\}
is given in Lemma

. Thus

for all

. Then by Lemma

, the functional

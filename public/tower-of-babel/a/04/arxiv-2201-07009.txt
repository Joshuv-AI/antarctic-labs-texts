The polyhedral geometry of Wajsberg hoops
Sara Ugolini

Abstract
. We show that the category of finitely presented Wajsberg hoops with homomorphisms is dually equivalent to a particular subcategory of rational polyhedra with ℤ \mathbb{Z} -maps. We use the duality to provide a geometrical characterization of finitely generated projective and exact Wajsberg hoops. As applications, we study logical properties of the positive fragment of Łukasiewicz logic. We show that, while deducibility in the fragment is equivalent to deducibility among positive formulas in Łukasiewicz logic, the same is not true for admissibility of rules. Moreover, we show that the unification type of Wajsberg hoops is nullary, while the exact unification type is unitary, therefore showing decidability of admissible rules in the fragment.



1.
Introduction

Residuated structures arise naturally in the study of various algebraic systems, such as ideals of rings
[
46
]
or lattice-ordered groups
[
8
]
, and have acquired a particular relevance in algebraic logic. Most notably, they encompass the equivalent algebraic semantics in the sense of Blok-Pigozzi
[
13
]
of so-called
substructural logics

[
25
]
. The latter are a large class of logical systems including classical logic and several of the most interesting non-classical logics, such as intuitionistic logic, fuzzy logics, and relevance logics to name a few. Algebraizability
à la
Blok-Pigozzi entails that the deducibility relation of an algebraizable logic can be characterized by means of the algebraic equational consequence of its equivalent algebraic semantics. Therefore, interesting properties of algebraizable logics can be studied via algebraic means (for details on algebraizability and a collection of general results on substructural logics see
[
25
]
).

Hoops are a particular variety of residuated monoids related to logic which were defined in an unpublished manuscript by Büchi and Owens, inspired by the work of Bosbach on partially ordered monoids
[
12
]
.
Hoops indeed correspond to commutative monoids endowed with a partial order
\leq
definable by means of the residuum
\to
of the monoid operation as
a\leq b
if and only if
a\to b=1
, and such an order is
natural
, in the sense that
a\leq b
if and only if there exists
c
such that
a=b\cdot c
. The first systematic study of hoops has been carried out by Ferreirim in her PhD thesis
[
21
]
and in her works with Blok
[
10
,
11
]
, while in her work with Aglianò and Montagna
[
2
]
they investigate the connection between hoops and fuzzy logics.

Wajsberg hoops play a special role in this framework. From the algebraic point of view, they can be used to describe subdirectly irreducible hoops, and the whole variety of hoops can be obtained as the join of iterated powers of the variety of Wajsberg hoops, in the sense defined in
[
11
]
.
Wajsberg hoops also have a peculiar connection with lattice-ordered abelian groups (abelian
\ell
-groups for short). In fact, the variety
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
of Wajsberg hoops is generated by its totally ordered members, that are, in loose terms, either negative cones of abelian
\ell
-groups, or
intervals
of abelian
\ell
-groups
[
4
]
.

Besides their purely algebraic interest, Wajsberg hoops are relevant structures in the realm of algebraic logic. Indeed, they are term-equivalent to the
0
-free subreducts of MV-algebras, the equivalent algebraic semantics of the infinite-valued Łukasiewicz logic, seen as residuated lattices. Therefore,
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
is the equivalent algebraic semantics of the positive (i.e.,
0
-free) fragment of the infinite-valued Łukasiewicz logic
Ł
, the latter seen as an extension of the Full Lambek Calculus with exchange
FL
e
, (thus in the language with constants
0,1
and binary connectives
\land,\lor,\cdot,\to
, see
[
25
]
).

We could actually rephrase the representation of totally ordered Wajsberg hoops in terms possibly more familiar to the reader expert in many-valued logics. Namely, totally ordered Wajsberg hoops are either cancellative hoops, i.e. hoops where the monoidal operation satisfies the cancellativity law, or MV-algebras, whose representation in terms of intervals of abelian
\ell
-groups with strong unit actually lifts to a categorical equivalence via the well-known Mundici’s functor
[
41
]
.

In the context of the algebraic semantics of many-valued logics, the relevance of Wajsberg hoops is also related to the study of the equivalent algebraic semantics of Hájek Basic Logic and its positive subreducts (BL-algebras and basic hoops). Given the well-known decomposition result in terms of Wajsberg hoops for totally ordered BL-algebras given by Aglianò and Montagna
[
3
]
, and the representation of free BL-algebras in
[
7
]
building on this result, the understanding of Wajsberg hoops is key to obtain interesting results in this framework.

The categorical approach in studying varieties connected to Wajsberg hoops and MV-algebras has shown to be fruitful in finding interesting connections with categories of
\ell
-groups
[
1
,
20
,
24
,
41
]
. Moreover, both abelian
\ell
-groups and abelian
\ell
-groups with strong order unit (equivalently, MV-algebras), enjoy a deep connection with geometrical objects. In particular, finitely presented structures are dually equivalent, respectively, to categories whose objects are closed polyhedral cones
[
9
]
and rational polyhedra
[
34
]
. In the case of MV-algebras, the geometrical approach has allowed the investigation of logical properties of Łukasiewicz logic (see for instance
[
43
,
34
]
).

In this contribution we will show that finitely presented Wajsberg hoops have an interesting geometrical dual as well, in particular, with what we will call
pointed
rational polyhedra. More precisely, we show how finitely presented Wajsberg hoops are dually equivalent to a (non-full) subcategory of rational polyhedra with
\mathbb{Z}
-maps, given by rational polyhedra in unit cubes
[0,1]^{n}
that contain the lattice point
{\bf 1}=(1,\ldots,1)
, and
pointed

\mathbb{Z}
-maps, that are
\mathbb{Z}
-maps that respect the lattice point
{\bf 1}
. In particular, we obtain this result by first demonstrating that Wajsberg hoops are equivalent to a subcategory of finitely presented MV-algebras, that we shall call in what follows
positive
, and then suitably restricting the Marra-Spada duality
[
34
]
.

The first geometrical approach to the study of Wajsberg hoops is in
[
4
]
, which contains a key step to understand the geometrical duality, that is, the description of the free
n
-generated Wajsberg hoop as a particular subreduct of the free MV-algebra over the same number of generators. We show how this result can be seen as a consequence of both general properties of varieties of positive subreducts and specific characteristics of MV-algebras.

The connection with the MV-algebraic duality with rational polyhedra will allow us to use the deep results obtained by Cabrer and Mundici about finitely generated projective MV-algebras
[
17
,
18
,
15
]
to describe finitely generated projective Wajsberg hoops. In particular, we show that no MV-algebra (or more precisely, its
0
-free reduct) is projective in the variety of Wajsberg hoops, and actually that finitely generated (nontrivial) projective Wajsberg hoops are necessarily unbounded. Interestingly enough, this implies that, in particular, the (
0
-free reduct of the) two-element Boolean algebra
2
is not projective in the variety of residuated lattices, while
2
is projective in every variety of bounded commutative integral residuated lattices, and in the variety of all bounded commutative integral residuated lattices it is the only finite projective algebra
[
5
]
.

The fact that Wajsberg hoops are the equivalent algebraic semantics of the positive fragment of Łukasiewicz logic will allow us to use our algebraic and geometric investigation to derive some analogies and differences between Łukasiewicz logic and its positive fragment, that we shall refer to as
\textbf{\L}^{+}
. In particular, we show that while deducibility in
\textbf{\L}^{+}
coincides with deducibility of positive terms in Łukasiewicz logic, the same is not true for admissibility of rules.

Moreover, via the algebraic approach to unification problems developed by Ghilardi
[
26
]
, we will show that the unification type of the variety of Wajsberg hoops, and thus of the positive fragment of Łukasiewicz logic, is nullary. This is in close analogy with the case of MV-algebras, and indeed our proof adapts to pointed rational polyhedra the pathological example given in
[
34
]
for the case of Łukasiewicz logic.
Moreover, via the algebraic approach to admissibility developed in
[
16
]
, we show that while the exact unification type of Łukasiewicz logic is finitary, the one of its positive fragment is unitary. This in particular implies decidability of the admissibility of rules in Wajsberg hoops and the positive fragment of Łukasiewicz logic.

2.
Preliminaries

We start by recalling the needed facts about hoops, for more details and further references the reader can consult
[
21
,
11
,
2
]
.

A
hoop
is an algebra
{\textbf{A}}=(A,\cdot,\to,1)
such that
(A,\cdot,1)
is a commutative monoid and:

(H1)

x\to x=1
,

(H2)

x\cdot(x\to y)=y\cdot(y\to x)
,

(H3)

(x\cdot y)\to z=x\to(y\to z)
.

A
Wajsberg hoop
is a hoop satisfying the following identity:

(x\to y)\to y=(y\to x)\to x\quad(T)

often called Tanaka’s equation thanks to Tanaka’s work in this framework
[
44
]
, although this particular identity seems to appear later in
[
28
]
.
Every hoop carries a natural order structure definable as:

x\leq y\mbox{ iff }x\to y=1

that is in general a meet-semilattice order, where
\land
is definable as:

x\land y=x\cdot(x\to y).

In the case of Wajsberg hoops this order is actually a lattice order, where the join operation is definable as:

x\lor y=(x\to y)\to y.

By Tanaka’s identity, the defined join operation is commutative, and in fact in the literature on BCK-algebras (T) is also called the
commutativity law
.

Moreover,
(\cdot,\to)
form a
residuated pair
with respect to this lattice order, that is to say:

x\cdot y\leq z\mbox{ iff }y\leq x\to z.

In fact, Wajsberg hoops can be seen as particular commutative residuated lattices. A
commutative residuated lattice
is an algebra
{\textbf{A}}=(A,\cdot,\to,\land,\lor,1)
where

(1)

(A,\cdot,1)
is a commutative monoid

(2)

(A,\land,\lor)
is a lattice

(3)

(\cdot,\to)
form a residuated pair.

We call a commutative residuated lattice
integral
if
1
is the top element in the lattice order, and
bounded
if it is integral and there is also an extra element
0
that is the bottom element of the lattice.
Commutative residuated lattices are a variety called
\mathsf{CRL}
, and their version with an extra constant
0
(not necessarily being the lower bound) is a variety called
\mathsf{FL}_{e}
. The last name comes from logic, as
\mathsf{FL}_{e}
-algebras are the equivalent algebraic semantics of the Full Lambek calculus with exchange (for more details on residuated structures and substructural logics see
[
25
]
). The subvariety of
\mathsf{FL}_{e}
consisting of bounded residuated lattices is called
\mathsf{FL}_{ew}
(where
w
stands for
weakening
in the associated logical system).

With respect to their structure theory, all varieties of residuated lattices are
ideal-determined
in the sense of
[
6
]
, and the role of ideals is played by
congruence filters
, that is, (non-empty) lattice filters closed under products.
In particular, there is a lattice-isomorphism between the lattice of congruence filters and the congruence lattice. Given a commutative integral residuated lattice
A
, the isomorphism is given by the following two maps, where
\theta
is any congruence of
A
and
F
is any congruence filter of
A
:

\theta\mapsto F_{\theta}=\{a\in A:(a,1)\in\theta\},\qquad F\mapsto\theta_{F}=\{(a,b):a\to b,b\to a\in F\}.

Finitely generated (or, equivalently, compact) congruences are then in bijection with finitely generated congruence filters.
Now, given
A
a commutative integral residuated lattice, and
X\subseteq A
, the congruence filter generated by
X
in
A
is the set

{\rm Fil}_{{\textbf{A}}}(X)=\{b\in A:a_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot a_{n}\leq b,\,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in X,\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

It then follows that finitely generated congruence filters correspond to principal congruence filters (it suffices to take the meet of the generators), and analogously finitely generated congruences are principally generated.

Wajsberg hoops fit in the residuated lattice framework since they are term equivalent to the variety
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
of commutative integral residuated lattices satisfying (T), and the divisibility condition:

x\land y=x\cdot(x\to y)\quad(div)

which ensure that the order is natural.
From now on, we shall see a Wajsberg hoop as an algebra in
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
, with the signature of residuated lattices
\{\cdot,\to,\land,\lor,1\}
.

Bounded Wajsberg hoops are term-equivalent to MV-algebras, the equivalent algebraic semantics of infinite-valued Łukasiewicz logic.
MV-algebras are usually introduced (see
[
19
]
) in the signature
\{\oplus,\neg,0\}
, and can be defined as algebras
{\textbf{A}}=(A,\oplus,\neg,0)
such that

(1)

\neg\neg x=x
,

(2)

x\oplus(\neg 0)=\neg 0
,

(3)

\neg(\neg x\oplus y)\oplus y=\neg(\neg y\oplus x)\oplus x
.

As mentioned in the introduction, both Wajsberg hoops and MV-algebras are closely related to lattice-ordered abelian groups, that we shall call
abelian
\ell
-groups
for short. An abelian
\ell
-group is an algebra
{\textbf{G}}=(G,+,-,0,\land,\lor)
where
(G,+,-,0)
is an abelian group,
(G,\land,\lor)
is a lattice, and the group operation distributes over the lattice operations.

Let
G
be an abelian
\ell
-group with a
strong unit

u
, i.e., an element
u
such that for all
x\in G
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
:
x\leq u+\ldots+u
(
n
times). Then Mundici’s
\Gamma
functor constructs an MV-algebra
\Gamma(G,u)=([0,u],\oplus_{u},\neg_{u},0)
defined by:

[0,u]=\{x\in G:0\leq x\leq u\},a\oplus_{u}b=(a+b)\land u,\neg_{u}\,a=u-a
. Every MV-algebra can be obtained in this way
[
41
]
.

Both MV-algebras and abelian
\ell
-groups can be seen in the framework of residuated lattices. The latter are indeed term-equivalent to commutative residuated lattices that satisfy
x\cdot(x\to 1)=1
, via the translation
x\cdot y:=x+y
,
1:=0
, and
x\to y:=-x+y
.

While MV-algebras, as defined above, are term equivalent to the subvariety
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}
of
\mathsf{FL}_{ew}
axiomatized by
(x\to y)\to y=x\lor y
, via the stipulations:

1:=\neg 0,\,0:=0,\,x\cdot y:=\neg(\neg x\oplus\neg y),\,x\to y:=\neg x\oplus y,\,x\lor y=(x\to y)\to y,\,x\land y=x\cdot(x\to y)

and

0:=0,\;\neg x=x\to 0,\;\;x\oplus y=\neg(\neg x\cdot\neg y).

Thus, as it often happens in the literature, in what follows we refer to MV-algebras as algebras in
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}
, in the signature
\{\cdot,\to,\land,\lor,0,1\}
.

Given any abelian
\ell
-group
G
seen as a residuated lattice, we define its
negative cone
to be the algebra
{\textbf{G}}^{-}=(G^{-},\cdot,\to_{1},\land,\lor,1)
on the subset of negative elements
G^{-}=\{x\in G:x\leq 1\}
with
x\to_{1}y=(x\to y)\land 1
. Negative cones of abelian
\ell
-groups are a variety of residuated lattices
[
8
]
, term equivalent to
cancellative hoops
, that is, hoops where the product satisfies the usual cancellativity law. Cancellative hoops are a subvariety of Wajsberg hoops, and moreover, a totally ordered Wajsberg hoop is either bounded (thus, equivalent to an MV-algebra) or cancellative
[
4
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Let us consider the varieties
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
and
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}
, respectively the varieties of Wajsberg hoops and MV-algebras. Both varieties are generated by an algebra defined over the real unit interval
[0,1]
, called
standard
. More precisely,
[0,1]_{\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}}=([0,1],\cdot_{\L},\to_{\L},\min,\max,0,1)
, with
\cdot_{\L}
and
\to_{\L}
being respectively Łukasiewicz t-norm and residuum:

x\cdot_{\L}y=\max\{0,x+y-1\},\;x\to_{\L}y=\min\{1,y-x+1\}.

The standard Wajsberg hoop
[0,1]_{\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}}
is the
0
-free reduct of
[0,1]_{\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}}
.

Since the algebras on
[0,1]
are generic for
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}
and
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
, i.e. they generate the two varieties, by a standard universal algebraic argument (see
[
36
, Lemma 4.98]
) the
n
-generated free algebras are algebras of functions from
[0,1]^{n}
to
[0,1]
generated by the projections and with operations defined componentwise by the ones on the standard algebra.
We point out that the understanding of finitely generated free algebras in this context is meaningful from the logical point of view, since the
n
-generated free algebra is isomorphic to the Lindenbaum algebra of
n
-variable formulas of the corresponding logic.

By the well-known McNaughton’s Theorem, the free
n
-generated MV-algebra is shown to be (isomorphic to) the algebra of McNaughton functions, that is, piecewise linear continuous functions with integer coefficients
[
35
,
42
]
. Thus, to each McNaughton function corresponds an equivalence class of MV-terms in the appropriate free algebra (equivalently, logically equivalent Łukasiewicz formulas). From now on, we will see the free
n
-generated MV-algebra as its representative
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n)
given by the McNaughton functions over
[0,1]^{n}
.

In
[
4
]
the authors show that the free Wajsberg hoop over
n
generators
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
can be characterized in terms of the free MV-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n)
in the following way. Let
{\bf 1}
be the point in the
n
-cube whose coordinates are all
1
, then:

(2.1)

\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)=\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n):f({\bf 1})=1\}.

In what follows we will refer to the McNaughton functions in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
as
Wajsberg functions
.

A key character of the rest of this paper is going to be played by finitely presented Wajsberg hoops.
A
finitely presented algebra
is an algebra with a finite number of generators, satisfying finitely many identities. In a variety
\mathsf{V}
, a finitely presented algebra is then (isomorphic to) a
quotient of a finitely generated free algebra modulo a finitely generated congruence.
In what follows, we will denote with
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{V}}(n)/f
a finitely presented algebra that is the quotient of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{V}}(n)
with respect to a principal congruence filter generated by an element
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{V}}(n)
.
The core of the present manuscript is a representation of finitely presented Wajsberg hoops in terms of geometrical objects. This connection stems from the following observation, that allows to connect principal filters with polyhedra in unit cubes.
Given any function
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n)
, let
O_{f}
be its one-set, i.e.

(2.2)

O_{f}=\{{\bf x}\in[0,1]^{n}:f({\bf x})=1\}.

Similarly, given an MV-term
t
, we will write
O_{t}
for the one-set of its associated McNaughton function.
Then a particular instance of Lemma 4.1 in
[
4
]
yields the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
. Then
g\in{\rm Fil}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)}(f)
if and only if
O_{f}\subseteq O_{g}
.

We shall see in what follows that one-sets of Wajsberg functions correspond to particular
rational polyhedra
. By a
(rational) convex polyhedron
(or
(rational) polytope
) of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
we mean the convex hull of finitely many points of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
(
\mathbb{Q}^{k}
respectively), and a (rational) polyhedron is a finite union of (rational) polytopes.

3.
Positive reducts

As it follows from (
2.1
), the free Wajsberg hoop on
n
generators
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
is (isomorphic to) a particular subreduct of the free MV-algebra over the same number of generators. We shall see how this arises from both general considerations and properties more specific to the MV-algebraic-theory.

Let us first consider a variety
\mathsf{V}
of
\mathsf{FL}_{e}
-algebras and the variety
\mathsf{V}_{0}
of their
0
-free subreducts. This last sentence makes sense because of the following (folklore) fact, which we prove here for the sake of the reader, since we did not find an appropriate reference.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathsf{V}
be a variety of
\mathsf{FL}_{e}
-algebras, then the class
\mathsf{V}_{0}
of its
0
-free subreducts is a variety of commutative residuated lattices.

Proof.

We show that
\mathsf{V}_{0}
is closed under homomorphic images, subalgebras, and direct products.

Suppose first that
{\textbf{S}}\in\mathsf{V}_{0}
is a subreduct of some
{\textbf{A}}\in\mathsf{V}
, and that
{\textbf{R}}=h({\textbf{S}})
for a homomorphism
h
of residuated lattices. Let us call
{\textbf{A}}_{0}
the
0
-free reduct of
A
, then
{\textbf{A}}_{0}\in\mathsf{V}_{0}
and
S
is a subalgebra of
{\textbf{A}}_{0}
, in symbols
{\textbf{S}}\leq{\textbf{A}}_{0}
. Since commutative residuated lattices have the congruence extension property (
[
25
, Lemma 3.57]
,
[
22
]
), if we set
\theta=\ker(h)
, there exists a congruence
\theta_{0}
of
{\textbf{A}}_{0}
such that
\theta_{0}\cap(S\times S)=\theta
. Then it follows from the Third Isomorphism Theorem (
[
14
, Theorem 6.18]
) that
{\textbf{S}}/\theta\leq{\textbf{A}}_{0}/\theta_{0}
. Finally, since
{\textbf{A}}_{0}
is bounded so is
{\textbf{A}}_{0}/\theta_{0}
, which then is the
0
-free reduct of a quotient of
A
. Hence,
{\textbf{R}}=h({\textbf{S}})
is a subreduct of an algebra in
\mathsf{V}
, and
\mathsf{V}_{0}
is closed under homomorphic images.

It is easy to see that
\mathsf{V}_{0}
is closed under subalgebras. Indeed, if
{\textbf{R}}\leq{\textbf{S}}
and
S
is a residuated lattice that is the subreduct of some
{\textbf{A}}\in\mathsf{V}
, it is clear that
R
is a subreduct of
A
as well.

Lastly, we show that
\mathsf{V}_{0}
is closed under direct products. Let
\{{\textbf{S}}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of subreducts of algebras in
\mathsf{V}
, more precisely say that
{\textbf{S}}_{i}\leq{\textbf{A}}_{i,0}
, where each
{\textbf{A}}_{i,0}
is the
0
-free reduct of some
{\textbf{A}}_{i}\in\mathsf{V}
. Then, denoting with
P
and
S
the algebraic operators denoting the class of direct products and subalgebras respectively,

\prod_{i\in I}{\textbf{S}}_{i}\in PS(\{{\textbf{A}}_{i,0}\}_{i\in I})\subseteq SP(\{{\textbf{A}}_{i,0}\}_{i\in I}).

Thus, it follows that
\prod_{i\in I}{\textbf{S}}_{i}
is a subalgebra of the
0
-free reduct of
\prod_{i\in I}{\textbf{A}}_{i}
, completing our proof.
∎

It is worth pointing out that the previous proof works in the very same way for not necessarily commutative varieties of bounded residuated lattices that do have the congruence extension property. Notice also that, in general, the class of
0
-free subreducts of a variety of
\mathsf{FL}
-algebras is a quasivariety. In fact, the closure under subalgebras and direct products requires no special properties, and it can be shown that the subreducts are closed under ultraproducts. The fact that the class of subreducts of a quasivariety is a quasivariety has been shown to hold in general by Mal’cev
[
37
]
.

We will write
\mathbf{T}_{\mathsf{V}_{0}}(X)
for the term algebra over
X
in the language of
\mathsf{V}_{0}
, and
\mathbf{T}_{\mathsf{V}}(X)
for the term algebra over the same set of generators
X
in the language of
\mathsf{V}
. Given any variety
\mathsf{W}
and any set of variables
X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, an
assignment
of
X
into an algebra
{\textbf{A}}\in\mathsf{W}
is a function
h
mapping each variable

to an element of

, say
h(x_{i})=a_{i}\in A
, for

. Then

extends to a homomorphism (that we will also call

) from the term algebra
\mathbf{T}_{\mathsf{W}}(X)
to

. Moreover, given terms
t(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),u(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
over the language of
\mathsf{W}
, we write
{\textbf{A}}\models t\approx u
if for any assignment

of the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
to elements of
A
,
h(t(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}))=h(u(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}))
in

. We write
\mathsf{W}\models t\approx u
if
{\textbf{A}}\models t\approx u
for all
{\textbf{A}}\in\mathsf{W}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathsf{V}
be a variety of
\mathsf{FL}_{ew}
-algebras and
\mathsf{V}_{0}
be the variety of its
0
-free subreducts. Let
X
be any set, then
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{V}_{0}}(X)
is isomorphic to the subalgebra of the
0
-free reduct of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{V}}(X)
generated by
X
.

Proof.

Clearly
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{V}_{0}}(X)
is exactly the subalgebra of the
0
-free reduct of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{V}}(X)
generated by
X
.
Thus, the thesis follows from the fact that, given terms
a,b\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{V}_{0}}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{V}}(X)
,
\mathsf{V}_{0}\models a\approx b
iff
\mathsf{V}\models a\approx b
.
Indeed, if
\mathsf{V}_{0}\not\models a\approx b
, there exists
{\textbf{A}}_{0}
subreduct of
{\textbf{A}}\in\mathsf{V}
that does not satisfy
a\approx b
for some assignments to the variables in
a,b
to elements of
{\textbf{A}}_{0}
. The same elements seen in
A
testify that also
\mathsf{V}\not\models a\approx b
.
Vice versa, if
\mathsf{V}\not\models a\approx b
, for instance
{\textbf{A}}\not\models a\approx b
, also the
0
-free reduct of
A
does not satisfy the identity, which means that
\mathsf{V}_{0}\not\models a\approx b
.
∎

We now focus on the case where
\mathsf{V}=\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}
and
\mathsf{V}_{0}=\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
, where the above proposition can be refined.
We call an MV-term
positive
if it is written in the
0
-free signature
\{\cdot,\to,\land,\lor,1\}
. We call an MV-term
negative
if it is the negation of a positive term, where as usual
\neg x:=x\to 0
.
The following result also follows from
[
7
, Lemma 1]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Every MV-term
t
is either equivalent in
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}
to a positive term or to a negative term.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on the construction of the term
t
.
For the base case, obviously the constant 1 and the generators are positive terms, while
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}\models 0\approx\neg 1
.

Let now
f,g
be positive terms, we show that
f*g,f*\neg g,\neg f*g,\neg f*\neg g
for
*\in\{\cdot,\to\}
are either equivalent to positive terms themselves or their negation is, which implies the claim.

Let us start with
*=\cdot
. Clearly
f\cdot g
is positive. The negation of
f\cdot\neg g
is equivalent to a positive term, indeed in
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}
,
\neg(f\cdot\neg g)=f\to\neg\neg g=f\to g
. For the last case,
\neg(\neg f\cdot\neg g)=(f\to(f\cdot g))\to g
(see
[
4
]
, but it can also be easily checked in the standard MV-algebra), which is positive.

Now we check
*=\to
. Clearly
f\to g
is positive, and
\neg f\to\neg g=\neg(\neg f\cdot g)=g\to f
. Moreover,
f\to\neg g=\neg(f\cdot g)
, thus its negation is equivalent to a positive term. Finally,
\neg f\to g=\neg(\neg f\cdot\neg g)=(f\to(f\cdot g))\to g
which is positive.

Notice that a term cannot be equivalent to both a positive and a negative MV-term. Indeed, if we evaluate on any MV-algebra all variables to
1
, such an evaluation of a positive term gives
1
, while it would give
0
on a negative term.
∎

Notice that in the proof of the lemma we used both involutivity and the identity
\neg(\neg f\cdot\neg g)=(f\to(f\cdot g))\to g
that does not hold in all involutive structures, thus the lemma cannot be generalized outside of
\mathsf{MV}
in a straightforward way.

Let us then call a function
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\sf MV}(n)

positive
if it corresponds to the equivalence class of a positive term.
Then the previous lemma also gives us a different point of view on the description of the finitely generated free Wajsberg hoops in
[
4
]
, indeed we can show that positive McNaughton functions are exactly those that are
1
in
{\bf 1}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

A function
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\sf MV}(n)
is positive if and only if
f({\bf 1})=1
.

Proof.

If a function
f
is positive, then the statement can be easily seen by induction on the construction of a positive term
f
corresponds to, given that the operations are defined pointwise.
Vice versa, since positive terms are
1
in
\bf 1
, negative terms are clearly
0
and the claim follows from Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Thus we get an alternative proof of Theorem 3.1 in
[
4
]
, as a consequence of Propositions
3.2
and
3.4
. A similar line of thought to show Aglianò and Panti’s result has been followed in
[
1
]
, however what we presented here is in higher generality and contains more details, as they are relevant for what follows.

Corollary 3.5
.

The free Wajsberg hoop over
n
generators is isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)=\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n):f({\bf 1})=1\}
.

We will see that the connection described in the last corollary lifts to a categorical equivalence between finitely presented Wajsberg hoops and a subcategory of finitely presented MV-algebras, given by quotients of free MV-algebras with respect to positive McNaughton functions.

As shown in
[
43
]
, finitely presented MV-algebras are isomorphic to algebras of McNaughton functions restricted to rational polyhedra.
In particular, given
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
, we write
\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{P})
, for the algebra of functions in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n)
restricted to
\mathscr{P}
:

(3.1)

\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{P})=\{f_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}}:f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n)\}

Moreover, a finitely presented MV-algebra of the kind
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n)/f
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{M}(O_{f})

[
43
]
.
We shall then now see how one can characterize one-sets of positive McNaughton functions.

Definition 3.6
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we call a polyhedron in
[0,1]^{n}

pointed
if it contains the vertex
{\bf 1}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathscr{P}
is a pointed rational polyhedron.

(2)

\mathscr{P}=O_{f}
for a positive function
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n)
.

Proof.

The proof follows from
[
43
, Corollary 2.10]
, stating that every rational polyhedron in
[0,1]^{n}
is the
1
-set of a McNaughton function
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n)
, and Proposition
3.4
, that shows that a McNaughton function
f
is positive if and only if
f({\bf 1})=1
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Consider a pointed rational polyhedron
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
, then if
g_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}}=h_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}}
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{P})
,
g
is positive if and only if
h
is positive.

Proof.

Since
\mathscr{P}
is pointed, it contains the point
1
.
Thus if two functions
g
and
h
in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n)
coincide over
\mathscr{P}
, they have the same value at
1
and the claim follows.
∎

The previous lemma justifies the following definition. For all unexplained notions of category theory we refer the reader to
[
32
]
.

Definition 3.9
.

We call
positive
an MV-algebra of the kind
\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{P})
, where
\mathscr{P}
is a pointed rational polyhedron.
Let
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n},\mathscr{Q}\subseteq[0,1]^{m}
be pointed rational polyhedra. A homomorphism of positive MV-algebras
\alpha:\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{P})\to\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{Q})
is
positive
if for all positive functions
h\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}}(n)
,
\alpha(h_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}})
is mapped to the restriction of a positive function
k
,
\alpha(h_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}})=k_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{Q}}
.

We call
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}^{+}
the category of positive MV-algebras with positive homomorphisms.

Notice that
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}^{+}
is a category since the identity maps are positive homomorphisms and a composition of positive homomorphisms is still positive. Moreover,
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}^{+}
is a (non-full) subcategory of finitely presented MV-algebras.

Let now
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
be a pointed rational polyhedron. We define the
Wajsberg hoop relative to
\mathscr{P}
,
\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{P})
, to be the algebra of functions in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
restricted to
\mathscr{P}
:

(3.2)

\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{P})=\{f_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}}:f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)\}

Notice that this is a well-defined Wajsberg hoop since the operations of the free algebra are defined pointwise.
We show that, analogously to the MV-algebraic case, all finitely presented Wajsberg hoops are of this kind (up to isomorphism).

Lemma 3.10
.

Given
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
, the map
\omega:\mathcal{W}(O_{f})\to\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)/f
defined as
\omega(g_{\upharpoonright O_{f}})=g/f
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

We first show that the map
\omega
is well-defined, that is, if
g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
, and
g({\bf x})=h({\bf x})
for
{\bf x}\in O_{f}
then
g/f=h/f
. By the correspondence between congruences and filter,
g/f=h/f
if and only if
g\to h,h\to g\in{\rm Fil}(f)
, the congruence filter generated by
f
in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
. This, by Lemma
2.1
, happens if and only if
O_{f}\subseteq O_{g\to h},O_{h\to g}
, that is, for all
{\bf x}\in O_{f},(g\to h)({\bf x})=1
and
(h\to g)({\bf x})=1
. This holds, since
g
and
h
coincide on
O_{f}
.

It is easy to see that
\omega
is an homomorphism. Indeed, clearly
\omega(1_{\upharpoonright O_{f}})=1/f
. Moreover, given
g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
and
*\in\{\cdot,\to\}
, since operations are defined pointwise,
\omega(g_{\upharpoonright O_{f}}*h_{\upharpoonright O_{f}})=\omega((g*h)_{\upharpoonright O_{f}})=(g*h)/f=g/f*h/f=\omega(g_{\upharpoonright O_{f}})*\omega(h_{\upharpoonright O_{f}})
.

We now prove that
\omega
is injective. Suppose
g_{\upharpoonright O_{f}}\neq h_{\upharpoonright O_{f}}
, then there is
{\bf x}\in O_{f}
such that
g({\bf x})\neq h({\bf x})
. Thus either
g({\bf x})\to h({\bf x})<1
or
h({\bf x})\to g({\bf x})<1
, which implies that either
O_{f}\not\subseteq O_{g\to h}
or
O_{f}\not\subseteq O_{h\to g}
, thus either
g\to h
or
h\to g\notin{\rm Fil}(f)
. We conclude that
g/f\neq h/f
, and thus
\omega
is injective. Surjectivity is easily checked, since given
g/f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)/f
,
\omega(g_{\upharpoonright O_{f}})=g/f
.
∎

Therefore we obtain the following.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
A
be a Wajsberg hoop, then the following are equivalent.

(1)

A
is finitely presented.

(2)

{\textbf{A}}\cong\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{P})
for a pointed polyhedron
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Directly follows from Lemma
3.10
and Lemma
3.7
. ∎

Let
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}_{\mathsf{fp}}
be the category of finitely presented Wajsberg hoops with homomorphisms, that is a full subcategory of the algebraic category of Wajsberg hoops.
We now define a map
\Phi:\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}^{+}\to\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}_{\mathsf{fp}}
. For all pointed rational polyhedra
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n},\mathscr{Q}\subseteq[0,1]^{m}
, and positive homomorphism
\alpha:\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{P})\to\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{Q})

(3.3)

\begin{split}\Phi(\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{P}))&=\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{P})\\
\Phi(\alpha):\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{P})\to\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{Q}),\;\Phi(\alpha)(f_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}})&=\alpha(f_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}}).\end{split}

Theorem 3.12
.

Finitely presented Wajsberg hoops with homomorphisms and positive finitely presented MV-algebras with positive homomorphisms are categorically equivalent.

Proof.

The fact that
\Phi
is a functor from
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}^{+}
to
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}_{\mathsf{fp}}
is easy to check and essentially follows from Lemmas
3.7
,
3.8
and
3.10
.

It suffices to show that
\Phi
is full, faithful and essentially surjective (
[
32
, Theorem 1, p. 91]
). Let us first show the latter. Let
A
be a finitely presented Wajsberg hoop, then by what we recalled in the preliminaries in Section
2
,
A
is isomorphic to a quotient of the kind
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)/f
, which by Lemma
3.10
, is isomorphic to
\mathcal{W}(O_{f})
. Since
f
is a positive function,
O_{f}
is a pointed rational polyhedron by Lemma
3.7
.
Since
\Phi(\mathcal{M}(O_{f}))=\mathcal{W}(O_{f})
,
\Phi
is essentially surjective.

We now show that
\Phi
is faithful. Consider two positive homomorphisms
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
from
\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{P})
to
\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{Q})
, that differ on some
f_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{P})
. Since either
f
is positive or
\neg f
is, it follows from the definition and involutivity (
\neg\neg x=x
in
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}
) that
\Phi(\alpha_{1})
and
\Phi(\alpha_{2})
either differ on
f_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}}
or on
\neg f_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}}
. Thus
\Phi
is faithful.

It is left to prove that the functor is full. Let
\beta:\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{P})\to\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{Q})
be a homomorphism of Wajsberg hoops. Consider the homomorphism
\beta^{+}:\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{P})\to\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{Q})
defined as follows. Suppose
\beta(f_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}})=g_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{Q}}
, then define
\beta^{+}(f_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}})=g_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{Q}}
and
\beta^{+}(\neg f_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}})=\neg g_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{Q}}
.
\beta^{+}
is well defined since given
f,h\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n)
that coincide over
\mathscr{P}
, they are either both positive or both negative (Lemma
3.8
). We show that
\beta^{+}
is a homomorphism. It suffices to check that it preserves a constant, product and negation. The fact that it preserves constants and negation follows directly from the definition. For the product, we show one of the cases, the other ones being similar. Let
h,k\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
, and suppose
\beta(h_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}})=x_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{Q}}
and
\beta(k_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}})=y_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{Q}}
, then

\begin{split}\beta^{+}(h_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}})\cdot\beta^{+}(\neg k_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}})=x_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{Q}}\cdot(\neg y_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{Q}})=\neg(x\to y)_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{Q}}\\
=\beta^{+}[\neg(h\to k)_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}}]=\beta^{+}((h_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}})\cdot(\neg k_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}}))\end{split}

Finally, clearly
\Phi(\beta^{+})=\beta
. Thus the functor is full and the proof is complete.
∎

We point out that there always is an adjunction between an algebraic category and the subcategory of the reducts given by the forgetful functor (see
[
1
]
for the case of
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
and
[
40
]
for a general approach). The connection we highlighted here is meant to provide a clear understanding of the geometrical duality in the following section.

4.
Duality with pointed rational polyhedra

In this section we present the duality between finitely presented Wajsberg hoops and pointed rational polyhedra, which follows from the categorical equivalence shown in the previous section, and the restriction to positive MV-algebras of the well-known duality between finitely presented MV-algebras and rational polyhedra in
[
34
]
.

In more details, in
[
34
]
the authors show that finitely presented MV-algebras with homomorphisms are dual to a category whose objects are rational polyhedra
\mathscr{P}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and the morphisms are
\mathbb{Z}
-maps between them. We recall that given a rational polyhedron
\mathscr{P}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a continuous map
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}):\mathscr{P}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, with
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
z
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-map (of
\mathscr{P}
) if for each
j=1,\ldots,m
,
z_{j}
is piecewise linear with integer coefficients.
For details on rational polyhedra and
\mathbb{Z}
-maps the reader is referred to
[
43
]
.

Since any rational polyhedron in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
\mathbb{Z}
-homeomorphic to a rational polyhedron in
[0,1]^{n}
(Claim 3.5 in Theorem 3.4 in
[
34
]
), the category of rational polyhedra in the duality with finitely presented MV-algebras can be restricted to account only for polyhedra in
[0,1]^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Here we choose to use this version of the duality since, although less general from the geometrical point of view, it has a clearer algebraic meaning that will be useful in the following sections: the polyhedra are the one-sets of McNaughton functions generating the principal filter associated to a finitely presented algebra. Another choice that we make here is that, given a finitely presented MV-algebra of the kind
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n)/f
, we consider the associated polyhedron to be
O_{f}
, the one-set of the function
f
, instead of (equivalently, in the case of MV-algebras, see
[
43
, Corollary 2.10 ]
) picking the zero-set of the function
f
.

By Lemma
3.7
, pointed polyhedra are mapped to positive MV-algebras by the Marra-Spada duality in
[
34
]
. We will now show what positive homomorphisms correspond to on the geometrical side. In the full duality in
[
34
]
, homomorphisms between finitely presented MV-algebras are exactly those obtainable in the following way via the functor
\mathcal{M}
from the category of rational polyhedra to finitely presented MV-algebras that established the duality. Let
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n},\mathscr{Q}\subseteq[0,1]^{m}
be rational polyhedra, and
\lambda:\mathscr{P}\to\mathscr{Q}
a
\mathbb{Z}
-map. Then the map dual to
\lambda
,
\mathcal{M}(\lambda)
, is such that:

f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{Q})\mapsto f\circ\lambda\in\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{P})

and all homomorphisms between
\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{Q})
and
\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{P})
can be obtained in this way. Therefore we obtain the following characterization.

Lemma 4.1
.

Given positive functions
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n),g\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(m)
, the following are equivalent.

(1)

\alpha:\mathcal{M}(O_{f})\to\mathcal{M}(O_{g})
is a positive homomorphism of positive finitely presented MV-algebras.

(2)

\alpha=\mathcal{M}(\lambda)
, where
\lambda:O_{g}\to O_{f}
maps
{\bf 1}\in O_{g}
to
{\bf 1}\in O_{f}
.

Proof.

(2) implies (1) by Proposition
3.4
. Vice versa, suppose (1) holds. Then by the duality in
[
34
]
, every such homomorphism
\alpha
is of the kind
\mathcal{M}(\lambda)
, for some
\mathbb{Z}
-map
\lambda
.
\lambda
necessarily maps
1
to
1
. Indeed, if not,
\alpha=\mathcal{M}(\lambda)
would not map (the restriction of) the identity map, which is positive, to a positive function, and therefore
\alpha
would not be positive, a contradiction.
∎

Thus we shall define the following class of
\mathbb{Z}
-maps.

Definition 4.2
.

Given pointed rational polyhedra
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
and
\mathscr{Q}\subseteq[0,1]^{m}
, we shall call a map
\zeta:=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}):\mathscr{P}\to\mathscr{Q}
a
pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-map
if it is the restriction to
\mathscr{P}
and co-restriction to
\mathscr{Q}
of a
\mathbb{Z}
-map from
[0,1]^{n}
to
[0,1]^{m}
such that
\zeta({\bf 1})={\bf 1}
.

Since
\mathbb{Z}
-maps are closed under compositions, so are pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-maps.
Examples of pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-maps are given by the identity function, a function constantly equal to
1
, the projection of a pointed polyhedron
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
onto the hyperplane
x^{n}=1
. Moreover, every Wajsberg function
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
is a pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-map of
[0,1]^{n}
into
[0,1]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
be a pointed rational polyhedron and consider a continuous function
\zeta=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}):\mathscr{P}\to[0,1]^{m}
.
\zeta
is a pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-map if and only if there are Wajsberg functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
such that
\zeta=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}}
.

Proof.

The statement follows from
[
43
, Proposition 3.2]
. Indeed,
\zeta
is a pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-map iff
\zeta=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}}
for some McNaughton functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n)
such that
f_{i}({\bf 1})=1
for
i=1,\ldots m
, iff
\zeta=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})_{\upharpoonright\mathscr{P}}
for some Wajsberg functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
.
∎

Let
\mathsf{P^{+}_{\mathbb{Z}}}
be the category of pointed rational polyhedra with pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-maps. Let
\mathcal{M}
be the previously described functor in the duality for MV-algebras, from rational polyhedra to finitely presented MV-algebras. Composing the restriction of
\mathcal{M}
to the category of
\mathsf{P^{+}_{\mathbb{Z}}}
with the functor
\Phi
(notice that the composition is well defined thanks to Lemmas
4.1
and
3.7
), we obtain a functor
\mathcal{W}
from
\mathsf{P^{+}_{\mathbb{Z}}}
to the category of finitely presented Wajsberg hoops. In particular, given pointed rational polyhedra
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
and
\mathscr{Q}\subseteq[0,1]^{m}
, and
\zeta:\mathscr{P}\to\mathscr{Q}
a pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-map:

•

\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{P})
is the algebra of restricted Wajsberg function as in
3.2
.

•

Given
f\in\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{Q})
,
\mathcal{W}(\zeta)(f)=f\circ\zeta\in\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{P})
.

We then obtain the following result.

Theorem 4.4

(Duality for finitely presented Wajsberg hoops)
.

The functor
\mathcal{W}
yields a duality between the category of pointed rational polyhedra
\mathsf{P^{+}_{\mathbb{Z}}}
and the category of finitely presented Wajsberg hoops
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}_{\mathsf{fp}}
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemmas
4.1
and
3.7
that the duality in
[
34
, Theorem 3.4]
restricts to
a duality between the categories
\mathsf{P^{+}_{\mathbb{Z}}}
and positive finitely presented MV-algebras with positive homomorphisms.
The claim then follows by the categorical equivalence in Theorem
3.12
.
∎

The duality we obtained has the very same essence of the one about finitely presented MV-algebras, indeed it could have been shown by adapting the proofs in
[
34
]
. Here however, we decided to highlight the connection between finitely presented Wajsberg hoops and the subcategory of positive finitely presented MV-algebras which we believe has interest of its own.

We point out the following description of monomorphisms and epimorphisms in the category of pointed polyhedra, that is, left and right cancellable morphisms respectively.
Following
[
15
]
, let us call
strict
a pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-map that is a
\mathbb{Z}
-homeomorphism (a homeomorphism via a
\mathbb{Z}
-map whose inverse is also a
\mathbb{Z}
-map) onto its image.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathscr{P},\mathscr{Q}
be pointed rational polyhedra, and
\zeta:\mathscr{P}\to\mathscr{Q}

(1)

(2)

(3)

Proof.

The proof of (3) follows from Theorem

Example 4.6

It is easy to see that

Proposition 4.7

Finitely presented subdirectly irreducible Wajsberg hoops are exactly

Proof.

First, it is well known that the algebras

For the converse direction, we first recall that subdirectly irreducible Wajsberg hoops are totally ordered. Indeed, the prelinearity equation

Thus necessarily

Otherwise, writing each

Notice that the analogous proof for MV-algebras shows that the finite MV-chains are exactly all of the finitely presented subdirectly irreducible MV-algebras.

5.
Projective and exact Wajsberg hoops

In this section we specialize the duality to two interesting classes of finitely presented Wajsberg hoops, that is, finitely generated projective algebras and exact algebras in
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
.

An algebra
P
is
projective
in a class of algebras
\mathsf{K}
if whenever there are
{\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}}\in\mathsf{K}
, a homomorphism
f:{\textbf{P}}\to{\textbf{A}}
and a surjective homomorphism
g:{\textbf{B}}\to{\textbf{A}}
, then there exists
h:{\textbf{P}}\to{\textbf{B}}
such that
f=g\circ h
. This algebraic notion of projectivity corresponds to a categorical notion (where surjective homomorphisms correspond to regular epimorphisms). Equivalently, in a variety of algebras
\mathsf{V}
,
P
is projective if and only if it is a retract of some free algebra
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{V}}(X)
. We recall that
A
is a retract of
F
if there are homomorphisms
i:{\textbf{A}}\to{\textbf{F}}
and
j:{\textbf{F}}\to{\textbf{A}}
such that
j\circ i=id_{{\textbf{A}}}
. Notice that, necessarily,
i
is injective and
j
is surjective.

An algebra
E
in a variety
\mathsf{V}
is called
exact
if it is (isomorphic to) a finitely generated subalgebra of a finitely generated free algebra. It follows that finitely generated projective algebras in a variety are a subclass of exact algebras.

We start by showing that we can indeed use the duality for finitely presented Wajsberg hoops to study exact algebras, and therefore projective algebras, in
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
. The following is the analogue of
[
43
, Corollary 6.6]
, we write the proof here in reference to our previous results for the sake of the reader.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
B
be a finitely generated subalgebra of a finitely presented Wajsberg hoop
A
. Then
B
is finitely presented.

Proof.

Suppose
{\textbf{A}}\cong\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{P})
, for
\mathscr{P}
some pointed polyhedron
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
. Suppose
B
is generated by
g_{1}\ldots g_{k}\in\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{P})
. Then
g=(g_{1}\ldots g_{k})
is a pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-map by Lemma
4.3
, and its image
g(\mathscr{P})\subseteq[0,1]^{k}
is a rational polyhedron (
[
43
, Lemma 3.4]
) and it is pointed because it is the image of a pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-map. By Lemma
4.8
,
{\textbf{B}}\cong\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{Q})
, thus by Theorem
3.11

B
is finitely presented.
∎

As an immediate consequence we get the next proposition.

Proposition 5.2
.

If a
n
-generated Wajsberg hoop is exact (or projective), then it is finitely presented, i.e. isomorphic to
\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{P})
for some pointed rational polyhedron
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
.

The fact that in a variety being projective is equivalent to being a retract of a free algebra (see
[
26
]
), applying the duality of Theorem
4.4
, allows us to get the following analogue of
[
17
, Theorem 1.2]
.
Let us call a
pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-retraction
a
\mathbb{Z}
-map
\zeta:[0,1]^{n}\to[0,1]^{n}
such that
\zeta^{2}=\zeta
. We call the image of a
\mathbb{Z}
-retraction
\zeta
,
\zeta([0,1]^{n})
, a
pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-retract
of
[0,1]^{n}
. The following is the analogue of
[
17
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
34
, Corollary 3.6]
, and it can be proved in a completely analogous way to the latter as a consequence of the duality in Theorem
4.4
.

Theorem 5.3
.

A
is an
n
-generated projective Wajsberg hoop if and only if it is isomorphic to
\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{P})
for a pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-retract
\mathscr{P}
of
[0,1]^{n}
.

The geometrical study of
\mathbb{Z}
-retracts has been carried out in the papers
[
17
,
18
,
15
]
. In particular, necessary conditions for a polyhedron are obtained, that yield a full characterization for the
1

In order to study the analogue of such characterization we need to introduce the notions of contractibility and strong regularity.
We say that a polyhedron is
contractible
if it is homotopically equivalent to a point. Strong regularity is usually defined as a property of triangulations (see
[
43
]
), however Cabrer shows an intrinsic characterization (
[
15
, Theorem 4.4]
) that we follow here: a polyhedron
\mathscr{P}
is
strongly regular
if and only if for each rational vector
v\in\mathscr{P}
and each

, there exists a rational vector
w\in P
such that the Euclidean distance
{\rm dist}(v,w)<\delta
, and, calling
{\rm den}(x)
the least common denominator of the coordinates of a point
x

{\rm den}(v)
and
{\rm den}(w)
are coprime (in the sense that
{\rm gcd}({\rm den}(v),{\rm den}(w))=1
).

Following
[
18
, Corollary 4.4, Theorem 3.5]
, if a rational polyhedron
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-retract then it is: contractible, strongly regular, and it contains a point with integer coordinates. In the one-dimensional case the converse also holds.
Since pointed polyhedra always include a vertex of
[0,1]^{n}
, we get the following.

Theorem 5.4
.

If
\mathscr{P}
is a pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-retract of
[0,1]^{n}
then

(1)

\mathscr{P}
is contractible,

(2)

\mathscr{P}
is strongly regular.

If
\mathscr{P}
is one-dimensional, the converse also holds.

Proof.

The proof is a direct consequence of
[
18
, Theorem 3.5]
.
∎

We can then see geometrically that the trivial algebra is projective, which is not surprising since it is the free algebra over the empty set of generators. Other projective Wajsberg hoops are the algebras in Example
4.9
.

Notice that contractibility implies connectedness, and the polyhedra associated to the algebras
{\textbf{W}}_{n}
for
n\geq 2
are not connected as seen in Example
4.6
.
Since we recall that
{\textbf{C}}_{\omega},{\textbf{C}}^{\omega}_{n}
are not finitely presented, we obtain the following consequences.

Corollary 5.5
.

The subdirectly irreducible Wajsberg hoops
{\textbf{C}}_{\omega},{\textbf{C}}^{\omega}_{n}
,
{\textbf{W}}_{n}
for
n\geq 2
are not projective in
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
, nor in any variety of residuated lattices that contains
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
.

In particular then the
2
-element residuated lattice
{\textbf{W}}_{2}
is not projective in
\mathsf{RL}
,
while its bounded version, the
2
-element Boolean algebra, is projective in every subvariety of
\mathsf{FL}
. Indeed, it corresponds to the free algebra on the empty set of generators.

We can actually obtain stronger negative results. Let us call a Wajsberg hoop
A

weakly projective
if whenever it is a homomorphic image of another Wajsberg hoop
B
, it is also isomorphic to one if its subalgebras.
This notion of weak projectivity is studied in relation to the study of the lattices of subquasivarieties
[
27
]
.
Clearly, being projective implies being weakly projective, but the opposite is in general not true. Notice that a weakly projective Wajsberg hoop is finitely presented by Lemma
5.1
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Nontrivial finitely generated weakly projective Wajsberg hoops are unbounded.

Proof.

Let
A
be
n
-generated and weakly projective in
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
. Then
A
is a homomorphic image of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
, and thus isomorphic to a subalgebra of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
. Since the operations in the free algebra are defined pointwise, and all functions in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
are continuous, it follows that the only idempotent element in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
is the function constantly equal to
1
. Thus a bounded algebra with more than one element cannot be embedded in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
, since there is no element in which to map its (necessarily idempotent) lower bound. Thus
A
is either trivial or unbounded.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Nontrivial finitely generated projective Wajsberg hoops are unbounded.

Thus no finitely generated positive reduct of an MV-algebra is projective in the variety of Wajsberg hoops.

We now turn our attention to the larger class of exact algebras, that can be characterized geometrically via the results in
[
15
]
and the duality of Theorem
4.4
.

Theorem 5.8
.

A finitely generated Wajsberg hoop is exact if and only if it is isomorphic to
\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{P})
, for a pointed rational polyhedron
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
, for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that:

•

\mathscr{P}
is connected;

•

\mathscr{P}
is strongly regular.

Proof.

Suppose
A
is a finitely generated exact Wajsberg hoop. Then by definition,
A
is isomorphic to a finitely generated subalgebra of a (finitely generated) free algebra. By Lemma
5.1

is finitely presented, thus it is isomorphic to
\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{P})
for some pointed rational polyhedron
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
(Theorem
3.11
). Via Theorem
4.4
and Proposition
4.5
, since
A
embeds into a finitely generated free algebra, there is an onto pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-map from
[0,1]^{n}
to
\mathscr{P}
, for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By
[
15
, Lemma 4.12]
, this implies that
\mathscr{P}
is connected and strongly regular.

Vice versa, suppose
A
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{W}(\mathscr{P})
, and
\mathscr{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
is connected and strongly regular. By
[
15
, Lemma 4.12]
, there is an onto
\mathbb{Z}
-map
\zeta:[0,1]^{l}\to\mathscr{P}
for some

. This implies that there is also a pointed
\mathbb{Z}
-map from

to
\mathscr{P}
. Indeed either

, and therefore

is a pointed
\mathbb{Z}

Therefore, by the duality in Theorem

and Proposition

∎

6.
Applications to logic

We will now use the results we obtained in the previous sections to investigate some interesting analogies and differences between MV-algebras and Wajsberg hoops, or, equivalently, between Łukasiewicz logic and its positive fragment
\textbf{\L}^{+}
. More precisely, we consider the infinite-valued Łukasiewicz logic
Ł
seen as an axiomatic extension of the Full Lambek Calculus with exchange
FL
e
, thus in the language with constants
0,1
and binary connectives
\land,\lor,\cdot,\to
(see
[
25
]
for details), and
\textbf{\L}^{+}
is its
0
-free fragment. If, as usual, we define negation by means of the constant
0
,
\neg x:=x\to 0
, notice that
\textbf{\L}^{+}
is also negation-free.

We shall see that, while deducibility in
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
can be reduced to deducibility among positive terms in
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}
, the same does not hold for admissibility of rules. Moreover, we will prove that while the unification type is the same in
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
and
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}
, the exact unification type is not.

We start by showing that quasiequations in Wajsberg hoops can be studied geometrically.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
t,u
be terms in the language of Wajsberg hoops over
n
variables. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}\models(t\approx 1)\Rightarrow(u\approx 1)

(2)

O_{t}\subseteq O_{u}
in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)

(3)

\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}\models(t\approx 1)\Rightarrow(u\approx 1)

Proof.

(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
follows from the fact that the standard Wajsberg hoop on
[0,1]
generates
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
also as a quasivariety
[
11
]
, which means that a quasiequation
(t\approx 1)\Rightarrow(u\approx 1)
holds in
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
if and only if it holds in
[0,1]_{\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}}
. Thus, (1) holds if and only if for each valuation

h
of
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
to
[0,1]_{\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}}
,
h(t)=1
implies
h(u)=1
, which is equivalent to saying that for all
{\bf x}\in[0,1]^{n}
,
f_{t}({\bf x})=1
implies
f_{u}({\bf x})=1
, where
f_{t},f_{u}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
are the Wajsberg functions associated to
t
and
u
respectively. Therefore, (1) and (2) are equivalent.

Given that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{MV}}(n)
,
(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
is shown in
[
43
, Corollary 1.10]
.
∎

As a consequence of algebraizability, we recall that a quasiequation of the kind
(t\approx 1)\Rightarrow(u\approx 1)
is valid in
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}
(
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
) if and only if
t
deduces
u
in
Ł
(
Ł
+
). Thus, we have also shown that deducibility in
\textbf{\L}^{+}
corresponds to deducibility of positive terms in Łukasiewicz logic.

In what follows, given a variety
\mathsf{V}
and two sets of identities
\Sigma,\Delta
in its language, we will write

to mean that for each identity
\delta\in\Delta

\Sigma\Rightarrow\delta
is valid in
\mathsf{V}
.

Another direct consequence of the geometrical representation, and another analogy between Wajsberg hoops and MV-algebras, is that the variety
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
is

in the sense of
[
47
]
: any finitely generated subalgebra of a finitely presented member is finitely presented (Lemma

This entails the fact that
\textbf{\L}^{+}
, as Łukasiewicz logic, has
right uniform (Maehara) interpolation
in the sense of
[
45
]
: a variety
\mathsf{V}
admits right uniform Maehara interpolation if for any finite sets of variables
X,Y
and finite set of equations
\Gamma
over variables in
X\cup Y
, there exists a finite set of equations
\Pi
over variables in
Y
such that
for any finite sets of equations

and
\Delta
over variables in

,

if and only if
\Pi,\Sigma\models_{\mathsf{V}}\Delta
.

Proposition 6.2
.

\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
admits right uniform Maehara interpolation.

Proof.

A variety admits right uniform deductive interpolation if and only if it is coherent and admits deductive interpolation
[
31
, Proposition 2.4]
.
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
is coherent for Lemma

, while deductive interpolation has been shown in
[
39
]
.
Moreover, by
[
45
, Theorem 3.13]
, a variety with right uniform deductive interpolation and the congruence extension property admits Maehara right uniform interpolation, and every commutative variety of residuated lattices has the congruence extension property
[

, Lemma 3.57]
.
∎

As it is clear from the above proof, right uniform Maehara interpolation implies right uniform deductive interpolation (in the sense of
[
45

). We recall that the Maehara interpolation property for Wajsberg hoops holds for the results in
[
39
,
38
]
. However, as

-groups, but unlike MV-algebras,

does not admit

admits left uniform deductive interpolation if for any finite sets

and finite set of equations
\Delta
with variables in

, there exists a finite set of equations
\Pi
over variables in

such that

and for any set of equations

. This property indeed fails trivially, as in
\ell
-groups (
[

), since there is no set of equations
\Pi(y)
such that
\Pi\models_{\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}}y\approx z
.

We shall now move to the main applications of the geometrical interpretation of finitely presented Wajsberg hoops, that involve the two related logical problems of unification and admissibility, via the algebraic approaches developed respectively in
[
26
]
and
[

]
.

6.1.
Unification

Finitely generated projective algebras in a variety that is the equivalent algebraic semantics of a logic are connected to unification problems for their corresponding logics via Ghilardi’s algebraic approach to unification
[
26
]
.

The usual notion of unification problem for a logic consists in a finite set of identities, and a solution or
unifier
is a substitution that makes the identities provable in the logic.

Ghilardi shows that in an algebraizable logic
\mathcal{L}
with equivalent algebraic semantics
\mathsf{V}_{\mathcal{L}}
, one can instead consider the following, equivalent, notion. An algebraic unification problem is a finitely presented algebra
A
in the variety, and a unifier is a homomorphism
u:{\textbf{A}}\to{\textbf{P}}
, where
P
is a projective algebra in
\mathsf{V}_{\mathcal{L}}
.
The underlying idea is that, given a symbolic unification problem given by the set of identities
\{s_{i}\approx t_{i}:i=1\ldots n\}
, where
s_{i},t_{i}
for
i=1\ldots n
are terms in the appropriate language over a set of variables
X
, the corresponding finitely presented algebra is
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{V}_{{\mathcal{L}}}}(X)/\theta
, where
\theta
is the congruence (finitely) generated by the pairs
\{(s_{i},t_{i}):i=1\ldots n\}
.
The algebraic unifiers can be ordered by
generality
in the following way: given two unifiers for a problem
A
, say
u_{1}:{\textbf{A}}\to{\textbf{P}}_{1}
and
u_{2}:{\textbf{A}}\to{\textbf{P}}_{2}
, we say that
u_{1}
is more general than
u_{2}
if there exists an homomorphism
h:P_{1}\to P_{2}
such that
h\circ u_{1}=u_{2}
. This gives a preorder on the set of unifiers, thus one can consider the associated partial order of unifiers that are “equally general”.

Then the
unification type
of the problem is said to be:
unitary
, if there is a maximum in the partial order of unifiers;
finitary
, if there are instead finitely many maximal elements;
infinitary
, if instead there are infinitely many maximal elements;
nullary
, otherwise. The unification type of the variety, and via Ghilardi’s results of the corresponding logic, is the worst unification type occurring for the unification problems in the variety.

In
[
34
]
the authors show that the unification type of MV-algebras, and hence of Łukasiewicz logic, is nullary. Indeed, using the duality with polyhedra, they show a unification problem with a co-final chain of order-type
\omega
of unifiers.

Let us turn our attention to the positive fragment
\textbf{\L}^{+}
, or equivalently, to unification problems in Wajsberg hoops.
First, notice that every unification problem in
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
has a solution, that is, there is always a homomorphism to the projective trivial Wajsberg hoop. Equivalently, the substitution mapping all variables to
1
unifies every identity. Whereas in Łukasiewicz logic, an identity is unifiable if and only if it is classically satisfiable (
[
29
, Lemma 3.3]
), or, from the algebraic point of view, if and only if there is a homomorphism to the
2
-element Boolean algebra, since
2
is projective and a retract of every projective MV-algebra
[
5
]
.

Moreover, while clearly if an identity is unifiable in
\textbf{\L}^{+}
it is unifiable in Łukasiewicz as well via the same substitution, unification in
\textbf{\L}^{+}
does not reduce to unification of positive identities in Łukasiewicz logic, since substitutions in Łukasiewicz logic may also make use of the constant
0
.

Example 6.3
.

Let us define in the language of Wajsberg hoops
x\oplus y:=(x\to(x\cdot y))\to y
, which in
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}
coincides with Łukasiewicz sum, and we write
2x
for
x\oplus x
. Consider now the identity
2x\to x\approx 1
. Since
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}\models 2x\to x\approx x\lor\neg x
, the one-set of
2x\to x
in
[0,1]
is given by
\{0,1\}
. Thus the identity algebraically corresponds to the finitely presented algebra
{\textbf{W}}_{2}
. A unifier for
{\textbf{W}}_{2}
would be a homomorphism to a (finitely generated) projective algebra, that is, a retract of a finitely generated free algebra. Since
{\textbf{W}}_{2}
is bounded, and the only idempotent element of a free finitely generated Wajsberg hoop is the top element, the only possible unifier is the homomorphism to
{\textbf{W}}_{1}
.

Equivalently, the identity only has as a solution in
\mathsf{W}\mathsf{H}
, (the equivalence class of) the substitution
\sigma(x)=1
. Whereas in
\mathsf{M}\mathsf{V}
,
\tau(x)=0
is also a solution, and
\sigma
and
\tau
are incomparable in the poset of unifiers.

Nonetheless, we show that we can suitably adapt the pathological example in
[
34
]
to our case, and conclude that the unification type of Wajsberg hoops, and thus of the positive fragment of Łukasiewicz logic, is nullary as well.

The unification problem we consider is equivalent to the one in
[
34
]
, with its corresponding polyhedron being the boundary of the unit square in
[0,1]^{2}
, which is a pointed rational polyhedron. In the MV-algebraic case, this corresponds to the identity
x\lor\neg x\lor y\lor\neg y\approx 1
, which is equivalent to the (more cumbersome) identity in the negation-free language of Wajsberg hoops:

(6.1)

[((x\to x^{2})\to x)\to x]\lor[((y\to y^{2})\to y)\to y]\approx 1

Direct computation indeed shows that the one-set of

6.2.
Admissibility

Checking the admissibility of rules is a problem closely connected to unification. Given two sets of formulas
\Sigma,\Gamma
in the language of a variety
\mathsf{V}
, we say that
\Sigma\Rightarrow\Gamma
is
\mathsf{V}
-
admissible
if every substitution that unifies all formulas of
\Sigma
in
\mathsf{V}
, it unifies at least one formula of
\Gamma
in
\mathsf{V}
. This is equivalent to the universal formula
\bigwedge\Sigma\Rightarrow\bigvee\Gamma
being valid in the free algebra over
\omega
generators in
\mathsf{V}

[
16
, Lemma 2.4]
. For details on the algebraic approach to admissibility we refer to
[
16
]
,
where the authors show that admissibility is related to what they call
exact unification
.

More precisely, they define a notion of
exact unification type
, based on a preorder on the set of unifiers for a unification problem that is weaker than the generality preorder. Then the authors prove that, given a rule
\Sigma\Rightarrow\Gamma
, it suffices to check admissibility with respect to substitutions in a set
S
which includes all the maximal unifiers in the new preorder
[
16
, Corollary 3.2]
. Here we are interested in the algebraic counterpart of this preorder.

Algebraically, studying the exact unification type corresponds to considering the set of
coaxact unifiers
. Given a finitely presented algebra
{\textbf{A}}\in\mathsf{V}
, a coexact unifier is a homomorphism onto an exact algebra
{\textbf{E}}\in\mathsf{V}
. Coexact unifiers are ordered as algebraic unifiers, that is, given two coexact unifiers for a problem
A
, say
u_{1}:{\textbf{A}}\to{\textbf{E}}_{1}
and
u_{2}:{\textbf{A}}\to{\textbf{E}}_{2}
, we say that
u_{1}
is more general than
u_{2}
if there exists an homomorphism
h:E_{1}\to E_{2}
such that
h\circ u_{1}=u_{2}
.

The study of the exact unification type is relevant since if a variety
\mathsf{V}
has unitary or finitary exact type, and there exists an algorithm for finding a finite complete set of unifiers, then checking admissibility in
\mathsf{V}
is decidable whenever the equational theory of
\mathsf{V}
is decidable.

While in
[
16
]
the authors prove that Łukasiewicz logic has finitary exact type, we will show that Wajsberg hoops, and therefore the positive fragment of Łukasiewicz logic, have instead unitary type.

The following useful fact is the analogue of
[
29
, Theorem 4.1, Corollary 4.2]
, we sketch the proof in our language for the sake of the reader.

Lemma 6.6
.

All free Wajsberg hoops
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{WH}}(n)
, for

, have the same universal theory.

Proof.

The statement is equivalent to showing that all free Wajsberg hoops satisfy the same rules

. Indeed, every such rule is a universal formula, and vice versa, the validity of a universal formula is equivalent to the validity of a set of such rules (see the discussion in

We now sketch the right-to-left direction. First, one can restrict to considering

Given a set of identities

Lemma 6.7

Let
\Sigma=\{t(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\approx 1\}

Let

Conclusions and future work

The duality in terms of rational polyhedra we presented in this manuscript shows to be fruitful to study logical properties of the positive fragment of Łukasiewicz logic, in the same fashion as in the MV-algebraic case. Via this representation, we derived some analogies and differences between Łukasiewicz logic and its positive fragment.

The duality we presented here is meant to have a clear algebraic interpretation, i.e., the pointed polyhedra are exactly the one-sets of the functions in the free Wajsberg hoop, and finitely presented Wajsberg hoops are the algebras of Wajsberg functions restricted to pointed polyhedra. However, a more general approach could be developed, in the lines of
[
33
,
34
]
. Moreover, the connections with the categorical equivalence in
[
24
]
in the more general setting shall be investigated.

Given the representation of free BL-algebras (and basic hoops) in
[
7
]
, the insight given by this work could also be useful to study finitely presented BL-algebras and basic hoops from a geometrical point of view.

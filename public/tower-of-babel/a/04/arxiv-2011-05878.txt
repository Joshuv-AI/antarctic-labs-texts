Kings in Multipartite Hypertournaments
Jiangdong Ai, Stefanie Gerke, Gregory Gutin

Abstract
In his paper “Kings in Bipartite Hypertournaments” (Graphs & \& Combinatorics 35, 2019), Petrovic stated two conjectures on 4-kings in multipartite hypertournaments. We prove one of these conjectures and give counterexamples for the other.

Introduction

Given two integers
n
and
k
,

,
a
k
-
hypertournament

T

contains exactly one of the
k!
tuples whose entries belong to

. For an arc

, we say that

precedes

A 2-hypertournament is merely an (ordinary) tournament. Hypertournaments have been studied in a large number of papers, see e.g.

,

,

Recently, Petrovic

introduced multipartite hypertournaments in a similar way. Let

Let

-hypertournament
(or,

We call

’s

of

The set of arcs of

A

-partite 2-hypertournament is a
p
-partite tournament
.

For

is the set of arcs of

which contain

precedes

We will write

We let

A

such that

We will call

Let

[

[

showed that every hypertournament has a 2-king.

Note that for every

, we have

|A_{H}(u,w)|+|A_{H}(w,u)|=\binom{n-2}{k-2}.
A

of

has the same partite sets as

and for every

and

If

then we can choose either

or
wu
for

For a graph
G=(V,E)
and

Gutin
[

Theorem 1

[

Every multipartite tournament with at most one transmitter contains a 4-king.

Petrovic

Every bipartite

In the same paper he conjectured the following:

Conjecture 3

[

Every multipartite
k

The next conjecture of Petrovic

Theorem 4

[

Every bipartite tournament

[

Every bipartite

In this paper, we will first show a couterexample to Conjecture

The Majority Lemma

The Majority Lemma, Lemma

To prove Lemma

Lemma 6
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph with partite sets
U
and
W

U
have degree at least
p\geq 1
and every vertex in
W
have degree at most
p

has a matching saturating

Proof.

By Hall’s theorem, if for every

|S|\leq|N_{G}(S)|
then

has a matching saturating
U.

Suppose that there is a subset

of

such that
|S|\geq|N_{G}(S)|+1.
Let
e
be the number of edges in the subgraph of

induced by
S\cup N_{G}(S)
and
observe that

p|S|\leq e\leq(|N(S)|-1)p+(2p-1)\leq(|S|-2)p+(2p-1)=|S|p-1,

a contradiction.
∎

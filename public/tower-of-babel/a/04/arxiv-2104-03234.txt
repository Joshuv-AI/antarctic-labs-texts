Bregman circumcenters: basic theory
Hui Ouyang, Xianfu Wang

Abstract
Circumcenters play an important role in the design and analysis of accelerating various iterative methods in optimization. In this work, we propose Bregman (pseudo-)circumcenters associated with finite sets. We show the existence and give explicit formulae for the unique backward and forward Bregman pseudo-circumcenters of finite sets. Moreover, we use duality to establish connections between backward and forward Bregman (pseudo-)circumcenters. Various examples are presented to illustrate the backward and forward Bregman (pseudo-)circumcenters of finite sets. Our general framework for circumcenters paves the way for the development of accelerating iterative methods by Bregman circumcenters.

Legendre functions and Bregman distances

Legendre functions are instrumental for our analysis.

Definition 2.1
.

[
2
, Definition 5.2 and Theorem 5.6]

Suppose that
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
. We say
f
is:

(i)

essentially smooth
,

\operatorname{dom}\partial f=\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f\neq\varnothing
,
f
is Gâteaux differentiable on
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f
, and
\lVert\nabla f(x_{k})\rVert\to+\infty

(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f
converging to some point in
\operatorname{bd}\operatorname{dom}f
.

(ii)

essentially strictly convex

(\partial f)^{-1}
is locally bounded on its domain and
f
is strictly convex on every convex subset of
\operatorname{dom}\partial f
.

(iii)

Legendre

f
is both essentially smooth and essentially strictly convex.

Fact 2.2
.

[
2
, Lemma 7.3(vii)]

Suppose that
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
with
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f\neq\varnothing
, that
f
is Gâteaux differentiable on
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f
, and that

Let

and

\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f
. Then
D_{f}(x,y)=D_{f^{*}}(\nabla f(y),\nabla f(x))

Fact 2.3
.

[
2
, Corollary 5.5 and Theorem 5.10]

Suppose that
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})

is Legendre if and only if

is. In this case, the gradient mapping
\nabla f:\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f\to\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f^{*}

Definition 2.4
.

[

Suppose that
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})

\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f\neq\varnothing

is Gâteaux differentiable on
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f

\displaystyle\operatorname{D}_{f}:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\to\left[0,+\infty\right]:(x,y)\mapsto\begin{cases}f(x)-f(y)-\langle\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle,\quad&\text{if }y\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f;\\
+\infty,\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Moreover, let

\mathcal{H}
. For every

of

of

onto

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{\operatorname{P}}^{f}_{C}(y):=\left\{u\in C\cap\operatorname{dom}f~:~(\forall c\in\operatorname{C})\operatorname{D}_{f}\left(u,y\right)\leq\operatorname{D}_{f}(c,y)\right\},\text{and}

Clearly, one recovers the Euclidean distance

The following result is a generalized version of

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}
be affinely independent points in
\mathcal{H}
, and let
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Set
\operatorname{I}:=\{1,\ldots,m\}
and

\displaystyle p:=z_{0}+(z_{1}-z_{0},\ldots,z_{m}-z_{0})G(z_{1}-z_{0},\ldots,z_{m}-z_{0})^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}-\langle z_{0},z_{1}-z_{0}\rangle\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{m}-\langle z_{0},z_{m}-z_{0}\rangle\\
\end{pmatrix},

where
G(z_{1}-z_{0},\ldots,z_{m}-z_{0})
is the
Gram matrix defined as

\displaystyle G(z_{1}-z_{0},\ldots,z_{m}-z_{0}):=\begin{pmatrix}\lVert z_{1}-z_{0}\rVert^{2}&\cdots&\langle z_{1}-z_{0},z_{m}-z_{0}\rangle\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\langle z_{m}-z_{0},z_{1}-z_{0}\rangle&\cdots&\lVert z_{m}-z_{0}\rVert^{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Then
\left\{x\in\operatorname{aff}\,(\{z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\})~:~(\forall i\in\operatorname{I})~\langle x,z_{i}-z_{0}\rangle=\lambda_{i}\right\}=\{p\}
.

Proof.

Denote by
\Omega:=\left\{x\in\operatorname{aff}\,(\{z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\})~:~(\forall i\in\operatorname{I})~\langle x,z_{i}-z_{0}\rangle=\lambda_{i}\right\}
.
According to the assumption and
[
7
, Fact 2.8]
,

z_{1}-z_{0},\ldots,z_{m}-z_{0}
are linearly independent.
Then by
[
7
, Fact 2.13]
, the Gram matrix
G(z_{1}-z_{0},\ldots,z_{m}-z_{0})
is invertible. Set

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{m}\\
\end{pmatrix}:=G(z_{1}-z_{0},\ldots,z_{m}-z_{0})^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}-\langle z_{0},z_{1}-z_{0}\rangle\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{m}-\langle z_{0},z_{m}-z_{0}\rangle\\
\end{pmatrix}.

So,
p=z_{0}+\alpha_{1}(z_{1}-z_{0})+\alpha_{2}(z_{2}-z_{0})+\cdots+\alpha_{m}(z_{m}-z_{0})\in\operatorname{aff}\,(\{z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\})
is well-defined.

On the other hand, using the definitions of
G(z_{1}-z_{0},\ldots,z_{m}-z_{0})
,
(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m})^{\intercal}

and
p
, we see that

\displaystyle G(z_{1}-z_{0},\ldots,z_{m}-z_{0})\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{m}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}-\langle z_{0},z_{1}-z_{0}\rangle\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{m}-\langle z_{0},z_{m}-z_{0}\rangle\\
\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\langle\alpha_{1}(z_{1}-z_{0})+\cdots+\alpha_{m}(z_{m}-z_{0}),z_{1}-z_{0}\rangle=\lambda_{1}-\langle z_{0},z_{1}-z_{0}\rangle\\
~~~~~~~~~~\vdots\\
\langle\alpha_{1}(z_{1}-z_{0})+\cdots+\alpha_{m}(z_{m}-z_{0}),z_{m}-z_{0}\rangle=\lambda_{m}-\langle z_{0},z_{m}-z_{0}\rangle\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\langle p-z_{0},z_{1}-z_{0}\rangle=\lambda_{1}-\langle z_{0},z_{1}-z_{0}\rangle\\
~~~~~~~~~~\vdots\\
\langle p-z_{0},z_{m}-z_{0}\rangle=\lambda_{m}-\langle z_{0},z_{m}-z_{0}\rangle\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle(\forall i\in\operatorname{I})\quad\langle p,z_{i}-z_{0}\rangle=\lambda_{i}.

Hence,
p\in\Omega
.

In addition, assume
q\in\Omega
. Denote by
L:={\operatorname{span}\,}\{z_{1}-z_{0},\ldots,z_{m}-z_{0}\}
. Then
\{q,p\}\subseteq\Omega\subseteq\operatorname{aff}\,(\{z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\})
implies that
q-p\in L
. Moreover,
\{q,p\}\subseteq\Omega
yields that

\displaystyle(\forall i\in\operatorname{I})\quad\langle q,z_{i}-z_{0}\rangle=\lambda_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle p,z_{i}-z_{0}\rangle=\lambda_{i},

which entails
q-p\in L^{\perp}
. Therefore,
q-p\in L\cap L^{\perp}=\{0\}
, that is,
q=p
.

Altogether, the proof is complete.
∎

7
Conclusions

In this work, we presented basic theory of Bregman circumcenters. We introduced backward and forward Bregman
(pseudo-)circumcenters. We also explored the existence and uniqueness of backward and forward Bregman (pseudo-)circumcenters, and presented explicit formulae of Bregman backward and forward pseudo-circumcenters. Various examples were given to illustrate these Bregman circumcenters.
The connections between backward and forward Bregman (pseudo-)circumcenters were also established
by duality.
We shall pursue applications of Bregman circumcenters in accelerating iterative methods in optimization in future.

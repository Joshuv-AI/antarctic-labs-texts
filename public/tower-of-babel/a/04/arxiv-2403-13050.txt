Tracy-Widom distribution in four-dimensional super-Yang-Mills theories Unité Mixte de Recherche 3681 du CNRS Preprint: IPhT-T24/008
Zoltan Bajnok 1 , Bercel Boldis 1,2 and Gregory P. Korchemsky 3

Abstract
Various observables in different four-dimensional superconformal Yang-Mills theories can be computed exactly as Fredholm determinants of truncated Bessel operators. We exploit this relation to determine their dependence on the ’t Hooft coupling constant. Unlike the weak coupling expansion, which has a finite radius of convergence, the strong coupling expansion is factorially divergent, necessitating the inclusion of nonperturbative, exponentially small corrections. We develop a method to systematically compute these corrections and discuss the resurgent properties of the resulting transseries.



Introduction

The Tracy-Widom distribution is a powerful tool to analyze a wide range of complex systems in physics. Discovered in the study of the statistics of the spacing of eigenvalues in random matrices, it was soon recognised to describe various phenomena in different fields, including quantum chaos, directed polymers, the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang equation, turbulence, etc. The universality of the Tracy-Widom distribution highlights the underlying connections between seemingly different physical phenomena
Corwin et al. 2022
.

It was recently recognized
Beccaria et al. 2022
that the Tracy-Widom distribution describes
a special class of observables in interacting four-dimensional superconformal
\mathcal{N}=2
and
\mathcal{N}=4
Yang-Mills theories (SYM) with the
SU(N_{c})
gauge group.
They include the free energy on a sphere, vacuum expectation values of circular Wilson loops, correlation functions of infinitely heavy half-BPS operators, flux tube correlations.
A remarkable feature of these observables (denoted as
{\cal F}(g)
) is that, for an arbitrary ’t Hooft coupling constant
\lambda=g^{2}_{{}_{\rm YM}}N_{c}
and to leading order in
1/N_{c}
, they can be expressed as determinants of certain semi-infinite matrices

\displaystyle e^{{\cal F}(g)}=\det_{1\leq n,m<\infty}(\delta_{nm}-K_{nm}(g))\,.

(1)

The dependence on the coupling constant
g=\sqrt{\lambda}/(4\pi)
is encoded in the properties of the matrix
K_{nm}(g)
. Surprisingly, it exhibits a universal form across all observables

\displaystyle K_{nm}(g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,\psi_{n}(x)\chi\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{x}}{2g}\Bigr)\psi_{m}(x)\,,

(2)

where the functions
\psi_{n}(x)
satisfy the orthogonality condition
\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\psi_{n}(x)\psi_{m}(x)=\delta_{nm}
and are given by the normalized Bessel functions

\psi_{n}(x)=(-1)^{n}\sqrt{2n+\ell-1}J_{2n+\ell-1}(\sqrt{x})/\sqrt{x}\,.

(3)

The parameter
\ell
is a non-negative integer.
The function
\chi(\sqrt{x}/(2g))
serves as a cutoff function in the integral (
2
). It suppresses the contribution from large
x
, and is conventionally called the symbol of the matrix. Its explicit form and the corresponding value of
\ell
depend on the choice of the observable.

The function (
1
) is known in the mathematical literature as a Fredholm determinant of the truncated Bessel operator
Basor and Ehrhardt 2003
.
It depends on the symbol function
\chi(x)
in a nontrivial way. For
\chi(x)=\theta(1-x)
, the determinant (
1
) yields the Tracy-Widom distribution – it describes the
eigenvalue spacing at the hard edge of the Laguerre unitary ensemble
Forrester 1993
. It can be computed explicitly in terms of solutions to the Painlevé V equation
Tracy and Widom 1994
.

In application to the four-dimensional SYM theories, we encounter the symbol functions of different forms:
In planar
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM, the expectation value of the circular Wilson loop is described by (
1
) and (
2
) for
\chi_{{}_{\rm W}}(x)=-(2\pi)^{2}/x^{2}
and
\ell=2

Beccaria et al. 2023
.
The flux tube correlations correspond to
\chi_{\text{f.t.}}(x)=2/(1-e^{x})
and
\ell=0,1

Beisert et al. 2007
;
Belitsky and Korchemsky 2020a
;
Basso et al. 2020

The four-point correlation function of infinitely-heavy half-BPS operators depends on two cross ratios (

and
\xi
), and it equals the square of the octagon form factor

;
Coronado 2020
. This form factor is given by (
1
) and (
2
) for
\chi_{\rm oct}(x)=(\cosh y+\cosh\xi)/(\cosh y+\cosh\sqrt{x^{2}+\xi^{2}})
and nonnegative integer
\ell

Kostov et al. 2019a
;
Kostov et al. 2019b
;
Belitsky and Korchemsky 2020b
;
Belitsky and Korchemsky 2021
. In
\mathcal{N}=2
SYM, the free energy on the sphere and the circular Wilson loop can be computed using localization

1
) and (

) for
\chi_{\text{loc}}(x)=-1/\sinh^{2}(x/2)
and
\ell=1,2

Beccaria et al. 2023

The presented examples only hint at the broader applicability of (
1
) and (
2

\mathcal{F}(g)
when evaluated for various symbol functions
\chi(x)

Weak coupling expansion.

At weak coupling, changing the integration variable in (
2
) as
x\to(2g)^{2}x
and expanding the integrand at small
g
, one obtains
K_{nm}(g)=O((g^{2})^{n+m+\ell-1})
. This enables us to expand the determinant (
1
) in powers of the matrix

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(g)=-\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits K-\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(K^{2})-\frac{1}{3}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(K^{3})+\dots

(4)

where
{\rm tr}(K^{L})=O(g^{2L(\ell+1)})
. The leading term in (
4
) is given by
Belitsky and Korchemsky 2021

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits K=g^{2(\ell+1)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-g^{2})^{k}{q_{\ell+k+1}(2\ell+2k)!\over k!(2\ell+k)!(\ell+k+1)!^{2}}\,,

(5)

where
q_{k}=2k\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,x^{2k-1}\chi(x)
is a moment of the symbol function.
For this integral to be well-defined, the function
\chi(x)
has to vanish sufficiently fast at infinity. One can verify that all the previously defined symbols, except
\chi_{{}_{\rm W}}(x)
, exhibit the behavior
\chi(x)\sim c\,e^{-x}
, where the normalization constant is
c_{\text{f.t.}}=-2
,
c_{\text{oct}}=2(\cosh y+\cosh\xi)
and
c_{\text{loc}}=-4
.

For
\chi_{{}_{\rm W}}(x)=-(2\pi)^{2}/x^{2}
, it follows from (
2
) that
K_{nm}(g)\sim g^{2}
. As a result, the relation (
5
) is replaced with
\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits K=-a\,(\pi g)^{2}
,
where
a=16\sum_{n\geq 1}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,\psi_{n}^{2}(x)/x=4/\ell
. In this case, the weak coupling expansion (
4
) can be resummed to all orders leading to the exact result
Beccaria et al. 2023

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{{}_{\rm W}}(g)=\log\Big(\Gamma(\ell)(2\pi g)^{1-\ell}{\tt I}_{\ell-1}(4\pi g)\Big)\,,

(6)

where
\tt I_{\ell-1}
is a modified Bessel function of the first kind. The relation (
6
) holds for any coupling constant.

As follows from (
6
),
e^{\mathcal{F}_{{}_{\rm W}}(g)}
is an entire function of the coupling constant.
For the remaining symbols satisfying
\chi(x)\sim c\,e^{-x}
as
x\to\infty
, the weak coupling expansion (
4
) exhibits a finite radius of convergence.
Indeed, replacing the moments in (
5
) with their large

behaviour,
q_{k}\sim c\,(2k)!
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits K\sim(-1)^{\ell}\frac{c}{4\pi}\log\left(g^{2}-g_{\star}^{2}\right)\,,

(7)

where
g_{\star}^{2}=-1/16
, or equivalently
\lambda_{\star}=-\pi^{2}
.
Repeating the same analysis for
\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(K^{L})
, one finds that each term of the expansion (
4
) has a logarithmic singularity at
g^{2}=g_{\star}^{2}
, leading to

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(g)\sim\kappa\log\left(g^{2}-g_{\star}^{2}\right),

(8)

where
\kappa=\arcsin(c/2)\left(\arcsin(c/2)-(-1)^{\ell}\pi\right)/(2\pi^{2})
.

Strong coupling expansion.

The strong coupling expansion of (

The function

;
Korepin et al. 1993
;
Tracy and Widom 1994

The first few coefficients look as

;

\displaystyle A_{0}=2I_{0}\,,\qquad A_{1}=\sqrt{\ell_{\beta}^{2}-\ell^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle A_{2}=-\frac{(4\ell_{\beta}^{2}-1)}{4}I_{1}\,,\qquad A_{3}=-\frac{3(4\ell_{\beta}^{2}-1)}{16}I_{1}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle A_{4}=-\frac{(4\ell_{\beta}^{2}-1)(16I_{1}^{3}+(4\ell_{\beta}^{2}-9)I_{2})}{128}\,,\quad\dots

Here the notation

Nonperturbative corrections

Truncated Bessel operator.

It is advantageous to represent the semi-infinite matrix (
2

\displaystyle\bm{K}_{\chi}\phi(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\,K(x,y)\chi\Big({\sqrt{y}\over 2g}\Big)\phi(y)

(16)

where
\phi(x)
is a test function and
K(x,y)
is given by an infinite sum of normalized Bessel functions (
3

K(x,y)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\psi_{n}(x)\psi_{n}(y)

The determinant of the semi-infinite matrix in (
1

operator (

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(g)=\log\det(1-\bm{K}_{\chi})

(17)

Using the method of differential equations

;
Korepin et al. 1993

Resurgence relations

To investigate the resurgence properties of the strong coupling expansion (
9
), we performed a high precision calculation of the first
400

terms of the perturbative series
f_{\text{oct}}(g)
. It proves convenient to change the expansion parameter to
g^{\prime}=g+\log(2)/\pi
.

Analysis of the numerical data revealed a factorial behavior in the expansion coefficients

\displaystyle f_{\text{oct}}(g)=\sum_{n\geq 1}{\alpha_{n}\over(4\pi g^{\prime})^{n}}\,,\qquad\quad\alpha_{n}\sim\Gamma_{n+1}\,,

(31)

where
\Gamma_{n+1}
is the shorthand notation for
\Gamma(n+1)
.
We therefore expect that a generalized Borel transformation

\mathcal{B}_{\delta}(s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\frac{s^{n+\delta}}{\Gamma_{n+\delta+1}}

(32)

should have singularity for real
s
. Indeed, we used the first
400
terms in (
32
) to construct the diagonal Pade approximant
for
\mathcal{B}_{\delta=0}(s)
. We observed that its poles condense on the real axis for
s<-1
and
s>2
, indicating that
the Borel transform has cuts along the real axis that start at
s=-1
and
s=2
, and potentially extend to higher values as well.

Based on numerical studies, we can parameterize the large order behaviour of the coefficients (
31
) as

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{n}\bigg(a_{k}^{(1)}\Gamma_{n+1-k}+a_{k}^{(2)}{\Gamma_{n+2-k}\over 2^{n+2-k}}+\dots\bigg)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\geq 0}\bigg(b_{k}^{(1)}{\Gamma_{n+1-k}\over 2^{n+1-k}}+b_{k}^{(2)}{\Gamma_{n+2-k}\over 4^{n+2-k}}+\dots\bigg).

(33)

The terms on the first and second lines are of the form
(-1)^{n}a_{k}^{(p)}\Gamma_{n+p-k}/p^{n+p-k}
and
b_{k}^{(p)}\Gamma_{n+p-k}/(2p)^{n+p-k}
, respectively,
with
p
positive integers. They produce cuts of the Borel transform (
32
) at
s=-1,-2,\dots
and

s=2,4,\dots
related to the locations of the non-perturbative corrections (
15
). These are logarithmic cuts for
\delta=0
and square root cuts for half-integer
\delta
.

Choosing
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
we can desingularise the square root cut of
\mathcal{B}_{\frac{1}{2}}(s)

This allowed us to extract the coefficients

Finding non-perturbative corrections at strong coupling is a notoriously difficult problem. Currently, there are very few examples of resurgence in
{\cal N}=4
SYM
Basso and Korchemsky 2009
;
Dorigoni and Hatsuda 2015
;
Aniceto 2016
;
Arutyunov et al. 2017
;
Dorigoni et al. 2021
. Our results provide a systematic treatment and new insights into the strong coupling regime of four-dimensional superconformal gauge theories, inviting further investigations within the AdS/CFT correspondence. In a dual string theory description, the obtained non-perturbative corrections might arise from world-sheet instantons describing specific world-sheet configurations that are intricately linked to the observables under study. In addition, the technique outlined above has important applications for determining the asymptotic behaviour of determinants of Bessel operators (see e.g.
Basor and Ehrhardt 2003
;
Basor and Chen 2003
).

The push-forwards and pull-backs of δ \delta -forms and applications to non-archimedean Arakelov geometry
Yulin Cai

Abstract
We study two kinds of push-forwards of δ \delta -forms and define the pull-backs of δ \delta -forms. As a generalization of Gubler-Künnemann, we prove the projection formula and the tropical Poincaré-Lelong formula. As an application, we follow the idea in [ 10 ] and generalize the notion of δ \delta -forms on algebraic varieties, this allows us to define the first Chern forms for any piecewise smooth metrics.

Introduction

In
[
6
]

X
(not necessarily algebraic or compact) over a complete non-archimedean field

[

In
[
10

\delta
-forms and
\delta
-currents on algebraic varieties. With their theory, they define the first Chern form

for a metrized line bundle
\overline{L}
, then the measure
c_{1}(\overline{L})^{n}
comes naturally. Moreover, they define the first Chern form for a so-called
\delta
-metric, which is a special piecewise smooth metric. They show that the canonical metrics of line bundles on abelian varieties are
\delta
-metrics.

Inspired by Gubler and Künnemann’s paper
[
10
]
, Mihatsch introduces the
\delta
-forms on tropical geometry and construct intersection product in
[
14
]
. In his another recent paper
[
13
]
, he considers
\delta
-forms on tropical spaces. Since the skeletons of good Berkovich spaces satisfy a tropical balance condition, he is able to define
\delta
-forms on good, strict Berkovich spaces.

The main object in this paper is to develop the theory of
\delta
-forms on tropical geometry, including the push-forward and pull-back and some important formulas: projection formula, tropical Poincaré-Lelong formula. As an application, following the idea in
[
10
]
, we can have more
\delta
-forms on algebraic varieties comparing to
[
10
]
, and we can define the first Chern form for any piecewise smooth metrics. Using the tropical Poincaré-Lelong formula, we prove the Poincaré-Lelong formula for
\delta
-currents.

2.1
Superforms

Definition 2.1
.

For an open subset
U\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
, a
(p,q)
-superform
on
U
is an element in

A^{p,q}(U):=C^{\infty}(U)\otimes\wedge^{p}(N_{\mathbb{R}})^{*}\otimes\wedge^{q}(N_{\mathbb{R}})^{*}.

Formally, a
(p,q)
-superform can be written as

\alpha=\sum\limits_{|I|=p,|J|=q}\alpha_{IJ}d^{\prime}x_{I}\wedge d^{\prime\prime}x_{J},

where
I
(resp.
J
) consists of
0<i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}\leq r
(resp.
0<j_{1}<\cdots<j_{q}\leq r
),
\alpha_{IJ}\in C^{\infty}(U)
and

d^{\prime}x_{I}\wedge d^{\prime\prime}x_{J}:=d^{\prime}x_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge d^{\prime}x_{i_{p}}\otimes d^{\prime\prime}x_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge d^{\prime\prime}x_{j_{p}}.

We denote

A(U):=\bigoplus\limits_{p,q=1}^{n}A^{p,q}(U),

which can be viewed as a
C^{\infty}(U)
-module.
The
wedge product
is defined in the usual way in
A(U)
.

There is a
differential operator

d^{\prime}:A^{p,q}(U)\rightarrow A^{p+1,q}(U)
defined by

d^{\prime}\alpha=\sum\limits_{|I|=p,|J|=q}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\partial\alpha_{IJ}}{\partial x_{i}}d^{\prime}x_{i}\wedge d^{\prime}x_{I}\wedge d^{\prime\prime}x_{J}.

Similarly, we define
d^{\prime\prime}:A^{p,q}(U)\rightarrow A^{p,q+1}(U)
by

\displaystyle d^{\prime\prime}\alpha

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{|I|=p,|J|=q}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}\frac{\partial\alpha_{IJ}}{\partial x_{j}}d^{\prime\prime}x_{j}\wedge d^{\prime}x_{I}\wedge d^{\prime\prime}x_{J}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{|I|=p,|J|=q}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{p}\frac{\partial\alpha_{IJ}}{\partial x_{j}}d^{\prime}x_{I}\wedge d^{\prime\prime}x_{j}\wedge d^{\prime\prime}x_{J}.

Moreover, we set
d=d^{\prime}+d^{\prime\prime}
.

We define the operation
{J}:A^{p,q}(U)\rightarrow A^{q,p}(U)
by

{J}\alpha=(-1)^{pq}\sum\limits_{|I|=p,|J|=q}\alpha_{IJ}\wedge d^{\prime}x_{J}\wedge d^{\prime\prime}x_{I}.

A superform
\alpha\in A^{p,p}(U)
is called
symmetric
if
\alpha=(-1)^{p}J\alpha
.

Obviously,
A^{p,q}(U)
defines a sheaf
{A}^{p,q}
on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
with respect to the general topology. We denote the space of
(p,q)
-superforms on
U
with compact support by
A_{c}^{p,q}(U)
.

Remark 2.2
.

(1)

We have obvious properties:
d^{\prime}d^{\prime}=d^{\prime\prime}d^{\prime\prime}=0
. For
\alpha\in A^{p,q}(U),\beta\in A^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(U)
, we have

d^{\prime}(\alpha\wedge\beta)=d^{\prime}\alpha\wedge\beta+(-1)^{p+q}\alpha\wedge d^{\prime}\beta.

(2)

For an affine map
F:N_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}\rightarrow N_{\mathbb{R}}
and any open subset

, we have a pull-back

\displaystyle F^{*}:A^{p,q}(U)

\displaystyle\rightarrow A^{p,q}(F^{-1}(U)),

\displaystyle d^{\prime}x_{I}\wedge d"x_{J}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum\limits_{|\widetilde{I}|=p,|\widetilde{J}|=q}F_{I\widetilde{I}}F_{J\widetilde{J}}d^{\prime}x_{\widetilde{I}}^{\prime}\wedge d"x_{\widetilde{J}}^{\prime},

which commutes with

, where

is the
(I,\widetilde{I})
-th minor of the linear map

(3)

In convention, for a smooth function

and

, where

is a basis of

corresponding to the linear functions

\frac{\partial f}{\partial v}:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}.

To define general intersection or
\wedge
in

, let us consider the simplest case, i.e. defining a bilinear map
PS^{p,q}\times B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},l^{\prime}}\rightarrow B^{p+p^{\prime},q+q^{\prime},l^{\prime}}

Definition 4.15
.

Let
\alpha\in PS(\widetilde{\Omega})
and
\beta\in B(\widetilde{\Omega})
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a polyhedral decomposition of
N_{\mathbb{R}}
that adapts
\alpha
and
\beta
, say

\alpha=(\alpha_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}}=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}^{0}}\alpha_{\sigma}[\sigma],\ \ \beta=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}}\beta_{\sigma}[\sigma].

We define their product as

\alpha\wedge\beta:=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}}\alpha_{\sigma}\wedge\beta_{\sigma}[\sigma].

Remark 4.16
.

(1)

We see that the definition is independent of the choice of
\mathcal{C}
, and we have
\alpha\wedge\beta\in B(\widetilde{\Omega})
by the balancing condition. Moreover, it defines a bilinear map

PS^{p,q}(\widetilde{\Omega})\times B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},l^{\prime}}(\widetilde{\Omega})\rightarrow B^{p+p^{\prime},q+q^{\prime},l^{\prime}}(\widetilde{\Omega}).

(2)

For any

and
\gamma\in A_{c}^{r-p-p^{\prime},r-q-q^{\prime}}(\widetilde{\Omega})

(\alpha\wedge\beta)(\gamma)=\sum\limits_{\sigma}\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}\cap\sigma}\alpha_{\sigma}\wedge\beta_{\sigma}\wedge\gamma=(-1)^{(p+q)(p^{\prime}+q^{\prime})}\beta(\alpha\wedge\gamma),

so this definition generalizes

2.15

Definition 4.17
.

Let
\alpha=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}^{l}}\alpha_{\sigma}[\sigma]\in B^{p,q,l}(\widetilde{\Omega})

\phi=(\phi_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}}\in PS^{0,0}(\widetilde{\Omega})
with polyhedral complex of definition

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathcal{C}^{l}\\
\tau<\sigma\text{ a facet}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{\sigma}|_{\tau}\otimes\omega_{\sigma,\tau}=\sum\limits_{i\in I}\beta_{i}\otimes v_{i}\in A_{\tau}^{p,q}(\widetilde{\Omega}\cap\tau)\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}N_{\tau,{\mathbb{R}}},

\tau<\sigma\text{ a facet}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\partial\phi_{\sigma}}{\partial\omega_{\sigma,\tau}}\alpha_{\sigma}|_{\tau}-\sum\limits_{i\in I}\dfrac{\partial\phi_{\tau}}{\partial v_{i}}\beta_{i}.

The

of

Example 4.19
.

Let
H\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
be an integral
{\mathbb{R}}
-affine space. Let
\phi=\max\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\}:N_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a piecewise linear function, i.e.
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}:N_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
are integral
{\mathbb{R}}
-affine functions. Set
V_{\phi}:=\{x\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\phi_{1}(x)=\phi_{2}(x)\}
. Then

\mathrm{div}(\phi)\cdot[H]=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $H\subset V_{\phi}$;}\\
\dfrac{\partial(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})}{\partial\omega_{V_{1},V_{\phi}}}[N:N_{H}+N_{\phi}]\cdot[V_{\phi}\cap H]&\text{ if $H\not\subset V_{\phi}$,}\end{cases}

where
V_{1}=\{x\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\phi_{1}(x)\geq\phi_{2}(x)\}
,
N_{\phi}=N\cap V_{\phi}
and
\omega_{V_{1},V_{\phi}}
is a normal vector over
V_{1}/V_{\phi}
. Moreover, let
\psi=\min\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\}
, then
\mathrm{div}(\psi)\cdot[H]=-\mathrm{div}(\phi)\cdot[H]
.

Proof.

Notice that for a given
H
, the corner locus
\mathrm{div}(\phi)\cdot[H]
is only dependent on
\phi|_{H}:H\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, so we can assume that
H=N_{\mathbb{R}}
. If
H\subset V_{\phi}
, i.e.
N_{\mathbb{R}}=V_{\phi}
then
\phi
is an integral
{\mathbb{R}}
-affine function. We take
\mathcal{C}=\{N_{\mathbb{R}}\}
which is not consisted of lower faces, then
\mathrm{div}(\phi)=0
. If
H\not\subset V_{\phi}
, we consider half-spaces
V_{1},V_{2}
divided by
V_{\phi}
such that
\phi|_{V_{1}}=\phi_{1}
and
\phi|_{V_{2}}=\phi_{2}
. We can take
\omega_{V_{2},V_{\phi}}=-\omega_{V_{1},V_{\phi}}
, then
\mathrm{div}(\phi)\cdot[N_{\mathbb{R}}]=m[V_{\phi}]
with

m=\dfrac{\partial(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})}{\partial\omega_{V_{1},V_{\phi}}}.

For the second case, in general
H\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
, we have
\mathrm{div}(\phi)\cdot[H]=\dfrac{\partial(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})}{\partial\omega_{H_{1},H_{\phi}}}[H_{\phi}]
, where
H_{1}=V_{\cap}H_{\phi}
and
H_{\phi}=V_{\phi}\cap H
. We consider the relation between
\omega_{H_{1},H_{\phi}}
and
\omega_{V_{1},V_{\phi}}
. Indeed,

{\mathbb{Z}}\overline{\omega_{H_{1},H_{\phi}}}\simeq N_{H}/(N_{H}\cap N_{\phi})\hookrightarrow N/N_{\phi}\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}\overline{\omega_{V_{1},V_{\phi}}}

and both sides are of rank
1
, so
\overline{\omega_{H_{1},H_{\phi}}}=[N:N_{H}+N_{\phi}]\overline{\omega_{V_{1},V_{\phi}}}
, this implies the first statement.

For the second one, assume that
H\not\subset V_{\phi}
, then

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}(\psi)\cdot[H]

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{div}(-\psi)\cdot[H]

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{\partial(-\phi_{1}-(-\phi_{2}))}{\partial(-\omega_{V_{1},V_{\phi}})}[N:N_{H}+N_{\phi}]\cdot[V_{\phi}\cap H]

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{div}(\phi)\cdot[H].

∎

Theorem 4.23

(
[
14
]
Theorem 4.1)
.

There is a unique way to define an associative bilinear product
\wedge:B(\widetilde{\Omega})\times B(\widetilde{\Omega})\rightarrow B(\widetilde{\Omega})
that satisfies the following properties:

(1)

the Leibniz rules with respect to

and

(2)

and
\beta\in B(\widetilde{\Omega})

\wedge
-product
\alpha\wedge\beta

(3)

for any

\mathrm{Supp}([\Delta]\wedge(\alpha\times\beta))\subset\Delta
and

\alpha\wedge\beta=p_{1,*}([\Delta]\wedge(\alpha\times\beta)),

We give a sketch of the proof.

Uniqueness. If such map exists, then for any

and

\displaystyle(d^{\prime}d^{\prime\prime}\phi)\wedge\beta=\partial^{\prime}d^{\prime\prime}_{P}\phi\wedge\beta+d^{\prime}_{P}d^{\prime\prime}_{P}\phi\wedge\beta=\partial^{\prime}(d_{P}^{\prime\prime}\phi\wedge\beta)+d_{P}^{\prime\prime}\phi\wedge\partial^{\prime}\beta+d_{P}^{\prime}d_{P}^{\prime\prime}\phi\wedge\beta.

(1)

The final one holds since the Leibniz rule holds with respect to

and
d^{\prime\prime}_{P}
for

\partial^{\prime}
.
This implies that

We fix integral coordinate functions

\displaystyle[\Delta]=d^{\prime}d^{\prime\prime}\phi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d^{\prime}d^{\prime\prime}\phi_{r},

Theorem 4.25

(The tropical Poincaré-Lelong formula)

For

From equality (

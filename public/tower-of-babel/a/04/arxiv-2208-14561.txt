Invariant metrics on current Lie algebras
R. García-Delgado

Abstract
. In this work we state conditions for a current Lie algebra 𝔤 ⊗ 𝒮 \mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S} to admit an invariant metric, where 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is a quadratic Lie algebra and 𝒮 \mathcal{S} is an associative and commutative algebra with unit. We also consider the reciprocal: if 𝔤 ⊗ 𝒮 \mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S} admits an invariant metric, we state necessary and sufficient conditions for 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} to admit an invariant metric. In particular, we show that if 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is an indecomposable quadratic Lie algebra, then 𝔤 ⊗ 𝒮 \mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S} admits an invariant metric if and only if 𝒮 \mathcal{S} also admits an invariant, symmetric and non-degenerate bilinear form. In addition, we prove a theorem similar to the double extension for 𝔤 ⊗ 𝒮 \mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S} , where 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is an indecomposable, nilpotent and quadratic Lie algebra.

Introduction

A
quadratic Lie algebra
is a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over a field
\mathbb{F}
, with Lie bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
, equipped with a non-degenerate, symmetric and invariant bilinear form
B:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{F}
. The invariant property means that
B(x,[y,z])=B([x,y],z)
, for all
x,y
and
z
in
\mathfrak{g}
. A bilinear form
B
satisfying these conditions is called an
invariant metric
. An example of a quadratic Lie algebra is a semisimple Lie algebra with Killing form as its invariant metric. In the solvable case, the Killing form degenerates, so this bilinear form cannot be an invariant metric. In fact, there are solvable Lie algebras that do not admit an invariant metric. The question of whether quadratic non-semisimple Lie algebras exist has an affirmative answer. An immediate example of such structures is a reductive Lie algebra. Another more elaborated example is as follows: Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and consider the co-adjoint representation
\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
. The vector space
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
, with Lie bracket defined by
[x+\alpha,y+\beta]_{\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}}=[x,y]+\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(x)(\beta)-\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}(y)(\alpha)
, and the invariant metric defined by
B(x+\alpha,y+\beta)=\alpha(y)+\beta(x)
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
\alpha,\beta
in
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
, make
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
into a quadratic non-semisimple Lie algebra, as
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is an Abelian ideal. Thus, the class of quadratic Lie algebras contains strictly the semisimple Lie algebras. Since the semisimple Lie algebras were classified (see
[
4
]
), the search for results to classify families of quadratic non-semisimple Lie algebras has been an active topic of research (see, for instance
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
or
[
5
]
).

In this work, we state results for quadratic Lie algebras constructed with the tensor product as described below. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and
\mathcal{S}
be a commutative and associative algebra over
\mathbb{F}
. The
current Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathcal{S}
is the Lie algebra in
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
, with bracket:

[x\otimes s,y\otimes t]_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}}=[x,y]\otimes s\,t,\,\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}\text{ and }s,t\in\mathcal{S}.

The process for extending a Lie algebra through tensor product has been applied to generalize results in Lie theory. For instance, in
[
9
]
is calculated the minimal dimension of a representation
\rho\!:\!\mathfrak{h}_{n}\!\otimes\mathcal{S}\!\to\!\mathfrak{gl}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
, where
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
is the
2n+1
-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra and
\mathcal{S}
is a quotient of the polynomial rings
\mathbb{F}[X]
. In
[
7
]
is obtained the Levi decomposition for the Lie algebra derivations
\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S})
. Concerning to quadratic Lie algebras, in
[
10
]
is studied the case when
\mathfrak{g}
is a simple Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric.

The aim of this work is to state results that can be applied for quadratic current Lie algebras of non-semisimple Lie algebras and to determine conditions so that a current Lie algebra admits an invariant metric. If
\mathfrak{g}
is a quadratic Lie algebra, in
Thm.
1.2
, we state necessary and sufficient conditions for
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
to admit an invariant metric. We also prove that if
\mathfrak{g}
is an indecomposable quadratic Lie algebra, then
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric if and only if
\mathcal{S}
also admits a non-degenerate, symmetric and invariant bilinear form (see
Cor.
1.3
). This result generalizes the case when
\mathfrak{g}
is a simple Lie algebra (see
[
10
]
). In addition, we state a result similar to the double extension for
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is an indecomposable, nilpotent and quadratic Lie algebra (see
Thm.
2.1
). In §4, we assume that
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric and we state conditions so that
\mathfrak{g}
admits an invariant metric. For that, we consider a surjective linear map
F:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\to\mathfrak{g}
, satisfying
F([x\otimes s,y\otimes 1]_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}})=[F(x\otimes s),y]
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
s
in
\mathcal{S}
. For each
s
in
\mathcal{S}
and a linear map
H:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
such that
F\circ H=\operatorname{Id}_{g}
, we set a linear map
\psi_{s}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
, and necessary and sufficient conditions to determine if
\psi_{s}
yields an invariant metric on
\mathfrak{g}
(see
Thm.
3.2
). Finally, in

3.3
, we show a particular case when the existence of an invariant metric on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
implies that
\mathfrak{g}
also admits one.

All algebras and vector spaces in this work are finite-dimensional over a field
\mathbb{F}
of zero characteristic.

Let
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
be the current Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathcal{S}
. For each
s
in
\mathcal{S}
, let
\iota_{s}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
be the linear map defined by
\iota_{s}(x)=x\otimes s
, for all
x
in
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\mathcal{S}
has unit
1
, then
\iota_{1}
is an injective Lie algebra homomorphism. Then we may say that the bracket in
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
extends the bracket in
\mathfrak{g}
. Henceforth we shall use the same symbol for both brackets; that is,
[x\otimes s,y\otimes t]=[x,y]\otimes s\,t
for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
.

A
centroid
of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a linear map
T:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
that commutes with the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
, that is
T\circ\operatorname{ad}(x)=\operatorname{ad}(T(x))
, for all
x
in
\mathfrak{g}
. The symbol
\Gamma(\mathfrak{g})
denotes the vector space generated by centroids of
\mathfrak{g}
.

We write
C(\mathfrak{g})=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid[x,y]=0,\text{ for all }y\in\mathfrak{g}\}
, for the
center
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

For a non-degenerate, symmetric and bilinear form

B:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{F}
, let
B^{\flat}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
be the map defined by

B^{\flat}(x)\,(y)=B(x,y)
, for all
x
and
y
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
Since
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}\mathfrak{g}
is assumed to be finite, one may consider the inverse map

B^{\sharp}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathfrak{g}
, of
B^{\flat}
. If
B
is an invariant metric, then the linear map
B^{\flat}
is an
isomorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
; that is,
B^{\flat}\circ\operatorname{ad}(x)=\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}\!(x)\circ B^{\flat}
, for all
x
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

1.
The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits an invariant metric

In this section, we state conditions so that
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric, where
\mathfrak{g}
is a quadratic Lie algebra. We start by setting a linear map
\Psi:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\to\left(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\right)^{\ast}
and conditions for it to be a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
-modules.

1.1 Proposition
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a quadratic Lie algebra over
\mathbb{F}
with invariant metric
B
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be an associative and commutative algebra with unit over
\mathbb{F}
. Then the following statements are equivalents:

(i)

There exists a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
-modules
\Psi:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\to\left(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\right)^{\ast}
.

(ii)

There exists a bilinear map
\alpha:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\Gamma\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)
, such that:

(1)

\alpha(s,s^{\prime})([\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}])=\alpha(ss^{\prime},1)([\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]),\quad\text{ for all }s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
Suppose that
\Psi:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\to(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S})^{\ast}
is a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
-modules. For each
s
in
\mathcal{S}
, let

\mathcal{F}(s)=B^{\sharp}\circ\iota_{s}^{\ast}\circ\Psi^{\ast}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\to\mathfrak{g}.

Then,
B^{\flat}\circ\mathcal{F}(s)=\iota_{s}^{\ast}\circ\Psi^{\ast}
and
\mathcal{F}(s)^{\ast}\circ B^{\flat}=\Psi\circ\iota_{s}
; thus:

(2)

B(x,\mathcal{F}(s)(y\otimes s^{\prime}))\!=\!\Psi(x\otimes s)(y\otimes s^{\prime}),\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\text{ and }s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}.

We claim that:

(3)

\mathcal{F}(s)\left([x\otimes s^{\prime},y\otimes t]\right)=[\mathcal{F}(st)(x\otimes s^{\prime}),y],

for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
s,s^{\prime},t
in
\mathcal{S}
. Indeed, take
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
s,s^{\prime},t
in
\mathcal{S}
. From (
2
) and using that
B
is invariant we get:

(4)

\begin{split}\Psi([z,x]\otimes ss^{\prime})(y\otimes t)&=B(z,[x,\mathcal{F}(ss^{\prime})(y\otimes t)]),\text{ and }\\
\Psi(z\otimes s)([x\otimes s^{\prime},y\otimes t])&=B(z,\mathcal{F}(s)([x\otimes s^{\prime},y\otimes t]))\end{split}

Due to
\Psi
is a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
-modules, then
\Psi([z,x]\otimes ss^{\prime})(y\otimes t)=\Psi(z\otimes s)([x\otimes s^{\prime},y\otimes t])
. Thus, from (
4
), it follows:

(5)

B(z,[x,\mathcal{F}(ss^{\prime})(y\otimes t)])=B(z,\mathcal{F}(s)([x\otimes s^{\prime},y\otimes t])).

Using that
B
is non-degenerate and that the Lie bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
is skew-symmetric, from (
5
) we obtain:

(6)

\mathcal{F}(s)([x\otimes s^{\prime},y\otimes t])=[x,\mathcal{F}(ss^{\prime})(y\otimes t)]=[\mathcal{F}(st)(x\otimes s^{\prime}),y],

which proves (
3
). Using (
3
) or (
6
), it follows:

(7)

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}(s)\circ\iota_{s^{\prime}}\left([x,y]\right)&=\mathcal{F}(s)([x,y]\otimes s^{\prime})=\mathcal{F}(s)([x\otimes s^{\prime},y\otimes 1])\\
\,&=[\mathcal{F}(s)\circ\iota_{s^{\prime}}(x),y]=[x,\mathcal{F}(ss^{\prime})(y\otimes 1)].\end{split}

From (
7
) we deduce that
\mathcal{F}(s)\circ\iota_{s^{\prime}}
belongs to
\Gamma(\mathfrak{g})
. Let
\alpha:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\Gamma(\mathfrak{g})
be defined by:

(8)

\alpha(s,s^{\prime})=\mathcal{F}(s)\circ\iota_{s^{\prime}},\quad\text{ for all }s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}.

Now we shall prove (
1
). Indeed, from (
7
) and (
8
), we get:

\displaystyle\alpha(s,s^{\prime})([x,y])

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}(s)\circ\iota_{s^{\prime}}([x,y])=[x,\mathcal{F}(ss^{\prime})(y\otimes 1)]

\displaystyle=[x,\alpha(ss^{\prime},1)(y)]=\alpha(ss^{\prime},1)([x,y]).

then (
1
) holds. By the commutativity of
\mathcal{S}
, then it follows:

(9)

\alpha(s,s^{\prime})(x)=\alpha(ss^{\prime},1)(x)=\alpha(s^{\prime},s)(x),\,\text{ for all }x\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]

Further, take
x
in
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, using that
\mathcal{S}
is associative and (
9
), we obtain:

(10)

\alpha(s,s^{\prime}t)(x)=\alpha(s(s^{\prime}t),1)(x)=\alpha((ss^{\prime})t,1)(x)=\alpha(ss^{\prime},t)(x).

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
Take
s
in
\mathcal{S}
. The bilinear map
(x,s^{\prime})\mapsto\alpha(s,s^{\prime})(x)
, induces the linear map
\mathcal{F}(s):\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\to\mathfrak{g},\,x\otimes s^{\prime}\mapsto\alpha(s,s^{\prime})(x)
, for all
x
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
s^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{S}
. Using that
\mathcal{S}
is associative and (
10
), we get:

(11)

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}(s)([x\otimes s^{\prime},y\otimes t])&=\mathcal{F}(s)\circ\iota_{s^{\prime}t}([x,y])=\alpha(s,s^{\prime}t)([x,y])\\
\,&=\alpha(st,s^{\prime})([x,y])=[\alpha(st,s^{\prime})(x),y]\\
\,&=[\mathcal{F}(st)\circ\iota_{s^{\prime}}(x),y]=[\mathcal{F}(st)(x\otimes s^{\prime}),y],\end{split}

for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and for all
s,s^{\prime},t
in
\mathcal{S}
. This proves (
3
) and (
6
). We define
\Psi:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\to\left(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\right)^{\ast}
by:

(12)

\Psi(x\otimes s)=B^{\flat}(x)\circ\mathcal{F}(s),\text{ for all }x\in\mathfrak{g}\text{ and }s\in\mathcal{S}.

We shall prove that
\Psi
is a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
-modules. Using that
B
is invariant and (
6
), we obtain:

(13)

\begin{split}&\Psi([x\otimes s,y\otimes s^{\prime}])(z\otimes t)=\Psi([x,y]\otimes ss^{\prime})(z\otimes t)\\
&=B^{\flat}([x,y])\circ\mathcal{F}(ss^{\prime})(z\otimes t)=B([x,y],\mathcal{F}(ss^{\prime})(z\otimes t))\\
\,&=B(x,[y,\mathcal{F}(ss^{\prime})(z\otimes t)])=B(x,\mathcal{F}(s)[y\otimes s^{\prime},z\otimes t])\\
\,&=B^{\flat}(s)\circ\mathcal{F}(s)([y\otimes s^{\prime},z\otimes t])=\Psi(x\otimes s)([y\otimes s^{\prime},z\otimes t]).\end{split}

Then,
\Psi
is a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
-modules.
∎

We have stated conditions for there to be a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
-modules
\Psi:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\to(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S})^{\ast}
. In the next result, we will state conditions so that
\Psi
yields an invariant metric on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
.

1.2 Theorem
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a quadratic Lie algebra with invariant metric
B
, and let
S
be an associative and commutative algebra with unit over
\mathbb{F}
. The current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric if and only if there exists a bilinear map
\alpha:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\Gamma(\mathfrak{g})
satisfying:

(i)

\alpha(s,t)([\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}])=\alpha(st,1)([\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}])
for all
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
.

(ii)

\bigcap_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{Ker}\left(\mathcal{F}(s)\right)=\{0\}
, where
\mathcal{F}(s):\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\to\mathfrak{g}
, is defined by:
\mathcal{F}(s)(x\otimes t)=\alpha(s,t)(x)
, for all
x
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
.

(iii)

\alpha(s,t)^{\ast}=\alpha(t,s)
, where
B(\alpha(s,t)(x),y)=B(x,\alpha(s,t)^{\ast}(y))
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
Suppose that
\alpha
satisfies
(i)
,
(ii)
and
(iii)
, and we will prove the existence of an invariant metric
\bar{B}
on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
. Let
x,y
be in
\mathfrak{g}
and
s,t
be in
\mathcal{S}
. The bilinear map
(x,s)\mapsto B^{\flat}(x)\circ\mathcal{F}(s)
, induces the linear map
\Psi:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\to\left(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\right)^{\ast}
,
x\otimes s\mapsto B^{\flat}(x)\circ\mathcal{F}(s)
(note that this is the same as the one defined in (
12
)). By (
13
) and
Prop.
1.1
.(i)
,
\Psi
is a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
-modules. We define the bilinear form
\bar{B}
on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
by
\bar{B}(x\otimes s,y\otimes t)=B(\alpha(t,s)(x),y)
, then:

(14)

\begin{split}\bar{B}(x\otimes s,y\otimes t)&=B(\alpha(t,s)(x),y)=B\left(\mathcal{F}(t)(x\otimes s),y\right)\\
\,&=\Psi(y\otimes t)(x\otimes s).\end{split}

We claim that
\bar{B}
is an invariant metric on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
. Indeed, let
x^{\prime}
be in
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
such that
\bar{B}^{\flat}(x^{\prime})=0
, then
\bar{B}(x^{\prime},y\otimes s)=0
. From (
14
), this amounts to say that
B(\mathcal{F}(s)(x^{\prime}),y)=0
. Since
B
is non-degenerate, then
\mathcal{F}(s)(x^{\prime})=0
which means that
x^{\prime}
lies in
\operatorname{Ker}(\mathcal{F}(s))
, where
s
is arbitrary. Then, by
(ii)
,
x^{\prime}=0
and
\bar{B}
is non-degenerate. In (
13
) we proved there that
\Psi
is a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
-modules, then
\bar{B}
is invariant. From
\alpha(s,t)^{\ast}=\alpha(t,s)
and due to
B
is symmetric, it follows:

\displaystyle\bar{B}(x\otimes s,y\otimes t)

\displaystyle=B(\mathcal{F}(t)(x\otimes s),y)=B(\alpha(t,s)(x),y)

\displaystyle=B(x,\alpha(t,s)^{\ast}(y))=B(x,\alpha(s,t)(y))

\displaystyle=B(\mathcal{F}(s)(y\otimes t),x)=\bar{B}(y\otimes t,x\otimes s),

then
\bar{B}
is symmetric and it is an invariant metric on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
.

(\Rightarrow)
Let
\bar{B}
be the invariant metric on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
and let
\Psi=\bar{B}^{\flat}
. Then
\Psi
is an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
-modules, which proves
(i)
of
Prop.
1.1
.

For each
s\in\mathcal{S}
, let
\mathcal{F}(s):\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\to\mathfrak{g}
be the linear map defined by:

(15)

\mathcal{F}(s)=B^{\sharp}\circ\iota_{s}^{*}\circ\bar{B}^{\flat},\,\text{ then }\,B^{\flat}\circ\mathcal{F}(s)=\iota_{s}^{\ast}\circ\bar{B}^{\flat}.

By (
3
) or (
6
), it follows
\mathcal{F}(s)([x\otimes s^{\prime},y\otimes t])=[\mathcal{F}(st)(x\otimes s^{\prime}),y]
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
s,s^{\prime},t
in
\mathcal{S}
. Let
\alpha(s,t):\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
be the map defined by
\alpha(s,t)(x)=\mathcal{F}(s)(x\otimes t)
. By (
7
), we have that
\alpha(s,t)
belongs to
\Gamma(\mathfrak{g})
. We shall prove that
\alpha(s,t)^{\ast}=\alpha(t,s)
. Using (
15
) we get:

(16)

\begin{split}B(\alpha(t,s)(x),y)&=B(\mathcal{F}(t)(x\otimes s),y)=B^{\flat}\circ\mathcal{F}(t)(x\otimes s)(y)\\
\,&=\iota^{\ast}\circ\bar{B}^{\flat}(x\otimes s)(y)=\bar{B}(x\otimes s,y\otimes t).\end{split}

Using the same arguments as in (
16
) and that
\bar{B}
is symmetric, we obtain:

(17)

B(\alpha(s,t)(y),x)\!=\!\bar{B}(y\otimes t,x\otimes s)\!=\!\bar{B}(x\otimes s,y\otimes t)\!=\!B(\alpha(t,s)(x),y)

Since
B
is non-degenerate, from
(iii)
and (
17
), we get
\alpha(s,t)^{\ast}=\alpha(t,s)
.

It remains to show that
\underset{s\in\mathcal{S}}{\bigcap}\operatorname{Ker}\left(\mathcal{F}(s)\right)=\{0\}
. Let
u
be in
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
such that
\mathcal{F}(s)(u)=0
for all
s
in
\mathcal{S}
. By (
15
),
\bar{B}(v\otimes s,u)=B\left(v,\mathcal{F}(s)(u)\right)=0
for all
v
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
s
in
\mathcal{S}
. Since
\bar{B}
is non-degenerate, then
u=0
.
∎

The following result proves that the existence of an invariant metric on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
, forces
\mathcal{S}
to admit a non-degenerate, symmetric, and invariant bilinear form, if
\mathfrak{g}
is a non-Abelian and indecomposable quadratic Lie algebra, over an algebraically closed field of zero characteristic.

1.3 Corollary
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a non-Abelian and indecomposable quadratic Lie algebra with invariant metric
B
, over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{F}
of zero characteristic. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a commutative and associative algebra with unit over
\mathbb{F}
. Then
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric if and only if
\mathcal{S}
admits a non-degenerate, symmetric and invariant bilinear form.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
If
\mathcal{S}
admits a non-degenerate, symmetric and invariant bilinear form
\gamma
, then the bilinear form
\bar{B}
on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
defined by:

\bar{B}(x\otimes s,y\otimes s^{\prime})=B(x,y)\gamma(s,s^{\prime})
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
s,s^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{S}
, is an invariant metric on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
.

(\Rightarrow)
By Jordan-Chevalley decomposition (see
[
4
]
,
Prop. 4.2
), the semisimple and nilpotent parts of any
B
-self adjoint centroid
T
of
\mathfrak{g}
, are also
B
-self adjoint centroids of
\mathfrak{g}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}
is an indecomposable quadratic Lie algebra over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{F}
of zero characteristic,
T
can be written as
T=\gamma\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}+N
, where
\gamma
is in
\mathbb{F}
,
\gamma\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is the semisimple part, and
N
is the nilpotent part of
T
. This follows from the fact that each eigenspace corresponding to an eigenvalue of a semisimple
B
-self adjoint centroid of
\mathfrak{g}
, is a non-degenerate ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
s
and
t
be in
\mathcal{S}
and consider the bilinear map
\alpha\!:\!\mathcal{S}\!\!\times\!\mathcal{S}\!\to\!\Gamma(\mathfrak{g})
of
Prop.
1.1
.(iii)
. As
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric, by
Thm.
1.2
.(iii)
,
\alpha(s,t)^{\ast}\!\!=\!\alpha(t,s)
, then
\bar{\alpha}(s,t)\!=\!\alpha(s,t)\!+\!\alpha(t,s)
is a
B
-self adjoint centroid of
\mathfrak{g}
(where
\bar{\alpha}(s,t)\!\!=\!\bar{\alpha}(t,s)
). Thus, there are an scalar
\gamma(s,t)
in
\mathbb{F}
and a nilpotent
B
-self adjoint centroid
\sigma(s,t)
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that:

(18)

\bar{\alpha}(s,t)=\gamma(s,t)\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}+\sigma(s,t).

We affirm that
\gamma
is an invariant, symmetric and non-degenerate bilinear form in
\mathcal{S}
. To prove this, we will use that for any
T
and
S
in
\Gamma(\mathfrak{g})
, the following holds:
(T\circ S)|_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}=(S\circ T)|_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}
.

Let
s,s^{\prime}
and
t
be in
\mathcal{S}
and take a non-zero element
x
in
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
.

Claim 1.
\gamma
is bilinear.
From

\bar{\alpha}(s+s^{\prime},t)=\gamma(s+s^{\prime},t)\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}+\sigma(s+s^{\prime},t)
and due to
\alpha
is bilinear, we get:

(19)

\begin{split}&\left(\gamma(s+s^{\prime},t)-\gamma(s,t)-\gamma(s^{\prime},t)\right)x=\\
&\left(\sigma(s,t)+\sigma(s^{\prime},t)-\sigma(s+s^{\prime},t)\right)(x)\end{split}

Note that the linear map
\left(\sigma(s,t)+\sigma(s^{\prime},t)-\sigma(s+s^{\prime},t)\right)|_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}
, is nilpotent, as
\sigma(s,t)|_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}
,
\sigma(s^{\prime},s)|_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}
and
\sigma(s+s^{\prime},t)|_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}
are nilpotent and commute. Since
x
is non zero, then, from (
19
) it follows:
\gamma(s+s^{\prime},t)-\gamma(s,t)-\gamma(s^{\prime},t)=0
, proving that
\gamma
is bilinear.

Claim 2.
\gamma
is invariant.
By
Thm.
1.2
.(i)
,
\alpha(s,s^{\prime}t)(x)=\alpha(ss^{\prime},t)(x)
and by (
18
) we get:

\displaystyle 2\,\alpha(s,s^{\prime}t)(x)

\displaystyle=\gamma(s,s^{\prime}t)x+\sigma(s,s^{\prime}t)(x),

\displaystyle 2\,\alpha(ss^{\prime},t)(x)

\displaystyle=\gamma(ss^{\prime},t)x+\sigma(ss^{\prime},t)(x).

Then,
\left(\sigma(s,s^{\prime}t)-\sigma(ss^{\prime},t)\right)(x)=\left(\gamma(ss^{\prime},t)-\gamma(s,s^{\prime}t)\right)x
. Observe that
(\sigma(s,s^{\prime}t)-\sigma(ss^{\prime},t))|_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}
is a nilpotent linear map, then we proceed as in
Claim 1
to get that
\gamma(s,s^{\prime}t)=\gamma(ss^{\prime},t)
, thus
\gamma
is invariant. Now we shall prove that
\gamma
is symmetric. Since
\gamma
is invariant and
\mathcal{S}
is commutative, we have
\gamma(s,t)=\gamma(st,1)=\gamma(ts,1)=\gamma(t,s)
, for all
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
; then
\gamma
is symmetric.

Claim 3.
\gamma
is non-degenerate.
We will use the following result:
Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space and let
\mathcal{N}
be a subspace of
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
consisting of commutative and nilpotent maps. Then, there exists
w\neq 0
in
V
such that
T(w)=0
for all
T
in
\mathcal{N}
.
The proof of this result can be obtained by induction on
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}V
.

Suppose there exists
t^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{S}
such that
\gamma(s,t^{\prime})=0
for all
s
in
\mathcal{S}
. Let
w\neq 0
be in
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
such that
\sigma(s,t^{\prime})(w)=0
for all
s
in
\mathcal{S}
. By (
18
) and
Prop.
1.1
.(ii)
,
\alpha(s,t^{\prime})(w)=0
. By
Thm.
1.2
and
Thm.
1.2
.(ii)
, this amounts to say that
\mathcal{F}(s)(w\otimes t^{\prime})=\alpha(s,t^{\prime})(w)=0
. Thus,
w\otimes t^{\prime}
lies in
\operatorname{Ker}(\mathcal{F}(s))
for all
s\in\mathcal{S}
. Given that
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric, by
Thm.
1.2
.(ii)
,
w\otimes t^{\prime}=0
, thus
t^{\prime}=0
, and
\gamma
is non-degenerate.
∎

1.1.
Heisenberg Lie algebra extended by a derivation

Let
\mathfrak{h}_{n}=V_{n}\oplus\mathbb{F}\hslash
be the
2n+1
-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra, with Lie bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
and
V_{n}
a
2n
-dimensional vector space. It is known that there exists a non-degenerate and skew-symmetric bilinear form
\omega:V_{n}\times V_{n}\to\mathbb{F}
, such that
[x,y]=\omega(x,y)\hslash
, for all
x
and
y
in
V_{n}
and
[\mathfrak{h}_{n},\mathbb{F}\hslash]=\{0\}
. The Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
does not admit an invariant metric. There is an extension of
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
by a derivation
D\in\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
, which admits an invariant metric. The underlying vector space of this extension is
\mathbb{F}D\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{n}
, and the Lie algebra structure is given by the semidirect product
\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D)=\mathbb{F}D\ltimes\mathfrak{h}_{n}
. Thereby, the Lie bracket
[\,\cdot,\,\cdot\,]_{D}
in
\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D)
can be expressed as:

[aD+x+b\hslash,a^{\prime}D+x^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\hslash]_{D}=aD\left(x^{\prime}\right)-a^{\prime}D(x)+\omega\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)\hslash,

for all
a,a^{\prime},b,b^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{F}
and
x,x^{\prime}
in
V_{n}
. In the following statements appear necessary and sufficient conditions for
\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D)
to admit an invariant metric (see
[
8
]
):

(i)

D(V_{n})\subset V_{n}
and
D(\hslash)=0
.

(ii)

Let
\phi=D|_{V_{n}}:V_{n}\to V_{n}
. Then
\phi
is invertible.

(iii)

\omega(\phi(x),y)=-\omega(x,\phi(y))
, for all
x
and
y
in
V_{n}
.

(iv)

C(\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D))=\mathbb{F}\hslash
.

Any quadratic Lie algebra structure in
\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D)
is isometric to a quadratic Lie algebra structure with invariant metric
B
on
\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D)
defined by:

(20)

\begin{split}x,y\in V_{n}\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,&B(x,y)=\omega\left(\phi^{-1}(x),y\right),\\
x\in V_{n}\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,&B(x,D)=B(x,\hslash)=0,\,\text{ and }\\
\,&B(D,D)=B(\hslash,\hslash)=0,\,\text{ and }\,B(D,\hslash)=1,\\
\end{split}

(see
[
8
]
). One can verify that
\Gamma\left(\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D)\right)=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D)},N\}
, where
N
is the linear map in
\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D)
such that
N(D)=\hslash
and
N^{2}=0
.

Let
\alpha:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\Gamma(\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D))
be a bilinear map and let
\gamma,\xi:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{F}
be the bilinear forms on
\mathcal{S}
such that:

(21)

\alpha(s,t)=\gamma(s,t)\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D)}+\xi(s,t)N,\,\text{ for all }s,t\in\mathcal{S}.

We claim that
\alpha
yields an invariant metric
\bar{B}
in
\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D)\otimes\mathcal{S}
, in the sense of
Thm.
1.2
, if and only if
\gamma
is non-degenerate, symmetric, and invariant form and
\xi
is symmetric. The invariant metric
\bar{B}
thus obtained is determined by the following assignments. Let
x,y
be in
V_{n}
and let
s,t
be in
\mathcal{S}
, then:

(22)

\begin{split}&\bar{B}(x\otimes s,y\otimes t)=B(x,y)\gamma(s,t),\\
&\bar{B}(D\otimes s,x\otimes t)=\bar{B}(\hslash\otimes s,\hslash\otimes t)=0,\\
&\bar{B}(D\otimes s,\hslash\otimes t)=\gamma(s,t),\,\text{ and }\,\bar{B}(D\otimes s,D\otimes t)=\xi(s,t).\end{split}

Indeed, suppose that
\alpha
yields an invariant metric
\bar{B}
on
\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D)\otimes\mathcal{S}
, in the sense of
Thm.
1.2
. Take a non-zero element
x
in
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
and
s,s^{\prime},t
in
\mathcal{S}
; since
\mathfrak{h}_{n}=[\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D),\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D)]_{D}
, by
Thm.
1.2
.(i)
,
\alpha(ss^{\prime},t)(x)=\alpha(s,s^{\prime}t)(x)
, which, by (
21
), it is equivalent to
\gamma(ss^{\prime},t)x=\gamma(s,s^{\prime}t)x
, from where
\gamma(ss^{\prime},t)=\gamma(s,s^{\prime}t)
and
\gamma
is invariant. Using the same argument as in
Claim 2
of
Cor.
1.3
, we get that
\gamma
is symmetric.

Now we shall prove that
\xi
is symmetric. Using (
8
), (
14
), (
20
) and (
21
), we have:

(23)

\begin{split}&\bar{B}(D\otimes s,D\otimes t)=B(\mathcal{F}(t)(D\otimes s),D)=B(\alpha(t,s)(D),D)\\
\,&=B(\gamma(t,s)D+\xi(t,s)N(D),D)=\xi(t,s)B(\hslash,D)=\xi(t,s).\\
\end{split}

Using that
\bar{B}
is symmetric in (
23
), we obtain that
\xi
is symmetric.
Take
x,y
in
V_{n}
and
s,s^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{S}
. Using the same argument as in (
23
) and the fact that
N(x)=0
, we obtain:
\bar{B}(x\otimes s,y\otimes t)=B(x,y)\gamma(s,t)
. By (
20
), we know that
B|_{V\times V}
is non-degenerate, then
\gamma
is non-degenerate. Using the same argument, we obtain the remaining cases of (
22
).

Now suppose that
\gamma
is a non-degenerate, symmetric, and invariant bilinear form and that
\xi
is symmetric and bilinear form in
\mathcal{S}
. Let
\alpha:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\Gamma(\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D))
be the bilinear map defined by:

(24)

\alpha(s,t)=\gamma(s,t)\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D)}+\xi(s,t)N,\,\text{ for all }s,t\in\mathcal{S}.

We shall verify that
\alpha
satisfies the conditions given in
Thm.
1.2
. Let
x
be in
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
and
s
and
t
be in
\mathcal{S}
. Note that
[\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D),\mathfrak{h}_{n}(D)]_{D}=\mathfrak{h}_{n}
and
\operatorname{Ker}(N)=\mathfrak{h}_{n}
, then
\alpha(s,t)(x)=\gamma(s,t)x
, and as
\gamma
is invariant, then
\alpha
satisfies
(i)
of
Thm.
1.2
.

To verify the conditions in
Thm.
1.2
.(ii)-(iii)
, first note that
\gamma
is non-degenerate, then given a basis
\{s_{1},\cdots,s_{\ell}\}
of
\mathcal{S}
(
\ell=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{S})
), there exists a basis
\{s^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,s^{\prime}_{\ell}\}
of
\mathcal{S}
such that
\gamma(s_{i},s^{\prime}_{j})=\delta_{ij}
, for all
1\leq i,j\leq\ell
. Let
x^{\prime}=x_{1}\otimes s_{1}+\ldots+x_{\ell}\otimes s_{\ell}
be such that
\mathcal{F}(s)(x^{\prime})=0
for all
s
in
\mathcal{S}
; recall that
\mathcal{F}(s)(z\otimes t)=\alpha(s,t)(z)
for all
z
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
(see (
12
)). From (
24
) it follows:

(25)

0=\mathcal{F}(s)(x^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\gamma(s,s_{i})x_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\xi(s,s_{i})N(x_{i}),\,\text{ for all }s\in\mathcal{S}.

Letting
s=s^{\prime}_{i}
in (
25
) and due to
\operatorname{Im}(N)=\mathbb{F}\hslash
, we get that for each
1\leq i\leq\ell
, there exists
a_{i}
in
\mathbb{F}
such that

, whence

. Since

, from (

), it follows

, for all
1\leq j\leq\ell
. Thus
a_{j}=0
for all
j
and

. Therefore,
\displaystyle{\bigcap_{s\in\mathcal{S}}}\operatorname{Ker}\left(\mathcal{F}(s)\right)=\{0\}
. Using that
\alpha
is symmetric, we have
\alpha(s,t)^{\ast}=\alpha(t,s)
, for all

and

in
\mathcal{S}
. Thus, by

1.2
,

which is determined by (

).

2.
A double extension theorem for quadratic current nilpotent Lie algebras

In this section we will show a structure result for a current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
equipped with an invariant metric, where
\mathfrak{g}
is an indecomposable, nilpotent and quadratic Lie algebra. To state this result, we will use Medina-Revoy theorem and the double extension for quadratic Lie algebras (see
[
6
]
). In addition, we will show how to extend the bilinear map
\alpha:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\Gamma(\mathfrak{g})
given in
Thm.
1.2
, to another higher-dimensional quadratic Lie algebra, keeping the same conditions to obtain a higher-dimensional quadratic current Lie algebra.

Let
\mathfrak{g}\neq 0
be an indecomposable, nilpotent and quadratic Lie algebra with invariant metric
B
, over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{F}
of zero characteristic. Let
\mathcal{A}
be the subspace generated by those elements
x
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
N(x)=0
, for all
B
-self adjoint nilpotent centroid
N
of
\mathfrak{g}
. In
Claim 3
, given in the proof of
Cor.
1.3
, we proved there that
\mathcal{A}
is non-zero and it is straightforward to see that it is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}
is nilpotent then
\mathcal{A}\cap C(\mathfrak{g})\neq\{0\}
. Let
c
be a non-zero element of
\mathcal{A}\cap C(\mathfrak{g})
. Due to
c
belongs to
C(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathfrak{g}
is indecomposable, then
c
belongs to
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, and
\mathbb{F}c
is contained in
(\mathbb{F}c)^{\perp}
. Using the Witt decomposition associated to
\mathbb{F}c
,
\mathfrak{g}
has the vector space decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{F}d\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbb{F}c
, where
(\mathbb{F}c)^{\perp}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbb{F}c
,
\mathfrak{h}
is a non-degenerate subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}=\mathbb{F}d\oplus\mathbb{F}c
,
B(d,d)=B(c,c)=0
and
B(c,d)=1
. In addition, let
B_{\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathbb{F}
be the restriction
B|_{\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}}
and let
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
be the skew-symmetric and bilinear map arising from the projection of the Lie bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
onto
\mathfrak{h}
, restricted to elements in
\mathfrak{h}
. By Medina-Revoy theorem (see
[
6
]
),
\mathfrak{h}
is a quadratic Lie algebra, with Lie bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}}
and invariant metric
B_{\mathfrak{h}}
. In addition, there exists a
B_{\mathfrak{h}}
-skew-symmetric derivation
D
of
\mathfrak{h}
such that:

(26)

[x,y]\!=\![x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}}\!+\!B_{\mathfrak{h}}(D(x),y)c,\,\text{ and }\,[d,x]\!=\!D(x),\,\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{h}.

We say that
\mathfrak{g}
is the
double extension of
\mathfrak{h}
by
D
and we write
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}(D)
.

Suppose that the current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric
\bar{B}
and consider the bilinear map
\alpha:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\Gamma(\mathfrak{g})
of
Thm.
1.2
, such that:

(27)

\bar{B}(x\otimes s,y\otimes t)=B(\alpha(t,s)(x),y)=B(x,\alpha(s,t)(y)),

for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
(see (
14
)). Then,
\alpha(s,t)+\alpha(t,s)
is a
B
-self adjoint centroid of
\mathfrak{g}
. As in the proof of
Cor.
1.3
, there are bilinear maps
\gamma:\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{F}
and
\sigma:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\Gamma(\mathfrak{g})
, such that:

\alpha(s,t)+\alpha(t,s)=\gamma(s,t)\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}+\sigma(s,t),\quad\text{ for all }s,t\in\mathcal{S},

where
\sigma(s,t)
is a
B
-self adjoint nilpotent centroid of
\mathfrak{g}
. The choice of
c
implies
\sigma(s,t)(c)=0
. Then, by
Thm.
1.2
.(i)
,
2\alpha(s,t)(c)=\gamma(s,t)c
, which proves that
\mathbb{F}c
is an
ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
invariant under
\alpha
, that is
\alpha(s,t)(\mathbb{F}c)\subset\mathbb{F}c
for all
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
. Similarly, by (
27
), we have:

(28)

B\left(\alpha(s,t)((\mathbb{F}c)^{\perp}),\mathbb{F}c\right)=B\left((\mathbb{F}c)^{\perp},\alpha(t,s)(\mathbb{F}c)\right)=\{0\},

for all
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
. Then,
(\mathbb{F}c)^{\perp}
is also an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
invariant under
\alpha
. Observe that both
\mathbb{F}c\otimes\mathcal{S}
and
(\mathbb{F}c)^{\perp}\otimes\mathcal{S}
are ideals of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
satisfying
\mathbb{F}c\otimes\mathcal{S}\subset(\mathbb{F}c)^{\perp}\otimes\mathcal{S}
. We claim that
(\mathbb{F}c)^{\perp}\otimes\mathcal{S}=(\mathbb{F}c\otimes\mathcal{S})^{\perp}
. Indeed, from (
27
) and (
28
) we deduce that
(\mathbb{F}c)^{\perp}\otimes\mathcal{S}\subset(\mathbb{F}c\otimes\mathcal{S})^{\perp}
. Now we will prove the other inclusion. Let
\bar{x}
be an element of
(\mathbb{F}c\otimes\mathcal{S})^{\perp}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{F}d\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbb{F}c
, we can write
\bar{x}
as:
\bar{x}=d\otimes s+\bar{y}+c\otimes t
, where
\bar{y}
is in
\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{S}
and
s,t
are elements of
\mathcal{S}
. Let
s^{\prime}
be an arbitrary element of
\mathcal{S}
. As
\bar{y}+c\otimes t
belongs to
(\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{S})\oplus(\mathbb{F}c\otimes\mathcal{S})
, and

(\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{S})\oplus(\mathbb{F}c\otimes\mathcal{S})\subset(\mathbb{F}c)^{\perp}\otimes\mathcal{S}\subset(\mathbb{F}c\otimes\mathcal{S})^{\perp},

then
\bar{B}(\bar{x},c\otimes s^{\prime})=\bar{B}(D\otimes s,c\otimes s^{\prime})=B(D,\alpha(s,s^{\prime})(c))=0
. Since
\mathbb{F}c
is an invariant ideal under
\alpha
, it follows:
B^{\flat}(\alpha(s,s^{\prime})(c))=0
, then
\alpha(s,s^{\prime})(c)=0
. Due to
c
lies in
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, by
Thm.
1.2
.(i)-(ii)
, we have
\mathcal{F}(s^{\prime})(c\otimes s)=\alpha(s^{\prime},s)(c)=\alpha(s,s^{\prime})(c)=0
, where
s^{\prime}
is arbitrary, then
c\otimes s
belongs to
\operatorname{Ker}(\mathcal{F}(s^{\prime}))
for all
s^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{S}
. Then, by
Thm.
1.2
.(ii)
,
c\otimes s=0
, thereby,
s=0
, and
\bar{x}=\bar{y}+c\otimes t
belongs to
(\mathbb{F}c)^{\perp}\otimes\mathcal{S}
, proving that
(\mathbb{F}c)^{\perp}\otimes\mathcal{S}=(\mathbb{F}c\otimes\mathcal{S})^{\perp}
. This implies that
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
has the following vector space decomposition:

(29)

\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}=(\mathbb{F}d\otimes\mathcal{S})\oplus(\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{S})\oplus(\mathbb{F}c\otimes\mathcal{S}),

where
(\mathbb{F}c\otimes\mathcal{S})^{\perp}=(\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{S})\oplus(\mathbb{F}c\otimes\mathcal{S})
,
\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{S}
is a non-degenerate subspace of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
and
(\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{S})^{\perp}=(\mathbb{F}d\otimes\mathcal{S})\oplus(\mathbb{F}c\otimes\mathcal{S})
. According to (
26
) and (
29
), the Lie bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
has the following decomposition:

\displaystyle=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}}\otimes s\,t+B_{\mathfrak{h}}(D(x),y)\,c\otimes s\,t,

\displaystyle[D\otimes s,x\otimes t]

\displaystyle=D(x)\otimes s\,t,\,\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{h},\,\text{ and }s,t\in\mathcal{S}.

The choice of
c
and
Thm.
1.2
.(i)
imply,
2\alpha(s,t)(c)=\gamma(s,t)c
. In the proof of
Cor.
1.3
, we showed there that
\gamma
is an invariant, symmetric and non-degenerate bilinear form on
\mathcal{S}
. Using (
27
) and

, we obtain,
\bar{B}(d\otimes s,c\otimes t)=B(d,\alpha(s,t)(c))=\frac{1}{2}\gamma(s,t)
, for all

in
\mathcal{S}
. Let

, then

in
\mathcal{S}
.

Let

\mathfrak{h}

, such that:

\alpha(s,t)(x)=\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(s,t)(x)+\beta(s,t,x)\,c\in\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbb{F}c.

Let
x,y
be in
\mathfrak{h}
; since

(see (

)), then

(31)

B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(s,t)(x),y)=B_{\mathfrak{h}}(x,\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(t,s)(y)),\,\text{ for all }s,t\in\mathcal{S}.

There are bilinear maps
\eta,\zeta:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{F}
and

, such that
\alpha(s,t)(d)=\eta(s,t)d+f(s,t)+\zeta(s,t)c
, for all
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
. From
B(\alpha(s,t)(d),z)=B(d,\alpha(t,s)(z))
, for any

in
\mathfrak{g}
, we get:
\eta=\gamma^{\prime}
,
\beta(t,s,x)=B_{\mathfrak{h}}(f(s,t),x)
and
\zeta
is symmetric; in such a way that:

(32)

\alpha(s,t)(d)=\gamma^{\prime}(s,t)d+f(s,t)+\zeta(s,t)c,\,\,\text{ for all }s,t\in\mathcal{S}.

We claim that:

\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(s,t)\text{ belongs to }\Gamma(\mathfrak{h}),\text{ and }D\circ\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(s,t)=\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(s,t)\circ D=\gamma^{\prime}(s,t)D,

for all
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
. Indeed, let
x,y
be in
\mathfrak{h}
; from

\alpha(s,t)([d,x])=[\alpha(s,t)(d),x]=[d,\alpha(s,t)(x)],

we get:

(33)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(s,t)\circ D

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\prime}(s,t)D+\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(f(s,t))=D\circ\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(s,t),\text{ and }

(34)

\displaystyle D(f(s,t))=0,

\displaystyle\text{ then }f(s,t)\in\operatorname{Ker}(D),\,\,\text{ for all }s,t\in\mathcal{S}.

Analogously, from
\alpha(s,t)([x,y])=[\alpha(s,t)(x),y]=[x,\alpha(s,t)(y)]
and using (
26
) along with (
30

\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(s,t)([x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}})=[\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(s,t)(x),y]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\Gamma(\mathfrak{h})
. Observe from (

\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(s,t)^{\ast}=\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(t,s)
, for all

in
\mathcal{S}
. In addition, since

satisfies the conditions of

1.2
, for the quadratic Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},B_{\mathfrak{h}})
. Thus, the current Lie algebra

admits an invariant metric given by the restriction from

to

.

Let

be in
\mathfrak{h}
. From

, we obtain:
\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(t,ss^{\prime})\circ D=\gamma^{\prime}(s,s^{\prime}t)D
, for all

and

, for any

in
\mathcal{S}
, which by (

.

We have given conditions to extend

, from
\mathfrak{h}
to
\mathfrak{h}(D)
, so that

) and (

)), and we showed that any current Lie algebra

is an indecomposable, nilpotent and quadratic Lie algebra. We gather these findings in the next result.

2.1 Theorem
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a quadratic Lie algebra with invariant metric
B_{\mathfrak{h}}
. Let
D
be a
B_{\mathfrak{h}}
-skew symmetric derivation of
\mathfrak{h}
and let
c
and
d
be symbols. In the vector space
\mathfrak{h}(D)=\mathbb{F}d\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbb{F}c
, consider the quadratic Lie algebra structure given by the double extension of
\mathfrak{h}
by
D
(see (
26
)). Let
\mathcal{S}
be an associative and commutative algebra with unit, admitting a non-degenerate, symmetric and invariant bilinear form
\gamma^{\prime}
. Let
f:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to C(\mathfrak{h})\cap\operatorname{Ker}(D)
be a bilinear map and let
\zeta:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{F}
be a symmetric and bilinear form on
\mathcal{S}
. Assume that the current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric and let
\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\Gamma(\mathfrak{h})
be the bilinear map of
Thm.
1.2
. If

\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(s,t)\circ D=D\circ\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(s,t)=\gamma^{\prime}(s,t)D,\text{ for all }s,t\in\mathcal{S},

then
\mathfrak{h}(D)\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric
\bar{B}
, given by:

(35)

\begin{split}\bar{B}(d\otimes s,c\otimes t)&=\gamma^{\prime}(s,t),\\
\bar{B}(x\otimes s,y\otimes t)&=B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(t,s)(x),y),\\
\bar{B}(d\otimes s,d\otimes t)&=\zeta(s,t),\,\text{ and }\,\bar{B}(c\otimes s,c\otimes t)=0,\\
\end{split}

for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
. In addition, any current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admitting an invariant metric
\bar{B}
can be constructed in this way, where
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}(D)
is an indecomposable, quadratic and nilpotent Lie algebra over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{F}
of zero characteristic, where
\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric.

Proof.

The proof that
\mathfrak{h}(D)\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric if
\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{S}
also admits one, it can be obtained by defining
\alpha:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\Gamma(\mathfrak{h}(D))
as follows:

\displaystyle\alpha(s,t)(x)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\mathfrak{h}}(s,t)(x)+B_{\mathfrak{h}}(f(s,t),x)c,\,\text{ for all }x\in\mathfrak{h},

\displaystyle\alpha(s,t)(d)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\prime}(s,t)\,d+f(s,t)+\zeta(s,t)c,

\displaystyle\alpha(s,t)(c)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\prime}(s,t)c,\,\text{ for all }s,t\in\mathcal{S}.

It is straightforward to verify that
\alpha
satisfies the conditions given in
(i)
,
(ii)
and
(iii)
of
Thm.
1.2
, in such a way that the invariant metric induced by
\alpha
on
\mathfrak{h}(D)\otimes\mathcal{S}
, is
\bar{B}
defined in (
35
). The proof that any current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
endowed with an invariant metric, it can be constructed as in the statement, where
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}(D)
is an indecomposable, quadratic and nilpotent Lie algebra, is given in the analysis we made at the beginning of this section.
∎

3.
The current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric

Now we assume that
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
is a quadratic Lie algebra, and we state conditions so that
\mathfrak{g}
admits an invariant metric. We start by setting a linear map
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
.

3.1 Proposition
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and
\mathcal{S}
be an associative and commutative algebra with unit over
\mathbb{F}
. Assume that the current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric
\bar{B}
. Let
F:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a surjective linear map. For each non-zero
s
in
\mathcal{S}
and a linear map
H:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
such that
F\circ H=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
, let
\psi_{s}
be the linear map
\psi_{s}=H^{\ast}\circ\bar{B}^{\flat}\circ\iota_{s}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
. Then, the ordered pair
(\psi_{s},F)
makes commutative the diagram:

(36)

\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{s}}
\scriptstyle{\iota_{s}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}^{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F^{\ast}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\bar{B}}^{\flat}}
\textstyle{\left(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\right)^{*}}

if and only if
\,\operatorname{Im}\left(\iota_{s}\right)^{\perp}=\operatorname{Ker}(F)
. If the ordered pair
(\psi_{s},F)
makes commutative the diagram (
36
), then
\psi_{s}
is bijective.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
If the ordered pair
(\psi_{s},F)
makes commutative the diagram (
36
), then
{\bar{B}}^{\flat}\circ\iota_{s}=F^{\ast}\circ\psi_{s}
which amounts to say:

(37)

\bar{B}(x\otimes s,y\otimes t)=\psi_{s}(x)\left(F(y\otimes t)\right),\quad\mbox{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\,\mbox{ and }t\in\mathcal{S}

Since
\psi_{s}
is equal to
H^{\ast}\circ\bar{B}\circ\iota_{s}
, then:

(38)

\psi_{s}(x)\left(F(y\otimes t)\right)=\bar{B}\left(x\otimes s,H(F(y\otimes t))\right).

Comparing (
37
) and (
38
), we deduce that
y\otimes t-H(F(y\otimes t))
belongs to
\operatorname{Im}(\iota_{s})^{\perp}
, for all
y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
t
in
\mathcal{S}
. Then
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}=\operatorname{Im}\left(\iota_{s}\right)^{\perp}+\operatorname{Im}(H)
and
\operatorname{Ker}(F)\subset\operatorname{Im}(\iota_{s})^{\perp}
. Let
u
be in
\operatorname{Ker}(\psi_{s})
, by (
37
),
\bar{B}^{\flat}(u\otimes s)=0
; the fact that
\bar{B}^{\flat}
is invertible implies
u\otimes s=0
. As
s
is non-zero,
u=0
, then
\psi_{s}
is bijective.

Now we shall prove that
\operatorname{Im}(\iota_{s})^{\perp}\subset\operatorname{Ker}(F)
. Let
y^{\prime}
be in
\operatorname{Im}(\iota_{s})^{\perp}
. As
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}=\operatorname{Ker}(F)\oplus\operatorname{Im}(H)
, there exist
w^{\prime}
in
\operatorname{Ker}(F)
and
z
in
\mathfrak{g}
such that
y^{\prime}=w^{\prime}+H(z)
, then
F(y^{\prime})=z
. Consider the dual map
\psi_{s}^{\ast}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
, of
\psi_{s}
which satisfies
\psi_{s}^{\ast}(x)(y)=\psi(y)(x)
, for all
x
and
y
in
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
u
be in
\mathfrak{g}
; by (
37
),
0=\bar{B}(\iota_{s}(u),y^{\prime})=\psi_{s}(u)\left(F(y^{\prime})\right)=\psi_{s}(u)(z)=\psi_{s}^{\ast}(z)(u)
. Since
u
is arbitrary, then
\psi_{s}^{\ast}(z)=0
. Due to
\psi_{s}
is bijective,
\psi_{s}^{\ast}
is also bijective, thus
z=0
. Hence
y^{\prime}=w^{\prime}
lies in
\operatorname{Ker}(F)
, and
\operatorname{Im}(\iota_{s})^{\perp}=\operatorname{Ker}(F)
.

(\Leftarrow)
Suppose that
\operatorname{Im}\left(\iota_{s}\right)^{\perp}=\operatorname{Ker}(F)
, for some non-zero
s
in
\mathcal{S}
. We shall prove that the ordered pair
(\psi_{s},F_{s})
makes commutative the diagram (
36
), where
\psi_{s}=H^{\ast}\circ\bar{B}^{\flat}\circ\iota_{s}
and
F\circ H=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Let
x,y
be in
\mathfrak{g}
and
t
be in
\mathcal{S}
. Then
y\otimes t\!-\!H\left(F(y\otimes t)\right)
lies in
\operatorname{Ker}(F)\!=\!\operatorname{Im}\left(\iota_{s}\right)^{\perp}
, and

\displaystyle\bar{B}(\iota_{s}(x),y\otimes t)

\displaystyle=\bar{B}\left(x\otimes s,H\left(F(y\otimes t)\right)\right)=\bar{B}^{\flat}(\iota_{s}(x))(H(F(y\otimes t)))

\displaystyle=H^{\ast}\circ\bar{B}^{\flat}\circ\iota_{s}(x)(F(y\otimes t))=\psi_{s}(x)(F(y\otimes t))

\displaystyle=F^{\ast}\circ\psi_{s}(x)(y\otimes t),\text{ then }\bar{B}^{\flat}\circ\iota_{s}=F^{\ast}\circ\psi_{s}.

Therefore,
(\psi_{s},F)
makes commutative the diagram (
36
).
∎

Now we are able to state conditions for determining if
\mathfrak{g}
admits an invariant metric.

3.2 Theorem
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and
\mathcal{S}
be a commutative and associative algebra with unit over
\mathbb{F}
. Assume that
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric
\bar{B}
. Then
\mathfrak{g}
admits an invariant metric if and only if there are a non-zero element
s
in
\mathcal{S}
, and an ordered pair
(\psi_{s},F)
in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{\ast})\times\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S},\mathfrak{g})
, such that:

(i)

(\psi_{s},F)
makes commutative the diagram (
36
).

(ii)
The map
\psi_{s}:\!\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
, satisfies
\psi_{s}(x)(y)\!=\!\psi_{s}(y)(x)
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

(iii)
The map
F\!:\!\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\!\to\mathfrak{g}
is surjective and it satisfies:
F([x\otimes t,y\otimes 1])\!=\![F(x\otimes t),y]
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
t
in
\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

(\mathbf{\Leftarrow})
Suppose there are an element
s\neq 0
in
\mathcal{S}
, and an ordered pair
(\psi_{s},F)
satisfying
(i),(ii)
and
(iii)
. Let
x,y,z
be in
\mathfrak{g}
. We claim that
B:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{F}
, defined by
B(x,y)=\psi_{s}(x)(y)
, is an invariant metric on
\mathfrak{g}
. From
(ii)
,
B
is symmetric. We claim that
B
is invariant. Let
t
be in
\mathcal{S}
. Using
(i)
-
(iii)
, and that
\bar{B}
is invariant, we get:

\begin{split}&\psi_{s}([x,y])\left(F(z\otimes t)\right)\!=\!(F^{\ast}\circ\psi_{s})([x,y])(z\otimes t)\!=\!(\bar{B}^{\flat}\circ\iota_{s})([x,y])(z\otimes t)\\
&=\!\bar{B}([x,y]\otimes s,z\otimes t)\!=\!\bar{B}(x\otimes s,[y,z]\otimes t)\!=\!\bar{B}^{\flat}(\iota_{s}(x))([y\otimes 1,z\otimes t])\\
&=\!F^{\ast}\circ\psi_{s}(x)([y\otimes 1,z\otimes t])\!=\!\psi_{s}(x)([y,F(z\otimes t)]).\\
\end{split}

Then,
\psi_{s}([x,y])\left(F(z\otimes t)\right)=\psi_{s}(x)([y,F(z\otimes t)])
. Whence, as
F_{s}
is surjective,
\psi_{s}([x,y])=\psi_{s}(x)\circ\operatorname{ad}(y)
, which implies that
\psi_{s}
is a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules, and
B
is an invariant form on
\mathfrak{g}
. As the ordered pair
(\psi_{s},F)
makes commutative the diagram (
36
),
Prop.
3.1
implies that
\psi_{s}
is bijective; thus
B
is an invariant metric on
\mathfrak{g}
.

(\Rightarrow)
Suppose that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits an invariant metric
B
. We shall prove the existence of an element
s\neq 0
in
\mathcal{S}
and a linear map
F:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\to\mathfrak{g}
, such that the ordered pair
(\psi_{s},F)
makes commutative the diagram (
36
), where
\psi_{s}=H^{\ast}\circ\bar{B}^{\flat}\circ\iota_{s}
, and
F\circ H=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Let us consider a basis
\{s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{m}\}
of
\mathcal{S}
, with
s_{1}=1
and let
\{p^{1},p^{2},\cdots,p^{m}\}\subset S^{\ast}
be its dual basis. Let
\widetilde{p}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}\to\mathfrak{g}
be the linear map defined by
\widetilde{p}(x\otimes s)=p^{1}(s)x
for all
x
in
\mathfrak{g}
and for all
s
in
\mathcal{S}
. We define the linear maps:

and

.
Then,

(39)

\displaystyle\bar{B}(x\otimes s,y\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=B\left(F(x\otimes s),y\right),\text{ and }

(40)

\displaystyle\bar{B}\left(H(x),y\otimes s\right)

\displaystyle=p^{1}(s)B(x,y),\,\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}\,\text{ and }s\in\mathcal{S}.

From (
39
), we get
F^{*}\circ B^{\flat}=\bar{B}^{\flat}\circ\iota_{1}
, so
\left(B^{\flat},F\right)
makes commutative the diagram (
36
). In addition, from (

, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
s
in
\mathcal{S}
, thus

satisfies the condition
(iii)
. Letting

in (

, and

, thus

satisfies the condition

.
∎

The following result shows a particular case when the existence of an invariant metric on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
implies that
\mathfrak{g}
also admits one.

3.3 Proposition
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra over
\mathbb{F}
such that
\mathfrak{g}\!\!=\!\![\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a
m
-dimensional associative and commutative algebra with unit, having a non-zero element
s
such that
s^{m}\!=\!0
and
s^{m-1}\!\neq\!0
. If
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric
\bar{B}
, then
\mathfrak{g}
also admits an invariant metric.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{S}
is
m
-dimensional and has a non-zero element
s
such that
s^{m}=0
and
s^{m-1}\neq 0
, then
\mathcal{S}=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{1,s,\ldots,s^{m-1}\}
. For each
t
in
\mathcal{S}
, consider the bilinear map
B_{t}
on
\mathfrak{g}
defined by
B_{t}(x,y)=\bar{B}(x\otimes t,y\otimes 1)
, for all
x
and
y
in
\mathfrak{g}
. Using the skew-symmetry property of the Lie bracket, and that
\bar{B}
is symmetric and invariant, we obtain:

(41)

\begin{split}B_{t}([x,y],z)\!&=\!\!\bar{B}([x\otimes 1,y\otimes t],z\otimes 1)\!=\!\!\bar{B}(x\otimes 1,[y,z]\otimes t)\\
\,&=\!\bar{B}(z\otimes t,[x,y]\otimes 1)\!=\!\!B_{t}(z,[x,y]),\text{ for all }x,y,z\in\!\mathfrak{g}.\end{split}

As
\mathfrak{g}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, we deduce from (
41
) that
B_{t}
is invariant and symmetric. We claim that
B_{s^{m-1}}
is non-degenerate. Let
k\geq 1
, using that
\bar{B}
is invariant, we get:

(42)

\bar{B}([\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\otimes s^{m-1},[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\otimes s^{k})=\bar{B}([[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}],\mathfrak{g}]\otimes s^{m-1+k},\mathfrak{g}\otimes 1)=\{0\},

Take
x
in
\mathfrak{g}
such that
B_{s^{m-1}}(x,\mathfrak{g})=\{0\}
. Then, by (
42
) we have:
\bar{B}(x\otimes\!s^{m-1}\!,\mathfrak{g}\otimes s^{k})\!\!=\!\{0\}
, for all
k\geq 0
, whence
\bar{B}(x\otimes s^{m-1},\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S})\!=\!\!\{0\}
. Since
\bar{B}
is non-degenerate, then
x\!\otimes\!s^{m-1}\!\!=\!0
, and
x\!\!=\!0
, thus
B_{s^{m-1}}
is non-degenerate.
∎

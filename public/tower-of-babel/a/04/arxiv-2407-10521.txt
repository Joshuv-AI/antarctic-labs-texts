On the small-time bilinear control of a nonlinear heat equation: global approximate controllability and exact controllability to trajectories
Alessandro Duca, Eugenio Pozzoli, Cristina Urbani

Abstract
In this work we analyse the small-time reachability properties of a nonlinear parabolic equation, by means of a bilinear control, posed on a torus of arbitrary dimension d d . Under a saturation hypothesis on the control operators, we show the small-time approximate controllability between states sharing the same sign. Moreover, in the one-dimensional case d = 1 d=1 , we combine this property with a local exact controllability result, and prove the small-time exact controllability of any positive states towards the ground state of the evolution operator. Keywords: bilinear control, heat equation, approximate controllability, exact controllability, moment problem, biorthogonal family 2010 MSC: 93B05, 35Q93, 35K05.

Introduction

In this paper we study controllability properties of the following Nonlinear Heat Equation on the
d
-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

\begin{split}\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)=\Big(\Delta-\kappa\psi(t,x)^{p}+\langle u(t),Q(x)\rangle\Big)\psi(t,x),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d},\ t>0,\\
\psi(t=0,\cdot)=\psi_{0}(\cdot),\end{cases}\end{split}

(NHE)

where
d\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the operator
\Delta=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}
is the Laplacian and
Q=(Q_{1},...,Q_{q},\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{q+2},q\in{\mathbb{N}},q\geq 2d+1,

is a fixed bounded measurable function. The set of potentials
(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{q})
is used to establish global approximate controllability properties, as detailed in Section
1.1
, while the set of potentials
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
ensures local exact controllability to trajectories, as described in Section
1.2
.
The
{\mathbb{R}}^{q+2}
-valued function
u\in L^{2}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{+},{\mathbb{R}}^{q+2})
plays the role of a control. It means that
u
is the function that can be chosen to steer the solution of the problem towards a desired state. Observe that our control depends only on time.

In numerous practical problems from chemistry, neurobiology, and life science, the evolution of a specific system is subject to a control which is not external, but rather a modification of the principal parameter of the evolution. In these cases, it can be appropriate to consider evolution equations in the presence of multiplicative controls. Such controls are called bilinear and have the form
\langle u(t),Q(x)\rangle
, as in (
NHE
), if only the time-dependent intensity
u
can be tuned, while the space-dependent potential
Q
is fixed.

An example of a parabolic model with a dynamic governed by a multiplicative control is the distributed parameter control model studied by Lenhart and Bath in
[
36
]
. In such work, the authors consider wildlife damage management to control the population of diffusive small
mammal species such as beavers, raccoons, and muskrats. These small mammal
species often damage human interests, and it is important to study the possibility of controlling their dispersal behaviour. The migratory habit of such animals obviously presents an additional complication. For instance, their removal from any habitat can cause the attraction of other individuals from nearby lands and a consequent increase of the trapping cost. In
[
36
]
, the authors study the dynamics of the population density of one of these species, described by a control model incorporating dispersive dynamics and a multiplicative control that represents trapping. In such work, the evolution is modelled by an equation of the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)=(\alpha\Delta+a-b\varphi(t,x)+p(t,x))\varphi(t,x),\qquad t>0,

(1.1)

where
\varphi
is the population density,
\alpha
is constant,
a
and
b
are growth parameters and
p
is the rate of trapping which is used as a control. When one can control the time-dependent intensity of the trapping but not its spatial distribution, it is possible to separate the variables of the function
p
and write

p(t,x)=u(t)\mu(x).

This choice leads to a new formulation for the evolution model where the actual control is the function
u
and the equation for
a=0
, takes the form of (
NHE
).

1.1
Global approximate controllability on the
d
-dimensional torus

Let us consider the following vector space:

\mathcal{H}_{0}:={\rm span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{q}\}.

We also introduce a collection of
d
elements of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

\mathcal{K}=\Big\{\big(1,0,\dots,0\big),\big(0,1,\dots,0\big),\dots,\big(0,\dots,1,0\big),\big(1,\dots,1\big)\Big\}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

Assumption I
.

The potentials satisfy
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{q}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
, and

\begin{split}&\big\{1,\cos\langle k,x\rangle,\ \sin\langle k,x\rangle\big\}_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\subset\mathcal{H}_{0}.\end{split}

(1.2)

In what follows, we denote by

\psi(t;\psi_{0},u)

the solution of (
NHE
) at time
t
, associated with the initial condition
\psi_{0}
and the control
u
. Of course, it is intended that we look at such solutions only when the existence is ensured (see Proposition
2.1
below).
We now present the first main result of the paper: a small-time global approximate controllability property of (
NHE
) between states that share the same sign.

Main Theorem A
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
be such that
s>d/2
,
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}},
and
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Suppose that Assumption I is satisfied. Then (
NHE
) verifies the following small-time approximate controllability properties.

(i)

Let
\psi_{0},\psi_{1}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
be such that
{\rm sign}(\psi_{0})={\rm sign}(\psi_{1})
. For any
\epsilon>0
and
T>0
, there exist
\tau\in[0,T]
and
(u_{1},...,u_{q})\in L^{2}((0,\tau),{\mathbb{R}}^{q})
, such that

\big\|\psi(\tau;\psi_{0},u)-\psi_{1}\big\|_{L^{2}}<\epsilon,

with
u=(u_{1},...,u_{q},0,0)
.

(ii)

Let
\psi_{0},\psi_{1}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
be such that
\psi_{0},\psi_{1}>0
(or
\psi_{0},\psi_{1}<0
).
For any
\epsilon>0
and
T>0
, there exists
(u_{1},...,u_{q})\in L^{2}((0,T),{\mathbb{R}}^{q})
, such that

\big\|\psi(T;\psi_{0},u)-\psi_{1}\big\|_{H^{s}}<\epsilon,

with
u=(u_{1},...,u_{q},0,0)
.

As stated in part
(i)
of Main Theorem A, with our technique we are able to prove small-time controllability between states sharing the same sign, approximately in
L^{2}
, but not in
H^{s}
for
s>0
. As pointed out in part
(ii)
, for positive states, we can improve the small-time approximate controllability to be also valid in
H^{s}
. In particular, the
H^{s}
-approximate control result for
s>d/2
, stated in part
(ii)
, will be crucial to derive small-time global exact controllability of (
NHE
), driving the solution from any positive
H^{3}
state towards the constant state. Such a result is contained in Main Theorem B below.
Main Theorem A is a specific case of Theorem
3.3
below, where approximate controllability is ensured with general control potentials
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{q}
satisfying specific saturation assumptions.

To the best of our knowledge, the most recent works on approximate controllability of nonlinear parabolic equations via multiplicative controls are
[
19
,
32
]
. In such works, the authors studied
1
-dimensional problems with globally Lipschitz continuous nonlinearities. They proved approximate controllability between states with the same number of sign changes when the time is sufficiently large. They considered controls whose space- and time-dependence can be chosen accordingly to the initial and final states. The novelties of Main Theorem A are thus the following:

•

The approximate controllability is achieved at arbitrarily small times.

•

The approximate controllability holds on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
for any
d\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
, that is, in arbitrary spatial dimensions.

•

The controls depend only on time, that is, the space-dependent potential
Q(x)
is fixed.

•

Equation (
NHE
) exhibits a polynomial nonlinearity.

1.2
Global exact controllability to the ground state solution for the
1
-dimensional problem

The second main result of our work is the exact controllability of (
NHE
) to the ground state solution in the
1
-dimensional case
d=1
. Let us consider the ordered eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of the Laplacian
-\Delta
(not counted with their multiplicity)

\lambda_{k}=k^{2},\qquad\forall\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(1.3)

Note that, except for the first one
\lambda_{0}=0
, all the eigenvalues are double. We denote by
\{c_{0},c_{k},s_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
the corresponding orthonormal eigenfunctions of
-\Delta

c_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}},\qquad c_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\cos(kx),\qquad s_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sin(kx),\qquad\forall\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*},

(1.4)

which form a Hilbert basis of
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}})
. Note that
c_{0}
represents the free evolution (
u=0
) of the linear (
\kappa=0
) heat equation (
NHE
) with initial condition
\psi_{0}=c_{0}
. Such a solution is usually called the
ground state solution
. Henceforth, we shall also use the notation
\Phi:=c_{0}
. To study the exact controllability of (
NHE
), we introduce the following additional assumption.

Assumption II
.

The potentials satisfy
Q_{1}=1
,
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in H^{3}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}})
and

\begin{split}\langle\mu_{1},c_{0}\rangle_{L^{2}}\neq 0,\ \ \ \ \ &\ \ \ \langle\mu_{2},c_{0}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0,\\
\exists\,b_{1},q_{1}>0\,:\>\lambda_{k}^{q_{1}}\left|\langle\mu_{1},c_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|\geq b_{1},\,\,&\text{ and }\,\,\langle\mu_{1},s_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0,\quad\forall\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*},\\
\exists\,b_{2},q_{2}>0\,:\>\lambda_{k}^{q_{2}}\left|\langle\mu_{2},s_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|\geq b_{2},\,\,&\text{ and }\,\,\langle\mu_{2},c_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0,\quad\forall\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}.\end{split}

(1.5)

We now state our second main result which ensures global small-time exact controllability of the 1-dimensional (
NHE
) to the ground state solution, starting from any positive state.

Main Theorem B
.

Let
d=1
,
\kappa\geq 0
and
p\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
. Suppose that Assumptions I and II are satisfied. Then, (
NHE
) is exactly controllable in
H^{3}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}})
to the ground state solution
\Phi
in any positive time from any positive state. More precisely, for any
T>0
and
\psi_{0}\in H^{3}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}})
such that
\psi_{0}>0
, there exists
u\in L^{2}((0,T),{\mathbb{R}}^{q+2})
, such that

\psi(T;\psi_{0},u)=\Phi.

Analogously, for any
T>0
and
\psi_{0}\in H^{3}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}})
such that
\psi_{0}<0
, there exists
u\in L^{2}((0,T),{\mathbb{R}}^{q+2})
, such that

\psi(T;\psi_{0},u)=-\Phi.

Main Theorem B yields small-time exact controllability to the ground state solution in
H^{3}
when
\kappa\geq 0
and
p\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
. This property is obtained by using the global
H^{s}
-approximate controllability result between positive states (Main Theorem A (ii)) together with a
H^{3}
-local exact controllability result to the ground state solution
\Phi
in any positive time (see Theorem
4.3
below). The specific choice of the parameters
\kappa
and
p
ensures that equation (
NHE
) is globally well-posed in
H^{3}
, which is crucial in our proof of the local exact controllability.

Note that the result of Main Theorem B can be achieved using five potentials. Indeed, one can consider

Q=(1,\cos(x),\sin(x),\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

The potentials
Q_{1}=1,Q_{2}=\sin(x),Q_{3}=\cos(x)
, satisfy Assumption I. Examples of functions
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
satisfying Assumption II are (see Example
4.2
below for further details)

\mu_{1}(x)=x^{3}(2\pi-x)^{3},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mu_{2}(x)=x^{3}(x-\pi)^{3}(x-2\pi)^{3}.

An interesting aspect of Main Theorem B is the validity of an exact controllability result on the torus where the Laplacian exhibits double eigenvalues. Indeed, the first step to prove local exact controllability of (
NHE
) is based on the solvability of a suitable moment problem. The method for showing local controllability of bilinear parabolic PDEs, introduced in
[
5
,
6
]
, is not directly applicable in our framework. In fact, we face the following two additional difficulties:

•

the presence of double eigenvalues is, a priori, an obstacle to the solvability of the moment problem at hand. We address the problem by “filtering" the spectrum of the Laplacian via the two potentials
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
.
We exploit hypotheses (
1.5
) so that
\mu_{1}
acts only on the frequencies associated with the eigenfunctions
\{c_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
\mu_{2}
only on those associated with
\{s_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}
. Hence, we decompose the moment problem into two subproblems, each characterized by simple eigenvalues, making both solvable;

•

in order to adapt the technique proposed in
[
6
,
5
]
, the solutions of equation (
NHE
) need to be globally defined and unique. These properties may be trivial in the linear case, whereas a more careful analysis should be developed for a nonlinear equation like (
NHE
). We propose a method which requires more regularity on the initial condition and on the control.

Some references

The classical controllability problem of parabolic-type equations as (
NHE
), with bilinear control, is a delicate matter even in the linear case (
\kappa=0
). The main reason is a structural obstacle, described in
[
10
]
, which makes controllability in
L^{2}
impossible. In detail, the results of
[
10
]
imply that the reachable set of (
NHE
), with
\kappa=0
, starting from any
\psi_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
, is contained in a countable union of compact subsets of
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
, hence it has dense complement. Therefore, this property prevents from obtaining any classical exact controllability result in
L^{2}
. Hence, we shall explore different notions of controllability, such as the approximate controllability or the exact controllability to trajectories.

Approximate controllability results via multiplicative controls have been obtained in
[
35
]
for
1
-D linear parabolic problems in sufficiently large times
T>0
and between non-negative states. In
[
35
]
, the control depends on both space and time (see also
[
34
]
). Similar results were proved in
[
18
]
for linear degenerate parabolic problems. Finally, approximate controllability properties of the nonlinear problem were established in
[
19
,
31
,
32
]
.

The techniques leading to approximate controllability of Main Theorem A are inspired by a saturating geometric control approach introduced by Agrachev and Sarychev in
[
1
,
2
]
in the case of additive controls. In such works, they proved global approximate controllability of the 2D Navier-Stokes and
Euler systems. Their approach has been extended to study other equations with additive controls (see, for instance,
[
3
,
41
,
44
,
45
]
). The saturating control methodology has been implemented for bilinear control problems in the recent work
[
25
]
of Nersesyan and the first author. In such paper, small-time approximate controllability between eigenmodes, for a non-linear Schrödinger equation, is proved. Main Theorem A is inspired by the approach developed in
[
25
]
. Nevertheless, the saturating technique for parabolic equation as (
NHE
) leads to different controllability properties, such as small-time approximate controllability between states that have the same sign. For additional results on small-time controllability of PDEs through bilinear control, achieved using similar methods, we refer also to
[
23
,
24
,
27
,
42
]
.

Another controllability property, not ruled out by the negative findings of
[
10
]
, is the exact controllability to trajectories. This property was firstly studied by Alabau-Boussouira, Cannarsa and Urbani in
[
5
,
6
]
in an abstract setting for parabolic PDEs by means of a scalar-input bilinear control.
An example of application of such results is the local and semi-global controllability of a heat equation as (
NHE
) (for
\kappa=0
) on the interval
(0,1)
. The methodology has been later extended in
[
17
]
to network-type domains to cope with condensation phenomena of the eigenvalues of the diffusion operator. The approach of
[
5
,
6
]
used for studying local exact controllability to eigensolutions relies on the solvability of a suitable moment problem related to the null controllability of a linearized version of the problem at hand. The resolution of moment problems has been intensively studied in literature over a long period, starting from the classical works
[
29
,
30
]
, to the more recent ones
[
7
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
17
,
20
,
21
,
33
]
.

2
Preliminaries

The aim of this section is to present some preliminary results. We start by ensuring existence and uniqueness of solutions of equation (
NHE
). In the second part of the section we prove a limit of conjugated dynamics, which is a key point in the proof of Main Theorem A.

2.1
Local and global well-posedness

We start by stating the following local well-posedness result for the Cauchy problem (
NHE
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
s>d/2
and
Q\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{q+2})
. For any
\psi_{0}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
and
u\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{R}^{q+2})

there exists a maximal time
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}(\psi_{0},u)>0
and a unique mild solution
\psi
of (
NHE
). Namely, for any
T<\mathcal{T}
,
\psi\in C^{0}([0,T],H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}))
and is represented by the formula

\psi(t;\psi_{0},u)=e^{t\Delta}\psi_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\left(\langle u(s),Q(x)\rangle\psi(s,x)-\kappa\psi(s,x)^{p+1}\right)ds.

Moreover, if
{\cal T}<+\infty
, then
\left\lVert\psi(t)\right\rVert_{H^{s}}\to+\infty
as
t\to{\cal T}^{-}
. In addition, we have the following properties.

i.

Assume that
\psi_{0},\phi_{0}\in B_{H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})}(0,R)
for some
R>0
and
u,v\in L^{2}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{+},{\mathbb{R}}^{q+2})
. Then, for any
0\leq T\leq\min\{{\cal T}(\psi_{0},u),{\cal T}(\phi_{0},v)\}
, there exists
C=C(u,v)
such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\psi(t;\psi_{0},u)-\psi(t;\phi_{0},v)\right\rVert_{H^{s}}\leq C\left(\left\lVert\psi_{0}-\phi_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}}+\left\lVert u-v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right).

(2.1)

ii.

Set
K=\|\psi\|_{C([0,T],H^{s})}+\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}
. There exists
\delta=\delta({\cal T}(\psi_{0},u),K)>0
such that, for any
\hat{\psi}_{0}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
and
\hat{u}\in L^{2}((0,T),{\mathbb{R}}^{q+2})
satisfying

\|\hat{\psi}_{0}-\psi_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\hat{u}-u\|_{L^{2}}<\delta,

(2.2)

problem (
NHE
) admits a unique mild solution
\hat{\psi}\in C\big([0,T],H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})\big)
with initial condition
\hat{\psi}_{0}
and control
\hat{u}
.

The proof of Proposition
2.1
follows from a fix point argument and from Sobolev embeddings for
s>d/2
. It can be found in Appendix
A
.

We further present a global well-posedness result for equation (
NHE
) in the case
d=1
,
\kappa\geq 0
and
p\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
d=1
,
p\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
,
\psi_{0}\in H^{3}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}})
,
Q\in H^{3}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}}^{q+2})
,
u\in H^{1}_{loc}((0,+\infty),{\mathbb{R}}^{q+2})
and
\kappa\geq 0
. Then, for any
T>0
there exists a unique mild solution
\psi\in C^{0}([0,T],H^{3}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}}))
of (
NHE
).

The proof of Proposition
2.2
follows from some energy estimates and can be found in Appendix
B
.

2.2
Small-time limit of conjugated dynamics

Let us introduce the nonlinear operator

{\mathbb{B}}(\varphi)(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{x_{j}}\varphi(x)\right)^{2},\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in C^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}).

(2.3)

Then, the following result holds true.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,
s>d/2
and
(Q_{1},...,Q_{q})\in H^{2s+1}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{q})
. Assume that
\psi_{0}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
,
(u_{1},...,u_{q})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
, and
\varphi\in H^{2s+1}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
to be non-negative. Then, there exists a constant
\delta_{0}>0
such that, for any
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
, the solution
\psi(t;e^{-\delta^{-1/2}\varphi}\psi_{0},\delta^{-1}u)
of (
NHE
) with
u=(u_{1},...,u_{q},0,0)
is well-defined in
[0,\delta]
. Furthermore, the following limit holds

e^{\delta^{-1/2}\varphi}\psi(\delta;e^{-\delta^{-1/2}\varphi}\psi_{0},\delta^{-1}u)\to e^{{\mathbb{B}}(\varphi)+\langle u,Q\rangle}\psi_{0}\quad\text{in $H^{s}$, as $\delta\to 0^{+}$}.

Proof.

Preliminaries.

For any
\delta>0
we set
\phi(t):=e^{\delta^{-1/2}\varphi}\psi(t;e^{-\delta^{-1/2}\varphi}\psi_{0},\delta^{-1}u)
. According to Proposition
2.1
,
\phi(t)
exists up to some maximal time
{\cal T}^{\delta}={\cal T}(e^{-\delta^{-1/2}\varphi}\psi_{0},\delta^{-1}u)
and it has the same regularity of
\psi
. Furthermore, if
{\cal T}^{\delta}<\infty

\|e^{-\delta^{-1/2}\varphi}\phi(t)\|_{H^{s}}\to+\infty\quad\text{as~$t\to{\cal T}^{\delta-}$.}

We introduce the following functions

\displaystyle w(t)=e^{\left({\mathbb{B}}(\varphi)+\langle u,Q\rangle\right)t}\psi_{0}^{\delta},\qquad v(t)=\phi(\delta t)-w(t),

(2.4)

where

\psi_{0}^{\delta}=e^{\delta^{1/4}\Delta}\psi_{0}\in H^{r}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
, with
r=s+2
, is such that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{0}-\psi_{0}^{\delta}\|_{H^{s}}\to 0,\qquad\text{ as $\delta\to 0^{+}$,}

(2.5)

and there exists
C>0
independent of
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{0}^{\delta}\|_{H^{s}}\leq C,\qquad\|\psi_{0}^{\delta}\|_{H^{r}}\leq C\delta^{-1/4},\qquad\text{for }\delta\leq 1.

(2.6)

Our aim is to show that
\phi(\delta)\xrightarrow{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}e^{\left({\mathbb{B}}(\varphi)+\langle u,Q\rangle\right)}\psi_{0}
in
H^{s}
. Thanks to (
2.5
) it is sufficient to prove that

\displaystyle\|v(t=1)\|_{H^{s}}\xrightarrow{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}0.

(2.7)

However, before proving (
2.7
), we need to ensure the existence of
\delta_{0}>0
small enough such that, for every
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
,
v(t)
is well-defined in
[0,1]
, that is,

\displaystyle\delta^{-1}{\cal T}^{\delta}\geq 1.

(2.8)

An intermediate inequality.
In view of (
2.6
), there exists
C>0
such that, for every
t\in[0,2]
,

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C,\quad\,\,\quad\quad\|w(t)\|_{H^{r}}\leq C{\delta^{-1/4}}.

(2.9)

We observe that
v
is a solution of the following equation

\begin{split}\partial_{t}v&=\delta\Delta(v+w)-\delta\kappa(e^{-\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varphi}(v+w))^{p}(v+w)-\delta^{1/2}(v+w)\Delta\varphi\\
&\quad-2\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(v+w)\cdot\nabla\varphi+{\mathbb{B}}(\varphi)v+\langle u,Q\rangle v,\end{split}

(2.10)

with initial condition

v(0)=\psi_{0}-\psi_{0}^{\delta}.

(2.11)

Let us start by assuming that
\psi_{0}\in H^{2s+2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
which implies
\psi(t)\in H^{2s+2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
and then
v(t)\in H^{2s+2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
for every
t\in(0,{\cal T}^{\delta})
.
Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
be such that
|\alpha|=|\alpha_{1}|+\ldots+|\alpha_{d}|\leq s
.
Thanks to the accreativity of the operator
-\Delta
, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\|\partial^{\alpha}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\delta\langle(-\Delta\partial^{\alpha}v),\partial^{\alpha}v\rangle_{L^{2}}+2\langle\delta\Delta w+\delta\kappa(e^{-\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varphi}(v+w))^{p}(v+w)

\displaystyle\quad-\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}(v+w)\Delta\varphi+2\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(v+w)\cdot\nabla\varphi+{\mathbb{B}}(\varphi)v+\langle u,Q\rangle v,\partial^{2\alpha}v\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\langle\delta\Delta w+\delta\kappa e^{-p\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varphi}(v+w)^{p+1}-\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}(v+w)\Delta\varphi

\displaystyle\quad+2\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(v+w)\cdot\nabla\varphi+{\mathbb{B}}(\varphi)v+\langle u,Q\rangle v,\partial^{2\alpha}v\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Big(\delta|\langle\partial^{\alpha}\Delta w,\partial^{\alpha}v\rangle_{L^{2}}|+\delta|\kappa|\big|\big\langle\partial^{\alpha}\big(e^{-p\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varphi}(v+w)^{p+1}\big),\partial^{\alpha}v\big\rangle_{L^{2}}\big|

\displaystyle\quad+\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}|\langle\partial^{\alpha}\big((v+w)\Delta\varphi\big),\partial^{\alpha}v\rangle_{L^{2}}|+\delta^{1/2}|\langle\partial^{\alpha}\big(\nabla(v+w)\cdot\nabla\varphi\big),\partial^{\alpha}v\rangle_{L^{2}}|

(2.12)

\displaystyle\quad+|\langle\partial^{\alpha}\big({\mathbb{B}}(\varphi)v\big),\partial^{\alpha}v\rangle_{L^{2}}|+|\big\langle\partial^{\alpha}\big(\langle u,Q\rangle v\big),\partial^{\alpha}v\big\rangle_{L^{2}}|\Big).

We observe that since all the functions involved in the above estimate are in
H^{2s+2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
, no boundary terms appear when integrating by parts.
Note that there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
\delta
, such that

|\langle\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi,\partial^{2\alpha}v\rangle_{L^{2}}|\leq C\|\varphi\|_{H^{2s+1}}\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}
and

\displaystyle\big|\big\langle\partial^{\alpha}\big(e^{-p\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varphi}(v+w)^{p+1}\big),\partial^{\alpha}v\big\rangle_{L^{2}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\|e^{-p\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\varphi\|_{H^{s+1}}^{s}\Big(p^{s}\delta^{-\frac{s}{2}}+1\Big)\|v+w\|_{H^{s}}^{p+1}\|v\|_{H^{s}}.

Hence, we deduce the existence of a constant
C>0
, independent of
\delta
, such that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\|\partial^{\alpha}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta\|w\|_{H^{r}}\|v\|_{H^{s}}+C\delta\|e^{-p\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}}(\delta^{-\frac{s}{2}}+1)\|v+w\|_{H^{s}}^{p+1}\|v\|_{H^{s}}+C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v+w\|_{H^{s}}\|v\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\delta^{1/2}\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+C\delta^{1/2}\|w\|_{H^{r}}\|v\|_{H^{s}}+C\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+C\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Now, we have that
\|e^{-\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}}(\delta^{-\frac{s}{2}}+1)\xrightarrow{\delta\rightarrow 0}0
thanks to the positivity of
\varphi
. Using (
2.9
) and the Young’s inequality we deduce that there exists
C>0
, independent of
\delta
, such that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\|\partial^{\alpha}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{3/4}\|v\|_{H^{s}}+C\delta\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{p+2}+C\delta\|v\|_{H^{s}}+C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\delta^{1/2}\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+C\delta^{1/4}\|v\|_{H^{s}}+C\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+C\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{1/2}+C(1+\delta^{1/2})\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+C\delta\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{p+2}.

The last relation holds for
t\leq\delta^{-1}{\cal T}^{\delta}
. We recall that
\|\cdot\|_{H^{s}}^{2}=\sum_{\underset{|\alpha|\leq s}{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}}\|\partial^{\alpha}\cdot\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
and therefore there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{1/2}+C(1+\delta^{1/2})\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+C\delta\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{p+2}.

By the Grönwall Lemma and recalling (
2.11
) we obtain that

\|v(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq e^{C(1+\delta^{1/2})t}\left(C\delta^{1/2}t+\|\psi_{0}-\psi_{0}^{\delta}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+C\delta\int_{0}^{t}\|v(y)\|_{H^{s}}^{p+2}{\textup{d}}y\right),

(2.13)

for
t\leq\delta^{-1}{\cal T}^{\delta}
and for every
\psi_{0}\in H^{2s+2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
. Finally, we can extend the validity of (
2.13
) for every
\psi_{0}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
thanks to item
ii.
of Proposition
2.1
and to the density of

H^{2s+2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
into
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
with respect to the
H^{s}
-norm.

Properties of the maximal time.
It remains to prove (

). We consider
\delta_{0}>0
sufficiently small so that, for

, we have
\|\psi_{0}-\psi_{0}^{\delta}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}<1/2
and then

\|v(0)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}<1/2.

Denote
\tau^{\delta}:=\sup\left\{t<\delta^{-1}{\cal T}^{\delta}:\|v(t)\|_{H^{s}}<1\right\}
. The above inequality yields
\tau^{\delta}>0
. If
\tau^{\delta}=+\infty
then (
2.8
) is obviously satisfied. Thus, let us assume that

\tau^{\delta}<+\infty.

To prove (
2.8
) we show that for
\delta_{0}>0
sufficiently small and
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
we have
\tau^{\delta}\geq 1
. Assume by contradiction that, for every
\delta_{0}>0
small, there exists
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
such that
\tau^{\delta}<1
. Thanks to (

\displaystyle 1=\|v(\tau^{\delta})\|_{H^{s}}^{2}<e^{C(1+\delta^{1/2})\tau^{\delta}}\left(C\delta^{1/2}\tau^{\delta}+\|\psi_{0}-\psi_{0}^{\delta}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+C\delta\int_{0}^{\tau^{\delta}}\|v(y)\|_{H^{s}}^{p+2}{\textup{d}}y\right).

(2.14)

We recall that, by definition,
\|v(t)\|_{H^{s}}<1
in

. For
\delta_{0}

\displaystyle e^{C(1+\delta^{1/2})\tau^{\delta}}\left(C\delta^{1/2}\tau^{\delta}+\|\psi_{0}-\psi_{0}^{\delta}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\right)<\frac{1}{2},

(2.15)

since

. Moreover,

\displaystyle e^{C(1+\delta^{1/2})\tau^{\delta}}\left(C\delta^{1/2}\tau^{\delta}+\|\psi_{0}-\psi_{0}^{\delta}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+C\delta\int_{0}^{\tau^{\delta}}\|v(y)\|_{H^{s}}^{p+2}{\textup{d}}y\right)<1,

\tau^{\delta}>1
for every

Finally,
1\in[0,\tau^{\delta})\subset[0,\delta^{-1}{\cal T}^{\delta})

A first simple consequence of Proposition

, concerning the control of (

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
be such that
s>d/2
and
1\in{\rm span}\{Q_{1},...,Q_{q}\}
. Assume that
\psi_{0}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
.
For any
\epsilon,T>0
there exists a constant control

such that the solution
\psi(t;\psi_{0},u)
of (
NHE
) is well-defined in
[0,T]
and

\|\psi(T;\psi_{0},u)\|_{H^{s}}<\epsilon.

Proof.

For any
\tilde{\epsilon}>0

e^{-c}<\frac{\tilde{\epsilon}}{2\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{s}}}\ \ \ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ \ \ \ \|e^{-c}\psi_{0}\|_{H^{s}}<\frac{\tilde{\epsilon}}{2}.

2.3

3
Small-time approximate controllability

The aim of this section is to prove the small-time approximate controllability results stated in Main Theorem A.

3.1
An intermediate controllability result

Given
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{q}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}),q\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
, we introduce the vector space

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{0}={\rm span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{q}\}.\end{split}

We define
\mathcal{H}_{j}
, for every
j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
, as the largest vector space whose elements
\psi
can be written in the form

\begin{split}\psi=\varphi_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\mathbb{B}}(\varphi_{k}),\qquad\varphi_{0},\dots,\varphi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{j-1},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}},\end{split}

(3.1)

where the non-linear operator
{\mathbb{B}}
is defined in (
2.3
). It is intended that the sum over an empty set of indices, i.e. for
n=0
, is equal to zero.
We also denote

\mathcal{H}_{\infty}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{j}.

(3.2)

Let us recall the following result from
[
25
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

[
25
, Proposition 2.6]

Assume that

\{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{q}\}=\{1,\sin\langle k,x\rangle),\cos\langle k,x\rangle\}_{k\in L},

for some
L\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Then,
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
, defined as in (
3.1
)-(
3.2
), is dense in
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}),s\geq 0
, if and only if

•

L
is a generator,

•

for any
l,m\in L
, there exists a family
\{n_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\sigma}\subset L
such that
l\not\perp n_{1},n_{j}\not\perp n_{j+1},j=1,\dots,\sigma-1,
and
n_{\sigma}\not\perp m
.

Observe that, due to Proposition
3.1
, Assumption I guarantees that the choice
L=\mathcal{K}
(cf. (
1.2
)) produces a space
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
that is dense in
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}),s\geq 0
.
We start by ensuring the following property of small-time approximate controllability for (
NHE
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
be such that
s>d/2
and
(Q_{1},...,Q_{q})\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{q})
be such that
1\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Assume that
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
is dense in
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
. Let
\psi_{0}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
and
\varphi\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
. For any
\epsilon,T>0
, there exist
\tau\in[0,T)
and
(u_{1},...,u_{q})\in L^{2}((0,\tau),{\mathbb{R}}^{q})
such that the solution
\psi(t;\psi_{0},u)
of (
NHE
) with control
u=(u_{1},...,u_{q},0,0)
is well-defined in
[0,\tau]
and

\|\psi(\tau;\psi_{0},u)-e^{\varphi}\psi_{0}\|_{H^{s}}<\epsilon.

Proof.

Let us recall that the concatenation
v*u
of two scalar control laws
u:[0,T_{1}]\to\mathbb{R}^{q+2},\ v:[0,T_{2}]\to\mathbb{R}^{q+2}
is defined on
[0,T_{1}+T_{2}]
as follows

(v*u)(t)=\begin{cases}u(t),&t\in[0,T_{1}],\\
v(t-T_{1}),&t\in(T_{1},T_{1}+T_{2}].\end{cases}

Such definition extends to controls with values in
\mathbb{R}^{q}
, componentwise.
We will often use the fact that

\psi(T_{1}+t;\psi_{0},v*u)=\psi(t;\psi(T_{1},\psi_{0},u),v),\quad t>0.

Let us start by assuming that the following property holds for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

(
P_{n}
)

for any
\psi_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
,
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
, and any
\varepsilon,T>0
, there exist
\tau\in[0,T)
and
(u_{1},...,u_{q}):[0,\tau]\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
piecewise constant such that the solution of (
NHE
) associated with the initial condition
\psi_{0}
and the control
u=(u_{1},...,u_{q},0,0)
satisfies

\left\|\psi(\tau;\psi_{0},u)-e^{\phi}\psi_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}<\varepsilon.

The property
(P_{n})
combined with the density feature implies the statement at once.

We are thus left to prove
(P_{n})
. An analogous property appeared in
[
25
, Theorem 2.2]
to study nonlinear Schrödinger equations with bilinear controls. We provide the proof for completeness. Let us proceed by induction on the index
n
.

Inductive basis:
n=0

If
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, there exists
(u_{1},...,u_{q})\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
such that
\phi(x)=\langle u,Q(x)\rangle
with
u=(u_{1},...,u_{q},0,0)
. Applying Proposition
2.3
, with
\varphi=0
, we deduce that there exists
\tau\in[0,T)
such that the solution of (
NHE
) associated with the constant control
u^{\tau}:=u/\tau
and with the initial condition
\psi_{0}
satisfies

\left\|\psi(\tau;\psi_{0},u^{\tau})-e^{\phi}\psi_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}<\varepsilon,

which proves the desired property.

Inductive step:
n\Rightarrow n+1

By assuming that
(P_{n})
holds, we prove
(P_{n+1})
. If
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{n+1}
, there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\phi_{0},\dots,\phi_{N}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
such that

\phi=\phi_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\mathbb{B}}(\phi_{j}).

Consider
\phi_{1}
and
c>0
such that
\tilde{\phi}_{1}=\phi_{1}+c\geq 0
and note that
{\mathbb{B}}(\tilde{\phi}_{1})={\mathbb{B}}(\phi_{1})
. Due to Proposition
2.3
, we can find
\delta\in(0,T/3)
such that

\left\|e^{\delta^{-1/2}\tilde{\phi}_{1}}\psi(\delta;e^{-\delta^{-1/2}\tilde{\phi}_{1}}\psi_{0},0)-e^{{\mathbb{B}}(\phi_{1})}\psi_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}}<\varepsilon/2.

Since
c\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
,
\phi_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
, then
\tilde{\phi}_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
. Thanks to the inductive hypothesis, for any
\varepsilon^{\prime},T,\delta>0
there exist
\delta^{\prime}\in(0,T/3)
and a piecewise constant control
u^{\delta^{\prime},\delta}=(u^{\delta^{\prime},\delta}_{1},...,u^{\delta^{\prime},\delta}_{q},0,0):[0,\delta^{\prime}]\to\mathbb{R}^{q+2}
such that

\left\|\psi(\delta^{\prime};\psi_{0},u^{\delta^{\prime},\delta})-e^{-\delta^{-1/2}\tilde{\phi}_{1}}\psi_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}}<\varepsilon^{\prime}.

(3.3)

Now, let the dynamics evolve freely in a time interval of length
\delta
, that is, we consider the control
0|_{[0,\delta]}=(0,\dots,0):[0,\delta]\to\mathbb{R}^{q+2}
.
From (
2.1
) we deduce that there exists
C=C(\delta)
such that

\left\|\psi(\delta^{\prime}+\delta;\psi_{0},0|_{[0,\delta]}*u^{\delta^{\prime},\delta})-\psi(\delta;e^{-\delta^{-1/2}\tilde{\phi}_{1}}\psi_{0},0)\right\|_{H^{s}}=\left\|\psi(\delta;\psi(\delta^{\prime};\psi_{0},u^{\delta^{\prime},\delta}),0)-\psi(\delta;e^{-\delta^{-1/2}\tilde{\phi}_{1}}\psi_{0},0)\right\|_{H^{s}}<C\varepsilon^{\prime}.

We use again the inductive hypothesis to deduce that there exist
\delta^{\prime\prime}\in(0,T/3)
and a piecewise constant control
u^{\delta^{\prime\prime},\delta}=(u^{\delta^{\prime\prime},\delta}_{1},...,u^{\delta^{\prime\prime},\delta}_{q},0,0):[0,\delta^{\prime\prime}]\to\mathbb{R}^{q+2}
such that

\left\|\psi(\delta^{\prime\prime};\psi(\delta;e^{-\delta^{-1/2}\tilde{\phi}_{1}}\psi_{0},0),u^{\delta^{\prime\prime},\delta})-e^{\delta^{-1/2}\tilde{\phi}_{1}}\psi(\delta;e^{-\delta^{-1/2}\tilde{\phi}_{1}}\psi_{0},0)\right\|_{H^{s}}<\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Then, thanks to (
2.1
), there exists
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(\|u^{\delta^{\prime\prime},\delta}\|_{L^{2}},\delta^{\prime\prime})
such that

\begin{split}&\left\|\psi(\delta^{\prime}+\delta+\delta^{\prime\prime};\psi_{0},u^{\delta^{\prime\prime},\delta}*0|_{[0,\delta]}*u^{\delta^{\prime},\delta})-e^{{\mathbb{B}}(\phi_{1})}\psi_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\left\|\psi(\delta^{\prime\prime};\psi(\delta^{\prime}+\delta;\psi_{0},0|_{[0,\delta]}*u^{\delta^{\prime},\delta}),u^{\delta^{\prime\prime},\delta})-\psi(\delta^{\prime\prime};\psi(\delta;e^{-\delta^{-1/2}\tilde{\phi}_{1}}\psi_{0},0),u^{\delta^{\prime\prime},\delta})\right\|_{H^{s}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left\|\psi(\delta^{\prime\prime};\psi(\delta;e^{-\delta^{-1/2}\tilde{\phi}_{1}}\psi_{0},0),u^{\delta^{\prime\prime},\delta})-e^{\delta^{-1/2}\tilde{\phi}_{1}}\psi(\delta;e^{-\delta^{-1/2}\tilde{\phi}_{1}}\psi_{0},0)\right\|_{H^{s}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left\|e^{\delta^{-1/2}\tilde{\phi}_{1}}\psi(\delta;e^{-\delta^{-1/2}\tilde{\phi}_{1}}\psi_{0},0)-e^{{\mathbb{B}}(\phi_{1})}\psi_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq C^{\prime}C\varepsilon^{\prime}+\varepsilon^{\prime}+\varepsilon/2.\end{split}

Choosing
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
small enough such that
C^{\prime}C\varepsilon^{\prime}+\varepsilon^{\prime}<\varepsilon/2
, we have then proved that the piecewise constant control
u^{\delta^{\prime\prime},\delta}*0|_{[0,\delta]}*u^{\delta^{\prime},\delta}
steers the initial state
\psi_{0}

\varepsilon
-close to the state

e^{{\mathbb{B}}(\phi_{1})}\psi_{0}

in time
\tau:=\delta^{\prime\prime}+\delta+\delta^{\prime}<T
.
We can now repeat the same argument for
\phi_{2}
, reasoning as if we were starting from the initial state
e^{{\mathbb{B}}(\phi_{1})}\psi_{0}
, and prove that the system can be driven arbitrarily close to the state
e^{{\mathbb{B}}(\phi_{1})+{\mathbb{B}}(\phi_{2})}\psi_{0}
in arbitrarily small time, and hence, by iteration, to
e^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbb{B}}(\phi_{i})}\psi_{0}
. By inductive hypothesis we conclude that there exists a piecewise constant control
u
leading the state
e^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbb{B}}(\phi_{i})}\psi_{0}
arbitrarily close to

e^{\phi_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbb{B}}(\phi_{i})}\psi_{0}

in arbitrarily small time. This completes the proof of property
(P_{n})
.
∎

3.2
Small-time global approximate controllability

In this section we prove a more general results which in fact has inspired Main Theorem A.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,
s>d/2
and let
(Q_{1},...,Q_{q})\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{q})

(i)

Let

{\rm sign}(\psi_{0})={\rm sign}(\psi_{1})
. For any
\epsilon>0
and
T>0
, there exist
\tau\in(0,T]
and
(u_{1},...,u_{q})\in L^{2}((0,\tau),{\mathbb{R}}^{q})
for which the solution
\psi(t;\psi_{0},u)
of (
NHE
) with control
u=(u_{1},...,u_{q},0,0)
is well-defined in
[0,\tau]
and satisfies

\|\psi(\tau;\psi_{0},u)-\psi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}<\epsilon.

(ii)

Let
\psi_{0},\psi_{1}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
be such that
\psi_{0},\psi_{1}>0
(or
\psi_{0},\psi_{1}<0
).
For any
\epsilon>0
and
T>0
, there exists
(u_{1},...,u_{q})\in L^{2}((0,T),{\mathbb{R}}^{q})
such that the solution
\psi(t;\psi_{0},u)
of (
NHE
) with control
u=(u_{1},...,u_{q},0,0)
is well-defined in
[0,T]
and satisfies

\|\psi(T;\psi_{0},u)-\psi_{1}\|_{H^{s}}<\epsilon.

Proof.

Let us start by proving
(i)
. Denote by
Z
the set of zeroes of
\psi_{j}
, for
j=0,1
, and its complement in
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
,
Z^{c}
. Consider for
\eta>0

and its complement in
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
,
Z^{c}_{\eta}
. For
\eta>0
, we have
Z^{c}_{\eta}\subset Z^{c}
and we define

\phi_{\eta}:=\rho_{\eta}\log(\psi_{1}/\psi_{0}),

where
\rho^{\eta}=1
in
Z^{c}_{\eta}
and
0
elsewhere.
Notice that
\phi_{\eta}
is well-defined because
\psi_{1}/\psi_{0}>0
on

, and
\phi_{\eta}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
.
We have

Hence, fixed any
\varepsilon,T>0
, we can choose
\eta>0
small enough such that

Now, by the density of
H^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
into
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
, there exists
\widetilde{\phi}_{\eta}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
such that

\|e^{\widetilde{\phi}_{\eta}}\psi_{0}-\psi_{1}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq\|e^{\widetilde{\phi}_{\eta}}\psi_{0}-e^{\phi_{\eta}}\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}+\|e^{\phi_{\eta}}\psi_{0}-\psi_{1}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}<\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}.

Then, we apply Proposition
3.2
with
\varphi=\widetilde{\phi}_{\eta}
and we deduce that there exist a time
\tau\in[0,T)
and a control
u=(u_{1},...,u_{q},0,0)\in L^{2}((0,\tau),{\mathbb{R}}^{q+2})
such that the solution
\psi(t;\psi_{0},u)
of (
NHE
) is well-defined in
[0,\tau]
and

\|\psi(\tau;\psi_{0},u)-e^{\varphi}\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

By the triangular inequality we conclude that

\|\psi(\tau;\psi_{0},u)-\psi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\psi(\tau;\psi_{0},u)-e^{\varphi}\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|e^{\varphi}\psi_{0}-\psi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}<\varepsilon,

and the first item of the Proposition is then proved.

The proof of
(ii)
follows from the same strategy used to prove
(i)
. However, we now consider the

-norm instead of the

-norm.
In this case, we substitute
\phi_{\eta}
with two functions
\phi_{1}=\log(\sign(\psi_{0})/\psi_{0})
and
\phi_{2}=\log(\sign(\psi_{1})\psi_{1})
, which are well-defined everywhere in
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
. Note that, according to our assumptions,
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
.
Proposition
3.2
with
\varphi=\phi_{1}
yields the existence of a control
u^{1}=(u^{1}_{1},...,u^{1}_{q},0,0):[0,\tau_{1})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{q+2}
that steers
\psi_{0}
close to the constant state
1
in time
\tau_{1}\leq T/2
. We apply again Proposition
3.2
with
\varphi=\phi_{2}
. Thus, we can find a control
u^{2}=(u^{2}_{1},...,u^{2}_{q},0,0):[0,\tau_{2})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{q+2}
such that the solution of (
NHE
) starting from a state close to
1
at time
\tau_{2}\leq T/2
reaches a neighbourhood of the final target
\psi_{1}
.
Hence, the approximate controllability in
H^{s}
can be achieved in time

by exploiting that
1
is a stationary solution of (

) associated with the control

, thanks to the assumption that

. Therefore, the control will be defined as

(u^{2}*u^{stat}*u^{1})(t)={\mathbf{1}}_{[0,\tau_{1})}u^{1}(t)+{\mathbf{1}}_{[\tau_{1},T-\tau_{2}]}u^{stat}(t-\tau_{1})+{\mathbf{1}}_{(T-\tau_{2},T]}u^{2}(t-T+\tau_{2}).

∎

4.1
Control of the linearized system

The result of Theorem

We first observe that the ground state

is solution of (

[0,T]
for

Let

\xi(s_{0})=\xi_{0},\end{cases}

(4.4)

with

Definition 4.4
.

The pair

. If

the pair

1

Remark 4.5
.

Let us observe that, fixed

and

Such regularity is called

The following result shows that the last two components of the control are used to drive the linear system (

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose that Assumption II is satisfied. Then,
(-\partial^{2}_{x},Q)
is smoothly null controllable in any time
T>0
. Namely, for any
\xi_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}})
, there exists
(v_{1},v_{2})\in H^{1}_{0}((0,T),{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
such that the solution of (
4.4
) with
(s_{0},s_{1})=(0,T)
and

satisfies

\xi(T;\xi_{0},v)=0.

Furthermore, there exist
\nu,T_{0}>0
such that

N(\tau)\leq e^{\nu/\tau},\quad\forall\,0<\tau\leq T_{0}.

(4.5)

Proof.

For all
T>0
, we want to prove the existence of
N(T)>0
such that for any initial condition
\xi_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}})
there exists a control
v=(0,...,0,v_{1},v_{2})
so that
\xi(T;0,\xi_{0},v)=0
. To this purpose, we first note that the operator
-\partial_{x}^{2}+p\kappa
exhibits double eigenvalues
\tilde{\lambda}_{j}=\lambda_{j}+p\kappa
with
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
(see definition (
1.3
) of
\lambda_{j}
) – with the exception of the first one – associated to the eigenfunctions
\{c_{0},c_{j},s_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}
, defined in (
1.4
).

Consider the reduced problem (
4.4
) with
\hat{Q}=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
and
(s_{0},s_{1})=(0,T)
. We look for a control of the form
\hat{v}=(v_{1},v_{2})
. We decompose the solution with respect to the Hilbert basis
\{c_{0},c_{k},s_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}
. Recalling that
\langle\mu_{1},s_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle\mu_{2},c_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0
, the smooth null controllability property is equivalent to finding
\hat{v}\in H^{1}_{0}((0,T);{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
such that

0=\xi(T;0,\xi_{0},\hat{v})=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}e^{-\tilde{\lambda}_{k}T}\langle\xi_{0},c_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}c_{k}+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}e^{-\tilde{\lambda}_{k}T}\langle\xi_{0},s_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}s_{k}\\
-\int_{0}^{T}v_{1}(s)\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}e^{-\tilde{\lambda}_{k}(T-s)}\langle\mu_{1}\Phi,c_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}c_{k}\,ds-\int_{0}^{T}v_{2}(s)\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}e^{-\tilde{\lambda}_{k}(T-s)}\langle\mu_{2}\Phi,s_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}s_{k}\,ds.

Note that
\Phi=c_{0}
is a constant. The above equation is satisfied when the following infinite number of identities hold true for a control function
\hat{v}=(v_{1},v_{2})

\begin{split}\langle\xi_{0},c_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}=c_{0}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\tilde{\lambda}_{k}s}v_{1}(s)\langle\mu_{1},c_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\,ds,\quad\forall\,k\in{\mathbb{N}},\\
\langle\xi_{0},s_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}=c_{0}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\tilde{\lambda}_{k}s}v_{2}(s)\langle\mu_{2},s_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\,ds,\quad\forall\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*},\end{split}

that can be rewritten in compact form as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{\lambda_{k}s}\tilde{v}_{1}(s)\,ds=d_{k}^{1},\quad\forall\,k\in{\mathbb{N}},

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{\lambda_{k}s}\tilde{v}_{2}(s)\,ds=d_{k}^{2},\quad\forall\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*},

(4.7)

where

d_{k}^{1}:=\frac{\langle\xi_{0},c_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}}{c_{0}\langle\mu_{1},c_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}}\quad\text{with}\quad\,k\in{\mathbb{N}},\quad\quad\quad d_{k}^{2}:=\frac{\langle\xi_{0},s_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}}{c_{0}\langle\mu_{2},s_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}}\quad\text{with}\quad\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*},

and

\tilde{v}_{1}(s)=e^{p\kappa s}v_{1}(s),\qquad\tilde{v}_{2}(s)=e^{p\kappa s}v_{2}(s).

Note that
d_{k}^{1}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
d_{k}^{2}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
, are well-defined thanks to Assumption II. We treat (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) as two separate moment problems. Let us start by solving (
4.6
). We seek for a function
\tilde{v}_{1}
such that

\tilde{v}_{1}^{\prime}(t)=r_{1}\left(\frac{T}{2}-t\right)e^{-(\frac{T}{2}-t)},\qquad r_{1}\in L^{2}\left(-\frac{T}{2},\frac{T}{2}\right).

Therefore, it should hold

\tilde{v}_{1}(t)-\tilde{v}_{1}(0)=\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{v}_{1}^{\prime}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{t}r_{1}\left(\frac{T}{2}-s\right)e^{-(\frac{T}{2}-s)}ds.

We require that
\tilde{v}_{1}(0)=\tilde{v}_{1}(T)=0
, which implies that
r_{1}
must satisfy

\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{T}{2}}r_{1}(s)e^{-s}ds=0.

(4.8)

Integrating by parts (
4.6
) and taking into account that
\tilde{v}_{1}(0)=\tilde{v}_{1}(T)=0
, we get for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}

\begin{split}d^{1}_{k}&=\int_{0}^{T}e^{\lambda_{k}s}\tilde{v}_{1}(s)ds=\frac{1}{\lambda}_{k}\left(\left.e^{\lambda_{k}t}\tilde{v}_{1}(t)\right|^{T}_{0}-\int_{0}^{T}e^{-(\frac{T}{2}-t)+\lambda_{k}t}r_{1}\left(\frac{T}{2}-t\right)dt\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{T}{2}}e^{-s+\lambda_{k}(\frac{T}{2}-s)}r_{1}(s)ds.\end{split}

The above identities can be rewritten as follows

\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{T}{2}}e^{-(1+\lambda_{k})s}r_{1}(s)ds=\lambda_{k}d^{1}_{k}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}.

For
k=0
we have that

\begin{split}d^{1}_{0}&=\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{v}_{1}(s)ds=\left.s\tilde{v}_{1}(s)\right|^{T}_{0}-\int_{0}^{T}sr_{1}\left(\frac{T}{2}-s\right)e^{-(\frac{T}{2}-s)}ds=\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{T}{2}}\left(\frac{T}{2}-s\right)r_{1}(s)e^{-s}ds\\
&=-\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{T}{2}}se^{-s}r_{1}(s)ds,\end{split}

where we have used (
4.8
). Define the family

\omega_{k}:=1+\lambda_{k},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*},

and the sequence
\tilde{d}^{1}_{kj}

\tilde{d}^{1}_{01}=-d_{0},\qquad\tilde{d}^{1}_{k1}=0,\qquad k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*},

\tilde{d}^{1}_{00}=0,\qquad\tilde{d}^{1}_{k0}=\lambda_{k}d^{1}_{k}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}.

Thus, we look for
r_{1}\in L^{2}\left(-\frac{T}{2},\frac{T}{2}\right)
such that

\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{T}{2}}s^{j}e^{-\omega_{k}s}r_{1}(s)ds=\tilde{d}^{1}_{kj},\qquad j=0,1,\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(4.9)

As proved in
[
12
, Theorem 1.5]
, there exists
T_{0}>0
such that for every
0<T<T_{0}
there exists a family

\{\sigma_{k,j}\}_{k,j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset L^{2}\left(-\frac{T}{2},\frac{T}{2}\right),

which is biorthogonal to

e_{kj}(s)=s^{j}e^{-\omega_{k}s},\qquad k,j\in{\mathbb{N}},

and moreover

\left\lVert\sigma_{k,j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(-T/2,T/2)}\leq Ce^{C\sqrt{\omega_{k}}+\frac{C}{T}},\qquad j=0,1,\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}.

Therefore, by defining

r_{1}(s):=\tilde{d}^{1}_{01}\sigma_{01}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\tilde{d}^{1}_{k0}\sigma_{k0}(s),

we deduce that
r_{1}
solves the moment problem (
4.9
). Let us finally show that
r_{1}\in L^{2}\left(-\frac{T}{2},\frac{T}{2}\right)
:

\begin{split}\left\lVert r_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(-T/2,T/2)}&\leq|\tilde{d}^{1}_{01}|\left\lVert\sigma_{01}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(-T/2,T/2)}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\tilde{d}^{1}_{k0}|\left\lVert\sigma_{k0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(-T/2,T/2)}\\
&\leq Ce^{C/T}|\tilde{d}^{1}_{01}|+C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}|d^{1}_{k}|e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}+C\sqrt{1+\lambda_{k}}+\frac{C}{T}}\\
&\leq Ce^{C/T}\left(\frac{|\langle\xi_{0},c_{0}\rangle_{L^{2}}|}{c_{0}|\langle\mu_{1},c_{0}\rangle_{L^{2}}|}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{2}e^{-T\lambda_{k}+C\sqrt{\lambda_{k}+1}}}{c_{0}^{2}|\langle\mu_{1},c_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}|^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\left\lVert\xi_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq Ce^{C/T}\left(1+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-T\lambda_{k}+C\sqrt{\lambda_{k}+1}}\right)^{1/2}\right)\left\lVert\xi_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}},\end{split}

where
q_{1}
is the parameter introduced in Assumption II. Let us analyse the behaviour with respect to
T
of the following series:

S(T):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-T\lambda_{k}+C\sqrt{\lambda_{k}+1}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\lambda^{2(q_{1}+1)}_{k}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}}\right)e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}+C\sqrt{\lambda_{k}+1}}.

(4.10)

For any
\lambda\geq 0
, we introduce the function
f(\lambda):=e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda+C\sqrt{\lambda+1}}
. Its derivative is given by

f^{\prime}(\lambda)=e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda+C\sqrt{\lambda}}\left(-\frac{T}{2}+\frac{C}{2\sqrt{\lambda+1}}\right),

(4.11)

and its maximum is attended at
\lambda=\left(\frac{C}{T}\right)^{2}-1
. Hence, for every

we have

S(T)\leq e^{\frac{T}{2}+\frac{C}{T}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}}\right)\leq e^{\frac{C}{T}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}}\right).

(4.12)

Now, for any
\lambda\geq 0
, we consider the function
g(\lambda):=\lambda^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda}
. From its derivative

g^{\prime}(\lambda)=\lambda^{2q_{1}+1}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda}\left(2(q_{1}+1)-\frac{T}{2}\lambda\right),

we deduce that

g(\lambda)\text{ is }\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{increasing }&\text{if }0\leq\lambda<\frac{4(q_{1}+1)}{T},\\
\\
\text{decreasing }&\text{if }\lambda\geq\frac{4(q_{1}+1)}{T},\end{array}\right.

(4.13)

and
g
has a maximum for
\lambda=\frac{4(q_{1}+1)}{T}
. We define the index

k_{1}:=k_{1}(T)=\sup\left\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}\,:\,\lambda_{k}\leq\frac{4(q_{1}+1)}{T}\right\}.

(4.14)

We can rewrite the sum in (
4.12
) as

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}}=\sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{1}-1}\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}}+\sum_{k_{1}\leq k\leq k_{1}+1}\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}}+\sum_{k\geq k_{1}+2}\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}}.

(4.15)

For any
1\leq k\leq k_{1}-1
, we have

\int_{\lambda_{k}}^{\lambda_{k+1}}\lambda^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda}d\lambda\geq(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k})\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}}\geq\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}},

(4.16)

and for any
k\geq k_{1}+2
, it holds that

\int_{\lambda_{k-1}}^{\lambda_{k}}\lambda^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda}d\lambda\geq(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k-1})\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}}\geq\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}}.

(4.17)

Therefore, we obtain that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}}\leq 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda}d\lambda+\sum_{k_{1}\leq k\leq k_{1}+1}\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}}.

(4.18)

Recalling that
g
has a maximum at
\lambda=\frac{4(q_{1}+1)}{T}
, we have that

\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}}\leq\left(\frac{4(q_{1}+1)}{T}\right)^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-2(q_{1}+1)},\quad\text{for }k=k_{1},k_{1}+1.

(4.19)

Moreover, we can rewrite the integral term in (
4.18
) as

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda}d\lambda&=\frac{2}{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2s}{T}\right)^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-s}ds\\
&=\left(\frac{2}{T}\right)^{2q_{1}+3}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-s}ds=\Gamma(2q_{1}+3)\left(\frac{2}{T}\right)^{2q_{1}+3},\end{split}

(4.20)

where by
\Gamma(\cdot)
we indicate the Euler integral of the second kind.
Therefore, thanks to (
4.19
) and (
4.20
) we conclude that there exist two positive constants
C^{1}_{q_{1}},C^{2}_{q_{1}}
such that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2(q_{1}+1)}e^{-\frac{T}{2}\lambda_{k}}\leq\frac{C^{1}_{q_{1}}}{T^{2q_{1}+2}}+\frac{C^{2}_{q_{1}}}{T^{2q_{1}+3}}.

(4.21)

From the above estimate we can prove that there exist positive constants
C
for which

\left\lVert r_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(-T/2,T/2)}\leq Ce^{C/T}\left\lVert\xi_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}},\quad\forall\,0<T<\min\{T_{0},1\},

(4.22)

and thus

\left\lVert(\tilde{v}_{1})^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq e^{T/2}\left\lVert r_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(-T/2,T/2)}\leq Ce^{C/T}\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{0},\quad\forall\,0<T<\min\{T_{0},1\}.

(4.23)

From the Poincaré inequality, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
\tilde{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(0,T)
it holds that
\left\lVert\tilde{v}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(0,T)}\leq C\left\lVert(\tilde{v}_{1})^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T)}
, and we deduce that

\left\lVert\tilde{v}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(0,T)}\leq C\left\lVert(\tilde{v}_{1})^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq Ce^{C/T}\left\lVert\xi_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}},\quad\forall\,0<T<\min\{T_{0},1\}.

(4.24)

Finally, recalling that
v_{1}(t)=e^{-p\kappa t}\tilde{v}_{1}(t)
, we conclude that

\begin{split}\left\lVert v_{1}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(0,T)}&=\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2p\kappa t}|\tilde{v}_{1}(t)|^{2}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\left(e^{-2pkt}|\tilde{v}_{1}(t)|\right)^{\prime}\right)^{2}dt\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\tilde{v}_{1}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(0,T)}\leq Ce^{C/T}\left\lVert\xi_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

for all
0<T\leq\min\{T_{0},1\}
.

The same computations are also valid for the second component of the control
v_{2}
and the proof is then completed.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

The choice of Assumption II in Proposition
4.6
is suitable to deduce the controllability result stated in Theorem
4.3
, and to show explicit examples of functions
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
as in Example

. However, the null controllability of (

) might be studied with less strict hypotheses than Assumption II.

•

The result may be ensured when

verify

\begin{split}\langle\mu_{1},c_{0}\rangle_{L^{2}}\neq 0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \langle\mu_{2},&c_{0}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0,\\
\exists\,a_{1},b_{1}>0,0<q_{1}\leq 1\,:\>e^{b_{1}\lambda_{k}^{q_{1}}}\left|\langle\mu_{1},c_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|\geq a_{1},\,\,&\text{ and }\,\,\langle\mu_{1},s_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0,\quad\forall\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*},\\
\exists\,a_{2},b_{2}>0,0<q_{2}\leq 1\,:\>e^{b_{2}\lambda_{k}^{q_{2}}}\left|\langle\mu_{2},s_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|\geq a_{2},\,\,&\text{ and }\,\,\langle\mu_{2},c_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0,\quad\forall\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}.\end{split}

It could be possible to adapt to our problem the theory developed in
[
38
]
in the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions (see also
[
9
, Section 6.1]
). Then, the strategy leading to Proposition
4.6
might ensure the null-controllability of (
4.4
) for every
T>T^{*}
where

is a minimal time which we expect to depend on
\max\{b_{1},b_{2}\}
.
Note that in
[
38
, Theorem 1.2]
the upper bound of the control cost holds only for

. The relevant case for our purposes is however
T^{*}=0
, which seems to need further investigations.

•

Another approach to establish null controllability of (
4.4
) may be the following. For any
f:{\mathbb{T}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, we introduce the decomposition
f=f^{s}+f^{a}
where

f^{s}(\cdot)=\frac{f(\cdot)+f(2\pi-\cdot)}{2},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ f^{a}(\cdot)=\frac{f(\cdot)-f(2\pi-\cdot)}{2}.

Now,
f^{s}
is symmetric and
f^{a}
is antisymmetric with respect to the point
x=\pi
. We apply such decomposition to (
4.4
) to obtain the following system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}\xi^{s}\\
\xi^{a}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\partial_{x}^{2}+\kappa p&0\\
0&-\partial_{x}^{2}+\kappa p\\
\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\xi^{s}\\
\xi^{a}\\
\end{pmatrix}+\Phi\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}^{s}&\mu_{2}^{s}\\
\mu_{1}^{a}&\mu_{2}^{a}\\
\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(4.25)

Problem (
4.25
) is now defined in

, where

and
L^{2}_{a}({\mathbb{T}})
are the spaces of symmetric and antisymmetric

and

and we note that, for any

, it holds

\displaystyle\xi^{s}|_{[0,\pi]}\in\{\psi\in H^{2}(0,\pi)\ :\ \partial_{x}\psi(0)=\partial_{x}\psi(\pi)=0\},\ \ \ \ \ \xi^{a}|_{[0,\pi]}\in H^{2}\cap H^{1}_{0}(0,\pi).

Thus, (

) is equivalent to the following problem in

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}Y(t,x)=A\cdot Y+\kappa\,p\,Y+\Phi\,B(x)\cdot V(t),&(t,x)\in(s_{0},s_{1})\times(0,\pi),\\
Y(s_{0})=Y_{0},\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle Y=\begin{pmatrix}y^{1}\\
y^{2}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad A=\begin{pmatrix}-\partial_{x}^{2}&0\\
0&-\partial_{x}^{2}\\
\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ \ B=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}^{2}&\mu_{2}^{2}\\
\mu_{1}^{1}&\mu_{2}^{1}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad V=\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad Y_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}y^{1}_{0}\\
y^{2}_{0}\\
\end{pmatrix},

where

(4.28)

The null controllability property of (

). Observe that Assumption II implies that

This kind of problem has been studied in the literature, mostly in the case of an operator

that does not depend on the space variable and/or the control

Proof.

Existence and uniqueness of solutions.

for every

\Phi

C([0,T],H^{1}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}}))

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\Phi(\xi)(t)-\Phi(\tilde{\xi})\right\rVert_{H^{1}}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\left(\langle u(s),Q(x)\rangle(\xi(s)-\tilde{\xi}(s))-\kappa(p+1)\psi(s)^{p}(\xi(s)-\tilde{\xi}(s))\right)ds\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\\

\begin{split}&\frac{\psi(t+\tau)-\psi(t)}{\tau}-\Psi(t)=\frac{1}{\tau}\left\{e^{(t+\tau)\Delta}\psi_{0}-e^{t\Delta}\psi_{0}\right\}+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{(t+\tau-s)\Delta}\left[-\kappa\,\psi(s)^{p+1}+\langle u(s),Q\rangle\psi(s)\right]ds\\

\end{split}

(B.2)

Now we multiply equation (

We now perform integrations by parts, and we get

\langle\partial_{t}(\partial_{x}w(t)),\partial_{x}w(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\partial^{2}_{x}w(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}-\kappa(p+1)\Phi^{p}\langle\partial_{x}w(t),\partial_{x}w(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
+\kappa\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{p+1}\binom{p+1}{j}\Phi^{p+1-j}j\langle y^{j-1}(t)\partial_{x}y(t),\partial_{x}w(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\langle\langle v^{1}(t),Q\rangle y(t),\partial^{2}_{x}w(t)\rangle_{L^{2}},

and therefore

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\partial_{x}w(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\partial^{2}_{x}w(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\kappa(p+1)\Phi^{p}\left\lVert\partial_{x}w(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
+\kappa(p+1)\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{p+1}\binom{p+1}{j}\Phi^{p+1-j}\left(\frac{\left\lVert y(t)\right\rVert^{2j}_{H^{1}}}{2}+\frac{\left\lVert\partial_{x}w(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{2}\right)+C_{Q}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{q+2}|v^{1,j}(t)|^{2}\left\lVert y(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{\left\lVert\partial^{2}_{x}w(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}{2},

where we have used that
\partial_{t}w=\partial_{t}y-\partial_{t}\xi\in L^{2}((s_{0},s_{1}),H^{1}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathbb{R}}))
(see Proposition
B.1
for
y
and Remark
4.5
for
\xi
). We apply the Grönwall’s Lemma, and we obtain that, for any
t\in[s_{0},s_{1}]
,

\left\lVert\partial_{x}w(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left(\int_{s_{0}}^{t}\kappa(p+1)\sum_{j=2}^{p+1}\binom{p+1}{j}\Phi^{p+1-j}\left\lVert y(s)\right\rVert^{2j}_{H^{1}}+C^{2}_{Q}\sum_{j=1}^{q+2}|v^{1,j}(s)|^{2}\left\lVert y(s)\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}ds\right)e^{A_{3}(s_{0},t)}

with

A_{3}(s_{0},t):=\int_{s_{0}}^{t}\Big(2\kappa(p+1)\Phi^{p}+\kappa(p+1)\sum_{j=2}^{p+1}\binom{p+1}{j}\Phi^{p+1-j}\Big)ds.

Taking the supremum over the interval
[s_{0},s_{1}]
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[s_{0},s_{1}]}\left\lVert\partial_{x}w(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\left(\int_{s_{0}}^{s_{1}}\kappa(p+1)\sum_{j=2}^{p+1}\binom{p+1}{j}\Phi^{p+1-j}\left\lVert y(s)\right\rVert^{2j}_{H^{1}}+C^{2}_{Q}\sum_{j=1}^{q+2}|v^{1,j}(s)|^{2}\left\lVert y(s)\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}ds\right)e^{A_{3}(s_{0},s_{1})}\\
&\quad\leq\left(\kappa\sigma(p+1)\sum_{j=2}^{p+1}\binom{p+1}{j}\Phi^{p+1-j}\sup_{t\in[s_{0},s_{1}]}\left\lVert y(t)\right\rVert^{2j}_{H^{1}}+C^{2}_{Q}\left\lVert v^{1}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(s_{0},s_{1})}\sup_{t\in[s_{0},s_{1}]}\left\lVert y(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)e^{A_{3}(s_{0},s_{1})}\\
&\quad\leq\left(2\kappa\sigma(p+1)^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{p+1}\binom{p+1}{j}\Phi^{p+1-j}\left(\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert^{2j}_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert v^{1}\right\rVert^{2j}_{L^{2}(s_{0},s_{1})}\right)+C^{2}_{Q}\left\lVert v^{1}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(s_{0},s_{1})}\left(\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert v^{1}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(s_{0},s_{1})}\right)\right)e^{A_{3}(s_{0},s_{1})}.\end{split}

Thanks to the assumption on
y_{0}
we deduce

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[s_{0},s_{1}]}&\left\lVert\partial_{x}w(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\left(2\kappa\sigma(p+1)^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{p+1}\binom{p+1}{j}\Phi^{p+1-j}\left(1+N^{2j}(\sigma)\right)\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert^{2j}_{H^{1}}+C_{Q}^{2}N^{2}(\sigma)\left(1+N^{2}(\sigma)\right)\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert^{4}_{H^{1}}\right)e^{A_{3}(s_{0},s_{1})}\\
&=\left(2\kappa\sigma(p+1)^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{p+1}\binom{p+1}{j}\Phi^{p+1-j}\left(1+N^{2j}(\sigma)\right)\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert^{2(j-2)}_{H^{1}}+C^{2}_{Q}N^{2}(\sigma)\left(1+N^{2}(\sigma)\right)\right)e^{A_{3}(s_{0},s_{1})}\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert^{4}_{H^{1}}\\
&=A_{3}(\sigma,\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}})^{2}\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert^{4}_{H^{1}},\end{split}

(C.6)

where

A_{3}(\sigma,\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}):=\\
\left(2\kappa\sigma(p+1)^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{p+1}\binom{p+1}{j}\Phi^{p+1-j}\left(1+N^{2j}(\sigma)\right)\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert^{2(j-2)}_{H^{1}}+C^{2}_{Q}N^{2}(\sigma)\left(1+N^{2}(\sigma)\right)\right)^{1/2}e^{A_{3}(s_{0},s_{1})/2}.

Finally, from (
C.5
) and (
C.6
), we conclude that

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[s_{0},s_{1}]}\left\lVert w(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}&\leq 2\left(\sup_{t\in[s_{0},s_{1}]}\left\lVert w(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\sup_{t\in[s_{0},s_{1}]}\left\lVert\partial_{x}w(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq 2\left(A_{2}(\sigma,\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}})^{2}+A_{3}(\sigma,\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}})^{2}\right)\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert^{4}_{H^{1}}\leq A_{4}^{2}(\sigma,\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}})^{2}\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert^{4}_{H^{1}},\end{split}

with

A_{4}(\sigma,\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}):=\sqrt{2}\left(2\kappa\sigma(p+1)^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{p+1}\binom{p+1}{j}\Phi^{p+1-j}\left(1+N(\sigma)^{2j}\right)\left\lVert y_{s_{0}}\right\rVert^{2(j-2)}_{H^{1}}+C^{2}_{Q}N^{2}(\sigma)\left(1+N(\sigma)^{2}\right)\right)^{1/2}\cdot\\
e^{\sigma(2\kappa(p+1)\Phi^{p}+\kappa(p+1)\sum_{j=2}^{p+1}\binom{p+1}{j}\Phi^{p+1-j}+1))/2}.

(C.7)

The proof is therefore concluded.
∎

Yohei Watanabe, Kento Uchida, Ryoki Hamano, Shota Saito, Masahiro Nomura, Shinichi Shirakawa

Abstract
. The covariance matrix adaptation evolution strategy (CMA-ES) is an efficient continuous black-box optimization method. The CMA-ES possesses many attractive features, including invariance properties and a well-tuned default hyperparameter setting. Moreover, several components to specialize the CMA-ES have been proposed, such as noise handling and constraint handling. To utilize these advantages in mixed-integer optimization problems, the CMA-ES with margin has been proposed. The CMA-ES with margin prevents the premature convergence of discrete variables by the margin correction, in which the distribution parameters are modified to leave the generation probability for changing the discrete variable. The margin correction has been applied to ( μ / μ w \mu/\mu_{\mathrm{w}} , λ \lambda )-CMA-ES, while this paper introduces the margin correction into (1+1)-CMA-ES, an elitist version of CMA-ES. The (1+1)-CMA-ES is often advantageous for unimodal functions and can be computationally less expensive. To tackle the performance deterioration on mixed-integer optimization, we use the discretized elitist solution as the mean of the sampling distribution and modify the margin correction not to move the elitist solution. The numerical simulation using benchmark functions on mixed-integer, integer, and binary domains shows that (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin outperforms the CMA-ES with margin and is better than or comparable with several specialized methods to a particular search domain.



1.
Introduction

Backgrounds

The covariance matrix adaptation evolution strategy (CMA-ES)
(
Hansen and
Ostermeier 1996
;
Hansen
et al. 2003
)
is an efficient optimization method in continuous black-box optimizations. The CMA-ES generates candidate solutions using a multivariate Gaussian distribution and performs black-box optimization by iteratively updating the distribution parameters. The CMA-ES possesses several attractive features. The well-tuned default hyperparameter setting
(
Hansen 2016
)
makes the CMA-ES quasi-hyperparameter-free, which does not require the cost of hyperparameter tuning. Owing to the invariance properties, the CMA-ES works well on a wide range of problems, such as non-separable and/or ill-conditioned problems. As another advantage, several components specialized for the CMA-ES have been proposed, such as noise handling
(
Hansen et al. 2009
;
Hellwig and Beyer 2020
)
, constraint handling
(
Hansen et al. 2009
;
Sakamoto and
Akimoto 2022
;
Arnold and Hansen 2012
)
, and multi-objective CMA-ES
(
Igel
et al. 2007
)
.

In the field of black-box optimization, there are several kinds of domains of the design variables, such as continuous, integer, and binary domains. The mixed-integer optimization problems contain both continuous and discrete variables.
Many studies have presented advanced black-box optimization methods
(
Rios and
Sahinidis 2013
;
Larson
et al. 2019
)
. However, most of them focus on only a part of the kinds of domains, especially the continuous and binary domains. In particular, the integer and mixed-integer optimization methods have not been investigated actively, even though there are a lot of real-world applications in these domains
(
Piermarini and
Roma 2021
;
Zhang
et al. 2020
;
Fujii
et al. 2018
;
Hazan
et al. 2018
)
.

The CMA-ES with margin

(
Hamano
et al. 2022
)
is an efficient mixed-integer optimization method that can inherit the advantages of the CMA-ES. To prevent the premature convergence of discrete variables, the CMA-ES with margin introduces a lower bound on the marginal probability, referred to as
the margin
, so that the samples are not fixed to a single discrete value. The CMA-ES with margin applies an affine transformation, called
the margin correction
, to ensure the margin.
The excellent performance of the CMA-ES with margin on the mixed-integer domain is confirmed in
(
Hamano
et al. 2022
)
. In principle, the CMA-ES with margin can be applied to the integer and binary optimization problems by setting the number of continuous variables to zero, where the performance in those cases has yet to be investigated.

Contributions

This paper introduces the margin correction of the CMA-ES with margin into the (1+1)-CMA-ES
(
Igel
et al. 2006
;
Suttorp
et al. 2009
)
, an elitist version of CMA-ES, and proposes
the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin
. The (1+1)-CMA-ES shows powerful optimization performance on unimodal functions in the continuous domain.

We introduce a revised update rule of the mean vector that prevents the premature convergence on mixed-integer problems and modify the margin correction not to move the mean vector.
We also propose a post-process that modifies the updated distribution parameters so that the behavior is not affected by the numerical errors in binary and integer optimizations.

The numerical simulation using benchmark functions on mixed-integer, integer, and binary domains shows that the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin outperforms the CMA-ES with margin. Moreover, compared to optimization methods designed solely for binary optimization, we found that the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin outperforms the compact genetic algorithm
(
Harik
et al. 1999
)
and population-based incremental learning
(
Baluja 1994
)
, and is comparable with (1+1)-EA. The experimental results show the potential of CMA-ES with margin as a universal optimizer for various variable-type problems.
This study provides new possibilities for developing discrete and mixed-integer optimization methods derived from the CMA-ES.

The CMA-ES with margin

Then, the distribution parameters are updated based on the ranking of candidate solutions. Introducing the weights
w_{1},\cdots,w_{\lambda}
satisfying
w_{1}\geq\cdots\geq w_{\mu}>0\geq w_{\mu+1}\geq\cdots\geq w_{\lambda}

\sum^{\mu}_{i=1}w_{i}=1
for
\mu\leq\lambda

(5)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}=\boldsymbol{m}^{(t)}+c_{m}\sum_{i=1}^{\mu}w_{i}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i:\lambda}-\boldsymbol{m}^{(t)})\kern 5.0pt,

where

In the update rule of the covariance matrix and step-size, two evolution paths
\boldsymbol{p}_{c}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

\boldsymbol{p}_{\sigma}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

\boldsymbol{p}_{c}^{(0)}=\boldsymbol{p}_{\sigma}^{(0)}=\mathbf{0}

(6)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{p}_{\sigma}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=(1-c_{\sigma})\boldsymbol{p}_{\sigma}^{(t)}+\sqrt{c_{\sigma}(2-c_{\sigma})\mu_{\mathrm{w}}}\sum_{i=1}^{\mu}w_{i}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i:\lambda}

(7)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{p}_{c}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=(1-c_{c})\boldsymbol{p}_{c}^{(t)}+h_{\sigma}^{(t+1)}\sqrt{c_{c}(2-c_{c})\mu_{\mathrm{w}}}\sum_{i=1}^{\mu}w_{i}\boldsymbol{y}_{i:\lambda}\kern 5.0pt,

2.2.
Margin Correction

After the update of the distribution parameters
\boldsymbol{m}^{(t)}
,
\boldsymbol{C}^{(t)}
and
\sigma^{(t)}
, the CMA-ES with margin modifies the updated mean vector
\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}
and the diagonal elements of
\boldsymbol{A}^{(t)}
corresponding to the integer (or binary) variables.
This modification maintains the probability of not generating the integer
[\bar{\boldsymbol{m}}^{(t+1)}]_{j}:=[\textsc{Enc}(\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)})]_{j}
nearest to the
j
-th element of mean vector larger than the margin parameter
\alpha
, i.e.,
\Pr([\bar{\boldsymbol{v}}]_{j}\neq[\bar{\boldsymbol{m}}^{(t+1)}]_{j})\geq\alpha
. The modification process is determined by whether
[\bar{\boldsymbol{m}}^{(t+1)}]_{j}
equals to the smallest integer
z_{j,1}
or largest integer
z_{j,K_{j}}
, or others.

Case of Smallest or Largest Integer

When
[\bar{\boldsymbol{m}}^{(t+1)}]_{j}
equals to
z_{j,1}
or
z_{j,K_{j}}
, the CMA-ES with margin modifies the mean vector as follows.
Let us define
\mathrm{CI}^{(t+1)}_{j,\gamma}
with
\gamma\in(0,1)
as

(11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{CI}^{(t+1)}_{j,\gamma}:=\sqrt{\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{ppf}}(\gamma)(\sigma^{(t+1)})^{2}\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{(t)}\boldsymbol{C}^{(t+1)}\boldsymbol{A}^{(t)}\rangle_{j}}\kern 5.0pt,

where
\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{ppf}}(\gamma)
is
\gamma
-quantile of
\chi^{2}
-distribution with
1
degree of freedom.
Then, the confidence interval of the probability
1-2\alpha
for the
j
-th element of sample
[\boldsymbol{v}]_{j}
becomes

(12)

\displaystyle\left[[\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}-\mathrm{CI}^{(t+1)}_{j,1-2\alpha},[\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}+\mathrm{CI}^{(t+1)}_{j,1-2\alpha}\right]\kern 5.0pt.

The CMA-ES with margin modifies the elements of the mean vector so that the midpoints exist in the confidence intervals.
Consequently, the modification of the
j
-th element of the mean vector reads

(13)

[\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}\leftarrow\ell\left([\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}\right)+\mathrm{sign}\left([\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}-\ell\left([\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}\right)\right)\\
\cdot\min\left\{\left|[\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}-\ell\left([\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}\right)\right|,\mathrm{CI}^{(t+1)}_{j,1-2\alpha}\right\}\kern 5.0pt,

where
\ell([\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j})\in\{\ell_{1|2},\ell_{K_{j}-1|K_{j}}\}
is the nearest midpoint to
j
-th element of the mean vector before the modification.
In this case, the
j
-th diagonal element of
\boldsymbol{A}^{(t)}
is not changed, i.e.,
\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{(t+1)}\rangle_{j}=\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{(t)}\rangle_{j}
.
We note that the elements of the mean vector corresponding to the binary variables are modified by the above-mentioned modification process.

Case of Other Integers

When
[\bar{\boldsymbol{m}}^{(t+1)}]_{j}
is in
\{z_{j,2},\cdots,z_{j,K_{j}-1}\}
, the CMA-ES with margin modifies
[\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}
and
\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{(t)}\rangle_{j}
as follows. Let us denote the nearest two midpoints to
[\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}
as

(14)

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathrm{low},j}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle:=\max\left\{l\in\left\{\ell_{j,k|k+1}\right\}_{k=1,\cdots,K_{j}-1}:l<[\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}\right\}\kern 5.0pt

(15)

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathrm{up},j}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle:=\min\left\{l\in\left\{\ell_{j,k|k+1}\right\}_{k=1,\cdots,K_{j}-1}:[\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}\leq l\right\}\kern 5.0pt.

The modification aims that both of

(16)

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{low}}

\displaystyle:=\Pr\left([\boldsymbol{v}]_{j}\leq\ell_{\mathrm{low},j}^{(t+1)}\right)\qquad\text{and}

(17)

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{up}}

\displaystyle:=\Pr\left(\ell_{\mathrm{up},j}^{(t+1)}<[\boldsymbol{v}]_{j}\right)

are maintained above
\alpha/2
after the margin correction.

As the first step of the margin correction, with
p_{\mathrm{mid}}:=1-p_{\mathrm{low}}-p_{\mathrm{up}}
, the corrected marginal probabilities are calculated as

(18)

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{low}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\max\{\alpha/2,p_{\mathrm{low}}\}

(19)

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\max\{\alpha/2,p_{\mathrm{up}}\}

(20)

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{low}}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=p_{\mathrm{low}}^{\prime}+\frac{1-p_{\mathrm{low}}^{\prime}-p_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime}-p_{\mathrm{mid}}}{p_{\mathrm{low}}^{\prime}+p_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime}+p_{\mathrm{mid}}-3\cdot\alpha/2}\left(p_{\mathrm{low}}^{\prime}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)

(21)

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=p_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime}+\frac{1-p_{\mathrm{low}}^{\prime}-p_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime}-p_{\mathrm{mid}}}{p_{\mathrm{low}}^{\prime}+p_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime}+p_{\mathrm{mid}}-3\cdot\alpha/2}\left(p_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\kern 5.0pt.

This maintains
p_{\mathrm{low}}^{\prime\prime}\geq\alpha/2
and
p_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime\prime}\geq\alpha/2
. Then
[\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}
and
\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{(t)}\rangle_{j}
are modified to satisfy

(22)

\displaystyle\Pr\left([\boldsymbol{v}]_{j}\leq\ell_{\mathrm{low},j}^{(t+1)}\right)

\displaystyle=p_{\mathrm{low}}^{\prime\prime}\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}\qquad\text{and}

(23)

\displaystyle\Pr\left(\ell_{\mathrm{up},j}^{(t+1)}<[\boldsymbol{v}]_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=p_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime\prime}\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}

after the margin correction. To achieve this,
[\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}
and
\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{(t)}\rangle_{j}
are modified as

(24)

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{low},j}^{(t+1)}\sqrt{q_{\mathrm{up}}}+\ell_{\mathrm{up},j}^{(t+1)}\sqrt{q_{\mathrm{low}}}}{\sqrt{q_{\mathrm{up}}}+\sqrt{q_{\mathrm{low}}}}

(25)

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{(t+1)}\rangle_{j}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{up},j}^{(t+1)}-\ell_{\mathrm{low},j}^{(t+1)}}{\sigma^{(t+1)}\sqrt{\langle\boldsymbol{C}^{(t+1)}\rangle_{j}}\left(\sqrt{q_{\mathrm{up}}}+\sqrt{q_{\mathrm{low}}}\right)}\kern 5.0pt,

where
q_{\mathrm{low}}=\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{ppf}}(1-2p_{\mathrm{low}}^{\prime\prime})
and
q_{\mathrm{up}}=\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{ppf}}(1-2p_{\mathrm{up}}^{\prime\prime})
.

2.3.
Default Hyperparameter Setting

The CMA-ES with margin shares the default hyperparameter setting with the CMA-ES proposed in
(
Hansen 2016
)
except for the margin parameter
\alpha
. The default setting of the margin parameter
\alpha=(\lambda N)^{-1}
is tuned by numerical simulations.
This default hyperparameter setting makes the CMA-ES with margin a quasi-hyperparameter-free optimization method that does not require hyperparameter tuning.

3.
(1+1)-CMA-ES with Margin

We propose a variant of CMA-ES with margin combined with the elitist strategy, termed
(1+1)-CMA-ES with margin
. The proposed method is obtained by introducing the margin correction in Section
2.2
to the (1+1)-CMA-ES
(
Suttorp
et al. 2009
)
. Algorithm
2
shows the optimization process of the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin.

3.1.
Overall Procedure

First, the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin generates a sample as

(26)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathrm{new}}

\displaystyle=(\boldsymbol{C}^{(t)})^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\xi}

(27)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{v}_{\mathrm{new}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{m}^{(t)}+\sigma^{(t)}\boldsymbol{A}^{(t)}\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathrm{new}}\kern 5.0pt,

where
\boldsymbol{\xi}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\,\mathrm{I}_{N})
.
Unlike in the CMA-ES with margin, the sample in (
2
), whose law is given by
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{m}^{(t)},(\sigma^{(t)})^{2}\boldsymbol{C}^{(t)})
, is not computed because the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin updates the mean vector using
\boldsymbol{v}_{\mathrm{new}}
(after discretization).

Then, the candidate solution
\bar{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\mathrm{new}}=\textsc{Enc}(\boldsymbol{v}_{\mathrm{new}})
is evaluated on the objective function
f
to be minimized, and the smoothed success rate
p_{\mathrm{succ}}^{(t)}\in[0,1]
is updated as

(28)

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{succ}}^{(t+1)}=(1-c_{p})p_{\mathrm{succ}}^{(t)}+c_{p}\mathbb{I}\{f(\bar{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\mathrm{new}})\leq f(\boldsymbol{m}^{(t)})\}\kern 5.0pt,

where
c_{p}>0
is the smoothing factor. The initial value of the smoothed success rate
p_{\mathrm{succ}}^{(0)}
is given by the target success rate
p_{\mathrm{target}}\in[0,1]
. Based on the
1/5
-success rule
(
Rechenberg 1973
)
, the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin updates the step-size as

(29)

\displaystyle\sigma^{(t+1)}=\sigma^{(t)}\exp\left(\frac{1}{d_{\sigma}}\cdot\frac{p_{\mathrm{succ}}^{(t+1)}-p_{\mathrm{target}}}{1-p_{\mathrm{target}}}\right)\kern 5.0pt.

The (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin updates the evolution path
\boldsymbol{p}_{c}^{(t)}
, covariance matrix
\boldsymbol{C}^{(t)}
, and mean vector
\boldsymbol{m}^{(t)}
when the evaluation value
f(\bar{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\mathrm{new}})
of new candidate solution is not inferior to the best evaluation value
f(\boldsymbol{m}^{(t)})
so far.
The update rules of
\boldsymbol{p}_{c}^{(t)}
and
\boldsymbol{C}^{(t)}
are given by

(30)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{p}_{c}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=(1-c_{c})\boldsymbol{p}_{c}^{(t)}+h^{(t+1)}\sqrt{c_{c}(2-c_{c})}\,\boldsymbol{y}_{\mathrm{new}}

(31)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{C}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\left(1-c_{1}+\delta(h^{(t+1)})\right)\boldsymbol{C}^{(t)}+c_{1}\boldsymbol{p}_{c}^{(t+1)}\left(\boldsymbol{p}_{c}^{(t+1)}\right)^{\mathrm{T}}\kern 5.0pt,

where
h^{(t+1)}=\mathbb{I}{\{p_{\mathrm{succ}}^{(t+1)}<p_{\mathrm{thresh}}\}}
and
\delta(h)=(1-h)c_{1}c_{c}(2-c_{c})
.
The value
h^{(t+1)}
stalls the update of the evolution path when the smoothed success rate is larger than a threshold
p_{\mathrm{thresh}}
. This prevents a fast increase of axes of the covariance matrix when the step-size is too small.
Note that, introducing the update based on Cholesky decomposition of the covariance matrix
(
Suttorp
et al. 2009
)
, the computational cost required in a single update, including the margin correction explained later, can be reduced to
O(N^{2})
.

As the candidate solution used in the update of the mean vector, we use the discretized elitist solution
\bar{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\mathrm{new}}
, i.e., we update the mean vector as
\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}=\bar{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\mathrm{new}}

3.3.
Margin Correction with Discretized Mean Vector

Since the original margin correction in Section
2.2
moves the updated mean vector, it reduces the effect of the usage of the encoded mean vector. Therefore, we refine the margin correction not to move the mean vector. Similar to the original margin correction, the modified margin correction applies different update rules of
\boldsymbol{A}^{(t)}
, which is determined by whether the element of the mean vector is the smallest integer or largest integer, or others.

Case of Smallest or Largest Integer

Instead of the mean vector, we update the matrix
\boldsymbol{A}^{(t)}
to satisfy
\Pr([\bar{\boldsymbol{v}}]_{j}\neq[{\boldsymbol{m}}^{(t+1)}]_{j})\geq\alpha
. This is satisfied when the distance between the mean vector
[\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}
and the nearest midpoint
\ell([\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j})
in
j
-th dimension is smaller than the confidence interval, i.e.,

(32)

\displaystyle\left|[{\boldsymbol{m}}^{(t+1)}]_{j}-\ell([\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j})\right|\leq\mathrm{CI}^{(t+1)}_{j,1-2\alpha}\kern 5.0pt.

To satisfy (
32
), when it is not satisfied,
\boldsymbol{A}^{(t)}
is updated as

(33)

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{(t+1)}\rangle_{j}=\frac{\left|[{\boldsymbol{m}}^{(t+1)}]_{j}-\ell([\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j})\right|}{\sigma^{(t+1)}\sqrt{\langle\boldsymbol{C}^{(t+1)}\rangle_{j}\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{ppf}}(1-2\alpha)}}\kern 5.0pt.

We update
\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{(t)}\rangle_{j}
to
\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{(t+1)}\rangle_{j}
when (
32
) is satisfied before the margin correction.

Case of Other Integers

We assume the integers
z_{j,1},\cdots z_{j,K_{j}}
for integer variable in
j
-th dimension are at even intervals, i.e., it satisfies
z_{j,k}-z_{j,k+1}=z_{j,k^{\prime}}-z_{j,k^{\prime}+1}
for all
k,k^{\prime}\in\{1,\cdots,K_{j}-1\}
.

This assumption can be easily satisfied by introducing a order-preserving bijective mapping from
\mathcal{Z}_{j}
to evenly-spaced integers. We note that, however, such mapping may change the problem characteristics.

Then, with the discretized mean vector,
p_{\mathrm{low}}
and
p_{\mathrm{up}}
in (

3.5.
Post-process for Discrete Optimization

In binary and integer optimizations, the covariance matrix
(\sigma^{(t)})^{2}\boldsymbol{C}^{(t)}
often converges rapidly. The (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin (and the CMA-ES with margin) can deal with such convergence by the increase of
\boldsymbol{A}^{(t)}
. However, due to the numerical error, the behavior of the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin becomes unstable. To address this problem, we introduce a post-process to reduce the numerical error without changing the algorithm of the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin in principle.
We change the step-size and diagonal elements of
\boldsymbol{A}^{(t+1)}
after the margin correction as

(34)

\displaystyle\sigma^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\sigma^{(t+1)}\cdot\min_{k=1,\cdots,N}\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{(t+1)}\rangle_{k}\kern 5.0pt.

(35)

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{(t+1)}\rangle_{j}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\frac{\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{(t+1)}\rangle_{j}}{\min_{k=1,\cdots,N}\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{(t+1)}\rangle_{k}}\quad\text{for }j=1,\cdots,N

This post-process maintains the covariance of
\boldsymbol{v}
in the next iteration and does not change the algorithm but only changes the implementation.

Experimental Setting

We used the following benchmark functions to be minimized, that were used in

•

\textsc{EllipsoidOneMax}(\bar{\boldsymbol{v}})=

\sum^{N_{\mathrm{co}}}_{j=1}\Bigl(1000^{\frac{j-1}{N_{\mathrm{co}}-1}}[\bar{\boldsymbol{v}}]_{j}\Bigr)^{2}+N_{\mathrm{bi}}-\sum^{N}_{j=N_{\mathrm{co}}+1}[\bar{\boldsymbol{v}}]_{j}

•

\textsc{EllipsoidLeadingOnes}(\bar{\boldsymbol{v}})=

4.3.
Experiment on Binary Optimization

Experimental Setting

We used the following benchmark functions to be maximized.

•

\textsc{OneMax}(\bar{\boldsymbol{v}})=\sum^{N}_{j=1}[\bar{\boldsymbol{v}}]_{j}

•

\textsc{LeadingOnes}(\bar{\boldsymbol{v}})=\sum^{N}_{j=1}\left(\prod^{j}_{k=1}[\bar{\boldsymbol{v}}]_{k}\right)

We compared the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin to the binary optimization methods, including the compact genetic algorithm (cGA), population-based incremental learning (PBIL), (1+1)-evolutionary algorithm ((1+1)-EA), in addition to the CMA-ES with margin. We set the sample size of PBIL as the default population size

The mutation rate of (1+1)-EA was set to

(
Folly and

As well as the integer case, the covariance
(\sigma^{(t)})^{2}\boldsymbol{C}^{(t)}
of the CMA-ES with margin and the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin often converges rapidly. For the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin, the post-process in (

In the CMA-ES with margin on the binary domain, since the post-process in (

(36)

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\frac{[\boldsymbol{m}^{(t+1)}]_{j}-\ell_{1|2,j}}{\sigma^{(t+1)}}+\ell_{1|2,j}\quad\text{for }j=1,\cdots,N

\displaystyle\sigma^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle\leftarrow 1\kern 5.0pt.

5.
Conclusion

We proposed the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin, which is derived by introducing the margin correction into (1+1)-CMA-ES. To prevent the premature convergence of continuous variables in mixed-integer optimization problems, we introduced the discretization of the mean vector and modified the margin correction not to move the mean vector. We also applied the post-process for binary and integer optimizations so that the behavior is not affected by the numerical errors. The experimental results on mixed-integer, integer, and binary domains show that the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin outperforms the CMA-ES with margin.
In the result on the binary domain, in particular, the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin achieves the first- or second-best performance among the well-known binary optimization methods.

There are a lot of additional components which specialize the CMA-ES to particular situations, such as constraint handling.
The development of novel discrete and mixed-integer optimization methods by transferring the components for CMA-ES to the (1+1)-CMA-ES with margin is one of our future works.
In addition, considering a few unsuccessful trials on some low-dimensional mixed-integer benchmark functions, the investigation of the relationship between the optimization performance and the hyperparameter setting is necessary to provide a more reliable default hyperparameter setting.

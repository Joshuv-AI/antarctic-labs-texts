Let

Motivations

A) to obtain a priori estimates for the solutions of non-linear PDEs.
B) applications in harmonic analysis and potential theory;

Part A) will be discussed in our forthcoming paper, which will focus on applications of our theory to regularity and qualitative properties of solutions of second-order elliptic PDEs as e.g.

Another one is presented in Section

Let us mention that second-order operators in the non-divergent form play a crucial role in the theory of elliptic PDE’s (see e.g.

). In the probality theory, these operators are an important subclass of generators of Feller semi-groups. For example,

By deriving the identities and inequalities, we intend to contribute to both analysis of PDEs and potential theory, where one deals with elliptic operators in non-divergent form.

This condition is equivalent to the estimate

2.4
Properties of Sobolev functions

The pointwise value of Sobolev function

When

For an in-depth treatment of the trace theory, we refer the reader to e.g.

Poincaré inequality

We will use the following variant of Poincaré inequality (see
[

, Theorem 13.19]
):

\int_{\Omega}|w(x)|\,\textup{d}x\leq C_{P}\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla w(x)\|\,\textup{d}x,\text{ where }w\in W_{0}^{1,1}(\Omega),

which holds for

)
and where

does not depend on

In some cases, we will also need the more general version that assumes only

Remark 2.10

(Equivalence of homogeneous and inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces on regular domains)
.

We recall that it is a consequence of the general Poincaré inequality that, on sufficiently regular bounded domains (e.g. those satisfying

. See e.g.
[

ACL characterisation property

We will need the following variant of Nikodym ACL
Characterisation Theorem, which can be found
e.g. in
[

, Section 1.1.3]
. Let us note that it holds in the Euclidean space but can be applied appropriately inside the regular domain as well.

Theorem 2.11

(Nikodym ACL Characterisation Theorem)
.

a

i)

Let
w\in~W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbf{R}^{n}})
. Then for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and for almost every
a\in\mathbf{R}^{i-1}\times\{0\}\times\mathbf{R}^{n-i}
the function

\mathbf{R}\ni t\mapsto w(a+te_{i})

(2.8)

is locally absolutely continuous on
\mathbf{R}
. In particular, for almost
every point
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
the distributional derivative
\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_{i}}
is
the same as the classical derivative at

.

ii)

Assume that
w\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
and
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and for almost every
a\in\mathbf{R}^{i-1}\times\{0\}\times\mathbf{R}^{n-i}
the function in
(
2.8
)
is
locally absolutely continuous on
\mathbf{R}
and all the derivatives

\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_{i}}
computed almost everywhere
are locally integrable on
\mathbf{R}^{n}
. Then
w
belongs to

W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
.

iii)

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
w\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbf{R}^{n}})
and
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
be an open
subset. Then

belongs to

if and only if

and
every derivative
\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_{i}}
computed almost everywhere belongs to the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
.

The following easy consequence of the ACL characterisation is left to the reader.

Lemma 2.12
.

If
f:[-R,R]\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
is absolutely continuous with values in the interval
[\alpha,\beta]
and

is a Lipschitz function, then
the function

is absolutely continuous on

. In particular, if

is locally Lipschitz and

belongs to

, where

belongs to

.

(the identity and the inequalities)

Remark 3.4

(interpretation of the term

The term

i)

5
Links with the literature in probability and potential theory

In this section, we discuss the links of our results with probability theory and potential theory, particularly with the analysis of generators of analytic semi-groups.

5.1
Integral chain-rule-type upper bound

Let us first focus on the pointwise chain rule for the composition
u\mapsto\widetilde{H}(u)
, under the suitable regularity assumptions.

When we deal with the function
u
of one variable, we have

(\widetilde{H}(u))^{\prime}=\widetilde{H}^{\prime}(u)u^{\prime}=Q(\widetilde{H})u^{\prime},\text{ where }\widetilde{H}\mapsto Q(\widetilde{H}):=\widetilde{H}^{\prime}\text{ is a linear operator}.

(5.1)

For second-order differential operators we have:

\displaystyle(\widetilde{H}(u))^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{H}^{\prime}(u)u^{\prime})^{\prime}=\widetilde{H}^{\prime\prime}(u)(u^{\prime})^{2}+\widetilde{H}^{\prime}(u)u^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\text{for a function of one variable},

\displaystyle P(\widetilde{H}(u))

\displaystyle=\widetilde{H}^{\prime}(u)Pu+\widetilde{H}^{\prime\prime}(u)\|\nabla u\|_{\mathbf{A}}^{2}

\displaystyle\text{in general, where $P$ is as in \hyperref@@ii[pe]{{{(\ignorespaces\ref*{pe}\/)}}}}.

(5.2)

There is no linear operator
T\colon\widetilde{H}\mapsto T(\widetilde{H})
such that the right-hand side above would be of the form
T(\widetilde{H})u^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
or
T(\widetilde{H})Pu
, respectively. Hence, for a second-order elliptic operator, the chain rule like
P\widetilde{H}(u)=T(\widetilde{H})Pu
does not hold in the pointwise sense.

On the other hand, it follows from our statements that, under suitable assumptions (as in either Theorem
4.1
or Theorem
4.4
), the non-linear term
\widetilde{H}^{\prime\prime}(u)\|\nabla u\|_{\mathbf{A}}^{2}
in

(
5.2
)
is integrally dominated by the linear term
\widetilde{H}^{\prime}(u)Pu
, where the linearity applies to
u
. Consequently,
the following integral chain-rule-type upper bound holds:

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|P(\widetilde{H}(u))|\,\textup{d}x\,&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{\hyperref@@ii[chainrule]{\textup{{(\ignorespaces\ref*{chainrule}\/)}}}}}}{{\leq}}\,\int_{\Omega}|\widetilde{H}^{\prime}(u)Pu|\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|(\widetilde{H})^{\prime\prime}(u)|\|\nabla u\|_{\mathbf{A}}^{2}\,\textup{d}x\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{\textup{Thms}\ \ref{structureA},\,\ref{simplification2}}}}{{\lesssim}}\hskip 19.91684pt\int_{\Omega}|\widetilde{H}^{\prime}(u)Pu|\,\textup{d}x\\
&=\int_{\Omega}|Q(\widetilde{H})Pu|\,\textup{d}x,\ \text{ where }Q(\widetilde{H})=(\widetilde{H})^{\prime}\ \text{as in \hyperref@@ii[one-var-chain]{{{(\ignorespaces\ref*{one-var-chain}\/)}}}}.\end{split}

(5.3)

For a similar conclusion in the one-variable setting, we refer to the inequalities from
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
, where
Pu=u^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
.

Deep results, concerning general variants of the pointwise chain rule formula
(
5.2
)
, dealing with the infinitesimal generators of the diffusion processes in place of the elliptic operator
P
, can be found in
[
2
, Lemma 1, p. 179]
.

In the setting of the fractional
\frac{\alpha}{2}
-Laplacian, there exist some known pointwise analogues of
(
5.3
)
; namely, the so-called pointwise Córdoba–Córdoba inequalities (see
[
12

):

Remark A.1

(about the condition

)
.

H
is strictly increasing on

, and thus

is strictly convex. In particular,

(0,B)
, whence

is strictly monotone near the endpoints of

and the limits

Namely, if we put

\widetilde{H}(s)-\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\to A\\

is increasing, and so it does not change sign on some neighbourhood of

is integrable near

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}(s)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{s}H(\tau)\,\textup{d}\tau,

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}(s):=-\int_{s}^{B}H(\tau)\,\textup{d}\tau.

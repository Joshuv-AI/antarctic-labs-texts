Modulational instability and ion-acoustic envelope solitons in four component plasmas
∗ N. A. Chowdhury, M. M. Hasan, A. Mannan, and A. A. Mamun

Abstract
Modulational instability (MI) of ion-acoustic waves (IAWs) has been theoretically investigated in a plasma system which is composed of inertial warm adiabatic ions, isothermal positrons, and two temperature superthermal electrons. A nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation is derived by using reductive perturbation method that governs the MI of the IAWs. The numerical analysis of the solution of NLS equation shows the existence of both stable (dark envelope solitons exist) and unstable (bright envelope solitons exist) regimes of IAWs. It is observed that the basic features (viz. stability of the wave profile and MI growth rate) of the IAWs are significantly modified by the superthermal parameter ( κ \kappa ) and related plasma parameters. The results of our present investigation should be useful for understanding different nonlinear phenomena in both space and laboratory plasmas.

II
Governing Equations

We consider an unmagnetized plasma system comprising of inertial warm adiabatic ions, isothermal
positrons, and two temperature superthermal electrons (hot and cold). At equilibrium, the quasi-neutrality condition
can be expressed as
n_{i0}+n_{p0}=n_{h0}+n_{c0}

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\frac{\partial n_{i}}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(n_{i}u_{i})=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial t}+u_{i}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x}=-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}-3\alpha n_{i}\frac{\partial n_{i}}{\partial x},

(3)

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{2}}=n_{c}+\gamma n_{h}-\sigma n_{i}-(1-\sigma+\gamma)n_{p}.

(4)

For inertialess cold electron, we can obtain the expressions for cold electron number densities as

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptn_{c}=\left[1-\frac{\phi}{(\kappa-3/2)}\right]^{-\kappa+{1/2}}

\displaystyle\hskip-24.18501pt=1+C_{1}\phi+C_{2}\phi^{2}+C_{3}\phi^{3}+\cdot\cdot\cdot\cdot,

(5)

where

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912ptC_{1}=\left(\frac{\kappa-{1/2}}{\kappa-{3/2}}\right),~~C_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\kappa^{2}-{1/4}}{(\kappa-{3/2})^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912ptC_{3}=\frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{(\kappa-{1/2})(\kappa+{1/2})(\kappa+{3/2})}{(\kappa-{3/2})^{3}}\right).\

For inertialess hot electron, we can obtain the expressions for hot electron number densities as

\displaystyle\hskip-31.2982ptn_{h}=\left[1-\frac{\mu\phi}{(\kappa-3/2)}\right]^{-\kappa+{1/2}}

\displaystyle\hskip-17.07182pt=1+C_{1}\mu\phi+C_{2}\mu^{2}\phi^{2}+C_{3}\mu^{3}\phi^{3}+\cdot\cdot\cdot\cdot.

(6)

Similarly for inertialess isothermal positron, we can obtain the expressions for positron number densities as

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055ptn_{p}=\mbox{exp}~(-\lambda\phi)

\displaystyle\hskip-32.72049pt=1-\lambda\phi+\frac{\lambda^{2}\phi^{2}}{2}-\frac{\lambda^{3}\phi^{3}}{6}+\cdot\cdot\cdot\cdot

Substituting equations

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38092pt\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{2}}=1+\gamma-\eta-\sigma n_{i}+\gamma_{1}\phi+\gamma_{2}\phi^{2}+\gamma_{3}\phi^{3}+\cdot\cdot,

\displaystyle\hskip-34.14322pt\eta=(1-\sigma+\gamma),~~~\gamma_{1}=\gamma\mu C_{1}+C_{1}+\eta\lambda,

\displaystyle\hskip-34.14322pt\gamma_{2}=\gamma\mu^{2}C_{2}+C_{2}-(\eta\lambda^{2})/2,

\displaystyle\hskip-34.14322pt\gamma_{3}=\gamma\mu^{3}C_{3}+C_{3}+(\eta\lambda^{3})/6,\

and

\alpha=\frac{T_{i}}{T_{c}}
,

,

,

.

In the above equations,

is the ion number density normalized by its equilibrium value

III
Derivation of the NLS Equation

To study the modulation of the IAWs in our considered plasma system, we will derive the NLS equation by
employing the RPM. So, we first introduce
the independent variables are stretched as

\displaystyle\hskip-110.96556pt\xi={\epsilon}(x-v_{g}t),~~~\tau={\epsilon}^{2}t,

(9)

where
v_{g}
is the envelope group velocity and
\epsilon~(0<\epsilon<1)
is a small (real) parameter.
Then we can write a general expression for the dependent variables

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptG(x,t)=G_{0}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\epsilon^{(m)}\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}G_{l}^{(m)}(\xi,\tau)~\mbox{exp}(il\Theta),

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptG_{l}^{(m)}=[n_{il}^{(m)},u_{il}^{(m)},\phi_{l}^{(m)}]^{T},~~G_{l}^{(0)}=[1,0,0]^{T},

(10)

where
\Theta=(kx-\omega t)
, simultaneously k and
\omega
are real variables representing the carrier wave number
and frequency, respectively.
G_{l}^{(m)}
satisfies the pragmatic condition
G_{l}^{(m)}=G_{-l}^{(m)^{*}}
, where the asterisk denotes
the complex conjugate. The derivative operators in the above equations are treated as follows:

\displaystyle\hskip-45.52458pt\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\rightarrow\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\epsilon v_{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}+\epsilon^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau},~~\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\rightarrow\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\epsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}.

(11)

Substituting equations
(9)-(11)
into equations
(2),(3)
, and
(8)
and the first order
(m=1)
equations with
(l=1)
, gives

\displaystyle\hskip-93.89418pt-i\omega n_{1}^{(1)}+iku_{1}^{(1)}=0,

\displaystyle\hskip-93.89418pt-i\omega u_{1}^{(1)}+ik\phi_{1}^{(1)}+ik\Omega n_{1}^{(1)}=0,

\displaystyle\hskip-93.89418pt\sigma n_{1}^{(1)}-k^{2}\phi_{1}^{(1)}-\gamma_{1}\phi_{1}^{(1)}=0,

(12)

where
\Omega=3\alpha

\displaystyle\hskip-68.28644ptn_{1}^{(1)}=\frac{k^{2}}{S}\phi_{1}^{(1)},~~~~~~~u_{1}^{(1)}=\frac{k\omega}{S}\phi_{1}^{(1)},

(13)

where

\displaystyle\hskip-119.50148pt\omega^{2}=\frac{\sigma k^{2}}{(k^{2}+\gamma_{1})}+k^{2}\Omega.

(14)

The second-order when
(m=2)
reduced equations with
(l=1)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptn_{1}^{(2)}=\frac{k^{2}}{S}\phi_{1}^{(2)}+\frac{2ik\omega(v_{g}k-\omega)}{S^{2}}\frac{\partial\phi_{1}^{(1)}}{\partial\xi},

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptu_{1}^{(2)}=\frac{k\omega}{S}\phi_{1}^{(2)}+\frac{i(v_{g}k-\omega)(\omega^{2}+k^{2}\Omega)}{S^{2}}\frac{\partial\phi_{1}^{(1)}}{\partial\xi},

\displaystyle\hskip-125.19194ptv_{g}=\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial k}=\frac{\sigma\omega^{2}-S^{2}}{k\sigma\omega}.

(16)

The amplitude of the second-order harmonics are found to be proportional to
|\phi_{1}^{(1)}|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-59.75095ptn_{2}^{(2)}=C_{4}|\phi_{1}^{(1)}|^{2},~~~~~~~n_{0}^{(2)}=C_{7}|\phi_{1}^{(1)}|^{2},

\displaystyle\hskip-59.75095ptu_{2}^{(2)}=C_{5}|\phi_{1}^{(1)}|^{2},~~~~~~~u_{0}^{(2)}=C_{8}|\phi_{1}^{(1)}|^{2},

\displaystyle\hskip-59.75095pt\phi_{2}^{(2)}=C_{6}|\phi_{1}^{(1)}|^{2},~~~~~~~\phi_{0}^{(2)}=C_{9}|\phi_{1}^{(1)}|^{2},

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054ptC_{4}=\frac{\Omega k^{6}+3\omega^{2}k^{4}+2C_{6}S^{2}k^{2}}{2S^{3}},

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054ptC_{6}=\frac{\sigma(\Omega k^{6}+3\omega^{2}k^{4})-2\gamma_{2}S^{3}}{2S^{3}{(4k^{2}+\gamma_{1})-2\sigma S^{2}k^{2}}},

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054ptC_{7}=\frac{2\omega v_{g}k^{3}+\Omega k^{4}+\omega^{2}k^{2}+C_{9}S^{2}}{S^{2}(v^{2}_{g}-\Omega)},

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054ptC_{8}=\frac{v_{g}C_{7}S^{2}-2\omega k^{3}}{S^{2}},

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054ptC_{9}=\frac{2\sigma\omega v_{g}k^{3}+\Omega\sigma k^{4}+\sigma k^{2}\omega^{2}-2\gamma_{2}S^{2}(v^{2}_{g}-\Omega)}{\gamma_{1}S^{2}(v^{2}_{g}-\Omega)-\sigma S^{2}}.\

(l=1)

\displaystyle\hskip 8.5359pt\left.+2S\gamma_{2}(C_{6}+C_{9})+3S\gamma_{3}-\frac{2\sigma\omega k^{3}(C_{5}+C_{8})}{S}\right].\

IV
Stability analysis

The evolution of IAWs is governed by the equation
(18)
, essentially depends on the coefficients product
PQ
.
Let us consider the harmonic modulated amplitude solution
\Phi=\Phi_{o}\exp(iQ{|\Phi_{o}|^{2}}\tau)
.
Following the standard stability analysis, one may perturb the amplitude by setting

\Phi=\hat{\Phi}_{0}+\epsilon\hat{\Phi}_{1,0}\exp[i({k_{MI}}\xi-{\omega_{MI}}\tau)]+c.c
(the
perturbation wave number
{k_{MI}}
and the frequency
{\omega_{MI}}
should be distinguished
from their carrier wave homolog quantities, denoted by
k
and
\omega
). Hence, the nonlinear
dispersion relation for the amplitude modulation
Sultana2011
;
Sabry2008
;
Schamel2002
is

\displaystyle\hskip-59.75095pt{\omega^{2}_{MI}}=P^{2}{k^{2}_{MI}}\left({k^{2}_{MI}}-2\frac{Q}{P}{|\Phi_{o}|^{2}}\right).

(19)

Clearly, if
PQ<0
,
{\omega_{MI}}
is always real for all values of
{k_{MI}}
, hence in this region the
IAWs is stable in the presence of small perturbation. On the other hand, when
PQ>0
, the MI
would set in as
{\omega_{MI}}
becomes imaginary and the envelope is unstable for

{k_{MI}}<k_{c}=\sqrt{2Q{|\Phi_{o}|}^{2}/P}
, where
k_{c}
is the critical value of the wave
number of modulation and
\Phi_{o}
is the amplitude of the carrier waves. In the region
PQ>0
and
{k_{MI}}<k_{c}
, the
growth rate (
\Gamma_{g}
) of MI is given by

\displaystyle\hskip-99.58464pt\Gamma_{g}=|P|~{k^{2}_{MI}}\sqrt{\frac{k^{2}_{c}}{k^{2}_{MI}}-1}.

(20)

Clearly, the maximum value
\Gamma_{g(max)}
of
\Gamma_{g}
is obtained at
{k_{MI}}=k_{c}/\sqrt{2}

and is given by
\Gamma_{g(max)}=|Q||\Phi_{0}|^{2}
.

The coeffficients of dispersion term
P
and nonlinear term
Q
are dependent on various plasma parameters,
such as
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\sigma,\mu,\lambda
and
\kappa
. Thus, these parameters may be controlled
the stability conditions of the IAWs. Therefore, we have investigated
the stability of the profile by depicting the ratio of
P/Q
versus
k
for different plasma parameters.
When the sign of the ratio
P/Q
is negative, the modulated envelope pulse is stable, while the sign of
the ratio
P/Q
is positive, the modulated envelope pulse will be unstable against external perturbations.
It is clear that both stable and unstable region are obtained from the figures
2-4
.
When
P/Q\rightarrow\pm\infty
, the corresponding value of
k(=k_{c})
is called critical or threshold
wave number for the onset of MI. This critical value separates the unstable (
P/Q>0
) from the stable region (
P/Q<0
) one.

V
Envelope solitons

If
PQ<0
, the modulated envelope pulse is stable and in this region dark envelope
solitons exist, on the other hand when
PQ>0
, the modulated envelope pulse which is unstable against external
perturbations and lead to formation of bright envelope solitons. A solution of equation
(18)

may be sought in the form
\Phi=\sqrt{\psi}~\mbox{exp}(i\theta)
, where
\psi
and
\theta
are real variables
which are determined by substituting into the NLS equation and separating real and imaginary parts. An
interested reader is referred to

;

;

;

;

;
Shalini2015
for details.
The different types of solution thus obtained are clearly summarized in the following paragraphs.

V.1
Bright solitons

When
PQ>0
, we find bright envelope solitons. The general analytical form of bright solitons reads

\displaystyle\hskip-79.6678pt\psi=\psi_{0}~\mbox{sech}^{2}\left(\frac{\xi-U\tau}{W}\right),

\displaystyle\hskip-79.6678pt\theta=\frac{1}{2P}\left[U\xi+\left(\Omega_{0}-\frac{U^{2}}{2}\right)\tau\right].

(21)

Here,
U
is the propagation speed (a constant),
W
is the soliton width, and
\Omega_{0}
oscillating frequency for
U=0
.
Figure
6
(a) represents the bright envelope solitons.

V.2
Dark solitons

When
PQ<0
, we find dark envelope solitons whose general analytical form reads as

\displaystyle\hskip-59.75095pt\psi=\psi_{0}~\mbox{tanh}^{2}\left(\frac{\xi-U\tau}{W}\right),

\displaystyle\hskip-59.75095pt\theta=\frac{1}{2P}\left[U\xi-\left(\frac{U^{2}}{2}-2PQ\psi_{0}\right)\tau\right].

(22)

Interestingly, in both of the latter two equation, the relation between soliton width

and the
constant maximum amplitude
\psi_{0}
are related by

\displaystyle\hskip-145.10922ptW=\sqrt{\frac{2|P/Q|}{\psi_{0}}}.

(23)

The ratio
P/Q
determines the soliton width

as
\psi_{0}W\sim(P/Q)^{1/2}
. So lower
P/Q
values suggest narrower solitons and vice versa.

VI
Discussion

a NLS equation is derived, which governs the evolution of IAWs. We have investigated the existence of both stable and unstable
regions for IAWs structures and the associated MI of electrostatic wave packets. The results, we have found from this
investigation which can be summarized as follows:

Weyl Estimates for spacelike hypersurfaces in de Sitter space
Daniel Ballesteros-Chavez, Wilhelm Klingenberg, Ben Lambert

Abstract
. We study the isometric spacelike embedding problem in scaled de Sitter space, and obtain Weyl-type estimates and the corresponding closedness in the space of embeddings.



1.
Introduction

The problem of isometric embedding of a closed surface of genus zero and positive curvature
(S^{2},g)
into Euclidean 3-space

\mathbb{R}^{3}
subsequently known as the Weyl problem was considered by Hermann Weyl in 1916. He outlined a method to prove the existence of an embedding using the continuity method and proved a curvature estimate. In his landmark thesis, Louis Nirenberg
[
10
]

solved the embedding problem under the assumption that the coefficients of
g
have continuous fourth order derivatives. More recently, interest in isometric embeddings into semi-Riemannian spaces has been kindled by Mu-Tao Wang and Shing-Tung Yau
[
14
]
due to their relation to the Brown-York quasilocal mass in general relativity.

A range of related Weyl estimates have previously been investigated in Euclidean spaces. We particularly note that Yanyan Li and Gilbert Weinstein
[
7
]
generalised the Weyl estimates to hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and Yanyan Li and Pengfei Guan investigated the degenerate curvature case
[
3
]
. Motivated by
[
14
]
, there has been surge of interest in isometric embeddings into Euclidean spaces, as this is a first step in the embedding into Minkowski space. For example , Chen-Yun Lin and Ye-Kai Wang used estimates on isometric embeddings in hyperbolic space to demonstrate the existence of isometric embeddings in Anti de Sitter space
[
8
]
. Recently Pengfei Guan and Siyuan Lu
[
4
]
established an estimate of the mean curvature of an immersed hypersurface with nonnegative extrinsic scalar curvature in a Riemannian manifold. Then Chunhe Li and Zhizhang Wang
[
6
]
obtained the openness of the collection of embeddable metrics. These results together with the a priori estimates obtained by Siyuan Lu
[
9
]
imply existence results for embedding into Riemannian manifolds.

In this paper we study a spacelike isometric embedding
of a metric on the sphere into de Sitter space
X:(S^{n},g)\to(S^{n,1}_{1},\bar{g})
, the former being a metric on the sphere with some curvature conditions, and the latter being de Sitter space, that is, the unit sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1,1}
with the induced metric. Thus, in respect of coordinates on
S^{n}
, we shall study solutions of the following system of equations for
X
:

(1)

\bar{g}(\nabla_{i}X,\nabla_{j}X)=g_{ij}.

Our results are as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
g
is a smooth metric of positive curvature on
S^{2}
. Then there exists a
\rho_{0}>0
such that for
0<\rho<\rho_{0}
, there is a bound
C(g,\rho)>0
such that any admissible isometric embedding of
(S^{2},g)
into
(S^{2,1}_{\rho},\overline{g})
satisfies

(2)

|A|^{2}\leq C(g,\rho).

Furthermore, if g is a smooth metric on
S^{n}
satisfies

(3)

\rho^{-2}n(n-2)<R<\rho^{-2}n(n-1),

for some

\rho>0
and the scalar curvature
R
,
then for any admissible isometric embedding of
(S^{n},g)
into
(S^{n,1}_{\rho},\overline{g})
we have

(4)

|A|^{2}\leq C(n,g,\rho).

Our second result is a compactness theorem for the graphical representation as in section 2.2, which implies closedness in the space of embeddings.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X:(S^{n},g)\to(S^{n,1}_{\rho},\bar{g})
be as in Theorem 1. Then there exists an rotation
R
of
S^{n,1}_{\rho}
such that
RX
is smooth graph over the round sphere
S^{n}_{\rho}\subset S^{n,1}_{\rho}
. Indeed for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exist constants
C_{k}=C_{k}(n,\rho,g)
such that the graph function
u
of
RX
satisfies

\|u\|_{C^{k}}\leq C_{k}\ .

Remark 3
.

We note that the scaling of de Sitter space is necessary. For example a short argument implies that there can be no isometric embedding of small spheres into de Sitter space of radius 1, see Lemma
7
.

Remark 4
.

The isometric embeddings of Mu-Tau Wang and Shing-Tung Yau in
[
14
]
are typically codimension 2 embeddings, utilising isometric embedding theorems into Euclidean spaces. The key difference in our approach is that we are considering a codimension 1 embedding into
S^{n,1}
. However, we also note that we could also consider this as a codimension 2 embedding into
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
.

2.
Geometry of hypersurfaces in de Sitter space

We take
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
to be
(n+2)
-dimensional Minkowski space, where we choose the standard orthogonal basis
E_{0},E_{1},\ldots,E_{n+1}
and
E_{0}
has Minkowski norm

\left\langle E_{0},E_{0}\right\rangle=:|E_{0}|^{2}=-1\ .

We consider the
(n+1)
-dimensional de Sitter space
S^{n,1}_{\rho}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
, which is the Lorentzian manifold consisting of the points
x=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+2}_{1}
with Minkowski length
\rho
:

(5)

(S^{n,1}_{\rho},\overline{g}):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+2}_{1}|\langle x,x\rangle=\rho^{2}\},

where
{\overline{g}}
is the induced metric. Note that here and throughout the paper the index indicates the signature of the metric, so
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
is
(n+2)
dimensional.
We will write geometric quantities on de Sitter space with a bar, namely
{\overline{g}}
,
{\overline{\nabla}}
,
{\overline{A}}
, etc. are the induced metric, covariant derivative and second fundamental form. We will write geometric quantities without a bar for quantities on the embedded hypersurface
(S^{n},g)\hookrightarrow(S^{n,1}_{\rho},\overline{g})
, so for example
g
and
\nabla
for the induced metric and covariant derivative on the isometric immersion
M
, while we will use
\nu
for the downward pointing timelike unit normal. Finally, we use a double bar for quantities on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
, e.g. we will write
{\overline{{\overline{\nabla}}}}
for the covariant derivative. When indices are repeated we will employ Einstein summation convention where we assume latin letters run in
1\leq i,j,k,\ldots\leq n
and greek letters run in
0\leq\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\ldots\leq n+1
.

2.1.
Curvature identities

Here we make explicit our chosen sign conventions and collect various standard geometric identities which will be useful later.

For any connection
\nabla
, we pick the conventions

R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z+\nabla_{[Y,X]}Z,\qquad R(X,Y,Z,A)=\left\langle R(X,Y)Z,A\right\rangle\ .

and for a nondegenerate hypersurface
\widehat{M}^{n}\subset\widetilde{M}^{n+1}
, we define the second fundamental form
A
by

(6)

\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}Y=\widehat{\nabla}_{X}Y+\epsilon A(X,Y)\nu

where
\epsilon=\left\langle\nu,\nu\right\rangle
and
\nu
is a local unit normal to
\widehat{M}^{n}
. As a result, we have the identity

-\left\langle\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}\nu,X\right\rangle=\left\langle\nu,\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}X\right\rangle=A(X,Y)\ .

These conventions give rise to the Codazzi and Gauss equations given by

\left\langle\widetilde{R}(X,Y)Z,\nu\right\rangle=\widehat{\nabla}_{Y}A(X,Z)-\widehat{\nabla}_{X}A(Y,Z)

and

\left\langle\widetilde{R}(X,Y)Z,V\right\rangle=\left\langle\widehat{R}(X,Y)Z,V\right\rangle+\epsilon[A(Y,Z)A(X,V)-A(X,Z)A(V,Y)]\ .

By differentiating the defining equation for
S^{n,1}_{1}(\rho)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
at
p\in S^{n,1}_{\rho}
for outward normal
{\overline{\nu}}=\rho^{-1}p
we have

{\overline{A}}_{\alpha\beta}=-\rho^{-1}{\overline{g}}_{\alpha\beta}

and so the Gauss equation yields

{\overline{R}}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=\rho^{-2}\left({\overline{g}}_{\alpha\gamma}{\overline{g}}_{\beta\delta}-{\overline{g}}_{\beta\gamma}{\overline{g}}_{\alpha\delta}\right)\ .

Applying (
6
) to
M\subset S^{n,1}_{\rho}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
we have the Gauss formula

(7)

{\overline{{\overline{\nabla}}}}_{i}X_{j}=\nabla_{i}X_{j}-A_{ij}\nu-\rho^{-1}g_{ij}\rho^{-1}X.

the Codazzi equation

(8)

\nabla_{k}A_{ij}=\nabla_{i}A_{kj}.

and the Gauss equation

(9)

R_{ijkl}=A_{il}A_{jk}-A_{ik}A_{jl}+\rho^{-2}\left(g_{ik}g_{jl}-g_{il}g_{jk}\right)

Note that the Ricci and the scalar curvature tensors in orthonormal frame are given respectively by

(10)

R_{ik}=g^{jl}R_{ijkl}=A_{jk}A^{j}_{i}-HA_{ik}+\rho^{-2}(n-1)g_{ik},

and

(11)

R=g^{ik}R_{ik}=|A|^{2}-H^{2}+\rho^{-2}n(n-1).

We also recall the well known formula for derivative interchange

(12)

\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}A_{ij}-\nabla_{l}\nabla_{k}A_{ij}=R_{kljr}A_{i}^{r}+R_{klir}A_{j}^{r}.

2.2.
Graphical hypersurfaces in de Sitter space

We denote the standard Euclidean round unit sphere by
(S^{n},\sigma)
, which we will think of as being the standard embedding in
{\operatorname{span}}\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{n+1}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
.
Given
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
\xi=\xi(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n})\in S^{n}
, we can re-write (
5
)
in polar coordinates and identify
S^{n,1}_{\rho}
with the points

(13)

Y(r,\xi)=\rho\sinh rE_{0}+\rho\cosh(r)\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}.

Let
u:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function and consider
M=\mbox{graph}(u)
, parametrised by

X:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to S^{n,1}_{\rho}
given by
X(\xi)=Y(\sinh(u(\xi)),\xi)
. We will use
D
to denote the standard covariant derivative for the metric
\sigma
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and throughout we will require the embedding to be spacelike.

Note here that we are choosing a particular isometrically embedded
S^{n}
as our “equator” given by the zero graph
u=0
based on our choice of
E_{0}
. Due to the invariance of
S^{n,1}_{\rho}
under isometries of
\mathbb{R}_{1}^{n+2}
and the noncompactness of possible choices of
E_{0}
, the choice of graph direction will be important for us later.

Writing the unit timelike vector
E_{r}:=\rho^{-1}\frac{\partial Y}{\partial r}
we have that

(14)

g_{ij}=\rho^{2}(\cosh^{2}(u)\sigma_{ij}-u_{i}u_{j})

the normal vector
\nu
can be written as

\nu=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh^{2}u-|Du|^{2}}}(\cosh(u)E_{r}+Du).

where as usual
Du=u_{i}\sigma^{ij}\xi_{i}
.

We define the tilt of
M
as

(15)

\tau:=\langle\nu,E_{0}\rangle,

and the height function by

(16)

\eta:=-\langle X,E_{0}\rangle,

and so in terms of the graph function
u
we have

(17)

\eta=\rho\sinh u,\qquad\tau=\frac{\cosh^{2}(u)}{\sqrt{\cosh^{2}(u)-|Du|^{2}}}\ .

M
is spacelike at a point if
g_{ij}
is positive definite, which is equivalent to
\tau
being finite at that point. We note that with an upper bound on
\tau
we have complete control of the eigenvalues of
g_{ij}
. A standard calculation yields that

(18)

A_{ij}=\rho\cosh^{-1}(u)\tau\left(D^{2}_{ij}u-2\tanh(u)D_{i}uD_{j}u+\sinh(u)\cosh(u)\sigma_{ij}\right).

2.3.
The PDE

We consider the isometric embedding as a prescribed curvature problem. These will be written in terms of symmetric polynomials of principal curvatures. We define the k-symmetric polynomial

P_{k}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{i}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\ldots\lambda_{i_{k}}\ .

We will be particularly interested in
P_{2}(\vec{\lambda})
where
\vec{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})
are the eigenvalues of
A
. This may also be written as
P_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(H^{2}-|A|^{2})
, where here we using the definition that
H=P_{1}(\vec{\lambda})
. We recall that curvature cone
\Gamma_{2}
is the connected component of
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|P_{2}(x)>0\}
which contains the positive cone. A hypersurface will be said to be
admissible
if at every point its principal curvatures satisfy
\vec{\lambda}\in\Gamma_{2}
.
We also define

P_{k,i}(\vec{\lambda}):=P_{k}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{i-1},0,\lambda_{i+1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})

By (
11
) we see that we have the equation

(19)

2F(\vec{\lambda}):=2P_{2}(\vec{\lambda})=\rho^{-2}n(n-1)-R=:\psi_{\rho},

where we see that
\psi_{\rho}
depends only on the prescribed metric and
\rho
. This is a prescribed symmetric curvature functional equation, and the condition for this to be admissible for convex surfaces is
\rho^{-2}n(n-1)-R>0
. This is an upper bound requirement on the total curvature - the opposite to Euclidean space. This comes from the sign on the normal in the Gauss equations.

We note here that
f(\vec{\lambda}):=\sqrt{F(\vec{\lambda})}
is a concave and we may calculate that

F^{ij}=Hg^{ij}-A^{ij}\ .

3.
Preliminary lemmata

Lemma 5
.

We have

(20)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\eta

\displaystyle=-\left\langle E_{0},X_{i}\right\rangle

(21)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\tau

\displaystyle=-A_{i}^{j}\left\langle X_{j},E_{0}\right\rangle=A_{i}^{j}\nabla_{j}\eta

(22)

\displaystyle|\nabla\eta|^{2}+\rho^{-2}\eta^{2}+1=\tau^{2}

Proof.

The calculations of first two derivatives is immediate from the definitions and our above sign conventions.

For (
22
), the ambient vector
E_{0}
may be decomposed in the following coordinates

(23)

\begin{split}E_{0}&=\nabla\eta-\tau\nu-\rho^{-2}\eta X.\end{split}

and so we have

(24)

-1=|\nabla\eta|^{2}-\tau^{2}+\rho^{-2}\eta^{2}\ .

∎

Lemma 6
.

We have the following second order relations.

(25)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{ij}\eta

\displaystyle=A_{ij}\tau-\rho^{-1}g_{ij}\eta

(26)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{ij}\tau

\displaystyle=\nabla^{k}\eta\nabla_{k}A_{ij}+\tau A^{2}_{ij}-\rho^{-1}A_{ij}\eta

(27)

\displaystyle\Delta A_{ij}-\nabla^{2}_{ij}H

\displaystyle=|A|^{2}A_{ij}-HA^{2}_{ij}+\rho^{-2}[nA_{ij}-Hg_{ij}]

Proof.

We calculate

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{ij}\eta

\displaystyle=-\left\langle E_{0},D^{2}_{ij}X-\nabla_{X_{i}}X_{j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle E_{0},{\overline{A}}_{ij}X-A_{ij}\nu\right\rangle

\displaystyle={\overline{A}}_{ij}\eta+A_{ij}\tau

\displaystyle=A_{ij}\tau-\rho^{-1}g_{ij}\eta

Next

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{ij}\tau

\displaystyle=\nabla_{i}A_{j}^{k}\nabla_{k}\eta+A_{j}^{k}\nabla_{ik}\eta

\displaystyle=\nabla^{k}\eta\nabla_{k}A_{ij}+\tau A^{2}_{ij}-\rho^{-1}A_{ij}\eta

Finally we demonstrate the Simons-like identity. Using the Codazzi(
8
), derivative interchange (
12
) and Gauss equations (
9
) we have

\displaystyle\nabla^{\ k}\nabla_{k}A_{ij}-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}A_{k}^{k}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}A_{j}^{k}-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}A_{j}^{k}

\displaystyle=R_{kils}g^{kl}A^{s}_{j}+R_{kijs}A^{ks}

\displaystyle=A_{i}^{k}A_{ks}A^{s}_{j}-A_{k}^{k}A_{is}A^{s}_{j}+A_{ij}A_{ks}A^{ks}-A_{kj}A^{ks}A_{is}

\displaystyle\qquad+\rho^{-2}(n-1)A_{ij}+\rho^{-2}A_{ij}-\rho^{-2}Hg_{ij}

\displaystyle=|A|^{2}A_{ij}-HA^{2}_{ij}+\rho^{-2}nA_{ij}-\rho^{-2}Hg_{ij}\ .

∎

We now briefly justify that for general isometric embedding problems, we need to scale de Sitter space to get reasonable estimates. We briefly demonstrate that we may not embed an arbitrarily small sphere into
S^{n,1}_{1}
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
S^{n}_{r}
be the standard round Euclidean sphere of radius
r\in(0,1)
. We have that

a)

For
n\geq 3
there does not exist an isometric embedding
X:S_{r}^{n}\to S^{n,1}_{1}
.

b)

For
n=2
there does not exist an admissible isometric embedding
X:S_{r}^{2}\to S^{2,1}_{1}
.

Proof.

This follows from the sectional curvature of the sphere and the Gauss equation. Suppose that such an embedding exists and take coordinates in the principal directions of the submanifold. As the embedding is isometric, (
9
) implies for any
i\neq j

\frac{1}{r^{2}}=R_{ijij}=-\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}+1

so

0<\frac{1}{r^{2}}-1=-\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\ ,

and so for any pair
i\neq j
,
\lambda_{i}
and
\lambda_{j}
must have opposite signs. In dimension 3 this is not possible. In dimension 2 this means that
P_{2}(\vec{\lambda})<0
and so the embedding is nowhere admissible.
∎

4.
Curvature in de Sitter space

We now prove an initial estimate for the mean curvature.

Lemma 8
.

Suppose that we have a convex isometric immersion into de Sitter space with metric satisfying
\rho^{-2}-n^{-1}\psi_{\rho}>0
and
\psi_{\rho}>0
. Then

(28)

H^{2}\leq\frac{(n-1)^{-1}}{\rho^{-2}-n^{-1}\psi_{\rho}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\Delta R-\psi_{\rho}^{2}+n\rho^{-2}\psi_{\rho}\right].

Proof.

At a maximum of
H
, we have
\nabla H=0
,
\nabla^{2}H\leq 0
. As a result, since
Hg^{ij}-A^{ij}\geq 0
, since
\psi_{\rho}>0
for small
\rho
and
\partial P_{2}/\partial\lambda_{j}\geq 0
, (see
[
1
]
),

H\Delta H-A^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla{j}H\leq 0\ .

Using the identity (
27
) from Lemma
6
, we compute as follows

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta R

\displaystyle=A^{ij}\Delta A_{ij}-H\Delta H+|\nabla A|^{2}-|\nabla H|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq A^{ij}(\Delta A_{ij}-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}H)

\displaystyle=|A|^{4}-H\operatorname{tr}A^{3}+\rho^{-2}\left(n|A|^{2}-H^{2}\right)

From equations (
19
) we have that
|A|^{2}=H^{2}-\psi_{\rho}
and so applying this twice we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta R

\displaystyle\geq H(H|A|^{2}-\operatorname{tr}A^{3})-\psi_{\rho}H^{2}+\rho^{-2}(n-1)H^{2}+\psi_{\rho}^{2}-n\rho^{-2}\psi_{\rho}\ .

Following Urbas
[
13
, equation 3.8]
, we note that using standard relations on symmetric polynomials

H|A|^{2}-\operatorname{tr}A^{3}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{1,i}(\vec{\lambda})\lambda_{i}^{2}=P_{1}(\vec{\lambda})P_{2}(\vec{\lambda})-3P_{3}(\vec{\lambda})

and so

H|A|^{2}-\operatorname{tr}A^{3}\geq P_{1}(\vec{\lambda})P_{2}(\vec{\lambda})-\frac{2(n-2)P_{2}^{2}(\vec{\lambda})}{(n-1)P_{1}(\vec{\lambda})}\geq\frac{2}{n}P_{1}(\vec{\lambda})P_{2}(\vec{\lambda})=\frac{1}{n}H\psi_{\rho}

where the first inequality uses the Newton inequalities (which apply regardless of curvature cone) and the second inequality follows from the Maclaurin inequality in
\Gamma_{2}
.

Therefore we obtain that

(29)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\Delta R&\geq(n^{-1}-1)\psi_{\rho}H^{2}+\psi_{\rho}^{2}+\rho^{-2}(n-1)H^{2}+\psi_{\rho}^{2}-n\rho^{-2}\psi_{\rho}\\
&=(n-1)(\rho^{-2}-n^{-1}\psi_{\rho})H^{2}+\psi_{\rho}^{2}-n\rho^{-2}\psi_{\rho},\end{split}

from which the claimed inequality follows.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem 1

Recall that we have

\psi_{\rho}=\rho^{-2}n(n-1)-R.

In order to apply Lemma
8
, we require

0<\psi_{\rho}<n\rho^{-2}
, equivalently

(30)

\rho^{-2}n(n-2)<R<\rho^{-2}n(n-1).

Note that we can write
H^{2}=|A|^{2}+2P_{2}
, and since we are assuming 2-convexity, the estimate for
|A|^{2}
will follow from the estimate of
H^{2}
.

If
n=2
then the left hand side becomes zero and, as
R
has a finite maximum. We may
see that if
\rho
is small enough we may obtain a curvature estimate depending only on
R

and it’s derivative.

6.
Proof of Theorem 2

Our first observation is that we need to choose a coordinate system that our estimates make reference to. This is because the Lorentz group
SO(1,n+1)
of isometries fixing the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
is
noncompact, and given any spacelike surface, and a given point on that surface, a rotated surface will have an arbitrarily large tilt at the corresponding point. As a result, it is clear that we must always choose a sensible graph direction or equivalently, sensible choices of base
E_{0},\ldots,E_{n+1}
. However, the curvature estimates from Theorem
1
hold regardless of graphical representation.
Note from (
23
) that the tangential projection to the surface of the vector
E_{0}
, which we denote by
E^{\top}_{0}
, coincides with
\nabla\eta
, hence we have by equation (
22
) we know

(31)

\tau^{2}=1+\rho^{-2}\eta^{2}+|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

Also from equation (
21
)
we have that

|\nabla\tau|^{2}\leq|A|^{2}|E_{0}^{\top}|^{2}\leq\tau^{2}|A|^{2}

or, using Theorem
1
,

|\nabla\log\tau|\leq|A|\leq C_{H}\ .

We now pick our coordinate system: By choosing any point
p\in S^{n}
, we may choose a basis such that
\nu(p)
is in direction
E_{0}
which implies that
\tau(p)=1
. In particular, due to (
22
) we also now know that
\eta(p)=0
. Therefore integrating from
p
we have that

\max\tau=\frac{\max\tau}{\tau(p)}\leq e^{C_{H}\operatorname{diam}(M)}=:C_{\tau}\ .

As
\operatorname{diam}(M)
is intrinsic and
C
depends only on
g
and
\tau
is bounded. By equation (
22
) we also have that

|\eta|\leq C_{\tau}\ .

These estimates together supply the required
C^{1}
bound as

\tau=\frac{\sqrt{1+\eta^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-\frac{|\tilde{\nabla}u|^{2}}{\eta^{2}+1}}}\ .

Using the graphical form of
A_{ij}
we therefore know that
\eta
, and therefore
u
are bounded in
C^{2}
.
Our estimates now follow the familiar path from PDE theory. We note that, by setting
f=\sqrt{F}=\sqrt{P_{2}}
we have that

f(\lambda)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(\rho^{-2}n(n-1)-R)}

which (by admissibility of the solution and the well-known convexity of
f
) is an elliptic PDE in
u
. Furthermore, due to our
C^{2}
estimates the second fundamental form stays in a compact subset of the
\Gamma_{2}
cone, and so this equation is uniformly elliptic. We may therefore apply Evans
[
2
]
and Krylov-Safonov
[
5
]
to see that
|u|_{C^{2+\alpha}}<C
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. As our graph is now bounded in
C^{2+\alpha}
,
R
is bounded in
C^{\alpha}
, and we may repeatedly apply elliptic Hölder estimates to get that
|u|_{C^{k+\alpha}}\leq C_{k+\alpha}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

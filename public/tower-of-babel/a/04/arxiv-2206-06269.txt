GLOBAL UNIFORM IN N ESTIMATES FOR SOLUTIONS OF A SYSTEM OF HARTREE–FOCK–BOGOLIUBOV TYPE IN THE GROSS–PITAVESKII REGIME.
Xiaoqi Huang

Abstract
. We extend the recent work of Chong et al., 2022 [ 7 ] to the critical case. More precisely, we prove global in time, uniform in N N estimates for the solutions ϕ \phi , Λ \Lambda and Γ \Gamma of a coupled system of Hartree–Fock–Bogoliubov type with interaction potential 1 N ​ V N ​ ( x − y ) = N 2 ​ v ​ ( N ⁡ ( x − y ) ) \frac{1}{N}V_{N}(x-y)=N^{2}v(N(x-y)) . We assume that the potential v v is small which satisfies some technical conditions, and the initial conditions have finite energy. The main ingredient is a sharp estimate for the linear Schrödinger equation with potential in 6+1 dimension, which may be of interest in its own right.

1.
Introduction

Consider the
N
–body linear Schrödinger equation which governs the time-evolution of
N
boson systems

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial_{t}}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Delta_{x_{j}}+\frac{1}{N}V_{N}(x_{i}-x_{j})\Big)\psi_{N}(t,\cdot)=0,

where
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
,
N
is large and
V_{N}(x)=N^{3}v(Nx)
for some Schwarz class potential
v
. The conditions on the potential will be discussed below.
A physically appealing case concerns initial data forming a tensor product of the same one–particle state, in spirit of the Bose-Einstein condensation. We refer to
[
20
]
for extensive background on Bose-Einstein condensation.

The goal is to find a rigorous, simple approximation to
\psi_{N}
which is consistent with

(1.2)

\psi_{approx}(t,x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\sim\phi(t,x_{1})\phi(t,x_{2})\dots\phi(t,x_{N})

in an appropriate sense, where
\phi
is often called the mean–field limit.

In the stationary case, a survey of results concerning the ground
state properties of the dilute bosonic gases can be found in
[
20
]
.
In the time dependent case, in the work of Erdös, Schlein and Yau
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
, by using the BBGKY hierarchies and the density matrix
\gamma_{N,t}
formalism, the convergence
of the exact dynamics to the mean–field limit is asserted in the trace norm as
N\rightarrow\infty
, provided that the
the mean–field limit
\phi(t,x)
satisfies the Gross–Pitaevskii equation

(1.3)

\frac{1}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial_{t}}\phi-\Delta\phi+8\pi a_{0}|\phi|^{2}\phi=0.

Here
a_{0}
is the scattering length of the potential
v
, physically, the scattering length measures the effective range of
the potential
V
, see e.g.,
[
12
]
for a precise definition of scattering length. Also in the recent work of Pickl
[
21
]
, the Gross–Pitaevskii equation is derived using a different method.
We refer to reader to
[
3
,
2
,
23
,
9
,
4
]
for more backgrounds on the problem of approximating the many-body Schrödinger dynamics and related results in the Gross–Pitaveskii regime.

The Hartree-Fock-Bogoliubov type equations are derived in Fock space,
which has been used in order to get estimates
for the rate of convergence of the approximation to the exact solution. In the mean–field regime, we also refer to the earlier work of Rodnianski and Schlein
[
23
]
, which gives the convergence rate for marginal densities
\gamma_{N,t}^{(k)}
, and the work of Lewin, Nam and Schlein
[
19
]
, which gives efficient direct estimates in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
using the Hartree state approach. In the Gross–Pitaveskii regime, we refer to the work of Brennecke and Schlein
[
6
]
for explicit rate of condensation.

The ideas behind the use of HFB system is to introduce pair excitations(given by a Bogoliubov transformation

e^{\mathcal{B}}
) as a second order correction to the mean field approximation.
The exact evolution of (
1.1
) is approximated by a construction involving two function: the condensate
\phi(t,x)
and a pair
excitation function
k(t,x,y)
, and it is

(1.4)

\psi_{approx}:=e^{-\sqrt{N}\mathcal{A}(\phi(t))}e^{\mathcal{B}(k(t))}\Omega

where

(1.5)

\mathcal{A}(\phi)=\int dx\{\bar{\phi}(x)a_{x}-\phi(x)a^{*}_{x}\}

with
a^{*}_{x}
and
a_{x}
being the creation and annihilation operator in Fock space, and
e^{-\sqrt{N}\mathcal{A}(\phi)}
is a unitary operator on Fock space, the Weyl operator. And

(1.6)

\mathcal{B}(k)=\frac{1}{2}\int dxdy\{\bar{k}(t,x,y)a_{x}a_{y}-k(t,x,y)a^{*}_{x}a^{*}_{y}\}.

The unitary operator
e^{\mathcal{B}(k)}
is the representation of an real symplectric matrix. Also,
\Omega
is the vacuum. See for instance
[
14
]
for background on this construction.

In order for
\psi_{approx}
to be an approximation to the exact evolution,
\phi
and
k
must satisfy certain PDEs. They were introduced in
[
14
]
and independently in a different
context in
[
1
]
, see also
[
16
]
,
[
15
]
for more details on the argument motivating the use of Hartree-Fock-Bogoliubov type equations. We also mention that in Benedikter, de Oliveira
and Schlein
[
2
]
, a similar approach is considered, where the authors impose the Gross–Pitaevskii equation equation for
\phi
and define
k
by an
explicit formula, and give a rate of convergence result in terms of the marginal density.

In the remaining of this paper, we shall focus on the analysis of the systems of PDEs. In particular, our main result, which we used in the analysis of nonlinear system, is the estimate for linear Schrödinger equation stated in the next section. The linear estimate in section 2 generalizes the recent work of Chong et al., 2022
[
7
]
to the critical case.

The functions described by these equations are:
the condensate
\phi(t,x)
and the density matrices

(1.7)

\Gamma(t,x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{1}{N}\Big(\overline{\mbox{\rm sh}(k)}\circ\mbox{\rm sh}(k)\Big)(t,x_{1},x_{2})+\bar{\phi}(t,x_{1})\phi(t,x_{2})

(1.8)

\Lambda(t,x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{1}{2N}\mbox{\rm sh}(2k)(t,x_{1},x_{2})+\phi(t,x_{1})\phi(t,x_{2})

where

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm sh}(k)

\displaystyle=k+\frac{1}{3!}k\circ\bar{k}\circ k+\dots

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm ch}(k)

\displaystyle=\delta(x-y)+\frac{1}{2!}\bar{k}\circ k+\dots

Here
(u\circ v)(x,y)=\int u(x,z)v(z,y)dz
, the pair excitation function
k
is an auxiliary function, which does not explicitly appear in the system.

There are several equivalent ways of expressing the equations, in this section we shall use a compact, matrix formulation as in
[
7
]
. We separate the condensate part from
the pair interaction part: define
\Gamma_{c}=\bar{\phi}\otimes\phi
,
\Lambda_{c}=\phi\otimes\phi
,
\Gamma_{p}=\frac{1}{N}\overline{\mbox{\rm sh}(k)}\circ\mbox{\rm sh}(k)
and
\Lambda_{p}=\frac{1}{2N}\mbox{\rm sh}(2k)
. Also denote
\rho(t,x)=\Gamma(t,x,x)

To write the Hartree-Fock-Bogoliubov equations in matrix notation, denote

V_{N}(x-y)=N^{3}v(N(x-y))

for some Schwarz class potential which will be discussed below. Define

\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}-\Gamma&-\bar{\Lambda}\\
\Lambda&\Gamma\end{pmatrix}=\Psi+\Phi

where

\Psi=\begin{pmatrix}-\Gamma_{p}&-\bar{\Lambda}_{p}\\
\Lambda_{p}&\Gamma_{p}\end{pmatrix}

\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}-\Gamma_{c}&-\bar{\Lambda}_{c}\\
\Lambda_{c}&\Gamma_{c}\end{pmatrix}

Finally, let

S_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}-I&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}

where
I
is the identity operator.

The evolution equations for
\Psi
and
\Phi
are

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Phi-[\Delta_{x}\delta(x-y)S_{3},\Phi]

(1.10)

\displaystyle=-[\big(V_{N}*\rho(t,x)\big)\delta(x-y)S_{3},\Phi]-[V_{N}\Psi^{*},\Phi]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Psi-[\Delta_{x}\delta(x-y)S_{3},\Psi]

(1.11)

\displaystyle=-[\big(V_{N}*\rho(t,x)\big)\delta(x-y)S_{3},\Psi]-\frac{1}{2N}[S_{3},V_{N}\Psi]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-[V_{N}\Omega^{*},\Psi]-\frac{1}{2N}[S_{3},V_{N}\Phi]

In addition, the condensate
\phi
satisfies

(1.12)

\displaystyle\Big\{

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i}\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x_{1}}\Big\}\phi(x_{1})

\displaystyle=-\int dy\{V_{N}(x_{1}-y)\Gamma(y,y)\}\phi(x_{1})

\displaystyle-\int dy\{V_{N}(x_{1}-y)\Gamma_{p}(y,x_{1})\}\phi(y)

\displaystyle+\int dy\{V_{N}(x_{1}-y)\Lambda_{p}(x_{1},y)\}\bar{\phi}(y)

Here
A^{*}(x,y)=\bar{A}(y,x),[A,B]=A\circ B-B\circ A
and
V_{N}
acts as pointwise multiplication by
V_{N}(x-y)
. See (
5.1
)-(

) for a scalar form of the above

The solutions
\phi,\,\Lambda_{p},\,\Lambda_{c}\,\Gamma_{p}
and

all depend on
N
. This has been suppressed to simplify the notation. However, we will always keep track
of dependence on

Next we review the conserved quantities of these equations, see
[

]
for more details. The first conserved quantities is the total

(1.13)

{\rm tr}\{\Gamma(t)\}=\|\phi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(dx)}^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\|\mbox{\rm sh}(k)(t,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(dxdy)}^{2}=1

From here we see that

(1.14)

\|\Lambda(t,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(dxdy)}\leq C

The second conserved quantity is the energy per particle

(1.15)

\displaystyle E(t)=

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle tr}\{\nabla_{x_{1}}\cdot\nabla_{x_{2}}\Gamma(t)\}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int dx_{1}dx_{2}\Big\{V_{N}(x_{1}-x_{2})|\Lambda(t,x_{1},x_{2})|^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int dx_{1}dx_{2}\Big\{V_{N}(x_{1}-x_{2})|\Gamma(t,x_{1},x_{2})|^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int dx_{1}dx_{2}\Big\{V_{N}(x_{1}-x_{2})\Gamma(t,x_{1},x_{1})\Gamma(t,x_{2},x_{2})\Big\}

\displaystyle-\int dx_{1}dx_{2}\Big\{V_{N}(x_{1}-x_{2})|\phi(t,x_{1})|^{2}|\phi(t,x_{2})|^{2}\Big\}

The above holds for any Schwarz potential
v
. In addition, in order to use the estimates of
[
8
]
, we assume

(1.16)

\displaystyle v\,\,\,\text{is spherically symmetric and}

\displaystyle v\geq 0,\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}(r)\leq 0.

For the initial conditions, we assume that

(1.17)

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\{\Gamma(0)\}\leq C_{0}

\displaystyle E(0)\leq C_{0}

The size of the initial condition

is important, as we shall see in the end of section 5,

Note that the kinetic energy is

(1.18)

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\{

\displaystyle\nabla_{x_{1}}\cdot\nabla_{x_{2}}\Gamma(t)\}=\int dx\Big\{|\nabla_{x}\phi(t,x)|^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2N}\int dx_{1}dx_{2}\Big\{|\nabla_{x_{1}}\mbox{\rm sh}(k)(t,x_{1},x_{2})|^{2}+|\nabla_{x_{2}}\mbox{\rm sh}(k)(t,x_{1},x_{2})|^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle+\big\|P_{|\eta|<20N}\Lambda\big\|_{collapsing}.

If

, and we use

and

.

Define

\langle\nabla_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}f=(1-\Delta_{x})^{\frac{1}{4}}f

.

Let

, define

(2.10)

\displaystyle{\bf S}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i}\frac{\partial}{dt}-\Delta_{x}-\Delta_{y}

\displaystyle{\bf S}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{x}+\Delta_{y}

Consider the equation

\displaystyle{\bf S}\Lambda(t,x,y)

\displaystyle+N^{2}v\big(N(x-y))H(t,x,y)

\displaystyle\Lambda(0,\cdot)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{0}

.

3.
Preliminary estimates for solutions to the linear Schrödinger equation

We will use the following Strichartz estimate. In 6+1 dimensions,

Theorem 3.1

(Theorem 2.4, 2.5 of
[
8
]
)
.

Let
{\bf S}u=f+g,\,\,u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}
. Then

\|u\|_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{\frac{6}{5}}(x-y)L^{2}(d(x+y))}+\|g\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{r}}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Now we shall present the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

{\bf S}u=f\,\,u(0,\cdot)=0

Then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{S}_{x,y}}\lesssim\big\|\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}f\big\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}+\big\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}f\big\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}.

Remark 3.3
.

A frequency localized version of the above Theorem appears in
[
7
]
, Proposition 4.7, which is also a motivation of the above theorem. The
{\mathcal{S}_{x,y}}
norm is crucial here, we do not expect the above estimate to be true if we replace it by the full Strichartz norm
{\mathcal{S}}
as in (
2.2
).

Proof.

The main ideas is to divide the frequency support of
u
into several regions, and use Strichartz estimate for the regions where
\tau\sim|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}
, and use Sobolev for the remaining regions. As we shall see later, the proof of Theorem
3.4
-
3.5
below uses essentially the same idea.

To begin with,
we shall use the decomposition
u=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{|\xi-\eta|\sim 2^{k}}u
, where for the case
k=0
, we are abusing notations a bit by letting
P_{|\xi-\eta|\sim 1}u
to denote the operator
P_{|\xi-\eta|<1}u
. We have the square function estimate(see e.g., Lemma 3.5 in
[
7
]
).

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{S}_{x,y}}

\displaystyle\sim\|\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|P_{|\xi-\eta|\sim 2^{k}}u|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{x,y}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|P_{|\xi-\eta|\sim 2^{k}}u\|_{\mathcal{S}_{x,y}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We shall focus on the dyadic pieces where
k\geq 1
, since by the Strichartz estimate and the Sobolev estimate, one can easily show that

\|P_{|\xi-\eta|<1}u\|_{\mathcal{S}_{x,y}}\lesssim\big\|\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}f\big\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}.

Now let
u_{k}=P_{|\xi-\eta|\sim 2^{k}}u
,
f_{k}=P_{|\xi-\eta|\sim 2^{k}}f
, and decompose
u_{k}=u_{k}^{1}+u_{k}^{2}+u_{k}^{3}
, where

(3.2)

\displaystyle{\bf S}\,u_{k}^{1}

\displaystyle=P_{10|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq 2^{k}}f_{k},\,\,\,\,\text{with initial conditions 0}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\,u_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{F}\Big(P_{10|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2^{k}}f_{k}\Big)}{\tau+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}},\,\,\text{this no longer has initial conditions 0}

\displaystyle{\bf S}\,u_{k}^{3}

\displaystyle=0,\,\,\,\,\text{a correction so that $u_{k}^{2}+u_{k}^{3}$ has initial condition 0}.

For
u_{k}^{1}
, by the Strichartz estimate

\displaystyle\|u_{k}^{1}\|_{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{k}\|_{{L^{2}(dt)L^{\frac{6}{5}}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y))}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{k}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\frac{k}{2}}\|f_{k}\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))},

where in the last line we used the fact that
f_{k}
is frequency supported in
|\xi-\eta|\sim 2^{k}
and Bernstein’s inequality, which is a (elementary) generalization of the classical Bernstein’s inequality to
L^{2}
valued functions.

Now we make another dyadic decomposition, write

(3.3)

f_{k}=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}P_{|\tau|\sim 2^{2k+\ell}}f_{k}=\sum_{\ell}f_{k,\ell}.

Note that for each fixed
\ell,k
, we have

\displaystyle\|f_{k,\ell}\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}\sim

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{\ell}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}f_{k,\ell}\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{\ell}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}P_{|\tau|\sim 2^{2k+\ell}}f\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}

where in the first line we used Bernstein’s inequality, and in the second line we used the fact that

(3.4)

\|P_{|\xi-\eta|\sim 2^{k}}f\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))},

which can be proved, for example, using the generalized Young’s inequality on the space of
L^{2}
valued functions.

Thus, by Minkowski’s inequality

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|u_{k}^{1}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{x,y}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{k}\|\sum_{\ell}f_{k,\ell}\|^{2}_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{k}\|f_{k,\ell}\|^{2}_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell}2^{-\frac{\ell}{4}}\Big(\sum_{k}\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}P_{|\tau|\sim 2^{2k+\ell}}f\|^{2}_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell}2^{-\frac{\ell}{4}}\Big(\|\big(\sum_{k}||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}P_{|\tau|\sim 2^{2k+\ell}}f|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2}_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}f\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))},

where in the last line we used the square function estimate in
t
variable.

For
u_{k}^{2}
, the denominator is comparable with
|\xi-\eta|^{2}+|\xi+\eta|^{2}\geq 2^{2k}\geq 100\tau
. Thus, by Sobolev’s estimates at an angle, which is Lemma 3.2 in
[
7
]
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|u_{k}^{2}\|

\displaystyle{}_{L^{2}(dt)L^{6}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}+\|u_{k}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{6}(dy)L^{2}(dx)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle u_{k}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Big(\langle\nabla_{x-y}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle\Big)^{-2}\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle f_{k}\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}.

This is also the place where we require the norm on the left side to be
{\mathcal{S}_{x,y}}
, since we do not have Sobolev-type estimates like

\displaystyle\|u_{k}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{6}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y))}\lesssim\|\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle u_{k}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}.

Let us first assume
|\xi+\eta|\leq|\xi-\eta|
, and make the
decomposition

(3.6)

f_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}P_{|\xi+\eta|\sim 2^{k-j}}f_{k}=\sum_{j}f_{k,j}.

Then for each fixed
k,j
, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|\Big(

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{x-y}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle\Big)^{-2}\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle f_{k,j}\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\|\langle\nabla_{x-y}\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}}\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{k,j}\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\|\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{k,j}\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\|\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}P_{|\xi+\eta|\sim 2^{k-j}}f\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))},

where in the third line we used Bernstein’s inequality, and in the last line we used the fact that

\|f_{k}\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}.

Thus, by Minkowski’s inequality

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|u_{k}^{2}\|

{}^{2}_{L^{2}(dt)L^{6}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}+\|u_{k}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{6}(dy)L^{2}(dx)}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\sum_{j}\big(\langle\nabla_{x-y}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle\big)^{-2}\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle f_{k,j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j}\Big(\sum_{k}\|\big(\langle\nabla_{x-y}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle\big)^{-2}\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle f_{k,\ell}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j}2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\Big(\sum_{k}\|\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}P_{|\xi+\eta|\sim 2^{k-j}}f\|^{2}_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j}2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\Big(\|\big(\sum_{k}|\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}P_{|\xi+\eta|\sim 2^{k-j}}f|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2}_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}

where in the last line we used the square function estimate in
x+y
variable.

The case
|\xi+\eta|\geq|\xi-\eta|
is similar.

For the other endpoint
p=\infty
, define

(3.8)

u_{k}^{2}=\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq k/2}P_{|\tau|\sim 2^{2k-\ell}}u_{k}^{2}=\sum_{\ell}u^{2}_{k,\ell}

and similarly

(3.9)

f_{k}=\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq k/2}P_{|\tau|\sim 2^{2k-\ell}}f_{k}=\sum_{\ell}f_{k,\ell}.

Note that for each fixed
\ell,k
, we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|u^{2}_{k,\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}(dt)L^{2}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\int\Big|\frac{\mathcal{F}\big(f_{k,\ell}\big)}{\tau+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}}\Big|d\tau\|_{L^{2}(d\xi)L^{2}(d\eta)}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle 2^{k/2-\ell/4}\|\langle\nabla_{x-y}\rangle^{-2}|\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}f_{k,\ell}\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle 2^{-\ell/4}\|\langle\nabla_{x-y}\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}}|\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}f_{k,\ell}\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-\ell/4}\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}f_{k,\ell}\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-\ell/4}\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}P_{|\tau|\sim 2^{2k-\ell}}f\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))},

where in the third line we used Bernstein’s inequality, and in the last line we used the fact that

\|f_{k}\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}.

By Minkowski’s inequality

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|u_{k}^{2}\|

{}^{2}_{L^{\infty}(dt)L^{2}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\sum_{\ell}u_{k,\ell}^{2}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(dt)L^{2}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{k:k\geq 2\ell}\|u_{k,\ell}^{2}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(dt)L^{2}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}2^{-\ell/4}\Big(\sum_{k:k\geq 2\ell}\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}P_{|\tau|\sim 2^{2k-\ell}}f\|^{2}_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}f\|_{L^{1}(d(x-y))L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x+y))}

where in the last line we used the square function estimate in
t
variable.

To deal with

, note that since

\displaystyle\|u_{k}^{3}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{x,y}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{k}^{3}(0,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}

Thus it can be treated as in the previous case.

∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let

{\bf S}u=f\,\,u(0,\cdot)=0
.
Then

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|\langle\nabla_{x-y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{\mathcal{S}_{x,y}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\big\|\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}f\big\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{\frac{6}{5}}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y))}

We shall use the decomposition
u=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{|\xi-\eta|\sim 2^{k}}u
, and the square function estimate

Since the right side of (

(which would simplify our argument compare with the previous theorem). Here again for the case

Now let

. We shall use the same decomposition as in (

), write

.

For

, by Strichartz and Bernstein’s inequality

\displaystyle\|\langle\nabla_{x-y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k}^{1}\|_{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle\nabla_{x-y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{k}\|_{{L^{2}(dt)L^{\frac{6}{5}}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y))}}

For

, the denominator is comparable with

. Thus, by Sobolev estimates at an angle, we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|\langle\nabla_{x-y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k}^{2}\|

\displaystyle{}_{L^{2}(dt)L^{6}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}+\|\langle\nabla_{x-y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{6}(dy)L^{2}(dx)}

For the other endpoint

, since we are in the region

, we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\|\langle\nabla_{x-y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{2}_{k}

\displaystyle\|_{L^{\infty}(dt)L^{2}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{k/2}\|\int\Big|\frac{\mathcal{F}\Big(P_{10|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\cdot 2^{k}}f_{k}\Big)}{\tau+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}}\Big|d\tau\|_{L^{2}(d\xi)L^{2}(d\eta)}

\displaystyle 2^{k}\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}\langle\nabla_{x-y}\rangle^{-2}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{2}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{\frac{6}{5}}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y))}.

To deal with

Theorem 3.5
.

Let

\displaystyle{\bf S}u=f,\,\,u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}

We have

\displaystyle\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle u\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{6}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y))}

Proof.

For the homogeneous estimate, it follows from the same argument as above. However, we can not apply Christ–Kiselev lemma here to get inhomogeneous estimate since
|\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}
does not commute with
1_{[0,t]}
when we write out the solution using Duhamel’s formula. Let
{\bf S}u=g
, with
u(0,\cdot)=0
, it suffices to prove

(3.43)

\displaystyle\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}u\|_{collapsing}\lesssim\|\langle\nabla_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla_{y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}g\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{r}}.

To prove this, we shall decompose the Fourier support
\tau
and
|\xi+\eta|
of
u
into finitely many regions.

Case 1:
\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq{10}(1+|\xi+\eta|)
.

In this case, we have

\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}u\|_{collapsing}\lesssim\|\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{collapsing},

thus the desired estimates follows from Lemma
3.8
.

Case 2: If
|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}>2(|\xi|+|\eta|)
.

Write
u=u^{1}+u^{2}
, where

(3.44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\,u^{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{F}f}{\tau+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}},\,\,\text{this no longer has initial conditions 0}

\displaystyle{\bf S}\,u^{2}

\displaystyle=0,\,\,\,\,\text{a correction so that $u^{1}+u^{2}$ has initial condition 0}.

In this case, it suffices to control
u_{1}
since
u_{2}
is only supported where
|\tau|=|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}
. The goodness about
u_{1}
is that it has the same Fourier support with
f
.
The strategy is based on

\displaystyle\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{1}\|

{}_{L^{\infty}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y)dt)}=\|\tau^{\frac{1}{4}}\frac{\mathcal{F}f}{\tau+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d\tau)d(\xi+\eta)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\int|\tau|^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big|\frac{\mathcal{F}\Big(f\Big)}{\tau+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}}\Big|d(\xi-\eta)\|_{L^{2}(d\tau d(\xi+\eta))}.

It suffices to show

(3.45)

\displaystyle\|\int|\tau|^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big|\frac{\mathcal{F}f}{\tau+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}}\Big|d(\xi-\eta)\|_{L^{2}(d\tau d(\xi+\eta))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,\||\nabla_{x}|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla_{y}|^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(dt)L^{q^{\prime}}(dx)L^{2}(dy)},

as well as

(3.46)

\displaystyle\|\int|\tau|^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big|\frac{\mathcal{F}f}{\tau+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}}\Big|d(\xi-\eta)\|_{L^{2}(d\tau d(\xi+\eta))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,\||\nabla_{x}|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla_{y}|^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(dt)L^{q^{\prime}}(dy)L^{2}(dx)},

for all admissible pairs
(p,q)
, where
\frac{2}{p}=\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{q},\,\,2\leq p<\infty
.

For
2\leq p<\infty
, by Cauchy-Schwarz, we have

(3.47)

\displaystyle\mathrm{LHS}\eqref{uk1}\lesssim\|\frac{|\tau|^{\frac{1}{4}}}{|\tau|}\int_{|\xi-\eta|<|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}}|\mathcal{F}f|d(\xi-\eta)\|_{L^{2}(d\tau d(\xi+\eta))}

\displaystyle\lesssim A\big\|{|\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla_{y}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{|\nabla_{x}|}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}f\big\|_{L^{2}(dt)d(x-y)d(x+y)}

where

(3.48)

\displaystyle A=\sup_{\tau,\xi+\eta}\frac{|\tau|^{\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{p}}}{|\tau|}\left(\int_{|\xi-\eta|<|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{|\xi|^{\frac{4}{p}-1}}{|\eta|}d(\xi-\eta)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Changing variables, this is something like

\displaystyle A=\sup_{\tau,|u|<|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{|\tau|^{\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{p}}}{|\tau|}\left(\int_{|v|<|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{|u+v|^{\frac{4}{p}-1}}{|u-v|}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

After a change of variables this is reduced to
\tau=1
, and
A
is bounded.

Since by Sobolev,

(3.49)

\displaystyle\big\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle|\nabla_{y}|^{\frac{1}{2}}{|\nabla_{x}|}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}f\big\|_{L^{2}(dt)d(x-y)d(x+y)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}{|\nabla_{x}|}^{\frac{3}{q}-\frac{3}{2}}|\nabla_{y}|^{\frac{1}{2}}{|\nabla_{x}|}^{\frac{1}{2}}f\big\|_{L^{2}(dtdxdy)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\||\nabla_{x}|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla_{y}|^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(dt)L^{q^{\prime}}(dx)L^{2}(dy)}.

Thus the proof of (
3.45
) is complete, and the proof of (
3.46
) is similar.

Case 3:
|\xi|+|\eta|>2|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}

In this case, due to Case 1, we can assume additionally that
|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}>10(1+|\xi+\eta|)
.
Thus
|\xi-\eta|>|\xi+\eta|
, so also
|\xi-\eta|>|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}
. As before, it suffices to show (
3.45
) and (
3.46
).

(3.50)

\displaystyle\mathrm{LHS}\eqref{uk1}\lesssim\|\int_{2|\xi-\eta|>|\xi+\eta|+|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}}|\frac{|\tau|^{\frac{1}{4}}|\mathcal{F}f|}{|\xi-\eta|^{2}}d(\xi-\eta)\|_{L^{2}(d\tau d(\xi+\eta))}

\displaystyle\lesssim A\big\|{|\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla_{y}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{|\nabla_{x}|}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}f\big\|_{L^{2}(dt)d(x-y)d(x+y)}

In this case,

(3.51)

\displaystyle A^{2}=\sup_{\xi+\eta,\tau}\int_{2|\xi-\eta|>|\xi+\eta|+|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{|\tau|^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}}{|\xi-\eta|^{4}}\frac{|\xi|^{\frac{4}{p}-1}}{|\eta|}d(\xi-\eta)

Again we scale to
|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}+|\xi+\eta|=1
and have to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{|v|>1}\frac{1}{|v|^{4}}\frac{|u+v|^{\frac{4}{p}-1}}{|u-v|}dv

This is bounded uniformly in
|u|<1
. As before, the rest of the proof follow from Sobolev’s inequality, and the proof of (
3.46
) is similar.

Case 4 :
\frac{1}{2}(|\xi|+|\eta|)<|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}<2(|\xi|+|\eta|)
,
In this case, due to Case 1, we can assume additionally that
|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}>10(1+|\xi+\eta|)

so in this case
|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim|\xi|\sim|\eta|\sim|\xi-\eta|
.

We shall use the decomposition
u=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{|\tau|\sim 2^{k}}u
, and the square function estimate

(3.52)

\displaystyle\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}u\|

\displaystyle{}_{L^{\infty}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y)dt)}

\displaystyle\sim\|\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|P_{|\tau|\sim 2^{k}}|\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}u|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y)dt)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{2k}\|P_{|\tau|\sim 2^{k}}u\|_{{L^{\infty}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y)dt)}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For each fixed dyadic piece
P_{|\tau|\sim 2^{k}}u
, in the current case, we have

P_{|\tau|\sim 2^{k}}u=P_{|\tau|\sim 2^{k}}P_{|\xi|\sim 2^{k/2}}P_{|\eta|\sim 2^{k/2}}u,

which implies

\displaystyle\|\big|P_{|\tau|\sim 2^{k}}u\|_{collapsing}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle\nabla_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}P_{|\xi|\sim 2^{k/2}}P_{|\eta|\sim 2^{k/2}}u\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{6}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y))}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k/2}\|\langle\nabla_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla_{y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}P_{|\eta|\sim 2^{k/2}}u\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{6}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y))}

where we used Bernstein’s inequality in rotated coordinates twice, see e.g., Lemma 3.1 in
[
7
]
for more details.

Thus,

(3.53)

\displaystyle\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y)dt)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\langle\nabla_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla_{y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}P_{|\eta|\sim 2^{k/2}}u\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{6}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y))}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\langle\nabla_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla_{y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}P_{|\eta|\sim 2^{k/2}}f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle\nabla_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla_{y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}

where we used Strichartz (Theorem
3.1
) in the second line, and square function estimates in
y
the the last line.

∎

4.

In this section, we shall see how we can apply the theorems in the previous section to prove Theorem

.
Throughout this section, besides (

\displaystyle\leq\|\langle\nabla_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla_{y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-itH}\Lambda_{0}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{x,y}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\langle\nabla_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla_{y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

where the second equality is a consequence of the existence of strong limit, and in the last inequality we used the special case of Theorem

Now we shall consider the inhomogeneous equation, let

\displaystyle{\bf S}\Lambda(t,x,y)

(8.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle\nabla_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla_{y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}G\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{r}}.

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{x+y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-i(t-s)H}G(s,\cdot)ds\|_{L^{2}(dtd(x+y))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle\nabla_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla_{y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}G\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{r}}.

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla_{y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{isH}G(s,\cdot)ds\|_{L^{2}(dxdy)}

\displaystyle=\|\langle\nabla_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla_{y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{iTH}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-i(T-s)H}G(s,\cdot)ds\|_{L^{2}(dxdy)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle\nabla_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla_{y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-i(T-s)H}G(s,\cdot)ds\|_{L^{2}(dxdy)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle\nabla_{x}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla_{y}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}G\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{r}},

where in the third line we used the special case of Theorem

\displaystyle{\bf S}\,u^{2}

The strategy is based on

\displaystyle\||\partial_{t}|^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{1}\|

\tau=1

By Sobolev’s estimate at an angle, we have

\displaystyle\big\|{|\nabla_{y}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{|\nabla_{x}|}^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\big\|_{L^{2}(dtd(x-y)d(x+y))}\lesssim\||\nabla_{x}|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla_{y}|^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(dt)L^{\frac{6}{5}}(d(x-y))L^{2}(d(x+y))}.

(8.15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHS}\eqref{8'}\lesssim\|\frac{|\tau|^{\frac{1}{4}}}{|\tau|}\int_{|\xi-\eta|<|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}}|\mathcal{F}f|d(\xi-\eta)\|_{L^{2}(d\tau d(\xi+\eta))}

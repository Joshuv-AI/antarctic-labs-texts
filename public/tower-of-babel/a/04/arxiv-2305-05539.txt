Abstract
. We give a conceptually simple and essentially one-dimensional approach to Rellich inequality in Euclidean space ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . In particular, we show that the radial part and the spherical part of the standard Laplacian form an angle in [ 0 , π / 2 ] [0,\pi/2] when n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 , a property known in the works of Evans-Lewis, Machihara-Ozawa-Wadade, and Bez-Machihara-Ozawa. Our proof here is direct and short.

1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathbf{R}}_{n}=\frac{n(n-4)}{4}
.
For proper
f
, we consider the following Rellich inequality:

{\mathbf{R}}_{n}^{2}\left\|\frac{f}{|x|^{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\Delta f\|_{2}^{2},

(1.1)

where
|\cdot|
and
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denote the Euclidean distance and the
L^{2}
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Note that (
1.1
) for
n=4
is trivial,
and if one wishes to treat general functions in the Sobolev space
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
it is necessary to restrict to
n\geq 5
.
For the Laplacian, we have

\Delta=\Delta_{r}+\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}},

where
\Delta_{r}
denotes the radial Laplacian
\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{n-1}{|x|}\partial_{r}
, with the radial derivative
\partial_{r}
given by
\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nabla
,
and
\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.
Let

L_{j}=\partial_{j}-\frac{x_{j}}{|x|}\partial_{r},\quad j=1,\cdots,n,

viewed as spherical-type derivatives.
Thus we have the following relation

\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}=|x|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{j}^{2}.

Note that
-\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{j}^{2}
is a non-negative operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Recently in
[
MOW17
]
and
[
BMO23
]
,
Machihara-Ozawa-Wadade and Bez-Machihara-Ozawa took the perspective of proving the Rellich inequalities in the framework of equalities,
and found the following beautiful equalities:
for
n\geq 2
and
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
,

\|\Delta f\|_{2}^{2}=\left\|\Delta_{r}f\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{j}^{2}f\right\|_{2}^{2}+2{\mathbf{R}}_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|\frac{L_{j}f}{|x|}\right\|_{2}^{2}+2\left\langle-\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{j}^{2}f_{*},f_{*}\right\rangle,

(1.2)

where
f_{*}=\partial_{r}f+\frac{n-4}{2}\frac{f}{|x|}
.
Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The last two terms in (
1.2
) are non-negative for
n\geq 4
.
This property can be found in Evans-Lewis’ continuation
[
EL05
]
of Laptev-Weidl type Hardy inequalities
[
LW99
]
.
Our aim here is to expose a direct, simple, and short proof of this property that is also independent of the explicit representation in (
1.2
) via
L_{j}
and
f_{*}
.

For convenience, set

\Delta_{s}=\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{j}^{2}.

Theorem 1.1

(Evans-Lewis)
.

For
n\geq 4
and
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
,

\text{{\rm Re}}\,\langle\Delta_{r}f,\Delta_{s}f\rangle\geq 0,

(1.3)

hence

\|\Delta f\|^{2}_{2}\geq\|\Delta_{r}f\|^{2}_{2}+\|\Delta_{s}f\|^{2}_{2}.

(1.4)

Remark 1.2
.

As observed in
[
BMO23
]
, for
n=3
one has

\|\Delta f\|^{2}_{2}\geq\|\Delta_{r}f\|^{2}_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\Delta f\|^{2}_{2}\geq\left(\frac{5}{8}\right)^{2}\|\Delta_{s}f\|^{2}_{2}.

In this
n=3
case it is interesting to have a refined angle description for (
1.3
).

For more on Rellich inequalities, see the references in
[
EL05
,
MOW17
,
BMO23
]
.
For related ideas on homogeneous groups, see Ruzhansky and Suragan
[
RS17
,
RS19
]
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
g
be a function defined on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.
A simple integration-by-parts argument shows that
\frac{d}{dr}
is a dissipative operator in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},r^{\alpha}dr)
for
\alpha\geq 0
1
1

1

See for example Hayashi-Ozawa’s revisit
[
HO17
, Section 3]
for Tosio Kato’s insightful and far-reaching extension
[
Kat71
]

of Landau-Kolmogorov inequalities to dissipative operators.
.
Hence, for
n\geq 4
,

\text{{\rm Re}}\,\left\langle\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}g,\frac{g}{r^{2}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},r^{n-1}dr)}\leq 0.

Also note that
\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{(n-2)-1}{r}\partial_{r}
is a dissipative operator in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},r^{(n-2)-1}dr)
, i.e.,

\text{{\rm Re}}\,\left\langle\left(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{(n-2)-1}{r}\partial_{r}\right)g,\frac{g}{r^{2}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},r^{n-1}dr)}\leq 0,

seen as/via the dissipativity of the Laplacian on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-2}
restricted to radial functions
2
2

2

This change-of-dimension trick is useful in wave propagation and its application to Strichartz estimates in the non-radial regime,
see for example Kato-Nakamura-Ozawa
[
KNO07
, Lemma 2.1]
.
.
The above two inequalities via the expansion into spherical harmonics lead to (
1.3
).

Remark 2.1
.

Using polar coordinates and Fubini theorem, we see that

\|\Delta_{r}f\|^{2}_{2}\geq{\mathbf{R}}_{n}^{2}\left\|\frac{f}{|x|^{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2},\quad f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}),

can be reduced to weighted Rellich inequalities on the half line, see
[
HS23
]
.
Combining this with (
1.4
) proposes an essentially one-dimensional approach to (
1.1
).

Remark 2.2
.

All together we reduce the Rellich inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to the dissipativity of
\frac{d}{dr}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},r^{n-4}dr)
for
n\geq 4
.
This is the essential part where
n\geq 4
plays a role.

A bundle perspective on contextuality Empirical models and simplicial distributions on bundle scenarios
Rui Soares Barbosa, Aziz Kharoof, Cihan Okay

Abstract
This paper provides a bundle perspective to contextuality by introducing new categories of contextuality scenarios based on bundles of simplicial complexes and simplicial sets. The former approach generalizes earlier work on the sheaf-theoretic perspective on contextuality, and the latter extends simplicial distributions, a more recent approach to contextuality formulated in the language of simplicial sets. After constructing our bundle categories, we also construct functors that relate them and natural isomorphisms that allow us to compare the notions of contextuality formulated in two languages. We are motivated by applications to the resource theory of contextuality, captured by the morphisms in these categories. In this paper, we develop the main formalism and leave applications to future work.

In the sheaf-theoretic framework, a scenario is described by a pair
(\Sigma,O)
consisting of a simplicial complex
\Sigma
and a family
O=\{O_{x}\}_{x\in\Sigma_{0}}
of sets indexed by the vertices of the simplicial complex.
Each vertex
x
represents a measurement with outcomes in the set
O_{x}
, and each simplex
\sigma\in\Sigma
represents a measurement context, a set of measurements that can be jointly performed.
It is convenient to represent the simplicial complex
\Sigma
as a category
\mathbf{C}_{\Sigma}

whose objects are the simplices of
\Sigma
and the morphisms are given by inclusions.
Then both measurements and the corresponding outcomes can be described as a functor

\mathcal{E}_{O}:\mathbf{C}_{\Sigma}\to\mathbf{Set}

that sends a measurement context
\sigma
to the set
\mathcal{E}_{O}(\sigma)=\prod_{x\in\sigma}O_{x}
of all possible outcome assignments.
A morphism
(\Sigma,O)\to(\Sigma^{\prime},O^{\prime})
between two scenarios
consists of two parts: a functor
\pi:\mathbf{C}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\to\mathbf{C}_{\Sigma}
induced by a simplicial relation that relates the measurement contexts, and a natural transformation
\alpha:\mathcal{E}_{O}\circ\pi\to\mathcal{E}_{O^{\prime}}
that specifies a mapping of outcomes over each context.
Since measurements in a context can be simultaneously performed, outcome statistics are represented by a probability distribution
e_{\sigma}
on the set
\mathcal{E}_{O}(\sigma)
of joint outcomes. The family
e=\{e_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
of distributions, called an empirical model, is required to satisfy an additional condition known as the non-signaling (or no-disturbance) condition: the measurement statistics over two contexts should
match on their intersection.
Morphisms of the category of scenarios are defined so that an empirical model
e
on
(\Sigma,O)
can be pushed forward along a morphism to produce a new empirical model on
(\Sigma^{\prime},O^{\prime})
. Operationally this coincides with the notion of simulating the empirical model
e
with the new one. By the push-forward construction, empirical models on scenarios can be turned into a functor

\mathsf{Emp}:\mathbf{Scen}\to\mathbf{Set}

Since we can consider probabilistic mixtures of empirical models, the target can be replaced by the category
\mathbf{Conv}_{R}
of convex sets for our choice of a semiring
R

Our first contribution in this paper is the notion of bundle scenario: A map

•

Discrete over vertices: Every two vertex of

To a scenario

Throughout the paper

Let

3
The category of bundle scenarios

In this section we introduce bundle scenarios of simplicial complexes and morphisms between them.
The resulting category
\mathbf{bScen}
is the category of bundle scenarios. We extend empirical models to bundle scenarios and assemble them into a functor
\mathsf{Emp}:\mathbf{bScen}\to\mathbf{Set}
. Our main result is Theorem
3.18
proving that in the left-hand diagram in (
1
) the functor
\mathcal{E}
is fully faithful and
\eta
is a natural isomorphism.
Most of the technical results concerning simplicial complexes are presented in Section
A
.

In the context of simplicial complexes locality is captured by the star construction:
The
star of a simplex

\sigma\in\Sigma
is defined by

\mathsf{St}(\sigma)=\{\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma:\,\sigma\subset\sigma^{\prime}\}.

Definition 3.1
.

A map of
f:\Gamma\to\Sigma
simplicial complexes is called

•

surjective
if it is surjective as a function between the set of simplices,

•

locally surjective
if
f|_{\gamma}:\mathsf{St}(\gamma)\to\mathsf{St}(f(\gamma))
is surjective for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
,

•

discrete over vertices

if
\{x,y\}\notin\Gamma
for every distinct
x,y\in\Gamma_{0}
with

f(x)=f(y)
.

A
bundle scenario
is a surjective, locally surjective map
f:\Gamma\to\Sigma
of simplicial complexes that is discrete over vertices.

The two properties, local surjectivity and being discrete over vertices,
can be characterized using lifting conditions (Propositions
A.2
and
A.3
). Therefore bundle scenarios can also be described in terms of lifting conditions (Corollary
A.4
).
This point of view will be fruitful when extending these notions to simplicial sets in Section
4
.

Our canonical example of a bundle scenario comes from a scenario
(\Sigma,O)
.
Associated to this scenario we can construct a bundle scenario

f_{(\Sigma,O)}:\mathcal{E}_{O}\Sigma\to\Sigma

where
\mathcal{E}_{O}\Sigma
is the simplicial complex with vertex set
(\mathcal{E}_{O}\Sigma)_{0}=\sqcup_{x\in\Sigma_{0}}O_{x}
and the set of simplices

\mathcal{E}_{O}\Sigma=\{(\sigma,s):\,\sigma\in\Sigma,\;s\in\prod_{x\in\sigma}O_{x}\}.

Each simplex
(\sigma,s)
is regarded as the subset
\sqcup_{x\in\sigma}s(x)\subset(\mathcal{E}_{O}\Sigma)_{0}
.
The simplicial complex map
f_{(\Sigma,O)}
is given by projection onto the first coordinate.

Proposition 3.2
.

The simplicial complex map

f_{(\Sigma,O)}:\mathcal{E}_{O}\Sigma\to\Sigma
is a bundle scenario.

Proof.

The outcome sets
O_{x}
being nonempty implies that
f=f_{(\Sigma,O)}
is surjective.
For a simplex
(\sigma,s)
consider the map between the stars

f_{(\sigma,s)}:\mathsf{St}(\sigma,s)\to\mathsf{St}(\sigma).

Given an element of
\mathsf{St}(\sigma)
, that is a simplex
\sigma^{\prime}
containing
\sigma
, consider
s^{\prime}\in\prod_{x\in\sigma^{\prime}}O_{x}
such that
s^{\prime}|_{\sigma}=s
. Then
(\sigma^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in\mathsf{St}(\sigma,s)
and it maps to
\sigma^{\prime}
under
f_{(\sigma,s)}
. This implies that
f
is locally surjective.
Finally,
f
is discrete over vertices since for
x\in\Sigma_{0}
and distinct
o,o^{\prime}\in O_{x}
we have
\{(x,o),(x,o^{\prime})\}\notin\mathcal{E}_{O}\Sigma
.
∎

\mathcal{E}_{O}\Sigma
that uses the notion of the category of elements of a functor.

Definition 3.3
.

Given a contravariant functor

.

(\hat{N}f)(\gamma)=f(\gamma)
.

The functor
\hat{N}
is in fact a monad, i.e., comes with two natural transformations:

•

\delta:\text{id}\to\hat{N}
whose component
\delta_{\Sigma}:\Sigma\to\hat{N}\Sigma
is defined by
\delta_{\Sigma}(x)=\{x\}
, and

•

\mu:\hat{N}\circ\hat{N}\to\hat{N}
whose component
\mu_{\Sigma}:\hat{N}^{2}\Sigma\to\hat{N}\Sigma
is defined by
\mu_{\Sigma}(\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}\})=\cup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}
.

Associated to a monad one can consider the Kleisli category
[
17
]
. For the nerve complex functor the Kleisli category
\mathbf{sComp}_{\hat{N}}
consists of simplicial complexes as objects and its morphisms are given by

\mathbf{sComp}_{\hat{N}}(\Sigma^{\prime},\Sigma)=\mathbf{sComp}(\Sigma^{\prime},\hat{N}\Sigma).

Proposition 3.5
.

The set of simplicial relations
\pi:\Sigma^{\prime}\to\Sigma
is in bijection with the set of morphisms
\Sigma^{\prime}\to\Sigma
in the Kleisli category
\mathbf{sComp}_{\hat{N}}
.

Proof.

A simplicial complex map
\Sigma^{\prime}\to\hat{N}\Sigma
is given by a function
\pi:\Sigma^{\prime}_{0}\to\Sigma
such that
\pi(\sigma^{\prime})=\cup_{x^{\prime}\in\sigma^{\prime}}\pi(x^{\prime})
is a simplex of
\Sigma
for all
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
. This is precisely a simplicial relation.
∎

We will regard simplicial relations as morphisms in the Kleisli category.
Given simplicial complex maps
\pi:\Sigma^{\prime}\to\hat{N}\Sigma
and
\pi^{\prime}:\Sigma^{\prime\prime}\to\hat{N}\Sigma^{\prime}
we write
\pi\diamond\pi^{\prime}:\Sigma^{\prime\prime}\to\hat{N}\Sigma
for the Kleisli composition, i.e., the composition in the category
\mathbf{sComp}_{\hat{N}}
.

3.2
Empirical models on bundle scenarios

A simplicial complex map that is discrete over vertices satisfies a very useful property which we will refer to through out the paper:

Remark 3.10
.

Let
f:\Gamma\to\Sigma
be a surjective simplicial complex map that is discrete over vertices.
For simplices
\sigma^{\prime}\subset\sigma\in\Sigma
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
with
f(\gamma)=\sigma
, there exists a unique simplex
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
such that
f(\gamma^{\prime})=\sigma^{\prime}
since
f
is discrete over vertices.

We will write

r_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}:f^{-1}(\sigma)\to f^{-1}(\sigma^{\prime})

for the map sending
\gamma
to the corresponding unique simplex
\gamma^{\prime}
specified by Remark
3.10
.
For a distribution
p\in D_{R}(f^{-1}(\sigma))
we define the restriction
p|_{\sigma^{\prime}}=D_{R}(r_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}})(p)
.
With this definition we have

(12)

p|_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\gamma^{\prime})=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\subset\gamma\in f^{-1}(\sigma)}p(\gamma).

Definition 3.11
.

An

is a family
p=(p_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
of distributions, where
p_{\sigma}\in D_{R}(f^{-1}(\sigma))
, such that
p_{\sigma}|_{\sigma^{\prime}}=p_{\sigma^{\prime}}
for every
\sigma^{\prime}\subset\sigma
in
\Sigma
.
We will denote the set of empirical models on
f
by
\mathsf{bEmp}(f)
.

.

•

, i.e., the diagonal map exists

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Let

4.1
Morphisms of simplicial scenarios

Defining morphisms between simplicial scenarios is easier than defining morphisms between bundle scenarios. In Section
3.1
, we introduced morphisms between bundle scenarios in two steps by defining type I and II morphisms. For simplicial scenarios, we can define morphisms in one step. Therefore this approach, while generalizing bundles of simplicial complexes, has a cleaner description of morphisms.

Definition 4.3
.

The category

\mathbf{sScen}
of simplicial (bundle) scenarios consists of objects given by simplicial (bundle) scenarios and morphisms
f\to f^{\prime}
given by pairs
(\pi,\alpha)
of simplicial set maps making the following diagram commute

(16)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}(E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\pi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

where
\pi^{*}(E)
is the pull-back along
\pi
.

Remark 4.4
.

The map
f^{\pi}
in Diagram (
16
) is also a simplicial scenario since pulling back a map preserves surjectivity, local surjectivity, and being discrete over vertices.
The latter two properties are described in terms of the liftings of certain diagrams, and liftings still exist under pull-backs.

The composition of
(\pi_{1},\alpha_{1}):f\to f^{\prime}
and
(\pi_{2},\alpha_{2}):f^{\prime}\to f^{\prime\prime}
is defined by

(17)

(\pi_{2},\alpha_{2})\circ(\pi_{1},\alpha_{1})=(\pi_{1}\circ\pi_{2},\alpha_{2}\circ\pi_{2}^{*}(\alpha_{1}))

where

\pi_{2}^{*}(\alpha_{1}):\pi_{2}^{*}(\pi_{1}^{*}(E))\to\pi_{2}^{*}(E^{\prime})

is the pull-back of
\alpha_{1}
along
\pi_{2}
.
This gives a well-defined category: The identity morphism of an object
f:E\to X
is given by
(\text{id}_{X},\text{id}_{E})
and the composition rule is associative as we prove next.

Lemma 4.5
.

The composition given by Equation (
17
) is associative.

Proof.

For
(\pi_{1},\alpha_{1}):f\to f^{\prime}
,
(\pi_{2},\alpha_{2}):f^{\prime}\to f^{\prime\prime}
, and
(\pi_{3},\alpha_{3}):f^{\prime\prime}\to f^{\prime\prime\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle(\pi_{3},\alpha_{3})\circ\left((\pi_{2},\alpha_{2})\circ(\pi_{1},\alpha_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=(\pi_{3},\alpha_{3})\circ(\pi_{1}\circ\pi_{2},\alpha_{2}\circ\pi_{2}^{\ast}(\alpha_{1}))

\displaystyle=\left((\pi_{1}\circ\pi_{2})\circ\pi_{3},\alpha_{3}\circ{\pi_{3}}^{\ast}\left(\alpha_{2}\circ\pi_{2}^{\ast}(\alpha_{1})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\pi_{1}\circ(\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{3}),\alpha_{3}\circ{\pi_{3}}^{\ast}(\alpha_{2})\circ{\pi_{3}}^{\ast}(\pi_{2}^{\ast}(\alpha_{1}))\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\pi_{1}\circ(\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{3}),\alpha_{3}\circ{\pi_{3}}^{\ast}(\alpha_{2})\circ(\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{3})^{\ast}(\alpha_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=(\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{3},\alpha_{3}\circ\pi_{3}^{\ast}(\alpha_{2}))\circ(\pi_{1},\alpha_{1}))

\displaystyle=\left((\pi_{3},\alpha_{3})\circ(\pi_{2},\alpha_{2})\right)\circ(\pi_{1},\alpha_{1}).

∎

Next we turn to the nerve functor
N:\mathbf{sComp}\to\mathbf{sSet}
.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\pi:\Sigma^{\prime}\to\hat{N}\Sigma
be a simplicial complex map and

denote the functor

.
We define

between two bundle scenarios

and

we define a morphism of

by the diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N(\pi^{*}(\hat{N}\Gamma))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N\Gamma^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N\Sigma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N\Sigma^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N\Sigma^{\prime}}

since the left square is a pull-back diagram (Lemma

), i.e.,

.

Proposition 4.7
.

The assignments

and
(\pi,\alpha)\mapsto(T(\pi),N\alpha)
specify a fully faithful functor

N:\mathbf{bScen}\to\mathbf{sScen}.

Proof.

For a bundle scenario
f:\Gamma\to\Sigma
the simplicial map

is a simplicial scenario by Proposition

. In addition, by Lemma
B.9
we have that

N(\delta_{\Sigma},\text{id}_{\Gamma})=(T(\delta_{\Sigma}),N(\text{id}_{\Gamma}))=(\text{id}_{N\Sigma},\text{id}_{N\Gamma})
.
Given two morphisms

(\pi,\alpha):f\to f^{\prime}
and

of

, by Lemmas

and

, we have

\displaystyle N((\pi^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})\circ(\pi,\alpha))

\displaystyle=N(\pi\diamond\pi^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}\circ(\pi^{\prime})^{\ast}(\hat{N}\alpha))

\displaystyle=(T(\pi\diamond\pi^{\prime}),N\left(\alpha^{\prime}\circ(\pi^{\prime})^{\ast}(\hat{N}\alpha)\right))

\displaystyle=(T(\pi)\circ T(\pi^{\prime}),N(\alpha^{\prime})\circ N\left((\pi^{\prime})^{\ast}(\hat{N}\alpha)\right))

4.2
Simplicial distributions on bundles

Definition 4.8
.

Let
f:E\to X
be a simplicial set map.
A
simplicial distribution
on
f

is a simplicial set map
p:X\to D_{R}(E)
that makes the following diagram commute:

(19)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{R}(E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{R}(X)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{R}(f)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}

We write
\mathsf{sDist}(f)

for the set of simplicial
distributions on
f
.

Proposition 4.9
.

A map
p:X\to D_{R}(E)
belongs to
\mathsf{sDist}(f)
if and only if
the support of
p_{n}(x)
is contained in
f_{n}^{-1}(x)
for all
x\in X_{n}
.

Proof.

The map
p
belongs to

\mathsf{sDist}(f)
if and only if for every
x\in X_{n}
we have

\sum_{e\in E_{n}}p_{n}(x)(e)\delta^{f_{n}(e)}=\delta^{x}.

Since
R
is a zero-sum-free semiring, this condition holds if and only if
p_{n}(x)(e)=0
for every
x\in X_{n}
and
e\in E_{n}
satisfying
f_{n}(e)\neq x
.
∎

Given simplicial sets
X
and
Y
we define

m:D_{R}(X)\times D_{R}(Y)\to D_{R}(X\times Y)

by sending
(p,q)\in D_{R}(X_{n})\times D_{R}(Y_{n})
to the distribution
p\cdot q
defined by

(20)

p\cdot q(\sigma,\theta)=p(\sigma)q(\theta)

for
\sigma\in X_{n}
and
\theta\in Y_{n}
.
It is straightforward to verify that Equation (
20
) respects the face and the degeneracy maps. For instance, for the face maps we have

\displaystyle D_{R}(d_{i}^{X}\times d_{i}^{Y})(m_{{n+1}}(p,q))(\sigma,\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d^{X}_{i}(\sigma^{\prime})=\sigma,\,d^{Y}_{i}(\theta^{\prime})=\theta}m_{{n+1}}(p,q)(\sigma^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{d^{X}_{i}(\sigma^{\prime})=\sigma,\,d^{Y}_{i}(\theta^{\prime})=\theta}p(\sigma^{\prime})q(\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{d^{X}_{i}(\sigma^{\prime})=\sigma}p(\sigma^{\prime})\sum_{d^{Y}_{i}(\theta^{\prime})=\theta}q(\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=D_{R}(d_{i}^{X})(p)(\sigma)\cdot D_{R}(d_{i}^{Y})(q)(\theta)

\displaystyle=m_{{n}}(D_{R}(d_{i}^{X})(p),D_{R}(d_{i}^{Y})(q))(\sigma,\theta).

The canonical map that goes in the other direction

D_{R}(\text{pr}_{1})\times D_{R}(\text{pr}_{2}):D_{R}(X\times Y)\to D_{R}(X)\times D_{R}(Y)

splits
m
, i.e.,
(D_{R}(\text{pr}_{1})\times D_{R}(\text{pr}_{2}))\circ m=\text{id}
.

Remark 4.10
.

Given a pair
(X,Y)
of simplicial sets consider the projection map

f_{(X,Y)}:X\times Y\to X

Simplicial distributions on
f_{(X,Y)}
coincide with simplicial distributions on
(X,Y)
in the sense of
[
2
]
, that is, with simplicial set maps
X\to D_{R}(Y)
.
To see this correspondence,
let
p
be a simplicial distribution in the sense of Definition
4.8
.
For every
\sigma\in X_{n}
we have that

(21)

{\sum_{\theta\in Y_{n}}p_{n}(\sigma)(-,\theta)=\delta^{\sigma}.}

This implies that
\text{supp}({p_{n}(\sigma)})
consists of pairs
(\sigma,\theta)
where
\theta\in\text{supp}({p_{n}(\sigma)(\sigma,-)})
. Therefore we can identify
{p_{n}(\sigma)}
with the distribution
{p_{n}(\sigma)}(\sigma,-)\in D_{R}(Y_{n})
. Given
p
, the desired simplicial set map is the composite

X\xrightarrow{p}D_{R}(X\times Y)\xrightarrow{D_{R}(\text{pr}_{2})}D_{R}(Y)

Conversely, a simplicial set map
q:X\to D_{R}(Y)
can be lifted to
D_{R}(X\times Y)
by the following composite:

X\xrightarrow{\text{id}\times q}X\times D_{R}(Y)\xrightarrow{\delta\times\text{id}}D_{R}(X)\times D_{R}(Y)\xrightarrow{m}D_{R}(X\times Y),

which belongs to
\mathsf{sDist}(f_{(X,Y)})
by Proposition
4.9
.
Note that
f_{(X,Y)}
fails to be a simplicial scenario (Definition
4.1
): In general, it is surjective and locally surjective, but not discrete over vertices.

4.2.1
Push-forward simplicial distributions

In this section we define the push-forward of a simplicial distribution along a morphism of
\mathbf{sScen}
. As a first step we define push-forward along
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
by introducing a distribution on the middle scenario
f^{\pi}
in Diagram (
16
).

Definition 4.11
.

Given a simplicial distribution
p
on
f:E\to X
and a simplicial set map
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
, we define a simplicial distribution on
f^{\pi}
by the composition

\pi_{*}(p):X^{\prime}\xrightarrow{p\circ\pi\times\delta_{X^{\prime}}}D_{R}(E)\times D_{R}(X^{\prime})\xrightarrow{m}D_{R}(E\times X^{\prime})

whose image lands in the simplicial subset
D_{R}(\pi^{*}E)
.

Let us unravel this construction.
Consider a simplicial distribution
p:X\to D_{R}(E)
on
f
.
The first observation which makes this construction work is that

D_{R}(f)\circ p\circ\pi=\delta_{X}\circ\pi=D_{R}(\pi)\circ\delta_{X^{\prime}}.

Therefore
p\circ\pi\times\delta_{X^{\prime}}
lands inside the pull-back
(D_{R}\pi)^{*}(D_{R}E)
.
The second observation is that for
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}_{n}
and
(e,x)\in E_{n}\times X_{n}
such that
f_{n}(e)\neq\pi_{n}(x)
, Proposition
4.9
implies that

p_{n}(\pi_{n}(x^{\prime}))(e)\cdot\delta^{x^{\prime}}(x)=0\cdot\delta^{x^{\prime}}(x)=0
. Therefore

m\circ(p\circ\pi\times\delta_{X^{\prime}})
lands inside the space of distributions
D_{R}(\pi^{*}E)
on the pull-back.
In general, for
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}_{n}
, we have the following formula

(22)

(\pi_{\ast}p)_{n}(x^{\prime})=p_{n}(\pi_{n}(x^{\prime}))\cdot\delta^{x^{\prime}},

which shows that
\pi_{\ast}p(x^{\prime})\in(f^{\pi})^{-1}(x^{\prime})
. Therefore
\pi_{\ast}(p)\in\mathsf{sDist}(f^{\pi})
by Proposition
4.9
.

Definition 4.12
.

Given a simplicial distribution
p
on
f:E\to X
and a morphism
(\pi,\alpha):f\to f^{\prime}
of simplicial scenarios we define the
push-forward
distribution, a simplicial distribution on
f^{\prime}
, by the following composite:

(23)

{(\pi,\alpha)_{*}p:X^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\pi_{\ast}(p)}D_{R}(\pi^{*}E)\xrightarrow{D_{R}(\alpha)}D_{R}(E^{\prime})}

To prove that the push-forward construction is functorial we need two preliminary results.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
p
be a simplicial distribution on
f:E\to X
.
For simplicial set maps
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
and
\pi^{\prime}:X^{\prime\prime}\to X^{\prime}
, we have
(\pi\circ\pi^{\prime})_{\ast}p={\pi^{\prime}}_{\ast}(\pi_{\ast}p)
.

Proof.

Given
x\in X_{n}^{\prime\prime}
Equation (
22
) implies that

(\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}(\pi_{\ast}p))_{n}(x)=(\pi_{\ast}p)_{n}(\pi^{\prime}_{n}(x))\cdot\delta^{x}=p_{n}\left(\pi_{n}(\pi^{\prime}_{n}(x))\right)\cdot\delta^{\pi^{\prime}_{n}(x)}\cdot\delta^{x}.

Therefore if we apply this map to
(e,\pi^{\prime}_{n}(x),x)\in((\pi^{\prime})^{\ast}\pi^{\ast}(E))_{n}
, we obtain
p_{n}\left(\pi_{n}(\pi^{\prime}_{n}(x))\right)(e)
.
On the other hand,

(\pi\circ\pi^{\prime})_{\ast}p(x)=p_{n}(\pi_{n}\circ\pi^{\prime}_{n}(x))\cdot\delta^{x}.

If we apply this map to the corresponding element
(e,x)\in(\pi\circ\pi^{\prime})^{\ast}(E)_{n}
, we obtain the same result.
∎

Lemma 4.14
.

Given a simplicial set map
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
,
a commutative diagram of simplicial set maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

and a distribution
p\in\mathsf{sDist}(f)
, we have

\pi_{\ast}(D_{R}(\alpha)\circ p)=D_{R}(\pi^{\ast}(\alpha))\circ\pi_{\ast}p.

Proof.

Given
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}_{n}
and
(e^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in\pi^{\ast}(E^{\prime})_{n}
, using Equation (
22
) we have

\displaystyle\pi_{\ast}(D_{R}(\alpha)\circ p)_{n}(x^{\prime})(e^{\prime},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(D_{R}(\alpha)\circ p)_{n}\left(\pi_{n}(x^{\prime})\right)(e^{\prime})

\displaystyle=D_{R}(\alpha_{n})\left(p_{n}(\pi_{n}(x^{\prime}))\right)(e^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\,:\,\,\alpha_{n}(e)=e^{\prime}}p_{n}\left(\pi_{n}(x^{\prime})\right)(e).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle D_{R}(\pi_{\ast}(\alpha)_{n})\left((\pi_{\ast}p)_{n}(x^{\prime})\right)(e^{\prime},x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi^{\ast}(\alpha)_{n}(e,x^{\prime})=(e^{\prime},x^{\prime})}(\pi_{\ast}p)_{n}(x^{\prime})(e,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\,:\,\,\alpha_{n}(e)=e^{\prime}}p_{n}\left(\pi_{n}(x^{\prime})\right)(e).

∎

Proposition 4.15
.

The push-forward construction in Definition
4.12
gives a functor

\mathsf{sDist}:\mathbf{sScen}\to\mathbf{Set}.

Proof.

For a simplicial scenario
f:E\to X
and
p\in\mathsf{sDist}(f)
, it is clear that

(\text{id}_{X},\text{id}_{E})_{\ast}p=p
.
Applying Lemma
4.13
and
4.14

to the morphisms

(\pi,\alpha):f\to f^{\prime}
and

(\pi^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}):f^{\prime}\to f^{\prime\prime}
of
\mathbf{sScen}
we obtain

\displaystyle(\pi^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})_{\ast}\left((\pi,\alpha)_{\ast}p\right)

\displaystyle=D_{R}(\alpha^{\prime})\circ\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}((\pi,\alpha)_{\ast}p)

\displaystyle=D_{R}(\alpha^{\prime})\circ\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}(D_{R}(\alpha)\circ\pi_{\ast}p)

\displaystyle=D_{R}(\alpha^{\prime})\circ D_{R}((\pi^{\prime})^{\ast}(\alpha))\circ\pi^{\prime}(\pi_{\ast}p)

\displaystyle=D_{R}\left(\alpha^{\prime}\circ(\pi^{\prime})^{\ast}(\alpha)\right)\circ\left((\pi\circ\pi^{\prime})_{\ast}p\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\pi\circ\pi^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}\circ(\pi^{\prime})^{*}(\alpha)\right)_{\ast}p

\displaystyle=((\pi^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})\circ(\pi,\alpha))_{\ast}p.

∎

4.3
The natural isomorphism

In this section we construct a natural isomorphism
\zeta:\mathsf{bEmp}\to\mathsf{sDist}\circ N
.
The first step is to construct a simplicial distribution on the nerve of a bundle scenario from a given empirical model on the bundle.

Definition 4.16
.

Let
f:\Gamma\to\Sigma
be a bundle scenario and
p\in\mathsf{bEmp}(f)
be an empirical model. We define a simplicial distribution
Np
on the simplicial scenario
Nf:N\Gamma\to N\Sigma
as follows:

(24)

(Np)_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n})=\begin{cases}p_{\cup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}}(\cup_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i})&(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n})\in(Nf)_{n}^{-1}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where

(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})\in(N\Sigma)_{n}
.

The fact that Equation (
24
) gives a well-defined simplicial distribution is proved in Lemma
B.13
.

Proposition 4.17
.

For a bundle scenario
f:\Gamma\to\Sigma
and

p\in\mathsf{bEmp}(f)
, defining
\zeta_{f}(p)=Np
gives a natural isomorphism

\zeta:\mathsf{bEmp}\to\mathsf{sDist}\circ N
.

Proof.

Given
p,q\in\mathsf{bEmp}(f)
such that

\zeta_{f}(p)=\zeta_{f}(q)
, we have

p_{\sigma}(\gamma)=(Np)_{1}(\sigma)(\gamma)=(Nq)_{1}(\sigma)(\gamma)=q_{\sigma}(\gamma).

Therefore
\zeta_{f}
is injective.
To prove surjectivity, let
\tilde{p}\in\mathsf{sDist}(Nf)
.
For every
\sigma\in\Sigma
we define
p_{\sigma}=\tilde{p}_{1}(\sigma)
.
To see that
p\in\mathsf{bEmp}(f)
, let
\sigma^{\prime}\subset\sigma
and
\gamma^{\prime}\in f^{-1}(\sigma^{\prime})
. Then by Equation (
12
) and Lemma
B.14

we have

\displaystyle p_{\sigma}|_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\subset\gamma\in f^{-1}(\sigma)}p_{\sigma}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\subset\gamma\in f^{-1}(\sigma)}\tilde{p}_{1}({\sigma})(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau\in f^{-1}(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}):\,\gamma^{\prime}\cup\tau\in\Gamma}\tilde{p}_{1}({\sigma^{\prime}\cup(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})})(\gamma^{\prime}\cup\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau\,:\,\,(\gamma^{\prime},\tau)\in(Nf)^{-1}(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})}\tilde{p}_{2}(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})(\gamma^{\prime},\tau)

\displaystyle=D_{R}(d_{2})(\tilde{p}_{2}\left(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})\right)(\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\tilde{p_{1}}\left(d_{2}(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})\right)(\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\tilde{p_{1}}(\sigma^{\prime})(\gamma^{\prime})=p_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\gamma^{\prime}).

Again, using Lemma
B.14
we obtain that
\zeta_{f}(p)=\tilde{p}
.

Now, we prove the naturality of
\zeta
. Given a morphism
(\pi,\alpha):f\to f^{\prime}
of
\mathbf{bScen}
, we prove that the following diagram commutes

(25)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{bEmp}(f)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{bEmp}(f^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{sDist}(Nf)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{sDist}(Nf^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\pi{,}\alpha)_{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\zeta_{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\zeta_{f^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(T(\pi){,}N\alpha)_{\ast}}

For this let
p\in\mathsf{bEmp}(f)
. By Lemma
B.16
and
B.15
we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{f^{\prime}}((\pi,\alpha)_{\ast}p)

\displaystyle=N(\alpha_{\ast}(\pi_{\ast}p))=D_{R}(N\alpha)\circ N(\pi_{\ast}p)

\displaystyle=D_{R}(N\alpha)\circ T(\pi)_{\ast}(Np)

\displaystyle=(T(\pi),N\alpha)_{\ast}(Np)

\displaystyle=(T(\pi),N\alpha)_{\ast}(\zeta_{f}(p)).

∎

Combining Proposition

Appendix A
Simplicial complexes

Simplicial complexes are one type of combinatorial model we use to represent spaces in this paper. This section introduces the simplicial complex that represents the standard topological
n
-simplex. These simplices can be related by maps of simplicial complexes that come from a certain category, called the simplex category. Using these morphisms, we provide alternative characterizations of local surjectivity and being discrete over vertices.

Let
\Delta:\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{sComp}
denote the functor that sends a set
U
to the simplicial complex
\Delta^{U}
given by the power set of
U
, i.e.,
(\Delta^{U})_{0}=U
and simplicial are all nonempty subsets of
U
.
A set map
g:U^{\prime}\to U
induces a map of simplicial complexes, which will be denoted by
g:\Delta^{U^{\prime}}\to\Delta^{U}
, defined by
g(\sigma^{\prime})=\{g(x^{\prime}):\,x^{\prime}\in\sigma^{\prime}\}
for
\sigma^{\prime}\subset U^{\prime}
. Next we introduce an important subcategory known as the simplex category and consider the restriction of the functor on this category.
The
simplex category

\mathbf{\Delta}
is defined as follows
[
18
]
:

•

Objects are given by
[n]=\{0,1,\cdots,n\}
where
n\geq 0
.

•

A morphism
\theta:[m]\to[n]
is an order preserving function, that is,

\theta(i)\leq\theta(j),\;\;\forall i\leq j\in[m].

There are two kinds of distinguished maps:

d^{i}:[n-1]\to[n]\;\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\;s^{j}:[n+1]\to[n],

where the face maps
d^{i}
skips
i
in the image, and the degeneracy maps
s^{j}
has a double preimage at
j
. An arbitrary morphism
\theta
can be written as a composite of face and degeneracy maps.

We can extend this category to the augmented simplex category
\mathbf{\Delta}_{+}
by adding the initial object
[-1]=\emptyset
.
We will write
\Delta^{n}
for the simplicial complex
\Delta^{[n]}
.
The assignment
[n]\mapsto\Delta^{n}
gives a functor

\Delta:\mathbf{\Delta}_{+}\to s\mathbf{Comp}.

Definition A.1
.

Let

be a category.
We say that

has the

if for every commutative diagram in

consisting of the solid arrows

A.2
Bundles of nerve complexes

The nerve complex functor
\hat{N}:\mathbf{sComp}\to\mathbf{sComp}
given in Definition
3.4

\mathbf{bScen}
. We show that this functor preserves bundle scenarios.

Proposition A.7
.

If
f:\Gamma\to\Sigma
is a bundle scenario then
\hat{N}f:\hat{N}\Gamma\to\hat{N}\Sigma
is also a bundle scenario.

Proof.

Consider
\{\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n}\}\in\hat{N}\Sigma
. We have
\cup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\in\Sigma
. Surjectivity of
f
implies that
there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
f(\gamma)=\cup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}
.

\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\subseteq\gamma
such that
f(\gamma_{i})=\sigma_{i}
.
This shows that
\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\}\in\hat{N}\Gamma
and

\hat{N}f(\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\})=\{\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n}\}
.
Now, we prove that
\hat{N}f
is discrete over vertices.
Consider distinct vertices
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
in
\hat{N}\Gamma
such that

\hat{N}f(\gamma_{1})=\hat{N}f(\gamma_{2})
.
This means that
f(\gamma_{1})=f(\gamma_{2})
. Since
f
is discrete over vertices we get that
|\gamma_{1}|=|\gamma_{2}|
.
On the other hand,
f(\gamma_{1}\cup\gamma_{2})=f(\gamma_{1})\cup f(\gamma_{2})=f(\gamma_{1})
and
|\gamma_{1}\cup\gamma_{2}|>|\gamma_{1}|
. We conclude that
\gamma_{1}\cup\gamma_{2}\notin\Gamma
, hence
\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\}\notin\hat{N}\Gamma
.
Finally, we prove that
\hat{N}f
is locally surjective. Given
\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\}\in\hat{N}\Gamma
, we want to prove the surjectivity of the restriction of

on
\mathsf{St}(\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\})
:

f|_{\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\}}:\mathsf{St}(\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\})\to\mathsf{St}(\{f(\gamma_{1}),\cdots,f(\gamma_{n})\}).

For
\{f(\gamma_{1}),\cdots,f(\gamma_{n}),\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{m}\}\in\mathsf{St}(\{f(\gamma_{1}),\cdots,f(\gamma_{n})\})
, the union

\cup_{i=1}^{n}f(\gamma_{i})\;\bigcup\;\cup_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}

\Sigma
, which implies that the union

belongs to
\mathsf{St}(f(\cup_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}))
.

is locally surjective, there exists
\cup_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}\subset\gamma\in\Gamma
such that

.

such that

. Thus
\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n},\tau_{1},\cdots,\tau_{m}\}\in\mathsf{St}(\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\})
and

\hat{N}f(\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n},\tau_{1},\cdots,\tau_{m}\})=\{f(\gamma_{1}),\cdots,f(\gamma_{n}),\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{m}\}.

∎

Appendix B
Simplicial sets

Simplicial sets are combinatorial models of spaces which have better expressive power than simplicial complexes. In this section we give basic definitions and introduce the simplicial set representing the standard topological simplex.

Definition B.1
.

A
simplicial set
is a functor
X:\mathbf{\Delta}^{\text{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
. Explicitly, a simplicial set consists of
the following data:

•

A sequence of sets
X_{0},X_{1},\cdots,X_{n},\cdots
for
n\geq 0
where
each
X_{n}
represents the set of
n
-simplices.

•

Face maps

d_{i}:X_{n}\to X_{n-1}\;\;\;\text{ for }0\leq i\leq n

representing the faces of a given simplex.

•

Degeneracy maps

s_{j}:X_{n}\to X_{n+1}\;\;\;\text{ for }0\leq j\leq n

representing the degenerate simplices.

The face and the degeneracy maps are subject to the simplicial identities
[
18
]
.
A map
f:X\to Z
of simplicial sets consists of a sequence of functions
f_{n}:X_{n}\to Z_{n}
, where
n\geq 0
, compatible with the face and the degeneracy maps in the sense that

d_{i}f_{n}(\sigma)=f_{n-1}(d_{i}\sigma)\;\;\text{ and }\;\;s_{j}f_{n}(\sigma)=f_{n+1}(s_{j}\sigma)

for all
0\leq i,j\leq n
and
\sigma\in X_{n}
.

Example B.2
.

The
d
-simplex
\Delta[d]
is the simplicial set whose set
n
-simplices are given by

\{\sigma^{a_{0}\cdots a_{n}}:\,0\leq a_{0}\leq\cdots\leq a_{d}\leq n,\,a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

The face maps deletes an index:
d_{i}(\sigma^{a_{0}\cdots a_{n}})=\sigma^{a_{0}\cdots a_{i-1}a_{i+1}\cdots a_{n}}
, and the degeneracy maps copies an index:
s_{j}(\sigma^{a_{0}\cdots a_{n}})=\sigma^{a_{0}\cdots a_{j}a_{j}\cdots a_{n}}
. The simplex
\sigma^{01\cdots d}
is the generating simplex of the simplicial set in the sense that any other simplex can be obtained by applying a sequence of face and degeneracy maps to this simplex.

\hat{N}(\Sigma)

B.3
Lemmas: Embedding bundle scenarios into simplicial scenarios

The nerve space construction
N
, which preserves bundle scenarios, in fact gives a functor
N:\mathbf{bScen}\to\mathbf{sScen}
. To be able to prove this we need to show that applying
N
to a morphism between two bundle scenarios produces a morphism between the associated simplicial bundle scenarios. Our main tool is a natural transformation
N\hat{N}\to N
.

Definition B.7
.

Given a simplicial complex
\Sigma
, we define a natural map
\tilde{\mu}_{\Sigma}:N\hat{N}\Sigma\to N\Sigma
by

(\tilde{\mu}_{\Sigma})_{n}(\{\sigma_{11},\cdots,\sigma_{1m_{1}}\},\cdots,\{\sigma_{n1},\cdots,\sigma_{nm_{n}}\})=(\cup_{i=1}^{m_{1}}\sigma_{1i},\cdots,\cup_{i=1}^{m_{n}}\sigma_{ni})

where
(\{\sigma_{11},\cdots,\sigma_{1m_{1}}\},\cdots,\{\sigma_{n1},\cdots,\sigma_{nm_{n}}\})\in(N\hat{N}\Sigma)_{n}
.

The map
T(\pi):N\Sigma^{\prime}\to N\Sigma
given in Definition
4.6
can be factored as

T(\pi):N\Sigma^{\prime}\xrightarrow{N\pi}N\hat{N}\Sigma\xrightarrow{\tilde{\mu}_{\Sigma}}N\Sigma

where
\tilde{\mu}_{\Sigma}
is as in Definition
B.7
.
Next we prove a sequence of results to be used in showing that the nerve space construction gives us a well-defined functor.

Lemma B.8
.

For a type I morphism given in Diagram (

)
the composition of the following squares is a pull-back

(46)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N\hat{N}\Gamma}

Let

\displaystyle(Nf^{\pi})_{n}

Lemma B.9

\displaystyle T(\pi)_{n}(\{x_{11},\cdots,x_{1m_{1}}\},\cdots,\{x_{n1},\cdots,x_{nm_{n}}\})

B.4

In this section we prove results that allow us to push-forward a simplicial distribution along a morphism of
\mathbf{sScen}
. We begin by showing that the construction

given in Definition

provides a well-defined simplicial distribution.

Lemma B.13
.

Np
is a well-defined simplicial distribution on
Nf
, i.e., it belongs to
\mathsf{sDist}(Nf)
.

Proof.

First, we prove that
(Np)_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})\in D_{R}((Nf)^{-1}_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n}))
. Using Remark
3.10
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n})\in(Nf)^{-1}_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})}(Np)_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n})\in(Nf)^{-1}_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})}p_{\cup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}}(\cup_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in f^{-1}(\cup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i})}p_{\cup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}}(\gamma)=1.

Now, we prove that
Np
respect the simplicial structure. Given

(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})\in(N\Sigma)_{n}
and

(\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\gamma}_{n-1})\in(N\Gamma)_{n-1}
, for
0<i<n
we have

\displaystyle D_{R}(d_{i}^{N\Gamma})

\displaystyle\left((Np)_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})\right)(\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\gamma}_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n})\,:\,\,d_{i}^{N\Gamma}(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n})=(\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\gamma}_{n-1})}(Np)_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})({\gamma}_{1},\cdots,{\gamma}_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n})\in(Nf)^{-1}_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})\,:\,\,\gamma_{1}=\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{i}\cup\gamma_{i+1}=\tilde{\gamma}_{i},\cdots,\gamma_{n}=\tilde{\gamma}_{n-1}}p_{\cup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}}(\cup_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}).

By Remark (
3.10
) there exists a unique
(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n})\in(Nf)^{-1}_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})
such that
\cup_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}=\cup_{i=1}^{n-1}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
.
Therefore the sum above is equal to
p_{\cup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}}(\cup_{i=1}^{n-1}\tilde{\gamma}_{i})
. On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle(Np)_{n-1}

\displaystyle\left(d_{i}^{N\Sigma}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})\right)(\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\gamma}_{n-1})

\displaystyle=(Np)_{n-1}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{i}\cup\sigma_{i+1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})(\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\gamma}_{n-1})

\displaystyle=p_{\cup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}}(\cup_{i=1}^{n-1}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}).

For
i=n
, using Equation (
12
) we have

\displaystyle D_{R}(d_{n}^{N\Gamma})

\displaystyle\left((Np)_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})\right)(\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\gamma}_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in f^{-1}(\sigma_{n})}(Np)_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})(\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\gamma}_{n-1},\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in f^{-1}(\sigma_{n})}p_{\cup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}}(\cup_{i=1}^{n-1}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}\cup\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\cup_{i=1}^{n-1}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}\subset\gamma^{\prime}\in f^{-1}(\cup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i})}p_{\cup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}}(\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=p_{\cup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}}|_{\cup_{i=1}^{n-1}\sigma_{i}}(\cup_{i=1}^{n-1}\tilde{\gamma}_{i})

\displaystyle=p_{\cup_{i=1}^{n-1}\sigma_{i}}(\cup_{i=1}^{n-1}\tilde{\gamma}_{i})

\displaystyle=(Np)_{n-1}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n-1})(\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\gamma}_{n-1})

\displaystyle=(Np)_{n-1}\left(d^{N\Sigma}_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})\right)(\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\gamma}_{n-1}).

The
i=0
case is similar. For the degeneracy maps, one can see that for
0\leq i\leq n
both

D_{R}(s_{i}^{N\Gamma})\left((Np)_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})\right)(\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\gamma}_{n+1})\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,(Np)_{n+1}\left(s_{i}^{N\Sigma}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})\right)(\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\gamma}_{n+1})

are equal to

p_{\cup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}}(\cup_{i=1}^{n+1}\tilde{\gamma}_{i})

if
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}=\emptyset
; otherwise, both are zero.
∎

Lemma B.14
.

Let

be a simplicial complex map that is discrete over vertices.
Given

and
(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})\in(N\Sigma)_{n}

Lemma B.15
.

\displaystyle D_{R}(N\alpha)_{n}

Lemma B.16
.

Let

Appendix C
Convex categories

In this section we recall the notion of a convex category introduced in
[
10
]
and some basic properties of these objects.
In the abstract setting convexity is defined using algebras over a monad.
A
monad
on a category
\mathbf{C}
is a functor
T:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{C}
together with natural transformations
\delta:\text{id}_{\mathbf{C}}\to T

Lemma C.5
.

For
R
-convex sets
X,Y
and
Q\in D_{R}(\mathbf{Conv}_{R}(X,Y))
, we have

(52)

\nu^{Y}\circ\Theta_{X,Y}(Q)\circ\nu^{X}=\nu^{Y}\circ\mu_{Y}\circ D_{R}(\Theta_{X,Y}(Q)).

Proof.

Given
p\in D_{R}(X)
, we have

\Theta_{X,Y}(Q)(\nu^{X}(p))=\sum_{\varphi\in\mathbf{Conv}_{R}(X,Y)}Q(\varphi)\delta^{\varphi(\nu^{X}(p))}=\sum_{\varphi\in\mathbf{Conv}_{R}(X,Y)}Q(\varphi)\delta^{\nu^{Y}\left(D_{R}(\varphi)(p)\right)}.

On the other hand

\displaystyle\mu_{Y}\left(D_{R}(\Theta_{X,Y}(Q))(p)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in D_{R}(Y)}\left(D_{R}(\Theta_{X,Y}(Q))(p)\right)(q)q

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in D_{R}(Y)}\left(\sum_{x\in X:\,(\Theta_{X,Y}(Q))(x)=q}p(x)\right)q

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in X}p(x)(\Theta_{X,Y}(Q))(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\sum_{\varphi\in\mathbf{Conv}_{R}(X,Y)}Q(\varphi)\delta^{\varphi(x)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varphi\in\mathbf{Conv}_{R}(X,Y)}Q(\varphi)\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\delta^{\varphi(x)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varphi\in\mathbf{Conv}_{R}(X,Y)}Q(\varphi)D_{R}(\varphi)(p).

We define
\tilde{Q}\in D_{R}(D_{R}(Y))
by

\tilde{Q}(q)=\sum_{\varphi:\,D_{R}(\varphi)(p)=q}Q(\varphi)
for
q\in D_{R}(Y)
.
This is a well-defined distribution since

\sum_{q\in D_{R}(Y)}\tilde{Q}(q)=\sum_{q\in D_{R}(Y)}\;\sum_{\varphi:\,D_{R}(\varphi)(p)=q}Q(\varphi)=\sum_{\varphi\in\mathbf{Conv}_{R}(X,Y)}Q(\varphi)=1.

In addition, we have

D_{R}(\nu^{Y})(\tilde{Q})=\sum_{q\in D_{R}(Y)}\tilde{Q}(q)\delta^{\nu^{Y}(q)}=\sum_{\varphi\in\mathbf{Conv}_{R}(X,Y)}Q(\varphi)\delta^{\nu^{Y}\left(D_{R}(\varphi)(p)\right)}

and

\mu_{Y}(\tilde{Q})=\sum_{q\in D_{R}(Y)}\tilde{Q}(q)q=\sum_{\varphi\in\mathbf{Conv}_{R}(X,Y)}Q(\varphi)D_{R}(\varphi)(p).

By Diagram (
48
) we obtain the result.
∎

Proposition C.7
.

Let
\mathbf{C}
be a (locally) small category and
\mathbf{E}
be an
R
-convex category.
Consider two functors
F,G:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{E}

and a natural transformation
\eta:F\to G
.
Let
\tilde{F}
and
\tilde{G}
be the transpose of
F
and
G

with respect to the adjunction
\mathbf{Cat}\dashv\mathbf{ConvCat}_{R}
; respectively.
Then
\eta
lifts to a natural transformation
\tilde{\eta}:\tilde{F}\to\tilde{G}
.

Proof.

We define
\tilde{\eta}_{X}=\eta_{X}
at an object
X
of
D_{R}(\mathbf{C})
.
Given
Q\in D_{R}(\mathbf{C})(X,Y)
, we will prove that
\eta_{Y}\circ\tilde{F}_{X,Y}(Q)=\tilde{G}_{X,Y}(Q)\circ\eta_{X}
. Firstly, we have

\tilde{F}_{X,Y}(Q)=\pi_{F(X),F(Y)}\left(D_{R}(F_{X,Y})(Q)\right)=\pi_{F(X),F(Y)}\left(\sum_{f\in C(X,Y)}Q(f)\delta^{F_{X,Y}(f)}\right),

see Remark 2.1 in
[
10
]
. Similarly, we have

\tilde{G}_{X,Y}(Q)=\pi_{G(X),G(Y)}\left(\sum_{f\in C(X,Y)}Q(f)\delta^{G_{X,Y}(f)}\right).

Now, by the functoriality of
\pi
and Lemma 3.2 from
[
10
]

we have

\displaystyle\eta_{Y}\circ\tilde{F}_{X,Y}(Q)

\displaystyle=\pi_{F(Y),G(Y)}(\delta^{\eta_{Y}})\circ\pi_{F(X),F(Y)}\left(\sum_{f\in C(X,Y)}Q(f)\delta^{F_{X,Y}(f)}\right)

\displaystyle=\pi_{F(X),G(Y)}\left(\delta^{\eta_{Y}}\ast(\sum_{f\in C(X,Y)}Q(f)\delta^{F_{X,Y}(f)})\right)

\displaystyle=\pi_{F(X),G(Y)}\left(\sum_{f\in C(X,Y)}Q(f)\delta^{\eta_{Y}\circ F_{X,Y}(f)}\right).

Similarly, we have

\tilde{G}_{X,Y}(Q)\circ\eta_{X}=\pi_{F(X),G(Y)}\left(\sum_{f\in C(X,Y)}Q(f)\delta^{G_{X,Y}(f)\circ\eta_{X}}\right).

The result follows from the naturality of
\eta
.
∎

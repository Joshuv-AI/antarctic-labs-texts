A power series expansion of Teukolsky linearised gravitational waves.
Leo Brewin

Abstract
A power series, suitable for use in a numerical relativity code, will be presented for the time symmetric Teukolsky linearised gravitational waves.



1
The Teukolsky metric

Any numerical relativity code worth its salt must demonstrate long term stability for
initial data based on weak perturbations around flat spacetimes. A popular example of such
spacetimes are the Teukolsky family of linearised gravitational waves

[
1
]
. One member of that family, and the main focus of this note, is
described by the metric

\displaystyle\begin{split}ds^{2}=&-dt^{2}+dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\\
&+\left(2-3\sin^{2}\theta\right)A(t,r)dr^{2}\\[5.0pt]
&-\left(A(t,r)-3(\sin^{2}\theta)C(t,r)\right)r^{2}d\theta^{2}\\[5.0pt]
&-\left(A(t,r)+3(\sin^{2}\theta)\left(C(t,r)-A(t,r)\right)\right)r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\\[5.0pt]
&-6r\left(\sin\theta\cos\theta\right)B(t,r)drd\theta\end{split}

(1.1)

where

\displaystyle A(t,r)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{r^{5}}\left(r^{2}F^{(2)}-3rF^{(1)}+3F\right)

(1.2)

\displaystyle B(t,r)

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{r^{5}}\left(-r^{3}F^{(3)}+3r^{2}F^{(2)}-6rF^{(1)}+6F\right)

(1.3)

\displaystyle C(t,r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4r^{5}}\left(r^{4}F^{(4)}-2r^{3}F^{(3)}+9r^{2}F^{(2)}-21rF^{(1)}+21F\right)

(1.4)

\displaystyle F^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d^{n}Q(t+r)}{dr^{n}}-\frac{d^{n}Q(t-r)}{dr^{n}}\right)

(1.5)

and where
Q(x)
is an arbitrary function of
x
. Note that this form of the metric differs
slightly from that given by Teukolsky. Here the function
F
has been expressed as an
explicit combination of ingoing and outgoing waves (thus ensuring time symmetric initial
data). Note also that the derivatives of
F
are taken with respect to
r
rather than
x

as used by Teukolsky. Consequently, the signs of the odd-derivatives of
F
in the
expressions for
A
,
B
and
C
have been flipped (see Teukolsky’s comment, last sentence
on page 746).

Baumgarte and Shapiro
[
2
]
chose

\displaystyle Q(x)=axe^{-x^{2}}

(1.6)

where
a
is a wave parameter (typically in the range
0<a<1
). Note also that this metric
is not an exact solution of the vacuum Einstein equations but rather they yield

G_{ab}=\mathcal{O}\left(a^{2}\right)
for
0<a<1
where
G_{ab}
are the components of the Einstein tensor.

There are two immediate problems in using the above form of the metric for supplying
numerical initial data. First, the underlying spherical polar coordinates are singular at

r=0
and second, almost all numerical relativity codes are based on Cartesian like
coordinates. One solution to this pair of problems is to sample the metric at points away
from
r=0
and to then interpolate that data to a Cartesian grid. Though this can be made to
work for the metric components, the interpolation will fail for other quantities like the
connection or the Riemann curvatures. A better approach is to express the metric in terms of
the Cartesian coordinates
(t,x,y,z)
. This is a standard (though tedious) computational
task. The result is

\displaystyle\begin{split}ds^{2}=&-dt^{2}+dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}\\[5.0pt]
&+\frac{a}{r^{4}}\left((3x^{2}z^{2}+3y^{2}r^{2}-r^{4})A(t,r)-6x^{2}z^{2}B(t,r)+3(x^{2}z^{2}-y^{2}r^{2})C(t,r)\right)dx^{2}\\[5.0pt]
&+\frac{a}{r^{4}}\left((3y^{2}z^{2}+3x^{2}r^{2}-r^{4})A(t,r)-6y^{2}z^{2}B(t,r)+3(y^{2}z^{2}-x^{2}r^{2})C(t,r)\right)dy^{2}\\[5.0pt]
&+\frac{a}{r^{4}}\left((3z^{4}-r^{4})A(t,r)+6z^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})B(t,r)+3(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}C(t,r)\right)dz^{2}\\[5.0pt]
&-6\frac{a}{r^{4}}\left(xy(x^{2}+y^{2})A(t,r)+2xyz^{2}B(t,r)-xy(r^{2}+z^{2})C(t,r)\right)dxdy\\[5.0pt]
&+6\frac{a}{r^{4}}\left(xz^{3}A(t,r)+xz(x^{2}+y^{2}-z^{2})B(t,r)-xz(x^{2}+y^{2})C(t,r)\right)dxdz\\[5.0pt]
&+6\frac{a}{r^{4}}\left(yz^{3}A(t,r)+yz(x^{2}+y^{2}-z^{2})B(t,r)-yz(x^{2}+y^{2})C(t,r)\right)dydz\end{split}

(1.7)

In this form it is clear that there may be numerical problems at
r=0
. The functions
A,B

and
C
each carry a
1/r^{5}
factor (as per equations (
1.2
–
1.4
)) while
the conversion to Cartesian coordinates has introduced a further factor of
1/r^{4}
. Thus
near the origin there is a possible
1/r^{9}
behaviour in the metric components. However, the
underlying physics (of weak gravitational waves) suggests that the metric should be
non-singular at
r=0
and thus the singular terms should somehow cancel out.

The remainder of this note will develop a power series expansion around
r=0
to explicitly
show that this is the case. That same series can then be used to supply initial data on
and near
r=0
without any numerical concerns.

2
Series expansion of the metric.

The objective here is to use a power series around
r=0
to show explicitly that the

A,B,C
and
g_{ab}
are all well behaved near
r=0
.

Notice that the
g_{ab}
depend linearly on
A,B
and
C
which in turn depend linearly on

F
. Thus a power series for the
g_{ab}
can be inferred from a power series for
F
.

Begin by writing
F
in the form

\displaystyle F(t,r)=a\left((t+r)e^{-2tr}-(t-r)e^{2tr}\right)e^{-t^{2}-r^{2}}

(2.1)

and then use a standard power series for the
e^{-2tr}
and
e^{2tr}
terms to obtain

\displaystyle e^{t^{2}+r^{2}}F

\displaystyle=a(2-4t^{2})r+a2^{3}t^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2!}-\frac{2t^{2}}{3!}\right)r^{3}+a2^{5}t^{4}\left(\frac{1}{4!}-\frac{2t^{2}}{5!}\right)r^{5}+\dots

(2.2)

\displaystyle=a\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}r^{2n+1}

(2.3)

with

\displaystyle f_{n}=2(2n+1-2t^{2})\frac{(2t)^{2n}}{(2n+1)!}

(2.4)

The remaining steps are straightforward – simply substitute the series expansion for
F

(
2.2
,
2.4
) into the equations for
A,B
and
C

(
1.2
–
1.4
) and finally the metric (
1.7
). The results
for the three functions
A,B
and
C
are

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=ae^{-t^{2}-r^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}a_{n}

(2.5)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=ae^{-t^{2}-r^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}b_{n}

(2.6)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=ae^{-t^{2}-r^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}c_{n}

(2.7)

where

{dgroup*}

a_{n}=12nr^{2n-4}\left(n-1\right)-24nr^{2n-2}+12r^{2n}

b_{n}=-24n^{2}r^{2n-2}+4nr^{2n-4}\left(n-1\right)\left(2n-1\right)+12r^{2n}\left(2n+1\right)-8r^{2n+2}

c_{n}=4nr^{2n-4}\left(n-1\right)\left(n^{2}-n+1\right)-8nr^{2n-2}{}\left(2n^{2}+1\right)+12r^{2n}\left(2n^{2}+2n+1\right)-16r^{2n+2}\left(n+1\right)+4r^{2n+4}

The full power series for the
g_{ab}
is given by

\displaystyle g_{ab}=\eta_{ab}+ae^{-t^{2}-r^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}g^{(n)}_{ab}

(2.8)

where

{dgroup*}

g^{(n)}_{xx}=-12nr^{2n-8}x^{2}\left(n-3\right)\left(n-2\right)\left(n-1\right)\left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right)+12nr^{2n-6}\left(n-2\right)\left(n-1\right)\left(nx^{2}-ny^{2}+4x^{4}+4x^{2}y^{2}-3x^{2}-y^{2}\right)-12nr^{2n-4}\left(n-1\right)\left(4nx^{2}-4ny^{2}+6x^{4}+6x^{2}y^{2}-8x^{2}-4y^{2}+1\right)+24nr^{2n-2}{}\left(3nx^{2}-3ny^{2}+2x^{4}+2x^{2}y^{2}-3x^{2}-3y^{2}+1\right)-12r^{2n}\left(4nx^{2}-4ny^{2}+x^{4}+x^{2}y^{2}-4y^{2}+1\right)+12r^{2n+2}\left(x-y\right)\left(x+y\right)

g^{(n)}_{yy}=-12nr^{2n-8}y^{2}\left(n-3\right)\left(n-2\right)\left(n-1\right)\left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right)+12nr^{2n-6}\left(n-2\right)\left(n-1\right)\left(-nx^{2}+ny^{2}+4x^{2}y^{2}-x^{2}+4y^{4}-3y^{2}\right)-12nr^{2n-4}\left(n-1\right)\left(-4nx^{2}+4ny^{2}+6x^{2}y^{2}-4x^{2}+6y^{4}-8y^{2}+1\right)+24nr^{2n-2}\left(-3nx^{2}+3ny^{2}+2x^{2}y^{2}-3x^{2}+2y^{4}-3y^{2}+1\right)-12r^{2n}\left(-4nx^{2}+4ny^{2}+x^{2}y^{2}-4x^{2}+y^{4}+1\right)-12r^{2n+2}\left(x-y\right)\left(x+y\right)

g^{(n)}_{zz}=12nr^{2n-8}\left(n-3\right)\left(n-2\right)\left(n-1\right)\left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right)^{2}-48nr^{2n-6}\left(n-2\right)\left(n-1\right)\left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right)\left(x^{2}+y^{2}-1\right)+24nr^{2n-4}\left(n-1\right)\left(3x^{4}+6x^{2}y^{2}-6x^{2}+3y^{4}-6y^{2}+1\right)-48nr^{2n-2}\left(x^{4}+2x^{2}y^{2}-3x^{2}+y^{4}-3y^{2}+1\right)+12r^{2n}\left(x^{4}+2x^{2}y^{2}-4x^{2}+y^{4}-4y^{2}+2\right)

g^{(n)}_{xy}=-12nr^{2n-8}xy\left(n-3\right)\left(n-2\right)\left(n-1\right)\left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right)+24nr^{2n-6}xy\left(n-2\right)\left(n-1\right)\left(n+2x^{2}+2y^{2}-1\right)-24nr^{2n-4}xy\left(n-1\right)\left(4n+3x^{2}+3y^{2}-2\right)+48nr^{2n-2}xy\left(3n+x^{2}+y^{2}\right)-12r^{2n}xy\left(8n+x^{2}+y^{2}+4\right)+24r^{2n+2}xy

g^{(n)}_{xz}=-12nr^{2n-8}xz\left(n-3\right)\left(n-2\right)\left(n-1\right)\left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right)+24nr^{2n-6}xz\left(n-2\right)\left(n-1\right)\left(2x^{2}+2y^{2}-1\right)-72nr^{2n-4}xz\left(n-1\right)\left(x^{2}+y^{2}-1\right)+24nr^{2n-2}xz\left(2x^{2}+2y^{2}-3\right)-12r^{2n}xz\left(x^{2}+y^{2}-2\right)

g^{(n)}_{yz}=-12nr^{2n-8}yz\left(n-3\right)\left(n-2\right)\left(n-1\right)\left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right)+24nr^{2n-6}yz\left(n-2\right)\left(n-1\right)\left(2x^{2}+2y^{2}-1\right)-72nr^{2n-4}yz\left(n-1\right)\left(x^{2}+y^{2}-1\right)+24nr^{2n-2}yz\left(2x^{2}+2y^{2}-3\right)-12r^{2n}yz\left(x^{2}+y^{2}-2\right)

Note that the above
g^{(n)}_{ab}
are manifestly non-singular at
r=0
.

3
Convergence.

The results presented in the previous section will be of little use if the series do not
converge in a suitable domain containing the origin
r=0
. It is actually quite easy to
demonstrate, using a standard ratio test, that each of the above power series converges
absolutely for all (finite)
x,y,z,t
. Consider, for example, the convergence of the
series for
g_{xy}
. A straightforward computation shows, for any finite

x,y,z,t
, that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{f_{n+1}g^{(n+1)}_{xy}}{f_{n}g^{(n)}_{xy}}\right|=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{4t^{2}r^{2}}{2n+3}\right|=0

(3.1)

Thus, as claimed, the power series for
g_{xy}
is absolutely convergent. The same method
can be applied to the remaining power series (with the same result).

(
1.2
–
1.7
). The resulting expression for the full metric

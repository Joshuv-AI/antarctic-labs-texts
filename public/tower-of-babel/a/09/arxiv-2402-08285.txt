Theoretical properties of angular halfspace depth
Stanislav Nagy 1, Petra Laketa 1

Abstract
. The angular halfspace depth ( ahD ) is a natural modification of the celebrated halfspace (or Tukey) depth to the setup of directional data. It allows us to define elements of nonparametric inference, such as the median, the inter-quantile regions, or the rank statistics, for datasets supported in the unit sphere. Despite being introduced in 1987, ahD has never received ample recognition in the literature, mainly due to the lack of efficient algorithms for its computation. With the recent progress on the computational front, ahD however exhibits the potential for developing viable nonparametric statistics techniques for directional datasets. In this paper, we thoroughly treat the theoretical properties of ahD . We show that similarly to the classical halfspace depth for multivariate data, also ahD satisfies many desirable properties of a statistical depth function. Further, we derive uniform continuity/consistency results for the associated set of directional medians, and the central regions of ahD , the latter representing a depth-based analogue of the quantiles for directional data.



1.
Nonparametrics of directional data and angular halfspace depth

Directional data analysis concerns the statistics of datasets bound to lie on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\left\|x\right\|=1\right\}
. Despite sharing similarities with multivariate statistical methods, the particular geometry of the sphere makes the analysis of directional data challenging. The sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is a symmetric compact manifold, which makes many statistical methods from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
fruitless or suboptimal when applied in
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
directly
(
Watson, 1983
,
Mardia and Jupp, 2000
,
Ley and Verdebout, 2017
)
.

We consider the nonparametric analysis for directional data and the concept of depth functions, a statistical tool that introduces elements of nonparametrics to multivariate or non-Euclidean spaces. In the past decades, depths have garnered great success in multivariate analysis
(
Donoho and Gasko, 1992
,
Liu et al., 1999
,
Zuo and Serfling, 2000
,
Mosler, 2013
,
Chernozhukov et al., 2017
,
Mosler and Mozharovskyi, 2022
)
. A prime example of a depth in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the
halfspace depth
(
hD
, also called
Tukey depth
, or
location depth
) that is defined for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a Borel probability measure
P
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

(1)

hD(x;P)=\inf\left\{P\left(H_{x,u}\right)\colon u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\right\},

where
H_{x,u}=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\left\langle y,u\right\rangle\geq\left\langle x,u\right\rangle\right\}
is the closed halfspace whose boundary passes through
x
with inner unit normal
u
. In words,
hD
evaluates the smallest
P
-mass of a halfspace that contains
x
. As such, it allows to devise a
P
-dependent ordering of the points from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
— a point
x
is said to be located deeper than
y
inside the mass of
P
if
hD(x;P)>hD(y;P)
. The deepest point in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, defined as (any) point that maximizes the depth function
x\mapsto hD(x;P)
over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, is a natural analogue of the median for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued measures and is called a
halfspace median
of
P
. A counterpart of quantiles (or, more precisely, inter-quantile regions) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are the
central regions
of
P
, given as the upper level sets of
hD

(2)

hD_{\alpha}(P)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon hD(x;P)\geq\alpha\right\}\quad\mbox{for }\alpha\geq 0.

These sets are known to be nested, convex, and compact for
\alpha>0
; their shapes capture the geometry of
P
. Both the multivariate medians and central regions are of great importance in nonparametric analysis of multivariate data, and have been studied extensively in the literature
(
Donoho and Gasko, 1992
,
Rousseeuw and Ruts, 1999
,
Zuo and Serfling, 2000
,
Dyckerhoff, 2017
)
. The halfspace depth is only one of many depth functions proposed in multivariate analysis. It is, nevertheless, the classical representative of multivariate depths.

Several depths suitable specifically for directional data have been proposed in the literature; we refer to
Liu and Singh, 1992
,
Agostinelli and Romanazzi, 2013
,
Ley et al., 2014
,
Pandolfo et al., 2018
,
Buttarazzi et al., 2018
,
Konen, 2022
, and
Hallin et al., 2022
. In this contribution, we scrutinize historically the first directional depth function proposed by
Small, 1987
. It is a version of
hD
from (
1
) suitable for measures in
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. For
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
P
a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, the
angular halfspace depth
(
ahD
, also known as
angular Tukey depth
) of
x
with respect to (w.r.t.)
P
is defined as

(3)

ahD\left(x;P\right)=\inf\left\{P\left(H_{0,u}\right)\colon u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\mbox{ and }x\in H_{0,u}\right\}.

In contrast to the standard
hD
in (
1
), in the definition of
ahD
one considers only halfspaces whose boundary passes through the origin
0
in the ambient space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and searches for a minimum
P
-mass among those that contain
x
.

The first rigorous studies of
ahD
were conducted by
Small, 1987
and
Liu and Singh, 1992
. Since then, no systematic investigation of the theory of
ahD
has been performed. In fact, it might come as a surprise how little attention did
ahD
receive in the literature, especially when compared with the abundant body of research on the classical
hD
. One explanation for this is the presumed high computational cost of the angular depth, coupled with a lack of efficient algorithms for its computation
(
Pandolfo et al., 2018
)
. The problem of exact and approximate computation of
ahD
was, however, recently resolved in
Dyckerhoff and Nagy, 2023
, which paved the way to explore the general theory and practice of
ahD
with its statistical applications.

This paper comprehensively studies the main theoretical properties of
ahD
, its associated median, and its central regions. We contrast
ahD
with
hD
and demonstrate that similarly to the halfspace depth in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, also the angular depth satisfies an array of plausible properties required from a proper depth function. After introducing the notations, we begin in Section
2
by drawing a direct relation between
ahD
on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and a variant of
hD
in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. Then, in Section
3
, we study
ahD
w.r.t. the desirable properties of a directional depth formulated recently in
Nagy et al., 2023
. We show that
ahD
, as the only directional depth function found in the literature, satisfies all the conditions from
Nagy et al., 2023
. In the final Section
4
we provide a list of additional characteristics of
ahD
, mainly related to the continuity of its median and the associated central regions. Those findings are important in statistical practice, as they guarantee the uniform consistency of the sample depth (
3
) when computed w.r.t. the empirical measure of a random sample
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
from distribution
P
. The proofs of all theoretical results are gathered in the Appendix.

3.
Desiderata for a directional depth function

There are several well-established properties that a depth
D
in a linear space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
should obey. It is typically agreed that
D
should be

•

affine invariant, meaning that it does not depend on the coordinate system in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

•

maximized at the center of symmetry of
P
for any
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\right)
symmetric;

•

monotonically decreasing as
x
moves along straight lines starting at the point of maximum of
D
; and

•

decaying uniformly to zero as
\left\|x\right\|\to\infty
.

This set of properties was postulated by
Zuo and Serfling, 2000
; for additional related axioms we refer to
Liu, 1990
,
Serfling, 2006
,
Mosler, 2013
, and
Mosler and Mozharovskyi, 2022
.

Compared to depths in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, much less is known about general depth functions defined in
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. One set of assumptions has been laid down recently in
Nagy et al., 2023
. There, a general
angular
(or
directional
)
depth function

was introduced as a bounded map
aD\colon\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)\to[0,\infty)
that fulfills (most of) the following properties for all
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
:

(
\mathrm{D}_{1}
)

Rotational invariance:

aD(x;P)=aD(Ox;P_{OX})
for all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and any orthogonal matrix
O\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, where
P_{OX}\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
is the distribution of the transformed random vector

with

;

(
\mathrm{D}_{2}
)

Maximality at center:
For any

(13)

\max\{aD(\mu;P),aD(-\mu;P)\}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}aD(x;P);

(
\mathrm{D}_{3}
)

Monotonicity along great circles:

aD(x;P)\leq aD(\left(\mu+\alpha(x-\mu)\right)/\left\|\mu+\alpha(x-\mu)\right\|;P)

for all

and

, where

is any point that satisfies

aD(\mu;P)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}aD(x;P);

(
\mathrm{D}_{4}
)

(
\mathrm{D}_{5}
)

Upper semi-continuity:

{\lim\sup}_{x_{n}\to x}aD(x_{n};P)\leq aD(x;P)\quad\mbox{for all }x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

where the sequence

.

Quasi-concavity:
All central regions

3.1.
Rotational invariance

The validity of

Theorem 2

The depth

Proof.

Take

is true.
∎

The result of

3.3.
Quasi-concavity of level sets

Just as for

(

D
6

)
for

Theorem 4
.

For any
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)

\alpha\geq 0

(18)

In particular,

satisfies both
(

D
3

)
and
(

D
6

)

Comparing Theorem

from

can be written as intersections of

Theorem 5
.

For any

\bigcap\left\{H\colon H\in\mathcal{H}_{0}\mbox{ and }P(H^{\mathsf{c}})<\alpha\right\}\subseteq ahD_{\alpha}(P).

It is interesting to see that the opposite inclusion from (

. Formula (

with

studied in convex geometry

uniform on a spherical convex body

3.4.
Minimality at the anti-median and constancy on a hemisphere

The depth
ahD
has an interesting property observed first in
Liu and Singh, 1992
. For any distribution on a sphere, there exists a hemisphere
H
on which
ahD
is constant. For each
x\in H
, we then have that
ahD(x;P)
is equal to the minimum
P
-mass of a hemisphere in
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Especially in connection with our property
(

D
4

)
, it is important to note that this property does not hold true for closed hemispheres.

Example 1
.

Take
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
a mixture of a uniform distribution on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{+}
with weight
1/2
and an atom of mass
1/2
at some point
z\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{0}
. Then,
ahD(z;P)=1/2
and
ahD(x;P)=0
for each
x\in\left(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{-}\cup\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{0}\right)\setminus\{z\}
. For points
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{+}
we have
ahD(x;P)\in(0,1/2)
. Thus, the unique angular halfspace median of
P
is
z
. The infimum
P
-mass of a (closed) hemisphere is
0
, but because of the point mass at
z
, no closed hemisphere in
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
has constant null
ahD
. Still,
ahD(-z;P)=0=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}ahD(x;P)
, and condition
(

D
4

)
is satisfied for this particular

.

The appropriate context to study the set of minimum
ahD
is that of flag halfspaces, recently introduced in
Pokorný et al., 2023

Definition
.

Define
\mathcal{F}
as the system of all sets
F
of the form

(20)

F=\{0\}\cup\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{d}G_{k}\right).

Here,

•

G_{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an open halfspace whose boundary passes through the origin.

•

For every
k=1,\dots,d-1
, the set
G_{k}
is an open halfspace inside the
k

G_{k+1}
, such that the relative boundary of
G_{k}
passes through the origin.

Any element of

is called a

A flag halfspace in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the union of

(i)

an open halfspace

whose boundary passes through the origin,

(ii)

a relatively open halfplane
G_{2}
, inside the plane
\partial G_{3}
, whose relative boundary passes through the origin, and

(iii)

a ray
\{0\}\cup G_{1}
from the origin

into one of the two directions in the line given by the relative boundary of

.

Flag halfspaces are interesting because of their connections with

. As proved in

can always be replaced by a minimum, if one searches through flag halfspaces instead of closed halfspaces. An analogous result turns out to be true also for

Theorem 6

For any
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
and

In particular, there always exists
F\in\mathcal{F}
such that

and
ahD(x;P)=P(F)
.

Armed with the notion of a flag halfspace, we now prove a sharp version of the claim on hemispheres of minimum

Theorem 7

Let
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)

For every

. In particular,
(

D
4

)
is true for

.

As a consequence of Theorem

(

D
4

)
for

if and only if

(22)

\min\{ahD(x;P),ahD(-x;P)\}=\min_{y\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}ahD(y;P).

In our Example

4.
Continuity and consistency properties

We now focus on continuity and consistency properties of
ahD
that are finer in nature than the simple requirement
(

D
5

)
. In Section

, we treat the set of
ahD

To state our results, recall that for compact sets

\delta_{H}(K,L)=\max\left\{\sup_{x\in K}\inf_{y\in L}\left\|x-y\right\|,\sup_{x\in L}\inf_{y\in K}\left\|x-y\right\|\right\}.

For closed sets

with

\mathbb{R}
is outer semi-continuous but not continuous at
Q
. To obtain corresponding directional distributions, we project our setup to the upper semi-circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{+}
of the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
using the inverse gnomonic projection from Section
2
. Then, we directly apply Theorem
1
.

4.2.
Continuity in measure

Under an appropriate smoothness condition (
S
), the mapping
ahD
can be shown to be uniformly continuous w.r.t. the weak convergence of measures.

Theorem 10
.

Suppose
\left\{P_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
is a sequence of measures such that
P_{n}\xrightarrow{w}P
as
n\to\infty
, where
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
satisfies the smoothness condition (
S
). Then we can write

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\left|ahD(x;P_{n})-ahD(x;P)\right|\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.

Without the smoothness condition (
S
), we cannot assert that the uniform continuity in Theorem
10
is true. This can be seen in an example where
P
is concentrated in
e_{d}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, but
P_{n}
is uniform on a spherical cap in
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
around
e_{d}
with (spherical) radius
1/n
. Then
P_{n}\xrightarrow{w}P
as
n\to\infty
and
ahD(e_{d};P)=1
, but
ahD(e_{d};P_{n})=1/2
for all
n
.

4.3.
Continuity of the central regions

We now follow
Dyckerhoff, 2017
, who proved that under certain conditions, the central regions
hD_{\alpha}(P)
from (
2
) are continuous in the Hausdorff distance as a function of
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\right)
. We adapt those results from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to the setup of directional data in
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
ahD
. For that, we will need a modification of the strict monotonicity condition formulated in
Dyckerhoff, 2017
for
hD
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We phrase a related requirement for
ahD
; we say that
ahD
is
strictly monotone
for
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
if for each
\alpha\in\left(\min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}ahD(x;P),\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}ahD(x;P)\right)
we have

(25)

ahD_{\alpha}(P)=\mathrm{cl}\left(\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon ahD(x;P)>\alpha\right\}\right),

where
\mathrm{cl}\left(A\right)
stands for the closure of the set
A\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Roughly speaking, strict monotonicity means that there are no regions of
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
of constant depth equal to
\alpha>\min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}ahD(x;P)
. Of course, due to Theorem
7
, we have to exclude the hemisphere of minimum
P
-mass (i.e.,
\alpha>\min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}ahD(x;P)
), since at
\alpha=\min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}ahD(x;P)
the condition (
25
) is never satisfied.

Theorem 11
.

Suppose that
ahD
is strictly monotone for
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
, and that
\left\{P_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
is a sequence of measures that satisfies

(26)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\left|ahD(x;P_{n})-ahD(x;P)\right|=0.

Then for any closed interval
A\subset(\min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}ahD(x;P),\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}ahD(x;P))
we can write

(27)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\alpha\in A}\delta_{H}\left(ahD_{\alpha}(P_{n}),ahD_{\alpha}(P)\right)=0.

The condition (
26
) is satisfied if
P
is smooth (that is, (
S
) is valid) by Theorem
10
. The additional condition (
25
) of strict monotonicity of
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
is more delicate. In the setup of
hD
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it was argued in
Laketa and Nagy, 2022
that smoothness of
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\right)
and the connectedness of its support already guarantee a variant of (
25
). In
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, however, this is not enough, as we demonstrate in the following example.

Example 4
.

Take
X\sim P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
, where the random variable
X\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
is encoded by its angle
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]
with the positive first coordinate axis in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The measure
P
is given as a mixture of four uniform distributions:

(i)

of mass
1/2
in the angle
(\pi/2,\pi]
,

(ii)

of mass
1/4
in the angle
(0,\pi/4]
,

(iii)

of mass
1/8
in the angle
[\pi/4,\pi/2)
, and

(iv)

of mass
1/8
in the angle
[-3\pi/4,-\pi/2)
.

See also Figure
2
.

Figure 2.
Example
4
: A distribution
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
that does not satisfy the condition of strict monotonicity (
25
) at
\alpha=3/8
. In the left panel, the outer red curve corresponds to the
ahD
of
x(\theta)=(\cos(\theta),\sin(\theta))^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
as a function of the angle

; the depth

is maximized at the angle

(solid straight line), and
ahD_{3/8}(P)
corresponds to the angles
\theta\in[\pi/4,3\pi/4]
(wedge with dashed boundary lines). The depth
ahD
is constant for
\theta\in[\pi/4,\pi/2]
, meaning that
ahD
is not strictly monotone for
P
. In the right panel, we have the function
\theta\mapsto ahD(x(\theta);P)
(blue curve).

The minimum
P
-mass of a hemisphere is
1/8
, and
ahD(x;P)=1/8
for all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}_{-}\cup\mathbb{S}^{1}_{0}
. For
x=(\cos(\theta),\sin(\theta))^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}

ahD

(28)

ahD(x;P)=\begin{cases}1/8&\mbox{for }\theta\in(-\pi,0],\\

In particular, for
\alpha=3/8
we have that
ahD_{\alpha}(P)
corresponds to the interval of angles
\theta\in[\pi/4,3\pi/4]
, but the (spherical) closure of the set
\left\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\colon ahD(x;P)>3/8\right\}
is the interval of angles
\theta\in[\pi/2,3\pi/4]
. The strict monotonicity condition (
25
) is, therefore, not satisfied for
\alpha=3/8
.

Construct now for
\varepsilon>0
small and fixed a sequence of measures
\left\{P_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
defined as similarly as
P
, but in the interval
\theta\in(\pi/2,\pi]
we put
P_{n}
-mass
1/2+(-1)^{n}\varepsilon
, and in the interval
(0,\pi/4]
we assign
P_{n}
-mass
1/4+(-1)^{n+1}\varepsilon
. In other words, for
n
odd we increase the mass in
(0,\pi/4]
slightly above
1/4
and decrease the mass in
(\pi/2,\pi]
below
1/2
, and for
n
even the other way around. We certainly have that
P_{n}
converges weakly to
P
as
n\to\infty
, and since
P
satisfies the smoothness condition (
S
), Theorem
10
also gives that
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|ahD(x;P_{n})-ahD(x;P)\right|
vanishes as
n\to\infty
. Nevertheless, a simple computation as in (
28
) gives that for
\alpha=3/8
we have
ahD_{\alpha}(P_{n})
contains the interval of angles
\theta\in(\pi/4,\pi/2]
for
n
odd, but not for
n
even. As such, the sequence of sets
\left\{ahD_{\alpha}(P_{n})\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
does not converge in the Hausdorff distance to any set, and (
27
) cannot be true.

Note that the problem with Example
4
does not rest in the fact that the support of
P
is disconnected; one could easily add another mixture component supported on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
to
P
with sufficiently low weight, and the same phenomenon appears. The core of the problem is in the symmetry of
P
in the angle
\theta\in[\pi/4,\pi/2)
, giving that any halfspace
H_{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
with
u=(\cos(\theta),\sin(\theta))^{\mathsf{T}}
and
\theta\in[3\pi/4,\pi/2]
has the same
P
-mass equal to
\alpha=3/8
. One natural way of enforcing (
25
) is to forbid these symmetries, as done in the following result.

Theorem 12
.

Suppose that
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
has a dominant hemisphere, meaning that there exists
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that for all
B\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\cap H_{u}
of non-null spherical Lebesgue measure we have
P(B)>P(-B)
. Assume, in addition, that
P
is smooth in the sense of (
S
). Then is the condition (
25
) satisfied for
P
.

Another way of obtaining the strict monotonicity condition (
25
) is to use directly Theorem
1
and assume that
P
is supported in a hemisphere
H
. Then, we can assume that after gnomonic projection to the tangent hyperplane at the pole of
H
, the (non-negative) measure
P_{\pm}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
satisfies smoothness and contiguity of its support as assumed in
Laketa and Nagy, 2022
.

4.4.
Large sample properties

As our final result, we consider the problem of estimating
ahD
from a random sample. We consider a sequence
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
of independent random variables defined on
\Omega
sampled from the distribution
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
. We attach to each
X_{i}\equiv X_{i}(\omega)
mass
1/n
and denote the resulting (random) empirical measure by
P_{n}(\omega)\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
. When computing the depth, we typically estimate the depth of the (unknown) distribution
P
by means of the sample depth, based on plugging the empirical measure
P_{n}(\omega)
into the depth (
3
) instead of
P
. The depth
ahD(x;P_{n}(\omega))
is called the sample angular halfspace depth of
x
. In the following result, we prove that the sample
ahD
almost surely uniformly approximates its population counterpart
ahD(\cdot;P)
as
n\to\infty
, and the same is true for the derived quantities
\mathfrak{M}(P_{n}(\omega))
and
ahD_{\alpha}(P_{n}(\omega))
.

Theorem 13
.

Suppose that
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,
is a sequence of independent random variables with distribution
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
, and let
P_{n}(\omega)\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
be the (random) empirical measure corresponding to
X_{1}(\omega),\dots,X_{n}(\omega)
.

(i)

Then we have

\mathsf{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega\colon\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\left|ahD(x;P_{n}(\omega))-ahD(x;P)\right|=0\right\}\right)=1.

(ii)

If
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
is such that
ahD
is strictly monotone for
P
, then for any closed interval
A\subset(\min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}ahD(x;P),\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}ahD(x;P))
we can write

\mathsf{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega\colon\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\alpha\in A}\delta_{H}\left(ahD_{\alpha}(P_{n}(\omega)),ahD_{\alpha}(P)\right)=0\right\}\right)=1.

(iii)

If
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
satisfies the conditions of part
(iii)
of Theorem
9
, then

\mathsf{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega\colon\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{H}\left(\mathfrak{M}(P_{n}(\omega)),\mathfrak{M}(P)\right)=0\right\}\right)=1.

Appendix A
Proofs of the theoretical results

A.1.
Proof of Theorem
1

In the definition of
ahD
in (
3
) we only consider halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose boundary passes through the origin. Denote by
G_{u}
the intersection of
\mathcal{G}
and
H_{0,u}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
. When considered as a subset of the affine space
\mathcal{G}
, the set
G_{u}
is a generalized halfspace:

(i)

it is a halfspace in
\mathcal{G}
if
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\setminus\left\{e_{d},-e_{d}\right\}
,

(ii)

an empty set if
u=-e_{d}
, and

(iii)

equals
\mathcal{G}
if
u=e_{d}
.

By the definition (
9
) of the gnomonic projection
\xi
, a point
y\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\setminus\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{0}
lies in
H_{0,u}
if and only if
\left\langle y,u\right\rangle=\left\langle\xi(y),u\right\rangle\left\langle y,e_{d}\right\rangle\geq 0
. If
y\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{+}
, this is equivalent with
\xi(y)\in G_{u}=H_{0,u}\cap\mathcal{G}
; for
y\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{-}
we have that
y\in H_{0,u}
if and only if
\left\langle\xi(y),u\right\rangle\leq 0
, that is
\xi(y)\in G_{-u}
. In terms of
P_{\pm}
we can thus express the
P
-mass of any
H_{0,u}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
as

(29)

P\left(H_{0,u}\right)=P_{+}\left(G_{u}\right)+P_{-}\left(G_{-u}\right).

Writing
G_{u}^{\circ}
and
\partial G_{u}
for the relative interior and the relative boundary of the
(d-1)
-dimensional generalized halfspace
G_{u}
in
\mathcal{G}
, for any
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we have
P\left(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{-}\right)=P_{-}\left(\mathcal{G}\right)=P_{-}\left(G_{-u}^{\circ}\right)+P_{-}\left(\partial G_{-u}\right)+P_{-}\left(G_{u}^{\circ}\right)=P_{-}\left(G_{-u}\right)+P_{-}\left(G_{u}^{\circ}\right)
, simply because the sets
G_{-u}
and
G_{u}^{\circ}
are disjoint and decompose
\mathcal{G}
. Thus, we can rewrite (
29
) to

(30)

P\left(H_{0,u}\right)=P\left(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{-}\right)+P_{+}\left(G_{u}\right)-P_{-}\left(G_{u}^{\circ}\right).

This holds true for any normal vector
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, including
u=e_{d}
or
-e_{d}
.

Since we assumed in (
8
) that
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{+}
, we know that
x\in H_{0,u}
if and only if
\xi(x)\in G_{u}
. Plugging (
30
) into (
3
) we obtain

\displaystyle ahD(x;P)

\displaystyle=P\left(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{-}\right)+\inf\left\{P_{+}\left(G_{u}\right)-P_{-}\left(G_{u}^{\circ}\right)\colon u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\mbox{ and }x\in H_{0,u}\right\}

\displaystyle=P\left(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{-}\right)+\inf\left\{P_{+}\left(G_{u}\right)-P_{-}\left(G_{u}^{\circ}\right)\colon u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\mbox{ and }\xi(x)\in G_{u}\right\}

\displaystyle=P\left(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{-}\right)+\inf\left\{P_{+}\left(H\right)-P_{-}\left(H^{\circ}\right)\colon H\in\mathcal{H}^{*}\mbox{ and }\xi(x)\in H\right\}

where in the last expression, it makes no difference whether the generalized halfspaces
\mathcal{H}^{*}
are considered in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, or
\mathcal{G}
, or
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
when identified with
\mathcal{G}
. Considering
\mathcal{H}^{*}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, we have proved the first part of our theorem.

Suppose now that
P_{-}
satisfies the continuity condition (
12
). Then,
P_{-}(\partial H)=0
for all
H\in\mathcal{H}^{*}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and thus
P_{-}(H^{\circ})=P_{-}\left(H\right)
, which allows us to write
P_{+}\left(H\right)-P_{-}\left(H^{\circ}\right)=P_{+}(H)-P_{-}(H)=P_{\pm}(H)
in (
11
).

A.2.
Proof of Theorem
3

We first use the portmanteau theorem
(
Dudley, 2002
, Theorem 11.1.1)
to show that the function

(31)

f\colon\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to[0,1]\colon\left(P,u\right)\mapsto P\left(H_{0,u}\right)

is upper semi-continuous at any measure
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
, and continuous at any
P
that satisfies (
S
). To see that, fix
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
and take
u_{n}\to u
in
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Since
u_{n}
converges to
u
, it is possible to find a sequence of orthogonal matrices
\left\{O_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
such that
O_{n}u_{n}=u
for each
n=1,2,\dots
, and
O_{n}
converges to the identity matrix
I_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. Define a measure
Q_{n}\in\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
as the distribution of
O_{n}X_{n}
with
X_{n}\sim P_{n}
. By Slutsky’s theorem
(
Jiang, 2010
, Theorem 2.13)
, we then have that
Q_{n}\xrightarrow{w}P
as
n\to\infty
. At the same time, for each
n=1,2,\dots
we can write

O_{n}H_{0,u_{n}}=\left\{O_{n}y\colon\left\langle y,u_{n}\right\rangle\geq 0\right\}=\left\{z\colon\left\langle O_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}z,u_{n}\right\rangle\geq 0\right\}=H_{0,O_{n}u_{n}},

where we used
O_{n}^{-1}=O_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}
as follows from the orthogonality of
O_{n}
and therefore, we have

(32)

P_{n}(H_{0,u_{n}})=\mathsf{P}\left(X_{n}\in H_{0,u_{n}}\right)=\mathsf{P}\left(O_{n}X_{n}\in O_{n}H_{0,u_{n}}\right)=Q_{n}(H_{0,u}).

Thus, we have a fixed closed halfspace
H_{0,u}
and a sequence of measures
\left\{Q_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)
such that
Q_{n}\xrightarrow{w}P
as
n\to\infty
. The portmanteau theorem
(
Dudley, 2002
, Theorem 11.1.1)
and (
32
) then give that

{\lim\sup}_{n\to\infty}f(P_{n},u_{n})={\lim\sup}_{n\to\infty}P_{n}(H_{0,u_{n}})={\lim\sup}_{n\to\infty}Q_{n}(H_{0,u})\leq P(H_{0,u})=f(P,u)

as we wanted to show. In the special case when also (
S
) is valid, we obtain that
H_{0,u}
is a continuity set of
P
, and the portmanteau theorem gives even

{\lim}_{n\to\infty}f(P_{n},u_{n})={\lim}_{n\to\infty}P_{n}(H_{0,u_{n}})={\lim}_{n\to\infty}Q_{n}(H_{0,u})=P(H_{0,u})=f(P,u).

Now for
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
denote

(33)

v(x)=\left\{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\colon x\in H_{0,u}\right\}=\left\{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\colon\left\langle x,u\right\rangle\geq 0\right\}=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\cap H_{0,x}

the set of all inner normals of halfspaces taken in the infimum in the definition (
3
) of
ahD(x;P)
. The set-valued mapping
v(x)
is continuous in
x
in the sense of Painlevé-Kuratowski convergence
(
Rockafellar and Wets, 1998
, Chapter 5.B)
, and
ahD
can be written as

ahD\left(x;P\right)=\inf_{u\in v(x)}f(P,u).

We have verified all the assumptions of Berge’s maximum theorem
(
Berge, 1997
, pp. 115–117)
on parametric optimization, which asserts that the depth function
ahD\colon\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathcal{P}\left({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\right)\to[0,1]\colon(x,P)\mapsto ahD(x;P)
is upper semi-continuous in both arguments for general
P
, and continuous in both arguments at any
P
satisfying (
S
).

A.3.
Proof of Theorem
4

Suppose first that
x\in ahD_{\alpha}(P)
, meaning that
ahD\left(x;P\right)\geq\alpha
. If
x
does not belong to the right-hand side of (
18
), then there must exist an open halfspace
G
whose boundary passes through the origin,
P(G^{\mathsf{c}})<\alpha
, and
x\notin G
. Take
H=G^{\mathsf{c}}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Now, we have
x\in H\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
P(H)<\alpha
, which contradicts
ahD\left(x;P\right)\geq\alpha
. Necessarily,
ahD_{\alpha}(P)\subseteq\bigcap\left\{G\colon G^{\mathsf{c}}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}\mbox{ and }P(G^{\mathsf{c}})<\alpha\right\}
.

For the other direction, suppose that
x\notin ahD_{\alpha}(P)
. Then we have
\alpha>ahD(x;P)=\inf_{H\in\mathcal{H}_{0}\colon x\in H}P(H)
, meaning that there exists a closed halfspace
H\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
such that
x\in H
and
P(H)<\alpha
. Consider the open halfspace
G=H^{\mathsf{c}}
. Then
x\notin G
,
G^{\mathsf{c}}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
P(G^{\mathsf{c}})=P(H)<\alpha
, meaning that
x
does not belong to the right-hand side of (
18
). This gives
ahD_{\alpha}(P)\supseteq\bigcap\left\{G\colon G^{\mathsf{c}}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}\mbox{ and }P(G^{\mathsf{c}})<\alpha\right\}
, and proves our claim.

A.4.
Proof of Theorem
5

Take any
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
that is not in
ahD_{\alpha}(P)
. Then
ahD\left(x;P\right)<\alpha
and therefore, there is a closed halfspace
H\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
such that
x\in H
and
P(H)<\alpha
. First, we show that we can also assume that
H
satisfies the additional property
x\in H^{\circ}
. Indeed, if
x\in\partial H
and
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is the inner normal of
H
, we choose
u_{n}=\cos(1/n)u+\sin(1/n)x
for
n=1,2,\dots
. Since
\left\langle x,u\right\rangle=0
we have

\left\|u_{n}\right\|^{2}=(\cos(1/n))^{2}\left\langle u,u\right\rangle+(\sin(1/n))^{2}\left\langle x,x\right\rangle=1,

and thus
u_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. In addition,

\left\langle x,u_{n}\right\rangle=\cos(1/n)\left\langle x,u\right\rangle+\sin(1/n)\left\langle x,x\right\rangle=\sin(1/n)>0,

and for
H_{n}=H_{0,u_{n}}
we can write
x\in H_{n}^{\circ}
for all
n
. Halfspaces
H_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
correspond to slightly tilted
H
so that

The following auxiliary lemma will be useful.

Let

be a set that is either open or finite in the sphere

(A)

the origin lies in the convex hull of

(B)

the origin lies in the convex hull of

(C)

\bigcup_{r\in R}H_{0,r}=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

(D)

\bigcup_{u\in U}H_{0,u}=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

Proof of Lemma

.

Since
U\subset R
, the convex hull of

is a subset of the convex hull of
R
, and
(A)
implies
(B)
. For the opposite implication, suppose that the origin lies in the convex hull of
R
. Carathéodory’s theorem
(
Schneider, 2014

gives that there exists

(not necessarily distinct) points

0=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\lambda_{i}\,r_{i}\mbox{ for some $0\leq\lambda_{i}$ with $\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\lambda_{i}=1$.}

Since

\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\lambda_{i}\left\|r_{i}\right\|>0

\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\frac{\lambda_{i}\left\|r_{i}\right\|}{\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}\lambda_{j}\left\|r_{j}\right\|}\frac{r_{i}}{\left\|r_{i}\right\|}=0,

and setting

(B)

.

Statements

We apply the proof of implication
(ComD)

\Rightarrow

(ComR)
from
Dyckerhoff, 2017

\mathbb{R}^{d}
are considered. Nevertheless, the fact that one works in

is used only in several arguments in the proofs, and many of the derivations work precisely in the same way for measures in
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

For starters, in
Dyckerhoff, 2017
it is assumed that the function

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
must be a depth in the sense of Definition 2.1 in

are all

(R1)

affine equivariant (for
\alpha\geq 0
),

(R2)

bounded (for
\alpha>0
),

(R3)

closed (for
\alpha>0
), and

(R4)

star-shaped (for all
\alpha\geq 0
).

The requirement
(R1)
of affine equivariance is, however, not used anywhere in the proof of Theorem 4.5 in
Dyckerhoff, 2017
. The (spherical counterparts of the) additional conditions
R2

are all satisfied for

, because

•

for all

and
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is itself bounded, giving
R2
,

•

each

, which verifies

, and

•

(spherical) star-shapedness of

in

follows immediately from the (spherical) convexity of

as proved in Theorem
4
.

Thus, the advances proved in
Dyckerhoff, 2017
can be used in our setup.

Mimicking the proof of Theorem 4.5 in
Dyckerhoff, 2017
for a depth

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we see that the core of the proof actually lies in proving

•

the continuity of the function
\alpha\mapsto D_{\alpha}(P)
in the Hausdorff metric (

), and

•

the bound

(39)

D_{\beta+\gamma}(P)\subseteq D_{\beta}(P_{n})\subseteq D_{\beta-\gamma}(P)\quad\mbox{for all $n\geq N$ and $\beta\geq\alpha_{1}$},

where
\alpha_{1}
is the lower endpoint of the interval

,

is a small enough constant, and

The continuity of the mapping
\alpha\mapsto D_{\alpha}(P)
is obtained in
Dyckerhoff, 2017

A.13.
Proof of Theorem
12

The condition of existence of a dominant hemisphere immediately implies that
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\setminus H_{u}
is an open hemisphere of minimum depth as in Theorem
6
. The smoothness of
P
and the continuity of
ahD
in
x
from Theorem
3
then assert that the whole closed hemisphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\cap H_{-u}
is of minimum
ahD
. To prove the strict monotonicity of
ahD
, we pick any point
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
from the
ahD
-median set of
P
. Because of the smoothness of
P
, we can argue as in
Laketa and Nagy, 2022
to show that
ahD_{\alpha}(P)
must have non-empty (spherical) interior for all
\alpha\leq\max_{y\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}ahD(y;P)
. Further, the dominant hemisphere condition says that for any

such that

we have

P(H^{\prime})=P(H^{\prime}\cap H)+P(H^{\prime}\setminus H)>P(H^{\prime}\cap H)+P(H\setminus H^{\prime})=P(H),

since
H^{\prime}\cap H\subset H_{u}
. This allows us to use the arguments as in the linear case and
hD
in parts (iii) and (v) of the proof of
Laketa and Nagy, 2022
, which together give (
25
).

Addressing Behavior Model Inaccuracies for Safe Motion Control in Uncertain Dynamic Environments
Minjun Sung, Hunmin Kim, Naira Hovakimyan

Abstract
Uncertainties in the environment and behavior model inaccuracies compromise the state estimation of a dynamic obstacle and its trajectory predictions, introducing biases in estimation and shifts in predictive distributions. Addressing these challenges is crucial to safely control an autonomous system. In this paper, we propose a novel algorithm SIED-MPC , which synergistically integrates Simultaneous State and Input Estimation (SSIE) and Distributionally Robust Model Predictive Control (DR-MPC) using model confidence evaluation. The SSIE process produces unbiased state estimates and optimal input gap estimates to assess the confidence of the behavior model, defining the ambiguity radius for DR-MPC to handle predictive distribution shifts. This systematic confidence evaluation leads to producing safe inputs with an adequate level of conservatism. Our algorithm demonstrated a reduced collision rate in autonomous driving simulations through improved state estimation, with a 54% shorter average computation time.



I
Introduction

Ensuring the safety of mobile robots and Autonomous Vehicles (AV) in uncertain environments with dynamic obstacles is a paramount challenge. While the risk of uncertainties can be reduced with accurate
estimation
and
prediction
, integration between these fields for safe motion control remains insufficient. Specifically, estimation methods often assume control input to be directly accessible or the controller to be known
[
1
]
, while predictions propagate a behavior model through obstacle dynamics assuming a reliable estimation is given
[
2
, Sec. 3]
. This circular assumption suggests a gap in the integration of estimation and prediction, limiting the potential for achieving truly safe robotic and AV systems.

The central challenge in integrating estimation and prediction lies in their reliance on a behavior model of an obstacle. A flawed behavior model not only biases the state estimator but also causes shifts in the predictive distribution. Furthermore, biases introduced by the estimation process itself can further exacerbate shifts in this distribution. Consequently, behavioral modeling techniques for accurate estimation and predictions have attracted significant interest for robots and AVs. Recent advancements in machine learning, particularly in processing sequential data, have substantially improved the reliability of predictive state sequences
[
2
, Sec. 5]
. However, even the most advanced models encounter corner cases and residual inaccuracies, resulting in discrepancies between the predicted and actual obstacle behaviors
[
3
,
4
]
. This raises the following questions:

1.

How can we systematically enhance the robustness of both estimation and prediction processes against errors in behavioral modeling?

2.

What advantages could be derived from addressing these processes in a unified manner?

In this work, we introduce a novel framework that synergistically combines Simultaneous State and Input Estimation (SSIE) and Distributionally Robust Model Predictive Control (DR-MPC) to address the above questions. Specifically, the SSIE process enables an unbiased estimation of an obstacle state by explicit estimation of the input gap induced by the behavior modeling error. This approach allows us to locally assess the
confidence
of the behavior model, which we leverage to compute an adequate level of conservatism in producing an obstacle’s distributive forecasts. The integrated controller, termed S
SIE
and confidence-based
D
istributionally Robust
MPC
(
SIED-MPC
), is presented as a computationally tractable algorithm that effectively mitigates safety concerns arising from inaccuracies in the behavior model. The efficacy of our algorithm - accurate state and input estimation, reduced collision rate, and controlled conservatism - is examined through AV traffic simulation.

II-B

Limitations of a Conventional Approach

Given the behavior model
{\hat{\pi}}
, it is common practice to formulate the following MPC problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathfrak{u}}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}r(x_{k+l},u_{k+l})+p(x_{k+L})

(4a)

s.t

\displaystyle x_{k+l+1}=f(x_{k+l},u_{k+l}),~l=0,\ldots,L-1

(4b)

\displaystyle d^{\hat{\pi}}_{k+l-1}={\hat{\pi}}(\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{k+l-1}),~l=1,\ldots,L~

(4c)

\displaystyle\hat{\xi}_{k+l}=g(\hat{\xi}_{k+l-1})+B_{k-1}d^{\hat{\pi}}_{k+l-1},~l=1,\ldots,L

(4d)

\displaystyle s(x_{k+l},\hat{\xi}_{k+l})\leq 0,~l=0,\ldots,L~

(4e)

\displaystyle u_{k+l}\in\mathbb{U},~l=0,\ldots,L-1,

(4f)

where
\mathfrak{u}=\{u_{k},\ldots,u_{k+L-1}\}

. The first element

of the optimal control sequence
\mathfrak{u}^{\star}
is applied and this process is repeated for each time step
[
15
]
. While employing this approach was successful in various scenarios, it inherently suffers from
two limitations
: 1) biased state estimation and 2) distribution shift in the state prediction.

Specifically, the input gap, represented by the difference
d^{\hat{\pi}}_{k-1}-d_{k-1}
, introduces a bias in the dynamical system (
2
). This compromises the assumption required by the KF-based methods that their uncertainties are zero-mean Gaussian
[
1
]
. On top of the this, repeated incorporation of predicted control inputs

in each prediction step (
4d
) compounds inaccuracies in the state prediction distribution. This distribution shift reduces the reliability of the safety constraint (
4e
), which could lead to undesirable outcomes. Fig.
1
visualizes the biased estimation and compounding effect of inaccurate behavior model in the predictive distribution.

Illustration of estimation bias and compounding predictive distribution shift induced by the behavior gap
{\hat{\pi}}-\pi
. Even small estimation error and behavior gap of an obstacle can result in large shifts in its predictive distributions. An unsafe trajectory can be predicted as safe.

To address the first limitation, we present the SSIE algorithm in Sec.
III
that computes the unbiased estimate of the current state through optimal estimation of the previous input. Then, we formulate a DR-MPC problem in Sec.
IV
to overcome the second challenge of distribution shift. We evaluate the confidence of the behavior model through the SSIE process and use it to determine the size of the ambiguity set such that the conservatism is controlled according to the local accuracy of the behavior model (Sec.
IV-B1
). The integrated method is presented as an
SIED-MPC
algorithm in Sec.
IV-C
. The efficacy of our method is analyzed in Sec.
V
.

III
Simultaneous State and Input Estimation

In this section, we propose the SSIE algorithm, a modification of the ISE algorithm
[
5
,
16
]

\xi

d^{\hat{\pi}}-d
at each time step along with their covariance matrices when the output
\zeta

Denote the estimates of the true state
\xi_{k}
at each time step
k

\hat{\xi}_{k}
, where
\hat{\xi}_{0}=\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{0}\sim\Xi_{0}}[\xi_{0}]
. For
k=1,2\ldots
, we linearize (
2a
) around
\hat{\xi}_{k-1}
to obtain

\displaystyle\xi_{k}=g(\hat{\xi}_{k-1})+A_{k-1}\tilde{\xi}_{k-1}+B_{k-1}d_{k-1}+\omega_{k-1}+e_{k-1},

(5)

where
\tilde{\xi}_{k-1}\triangleq\xi_{k-1}-\hat{\xi}_{k-1}
and
A_{k-1}\triangleq\frac{\partial g(\xi)}{\partial\xi}\big|_{\xi=\hat{\xi}_{k-1}}
. Higher order errors are lumped in
e_{k-1}
, which we assume to be negligible in the remainder of this work. Furthermore, we assume that
\|A_{k-1}\|\triangleq\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\xi}}/\{\mathbf{0}\}}\frac{\|A_{k-1}\xi\|_{2}}{\|\xi\|_{2}}
is bounded.

IV
Distributionally Robust Safety Constraint

In this section, we propose a method to address the distributional shifts that result from an inaccurate behavior model. We begin by explaining the widely recognized CVaR risk measure in Sec.
IV-A
to transform the deterministic safety constraint (
4e
) into a probabilistic constraint. Subsequently, in Sec.
IV-B
, we develop a distributionally robust optimization (DRO) problem that incorporates a confidence-based ambiguity. We also present its tractable reformulation for its practical implementation.

IV-A

Conditional Value at Risk

Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) has been widely used in robotics, finance, and other domains for its adequacy in capturing risks in an extreme tail and has a tractable form when used in risk-aware optimization problems
[
18
]
. CVaR of a random loss
S\sim\mathcal{P}
measures the expected value of
S
in the conditional distribution of its upper
(1-\alpha)
tail for
\alpha\in[0,1)

[
19
]
and is expressed as

\textrm{CVaR}^{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}[S]\triangleq\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\mathcal{P}}\left[z+\frac{\max\{S-z,0\}}{1-\alpha}\right].

(18)

We aim to convert deterministic safety constraint (
4e
) into a relaxed probabilistic constraint using CVaR to accommodate the stochastic characteristics of the obstacle trajectory predictions (
2
). To this end, plug (
4e
) into (
18
) to obtain

\textrm{CVaR}^{\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+l|k}}_{\alpha}[s(x_{k+l},\hat{\xi}_{k+l})]\leq 0,~l=1,2,\ldots,

where
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+l|k}
is the nominal distribution of the loss function
s(x_{k+l},\hat{\xi}_{k+l})
. In the following, we explain the two steps to derive
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+l|k}
: 1) we propagate multi-step prediction of the nominal obstacle state distribution and 2) compute the approximation of the loss distribution.

IV-A
1
Propagating Nominal State Distribution

The mean state predictions, denoted
\hat{\mu}^{\xi}_{k+l}
for
l=0,1,\ldots
, are initialized for
l=0
as
\hat{\mu}^{\xi}_{k}=\hat{\xi}_{k}
, and are derived at each
k
by the SSIE procedure. By autoregressively applying the given policy model
{\hat{\pi}}
, we obtain the mean state predictions as

\hat{\mu}^{\xi}_{k+l|k}=g(\hat{\mu}^{\xi}_{k+l-1|k})+B_{k+l-1}d^{\hat{\pi}}_{k+l-1},\quad l=1,2,\ldots.

(19)

Likewise, we propagate the covariance matrix as

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}^{\xi}_{k+l|k}=A_{k+l}\hat{\Sigma}^{\xi}_{k+l-1|k}A_{k+l}^{\top}+Q,

(20)

where we assumed the invariance of the state prediction error (
12
) to hold throughout the multi-step predictions.

IV-A
2
Computing Nominal Loss Distribution

The Gaussian approximation of the nominal loss distribution
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+l|k}
is obtained by the first-order Taylor series expansion of the loss function around the nominal state distribution as
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+l|k}=\mathcal{N}({\hat{\mu}}^{s}_{k+l},{\hat{\sigma}}^{s}_{k+l})
, where

\displaystyle{\hat{\mu}}^{s}_{k+l}

\displaystyle=s(x_{k+l},\hat{\mu}^{\xi}_{k+l})

(21a)

\displaystyle{\hat{\sigma}}^{s}_{k+l}

\displaystyle=\nabla s(x_{k+l},\hat{\mu}^{\xi}_{k+l})^{\top}\hat{\Sigma}^{\xi}_{k+l}\nabla s(x_{k+l},\hat{\mu}^{\xi}_{k+l}).

(21b)

The derivation follows the similar spirit to Section
III
(or see
[
20
, Section 4.3]
).

IV-B

Distributionally Robust CVaR

The nominal state distribution is subject to compounding error induced by the input gap at each time step. To address this problem, we consider a set of distributions
\mathbb{P}_{k+l|k}
around
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+l|k}
, within which the worst-case safety loss is searched for. Correspondingly, the DR-CVaR measure is defined as

\displaystyle\textrm{DR-CVaR}^{\hat{{\mathcal{P}}}_{k+l|k}}_{\alpha,\theta}\left[s(x_{k+l},\hat{\xi}_{k+l|k})\right]

(22)

\displaystyle\triangleq\sup_{\hat{\mathcal{P}}\in\mathbb{P}_{k+l|k}}\textrm{CVaR}^{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}_{\alpha}\left[s(x_{k+l},\hat{\xi}_{k+l|k})\right].

Two questions arise: 1) How can we choose the
ambiguity set

\mathbb{P}_{k+l|k}
? 2) Given the search space
\mathbb{P}_{k+l|k}
, how can we solve for the outer supremum? To answer these questions, we introduce the Wasserstein ambiguity set
[
21
]
and propose a systematic method to choose the
radius
of an ambiguity set. Then, we upper bound the constraint
DR-CVaR
for computational tractability in solving the DRO problem.

Definition 1
.

A Wassersteien ambiguity set of a ball of radius
\theta>0
centered at
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+l|k}
is defined as

\mathbb{P}_{k+l|k}\triangleq\{\hat{\mathcal{P}}\in\mathfrak{F}(\mathbb{R})|W_{2}(\hat{\mathcal{P}},\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+l|k})\leq\theta\},

where
\mathfrak{F}(\mathbb{R})
is the family of all probability distributions with finite second moments supported on
\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, for some distributions
\mathcal{R}_{\{1,2\}}\in\mathfrak{F}(\mathbb{R})
,
W_{2}(\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2})
is the 2-Wasserstein distance between the two distributions:

\displaystyle W_{2}(\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2})\triangleq

\displaystyle\left(\inf_{\psi\in\Psi(\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2})}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\|r_{1}-r_{2}\|^{2}_{2}~\psi(dr_{1},dr_{2})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\Psi(\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2})
denotes the set of all joint probability distributions of
r_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
r_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
with marginals
\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}
, respectively
[
10
, Definition 1]
.

IV-B
1
Confidence-Based Ambiguity Set

A commonly used approach to define
\theta
is to heuristically set
\theta
sufficiently large to cover the maximum plausible distribution shifts
[
12
]
. However, such approach lacks flexibility and reduces the feasible solution set, often encountering computational challenges
[
22
]
. Inspired by
[
23
]
, we use the input gap and its covariance estimate
\hat{\Delta}_{k}
and
\Sigma^{\Delta}_{k}
to systematically choose
\theta
.

Specifically, to evaluate the confidence of the behavior model, denoted as
F^{\hat{\pi}}_{k}
, we cache the last
m
-step estimates
\{(\hat{\Delta}_{k-m},\Sigma^{\Delta}_{k-m}),\ldots,(\hat{\Delta}_{k-1},\Sigma^{\Delta}_{k-1})\}
to compute

F^{\hat{\pi}}_{k}\triangleq\sqrt{\frac{1}{m}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\hat{\Delta}^{\top}_{k-l}(\Sigma^{\Delta}_{k-l})^{-1}\hat{\Delta}_{k-l}}.

(23)

A smaller
F^{\hat{\pi}}_{k}
indicates that the input gap distribution is reliably close to zero.

We propose the following dynamic ambiguity set model that controls the conservatism in the DRO problem based on the accuracy of the behavior model:

\mathbb{P}_{k+l|k}\triangleq\{\hat{\mathcal{P}}\in\mathfrak{F}(\mathbb{R})|W_{2}(\hat{\mathcal{P}},\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+l|k})\leq\theta(F^{\hat{\pi}}_{k})\},

(24)

where
\theta(F^{\hat{\pi}}_{k})\triangleq\theta_{\max}\tanh(\tau F^{\hat{\pi}}_{k})
.
In this model,
\theta_{\max}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
represents the maximum radius, and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a temperature parameter that modulates the sensitivity of the ambiguity radius to confidence
F^{\hat{\pi}}_{k}
.

IV-B
2
Tractable Reformulation

Evaluating the safety constraint (
22
) with the dynamic ambiguity set (
24
) presents a computational challenge as it involves solving an infinite dimensional optimization problem. To address this, we turn to the following Lemma:

Lemma IV.1
.

(Gelbrich Bound
[
24
, Theorem 2.1]
)
Let two distributions
\hat{\mathcal{P}}
and
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+l|k}
on
\mathbb{R}
have means
{\hat{\mu}}^{s},{\hat{\mu}}^{s}_{k+l}\in\mathbb{R}
and variances
{\hat{\sigma}}^{s},{\hat{\sigma}}^{s}_{k+l}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, respectively. Then, the following inequality holds:

W_{2}(\hat{\mathcal{P}},\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+l|k})\geq\sqrt{({\hat{\mu}}^{s}-{\hat{\mu}}^{s}_{k+l})^{2}+({\hat{\sigma}}^{s}-{\hat{\sigma}}^{s}_{k+l})^{2}}.

The bound is exact if
\hat{\mathcal{P}}
and
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+l|k}
are elliptical distributions with the same density generator.

Theorem IV.2
.

Suppose that the nominal distribution of the loss
s(x_{k+l},\hat{\xi}_{k+1|k})
is given as
\mathcal{N}(\mu^{s}_{k+l},(\sigma^{s}_{k+l})^{2})
, where
\mu^{s}_{k+l}
and
\sigma^{s}_{k+l}
are defined in (
21a
) and (
21b
), respectively. Then, we have the following upper-bound for the
DR-CVaR
with the radius
\theta(F^{\hat{\pi}}_{k})
:

\displaystyle\textrm{DR-CVaR}_{\alpha,\theta(F^{\hat{\pi}}_{k})}^{\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+l|k}}[s(x_{k+l},\hat{\xi}_{k+l})]

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~\leq\mu^{s}_{k+l}+\gamma\sigma^{s}_{k+l}+\theta(F^{\hat{\pi}}_{k})\sqrt{1+\gamma^{2}},

(25)

where
\gamma\triangleq\sqrt{\alpha/(1-\alpha)}
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{k+l|k}^{\theta}
denote an ambiguity set around the estimated mean
{\hat{\mu}}^{s}_{k+l}
and variance
({\hat{\sigma}}^{s}_{k+l})^{2}
, i.e.
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{k+l|k}^{\theta}\triangleq\{(\mu,\sigma^{2})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}~|~(\mu-{\hat{\mu}}^{s}_{k+l})^{2}+(\sigma-{\hat{\sigma}}^{s}_{k+l})^{2}\leq\theta^{2}\}.

It follows from Lemma
IV.1
that
\mathbb{P}_{k+l|k}\subseteq\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{k+l|k}^{\theta}

[
10
, Proposition 1]
, which gives

\displaystyle\textrm{DR-CVaR}_{\alpha,\theta(F^{\hat{\pi}}_{k})}^{\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+l|k}}[s(x_{k+l},\hat{\xi}_{k+l})]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\bar{\mathcal{P}}\in\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{k+l|k}^{\theta}}\textrm{CVaR}^{\bar{\mathcal{P}}}_{\alpha}[s(x_{k+l},\hat{\xi}_{k+l|k})]

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\mu_{k+l},\sigma^{2}_{k+l})\in\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{k+l|k}^{\theta}}\sup_{\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R},\mu_{k+l},\sigma_{k+l}^{2})}\textrm{CVaR}^{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}[s(x_{k+l},\hat{\xi}_{k+l})],

where
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R},\mu_{k+l},\sigma_{k+l})
denotes the Chebyshev uncertainty set that contains all distributions on
\mathbb{R}
with mean
\mu_{k+l}
and variance
\sigma_{k+l}

[
10
]
.

The inner supremum has a closed form solution given by

\sup_{\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R},\mu_{k+l},\sigma_{k+l}^{2})}\textrm{CVaR}^{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}[s(x_{k+l},\hat{\xi}_{k+l})]=\mu_{k+l}+\gamma\sigma_{k+l},

where
\gamma\triangleq\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}

[
25
, Proposition 2]
. For a compact search space (finite second moment), we can rewrite the outer supremum as the following optimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{(\mu_{k+l},\sigma_{k+l})}-\mu_{k+l}-\gamma\sigma_{k+l}

s.t.

\displaystyle(\mu-{\hat{\mu}}^{s}_{k+l})^{2}+(\sigma-{\hat{\sigma}}^{s}_{k+l})^{2}\leq\theta^{2}.

The dual problem is obtained as

\displaystyle\max_{\lambda\geq 0}\min_{\mu,\sigma}\mathcal{L}(\mu_{k+l},\sigma_{k+l},\lambda)

\displaystyle=\max_{\lambda\geq 0}~({\hat{\mu}}^{s}_{k+l}+\frac{1}{2\lambda})+\gamma({\hat{\sigma}}^{s}_{k+l}+\frac{\gamma}{2\lambda})-\lambda\theta^{2},

(26)

where
\mathcal{L}
is the Lagrangian and
\lambda
is its associated multiplier. The solution to this problem is obtained as
\lambda=\frac{1}{\theta}\sqrt{\frac{1+\gamma^{2}}{2}}
, substituting which into (
26
) gives the desired result (
25
).
∎

VI
Conclusion

In this work, we proposed a safe motion control framework that can address both the estimation bias and the predictive distribution shift caused primarily by the inaccuracies of a behavior model. We derived the SSIE algorithm to produce an unbiased obstacle state estimation and an optimal input gap estimation when only a nominal behavior model is given. Leveraging the results from the SSIE, we proposed a confidence-based DR-MPC problem to address the shift in predictive distribution that reasonably adjusts the conservatism of the control.The integrated
SIED-MPC
algorithm demonstrated improved collision avoidance in an autonomous driving scenario with enhanced state estimation accuracy and shorter computation.

Manifolds of Lie-Group-Valued Cocycles and Discrete Cohomology
Alexandru CHIRVASITU and Jun PENG

Abstract
Consider a compact group G G acting on a real or complex Banach Lie group U U , by automorphisms in the relevant category, and leaving a central subgroup K ≤ U K\leq U invariant. We define the spaces Z n K ​ ( G , U ) {}_{K}Z^{n}(G,U) of K K -relative continuous cocycles as those maps G n → U {G^{n}\to U} whose coboundary is a K K -valued ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -cocycle; this applies to possibly non-abelian U U , in which case n = 1 n=1 . We show that the Z n K ​ ( G , U ) {}_{K}Z^{n}(G,U) are analytic submanifolds of the spaces C ⁡ ( G n , U ) C(G^{n},U) of continuous maps G n → U G^{n}\to U and that they decompose as disjoint unions of fiber bundles over manifolds of K K -valued cocycles. Applications include: (a) the fact that Z n ​ ( G , U ) ⊂ C ⁡ ( G n , U ) {Z^{n}(G,U)\subset C(G^{n},U)} is an analytic submanifold and its orbits under the adjoint of the group of U U -valued ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -cochains are open; (b) hence the cohomology spaces H n ​ ( G , U ) H^{n}(G,U) are discrete; (c) for unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebras A A and B B with A A finite-dimensional the space of morphisms A → B A\to B is an analytic manifold and nearby morphisms are conjugate under the unitary group U ⁡ ( B ) U(B) ; (d) the same goes for A A and B B Banach, with A A finite-dimensional and semisimple; (e) and for spaces of projective representations of compact groups in arbitrary C ∗ C^{*} algebras (the last recovering a result of Martin’s).

Introduction

The results below all revolve around the familiar pattern of rigidity in the presence of semisimplicity. Both terms are ambiguous and multiform, but roughly speaking:

•

‘rigidity’ means that “nearby” structures (morphisms, derivations, cocycles, etc.) are mutually equivalent, whatever that might mean;

•

while ‘semisimplicity’ is a stand-in for any of a number of behaviors having to do with cohomology vanishing of some type: semisimplicity in the representation-theoretic sense
[
24
, Definition 2.1]
for Lie or associative algebras or for compact groups (which amounts to the vanishing of
\mathrm{Ext}
functors),
separability

[
36

for associative algebras (i.e., Hochschild-cohomology triviality
[

), etc.

By way of an illustration, and to give as good an entry point to the discussion as any other, consider
[

Preliminaries

Apart from explicit warnings to the contrary, Banach algebras are assumed unital, as are morphisms. Per standard practice (e.g.,
[
15
, Definition 2.1.8]
), we assume throughout that in a unital Banach algebra the multiplicative unit has norm 1:
\|1\|=1
.

Some assorted notation:

•

Ban
is the category of Banach spaces and continuous linear maps, and
\text{\sc Ban}(E,F)
or
L(E,F)
is the space of continuous linear morphisms between two Banach spaces.

Banach spaces (and algebras) are either real or complex: the discussion can be carried out in parallel, so the discussion can be kept agnostic of the ground field.

•

BAlg
is the category of (unital) Banach algebras and continuous unital algebra morphisms (all of them, not just those of norm
\leq 1
; see Remark
2.1
).

The space of morphisms
A\to B
is denoted by
\text{\sc BAlg}(A,B)
is the space of unital-Banach-algebra morphisms
A\to B
: continuous, linear, unital, multiplicative maps.

•

Similarly,
C^{*}(A,B)
is the space of unital
C^{*}
-algebra morphisms between unital
C^{*}
-algebras
A
and
B
.

•

A^{\times}
is the multiplicative group of invertible elements in a Banach algebra
A
.

•

U(A)
is the unitary group of a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
.

Remark 2.1
.

While morphisms of
C^{*}
-algebras automatically have norm
1
(as follows, for instance, from
[
4
, Proposition 1.6.2]
), this is not the case, in general, for Banach algebras: the algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[\varepsilon]/\big(\varepsilon^{2}\big)
of
dual numbers
, equipped with an arbitrary Banach-algebra norm. Its endomorphisms are in bijection with
{\mathbb{C}}
, determined uniquely by how they scale
\varepsilon
.

It is not uncommon, in the literature, to work with the category
\text{\sc Ban}_{1}
of Banach spaces and
short
linear maps, i.e., those of norm at most
1
: see
[
1
, Example 1.48]
,
[
37
]
, etc. In many ways,
\text{\sc Ban}_{1}
is better behaved than the larger category

of Banach spaces and arbitrary linear continuous maps. In that context one would then consider only Banach-algebra morphisms of norm
\leq 1
; for our purposes though, this constraint is less natural and not particularly useful. For that reason, we emphasize that
\text{\sc BAlg}(A,B)

3
Cocycle manifolds and discrete cohomology

In the discussion below, we will frequently have to work with spaces

for compact (always Hausdorff) spaces

. They will always be equipped with the

[

As explained in

3.1
Group cohomology (possibly non-abelian)

The setup will be that of compact (occasionally finite) groups
G
acting on Banach Lie groups
U
so as to preserve the relevant structure (i.e., by analytic group automorphisms). Actions are always assumed continuous in the strongest reasonable sense:

G\times U\to U

is continuous. One frequently considers weaker notions of continuity (e.g.,
[
45
, Section X.1]
), but those will not play a role below.

For finite
G
, the reader can find quick treatments of the pertinent material on the resulting

•

cohomology groups

H^{n}(G,U)
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
for abelian
U
;

•

and
H^{n}(G,U)
,

for arbitrary

in, say,
[
42
, Chapter VII and its Appendix]
or
[
44
, Sections I.2 and I.5]
.

Briefly, the non-abelian machinery is as follows. Given an action

G\times U\ni(g,u)\xmapsto{\hskip 8.19447pt}{}^{g}u\in U

(3.1)

of a group
G
(typically finite, for us) on a Lie group
U
(possibly non-commutative and frequently infinite-dimensional), the
U
-valued cocycles
attached to the action are the maps

G\ni g\mapsto u_{g}\in U

(3.2)

satisfying the cocycle condition

u_{gh}=u_{g}\cdot{}^{g}u_{h},\qquad\forall g,h\in G.

(3.3)

The space of all such cocycles is denoted by
Z^{1}(G,U)
. Two cocycles
(u_{g})_{g}
and
(v_{g})_{g}
are declared
equivalent
or
cohomologous
if there is some

with

v_{g}=w^{-1}\cdot u_{g}\cdot{}^{g}w,\qquad\forall g\in G,

and the set
H^{1}(G,U)
of equivalence classes is the
first cohomology set
of
G
with values in
U
.

These objects appear often in the theory of operator algebras (e.g.,
[
45
, Definition X.1.4]
), in the process of deforming or “twisting” group actions on said algebras.

A smattering of other symbols featuring below:

•

C^{n}(G,U):=C(G^{n},U)
is simply the space of maps
G^{n}\to U
(which maps we refer to as
n
-cochains
). It admits an obvious analytic-manifold structure, topology, etc., and is a group when
U
is abelian.

Suppose

is abelian. As explained in
[
42
, Section VII.3]
, the space

can also be identified with that of
G

G^{n+1}\to U
(with

•

We have differentials

which compute the
cohomology

H^{n}(G,U)
as
H^{n}(G,U)=\ker\delta^{n}/\im\delta^{n-1}
.

•

Z^{n}(G,U)\subset C^{n}(G,U)
is the space of
n
-cocycles
(with
n=0,1
if
U
is non-abelian). For abelian

, this is simply

.

•

When
U
is abelian,

is the space of

-coboundaries
. We thus have

In all of the above, maps

Lemma 3.1

Let

, equipped with its quotient topology, is discrete.

(4)

Every cocycle
u=(u_{g})_{g}
has a neighborhood
{\mathcal{U}}
in
Z^{1}(G,U)
admitting an analytic map

{\mathcal{U}}\ni\left({u^{\prime}}=(u^{\prime}_{g})_{g}\right)\xmapsto{\hskip 8.19447pt}v_{u^{\prime}}\in U

such that

\displaystyle v_{u}=1\qquad\text{and}\qquad u^{\prime}_{g}=v_{u^{\prime}}^{-1}\cdot u_{g}\cdot{}^{g}v_{u^{\prime}},\qquad\forall g\in G,\quad u^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}.

Proof.

We address the claims sequentially.

Parts
(
1
)
and
(
2
)
:
Fix, throughout the discussion, a 1-cocycle

u=(u_{g})_{g\in G}\in Z^{1}(G,U)

and the resulting twisted action (
3.5
) of
G
on the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{u}}
.

A sufficiently small neighborhood
{\mathcal{V}}
of
u
in the Lie group

consists of elements of the form

v=(v_{g})_{g},\qquad v_{g}=\exp(X_{g})\cdot u_{g}

for small-norm elements
X_{g}\in{\mathfrak{u}}
, with
\exp\colon{\mathfrak{u}}\to U
the
exponential
(or
exponential function

[
33
, discussion following Definition IV.1]
) of the Lie group.

The additional condition that

itself be a 1-cocycle reads

\exp(X_{gh})u_{gh}=\exp(X_{g})u_{g}\cdot\exp\left({}^{g}X_{h}\right){}^{g}u_{h}.

Or:

\displaystyle\exp(X_{gh})u_{gh}

\displaystyle=\exp(X_{g})\cdot u_{g}\exp({}^{g}X_{h})u_{g}^{-1}\cdot u_{g}\cdot{}^{g}u_{h}

\displaystyle=\exp(X_{g})\cdot u_{g}\exp({}^{g}X_{h})u_{g}^{-1}\cdot u_{gh}\quad\text{by \eqref{eq:cocyccond}}

\displaystyle=\exp(X_{g})\cdot\exp(\operatorname{Ad}_{u_{g}}\,{}^{g}X_{h})\cdot u_{gh}\quad\text{by the definition of the adjoint action}

\displaystyle=\exp(X_{g})\cdot\exp(g\triangleright X_{h})\cdot u_{gh}\quad\text{by the definition of the action `$\triangleright$'}.

The rightmost factor cancels out to give

\exp(X_{gh})=\exp(X_{g})\cdot\exp(g\triangleright X_{h}).

By the
BCH
formula (

:
[
34
, Definition IV.1.3 and Corollary IV.1.10]
), this translates to

X_{gh}=X_{g}+g\triangleright X_{h}+\frac{1}{2}\left[X_{g},g\triangleright X_{h}\right]+\cdots.

When all norms
\|g\triangleright X_{h}\|
are sufficiently small (which we may as well assume), the right-hand partial sum starting with the summand
\frac{1}{2}[-,-]
onward is
O(\|X_{g}\|\cdot\|g\triangleright X_{h}\|)
in “big O notation”
[
6
]
: its norm is dominated by
C\|X_{g}\|\cdot\|g\triangleright X_{h}\|
for some fixed constant
C>0
.

If

is of order
\varepsilon
,

\left(X_{gh}-X_{g}-g\triangleright X_{h}\right)_{g,h}\in B^{2}(G,{\mathfrak{u}})

is a 2-coboundary and hence a 2-cocycle of size

. It follows from Lemma

that we can recover it as the coboundary of a

X_{gh}-X_{g}-g\triangleright X_{h}=Y_{gh}-Y_{g}-g\triangleright Y_{h}

with

, i.e.,

.
Furthermore, the same Lemma

The fact that

is in

is of order

also ensures that this map can be inverted, so we have an analytic chart on

{\mathcal{U}}\ni(\exp(X_{g})\cdot u_{g})_{g}\xmapsto{\hskip 8.19447pt}(X_{g})_{g}\xmapsto{\hskip 8.19447pt}(X_{g}-Y_{g})\in Z^{1}(G,{\mathfrak{u}}),

onto some neighborhood of

. This suffices to prove (

) and (
2

split, which is indeed the case, once more by Lemma
3.1
.

Parts
(
3
)
and
(
4
)
:
Fix
u=(u_{g})_{g}\in Z^{1}(G,U)
.
The differential of the map

U\ni v\mapsto\big(v^{-1}\cdot u_{g}\cdot{}^{g}v\big)_{g}\in Z^{1}(G,U)

(3.6)

at the origin

is nothing but the cochain differential

(for the twisted action ‘

(by part (

).

This makes (

[

•

By

),

And, also by

Proposition 4.7

Let

(1)

If

(2)

The converse holds if

Proof.

We prove the two claims in the order in which they were stated.

(1) Every element in the orbit of
\varphi

semisimple
Banach algebra
\overline{A}:=A/\operatorname{rad}A
and the claim follows from the openness of the
B^{\times}
-orbits in
\text{\sc BAlg}\big(\overline{A},B\big)
(Theorem
4.4
(

(2) As for the converse, note first that in finite-dimensional, semisimple algebras nilpotent elements are conjugate to their (real, say) non-zero scalings. It follows that if
J\subset A
is the top non-zero power of the radical,
\varphi
will be conjugate to a morphism that scales every element of
J

. The closure of the orbit of
\varphi
thus contains morphisms that annihilate

\max\{n\mid(\operatorname{rad}A)^{n}\neq 0\}.

The proof is complete.

Lemma 4.8

A finite-dimensional real algebra

(an element of

This will be little more than a pointer to the extensive literature on Hochschild cohomology, and an opportunity to fix some notation.

For the purely algebraic theory the reader can consult, for instance,

[

\displaystyle\delta^{n}f(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n+1}):={}

4.3
Group/Hochschild cocycles as tangent spaces

We can now return to the main thread, supplementing Theorems

and
4.4
with the requisite tangent-bundle information. The statement of Theorem

requires one more prefatory discussion, on how Hochschild cohomology meshes with

To wit, suppose

is a complex Banach space equipped with

•

a
*
-structure (as usual: an involutive conjugate-linear continuous operation);

•

and an
A
-bimodule structure for a

-algebra

•

so that the two are compatible in the guessable sense:

(am)^{*}=m^{*}a^{*}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(ma)^{*}=a^{*}m^{*},\qquad\forall a\in A,\quad m\in E.

There is then a
*
-structure on cochains given by

f^{*}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}):=f(a_{n}^{*},\dots,a_{1}^{*})^{*},\qquad\forall f\in C^{n}(A,E)

(note the argument order reversal), which intertwines the differentials

of (

and so on.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let

and

(1)

\varphi

(2)

-cocycles for the

-bimodule structure on

(1) As in the proof of Theorem

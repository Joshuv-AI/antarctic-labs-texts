Generalized Wedderburn Rank Reduction
Oskar Kędzierski

Abstract
We generalize the Wedderburn rank reduction formula by replacing the inverse with the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse. In particular, this allows one to remove the non–singularity of a certain matrix from assumptions. The results implies in a straightforward way Nystroem, CUR decompositions, meta-factorization, and a result of Ameli, Shadden [ 1 ] . We investigate which properties of the matrix are inherited by the generalized Wedderburn reduction. Reductions leading to the best low-rank approximation are explicitly described in terms of singular vectors. We give a self–contained calculation of the range and the nullspace of the projection A ​ ( B ​ A ) + ​ B A(BA)^{+}B and prove that any projection can be expressed in this way.

The classical Wedderburn rank reduction formula, cf.

Lemma 1.1

(Wedderburn rank reduction formula)
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times n}
be any matrix. Let
x\in\mathbb{K}^{n},\ y\in\mathbb{K}^{m}
.
Assume that

\omega=y^{*}Ax\neq 0.

Then

\rank(A-\omega^{-1}Axy^*A)=\rank A-1.

In general, if
X\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times k},\ Y\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times k}
and
M\in\mathbb{K}^{k\times k}
is given by

M=Y^{*}AX,

is invertible then

\rank(A-AXM^{-1}Y^*A)=\rank A-k.

For the sake of completeness, we include a brief proof of both statements, although they follow from the more general result, cf. Theorem
5.1
.

Proof.

It follows from that assumptions that
Ax\neq 0
and

(A-\omega^{-1}Axy^{*}A)x=0.

Therefore

\rank(A-\omega^{-1}Axy^*A)\leq\rank A-1.

Assume now
(A-\omega^{-1}Axy^{*}A)u=0
. Let
\lambda=\omega^{-1}y^{*}Au
, then

A(u-\lambda x)=0,

hence
u\in\mathcal{N}\left(A\right)+\mathbb{K}x
. Therefore

\rank(A-\omega^{-1}Axy^*A)=\rank A-1.

In the general case
\rank AX=k
(otherwise
\rank M<k
) and the proof is similar.
That is

\left(A-AXM^{-1}Y^{*}A\right)X=0,

and the
k
columns of
X
are linearly independent. On the other hand
AX\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times k}
is of rank
k
, so no linear combination of columns of
X
is contained in the nullspace of
A
. Since
\mathcal{N}\left(A\right)\subset\mathcal{N}\left(A-AXM^{-1}Y^{*}A\right)

\rank\left(A-AXM^{-1}Y^{*}A\right)=n-\dim\mathcal{N}\left(A-AXM^{-1}Y^{*}A\right)\leq n-\left((n-\rank{A})+k\right)=\rank A-k.

Let
U\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times k}
be any matrix such that

\left(A-AXM^{-1}Y^{*}A\right)U=0.

Let
\Lambda=M^{-1}Y^{*}AU
then

A(U-X\Lambda)=0,

hence

U\in\mathcal{N}\left(A\right)+X\Lambda.

This implies that

\mathcal{N}\left(A-AXM^{-1}Y^{*}A\right)\subset\mathcal{N}\left(A\right)+\mathcal{C}\left(X\right)\cong\mathcal{N}\left(A\right)\oplus\mathcal{C}\left(X\right),

and finally

\rank\left(A-AXM^{-1}Y^{*}A\right)=\rank A-k.

∎

where

, where

The following facts about Moore–Penrose pseudoinverses will be used in the paper tacitly.

\displaystyle A{A}^{+}\!A=A,

(3.1)

\displaystyle{A}^{+}\!A{A}^{+}\!={A}^{+}\!,

(3.2)

(3.3)

\displaystyle(X{A}^{+}\!)^{*}=X{A}^{+}\!.

(3.4)

In particular,
A{A}^{+}\!

{A}^{+}\!A
are orthogonal projections and

\mathcal{C}\left(A{A}^{+}\!\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(A\right),\quad\mathcal{N}\left(A{A}^{+}\!\right)=\mathcal{N}\left(A^{*}\right),

The following fact was proven by Greville, and it will be referred to as Greville’s condition.

{(AB)}^{+}\!={B}^{+}\!{A}^{+}\!\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{C}\left(BB^{*}A^{*}\right)\subset\mathcal{C}\left(A^{*}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{C}\left(A^{*}AB\right)\subset\mathcal{C}\left(B\right).

(3.5)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times n},\ B\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times k}
be two matrices. Then

\mathcal{C}\left(AB\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(ABB^{*}\right),\quad\mathcal{N}\left(AB\right)=\mathcal{N}\left(A^{*}AB\right).

Proof.

The first equation is true because
\mathcal{C}\left(BB^{*}\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(B\right)
and the second because
\mathcal{C}\left(A\right)\cap\mathcal{N}\left(A^{*}\right)=0
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\rank A=\rank B=\rank AB
then

\mathcal{N}\left(AB\right)=\mathcal{N}\left(B\right).

Proof.

Clearly

\mathcal{C}\left(B^{*}A^{*}\right)\subset\mathcal{C}\left(B^{*}\right),

but the dimensions are equal.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

If
\rank A=\rank B=\rank AB
then

{(AB)}^{+}\!={B}^{+}\!{A}^{+}\!\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{N}\left(B^{*}\right)=\mathcal{N}\left(A\right).

Proof.

The condition
\mathcal{N}\left(B^{*}\right)\subset\mathcal{N}\left(B^{*}A^{*}A\right)
reduces to
\mathcal{N}\left(B^{*}\right)\subset\mathcal{N}\left(A\right)
and the condition

\mathcal{N}\left(A\right)\subset\mathcal{N}\left(ABB^{*}\right)
reduces to
\mathcal{N}\left(A\right)\subset\mathcal{N}\left(B^{*}\right)
. Those are dual to the Greville’s conditions (
3.5
).
∎

4
Projection Formula

The classical result is well–known in the following form.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times p},\ B\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times q}
be matrices such that

\mathcal{C}\left(A\right)\oplus\mathcal{N}\left(B^{*}\right)=\mathbb{K}^{m}.

Then the matrix of the projection onto
\mathcal{C}\left(A\right)
along
\mathcal{N}\left(B^{*}\right)
is given by

P=A{(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!B^{*}.

Proof.

Since

P^{2}=A\left({(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!B^{*}A{(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!\right)B^{*}=A{(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!B^{*}=P,

P
is a projection. The assumption implies that
\rank A=\rank B=\rank B^{*}A
and therefore

\mathcal{C}\left(P\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(A\right)
as
\mathcal{C}\left(P\right)\subset\mathcal{C}\left(A\right)
and they have the same dimensions. Similarly
\mathcal{N}\left(P\right)=\mathcal{N}\left(B^{*}\right)
, by Lemma
3.2
.
∎

It turns out that the assumptions on the matrices
A,B
may be dropped and the range of
P
will become smaller while the null space will become larger. The same result was obtained in
[
8
]
by Černý but here we do not use the Zlobec formula.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times p},\ B\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times q}
be any matrices.
Let

P=A{(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!B^{*}.

Then
P
is a projection of
\rank P=\rank(B^*A)
and

\mathcal{C}\left(P\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(AA^{*}B\right),\quad\mathcal{N}\left(P\right)=\mathcal{N}\left(A^{*}BB^{*}\right).

Proof.

By direct computation
P^{2}=P
. The rank of a projection is equal to its trace. Therefore,

\Tr P=\Tr\left(A{(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!B^{*}\right)=\Tr\left(B^{*}A{(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!\right)=\rank(B^*A).

First, we compute the null space of
P
. Assume

Px=A{(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!B^{*}x=0,

by multiplying by
B^{*}
on the left

B^{*}A{(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!B^{*}x=0,

that is, since
\mathcal{N}\left(B^{*}A{(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!\right)=\mathcal{N}\left((B^{*}A)^{*}\right)

B^{*}x\in\mathcal{N}\left(A^{*}B\right)\Longrightarrow A^{*}BB^{*}x=0.

Assume now
A^{*}BB^{*}x=0
. Multiplying this equation on the left by
{(A^{*}B)}^{+}\!
gives

{(A^{*}B)}^{+}\!A^{*}BB^{*}x=0,

and therefore

0={(A^{*}B)}^{+}\!A^{*}BB^{*}x=\left({(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!\right)^{*}\left(B^{*}A\right)^{*}B^{*}x=\left(\left(B^{*}A\right){(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!\right)^{*}B^{*}x=

=\left(B^{*}A\right){(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!B^{*}x=B^{*}\left(A{(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!B^{*}\right)x=B^{*}Px.

Multiplying the equation
B^{*}Px=0
by
A{(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!
on the left we see that
PPx=0
, that is,
Px=0
.
Finally

\mathcal{N}\left(P\right)=\mathcal{N}\left(A^{*}BB^{*}\right).

Now

P^{*}=B{\left(A^{*}B\right)}^{+}\!A^{*},

and by the previous conclusion

\mathcal{N}\left(P^{*}\right)=\mathcal{N}\left(B^{*}AA^{*}\right).

Since
\mathcal{C}\left(P\right)\oplus\mathcal{N}\left(P^{*}\right)
is an orthogonal decomposition then

\mathcal{C}\left(P\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(AA^{*}B\right).

∎

5
Generalized Wedderburn Rank Reduction

The following theorem generalizes classical Wedderburn rank reduction, cf. Lemma
1.1
. The idea of proof is similar.

Theorem 5.1

(generalized Wedderburn rank reduction formula)
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times n},\ X\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times p},\ Y\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times q}
. Assume that
\rank Y^{*}AX=k
(matrix
Y^{*}AX
is possibly a non–square matrix). Then

\rank\left(A-(AX){(Y^{*}AX)}^{+}\!(Y^{*}A)\right)=\rank A-k.

Proof.

Let
B=A-(AX){(Y^{*}AX)}^{+}\!(Y^{*}A)
. Clearly,
\mathcal{N}\left(A\right)\subset\mathcal{N}\left(B\right)
.
We need to find additional
k
linearly independent vectors in
\mathcal{N}\left(B\right)
.
Again, as
B(X{(Y^{*}AX)}^{+}\!)=0

\mathcal{C}\left(X{(Y^{*}AX)}^{+}\!\right)\subset\mathcal{N}\left(B\right).

Note that

\mathcal{C}\left({(Y^{*}AX)}^{+}\!\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(X^{*}A^{*}Y\right)\subset\mathcal{C}\left(X^{*}\right),

but
\mathcal{C}\left(X^{*}\right)\cap\mathcal{N}\left(X\right)=0
therefore

\rank X{(Y^{*}AX)}^{+}\!=k.

We claim that

\mathcal{C}\left(X{(Y^{*}AX)}^{+}\!\right)\cap\mathcal{N}\left(A\right)=0.

To show this assume that there exists
y\in\mathbb{K}^{q}
such that

x=X{(Y^{*}AX)}^{+}\!y,\quad Ax=0,

i.e.
x\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
lies in the intersection. Multiplying the first equation by
Y^{*}A
on the left and applying the second one gives

(Y^{*}AX){(Y^{*}AX)}^{+}\!y=0.

This means that
y
lies in the kernel of the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{C}\left(Y^{*}AX\right)
, i.e.

y\in\mathcal{N}\left((Y^{*}AX)^{*}\right)=\mathcal{N}\left({(Y^{*}AX)}^{+}\!\right).

Therefore
x=0
. To finish the proof it is enough to show that

\mathcal{N}\left(B\right)=\mathcal{N}\left(A\right)\oplus\mathcal{C}\left(X{(Y^{*}AX)}^{+}\!\right).

By the previous considerations, the right hand side is contained in the left one. Let
Bx=0
. That is

A(x-X{(Y^{*}AX)}^{+}\!(Y^{*}A)x)=0,

i.e.
x-X{(Y^{*}AX)}^{+}\!(Y^{*}A)x\in\mathcal{N}\left(A\right)
and the second term is in
\mathcal{C}\left(X{(Y^{*}AX)}^{+}\!\right)
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 7.1

In this section, (non)uniqueness of the presentation of a projection is discussed. Then, the properties shared by the matrix and its generalized Wedderburn rank reduction are listed (Remark

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times p},B\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times q}
and
C\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times s},D\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times t}
.
Let

.
Then

A{(B^{*}A)}^{+}\!B^{*}=C{(D^{*}C)}^{+}\!D^{*}

if and only if

\mathcal{C}\left(S\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(T\right),\ \mathcal{N}\left(S\right)=\mathcal{N}\left(T\right),\quad\text{or equivalently}\quad\mathcal{C}\left(S\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(T\right),\quad\mathcal{C}\left(S^{*}\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(T^{*}\right),

\text{or}\quad S{S}^{+}\!=T{T}^{+}\!,\quad{S}^{+}\!S={T}^{+}\!T.

Proof.

Two projections are equal if and only if they have the same range and null space, that is, by Lemma
4.1

\mathcal{C}\left(AA^{*}B\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(CC^{*}D\right),\quad\mathcal{N}\left(A^{*}BB^{*}\right)=\mathcal{N}\left(C^{*}DD^{*}\right),

\mathcal{C}\left(B\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(BB^{*}\right),\mathcal{C}\left(D\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(DD^{*}\right)

\mathcal{C}\left(AA^{*}BB^{*}\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(CC^{*}DD^{*}\right),\quad\mathcal{C}\left(BB^{*}AA^{*}\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(DD^{*}CC^{*}\right),

which finishes the proof.

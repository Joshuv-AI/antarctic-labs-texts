Universal methods for variational inequalities: deterministic and stochastic cases
Anton Klimza, Alexander Gasnikov, Fedor Stonyakin, Mohammad Alkousa

Abstract
In this paper, we propose universal proximal mirror methods to solve the variational inequality problem with Hölder-continuous operators in both deterministic and stochastic settings. The proposed methods automatically adapt not only to the oracle’s noise (in the stochastic setting of the problem) but also to the Hölder continuity of the operator without having prior knowledge of either the problem class or the nature of the operator information. We analyzed the proposed algorithms in both deterministic and stochastic settings and obtained estimates for the required number of iterations to achieve a given quality of a solution to the variational inequality. We showed that, without knowing the Hölder exponent and Hölder constant of the operators, the proposed algorithms have the least possible in the worst-case sense complexity for the considered class of variational inequalities. We also compared the resulting stochastic algorithm with other popular optimizers for the task of image classification.

Introduction

Variational inequalities (VIs) often arise in a wide variety of mathematical problems, such as optimization, optimal control, partial differential equations, mechanics, and finance. For a comprehensive overview, please refer to
Konnov (2007)
;
Facchinei and Pang (2003)
;
Elliott (1987)
;
Nagurney (1993)
. Variational inequalities cover as a special case many optimization problems such as minimization problems, saddle point problems, and fixed point problem (see Examples
1
,
2
and
3
below). They play a key role in solving equilibrium and complementarity problems
Facchinei and Pang (2003)
;
Harker and Pang (1990)
, and have many applications in smooth and non-smooth optimization problems
Nesterov (2005)
, also in saddle point problems that are an important part of the machine learning research
Arjovsky and Chintala (2017)
;
Kniaz et al. (2021)
, generative adversarial networks
Goodfellow et al. (2020)
, supervised/unsupervised learning
Bach et al. (2012)
;
Bach et al. (2008)
;
Esser et al. (2010)
;
Joachims (2005)
;
Xu et al. (2004)
, reinforcement learning
Jin and Sidford (2020)
;
Omidshafiei et al. (2017)
, adversarial training
Madry et al. (2017)
, and generative models
Daskalakis et al. (2017)
;
Goodfellow et al. (2014)
;
Gidel et al. (2018)
.

Numerous researchers have dedicated their efforts to exploring theoretical aspects related to the existence and stability of solutions and constructing iterative methods for solving VIs. A significant contribution to the development of numerical methods for solving VIs was made in the 1970’s, when the extragradient method was proposed in
Korpelevich (1976)
. More recently, Nemirovski in his seminal work
Nemirovski (2004)
proposed a non-Euclidean variant of this method, called Mirror Prox algorithm, which can be applied to Lipschitz continuous operators. Different methods with similar complexity were also proposed in
Auslender and Teboulle (2005)
;
Gasnikov et al. (2019)
;
Monteiro and Svaiter (2010)
;
Nesterov (2007)
;
Solodov and Svaiter (1999)
. Besides that, in
Nesterov (2007)
, Nesterov proposed a method for variational inequalities with a bounded variation of the operator, i.e., with a non-smooth operator. He also raised the question of whether it is possible to propose a method that automatically “adjusts to the actual level of smoothness of the current problem instance.” One of the goals of this paper is to propose such an algorithm. There is also extensive
literature on variations of extragradient method that avoid taking two steps or two gradient computations per iteration, and so on (see for example
Hsieh et al. (2019)
;
Malitsky and Tam (2020)
).

Furthermore, the rising significance of stochastic methods in large-scale computations has driven interest in stochastic extragradient methods. In
Juditsky et al. (2011)
, the authors studied stochastic Mirror-Prox methods for VIs with compact convex feasible sets, and in
Mishchenko et al. (2020)
it improved the stochastic Mirror-Prox method for VIs by using a single sample per iteration. In
Hsieh et al. (2019)
, the authors showed stochastic variants of the single-call type extragradient methods. Yet, with a constant stepsize, the iterates of these methods only converge to a neighborhood of the solution set. Diminishing stepsizes do ensure convergence, but they empirically slow down the performance. For more information about stochastic VIs, see
Beznosikov et al. (2023)
and references therein.

Similarly to a more general class of optimization problems with so-called Hölder-continuous functions, which cover both smooth and non-smooth problems, and for which authors proposed so-called universal algorithms that do not use the information about the Hölder exponent and Hölder constant
Kamzolov et al. (2021)
;
Baimurzina et al. (2019)
;
Ghadimi et al. (2019)
;
Gasnikov and Nesterov (2018)
;
Nesterov (2015)
(the main advantage of the universal method is that it adjusts itself to the smoothness of the problem and does not require input parameters), there are many works for VIs with a more general class of operators, called Hölder-continuous, which covers both cases of operators with bounded variation and Lipschitz-continuous operators. VIs with Hölder-continuous monotone operators were considered in
Nemirovski (2004)
, where a special choice of the stepsize for the Mirror Prox algorithm led to the optimal complexity for this class of problems
Nemirovskii and Yudin (1983)
. The authors of
Dang and Lan (2015)
consider variational inequalities with non-monotone Hölder-continuous operators. Both papers use the Hölder constant and exponent to define the stepsize of the proposed methods. In
Bach and Levy (2019)
, for solving monotonic stochastic variational inequalities, authors proposed a universal method based on the proximal mirror method. Essentially, the standard proximal mirror method is used, in which the Lipshitz constant is proposed to be selected in a special way, similar to the method used in Adagrad. However, this method is not fully adaptive because, like Adagrad, the step selection strategy makes significant use of information about the dimension of the problem. A fully adaptive method for solving smooth stochastic monotonic variational inequalities was constructed (with minor reservations) in the work
Iusem et al. (2019)
and in
Stonyakin et al. (2022)
;
Stonyakin et al. (2021)
for the inexact oracle.

Recently, in
Rodomanov et al. (2024)
the authors propose a universal gradient descent for stochastic convex optimization problems. In this paper, we propose an analog of this method for deterministic and stochastic variational inequalities. We propose new universal algorithms, called ”Universal Mirror Proximal Method”, for variational inequality problem (
1
), in deterministic and stochastic settings.

Our main contributions can be summarized as follows:

•

We propose universal algorithms for the variational inequality problem in both deterministic and stochastic settings. These algorithms automatically adapt not only to the oracle’s noise (in the stochastic setting of the problem) but also to the Hölder continuity of the operator without having prior knowledge of either the problem class or the nature of the operator information.

•

We analyze the proposed algorithm for the problems in the deterministic setting with Hölder continuous operators and conclude its optimal convergence rate.

Preliminaries and Problem Statement

We will use
\langle x,y\rangle:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}
to denote the standard inner product of
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\|x\|=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}
to denote the Euclidean norm. The operator
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
denotes the full mathematical expectation. We also use the notation
|x|_{+}:=\max\{0,x\}
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact and convex subset, bounded by a constant
D\geq\max_{x,y\in Q}\|x-y\|
. In this paper, we consider the following Minty variational inequality

\text{Find}\quad x^{*}\in Q:\quad\langle g(x),x^{*}-x\rangle\leq 0\quad\forall x\in Q,

(1)

where
g:Q\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a continuous, monotone operator, i.e.,

\langle g(x)-g(y),x-y\rangle\geq 0\quad\forall x,y\in Q.

(2)

We called that the operator
g
satisfies the Hölder condition on
Q
(or
g
is a Hölder-continuous operator), if for some
\nu\in[0,1]
and
L_{\nu}\geq 0
, it holds the following inequality

\|g(x)-g(y)\|\leq L_{\nu}\|x-y\|^{\nu}\quad\forall x,y\in Q,

(3)

where we refer to
\nu
as Hölder exponent and to
L_{\nu}
as Hölder constant.

Under the assumption of continuity and monotonicity of the operator
g
, the problem (
1
) is equivalent to a Stampacchia
Giannessi (1998)
(or strong
Nesterov (2007)
) variational inequality, in which the goal is to find
x^{*}\in Q
such that

\langle g(x^{*}),x^{*}-x\rangle\leq 0,\quad\forall x\in Q.

(4)

To emphasize the extensiveness of the problem (
1
) (or (
4
)),
Let us consider three common use cases for VIs.

Example 1

(Minimization problem)
.

Let us consider the minimization problem

\min_{x\in Q}f(x),

(5)

and assume that
g(x)=\nabla f(x)
, where
\nabla f(x)
denotes to the (sub)gradient of
f
at
x
. Then, if
f
is convex, it can be proved that
x^{*}\in Q
is a solution to (
4
) if and only if
x^{*}\in Q
is a solution to (
5
).

Example 2

(Saddle point problem)
.

Let us consider the saddle point problem

\min_{u\in Q_{u}}\max_{v\in Q_{v}}f(u,v),

(6)

and assume that
g(x):=g(u,v)=\left(\nabla_{u}f(u,v),-\nabla_{v}f(u,v)\right)^{\top}
, where
Q=Q_{u}\times Q_{v}
with
Q_{u}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}},Q_{v}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}
. Then if
f
is convex in
u
and concave in
v
, it can be proved that
x^{*}\in Q
is a solution to (
4
) if and only if
x^{*}=(u^{*},v^{*})\in Q
is a solution to (
6
).

Example 3

(Fixed point problem)
.

Let us consider the fixed point
problem

\text{find}\;\;x^{*}\in Q\;\;\text{such that}\quad F(x^{*})=x^{*},

(7)

where
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an operator. By taking
g(x)=x-F(x)
, it can be proved that
x^{*}\in Q=\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a solution to (
4
) if
g(x^{*})=0
, i.e.,
x^{*}
is a solution to (
7
).

Following
Nesterov (2007)
;
Antonakopoulos et al. (2020)
, to assess the quality of a candidate solution
\widehat{x}
, we use the following restricted gap (or merit) function

\operatorname{Gap}(\widehat{x})=\max_{u\in Q}\langle g(u),\widehat{x}-u\rangle.

(8)

Thus, our goal is to find an approximate solution to the problem, that is, a point
\widehat{x}\in Q
such that the following inequality holds

\operatorname{Gap}(\widehat{x})=\max_{u\in Q}\langle g(u),\widehat{x}-u\rangle\leq\varepsilon,

(9)

for some
\varepsilon>0
.

As already mentioned,
Nemirovski (2004)
proposed Mirror Prox algorithm under the assumption of
L_{1}
-Lipschitz continuity of the operator, i.e.,
g
satisfies (
3
) with
\nu=1
and
L_{1}
. This method has complexity
O\left(L_{1}D^{2}/\varepsilon\right)
, where
D
characterizes the diameter of the set
Q
and
\varepsilon
is the desired accuracy. By complexity we mean the number of iterations of an algorithm to find a point
\widehat{x}\in Q
such that (
9
) holds. For the case of variational inequalities with bounded variation of the operator
g
, i.e.,
g
satisfying (
3
) with
\nu=0
and
L_{0}

Nesterov (2007)
proposed a method with complexity
O\left(L_{0}^{2}D^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}\right)
. The method for variational inequalities with Hölder-continuous monotone operator
Nemirovski (2004)
has the complexity
O\left(\left(L_{\nu}/\varepsilon\right)^{2/(1+\nu)}D^{2}\right),

which is optimal for for the case of
\nu=1
and for the case of
\nu=0

Nemirovskii and Yudin (1983)
;
Ouyang and Xu (2021)
.

For the stochastic setting of the problem (
1
) (or (
4
)), we consider the following operator

g(x)=\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[g(x,\xi)\right],

(10)

where
\xi
is a random variable, and
g(\cdot,\xi):Q\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a stochastic operator, which satisfies the following assumption.

Assumption 1

(Bounded variance)
.

For all
\xi
with any distribution, and
x\in Q
, the unbiased operator
g(\cdot,\xi)
has uniformly bounded variance, i.e., it holds the following inequality

\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[\|g(x,\xi)-g(x)\|^{2}\right]\leq\sigma^{2},

(11)

for some
\sigma>0
.

3
Universal proximal mirror method

In this section, for problem (
1
), we introduce new algorithms called Universal Mirror Proximal (UMP) for both deterministic and stochastic settings. For the deterministic setting, the algorithm is listed below as Algorithm
1
, whereas for the stochastic setting, the algorithm is listed as Algorithm
2
. These algorithms automatically adapt not only to the oracle’s noise (in the stochastic setting of the problem) but also to the Hölder continuity of the operator
g
without having prior knowledge of either the problem class or the nature of the operator information, i.e., without knowing neither the Hölder exponent
\nu
, nor the corresponding Hölder constant
L_{\nu}
.

3.1
Analysis for deterministic setting

In this subsection, we provide an analysis for Algorithm
1
, when the setting of the problem (
1
) is deterministic.

Algorithm 1

Universal Mirror Proximal Method (UMP) (for the deterministic setting of the problem).

Inputs:

z_{0}=\arg\min\limits_{u\in Q}d(u),\ L_{0}=\|g(z_{0})\|
, and diameter
D>0
.

1:

for

k=0,1,\ldots

do

2:

w_{k}=\arg\min\limits_{x\in Q}\left(\left\langle g(z_{k}),x-z_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\left\|z_{k}-x\right\|^{2}\right),

3:

z_{k+1}=\arg\min\limits_{x\in Q}\left(\left\langle g(w_{k}),x-w_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\left\|z_{k}-x\right\|^{2}\right),

4:

L_{k+1}=L_{k}+\max\left\{0,\frac{2\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\rangle-L_{k}\left\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}}{D^{2}+\left\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}}\right\}.

5:

end

for

At the first, let us prove the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex set and
g:Q\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an operator. The function
h_{k}:Q\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, which defined on
Q
as

h_{k}(x)=\left\langle g(w),x-w\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z-x\|^{2}\quad\forall x,w,z\in Q,

(12)

is
L_{k}
-strongly convex, for some
L_{k}>0
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1]
and
x,y\in Q
. We have

\displaystyle h_{k}(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y)

\displaystyle=\left\langle g(w),\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y-w\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y-z\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha\left\langle g(w),x-w\right\rangle+(1-\alpha)\left\langle g(w),y-w\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y-z\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha h_{k}(x)+(1-\alpha)h_{k}(y)+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y-z\|^{2}-\alpha\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|x-z\|^{2}-(1-\alpha)\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|y-z\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha h_{k}(x)+(1-\alpha)h_{k}(y)+L_{k}\frac{\alpha^{2}-\alpha}{2}\|x\|^{2}+L_{k}\frac{(1-\alpha)^{2}-(1-\alpha)}{2}\|y\|^{2}+L_{k}\alpha(1-\alpha)\left\langle x,y\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\alpha h_{k}(x)+(1-\alpha)h_{k}(y)-\frac{L_{k}\alpha(1-\alpha)}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}.

Thus, we get the following inequality

h_{k}\left(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y\right)\leq\alpha h_{k}(x)+(1-\alpha)h_{k}(y)-\frac{L_{k}\alpha(1-\alpha)}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}.

This means that the function
h_{k}
is
L_{k}
-strongly convex.
∎

Now, for
z_{k}
and
w_{k}
in Algorithm
1
, for every
k\geq 0
, let us set

h_{k}(x)=\left\langle g(w_{k}),x-w_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-x\|^{2}\quad\forall x\in Q.

Then, from item 3 in Algorithm
1
, we have

z_{k+1}=\arg\min\limits_{x\in Q}h_{k}(x)\quad\text{for}\quad w=w_{k},\ z=z_{k},

(13)

and, by Lemma
1
and (
13
) we obtain the following inequality

h_{k}(y)\geq h_{k}(z_{k+1})+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|y-z_{k+1}\|^{2},\quad\forall y\in Q.

(14)

Thus, for
w_{k},z_{k},\,\forall k\geq 0
in Algorithm
1
, from (
14
) for any
y\in Q
, we get

\left\langle g(w_{k}),y-w_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-y\|^{2}\geq\left\langle g(w_{k}),z_{k+1}-w_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-y\|^{2},

i.e.,

\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-y\right\rangle\leq\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\rangle-\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-y\|^{2}+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-y\|^{2}.

(15)

By adding
\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-y\|^{2}
to the both sides of (
15
), we get

\displaystyle\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-y\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-y\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\rangle-\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}+\frac{L_{k+1}-L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-y\|^{2}

(16)

\displaystyle+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-y\|^{2}.

Since
D\geq\max_{x,y\in Q}\|x-y\|
, and
L_{k+1}\geq L_{k}
(see item 4 in Algorithm
1
) we get

\displaystyle\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-y\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-y\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\rangle-\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}+\left(L_{k+1}-L_{k}\right)\frac{D^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-y\|^{2}.

We will look for
L_{k+1}
such that

\left(L_{k+1}-L_{k}\right)\frac{D^{2}}{2}=\left|\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\rangle-\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}\right|_{+}.

(17)

Then, we get

\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-y\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-y\|^{2}\leq D^{2}\left(L_{k+1}-L_{k}\right)+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-y\|^{2}.

(18)

Through the telescopic sum, we get

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\left\langle g(w_{i}),w_{i}-y\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-y\|^{2}\leq D^{2}\left(L_{k+1}-L_{0}\right)+\frac{L_{0}}{2}\|z_{0}-y\|^{2}.

Thus, we have

\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left\langle g(w_{i}),w_{i}-y\right\rangle\leq\frac{D^{2}L_{k+1}}{k}.

(19)

Let us set
\widehat{w}:=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k}w_{i}
, then we have

\operatorname{Gap}(\widehat{w})=\max\limits_{y\in Q}\left\langle g(y),\widehat{w}-y\right\rangle=\max\limits_{y\in Q}\frac{1}{k}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\left(\left\langle g(w_{i}),w_{i}-y\right\rangle+\left\langle g(y)-g(w_{i}),w_{i}-y\right\rangle\right).

(20)

Since
g
is a monotone operator, then
\left\langle g(y)-g(w_{i}),w_{i}-y\right\rangle\leq 0
, and as a result from (
19
) and (
20
), we get

\operatorname{Gap}(\widehat{w})\leq\frac{D^{2}L_{k+1}}{k}.

We have obtained an estimate of the convergence rate of the method for a given choice of
L_{k+1}
.

Let us find an upper bound to

to formulate the convergence rate depending on

. Using (

) we could not get a sufficient upper bound for
L_{k+1}
, thus instead (

\left(L_{k+1}-L_{k}\right)\frac{D^{2}}{2}=\left|\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\rangle-\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}\right|_{+}.

(21)

Thus, for any

, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-y\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-y\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(D^{2}+\|z_{k+1}-z_{k}\|^{2}\right)\left(L_{k+1}-L_{k}\right)+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-y\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2D^{2}\left(L_{k+1}-L_{k}\right)+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-y\|^{2}.

From this, in a similar way as in the previous, we get the following estimate

\operatorname{Gap}(\widehat{w})\leq\frac{2D^{2}L_{k+1}}{k}.

\left\langle g(z_{k}),z_{k+1}-z_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}\geq\left\langle g(z_{k}),w_{k}-z_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2},

i.e,

\left\langle g(z_{k}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}+z_{k}-w_{k}\right\rangle-\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}\leq\left\langle g(z_{k}),z_{k}-w_{k}\right\rangle-\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}.

Thus, we get

\left\langle g(z_{k}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\rangle\leq\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}.

(27)

From (
24
) and (
27
), we find

\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\rangle\leq L_{\nu}\|w_{k}-z_{k}\|^{\nu}\|w_{k}-z_{k+1}\|+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}.

(28)

Thus, from (
23
) and (
28
), we get the following

\left(L_{k+1}-L_{k}\right)\frac{D^{2}}{2}\leq L_{\nu}\|w_{k}-z_{k}\|^{\nu}\|w_{k}-z_{k+1}\|+\frac{L_{k}-L_{k+1}}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{L_{k+1}-L_{k}}{2}\left(D^{2}+\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}-\|z_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2}-\|z_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq L_{\nu}\|w_{k}-z_{k}\|^{\nu}\|w_{k}-z_{k+1}\|

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\left(\|z_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2}+\|z_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}\right).

Let’s denote
R=D^{2}+\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}-\|z_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2}-\|z_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}
. Then, we have

\frac{L_{k+1}-L_{k}}{2}R\leq L_{\nu}\|w_{k}-z_{k}\|^{\nu}\|w_{k}-z_{k+1}\|-\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\left(\|z_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2}+\|z_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}\right).

(29)

Let us define the function
f(x,y):=L_{\nu}x^{\nu}y-\frac{1}{2}L_{k+1}(x^{2}+y^{2})
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which is concave for any
L_{\nu}>0,L_{k+1}>0
and
\nu\in(0,1)
. This function attains its maximal value at the point
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, such that

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}=\nu L_{\nu}x^{\nu-1}y-L_{k+1}x=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}=L_{\nu}x^{\nu}-L_{k+1}y=0.

From these equations, we get

y=\frac{L_{k+1}x^{2-\nu}}{\nu L_{\nu}}\quad\text{and}\quad y=\frac{L_{\nu}x^{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}.

Thus, we have

\frac{L_{k+1}x^{2-\nu}}{\nu L_{\nu}}=\frac{L_{\nu}x^{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\left(\frac{\sqrt{\nu}L_{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\nu}},\quad y=\left(\frac{\nu^{\nu/2}L_{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\nu}},

and therefore we get

\displaystyle f(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq L_{\nu}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\nu}L_{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{\nu}{1-\nu}}\left(\frac{\nu^{\nu/2}L_{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\nu}}-\frac{1}{2}L_{k+1}\left(\left(\frac{\sqrt{\nu}L_{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{2}{1-\nu}}+\left(\frac{\nu^{\nu/2}L_{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{2}{1-\nu}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\nu}^{\frac{\nu}{1-\nu}}L_{\nu}^{\frac{2}{1-\nu}}}{L_{k+1}^{\frac{1+\nu}{1-\nu}}}\left(1-\frac{\nu^{1/\nu}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{{\nu}^{\frac{\nu}{1-\nu}}\left(1-{\nu}^{1/\nu}\right)L_{\nu}^{\frac{2}{1-\nu}}}{2L_{k+1}^{\frac{1+\nu}{1-\nu}}}.

(30)

By setting
x:=\|w_{k}-z_{k}\|,y:=\|w_{k}-z_{k+1}\|
(in
f(x,y)
) and
p=\frac{1+\nu}{1-\nu}
, then from (
30
) we find the following inequality

L_{\nu}\|w_{k}-z_{k}\|^{\nu}\|w_{k}-z_{k+1}\|-\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\left(\|z_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2}+\|z_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}\right)\leq\frac{{\nu}^{\frac{\nu}{1-\nu}}\left(1-{\nu}^{1/\nu}\right)L_{\nu}^{p+1}}{2L_{k+1}^{p}}.

Thus, from (
29
) we find

\frac{L_{k+1}-L_{k}}{2}R\leq\frac{{\nu}^{\frac{\nu}{1-\nu}}\left(1-{\nu}^{1/\nu}\right)L_{\nu}^{p+1}}{2L_{k+1}^{p}},

i.e.,

(p+1)L_{k+1}^{p}\left(L_{k+1}-L_{k}\right)\leq\frac{{\nu}^{\frac{\nu}{1-\nu}}\left(1-{\nu}^{1/\nu}\right)(p+1)L_{\nu}^{p+1}}{R}:=\alpha.

Note that, since
L_{k+1}\geq L_{k}
, we have

(p+1)L_{k+1}^{p}(L_{k+1}-L_{k})\geq(p+1)\int_{L_{k}}^{L_{k+1}}t^{p}dt=L_{k+1}^{p+1}-L_{k}^{p+1}.

Then

L_{k+1}^{p+1}-L_{k}^{p+1}\leq\alpha,

and from the telescopic sum, we get

L_{k+1}^{p+1}\leq k\alpha+L_{0}^{p+1}.

(31)

For a sufficiently large
k
, we have
L_{0}^{p+1}\leq k\alpha
, then from (
31
) with
p=\frac{1+\nu}{1-\nu}
, we get

L_{k+1}\leq(2k\alpha)^{\frac{1}{p+1}}=\left(2k\,\frac{{\nu}^{\frac{\nu}{1-\nu}}\left(1-{\nu}^{1/\nu}\right)(p+1)L_{\nu}^{p+1}}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{p+1}}=k^{\frac{1-\nu}{2}}\frac{{\nu}^{\nu/2}\left(1-{\nu}^{1/\nu}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{2}}L_{\nu}}{\left(\frac{1-\nu}{4}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{2}}R^{\frac{1-\nu}{2}}}\leq\left(\frac{4k}{R}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{2}}L_{\nu}.

(32)

By the parallelogram identity
2\|a\|^{2}+2\|b\|^{2}=\|a+b\|^{2}+\|a-b\|^{2}
with
a=z_{k}-w_{k},b=z_{k+1}-w_{k}
, we find

R=D^{2}+\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}-\|z_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2}-\|z_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}=D^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|z_{k}+z_{k+1}-2w_{k}\|^{2}\geq\frac{D^{2}}{2}.

(33)

Thus, from (
32
) and (
33
), we get the following upper bound of the
L_{k+1}

L_{k+1}\leq\left(\frac{8k}{D^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{2}}L_{\nu}.

∎

By combining (
22
) with the result of Lemma
2
, we have come to the following result, which indicates the convergence rate of the method in the deterministic case.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that the operator
g
is monotone and Hölder continuous with constant
L_{\nu}
for some
\nu\in(0,1)
, given on a convex compact subset
Q
bounded by a constant
D
. Then for problem (
1
), by Algorithm
1
, it holds the following inequality

\operatorname{Gap}(\widehat{w})\leq\frac{16L_{\nu}D^{1+\nu}}{(8k)^{\frac{1+\nu}{2}}},

where
\widehat{w}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k}w_{i}
. Consequently, to achieve an
\varepsilon
-solution to (
1
), i.e.,
\operatorname{Gap}(\widehat{w})\leq\varepsilon
, it needs to make

k=O\left(\inf\limits_{\nu\in[0,1]}\left(\frac{16L_{\nu}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{2}{1+\nu}}\frac{D^{2}}{8}\right)=O\left(\inf\limits_{\nu\in[0,1]}\left(\frac{L_{\nu}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{2}{1+\nu}}2^{\frac{5-3\nu}{1+\nu}}D^{2}\right).

oracle calls of Algorithm
1
.

3.2
Analysis for stochastic setting

In this subsection, we provide an analysis for Algorithm
2
, when the setting of the problem (
1
) is stochastic. Algorithm
2
is adaptive concerning the Hölder exponent
\nu
, Hölder constant
L_{\nu}
, and variance of the stochastic oracle
\sigma
. We mention that the adaptivity according to the parameter
\sigma
is somewhat different from the adaptivity according to the parameter
L
. The adaptivity according to
\sigma
can be understood in the sense that the
\sigma
absences in the steps of Algorithm
2
, but it is not selected in the method as the selection of the parameter
L
(see item 4 in Algorithm
2
).

Algorithm 2

Universal Mirror Proximal Method (UMP) (for the stochastic setting of the problem).

Inputs:

z_{0}=\arg\min\limits_{u\in Q}d(u),\ L_{0}=\|g(z_{0},\xi_{z_{0}})\|
for a generated random variable
\xi_{z_{0}}
, and diameter
D>0
.

1:

for

k=0,1,\ldots

do

2:

w_{k}=\arg\min\limits_{x\in Q}\left(\left\langle g(z_{k},\xi_{z_{k}}),x-z_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\left\|z_{k}-x\right\|^{2}\right),

3:

z_{k+1}=\arg\min\limits_{x\in Q}\left(\left\langle g(w_{k},\xi_{w_{k}}),x-w_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\left\|z_{k}-x\right\|^{2}\right),

4:

L_{k+1}=L_{k}+\max\left\{0,\frac{2\left\langle g(w_{k},\xi_{w_{k}}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\rangle-L_{k}\left\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}}{D^{2}+\left\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}}\right\}.

5:

end

for

We have
g(x)=\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{x}}[g(x,\xi_{x})]
, but we can only get the recalculation of
L_{k+1}
,
w_{k}
and
z_{k+1}
using noisy operator
g(x,\xi_{x})
.

Let us repeat all estimates from (
12
) to (
16
) for the operator
g(x,\xi_{x})
instead of
g(x)
and obtain:

\displaystyle\left\langle g(w_{k},\xi_{w_{k}}),w_{k}-y\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-y\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle g(w_{k},\xi_{w_{k}}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\rangle-\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{L_{k+1}-L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-y\|^{2}+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-y\|^{2}.

We again look for
L_{k+1}
such that:

\frac{\left(L_{k+1}-L_{k}\right)D^{2}}{2}=\left|\left\langle g(w_{k},\xi_{w_{k}}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\rangle-\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}\right|_{+}.

Using this formula for
L_{k+1}
we get

\left\langle g(w_{k},\xi_{w_{k}}),w_{k}-y\right\rangle+\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-y\|^{2}\leq 2D^{2}(L_{k+1}-L_{k})+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-y\|^{2}.

(34)

Since
\xi_{w_{k}}
is independent with
w_{k}
, then
\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{w_{k}}}\left[\left\langle g(w_{k},\xi_{w_{k}}),w_{k}-y\right\rangle\right]=\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-y\right\rangle
. Thus, by applying the expected value to both parts of the inequality (
34
), we get

\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-y\right\rangle+\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{w_{k}}}\left[\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-y\|^{2}\right]\leq 2D^{2}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{w_{k}}}\left[L_{k+1}\right]-L_{k}\right)+\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-y\|^{2}.

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{w_{k-1}}}\left[\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-y\right\rangle\right]+\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{w_{k}}}\left[\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\|z_{k+1}-y\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2D^{2}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{w_{k}}}\left[L_{k+1}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{w_{k-1}}}\left[L_{k}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{w_{k-1}}}\left[\frac{L_{k}}{2}\|z_{k}-y\|^{2}\right].

Using the telescopic sum, we get

\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left\langle g(w_{i}),w_{i}-y\right\rangle\right]\leq\frac{2D^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[L_{k+1}\right]}{k}.

(35)

For
\widehat{w}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k}w_{i}
, we have

\operatorname{Gap}(\widehat{w})=\max\limits_{y\in Q}\left\langle g(y),\widehat{w}-y\right\rangle=\max\limits_{y\in Q}\frac{1}{k}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\left(\left\langle g(w_{i}),w_{i}-y\right\rangle+\left\langle g(y)-g(w_{i}),w_{i}-y\right\rangle\right).

(36)

Since
g
is a monotone operator, then
\left\langle g(y)-g(w_{i}),w_{i}-y\right\rangle\leq 0
, and as a result from (
35
) and (
36
), we get

\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[\operatorname{Gap}(\widehat{w})\right]\leq\frac{2D^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[L_{k+1}\right]}{k}.

(37)

Our goal now is finding an upper bound to
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[L_{k+1}\right]
. To this goal, let us first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3
.

Given a sequence of positive numbers
x_{k},\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}
. Let us assume that for any
k\geq 0
there exists
p,q\geq 1
such that at least one of the following two conditions is satisfied

x_{k+1}^{p}\leq\alpha_{k}+x_{k}^{p},\quad\text{and}\quad x_{k+1}^{q}\leq\beta_{k}+x_{k}^{q}.

Then

x_{k+1}\leq\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\beta_{i}\right)^{1/q}+x_{0}.

(38)

Proof.

Let us prove (
38
) by induction on
k\geq 0
.

•

Let
x_{1}^{p}\leq\alpha_{0}+x_{0}^{p}
, then
x_{1}\leq\left(\alpha_{0}+x_{0}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\alpha_{0}^{1/p}+\beta_{0}^{1/q}+x_{0}
.

•

Let
x_{1}^{q}\leq\beta_{0}+x_{0}^{q}
, then
x_{1}\leq\left(\beta_{0}+x_{0}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq\alpha_{0}^{1/p}+\beta_{0}^{1/q}+x_{0}
.

Let us assume that
x_{k}\leq\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\right)^{1/q}+x_{0}
. Then,

If
x_{k+1}^{p}\leq\alpha_{k}+x_{k}^{p}
, then we get

x_{k+1}\leq\left(\alpha_{k}+\left(\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\right)^{1/q}+x_{0}\right)^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

(39)

Let us consider the vectors
(\alpha_{k},0,0)
and
\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i},\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\right)^{p/q},x_{0}^{p}\right)
. Then, by the inverse triangle inequality for Minkowski space, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}+\left(\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\right)^{1/q}+x_{0}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(\alpha_{k}^{1/p}+0^{1/p}+0^{1/p}\right)^{p}+\left(\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\right)^{p/q\ \cdot\ 1/p}+\left(x_{0}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left(\alpha_{k}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\right)^{p/q\ \cdot\ 1/p}+\left(x_{0}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\right)^{p}.

Thus, from (
39
), we find

x_{k+1}\leq\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}\right)^{1/p}+\left(0+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\right)^{1/q}+x_{0}\leq\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\beta_{i}\right)^{1/q}+x_{0}.

If
x_{k+1}^{q}\leq\beta_{k}+x_{k}^{q}
, then we get

x_{k+1}\leq\left(\beta_{k}+\left(\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\right)^{1/q}+x_{0}\right)^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

(40)

Let us consider the vectors
(0,\beta_{k},0)
and
\left(\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}\right)^{q/p},\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i},x_{0}^{q}\right)
. Then, by the inverse triangle inequality for Minkowski space, we have

\displaystyle\beta_{k}+\left(\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\right)^{1/q}+x_{0}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle=\left(0^{1/q}+\beta_{k}^{1/q}+0^{1/q}\right)^{q}+\left(\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}\right)^{q/p\ \cdot\ 1/q}+\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\right)^{1/q}+\left(x_{0}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}\right)^{q/p\ \cdot\ 1/q}+\left(\beta_{k}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\beta_{i}\right)^{1/q}+\left(x_{0}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right)^{q}.

Thus, from (
40
), we find

x_{k+1}\leq\left(0+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\beta_{i}\right)^{1/q}+x_{0}\leq\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\beta_{i}\right)^{1/q}+x_{0}.

∎

Now, let us prove the following lemma, which gives an upper bound to
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[L_{k+1}\right]
.

Lemma 4
.

Assume that the operator
g
is monotone and Hölder continuous with constant
L_{\nu}
for some
\nu\in(0,1)
, given on a convex compact subset
Q
bounded by a constant
D
. Then for problem (
1
), in the stochastic setting under Assumption
1
, by Algorithm
2
, the following inequality holds

\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[L_{k+1}\right]\leq 2\left(\frac{8k}{D^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{2}}L_{\nu}+\frac{\sqrt{8k}}{D}\sigma+L_{0}.

Proof.

If the right side is negative, then we get
L_{k+1}=L_{k}
, otherwise

\displaystyle\frac{\left(L_{k+1}-L_{k}\right)D^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle g(w_{k},\xi_{w_{k}}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\rangle-\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle g(w_{k},\xi_{w_{k}})-g(w_{k}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\rangle+\left\langle g(w_{k}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\rangle-\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\|z_{k}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}.

Similar to Lemma
2
from (
23
) to (
28
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(L_{k+1}-L_{k}\right)\frac{R}{2}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\nu}\|w_{k}-z_{k}\|^{\nu}\|w_{k}-z_{k+1}\|-\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\left(\|z_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2}+\|z_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle g(w_{k},\xi_{w_{k}})-g(w_{k}),w_{k}-z_{k+1}\right\rangle.

Let’s denote
\delta_{k}:=\|g(w_{k},\xi_{w_{k}})-g(w_{k})\|
, then

\left(L_{k+1}-L_{k}\right)\frac{R}{2}\leq L_{\nu}\|w_{k}-z_{k}\|^{\nu}\|w_{k}-z_{k+1}\|-\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\left(\|z_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2}+\|z_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}\right)+\delta_{k}\|w_{k}-z_{k+1}\|.

Let us define the function
f(x,y):=L_{\nu}x^{\nu}y+\delta_{k}y-\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right)
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which is concave for any
L_{\nu}>0,L_{k+1}>0,\delta_{k}
and
\nu\in(0,1)
. This function attains its maximal value at the point
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, such that

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}=\nu L_{\nu}x^{\nu-1}y-L_{k+1}x=0,\quad\text{and}\quad L_{\nu}x^{\nu}-L_{k+1}y+\delta_{k}=0.

From these equations, we get

y=\frac{L_{k+1}x^{2-\nu}}{\nu L_{\nu}}\quad\text{and}\quad y=\frac{L_{\nu}x^{\nu}+\delta_{k}}{L_{k+1}}.

Thus, we have

\frac{L_{k+1}x^{2-\nu}}{\nu L_{\nu}}=\frac{L_{\nu}x^{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}+\frac{\delta_{k}}{L_{k+1}}.

Let’s substitute
x=C_{k}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\nu}L_{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\nu}}
, then
y=C_{k}^{2-\nu}\left(\frac{\nu^{\nu/2}L_{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\nu}}
and it holds the following inequality

\frac{L_{k+1}C_{k}^{2-\nu}}{\nu L_{\nu}}\left(\frac{L_{k+1}}{\nu^{\nu/2}L_{\nu}}\right)^{\frac{2-\nu}{1-\nu}}=\frac{L_{\nu}C_{k}^{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\left(\frac{L_{k+1}}{\nu^{\nu/2}L_{\nu}}\right)^{\frac{\nu}{1-\nu}}+\frac{\delta_{k}}{L_{k+1}}.

Thus,

C_{k}^{2-\nu}-C_{k}^{\nu}-\frac{\delta_{k}}{L_{k+1}}\left(\frac{L_{k+1}}{\nu^{\nu/2}L_{\nu}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\nu}}=0.

Since
\delta_{k}\geq 0
, the maximum of the function
f(x,y)
is achieved at
y\geq 0
, then
C_{k}\geq 0
.

Now let’s substitute
x=C_{k}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\nu}L_{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\nu}}
and
y=C_{k}^{2-\nu}\left(\frac{\nu^{\nu/2}L_{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\nu}}
in
f(x,y)
, then we get

\displaystyle f(x,y)

\displaystyle=C_{k}^{2}L_{\nu}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\nu}L_{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{\nu}{1-\nu}}\left(\frac{\nu^{\nu/2}L_{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\nu}}-\frac{L_{k+1}}{2}\left(C_{k}^{2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\nu}L_{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{2}{1-\nu}}+C_{k}^{4-2\nu}\left(\frac{\nu^{\nu/2}L_{\nu}}{L_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{2}{1-\nu}}\right)

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we show known improvements to the proximal mirror method
Nemirovski (2004)
and propose our universal version in both deterministic and stochastic settings of the problem, based on the ideas of universal gradient descent
Rodomanov et al. (2024)
. We prove the theoretical convergence of the proposed universal proximal mirror methods and obtain an estimate of the convergence rate in the deterministic case

O\left(\inf\limits_{\nu\in[0,1]}\left(\frac{L_{\nu}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{2}{1+\nu}}2^{\frac{5-3\nu}{1+\nu}}D^{2}\right).

In the stochastic case, we obtain the following estimate for the convergence rate

\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[\operatorname{Gap}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}w_{i}\right)\right]\leq 32\left(\frac{D^{2}}{8k}\right)^{\frac{1+\nu}{2}}L_{\nu}+\frac{32D}{\sqrt{8k}}\sigma+\frac{2D^{2}L_{0}}{k}.

We also compare the stochastic method’s performance with popular optimizers on the classical minimization problem. The experiment shows that the proposed method achieves similar results for loss minimization.

In the future, we plan to conduct more experiments with other models and compare the performance of the universal proximal mirror methods with different methods for saddle-point problems, namely the problem of training generative adversarial networks. It is also planned to study the behavior of the proposed methods with restarts to accelerate its convergence rate for variational inequalities with strongly monotone operators. Generalize the proposed algorithms and the conducted analysis to be convenient with any norm (i.e., with an arbitrary prox function and Bregman divergence) not necessarily Euclidean. Also, we plan to investigate adaptive batch size strategies (see eg.
Lau et al. (2024)
) to solve the variational inequality problem in the stochastic setting for more general operators.

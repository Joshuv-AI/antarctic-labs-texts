The Cauchy problem for the periodic Kadomtsev–Petviashvili–II equation below L 2 L^{2}
Sebastian Herr, Robert Schippa, Nikolay Tzvetkov

Abstract
Résumé. On généralise le résultat de Bourgain démontrant le caractère bien posé dans L 2 L^{2} de l’équation KP-II périodique à données initiales initiales distributions de Schwartz appartenant à des espaces de Sobolev d’indices négatifs. Un élément clé de la preuve est une nouvelle estimation de Strichartz sur des intervalles de temps dépendant de la localisation en fréquence de la condition initiale. Cette nouvelle estimation de Strichartz est le résultat de la combinaison d’une inégalité récente de presque orthogonalité obtenue par Guth–Maldague–Oh et des estimées de Strichartz semi-classiques dans l’esprit des travaux de Staffilani-Tatatru et Burq-Gérard-Tzvetkov. Nous utilisons également une variante d’une estimation de Strichartz bilinéaire due à Bourgain, sur des intervalles de temps dépendant de la fréquence spatiale. Cette estimation utilise des arguments de bi-orthogonalité dans l’esprit de la preuve de l’estimée de la fonction carré de Córdoba–Fefferman.

Titre : Le problème de Cauchy pour l’équation KP-II périodique dans des espaces de Sobolev d’indice négatif

1.
Introduction

In this article we are concerned with the local well-posedness of the KP-II equation on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, where
\mathbb{T}
is the circle
\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
:

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{3}u+\partial_{y}^{2}\partial_{x}^{-1}u&=u\partial_{x}u,\quad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}).\end{array}\right.

Above
u_{0}
denotes a real-valued distribution on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
with
\hat{u}_{0}(0,\eta)=0
in the anisotropic Sobolev space
H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
with norm

(2)

\|f\|^{2}_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}=\sum_{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}|\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)|^{2}.

With the vanishing condition on the Fourier coefficients, we can explain
\partial_{x}^{-1}
as a Fourier multiplier

\widehat{\partial_{x}^{-1}f}(\xi,\eta)=(i\xi)^{-1}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta).

The vanishing mean condition is propagated by the nonlinear evolution.

Bourgain proved analytic local well-posedness of (
1
) for
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
in
[
4
]
. Since the
L^{2}
-norm is conserved, global well-posedness in
L^{2}
follows for real-valued initial data. Here we seek to improve the result on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
in
[
4
]
by showing local well-posedness with regularity
below
L^{2}
, i.e.,
s<0
in (
1
).

By local well-posedness we refer to existence, uniqueness, and continuity of the data-to-solution mapping
S_{T}:H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\to C([0,T],H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
,
S_{T}(u_{0})=u
with
T=T(s,\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))})
. We obtain the data-to-solution mapping as unique continuous extension of the data-to-solution mapping on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, which allows us to work with smooth solutions. The analytic local well-posedness in
[
4
]
is a consequence of the contraction mapping principle. Presently, we combine the Fourier restriction analysis
[
4
]
with the energy method on frequency-dependent time scales
[
23
,
18
]
. We implement this argument via short-time Fourier restriction norms as introduced by Ionescu–Kenig–Tataru. Notably, Koch–Tzvetkov
[
23
]
previously combined energy arguments with Strichartz estimates on frequency-dependent time scales to show low regularity well-posedness of the Benjamin–Ono equation.

We remark that on the Euclidean space the KP-II equation has the scaling symmetry

u\to u_{\lambda}(t,x,y)=\lambda^{2}u(\lambda^{3}t,\lambda x,\lambda^{2}y).

On
\mathbb{R}^{2}
this distinguishes the scaling-critical Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2},0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Hadac–Herr–Koch
[
14
]
showed global well-posedness and scattering for initial data in
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2},0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
under suitable smallness assumption.

A few more remarks on the model are in order:
The KP-II equation was proposed by Kadomtsev and Petviashvili
[
19
]
as model for two-dimensional water waves (see also Ablowitz-Segur
[
1
]
). Solutions to (
1
) which do not depend on the
y
-variable solve the KdV equation:

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{xxx}u=u\partial_{x}u.

Understanding the stability of solitons to the KdV equation under transverse perturbations was a central question upon the proposal of KP-equations.

Like the KdV equation, the KP-equations are known to be completely integrable. A Lax pair formulation was obtained by Dryuma
[
9
]
and as a consequence an infinite number of conserved quantities. Two conserved quantities for (
1
) read

\begin{split}M&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u^{2}dxdy,\\
E&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\big((\partial_{x}u)^{2}-\frac{1}{3}u^{3}-(\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u)^{2}\big)dxdy.\end{split}

Due to a lack of coercivity, which is evident for
E
, the mass
M
seems to be the only conservation law, which can be used to show global well-posedness. Recently, complete integrability techniques have been successfully employed to establish low regularity well-posedness in Sobolev spaces for various equations: Firstly, a priori estimates up to the scaling critical regularity
[
22
,
21
]
were proved and later even global well-posedness
[
20
,
15
]
via the method of commuting flows introduced by Killip-Vişan. Whether the arguments extend to two-dimensional models remains open. Our approach does not rely on the complete integrability; the symmetries it depends on are the real-valuedness of solutions and a conservative derivative nonlinearity.

In
[
4
]
Fourier restriction spaces were used (previously introduced in
[
2
,
3
]
) to show analytic well-posedness in
L^{2}
. We use short-time Fourier restriction spaces introduced in
[
18
]
. A second key ingredient is an
L^{4}_{t,x,y}
-Strichartz estimate with sharp (up to endpoints) derivative loss. This is based on the very recent work by Guth–Maldague–Oh
[
13
]
on
\ell^{2}
-Fourier decoupling. It is combined with the dispersive inequality on a semiclassical time scale.
This novel approach to low-regularity well-posedness of quasilinear dispersive equations does apply to other models as well, see e.g. the recent paper of the second author
[
27
]
.

In the non-periodic setting the Strichartz estimate

\|e^{-t(\partial_{x}^{3}+\partial_{y}^{2}\partial_{x}^{-1})}u_{0}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

which was obtained by Saut in
[
26
]
, plays a fundamental role
[
14
]
. The example in Subsection
3.4
shows that in the periodic setting the estimate

\|e^{-t(\partial_{x}^{3}+\partial_{y}^{2}\partial_{x}^{-1})}u_{0}\|_{L^{4}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}

fails for
s<1/8
, while in Theorem
3.6
we prove that this estimate holds true for
s>1/8
if the transversal frequencies are not too large.

In the following we survey the key ingredients of our analysis. Denote the dispersion relation by

\omega(\xi,\eta)=\xi^{3}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi}.

A key quantity is the resonance function:

\begin{split}\Omega(\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{2})&=\omega(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})-\omega(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})-\omega(\xi_{2},\eta_{2})\\
&=3\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})+\frac{(\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1})^{2}}{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})}.\end{split}

Since we impose a vanishing-mean condition on the initial data, which is preserved under the nonlinear evolution, the frequencies satisfy
\xi_{i}\neq 0
,
\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\neq 0
.

We let
\Omega_{\mathrm{KdV}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=3\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})
. Suppose that
|\xi_{i}|\sim N_{i}
and
|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|\sim N_{3}
for dyadic numbers
N_{i}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. We note that we always have

(3)

|\Omega|\gtrsim N_{\max}^{2}N_{\min},\quad N_{\max}=\max_{i=1,2,3}N_{i},\;N_{\min}=\min_{i=1,2,3}N_{i},

which was one key aspect in
[
4
]
. For comparison consider the KP-I equation

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{3}u-\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}u=u\partial_{x}u

with dispersion relation given by

\bar{\omega}(\xi,\eta)=\xi^{3}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi},

in which case the resonance relation reads

\bar{\Omega}(\xi,\eta)=3\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})-\frac{(\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1})^{2}}{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})}.

In conclusion, in the KP-I case the resonance function can become much smaller than the KdV resonance relation. The lower bound (
3
) manifests a defocusing effect in the nonlinear evolution of the KP-II equation, which makes the nonlinear evolution better behaved than the related KP-I equation. It is known that the latter cannot be solved via the contraction mapping principle on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(see
[
24
]
). Indeed, the global well-posedness of the KP-I equation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in the energy space was established by Ionescu–Kenig–Tataru
[
18
]
combining Fourier restriction analysis with the energy method on frequency-dependent time scales. In addition, it is noteworthy that the characteristic surface given by the dispersion relation for the KP-II equation –contrary to the KP-I case– is non-elliptic, which makes the proof of the periodic Strichartz estimates more difficult.

For the following discussion let us focus on the resonant case
|\Omega|\sim N_{\max}^{2}N_{\min}
and suppose by symmetry that
N_{1}\sim N_{3}
,
N_{2}\lesssim N_{1}
. A simplified version of the bilinear Strichartz estimate due to Bourgain (see Proposition
4.2
for the precise statement) reads as a convolution estimate in Fourier variables as follows:

(4)

\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\lesssim N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{\max}/D\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.

Above
f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2})
with

\operatorname{supp}(f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}})\subseteq\{(\tau,\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|\xi|\sim N_{i},\;|\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta)|\lesssim L_{i}\}.

We denote
L_{\max}=\max(L_{1},L_{2})
,
L_{\min}=\min(L_{1},L_{2})
, and
D
denotes transversality in the
\eta
-frequencies within the supports of
f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}
and
f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}
:

(5)

\big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta-\eta_{1}}{\xi-\xi_{1}}\big|\gtrsim D.

More precisely,

\partial_{\eta_{1}}\Omega(\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\xi-\xi_{1},\eta-\eta_{1})=2\big(\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta-\eta_{1}}{\xi-\xi_{1}}\big),

which leads to estimates on the number of contributing
\eta
-frequencies for fixed
\xi
-frequencies. Using endpoint versions of the Fourier restriction norm spaces yields always a gain of
(N_{1}^{2}N_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
by the lower bound on the resonance function. This compensates for the derivative loss giving rise to a factor of
N_{1}
. Together with (
4
) this gives an (oversimplified) explanation to
L^{2}
-well-posedness.

To break the
L^{2}
-barrier set in
[
4
]
, we add the following ingredients:

(i)

Frequency-dependent time localization
T=T(N_{1})=N_{1}^{-\alpha}
, which improves the bilinear Strichartz estimates in case of a High-Low interaction
N_{2}\ll N_{1}
with large transversality
D\gtrsim N_{1}
,

(ii)

Bilinear Strichartz estimates, which improve (
4
) in case of High-Low-interaction
N_{2}\ll N_{1}
with small transversality
D\ll N_{1}
by using a Córdoba–Fefferman
[
6
,
7
,
10
]
square function estimate (in
[
4
, Section 4]
more direct counting arguments are used),

(iii)

Novel linear
L^{4}_{t,x,y}
-Strichartz estimates, which we show using
\ell^{2}
-decoupling. These are most useful in case of resonant High-High-interaction
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim N_{3}
.

Following Ionescu–Kenig–Tataru
[
18
]
, we need to establish estimates for the solutions in short-time Fourier restriction spaces
F^{s}(T)
. The “dual” spaces to estimate the nonlinearity are denoted by
\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)
. Since frequency-dependent time localization erases the dependence of the solution on the initial data for high frequencies, we need to establish energy estimates in spaces
E^{s}(T)
, which estimate the
L^{\infty}
-norm in time of every dyadic frequency component. For solutions the estimates read as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}&\lesssim\|u\|_{E^{s}(T)}+\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)},\\
\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)}&\lesssim T^{\delta}\|u\|^{2}_{F^{s}(T)},\\
\|u\|^{2}_{E^{s}(T)}&\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+T^{\delta}\|u\|^{3}_{F^{s}(T)}.\end{array}\right.

For a time localization parameter
\alpha\in(0,\alpha^{*})
, with threshold
\alpha^{*}>0
to be determined below, it turns out that the above estimates hold true for
s\geq-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\varepsilon
.
This gives a priori estimates

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

Secondly, we show estimates for differences of solutions
v=u_{1}-u_{2}
, which solve the difference equation

\partial_{t}v+\partial_{x}^{3}v+\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}v=\partial_{x}(v(u_{1}+u_{2}))

at lower regularity
s^{\prime}=20s
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|v\|_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)}&\lesssim\|v\|_{E^{s^{\prime}}(T)}+\|\partial_{x}(v(u_{1}+u_{2}))\|_{N^{s^{\prime}}(T)},\\
\|\partial_{x}(v(u_{1}+u_{2}))\|_{N^{s^{\prime}}(T)}&\lesssim T^{\delta}\|v\|_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)}(\|u_{1}\|_{F^{s}(T)}+\|u_{2}\|_{F^{s}(T)}),\\
\|v\|^{2}_{E^{s^{\prime}}(T)}&\lesssim\|v(0)\|_{H^{s^{\prime},0}}^{2}+T^{\delta}\|v\|_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)}^{2}(\|u_{1}\|_{F^{s}(T)}+\|u_{2}\|_{F^{s}(T)}).\end{array}\right.

With a priori estimates for
\|u_{i}\|_{F^{s}(T)}\lesssim\|u_{i}(0)\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}
at hand, we find for times
T=T(\|u_{i}(0)\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})})
:

\|v\|_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)}\lesssim\|v(0)\|_{H^{s^{\prime},0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

This shows Lipschitz continuity of the data-to-solution mapping in the
H^{s^{\prime}}
-topology with more regular solutions
u_{i}(0)\in H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T})
. To infer continuous dependence, but no uniform continuous dependence, we invoke the frequency envelope argument by Tao
[
29
]
. For details we refer to the recent treatise by Ifrim–Tataru
[
17
]
.

A simplified version of our local well-posedness result reads as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(Local well-posedness below
L^{2}
)
.

Let
s>-\frac{1}{90}
. Then (
1
) is locally well-posed.

A more detailed version is provided in Section
9
, where the proof is concluded with the short-time estimates at hand. The argument does not yield analyticity of the flow map, and the precise regularity of the flow map in Sobolev spaces of negative order remains open (see also Remark
5.1
). The argument in
[
11
]
shows that for

s<-1/4
one has
C^{3}
-illposedness in
H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, see Section
10
.

Finally, we remark that our result does not depend on the periods of the torus. Let
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
,
\mathbb{T}_{\gamma}=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\gamma\mathbb{Z})
, and
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\gamma}=\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}_{\gamma}
. We can as well consider (
1
) on the possibly irrational torus:

(6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{3}u+\partial^{2}_{y}\partial_{x}^{-1}u&=u\partial_{x}u,\quad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\gamma},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\gamma}).\end{array}\right.

Since the novel
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimates are proved using
\ell^{2}
-decoupling, these hold as well on the irrational torus. It is easy to see that the bilinear Strichartz estimates from Section
4
hold as well on
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\gamma}
. Hence, we obtain the same regularity results for (
6
) like for (
1
). In the follow-up work
[
16
]
we have extended the arguments to cylinders
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
. Here one additionally has to take into account the possibility of small frequencies
|\xi|\ll 1
as already outlined by Bourgain
[
4
, Section 8]
when he extended his result to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

C\geq 1

A\leq CB

A\sim B

B_{d}(x_{0},R)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x-x_{0}|\leq R\}

\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})

.

-norm on
\mathbb{T}^{k}
,
k\geq 1
,
p\in(1,\infty)

.

2.
Notation and Function spaces

2.1.
Fourier transform and Littlewood-Paley decomposition

The space-time Fourier transform is given by

(\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}u)(\tau,\xi,\eta)=\hat{u}(\tau,\xi,\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}}e^{-it\tau}e^{-ix\xi}e^{-iy\eta}u(t,x,y)\,dx\,dy\,dt.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
,
f\in L^{1}_{\tau}\ell^{1}_{\xi,\eta}
. The inverse Fourier transform is defined by

u(t,x,y)=\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}^{-1}[f]=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{it\tau}e^{ix\xi}e^{iy\eta}f(\tau,\xi,\eta).

Let
\eta_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(0,2))
with
\eta_{0}\equiv 1
on
[0,1]
and
\eta_{0}
be radially decreasing.
We define

\eta_{1}(\tau)=\eta_{0}(\tau/2)-\eta_{0}(\tau).

For
L\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
we set

\eta_{L}(\tau)=\eta(\tau/L).

Let
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
denote a dyadic number. We define frequency projections (with respect to the
\xi
-frequencies)
P_{N}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2})
by

\widehat{P_{N}u}(\tau,\xi,\eta)=1_{[N,2N)}(\xi)\hat{f}(\tau,\xi,\eta).

2.2.
Definition of function spaces

In this subsection, we introduce all function spaces which are used in the subsequent analysis and some of their elementary and well-known properties, we also refer to
[
4
,
18
]
.

The dispersion relation is given by

\omega(\xi,\eta)=\xi^{3}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi}.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};\mathbb{C})
and suppose in the following that
\hat{u}_{0}(0,\eta)=0
for all
\eta\in\mathbb{Z}
. This mean-zero condition is clearly preserved by the nonlinear evolution.
The linear propagation on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is defined by:

S_{\mathrm{KP}}(t)u_{0}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\\
\xi\neq 0\end{subarray}}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t\omega(\xi,\eta))}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi,\eta).

We define modulation projections for
L\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}\cup\{0\}
on functions
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2})
by

\widehat{Q_{L}u}(\tau,\xi,\eta)=\eta_{L}(\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta))\hat{u}(\tau,\xi,\eta)\text{ and }Q_{\leq L}u=\sum_{K\leq L}Q_{K}u.

The sets describing the Fourier support are denoted by

D_{N,L}=\{(\tau,\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\,|\xi|\sim N,\;|\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta)|\sim L\}.

The sets
D_{N,\leq L}
are defined correspondingly.

We introduce a modulation weight.
For
g:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
with
g(\tau,\xi,\eta)=0
unless
|\xi|\in[N,2N)
we define

\|g\|_{X_{N}}=\sum_{L\geq 1}L^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+L/N^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\eta_{L}(\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta))g\|_{L^{2}}.

The weight
(1+L/N^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}
is supposed to improve estimates in the High-Low-interaction when the low frequency carries the high modulation and has already been used in
[
4
]
. We define the norm without this weight by

(7)

\|g\|_{\bar{X}_{N}}=\sum_{L\geq 1}L^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{L}(\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta))g\|_{L^{2}}.

For
\alpha>0
the short-time Fourier restriction spaces at frequencies
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
are defined by

F_{N}=\{u\in C(\mathbb{R};L^{2}):P_{N}u=u,\;\|u\|_{F_{N}}<\infty\}

with

\|u\|_{F_{N}}=\sup_{t_{k}\in\mathbb{R}}\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[\eta_{0}(N^{\alpha}(t-t_{k}))P_{N}u]\|_{X_{N}}.

This can be localized in time
T\in(0,1]
in the usual way

F_{N}(T)=\{u\in C([0,T];L^{2}):P_{N}u=u,\;\|u\|_{F_{N}(T)}<\infty\}

with

\|u\|_{F_{N}(T)}=\inf_{\tilde{u}|_{[0,T]}=u|_{[0,T]}}\|\tilde{u}\|_{F_{N}}.

We take the infimum over all frequency-localized functions
\tilde{u}\in C(\mathbb{R};L^{2})
which coincide with
u
on the interval
[0,T]
.

The dual norm dictated by the Duhamel formula is given by

\|u\|_{\mathcal{N}_{N}}=\sup_{t_{k}\in\mathbb{R}}\|(\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta)+iN^{\alpha})^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[\eta_{0}(N^{\alpha}(t-t_{k}))P_{N}u]\|_{X_{N}}

The time-localized space
\mathcal{N}_{N}(T)
is defined like above.

The following lemma allows us to localize to shorter time intervals than required by the short-time norms:

Lemma 2.1
.

For
M\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, and
f_{N}\in\bar{X}_{N}
, the following estimate holds:

\begin{split}&\sum_{J\geq M}J^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{J}(\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta))\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|f_{N}(\tau,\xi,\eta)\big|M^{-1}(1+M^{-1}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|)^{-4}d\tau^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\quad+M^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{\leq M}(\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta))\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f_{N}(\tau^{\prime},\xi,\eta)|M^{-1}(1+M^{-1}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|)^{-4}d\tau^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim\|f_{N}\|_{\bar{X}_{N}}\end{split}

with implicit constant independent of
K
and
L
.

The following is immediate:

Lemma 2.2
.

For
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and
M\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, and
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[\gamma(M(t-t_{0}))\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{t,x,y}(f_{N})]\|_{\bar{X}_{N}}\lesssim\|f_{N}\|_{\bar{X}_{N}}.

The implicit constant is independent of
M
,
N
, and
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.

To interpret the above, suppose we are estimating an expression
\gamma(T^{\prime-1}(t-t_{0}))u_{N}
which is localized at frequencies
N
and on time scales
T^{\prime}\ll T(N)=N^{-\alpha}
. When we do not use the weight
(1+L/N^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}
, we can estimate the expressions

\sum_{L\geq(T^{\prime})^{-1}}L^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{N,L}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\lesssim\|u_{N}\|_{F_{N}},

where

f_{N,L}=\begin{cases}1_{D_{N,\leq T^{\prime}}}\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[\gamma(T^{\prime-1}(t-t_{0})u_{N}],&\quad L=T^{\prime},\\
1_{D_{N,L}}\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[\gamma(T^{\prime-1}(t-t_{0})u_{N}],&\quad L>T^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Suppose we want to take advantage of the low modulation weight. In comparison with the above, we can only dispose the higher time localization if it does not exceed the size of the modulation weight. We have the following variant of Lemma
2.1
with straightforward proof, see e.g.
[
16
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
2.1
, for
M\lesssim N^{3}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, and
f_{N}\in X_{N}
, the following estimate holds:

\begin{split}&\sum_{J\geq M}J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+J/N^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\eta_{J}(\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta))\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|f_{N}(\tau,\xi,\eta)\big|M^{-1}(1+M^{-1}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|)^{-4}d\tau^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\quad+M^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{\leq M}(\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta))\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f_{N}(\tau^{\prime},\xi,\eta)|M^{-1}(1+M^{-1}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|)^{-4}d\tau^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim\|f_{N}\|_{X_{N}}\end{split}

with implicit constant independent of
K
and
L
.

Correspondingly,

Lemma 2.4
.

For
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and
M\lesssim N^{3}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, and
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[\gamma(M(t-t_{0}))\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{t,x,y}(f_{N})]\|_{X_{N}}\lesssim\|f_{N}\|_{X_{N}}.

The implicit constant is independent of
M
,
N
, and
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Suppose we are estimating an expression
\gamma(T^{\prime-1}(t-t_{0})u_{N})
which is localized at frequencies
N
and on times
T^{\prime}\ll T(N)=N^{-\alpha}
. When we use the weight
(1+L/N^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}
, we can estimate the expressions

\sum_{L\geq(T^{\prime})^{-1}}L^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+L/N^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f_{N,L}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\lesssim\|u_{N}\|_{F_{N}},

provided that
T^{\prime}\lesssim N^{3}
.

We recall the embedding
F^{s}(T)\hookrightarrow C([0,T],H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
18
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and
T\in(0,1]
. Then we have

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}.

We assemble the time-localized function spaces by Littlewood-Paley theory:

\begin{split}\|u\|^{2}_{F^{s}(T)}&=\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s}\|P_{N}u\|_{F_{N}(T)}^{2},\\
\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)}&=\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s}\|P_{N}u\|_{\mathcal{N}_{N}(T)}^{2}.\end{split}

We shall see (Section
5
for details) that
\alpha=0+
suffices for a short-time bilinear estimate at negative regularity. For the energy estimate large
\alpha
is less favorable because we have to divide the time-interval
[0,T]
into more and more subintervals of length
N^{-\alpha}
to estimate frequency-localized components of solutions
P_{N}u
.

The “energy space” is given by

\begin{split}E^{s}(T)&=\{u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})):\|u\|_{E^{s}(T)}<\infty\}\text{ with }\\
\|u\|^{2}_{E^{s}(T)}&=\|P_{\leq 8}u(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{N\geq 8}N^{2s}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|P_{N}u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

The energy estimate in short-time Fourier restriction spaces takes the following form:

Lemma 2.6

(Linear energy estimate)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in E^{s}(T)\cap F^{s}(T)
,
v\in\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)
such that
\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{3}u+\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}u=v
. Then the following estimate holds:

\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}\lesssim\|u\|_{E^{s}(T)}+\|v\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)}.

In the following we see how to trade modulation regularity for powers of the time scale. Define the following variant of
X_{N}
-spaces for
b\in\mathbb{R}
:

\|f_{N}\|_{X_{N}^{b}}=\sum_{L\geq 1}L^{b}\|\eta_{L}(\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta))f_{N}(\tau,\xi,\eta)\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.

The short-time spaces
F_{N}^{b}
,
\mathcal{N}_{N}^{b}
are defined like above, but based on
X_{N}^{b}
instead
X_{N}
. We have the following:

Lemma 2.7

(
[
12
, Lemma 3.4]
)
.

Let
T\in(0,1]
, and
b<\frac{1}{2}
. Then it holds:

\|P_{N}u\|_{F_{N}^{b}}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{2}-b-}\|P_{N}u\|_{F_{N}}

for any function
u:[-T,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
.

The lemma is used to obtain mitigating factors
T^{\delta}
for the short-time bilinear and energy estimates to handle large initial data.

3.
Linear Strichartz estimates

3.1.
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimate for KP-II

In the following we consider functions
f:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
with

\operatorname{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq\{|\xi|\sim N,\,|\eta|\lesssim N^{2}\}.

This region matches unit frequencies after carrying out anisotropic scaling
\xi\to\xi/N
,
\eta\to\eta/N^{2}
, which is a symmetry of the KP-II dispersion relation.

3.2.
Notation and general decoupling result

We introduce notation to formulate the result from
[
13
]
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function. We define the manifold

\mathcal{M}_{\phi}=\{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\phi(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})):(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in[0,1]^{2}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}.

\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
denotes the
\delta
-neighborhood of the manifold
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
. We define
\delta
-flat sets, which trivialize the Fourier extension operator associated to
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
. For
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
we let
f_{S}
denote the Fourier projection to
S\times\mathbb{R}
. Notably, for technical reaonss a smooth cutoff is preferable; see
[
13
]
.

Definition 3.1

(
\delta
-flat set)
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth. We say that
S\subseteq[0,1]^{2}
is
(\phi,\delta)
-flat if

\sup_{u,v\in S}\big|\phi(u)-\phi(v)-\nabla\phi(u)\cdot(u-v)\big|\leq\delta.

The following
\ell^{2}
-decoupling result was recently proved by Guth–Maldague–Oh:

Theorem 3.2

(
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function, and
0<\delta<1
, and
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a sufficiently large number
A
depending on
\varepsilon
and
\phi
satisfying the following: For any
\delta>0
there exists a collection
\mathcal{S}_{\delta}
of finitely overlapping sets
S
such that

(i)

the overlapping is
\mathcal{O}(\log(\delta^{-1}))
such that for the indicator functions
\chi_{S}
the following estimate holds:

\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\delta}}\chi_{S}\leq C_{\varepsilon,\phi}\log(\delta^{-1}),

(ii)

any
S\in\mathcal{S}_{\delta}
is
(\phi,A\delta)
-flat,

(iii)

the following estimate holds:

\|f\|_{L^{4}}\leq C_{\varepsilon,\phi}\delta^{-\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\delta}}\|f_{S}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all functions with Fourier support contained in
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
.

3.3.
Linear Strichartz estimates for KP-II

As an application of Theorem
3.2
, we prove the following essentially sharp
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimate for the KP-II equation:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
with

\operatorname{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq A_{N,N^{2}}:=\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|\xi|\sim N,\quad|\eta|\lesssim N^{2}\}.

Then the following Strichartz estimate holds:

\|S_{\mathrm{KP}}(t)f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\frac{1}{8}+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

Having Theorem
3.2
at disposal, the key point to obtain favorable Strichartz estimates is to find an upper bound on the size of
(\omega,\delta)
-flat sets. This is the content of the following proposition:

Proposition 3.4

(Bound on flat sets)
.

Consider the manifold

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{KP}}=\{(\xi,\eta,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|\xi|\sim 1,\;|\eta|\lesssim 1,\;\tau=\omega(\xi,\eta)\}.

Then
\delta
-flat sets
S\subseteq\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|\xi|\sim 1,\;|\eta|\lesssim 1\}
are contained in balls of size comparable to
\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}
.

Taking Proposition
3.4
for granted, we finish the proof of Theorem
3.3
:

Proposition
3.4
implies Theorem
3.3
.

By
L^{2}
-boundedness of the propagator it suffices to consider times
t\in[0,1/10]
.
In the first step we normalize to unit frequencies by the anisotropic scaling
x\to Nx
,
y\to N^{2}y
,
t\to N^{3}t
which corresponds to
\xi\to\xi/N
,
\eta\to\eta/N^{2}
and leaves the characteristic surface invariant:

\begin{split}&\hskip 9.24994pt\Big\|\sum_{(\xi,\eta)\in A_{N,N^{2}}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t(\xi^{3}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi})}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)\Big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1/10]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\\
&=N^{-\frac{3}{4}}N^{-\frac{1}{4}}N^{-\frac{2}{4}}\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt\times\Big\|\sum_{(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in A_{1,1}\cap(\mathbb{Z}/N\times\mathbb{Z}/N^{2})}e^{i(x^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}((\xi^{\prime})^{3}-(\eta^{\prime})^{2}/\xi^{\prime})}\underbrace{\hat{f}(N\xi^{\prime},N^{2}\eta^{\prime})}_{=:b_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}}\Big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}([0,N^{3}/10]\times N\mathbb{T}\times N^{2}\mathbb{T})}.\end{split}

We use spatial periodicity in
x
and
y
to obtain an isotropic domain of integration:

\begin{split}&\quad\Big\|\sum_{(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in A_{1,1}\cap(\mathbb{Z}/N\times\mathbb{Z}/N^{2})}e^{i(x^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}((\xi^{\prime})^{3}-(\eta^{\prime})^{2}/\xi^{\prime})}b_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}\Big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}([0,N^{3}/10]\times N\mathbb{T}\times N^{2}\mathbb{T})}\\
&\lesssim N^{-\frac{2}{4}}N^{-\frac{1}{4}}\Big\|\sum_{(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in A_{1,1}\cap(\mathbb{Z}/N\times\mathbb{Z}/N^{2})}e^{i(x^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}((\xi^{\prime})^{3}-(\eta^{\prime})^{2}/\xi^{\prime}))}b_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}\Big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(B_{N^{3}})}.\end{split}

Above and in the following
B_{M}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|x|\leq M\}
denotes a ball of radius
M>0
in three dimensions. Now we use continuous approximation and approximate the Dirac comb

f_{b}=\sum_{(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in A_{1,1}\cap(\mathbb{Z}/N\times\mathbb{Z}/N^{2})}b_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}\delta_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

with a mollified version:

f_{b,\lambda}(\xi,\eta)=\sum_{(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in A_{1,1}\cap(\mathbb{Z}/N\times\mathbb{Z}/N^{2})}b_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}\psi_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},\lambda}(\xi,\eta)

with
\psi_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},\lambda}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\operatorname{supp}(\psi_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},\lambda})\subseteq B_{2}((\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}),1/\lambda)
such that
\psi_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime},\lambda}\to\delta_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}\text{ in }\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{KP}}
denote the Fourier extension operator associated to the KP-II characteristic surface:

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{KP}}f=\int_{\{|\xi|\sim 1,\;|\eta|\lesssim 1\}}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t\omega(\xi,\eta))}f(\xi,\eta)d\xi d\eta

Let
\delta:=N^{-3}
. We can choose
\lambda
large enough such that

\big\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{KP}}f_{b}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(B_{\delta^{-1}})}\lesssim\big\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{KP}}f_{b,\lambda}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(B_{\delta^{-1}})}.

Let
w_{B_{\delta^{-1}}}
be a weight which is compactly supported in Fourier space on a ball of size
\delta
, comparable to
1
on
B_{\delta^{-1}}
, and rapidly decaying off
B_{\delta^{-1}}
. We can dominate

\big\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{KP}}f_{b}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(B_{\delta^{-1}})}\lesssim\big\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{KP}}f_{b,\lambda}\cdot w_{B_{\delta^{-1}}}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

The function
F=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{KP}}f_{b,\lambda}\cdot w_{B_{\delta^{-1}}}
has Fourier support in a
\delta
-neighbourhood of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{KP}}
. We can apply Theorem
3.2
to find

\|F\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{S}_{\delta}}\|F_{\theta}\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

with
\theta
denoting rectangles at most of size
\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}\times\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}
, which follows from Proposition
3.4
, and
\mathcal{S}_{\delta}
being a
\log(\delta^{-1})
-overlapping cover. We reverse the continuous approximation (cf.
[
27
, Section 2]
) by letting
\lambda\to\infty
:

\begin{split}\|F_{\theta}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}&=\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{KP}}f_{b,\theta,\lambda}\cdot w_{B_{\delta^{-1}}}\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&\to\big\|\sum_{(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\times\mathbb{Z}/N^{2})\cap\theta}e^{i(x^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}\omega(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}))}b_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(w_{B_{N^{3}}})}.\end{split}

Observe that
\theta
is a rectangle of size at most
1/N\times 1/N
, which means that
\#\{\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}/N:\xi^{\prime}\in\pi_{\xi^{\prime}}\theta\}
is
\mathcal{O}(1)
. By rapid decay and periodicity it suffices to estimate

\big\|\sum_{(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\times\mathbb{Z}/N^{2})\cap\theta\cap A_{1,1}}e^{i(x^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}+\eta^{\prime}y^{\prime}+t^{\prime}\omega(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}))}b_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(B_{N^{3}})}.

We use periodicity of
x^{\prime}
with period
N
and of
y^{\prime}
with period
N^{2}
to reverse the enlargement of the domain of integration:

\begin{split}&\quad\big\|\sum_{(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\times\mathbb{Z}/N^{2})\cap\theta}e^{i(x^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}\omega(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}))}b_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(B_{N^{3}})}\\
&\lesssim N^{\frac{2}{4}}N^{\frac{1}{4}}\big\|\sum_{(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\times\mathbb{Z}/N^{2})\cap\theta}e^{i(x^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}\omega(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}))}b_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}([0,N^{3}]\times N\mathbb{T}\times N^{2}\mathbb{T})}.\end{split}

We can moreover reverse the scaling to find:

\begin{split}&\quad\big\|\sum_{(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\times\mathbb{Z}/N^{2})\cap\theta}e^{i(x^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}\omega(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}))}b_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}([0,N^{3}]\times N\mathbb{T}\times N^{2}\mathbb{T})}\\
&=N^{\frac{3}{4}}N^{\frac{1}{4}}N^{\frac{2}{4}}\big\|\sum_{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap\theta_{N,N^{2}}}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t\omega(\xi,\eta))}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}.\end{split}

In the above display
\theta_{N,N^{2}}
denotes the anisotropic dilation of
\theta
with dilation factor
N
into
\xi
and
N^{2}
into
\eta
-direction.
We remark that finally every scaling to unit frequencies has been reversed and so has been the enlargement of the domain of integration by periodicity. All scaling factors cancel and we obtain

(8)

\|S_{\mathrm{KP}}(t)f_{\theta}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{\theta\in S_{\delta}}\|S_{\mathrm{KP}}(t)f_{\theta_{N,N^{2}}}\|_{L_{t,x,y}^{4}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By Proposition
3.4

\theta_{N,N^{2}}
is at most of size
\sim 1\times N
. So we can find intervals
I_{\theta,\xi}
and
I_{\theta,\eta}
of length
\sim 1
and
N
, respectively, such that

\theta_{N,N^{2}}\subseteq I_{\theta,\xi}\times I_{\theta,\eta}\subseteq\tilde{\theta}_{N,N^{2}},

where
\tilde{\theta}_{N,N^{2}}
denotes a slight enlargement of
\theta_{N,N^{2}}
.

By an application of Minkowski’s inequality we have

(9)

\begin{split}&\quad\big\|\sum_{(\xi,\eta)\in A_{N,N^{2}}\cap\theta_{N,N^{2}}}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t(\xi^{3}-\eta^{2}/\xi))}b_{\xi,\eta}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{\xi\in I_{\theta,\xi}}\big\|\sum_{\eta\in I_{\theta,\eta}}e^{i(y\eta-t\eta^{2}/\xi)}b_{\xi,\eta}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T})}.\end{split}

A change of variables, keeping in mind that
|\xi|\sim N
, implies

(10)

\begin{split}\big\|\sum_{\eta\in I_{\theta,\eta}}e^{i(y\eta-t\eta^{2}/\xi)}b_{\xi,\eta}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T})}&\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{4}}\big\|\sum_{\eta\in I_{\theta,\eta}}e^{i(y\eta-t^{\prime}\eta^{2})}b_{\xi,\eta}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,y}([0,N^{-1}]\times\mathbb{T})}\\
&\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{8}}\big\|\sum_{\eta\in I_{\theta,\eta}}e^{i(y\eta-t^{\prime}\eta^{2})}b_{\xi,\eta}\big\|_{L^{8}_{t}([0,N^{-1}],L^{4}_{y}(\mathbb{T}))}.\end{split}

This expression can be estimated by short-time (lossless) Strichartz estimates due to Staffilani–Tataru
[
28
]
and Burq–Gérard–Tzvetkov
[
5
]
after applying Galilean invariance to shift the

-frequencies to an interval of length

centered at the origin:

(11)

\big\|\sum_{\eta\in I_{\theta,\eta}}e^{i(y\eta-t^{\prime}\eta^{2})}b_{\xi,\eta}\big\|_{L_{t}^{8}([0,N^{-1}],L^{4}(\mathbb{T}))}\lesssim\big(\sum_{\eta\in I_{\theta,\eta}}|b_{\xi,\eta}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Taking (
9
) and (
11
) together gives

\begin{split}&\quad\big\|\sum_{(\xi,\eta)\in A_{N,N^{2}}\cap\theta_{N,N^{2}}}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t(\xi^{3}-\eta^{2}/\xi))}b_{\xi,\eta}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T})}\\
&\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{8}}\big(\sum_{(\xi,\eta)\in\theta_{N,N^{2}}}|b_{\xi,\eta}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

Plugging this estimate into (
8
) and using the overlap being bounded with
\log(N)
we find

\|S_{\mathrm{KP}}(t)f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\frac{1}{8}+2\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

The proof is complete.

It remains to show Proposition
3.4
:

Proof of Proposition
3.4
.

We follow the argument of
[
13
, Remark 4.1]
. It suffices to check that for
\gamma(t)
parametrizing a line with unit speed we have

(12)

\omega(\gamma(t))=a_{0}+a_{1}t+a_{2}t^{2}+a_{3}t^{3}+E(t)

with
|a_{2}|+|a_{3}|\gtrsim 1
and
|E(t)|\leq ct^{4}
. Then we can infer that for
u,v\in R
,
|u-v|\ll 1

First, we deal with the simpler case

\begin{split}(\xi_{0}+t\xi^{\prime})^{3}-\frac{(\eta_{0}+t\eta^{\prime})^{2}}{\xi_{0}+t\xi^{\prime}}&=\xi_{0}^{3}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{\prime})-\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}+2t\eta_{0}\eta^{\prime}+t^{2}(\eta^{\prime})^{2}}{\xi_{0}}(1+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{\prime}))\\

a_{2}

3.4.
Sharpness of the estimate

We conclude the section with an example showing sharpness up to endpoints:

Define

\begin{split}\|S_{\mathrm{KP}}(t)f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}&=\big\|\sum_{\xi=N,\eta=0,\ldots,N^{\frac{1}{2}}}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t\omega(\xi,\eta))}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\\

3.5.
Improved estimate on frequency-dependent time intervals

We record the following refinement on frequency-dependent times:

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
, and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
with

\operatorname{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq A_{N,N^{2}}:=\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|\xi|\sim N,\quad|\eta|\lesssim N^{2}\}.

(i)

The following Strichartz estimate holds:

(14)

\|S_{\mathrm{KP}}(t)f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,N^{-\alpha}]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\frac{1}{8}+\frac{\alpha}{12}-\frac{\alpha}{8}+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

(ii)

If it holds
|\eta|\ll N^{2}
for any

(15)

\|S_{\mathrm{KP}}(t)f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,N^{-\alpha}]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\frac{1}{8}-\frac{\alpha}{8}+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

Démonstration.

We follow the arguments from the proof of Theorem
3.3
.
The key ingredient remains
\ell^{2}
-decoupling, which after using the anisotropic scaling
t\to N^{3}t
,
x\to Nx
,

yields the following inequality:

\|S_{\mathrm{KP}}(t)f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,N^{-\alpha}]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{S}_{\delta}}\|S_{\mathrm{KP}}(t)f_{\tilde{\theta}}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,N^{-\alpha}]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Presently, we have
\delta^{-1}=N^{3-\alpha}
, and after Galilean transform (see Remark
3.5
) we obtain anisotropically inflated
\tilde{\theta}
of size at most
N\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}\times N^{2}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}=N^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}\times N^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}
. More precisely, we firstly rescale to a parallelogram of size
N\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}\times N^{2}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim N^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}\times N^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}
. Now we pick the integer centers
\eta_{1}
,
\eta_{2}
of the (long)
N^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}}
-intervals at the
\xi
-endpoints
\xi_{1}
,
\xi_{2}

|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}|\sim N^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}
. We choose

such that

\eta_{2}-A\xi_{2}=\eta_{2}^{\prime}=\eta_{1}^{\prime}=\eta_{1}-A\xi_{1}.

This Galilean transform maps the
N^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}\times N^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}
-parallelogram to a rectangle of the same size. But this transform does not necessarily leave the frequency lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

and see that after transform

Let

\begin{split}\xi^{3}&=(\xi_{0}+\xi^{\prime})^{3}=\xi_{0}^{3}+3\xi_{0}^{2}\xi^{\prime}+3\xi_{0}(\xi^{\prime})^{2}+(\xi^{\prime})^{3}=\text{Lin}(\xi^{\prime})+3\xi_{0}(\xi^{\prime})^{2}+\mathcal{O}(N^{\alpha}),\\

We remain with the pruned phase function

\eta_{0}\in[k-2,k+2]
and by the Fourier support condition on
f
we have
\xi^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}
with
|I^{\prime}|\sim 1/k\sim 1/\eta_{0}
.

Now we change to continuous approximation

\begin{split}&\quad\big\|\sum_{(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in A^{I^{\prime}}_{1,1}\cap(\mathbb{Z}/N\times\mathbb{Z}/N^{2})}e^{i(x^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}((\xi^{\prime})^{3}-\frac{(\eta^{\prime}+\eta_{0})^{2}}{\xi^{\prime}})}a_{\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}\big\|^{4}_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(B_{N^{3}})}\\
&\lesssim\big\|\int_{I^{\prime}\times[-1,1]}e^{i(x^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}((\xi^{\prime})^{3}-\frac{(\eta^{\prime}+\eta_{0})^{2}}{\xi^{\prime}}))}f(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime}d\eta^{\prime}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(B_{N^{3}})}^{4}.\end{split}

We normalize the
\xi^{\prime}
-support by
\xi^{\prime}=\xi_{0}+\xi^{\prime\prime}/\eta_{0}
and correspondingly let
x^{\prime}=\eta_{0}x^{\prime\prime}
. We obtain

\begin{split}&\big\|\int_{I^{\prime}\times[-1,1]}e^{i(x^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}((\xi^{\prime})^{3}-\frac{(\eta^{\prime}+\eta_{0})^{2}}{\xi^{\prime}}))}f(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime}d\eta^{\prime}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(B_{N^{3}})}^{4}\\
&\lesssim\eta_{0}\big\|\int_{(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in[-1,1]^{2}}e^{i(x^{\prime\prime}\xi^{\prime\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}\bar{\omega}(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime}))}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime\prime}d\eta^{\prime}\big\|^{4}_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime}}(\mathcal{E}_{N^{3}\times N^{3}/\eta_{0}\times N^{3}})}.\end{split}

We cover the space-time ellipsoid

\mathcal{E}_{N^{3}\times N^{3}/\eta_{0}\times N^{3}}=\{(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\,|t|\leq N^{3},\;|x|\leq N^{3}/\eta_{0},\;|y|\leq N^{3}\}

with balls
(B_{\delta^{-1}})
of size
\delta^{-1}=N^{3}/\eta_{0}
and seek to apply decoupling to the oscillatory integral

\begin{split}&\quad\big\|\int_{(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in(-1,1)^{2}}e^{i(x^{\prime\prime}\xi^{\prime\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}\bar{\omega}(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime}))}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime\prime}d\eta^{\prime}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime}}(B_{\delta^{-1}})}\\
&\lesssim\big\|w_{B_{\delta^{-1}}}\int_{(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in(-1,1)^{2}}e^{i(x^{\prime\prime}\xi^{\prime\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}\bar{\omega}(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime}))}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime\prime}d\eta^{\prime}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.\end{split}

Invoking Theorem
3.2
we find

\begin{split}&\quad\big\|w_{B_{\delta^{-1}}}\int_{(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in(-1,1)^{2}}e^{i(x^{\prime\prime}\xi^{\prime\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}\bar{\omega}(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime}))}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime\prime}d\eta^{\prime}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta:\delta-\text{flat}\\
\text{set of }\bar{\omega}\end{subarray}}\big\|w_{B_{\delta^{-1}}}\int_{\theta}e^{i(x^{\prime\prime}\xi^{\prime\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}\bar{\omega}(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime}))}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime\prime}d\eta^{\prime}\big\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

As a consequence of Minkowski’s inequality we obtain decoupling on
\mathcal{E}_{N^{3}\times N^{3}/\eta_{0}\times N^{3}}
into
\delta
-flat sets (recall
\delta=\eta_{0}/N^{3}
):

\begin{split}&\hskip 9.24994pt\big\|\int_{(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in(-1,1)^{2}}e^{i(x^{\prime\prime}\xi^{\prime\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}\bar{\omega}(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime}))}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime\prime}d\eta^{\prime}\big\|^{4}_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime}}(\mathcal{E}_{N^{3}\times N^{3}/\eta_{0}\times N^{3}})}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{\theta:\delta\text{-flat set}}\big\|\int_{(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in\theta}e^{i(x^{\prime\prime}\xi^{\prime\prime}+y^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+t^{\prime}\bar{\omega}(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime}))}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime\prime}d\eta^{\prime}\big\|^{2}_{L^{4}_{t^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime}}(w_{\mathcal{E}_{N^{3}\times N^{3}/\eta_{0}\times N^{3}}})}\big)^{2}.\end{split}

Now we reverse all the scalings, the inflation by periodicity and the continuous approximation to find the
\ell^{2}
-decoupling inequality

(22)

\begin{split}&\quad\big\|\sum_{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t\omega(\xi,\eta))}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta:\delta\text{-flat}\\
\text{set for }\bar{\omega}\end{subarray}}\big\|\sum_{(\xi,\eta)\in\tilde{\theta}}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t\omega(\xi,\eta))}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)\big\|^{2}_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

By Proposition
3.7
the
\delta
-flat sets for
\bar{\omega}
can be contained into rectangles with long side of length at most
\eta_{0}\cdot\eta_{0}^{\frac{1}{3}}N^{-1}
into direction
\mathfrak{n}_{1}=(\xi_{1}^{\prime},\eta_{1}^{\prime})
with
|\xi^{\prime}|\gtrsim 1
and for
(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\eta})\in R^{\prime}
into
\eta^{\prime}
-direction of length at most
\eta_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{-\frac{3}{2}}
. When reversing the scaling and the normalization of the Fourier support
\xi^{\prime}=\xi_{0}+\xi^{\prime\prime}/\eta_{0}
we find length of
\eta_{0}^{\frac{1}{3}}
into
\mathfrak{n}_{1}
-direction and
\eta_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{1}{2}}
into
\eta^{\prime}
-direction. After applying Bernstein’s inequality in
\xi

, we find

(23)

\begin{split}&\quad\big\|\sum_{(\xi,\eta)\in\tilde{\theta}}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t\omega(\xi,\eta))}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\\
&\lesssim\eta_{0}^{\frac{1}{12}}\big(\sum_{\xi\in\pi_{\xi}(\tilde{\theta})}\big\|\sum_{\eta\in I_{\xi}}e^{i(y\eta+t\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi})}a_{\xi,\eta}\big\|^{2}_{L^{4}_{t,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T})}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

By the above we have
|I_{\xi}|\sim\eta_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Recall that
|\xi|\sim N
. We carry out a change of variables

\big\|\sum_{\eta\in I_{\xi}}e^{i(y\eta+t\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi})}a_{\xi,\eta}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{4}}\big\|\sum_{\eta\in I_{\xi}}e^{i(y\eta+t\eta^{2})}a_{\xi,\eta}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,y}([0,N^{-1}]\times\mathbb{T})}.

Now we apply Hölder’s inequality in time and a lossless Strichartz estimate on frequency dependent times due to

28
]
and
[
5
]
noting that
|I_{\xi}|\lesssim N
by assumption
|\eta_{0}|\lesssim N
:

(24)

\begin{split}\big\|\sum_{\eta\in I_{\xi}}e^{i(y\eta+t\eta^{2})}a_{\xi,\eta}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,y}([0,N^{-1}]\times\mathbb{T})}&\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{4}}\big\|\sum_{\eta\in I_{\xi}}e^{i(y\eta+t\eta^{2})}a_{\xi,\eta}\big\|_{L^{8}_{t}([0,N^{-1}],L^{4}_{y}(\mathbb{T}))}\\
&\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{8}}\|a_{\xi,\eta}\|_{\ell^{2}_{\eta}}.\end{split}

Taking (

) and (

(25)

\begin{split}&\quad\big\|\sum_{(\xi,\eta)\in\tilde{\theta}}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t\omega(\xi,\eta))}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\\
&\lesssim\eta_{0}^{\frac{1}{12}}N^{\frac{1}{8}}\|a_{\xi,\eta}\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi,\eta\in\tilde{\theta}}}.\end{split}

Plugging (

) into (

) we complete the proof by
\log(N)
-overlap of
\tilde{\theta}
.

For future reference we record the following consequence of the well-known transfer principle - for a function localized in modulation the estimates for free solutions hold with a modulation weight:

Proposition 3.9

(Transfer principle for
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimates)
.

Let
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, and
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2})
be a function with
\operatorname{supp}(u)\subseteq\{(\tau,\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\xi\in A,\,\eta\in B,\,|\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta)|\leq L\}
. Assume that the estimate

\|S_{\mathrm{KP}}(t)f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim C(A,B)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}

holds for functions with
\operatorname{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\xi\in A,\,\eta\in B\}
. Then the following estimate holds:

\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}[u]\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim C(A,B)L^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t,x,y}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

Démonstration.

This follows from the representation

\mathcal{F}^{-1}[u](t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-L}^{L}e^{it\tau}S_{\mathrm{KP}}(t)f(\tau)d\tau,\;f(\tau):=\mathcal{F}_{t}(S_{\mathrm{KP}}(-\cdot)\mathcal{F}^{-1}[u])(\tau),

the assumed estimate, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and Plancherel.
∎

Bilinear Strichartz estimates

The following Strichartz estimate was proved in
[
4
, Section 4]
:

Proposition 4.1

(Bourgain)
.

Let
f_{j}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
such that for
(\tau_{j},\xi_{j},\eta_{j})\in\operatorname{supp}(f_{j})
the following holds:

\xi_{j}\in I_{j},\;|\xi_{j}|\sim N_{j},\;|\tau_{j}-\omega(\xi_{j},\eta_{j})|\leq L_{j},

where
I_{j}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is an interval and
N_{j}
,
L_{j}
are dyadic numbers. Let
L_{\max}=\max(L_{1},L_{2})
and
N_{\max}=\max(N_{1},N_{2})
. Then the estimate holds:

\|f_{1}*f_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2})}\lesssim B(N_{1},N_{2},L_{1},L_{2},|I_{1}|,|I_{2}|)\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}},

where

(26)

B\lesssim L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\max}^{\frac{1}{4}}N_{\min}^{\frac{1}{4}}+L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}I_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\min\{L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\max}^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\big(\frac{N_{\min}}{N_{\max}}\big)^{\frac{1}{4}},L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\max}^{\frac{1}{4}}N_{\min}^{\frac{1}{4}}I_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\}.

Here
X_{\min}=\min\{X_{1},X_{2}\}
and
X_{\max}=\max\{X_{1},X_{2}\}
for
X\in\{L,N\}
. Moreover,
I_{\min}=\min(|I_{1}|,|I_{2}|)
and
I_{\max}=\max(|I_{1}|,|I_{2}|)
.

We could apply this in the proof of Theorem
1.1
and with Theorem
3.3
would still get a result below
L^{2}
. Instead, we use Proposition
4.4
below, which we hope makes the argument easier to follow.
Before stating Proposition
4.4
, we state the well-known bilinear estimate based on transversality, recall (
5
), which is frequently used. The proof is implicit in
[
4
, Section 4]
, we give the details for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
f_{i}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2})
satisfy the assumptions of Proposition
4.1
and suppose in addition

\big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\big|\sim D

for
(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\in\operatorname{supp}_{\xi,\eta}f_{i}
. Then the following estimate holds:

\|f_{1}*f_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2})}\lesssim I_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{\max}/D\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2})}.

Démonstration.

By convolution constraint and almost orthogonality we can suppose that the
\xi
-support of
f_{i}
,
i=1,2
is contained in intervals of length
I_{\min}
. Suppose by symmetry that
L_{1}=L_{\min}
. We apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to find

\begin{split}&\quad\int\big|\int f_{1}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\eta_{1})f_{2}(\tau-\tau_{1},\xi-\xi_{1},\eta-\eta_{1})d\tau_{1}d\xi_{1}d\eta_{1}\big|^{2}d\tau d\xi d\eta\\
&\lesssim\sup_{\tau,\xi,\eta}A(\tau,\xi,\eta)\iint|f_{1}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\eta_{1})|^{2}|f_{2}(\tau-\tau_{1},\xi-\xi_{1},\eta-\eta_{1})|^{2}d\tau_{1}d\xi_{1}d\eta_{1}d\tau d\xi d\eta\\
&\lesssim\text{meas}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}}A(\tau,\xi,\eta)\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}^{2}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau\xi\eta}}^{2}\end{split}

with

\begin{split}A(\tau,\xi,\eta)&=\{(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}:(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\in\operatorname{supp}(f_{1}),\\
&\quad(\tau-\tau_{1},\xi-\xi_{1},\eta-\eta_{1})\in\operatorname{supp}(f_{2})\}.\end{split}

We estimate the measure as follows:
By assumption we have

(27)

\#\{\xi_{1}\in\pi_{\xi_{1}}(A(\tau,\xi,\eta))\}\lesssim I_{\min}.

For fixed
\xi_{1},\xi,\eta
and
\tau
we count the number of
\eta_{1}
by writing

\begin{split}\tau_{1}-\omega(\xi_{1},\eta)+((\tau-\tau_{1})-\omega(\xi-\xi_{1},\eta-\eta_{1}))&=\tau-\omega(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})-\omega(\xi-\xi_{1},\eta-\eta_{1})\\
&=:g_{\tau,\xi,\xi_{1},\eta}(\eta_{1}).\end{split}

This shows that
g_{\tau,\xi,\xi_{1},\eta_{1}}
ranges in an interval of at most
L_{\max}
and since

\partial_{\eta_{1}}g=2\big(\frac{\eta-\eta_{1}}{\xi-\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}\big),\quad|\partial_{\eta_{1}}g|\sim D,

we can estimate the number of
\eta_{1}
for fixed
\xi_{1}
by the mean-value theorem:

(28)

\#\{\eta_{1}:(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\in A_{\xi_{1},\eta_{1}}(\tau,\xi,\eta)\}\lesssim\langle L_{\max}/D\rangle.

For fixed
\xi_{1}
,
\eta_{1}
we have

(29)

|\{\tau_{1}\in\mathbb{R}:(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\in A(\tau,\xi,\eta)\}|\lesssim L_{\min}.

Taking (
27
), (
28
), and (
29
) together we complete the proof.
∎

We record the following bilinear estimate hinging on second-order transversality, which is again implicit in

, Section 4]
:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
N_{i},L_{i}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
,
N_{2}\lesssim N_{1}
, and
f_{i}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2})
satisfy the assumptions of Proposition
4.1
. Suppose that the intervals
I_{i}
satisfy
|I_{i}|\leq A
for
i=1,2
. Assume for
(\tau_{i},\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\in\operatorname{supp}(f_{i})
that

|\xi_{i}|\sim N_{i},\quad\xi_{i}\in I_{i},\quad|\tau_{i}-\omega(\xi,\eta)|\leq L_{i}.

Then the following estimate holds:

\|f_{1}*f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\lesssim\langle A\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{\max}N_{2}\rangle^{\frac{1}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}}.

Démonstration.

The argument follows along the lines of the previous proof. The difference is that we are considering the second derivative in
\eta_{1}
of
f_{\tau,\xi,\xi_{1}}
:

\partial_{\eta_{1}}^{2}f=2\big(\frac{1}{\xi_{1}}+\frac{1}{\xi-\xi_{1}}\big).

We can suppose that
\xi_{1}
and

are of the same sign, as a consequence of the symmetry of the dispersion relation and complex conjugation. We find the bound

), (
4
), and (

The following bilinear estimate relates to a special case of Proposition
4.1
and can be proved with the arguments in
[
4
]
. Here we give a different proof based on the biorthogonality, which underpins the proof of the Córdoba-Fefferman square function estimate
[
7
]
(see also
[
10
,
6
]
). We refer to
[
8
, Section 3.2]
for a modern treatise.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
D^{*}\geq 2
,
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
such that for
(\tau_{i},\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\in\operatorname{supp}(f_{i})
the following holds:

|\xi_{i}|\sim N_{i},\;|\tau_{i}-\omega(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})|\lesssim L_{i},\;\big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\big|\lesssim D^{*}

for dyadic numbers satisfying
N_{2}\ll N_{1}
and
N_{1}^{\alpha}\leq L_{\max}\lesssim N_{1}^{2}N_{2}
.
Then the following estimate holds:

(30)

\|f_{1}*f_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2})}\lesssim\log(D^{*})(N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}+N_{2}^{\frac{1}{4}}N_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}})\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}}.

Démonstration.

We carry out a dyadic decomposition in
D
such that we can suppose in the following that

\big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\big|\sim D.

Summation over
(\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq D\leq D^{*}
incurs the logarithmic loss in
D^{*}
.

Applying Proposition
4.2
directly yields

\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\lesssim N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{\max}/D\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}},

which suffices for
D\gtrsim N_{1}
, but does not in case
D\ll N_{1}
.

In the latter case we shall see that we can significantly decrease the effective
\xi
-support using a variant of the
Córdoba-Fefferman square function estimate.
By symmetry of the dispersion relation, we suppose in the following that
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}>0
.

We rewrite the convolution constraint as

\begin{cases}\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}&=\xi_{3}+\xi_{4},\\
\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}&=\eta_{3}+\eta_{4},\\
\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}&=\tau_{3}+\tau_{4}.\end{cases}\Leftrightarrow\begin{cases}\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}&=\xi_{3}+\xi_{4},\\
\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}&=\eta_{3}+\eta_{4},\\
\xi_{1}^{3}-\frac{\eta_{1}^{2}}{\xi_{1}}+\xi_{2}^{3}-\frac{\eta_{2}^{2}}{\xi_{2}}&=\xi_{3}^{3}-\frac{\eta_{3}^{2}}{\xi_{3}}+\xi_{4}^{3}-\frac{\eta_{4}^{2}}{\xi_{4}}+\mathcal{O}(L_{\max}).\end{cases}

From the third line we subtract
\omega(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})=\omega(\xi_{3}+\xi_{4},\eta_{3}+\eta_{4})
to find

\begin{cases}\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}&=\xi_{3}+\xi_{4},\\
\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}&=\eta_{3}+\eta_{4},\\
3\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})-\frac{(\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1})^{2}}{(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\xi_{1}\xi_{2}}&=3\xi_{3}\xi_{4}(\xi_{3}+\xi_{4})-\frac{(\eta_{3}\xi_{4}-\eta_{4}\xi_{3})^{2}}{(\xi_{3}+\xi_{4})\xi_{3}\xi_{4}}+\mathcal{O}(L_{\max}).\end{cases}

Note that

\begin{split}\frac{(\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1})^{2}}{(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\xi_{1}\xi_{2}}&=\frac{(\eta_{1}/\xi_{1}-\eta_{2}/\xi_{2})^{2}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}}{\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}}=\mathcal{O}(D^{2}\cdot N_{2}),\\
\frac{(\eta_{3}\xi_{4}-\eta_{4}\xi_{3})^{2}}{(\xi_{3}+\xi_{4})\xi_{3}\xi_{4}}&=\mathcal{O}(D^{2}\cdot N_{2}).\end{split}

We rescale to unit frequencies using the anisotropic scaling
\xi_{i}\to\xi_{i}/N_{1}
,
\eta_{i}\to\eta_{i}/N_{1}^{2}
:

\begin{cases}\xi^{\prime}_{1}+\xi^{\prime}_{2}&=\xi^{\prime}_{3}+\xi^{\prime}_{4},\\
\eta^{\prime}_{1}+\eta^{\prime}_{2}&=\eta^{\prime}_{3}+\eta^{\prime}_{4},\\
\xi^{\prime}_{1}\xi^{\prime}_{2}(\xi^{\prime}_{1}+\xi^{\prime}_{2})&=\xi^{\prime}_{3}\xi^{\prime}_{4}(\xi^{\prime}_{3}+\xi^{\prime}_{4})+\mathcal{O}(\frac{D^{2}N_{2}}{N_{1}^{3}}+\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{1}^{3}}).\end{cases}

Let
A=\frac{D^{2}N_{2}}{N_{1}^{3}}+\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{1}^{3}}
and note that
A\ll 1
under the above assumptions. The above implies by
\xi_{1}^{\prime}+\xi_{2}^{\prime}=\xi_{3}^{\prime}+\xi_{4}^{\prime}\sim 1

\begin{cases}\xi_{1}^{\prime}+\xi_{2}^{\prime}&=\xi_{3}^{\prime}+\xi_{4}^{\prime},\\
\xi_{1}^{\prime}\xi_{2}^{\prime}&=\xi_{3}^{\prime}\xi_{4}^{\prime}+\mathcal{O}(A).\end{cases}

Furthermore, by squaring the first line and subtracting the second line twice,

\begin{cases}\xi_{1}^{\prime}+\xi_{2}^{\prime}&=\xi_{3}^{\prime}+\xi_{4}^{\prime},\\
(\xi_{1}^{\prime})^{2}+(\xi_{2}^{\prime})^{2}&=(\xi_{3}^{\prime})^{2}+(\xi_{4}^{\prime})^{2}+\mathcal{O}(A).\end{cases}

By the bilinear Córdoba–Fefferman square function estimate, this yields an almost orthogonal decomposition of
\xi^{\prime}_{i}
into intervals of length
A
. For self-containedness we include the argument. The second line of the above display implies by convolution constraint and the first line

(\xi_{1}^{\prime})^{2}+(\xi_{2}^{\prime})^{2}=(\xi_{3}^{\prime})^{2}+(\xi_{4}^{\prime})^{2}+\mathcal{O}(A)\Rightarrow 2(\xi_{1}^{\prime}-\xi_{2}^{\prime})(\xi_{2}^{\prime}-\xi_{4}^{\prime})=\mathcal{O}(A).

Since
|\xi_{1}^{\prime}-\xi_{2}^{\prime}|\sim 1
by
N_{2}\ll N_{1}
and rescaling, we find
|\xi_{2}^{\prime}-\xi_{4}^{\prime}|\lesssim A
and correspondingly,
|\xi_{1}^{\prime}-\xi_{3}^{\prime}|\lesssim A
. For the
\xi_{i}
-frequencies after scaling back this translates into an almost orthogonal decomposition into intervals in
\mathbb{Z}
of length

\ell=\langle AN_{1}\rangle\sim\frac{D^{2}N_{2}}{N_{1}^{2}}+\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{1}^{2}}+1.

We denote the additional decomposition in
\xi
into intervals of length
\ell
by

f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}=\sum_{I_{i}}f^{I_{i}}_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}

to obtain the square function estimate

(31)

\int|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{I_{1},I_{2}}\int|f^{I_{1}}_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f^{I_{2}}_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}|^{2}.

In case
D\sim\big(\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}
we apply the bilinear Strichartz estimate from Proposition
4.3
to find

\|f^{I_{1}}_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f^{I_{2}}_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\langle L_{\max}^{\frac{1}{2}}/N_{1}\rangle L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}(L_{\max}N_{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f^{I_{i}}_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.

Then the claim follows from the almost orthogonal summation over
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
. This argument is omitted in the following for brevity. If
L_{\max}\lesssim N_{1}^{2}
, then we can use the lower bound on
L_{\max}
to find after plugging in the almost orthogonal decomposition (
31
)

\begin{split}\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}&\lesssim L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\max}^{\frac{1}{2}}(N_{1}^{-\alpha}N_{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}\\
&\lesssim\langle N_{1}^{-\alpha}N_{2}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.\end{split}

If
L_{\max}\gtrsim N_{1}^{2}
, we use the upper bound
L_{\max}\lesssim N_{1}^{2}N_{2}
to obtain

\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\lesssim N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}/N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.

Next, suppose that
\big(\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ll D\lesssim N_{1}
: In this case we obtain an almost orthogonal decomposition of
\xi
-frequencies (
31
) into intervals of length

\langle AN_{1}\rangle\sim\frac{D^{2}N_{2}}{N_{1}^{2}}+1.

In case
D^{2}N_{2}\lesssim N_{1}^{2}
, we apply Proposition
4.3
after plugging in (
31
) to find

\begin{split}\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}&\lesssim L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}(L_{\max}N_{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}\\
&\lesssim(N_{2}N_{1}^{-\alpha})^{\frac{1}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.\end{split}

This yields an acceptable contribution.
In the ultimate estimate we used the minimum size of
L_{\max}
.

We turn to
D^{2}N_{2}\gtrsim N_{1}^{2}
. In this case we apply Proposition
4.2
after plugging in (
31
) to find

\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\lesssim\frac{DN_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N_{1}}L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{\max}/D\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.

Summing in
D\lesssim N_{1}
we obtain by the minimum size
L_{\max}\gtrsim N_{1}^{\alpha}

\sum_{\frac{N_{1}}{N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim D\lesssim N_{1}}\frac{DN_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N_{1}}L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{\max}/D\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}\lesssim N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Outline of the proof of the nonlinear estimates

In this section we explain our argument to show the short-time Fourier restriction estimates for the new well-posedness results in broad strokes. Technical details are provided in the forthcoming sections.

5.1.
Estimating solutions at negative Sobolev regularity

We begin with a description of Fourier restriction norms without frequency-dependent time localization. Fourier restriction norms for
s,b\in\mathbb{R}
with modulation weight are defined as

\begin{split}X^{s,b}&=\{u:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}\;|\|u\|_{X^{s,b}}<\infty\},\\
\|u\|^{2}_{X^{s,b}}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\\
\xi\neq 0\end{subarray}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\langle\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta)\rangle^{2b}\langle(\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta))/|\xi|^{3}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}|\hat{u}(\tau,\xi,\eta)|^{2}d\tau.\end{split}

In the limiting case
b=\frac{1}{2}
a modification becomes necessary for the transfer principle to remain valid. We denote the modified space by
\bar{X}^{s,\frac{1}{2}}
. To avoid technicalities, we do not elaborate on Besov refinements like in (
7
), which salvage the transfer principle in the limiting case. We refer to
[
25
, p. 656]
for details on a possible refinement in the limiting case
b=1/2
. Indeed, the Besov refinements coincide with the
X^{s,b}
-norm for functions localized in frequency and modulation. For this reason, we pretend to carry out the estimates in the
X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}
-norm.

Bourgain showed analytic well-posedness via the estimates for solutions

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|u\|_{\bar{X}^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}&\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\bar{X}^{0,-\frac{1}{2}}},\\
\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\bar{X}^{0,-\frac{1}{2}}}&\lesssim\|u\|^{2}_{\bar{X}^{0,\frac{1}{2}}},\end{array}\right.

and a corresponding estimate for differences of solutions (though the arguments to carry out the crucial bilinear estimate are identical).

After Littlewood-Paley decomposition we can assume the functions to be dyadically localized, which up to summation question reduces the crucial bilinear estimate to

N^{s}\|P_{N}\partial_{x}(P_{N_{1}}u_{1}P_{N_{2}}u_{2})\|_{\bar{X}^{0,-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim N_{1}^{s}\|P_{N_{1}}u\|_{\bar{X}^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}N_{2}^{s}\|P_{N_{2}}u_{2}\|_{\bar{X}^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}.

We have by duality

\|P_{N}\partial_{x}(P_{N_{1}}u_{1}P_{N_{2}}u_{2})\|_{\bar{X}^{0,-\frac{1}{2}}}=\sup_{\|u\|_{\bar{X}^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}=1}\iint P_{N}\overline{u}\partial_{x}(P_{N_{1}}u_{1}P_{N_{2}}u_{2})dxdydt.

We introduce modulation projections to write

\iint P_{N}\overline{u}\partial_{x}(P_{N_{1}}u_{1}P_{N_{2}}u_{2})=\sum_{L,L_{1},L_{2}}\iint(P_{N}Q_{L}\partial_{x}\overline{u})\,(P_{N_{1}}Q_{L_{1}}u_{1})\,(P_{N_{2}}Q_{L_{2}}u_{2}).

For
i=1,2
we let

f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}=|\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[P_{N_{i}}Q_{L_{i}}u_{i}]|

and
f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}=|\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[P_{N}Q_{L}u]|
. The bound

\iint P_{N}Q_{L}\overline{u}\partial_{x}(P_{N_{1}}Q_{L_{1}}u_{1}P_{N_{2}}Q_{L_{2}}u_{2})\lesssim NC(N,N_{1},N_{2})\|P_{N}Q_{L}u\|_{\bar{X}^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|P_{N_{i}}Q_{L_{i}}u\|_{\bar{X}^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}

is for
L_{i}\lesssim N_{i}^{3}
implied by

\int f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})d\xi d\eta d\tau\lesssim C(N,N_{1},N_{2})\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.

For modulations
L_{i}\gg N_{i}^{3}
the weight
(1+L_{i}/N_{i}^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}
must be taken into account.

Suppose in the following that the frequencies are comparable
N\sim N_{1}\sim N_{2}
.
Recall the defocusing character of the resonance function

\Omega(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=3\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})+\frac{(\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1})^{2}}{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})}.

By convolution constraint, we have

-\Omega=\tau-\omega(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})-(\tau_{1}-\omega(\xi_{1},\eta_{1}))-(\tau_{2}-\omega(\xi_{2},\eta_{2}))\Rightarrow L_{\max}\gtrsim N_{1}N_{2}N.

Problematic interactions arise in the
resonant case

N_{1}^{3}\sim L_{\max}\gg L_{\text{med}}
.
Suppose by symmetry
L_{\max}\sim L_{3}
and note that this is the minimal value
L_{\max}
can attain. By an almost orthogonality argument and Galilean invariance we can suppose the localization
|\eta_{i}|\lesssim N_{1}^{2}
.

By an application of Hölder’s inequality together with the
L^{4}_{t,x,y}
-Strichartz estimates provided by Theorem
3.3
yields

\begin{split}\big|\int f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})d\tau d\xi d\eta\big|&\leq\|f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{t,x,y}[f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}]\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\|f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}N_{1}^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N_{1}^{-\frac{5}{4}+\varepsilon}\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.\end{split}

This shows that the resonant High
\times
High
\rightarrow
High-interaction can be estimated without frequency-dependent time localization at regularities
s>-\frac{1}{4}
.

Let
M\geq N_{1}
. If
L_{\max}\sim M^{2}N_{1}\gg N_{1}^{3}
, but
L_{\text{med}}\ll L_{\max}
, which is referred to as
non-resonant case
, the corresponding localization
|\eta_{i}|\lesssim M^{2}N_{1}
leads to lossier
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimates provided by Proposition
3.8
. Still an estimate via
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimates remains possible for negative Sobolev regularity provided that
M\lesssim N_{1}^{1+a_{1}}
for some
a_{1}>0
to be determined. On the other hand, for
M\gtrsim N_{1}^{1+a_{1}}
, where we only use that
a_{1}>0
, since the transversality constant satisfies

D\sim\big|\frac{\eta-\eta_{1}}{\xi-\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}\big|\gtrsim N_{1}^{1+a_{1}},

we can use a bilinear Strichartz estimate to find

\begin{split}\big|\int f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})d\tau d\eta d\xi\big|&\leq\|f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim(N_{1}^{3+2a_{1}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.\end{split}

Interpolation between the new
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimates and the bilinear
L^{2}
-Strichartz estimate already due to Bourgain is the key to show the new well-posedness results.

We remark that there is also the
strongly non-resonant case
,
L_{\max}\sim L_{\text{med}}
. In this case, since
L_{\max}\gtrsim N_{1}^{3}
, the bilinear Strichartz estimate provided by Proposition
4.1
(more precisely, using the ultimate constant in (
26
)) allows us to cover the full subcritical range.

Suppose
L_{\max}=L_{3}
, and
L_{\text{med}}=L_{1}
by symmetry. We have

\begin{split}L^{-\frac{1}{4}}N^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|1_{D_{N,L}}(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}&\lesssim L^{-\frac{1}{4}}N^{-\frac{3}{4}}N^{\frac{3}{4}}L_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{1}^{\frac{1}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim L^{-\frac{1}{4}}N^{-\frac{3}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.\end{split}

The strongly non-resonant case never poses a problem.

Next, consider the High
\times
Low
\rightarrow
High-interaction
N_{1}\sim N_{3}\gg N_{2}
and suppose that
L_{\max}=N_{1}^{2}N_{2}\gg L_{\text{med}}
. Moreover, suppose that
L_{3}=L_{\max}
. If
N_{2}\lesssim N_{1}^{1-a_{2}}
, the linear
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimates are not effective anymore. In case of small transversality
D\lesssim N_{2}
it is a consequence of the biorthogonality used in the proof of the Córdoba–Fefferman square function estimate to effectively reduce the
\xi
-frequency support:

(32)

\int f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})d\tau d\xi d\eta\lesssim\|f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\sum_{I_{1}\sim I_{2}}\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}^{I_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}^{I_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.

The argument states in summary that there is an almost orthogonal decomposition of the
\xi_{i}
-frequencies of
f
into intervals
I_{i}
of length
N_{2}(N_{2}/N_{1})
; the resulting functions with additionally restricted frequency support are denoted by
f_{I_{i}}
. With
I_{1}\sim I_{2}
we mean that for any interval
I_{1}
there are only finitely many
I_{2}
such that

\int f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}(f^{I_{1}}_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f^{I_{2}}_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})\neq 0.

With (
32
) at hand, the bilinear Strichartz estimates (Proposition
4.1
,
4.2
) become more effective.

The interaction we cannot overcome without frequency-dependent time localization is the High
\times
Low
\rightarrow
High-interaction with
N_{2}\lesssim N_{1}^{1-a_{2}}
and large transversality
D\sim N_{1}
. In this case the the bilinear Strichartz estimate only yields

(33)

\begin{split}&\quad\int f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})d\tau d\xi d\eta\\
&\lesssim\|f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim(N_{1}^{2}N_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}L_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}(L_{1}\wedge L_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle(L_{1}\vee L_{2})/N_{1}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.\end{split}

But in case
L_{1}\sim L_{2}\sim 1
there is no effective smoothing. However, frequency-dependent time localization
T=T(N)=N^{-\alpha}
increases the minimum size of modulations
L_{\min}\gtrsim N_{1}^{\alpha}
, and short-time bilinear estimates can be carried out at negative Sobolev regularity.
Moreover note, if
L_{2}=L_{\max}\gg L_{\text{med}}
, i.e., the low frequency carries the high modulation the transversality is weaker, but the modulation weight
(1+L_{i}/N_{i}^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}
comes to rescue. We can estimate the derivative nonlinearity by the above argument for
s\geq-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\varepsilon
as a consequence of the bilinear Strichartz estimate and frequency-dependent time localization.

Introducing frequency-dependent time localization to mitigate the derivative nonlinearity necessitates also to prove energy estimates with frequency-dependent time localization.
Regarding short-time energy estimates, we use essentially the same modulation localized estimates like for the short-time bilinear estimates. When carrying out the estimates for solutions, we take advantage of the symmetries of the equation, i.e., real-valuedness and conservative derivative nonlinearity, to assign the derivative always to the low frequency. We obtain several conditions on the regularity when estimating solutions as outlined above. Choosing
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{14}
, we can fit all constraints under the umbrella

s\geq-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\varepsilon.

5.2.
Estimating differences of solutions

When estimating the energy for differences of solutions, we are lacking the symmetry which allows us to integrate by parts. Let
v=u_{1}-u_{2}
denote the difference of two solutions. A particular case we have to estimate is the High
\times
Low-interaction,
N_{2}\lesssim N_{1}^{1-\kappa}\lesssim N_{1}\sim N_{3}
given by

(34)

\iint P_{N_{1}}v\partial_{x}(P_{N_{2}}vP_{N_{3}}u_{i})dxdt.

Relying again on the bilinear estimate, this interaction can only be controlled estimating the difference of the solution at lower regularity than the solution itself. Using the estimate from (
33
) we are led to the condition, taking into account frequency-dependent time localization and the derivative loss,

N_{1}^{2s^{\prime}}N_{1}^{1+\alpha}(N_{1}^{2}N_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\lesssim N_{1}^{s^{\prime}}N_{2}^{s^{\prime}}N_{1}^{s}.

This gives the constraint

s^{\prime}\leq\frac{s-\alpha/2}{\kappa}.

This suggests to choose
\kappa
as large as possible to take advantage of the frequency difference as much as possible.
But when considering the case
N_{2}\gtrsim N_{1}^{1-\kappa}
applying two
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimates leads us to the condition

N_{1}^{2s^{\prime}}N_{1}^{1+\alpha}(N_{1}^{2}N_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{8}+\varepsilon}N_{1}^{\frac{1}{8}+\varepsilon}\lesssim N_{1}^{s^{\prime}}N_{2}^{s^{\prime}}N_{1}^{s}.

This condition shows that
\kappa<2/3
is admissible for an estimate at negative regularities. But the regularity increases choosing
\kappa
large, so for this estimate a small
\kappa
is more desirable. We need to compromise these two estimates and choose to simplify the expressions
\kappa=\frac{1}{8}
and
s^{\prime}=20s
. Taking all cases into considerations, we find the short-time energy estimate to hold true for
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{36}
with
s^{\prime}=20s
and

-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\varepsilon\leq s\leq-\frac{5\alpha}{12}.

When considering the short-time estimate of the nonlinearity for differences of solutions

\|\partial_{x}(u_{i}v)\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s^{\prime}}(T)}\lesssim T^{\delta}\|u_{i}\|_{F^{s}(T)}\|v\|_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)},

the High
\times
High
\rightarrow
Low-case requires particular attention. Here we need to further reduce the time-localization
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{45}
to be able to prove the short-time nonlinear estimate for
s=-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\varepsilon
and
s^{\prime}=20s
.

The fact that the thresholds for time-localizations do not match exactly
indicates that the interpolation argument is non-optimal. Here it is the principle which matters: we can prove local well-posedness below
L^{2}
; the optimization of the non-linear expressions is presently not pursued.

Remark 5.1
.

It remains open whether it is possible to solve the KP-II equation on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
below
L^{2}
via Picard iteration. This seems to be tied to a possible improvement of Bourgain’s bilinear Strichartz estimate from Proposition
4.1
also for large transversality
D\sim N_{1}
. It is conceivable that bilinear decoupling estimates play a role for further improvement.
It might also make a difference whether the Cauchy problem is posed on a rational or irrational torus.

6.
Short-time nonlinear estimates

The purpose of this section is to propagate nonlinear interactions of solutions and differences of solutions in short-time function spaces. We show the following:

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\alpha>0,
and
\varepsilon>0
.

(i)

Let
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{4}
, and define

s_{1}(\alpha,\varepsilon)=\max(-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\varepsilon,-\frac{1}{8}+\frac{3\alpha}{8}+\varepsilon).

Then there is
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that the following estimate holds for all
T\in(0,1]
and
0>s\geq s_{1}(\alpha,\varepsilon)
:

(35)

\|\partial_{x}(u_{1}u_{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)}\lesssim T^{\delta}\|u_{1}\|_{F^{s}(T)}\|u_{2}\|_{F^{s}(T)}.

(ii)

Let
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{7}
, and define

s_{2}(\alpha,\varepsilon)=\max(-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\varepsilon,\frac{3\alpha-1}{84}+\varepsilon).

Let
0>s\geq s_{2}(\alpha,\varepsilon)
. Then it holds for
s^{\prime}=20s
:

(36)

\|\partial_{x}(uv)\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s^{\prime}}(T)}\lesssim T^{\delta}\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}\|v\|_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)}.

Remark 6.2
.

We note that choosing
\alpha
small enough, we can fit the conditions under one umbrella.
Indeed, choosing
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{7}
, we find that

s_{1}(\alpha,\varepsilon)=-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\varepsilon.

Moreover, for
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{45}
, we obtain

s_{2}(\alpha,\varepsilon)=-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\varepsilon.

Démonstration.

First, we turn to the proof of (i).
The claim follows from dyadic summation of frequency localized estimates:

(37)

\|P_{N}\partial_{x}(P_{N_{1}}u_{1}P_{N_{2}}u_{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}_{N}(T)}\lesssim C(N,N_{1},N_{2})T^{\delta}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|P_{N_{i}}u_{i}\|_{F_{N_{i}}(T)}.

The constant
C(N,N_{1},N_{2})
has to take into account the derivative loss, which gives a factor of
N
, and possibly increasing the time localization in the High
\times
High
\rightarrow
Low-interaction
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gg N
. The backbone of (
35
) is formed by estimates

L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|1_{D_{N,L}}(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\lesssim C^{\prime}(N,N_{1},N_{2})L_{\max}^{0-}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}},

or a variant with modulation weights
(1+L/N^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}
, or
(1+L_{i}/N_{i}^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}
. Above
\operatorname{supp}(f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}})\subseteq D_{N_{i},L_{i}}
. The room in the modulation variable comes in handy for the dyadic summation and to apply Lemma
2.7
, which yields the additional gain of
T^{\delta}
.

We carry out standard reductions to reduce (
37
) to estimates localized in modulation. In the following we suppose that
N_{1}\geq N_{2}
by symmetry.

High-Low- / High-High-interaction
:
N\sim N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}
. Consider extensions
P_{N_{i}}\tilde{u}_{i}
of
P_{N_{i}}u_{i}
such that

\|P_{N_{i}}\tilde{u}_{i}\|_{F_{N_{i}}}\leq 2\|P_{N_{i}}u_{i}\|_{F_{N_{i}}(T)}.

To lighten the notation, we redenote
P_{N_{i}}\tilde{u}_{i}
as
u_{i,N_{i}}
. By the definition of the
\mathcal{N}_{N}
-norm we bound the left hand side of (
37
) by

\sup_{t_{N}\in\mathbb{R}}\|(\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta)+iN^{\alpha})^{-1}\xi\,1_{A_{N}}(\xi)\,(f_{1,N_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2}})\|_{X_{N}}

with

f_{i,N_{i}}=\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[u_{i,N_{i}}\cdot\eta_{0}(N^{\alpha}(t-t_{N}))].

For
L_{i}\geq N^{\alpha}
we define

f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}=\begin{cases}1_{D_{N_{i},\leq L_{i}}}f_{i,N_{i}},&\quad L_{i}=N^{\alpha},\\
1_{D_{N_{i},L_{i}}}f_{i,N_{i}},&\quad L_{i}>N^{\alpha}.\end{cases}

By the function space properties it suffices to obtain an estimate

\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{L\geq N^{\alpha}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+L/N^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}N\|1_{D_{N,L}}(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim C(N,N_{1},N_{2})\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+L_{i}/N_{i}^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.\end{split}

We suppose in the following without loss of generality that
f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
Denote

\|1_{D_{N,L}}(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\leq(L_{1}\wedge L_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{\frac{3}{4}}(L_{1}\vee L_{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.

Interpolation of this estimate with trilinear estimates

\begin{split}&\quad L^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+L/N^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|1_{D_{N,L}}(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim C(N,N_{1},N_{2})\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+L_{i}/N_{i}^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.\end{split}

allows us to gain a factor

in the above display at the cost of

, which is always admissible. This allows us to apply Lemma

to obtain the factors

in (

High-High-interaction:

\bullet

Resonant subcase:
Suppose that

Clearly,

. So the additional time localization incurs a factor of
(N_{1}/N)^{\alpha}
and we are left with proving estimates

\begin{split}&\quad\sup_{t_{N}\in\mathbb{R}}\|(\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta)+iN^{\alpha})^{-1}\xi 1_{A_{N}}(\xi)\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[u_{1,N_{1}}\gamma(N_{1}^{\alpha}(t-t_{N_{1}}))]\\
&\quad*\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[u_{2,N_{2}}\gamma(N_{1}^{\alpha}(t-t_{N_{1}}))\eta_{0}(N^{\alpha}(t-t_{N}))]\|_{X_{N}}\\
&\lesssim C(N,N_{1},N_{2})\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[u_{i,N_{i}}\gamma(N_{1}^{\alpha}(t-t_{N_{1}}))\eta_{0}(N^{\alpha}(t-t_{N}))]\|_{X_{N_{i}}}\end{split}

because

\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[u_{i,N_{i}}\gamma(N_{1}^{\alpha}(t-t_{N_{1}}))\eta_{0}(N^{\alpha}(t-t_{N}))]\|_{X_{N_{i}}}\lesssim\|u_{i,N_{i}}\|_{F_{N_{i}}}.

We introduce the notation

f_{1,N_{1}}=\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[u_{1,N_{1}}\gamma(N_{1}^{\alpha}(t-t_{N_{1}}))],\;f_{2,N_{2}}=\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[u_{2,N_{2}}\gamma(N_{1}^{\alpha}(t-t_{N_{1}}))\eta_{0}(N^{\alpha}(t-t_{N}))]

for the space-time Fourier transforms and break its support according to modulation localization:

f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}=\begin{cases}1_{D_{N_{i},L_{i}}}f_{i,N_{i}},\quad&L_{i}>N_{1}^{\alpha},\\
1_{D_{N_{i},\leq L_{i}}}f_{i,N_{i}},\quad&L_{i}=N_{1}^{\alpha}.\end{cases}

Again we suppose by the invariance of the norms that
f_{i}\geq 0
.
The minimum modulation localization
N_{1}^{\alpha}
reflects the frequency-dependent time localization
T=T(N_{1})=N_{1}^{-\alpha}
. With the above notation we reduce to

\begin{split}&\quad L^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+L/N^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim C(N,N_{1},N_{2})\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+L_{i}/N_{i}^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\end{split}

with

, from which (

N
and additional time localization
(N_{1}/N)^{\alpha}
.

&\lesssim(N_{1}^{2}N_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(N_{2}^{\frac{1}{4}}N_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}}+N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}})\log(N_{1})\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+L_{i}/N_{i}^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.\end{split}

In case

we can use the analysis from Proposition

, which gives a more favorable estimate.

In the non-resonant case

,

, we again use the bilinear estimate in case

with

. This yields the same estimate like above.
If

, we note that we have the transversality

Then we can use the Hölder’s inequality and the bilinear Strichartz estimate from Proposition
4.2
to find

\begin{split}&\quad\int(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}d\xi d\eta d\tau\\
&\leq\|f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim(M^{3})^{-\frac{1}{2}}L_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}(L_{2}/N_{2}^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{\text{med}}/N_{2}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\log(M)\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}\\

Summing over

finishes the proof.

Clearly, for the second term it is immediate how the derivative acts on low frequencies. We turn to the first term. Write for

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}P_{N}u\partial_{x}[P_{N}(uP_{K}u)-P_{N}uP_{K}u]\\

We denote the symbol by

9.
Improved local well-posedness

In this section we conclude the local well-posedness below
L^{2}
based on the short-time analysis from previous sections. Starting point of our analysis is Bourgain’s global
L^{2}
-well-posedness result for real-valued initial data with persistence of regularity:

Theorem 9.1

(Global well-posedness of KP-II,
[
4
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
be a real-valued function with
\int_{\mathbb{T}}u_{0}(x,y)dx=0
. Then (
1
) is globally well-posed.

We denote the data-to-solution mapping by
S_{*}^{0}:L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\rightarrow C(\mathbb{R};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
, which assigns real-valued initial data
u_{0}
with
\int_{\mathbb{T}}u_{0}(x,y)dx=0
to global solutions
u
to (
1
).

We show the following improved local well-posedness result, which has been summarized in the Introduction:

Theorem 9.2

(Improved local well-posedness for KP-II)
.

Let
0>s>-\frac{1}{90}
, and
R>0
. Then
S^{0}_{*}
admits a unique continuous extension

S_{T}^{s}:H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\to C([0,T],H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))

with

provided that
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leq R

The proof is carried out in three steps:

(i)

We establish a priori estimates for solutions to (
1
) at negative Sobolev regularities on times
T=T(s,\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})})
.

(ii)

We prove a priori estimates for differences of solutions at lower regularities
s^{\prime}=20s
on times
T=T(s,\|u_{01}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})},\|u_{02}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})})

(iii)

The proof of continuous dependence in the
H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
-topology is concluded using frequency envelopes.

We begin with a priori estimates:

Theorem 9.3

(A priori estimates at Sobolev regularity of negative order)
.

Let
s>-\frac{1}{20}
and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
a real-valued function with vanishing mean
\int_{\mathbb{T}}u_{0}(x,y)dx=0
. Then there is
T=T(s,\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}})
such that we have the following a priori estimates:

(95)

\|u\|_{F^{\bar{s}}(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\bar{s},0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}

for any
\bar{s}\in[s,0)
.
T=T(s,\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}})
can be chosen to be decreasing in
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}}
.

Démonstration.

We choose
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{10}
and
\varepsilon>0
such that
s=-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\varepsilon
.
Note that by Theorem
9.1
we have global solutions emanating from
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

We invoke Lemma
2.6
, Proposition
6.1
, and Proposition
8.1
to find
\delta>0
such that the following set of estimates holds with frequency-dependent time localization given by
T=T(N)=N^{-\alpha}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}&\lesssim\|u\|_{E^{s}(T)}+\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)},\\
\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)}&\lesssim T^{\delta}\|u\|^{2}_{F^{s}(T)},\\
\|u\|^{2}_{E^{s}(T)}&\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+T^{\delta}\|u\|^{3}_{F^{s}(T)}.\end{array}\right.

Taking the estimates together gives

(96)

\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}+T^{\delta}\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}^{2}+T^{\delta}\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}^{3/2}.

By the limiting properties (see
[
18
]
) of the short-time function spaces it holds

\limsup_{T\downarrow 0}\|u\|_{E^{s}(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\text{ and }\lim_{T\downarrow 0}\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)}=0,

therefore we find

\limsup_{T\downarrow 0}\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

With this limiting property at hand, it is standard to argue by a continuity argument that (
96
) implies (
95
) for
\bar{s}=s
. To obtain the persistence of regularity, we consider the same time localization and invoke Lemma
2.6
, Proposition
6.1
, and Proposition
8.1
again to find the set of estimates:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|u\|_{F^{\bar{s}}(T)}&\lesssim\|u\|_{E^{\bar{s}}(T)}+\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{\bar{s}}(T)},\\
\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{\bar{s}}(T)}&\lesssim T^{\delta}\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}\|u\|_{F^{\bar{s}}(T)},\\

This can be subsumed as

\|u\|_{F^{\bar{s}}(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\bar{s},0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}+T^{\delta}\|u\|_{F^{\bar{s}}(T)}(\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}+\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}^{\frac{1}{2}}).

By the a priori estimates

\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}

already established, we find upon choosing
T=T(s,\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})})
smaller if necessary

The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 9.4

(Lipschitz continuous dependence in rough topology)

such that we have the following estimate on the difference of solutions

with

(97)

\|v\|_{F^{20s}(T)}\lesssim\|v(0)\|_{H^{20s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

can be chosen to be decreasing in
\|u_{0i}\|_{H^{s,0}}
,
i=1,2

Démonstration.

Choose
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{45}
and
\varepsilon>0
such that there

, and Proposition

Choosing

We turn to the final part in the argument: the conclusion of local well-posedness via frequency envelopes. We follow the presentation of Ifrim–Tataru
[
17
]
and shall be brief to avoid repetition.

We introduce the notation

\big[\frac{N}{M}\big]=\max\big(\frac{N}{M},\frac{M}{N}\big).

Definition 9.5

(Frequency envelopes in
H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
)
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. A sequence
(c_{N})_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}
is referred to as (sharp) frequency envelope for
u_{0}
, if it satisfies, for some
\delta>0

(a)

energy estimates
\|P_{N}u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leq c_{N}
,

(b)

the slowly varying property:
\frac{c_{N}}{c_{M}}\lesssim\big[\frac{N}{M}\big]^{\delta}
,

(c)

\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\approx\sum_{N\geq 1}c_{N}^{2}
.

Before we construct sharp frequency envelopes for given initial data, we derive a frequency envelope bound for solutions to truncated initial data. Suppose that
u_{0}^{H}=P_{\leq H}u_{0}
and that
(c_{N})
is a sharp frequency envelope of
u_{0}^{H}
. Then, the following bounds hold:

(i)

Uniform bounds:
\|P_{N}u_{0}^{H}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim c_{N}
,

(ii)

High frequency bounds:
\|u_{0}^{H}\|_{H^{s+j,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim H^{j}c_{H}
,

(iii)

Difference bounds:
\|u_{0}^{2H}-u_{0}^{H}\|_{H^{20s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim H^{19s}c_{H}
.

As a consequence of the a priori estimates from Theorem
9.3
and the estimates for solutions to the difference equation Theorem
9.4
, we have the following estimates for the global solutions
u^{H}
emanating from
u_{0}^{H}
on the time scale
T=T(s,\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})})
:

(i)

High frequency bounds:

(98)

\|u^{H}\|_{C_{T}H^{s+j,0}}\lesssim_{j}H^{j}c_{H},\quad j>0,

(ii)

Difference bounds:

(99)

\|u^{2H}-u^{H}\|_{C_{T}H^{20s,0}}\lesssim H^{19s}c_{H}.

We obtain as a consequence

(100)

\|P_{K}u^{H}\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}\lesssim_{N}c_{K}(K/H\vee 1)^{-N}.

By using the difference bounds (
99
) we show first that
u^{H}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C([0,T],H^{20s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
. Indeed, for
H^{\prime}>H
, we have

\|u^{H^{\prime}}-u^{H}\|_{C_{T}H^{20s,0}}\lesssim\sum_{H\leq M<H^{\prime}}\|u^{2M}-u^{M}\|_{C_{T}H^{20s,0}}\lesssim H^{19s}\big(\sum_{M\geq H}c_{M}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which implies convergence to a limiting solution
u\in C([0,T],H^{20s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
satisfying

\|u-u^{H}\|_{C_{T}H^{20s,0}}\lesssim H^{19s}\big(\sum_{M\geq H}c_{M}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Next, we upgrade this convergence to
C([0,T],H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
:

\displaystyle\|u-u^{H}\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}\leq\|P_{\leq H}(u-u^{H})\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}+\|P_{>H}(u-u^{H})\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}

Concerning the first term, we simply use the above bound to obtain

\|P_{\leq H}(u-u^{H})\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}\lesssim H^{-19s}\|u-u^{H}\|_{C_{T}H^{20s,0}}\lesssim\big(\sum_{M\geq H}c_{M}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We decompose the second term

\displaystyle\|P_{>H}(u-u^{H})\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{K>H}\|P_{K}(u-u^{H})\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then,
\|P_{K}(u-u^{H})\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}\leq I_{\leq K}+I_{>K}
for

\displaystyle I_{\leq K}=

\displaystyle\sum_{M\leq K}\|P_{K}(u^{2M}-u^{M})\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}},

\displaystyle I_{>K}=

\displaystyle\sum_{M>K}\|P_{K}(u^{2M}-u^{M})\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}

From (
100
) (with
N=1
) and the slowly varying property we obtain

I_{\leq K}\lesssim\sum_{M\leq K}\|P_{K}(u^{M})\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}\lesssim\sum_{M\leq K}\frac{M}{K}c_{M}\lesssim c_{K}.

From (
99
) and the slowly varying property we infer that

\displaystyle I_{>K}\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{M>K}K^{-19s}\|P_{K}(u^{2M}-u^{M})\|_{C_{T}H^{20s,0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{M>K}\Big(\frac{K}{M}\Big)^{-19s}c_{M}\lesssim c_{K}.

In summary, we obtain

(101)

\|u-u^{H}\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}\lesssim\big(\sum_{M\geq H}c_{M}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which tends to
0
as
H\to\infty
.

Consequently, we obtain an approximation of
u
with global solutions
u^{H}
. This is the key ingredient in the proof of continuous dependence, for which we consider next a sequence
u_{j,0}\to u_{0}
in
H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. The emanating solutions are denoted by
u_{j}
,
u
, respectively. The frequency truncations are denoted by
u_{j,0}^{H}
,
u_{0}^{H}
, respectively, and the emanating solutions of the frequency truncations by
u^{H}_{j}
and
u^{H}
.

We sketch the construction of a sharp
frequency envelope
(c_{N})
for
u_{0}
. As initial guess we consider
d_{N}=\|P_{N}u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}
. A frequency envelope is obtained from mollifying:
c_{N}=\sum_{M\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}d_{M}\big[\frac{M}{N}\big]^{-\delta}
, for some
0<\delta<19|s|
. By Young’s inequality it holds

\|(c_{N})\|_{\ell^{2}_{N}}\lesssim\|(d_{N})\|_{\ell^{2}}\sum_{M\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}M^{-\delta}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}},

therefore property c) is satisfied.
To check the slowly varying property, we note that

c_{N}=\sum_{J\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}d_{J}\Big[\frac{J}{N}\Big]^{-\delta}\leq\sum_{J\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}d_{J}\Big[\frac{J}{M}\Big]^{-\delta}\Big[\frac{M}{N}\Big]^{\delta}\lesssim c_{M}\big[\frac{M}{N}]^{\delta}.

Similarly, we construct a sharp frequency envelope
(c^{(j)}_{N})
for
u_{0,j}
such that we have
c^{(j)}\to c
in
\ell^{2}
.

We use the triangle inequality and (
101
) to write

\begin{split}\|u_{j}-u\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}&\leq\|u_{j}-u_{j}^{H}\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}+\|u_{j}^{H}-u^{H}\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}\\
&\quad+\|u^{H}-u\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}\\
&\lesssim\|c^{(j)}_{\geq H}\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|u_{j}^{H}-u^{H}\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}+\|c_{\geq H}\|_{\ell^{2}}.\end{split}

By Theorem
9.1
, we have as
j\to\infty

\|u_{j}^{H}-u^{H}\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}\leq\|u_{j}^{H}-u^{H}\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{j,0}^{H}-u_{0}^{H}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0.

Consequently,

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\|u_{j}-u\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}\lesssim\|c_{\geq H}\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Finally, letting
H\to\infty
, we infer

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|u_{j}-u\|_{C_{T}H^{s,0}}=0

and the proof is complete.
\hfill\Box

10.
Sharpness of Bourgain’s bilinear Strichartz estimate

In this section, we prove that Bourgain’s estimate provided in Proposition
4.1
is sharp in general. To this end, we construct
f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}
such that

\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\sim N_{2}^{\frac{1}{4}}\min(L_{1},L_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\max(L_{1},L_{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.

We use the same example as in
[
11
]
(which in turn goes back an unpublished manuscript of the third author).
Let
f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}
be a characteristic function, supported on a single
\xi
-node with
\xi_{i}=N_{i}
,
1\leq N_{2}\leq N_{1}
, and the
\eta
-support be intervals of length
N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then we can arrange that for
(\tau_{i},\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\in\operatorname{supp}(f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}})
:

\big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\big|\lesssim N_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Moreover, we let
\tau_{i}
range in intervals of length
\sim 1
, such that

\big|\tau_{i}-(\xi_{i}^{3}-\frac{\eta_{i}^{2}}{\xi_{i}})\big|\lesssim 1.

Clearly,

\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}\sim N_{2}^{\frac{1}{4}}.

And moreover, on a
\tau
-interval of length
1
, for
\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}
and for
\eta
in an interval of length
\sim N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}
we have

f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}(\tau,\xi,\eta)\sim N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}},

and the support contains an
\eta
-interval of length
\sim N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Consequently,

\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\gtrsim N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{4}}.

We obtain

\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\gtrsim N_{2}^{\frac{1}{4}}\min(L_{1},L_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\max(L_{1},L_{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}.

Based on this example, the same computation as in
[
11
]
for the cylinder
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
can be done in our setting, which shows
C^{3}
-ill-posedness of the Cauchy problem in
H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
for
s<-\frac{1}{4}
. Semilinear ill-posedness for
s\in(-\frac{1}{4},0)
remains open. As mentioned in Remark
5.1
, semilinear local well-posedness hinges on an improvement of Bourgain’s bilinear
L^{2}
-estimate.

Heat kernel for reflected diffusion and extension property on uniform domains
Mathav Murugan

Abstract
We study reflected diffusion on uniform domains where the underlying space admits a symmetric diffusion that satisfies sub-Gaussian heat kernel estimates. A celebrated theorem of Jones (Acta Math. 1981) states that uniform domains in Euclidean space are extension domains for Sobolev spaces. In this work, we obtain a similar extension property for metric spaces equipped with a Dirichlet form whose heat kernel satisfies a sub-Gaussian estimate. We introduce a scale-invariant version of this extension property and apply it to show that the reflected diffusion process on such a uniform domain inherits various properties from the ambient space, such as Harnack inequalities, cutoff energy inequality, and sub-Gaussian heat kernel bounds. In particular, our work extends Neumann heat kernel estimates of Gyrya and Saloff-Coste (Astérisque 2011) beyond the Gaussian space-time scaling. Furthermore, our estimates on the extension operator imply that the energy measure of the boundary of a uniform domain is always zero. This property of the energy measure is a broad generalization of Hino’s result (PTRF 2013) that proves the vanishing of the energy measure on the outer square boundary of the standard Sierpiński carpet equipped with the self-similar Dirichlet form.

Introduction

The goal of this work is obtaining heat kernel (transition probability) estimates for reflected diffusion on ‘nice domains’ when the underlying space admits a symmetric diffusion process with sub-Gaussian heat kernel estimates.
We wish to understand if the corresponding reflected diffusion on these domains also satisfy similar heat kernel estimates as the underlying space.
A more general guiding question is the following:
what properties are inherited by a domain from the ambient space?

Gaussian estimates of the heat kernel for symmetric diffusion have been known to hold in a broad range of settings including manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature

This Gaussian heat kernel estimate is congruous with the

;

The ‘nice domains’ we consider in this work are uniform domains.
Uniform domains were introduced independently by Martio and Sarvas

A novel feature of the work is to use

Given a space of functions

In our work, the relevant function spaces will be domain of Dirichlet forms. Our function spaces can be thought of as an abstraction of the

To explain the relationship between reflected diffusion and the extension problem, we recall the constructions of reflected Brownian motion on Euclidean domains. The different constructions using stochastic differential equation (SDE) and Dirichlet forms have their origins in the works of Skorokhod

and Fukushima

For a smooth domain

Given a smooth domain

{\mathcal{E}}_{U}(f,f):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{U}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\nabla f\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}(x)\,dx,

for all

denotes the subspace of functions in

If

As pointed out in

-Sobolev extension domain (that is, there is a bounded linear operator

. These two approaches lead to the

The Dirichlet form approach has the advantage that it also works when the domain is not smooth and more importantly when the ambient space is not smooth.

Gyrya and Saloff-Coste show that for any symmetric diffusion satisfying Gaussian heat kernel bounds, the reflected diffusion on any uniform domain (or more generally, inner uniform domains) also satisfies Gaussian heat kernel estimates
(
GyS
, Theorem 3.10)

(

is the domain of the Dirichlet form for the reflected diffusion on

Our main result answers these questions. As mentioned earlier, we use the extension result to obtain heat kernel bounds. Stated informally, our main results are:

(i)

For any symmetric diffusion satisfying sub-Gaussian heat kernel bounds, the corresponding reflected diffusion on any uniform domain also satisfies sub-Gaussian heat kernel estimates with the same space-time scaling (Theorem
2.8

(ii)

In the same setting as (i), there is a bounded linear
extension
map from the domain of the Dirichlet form for the reflected diffusion on a uniform domain to the domain of the Dirichlet form for the diffusion on the ambient space. This extension map is bounded
at all locations and scales
(Theorem
2.7

(iii)

In the same setting as (i), the energy measure of any function in the domain of the Dirichlet form on the boundary of any uniform domain is identically zero (Theorem
2.9
).

As mentioned above the results (i) and (ii) above can be viewed as analogues of

Since the domain of the Dirichlet form for reflected diffusion is the Sobolev space

To explain this connection to the extension problem, we recall that sub-Gaussian heat kernel estimate for symmetric diffusion can be characterized by the volume doubling property and functional inequalities such as the Poincaré inequality and cutoff Sobolev inequality. While the proof of Poincaré inequality for the reflected diffusion follows the same line of reasoning as

We use the extension property to obtain cutoff Sobolev inequality for the Dirichlet form corresponding to the reflected diffusion using the similar property in the larger ambient space (Proposition

In other words, we obtain a functional inequality in a domain using the corresponding inequality in the ambient space using the extension property. Since the cut-off Sobolev has a local and scale-invariant nature, we need to show certain scale-invariant estimates on the extension operator (cf. (

Although our motivation behind obtaining the extension property is to prove heat kernel estimates, there are several works over a long period of time on the extension property for its intrinsic interest and other applications; cf.

;

The cut-off Sobolev inequality obtained using scale-invariant bounds on the extension operator along Poincaré inequality for the reflected

Our construction of the extension operator is based on Whitney covers of the domain

\Omega_{o}\subset[\Omega]_{\epsilon},\quad\Omega_{i}^{c}\subset[\Omega^{c}]_{\epsilon},\quad\mbox{where $[A]_{\epsilon}$ denotes the $\epsilon$-neighborhood of $A$.}

Informally, every bounded domain can be
\epsilon
-approximated by uniform domains from outside and inside for any
\epsilon>0
.

2.3
Metric measure Dirichlet space and energy measure

Let
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
be a
symmetric Dirichlet form
on
L^{2}(X,m)
;
that is,
\mathcal{F}
is a dense linear subspace of
L^{2}(X,m)
, and

\mathcal{E}:\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{R}

is a non-negative definite symmetric bilinear form which is
closed

(
\mathcal{F}
is a Hilbert space under the inner product
\mathcal{E}_{1}:=\mathcal{E}+\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(X,m)}
)
and
Markovian
(
f^{+}\wedge 1\in\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{E}(f^{+}\wedge 1,f^{+}\wedge 1)\leq\mathcal{E}(f,f)
for any
f\in\mathcal{F}
).
Recall that
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
is called
regular
if

\mathcal{F}\cap C_{\mathrm{c}}(X)
is dense both in
(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}_{1})

and in
(C_{\mathrm{c}}(X),\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{sup}})
, and that

(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
is called
strongly local
if
\mathcal{E}(f,g)=0

for any
f,g\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mathop{{\rm supp}}_{m}[f]
,
\mathop{{\rm supp}}_{m}[g]
compact and

\mathop{{\rm supp}}_{m}[f-a\mathds{1}_{X}]\cap\mathop{{\rm supp}}_{m}[g]=\emptyset
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Here

C_{\mathrm{c}}(X)
denotes the space of
\mathbb{R}
-valued continuous functions on
X
with compact support, and
for a Borel measurable function
f:X\to[-\infty,\infty]
or an

m
-equivalence class
f
of such functions,
\mathop{{\rm supp}}_{m}[f]
denotes the support of the measure
|f|\,dm
,
i.e., the smallest closed subset
F
of
X
with
\int_{X\setminus F}|f|\,dm=0
,
which exists since
X
has a countable open base for its topology; note that

\mathop{{\rm supp}}_{m}[f]
coincides with the closure of
X\setminus f^{-1}(0)
in
X
if
f
is continuous.
The pair
(X,d,m,\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
of a metric measure space
(X,d,m)
and a strongly local,
regular symmetric Dirichlet form
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
on
L^{2}(X,m)
is termed
a
metric measure Dirichlet space
, or an
MMD space
in abbreviation. By Fukushima’s theorem about regular Dirichlet forms, the MMD space corresponds to a symmetric Markov processes on
X
with continuous sample paths
(
FOT
, Theorem 7.2.1 and 7.2.2)
.
We refer to
FOT
;
CF
for details of the theory of symmetric Dirichlet forms.

We recall the definition of energy measure.
Note that
fg\in\mathcal{F}

for any
f,g\in\mathcal{F}\cap L^{\infty}(X,m)
by
(
FOT
, Theorem 1.4.2-(ii))

and that
\{(-n)\vee(f\wedge n)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{F}
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}(-n)\vee(f\wedge n)=f
in norm in
(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}_{1})

by
(
FOT
, Theorem 1.4.2-(iii))
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(X,d,m,\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
be an MMD space.
The
energy measure

\Gamma(f,f)
of
f\in\mathcal{F}

associated with
(X,d,m,\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
is defined,
first for
f\in\mathcal{F}\cap L^{\infty}(X,m)
as the unique (
[0,\infty]
-valued)
Borel measure on
X
such that

\int_{X}g\,d\Gamma(f,f)=\mathcal{E}(f,fg)-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(f^{2},g)\qquad\textrm{for all $g\in\mathcal{F}\cap C_{\mathrm{c}}(X)$,}

(2.2)

and then by

\Gamma(f,f)(A):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Gamma\bigl((-n)\vee(f\wedge n),(-n)\vee(f\wedge n)\bigr)(A)

for each Borel subset
A
of
X
for general
f\in\mathcal{F}
.

Associated with a Dirichlet form is a
strongly continuous contraction semigroup

(P_{t})_{t>0}
; that is, a family of symmetric bounded linear operators
P_{t}:L^{2}(X,m)\to L^{2}(X,m)
such that

P_{t+s}f=P_{t}(P_{s}f),\quad\left\lVert P_{t}f\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2},\quad\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\left\lVert P_{t}f-f\right\rVert_{2}=0,

for all
t,s>0,f\in L^{2}(X,m)
. In this case, we can express
({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
in terms of the semigroup as

{\mathcal{F}}=\{f\in L^{2}(X,m):\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{t}\langle f-P_{t}f,f\rangle<\infty\},\quad{\mathcal{E}}(f,f)=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{t}\langle f-P_{t}f,f\rangle,

(2.3)

for all
f\in{\mathcal{F}}
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product in
L^{2}(X,m)

(
FOT
, Theorem 1.3.1 and Lemmas 1.3.3 and 1.3.4)
. It is known that
P_{t}
restricted to
L^{2}(X,m)\cap L^{\infty}(X,m)
extends to a linear contraction on
L^{\infty}(X,m)

(
CF
, pp. 5 and 6)
. If
P_{t}1=1
(
m
a.e.) for all
t>0
, we say that the corresponding Dirichlet form
({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
is
conservative
.

Definition 2.5

(Local Dirichlet space and its energy measure)
.

For an open set
U\subset X
of an MMD space
(X,d,m,{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
, we define the local Dirichlet space
\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{loc}}(U)
as

\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{loc}}(U):=\Biggl\{f\Biggm|\begin{minipage}[285.0pt]$f$ is an $m$-equivalence class of $\mathbb{R}$-valued Borel measurable functions
on $U$ such that $f\mathds{1}_{V}=f^{\#}\mathds{1}_{V}$ $m$-a.e.\ for some $f^{\#}\in\mathcal{F}$
for each relatively compact open subset $V$ of $U$
\end{minipage}\Biggr\}

(2.4)

and the energy measure
\Gamma_{U}(f,f)
of
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{loc}}(U)
associated with

(X,d,m,\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
is defined as the unique Borel measure on
U

such that
\Gamma_{U}(f,f)(A)=\Gamma(f^{\#},f^{\#})(A)
for any relatively compact
Borel subset
A
of
U
and any
V,f^{\#}
as in (
2.4
) with
A\subset V
;
note that
\Gamma(f^{\#},f^{\#})(A)
is independent of a particular choice of such
V,f^{\#}
. We define

\mathcal{F}(U):=\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{\operatorname{loc}}(U):\int_{U}f^{2}\,dm+\int_{U}\Gamma_{U}(f,f)<\infty\},

(2.5)

and the bilinear form
({\mathcal{E}}_{U},{\mathcal{F}}(U))
as

{\mathcal{E}}_{U}(f,f)=\int_{U}\Gamma_{U}(f,f),\quad\mbox{for all $f\in{\mathcal{F}}(U)$.}

(2.6)

The form
({\mathcal{E}}_{U},{\mathcal{F}}(U))
need not be a regular Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(\overline{U},m)
in general. A sufficient condition for
({\mathcal{E}}_{U},{\mathcal{F}}(U))
to be a regular Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(\overline{U},m)
is given in Lemma
4.2
. If
({\mathcal{E}}_{U},{\mathcal{F}}(U))
is a regular Dirichlet form on

reflected diffusion
on
\overline{U}
.

2.4
Sub-Gaussian heat kernel estimates

Let
\Psi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be a continuous increasing bijection of
(0,\infty)
onto itself, such that for all
0<r\leq R
,

C^{-1}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\beta_{1}}\leq\frac{\Psi(R)}{\Psi(r)}\leq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\beta_{2}},

(2.7)

for some constants
1<\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}
and
C>1
.
If necessary, we extend
\Psi
by setting
\Psi(\infty)=\infty
.
Such a function
\Psi
is said to be a
scale function
.
For
\Psi
satisfying (
2.7
), we define

\Phi(s)=\sup_{r>0}\left(\frac{s}{r}-\frac{1}{\Psi(r)}\right).

(2.8)

Definition 2.6

(
\operatorname{HKE(\Psi)}
)
.

Let
(X,d,m,\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
be an MMD space, and let
\left\{P_{t}\right\}_{t>0}

denote its associated Markov semigroup. A family
\left\{p_{t}\right\}_{t>0}
of non-negative
Borel measurable functions on
X\times X
is called the

heat kernel
of
(X,d,m,\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
, if
p_{t}
is the integral kernel
of the operator
P_{t}
for any
t>0
, that is, for any
t>0
and for any
f\in L^{2}(X,m)
,

P_{t}f(x)=\int_{X}p_{t}(x,y)f(y)\,dm(y)\qquad\mbox{for $m$-almost all $x\in X$.}

We say that
(X,d,m,\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
satisfies the
heat kernel estimates

\operatorname{HKE(\Psi)}
, if there exist
C_{1},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\delta\in(0,\infty)

and a heat kernel
\left\{p_{t}\right\}_{t>0}
such that for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{m\bigl(B(x,\Psi^{-1}(t))\bigr)}\exp\left(-c_{1}t\Phi\left(c_{2}\frac{d(x,y)}{t}\right)\right)\qquad\mbox{for $m$-a.e.~$x,y\in X$,}

(2.9)

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{3}}{m\bigl(B(x,\Psi^{-1}(t))\bigr)}\qquad\mbox{for $m$-a.e.~$x,y\in X$ with $d(x,y)\leq\delta\Psi^{-1}(t)$,}

(2.10)

where
\Phi
is as defined in (
2.8
).

2.5
Main results

We are now ready to state the main results. The setting is an MMD space that satisfies sub-Gaussian heat kernel bounds.
Our first result is the existence of a bounded extension operator. This operator is bounded globally (see (
2.13
), (
2.14
)) and also satisfies good
scale-invariant
bounds (see (
2.11
), (
2.12
))

Theorem 2.7

(Extension property)
.

Let
(X,d,m,{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
be an MMD space that satisfies the heat kernel estimate
\operatorname{HKE(\Psi)}
for some scale function
\Psi
and let
m
be a doubling measure. Let
U
be a uniform domain
U
and let
({\mathcal{E}}_{U},{\mathcal{F}}(U))
denote the bi-linear form in Definition
2.5
. There is a linear operator
E:{\mathcal{F}}(U)\to{\mathcal{F}}
such that the restriction of
E(f)
to

is
f
for all
f\in{\mathcal{F}}
(that is,
E
is an extension operator).
Furthermore, there exist
C,K\in(1,\infty),c\in(0,1)
such that for all
x\in\overline{U}
, and
f\in{\mathcal{F}}(U)
, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma(E(f),E(f))(B(x,r))

\displaystyle\leq C\Gamma_{U}(f,f)(B_{U}(x,Kr)),\quad\mbox{for all $0<r<c{\mathop{{\rm diam}}}(U)$;}

(2.11)

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,r)}{\left|\kern-1.07639ptE(f)\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{U}(x,Kr)}f^{2}\,dm\quad\mbox{for all $r>0$;}

(2.12)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}(E(f),E(f))

\displaystyle\leq C\left({\mathcal{E}}_{U}(f,f)+\frac{1}{\Psi({\mathop{{\rm diam}}}(U))}\int_{U}f^{2}\,dm\right);

(2.13)

\displaystyle\int_{X}{\left|\kern-1.07639ptEf\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{U}f^{2}\,dm.

(2.14)

Here
\Gamma,\Gamma_{U}
denote the energy measures of
({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
and
({\mathcal{E}}_{U},{\mathcal{F}}(U))
respectively.

In (
2.13
) above, we interpret
\frac{1}{\Psi(\infty)}=0
in case
{\mathop{{\rm diam}}}(U)=\infty
.

Our second main result is that the reflected diffusion on any uniform domain satisfies a sub-Gaussian heat kernel estimate and that the Dirichlet form approach defines a symmetric Markov process on
\overline{U}
.

Theorem 2.8

(Heat kernel estimate for reflected diffusion)
.

Let
(X,d,m,{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
be an MMD space that satisfies the heat kernel estimate
\operatorname{HKE(\Psi)}
for some scale function
\Psi
and let
m
be a doubling measure. Then for any uniform domain
U
, the bi-linear form
({\mathcal{E}}_{U},{\mathcal{F}}(U))
is a strongly-local regular Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(\overline{U},m)
. Moreover, the corresponding MMD space
(\overline{U},d,m,{\mathcal{E}}_{U},{\mathcal{F}}(U))
satisfies the heat kernel estimate
\operatorname{HKE(\Psi)}
.

Finally, we show that the energy measure of any function vanishes on the boundary of any uniform domain.

Theorem 2.9

(Energy measure of the boundary)
.

Let
(X,d,m,{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
be an MMD space that satisfies the heat kernel estimate
\operatorname{HKE(\Psi)}
for some scale function
\Psi
and let
m
be a doubling measure. Then for any uniform domain
U
and any
f\in{\mathcal{F}}
, we have

\Gamma(f,f)(\partial U)=0,

where
\Gamma(f,f)
denotes the corresponding energy measure.

We briefly mention a probabilistic consequence of the above property of the energy measure. Let
\mu
be a smooth measure whose quasi-support is
\partial U
. In

, Theorem
2.9
is used to show that the trace process corresponding to
\mu
on
\partial U
is a pure jump process by
(

, Theorems 5.2.2, 5.2.15, and Corollary 5.6.1)
. Hence Theorem

is a starting point to study jump process on

that is a trace of reflected diffusion on

. We obtain heat kernel estimates for the trace (jump) process on the boundary of reflected diffusion on uniform domains in
KM23+
.

3.1
Whitney covering

We recall the notion of a
\epsilon
-Whitney cover from
(
GyS
, Definition 3.16)
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)
and
U\subsetneq X
.
We say a collection of balls
\mathfrak{R}:=\left\{B_{U}(x_{i},r_{i}):x_{i}\in U,r_{i}>0,i\in I\right\}
is an
\epsilon
-Whitney cover if it satisfies the following properties:

(i)

The collection of sets
\{B_{U}(x_{i},r_{i}),i\in I\}
are pairwise disjoint.

(ii)

The radii
r_{i}
satisfy

r_{i}=\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\delta_{U}(x_{i}),\quad\mbox{for all $i\in I$.}

(iii)

\bigcup_{i\in I}B_{U}(x_{i},K_{\epsilon}r_{i})=U
, where
K_{\epsilon}=2(1+\epsilon)\in(2,3)
.

Note that since
K_{\epsilon}<3
by (iii), we have

\bigcup_{i\in I}B_{U}(x_{i},3r_{i})=U.

The existence of such a Whitney cover follows from Zorn’s lemma as we recall below. We also record a few elementary geometric properties of the Whitney cover for future use.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
U\subsetneq X
be a non-empty open set and
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)
.

(a)

There exists an
\epsilon
-Whitney cover
\mathfrak{R}=\left\{B_{U}(x_{i},r_{i}):x_{i}\in U,r_{i}>0,i\in I\right\}
of
U
such that the following properties hold.

(b)

(distance to boundary) For any
B_{U}(x_{i},r_{i})\in\mathfrak{R}
and for any
y\in B_{U}(x_{i},3r_{i})
, we have

\frac{1-2\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\delta_{U}(x_{i})<\delta_{U}(y)<\frac{1+4\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\delta_{U}(x_{i})

(3.1)

(c)

(radius comparison) For any
\lambda>1,\epsilon\in(1,1/2)
such that
(\lambda-1)\epsilon<1
, for any
\epsilon
-Whitney cover
\mathfrak{R}=\left\{B_{U}(x_{i},r_{i}):x_{i}\in U,r_{i}>0,i\in I\right\}
of
U
, and for any
i,j\in I
such that
B_{U}(x_{i},\lambda r_{i})\cap B_{U}(x_{j},\lambda r_{j})\neq\emptyset
, we have

\frac{1-(\lambda-1)\epsilon}{1+(\lambda+1)\epsilon}r_{j}\leq r_{i}\leq\frac{1+(\lambda+1)\epsilon}{1-(\lambda-1)\epsilon}r_{j}.

(3.2)

In particular, if
i,j\in I,i\neq j,B_{U}(x_{i},\lambda r_{i})\cap B_{U}(x_{j},\lambda r_{j})\neq\emptyset
implies

r_{i}\vee r_{j}\leq d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq\lambda\left(1+\frac{1+(\lambda+1)\epsilon}{1-(\lambda-1)\epsilon}\right)(r_{i}\wedge r_{j}).

(3.3)

(d)

(bounded overlap) Let
(X,d)
be a doubling metric space and let
\epsilon
-Whitney cover
\mathfrak{R}=\left\{B_{U}(x_{i},r_{i}):x_{i}\in U,r_{i}>0,i\in I\right\}
of
U\subsetneq X
. Then there exists a constant
C\in[1,\infty)
such that

\sum_{i\in I}\mathds{1}_{B_{U}(x_{i},r_{i}/\epsilon)}\leq C.

(e)

Let
(X,d)
is a doubling metric space and
\epsilon\in(0,1/5)
. Then for any
\epsilon
-Whitney cover
\mathfrak{R}
of
U
there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\#\{B_{U}(x_{i},r_{i})\in\mathfrak{R}:B_{U}(x_{i},6r_{i})\cap B_{U}(x,6r)\neq\emptyset\}\leq N\quad\mbox{for all $B_{U}(x,r)\in\mathfrak{R}$.}

Proof.

(a)

Let
\Omega
denote the partially ordered (by inclusion) set consisting of collection of balls
\{B_{U}(x_{i},r_{i}):x_{i}\in U,r_{i}>0,i\in I\}
that satisfies the conditions (i) and (ii) in Definition
3.1
.
If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a chain, then it easy to see that
\bigcup_{\mathfrak{A}\in{\mathcal{C}}}\mathfrak{A}\in\Omega
.
So by Zorn’s lemma, there exists a maximal element
\mathfrak{R}=\left\{B_{U}(x_{i},r_{i}):x_{i}\in U,r_{i}>0,i\in I\right\}\in\Omega
.

Clearly,
\mathfrak{R}
satisfies properties (i) and (ii) in Definition
3.1
. Next, we show that Definition
3.1
(iii) also holds.
Suppose to the contrary that (iii) does not hold, then there exists
y\notin U\setminus\bigcup_{i\in I}B_{U}(x_{i},K_{\epsilon}r_{i})
and hence

d(x_{i},y)\geq K_{\epsilon}r_{i}=\frac{K_{\epsilon}\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\delta_{U}(x_{i})\quad\mbox{for all $i\in I$.}

(3.4)

Since
\mathfrak{R}
is maximal, there exists
j\in I
such that
B(x_{j},r_{j})\cap B(y,\epsilon\delta_{U}(y)/(1+\epsilon))\neq\emptyset
. Therefore by the triangle inequality and Definition
3.1
(ii), we have

d(x_{j},y)<\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\left(\delta_{U}(x_{j})+\delta_{U}(y)\right).

(3.5)

By (
3.5
),

\delta_{U}(y)\leq d(y,x_{j})+\delta_{U}(x_{j})<\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\left(\delta_{U}(x_{j})+\delta_{U}(y)\right)+\delta_{U}(x_{j})

and hence

\delta_{U}(y)<(1+2\epsilon)\delta_{U}(x_{j}).

(3.6)

Combining (
3.4
) and (
3.5
),

\frac{K_{\epsilon}\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\delta_{U}(x_{j})\leq d(x_{j},y)<\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\left(\delta_{U}(x_{j})+\delta_{U}(y)\right)

which implies

(K_{\epsilon}-1)\delta_{U}(x_{j})<\delta_{U}(y)<(1+2\epsilon)\delta_{U}(x_{j}).

(3.7)

This yields the desired contradiction since
K_{\epsilon}=2(1+\epsilon)
.

(b)

Since
y\in B_{U}(x_{i},3r_{i})
, we have

\delta_{U}(y)<\delta_{U}(x_{i})+3r_{i}=\delta_{U}(x_{i})\left(1+\frac{3\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\right)

which implies the upper bound on
\delta_{U}(y)
.
For the lower bound, we use

\delta_{U}(x_{i})<\delta_{U}(y)+3r_{i}=\delta_{U}(y)+\frac{3\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\delta_{U}(x_{i}),\quad\delta_{U}(y)>\frac{1-2\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\delta_{U}(x_{i}).

(c)

If
B(x_{i},\lambda r_{i})\cap B(x_{j},\lambda r_{j})\neq\emptyset
, then by the triangle inequality

\displaystyle\delta_{U}(x_{j})\leq\delta_{U}(x_{i})+d(x_{i},x_{j})<\delta_{U}(x_{i})+\lambda\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}(\delta_{U}(x_{i})+\delta_{U}(x_{j}))

and hence
(1-(\lambda-1)\epsilon)\delta_{U}(x_{j})<(1+(\lambda+1)\epsilon)\delta_{U}(x_{j})
. This is equivalent to (
3.2
).
The lower bound on
d(x_{i},x_{j})
follows from
B(x_{i},r_{i})\cap B(x_{j},r_{j})=\emptyset
while the upper bound follows from
B(x_{i},\lambda r_{i})\cap B(x_{j},\lambda r_{j})\neq\emptyset
, the triangle inequality and (
3.2
).

(d)

Suppose that
y\in B_{U}(x_{i},r_{i}/\epsilon)
for some
i\in I
. By the triangle inequality,

\delta_{U}(x_{i})\leq d(x_{i},y)+\delta_{U}(y)<\frac{r_{i}}{\epsilon}+\delta_{U}(y)\leq\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}\delta_{U}(x_{i})+\delta_{U}(y).

Similarly,

\delta_{U}(y)<\delta_{U}(x_{i})+\frac{r_{i}}{\epsilon}=\delta_{U}(x_{i})\frac{2+\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}

Therefore

\frac{\epsilon}{2+\epsilon}\delta_{U}(y)<r_{i}<\delta_{U}(y)\quad\mbox{whenever $y\in B_{U}(x_{i},r_{i}/\epsilon),i\in I$.}

(3.8)

Therefore the set
A_{y}:=\{x_{i}:i\in I,y\in B(x_{i},r_{i}/\epsilon)\}
is contained in
B_{U}(y,\delta_{U}(y)/\epsilon)
and any two distinct points in
A_{y}
is separated by a distance of at least
{\epsilon}\delta_{U}(y)/(2+\epsilon)
. The desired conclusion follows from the metric doubling property.

(e)

This is an easy consequence of (d) and the metric doubling property. ∎

3.2
Basic properties of uniform domains

An important property of uniform domains is that is satisfies a corkscrew condition whose definition we recall below.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
V\subset X
. We say that
V
satisfies the
corkscrew condition
if there exists
\epsilon>0
such that for all
x\in\overline{V}
and
0<r\leq{\mathop{{\rm diam}}}(V)
, the set
B(x,r)\cap V
contains a ball of with radius
\epsilon r
.

Every uniform domains satisfies the corkscrew condition as we recall now.
The same argument presented in
(
BS
, Lemma 4.2)
for length uniform domains also shows that every
A
-uniform domain (with our weaker definition of uniform domains) also satisfies the corkscrew condition.

Lemma 3.4
.

(
BS
, Lemma 4.2)

Let
U\subsetneq X
be an
A
-uniform domain. For any
x\in\overline{U},r>0
such that
U\setminus B(x,r)\neq\emptyset
(in particular, if
r<{\mathop{{\rm diam}}}(U,d)/2
), there exists a ball
B(y,r/(3A))\subset U\cap B(x,r)
with radius
r/(3A)
.

The doubling property of a measure
m
is preserved under restriction to uniform domains. This is the content of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

(
BS
, Theorem 2.8)

Let
m
be a doubling measure on
X
and let
U\subsetneq X
be a non-empty uniform domain. Then

m(\partial U)=0

and

m
is doubling on
\overline{U}
and doubling on
U
.

Proof.

By the corkscrew condition (Lemma
3.4
) and
(
BS
, Theorem 2.8)
we have that
m
is doubling on
\overline{U}
and on
U
.

To see that
m(\partial U)=0
, note that by the corkscrew condition and the doubling property on
\overline{U}
we have

\limsup_{r\downarrow 0}\fint_{B(x,r)}\mathds{1}_{\partial U}(y)\,m(dy)\leq 1-\liminf_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{m(U\cap B(x,r))}{m(B(x,r))}<1,

for all
x\in\partial U
. By the Lebesgue differentiation theorem
(
Hei
, Theorem 1.8)
, we conclude that
m(\partial U)=0
.
∎

We recall some well known properties of doubling measures on a metric space
(X,d)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
m
be a doubling measure on

where
V\subset X
with doubling constant

as given in Definition

m(V\cap B(x,s))\leq D_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{d(x,y)+s}{r}\right)^{\alpha}m(V\cap B(y,r)),\quad\mbox{for all $x\in V,0<r<s<\infty$,}

where

If

satisfies the metric doubling; that is there exists

such that every ball

can be covered by at most

.

4
Sub-Gaussian heat kernel estimates

In this section, we recall some background material on Dirichlet forms, extension domains, and sub-Gaussian heat kernel estimates. We show that in a extension domain the reflected Dirichlet form is regular.

4.1
Extension domain

Note that we always have the inclusion

{\mathcal{F}}(U)\subseteq\{f\in L^{2}(U,m):\mbox{there exists $\widetilde{f}\in{\mathcal{F}}$ such that $f=\widetilde{f}$, $m$-a.e. on $U$}\}.

(4.1)

A natural question is if the above inclusion is an equality.
The following notion plays a central role in this work.

Definition 4.1

(Extension domain)
.

Let
(X,d,m,{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
be an MMD space and let
U\subset X
be open. We say that
U
is an
extension domain
for
(X,d,m,{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
, if there is a bounded linear map

such that

is an extension map; that is,
E(f)=f

m
-a.e. on

. Here the boundedness of

is with respect to the inner products
{\mathcal{E}}_{U}(\cdot,\cdot)+\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(U,m)},{\mathcal{E}}(\cdot,\cdot)+\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(X,m)}
on
{\mathcal{F}}(U)
and
{\mathcal{F}}
respectively.

Note that the inclusion (
4.1
) is an equality for any extension domain

.
An immediate consequence of the extension property is that the regular Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(X,m)
induces a regular Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(\overline{U},m)

By the correspondence between regular Dirichlet forms and symmetric Markov processes
(

, Theorem 7.2.1 and 7.2.2)
, this corresponds to a
m

(\overline{U},m)
which is called the

\overline{U}

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space such that all bounded sets are precompact.
Let
({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
be strongly local regular Dirichlet form. If

Proof.

We equip

with the corresponding inner products
{\mathcal{E}}_{U}(\cdot,\cdot)+\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(U,m)}
, and
{\mathcal{E}}(\cdot,\cdot)+\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(X,m)}

Let
E:{\mathcal{F}}(U)\to{\mathcal{F}}
be an extension operator as given in Definition
4.1
. If

is a Cauchy sequence in
{\mathcal{F}}(U)
, then

is a Cauchy sequence in
{\mathcal{F}}
(since

is a bounded operator). Since

is complete,

converges to a limit, say

In general, we note that the Dirichlet form

5.3

We recall an elementary lemma about bounded degree graphs that is used to obtain energy bounds on the extended function. A unweighted version of this lemma is contained in
(

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
G=(V_{G},E_{G})
be a bounded degree graph with
\deg(x)\leq M
for all
x\in V_{G}
. Let
d_{G}
denote the graph distance on
V_{G}
. Let

be such that

Then for any
L\geq 1,f:V_{G}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

\sum_{x\in V_{G}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in V_{G},\\
d_{G}(x,y)\leq L\end{subarray}}{\left|\kern-1.07639ptf(x)-f(y)\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}m(x)\leq LC^{L}M^{2(L+1)}\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in V_{G},\\
d_{G}(x,y)\leq 1\end{subarray}}{\left|\kern-1.07639ptf(x)-f(y)\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}m(x).

(5.15)

Proof.

For every ordered pair
(x,y)\in V_{G}\times V_{G}
such that
d_{G}(x,y)\leq L
, we pick a path
\gamma(x,y)
of vertices
x=x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}=y
such that
n=d_{G}(x,y)\leq L
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
5.14
), we have

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639ptf(x)-f(y)\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}m(x)

\displaystyle\leq d_{G}(x,y)\sum_{i=0}^{d_{G}(x,y)-1}{\left|\kern-1.07639ptf(x_{i})-f(x_{i+1})\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}m(x)

(5.16)

\displaystyle\leq LC^{L}\sum_{i=0}^{d_{G}(x,y)-1}{\left|\kern-1.07639ptf(x_{i})-f(x_{i+1})\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}m(x_{i}).

(5.17)

For each edge
(z_{1},z_{2})
the term
{\left|\kern-1.07639ptf(z_{i})-f(z_{i+1})\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}m(z_{i})
arises from at most

(for all

) pairs

such that

. By summing (
5.16
) over all such pairs

and using this observation, we obtain (

∎

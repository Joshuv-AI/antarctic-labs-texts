The b b -symbol weight hierarchy of the Kasami codes
Hongwei Zhu, Minjia Shi

Abstract
The symbol-pair read channel was first proposed by Cassuto and Blaum. Later, Yaakobi et al. generalized it to the b b -symbol read channel. It is motivated by the limitations of the reading process in high density data storage systems. One main task in b b -symbol coding theory is to determine the b b -symbol weight hierarchy of codes. In this paper, we study the b b -symbol weight hierarchy of the Kasami codes, which are well known for their applications to construct sequences with optimal correlation magnitudes. The complete symbol-pair weight distribution of the Kasami codes is determined.

Kasami codes,

-symbol metric,

Introduction

Let us introduce two metrics, which are two different generalizations of the Hamming metric. The first metric is the
b
-th generalized Hamming weight metric. It was proposed by Wei
[
27
]
to characterize the code’s performance on the wire-tap channel of type II. The
b
-th generalized Hamming weight metric is defined as follows.
Let
C
be an
[n,k]
linear code over a finite field. For any subcode
D\subset C
, the support of
D
is defined to be

\chi(D)=\{i:0\leq i\leq n-1|c_{i}\neq 0~\hbox{for some}~(c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in D\}.

The
b
-th generalized Hamming weight of a code
C
is the smallest support of a
b
-dimensional subcode of
C
with
1\leq b\leq k
.
We use
\mathbf{d}_{b}(C)
to denote the minimum
b
-th generalized Hamming distance of
C
.
When
b=1
,
\mathbf{d}_{1}(C)
is the minimum Hamming distance of
C
.
The set

\{\mathbf{d}_{b}(C)|1\leq b\leq k\}

is called the weight hierarchy of
C
. To distinguish it from the later definition, let us call it the generalized weight hierarchy in this paper.

The second metric is the
b
-symbol metric. Cassuto and Blaum
[
2
,
1
]
first proposed the concept of the symbol-pair channel in 2010. This new paradigm is motivated by the limitations of the reading process in high data storage systems.
Later, Yaakobi et. al
[
29
]
generalized the symbol-pair metric to
b
-symbol metric. The
b
-symbol metric is defined as follows.
Let
b
be a positive integer with
1\leq b\leq n
. Let
F
be a finite field and let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
and
\mathbf{y}=(y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1})
be two vectors which belong to
F^{n}
. The
b
-symbol weight of
\mathbf{x}
, denoted by
w_{b}(\mathbf{x})
, is the Hamming weight of
\pi_{b}(\mathbf{x})
, where
\pi_{b}(\mathbf{x})\in(F^{b})^{n}
and

\pi_{b}(\mathbf{x})=((x_{0},\ldots,x_{b-1}),(x_{1},\ldots,x_{b}),\ldots,(x_{n-1},\ldots,x_{b+n-2({\rm mod~}n)})).

w_{1}(\mathbf{x})
denotes the Hamming weight of
\mathbf{x}
.
The
b
-symbol distance of
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
, denoted by
d_{b}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
, is the
b
-symbol weight of
\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}
. For a code
C
,
d_{b}(C)
denotes the minimum
b
-symbol distance of
C
. When
b=1
,
d_{1}(C)
is also the minimum Hamming distance of
C
.
The
b
-symbol metric is also called symbol-pair metric if
b=2
.
The set

\{d_{b}(C)|1\leq b\leq n\}

is called the
b
-symbol weight hierarchy of
C
.
Note that
C
could be an unrestricted code under the
b
-symbol metric. If
C
is a cyclic code (or a constacyclic code), then the
b
-symbol weight hierarchy of
C
has the following property:

d_{1}(C)<d_{2}(C)<\cdots<d_{k-1}(C)<d_{k}(C)=d_{k+1}(C)=\cdots=d_{n}(C)=n.

The generalized Hamming weight hierarchy of
C
has a similar property, and
C
could not be cyclic. For the same cyclic code
C
, it’s minimum
b
-th generalized Hamming distance and minimum
b
-symbol distance have the following relationship.

Theorem 1
.

[
25
]

If
C
is cyclic, then
d_{b}(C)\geq\mathbf{d}_{b}(C)
with
1\leq b\leq k.

For more details about the connections between these two metrics we refer the reader to
[

b
-symbol weight hierarchy is, we elaborate on our motivation for studying the

Preliminaries

Let
m
be a positive integer and
q=2^{m}
. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote the finite field with
q

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
. Let the Frobenius trace from
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}

{\rm Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}/{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}x^{q^{i}}.

For the sake of convenience, we denote the trace functions from
\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}
by
{\rm T}_{k}.

Definition 3
.

Let
{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha,\beta)
with
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a vector of length
2^{2m}-1
, indexed by the elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}

{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha,\beta)=({\rm T}_{2m}(\alpha x)+{\rm T}_{m}(\beta x^{q+1}),x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}).

Then the Kasami code is a linear code with parameters
[2^{2m}-1,3m,2^{2m-1}-2^{m-1}]
defined by

C=\{{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha,\beta)|\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}},\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}.

Let
\theta
be a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}.
Let
x
run through

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
in the following order

\theta,\theta^{2},\theta^{3},\ldots,\theta^{q^{2}-1}.

(1)

Let
\eta=\theta^{q+1}
which is a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}.

Therefore,
{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha,\beta)
can be written as

({\rm T}_{2m}(\alpha\theta)+{\rm T}_{m}(\beta\eta),{\rm T}_{2m}(\alpha\theta^{2})+{\rm T}_{m}(\beta\eta^{2}),\ldots,{\rm T}_{2m}(\alpha\theta^{q^{2}-1})+{\rm T}_{m}(\beta\eta^{q^{2}-1})).

For a vector
\mathbf{x}=(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
, let
\tau(x)
denote the vector
(x_{n-1},x_{0},\ldots,x_{n-2})
obtained from
\mathbf{x}
by the cyclic shift of the coordinates
i\mapsto i+1{\rm~mod~}n.

The following result plays an indispensable role in the computation of Hamming weight distribution and generalized weight hierarchy of the Kasami codes. Of course, it is still very important to this paper.

Lemma 4
.

[
14
]

Let
S(\alpha,\beta)
be defined by

S(\alpha,\beta)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}}(-1)^{{\rm T}_{2m}(\alpha x)+{\rm T}_{m}(\beta x^{q+1})}.

Then

S(\alpha,\beta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}q^{2}-1,&\hbox{if $\beta=0$ and $\alpha=0$;}\\
-1,&\hbox{if $\beta=0$ and $\alpha\neq 0$;}\\
-q-1,&\hbox{if $\beta\neq 0$ and ${\rm T}_{m}(\frac{\alpha^{q+1}}{\beta})=0$;}\\
q-1,&\hbox{if $\beta\neq 0$ and ${\rm T}_{m}(\frac{\alpha^{q+1}}{\beta})=1$.}\end{array}\right.

An important property of trace functions is that
{\rm T}_{m}(\beta)
takes on each value in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
equally often, i.e.,

times, where

Lemma 9
.

Let
\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{m}\}
be a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Let

U_{i}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}|{\rm T}_{m}(x\beta_{i})=1\}

with

. Then

\left|\cap_{i\in\mathcal{M}}U_{i}\right|=2^{m-|\mathcal{M}|},

where
\mathcal{M}
is a subset of
\{1,2,\ldots,m\}.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{M}=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{t}\}.

The element
x
in
\cap_{i\in\mathcal{M}}U_{i}
is a solution of the system of equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm T}_{m}(x\beta_{i_{1}})=1;\\
{\rm T}_{m}(x\beta_{i_{2}})=1;\\
~~~~~~~~\vdots\\
{\rm T}_{m}(x\beta_{i_{t}})=1.\end{array}\right.

By
[
17

, the determinant
\det\left((\beta_{i}^{q^{j-1}})_{m\times m}\right)
is not zero if and only if
\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{m}\}
are linearly independent over

. Then the desired result follows.

\displaystyle-(b-m)\sum_{(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m-1}}S(\alpha\Theta_{m},\beta\Omega_{m})

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=m+1}^{2m-1}(b-j)\sum_{(u_{1},\ldots,u_{j-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{j-1}}S(\alpha\Theta_{j},\beta\Omega_{j})

\displaystyle-(b-2m)\sum_{(u_{1},\ldots,u_{2m-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m-1}}S(\alpha\Theta_{2m},\beta\Omega_{2m})

\displaystyle\left.-\sum_{j=2m+1}^{b-1}(b-j)\sum_{(u_{1},\ldots,u_{j-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{j-1}}S(\alpha\Theta_{j},\beta\Omega_{j})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{b}}\left[(2^{2m+1}-1)(2^{2m}-1)-bS(\alpha,\beta)-\sum_{i=10}^{14}\Delta_{i}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{2m}+2^{m}-1-\frac{1}{2^{b-2m}}-\frac{1}{2^{b}}\left(b(S(\alpha,\beta)+2^{m}+1)-2^{m+1}\sum_{j=1}^{b-1}(b-j)\#\mathcal{T}_{3}(j;\alpha,\beta)\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.4

The
b
-symbol weight enumerator of the Kasami code is determined if

.

Theorem 27
.

For
3m<b\leq n
, the
b
-symbol weight enumerator of the Kasami code
is

A(T)=1+(2^{3m}-1)T^{q^{2}-1}.

Proof.

Every nonzero codeword in a cyclic code is generated by a recursion of degree
k
and thus has at most
k-1
consecutive zeroes. Since the Kasami code is a cyclic code with dimension
k=3m
, there is no
b
consecutive zeroes. Therefore,
w_{b}({\mathbf{c}}(\alpha,\beta))=q^{2}-1
for any
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{(0,0)\}.

∎

Theorem 28
.

Let
{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha,\beta)
be a codeword of the Kasami code and let
d_{b}(C)
denote the minimal
b
-symbol distance of the Kasami code, where

.

•

If
\alpha=\beta=0
, then
w_{b}({\mathbf{c}}(\alpha,\beta))=0
for
1\leq b\leq n.

•

If
\alpha=0
and
\beta\neq 0
, then
w_{b}({\mathbf{c}}(\alpha,\beta))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(2^{b}-1)(2^{2m-b}+2^{m-b}),&\hbox{if $1\leq b\leq m$;}\\
n,&\hbox{if $m+1\leq b\leq n$.}\end{array}\right.

•

If
\alpha\neq 0
and
\beta=0
, then
w_{b}({\mathbf{c}}(\alpha,\beta))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(2^{b}-1)2^{2m-b},&\hbox{if $1\leq b\leq 2m$;}\\
n,&\hbox{if $2m+1\leq b\leq n$.}\end{array}\right.

•

If

and

n,&\hbox{if $3m+1\leq b\leq n$.}\end{array}\right.

Non-homogeneous fourth order elliptic inequalities with the convolution term
Zhe Yu

Abstract
We are concerned with the study of the twin non-local inequalities featuring non-homogeneous differential operators − Δ 2 ​ u + λ ​ Δ ​ u ≥ ( K α , β ∗ u p ) ​ u q in ​ ℝ N ​ ( N ≥ 1 ) , \displaystyle-\Delta^{2}u+\lambda\Delta u\geq(K_{\alpha,\beta}*u^{p})u^{q}\quad\text{ in }\mathbbm{R}^{N}(N\geq 1), and Δ 2 ​ u − λ ​ Δ ​ u ≥ ( K α , β ∗ u p ) ​ u q in ​ ℝ N ​ ( N ≥ 1 ) , \displaystyle\;\;\,\Delta^{2}u-\lambda\Delta u\geq(K_{\alpha,\beta}*u^{p})u^{q}\quad\text{ in }\mathbbm{R}^{N}(N\geq 1), with parameters λ , p , q > 0 \lambda,p,q>0 , 0 ≤ α ≤ N 0\leq\alpha\leq N and β > α − N \beta>\alpha-N . In the above inequalities the potential K α , β K_{\alpha,\beta} is given by K α , β ​ ( x ) = | x | − α ​ log β ⁡ ( 1 + | x | ) K_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=|x|^{-\alpha}\log^{\beta}(1+|x|) while K α , β ∗ u p K_{\alpha,\beta}*u^{p} denotes the standard convolution operator in ℝ N \mathbbm{R}^{N} . Introduction

This paper is concerned with the study of non-negative solutions to the following inequalities involving the bi-harmonic operator and with the convolution term:

-\Delta^{2}u+\lambda\Delta u\geq(K_{\alpha,\beta}*u^{p})u^{q}\quad\text{in }\;\;\mathbbm{R}^{N}(N\geq 1),

(
P^{-}
)

and

\;\;\,\Delta^{2}u-\lambda\Delta u\geq(K_{\alpha,\beta}*u^{p})u^{q}\quad\text{in }\;\;\mathbbm{R}^{N}(N\geq 1).

(
P^{+}
)

The above inequalities assume that
N\geq 1
,
\lambda,p,q>0
and

K_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}\log^{\beta}(1+\lvert x\rvert),\quad\text{where }0\leq\alpha\leq N,\,\beta>\alpha-N.

(1.1)

We note that

K_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbbm{R}^{N})
and

\displaystyle K_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\simeq|x|^{-\alpha+\beta}\mbox{ as }|x|\to 0,

\displaystyle K_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\simeq|x|^{-\alpha}\log^{\beta}|x|\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty.

The quantity
K_{\alpha,\beta}*u^{p}
represents the convolution operation in
\mathbbm{R}^{N}
given by

(K_{\alpha,\beta}\ast u^{p})(x)=\int_{\mathbbm{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{p}(y)\log^{\beta}(1+\lvert x-y\rvert)}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{\alpha}}dy\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbbm{R}^{N}.

This paper will examine classical non-negative solutions of (

P
-

) (respectively of (

P
+

)), that is, functions
u\in\mathcal{C}^{4}(\mathbbm{R}^{N})
such that

u(x)\geq 0\;,\;\;\big(K_{\alpha,\beta}*u^{p}\big)(x)<\infty\quad\mbox{for all }\;x\in\mathbbm{R}^{N},

(1.2)

and
u
satisfies (

P
-

) (respectively of (

P
+

)) pointwise in
\mathbbm{R}^{N}
.

For
\alpha\in(0,N)
and
\beta=0
the potential
K_{\alpha,0}
becomes the standard Riesz potential which plays a crucial role in potential theory and partial differential equations. Beyond this setting, the case
\alpha=N
is related to the exponential transform of a domain as introduced in
[
19
]
(see also
[
33
]
). Precisely, given a domain
D\subset\mathbbm{R}^{N}
, we set

E_{D}(x)=\exp\left\{-\frac{2}{\lvert\partial B_{1}\rvert}\int_{C}K_{N,0}(x-y)dy\right\}\text{ for all }x\in\mathbbm{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{D}.

By polarization, this definition is extended to points
x\in D
by

H_{D}(x)=\lim\limits_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{R^{2}E_{{B_{R}}\setminus D(x)}}.

We ascertain from
[
19
]
that
E_{\Omega}
is superharmonic in
\mathbbm{R}^{N}\setminus D
, while
H_{D}
is subharmonic in
D
. Also,
H_{D}
has explicit expression for common domains
D
whose boundary is described by quadratic polynomials. In particular, when
D
is the unit ball
B_{1}
in
\mathbbm{R}^{N}
, one has
H_{B_{1}}=(1-\lvert x\rvert^{2})^{-1}
. The exponential transform
E_{D}
was used in
[
20
]
to study the regularity of free boundaries in two dimensions. Furthermore, potentials of type
K_{N,\beta}
are related to logarithmic Besov spaces
[
4
,
25
]
.

The study of various non-homogeneous higher order differential operators has attracted a significant attention in recent years. Results involving the operator
\Delta^{2}+\Delta
can be found in
[
8
,
24
,
27
]
. In the present work, we study a class of differential inequalities involving the operators
-\Delta^{2}+\lambda\Delta
and
\Delta^{2}-\lambda\Delta
,
\lambda>0
. The operator
\Delta^{2}u-\lambda\Delta u
appears in Mechanics, in the study of suspension bridges in the early 20th century. In dimension
N=1
, we have

\Delta^{2}u-\lambda\Delta u=u_{xxxx}-\lambda u_{xx},

and the suspension bridges equation reads

u_{xxxx}-(H+h(u))u_{xx}+\frac{q}{H}h(u)=p(x),\quad\text{where }0\leq x\leq L.

(1.3)

In above equation,
L>0
is the length of the beam,
q
and
p(x)
are the dead and live loads per unit length applied to the beam, and the non-local term
h(u)
is the additional tension in the cable produced by the live load
p
. The equation (
1.3
) is called the Melan equation
[
26
]
in suspension bridges theory introduced in 1913 (see also
[
2
,
9
,
15
]
for further results on this topic as well as
[
1
,
14
]
for suspension bridge equation with non-local terms). In 1980s, the authors demonstrated that the nonlinear feature of such non-homogeneous operator provided a good fit in the study of travelling waves in suspension bridges in
[
22
]
and
[
28
]
. Later, in
[
23
,
29
]
, the problem with a more general nonlinear non-negative term
f(x,u)
, such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta^{2}u-\lambda\Delta u=f(x,u)&\quad\text{in }&\Omega,\\
&\Delta u=0,u=0&\quad\text{on }&\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

was investigated in the case where
\Omega
is smooth and bounded. Ground state solutions of the above equation were discussed in the recent work
[
13
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbbm{R}^{N})
,
f\geq 0
and let
K_{\alpha,\beta}
be given by (
1.1
).

(i)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

(K_{\alpha,\beta}*f)(x)\geq C\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}\log^{\beta}(1+\lvert x\rvert)\quad\text{in }\mathbbm{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}.

(2.1)

Furthermore, if
f(x)\geq c\lvert x\rvert^{-N}
in
\mathbbm{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}
for some
c>0
and
\beta\leq 0
, then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(K_{\alpha,\beta}*f)(x)\geq C|x|^{-\alpha}\log^{1+\beta}(1+\lvert x\rvert)\quad\text{in }\mathbbm{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}.

(2.2)

(ii)

Suppose

f(x)\geq c\lvert x\rvert^{-\sigma}\log^{\kappa}(1+\lvert x\rvert)\quad\text{in }\mathbbm{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}\quad\text{for some }c>0,\kappa\in\mathbbm{R}\text{ and }\sigma\geq 0.

Then, for all
x\in\mathbbm{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}
one has:

\begin{split}(K_{\alpha,\beta}*f)(x)=\infty\quad&\text{if }\;\;N-\alpha-\sigma>0,\\
&\text{or }\;N-\alpha-\sigma=0,\quad 1+\beta+\kappa\geq 0.\end{split}

Furthermore, there exists a constant
C>0
, such that for all
x\in\mathbbm{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}
one has:

(K_{\alpha,\beta}*f)(x)\geq C\left\{\begin{aligned} &\lvert x\rvert^{N-\alpha-\sigma}\log^{\beta+\kappa}(1+\lvert x\rvert)&\text{if }N-\alpha-\sigma<0,&\\[7.22743pt]
&\log^{1+\beta+\kappa}(1+\lvert x\rvert)&\text{if }N-\alpha-\sigma=0,&\quad 1+\beta+\kappa<0.\end{aligned}\right.

(iii)

Suppose

f(x)\leq c(A+\lvert x\rvert)^{-\sigma}\log^{\kappa}(A+\lvert x\rvert)\quad\text{in }\mathbbm{R}^{N},

for some
c>0
,
\sigma\geq N-\alpha
,
A>1
and
\kappa\in\mathbbm{R}
.
Then, there exists
C=C(N,\alpha,\beta,\sigma,\kappa,A)>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbbm{R}^{N}
one has following estimates:

If
\alpha<N
, then

(K_{\alpha,\beta}*f)(x)\leq C\begin{cases}\log^{1+\beta+\kappa}(A+|x|)&\text{if }\sigma=N-\alpha,\,1+\beta+\kappa<0,\\[5.69046pt]
(A+\lvert x\rvert)^{N-\alpha-\sigma}\log^{\beta+\kappa}(A+\lvert x\rvert)&\text{if }N-\alpha<\sigma<N,\\[5.69046pt]
(A+\lvert x\rvert)^{-\alpha}\log^{\beta}(A+\lvert x\rvert)&\text{if }\sigma>N\text{ or }\sigma=N,\,\kappa<-1,\\[5.69046pt]
(A+\lvert x\rvert)^{-\alpha}\log^{1+\beta+\kappa}(A+\lvert x\rvert)&\text{if }\sigma=N,\,\kappa>-1,\\[5.69046pt]
(A+|x|)^{-\alpha}\log^{\beta}(A+|x|)\log(\log(e+|x|))&\text{if }\sigma=N,\,\kappa=-1.\\[5.69046pt]
\end{cases}

If
\alpha=N
, then

(K_{\alpha,\beta}*f)(x)\leq C\begin{cases}(A+|x|)^{-\sigma}\log^{1+\beta+\kappa}(A+\lvert x\rvert)&\text{if }0<\sigma<N,\\[5.69046pt]
(A+|x|)^{-N}\log^{1+\beta+\kappa}(A+\lvert x\rvert)&\text{if }\sigma=N,\,\kappa>-1\\[5.69046pt]
(A+|x|)^{-N}\log^{\beta}(A+|x|)&\text{if }\sigma>N\text{ or }\sigma=N,\,\kappa<-1,\\[5.69046pt]
(A+|x|)^{-N}\log^{\beta}(A+|x|)\log(\log(e+|x|))&\text{if }\sigma=N,\,\kappa=-1.\\[5.69046pt]
\end{cases}

Proof.

(i) Let
x\in\mathbbm{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}
. Then, for all
y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}
one has

\frac{|x|}{2}\leq|x|-|y|\leq|x-y|\leq|x|+|y|\leq\frac{3}{2}|x|.

Thus

K_{\alpha,\beta}(x-y)\geq c|x|^{-\alpha}\log^{\beta}(1+|x|)\quad\text{for some }c>0.

It follows that

\displaystyle(K_{\alpha,\beta}*f)(x)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|y|\leq\frac{1}{2}}K_{\alpha,\beta}(x-y)f(y)dy

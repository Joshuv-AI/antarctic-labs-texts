Pre-foliations of co-degree one on ℙ ℂ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}} with a flat Legendre transform
Samir Bedrouni

Abstract
A holomorphic pre-foliation ℱ = ℓ ⊠ ℱ \mathscr{F}=\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{F} of co-degree 1 1 and degree d d on ℙ ℂ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}} is the data of a line ℓ \ell of ℙ ℂ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}} and a holomorphic foliation ℱ \mathcal{F} on ℙ ℂ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}} of degree d − 1 . d-1. We study pre-foliations of co-degree 1 1 on ℙ ℂ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}} with a flat Legendre transform (dual web). After having established some general results on the flatness of the dual d d -web of a homogeneous pre-foliation of co-degree 1 1 and degree d d , we describe some explicit examples and we show that up to automorphism of ℙ ℂ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}} there are two families and six examples of homogeneous pre-foliations of co-degree 1 1 and degree 3 3 on ℙ ℂ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}} with a flat dual web. This allows us to prove an analogue for pre-foliations of co-degree 1 1 and degree 3 3 of a result, obtained in collaboration with D. Marín, on foliations of degree 3 3 with non-degenerate singularities and a flat Legendre transform. We also show that the dual web of a reduced convex pre-foliation of co-degree 1 1 on ℙ ℂ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}} is flat. This is an analogue of a result on foliations of ℙ ℂ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}} due to D. Marín and J. V. Pereira. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification. — 14C21, 32S65, 53A60.

In this section, we briefly recall the definitions of the fundamental form and the curvature of a

Let us first assume that

For

Following

\eta_{rst}=\eta(\mathcal{F}_{r}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{s}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{t})
as the unique meromorphic

(1.1)

{\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}\mathrm{d}(\delta_{st}\,\omega_{r})&=&\eta_{rst}\wedge\delta_{st}\,\omega_{r}\\
\mathrm{d}(\delta_{tr}\,\omega_{s})&=&\eta_{rst}\wedge\delta_{tr}\,\omega_{s}\\
\mathrm{d}(\delta_{rs}\,\omega_{t})&=&\eta_{rst}\wedge\delta_{rs}\,\omega_{t}\end{array}\right.}

One calls

(1.2)

\hskip 19.91692pt\eta(\mathcal{W})=\eta(\mathcal{F}_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{d})=\sum_{1\leq r<s<t\leq d}\eta_{rst}.

One can easily check that

is a meromorphic

-form with poles along the discriminant

of

and that it is well-defined up to addition of a closed logarithmic

-form

Now, if

is a (not necessarily completely decomposable)

The

\displaystyle K(\mathcal{W})=\mathrm{d}\,\eta(\mathcal{W}).

\check{\Sigma}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\ell}
denotes the set of lines dual to the points of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{F}}^{\ell}:=\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{F}\cap\ell.

2.
If the line
\ell
is not invariant by
\mathcal{F}
, then

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{F})=\Delta(\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F})\cup\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F}}(\ell)\cup\check{\Sigma}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\ell}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{F})=\Delta(\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F})\cup\mathrm{Tang}(\mathrm{Leg}\ell,\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F}).

When
\ell
is not invariant by
\mathcal{F},
we obtain by an argument of
[
1

that

\mathrm{Tang}(\mathrm{Leg}\ell,\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F})=\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F}}(\ell)\cup\check{\Sigma}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\ell}.

Let us assume that
\ell
is invariant by
\mathcal{F}
and show that
\mathrm{Tang}(\mathrm{Leg}\ell,\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F})=\check{\Sigma}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\ell}.
Let
s\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{F}}^{\ell}.
The fact that
s\in\ell
(resp.
s\in\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{F}
) implies that the line
\check{s}
dual to
s
is invariant by
\mathrm{Leg}\ell
(resp. by
\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F}
). Thus
\check{s}\subset\mathrm{Tang}(\mathrm{Leg}\ell,\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F})
, hence
\check{\Sigma}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\ell}\subset\mathrm{Tang}(\mathrm{Leg}\ell,\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F}).
Conversely, let
\mathcal{C}
be an irreducible component of
\mathrm{Tang}(\mathrm{Leg}\ell,\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F}).
Let us show that
\mathcal{C}
is invariant by
\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F}.
Assume by contradiction that
\mathcal{C}
is transverse to
\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F}.
Let
m
be a generic point of
\mathcal{C}.
Denote by
\check{\ell}\in\mathbb{\check{P}}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
the dual point of
\ell
; then the line
(\check{\ell}m)
is not invariant by
\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F}
and is tangent to
\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F}
at
m.
Since
\ell
is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant, the point
\check{\ell}
is singular for
\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F}
; it is therefore also a tangency point between
\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F}
and
(\check{\ell}m).
The number of tangency points between
\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F}
and
(\check{\ell}m)
is then
\geq 2
, which contradicts the equality
\deg(\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F})=1.
Hence the invariance of
\mathcal{C}
by
\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{F}
is proved. Then
\mathcal{C}
is also invariant by
\mathrm{Leg}\ell
and is therefore a line passing through
\check{\ell}.
There therefore exists
s\in\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathcal{C}=\check{s}
; since
\check{\ell}\in\mathcal{C}
, we have
s\in\ell
and therefore
s\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{F}}^{\ell}.
Consequently,
\mathcal{C}\subset\check{\Sigma}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\ell}.

∎

We will now apply the above lemma to the case of a homogeneous pre-foliation
\mathscr{H}=\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}
of co-degree

\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}.
If

If
\ell=L_{\infty}
then
\ell
is invariant by
\mathcal{H}
and Lemma
2.1
ensures that

\Delta(\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{H})=\Delta(\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{H})\cup\check{\Sigma}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\infty},

where
\check{\Sigma}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\infty}
denotes the set of lines dual to the points of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}^{\infty}:=\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{H}\cap L_{\infty}.

Assume that
\ell
passes through the origin. If
\ell
is not invariant by
\mathcal{H}
, then, according to
[

, we have

Since the line

If
\ell

.

3
Flatness of the dual web of a co-degree one homogeneous pre-foliation

Our first result shows that, for a homogeneous foliation
\mathcal{H}
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}},
the webs
\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{H}
and
\mathrm{Leg}(L_{\infty}\boxtimes\mathcal{H})
have the same curvature, so that we have equivalence between the flatness of
\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{H}
and that of
\mathrm{Leg}(L_{\infty}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
d\geq 3
be an integer and let
\mathcal{H}
be a homogeneous foliation of degree
d-1
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}.
Then

K(\mathrm{Leg}(L_{\infty}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}))=K(\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{H}).

In particular, the
d
-web
\mathrm{Leg}(L_{\infty}\boxtimes\mathcal{H})
is flat if and only if the
(d-1)
-web
\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{H}
is flat.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a homogeneous foliation of degree
2
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}.
Then the
3
-web
\mathrm{Leg}(L_{\infty}\boxtimes\mathcal{H})
is flat.

To establish Theorem
3.1
, we will need the following definition and theorem.

Definition 3.3

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{d}
be a regular
d
-web on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0).
A
transverse symmetry
of
\mathcal{W}
is a germ of vector field
\mathrm{X}
which is transverse to the foliations
\mathcal{F}_{i}
(
i=1,\ldots,d
) and whose local flow
\exp(t\mathrm{X})
preserves the
\mathcal{F}_{i}
’s.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
d\geq 3
be an integer and let
\mathcal{W}_{d-1}
be a regular
(d-1)
-web on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
which admits a transverse symmetry
\mathrm{X}.
Denote by
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{X}}
the foliation defined by
\mathrm{X}.
Then

K(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{X}}\boxtimes\mathcal{W}_{d-1})=K(\mathcal{W}_{d-1}).

In particular, the
d
-web
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{X}}\boxtimes\mathcal{W}_{d-1}
is flat if and only if the
(d-1)
-web
\mathcal{W}_{d-1}
is flat.

Before proving this theorem, let us briefly recall the definition of the rank
\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{W})
of a regular
d
-web
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{d}
on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0).
For
1\leq i\leq d,
let
\omega_{i}
be a
1
-form defining the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{i}.
One defines the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{W})
of
abelian relations
of
\mathcal{W}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{W}):=\Big\{(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{d})\in(\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2},0))^{d}\hskip 2.84526pt\Big|\hskip 2.84526pt\forall i=1,\ldots,d,\hskip 2.84526pt\mathrm{d}\eta_{i}=0,\hskip 2.84526pt\eta_{i}\wedge\omega_{i}=0\hskip 2.84526pt\text{ and }\hskip 2.84526pt\sum_{i=1}^{d}\eta_{i}=0\Big\}.

Then
\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{W}):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{W})
. One has the following optimal bound (
cf.

[
16
, Chapter 2]
):

\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{W})\leq\pi_{d}:=\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}.

Recall also that every
d
-web of maximal rank (
i.e.
of rank
\pi_{d}
) is necessarily flat by
Mihăileanu
’s criterion (
cf.

[
16
, Theorem 6.3.4]
).

In fact, Theorem
3.4
is an analogue for flat webs of a result on webs of maximal rank, due to
Marín
-
Pereira
-
Pirio
, namely:

Theorem 3.5

(
[
12
]
, Theorem 1)
.

With the notations of Theorem
3.4
, one has

\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{X}}\boxtimes\mathcal{W}_{d-1})=\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{W}_{d-1})+(d-2).

In particular,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{X}}\boxtimes\mathcal{W}_{d-1}
is of maximal rank if and only if
\mathcal{W}_{d-1}
is of maximal rank.

The proof of Theorem
3.4
consists essentially in applying this result for
d=3.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Writing
\mathcal{W}_{d-1}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{d-1}
, we have

\displaystyle K(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{X}}\boxtimes\mathcal{W}_{d-1})=K(\mathcal{W}_{d-1})+\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq d-1}K(\mathcal{W}_{3}^{i,j}),

where
\mathcal{W}_{3}^{i,j}:=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{X}}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{i}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{j}.
Moreover, since
\mathrm{X}
is a transverse symmetry of the
2
-web
\mathcal{F}_{i}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{j}
and since every
2
-web is of maximal rank, equal to
0
, Theorem 1 of
[
12
]
(
cf.
Theorem
3.5
above) implies that the
3
-web
\mathcal{W}_{3}^{i,j}
is of maximal rank, equal to
1,
so that
K(\mathcal{W}_{3}^{i,j})\equiv 0
, hence the announced equality holds.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By
[
4
, Section 2]
, we can locally decompose the
d
-web
\mathrm{Leg}(L_{\infty}\boxtimes\mathcal{H})
as

\mathrm{Leg}(L_{\infty}\boxtimes\mathcal{H})=\mathrm{Leg}(L_{\infty})\boxtimes\mathcal{W}_{d-1},

where
\mathcal{W}_{d-1}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{d-1}
and, for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\},

\mathcal{F}_{i}
is given by
\check{\omega}_{i}:=\lambda_{i}(p)\mathrm{d}q-q\mathrm{d}p
, with
\lambda_{i}(p)=p-p_{i}(p)
and
\{p_{i}(p)\}={\mspace{2mu}\underline{\mspace{-2mu}\mathcal{G}\mspace{-2mu}}\mspace{2mu}}_{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}(p).
Now, the vector field
\mathrm{X}:=q\frac{\partial}{\partial q}
defines the radial foliation
\mathrm{Leg}(L_{\infty})
and is a transverse symmetry of the web
\mathcal{W}_{d-1}.
Therefore,
K(\mathrm{Leg}(L_{\infty}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}))=K(\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{H})
by Theorem
3.4
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

We can also prove Theorem
3.1
directly, without using results on webs of maximal rank. Indeed, putting
\mathcal{W}_{3}^{i,j}:=\mathrm{Leg}(L_{\infty})\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{i}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{j}
, for all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
with
i\neq j,
we have

\displaystyle K(\mathrm{Leg}(L_{\infty}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}))=K(\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{H})+\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq d-1}K(\mathcal{W}_{3}^{i,j}).

The foliation
\mathrm{Leg}(L_{\infty})
being defined by
\check{\omega}_{0}:=\mathrm{d}p,
a direct computation using formula (
1.1
) shows that

\displaystyle\eta(\mathcal{W}_{3}^{i,j})=\frac{\mathrm{d}\Big((\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j})(p)\Big)}{(\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j})(p)}+\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{q},

so that
K(\mathcal{W}_{3}^{i,j})=\mathrm{d}\eta(\mathcal{W}_{3}^{i,j})\equiv 0,
hence
K(\mathrm{Leg}(L_{\infty}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}))=K(\mathrm{Leg}\mathcal{H}).

The following theorem gives an important characterization of the flatness of the dual web of a co-degree one homogeneous pre-foliation.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\mathscr{H}=\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}
be a homogeneous pre-foliation of type
(1,d)
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
with
d\geq 3.
If the line
\ell
is invariant (resp. not invariant) by
\mathcal{H}
, then the
d
-web
\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{H}
is flat if and only if its curvature
K(\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{H})
is holomorphic on
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\hskip 0.56905pt\mathrm{tr}})
(resp. on
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\hskip 0.56905pt\mathrm{tr}})\cup D_{\ell}=\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\hskip 0.56905pt\mathrm{tr}}\cup\ell)
).

To prove this theorem, we will need the following lemma, which is a reformulation of Lemma 3.1 of
[
2
]
in terms of homogeneous pre-foliations.

Lemma 3.8

(
[
2
]
, Lemma 3.1)
.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be a homogeneous pre-foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}.
If the curvature of
\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{H}
is holomorphic on
\mathbb{\check{P}}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}\hskip-0.85358pt\setminus\hskip-0.85358pt\check{O},
then
\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{H}
is flat.

We will also need the following proposition, which has its own interest.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{W}_{\nu}
be a germ of
\nu
-web on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
with
\nu\geq 2.
Assume that
\Delta(\mathcal{W}_{\nu})
has an irreducible component

totally invariant by
\mathcal{W}_{\nu}
and of minimal multiplicity
\nu-1.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a germ of foliation on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
leaving
C
invariant and let
\mathcal{W}_{d-\nu-1}
be a germ of regular
(d-\nu-1)
-web on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)

Then the curvature of the
d
-web

Proof.

As in the beginning of the proof of
[

\mathrm{T}\mathcal{F}|_{U}=\{\mathrm{d}y+yh(x,y)\mathrm{d}x=0\},

\Small

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}\mathcal{W}_{\nu}|_{U}=\left\{\mathrm{d}y^{\nu}+y\Big(a_{\nu-1}(x,y)\mathrm{d}y^{\nu-1}\mathrm{d}x+\cdots+a_{0}(x,y)\mathrm{d}x^{\nu}\Big)=0\right\}

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}\mathcal{W}_{d-\nu-1}|_{U}=\left\{\prod\limits_{l=1}^{d-\nu-1}(\mathrm{d}x+g_{l}(x,y)\mathrm{d}y)=0\right\}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle K(\pi^{*}\mathcal{W})=K\big(\pi^{*}(\mathcal{W}_{\nu}\boxtimes\mathcal{W}_{d-\nu-1})\big)+\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq d-1}K(\mathcal{F}_{0}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{i}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{j}).

[

ensures that

\{y=0\}

On the other hand, since

is invariant by
\mathcal{F}_{0}
and

[

As a consequence

is holomorphic along
C
.

\mathcal{H}
has a transverse inflection line
\ell=(\alpha\,x+\beta\,y=0)
of maximal order
d-2
. Put
\mathscr{H}:=\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}.
Then the curvature of
\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{H}
is holomorphic along
D_{\ell}
if and only if the
2
-form
\mathrm{d}\omega
vanishes on the line
\ell.

Remark 3.27
.

When
d\geq 4
the condition
\scriptscriptstyle\langle\!\langle

\mathrm{d}\omega

\ell

\!\scriptscriptstyle\,\rangle\!\rangle
also expresses the holomorphy of the curvature of

. Thus Corollary

of

4
Flatness and homogeneous pre-foliations
\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}
of co-degree
1
such that
\deg\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}}=2

In this section we propose to classify, up to automorphism of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
, all homogeneous pre-foliations
\mathscr{H}=\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}
of co-degree
1
and degree
d\geq 3
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\deg\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}}=2
and the
d
-web
\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{H}
is flat. The equality
\deg\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}}=2
holds if and only if the type
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}}
of
\mathcal{H}
is of one of the following three forms:
2\cdot\mathrm{R}_{d-2}
,
2\cdot\mathrm{T}_{d-2}
,
1\cdot\mathrm{R}_{d-2}+1\cdot\mathrm{T}_{d-2}.
According to
[
4
, Proposition 4.1]
, every homogeneous foliation of type
2\cdot\mathrm{R}_{d-2}
is linearly conjugate to the convex foliation
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{d-1}
defined by the
1
-form

\omega_{1}^{d-1}=y^{d-1}\mathrm{d}x-x^{d-1}\mathrm{d}y.

The homogeneous foliations of type
2\cdot\mathrm{T}_{d-2}
, resp.
1\cdot\mathrm{R}_{d-2}+1\cdot\mathrm{T}_{d-2},
are given, up to linear conjugation, by

\displaystyle\omega_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)=(x^{d-1}+\lambda\,y^{d-1})\mathrm{d}x+(\mu\,x^{d-1}-y^{d-1})\mathrm{d}y,\quad\text{where}\hskip 2.84526pt\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{C},\text{with}\hskip 2.84526pt\lambda\mu\neq-1,

resp.

\displaystyle\omega_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)=(x^{d-1}+\lambda y^{d-1})\mathrm{d}x+x^{d-1}\mathrm{d}y,\quad\text{where}\hskip 2.84526pt\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*},

cf.
proof of
[
4
, Proposition 4.1]
. We will denote by
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)
, resp.
\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)
, the foliation defined by
\omega_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)
, resp.
\omega_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda).

In the following three lemmas,
\ell
denotes a line of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
such that
O\in\ell
or
\ell=L_{\infty}.

Lemma 4.1
.

The
d
-web
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{1}^{d-1})
is flat if and only if, up to linear conjugation,
\ell=L_{\infty}
or
\ell\in\{x(y-x)(y-\xi\,x)=0\},
where
\xi=\mathrm{exp}\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{d-2}\right)
.

Proof.

Note first of all that the foliation
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{d-1}
has inflection divisor

\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}^{d-1}}=\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}^{d-1}}^{\hskip 0.56905pt\mathrm{inv}}=(d-1)z\,x^{d-1}y^{d-1}\big(y^{d-2}-x^{d-2}\big).

i.
If
\ell
is invariant by
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{d-1}
, then
\ell\in\{xyz(y-\xi^{2k}x)=0,\,k=0,\ldots,d-3\}
and the
d
-web
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{1}^{d-1})
is flat (Corollary
3.11
).

ii.
Assume that
\ell
is not invariant by
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{d-1}
; then
\ell=(y-\rho x=0)
with
\rho(\rho^{d-2}-1)\neq 0.
According to Corollary
3.22
, the
d
-web
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{1}^{d-1})
is flat if and only if
Q(1,\rho;-\rho,1)=0,
where

\displaystyle Q(x,y;-\rho,1)=-\frac{\partial{P}}{\partial{x}}-\rho^{d-1}\frac{\partial{P}}{\partial{y}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad P(x,y;-\rho,1)=-\frac{y^{d-1}-(\rho x)^{d-1}}{y-\rho x}=-\sum_{i=0}^{d-2}\rho^{i}x^{i}y^{d-2-i}.

Thus
Q(1,\rho;-\rho,1)=\frac{1}{2}(d-1)(d-2)\rho^{d-2}(\rho^{d-2}+1)
, and the flatness of
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{1}^{d-1})
is equivalent to
\rho^{d-2}=-1
and therefore to
\ell\in\{y-\xi^{2k+1}x=0,\,k=0,\ldots,d-3\}.

In the two cases considered,
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{1}^{d-1})
is flat if and only if, up to conjugation,
\ell=L_{\infty}:=(z=0)
or
\ell\in\{x(y-x)(y-\xi\,x)=0\}.
Indeed, putting
\varphi_{1}(x,y)=(y,x)
and
\varphi_{2}(x,y)=(x,\xi^{2k}y)
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}^{*}(y\omega_{1}^{d-1})=-x\omega_{1}^{d-1},

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526pt\varphi_{2}^{*}\left((y-\xi^{2k}x)\omega_{1}^{d-1}\right)=\xi^{4k}(y-x)\omega_{1}^{d-1},

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526pt\varphi_{2}^{*}\left((y-\xi^{2k+1}x)\omega_{1}^{d-1}\right)=\xi^{4k}(y-\xi\,x)\omega_{1}^{d-1}.

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

The
d
-web
\mathrm{Leg}\big(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)\big)
is flat if and only if, up to linear conjugation, one of the following cases occurs:

(i)

\ell=L_{\infty}
and
d=3
;

(ii)

\ell=L_{\infty},

d\geq 4
and
\lambda=\mu=0
;

(iii)

\ell=(x=0)
and
\lambda=\mu=0
;

(iv)

\ell=(y-x=0),

d\geq 4
and
(\lambda,\mu)=\left(\frac{3}{d},-\frac{3}{d}\right)
;

(v)

\ell=(y-\xi^{\prime}\,x=0),

d\geq 4
and
(\lambda,\mu)=\left(\frac{3\xi^{\prime}}{d},-\frac{3}{d\xi^{\prime}}\right)
, where
\xi^{\prime}=\mathrm{exp}\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{d}\right)
.

Proof.

We have
\omega_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)=A(x,y)\mathrm{d}x+B(x,y)\mathrm{d}y
, where
A(x,y)=x^{d-1}+\lambda\,y^{d-1}
and
B(x,y)=\mu\,x^{d-1}-y^{d-1}
; an immediate computation shows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)}^{\hskip 0.56905pt\mathrm{inv}}=z(x^{d}+\mu\,x^{d-1}y+\lambda\,xy^{d-1}-y^{d})

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)}^{\hskip 0.56905pt\mathrm{tr}}=x^{d-2}y^{d-2}.

1.
If
\ell=L_{\infty}
and
d=3,
then the web
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{2}(\lambda,\mu))
is flat by Corollary
3.2
.

2.
Assume that
\ell=L_{\infty}
and
d\geq 4.
Then, according to
[
4
, Theorem 3.1 and 3.8]
, the web
\mathrm{Leg}(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu))
is flat if and only if
\mathrm{d}\big(\omega_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)\big)
vanishes on the two lines
xy=0.
Now,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\big(\omega_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)\big)\Big|_{x=0}=-(d-1)\lambda\,y^{d-2}\mathrm{d}x\wedge\mathrm{d}y\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{d}\big(\omega_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)\big)\Big|_{y=0}=(d-1)\mu\,x^{d-2}\mathrm{d}x\wedge\mathrm{d}y.

Therefore
\mathrm{Leg}(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu))
is flat if and only if
\lambda=\mu=0
; hence the same holds for
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu))
(Theorem
3.1
).

3.
Let us consider the case where
\ell\in\{xy=0\}.
Up to permuting the coordinates
x
and
y
, we can assume that

\ell=(x=0).
According to Theorem
3.7
, the
d
-web
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu))
is flat if and only if its curvature is holomorphic on
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)}(\{xy=0\}).
Now, on the one hand,
K(\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)))
is holomorphic on
D_{\ell}=\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)}(\ell)
if and only if
\mathrm{d}\big(\omega_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)\big)
vanishes on
\ell=(x=0)
(Corollary
3.26
),
i.e.
if and only if
\lambda=0.
On the other hand, according to Corollary
3.17
,
K(\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)))
is holomorphic on
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)}(\{y=0\})
if and only if

0=(d-3)\big(\partial_{x}B(1,0)-\partial_{y}A(1,0)\big)+3(d-1)B(1,0)=d(d-1)\mu\Longleftrightarrow\mu=0.

It follows that
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu))
is flat if and only if
\lambda=\mu=0.

4.
Let us examine the case where
\ell=(y-\rho\,x=0)
with
\rho\neq 0.
By Corollary
3.17
,
K(\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)))
is holomorphic on
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda,\mu)}(\{xy=0\})
if and only if

\Small

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[l]{l}0=-(d-3)\Big(\partial_{x}B(0,-1)-\partial_{y}A(0,-1)\Big)-3(d-1)\Big(A(0,-1)+\rho B(0,-1)\Big)=(-1)^{d}(d-1)(d\lambda-3\rho)\\
\\
0=-\rho(d-3)\Big(\partial_{x}B(1,0)-\partial_{y}A(1,0)\Big)-3(d-1)\Big(A(1,0)+\rho B(1,0)\Big)=-(d-1)(d\rho\mu+3),\end{array}\right.

i.e.
if and only if
\lambda=\lambda_{0}:=\frac{3\rho}{d}
,
\mu=\mu_{0}:=-\frac{3}{d\rho}
and
d\neq 3
, because
\lambda\mu\neq-1.
We now distinguish two cases according to whether or not
\ell
is invariant by
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}).

4.1.
Assume that
\ell
is invariant by
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}).
Then the dual web of
\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})
is flat by Theorem
3.7
. Since
\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})}^{\hskip 0.56905pt\mathrm{inv}}\Big|_{y=\rho x}=\left(\frac{3}{d}-1\right)\left(\rho^{d}-1\right)z\,x^{d},
the invariance of
\ell
by
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})
is equivalent to
\rho^{d}=1
; as a consequence
(\rho,\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})\in\left\{\Big(\xi^{\prime 2k},\frac{3\xi^{\prime 2k}}{d},-\frac{3}{d\xi^{\prime 2k}}\Big),k=0,\ldots,d-1\right\}.
Up to conjugation,
(\rho,\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})=(1,\frac{3}{d},-\frac{3}{d})
; indeed, putting
\varphi(x,y)=(x,\xi^{\prime 2k}y)
we have

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}\left(\big(y-\xi^{\prime 2k}x\big)\omega_{2}^{d-1}\left(\tfrac{3\xi^{\prime 2k}}{d},-\tfrac{3}{d\xi^{\prime 2k}}\right)\right)=\xi^{\prime 2k}\left(y-x\right)\omega_{2}^{d-1}\left(\tfrac{3}{d},-\tfrac{3}{d}\right).

4.2.
Assume that
\ell
is not invariant by
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}).
Then, by Theorem
3.7
and Corollary
3.20
, the flatness of
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}))
translates into
Q(1,\rho;-\rho,1)=0,
where

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102ptQ(x,y;-\rho,1)=\left(\mu_{0}-\rho^{d-1}\right)\frac{\partial{P}}{\partial{x}}-\left(\lambda_{0}\rho^{d-1}+1\right)\frac{\partial{P}}{\partial{y}}

and

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102ptP(x,y;-\rho,1)=\frac{\left(\lambda_{0}\mu_{0}+1\right)\left(y^{d-1}-(\rho x)^{d-1}\right)}{y-\rho x}=\left(\lambda_{0}\mu_{0}+1\right)\sum_{i=0}^{d-2}\rho^{i}x^{i}y^{d-2-i}.

Hence
Q(1,\rho;-\rho,1)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{3}{d}-1\right)\left(\frac{3}{d}+1\right)^{2}(d-1)(d-2)\rho^{d-3}(\rho^{d}+1)
, and consequently
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}))
is flat if and only if
\rho^{d}=-1
, hence if and only if
(\rho,\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})\in\left\{\Big(\xi^{\prime 2k+1},\frac{3\xi^{\prime 2k+1}}{d},-\frac{3}{d\xi^{\prime 2k+1}}\Big),k=0,\ldots,d-1\right\}
, or equivalently, if and only if, up to conjugation,
(\rho,\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})=\Big(\xi^{\prime},\frac{3\xi^{\prime}}{d},-\frac{3}{d\xi^{\prime}}\Big),
because

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}\left(\big(y-\xi^{\prime 2k+1}x\big)\omega_{2}^{d-1}\left(\tfrac{3\xi^{\prime 2k+1}}{d},-\tfrac{3}{d\xi^{\prime 2k+1}}\right)\right)=\xi^{\prime 2k}\left(y-\xi^{\prime}x\right)\omega_{2}^{d-1}\left(\tfrac{3\xi^{\prime}}{d},-\tfrac{3}{d\xi^{\prime}}\right).

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

The
d
-web
\mathrm{Leg}\big(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)\big)
is flat if and only if one of the following cases holds:

(i)

\ell=L_{\infty}
and
d=3
;

(ii)

\ell=(dy+3x=0),

d\geq 4
and
\lambda=\dfrac{(-1)^{d}(d-3)d^{d-2}}{3^{d-1}}
;

(iii)

\ell=(dy+3x=0)
and
\lambda=\dfrac{(-1)^{d}(d+3)d^{d-2}}{3^{d-1}}.

Proof.

We have
\omega_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)=A(x,y)\mathrm{d}x+B(x,y)\mathrm{d}y
, where
A(x,y)=x^{d-1}+\lambda y^{d-1}
and
B(x,y)=x^{d-1}
; an immediate computation leads to

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)}^{\hskip 0.56905pt\mathrm{inv}}=z\,x^{d-1}\big(x^{d-1}+x^{d-2}y+\lambda\,y^{d-1}\big)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)}^{\hskip 0.56905pt\mathrm{tr}}=y^{d-2}.

1.
Assume that
\ell=L_{\infty}.
If
d=3
, then the web
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{3}^{2}(\lambda))
is flat, thanks to Corollary
3.2
. For
d\geq 4,
the webs
\mathrm{Leg}(\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda))
and
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda))
have the same curvature (Theorem
3.1
) and cannot be flat. Indeed, we have

\mathrm{d}\big(\omega_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)\big)\Big|_{y=0}=(d-1)x^{d-2}\mathrm{d}x\wedge\mathrm{d}y\not\equiv 0;

this implies, according to
[
4
, Theorem 3.8]
, that
K(\mathrm{Leg}(\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)))
cannot be holomorphic along
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)}(\{y=0\}).

2.
If
\ell=(y=0),
then the fact that
\mathrm{d}\big(\omega_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)\big)
does not vanish on
\ell
implies, by Corollary
3.26
, that
K(\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)))
cannot be holomorphic on
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)}(\ell),
so that
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda))
cannot be flat.

3.
Assume that
\ell=(x-\rho\,y=0),
where
\rho\in\mathbb{C}.
By Corollary
3.17
,
K(\mathrm{Leg}(\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)))
is holomorphic on
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)}(\{y=0\})
if and only if

\displaystyle 0=(d-3)\Big(\partial_{x}B(1,0)-\partial_{y}A(1,0)\Big)+3(d-1)\Big(B(1,0)+\rho A(1,0)\Big)=(d-1)(3\rho+d),

hence if and only if
\rho=-\frac{d}{3}
, which is equivalent to
\ell=\ell_{0}
where
\ell_{0}=(dy+3x=0).
Then we have to distinguish two cases:

3.1.
If
\ell_{0}
is invariant by
\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)
, then Theorem
3.7
ensures that the
d
-web
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell_{0}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda))
is flat; since

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)}^{\hskip 0.56905pt\mathrm{inv}}\Big|_{x=-\frac{d}{3}y}=-d^{d-1}z\left(\frac{y}{3}\right)^{2d-2}\left((-1)^{d}3^{d-1}\lambda-(d-3)d^{d-2}\right),

the invariance of
\ell_{0}
by
\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda)
is characterized by
\lambda=\dfrac{(-1)^{d}(d-3)d^{d-2}}{3^{d-1}}
and
d\neq 3
, because
\lambda\neq 0.

3.2.
Assume that
\ell_{0}
is not invariant by
\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda).
Then, according to Theorem
3.7
and Corollary
3.20
, the
d
-web
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell_{0}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda))
is flat if and only if
Q(d,-3;3,d)=0,
where

\displaystyle\hskip-145.10922ptQ(x,y;3,d)=d^{d-1}\frac{\partial{P}}{\partial{x}}-\left(d^{d-1}+(-3)^{d-1}\lambda\right)\frac{\partial{P}}{\partial{y}}

and

\displaystyle\hskip-145.10922ptP(x,y;3,d)=\frac{\lambda\left((dy)^{d-1}-(-3x)^{d-1}\right)}{dy+3x}=\lambda\sum_{i=0}^{d-2}(-3x)^{i}(dy)^{d-2-i}.

Thus
\Small
Q(d,-3;3,d)=-\frac{1}{6}\lambda(d-1)(d-2)(3d)^{d-2}\Big(3^{d-1}\lambda-(-1)^{d}(d+3)d^{d-2}\Big)
and the flatness of
\mathrm{Leg}(\ell_{0}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda))
translates into
\lambda=\dfrac{(-1)^{d}(d+3)d^{d-2}}{3^{d-1}}.

∎

Lemmas
4.1
,
4.2
and
4.3
imply the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathscr{H}=\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{H}
be a homogeneous pre-foliation of co-degree
1
and degree
d\geq 3
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}.
Assume that
\deg\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}}=2,
or equivalently that the map
{\mspace{2mu}\underline{\mspace{-2mu}\mathcal{G}\mspace{-2mu}}\mspace{2mu}}_{\mathcal{H}}
has exactly two critical points. Then, for
d\geq 4
the web
\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{H}
is flat if and only if
\mathscr{H}
is linearly conjugate to one of the ten following pre-foliations

1.

\mathscr{H}_{1}^{d}=L_{\infty}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{1}^{d-1}
;

2.

\mathscr{H}_{2}^{d}=\{x=0\}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{1}^{d-1}
;

3.

\mathscr{H}_{3}^{d}=\{y-x=0\}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{1}^{d-1}
;

4.

\mathscr{H}_{4}^{d}=\{y-\xi\,x=0\}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{1}^{d-1}
,  where
\xi=\mathrm{exp}\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{d-2}\right)
;

5.

\mathscr{H}_{5}^{d}=\{x=0\}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(0,0)
;

6.

\mathscr{H}_{6}^{d}=\{dy+3x=0\}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda_{0})
,  where
\lambda_{0}=\frac{(-1)^{d}(d+3)d^{d-2}}{3^{d-1}}
;

7.

\mathscr{H}_{7}^{d}=\{dy+3x=0\}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{3}^{d-1}(\lambda_{1})
,  where
\lambda_{1}=\frac{(-1)^{d}(d-3)d^{d-2}}{3^{d-1}}
;

8.

\mathscr{H}_{8}^{d}=L_{\infty}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}(0,0)
;

9.

\mathscr{H}_{9}^{d}=\{y-x=0\}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}\big(\tfrac{3}{d},-\tfrac{3}{d}\big)
;

10.

\mathscr{H}_{10}^{d}=\{y-\xi^{\prime}\,x=0\}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{d-1}\big(\tfrac{3\xi^{\prime}}{d},-\tfrac{3}{d\xi^{\prime}}\big)
,  where

\xi^{\prime}=\mathrm{exp}\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{d}\right)
.

For
d=3
the web
\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{H}
is flat if and only if, up to linear conjugation, either
\mathscr{H}
is one of the six pre-foliations
\mathscr{H}_{1}^{3},\mathscr{H}_{2}^{3},\ldots,\mathscr{H}_{6}^{3},
or
\mathscr{H}
is of one of the following two types

11.

\mathscr{H}_{7}^{3}(\lambda)=L_{\infty}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{3}^{2}(\lambda)
,  where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
;

12.

\mathscr{H}_{8}^{3}(\lambda,\mu)=L_{\infty}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{2}^{2}(\lambda,\mu)
,  where
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{C}
with
\lambda\mu\neq-1.

Combining Proposition
4.4
with the fact that every homogeneous foliation of degree
2
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
has degree of type
2
, we obtain the classification, up to automorphism of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
, of homogeneous pre-foliations of type
(1,3)
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
whose dual web is flat.

Corollary 4.5
.

Up to automorphism of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
, there are six examples and two families of homogeneous pre-foliations of co-degree
1
and degree
3
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
with a flat
Legendre
transform, namely:

1.

\mathscr{H}_{1}^{3}=L_{\infty}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{1}^{2}
;

2.

\mathscr{H}_{2}^{3}=\{x=0\}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}_{1}^{2}

of Lemma 6.7 of
[
4
]
on foliations of degree
3.
It plays a key role in the proof of Theorem
H
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\mathscr{F}=\ell\boxtimes\mathcal{F}
be a pre-foliation of co-degree
1
and degree
3
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}.
Assume that the
3
-web
\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{F}
is flat and that the foliation
\mathcal{F}
has a non-degenerate singularity
m
satisfying
\mathrm{BB}(\mathcal{F},m)\neq 4.
Then, through the point
m
pass exactly two
\mathcal{F}
-invariant lines.

Proof.

The hypotheses
\mu(\mathcal{F},m)=1
and
\mathrm{BB}(\mathcal{F},m)\neq 4
ensure the existence of an affine chart
(x,y)
of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
in which

m=(0,0)
and
\mathcal{F}
is defined by a
1
-form
\omega_{0}
of type
\omega_{0}=\omega_{0,1}+\omega_{0,2}+\omega_{0,3},
where

\displaystyle\omega_{0,1}=\lambda\hskip 0.28453pty\mathrm{d}x+\mu\hskip 0.28453ptx\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle\omega_{0,2}=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{2}a_{i}\hskip 0.28453ptx^{2-i}y^{i}\right)\mathrm{d}x+\left(\sum_{i=0}^{2}b_{i}\hskip 0.28453ptx^{2-i}y^{i}\right)\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle\omega_{0,3}=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{2}c_{i}\hskip 0.28453ptx^{2-i}y^{i}\right)(x\mathrm{d}y-y\mathrm{d}x),

with
\lambda\mu(\lambda+\mu)\neq 0.

The only lines passing through
m
and which can be invariant by
\mathcal{F}
are
(x=0)
and
(y=0).
Indeed, denote by
\mathrm{R}=x\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x}}+y\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{y}}
the radial vector field centered at
m
; if
L=(ux+vy=0)
is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant, then
ux+vy
must divide the tangent cone
\mathrm{C}_{\omega_{0,1}}:=\omega_{0,1}(\mathrm{R})=(\lambda+\mu)xy,
so that
u=0
or
v=0.

We will show that indeed
(x=0)
and
(y=0)
are invariant by
\mathcal{F},
which will establish the lemma. We have to prove that
a_{0}=b_{2}=0,
since the invariance by
\mathcal{F}
of
(x=0)
, resp.
(y=0),
is equivalent to the vanishing of
b_{2}
, resp.
a_{0}.

If
\ell=\{\alpha\,x+\beta\,y+\gamma=0\}
then, in the affine chart
(p,q)
of
\mathbb{\check{P}}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
corresponding to the line
\{y=px-q\}\subset{\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}},
the
3
-web
\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{F}
is described by the symmetric
3
-form

\Small

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\check{\omega}=\big((\gamma-\beta\,q)\mathrm{d}p+(\alpha+\beta\,p)\mathrm{d}q\big)\check{\omega}_{0},

where

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\check{\omega}_{0}=\mu\,p\mathrm{d}p\mathrm{d}q+(a_{0}+b_{0}p+c_{0}q)\mathrm{d}q^{2}+\Big(\lambda\,\mathrm{d}p+(a_{1}+b_{1}p+c_{1}q)\mathrm{d}q\Big)(p\mathrm{d}q-q\mathrm{d}p)+\big(a_{2}+b_{2}p+c_{2}q\big)\big(p\mathrm{d}q-q\mathrm{d}p\big)^{2}.

Assume by contradiction that
a_{0}\neq 0.
Consider the family of automorphisms
\varphi=\varphi_{\varepsilon}=(a_{0}\hskip 0.28453pt\varepsilon\hskip 0.28453ptp,\hskip 0.28453pta_{0}\hskip 0.28453pt\varepsilon^{2}\hskip 0.28453ptq).
We see that if
\gamma\neq 0
,  resp.
\gamma=0
and
\alpha\neq 0,
resp.
\gamma=\alpha=0,
then

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{-5}\gamma^{-1}a_{0}^{-4}\varphi^{*}\check{\omega}=\theta_{1}\eta,

\displaystyle\text{resp.}\hskip 2.84526pt\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{-6}\alpha^{-1}a_{0}^{-4}\varphi^{*}\check{\omega}=\theta_{2}\eta,

\displaystyle\text{resp.}\hskip 2.84526pt\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{-7}\beta^{-1}a_{0}^{-5}\varphi^{*}\check{\omega}=\theta_{3}\eta,

where

\displaystyle\hskip-3.98337pt\theta_{1}=\mathrm{d}p,

\displaystyle\theta_{2}=\mathrm{d}q,

\displaystyle\theta_{3}=p\mathrm{d}q-q\mathrm{d}p,

\displaystyle\eta=-\lambda\,q\mathrm{d}p^{2}+(\lambda+\mu)p\mathrm{d}p\mathrm{d}q+\mathrm{d}q^{2}.

For
i=1,2,3,
put
\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(i)}=\mathcal{F}_{i}\boxtimes\mathcal{W}_{2},
where
\mathcal{W}_{2}
(resp.
\mathcal{F}_{i}
) denotes the
2
-web (resp. the foliation) defined by
\eta
(resp. by
\theta_{i}
). It follows that if
\gamma\neq 0
,  resp.
\gamma=0
and
\alpha\neq 0,
resp.
\gamma=\alpha=0,
then the closure of the
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{\check{P}}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}})
-orbit of
\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{F}
contains the
3
-web
\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(1)},
resp.
\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(2)},
resp.
\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(3)}.
Now, since
\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{F}
is flat by hypothesis, every
3
-web belonging to
\overline{\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{F})}
is also flat. Therefore, to obtain a contradiction, it suffices to show that for every
i=1,2,3,

\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(i)}
is not flat. Since
\Delta(\eta)=f(p,q):=4\lambda\,q+(\lambda+\mu)^{2}p^{2}
, it suffices again to show that for every
i=1,2,3,
the curvature of
\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(i)}
is not holomorphic along the component
\mathcal{C}=\{f(p,q)=0\}\subset\Delta(\mathcal{W}_{2}),
which is a parabola, because
\lambda(\lambda+\mu)\neq 0.

First of all, let us note that
\mathcal{C}
is not invariant by
\mathcal{W}_{2}
, since putting
\eta_{0}=(\lambda+\mu)p\mathrm{d}p+2\mathrm{d}q,
we have

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38092pt\eta\big|_{\mathcal{C}}=\left(\frac{\eta_{0}}{2}\right)^{2}\qquad\qquad\text{and}\qquad\qquad\eta_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}f=-4\mu(\lambda+\mu)p\mathrm{d}p\wedge\mathrm{d}q\not\equiv 0.

Let us consider the case where
i\in\{1,2\}.
Since

\displaystyle\hskip 36.13512pt\eta_{0}\wedge\theta_{1}\Big|_{\mathcal{C}}=-2\mathrm{d}p\wedge\mathrm{d}q\not\equiv 0\qquad\qquad\text{and}\qquad\qquad\eta_{0}\wedge\theta_{2}\Big|_{\mathcal{C}}=(\lambda+\mu)p\mathrm{d}p\wedge\mathrm{d}q\not\equiv 0,

we have
\mathcal{C}\not\subset\mathrm{Tang}(\mathcal{W}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{i}).
Therefore, according to
[
13
, Theorem 1]
(
cf.

[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
), the curvature
K(\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(i)})
is holomorphic on
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is invariant by
\mathcal{F}_{i},
which is impossible, because each
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is a pencil of lines and hence cannot admit a parabola as an invariant curve.

Let us now examine the case where
i=3.
In this case
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathrm{Tang}(\mathcal{W}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{3})
if and only if
\lambda=\mu,
because

\displaystyle\eta_{0}\wedge\theta_{3}\Big|_{\mathcal{C}}=\frac{1}{2\lambda}(\lambda-\mu)(\lambda+\mu)p^{2}\mathrm{d}p\wedge\mathrm{d}q\equiv 0\Longleftrightarrow\lambda=\mu.

If
\lambda\neq\mu
, then, as above, we can apply Theorem 1 of
[
13
]
and assert that
K(\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(3)})
cannot be holomorphic on
\mathcal{C}.

We therefore assume that
\lambda=\mu
and prove that
K(\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(3)})\not\equiv 0.
The pull-back of
\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(3)}
by the rational map
\psi(p,q)=\left(p,\mu(q^{2}-p^{2})\right)
writes as
\psi^{*}\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(3)}=\mathcal{F}_{3}^{(1)}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{3}^{(2)}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{3}^{(3)}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3}^{(1)}\hskip 0.28453pt:\hskip 0.28453pt(p^{2}+q^{2})\mathrm{d}p-2pq\mathrm{d}q=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3}^{(2)}\hskip 0.28453pt:\hskip 0.28453pt(p+q)\mathrm{d}p-2q\mathrm{d}q=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3}^{(3)}\hskip 0.28453pt:\hskip 0.28453pt(p-q)\mathrm{d}p-2q\mathrm{d}q=0.

Using formula (
1.1
), a direct computation leads to

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\eta(\psi^{*}\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(3)})=-\frac{p\mathrm{d}p}{q^{2}}+\frac{4\mathrm{d}q}{q}+\frac{\mathrm{d}(p^{2}-q^{2})}{p^{2}-q^{2}},

so that

K(\psi^{*}\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(3)})=\mathrm{d}\eta(\psi^{*}\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(3)})=-\frac{2p}{q^{3}}\mathrm{d}p\wedge\mathrm{d}q\not\equiv 0,

hence
\psi^{*}K(\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(3)})=K(\psi^{*}\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(3)})\not\equiv 0
and therefore
K(\mathcal{W}_{3}^{(3)})\not\equiv 0.

We have thus shown that
a_{0}=0
, which means that the line
(y=0)
is invariant by
\mathcal{F}
. Exchanging the roles of the coordinates
x
and
y
, the same argument shows that
b_{2}=0
,
i.e.
that the line
(x=0)
is also invariant by
\mathcal{F}.

∎

Before starting the proof of Theorem
H
, let us recall (
cf.

[
8
]
) that if
\mathcal{F}
is a foliation of degree
d
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
then

(6.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{F}}\mu(\mathcal{F},s)=d^{2}+d+1

and

\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{F}}\mathrm{BB}(\mathcal{F},s)=(d+2)^{2}.

Proof of Theorem
H
.

Write
\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{F}=\Sigma^{1}\cup\Sigma^{2}
, where

\displaystyle\Sigma^{1}=\{s\in\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{F}\hskip 2.84526pt\colon\mathrm{BB}(\mathcal{F},s)=4\}

and

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}=\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{F}\setminus\Sigma^{1}.

For
i=1,2,
put
\kappa_{i}=\#\hskip 1.42262pt\Sigma^{i}
. By hypothesis, we have
\deg\mathcal{F}=2
and, for any
s\in\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{F}
,
\mu(\mathcal{F},s)=1
. Formulas (
6.1
) then give

(6.2)

\displaystyle\#\hskip 1.42262pt\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{F}=\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}=7

and

\displaystyle 4\kappa_{1}+\sum_{s\in\Sigma^{2}}\mathrm{BB}(\mathcal{F},s)=16.

It follows that
\Sigma^{2}
is non-empty. Let
m
be a point of
\Sigma^{2}
; by Lemma
6.2
through the point
m
pass exactly two
\mathcal{F}
-invariant lines
D_{m}^{(1)}
and
D_{m}^{(2)}
. Then, for
i=1,2
, Proposition
6.1
ensures the existence of a homogeneous pre-foliation
\mathscr{H}^{(i)}_{m}=\ell_{m}^{(i)}\boxtimes\mathcal{H}^{(i)}_{m}
of type
(1,3)
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
belonging to
\overline{\mathcal{O}(\mathscr{F})}
and such that the line
D_{m}^{(i)}
is
\mathcal{H}^{(i)}_{m}
-invariant. Since
\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{F}
is flat by hypothesis, so are
\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{H}^{(1)}_{m}
and
\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{H}^{(2)}_{m}.
Therefore,
\mathscr{H}^{(i)}_{m}
(
i=1,2
) is linearly conjugate to one of the eight models of Corollary
4.5
. For
i=1,2,
Proposition
6.1
also ensures that

(
\mathfrak{a}
)

if
\ell\neq D_{m}^{(i)}
, then
\ell_{m}^{(i)}\neq L_{\infty}
;

(
\mathfrak{b}
)

\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{F}\cap D_{m}^{(i)}=\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{H}^{(i)}_{m}\cap D_{m}^{(i)}
;

(
\mathfrak{c}
)

\forall\hskip 1.42262pts\in\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{H}^{(i)}_{m}\cap D_{m}^{(i)},\quad\mu(\mathcal{H}^{(i)}_{m},s)=1\hskip 5.69054pt\text{and}\quad\mathrm{CS}(\mathcal{H}^{(i)}_{m},D_{m}^{(i)},s)=\mathrm{CS}(\mathcal{F},D_{m}^{(i)},s)
.

Since
\mathrm{CS}(\mathcal{F},D_{m}^{(1)},m)\mathrm{CS}(\mathcal{F},D_{m}^{(2)},m)=1,
we have

(6.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{CS}(\mathcal{H}^{(1)}_{m},D_{m}^{(1)},m)\mathrm{CS}(\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{m},D_{m}^{(2)},m)=1.

Let us first assume that
\ell\neq D_{m}^{(i)}
for
i=1,2
; this is obviously the case if
\ell
is not invariant by
\mathcal{F}.
Then, by (
\mathfrak{a}
), we have
\ell_{m}^{(i)}\neq L_{\infty}
for
i=1,2.
Therefore, each of the
\mathscr{H}^{(i)}_{m}
is conjugate to one of the five pre-foliations
\mathscr{H}_{j}^{3},j=2,\ldots,6,
so that each of the
\mathcal{H}^{(i)}_{m}
is conjugate to one of the three foliations
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{2}
,
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{2}(0,0),

\mathcal{H}_{3}^{2}(-2)
(Corollary
4.5
). Consulting Table
1
and using equality (
6.3
) as well as relations (
\mathfrak{b}
) and (
\mathfrak{c}
), we see that

\Small

(6.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{CS}(\mathcal{H}^{(1)}_{m},D_{m}^{(1)},m)=\mathrm{CS}(\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{m},D_{m}^{(2)},m)=-1,

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69054pt\#\hskip 1.42262pt(\Sigma^{1}\cap D_{m}^{(1)})=\#\hskip 1.42262pt(\Sigma^{1}\cap D_{m}^{(2)})=2,

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69054pt\Sigma^{2}\cap D_{m}^{(1)}=\Sigma^{2}\cap D_{m}^{(2)}=\{m\}.

Let us now assume that the line
\ell
is equal to one of the lines
D_{m}^{(i)}
, say
\ell=D_{m}^{(2)}.
Let us show that equalities (
6.4
) still hold. Since
\ell\neq D_{m}^{(1)}
,
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}_{m}
is conjugate to one of the foliations
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{2}
,
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{2}(0,0),

\mathcal{H}_{3}^{2}(-2).
Moreover
\Sigma^{2}\cap D_{m}^{(1)}=\{m\}
; indeed, if
\Sigma^{2}\cap D_{m}^{(1)}
contained another point
m^{\prime}\neq m,
we would have
\ell\neq D_{m^{\prime}}^{(i)}
for
i=1,2,
so that
\{m^{\prime}\}=\Sigma^{2}\cap D_{m^{\prime}}^{(i)}=\Sigma^{2}\cap D_{m}^{(1)}\supset\{m,m^{\prime}\}
, which is impossible. From Table
1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathrm{CS}(\mathcal{H}^{(1)}_{m},D_{m}^{(1)},m)=\mathrm{CS}(\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{m},\ell,m)=-1\qquad\qquad\text{and}\qquad\qquad\#\hskip 1.42262pt(\Sigma^{1}\cap D_{m}^{(1)})=2,

hence

\displaystyle\hskip-178.11409pt\mathrm{CS}(\mathcal{F},D_{m}^{(1)},m)=\mathrm{CS}(\mathcal{F},\ell,m)=-1.

Since these equalities are valid for any choice of
m\in\Sigma^{2}\cap\ell
and since every line of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
cannot contain more than
\deg\mathcal{F}+1=3
singular points of
\mathcal{F},
the
Camacho
-
Sad
formula (
see

[
10
]
)
\sum_{s\in\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{F}\cap\ell}\mathrm{CS}(\mathcal{F},\ell,s)=1
implies that

\displaystyle\#\hskip 1.42262pt(\Sigma^{1}\cap\ell)=2\qquad\qquad\text{and}\qquad\qquad\Sigma^{2}\cap\ell=\{m\}.

Equalities (
6.4
) are thus established in all cases. It follows in particular that
\mathrm{BB}(\mathcal{F},m)=0.
The point
m\in\Sigma^{2}
being arbitrary,
\Sigma^{2}
consists of
s\in\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathrm{BB}(\mathcal{F},s)=0
. System (
6.2
) then rewrites as
\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}=7
and
4\kappa_{1}=16,
whose unique solution is
(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})=(4,3)
, that is
\mathrm{Sing}\mathcal{F}=\Sigma^{1}\cup\Sigma^{2},\hskip 5.69054pt\#\hskip 1.42262pt\Sigma^{1}=4
and
\#\hskip 1.42262pt\Sigma^{2}=3.
Since
\Sigma^{2}\cap(D_{m}^{(1)}\cup D_{m}^{(2)})=\{m\}
,
\mathcal{F}
has
3\cdot 2=6
invariant lines, which means that
\mathcal{F}
is reduced convex. It then follows from the classification of convex foliations of degree two (
cf.

[
11
, Proposition 7.4]
or
[
5
, Theorem A]
) that
\mathcal{F}
is linearly conjugate to the
Fermat
foliation
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{2}.
We conclude by noting that if the line
\ell
is not invariant by
\mathcal{F}
, the flatness of
\mathrm{Leg}\mathscr{F}
and Proposition
F
imply that
\ell
must join two non-radial singularities of
\mathcal{F}.

∎

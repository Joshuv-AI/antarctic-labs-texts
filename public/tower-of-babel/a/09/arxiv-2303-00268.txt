Miyaoka type inequality for terminal threefolds with nef anti-canonical divisors
Masataka Iwai, Chen Jiang, Haidong Liu

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Miyaoka type inequality on Chern classes of terminal projective 3 3 -folds with nef anti-canonical divisors. Let X X be a terminal projective 3 3 -fold such that − K X -K_{X} is nef. We show that if c 1 ​ ( X ) ⋅ c 2 ​ ( X ) ≠ 0 c_{1}(X)\cdot c_{2}(X)\neq 0 , then c 1 ​ ( X ) ⋅ c 2 ​ ( X ) ≥ 1 252 c_{1}(X)\cdot c_{2}(X)\geq\frac{1}{252} ; if further X X is not rationally connected, then c 1 ​ ( X ) ⋅ c 2 ​ ( X ) ≥ 4 5 c_{1}(X)\cdot c_{2}(X)\geq\frac{4}{5} and this inequality is sharp. In order to prove this, we give a partial classification of such varieties along with many examples. We also study the nonvanishing of c 1 ​ ( X ) dim X − 2 ⋅ c 2 ​ ( X ) c_{1}(X)^{\dim X-2}\cdot c_{2}(X) for terminal weak Fano varieties and prove a Miyaoka–Kawamata type inequality.

1.
Introduction

In
[
26
]
*Question 4.8, the third author proposed the following question, which
is a mirror version of Kollár’s expectation
[
22
]
*Remark 3.6 for minimal varieties.

Question 1.1
.

Let
X
be a terminal projective variety of dimension
n
such that
-K_{X}
is nef.

(1)

If
c_{1}(X)^{n-2}\cdot c_{2}(X)=0
, is there a classification of
X
?

(2)

If
c_{1}(X)^{n-2}\cdot c_{2}(X)>0
, is there a constant
\epsilon>0

depending only on
n
such that
c_{1}(X)^{n-2}\cdot c_{2}(X)\geq\epsilon
?

This expectation heavily depends on Ou’s result
[
37
]
*Corollary 1.5 on the pseudo-effectivity of the second Chern classes, which implies that for a terminal projective variety
X
of dimension
n
such that
-K_{X}
is nef,

(1.1)

\displaystyle c_{1}(X)^{n-2}\cdot c_{2}(X)\geq 0.

It can be viewed as a mirror version of Miyaoka’s inequality
[
30
]
*Corollary 6.4
on the second Chern classes.

When
\dim X=2
, that is, when
X
is a smooth projective surface such that
-K_{X}
is nef, the answer to Question
1.1
is clear and well-known.
If
c_{2}(X)=0
, then it is well-known that
X
admits a finite étale cover by an abelian surface or a

\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over an elliptic curve (see
[
37
]
*Theorem 1.8 and the references therein).
If
c_{2}(X)\neq 0
, then the Noether’s formula
c_{2}(X)=12\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})-c_{1}(X)^{2}
implies that
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})\geq 1
and hence
c_{2}(X)\geq 3
as
c_{1}(X)^{2}\leq 9
; furthermore, if
X
is not rational (or
-K_{X}
is not big), then
c_{1}(X)^{2}=0
and
c_{2}(X)\geq 12
.

The goal of this paper is to study Question
1.1
in dimension 3, which is also motivated by the classification theory of
3
-folds.
For a terminal projective
3
-fold
X
with
K_{X}\equiv 0
,
c_{1}(X)\cdot c_{2}(X)=0
holds trivially. Such
3
-folds are known as “Calabi–Yau
3
-folds" and have already been studied in many references (for example,
[
19
,
32
,
35
,
36
,
16
]
), so we will always assume that
K_{X}\not\equiv 0
.
In this case, there are also many interesting results, see

[
1
,
7
,
43
,
4
,
29
,
25
,
44
]
and the references therein.

Our main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a terminal projective
3
-fold such that
-K_{X}
is nef. Suppose that
c_{1}(X)\cdot c_{2}(X)\neq 0
.

(1)

Then
c_{1}(X)\cdot c_{2}(X)\geq\frac{1}{252}
.

(2)

If
-K_{X}
is not big, then
c_{1}(X)\cdot c_{2}(X)\geq\frac{2}{5}
.

(3)

If
X
is not rationally connected, then
c_{1}(X)\cdot c_{2}(X)\geq\frac{4}{5}
.

Theorem
1.2
is obtained by a partial classification of terminal projective
3
-folds with nef anti-canonical divisors.
We briefly summarize the results in the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a terminal projective
3
-fold such that
-K_{X}
is nef and
K_{X}\not\equiv 0
.
Then
X
is one of the following:

(1)

(Theorem
5.1
, cf.
[
1
]
)
X
is smooth and

(a)

X
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over an abelian surface or a bi-elliptic surface;

(b)

X
admits a locally trivial fibration over an elliptic curve, whose fibers are rationally connected;

(c)

X
is a product of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with a K3 surface;

(d)

X
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over an Enriques surface,
which is trivialized by the universal double étale cover;

(2)

X
is rationally connected;

(3)

(Theorem
6.2
)
X
is singular and not rationally connected, and
X=X^{\prime}/G
where
X^{\prime}
is either a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over an abelian surface or a product of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with a K3 surface, and
G
is a group acting on
X^{\prime}
which can be classified.

From this classification, we can describe the structure of
X
when
c_{1}(X)\cdot c_{2}(X)=0
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
X
be a terminal projective
3
-fold such that
-K_{X}
is nef and
K_{X}\not\equiv 0
.
Then
c_{1}(X)\cdot c_{2}(X)=0
if and only if
X
is one of the following:

(1)

X
admits a quasi-étale cover by a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over an abelian surface;

(2)

X
admits a locally trivial fibration over an elliptic curve, whose fibers are rationally connected;

(3)

X
is rationally connected,
K_{X}^{3}=0
, and the set
\mathcal{R}_{X}
of local indices of the virtue singularities (see §
3
for the definition) belongs to
Table
1
of Theorem
4.1
except types
(5),(6)
and
(10)
.

Remark 1.5
.

The lower bound of Theorem
1.2
(3) is sharp by Example
6.4
, while the lower bounds of Theorem
1.2
(1)(2) might not be sharp. The main difficulty is the case when
X
is non-Gorenstein and rationally connected, in which case there are no good methods to classify
X
in a more explicit way. For example, one natural strategy in birational geometry is to run a minimal model program, and then describe each step of the minimal model program and classify the outcomes (which are Mori fiber spaces in this case). But so far neither of these can be done in an explicit way as singularities are involved. Also, we will lose the nefness of
-K_{X}
when running the minimal model program.

Finally, we focus on the case when
-K_{X}
is nef and big. Such
X
is usually called a
weak Fano
variety; and
X
is called a
Fano
variety if
-K_{X}
is ample. Terminal weak Fano
3
-folds have been also studied in many references (see
[
8
,
17
]
and the references therein).
In fact,
c_{1}c_{2}>2
for the majority of known examples of terminal Fano
3
-folds (see Example
7.1
). So it is interesting to improve the lower bound of
c_{1}c_{2}
for terminal weak Fano
3
-folds or to find new examples with small

c_{1}c_{2}
.

Another interesting problem is whether there exist terminal weak Fano
3
-folds
with
c_{1}c_{2}=0
. In this direction, we prove that this can not happen.

Theorem 1.6

(Corollary
7.3
)
.

Let
X
be a terminal weak Fano variety of dimension
n
.
Then
c_{1}(X)^{n-2}\cdot c_{2}(X)>0
.

As a corollary, we can show a Miyaoka–Kawamata type inequality (see
[
20
,
5
]
for the case
\rho(X)=1
).

Corollary 1.7

(Corollaries
7.4
and
7.6
)
.

There exists a positive rational number
b
depending only
on
n
such that the inequality

c_{1}(X)^{n}\leq b\cdot c_{1}(X)^{n-2}\cdot c_{2}(X)

holds for any terminal weak Fano variety
X
of dimension
n
.
In particular, we can choose
b=2^{4}\cdot 3^{6}\cdot 7
when
n=3
.

Remark 1.8
.

The universal upper bound
b=2^{4}\cdot 3^{6}\cdot 7
in Corollary
1.7
is far from being sharp.
We would conjecture that
b
should be 3, the same as the constant in
[
30
]
*Theorem 1.1 (see Remark
7.7
).

Remark 1.9
.

Recently, the third author and Jie Liu proved in
[
27
]
that
for terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano varieties, i.e.,
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminal Fano varieties with Picard number one,
the bound
b
in Corollary
1.7
can be improved to be
4
(and to be
25/8
in dimension 3).

2.
A structure theorem of Cao–Höring and Matsumura–Wang

Let
X
be a terminal projective variety such that
-K_{X}
is nef.
There exists a structure theorem of
X

by
[
7
]
*Theorem 1.3
for smooth cases and
[
29
]
*Theorem 1.1 for klt cases. We recall these results in the setting of terminal singularities as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a proper surjective morphism between normal varieties with connected fibers. Then
f
is called a
locally constant fibration
if
f
is a locally trivial fiber bundle with a representation

\rho\colon\pi_{1}(Y)\to\Aut(F),

where
\pi_{1}(Y)
is the fundamental group of
Y
and
F
is the fiber of
f
, such that
X
is isomorphic to the quotient of
\widetilde{Y}\times F
by the action of
\pi_{1}(Y)
given by
\delta\cdot(\widetilde{y},z)=(\delta\cdot\widetilde{y},\rho(\delta)(z))
where
\widetilde{Y}
is the universal cover of
Y
.

Theorem 2.2

(cf.
[
7
,
40
,
29
]
)
.

Let
X
be a terminal projective variety such that
-K_{X}
is nef. Then,
there exists a finite cover
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
étale in codimension
2

such that
X^{\prime}
admits a holomorphic MRC fibration
f\colon X^{\prime}\to Y

satisfying the following properties:

(1)

f
is locally constant;

(2)

any fiber
F
of
f
is a rationally connected variety with terminal singularities;

(3)

Y
is a terminal projective variety with
K_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
.

Moreover, if
X
is smooth,
then we may choose
\pi
to be the identity map and
Y
is smooth.

Proof.

The existence of
\pi
and
f
is by
[
29
]
*Theorem 1.1 and the last statement is by
[
7
]
*Theorem 1.3.

Here note that by
[
29
]
*Theorem 1.1,
\pi
is étale in codimension
1
and
Y,F
are klt. So we shall explain why
\pi
is étale in codimension
2
and
Y,F
are terminal in our setting.

Since
X
is terminal, the singular locus
\Sing X
is of codimension at least 3 by
[
24
]
*Corollary 5.18.
Since
\pi
is étale in codimension
1
,

\pi
is actually étale over the smooth locus
X\setminus\Sing X
by the purity of ramification locus
(Zariski’s purity theorem). Therefore,
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
is étale in codimension
2
.

Since
X
is terminal,
X^{\prime}
is also terminal by
[
24
]
*Proposition 5.20;
then
Y,F
are also terminal since
f
is locally constant.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
F
be a rationally connected projective variety with rational singularities. Then
H^{j}(F,\mathcal{O}_{F})=0
for
j\geq 1
. In particular,
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{F})=1
.

Proof.

As
F
has only rational singularities, it suffices to show that for a resolution
F^{\prime}
of
F
,
H^{j}(F^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{F^{\prime}})=0
for
j\geq 1
. Note that
F^{\prime}
is again rationally connected, so the conclusion follows from

[
23
]
*Corollary IV.3.8.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
f\colon X^{\prime}\to Y
be the fibration in Theorem
2.2
. Then
h^{i}(X^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}})=h^{i}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})

for each
i\geq 0
. In particular,
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}})=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Y})
.

Proof.

Since any fiber
F
of
f
is rationally connected and terminal,

H^{j}(F,\mathcal{O}_{F})=0
for
j\geq 1
by Lemma
2.3
. In particular,

R^{j}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}=0
for
j\geq 1
as the fibration is locally constant. Then
h^{i}(X^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}})=h^{i}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
by the Leray spectral sequence.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
X
be a terminal projective
3
-fold such that
-K_{X}
is nef. If the irregularity
q(X):=h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})>0
, then
X
is smooth.

Proof.

By
[
40
]
*Theorem A or
[
29
]
*Corollary 4.9, the Albanese map of
X
is locally constant and surjective. Any fiber of the Albanese map of
X
has dimension at most
2
and has terminal singularities, so it is smooth, which implies that
X
is smooth.
∎

If a normal variety
X
is smooth in codimension
k
, then by extending from the smooth locus, the Chern classes
c_{i}(X)
can be defined as elements sitting in the Chow groups
A^{i}(X)
for
i\leq k
. In particular,
c_{1}(X)
and
c_{2}(X)
are well-defined for terminal varieties.
The following lemma for Chern classes is easy but useful.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
X
be a normal projective
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein variety of dimension
n
which is smooth in codimension
2
. Let
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a finite morphism which is
étale in codimension
2
, then
c_{i}(X^{\prime})=\pi^{*}c_{i}(X)
for
i=1,2
.
In particular,
c_{1}(X^{\prime})^{n-2}\cdot c_{2}(X^{\prime})=\deg\pi\cdot c_{1}(X)^{n-2}\cdot c_{2}(X)
.

3.
Reid’s formula and holomorphic Euler characteristics

A
basket

B
is a collection of pairs of coprime integers (permitting weights). Let
X
be a terminal projective
3
-fold. According to Reid
[
39
]
,
there is a basket of virtual orbifold points

B_{X}=\{(b_{i},r_{i})\mid i=1,\cdots,s;0<b_{i}\leq\frac{r_{i}}{2};b_{i}\text{ is coprime to }r_{i}\}

associated to
X
, where a pair
\left(b_{i},r_{i}\right)
corresponds to an orbifold point
Q_{i}
of type
\frac{1}{r_{i}}\left(1,-1,b_{i}\right)
.
Here recall that every singularity of
X
, as a
3
-dimensional terminal singularity, can be locally deformed into a set of cyclic quotient singularities, and these cyclic quotient singularities are called
virtual orbifold points
of
X
.
Denote by
\mathcal{R}_{X}
the collection of
r_{i}
(permitting weights) appearing in
B_{X}
or as a set of integers with weights where weights appear in superscripts, say for example,

\mathcal{R}_{X}=\{3,3,7,11\}=\{3^{2},7,11\}.

Note that the Cartier index
r_{X}
of
K_{X}
is just
{\text{l.c.m.}}\{r_{i}\mid r_{i}\in\mathcal{R}_{X}\}
.

According to Reid
[
39
]
*10.3,
for any positive integer
n
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\chi(-nK_{X})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12}n(n+1)(2n+1)\left(-K_{X}^{3}\right)+(2n+1)\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})-l(n+1)

where
l(n+1)=\sum_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\overline{jb_{i}}\left(r_{i}-\overline{jb_{i}}\right)}{2r_{i}}
and the first sum runs over
B_{X}
. Here
\overline{jb_{i}}
means the smallest non-negative residue of
jb_{i}\bmod r_{i}
.

In this case,
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
is related to
c_{1}(X)\cdot c_{2}(X)
and the singularities
as follows:

Theorem 3.1

(
[
19
,
39
]
)
.

Let X be a terminal projective
3
-fold. Then

(3.2)

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})=\frac{1}{24}c_{1}(X)\cdot c_{2}(X)+\frac{1}{24}\sum_{r_{i}\in\mathcal{R}_{X}}(r_{i}-\frac{1}{r_{i}}).

As a corollary, we get the finiteness of singularities of terminal
3
-folds with nef anti-canonical divisors.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
X
be a terminal projective
3
-fold
X
such that
-K_{X}
is nef and
K_{X}\not\equiv 0
. Then the following hold:

(1)

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})\in\{0,1,2\}
. Moreover,
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})\neq 0
if
X
is singular.

(2)

There are only finitely many possibilities for
\mathcal{R}_{X}
,
B_{X}
, and
c_{1}(X)\cdot c_{2}(X)
.

Proof.

(1) We may assume that
X
is not rationally connected by Lemma
2.3
.

Let
f\colon X^{\prime}\to Y
be the fibration in Theorem
2.2
. Then
\dim Y\in\{1,2\}
as
K_{X}\not\equiv 0
and
X
is not rationally connected. Moreover,
Y
is smooth and
K_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
. Hence
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}})=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Y})\in\{0,1,2\}
by Proposition
2.4
and the classical classification of curves and surfaces.

Let
q(X):=h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
be the irregularity of
X
. If
q(X)>0
, then
X
is smooth by Lemma
2.5
. Hence

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}})\in\{0,1,2\}
by Theorem
2.2
.

If
q(X)=0
(which contains the case that
X
is singular), then as
X^{\prime}\to X
is finite,

h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\leq h^{2}(X^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}})=h^{2}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})\leq 1

by Proposition
2.4
and the classical classification of curves and surfaces. Also
h^{3}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
as
K_{X}\not\equiv 0
.
Hence

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})=1+h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\in\{1,2\}.

(2) By
[
37
]
*Corollary 1.5,
c_{1}(X)\cdot c_{2}(X)\geq 0
. Hence by Theorem
3.1
, as

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{r_{i}\in\mathcal{R}_{X}}(r_{i}-\frac{1}{r_{i}})\leq 24\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})\leq 48,

there are only finitely many possibilities for
\mathcal{R}_{X}
and hence also for
B_{X}
.
Therefore, by Theorem
3.1
again, there are only finitely many possibilities for
c_{1}(X)\cdot c_{2}(X)
. ∎

If
-K_{X}
is nef but not big, then there is a strong restriction on
B_{X}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a terminal projective
3
-fold such that

-K_{X}
is nef but not big. Then
l(n+1)
is an integer for any positive integer
n
. In particular,
l(2)=\sum_{i}\frac{b_{i}(r_{i}-b_{i})}{2r_{i}}
is an integer.

Proof.

Since
(-K_{X})^{3}=0
, by (
3.1
),
l(n+1)=(2n+1)\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})-\chi(-nK_{X})
is an integer.
∎

Lemma 6.1

Proof.

Since the sheaf

By the same calculations, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}4c_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{X})H_{\varepsilon}^{n-2}-c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X})^{2}H_{\varepsilon}^{n-2}&\geq 2\left(\sum_{1\leq k\leq l}a_{k}\right)^{-1}\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq l}a_{i}\sum_{1\leq j\leq l}a_{j}-\sum_{1\leq i\leq l}\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{r_{i}}\right)-\sum_{1\leq i\leq l}a_{i}+O(\varepsilon)\\
&=\left(\sum_{1\leq k\leq l}a_{k}\right)^{-1}\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq l}a_{i}\sum_{1\leq j\leq l}a_{j}-2\sum_{1\leq i\leq l}\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{r_{i}}\right)+O(\varepsilon)\\
&\geq\left(\sum_{1\leq k\leq l}a_{k}\right)^{-1}\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq l}a_{i}\sum_{1\leq j\leq l}a_{j}-\frac{2a_{1}}{r_{1}}\sum_{1\leq i\leq l}a_{i}\right)+O(\varepsilon)\\
&=\frac{(r_{1}-2)a_{1}}{r_{1}}+\sum_{2\leq j\leq l}a_{j}+O(\varepsilon).\\
\end{split}

Therefore, if
r_{1}>2
or
a_{2}>0
, then by taking
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain

4c_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{X})c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X})^{n-2}-c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X})^{n}\geq\frac{(r_{1}-2)a_{1}}{r_{1}}+\sum_{2\leq j\leq l}a_{j}>0.

If
r_{1}=2
and
a_{2}=0
, then by the same argument after Claim
1
of Theorem
7.2
,
we have
n=r_{1}=2
.

Indeed, if
n>2
, then from the discussion after Claim
1
in Theorem
7.2
,
there exists a dominant rational map
f\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
such that the Zariski closure of the general fiber is a rationally connected surface.
From
n>2
, we have
\dim Y\geq 1
, which is a contradiction by using the same argument after Claim
2
.
Hence, we can conclude that
n=r_{1}=2
.

In this case,
X
is a surface and
\mathcal{T}_{X}
is
H_{\varepsilon}
-semistable, thus by the Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality, we obtain
4c_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{X})\geq c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X})^{2}
.
∎

Abstract
. In this paper, a new approach of defining Steiner symmetrization of coercive convex functions is proposed and some fundamental properties of the new Steiner symmetrization are proved. Further, using the new Steiner symmetrization, we give a different approach to prove a functional version of the Blaschke-Santaló inequality due to Ball [ 2 ] .

Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to introduce a new way of defining
Steiner symmetrization for coercive convex functions, and to explore
its applications. Our new definition is motivated by and can be

is more suitable for certain functional problems.

Steiner symmetrization is a type of rearrangement. In the 1970s,
interest in rearrangements was renewed, as mathematicians began to
look for geometric proofs of functional inequalities. Rearrangements
were generalized from smooth or convex bodies to measurable sets and
to functions in Sobolev spaces. Functional Steiner symmetrization,
as a kind of important rearrangement of functions, has been studied
in
[
1
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
14
]
. In
[
7
]
,
Brascamp, Lieb, and Luttinger established that the spherical
symmetrization of a nonnegative function can be approximated in

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
by a sequence of
Steiner symmetrizations and rotations. In
[
8
]
, Burchard
proved that Steiner symmetrization is continuous in
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
,

1\leq p<\infty
, for every dimension
n\geq 1
, in the sense that

f_{k}\rightarrow f
in
W^{1,p}
implies
Sf_{k}\rightarrow Sf
in

W^{1,p}
. In
[
14

, Fortier gave a thorough review and
exposition of results regarding approximating the symmetric
decreasing rearrangement by polarizations and Steiner
symmetrizations.

For a nonnegative measurable function
f
, the familiar definition of its Steiner
symmetrization (see
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
14
]
) is defined as following:

Definition 1
.

For a measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
let
m
denote the Lebesgue measure, if
m([f>t])<+\infty
for all

t>0
, then its Steiner symmetrization is defined as

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{u}f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{X}_{S_{u}E(t)}(x)dt,

(1.1)

where
S_{u}E(t)
is the Steiner symmetrization of the level set

E(t):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(x)>t\}
about
u^{\perp}
and
\mathcal{X}_{A}
denotes the characteristic function of set
A
.

During the study of the analogy between convex bodies and
log-concave functions, Artstein-Klartag-Milman in
[
1
]

defined another functional Steiner transformation as follows:

Definition 2
.

For a coercive convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and a hyperplane
H=u^{\bot}
(
u\in S^{n-1}
) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

, where

x^{\prime}\in H

symmetrization

\widetilde{S}_{u}f
of

\displaystyle(\widetilde{S}_{u}f)(x)=\inf_{t_{1}+t_{2}=t}[\frac{1}{2}f(x^{\prime}+2t_{1}u)+\frac{1}{2}f(x^{\prime}-2t_{2}u)].

(1.2)

In this paper, we introduce a new way of defining the
functional Steiner symmetrization for coercive convex functions.
A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, not identically
+\infty
, is called

convex
if

f(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y)\leq\alpha f(x)+(1-\alpha)f(y)

for all
x
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for

0\leq\lambda\leq 1
. A convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}

if

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}f(x)=+\infty

Definition 3
.

For
a coercive convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and a hyperplane
H=u^{\bot}
(
u\in S^{n-1}
) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
any
x=x^{\prime}+tu
, where

x^{\prime}\in H

symmetrization

\displaystyle(S_{u}f)(x)=\sup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\inf_{t_{1}+t_{2}=t}[\lambda f(x^{\prime}+2t_{1}u)+(1-\lambda)f(x^{\prime}-2t_{2}u)].

(1.3)

Our definition
S_{u}f
is motivated by and can be regarded as an
improvement of
\widetilde{S}_{u}f
in Definition
2

When compared with
\bar{S}_{u}f
in Definition

parabola-like (one-dimension) cure once at a time instead of
symmetrizing the level set as in
\bar{S}_{u}f

Blaschke-Santaló inequality for even convex functions.

For a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, its polar about

z
is defined by
K^{z}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sup_{y\in K}\langle x-z,y-z\rangle\leq 1\}
. For a log-concave function

f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, its polar about
z
is
defined by

\displaystyle f^{z}(x)=\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{e^{-\langle x-z,y-z\rangle}}{f(y)}.

(4.1)

To better understand this definition recall the classical Legendre
transform: For a function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, its Legendre transform about
z
is defined by

\mathcal{L}^{z}\phi(x)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}[\langle x-z,y-z\rangle-\phi(y)]
. From above definition of polarity, if

f(x)=e^{-\phi(x)}
, where
\phi(x)
is a convex function, then

f^{z}(x)=e^{-\mathcal{L}^{z}\phi(x)}
. Since
\mathcal{L}^{z}(\mathcal{L}^{z}\phi)=\phi
for a convex function
\phi
,

(f^{z})^{z}=f
. For
z=0
, we denote
\mathcal{L}^{0}\phi=\mathcal{L}\phi
.

For a convex body
K
, its Santaló point
s(K)
satisfies

Vol(K^{s(K)})=\min_{z}Vol(K^{z})
. The Blaschke-Santaló
inequality
[
3
,
29
]
states that
Vol(K)Vol(K^{s(K)})\leq Vol(B_{2}^{n})^{2}
, where
B_{2}^{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|\leq 1\}
is the
Euclidean ball (
|\cdot|
denote the Euclidean norm). The functional
Blaschke-Santaló inequality of log-concave functions is the
analogue of Blaschke-Santaló inequality of convex bodies. If

f
is a nonnegative integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

f^{0}
has its barycenter at
0
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{0}(y)dy\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}}dx\right)^{2}=(2\pi)^{n}.

In the special case where the function
f
is even, this result
follows from an earlier inequality of Ball
[
2
]
; and in

[
15
]
, Fradelizi and Meyer prove something more general (see
also
[
22
]
). Recently, Lehec
[
23
]
gave a direct proof
of the functional Blaschke-Santaló inequality.

In this paper, inspired by the proof of K. Ball
[
2
]
for
Santaló inequality for centrally symmetric convex bodies, we
prove functional Blaschke-Santaló inequality for even convex
functions. For the non-even case, we can prove the inequality by the
similar method, but we don’t prove it here.

Theorem 4.1
.

(K. Ball,
[
2
]
) Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be
an even convex function. Assume that
0<\int e^{-f}<\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\int e^{-f}\int e^{-\mathcal{L}f}\leq(2\pi)^{n}.

(4.2)

First, we give the following lemmas.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be an even convex function
and
u\in S^{n-1}
. Assume that
0<\int e^{-f}<\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\int e^{-\mathcal{L}f}\leq\int e^{-\mathcal{L}(S_{u}f)}.

(4.3)

Proof.

After a linear transformation, it may be supposed that

H=u^{\perp}=\{(x_{i})_{i=1}^{i=n}:x_{n}=0\}
. For
f
and

t\in\mathbb{R}
, we define a new function

f_{(t)}(x^{\prime}):=f(x^{\prime}+tu)
, where
x^{\prime}\in H
.

By the definition of Steiner symmetrization, for

x^{\prime}=x_{1}^{\prime}+x_{2}^{\prime}
, where
x^{\prime}
,

x_{1}^{\prime}
and
x_{2}^{\prime}\in H
, let
(x^{\prime},t)
denote

x^{\prime}+tu
, we have

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}(S_{u}f))_{(t)}(x^{\prime})=(\mathcal{L}(S_{u}f))(x^{\prime}+tu)

(4.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{(y^{\prime},s)\in H\times\mathbb{R}}\left[\langle(x^{\prime},t),(y^{\prime},s)\rangle-(S_{u}f)(y^{\prime}+su)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{(y^{\prime},s)\in H\times\mathbb{R}}[\langle(x^{\prime},t),(y^{\prime},s)\rangle-\sup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\inf_{s_{1}+s_{2}=s}(\lambda f(y^{\prime}+2s_{1}u)+(1-\lambda)f(y^{\prime}-2s_{2}u))]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{(y^{\prime},s)\in H\times\mathbb{R}}\inf_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\sup_{s_{1}+s_{2}=s}[\langle(x^{\prime},t),(y^{\prime},s)\rangle-(\lambda f(y^{\prime}+2s_{1}u)+(1-\lambda)f(y^{\prime}-2s_{2}u))]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{(y^{\prime},s)\in H\times\mathbb{R}}\sup_{s_{1}+s_{2}=s}[\langle(x^{\prime},t),(y^{\prime},s)\rangle-(\frac{1}{2}f(y^{\prime}+2s_{1}u)+\frac{1}{2}f(y^{\prime}-2s_{2}u))]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{(y^{\prime},s)\in H\times\mathbb{R}}\sup_{s_{1}\in\mathbb{R}}[\langle(x^{\prime},t),(y^{\prime},s)\rangle-(\frac{1}{2}f(y^{\prime}+2s_{1}u)+\frac{1}{2}f(y^{\prime}+2(s_{1}-s)u))]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sup_{(y^{\prime},s)\in H\times\mathbb{R}}\sup_{s_{1}\in\mathbb{R}}[\langle(2x_{1}^{\prime},t),(y^{\prime},2s_{1})\rangle-f(y^{\prime}+2s_{1}u)]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{(y^{\prime},s)\in H\times\mathbb{R}}\sup_{s_{1}\in\mathbb{R}}[\langle(2x_{2}^{\prime},-t),(y^{\prime},2s_{1}-2s)\rangle-f(y^{\prime}+2(s_{1}-s)u)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[(\mathcal{L}f)(2x_{1}^{\prime}+tu)+(\mathcal{L}f)(2x_{2}^{\prime}-tu)],

where the first inequality is by choosing
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
and
the second inequality is by
\sup\sup(A+B)\leq\sup\sup A+\sup\sup B
.

Since
x_{1}^{\prime}
and
x_{2}^{\prime}
are arbitrary, by
(
4.4
), we can get

\displaystyle\left(e^{-(\mathcal{L}(S_{u}f))_{(t)}}\right)(x^{\prime})\geq\sup_{x_{1}^{\prime}+x_{2}^{\prime}=x^{\prime}}\left(e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}f)_{(t)}(2x_{1}^{\prime})}\times e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}f)_{(-t)}(2x_{2}^{\prime})}\right).

(4.5)

By (
4.5
) and Prékopa-Leindler inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{H}e^{-(\mathcal{L}(S_{u}f))_{(t)}}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{H}e^{-(\mathcal{L}f)_{(t)}(x^{\prime})}dx^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{H}e^{-(\mathcal{L}f)_{(-t)}(x^{\prime})}dx^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(4.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{H}e^{-(\mathcal{L}f)_{(t)}(x^{\prime})}dx^{\prime},

where the last equality is by
\mathcal{L}f
is even (since
f
is
even). Thus, by Fubini’s theorem, we can get the desired inequality.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
h(t)
be an increasing convex function defined on
[0,+\infty)

and
\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-h(t)}dt<\infty
. Let
\mathcal{L}(h(|\cdot|))
denote the Legendre transform of function
h(|x|)

defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-h(|x|)}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-(\mathcal{L}(h(|\cdot|)))(x)}dx\leq(2\pi)^{n}.

(4.7)

Proof.

By spherical coordinate transformation, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-h(|x|)}dx=\int_{S^{n-1}}\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-h(r)}r^{n-1}dr\right]d\omega.

(4.8)

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
x=t_{x}\theta_{x}
, where

\theta_{x}=\frac{x}{|x|}\in S^{n-1}
for
|x|\neq 0
and
\theta_{x}
is
any unit vector for
|x|=0
, and
t_{x}=|x|
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(h(|\cdot|))(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\langle x,y\rangle-h(|y|))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta_{y}\in S^{n-1},t_{y}\geq 0}(\langle t_{x}\theta_{x},t_{y}\theta_{y}\rangle-h(t_{y}))=\sup_{t_{y}\geq 0}(t_{x}t_{y}-h(t_{y})).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-(\mathcal{L}(h(|\cdot|)))(x)}dx=\int_{S^{n-1}}\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(e^{-\sup_{t\geq 0}(rt-h(t))}\right)r^{n-1}dr\right]d\omega.

(4.9)

For
r\in[0,+\infty)
, let
f_{1}(r)=\left(e^{-h(r)}\right)r^{n-1}
,

f_{2}(r)=\left(e^{-\sup_{t\geq 0}(rt-h(t))}\right)r^{n-1}
and

f_{3}(r)=\left(e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}\right)r^{n-1}
. Next, we shall
prove that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}f_{1}(r)dr\int_{0}^{+\infty}f_{2}(r)dr\leq\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}f_{3}(r)dr\right)^{2}.

(4.10)

Let
g_{i}(t)=f_{i}(e^{t})e^{t}
for
i=1,2,3,
then

\int_{0}^{+\infty}f_{i}(r)dr=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g_{i}(t)dt
and for every

s,t\in\mathbb{R}
,
g_{1}(s)g_{2}(t)\leq\left(g_{3}(\frac{s+t}{2})\right)^{2}
. Hence inequality (
4.10
)
follows from Prékopa-Leindler inequality.

By (
4.8
), (
4.9
) and (
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-h(|x|)}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-(\mathcal{L}(h(|\cdot|)))(x)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{n}^{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}f_{1}(r)dr\int_{0}^{+\infty}f_{2}(r)dr\leq\omega_{n}^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{2}=(2\pi)^{n},

where
\omega_{n}=n\pi^{n/2}/\Gamma(1+\frac{n}{2})
is the surface
area of Euclidean unit ball.
∎

Computing the cohomology of constructible étale sheaves on curves
Christophe Levrat

Abstract
We present an explicit expression of the cohomology complex of a constructible sheaf of abelian groups on the small étale site of an irreducible curve over an algebraically closed field, when the torsion of the sheaf is invertible in the field. This expression only involves finite groups, and is functorial in both the curve and the sheaf. In particular, we explain how to compute the Galois action on this complex. We also present an algorithm which computes it and study its complexity.

Introduction

Let
X_{0}
be an algebraic curve over a field
k
. Let
n
be a positive integer invertible in
k_{0}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{0}
be a constructible sheaf of
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-modules on the (small) étale site of
X_{0}
. Denote by
k
a separable closure of
X_{0}
, by
X
the base change of
X_{0}
to
k
, and by
\mathcal{F}
the restriction of
\mathcal{F}_{0}
to
X
. The étale cohomology complex
\RG(X,\mathcal{F})
is equipped with an action of
\Gal(k|k_{0})
. Given the curve
X
and a suitable explicit description of the sheaf
\mathcal{F}
, we are interested in computing a finite extension
k_{1}
of
k_{0}
and a complex of
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})[\Gal(k_{1}|k_{0})]
-modules which represents
\RG(X,\mathcal{F})
.

The computability of étale cohomology groups of torsion sheaves on schemes of finite type over algebraically closed fields was proved in 2014 by Poonen, Testa and van Luijk
[
PTv15
, Th. 7.9]
in characteristic zero, and by Madore and Orgogozo in arbitrary characteristic
[
MO15
, Th. 0.1]
. However, the algorithms described in these articles are not efficient enough to be used in practice, and the only known result about their complexity is that Madore and Orgogozo’s algorithm is primitive recursive
[
Lev22
, Prop. 4.1.9]
. In the case of smooth curves, some more efficient algorithms are known.
When
X
is a smooth projective curve,
\HH^{1}(X,\mu_{n})
is canonically isomorphic to the
n
-torsion of
\Pic(X)
; two algorithms, one developed by Huang and Ierardi
[
HI98
]
, the other by Couveignes
[
Cou09
]
, compute this group when the base field
k_{0}
is finite. Jin’s algorithm
[
Jin20
]
computes
\HH^{1}(X,\mathcal{F})
where
X
is a smooth curve and
\mathcal{F}
is locally constant.

In this paper, we consider the case where
X_{0}
is an integral curve over a field, and
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is a constructible sheaf (or even a complex of such sheaves) on
X_{0}
. We present an explicit expression of the cohomology complex
\RG(X,\mathcal{F})
with the aforementioned Galois action, as well as an algorithm which computes this complex (under some classical computability assumptions on the field
k_{0}
). The latter makes use of an existing algorithm computing
\HH^{1}(X,\mu_{n})
, such as those mentioned above. We also provide complexity bounds for this method. In particular, in the case of locally constant sheaves on smooth projective curves over finite fields, the complexity of computing
\HH^{1}(X,\mathcal{F})
using this algorithm is lower than Jin’s.
In the case where the base field
k_{0}
is finite, we present an idea that should a allow to reduce the complexity of this algorithm, and explain why this would be a crucial step towards developing a polynomial-time point counting algorithm for surfaces.

In section
2
, we investigate the properties of the minimal Galois cover of a scheme trivialising the
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-torsors on this scheme, as well as its construction in the case of curves. In section
3
, we explain how to compute the cohomology of a locally constant sheaf on a scheme of cohomological dimension at most
1
. Section
4
contains the proof of the main theorem: an explicit expression of
\RG(X,-)
when
X
is a curve over a field of cohomological dimension at most
1
. We then present in section
5
the algorithms used to compute the cohomology of a constructible sheaf on such a curve, as well as their complexity. We also describe the potential application of our algorithms to point counting on surfaces over finite fields. In sections
6
and
7
, we illustrate these algorithms in two situations.

Terminology
.

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, the word
cover
denotes a finite étale map. A
Galois cover
is always supposed to be connected.

2.1
General construction and properties

Let
n
be a positive integer. We will denote by
\Lambda
the ring
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. For a (discrete)
\Lambda
-module
M
, we will denote by
M^{\vee}
its
\Lambda
-dual.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a locally compact topological group. Consider the abelian group
\Lambda
, with the trivial action of
G
. Suppose
\HH^{1}(G,\Lambda)
is finite. There is a unique closed normal subgroup
S
of
G
such that
G/S
is isomorphic to the
\Lambda
-dual
\HH^{1}(G,\Lambda)^{\vee}
of the continuous cohomology group
\HH^{1}(G,\Lambda)
. Moreover,
S
is a characteristic subgroup of
G
.

Proof.

Define
S
as the closure in
G
of
G^{n}[G,G]
. This group
S
is a characteristic subgroup of
G
because
G^{n}
and
[G,G]
are stable by automorphisms.
Since
\Lambda
is an
n
-torsion abelian group, there is a canonical isomorphism

\Hom_{\mathrm{cont}}(G/S,\Lambda)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,\Lambda)=\HH^{1}(G,\Lambda).

Pontryagin duality
[
HM20
, Th. 7.63]
applied to the locally compact abelian group
G/S
yields the following isomorphism:

\Hom_{\mathrm{cont}}(G/S,\Lambda)^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\sim}G/S.

Now let
H
be a closed normal subgroup of
G
such that
G/H
is isomorphic to
\HH^{1}(X,\Lambda)^{\vee}
. It necessarily contains
S
since
G/H
is a
\Lambda
-module.
Since
G/S
and
G/H
have the same finite cardinality,
H=S
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
X
be an integral noetherian scheme such that
\HH^{1}(X,\Lambda)
is finite. Up to isomorphism, there is a unique étale Galois cover
{X^{\langle n\rangle}}
of
X
with automorphism group isomorphic to
\HH^{1}(X,\Lambda)^{\vee}
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from
2.1
, the canonical isomorphism

\HH^{1}(\pi_{1}(X),\Lambda)\xrightarrow{\sim}\HH^{1}(X,\Lambda)

and Grothendieck-Galois theory.
∎

From now on, given such a scheme
X
, we will always denote by
{X^{\langle n\rangle}}
a Galois cover of
X
as in the previous corollary.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X
be an integral noetherian scheme such that
\HH^{1}(X,\Lambda)
is finite. For any finitely generated
\Lambda
-module
M
, the morphism
\HH^{1}(X,M)\to\HH^{1}({X^{\langle n\rangle}},M)
is trivial.

Proof.

Recall that
{X^{\langle n\rangle}}
corresponds to the open subgroup
S
of
\pi_{1}(X)
, which is the closure of the subgroup
\pi_{1}(X)^{n}[\pi_{1}(X),\pi_{1}(X)]
. Since
M
is
n
-torsion, any map
\pi_{1}(X)\to M
is trivial on
S
, hence
\Hom(\pi_{1}(X),M)\to\Hom(\pi_{1}({X^{\langle n\rangle}}),M)
is trivial.
∎

2.2
Explicit construction in the case of curves

Let
U
be a smooth integral curve over a field
k
. Denote by
K
its function field. Let
n
be a positive integer invertible in
k
. Recall the following description of
\HH^{1}(U,\mu_{n})
in terms of divisors on
U
.

Lemma 2.4
.

The group
\HH^{1}(U,\mu_{n})
is canonically isomorphic to the quotient of

\{(D,f)\in\Div(U)\times K^{\times}\mid nD=\mathrm{div}(f)\}

by the subgroup of pairs
(D,f)
with
f\in(K^{\times})^{n}
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the corresponding description in terms of invertible sheaves given in
[
Stacks
, 040Q]
.
∎

We will denote by
[D,f]
the class of the pair
(D,f)
in
\HH^{1}(U,\mu_{n})
.
Now suppose that
k
is separably closed. Let
X
be the smooth compactification of
U
. Denote by
g
the genus of
X
. Consider the closed complement
Z=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\}
of
U
in
X
.
When
r=0
the lemma above is the usual description of
\HH^{1}(X,\mu_{n})
as the group of
n
-torsion points on the Jacobian of
X
.
When
r\geqslant 1
, the Gysin sequence

0\to\HH^{1}(X,\mu_{n})\to\HH^{1}(U,\mu_{n})\to\HH^{0}(Z,\Lambda)\to\Lambda\to 0

shows that
\HH^{1}(U,\mu_{n})
is a free
\Lambda
-module of rank
2g-1+r
. Consider a basis
([D_{i},g_{i}])_{1\leqslant i\leqslant s}
of
\HH^{1}(U,\mu_{n})
. For every integer
j\in\{1\dots s\}
, we denote by
V_{j}
the normalisation of
U
in
K(\sqrt[n]{g_{1}},\dots,\sqrt[n]{g_{j}})
, by
\phi_{j}
the induced cover
U_{j}\to U
, and set
V=V_{s}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

The cover
\phi\colon V\to U
is isomorphic to
{U^{\langle n\rangle}}\to U
.

Proof.

First of all, each cover
V_{i}\to V_{i-1}
is étale, because it is constructed by taking an
n
-th root of a function whose valuation at each point is a multiple of
n
. Let us check by induction on
j\in\{1,\dots,r\}
that
V_{j}\to V_{j-1}
is Galois with group
\mu_{n}(k)
. This is obviously true for
j=1
.
For any
i\in\{1,\dots,j-1\}
the extension
V_{i}=V_{i-1}(\sqrt[n]{g_{i}})
of
V_{i-1}
is Galois with group
\mu_{n}(k)
by induction hypothesis.
The Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence yields an exact sequence

0\to\HH^{1}(\mu_{n}(k),\Lambda)\to\HH^{1}(V_{i-1},\Lambda)\to\HH^{1}(V_{i},\Lambda).

Therefore, the kernel of
\phi_{i}^{\star}\colon\HH^{1}(U,\Lambda)\to\HH^{1}(V_{i},\Lambda)
is the direct sum
\Lambda[D_{1},g_{1}]\oplus\dots\oplus\Lambda[D_{i},g_{i}]\simeq\Lambda^{i}
, and
[D_{j},g_{j}]
is not in the kernel; the order of
\phi_{i}^{\star}[D_{j},g_{j}]
in
\HH^{1}(V_{i},\mu_{n})
is still
n
.
Thus,
V\to U
is finite étale of order
n^{r}
. The field
k
being separably closed, the extension
k(V)
of
k(U)
is the splitting field of the polynomials
T^{n}-g_{1},\dots,T^{n}-g_{r}
, so it is Galois. The morphism
V\to U
is therefore an étale Galois covering. An element of the group
\Aut(V|U)
is an automorphism defined by
(\sqrt[n]{g_{1}}\mapsto\zeta_{1}\sqrt[n]{g_{1}},\dots,\sqrt[n]{g_{r}}\mapsto\zeta_{r}\sqrt[n]{g_{r}})
, where the
\zeta_{i}
are
n
-th roots of unity in
k
; the group
\Aut(V|U)
is therefore canonically isomorphic to
\Hom_{\Lambda}(\HH^{1}(U,\mu_{n}),\mu_{n})=\HH^{1}(U,\Lambda)^{\vee}
.
2.1
now ensures that
V
is isomorphic to
{U^{\langle n\rangle}}
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

We will often consider the following situation. Let
V\to U
be an étale Galois cover of smooth integral curves over
k
. The cover
{V^{\langle n\rangle}}\to U
is still Galois because
{V^{\langle n\rangle}}\to V
is characteristic. Sometimes, we will need to compute a subcover
V^{\prime}\to V
of
{V^{\langle n\rangle}}
by taking
n
-th roots of functions
g_{1},\dots,g_{t}
generating a submodule of
\HH^{1}(V,\mu_{n})
. In that case,
V^{\prime}\to U
is still Galois if and only if the submodule generated by
g_{1},\dots,g_{t}
is stable under the action of
\Aut(V|U)
. This is the case for instance if we take the submodule of elements of
\HH^{1}(V,\mu_{n})
defined over some subfield of
k
over which the elements of
\Aut(V|U)
are also defined.

. Let
V^{\prime}_{0}\to V_{0}
be the normalisation of
V_{0}
in the function field
k_{0}(\alpha,x,y)(\sqrt[n]{g^{\prime}_{1}},\dots,\sqrt[n]{g^{\prime}_{r}})
. The curve
V^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the
\Gal(L|k_{0})
-orbit of
V_{1}^{\langle n\rangle}
; it has
[L:k_{0}]
connected components, and the map
\HH^{1}(V,\Lambda)\to\HH^{1}(V^{\prime},\Lambda)
is trivial.

Note that since the degree of
V^{\prime}_{0}\to(V_{0}\times_{k_{1}}L)
is
n^{r}
and that of
V_{0}\times_{k_{1}}L\to V_{0}
is
[L:k_{1}]
, the degree of
V^{\prime}_{0}\to V_{0}
is
n^{r}[L:k_{1}]
.

Proposition 2.9
.

The cover
V^{\prime}_{0}\to V_{0}
is characteristic, i.e. if
V_{0}
is an étale Galois cover of a curve
U_{0}
, then
V_{0}^{\prime}\to U_{0}
is still Galois.

Proof.

Consider the situtation where
V_{0}
is an étale Galois cover of a smooth integral
k_{0}
-curve
U_{0}
. Here is how to explicitly compute the automorphism group of
V_{0}\to U_{0}
.
The map
V^{\prime}_{0}\to V_{0}
has degree
n^{r}[L:k_{1}]
. Using the notations above, set
z_{i}=\sqrt[n]{g^{\prime}_{i}}
. The elements of
\Aut(k_{0}(V^{\prime}_{0})|k_{0}(V_{0}))
are defined by
t\mapsto\sigma(t),z_{i}\mapsto\zeta_{i}z_{i}
where
\sigma\in\Gal(L|k_{1})
and
\zeta_{i}\in\mu_{n}(L)
. There are
\deg(V^{\prime}_{0}\to V_{0})=n^{r}[L:k_{1}]
such automorphisms since
\mu_{n}(k)\subset L
, hence the cover
V^{\prime}_{0}\to V_{0}
is Galois.
Let us now compute the automorphism group of
V^{\prime}_{0}\to U_{0}
. Its elements are defined by

(t,x,y,z_{1},\dots,z_{r})\mapsto(\sigma(t),x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}_{1},\dots,z^{\prime}_{r})

where
\sigma\in\Gal(L|k_{0})
, the pair
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
is the image of
(x,y)
under a
U_{0}
-automorphism of
V_{0}
whose image in
\Gal(k_{1}|k_{0})
is the same as that of
\sigma
, and the elements
z^{\prime}_{i}\in k_{0}(V^{\prime}_{0})
satisfy
{z^{\prime}_{i}}^{n}=\phi(g^{\prime}_{i})
.
As expected, there are
\deg(V^{\prime}_{0}|U_{0})=n^{r}[L:k_{1}]\deg(V_{0}\to U_{0})
elements in
\Aut(V^{\prime}_{0}|U_{0})
, and
V^{\prime}_{0}\to U_{0}
is Galois.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

If need be, the Galois extension
L
of
k_{0}
may be chosen to be a little larger, for instance to make sure that the points at infinity of the curve
V^{\prime}
are defined over
L
. This does not affect any of the previous proofs.

3
Schemes of cohomological dimension 1

Let
n
be a positive integer, and
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. Let
X
be an integral noetherian scheme, and
{\bar{\eta}}
be a generic geometric point of
X
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{L}
be a finite locally constant sheaf of
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-modules on
X
. Let
Y\to X
be an étale Galois cover such that the morphism

\HH^{1}(X,\mathscr{L})\to\HH^{1}(Y,\mathscr{L}|_{Y})

is trivial. Then the morphism

\tau_{\leqslant 1}\RG(\Aut(Y|X),\mathscr{L}_{{\bar{\eta}}})\to\tau_{\leqslant 1}\RG(X,\mathscr{L})

is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
G
be the automorphism group of
Y\to X
. The associated Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence yields the following short exact sequence:

0\to\HH^{1}(G,\mathscr{L}_{{\bar{\eta}}})\to\HH^{1}(X,\mathscr{L})\to\HH^{0}(G,\HH^{1}(Y,\mathscr{L}|_{Y}))

Hence the map
\HH^{1}(G,\mathscr{L}_{{\bar{\eta}}})\to\HH^{1}(X,\mathscr{L})
is an isomorphism, and

\RG(G,\mathscr{L}_{{\bar{\eta}}})\to\RG(X,\mathscr{L})

yields isomorphisms on cohomology groups in degree 0 and 1.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

If in addition
X
has cohomological dimension 1 then

\tau_{\leqslant 1}\RG(\Aut(Y|X),\mathscr{L}_{{\bar{\eta}}})\to\RG(X,\mathscr{L})

is a quasi-isomorphism.

Remark 3.3
.

Here is how to construct a cover
Y
as in the proposition. Pick an étale Galois cover
W\to X
such that
\mathscr{L}|_{W}
is a constant sheaf. If
\HH^{1}(W,\Lambda)
is finite, set
Y=W^{\langle n\rangle}
: since
Y\to W
is characteristic, the cover
Y\to X
is still Galois, and
2.3
ensures that
\HH^{1}(X,\mathscr{L})\to\HH^{1}(Y,\mathscr{L}|_{Y})
is trivial.

Given a profinite group
H
, we will denote by
P_{H}(\Lambda)
the usual projective resolution (sometimes called
bar resolution
) of the trivial
\Lambda[[H]]
-module
\Lambda
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose
X
is of cohomological dimension 1. Let
\mathscr{L}=[\mathscr{L}^{0}\to\mathscr{L}^{1}\to\dots\to\mathscr{L}^{s}]
be a complex of finite locally constant sheaves of
\Lambda
-modules on
X
, and
Y
be an étale Galois cover of
X
such that each map
\HH^{1}(X,\mathscr{L}^{i})\to\HH^{1}(Y,\mathscr{L}^{i}|_{Y})
is trivial. Write
G=\Aut(Y|X)
. Consider the double complex
B^{\bullet,\bullet}
defined by
B^{i,j}=\Hom_{\Lambda[G]}(\tau_{\geqslant-1}P_{G}^{-j}(\Lambda),\mathscr{L}_{\bar{\eta}}^{i})
. Then
\RG(X,\mathscr{L})
is represented by the total complex
\Tot B^{\bullet,\bullet}
.

Proof.

We wish to compute
\RG(X,\mathscr{L})=\RG(\pi_{1}(X),\mathscr{L}_{\bar{\eta}})=\RHom_{\Lambda[[\pi_{1}(X)]]}(\Lambda,\mathscr{L}_{\bar{\eta}})
, which is represented by the complex

\Hom^{\bullet}_{\Lambda[[\pi_{1}(X)]]}(P_{\pi}(\Lambda),\mathscr{L}_{\bar{\eta}})=\Tot(A^{\bullet,\bullet})

where
A^{i,j}=\Hom_{\Lambda[[\pi_{1}(X)]]}(P_{\pi_{1}(X)}^{-j}(\Lambda),\mathscr{L}_{\bar{\eta}}^{i})
.
The map
B^{\bullet,\bullet}\to A^{\bullet,\bullet}
induced by the quotient map
\pi_{1}(X)\to G
defines a morphism between the spectral sequence associated to these quotients.
Recall that for each
i\in\{1\dots s\}
, the map
\tau_{\leqslant 1}\Hom_{\Lambda[G]}(P_{G}(\Lambda),\mathscr{L}^{i}_{\bar{\eta}})\to\Hom_{\Lambda[[\pi_{1}(X)]]}(P_{\pi_{1}(X)}(\Lambda),\mathscr{L}^{i}_{\bar{\eta}})
is a quasi-isomorphism.
Since the functor
\Hom(-,\mathscr{L}^{i}_{\bar{\eta}})
is left exact,
\Hom_{\Lambda[G]}(\tau_{\geqslant-1}P_{G}(\Lambda),\mathscr{L}^{i}_{\bar{\eta}})=\tau_{\leqslant 1}\Hom_{\Lambda[H]}(P_{G}(\Lambda),\mathscr{L}^{i}_{\bar{\eta}})
.
Hence the map
B^{\bullet,\bullet}\to A^{\bullet,\bullet}
defines, on each column, a quasi-isomorphism of complexes. It therefore induces an isomorphism between the first pages of the corresponding spectral sequences (for the upward orientation) associated to
B^{\bullet,\bullet}
and
A^{\bullet,\bullet}
: in position
(i,j)
, it is the isomorphism
\HH^{j}(H,\mathscr{L}_{\bar{\eta}}^{i})\to\HH^{j}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathscr{L}_{\bar{\eta}}^{i})
for
j\leqslant 1
, and
0\to\HH^{j}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathscr{L}_{\bar{\eta}}^{i})=0
otherwise. Therefore, the map between the abutments of these two spectral sequences is an isomorphism, i.e. the map
\Tot(B^{\bullet,\bullet})\to\Tot(A^{\bullet,\bullet})
is a quasi-isomorphism.
∎

4
Explicit computation of
\RG
of a (possibly singular) curve

In this section, we are going to describe how to compute the cohomology of a complex of constructible sheaves on a curve. Let
k_{0}
be a field, and
k
be a separable closure of
k_{0}
. Consider a geometrically irreducible curve
X_{0}
over
k_{0}
, and its base change
X
over
k
. We are allowed to make the following additional assumptions, which do not alter the computed cohomology complex.

•

The field
k_{0}
is perfect, hence
k
is algebraically closed: the perfect closure
k_{0}^{\rm pf}
of
k_{0}
being a purely inseparable extension, the base change
-\times_{k_{0}}k_{0}^{\rm pf}
induces an isomorphism on cohomology.

•

The curve
X
is reduced: being a universal homeomorphism, the map
X_{\rm red}\to X
induces an isomorphism on cohomology.

•

The curve
X
has at worst ordinary (also called multicross, see
[
Stacks
, 0C1P]
) singularities: the seminormalisation map
X^{\rm sn}\to X
being a universal homeomorphism
[
Stacks
, 0EUS]
, it induces an isomorphism on cohomology, so we may assume
X
is seminormal. Since a seminormal curve over an algebraically closed field has at worst ordinary singularities
[
Dav78
, §2, Cor. 1]
, we may assume this is the case for
X
.

4.1
Cohomology with support in a 0-dimensional subscheme

Let
k
be a algebraically closed field. Let
n
be an integer invertible in
k
, and
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. Let
X
be an integral curve over
k
. Consider a nonempty zero-dimensional subscheme

, and its open complement

. Let
\RG_{Z}(X,-)
denote cohomology with support in

. Since
\RG_{Z}(X,-)=\oplus_{z\in Z}\RG_{z}(X,-)
, we consider a single point
z\in Z
and focus on computing
\RG_{z}(X,-)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{L}
be a finite locally constant sheaf on
U
.

\bullet

\HH^{0}_{z}(X,j_{!}\mathscr{L})=\HH^{0}_{z}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})=\HH^{1}_{z}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})=0

\bullet

\HH^{1}_{z}(X,j_{!}\mathscr{L})=\HH^{0}(z,\mathscr{L})

\bullet

\HH^{2}_{z}(X,j_{!}\mathscr{L})=\HH^{2}_{z}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})

\bullet

For all
i\geqslant 3
,
\HH^{i}_{z}(X,j_{!}\mathscr{L})=\HH^{i}_{z}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})=0
.

Proof.

Since
\HH^{0}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})\to\HH^{0}(X-z,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})
is an isomorphism and
\HH^{1}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})\to\HH^{1}(X-z,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})
is always a monomorphism, the long exact sequence for cohomology with support associated to
j_{\star}\mathscr{L}
shows that
\HH^{0}_{z}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})=\HH^{1}_{z}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})=0
.
Moreover, for any
j\geqslant 3
, the groups
\HH^{j-1}(X-z,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})
and
\HH^{j}(X,\mathscr{L})
are trivial, hence
\HH^{j}_{z}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})=0
.
Recall that
\HH^{i}_{z}(X,i_{\star}-)=\HH^{i}(z,-)
. The long exact sequence of
\HH^{i}_{z}(X,-)
associated to the short exact sequence

0\to j_{!}\mathscr{L}\to j_{\star}\mathscr{L}\to i_{\star}i^{\star}j_{\star}\mathscr{L}\to 0

shows that
\HH^{0}_{z}(X,j_{!}\mathscr{L})=0
,
\HH^{1}_{z}(X,j_{!}\mathscr{L})=\HH^{0}(Z,\mathscr{L})
and
\HH^{2}_{z}(X,j_{!}\mathscr{L})=\HH^{2}_{z}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})
.
The groups
\HH^{i}_{z}(X,j_{!}\mathscr{L})
are also trivial as soon as
i\geqslant 3
.
∎

Let us now compute the group
\HH^{2}_{z}(X,j_{!}\mathscr{L})=\HH^{2}_{z}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})
. From now on, we assume
X
to have only ordinary singularities. Let
z_{1},\dots,z_{r}
be the preimages of
z
in the normalisation
{\tilde{X}}
of
X
. Denote by
X_{\bar{z}}
the strict henselisation of
X
at
z
. It contains one closed point
z^{\prime}
, as well as
r
minimal primes
z^{\prime}_{1},\dots,z^{\prime}_{r}
. Set
U_{\bar{z}}\coloneqq U\times_{X}X_{\bar{z}}
. Consider the following cartesian diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{\bar{z}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{\bar{z}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces z^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}

The following proof is given in
[
Mil06
, II, Prop. 1.1]
in the special case of smooth curves.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a sheaf on
U_{\bar{z}}
. For every positive integer

, the group
\HH^{q}(X_{\bar{z}},j^{\prime}_{!}\mathcal{F})
is trivial.

Proof.

The assertion holds for

since
\HH^{0}(X_{\bar{z}},j^{\prime}_{!}\mathcal{F})
is the kernel of the map
\HH^{0}(X_{\bar{z}},j^{\prime}_{\star}\mathcal{F})\to\HH^{0}(X_{\bar{z}},i^{\prime}_{\star}i^{\prime\star}j_{\star}\mathcal{F})
, which is simply the identity map of
\HH^{0}(U_{\bar{z}},\mathcal{F})
. Let us first show that for any injective sheaf
J
on
U_{\bar{z}}
, the sheaf
j^{\prime}_{!}\mathcal{F}
on
X
is acyclic. To this end, we are going to prove that

0\to j^{\prime}_{!}J\to j^{\prime}_{\star}J\to i^{\prime}_{\star}i^{\prime\star}j^{\prime}_{\star}J\to 0

is an injective resolution of
j^{\prime}_{!}J
;
the long exact sequence associated to this short exact sequence then shows that
\HH^{q}(X_{\bar{z}},j^{\prime}_{\star}J)=0
for any

.
Fix separable closures of

, and denote by

the associated Galois groups. The functor

may be rewritten as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Mod_{I_{1}}\times\dots\times\Mod_{I_{r}}&\to&\Ab\\
(M_{1},\dots,M_{r})&\mapsto&M_{1}^{I_{1}}\times\dots\times M_{r}^{I_{r}}\end{array}

This functor admits a left adjoint, which sends an abelian group
M
to
(M,\dots,M)
with trivial

-action. Since this left adjoint is exact,
i^{\prime\star}j^{\prime}_{\star}
sends injectives to injectives. The functors

and

also send injectives to injectives, therefore the short exact sequence above is an injective resolution of

.
We are now ready to prove the result. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on

, and let

be an injetive resolution of

. Then
j^{\prime}_{!}J^{\bullet}
is an acyclic resolution of

, and
\HH^{q}(X_{\bar{z}},j^{\prime}_{!}\mathcal{F})=\HH^{q}(\Gamma(X_{\bar{z}},j^{\prime}_{!}J^{\bullet}))

under the

Let

be the normalisation map. Set
\tilde{z}\coloneqq z\times_{X}{\tilde{X}}
. Denote by

the generic points of the strict henselisations of

at the preimages

. The normalisation of

is an isomorphism.

.

Let
\mathscr{L}
be a finite locally constant sheaf on

. The map

\RG_{z}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})\to\RG_{\tilde{z}}({\tilde{X}},\nu^{\star}j_{\star}\mathscr{L})

is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof.

4.1
shows that it suffices to prove that

\HH^{2}_{z}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})\to\HH^{2}_{\tilde{z}}({\tilde{X}},\nu^{\star}j_{\star}\mathscr{L})=\HH^{2}_{z_{1}}({\tilde{X}},\nu^{\star}j_{\star}\mathscr{L})\times\dots\times\HH^{2}_{z_{r}}({\tilde{X}},\nu^{\star}j_{\star}\mathscr{L})

Let us compute
\HH^{2}_{z}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})
. By excision, there is a canonical isomorphism

\HH^{2}_{z}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})\xrightarrow{\sim}\HH^{2}_{z^{\prime}}(X_{\bar{z}},g^{\star}j_{!}\mathscr{L})=\HH^{2}_{z^{\prime}}(X_{\bar{z}},j^{\prime}_{!}g^{\prime\star}\mathscr{L}).

The long exact sequence in cohomology with support on

for the sheaf

reads:

\HH^{1}(X_{\bar{z}},j^{\prime}_{!}g^{\prime\star}\mathscr{L})\to\HH^{1}(U_{\bar{z}},g^{\prime\star}\mathscr{L})\to\HH^{2}_{z^{\prime}}(X_{\bar{z}},j^{\prime}_{!}g^{\prime\star}\mathscr{L})\to\HH^{2}(X_{\bar{z}},j^{\prime}_{!}g^{\prime\star}\mathscr{L}).

ensures that the map

, the group

. On the other hand, for

Corollary 4.4
.

For each point
z\in Z\times_{X}{\tilde{X}}
, choose a place of

above

and denote by

\RG_{Z}(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})
.

Proof.

This is merely rephrasing

.
∎

4.2
Cohomology of constructible sheaves

be an algebraically closed field. Let

be an integer invertible in

5
Algorithmic aspects

5.1
Representing curves and sheaves

Representing curves

A smooth projective curve over a field
k_{0}
is defined by a (possibly singular) plane model given by a polynomial in two variables. A closed subscheme of such a curve is defined by equations; an open subscheme is defined by its closed complement. A morphism of smooth curves is given by a morphism of plane models, i.e. by two polynomials in two variables.
A curve
X
with ordinary singularities is defined by its normalisation
{\tilde{X}}
, as well as the subsets of points of
{\tilde{X}}
that have the same singular image in
X
.

Representing sheaves

Let
X
be a smooth curve over an algebraically closed fied
k
. A constructible sheaf of abelian groups on the étale site of
X
will be described by the following gluing data:

\bullet

A closed 0-dimensional subscheme
Z
of
X
, defined by an equation;

\bullet

A finite locally constant sheaf
\mathscr{L}
on the open complement
U
of
Z
, defined by a Galois cover
V\to U
and the action of the group
G=\Aut(V|U)
on the
\Lambda
-module
M=\HH^{0}(V,\mathscr{L})
;

\bullet

For each point
z\in Z
, the
\Lambda
-module
\mathcal{F}_{z}
defined by generators and relations;

\bullet

For each point
z\in Z
, the gluing morphism
\phi_{z}\colon\mathcal{F}_{z}\to(j_{\star}\mathscr{L})_{z}=\HH^{0}(I_{z},M)
, where
I_{z}\subset G
is the stabiliser of a preimage of
z
in
V
.

This data also allows to represent morphisms and direct sums of sheaves in a very straightforward manner, as well as to compute tensors products and
\Hom
-sheaves; see
[
Lev22
, §III.4]
for more details. While this representation of constructible sheaves might not be the first that comes to mind, it is well suited to our computation of cohomology groups. The usual ways of defining constructible sheaves (as cokernel of
f_{!}\Lambda\to g_{!}\Lambda
with
f,g
étale or kernel of
f_{\star}\Lambda\to g_{\star}\Lambda
with
f,g

[
Lev22
, §III.3]
).

5.2
Computing the cohomology of
\mu_{n}
: existing algorithms

Our methods rely on existing algorithms which, given a smooth integral curve
X
over an algebraically closed field
k
, compute
\HH^{1}(X,\mu_{n})
. Recall that we need to be able to compute it even for affine curves, which can prove to be a bit trickier than in the projective case.

The most efficient algorithm computing
\HH^{1}(X,\mu_{n})
, developed by Couveignes, only applies to projective curves over finite fields, and actually requires prior knowledge of the characteristic polynomial of the Frobenius endomorphism of
X
; since it makes use of some properties of the Frobenius and the group structure of
\Pic^{0}(X)[n]
, adapting it to the cohomology of affine curves, or of curves over other types of fields does not seem easy. Given a curve of genus
g
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, described by an ordinary plane model of degree
d
, it computes
\Pic^{0}(X)(\mathbb{F}_{q})[n]
in time polynomial in
d,g,\log q,n

[
Cou09
, Th. 1]
.

While it was also first described only for projective curves over finite fields, Huang and Ierardi’s method
[
HI98
]
applies to more general settings. Their algorithm constructs an affine scheme whose points correspond divisors whose
n
-th power is the divisor of a rational function, and then finds a point in each irreducible component of this scheme, which is enough to find a representative of every
n
-torsion class in
\Pic^{0}(X)
. This strategy readily adapts to the computation of division by
n
in

Let

\ell

The Lefschetz theorem reduces this question to computing the trace of the Frobenius on
\HH^{i}(X,\mathbb{F}_{\ell})

[

[

that this strategy might allow to compute
|X(k_{0})|
in time polynomial in
\log(q)
. Here is where we stand on this conjecture.
The sheaf
\mathcal{F}\coloneqq{\rm R}^{1}\pi_{\star}\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
is a constructible sheaf on the projective line. It is locally constant on the open subset
U
of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
over which the fibres of
\pi
are smooth curves. For

\RG(X_{z},\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. Moreover, given an explicit description of
\mathcal{F}
, we know how to compute
\RG(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{F})
. Denote by

the generic fibre of
\pi
, and by

its genus. A trivialising cover

of
\mathcal{F}|_{U}
is given by the normalisation of

in an extension of
K
of
k(t)
over which
\Pic(X_{{\bar{\eta}}})[\ell]
is defined. The following data helps us estimate the complexity we need for the different steps of the algorithms:

•

the degree
[K:k(t)]
is smaller than
\ell^{4g_{\bar{\eta}}^{2}}
;

•

the number

\pi
and not on

;

•

of

.

Computing the whole cover

For the moment, this is nothing more than wishful thinking: all existing algorithms to compute

, even for projective curves, have complexity exponential either in
\log(q)
or in the genus of

. However, there is some hope. Harvey’s algorithm

, which computes the zeta function of hyperelliptic curves, reaches an average polynomial-time complexity. Combined with Couveignes’ algorithm, this allows for an average polynomial-time complexity for the computation of

in the case where
V
is a (projective) hyperelliptic curve.

Computation of

The group

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Proj k[y,z,t,h]/(z^{2}-(y^{2}-h^{2}),t^{2}-(y^{2}+h^{2}))&\longrightarrow&\Proj k[y,h]\\

Computation of

\sigma_{2}\colon z\mapsto-z

\gamma_{2}=\sigma_{3}\gamma

6.3
Cohomology of a locally constant sheaf

The sheaf
\mathcal{F}\coloneqq f_{\star}\Lambda
is locally constant on
U
, trivialised by the covering
f\colon V\to U
since
f^{\star}f_{\star}\Lambda\simeq\Lambda^{2}
. It corresponds to the
\Aut(V|U)
-module
\Lambda^{2}
, where the non-trivial element of
\Aut(V|U)
exchanges the two copies of
\Lambda
. Since
f
is finite,
{\rm R}f_{\star}\Lambda=(f_{\star}\Lambda)[0]
and there is a canonical isomorphism

\RG(U,f_{\star}\Lambda)=\RG(V,\Lambda).

We therefore expect to find

\HH^{1}(U,\mathcal{F})=\HH^{1}(V,\Lambda)\simeq\Lambda^{3}.

Computing
\RG(U,\mathcal{F})

We know that
\RG(U,\mathcal{F})
is represented by the following two-term complex:

\Lambda^{2}\to\Homcr(G,\Lambda^{2}).

A crossed homomorphism
f\colon G\to\Lambda^{2}
is determined by the images of
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3},\gamma
. Using the relations
\gamma\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{1}\gamma,\gamma\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{3}\gamma
and
\gamma^{2}=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}
, we see that such a map is uniquely determined by a tuple
(a,a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in\Lambda^{4}
; the corresponding map
f
is defined by
f(\sigma_{1})=(a_{1},a_{1})
,
f(\sigma_{2})=(a_{2},a_{3})
,
f(\sigma_{3})=(a_{3},a_{2})
and
f(\gamma)=(a,a+a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3})
. The principal crossed homomorphisms correspond to
(0,0,0,0)
and
(1,0,0,0)
. Hence the complex above is isomorphic to

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Lambda^{2}&\longrightarrow&\Lambda^{4}\\
(a,b)&\longmapsto&(a+b,0,0,0)\end{array}

and its cohomology groups are
\Lambda
et
\Lambda^{3}
, as expected.

Computing the Galois action

The action of
\mathfrak{G}_{0}=\Gal(k|k_{0})
on
\Aut(Y|X)
clearly factors through the quotient
\Gal(k_{0}(\sqrt{-1})|k_{0})
. The latter group is generated by
\phi\colon\sqrt{-1}\mapsto-\sqrt{-1}
. The automorphism
\phi
acts trivially on
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}
, and
\phi\cdot\gamma=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}\gamma\colon(y,z,t)\mapsto(-\sqrt{-1}y,-\sqrt{-1}z,-\sqrt{-1}t)
. The action of
\phi
on
\Lambda^{2}
is trivial, and its action on
\Homcr(G,\Lambda^{2})\simeq\Lambda^{4}
is
(\phi\cdot f)(x)=\phi f(\phi^{-1}x)=f(\phi^{-1}x)
. Explicitly, since
\phi\cdot\gamma=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}\gamma
, this action is given by

\phi\cdot(a,a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})=(a+a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}).

6.4
Cohomology of a constructible sheaf

We still consider a field
k_{0}
of odd characteristic, where
-1
is not a square. Define
k_{1}=k_{0}(\sqrt{-1})
.
Let us now consider the ramified cover of projective curves
\bar{f}\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
defined by
f
, and the sheaf
\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\bar{f}_{\star}\Lambda
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Since
\bar{f}
is unramified outside
0,\infty
, the sheaf
\mathcal{F}
is locally constant on the open subset
\mathbb{G}_{m}=\mathbb{P}^{1}-\{0,\infty\}
, and
\mathscr{L}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}|_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
is trivialised by
\bar{f}|_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{G}_{m},y\mapsto y^{2}
. The covering
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\langle n\rangle}
is also
\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{G}_{m},z\mapsto z^{2}
, and the composite

\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\langle n\rangle}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}\xrightarrow{f}\mathbb{G}_{m}

is given by
z\mapsto z^{4}
. Its automorphism group is
G=\langle\gamma\colon z\mapsto\sqrt{-1}z\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
, and the inertia subgroups at
0
and
\infty
are both equal to
G
. Denote by
j
the inclusion
\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We have
(j_{\star}\mathscr{L})_{0}=\Lambda
and
(j_{\star}\mathscr{L})_{\infty}=\Lambda
.
The adjunction units
\mathcal{F}_{0}\to(j_{\star}\mathscr{L})_{0}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}\to(j_{\star}\mathscr{L})_{\infty}
are the identity maps
\Lambda\to\Lambda
.

Computing
\RG(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathscr{L})

The crossed homomorphisms
G\to M=\mathscr{L}_{{\bar{\eta}}}\simeq\Lambda^{2}
are uniquely determined by the image of
(a,b)\in\Lambda^{2}
under
\gamma

is the following.

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Lambda^{2}&\longrightarrow&\Homcr(G,\Lambda^{2})\\
(a,b)&\longmapsto&\left[\gamma\mapsto(a+b,a+b)\right]\end{array}

Therefore
\HH^{1}(G,M)
is isomorphic to
\Lambda
, and the kernel of the map
\Lambda^{2}\to\HH^{1}(G,M)
sending a crossed homomorphism to its cohomology class is
\langle(1,1)\rangle
; this map can be rewritten as

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Lambda^{2}&\longrightarrow&\Lambda\\
(a,b)&\longmapsto&a+b.\end{array}

Computing
\RG(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})

The element
\RG(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})[1]\in\DD^{b}_{c}(X,\Lambda)
is the cone of

\tau_{\leqslant 1}\RG(G,M)\to\HH^{1}(I_{0},M)[-1]\oplus\HH^{1}(I_{\infty},M)[-1]=\Lambda^{2}[-1].

Therefore,
\RG(X,j_{\star}\mathscr{L})
is represented by the complex

\Lambda^{2}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{2}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{2}

where both morphisms are given by
(a,b)\mapsto(a+b,a+b)
.

\RG(X,\mathcal{F})

7
Second example: sheaves on subschemes of an elliptic curve

The examples in this section illustrate a non-trivial case in which we can easily compute the cohomology of sheaves using only functions that are already available in current computer algebra systems: when the locally constant part of the sheaf is trivialised by a subscheme of a hyperelliptic curve.

7.1
The covering

Consider the finite field
k_{0}=\mathbb{F}_{11}
, and the integer
n=2
invertible in
k_{0}
. Consider the field extension
\mathbb{F}_{121}=\mathbb{F}_{11}(a)
, where
a
generates the cyclic group
\mathbb{F}_{121}^{\times}
and
a^{2}+7a+2=0
. Denote by
\bar{E}
the elliptic curve over
k=\overline{\mathbb{F}_{11}}
defined by the affine Weierstrass equation
y^{2}=(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)
.
Let
\bar{C}
be the genus 2 curve over
k
given by the affine equation
y^{2}=(x^{2}-1)(x^{2}-2)(x^{2}-3)
. The curve
\bar{C}
has two points at infinity
\infty_{+},\infty_{-}
which do not lie on the affine open defined by this equation. Consider the degree 2 covering
\bar{f}\colon\bar{C}\to\bar{E}
given by
(x,y)\mapsto(x^{2},y)
. It is ramified at the affine points
P=(0,4)
and
Q=(0,7)
of
\bar{C}
, whose images in
E
are respectively
(0,4)
and
(0,7)
. Denote by
C=\bar{C}-\{P,Q\}
and
E=\bar{E}-\{\bar{f}(P),\bar{f}(Q)\}
the affine curves obtained from
\bar{C}
and
\bar{E}
by removing the ramification locus. Denote by
f\colon C\to E
the étale Galois covering induce by
\bar{f}
. Both curves
C
and
E
are obtained by base change from curves
C_{0}
and
E_{0}
defined over
k_{0}=\mathbb{F}_{11}
.

Computing the Galois group of
C^{\langle n\rangle}\to E
will allow us to determine the cohomology of any locally constant sheaf on
E
trivialised by
f
. First, we need to compute
\HH^{1}(C,\mu_{2})
. This is where we need to cheat a little since no algorithm performing this computation for a general curve has been implemented yet; fortunately,
\bar{C}
being a genus two curve, we have other means of finding a generating set for this group.

Computing
\HH^{1}(C,\mu_{2})

Denote by

P_{1}^{\pm}=(\pm 1,0),\qquad P_{2}^{\pm}=(\pm a^{6},0),\qquad P_{3}^{\pm}=(\pm 5,0)

the points of
C
with
y
-coordinate 0. A basis of
\Jac(\bar{C})[2]
is given by the classes of the divisors

D_{1}\coloneqq P_{1}^{+}-P_{1}^{-},\qquad D_{2}\coloneqq P_{2}^{+}-P_{2}^{-},\qquad D_{3}\coloneqq P_{2}^{+}-P_{3}^{+},\qquad D_{4}\coloneqq P_{1}^{+}-P_{3}^{-}.

The rational functions

f_{1}\coloneqq\frac{x-1}{x+1},\qquad f_{2}\coloneqq\frac{x-a^{6}}{x+a^{6}},\qquad f_{3}\coloneqq\frac{x-a^{6}}{x-5},\qquad f_{4}\coloneqq\frac{x-1}{x+5}

all satisfy
2D_{i}=\mathrm{div}(f_{i})
. Denote by
D_{5}
the divisor
P-Q
, which is linearly equivalent to two times the divisor

\bar{D}_{5}\coloneqq(a^{41},a^{29})+(-a^{41},a^{29})-(\infty_{+}+\infty_{-}).

We found this divisor
\bar{D}_{5}
using a brute-force search on
\Jac(\bar{C})(\mathbb{F}_{121})
; for hyperelliptic curves such as
\bar{C}
, there should be a more elegant way of doing this using division polynomials (see e.g.
[
Eid23
, Theorem C]
). The divisor of the rational function

f_{5}=\frac{y+a^{8}x^{2}+7}{x}

is
2\bar{D}_{5}-D_{5}
. Therefore, an
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-basis of

is given by

(D_{1},f_{1}),\dots,(D_{4},f_{4}),(\bar{D}_{5},f_{5}).

Computing

The covering

with group

is defined by its function field

where

. Recall that we never need to compute a smooth model of

. For reasons explained in section

, we choose to replace

with

, which still yields the same function field.
The group

of the generator

of

. First, we compute the divisors

\sigma^{\star}D_{4}&=&D_{2}+D_{4}+\mathrm{div}(h_{4})&\text{where}&h_{4}=\frac{y}{x^{3}+a^{80}x^{2}+a^{103}x+a^{114}}\\

,

and

. Since

is a square root of

in

is a preimage of

. In particular, since

, the automorphism

is given by

Hence

and the order of

as an element of

is 4. Furthermore,

et

.

. Its commutator subgroup is

on

, which can be done very easily since the elements of

are defined by their action on coordinates of points. Predictably,

acts trivially on the

; it also sends

to

7.2
Ramification

Here is how to compute the preimages of the points
P
and
Q
in
C^{\langle n\rangle}
. We have computed its function field as
k(C^{\langle n\rangle})=k(C)(z_{1}^{2}-f_{1},\dots,z_{5}^{2}-f_{5})

C^{\langle n\rangle}
. For each

P
and
Q

\begin{array}[]{rl}\Spec k[x,y,z_{1},\dots,z_{5}]/&(y^{2}-(x^{2}-1)(x^{2}-2)(x^{2}-3),\\

&(x-5)z_{3}^{2}-(x-a^{6}),\\

Octagon with finite bridge: free fermions and determinant identities
Ivan Kostov, and Valentina B. Petkova

Abstract
We continue the study of the octagon form factor which helps to evaluate a class of four-point correlation functions in 𝒩 = 4 {\cal N}=4 SYM theory. The octagon is characterised, besides the kinematical parameters, by a “bridge” of ℓ \ell propagators connecting two non-adjacent operators. In this paper we construct an operator representation of the octagon with finite bridge as an expectation value in the Fock space of free complex fermions. The bridge ℓ \ell appears as the level of filling of the Dirac sea. We obtain determinant identities relating octagons with different bridges, which we derive from the expression of the octagon in terms of discrete fermionic oscillators. The derivation is based on the existence of a previously conjectured similarity transformation, which we find here explicitly.

Introduction

A new non-perturbative approach for the computation of the
correlation functions of single-trace operators in the
{\cal N}=4

supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory has been developed in the past five
years

;

to decompose the correlation function into elementary blocks called
hexagon form factors, or shortly hexagons, which are almost uniquely
determined by the huge symmetry of the theory. Being formulated in
terms if infinite-volume form factors, the prescription involves
divergencies and, in spite of some important progress
BGK
, it
still awaits an appropriate regularisation procedure.

Remarkably, a class of four-point functions of half-BPS operators with
large R-charges and specially tuned polarisations, discovered in

Coronado:2018ypq
;
Coronado:2018cxj

and can be evaluated exactly for any value of the ’t Hooft coupling.
In these correlation functions the hexagons couple only pairwise. The
composite form factors representing two paired hexagons, named

, completely factorise. The factorisation was shown to take
place in all orders of the
1/N_{c}
expansion

\ell

The two constituent hexagons are bound by exchanging virtual particles

Belitsky:2019fan
;
Belitsky:2020qrm

Belitsky:2020qrm
;
Belitsky:2020qir

The Fock space for the complex fermions is a direct sum of sectors

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathbb{O}}_{\ell}&=\langle\,\ell|\exp[{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\psi\mathbf{K}\psi]\ \exp[-{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\psi^{*}\mathbf{C}\psi^{*}]|\ell\,\rangle\,,\end{aligned}

(1)

The Fock-space realisation (

The octagon from free fermions

2.1
The sum over virtual particles as a Coulomb gas

The role of this subsection is to remind the notations and make the
presentation self-consistent. The octagon
{\mathbb{O}}_{\ell}={\mathbb{O}}_{\ell}(z,\bar{z},\alpha,\bar{\alpha})
is characterised by four points
x_{1},...,x_{4}
in
the Euclidean space and four polarisations
y_{1},...,y_{4}
, as well as
by the length
\ell
of the bridge separating the two hexagons which
should be crossed by the virtual particles. The bridge summarises the
effect of a stack of
\ell
tree-level propagators connecting the
operators
{\mathcal{O}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{4}
. The octagon is also a function of the

R

the conformal symmetry, the dependence on
x_{i},y_{i}
is only through
the cross ratios in the coordinate and in the flavour spaces

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} z\bar{z}&={x_{12}^{2}x_{34}^{2}\over x_{13}^{2}x_{24}^{2}},\ \qquad(1-z)(1-\bar{z})={x_{14}^{2}x_{23}^{2}\over x_{13}^{2}x_{24}^{2}},\\

(9)

where

For the cross ratios in the Euclidean space we adopt the exponential

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} z&={e^{-\xi+i\phi}},\quad\bar{z}={e^{-\xi-i\phi}},\quad\alpha=e^{\varphi-\xi+i\theta},\quad\bar{\alpha}=e^{\varphi-\xi-i\theta}.\end{aligned}

(10)

The parameters
\phi
and
\xi

\varphi
and

respectively flavour, space. We consider Euclidean metric where the

The octagon represents two hexagons glued together by inserting a
complete set of virtual states in the Hilbert space associated with
the common mirror edge. An
n
-particle virtual state is
characterised by the rapidities
u_{i}
and the bound-state numbers

a_{i}

factorises into one-particle factors

and two-particle
interactions

weights. The octagon thus is expanded as a series of multiple

weights

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{{\mathbb{O}}}_{\ell}&={\textstyle{1\over 2}}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{1\over n!}\sum_{a_{1},...,a_{n}\geq 1}\int\prod_{j=1}^{n}{du_{j}\over 2\pi i}\ W^{\pm}_{a_{j}}(u_{j})\ \prod_{j<k}^{n}W_{a_{j},a_{k}}(u_{j},u_{k}).\end{aligned}

(11)

\bullet
Bi-local weights.
The bi-local
weights are defined in terms of a single function

\displaystyle W(u,v)={x(u)-x(v)\over x(u)x(v)-1}

(12)

where the function
x(u)
is defined by the Zhukovsky map

\displaystyle{u/g}=x+{1/x}

(13)

transforming the physical sheet in the rapidity plane into the
exterior of the unit circle. Namely

\displaystyle W_{a,b}(u,v)=W(u\!+\!\textstyle{i\over 2}a,v\!+\!\textstyle{i\over 2}b)\,W(u\!+\!\textstyle{i\over 2}a,v\!-\!\textstyle{i\over 2}b)\,W(u\!-\!\textstyle{i\over 2}a,v\!+\!\textstyle{i\over 2}b)\,W(u\!-\!\textstyle{i\over 2}a,v\!-\!\textstyle{i\over 2}b).

(14)

\bullet
Local weights.
The one-particle factors are

\displaystyle W^{\pm}_{a}(u,\xi)={1\over g}(-1)^{a}\chi^{\pm}_{a}\ \,\Omega_{\ell}(u+\textstyle{i\over 2}a,\xi)\ \Omega_{\ell}(u-\textstyle{i\over 2}a,\xi)\,\times W(u+\textstyle{i\over 2}a,u-\textstyle{i\over 2}a).

(15)

where

\displaystyle\Omega_{\ell}(u,\xi)={1\over x^{\ell}}\,{e^{ig\xi\,[x-1/x]}\over x-1/x}=g\,{e^{ig\xi\,[x-1/x]}\over x^{\ell}}\ {d\log x\over du}\,,

(16)

\chi^{\pm}_{a}
is essentially the character of the

-th
antisymmetric representation of
{\mathfrak{psu}}(2|2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \chi^{\pm}_{a}(\phi,\varphi,\theta)=(-1)^{a}\ {\sin(a\phi)\over\sin\phi}\ \left[2\cos\phi-2\cosh(\varphi\pm i\theta)\right].\end{aligned}

(17)

For simplicity we will assume that
\theta=0

\Omega_{\ell}(u,\xi)
reflects the form of the momentum and the energy of the
mirror magnons as functions of the rapidity

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{p}_{a}(u)&=\textstyle{1\over 2}g(x-{1\over x})_{u+ia/2}+{\textstyle{1\over 2}}g(x-{1\over x})_{u-ia/2},\\
\quad\tilde{E}_{a}(u)&=\log x|_{u+ia/2}+\log x|_{u-ia/2}.\end{aligned}

(18)

The

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{W}(t)&=1+\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{a}\chi_{a}\,e^{-at}=1-{\cosh\varphi-\cos\phi\over\cosh t-\cos\phi}.\end{aligned}

3.1
The original and the simplified octagon kernels

In this subsection we remind the definition of the two kernels.

5
Conclusion

In this paper we completed the study of the octagon form factor
started in
Kostov:2019stn
;
Kostov:2019auq
in the following two
aspects. First, we gave a precise Fock space description of the
fermionic representation outlined there, in which the length of the
bridge determines the level of the Dirac sea. Second, we found
explicitly the similarity transformation conjectured there, which
leads, by simplifying the octagon kernel, to the determinant formula
for the octagon. Such similarity transformation is not unique and, as
we already mentioned, another solution has been found independently by
Belitsky and Korchemsky
Belitsky:2020qir
. The interpretation
we found for the bridge length allowed us to express the ratio of two
octagons with different bridges as a determinant of finite size
involving the resolvent of the octagon kernel.

The free fermions proposed here as a device to handle the diagonal
symmetric part of the weights of the virtual particles might be useful
for studying other observables which can take the form
(
121
), that is, a vacuum expectation value of an element of

GL(\infty)
. Such objects can be deformed by an infinite set of
commuting flows associated with the modes of the fermion current,
following the recipe of
JimboMiwa-tau
, turning them into

\tau
-functions of the Toda lattice hierarchy. Half of these flows
can be associated with the conserved charges in the spin chain
description of
{\cal N}=4
SYM.

In the case of the octagon, the kinematical parameters
\xi
and

\phi
can be associated, at least in the light-like limit, with the
“times” coupled to the modes
J_{\pm 1}
of the fermion current.
Indeed, it was shown in
Belitsky:2020qir
that certain scaling
in the light-like limit the octagon satisfies the radial 2D Toda
lattice equation. Slightly generalising, we can consider the scaling
limit

\displaystyle\phi=i\pi+i{s\over g},\ \xi={\sigma\over g},\quad g\to 0\qquad(s>\sigma)

(124)

where their solution takes the form

\displaystyle{\mathbb{O}}_{\ell}=e^{-\hat{s}^{2}}\ \det\left[I_{j-k}(2\hat{s})\right]_{j,k=1,...,\ell},

(125)

where
I_{n}
are modified Bessel functions and
\hat{s}=\sqrt{s^{2}-\sigma^{2}}
.
The octagon in this limit satisfies the full 2D Toda lattice equation
with time variable
s
and space variable
\sigma
,

\displaystyle{1\over 4}\left(\partial_{s}^{2}-\partial_{\sigma}^{2}\right)\Phi_{\ell}+e^{\Phi_{\ell+1}-\Phi_{\ell}}-e^{\Phi_{\ell}-\Phi_{\ell-1}}=0\,,\ \ \ \Phi_{\ell}=\log{{\mathbb{O}}_{\ell+1}\over{\mathbb{O}}_{\ell}}\,,\ \ \ell\geq 1\,.

(126)

A clue about the general validity of (
126
) would be a direct
proof using the fermion representation, which is still to be found.

Finally, let us mention two intriguing recent observations which
suggest to look for a unified formalism working both for the BMN and
the GKP vacua. First, in the null square limit, the authors of

Belitsky:2019fan
noticed that the anomalous dimension
characterising the light-like octagon has an alternative
representation similar to the cusp anomalous dimension. By still
unclear reasons, the light-like limit of the octagon can also be
obtained by choosing as a weight function
\chi(\tau)=2/(e^{\tau/g}-1)
.
Second, it was shown in
Basso:2020xts
that the six-gluon
amplitude in certain kinematical limit can be expressed in terms of
the so called tilted BES kernel which becomes the BES kernel or the
(light-like) octagon kernel for particular values of the tilting
angle. The tilted kernel by angle
\alpha
is obtained by replacing in
(
37
)
i\to i\,e^{-i\alpha}
which leads to (
70
)
with
\sin\left[(m-n)\pi/2\right]
replaced by
\sin\left[(m-n)(\pi/2-\alpha)\right]
. The fermionic representation and the subsequent analysis (except
for section
4
which is relevant only to the case

\alpha=0
) can be generalised to a generic angle
\alpha
. In particular, we
checked that the flow equation obtained in appendix
B

holds for the tilted kernel as well.

A Note on L 1 − L^{1}- contractive property of the solutions of the scalar conservation laws through the method by Lax-Oleĭnik.
Abhishek Adimurthi

Abstract
. In this note, we study the L 1 − L^{1}- contractive property of the solutions the scalar conservation laws, got by the method of Lax-Oleĭnik. First, it is proved when f is merely convex and the initial data is in L ∞ ​ ( ℝ ) L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}) . And then, it is shown for the case when the initial data is in L 1 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{1}(\mathbb{R}) with the convex flux having super-linear growth. Finally, the L 1 − L^{1}- contractive property is shown for the scalar conservation laws with the initial data in L 1 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{1}(\mathbb{R}) and the flux is “semi-super-linear”. This entire note does not assume any results mentioned through the approach by Kruzkov.



Introduction.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
be real-valued function ,
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, for which let
u
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty))
be a weak solution of the scalar conservation law,

(1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}u+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\big[f(u)\big]&=0;\quad x\in\mathbb{R},t>0,\\
u(x,0)&=u_{0}(x);\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\end{aligned}\right.

i.e, in the weak sense, one can write (
1

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left(u\varphi_{t}+f(u)\varphi_{x}\right)dxdt+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u_{0}(x)\varphi(x,0)\ dx=0,

for all test functions
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty))
, with
\varphi_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi
and
\varphi_{x}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\varphi
.
In general, (
2
) can admit many solutions. A question of interest to ask here is for what set of functions does (
2
) admit a unique solution?

It is mentioned in
[
Eva98
]
that if the function
f
is taken to be uniformly convex, i.e

and there exists

[

]

[

]

The explicit formula then gives Oleĭnik-one-sided inequality :

(3)

u(x+z,t)-u(x,t)\leq C(1+t^{-1})z,

for some
C\geq 0
and for a.e
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
t>0
,
z>0
.

It is shown in
[
Ole59
]
that there exist a unique solution in terms of (
2
), i.e if
u_{1}
and

satisfy the Oleĭnik-one-sided inequality and have the same initial condition, then

a.e.

On the other hand, using the vanishing viscosity method, it is proved in
[

]
that the PDE (
1

Definition

1

Definition 1
.

Fix

For each

Introduction

It is well known that the theory of maximal functions plays a central role in harmonic analysis and partial
differential equations. Which has attracted a lot of attention in the last decades. Our topic in this paper originated in the estimate for maximal function. Let us begin with a definition of the
maximal function

M_{t}
along the straight line
(t,t)
,

\displaystyle M_{t}f(x):=\sup_{u\in(0,\infty)}\frac{1}{u}\left|\int_{0}^{u}f(x_{1}-t,x_{2}-t)\,\textrm{d}t\right|

for appropriate functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Here and hereafter,
x:=(x_{1},x_{2})
. From Stein
[
36
]
, it follows that
M_{t}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for all
p\in(1,\infty]
because
M_{t}
is essentially the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. By a straightforward change of
variables, we can rewrite

\displaystyle M_{t}f(x)=\sup_{u\in(0,\infty)}\left|\int_{0}^{1}f(x_{1}-ut,x_{2}-ut)\,\textrm{d}t\right|.

It is natural to replace the straight line
(t,t)
in
M_{t}
by a general plane curve
(t,\gamma(t))
, such as the parabola
(t,t^{2})
, where
\gamma
satisfies some suitable smoothness and curvature conditions. We now consider the
maximal function

M_{\gamma}
along a curve
(t,\gamma(t))
,

\displaystyle M_{\gamma}f(x):=\sup_{u\in(0,\infty)}\left|\int_{0}^{1}f(x_{1}-ut,x_{2}-u\gamma(t))\,\textrm{d}t\right|.

For notational convenience, we define the operator
T
along a dilated plane curve
(ut,u\gamma(t))
as

(1.1)

\displaystyle Tf(x,u):=\int_{0}^{1}f(x_{1}-ut,x_{2}-u\gamma(t))\,\textrm{d}t,

then

\displaystyle M_{\gamma}f(x)=\sup_{u\in(0,\infty)}\left|Tf(x,u)\right|.

It is natural to ask whether the
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
estimate for
M_{\gamma}
is valid or not. However, by Liu and Yu
[
24
, Remark 1.3]
, the maximal function
M_{\gamma}
does not map
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
to
L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
(even when
\sup_{u\in(0,\infty)}
in
M_{\gamma}
is replaced by
\sup_{u\in(0,1)}
), unless
p=q
. Related works can be found in
[
2
,
35
,
16
,
18
]
and references therein. However, if we take supremum over
u\in[1,2]
in
M_{\gamma}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\bar{M}_{\gamma}f(x):=\sup_{u\in[1,2]}\left|Tf(x,u)\right|.

Then
\bar{M}_{\gamma}
is actually bounded from
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
to
L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for some
q>p
, see
[
24
]
. Indeed, this result can be rewritten as

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left\|Tf\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}L_{u}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

This phenomenon is called
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
-improving. We refer the reader to
[
43
,
30
,
19
,
20
]
for the details of this phenomenon.

Our goal in this paper is to establish the
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L_{x,u}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])
estimate for
T
. By
\textrm{H}\ddot{\textrm{o}}\textrm{lder}
’s inequality, it is easy to see that, when
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
verifies the inequality (
1.2
), we have the
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L_{x,u}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])
estimate for
T
. However, the
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
region for
T:\ L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L_{x,u}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])
becomes large. We now state our main results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
\gamma\in C^{N}(0,1]
with
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough,
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\gamma(t)=0
and
\gamma
is monotonic on
(0,1]
. Moreover,
\gamma
satisfies the following two conditions:

(i)

there exist positive constants
\{C^{(j)}_{1}\}_{j=1}^{2}
such that
|\frac{t^{j}\gamma^{(j)}(t)}{\gamma(t)}|\geq C^{(j)}_{1}
for any
t\in(0,1]
;

(ii)

there exist positive constants
\{C^{(j)}_{2}\}_{j=1}^{N}
such that
|\frac{t^{j}\gamma^{(j)}(t)}{\gamma(t)}|\leq C^{(j)}_{2}
for any
t\in(0,1]
.

Then, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for all
f\in L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\displaystyle\left\|Tf\right\|_{L_{x,u}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

if
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in\square\cup\{(0,0)\}\cup\{(\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{3})\}
and

. Here and hereafter, the trapezium
\square:=\{(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}):\ \frac{2}{p}-1\leq\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}>\frac{1}{3p},\frac{1}{q}>\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{3}\}

.

We show the conditions of

in Theorem

is sharp except for some borderline cases. Moreover, we can also find that the region of
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
in Theorem
1.1
remains unchanged when
\omega\in(0,2)
, but decreases as

increases when

. Since the curvature of a curve is key in the proofs, which may provide an underlying cause of this phenomenon. At the same time, for the infinitely flat curve

The local smoothing phenomenon

Recall that

\displaystyle Tf(x,u)=\int_{0}^{1}f(x_{1}-ut,x_{2}-u\gamma(t))\,\textrm{d}t

with
u\in[1,2]
, which is an operator of convolution type. When
u=1
,
Tf(\cdot,1)
is a classical operator in harmonic analysis. For some related results, we refer to
[
21
,
28
,
29
,
40
,
41
]
and the references therein. In this section, our main goal is to determine the almost optimal range of exponents
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
such that the
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
estimate for
T
holds uniformly for
u\in[1,2]
. We now state our result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume
\gamma\in C^{2}(0,1]
,
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\gamma(t)=0
and
\gamma
is monotonic on
(0,1]
. Moreover,
\gamma
satisfies the following two conditions:

(i)

there exist positive constants
\{C^{(j)}_{1}\}_{j=1}^{2}
such that
|\frac{t^{j}\gamma^{(j)}(t)}{\gamma(t)}|\geq C^{(j)}_{1}
for any
t\in(0,1]
;

(ii)

there exists a positive constant
C^{(1)}_{2}
such that
|\frac{t\gamma^{\prime}(t)}{\gamma(t)}|\leq C^{(1)}_{2}
for any
t\in(0,1]
.

Then, there exists a positive constant
C
uniformly for
u\in[1,2]
such that for all
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\|Tf(\cdot,u)\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

if

(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in\ \vartriangle
and
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
satisfy
1+(1+\omega)(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})>0
.

On the other hand, (
2.1
) holds only if

(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in\ \vartriangle
and
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
satisfy
1+(1+\omega)(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})\geq 0

Putting things together, by (

A key local smoothing estimate
.

Consider the
Fourier integral operators

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}f(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\phi(z,\xi)}a(z,\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)\,\textrm{d}\xi,

where
a(z,\xi)
is a symbol of order
\sigma
. Here and hereafter,
z
is used to denote a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
comprised of the space-time variables
(x,u)
. Suppose that
\textrm{supp}~a(\cdot,\xi)
is contained in a fixed compact set and
\phi(z,\cdot)
is a homogeneous function of degree
1
. We say that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the
cinematic curvature condition
if the following conditions are satisfied:

\bullet

Non-degeneracy condition.
For all
(z,\xi)\in\textrm{supp}~a

\displaystyle\textrm{rank}~\partial^{2}_{z\xi}\phi(z,\xi)=n.

\bullet

Cone condition.
Consider the Gauss map
G:\ \textrm{supp}~a\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n}
by
G(z,\xi):=\frac{G_{0}(z,\xi)}{|G_{0}(z,\xi)|}
, where

\displaystyle G_{0}(z,\xi):=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{\xi_{j}}\partial_{z}\phi(z,\xi).

The curvature condition

\displaystyle\textrm{rank}~\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\langle\partial_{z}\phi(z,\xi),G(z,\xi_{0})\rangle|_{\xi=\xi_{0}}=n-1

holds for all
(z,\xi_{0})\in\textrm{supp}~a
.

Geometrically, the curvature condition of course means that, for fixed
z_{0}
, the cone

\Gamma_{z_{0}}:=\left\{\partial_{z}\phi(z_{0},\eta):\ \eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}~\textrm{in}~\textrm{a}~\textrm{conic}~\textrm{neighbourhood}~\textrm{of}~\eta_{0}\right\}

is a smooth conic manifold of dimension
n
with
n-1
non-vanishing principal curvatures at every point. We can see that the phase featured in the half-wave propagator, i.e.,
\phi(z,\xi)=x\cdot\xi-u|\xi|
, satisfies the cinematic curvature condition.

Now, we show some local smoothing estimates for
\mathcal{F}
in (

3.6
). To state the following propositions we introduce some definitions. Let
\bar{\psi}:\ \mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function supported on

with

and

Proposition 3.2

Let

For the case

Proof of (i) of Theorem

Since

\sup_{s\in(0,t)}\frac{\ln|\gamma(s)|}{\ln s}\leq\sup_{s\in(0,\tau)}\frac{\ln|\gamma(s)|}{\ln s}<\omega+\epsilon~~\textrm{and}~~\sup_{s\in(0,t)}\frac{\ln|\gamma(s)|}{\ln s}\geq\omega.

Hence, there exists

\frac{\ln|\gamma(t^{\prime})|}{\ln t^{\prime}}\in(\omega-\epsilon,\omega+\epsilon)

is strictly decreasing and

. Then we clearly have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left|\gamma(t_{i})\right|={t_{i}}^{\log_{t_{i}}{|\gamma(t_{i})|}}={t_{i}}^{\frac{\ln|\gamma(t_{i})|}{\ln t_{i}}}\in\left({t_{i}}^{\omega+\epsilon},{t_{i}}^{\omega-\epsilon}\right).

On the other hand, we claim that

(4.2)

\displaystyle t_{i}\left(t_{i}\left|\gamma(t_{i})\right|\right)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim 1,

where the implicit constant is independent of
i
. Indeed, let
S_{t_{i}}:=[-t_{i},t_{i}]\times[-|\gamma(t_{i})|,|\gamma(t_{i})|]
and

\Lambda_{\chi_{S_{t_{i}}}}(x,u):=\left|\left\{t\in[0,1]:\ (x_{1}-ut,x_{2}-u\gamma(t))\in S_{t_{i}}\right\}\right|.

Then, for any
x\in S_{t_{i}}/2
, by the doubling condition (
2.3
), there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
small enough such that, for any
t\in[0,\delta t_{i}]
, we have
|x_{1}-ut|\leq t_{i}/2+2\delta t_{i}\leq t_{i}
and
|x_{2}-u\gamma(t)|\leq|\gamma(t_{i})|/2+2|\gamma(\delta t_{i})|\leq|\gamma(t_{i})|
, which further leads to the pointwise estimate
\Lambda_{\chi_{S_{t_{i}}}}(x,u)\geq\delta t_{i}\chi_{S_{t_{i}}/2}(x)
. Noticing that
T(\chi_{S_{t_{i}}})(x,u)\geq\Lambda_{\chi_{S_{t_{i}}}}(x,u)
and the assumption that
\|Tf\|_{L_{x,u}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle|S_{t_{i}}|\lesssim\left|\left\{(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2]:\ T(\chi_{S_{t_{i}}})(x,u)\geq\delta t_{i}\right\}\right|\lesssim\left(t_{i}^{-1}\left\|\chi_{S_{t_{i}}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\right)^{q}.

Combining this with the fact that
|S_{t_{i}}|\approx t_{i}|\gamma(t_{i})|
and
\|\chi_{S_{t_{i}}}\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\approx(t_{i}|\gamma(t_{i})|)^{\frac{1}{p}}
, we obtain (
4.2
).

A theorem of
\textrm{H}\ddot{\textrm{o}}\textrm{rmander}

[
14
]
implies that
\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{p}
. Combining (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle 1\gtrsim t_{i}\left(t_{i}\left|\gamma(t_{i})\right|\right)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\gtrsim t_{i}\left(t_{i}\left({t_{i}}^{\omega-\epsilon}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}={t_{i}}^{1+(1+\omega-\epsilon)\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}.

Notice that the sequence
\{t_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset(0,1)
is strictly decreasing and
\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}t_{i}=0
, so that

(4.3)

\displaystyle 1+(1+\omega-\epsilon)\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\geq 0

for all
\epsilon>0
. Let
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
in (
4.3
), it follows that
1+(1+\omega)(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})\geq 0
, and so we have (i) of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of (ii) of Theorem
1.2
.

As above, the condition
\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{p}
follows from a theorem of
\textrm{H}\ddot{\textrm{o}}\textrm{rmander}

[
14
]
. Hence, we should only to show that
\frac{2}{p}-1\leq\frac{1}{q}
. For any
\epsilon>0
, let
D
be the
\epsilon
neighborhood of the curve
(ut,u\gamma(t))
with
t\in(0,1]
and
u\in[1,2]
. Then, for any
x\in D
, there exists
t_{0}\in[0,1]
such that
|(x_{1}-ut_{0},x_{2}-u\gamma(t_{0}))|\leq\epsilon
. Therefore, for any
t\in(t_{0}-\epsilon,t_{0}+\epsilon)
, there exists
\delta\in(0,\infty)
such that
|(x_{1}-ut,x_{2}-u\gamma(t))|\leq\delta\epsilon
. Here, we used the triangle inequality and the estimate that
|\gamma(t)-\gamma(t_{0})|\lesssim|t-t_{0}|\lesssim\epsilon
. Furthermore, we have
T(\chi_{B(0,\delta\epsilon)})(x,u)\gtrsim\epsilon\chi_{D}(x)
. As an immediate consequence of
\|Tf\|_{L_{x,u}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|D|\lesssim\left|\left\{(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2]:\ T(\chi_{B(0,\delta\epsilon)})(x,u)\gtrsim\epsilon\right\}\right|\lesssim\left(\epsilon^{-1}\left\|\chi_{B(0,\delta\epsilon)}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\right)^{q}.

It is easy to check that
|D|\approx\epsilon
and
\|\chi_{B(0,\delta\epsilon)}\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\approx\epsilon^{\frac{2}{p}}
. This readily implies that
\epsilon^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{2}{p}+1}\lesssim 1
holds for all
\epsilon>0
. Thus, via letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
we obtain that
\frac{2}{p}-1\leq\frac{1}{q}
and, hence, we have (ii) of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of (iii) of Theorem
1.2
.

A simple calculation gives that the adjoint operator
T^{*}
of
T
can be written as

\displaystyle T^{*}g(x):=\int_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}g(x_{1}+ut,x_{2}+u\gamma(t),u)\,\textrm{d}t\,\textrm{d}u,

and the boundedness
\|Tf\|_{L_{x,u}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
is equivalent to

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left\|T^{*}g\right\|_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\|g\|_{L_{x,u}^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}.

For any
\epsilon>0
, let
L
be the
\epsilon
neighborhood of the curve
(t,\gamma(t))
with
t\in(0,1]
. Then, for any
x\in L
, there exists
t_{0}\in[0,1]
such that
|(x_{1}-t_{0},x_{2}-\gamma(t_{0}))|\leq\epsilon
. Therefore, for any
-x\in L
,
t\in(t_{0}-\epsilon,t_{0}+\epsilon)
and
u\in[1,1+\epsilon]
, there exists
\delta\in(0,\infty)
such that
|(x_{1}+ut,x_{2}+u\gamma(t))|\leq\delta\epsilon
. Here, we used the triangle inequality and the estimates that
|\gamma(t)-\gamma(t_{0})|\lesssim|t-t_{0}|\lesssim\epsilon
and
|\gamma(t_{0})|\lesssim 1
. As a result, by setting
W:=B(0,\delta\epsilon)\times[1,1+\epsilon]
, for
-x\in L
, we have

\displaystyle T^{*}\chi_{W}(x)\geq\int_{1}^{1+\epsilon}\int_{t_{0}-\epsilon}^{t_{0}+\epsilon}\chi_{W}(x_{1}+ut,x_{2}+u\gamma(t),u)\,\textrm{d}t\,\textrm{d}u\gtrsim\epsilon^{2},

i.e., we have
T^{*}\chi_{W}(x)\gtrsim\epsilon^{2}\chi_{L}(-x)
. According to (
4.4
), we assert that

\displaystyle|L|\lesssim\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\ T^{*}\chi_{W}(x)\gtrsim\epsilon^{2}\right\}\right|\lesssim\left(\epsilon^{-2}\left\|\chi_{W}\right\|_{L_{x,u}^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\right)^{p^{\prime}}.

This, combined with the fact that
|L|\approx\epsilon
and
\|\chi_{W}\|_{L_{x,u}^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\approx\epsilon^{\frac{3}{q^{\prime}}}
, implies that
\epsilon^{\frac{3}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim 1
holds for all
\epsilon>0
. Then, we get
\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{3p}
by letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, which is (iii) of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of (iv) of Theorem
1.2
.

It is easy to see that (i) implies (iv) of Theorem
1.2
, when
\omega\in[2,\infty)
. Furthermore, from (ii) and (iii) of Theorem
1.2
, the region of
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
in Theorem
1.1
remains unchanged, when
\omega\in(0,\frac{3}{2})
. On the other hand, the points
D(\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{3})
and
C(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{6})
are the endpoints in the proof. Therefore, we may conjecture that the region of
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
in Theorem
1.1
also remains unchanged for all
\omega\in(0,2)
, which further implies that we can expect (iv) of Theorem
1.2
. Therefore, the
\omega=2
is a critical point, and we may need a Taylor’s expansion of order
2
as in (
4.5
).

We now give the proof of (iv) of Theorem
1.2
. From the condition (i) of Theorem
1.1
, there exists
\tau_{0}\in(0,1]
such that
\gamma^{\prime}(\tau_{0})\neq\gamma(\tau_{0})
. One may assume without loss of generality that
\tau_{0}=1
,
\gamma(t)
is increasing on
(0,1]
and
\gamma(1)=1
. Then
\gamma^{\prime}(1)\neq 1
. Let
e_{1}:=(-1,-\gamma^{\prime}(1))/\sqrt{1+\gamma^{\prime}(1)^{2}}
and
e_{2}:=(-\gamma^{\prime}(1),1)/\sqrt{1+\gamma^{\prime}(1)^{2}}
be two orthogonal unit vectors. For any
\epsilon>0
, set

Q:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\ |x\cdot e_{1}|\leq 6\epsilon~~\textrm{and}~~|x\cdot e_{2}|\leq[3+2|\gamma^{\prime\prime}(1)|]\epsilon^{2}\right\}.

We choose a subset
\{u_{i}\}\subset[1,2]
such that
|u_{i+1}-u_{i}|=2\epsilon^{2}
for all
i
. For each
u_{i}
, denote

\Omega_{u_{i}}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\ |\left(x-(u_{i},u_{i})\right)\cdot e_{1}|\leq\epsilon~\textrm{and}~|\left(x-(u_{i},u_{i})\right)\cdot e_{2}|\leq\epsilon^{2}\right\}.

For any
x\in\Omega_{u_{i}}
and
t\in[1-\epsilon/\sqrt{1+\gamma^{\prime}(1)^{2}},1]
, Taylor’s expansion yields

(4.5)

\displaystyle\gamma(t)=1+\gamma^{\prime}(1)(t-1)+\frac{\gamma^{\prime\prime}(1)}{2}(t-1)^{2}+o\left((t-1)^{2}\right),

where
o((t-1)^{2})
means
o((t-1)^{2})/(t-1)^{2}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow 1^{-}
. Furthermore, by (
4.5
), a simple calculation gives
|((u_{i}t,u_{i}\gamma(t))-(u_{i},u_{i}))\cdot e_{1}|\leq 4\epsilon
and
|((u_{i}t,u_{i}\gamma(t))-(u_{i},u_{i}))\cdot e_{2}|\leq 2|\gamma^{\prime\prime}(1)|\epsilon^{2}
for
\epsilon>0
small enough. By the triangle inequality, we may conclude that
|(x-(u_{i}t,u_{i}\gamma(t)))\cdot e_{1}|\leq 5\epsilon
and
|(x-(u_{i}t,u_{i}\gamma(t)))\cdot e_{2}|\leq[1+2|\gamma^{\prime\prime}(1)|]\epsilon^{2}
. Therefore, for any
u\in(u_{i}-\epsilon^{2},u_{i}+\epsilon^{2})
, the triangle inequality also gives
|(x-(ut,u\gamma(t)))\cdot e_{1}|\leq 6\epsilon
and
|(x-(ut,u\gamma(t)))\cdot e_{2}|\leq[3+2|\gamma^{\prime\prime}(1)|]\epsilon^{2}
, where
\epsilon>0
small enough.

The above argument implies that
(x_{1}-ut,x_{2}-u\gamma(t))\in Q
if
x\in\Omega_{u_{i}}
,
u\in(u_{i}-\epsilon^{2},u_{i}+\epsilon^{2})
and
t\in[1-\epsilon/\sqrt{1+\gamma^{\prime}(1)^{2}},1]
. Consequently, for
\epsilon>0
small enough, one may obtain

\displaystyle T\chi_{Q}(x,u)\geq\int_{1-\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{1+\gamma^{\prime}(1)^{2}}}}^{1}\chi_{Q}(x_{1}-ut,x_{2}-u\gamma(t))\,\textrm{d}t=\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{1+\gamma^{\prime}(1)^{2}}}

for any
x\in\Omega_{u_{i}}
and
u\in(u_{i}-\epsilon^{2},u_{i}+\epsilon^{2})
. By
\gamma^{\prime}(1)\neq 1
, we have
\Omega_{u_{i}}\cap\Omega_{u_{i^{\prime}}}=\emptyset
for any
i\neq i^{\prime}
. Notice that the number of the elements in
\{u_{i}\}\subset[1,2]
is equivalent to
\epsilon^{-2}
. Then clearly

\displaystyle\left\|T\chi_{Q}\right\|_{L_{x,u}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\geq\left(\sum_{i}\int_{u_{i}-\epsilon^{2}}^{u_{i}+\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{u_{i}}}\left|T\chi_{Q}\right|^{q}\,\textrm{d}x\,\textrm{d}u\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\gtrsim\epsilon^{1+\frac{3}{q}}.

This, combined with
\|\chi_{Q}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\approx\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p}}
and
\|Tf\|_{L_{x,u}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
, leads to that
\epsilon^{1+\frac{3}{q}-\frac{3}{p}}\lesssim 1
holds for all
\epsilon>0
small enough. Let
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, it follows that
\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{3}
. Thus (iv) of Theorem
1.2
is proved.

Therefore, putting things together we finish the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

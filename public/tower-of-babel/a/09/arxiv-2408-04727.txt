Deterministic Approximate Counting of Colorings with fewer than 2 ​ Δ 2\Delta Colors via Absence of Zeros


Abstract
Let Δ , q ≥ 3 \Delta,q\geq 3 be integers. We prove that there exists η ≥ 0.002 \eta\geq 0.002 such that if q ≥ ( 2 − η ) ​ Δ q\geq(2-\eta)\Delta , then there exists an open set 𝒰 ⊂ ℂ \mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{C} that contains the interval [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] such that for each w ∈ 𝒰 w\in\mathcal{U} and any graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) of maximum degree at most Δ \Delta , the partition function of the anti-ferromagnetic q q -state Potts model evaluated at w w does not vanish. Introduction

The algorithmic problem of designing an algorithm to (approximately) compute the number of
q
-colorings of a graph
G
has received a lot of interest in the past thirty years.
The main challenge is to design for each pair of positive integer
\Delta,q
such that
q\geq\Delta+1
an algorithm that on input of an
n
-vertex graph
G
of maximum degree at most
\Delta
and
\varepsilon>0
outputs the number of
q
-colorings of
G
within an
\exp(\varepsilon)
relative error in time
poly(
n/\varepsilon
)
.
We note that if
q<\Delta
, approximating the number of proper
q
-colorings of a graph of maximum degree
\Delta
is
NP-hard

[
GSVhardnesscoloring
]
(provided
q
is even), even for triangle-free graphs.
Thus far the only nontrivial cases for which such an algorithm is known to exist correspond to
\Delta=3
and
q\geq 4

[
Luetalfptasforcubic
]
.
Below we will say more about the status of this problem in general.

Over the past thirty years several algorithmic approaches have been developed to approximately count the number of proper colorings (as well as for several other counting problems) including variations of the celebrated MCMC method
[
chen2019improved
,
Jerrumsimple
]
, the correlation decay approach
[
BanGar
,
GKfptas
,
Weitz
]
and the interpolation method
[
Barbook
,
BDPR21
,
LSS2Delta
,
PatReg17
]
.
The latter two approaches yield
deterministic
algorithms as opposed to the MCMC-based method.

The interpolation method is based on the existence of a zero-free region for an associated family of polynomials whose evaluations count the number of proper colorings.
In the present paper this role is taken by the
partition function of the Potts model
, which we will introduce shortly.
The main focus of the present paper is to provide an improved zero-free region for this polynomial, which, as a direct corollary, yields efficient deterministic approximation algorithms for counting proper colorings.

The partition function of the Potts model.

For a graph
G=(V,E)
, a positive integer
q
and

, the
partition function of the Potts model
is defined as

Z_{G}(q;w):=\sum_{\phi:V\to[q]}w^{m(\phi)},

(1)

where
[q]:=\{1,\ldots,q\}
, and

denotes the number of

edges, i.e., the number of edges

such that
\phi(u)=\phi(v)
.
We note that
Z_{G}(q;0)
equals the number of proper
q
-colorings of
G
.
In statistical physics, one usually takes

, where

denotes the coupling constant, and

the inverse temperature. Here

corresponds to the anti-ferromagnetic case, while

corresponds to the ferromagnetic case.

Partly motivated by the classical Lee-Yang
[

]

[

]

(for the latter, one has to extend the partition function of the Potts model to the partition function of the random cluster model, where

can also take non-integer values).

Absence of zeros in computer science.

More recently, there has been an increasing interest in understanding the location of these complex zeros from the perspective of computer science and probability theory.
This interest comes from the fact that zero-free regions for the partition functions of models, such as the Potts model, yield efficient deterministic approximation algorithms

In particular, open sets

such that

The
2\Delta
bound of Liu, Sinclair, and Srivastava
[
LSS2Delta
]
.

The deterministic algorithm of Liu, Sinclair, and Srivastava
[
LSS2Delta
]
is based on Barvinok’s interpolation method. Their main contribution lies in proving a zero-free region for the partition function of the Potts model.
They essentially prove the following more general statement, allowing them to deduce zero-free regions from probabilistic statements.

(\star)

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a class of graphs of maximum degree at most
\Delta
. Suppose that
q
is such that for all
w\in[0,1]
and for any rooted graph
(G,v)
(
G\in\mathcal{G}
) in which some of its vertices are precolored, when drawing a random coloring from the Potts model with parameter
w
the marginal probability that
v
gets color
i
(assuming that
i
is not used on the neighbors of
v
) is bounded by
\tfrac{1}{d+2}
, where
d
denotes the number of neighbors of
v
that are not precolored.
Then there exists an open set
\mathcal{U}
containing
[0,1]
such that
Z_{G}(q;w)\neq 0
for all
w\in\mathcal{U}
and
G\in\mathcal{G}
.

It is not difficult to see that for
q\geq 2\Delta
this condition is satisfied for the class of all graphs of maximum degree at most
\Delta
, and hence this immediately gives the desired zero-freeness and approximation algorithm via the interpolation method.
For triangle free graphs, the condition is satisfied provided
q\geq 1.7633\Delta+\beta
where
\beta
is an absolute constant, see
[
LSS2Delta
]
for the precise statement.
It is easy to see that for
q<2\Delta
there are examples of graphs where the condition in
(\star)
is not satisfied.
Unfortunately, the proof of
(\star)
as given in
[
LSS2Delta
]
is somewhat technical, making it difficult to see how to push the bounds on
q
below
2\Delta
.

Our contributions.

This brings us to the contributions of the present paper.
One of our contributions is that we give a new proof of the existence of the zero-free region for
q\geq 2\Delta
, which is shorter and arguably more transparent and less technical.
Secondly, we are able to take advantage of the local structure around the root vertex for graphs where the condition in
(\star)
is not met, and thereby provide a modest improvement on the result of Liu, Sinclair, and Srivastava
[
LSS2Delta
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a constant
\eta\geq 0.002
such that for all integers
\Delta\geq 3
and
q\geq(2-\eta)\Delta
there exists an open set
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{C}
containing the interval
[0,1]
such that for each
w\in\mathcal{U}
and graph
G
of maximum degree at most
\Delta
,
Z_{G}(q,w)\neq 0
.

As a direct corollary, we have the following result breaking the
q=2\Delta
barrier for designing efficient
deterministic
approximation algorithms for counting proper colorings based on absence of zeros.

Corollary 1.2
.

There exists a constant
\eta\geq 0.002
such that for all integers
\Delta\geq 3
and
q\geq(2-\eta)\Delta
and
w\in[0,1]
there exists a deterministic algorithm which given an
n
-vertex graph of maximum degree at most
\Delta
and
\varepsilon>0
computes a number
\xi
satisfying

e^{-\varepsilon}\leq\frac{Z_{G}(q,w)}{\xi}\leq e^{\varepsilon}

in time polynomial in
n/\varepsilon
.

We note that this is indeed a direct corollary of Theorem
1.1
using Barvinok’s interpolation method
[
Barbook
]
in combination with the improvement due to Patel and the last author of the present paper
[
PatReg17
]
.
An explanation of how this fits the framework of
[
PatReg17
]
can be found in the proof of
[
BDPR21
, Corollary 1]
and, therefore, we omit a proof here.

Another consequence of Theorem
1.1
is that for
q\geq(2-\eta)\Delta
and
w\in(0,1)
the random variable defined as the number of monochromatic edges in a random sample from the Potts model (with parameters

and

) on a graph of maximum degree

satisfies a (local) central limit theorem.
This follows almost directly from the results in
[
Michelencentrallimit
,
localcentrallimit
,

]
and we refer the interested reader to these papers for the relevant details.

Organization and conventions.

The remainder of the paper is dedicated to proving Theorem
1.1
, first for
\eta=0
and later for
\eta>0

In the next section we give a detailed technical outline of our approach, at the end of which the reader may find an overview of the remainder of the paper.

While our arguments can for example be extended to list-coloring, we opt for focusing on just colorings so as to limit any technical overhead and hopefully making the proof more transparent. We comment on the extension to list-colorings and other possible extensions in Section
.

Although we could slightly improve our lower bound on

Our current established bounds do not seem to allow us to replace the lower bound of
0.002
by
0.003
.
We comment on possible approaches for improvement in Section
.

Outline of approach and setup

In this section, we give a detailed outline of our approach, which is inspired by
[
Barbook
,
BDPR21
,
LSS2Delta
]
and uses several concepts developed in these papers.
The main idea is to use induction to prove a result about partition functions of graphs with certain pre-colored vertices.
To carry out the induction, we will need good control over how changing the color of a vertex affects the partition function.
We next introduce some definitions that will be used throughout the paper and that will facilitate the discussion of the proof outline.

2.1
Definitions and expanded theorem statement

Let
q>0
be an integer, let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, let
S\subset V
and let
\phi:S\to[q]:=\{1,\ldots,q\}
.
We call the triple
(G,S,\phi)

q
-colored graph
.
Often we will just say that

is a partially
q
-colored graph, omitting the reference to

and
\phi
.
A vertex

will be called

will be called a
free

We say that a color

is

if either
v
is pinned, or if
v
has a neighbor in
S
which is assigned the color
j
by
\phi
, otherwise color
j
is called
free at
v
.

The partition function of the Potts model of a partially
q
-colored graph

is defined as

\displaystyle Z_{G}(w):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi:V(G)\to[q]\\
\psi_{|S}=\phi\end{subarray}}w^{m(\psi)}.

(2)

Note that we remove the
q
from the argument of
Z_{G}
compared to (
1

is already implicit in

.

Given a partially
q
-colored graph
(G,S,\phi)
,
we can always assume that each pinned vertex

is a leaf of

(i.e. has degree
1
) without changing the partition function, by iteratively replacing each pinned vertex
v
by
d=\deg(v)

, connecting each of them to a unique neighbor of
v
.
We denote by
\mathcal{G}_{\Delta,q}^{\bullet}
the set of pairs
(G,v)
, where

is a connected partially
q
-colored graph of maximum degree
\Delta
and
v
is a free vertex of
G
and where the pinned vertices of

are all leaves and form an independent set.
For such a pair let us define the vector
{\bf c}_{G,v}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}^{q}
, where the

, denotes the number of pinned neighbors of

that are colored with color

as the vector of blocked colors at

.
As a convention, we will write vectors in boldface, while entries of vectors are denoted in plain typeface.

If
w\geq 0
and if
Z_{G}(w)\neq 0
there is an associated probability measure,
\mathbb{P}_{G,w}
, on the collection of all colorings
\psi:V\to[q]
that coincide with
\phi
on

, whose probability mass function is defined by

\displaystyle\mu_{G,w}(\psi):=\frac{w^{m(\psi)}}{Z_{G}(w)}.

We will use capital letters to denote random variables. In particular, we denote the probability that vertex
v
is assigned color
j
when sampling a coloring from this distribution by
\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=j]
.
When
w
is clear from the context, we often simply write
\mathbb{P}_{G}
instead of
\mathbb{P}_{G,w}
.

For
w\in\mathbb{C}
and a free vertex
v
of a partially
q
-colored graph
G
we consider the ratio

\tilde{R}_{G,v;i,j}(w):=\frac{Z^{i}_{G,v}(w)}{Z^{j}_{G,v}(w)},

as a rational function in

. Here
Z^{j}_{G,v}(w)
denotes the sum (
2
) restricted to those
\psi
that assign color
j
to the vertex

. Note that
\tilde{R}_{G,v;i,j}(w)
only depends on the connected component of
G
that contains

.

To prove that
Z_{G}(w)\neq 0
for some
w\in\mathbb{C}
and a partially
q
-colored graph, it suffices to inductively show that
Z^{j}_{G,v}(w)\neq 0
for some color

and that the ratios
\tilde{R}_{G,v;i,j}(w)

In
[
BDPR21

In
[

]

This is also the approach we follow in the present paper.
To do so, we will change coordinates and work with

. Define

\displaystyle R_{G,v;i,j}(\tilde{w}):=\log(\tilde{R}_{G,v;i,j}(\tilde{w})),

under the implicit assumption that

We often just write

The

First we claim that the partition function

We will say that the graphs
G_{i}
are obtained from
G
via the
telescoping procedure
with respect to the colors
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
and the ordering of
N(v)
. See Figure
2.2
for an illustration of this.

For concreteness, we will continue the discussion for the case
\ell_{1}=1
and
\ell_{2}=q
.
By symmetry, we may of course always relabel the colors so that this is without loss of generality.

Define for
{\bf c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}^{q}
,
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{C}^{q-1}
and
\tilde{w}\in\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle P_{\bf c}({\tilde{w}},{\bf x})={\tilde{w}}^{c_{1}+1}e^{x_{1}}+\sum_{j=2}^{q-1}{\tilde{w}}^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}+{\tilde{w}}^{c_{q}},

(5)

\displaystyle Q_{\bf c}({\tilde{w}},{\bf x})={\tilde{w}}^{c_{1}}e^{x_{1}}+\sum_{j=2}^{q-1}{\tilde{w}}^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}+{\tilde{w}}^{c_{q}+1}.

(6)

We tend to omit the subscript
\bm{c}
if it is clear from the context.
We now define the vectors
{\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w})\in\mathbb{C}^{q-1}
by
R^{i}_{j}(\tilde{w})=R_{\overline{G}_{i},v_{i};j,q}({\tilde{w}})
for
j=1,\ldots,q-1
(implicitly assuming that these log-ratios are well-defined), and
{\bf c}^{i}={\bf c}_{G,v_{i}}
.
Then we observe that

\displaystyle P_{{\bf c}^{i}}({\tilde{w}},{\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w}))=\frac{Z_{{\hat{G}}_{i},\hat{v}_{i}}^{1}(\tilde{w})}{Z_{\overline{G}_{i},v_{i}}^{q}(\tilde{w})}\text{ and }Q_{{\bf c}^{i}}({\tilde{w}},{\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w}))=\frac{Z_{{\hat{G}}_{i},\hat{v}_{i}}^{q}(\tilde{w})}{Z_{\overline{G}_{i},v_{i}}^{q}(\tilde{w})},

and therefore by equation (
4
) we have

R_{G,v;1,q}=\sum_{i=1}^{\deg(v)}\log\left(\frac{P_{\bf c}({\tilde{w}},{\bf R}^{i})}{Q_{\bf c}({\tilde{w}},{\bf R}^{i})}\right).

(7)

Next define the function
F_{{\tilde{w}},{\bf c}}:\mathbb{C}^{q-1}\setminus\{{\bf x}\mid P_{\bf c}({\tilde{w},\bf x})=0\text{ or }Q_{\bf c}(\tilde{w},{\bf x})=0\}\to\mathbb{C}
by

F_{{\tilde{w}},\bf c}({\bf x})=\log\left(\frac{P_{\bf c}({\tilde{w}},{\bf x})}{Q_{\bf c}({\tilde{w}},{\bf x})}\right).

(8)

Using this function we can succinctly express (
7
) as

\displaystyle R_{G,v;1,q}(\tilde{w})=\sum_{i=1}^{\deg(v)}F_{\tilde{w},{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w})).

(9)

We now use (
9
) to express the difference between
R_{G,v;1,q}(w)
for
w\in[0,1]
and its perturbation
R_{G,v;1,q}(\tilde{w})
(for
\tilde{w}
near
w
) as follows

\displaystyle R_{G,v;1,q}(w)-R_{G,v;1,q}(\tilde{w})=\sum_{i=1}^{\deg(v)}\left(F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(w))-F_{\tilde{w},{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w})\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\deg(v)}\left(F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(w))-F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w}))\right)+\left(F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w}))-F_{\tilde{w},{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w}))\right).

(10)

It is not hard to see that we can make
|F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w}))-F_{\tilde{w},{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w}))|
arbitrarily small by choosing
\tilde{w}
close enough to
w
by continuity of
F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}
as a function of
w
, provided the vectors
{\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w})
lie in a bounded set. We will formally verify this in Section
4
.

Bounding
F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(w))-F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w}))
is much more work and to do so we will use the gradient of
F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}
.
We have

\displaystyle F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}

\displaystyle({\bf R}^{i}(w))-F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w}))=

(11)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}\left(t{\bf R}^{i}(w)+(1-t){\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w})\right),{\bf R}^{i}(w)-{\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w})\rangle dt,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the standard inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{q-1}.

This motivates us to investigate the gradient of
F_{w,{\bf c}}
.
For this purpose let us define for a partially
q
-colored graph
H
with a free vertex
v
and a color
\ell\in[q]
the partially
q
-colored graph
H^{+\ell}
by attaching a pinned leaf of color
\ell
to
v
.
For
w\in[0,1]
the vector
\textbf{P}_{H,v}(w)
in
\mathbb{R}^{q-1}
is defined by

{P}_{H,v;j}(w)=\mathbb{P}_{H^{+1},w}[\Phi(v)=j]-\mathbb{P}_{H^{+q},w}[\Phi(v)=j].

(12)

The next lemma tells us that the gradient of
F_{w,{\bf c}_{H,v}}
evaluated at a real log-ratio vector is exactly the vector
{\bf P}_{H,v}(w)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
w\in[0,1]
.
Let
(H,v)\in\mathcal{G}_{\Delta,q}^{\bullet}
and let
{\bf c}={\bf c}_{H,v}
be the vector of blocked colors at
v
.
Let
{\bf R}\in\mathbb{R}^{q-1}
be the vector defined by
R_{j}=R_{\overline{H},v;j,q}(w)
.
Then

\nabla F_{w,{\bf c}}({\bf R})={\bf P}_{H,v}(w).

Proof 2.5
.

Writing
P=P_{\bf c}(w,{\bf x})
and
Q=Q_{\bf c}(w,{\bf x})
, we have
F_{w,\bf c}({\bf x})=\log\left(\frac{P}{Q}\right)
and therefore by standard rules of partial derivatives,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{w,{\bf c}}}{\partial x_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q}{P}\cdot\frac{Q\tfrac{\partial P}{x_{j}}-P\tfrac{\partial Q}{\partial x_{j}}}{Q^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{Qw^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}+\delta_{1,j}Qw^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}(w-1)-Pw^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}}{PQ}

\displaystyle=\frac{(w-1)w^{c_{1}}e^{x_{1}}}{P}\delta_{1,j}+\frac{w^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}(Q-P)}{PQ}

\displaystyle=\frac{(w-1)w^{c_{1}}e^{x_{1}}}{P}\delta_{1,j}+\frac{w^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}}{P}-\frac{w^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}}{Q}.

Noting that
w^{c_{j}}e^{R_{j}}=\tfrac{Z_{H,v}^{j}(w)}{Z_{H,v}^{q}(w)}
and hence

P=w\frac{Z_{H,v}^{1}(w)}{Z_{H,v}^{q}(w)}+\sum_{j=2}^{q}\frac{Z_{H,v}^{j}(w)}{Z_{H,v}^{q}(w)}=\frac{Z_{H^{+1},v}(w)}{Z_{H,v}^{q}(w)},

and similarly,

Q=w\frac{Z_{H,v}^{q}(w)}{Z_{H,v}^{q}(w)}+\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}\frac{Z_{H,v}^{j}(w)}{Z_{H,v}^{q}(w)}=\frac{Z_{H^{+q},v}(w)}{Z_{H,v}^{q}(w)},

we see that

\frac{\partial F_{w,{\bf c}}}{\partial x_{j}}({\bf R})=\mathbb{P}_{H^{+1},w}[\Phi(v)=1]-\mathbb{P}_{H^{+q},w}[\Phi(v)=1]

i.e.
\nabla F_{w,{\bf c}}({\bf R})={\bf P}_{H,v}(w)
, as desired.

To use (
11
), we actually need to understand the gradient of
F_{w,{\bf c}}
evaluated at the vector
tR_{\overline{G}_{i},v_{i};j,q}(\tilde{w})\allowbreak+(1-t)R_{\overline{G}_{i},v_{i};j,q}(\tilde{w})
for some small perturbation
\tilde{w}
of
w
.
However, it is not difficult to see that for
\tilde{w}
small enough and
q>\Delta+1
, this gradient can be made arbitrarily close to
{\bf P}_{{G}_{i},v_{i}}(w)
independent of the graph.
We verify this formally in Section
4
.

So to bound (
11
), the essential ingredient is to bound the absolute value of the inner product of the vector
{\bf P}_{{G}_{i},v_{i}}(w)
with the vector
{\bf R}_{\overline{G}_{i},v_{i};j,q}(w)-{\bf R}_{\overline{G}_{i},v_{i};j,q}(\tilde{w})
for each
i=1,\ldots,d
.
This motivates us to develop bounds on the marginal probability of the root vertex, which we will do in Section
3
and Section
6
.

With the bounds from Section
3
and Section
4
we give a proof of Theorem
1.1
for
\eta=0
in Section
5
.
We do this for two reasons.
First of all, it makes it easier to see the structure of the technically more involved proof for the proof when
\eta>0
, and secondly, it gives an alternative and arguably more transparent proof of the result of Liu, Sinclair, and Srivastava
[
LSS2Delta
]
.
The ingredients that we use are pretty much the same as in
[
LSS2Delta
]
; the main difference is that we do not bound the real part and imaginary part of the log-ratio difference separately as is done in
[
LSS2Delta
]
, but our novel contribution is to make use of the symmetry between the colors to bound the absolute value of the inner product of the vector
{\bf P}_{G,v}(w)
with the vector
{\bf R}^{i}(w)-{\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w})
in terms of a marginal probability of the root vertex times the maximum of

|{R}_{\overline{G}_{i},v_{i},j,q}(w)-{R}_{\overline{G},v;j,q}(\tilde{w})|\textrm{ and }|{R}_{\overline{G}_{i},v_{i};j,1}(w)-{R}_{\overline{G},v;j,1}(\tilde{w})|,

(13)

over all
j=1,\ldots,q
(see Lemma
4.6
), which in our proof we show is bounded by
\varepsilon_{2}
, by induction.
Since for
q\geq 2\Delta
this marginal probability is easily seen to be bounded by
1/\Delta
(see Lemma
3.1
below), this allows to conclude that
|R_{G,v;1,q}(w)-R_{G,v;1,q}(\tilde{w})|
is again bounded by
\varepsilon_{2}
. See Section
5
for the details.

In Section
we finally give a proof of Theorem
2.1
for
\eta>0
.
Here we build on the approach for
\eta=0
and carefully make use of the structure of the local neighborhood of the vertex
v
to show that either both terms in (
13
) are actually smaller than
\varepsilon_{2}
or that we can use sharper bounds on the marginal probability obtained in Section
6
that are valid in a more restricted setting.

In an appendix, we collect an alternative proof of a proposition found in Section
4
that is more hands on, but has the advantage that it gives concrete dependencies on how small
\varepsilon_{1}
should be in terms of
\Delta
.
We conclude with some questions and remarks in Section
.

3
Basic bounds on marginal probabilities of the root vertex

Let
\Delta
and
q>\Delta+1
be positive integers and let
w\in[0,1]
.
Let
G
be a partially
q
-colored graph of maximum degree at most
\Delta
and let
v
be a vertex of
G
of degree
d
.
To prove our main result we will need bounds on the marginal probability of the root vertex
\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=j]
.
In this section we collect upper and lower bounds on this quantity that are well known in the literature, but we provide proofs for the sake of completeness and because we will build on these proofs later on.
We start with an upper bound.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Delta
and
q>\Delta+1
be positive integers.
Assume that
G
is a partially
q
-colored graph of maximum degree at most
\Delta
and let
v
be a vertex of
G
of degree
d
and free degree
f
.
Denote by
b
the number of blocked colors at
v
.
Then for any
w\in[0,1]
any color
j
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=j]\leq\frac{w^{c_{j}}}{q-(f+b)+(f+b)w},

where
c_{j}={c}_{G,v;j}
.
In particular,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=j]\leq\frac{w^{c_{j}}}{q-d+dw}.

Proof 3.2
.

We can expand
\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=1]
over the colorings of the neighbors of
v
.
This yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=j]=\sum_{\kappa:N(v)\to[q]}\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=j\mid E_{\kappa}]\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[E_{\kappa}],

(14)

where
E_{\kappa}
denotes the event that the random coloring
\Phi
agrees with
\kappa
on the neighbors
N(v)
of
v
.
Note that when
w>0
, we always have that
\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[E_{\kappa}]>0
, while if
w=0
we only sum over those
\kappa
for which
\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[E_{\kappa}]>0
.

Now to bound
\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=j]
, we simply need a bound on
\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=j\mid E_{\kappa}]
for any
\kappa
for which
\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[E_{\kappa}]>0
.
Let us fix such a
\kappa
and denote by
d_{i}
for
i\in[q]
the number of neighbors of
v
colored with color
i
.
Then by the Markov property and using the convention that
w^{0}=1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=j\mid E_{\kappa}]

\displaystyle=\frac{w^{d_{j}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{q}w^{d_{i}}}=\frac{w^{d_{j}}}{q-\sum_{i=1}^{q}(1-w^{d_{i}})}

(15)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{w^{c_{j}}}{q-(1-w)(b+f)},

(16)

where
c_{j}=c_{G,v;j}
denotes the number of pinned neighbors with color
j
in
G
,
b
denotes the number of blocked colors at
v
, and
f
the free degree of
v
.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that the lemma above is essentially tight for
w=0
and
j
such that
c_{j}=0
. Indeed consider a vertex whose neighborhood is a clique of size
\Delta-1
such that for each neighbor color
j
is blocked.

Next we provide a lower bound on the marginal probability.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Delta
and
q
be positive integers, and let
q\geq(1+\alpha)\Delta+1
for some
\alpha>0
.
Assume that
G
is a partially
q
-colored graph of maximum degree at most
\Delta
and let
v
be a vertex of
G
.
Then, for any
w\in[0,1]
and any color
j\in[q]
not appearing on the neighbors of
v
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=j]\geq\frac{1}{e^{1/\alpha}q}.

Proof 3.5
.

To prove a lower bound on the marginal probability of the root vertex getting a free color
j
, we again look at (
14
) and note that we can lower bound this by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=j]\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\kappa:N(v)\to[q]\\
j\notin\kappa(N(v))\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=j\mid E_{\kappa}]\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[E_{\kappa}].

Given
\kappa:N(v)\to[q]
such that
\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[E_{\kappa}]\neq 0
and
\kappa
does not use color
j
we can lower bound
\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=j\mid E_{\kappa}]
by
1/q
by (
15
).
This implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=j]\geq\frac{1}{q}\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[j\notin\Phi(N(v))].

Let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}
be the free neighbors of
v
in
G
.
We can then express

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[j\notin\Phi(N(v))]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[1\notin\Phi(\{u_{1},\ldots u_{d-1}\})\mid\Phi(u_{d})\neq j]\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(u_{d})\neq j]

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(u_{i})\neq j\mid E_{i}]

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(1-\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(u_{i})=j\mid E_{i}]\right),

(17)

where
E_{i}
denotes the event that the random coloring
\Phi
does not assign color
j
to the vertices
u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{d}
, which has a nonzero probability since
q>\Delta+1
.
Arguing as above (regardless of the event
E_{i}
, we can expand the marginal probability as a sum over
\kappa
as in (
14
), but now we only sum over those
\kappa
that do not assign color
j
to the vertices
u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{d}
) we can bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(u_{i})=j\mid E_{i}]\leq\frac{1}{q-\Delta+\Delta w}.

Therefore, assuming
q-\Delta\geq\alpha\Delta+1
for some
\alpha>0
, we obtain the lower bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G}[j\notin\Phi(N(v))]\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{q-\Delta+\Delta w}\right)^{d}\geq\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha\Delta+1}\right)^{\alpha\Delta}\right)^{1/\alpha}\geq e^{-1/\alpha}.

(18)

We conclude that
\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=1]
is lower bounded by
\tfrac{1}{e^{1/\alpha}q}
.

4
The behavior of
F_{w,{\bf c}}
and
\nabla F_{w,{\bf c}}

As indicated in Section
2
, we need to show that behavior of
F_{w,{\bf c}}
(as defined in (
8
)) and
\nabla F_{w,\bf{c}}
as a function of
w
is not too wild.

Let
\Delta,q
be positive integers such that
q\geq(1+\alpha)\Delta+1
for some
\alpha>0
.
We call a vector
{\bf c}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}^{q}
a
valid color vector
if there exists a rooted graph
(G,v)\in\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{\Delta,q}
such that
{\bf c}={\bf c}_{G,v}
.
Now fix a valid color vector
\bf c
and let us define the set

\mathcal{R}_{\bf c}=\left\{\left(w,\left(R_{\overline{G},v;j,q}(w)\right)_{j=1,\dots,q-1}\right)~|~(G,v)\in\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{\Delta,q},{\bf c}={\bf c}_{G,v},w\in[0,1]\right\}.

(19)

Define
F:\mathcal{R}_{\bf c}\to\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{C}
by
F(w,{\bf R})=F_{w,\bf c}({\bf R})
for
(w,{\bf R)}\in\mathcal{R}_{\bf c}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Delta,q
be positive integers such that
q\geq(1+\alpha)\Delta+1
for some
\alpha>0
, and let
{\bf c}
be a valid color vector.
Then the set
F(\mathcal{R}_{\bf c})
is bounded.

Proof 4.2
.

For any
(w,{\bf R})\in\mathcal{R}_{\bf c}
,
j\in[q-1]
we have

\exp(R_{j})=\frac{Z^{j}_{\overline{G},v}(w)}{Z^{q}_{\overline{G},v}(w)}=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\overline{G},w}[\Phi(v)=j]}{\mathbb{P}_{\overline{G},w}[\Phi(v)=q]},

and therefore by Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.4
,

\frac{q-\Delta}{qe^{1/\alpha}}\leq\exp(R_{j})\leq\frac{qe^{1/\alpha}}{q-\Delta}.

Then, since at least
q-\Delta
entries of
{\bf c}
are equal to
0
and
w\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\frac{(q-\Delta)^{2}}{qe^{1/\alpha}}\leq

\displaystyle P_{\bf c}(w,{\bf R})\leq\frac{q^{2}e^{1/\alpha}}{q-\Delta},

\displaystyle\frac{(q-\Delta)^{2}}{qe^{1/\alpha}}\leq

\displaystyle Q_{\bf c}(w,{\bf R})\leq\frac{q^{2}e^{1/\alpha}}{q-\Delta},

where we recall that
P_{\bf c}
and
Q_{\bf c}
are defined in (
5
) and (
6
), respectively.
It thus follows that

\frac{(q-\Delta)^{3}}{q^{3}e^{2/\alpha}}\leq\left|\frac{P_{\bf c}(w,{\bf R})}{Q_{\bf c}(w,{\bf R})}\right|\leq\frac{q^{3}e^{2/\alpha}}{(q-\Delta)^{3}}.

Since
F_{c}=\log(P_{\bf c}/Q_{\bf c})
, this implies that
F(\mathcal{R}_{\bf c})
is a bounded set, as desired.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\alpha>0
and let
q,\Delta
be positive integers such that
q\geq(1+\alpha)\Delta+1
.
Then for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
there exists a
\delta>0
such that the following holds.
Let
(G,v)\in\mathcal{G}_{\Delta,q}^{\bullet}
, and let
w\in[0,1]
.
Let
{\bf R}\in\mathbb{R}^{q-1}
be the vector defined by
R_{j}=R_{\overline{G},v;j,q}(w)
.
Then

(i)

if
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{C}^{q-1}
and
\|{\bf R}-{\bf x}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta
, then

\|{\bf P}_{G,v}(w)-\nabla F_{w,{\bf c}}({\bf x})\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon;

(ii)

if
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{C}^{q-1}
and
\|{\bf R}-{\bf x}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta
and
|\tilde{w}-w|\leq\delta
, then

|F_{w,{\bf c}_{G,v}}({\bf x})-F_{\tilde{w},{\bf c}_{G,v}}({\bf x})|\leq\varepsilon.

Remark 4.4
.

Below we give a concise proof using a compactness argument, but which does not display how
\delta
depends on
\varepsilon
.
We refer the reader to Appendix
for an explicit proof of this proposition, where we also show
\delta
can be taken of the form
\min\left\{C(\alpha)\varepsilon,C(\alpha)/\Delta\right\}
for some constant
C(\alpha)
that only depends on
\alpha
.

Proof 4.5
.

Let us fix a valid color vector
{\bf c}
.
Since by the previous lemma we know that
F
is bounded on
\mathcal{R}_{c}
, we know that there exists
an
r>0
such that
F
is well-defined and bounded on the compact set

\mathcal{D}_{\bm{c}}:=\overline{B(\mathcal{R}_{\bm{c}},r)}\subseteq\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{q-1},

where we view
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{q-1}
as the direct sum of the normed space
\mathbb{C}
with the absolute value and
\mathbb{C}^{q-1}
with the
\infty
-norm, and where
\overline{B(\mathcal{R}_{\bm{c}},r)}
denotes the closure of the set of points that have distance at most
r
to
\mathcal{R}_{\bm{c}}
.

Now
\nabla F_{w,\bm{c}}:\mathcal{D}_{\bm{c}}\to\mathbb{C}^{q-1}
(defined by
(w,{\bf x})\mapsto\nabla F_{w,\bf c}({\bf x})
) is a continuous function on a compact set.
Therefore, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists an
r\geq\delta_{1,\bf c}>0
, such that
if
(w,{\bf x}),(w,{\bf y})\in\mathcal{D}_{\bf c}
and
\|{\bf x}-{\bf y}\|_{1}\leq\delta_{1,\bf c}
, then

\|\nabla F_{w,\bf c}({\bf x})-\nabla F_{w,\bf c}({\bf y})\|\leq\varepsilon.

Similarly, there exists
\delta_{2,{\bf c}}
such that for any
(w,{\bf x}),(w^{\prime},\bf y)\in\mathcal{D}_{\bf c}
we have

|F_{w,{\bf c}}({\bf x})-F_{w^{\prime},{\bf c}}({\bf y})|\leq\varepsilon.

There are finitely many choices of
\bf c
for a given
q
and
\Delta
, therefore we can choose
\delta^{\prime}:=\min\{\delta_{1,\bf c},\delta_{2,{\bf c}}\mid{\bf c}\text{ valid color vector}\}>0
and set
\delta=\delta^{\prime}/2
.

For this choice of
\delta
we have for the given
(w,{\bf R})\in\mathcal{R}_{{\bf c}_{G,v}}
and any
{\bf x}
such that
\|{\bf x}-{\bf R}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta
,

\|{\bf P}_{G,v}-\nabla F_{w,{\bf c}_{G,v}}({\bf x})\|_{1}=\|\nabla F_{w,{\bf c}_{G,v}}({\bf R})-\nabla F_{w,{\bf c}_{G,v}}({\bf x})\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon,

and similarly, for any
(\tilde{w},{\bf x})
such that
|\tilde{w}-w|\leq\delta
and
\|{\bf R}-{\bf x}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta
(so that
\|(w,{\bf R})-(\tilde{w},{\bf x})\|\leq\delta^{\prime}
) we have

|F_{w,{\bf c}_{G,v}}({\bf R})-F_{\tilde{w},{\bf c}_{G,v}}({\bf x})|\leq\varepsilon.

This finishes the proof.

The next lemma allows us in combination with Proposition

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
G
be a partially
q
-colored graph and let

be a free vertex of

Let
w\in[0,1]
and let
{\bf P}_{G,v}
be defined as in (

Let
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{C}^{q-1}
and let
\widehat{{\bf x}}
be defined by
\widehat{x}_{1}=-x_{1}

\widehat{x}_{j}=x_{j}-x_{1}

Then

|\langle{\bf P}_{G,v},{\bf x}\rangle|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1-w)\mathbb{P}_{G^{+1},w}[\Phi(v)=q]\cdot\|{\bf x}\|_{\infty}&\textrm{if $\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=1]\leq\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=q]$}\\

Proof 4.7
.

First of all, note that possibly after multiplication of the coordinates of
{\bf x}
by

for some

, we may assume that

|\langle{\bf P}_{G,v},{\bf x}\rangle|=\langle{\bf P}_{G,v},{\bf x}\rangle=\langle{\bf P}_{G,v},\Re({\bf x})\rangle,

since
{\bf P}_{G,v}
is real valued.
We may therefore restrict to real vectors
{\bf x}
.

Next observe that the first coordinate of
{\bf P}_{G,v}
is non-positive, since

\displaystyle P_{G,v;1}(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{wZ_{G,v}^{1}(w)}{Z_{G}(w)-(1-w)Z_{G,v}^{1}(w)}-\frac{Z_{G,v}^{1}(w)}{Z_{G}(w)-(1-w)Z_{G,v}^{q}(w)}

Note that with similar reasoning it follows that

\mathbb{P}_{G^{+1},w}[\Phi(v)=q]-\mathbb{P}_{G^{+q},w}[\Phi(v)=q]\geq 0.

(20)

Next we distinguish the two cases.

\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=1]\leq\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=q]

and for

\displaystyle P_{G,v;j}

\displaystyle=\frac{Z_{G,v}^{j}(w)}{Z_{G}(w)-(1-w)Z_{G,v}^{1}(w)}-\frac{Z_{G,v}^{j}(w)}{Z_{G}(w)-(1-w)Z_{G,v}^{q}(w)}\leq 0.

\displaystyle\langle{\bf P}_{G,v},{\bf x}\rangle

\displaystyle=P_{G,v;1}x_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{q-1}P_{G,v;j}(x_{1}+\widehat{x}_{j})

\displaystyle=x_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}P_{G,v;j}+\sum_{j=2}^{q-1}P_{G,v;j}\widehat{x}_{j}

\displaystyle=-x_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{G^{+1},w}[\Phi(v)=q]-\mathbb{P}_{G^{+q},w}[\Phi(v)=q])+\sum_{j=2}^{q-1}P_{G,v;j}\widehat{x}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}\widehat{P}_{G,v;j}\widehat{x}_{j}=\langle{\bf\widehat{P}}_{G,v},\widehat{{\bf x}}\rangle,

\widehat{P}_{G,v;j}=P_{G,v;j}

and
\widehat{P}_{G,v;1}=\mathbb{P}_{G^{+1}}[v=q]-\mathbb{P}_{G^{+q}}[v=q])

We note that by construction and (

\displaystyle\langle{\bf\widehat{P}}_{G,v},{\bf\widehat{x}}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\widehat{{\bf x}}\|_{\infty}\|{\bf\widehat{P}}_{G,v}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\|\widehat{{\bf x}}\|_{\infty}|P_{G,v;1}|\leq\|\widehat{{\bf x}}\|_{\infty}(1-w)\mathbb{P}_{G^{+q}}[v=1],

where the last inequality follows in the same way as (

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

\displaystyle|\tilde{w}-w|<\varepsilon_{1}\quad\text{ and }\quad|x_{j}-R_{\overline{G},v;j,\ell}(w)|<\varepsilon_{2},\text{ for all }j,\ell\in[q]

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j\neq\ell}\tilde{w}^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}+\tilde{w}^{c_{\ell}+\tau}\right|\geq C(\alpha)\Delta,

(22)

where

Proof 5.2

(Proof of Theorem
2.1
for
\bm{\eta=0}
)
.

Throughout the proof

and

are fixed and whenever we refer to a graph we in fact mean a partially
q
-colored graph.

We will prove the theorem by induction on the number of free vertices of the graph, the base case being a connected rooted graph
(G,v)\in\mathcal{G}_{q}^{\bullet}
where
v
is the only free vertex of
G
.
Since the pinned vertices of
G
form an independent set,
(\overline{G},v)
is just an isolated vertex and hence for any pair of colors
i,j

R_{\overline{G},v;i,j}(\tilde{w})=\log(1)=0
for any

Now for the second part, we apply Lemma
5.1
with
\alpha=2/3

{\bf R}={\bf x}={\bf 0}
and
\tau=0
to see that provided
\varepsilon_{1}\leq C_{1}(2/3)
and
\varepsilon_{2}\leq\pi/8

\displaystyle|Z_{G}(\tilde{w})|=\left|\sum_{j=1}^{q}\tilde{w}^{c_{j}}\right|\geq C(2/3)\Delta>0,

and hence in particular,
Z_{G}(\tilde{w})\neq 0
.
This finishes the verification of the base case.

Next consider a partially
q
-colored graph
G
of maximum degree at most
\Delta
with more than one free vertex, all of whose pinned vertices are all leaves and form an independent set.
Let
v
be a free vertex of free degree

.
We need to show

and
Z_{G}(\tilde{w})\neq 0
.
By symmetry, we may assume that
i=1,j=q

Note that by induction these log-ratios are well-defined since

for all

in

in
\overline{G}

Choose an ordering of the free neighbors

Let

\displaystyle|R_{\overline{G},v;1,q}(\tilde{w})-R_{\overline{G},v;1,q}(w)|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\underbrace{|F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(w))-F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w}))|}_{A_{i}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\underbrace{|F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w}))-F_{\tilde{w},{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(\tilde{w}))|}_{B_{i}},

\displaystyle A_{i}=|F_{w,{\bf c}^{i}}({\bf R}^{i}(w))

\displaystyle B_{i}\leq\varepsilon_{2}/(3\Delta^{2}).

\displaystyle|R_{\overline{G},v;1,q}(\tilde{w})-R_{\overline{G},v;1,q}(w)|

\displaystyle 0\neq\frac{Z_{G}(\tilde{w})}{Z_{G,v}^{\ell}(\tilde{w})}=\sum_{j=1}^{q}{\tilde{w}}^{c_{j}}e^{R_{\overline{G},v;j,\ell}(\tilde{w})}.

Additionally, we need

Remark 5.4

Note that the proof actually shows that we can inductively bound the log-ratio difference by

Lemma 5.5

(Barvinok
[
Barbook
, Lemma 3.6.3]
)
.

Let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be non-zero vectors such that the angle between any vectors

and
u_{j}
is at most
\varphi
for some
\varphi\in[0,2\pi/3)

Then the

all lie in a cone of angle at most

\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k}u_{j}\right|\geq\cos(\varphi/2)\sum_{j=1}^{k}|u_{j}|.

Proof 5.6

(Proof of Lemma
5.1
)
.

For the proof we may without loss of generality assume that
\ell=q
.

Let
w^{\star}=\varepsilon_{1}/\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{8\Delta}\right)
.
We distinguish the cases
w\leq w^{\star}
and
w>w^{\star}
.

First consider the case
w\leq w^{\star}

By Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.4
, and by noting that
q/(q-\Delta)
is decreasing in

for
q>\Delta
, we have that

\frac{\alpha\Delta+1}{\left((1+\alpha)\Delta+1\right)e^{1/\alpha}}\leq\frac{q-\Delta}{qe^{1/\alpha}}\leq\exp(R_{j})\leq\frac{qe^{1/\alpha}}{q-\Delta}\leq\frac{\left((1+\alpha)\Delta+1\right)e^{1/\alpha}}{\alpha\Delta+1},

and therefore in particular

\frac{\alpha}{(1+\alpha)e^{1/\alpha}}\leq\exp(R_{j})\leq\frac{(1+\alpha)e^{1/\alpha}}{\alpha}.

(28)

Thus, by assumption we have
M^{-1}e^{-\varepsilon_{2}}\leq e^{x_{j}}\leq Me^{\varepsilon_{2}}
, where
M=\frac{(1+\alpha)e^{1/\alpha}}{\alpha}
, and that all
e^{x_{j}}
are contained in a cone of angle at most
2\varepsilon_{2}\leq\pi/4
, centered around the real axis.
Moreover, we remark that at least
q-\Delta
entries of
\bm{c}
are zero (including possibly
c_{q}
). Let
S_{0}\subset[q-1]
denote the indices for which this is the case.
We obtain

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}\tilde{w}^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}+\tilde{w}^{c_{q}+\tau}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\sum_{j\in S_{0}}\tilde{w}^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}\right|-\left|\sum_{j\in[q-1]\setminus{S_{0}}}\tilde{w}^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}+\tilde{w}^{c_{q}+\tau}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\cos(\varepsilon_{2})\sum_{j\in S_{0}}|e^{x_{j}}|-\sum_{j\in[q-1]\setminus{S_{0}}}|\tilde{w}|^{c_{j}}|e^{x_{j}}|-|\tilde{w}^{c_{q}+\tau}|

\displaystyle\geq\cos(\varepsilon_{2})\frac{(q-\Delta-1)}{Me^{\varepsilon_{2}}}-\Delta e^{\varepsilon_{2}}M(\varepsilon_{1}+w^{\star})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(q-\Delta-1)}{2M}-2\Delta M(\varepsilon_{1}+w^{\star}).

In the second step we used Lemma
5.5
, and in the last step we used the fact that
\varepsilon_{2}\leq\pi/8<\frac{1}{2}
thus
\cos(\varepsilon_{2})e^{-\varepsilon_{2}}\geq 1/2
.

For the case
w>w^{\star}
, we find that

\displaystyle|\arg(\tilde{w}^{c_{j}+\tau}e^{x_{j}})|\leq(c_{j}+\tau)|\arg{\tilde{w}}|+\varepsilon_{2}\leq(c_{j}+\tau)\arcsin\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{w^{\star}}\right)+\varepsilon_{2}\leq\Delta\frac{\pi}{8\Delta}+\frac{\pi}{8}\leq\frac{\pi}{4}.

Using Lemma
5.5
again, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}\tilde{w}^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}+\tilde{w}^{c_{q}+\tau}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}\left|\tilde{w}^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}\right|+\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\left|\tilde{w}^{c_{q}+\tau}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\sum_{j\in S_{0}}\left|\tilde{w}^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(q-\Delta-1)}{M}\frac{\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)}{e^{\varepsilon_{2}}}.

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(q-\Delta-1)}{3M}.

Combining the two cases, we may conclude that

\left|\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}\tilde{w}^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}+\tilde{w}^{c_{q}+\tau}\right|\geq\frac{(q-\Delta-1)}{3M}-2\Delta M\varepsilon_{1}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{8\Delta}\right)}\right).

Let us further bound this by observing that

\frac{q-\Delta-1}{M}\geq\frac{\alpha^{2}}{(1+\alpha)e^{1/\alpha}}\Delta,

and

\displaystyle 1+\frac{1}{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{8\Delta}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{8\Delta}\right)+1}{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{8\Delta}\right)}\leq\frac{2}{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{8\Delta}\right)}\leq\frac{2}{\left(\frac{\pi}{8\Delta}\right)/2}=\frac{32\Delta}{\pi}\leq 11\Delta.

This yields us

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}\tilde{w}^{c_{j}}e^{x_{j}}+\tilde{w}^{c_{q}+\tau}\right|\geq\left(\underbrace{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{3(1+\alpha)e^{1/\alpha}}}_{f(\alpha)}-\underbrace{22\frac{e^{1/\alpha}(1+\alpha)}{\alpha}}_{g(\alpha)}\varepsilon_{1}\Delta\right)\Delta.

Rewriting the right-hand side as
(f(\alpha)-g(\alpha)\varepsilon_{1}\Delta)\Delta
, we see that if
0\leq\varepsilon_{1}\leq\frac{f(\alpha)}{2g(\alpha)\Delta}
, then

(f(\alpha)-g(\alpha)\varepsilon_{1}\Delta)\Delta\geq\tfrac{1}{2}f(\alpha)\Delta.

This means that the choice
C(\alpha)=\tfrac{1}{2}f(\alpha)
and
C_{1}(\alpha)=\frac{f(\alpha)}{2g(\alpha)}
satisfies the condition of the lemma.

We collect here some results that improve on the bounds on the marginal probability of the root vertex from Lemma
3.1
, under additional assumptions on the local structure of the neighborhood of the root vertex that we will utilize in the proof of the main theorem for
\eta>0
in the next section.
For the proof of Theorem
2.1
one only needs the conclusions of Corollaries
and
. Apart from that, this section can be read independently of the next section.

6.1
Local influences on the probabilities

Our first lemma shows how to improve the bound from Lemma
3.1
under the additional assumption that not all neighbors of the root vertex have color
j
blocked.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
q
and
\Delta
be positive integers such that
q\geq(1+\alpha)\Delta+1
for some
\alpha>0
and let
G
be a partially
q
-colored graph of maximum degree at most
\Delta
and let
v\in V(G)
be a free vertex of degree
d
and with free-degree
f
.
Let
\gamma\in[0,1]
and let
j\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
be a free color at
v
.
Suppose that color
j
is blocked for at most
(1-\gamma)f
of the free neighbors of
v

Then for any
w\in[0,1]
,

\mathbb{P}_{G,w}[\Phi(v)=j]\leq\frac{(1-w)\exp\left(\tfrac{-\gamma f}{qe^{1/\alpha}}\right)+w}{q-d+dw}.

Proof 6.2
.

Throughout we will fix
w
and just write
\mathbb{P}_{G}
instead of
\mathbb{P}_{G,w}
.
By (
14
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G}[\Phi(v)=j]=\sum_{\kappa}\mathbb{P}_{G}[\Phi(v)=j\mid E_{\kappa}]\mathbb{P}_{G}[E_{\kappa}],

where
E_{\kappa}
denotes the event that the random coloring
\Phi
agrees with
\kappa
on
N(v)
, and we sum over all colorings
\kappa
of the neighbors of
v
for which
\mathbb{P}_{G}[E_{\kappa}]\neq 0
.
By (
16
), for a given
\kappa
that does not use color
j
, we can bound
\mathbb{P}_{G}[v=j\mid E_{\kappa}]
by
\tfrac{1}{q-d+dw}
, and if
\kappa
does use color
j
, then we bound this probability by
\tfrac{w}{q-d+dw}
.
So we can bound
\mathbb{P}_{G}[\Phi(v)=j]
by

\displaystyle\frac{1-w}{q-d+dw}\mathbb{P}_{G}[j\notin\Phi(N(v))]+\frac{w}{q-d+dw}.

Our next step is to bound
\mathbb{P}_{G}[j\notin\Phi(N(v))]
.
Let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{t}
be the free neighbors of
v
for which color
j
is not blocked.
Note that
t\geq\gamma f.

17
), we can write

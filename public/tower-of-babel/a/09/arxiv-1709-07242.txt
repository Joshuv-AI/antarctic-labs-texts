The ATLAS Collaboration

Abstract
A search for heavy neutral Higgs bosons and Z ′ Z^{\prime} bosons is performed using a data sample corresponding to an integrated luminosity of from proton--proton collisions at s = 13 TeV \sqrt{s}=$13\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}$ recorded by the ATLAS detector at the LHC during 2015 and 2016. The heavy resonance is assumed to decay to τ + ​ τ − \tau^{+}\tau^{-} with at least one tau lepton decaying to final states with hadrons and a neutrino. The search is performed in the mass range of 0.2– 2.25 TeV 2.25\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV} for Higgs bosons and 0.2– 4.0 TeV 4.0\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV} for Z ′ Z^{\prime} bosons. The data are in good agreement with the background predicted by the Standard Model. The results are interpreted in benchmark scenarios. In the context of the hMSSM scenario, the data exclude tan ⁡ β > 1.0 \tan\beta>1.0 for m A = 0.25 TeV m_{A}=$0.25\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}$ and tan ⁡ β > 42 \tan\beta>42 for m A = 1.5 TeV m_{A}=$1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}$ at the 95% confidence level. For the Sequential Standard Model, Z SSM ′ Z^{\prime}_{\mathrm{SSM}} with m Z ′ < 2.42 TeV m_{Z^{\prime}}<$2.42\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}$ is excluded at 95% confidence level, while Z NU ′ Z^{\prime}_{\mathrm{NU}} with m Z ′ < 2.25 TeV m_{Z^{\prime}}<$2.25\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}$ is excluded for the non-universal G ⁡ ( 221 ) G(221) model that exhibits enhanced couplings to third-generation fermions.

2
ATLAS detector

The ATLAS detector
[
43
]
at the LHC covers nearly the entire solid
angle around the collision point.
3
3

3

ATLAS uses a right-handed coordinate system with its origin at the nominal interaction point
in the centre of the detector and the
z
-axis along the beam pipe.
The
x
-axis points from the interaction point to the centre of the LHC ring,
and the
y
-axis points upwards.
Cylindrical coordinates
(r,\phi)
are used in the transverse plane,

\phi
being the azimuthal angle around the
z
-axis.
The pseudorapidity is defined in terms of the polar angle
\theta
as
\eta=-\ln\tan(\theta/2)
.
Angular distance is measured in units of
\Delta R\equiv\sqrt{(\Delta\eta)^{2}+(\Delta\phi)^{2}}
.
It consists of an inner tracking detector surrounded
by a thin superconducting solenoid, electromagnetic and hadronic calorimeters,
and a muon spectrometer incorporating three large superconducting toroid magnets.
The inner-detector system is immersed in a
2\text{\,}\mathrm{T}
axial magnetic field
and provides charged-particle tracking in the range
|\eta|<2.5
.

The high-granularity silicon pixel detector covers the vertex region and typically provides
four measurements per track. The innermost layer, known as the insertable B-Layer
[
44
]
, was added in 2014 and provides
high-resolution hits at small radius to improve the tracking performance. The pixel detector is
surrounded by the silicon microstrip tracker, which usually provides four two-dimensional measurement
points per track. These silicon detectors are complemented by the transition radiation tracker,
which enables radially extended track reconstruction up to
|\eta|=2.0
. The transition radiation
tracker also provides electron identification information based on the fraction of hits (typically
30 in total) above a higher energy-deposit threshold corresponding to transition radiation.

The calorimeter system covers the pseudorapidity range
|\eta|<4.9
.
Within the region
|\eta|<3.2
, electromagnetic calorimetry is provided by barrel and
endcap high-granularity lead/liquid-argon (LAr) electromagnetic calorimeters,
with an additional thin LAr presampler covering
|\eta|<1.8
,
to correct for energy loss in material upstream of the calorimeters.
Hadronic calorimetry is provided by the steel/scintillator-tile calorimeter,
segmented into three barrel structures within
|\eta|<1.7
, and two copper/LAr hadronic endcap
calorimeters that cover
1.5<|\eta|<3.2
. The solid angle coverage is completed with forward
copper/LAr and tungsten/LAr calorimeter modules, optimised for electromagnetic and hadronic measurements
respectively, in the region
3.1<|\eta|<4.9
.

The muon spectrometer comprises separate trigger and high-precision tracking chambers measuring the
deflection of muons in a magnetic field generated by superconducting air-core toroids.
The precision chamber system covers the region
|\eta|<2.7
with three layers of monitored drift tubes,
complemented by cathode strip chambers in the forward region, where the background is highest.
The muon trigger system covers the range
|\eta|<2.4
with resistive plate chambers in the barrel, and
thin gap chambers in the endcap regions.

A two-level trigger system is used to select interesting events
[
45
,
46
]
.
The level-one trigger is implemented in hardware and uses a subset of detector information
to reduce the event rate to a design value of at most
100\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
.
This is followed by the software-based high-level trigger, which reduces the event rate to
1\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
.

4
Event reconstruction

Electron candidates are reconstructed from energy deposits in the
electromagnetic calorimeter associated with a charged-particle track measured
in the inner detector
[
118
,
119
,
120
]
.
The electron candidates are required to pass a “loose” likelihood-based
identification selection, to have a transverse momentum
p_{\text{T}}>$15\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}$
and to be in the fiducial volume of the inner detector,

|\eta|<2.47
. The transition region between the barrel and endcap
calorimeters (
1.37<|\eta|<1.52
) is excluded.

Muon candidates are reconstructed in the range
|\eta|<2.5
by matching tracks
found in the muon spectrometer to tracks found in the inner
detector
[
121
]
. The tracks of the muon candidates are re-fitted
using the complete track information from both detector systems. They are
required to have a transverse momentum
p_{\text{T}}>$7\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}$
and to pass a
“loose” muon identification requirement.

The selected lepton (electron or muon) in the
\tau_{\mathrm{lep}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel must then have

p_{\text{T}}>$30\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}$
and pass a “medium” identification requirement. This lepton is
considered isolated if it meets
p_{\text{T}}
- and
\eta
-dependent isolation criteria utilising
calorimetric and tracking information. The criteria correspond to an efficiency
of 90% (99%) for a transverse momentum of
p_{\text{T}}=25\penalty\ (60)\,\text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
. The efficiency
increases with lepton
p_{\text{T}}
as the requirements are relaxed to account for the
decreased background from misidentified jets.

Jets are reconstructed from topological clusters of energy depositions

[
122
]
in the calorimeter using the anti-
k_{t}

algorithm
[
123
,
124
]
, with a radius parameter value
R=0.4
. The average energy contribution from pile-up is subtracted according to
the jet area and the jets are calibrated as described in
Ref
[
125
]
. They are required to have
p_{\text{T}}>$20\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}$
and

|\eta|<2.5
. To reduce the effect of pile-up, a jet vertex tagger algorithm
is used for jets with
p_{\text{T}}<$60\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}$
and
|\eta|<2.4
. It employs a
multivariate technique based on jet energy, vertexing and tracking variables to
determine the likelihood that the jet originates from or is heavily
contaminated by pile-up
[
126
]
. In order to identify jets
containing
b
-hadrons (
b
-jets), a multivariate algorithm is used, which is
based on the presence of tracks with a large impact parameter with respect to
the primary vertex, the presence of displaced secondary vertices and the
reconstructed flight paths of
b
- and
c
-hadrons associated with the
jet
[
127
,
128
]
. The algorithm has an average
efficiency of 70% for
b
-jets and rejections of approximately 13, 56 and 380
for
c
-jets, hadronic tau decays and jets initiated by light quarks or gluons,
respectively, as determined in simulated
t\bar{t}
events.

Hadronic tau decays are composed of a neutrino and a set of visible decay
products (
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
), typically one or three charged pions and up to two
neutral pions. The reconstruction of the visible decay products is seeded by
jets
[
129
]
. The
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidates must have
p_{\text{T}}>25\penalty\ (45)\,\text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
in the
\tau_{\mathrm{lep}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
(
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
) channel,
|\eta|<2.5
excluding

1.37<|\eta|<1.52
, one or three associated tracks and an electric charge of

\pm 1
. The leading-
p_{\text{T}}

\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidate in the
\tau_{\mathrm{lep}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel and the
two leading-
p_{\text{T}}

\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidates in the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel are then selected
and all remaining candidates are considered as jets. A Boosted Decision Tree
(BDT) identification procedure, based on calorimetric shower shapes and
tracking information is used to reject backgrounds from
jets
[
130
,
131
]
. Two
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}

identification criteria are used: “loose” and “medium”, specified in
Section
5
. The criteria correspond to efficiencies of about 60%
(50%) and 55% (40%) in
Z/\gamma^{\ast}\to\tau\tau
events and rejections of about 30 (30)
and 50 (100) in multijet events, for one-track (three-track)
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}

candidates, respectively. An additional dedicated likelihood-based veto is
used to reduce the number of electrons misidentified as
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
in the

\tau_{\mathrm{lep}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel, providing 95% efficiency and a background rejection between
20 and 200, depending on the pseudorapidity of the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidate.

Geometrically overlapping objects are removed in the following order:

1.

jets within
\Delta R=0.2
of selected
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidates are excluded,

2.

jets within
\Delta R=0.4
of an electron or muon are excluded,

3.

any
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidate within
\Delta R=0.2
of an electron or muon is excluded,

4.

electrons within
\Delta R
= 0.2 of a muon are excluded.

The missing transverse momentum,
\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}
, is calculated as the
negative vectorial sum of the
\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}
of all fully
reconstructed and calibrated physics
objects
[
132
,
133
]
. This procedure
includes a “soft term”, which is calculated using the inner-detector
tracks that originate from the hard-scattering vertex but are not associated with
reconstructed objects.

5
Event selection

5.1
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel

Events in the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel are recorded using single-tau triggers with
p_{\text{T}}
thresholds of 80, 125 or
160\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}
, depending on the data-taking period.
Events must contain at least two
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidates with
p_{\text{T}}>$65\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}$
and no electrons or muons. The leading-
p_{\text{T}}

\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidate
must be geometrically matched to the trigger signature and must exceed the
trigger
p_{\text{T}}
threshold by
5\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}
. The leading and sub-leading
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidates must satisfy the “medium” and “loose” identification criteria,
respectively. They must also have opposite electric charge and be back to back
in the transverse plane:
|\Delta\phi(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\tau_{1}},\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\tau_{2}})|>$2.7\text{\,}\mathrm{r}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{d}$
, as tau leptons from the decay of heavy neutral resonances are
typically produced back to back in the transverse plane. The signal acceptance
times efficiency for this selection (calculated with respect to all possible
ditau final states) varies between 1% and 7% for signals with masses of

0.35\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}
or higher. The maximum occurs for signals with masses of around

0.9\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}
, degradations occur at lower masses due to the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}

p_{\text{T}}
thresholds and at higher masses due to the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
reconstruction and identification efficiencies. A summary of the selection is given in
Table
1
of Section
6
.

5.2
\tau_{\mathrm{lep}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel

Events in the
\tau_{\mathrm{lep}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel are recorded using single-electron and single-muon
triggers with
p_{\text{T}}
thresholds ranging from 20 to
140\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}
and various
isolation criteria. The events must contain at least one
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidate
passing the medium identification, exactly one isolated lepton (from here on
referred to as
\ell
) that is geometrically matched to the trigger signature
(implying
|\eta|<2.4
in the
\tau_{\mu}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel), and no additional
reconstructed leptons. The identified
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidate must have

|\eta|<2.3
to reduce background from misidentified electrons. The isolated
lepton and identified
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidate must have opposite electric charge
and be back to back in the transverse plane:
|\Delta\phi(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ell},\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}})|>$2.4\text{\,}\mathrm{r}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{d}$
. To reduce background
from
W\text{+\,jets}
production, the transverse mass of the isolated lepton and the
missing transverse momentum,

m_{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ell},\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}})\equiv\sqrt{2p_{\text{T}}^{\ell}E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}\left[1-\cos\Delta\phi(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ell},\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}})\right]}\,,

must be less than
40\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}
. To reduce background from
Z\rightarrow ee
production in
the
\tau_{e}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel, events where the isolated lepton and identified
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidate have an invariant mass between 80 and
110\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}
are rejected.
The signal acceptance times efficiency for this selection
also varies between 1% and 7%, but the maximum
occurs at lower masses due to the lower
p_{\text{T}}
thresholds on the tau decay products.
A summary of the selection is given
in Table
2
of Section
6
.

5.3
Event categories

Events satisfying the selection criteria in the
\tau_{\mathrm{lep}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
and
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channels are
categorised to exploit the different production modes in the MSSM. Events containing
at least one
b
-tagged jet enter the
b
-tag category, while events containing
no
b
-tagged jets enter the
b
-veto category. The categorisation is not used
for the
Z^{\prime}
search.

5.4
Ditau mass reconstruction

The ditau mass reconstruction is important for achieving good separation
between signal and background. However, ditau mass reconstruction is challenging due
to the presence of neutrinos from the
\tau
-lepton decays.
Therefore, the mass reconstruction used for both the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
and
\tau_{\mathrm{lep}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channels
is the total transverse mass, defined as:

m_{\mathrm{T}}^{\text{tot}}\equiv\sqrt{(p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\tau_{1}}+p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\tau_{2}}+E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}})^{2}-(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\tau_{1}}+\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\tau_{2}}+\mathbf{E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}})^{2}}\,,

where
\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\tau_{1}}
and
\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\tau_{2}}
are the momenta
of the visible tau decay products (including
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
and
\tau_{\mathrm{lep}}
) projected into the transverse plane.
More complex mass reconstruction techniques were investigated, but they did not improve the expected sensitivity.

6
Background estimation

The dominant background contribution in the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel is from multijet production, which is estimated using a data-driven technique, described in
Section
6.1
. Other important background contributions come
from
Z/\gamma^{\ast}\to\tau\tau
production at high
m_{\mathrm{T}}^{\text{tot}}
in the
b
-veto category,
t\bar{t}
production
in the
b
-tag category, and to a lesser extent
W(\to\ell\nu)\mathrm{+jets}
, single top-quark, diboson and
Z/\gamma^{\ast}(\to\ell\ell)\mathrm{+jets}
production. These contributions are estimated using simulation.
Corrections are applied to the simulation to account for mismodelling of the
trigger, reconstruction, identification and isolation efficiencies, the
electron to
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
misidentification rate and the momentum scales and
resolutions. To further improve the modelling in the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel, events in
the simulation that contain quark- or gluon-initiated jets (henceforth called
jets) that are misidentified as
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidates are weighted by
fake-rates measured in
W\text{+\,jets}
and
t\bar{t}
control regions in data.

The dominant background contribution in the
\tau_{\mathrm{lep}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel arises from
processes where the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidate originates from a jet. This
contribution is estimated using a data-driven technique similar to the

\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel, described
in Section
6.2
. The events are divided into those where the
selected lepton is correctly identified, predominantly from
W\text{+\,jets}
(
t\bar{t}
)
production in the
b
-veto (
b
-tag) channel, and those where the selected lepton
arises from a jet, predominantly from multijet production. Backgrounds where
both the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
and lepton candidates originate from electrons, muons or
taus (real-lepton) arise from
Z/\gamma^{\ast}\to\tau\tau
production in the
b
-veto category and
t\bar{t}
production in the
b
-tag category, with minor contributions from
Z/\gamma^{\ast}\to\ell\ell
, diboson and single top-quark production. These contributions are estimated using simulation.
To help constrain the normalisation of the
t\bar{t}
contribution, a
control region rich in
t\bar{t}
events (CR-T) is defined and included in the
statistical fitting procedure. The other major background contributions can be
adequately constrained in the signal regions. Events in this control region
must pass the signal selection for the
b
-tag category, but the

m_{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ell},\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}})
selection is replaced by

m_{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ell},\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}})>110\,(100)\,\text{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
in the
\tau_{e}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
(
\tau_{\mu}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
) channel.
The tighter selection in the
\tau_{e}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel is used to help suppress the larger
multijet contamination. The region has
\sim
90%
t\bar{t}
purity.

The contribution of multijet events in the signal region (SR) of the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}

Events in CR-1 must pass the same selection as SR, but the sub-leading

\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidate must fail
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
identification. The non-multijet contamination in this region,
N_{\mathrm{non-MJ}}^{\mathrm{CR-1}}
, amounts to
\sim
1.6\%

(
\sim
7.0\%
) in the
b
-veto (
b
-tag) channel, and is subtracted using
simulation. Events in DJ-FR (the dijet fakes-region) are used to measure
fake-factors (
f_{\mathrm{DJ}}
), which are defined as the ratio of the number
of
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}

fake-factors are used to weight the events in CR-1 to estimate
the multijet contribution:

N_{\mathrm{multijet}}^{\mathrm{SR}}=\mbox{$f_{\mathrm{DJ}}$}\times\left(N_{\mathrm{data}}^{\mathrm{CR-1}}-N_{\mathrm{non-MJ}}^{\mathrm{CR-1}}\right)\,.

\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}

selected by single-jet triggers with
p_{\text{T}}
thresholds ranging from

60380\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}
, with all but the highest-threshold trigger being
prescaled. They must contain at least two
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidates, where the
leading candidate has
p_{\text{T}}>$85\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}$
and also exceeds the trigger
threshold by
10\%
, and the sub-leading candidate has
p_{\text{T}}>$65\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}$
. The

\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidates must have opposite charge sign, be back to back in the
transverse plane,
|\Delta\phi(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\tau_{1}},\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\tau_{2}})|>2.7
rad and
the
p_{\text{T}}
of the sub-leading
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
must be at least 30% of the leading

\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}

p_{\text{T}}
. The fake-factors are measured using the sub-leading
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidate to avoid trigger bias and to be consistent with their application in

\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}

p_{\text{T}}
and the sub-leading
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}

-veto categories, defining T-FR and W-FR, respectively.

(non-

(W-FR) using simulation. The fake-rates are measured using the leading-

6.2.1
Multijet events

The multijet contributions in both CR-1 (

)
and SR (

\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}

The non-multijet background is subtracted using simulation and the
events are weighted first by the lepton-isolation fake-factor (
f_{\mathrm{L}}
), yielding

N_{\mathrm{multijet}}^{\mathrm{CR-1}}
, and then by the multijet tau fake-factor (
f_{\mathrm{MJ}}
):

\displaystyle N_{\mathrm{multijet}}^{\mathrm{CR-1}}

\displaystyle=\mbox{$f_{\mathrm{L}}$}\times\left(N_{\mathrm{data}}^{\mathrm{CR-2}}-N_{\mathrm{non-MJ}}^{\mathrm{CR-2}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle N_{\mathrm{multijet}}^{\mathrm{SR}}

\displaystyle=\mbox{$f_{\mathrm{MJ}}$}\times N_{\mathrm{multijet}}^{\mathrm{CR-1}}\,.

The fake-factor

6.2.2
Non-multijet events

The contribution from

(and

The fake-factor
f_{\mathrm{W}}
is measured in the
W\text{+\,jets}
fakes-region (W-FR) defined in Section

6.2.4
Lepton isolation fake-factor

The fake-factor
f_{\mathrm{L}}
is measured in L-FR, which must have exactly one

and

t\bar{t}

the
f_{\mathrm{L}}

of lepton
|\eta|

category (
b
-veto,
b
-tag) and into two regions of muon
p_{\text{T}}
, due to differences
in the isolation criteria of the low- and high-
p_{\text{T}}
triggers in the
\tau_{\mu}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel.

7
Systematic uncertainties

Uncertainties affecting the simulated signal and background
contributions are discussed in Section
7.1
. These include uncertainties associated with the
determination of the integrated luminosity, the detector simulation, the
theoretical cross sections and the modelling from the event generators.
Uncertainties associated with the data-driven background estimates are
discussed in Section
7.2
.

7.1
Uncertainties in simulation estimates

The uncertainty in the combined 2015+2016 integrated luminosity is 3.2%, which
affects all simulated samples. It is derived, following a methodology similar
to that detailed in Ref.
[
134
]
, from a preliminary calibration of
the luminosity scale using
x
–
y
beam-separation scans performed in August
2015 and May 2016. The uncertainty related to the overlay of pile-up events is
estimated by varying the average number of interactions per bunch crossing by
9%. The uncertainties related to the detector simulation manifest themselves through the
efficiency of the reconstruction, identification and triggering algorithms, and
the energy scale and resolution for electrons, muons,
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
, (
b
-)jets
and the
\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}
soft term. These uncertainties are considered for all simulated
samples; their impact is taken into account when estimating signal and
background contributions and when subtracting contamination from regions
in the data-driven estimates. The effects of the particle energy-scale uncertainties
are propagated to
\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}
. The uncertainty in the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
identification
efficiency as determined from measurements of
Z\rightarrow\tau\tau
events is 5–6%.
At high
p_{\text{T}}
, there are no abundant sources of
real hadronic tau decays from which an efficiency measurement could be made.
Rather, the tau identification is studied in high-
p_{\text{T}}
dijet events as a
function of the jet
p_{\text{T}}
, which indicates that there is no degradation in the
modelling of the detector response as a function of the
p_{\text{T}}
of tau
candidates. Based on the limited precision of these studies, an additional
uncertainty of 20%/
TeV
(25%/
TeV
) for one-track (three-track)
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
candidates with

p_{\text{T}}>$150\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}$
is assigned. The
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
trigger efficiency uncertainty
is 3–14%.
The uncertainty in the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
energy scale is 2–3%. The probability for
electrons to be misidentified as
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}
is measured with a precision of
3–14%
[
131
]
. The electron, muon, jet and
\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}
systematic uncertainties described above are found to have a very small impact.

Theoretical cross-section uncertainties are taken into account for all
backgrounds estimated using simulation. For

[

and include variations of the PDF

7.2
Uncertainties in data-driven estimates

Uncertainties in the multijet estimate for the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel
(Section
6.1.1
) arise from the fake-factors
f_{\mathrm{DJ}}
.
These include a 10–50% uncertainty from the limited size of the DJ-FR and an
uncertainty of up to 50% from the subtraction of the non-multijet contamination. An additional uncertainty is considered when applying the
fake-factors in the
b
-tag category, which accounts for changes in the jet
composition with respect to the inclusive selection of the DJ-FR. As the
differences are extracted from comparisons in control regions, they are
one-sided.

The uncertainty in the fake-rates used to weight simulated non-multijet events in the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel (Section
6.1.2
)
is dominated by the limited size of the fakes regions and can reach 40%.

Uncertainties in the multijet estimate for the
\tau_{\mathrm{lep}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel
(Section
6.2.1
) arise from the fake-factors
f_{\mathrm{MJ}}
and
f_{\mathrm{L}}
. The applicability of
f_{\mathrm{MJ}}
measured in MJ-FR to CR-1 is
investigated by studying
f_{\mathrm{MJ}}
as a function of the lepton isolation and the
observed differences are assigned as a systematic uncertainty. The statistical
uncertainty from the limited size of MJ-FR is significant, particularly for the
smaller 2015 dataset. The impact of a potential mismodelling in the subtraction
of simulated non-multijet events containing non-isolated leptons is
investigated by varying the subtraction by 50%, but is found to be small
compared to the other sources of systematic uncertainty. A constant
uncertainty of 20% in
f_{\mathrm{MJ}}
is used to envelop these variations. A 50%
uncertainty is assigned to the sequential

|\Delta\phi(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}},\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}})|
correction.

The applicability of
f_{\mathrm{L}}
measured in L-FR to events in MJ-FR is investigated
by altering the
m_{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ell},\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}})
selection and the observed
differences are assigned as a systematic uncertainty. A 20% uncertainty in
the background subtraction in L-FR is considered, motivated by observations of
the tau identification performance in
W\text{+\,jets}
events. The statistical
uncertainty from the limited size of L-FR is also considered, but is
relatively small. The total uncertainty in
f_{\mathrm{L}}
is 5–50%.

Uncertainties in the data-driven
W\text{+\,jets}
and
t\bar{t}
estimates for the
\tau_{\mathrm{lep}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel (Section
6.2.2
) arise from the fake-factors
f_{\mathrm{W}}
and the subtraction of contributions from CR-1. The applicability of
f_{\mathrm{W}}
measured in W-FR to CR-1 is investigated by studying
f_{\mathrm{W}}
as a function
of
m_{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ell},\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}})
and the observed differences (up to
\sim
10%)
are assigned as a systematic uncertainty. A 30% uncertainty is assigned to the
sequential
|\Delta\phi(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}},\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}})|
correction, based on
variations observed as a function of
\tau_{\mathrm{had}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{vis}}

p_{\text{T}}
. Due to the large
contamination for
b
-tag events in W-FR, a 50% uncertainty is assigned to
the correction factor applied to the
b
-veto parameterisation. The subtraction
of the simulated samples in CR-1 is affected by experimental uncertainties
and uncertainties in production cross sections, which amount to 10%. The total
uncertainty in the multijet estimate in CR-1 is also propagated to the
subtraction.

8.1
Fit model

The parameter of interest is the signal strength,
\mu
. It is defined as the
ratio of the observed to the predicted value of the cross section times
branching fraction, where the prediction is evaluated at a particular
point in the parameter space of the theoretical model in question (MSSM or
Z^{\prime}
benchmark scenarios). Hence, the value
\mu=0
corresponds to the absence of a
signal, whereas the value
\mu=1
indicates the presence of a signal as
predicted by the model. To estimate
\mu
, a likelihood function constructed
as the product of Poisson probability terms is used. A term is included for
each bin in the
m_{\mathrm{T}}^{\text{tot}}
distributions from the
\tau_{e}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
,
\tau_{\mu}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
and
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channels. When fitting MSSM models to the data, the distributions are separated
into
b
-tag and
b
-veto events to enhance sensitivity to the gluon–gluon fusion and

b
-associated production modes, while the inclusive distributions are used for

Z^{\prime}
models. In all cases, the distributions in the CR-T regions of
the
\tau_{e}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
and
\tau_{\mu}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channels are added, which help constrain uncertainties in
the
t\bar{t}
background. Signal and background predictions depend on systematic
uncertainties, which are parameterised as nuisance parameters that are
constrained using Gaussian probability density functions. The asymptotic
approximation is used with the test statistic

\tilde{q}_{\mu}

[
141
]
to compare the likelihoods
of the null hypothesis (SM only) and the assumed signal hypothesis (SM
plus signal) given the data. The bin widths are chosen to ensure a sufficient number of
background events in each bin. The results from the
\tau_{e}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
,
\tau_{\mu}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
and
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channels are combined to improve the sensitivity to signal. For ditau resonance
masses below about
0.6\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}
, the sensitivity is dominated by the
\tau_{\mathrm{lep}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channels,
while the
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channel is most sensitive in the higher mass range.

8.2
Cross-section limits

The data are found to be in good agreement with the predicted background
yields, and the results are given in terms of exclusion limits. These are set
using the modified frequentist CL
s
method
[
142
]
. Upper
limits on the cross section times branching fraction for
\phi
and
Z^{\prime}
bosons are set at the 95% confidence level (CL) as a function of the boson
mass. They are obtained by multiplying the extracted limits on
\mu
by the
respective predicted cross sections. The
\phi
boson limits assume the natural
width of the boson to be negligible compared to the experimental resolution (as
expected over the probed MSSM parameter space). They cover the mass range
0.2–
2.25\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}
and are shown separately for gluon–gluon fusion and

b
-quark associated production. The limits on
Z^{\prime}
bosons are calculated
assuming an SSM
Z^{\prime}
and extend up to
4\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}
. The limits are shown
in Figures
7(a)
–
7(c)
. They are in the
range 0.78–
0.0058\text{\,}\mathrm{pb}
(0.70–
0.0037\text{\,}\mathrm{pb}
) for
gluon–gluon fusion (
b
-associated) production of scalar bosons with masses of
0.2–
2.25\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}
and 1.56–
0.0072\text{\,}\mathrm{pb}
for Drell–Yan
production of
Z^{\prime}
bosons with masses of 0.2–
4\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}
. A small
downward fluctuation at a mass of
\sim
0.3\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}
is observed in all
limits, while a small upward fluctuation for gluon–gluon fusion and
Z^{\prime}
bosons is seen around
0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}
and a broad deficit is seen for the

b
-quark associated production over the entire mass range. These features
arise primarily because of a deficit of events in the range 200–
250\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}

followed by a mild excess in the range 300–
400\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}
in
Figure
5(c)
, and by a consistent deficit of events across the whole
range in Figure
5(d)
. Modifications of the
Z^{\prime}
chiral coupling
structure can result in changes of up to 40% in the
Z^{\prime}
cross-section
limits. Reducing the
Z^{\prime}

\sim
30%,
while increasing the width to 36% can degrade the limits by up to
\sim

9
Conclusion

A search for neutral Higgs bosons as predicted in the Minimal Supersymmetric
Standard Model and
Z^{\prime}
bosons decaying to a pair of
\tau
-leptons is
performed using a data sample from proton–proton collisions at
\sqrt{s}=13

TeV
recorded by the ATLAS detector at the LHC, corresponding to an
integrated luminosity of
36.1\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1}
. The
\tau_{e}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
,
\tau_{\mu}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
and
\tau_{\mathrm{had}}\tau_{\mathrm{had}}
channels are
analysed and no indication of an excess over the expected SM background is
found. Upper limits on the cross section for the production of scalar and

Z^{\prime}
bosons times the branching fraction to ditau final states are set at
95% CL, significantly increasing the sensitivity and the explored mass range
compared to previous searches. They are in the range
0.78–
0.0058\text{\,}\mathrm{pb}
(0.70–
0.0037\text{\,}\mathrm{pb}
) for
gluon–gluon fusion (
b
-associated) production of scalar bosons with masses of
0.2–
2.25\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}
and 1.56–
0.0072\text{\,}\mathrm{pb}
for Drell–Yan
production of
Z^{\prime}
bosons with masses of 0.2–
4\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}
. In the
context of the hMSSM scenario, the most stringent limits for the combined
search exclude
\tan\beta>1.0
for
m_{A}=$0.25\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}$
and
\tan\beta>42
for
m_{A}=$1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}$
at 95% CL. In the context of the
Sequential Standard Model,
Z^{\prime}_{\mathrm{SSM}}
bosons with masses less than

2.42\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}
are excluded at 95% CL, while
m_{Z^{\prime}_{\mathrm{NU}}}<2.25
–
2.60\text{\,}\mathrm{TeV}
is excluded in the range
0.03<\sin^{2}\phi<0.5
in the non-universal
G(221)
model.

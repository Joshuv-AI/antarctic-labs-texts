On the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions to elliptic equations with a singular drift
Misha Chernobai, Tim Shilkin

Abstract
In this paper we study the Dirichlet problem for a scalar elliptic equation in a bounded Lipschitz domain Ω ⊂ ℝ 3 \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3} with a singular drift of the form b 0 = b − α ​ x ′ | x ′ | 2 b_{0}=b-\alpha\frac{x^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}|^{2}} where x ′ = ( x 1 , x 2 , 0 ) x^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2},0) , α ∈ ℝ \alpha\in\mathbb{R} is a parameter and b b is a divergence free vector field having essentially the same regularity as the potential part of the drift. Such drifts naturally arise in the theory of axially symmetric solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations. For α < 0 \alpha<0 the divergence of such drifts is positive which potentially can ruin the uniqueness of solutions. Nevertheless, for α < 0 \alpha<0 we prove existence and Hölder continuity of a unique weak solution which vanishes on the axis Γ := { x ∈ ℝ 3 : | x ′ | = 0 } \Gamma:=\{~x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:~|x^{\prime}|=0~\} .



1
Introduction and Main Results

Assume
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded domain with the Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. Without loss of generality we can assume
\Omega
contains the origin. We consider the
following boundary value problem:

\left\{\quad\begin{gathered}-\Delta u+b_{0}\cdot\nabla u\ =\ -\mathop{\rm div}f\qquad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\\
u|_{\partial\Omega}\ =\ 0.\end{gathered}\right.

(1.1)

Here
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is unknown,
b_{0}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
and

f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
are given functions.

In this paper we study the problem (
1.1
) for the drift
b_{0}
of a special form. Our motivating example is

b_{0}(x)=-\alpha\,\frac{x^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}|^{2}},\qquad x^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2},0),\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

(1.2)

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is a parameter. Clearly, in this case the drift does not belong to
L_{2}(\Omega)
, so instead we assume that
b_{0}
belongs to some critical
weak Morrey space

b_{0}\in L^{2,1}_{w}(\Omega),

(1.3)

where

L^{p,\lambda}_{w}(\Omega)
is the weak Morrey space equipped
with the quasinorm

\|b_{0}\|_{L^{p,\lambda}_{w}(\Omega)}\ :=\ \sup\limits_{x_{0}\in\Omega}\sup\limits_{r<1}\,r^{-\frac{\lambda}{p}}\,\|b_{0}\|_{L_{p,w}(B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)}

and
L_{p,w}(\Omega)
is the weak Lebesgue space equipped with the quasinorm

\|b_{0}\|_{L_{p,w}(\Omega)}\ :=\ \sup\limits_{s>0}s~|\{~x\in\Omega:~|b_{0}(x)|>s~\}|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(1.4)

We define the bilinear form
\mathcal{B}[u,\eta]
by

\mathcal{B}[u,\eta]\ :=\ \int\limits_{\Omega}\eta\,b_{0}\cdot\nabla u~dx.

(1.5)

Note that for

b_{0}
satisfying (
1.3
) the bilinear form (
1.5
)
generally speaking is not well-defined for
u\in W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)
where we denote by
W^{k}_{p}(\Omega)
the standard Sobolev space, see notation at the end of this section. Nevertheless,
\mathcal{B}[u,\eta]
is
well-defined at least for
u\in W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
with
p>2
and

\eta\in L_{\frac{2p}{p-2}}(\Omega)
. So,
instead of the standard notion of weak solutions from the energy
class
W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)
we introduce the definition of
p
-weak
solutions to the problem (
1.1
), see also the related definitions in
[
15
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
23
]
:

Definition 1.1
.

Assume
p>2
,
b_{0}\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
,
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
, and
f\in L_{1}(\Omega)
.
We say
u
is a
p
-weak solution to the problem
(
1.1
) if
u\in\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{p}}(\Omega)

and
u
satisfies the identity

\begin{gathered}\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx\ +\ \mathcal{B}[u,\eta]\ =\ \int\limits_{\Omega}f\cdot\nabla\eta~dx,\qquad\forall~\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).\end{gathered}

(1.6)

If
b\in L_{2}(\Omega)
and
u\in\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{2}}(\Omega)
satisfy (
1.6
) then we call
u
a weak solution to the problem (
1.1
). Obviously, in this case
p
-weak solutions are some subclass of weak solutions.

Note that if
u
is a weak or a
p
-weak solution to (
1.1
) and
f\in L_{2}(\Omega)
then by density arguments we can extend the class of test functions in (
1.6
) from
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
to all functions
\eta\in\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{2}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\infty}(\Omega)
.
We can consider two special cases of the drift
b_{0}
:

\mathop{\rm div}b_{0}\,\leq\,0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega),

(1.7)

or

\mathop{\rm div}b_{0}\,\geq\,0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega),

(1.8)

where we denote by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
the space of distibutions on
\Omega
.
The principal difference between the cases (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) is due to the fact that under the assumption (
1.7
) the quadratic form
\mathcal{B}[u,u]
provides (at least, formally) a positive support to the quadratic form of the elliptic operator in (
1.1
), while in the case of (
1.8
) the quadratic form
\mathcal{B}[u,u]
is non-positive and hence it “shifts” the operator to the “spectral area”. For example, it is well-known that in the case (
1.8
) the uniqueness for the problem (
1.1
)
can be violated even for smooth solutions.
Indeed, the function
u(x)=c(1-|x|^{2})
is a solution to the problem (
1.1
) in the unite ball
\Omega=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,|x|<1\,\}
corresponding to
b_{0}(x)=n\,\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}
with
b_{0}\in L_{n,w}(\Omega)
satisfying (
1.8
), see also the discussion in
[
6
]
,
[
12
]
.

The case (
1.7
) under the assumption (
1.3
) was studied in
[
7
]
(see also
[
6
]
for the 2D case). In this paper we focus on the case (
1.8
) which is much more subtle and for now we are not able to treat it in full generality. So, we restrict ourselves to the drifts with the potential part of some specific form. Namely, we assume that

b_{0}(x)\ =\ b(x)\,+\,\alpha\,\frac{x^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}|^{2}},

(1.9)

where
b:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfies the divergence-free condition (in the sense of distributions)

\mathop{\rm div}b=0\qquad\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega).

(1.10)

Note that from (
1.3
) we obtain

b\in L^{2,1}_{w}(\Omega).

(1.11)

The relation (
1.9
) can be viewed as the Helmogoltz decomposition of the vector field
b_{0}

b_{0}\ =\ b+\alpha\,\nabla h\quad\mbox{a.e. in}\quad\Omega,

(1.12)

where the potential part of the drift
h:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is specified by

h(x)\,=\,\ln\frac{1}{|x^{\prime}|}.

(1.13)

In this case we have

-\Delta h\ =\ 2\pi\,\delta_{\Gamma}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega),\qquad\Gamma\,=\,\{\,x\in\bar{\Omega}:\,x^{\prime}=0\,\},

(1.14)

where
\delta_{\Gamma}
is the delta-function concentrated on
\Gamma
, i.e.

\langle\delta_{\Gamma},\varphi\rangle\ :=\ \int\limits_{\Gamma}\varphi(x)\,dl_{x},\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Certainly, in the case of (
1.9
) the identity (
1.6
) reduces to

\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot(\nabla\eta+b\eta)\,dx\,+\,2\pi\,\alpha\,\int\limits_{\Gamma}u(x)\eta(x)\,dl_{x}\,=\,\int\limits_{\Omega}f\cdot\nabla\eta~dx,\quad\forall\,\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

(1.15)

Note that for
u\in W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
with
p>2
the trace
u|_{\Gamma}
of
u
on
\Gamma
satisfies

u|_{\Gamma}\in W^{1-\frac{2}{p}}_{p}(\Gamma),

(1.16)

where
W^{s}_{p}(\Gamma)
,
s>0
, is the Slobodetskii-Sobolev space, see, for example,
[
2
]
. So, for
p
-weak solutions in the sense of Definition
1.1

the second term in the left-hand side of (
1.15
) is well-defined.
Note also that the condition (
1.8
) corresponds to
\alpha\leq 0
in (
1.9
) and (
1.15
).

The drift of type (
1.9
) plays an important role in the theory of axially symmetric solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations, see, for example,

[
19
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
32
]
,
[
33
]
,

[
35
]
,
[
38
]
.
In the axially symmetric case the Navier-Stokes system can reduced to the scalar equation

\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\Big(v-\alpha\,\frac{x^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}|^{2}}\Big)\cdot\nabla u\ =\ 0\qquad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T),

(1.17)

where
v=v(x,t)
is the divergence-free
velocity field and
u=u(x,t)
is some
auxiliary scalar function. For example, for axially symmetric
solutions without swirl (i.e. if
v(x,t)=v_{r}(r,z,t){e}_{r}+v_{z}(r,z,t){e}_{z}
where
{e}_{r}
,
{e}_{\varphi}
,
{e}_{z}
is the
standard cylindrical basis) the equation (
1.17
) is satisfied
for
\alpha=2
and
u=\frac{\omega_{\varphi}}{r}
, where
\omega_{\varphi}:=v_{r,z}-v_{z,r}
and
r=|x^{\prime}|
. In the case of general axially symmetric
solutions
v(x,t)=v_{r}(r,z,t){e}_{r}+v_{\varphi}(r,z,t){e}_{\varphi}+v_{z}(r,z,t){e}_{z}
the equation (
1.17
) holds for
\alpha=-2
and

u=rv_{\varphi}
.

It is well-known in the Navier-Stokes theory (see

[
19
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
33
]
,

[
35
]
,
[
38
]
) that while in the case
\alpha>0
some
results like Liouville-type theorems assume no special conditions on
the solutions
u
to the equation (
1.17
) besides a proper decay
of the drift
v
, the analogues results in the case
\alpha<0
require
the additional condition
u|_{\Gamma}=0
. Our equation (
1.1
) under the assumption (
1.9
) can be considered as
the elliptic model for the general equation (
1.17
). The main goals of the present paper is to investigate the equation (
1.17
) from the point of view of the “general theory” (i.e. without the assumption on the axial symmetry of
u
and other specific properties of solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations). In particular, we would like to clarify the role which the condition
u|_{\Gamma}=0
plays in the theory. On the other hand, our present contribution can be viewed as a 3D extension of the results obtained earlier in
[
6
]
in the 2D case.

The main result the present paper are the following two theorems:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain containing the origin,
b_{0}
is given by (
1.9
) with
\alpha<0
,
b
satisfies (
1.10
), (
1.11
) and

f\in L_{q}(\Omega)
with
q>3
. Then every
p
–weak solution to the problem (
1.1
) satisfying the condition

u|_{\Gamma}\ =\ 0

(1.18)

is Hölder continuous. Namely,
there exists
\mu\in(0,1)
depending only on
q
,
\alpha
,
\|b\|_{L^{2,1}_{w}(\Omega)}
and the Lipschitz constant of
\partial\Omega

such that
if for some
p>2
a function
u
is a
p
–weak solution to the problem (
1.1
) corresponding to the right-hand side

and satisfying the condition
(
1.18
)
in the sense of traces

is Hölder continuous on
\bar{\Omega}
with the exponent
\mu

\|u\|_{C^{\mu}(\bar{\Omega})}\ \leq\ c~\|f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)},

(1.19)

holds with the

depending only on
\Omega
,
\alpha
,
q
,
\|b\|_{L^{2,1}_{w}(\Omega)}
and the Lipschitz constant of
\partial\Omega
.

We emphasize that the Hölder exponent

1.1

only to have the trace in (

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain containing the origin,

is given by (
1.9
) with
\alpha<0
,

satisfies (
1.10
), (
1.11
) and assume

depending only on
\alpha
,

,

and the Lipschitz constant of

to

1.1
) satisfying the condition (
1.18
)
Moreover, this solution satisfies the estimate

\|u\|_{W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ c~\|f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)},

(1.20)

with a
constant
c>0
depending only on
\Omega
,
\alpha
,
q
,
\|b\|_{L^{2,1}_{w}(\Omega)}
and the Lipschitz constant of
\partial\Omega
.

Note that the condition (
1.18

So, our main message is: if the drift

and
[

). From our point of view the most interesting result in Theorem

is the existence of solutions satisfying the condition (
1.18

Auxiliary results

In this section we present several auxiliary results. Within this section we assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain for arbitrary
n\geq 2
. The first result shows that by relaxing an exponent of the integrability of a function we can always switch from a weak Morrey norm to a regular one.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
p\in(1,n)
and
1\leq q<p\leq r\leq n
there are positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
depending only on
n
,
p
,
q
and
\Omega
such that

c_{1}\,\|b\|_{L^{q,n-q}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,\|b\|_{L^{p,n-p}_{w}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,c_{2}\,\|b\|_{L^{r,n-r}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

The result follows from the Hölder inequality for Lorentz norms, see

[
14
, Section 4.1]
.
∎

The next result is the estimate of the quadratic form corresponding to the drift term satisfying (
1.9
) and (
1.10
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume
p>2
,
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
and

is given by (
1.9
) with

satisfying (

).
Then for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and any

the
bilinear form

\mathcal{B}[u,\eta]
defined in (
1.5
) satisfies the identity

\mathcal{B}[u,u]\ =\ \pi\alpha\,\int\limits_{\Gamma}|u(x)|^{2}~dl_{x}

(2.1)

where the integral in the right-hand side is understood in the sense of traces.

Proof.

For a smooth function

the relation (

) follows by integration by parts. For an arbitrary function

to

.
∎

is the well-known extension of the result of C. Fefferman

Proposition 2.3

Assume

\int\limits_{\Omega}|V|\,|u|^{p}\,dx\ \leq\ c_{n,r,p}\,\|V\|_{L^{r,n-rp}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}^{p},\qquad\forall\,u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

with the constant

,

Our next result is the estimate of the bilinear form corresponding to the drift term. This result is a direct consequence of Proposition

.

,

If we additionally assume

) and (

3
Boundedness of weak solutions

In this section we establish global boundedness of
p
–weak solutions to the problem (

). Note that this result holds for a supercritical drift

, so we do not need the critical condition (

) in this section. Note also, that our result is global, i.e. it is heavily based on the homogeneous (or, more generally, regular) Dirichlet boundary conditions in (

) and this result is not valid in the local setting, see, for example, counterexamples and discussion in
[

]
. For the related results in local setting we see the recent paper
[

]
and reference there.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume
p>2
,
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
and

is given by (

) with

satisfying (

). Assume

and

.
Then for any

any
p
-weak
solution

to
the problem (
1.1
) satisfying (
1.18
) is essentially bounded and satisfies the estimate

\|u\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ c~\|f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)},

(3.1)

with a
constant
c>0
depending only on
\Omega
and
q
.

Proof.

For
m>0
we define a truncation
T_{m}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by
T_{m}(s):=m
for
s\geq m
,
T_{m}(s)=s
for
s<m
.
For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
we also denote
(s)_{+}:=\max\{s,0\}
.
Now we fix some
m>0
and
and denote
\bar{u}:=T_{m}(u)
.
Then for any
k\geq 0
we have

(\bar{u}-k)_{+}\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{p}}(\Omega),\qquad\nabla(\bar{u}-k)_{+}=\chi_{\Omega[k<u<m]}\nabla u

where
\Omega[k<u<m]=\{\,x\in\Omega:\,k<u(x)<m\,\}
. Define
\eta:=(\bar{u}-k)_{+}
and note that from (
1.18
) for any
m\geq 0
and any
k\geq 0
we obtain

(u-m)_{+}|_{\Gamma}=0,\qquad\eta|_{\Gamma}\,=\,0

(3.2)

in the sense of traces.
Approximating
\eta
by smooth functions we can take
\eta
as a test function in (
1.6
). For
k\geq m
we have
\eta\equiv 0
and hence
\mathcal{B}[u,\eta]=0
. For

we obtain

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{B}[u,\eta]\ =\ \mathcal{B}[\eta,\eta]\ +\ (m-k)\,\int\limits_{\Omega}b_{0}\cdot\nabla(u-m)_{+}\,dx.\end{gathered}

From Proposition
2.2
taking into account (

) we obtain

. On the other hand, from (

) and (

) taking into account (

and hence

.
So, for
A_{k}:=\{\,x\in\Omega:\,\bar{u}(x)>k\,\}
from (

which implies (see

.

Applying the same procedure to

∎

4
De Giorgi classes

In this section we introduce the modified De Giorgi classes which are convenient for the study of solutions to the elliptic equations with coefficients from Morrey spaces. These classes were used before in
[
7
]
. In this section we use the following notation: for
u\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)
and
B_{\rho}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
we denote

m(x_{0},\rho):=\inf\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u,\qquad M(x_{0},\rho):=\sup\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u,\qquad\omega(x_{0},\rho):=M(x_{0},\rho)-m(x_{0},\rho).

Definition 4.1
.

Assume
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain,
n\geq 2
.
For
u\in W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)
we define
A_{k}:=\{\,x\in\Omega:\,u(x)>k\,\}
. We say
u\in DG(\Omega,k_{0})
if there exist constants
\gamma>0
,
F>0
,
\beta\geq 0
,
q>n
such that for any
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
, any
0<\rho<R
and any
k\geq k_{0}
the following inequality holds

\begin{gathered}\int\limits_{A_{k}\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\ \leq\ \frac{\gamma^{2}}{(R-\rho)^{2}}\left(1+\frac{R^{\beta}}{(R-\rho)^{\beta}}\right)\,\int\limits_{A_{k}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})}|u-k|^{2}\,dx\ +\\
+\ F^{2}\,|A_{k}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}.\end{gathered}

(4.1)

To avoid overloaded notation, when we need to specify constants in Definition
4.1
we allow some terminological license and say that the class
D(\Omega,k_{0})
corresponds to the constants
\gamma
,
F
,
\beta
,
q
instead of including these constants in the notation of the functional class.

The main result of this section is the following weak version of the maximum principle:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
n\geq 2
.
Denote by
DG(\Omega,k_{0})
the De Giorgi class with parameters
\gamma
,
\beta
,
q
,

and assume

. Then
u
is locally essentially bounded from above in
\Omega
and for any
\delta\in(0,1)
there exists
\theta\in(0,1)
depending only on
\delta
,
\gamma
,
\beta
,

such that for any
B_{4R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
if

\big|\{\,x\in B_{2R}(x_{0}):~u(x)\leq k_{0}\,\}\big|\ \geq\ \delta\,|B_{2R}|

(4.2)

then

\sup\limits_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u\,\leq\,(1-\theta)\,\sup\limits_{B_{4R}(x_{0})}u\,+\,\theta\,k_{0}\,+c_{1}\,F\,R^{1-\frac{n}{q}},

(4.3)

where

depends only on
n
,
\gamma
,
\beta
,

.

Proposition

is a simple combination of statements of Lemmas

—

[

]
, see also
[

]

Lemma 4.1

\sup\limits_{B_{R}(x_{0})}~(u-k_{0})_{+}\ \leq\ c_{*}\,\left[\Big(\ \ \ -\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\limits_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}|(u-k_{0})_{+}|^{2}~dx\Big)^{1/2}+F\,R^{1-\frac{n}{q}}\right]

,

and

.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume

. Then there exists

,

of the De Giorgi class such that for any

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume

Here

is the constant from Lemma

and we denote

provides the control of the oscillation of a function belonging to the modified De Giorgi class if the assumption (

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume
B_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\,|x|<R\}
.
Assume

,
b\in L_{p^{\prime}}(B_{2})
,

satisfies
\mathop{\rm div}b=0

, and

. Assume

\alpha\not=0
and

0\leq v\leq 2\quad\mbox{in}\quad B_{2},\qquad v|_{\Gamma}\geq 1,\qquad\Gamma:=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:~x_{1}=x_{2}=0~\}.

Then there exists constants

and

in the explicit way specified below such that if

\big|\{\,x\in B_{2}:~v(x)\geq\lambda_{1}\,\}\big|\ \geq\ \delta_{1}.

Proof.

Assume there exist
g\in L_{p}(B_{2})
and
v\in W^{1}_{p}(B_{2})

satisfying (
4.9
), (
4.10
),
(
4.11

|\{\,x\in B_{2}:\,v(x)>\lambda_{1}\,\}|\ \leq\ \delta_{1}.

2\pi\alpha\int\limits_{\Gamma\cap B_{2}}v(x)\eta(x)\,dl_{x}\ =\ \int\limits_{B_{2}}v\Big(\Delta\eta+b\cdot\nabla\eta\Big)~dx\ +\ \int\limits_{B_{2}}g\cdot\nabla\eta~dx.

\int\limits_{B_{2}[v>\lambda_{1}]}v\Big(\Delta\eta+b^{(\alpha)}\cdot\nabla\eta\Big)~dx\ \leq\ \|v\|_{L_{\infty}(B_{2})}\,\|\eta\|_{C^{2}(\bar{B}_{2})}\,\Big(1+\|b\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}(B_{2})}\Big)|B_{2}[v>\lambda_{1}]|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

so that

u^{v}\in C^{\mu}(\bar{\Omega})
and the estimate

\|u^{v}\|_{W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)}\,+\,\|u^{v}\|_{C^{\mu}(\bar{\Omega})}\,\leq\,c\,\Big(\|f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}\,+\,\|b\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}\Big)

(6.6)

holds. Define the operator

1.1

6.1

,

which implies the operator

is continuous. Moreover, from (
6.6
)

it is easy to see that the operator

Assume

and

satisfies

Denote

. Then

is a unique

\left\{\quad\begin{gathered}-\Delta u+\Big(\lambda b-\alpha\,\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\Big)\cdot\nabla u\ =\ -\mathop{\rm div}f\qquad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\\

1.
Introduction

This article investigates the existence, uniqueness,
L_{p}
-regularity, and maximal Hölder regularity of a solution to stochastic time fractional Burgers’ equations (STFBEs) driven by space-time white noise. We consider

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=Lu+\bar{b}^{i}uu_{x^{i}}+\partial_{t}^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u)dW_{t},\quad(\omega,t,x)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\quad u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},

(1.1)

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\beta<\frac{3}{4}\alpha+\frac{1}{2}
, and
d<4-\frac{2(2\beta-1)_{+}}{\alpha}
. The operators
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
and
\partial_{t}^{\beta}
are the Caputo fractional derivatives of order
\alpha
and
\beta
, and the operator
L
is the second order
random
differential operator defined as follows:

\begin{gathered}(Lu)(\omega,t,x)=a^{ij}(\omega,t,x)u_{x^{i}x^{j}}+b^{i}(\omega,t,x)u_{x^{i}}+c(\omega,t,x)u.\end{gathered}

The random coefficients
a^{ij}
,
b^{i}
, and
c
are predictable, differentiable (or continuous), and bounded functions. The diffusion coefficient
\sigma(u)=\sigma(\omega,t,x,u)
is a predictable and measurable function satisfying growth conditions and Lipschitz continuity in
u
. The detailed conditions on
a^{ij}
,
b^{i}
,
c
, and
\sigma
are described in Assumptions
3.1
and
3.3
. The random measure

is induced from an
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-valued cylindrical Wiener process
W_{t}
.

When
\alpha=\beta=1
in equation (
1.1
), the equation is said to be a stochastic Burgers’ equation (SBE) of form

\partial_{t}u=Lu+\bar{b}uu_{x}+\sigma(u)\dot{W},\quad(\omega,t,x)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R};\quad u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},

(1.2)

where
\dot{W}
is the space-time white noise.
Numerous studies have been conducted on the equation (
1.2
), but we only refer to the reader to
[
13
,
14
,
29
]
. In
[
13
]
, the author proved the uniqueness, existence, and continuity of a solution to a semilinear equation, including an equation of type (
1.2
) on the unit interval
(0,1)
. Additionally, the same properties of a solution on
\mathbb{R}
were obtained in
[
14
]
when the

bounded conditions on

were imposed. In
[
29
]
, the authors investigated the Hölder regularity and moment estimates of the random field solution to (
1.2

and
\bar{b}=-1

In contrast, (deterministic) partial differential equations with Caputo fractional derivatives have been used in many fields, such as electrochemical processes
[
5
,
19
]
, dielectric polarization
[
33
]
, viscoelastic materials
[
32
]
, biology
[
31
]

[

18
]
. Especially, equation (

\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\sigma(u)=0
is called a time fractional Burgers’ equation (TFBE), which describes the propagation of waves through viscous media (
[

2
]
). Indeed, various researches have been conducted on numerical analysis for the TFBE (see
[
3
,
9
,
10
,
20

30

). From a mathematical standpoint, it is reasonable to wonder whether it is possible to demonstrate the uniqueness and existence of a solution to STFBE (
1.1
), and also to obtain the Hölder regularity of the solution. To the best of our knowledge,
[
36
]
is the only study that answers this question. The authors of
[
36
]
demonstrate the existence, uniqueness, and regularity of the

solution to SBEs with fractional derivatives in time and space on a bounded domain

In this paper, we provide the

uniqueness, existence, and regularity of a

solution to equation (
1.1
) with
random
second order differential operator

on the
whole spatial domain

\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Additionally, we achieve the Hölder regularity of the solution in time and space. In detail, if

denotes the solution to equation (

and small constant

\begin{gathered}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(\omega,\cdot,x)|_{C^{\left[\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\left(2-(2\beta-1)_{+}/\alpha-d/2\right)\wedge 1\right)+\frac{(2\beta-1)_{-}}{2}\right]\wedge 1-\varepsilon}([0,\tau])}<\infty,\\
\sup_{t\leq\tau}|u(\omega,t,\cdot)|_{C^{\left(2-(2\beta-1)_{+}/\alpha-d/2\right)\wedge 1-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty.\end{gathered}

(1.3)

where

, and

is the Hölder spaces. Observe that the behavior of the Hölder regularity of the solution in time changes from

Several remarks about the proof are made. The proof strategy for the main theorem (Theorem
3.5
) is based on
[
16
]
. However, some differences exist because since it is not certain that Itô’s formula and the maximum principle hold for STFBE (
1.1

Thus, the proof proceeds as follows. As in
[
16
]
, we focus on proving the uniqueness and existence of the
L_{p}
solution in each (large)
p>2
, and the main difficulty is to demonstrating the existence of the solutions. Hence, we consider the cut-off form of equation (
1.1
) to obtain local solutions. Afterward, we construct a global solution candidate
u
by pasting the local solutions (Lemma
4.3
and Remark
4.4
). A uniform

bound of the diffusion coefficient

(Lemma
4.5

4.8

4.6

To obtain the maximal Hölder regularity of the solution to equation (
1.1
), we require two components: the Hölder embedding theorem for the solution space
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\gamma}(\tau)
(Theorem
2.16
) and the uniqueness of the solution in

by employing the Hölder embedding theorem for the solution space (Theorem

and Theorem

In this section, we introduce the definitions and properties of space-time white noise, fractional calculus, and stochastic Banach spaces. Throughout this paper,
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)

\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}

First, we present the space-time white noise
\dot{W}
to understand the stochastic part of (
1.1

Definition 2.1

(Space-time white noise)
.

A generalized random field
\dot{W}
is said to be the space-time white noise if it is a centered Gaussian random field such that its covariance is given by

\mathbb{E}\,\dot{W}(h)\,\dot{W}(g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(t,x)g(t,x)dxdt,\quad\forall h,g\in L_{2}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Remark 2.2
.

We employ a series of Itô’s stochastic integral to interpret the stochastic part of equation (
1.1
). More precisely, let
\{\eta^{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
be an orthonormal basis on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. If we define

w_{t}^{k}:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta^{k}(x)\dot{W}(ds,dx)

using the Walsh integral (see
[
35
]
), then
\{w_{t}^{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a set of one dimensional independent Wiener processes. Then, if we set (see
[
26
, Section 8.3]
, and
[
23
, Section 7]
)

W_{t}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\eta^{k}w_{t}^{k},

then
W_{t}
is an
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-valued cylindrical Wiener process and
dW_{t}=\sum_{k}\eta^{k}dw_{t}^{k}
. Thus, equation (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=Lu+\bar{b}^{i}uu_{x^{i}}+\partial_{t}^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u)\eta^{k}dw^{k}_{t},\quad(\omega,t,x)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\quad u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\alpha>0

\varphi\in L_{1}((0,T))
, the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of the order
\alpha

I_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi(t):=(I^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi)(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\varphi(s)ds\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t\in(0,T),

Remark 2.4
.

For any
q\in[1,\infty]

\|I^{\alpha}\varphi\|_{L_{q}((0,T))}\leq N(\alpha,p,T)\|\varphi\|_{L_{q}((0,T))}.

(2.1)

Therefore,

is well-defined and finite for almost all

.
Additionally, Fubini’s theorem implies that, for

I^{\alpha+\beta}\varphi(t)=I^{\alpha}I^{\beta}\varphi(t).

Definition 2.5
.

For
\alpha>0
, let

be a nonnegative integer such that

is a real-valued function on

(i)

D_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(n-\alpha)}\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{n-\alpha-1}\varphi(s)ds.

(ii)

The Caputo fractional derivative
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi
is defined as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\Gamma(n-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{n-\alpha-1}\varphi^{(n)}(s)ds

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\Gamma(n-\alpha)}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{n-\alpha-1}\left[\varphi^{(n-1)}(s)-\varphi^{(n-1)}(0)\right]ds.

Remark 2.6

(i)

For any

3.
Main Results

This section presents the uniqueness, existence,
L_{p}
-regularity, and Hölder regularity of the solution to the following equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u

\displaystyle=Lu+\bar{b}^{i}uu_{x^{i}}+\partial^{\beta}_{t}\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u)\eta^{k}dw_{s}^{k},\quad t>0;\quad u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},

(and their derivatives) are uniformly bounded (see Assumption

) and it is used to obtain a uniform

In Theorem

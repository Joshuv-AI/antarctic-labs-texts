Policy Gradient Method For Robust Reinforcement Learning
Yue Wang, Shaofeng Zou

Abstract
This paper develops the first policy gradient method with global optimality guarantee and complexity analysis for robust reinforcement learning under model mismatch. Robust reinforcement learning is to learn a policy robust to model mismatch between simulator and real environment. We first develop the robust policy (sub-)gradient, which is applicable for any differentiable parametric policy class. We show that the proposed robust policy gradient method converges to the global optimum asymptotically under direct policy parameterization. We further develop a smoothed robust policy gradient method, and show that to achieve an ϵ \epsilon -global optimum, the complexity is 𝒪 ⁡ ( ϵ − 3 ) \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3}) . We then extend our methodology to the general model-free setting, and design the robust actor-critic method with differentiable parametric policy class and value function. We further characterize its asymptotic convergence and sample complexity under the tabular setting. Finally, we provide simulation results to demonstrate the robustness of our methods.



1
Introduction

In practical reinforcement learning (RL)
(
Sutton & Barto 2018
)
applications, the training environment may often times deviate from the test environment, resulting in a model mismatch between the two.
Such model mismatch could be because of, e.g., modeling error between simulator and real-world applications, model deviation due to non-stationarity of the environment, unexpected perturbation and potential adversarial attacks. This may lead to a significant performance degradation in the testing environment.

To solve the issue of model mismatch, a framework of robust Markov decision process (MDP) was introduced in
(
Bagnell et al. 2001
;
Nilim & El Ghaoui 2004
;
Iyengar 2005
)
, where the MDP model is not fixed but comes from some uncertainty set. The goal of robust RL is to find a policy that optimize the worst-case performance over all possible MDP models in the uncertainty set. Value-based approaches have been extensively studied under the tabular setting and with function approximation, e.g.,
(
Iyengar 2005
;
Nilim & El Ghaoui 2004
;
Badrinath & Kalathil 2021
;
Wiesemann et al. 2013
;
Roy et al. 2017
;
Tamar et al. 2014
;
Lim et al. 2013
;
Bagnell et al. 2001
;
Satia & Lave Jr 1973
;
Xu & Mannor 2010
;
Wang & Zou 2021
)
. There are also other approaches that are shown to be successful empirically, e.g., based on adversarial training,
(
Vinitsky et al. 2020
;
Pinto et al. 2017
;
Abdullah et al. 2019
;
Hou et al. 2020
;
Rajeswaran et al. 2017
;
Atkeson & Morimoto 2003
;
Morimoto & Doya 2005
;
Huang et al. 2017
;
Kos & Song 2017
;
Lin et al. 2017
;
Pattanaik et al. 2018
;
Mandlekar et al. 2017
)
, which however lack theoretical robustness and optimality guarantee.

The policy gradient method
(
Williams 1992
;
Sutton et al. 1999
;
Konda & Tsitsiklis 2000
;
Kakade 2001
)
, which models and optimizes the policy directly, has been widely used in RL thanks to its ease of implementation in model-free setting, scalability to large/continuous state and action spaces, and applicability to any differentiable policy parameterization. Despite a large body of empirical and theoretical work on policy gradient method,
development of policy gradient approach for robust RL with provable robustness to model mismatch and optimality guarantee still remains largely open in the literature.

In this paper, we develop the first policy gradient method for robust RL under model mismatch with provable robustness, global optimality and complexity analysis. In this paper, we focus on the
R
-contamination uncertainty set model
(
Huber 1965
;
Du et al. 2018
;
Huber & Ronchetti 2009
;
Wang & Zou 2021
;
Nishimura & Ozaki 2004
;
Prasad et al. 2020a
;
Prasad et al. 2020b
)
. Our robust policy gradient method inherits advantages of vanilla policy gradient methods and their variants, and provide provable guarantee on global optimality and robustness. In particular, the challenges and our major contributions are summarized as follows.

•

Robust RL aims to optimize the worst-case performance, named robust value function, where the worst-case is taken over some uncertainty set of MDPs. However, the robust value function involves a “max” and thus may not be differentiable in the policy. Our first contribution in this paper is the development of robust policy gradient, where we derive the Fréchet sub-gradient of the robust value function, and further show that it is the gradient almost everywhere. We would like to highlight that our robust policy gradient applies to any differentiable and Lipschitz policy class.

•

Motivated by recent advancements on the global optimality of vanilla policy gradient methods, we are interested in a natural question that whether the global optimum of robust RL can be attained by our robust policy gradient method. The major challenge lies in that the robust value function involves a “max” over the uncertainty set, and thus has a much more complicated landscape than the vanilla value function. We consider the direct parametric policy class and show that the robust value function satisfies the Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) condition
(
Polyak 1963
;
Lojasiewicz 1963
)
, and our robust policy gradient method converges to a global optimum almost surely.

•

The robust value function may not be differentiable everywhere, which is the major challenge in the convergence rate analysis. We then design a smoothed robust policy gradient method as an approximation, where the corresponding smoothed objective function is differentiable. We show that smoothed robust policy gradient method converges to an
\epsilon
-global optimum of the original non-differentiable robust RL problem with a complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
.

•

To understand the fundamentals of our robust policy gradient method, our results discussed so far focus on the ideal setting assuming perfect knowledge of the (smoothed) robust policy gradient. Although this is a commonly used setting in recent studies of vanilla policy gradient, e.g.,
(
Agarwal et al. 2021
;
Cen et al. 2021
;
Mei et al. 2020
;
Bhandari & Russo 2019
)
, such knowledge is typically unknown in practice and need to be estimated from samples. We then focus on the model-free setting, where only samples from the centroid of the uncertainty set are available, and we
design a model-free robust actor-critic algorithm. Our robust actor-critic can be applied with arbitrary differential parametric policy class and value function approximation in practice. Theoretically, we prove the global optimality of our robust actor critic method under the tabular setting with direct parametric policy class.

;

)

(

;

;

;

)
have been successful in various applications, e.g.,
(
Schulman et al. 2015
;

)
. Despite the surge of interest in policy gradient methods, theoretical understanding remains limited to convergence to local optimum and stationary points. It was not until recently that the global optimality of various policy gradient methods was established
(
Bhandari & Russo 2021
;
Bhandari & Russo 2019
;
Agarwal et al. 2021
;
Mei et al. 2020
;

;

;

;
Cen et al. 2021
;
Zhang et al. 2020a
;

)
.
In this paper, we focus on policy gradient methods for robust RL. The major new challenge lies in that the robust value function is not differentiable everywhere, and the landscape of the robust value function is much more complicated than the vanilla value function.

Value-based approach for robust RL.
Robust MDP was introduced and studied in
(
Iyengar 2005
;
Nilim & El Ghaoui 2004
;

;

;

;
Lim & Autef 2019
;
Xu & Mannor 2010
;
Yu & Xu 2015
;
Lim et al. 2013
;
Tamar et al. 2014
)
, where the uncertainty set is assumed to be
known
to the learner, and the problem can be solved using dynamic programming.
Later, the studies were generalized to the model-free setting where stochastic samples from the central MDP of the uncertainty set are available in an online fashion
(
Roy et al. 2017
;
Badrinath & Kalathil 2021
;
Wang & Zou 2021
;
Tessler et al. 2019
)
and an offline fashion
(
Zhou et al. 2021
;

;

;

;

;

)
.
In this paper, we focus on approaches that model and optimize the policy directly, and develop robust policy gradient method. Our method inherits advantages of policy gradient, and has a broader applicability than value-based method for large-scale problems.

Direct policy search for robust RL.

Robust policy gradient method for constrained MDP was studied in
(
Russel et al. 2020
)
, however, there are mistakes in the gradient derivation. More specifically, the fact that the worst-case transition kernel is a function of the policy was ignored when deriving the gradient. A recent paper
(
Derman et al. 2021
)
showed the equivalence between robust MDP and regularized MDP, and developed a policy gradient method for the case with only reward uncertainty. In
(
Derman et al. 2021
)
, it was discussed that it is difficult to their methods extend to problems with uncertain transition kernel because of the dependency between the worst-case transition kernel and the policy.
(
Eysenbach & Levine 2021
)
showed a similar result that maximum entropy regularized MDP is robust to model mismatch.
In this paper, we focus on the challenging problem with uncertain transition kernel, and derive the robust policy gradient.
We also note that a separate line of work
(
Zhang et al. 2021c
;
Zhang et al. 2021b
)
studies the corruption-robust RL problems, where the goal is to learn a robust policy to data corruption, and is fundamentally different from the problem in this paper.

Preliminaries

Markov Decision Process.

An MDP
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathsf{P},c,\gamma)
is specified by: a state space
\mathcal{S}
, an action space
\mathcal{A}
, a transition kernel
\mathsf{P}=\left\{p^{a}_{s}\in\Delta(\mathcal{S}),a\in\mathcal{A},s\in\mathcal{S}\right\}
1
1

1

\Delta(\mathcal{S})
denotes the
(|\mathcal{S}|-1)
-dimensional probability simplex on
\mathcal{S}
.
, where
p^{a}_{s}
is the distribution of the next state over
\mathcal{S}
upon the agent taking action
a
in state
s
, a cost function
c:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to[0,1]
, and a discount factor
\gamma\in[0,1)
. At each time step
t
, the agent in state
s_{t}
takes an action
a_{t}
. The environment then transits to the next state
s_{t+1}
according to the distribution
p^{a_{t}}_{s_{t}}
, and provides a cost signal
c(s_{t},a_{t})\in[0,1]
to the agent.

A stationary policy
\pi:\mathcal{S}\to\Delta(\mathcal{A})
maps any state to a distribution over the action space
\mathcal{A}
. More specifically, in state
s
, the agent takes action
a
with probability
\pi(a|s)
.
The value function of a stationary policy
\pi
starting from
s\in\mathcal{S}
measures the expected accumulated discounted cost by following policy
\pi
:

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}c(S_{t},A_{t})|S_{0}=s,\pi\right]
, and the goal is to find a policy that minimizes the value function for any initial state
s\in\mathcal{S}
.

Robust MDP.

The transition kernel of the robust MDP is not fixed but is from some uncertainty set
\mathcal{P}
. In this paper, we focus on the
(s,a)
-rectangular uncertainty set
(
Nilim & El Ghaoui 2004
;
Iyengar 2005
)
, i.e.,
\mathcal{P}=\bigotimes_{s,a}\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}
, where
\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}\subseteq\Delta(\mathcal{S})
.
At each time step, after the agent takes an action, the environment transits to the next state following any transition kernel
\mathsf{P}\in\mathcal{P}
, and the choice of kernels can be time-varying. A sequence of transition kernel
\kappa=(\mathsf{P}_{0},\mathsf{P}_{1}...)\in\bigotimes_{t\geq 0}\mathcal{P}
can be viewed as a policy chosen by the nature and is referred to as the nature’s policy.

The robust value function of a policy
\pi
is defined as the worst-case expected accumulated discounted cost over
\kappa
when following
\pi
and starting from
s
:

\displaystyle V^{\pi}(s)\triangleq\max_{\kappa\in\bigotimes_{t\geq 0}\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}c(S_{t},A_{t})|S_{0}=s,\pi\right].

(1)

The robust action-value function can be defined:

Q^{\pi}(s,a)\triangleq\max_{\kappa\in\bigotimes_{t\geq 0}\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}c(S_{t},A_{t})|S_{0}=s,A_{0}=a,\pi\right].
It has been shown that
V^{\pi}(s)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)Q^{\pi}(s,a)

(
Nilim & El Ghaoui 2004
;
Iyengar 2005
)
.

The robust Bellman operator of a policy
\pi
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{\pi}V(s)\triangleq\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)\left(c(s,a)+\gamma\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V)\right),

(2)

where
\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V)\triangleq\max_{p\in\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}p^{\top}V
is the support function of
V
on
\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}
.
\mathbf{T}_{\pi}
is a contraction and
V^{\pi}
is the unique fixed point
(
Nilim & El Ghaoui 2004
;
Iyengar 2005
;
Puterman 2014
)
.

Define the expected worst-case total cost function under the initial distribution
\rho
as
J_{\rho}(\pi)\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\rho}[V^{\pi}(S)].

The goal of the agent is to find an optimal policy that minimizes
J_{\rho}(\pi)
:

\min_{\pi}J_{\rho}(\pi).

R
-Contamination Uncertainty Set.

In this paper, we focus on an adversarial model of the uncertainty set,
R
-contamination, where the nature could arbitrarily perturb the state transition of the MDP with a small probability. Let
\mathsf{P}=\bigotimes_{s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}}p_{s}^{a}
be a transition kernel. The
R
-contamination uncertainty set centered at
\mathsf{P}
is defined as
\mathcal{P}\triangleq\bigotimes_{s,a}\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}\triangleq\left\{(1-R)p^{a}_{s}+Rq|q\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})\right\},s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}.

(3)

This uncertainty set model is widely used in the literature of robust learning and optimization, e.g.,
(
Huber 1965
;
Du et al. 2018
;
Wang & Zou 2021
;
Huber & Ronchetti 2009
;
Nishimura & Ozaki 2004
;
Nishimura & Ozaki 2006
;
Prasad et al. 2020a
;
Prasad et al. 2020b
)
. The
R
-contamination set models the scenario where the state transition could be arbitrarily perturbed with a small probability
R
, hence is more suitable for systems suffering from random perturbations, adversarial attacks, and outliers in sampling.
R
-contamination set can also be connected to uncertainty sets defined by total variation, KL-divergence and Hellinger distance via inequalities, e.g., Pinsker’s inequality. On the other hand, the
R
-contamination model is more clean and straightforward, which makes the derivation of the robust policy gradient, and the convergence and complexity analyses tractable.

Under the
R
-contamination model, the support function can be easily computed as follows:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{P}^{a}_{s}}(V)=(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{a}_{s,{s^{\prime}}}V({s^{\prime}})+R\max_{{s^{\prime}}}V({s^{\prime}}),

(4)

where
p^{a}_{s,{s^{\prime}}}=p^{a}_{s}(s^{\prime})
.

3
Robust Policy Gradient

Consider a parametric policy class
\Pi_{\Theta}=\left\{\pi_{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\right\}
. Denote
J_{\rho}(\pi_{\theta})
by
J_{\rho}(\theta)
. Robust RL aims to find an optimal policy
\pi_{\theta^{*}}\in\Pi_{\Theta}
that minimizes the expected worst-case accumulated discounted cost:

\displaystyle{\theta^{*}}\in\arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta}J_{\rho}(\theta).

(5)

Let
J_{\rho}^{*}\triangleq\min_{\theta\in\Theta}J_{\rho}(\theta)
.
Recall the definition of
V^{\pi}(s)
in (
1
). Due to the
\max
over
\kappa
,
V^{\pi}(s)
may not be differentiable. To solve this issue, we adopt the Fréchet sub-differential
(
Kruger 2003
)
.

Definition 3.1
.

For a function
f:\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, a vector
u\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is called a Fréchet sub-differential of
f
at
x
, if

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\inf_{h\neq 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)-\langle h,u\rangle}{\|h\|}\geq 0.

(6)

The set of all the sub-differential of
f
at
x
is denoted by
\partial f(x)
.

Clearly, when
f(x)
is differentiable at
x
,
\partial f(x)=\left\{\nabla f(x)\right\}
.

Without loss of generality, we assume that the parametric policy class is differentiable and Lipschitz.

Assumption 3.2
.

The policy class
\Pi_{\Theta}
is differentiable and
k_{\pi}
-Lipschitz, i.e., for any
s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}
and
\theta\in\Theta
, there exists a universal constant
k_{\pi}
, such that

\|\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|s)\|\leq k_{\pi}.

This assumption can be easily satisfied by many policy classes, e.g., direct parameterization
(
Agarwal et al. 2021
)
, soft-max
(
Mei et al. 2020
;
Li et al. 2021
;
Zhang et al. 2021a
;
Wang & Zou 2020
)
, or neural network with Lipschitz and smooth activation functions
(
Du et al. 2019
;
Neyshabur 2017
;
Miyato et al. 2018
)
.

Define the discounted visitation distribution as

d^{\pi}_{s}(s^{\prime})\triangleq(1-\gamma+\gamma R)\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\gamma^{t}(1-R)^{t}\cdot\mathbb{P}(S_{t}=s^{\prime}|S_{0}=s,\pi),

and let

d^{\pi}_{\rho}(s^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\rho}[d^{\pi}_{S}(s^{\prime})].

Denote
s_{\theta}\triangleq\arg\max_{s}V^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)
. Then, the (sub-)gradient of
J_{\rho}(\theta)
can be computed in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.3
.

(Robust Policy Gradient)
Consider a class of policies
\Pi_{\Theta}
satisfying
3.2
.
For any distribution
\rho
, denote

\displaystyle\psi_{\rho}(\theta)\triangleq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma R}{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma R)}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s_{\theta}}(s)\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|s)Q^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{\rho}(s)\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|s)Q^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a),

(7)

Then,
(1) almost everywhere in
\Theta
,
J_{\rho}(\theta)
is differentiable and
\psi_{\rho}(\theta)=\nabla J_{\rho}(\theta)
; and
(2) at non-differentiable
\theta
,
\psi_{\rho}(\theta)\in\partial J_{\rho}(\theta)
.

If we set
R=0
, i.e., the uncertainty set
\mathcal{P}
reduces to a singleton
\{\mathsf{P}\}
and there is no robustness, then
\psi_{\rho}(\theta)
in (
3.3
) reduces to the vanilla policy gradient
(
Sutton et al. 1999
)
.

As can be seen from (
3.3
), the robust policy (sub)-gradient is a function of the robust Q-function. Note that in robust RL,
J_{\rho}(\theta)
may not be differentiable everywhere, and therefore, the sub-gradient is needed.
The mistake in
(
Russel et al. 2020
)
is due to the ignorance of the dependence of the worst-case kernel on
\theta
, and their robust policy gradient is a function of the vanilla Q-function not the robust Q-function, which should not be the case.

In policy gradient approaches, the agent often has the challenge of exploration. If
\rho
highly concentrates on a subset of
\mathcal{S}
, then the agent may not be able to explore all the states and may end up with a sub-optimal solution. To solve this issue, we introduce an optimization measure
\mu
satisfying
\mu_{\min}\triangleq\min_{s}\mu(s)>0

(
Agarwal et al. 2021
)
. The initial state distribution
\rho
is called the performance measure. As we will show in the next section, although we want to minimize
\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[V^{\pi}]
, we can perform sub-gradient descent with respect to
J_{\mu}\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[V^{\pi}]
and the algorithm can still find an optimum of
\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[V^{\pi}]
.

Given the robust policy gradient in
Theorem

3.3
, we design our robust policy gradient algorithm in
Algorithm

1
. We note that our
Algorithm

1
can be applied to any arbitrarily parameterized policy class that is differentiable and Lipschitz. Here
\mathbf{\prod}_{\Theta}
denotes the projection onto
\Theta
.

:
T
,
\alpha_{t}

:
\theta_{0}

t=0,1,...,T-1

do

4
Global Optimality: Direct Parameterization

In this section, we show that the robust objective function
J_{\rho}(\theta)
satisfies the Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) condition when the direct parametric policy class is used, i.e.,
\Theta=(\Delta(\mathcal{A}))^{|\mathcal{S}|}
and
\pi_{\theta}(a|s)=\theta_{s,a}
, and we further show the global optimality of
Algorithm

1
.

Algorithm

1
is in fact a sub-gradient descent algorithm for a non-convex function
J_{\mu}
. Following classic results from stochastic approximation and optimization
(
Beck 2017
;
Borkar 2009
;
Borkar & Meyn 2000
)
,
Algorithm

1
is expected to converge to stationary points only. Showing the global optimality requires further characterization of
J_{\mu}
, which involves the “max” over
\kappa
, and is thus more challenging than the vanilla non-robust case.

We first show that the robust objective function
J_{\rho}
satisfies the PL-condition under the direct parameterization.
Informally, a function
f(\theta)
is said to satisfy the PL condition if

f(\theta)-f(\theta^{*})\leq\mathcal{O}(F(\nabla f(\theta))),

for some suitable scalar notion of first-order stationarity
F
, which measures how large the gradient is
(
Karimi et al. 2016
;
Bolte et al. 2007a
)
.
This condition implies that if a solution is close to some first-order stationary point, the function value is then close to the global optimum.

Theorem 4.1

(PL-Condition)
.

Under direct policy parameterization and for any optimization measure
\mu\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
and performance measure
\rho\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
,

\displaystyle J_{\rho}(\theta)-J_{\rho}^{*}\leq C_{PL}\max_{\hat{\pi}\in(\Delta(\mathcal{A}))^{|\mathcal{S}|}}\left\langle\pi_{\theta}-\hat{\pi},\psi_{\mu}(\theta)\right\rangle,

(8)

where
C_{PL}=\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)\mu_{\min}}
.

Note that
\psi_{\mu}(\theta)
on the right hand side of (
8
) is a sub-gradient of
J_{\mu}
, and
J_{\rho}(\theta)
on the left hand side of (
8
) is the objective function with respect to
\rho
. Therefore, for any optimization measure
\mu
, a stationary point of
J_{\mu}
is a global optimum of
J_{\rho}
.
Thus the PL-condition in
Theorem

4.1
with results from stochastic approximation will lead to the global optimality of Algorithm
1
in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

If
\alpha_{t}>0
,
\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\alpha_{t}=\infty
and
\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\alpha^{2}_{t}<\infty
, then Algorithm
1
converges to a global optimum of
J_{\rho}(\theta)
almost surely.

Theorem
4.2
suggests that our robust policy gradient algorithm converges to a global optimum, which matches the global optimality results for vanilla policy gradients in e.g.,
(
Agarwal et al. 2021
;
Mei et al. 2020
)
. However, the analysis here is much more challenging due to the non-differentiable objective function and min-max problem structure.

5
Smoothed Robust Policy Gradient

It is in general challenging to analyze the convergence rate of
Algorithm

1
, which is a projected sub-gradient descent algorithm for non-differentiable non-convex function. In this section, we construct a smoothed approximation
J_{\sigma,\rho}
, which converges to
J_{\rho}
as
\sigma\rightarrow\infty
. We develop a smoothed robust policy gradient, and show that the smoothed
J_{\sigma,\rho}
satisfies the PL-condition. We characterize its global optimality and show that to achieve an
\epsilon
-global optimal, the complexity is
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
.

For convenience, in the remaining of this paper we assume that
\rho=\mu
and
\rho_{\min}>0
, and we omit the subscript
\rho
in
J_{\rho}
and
J_{\sigma,\rho}
. The algorithm design and theoretical results can be similarly extended to the general setting with
\rho\neq\mu
as in
Section

3
and
Section

4
.

We use the LogSumExp (LSE) operator to approximate the
\max
operator, where

\text{LSE}(\sigma,V)=\frac{\log(\sum^{d}_{i=1}e^{\sigma V(i)})}{\sigma}

for
V\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and some
\sigma>0
.
The approximation error
|\text{LSE}(\sigma,V)-\max V|\to 0
as
\sigma\to\infty
. By replacing
\max
in (
2
) using LSE, the smoothed Bellman operator is

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{\sigma}^{\pi}V(s)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{A\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}\bigg[c(s,A)+\gamma(1-R)\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{A}_{s,{s^{\prime}}}V({s^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\gamma R\cdot\text{LSE}(\sigma,V)\bigg].

(9)

The reason why we do not use soft-max is because with soft-max, the induced Bellman operator is not a contraction anymore
(
Asadi & Littman 2017
;
Wang & Zou 2021
)
.
With LSE,
\mathbf{T}_{\sigma}^{\pi}
is a contraction and has a unique fixed point
(
Wang & Zou 2021
)
, which we denote by
V^{\pi}_{\sigma}
and name as the smoothed robust value function. We can also define the smoothed robust action-value function
Q^{\pi}_{\sigma}(s,a)\triangleq c(s,a)+\gamma(1-R)\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{a}_{s,{s^{\prime}}}V^{\pi}_{\sigma}({s^{\prime}})+\gamma R\cdot\text{LSE}(\sigma,V^{\pi}_{\sigma})
.

Clearly,
V^{\pi}_{\sigma}
is differentiable in
\theta
and it converges to
V^{\pi}
as
\sigma\to\infty
.
We then define the smoothed objective function as

\displaystyle J_{\sigma}(\theta)=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\rho(s)V^{\pi_{\theta}}_{\sigma}(s),

(10)

and let

J_{\sigma}^{*}=\min_{\theta}J_{\sigma}(\theta).

Note

is an approximation of
J(\theta)
, and
J_{\sigma}^{*}
also converges to
J^{*}
as
\sigma\to\infty
. In the following theorem, we derive the gradient of
J_{\sigma}
, which holds for any differentiable policy class.

Theorem 5.1
.

Consider a policy class
\Pi_{\Theta}
that is differentiable.
The gradient of
J_{\sigma}(\theta)
is

\displaystyle\nabla J_{\sigma}(\theta)=B(\rho,\theta)+\frac{\gamma R\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}B(s,\theta)}{(1-\gamma)\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}},

(11)

where
B(s,\theta)\triangleq\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\\
\cdot Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)
, and
B(\rho,\theta)\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\rho}[B(S,\theta)]
.

6
Robust Actor-Critic

The results discussed so far assume full knowledge of robust value functions and visitation distributions, and thus the (smoothed) robust policy gradient is exactly known. Although this is a commonly used setting for theoretical analysis e.g., in
(
Agarwal et al. 2021
;
Mei et al. 2020
;
Bhandari & Russo 2019
;
Cen et al. 2021
)
, such knowledge is usually unknown in practice. In this section, we focus on the practical
model-free
setting where only training samples from the centroid transition kernel
\mathsf{P}
can be obtained.

As can be seen from (
3.3
) and (
11
), to obtain the (smoothed) robust policy (sub)-gradient, we first need to estimate the robust value function. Robust value function measures the performance on the worst-case transition kernel which is typically different from the one that generates samples. However, Monte Carlo (MC) method can only be used to estimate the value function on the kernel that generates the samples.

To solve this issue, we design a robust TD algorithm, and combine it with our robust policy gradient descent to design the robust actor-critic algorithm.
Consider a parametric robust action value function
Q_{\zeta}
, e.g., linear function approximation, neural network. The robust TD algorithm is given in
Algorithm

. Note that by replacing
\max
in the algorithm by

8
Discussions

In this paper, we develop direct policy search method for robust RL. Our robust algorithms can be applied with arbitrary differentiable value function approximation and policy parameterization, and thus is scalable to problems with large state and action spaces.
In this paper, the analysis is for the direct policy parameterization. Our approach can also be extended to establish the global optimality under other policy parameterizations, e.g., softmax. It is also of future interest to develop robust natural policy gradient approaches for robust RL.
We focus on the
R
-contamination model for the uncertainty set, which can be closely related to sets defined by total variation and Kullback-Leibler divergence using Pinsker’s inequality. It is also of future interest to investigate model-free approaches for other uncertainty sets defined via e.g., total variation, Kullback-Leibler divergence, Wasserstain distance.

A.1
Robust Value Function Is Lipschitz

In this section, we show that the robust value function is Lipschitz.

Lemma A.1
.

Under Assumption
3.2
, the robust value function is Lipschitz in
\theta
, i.e., for any
s\in\mathcal{S}
, any
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\Theta
,

\displaystyle|V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s)|\leq\frac{k_{\pi}|\mathcal{A}|}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|\triangleq L_{V}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|,

(17)

where
L_{V}=\frac{k_{\pi}|\mathcal{A}|}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}
.

Proof.

For any
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\Theta
, we have that

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|s)Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s,a))-\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|s)Q^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s,a))

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|s)Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s,a))-\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|s)Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s,a))+\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|s)Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s,a))-\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|s)Q^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s,a))

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|s)-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|s))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s,a)+\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|s)(Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s,a)-Q^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s,a))

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|s)-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|s))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s,a)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|s)\left(\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{a}_{s,{s^{\prime}}}\left(V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}({s^{\prime}})\right)+\gamma R\left(\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}-\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|s)-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|s))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s,a)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})\underbrace{(V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}({s^{\prime}}))}_{(c)}+\gamma R\left(\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}-\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}\right),

(18)

where the equation
(a)
is from the fact that
Q^{\pi}
is the fixed point of the robust Bellman operator
\mathbf{T}_{\pi}
, i.e.,
Q^{\pi}(s,a)=c(s,a)+\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{a}_{s,s^{\prime}}V^{\pi}(s^{\prime})+\gamma R\max V^{\pi}
, where
V^{\pi}(s)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)Q^{\pi}(s,a)
, and equation
(b)
is because
\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|s)p^{a}_{s,{s^{\prime}}}
. Note that the term
(c)

V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}({s^{\prime}})
is again the difference between robust value functions, hence apply (
A.1
) recursively, and we obtain that

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|s)-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|s))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s,a)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}}){(V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}({s^{\prime}}))}+\gamma R\left(\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}-\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|s)-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|s))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s,a)+\gamma R\left(\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}-\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})\bigg(\sum_{a^{\prime}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a^{\prime}|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a^{\prime}|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma R\left(\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}-\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}\right)+\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime\prime}}|S_{0}={s^{\prime}},\pi_{\theta_{2}})(V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime\prime}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}({s^{\prime\prime}}))\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|s)-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|s))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s,a)+\gamma R\left(\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}-\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})\sum_{a^{\prime}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a^{\prime}|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a^{\prime}|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma R\gamma(1-R)\left(\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}-\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma^{2}(1-R)^{2}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime\prime}}|S_{0}={s^{\prime}},\pi_{\theta_{2}})(V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime\prime}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}({s^{\prime\prime}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\mathbb{P}(S_{0}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})+\gamma(1-R)\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})\right)\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(x,a)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma R(1+\gamma(1-R))\left(\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}-\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma^{2}(1-R)^{2}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime\prime}}|S_{0}={s^{\prime}},\pi_{\theta_{2}})(V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime\prime}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}({s^{\prime\prime}})).

(19)

Note that in the last term,
\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime\prime}}|S_{0}={s^{\prime}},\pi_{\theta_{2}})=\mathbb{P}(S_{2}={s^{\prime\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})
, hence the last term can be written as
\gamma^{2}(1-R)^{2}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S_{2}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})(V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}({s^{\prime}}))
. Then the difference between robust value functions can be further written as

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\mathbb{P}(S_{0}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})+\gamma(1-R)\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})\right)\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma R(1+\gamma(1-R))\left(\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}-\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma^{2}(1-R)^{2}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S_{2}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})(V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}({s^{\prime}})).

(20)

Recursively applying this equation, we have that

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\gamma(1-R))^{k}\mathbb{P}(S_{k}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma R(\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}(\gamma(1-R))^{k})\left(\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}-\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\gamma(1-R))^{k}\mathbb{P}(S_{k}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta_{2}})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\left(\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}-\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}\right).

(21)

The first term can be treated as an expectation under the discounted visitation distribution:

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(d^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\left(\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}-\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}\right),

(22)

where
d^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})=(1-\gamma(1-R))\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}(1-R)^{k}\left(\mathbb{P}(S_{k}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s_{0},\pi)\right)
.

Taking absolute value on both sides, we then have that

\displaystyle|V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(d^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\left(\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}-\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}\right)\bigg|.

(23)

We then consider the second term
\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}-\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}
in (
A.1
). Without loss of generality, assume that
V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})\geq V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{2}})
.
Then
|\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}-\max V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}|=V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{2}})=V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})+V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{2}})\leq V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})
because
V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})\leq V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{2}})
. Then (
A.1
) can be written as

\displaystyle|V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(d^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\left|\left(V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})\right)\right|.

(24)

Note that this inequality holds for any state
s
. By setting
s=s_{\theta_{1}}
we have

\displaystyle|V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(d^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}_{s_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}|\left(V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})\right)|,

(25)

which implies that

\displaystyle|V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})|\leq\left|\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(d^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}_{s_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)\right)\right|.

(26)

Plugging this inequality in (
A.1
) implies that

\displaystyle|V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(d^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\frac{\gamma R}{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma R)}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(d^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}_{s_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)\right)\right|.

(27)

Note that from
c(s,a)\in[0,1]
,
Q^{\pi}(s,a)=\max_{\kappa}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa}[\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\gamma^{t}c(S_{t},A_{t})|S_{0}=s,A_{0}=a,\pi]\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
for any
\pi,s,a
; and from Assumption
3.2
,
|\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}})|\leq k_{\pi}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|
, hence we have that

\displaystyle|V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s)|\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}+\frac{\gamma R}{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma R)}\right)\frac{k_{\pi}|\mathcal{A}|}{1-\gamma}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{\pi}|\mathcal{A}|}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|,

(28)

which completes the proof.
∎

The following corollary is straightforward hence the proof is omitted:

Corollary A.2
.

The robust action-value functions
Q^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a)
for any
s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}
and the objective function

are Lipschitz in
\theta
with constant
L_{V}
, i.e., for any
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\Theta
,

\displaystyle|Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s,a)-Q^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s,a)|\leq L_{V}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|,

(29)

\displaystyle|J_{\rho}(\theta_{1})-J_{\rho}(\theta_{2})|\leq L_{V}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|.

(30)

A.2
Proof of Theorem
3.3
: Sub-gradient of Robust Value Function

In this section, we derive the sub-gradient of robust value function
J_{\rho}(\theta)
and prove Theorem
3.3
.

Denote
s_{\theta}=\arg\max_{s}V^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)
, and define

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}_{s}(\theta)

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{\gamma R}{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma R)}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a),

(31)

Recall from (
3.3
) that
\psi_{\rho}(\theta)
is the average of
\hat{\phi}_{s}(\theta)
under distribution
\rho
. In the following, we show that
\hat{\phi}_{s}(\theta)\in\partial V^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)
, and hence naturally,
\psi_{\rho}(\theta)\in\partial J_{\rho}(\theta)
. In Section
A.2.1
, we will demonstrate how we derive the expression of
\hat{\phi}_{s}(\theta)
.

From (
A.1
) we can show that

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(d^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\left(V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{2}})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(d^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\left(V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{2}})+V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{2}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{2}})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(d^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\left(V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{2}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{2}})\right),

(32)

where the last inequality is from
V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{1}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{2}})\geq 0
. Set
s=s_{\theta_{2}}
in the LHS of (
A.2
), we further have that

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s_{\theta_{2}})-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s_{\theta_{2}})\geq\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(d^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}_{s_{\theta_{2}}}({s^{\prime}})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)\right).

(33)

Plug (
33
) back into (
A.2
), it follows that

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(d^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma R}{(1-\gamma+\gamma R)(1-\gamma)}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(d^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}_{s_{\theta_{2}}}({s^{\prime}})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|{s^{\prime}}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)\right).

(34)

Hence for any
\theta,\theta+h\in\Theta
, we have that

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{\theta+h}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left(\pi_{\theta+h}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\right)Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma R}{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma R)}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left(\pi_{\theta+h}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\right)Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a).

(35)

We then can show that

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{\theta+h}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)-\langle\hat{\phi}_{s}(\theta),h\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left(\pi_{\theta+h}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\right)Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\langle h,\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\rangle Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma R}{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma R)}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left(\pi_{\theta+h}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\right)Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\langle h,\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\rangle Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a).

(36)

The first two terms in (
A.2
) can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left(\pi_{\theta+h}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\right)Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\langle h,\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\rangle Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left(\left(\pi_{\theta+h}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\right)Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)-\langle h,\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\rangle Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right).

(37)

Moreover note that

\displaystyle\left(\pi_{\theta+h}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\right)Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)-\langle h,\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\rangle Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)

\displaystyle=\left(\pi_{\theta+h}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\right)Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)-\langle h,\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\rangle Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)

\displaystyle\quad+\langle h,\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\rangle Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)-\langle h,\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\rangle Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a).

(38)

Since
\pi_{\theta}
is differentiable, hence

\displaystyle\lim_{\|h\|\to 0}\frac{\pi_{\theta+h}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\langle h,\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\rangle}{\|h\|}=0.

(39)

Because
Q^{\pi_{\theta}}\geq 0
for any
\theta
, we further have that

\displaystyle\lim_{\|h\|\to 0}\frac{\left(\pi_{\theta+h}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\right)Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)-\langle h,\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\rangle Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)}{\|h\|}\geq 0.

(40)

For the remaining term in (
A.2
), note that
\lim_{h\to 0}Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)-Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)=0
as
Q^{\pi_{\theta}}
is Lipschitz in
\theta
, thus

\displaystyle\lim_{\|h\|\to 0}\frac{\langle h,\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})(Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)-Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a))\rangle}{\|h\|}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\|h\|\to 0}{\left\langle\frac{h}{\|h\|},\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})(Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)-Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a))\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\|h\|\to 0}\langle e_{h},\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})(Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)-Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a))\rangle,

(41)

where
e_{h}
is the normalized vector of
h
, and
\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})(Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)-Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a))
is a vector of dimension
|\mathcal{S}|\times|\mathcal{A}|
.

Clearly,
\lim_{\|h\|\to 0}\langle e_{h},\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})(Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)-Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a))\rangle=0
, which is also due to the Lipschitz of
Q^{\pi_{\theta}}
.
Hence combining all these inequalities in (
40
) and (
A.2
), we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{\|h\|\to 0}\inf_{h\neq 0}\frac{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left(\left(\pi_{\theta+h}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\right)Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)-\langle h,\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\rangle Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)}{\|h\|}\geq 0.

(42)

Similarly, we can also show that for the remaining terms in (
A.2
),

\displaystyle\lim_{\|h\|\to 0}\inf_{h\neq 0}\frac{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left(\left(\pi_{\theta+h}(a|{s^{\prime}})-\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\right)Q^{\pi_{\theta+h}}({s^{\prime}},a)-\langle h,\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})\rangle Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)\right)}{\|h\|}\geq 0.

(43)

Hence

\displaystyle\lim_{\|h\|\to 0}\inf_{h\neq 0}\frac{V^{\pi_{\theta+h}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)-\langle\hat{\phi}_{s}(\theta),h\rangle}{\|h\|}\geq 0,

(44)

and this implies that
\hat{\phi}_{s}(\theta)\in\partial V^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)
for any
\theta
.

Now we consider
J_{\rho}
. From the definition,
J_{\rho}(\theta)=\sum_{{s}\in\mathcal{S}}\rho({s})V^{\pi_{\theta}}({s})
. Hence we have that

\displaystyle\partial J_{\rho}(\theta)

\displaystyle\supseteq\sum_{{s}\in\mathcal{S}}\rho({s})\partial V^{\pi_{\theta}}({s})

\displaystyle\ni\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\rho(s)\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma R}{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma R)}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{\rho}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})Q^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)

\displaystyle\triangleq\psi_{\rho}(\theta),

(45)

which implies
\psi_{\rho}(\theta)\in\partial J_{\rho}(\theta)
.

A.2.1
Derivation of The Sub-gradient
\hat{\phi}_{s}(\theta)

In this section we show how we derive the expression of the sub-gradient
\hat{\phi}_{s}
.

Recall that
\max_{s}V^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)
is Lipschitz in
\theta
as shown in Section
A
. From the Rademacher’s theorem
(
Federer 2014
)
, we know that
\max_{s}V^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)
is differentiable almost everywhere. Hence at those differentiable
\theta
, we define
\phi(\theta)=\nabla\max_{s}V^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)
, which can be also viewed as a sub-gradient of
\max_{s}V^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)
, i.e.,
\phi(\theta)\in\partial\max_{s}V^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)
.

It is known from
(
Kruger 2003
)
that
\partial(f)+\partial(g)\subseteq\partial(f+g)
, hence we have that for any
s\in\mathcal{S}
,

\displaystyle\partial V^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)

Note that each entry of

When
\pi_{\theta}

Similar to (
A.1
) to (
A.1
) without taking the absolute value, we have that

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(d^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}_{s}(s^{\prime})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|s^{\prime})-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|s^{\prime}))Q^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s^{\prime},a)\right)\right)

Set
\theta_{1}=\theta
and
\theta_{2}=\theta^{*}
, where

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta^{*}}}(s)

Note that

\displaystyle J_{\rho}(\theta)-J^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\rho(s)(V^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)-V^{\pi_{\theta^{*}}}(s))

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{1}{1-\gamma(1-R)}d^{\pi_{\theta*}}_{\rho}(s^{\prime})+\frac{\gamma R}{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma R)}d^{\pi_{\theta*}}_{s_{\theta}}(s^{\prime})\right)\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}(\pi_{\theta}(a|s^{\prime})-\pi_{\theta*}(a|s^{\prime}))Q^{\pi_{\theta}}(s^{\prime},a)\right)

(59)

A.4
Proof of Theorem
4.2
: Convergence and Global Optimality

The theorem can be proved following the standard results of stochastic differential inclusion in
(
Ruszczyński 2020
;
Majewski et al. 2018
;
Borkar 2009
;
Borkar & Meyn 2000
)
.

Lemma A.3
.

(Theorem 4.1 in
(
Majewski et al. 2018
)
)
If the sequence
\left\{\theta_{t}\right\}
is generated by a stochastic algorithm
\theta_{t+1}\leftarrow\mathbf{\prod}_{K}(\theta_{t}-\alpha_{t}Y_{t})
, where
Y_{t}\in F(\theta_{t})
for some set-value function
F
, where
F(\theta)=-\partial_{C}f(\theta)
for some function
f
, where
\partial_{C}
denotes the Clarke sub-gradient
(
Clarke 1990
)
. Denote the normal cone
N_{K}(\theta)\triangleq\left\{g\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\langle g,\theta^{\prime}-\theta\rangle\leq 0\text{ for any }\theta^{\prime}\in K\right\}
.
If

(1). the set-valued map
\theta\mapsto F(\theta)
is upper hemicontinuous, convex-compact valued and locally bounded;

(2). the step-sizes satisfy
\alpha_{t}>0
,
\sum^{\infty}_{t}\alpha_{t}=\infty
and
\sum^{\infty}_{t}\alpha_{t}^{2}<\infty
;

(3). denote the stationary points
\mathcal{X}^{*}\triangleq\left\{x:0\in\partial_{C}f(x)+N_{K}(x)\right\}
, and
f(\mathcal{X}^{*})
has no interior;

then
\theta_{t}
convergence to
\mathcal{X}^{*}
almost surely.

Note that according to
(
Clarke 1990
;
Bolte et al. 2007b
)
,
\partial J_{\mu}({\theta})\subseteq\partial_{C}J_{\mu}({\theta})
, hence a Fréchet sub-gradient is also a Clarke sub-gradient, and our robust policy gradient method can be viewed as a Clarke sub-gradient descent method.

It is shown in Proposition 2.1.5 and Proposition 2.1.2 in
(
Clarke 1990
)
that the Clarke sub-gradient of a Lipschitz function is convex-compact valued, locally bounded, and lower hemicontinuous. Hence the map
-\partial J_{C}^{\mu}(\theta)
satisfies assumption (1). The assumption (2) is easy to satisfy, e.g.,
\alpha_{t}=\frac{1}{t+1}
.

Before verifying Assumption (3), we first show a lemma.

Lemma A.4
.

For any
\hat{\theta}\in\left\{\theta:0\in\partial J_{C}^{\mu}(\theta)\right\}
,
J_{\rho}(\hat{\theta})=J_{\rho}^{*}
.

Proof.

From Rademacher’s theorem
(
Federer 2014
)
, we know that
J_{\mu}(\theta)
is differentiable almost everywhere. And from
(
Kruger 2003
)
, we have that if
J_{\mu}(\theta)
is differentiable at
\theta
, then
\partial J_{\mu}(\theta)=\left\{\nabla J_{\mu}(\theta)\right\}
and hence
\psi_{\mu}(\theta)=\nabla J_{\mu}(\theta)
.

Now consider any
\hat{\theta}\in\left\{\theta:0\in\partial J_{C}^{\mu}(\theta)\right\}
. From
(
Clarke 1990
;
Zhang et al. 2020b
)
, the Clarke sub-gradient can be viewed as a convex hull of limit gradients:

\displaystyle\partial_{C}J_{\mu}(\hat{\theta})=\text{conv}\left(\left\{g:\exists\theta_{n}\to\hat{\theta},\text{ s.t. }\nabla J_{\mu}(\theta_{n})\to g\right\}\right).

(65)

Denote
S\triangleq\left\{g:\exists\theta_{n}\to\hat{\theta},\text{ s.t. }\nabla J_{\mu}(\theta_{n})\to g\right\}
. Note that if
0\in\text{conv}(S)
, then
\exists\lambda_{i}\in[0,1]\text{ and }g_{i}\in S,i=1,...,k
, such that
\sum_{i}^{k}\lambda_{i}=1
and
0=\sum_{i}^{k}\lambda_{i}g_{i}
. Note that
g_{i}\in S
is a limit of a sequence of gradient
\left\{\psi_{\mu}(\theta_{i}^{n})\right\}_{n}
where
\theta^{n}_{i}\to\hat{\theta}
. However, note that from (
53
), every entry of
\psi_{\mu}(\theta)
is non-negative for any
\theta
, and hence
g_{i}
also has non-negative entries.

This implies that
\exists j\in\left\{1,...,k\right\}
, such that
g_{j}=0
. This further implies that there exists a sequence
{\theta}^{t}_{j}\to\hat{\theta}
, with
\psi_{\mu}(\theta^{t}_{j})=\nabla J_{\mu}({\theta}^{t}_{j})\to 0
, i.e.,
\|\nabla J_{\mu}(\theta^{t}_{j})\|\to 0
.

From the PL-condition in Theorem
4.1
, we know that
J_{\rho}(\theta^{t}_{j})-J_{\rho}^{*}\leq C_{PL}|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|\|\nabla J_{\mu}(\theta^{t}_{j})\|\to 0
and hence
J_{\rho}(\theta^{t}_{j})\to J_{\rho}^{*}
. As
J_{\rho}(\theta)
is a continuous function,
J_{\rho}(\theta^{t}_{j})\to J_{\rho}(\hat{\theta})
and hence
J_{\rho}(\hat{\theta})=J_{\rho}^{*}
. This means any
\hat{\theta}\in\left\{\theta:0\in\partial_{C}J_{\mu}(\theta)\right\}
is a global optimal point, i.e.,
J_{\rho}(\hat{\theta})=J_{\rho}^{*}
.
∎

We then verify the last assumption (3). First note that from the definition of Clarke sub-differential, it can be verified that
\mathcal{X}^{*}\subseteq\left\{\theta:0\in\partial_{C}f(\theta)\right\}
. Hence from Lemma
A.4
,
\mathcal{X}^{*}
is a subset of global optimal points of
J_{\mu}
(set
\rho=\mu
in Lemma
A.4
), hence
J_{\mu}(\mathcal{X}^{*})=\left\{J_{\mu}^{*}\right\}
is a singleton and contains no interior.

Hence the lemma
A.3
holds for our robust policy gradient algorithm. Then, by the lemma, any sequence
\left\{\theta_{t}\right\}
generated by Algorithm
1
converges to a stationary point a.s., i.e.,
\lim_{t}{\theta_{t}}\in\left\{\theta:0\in\partial J_{C}^{\mu}(\theta)+N_{(\Delta(\mathcal{A}))^{|\mathcal{S}|}}(\theta)\right\}\subseteq\left\{\theta:0\in\partial J_{C}^{\mu}(\theta)\right\}
almost surely.

Note that Lemma
A.4
shows that any
\theta\in\left\{\theta:0\in\partial J_{C}^{\mu}(\theta)\right\}
is a global optimal point of
J_{\rho}
, and this implies that the sequence
\left\{\theta_{t}\right\}
generated by the Algorithm
1
converges to the global optimum of
J_{\rho}
, i.e.,
J_{\rho}(\theta_{t})\to J_{\rho}^{*}
almost surely. And hence this completes the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

B.1
Proof of Theorem
5.1
: Gradient of
J_{\sigma}

In this section we derive the gradient of
J_{\sigma}(\theta)\triangleq\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\rho({s^{\prime}})V^{\pi_{\theta}}_{\sigma}({s^{\prime}})
, and prove Theorem
5.1
.

From the fact that
V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{\theta}(a|s)Q^{\pi_{\theta}}_{\sigma}(s,a)
, we have that

\displaystyle\nabla V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)

\displaystyle=\nabla\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{\theta}(a|s)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|s)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a)+\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{\theta}(a|s)\nabla Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|s)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a)+\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{\theta}(a|s)\nabla\left(c(s,a)+\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{a}_{s,{s^{\prime}}}V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}})+\gamma R\cdot\text{LSE}(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|s)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a)+\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{\theta}(a|s)\left(\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{a}_{s,{s^{\prime}}}\nabla V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}})+\gamma R\cdot\nabla\text{LSE}(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|s)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a)+\gamma R\cdot\nabla\text{LSE}(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}})+\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{\theta}(a|s)\left(\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{a}_{s,{s^{\prime}}}\nabla V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|s)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a)+\gamma R\cdot\nabla\text{LSE}(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}})+\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{\theta}(a|s)\left(\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{a}_{s,{s^{\prime}}}\nabla\left(\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{\theta}(a^{\prime}|{s^{\prime}})Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a^{\prime})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|s)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a)+\gamma R\cdot\nabla\text{LSE}(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}})+\gamma(1-R)\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{\theta}(a|s)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{a}_{s,{s^{\prime}}}\sum_{a^{\prime}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a^{\prime}|{s^{\prime}})Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma(1-R)\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{\theta}(a|s)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{a}_{s,{s^{\prime}}}\sum_{a^{\prime}}\pi_{\theta}(a^{\prime}|{s^{\prime}})\nabla Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|s)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a)+\gamma R\cdot\nabla\text{LSE}(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}})+\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta})\sum_{a^{\prime}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a^{\prime}|{s^{\prime}})Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S_{1}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta})\sum_{a^{\prime}}\pi_{\theta}(a^{\prime}|{s^{\prime}})\nabla Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a^{\prime})

\displaystyle=...

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}(1-R)^{k}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S_{k}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|s^{\prime})Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\nabla\text{LSE}(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|s^{\prime})Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\nabla\text{LSE}(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}),

(66)

where
(a)
is from the definition of
Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}
, and
(b)
is to recursively apply the previous steps.

To simplify the notations, we denote
B(s,\theta)\triangleq\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)
. We then study the second term
\nabla\text{LSE}(V^{\pi_{\theta}}_{\sigma})
in (
B.1
). Note that

\displaystyle\nabla\text{LSE}(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}})

\displaystyle=\nabla\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}\log\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{\nabla\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}\right)}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}\sigma\nabla V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}\nabla V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}}.

(67)

Then we plug (
B.1
) in (
B.1
), we have that

\displaystyle\nabla\text{LSE}(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\left(e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}\left(B(s,\theta)+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\nabla\text{LSE}(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}B(s,\theta)}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}}+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\nabla\text{LSE}(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}).

(68)

This implies that

\displaystyle\nabla\text{LSE}(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}})=\frac{1-\gamma+\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}B(s,\theta)}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}}.

(69)

Hence plugging it in (
B.1
) implies that

\displaystyle\nabla V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)=B(s,\theta)+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}B(s,\theta)}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}}.

(70)

And finally it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\nabla J_{\sigma}(\theta)=B(\rho,\theta)+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}B(s,\theta)}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}},

(71)

where
B(\rho,\theta)\triangleq\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{\rho}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|{s^{\prime}})Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}({s^{\prime}},a)
.

B.2
Proof of Lemma
5.4
: Smoothness of
J_{\sigma}

In this section we prove Lemma
5.4
that the smoothed robust value function
J_{\sigma}(\theta)
is
L_{\sigma}
-smooth in
\theta
.
It has been shown in
(
Wang & Zou 2021
)
that
\|V_{\sigma}^{\pi}-V^{\pi}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}
. Moreover we have that

\displaystyle\|Q^{\pi}_{\sigma}-Q^{\pi}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\max_{s,a}|\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\gamma(1-R)p^{a}_{s,s^{\prime}}(V^{\pi}_{\sigma}(s^{\prime})-V^{\pi}(s^{\prime}))+\gamma R(\text{LSE}(V^{\pi}_{\sigma})-\max V^{\pi})|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(1-R)\|V_{\sigma}^{\pi}-V^{\pi}\|_{\infty}+|\gamma R(\text{LSE}(V^{\pi}_{\sigma})-\max V^{\pi})|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(1-R)\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}+\gamma R|\text{LSE}(V^{\pi}_{\sigma})-\text{LSE}(V^{\pi})|+\gamma R|\text{LSE}(V^{\pi})-\max V^{\pi}|

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\gamma(1-R)\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}+\gamma R\|V_{\sigma}^{\pi}-V^{\pi}\|_{\infty}+\gamma R|\text{LSE}(V^{\pi})-\max V^{\pi}|

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\frac{\gamma^{2}R}{1-\gamma}\frac{\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}+\gamma R\frac{\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma},

(72)

where
(a)
is from the fact that
|\text{LSE}(V_{1})-\text{LSE}(V_{2})|\leq\|V_{1}-V_{2}\|_{\infty}
((59) in
(
Wang & Zou 2021
)
), and
(b)
is because
\text{LSE}(V)-\max V\leq\frac{\log|S|}{\sigma}
((61) in
(
Wang & Zou 2021
)
).

Note that if we define a new cost function
c^{\prime}(s,a)\triangleq c(s,a)+\gamma R\frac{\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}
, the robust action-value function w.r.t. new cost function
c^{\prime}
is
Q^{\pi}_{c^{\prime}}(s,a)=\max_{\kappa}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa}[\sum^{\infty}_{t=0}\gamma^{t}c^{\prime}(S_{t},A_{t})^{t}|S_{0}=s,A_{0}=a,\pi]\geq\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}
for any
s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A},\pi
. Hence the smoothed robust value function w.r.t.
c^{\prime}
is non-negative:
Q^{\pi}_{c^{\prime},\sigma}(s,a)\geq Q_{c^{\prime}}^{\pi}(s,a)-\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}\geq 0
for any
s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A},\pi
.

This means by define a new cost function
c^{\prime}
,
Q^{\pi}_{c^{\prime},\sigma}
and
V^{\pi}_{c^{\prime},\sigma}
are non-negative. In the remaining parts, we omit the subscript
c^{\prime}
and denote the smoothed robust value functions w.r.t.
c^{\prime}
by
Q^{\pi}_{\sigma}
and
V^{\pi}_{\sigma}
.

On the other had, the upper bounds on them can be easily derived:

\displaystyle Q^{\pi}_{\sigma}(s,a)

\displaystyle\leq Q_{c^{\prime}}^{\pi}(s,a)+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}(1+\gamma R\frac{\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma})+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}(1+2\gamma R\frac{\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}),

(73)

\displaystyle V^{\pi}_{\sigma}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)Q^{\pi}_{\sigma}(s,a)\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}(1+2\gamma R\frac{\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}).

(74)

We denote these upper bounds by
C_{\sigma}
.
Hence we have showed that
0\leq Q^{\pi}_{\sigma}(s,a)\leq C_{\sigma}
and
0\leq V^{\pi}_{\sigma}(s)\leq C_{\sigma}
for any
s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A},\pi
.

We then prove Lemma
5.4
.
We first show that
B(s,\theta)
is Lipschitz. From (
70
), we know that

\displaystyle\|\nabla V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)\|\leq\max_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\|B(s,\theta)\|\left(1+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\right)\leq\left(\frac{1-\gamma+\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\right)\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}|\mathcal{A}|k_{\pi}\sup_{s,a}|Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a)|,

(75)

which is from
\|B(s,\theta)\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}|\mathcal{A}|k_{\pi}\sup_{s,a}|Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a)|
for any
\theta\in\Theta,s\in\mathcal{S}
.

As we showed in (
73
),
\|Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\sigma}
. Hence the gradient of
V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}
can be bounded as

\displaystyle\|\nabla V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}|\mathcal{A}|k_{\pi}C_{\sigma}\triangleq C^{V}_{\sigma},

(76)

and this means
V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)
is
C^{V}_{\sigma}
-Lipschitz for any
s\in\mathcal{S}
. Moreover we have that

\displaystyle|Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s,a)-Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s,a)|

\displaystyle=|\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{a}_{s,s^{\prime}}\gamma(1-R)(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(s^{\prime})-V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(s^{\prime}))+\gamma R(\text{LSE}(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}})-\text{LSE}(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}))|

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\gamma(1-R)C^{V}_{\sigma}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|+\gamma RC^{V}_{\sigma}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|

\displaystyle=C^{V}_{\sigma}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|,

(77)

where inequality
(a)
is from
|\text{LSE}(V_{1})-\text{LSE}(V_{2})|\leq\|V_{1}-V_{2}\|_{\infty}
and
V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)
is
C^{V}_{\sigma}
-Lipschitz.

It was showed in
(
Achiam et al. 2017
;
Touati et al. 2020
)
that

\displaystyle d_{\text{TV}}(d^{\pi_{1}}_{s},d^{\pi_{2}}_{s})\leq\frac{2\gamma(1-R)}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\mathbb{E}_{S\sim d^{\pi_{2}}_{s}}\left[d_{\text{TV}}(\pi_{1}(\cdot|S),\pi_{2}(\cdot|S))\right],

(78)

hence we have that for any function
f(s,\theta)
defined on
\mathcal{S}\times\Theta
,

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}_{S\sim d_{s}^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}}[f(S,\theta)]-\mathbb{E}_{S\sim d_{s}^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}}[f(S,\theta)]\|

\displaystyle\leq 2d_{\text{TV}}(d_{s}^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}},d_{s}^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}})\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S},\theta\in\Theta}\|f(s,\theta)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\gamma(1-R)}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{2}k_{\pi}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S},\theta\in\Theta}\|f(s,\theta)\|,

(79)

where the last inequality is from

\displaystyle d_{\text{TV}}(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(\cdot|s),\pi_{\theta_{2}}(\cdot|s))=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}|\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|s)-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|s)|\leq\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{2}k_{\pi}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|.

(80)

This implies that

\displaystyle\|B(s,\theta_{1})-B(s,\theta_{2})\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\|\mathbb{E}_{S\sim d_{s}^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}}[f(S,\theta_{1})]-\mathbb{E}_{S\sim d_{s}^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}}[f(S,\theta_{2})]\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\|\mathbb{E}_{S\sim d_{s}^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}}[f(S,\theta_{1})]-\mathbb{E}_{S\sim d_{s}^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}}[f(S,\theta_{2})]+\mathbb{E}_{S\sim d_{s}^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}}[f(S,\theta_{2})]-\mathbb{E}_{S\sim d_{s}^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}}[f(S,\theta_{2})]\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\left(\mathbb{E}_{S\sim d^{\theta_{1}}_{s}}\|f(S,\theta_{1})-f(S,\theta_{2})\|+\frac{2k_{\pi}|\mathcal{A}|\gamma(1-R)}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S},\theta\in\Theta}\|f(x,\theta)\|\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|\right),

(81)

where
f(x,\theta)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|x)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(x,a)
, and the last inequality is from (
B.2
).

Note that for any
s\in\mathcal{S}
and
\theta\in\Theta
,

\displaystyle\|f(s,\theta)\|=\left\|\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|x)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(x,a)\right\|\leq|\mathcal{A}|k_{\pi}C_{\sigma},

(82)

and

\displaystyle\|f(x,\theta_{1})-f(x,\theta_{2})\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left(\nabla\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|x)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(x,a)-\nabla\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|x)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(x,a)\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left(\nabla\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a|x)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(x,a)-\nabla\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|x)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(x,a)\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left(\nabla\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|x)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{1}}}(x,a)-\nabla\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a|x)Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{2}}}(x,a)\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{A}|C_{\sigma}l_{\pi}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|+|\mathcal{A}|k_{\pi}C^{V}_{\sigma}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|

\displaystyle=\left(|\mathcal{A}|C_{\sigma}l_{\pi}+|\mathcal{A}|k_{\pi}C^{V}_{\sigma}\right)\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|,

(83)

where the last inequality is because
\nabla\pi_{\theta}(a|x)
is
l_{\pi}
-Lipschitz, and
Q_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(x,a)
is
C^{V}_{\sigma}
-Lipschitz.

Hence plugging (
82
) and (
B.2
) in (
B.2
) implies that

\displaystyle\|B(s,\theta_{1})-B(s,\theta_{2})\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\left(|\mathcal{A}|C_{\sigma}l_{\pi}+|\mathcal{A}|k_{\pi}C^{V}_{\sigma}\right)\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2|\mathcal{A}|^{2}\gamma(1-R)}{(1-\gamma+\gamma R)^{2}}k_{\pi}^{2}C_{\sigma}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|

\displaystyle\triangleq k_{B}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|,

(84)

Hence we showed that
B(s,\theta)
and
B(\rho,\theta)
are both
k_{B}
-Lipschitz.

Next we show that
\frac{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}B(s,\theta)}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}}
is also Lipschitz. Note that it is the
inner production of
B(\theta)\triangleq(B(s_{1},\theta),...,B(s_{|\mathcal{S}|},\theta))
and
p^{\theta}_{\sigma}\triangleq\left(\frac{e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s_{1})}}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}},...,\frac{e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s_{|\mathcal{S}|})}}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}}\right)
. Hence

\displaystyle\langle B(\theta_{1}),p_{\sigma}^{\theta_{1}}\rangle-\langle B(\theta_{2}),p_{\sigma}^{\theta_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle B(\theta_{1}),p_{\sigma}^{\theta_{1}}\rangle-\langle B(\theta_{2}),p_{\sigma}^{\theta_{1}}\rangle+\langle B(\theta_{2}),p_{\sigma}^{\theta_{1}}\rangle-\langle B(\theta_{2}),p_{\sigma}^{\theta_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle B(\theta_{1})-B(\theta_{2}),p^{\theta_{1}}_{\sigma}\rangle+\langle B(\theta_{2}),p^{\theta_{1}}_{\sigma}-p^{\theta_{2}}_{\sigma}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|B(\theta_{1})-B(\theta_{2})\|+\|B(\theta_{2})\|\|p^{\theta_{1}}_{\sigma}-p^{\theta_{2}}_{\sigma}\|,

(85)

where the last inequality is because
\|p^{\theta}_{\sigma}\|\leq 1
for any
\theta\in\Theta
.

The Lipschitz smoothness of
p^{\theta}_{\sigma}
can be showed as follows. Note that

\displaystyle\nabla p^{\theta}_{\sigma}(j)

\displaystyle=\nabla\left(\frac{e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(j)}}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(j)}\sigma\nabla V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(j)\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}\right)-\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}\sigma\nabla V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)\right)e^{V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(j)}}{\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma e^{V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(j)}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\left(e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}\left(\nabla V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(j)-\nabla V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)\right)\right)}{\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}\right)^{2}},

(86)

and hence

\displaystyle\|\nabla p^{\theta}_{\sigma}(j)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma e^{V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(j)}\cdot 2\max_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\|\nabla V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)\|}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s)}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sigma C^{V}_{\sigma},

(87)

which is from (
76
).

Thus we have
\|p^{\theta_{1}}_{\sigma}-p^{\theta_{2}}_{\sigma}\|=\sqrt{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\left(p^{\theta_{1}}_{\sigma}(s)-p^{\theta_{2}}_{\sigma}(s)\right)^{2}}\leq 2\sigma\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|}C^{V}_{\sigma}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|
. Moreover, note that

\displaystyle\|B(\theta_{1})-B(\theta_{2})\|\leq\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|}k_{B}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|,

(88)

combining both inequality implies that

Q
-function as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}^{\pi}_{\sigma}Q(s,a)=c(s,a)+\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{a}_{s,{s^{\prime}}}\left(\sum_{b}\pi(b|{s^{\prime}})Q({s^{\prime}},b)\right)+\gamma R\cdot\text{LSE}\left(\sum_{b}\pi(b|{s^{\prime}})Q({s^{\prime}},b)\right).

(115)

Note that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{T}^{\pi}_{\sigma}Q_{1}-\mathbf{T}^{\pi}_{\sigma}Q_{2}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\max_{s,a}\bigg|\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{a}_{s,{s^{\prime}}}\left(\sum_{b}\pi(b|{s^{\prime}})(Q_{1}({s^{\prime}},b)-Q_{2}({s^{\prime}},b))\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma R\cdot\text{LSE}\left(\sum_{b}\pi(b|{s^{\prime}})Q_{1}({s^{\prime}},b)\right)-\gamma R\cdot\text{LSE}\left(\sum_{b}\pi(b|{s^{\prime}})Q_{2}({s^{\prime}},b)\right)\bigg|

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\gamma(1-R)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p^{a}_{s,{s^{\prime}}}\|Q_{1}-Q_{2}\|_{\infty}+\bigg|\gamma R\cdot\left(\sum_{b}\pi(b|{s^{\prime}})(Q_{1}({s^{\prime}},b)-Q_{2}({s^{\prime}},b))\right)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\|Q_{1}-Q_{2}\|_{\infty},

(116)

where
(a)
is from the fact that
LSE
is
1
-Lipschitz for any
\sigma>0
, i.e.,
|\text{LSE}(V_{1})-\text{LSE}(V_{2})|\leq\|V_{1}-V_{2}\|_{\infty}
. Therefore,
\mathbf{T}^{\pi}_{\sigma}
is a contraction and
Q^{\pi}_{\sigma}
is its fixed point. Hence following
(
Borkar & Meyn 2000
)
, smoothed robust TD converges to its fixed point
Q^{\pi}_{\sigma}
almost surely.

The proof for the convergence of the non-smoothed robust TD (Algorithm
5
) follows similarly, and is omitted here.
∎

C.2
Robust Actor-Critic under Tabular Setting

Algorithm 6

:

,

,

, M

We then show the convergence of Algorithm
6
. First we show that the update
g_{t}
is an unbiased estimate of the gradient
\nabla J_{\sigma}
if
Q_{t}=Q^{\pi}_{\sigma}
.

Lemma C.2
.

If
Q_{t}=Q_{\sigma}^{\theta_{t}}
, then
g_{t}
is an unbiased estimate of
\nabla J_{\sigma}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[g_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=\nabla J_{\sigma}(\theta_{t}),

(117)

where
\mathcal{F}_{t}
denotes the
\sigma
-field generated by all the randomness until the
t
-th iteration.

Proof.

First note that
T^{i}
is generated following the geometry distribution
\textbf{Geom}(1-\gamma+\gamma R)
, thus for any
s,{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S_{T^{i}}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(T^{i}=k)\mathbb{P}(S_{k}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-\gamma+\gamma R)(\gamma-\gamma R)^{k}\mathbb{P}(S_{k}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta})

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma+\gamma R)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\gamma-\gamma R)^{k}\mathbb{P}(S_{k}={s^{\prime}}|S_{0}=s,\pi_{\theta})

\displaystyle=d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{s}({s^{\prime}}).

(118)

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[B^{i}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta_{t}}(a|S^{i}_{T^{i}})Q_{t}(S^{i}_{T^{i}},a)\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\rho(s)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(S^{i}_{T^{i}}={s^{\prime}}|S^{i}_{0}=s)\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta_{t}}(a|{s^{\prime}})Q_{t}({s^{\prime}},a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}_{\rho}({s^{\prime}})\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta_{t}}(a|{s^{\prime}})Q_{t}({s^{\prime}},a)

\displaystyle=B(\rho,\theta_{t}).

(119)

According to the algorithm,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(x^{i}_{0}=s^{\prime})=\frac{e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V^{t}(s)}},

(120)

hence we further have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[D^{i}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(x^{i}_{0}=s^{\prime})\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(x^{i}_{T^{i}}=s^{\prime\prime}|x^{i}_{0}=s^{\prime})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta_{t}}(a|s^{\prime\prime})Q_{t}(s^{\prime\prime},a)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V^{t}(s)}}\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}_{s^{\prime}}(s^{\prime\prime})\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta_{t}}(a|s^{\prime\prime})Q_{t}(s^{\prime\prime},a)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V^{t}(s)}}B(s^{\prime},\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{e^{\sigma V^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}_{\sigma}(s^{\prime})}}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}_{\sigma}(s^{\prime})}}B(s^{\prime},\theta).

(121)

Combining (
C.2
) and (
C.2
) thus implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[g^{i}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=B(\rho,\theta_{t})+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{e^{\sigma V^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}_{\sigma}(s^{\prime})}}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}_{\sigma}(s^{\prime})}}B(s^{\prime},\theta)

\displaystyle=\nabla J_{\sigma}(\theta_{t}),

(122)

which completes the proof.
∎

However, in Algorithm
6
we use an estimate
Q_{t}
instead of
Q_{\sigma}^{\theta_{t}}
, and therefore the estimate
g_{t}
may be biased. The next lemma develops an upper bound on this bias.

Lemma C.3
.

Consider Algorithm
6
. If
T_{c}
is chosen such that
\|Q_{T_{c}}-Q_{\sigma}^{\theta_{t}}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon_{\text{est}}
, then we have that for any
i,t
,

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}[g^{i}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]-\nabla J_{\sigma}(\theta_{t})\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}e^{\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}}\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|(\epsilon_{\text{est}}+C_{\sigma})}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|\epsilon_{\text{est}}}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}+\frac{|\mathcal{A}|\epsilon_{\text{est}}}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{\text{est}}+\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}e^{\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}}).

(123)

Proof.

We first have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[B^{i}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta_{t}}(a|s^{i}_{T^{i}})Q_{t}(s^{i}_{T^{i}},a)\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta_{t}}(a|s^{i}_{T^{i}})Q_{\sigma}^{\theta_{t}}(s^{i}_{T^{i}},a)\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta_{t}}(a|s^{i}_{T^{i}})(Q_{t}(s^{i}_{T^{i}},a)-Q_{\sigma}^{\theta_{t}}(s^{i}_{T^{i}},a))\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=B(\rho,\theta_{t})+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta_{t}}(a|s^{i}_{T^{i}})(Q_{t}(s^{i}_{T^{i}},a)-Q_{\sigma}^{\theta_{t}}(s^{i}_{T^{i}},a))\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right].

(124)

We can show that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta_{t}}(a|s^{i}_{T^{i}})(Q_{t}(s^{i}_{T^{i}},a)-Q_{\sigma}^{\theta_{t}}(s^{i}_{T^{i}},a))\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\right\|\leq\frac{|\mathcal{A}|\epsilon_{\text{est}}}{1-\gamma+\gamma R},

(125)

which is from
\|\nabla\pi_{\theta_{t}}(a|s)\|=1
and
\mathbb{E}[|Q_{t}(s^{i}_{T^{i}},a)-Q_{\sigma}^{\theta_{t}}(s^{i}_{T^{i}},a)|]\leq\epsilon_{\text{est}}
. Hence

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{E}[B^{i}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]-B(s,\theta_{t})\right\|\leq\frac{|\mathcal{A}|\epsilon_{\text{est}}}{1-\gamma+\gamma R},

(126)

which means the bias of
B^{i}_{t}
is bounded by
\mathcal{O}({\epsilon_{\text{est}}})
.

We then bound the bias of
D^{i}_{t}
. We first have that

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B(s^{\prime},\theta_{t})e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}-\mathbb{E}[D^{i}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B(s^{\prime},\theta_{t})e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}-\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}}{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}_{s^{\prime}}(s)\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(a|s)Q_{t}(s,a).

(127)

Define
B_{t}(s^{\prime})\triangleq\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}_{s^{\prime}}(s)\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta_{t}}(a|s)Q_{t}(s,a)
, then the bias of
D^{i}_{t}
can be further written as

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B(s^{\prime},\theta_{t})e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}-\mathbb{E}[D^{i}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B(s^{\prime},\theta_{t})e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}-\frac{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}B_{t}(s^{\prime})}{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B(s^{\prime},\theta_{t})e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}-\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B_{t}(s^{\prime})e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s)}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}}{\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B(s^{\prime},\theta_{t})e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}-\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B_{t}(s^{\prime})e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}{\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}\right)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B_{t}(s^{\prime})e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}-\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B_{t}(s^{\prime})e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s)}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}}{\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}\right)}.

(128)

The first term can be bounded as

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B(s^{\prime},\theta_{t})e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}-\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B_{t}(s^{\prime})e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}{\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}(B(s^{\prime},\theta_{t})-B_{t}(s^{\prime}))}{\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\mathcal{A}|\epsilon_{\text{est}}}{1-\gamma+\gamma R},

(129)

where the last inequality is from (
126
).

We then bound the second term in (
C.2
). Consider the numerator,

\displaystyle\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B_{t}(s^{\prime})e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}-\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B_{t}(s^{\prime})e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s)}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}B_{t}(s^{\prime})\right)-\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s)}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}B_{t}(s^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}B_{t}(s^{\prime})\right)-\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}B_{t}(s^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}B_{t}(s^{\prime})\right)-\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s)}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}B_{t}(s^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}-e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}\right)B_{t}(s^{\prime}))\right)}_{(a)}+\underbrace{\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s)}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}B_{t}(s^{\prime})\right)}_{(b)}.

(130)

To bound term
(a)
, we first have that

\displaystyle e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}-e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}=(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})-V_{t}(s^{\prime}))\sigma e^{\sigma(V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})+\lambda(V_{t}(s^{\prime})-V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})))}\leq\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}e^{\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}},

(131)

where the first equation is from the mean-value theorem for some
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
, and the last inequality is because
V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})-V_{t}(s^{\prime})\leq\epsilon_{\text{est}}
and
1-\lambda\leq 1
. Hence term
(a)
can be bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}-e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}\right)B_{t}(s^{\prime}))\right)\right\|\leq\left\|\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}\right)\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}e^{\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}}\right\|\sup_{s}\|B_{t}(s)\|.

(132)

To bound term
(b)
, for some
\lambda\in[0,1]
and by the mean value theorem, we have that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s)}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}B_{t}(s^{\prime})\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}B_{t}(s^{\prime})\right)\right\|\left|\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\sigma e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s)+\sigma(1-\lambda)(V_{t}(s)-V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s))}(V_{t}(s)-V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s))\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}e^{\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}}\sup_{s}\|B_{t}(s)\|\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}\right)\left(\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s)}\right).

(133)

Hence combine the bounds on
(a)
and
(b)
, we obtain a bound of the second term in (
C.2
):

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B(s^{\prime},\theta_{t})e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}-\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B_{t}(s^{\prime})e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}{\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}\right)}\right\|\leq 2\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}e^{\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}}\sup_{s}\|B_{t}(s)\|.

(134)

Note that
B_{t}(s^{\prime})=\frac{1}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}d^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}_{s^{\prime}}(s)\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\nabla\pi_{\theta_{t}}(a|s)Q_{t}(s,a)
, and
\|Q_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon_{\text{est}}+\|Q_{\sigma}^{\theta_{t}}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon_{\text{est}}+C_{\sigma}
, hence

\displaystyle\sup_{s}\|B_{t}(s)\|\leq\frac{|\mathcal{A}|(\epsilon_{\text{est}}+C_{\sigma})}{1-\gamma+\gamma R},

(135)

and thus,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B(s^{\prime},\theta_{t})e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}-\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B_{t}(s^{\prime})e^{\sigma V_{t}(s)}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}{\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{t}(s^{\prime})}\right)\left(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}\right)}\right\|\leq 2\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}e^{\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|(\epsilon_{\text{est}}+C_{\sigma})}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}.

(136)

Finally combining the bounds in (
C.2
) and (
136
) and plugging them in (
C.2
) implies that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B(s^{\prime},\theta_{t})e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}-D^{i}_{t}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}e^{\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|(\epsilon_{\text{est}}+C_{\sigma})}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}+\frac{|\mathcal{A}|\epsilon_{\text{est}}}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}.

(137)

Hence the bias of
g^{i}_{t}
can be bounded as follows

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}[g^{i}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]-\nabla J_{\sigma}(\theta_{t})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbb{E}[B^{i}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]-B(\rho,\theta_{t})\|+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}B(s^{\prime},\theta_{t})e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}e^{\sigma V_{\sigma}^{\pi_{\theta_{t}}}(s^{\prime})}}-D^{i}_{t}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}e^{\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}}\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|(\epsilon_{\text{est}}+C_{\sigma})}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|\epsilon_{\text{est}}}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}+\frac{|\mathcal{A}|\epsilon_{\text{est}}}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}

\displaystyle\triangleq b_{g}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{\text{est}}+\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}e^{\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}}).

(138)

∎

This theorem implies that the Algorithm
4
is actually a projected stochastic gradient descent with bias
b_{g}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{\text{est}}+\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}e^{\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}})
.

C.3
Proof of Theorem
6.1
: Global Convergence of Robust Actor-Critic under Tabular Setting

Denote
\Omega_{t}=g_{t}-\nabla J_{\sigma}(\theta_{t})
, and define the stochastic gradient map by
H_{t}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}(\theta_{t}-\mathbf{\prod}_{(\Delta(\mathcal{A})^{|\mathcal{S}|}}(\theta_{t}-\alpha_{t}g_{t}))
. Note that
J_{\sigma}
is
L_{\sigma}
-smooth, hence similar to the proof of Theorem 1 in
(
Ghadimi et al. 2016
)
, we can show that

\displaystyle J_{\sigma}(\theta_{t+1})

\displaystyle\leq J_{\sigma}(\theta_{t})-\left(\alpha_{t}-\frac{L_{\sigma}}{2}\alpha_{t}^{2}\right)\|H_{t}\|^{2}+\alpha_{t}\langle\Omega_{t},G_{t}\rangle+\alpha_{t}\|\Omega_{t}\|\|H_{t}-G_{t}\|

\displaystyle\leq J_{\sigma}(\theta_{t})-\left(\alpha_{t}-\frac{L_{\sigma}}{2}\alpha_{t}^{2}\right)\|H_{t}\|^{2}+\alpha_{t}\langle\Omega_{t},G_{t}\rangle+\alpha_{t}\|\Omega_{t}\|^{2},

(139)

where
G_{t}\triangleq\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}(\theta_{t}-\mathbf{\prod}_{(\Delta(\mathcal{A})^{|\mathcal{S}|}}(\theta_{t}-\alpha_{t}\nabla J_{\sigma}(\theta_{t})))
, and the last inequality is from Proposition 1 in
(
Ghadimi et al. 2016
)
. Summing up from
t=0
to
T-1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}(\alpha_{t}-L_{\sigma}\alpha_{t}^{2})\|H_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq J_{\sigma}(\theta_{0})-J_{\sigma}(\theta_{T+1})+\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}\left(\alpha_{t}\langle\Omega_{t},G_{t}\rangle+\alpha_{t}\|\Omega_{t}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq J_{\sigma}(\theta_{0})-J_{\sigma}^{*}+\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}\left(\alpha_{t}\langle\Omega_{t},G_{t}\rangle+\alpha_{t}\|\Omega_{t}\|^{2}\right).

(140)

Note that
G_{t}
is deterministic given
\theta_{t}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}_{t}
. Hence we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle\Omega_{t},G_{t}\rangle|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=\langle\mathbb{E}[\Omega_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}],G_{t}\rangle\leq\|G_{t}\|b_{g}.

(141)

Define
\Omega_{t}^{j}=g^{j}_{t}-\nabla J_{\sigma}(\theta_{t})
. Then we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\Omega_{t}\|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\frac{\sum^{M}_{j=1}\Omega_{t}^{j}}{M}\right\|^{2}\bigg|\mathcal{F_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i,j}\left\langle\frac{\Omega_{t}^{j}}{M},\frac{\Omega_{t}^{i}}{M}\right\rangle\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\sum^{M}_{i=1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\frac{\Omega_{t}^{i}}{M}\right\|^{2}+\sum_{i\neq j}\left\langle\frac{\Omega_{t}^{j}}{M},\frac{\Omega_{t}^{i}}{M}\right\rangle\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sup_{i}\mathbb{E}[\|\Omega^{i}_{t}\|^{2}]}{M}+\|\mathbb{E}[\Omega^{i}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sup_{i}\mathbb{E}[\|\Omega^{i}_{t}\|^{2}]}{M}+b_{g}^{2},

(142)

where
(a)
is from the fact that
\Omega_{t}^{i}
and
\Omega_{t}^{j}
are independent for
i\neq j
.

Note that for any
j
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\Omega^{j}_{t}\|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\|g^{j}_{t}-\nabla J_{\sigma}(\theta_{t})^{2}\|]\leq 2\left(\sup_{j}\|g^{j}_{t}\|^{2}+\sup_{\theta}\|\nabla J_{\sigma}(\theta)\|^{2}\right).

(143)

And we have shown that

\displaystyle\sup_{j}\|g^{j}_{t}\|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}+1\right)^{2}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma+\gamma R)^{2}}(\sup_{t}\|Q_{t}\|)^{2}\leq\left(\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}+1\right)^{2}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|^{2}}{(1-\gamma+\gamma R)^{2}}(C_{\sigma}+\epsilon_{\text{est}})^{2}\triangleq C^{2}_{g},

(144)

and hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\Omega^{j}_{t}\|^{2}]\leq 2\left(C_{g}^{2}+(C^{V}_{\sigma})^{2}\right),

(145)

where we use the fact
\|\nabla J_{\sigma}\|\leq C_{\sigma}^{V}
.

Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\Omega_{t}\|^{2}]\leq b_{g}^{2}+\frac{2\left(C_{g}^{2}+{C^{V}_{\sigma}}^{2}\right)}{M}\triangleq C_{\Omega}.

(146)

Thus plugging all the inequalities (
141
) and (
146
) in (
C.3
), we have that

\displaystyle\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}(\alpha_{t}-L_{\sigma}\alpha_{t}^{2})\mathbb{E}\left[\|H_{t}\|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\leq J_{\sigma}(\theta_{0})-J_{\sigma}^{*}+\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}\alpha_{t}\|G_{t}\|b_{g}+\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}\alpha_{t}C_{\Omega}.

(147)

Note that
\|G_{t}\|\leq\|\nabla J_{\sigma}(\theta_{t})\|\leq C^{V}_{\sigma}
, hence the last inequality becomes

\displaystyle\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}(\alpha_{t}-L_{\sigma}\alpha_{t}^{2})\mathbb{E}\left[\|H_{t}\|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\leq J_{\sigma}(\theta_{0})-J_{\sigma}^{*}+\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}\alpha_{t}C^{V}_{\sigma}b_{g}+\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}\alpha_{t}C_{\Omega}.

(148)

Set
\alpha_{t}=\frac{1}{2L_{\sigma}}
, it then follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4L_{\sigma}}\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}\mathbb{E}\left[\|H_{t}\|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\leq J_{\sigma}(\theta_{0})-J_{\sigma}^{*}+\frac{TC^{V}_{\sigma}b_{g}}{2L_{\sigma}}+\frac{T}{2L_{\sigma}}C_{\Omega},

(149)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|H_{U}\|^{2}]\leq\frac{4L_{\sigma}(J_{\sigma}(\theta_{0})-J_{\sigma}^{*})}{T}+2C^{V}_{\sigma}b_{g}+2C_{\Omega},

(150)

where
U\sim\text{Uniform}(0,...,T-1)
.
Similar to Corollary 3 in
(
Ghadimi et al. 2016
)
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|G_{U}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\|H_{U}\|^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[\|H_{U}-\nabla J_{\sigma}(\theta_{U})\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{E}\left[\|H_{U}\|^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Omega_{U}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8L_{\sigma}(J_{\sigma}(\theta_{0})-J_{\sigma}^{*})}{T}+4C^{V}_{\sigma}b_{g}+6C_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\triangleq\epsilon_{G}.

(151)

Note that
G_{t}
is fully determined by
\theta_{t}
, hence we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|G_{t}\|^{2}\right]=\sum_{\theta\in(\Delta(\mathcal{A}))^{|\mathcal{S}|}}\mathbb{P}(\theta_{t}=\theta)\|G(\theta)\|^{2},

(152)

where
G(\theta)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\theta-\mathbf{\prod}_{(\Delta(\mathcal{A})^{|\mathcal{S}|}}(\theta-\alpha\nabla J_{\sigma}(\theta))\right)
, and we denote
\theta^{+}=\theta-\alpha G(\theta)
.

If we denote
\|G(\theta)\|^{2}\triangleq\epsilon_{\theta}
, then
\int_{{\theta}\in(\Delta(\mathcal{A}))^{|\mathcal{S}|}}\epsilon_{\theta}d\mathbb{P}(\theta_{t}={\theta})=\mathbb{E}[\|G_{t}\|^{2}]
.
Following the proof of Theorem
5.5
, and because of
\|G({\theta})\|=\sqrt{\epsilon_{\theta}}
, we have

\displaystyle-\nabla J_{\sigma}({\theta}^{+})\in N_{(\Delta(\mathcal{A}))^{|\mathcal{S}|}}({\theta}^{+})+\sqrt{\epsilon_{\theta}}B_{2}.

(153)

From the PL-condition in Theorem
5.2
, we further have that

\displaystyle J_{\sigma}({\theta}^{+})-J_{\sigma}^{*}

\displaystyle\leq C_{PL}\max_{\hat{\pi}\in(\Delta(\mathcal{A}))^{|\mathcal{S}|}}\langle\pi_{{\theta}^{+}}-\hat{\pi},\nabla J_{\sigma}({\theta}^{+})\rangle+\left(\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\right)\frac{2\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{PL}\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|\epsilon_{\theta}}+\left(\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\right)\frac{2\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}.

(154)

Hence we have that for any
t
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[J_{\sigma}(\theta_{t}^{+})-J_{\sigma}^{*}]

\displaystyle=\int_{{\theta}\in\Theta}(J_{\sigma}({\theta}^{+})-J_{\sigma}^{*})d\mathbb{P}(\theta_{t}={\theta})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\theta}\in\Theta}2C_{PL}\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|\epsilon_{\theta}}\mathbb{P}(\theta_{t}={\theta})+\left(\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\right)\frac{2\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{PL}\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|}\sqrt{\int_{{\theta}\in\Theta}\epsilon_{\theta}d\mathbb{P}(\theta_{t}={\theta})}+\left(\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\right)\frac{2\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}

\displaystyle=2C_{PL}\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\|G_{t}\|^{2}]}+\left(\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\right)\frac{2\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}.

(155)

And further we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[J_{\sigma}(\theta_{U}^{+})-J_{\sigma}^{*}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}\mathbb{E}[J_{\sigma}(\theta_{t}^{+})-J_{\sigma}^{*}]}{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}2C_{PL}\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\|G_{t}\|^{2}]}}{T}+\left(\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\right)\frac{2\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{PL}\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|}\sqrt{\frac{\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}\mathbb{E}[\|G_{t}\|^{2}]}{T}}+\left(\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\right)\frac{2\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{PL}\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|\epsilon_{G}}+\left(\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\right)\frac{2\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}.

(156)

Now consider
J_{\sigma}(\theta_{U+1})
. Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[J_{\sigma}(\theta_{U+1})-J_{\sigma}(\theta_{U}^{+})]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[C^{V}_{\sigma}\|\theta_{U}^{+}-\theta_{U+1}\|]

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}C^{V}_{\sigma}\alpha\mathbb{E}[\|g_{U}-\nabla J_{\sigma}(\theta_{U})\|]

\displaystyle=C^{V}_{\sigma}\alpha\mathbb{E}[\|{\Omega}_{U}\|]

\displaystyle\leq C^{V}_{\sigma}\alpha\sqrt{C_{\Omega}},

(157)

where
(a)
is from Lemma 2 in
(
Ghadimi et al. 2016
)
, and the last inequality is from (
146
) and the fact that
\mathbb{E}[\|\Omega_{t}\|]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\|{\Omega}_{t}\|^{2}]}\leq\sqrt{C_{\Omega}}
for any
t
.

Hence we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[J_{\sigma}(\theta_{U+1})-J^{*}_{\sigma}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[J_{\sigma}(\theta_{U+1})-J_{\sigma}(\theta_{U}^{+})+J_{\sigma}(\theta_{U}^{+})-J^{*}_{\sigma}]

\displaystyle\leq C^{V}_{\sigma}\alpha\sqrt{C_{\Omega}}+2C_{PL}\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|\epsilon_{G}}+\left(\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\right)\frac{2\log|\mathcal{S}|}{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq 2C^{V}_{\sigma}\frac{\epsilon}{L_{\sigma}}+2C_{PL}\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|\epsilon_{G}}+\epsilon.

(158)

Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle|J_{\sigma}(\theta)-J(\theta)|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon,

\displaystyle|J^{*}_{\sigma}-J^{*}|\leq\epsilon,

(159)

hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[J(\theta_{U+1})-J^{*}]

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon+2C^{V}_{\sigma}\frac{\epsilon}{L_{\sigma}}+2C_{PL}\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|\epsilon_{G}}+\epsilon

\displaystyle=2C_{PL}\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|}\sqrt{\frac{8L_{\sigma}(J_{\sigma}(\theta_{0})-J_{\sigma}^{*})}{T}+4C^{V}_{\sigma}b_{g}+6C_{\Omega}}+3\epsilon+2C^{V}_{\sigma}\frac{\epsilon}{L_{\sigma}}.

(160)

Plug in the definition of
C_{\Omega}
, and we further have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[J(\theta_{U+1})-J^{*}]

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon+2C^{V}_{\sigma}\frac{\epsilon}{L_{\sigma}}+2C_{PL}\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|\epsilon_{G}}+\epsilon

\displaystyle=2C_{PL}\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|}\sqrt{\frac{8L_{\sigma}(J_{\sigma}(\theta_{0})-J_{\sigma}^{*})}{T}+4C^{V}_{\sigma}b_{g}+6\left(b_{g}^{2}+\frac{2\left(C_{g}^{2}+{C^{V}_{\sigma}}^{2}\right)}{M}\right)}+3\epsilon+2C^{V}_{\sigma}\frac{\epsilon}{L_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{PL}\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|}\left(\sqrt{\frac{16L_{\sigma}C_{\sigma}}{T}}+\sqrt{4C^{V}_{\sigma}b_{g}+6b_{g}^{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{12\left(C_{g}^{2}+{C^{V}_{\sigma}}^{2}\right)}{M}}\right)

(161)

Hence if we set

\displaystyle b_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2\sqrt{6|\mathcal{S}|}C_{PL}},

(162)

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\frac{48(C_{g}^{2}+{C^{V}_{\sigma}}^{2})|\mathcal{S}|C_{PL}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}},

(163)

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\frac{64L_{\sigma}C_{\sigma}|\mathcal{S}|C_{PL}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}},

(164)

we have that

\displaystyle\min_{1\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}[J(\theta_{t})]\leq\mathbb{E}[J(\theta_{U+1})-J^{*}]

\displaystyle\leq 6\epsilon+2C^{V}_{\sigma}\frac{\epsilon}{L_{\sigma}}\leq 7\epsilon.

(165)

Appendix D
Useful Lemmas

Lemma D.1
.

Let
F(x)=\max\left\{f_{1}(x),...,f_{n}(x)\right\}
, and for any
x
, denote
I(x)\triangleq\arg\max_{i}\left\{f_{i}(x)\right\}
. Then
\left\{\partial f_{i}(x):i\in I_{x}\right\}\subseteq\partial F(x)
.

Proof.

From the definition, we know that for any
i\in I_{x}
and
g\in\partial f_{i}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle\lim\inf_{y\to x}\frac{f_{i}(y)-f_{i}(x)-\langle g,y-x\rangle}{\|y-x\|}\geq 0.

(166)

It can be showed that
g\in\partial F(x)
as follows:

\displaystyle\lim\inf_{y\to x}\frac{F(y)-F(x)-\langle g,y-x\rangle}{\|y-x\|}\geq\lim\inf_{y\to x}\frac{f_{i}(y)-f_{i}(x)-\langle g,y-x\rangle}{\|y-x\|}\geq 0,

(167)

which is from
F(y)\geq f_{i}(y)
and
F(x)=f_{i}(x)
. And this completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle=2\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}e^{\sigma\epsilon_{\text{est}}}\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|(\epsilon_{\text{est}}+C_{\sigma})}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}+\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|\epsilon_{\text{est}}}{1-\gamma+\gamma R}+\frac{|\mathcal{A}|\epsilon_{\text{est}}}{1-\gamma+\gamma R},

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\gamma R}{1-\gamma}+1\right)\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{(1-\gamma+\gamma R)}(C_{\sigma}+\epsilon_{\text{est}}),

\displaystyle=b_{g}^{2}+\frac{2\left(C_{g}^{2}+{C^{V}_{\sigma}}^{2}\right)}{M},

\displaystyle=\frac{8L_{\sigma}(J_{\sigma}(\theta_{0})-J_{\sigma}^{*})}{T}+4C^{V}_{\sigma}b_{g}+6C_{\Omega}.

(168)

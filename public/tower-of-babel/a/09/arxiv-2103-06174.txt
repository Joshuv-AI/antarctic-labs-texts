Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is two-fold: we present some matrix inequalities of log-majorization type for eigenvalues indexed by a sequence; we then apply our main theorem to generalize and improve the Hua-Marcus’ inequalities. Our results are stronger and more general than the existing ones.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
be the space of
m\times n
complex matrices.
For
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, we denote the eigenvalues
of
A
by
\lambda_{1}(A),\lambda_{2}(A),\dots,\lambda_{n}(A)

and the singular values of
A
by

\sigma_{1}(A),\sigma_{2}(A),\dots,\sigma_{n}(A)
. If
\lambda_{1}(A),\lambda_{2}(A),\dots,\lambda_{n}(A)

are all real, we arrange them in decreasing order:

\lambda_{1}(A)\geq\lambda_{2}(A)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(A)
. The singular values are always
ordered decreasingly:
\sigma_{1}(A)\geq\sigma_{2}(A)\geq\dots\geq\sigma_{n}(A)
.

Horn conjecture …

There is a large family of matrix inequalities concerning the eigenvalues and singular values of the product (including Schur or Hadamard product)
and sum of matrices. These inequalities may be sorted in four types:

\sum\zeta_{{}_{\mathcal{I}}}(A+B),\,\prod\zeta_{{}_{\mathcal{I}}}(A+B),\,\sum\zeta_{{}_{\mathcal{I}}}(AB),\,\prod\zeta_{{}_{\mathcal{I}}}(AB)
, where

A
and
B
are generic matrices, and
\zeta_{{}_{\mathcal{I}}}(\cdot)
represents selected
eigenvalues or singular values indexed by a sequence
{\mathcal{I}}
. The inequalities in sum
\sum
are usually called majorization type (
[
7
, p. 45]
,

[
13
]
), while the ones in product
\prod
are referred to as logarithmic (log-)
majorization type.
See
[
13
, p. 16]
,
[
3
,
9
]
).
For example, the following inequalities
[
6
]

(or
[
13
, G.2.a, p. 333]
)
are log-majorization type with
\mathcal{I}=\{k,\dots,n\}
: for each
k=1,\dots,n
,

\prod\limits_{t=k}^{n}\lambda_{t}(A+B)\geq\prod\limits_{t=k}^{n}[\lambda_{t}(A)+\lambda_{t}(B)]\geq\prod\limits_{t=k}^{n}\lambda_{t}(A)+\prod\limits_{t=k}^{n}\lambda_{t}(B),

(1.1)

where
A
and
B
are
n\times n
positive semidefinite matrices.

Closely related inequalities of the same type, due to Oppenheim
[
14
]
(or
[
13
, F.2, p. 685]
), are, for
n\times n
positive semidefinite
A
and
B
, and for each
k=1,\dots,n,

\displaystyle\left[\prod\limits_{t=k}^{n}\lambda_{t}(A+B)\right]^{\frac{1}{n-k+1}}\geq

\displaystyle\left[\prod\limits_{t=k}^{n}\lambda_{t}(A)\right]^{\frac{1}{n-k+1}}+\left[\prod\limits_{t=k}^{n}\lambda_{t}(B)\right]^{\frac{1}{n-k+1}},

(1.2)

which give the Minkowski inequality by setting
k=1

(
[
13
, p. 685]
or
[
23
, p. 215]
)

\displaystyle\left[\det(A+B)\right]^{\frac{1}{n}}\geq\left[\det(A)\right]^{\frac{1}{n}}+\left[\det(B)\right]^{\frac{1}{n}}.

(1.3)

In
[
20
]
, we showed majorization inequalities of
\sum\zeta_{{}_{\mathcal{I}}}(AB)
for Hermitian matrices
A
and
B
with an arbitrary index set
\mathcal{I}
. In this paper, we present
some log-majorization inequalities of
\prod\zeta_{{}_{\mathcal{I}}}(A+B)
with any index set

\mathcal{I}
for positive semidefinite matrices
A
and
B
, extending (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).
Our theorems are more general and stronger than some existing results. We apply
main results to improve the Hua-Marcus inequalities for contractive matrices.

2.
Some lemmas

Let
X^{*}
denote the conjugate transpose of matrix or vector
X
.
For a square matrix
A
, we write
A\geq 0
if
A
is
positive semidefinite and
A>0
if
A
is positive definite. For Hermitian matrices
A,B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
,
we write
A\geq B
if
A-B\geq 0
.

Through the paper,
let
n
be a positive integer. Let
\mathcal{I}=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
, where

1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n
is any subsequence of
1,\dots,n
,

k=1,\dots,n
. If
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is positive semidefinite, then

\lambda_{i_{1}}(A)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{i_{k}}(A)
are
k
eigenvalues of
A

indexed by
\mathcal{I}=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
. For
t=1,\dots,k
,
setting
i_{t}=t
gives
the first
k
largest eigenvalues of
A
; putting
i_{t}=n-k+t
gives
the last
k
smallest eigenvalues of
A
.

Lemma 2.1

(Hoffman
[
1
, Cor. 2.5]
)
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be positive semidefinite. Then

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{\mathbb{S}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathbb{S}_{k}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\hfill\atop\dim\mathbb{S}_{t}=i_{t}}\min\limits_{{x}_{t}\in\mathbb{S}_{t},({x}_{r},{x}_{s})=\delta_{rs}\hfill\atop U_{k}=\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\right)}\det({U_{k}^{*}AU_{k}}),

where
\delta_{rs}
is the Kronecker delta, i.e.,
\delta_{rs}=1
if
r=s
, or 0 otherwise.

Lemma 2.2

(Lidskiǐ
[
11
]
)
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be positive semidefinite. Then

\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}(A)\lambda_{n-t+1}(B)\leq\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}(AB)\leq\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}(A)\lambda_{t}(B).

(2.1)

More general inequalities of (
2.1
) for singular values are
due to Gel’fand and Naimark (see, e.g.,
[
13
, p. 340]
). The inequalities
on the left-hand side of (
2.1
)
can be found explicitly in
[
17
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
and
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
be nonnegative real numbers.
Then

\prod\limits_{t=1}^{n}(a_{t}+b_{t})\geq\prod\limits_{t=1}^{n}a_{t}+\prod\limits_{t=1}^{n}b_{t}+(2^{n}-2)\left[\prod\limits_{t=1}^{n}(a_{t}b_{t})\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.2)

In particular, for

x,y\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\big(x^{\frac{1}{n}}+y^{\frac{1}{n}}\big)^{n}\geq x+y+\left(2^{n}-2\right)(xy)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.3)

Proof.

Use induction on
n
and apply the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality.
∎

We remark that Lemma
2.3
implies immediately
a result of Hartfiel
[
8
]
:

\det(A+B)\geq\det A+\det B+(2^{n}-2)[\det A\det B]^{\frac{1}{2}}

(2.4)

because of the fact that any two positive semidefinite matrices
of the same size are simultaneously
∗
-congruent to diagonal matrices (see, e.g.,
[
23
, p. 209]
).

3.
Main Results

We begin with a result that is important to the proof of our main theorem.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

D={\rm{diag}}\big(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n}\big)
, where

\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq 0
, and let

U_{k}=\left(u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\right)=\left(u_{ij}\right)
be a partial isometry,
i.e.,
U_{k}
is an
n\times k

matrix such that
U^{*}_{k}U_{k}=I_{k}
. If
m\geq 1
and
u_{i1}=0
for all
i>m
, then

\displaystyle\det(U^{*}_{k}DU_{k})\geq\lambda_{m}\det\left[\left(u_{2},\dots,u_{k}\right)^{*}D_{m}\left(u_{2},\dots,u_{k}\right)\right],

(3.1)

where
D_{m}={\rm{diag}}\big(\underbrace{\lambda_{m},\dots,\lambda_{m}}_{m},\lambda_{m+1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\big).

Proof.

Let
V=\left(u_{2},\dots,u_{k}\right)
. Since
D\geq D_{m}

and
u_{i1}=0
for
i>m
, we have

\displaystyle\det(U_{k}^{*}DU_{k})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\det(U_{k}^{*}D_{m}U_{k})=\det\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{m}u^{*}_{1}u_{1}&\lambda_{m}u^{*}_{1}V\\
\lambda_{m}V^{*}u_{1}&V^{*}D_{m}V\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{m}&0\\
0&V^{*}D_{m}V\end{pmatrix}=\lambda_{m}\det\left(V^{*}D_{m}V\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{m}\det\left[\left(u_{2},\dots,u_{k}\right)^{*}D_{m}\left(u_{2},\dots,u_{k}\right)\right].

∎

We are ready to present our main result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be
n\times n
positive semidefinite matrices. Then

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}^{\frac{1}{k}}(A+B)\geq\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}^{\frac{1}{k}}(A)+\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{n-t+1}^{\frac{1}{k}}(B)

(3.2)

and

\displaystyle{\small\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}(A+B)\geq\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}(A)+\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{n-t+1}(B)+(2^{k}-2)\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\left[\lambda_{i_{t}}(A)\lambda_{n-t+1}(B)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\!.\;}

(3.3)

Proof.

If
n=1
, the inequalities become equalities and
hold trivially. Let
n\geq 2
. By spectral decomposition,
there exists a unitary matrix

U=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, where

u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{n}
are orthonormal eigenvectors associated with

\lambda_{1}(A),\lambda_{2}(A),\dots,

\lambda_{n}(A)
, respectively, such that

A=U\,{\rm{diag}}(\lambda_{1}(A),\lambda_{2}(A),\dots,\lambda_{n}(A))U^{*}.

For each
i_{t}
in the sequence
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}
,
let

\mathbb{S}^{(0)}_{t}={\rm{Span}}(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{i_{t}}),

t=1,2,\dots,k.

Then

\mathbb{S}^{(0)}_{1}\subset\mathbb{S}^{(0)}_{2}\subset\cdots\subset\mathbb{S}^{(0)}_{k}

and
\dim\big(\mathbb{S}^{(0)}_{t}\big)={i_{t}},

t=1,2,\dots,k.

Let
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
be any set of orthonormal vectors,
where
x_{t}\in\mathbb{S}^{(0)}_{t},

t=1,2,\dots,k.
Let

x_{t}=a_{1t}u_{1}+\cdots+a_{i_{t}t}u_{i_{t}}+0u_{i_{t}+1}+\dots+0u_{i_{k}}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{i_{k}})\alpha_{t}
,
where
\alpha_{t}=(a_{1t},\dots,a_{i_{t}t},0,\dots,0)^{\tiny T}

(here
T
is for transpose),

t=1,2,\dots,k
. Then

U_{k}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=(u_{1},\dots,u_{i_{k}})(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}).

Let
V_{k}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})\in\mathbb{C}^{i_{k}\times k}
. Since

\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
and
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{i_{k}}\}
are orthonormal sets,
we see that

V_{k}^{*}V_{k}=I_{k}
. So
V_{k}
is a partial isometry, and
the components of the first column of
V_{k}

are zeros except the first
i_{1}
components (i.e.,
the last
i_{k}-i_{1}
components of
\alpha_{1}
are all equal to zero).

In Lemma
3.1
, setting
n=i_{k}
,
m=i_{1}
,
U_{k}=V_{k}
, and applying (
3.1
) to the
i_{k}\times i_{k}
matrix

D_{i_{1}}=\mathop{\rm diag}(\underbrace{\lambda_{i_{1}}(A),\dots,\lambda_{i_{1}}(A)}_{i_{1}}\,,\lambda_{{i_{1}}+1}(A),\dots,\lambda_{i_{k}}(A)),

we obtain

\displaystyle\det(U^{*}_{k}AU_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\big[(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})^{*}(u_{1},\dots,u_{i_{k}})^{*}A(u_{1},\dots,u_{i_{k}})(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\big[(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})^{*}\mathop{\rm diag}(\lambda_{1}(A),\dots,\lambda_{i_{k}}(A))(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\big[V_{k}^{*}\mathop{\rm diag}(\lambda_{1}(A),\dots,\lambda_{i_{k}}(A))V_{k}\big]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda_{i_{1}}(A)\det\big[(\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{k})^{*}D_{i_{1}}(\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{k})\big].

Repeatedly using Lemma
3.1
, we get

\det(U_{k}^{*}AU_{k})\geq\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}(A).

(3.4)

A minimax eigenvalue result of Fan (see
[
4
,
5
]

or
[
13
, A.3.a, p. 787]
) ensures

\displaystyle\det(U_{k}^{*}BU_{k})\geq\min_{U^{*}U=I_{k}}\det(U^{*}BU)=\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{n-t+1}(B).

An application of the Minkowski inequality (
1.3
) together with (
3.4
) reveals

\displaystyle\det\big[{U_{k}^{*}(A+B)U_{k}}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det({U_{k}^{*}AU_{k}+U_{k}^{*}BU_{k}})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left[\big(\det({U_{k}^{*}AU_{k}})\big)^{\frac{1}{k}}+\big(\det({U_{k}^{*}BU_{k}})\big)^{\frac{1}{k}}\right]^{k}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left[\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda^{\frac{1}{k}}_{i_{t}}(A)+\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda^{\frac{1}{k}}_{n-t+1}(B)\right]^{k}.

Thus, by Lemma
2.1
, we derive (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) as follows:

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}(A+B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{\mathbb{S}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathbb{S}_{k}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\hfill\atop\dim\mathbb{S}_{t}=i_{t}}\min\limits_{{y}_{t}\in\mathbb{S}_{t},\,({y}_{r},{y}_{s})=\delta_{rs}\hfill\atop W_{k}=\left(y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\right)}\det\big[{W_{k}^{*}(A+B)W_{k}}\big]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\limits_{{x}_{t}\in\mathbb{S}^{(0)}_{t}\,({x}_{r},{x}_{s})=\delta_{rs}\hfill\atop U_{k}=\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\right)}\det\big[{U_{k}^{*}(A+B)U_{k}}\big]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left[\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda^{\frac{1}{k}}_{i_{t}}(A)+\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda^{\frac{1}{k}}_{n-t+1}(B)\right]^{k}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}(A)+\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{n-t+1}(B)

\displaystyle+(2^{k}-2)\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\left[\lambda_{i_{t}}(A)\lambda_{n-t+1}(B)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The last inequality is by (
2.3
)
with
x=\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}(A)
and
y=\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{n-t+1}(B)
.
∎

In (
3.2
), letting
i_{t}=n-k+t
,
t=1,2,\ldots,k
, we arrive at the inequalities
(
1.2
) of Oppenhiem for the product of
k
smallest eigenvalues. Setting
i_{t}=t
,
t=1,2,\ldots,k
, we obtain analogous

for the product of
k
largest eigenvalues.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be
n\times n
positive semidefinite matrices. Then

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{t}^{\frac{1}{k}}(A+B)\geq\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{t}^{\frac{1}{k}}(A)+\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{n-t+1}^{\frac{1}{k}}(B).

(3.5)

What follows is a lower bound for the
product of any two eigenvalues of the sum in terms of the eigenvalues of individual matrices.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be
n\times n
positive semidefinite matrices. Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(A+B)\lambda_{j}(A+B)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(A)\lambda_{j}(A)+\lambda_{n-1}(B)\lambda_{n}(B)

\displaystyle+2\big[\lambda_{i}(A)\lambda_{j}(A)\lambda_{n-1}(B)\lambda_{n}(B)\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Remark: In view of Fiedler’s (
1.1
) and inequalities
(
3.3
), it is tempting to have

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}(A+B)\geq\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\big[\lambda_{i_{t}}(A)+\lambda_{n-t+1}(B)\big].

(3.6)

However, this need not be true. Take

,

. Then
the left-hand side of (

) is 0, while the

. See, e.g.,

[

, p. 274]
).

The following inequality in (

) is Fiedler’s 1st inequality
in (

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be positive semidefinite matrices. Then

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=n-k+1}^{n}\lambda_{t}(A+B)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=n-k+1}^{n}\big[\lambda_{t}(A)+\lambda_{t}(B)\big]

(3.7)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=n-k+1}^{n}\lambda_{t}(A)+\prod\limits_{t=n-k+1}^{n}\lambda_{t}(B)

(3.8)

\displaystyle+\left(2^{k}-2\right)\prod\limits_{t=n-k+1}^{n}\left[\lambda_{t}(A)\lambda_{t}(B)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{t}(A+B)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{t}(A)+\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{n-t+1}(B)

Remark: In view of (

), it is appealing in the display (

\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{t}(A+B)\;\;\geq\;\;\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\big[\lambda_{t}(A)+\lambda_{t}(B)\big]\;\;\mbox{or}\;\;\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\big[\lambda_{t}(A)+\lambda_{n-t+1}(B)\big].

,

4.
Hua-Marcus inequalities for contractions

A matrix
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
is said to be (strictly)
contractive
if
I_{n}-A^{*}A\geq 0
(
>0
), equivalently, the largest singular value (i.e., the spectral norm)
of
A
is less than or equal to (resp.
<
)
1
. We use

\mathcal{C}_{m\times n}
to denote the set of
m\times n
contractive matrices and

\mathcal{SC}_{m\times n}
to denote the
set of
m\times n
strictly
contractive matrices.

In this section we apply our main theorem to derive some inequalities of Hua-Marcus type
for contractive matrices.
We begin by citing Hua’s results in
[
10
]
.

Theorem 4.1

(Hua
[
10
, Theorems 1 and 2]
)
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{SC}_{n\times n}
. Then

\displaystyle\det(I-A^{*}A)\det(I-B^{*}B)+\left|\det(A-B)\right|^{2}\leq\left|\det(I-A^{*}B)\right|^{2}.

(4.1)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\det(I-A^{*}A)\det(I-B^{*}B)\leq\left|\det(I-A^{*}B)\right|^{2}.

(4.2)

Equality in (
4.1
) holds if and only if
A=B
.

The following
[
23
, Theorem 7.18]
is a reversal of the Hua inequality
4.1

for general
n\times n

matrices
A
and
B
that need not be contractive:

\displaystyle\left|\det(I-A^{*}B)\right|^{2}\leq\det(I+A^{*}A)\det(I+B^{*}B)-\left|\det(A+B)\right|^{2}.

(4.3)

Marcus
[
12
]
extended the Hua
inequality (
4.2
) to the inequalities of eigenvalues.

Theorem 4.2

(Marcus
[
12
, Theorem]
)
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{C}_{n\times n}
. Then

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}|\lambda_{n-t+1}(I-A^{*}B)|^{2}\geq\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\big[1-\lambda_{t}(A^{*}A)\big]\big[1-\lambda_{t}(B^{*}B)\big].

(4.4)

Since
|\det(I-A^{*}B)|=\prod\limits_{t=1}^{n}|\lambda_{n-t+1}(I-A^{*}B)|=\prod\limits_{t=1}^{n}\sigma_{n-t+1}(I-A^{*}B)
,
by Weyl’s log-majorization inequality (
[
18
]
or
[
13
, p. 317]
), the
eigenvalues in absolute values are log-majorized by the singular values, we get
the stronger inequality than (
4.4
):

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\sigma^{2}_{n-t+1}(I-A^{*}B)\geq\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\big[1-\lambda_{t}(A^{*}A)\big]\big[1-\lambda_{t}(B^{*}B)\big].

(4.5)

Related inequalities of Hua-Marcus type are seen in

[
2
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
21
,
22
]
.

The following result is stronger and more general than

4.5
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{SC}_{n\times n}
. Then

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\sigma^{2}_{i_{t}}\left(I-A^{*}B\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\big[1-\lambda_{t}(A^{*}A)\big]\big[1-\lambda_{n-i_{t}+1}(B^{*}B)\big]

(4.7)

\displaystyle+\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\frac{\big[1-\lambda_{t}(A^{*}A)\big]\,\sigma^{2}_{n-t+1}(A-B)}{1-\lambda_{n-t+1}(A^{*}A)}

\displaystyle+(2^{k}-2)\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\big[1-\lambda_{t}(A^{*}A)\big]\big[\lambda_{i_{t}}(F)\lambda_{n-t+1}(H)\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\!,\;

where
F=I-B^{*}B
and
H=(A-B)^{*}(I-AA^{*})^{-1}(A-B).

Proof.

For
A,B\in\mathcal{SC}_{n\times n}
,
let

F=I-B^{*}B\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;H=(A-B)^{*}(I-AA^{*})^{-1}(A-B).

Then (see
[
10
, Theorem 1]
or
[
23
, pp. 230-231]
)

F+H=(I-B^{*}A)(I-A^{*}A)^{-1}(I-A^{*}B).

(4.8)

Applying the left inequality of (
2.1
) with
i_{t}=n-t+1
to
H
reveals

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{n-t+1}(H)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{n-t+1}\big[(I-AA^{*})^{-1}(A-B)(A-B)^{*}\big]

(4.9)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{n-t+1}\big[(I-AA^{*})^{-1}\big]\sigma^{2}_{n-t+1}\big(A-B\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}[1-\lambda_{n-t+1}(A^{*}A)]^{-1}\sigma^{2}_{n-t+1}\big(A-B\big).

Applying the right inequality of
(
2.1
) to
F+H
in the product form in (
4.8
) yields

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}\left(F+H\right)\leq\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\left[1-\lambda_{t}(A^{*}A)\right]^{-1}\sigma^{2}_{i_{t}}\left(I-A^{*}B\right).

(4.10)

Thus

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\sigma^{2}_{i_{t}}\left(I-A^{*}B\right)\geq\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\left[1-\lambda_{t}(A^{*}A)\right]\lambda_{i_{t}}\left(F+H\right).

(4.11)

By inequalities (
3.3
) and (
4.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}\left(F+H\right)

(4.12)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}(F)+\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{n-t+1}(H)+(2^{k}-2)\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\left[\lambda_{i_{t}}(F)\lambda_{n-t+1}(H)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\left[1-\lambda_{n-i_{t}+1}(B^{*}B)\right]+\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\left[1-\lambda_{n-t+1}(A^{*}A)\right]^{-1}\sigma^{2}_{n-t+1}(A-B)

\displaystyle+(2^{k}-2)\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\left[\lambda_{i_{t}}(F)\lambda_{n-t+1}(H)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Combining (
4.11
) and (
4.12
), we obtain the desired inequalities.
∎

Setting
k=n
in Theorem
4.3
gives a stronger version of
the Hua’s inequality.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{SC}_{n\times n}
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\det\left(I-A^{*}B\right)\right|^{2}\geq\det\left(I-A^{*}A\right)\det\left(I-B^{*}B\right)+\left|\det\left(A-B\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+(2^{n}-2)\big[\det\left(I-A^{*}A\right)\det\left(I-B^{*}B\right)\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\det\left(A-B\right)\right|.

Below is a reversal inequality of the previous theorem.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
. Then

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\sigma^{2}_{i_{t}}\big(I-A^{*}B\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\big[1+\lambda_{i_{t}}(A^{*}A)\big]\big[1+\lambda_{t}(B^{*}B)\big]-\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\sigma^{2}_{n-t+1}\big(A+B\big)

(4.13)

\displaystyle-(2^{k}-2)\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\sigma_{i_{t}}\big(I-A^{*}B\big)\sigma_{n-t+1}\big(A+B\big).

Proof.

For
A,B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, the following matrix identity holds (see, e.g.,
[
23
, pp. 228]
):

I+A^{*}A=P+Q

where

P=(A+B)^{*}(I+BB^{*})^{-1}(A+B),

Q=(I-A^{*}B)(I+B^{*}B)^{-1}(I-A^{*}B)^{*}.

By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{n-t+1}(P)

\displaystyle\geq\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\big[1+\lambda_{t}(BB^{*})\big]^{-1}\sigma^{2}_{n-t+1}\big(A+B\big),

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}(Q)

\displaystyle\geq\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\big[1+\lambda_{t}(B^{*}B)\big]^{-1}\sigma^{2}_{i_{t}}\big(I-A^{*}B\big).

(4.14)

Using (
3.3
) in Theorem
3.1
, we derive

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\big[1+\lambda_{i_{t}}(A^{*}A)\big]=\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}(P+Q))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{n-t+1}(P)+\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{t}}(Q)+(2^{k}-2)\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}[\lambda_{n-t+1}(P)\lambda_{i_{t}}(Q)]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\big[1+\lambda_{t}(B^{*}B)\big]^{-1}\bigg[\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\sigma^{2}_{i_{t}}\big(I-A^{*}B\big)+\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\sigma^{2}_{n-t+1}\big(A+B\big)

\displaystyle+(2^{k}-2)\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\sigma_{i_{t}}\big(I-A^{*}B\big)\sigma_{n-t+1}\big(A+B\big)\bigg].

Multiplying by
\prod\limits_{t=1}^{k}\big[1+\lambda_{t}(B^{*}B)\big]
,
we obtain the desired inequalities (
4.13
).
∎

As (
4.3
) is a reversal of Hua’s (
4.1
), the following result, as a special case of the Theorem
4.4
by setting
k=n
,
may be viewed as a counterpart of Corollary

.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
. Then

\displaystyle\big|\det\left(I-A^{*}B\right)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\det\big(I+A^{*}A\big)\det\left(I+B^{*}B\right)-\big|\det\left(A+B\right)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle-(2^{n}-2)\big|\det\left(I-A^{*}B\right)\big|\big|\det\left(A+B\right)\big|.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\big|\det\left(I-A^{*}B\right)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\det\left(I+A^{*}A\right)\det\left(I+B^{*}B\right)-\big|\det\left(A+B\right)\big|^{2}.

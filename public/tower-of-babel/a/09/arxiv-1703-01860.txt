Abstract
. The parameterized model-checking problem for a class of first-order sentences (queries) asks to decide whether a given sentence from the class holds true in a given relational structure (database); the parameter is the length of the sentence. We study the parameterized space complexity of the model-checking problem for queries with a bounded number of variables. For each bound on the quantifier alternation rank the problem becomes complete for the corresponding level of what we call the tree hierarchy, a hierarchy of parameterized complexity classes defined via space bounded alternating machines between parameterized logarithmic space and fixed-parameter tractable time. We observe that a parameterized logarithmic space model-checker for existential bounded variable queries would allow to improve Savitch’s classical simulation of nondeterministic logarithmic space in deterministic space O ⁡ ( log 2 ⁡ n ) O(\log^{2}n) . Further, we define a highly space efficient model-checker for queries with a bounded number of variables and bounded quantifier alternation rank. We study its optimality under the assumption that Savitch’s Theorem is optimal.

\|\varphi\|\geq c\cdot w+c.

(18)

If this is not the case, then
\mathbb{A}
uses “brute force”, that is,
it runs the algorithm from Lemma
26
.

Now suppose (
18
) holds. Choosing
c\geq 3
this implies that the
syntax tree of
\varphi
has at least 3 nodes. Then
\mathbb{A}
computes in space

O(\log|\varphi|)
a subformula
\varphi_{0}
of
\varphi
such that

\|\varphi\|/3\leq\|\varphi_{0}\|\leq 2\|\varphi\|/3.

(19)

Observe that
\varphi_{0}
is a
\Sigma_{1}
-formula. Let
\bar{y}=y_{1}\cdots y_{|\bar{y}|}
list the free variables of
\varphi_{0}
and note

|\bar{y}|\leq w(\varphi_{0})\leq w(\varphi)\leq w.

(20)

Recall from the intuitive sketch of the proof that we intend to call
\mathbb{A}

recursively on a “rest” formula
\varphi_{1}^{\bar{b}}
where
\bar{b}
is a

|\bar{y}|
-tuple from
A
. To define this formula, let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{\max\{|\bar{y}|,1\}}
be new constant symbols. For every free variable

y_{i}
of
\varphi_{0}
check whether it has an occurrence in
\varphi_{0}
which
is not a free occurrence in
\varphi
. If such an occurrence exists, all the
free occurrences of
y_{i}
in
\varphi_{0}
appear within a uniquely determined
subformula
\exists y_{i}\chi
of
\varphi
containing
\varphi_{0}
as a
subformula, where
\exists y_{i}
binds these occurrences, that is, the free
occurrences of
y_{i}
in
\varphi_{0}
are also free in
\chi
. Replace in

\varphi
the subformula
\exists y_{i}\chi
by
\exists y_{i}(y_{i}{=}c_{i}\wedge\chi)
. Let
\varphi_{1}
denote the resulting formula. Note that
\varphi_{1}

does not depend on the order of how these replacements for the
y_{i}
are
performed.

Moreover, using (
20
),

\big\|\varphi_{1}\big\|\leq\|\varphi\|+2|\bar{y}|\leq\|\varphi\|+2w.

(21)

The algorithm
\mathbb{A}
then loops through
\bar{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{|\bar{y}|})\in A^{|\bar{y}|}

and does two recursive calls:

(R0)

Recursively call
\mathbb{A}(\varphi_{0},w,\mathbf{A},\bar{b})
to
check whether
\mathbf{A}\models\varphi_{0}(\bar{b})
. If
\mathbf{A}\models\varphi_{0}(\bar{b})
, then replace the subformula
\varphi_{0}
in
\varphi_{1}

by
c_{1}{=}c_{1}
; otherwise by
\neg c_{1}{=}c_{1}
.

Let
\varphi^{\bar{b}}_{1}
be the resulting formula. Further, let
\mathbf{A}^{\bar{b}}
be the expansion of
\mathbf{A}
that interprets the constants

c_{1},\ldots,c_{|\bar{y}|}
by
b_{1},\ldots,b_{|\bar{y}|}
respectively.

(R1)

Recursively call
\mathbb{A}\big(\varphi^{\bar{b}}_{1},w,\mathbf{A}^{\bar{b}},\bar{a}\big)
and output its answer.

Note that in (R1) we have
w(\varphi_{1}^{\bar{b}})\leq w(\varphi)\leq w
, so the
algorithm recurses to an instance of our problem. It is routine to verify
that

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\models\varphi(\bar{a})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\text{there exists $\bar{b}\in A^{|\bar{y}|}$ such that $\mathbf{A}^{\bar{b}}\models\varphi_{1}^{\bar{b}}(\bar{a})$}.

Thus,
\mathbb{A}
correctly decides whether
\mathbf{A}\models\varphi(\bar{a})
. It
remains to show that
\mathbb{A}
can be implemented in the allowed space.

We first estimate the depth of the recursion. In (R0) the algorithm recurses
to formula
\varphi_{0}
and
\|\varphi_{0}\|\leq 2\|\varphi\|/3
by (
19
). In (R1) the algorithm recurses on
\varphi_{1}^{\bar{b}}
, a
formula obtained from
\varphi_{1}
by replacing the subformula
\varphi_{0}
by
an atomic formula or the negation of an atomic formula. Then

\|\varphi_{1}^{\bar{b}}\|\leq\|\varphi\|+2w-\|\varphi_{0}\|+2\leq 2\|\varphi\|/3+2w+2=3\|\varphi\|/4+(2w+2-\|\varphi\|/12)

where the inequalities hold by (
21
) and (
19
),
respectively. Provided
c
in (
18
) is large enough, this implies

\|\varphi_{1}^{\bar{b}}\|\leq 3\|\varphi\|/4
. It follows that the recursion
depth is
O(\log\|\varphi\|)
.

In each recursive call the structure the algorithm recurses to is determined
by a tuple
\bar{b}
from the loop. This tuple has length at most
w

(cf. (
20
)). The formula
\varphi_{1}^{\bar{b}}
in (R1) is determined
by the truth value of
\mathbf{A}\models\varphi_{0}(\bar{b})
. The algorithm
recurses either

(P0)

to the formula
\varphi_{0}
, or

(P1)

to the formula
\varphi_{1}^{\bar{b}}
as defined if
\mathbf{A}\models\varphi_{0}(\bar{b})
, or

(P2)

to the formula
\varphi_{1}^{\bar{b}}
as defined if
\mathbf{A}\not\models\varphi_{0}(\bar{b})
.

To implement the recursion,
\mathbb{A}
maintains a sequence of tuples
\bar{b}_{1},\ldots,\bar{b}_{d}
and a sequence of “possibilities”
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})\in{\{0,1,2\}}^{d}
. The length
d
is bounded by
O(\log\|\varphi\|)
, the
depth of the recursion. In a recursive call as described above the sequence
of tuples is expanded by the tuple
\bar{b}
from the loop, and the
possibility sequence by
0,1,2
depending on which recursion from (P0),
(P1), (P2) is taken. Both sequences can be stored in allowed space, namely

O(d\cdot w\cdot\log|A|)\leq O(\log\|\varphi\|\cdot w\cdot\log|A|).

We still have to explain how, given
(\bar{b}_{1},\ldots,\bar{b}_{d})
and

(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})
, the algorithm determines the corresponding formula

\psi
and structure
\mathbf{B}
it has to recurse to. The structure
\mathbf{B}
is
an expansion of the input structure
\mathbf{A}
by a sequence of constants
interpreted by the sequence
\bar{b}_{i_{1}}\cdots\bar{b}_{i_{\ell}}
where

i_{1}<\cdots<i_{\ell}
list those indices
j\in[d]
such that
p_{j}\neq 0
.
This structure can be computed from the two sequences and the input in
logarithmic space. Note that
\mathbf{B}
is an expansion of
\mathbf{A}
by
interpreting at most
w\cdot d
many constants, so

\log|\mathbf{B}|\leq\log(|\mathbf{A}|+2\cdot w\cdot d)\leq\log|\mathbf{A}|+\log w+\log\log\|\varphi\|+O(1).

(22)

To determine the formula
\psi
, consider the function
R
that maps a
formula
\chi
and a number
i\leq 2
to the formula according to (P
i
). Since
we defined the recursion possibilities (P
i
) only on formulas
satisfying (
18
), let us agree that
R(\chi,i):=\chi
on formulas
violating (
18
).
The desired formula
\psi
is obtained by iterating this function along

(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})
: compute
\psi_{1}:=R(\varphi,p_{1})
,
\psi_{2}:=R(\psi_{1},p_{2})
, … and output
\psi:=\psi_{d}
. Note that all these
formulas have length
O(|\varphi|)
, and each iteration step is computable in
space
O(\log|\varphi|)
. Hence, the whole iteration can be implemented in
space

O(d\cdot\log|\varphi|)\leq O(\log\|\varphi\|\cdot\log|\varphi|).

If
(\psi,w,\mathbf{B},\bar{b})
is such that
\|\psi\|
violates (
18
),
i. e.
\|\psi\|<c\cdot w+c
, then
\mathbb{A}
invokes the “brute force” algorithm
from Lemma
26
. This requires space

O\big(w\cdot\log|A|+\log|\psi|+\log|\mathbf{B}|\big).

By
|\psi|\leq O(|\varphi|)
and (
22
), this is allowed space.

Remark 28
.

Recall Example
2.2
defines a pl-reduction from

p\textsc{-{stcon${}_{\leq}$}}
to
\textsc{$p$-mc}(\Sigma_{1}^{2})
that maps an instance
(\mathbf{G},s,t,k)
of
p\textsc{-{stcon${}_{\leq}$}}
to an instance
(\mathbf{A},\varphi)
of

\textsc{$p$-mc}(\Sigma_{1}^{2})
with
|\varphi|\leq O(k)
. Combining with the algorithm
from Proposition

we thus decide
p\textsc{-{stcon${}_{\leq}$}}
in
space

O(\log k\cdot\log|G|).

(23)

This is a slightly more detailed statement of Savitch’s
Theorem
21
. As pointed out by an anonymous reviewer, a kind of
converse holds, namely, Proposition
27
can be derived from
Savitch’s Theorem by means of a reduction:

Proof 6.3

(Sketch of a second proof of Proposition
27
.)
.

Given an instance
(\mathbf{A},\varphi)
of
\textsc{$p$-mc}(\Sigma_{1})
construct the
following directed graph
\mathbf{G}
. Its vertices
G
are triples

(\psi,\alpha,b)
where
b\in\{0,1\}
is a bit and
\psi,\alpha
are as in
the proof of Theorem
13
:
\psi
is a subformula of

\varphi
and
\alpha
is an assignment of its free variables. Again we
assume that negations appear only in front of atoms. Note

|G|\leq 2\cdot\|\varphi\|\cdot|A|^{w(\varphi)}.

(24)

The goal is to define the edges of
\mathbf{G}
in such a way that there is a
path from
s:=(\varphi,\emptyset,0)
to
t:=(\varphi,\emptyset,1)
in
\mathbf{G}
if and only if
\mathbf{A}\models\varphi
. Moreover, if this is the case,
then there is such a path of length at most

. This allows to

\mathbf{A}\models\varphi
by running Savitch’s algorithm on

(\mathbf{G},s,t,k)
. By (

) and (

) this needs

) and thus proves Proposition

(it
will be clear that this space suffices to construct

).

It remains to define the edges of
\mathbf{G}
. If
\psi
is an atom or a
negated atom, add an edge from
(\psi,\alpha,0)
to
(\psi,\alpha,1)
if and
only if

\psi
in
\mathbf{A}
.

If
\psi
is
(\psi_{0}\vee\psi_{1})
, add edges from
(\psi,\alpha,0)
to

(\psi_{0},\alpha_{0},0)
and
(\psi_{1},\alpha_{1},0)

(\psi_{0},\alpha_{0},1)
and
(\psi_{1},\alpha_{1},1)
to
(\psi,\alpha,1)

\alpha_{0}
and

\psi_{0}
and

If
\psi
is
(\psi_{0}\wedge\psi_{1})
, add edges from
(\psi,\alpha,0)
to

(\psi_{0},\alpha_{0},0)
, from
(\psi_{0},\alpha_{0},1)

(\psi_{1},\alpha_{1},0)
,
and from
(\psi_{1},\alpha_{1},0)

If
\psi
is

, then add, for every

(\psi,\alpha,0)

(\chi,\beta_{a},0)
and from
(\chi,\beta_{a},1)
to

(\psi,\alpha,1)
; here,
\beta_{a}

We now extend the space bound from the previous proposition to

\textsc{mc}(\Sigma_{t})

.

Proof 6.4

(Proof of Theorem
25
.)
.

Similarly as in the previous proposition, we give an algorithm
\mathbb{A}
deciding

Instance:

a
\Sigma_{t}
-formula
\varphi
, a natural number
w\geq w(\varphi)
, a
structure
\mathbf{A}
and
\bar{a}\in A^{w}
.

Problem:

\mathbf{A}\models\varphi(\bar{a})
?

on an instance
(\varphi,w,\mathbf{A},\bar{a})
of length
n
in

allowed space

O\big(\log\|\varphi\|\cdot w\cdot\log|A|+\log\|\varphi\|\cdot\log|\varphi|+\log n\big).

For
t=1
this is what has been shown in the proof of
Proposition
27
. So we assume

and proceed
inductively.

Let
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{r}\in\Pi_{t-1}
be such that
\varphi
results from
these formulas by existential quantification and positive Boolean
combinations. Of course, such formulas
\psi_{j}
are computable in
logarithmic space from
\varphi
. For

be the number of variables occurring freely in

and let
\bar{x}_{j}

be a length

Let

be the structure with universe

that interprets for each

by

Moreover

|\mathbf{A}^{*}|\leq\|\varphi\|+|A|+\|\varphi\|\cdot|A|^{w}.

Define the formula

in

More importantly,

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{*}\models\varphi^{*}(\bar{a}).

Since

-formula with

By (

) and (

), this is allowed space.

7.
Summary and future directions

We have studied the parameterized space complexity of model-checking bounded
variable first-order logic, i. e.
\textsc{$p$-mc}(\textup{FO}^{s})
for fixed
s\geq 2
. We
stratified the problem into subproblems according quantifier alternation rank
and showed (Theorem
13
) that the respective subproblems

\textsc{$p$-mc}(\Sigma^{s}_{t})
are complete for the levels of the tree-hierarchy

\textup{TREE}[t]
, the alternation hierarchy above the class TREE from
[
CM15
]
.
We further showed that allowing function symbols does not increase the space
complexity of these problems (Theorem
18
). This gives a quite
fine-grained picture of the space complexities
up to pl-reductions.

However, it is open whether the tree-hierarchy is strict even under some
plausible complexity assumption. We showed it does not collapse to
para-L

if Savitch’s Theorem is optimal, in fact, then already
\textup{PATH}\neq\textup{para-L}

(Theorem
2
). It follows that
\textsc{$p$-mc}(\Sigma^{2}_{1})

cannot be solved in parameterized logarithmic space if Savitch’s Theorem is
optimal. Under this assumption we proved a stronger result
(Theorem
4
) stating, intuitively, that the naïve
model-checking algorithm is space-optimal.

Finally, Theorem
4
gives a highly space-efficient
model-checking algorithm for
\textsc{$p$-mc}(\Sigma^{s}_{t})
. We presented two
constructions, a direct one and another, pointed out to us by an anonymous
reviewer, via a reduction of
\textsc{$p$-mc}(\Sigma_{1}^{2})
to
p
-
\textsc{stcon}_{\leq}
and
Savitch’s algorithm.

We view the results about the tree hierarchy as a contribution to the
fine-structure theory of FPT (cf.
[
Mül14
]
). In fact,
Theorems
6
and
1
indicate that the tree
hierarchy contains many natural parameterized problems. However, as pointed
out by an anonymous reviewer, Theorem
1
(2) implies
that problems in
\textup{TREE}[*]
have shallow circuits, and hence, intuitively,

\textup{TREE}[*]
is a small subclass of FPT. Parameterized circuit complexity is
another emerging theory about the fine-structure of FPT
[
EST15
,
BST15
,
BT18
,
CF18
,
CMY18
,
CF19
]
.

We repeat the questions whether
PATH
or
TREE
are closed under
complementation. We noted that a positive answer for
TREE
would imply a
collapse of the tree hierarchy (Corollary
14
). We do not know
whether this also follows from
PATH
being closed under complementation.

As a further structural question we do not know how
para-NL
relates to the
tree hierarchy. Proposition
10
(1) gives only a partial
answer.

We showed that the straightforward model-checking algorithm is space-optimal
under the hypothesis that Savitch’s Theorem is optimal
(Theorem
3
). We conjecture that similar optimality results can
be derived for other natural algorithms as well. Future work will show to
what extent the hypothesis that Savitch’s Theorem is optimal can play a role
in space complexity similar to the one played by the ETH in time complexity.

Instantons in ϕ 4 {{\phi^{4}}} Theories: Transseries, Virial Theorems and Numerical Aspects
Ludovico T. Giorgini, Ulrich D. Jentschura, Enrico M. Malatesta, Tommaso Rizzo, Jean Zinn-Justin

Abstract
We discuss numerical aspects of instantons in two- and three-dimensional ϕ 4 \phi^{4} theories with an internal O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) symmetry group, the so-called N N -vector model. Combining asymptotic transseries expansions for large argument with convergence acceleration techniques, we obtain high-precision values for certain integrals of the instanton that naturally occur in loop corrections around instanton configurations. Knowledge of these numerical properties are necessary in order to evaluate corrections to the large-order factorial growth of perturbation theory in ϕ 4 \phi^{4} theories. The results contribute to the understanding of the mathematical structures underlying the instanton configurations.

[

.
Instantons also constitute

[

.
Here, we derive a semi-analytic representation which

In two-dimensional and three-dimensional

II
Fundamentals of Instantons in
{{\phi^{4}}}
Theories

II.1
Instanton Equations

For the consideration of the instanton configuration,
it is sufficient to consider the
D
-dimensional scalar theory,
with the action

S[\phi]=\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,\left[\frac{1}{2}\,\left(\vec{\nabla}\phi(\vec{x})\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\,\phi(\vec{x})^{2}+\frac{g}{4}\,\phi(\vec{x})^{4}\right]\,,

(1)

where
\vec{x}
is a
D
-dimensional vector.
Consideration of the variation
\delta S[\phi]

leads to the defining equation of the instanton,

-\vec{\nabla}^{2}\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})+\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})\,+g\,\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})^{3}=0\,.

(2)

Differentiation with respect to a coordinate leads to
the equation of the zero mode
\partial_{\mu}\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})
,

\left(-\vec{\nabla}^{2}+1+3\,g\,\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})^{2}\right)\partial_{\mu}\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})=0\,.

(3)

where
\mu=1,\dots,D
.
In a quartic theory, the instanton solution
exists only for negative
g
, because the tunneling
can proceed only through a barrier.
Therefore, with the scaling

\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})=\sqrt{-\frac{1}{g}}\,\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})\,,

(4)

the equations for the instanton and the zero mode are,
respectively,

\displaystyle\left(-\vec{\nabla}^{2}+1-\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})^{2}\right)\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})=

\displaystyle\;0\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\left(-\vec{\nabla}^{2}+1-3\,\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})^{2}\right)\partial_{\mu}\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})=

\displaystyle\;0\,.

(6)

In a theory with an internal
O(N)

symmetry group, one has the following instanton,

\underline{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})=\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})\,\underline{u}=\sqrt{-\frac{1}{g}}\,\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})\,\underline{u}\,,

(7)

where vectors in the internal space are
designated by underlining, and we can choose

\underline{u}=\{1,0,\cdots,0\}^{\rm T}\,.

(8)

As is evident from Figs. 2 and 3 of Ref.
[
1
]
,
there is a sign ambiguity in the
choice of the instanton, and the degeneracy
under the operation
\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}\to-\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}

needs to be taken into account when
using dispersion relations.
Indeed, via dispersion relations,
one can establish that the instanton action
A
,
defined via

S[\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}]=-A/g\,,

(9)

governs the large-order behavior of the perturbative
coefficients
{\sf G}_{K}
in the
K
th order
of the expansion in
g
of the
n
-point
correlation functions in a
D
-dimensional

O(N)
theory
[
4
]
.
In the notation adopted in Eq. (1.9) of Ref.
[
4
]
,
we have

\displaystyle{\sf G}_{K}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{c(N,D)}{\pi}\,\left(\frac{1}{A}\right)^{(n+N+D-1)/2}\,\left(-\frac{1}{A}\right)^{K}\,

(10)

\displaystyle\;\times\Gamma\left(K+\frac{n+N+D-1}{2}\right)\,[1+{\mathcal{O}}(1/K)]\,.

(11)

Here,
c(N,D)
is a constant coefficient
to be determined separately for each

N
and
D
.

II.2
Instantons and Large–Order Behavior

The connection between instantons and large-order behavior is usually obtained
by saddle-point evaluations of contour integrals
[
19
]
. In the
following, we will mention a less known derivation
[
20
]
that has the
advantage of being simpler and more intuitive. The basic idea is that
Feynman diagrams of the
\phi^{4}
theory at large orders
K\gg 1
are
essentially random regular graphs with connectivity 4 and size
K
.
For a large number of vertices, it is known that random regular graphs have a locally
tree-like structure (with the size of the loops growing as
\log K
). This
allows us to write an iterative equation that turns out to be equivalent to the
instanton equation (
2
). The Feynman rules imply that there
is a factor
1/(\vec{k}_{i}^{2}+m^{2})
for each line
i
in the graph and a
Dirac-
\delta

function on each vertex ensuring the conservation of momentum. Invoking the
tree-like structure, one can then write the following equation (see also
Fig.
1
)

g(\vec{k})=\frac{1}{\vec{k}^{2}+m^{2}}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{D}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{D}}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{D}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{D}}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{D}k_{3}}{(2\pi)^{D}}\\
g(\vec{k}_{1})g(\vec{k}_{2})g(\vec{k}_{3})\delta^{(D)}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2}+\vec{k}_{3}-\vec{k})\,.

(12)

So, multiplying everything by
\vec{k}^{2}+m^{2}
, the previous equation
in real space is equivalent to

(-\vec{\nabla}^{2}+m^{2})\,g(\vec{x})=g^{3}(\vec{x})\,,

(13)

which is the instanton equation for the
\phi^{4}
theory for
g(\vec{x})=\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})
.

Using a standard procedure (similar in spirit to the cavity method from
spin-glass theory
[
21
]
), one can identify the action in
Eq. (
1
) as the Bethe free energy of the problem. This
allows us to derive in a simple way the instanton equation and the action; the

1/K
correction about the instanton
[
4
,
5
]

corresponds to the
1/K
finite size
correction to the Bethe lattice random graphs.

III
Three–Dimensional Instanton

III.1
Large Argument

We use the fact that

\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})=\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}(|\vec{x}|)=\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)
is radially
symmetric and this constitutes am “
S
state” in the formalism
adopted in atomic physics
[
22
,
23
]
.
The equation fulfilled by the instanton

\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)=\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)
(including the dimension

D
in the superscript) in three dimensions is
[see Eq. (
5
)],

-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)-\frac{2}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)+\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)-[\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)]^{3}=0\,.

(14)

We are attempting to find a systematic expansion
of the solution of Eq. (
14
),
and do so for large argument
r\to\infty

in the current section.
A remark might be in order.
Namely, linear second-order differential equations
typically have solutions regular and irregular
at infinite argument. The equations defining the
instanton, by contrast, are highly nonlinear, and
hence this consideration does not apply.
In fact, the asymptotics for large argument
uniquely determine the instanton solution.
In this context, it is instructive to
recall
[
24
]
that
the uniqueness of the solution for a

nonlinear
differential equation,
determined by a given initial condition or asymptotic behavior,
constitutes a pivotal factor in the emergence
of a range of complex phenomena, including
chaos. This uniqueness leads to behaviors which are sensitive to the small
variations in the initial
conditions
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
.
Furthermore, this intrinsic uniqueness in nonlinear differential equations is
analogous to the sensitive dependency on initial conditions observed in fluid
dynamics, particularly in the transition from laminar to turbulent flow, where
even minor perturbations can drastically alter the flow patterns, echoing the
underlying chaotic dynamics described in fluid mechanics
research
[
28
]
.

The instanton goes to zero as
r\to\infty
, and so
one can neglect the term
[\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)]^{3}\ll\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)

in a first approximation.
Then, one obtains the relation

-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)-\frac{2}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)+\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)\approx 0\,.

(15)

Our
ansatz

\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)=\frac{\exp(-r)}{r}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{r^{n}}\,,

(16)

is a nonanalytic expansion in the variable
1/r
,
for large
r
. In fact, in The variable
\chi=1/r
, the
expansion (
16
) constitutes a
nonanalytic (resurgent, transseries) expansion in the variables

\chi
and
\exp(-1/\chi)

(see Refs.
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
),
and
\chi=0
becomes a singular point of the
differential equation. The importance of
nonanalytic exponentials (resurgent expansions) in the solution of
differential equations with singular points has been stressed in
Ref.
[
12
]
.
The substitution
\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)=g(r)/r
takes
Eq. (
15
) into the form

-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}g(r)+g(r)=0\,,\qquad g(r)={\mathcal{C}}\,\exp(-r)\,,

(17)

for which the solution regular at infinity is just
\exp(-r)
.
Hence, the
ansatz
(
16
) collapses to a single term,
with
{\mathcal{C}}
being an overall constant, and reads

\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)={\mathcal{C}}\,\frac{\exp(-r)}{r}\,,

(18)

for which the approximate equality
in Eq. (
15
) becomes an exact equality.
Here,
{\mathcal{C}}
is a coefficient which can be determined numerically.
A 60-figure result for
{\mathcal{C}}
is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}=

\displaystyle\;2.712\,808\,360\,940\,844\,770\,465\,994\,573\,657

\displaystyle\;\phantom{2.}808\,840\,265\,350\,950\,750\,281\,746\,458\,229(1)\,.

(19)

One can now approximate, in Eq. (
14
),

-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)-\frac{2}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)+\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)-\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)^{3}\\
\approx-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)-\frac{2}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)+\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)-\frac{\exp(-3r)}{r^{3}}=0\,.

(20)

The structure of this equation justifies the
ansatz

\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)={\mathcal{C}}\,\frac{\exp(-r)}{r}+\frac{\exp(-3r)}{r}\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{r^{n}}+\dots\,.

(21)

Matching of the
b_{n}
coefficients leads to the result,

\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)={\mathcal{C}}\,\frac{\exp(-r)}{r}-{\mathcal{C}}^{3}\,\frac{\exp(-3r)}{8r^{3}}\,\left(1-\frac{3}{2r}+\frac{21}{8\,r^{2}}-\frac{45}{8r^{3}}+\frac{465}{32\,r^{4}}-\frac{2835}{64\,r^{5}}+\frac{40005}{256\,r^{6}}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{-7})\right)+\dots\,.

(22)

This expression, cubed, generates terms proportional to

\left[\frac{\exp(-r)}{r}+\frac{\exp(-3r)}{r^{3}}\right]^{3}\to\left(\frac{\exp(-r)}{r}\right)^{2}\times\frac{\exp(-3r)}{r^{3}}=\frac{\exp(-5r)}{r^{5}}
.
Now, we enter with the
ansatz

\displaystyle\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)=

\displaystyle\;{\mathcal{C}}\,\frac{\exp(-r)}{r}-{\mathcal{C}}^{3}\,\frac{\exp(-3r)}{8r^{3}}\,\left(1-\frac{3}{2r}+\frac{21}{8\,r^{2}}-\frac{45}{8r^{3}}+\frac{465}{32\,r^{4}}-\frac{2835}{64\,r^{5}}+\frac{40005}{256\,r^{6}}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{-7})\right)

\displaystyle\;+\frac{\exp(-5r)}{r}\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}}{r^{n}}+\dots\,,

(23)

again into Eq. (
14
), match the coefficients
c_{n}
, and find

\displaystyle\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)=

\displaystyle\;{\mathcal{C}}\,\frac{\exp(-r)}{r}-{\mathcal{C}}^{3}\,\frac{\exp(-3r)}{8r^{3}}\,\left(1-\frac{3}{2r}+\frac{21}{8\,r^{2}}-\frac{45}{8r^{3}}+\frac{465}{32\,r^{4}}-\frac{2835}{64\,r^{5}}+\frac{40005}{256\,r^{6}}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{-7})\right)

\displaystyle\;+{\mathcal{C}}^{5}\,\frac{\exp(-5r)}{64r^{5}}\,\left(1-\frac{19}{6r}+\frac{151}{18\,r^{2}}-\frac{815}{36r^{3}}+\frac{56921}{864\,r^{4}}-\frac{1094215}{5184\,r^{5}}+\frac{2592553}{3456\,r^{6}}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{-7})\right)\,.

(24)

Finally, with the contribution
of order
\exp(-7r)
included, we have

\displaystyle\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)=

\displaystyle\;{\mathcal{C}}\,\frac{\exp(-r)}{r}-{\mathcal{C}}^{3}\,\frac{\exp(-3r)}{8r^{3}}\,\left(1-\frac{3}{2r}+\frac{21}{8\,r^{2}}-\frac{45}{8r^{3}}+\frac{465}{32\,r^{4}}-\frac{2835}{64\,r^{5}}+\frac{40005}{256\,r^{6}}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{-7})\right)

\displaystyle\;+{\mathcal{C}}^{5}\,\frac{\exp(-5r)}{64r^{5}}\,\left(1-\frac{19}{6r}+\frac{151}{18\,r^{2}}-\frac{815}{36r^{3}}+\frac{56921}{864\,r^{4}}-\frac{1094215}{5184\,r^{5}}+\frac{2592553}{3456\,r^{6}}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{-7})\right)

\displaystyle\;-{\mathcal{C}}^{7}\,\frac{\exp(-7r)}{512r^{7}}\,\left(1-\frac{29}{6r}+\frac{271}{16\,r^{2}}-\frac{3943}{72r^{3}}+\frac{614143}{3456\,r^{4}}-\frac{8322275}{13824\,r^{5}}+\frac{80215771}{36864\,r^{6}}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{-7})\right)\,.

(25)

For the term proportional to
\exp(-3r)
, we find the
compact formula,

-{\mathcal{C}}^{3}\,\frac{\exp(-3r)}{8r^{3}}\,\left(1-\frac{3}{2r}+\frac{21}{8\,r^{2}}-\frac{45}{8r^{3}}+\frac{465}{32\,r^{4}}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{-5})\right)=-{\mathcal{C}}^{3}\,\frac{\exp(-r)}{r}\,\left(\textrm{Ei}(-2r)-2\exp(2r)\textrm{Ei}(-4r)\right)\,\,,

(26)

where
\textrm{Ei}(r)
the exponential integral function,
but we were unable to find general expressions for the
terms in the series multiplying the exponential factors

\exp(-5r)
and
\exp(-7r)
.

Transseries in the variable
\chi=1/r
have been encountered

[

.

III.2
Small Argument

We recall the equation fulfilled by the instanton
[see Eq. (
14
)]

-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)-\frac{2}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)+\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)-\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)^{3}=0\,.

(28)

Plugging in a polynomial
ansatz
into Eq. (
28
), with

\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(0)={\mathcal{F}}=4.337\,387\,679\,976\dots

(29)

[see also Eq. (
62c
)], one finds

\displaystyle\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)=

\displaystyle\;{\mathcal{F}}+\frac{1}{6}\,({\mathcal{F}}-{\mathcal{F}}^{3})\,r^{2}+\frac{1}{120}({\mathcal{F}}-4\,{\mathcal{F}}^{3}+3\,{\mathcal{F}}^{5})\,r^{4}

\displaystyle\;+\frac{{\mathcal{F}}-17\,{\mathcal{F}}^{3}+35\,{\mathcal{F}}^{5}-19\,{\mathcal{F}}^{7}}{5040}\,r^{4}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{6})\,.

(30)

Only even powers of
r
contribute.
Using computer algebra
[
35
]
, one can easily
determine all coefficients up to order
r^{80}
,
and write

\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{2n}\,r^{2n}\,.

(31)

A closer inspection reveals that
the series of the

is factorially divergent and
alternating. Still, one can use summation techniques
to confirm the result (
27
)
at the matching point
\xi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r=3.0)

[see also Eq. (

)].
In the summation process, we have used

[62/62]
–Padé approximations
[
33
]
in order
to sum the divergent perturbative series at

r=3
, or alternatively
117
-order Weniger–Levin
transformations
[
34
]
.
This leads to the desired accuracy in the intermediate region.
Improvements of the numerical accuracy
are possible when one expands the instanton about
additional reference points (e.g., where
r
assumes
the value of a small integer) and concatenates the
expansions in regions of overlap.

IV
Two–Dimensional Instanton

IV.1
Large Argument

In two dimensions, the instanton is
equally radially symmetric (see Fig.
2
),
and we can write

\xi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})=\xi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)
.
The equation fulfilled by the instanton is

-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}\xi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\xi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)+\xi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)-\xi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)^{3}=0\,.

(32)

Just like in the three-dimensional case (see Sec.
III.1
),
the instanton goes exponentially to zero as
r\to\infty
, and so
one can neglect the term
[\xi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)]^{3}
in a first approximation.
Then, one obtains the relation
[see also Eq. (
15
)]

-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}\xi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\xi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)+\xi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)\approx 0\,.

(33)

By a similar analysis as described for the three-dimensional
case, one obtains

\displaystyle\xi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)=

\displaystyle\;{\mathcal{D}}\,\frac{\exp(-r)}{r^{1/2}}\,\left(1-\frac{1}{8r}+\frac{9}{128\,r^{2}}-\frac{75}{1024\,r^{3}}+\frac{3675}{32768\,r^{4}}-\frac{59535}{262144\,r^{5}}+\frac{2401245}{4194304\,r^{6}}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{-7})\right)

\displaystyle\;-{\mathcal{D}}^{3}\,\frac{\exp(-3r)}{8r^{3/2}}\,\left(1-\frac{9}{8r}+\frac{213}{128\,r^{2}}-\frac{3215}{1024r^{3}}+\frac{238563}{32768\,r^{4}}-\frac{5283711}{262144\,r^{5}}+\frac{273186513}{4194304\,r^{6}}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{-7})\right)

\displaystyle\;+{\mathcal{D}}^{5}\,\frac{\exp(-5r)}{64r^{5/2}}\,\left(1-\frac{53}{24r}+\frac{589}{128\,r^{2}}-\frac{96193}{9216r^{3}}+\frac{23551553}{884736\,r^{4}}-\frac{544320827}{7077888\,r^{5}}+\frac{85429251785}{339738624\,r^{6}}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{-7})\right)

\displaystyle\;-{\mathcal{D}}^{7}\,\frac{\exp(-7r)}{512r^{7/2}}\,\left(1-\frac{79}{24r}+\frac{10037}{1152\,r^{2}}-\frac{629833}{27648r^{3}}+\frac{55541473}{884736\,r^{4}}-\frac{1326870785}{7077888\,r^{5}}+\frac{23212812833}{37748736\,r^{6}}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{-7})\right)\,.

(34)

Just as in the three-dimensional case, we
can find a compact expression for the
leading term, which for
D=2
is of order
\exp(-r)/\sqrt{r}
,

{\mathcal{D}}\,\frac{\exp(-r)}{r^{1/2}}\,\left(1-\frac{1}{8r}+\frac{9}{128\,r^{2}}-\frac{75}{1024\,r^{3}}+\frac{3675}{32768\,r^{4}}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{-5})\right)={\mathrm{i}}\,{\mathcal{D}}\,H^{(1)}_{0}({\mathrm{i}}r)\,.

(35)

Here,
H^{(1)}_{\alpha}(r)
is the Hankel function
[
36

V
Instantons and Virial Theorems

V.1
Derivation of the Virial Theorems

For the following investigations, it is instrumental to derive several
relations fulfilled by the instanton; these constitute virial theorems. We
consider the general action

{\mathcal{S}}(\phi)=\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\left[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu}\left(\vec{\nabla}\phi(\vec{x})\right)^{2}+{\mathcal{V}}\bigl(\phi(\vec{x})\bigr)\right]\,,

(42)

where in the case of the action (
1
),
one has

{\mathcal{V}}\bigl(\phi(\vec{x})\bigr)=\frac{1}{2}\phi(\vec{x})+\frac{1}{4}g\,\phi^{4}(\vec{x})\,.

(43)

We assume that the field equation,
obtained by variational calculus,
has a finite action solution
\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(\vec{x})
.
If the action
{\mathcal{S}}(\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}})
is finite
so is the action
{\mathcal{S}}(\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}},\lambda)
,
obtained from
{\mathcal{S}}(\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}})={\mathcal{S}}(\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}},\lambda=1)
by the replacement

\phi(\vec{x})\to\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(\lambda\,\vec{x})
.
If we change variables in the action,
setting
\lambda\vec{x}=\vec{x}^{\prime}
, we find

{\mathcal{S}}[\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}},\lambda]=\lambda^{2-D}\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,\left[\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{\mu}\left(\vec{\nabla}\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(\vec{x})\right)^{2}\right]\\
+\lambda^{-D}\,\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,{\mathcal{V}}\bigl(\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(\vec{x})\bigr)\,.

(44)

Because
\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(x)
satisfies the field equation,
the variation of the action vanishes for
\lambda=1
,
i.e., we have the equation
\left.\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}\lambda}{\mathcal{S}}[\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}},\lambda]\right|_{\lambda=1}=0
.

\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x^{\prime}\,\left[\frac{D-2}{2}\left(\vec{\nabla}\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(\vec{x}^{\prime})\right)^{2}+D\,{\mathcal{V}}\bigl(\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(\vec{x}^{\prime})\bigr)\right]=0\,.

(45)

This relation allows us to express
the kinetic term [integral of
(\vec{\nabla}\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}})^{2}
]
in terms of the potential term
(integral of
{\mathcal{V}}(\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}})
and vice versa.
The classical action
{\mathcal{S}}[\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}]

can thus be expressed in terms of the kinetic term only:

{\mathcal{S}}[\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}]=\frac{1}{D}\;\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,\left(\vec{\nabla}\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(\vec{x})\right)^{2}\,,

(46)

a form that shows that
{\mathcal{S}}(\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}})
is always positive.
The second derivative of
{\mathcal{S}}(\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}},\lambda)

reads as

\left.\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}}{\left({\mathrm{d}}\lambda\right)^{2}}{\mathcal{S}}[\phi_{c},\lambda]\right|_{\lambda=1}=\left(2-D\right)\,\int\left[\partial_{\mu}\phi_{c}(x)\right]^{2}{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,.

(47)

For
D\geq 2
, this result shows that the solution is not a local minimum of
the action and, thus, the so-called longitudinal
fluctuation operator
{{\bf M}}_{\mathrm{L}}
, defined as

{{\bf M}}_{\mathrm{L}}(\vec{x},\vec{x}^{\prime})=\left.\frac{\delta^{2}{\mathcal{S}}}{\delta\phi(\vec{x})\,\delta\phi(\vec{x}^{\prime})}\right|_{\phi=\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}\,,

(48)

has at least one negative eigenvalue
[
32
,
4
]
.

In the example of potentials of special form

{\mathcal{V}}(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\,\phi^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,g\,\phi^{M}\,,

(49)

one can derive an additional relation.
If the action
{\mathcal{S}}[\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}]
is
finite, so is the following action
obtained by the replacement

\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}\to\lambda\,\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}
,

{\mathcal{S}}[\lambda\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}]=\lambda^{2}\,\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\vec{\nabla}\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(\vec{x})\right)^{2}+\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}^{2}(\vec{x})\right]\\
+\frac{1}{4}\,\lambda^{M}\,g\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}^{M}(\vec{x})\,.

(50)

Again, if
\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}
is the
instanton solution, then the derivative with
respect to
\lambda
must vanish for
\lambda=1
.
One obtains further relations, in addition to (
46
),

{\mathcal{S}}(\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}})=-\frac{g}{8}(M-2)\,\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,\phi^{M}_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(\vec{x})\\
\mathop{=}^{M=4}-\frac{g}{4}\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,\phi^{4}_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(\vec{x})\qquad(M=4)\,.

(51)

This relation is consistent with the
fact that the instanton exists only for negative
g
.
Thus, one can express the instanton action as follows,

{\mathcal{S}}(\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}})=\frac{M-2}{2D-M(D-2)}\,\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}^{2}(x)\\
\mathop{=}^{M=4}\frac{1}{4-D}\,\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}^{2}(x)\,.

(52)

We have used Eqs. (
46
) and (
51
).
In particular, Eqs. (
51
) and (
52
)
are consistent only if the
denominator in the expression on the right-hand side
of Eq. (
52
) is positive, which implies

M\leq\frac{2D}{D-2}\,,

(53)

and, therefore,
the field theory must be super-renormalizable or at least renormalizable. At
the special dimension
D=2M/(M-2)
, where the theory is renormalizable,
one finds the paradoxical result

\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}^{2}(x)=0
. This implies that
only the massless equation (where the coefficient of

\phi^{2}
in the original action vanishes),
has instanton solutions (see also Appendix
A
). Finally, one
verifies that the second derivative at
\lambda=1
is negative, confirming the
existence of a negative eigenvalue of
{\bf M}_{\mathrm{L}}
for all dimensions.

V.2
Summary of the Virial Theorems

We summarize. From Eqs. (
46
), (
51
)
and (
52
), we have for the

\phi^{4}
theory with the action (
1
),

{\mathcal{S}}(\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}})=\frac{1}{D}\;\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,\left(\vec{\nabla}\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(\vec{x})\right)^{2}=-\frac{g}{4}\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,\phi^{4}_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(\vec{x})\\
=\frac{1}{4-D}\,\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}^{2}(\vec{x})\,.

(54)

With the scaling given by Eqs. (
4

the action of the instanton becomes (
g<0

\displaystyle\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(\vec{x})=

\displaystyle\;\sqrt{-\frac{1}{g}}\,\xi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(r)\,,\quad r=|\vec{x}|\,,\quad{\mathcal{S}}(\phi_{{\mathrm{cl}}})=-\frac{A}{g}>0\,.

(55a)

We have three equivalent representations
of the action
A

A=\frac{1}{D}\,\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\left(\vec{\nabla}\xi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(r)\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\,\int{\mathrm{d}}^{D}x\,\xi^{4}_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(r)\\

(55b)

(56)

where
\Omega_{D}=2\pi^{D/2}/\Gamma({\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}D)

is the generalized surface of the
(D-1)
-dimensional
unit sphere embedded in
D
-dimensional space.

V.3
Asymptotic Behavior

The asymptotic behavior of the radial instanton
equation

\left[-\left(\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}r}\right)^{2}-\frac{D-1}{r}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}r}+1\right]\xi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}(r)-\xi_{{\mathrm{cl}}}^{3}(r)=0

for large

This would mean that

\xi^{(D)}_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)\propto{\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}}\;r^{1-D/2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2r}}\;{\mathrm{e}}^{-r}=\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{-r}}{r^{D/2-1/2}}\,.

(59)

Our formulas (
25
) and (
34
)
confirm this asymptotic behavior.

Conclusions

We have analyzed the properties of
instanton solutions in
O(N)
-symmetric
quartic field theories in
D=2
and

D=3
dimensions.
The basic formulation for the quartic
instanton has been given in in Sec.
II
.
We concentrate on the three-dimensional
instanton (
D=3
) in Sec.
III
,
which is phenomenologically the most
interesting case.
We derive asymptotic expansions for
large argument in the form of a transseries
[Eq. (
25

asymptotic power series [Eq. (
30
)]
for small argument.
The quartic instanton in
D=2
is discussed in Sec.
IV
.
Virial theorems are derived in Sec.
V
.
Instanton integrals are given in Sec.
VI
,
with a precision approaching the
realm of applicability of the PSLQ
algorithm
[
38
,
39
,
40
,
41
]

which is designed to search for analytic expressions
of integrals in terms of known constants
such as the Euler constant
\gamma_{E}=0.57721\dots
,
various Riemann zeta functions, powers of
\pi
,
and multiplicative combinations of these constants.
We can report that we have carried out a limited set of searches
with the same constants that were used in
Eq. (A11) of Ref.
[
4
]
without success.
A more detailed search might constituent a possible
direction for the future.

In a quartic theory, the instanton solution
exists only for negative
g
, because the tunneling
can proceed only through a barrier, and the latter exists
only negative coupling
g<0
. The imaginary
part of the partition function,
and of correlation functions, obtained by expanding
about the instanton solution, is
proportional (see Ref.
[
4
]

to
\exp(-(-A/g))=\exp(A/g)
,
where

for

The instanton action

Our calculations suggest that instanton solutions
in quartic theories cannot be expressed
in closed analytic form, except for the

D=1
. We also note that the general
properties of the instanton
in massive theories are valid

D<4

The dimension 4 is singular,

The cases of a massless quartic theory

A

Our investigations indicate that,

(see Appendixes

and

\phi^{4}

Nevertheless, in view

.

Appendix A
Four–Dimensional Massless Quartic Theory

The existence of instantons in the renormalizable
(but not super-renormalizable) quartic
theory in four dimensions has been anticipated
in Sec.
V.1
. We consider the action

{\mathcal{S}}[\phi]=\int{\mathrm{d}}^{4}x\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\vec{\nabla}\phi\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,g\,\phi^{4}\right]\,.

(67)

The corresponding field equation is

-\vec{\nabla}^{2}\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})+g\,\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}^{3}(\vec{x})=0
.
We know that the solution of minimal action is spherically
symmetric, thus we set

\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}(x)=\sqrt{-\frac{1}{g}}\,\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)\,,

(68)

where
r=|\vec{x}|
.
We then obtain a differential equation

\left[\left(\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}r}\right)^{2}+\frac{3}{r}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}r}+\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}(r)\right]\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)=0
.
The solution is

\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{1+r^{2}}

(69)

The instanton action is

S[\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}]=-\frac{A}{g}\,,\qquad A=\frac{8\pi^{2}}{3}\,.

(70)

The instanton integrals,
I_{n}=\int{\mathrm{d}}^{4}x\,\xi_{\mathrm{cl}}(r)^{n}
,
for
n=3,4,6
, are

I_{3}=8\sqrt{2}\,\pi^{2}
,

I_{4}=\frac{32\,\pi^{2}}{3}
,

I_{6}=\frac{128\,\pi^{2}}{5}
.

Appendix B

Another example of the existence of

-\vec{\nabla}^{2}\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}(\vec{x})+g\,\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}(\vec{x})=0

(72)

where

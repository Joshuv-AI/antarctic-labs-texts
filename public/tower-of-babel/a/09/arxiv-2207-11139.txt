Moduli of representations of one-point extensions
Arif Dönmez, Markus Reineke

Abstract
. We study moduli spaces of (semi-)stable representations of one-point extensions of quivers by rigid representations. This class of moduli spaces unifies Grassmannians of subrepresentations of rigid representations and moduli spaces of representations of generalized Kronecker quivers. With homological methods, we find numerical criteria for non-emptiness and results on basic geometric properties, construct generating semi-invariants, expand the Gel’fand MacPherson correspondence, and derive a formula for the Poincaré polynomial in singular cohomology of these moduli spaces.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we construct and study moduli spaces parametrizing isomorphism classes of representations of so-called one-point extensions of path algebras of quivers. This constitutes a class of algebras of global dimension two, for which many of the favourable properties of moduli spaces of representations of quivers still hold. Namely, we find numerical criteria for non-emptiness and results on basic geometric properties, construct generating semi-invariants, expand the Gel’fand MacPherson correspondence, and derive a formula for the Poincaré polynomial in singular cohomology of these moduli spaces. We explicitly apply the developed theory in several examples.

To do this, we fix a path algebra
A=kQ
of a finite quiver, which we extend by a representation
T
of
A
to the one-point extension algebra
A[T]
.
We construct standard projective resolutions for representations of
A[T]
. One of the most important consequences of this is an explicit description of the space
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}
of representations, which allows us to conclude its vanishing on so-called full representations (under the assumption of
T
being rigid). See Theorems
3.1
,
3.3
,
3.4
,
3.6
for precise formulations. Moreover, the standard resolutions allow us to calculate the Euler form of
A[T]
in Theorem
3.7
, Corollary
3.8
,
3.10
.
After these preparations, we consider the representation varieties of
A[T]
, interpret the found homological properties in this geometric setting, and rediscover some results by Schofield and Crawley-Boevey (with different methods) in Theorem
4.3
, Corollary
4.4
,
4.5
. Moreover, in this way we can determine the Zariski tangent space of the representation variety in each point (Theorem
4.1
) and conclude that the open subset of full representations is smooth and irreducible (Theorem
4.8
).

We follow the GIT approach of King in the construction of moduli spaces. For this, we choose a canonical stability condition, such that the resulting spaces unify quiver Grassmannians of subrepresentations of rigid representations (Theorem
7.1
) and moduli spaces of representations of generalized Kronecker quivers. We find a numerical criterion for semistability in Theorem
5.2
, which allows us to conclude that semi-stable representations are full representations (Corollary
5.7
). In this way, we can apply the above geometric properties of representation varieties to prove that the resulting moduli spaces are smooth, irreducible and of expected dimension in Theorem
5.8
.

After this, we prove a relative version of a recursive numerical criterion (
[
9
]
) for non-emptiness of the semi-stable locus in Theorems
5.9
,
5.10
. A set of generators for the ring of semi-invariants, closely following Schofield and Van den Bergh (
[
13
]
) is given in section
6
.

Moreover, we find a form of Gel’fand MacPherson correspondence in terms of these moduli spaces in Theorem
7.1
. We finish the paper by deriving a recursive formula to determine the Poincaré polynomial of the moduli spaces (Theorem
8.2
).

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}_{A[T]}^{2}(\tilde{M},\tilde{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{A}\big(\Omega_{A}\otimes_{A}(T\otimes_{k}V),N\big)\big/\mathrm{im}(h^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{A}\left(\Omega_{A}\otimes_{A}(T\otimes_{k}V),N\right)/\mathrm{im}([\kappa_{T\otimes V}^{*}\;\;\;\;(\Omega_{A}\otimes f)^{*}])

\displaystyle\overset{(\ref{eq:HilfsGleichungx})}{=}

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{Hom}_{A}\left(\Omega_{A}\otimes_{A}(T\otimes_{k}V),N\right)/\mathrm{im}((\Omega_{A}\otimes f^{\prime})^{*})\right)/\mathrm{im}(\overline{\kappa_{T\otimes V}^{*}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\Omega_{A}\otimes_{A}\mathrm{ker}(f),N)/\mathrm{im}(\overline{\kappa_{T\otimes V}^{*}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),N).

We thus arrive at the folllowing description of
\mathrm{Ext}_{A[T]}^{2}
:

Theorem 3.4
.

For
A[T]
-modules
\tilde{M}=\big(M,V,f:T\otimes_{k}V\to M\big)
,
\tilde{N}=\big(N,W,g:T\otimes_{k}W\to N\big)
there is an isomorphism:

\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{A[T]}(\tilde{M},\tilde{N})~\xrightarrow{\sim}~\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),N).

In particular, if
T
is projective, so is
\mathrm{ker}(f)
, thus
{\rm Ext}^{2}_{A[T]}
vanishes identically. In other words,
A[T]
is again hereditary in this case.

Definition 3.5
.

We call an
A[T]
-module
\tilde{M}=\big(M,V,f:T\otimes_{k}V\to M\big)

full
, if
f
is a surjective map.

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(T,T)=0
. Then, for full
A[T]
-modules
\tilde{M},\tilde{N}
, the vanishing property

\mathrm{Ext}_{A[T]}^{2}(\tilde{M},\tilde{N})=0

holds.

Proof.

Write
\tilde{M}=\big(M,V,f:T\otimes_{k}V\to M\big)
and
\tilde{N}=\big(N,W,g:T\otimes_{k}W\to N\big)
.
We consider

0\to\mathrm{ker}(g)\to T\otimes_{k}W\xrightarrow{g}N\to 0

and apply
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),?)
and obtain the long exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),\mathrm{ker}(g))\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),T\otimes_{k}W)\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),N)

\to\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),\mathrm{ker}(g))\to\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),T\otimes_{k}W)\to\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),N)\to 0.

Now we will show that
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),N)=0
holds by showing that

\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),T\otimes_{k}W)=0

holds. We also have:

0\to\mathrm{ker}(f)\to T\otimes_{k}V\xrightarrow{f}M\to 0.

Applying
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(?,T\otimes_{k}W)
to this, we obtain the long exact sequence:

0\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(M,T\otimes_{k}W)\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(T\otimes_{k}V,T\otimes_{k}W)\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),T\otimes_{k}W)

\to\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(M,T\otimes_{k}W)\to\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(T\otimes_{k}V,T\otimes_{k}W)\to\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),T\otimes_{k}W)\to 0.

Since
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(T,T)=0
, in particular
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(T\otimes_{k}V,T\otimes_{k}W)=0
holds.

So
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),T\otimes_{k}W)=0
and therefore
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),N)=0
. Using Theorem
3.4
, we conclude the proof.
∎

3.3.
Derivations and the Euler form of
A[T]

Let
\tilde{M}=\big(M,V,f:T\otimes_{k}V\to M\big),\tilde{N}=\big(N,W,g:T\otimes_{k}W\to N\big)
be
A[T]
-modules. To shorten notation, we define
B=A[T]
. In this section we determine the dimension of a space of derivations

\dim\mathrm{Der}_{\tilde{R}}\big(B,\mathrm{Hom}_{\tilde{R}}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})\big).

To do this, we consider the canonical exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\tilde{R}}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})\xrightarrow{c}\mathrm{Der}_{\tilde{R}}\big(B,\mathrm{Hom}_{\tilde{R}}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})\big)

\to\mathrm{Ext}_{B}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})\to 0,

where
c
is defined as

\big(\beta_{i}:\tilde{N}_{i}\to\tilde{M}_{i}\big)_{i\in Q_{0}}\mapsto\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in Q_{1}\\
\alpha:i\to j\end{subarray}}\tilde{M}_{\alpha}\beta_{i}-\beta_{j}\tilde{N}_{\alpha}.

We obtain the equality:

\dim\mathrm{Der}_{\tilde{R}}\big(B,\mathrm{Hom}_{\tilde{R}}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})\big)

=

-\left(\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})-\dim\mathrm{Ext}_{B}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})\right)+\mathrm{Hom}_{\tilde{R}}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})
.

On the other hand, we have the following description:

We consider the standard projective resolution of
\tilde{N}
(Theorem
3.3
)

P_{\bullet}(\tilde{N})\to\tilde{N}\to 0

and the induced cochain complex
\mathsf{C}:=\mathrm{Hom}_{B}\big(P_{\bullet}(\tilde{N}),\tilde{M}\big)
:

\mathsf{C}:\ldots\to 0\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\tilde{R}}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A}\big(\Omega_{A}\otimes_{A}N,M\big)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}_{R}\big(T\otimes_{k}W,M\big)

\xrightarrow{h^{*}}\mathrm{Hom}_{A}\big(\Omega_{A}\otimes_{A}(T\otimes_{k}W),M\big)\to 0\to...

Then
H^{i}(\mathsf{C})=\mathrm{Ext}_{A[T]}^{i}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})~~~(i=0,1,2)
.

This way we obtain the equality:

\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})-\dim\mathrm{Ext}_{B}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})+\dim\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{B}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})

=

\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{\tilde{R}}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})-\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{A}\big(\Omega_{A}\otimes_{A}N,M\big)-\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{R}\big(T\otimes_{k}W,M\big)+\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{A}\big(\Omega_{A}\otimes_{A}(T\otimes_{k}W),M\big)
.

We easily determine:

\displaystyle\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{A}\big(\Omega_{A}\otimes_{A}N,M\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in Q_{1}\\
\alpha:i\to j\end{subarray}}\dim N_{i}\cdot\dim M_{j},

\displaystyle\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{R}\big(T\otimes_{k}W,M\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim W\cdot\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}\dim T_{i}\cdot\dim M_{i},

\displaystyle\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{A}\big(\Omega_{A}\otimes_{A}(T\otimes_{k}W),M\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim W\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in Q_{1}\\
\alpha:i\to j\end{subarray}}\dim T_{i}\cdot\dim M_{j}.

By using the characterization of
\mathrm{Ext}_{A[T]}^{2}
of Theorem
3.4
, we end up in:

Theorem 3.7
.

For finite-dimensional
A[T]
-modules
\tilde{M}=\big(M,V,f:T\otimes_{k}V\to M\big)
and
\tilde{N}=\big(N,W,g:T\otimes_{k}W\to N\big)
we have

\dim\mathrm{Der}_{\tilde{R}}\big(B,\mathrm{Hom}_{\tilde{R}}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})\big)~~=~~\dim\mathrm{Der}_{R}\big(A,\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(N,M)\big)

~+~\dim W\cdot\langle T,M\rangle_{A}~~+~~\dim\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(g),M),

where
\langle.,.\rangle_{A}
denotes the homological Euler-form of
A
.

Corollary 3.8
.

If we assume
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(T,T)=0
, then for full
A[T]
-modules
\tilde{M}
and
\tilde{N}
we have

\dim\mathrm{Der}_{\tilde{R}}\big(B,\mathrm{Hom}_{\tilde{R}}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})\big)~~=~~\dim\mathrm{Der}_{R}\big(A,\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(N,M)\big)+\dim W\cdot\langle T,M\rangle_{A}.

Proof.

The claim follows immediately using Theorem
3.7
and
3.6
.
∎

We notate a dimension vector
\vec{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{\hat{Q}_{0}}
as a tuple
(s,d)
, where
s=\vec{d}_{\infty}
and
d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
.

Definition 3.9
.

For dimension vectors
(s,d),(s^{\prime},d^{\prime})\in\mathbb{N}^{\hat{Q}_{0}}
we set

\langle(s,d),(s^{\prime},d^{\prime})\rangle_{A[T]}:=ss^{\prime}-s\cdot\langle\underline{\mathrm{dim}}T,d^{\prime}\rangle_{Q}+\langle d,d^{\prime}\rangle_{Q}.

Corollary 3.10
.

For the homological Euler-form of
A[T]
the following identity holds:

\langle\tilde{M},\tilde{N}\rangle_{A[T]}~=~\langle\underline{\mathrm{dim}}\tilde{M},\underline{\mathrm{dim}}\tilde{N}\rangle_{A[T]},

where
\tilde{M},\tilde{N}
are (finite-dimensional)
A[T]
-modules and
\langle.,.\rangle_{Q}
denotes the Euler-form of
Q
.

Proof.

The identity follows from the above discussion.
∎

4.
Varieties of representations of one-point extensions

For all standard notions on varieties of representations of algebras, we refer to
[
5
]
. Let
(s,d)
be a dimension vector of
\hat{Q}
. Using the isomorphism
A[T]\simeq k\hat{Q}/(\mathcal{R})
, we can realize the
variety of representations of
A[T]
with dimension
(s,d)
as a (Zariski-)closed subvariety of the variety of representations of
\hat{Q}
with dimension
(s,d)
denoted by
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(\hat{Q})
:

\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T])\overset{\mathrm{clsd}}{\subseteq}\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(\hat{Q}).

4.1.
The Zariski-tangent space

For
\tilde{M}\in\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T])
we have (see
[
5
, Example 3.10.]
):

\mathsf{T}_{\tilde{M}}\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T])~=~\mathrm{Der}_{\tilde{R}}\big(A[T],\mathrm{End}_{\tilde{R}}(\tilde{M})\big).

We set
t:=\underline{\mathrm{dim}}\,T
and get by using Theorem
3.7
:

Theorem 4.1
.

For
\tilde{M}=\big(M,V,f:T\otimes_{k}V\to M\big)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T])
we have

\dim\mathsf{T}_{\tilde{M}}\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}\big(A[T]\big)~=~\dim\mathrm{Rep}_{d}(Q)+s\cdot\langle t,d\rangle_{Q}+\dim\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),M).

4.2.
A well-behaved subvariety in the rigid case

We consider

\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}\big(A[T]\big)~:=~\bigg\{(f,M)\;:\;M\in\mathrm{Rep}_{d}(Q),\;\,\mathrm{rank}(f)=\gamma_{T^{s},d}\bigg\}

\overset{\text{open}}{\subseteq}\,\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}\big(A[T]\big),

where
\gamma_{T^{s},d}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
denotes the
general rank
of homomorphism from
T^{s}
to a representation of dimension
d
(see
[
12
, Section 5]
).

Theorem 4.2
.

If we assume
\gamma_{T^{s},d}=d
and
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(T,T)=0
, then
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}\big(A[T]\big)

is smooth and every component has dimension

\dim\mathrm{Rep}_{d}(Q)+s\cdot\langle\underline{\mathrm{dim}}\,T,d\rangle_{Q},

i.e.
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}\big(A[T]\big)
is a local complete intersection.

Proof.

By counting the explicit defining polynomial equations

\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T])\overset{\mathrm{clsd}}{\subseteq}\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(\hat{Q}),

induced by (
1
), we find that every (nonempty) component of
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T])
has dimension
\geq\dim\,\mathrm{Rep}_{d}(Q)+s\cdot\langle\underline{\mathrm{dim}}\,T,d\rangle_{Q}
.

On the other hand, for each
\tilde{M}\in\mathrm{Rep}^{\mathrm{full}}_{(s,d)}(A[T])
we have

\dim_{\tilde{M}}\mathrm{Rep}^{\mathrm{full}}_{(s,d)}(A[T])\leq\dim\mathsf{T}_{\tilde{M}}\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T]).

The claim follows immediately by using the dimension formula in Theorem
4.1
.
∎

4.3.
On homomorphisms from a fixed representation

We denote the open dense subset

\bigg\{M\in\mathrm{Rep}_{d}(Q)\;:\;\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(T,M)=\mathrm{hom}(T,d)~~\text{und}~~\gamma_{T,M}=\gamma_{T,d}\bigg\}

of
\mathrm{Rep}_{d}(Q)
by
\mathcal{V}_{T,d}
. Here,
\mathrm{hom}(T,d)
is the dimension of the space of homomorphisms from the fixed representation
T
to a general representation of dimension vector
d
(see
[
3
]
). Moreover,
\gamma_{T,M}
is the unique maximal rank of homomorphisms from
T
to
M
.

We consider the regular map

\tilde{\pi}:\mathrm{Rep}_{(1,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}\big(A[T]\big)\to\mathrm{Rep}_{d}(Q),\,(f,M)\mapsto M,

which induces a regular map

\pi:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}_{T,d},

where
\mathcal{V}
is the open preimage
\tilde{\pi}^{-1}(\mathcal{V}_{T,d})\subseteq\mathrm{Rep}_{(1,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}\big(A[T]\big)
.

Obviously
\mathcal{V}_{T,d}
is irreducible and the fibers of
\pi
are irreducible of dimension
\mathrm{hom}(T,d)
. So there is a unique irreducible component
\mathcal{V}_{0}
of
\mathcal{V}
of maximal dimension which dominates
\pi
, that is, we have
\overline{\pi(\mathcal{V}_{0})}=\mathcal{V}_{T,d}
(Proposition
4.7
)and

\dim\mathcal{V}=\dim\mathcal{V}_{0}=\dim\mathrm{Rep}_{d}(Q)+\mathrm{hom}(T,d).

Since the regular points in
\mathcal{V}
form an open dense subset, there is regular point
\tilde{M}
in the irreducible component
\mathcal{V}_{0}
, and we have:

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{V}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{\tilde{M}}\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\mathsf{T}_{\tilde{M}}\mathcal{V}~=~\dim\mathsf{T}_{\tilde{M}}\mathrm{Rep}_{(1,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}\big(A[T]\big).

Using the description of the tangent space by derivations, we thus find:

Theorem 4.3
.

We have

\mathrm{hom}(T,d)~=~\langle t,d\rangle_{Q}~+~\dim\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),M)

for a general
A
-module homomorphism
f:T\to M
.

As a side remark, we rediscover the following result of Schofield
[
12
]
and Crawley-Boevey
[
3
]
:

Corollary 4.4
.

We have

\mathrm{hom}(T,d)~=~\langle\gamma_{T,d},d\rangle_{Q}~+~\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),M)

for a general
A
-module homomorphism
f:T\to M
.

Proof.

Use the the Euler form of
Q
and the identity

\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),M)~-~\dim\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\mathrm{ker}(f),M)~~=~~\langle\underline{\mathrm{dim}}\,\mathrm{ker}(f),d\rangle.

∎

Corollary 4.5
.

If we assume
\gamma_{T,d}=d
and
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(T,T)=0
, then
\mathrm{hom}(T,d)~=~\langle t,d\rangle_{Q}
.

4.4.
An irreducible component in the rigid case

We need the following facts from algebraic geometry:

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a regular map of affine varieties. Then there is an open dense subset
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
such that for all
x\in\mathcal{U}

\mathsf{T}_{x}\pi^{-1}\big(\pi(x)\big)=\mathrm{ker}(\mathsf{d}\pi_{x}).

Proof.

See for example
[
4
]
.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a regular map of quasi-projective varieties.

If
\mathcal{Y}
is irreducible and all fibers of
f
are irreducible and of same dimension
d
(in particular
f
is surjective), then:

a)

There is a unique irreducible component
\mathcal{X}_{0}
of
\mathcal{X}
that dominates
\mathcal{Y}
, i.e.
\overline{f(\mathcal{X}_{0})}=\mathcal{Y}
.

b)

Each irreducible component
\mathcal{X}_{i}
of
\mathcal{X}
is a union of fibers of
f
. Its dimension is equal to
\dim\overline{f(\mathcal{X}_{i})}+d
.

In particular, we can conclude
\mathcal{X}
is irreducible if either of the following holds:

i)

\mathcal{X}
is equidimensional.

ii)

f
is closed.

Proof.

See
[
6
]
.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

If we assume
\gamma_{T^{s},d}=d
and
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(T,T)=0
, then
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}\big(A[T]\big)
is irreducible and smooth of dimension

\dim\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}\big(A[T]\big)~=~\dim\mathrm{Rep}_{d}(Q)+s\cdot\langle t,d\rangle_{Q}.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.2
, it remains to prove that
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}\big(A[T]\big)
is irreducible. We consider the dominant map

\tilde{\pi}:\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}\big(A[T]\big)\to\mathrm{Rep}_{d}(Q),\tilde{M}=(M,k^{s},f)\mapsto M.

For each
\tilde{M}=(M,k^{s},f)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}\big(A[T]\big)
the regular map

\mathrm{Rep}_{d}(Q)\to\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}\big(A[T]\big),\,N\mapsto(N,k^{s},0)

induces a split mono for the differential
\tilde{\pi}
in
\tilde{M}

\mathsf{d}\tilde{\pi}_{\tilde{M}}:\mathsf{T}_{\tilde{M}}\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}\big(A[T]\big)\to\mathsf{T}_{M}\mathrm{Rep}_{d}(Q),

i.e.
\mathsf{d}\tilde{\pi}_{\tilde{M}}
is surjective.
For each
\tilde{M}=(M,k^{s},f)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}\big(A[T]\big)
we have

\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(T^{s},M)~~\simeq~~\mathsf{T}_{\tilde{M}}\tilde{\pi}^{-1}\big(\tilde{\pi}(\tilde{M})\big).

There is an open dense subset
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}\big(A[T]\big)
such that for each
\tilde{M}\in\mathcal{U}
we have

\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(T^{s},M)~\simeq~\mathrm{ker}(\mathsf{d}\tilde{\pi}_{\tilde{M}})

(Theorem
4.6
). Since
\mathsf{d}\tilde{\pi}_{\tilde{M}}
is surjective, by using Theorem
4.5
we obtain:

\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(T^{s},M)~=~\mathrm{hom}(T^{s},d)

for each
\tilde{M}\in\mathcal{U}
.
So

\mathcal{U}~~\subseteq~~\bigg\{(M,k^{s},f)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}(A[T])\;:\;\mathrm{hom}(T^{s},M)=\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(T^{s},M)\bigg\}=:\mathcal{V}.

Since
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}(A[T])
is dense,

\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}(A[T])
is dense, too.

The map
\tilde{\pi}
induces a surjective regular map

\pi:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}_{T^{s},d},(M,k^{s},f)\mapsto M,

where
\mathcal{V}_{T^{s},d}\subseteq\mathrm{Rep}_{d}(Q)
is dense (and thus irreducible). All fibers of
\pi
are irreducible of equal dimension and
\mathcal{V}
is equidimensional, thus
\mathcal{V}
has to be irreducible by Theorem
4.7
.
Using
\overline{\mathcal{V}}=\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}(A[T])
, we can conclude the claim.
∎

5.
Semistability

For all notions concerning stability and moduli spaces of representations we refer to
[
9
]
. For an
A[T]
-module
\tilde{M}=(M,V,f:T\otimes_{k}V\to M)
we define its slope

\mu\big(\tilde{M}\big)~=~\mu\big(M,V,f\big):=~\frac{\dim V}{\dim V+\dim M}.

Definition 5.1
.

An
A[T]
-module
\tilde{M}=(M,V,f:T\otimes_{k}V\to M)
is called
semi-stable
(resp.
stable
) if

\mu(U)\leq\mu(\tilde{M})
(resp.
\mu(U)<\mu(\tilde{M})
) for all proper subrepresentation
0\neq U\subset\tilde{M}
.

5.1.
A first criterion

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\tilde{M}=(M,V,f:T\otimes_{k}V\to M)
be an
A[T]
-module with
\tilde{M}_{\infty}\neq 0
. Then
\tilde{M}
is (semi-)stable iff for all subspaces
W\subseteq\tilde{M}_{\infty}
the following inequality is fulfilled:

\dim M\cdot\frac{\dim W}{\dim V}\underset{(=)}{<}\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}\dim\left(\sum_{l=1}^{t_{i}}\tilde{M}_{\rho_{l,(i)}}(W)\right).

Proof.

For all subobjects
\tilde{U}=(U,W,g)\subsetneq\tilde{M}
, we have:

\mu(\tilde{U})\underset{(=)}{<}\mu(\tilde{M})\Leftrightarrow\dim M\cdot\frac{\dim W}{\dim V}\underset{(=)}{<}\dim U.

We can easily determine the total space of the smallest subobject
\overline{W}
of
\tilde{M}
containing a given
W\subset V
. Namely, we have

\overline{W}_{i}~=~\sum_{l=1}^{t_{i}}\tilde{M}_{\rho_{l,(i)}}(W)

for each
i\in Q_{0}
.
∎

5.2.
An observation on the Harder-Narasimhan filtration

At first we recall the notion of Harder-Narasimhan filtration.

Definition 5.3
.

a)

A dimension vector
(s,d)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}Q_{0}
is called
(semi-)stable
if there is a
(semi-)stable representation of
A[T]
with dimension vector
(s,d)
.

b)

A tuple

(s,d)^{*}~=~\big((s^{1},d^{1}),\ldots,(s^{r},d^{r})\big)~~\in\big(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}Q_{0}\big)^{r}

of dimension vectors is of
HN-type
if each
(s^{l},d^{l})
(
l=1,\ldots,r
) is semi-stable and

\mu(s^{1},d^{1})>\ldots>\mu(s^{r},d^{r}).

c)

A filtration

0=\tilde{M}_{0}\subset\tilde{M}_{1}\subset\ldots\subset\tilde{M}_{r}=\tilde{M}

of a representation
\tilde{M}
(of
A[T]
) is called Harder-Narasimhan (
HN
) if each quotient
\tilde{M}_{l}/\tilde{M}_{l-1}
(
l=1,\ldots,r
) is semi-stable and

\mu\big(\tilde{M}_{1}/\tilde{M}_{0}\big)>\mu\big(\tilde{M}_{2}/\tilde{M}_{1}\big)>\ldots>\mu\big(\tilde{M}_{r}/\tilde{M}_{r-1}\big).

Proposition 5.4
.

Every representation
\tilde{M}
of
A[T]
admits a unique Harder-Narasimhan filtration.

Proof.

See
[
9
]
.
∎

Definition 5.5
.

For a HN type
(s,d)^{*}
we denote by

\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)^{*}}^{HN}(A[T])\subseteq\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T])

the subset of representations whose HN filtration is of type
(s,d)^{*}
.
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)*}^{HN}(A[T])
is called
HN stratrum
for the HN type
(s,d)^{*}
.
More generally, we denote by

Corollary 5.7
.

For
(s,d)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
we have the following connection between the semi-stable representations and full representations:

\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}(A[T])~\subseteq~\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}(A[T]).

5.3.
Geometric consequences for the moduli space

The linear algebraic group

\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}:=\Gl_{s}(k)\times\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\Gl_{d_{i}}(k)

acts on
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(\hat{Q})
via the base change action

(g_{i})_{i}\cdot(\tilde{M}_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\hat{Q}_{1}}=\big(g_{j}\tilde{M}_{\alpha}g_{i}^{-1}\big)_{\alpha:i\to j}.

\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T])
is stable under this
\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}
-action. By definition, the
\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}
-orbits in
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T])
correspond bijectively to isomorphism classes
[\tilde{M}]
of representations of
A[T]
of dimension vector
(s,d)
. We consider the stability function
\tilde{\Theta}
for
\hat{Q}
given by
\tilde{\Theta}_{\infty}=1
and
\tilde{\Theta}_{i}=0
for
i\in Q_{0}
. The associated slope function on representations of
\hat{Q}
coincides with the slope function
\mu
on
A[T]
-modules. This allows us to define moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}\big(A[T]\big)
resp.
\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{st}}\big(A[T]\big)
as the algebraic quotient of
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}(A[T])
, resp. the geometric quotient of
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{st}}(A[T])
, by
\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}
.

Theorem 5.8
.

Assume
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(T,T)=0
and
\gamma_{T^{s},d}=d
.

If
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{st}}\big(A[T]\big)\neq\emptyset
, then both
\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}\big(A[T]\big)
and
\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{st}}\big(A[T]\big)
are irreducible and smooth of dimension

\dim\,\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}\big(A[T]\big)~=~1-\langle(s,d),(s,d)\rangle_{A[T]}.

Proof.

We calculate fibre dimensions for the geometric quotient

\pi:\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{st}}\big(A[T]\big)\to\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{st}}\big(A[T]\big),

and use Corollary
5.7
, Theorem
3.6
and the fact that the endomorphism rings of stable representations are trivial.
∎

Next, we introduce the Harder-Narasimhan stratification. Note that the term stratification is used in a weak sense, meaning a finite decomposition of a variety into locally closed subsets.

5.4.
Harder-Narasimhan stratification

In this section we write
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}
for
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}(A[T])
to simplify notation.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let assume
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(T,T)=0
and
\gamma_{T^{s},d}=d
.

The HN-strata for the HN-types

(s,d)^{*}~=~\big((s^{1},d^{1}),\ldots,(s^{r},d^{r})\big)

with weight
(s,d)
, i.e.
(s,d)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(s^{i},d^{i})
, and
s^{r}\neq 0
define a stratification of
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}
.

The codimension of
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)^{*}}^{HN}
in
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}
is given by:

-\sum_{k<l}\langle(s^{k},d^{k}),(s^{l},d^{l})\rangle_{A[T]}.

Proof.

Let
F^{*}:0=F^{0}\subset F^{1}\subset\ldots\subset F^{r}
be a flag of type
(s,d)^{*}
in the
\hat{Q}_{0}
-graded vector space
k^{d}\times k^{s}=\oplus_{i\in Q_{0}}k^{d_{i}}~\times~k^{s}
, i.e.
F^{l}/F^{l-1}\simeq k^{d^{l}}\times k^{s^{l}}
for
l=1,\ldots,r
, and denote by
F_{i}^{l}
the
i
-component of
F^{l}
.

Denote by
\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{(s,d)^{*}}
the closed subvariety of
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T])
of representations
\tilde{M}
which are compatible with
F^{*}
, i.e.

\tilde{M}_{\gamma}(F_{i}^{l})\subset F_{j}^{l}
for
l=1,\ldots,r
and for all arrows
(\gamma:i\to j)
in
\hat{Q}
.
We have the regular map

\tilde{p}_{(s,d)^{*}}:\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{(s,d)^{*}}\to\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{1},d^{1})}(A[T])\times\ldots\times\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{r},d^{r})}(A[T])

given by the projection
\tilde{p}_{(s,d)^{*}}
mapping
\tilde{M}\in\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{(s,d)^{*}}
to the sequence of subquotients with respect to
F^{*}
.
The map
\tilde{p}_{(s,d)^{*}}
induces a regular map

p_{(s,d)^{*}}:\mathcal{Z}_{(s,d)^{*}}\to\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{1},d^{1})}\times\ldots\times\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{r},d^{r})},

where

\mathcal{Z}_{(s,d)^{*}}:=\tilde{p}_{(s,d)^{*}}^{-1}\big(\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{1},d^{1})}\times\ldots\times\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{r},d^{r})}\big)\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{(s,d)^{*}}~~~\text{open.}

A minute reflection shows that

\mathcal{Z}_{(s,d)^{*}}\subset\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)},

and it is a locally closed subset. By Theorem
4.8
,

\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)},\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{1},d^{1})},\ldots,\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{r},d^{r})}~~~\text{are irreducible.}

We set

e^{*}_{2}~:=~\big((s^{1},d^{1}),(s^{2},d^{2})\big).

We obtain the regular map

p_{e^{*}_{2}}:\mathcal{Z}_{e^{*}_{2}}\to\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{1},d^{1})}\times\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{2},d^{2})},

with fibers at
(\tilde{M}_{1},\tilde{M}_{2})\in\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{1},d^{1})}\times\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{2},d^{2})}
given by

p_{e^{*}_{2}}^{-1}(\tilde{M}_{1},\tilde{M}_{2})~=~\mathrm{Der}_{\tilde{R}}\big(B,\mathrm{Hom}_{\tilde{R}}(\tilde{M}_{2},\tilde{M}_{1})\big).

Thus all fibers are irreducible and of equal dimension (Corollary
5.7
and
3.8
). By Theorem
4.7
we have

\dim\mathcal{Z}_{e^{*}_{2}}~=~\dim p_{e^{*}_{2}}^{-1}(\tilde{M}_{1},\tilde{M}_{2})+\dim\left(\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{1},d^{1})}\times\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{2},d^{2})}\right).

Now we set

\displaystyle\overline{e}^{*}_{3}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\big((s^{1},d^{1})+(s^{2},d^{2}),(s^{3},d^{3})\big),

\displaystyle e^{*}_{3}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\big((s^{1},d^{1}),(s^{2},d^{2}),(s^{3},d^{3})\big).

Therefore we obtain the regular map

p_{\overline{e}^{*}_{3}}:\mathcal{Z}_{\overline{e}^{*}_{3}}\to\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{1},d^{1})+(s^{2},d^{2})}\times\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{3},d^{3})}.

Since

Z_{e^{*}_{3}}=p_{\overline{e}^{*}_{3}}^{-1}(\mathcal{Z}_{e^{*}_{2}}\times\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{3},d^{3})}),

the map
p_{\overline{e}^{*}_{3}}
induces a regular map

\mathcal{Z}_{e^{*}_{3}}\to\mathcal{Z}_{e^{*}_{2}}\times\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{3},d^{3})}.

As above, we see that this regular map has irreducible fibers of equal dimension, that is, for
(\tilde{M}_{1},\tilde{M}_{3})\in\mathcal{Z}_{e^{*}_{2}}\times\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{3},d^{3})}
, we have:

\dim\mathcal{Z}_{e^{*}_{3}}~=~\dim\mathrm{Der}_{\tilde{R}}\big(B,\mathrm{Hom}_{\tilde{R}}(\tilde{M}_{3},\tilde{M}_{1})\big)+\dim\mathcal{Z}_{e^{*}_{2}}+\dim\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{3},d^{3})}.

Inductively, we obtain in this way a regular map

\mathcal{Z}_{e^{*}_{r}}\to\mathcal{Z}_{e^{*}_{r-1}}\times\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{r},d^{r})}

with irreducible fibers of equal dimension, where

\displaystyle e^{*}_{r-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big((s^{1},d^{1}),\ldots,(s^{r-1},d^{r-1})\big),

\displaystyle e^{*}_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(s,d)^{*}.

Summing up, for
(\tilde{M}_{1},\tilde{M}_{r})\in\mathcal{Z}_{e^{*}_{r-1}}\times\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{r},d^{r})}
this yields:

\dim\mathcal{Z}_{(s,d)^{*}}~=~\dim\mathrm{Der}_{\tilde{R}}\big(B,\mathrm{Hom}_{\tilde{R}}(\tilde{M}_{r},\tilde{M}_{1})\big)+\dim\mathcal{Z}_{e^{*}_{r-1}}+\dim\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{r},d^{r})}.

Together with the formula in Corollary
3.8

\dim\mathcal{Z}_{(s,d)^{*}}~=~\sum_{n<l}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in Q_{1}\\
\alpha:i\to j\end{subarray}}d_{i}^{l}d_{j}^{n}+s^{l}\langle t,d^{n}\rangle_{Q}~~+~~\sum_{i=1}^{r}\dim\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{i},d^{i})}.

To simplify the notation in the following, we write
\mathcal{Z}
(resp.
p
) for
\mathcal{Z}_{(s,d)^{*}}
(resp.
p_{(s,d)^{*}}
).
The preimage of

\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{1},d^{1})}^{\mathrm{sst}}\times\ldots\times\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{r},d^{r})}^{\mathrm{sst}}

under
p
gives us an open subvariety
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
of
\mathcal{Z}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}
. Since the varieties

\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{1},d^{1})},\ldots,\mathrm{Rep}_{(s^{r},d^{r})}

are irreducible, we see in a similar manner to
\mathcal{Z}
that
\dim\mathcal{Z}_{0}=\dim\mathcal{Z}
holds.

The action of
\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}
on
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T])
induces actions of the parabolic subgroup
\mathcal{P}_{(s,d)^{*}}
of
\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}
, consisting of elements fixing the flag
F^{*}
, on
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
and
\mathcal{Z}
. The image of the associated fiber bundle
\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}\times^{\mathcal{P}_{(s,d)^{*}}}\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}
under the action morphism
m
equals
\mathrm{Rep}^{(s,d)^{*}}_{(s,d)}(A[T])
, which is thus a closed subvariety of
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T])
. The image of
\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}\times^{\mathcal{P}_{(s,d)^{*}}}\mathcal{Z}_{0}
under
m
equals
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)^{*}}^{HN}
, and
\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}\times^{\mathcal{P}_{(s,d)^{*}}}\mathcal{Z}_{0}
is the full preimage. By the uniqueness of the HN filtration, the morphism
m
is bijective over
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)^{*}}^{HN}
, which therefore is a locally closed subvariety of
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T])
.

The canonical map
\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}\times^{\mathcal{P}_{(s,d)^{*}}}\mathcal{Z}_{0}\to\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}\big/\mathcal{P}_{(s,d)^{*}}
is (Zariski) locally trivial. Therefore

\dim\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}\times^{\mathcal{P}_{(s,d)^{*}}}\mathcal{Z}_{0}~~~=~~(\dim\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}~-~\mathcal{P}_{(s,d)^{*}})~~+~~\dim\mathcal{Z}_{0}.

The codimension of
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)^{*}}^{HN}
in
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}
is now easily computed as

-\sum_{n<l}\langle(s^{n},d^{n}),(s^{l},d^{l})\rangle_{A[T]},

using the identity
\langle d,d\rangle_{Q}=\dim\mathcal{G}_{d}-\dim\mathrm{Rep}_{d}(Q)
and the above description of
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
.
∎

From this description, we can derive a recursive criterion for the existence of semi-stable representations:

Theorem 5.10
.

Let us assume
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(T,T)=0
.

A dimension type
(s,d)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}Q_{0}
is semi-stable if and only if
\gamma_{T^{s},d}=d
and there exists no HN type

(s,d)^{*}~=~\big((s^{1},d^{1}),\ldots,(s^{r},d^{r})\big)

with weight
(s,d)
and
s^{r}\neq 0
such that

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\langle(s^{n},d^{n}),(s^{l},d^{l})\rangle_{A[T]}~=~0\;\;\;\;\;\text{(for all $1\leq n<l\leq r$)}.

Proof.

Let

H:=\left\{(s,d)^{*}\,:\,\exists\,r\in\mathbb{N}_{>1},\,\text{$(s,d)^{*}=\big((s^{1},d^{1}),\ldots,(s^{r},d^{r})\big)$ is of HN-type}\right.

\left.\text{with weight $(s,d)$ and $s^{r}\neq 0$}\right\}.

Obviously
H
is a finite set. Using Theorem
5.6
we get

\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}~~=~~\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}~\sqcup\bigsqcup_{(s,d)^{*}\in H}\mathrm{Rep}^{HN}_{(s,d)^{*}}.

If
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}\neq\emptyset
,
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}
is of equal dimension like
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}
. So

\mathrm{codim}_{\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}}\mathrm{Rep}^{HN}_{(s,d)^{*}}>0

for
(s,d)^{*}\in H
implies
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}\neq\emptyset
.

Let
n<l
. Since
(s^{n},d^{n})
resp.
(s^{l},d^{l})
are semi-stable, we find semi-stable representations
\tilde{N}
resp.
\tilde{M}

of
A[T]
with
\underline{\mathrm{dim}}\,\tilde{N}=(s^{n},d^{n})
resp.
\underline{\mathrm{dim}}\,\tilde{M}=(s^{l},d^{l})
and by using Corollary
3.6
and
5.7
we get the relation

\langle(s^{n},d^{n}),(s^{l},d^{l})\rangle_{A[T]}~~=~~\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{A[T]}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})~-~\dim\mathrm{Ext}_{A[T]}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M}).

From
\mu(s^{n},d^{n})>\mu(s^{l},d^{l})
we deduce
\mathrm{Hom}_{A[T]}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})=0
, in particular

\langle(s^{n},d^{n}),(s^{l},d^{l})\rangle_{A[T]}~~=~~~-~\dim\mathrm{Ext}_{A[T]}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M}).

So the equation

\sum_{n<l}\langle(s^{n},d^{n}),(s^{l},d^{l})\rangle_{A[T]}~~=~~0

is fulfilled if and only if

\langle(s^{n},d^{n}),(s^{l},d^{l})\rangle_{A[T]}~=~0

for all
1\leq n<l\leq r
.
∎

6.
Generating semi-invariants

In this section we assume that
Q
is an acyclic quiver. We determine a set of functions generating the ring of semi-invariants for given dimension vector
(s,d)\in\mathbb{N}\hat{Q}_{0}
on
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T])
under the
\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}
base change action.

We start with a general observation:

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a linear reductive group and
\mathcal{X}
an affine
\mathcal{G}
-variety. Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
be a closed and
\mathcal{G}
-stable subset. Then, for every semi-invariant function
f:\mathcal{A}\to k
there is a semi-invariant
\tilde{f}:\mathcal{X}\to k
such that
\tilde{f}|_{\mathcal{A}}=f
holds.

Proof.

Let
\chi
be a character of
\mathcal{G}
.
We consider the action of
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathcal{X}\times k
given by

g.(x,\lambda)~:=~(gx,\chi(g)\lambda),

where
(x,\lambda)\in\mathcal{X}\times k,\,g\in\mathcal{G}
.

Since
\mathcal{A}\times k~\subseteq~\mathcal{X}\times k
is closed and
\mathcal{G}
-stable, the categorical quotient
(\mathcal{A}\times k)//\mathcal{G}~\subseteq~(\mathcal{X}\times k)//\mathcal{G}
is closed, i.e.

k[\mathcal{X}\times k]^{\mathcal{G}}\to k[\mathcal{A}\times k]^{\mathcal{G}},\,f\mapsto f|_{\mathcal{A}\times k}

is surjective.
∎

In the following, to simplify the notation we denote by
\hat{A}
the path algebra of the one-point extended quiver
\hat{Q}
.

Let
\tilde{N}
be a representation of
\hat{Q}
with projective resolution

0\to\bigoplus_{v\in\hat{Q}_{0}}\hat{A}e_{v}^{b(v)}\xrightarrow{\theta}\bigoplus_{v\in\hat{Q}_{0}}\hat{A}e_{v}^{a(v)}\to\tilde{N}\to 0.

For
\tilde{M}\in\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(\hat{Q})
we apply the functor
\mathrm{Hom}_{\hat{A}}(?,\tilde{M})
to this resolution and get

0\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\hat{A}}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\hat{A}}(\bigoplus_{v\in\hat{Q}_{0}}\hat{A}e_{v}^{a(v)},\tilde{M})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}_{\hat{A}}(\theta,\tilde{M})}

\mathrm{Hom}_{\hat{A}}(\bigoplus_{v\in\hat{Q}_{0}}\hat{A}e_{v}^{b(v)},\tilde{M})\to\mathrm{Ext}_{\hat{A}}(\tilde{N},\tilde{M})\to 0.

The condition
\langle\underline{\mathrm{dim}}\,\tilde{N},(s,d)\rangle_{\hat{Q}}=0
is equivalent to

\sum_{v\in\hat{Q}_{0}}a(v)\hat{d}_{v}~=~\sum_{v\in\hat{Q}_{0}}b(v)\hat{d}_{v},

that is, in this case we end up with a linear map between vector spaces of equal dimension. Schofield and Van den Bergh proved (
[
13
]
) that all semi-invariant

functions arise as linear combination of functions

\omega_{\tilde{N}}:\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(\hat{Q})\to k,\,\tilde{M}\mapsto\det(\mathrm{Hom}_{{\hat{A}}}(\theta,\tilde{M}))

induced by representations
\tilde{N}
of
\hat{Q}
with
\langle\underline{\mathrm{dim}}\,\tilde{N},(s,d)\rangle_{\hat{Q}}=0
.

Using the relation
\hat{A}\twoheadrightarrow A[T]
we can conclude with Lemma
6.1
:

The functions

for representations
\tilde{N}
of

such that
\langle\underline{\mathrm{dim}}\,\tilde{N},(s,d)\rangle_{\hat{Q}}=0

\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}(A[T])

7.
Higher Gel’fand MacPherson correspondence

We first recall the definition of quiver Grassmannians (see for example
[
1
]
). For a quiver
Q
, a representation
X
of
Q
of dimension vector
d
and another dimension vector
e\leq d
, we define
\mathrm{Gr}^{e}_{Q}(X)
as the set of subrepresentations
U
of
X
of dimension vector
d-e
. This carries a natural scheme structure as the geometric quotient by the base change group
\mathcal{G}_{d}
of the set
{\rm Hom}_{Q}(X,e)_{e}
of surjections (that is, rank
e
maps) from
X
to a representation of dimension vector
e
.

From now on, we assume
\mathrm{End}_{A}(T)=k
,
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(T,T)=0
and
\gamma_{T^{s},d}=d
.

In this case, the regular map

\phi:\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}(A[T])\to\mathrm{Hom}_{Q}(T^{s},d)_{d},\big(M,f:T^{s}\to M\big)\mapsto f,

which is always a locally trivial fiber bundle (
[
3
, Lemma 1.2.]
), has single element fibres, and thus is an isomorphism of varieties. Moreover, we have the
\mathcal{G}_{d}
-bundle

\kappa:\mathrm{Hom}_{Q}(T^{s},d)_{d}\to\mathrm{Gr}^{d}_{Q}(T^{s}),(\theta_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}\mapsto\mathrm{ker}(\oplus_{i\in Q_{0}}\theta_{i}).

All in all, we achieve a
\mathcal{G}_{d}
-bundle

\psi:\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}(A[T])\to\mathrm{Gr}^{d,\mathrm{sst}}_{Q}(T^{s}),

where
\mathrm{Gr}^{d,\mathrm{sst}}_{Q}(T^{s}):=\kappa\circ\phi\bigg(\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}(A[T])\bigg)\subseteq\mathrm{Gr}^{d}_{Q}(T^{s})
is open.

We thus have an induced map

\pi:\mathrm{Gr}^{d,\mathrm{sst}}_{Q}(T^{s})\to\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}\big(A[T]\big).

The linear reductive group
\mathrm{Aut}_{Q}(T^{s})
acts naturally on
\mathrm{Gr}_{Q}^{d}(T^{s})
and for
\big[\tilde{M}\big]\in\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}\big(A[T]\big)
clearly we have

\pi^{-1}\big([\tilde{M}]\big)=\mathrm{Aut}_{Q}(T^{s}).[(M,f)].

We thus find:

Theorem 7.1

(Higher Gel’fand MacPherson correspondence)
.

There is an isomorphism of varieties:

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{sst}}_{(s,d)}\big(A[T]\big)~\simeq~\mathrm{Gr}^{d,\mathrm{sst}}_{Q}(T^{s})\big/\mathrm{Aut}_{Q}(T^{s}).

Proof.

By Theorem
5.8

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{sst}}_{(s,d)}\big(A[T]\big)
is smooth, in particularly normal. Theorem
4.8
shows that the quiver Grassmanian of a representation without self-extensions is irreducible. Since
\pi
is surjective the claim follows from
[
7
, Theorem 4.2]
.
∎

8.
Motive of the moduli space

In this section, we assume
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(T,T)=0
and
\gamma_{T^{s},d}=d
.

As an application of the arguments in Theorem
5.9
and the explicit recursive formula given there, we derive a formula for the motive of the (smooth) moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}(\mathbb{C}):=\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}\big(A[T]\big)
(over
\mathbb{C}
).

To achieve this, we will follow closely the strategy of
[
8
, Section 6]
, but replace counts of rational points over finite fields by motives as in the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 3.5]
. We denote by
K_{0}({\rm Var}/\mathbb{C})
the free abelian group generated by representatives
[X]
of isomorphism classes of complex varieties
X
, modulo the relation
[X]=[C]+[U]
if
C
is isomorphic to a closed subvariety of
X
with open complement isomorphic to
U
. Multiplication in
K_{0}({\rm Var}/\mathbb{C})
is given by
[X]\cdot[Y]=[X\times Y]
. We denote by
\mathbb{L}
the class of the affine line; the following calculations will be performed in the localization

\mathcal{K}=K_{0}({\rm Var}/\mathbb{C})[\mathbb{L}^{-1},(1-\mathbb{L}^{n})^{-1}\,:\,n\geq 1].

At this point, we recall a notation from the subsection
5.4
. Let

H:=\left\{(s,d)^{*}\,:\,\exists\,r\in\mathbb{N}_{>1},\,\text{$(s,d)^{*}=\big((s^{1},d^{1}),\ldots,(s^{r},d^{r})\big)$ is of HN-type}\right.

\left.\text{with weight $(s,d)$ and $s^{r}\neq 0$}\right\}.~~~~~~~

In this section, we write
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}
for
\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{full}}(A[T])
and
\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}
for
\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}\big(A[T]\big)
.

8.1.
Motive

Obviously
H
is finite and by Theorem
5.6
we have

\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}~~=~~\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}~\sqcup\bigsqcup_{(s,d)^{*}\in H}\mathrm{Rep}^{HN}_{(s,d)^{*}}

and thus

[\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}]~~=~~[\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}]~-~\sum_{(s,d)^{*}\in H}[\mathrm{Rep}^{HN}_{(s,d)^{*}}]

in
\mathcal{K}
. We then find

\frac{[\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}]}{[\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}]}~~=~~\frac{[\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}]}{[\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}]}~-~\sum_{(s,d)^{*}\in H}\frac{[\mathrm{Rep}^{HN}_{(s,d)^{*}}]}{[\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}]}.

Fix
(s,d)^{*}\in H
. As in the proof of Theorem
5.9
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rep}^{HN}_{(s,d)^{*}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}\times^{\mathcal{P}_{(s,d)^{*}}}\mathcal{Z}_{0}\,,

\displaystyle{[\mathcal{Z}_{0}]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}^{\sum_{n<l}\sum_{\alpha:i\to j}d_{i}^{l}d_{j}^{n}+s^{l}\langle t,d^{n}\rangle_{Q}}~~\cdot~~\prod_{i=1}^{r}[\mathrm{Rep}_{(d^{i},s^{i})}^{\mathrm{sst}}],

and we arrive at

[\mathrm{Rep}^{HN}_{(s,d)^{*}}]~~=~~\frac{[\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}]}{[\mathcal{P}_{(s,d)^{*}}]}~\cdot~\mathbb{L}^{\sum_{n<l}\sum_{\alpha:i\to j}d_{i}^{l}d_{j}^{n}+s^{l}\langle t,d^{n}\rangle_{Q}}~~\cdot~~\prod_{i=1}^{r}[\mathrm{Rep}_{(d^{i},s^{i})}^{\mathrm{sst}}].

This provides us with the motivic HN-recursion:

Theorem 8.1
.

\frac{[\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}]}{[\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}]}~~~=~~~\frac{[\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}]}{[\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}]}~~~-~~\sum_{(s,d)^{*}\in H}\frac{\mathbb{L}^{\sum_{n<l}\sum_{\alpha:i\to j}d_{i}^{l}d_{j}^{n}+s^{l}\langle t,d^{n}\rangle_{Q}}}{[\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)^{*}}]}~~\cdot~~\prod_{i=1}^{r}[\mathrm{Rep}_{(d^{i},s^{i})}^{\mathrm{sst}}]\;\;\,.

Using the arguments of
[
8
, Theorem 6.7.]
, we see that the following relation

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbf{H}^{i}\big(\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}},\mathbb{Q}\big)\mathbb{L}^{i/2}~~=~~[\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}]

holds and we obtain:

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
(s,d)
be a dimension vector such that semi-stability and stability coincide. Then, with
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}:=\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}/k^{\times}
we have:

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbf{H}^{i}\big(\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}},\mathbb{Q}\big)\mathbb{L}^{i/2}~=~\frac{[\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}]}{[\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}]}~~~~-

(\mathbb{L}-1)\sum_{(s,d)^{*}\in H}\frac{1}{[\mathcal{P}_{(s,d)^{*}}]}\cdot\mathbb{L}^{\sum_{n<l}\sum_{\alpha:i\to j}d_{i}^{l}d_{j}^{n}+s^{l}\langle t,d^{n}\rangle_{Q}}~~\cdot~~\prod_{i=1}^{r}[\mathrm{Rep}_{(d^{i},s^{i})}^{\mathrm{sst}}]\;\;\,.

Proof.

As in
[
8
, Proposition 6.6.]
we have

[\mathcal{M}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}]~=~\frac{[\mathrm{Rep}_{(s,d)}^{\mathrm{sst}}]}{[\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{(s,d)}]},

and the claim follows from the previous theorem.
∎

Lemma 8.3

Let

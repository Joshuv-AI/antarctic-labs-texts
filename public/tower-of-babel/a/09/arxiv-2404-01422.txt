On Strong Bounds for Trotter and Zeno Product Formulas with Bosonic Applications
Tim Möbus

Abstract
The Trotter product formula and the quantum Zeno effect are both indispensable tools for constructing time-evolutions using experimentally feasible building blocks. In this work, we discuss assumptions under which quantitative bounds can be proven in the strong operator topology on Banach spaces and provide natural bosonic examples. Specially, we assume the existence of a continuously embedded Banach space, which relatively bounds the involved generators and creates an invariant subspace of the limiting semigroup with a stable restriction. The slightly stronger assumption of admissible subspaces is well-recognized in the realm of hyperbolic evolution systems (time-dependent semigroups), to which the results are extended. By assuming access to a hierarchy of continuously embedded Banach spaces, Suzuki-higher-order bounds can be demonstrated. In bosonic applications, these embedded Banach spaces naturally arise through the number operator, leading to a diverse set of examples encompassing notable instances such as the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup and multi-photon driven dissipation used in bosonic error correction.

2.1
Strongly continuous semigroups

A quantum mechanical evolution is often described by a differential equation called the master equation, for which strongly continuous (
C_{0}
-)semigroups and
(C_{0})
-evolution systems constitute an important toolbox for its solution theory. More details can for example be found in

[

, Thm. II.1.4]

(8)

\mathcal{L}(x)=\lim\limits_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}(T_{t}(x)-x)\,,

for any

(9)

x\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:t\mapsto T_{t}(x)\text{ differentiable on }\mathbbm{R}_{\geq 0}\}\,.

This justifies the notation
T_{t}=e^{t\mathcal{L}}

-semigroup satisfies

(10)

\|T_{t}\|_{\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}}\leq ce^{\omega t}

\omega\geq 0

\frac{d}{dt}x(t)=\mathcal{L}(x(t))\quad x(0)\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})\quad\text{and}\quad t\geq 0\,.

The above equation can be integrated in the following sense. For

One way to verify that a vector-valued function on a compact interval is integrable is to prove continuity of the function

For

4
Trotter-like product formulas

In this section, we discuss quantitative bounds for Trotter-like product formulas. We begin with the fundamental Trotter product formula
[
57
]
and enhance the convergence rate through the Suzuki approach
[
53
]
. Additionally, we generalize the involved semigroups to evolution systems. Finally, we delve into bosonic examples, in particular form bosonic Hamiltonian learning, as well as error correction. Most of our proofs rely on a telescopic sum approach known for example from proving the Lie-Trotter product formula
[
45
,
59
]
and generalized in the following.

Lemma 4.1

Let
I_{T}\ni(t,s)\mapsto F(t,s)\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
be a vector-valued map with
\|F(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}}\leq 1
and
F(s,s)=\mathbbm{1}
for all
(t,s)\in I_{T}\coloneqq\{(t,s)\,|\,T\geq t\geq s\geq 0\}
. Moreover, let
T(t,s)
be an evolution system, then for all
(t,s)\in I_{T}

\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}F(s_{j},s_{j-1})x-T(t,s)x\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq n\max_{j\in\{1,...,n\}}\left\{\|\left(F(s_{j},s_{j-1})-T(s_{j},s_{j-1})\right)T(s_{j-1},s_{0})x\|_{\mathcal{X}}\right\}\,,

for any increasing sequence
s=s_{0}<s_{1}<...<s_{n-1}<s_{n}=t
. Note that the product over
F(s_{j},s_{j-1})
is in decreasing order from the left to the right.

Proof.

The statement directly follows by the following telescopic sum
[
45
,
59
]
:

(33)

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}F_{j}x-\prod_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}F_{j}\right)(F_{k}-T_{k})\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}T_{j}\right)x\,,

where the products are in decreasing order from the left to the right. Then, the definition
T_{j}=T(s_{j},s_{j-1})
, the semigroup property, i.e.

T(t,s)=T(s_{n},s_{n-1})\circ T(s_{n-1},s_{n-2})\circ\cdots\circ T(s_{1},s_{0})\eqqcolon\prod_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}\,,

and the contractivity of
F(t,s)
combined with the submultiplicativity of the norm finish the proof.
∎

As mentioned, the quantum Zeno effect is an operator product formula of a contractive projection

\displaystyle V(s_{j},s_{j-1})Px_{j}-T(s_{j},s_{j-1})Px_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{1}\Biggl(PV(s_{j},s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}))\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1})}P-T(s_{j},s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}))P\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1})}P\Biggr)x_{j}d\tau_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\Biggl(\tau_{1}PV(s_{j},s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}\tau_{2}))\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}\tau_{2})}\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1})}P

\displaystyle-\tau_{1}T(s_{j},s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}\tau_{2}))P\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}\tau_{2})}P\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1})}P\Biggr)x_{j}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{1}\,,

where
x_{j}\coloneqq PT(s_{j-1},s_{0})Px
. Note that the integral above is again well-defined by Lemma
2.1
as well as
[
12
, Lem. B.15]
. As in the proof of Proposition
5.1
, we use the assumption to show

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\tau_{1}PV(s_{j},s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}))\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}\tau_{2})}\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1})}Px_{j}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{1}\|_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{1}}{2}\|T(s_{j-1},s_{0})Px\|_{\mathcal{Y}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}e^{\omega}\|Px\|_{\mathcal{Y}}

for
c_{1}=\frac{a_{1},b_{1}}{2}
with
a_{1},b_{1}\geq 0
and similarly

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\tau_{1}T(s_{j},s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}))P\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}\tau_{2})}P\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1})}Px_{j}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{1}\|_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}e^{\omega}\|x_{j}\|_{\mathcal{Y}}

for
c_{2}=\frac{a_{2},b_{2}}{2}
with
a_{2},b_{2}\geq 0
. Rescaling and combining both bounds show

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}PV(s_{j},s_{j-1})

\displaystyle Px-T(t,0)Px\Bigr\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\frac{ct^{2}}{n}e^{t\omega}\|Px\|_{\mathcal{Y}}

with
c=2\max\{c_{1},c_{2}\}
.
∎

5.2
Time-dependent Zeno with general measurement

In the next step, we generalize Theorem I in
[
37
]
to
C_{0}
-evolution systems. For that, we use again the help of a reference subspace
\mathcal{Y}
:

Theorem 5.3

Let
I_{T}\ni(t,s)\mapsto V(t,s)\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
be a
C_{0}
-evolution system with generator family
(\mathcal{L}_{t},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}_{t}))_{t\in[0,T]}
,
M\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
a contraction, and
P
a projection satisfying

(46)

\norm{M^n-P}_{\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}}\leq\delta^{n}

for
\delta\in(0,1)
and all
n\in\mathbbm{N}
. Moreover, we assume the asymptotic Zeno condition:

(47)

\norm{PV(t,s)(\1-P)}_{\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}}\leq(t-s)b\quad\text{and}\quad\norm{(\1-P) V(t,s)P}_{\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}}\leq(t-s)b

for
b\geq 0
and all
t\in[0,T]
. Furthermore, there is a Banach space
(\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{X},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Y}})
invariant under
P
so that the closure of
(P\mathcal{L}_{t}P,\mathcal{Y})_{t\in[0,T]}
generates a unique contractive
C_{0}
-evolution system
T(t,s)
for
(t,s)\in I_{T}
commuting with
P
. Moreover, assume that
\mathcal{L}_{t}P
,
P\mathcal{L}_{t}P
, and
P\mathcal{L}_{t}P\mathcal{L}_{s}P
are well-defined on
\mathcal{Y}
and relatively
\mathcal{Y}
-bounded for all
(t,s)\in I_{T}
, and
(\mathcal{Y},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Y}})
is a
P\mathcal{L}_{t}P
-stable subspace, in particular
\|T(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}}\leq\tilde{c}e^{(t-s)\omega}
. Then, there are
c_{1},c_{2}\geq 0
so that

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}MV(s_{j},s_{j-1})x-T(t,0)Px\Bigr\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\left(\delta^{n}+\frac{c_{1}(t+t^{2})}{n}\right)\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}}+c_{2}\frac{t^{2}}{n}e^{t\omega}\|Px\|_{\mathcal{Y}}

for all
t\geq 0
,
s_{0}=0<\frac{t}{n}<...<\frac{t(n-1)}{n}<t=s_{n}
,
x\in\mathcal{Y}
and
n\in\mathbbm{N}
.

Proof.

As in the proof of
[
37
, Thm. 5.1]
, we approximate
M
by
P
in the first step. To do so, we follow the proof of Lemma 5.2 in
[
37
]
. For that

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}

\displaystyle(P+(\mathbbm{1}-P)M)V(s_{j},s_{j-1})x-\prod_{j=1}^{n}PV(s_{j},s_{j-1})x\Bigr\|_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|\sum_{\tau\in\{0,1\}^{n}\backslash(1,...,1)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\tau_{j}P+(1-\tau_{j})(\mathbbm{1}-P)M)V(s_{j},s_{j-1})x\Bigr\|_{\mathcal{X}}\,.

Next, we analyse how many transitions in terms of
PV(s_{j},s_{j-1})(1-P)
exist in the above sum. This is specified in Lemma 5.4 in
[
37
]
. Next, we use the bounds

\|M(\mathbbm{1}-P)V(s_{j},s_{j-1})\|_{\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}}\leq\delta\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|M(\mathbbm{1}-P)V(s_{j},s_{j-1})PMV(s_{j+1},s_{j})\|\leq\delta\frac{b}{n}

and follow the calculations on
[
37
, p. 12]
to show

(48)

\norm{\prod_{j=1}^n MV(s_{j},s_{j-1})x-\prod_{j=1}^n PV(s_{j},s_{j-1})Px}_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\left(\delta^{n}+\frac{tb}{n}+\frac{1}{n}\frac{tb(2+tb)(\delta-\delta^{n})}{1-\delta}e^{2b}\right)\norm{x}_{\mathcal{X}}

for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Then, we follow the proof of Theorem
5.2
and apply Proposition
4.1
to

F(t,s)=PV(t,s)P

defined on
P\mathcal{X}
w.r.t. the sequence
s_{0}=0<\frac{t}{n}<...<\frac{t(n-1)}{n}<t=s_{n}
so that

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}PV(s_{j},s_{j-1})

\displaystyle Px-T(t,0)Px\Bigr\|_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\leq n\max_{j\in\{1,...,n\}}\|\left(PV(s_{j},s_{j-1})P-T(s_{j},s_{j-1})\right)T(s_{j-1},s_{0})Px\|_{\mathcal{X}}\,.

Then, we use
T(s_{j-1},s_{0})Px=PT(s_{j-1},s_{0})Px
and the integral equation (
13
) to show with
s_{j}(\tau)=s_{j}+\frac{(1-\tau_{1})t}{n}

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle V(s_{j},s_{j-1})Px_{j}-T(s_{j},s_{j-1})Px_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{n}\int_{0}^{1}\Biggl(PV(s_{j},s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}))\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1})}P-T(s_{j},s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}))P\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1})}P\Biggr)x_{j}d\tau_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{n}\int_{0}^{1}\Biggl(PV(s_{j},s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}))(\mathbbm{1}-P)\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1})}P

\displaystyle+PV(s_{j},s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}))P\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1})}P-T(s_{j},s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}))P\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1})}P\Biggr)x_{j}d\tau_{1}

and for the last two terms

\displaystyle\frac{t}{n}\int_{0}^{1}\Biggl(

\displaystyle PV(s_{j},s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}))P\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1})}P-T(s_{j},s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}))P\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1})}P\Biggr)x_{j}d\tau_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}}{n^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\Biggl(\tau_{1}PV(s_{j},s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}\tau_{2}))(P+\mathbbm{1}-P)\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}\tau_{2})}P\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1})}P

\displaystyle-\tau_{1}T(s_{j},s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}\tau_{2}))P\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1}\tau_{2})}P\mathcal{L}_{s_{j-1}(\tau_{1})}P\Biggr)x_{j}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{1}\,,

where
x_{j}\coloneqq PT(s_{j-1},s_{0})Px
. Note that the integral above is again well-defined by Lemma
2.1
or
[
12
, Lem. B.15]
. Next, we use the relative
\mathcal{Y}
-boundedness as in Proposition
5.1
, but also the asymptotic Zeno condition, which implies boundedness for all
t\in[0,T]

\|P\mathcal{L}_{t}(\mathbbm{1}-P)\|_{\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}},\|(\mathbbm{1}-P)\mathcal{L}_{t}P\|_{\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}}\leq b\,.

Then,
\|P\mathcal{L}_{t}Px\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq a_{1}\|x\|_{\mathcal{Y}}
and
\|P\mathcal{L}_{t}P\mathcal{L}_{s}Px\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq a_{2}\|x\|_{\mathcal{Y}}
for all
(t,s)\in I_{T}
show

\displaystyle\|PV(s_{j},s_{j-1})Px_{j}-T(s_{j},s_{j-1})Px_{j}\|_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{2}}{n^{2}}\left(b^{2}\|x_{j}\|_{\mathcal{X}}+\left(\frac{a_{1}b}{2}+a_{2}\right)\|x_{j}\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\right)\,.

to the above bound shows

\displaystyle\|PV(s_{j},s_{j-1})Px_{j}-T(s_{j},s_{j-1})Px_{j}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\frac{t^{2}}{n^{2}}\left(b^{2}\|Px\|_{\mathcal{X}}+\left(\frac{a_{1}b}{2}+a_{2}\right)e^{\omega}\|Px\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\right)\,.

Combining the bound finishes the proof of the result.
∎

5.3
Application: The
l
-photon driven dissipation in error correction

We continue with the example in Section
4.4
about time-(in)dependent
C_{0}
-semigroups generated by GKSL-operators (
37
), in particular, Sobolev preserving semigroups that satisfy Equation
22
(see Sec.
4.4
and
2.2
for more details). In the following, we shortly repeat the proof of these two properties for the
l
-photon driven dissipation for simplicity in one-mode
[
20
, Lem. 5.3]
and give two specific examples for projections satisfying the assumptions of the statements before (
5.1
, Theorem
5.2
,
5.3
). The
l
-photon driven dissipation (
44
) defined for
x\in\mathcal{T}_{f}
by

(49)

\mathcal{L}[a^{l}-\alpha^{l}][x]=(a^{l}-\alpha^{l})x((a^{\dagger})^{l}-\bar{\alpha}^{l})-\frac{1}{2}\left\{((a^{\dagger})^{l}-\bar{\alpha}^{l})(a^{l}-\alpha^{l}),x\right\}

is the fundamental dynamics for the bosonic error correction code introduced in
[
34
,
22
,
21
]
. Using the CCR and the simple commutation relation
af(N+j\mathbbm{1})=f(N+(j+1)\mathbbm{1})a
for any real-valued function
f:\mathbbm{R}\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}
, we have for
f(x)=(x+1)^{k/2}1_{x\geq-1}
and
g_{l}(x)\coloneqq f(x)-f(x-l)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[a_{1}^{l}-\alpha^{l}]^{\dagger}(f(N))

\displaystyle=-(N-(l-1)\mathbbm{1})\cdots Ng_{l}(N)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\overline{\alpha}^{l}a^{l}g_{l}(N)+\alpha^{l}g_{l}(N)(a^{\dagger})^{l}\right)\,.

Note that the first term contributes a diagonal and the others, two off-diagonals with respect to the Fock basis. The operator can be decomposed in blocks defined via
a=\frac{h_{l}(n)g_{l}(n)}{2}
,
b=\frac{h_{l}(n+l)g_{l}(n+l)}{2}
, and
c=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{h_{l}(n+l)}\bar{\alpha}^{l}g_{l}(n+l)
by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\Bigl(-a\ket{n}\bra{n}-b\ket{n+l}\bra{n+l}+c^{*}\ket{n}\bra{n+l}+c\ket{n+l}\bra{n}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(-h_{l}(n)g_{l}(n)\ket{n}\bra{n}-h_{l}(n+l)g_{l}(n+l)\ket{n+l}\bra{n+l}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{h_{l}(n+l)}\left(\bar{\alpha}^{l}g_{l}(n+l)\ket{n}\bra{n+l}+\alpha^{l}g_{l}(n+l)\ket{n+l}\bra{n}\right)\Bigr)

for
n\in\mathbbm{N}
and the real valued function
h_{l}:\mathbbm{R}\rightarrow\mathbbm{R},h_{l}(x)\coloneqq(x-(l-1)\mathbbm{1})\cdots x1_{x\geq l}
. By considering the eigenvalues of the above operator, it can be upper bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\Bigl(-a\ket{n}\bra{n}-b\ket{n+l}\bra{n+l}+c^{*}\ket{n}\bra{n+l}+c\ket{n+l}\bra{n}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\left(-a-b+\sqrt{(a-b)^{2}+4|c|^{2}}\right)\Bigl(\ket{n}\bra{n}+\ket{n+l}\bra{n+l}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(-\min\{a,b\}+|c|\right)\Bigl(\ket{n}\bra{n}+\ket{n+l}\bra{n+l}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(-h_{l}(n)g_{l}(n)+\sqrt{h_{l}(n+l)}|\alpha|^{l}g_{l}(n+l)\right)\Bigl(\ket{n}\bra{n}+\ket{n+l}\bra{n+l}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(-(n+1)^{k/2-1+l}+c_{k}^{(l)}\right)\Bigl(\ket{n}\bra{n}+\ket{n+l}\bra{n+l}\Bigr)\,,

where the constant
c_{k}^{(l)}\geq 0
exists because the first term is of negative leading order (a more detailed discussion can be found in
[
20
, Lem. 5.3]
). All together, we achieve

\mathcal{L}[a^{l}-\alpha^{l}]^{\dagger}(f(N))\leq-\frac{l}{2}(N+\mathbbm{1})^{k/2-1+l}+c_{k}^{(l)}\mathbbm{1}\,,

which implies Assumption
22
and a better Sobolev preserving constant
[
20
, Prop. 5.1]
:

\displaystyle\tr\big[\cL[a^l-\alpha^l ](\rho)(N+\mathbbm{1})^{k/2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{l}{2}\tr[\rho\,(N+\mathbbm{1})^{k/2}\big]+c_{k}^{(l)}\,.

However, we are interested in the Sobolev preserving property of
(P\mathcal{L}P,\mathcal{D}(P\mathcal{L}P))
because this is one way to find the right Banach space
\mathcal{Y}=W^{k,1}
in the statements above. Here, the idea is to choose
k
large enough such that the relative boundedness assumption is achieved (for comparison see Equation
42
). However, this strongly depends on
P
. To analyze this, we will consider two cases: First,
P(\cdot)=K\cdot K^{\perp}
for a projection
K
diagonal in the Fock basis. Then, the same reasoning as above proves the well-definedness as well as the Sobolev preserving property of the semigroup defined by
P\mathcal{L}P
. The same holds true for the time-dependent analogues generated by (Eq.
16
). Another example is the
Z(\theta)
gate of the bosonic error correction code discussed in
[
34
,
22
,
21
]
. The physical codespace to which the evolution is exponentially converging (see Eq.
53
) and
[
1
]
) is defined by

\mathcal{C}_{l}\coloneqq\textrm{span}\left\{\outerproduct{\alpha_1}{\alpha_2}\,:\,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\left\{\alpha e^{\frac{i2\pi j}{l}}\,|\,j\in\{0,...,l-1\}\right\}\right\}\,,

where
\ket{\alpha}
denotes the coherent state. In particular, we are interested in the case

\ket{CAT_{\alpha}^{+}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(1+e^{2|\alpha|^{2}})}}\left(\ket{\alpha}+\ket{-\alpha}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\ket{CAT_{\alpha}^{-}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(1-e^{2|\alpha|^{2}})}}\left(\ket{\alpha}-\ket{-\alpha}\right)\,.

Note that the above CAT-states are orthonormal. Then, a possible rotation around the

X(\theta)=\cos(\frac{\theta}{2})(P^{+}_{\alpha}+P^{-}_{\alpha})+i\sin(\frac{\theta}{2})X_{\alpha}\,,

6
Conclusion

To summarize, we have seen a range of quantitative error bounds for generalizations of Trotter’s product formula and the quantum Zeno effect in the case of
C_{0}
-semigroups. Our primary contribution lies not only in the extension of the Trotter product formula and the quantum Zeno effect to evolution systems (time-dependent semigroups) but also in providing Suzuki-higher order error bounds for the Trotter-Suzuki product formula in the strong operator topology and a large class of bosonic examples. Moreover, we discussed some examples to explore possible definitions of the abstract reference space
\mathcal{Y}
.

For applications, a desirable line of research would entail a detailed discussion of possible choices of
\mathcal{Y}
as well as examples which does not satisfy the assumption. For the quantum Zeno effect, a generalization of the convergence assumption of the contraction
M
, as discussed in Remark
6
, would be fruitful. Here, an interesting interplay of the convergence assumption as well as the regularity of the semigroup quantified by the stable subspace could come up.

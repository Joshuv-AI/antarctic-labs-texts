Network reduction and absence of Hopf Bifurcations in dual phosphorylation networks with three Intermediates
Elisenda Feliu and Nidhi Kaihnsa

Abstract
. Phosphorylation networks, representing the mechanisms by which proteins are phosphorylated at one or multiple sites, are ubiquitous in cell signalling and display rich dynamics such as unlimited multistability. Dual-site phosphorylation networks are known to exhibit oscillations in the form of periodic trajectories, when phosphorylation and dephosphorylation occurs as a mixed mechanism: phosphorylation of the two sites requires one encounter of the kinase, while dephosphorylation of the two sites requires two encounters with the phosphatase. A still open question is whether a mechanism requiring two encounters for both phosphorylation and dephosphorylation also admits oscillations. In this work we provide evidence in favor of the absence of oscillations of this network by precluding Hopf bifurcations in any reduced network comprising three out of its four intermediate protein complexes. Our argument relies on a novel network reduction step that preserves the absence of Hopf bifurcations, and on a detailed analysis of the semi-algebraic conditions precluding Hopf bifurcations obtained from Hurwitz determinants of the characteristic polynomial of the Jacobian of the system. We conjecture that the removal of certain reverse reactions appearing in Michaelis-Menten-type mechanisms does not have an impact on the presence or absence of Hopf bifurcations. We prove an implication of the conjecture under certain favorable scenarios and support the conjecture with additional example-based evidence. Keywords: Dual-phosphorylation network, Hopf bifurcation, Hurwitz matrices, semi-algebraic sets, oscillation. 2020 MSC: 37N25, 92C40, 92C45,

Introduction

Protein phosphorylation networks are important mechanisms for cell signalling and control
[
rendall-MAPK
,
qiao:oscillations
]
. Typically, phosphorylation networks involve a substrate
S
that exists in different phosphorylation states, and phosphorylation and dephosphorylation events are catalyzed by one or multiple enzymes. A simple well-studied scenario comprises a single kinase
K
, responsible for catalyzing all phosphorylation events, and a single phosphatase
F
, catalyzing all dephosphorylation events.
When the substrate has multiple phosphorylation sites, full (de)phosphorylation can proceed by means of different mechanisms
[
SH09
]
, depending for example on whether one encounter of the substrate and the kinase can lead to the phosphorylation of one or multiple sites (e.g. yielding distributive and processive mechanisms), or on whether phosphorylation needs to occur in a specific order or not (e.g. yielding sequential, cyclic, or unordered phosphorylation).

Under the assumptions of mass-action kinetics, these mechanisms have been extensively studied e.g.,
[
CMS
,
conradi-shiu-review
,
CFM
,
G-distributivity
,
G-PNAS
,
rendall-feliu-wiuf
,
rendall-2site
]
.
Two main open questions concern, on one hand, the maximal number of positive steady states that the sequential and distributive phosphorylation cycle with
n
sites admits
[
FHC14
]
, and on the other, the existence of oscillations, and in particular, Hopf bifurcations, in the sequential and distributive dual phosphorylation cycle. In this paper we focus on the latter, and bring forward new ideas to tackle computationally prohibitive approaches.

Specifically, we consider the following reaction network with ordered and distributive phosphorylation:

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{0}+K{}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits}$}\hss}\raisebox{-0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits}$}}{}KS_{0}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}S_{1}+K{}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits}$}\hss}\raisebox{-0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits}$}}{}KS_{1}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}S_{2}+K\\
S_{2}+F{}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits}$}\hss}\raisebox{-0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits}$}}{}FS_{2}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}S_{1}+F{}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits}$}\hss}\raisebox{-0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits}$}}{}FS_{1}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}S_{0}+F,\end{split}

where

If the phosphorylation mechanism proceeds processively, that is, as

The latter was first established in
[

]
, and consequently in
[
CMS
]
, the authors carry out a detailed algebraic analysis of the parameter space that gives rise to Hopf bifurcations.
The similarities between the processive and the distributive mechanisms makes one think that network (
1

[
qiao:oscillations
]
. Finally,
network (
1

[
weber:osc
]
. Despite this evidence and efforts, Hopf bifurcations have not been found. As a matter of fact, it has been shown in
[

that if two intermediate protein complexes among the four

are removed from (

A simple Hopf bifurcation arises when a single pair of complex-conjugate eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix of the system crosses the imaginary axis with a varying parameter. For stability, one often requires the
remaining eigenvalues to have negative real part as the parameter changes. The question of precluding or establishing a simple Hopf bifurcation in mass-action kinetics translates to checking some semi-algebraic conditions
due to Liu
[
Liu
]
, see also
[

,

,

]

Hurwitz matrices

obtained from the characteristic polynomial of the Jacobian matrix and its coefficient

of the monomial with lowest degree. The semi-algebraic criterion for finding Hopf bifurcations
requires the existence of parameters and steady states for which
\det(H_{1})>0,\dots,\det(H_{s-2})>0
,
\det(H_{s-1})=0
and

. This criterion reduces the problem into checking the signs that some polynomials attain simultaneously. For network (
1

\det(H_{1})>0,\dots,\det(H_{s-2})>0
hold as these polynomials have only positive coefficients. Additionally,

\det(H_{s-1})
and
a_{s}
have terms both with positive and negative coefficients, and hence there are no apparent obstructions to achieve
a_{s}>0
while
\det(H_{s-1})=0
. However, for the simplified networks with two intermediates explained above, it was shown that
a_{s}>0
implied

, and hence the semi-algebraic system does not have a solution
[

]
.

In principle, the approach in

, and the rest of the network, the absence of Hopf bifurcations in the reduced network implies the absence in the original network (Theorem

Afterwards, we show that any subnetwork of network (

As network (

S_{2}+F{}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits}$}\hss}\raisebox{-0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits}$}}{}FS_{2}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}S_{1}+F{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}S_{0}+F.\end{split}

2.
Background

In this section we start by establishing the basic set up of reaction networks and revisit the parametrization of the Jacobian matrices of a network evaluated at the steady states using convex parameters. More details can be found for example in
[
feinberg-book
]
. Afterwards we review Hurwitz matrices and recall a sufficient condition based on their determinants for the absence of Hopf bifurcations.

2.1.
Reaction networks

Given
n

species

X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
, a complex is a non-negative integer linear combination
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}X_{i}
of the species, i.e.
\alpha_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
. A (mass-action)
reaction network
is a labelled digraph
G
with complexes as vertices. The edges of the graph are called
reactions
and are of the form

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{ij}X_{i}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits^{\mkern 5.0mu{\kappa}_{j}\mkern 9.0mu}}{}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{ij}X_{i},\qquad j=1,\dots,r,

where
{\kappa}_{j}>0
for
j=1,\dots,r
is called a
reaction rate constant
.
The set of reactions is assumed ordered, and the ordering is implicitly given by the subindex of the label.
The source complex of a reaction is called the
reactant
and the target complex the
product
.
We let
N=(\beta_{ij}-\alpha_{ij})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n\times r}
be the
stoichiometric matrix
, whose columns encode the net-production of the species in each reaction, and

B=(\alpha_{ij})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n\times r}
the
reactant
matrix, whose columns encode the coefficients of the reactant.
If
\alpha_{kj}=\beta_{kj}
for all
j
, then

X_{k}
is said to be a
catalyst
.

The concentration of species
X_{i}
is denoted by
x_{i}
.
Under the assumption of mass-action kinetics, the time evolution of the concentrations of the species is determined by a polynomial system of ordinary differential equations (ODE), which depends on
{\kappa}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}_{>0}
, and is of the form

(2)

\displaystyle\dot{x}=f_{\kappa}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0},\qquad\text{where}\quad f_{\kappa}(x)=N\diag(\kappa)x^{B}.

Here we use the standard notation for the vector
x^{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
to be given by
(x^{B})_{j}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{\alpha_{ij}}
.
The trajectories of (
2
) are confined to parallel translates of
\im(N)
, and
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
and
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
are forward invariant by (
2
)
[
volpert
]
.
A
steady state
is a point
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
such that

f_{\kappa}(x^{*})=0
.

2.2.
Convex parameters

For the study of Hopf bifurcations, we need to consider the eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix of (
2
) evaluated at the positive steady states (i.e., steady states in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
). The set of Jacobian matrices for all vectors of reaction rate constants and all positive steady states admits a parametrization in terms of the
convex parameters
. These parameters were first introduced by Clarke in
[
Clarke
]
and are reviewed next.

Given a network
G
with
n
species and
r
reactions, the set
\ker(N)\cap\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}
is a convex pointed polyhedral cone, and hence admits a minimal generating set of extreme vectors
E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}
(which are unique up to scaling). We define the
extreme matrix

E\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times m}
to be the matrix with columns
E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}
. Although the matrix is not uniquely determined, any two such matrices differ in a rescaling and reordering of the columns. These operations have no effect in what follows, and hence, when referring to “the extreme matrix”, we implicitly mean that a choice has been made.

For
h=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
and
{\ell}=({\ell}_{1},\ldots,{\ell}_{m})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{m}
,
we consider the matrix

(3)

\displaystyle J(h,{\ell}):=N\diag(E{\ell})B^{\top}\diag(h)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}.

If
E
does not have a zero row, then from
[
TF
, Eq (12) and Cor. 7(a)]
follows that

(4)

\{J(h,{\ell}):h\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n},\ {\ell}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{m}\}=\{J_{f_{\kappa}}(x):f_{\kappa}(x)=0,\ {\kappa}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}_{>0},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}\},

where
J_{f_{\kappa}}(x)=\Big(\tfrac{\partial f_{{\kappa},i}}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\Big)
is the Jacobian matrix of
f_{\kappa}
evaluated at
x
.
The vector
(h,{\ell})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{m}
is called the
vector of convex parameters
throughout the article.

In view of (
4
), to study Hopf bifurcations we will focus on the characteristic polynomial of
J(h,{\ell})
for all
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
and
{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{m}
.
For
s=\rk(N)
, given
(h,{\ell})
, the characteristic polynomial
\det(zI_{n}-J(h,{\ell}))
of
J(h,{\ell})
factors as

\displaystyle\det(zI_{n}-J(h,{\ell}))=z^{n-s}\big(z^{s}+a_{1}(h,{\ell})z^{s-1}+\ldots+a_{s}(h,{\ell})\big).

Here
I_{n}
is the identity matrix of size
n
.
We will thus focus on the following polynomial

(5)

p_{(h,{\ell})}:=z^{s}+a_{1}(h,{\ell})z^{s-1}+\ldots+a_{s}(h,{\ell})\quad\in\mathbb{R}[z],

which we refer to as the
reduced characteristic polynomial
of
J(h,{\ell})
. By construction, the coefficients of
p_{(h,{\ell})}
are polynomials in
h,{\ell}
.

2.3.
Hurwitz determinants

A (simple) Hopf bifurcation in a parametric ODE system with one parameter
\mu\in\mathbb{R}

requires the existence of a parameter value
\mu_{0}
and a steady state
x_{0}
such that the Jacobian matrix of the system at
x_{0}
has a pair of purely imaginary eigenvalues, while the other eigenvalues have non-zero real part. It is also typically required that the other eigenvalues have negative real part.
Therefore, one needs to study the roots of the reduced characteristic polynomial of the Jacobian matrix, and we will use a criterion based on the Hurwitz matrices.
Specifically, for a univariate polynomial
p=a_{0}z^{s}+a_{1}z^{s-1}+\ldots+a_{s}
and
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, the
i
-th
Hurwitz matrix
is:

(6)

\displaystyle H_{i}:=\begin{bmatrix}a_{1}&a_{0}&0&0&0&\ldots&0\\
a_{3}&a_{2}&a_{1}&a_{0}&0&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
a_{2i-1}&a_{2i-2}&a_{2i-3}&a_{2i-4}&a_{2i-5}&\ldots&a_{i}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{i\times i},

where the
(k,l)
entry is
a_{2k-l}
when
0\leq 2k-l\leq s
and 0 otherwise. We refer to
\det(H_{i})
as the
i
-th
Hurwitz determinant
. Note that
\det(H_{s})=a_{s}\det(H_{s-1})
.

The following proposition is a direct consequence of
[
KW
, Corollary 3.2]
and
[
CFM
, Proposition 1]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For a reaction network with
s=\rk(N)\geq 2
and
(h,{\ell})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{m}
, let

H_{i}(h,{\ell})
be the
i
-th Hurwitz matrix of the reduced characteristic polynomial
p_{(h,{\ell})}
of
J(h,{\ell})
as defined in (
5
) and let
a_{s}(h,{\ell})
be its constant term.
Assume that for all
(h,{\ell})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{m}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\det(H_{i}(h,{\ell}))>0

\displaystyle\quad\text{ for all }\quad i=1,\ldots,s-2,

\displaystyle\det(H_{s-1}(h,{\ell}))\neq 0

\displaystyle\quad\text{ if }\quad a_{s}(h,{\ell})>0.

Then for all
(h,\ell)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{>0}
,
p_{(h,{\ell})}
does not have a pair of purely imaginary roots. In particular the parametric ODE system (
2
) does not admit a simple Hopf bifurcation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
.

We note that the Hurwitz determinants
\det(H_{i}(h,{\ell}))
can be regarded as polynomials in
h,\ell
, and when doing so we simply write

\det(H_{i})\in\mathbb{R}[h,\ell]
. Then, Proposition
2.1
expresses the problem of precluding Hopf bifurcations as
identifying whether a semi-algebraic set is nonempty.

3.
Removing reversible reactions with intermediates and Hopf bifurcations

As biologically relevant networks often have a large number of species and parameters, the Hurwitz determinants
are large polynomials with many terms.
This makes the problem of checking the semi-algebraic conditions in Proposition
2.1
computationally challenging. In these cases it is advantageous to exploit the structure of the network. A particularly recurring motif in relevant networks, including the well-known
Michaelis-Menten mechanism, is of the form

(7)

y{}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits}$}\hss}\raisebox{-0.94722pt}{$\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\to}}\limits}$}}{}Y{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y^{\prime},

where
y,y^{\prime}
are complexes in species other than
Y
. The dual phosphorylation network in
(
1
) has four such motifs, with the species
Y
being one of
KS_{0},KS_{1},FS_{2}
and
FS_{1}
.

In this section we provide evidence for the reverse reaction
Y{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y
having no influence on whether the network admits Hopf bifurcations or not, and show that this holds when extra conditions are imposed on
y,y^{\prime}
and the rest of the reactions of the network.

Given a reaction network
G
containing a motif of the form (
7
), we let
G^{\prime}
denote the reaction network obtained from
G
by removing the reaction
Y{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y
. We will refer to this operation as “removing the backward reaction” or making the “reversible reaction irreversible”. Note that
G
and
G^{\prime}
have the same number of species.
We conjecture that the following holds.

Conjecture 3.1
.

Consider a reaction network
G
with
n
species and having motif (
7
) such that the species
Y
does not appear in any other complex of the network, and let
G^{\prime}
be the network obtained by removing the backward reaction of the motif.
Then,
G
admits a Hopf bifurcation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
if and only if
G^{\prime}
admits a Hopf bifurcation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
.

One way to approach Conjecture
3.1
is to first compare the capacity of the characteristic polynomials associated with the two networks
G,G^{\prime}
to admit purely imaginary roots for some parameter values and steady state.
In Lemma
it will be shown that if the extreme matrix
E
of
G
has
m
columns, then the extreme matrix
E^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
has
m-1
columns.
We let
p_{(h,{\ell})}
be the reduced characteristic polynomial of the Jacobian matrix
J(h,\ell)
of
G
in convex parameters
(h,\ell)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{>0}
, c.f. (
3
), (
5
), and

p_{(h,{\ell}^{\prime})}^{\prime}
be the reduced characteristic polynomial of the Jacobian matrix of
G^{\prime}
,

(8)

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(h,{\ell}^{\prime}):=N^{\prime}\diag(E^{\prime}{\ell}^{\prime})(B^{\prime})^{\top}\diag(h),\qquad(h,{\ell}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-1}_{>0},

where
N^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
are the stoichiometric and reactant matrices of
G^{\prime}
, respectively.

Conjecture 3.2
.

Consider a reaction network
G
having motif (
7
) such that the species
Y
does not appear in any other complex of the network.
With the notation above,
p_{(h,{\ell})}
has purely imaginary roots for some
(h,{\ell})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{>0}
if and only if
p_{(h,{\ell}^{\prime})}^{\prime}
has purely imaginary roots for some
(h,{\ell}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-1}_{>0}
.

Conjecture
3.2
does not directly imply Conjecture
3.1
, as a Hopf bifurcation requires in addition that there is a pair of eigenvalues that crosses the imaginary axis as a parameter varies. However, if Conjecture
3.2
is true, then a way to preclude Hopf bifurcations for
G
is to preclude the existence of a pair of purely imaginary roots of
p_{(h,{\ell}^{\prime})}^{\prime}
for all
(h,{\ell}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-1}_{>0}
. Since the coefficients of
p_{(h,{\ell}^{\prime})}^{\prime}
depend on one less variable than those of
p_{(h,{\ell})}
, the computational cost is reduced. Additionally, for networks like (
1
), with four such motifs, the reduction would be by four variables.

One implication of Conjecture
3.2
follows from our next conjecture, stating that the set of reduced characteristic polynomials for
G
is contained in that of
G^{\prime}
.

Conjecture 3.3
.

Consider a reaction network

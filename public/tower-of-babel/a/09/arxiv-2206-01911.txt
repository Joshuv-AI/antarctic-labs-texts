Higher moments of the pair correlation function for Sato-Tate sequences
Jewel Mahajan, Kaneenika Sinha

Abstract
. In [ BS19 ] , Balasubramanyam and the second named author derived the first moment of the pair correlation function for Hecke angles lying in small subintervals of [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] upon averaging over large families of Hecke newforms of weight k k with respect to Γ 0 ​ ( N ) \Gamma_{0}(N) . The goal of this article is to study higher moments of this pair correlation function. For an integer r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 , we present bounds for its r r -th power moments. We apply these bounds to record lower order error terms in the computation of the second and third moments. As a result, one can obtain the convergence of the second and third moments of this pair correlation function for suitably small intervals, and under appropriate growth conditions for the size of the families of Hecke newforms.

Introduction

Let
k
and
N
be positive integers with
k
even. Let
S(N,k)
denote the space of modular cusp forms of weight
k
with respect to
\Gamma_{0}(N).
For
n\geq 1,
let
T_{n}
denote the
n
-th Hecke operator acting on
S(N,k).
We denote the set of Hecke newforms in
S(N,k)
by
\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
. Any
f(z)\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
has a Fourier expansion

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}}a_{f}(n)q^{n},\qquad q=e^{2\pi iz},

where

a_{f}(1)=1
and

\frac{T_{n}(f(z))}{n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}}=a_{f}(n)f(z),\,n\geq 1.

We denote
s(N,k)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|
and note that
s(N,k)
is the dimension of the subspace of primitive cusp forms in
S(N,k)
.

Let
p
be a prime number with
(p,N)=1.
By a theorem of Deligne, the eigenvalues
a_{f}(p)
lie in the interval
[-2,2].
We denote
a_{f}(p)=2\cos\pi\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})},\,
with
\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\in[0,1]
.

The Sato-Tate conjecture, now a theorem
[
BLGHT11
]
, is the assertion that if
f
is a non-CM newform in
\mathcal{F}_{N,k},
then the Sato-Tate sequence

(1)

\{\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,p\text{ prime},\,(p,N)=1\}\subseteq[0,1]

is equidistributed in the interval
[0,1]
with respect to the measure
\mu(t)dt
, where
\mu(t)=2\sin^{2}(\pi t)
.
That is, for an interval
I=[a,b]
such that
0\leq a<b\leq 1
,

(2)

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{\pi_{N}(x)}\#\{p\leq x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,(p,N)=1,\,\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\in[a,b]\}=\int_{I}\mu(t)dt,

where
\pi_{N}(x)
denotes the number of primes
p\leq x
such that
(p,N)=1
. Henceforth, for an interval
I
,
\mu(I)
denotes the measure
\int_{I}\mu(t)dt
.

We “straighten out” the Sato-Tate sequence into a uniformly distributed sequence by defining

H(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{0}^{\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}}\mu(t)dt.

As an immediate consequence of (
2
), we see that the sequence
\{H(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,(p,N)=1\}
is uniformly distributed in the interval
[0,1]
.

A study of the moments of the pair correlation function for the sequence
\{H(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,(p,N)=1\}
as one varies
f
over appropriate families
\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
was initiated in
[
BS19
]
. This study is primarily motivated by a question of Katz and Sarnak that compares the spacings between straightened Hecke angles to spacings between points arising from independent and uniformly distributed random variables in the unit interval. One way to address these questions is via the pair correlation function, which looks at the spacings between unordered elements of a uniformly distributed sequence. In this context, the question of Katz and Sarnak can be stated as follows:

Question 1

(Katz, Sarnak
[
KS99
]
)
.

For any
s>0
, the pair correlation function of the sequence
\{H(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,p\text{ prime},\,(p,N)=1\}
is defined as:

R(x,s)(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\pi_{N}(x)}\#\left\{(p,q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,p\neq q\leq x,\,\begin{array}[]{c}(p,N)=(q,N)=1,\\
H(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})})-H(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})})\in\left[\frac{-s}{\pi_{N}(x)},\frac{s}{\pi_{N}(x)}\right]+\mathbb{Z}\end{array}\right\}.

For any
s>0
, does the limit
\lim_{x\to\infty}R(x,s)(f)
exist and is it equal to
2s
?

If the answer is yes, we say that the sequence
\{H(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})})\}
has Poissonnian pair correlation.

A variation of the question above was addressed in
[
BS19
]
by restricting
\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}
to short intervals
I
, such that
|I|\to 0
as
x\to\infty
.

Question 2
.

Let
0<\psi<1
and
I_{\delta}
denote intervals of the form

\left[\psi-\delta,\psi+\delta\right],\,\delta=\delta(x)\to 0\text{ as }x\to\infty.

Suppose

(3)

\#\left\{p\leq x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,(p,N)=1,\,\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\in I_{\delta}\right\}\sim\pi_{N}(x)\mu(I_{\delta})\text{ as }x\to\infty.

We define

(4)

\widetilde{R}_{\delta}(x,s)(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\pi_{N}(x)\mu(I_{\delta})}\#\left\{(p,q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,p\neq q\leq x,\,\begin{array}[]{c}(p,N)=(q,N)=1,\,\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})},\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})})\in I_{\delta},\\
H(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})})-H(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})})\in\left[\frac{-s}{\pi_{N}(x)},\frac{s}{\pi_{N}(x)}\right]\end{array}\right\}.

Does the limit
\lim_{x\to\infty}\widetilde{R}_{\delta}(x,s)(f)
exist and is it equal to
2s
?

for which (

]

]

Theorem

(Thorner,
[
Tho21
]
)
.

Let

be a monotonically increasing function with
\lim_{x\to\infty}F(x)=\infty

we have,

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{\pi_{N}(x)}\#\{p\leq x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,(p,N)=1,\,\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\in I\}=\mu(I).

In particular, if

\mu(I_{\delta})\geq\frac{\operatorname{log}(kN\operatorname{log}x)F(x)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{log}x}},

then

\#\{p\leq x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,(p,N)=1,\,\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\in I_{\delta}\}\sim\pi_{N}(x)\mu(I_{\delta})\text{ as }x\to\infty.

One simplifies Question
2
as follows: for
0<\psi<1
, henceforth, we denote
A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=2\sin^{2}\pi\psi
. Let us consider intervals

\mathcal{I}_{L}(\psi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left[\psi-\frac{1}{AL},\psi+\frac{1}{AL}\right],

such that
L=L(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
, and (

) holds for
\delta=1/AL
. Then, as

(5)

\operatorname{\mathcal{L}_{\textit{f}}}\,(\psi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#\left\{p\leq x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,(p,N)=1,\,\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\in\mathcal{I}_{L}(\psi)\right\}\sim\pi_{N}(x)\mu_{\frac{1}{AL}}.

The advantage of localizing our intervals around
\psi
is that the Sato-Tate density
2\sin^{2}\pi t\sim A
is essentially constant in short intervals and the straightening of the Hecke angles is more or less equivalent to rescaling them. That is,

(6)

\operatorname{\mathcal{L}_{\textit{f}}}\,(\psi)\sim\pi_{N}(x)\int_{\psi-\frac{1}{AL}}^{\psi+\frac{1}{AL}}2\sin^{2}\pi t\,dt\sim A\frac{2}{AL}\pi_{N}(x)=\frac{2\pi_{N}(x)}{L}.

If
\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})},\,\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\in\mathcal{I}_{L}(\psi)
, then

(7)

H(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})})-H(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})})=\int_{\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}}^{\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}}2\sin^{2}\pi t\,dt\sim A(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}-\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})})\text{ as }x\to\infty.

Thus,

\widetilde{R}_{\frac{1}{AL}}(x,s)(f)=\frac{1}{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}_{\textit{f}}}\,(\psi)}\#\left\{(p,q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,p\neq q\leq x,\,\begin{array}[]{c}(p,N)=(q,N)=1,\,\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}),\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}\in\mathcal{I}_{L}(\psi),\\
H(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})})-H(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})})\in\left[\frac{-s}{\pi_{N}(x)},\frac{s}{\pi_{N}(x)}\right]\end{array}\right\}

\sim\frac{1}{\operatorname{\mathcal{L}_{\textit{f}}}\,(\psi)}\#\left\{(p,q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,p\neq q\leq x,\,\begin{array}[]{c}(p,N)=(q,N)=1,\,\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})},\,\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}\in\mathcal{I}_{L}(\psi),\\
\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}-\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}\in\widetilde{I}_{x}\end{array}\right\},

where

\widetilde{I}_{x}=\left[\frac{-s}{A\pi_{N}(x)},\frac{s}{A\pi_{N}(x)}\right].

The pair correlation function of a sequence is obtained by evaluating some exponential sums related to the sequence. In the case of Hecke angles, we have to remove the imaginary parts of these sums in order to apply existing techniques. Therefore, we modify the above question and consider the families

\mathcal{A}_{f,x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\text{ mod 1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,p\leq x,\,(p,N)=1\}.

As explained in Section
2
, the pair correlation function of the families
\mathcal{A}_{f,x}
turns out to be asymptotic to

{R}_{1/L}(x,s)(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{L}{8\pi_{N}(x)}\sum_{(p,q)\atop{p,q\leq x\atop{(p,N)=(q,N)=1\atop{p\neq q}}}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\left[-\frac{1}{A},\frac{1}{A}\right]}(L(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}-\psi+n))\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\left[-\frac{1}{A},\frac{1}{A}\right]}(L(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}-\psi+n))

\times\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\left[-\frac{s}{2A},\frac{s}{2A}\right]}(\pi_{N}(x)(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}+n)).

While the function
{R}_{1/L}(x,s)(f)
is difficult to study (we explain this in Sections
2.1
and
2.3
), one way to address its convergence can be through the method of moments. That is, one may study the moments

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}\left(R_{1/L}(x,s)(f)\right)^{r},\,r\in\mathbb{N}.

The perspective of averaging quantities related to
f
over all Hecke newforms (or eigenforms) goes back to the work of
[
Sar87
]
,
[
CDF97
]
and
[
Ser97
]
. In order to approach difficult arithmetic questions pertaining to a Hecke newform
f
(such as the distribution or spacing properties of Hecke angles
\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}
), one can ask what happens to those questions “on average” over families of eigenforms. Summing over all Hecke newforms (or eigenforms as the case may be) allows us to bring in techniques such as the Eichler-Selberg trace formula for the trace of Hecke operators acting on subspaces of cusp forms of weight
k
with respect to
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. For example, Conrey, Duke and Farmer
[
CDF97
]
used the trace formula to prove that the Sato-Tate conjecture holds on average over large families. That is, if
k>e^{x}
, they showed that

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{1,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{1,k}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\#\{p\leq x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\in[a,b]\}\right)=\int_{I}2\sin^{2}\pi t\,dt,

In
[
Nag06
]
, it is shown that the above asymptotic holds when
\frac{\operatorname{log}k}{\operatorname{log}x}\to\infty\text{ as }x\to\infty.

In
[
BS19
]
, this approach of averaging is adopted in the investigation of the pair correlation function for the Hecke angles. Since we also let the levels
N
vary, the growth conditions take into account the contribution coming from them. Moreover, it is feasible to consider a smooth variant of
R_{1/L}(x,s)(f)
. This leads to the following theorem:

Theorem 1

(Balasubramanyam, Sinha,
[
BS19
]
)
.

Let us consider families
\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
with levels
N=N(x)
and even weights
k=k(x).
Let
g,\,\rho
be real valued, even functions
\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
in the Schwartz class with Fourier transforms supported in the interval
[-1,1]
.
Let
0<\psi<1,\,\psi\neq 1/2
. Define
A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=2\sin^{2}\pi\psi
. Define

(8)

\rho_{L}(\theta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho(L(\theta+n))\text{ for }L=L(x)\geq 1,

(9)

G_{x}(\theta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}g\left(\pi_{N}(x)(\theta+n)\right),

and the smoothened pair correlation function,

R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{L}{8\pi_{N}(x)}\sum_{p,q\leq x\atop{(p,N)=(q,N)=1\atop{p\neq q}}}\rho_{L}(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}-\psi)\rho_{L}(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}-\psi)G_{x}(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}).

[Note: A detailed discussion of the above definitions is presented in Section
2
.]

(a)

We have

(10)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)=\frac{T(g,\rho)}{4L}\\
&+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\right)+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{L(\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x)^{2}}{\pi_{N}(x)}\right)+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{x^{\pi_{N}(x)c}8^{\nu(N)}}{kN}\right),\end{split}

where

(11)

T(g,\rho)=\sum_{l\geq 1}(U(l)-U(l-1))^{2}\widehat{g}\left(\frac{l}{\pi_{N}(x)}\right),

with

U(l)=\widehat{\rho}\left(\frac{l}{L}\right)(2\cos 2\pi l\psi)-\widehat{\rho}\left(\frac{l+1}{L}\right)(2\cos 2\pi(l+1)\psi).

In the error term,
c
refers to an absolute positive constant.

(b)

If we choose
L
such that

L=\operatorname{o}\left(\frac{\pi_{N}(x)}{(\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x)^{2}}\right),

and consider families
\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
with levels
N=N(x)
and even weights
k=k(x)
such that

\frac{\operatorname{log}\left(k{N}/8^{\nu(N)}\right)}{x}\to\infty\text{ as }x\to\infty,

then,

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)\sim\frac{T(g,\rho)}{4L}\text{ as }x\to\infty.

Furthermore,

\frac{T(g,\rho)}{4L}\sim A^{2}\widehat{g}(0){\rho\ast\rho}(0)\text{ as }x\to\infty.

We make a few remarks here.

(1)

The above theorem tells us that the first moment of the pair correlation function

R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)
upon averaging over all newforms
f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
is asymptotic to
A^{2}\widehat{g}(0){\rho\ast\rho}(0)
. However, we require the size of the families
\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
to grow rapidly for this asymptotic to hold. This limitation comes from the estimation of a term in the Eichler-Selberg trace formula. The elliptic term in the trace formula leads to estimates of the form

\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{x^{D\pi_{N}(x)}8^{\nu(N)}}{k{N}}\right)

for a positive constant
D
in the pair correlation sum. The use of alternative trace formulas such as the Petersson trace formula leads to lower values of
D
than those obtained by the Eichler-Selberg trace formula, but causes the same problem if we want the above error term to go to zero.

(2)

In
[
BS19
]
, the first moment of
R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)
upon averaging over
f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
was obtained for positive integers
N
and
k
such that
N
is prime and
k
is even. The techniques can be readily generalized to all levels
N
. Accordingly, a modified version of the result of
[
BS19
]
has been stated above.

The above theorem about the first moment

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)

naturally leads us to questions about higher moments

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}\left(R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)\right)^{r},\,r\geq 2.

The primary objective of this article is to address this question. In this direction, we prove the following theorems.

First, we present a bound for the
r
-th moments of

. In order to state this bound, we first introduce the following notation.

.

Theorem 2
.

Let us consider families
\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
with levels
N=N(x)
and even weights
k=k(x)
. Let
g
,
\rho
be real valued, even functions
\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
in the Schwartz class with Fourier transforms supported in
[-1,1]
and
L=L(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
. Let
0<\psi<1
,
\psi\neq 1/2
, and let
A=2\sin^{2}\pi\psi
.
Let
\rho_{L},\,G_{x},\,R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)
and
T(g,\rho)
be as defined in Theorem
1
, and let
L(x)<\frac{\pi_{N}(x)}{(\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x)^{2}}.

For any integer

, we have

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}\left(R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)\right)^{r}-\left(\frac{T(g,\rho)}{4L}\right)^{r}\\
&\ll_{r}\sum_{t=2}^{2r}\frac{L^{3r-2t}}{\pi_{N}(x)^{2r-t}}+\frac{L^{1/2}\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x}{\pi_{N}(x)^{1/2}}\\
&\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}\sum_{t=2}^{2r-1}\sum_{\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{P}(r,t,j)}\frac{L^{P(\mathcal{P})}}{\pi_{N}(x)^{Q(\mathcal{P})}}\left(\begin{cases}(\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x)^{t}&\text{ if }t\leq j\\
1&\text{ if }t>j\\
\end{cases}\right),\\
&+\frac{x^{E(r)\pi_{N}(x)}8^{\nu(N)}}{kN},\end{split}

where

P(\mathcal{P})=\begin{cases}3r-2j-t-\sum_{i\geq 2}x_{i,0}+\sum_{i\geq 1}x_{i,i}&\text{ if }t\leq j\\
3r-j-2t-\sum_{i\geq 2}x_{i,0}+\sum_{i\geq 1}x_{i,i}&\text{ if }t>j,\\
\end{cases}

Q(\mathcal{P})=\begin{cases}3r-j-t-\sum_{i\geq 1}x_{i,0}+\sum_{i\geq 2}x_{i,i}&\text{ if }t\leq j\\
2r-2j-\sum_{i\geq 1}x_{i,0}+\sum_{i\geq 2}x_{i,i}&\text{ if }t>j,\\
\end{cases}

and

is a quantity that depends only on
r
.

The above theorem gives us a bound that addresses all the higher-power moments of
R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)
. For the
r
-th moment

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}\left(R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)\right)^{r}

to converge to

\left(\frac{T(g,\rho)}{4L}\right)^{r},

we need to make an appropriate choice of
L
such that the “error terms”

\begin{split}&\sum_{t=2}^{2r}\frac{L^{3r-2t}}{\pi_{N}(x)^{2r-t}}+\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}\sum_{t=2}^{2r-1}\sum_{\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{P}(r,t,j)}\frac{L^{P(\mathcal{P})}}{\pi_{N}(x)^{Q(\mathcal{P})}}\left(\begin{cases}(\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x)^{t}&\text{ if }t\leq j\\
1&\text{ if }t>j\\
\end{cases}\right)\\
&+\frac{x^{E(r)\pi_{N}(x)}8^{\nu(N)}}{kN},\end{split}

converge to 0.

•

The error term

\frac{x^{E(r)\pi_{N}(x)}8^{\nu(N)}}{kN}\to 0

if we consider families
\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
with levels
N=N(x)
and even weights
k=k(x)
such that

\frac{\operatorname{log}\left(k{N}/8^{\nu(N)}\right)}{x}\to\infty\text{ as }x\to\infty.

•

A choice of
L
such that

L(x)=\operatorname{o}\left(\pi_{N}(x)^{2/3}\right)

will ensure that

\sum_{t=2}^{2r}\frac{L^{3r-2t}}{\pi_{N}(x)^{2r-t}}\to 0\text{ as }x\to\infty.

•

The obstruction to obtaining the convergence of the
r
-th power moments of
R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)
for any
r\geq 2
comes from the sum

\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}\sum_{t=2}^{2r-1}\sum_{\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{P}(r,t,j)}\frac{L^{P(\mathcal{P})}}{\pi_{N}(x)^{Q(\mathcal{P})}}\left(\begin{cases}(\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x)^{t}&\text{ if }t\leq j\\
1&\text{ if }t>j\\
\end{cases}\right).

Individual components of this sum depend on multiple parameters such as the choice of
t
, choice of
j
, and the values
x_{i,d}
coming from each partition in
\mathcal{P}(r,t,j)
. As
r
increases, the size of
\mathcal{P}(r,t,j)
increases quite rapidly, and the parameters
x_{i,d}
in each partition behave differently. As a result, in the above sum, we are currently unable to determine a uniform choice for
L(x)
which will ensure that each of its components would go to 0. Theorem
2
, therefore, is a first step towards the study of higher-power moments of
R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)
.

•

For specific values of
r
, we can list out the set of partitions
\mathcal{P}(r,t,j)
, apply Theorem
2
and make a choice of
L
that lets each error term in the theorem go to 0. For these values of

, we are able to obtain the convergence of the moments
\left(R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)\right)^{r}
for clearly specified choices for
L(x)
, and under appropriate growth conditions for
|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|
.

In the context of the second moment, by applying Theorem
2
, we have the following theorem.

2.
The pair correlation function for Hecke angles

Recall,

(14)

\mathcal{A}_{f,x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\text{ mod 1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,p\leq x,\,(p,N)=1\}\text{ as }x\to\infty.

Note that the family
\mathcal{I}_{L}\cap\mathcal{A}_{f,x}
has cardinality

\operatorname{\mathcal{L}_{\textit{f}}}\,(\psi)+\operatorname{\mathcal{L}_{\textit{f}}}\,(1-\psi)\sim\frac{4\pi_{N}(x)}{L}\text{ as }x\to\infty.

Therefore, the mean spacing of the family
\mathcal{A}_{f,x}
of Hecke angles in the intervals
\mathcal{I}_{L}
is

(15)

\frac{|\mathcal{I}_{L}|}{\#(\mathcal{I}_{L}\cap\mathcal{A}_{f,x})}\sim\frac{1}{2A\pi_{N}(x)}\text{ as }x\to\infty.

We have the following lemma:

Lemma 4
.

Let
0<\psi<1
and
\psi\neq 1/2
. Then,

\begin{split}&\#\left\{(i,j)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,i\neq j,\,x_{i},x_{j}\in(\mathcal{I}_{L}\cap\mathcal{A}_{f,x}),\,x_{i}-x_{j}\in\left[-\frac{s}{2A\pi_{N}(x)},\frac{s}{2A\pi_{N}(x)}\right]\right\}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(p,q)\atop{p,q\leq x\atop{(p,N)=(q,N)=1\atop{p\neq q}}}}\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\left[-\frac{1}{A},\frac{1}{A}\right]}(L(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}-\psi+n))\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\left[-\frac{1}{A},\frac{1}{A}\right]}(L(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}-\psi+n))\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\left[-\frac{s}{2A},\frac{s}{2A}\right]}(\pi_{N}(x)(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}+n))\Big).\end{split}

Proof.

Let

I_{x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left[-\frac{s}{2A\pi_{N}(x)},\frac{s}{2A\pi_{N}(x)}\right].

By a careful counting, we obtain

\begin{split}&\#\left\{(i,j)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,i\neq j,\,x_{i},x_{j}\in(\mathcal{I}_{L}\cap\mathcal{A}_{f,x}),\,x_{i}-x_{j}\in I_{x}\right\}\\
&=\sum_{(p,q)\atop{p,q\leq x\atop{(p,N)=(q,N)=1\atop{p\neq q}}}}(\chi_{\mathcal{I}_{L}}(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})})+\chi_{\mathcal{I}_{L}}(1-\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}))(\chi_{\mathcal{I}_{L}}(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})})+\chi_{\mathcal{I}_{L}}(1-\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}))\\
&\times 2B(\chi,\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})},\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})},s),\end{split}

where

B(\chi,\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})},\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})},s)=\chi_{I_{x}}(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}-\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}-1)+\chi_{I_{x}}(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}-\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})})+\chi_{I_{x}}(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}-\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}+1)

+\chi_{I_{x}}(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}+\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})})+\chi_{I_{x}}(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}+\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}-1)+\chi_{I_{x}}(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}+\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}-2).

The lemma follows immediately.

\square

Thus, the pair correlation function for the families
\mathcal{A}_{f,x}\cap\mathcal{I}_{L}
is

(16)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{L}\cap\mathcal{A}_{f,x}|}\#\left\{(i,j)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,i\neq j,\,x_{i},x_{j}\in(\mathcal{I}_{L}\cap\mathcal{A}_{f,x}),\,x_{i}-x_{j}\in\left[-\frac{s}{2A\pi_{N}(x)},\frac{s}{2A\pi_{N}(x)}\right]\right\}\\
&\sim\frac{L}{8\pi_{N}(x)}\sum_{(p,q)\atop{p,q\leq x\atop{(p,N)=(q,N)=1\atop{p\neq q}}}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\left[-\frac{1}{A},\frac{1}{A}\right]}(L(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}-\psi+n))\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\left[-\frac{1}{A},\frac{1}{A}\right]}(L(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}-\psi+n))\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\left[-\frac{s}{2A},\frac{s}{2A}\right]}(\pi_{N}(x)(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}+n)).\end{split}

We consider a smooth analogue of the right hand side.

Let
\rho
be an even test function in the Schwartz class such that the Fourier transform
\widehat{\rho}
of
\rho
is smooth and compactly supported, and normalized so that

\sup\{|t|\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\widehat{\rho}(t)\neq 0\}=1.

We define

\rho_{L}(\theta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho(L(\theta+n)).

\rho_{L}(\theta)
is a 1-periodic function, localized to a scale of
1/L
, and can be thought of as a smooth version of a function that counts points
\theta
such that
|\theta|<1/L
. It has the Fourier expansion

(17)

\begin{split}&\rho_{L}(\theta)=\sum_{|l|\leq L}\widehat{\rho_{L}}(l)e(l\theta)=\widehat{\rho_{L}}(0)+\sum_{1\leq l\leq L}\widehat{\rho_{L}}(l)2\cos(2\pi l\theta),\end{split}

where
\widehat{\rho_{L}}(l)=\frac{1}{L}\widehat{\rho}\left(\frac{l}{L}\right)
.

Similarly, let
g
be an even test function satisfying the same properties as
\rho
, that is, an even test function such that the Fourier transform
\widehat{g}
of
g
is smooth and compactly supported, and normalized so that

\sup\{|t|\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\widehat{g}(t)\neq 0\}=1.

We define

(18)

\begin{split}&G_{x}(\theta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}g\left(\pi_{N}(x)(\theta+n)\right)\\
&=\sum_{|n|\leq\pi_{N}(x)}\widehat{G_{x}}(n)e(n\theta)\\
&=\widehat{G_{x}}(0)+\sum_{1\leq n\leq\pi_{N}(x)}\widehat{G_{x}}(n)2\cos(2\pi n\theta),\end{split}

where
\widehat{G_{x}}(n)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\pi_{N}(x)}\widehat{g}\left(\frac{n}{\pi_{N}(x)}\right).

Similar to the case of
\rho_{L}
, the function
G_{x}(\theta)
is a 1-periodic function, localized to a scale of
1/\pi_{N}(x)
, and therefore, effectively counts points
\theta
such that
|\theta|<1/\pi_{N}(x)
.

The smooth analogue of the right hand side of (
16
) is defined as

(19)

R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{L}{8\pi_{N}(x)}\sum_{(p,q)\atop{p\neq q\leq x\atop{(p,N)=(q,N)=1}}}\rho_{L}(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}-\psi)\rho_{L}(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}-\psi)G_{x}(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}).

We recall the following classical result, which gives a recursive relation between
a_{f}(p^{l}),

l\geq 0.

For an integer
l\geq 0,

(20)

2\cos 2\pi l\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}=\begin{cases}2,&\text{ if }l=0,\\
a_{f}(p^{2l})-a_{f}(p^{2l-2}),&\text{ if }l\geq 1.\end{cases}

Denote

U(l)=\widehat{\rho}\left(\frac{l}{L}\right)(2\cos 2\pi l\psi)-\widehat{\rho}\left(\frac{l+1}{L}\right)(2\cos 2\pi(l+1)\psi),\,0\leq l\leq L

and

G(n)=\widehat{g}\left(\frac{n}{\pi_{N}(x)}\right),\,0\leq n\leq\pi_{N}(x).

We have,

(21)

\begin{split}&\rho_{L}(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}-\psi)=\rho_{L}(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}-\psi)+\rho_{L}(-\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}-\psi)\\
&=\sum_{|l|\leq L}\widehat{\rho_{L}}(l)\left\{e(l(\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}-\psi))+e(l(-\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}-\psi))\right\}\\
&=\sum_{|l|\leq L}\widehat{\rho_{L}}(l)e(-l\psi)2\cos(2\pi l\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})})\\
&=\left(2\widehat{\rho_{L}}(0)+\sum_{1\leq l\leq L}\widehat{\rho_{L}}(l)(2\cos 2\pi l\psi)2\cos(2\pi l\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})})\right).\\
&=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{0\leq l\leq L}U(l)a_{f}(p^{2l}).\end{split}

Similarly,

\begin{split}&G_{x}\left(\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\pm\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{\pi_{N}(x)}\sum_{|n|\leq\pi_{N}(x)}\widehat{g}\left(\frac{n}{\pi_{N}(x)}\right)e\left(\pm n\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})}\pm n\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})}\right))\\
&=\frac{1}{\pi_{N}(x)}\left(4G(0)+\sum_{n\geq 1}2G(n)(2\cos 2\pi n\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{p})})(2\cos 2\pi n\operatorname{\theta_{\textit{f}}\,(\textit{q})})\right),\end{split}

Using the above Fourier expansions,

(22)

\begin{split}R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)&=\frac{L}{8\pi_{N}(x)}\frac{1}{L^{2}\pi_{N}(x)}\sum_{(p,q)\atop{p\neq q\leq x\atop{(p,N)=(q,N)=1}}}\left[\sum_{l\geq 0}U(l)a_{f}(p^{2l})\right]\left[\sum_{l^{\prime}\geq 0}U(l^{\prime})a_{f}(q^{2l^{\prime}})\right]\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left[4G(0)+\sum_{n\geq 1}2G(n)(a_{f}(p^{2n})-a_{f}(p^{2n-2}))(a_{f}(q^{2n})-a_{f}(q^{2n-2}))\right]\\
\end{split}

We now denote

T_{1}(p)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{l\geq 0}U(l)a_{f}(p^{2l}),

T_{2}(q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{l^{\prime}\geq 0}U(l^{\prime})a_{f}(q^{2l^{\prime}})

and

T_{3}(p,q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}G(n)A(p,q,n),

where

A(p,q,n)=\begin{cases}4&\text{ if }n=0\\
2(a_{f}(p^{2n})-a_{f}(p^{2n-2}))(a_{f}(q^{2n})-a_{f}(q^{2n-2}))&\text{ if }n\geq 1.\end{cases}

Thus, we get

(23)

R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)=\frac{1}{8\pi_{N}(x)^{2}L}\sum_{(p,q)\atop{p\neq q\leq x\atop{(p,N)=(q,N)=1}}}T_{1}(p)T_{2}(q)T_{3}(p,q).

Since
\widehat{\rho}
and
\widehat{g}
are continuous and compactly supported, we have the bounds
|U(l_{i})|,\,|U(k_{i})|,\,|G(n_{i})|\ll 1
, which will be used in the calculations below.

2.1.
Equidistribution properties of Hecke angles in small scales

In this section, we explain the connection between the error terms in the Sato-Tate distribution theorem and the distribution of the families
\mathcal{A}_{f,x}
(defined in (
14
)) in shrinking intervals
\mathcal{I}_{L}
where
L=L(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty.
As we will see below, this provides an insight into the difficulties in obtaining the pair correlation function for a deterministic
f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
, and why it helps to consider a
random

f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
instead.

The question is, what growth conditions on
L=L(x)
are sufficient to ensure that

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}_{f,x}|}\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{f,x}}\rho_{L}(\theta-\psi)=\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{L}(t-\psi)\mu(t)dt?

Define

N_{\rho,L,f}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}_{f,x}}\rho_{L}(\theta-\psi).

By (
21
),

(24)

\begin{split}\frac{N_{\rho,L,f}(x)}{|\mathcal{A}_{f,x}|}=&\frac{1}{2\pi_{N}(x)}\left(2\widehat{\rho_{L}}(0)\pi_{N}(x)+\sum_{1\leq l\leq L}\widehat{\rho_{L}}(l)(2\cos 2\pi l\psi)\sum_{p\leq x\atop{(p,N)=1}}(a_{f}(p^{2l})-a_{f}(p^{2l-2}))\right)\\
=&\frac{1}{2\pi_{N}(x)}\sum_{0\leq l\leq L}\frac{U(l)}{L}\sum_{p\leq x\atop{(p,N)=1}}a_{f}(p^{2l}).\end{split}

It is easy to see that

\frac{U(0)}{2L}=\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{L}(t-\psi)\mu(t)dt.

The following proposition is a consequence of Thorner’s discrepancy estimates.

Proposition 5
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
k\geq 2
be integers with
k
even. Let
f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
be a non-CM newform. Let
\rho
be as defined in (
8
). If
0<\psi<1
and
L
is chosen such that

L\leq\frac{c_{11}\sqrt{\operatorname{log}x}}{2\sqrt{\operatorname{log}(kN\operatorname{log}x)}}

for a suitably small constant
c_{11}
, we have

(25)

\frac{N_{\rho,L,f}(x)}{2\pi_{N}(x)}\sim\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{L}(t-\psi)\mu(t)dt\text{ as }x\to\infty.

Proof.

The key ingredient in the proof is the following estimate which follows from
[
Tho21
, Proposition 2.1]
. Let
f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
be a non-CM newform. Then there exist constants
c_{9}
(suitably large) and
c_{10}
and
c_{11}
(suitably small) such that if

2l\leq c_{11}\sqrt{\operatorname{log}x}/\sqrt{\operatorname{log}(kN\operatorname{log}x)},

then

(26)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
(p,N)=1\end{subarray}}a_{f}\left(p^{2l}\right)\ll l^{2}\pi_{N}(x)\left(x^{-\frac{1}{2c_{9}l}}+e^{-\frac{c_{10}\operatorname{log}x}{4l^{2}\operatorname{log}(2kNl)}}+e^{-\frac{c_{10}\sqrt{\operatorname{log}x}}{\sqrt{2l}}}\right).

Note that
\widehat{\rho}
is a compactly supported, continuous function and therefore, absolutely bounded. Thus,

\frac{U(l)}{L}=\frac{1}{L}\left[\widehat{\rho}\left(\frac{l}{L}\right)\cos 2\pi l\psi-\widehat{\rho}\left(\frac{l+1}{L}\right)\cos 2\pi(l+1)\psi\right]\ll\frac{1}{L}.

Choosing

L\leq\frac{c_{11}\sqrt{\operatorname{log}x}}{2\sqrt{\operatorname{log}(kN\operatorname{log}x)}},

we have

\begin{split}&\frac{N_{\rho,L,f}(x)}{2\pi_{N}(x)}-\frac{U(0)}{2L}\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi_{N}(x)}\sum_{1\leq l\leq L}\frac{U(l)}{L}\sum_{p\leq x\atop{(p,N)=1}}a_{f}(p^{2l})\\
&\ll\frac{1}{L\pi_{N}(x)}\sum_{1\leq l\leq L}l^{2}\left(x^{-\frac{1}{2c_{9}l}}+e^{-\frac{c_{10}\operatorname{log}x}{4l^{2}\operatorname{log}(2kNl)}}+e^{-\frac{c_{10}\sqrt{\operatorname{log}x}}{\sqrt{2l}}}\right)\\
&\ll\frac{L^{2}}{\pi_{N}(x)}\left(x^{-\frac{1}{2c_{9}L}}+e^{-\frac{c_{10}\operatorname{log}x}{4L^{2}\operatorname{log}(2kNL)}}+e^{-\frac{c_{10}\sqrt{\operatorname{log}x}}{\sqrt{2L}}}\right).\\
\end{split}

The above term
\to 0\text{ as }x\to\infty
,
if
L\ll\sqrt{\operatorname{log}x}/\sqrt{\operatorname{log}(kN\operatorname{log}x)}.
This proves the proposition.

\square

The limitation of the above proposition is in the range of
L
for which it holds. Is it possible to obtain the asymptotic (
25
) for a larger range of
L
, for example,
L(x)\ll x^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0?
It turns out that this can be done if one assumes strong analytic hypotheses on symmetric power
L
-functions corresponding to a non-CM newform
f
with squarefree level
N
. In this respect, using conditional discrepancy estimates of Rouse and Thorner
[
RT17
]
, we have the following proposition:

Proposition 6
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
k\geq 2
be integers with
N
squarefree and
k
even. Let
f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
be a non-CM newform such that for each
l\geq 0
, the following hypotheses hold:

(1)

The symmetric power
L
-function
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{l}f)
is the
L
-function of a cuspidal automorphic representation on
GL_{l+1}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
.

(2)

The Generalized Riemann hypothesis holds for
L(s,\operatorname{Sym}^{l}f)
.

Let
\rho
be as defined in (
8
). If
0<\psi<1
and
L
is chosen such that

L=\operatorname{o}\left(\frac{x^{1/2c}}{(\operatorname{log}x)^{2/c}}\right)

for a constant
c>1
, we have

\frac{N_{\rho,L,f}(x)}{2\pi_{N}(x)}\sim\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{L}(t-\psi)\mu(t)dt\text{ as }x\to\infty.

Proof.

By
[
RT17
, Proposition 3.3]
, if
l\geq 1
and
x\geq 5\times 10^{5}
,

(27)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
(p,N)=1\end{subarray}}a_{f}\left(p^{2l}\right)\ll(l\operatorname{log}l)\sqrt{x}\operatorname{log}x\operatorname{log}(N(k-1)).

As in the proof of Proposition
5
, we have, for
x\geq 5\times 10^{5}
,

\begin{split}&\frac{N_{\rho,L,f}(x)}{2\pi_{N}(x)}-\frac{U(0)}{2L}=\frac{1}{2\pi_{N}(x)}\sum_{1\leq l\leq L}\frac{U(l)}{L}\sum_{p\leq x\atop{(p,N)=1}}a_{f}(p^{2l})\\
&\ll L\operatorname{log}L\frac{\sqrt{x}\operatorname{log}x}{\pi_{N}(x)}\operatorname{log}(N(k-1))\\
&\ll L\operatorname{log}L\frac{(\operatorname{log}x)^{2}}{\sqrt{x}}\operatorname{log}(N(k-1)).\end{split}

Let us choose
L(x)
such that

L(x)=\operatorname{o}\left(\frac{x^{1/2c}}{(\operatorname{log}x)^{2/c}}\right)\text{ for }c>1.

Then,

L\operatorname{log}L\frac{(\operatorname{log}x)^{2}}{\sqrt{x}}\operatorname{log}(N(k-1))\to 0\text{ as }x\to\infty.

\square

By the results of Newton and Thorne
[
NT21a
]
,
[
NT21b
]
, the hypothesis (1) in the above proposition is now known to be true for all
l\geq 1
. Therefore, in comparison to Proposition
5
, we have a larger range of
L
for which (
25
) holds, under GRH.

In what follows, we make some remarks here about
N_{\rho,L,f}(x)
for a random
f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
. We derive the expected value of
N_{\rho,L,f}(x)
as we average over all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
(not just non-CM newforms). As we will see, averaging enables us to obtain the asymptotic in (
25
) over a more flexible range of
L
.

We first introduce the following notation: for any
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\mathcal{F}_{N,k}\to\mathbb{C}
, we denote

\langle\phi\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}\phi(f).

We recall an estimate that follows from the Eichler-Selberg trace formula for the trace of Hecke operators on the space of primitive cusp forms of weight
k
and level
N
(see
[
MS10
, Section 3]
).

Proposition 7
.

Let
k
be a positive even integer and
N
be a positive integer. For a positive integer
n>1
such that
(n,N)=1,
we have

(28)

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}a_{f}(n)=\begin{cases}\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}{n^{1/2}}+\operatorname{O}\left(n\sigma_{0}(n)4^{\nu(N)}\right),&\text{ if }n\text{ is a square }\\
\operatorname{O}\left(n\sigma_{0}(n)4^{\nu(N)}\right),&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Here,
\sigma_{0}(n)
refers to the number of positive divisors of
n
and the implied constant in the error term is absolute.

We deduce the following corollary from (
28
).

Corollary 8
.

Let
k
be a positive even integer and
N
be a positive integer. For a prime
p
such that
(p,N)=1
and
l\geq 1
, we have

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}a_{f}(p^{2l})=\frac{1}{p^{l}}+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{lp^{2l}4^{\nu(N)}}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\right).

We use the above estimate to prove the following proposition, which tells us that an average version of (
25
) holds over a range of
L
that grows with the size of the families
\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
under consideration.

Proposition 9
.

Consider families
\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
with levels
N=N(x)
and even weights
k=k(x)
such that

\frac{\operatorname{log}\left(|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|/4^{\nu(N)}\right)}{\operatorname{log}x}\to\infty\text{ as }x\to\infty.

Let
\rho
be as chosen above. If
0<\psi<1
and
L
is chosen such that

L=\operatorname{o}\left(\frac{\operatorname{log}\left(|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|/4^{\nu(N)}\right)}{\operatorname{log}x}\right),

we have

\left\langle\frac{N_{\rho,L,f}(x)}{2\pi_{N}(x)}\right\rangle\sim\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{L}(t-\psi)\mu(t)dt\text{ as }x\to\infty.

Proof.

Applying (
24
), Corollary
8
and the estimate
U(l)\ll 1
,

\begin{split}&\left\langle\frac{N_{\rho,L,f}(x)}{2\pi_{N}(x)}-\frac{U(0)}{2L}\right\rangle\\
&=\frac{1}{\pi_{N}(x)}\sum_{1\leq l\leq L}\frac{U(l)}{L}\sum_{p\leq x\atop{(p,N)=1}}\left\langle a_{f}(p^{2l})\right\rangle\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi_{N}(x)}\sum_{1\leq l\leq L}\frac{U(l)}{L}\sum_{p\leq x\atop{(p,N)=1}}\left(\frac{1}{p^{l}}+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{lp^{2l}4^{\nu(N)}}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\right)\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi_{N}(x)L}\sum_{1\leq l\leq L}\sum_{p\leq x\atop{(p,N)=1}}\frac{U(l)}{p^{l}}+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{4^{\nu(N)}x^{2L}\pi_{N}(x)}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\right)\\
&=\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x}{\pi_{N}(x)}\right)+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{4^{\nu(N)}x^{2L}}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\right)\\
\end{split}

\text{ If }L=\operatorname{o}\left(\frac{\operatorname{log}\left(|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|/4^{\nu(N)}\right)}{\operatorname{log}x}\right),\text{ then, }x^{2L}=\operatorname{o}\left(\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}{4^{\nu(N)}}\right).

Thus, we have

\left\langle\frac{N_{\rho,L,f}(x)}{2\pi_{N}(x)}-\frac{U(0)}{2L}\right\rangle\to 0\text{ as }x\to\infty.

\square

2.2.
Remarks on dimension formulas for
S(N,k)

Formulas and bounds for
|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|
, that is, the dimension of the space of primitive cusp forms in
S(N,k)
have been well studied
[
Mar05
]
. It can be shown (
[
Mar05
, Theorem 6(c)]
) that for a positive integer
N
,

(29)

\frac{A_{0}(k-1)}{12}\phi(N)+\operatorname{O}(\sqrt{N})<|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|<\frac{(k-1)}{12}\phi(N)+\operatorname{O}\left(2^{\nu(N)}\right),

where

A_{0}=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}-p}\right)\approx 0.373956.

From the above, one gets the following estimate.

Lemma 10
.

Let
N
and
k
be positive integers with
k
even. Then, as
N+k\to\infty
,

|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|\gg\frac{Nk}{2^{\nu(N)}},

where
\nu(N)
denotes the number of distinct prime divisors of
N
.

Proof.

Note that
\phi(N)=N\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\geq\frac{N}{2^{\nu(N)}}
. By (
29
),

|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|>\frac{A_{0}(k-1)}{12}\frac{N}{2^{\nu(N)}}+\operatorname{O}(\sqrt{N}),

and therefore,

|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|\frac{2^{\nu(N)}}{kN}>\frac{A_{0}(k-1)}{12k}+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{2^{\nu(N)}}{k\sqrt{N}}\right).

But,
\frac{2^{\nu(N)}}{k\sqrt{N}}\to 0
if
N+k\to\infty
.

This proves the lemma.

\square

Therefore,

(30)

\frac{n\sigma_{0}(n)4^{\nu(N)}}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\ll\frac{n\sigma_{0}(n)8^{\nu(N)}}{k{N}}.

By Corollary
8
,

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}a_{f}(p^{2l})=\frac{1}{p^{l}}+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{lp^{2l}8^{\nu(N)}}{k{N}}\right).

We therefore deduce the following corollary from Proposition
9
.

Corollary 11
.

We consider families
\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
with levels
N=N(x)
and even weights
k=k(x)
such that

\frac{\operatorname{log}\left(k{N}/8^{\nu(N)}\right)}{\operatorname{log}x}\to\infty\text{ as }x\to\infty.

Let
\rho
be as chosen above. If
0<\psi<1
and
L
is chosen such that

L=\operatorname{o}\left(\frac{\operatorname{log}\left(k{N}/8^{\nu(N)}\right)}{\operatorname{log}x}\right),

we have

\left\langle\frac{N_{\rho,L,f}(x)}{2\pi_{N}(x)}\right\rangle\sim\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{L}(t-\psi)\mu(t)dt\text{ as }x\to\infty.

Remark 12
.

The above estimates tell us that if
N
varies over prime levels, then the above asymptotic will hold for families
\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
such that

\frac{\operatorname{log}kN}{\operatorname{log}x}\to\infty\text{ as }x\to\infty.

If
N=1
, then the above asymptotic will hold for families
\mathcal{F}_{1,k}
such that

\frac{\operatorname{log}k}{\operatorname{log}x}\to\infty\text{ as }x\to\infty.

2.3.
Remarks on the pair correlation function

In Section
2.1
, we saw that Thorner’s unconditional estimate (
26
) and the conditional estimate of Rouse and Thorner (
27
) for sums
\sum_{p\leq x}a_{f}(p^{2u})
play a pivotal role in deriving equidistribution properties of Hecke angles. By (
22
), these sums also appear in the pair correlation function
R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)
. But, we need estimates for these sums for
u
as large as
\pi_{N}(x)
, where as (
26
) holds for

u\ll\sqrt{\operatorname{log}x}/\sqrt{\operatorname{log}(kN\operatorname{log}x)}.

The conditional estimate (
27
) holds for all
u\geq 1
, if
x
is sufficiently large. However, when we apply this estimate to (
22
), we get

R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)\ll x(\operatorname{log}x)^{2}\operatorname{log}^{2}(N(k-1)),

which is not enough to determine the convergence of
R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)
as
x\to\infty
.

On the other hand, one can apply the trace formula estimates in Proposition
7
to obtain the limit

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)=A^{2}\widehat{g}(0)\rho\ast\rho(0),

albeit for rapidly increasing families
\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
, parametrized by

(31)

\frac{\operatorname{log}\left(k{N}/8^{\nu(N)}\right)}{x}\to\infty.

This was the main observation of Theorem
1

[
BS19
]
.

The main theorems of this article, Theorems
2
and
3
make the following fundamental observations over and above the results of
[
BS19
]
.

(1)

To simplify the
r
-th power moment

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}(R_{2}(g,\rho)(f))^{r}

addressed in this article, we require a delicate balancing act between estimates for several sums of type

\left\langle\sum_{p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{r}\leq x}a_{f}(p_{1}^{2u_{1}})a_{f}(p_{2}^{2u_{2}})\dots a_{f}(p_{r}^{2u_{r}})a_{f}(q_{1}^{2v_{1}})a_{f}(q_{2}^{2v_{2}})\dots a_{f}(q_{r}^{2v_{r}})\right\rangle

and the ranges of
u_{i}
and
v_{i}
in each of these sums. We also have to account for repetitions among the primes. Due to multiplicative relationships between
a_{f}(p^{2u})
for various prime powers
\,u\geq 0
, the above sum can be written in terms of appropriate polynomials (this is explained in Notations
20
(c) and
25
(c)). Here, we are called upon to record the growth of each coefficient in such a polynomial. By tracking and combining all the above parameters and a multilayered application of the Eichler-Selberg trace formula, we are able to obtain Theorem
2
.

(2)

The second observation is that for small values of
r
, we obtain the convergence of the
r
-th moment of
R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)
with the same choice of
L
and the same growth conditions for
\mathcal{F}_{N,k}
as those required for the convergence of the first moment of
R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)
. This is explained in Section
5.1
. However, we have obstructions in asserting such a convergence for larger values of
r
due to the non-uniform behaviour of the parameters coming from several different set-partitions in Theorem
2
.

(3)

The Katz-Sarnak conjecture implies that as
N+k\to\infty
,

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}\left(R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)\right)^{2}=\left(\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}(R_{2}(g,\rho)(f))\right)^{2}.

That is,

\mathbb{E}[(R_{2}(g,\rho)(f))^{2}]\sim\mathbb{E}[(R_{2}(g,\rho)(f))]^{2}\text{ as }x\to\infty.

By Theorem
3
, we are able to obtain this asymptotic for
(N(x),k(x))
such that

\frac{\operatorname{log}\left(kN/8^{\nu(N)}\right)}{x}\to\infty.

3.
Properties of Hecke eigenvalues and estimates

In this section, we collect multiplicative properties of Hecke eigenvalues and estimates for their averages. These will be used in the proof of Theorems
2
,
3
and
4
. We start with the following classical multiplicative relationship between Hecke eigenvalues.

Lemma 13
.

For primes
p_{1},\,p_{2}
coprime to the level
N
and nonnegative integers
i,\,j,

a_{f}(p_{1}^{i})a_{f}(p_{2}^{j})=\begin{cases}a_{f}(p_{1}^{i}p_{2}^{j}),&\text{ if }p_{1}\neq p_{2},\\
\sum_{l=0}^{\min{(i,j)}}a_{f}(p_{1}^{i+j-2l}),&\text{ if }p_{1}=p_{2}.\end{cases}

Moreover, if
p_{1}=p_{2},
then

\left(a_{f}(p_{1}^{2n_{1}})-a_{f}(p_{1}^{2n_{1}-2})\right)\left(a_{f}(p_{2}^{2n_{2}})-a_{f}(p_{2}^{2n_{2}-2})\right)

=\begin{cases}a_{f}(p_{1}^{2n_{1}+2n_{2}})-a_{f}(p_{1}^{2n_{1}+2n_{2}-2})+a_{f}(p_{1}^{|2n_{1}-2n_{2}|})-a_{f}(p_{1}^{|2n_{1}-2n_{2}|-2}),&\text{ if }n_{1}\neq n_{2},\\
a_{f}(p_{1}^{4n_{1}})-a_{f}(p_{1}^{4n_{1}-2})+2,&\text{ if }n_{1}=n_{2}.\end{cases}

Lemma 14
.

For a prime
p
coprime to the level
N
and integers
l\geq 0
and
n\geq 1
,

a_{f}(p^{2l})\left(a_{f}(p^{2n})-a_{f}(p^{2n-2})\right)=a_{f}(p^{2l+2n})+\begin{cases}a_{f}(p^{2l-2n})&\text{ if }l\geq n,\\
-a_{f}(p^{2n-2l-2})&\text{ if }l<n.\\
\end{cases}

The following propositions form the key to a major part of the calculations in the rest of this article.

Proposition 15
.

Let
k
be a positive even integer and
N
be a positive integer. Let
n>1
be a positive integer such that
n
is a square. Then, for any fixed real number
c^{\prime}>1
,

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}a_{f}(n)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{n^{c^{\prime}}8^{\nu(N)}}{k{N}}\right).

Here, the implied constant in the error term is absolute.

Proof.

By Proposition
7
,

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}a_{f}(n)=\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}{n^{1/2}}+\operatorname{O}\left(n\sigma_{0}(n)4^{\nu(N)}\right).

By an estimate from elementary number theory,

\sigma_{0}(n)\ll_{\epsilon}n^{\epsilon}\text{ for any }\epsilon>0.

We also have, by Lemma
10
,

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\ll\frac{2^{\nu(N)}}{Nk}.

The proposition follows by combining the above estimates.

\square

Proposition 16
.

Let
k=k(x)
and
N=N(x)
be positive integers with
k
even. Let

\sum_{(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{t})}^{(1)}

denote a sum over
t
distinct primes, where each prime is less than or equal to
x
.

If
(m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{t})=(0,0,\dots,0)
, then

(32)

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{t})}^{(1)}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}a_{f}(p_{1}^{2m_{1}}p_{2}^{2m_{2}}\dots p_{t}^{2m_{t}})=\pi_{N}(x)(\pi_{N}(x)-1)(\pi_{N}(x)-2)\dots(\pi_{N}(x)-(t-1)).

For a tuple
\underline{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},\dots,m_{t})
, let

Y(\underline{m})=\#\{1\leq i\leq t\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,m_{i}=0\}.

For an integer
a
such that
0\leq a\leq t-1
, let

\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},\dots,m_{t})}^{(a)}

denote a sum over a subset of the set of
t
-tuples

\{\underline{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},\dots,m_{t})\in\mathbb{Z}^{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,Y(\underline{m})=a\text{ and }m_{i}\leq M_{i}\text{ for each }1\leq i\leq t\}.

Then,

(33)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{t})}^{(1)}\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},\dots,m_{t})}^{(a)}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}a_{f}(p_{1}^{2m_{1}}p_{2}^{2m_{2}}\dots p_{t}^{2m_{t}})\\
&=\operatorname{O}\left(\pi_{N}(x)^{a}(\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x)^{t-a}\right)+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{\pi_{N}(x)^{t}x^{(2M_{1}+2M_{2}+\dots+2M_{t})c^{\prime}}8^{\nu(N)}}{k{N}}\right).\end{split}

Here,
c^{\prime}
is any real number greater than 1 and the implied constant in the error terms depends only on
t
.

Proof.

The equality in (
32
) is immediate.

Let us consider a tuple
\underline{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},\dots,m_{t})
such that
Y(\underline{m})=a
for some
0\leq a\leq t-1
.

If
a=0
, that is, if
m_{1},m_{2},\dots m_{t}\neq 0
, then by Proposition
15
,

(34)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},\dots,m_{t})}^{(0)}\sum_{(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{t})}^{(1)}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}a_{f}(p_{1}^{2m_{1}}p_{2}^{2m_{2}}\dots p_{t}^{2m_{t}})\\
&=\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},\dots,m_{t})}^{(0)}\sum_{(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{t})}^{(1)}\left[\frac{1}{p_{1}^{m_{1}}p_{2}^{m_{2}}\dots p_{t}^{m_{t}}}+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{\left(p_{1}^{2m_{1}}p_{2}^{2m_{2}}\dots p_{t}^{2m_{t}}\right)^{c^{\prime}}8^{\nu(N)}}{k{N}}\right)\right]\\
&\ll_{t}(\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}x)^{t}+\frac{\pi_{N}(x)^{t}x^{(2M_{1}+2M_{2}+\dots+2M_{t})c^{\prime}}8^{\nu(N)}}{k{N}}.\end{split}

Now, suppose
a>0
. We denote
t^{\prime}=t-a
. Clearly,
1\leq t^{\prime}\leq t
.

Without loss of generality, let us assume that
m_{1}=m_{2}=\dots=m_{t^{\prime}}\neq 0
and
m_{t^{\prime}+1},m_{t^{\prime}+2},\dots m_{t}=0
.

Applying Proposition
15
and equation (
32
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{t})}^{(1)}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}a_{f}(p_{1}^{2m_{1}}p_{2}^{2m_{2}}\dots p_{t}^{2m_{t}})\\
&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{t})}^{(1)}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}a_{f}(p_{1}^{2m_{1}}p_{2}^{2m_{2}}\dots p_{t^{\prime}}^{2m_{t^{\prime}}})\\
&\ll_{t}\pi_{N}(x)^{a}\sum_{(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{t^{\prime}})}^{(1)}\left(\frac{1}{p_{1}^{m_{1}}p_{2}^{m_{2}}\dots p_{t^{\prime}}^{m_{t^{\prime}}}}+O\left(\ \frac{8^{\nu(N)}p_{1}^{2m_{1}c^{\prime}}p_{2}^{2m_{2}c^{\prime}}\dots p_{t^{\prime}}^{2m_{t^{\prime}}c^{\prime}}}{k{N}}\right)\right).\\
\end{split}

Thus,

(35)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{N,k}|}\sum_{(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{t})}^{(1)}\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},\dots,m_{t})}^{(a)}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{N,k}}a_{f}(p_{1}^{2m_{1}}p_{2}^{2m_{2}}\dots p_{t}^{2m_{t}})\\

\begin{split}&a_{f}(s^{2l_{u_{k+1}}})\left(a_{f}(s^{2l_{u_{1}}})a_{f}(s^{2l_{u_{2}}})\dots a_{f}(s^{2l_{u_{k}}})\right)\\

5.3.
Conclusion

As we saw above, Theorem
2
gives us an “all-purpose” bound for the higher power moments of
R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)
. The application of this theorem, even for small values of
r
can become tedious. For example, if
r=4
, we have
1\leq j\leq 4
and
2\leq t\leq 7
. For each choice of
(t,j)
, we have to list out the partitions of
\mathcal{P}(4,t,j)
and evaluate the contribution to (
83
) for each of them. So, we have to consider 24 cases of
\mathcal{P}(4,t,j)
, and each case has numerous partitions. The parameters
x_{i,d}
can take different values for different partitions in
\mathcal{P}(r,t,j)
. As
r
increases, the number of cases, as well as the number of partitions in each case grow rapidly. Nonetheless, the explicit terms for the bound in Theorem
2
give us a template to compute the
r
-th power moment for a fixed
r
. Thus, for each
r
, we may try to establish a range of
L
for which this moment would converge to
(T(g,\rho)/4L)^{r}
. However, due to the unwieldy behaviour of
\mathcal{P}(r,t,j)
, we are currently unable to specify either a “uniform” range of
L
, or a range of
L
that depends on
r
such that
r
-th power moments of
R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)
would converge to
(T(g,\rho)/4L)^{r}
for all
r
. More precisely, we conclude with the following open questions:

(1)

Can we determine a function
L=L(x)\to\infty
such that

\left\langle R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)^{r}\right\rangle\sim\left(\frac{T(g,\rho)}{4L}\right)^{r}\text{ as }x\to\infty?

for
all
integers
r\geq 2
?

(2)

Let
r\geq 2
be a fixed integer. Can we determine a function
L=L_{r}=L_{r}(x)\to\infty
(which depends on
r
) such that

\left\langle R_{2}(g,\rho)(f)^{r}\right\rangle\sim\left(\frac{T(g,\rho)}{4L_{r}}\right)^{r}\text{ as }x\to\infty?

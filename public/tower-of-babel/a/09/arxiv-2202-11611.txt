Node Polynomials for Curves on Surfaces This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Enumerative and Gauge-Theoretic Invariants in honor of Lothar Göttsche on the occasion of his 60th birthday. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/Gottsche.html
Steven KLEIMAN a and Ragni PIENE b

Abstract
We complete the proof of a theorem we announced and partly proved in [ Math. Nachr. 271 (2004), 69–90, math.AG/0111299]. The theorem concerns a family of curves on a family of surfaces. It has two parts. The first was proved in that paper. It describes a natural cycle that enumerates the curves in the family with precisely r r ordinary nodes. The second part is proved here. It asserts that, for r ≤ 8 r\leq 8 , the class of this cycle is given by a computable universal polynomial in the pushdowns to the parameter space of products of the Chern classes of the family.

Introduction

This paper is the fourth in a series about enumerating nodal curves on
smooth complex surfaces. Here we complete the proof of Theorem 2.5 on p. 74 in
[
16
]
. It has two parts. The first was proved in
[
16
]
. It describes a natural cycle
U(r)
on the parameter space
of a family of pairs of a surface and of a curve on it;
U(r)

enumerates the curves with precisely
r
ordinary nodes. The second part
is proved here. It asserts that, for
r\leq 8
, the class
[U(r)]
is
given by a computable universal polynomial in the pushdowns of products
of the Chern classes of the family.

The second part was not proved in
[
16
]
, because we believed our
approach, inspired by Vainsencher’s paper
[
27
]
, would eventually
yield an algorithm for computing the entire polynomial for
[U(r)]
not
only for
r\leq 7
, but also for
r=8
and perhaps for all
r
. So we
chose to publish only the construction of
U(r)
and to postpone the
rest. Unfortunately, we were too optimistic. Thus here we work out an
ad hoc determination of the polynomial for
r=8
; specifically, we show
that the “correction term” is independent of the family, and so can be
found by working out a particular example, such as we did in

[
16
, Example 3.8, p. 80]
.

In
[
16
, Remark 2.7, p. 74]
we conjectured that the class

[U(r)]
is given for all
r
by a universal polynomial in certain
classes
y(a,b,c)
, defined here in Section
4.4
, which
are pushdowns of products of the (relative) Chern classes of the family.
Moreover, this polynomial should be of the form
P_{r}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})/r!
,
where
P_{r}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
is the
r
th (complete) Bell polynomial and
the
a_{i}
are linear polynomials in the
y(a,b,c)
.

Göttsche
[
9
]
had already conjectured the special case
where the pairs consist of a fixed surface and of the divisors in a
linear system. This celebrated conjecture was proved independently by
Tzeng
[
26
]
and Kool–Shende–Thomas
[
18
]
. For the
history of the case of plane curves, see
[
16
, Remark 3.7, p. 78]

and the more recent
[
4
]
.

A part of our conjecture has now been proved by Laarakker

[
19
, Theorem A, p. 4921]
. He defined a cycle
\gamma(r)

which, under suitable genericity assumptions on the family, is supported
on
U(r)
, and its class
[\gamma(r)]
is given by a universal
polynomial in the
y(a,b,c)
. Although he did not prove that the
polynomial is Bell (see his footnote
[
19
, p. 4918]
), he
did prove that
[\gamma(r)]
is “multiplicative” when the family of
surfaces is a direct sum of families over the same base (see

[
19
, Lemma 5.5 and Remark 5.6, p. 4936]
). When the family
is trivial, Göttsche had observed that this multiplicative property
implies the polynomial is Bell.
However, when the family is
nontrivial, the multiplicative property is insufficient.

In
[
16
]
we applied our theorem in several enumerations involving
nontrivial families of surfaces, including the family of all planes in

\mathbb{P}^{4}
. In
[
19
, Theorem B, p. 4922]
Laarakker proved
that the number of
r
-nodal plane curves of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}

meeting the appropriate number of general lines, is given by a universal
polynomial in
d
of degree
\leq 9+2r
. Moreover, he explicitly computed
the polynomial for
r\leq 12
. In
[
21
]
Mukherjee,
Paul, and Singh did the same; they obtained a recursive formula, and
verified that their results agree with Laarakker’s. In
[
5
]

Das and Mukherjee treated the case where the curves may have one
additional nonnodal singularity. In
[
22
]
Mukherjee and
Singh did the same for rational curves.

In
[
16
, Remark 2.7, p. 74]
we conjectured that universal
polynomials also enumerate curves with any given equisingularity type.
In
[
13
, Theorem 10.1, p. 713]
Kazaryan gave a “topological
justification” of our conjecture, but gave no algebraic proof. He
worked with a linear system on a fixed surface, and found several
explicit formulas for curves with singularities of codimension
\leq 7
.
A few of these formulas had been given in
[
15
, Theorem 1.2, p. 210]
. In
[
2
]
Basu and Mukherjee gave recursive
formulas for the number of curves in a linear system on a fixed surface
that have
r
nodes and one additional singularity of codimension
\leq 8-r
. In particular, their formula for
8
-nodal curves recovers ours in
this case; see
[
15
, Theorem 1.1, p. 210]
.

In
[
20
]
Li and Tzeng and, independently in
[
23
]
,
Rennemo proved the existence of universal polynomials enumerating
divisors with isolated singularities of given topological or analytical
types in a trivial family of varieties of arbitrary dimension.

In short, we work here over an algebraically closed field of
characteristic
0
with pairs
(F/Y,D)
, where
Y
is a Cohen–Macaulay
algebraic scheme,
F/Y
is a smooth projective family of surfaces, and

D
is a relative, or
Y
-flat, effective divisor on
F
. We let
\pi\colon F\to Y
denote the structure map.

In Section
2
, given a pair
(F/Y,D)
, we recall from

[
15
, pp. 226–227]
the construction and elementary properties
of its induced pairs
(F_{i}/X_{i},D_{i})
. Then we prove some further
properties. Intuitively,
(F_{i}/X_{i},D_{i})
represents a family of curves
that sit on blowups of the surfaces of
F/Y
and that have one less

i
-fold point.

In Section
3
, the main results are Lemmas
3.2

and
3.3
, which concern properties of certain subschemes
of the relative Hilbert scheme
\Hilb_{D/Y}^{3r}
.
In Section
4
, we develop some results of bivariant
intersection theory for use in the subsequent sections. Our treatment
here generalizes and improves our shorter one in
[
15
]
.

In Section
5
, we state the main theorem, Theorem
5.4
.
Then we prove a key recursion relation; we prove the theorem for
r\leq 7
; and we explain what more is needed for
r=8
. The difficulty is
that the induced pair
(F_{2}/X_{2},D_{2})
does not satisfy the hypotheses of
the theorem, as
D_{2}/X_{2}
has nonreduced fibers in codimension
7

above the relative quadruple-point locus
X_{4}
of
D/Y
.

Therefore, the recursion that works for
r\leq 7
must be corrected
accordingly. In Section
6
we find an expression for the
correction term, and in Section
7
we prove that the
correction term is equal to
C[X_{4}]
for some integer
C
that is
independent of the given
(F/Y,D)
. Our proof illustrates the advantage
of developing intersection theory over any universally catenary
Noetherian base. Thus, to complete the proof of the theorem, it
suffices to compute the integer
C
in a particular case, such as that
of
8
-nodal quintic plane curves, which we did in

[
16
, Example 3.8, p. 80]
.

However, our proof requires an additional genericity hypothesis: the
analytic type of a fiber of
D
at an ordinary quadruple point must not
remain constant along any irreducible component of
X_{4}
. This
hypothesis comes into play at just one spot in the proof of Lemma
7.4
to ensure a certain map is flat. We believe that Lemma
7.4
and Theorem
5.4
hold without this hypothesis.
At any rate, the hypothesis is usually fulfilled in practice.

Lemma 2.2

Lemma 2.6

that each

has weight at most that of

.
So

. Hence

Y(\mathbf{D})=q(G(\mathbf{D}))\smallsetminus\bigl(Y(\infty)\cup\big({\textstyle\bigcup}\{q(G(\mathbf{D}^{\prime}))\mid\mathbf{D}^{\prime}\in\Sigma\text{ and }\deg\mathbf{D}^{\prime}>d\}\big)\bigr).

But

To prove the second bound in (

), note that

Thus, as desired,

Finally, suppose

Then, as we have just seen, there is an injection

, and each

has weight at most that
of

. But there are only finitely many such

, as
desired. ∎

Definition 2.7
.

We say that

Fix

3
Virtual double points

The minimal Enriques diagram
r\mathbf{A}_{1}
consists of
r
roots of weight
2 and no other vertices. The corresponding scheme
G(r\mathbf{A}_{1})
is
particularly important, as it is equal to the subscheme of the Hilbert
scheme
\Hilb^{3r}_{D/Y}
associated to the geometric fibers of
D/Y

with at least
r
distinct singular points. Moreover, we need to
consider it for various
(F/Y,D)
and
r
. So, for clarity, we set

G(F/Y,D;r):=G(r\mathbf{A}_{1})
.

In this section, we first recall the basic properties of

, which were treated in
[
17
, Proposition 5.9,
p. 439]
. Then we fix
r\geq 1
, and assume
(F/Y,D)
is
r
-generic.
For each

, we find a natural large open subscheme of

such that the associated geometric fibers of

have exactly

r_{i}
nodes. None lies on the exceptional divisor of a fiber of

.
Further, adding the exceptional divisor to the fiber of

yields
a fiber of

,
which is dense in the preimage of

. These results are treated in

and

below for later use.

3.1
.

Subschemes of the Hilbert scheme.

Fix
r
. If

, let
H(r)
denote the open subscheme of
\Hilb^{r}_{F/Y}
over
which the universal family is smooth; in other words,

parameterizes the unions of
r
distinct reduced points in the geometric

. By convention, if

, then

and

are both equal to

; if

, then both are empty.

If

, then Proposition 5.9 on p. 439 in
[

]
asserts that

and that the map
\Psi\colon H(r)\to\Hilb^{3r}_{F/Y}
is
given on

-points, where

is a

-scheme, by sending a subscheme

of

, say with ideal

, to the subscheme

with ideal

, and for

, set

. Finally, for an arbitrary

and for

, set

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix
r\geq 1
. Assume that
(F/Y,D)
is
r
-generic and that
Y(\infty)

is empty. Then there is an open subscheme

such that
(1)

for every

,

\vskip-2.15277pt\cod_{y}(Y\smallsetminus U,Y)\geq r+1

(3.1)

and
(2)
for every

with

, if we set

U_{i}:=\big(\pi^{-1}U\big)\cap(X_{i}\smallsetminus X_{i+1})\qquad\text{and}\qquad V_{i}:=\big(\pi^{-1}U\big)\cap(X_{i-1}\smallsetminus X_{i+1}),

is dense in

, and there is a natural isomorphism of

-schemes

\gamma_{i}\colon\ H_{i}\times_{F}U_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{$\longrightarrow$}}\limits^{\sim}}H_{i-1}\times_{F}U_{i}.

Proof.

Let

be the complement in

of the union of the closures of those

with
\cod\mathbf{D}\geq r+1
. Then

is open since there are only
finitely many nonempty

by Lemma

. By the same

) implies (

). Thus (1) holds.

Fix

such that

. Then

. Let

be
a component of
X_{i}
; let

)
yields that

is dense in

Let

, and

-fold point at

We define

Lemma 3.3

, the closed

H_{i-1}\times_{F}U_{i}

Consider any
T

be the preimage
of

is an open subscheme, as

.

Let
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}_{T}
denote the ideal of

. Then it suffices
to prove that the stalk
\mathcal{I}_{t}
vanishes for all

for the

is coherent, the

, where

vanishes form an open subset

. By hypothesis,

.
But, if

, then

; whence,

.
So

. Give

the induced structure as an open subscheme of

T
. Then
T^{\prime}
is the closed subscheme of
T^{\prime\prime}
with ideal
\mathcal{I}\mid_{T^{\prime\prime}}
. But

. Thus

is equal to the open
subscheme
T^{\prime\prime}
.

Given

, to check if
\mathcal{I}_{t}
vanishes, we may replace

by

\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{T,t})
. Thus we may assume that

is of the form

\Spec(A)
, where

is local and that

is nonempty. Then it
suffices to prove the ideal

of

vanishes, or

There exists a flat local homomorphism

such that

is
complete and its residue class field is algebraically closed. Then

is faithfully flat. So

vanishes if

does. Thus
we may replace

by

, and so assume that

is complete and its

Consider the composition

. Via it,

is
the preimage of

. Hence

if and only if
(D_{i}^{\prime})_{T}
is
effective, owing to Lemma

. By the same token,

is effective.

Consider the local ring

of

at the closed point of the center
of the blowing up

be its completion. Since

is faithfully flat,

is effective if and only if

As

is a smooth family of surfaces and as

Remark 5.6
.

Assume
(F/Y,D)
is the direct sum of
two pairs
(F^{\prime}/Y,D^{\prime})
and
(F^{\prime\prime}/Y,D^{\prime\prime})
over the same base. Then
the
r
-nodal curves of
D\to Y
consists of the unions of the

(r-i)
-nodal curves of
D^{\prime}\to Y
and
the
i
-nodal curves of
D^{\prime\prime}\to Y
for
i=0,\dots,r
. Hence, the
existence of a universal polynomial for
r
-nodal curves implies that
the generating series for
(F/Y,D)
is equal to the product of the
generating series for
(F^{\prime}/Y,D^{\prime})
and
(F^{\prime\prime}/Y,D^{\prime\prime})
. This fact was
observed by Göttsche in the case of a trivial family

[
9
, p. 525]
, and by Laarakker in the general case

[
19
, Section 5.1, p. 4935]
. In the case of a trivial family,
Göttsche observed that this multiplicativity implies that the
universal polynomials are Bell polynomials. However, as observed by
Laarakker, this conclusion does not follow in the case of a nontrivial
family.

Let
a_{j}(D)
,
a_{j}(D^{\prime})
,
a_{j}(D^{\prime\prime})
be the classes, introduced in
Theorem
5.4
, for the three pairs. Clearly,

a_{j}(D)=a_{j}(D^{\prime})+a_{j}(D^{\prime\prime})
. So (for
r\leq 8
),
in the notation of (
5.2
),

u(D,r)=\frac{1}{r!}P_{r}(a_{\bullet}(D))\cap[Y]=\frac{1}{r!}P_{r}(a_{\bullet}(D^{\prime})+a_{\bullet}(D^{\prime\prime}))\cap[Y].

By the binomial property of the Bell polynomials,
the right-hand side is equal to

\sum_{i=0}^{r}\frac{1}{(r-i)!}P_{r-i}(a_{\bullet}(D^{\prime}))\frac{1}{i!}P_{i}(a_{\bullet}(D^{\prime\prime}))\cap[Y].

Hence the Bell polynomial shape of the universal polynomials is in
agreement with the multiplicative property of the generating series of
(F/Y,D)
.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{7!}P_{7}(a_{\bullet}(D_{2}))\cap[X_{2}]\!-u(D_{2},7)=\frac{1}{7!}e(W_{1})\!-\frac{3!}{7!}e(W_{2})\!-\frac{4!}{7!}e(W_{3})\!-\frac{5!}{7!2!}e(W_{4})\!-\frac{6!}{7!3!}e(W_{5}).

Proof.

Recall that the classes
a_{s}(D_{2})
on
X_{2}
are obtained
by pushing down the classes
b_{s}(D_{2})
on
F_{2}
obtained by applying
Algorithm 2.3 of
[
16
, p. 72]
to the pair
(F_{2}/X_{2},D_{2})
. In
the case that
X_{4}=\varnothing
, the Algorithm would have produced the
formula
u(D_{2},7)=\frac{1}{7!}P_{7}(a_{\bullet}(D_{2}))\cap[X_{2}]
.
Removing the classes

, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{7!}P_{7}(a_{\bullet}(D_{2}))\cap[X_{2}]={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{7!}\bigl(\pi_{7\#}\big[\mathfrak{X}_{2}^{(7)}\big]-e(W_{1})\bigr)-\frac{3!}{7!}\bigl(\pi_{4\#}\big[\mathfrak{X}_{3}^{(4)}\big]-e(W_{2})\bigr)

\displaystyle-\frac{4!}{7!}\bigl(\pi_{4\#}\big[\mathfrak{X}_{2}\big(D_{3}^{(4)}\big)\big]-e(W_{3})\bigr)

\displaystyle-\frac{5!}{7!2!}\bigl(\pi_{4\#}\big[\mathfrak{X}_{2}\big(D^{(4)}-E^{(3)}\big)\big]-e(W_{4})\bigr)

Lemma

7
Independence of the correction term

In this section, we prove Theorem
7.5
, which asserts that the
correction term (
5.3
) is equal to
C[X_{4}]
, where
C
is
independent of the strongly 8-generic pair
(F/Y,D)
with

Y(\infty)=\varnothing
. We work locally analytically on
F
at a general
closed point
x
in
X_{4}
. Section
7.1
describes the local
setup. Lemma
7.2
asserts that locally we have the
properness we need to pushdown classes. Lemma
7.3
asserts that
the key classes
e(W_{i})
pull back to their local counterparts
e\big(\widehat{W}_{i}\big)
.

Lemma
7.4
asserts that the coefficient in
e\big(\widehat{W}_{i}\big)
of

\big[\widehat{X}_{4}\big]
depends only on the analytic type of the ordinary quadruple
point
x\in D_{\pi(x)}
; namely, on the cross ratio of the four tangents
at
x
. Its proof requires
(F/Y,D)
to be strongly 8-generic.
Finally, we prove Theorem
7.5
by exhibiting a pair
(F/Y,D)
,
where
X_{4}
is irreducible and where any given value of the cross ratio
appears at some
x\in X_{4}
.

7.1
.

The local setup.

Fix an 8-generic pair
(F/Y,D)
with
Y(\infty)=\varnothing
, and a
general closed point
x\in X_{4}
. By
general

x

lies on a single irreducible component
Z
of
X_{4}
and that
x
is an

D_{\pi(x)}
. Let us arrange for every
x\in X_{4}
to be general as follows. First, if two components
Z^{\prime}
and

Z^{\prime\prime}
of
X_{4}
meet, then
\dim(Z^{\prime}\cap Z^{\prime\prime})<\dim X_{4}

\cod\pi(Z^{\prime}\cap Z^{\prime\prime})>\cod\pi(X_{4})

\cod(\pi(X_{4}),Y)=8
by
(
2.11
) as
(F/Y,D)

Z^{\prime}\cap Z^{\prime\prime}
. Second we may discard the locus of
y\in Y
, where
D_{y}
has a singularity
x
worse than an ordinary quadruple point, again because

(F/Y,D)
is 8-generic.

Set
\widetilde{F}:=\Spec\mathcal{O}_{F,x}
and
\widetilde{Y}:=\Spec\mathcal{O}_{Y,\pi(x)}
and

\widetilde{D}:=\Spec\mathcal{O}_{D,x}
. Denote the induced pair of
\big(\widetilde{F}/\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{D}\big)
by
\big(\widetilde{F}_{2}/\widetilde{X}_{2},\widetilde{D}_{2}\big)
. The bundles of relative
principal parts are compatible not only with the base change
\widetilde{Y}\to Y
, but also with the maps
\widetilde{F}\to F
and
\widetilde{F}^{(j)}\to F^{(j)}
;
cf.
[
11
, Proposition 16.4.14, p. 22]
. So although the
\widetilde{X}_{i}

for
i\geq 2
are defined in terms of
\big(\widetilde{F}/\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{D}\big)
, we have

\widetilde{X}_{i}=\Spec\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{i},x}
. Similarly, the schemes
constructed in Section
6
for
(F_{2}/X_{2},D_{2})
induce the
corresponding schemes for
\big(\widetilde{F}_{2}/\widetilde{X}_{2},\widetilde{D}_{2}\big)
. Denote by

\widetilde{W}^{(j)}_{i}
the scheme corresponding to
W^{(j)}_{i}
; see (
6.2
).

Next consider the completions of the local rings, giving us a pair
\big(\widehat{F}/\widehat{Y},\widehat{D}\big)
. Replacing the principal parts bundles by their
completions, cf.
[
6
, Example 16.14, p. 416]
, construct the

\widehat{X}_{i}
, the induced pair
\big(\widehat{F}_{2}/\widehat{X}_{2},\widehat{D}_{2}\big)
, and the
corresponding schemes of Section
6
. Since the complete
principal parts bundles are pullbacks,
\widehat{X}_{i}=\Spec\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{i},x}
, and all the schemes of Section
6
for

(F_{2}/X_{2},D_{2})
pull back to the corresponding schemes for
\big(\widehat{F}_{2}/\widehat{X}_{2},\widehat{D}_{2}\big)
. Denote by
\widehat{W}^{(j)}_{i}
the scheme
corresponding to
\widetilde{W}^{(j)}_{i}
, so to
W^{(j)}_{i}
.

The classes
e(W_{i})
of Section
6.3
are sums of pushdowns of
classes on the
W^{(j)}_{i}
. By Lemma
7.2
below, the
\widehat{W}^{(j)}_{i}
are proper over
\widehat{X}_{2}
; hence, we may form the
corresponding classes
e(\widehat{W}_{i})
for the pair
\big(\widehat{F}_{2}/\widehat{X}_{2},\widehat{D}_{2}\big)
. Denote them by
e(\widehat{W}_{i})
.

Let
\epsilon\colon\widehat{X}_{2}\to X_{2}
denote the composition of the flat maps

\widehat{X}_{2}\to\widetilde{X}_{2}
and
\widetilde{X}_{2}\to X_{2}
. Then
\big[\widehat{X}_{4}\big]=\epsilon^{*}[X_{4}]
, and Lemma
7.3
asserts
e(\widehat{W}_{i})=\epsilon^{*}e(W_{i})
.

Each
e(W_{i})
is the class of a cycle
U_{i}
on
X_{4}
of dimension
\dim X_{4}
. Say that the component
Z
of
X_{4}
containing
x
appears in

U_{i}
with coefficient
C^{\prime}_{i}
and in the fundamental cycle
|X_{4}|
with
coefficient
C^{\prime\prime}_{i}
. Set
C_{i}:=C_{i}^{\prime}/C_{i}^{\prime\prime}
. Then the cycles
U_{i}

and
C_{i}|X_{4}|
become equal after restriction to a neighborhood of

,
so the classes
e(W_{i})
and
C_{i}[X_{4}]
do too. Thus

e\big(\widehat{W}_{i}\big)=C_{i}\big[\widehat{X}_{4}\big];

(7.1)

furthermore,

is independent of the choice of

in

.

Lemma 7.2
.

The schemes
\widehat{W}^{(j)}_{i}
are proper over
\widehat{X}_{2}
.

Proof.

Let us first show that the schemes
\widetilde{W}^{(j)}_{i}
and
W^{(j)}_{i}|_{\widetilde{X}_{2}}
have the same support. It suffices to consider only the

W^{(j)}_{1}
as the other cases are similar.

Let
E_{j}\subset F^{(j)}
be the union of the strict transforms
of the exceptional divisors
E^{(1)},\dots,E^{(j)}
; see

[
17
, Definition 3.5, p. 423]
. It follows from the description in
Section
6
that
W^{(j)}_{1}
is supported in
E_{j}
. The fibers
of the exceptional divisor of
\widetilde{F}_{2}
are the same as the
corresponding fibers of the exceptional divisor of
F_{2}
. Hence, above

x\in\widetilde{X}_{2}
, the fiber of
E^{(j)}
lies in
\widetilde{F}_{2}^{(j)}
. Thus

W^{(j)}_{1}|_{\widetilde{X}_{2}}
has support in
\widetilde{F}^{(j)}_{2}
, and it is the
same as the support of
\widetilde{W}^{(j)}_{i}
; moreover, this support is proper
over
\widetilde{X}_{2}
.

For
w\in W^{(j)}_{i}|_{\widetilde{X}_{2}}
, the map
\mathcal{O}_{F^{(j)}_{2},w}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{W^{(j)},w}
pulls back to
\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{F}^{(j)}_{2},w}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{W}^{(j)},w}
. But clearly
\mathcal{O}_{F^{(j)}_{2},w}=\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{F}^{(j)}_{2},w}
;
hence
\mathcal{O}_{W^{(j)}_{i},w}=\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{W}^{(j)_{1}},w}
. Hence
\widetilde{W}^{(j)}_{i}=W^{(j)}_{i}|_{\widetilde{X}_{2}}
. Therefore,
\widetilde{W}^{(j)}_{i}
is proper
over
\widetilde{X}_{2}
. Thus the pullback
\widehat{W}^{(j)}_{i}
is proper over
\widehat{X}_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

We have
e\big(\widehat{W}_{i}\big)=\epsilon^{*}e(W_{i})
.

Proof.

It suffices to check that each summand of
e(W_{i})

pulls back to the corresponding summand of
e\big(\widehat{W}_{i}\big)
. Here we
only consider the first summand of
e(W_{1})
, since the other cases
are similar.

In (
6.1
), we defined the schemes
\overline{F}_{2}^{(j)}
. Denote
by
\epsilon_{2}^{(j)}\colon\overline{\widehat{F}}_{2}^{(j)}\to\overline{F}_{2}^{(j)}

the map induced by the map
\epsilon
defined in Section
7.1
. Notice that, as
\epsilon_{2}^{(7)}
is flat,

s\big(\widehat{W}_{1}^{(7)},\overline{\widehat{F}}_{2}^{(7)}\big)=s\big(\epsilon_{2}^{(7)-1}W_{1}^{(7)},\epsilon_{2}^{(7)-1}\overline{F}_{2}^{(7)}\big)=\epsilon_{2}^{(7)*}s\big(W_{1}^{(7)},\overline{F}_{2}^{(7)}\big);

\widehat{\pi}_{\#}^{(j)}\epsilon_{2}^{(j)*}=\epsilon_{2}^{(j-1)*}\pi_{\#}^{(j)}

Thus

\displaystyle e\big(\widehat{W}_{i}\big)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\pi}^{(1)}_{\#}\dotsb\widehat{\pi}^{(7)}_{\#}\big\{c\big(\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{(7)}\big)\cap s\big(\widehat{W}_{1}^{(7)},\overline{\widehat{F}}_{2}^{(7)}\big)\big\}_{\dim X_{4}}

Lemma 7.4
.

Assume
(F/Y,D)
is strongly
8
-generic. Then
C_{i}
depends just on
the analytic type of
D_{\pi(x)}
at
x
, but is otherwise inpenendent
of the choice of
(F/Y,D)
.

Proof.

Recall that
x
is an ordinary quadruple point of
\widehat{D}_{\widehat{\pi}(x)}
.
Let
(\mathbb{V}/\mathbb{B},\mathbb{D})
be its versal deformation; see

[
12
, Example 14.0.1, p. 101 and Theorem 14.1, p. 103]
.
Recall how
(\mathbb{V}/\mathbb{B},\mathbb{D})
is constructed. Take variables

t_{1},\dots,t_{9},u,v
. Identify
\widehat{F}_{\pi(x)}
with
\Spec k[[u,v]]
.
Say
\widehat{D}_{\pi(x)}
is defined by
f(u,v)
in
k[[u,v]]
, and choose

g_{1},\dots,g_{9}
in
k[u,v]
whose classes in
k[[u,v]]/(f,f_{u},f_{v})
form
a basis of that vector space. Then

\mathbb{B}:=\Spec k[t_{1},\dots,t_{9}]\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{D}:=\Spec B[[u,v]]\big/\Bigl(f+\sum t_{i}g_{i}\Bigr).

Note that

(\mathbb{V}/\mathbb{B},\mathbb{D})
depends just on the analytic type of

D_{\pi(x)}
at
x
.

Since
x\in\widehat{D}_{\pi(x)}
is an ordinary quadruple point,

f=f_{4}+f_{5}+\cdots
, where
f_{4}
is a product of independent linear forms.
Choose the
g_{i}
so that only
g_{9}\in(u,v)^{4}
. Define
\mathbb{B}_{4}

by the vanishing of
t_{1},\dotsc,t_{8}
. Then
b\in\mathbb{B}
lies in
\mathbb{B}_{4}
iff the fiber
\mathbb{D}_{b}
has a quadruple point.

Recall from
[
12
, Theorem 14.1, p. 103]
that there exists a
map
\delta\colon\widehat{Y}\to\mathbb{B}
such that
\widehat{D}
and
\mathbb{D}\times_{\mathbb{B}}\widehat{Y}
become isomorphic after completion along their fibers over

\widehat{\pi}(x)
. Since
\widehat{D}
is complete at
x
, it is already complete
along its fiber. Form the completions
\widehat{\mathbb{V}}
,
\widehat{\mathbb{B}}
,
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}
,
\widehat{\mathbb{B}}_{4}
at the origin
b_{0}
of
\mathbb{B}
. Then
\delta\colon\widehat{Y}\to\mathbb{B}
factors through a map
\widehat{\delta}\colon\widehat{Y}\to\widehat{\mathbb{B}}
,
and
\widehat{D}
is isomorphic to the completion of
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}\times_{\widehat{\mathbb{B}}}\widehat{Y}
along its fiber over
\widehat{\pi}(x)
. Each
subscheme
\widehat{X}_{i}
of
\widehat{D}
is the pullback of the corresponding
subscheme of
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}\times_{\widehat{\mathbb{B}}}\widehat{Y}
, which, in turn, is
the pullback of the corresponding subscheme
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{i}
of
\widehat{\mathbb{D}}
.

Let us show
\widehat{\delta}\colon\widehat{Y}\to\widehat{\mathbb{B}}
is flat. Notice

\widehat{\delta}\big(\widehat{\pi}\big(\widehat{X}_{4}\big)\big)\subset\widehat{\mathbb{B}}_{4}
. But

\widehat{\delta}\big(\widehat{\pi}\big(\widehat{X}_{4}\big)\big)\neq\{b_{0}\}
because
(F/Y,D)
is strongly
8-generic. However,
\dim\widehat{\mathbb{B}}_{4}=1
. It follows that

\cod\big(\delta^{-1}(b_{0}),\widehat{\pi}\big(\widehat{X}_{4}\big)\big)=1
. Now,
\cod\big(\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{X}_{4}),\widehat{Y}\big)=8
by (
2.11
) since
(F/Y,D)
is 8-generic. So

\cod\big(\widehat{\delta}^{-1}(b_{0}),\widehat{Y}\big)=9
. But
\widehat{Y}
is
Cohen–Macaulay, and
\widehat{\mathbb{B}}
is smooth. Thus
\widehat{\delta}
is flat
by
[
10
, Proposition 15.4.2, p. 230]
.

Form the class
e\big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{i}\big)
for
\big(\widehat{\mathbb{V}}/\widehat{\mathbb{B}},\widehat{\mathbb{D}}\big)
analogous to the class
e\big(\widehat{W}_{i}\big)
for
\big(\widehat{F}/\widehat{Y},\widehat{D}\big)
. The map
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{2}\times_{\widehat{\mathbb{B}}}\widehat{Y}\to\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{2}

is flat, as it is induced by
\widehat{\delta}
. Since the map
\widehat{X}_{2}\to\mathbb{X}_{2}\times_{\mathbb{B}}\widehat{Y}
is also flat, we can argue as in the proof of
Lemma
7.3
to conclude that
e\big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{i}\big)
pulls back to
e\big(\widehat{W}_{i}\big)
. Owing to the same flatness, the fundamental class

\big[\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{4}\big]
on
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{2}
pulls back to the fundamental class
\big[\widehat{X}_{4}\big]
on
\widehat{X}_{2}
.

Form the equation
e\big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{i}\big)=\mathbb{C}_{i}\big[\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{4}\big]
on

analogous to

on
\widehat{X}_{2}
, see (
7.1
). The former equation pulls back to the latter owing to
the preceding paragraph. Thus
C_{i}=\mathbb{C}_{i}
. But
\mathbb{C}_{i}
depends
just on
(\mathbb{V}/\mathbb{B},\mathbb{D})
, so just on the analytic type of

D_{\pi(x)}
at
x
. Thus
C_{i}
depends just on the analytic type of

D_{\pi(x)}
at
x
.

Theorem 7.5
.

Assume
(F/Y,D)
is strongly
8
-generic. Then the correction term

(
5.3
)
is equal to
C[X_{4}]
, where
C
is independent of
the choice of
(F/Y,D)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.4
, each
C_{i}
depends just on the analytic
type of
D_{\pi(x)}
at
x
; that is, on the cross ratio of the four
tangents at
x
. By the last line in Section
7.1
,
furthermore,
C_{i}
is independent of the choice of
x
in
Z
. Below,
we exhibit a pair where
X_{4}
is irreducible and where any given value
of the cross ratio appears at some
x\in X_{4}
. It follows that
C_{i}
is
independent of the choice of
(F/Y,D)
. Finally, Proposition
6.4
now implies that
C
is independent too, as desired

To build the pair, say
k
is the base field, take variables

t_{1},\dotsc,t_{8},t,u,v
, and set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=k\biggl[t_{1},\dotsc,t_{8},t,\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{t-1}\biggr],\qquad\mathcal{B}:=A[u,v],\qquad\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{B}/(g),

where

\displaystyle g:=t_{1}+t_{2}u+t_{3}v+t_{4}u^{2}+t_{5}uv+t_{6}v^{2}+t_{7}u^{2}v+t_{8}uv^{2}+uv(u-v)(u-tv).

Set
Y:=\Spec\mathcal{A}
and
F:=\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k}\times Y
and
D:=\overline{\Spec\mathcal{C}}
.

Then
X_{4}\subset\Spec\mathcal{B}
. Its ideal
\mathcal{I}
is generated by the
partial derivatives with respect to
u
and
v
of
g
up to order
three; so
I=(t_{1},\dots,t_{8},u,v)
. It follows that
X_{4}=\Spec(\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{I})=\Spec k\big[t,\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{t-1}\big]
. Thus
X_{4}
is
irreducible.

Given
c\in k
, let
x\in X_{4}
be the point with
t=c
. Then the fiber

D_{\pi(x)}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k}
is equal to the four lines

uv(u-v)(u-cv)
through
(0:0:1)
with cross ratio equal to
c
. Thus
all cross ratios appear in this family, as desired.
∎

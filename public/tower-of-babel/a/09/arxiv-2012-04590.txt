Extensions of toric line bundles
Klaus Altmann, Amelie Flatt, Lutz Hille

Abstract
. For any two nef line bundles ℒ + ≔ 𝒪 X ​ ( Δ + ) {\mathcal{L}}^{+}\coloneqq{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\Delta^{+}) and ℒ − ≔ 𝒪 X ​ ( Δ − ) {\mathcal{L}}^{-}\coloneqq{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\Delta^{-}) on a toric variety X represented by lattice polyhedra Δ + \Delta^{+} respectively Δ − \Delta^{-} , we present the universal equivariant extension of ℒ − {\mathcal{L}}^{-} by ℒ + {\mathcal{L}}^{+} under use of the connected components of the set theoretic difference Δ − ∖ Δ + \Delta^{-}\setminus\Delta^{+} .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Spotting Cohomology

Consider a projective toric variety
X=\mathbb{P}(\Delta)
corresponding to a lattice polytope
\Delta\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}
,
M_{\mathbb{R}}=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
for a lattice
M
,
over
\mathbb{C}
.
A torus invariant Cartier divisor
D
on
X
can be represented
by a pair
(\Delta^{+},\Delta^{-})
of lattice polytopes, where both
polytopes encode nef divisors
D_{\Delta^{+}}
and
D_{\Delta^{-}}
on
X
and
D=D_{\Delta^{+}}-D_{\Delta^{-}}
.
Denote the associated line bundle by
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\eqqcolon{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\Delta^{+}-\Delta^{-})
.

It is well-known that the cohomology groups
\operatorname{H}^{i}\big(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\big)
of the line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)
of a torus invariant Cartier divisor
D
are
M
-graded (compare for example
[
CLS11
, §9.1]
or
[
Ful93
, Section 3.5]
):

(1.1)

\operatorname{H}^{i}\big(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\big)=\bigoplus_{m\in M}\operatorname{H}^{i}\big(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\big)_{m}.

By
[
ABKW20
, Thm. III.6]

or
[
AP20
]
we can describe
their homogeneous component of degree
m\in M
by

(1.2)

\operatorname{H}^{i}\big(X,\,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\Delta^{+}-\Delta^{-})\big)_{m}=\widetilde{\operatorname{H}}^{i-1}\big(\Delta^{-}\setminus(\Delta^{+}-m),\,\mathbb{C}\big),

where
\widetilde{\operatorname{H}}^{i-1}(Z,\mathbb{C})
on the right hand side denotes the reduced singular cohomology of a topological space
Z
with complex coefficients,
(\Delta^{+}-m)
denotes the polytope
\Delta^{+}
shifted by the lattice point
-m\in M
and
\Delta^{-}\setminus(\Delta^{+}-m)
denotes the set-theoretic difference of the two polytopes.
Recall the
(-1)
-st reduced singular cohomology:

(1.3)

\widetilde{\operatorname{H}}^{-1}(Z,\mathbb{C})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{C},&\mbox{if }Z=\emptyset,\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

The
0
-th reduced cohomology is the quotient
\widetilde{\operatorname{H}}^{0}(Z,\mathbb{C})=\operatorname{H}^{0}(Z,\mathbb{C})\big/\operatorname{H}^{0}(\{\cdot\},\mathbb{C})
, and thus, its dimension is the number of connected components of
Z
minus 1.

1.2.
The Example
\mathbb{F}_{1}

We look at the first Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{1}
as a first example.
Consider the projections
p_{1}\colon\mathbb{F}_{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
as a ruled surface
and
p_{2}\colon\mathbb{F}_{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
as a blow-up.
We use
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}(1,0):=p_{1}^{*}\,{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)

and
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}(0,1):=p_{2}^{*}\,{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1)

as a basis for
\operatorname{Pic}\mathbb{F}_{1}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
For
i,j\geq 0
the sheaves
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}(i,j)

correspond to lattice polytopes
\Delta_{(i,j)}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For example, for

{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}(0,0)
,
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}(1,0)
,

{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}(0,1)
,
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}(0,2)
,
and the ample
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}(1,1)
, the lattice polytopes look as follows:

\Delta_{(0,0)}

\Delta_{(1,0)}

\Delta_{(0,1)}

\Delta_{(0,2)}

\Delta_{(1,1)}

The red dot indicates the position of the origin in each figure, fixing the exact position of each polytope within the plane.
We see an example of the result quoted in
subsection (
1.1
).
Consider the polytope
\Delta_{(0,2)}
shaded in yellow and the polytope
\Delta_{(1,0)}+(0,1)
, that is,
\Delta_{(1,0)}
shifted by
(0,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, depicted in
orange
:

The two connected components of the set-theoretic difference
\Delta_{(0,2)}\setminus\big(\Delta_{(1,0)}+(0,1)\big)
provide a one-dimensional
0
-th reduced singular cohomology

(1.4)

\widetilde{\operatorname{H}}^{0}\big(\Delta_{(0,2)}\setminus(\Delta_{(1,0)}+(0,1))\big),

and so by
[
ABKW20
]
and
[
AP20
]
a one-dimensional piece (in fact, the only one) of

(1.5)

\operatorname{H}^{1}\big(\mathbb{F}_{1},\;{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}(\Delta_{(1,0)}-\Delta_{(0,2)})\big)=\operatorname{H}^{1}\big(\mathbb{F}_{1},\;{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}(1,-2)\big),

sitting in degree
m=-(0,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

2.
Toric Geometry

We introduce some basics of toric geometry central to this paper. Readers not familiar with toric geometry can take a look at one of the numerous introductory texts, for example
[
CLS11
]
,
[
Ful93
]
,
[
Dan78
]
, or
[
Dem70
]
.

Let
M\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}
be a lattice and
N=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}
its dual lattice. There is a natural pairing

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon M\times N\to\mathbb{Z}.

We consider the algebraic torus
T=\Spec\mathbb{C}[M]
.
The isomorphism
M\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}
induces an isomorphism
T\cong(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{r}
.
The lattice
M
can be recovered as the
character lattice

\Hom(T,\mathbb{C}^{*})
.
We denote the character of
M\ni m\mapsto(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
by

\chi^{m}\colon T\to\mathbb{C}^{*},\,(t_{1},\dots,t_{r})\mapsto t_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots t_{r}^{a_{r}}.

The dual lattice
N
corresponds to the
group of
1
-parameter subgroups

\Hom(\mathbb{C}^{*},T)
. Here
N\ni n\mapsto(b_{1},\dots,b_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
corresponds to

\lambda^{n}\colon\mathbb{C}^{*}\to T,\,t\mapsto(t^{b_{1}},\dots,t^{b_{r}}).

A
toric variety
is an irreducible variety containing an algebraic torus
T\cong(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{r}
as an open dense subset, such that the action of the torus on itself by multiplication extends to an algebraic action on the whole variety
[
CLS11
, Def. 3.1.1]
.
We sketch how
normal toric varieties
can be constructed from cones and fans in
N_{\mathbb{R}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.

By a
cone
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
we mean a convex subset

\sigma=\cone(S)=\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}v_{i}\,|\;\lambda_{i}\geq 0\}

generated by a finite set
S=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}\subseteq N
.
The
dual cone
to
\sigma
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
is

\sigma^{\vee}=\{u\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\,|\;\langle u,v\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }v\in\sigma\}.

A cone
\sigma
is
pointed
if
\sigma\cap(-\sigma)=\{0\}
. We write
\tau\preceq\sigma
whenever
\tau
is a
face
of
\sigma
.
A pointed cone
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
leads to an
affine toric variety

\mathbb{T}\mathbb{V}(\sigma)\coloneqq\Spec\mathbb{C}[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]
.
The inclusion of a face
\tau\preceq\sigma
induces an open embedding
\mathbb{T}\mathbb{V}(\tau)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{T}\mathbb{V}(\sigma)
. In particular, the inclusion of the origin induces an open embedding of the torus
T=\Spec\mathbb{C}[M]\hookrightarrow\mathbb{T}\mathbb{V}(\sigma)
.

A
fan

\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a finite collection of pointed cones that is closed under taking faces and such that the intersection of two cones is a face of each.
The affine toric varieties
U_{\sigma}\coloneqq\mathbb{T}\mathbb{V}(\sigma)
associated to the cones
\sigma
in a fan
\Sigma
glue together to a
normal separated
toric variety
\mathbb{T}\mathbb{V}(\Sigma)
with open affine charts the
U_{\sigma}

[
CLS11
, Thm. 3.1.5]
.

A
polyhedron

\Delta
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
is the intersection of finitely many closed half spaces

(2.1)

\Delta=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\,|\;\langle m,v_{i}\rangle\geq-\lambda_{i},\,i=1,\dots,s\}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}H_{i}^{+},

where
H_{i}^{+}=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\,|\;\langle m,v_{i}\rangle\geq-\lambda_{i}\}
for some inward pointing normal vector
v_{i}\in N_{\mathbb{R}}
and some scalar
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
.
A compact polyhedron is called a
polytope
. A polyhedron
\Delta
can be written as the
Minkowski sum

\Delta=\nabla+\delta
of a polytope
\nabla
and its
tail cone

\delta=\tail(\Delta)=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\,|\;u+m\in\Delta\text{ for all }u\in\Delta\}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}.

To a full-dimensional lattice polyhedron
\Delta
we associate its
inner normal fan

(2.2)

\mathcal{N}(\Delta)\coloneqq\{\tau\,|\;\tau\preceq\sigma_{m},\,m\in\vertset(\Delta)\}\text{ with }|\mathcal{N}(\Delta)|=\tail(\Delta)^{\vee},

whose maximal cones
\sigma_{m}
are given by

\sigma_{m}\coloneqq(\cone(\Delta-m))^{\vee}=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot(\Delta-m)^{\vee}\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}.

This yields a semiprojective toric variety
\mathbb{P}(\Delta)\coloneqq\mathbb{T}\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{N}(\Delta))
.
We will assume all polyhedra to have at least one vertex. The normal fan of such a polyhedron will have convex support of full dimension.

2.1.
Divisors on Toric Varieties

Let
\Sigma
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
with convex support of full dimension
r=\dim N_{\mathbb{R}}
, for example, the normal fan of a full-dimensional lattice polyhedron in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
X\coloneqq\mathbb{T}\mathbb{V}(\Sigma)
be the toric variety given by
\Sigma
.

Every lattice polyhedron
\Delta\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}
with tail cone
|\Sigma|^{\vee}
and whose normal fan
\mathcal{N}(\Delta)
is refined by
\Sigma
gives rise to a nef Cartier divisor
D_{\Delta}
on
X
by the following construction:
If
\Sigma
refines
\mathcal{N}(\Delta)
then the function

|\Sigma|\to\mathbb{R},\,v\mapsto\min\langle\Delta,v\rangle

is linear on the cones of
\Sigma
.
So for each
\sigma\in\Sigma
there is some
m_{\sigma}\in\Delta\cap M
such that
\min\langle\Delta,v\rangle=\langle m_{\sigma},v\rangle
for each
v\in\sigma
.
The lattice point
m_{\sigma}
is a vertex of
\Delta
and if
\sigma\in\Sigma(r)
is a maximal cone, then
m_{\sigma}
is uniquely determined. Note that

\Delta+\sigma^{\vee}=m_{\sigma}+\sigma^{\vee}.

Let
D_{\Delta}
be the Cartier divisor with Cartier data
\{m_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
, that is, locally on
U_{\sigma}\subseteq X
given by
D_{|U_{\sigma}}=\operatorname{div}(\chi^{-m_{\sigma}})_{|U_{\sigma}}
.
Then for the associated line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\Delta)\coloneqq{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D_{\Delta})
:

(2.3)

\Gamma(U_{\sigma},{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\Delta))=\bigoplus_{m\in(\Delta+\sigma^{\vee})\cap M}\mathbb{C}\cdot\chi^{m}=\chi^{m_{\sigma}}\cdot\mathbb{C}[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]=\mathbb{C}[m_{\sigma}+(\sigma^{\vee}\cap M)],

so the vertex
m_{\sigma}
of
\Delta
encodes the local sections of the line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\Delta)
over the affine open
U_{\sigma}
. The line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\Delta)
is
ample
if and only if
\mathcal{N}(\Delta)=\Sigma
.

We denote the set of lattice polyhedra with prescribed tail cone
\delta
by
\operatorname{Pol}_{\delta}^{+}

and the set of lattice polyhedra
compatible
with
\Sigma
, that is, with tail cone
|\Sigma|^{\vee}
and whose normal fan is refined by
\Sigma
, by
\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
.
The set
\operatorname{Pol}_{\delta}^{+}
forms a semigroup with respect to Minkowski addition. For a fan
\Sigma
the set
\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
forms a finitely generated subsemigroup. The union over the
\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
with
|\Sigma|^{\vee}=\delta
is the semigroup
\operatorname{Pol}_{\delta}^{+}
.
These semigroups are cancellative,
which is caused by the presence of the prescribed common tail cone
\delta
:
It makes sure that a polyhedron
\Delta\in\operatorname{Pol}_{\delta}^{+}

is uniquely determined by the values of

\min\langle\Delta,v\rangle
with
v
running through
\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
.
Hence, these semigroups embed
into their respective Grothendieck groups of formal differences:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pol}_{\delta}^{+}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Pol}_{\delta}=\{\Delta^{+}-\Delta^{-}\,|\;\Delta^{+},\Delta^{-}\in\operatorname{Pol}_{\delta}^{+}\},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Pol}(\Sigma)=\{\Delta^{+}-\Delta^{-}\,|\;\Delta^{+},\Delta^{-}\in\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)\}.

On a quasiprojective toric variety
X=\mathbb{T}\mathbb{V}(\Sigma)
, every Cartier divisor
D
can be written (non-uniquely) as a difference

D=D^{+}-D^{-}

with both
D^{+}
and
D^{-}
nef Cartier divisors
[
CLS11
, Thm. 6.3.22]
.
In particular every Cartier divisor on
X
can be represented by a pair of lattice polyhedra
(\Delta^{+},\Delta^{-})
compatible with the fan
\Sigma
, that is, an element of the Grothendieck group
\operatorname{Pol}(\Sigma)
.

3.
From Complexes of Polyhedra To Complexes of Sheaves

3.1.
The Koszul Complex of Polyhedra

In this section we construct “exact sequences of polyhedra” that induce exact sequences of split vector bundles on a toric variety
X=\mathbb{T}\mathbb{V}(\Sigma)
over
\mathbb{C}
. We start out on the polyhedral side and let
\Sigma
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
with convex support of full dimension.
Let
\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
denote the set of lattice polyhedra in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
compatible with
\Sigma
.
Also include the empty set in
\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
.

Definition 1
.

A
\Sigma
-family of polyhedra
is a finite set

S=\{\nabla_{i}\,|\;i\in I\}

of lattice polyhedra
\nabla_{i}\in\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\bigcup_{i\in I}\nabla_{i}\eqqcolon\nabla\in\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
is a lattice polyhedron compatible with
\Sigma
;

(2)

all intersections
\nabla_{I^{\prime}}\coloneqq\bigcap_{i\in I^{\prime}}\nabla_{i}

with
\emptyset\neq I^{\prime}\subseteq I

are either empty or compatible with
\Sigma
,
that is,
\nabla_{I^{\prime}}\in\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
.
Furthermore, set
\nabla_{\emptyset}\coloneqq\nabla
.

We consider two categories and a functor associated to a
\Sigma
-family between them.

Definition 2
.

Let
2^{I}
be the
poset category
associated to the power set of the finite set
I
, that is, objects are subsets of
I
and there exists a unique morphism from
I^{\prime}
to
I^{\prime\prime}
whenever
I^{\prime}\subseteq I^{\prime\prime}
.
Let
\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
also denote the
category of lattice polyhedra compatible with the fan

\Sigma
, that is, objects are compatible lattice polyhedra as defined above or the empty set and for two polyhedra
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
in
\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
we define

(3.1)

\Hom_{\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})=\begin{cases}\{\text{inclusion}\}&\,\text{ if }\Delta_{1}\subseteq\Delta_{2},\\
\emptyset&\,\text{ if }\Delta_{1}\nsubseteq\Delta_{2}.\end{cases}

Given an
\Sigma
-family of polyhedra
S
we can define a contravariant functor

(3.2)

F_{S}\colon 2^{I}\to\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)\hskip 10.00002ptI^{\prime}\mapsto\nabla_{I^{\prime}}=\bigcap_{i\in I^{\prime}}\nabla_{i},\hskip 10.00002pt(I^{\prime}\subseteq I^{\prime\prime})\mapsto(\nabla_{I^{\prime\prime}}\hookrightarrow\nabla_{I^{\prime}}).

In general, given any contravariant functor
F\colon 2^{I}\to\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
, we define a subcomplex of the Koszul complex
\bigwedge^{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\mathbb{C}^{I}
as follows.

Definition 3
.

For a contravariant functor
F\colon 2^{I}\to\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
let

(3.3)

{\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{p}:=\operatorname{span}\{e_{I^{\prime}}\,|\;\#I^{\prime}=p\mbox{ and }F(I^{\prime})\neq\emptyset\}\subseteq\bigwedge^{p}\mathbb{C}^{I}.

Here
e_{I^{\prime}}\coloneqq\underset{i\in I^{\prime}}{\bigwedge}e_{i}
, where
\{e_{i}\,|\;i\in I\}
,
denotes the canonical basis of
\mathbb{C}^{I}
.

The map
d\colon{\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{p+1}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{p}
is defined as for the Koszul complex
\bigwedge^{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\mathbb{C}^{I}
.
For a fixed total order on
I
, say
I=\{1<\dots<\#I\}
, and
I^{\prime}=\{i_{0}<\dots<i_{p}\}\subseteq I
, we have

(3.4)

d(e_{I^{\prime}})=\sum_{j=0}^{p}(-1)^{j}e_{I^{\prime}\setminus\{i_{j}\}}=\sum_{i\in I^{\prime}}(-1)^{|i|}e_{I^{\prime}\setminus\{i\}},

where
|i|
refers to the index
j
of
i=i_{j}
in
I^{\prime}
.

Recall that the polyhedra
F(I^{\prime})\in\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
are contained in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
.
For each
m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
we define the
evaluation subcomplex
(“at
m
”) as

(3.5)

{\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{p}(m):=\operatorname{span}\{e_{I^{\prime}}\,|\;\#I^{\prime}=p\mbox{ and }m\in F(I^{\prime})\}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{p}\subseteq\bigwedge^{p}\mathbb{C}^{I}

with the restrictions
d_{|{\mathcal{C}}_{p+1}^{F}(m)}\colon{\mathcal{C}}_{p+1}^{F}(m)\to{\mathcal{C}}_{p}^{F}(m)
as boundary maps.

Lemma 4
.

For a
\Sigma
-family
S
set
F\coloneqq F_{S}
.

(i)

If
m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\nabla
, then

{\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{p}(m)=0
for all
p\geq 0
.
In particular,
{\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(m)
is exact.

(ii)

If
m\in\nabla\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}
, then
{\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(m)
is still exact.

(iii)

The complex
{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}=\sum_{m\in M}{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}(m)
is exact.

Proof.

Statement (i) is clear because
F(I^{\prime})\subseteq\nabla
for all
I^{\prime}\subseteq I
.

For statement (ii) set
I_{m}:=\{i\in I\,|\;m\in F(i)\coloneqq F(\{i\})=\nabla_{i}\}
.
Then for
I^{\prime}\subseteq I
we have
I^{\prime}\subseteq I_{m}
if and only if
m\in\bigcap_{i\in I^{\prime}}F(i)=F(I^{\prime})
.
Therefore
{\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(m)
equals
\bigwedge^{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\mathbb{C}^{I_{m}}
, which is exact
[
Wei95
, Cor. 4.5.5]
.

For statement (iii) first note that we have

{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}=\sum_{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}(m)=\sum_{m\in\nabla\cap M}{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}(m).

By (i) and (ii) each of the summands
{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}(m)
is exact. For any subset
M^{\prime}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}
the intersection of the evaluation subcomplexes for
m\in M^{\prime}
is

\bigcap_{m\in M^{\prime}}{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}(m)=\bigwedge^{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\mathbb{C}^{I_{M^{\prime}}}
,
where
I_{M^{\prime}}\coloneqq\{i\in I\,|\;M^{\prime}\subseteq F(i)=\nabla_{i}\}
.
Furthermore, we have for
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
:

({\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}(m_{1})+{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}(m_{2}))\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}(m_{3})=({\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}(m_{1})\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}(m_{3}))+({\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}(m_{2})\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}(m_{3})),

because the
{\mathcal{C}}_{p}^{F}(m_{i})
are all subspaces of
{\mathcal{C}}_{p}^{F}
spanned by a subset of the prescribed basis
\{e_{I^{\prime}}\,|\;I^{\prime}\subseteq I,\#I^{\prime}=p,\,F(I^{\prime})\neq\emptyset\}
of
{\mathcal{C}}_{p}^{F}
.

Now, we switch to a
slightly more general setup. Assume that
{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{i}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}

(
i=1,\ldots,k
) are complexes such that
all their mutual intersections are exact and

\textstyle(\sum_{i\in I}{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{i})\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{j}=\sum_{i\in I}({\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{i}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{j})

for any
j
and
I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}
.
Then
\sum_{i=1,\dots,k}{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{i}
is exact, too.
The proof uses induction by
k
and exploits the short exact sequence

0\to\big(\sum_{i=1,\dots,k-1}{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{i}\big)\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{k}\to\big(\sum_{i=1,\dots,k-1}{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{i}\big)\oplus{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{k}\to\sum_{i=1,\dots,k}{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{i}\to 0.

The generalization of the setup is needed to ensure
that the induction hypothesis
implies that, besides the central term, the left most complex is exact, too.

Finally, we apply the previous claim to the complexes

{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{i}:={\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(m)
where
m\in\nabla\cap M
replaces

i=1,\ldots,k
.
Note that even though the set
\nabla\cap M
may be infinite, the induction ends after finitely many steps since the Koszul complex
\bigwedge^{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\mathbb{C}^{I}
is finite-dimensional and all
{\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(m)\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\subseteq\bigwedge^{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\mathbb{C}^{I}
.
∎

There is a particularly nice case in which exactness of the evaluation subcomplexes for all lattice points
m\in M
is equivalent to exactness for all
m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma 6
.

Assume that
\Sigma
consists of a single cone
\sigma
and its faces, with
\sigma^{\vee}
smooth and full-dimensional. Then for any contravariant functor
F\colon 2^{I}\to\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
, the complexes

{\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(m)
are exact for all
m\in M
if and only they are exact for all

m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Remark 7
.

This situation occurs naturally when we consider smooth affine toric varieties
\mathbb{T}\mathbb{V}(\sigma)
or an affine open subset
U_{\sigma}\subseteq\mathbb{T}\mathbb{V}(\Sigma)
for a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
. In the latter case the polyhedra
F(I^{\prime})
are changed to
F(I^{\prime})+\sigma^{\vee}
when considering the affine open
U_{\sigma}
(see section (
3.2
)). In the case of a functor
F_{S}
associated to a
\Sigma
-family
S
the
\nabla_{i}
become
\nabla_{i}+\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
and the
\nabla_{I^{\prime}}
for
I^{\prime}\subseteq I
become
\nabla_{I^{\prime}}+\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
.

Proof of the Lemma.

Because
\sigma^{\vee}
is smooth, we may choose coordinates such that
M=\mathbb{Z}^{r}
and
\sigma^{\vee}=\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}
.
For
F(I^{\prime})\in\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
we either have
F(I^{\prime})=\emptyset
, in which case
m\in F(I^{\prime})\Leftrightarrow\lfloor m\rfloor\in F(I^{\prime})
is clear, or
F(I^{\prime})
has only one vertex and
can be written as

F(I^{\prime})=r_{I^{\prime}}+\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}\text{ for some }r_{I^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}.

We obtain for every
m\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
that

m\in F(I^{\prime})\Leftrightarrow m\geq r_{I^{\prime}}\Leftrightarrow\lfloor m\rfloor\geq r_{I^{\prime}}\Leftrightarrow\lfloor m\rfloor\in F(I^{\prime}),

where
\lfloor m\rfloor
and the relation
\geq
are meant componentwise.
Hence,
{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}(m)={\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F}(\lfloor m\rfloor)
, proving the claim.
∎

3.2.
Localization of the Koszul Complex

We use the notation of section (
3.1
).
In section (
4

Then the complexes constructed in subsection (
3.1

maps between the global sections of them, and thus among the sheaves

look at the affine charts

for cones

\sigma\in\mathcal{N}(\Delta)

Definition 9
.

For a contravariant functor
F\colon 2^{I}\to\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
,
where
|\Sigma|
is convex and full-dimensional in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, and a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
define the functor

(3.6)

F^{\sigma}\colon 2^{I}\to\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\sigma),\,I^{\prime}\mapsto F(I^{\prime})+\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}.

Because
F(I^{\prime})\in\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\Sigma)
has tail cone
|\Sigma|^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
or is empty and
\sigma\in\Sigma
implies
|\Sigma|^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\subseteq\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
, the image
F(I^{\prime})+\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
has tail cone
|\Sigma|^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}+\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}=\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
or is empty. If
\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
is full-dimensional in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
, then the normal fan of
F(I^{\prime})+\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
consists of just
\sigma
with its faces, that is,
F(I^{\prime})+\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\sigma)
.

Proposition 10
.

For a polyhedron
\Delta
and a finite set
I
,
let
F\colon 2^{I}\to\operatorname{Pol}^{+}(\mathcal{N}(\Delta))
be a contravariant functor satisfying the conclusions (i) - (iii) of Lemma

Then the complexes

{\mathcal{C}}^{F^{\sigma}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(m)
are exact
for all
m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
and for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{N}(\Delta)
.

Remark 11
.

Moreover, this claim remains true if we consider any polyedra compatible with the fan
\mathcal{N}(\Delta)
, not just lattice polyhedra.
This generalization will be important for the induction performed in the upcoming proof.

Proof.

Step 1.
Assume that
r=\rank M=1
.
Then
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is a subset of the real line and its normal fan
\mathcal{N}(\Delta)
can contain
the origin
\sigma=\{0\}
,
the rays
\sigma=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\sigma=\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}

and the real line
\sigma=\mathbb{R}
as cones.
For
\sigma=\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma=\{0\}
the claim is clear from the assumptions.
We deal with the case
\sigma^{\vee}=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
(the case
\sigma^{\vee}=\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}
is analogous).
A polyhedron
F(I^{\prime})
compatible with
\mathcal{N}(\Delta)
is either empty or an interval of the form
[a,b]
with
-\infty<a\leq b\leq\infty
.
For
F(I^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
, let
a(I^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}
denote the start point of the interval
F(I^{\prime})
and
b(I^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
the end point.
Let
A(F)\coloneqq\{a(I^{\prime})\,|\;F(I^{\prime})\neq\emptyset\}
and
B(F)\coloneqq\{b(I^{\prime})\,|\;F(I^{\prime})\neq\emptyset\}
be the sets of start and end points, respectively.
Since
I
is finite,
A(F)\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and
B(F)\subseteq\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
are finite.

We understand the local complex
{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F^{\sigma}}
in terms of global evaluation subcomplexes:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{F^{\sigma}}_{p}(m)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{span}\{e_{I^{\prime}}\,|\;\#I^{\prime}=p\mbox{ and }m\in F(I^{\prime})+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{span}\{e_{I^{\prime}}\,|\;\#I^{\prime}=p\mbox{ and }a(I^{\prime})\leq m\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m^{\prime}\leq m}{\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{p}(m^{\prime})\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{p}.

We make three observations:

(1)

For
m\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
m<b
for all
b\in B(F)
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{F^{\sigma}}_{p}(m)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{span}\{e_{I^{\prime}}\,|\;\#I^{\prime}=p\mbox{ and }a(I^{\prime})\leq m\}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{span}\{e_{I^{\prime}}\,|\;\#I^{\prime}=p\mbox{ and }a(I^{\prime})\leq m\leq b(I^{\prime})\}={\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{p}(m).

So for
m\ll 0
the local complexes
{\mathcal{C}}^{F^{\sigma}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(m)
stabilize to the exact sequence
{\mathcal{C}}^{F}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(m)
. Recall that
B(F)
is finite, so
m
can be chosen small enough.

(2)

For
m^{\prime}\leq m
we have embeddings
{\mathcal{C}}^{F^{\sigma}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(m^{\prime})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{F^{\sigma}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(m)
of complexes.

(3)

A non-trivial jump from

{\mathcal{C}}^{F^{\sigma}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(<m):=\sum_{m^{\prime}<m}{\mathcal{C}}^{F^{\sigma}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(m^{\prime})

to
{\mathcal{C}}^{F^{\sigma}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(m)
can only occur at a starting point
m=a(I^{\prime})\in A(F)
for some
I^{\prime}\subseteq I
.

We inductively show exactness of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}^{F^{\sigma}}(m)
for all
m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}

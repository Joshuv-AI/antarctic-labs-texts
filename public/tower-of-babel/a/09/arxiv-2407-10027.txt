The first Dirichlet eigenvalue and the width Thanks: The author was partially supported by NSFC 12141103.
Guoyi Xu

Abstract
. For a geodesic ball with non-negative Ricci curvature and mean convex boundary, it is known that the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of this geodesic ball has a sharp lower bound in term of its radius. We show a quantitative explicit inequality, which bounds the width of geodesic ball in terms of the spectral gap between the first Dirichlet eigenvalue and the corresponding sharp lower bound. Mathematics Subject Classification: 53B20,58C40, 58J05.



1.
Introduction

If a complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
with
Rc\geq 0
contains a geodesic line, then from Cheeger-Gromoll splitting theorem, we know that
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to
N^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
.

The above splitting theorem has a local stable version established by Cheeger-Colding
[
7
]
. The local splitting theorem says, if there is a line segment
\gamma\subseteq(M^{n},g)
, then in the mid-point
q
of
\gamma
, there exists a small neighborhood
B_{q}(R)
which is Gromov-Hausdorff close to
B(R)\subseteq N^{n-1}\times[-R,R]
; here
N^{n-1}
is a length space and
B(R)
is a metric ball in
N^{n-1}\times[-R,R]
with the product metric. Also see
[
26
]
for some quantitative discussion.

The above stable result forms the basis of Cheeger-Colding’s theory of Ricci limit spaces. We are interested in the diameter of
N^{n-1}
. Note the key technical tool to prove those splitting results is linear growth harmonic function. Another key fact is that the ‘model’ space is a cylinder.

We recall the definition of the width. For Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
where
g
is the Riemannian metric, the width of
M^{n}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(M^{n})=\inf_{f\in\mathrm{Lip}(M^{n})}\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{diam}(f^{-1}(t)),

where
\mathrm{Lip}(M^{n})
is the set of all Lipschitz functions defined on
M^{n}
, and
\mathrm{diam}(f^{-1}(t))
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(f^{-1}(t))=\max_{x,y\in f^{-1}(t)}d_{g}(x,y);

and
d_{g}(x,y)
the distance between
x,y
with respect to Riemannian metric
g
.

In the above context, the width of a geodesic ball depends on two terms. The first term is the Gromov-Hausdorff distance between the geodesic ball and the ‘model’ cylinder, and the second term is the width of the ‘model’ cylinder
N^{n-1}\times[-R,R]
. Note the width of the ‘model’ cylinder
N^{n-1}\times[-R,R]
is not bigger than the diameter of
N^{n-1}
, when
R
has much larger scale comparing the scale of
N^{n-1}
(see Figure
1
).

Figure 1.
Local splitting and ‘model’ cylinder

We are interested in obtaining an ‘analytic’ characterization of the different scales in a geodesic ball with
Rc\geq 0
. One conjecture of Hang and Wang
[
12
]
as follows partially motivates our study along this direction:

Conjecture 1.1
.

Assume
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded convex open subset with smooth boundary, then there is a universal constant
c>0
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(\Omega)\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{2}-\pi^{2}\geq c\cdot(\frac{\mathcal{W}(\Omega)}{\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)})^{2},

where
\mu_{1}(\Omega)
is the first non-zero Neumann eigenvalue of
\Omega
.

Note Payne-Weinberger
[
20
]
proved that
\mu_{1}(\Omega)\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{2}}
and Hang-Wang
[
12
]
showed the equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is isometric to an interval of
\mathbb{R}
. The above conjecture points out: the spectral gap between the scaling invariant eigenvalue
\mu_{1}(\Omega)\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{2}
and the sharp lower bound
\pi^{2}
, possibly bounds the square of the scaling invariant width
\frac{\mathcal{W}(\Omega)}{\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)}
.

In
[
5
]
, using localization technique developed
[
4
]
(also see
[
15
]
), the inequality between the spectral gap and diameter difference is obtained on manifolds with positive Ricci curvature among more general context.

In this paper, we prove an inequality linking the spectral gap (between the first Dirichlet eigenvalue and the corresponding sharp lower bound) with the width, in similar form as (
1.1
).

Recall the inscribed radius of Riemannian manifold
M^{n}
is defined as
\displaystyle\mathrm{IR}(M^{n})\vcentcolon=\sup_{x\in M^{n}}d(x,\partial M^{n})
. For the convex region
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
, Hersch
[
13
]
showed
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\cdot\mathrm{IR}(\Omega)^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\geq 0
. Later, for convex region
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Mendez-Hernandez
[
19
]
(also see Protter
[
22
]
) gave a proof of the inequality

(1.2)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\cdot\mathrm{IR}(\Omega)^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\geq\frac{(n-1)\pi^{2}}{4}\cdot\Big(\frac{\mathrm{IR}(\Omega)}{\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)}\Big)^{2}.

Kasue
[
14
]
generalized Hersch’s result to manifolds case. More specifically, he proved that for any
n
-dim bounded region
\Omega
with
Rc\geq 0
and mean convex boundary, Hersch’s inequality also holds. He also showed that the equality case holds if and only if
\Omega
is isometric to
[0,2\cdot\mathrm{IR}(\Omega)]\times\tilde{\Omega}
, where
\tilde{\Omega}
is an
(n-1)
-dim bounded region with
Rc\geq 0
and mean convex boundary.

In
1999
, among other things, Yang
[
27
]
used the gradient estimate method (developed by Li-Yau
[
16
]
and Zhong-Yang
[
28
]
) to present a different proof of Kasue’s result. Recently Wang, Zhou and the author
[
24
]
prove that
\lambda_{1}(B_{p}(R))\cdot R^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{16}>0
holds for any geodesic ball in non-compact complete Riemannian manifold with
Rc\geq 0
. And the model space in
[
24
]
is a tube, see Figure
2
.

Motivated by Conjecture
1.1
and the results in
[
14
]
,
[
24
]
, we try to bound the width of geodesic ball, by the gap between the first Dirichlet eigenvalue and the sharp lower bound. Our main result in this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

There is
C(n)>0
, such that for any
n
-dim geodesic ball
B_{p}(R)
with
Rc\geq 0
and mean convex boundary (i.e. the mean curvature of
\partial B_{p}(R)
with respect to the outforward unit normal vector of
\partial B_{p}(R)
is non-negative), we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(B_{p}(R))\cdot R^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{16}\geq C(n)\Big\{\frac{\mathcal{W}(B_{p}(R))}{R}\Big\}^{\frac{4(2n+1)(8n+15)}{n+2}}.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.2
partially can be viewed as the stability version of the rigidity result proved in
[
14
]
. In this paper we only discuss the case of
\Omega=B_{p}(R)
. Note the geodesic balls in complete Riemannian manifolds with warped product metric
g=dr^{2}+f^{2}(r)\cdot d\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
Rc(g)\geq 0
, whose center is the origin of the warped product metric (i.e.
r=0
); have mean convex boundary.

Recall that by
[
24
]
, the inequality
\lambda_{1}(B_{p}(R))\cdot R^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{16}>0
always holds for any geodesic ball in non-compact complete Riemannian manifold with
Rc\geq 0
. And we hope to extend our study to any geodesic balls in non-compact complete Riemannian manifolds in the future.

The power
\frac{4(2n+1)(8n+15)}{n+2}
of
\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{W}(B_{p}(R))}{R}
in Theorem
1.2
is not sharp. Comparing the quantitative isoperimetric inequality (see
[
11
]
), Faber-Krahn inequality (see
[
2
]
) and (
1.2
); whether the sharp power is
2
or not, is an interesting question.

2.
Spectral gap bounds the volume difference of level sets

In the rest of the paper, unless otherwise mentioned, we assume
u>0
is a normalized eigenfunction with respect to the
1
st Dirichlet eigenvalue in
B=B_{p}(R)
and
\displaystyle\max_{x\in B}|u(x)|=1
, where
B
is a geodesic ball with
Rc\geq 0
and mean convex boundary. Also we define
\psi(x)=\sin(\frac{\pi}{4R}\rho(x))
where
\rho(x)=d(x,\partial B_{p}(R))
. Furthermore, we always assume

\displaystyle\epsilon\vcentcolon=\lambda-\frac{\pi^{2}}{16R^{2}},

where
\lambda=\lambda_{1}(B_{p}(R))
and
\epsilon\in(0,R^{-2})
. For convenience, for any
C\cdot\epsilon
, where
C>0
is some universal constant; we also use
\epsilon
to denote
C\cdot\epsilon
in the rest argument.

In the rest of paper, for any
x,y\in B
, we also use
|xy|
to denote the distance
d_{g}(x,y)
and
\overline{x,y}
to denote one geodesic segment between
x,y
.

Let
v=\frac{\sin^{-1}u}{\sqrt{\lambda}}
, from
[
27
]
, we know that

(2.1)

\displaystyle|\nabla v|\leq 1.

The following key equality of spectral gap is the starting point of our paper.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
u,\psi,\epsilon
defined as above, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{B}|\nabla(\frac{u}{\psi})|^{2}\cdot\psi^{2}}{\int_{B}u^{2}}+\frac{\frac{\pi}{4R}\int_{B}u^{2}\cot(\frac{\pi}{4R}\rho)\cdot(-\Delta\rho)}{\int_{B}u^{2}}.

Remark 2.2
.

Define
f(x)=d(p,x)
, then
\rho(x)=R-f(x)
. It is well-known that
\Delta f(x)=H(x)
in the sense of the distribution, where
H
is the mean curvature of
\partial B_{p}(f(x))
(see
[
17
, Page
33
]
). From
\partial B
is mean convex and
Rc\geq 0
, we get
\Delta\rho=-\Delta f=-H\leq 0
, where
\Delta\rho
is again in the sense of the distribution. Therefore, both two terms on the right side of the equality are non-negative. Also the above equation gives another proof of
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\cdot\mathrm{IR}(\Omega)^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\geq 0
if we replace
\psi
by the function
\sin(\frac{\pi}{2\mathrm{IR}(\Omega)}\tilde{\rho}(x))
, where
\tilde{\rho}(x)=d(x,\partial\Omega)
.

Proof:
Define
\eta(x)=\frac{u}{\psi}(x)
, now we have

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{B}u^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{B}|Du|^{2}=\int_{B}|\nabla(\psi\cdot\eta)|^{2}=\int_{B}\eta^{2}\cdot|\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\nabla\eta|^{2}\psi^{2}+\nabla(\eta^{2})\cdot(\psi\nabla\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\psi^{2}+\eta^{2}\cdot|\nabla\psi|^{2}-\eta^{2}\nabla(\psi\nabla\psi)=\int_{B}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\psi^{2}-\eta^{2}\psi\Delta\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}}{16R^{2}}\int_{B}u^{2}+\int_{B}|\nabla(\frac{u}{\psi})|^{2}\cdot\psi^{2}+\frac{\pi}{4R}\int_{B}u^{2}\cot(\frac{\pi}{4R}\rho)\cdot(-\Delta\rho).

∎

Firstly we get that the ratio between eigenfunction and distance function is close to
1
at the center of the ball.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have

\displaystyle|u(p)-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}|\leq R^{2}\epsilon.

Proof:
Assume
\bar{p}\in\partial B_{p}(R)
, from (
2.1
) we get

\displaystyle\sin^{-1}u(p)-\sin^{-1}u(\bar{p})\leq\sqrt{\lambda}R\leq\frac{\pi}{4}+R^{2}\epsilon,

which implies
u(p)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}+R^{2}\epsilon
.

On the other hand, assume
u(q)=1
. Note
d(p,q)\leq R
we have

\displaystyle\sin^{-1}u(q)-\sin^{-1}u(p)\leq\sqrt{\lambda}d(p,q)\leq\frac{\pi}{4}+R^{2}\epsilon;

which implies
u(p)\geq\sin(\frac{\pi}{4}-R^{2}\epsilon)\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}-R^{2}\epsilon
.

∎

In the rest of the paper, unless otherwise mentioned, the constant
b\in(0,1)
is a very small number to be determined later.

Lemma 2.4
.

We have
B_{p}((1-b)R)=\{bR<\rho<(2-b)R\}
.

Proof:
Firstly note that
B_{p}((1-b)R)\subseteq\{bR<\rho<(2-b)R\}
.

Secondly note
\partial B_{p}(R)
is a compact set; then for any
x
with
\rho(x)\in(bR,(2-b)R)
, there is a point
\hat{x}\in\partial B_{p}(R)
with
\rho(x)=d(x,\hat{x})
.

Note the tangent vector of the geodesic segment
\overline{x,\hat{x}}
at
\hat{x}
is perpendicular to the tangent space of
\partial B_{p}(R)
at
\hat{x}
, and one writes
\overline{x,\hat{x}}\perp\partial B_{p}(R)
. This implies

\displaystyle\overline{p,\hat{x}}\subseteq\overline{x,\hat{x}},\quad\quad\text{or}\quad\quad\overline{x,\hat{x}}\subseteq\overline{p,\hat{x}};

because
\overline{p,\hat{x}}\perp\partial B_{p}(R)
at
\hat{x}
too. The conclusion follows from the above directly.

∎

Proposition 2.5

\displaystyle\epsilon R^{2}\leq C_{1}(n)b^{n+2},

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in B_{p}((1-b)R)}|\frac{u}{\psi}(x)-1|\leq C_{2}(n)(\epsilon R^{2})^{\frac{1}{n+2}}b^{-1}.

.

Because of Proposition
2.5
, in fact we can analyze the metric structure of cylinder type region, using eigenfunctions instead of harmonic functions. However in the context of

, the harmonic function
h
(see Definition

(see

Proof:

Step (1)
. We use the notation
w=\frac{u}{\psi}
in the rest argument. From Lemma
2.4
, we get
\psi\big|_{B_{p}((1-b)R)}\geq\sin(\frac{\pi}{4R}b)\geq\frac{1}{2}b
.

Note
|\nabla v|\leq 1=|\nabla\rho|
, where
|\nabla\rho|=1
is in the almost everywhere sense, because
\rho
is not smooth. We can get

\displaystyle u\leq\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}\rho).

Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{u}{\psi}\leq\frac{\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}\rho)}{\sin(\frac{\pi}{4R}\rho)}\leq 2\sqrt{\lambda}R.

Now we know that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{p}((1-b)R)}|\nabla w|\leq|\frac{|\nabla u|}{\psi}|+|\frac{u\nabla\psi}{\psi^{2}}|\leq\frac{2\sqrt{\lambda}}{b}+\frac{4\sqrt{\lambda}}{b}\leq C(n)(bR)^{-1}.

Step (2)
. From Neumann Poincare inequality for geodesic ball (see
[
3
]
) and Proposition
2.1
, note
|u|\leq 1
; we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{p}((1-\frac{1}{2}b)R)}|w(x)-\fint_{B_{p}((1-\frac{1}{2}b)R)}w|^{2}dx\leq C(n)R^{2}\int_{B_{p}((1-\frac{1}{2}b)R)}|\nabla w|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(n)R^{2}\frac{\int_{B}|\nabla w|^{2}\psi^{2}}{b^{2}}\leq C(n)\epsilon R^{2}\frac{\int_{B}u^{2}}{b^{2}}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(n)\frac{\epsilon R^{2}}{b^{2}}V(B).

Let
A_{\eta}=\{x\in B_{p}((1-\frac{1}{2}b)R):|w(x)-\fint_{B_{p}((1-\frac{1}{2}b)R)}w|\geq\eta\}
, where
\eta=(\epsilon R^{2})^{\frac{1}{n+2}}b^{-1}
. Then from (

(2.6)

\displaystyle V(A_{\eta})\leq C_{3}(n)V(B_{p}(R))\cdot\frac{\epsilon R^{2}}{b^{2}\eta^{2}}.

For any

such that if

\displaystyle V(B_{x}(r_{3}R))

\displaystyle\geq(\frac{r_{3}}{2})^{n}\cdot V(B_{x}(2R))\geq(\frac{r_{3}}{2})^{n}\cdot V(B_{p}(R))>\frac{C_{3}(n)V(B_{p}(R))}{\eta^{2}}\cdot\frac{\epsilon R^{2}}{b^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq V(A_{\eta}).

Now we can find
C_{1}(n)>0
, such that if

\displaystyle r_{3}=C_{4}(n)\cdot(\frac{\epsilon R^{2}}{\eta^{2}b^{2}})^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{40}b.

\displaystyle|w(x)-\fint_{B_{p}((1-\frac{1}{2}b)R)}w|\leq|w(x)-w(\check{x})|+|w(\check{x})-\fint_{B_{p}((1-\frac{1}{2}b)R)}w|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{{B_{p}((1-\frac{1}{2}b)R)}}|\nabla w|\cdot d(x,\check{x})+\eta\leq\eta+r_{3}\cdot\frac{C(n)}{b}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq\eta+C_{4}(n)\cdot(\frac{\epsilon R^{2}}{\eta^{2}b^{2}})^{\frac{1}{n}}\cdot\frac{1}{b}\leq C(n)(\epsilon R^{2})^{\frac{1}{n+2}}b^{-1}.

From the above volume comparison between level sets and Cheeger-Colding

Proof:

, let

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle\int_{A}\sin(\frac{\pi}{2R}\rho)(-\Delta\rho)\geq\sin(\frac{b\pi}{2})\cdot\int_{A}(-\Delta\rho)\geq b\cdot\Big\{V(\rho^{-1}(bR))-V(\rho^{-1}((2-b)R))\Big\}.

3.
Two type estimates by the volume difference

In this section, the function
h
is defined as follows:

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\Delta h(x)=0,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad x\in\rho^{-1}(bR,(2-b)R),\\
&h(x)=\rho(x),\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad x\in\rho^{-1}(bR)\cup\rho^{-1}((2-b)R).\end{array}\right.

3.1.
The integral estimates of harmonic functions

Lemma 3.1
.

There are
C(n)>0
such that

\displaystyle\fint_{\rho^{-1}(bR,(2-b)R)}|\nabla(h-\rho)|^{2}\leq C(n)\frac{\epsilon R^{2}}{b},

\displaystyle\fint_{\rho^{-1}(2bR,(2-2b)R)}|\nabla^{2}h|^{2}\leq\frac{C(n)}{(bR)^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon R^{2}}{b}},

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\rho^{-1}(2bR,(2-2b)R)}|h-\rho|(x)\leq C(n)\Big(b\epsilon R^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n+2}}\cdot\frac{R}{b}.

Proof:

Step (1)
. Let

and
A_{1}=\rho^{-1}(2bR,(2-2b)R)
. Firstly by the Maximum principle, we get
(\rho-h)\big|_{A}\geq 0
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{A}|\nabla(h-\rho)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{A}(\rho-h)\Delta(h-\rho)=\int_{A}(\rho-h)\cdot(-\Delta\rho)\leq\sup_{A}(\rho-h)\cdot\int_{A}(-\Delta\rho)

\displaystyle\leq(2-b)R\cdot\Big[V(\rho^{-1}(bR))-V\big(\rho^{-1}((2-b)R)\big)\Big].

Note
B_{p}((1-b)R)\subseteq A
, from Lemma
2.7
we have

\displaystyle\fint_{A}|\nabla(h-\rho)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{A}|\nabla(h-\rho)|^{2}}{V(B_{p}(R))}\cdot(\frac{R}{(1-b)R})^{n}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\frac{(2-b)R\cdot\Big[V(\rho^{-1}(bR))-V\big(\rho^{-1}((2-b)R)\big)\Big]}{V(B_{p}(R))}\leq C(n)\frac{\epsilon R^{2}}{b}.

Step (2)
. From Bochner formula and
Rc\geq 0
, we get
|\nabla^{2}h|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\Delta(|\nabla h|^{2})
. Therefore choosing cut-off function
\varphi
with
\varphi\big|_{A_{1}}=1
and
\varphi|_{B-A}=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{A_{1}}|\nabla^{2}h|^{2}\leq\int_{A}|\nabla^{2}h|^{2}\cdot\varphi\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{A}\varphi\cdot\Delta(|\nabla h|^{2}-1)\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{A}\Delta\varphi\cdot(|\nabla h|^{2}-1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sup_{A}|\Delta\varphi|\int_{A}|\nabla(h-\rho)|\cdot(|\nabla h|+1)\leq\frac{C(n)}{(bR)^{2}}\int_{A}|\nabla(h-\rho)|\cdot(|\nabla(h-\rho)|+2)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(n)}{(bR)^{2}}\Big\{\int_{A}|\nabla(h-\rho)|^{2}+\sqrt{V(A)\cdot\int_{A}|\nabla(h-\rho)|^{2}}\Big\}.

By volume comparison Theorem and the above, also using (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\fint_{A_{1}}|\nabla^{2}h|^{2}\leq\frac{C(n)}{(bR)^{2}}\Big\{\fint_{A}|\nabla(h-\rho)|^{2}+\sqrt{\fint_{A}|\nabla(h-\rho)|^{2}}\Big\}\leq\frac{C(n)}{(bR)^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon R^{2}}{b}}.

Step (3)
. Note
A\subseteq B_{p}(R)
, from the domain monotonicity of Dirichlet eigenvalues (see
[

17
]
); we know that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(A)\geq\lambda_{1}(B_{p}(R))>\frac{\pi^{2}}{16R^{2}}.

Combining the result from Step (1), we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\fint_{A}|h-\rho|^{2}\leq\lambda_{1}(A)^{-1}\fint_{A}|\nabla(h-\rho)|^{2}\leq C(n)\frac{\epsilon R^{2}}{b}R^{2}.

Let
A_{2}=\rho^{-1}(\frac{3}{2}bR,(2-\frac{3}{2})bR)
, from Cheng-Yau’s gradient estimate for positive harmonic function
h
, we get

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{A_{2}}|\nabla(h-\rho)|\leq\sup_{A_{2}}(|\nabla h|+1)\leq\frac{C(n)}{bR}\sup_{A}h(x)+1\leq\frac{C(n)}{b}.

For
\eta>0
to be determined later, we define

\displaystyle W=h-\rho,\quad\quad\quad\Omega_{\eta}=\{z\in A_{2}:|W(z)|\leq\eta\}.

Let
r_{\eta}=C(n)R\cdot\Big(\frac{\epsilon R^{2}}{b}\frac{R^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n}}
, for
x\in A_{1}
we have
B_{x}(r_{\eta})\subseteq A_{2}
. From (
3.3
) we can get

\displaystyle\frac{V(B_{x}(r_{\eta}))}{V(A)}

\displaystyle\geq(\frac{r_{\eta}}{2R})^{n}>C(n)\frac{\epsilon R^{2}}{b}R^{2}\eta^{-2}\geq\eta^{-2}\fint_{A}|h-\rho|^{2}\geq\frac{V(A_{2}-\Omega_{\eta})}{V(A)}.

Therefore
V(B_{x}(r_{\eta}))>V(A_{2}-\Omega_{\eta})
.

Note
B_{x}(r_{\eta})\subseteq A_{2}
, then we get that
B_{x}(r_{\eta})\cap\Omega_{\eta}\neq\emptyset
. Choose
\check{x}\in B_{x}(r_{\eta})\cap\Omega_{\eta}
, now by (
3.4
), we get

\displaystyle|W(x)|\leq|W(x)-W(\check{x})|+|W(\check{x})|\leq\eta+r_{\eta}\cdot\sup_{A_{2}}|\nabla W|\leq\eta+\frac{C(n)R}{b}\cdot\Big(\frac{\epsilon R^{2}}{b}\frac{R^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Now choose

. The conclusion follows from the above.

We know

\displaystyle\delta r\cdot V(\check{I})\leq\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}+\mathcal{H}.

And there is

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}dx_{1}dx_{2}dy_{1}dy_{2}\int_{0}^{\rho(x_{1})-b_{1}}G(\xi_{x_{1}}(t))dt

\displaystyle\leq V(B_{\pi^{b_{0}}(x)}(\delta r)\times B_{y}(\delta r)\times B_{\pi^{b_{0}}(y)}(\delta r))\cdot\int_{B_{x}(\delta r)}dx_{1}\int_{0}^{\rho(x_{1})-b_{1}}G(\xi_{x_{1}}(t))dt.

By the mean convex property, we know that the volume element is increasing along
\xi_{x}(t)
, which is the gradient curve of
-\rho
. Now we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{x}(\delta r)}dx_{1}\int_{0}^{\rho(x_{1})-b_{1}}G(\xi_{x_{1}}(t))dt\leq\int_{b_{2}-\delta r}^{b_{2}+\delta r}ds\int_{\rho^{-1}(s)}dx_{1}\int_{0}^{s-b_{1}}G(\xi_{x_{1}}(t))dt

\displaystyle=\int_{b_{2}-\delta r}^{b_{2}+\delta r}ds\int_{0}^{s-b_{1}}dt\int_{\rho^{-1}(s)}G(\xi_{x_{1}}(t))dx_{1}

\displaystyle=C(n)\delta r\cdot\int_{\rho^{-1}(b_{1},b_{2}+\delta r)}G(y)dy.

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\Omega}dx_{1}dx_{2}dy_{1}dy_{2}\int_{0}^{\rho(x_{1})-b_{1}}G(\xi_{x_{1}}(t))dt}{V(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C(n)\delta r\cdot\int_{\rho^{-1}(b_{1},b_{2}+\delta r)}G(y)dy}{V(B_{x}(\delta r))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(n)\delta r\cdot\int_{\rho^{-1}(r,2R-r)}G(y)dy}{V(A)}\cdot\frac{V(B_{p}(R))}{V(B_{x}(\delta r))}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)(\delta r)\cdot(\frac{R}{\delta r})^{n}\cdot(\fint_{A}G^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

\displaystyle\leq V(B_{x}(\delta r)\times B_{y}(\delta r)\times B_{\pi^{b_{0}}(y)}(\delta r))\cdot C(n)\delta r\int_{\rho^{-1}(b_{1},b_{0}+\delta r)}G;

\displaystyle\leq V(B_{x}(\delta r)\times B_{\pi^{b_{0}}(x)}(\delta r)\times B_{\pi^{b_{0}}(y)}(\delta r))\cdot C(n)\delta r\int_{\rho^{-1}(b_{1},b_{2}+r+\delta r)}G;

Step (2)

Therefore we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{H}dx_{1}dx_{2}dy_{1}dy_{2}}{V(\Omega)}\leq C(n)\delta^{-2n}r^{2}(\fint_{A}H^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

4.3
), (
4.5

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}+\mathcal{H}}{\delta rV(\Omega)}\leq C(n)\Big\{\delta^{-2n-1}r(\fint_{A}H^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\big(\frac{R}{\delta r}\big)^{n}(\fint_{A}G^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\}.

Then there is

such that if

then

and the conclusion follows.

∎

Theorem A.1
.

Assume
M
is an arbitrary connected Riemannian manifold, and
\tau_{1}(s),\ \tau_{2}(s):(0,T)\rightarrow M
are (possibly constant) geodesics on
M
. Let
|\cdot,\cdot|
be the distance function on
M
. Assume

(i)
\rho(s)=|\tau_{1}(s),\tau_{2}(s)|
is differentiable at
s=s_{0}
;

(ii)
\tau_{1}(s_{0})\neq\tau_{2}(s_{0})
, and there exists a minimal (unit speed) geodesic
\phi
joining
\tau_{1}(s_{0})
to
\tau_{2}(s_{0})
(with domain
[0,\rho(s_{0})]
).

Then

\frac{d\rho(s)}{ds}|_{s=s_{0}}=\langle\phi^{\prime}(\rho(s_{0})),\tau_{2}^{\prime}(s_{0})\rangle-\langle\phi^{\prime}(0),\tau_{1}^{\prime}(s_{0})\rangle.

Proof.

Let
V_{1}(t)
,
V_{2}(t)
be parallel vector fields along
\phi
with

V_{1}(0)=\tau_{1}^{\prime}(s_{0}),\ \ V_{2}(\rho(s_{0}))=\tau_{2}^{\prime}(s_{0}),

and construct a family of smooth curves:

\Phi(t,s)=\exp_{\phi(t)}[(s-s_{0})((1-\frac{t}{\rho(s_{0})})V_{1}(t)+\frac{t}{\rho(s_{0})}V_{2}(t))],\ \ t\in[0,\rho(s_{0})],\ \ s\in(s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon).

By the first variation formula,

\frac{dl(\Phi(\cdot,s))}{ds}|_{s=s_{0}}=\langle\phi^{\prime}(\rho(s_{0})),\tau_{2}^{\prime}(s_{0})\rangle-\langle\phi^{\prime}(0),\tau_{1}^{\prime}(s_{0})\rangle.

Since
\rho(s)
is differentiable at
s_{0}
, noticing the fact that

\Phi(t,s_{0})=\phi(t),\ \ l(\Phi(\cdot,s_{0}))=\rho(s_{0}),\ \ l(\Phi(\cdot,s))\geq\rho(s),\ \forall s,

we get the result.
∎

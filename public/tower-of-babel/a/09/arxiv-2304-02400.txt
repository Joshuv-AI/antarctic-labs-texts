Abstract
This contribution discusses a new perception of the structure of compound nuclei by introducing intermediate states of the Feshbach formalism of nuclear reactions in the Interacting Boson Model of nuclear structure. The stake is to explore the manifestation of the unitary limit in heavy, even-even nuclei. Interactions that govern Feshbach resonances of cold and dilute atomic gases suggest the formulation of an IBM-compound Hamiltonian for scattering two neutrons ( 2 ​ n 2n ) from a heavy, even-even target ( A A ). The solutions of the corresponding coupled channel equations host a 2 ​ n 2n -IBM state resonance. It turns out that the unitary limit is measurable in a heavy A + 2 ​ n A+2n compound nucleus at low temperatures. That measurement is feasible through the fluctuations of the cross-sections that tune the 2 ​ n 2n - A A scattering length.



I
Introduction

Heavy, even-even nuclei serve as an inclusive testing ground for a remarkable pluralism of physical phenomena that range from Bardeen Cooper Schriefer behavior of nucleon pairs at valence shells to boson condensates for the emergence of the nuclear shapes as well as to quantum critical points in Shape/Phase transitions
CJC
. The Interacting Boson Model provides a group theoretical framework that synthesizes a simple and integrated approach out of such a diversity of physical aspects in nuclear physics [2]. On the other hand, cold and dilute atomic gases manifest the so-called unitary limit in the vicinity of Feshbach resonances
Randeria
. The unitary limit serves as a theoretical benchmark that, like IBM, accompanies a similar but enriched diversity of physical aspects ranging from the BCS-Bose Einstein Condensation crossover to a quantum critical point. In parallel, the unitary limit refers to a strong coupling problem that is describable in terms of a Conformal Field Theory
CFT
. The theoretical application of the unitary limit in nuclear physics was first realized in light nuclei
light
. It emerged parallel with the development of Effective Field Theories
EFTs
. This contribution briefly reviews the first exploration of the unitary limit in heavy even-even nuclei
Georgoudis
. Apart from the phenomenological insights that this contribution focuses on, that exploration initiated the examination of conformal symmetry within the group theoretical framework of IBM. Conformal invariance arises at the critical point of a second-order phase transition
Fisher
as well as in Quantum Chromo Dynamics at the limit of a large number of gluons
Gross
. In that perspective, introducing the unitary limit in heavy even-even nuclei commences the investigation of algebraic relations between the symmetries of the IBM with the conformal symmetry of second-order critical points. In parallel, it explores algebraic relations with the limit of conformal invariance in a strong coupling problem (unitary limit) that is amenable to be incorporated afterward with QCD.

The open-closed channel crossing during an atom-atom collision is the underlying mechanism that realizes the unitary limit. The open channel reflects a scattering state of two cold atoms, while the closed channel is the bound state of a diatomic molecule formed by these same cold atoms. Channels’ crossing means the coincidence in the energy of two different channels. In the Feshbach formalism for reactions, the open-closed channel crossing is achieved through the resonating energy of the open channel with the energy of an intermediate state of the closed channel. That resonating energy gives rise to a resonance that manifests the intermediate state of the closed channel. In atomic and molecular physics, these resonances are the celebrated Feshbach resonances

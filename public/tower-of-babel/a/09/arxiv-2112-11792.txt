Two pointsets in PG ⁡ ( 2 , q n ) \mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n}) and the associated codes
Vito Napolitano, Olga Polverino, Paolo Santonastaso, Ferdinando Zullo

Abstract
. In this paper we consider two pointsets in PG ⁡ ( 2 , q n ) \mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n}) arising from a linear set L L of rank n n contained in a line of PG ⁡ ( 2 , q n ) \mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n}) : the first one is a linear blocking set of Rédei type, the second one extends the construction of translation KM-arcs. We point out that their intersections pattern with lines is related to the weight distribution of the considered linear set L L . We then consider the Hamming metric codes associated with both these constructions, for which we can completely describe their weight distributions. By choosing L L to be an 𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} -linear set with a short weight distribution, then the associated codes have few weights . We conclude the paper by providing a connection between the Γ ​ L \Gamma\mathrm{L} -class of L L and the number of inequivalent codes we can construct starting from it.

1.
Introduction

In the study of the geometry of
\mathrm{PG}(r,q)
, special attention has been paid to the study of its subsets of points based on their behavior with respect to the intersections they have with all the members of one or more assigned families of subspaces. Such sets, when the prescribed family of the subspaces is that of the hyperplanes and the range of the size of their intersections with hyperplanes is small, are related with other combinatorial objects such as for example graphs, some classes of difference sets and linear codes with few weights, see e.g.
[
8
,
9
,
16
,
18
,
35
]
. When considering this last relationship, to which we are interested in this paper, the existence problem takes on a special role. Indeed, such sets gives rise to a linear code with few weights whose generator matrix is given by a matrix whose columns are the coordinates of the points of the given set and vice versa, given a linear code with few weights, the columns of (one of) its generator matrix seen as coordinates of points in a projective space form a (multi)set with few intersection sizes with respect to the hyperplanes of the space.

Our aim is to find codes arising from linear sets (or pointsets related to them) having
few
weights in the spirit of
[
30
,
38
]
, see also
[
1
, Section 4]
. Let
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})=\mathrm{PG}(V,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}})
, with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}(V)=3
, up to coordinatize, we can suppose that
V={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{3}
.
In this paper, we exploit two constructions of pointsets in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})
arising from linear sets of rank
n
on a projective line. Let
L_{U}
be a linear set of rank
n
in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
(up to equivalence)
L_{U}=\{\langle(x,f(x)\rangle\colon x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}\}\subseteq\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
, where
f(x)
is a
q
-polynomial. Then we may define the following two pointsets of
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})
:

(1)

\mathcal{B}_{f}=\mathcal{G}_{f}\cup\mathcal{D}_{f}
;

(2)

\mathcal{C}_{f}=\mathcal{G}_{f}\cup(\ell_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{f})
,

where
\mathcal{D}_{f}=\{\langle(x,f(x),0)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\}
and
\mathcal{G}_{f}=\{\langle(x,f(x),1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\}
.
The pointset
\mathcal{B}_{f}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear blocking set of Rédei type, whereas the pointset
\C_{f}
extends the pointset used to construct translation KM-arcs introduced in
[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
.
We study their intersections with lines in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})
and we point out that the pattern of their intersections with lines is strongly related to the weight distribution of the linear set
\mathcal{D}_{f}
.
This investigation allows us to construct a large number of linear codes for which we can completely describe the weight distribution, also obtaining linear codes with few weights.
Finally we prove that the
\Gamma\mathrm{L}
-class of
\mathcal{D}_{f}
can provide an estimation of the number of inequivalent codes one can get from a fixed
\mathcal{B}_{f}
or
\mathcal{C}_{f}
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
contains basic definitions and a brief survey on linear sets and on the relationship between linear codes and multisets of points in a projective space, in order to make the paper self–contained. In Section
3
, we investigate the pointsets
\mathcal{B}_{f}
and
\mathcal{C}_{f}
of
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})
by determining their intersections with lines. In Section
4
, using the well–known equivalence between linear codes and multisets of a projective space, we describe the parameters of the related codes. Finally, Section
6
is devoted to the study of the relation between the projective equivalence of the pointsets
\mathcal{B}_{f}
or
\mathcal{C}_{f}
and the related codes.

2.2.
Linear codes and (multi)sets of points in finite projective spaces

A
q
-
ary linear code

\C
is any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}
.
If
\C
has dimension
r
, we say that
\C
is an
[m,r]_{q}
-
code
.
A
generator matrix

G
for an
[m,r]_{q}
-code
\C
is an
(r\times m)
-matrix over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
whose rows form a basis of
\C
, i.e.

\C=\{\mathbf{x}G\colon\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{r}\}.

We may consider
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}
as a metric space endowed with the
Hamming distance

d
, i.e.
d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is the
number of entries in which
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
differ, with
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}
.
The
Hamming support
of a vector
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}
is
\sigma(\mathbf{x})=\{i:x_{i}\neq 0\}
and its
Hamming weight
is
w(\mathbf{x})=\lvert\sigma(\mathbf{x})\rvert
or equivalently
w(\mathbf{c})=d(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{0})
.
Recall that the minimum weight of a linear code coincides with its minimum distance.
A
q
–ary linear code of length
m
, dimension
r
and minimum distance
d
is referred to as an
[m,r,d]_{q}
-code or as an
[m,r]_{q}
-code if the minimum distance is not relevant/known. A code
\C
is said to be Hamming non-degenerate if
\cup_{\mathbf{c}\in\C}\sigma(\mathbf{c})=\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
Let denote by
A_{i}
the number of codewords of
\C
with Hamming weight
i
.
The
(Hamming) weight enumerator
is defined as the following polynomial:

1+A_{1}z+\ldots+A_{m}z^{m}.

This polynomial gives a good deal of information about the code and it is an important invariant under code equivalence, and has been calculated for few families of codes. Also, it is used in the probability theory involved with different ways of decoding.
An
\ell
-
weight code

\C
is an
[m,r]_{q}
-code having
\ell
nonzero weights
w_{1}<\ldots<w_{\ell}
, i.e. if the sequence
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{m})
have exactly
\ell
nonzero entries. If
\ell\leq r
we say that the code has
few weights
.
Much of the focus on linear codes to date has been on codes with few weights, especially on two and three-weight codes, for their applications in secret sharing
[
16
]
, authentication codes
[
17
]
and their connections with association schemes
[
9
,
8
]
and with graphs
[
35
]
.

A matrix in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m\times m}
is said to be a
monomial matrix
if it has exactly one nonzero entry in each row and column. Two
[m,r,d]_{q}
-codes
\C_{1}
and
\C_{2}
are said to be (monomially)
equivalent
if
\C_{2}=\C_{1}M=\{\mathbf{c}M\colon\mathbf{c}\in\C_{1}\}
for some monomial matrix
M\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m\times m}
. Note that any monomial matrix
M\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m\times m}
is the product
M=PD
where
P\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m\times m}
is a permutation matrix and
D\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m\times m}
is a invertible diagonal matrix. Then, the linear codes
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
with generator matrix
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
respectively, are equivalent if and only if there exist
S\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}(q)
,
P
permutation matrix and
D
non diagonal matrix such that
G_{2}=SG_{1}PD
.

Denote
[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
Correspondingly, two
[m,r,d]_{q}
-codes
\C_{1}
and
\C_{2}
are equivalent if and only if there exists a permutation
\theta:[m]\rightarrow[m]
and there exist
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
such that

\C_{2}=\{(\beta_{1}x_{\theta(1)},\ldots,\beta_{m}x_{\theta(m)})\colon(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\C_{1}\}.

The set of equivalence classes of non-degenerate
[m,r,d]_{q}
–codes will be denoted by
\mathcal{C}[m,r,d]_{q}
.

A
projective
[m,r,d]_{q}
–system

(\mathcal{P},\mathrm{m})
is a finite multiset, where
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathrm{PG}(r-1,q)
is a set of points not
all of which lie in a hyperplane, and
\mathrm{m}:\mathrm{PG}(r-1,q)\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is the multiplicity function, with
\mathrm{m}(P)>0
if and only if
P\in\mathcal{P}
and
\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathrm{m}(P)=m
. The parameter
d
is defined by

m-d=\max\left\{\sum_{P\in H}\mathrm{m}(P):H\subseteq\mathrm{PG}(r-1,q),\dim(H)=r-2\right\},

see e.g.
[
21
,
36
]
.

Two projective
[m,r,d]_{q}
–systems
(\mathcal{P},\mathrm{m})
and
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathrm{m}^{\prime})
are
equivalent
if there exists a projective isomorphism
\phi\in\mathrm{PGL}(r,q)
mapping
\mathcal{P}
to
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
that preserves the multiplicities of the points, i.e.

\mathrm{m}(P)=\mathrm{m}^{\prime}(\phi(P))
for every
P\in\mathrm{PG}(r-1,q)
. The set of all equivalence classes of projective
[m,r,d]_{q}
systems will be denoted by
\mathcal{P}[m,r,d]_{q}
.

There exists a 1-to-1 correspondence between
\mathcal{C}[m,r,d]_{q}
and
\mathcal{P}[m,r,d]_{q}
. This correspondence can be formalized by two maps

\Phi:\mathcal{C}[m,r,d]_{q}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}[m,r,d]_{q},

\Psi:\mathcal{P}[m,r,d]_{q}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}[m,r,d]_{q}

that are defined as follows.

For a given equivalence class
[\C]
of non-degenerate
[m,r,d]_{q}
–codes, consider a generator matrix
G\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{r\times m}
of one of the codes in
[\C]
. Let
\mathbf{g}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{g}_{m}
be the columns of
G
and define the set
\mathcal{P}=\{\langle\mathbf{g}_{1}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}},\ldots,\langle\mathbf{g}_{m}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\}\subseteq\mathrm{PG}(r-1,q)
. Moreover, define the multiplicity function
\mathrm{m}
as

\mathrm{m}(P)=\lvert\{i:P=\langle\mathbf{g}_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\}\rvert.

Then, the map
\Phi
is defined as
\Phi([\C])=[(\mathcal{P},\mathrm{m})]
. On the other hand, for a given equivalence class
[(\mathcal{P},\mathrm{m})]
of projective
[m,r,d]_{q}
–systems we construct a matrix
G
by taking as columns a representative of each point
P_{i}
in
\mathcal{P}
, counted with multiplicity
\mathrm{m}(P_{i})
. Let
\C=\mathrm{rowsp}(G)
, where
\mathrm{rowsp}(G)
is the space generated by the rows of
G
. We say that
\C
is the code
associated
with
(\mathcal{P},\mathrm{m})
. We then set
\Psi([\mathcal{P},\mathrm{m}])=[\mathrm{rowsp}(G)]
.
One can easily check the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

\Psi
and
\Phi
are well-posed and are the inverse of each other. In particular, there is a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathcal{C}[m,r,d]_{q}
and
\mathcal{P}[m,r,d]_{q}
.

This correspondence gives also information on the weight distribution of the code associate with a projective system. More precisely, let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[m,r,d]_{q}
-code. Let
G\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{r\times m}
be a generator matrix of
\C
. Let
\mathbf{g}_{i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,m
be the
i
-th column of
G
. The Hamming weight of a codeword
\mathbf{v}G\in\C
is

w(\mathbf{v}G)=m-\lvert\{i:\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{g}_{i}=0\}\rvert

where
\mathbf{v}^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{z}\colon\mathbf{z}\cdot\mathbf{v}=0\}
and
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}u_{i}v_{i}
, if
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
and
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})
. Let
(\mathcal{P},\mathrm{m})
be the multiset where
\mathcal{P}=\{[\mathbf{g}_{1}],\ldots,[\mathbf{g}_{m}]\}\subseteq\mathrm{PG}(r-1,q)
and with multiplicity function
\mathrm{m}
as

\mathrm{m}(P)=\lvert\{i:P=[\mathbf{g}_{i}]\}\rvert.

We have that the codeword
{\mathbf{v}}G
has weight

if and only if
the projective hyperplane

v_{1}x_{1}+v_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+v_{r}x_{r}=0

contains

points of
(\mathcal{P},\mathrm{m})
.
So, the number of distinct nonzero weights of
\C
corresponds to the distinct sizes of the intersections of
({\mathcal{P}},\mathrm{m})
with all the hyperplanes.
See also
[
36
]

3.
Two pointsets in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})

Let
(0\leq)m_{1}<\cdots<m_{s}(\leq q^{r-1}+q^{r-2}\cdots+q+1)
be
s
non–negative integers, a set

In this section, we are going to construct sets of points of

with few intersection numbers with respect to the lines associated with

–linear sets arising from

Let

\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})

via the line
\ell_{\infty}=\mathrm{PG}(T,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}})

Let

be any
q
-polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[x]
, then

\mathrm{Im}\left(\frac{f(x)}{x}\right)=\left\{\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}\colon x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}},x\neq y\right\},

and hence
\mathrm{Im}\left(\frac{f(x)}{x}\right)

6.
Equivalence

In this section we determine the relationship between
q
-polynomials defining equivalent linear codes.
To this aim recall that
q=p^{h}
and also the following definition from
[
11
]
. Two
q
-polynomials
f(x)
and
g(x)
in
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
are
equivalent
if the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces

U_{f}=\{(x,f(x))\,:\,x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,U_{g}=\{(x,g(x))\,:\,x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\}

are equivalent under the action of the group
\Gamma{\mathrm{L}}(2,q^{n})
, i.e. if there exists an element
\varphi\in\Gamma{\mathrm{L}}(2,q^{n})
such that
U_{f}^{\varphi}=U_{g}
.
Moreover, if
U_{f}
and
U_{g}
are equivalent under the action of
\mathrm{GL}(2,q^{n})
we say that
f(x)
and
g(x)
are
linearly equivalent
.

Note that if
f(x)
is a
q
-polynomial in
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
, then the pointset

\mathcal{B}_{f}=\mathcal{G}_{f}\cup\mathcal{D}_{f}=\{\langle(x,f(x),\alpha)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\,:\,x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}},\,\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q},(x,\alpha)\neq(0,0)\},

defined in Section 3, is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set of rank
n+1
in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})
. Such a set of points is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear blocking set
of the projective plane
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})
and
since
|\mathcal{B}_{f}\setminus\ell_{\infty}|=|\mathcal{B}_{f}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{f}|=q^{n}
,
\mathcal{B}_{f}
is a blocking set
of Rédei type
having the line
\ell_{\infty}
as a
Rédei line
.
Also every line of
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})
intersects
\mathcal{B}_{f}
in a number of points congruent to 1 modulo
q
and hence
\mathcal{B}_{f}
has exponent at least
h
, where the
exponent
of
\mathcal{B}_{f}
is
the maximal integer
e
(
0\leq e\leq hn)
such that
|\ell\cap\mathcal{B}_{f}|\equiv\,1\,(mod\,\,p^{e})
for every line
\ell
in

\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})
(see e.g.
[
32
, Section 3.1]
for more details on linear blocking sets). Starting from an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear blocking set of Rédei type
\mathcal{B}
with Rédei line
\ell
we can consider the following set

\mathcal{C}=(\mathcal{B}\setminus\ell)\cup(\ell\setminus\mathcal{B}).

The pointsets in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})
obtained in this way will be called
co-blocking sets of Rédei type
with Rédei line
\ell
.
The construction
\mathcal{C}_{f}
is such an example.
Following the arguments used in the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 5.5]
we may state the following result.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
f(x)
and
g(x)
be
q
-polynomials in
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
admitting
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
as maximum field of linearity.
Then
\mathcal{B}_{f}
and
\mathcal{B}_{g}
are
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(3,q^{n})
-equivalent if and only if the polynomials
f(x)
and
g(x)
are equivalent.
Also,
\mathcal{B}_{f}
and
\mathcal{B}_{g}
are
\mathrm{PGL}(3,q^{n})
-equivalent if and only if
f(x)
and
g(x)
are linearly equivalent.

Proof.

First of all note that if
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is the maximum field of linearity of the polynomial
f(x)
, then
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is the maximum field of linearity of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
U_{f}
and hence by
[
10
, Proposition 2.3]
there exists a point
P
of
\ell_{\infty}
of weight one in
\mathcal{B}_{f}
. This means that there exists at least one line of
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})
through the point
P
intersecting
\mathcal{B}_{f}
in exactly
q+1
points, i.e. the blocking set
\mathcal{B}_{f}
has exponent
h
, and by
[
14
, Theorem 4.1]
.
If
\mathcal{B}_{f}
has more than one Rédei line, then also
\mathcal{B}_{g}
admits more than one Rédei line as well. By
[
26
, Theorem 5]
it follows that
\mathcal{B}_{f}
and
\mathcal{B}_{g}
are
\mathrm{PGL}(3,q^{n})
-equivalent to
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(x)}
. Recall that the size of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(x)}
is
q^{n}+q^{n-1}+1
and all the Rédei lines of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(x)}
pass through the point
\langle(1,0,0)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
which has weight
n-1
in
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(x)}
.
This implies that the Rédei lines of
\mathcal{B}_{f}
meet
\mathcal{B}_{f}
in an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set of rank
n
of size
q^{n-1}+1
and containing a point of weight
n-1
, that is an
(n-1)
-club.
Since
\ell_{\infty}
is a Rédei line, then by
[
15
, Theorem 2.3]
, we have that
U_{f}
is
\mathrm{GL}(2,q^{n})
-equivalent to
U_{\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(x)}
and the assertion is proved.
Now, suppose that both
\mathcal{B}_{f}=L_{V_{1}}
and
\mathcal{B}_{g}=L_{V_{2}}
have only one Rédei line, which is the line
\ell_{\infty}
.
In particular,

V_{1}=\overline{U}_{f}\oplus\langle(0,0,1)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\,\,\text{and}\,\,V_{2}=\overline{U}_{g}\oplus\langle(0,0,1)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},

where

\overline{U}_{f}=\{(x,f(x),0)\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\}

and

\overline{U}_{g}=\{(x,g(x),0)\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\}.

So,
\mathcal{B}_{f}\cap\ell_{\infty}=L_{\overline{U}_{f}}
and
\mathcal{B}_{g}\cap\ell_{\infty}=L_{\overline{U}_{g}}
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{B}_{f}
and
\mathcal{B}_{g}
are
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(3,q^{n})
-equivalent.
By
[
7
, Proposition 2.3]
, there exists
\varphi\in\mathrm{\Gamma L}(3,q^{n})
such that
\varphi(V_{1})=V_{2}
.
Since
\ell
is the unique Rédei line, we also have
\varphi(\overline{U}_{f})=\overline{U}_{g}
, i.e.
U_{f}
and
U_{g}
are
\mathrm{\Gamma L}(2,q^{n})
-equivalent.
Conversely, suppose that
U_{f}
and
U_{g}
are
\mathrm{\Gamma L}(2,q^{n})
-equivalent via a map
\mu\in\mathrm{\Gamma L}(2,q^{n})
. Then we may extend
\mu
to an element
\varphi\in\mathrm{\Gamma L}(3,q^{n})
such that
\varphi(\overline{U}_{f})=\overline{U}_{g}
and
\varphi((0,0,1))=(0,0,1)
, so that
\varphi(V_{1})=V_{2}
and therefore
\mathcal{B}_{f}
and
\mathcal{B}_{g}
are
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(3,q^{n})
-equivalent.
The last part can be proved in a similar way.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

In
[
26
, Theorem 5]
, the following is stated. Let
\mathcal{B}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear blocking set of
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})
of size
|\mathcal{B}|\geq q^{n}+q^{n-1}+1
. If
\mathcal{B}
has at least two Rédei lines then it is
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(3,q^{n})
-equivalent to the blocking set
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(x)}
.
However, following its proof, one can replace
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(3,q^{n})
by
\mathrm{PGL}(3,q^{n})
.

As a consequence of the previous result we get the following theorem.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
f(x)
be a
q
-polynomial in
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
having
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
as maximum field of linearity and let
s
be the
\mathrm{\Gamma L}
-class of
\mathcal{D}_{f}
.
Then
s
is the number of
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(3,q^{n})
-inequivalent
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear blocking sets of the form
\mathcal{B}_{g}
of
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})
containing
\mathcal{D}_{f}
.

Proof.

Let
U_{1},U_{2},\dots,U_{s}
be
s
distinct
\GammaL
-representatives of
\mathcal{D}_{f}
. Since
\langle(0,1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\not\in\mathcal{D}_{f}
, we may write

U_{i}=U_{g_{i}}=\{(x,g_{i}(x),0)\,:\,x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\}

where the
g_{i}(x)
’s are pairwise inequivalent
q
-polynomials.
Hence, since
\mathcal{D}_{f}=L_{U_{i}}=\mathcal{D}_{g_{i}}
we get

\mathrm{Im}\left(\frac{f(x)}{x}\right)=\mathrm{Im}\left(\frac{g_{i}(x)}{x}\right)

for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
. So, by
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
,
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is the maximum field of linearity of
g_{i}(x)
for any
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
.
Now, we have

\mathcal{B}_{g_{i}}=\{\langle(x,g_{i}(x),\alpha)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\,:\,x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}},\,\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q},(x,\alpha)\neq(0,0)\},

for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
.
Noting that
\mathcal{B}_{g_{i}}\cap\ell_{\infty}=\mathcal{D}_{g_{i}}=\mathcal{D}_{f}
, Theorem
6.1
implies the assertion.
∎

Irreducibility and Singularities of Some Nested Hilbert Schemes
Tim Ryan, Gregory Taylor

Abstract
. Let S S be a smooth projective surface over ℂ \mathbb{C} . We study the local and global geometry of the nested Hilbert scheme of points S [ n , n + 1 , n + 2 ] S^{[n,n+1,n+2]} . In particular, we show that S [ n , n + 1 , n + 2 ] S^{[n,n+1,n+2]} is an irreducible local complete intersection with klt singularities. 2.1.
Nested Hilbert Schemes of Points

The parameter space of unordered
n
-tuples of points on
S
is the symmetric product
S^{(n)}
, which is the cartesian product
S^{n}
mod the action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
Unfortunately, the symmetric product is singular along the locus of double (and more) points.
The Hilbert scheme of points,
S^{[n]}
which parameterizes subschemes of
S
with constant Hilbert polynomial
n
, is a resolution of singularities of the symmetric product via the Hilbert-Chow morphism

\mathrm{hc}:S^{[n]}\to S^{(n)},

which maps a subscheme to its support with multiplicities.

The nested Hilbert scheme
S^{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}
parametrizes
k
-tuples of subschemes with respective constant Hilbert polynomials
n_{1}
through
n_{k}
which are nested inside of each other, i.e.

S^{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}=\{(\xi_{n_{1}},\dots,\xi_{n_{k}})\subset S^{[n_{1}]}\times\dots\times S^{[n_{k}]}:\xi_{n_{1}}\subset\dots\subset\xi_{n_{k}}\}.

In other words, the nested Hilbert scheme is the incidence correspondence inside of the product
S^{[n_{1}]}\times\dots\times S^{[n_{k}]}
, which defines the scheme structure on it.
As a subscheme of the product,
S^{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}
comes equipped with the projections

\pi_{n_{i}}=\mathrm{pr}_{n_{i}}|_{S^{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}}:S^{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}\to S^{[n_{i}]}\text{ and}

\phi_{n_{i}}:S^{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}\to S^{[n_{1},\dots,\widehat{n_{i}},\dots n_{k}]}.

That is
\pi_{n_{i}}(\xi_{n_{1}},\dots,\xi_{n_{k}})=\xi_{n_{i}}
, and
\phi_{n_{i}}(\xi_{n_{1}},\dots,\xi_{n_{k}})=(\xi_{n_{1}},\dots,\widehat{\xi_{n_{i}}},\dots,\xi_{n_{k}})
.
They also come with residual maps

\mathrm{res}_{[n_{i},n_{j}]}:S^{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}\to S^{(n_{j}-n_{i})}

which send a nest of subschemes to the support (with multiplicity) of the difference of two subschemes in the nesting.
This morphism is particularly useful in the case that
n_{j}-n_{i}=1
, in which case

S^{(n_{j}-n_{i})}=S^{(1)}=S^{[1]}=S.

In the case when the nested Hilbert scheme has only two indices, we drop the subscript on the map.
Note, the notation for each of these maps is slightly abusive as they do specify which nested Hilbert scheme is the domain. However, the domain will be clear when we use them so no confusion arises.

In order to study the nested Hilbert schemes, it is often useful to consider the universal families over them.
These are the (possibly reducible) incidence correspondence

Z_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}=\{(p,(\xi_{n_{1}},\dots,\xi_{n_{k}})):p\in\mathrm{supp}(\xi_{n_{k}})\}.

u
. The variety
D
is stratified by rank varieties

D_{r}=\{(x,\phi_{x}):\textnormal{rank}(\phi_{x})\leq r\}.

For some
x\in X
, let
y=u(x)=(x,\langle u^{\vee}|_{x}\rangle)
. Let
r
be the rank of
u|_{x}
. It is well known (see e.g.
[
Hir
]
) that
D_{r}
is smooth away from
D_{r-1}
and the normal bundle at
y
is given by

N_{D_{r}/D}|_{y}\cong\textnormal{Hom}_{k}(\ker(u^{\vee}|_{x}),\textnormal{Coker}\,(u^{\vee}|_{x})).

Definition 4.1
.

The
rank Kodaira-Spencer (RKS) map
is the composition

\theta_{x}:T_{x}X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle du}}{{\to}}T_{y}D\to N_{D_{r}/D}|_{y}=\textnormal{Hom}_{k}(\ker(u^{\vee}|_{x}),\textnormal{Coker}\,(u^{\vee}|_{x}))

where the latter map is the natural quotient map.

There is a convenient smoothness criterion for
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{I}_{Z})
in terms of the RKS map.

Proposition 4.2

(
[
Hir
, Proposition 3.5]
)
.

Let
(x,\langle v\rangle)\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{I}_{Z})
(note that
v\in\ker(u^{\vee}|_{x})
). Suppose the composition

T_{x}X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\theta_{x}}}{{\to}}\textnormal{Hom}_{k}(\ker(u^{\vee}|_{x}),\textnormal{Coker}\,(u^{\vee}|_{x}))\to\textnormal{Hom}_{k}(\langle v\rangle,\textnormal{Coker}\,(u^{\vee}|_{x}))

where the first map is RKS and the second map is the natural restriction, is surjective. Then
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{I}_{Z})
is smooth at
(x,\langle v\rangle)
.

By applying
\textnormal{Hom}_{X}(-,k(x))
to the exact sequence (
1
), we get an exact sequence

0\to\textnormal{Hom}_{X}(\mathcal{I}_{Z},k(x))\to F|_{x}^{\vee}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u^{\vee}|_{x}}}{{\to}}E_{x}^{\vee}\to\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{X}(\mathcal{I}_{Z},k(x))\to 0.

Thus
\textnormal{Hom}_{X}(\mathcal{I}_{Z},k(x))\cong\ker(u^{\vee}|_{x})
and
\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{X}(\mathcal{I}_{Z},k(x))\cong\textnormal{Coker}\,(u^{\vee}|_{x})
. Moreover, one sees from the Koszul resolution that
T_{x}X=\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{X}(k(x),k(x))
. Thus, we can rewrite the RKS map as a map

\theta_{x}:\textnormal{Ext}_{X}^{1}(k(x),k(x))\to\textnormal{Hom}_{k}(\textnormal{Hom}_{X}(\mathcal{I}_{Z},k(x)),\textnormal{Ext}_{X}^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{Z},k(x)))\cong\textnormal{Ext}^{1}(\textnormal{Hom}(\mathcal{I}_{Z},k(x))\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z},k(x)).

Proposition 4.3

(
[
Tik
, §2]
)
.

The RKS map

\theta_{x}:\textnormal{Ext}_{X}^{1}(k(x),k(x))\to\textnormal{Ext}^{1}(\textnormal{Hom}(\mathcal{I}_{Z},k(x))\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z},k(x))

is obtained by applying
\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{X}(-,k(x))
to the evaluation map
\textnormal{Hom}(\mathcal{I}_{Z},k(x))\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z}\to k(x)
.

4.1.
Smoothness of
S^{[n,n+1]}

Recall that the natural map
\textnormal{res}_{[n,n+1]}\times\pi_{n}:S^{[n,n+1]}\to S\times S^{[n]}
is the blowup of the universal family
S^{[1,n]}
. Since
S^{[1,n]}
is a Cohen-Macaulay subscheme of codimension two in
S\times S^{[n]}
, we can apply the machinery above to establish smoothness of
S^{[n,n+1]}
.
In particular, we have a resolution

(2)

0\to E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u}}{{\to}}F\to\mathcal{I}_{S^{[1,n]}}\to 0

where
E,F
are vector bundles.

In what follows, we assume that
S
is a smooth surface. Since smoothness of
S^{[n,n+1]}
is local on
S
, we may assume that
\textnormal{Pic}^{0}(S)
is trivial. We let
X=S\times S^{[n]}
and
Z=S^{[1,n]}
. We denote by
\pi_{1}:X\to S
and
\pi_{2}:X\to S^{[n]}
the projection maps.

Theorem 4.4

(
[
Tik
]
)
.

Let
x=(p,\xi)\in X=S\times S^{[n]}
. The RKS map (see Definition
4.1
)

\theta_{x}:\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{X}(k(x),k(x))\to\textnormal{Ext}_{X}^{1}\left(\textnormal{Hom}_{X}(\mathcal{I}_{Z},k(x))\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z},k(x)\right)

is surjective. Thus,
S^{[n,n+1]}
is smooth.

In fact, Tikhomirov proves a stronger statement. Namely, the restriction of
\theta_{x}
to the subspace
T_{\xi}S^{[n]}
is surjective. To proceed, we transfer this statement to a cohomological statement on the surface. Since
\textnormal{Pic}^{0}(S)
is trivial, we have an isomorphism

\psi:T_{\xi}S^{[n]}=\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{S^{[n]}}(k(\xi),k(\xi))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\xi},\mathcal{I}_{\xi}).

In terms of extensions, this isomorphism is given by pulling back along
\pi_{2}
and tensoring with
\mathcal{I}_{Z}
. Note that
\pi_{2}^{*}k(\xi)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z}=\mathcal{I}_{\xi}
. Thus, given an extension
0\to k(\xi)\to\eta\to k(\xi)\to 0
with extension class
e\in\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{S^{[n]}}(k(\xi),k(\xi))
, we have the extension

\psi(e):0\to\mathcal{I}_{\xi}\to\pi_{2}^{*}\eta\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z}\to\mathcal{I}_{\xi}\to 0

of sheaves on
S
. Note that
-\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z}
is exact since
\mathcal{I}_{Z}
has a minimal free resolution of length two.

5.
Resolution of singularities of
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}

5.1.
Resolution of singularities for
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}

In this section, we construct a resolution of singularities of
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
.
Recall that the map
(\textnormal{res}_{[n+1,n+2]},\phi_{n+2}):S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}\to S\times S^{[n,n+1]}
is the projectivization of the ideal sheaf of the subscheme
Z_{[n,n+1]}=\{(p,\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1}):p\in\xi_{n+1}\}
.
Since
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
is irreducible,
\textnormal{res}_{[n+1,n+2]}
is in fact the blowup of
S\times S^{[n,n+1]}
at the locus
Z_{[n,n+1]}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The subscheme
Z_{[n,n+1]}
has two irreducible components, which are isomorphic to
S^{[1,n,n+1]}
and
S^{[n,n+1]}
.

Proof.

There is an obvious map
S^{[1,n,n+1]}\to Z_{[n,n+1]}
whose image is the collection of triples
(p,\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1})
with
p\in\xi_{n}
. This map is an isomorphism onto its image. Since both
Z_{[n,n+1]}
and
S^{[1,n,n+1]}
have dimension
2n+2
, the image of this map is an irreducible component. There is another natural map
S^{[n,n+1]}\to Z_{[n,n+1]}
given by
(\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1})\mapsto(\textnormal{res}(\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1}),\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1})
. Similarly, this map is an isomorphism onto an irreducible component of
Z_{[n,n+1]}
. To conclude, notice that every element of
Z_{[n,n+1]}
is in the image of one of these maps.
∎

Since
Z_{[n,n+1]}
is reducible, it is unsurprising that
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
is singular, and it suggests an approach to finding a resolution. We obtain a smooth model of
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
by blowing up these irreducible components one at a time. First, we blowup the singular component
W_{1}\cong S^{[1,n,n+1]}
and show that the result is smooth, following the strategy in Section
4
.
To start that, we need to identify the tangent space of
S^{[n,n+1]}
at a point. This was done for general nested Hilbert schemes in
[
Che
]
, and we recall the relevant special case below.

Remark 5.3
.

We can give a more geometric description of the spaces
X_{1},X_{2}
from the theorem above. The blowup
\mathrm{Bl}_{W_{1}}(S\times S^{[n,n+1]})
is isomorphic to the variety

S^{[n,(n+1)^{2}]}=\{(\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1},\xi^{\prime}_{n+1}):\xi_{n}\subseteq\xi_{n+1}\text{ and }\xi_{n}\subseteq\xi^{\prime}_{n+1}\}\subseteq S^{[n]}\times S^{[n+1]}\times S^{[n+1]}.

An alternate approach to the smoothness of
X_{1}=S^{[n,(n+1)^{2}]}
is to mimic the approach of
[
Che
]
. That is, one assumes that
S=\mathbb{P}^{2}
. It then suffices to establish smoothness at the Borel fixed points, which are subschemes defined by monomial ideals. We settled on our approach to emphasize the paper
[
Tik
]
and the connections with
[
JL
]
.

The vareity
X_{2}
surjects onto
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
. Thus, to describe
X_{2}
as a variety parameterizing certain collections of subschemes of
S
, each point of
X_{2}
should determine a length
n+2
subscheme. Note that the proper transform of
W_{2}\cong S^{[n,n+1]}
in
S^{[n,(n+1)^{2}]}
is the locus triples
(\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1},\xi^{\prime}_{n+1})
such that
\xi_{n+1}=\xi^{\prime}_{n+1}
. So away from
\overline{W_{2}}
, the subscheme
\xi_{n+2}
is easily determined. Indeed if
(\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1},\xi_{n+1}^{\prime})\notin\overline{W_{2}}
, then
\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{n+1}}\cap\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{n+1}^{\prime}}
is an ideal of colength
n+2
. On the other hand, if
\xi=(\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1},\xi_{n+1})\in\overline{W_{2}}
, the fiber of the blowup
X_{2}\to X_{1}
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Each point in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
gives a vector
[v]
in the projectivized tangent space to
S
at
\textnormal{res}(\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1})
, and we obtain the
(n+2)
-scheme by taking the flat limit of a point colliding with
\xi_{n+1}
along the tangent vector
v
.

5.2.
Singularities

We can use the resolution of singularities of
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
to study the singularities of it and other related nested Hilbert schemes.

Proposition 5.4
.

The two-step nested Hilbert scheme
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
is lci.

Proof.

First, note that
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
is smooth away from the exceptional locus of the map
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{I}_{Z_{[n,n+1]}}):S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}\to S\times S^{[n,n+1]}
. Thus, it suffices to show that for each
\zeta\in S\times S^{[n,n+1]}
, the scheme
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{I}_{Z_{[n,n+1]},\zeta})
is Gorenstein.
Recall that
Z_{[n,n+1]}
is a codimension two Cohen-Macaulay subscheme of the smooth variety
S\times S^{[n,n+1]}
. So for any
\zeta=(p,\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1})\in Z_{[n,n+1]}
, the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{Z_{[n,n+1]},\zeta}
is a Cohen-Macaulay module over the regular ring
R=\mathcal{O}_{S\times S^{[n,n+1]},\zeta}
. So we have an exact sequence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus^{b_{2}}R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus^{b_{1}}R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{I}_{Z_{[n,n+1]},\zeta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}

Note that
b_{1}=b_{2}+1
. This sequence presents
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{I}_{Z_{[n,n+1]},\zeta})
as a subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{b_{1}-1}_{R}
. Pulling back
M
along the projection map
\pi:\mathbb{P}^{b_{1}-1}_{R}\to\textnormal{Spec}{R}
and composing with the tautological quotient, we get the following diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus^{b_{2}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{b_{1}-1}_{R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus^{b_{1}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{b_{1}-1}_{R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{b_{1}-1}_{R}}(1).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}M}

Then
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{I}_{Z_{[n,n+1]}})
is scheme-theoretically defined by the vanishing of
\sigma
so is cut out by
b_{2}=b_{1}-1
equations. By Theorem
3.10
,
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{I}_{Z_{[n,n+1]}})=S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
is irreducible of dimension
2n+4
so has codimension
b_{1}-1
in
\mathbb{P}^{b_{1}-1}_{R}
. Thus,
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
is lci.
∎

Similarly, the resolution of
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
immediately implies that it is log terminal.

Corollary 5.5
.

The two-step nested Hilbert scheme
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
is log terminal.

Proof.

Recall that
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
and its resolution
\widetilde{S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}}
are constructed as follows
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}=\mathrm{Bl}_{Z_{[n,n+1]}}S\times S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}}=\mathrm{Bl}_{\overline{W_{2}}}X_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\times S^{[n,n+1]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{1}=\mathrm{Bl}_{W_{1}}(S\times S^{[n,n+1]})}

where the arrows are blowups and the
W_{i}
’s are the irreducible components of
Z_{[n,n+1]}
. Hence
\widetilde{S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}}
is a small resolution. Since
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
is Gorenstein, this implies it is log terminal.
∎

Since the map
\phi_{1}:S^{[1,n,n+1,n+2]}\to S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
is finite and flat and the target is Cohen-Macaulay, we obtain the following as an immediate corollary.

Corollary 5.6
.

The nested Hilbert scheme
S^{[1,n,n+1,n+2]}
is Cohen-Macaulay.

From our description of
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
as a local complete intersection in a smooth variety, we obtain a Koszul complex that implies that
S^{[1,n+1,n+2]}
has rational singularities.

Corollary 5.7
.

For
n\geq 2
, the nested Hilbert scheme
S^{[1,n,n+1]}
has rational singularities.

Proof.

Since
S^{[n-1,n,n+1]}
has rational singularities, it suffices to show that the map
\rho:S^{[n-1,n,n+1]}\to S^{[1,n,n+1]}
given by

\rho(\xi_{n-1},\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1})=\left(\textnormal{res}(\xi_{n-1},\xi_{n}),\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1}\right)

satisfies
R^{i}\rho_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S^{[n-1,n,n+1]}}=0
for
i>0
. As in the proof of Proposition
5.4
, we have an exact sequence

0\to E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u}}{{\to}}F\to\mathcal{I}_{Z_{[n-1,n]}}\to 0

where
E,F
are vector bundles. Then
S^{[n-1,n,n+1]}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{I}_{Z_{[n-1,n]}})
is the zero-subscheme of the map
pi_{F}^{*}E\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(F)}(1)
. We get a Koszul complex

\cdots\to\bigwedge^{2}\pi^{*}E(-1)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(F)}\to\mathcal{O}_{S^{[n-1,n,n+1]}}\to 0

which implies the requisite vanishing.
∎

We also use Riemann-Hurwitz to get the following.

Corollary 5.8
.

S^{[1,n,n+1]}
is not
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein.

Proof.

The forgetful map
\phi_{1}:S^{[1,n,n+1]}\to S^{[n,n+1]}
is finite and ramified over the locus

B_{[n]}=\{(p,\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1}):p\subset\xi_{n}\subset\xi_{n+1}\text{ and }\xi_{n}\text{ is non-reduced}\}.

There are two Weil divisors that dominate
B_{[n]}
, denoted by
E_{[n,n+1]}^{1}
and
E_{[n,n+1]}^{2}
.
Each of these generically parameterizes triples where
\xi_{n}
consists of
n-2
reduced points and a double point.
In
E_{[n]}^{2}
(
E_{[n]}^{1}
), the double point is supported (not) at
p
.
By an entirely analogous argument to the one in the proof of Theorem 7.6 of
[
Fog2
]
for the case of
S^{[1,n]}
, these are not
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, but their sum is.
Let
r_{i}
be the ramification index of
E_{[n]}^{i}
over
B_{[n]}
, and
K(*)
be the function field of
*
. From the identity

r_{1}\left[K\left(E_{[n]}^{1}\right):K\left(B_{[n]}\right)\right]+r_{2}\left[K\left(E_{[n]}^{2}\right):K\left(B_{[n]}\right)\right]=n,

we see that
r_{1}=2
and
r_{2}=1
since
\left[K\left(E_{[n]}^{1}\right):K\left(B_{[n]}\right)\right]=1
and
\left[K\left(E_{[n]}^{2}\right):K\left(B_{[n]}\right)\right]=n-2
.
Then Riemann-Hurwitz formula for finite maps gives that

K_{S^{[1,n,n+1]}}=\phi_{1}^{*}\left(K_{S^{[n,n+1]}}\right)+E_{[n]}^{1}

so
K_{S^{[1,n,+2]}}
cannot be
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.
∎

6.
Further geometric results

In this section, we study a bit of the birational geometry of
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
when
S
is a smooth surface.
In particular, we compute its Picard group and its canonical divisor when
h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})=0
.
The results may of course be generalized to the case of surfaces with irregularity with a bit of care.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
S
be a smooth surface with
h^{1}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})=0
.
Then

\mathrm{Pic}\left(S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}\right)\cong\mathrm{Pic}(S)^{3}\bigoplus\mathbb{Z}^{3}

Proof.

By
[
RY
]
,
\mathrm{Pic}\left(S^{[n,n+1]}\right)=\mathrm{Pic}(S)^{2}\bigoplus\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

The exceptional divisor of the blow up is irreducible by Theorem
3.10
,
so

\mathrm{Pic}(\mathrm{Bl}_{W_{1}})\cong\mathrm{Pic}(S\times S^{[n,n+1]})\oplus\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathrm{Pic}(S)^{3}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{3}.

Then
\widetilde{S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}}
is the blow up of
\mathrm{Bl}_{W_{1}}
along a smooth irreducible subvariety of codimension 2, so we have

\mathrm{Pic}\left(\widetilde{S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}}\right)\cong\mathrm{Pic}(\mathrm{Bl}_{W_{1}})\oplus\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathrm{Pic}(S)^{3}\bigoplus\mathbb{Z}^{4}.

Since
\widetilde{S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}}
is smooth, this is also its Weil divisor group.
The resolution of singularities is an isomorphism in codimension two so that is also the Weil divisor group of
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
.

As the singularities of
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
are in codimension two and it is Cohen-Macaulay by Theorem 5.8.6 of
[
Gro
]
, it is normal.
Since it is normal, the Picard group injects into the Weil divisor group.

Since the exceptional divisor is Cartier, the Picard group of
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
contains
\mathrm{Pic}(S)^{3}\bigoplus\mathbb{Z}^{3}
where those are generated by the exceptional divisor and the pullbacks from
S\times S^{[n,n+1]}
.
This part of the Picard group is exactly one copy of
\mathbb{Z}
smaller than the Weil divisor group.
Since the resolution of
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
is a small contraction, it is not
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.
This implies that the remaining factor of the Weil divisor group must not have any
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors in it.
As a result, the Picard group is exactly what was claimed.
∎

We can also give an alternative description of Picard group.
It is easy to see that
\pi_{n}^{*}
,
\pi_{n+1}^{*}
, and
\pi_{n+2}^{*}
are injective maps of Picard groups.
Further, it is not difficult to see that the pullbacks of divisors from any pair of distinct Hilbert schemes are numerically distinct.
In other words, the map
(\pi_{n}^{*},\pi_{n+1}^{*},\pi_{n+2}^{*})
is injective.
Since the Picard groups are free group of the same size, we get

\mathrm{Pic}\left(S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}\right)\cong\mathrm{Pic}\left(S^{[n]}\right)\oplus\mathrm{Pic}\left(S^{[n+1]}\right)\oplus\mathrm{Pic}\left(S^{[n+2]}\right).

Proposition 6.2
.

The class of the canonical divisor of
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
is

K_{S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}}=\pi_{n+2}^{*}(K_{S})+\pi_{n+2}^{*}(B[n+2])-\pi_{n}^{*}(B[n]).

Proof.

The Hilbert scheme is a crepant resolution of the symmetric product
[
Bea
]
so its canonical divisor is
K_{S}[n]=\mathrm{hc}^{*}(K_{S}^{\boxtimes n})
where
\boxtimes
means the descent to the symmetric product of the tensor product of the pullback of
K_{S}
along each projection of
S^{n}
to
S
.
As
S^{[n,n+1]}
is the blow-up of
S\times S^{[n]}
along a codimension two subvariety which is smooth in codimension one (codimension three in the product), its canonical divisor is

K_{S^{[n,n+1]}}=\mathrm{res}^{*}(K_{S})+\pi_{n}^{*}(K_{S}[n])+E_{(\mathrm{res},\pi_{n})}=\pi_{n+1}^{*}(K_{S}[n+1])+E_{(\mathrm{res},\pi_{n})}.

Since we are blowing up codimension two varieties which are smooth in codimension one (codimension three in the product), we see that
\widetilde{S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}}
has canonical bundle

K_{\widetilde{S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}}}=\mathrm{res}_{[n+1,n+2]}^{*}(K_{S})+\phi_{[n+2]}^{*}(K_{S^{[n,n+1]}})+E_{1}+E_{2}

where
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are the exceptional divisors of the successive blow-ups in the construction of
\widetilde{S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}}
.
Since the resolution is a small resolution, it is crepant so the canonical is unchanged in the Picard group of
S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}
.
On there, this can be simplified to

K_{S^{[n,n+1,n+2]}}=\pi_{n+2}^{*}(K_{S})+\pi_{n+2}^{*}(B[n+2])-\pi_{n}^{*}(B[n])

since
\pi_{n+2}^{*}(B[n+2])
is the union of
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
, the pullback of the exceptional divisor of the blow-up defining
S^{[n,n+1]}
, and
\pi_{n}^{*}(B[n])
.
∎

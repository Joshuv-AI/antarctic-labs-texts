Spectral stability of shock profiles for hyperbolically regularized systems of conservation laws
Johannes Bärlin

Abstract
We report a proof that under natural assumptions shock profiles viewed as heteroclinic travelling wave solutions to a hyperbolically regularized system of conservation laws are spectrally stable if the shock amplitude is sufficiently small. This means that an associated Evans function ℰ : Λ → ℂ \mathcal{E}:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{C} with Λ ⊂ ℂ \Lambda\subset\mathbb{C} an open superset of the closed right half plane ℍ + ≡ { κ ∈ ℂ : Re ​ κ ≥ 0 } \mathbb{H}^{+}\equiv\{\kappa\in\mathbb{C}:\textnormal{Re}\,\kappa\geq 0\} , has only one zero, namely a simple zero at 0 0 . The result is analogous to the one obtained in [ 13 ] and [ 52 ] for parabolically regularized systems of conservation laws, and also distinctly extends findings on hyperbolic relaxation systems in [ 49 , 52 , 61 ] .

When studying the stability of travelling wave solutions associated to shocks of, e.g., viscous conservation laws one is met with the problem of the absence of a spectral gap in the sense that the essential spectrum touches the imaginary axis. At first glance this limits the scope of results of an ansatz by Evans function theory since an analytic extension of the Evans function into the essential spectrum is necessary
[

. But after the discovery that Evans bundles of shock profiles have suitable analytic extensions

[

,

]
and hyperbolic relaxation systems
[

,
48
]
. Whether such an implication also holds for hyperbolically regularized systems of conservation laws such as (

4
Grassmannian flows

An important tool in the following investigations are flows on Grassmann manifolds that are induced on the latter by the flow of a linear ordinary differential equation (see
[
1
]
). In this section the definitions and results needed in the later analysis are presented including a derivation of the well-known algebraic Riccati equation that governs the evolution of vector spaces under a linear differential equation. More background material may be found in many geometry textbooks, such as
[
24
]
and
[
39
]
. Furthermore, flows on Grassmannian manifolds are a popular topic in control theory, see e.g.
[
6
]
, where the algebraic Riccati equation is treated, too. In
[
17
]
the reader may find a nice overview of the topic and some characterizations of smoothness of functions taking values in Grassmannian manifolds.

Let
d,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\leq d
. Set

\displaystyle M_{d,m}:=\{B\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times m}:\;\textnormal{rank}\,B=m\}

and for
B\in M_{d,m}
denote by
\textnormal{span}\,B
the
m
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
spanned by the columns of
B
. For any matrix
B\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times m}
we will write

\displaystyle B=\begin{pmatrix}B_{1}\\
B_{2}\end{pmatrix}

for appropriate matrices
B_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
and
B_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times m}
, and
B=(b_{j})_{j}
will mean that
b_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
is the
j
-th column of
B
.
M_{d,m}
is an open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{d\times m}
and is endowed with the standard analytic structure as an open submanifold of
\mathbb{C}^{d\times m}
. The set

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}^{d}_{m}(\mathbb{C}):=\{X|\;X\;\textnormal{is an}\;m\;\textnormal{dim. subspace of}\;\mathbb{C}^{d}\}.

can be given an analytic structure yielding a compact complex analytic manifold of dimension
(d-m)m
which is called a Grassmann or Grassmannian manifold. For an ordered basis
\mathcal{B}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d})
of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
canonical local coordinates of
\mathscr{G}^{d}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
centered at
X=\textnormal{span}\,\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\}
are defined by

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times m}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathscr{G}^{d}_{m}(\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle\mapsto\textnormal{span}\,M_{\mathcal{B}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}_{m}\\
T\end{pmatrix}

where

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{B}}=(b_{1}\ldots b_{d})\in\textnormal{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{C}).

Note
\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(0)=X
. The analytic manifolds
M_{d,m}
and
\mathscr{G}^{d}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
are connected by the fact that

\displaystyle\pi_{d,m}:M_{d,m}\rightarrow\mathscr{G}^{d}_{m}(\mathbb{C}),\;B\mapsto\textnormal{span}\,B

is an analytic quotient map.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}
and consider the linear ordinary differential equation

(21)

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}=A\xi\;\;(\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{d})

with flow
((t,\xi)\mapsto e^{At}\xi)
. For
X\in\mathscr{G}^{d}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
with basis
\mathcal{B}_{X}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})
the Grassmannian flow on
\mathscr{G}^{d}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
associated to (
21
) starting in
X
is defined by

\displaystyle X(t)=\pi_{d,m}((e^{At}b_{j})_{j\leq m})=\textnormal{span}\,(e^{At}b_{j})_{j\leq m}.

The definition of
(t\mapsto X(t))
is independent of the choice of basis of
X
and gives rise to a flow
((t,X)\mapsto\Phi(t,X))
on
\mathscr{G}^{d}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
by letting
(t\mapsto\Phi(t,X))
be the Grassmannian flow on
\mathscr{G}^{d}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
associated to (
21
) starting in
X
. Augment
\mathcal{B}_{X}
to a basis
\mathcal{B}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d})
of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Then the map

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times m}

\displaystyle\rightarrow M_{d,m}

\displaystyle(t,T)

\displaystyle\mapsto e^{At}M_{\mathcal{B}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}_{m}\\
T\end{pmatrix}

is smooth in
t
and analytic in
T
. For all
(t,T)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times m}
we have by definitions

(22)

\displaystyle\Phi(t,\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(T))=\pi_{d,m}\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{B}}(t,T)

which shows that
\Phi
is smooth in
t
and analytic in
X
w.r.t. to the analytic structure of
\mathscr{G}^{d}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
. We give a local representation of the vector field induced by
\Phi
on
\mathscr{G}^{d}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
by calculating the derivative

\displaystyle\Gamma^{m}(A)X:=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}\Phi(t,X)\in T\mathscr{G}^{d}_{m}(\mathbb{C}).

in
\phi_{\mathcal{B}}
coordinates. First we are interested in the differential of
\psi:=\phi^{-1}\circ\pi|_{U}
where
\phi=\phi_{\mathcal{B}},\pi=\pi_{d,m}
and
U:=\pi^{-1}(\phi(\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times m}))
is open. For
B\in U
there is a
T\in C^{d-m\times m}
with
\textnormal{span}\,B=\phi(T)
. Hence, we may find
E\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
such that

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{B}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}_{m}\\
T\end{pmatrix}=BE\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}_{m}\\
T\end{pmatrix}=\tilde{B}E

with
\tilde{B}=M_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}B
. We deduce that
\tilde{B}_{1}
is invertible since
\tilde{B}_{1}E=\mathds{1}_{m}
, and that
T=\tilde{B}_{2}\tilde{B}_{1}^{-1}
holds. Vice versa, if for
B\in M_{d,m}
the matrix
\tilde{B}=M_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}B
has invertible submatrix
\tilde{B}_{1}
then

\displaystyle\textnormal{span}\,\tilde{B}=\textnormal{span}\,\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}_{m}\\
\tilde{B}_{2}\tilde{B}_{1}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

because multiplying
\tilde{B}
by
\tilde{B}_{1}^{-1}
from the right results only in a change of basis of
\textnormal{span}\,B
. This implies
\textnormal{span}\,B=\phi(\tilde{B}_{2}\tilde{B}_{1}^{-1})
and we have found

\displaystyle U=\{B\in M_{d,m}:\;\tilde{B}_{1}\in\textnormal{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{C})\;\textnormal{where}\;\tilde{B}=M_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}B\}.

From these considerations we find that the map
\psi
is given by
\psi=\tilde{\psi}\circ M_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}
where

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}:\tilde{U}:=M_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}U

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times m},

\displaystyle\tilde{B}

\displaystyle\mapsto\tilde{B}_{2}\tilde{B}_{1}^{-1}.

The differential of
\tilde{\psi}
at
\tilde{B}\in\tilde{U}
is calculated to be

(23)

\displaystyle D\tilde{\psi}(\tilde{B}):\mathbb{C}^{d\times m}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times m},E\mapsto E_{2}\tilde{B}_{1}^{-1}-\tilde{B}_{2}\tilde{B}_{1}^{-1}E_{1}\tilde{B}_{1}^{-1}.

If
\tilde{B}_{1}=\mathds{1}_{m}
then

\displaystyle D\tilde{\psi}(\tilde{B})E=E_{2}-\tilde{B_{2}}E_{1}=(-\tilde{B}_{2},\mathds{1}_{n-m})E.

We find

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}(\phi^{-1}\circ\Phi)(t,\phi(T))

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}(\psi\circ\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{B}})(t,T)

\displaystyle=D\psi(\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{B}}(0,T))\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{B}}(t,T)

\displaystyle=D\tilde{\psi}\left(M_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}M_{\mathcal{B}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}_{m}\\
T\end{pmatrix}\right)M_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}AM_{\mathcal{B}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}_{m}\\
T\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=(-T,\mathds{1}_{d-m})A_{\mathcal{B}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}_{m}\\
T\end{pmatrix}

where
A_{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}
is the representative of
A
with respect to
\mathcal{B}
, i.e.
A_{\mathcal{B}}=M_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}AM_{\mathcal{B}}
. Hence, in canonical local coordinates
\phi_{\mathcal{B}}
the Grassmannian flow consists of solutions to the Riccati-type equation

(24)

\displaystyle T^{\prime}=(-T,\mathds{1}_{d-m})A_{\mathcal{B}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}_{m}\\
T\end{pmatrix}\;\;\textnormal{on}\;\;\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times m}.

The global expression for the vector field associated to
\Phi
reads

(25)

\displaystyle X^{\prime}=\Gamma^{m}(A)X\;\;\textnormal{on}\;\;\mathscr{G}^{d}_{m}(\mathbb{C}).

The fact that the Grassmannian flow induced by a linear ordinary differential equations is locally of Riccati-type is of course well-known. This is summarized in the following lemma which is also a more general form of Lemma 3 (i) in
[
13
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be an ordered basis of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Then in canonical local coordinates
\phi_{\mathcal{B}}
the Grassmannian flow on
\mathscr{G}^{d}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
associated to (
21
) consists of solutions to the Riccati-type equation

(26)

\displaystyle T^{\prime}=(-T,\mathds{1}_{d-m})A_{\mathcal{B}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}_{m}\\
T\end{pmatrix}\;\;\textnormal{on}\;\;\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times m}

where
A_{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}
is the representative of
A
w.r.t. the basis
\mathcal{B}
. If
A_{\mathcal{B}}
is diagonal with

\displaystyle A_{\mathcal{B}}=\textnormal{diag}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{d})

then (
26
) reads for
1\leq j\leq d-m,1\leq k\leq m

\displaystyle(T)_{jk}^{\prime}=(\mu_{m+j}-\mu_{k})(T)_{jk}.

On
\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times m}
let
\langle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\rangle
denote the Frobenius inner product given by

\displaystyle\langle\langle E,F\rangle\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{d-m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}(E)_{jk}\overline{(F)}_{jk}\;\;(E,F\in\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times m})

and let
||\cdot||
be the norm on
\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times m}
induced by
\langle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\rangle
. We give some useful consequences of Lemma
4.1
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{B}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d})
be an ordered basis of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
such that
X:=\textnormal{span}\,\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\}
and
Y:=\textnormal{span}\,\{b_{m+1},\ldots,b_{d}\}
are complementary invariant subspaces of
A
. Then

\displaystyle A_{\mathcal{B}}=\begin{pmatrix}A^{X}&0\\
0&A^{Y}\end{pmatrix}

for appropriate
A^{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times m},A^{Y}\in\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times(d-m)}
and the local representation of (
25
) at
X
in
\phi_{\mathcal{B}}
-coordinates is given by the linear ordinary differential equation

(27)

\displaystyle T^{\prime}=-TA^{X}+A^{Y}T.

If there exist
c^{X},c^{Y}\in\mathbb{R}
with
c^{X}+c^{Y}>0
and for all
\eta^{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{m},\eta^{Y}\in\mathbb{C}^{d-m}

(28)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \textnormal{Re}\,\langle\eta^{X},A^{X}\eta^{X}\rangle&\geq c^{X}|\eta^{X}|^{2}\\
\textnormal{Re}\,\langle\eta^{Y},A^{Y}\eta^{Y}\rangle&\leq-c^{Y}|\eta^{Y}|^{2}\end{aligned}\right.

then
X
is a hyperbolic attractor for the Grassmannian flow
\Phi
. For any
r>0
the set
\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(B_{r}(0))
is a positively invariant neighbourhood of
X
in
\mathscr{G}^{d}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

The assertion on
A_{\mathcal{B}}
and and the local form of
\Gamma^{m}
follow immediately by linear algebra and (
24
). Denote by

\displaystyle F(T):=-TA^{X}+A^{Y}T

the local representation of the vector field
\Gamma^{m}
as given in (
27
).
X
is a fixed point of
\Phi
since
\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(0)=X
and
F(0)=0
. We show that
DF(0)
is negative definite and that for any
r>0
the function

\displaystyle L:B_{r}(0)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle\mapsto||T||^{2}

is a strict Lyapunov function for (
24
). The differential of
F
at
0
is

\displaystyle DF(0)E=-EA^{X}+A^{Y}E\;\;(E\in\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times m}).

For
E\in\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times m}
denote by
\tilde{E}_{i}
the
i
-th row of
E
and by
E_{j}
the
j
-th column of
E
. Then

\displaystyle 2\textnormal{Re}\,\langle\langle E,DF(0)E\rangle\rangle=

\displaystyle-\langle\langle E,EA^{X}\rangle\rangle+\langle\langle E,A^{Y}E\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\langle EA^{X},E\rangle\rangle+\langle\langle A^{Y}E,E\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{d-m}\langle\tilde{E}_{i},(A^{X})^{t}\tilde{E}_{i}\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle E_{j},A^{Y}E_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{d-m}\langle(A^{X})^{t}\tilde{E}_{i},\tilde{E}_{i}\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle A^{Y}E_{j},E_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{d-m}\textnormal{Re}\,\langle\tilde{E}_{i},(A^{X})^{t}\tilde{E}_{i}\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\textnormal{Re}\,\langle E_{j},A^{Y}E_{j}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(-c^{X}\sum_{i=1}^{d-m}|\tilde{E}_{i}|^{2}-c^{Y}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|E_{j}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2(c^{X}+c^{Y})||E||^{2},

i.e.
DF(0)
is negative definite and
X
a hyperbolic fixed point of
\Phi
since
c^{X}+c^{Y}>0
. Note that for the last inequality we have used

\displaystyle\langle\eta,A^{t}\eta\rangle=\langle\overline{\eta},A\overline{\eta}\rangle.

for
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. By exactly the same calculation we find

\displaystyle L(T)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=2\textnormal{Re}\,\langle\langle T,T^{\prime}\rangle\rangle=2\textnormal{Re}\,\langle\langle T,F(T)\rangle\rangle\leq-2(c^{X}+c^{Y})||T||^{2}

showing that
L
is a strict Lyapunov function and

is positively invariant for (
27
).
∎

Corollary 4.3

Let

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \textnormal{Re}\,\langle\eta^{Y},A^{Y}\eta^{Y}\rangle&\leq-c|\eta^{Y}|^{2}\\

\displaystyle V^{Z}:=\left\{E\in\mathbb{C}^{(d-m)\times m}\Big|\;\exists E_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{m_{2}\times m}:E=\begin{pmatrix}0\\

Lemma 4.4

For every

\displaystyle R(\tau)^{-1}A(\tau)R(\tau)=\begin{pmatrix}A^{>}(\tau)&0\\

with

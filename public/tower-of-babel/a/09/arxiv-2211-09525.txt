On Perverse sheaves of a Coxeter hyperplane arrangement of type 𝒜 n \mathcal{A}_{n}
Umesh V Dubey, Subham Sarkar

Abstract
. Kapranov and schechtman gave quiver description of perverse sheaves on real hyperplane arrangements. We used this description to relate the perverse sheaves on Coxeter hyperplane arrangements of type 𝒜 n \mathcal{A}_{n} for different values of n n . As a consequence we prove that the simple perverse sheaves whose stalk on open cells are zero are induced from the perverse sheaves on lower dimension arrangements.

Real hyperplane arrangements

Let

3.2.
Double representation for Coxeter arrangement

From Theorem
2.5
, the abelian category
\mathscr{P}er(V_{{\mathds{C}}}^{n},S^{n})
is equivalent to the abelian category
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}
, the full subcategory of double representations of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}
satisfying
monotonicity
,
transitivity
and
invertibility
. Let us denote
\mathcal{J}_{n}:=\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}
.
Reacll that the hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}
is isomorphic to the hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}
in
{\mathds{R}}^{n}
for all
n
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A}_{n+1}}
induces an arrangement on
L^{n+1}_{i,j}
. The induced arrangement on
L^{n+1}_{i,j}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}_{W_{n}^{i,j}}
. By Theorem
3.5
there is an order preserving bijection between posets of faces of the above two arrangements
\mathcal{H}_{{\mathds{R}}^{n}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{W_{n}^{i,j}}
. In particular, there is an order preserving embedding of posets

\iota_{L_{i,j}^{n+1}}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n+1}}.

Define

\tilde{\Phi}_{L_{i,j}^{n+1}}:\operatorname{Rep}_{2}(\mathcal{A}_{n})\rightarrow\operatorname{Rep}_{2}(\mathcal{A}_{n+1})

\psi=(E_{C},\gamma_{C^{\prime}C},\delta_{CC^{\prime}})_{(C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}})}\mapsto\tilde{\Phi}_{L_{i,j}^{n+1}}\psi:=(\tilde{E}_{D},\tilde{\gamma}_{D^{\prime}D},\tilde{\delta}_{DD^{\prime}})_{(D,D^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n+1}})},

where

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{D}

\displaystyle=E_{\iota_{L_{i,j}^{n+1}}(\tilde{C})}\hskip 10.0pt\text{ if }D=\iota_{L_{i,j}^{n+1}}(\tilde{C})

\displaystyle=0,\text{ otherwise.}

Proposition 3.6
.

For any
1\leq i<j\leq n+1
,
\tilde{\Phi}_{L_{i,j}^{n+1}}
induces an exact and fully-faithful functor

\Phi_{L_{i,j}^{n+1}}:\mathcal{J}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{J}_{n+1}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{J}_{n+1|L^{n+1}_{i,j}}
be the full subcategory of
\mathcal{J}_{n+1}
consisting of double representations of poset associated to the induce hyperplane arrangement on
L^{n+1}_{i,j}
.
Thus any element of
\mathcal{J}_{n+1|L^{n+1}_{i,j}}
can be identified with
(E_{C},\gamma_{C^{\prime}C},\delta_{CC^{\prime}})_{(C,C^{\prime}\in\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}})}
where
\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}:=\iota_{L_{i,j}^{n+1}}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}})
.
In order to prove
\tilde{\Phi}_{L_{i,j}^{n+1}}
induces the following functor

\Phi_{L_{i,j}^{n+1}}:\mathcal{J}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{J}_{n+1|L^{n+1}_{i,j}}\subseteq\mathcal{J}_{n+1},

it is enough to show that
\tilde{\Phi}_{L_{i,j}^{n+1}}(\psi)
satisfies monotonicity, transitivity and invertibility. For simplicity, we consider the case
i=1
and
j=n+1
.

Monotonicity

:
For any
\psi=(E_{C},\gamma_{C^{\prime}C},\delta_{CC^{\prime}})_{(C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}})}\in\mathcal{J}_{n}
, by montonicity one has

\gamma_{C^{\prime}C}\delta_{CC^{\prime}}=Id_{E_{C}},

where
C^{\prime}
,
C\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}
satisfying
C^{\prime}\leq C
.
Hence
\iota_{L_{1,n+1}^{n+1}}(C^{\prime})\leq\iota_{L_{1,n+1}^{n+1}}(C)
. Thus
\tilde{\Phi}_{L_{i,j}^{n}}(\psi)
satisfies monotonicity relation for
D=\iota_{L_{1,n+1}^{n+1}}(C)
and
D^{\prime}=\iota_{L_{1,n+1}^{n+1}}(C^{\prime})
. For any
D\subset(V^{n+1}\setminus L^{n+1}_{1,n+1})
, one has
E_{D}=0
and
\delta_{DD^{\prime}}
is the zero map. Hence monotonicity is satisfied by
\Phi_{L_{1,n+1}^{n+1}}(\psi)
.

Transitivity

:
For a collinear triple
(A,B,C)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}^{3}
, using the transitivity relation on
\phi
one obtains
\phi_{AC}=\phi_{BC}\phi_{AB}
. In order to check transitivity for
\Phi_{L_{1,n+1}^{n+1}}(\psi)
, it is enough to prove transitivity for all collinear triples in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n+1}}^{3}
.

If
A
,
B
and
C\subset L^{n+1}_{1,n+1}
, then collinear triple
(A,B,C)
is a image of a collinear triple in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}
. Hence transitivity of such a triple is satisfied by the transitivity relation of
\psi
. If any two of
A
,
B
and
C
lies in
L^{n+1}_{1,n+1}
then the third one must lie on
L^{n+1}_{1,n+1}
, as
L^{n+1}_{1,n+1}
is a linear subspace of
V^{n+1}
. Hence the transitivity is satisfied for such triple follows from the previous case. If
A
,
C\subset(V^{n+1}\setminus L^{n+1}_{1,n+1})
and
B\subset L^{n+1}_{1,n+1}
, then from definition it follows that
E_{A}=E_{C}=0
with
\phi_{AC}=0
and
\phi_{AB}=0
. Hence
\phi_{AC}=\phi_{BC}\phi_{AB}=0
. Similarly if none of the
A
,
B
and
C
lies in
L^{n+1}_{1,n+1}
, then
E_{A}=E_{B}=E_{C}=0
and
\phi_{AC}=\phi_{BC}=\phi_{AB}=0
. Thus transitivity holds for
\Phi_{L_{1,n+1}^{n+1}}(\psi)
.

Invertibility

:
Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two faces opposes through a
(d-1)
-dimensional face
B
. Then
\phi_{C_{1}C_{2}}
is an isomorphism. Thus either
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
both lies in
L^{n+1}_{1,n+1}
or both do not lie in
L^{n+1}_{1,n+1}
. In any case it is an isomorphism.

∎

Theorem 3.7
.

1\leq i<j\leq n+1
, there is a fully-faithful exact functor compatible with the Verdier duality

\Lambda_{L_{i,j}^{n+1}}:\mathscr{P}er(V_{{\mathds{C}}}^{n},\mathcal{S}^{n})\rightarrow\mathscr{P}er(V_{{\mathds{C}}}^{n+1},\mathcal{S}^{n+1}),

(21)

which makes the following diagram commutative

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{P}er(V_{{\mathds{C}}}^{n},\mathcal{S}^{n})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(h_{(-1)}\circ k_{*})[-1]}

In this diagram,

]
], where

The functor

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a constructible sheaves on the hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{n+1}
. Then we have the following.

(1)

If
\mathcal{F}\in\mathscr{P}er(V_{{\mathds{C}}}^{n+1},\mathcal{S}^{n+1})
is irreducible then
\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathds{C}}}(E_{C}(\mathcal{F}))=0
or
1
, where
C
be any open cell in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n+1}}
.

(2)

Suppose
\mathcal{F}\in\mathscr{P}er(V_{{\mathds{C}}}^{n+1},\mathcal{S}^{n+1})
is irreducible and
\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathds{C}}}(E_{C}(\mathcal{F}))=0
where
C
be any open cell in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n+1}}
. Then there exists
\mathcal{G}\in\mathscr{P}er(V_{{\mathds{C}}}^{n},\mathcal{S}^{n})
and
L_{i,j}^{n+1}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A}_{n+1}}
such that

\Lambda_{L_{i,j}^{n+1}}(\mathcal{G})=\mathcal{F}.

Proof.

(1)

For any two open cell
C
and
C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}
, there exist

\phi_{CC^{\prime}}:E_{C}(\mathcal{F})\longrightarrow E_{C^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}),

which is an isomorphism by invertibility. Thus if
E_{\lambda}\subset E_{C}(\mathcal{F})
be a proper subspace, one necessarily has a proper subspace
\phi_{CC^{\prime}}(E_{\lambda})\subset E_{C^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})
. Since,
by monotonicity, for any faces
D
,
C
satisfying
D\leq C
, one has a injective map
\delta_{DC}:E_{C}(\mathcal{F})\longrightarrow E_{D}(\mathcal{F})
. Hence one can generate a proper subrepresentation of
\left(E_{C}(\mathcal{F}),\delta_{C^{\prime}C},\gamma_{CC^{\prime}}\right)_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}}
by taking translates of
E_{\lambda}
under various maps
\delta_{\star,\star}
.

Thus if
\left(E_{C}(\mathcal{F}),\delta_{C^{\prime}C},\gamma_{CC^{\prime}}\right)_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}}
is irreducible then
E_{C}(\mathcal{F})
is irreducible if and only if
\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathds{C}}}(E_{C}(\mathcal{F}))=0
or
1
.

(2)

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is irreducible and there exist two distinct codimension one cell
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
and two distinct hyperplanes
H_{1}
and
H_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{n+1}
such that for all
i

\displaystyle E_{D_{i}}(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\neq 0

\displaystyle D_{i}\subset

\displaystyle H_{i}

There exist an open cell
C
in
\mathcal{A}_{n+1}
such that
(D_{1},C,D_{2})
form a collinear triple. Indeed, any line joining
d_{1}\in D_{1}
and
d_{2}\in D_{2}
must intersect an open cell, Since
D_{i}
is a connected component of
H_{i}\setminus\cup_{H_{j}(\neq H_{i})\in\mathcal{A}_{n+1}}\left(H_{j}\cap H_{k}\right)
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Then by transitivity

\phi_{D_{1}D_{2}}=\phi_{C_{2}D_{2}}\phi_{D_{1}C_{2}}=0

Similarly there exist
L\subset H_{1}\cap H_{2}
, a cell of codimension one in
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
such that
(D_{1},L,D_{2})
forms a collinear triple. Then
\phi_{D_{1}D_{2}}=\gamma_{LD_{2}}\delta_{D_{1}L}=0
.
Hence

\operatorname{Im}(\delta_{D_{1}L})\subset\operatorname{Ker}(\gamma_{LD_{2}}).

In other words
E_{L}(\mathcal{F})
contains
\operatorname{Im}(\delta_{D_{1}L})
and
\operatorname{Im}(\delta_{D_{2}L})
as direct summand. Similarly it happen in all other cells contained in
H_{1}\cap H_{2}
. Hence
E_{C}(\mathcal{F})
can not be irreducible, which a contradiction.

∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\in\mathscr{P}er(V_{{\mathds{C}}}^{2},\mathcal{S}^{2})
be be a perverse sheaves on the hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{2}
. If
\mathcal{F}
is irreducible and
\operatorname{dim}E_{C}(\mathcal{F})=1
, where
C
be an open cells, then
\operatorname{dim}E_{D}(\mathcal{F})\leq 2
, for any cell
D
of dimension one.

Proof.

Let
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
be two open cell and
D
be a cell of dimension one in
\mathcal{A}_{2}
satisfying

C_{1}\geq D\leq C_{2}.

Hence by monotonicity,

E_{C_{1}}(\mathcal{F})\hookrightarrow E_{D}(\mathcal{F})\text{ and }E_{C_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\hookrightarrow E_{D}(\mathcal{F}).

Since
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are opposed by
D_{1}
, by invertibility,

\phi_{DC_{2}}\circ\phi_{C_{1}D}:E_{C_{1}}(\mathcal{F})\cong E_{C_{2}}(\mathcal{F})

is an isomorphism. Moreover
\phi_{C_{1}O}:E_{C_{1}}(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow E_{O}(\mathcal{F})
can be factorised by
\phi_{C_{1}O}=\phi_{DO}\phi_{C_{1}D}
. Similarly
\phi_{C_{2}O}=\phi_{DO}\phi_{C_{2}D}
.
Moreover, for any open cells
C
and
C^{\prime}
, one necessarily has
E_{C}(\mathcal{F})\cong E_{C^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})
. Observe that any cell
D
of dimension one can be dominated by exactly two open cells.
Therefore image of
\phi_{C_{1}D}
and image of
\phi_{C_{2}D}
will generate a subspace of
E_{D}(\mathcal{F})
of dimension at most two. Since
\mathcal{F}
is irreducible,
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{F})
is irreducible (Theorem
2.5
). Hence image of
\phi_{C_{2}D}
and
\phi_{C_{2}D}
would generate
E_{D}(\mathcal{F})
.

∎

Remark 3.10
.

Since the classification of irreducible perverse sheaves of Coxeter arrangement of type
\mathcal{A}_{2}
was given by Rowley under the assumption that
\operatorname{dim}(E_{D}(\mathcal{F})\leq 2
for any cell
D
of dimension one in
\mathcal{A}_{2}
. Using the above corollary we obtain that this classification is a complete classification of irreducible perverse sheaves in
\mathcal{A}_{2}
.

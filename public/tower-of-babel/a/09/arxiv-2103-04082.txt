Bifurcation from Infinity of the Schrödinger Equation via Invariant Manifolds
Chunqiu Li, Jintao Wang

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the bifurcation from infinity of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation − Δ ​ u + V ⁡ ( x ) ​ u = λ ​ u + f ⁡ ( x , u ) , x ∈ ℝ N . -\Delta u+V(x)u=\lambda u+f(x,u),\hskip 11.38092ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{N}. We treat this problem in the framework of dynamical systems by considering the corresponding parabolic equation on unbounded domains. Firstly, we establish a global invariant manifold for the parabolic equation on ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} . Then, we restrict the parabolic equation to this invariant manifold, which generates a system of finite dimension. Finally, we use the Conley index theory and the shape theory of attractors to establish some new results on bifurcations from infinity and multiplicity of solutions of the Schrödinger equation under an appropriate Landesman-Lazer type condition. Keywords : Conley index; Bifurcation from infinity; Invariant manifold; Schrödinger equation; Parabolic equation MSC2010 : 37B30, 35B32, 35K55, 58J55

Introduction

In this paper we are concerned with the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation

-\Delta u+V(x)u=\lambda u+f(x,u),\hskip 11.38092ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.1)

where the potential
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
N\geqslant 1
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is the bifurcation parameter, and
f
is bounded and satisfies the following Landesman-Lazer type condition

\liminf\limits_{s\rightarrow+\infty}f(x,s)\geqslant\overline{f}>0,\hskip 22.76228pt\limsup\limits_{s\rightarrow-\infty}f(x,s)\leqslant-\underline{f}<0

(1.2)

uniformly with respect to
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
(where
\overline{f}
and
\underline{f}
are independent of
x
). We are basically interested in the bifurcation from infinity and multiplicity of solutions of the equation.

The bifurcation and multiplicity of elliptic equations near resonance have aroused much interest in the past decades. This topic can be traced back to the earlier work of Mawhin and Schmitt
[
19
]
, where the authors studied the problem of elliptic equations of the following form

-\Delta u=\lambda u+f(x,u)

(1.3)

on a bounded domain, where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
f
satisfies an appropriate Landesman-Lazer type condition. It was shown that if
\mu_{k}
is an eigenvalue of odd multiplicity, then (
1.3
) has at least two distinct solutions for
\lambda
on one side of
\mu_{k}
but close to
\mu_{k}
, and at least one solution for
\lambda
on the other side. Later Schmitt and Wang
[
30
]
extended this result to the case when
\lambda

crosses each eigenvalue
\mu_{k}
on
bifurcations from infinity for operator equations to cover the case of even multiplicity. Recently, Li, Li and Zhang
[
15
]
presented a dynamical argument for Schmitt and Wang’s result on (
1.3
). The interested reader is referred to
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
17
,
20
,
22
,
23
,
29
,
32
]
, etc., on bifurcations from infinity and multiplicity for elliptic equations under various boundary conditions.

The bifurcation from infinity of the equation (
1.1
) on unbounded domains is also widely investigated; see
[
3
,
14
,
10
,
11
,
27
,
28
]
and the references
therein. However, comparing to the case of bounded domains, the problem on unbounded domains is more difficult. The main reason is that the spectrum of the operator
A:=-\Delta+V(x)
is not discrete in general, and may be quite complicated, which depends on the potential. Under some additional conditions on the potential
V
and
f
, Kryszewski and Szulkin
[
14
]
, using the degree theory and a variational approach, considered the asymptotic linear Schrödinger equation (
1.1
). They showed that if
\lambda_{0}
is an isolated eigenvalue for the linearization at infinity, then there exists a sequence
(u_{n},\lambda_{n})
of solutions of (
1.1
) such that

\|u_{n}\|\rightarrow\infty\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{and \hskip 11.38092pt$\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\lambda_{0}$,\hskip 22.76228ptas \hskip 11.38092pt$n\rightarrow\infty$,}

which extends the results of Stuart
[
27
]
.
Later, by using degree theory and Conley index theory, Ćwiszewski and Kryszewski
[
3
]
studied the bifurcation from infinity of elliptic equations on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and proved that if the bifurcation parameter is an eigenvalue of the Hamiltonian, then the bifurcation from infinity also occurs under some suitable conditions. Motivated by these works mentioned above, in this paper we further study the bifurcation from infinity and multiplicity of the Schröding equation (
1.1
) near an isolated eigenvalue
\lambda_{*}
of the operator
A=\Delta+V(x)
. We will show that under some appropriate conditions on
f
and the potential
V
, there exists
\theta>0
such that for each
\lambda\in[\lambda_{*}-\theta,\lambda_{*})
, (
1.1
) has at least three distinct solutions
e_{\lambda}^{1},e_{\lambda}^{2}
and
e_{\lambda}^{3}
with

\|e_{\lambda}^{i}\|\rightarrow\infty,\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{as}\hskip 11.38092pt\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{*}^{-},\hskip 11.38092pti=1,2,

(1.4)

whereas
e_{\lambda}^{3}
remains bounded on
[\lambda_{*}-\theta,\lambda_{*})
. The “dual” version of our results also holds true if, instead of (
1.2
), we assume that

\limsup\limits_{s\rightarrow+\infty}f(x,s)\leqslant-\overline{f}<0,\hskip 22.76228pt\liminf\limits_{s\rightarrow-\infty}f(x,s)\geqslant\underline{f}>0

(1.5)

uniformly with respect to
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Our method in this work is arranged as follows. Instead of transforming (
1.1
) into an operator equation and using the topological degree or variational methods, we regard the problem as the stationary one of the following parabolic equation

u_{t}-\Delta u+V(x)u=\lambda u+f(x,u),\hskip 11.38092ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.6)

Specifically, let
\Phi_{\lambda}
be the semiflow generated by (
1.6
) and

\lambda_{*}
be an isolated eigenvalue of the operator
A=-\Delta+V(x)
. Assume that the Lipschitz constant
L_{f}
of
f
satisfies

F_{\mu}L_{f}<1

for
\mu\in(\frac{1}{4}\beta,\frac{3}{4}\beta)
fixed, where
F_{\mu}
and
\beta>0
are given by (
4.4
) and (
3.2
), respectively.
We first present an existence result on a global invariant manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
of the equation (
1.6
) for
\lambda
near
\lambda_{*}
. Then the equation (
1.6
) can be restricted to this invariant manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
, and therefore it is reduced to a system of finite dimension. This allows us to apply the Conley index theory to the reduced system. By virtue of the shape theory of attractors
[
13
]
, it can be shown that there exists
\theta>0
such that for each
\lambda\in[\lambda_{*}-\theta,\lambda_{*})
, the system (
1.6
) can bifurcate from infinity a compact invariant set
{\mathcal{S}}_{\lambda}^{\infty}
, which takes the shape of a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
, where
m
denotes the algebraic multiplicity of
\lambda_{*}
. Based on this fact, one can further verify that
{\mathcal{S}}_{\lambda}^{\infty}
contains at least two distinct equilibria
e_{\lambda}^{1}
and
e_{\lambda}^{2}
of
\Phi_{\lambda}
, which are actually solutions of (
1.1
) satisfying (
1.4
).

Besides, using the Landesman-Lazer type condition (
1.2
), we show a fundamental result on
f
(see Lemma
4.1
), which plays a crucial role in establishing our main results for (
1.1
). Then we prove that (
1.1
) has a solution
e_{\lambda}^{3}
for
\lambda
belonging to a two-sided neighborhood
\Lambda
of
\lambda_{*}
,
which remains bounded on
\Lambda
.
We remark that this lemma extends the result established in
[
15
,
16
]
(see
[
15
, Lemma 5.2]
or
[
16
, Lemma 6.7]
) to the case of unbounded domains, and can be seen as a nontrivial extension. This is because it is very hard to verify this result by directly using the techniques given in
[
15
,
16
]
. To avoid the difficulty caused by unbounded domains, we establish this lemma on some finite dimensional subspace of the phase space, which is inspired by the finite-dimension property of the invariant manifold.

It is worth mentioning that we treat the problem (
1.1
) in the framework of dynamical systems, and deal with it via the approach of a pure dynamical nature, which is different from those in the literature. Moreover, the method that we restrict the system (
1.6
) to the global invariant manifold can help us avoid the verification of the compactness for the semiflow
\Phi_{\lambda}
. In general, it is not easy to verify that the semiflow is asymptotically compact for a parabolic equation on unbounded domains.

This work is organized as follows. Section 2 is concerned with some
preliminaries. In Section 3 we first establish a global invariant manifold of the equation (
1.6
) on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and then we reduce (
1.6
) to this invariant manifold. In Section 4, we present a detailed discussion on dynamical behaviors of the reduced system, and establish our main results on bifurcations from infinity and multiplicity of solutions of the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
).

2
Preliminaries

In this section we first make some preliminaries.

2.1
Local semiflows

Let
X
be a complete metric space.

A
local semiflow

\Phi
on
X
is a continuous mapping from an
open set
\mathcal{D}(\Phi)\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}\times X
to
X
that
satisfies the following properties

(1)

For every
x\in X
, there is
0<T_{x}\leqslant\infty
, such that

(t,x)\in\mathcal{D}(\Phi)\Longleftrightarrow t\in[0,T_{x});

(2)

\Phi(0,\cdot)={\rm id}_{X}
, and

\Phi(t+s,x)=\Phi(t,\Phi(s,x))

for any
x\in X
and
t,s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
t+s\leqslant T_{x}
.

Here, the number
T_{x}
is called the
escape time
of
\Phi(t,x)
. For simplicity, we rewrite
\Phi(t,x)
as
\Phi(t)x
.

Given an interval
J\subset\mathbb{R}
. A continuous mapping
\gamma:J\rightarrow X
is called a
trajectory
(or
solution
) of
\Phi
on
J

if

\gamma(t)=\Phi(t-s)\gamma(s),\hskip 11.38092pt\forall t,s\in J,\,t\geqslant s.

We call a trajectory
\gamma
on
\mathbb{R}
a
full trajectory
. The orbit of a full trajectory
\gamma
is the set

{\rm orb}(\gamma)=\{\gamma(t):t\in\mathbb{R}\},

which is simply called
a full orbit
.

The
\omega
-limit set

\omega(\gamma)
and
\omega^{*}
-limit set

\omega^{*}(\gamma)
of a full trajectory
\gamma
are defined, respectively, by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\omega(\gamma)=\{y\in X:\,\,\,\,\mbox{there exists }t_{n}\rightarrow\infty\mbox{ such that }\gamma(t_{n})\rightarrow y\},\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{ll}\omega^{*}(\gamma)=\{y\in X:\,\,\,\,\mbox{there exists }t_{n}\rightarrow-\infty\mbox{ such that }\gamma(t_{n})\rightarrow y\}.\end{array}

Let
N
be a subset of
X
. We say that
\Phi

does not explode
in
N
, if
\Phi([0,T_{x}))x\subset N
implies that
T_{x}=\infty
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
24
]

A set
N\subset X
is said to be admissible, if for every sequences
x_{n}\in N
and
t_{n}\rightarrow\infty
satisfying
\Phi([0,t_{n}])x_{n}\subset N
for
all
n
, then the sequence
\Phi(t_{n})x_{n}
has a convergent subsequence.

A set
N
is said to be strongly admissible, if it is admissible and
moreover,
\Phi
does not explode in
N
.

Definition 2.2
.

\Phi
is said to be asymptotically compact on
X
, if every bounded
set
B\subset X
is strongly admissible.

A set
S\subset X
is said to be
positively invariant
(resp.,
invariant
), if
\Phi(t)S\subset S
(resp.,
\Phi(t)S=S
) for any
t\geqslant 0
.

A compact invariant set
{\mathcal{A}}\subset X
is called an
attractor
of
\Phi
, if it attracts a neighborhood
U
of itself, that is,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}{\rm d}_{H}\left(\Phi(t)U,{\mathcal{A}}\right)=0.

2.2
Sectorial operators and spectral sets

Let
X
be a Banach space. A closed densely defined linear operator
A:D(A)\subset X\rightarrow X
is called a
sectorial operator
, if there are real numbers
\phi\in(0,\pi/2),a\in\mathbb{R}
and
M\geqslant 1
such that the sector

S_{a,\phi}=\{\lambda:\phi\leqslant|{\rm arg}(\lambda-a)|\leqslant\pi,\hskip 11.38092pt\lambda\neq a\}

is contained in the resolvent set of
A,
and

\|(\lambda I-A)^{-1}\|\leqslant M/|\lambda-a|

for each
\lambda\in S_{a,\phi}
, where
I
denotes the identity on
X.

Let
A
be a sectorial operator on
X
. By
\sigma(A)
we denote the spectrum of
A.
If
\min_{z\in\sigma(A)}{\rm Re}\,z>0,
then
one can define the fractional powers of
A
as follows: for each
\alpha>0,

A^{-\alpha}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{\alpha-1}{\rm e}^{-At}{\rm d}t,

where
\begin{array}[]{l}\Gamma(s)=\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{s-1}{\rm e}^{-t}{\rm d}t\end{array}
is the Gamma function. Let
A^{\alpha}
be the inverse of
A^{-\alpha}
with
D(A^{\alpha})=R(A^{-\alpha}).

Let
\alpha\geqslant 0
. Denote
X^{\alpha}=D(A_{1}^{\alpha})
and equip
X^{\alpha}
with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}
defined by

\|u\|_{\alpha}=\|A^{\alpha}_{1}u\|,\hskip 11.38092ptu\in X^{\alpha},

where
A_{1}=A+aI
, and
a
is a real number such that
\min_{z\in\sigma(A_{1})}{\rm Re}z>0
.
It is easy to see that
X^{\alpha}
is a Banach space, which is called the
fractional power of
X
. For more notions and results on sectorial operators, the interested reader is referred to Henry
[
12
, Chapter I]
for details.

Now let us briefly recall some notions on spectral sets; see
[
14
]
and
[
26
]
, etc., for details.

Let
X,Y
be (real) Banach spaces. A closed densely defined linear operator
L:D(L)\subset X\rightarrow Y
is called a
Fredholm operator
if the range
R(L)
is closed and
\dim N(L)<\infty,{\rm codim}\,R(L)<\infty,
where
N(L)
is the kernel of
L
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
E
be a real Hilbert space and
L:D(L)\subset E\rightarrow E
be a self-adjoint linear operator. The essential spectrum
\sigma_{e}(L)
of
L
is defined by the set

\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:L-\lambda I\,\,\mbox{ is not a Fredholm operator}\},

Remark 2.1
.

One may immediately deduce from the definition that
\sigma_{e}(L)\subset\sigma(L)
and that
\sigma(L)\setminus\sigma_{e}(L)
consists of isolated eigenvalues of finite multiplicity; see also
[
14
]
.

2.3
Conley index

For the readers’ convenience, we finally recall the definition of Conley index; see

[
7
,
18
]
and
[
24
]
, etc., for details.

Let
\Phi
be a local semiflow on
X
. From now on we will always assume
\Phi
is asymptotically compact.

A compact invariant set
S
is said to be
isolated
, if
there exists a neighborhood
N
of
S
such that
S

is the maximal compact invariant set of
\Phi
in
\overline{N}
.
Correspondingly, the set
N
is called an

isolating neighborhood
of
S
.

Let
B
be a bounded closed subset of
X
.
x\in\partial B
is called a
strict ingress
(resp.,
strict egress
,
bounce-off
) point of
B
, if for each trajectory
\gamma:[-\tau,s]\rightarrow X
with
\gamma(0)=x
, where
\tau\geqslant 0
,
s>0
, the following conditions are satisfied:

(1) there exists
0<\varepsilon<s
such that

\gamma(t)\in\mbox{int}B\,\,\,(\mbox{resp., }\,\gamma(t)\not\in B,\,\,\,\mbox{resp., }\,\gamma(t)\not\in B),\hskip 22.76228pt\forall\,t\in(0,\varepsilon);

(2) if
\tau>0
, then there is a number
\delta\in(0,\tau)
such that

\gamma(t)\not\in B\,\,\,(\mbox{resp., }\,\gamma(t)\in\mbox{int}B,\,\,\,\mbox{resp., }\,\gamma(t)\not\in B),\hskip 22.76228pt\forall\,t\in(-\delta,0).

By
B^{i}
(resp.,
B^{e}
,
B^{b}
), we denote the set of all strict ingress (resp., strict egress, bounce-off) points of the closed set
B
, and write
B^{-}=B^{e}\cup B^{b}
.

A set
B\subset X
is called an
isolating block

[
24
]
if
B^{-}
is closed and
\partial B=B^{i}\cup B^{-}.

Let
N,E
be two closed subsets of
X
.
E
is called an
exit
set
of
N
, if the following properties hold:

(1)

E
is
N-positively invariant
, that is, for each
x\in E
and
t\geqslant 0
,

\Phi([0,t])x\subset N\Longrightarrow\Phi([0,t])x\subset E;

(2)

for every
x\in N,
if
\Phi(t_{1})x\not\in N
for some

t_{1}>0
, then there is
t_{0}\in[0,t_{1}]
such that
\Phi(t_{0})\in E
.

Let
S
be a compact isolated invariant set of
\Phi
. A pair of bounded closed
subsets
(N,E)
is said to be an
index pair
of
S
, if (1)
N\setminus E
is an isolating neighborhood of

S;
(2)
E
is an exit set of
N
.

By
[
24
]
, one can deduce that if
B
is a bounded isolating block, then
(B,B^{-})
is an index pair of the maximal compact invariant set
S
in
B
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(N,E)
be an index pair of
S
. The homotopy Conley index
of
S
is defined to be the homotopy type
[(N/E,[E])]
of the
pointed space
(N/E,[E])
, denoted by
h(\Phi,S).

3
Invariant manifolds of nonlinear evolution equations

In this section, we establish an existence result on global invariant manifolds of the following nonlinear evolution equation

u_{t}+Au=\lambda u+f(u)

(3.1)

on a Banach space
X
, where
A
is a sectorial operator on
X
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, and
f
is a globally Lipschitz mapping from
X^{\alpha}
to
X
for some
0\leqslant\alpha<1
.

Let
\|\cdot\|
and
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}
denote the norms of
X
and
X^{\alpha}
, respectively.

3.1
Prelimiaries

Denote
\sigma(A)
the spectrum of
A
and

{\rm Re}\,\sigma(A):=\{{\rm Re}\,z:z\in\sigma(A)\}.

Suppose
\lambda_{0}
is an isolated eigenvalue of
A
with finite multiplicity, and that the spectrum
\sigma(A)
has a decomposition
\sigma(A)=\sigma^{1}\cup\sigma^{2}\cup\sigma^{3}
with

\sigma^{1}=\sigma(A)\cap\{{\rm Re}\lambda\leqslant\beta_{1}\},\hskip 11.38092pt\sigma^{2}=\{\lambda_{0}\},\hskip 11.38092pt\sigma^{3}=\sigma(A)\cap\{{\rm Re}\lambda\geqslant\beta_{2}\},

where
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
are real numbers. It can be easily seen that

{\rm Re}\,\sigma(A)\cap(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})=\{{\rm Re}\lambda_{0}\}.

Correspondingly, the space
X
can be decomposed as

X=X^{1}\oplus X^{2}\oplus X^{3},

and the space
X^{2}
is finite dimensional. In addition, we assume the space
X^{1}
is also finite dimensional. Denote

P_{i}:X\rightarrow X^{i},\,i\in\{1,2,3\}

the projection from
X
to
X_{i}
.

Pick a positive number
\beta
with

\beta<\min\{{\rm Re}\lambda_{0}-\beta_{1},\beta_{2}-{\rm Re}\lambda_{0}\},

(3.2)

and write
J=({\rm Re}\lambda_{0}-\frac{1}{4}\beta,{\rm Re}\lambda_{0}+\frac{1}{4}\beta).
Let
A_{\lambda}=A-\lambda I,\lambda\in J
and denote
A^{i}=A_{\lambda}|_{X^{i}}
. Then by the basic knowledge on sectorial operators (see e.g.,
[
12
, Theorems 1.5.3, 1.5.4]
), one deduces that there exists
M>0
(depending on
A
) such that for each
\alpha\in[0,1)
,

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{\alpha}{{\rm e}}^{-A^{1}t}\|\leqslant M{\rm e}^{\frac{3}{4}\beta t},\hskip 11.38092pt\|{\rm e}^{-A^{1}t}\|\leqslant M{\rm e}^{\frac{3}{4}\beta t},\hskip 22.76228ptt\leqslant 0,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{\alpha}{\rm e}^{-A^{2}t}\|\leqslant M{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{4}\beta|t|},\hskip 11.38092pt\|{\rm e}^{-A^{2}t}\|\leqslant M{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{4}\beta|t|},\hskip 22.76228ptt\in\mathbb{R},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{\alpha}{\rm e}^{-A^{3}t}P_{3}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\|\leqslant M{\rm e}^{-\frac{3}{4}\beta t},\hskip 11.38092pt\|\Lambda^{\alpha}{\rm e}^{-A^{3}t}\|\leqslant Mt^{-\alpha}{\rm e}^{-\frac{3}{4}\beta t},\hskip 22.76228ptt>0,

(3.5)

where
\Lambda=A+aI
, and
a
is a positive number such that
{\rm Re}\,\sigma(\Lambda)>0
. The first estimates in (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) hold true as the spaces
X^{1}
and
X^{2}
are finite dimensional.

For a given
\mu\geqslant 0
, we define a space by

{\mathcal{X}}_{\mu}=\big\{u\in{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{R};X^{\alpha}):\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{-\mu|t|}\|u(t)\|_{\alpha}<\infty\big\},

(3.6)

which is equipped with the following norm

\|u\|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\mu}}=\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{-\mu|t|}\|u(t)\|_{\alpha}<\infty.

Then it can be easily seen that
{\mathcal{X}}_{\mu}
is a Banach space.

Now we rewrite the equation (
3.1
) as

u_{t}+A_{\lambda}u=f(u).

(3.7)

It is well-known that the Cauchy problem of (
3.7
) is well-posed in
X^{\alpha}
; see e.g.,
[
12
, Theorem 3.3.3]
. Specifically, for each
u_{0}\in X^{\alpha},
there exist
T>0
and a (unique) continuous function
u:[0,T)\rightarrow X^{\alpha}
with
u(0)=u_{0}
such that the equation (
3.7
) is satisfied on
(0,T)
.

Denote
\Phi_{\lambda}
the semiflow generated by the equation (
3.7
).

Let us start with a fundamental result on the solution of (
3.7
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mu\in(\frac{1}{4}\beta,\frac{3}{4}\beta)
. Assume
u\in{\mathcal{X}}_{\mu}.
Then
u
is a full solution of (
3.7
) if and only if
u
satisfies

\displaystyle u(t)=

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-A^{2}t}P_{2}u(0)+\!\int^{t}_{0}{\rm e}^{-A^{2}(t-s)}P_{2}f(u(s)){\rm d}s+\!\int^{t}_{-\infty}{\rm e}^{-A^{3}(t-s)}P_{3}f(u(s)){\rm d}s

\displaystyle-\int^{\infty}_{t}{\rm e}^{-A^{1}(t-s)}P_{1}f(u(s)){\rm d}s.

(3.8)

Proof.

Assume
u\in{\mathcal{X}}_{\mu}
. Let
u
be a full solution of (
3.7
). Then

u(t)=u^{1}(t)+u^{2}(t)+u^{3}(t),\hskip 11.38092ptt\in\mathbb{R},

(3.9)

where
u^{i}(t)=P_{i}u(t),i\in\{1,2,3\}.
It is easy to see that
u^{i}(t)
can be expressed, respectively, as

\displaystyle u^{2}(t)={\rm e}^{-A^{2}t}u^{2}(0)+\int^{t}_{0}{\rm e}^{-A^{2}(t-s)}P_{2}f(u(s)){\rm d}s,\hskip 11.38092ptt\in\mathbb{R},

(3.10)

\displaystyle u^{1}(t)={\rm e}^{-A^{1}(t-t_{0})}u^{1}(t_{0})+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}{\rm e}^{-A^{1}(t-s)}P_{1}f(u(s)){\rm d}s,\hskip 11.38092ptt\leqslant t_{0},

(3.11)

\displaystyle M\,{\rm e}^{-\frac{3}{4}\beta t}{\rm e}^{(\frac{3}{4}\beta-\mu)t_{0}}({\rm e}^{\mu t_{0}}\|u(t_{0})\|_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\,M{\rm e}^{-\frac{3}{4}\beta t}{\rm e}^{(\frac{3}{4}\beta-\mu)t_{0}}\|u\|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\mu}}\rightarrow 0\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{as}\hskip 11.38092ptt_{0}\rightarrow-\infty.

On the other hand, if (

∎

4

In this section we study the bifurcation from infinity of the Schrödinger equation (

) for
\lambda=\lambda_{n}

\|u_{n}\|_{\infty}\rightarrow\infty,\hskip 11.38092ptn\rightarrow\infty.

(4.25)

On the other hand, we infer from Lemma

that there are

Since

and

h(\phi_{\lambda},K_{\infty}(\phi_{\lambda}))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Sigma^{0},\hskip 22.76228pt\lambda\in(\lambda_{*}-\frac{\beta}{4},\lambda_{*});\\[4.30554pt]

By virtue of Lemma

Theorem 4.3
.

Let the assumptions
(
\mathbf{A1}
)-(
\mathbf{A3}
)
hold. Assume the Lipschitz constant
L_{f}
of
f
satisfies
(
F
)
. Then there exists
\theta_{1}\in(0,\frac{1}{4}\beta)
such that for each
\lambda\in\Lambda_{1}:=[\lambda_{*}-\theta_{1},\lambda_{*})
, the system
\Phi_{\lambda}
has a compact invariant set
{\mathcal{S}}^{\infty}_{\lambda}
, which takes the shape of
(m-1)
-dimensional topological sphere
\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
, and

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{*}^{-}}\min\{\|w\|:w\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\infty}_{\lambda}\}=\infty.

(4.28)

Moreover, there exists a two-sided neighborhood
\Lambda
of
\lambda_{*}
such that for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
\Phi_{\lambda}
has a compact invariant set
{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}
, which remains bounded on
\Lambda
.

Proof.

Let
\theta_{1}
be given in Lemma
4.2
. By virtue of Lemma
4.2
(3), we see that for each
\lambda\in\Lambda_{1}
, the semiflow
\phi_{\lambda}
generated by (
4.8
) has a positively invariant set
{\rm B}_{\lambda}:={\rm B}_{2}[r_{\lambda},R_{\lambda}]
. Note that
Y_{2}
is finite dimensional. Then one can conclude from the attractor theory (see e.g.,
[
6
,
31
]
) that the set

{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}^{\infty}=\bigcap_{s\geqslant 0}\overline{\bigcup_{t\geqslant s}\phi_{\lambda}(t){\rm B}_{\lambda}}

is a global attractor of the semiflow
\phi_{\lambda}
restricted on
{\rm B}_{\lambda}
.

By the basic knowledge on algebraic topology, it can be easily seen that
{\rm B}_{\lambda}
has the homotopy type of an
(m-1)
-dimensional topological sphere. Thus
{\rm B}_{\lambda}
enjoys the same shape of
\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
. Thanks to the shape theory of attractors (see
[
13
, Theorem 3.6]
or
[
25
,
34
]
), we conclude that
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}^{\infty}\subset{\rm B}_{\lambda}
has the shape of
\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
.

Set

{\mathcal{S}}_{\lambda}^{\infty}=\{w+\xi_{\lambda}(w):w\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}^{\infty}\}.

Then
{\mathcal{S}}_{\lambda}^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}
is a compact invariant set of the system
\Phi_{\lambda}
generated by (
4.2
)-(
4.3
), which takes the shape of an
(m-1)
-dimensional sphere. By the construction of
{\rm B}_{\lambda}
, we see that
r_{\lambda},R_{\lambda}\rightarrow\infty
as
\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{*}^{-}
, from which one can conclude that (
4.28
) holds true.

Let
\Lambda
be obtained in Theorem
4.2
. Then we infer from the proof of Theorem
4.2

\lambda\in\Lambda

\phi_{\lambda}
has a compact invariant set
K_{\lambda}
, which remains bounded on
\Lambda
.
Similarly, set

Clearly,

is the desired compact invariant set of
\Phi_{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in\Lambda

As a consequence of Theorem
4.3
, we can obtain our main results on bifurcations from infinity and multiplicity of the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
).

Theorem 4.4
.

Let the assumptions
(
\mathbf{A1}
)
-
(
\mathbf{A3}
)
hold and
\lambda_{*}\in\sigma_{p}
. Assume the Lipschitz constant
L_{f}
of
f
satisfies
(
F
)
. Then there exists
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{4}\beta)
such that for each
\lambda\in\Lambda_{2}:=[\lambda_{*}-\theta,\lambda_{*}),

the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) has at least three distinct nontrival solutions
e_{\lambda}^{1},e_{\lambda}^{2},e^{3}_{\lambda}
with

\|e^{i}_{\lambda}\|\rightarrow\infty,\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{as \,\,$\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{*}^{-}$,\hskip 11.38092pt$i=1,2$,}

whereas
e_{\lambda}^{3}
remains bounded on
\Lambda_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\theta_{1}
and
\Lambda
be given in Theorem
4.3
. Pick a positive number
\theta
such that
\theta<\min\{\theta_{1},\delta\}
, where
\delta>0
is chosen in the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
By virtue of Theorem
4.3
, we deduce that for each
\lambda\in[\lambda_{*}-\theta,\lambda_{*})
, the system
\Phi_{\lambda}
has a compact invariant set
{\mathcal{S}}^{\infty}_{\lambda}
.
In what follows we show that
{\mathcal{S}}^{\infty}_{\lambda}
has at least two distinct nontrivial stationary solutions
e^{1}_{\lambda}
and
e^{2}_{\lambda}
, which are actually the solutions of (
1.1
).

Observing that
{\mathcal{S}}^{\infty}_{\lambda}
has the shape of
\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
and
m\geqslant 1
, we deduce that
{\mathcal{S}}^{\infty}_{\lambda}
has at least two distinct points
u
and
v
. If
u
and
v
are equilibria, then the result holds true. Now we suppose that
u
is not an equilibrium. Then there exists a full solution
\gamma=\gamma(t)
of
\Phi_{\lambda}
in
{\mathcal{S}}_{\lambda}^{\infty}
such that
\gamma(0)=u
. Since
\Phi_{\lambda}
is a gradient system, we conclude that

\omega(\gamma)\cap\omega^{*}(\gamma)=\emptyset.

Noticing that
\omega(\gamma)
and
\omega^{*}(\gamma)
consist of equilibrium points, one can see that
\Phi_{\lambda}
has two distinct equilibria in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\infty}_{\lambda}
. Recalling that
{\mathcal{S}}^{\infty}_{\lambda}
satisfies (
4.28
), we conclude that the equation (
1.1
) has two distinct nontrivial solutions
e^{1}_{\lambda}
and
e^{2}_{\lambda}
with

\|e^{i}_{\lambda}\|\rightarrow\infty,\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{as \,$\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{*}^{-}$,\hskip 11.38092pt$i=1,2$.}

Theorem
4.3
also asserts that for each
\lambda\in\Lambda=[\lambda_{*}-\delta,\lambda_{*}+\delta]
, the system
\Phi_{\lambda}
has a compact invariant set
{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}
, which remains bounded on
\Lambda
. Thus
\Phi_{\lambda}
has at least one equilibrium
e_{\lambda}^{3}
in
{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}
. Therefore
e_{\lambda}^{3}
is the solution of the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) fulfilling the requirements of the theorem for
\lambda\in\Lambda_{2}
. The proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

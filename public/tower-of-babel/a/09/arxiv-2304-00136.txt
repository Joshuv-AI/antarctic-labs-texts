Stabilisation, scanning and handle cancellation


Abstract
In this note we describe a family of arguments that link the homotopy-type of a) the diffeomorphism group of the disc D n D^{n} , b) the space of co-dimension one embedded spheres in S n S^{n} and c) the homotopy-type of the space of co-dimension two trivial knots in S n S^{n} . We also describe some natural extensions to these arguments. We begin with Cerf’s ‘upgraded’ proof of Smale’s theorem, that the diffeomorphism group of S 2 S^{2} has the homotopy-type of the isometry group. This entails a canceling-handle construction, related to the ‘scanning’ maps of spaces of embeddings [ 4 ] Emb ⁡ ( D n − 1 , S 1 × D n − 1 ) → Ω j ​ Emb ​ ( D n − 1 − j , S 1 × D n − 1 ) {\mathrm{Emb}}(D^{n-1},S^{1}\times D^{n-1})\to\Omega^{j}{\mathrm{Emb}}(D^{n-1-j},S^{1}\times D^{n-1}) . We further give a Bott-style variation on Cerf’s construction, and a related Embedding Calculus framework for these constructions. We use these arguments to prove that the monoid of Schönflies spheres π 0 ​ Emb ​ ( S n − 1 , S n ) \pi_{0}{\mathrm{Emb}}(S^{n-1},S^{n}) is a group with respect to the connect-sum operation, for all n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 . This last result is perhaps only interesting when n = 4 n=4 , as when n ≠ 4 n\neq 4 it follows from the resolution of the various generalized Schönflies problems.

In Cerf’s landmark paper
[

, somewhat overlooked is a novel proof of Smale’s theorem, that the group of diffeomorphisms

{\mathrm{Diff}}(D^{2})\to\Omega^{2}GL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
has a left homotopy-inverse. Cerf states his theorem

of
{\mathrm{Diff}}(D^{2})
inject into the homotopy groups of
\Omega^{2}GL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. Since the latter homotopy groups are trivial, and
diffeomorphism groups of compact manifolds have the homotopy-type of countable CW-complexes
[
31
]

{\mathrm{Diff}}(D^{2})

is contractible via the Whitehead Theorem. In this paper we use the notation that if

is a manifold with boundary,
{\mathrm{Diff}}(N)
denotes the group of
diffeomorphisms of
N
that restrict to the identity on
\partial N
. We will use the same conventions for embedding spaces, i.e.

{\mathrm{Emb}}(N,M)
denotes the space of smooth embeddings of
N
in

N
and

have boundary these maps will all restrict to
one given map

Smale’s proof that

is contractible uses the Poincaré-Bendixson Theorem to guarantee the flow of the
vector fields he uses terminate in finite time. As the Poincaré-Bendixson
theorem is only available in dimension two, it limits the applicability of Smale’s argument. We should note, there
have been attempts to broaden the applicability of a Smale-type argument, by studying spaces of closed
1
-forms.
See for example the two papers of Laudenbach and Blank
[
25
]

[
26
]
for a sampling. Since Cerf’s argument does not depend on
Poincaré-Bendixson, it allows for greater applicability. The headline consequences of Cerf’s arguments
are that the diffeomorphism group
{\mathrm{Diff}}(D^{n})
has the same homotopy-type as
\Omega{\mathrm{Emb}}(D^{n-1},D^{n})
and also that
the embedding space
{\mathrm{Emb}}(D^{n-1},D^{n})
is a homotopy-retract of
\Omega{\mathrm{Emb}}(D^{n-2},D^{n})
. Putting these two results
together, the homotopy groups of
{\mathrm{Diff}}(D^{n})
inject into the homotopy groups of
\Omega^{2}{\mathrm{Emb}}(D^{n-2},D^{n})
for all
n
.
While Cerf states these as his theorems, his techniques prove much more. It is the purpose of this paper to

Cerf’s techniques

In Cerf’s paper
[

the main results we highlight concern three maps:

1.

{\mathrm{Diff}}(D^{n})\to\Omega{\mathrm{Emb}}(D^{n-1},D^{n})

2.

1.4
Schönflies

An interesting observation in
[
4
]
is that the ‘stacking’ operation, while appearing to
be just a monoid structure on the space
{\mathrm{Emb}}(D^{n-1},S^{1}\times D^{n-1})

group-like, i.e. the induced monoid structure on
\pi_{0}{\mathrm{Emb}}(D^{n-1},S^{1}\times D^{n-1})

One consequence of this is an argument the monoid of Schönflies spheres

is a group using the
relative connect-sum operation. There is a classical argument due to Kervaire-Milnor that this monoid has inverses. Our argument
is characteristically different, in that we construct an onto homomorphism from a group, i.e. in a weak sense we give a presentation of
the monoid of Schönflies spheres. This appears in Section

In this paper we outline what is known about such scanning maps, and where some potentially interesting future

3
Bott handles and miscellany

The homotopy-equivalence
{\mathrm{Diff}}(D^{n})\simeq\Omega{\mathrm{Emb}}(D^{n-1},D^{n})
can be extrapolated to a homotopy-equivalence

{\mathrm{Diff}}(I\times N)\simeq\Omega{\mathrm{Emb}}(\{\frac{1}{2}\}\times N,I\times N)
, and scanning maps

{\mathrm{Diff}}(D^{k}\times N)\to\Omega{\mathrm{Emb}}(D^{k-1}\times N,D^{k}\times N)\to\cdots\to\Omega^{j}{\mathrm{Emb}}(D^{k-j}\times N,D^{k}\times N).

Whereas the scanning of Section
2
could be viewed as an argument where the intermediate space is that of the space of
canceling handles, i.e. vanilla Morse theory, the scanning above has intermediate space the space Bott-style canceling handles,
i.e. the kinds of
handles that occur with Bott-style Morse functions (functions on manifolds where the critical point sets are manifolds and
the Hessian is non-degenerate on these critical submanifolds
[
1
]
). For Bott-style Morse functions ‘handle’ attachments
are disc bundles over manifolds, whereas in standard Morse theory one attaches disc bundles
over points, i.e. plain discs. Specifically, an adjunction where one attaches a disc-bundle over
M
,
M\ltimes D^{k}
to another manifold

N
along an embedding
M\ltimes\partial D^{k}\to\partial N

is what is called a Bott-style handle attachment
[
1
]
, as these sorts of attachments occur for Bott-type Morse functions, i.e. functions

W\to{\mathbb{R}}
whose critical points are manifolds and the Hessian is non-degenerate on the normal bundle fibers. Bott-style
Morse functions typically occur when functions have symmetry, for example the trace of a matrix is a Bott-style Morse function
on the orthogonal group
O_{n}
. The critical points of this function are the square roots of the identity matrix
I
, thus copies of
Grassman manifolds. As a concrete example, the trace functional expresses
SO_{3}
as the tautological line bundle over

\mathbb{R}P^{2}
union a
3
-handle.

The analogue to Theorem
2.1
in the Bott case has the form of a homotopy-equivalence

{\mathrm{Emb}}(M\ltimes D^{k},N\setminus\nu(M\ltimes D^{k-1}))\simeq\Omega{\mathrm{Emb}}(M\ltimes D^{k-1},N).

Given that our scanning maps are highly structured, they would appear to be a potentially useful
device for exploring the homotopy-types of diffeomorphism groups like
{\mathrm{Diff}}(D^{n})
,
{\mathrm{Diff}}(S^{1}\times D^{n})
and generally
product manifolds
{\mathrm{Diff}}(N\times D^{k})
, in particular for studying spaces of pseudoisotopies. From this perspective
there is perhaps a similarly overlooked element of Embedding Calculus
[
29
]

[
36
]
that is relevant.

For example, given a manifold
M
, let
\mathcal{O}_{k}(M)
be the category of open subsets of
M
diffeomorphic to a disjoint union
of at most
k
open balls, arrows given by inclusion maps. Given
U\in\mathcal{O}_{k}(M)
let

F(U)
be
{\mathrm{Emb}}(U\times D^{j},M\times D^{j})
, i.e.
smooth embeddings of
U\times D^{j}
in
M\times D^{j}
that restrict to the standard inclusion on
U\times\partial D^{j}
.
The
k
-th stage of the Taylor tower could be taken to be
T_{k}F(U)={\mathrm{holim}}_{V\in\mathcal{O}_{k}(U)}F(V)
. From this perspective,
the scanning map is the evaluation map to the first stage of the Taylor tower. Higher stages of the Taylor tower are built from
spaces of generalized string links (in the sense that the Goodwillie-Weiss-Klein embedding calculus is built from
configuration spaces), and similarly the layers will be a relative section space. This Taylor tower maps
to the GWK-Taylor tower so it should converge when the co-dimension of the embeddings are sufficiently large. Minimally
from the above it will have embeddings as a homotopy retract. The
rate of convergence of this Taylor tower we suspect will often be greater – for example by Cerf’s theorem

{\mathrm{Diff}}(D^{n})\simeq\Omega{\mathrm{Emb}}(D^{n-1},D^{n})
the first stage when
M=I
is homotopy-equivalent to

{\mathrm{Diff}}(I\times D^{j-1})\simeq{\mathrm{Diff}}(D^{j})
.
The potential for this framework is that it may provide more manageable
inductive steps for practical computations of homotopy and homology groups of embedding spaces,
as one is no longer comparing an embedding space
directly with configuration spaces. Spaces of string links have been the subject of some recent investigations
by Koytcheff
[
22
]
, Turchin and Tsopméné
[
35
]

[
22
]
, including a description of some of their low-dimensional
homotopy groups
[
22
]
as well as an operad action
[
23
]
, so we may not be far removed from being able to analyze these

string link Taylor towers.

String links appear in two essential ways in both
[
4
]
and

[
5
]
. Specifically, barbell diffeomorphism families are the induced diffeomorphisms coming from
the low-dimensional homotopy groups of
spaces of
2
-component string-links. Moreover, the map we use to detect our diffeomorphisms of
S^{1}\times D^{n-1}
has the form

{\mathrm{Diff}}(S^{1}\times D^{n-1})\to\Omega^{n-2}{\mathrm{Emb}}(D^{1},S^{1}\times D^{n-1})
. If we take the lifts of an element
of
{\mathrm{Emb}}(D^{1},S^{1}\times D^{n-1})
to the universal cover, we get an equivariant, infinite-component string link in

{\mathbb{R}}\times D^{n-1}
. Thus string links would appear to be a relatively efficient machine for investigating embedding
spaces and diffeomorphism groups. It would be very interesting to see the relative rate of convergence of the above
Taylor towers, compared to the standard Embedding Calculus
[
15
]
.

A Primal-Dual Framework for Transformers and Neural Networks
Tan M. Nguyen*, Tam Nguyen*, Nhat Ho, Andrea L. Bertozzi, Department of Mathematics, Richard G. Baraniuk**, Department of ECE, Stanley J. Osher**, Department of Mathematics, University of California, Los Angeles, sjo@math.ucla.edu

Abstract
Self-attention is key to the remarkable success of transformers in sequence modeling tasks including many applications in natural language processing and computer vision. Like neural network layers, these attention mechanisms are often developed by heuristics and experience. To provide a principled framework for constructing attention layers in transformers, we show that the self-attention corresponds to the support vector expansion derived from a support vector regression problem, whose primal formulation has the form of a neural network layer. Using our framework, we derive popular attention layers used in practice and propose two new attentions: 1) the Batch Normalized Attention (Attention-BN) derived from the batch normalization layer and 2) the Attention with Scaled Head (Attention-SH) derived from using less training data to fit the SVR model. We empirically demonstrate the advantages of the Attention-BN and Attention-SH in reducing head redundancy, increasing the model’s accuracy, and improving the model’s efficiency in a variety of practical applications including image and time-series classification. † † ∗ Co-first authors. ∗∗ Co-last authors. 1.1
Background: Self-Attention

For a given input sequence
{\mathbf{X}}:=[{\bm{x}}_{1},\cdots,{\bm{x}}_{N}]^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times D_{x}}
of
N
feature vectors, self-attention transforms
{\mathbf{X}}
into the output sequence
{\mathbf{H}}
in the following two steps:

Step 1.
The input sequence
{\mathbf{X}}
is projected into the query matrix
{\mathbf{Q}}
, the key matrix
{\mathbf{K}}
, and the value matrix
{\mathbf{V}}
via three linear transformations

{\mathbf{Q}}={\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{W}}_{Q}^{\top};{\mathbf{K}}={\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{W}}_{K}^{\top};{\mathbf{V}}={\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{W}}_{V}^{\top},

where
{\mathbf{W}}_{Q},{\mathbf{W}}_{K}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D\times D_{x}}
, and
{\mathbf{W}}_{V}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D_{v}\times D_{x}}
are the weight matrices. We denote
{\bm{Q}}:=[{\bm{q}}_{1},\cdots,{\bm{q}}_{N}]^{\top},{\mathbf{K}}:=[{\bm{k}}_{1},\cdots,{\bm{k}}_{N}]^{\top}
, and
{\mathbf{V}}:=[{\bm{v}}_{1},\cdots,{\bm{v}}_{N}]^{\top}
, where the vectors
{\bm{q}}_{i},{\bm{k}}_{i},{\bm{v}}_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,N
are the query, key, and value vectors, respectively.

Step 2.
The output sequence
{\mathbf{H}}:=[{\bm{h}}_{1},\cdots,{\bm{h}}_{N}]^{\top}
is then computed as follows

{\mathbf{H}}={\rm softmax}\Big({{\mathbf{Q}}}{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\top}/{\sqrt{D}}\Big){\bf V}:={\mathbf{A}}{{\mathbf{V}}},

(1)

where the softmax function is applied to each row of the matrix
{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{K}}^{\top}/\sqrt{D}
. The matrix
{\mathbf{A}}:={\rm softmax}\Big(\frac{{{\mathbf{Q}}}{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\top}}{\sqrt{D}}\Big)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times N}
and its component
a_{ij}
for
i,\,j=1,\cdots,N
are called the attention matrix and attention scores, respectively. For each query vector
{\bm{q}}_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,N
, an equivalent form of Eqn. (
1
) to compute the output vector
{\bm{h}}_{i}
is given by

{\bm{h}}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\rm softmax}\Big({{\bm{q}}}_{i}^{\top}{{\bm{k}}}_{j}/\sqrt{D}\Big){{\bm{v}}}_{j}.

(2)

The self-attention computed by Eqn. (
1
) and (
2
) is called the scaled dot-product or softmax attention. In our paper, we call a transformer that uses this attention the softmax transformer. The structure that the attention matrix
{\mathbf{A}}
learns from training determines the ability of the self-attention to capture contextual representation for each token. Additionally, a residual connection can be added to the output of the self-attention layer,
{\bm{h}}_{i}={\bm{x}}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\rm softmax}\Big({{\bm{q}}}_{i}^{\top}{{\bm{k}}}_{j}/\sqrt{D}\Big){{\bm{v}}}_{j}.

Multi-head Attention (MHA).
In MHA, multiple heads are concatenated to compute the final output. This MHA mechanism allows transformers to capture more diverse attention patterns and increase the capacity of the model. Let
H
be the number of heads and
{\mathbf{W}}_{O}^{\text{multi}}=\left[{\mathbf{W}}_{O}^{1},\dots,{\mathbf{W}}_{O}^{H}\right]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D_{v}\times HD_{v}}
be the projection matrix for the output where
{\mathbf{W}}_{O}^{1},\dots,{\mathbf{W}}_{O}^{H}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D_{v}\times D_{v}}
. The MHA is defined as

\textrm{MultiHead}(\{{\mathbf{H}}\}_{s=1}^{H})=\textrm{Concat}({\mathbf{H}}^{1},\dots,{\mathbf{H}}^{H}){\mathbf{W}}_{O}^{\text{multi}\top}=\sum_{s=1}^{H}{\mathbf{H}}^{s}{\mathbf{W}}_{O}^{s\top}=\sum_{s=1}^{H}{\mathbf{A}}^{s}{{\mathbf{V}}^{s}}{\mathbf{W}}_{O}^{s\top}.

(3)

Despite their remarkable success, most attention layers are developed based on heuristic approaches, and a coherent
principled framework for synthesizing attention layers has remained elusive.

1.2
Contribution

We derive the self-attention as the support vector expansion of a given support vector regression (SVR) problem. The primal representation of the regression function has the form of a neural network layer. Thus, we establish a primal-dual connection between an attention layer in transformers and a neural network layer in deep neural networks. Our framework suggests a principled approach to developing an attention mechanism:
Starting from a neural network layer and a support vector regression problem, we derive the dual as a support vector expansion to attain the corresponding attention layer.
We then employ this principled approach to invent two novel classes of attentions: the Batch Normalized Attention (Attention-BN) derived from the batch normalization layer in deep neural networks and the Attention with Scaled Heads (Attention-SH) resulting from solving the support vector regression model with less amount of training data. Our contribution is three-fold.

1.

We derive self-attention as a support vector expansion that solves a SVR problem, thus providing a principled primal-dual framework to study and develop self-attentions.

2.

We re-derive popular attentions, such as the linear attention
(
Katharopoulos et al., 2020
)
, the sparse attention
(
Child et al., 2019
)
, and the multi-head attention
(
Vaswani et al., 2017
)
, from our proposed framework.

3.

We develop two new attention mechanism: the Batch Normalized Attention (Attention-BN) and the Attention with Scaled Heads (Attention-SH) using our proposed framework.

We empirically demonstrate that 1) the Attention-BN significantly outperforms the baseline softmax and linear attention and 2) the Attention-SH performs better while being more efficient than the same baselines on a variety of practical tasks including image and time-series classification.

2
Primal-Dual Interpretation of Self-Attention

We first provide a primal-dual interpretation of self-attention as a support vector regression problem in Section
2.1
. Based on that primal-dual framework, we derive popular attention mechanisms as the support vector expansion in Section
2.2
. Finally, we introduce two new attention mechanisms in Section
2.3
, the Attention-BN and Attention-SH.

2.1
Attention as a Support Vector Regression Model

In this section, we derive self-attention from a support vector regression problem. Suppose we are given a training data
\{({\bm{k}}_{1},{\bm{y}}_{1}),\dots,({\bm{k}}_{N},{\bm{y}}_{N})\}\subset\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{Y}
, where
\mathcal{K}={\mathbb{R}}^{D}
and
\mathcal{Y}={\mathbb{R}}^{D_{v}}
. Here,
{\bm{k}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{k}}_{N}
are attention keys in self-attention, and
{\bm{y}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{y}}_{N}
are the training targets. We consider the function
f
, taking the form

\displaystyle{\bm{y}}

\displaystyle=f({\bm{x}}):={\mathbf{W}}\frac{\Phi({\bm{x}})}{h({\bm{x}})}+{\bm{b}},

(4)

where
{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{K}={\mathbb{R}}^{D}
,
\Phi({\bm{x}})=[\phi_{1}({\bm{x}}),\dots,\phi_{D_{\phi}}({\bm{x}})]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D_{\phi}}
,
{\mathbf{W}}=[{\bm{w}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{w}}_{D_{v}}]^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D_{v}\times D_{\phi}}
,
{\bm{b}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D_{v}}
, and
h({\bm{x}})
is a vector-scalar function. We fit the function
f
to the training data
\{({\bm{k}}_{1},{\bm{y}}_{1}),\dots,({\bm{k}}_{N},{\bm{y}}_{N})\}
with an
\mathbb{L}_{2}
regularization on
{\mathbf{W}}
, i.e., a ridge regression, by solving the following convex optimization problem:

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{W}}\\
{\bm{\xi}}_{j},\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j},j=1,\ldots,N\end{subarray}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|^{2}_{\text{F}}+C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\left({\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)+\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\|{\bm{w}}_{d}\|^{2}+C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\left({\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)+\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\right)

(5)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{\bm{y}}_{j}(d)-{\bm{w}}_{d}^{\top}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})/h({\bm{k}}_{j})-{\bm{b}}(d)\leq\epsilon+{\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)\\
{\bm{w}}_{d}^{\top}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})/h({\bm{k}}_{j})+{\bm{b}}(d)-{\bm{y}}_{j}(d)\leq\epsilon+\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\\
{\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d),\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\geq 0\end{cases},\;j=1,\ldots,N,\;d=1,\ldots,D_{v}.

The Eqn.
5
implies that there exists a function
f
that can approximates all pairs
({\bm{k}}_{j},{\bm{y}}_{j})
with
\epsilon
precision. The additional slack variables
{\bm{\xi}}_{j},\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}
relax this assumption and allows some of the training set data points to have the training error greater than
\epsilon
just as in the soft-margin SVM
(
Cortes & Vapnik, 1995
;
Schölkopf et al., 2002
)
.
C>0
is a constant determining the trade between the complexity penalizer
\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\|{\bm{w}}_{d}\|^{2}
, i.e., the flatness of
f
, and the amount up to which deviations larger than
\epsilon
are tolerated.

In order to derive the self-attention from the support vector regression defined by the optimization problem
5
, the key idea to construct the Lagrangian from Eqn.
5
and find the representation of the
{\bm{w}}_{d}
,
d=1,\ldots,D_{v}
, in terms of the dual variables. We define the Lagrangian function as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\|{\bm{w}}_{d}\|^{2}+C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\left({\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)+\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\left({\bm{\eta}}_{j}(d){\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)+\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}_{j}(d)\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\right)

(6)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d)\left(\epsilon+{\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)-{\bm{y}}_{j}(d)+{\bm{w}}_{d}^{\top}\frac{\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})}{h({\bm{k}}_{j})}+{\bm{b}}(d)\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d)\left(\epsilon+\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)+{\bm{y}}_{j}(d)-{\bm{w}}_{d}^{\top}\frac{\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})}{h({\bm{k}}_{j})}-{\bm{b}}(d)\right),

where
{\bm{\eta}}_{j}
,
\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}_{j}
,
{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}
and
\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}
are Lagrange multipliers. These dual variables have to satisfy positivity constraints, i.e.,
{\bm{\eta}}_{j}(d),\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}_{j}(d),{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d),\,\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d)\geq 0,\,\forall j=1,\ldots,N,\;\forall d=1,\ldots,D_{v}
. It follows from the saddle point condition that the partial derivatives of the Lagrangian function
\mathcal{L}
with respect to the primal variables
\left({\bm{w}}_{d},{\bm{b}}(d),\{{\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d),\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\}_{j=1}^{N}\right)
,
d=1,\ldots,D_{v}
, have to vanish for optimality, namely, we have:

\displaystyle\partial_{{\bm{b}}(d)}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d)-{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d))=0\Rightarrow\sum_{j=1}^{N}({\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d))=0,

(7)

\displaystyle\partial_{{\bm{w}}_{d}}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle={\bm{w}}_{d}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}({\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d))\frac{\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})}{h({\bm{k}}_{j})}=0\Rightarrow{\bm{w}}_{d}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}({\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d))\frac{\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})}{h({\bm{k}}_{j})},

(8)

\displaystyle\partial_{{\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=C-{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d)-{\bm{\eta}}_{j}(d)=0,\,\,\,\partial_{\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)}\mathcal{L}=C-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d)-\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}_{j}(d)=0.

(9)

Let
{\bm{v}}_{j}=[\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(1)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(1)}{h({\bm{k}}_{j})},\dots,\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(D_{v})-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(D_{v})}{h({\bm{k}}_{j})}]^{\top}
,
j=1,\dots,N
, and substitute Eqn.
8
into Eqn.
4
, we obtain the following support vector expansion of the linear basis function
f
:

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(1)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(1)}{h({\bm{k}}_{j})}\frac{\Phi({\bm{x}})^{\top}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})}{{h({\bm{x}})}},\dots,\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(D_{v})-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(D_{v})}{h({\bm{k}}_{j})}\frac{\Phi({\bm{x}})^{\top}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})}{{h({\bm{x}})}}\right]^{\top}+{\bm{b}},

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\Phi({\bm{x}})^{\top}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})}{{{h({\bm{x}})}}}{\bm{v}}_{j}+{\bm{b}}.

(10)

Remark 1

Notice that from Eqn.
9
and the conditions
{\bm{\eta}}_{j}(d),\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}_{j}(d),{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d),\,\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d)\geq 0
, we can prove that
{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d),\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d)\in[0,C]
. Furthermore, we can show that
{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d)*\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d)=0

(
Smola & Schölkopf, 2004
;
Schölkopf et al., 2002
)
. As a result,
{\bm{v}}_{j}(d)\in\left[-\frac{C}{h({\bm{k}}_{j})},\frac{C}{h({\bm{k}}_{j})}\right]
,
d=1,\dots,D_{v}
.

Deriving Softmax Attention.
Choosing the appropriate
h({\bm{x}})
and
\Phi({\bm{x}})
allows us to derive the popular softmax attention given in Eqn.
1
and
2
. In particular, if we choose
h({\bm{x}}):=\sum_{j}^{N}\Phi({\bm{x}})^{T}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})
, Eqn.
10
becomes

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\Phi({\bm{x}})^{\top}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})}{{{\sum_{j^{\prime}}^{N}\Phi({\bm{x}})^{T}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j^{\prime}})}}}{\bm{v}}_{j}+{\bm{b}}=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Phi({\bm{x}})^{\top}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j}){\bm{v}}_{j}}{{{\sum_{j^{\prime}}^{N}\Phi({\bm{x}})^{T}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j^{\prime}})}}}+{\bm{b}}.

(11)

We then select
\Phi({\bm{x}})=\left(a^{(0)}_{l_{0}},a^{(1)}_{1},\dots,a^{(1)}_{l_{1}},\dots,a^{(t)}_{1},\dots,a^{(t)}_{l_{t}},\dots\right)
where
l_{t}={D+t-1\choose t}
and

\displaystyle a^{(t)}_{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{(x_{1}/\sqrt[4]{D})^{n_{1}}\dots(x_{D}/\sqrt[4]{D})^{n_{D}}}{\sqrt{n_{1}!\dots n_{D}!}}\,\,\,|\,\,\,n_{1}+\dots+n_{D}=t,\,\,1\leq l\leq l_{t}.

(12)

Since

\displaystyle\exp{({\bm{x}}^{T}{\bm{y}})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{({\bm{x}}^{T}{\bm{y}})^{t}}{t!}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{1}+\dots+n_{D}=t}\left(\frac{x_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots x_{D}^{n_{D}}}{\sqrt{n_{1}!\dots n_{D}!}}\right)\left(\frac{y_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots y_{D}^{n_{D}}}{\sqrt{n_{1}!\dots n_{D}!}}\right),

(13)

then Eqn.
27
becomes

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\exp{({\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{k}}_{j}/\sqrt{D})}}{{{\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{N}\exp{({\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{k}}_{j^{\prime}}/\sqrt{D})}}}}{\bm{v}}_{j}+{\bm{b}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\text{softmax}\left({\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{k}}_{j}/\sqrt{D}\right){\bm{v}}_{j}+{\bm{b}}.

(14)

Let
{\bm{x}}={{\bm{q}}}_{i}
,
{\bm{b}}=0
and relax the boundness constraint of
{\bm{v}}_{j}
in Remark
1
. Eqn.
30
becomes Eqn.
2
of the softmax attention
(
Vaswani et al., 2017
)
. We summarize our results in the following theorem.

Theorem 1
(Softmax Attention as a Support Vector Expansion)

Given the function
f
defined in Eqn.
4
with
h({\bm{x}}):=\sum_{j}^{N}\Phi({\bm{x}})^{T}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})
and the support vector regression problem defined in Eqn.
5
, we set
{\bm{b}}=0
, choose
\Phi({\bm{x}})
as in Eqn.
28
, and relax the boundness constraint of the variables
{\bm{v}}_{j}=[\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(1)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(1)}{h({\bm{k}}_{j})},\dots,\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(D_{v})-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(D_{v})}{h({\bm{k}}_{j})}]^{\top}
, where
{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}
and
\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}
are dual variables of Eqn.
5
,
j=1,\dots,N
. Then, the support vector expansion of
f
derived from Eqn.
5
has the form of a softmax attention

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\text{softmax}\left({\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{k}}_{j}/\sqrt{D}\right){\bm{v}}_{j}.

(15)

Remark 2

Since
{\bm{b}}
is set to 0, the centering constraint of
{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}
and
\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}
in Eqn.
7
can be ignored.

Remark 3

Theorem
1
and its derivation can be easily extended to capture the full form of the softmax attention with the residual connection, the query matrix projection
{\mathbf{W}}_{Q}
, the key matrix projection
{\mathbf{W}}_{K}
, and the value matrix projection
{\mathbf{W}}_{V}
. We include this result in Appendix
F
.

Remark 4

The primal representation of the function
f
as in Eqn.
4
has the form of a neural network layer where
{\mathbf{W}}
is the weight,
{\bm{b}}
is the bias term,
\Phi({\bm{x}})
is the input, and
h({\bm{x}})
is the normalization term. Thus, an attention layer and a neural network layer are primal-dual of each other.

A principled approach to developing an attention mechanism.
The observation in Remark
4
suggests a principled way to construct an attention layer:
Starting from a neural network layer and a support vector regression problem, we derive the dual as a support vector expansion to attain the corresponding attention layer.
Using this approach, we derive popular attention mechanisms in Section
2.2
and propose our new attention mechanisms in Section
2.3
.

2.2.1
Linear Attention

The Eqn.
27
, which is obtained when choosing
h({\bm{x}}):=\sum_{j}^{N}\Phi({\bm{x}})^{T}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})
, already matches the formula of the linear attention. Here, we can let
{\bm{b}}=0
as above and select the function
\Phi
that results in a positive similarity function, e.g.
\Phi({\bm{x}})=\text{elu}({\bm{x}})+1
, as in
(

)
.

2.2.2
Sparse Attention

The sparse attention
(
Child et al., 2019
)
can be derived by fitting the function
f
in Eqn.
4
using a different subset
\{({\bm{k}}_{m_{x}(1)},{\bm{y}}_{m_{x}(1)}),\dots,({\bm{k}}_{m_{x}(M)},{\bm{y}}_{m_{x}(M)})\}
of training data
\{({\bm{k}}_{1},{\bm{y}}_{1}),\dots,({\bm{k}}_{N},{\bm{y}}_{N})\}
for each input data
{\bm{x}}
, where
\mathcal{M}_{{\bm{x}}}=\{m_{{\bm{x}}}(1),\dots,m_{{\bm{x}}}(M)\}\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
.
The support vector expansion of
f
is then given by

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{M}_{{\bm{x}}}}(j)\frac{\Phi({\bm{x}})^{\top}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})}{{{h({\bm{x}})}}}{\bm{v}}_{j}+{\bm{b}}

(16)

where
\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{M}_{{\bm{x}}}}(j)=\left[j\in\mathcal{M}_{{\bm{x}}}\right]:=\begin{cases}1\,\,\text{if }j\in\mathcal{M}_{{\bm{x}}}\\
0\,\,\text{otherwise}\end{cases}
. Note that the subsets
\mathcal{M}_{{\bm{x}}}
are different for different
{\bm{x}}
. When letting
{\bm{x}}={\bm{q}}_{i}
where
{\bm{q}}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,N
, are the query vectors and choosing
\Phi,h,{\bm{b}}
as in Section
2.1
, we can obtain the sparse attention in
(
Child et al., 2019
)
where the binary matrix
{\mathbf{M}}=\left(\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{M}_{{\bm{q}}_{i}}}(j)\right)_{i,j=1}^{N}
becomes the sparse masking matrix.

2.2.3
Multi-head Attention (MHA)

The MHA can be derived by solving multiple support vector regression problems and then linearly combining their outputs. In particular, given
H
training datasets
\{({\bm{k}}^{1}_{1},{\bm{y}}^{1}_{1}),\dots,({\bm{k}}^{1}_{N},{\bm{y}}^{1}_{N})\},\dots,\{({\bm{k}}^{H}_{1},{\bm{y}}^{H}_{1}),\dots,({\bm{k}}^{H}_{N},{\bm{y}}^{H}_{N})\}\subset\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{Y}
, where
\mathcal{K}={\mathbb{R}}^{D}
and
\mathcal{Y}={\mathbb{R}}^{D_{v}}
. We define the function
f
applied on the input vector
{\bm{x}}=[{\bm{x}}^{1},\dots,{\bm{x}}^{H}]
as follows

\displaystyle{\bm{y}}=f({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{s=1}^{H}{\mathbf{W}}^{s}_{O}{\bm{y}}^{s}=\sum_{s=1}^{H}{\mathbf{W}}^{s}_{O}f^{s}({\bm{x}}^{s})=\sum_{s=1}^{H}{\mathbf{W}}^{s}_{O}\left({\mathbf{W}}^{s}\frac{\Phi^{s}({\bm{x}}^{s})}{h^{s}({\bm{x}}^{s})}+{\bm{b}}^{s}\right),

(17)

where each function
f^{s}({\bm{x}}^{s})={\mathbf{W}}^{s}\frac{\Phi^{s}({\bm{x}}^{s})}{h^{s}({\bm{x}}^{s})}+{\bm{b}}^{s}
is fitted to the training dataset
\{({\bm{k}}^{s}_{1},{\bm{y}}^{s}_{1}),\dots,({\bm{k}}^{s}_{N},{\bm{y}}^{s}_{N})\}
. Following the same derivation and choosing
\{\Phi^{s},h^{s},{\bm{b}}^{s}\}_{s=1}^{H}
as in Section
2.1
, we can rewrite
f({\bm{x}})
in terms of the support vector expansions of the individual functions
f^{s}({\bm{x}}^{s})
, which are the individual softmax attentions

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{H}{\mathbf{W}}^{s}_{O}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\Phi^{s}({\bm{x}}^{s})^{\top}\Phi^{s}({\bm{k}}^{s}_{j})}{{{h^{s}({\bm{x}}^{s})}}}{\bm{v}}^{s}_{j}+{\bm{b}}^{s}\right)=\sum_{s=1}^{H}{\mathbf{W}}^{s}_{O}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\text{softmax}\left({\bm{x}}^{s\top}{\bm{k}}^{s}_{j}/\sqrt{D}\right){\bm{v}}^{s}_{j}\right).

(18)

Comparing Eqn.
18
and Eqn.
3
, we see that Eqn.
18
computes the MHA when choosing
{\bm{x}}^{s}={\bm{q}}^{s}_{i}
where
{\bm{q}}^{s}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,N
, are the query vectors at the
s^{\text{th}}
head.

2.3
Deriving New Attention Mechanisms: Batch Normalized Attention and Multiresolution Head Attention

In this section, we employ our primal-dual framework to develop new attention mechanisms. In particular, we derive: 1) the Batch Normalized Attention from employing the batch normalization
(
Ioffe & Szegedy, 2015
)
; and 2) the Attention with Scaled Heads from using different amounts of training data. By 1) and 2), we demonstrate that
new attentions can be invented by modifying the primal neural network layer and the support vector regression problem in our framework, respectively
.

2.3.1
Batch Normalized Attention

We incorporate the batch normalization into the primal form of the function
f
in Eqn.
4
. Given a training data
\{({\bm{k}}_{1},{\bm{y}}_{1}),\dots,({\bm{k}}_{N},{\bm{y}}_{N})\}\subset\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{Y}
, where
\mathcal{K}={\mathbb{R}}^{D}
and
\mathcal{Y}={\mathbb{R}}^{D_{v}}
as in Section
2.1
, the resultant
f
is defined as follows

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle:={\mathbf{W}}\frac{\Phi(({\bm{x}}-{\bm{\mu}})\odot\textbf{s}^{-1})}{h(({\bm{x}}-{\bm{\mu}})\odot\textbf{s}^{-1})}+{\bm{b}},

(19)

where

\displaystyle{\bm{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\bm{k}}_{j},\,\,\textbf{s}^{-1}=\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma^{2}_{1}+\epsilon}},\dots,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma^{2}_{D}+\epsilon}}\right]^{\top},\,\,\sigma^{2}_{d}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}({\bm{k}}_{j}(d)-{\bm{\mu}}(d))^{2}.

(20)

Here,
d=1,\dots,D
, and the mean subtraction and division by the standard deviation is performed element-wise along the feature dimension of
{\bm{x}}
. Following the same derivation as in Section
2.1
, we derive the following support vector expansion of
f

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\Phi(({\bm{x}}-{\bm{\mu}})\odot\textbf{s}^{-1})^{\top}\Phi(({\bm{k}}_{j}-{\bm{\mu}})\odot\textbf{s}^{-1})}{{{h(({\bm{x}}-{\bm{\mu}})\odot\textbf{s}^{-1})}}}{\bm{v}}_{j}+{\bm{b}}.

(21)

Here,
{\bm{v}}_{j}=\left[\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(1)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(1)}{h(({\bm{k}}_{j}-{\bm{\mu}})\odot\textbf{s}^{-1})},\dots,\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(D_{v})-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(D_{v})}{h(({\bm{k}}_{j}-{\bm{\mu}})\odot\textbf{s}^{-1})}\right]^{\top}
, where
{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}
and
\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}
are the dual variables,
j=1,\dots,N
. Same as in Section
2.1
, in Eqn.
21
, we choose
\Phi
as in Eqn.
28
,
h({\bm{x}}):=\sum_{j}^{N}\Phi({\bm{x}})^{T}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})
, and
{\bm{b}}=0
to obtain the the Batch Normalized Attention, which is defined as follows.

Definition 1
(Batch Normalized Attention)

Given a set of the key and value vectors
\{{\bm{k}}_{j},{\bm{v}}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
, for each query vector
{\bm{q}}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,N
, the Batch Normalized Attention (Attention-BN) computes the corresponding output vector
{\bm{h}}_{i}
of the query
{\bm{q}}_{i}
by the following attention formula:

\displaystyle{\bm{h}}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\text{softmax}\left((({\bm{q}}_{i}-{\bm{\mu}})\odot\textbf{s}^{-1})^{\top}(({\bm{k}}_{j}-{\bm{\mu}})\odot\textbf{s}^{-1})/\sqrt{D}\right){\bm{v}}_{j},

(22)

where

\displaystyle{\bm{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\bm{k}}_{j},\,\,\textbf{s}^{-1}=\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma^{2}_{1}+\epsilon}},\dots,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma^{2}_{D}+\epsilon}}\right]^{\top},\,\,\sigma^{2}_{d}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}({\bm{k}}_{j}(d)-{\bm{\mu}}(d))^{2}.

(23)

The Effect of Normalization.
Expanding the dot product in the Attention-BN (see Appendix
E
), Eqn.
22
becomes

\displaystyle{\bm{h}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\text{softmax}\left(\sum_{d=1}^{D}\frac{{\bm{q}}_{i}(d){\bm{k}}_{j}(d)-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{N}{\bm{k}}_{j^{\prime}}(d){\bm{k}}_{j}(d)}{\sqrt{D}(\sigma^{2}_{d}+\epsilon)}\right){\bm{v}}_{j}.

(24)

Eqn.
24
implies that in the Attention-BN, the similarity between the query
{\bm{q}}_{i}
and the key
{\bm{k}}_{j}
is adjusted by the similarity between the key
{\bm{k}}_{j}
and all the keys
{\bm{k}}_{j^{\prime}}
,
j^{\prime}=1,\dots,N
. In particular, if the key
{\bm{k}}_{j}
is too similar to other keys, the query
{\bm{q}}_{i}
will attend to it less and vice versa.

2.3.2
Attention with Scaled Heads

The
Attention with Scaled Heads
, named Attention-SH, is derived based on the derivation of the MHA in Section
2.2.3
.
The key idea underlying the Attention-SH is to train multiple support vector regression problems using different amounts of training data. In particular, the Attention-SH follows Eqn.
17
in Section
2.2.3
and defines the same regression function
f
as the MHA. However, the Attention-SH fits the function
f^{s}
,
s=1,\dots,H
, in Eqn.
17
with training sets
\{({\bm{k}}^{1}_{1},{\bm{y}}^{1}_{1}),\dots,({\bm{k}}^{1}_{N_{1}},{\bm{y}}^{1}_{N_{1}})\},\dots,\{({\bm{k}}^{H}_{1},{\bm{y}}^{H}_{1}),\dots,({\bm{k}}^{H}_{N_{H}},{\bm{y}}^{H}_{N_{H}})\}\subset\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{Y}
of different sizes
N_{1},\dots,N_{H}
, where
\mathcal{K}={\mathbb{R}}^{D}
and
\mathcal{Y}={\mathbb{R}}^{D_{v}}
. The resultant support vector expansion yields the formula of the Attention-SH as in the following definition.

Definition 2
(Attention with Scaled Heads)

Given
H
sets of the key and value vectors
\{{\bm{k}}^{1}_{j},{\bm{v}}^{1}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{1}},\dots,\{{\bm{k}}^{H}_{j},{\bm{v}}^{H}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{H}}
, for each set of H query vectors
{\bm{q}}^{1}_{i},\dots,{\bm{q}}^{H}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,N
, the Attention with Scaled Heads (Attention-SH) computes the corresponding output vector
{\bm{h}}_{i}
of the queries
{\bm{q}}^{1}_{i},\dots,{\bm{q}}^{H}_{i}
by the following attention formula:

\displaystyle{\bm{h}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{H}{\mathbf{W}}^{s}_{O}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\text{softmax}\left({\bm{q}}_{i}^{s\top}{\bm{k}}^{s}_{j}/\sqrt{D}\right){\bm{v}}^{s}_{j}\right).

(25)

Remark 5

For a given input sequence
{\mathbf{X}}:=[{\bm{x}}_{1},\cdots,{\bm{x}}_{N}]^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times D_{x}}
of
N
feature vectors in self-attention, in order to generate the sets of
\{{\bm{k}}^{s}_{j},{\bm{v}}^{s}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{s}}
at the scale
s^{th}
, we can downsample the input
{\mathbf{X}}
before projecting into the key matrix
{\mathbf{K}}
and the value matrix
{\mathbf{V}}
. There are multiples approaches to downsampling
{\mathbf{X}}
, such as using the average-pooling, max-pooling, 1-D convolution, or K-means clustering. In this paper, we employ the average-pooling to downsample
{\mathbf{X}}
.

Linear Attention with Batch Normalization and Scaled Heads.
The Attention-BN/SH can be extended to use with the linear attention. In particular, in the Linear Attention-BN/SH, we replace the softmax kernel in Eqn.
22
and Eqn.
25
by the linear kernel, respectively.

(

(

(
Katharopoulos et al., 2020
)
, the resultant Linear Attention-BN and Linear Attention-SH yield similar advantages mentioned in (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv) over the baseline linear attention.

{\bm{\mu}}

\beta

Related Work

Interpretation of Attention Mechanism.

Recent works have focused on understanding the attention mechanism in transformers from different perspectives.
(
Tsai et al., 2019
)
considers attention as a weighted moving average over the inputs via a smoothing kernel.
(
Nguyen et al., 2022
)
draws a connection between self-attention and nonparametric kernel regression. With this understanding, the work explores better regression estimators, e.g. the generalized Fourier nonparametric regression estimator, to improve transformers. In addition,
(
Cao, 2021
)
then shows that the linear transformer
(
Katharopoulos et al., 2020
)
corresponds to a Petrov-Galerkin
projection
(
Reddy, 2004
)
and proves that the softmax normalization in the softmax attention is sufficient but not necessary.
Other works that employ ordinary/partial differential equations to provide an interpretation for attention include
(
Lu et al., 2019
;
Sander et al., 2022
)
. From a probabilistic perspective,
(
Tang & Matteson, 2021
;
Gabbur et al., 2021
;
Zhang & Feng, 2021
)
propose Gaussian mixture model frameworks to study the self-attention in transformers. Using graph-structured learning and message passing in graphical models is another attempt at understanding the attention mechanism
Wang et al. (2018)
;
Shaw et al. (2018)
;
Kreuzer et al. (2021)
. Optimization perspectives of attention mechanisms are recently explored.
(
Sander et al., 2022
)
connects transformers with an optimization process across iterations by specifically constructing the core energy function.
(
Sahiner et al., 2022
)
derive finite-dimensional convex equivalence of attentions that can be solved for global optimality. Different from these approaches, our primal-dual framework focuses on deriving attention as the dual expansion of a primal neural network layer via solving a support vector regression problem. This framework allows us to not only explain many different types of attention mechanisms but also create new ones.

Efficient Transformers.

Recently, efficient transformers have been studied
(
Roy et al., 2021
)
. Among them are sparse transformers which incorporate sparse structures into the attention matrix
(
Parmar et al., 2018
;
Liu et al., 2018
;
Qiu et al., 2019
;
Child et al., 2019
;
Beltagy et al., 2020
)
. Another class of efficient transformers are models that aim to have better coverage by integrating different access patterns
(
Child et al., 2019
;
Ho et al., 2019
)
, which can also be learned from the data
(
Kitaev et al., 2020
;
Roy et al., 2021
;
Tay et al., 2020
)
. An emerging body of work is proposed to distill and prune the model, including
(
Sanh et al., 2019
;
Sun et al., 2019
;
Voita et al., 2019
;
Sajjad et al., 2020
)
.
In other works, a side memory module is utilized in order to access multiple tokens simultaneously
(
Lee et al., 2019
;
Sukhbaatar et al., 2019
;
Asai & Choi, 2020
;
Beltagy et al., 2020
)
. Low-rank and kernelization methods have been proposed to improve the efficiency of self-attention calculation
(
Tsai et al., 2019
;
Wang et al., 2020
;
Katharopoulos et al., 2020
;
Choromanski et al., 2021
;
Shen et al., 2021
;
Peng et al., 2021
)
.
Our Attention-SH/BN+SH is orthogonal to these methods.

6
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we derive self-attention as a support vector expansion that solves a support vector regression (SVR) problem and provide a principled primal-dual framework to analyze and synthesize attention mechanisms. We then use our framework to invent two new attention mechanisms, the Batch Normalized Attention (Attention-BN) and the Attention with Scaled Heads (Attention-SH), that improve the accuracy and the efficiency of the baseline softmax attention. In our work, we approximate and learn the dual variables
{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}
and
\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}
using the value vector
{\bm{v}}_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,N
in self-attention. It is natural to include more inductive biases and structures of those dual variables from solving the dual optimization problem of SVR into the value vectors
{\bm{v}}_{j}
.
Furthermore, extending our framework to explain the attention modules that compute the attention using neural network layers or convolutional neural network layers applying on the input feature, such as the Convolutional Block Attention Module
(
Woo et al., 2018
)
, is an interesting research direction
.
We leave these exciting research ideas as future work.

Appendix D
Deriving Softmax Attention.

Choosing the appropriate
h({\bm{x}})
and
\Phi({\bm{x}})
allows us to derive the popular softmax attention given in Eqn.
1
and
2
. In particular, if we choose
h({\bm{x}}):=\sum_{j}^{N}\Phi({\bm{x}})^{T}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})
, Eqn.
10
becomes

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\Phi({\bm{x}})^{\top}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j})}{{{\sum_{j^{\prime}}^{N}\Phi({\bm{x}})^{T}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j^{\prime}})}}}{\bm{v}}_{j}+{\bm{b}}=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Phi({\bm{x}})^{\top}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j}){\bm{v}}_{j}}{{{\sum_{j^{\prime}}^{N}\Phi({\bm{x}})^{T}\Phi({\bm{k}}_{j^{\prime}})}}}+{\bm{b}}.

(27)

We then select
\Phi({\bm{x}})=\left(a^{(0)}_{l_{0}},a^{(1)}_{1},\dots,a^{(1)}_{l_{1}},\dots,a^{(t)}_{1},\dots,a^{(t)}_{l_{t}},\dots\right)
where
l_{t}={D+t-1\choose t}
and

\displaystyle a^{(t)}_{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{(x_{1}/\sqrt[4]{D})^{n_{1}}\dots(x_{D}/\sqrt[4]{D})^{n_{D}}}{\sqrt{n_{1}!\dots n_{D}!}}\,\,\,|\,\,\,n_{1}+\dots+n_{D}=t,\,\,1\leq l\leq l_{t}.

(28)

Since

\displaystyle\exp{({\bm{x}}^{T}{\bm{y}})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{({\bm{x}}^{T}{\bm{y}})^{t}}{t!}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{1}+\dots+n_{D}=t}\left(\frac{x_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots x_{D}^{n_{D}}}{\sqrt{n_{1}!\dots n_{D}!}}\right)\left(\frac{y_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots y_{D}^{n_{D}}}{\sqrt{n_{1}!\dots n_{D}!}}\right),

(29)

then Eqn.
27
becomes

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{1}+\dots+n_{D}=t}\left(\frac{\left(\frac{x_{1}}{\sqrt[4]{D}}\right)^{n_{1}}\dots\left(\frac{x_{D}}{\sqrt[4]{D}}\right)^{n_{D}}}{\sqrt{n_{1}!\dots n_{D}!}}\right)\left(\frac{\left(\frac{k_{j1}}{\sqrt[4]{D}}\right)^{n_{1}}\dots\left(\frac{k_{jD}}{\sqrt[4]{D}}\right)^{n_{D}}}{\sqrt{n_{1}!\dots n_{D}!}}\right)}{{{\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{N}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{1}+\dots+n_{D}=t}\left(\frac{\left(\frac{x_{1}}{\sqrt[4]{D}}\right)^{n_{1}}\dots\left(\frac{x_{D}}{\sqrt[4]{D}}\right)^{n_{D}}}{\sqrt{n_{1}!\dots n_{D}!}}\right)\left(\frac{\left(\frac{k_{j^{\prime}1}}{\sqrt[4]{D}}\right)^{n_{1}}\dots\left(\frac{k_{j^{\prime}D}}{\sqrt[4]{D}}\right)^{n_{D}}}{\sqrt{n_{1}!\dots n_{D}!}}\right)}}}{\bm{v}}_{j}+{\bm{b}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\exp{\left(\left(\frac{{\bm{x}}}{\sqrt[4]{D}}\right)^{\top}\frac{{\bm{k}}_{j}}{\sqrt[4]{D}}\right)}}{{{\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{N}\exp{\left(\left(\frac{{\bm{x}}}{\sqrt[4]{D}}\right)^{\top}\frac{{\bm{k}}_{j^{\prime}}}{\sqrt[4]{D}}\right)}}}}{\bm{v}}_{j}+{\bm{b}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\exp{({\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{k}}_{j}/\sqrt{D})}}{{{\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{N}\exp{({\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{k}}_{j^{\prime}}/\sqrt{D})}}}}{\bm{v}}_{j}+{\bm{b}}.

(30)

in Remark

Suppose we are given a training data
\{({\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{y}}_{1}),\dots,({\bm{x}}_{N},{\bm{y}}_{N})\}\subset\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}

\displaystyle{\bm{y}}=f({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle:={\mathbf{W}}\frac{\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}})}{h({\bm{x}})}+{\bm{x}}+{\bm{b}},

(32)

where
{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{X}={\mathbb{R}}^{D_{x}}
,
{\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}=[{\bm{w}}^{\text{proj}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{w}}^{\text{proj}}_{D}]^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D\times D_{x}}
,
\Phi(\cdot)=[\phi_{1}(\cdot),\dots,\phi_{D_{\phi}}(\cdot)]:{\mathbb{R}}^{D}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{D_{\phi}}
,
{\mathbf{W}}=[{\bm{w}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{w}}_{D_{v}}]^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D_{v}\times D_{\phi}}
,
{\bm{b}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D_{v}}
, and
h({\bm{x}})
is a vector-scalar function. We fit the function
f
to the training data
\{({\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{y}}_{1}),\dots,({\bm{x}}_{N},{\bm{y}}_{N})\}
with an
\mathbb{L}_{2}
regularization on
{\mathbf{W}}
and
{\mathbf{W}}^{proj}
by solving the following convex optimization problem:

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}\\
{\bm{\xi}}_{j},\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j},j=1,\ldots,N\end{subarray}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\|{\bm{w}}_{d}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\|{\bm{w}}^{\text{proj}}_{d}\|^{2}+C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\left({\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)+\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\right)

(33)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{\bm{y}}_{j}(d)-{\bm{w}}_{d}^{\top}\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}}_{j})/h({\bm{x}}_{j})-{\bm{x}}_{j}-{\bm{b}}(d)\leq\epsilon+{\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)\\
{\bm{w}}_{d}^{\top}\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}}_{j})/h({\bm{x}}_{j})+{\bm{x}}_{j}+{\bm{b}}(d)-{\bm{y}}_{j}(d)\leq\epsilon+\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\\
{\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d),\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\geq 0\end{cases},\;j=1,\ldots,N,\;d=1,\ldots,D_{v}.

The Lagrangian of the optimization problem
33
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\|{\bm{w}}_{d}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\|{\bm{w}}^{\text{proj}}_{d}\|^{2}+C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\left({\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)+\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\left({\bm{\eta}}_{j}(d){\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)+\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}_{j}(d)\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\right)

(34)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d)\left(\epsilon+{\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)-{\bm{y}}_{j}(d)+{\bm{w}}_{d}^{\top}\frac{\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}}_{j})}{h({\bm{x}}_{j})}+{\bm{x}}_{j}+{\bm{b}}(d)\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d)\left(\epsilon+\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)+{\bm{y}}_{j}(d)-{\bm{w}}_{d}^{\top}\frac{\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}}_{j})}{h({\bm{x}}_{j})}-{\bm{x}}_{j}-{\bm{b}}(d)\right),

Similar to the derivation in Section
2.1
, the partial derivatives of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
with respect to the primal variable
{\bm{w}}_{d}
,
d=1,\ldots,D_{v}
, have to vanish for optimality, which leads to

\displaystyle\partial_{{\bm{w}}_{d}}\mathcal{L}_{1}

\displaystyle={\bm{w}}_{d}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}({\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d))\frac{\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}}_{j})}{h({\bm{x}}_{j})}=0\Rightarrow{\bm{w}}_{d}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}({\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d))\frac{\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}}_{j})}{h({\bm{x}}_{j})}.

(35)

Note that here we only find the form of the optimal solution for
{\mathbf{W}}=[{\bm{w}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{w}}_{D_{v}}]^{\top}
. The optimal value of
{\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}
can then be found by optimization algorithm such as the (stochastic) gradient descent when training the transformer.

Let
{\bm{v}}_{j}=[\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(1)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(1)}{h({\bm{x}}_{j})},\dots,\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(D_{v})-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(D_{v})}{h({\bm{x}}_{j})}]^{\top}
,
j=1,\dots,N
, we obtain the following support vector expansion of the function
f
:

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})=\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N}({\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(1)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(1))\frac{\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}}_{j})}{h({\bm{x}}_{j})},\dots,\sum_{j=1}^{N}({\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(D_{v})-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(D_{v}))\frac{\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}}_{j})}{h({\bm{x}}_{j})}\right]^{\top}\frac{\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}})}{{h({\bm{x}})}}+{\bm{x}}+{\bm{b}},

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(1)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(1)}{h({\bm{x}}_{j})}\frac{\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}})^{\top}\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}}_{j})}{{h({\bm{x}})}},\dots,\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(D_{v})-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(D_{v})}{h({\bm{x}}_{j})}\frac{\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}})^{\top}\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}}_{j})}{{h({\bm{x}})}}\right]^{\top}+{\bm{x}}+{\bm{b}},

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}})^{\top}\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}}_{j})}{{{h({\bm{x}})}}}{\bm{v}}_{j}+{\bm{x}}}_{\text{Residual connection}}+{\bm{b}}.

(36)

Here, the support vector expansion of
f
already includes a residual connection. The softmax attention can then be derived by selecting
h({\bm{x}}):=\sum_{j}^{N}\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}})^{T}\Phi({\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}}_{j})
and choosing
\Phi
as in Eqn.
28
in Section
2.1
. Note that in Eqn.
36
,
\{{\bm{x}}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
and
{\bm{x}}
are the training samples and test sample, respectively. In order to derive the key, query, and value matrix projections in attention, we can then relax Eqn.
36
by letting
{\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}}_{j}={\mathbf{W}}_{K}{\bm{x}}_{j}
,
{\mathbf{W}}^{\text{proj}}{\bm{x}}={\mathbf{W}}_{Q}{\bm{x}}
,
{\bm{v}}_{j}={\mathbf{W}}_{V}{\bm{x}}_{j}
and choosing the test sample
{\bm{x}}
among the training samples
\{{\bm{x}}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
.

Remark 6

Here, for self-attention, we choose the test sample
{\bm{x}}
among the training samples
\{{\bm{x}}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
to compute the attention score of a token to other tokens in the same sequence. For cross-attention where a token in a sequence attends to tokens in another sequence, this constraint can be removed.

Appendix G
2D-Convolution Attention

In this section, we discuss attention with 2D-convolution. Suppose we are given a training data
\{({\bm{x}}^{train}_{1},{\bm{y}}^{train}_{1}),\dots,({\bm{x}}^{train}_{N_{H}\times N_{W}},{\bm{y}}^{train}_{N_{H}\times N_{W}})\}\subset\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
, where
\mathcal{X}={\mathbb{R}}^{D_{x}}
and
\mathcal{Y}={\mathbb{R}}^{D_{v}}
. Here,
{\bm{x}}^{train}_{1},\dots,{\bm{x}}^{train}_{N_{H}\times N_{W}}
are the training inputs, and
{\bm{y}}^{train}_{1},\dots,{\bm{y}}^{train}_{N_{H}\times N_{W}}
are the training targets. Let
{\mathbf{X}}^{train}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}\times D_{x}}
be the 3D-tensor of training inputs, where
{\mathbf{X}}^{train}(h,w,d)={\bm{x}}^{train}_{N_{W}\times(h-1)+w}(d)
. Given a new set of inputs
\{{\bm{x}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{x}}_{N_{H}\times N_{W}}\}\subset\mathcal{X}
and the corresponding 3D-tensor
{\mathbf{X}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}\times D_{x}}
of these inputs, where
{\mathbf{X}}(h,w,d)={\bm{x}}_{N_{W}\times(h-1)+w}(d)
. We consider the function
f
applying on the 3D-tensor
{\mathbf{X}}
and taking the following form

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}}_{i})={\mathbf{W}}\frac{\Phi(\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}({\mathbf{X}},s))(i))}{h({\bm{x}}_{i})},\,\,i=1,\dots,N_{H}\times N_{W}

(37)

where
Conv2D
is the depth-wise 2D-convolution
(
Howard et al., 2017
)
, with the kernel size
s\times s
and identical kernel channels, applied on the input tensor
{\mathbf{X}}
. Here, the last dimension of
{\mathbf{X}}
, i.e.,
D_{x}
, is the depth. Also,
\Phi({\bm{x}})=[\phi_{1}({\bm{x}}),\dots,\phi_{D_{\phi}}({\bm{x}})]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D_{\phi}}
,
{\mathbf{W}}=[{\bm{w}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{w}}_{D_{v}}]^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D_{v}\times D_{\phi}}
,
{\bm{b}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D_{v}}
, and
h
is a vector-scalar function. We fit the function
f
to the training data
\{({\bm{x}}^{train}_{1},{\bm{y}}^{train}_{1}),\dots,({\bm{x}}^{train}_{N_{H}\times N_{W}},{\bm{y}}^{train}_{N_{H}\times N_{W}})\}
with an
\mathbb{L}_{2}
regularization on
{\mathbf{W}}
, i.e., a ridge regression, by solving the following convex optimization problem:

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{W}}\\
{\bm{\xi}}_{j},\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j},j=1,\ldots,N_{H}\times N_{W}\end{subarray}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|^{2}_{\text{F}}+C\sum_{j=1}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\left({\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)+\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\|{\bm{w}}_{d}\|^{2}+C\sum_{j=1}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\left({\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)+\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\right)

(38)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{\bm{y}}^{train}_{j}(d)-{\bm{w}}_{d}^{\top}\ \frac{\displaystyle\Phi(\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}({\mathbf{X}}^{train},s))(j))}{\displaystyle h({\bm{x}}^{train}_{j})}-{\bm{b}}(d)\leq\epsilon+{\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)\\
{\bm{w}}_{d}^{\top}\ \frac{\displaystyle\Phi(\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}({\mathbf{X}}^{train},s))(j))}{\displaystyle h({\bm{x}}^{train}_{j})}+{\bm{b}}(d)-{\bm{y}}^{train}_{j}(d)\leq\epsilon+\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\\
{\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d),\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\geq 0,\;j=1,\ldots,N_{H}\times N_{W},\;d=1,\ldots,D_{v}.\end{cases}

The Lagrangian of the optimization problem
38
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\|{\bm{w}}_{d}\|^{2}+C\sum_{j=1}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\left({\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)+\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\left({\bm{\eta}}_{j}(d){\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)+\tilde{{\bm{\eta}}}_{j}(d)\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)\right)

(39)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d)\left(\epsilon+{\bm{\xi}}_{j}(d)-{\bm{y}}^{train}_{j}(d)+{\bm{w}}_{d}^{\top}\ \frac{\displaystyle\Phi(\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}({\mathbf{X}}^{train},s))(j))}{\displaystyle h({\bm{x}}^{train}_{j})}+{\bm{b}}(d)\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{v}}\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d)\left(\epsilon+\tilde{{\bm{\xi}}}_{j}(d)+{\bm{y}}^{train}_{j}(d)-{\bm{w}}_{d}^{\top}\ \frac{\displaystyle\Phi(\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}({\mathbf{X}}^{train},s))(j))}{\displaystyle h({\bm{x}}^{train}_{j})}-{\bm{b}}(d)\right),

Similar to the derivation in Section
2.1
in the main text, the partial derivatives of
\mathcal{L}
with respect to the primal variable
{\bm{w}}_{d}
,
d=1,\ldots,D_{v}
, have to vanish for optimality, which leads to

\displaystyle\partial_{{\bm{w}}_{d}}\mathcal{L}={\bm{w}}_{d}-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}}({\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d))\frac{\displaystyle\Phi(\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}({\mathbf{X}}^{train},s))(j))}{\displaystyle h({\bm{x}}^{train}_{j})}=0

(40)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow{\bm{w}}_{d}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}}({\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(d)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(d))\frac{\displaystyle\Phi(\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}({\mathbf{X}}^{train},s))(j))}{\displaystyle h({\bm{x}}^{train}_{j})}.

(41)

Let
{\bm{v}}_{j}=[\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(1)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(1)}{h({\bm{x}}^{train}_{j})},\dots,\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(D_{v})-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(D_{v})}{h({\bm{x}}^{train}_{j})}]^{\top}
,
j=1,\dots,N_{H}\times N_{W}
, and substitute Eqn.
41
into Eqn.
38
, we obtain the following support vector expansion of the linear basis function
f
:

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}}_{i})

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}}\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(1)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(1)}{h({\bm{x}}^{train}_{j})}\frac{{\mathbf{A}}_{ij}}{{h({\bm{x}}_{i})}},\dots,\sum_{j=1}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}}\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}(D_{v})-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}_{j}(D_{v})}{h({\bm{x}}^{train}_{j})}\frac{{\mathbf{A}}_{ij}}{{h({\bm{x}}_{i})}}\right]^{\top}+{\bm{b}},

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}}\frac{{\mathbf{A}}_{ij}}{{{h({\bm{x}}_{i})}}}{\bm{v}}_{j}+{\bm{b}},

(42)

where
{\mathbf{A}}_{ij}:=\Phi(\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}({\mathbf{X}},s))(i))^{\top}\Phi(\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}({\mathbf{X}}^{train},s))(j))
.

Same as in Section
2.1
, we set
{\bm{b}}_{s}=0
. To derive the softmax normalization in attention, we choose
h({\bm{x}}_{i}):=\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\mathbf{A}}_{ij}
and select
\Phi
as in Eqn.
28
. Let the training inputs
\{{\bm{x}}^{train}_{1},\dots,{\bm{x}}^{train}_{N_{H}\times N_{W}}\}\subset\mathcal{X}
be the attention keys
\{{\bm{k}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{k}}_{N_{H}\times N_{W}}\}\subset\mathcal{K}
, where
\mathcal{K}={\mathbb{R}}^{D}
, in self-attention. Also, let the new inputs
\{{\bm{x}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{x}}_{N_{H}\times N_{W}}\}\subset\mathcal{X}
be the attention queries
\{{\bm{q}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{q}}_{N_{H}\times N_{W}}\}\subset\mathcal{K}
in self-attention. We define the 2D-Convolution Attention (Attention-Conv2D) as follows:

Definition 3
(2D-Convolution Attention)

Given a set of the key and value vectors
\{{\bm{k}}_{j},{\bm{v}}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}}
, and a set of the query vectors
\{{\bm{q}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}}
. Denote the key tensor and the query tensor by
{\mathbf{K}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}\times D}
and
{\mathbf{Q}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{H}\times N_{W}\times D}
, respectively, where
{\mathbf{K}}(h,w,d)={\bm{k}}_{N_{W}\times(h-1)+w}(d)
and
{\mathbf{Q}}(h,w,d)={\bm{q}}_{N_{W}\times(h-1)+w}(d)
. The 2D-Convolution Attention (Attention-Conv2D) computes the corresponding output vector
{\bm{h}}_{i}
of the query
{\bm{q}}_{i}
by the following attention formula:

\displaystyle{\bm{h}}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\text{softmax}\left(\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}({\mathbf{Q}},s))(i)^{\top}\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}({\mathbf{K}},s))(j)/\sqrt{D}\right){\bm{v}}_{j},

(43)

where the
Conv2D(\cdot,s)
is the depth-wise 2D-convolution
(
Howard et al., 2017
)
with the kernel size
s\times s
and identical kernel channels.

Remark 7
(Convolutional Projection for Attention in the Convolutional vision Transformer)

The convolutional projections used in the Convolutional vision Transformer (CvT)
(
Wu et al., 2021
)
can be derived from Eqn.
42
by letting the training input tensor
{\mathbf{X}}^{train}
to be the 2D input matrix of size
N\times D_{x}
of the self-attention layer (see Section
1.1
in the main text) reshaped into a 3D tensor of size
N_{H}\times N_{W}\times D_{x}
where
N=N_{H}\times N_{W}
. Here, to avoid confusion, we denote the input of the self-attention layer by
{\mathbf{X}}^{input}
and its reshaped version by
\text{Reshape2D}({\mathbf{X}}^{input})
. We then replace the depth-wise 2D-convolution by the depth-wise separable 2D-convolution in
(
Wu et al., 2021
)
and remove the constraint that the kernels have identical channels. In order to derive the convolutional projections for the keys, queries, and values in CvT, for
i,j=1,\dots,N
, we let

\displaystyle{\bm{k}}_{j}

\displaystyle=\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}({\mathbf{X}}^{train},s))(j))=\Phi(\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}(\text{Reshape2D}({\mathbf{X}}^{input}),s,{\mathbf{W}}_{K}))(j),

\displaystyle{\bm{q}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}({\mathbf{X}},s))(i))=\Phi(\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}(\text{Reshape2D}({\mathbf{X}}^{input}),s,{\mathbf{W}}_{Q}))(i),

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}_{j}

\displaystyle=\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}({\mathbf{X}}^{train},s))(j))=\Phi(\text{Flatten}(\text{Conv2D}(\text{Reshape2D}({\mathbf{X}}^{input}),s,{\mathbf{W}}_{V}))(j).

(44)

Here, we specify the kernel/filter
{\mathbf{W}}_{K}
,
{\mathbf{W}}_{Q}
, and
{\mathbf{W}}_{V}
to emphasize that the convolutional projections in CvT uses different kernels to compute keys, queries, and values in self-attention. Eqn.
44
matches the convolutional projects in CvT. By choosing
h
and
\Phi
similar to above, we can derive the convolutional attention in CvT.

Appendix H
1D-Convolution Attention

Following the derivation for the Attention-Conv2D in Appendix
G
above, we can derive the 1D-Convolution Attention (Attention-Conv1D) in a similar way by letting
{\mathbf{X}}^{train}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D_{x}}
and
{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D_{x}}
be 2D-matrices of training inputs and new inputs, respectively, and by replacing
Conv2D
by
Conv1D
, which is the depth-wise 1D-convolution, with the kernel size
s\times 1
and identical kernel channels, applied on the input tensor
{\mathbf{X}}
. Here, the last dimension of
{\mathbf{X}}
, i.e.,
D_{x}
, is the depth. We define the 1D-Convolution Attention (Attention-Conv1D) as follows:

Definition 4
(1D-Convolution Attention)

Given a set of the key and value vectors
\{{\bm{k}}_{j},{\bm{v}}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
, and a set of the query vectors
\{{\bm{q}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
. Denote the key matrix and the query matrix by

, respectively. The 1D-Convolution Attention (Attention-Conv1D) computes the corresponding output vector
{\bm{h}}_{i}
of the query
{\bm{q}}_{i}
by the following attention formula:

\displaystyle{\bm{h}}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\text{softmax}\left(\text{Conv1D}({\mathbf{Q}},s)(i)^{\top}\text{Conv1D}({\mathbf{K}},s)(j)/\sqrt{D}\right){\bm{v}}_{j},

(45)

where the
Conv1D(\cdot,s)
is the depth-wise 1D-convolution with the kernel size
s\times 1
and identical kernel channels.

Appendix I
Attention with Batch Normalization and scaled heads

The Attention-BN+SH combines both the Attention-BN and Attention-SH. The Attention-BN+SH fits the function
f^{s}
,
s=1,\dots,H
, in Eqn.
17
with training sets
\{({\bm{k}}^{1}_{1},{\bm{y}}^{1}_{1}),\dots,({\bm{k}}^{1}_{N_{1}},{\bm{y}}^{1}_{N_{1}})\},\dots,\{({\bm{k}}^{H}_{1},{\bm{y}}^{H}_{1}),\dots,({\bm{k}}^{H}_{N_{H}},{\bm{y}}^{H}_{N_{H}})\}\subset\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{Y}
of different sizes
N_{1},\dots,N_{H}
, where
\mathcal{K}={\mathbb{R}}^{D}
and
\mathcal{Y}={\mathbb{R}}^{D_{v}}
. The function
f^{s}
is defined as:

\displaystyle f^{s}({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle:={\mathbf{W}}^{s}\frac{\Phi(({\bm{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}^{s})\odot\textbf{s}^{s^{-1}})}{h^{s}(({\bm{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}^{s})\odot\textbf{s}^{s^{-1}})}+{\bm{b}}^{s},

(46)

where

\displaystyle{\bm{\mu}}^{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}{\bm{k}}^{s}_{j},\,\,\textbf{s}^{s^{-1}}=\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma^{s^{2}}_{1}+\epsilon}},\dots,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma^{s^{2}}_{D}+\epsilon}}\right]^{\top},\,\,\sigma^{s^{2}}_{d}=\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}({\bm{k}}^{s}_{j}(d)-{\bm{\mu}}^{s}(d))^{2}.

(47)

Following the same derivation as in Section
2.1
, we derive the following support vector expansion of
f^{s}

\displaystyle f^{s}({\bm{x}})=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\frac{\Phi(({\bm{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}^{s})\odot\textbf{s}^{s^{-1}})^{\top}\Phi(({\bm{k}}^{s}_{j}-{\bm{\mu}}^{s})\odot\textbf{s}^{s^{-1}})}{{{h^{s}(({\bm{x}}-{\bm{\mu}}^{s})\odot\textbf{s}^{s^{-1}})}}}{\bm{v}}^{s}_{j}+{\bm{b}}^{s}.

(48)

Here,
{\bm{v}}^{s}_{j}=\left[\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}^{s}_{j}(1)-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}^{s}_{j}(1)}{h^{s}(({\bm{k}}^{s}_{j}-{\bm{\mu}}^{s})\odot\textbf{s}^{s^{-1}})},\dots,\frac{{\bm{\alpha}}^{s}_{j}(D_{v})-\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}^{s}_{j}(D_{v})}{h^{s}(({\bm{k}}^{s}_{j}-{\bm{\mu}}^{s})\odot\textbf{s}^{{s^{-1}}})}\right]^{\top}
, where
{\bm{\alpha}}^{s}_{j}
and
\tilde{{\bm{\alpha}}}^{s}_{j}
are the dual variables,
j=1,\dots,N
. Same as in Section
2.1
, in Eqn.
48
, we choose
\Phi
as in Eqn.
28
,
h^{s}({\bm{x}}):=\sum_{j}^{N_{s}}\Phi({\bm{x}})^{T}\Phi({\bm{k}}^{s}_{j})
, and
{\bm{b}}^{s}=0
to obtain the Batch Normalized Attention with Scaled Heads (Attention-BN+SH), which is defined as follows:

Definition 5
(Batch Normalized Attention with Scaled Heads)

Given
H
sets of the key and value vectors
\{{\bm{k}}^{1}_{j},{\bm{v}}^{1}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{1}},\dots,\{{\bm{k}}^{H}_{j},{\bm{v}}^{H}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{H}}
, for each set of H query vectors
{\bm{q}}^{1}_{i},\dots,{\bm{q}}^{H}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,N
, the Batch Normalized Attention with Scaled Heads (Attention-BN+SH) computes the corresponding output vector
{\bm{h}}_{i}
of the queries
{\bm{q}}^{1}_{i},\dots,{\bm{q}}^{H}_{i}
by the following attention formula:

\displaystyle{\bm{h}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{H}{\mathbf{W}}^{s}_{O}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\text{softmax}\left((({\bm{q}}^{s}_{i}-{\bm{\mu}}^{s})\odot\textbf{s}^{s^{-1}})^{\top}(({\bm{k}}^{s}_{j}-{\bm{\mu}}^{s})\odot\textbf{s}^{s^{-1}})/\sqrt{D}\right){\bm{v}}^{s}_{j}\right),

(49)

where

\displaystyle{\bm{\mu}}^{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}{\bm{k}}^{s}_{j},\,\,\textbf{s}^{s^{-1}}=\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma^{s^{2}}_{1}+\epsilon}},\dots,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sigma^{s^{2}}_{D}+\epsilon}}\right]^{\top},\,\,\sigma^{s^{2}}_{d}=\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}({\bm{k}}^{s}_{j}(d)-{\bm{\mu}}^{s}(d))^{2}.

(50)

Following the same Remark
5
in Section
2.3.2
, given input sequence
{\mathbf{X}}:=[{\bm{x}}_{1},\cdots,{\bm{x}}_{N}]^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times D_{x}}
of
N
feature vectors in self-attention, in order to generate the sets of
\{{\bm{k}}^{s}_{j},{\bm{v}}^{s}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{s}}
at the scale
s^{th}
, we can downsample the input
{\mathbf{X}}
before projecting into the key matrix
{\mathbf{K}}
and the value matrix
{\mathbf{V}}
. In this paper, we use the average-pooling to downsample
{\mathbf{X}}
.

As in the same case of Attention-BN, for Attention-BN+SH, recentering queries and keys alone are sufficient for accuracy improvement, and we weight the mean
{\bm{\mu}}
in Eqn
49
with a constant
\beta
. Hence Eqn.
49
is simplified to:

\displaystyle{\bm{h}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{H}{\mathbf{W}}^{s}_{O}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\text{softmax}\left(({\bm{q}}^{s}_{i}-\beta{\bm{\mu}}^{s})^{\top}({\bm{k}}^{s}_{j}-\beta{\bm{\mu}}^{s})/\sqrt{D}\right){\bm{v}}^{s}_{j}\right).

(51)

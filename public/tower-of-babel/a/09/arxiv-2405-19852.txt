Harmonic K K -quasiconformal Koebe functions: construction and application to Pavlović’s problem
Zhi-Gang Wang, Xiao-Yuan Wang, Antti Rasila, and Jia-Le Qiu

Abstract
. We first construct the harmonic K K -quasiconformal Koebe functions, filling a long-standing foundational gap in geometric function theory. This construction provides a unified parametric candidate extremal function framework for conformal mappings, quasiconformal mappings, and harmonic mappings, and we formulate related conjectures for the extremal theory of harmonic K K -quasiconformal mappings. By combining this construction with Astala and Koskela’s H p H^{p} -theory for quasiconformal mappings, we establish a sharp result concerning the optimal order of harmonic K K -quasiconformal mappings with bounded Schwarzian norm in harmonic Hardy spaces. Motivated by the work of Chuaqui, Hernández, and Martín’s [Math. Ann. 367, 1099–1122 (2017)], this result gives a partial solution to Pavlović’s 2014 open problem on the embeddings of harmonic quasiconformal mappings into Hardy spaces, and outlines a path toward its complete solution.

31A05, 30C55, 30C62, 30H10.

1.
Introduction and statement of the main result

Following the seminal work of Clunie and Sheil-Small
[
9
]
, univalent harmonic mappings have emerged as a central topic at the interface of complex analysis, differential geometry, geometric topology, Teichmüller theory, nonlinear dynamics, and numerical analysis. Despite remarkable progress over recent decades, several fundamental problems remain strikingly open. Most notably, sharp analogues of Bieberbach’s conjecture (de Branges’s theorem
[
11
]
) and the Koebe one-quarter covering theorem, cornerstones of classical conformal mapping theory, still lack definitive solutions in the harmonic quasiconformal setting. Within geometric function theory, a long-standing bottleneck persists: there exists no effective framework for constructing and rigorously establishing Koebe-type conjectures for harmonic
K
-quasiconformal (
HQC
) mappings. This critical gap has severely hampered the systematic development of extremal theory and canonical example construction in the field.

Linear properties, the maximum modulus principle, qualitative results in quasiconformal geometry and the like extend trivially to the setting of harmonic quasiconformal geometric function theory; however, the essence of classical geometric function theory, centered on extremal functions, coefficient conjectures, sharp distortion inequalities and Loewner theory, cannot be extended trivially at all, and a completely new theoretical framework must be established. This is precisely the core value and research significance of harmonic quasiconformal geometric function theory.

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the class of complex-valued harmonic functions

f=h+\overline{g}
in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, normalized by the conditions

f(0)=f_{z}(0)-1=0,

which have the
form

(1.1)

f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}
the class of normalized analytic functions, which is a subclass of

\mathcal{H}
with
g(z)\equiv 0
.
Let
\mathcal{S_{\mathcal{H}}}
be the class of harmonic functions
f\in\mathcal{H}

that are univalent and sense-preserving in
\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, we denote by
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}
the subclass of
\mathcal{S_{\mathcal{H}}}
with the additional condition
f_{\overline{z}}(0)=0
. Since there exist reciprocal transformations between the classes
\mathcal{S_{\mathcal{H}}}

and
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}
, we usually focus our attention on the class
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}
. Clearly, the classical class
\mathcal{S}
of normalized univalent analytic functions is a subclass of the class

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
.

1.1.
Harmonic quasiconformal mappings

We say that
f
belongs to the class
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
of
HQC
mappings, where
K\geq 1
is a constant, if
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
and its dilatation defined by

\omega:=\frac{g^{\prime}}{h^{\prime}}

satisfies the condition

\left|\omega\right|\leq k
, where
k\in[0,1)
is given by

k:=\frac{K-1}{K+1}\quad(K\geq 1).

A function
f
is called a harmonic quasiconformal mapping, if it belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
for some
K\geq 1
.

For convenience, we define

\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(K):=\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)\cap\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}.

Note that

\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(1)=\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(1)=\mathcal{S}.

In recent years,
Kalaj
[
18
]
obtained the extended form of Lindelöf theorem for harmonic quasiconformal mappings.
Kalaj
[

]
established sharp Riesz-type inequalities for harmonic mappings on the open unit disk in the complex plane.
Wang, Shi, and Jiang
[

]
investigated harmonic quasiconformal mappings associated with asymmetric vertical strips.
Sun, Rasila, and Jiang
[
30
]
considered linear combinations of harmonic quasiconformal mappings convex
in one direction.
Chuaqui, Hernández, and Martín
[
8
]

studied the affine and linear invariant families of
harmonic mappings, and obtained the sharp order of harmonic mappings with bounded Schwarzian norm.
Partyka, Sakan, and Zhu
[
25
]
provided various properties of harmonic quasiconformal mappings with the
convex holomorphic part.
Liu and Zhu
[
22
]
studied the Riesz conjugate functions theorem for harmonic quasiconformal
mappings. Todorčević’s monograph
[
31
]
gives recent developments on higher-dimensional harmonic quasiconformal mappings and hyperbolic type metrics.

1.2.
Hardy spaces for harmonic and quasiconformal mappings

Let
0<p\leq\infty
. A complex-valued function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
is said to be in the
Hardy space
for
p
, if the integral mean

M_{p}(r,f):=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|f(re^{i\theta})\right|^{p}d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&(0<p<\infty),\\
\ \ \sup\limits_{{\left|z\right|=r}}\left|f(z)\right|&(p=\infty),\end{cases}

is defined for all
r\in(0,1)
, and

\sup\limits_{0<r<1}M_{p}(r,f)<\infty.

We record the following special cases of this definition. The classical Hardy space of analytic functions is denoted by
H^{p}

and the one of
K
-quasiconformal mappings
[
4
]
is denoted by
H^{p}_{K}
. Similarly, we say that a harmonic function
f
is in a
harmonic Hardy space

h^{p}
, if it is harmonic and satisfies the above condition. We denote the space of
HQC
mappings by
h^{p}_{K}
.

1.3.
The order of a Hardy space

For a class
\mathcal{G}
of complex-valued functions in
\mathbb{D}
, we define its
order
as a constant
\gamma\geq 0
such that
\mathcal{G}
is contained the corresponding Hardy space
H^{p}
for all
p\in(0,\gamma)
.

We recall the following special case of the result due to Astala and Koskela
[
4
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Theorem A
.

If
f
is a
K
-quasiconformal mapping in
\mathbb{D}
, then
f\in H^{p}_{K}
for

0<p<\frac{1}{2K}\quad(K\geq 1),

and the constant
1/(2K)
is sharp.

In 1990, Abu-Muhanna and Lyzzaik
[
2
, Theorem 2]
established the following result showing that for a harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}
, the functions
h
and
g
belong to the Hardy space
H^{p}
, and
f
belongs to harmonic Hardy spaces
h^{p}
, respectively, for a suitable choice of
p
.

Theorem B
.

If
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
, then
h,\,g\in H^{p}
and
f\in h^{p}
for every
p
with

0<p<\frac{1}{(2\beta+2)^{2}},

where

\beta:=\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}\left|a_{2}\right|.

In 1996, the order

\frac{1}{(2\beta+2)^{2}}

in Theorem
B
was improved to
1/\beta^{2}
by Nowak in
[
24
, Theorem 1]
, where she conjectured that the order is actually
1/\beta
. Moreover, she obtained the sharp results

f\in h^{p}\quad\left(0<p<\frac{1}{2}\right)

for the class
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{H}}
of convex harmonic mappings, and

f\in h^{p}\quad\left(0<p<\frac{1}{3}\right)

for the class
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{H}}
of close-to-convex harmonic mappings. Even today, proving Nowak’s conjecture is seen as a

challenging
problem.

We also note that Aleman and Martín
[
3
]
proved that convex harmonic mappings are not necessarily in
h^{1/2}
, which provided a negative answer
to a question raised by Duren
[
15
, p. 153, Sect. 8.5]
. Laitila, Nieminen, Saksman, and Tylli
[
20
]
considered the rigidity of composition operators involving harmonic Hardy spaces
h^{p}
. Melentijević and Božin
[
23
]
obtained sharp Riesz-Fejér inequality for harmonic Hardy spaces
h^{p}
.

An analytic function
h
in
\mathbb{D}
has valency
n
if
h
takes no value
more than
n
times. More generally, for a function
h
analytic in
\mathbb{D}
, let
W(R)
denote
the area (regions covered multiply being counted multiply) of the image of
\mathbb{D}
under

h
that lies in the closed disk
\left|w\right|\leq R
. If
W(R)\leq n\pi R^{2}
for all
R>0
, where
n
is a positive number, then we say that
h
has mean valency
n
(cf.
[
10
]
).

In 2024, Das and Sairam Kaliraj
[
10
, Theorem 1]

proved that Nowak’s conjecture is true with the additional condition that
h^{\prime}
has a
finite mean valency
. Noting that the conjectured
\beta
of the class
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
is equal to
3
(see
[
9
]
), and it is consequently conjectured by Duren
[
15
, p. 152, Sect. 8.5]
that

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}\subset h^{{1}/{3}}.

1.4.
Affine and linear invariant families

For
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
, following the terminology of
[
29
]
, a family is said to be an
affine
and linear invariant family
(AL family) if it is closed under both the
Koebe transform

K_{\zeta}(f)(z):=\frac{f\left((z+\zeta)/\left(1+\overline{\zeta}z\right)\right)-f(\zeta)}{\left(1-\left|\zeta\right|^{2}\right)h^{\prime}(\zeta)}\quad(\left|\zeta\right|<1)

and
affine changes

A_{\varepsilon}(f)(z):=\frac{f(z)-\overline{\varepsilon f(z)}}{1-\overline{\varepsilon}g^{\prime}(0)}\quad(\left|\varepsilon\right|<1).

In 1990, Sheil-Small
[
29
]
offered an in-depth study of affine and linear invariant families
\mathcal{F}
of
harmonic mappings
f=h+\overline{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
. The
order
of an AL family is given by

\alpha(\mathcal{F}):=\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\left|a_{2}(f)\right|=\frac{1}{2}\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\left|h^{\prime\prime}(0)\right|.

Indeed, many important characterizations of the AL family
\mathcal{F}
are determined
by its
order
. Clearly, the class
\mathcal{S_{\mathcal{H}}}
is a typical example of the affine and linear invariant families.

1.5.
Harmonic pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives

The classical pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of locally univalent analytic functions
h
(cf.
[
14
]
),
which are, respectively, defined by

P_{h}(z):=\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}

and

S_{h}(z):=\left(\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{2}.

For a locally univalent sense-preserving harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
, Hernández and Martín
[
17
]
introduced
the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of
f
as follows:

P_{f}:=\left(\log J_{f}\right)_{z}=P_{h}-\frac{\omega^{\prime}\,\overline{\omega}}{1-\left|\omega\right|^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{f}&:=\left(\log J_{f}\right)_{zz}-\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\log J_{f}\right)_{z}\right]^{2}\\
&\,\,=S_{h}+\frac{\overline{\omega}}{1-\left|\omega\right|^{2}}\left(\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{h^{\prime}}\omega^{\prime}-\omega^{\prime\prime}\right)-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\omega^{\prime}\,\overline{\omega}}{1-\left|\omega\right|^{2}}\right)^{2},\end{split}

where

J_{f}:=\left|f_{z}\right|^{2}-\left|f_{\overline{z}}\right|^{2}

is the Jacobian of
f
.
The corresponding pre-Schwarzian norm
\|P_{f}\|
and Schwarzian norm

\|S_{f}\|
of
f
are defined, respectively, by

\|P_{f}\|:=\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left|P_{f}(z)\right|\left(1-\left|z\right|^{2}\right)

and

\|S_{f}\|:=\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left|S_{f}(z)\right|\left(1-\left|z\right|^{2}\right)^{2}.

We note that Chuaqui, Duren, and Osgood
[
7
]
have given an alternative definition of harmonic Schwarzian derivative
with restriction on its dilatation.

1.6.
A problem of Pavlović

Because univalent harmonic mappings are not necessarily
quasiconformal
, the constant
1/(2K)
in Theorem
A
may not be sharp for the class of harmonic
K
-quasiconformal mappings. Based on Theorems
A
and
B
, Pavlović
[
26
, p. 315, Problem 10.2]
in 2014 proposed the following natural problem.

Problem 1
.

Find the sharp

p_{0}=p_{0}(K)

such that every harmonic
K
-quasiconformal mapping belongs to
h^{p}_{K}
for
p<p_{0}
.

Remark 1
.

One of keys to solve the above problem is determination of
\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)}\left|a_{2}\right|
of the family
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
.
But, at present, it seems that this is a
hard
problem (see
[
6
, Proposition 1.6]
and
Conjecture
3
in Sect.
3
).

Curiously, Das, Huang, and Rasila
[
12
, Theorem 3]
established the optimal orders for convex and close-to-convex harmonic
K
-quasiconformal mappings to belong to harmonic Hardy spaces, respectively; notably, these results are independent of the quasiconformal constant
K
, i.e.,

f\in h^{p}\quad\left(0<p<1\right)

for the class
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
of
convex harmonic quasiconformal mappings, and

f\in h^{p}\quad\left(0<p<\frac{1}{2}\right)

for the class
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
of
close-to-convex harmonic quasiconformal mappings.

Note that Ponnusamy, Qiao, and Wang
[
28
, Theorem 4.2]
also obtained a sharp result regarding uniformly locally univalent harmonic quasiconformal mappings belong to harmonic Hardy space.

Denote by
\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K)
the subclass of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
, where
h^{\prime}
has a
finite mean valency
. We now give the following characteristic of the class
\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K)
, which will play a crucial role in our study.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose that
f=h+\overline{g}\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K)
. If

\phi(K):=\sup\limits_{f\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K)}\left|a_{2}\right|.

Then

(1)

for
\phi(K)\leq 2K
, we have

f\in h^{p}\quad\left(0<p<\frac{1}{2K}\right);

(2)

for
\phi(K)>2K
, we have

f\in h^{p}\quad\left(0<p<\frac{1}{\phi(K)}\right).

Both of these bounds are sharp.

Proof.

By the fact that
\phi(K)
is a real function with respect to
K
,

\phi(K)\geq 1
for
K\geq 1
(see
[
27
]
), using the similar method as in Das and Sairam Kaliraj
[
10
, Theorem 1]
,
we confirm that if
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K)
, then
f\in h^{p}
for

0<p<\frac{1}{\phi(K)}.

Note that, by Theorem
A
and taking the value of

\min\limits_{K\geq 1}\left\{\frac{1}{\phi(K)},\ \frac{1}{2K}\right\}.

We obtain the assertion of
Proposition
1
.
∎

For convenience, we denote the subclass of
{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K)
(resp.
\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K)
) with

\|S_{f}\|\leq\lambda

by
{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)
(resp.
\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)
), where
K\geq 1
and
\lambda\geq 0
. Now, we establish the following relationship between
the family
\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)
of
HQC
mappings with bounded Schwarzian norm and harmonic Hardy spaces
h^{p}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)
with
K\geq 1
and
\lambda\geq 0
. Then

(1)

for
0\leq\lambda\leq 6
and
K\geq 1
, we have

f\in h^{p}\quad\left(0<p<\frac{1}{2K}\right);

(2)

for
\lambda>6
and
K\geq K_{1}>1
, we have

f\in h^{p}\quad\left(0<p<\frac{1}{2K}\right);

(3)

for
\lambda>6
and
1\leq K<K_{1}
, we have

f\in h^{p}\quad\left(0<p<\frac{1}{\varphi(K,\lambda)}\right),

where

(1.2)

\varphi(K,\lambda):=\sup\limits_{f\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)}\left|a_{2}\right|=\sqrt{1+\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{K-1}{K+1}\right)^{2}}+\frac{K-1}{2\left({K+1}\right)},

and

K_{1}
is the unique solution in
(1,+\infty)
of the equation

(1.3)

16K^{4}+24K^{3}-(2\lambda-11)K^{2}-2(2\lambda-1)K-2\lambda-5=0.

All of these results are sharp.

Remark 2
.

For
K=1
and
0\leq\lambda\leq 6
,
the first part of Theorem
1
matches the classical analytic case, as originally examined by Pommerenke
[
27
]
in 1964.

This paper employs the weighted shearing technique to construct the first harmonic
K
-quasiconformal Koebe functions for the class, resolving a long-standing core open problem in the field. The constructed harmonic quasiconformal Koebe-type function serves as a candidate extremal function for this class, and a rigorous proof for the corresponding coefficient conjecture remains elusive to date, its difficulty being analogous to that of the classical Bieberbach conjecture. This construction enables the systematic extension of classical results from conformal mapping theory to the harmonic quasiconformal setting, thereby inaugurating a new systematic research program in geometric function theory.
We also establish the sharp order for harmonic
K
-quasiconformal mappings with bounded Schwarzian norm. Furthermore, employing a fundamental result on the
H^{p}
theory for quasiconformal mappings due to Astala and Koskela
[
4
]
, we determine the optimal order for the family of harmonic
K
-quasiconformal mappings with bounded Schwarzian norm that lie in harmonic Hardy spaces. This partially resolves an open problem posed by Pavlovi’c
[
26
]
in 2014.

2.1.
Estimation of
\left|a_{2}\right|
of a univalent harmonic mapping

The problem of determining the sharp upper bound of
|{a_{2}}|
in the class

\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}

is still an
open
problem. Clunie and Sheil-Small
[
9
]
once proved that

\left|a_{2}\right|<12172.

Later, Sheil-Small
[
29
]
improved it to

\left|a_{2}\right|<57.

The estimate

\left|a_{2}\right|<49

was
proved by Duren
[
15
, p. 96, Sect. 6.3]
.
To our knowledge, the latest bound is

\left|a_{2}\right|<20.9197,

which was established
by Abu-Muhanna, Ali, and Ponnusamy
[

]
in 2019.
It has been conjectured by Clunie and Sheil-Small
[

where the equality is reached by the

\mathbb{K}

This is the expected candidate for extremal mappings in the class
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}
of univalent harmonic mappings.

Estimates of

3.
Harmonic
K
-quasiconformal Koebe functions

A long-standing bottleneck in the study of harmonic quasiconformal mappings lies in the absence of a canonical Koebe-type extremal function, which has significantly impeded the advancement of its extremal theory as well as the generalization of classical conformal mapping results to the harmonic quasiconformal framework. In this section, we remedy this critical gap by constructing such functions for the first time.

In what follows, we construct the harmonic
K
-quasiconformal Koebe functions

f_{k}(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}

via the
weighted shearing technique
, an approach adapted from the classical shearing method introduced by Clunie and Sheil-Small
[
9
]
.
The defining relations for its components are given by

h(z)-g(z)=\frac{z}{(1-z)^{2}}

and

\omega(z)=\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}=kz\quad(0\leq k<1).

Solving the above system of differential equations and normalization, for
0\leq k<1
, we obtain

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{k}(z)&=\frac{1}{(k-1)^{3}}{\left[\frac{(k-1)(1-3k+2kz)z}{(1-z)^{2}}+k(k+1)\log\left(\frac{1-z}{1-kz}\right)\right]}\\
&\qquad\ \ \ +\frac{k}{(k-1)^{3}}\overline{{\left[\frac{(1-k)(1+k-2z)z}{(1-z)^{2}}+(k+1)\log\left(\frac{1-z}{1-kz}\right)\right]}}.\end{split}

The power series form of
f_{k}
is

(3.2)

\displaystyle f_{k}(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}A(n,k)z^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}B(n,k)\overline{z}^{n},

where

(3.3)

\displaystyle A(n,k):=\frac{(1-k)^{2}n^{2}-2k(1-k)n+k(1+k)(1-k^{n})}{(1-k)^{3}\,n}

and

(3.4)

\displaystyle B(n,k):=\frac{k(1-k)^{2}n^{2}-2k(1-k)n+k(1+k)(1-k^{n})}{(1-k)^{3}\,n}.

By noting that the coefficient conjecture of Clunie and Sheil-Small
[
9
]
holds for the close-to-convex, starlike and typically real harmonic mappings, and harmonic quasiconformal mappings is a subclass of univalent harmonic mappings, the Bieberbach-type coefficient conjecture for
HQC
is confirmed valid by Li and Ponnusamy
[
21
]
for close-to-convex, starlike, and typically real harmonic quasiconformal mappings (see also
[
12
]
). For recent investigations on Koebe-type functions in the setting of harmonic quasiconformal mappings, see
[
32
]
.

It is natural to conjecture that the function
f_{k}(z)
serves as the extremal mapping for the class
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
of
HQC
mappings. This not only opens a novel perspective on the class
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
, but also provides a fundamental bridge connecting the theories of classical conformal mappings, harmonic mappings, quasiconformal mappings, Teichmüller spaces, and even SLE theory.

Indeed, the above formulas lead us to conjecture that the extremal function in
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
is unique and coincides with the harmonic
K
-quasiconformal Koebe functions.

Conjecture 1
.

(
HQC Bieberbach conjecture
)

For
K\geq 1
, let
f=h+\overline{g}\in{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(K)}
, where

h(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\ {\rm and}\ g(z)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}.

Then

\left|a_{n}\right|\leq A(n,k),\,\left|b_{n}\right|\leq B(n,k),\,{\rm and}\,\left|\left|a_{n}\right|-\left|b_{n}\right|\right|\leq n\,\,{\rm for}\,\,n\geq 2,

where
A(n,k)
and
B(n,k)
are given by (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), respectively, with

k=\frac{K-1}{K+1}\quad(K\geq 1).

However, this somewhat is premature in view that the related conjecture for the class
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
(see e.g.,
[
15
, pp. 87–88]
) remains
an
open
problem.

By the above coefficient conjecture for the class
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
, we obtain the conjectured sharp upper bound of
\left|a_{2}\right|
, which will play a distinguished role to study various characterizations for the class
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
(or even the class
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}
).

Conjecture 2
.

Suppose that
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
. Then

\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(K)}\left|a_{2}\right|=\frac{5K+3}{2K+2}.

The bound is expected to be sharp.

Conjecture 3

Suppose that

\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)}\left|a_{2}\right|=\frac{3K+1}{K+1},

and, in particular, we have

f\in h^{p}\quad\left(0<p<\frac{1}{2K}\right).

The bound for the order is expected to be sharp.

Conjecture 4

For

Proof of Theorem
1

We start by noting the following variant of the classical Schwarz lemma.

Lemma 1
.

(
[
5
]
)

Suppose that
0<k\leq 1
and
\omega
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with

\omega(0)=0\ \textit{and}\ \left|\omega(z)\right|\leq k.

Then

\left|\omega(z)\right|\leq k\left|z\right|\ \textit{and}\ \left|\omega^{\prime}(0)\right|\leq k.

Equality holds for the functions

\omega(z)=k\,e^{i\theta}z\quad(\theta\in[0,2\pi)).

Now, we provide the sharp upper bound of
\left|b_{2}\right|
for the class
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
, which will play a crucial role in determining the sharp upper bound of
\left|a_{2}\right|
of the family of harmonic
K
-quasiconformal mappings with bounded Schwarzian norm.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
, where

h(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\ {\textit{a}nd}\ g(z)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}.

Then the sharp inequality

(4.1)

\left|b_{2}\right|\leq\frac{K-1}{2(K+1)}

holds.

Proof.

For
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
,
we see that its analytic dilatation

\omega=\frac{g^{\prime}}{h^{\prime}}

satisfies the condition

\left|\omega(z)\right|\leq k\quad(0\leq k<1).

Moreover, if
f\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
, we know that
g^{\prime}(0)=0
, i.e.,
\omega(0)=0
. Thus, the assertion of
Lemma
1
shows that

\left|\omega^{\prime}(0)\right|\leq k\quad(0\leq k<1).

By
[
15
, p. 87, Sect. 5.4]
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\omega(z)&=\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{2b_{2}z+3b_{3}z^{2}+\cdots}{1+2a_{2}z+\cdots}=2b_{2}z+(3b_{3}-4a_{2}b_{2})z^{2}+\cdots,\end{split}

therefore,

\left|\omega^{\prime}(0)\right|=\left|2b_{2}\right|\leq k=\frac{K-1}{K+1},

which implies that the desired assertion of Lemma
2
is true. Equality holds for the functions

\omega(z)=k\,e^{i\theta}z\quad\left(0\leq k<1;\,\theta\in[0,2\pi)\right).

This completes the proof of Lemma
2
.
∎

In what follows, we introduce the
strongly affine transformation
for mappings
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
, defined by

\widetilde{A}_{\xi}(f)(z):=\frac{f(z)-\overline{\xi f(z)}}{1-\overline{\xi}g^{\prime}(0)},

where the parameter
\xi
satisfies

(4.2)

|\xi|\leq\eta\quad\left(0\leq\eta<1\right).

A family
\mathcal{\widetilde{F}}
is said to be a
strongly affine and linear invariant family

(SAL family) if it is closed under both the Koebe transform and strongly affine transformation.

Lemma 3
.

The families
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
and
{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)
belong to
\mathcal{\widetilde{F}}
of strongly affine and linear invariant family.

Proof.

It easily to check that the family
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
(resp.
{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)
) is a linear invariant family (see
[
13
, p. 9]
).
Let
\widetilde{A}_{\xi}(f)(z)=H(z)+\overline{G}(z)
, and denote its complex dilatation by

\widetilde{\omega}(z)=\frac{G^{\prime}(z)}{H^{\prime}(z)}.

Note that the constraint

|\xi|\leq\frac{k-|\omega(z)|}{1-k|\omega(z)|}<1\quad\left(|\omega(z)|=\left|\frac{g^{\prime}}{h^{\prime}}\right|\leq k;\ 0\leq k<1\right)

on
\xi
is equivalent to

\left|\frac{\omega(z)-\xi}{1-\overline{\xi}\omega(z)}\right|\leq k<1.

In conjunction with the definition of
\widetilde{\omega}(z)
, we have

|\widetilde{\omega}(z)|=\left|\frac{G^{\prime}(z)}{H^{\prime}(z)}\right|=\left|\frac{g^{\prime}-\xi h^{\prime}}{h^{\prime}-\overline{\xi}g^{\prime}}\right|=\left|\frac{\omega(z)-\xi}{1-\overline{\xi}\omega(z)}\right|\leq k<1,

which shows that the family
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
(resp.
{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)
) is a strongly affine invariant family.
Thus, we deduce that the families
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}(K)
and
{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)
are strongly affine and linear invariant families.
∎

We now turn our attention to the following result involving the order of the family
\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)
. Then

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{f\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)}\left|a_{2}\right|=\sqrt{1+\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{K-1}{K+1}\right)^{2}}+\frac{K-1}{2\left({K+1}\right)}.

Proof.

By harmonic quasiconformal Koebe function
f_{k}
and Remark
5
,
we see that
f_{k}\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)
, and
inequality (
4.1
) also holds for this family. Note that Theorem
D
remains valid for the strongly family
\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)
.
By Lemmas
2
and
3
, and using the similar method as in Theorem
D
,
we get the assertion of Proposition
2
.
∎

In what follows, we are ready to give the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

If
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)
, it follows from Proposition
2
that

\sup\limits_{f\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}}(K,\lambda)}\left|a_{2}\right|=\sqrt{1+\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{K-1}{K+1}\right)^{2}}+\frac{K-1}{2\left({K+1}\right)}\geq 1.

For convenience, we define a real function
\Phi(K)
with one parameter
\lambda\,(\lambda\geq 0)
as follows:

(4.3)

\Phi(K):=\sqrt{1+\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{K-1}{K+1}\right)^{2}}+\frac{K-1}{2\left({K+1}\right)}-2K\quad(K\geq 1).

Now, we divide the proof into three cases.

(i) When
0\leq\lambda\leq 6
and
K\geq 1
, we know that
\Phi(K)
is a decreasing continuous function with respect to
K

in
[1,+\infty)
,
and

\Phi(1)=\sqrt{1+\frac{\lambda}{2}}-2\leq 0,

which implies that

\Phi(K)\leq 0\quad(K\geq 1).

Thus, we have

(4.4)

\varphi(K,\lambda)=\sqrt{1+\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{K-1}{K+1}\right)^{2}}+\frac{K-1}{2\left({K+1}\right)}\leq 2K,

that is,

(4.5)

\frac{1}{\varphi(K,\lambda)}\geq\frac{1}{2K}.

By Proposition
1
,
it shows that

f\in h^{p}\quad\left(0<p<\frac{1}{2K}\right).

(ii) When
\lambda>6
, we know that

\Phi^{\prime}(K)<0\quad(K\geq 1).

Furthermore, we find that

\Phi(1)=\sqrt{1+\frac{\lambda}{2}}-2>0

and

\Phi\left(\lambda\right)<0.

Applying the zero point theorem to the real continuous function
\Phi(K)
in bounded closed interval

[1,\,\lambda]
, we deduce that the equation

\sqrt{1+\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{K-1}{K+1}\right)^{2}}+\frac{K-1}{2\left({K+1}\right)}=2K\quad(K\geq 1;\,\lambda>6),

which is equivalent to the equation (
1.3
), exists a unique solution
K_{1}
in
(1,+\infty)
.
If
K\geq K_{1}>1
, we know that
(
4.5
) also holds. By Proposition
1
, it implies that

f\in h^{p}\quad\left(0<p<\frac{1}{2K}\right).

(iii) When
\lambda>6
and
1\leq K<K_{1}
, we see that

\varphi(K,\lambda)=\sqrt{1+\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{K-1}{K+1}\right)^{2}}+\frac{K-1}{2\left({K+1}\right)}>2K,

or equivalently,

\frac{1}{\varphi(K,\lambda)}<\frac{1}{2K}.

By Proposition
1
,
it means that

f\in h^{p}\quad\left(0<p<\frac{1}{\varphi(K,\lambda)}\right).

The proof of Theorem
1
is thus completed.
∎

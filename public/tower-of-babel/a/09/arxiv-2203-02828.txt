Universal anomalous turbulent diffusion in quantum fluids
Satoshi Yui, Yuan Tang, Wei Guo, Hiromichi Kobayashi, Makoto Tsubota

Abstract
In classical viscous fluids, turbulent eddies are known to be responsible for the rapid spreading of embedded particles. But in an inviscid quantum fluid where the turbulence is induced by a chaotic tangle of quantized vortices, dispersion of the particles is achieved via a non-classical mechanism, i.e., their binding to the evolving quantized vortices. However, there is limited existing knowledge on how the vortices diffuse and spread in turbulent quantum fluids. Here we report a systematic numerical study of the apparent diffusion of vortices in a random vortex tangle in superfluid helium-4 using full Biot-Savart simulation. We reveal that the vortices in pure superfluid exhibit a universal anomalous diffusion (superdiffusion) at small times, which transits to normal diffusion at large times. This behavior is found to be caused by a generic scaling property of the vortex velocity, which should exist in all quantum fluids where the Biot-Savart law governs the vortex motion. Our simulation at finite temperatures also nicely reproduces recent experimental observations. The knowledge obtained from this study may form the foundation for understanding turbulent transport and universal vortex dynamics in various condensed-matter and cosmic quantum fluids.

So far, there have been very limited studies on the apparent diffusion of vortices in a quantum-fluid turbulence. On the theoretical side, the overall expansion of a decaying random vortex tangle near a solid wall

However, since the experiment was conducted only in a narrow temperature range (i.e., 1.7 K to 2.0 K), it is not clear whether the observed phenomenon holds at other temperatures and whether it is truly generic for all quantum fluids.

In this paper, we report a systematic numerical study of the apparent diffusion of individual vortices in a random vortex tangle in He II using the full Biot-Savart simulation
Adachi et al. 2010
. A random tangle does not generate any large-scale flows to advect the vortices
Volovik 2003
;
Walmsley and Golov 2008
, which makes it feasible to observe intrinsic vortex diffusion behaviors that are characteristics of the Biot-Savart law. We reveal that in pure superfluid the vortices indeed undergo superdiffusion at small times with a
universal
diffusion exponent regardless how dense the tangle is. At large times, the superdiffusion transits to normal diffusion due to vortex reconnections. Our analysis shows that this universal diffusion behavior is caused by a generic temporal correlation of the vortex velocity, which should exist in all quantum fluids where the Biot-Savart law applies. At finite temperatures, the viscous effect is found to only mildly affect the vortex diffusion, which nicely explains the experimental observations.

Note that random vortex tangles are indeed ubiquitous: they can be produced by counterflow in quantum two-fluid systems such as He II
Vinen 1957a
;
Gao et al. 2016a
, atomic BECs
Takeuchi et al. 2010
, superfluid neutron stars
Greenstein 1970
;
Haskell et al. 2020
, and dark matter BECs
Hartman et al. 2021
;
Sikivie and Yang 2009
; and they can also spontaneously emerge following a second-order phase transition in quantum fluids via the Kibble-Zurek mechanism
Zurek 1985
;
Stagg et al. 2016
. Therefore, the knowledge obtained in our study may also offer valuable insights into the evolution and quenching dynamics of these diverse quantum-fluid systems.

Vortex diffusion in a pure superfluid.
–Like many other quantum fluids, He II can be considered as a mixture of two miscible fluid components, i.e., an inviscid superfluid (the condensate) and a viscous normal fluid (collection of thermal quasiparticles)
Landau and Lifshitz 1987
. The normal-fluid fraction in He II drops with decreasing temperature and becomes negligible below 1 K. Experimentally, a random vortex tangle can be produced in pure superfluid helium at zero-temperature limit by injecting small vortex rings
Walmsley and Golov 2008
. Here, we adopt a similar method numerically to study vortex diffusion in pure superfluid. As shown in Fig.
1
(a), we first place 64 randomly oriented vortex rings (radius:
R_{in}=0.11
mm) in a cubical computational box (side length:
D=1
mm) with periodic boundary conditions in all three axial directions. These vortice are described by the vortex filament model
Schwarz 1988
, and each vortex filament is discretized into a series of points. In the absence of the normal fluid, a vortex-filament point at
\mathbf{s}
moves at the local superfluid velocity
\mathbf{v}_{s}(\mathbf{s})
induced by all the vortices as given by the Boit-Savart law
Schwarz 1988
;
Tsubota et al. 2000
:

\frac{d\mathbf{s}}{dt}=\mathbf{v}_{s}(\mathbf{s})=\frac{\kappa}{4\pi}\int\frac{(\mathbf{s_{1}}-\mathbf{s})\times d\mathbf{s_{1}}}{|\mathbf{s_{1}}-\mathbf{s}|^{3}}.

(1)

R_{in}

Mechanism of the universal vortex diffusion.
–To explain the physical mechanism of the observed vortex diffusion behavior, we first note that superdiffusion has been observed in various physical and non-physical systems
Solomon et al. 1993
;
Harris et al. 2012
;
Sagi et al. 2012
;
Raichlen et al. 2014
. For systems involving random walkers, the appearance of superdiffusion is usually attributed to the so-called Lévy flights, i.e., occasional long-distance hops of the random walkers
Bouchaud and Georges 1990
. These flights lead to a power-law tail of the walker’s displacement distribution
P(\Delta x)\propto|\Delta x|^{-\alpha}
, which is flat enough (i.e.,
\alpha<3
) to cause superdiffusion
Bouchaud and Georges 1990
. In He II, large displacements of the vortex-filament points over a short time can occur at the locations where the vortices reconnect
Bewley et al. 2008
;
Paoletti et al. 2008
. However, we find that these reconnections always result in a tail of the vortex displacement distribution
P(\Delta x)
steeper than
|\Delta x|^{-3}
(see Supplemental Materials). Therefore, they cannot account for the observed vortex superdiffusion. On the other hand, superdiffusion may emerge if the motion of the walkers is not completely random but has extended temporal correlations
Bouchaud and Georges 1990
;
Davison and Cox 1989
. To see this, we write the MSD of a vortex-filament point
\langle\Delta x^{2}(t)\rangle
in terms of its velocity
v_{x}(t)
as
Mazzitelli and Lohse 2004
:

\langle\Delta x^{2}(t)\rangle=2\int_{0}^{t}dt_{0}\int_{0}^{t-t_{0}}dt^{\prime}\langle v_{x}(t_{0})v_{x}(t_{0}+t^{\prime})\rangle.

(2)

For a fully developed random tangle, the vortex-velocity temporal correlation function
C_{x}(t^{\prime},t_{0})=\langle v_{x}(t_{0})v_{x}(t_{0}+t^{\prime})\rangle
only depends on the lapse time
t^{\prime}
, i.e.,
C_{x}(t^{\prime},t_{0})=C_{x}(t^{\prime})
. If
C_{x}(t^{\prime})
shows a power-law scaling
C_{x}(t^{\prime})\propto t^{\prime-\beta}
over a large time interval,
\langle\Delta x^{2}(t)\rangle
then scales as
\langle\Delta x^{2}(t)\rangle\propto t^{2-\beta}
in the same time interval and can exhibit superdiffusion when
\beta<1
. In Fig.
2
(a), we show the calculated
C_{x}(t^{\prime})
for a representative tangle with
\ell=0.16
mm. There is a clear power-law scaling with a fitted exponent
\beta=0.42
, which leads to
\langle\Delta x^{2}(t)\rangle\propto t^{1.58}
, matching nicely the superdiffusion exponent reported in Fig.
1
. Similar results are obtained for other cases at different
\ell
, which reveals that the universal vortex superdiffusion at small
t
is caused by the temporal correlation of the vortex velocity. This correlation should be an intrinsic property of the Biot-Savart law applicable to all superfluids.

Finite-temperature effect.
–At finite temperatures, the vortices experience a drag force as they move through the normal fluid due to scattering of the thermal quasiparticles in He II
Vinen 1957b
. The velocity of a vortex-filament point at
\mathbf{s}
is now given by

;
Tsubota et al. 2000
:

d\mathbf{s}/dt=\mathbf{v}_{s}(\mathbf{s})+\alpha\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\times(\mathbf{v}_{n}-\mathbf{v}_{s})-\alpha^{\prime}\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\times[\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\times(\mathbf{v}_{n}-\mathbf{v}_{s})],

(3)

where
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
are temperature dependant mutual friction coefficients for He II

is the unit tangent vector along the filament, and

\mathbf{v}_{n}=0
). The second method is via thermal counterflow as adopted in the experiment conducted by Tang
et al

Discussion.
–The weak temperature dependence of
\gamma_{1}
indeed involves interesting physics. To explain it, we again refer to the vortex-velocity temporal correlation
C_{x}(t^{\prime})
. In Fig.
5
, we show the normalized
C_{x}(t^{\prime})/C_{x}(0)
curves for vortex tangles produced by counterflow at various
T
with
\ell
in the range of
0.14-0.16
mm. The
C_{x}(t^{\prime})/C_{x}(0)
curve for the random tangle produced by ring injections at 0 K with
\ell=0.16
mm is also included as a reference. Compared to the
T=0
curve, a major difference of the curves at finite
T
is that they saturate to 1 at larger
t^{\prime}
. This saturation, which corresponds to ballistic motion of the vortices (i.e.,
\langle\Delta x^{2}(t)\rangle\propto t^{2}
) according to equation
2
, is controlled by how the turbulent energy decays. At 0 K, the turbulent energy can cascade down to scales smaller than
\ell
by exciting Kelvin waves on the vortex lines
Kivotides et al. 2001
. These waves result in small-scale deformations and kinks on the vortices (see movies in the Supplemental Materials), which can cause rapid turning of the vortex-velocity vector
Tsubota et al. 2000
. This process suppresses the velocity temporal correlation and is the reason why
C_{x}(t^{\prime})
at 0 K remains far from saturation even down to
t^{\prime}=10^{-3}
s. At finite
T
, the mutual friction from the normal fluid damps out the Kelvin waves and terminates the energy cascade at scales comparable to
\ell

Gao et al. 2016b
;
Gao et al. 2018
. Therefore, small-scale deformations on the vortices are all smoothed out, which helps extending the ballistic motion of the vortices to larger
t^{\prime}
. This extension first makes the
C_{x}(t^{\prime})/C_{x}(0)
curve steeper in the power-law region, thereby leading to a larger exponent
\beta
as compared to the 0 K case. As
T
further increases, the power-law region gradually shrinks and levels off, which reduces
\beta
. Although
\beta
and
\gamma_{1}
are no longer correlated exactly as
\gamma_{1}=2-\beta
due to the presence of the saturation regime, the decrease in
\beta
as
T
increases still causes
\gamma_{1}
to gradually rise as observed.

In summary, our numerical study has produced a comprehensive understanding of the intriguing diffusion behaviors of the quantized vortices in a random vortex tangle. This study may stimulate future research on hidden universal scaling laws in quantum-fluid turbulence pertinent to trajectory statistics of individual vortices.

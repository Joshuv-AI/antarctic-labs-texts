On Extrapolation of Carleson measures
John Garnett

Abstract
. Extrapolation of Carleson measures is a technique originated by [LM] which has become an essential step for several important results. See for example [HL], [AHLT], [AHMTT] and more recently [HM], [HMM], and [HMMTZ]. Here we recast the proof of the extrapolation theorems in [AHLT] and [HMM] using the stopping time - generations language from [C1], [C2] or [CG] but no new idea. We give three equivalent versions of the theorem having the same proof. The first is for dyadic cubes in [ 0 , 1 ] d , [0,1]^{d}, the second is an abstract formulation of the result on Ahlfors regular sets from [HM], and the third is the version in [AHLT] which follows from the other two.

1.
The dyadic case

In
{\mathbb{R}}^{d},~d\geq 1
, let
{\mathscr{D}}
denote the set of closed dyadic cubes

Q=\bigcup_{k=1}^{d}\{j_{k}2^{-n}\leq x_{k}\leq(j_{k}+1)2^{-n}\}\subset Q_{0}=[0,1]^{d},n\in{\mathbb{N}},j_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

For
Q\in{\mathscr{D}}
write
\ell(Q)=2^{-n}
for its side length and
|Q|=2^{-nd}
for its measure and
define

Q^{*}=Q\times(0,\ell(Q)]\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+},

T(Q)=Q\times(\ell(Q)/2,\ell(Q)]={Q}^{*}\setminus\cup\{Q_{1}^{*}:Q_{1}\subsetneqq Q\},

and

{\mathscr{D}}(Q)=\{Q^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{D}}:Q^{\prime}\subset Q\}.

A Borel measure
\mu
on
Q_{0}^{*}
is a
Carleson measure
if

(1.1)

C_{1}(\mu)=\sup_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}}\frac{\mu(Q^{*})}{|Q|}<\infty.

Let
\nu
be another Borel measure on
Q_{0}^{*}
satisfying

(1.2)

C_{2}(\nu)=\sup_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}}\frac{\nu(T(Q))}{|Q|}<\infty,

a condition clearly necessary for
\nu
to be a Carleson measure.

Theorem 1.1:
Let
\mu
and
\nu
be Borel measures on
Q_{0}^{*}
satisfying (1.1) and (1.2) respectively. Assume there exist constants
\delta>0
and
C
such that

(1.3)

\nu(Q^{*}\setminus\bigcup_{{\mathscr{F}}}Q^{\prime*})\leq C|Q|

whenever
Q\in{\mathscr{D}}
and
{\mathscr{F}}\subset{\mathscr{D}}(Q)
is a set of subcubes of
Q
with disjoint interiors for which

(1.4)

\sup_{Q^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{D}}(Q)}\frac{\mu(Q^{\prime*}\setminus\bigcup_{{\mathscr{F}}}Q^{\prime\prime*})}{|Q^{\prime}|}\leq\delta.

Then
\nu
is a Carleson measure with constant

(1.5)

C_{1}(\nu)\leq\biggl(C_{2}(\nu)+\frac{2^{d}C}{2^{d}-1}\biggr)\frac{C_{1}(\mu)}{\delta}.

Careful reading will show that the proof below is little more than a reformulation of the arguments in Sections 7 and 9 of [HM].

Proof:
When proving Theorem 1.1. we can assume

E=\bigcup_{{\mathscr{D}}}\partial Q^{*}
satisfies
\mu(E)=\nu(E)=0
by pushing some mass off each
\partial Q^{*}
and thus we can treat all rectangles as open or closed sets when taking unions or intersections.

Fix
\delta>0
for which (1.4) implies (1.3). For each
Q\in{\mathscr{D}}
we will define a subset
U(Q)\subset Q^{*}
and a pairwise disjoint family
G_{1}(Q)\subset{\mathscr{D}}(Q)
so that

(1.6)

U(Q)=Q^{*}\setminus\bigcup_{G_{1}(Q)}Q^{\prime*}.

Further define by induction

G_{n}(Q)=\bigcup\{G_{1}(Q^{\prime}):Q^{\prime}\in G_{n-1}(Q)\}

and
G_{0}(Q)=\{Q\}.
Then the family

\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\{U(Q^{\prime}):Q^{\prime}\in G_{n}(Q)\}

is pairwise disjoint and

(1.7)

\nu(Q^{*})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{G_{n}(Q)}\nu(U(Q^{\prime})).

For each
Q
and
n\geq 1
write

{\mathscr{E}}_{n}(Q)=\{Q_{n}\subset Q:\ell(Q_{n})=2^{-n}\ell(Q)\}.

Also define

(1.8)

{\mathscr{B}}=\{Q:\mu(T(Q))\geq\delta|Q|\}.

Fix
Q\in{\mathscr{D}}
. For
Q^{\prime}\subset Q
the definitions of
U(Q^{\prime})
and
G_{1}(Q^{\prime})
depend on whether or not
Q^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{B}}.

When
Q\in{\mathscr{B}}
take
G_{1}(Q)={\mathscr{E}}_{1}(Q)
and
U(Q)=T(Q).
Then for any
Q
(1.2), (1.8) and disjointness yield

(1.9)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{{\mathscr{B}}\cap G_{n}(Q)}\nu(U(Q^{\prime}))\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\delta}\sum_{{\mathscr{B}}\cap{\mathscr{D}}(Q)}\mu(T(Q^{\prime}))\leq\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{\delta}|Q|,

so that when estimating (1.7) we need only study
Q^{\prime}\notin{\mathscr{B}}.

When
Q\in{\mathscr{D}}\setminus{\mathscr{B}}
we define by induction sets
{\mathscr{F}}_{n}(Q)\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}{\mathscr{E}}_{k}(Q)
so that

{\mathscr{F}}_{n}(Q)\subset{\mathscr{F}}_{n+1}(Q)

and so that (1.4) holds for
Q
and

\widetilde{{\mathscr{F}}_{n}}(Q)={\mathscr{F}}_{n}(Q)\cup{\mathscr{R}}_{n+1}(Q)

where

{\mathscr{R}}_{n+1}(Q)={\mathscr{E}}_{n+1}(Q)\setminus\bigcup_{Q^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{F}}_{n}(Q)}{\mathscr{D}}(Q^{\prime}).

Then (1.4) will hold for
Q
and

{\mathscr{F}}(Q)=\bigcup_{n}{\mathscr{F}}_{n}(Q).

Step I:

n=1.
Include
{\mathscr{B}}\cap{\mathscr{E}}_{1}(Q)\subset{\mathscr{F}}_{1}(Q)
and then consider the family of subsets

{\mathscr{S}}\subset{\mathscr{E}}_{1}(Q)\setminus{\mathscr{B}}
such that (1.4) holds for
Q

and
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{{\mathscr{S}}}
where

{\mathscr{F}}_{{\mathscr{S}}}={\mathscr{S}}\cup({\mathscr{E}}_{1}(Q)\setminus{\mathscr{B}})
. This family is non-empty because it contains

{\mathscr{E}}_{1}(Q)\setminus{\mathscr{B}}
since
Q\notin{\mathscr{B}}.

Order this set family by inclusion, let
{\mathscr{S}}_{1}(Q)
be a minimal element and define

{\mathscr{F}}_{1}(Q)={\mathscr{S}}_{1}(Q)\cup({\mathscr{B}}\cap{\mathscr{E}}_{1}(Q)).

Then (1.4) holds for

\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{1}(Q).

Step II:
Now assume

n\geq 1
and
{\mathscr{F}}_{n}(Q)
has been constructed. If
\bigcup_{{\mathscr{F}}_{n}(Q)}Q^{\prime}=Q
stop the construction for
Q
. If not, include

{\mathscr{B}}\cap{\mathscr{R}}_{n}(Q)\subset{\mathscr{F}}_{n+1}(Q)

and consider the family of subsets

{\mathscr{S}}\subset{\mathscr{R}}_{n}(Q)\setminus{\mathscr{B}}
such that when

(1.10)

{\mathscr{F}}_{{\mathscr{S}}}={\mathscr{F}}_{n}(Q)\cup{\mathscr{S}}\cup({\mathscr{B}}\cap{\mathscr{R}}_{n+1}(Q))

(1.4) holds for
Q
and

\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{{\mathscr{S}}}={\mathscr{F}}_{{\mathscr{S}}}\cup\bigl({\mathscr{E}}_{n+2}(Q)\setminus\bigcup_{Q^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{F}}_{{\mathscr{S}}}}{\mathscr{D}}(Q^{\prime})\bigr).

This family is non-empty because by induction (1.4) holds for
Q
and

\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{{\mathscr{S}}}
when
{\mathscr{S}}={\mathscr{R}}_{n+1}(Q).
Order this finite set family by inclusion, let

{\mathscr{S}}_{n+1}(Q)
be a minimal member, and define

{\mathscr{F}}_{n+1}(Q)
by (1.6) with
{\mathscr{S}}={\mathscr{S}}_{n+1}(Q).
Then (1.4) holds for
Q
and
\widetilde{{\mathscr{F}}_{n+1}}(Q).

Moreover we have:

Lemma 1.2:
If
Q^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{F}}(Q)\setminus{\mathscr{B}}
there exists

\widetilde{Q}^{\prime}
such that
Q^{\prime}\subset\widetilde{Q}^{\prime}\subset Q
and

(1.11)

\mu(\widetilde{Q}^{\prime*}\setminus\bigcup_{{\mathscr{F}}(Q)}Q^{\prime\prime*})\geq\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2^{d}}\biggr)\delta|\widetilde{Q}^{\prime}|.

Proof:
We have
Q^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{F}}_{n}
for some first
n
and by the minimality
of
{\mathscr{S}}_{n}
there exists
\widetilde{Q}^{\prime}\supsetneqq Q^{\prime}
such that

\mu\bigl(\widetilde{Q}^{\prime*}\setminus\bigcup\{Q^{\prime\prime*}:Q^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathscr{F}}_{n}(Q)\cup{\mathscr{E}}_{n+1}(Q)\}\bigr)+\mu(T(Q^{\prime}))>\delta|\widetilde{Q}^{\prime}|

while
\mu(T(Q^{\prime}))<\frac{\delta}{2^{d}}|\widetilde{Q}^{\prime}|
since
Q^{\prime}\notin{\mathscr{B}}.
Therefore (1.11) holds.

When
Q^{\prime}\notin{\mathscr{B}}
, (1.4) holds for
Q^{\prime}
and
{\mathscr{F}}(Q^{\prime})
so that by (1.3)

\nu(U(Q^{\prime}))\leq C|Q^{\prime}|
and therefore

(1.12)

\sum_{n}\sum_{G_{n}(Q)\setminus{\mathscr{B}}}\nu(U(Q^{\prime}))\leq C\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{G_{n}(Q)\setminus{\mathscr{B}}}|Q^{\prime}|.

By Lemma 1.2 we can cover
\bigcup_{{\mathscr{F}}(Q)\setminus{\mathscr{B}}}Q^{\prime}
by the pairwise disjoint family
{\mathscr{H}}(Q)
of maximal dyadic cubes
\widetilde{Q}^{\prime}
that satisfy (1.11). Then since both families
{\mathscr{H}}(Q)
and
{\mathscr{F}}(Q)\setminus{\mathscr{B}}
are pairwise disjoint. (1.11) yields

(1.13)

\sum_{{\mathscr{F}}(Q)\setminus{\mathscr{B}}}|Q^{\prime}|\leq\sum_{{\mathscr{H}}(Q)}|\widetilde{Q}^{\prime}|\leq\frac{2^{d}}{2^{d}-1}\frac{1}{\delta}\mu(U(Q)),

and (1.13), (1.12) and (1.1) give

(1.14)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{G_{n}(Q)\setminus{\mathscr{B}}}\nu(Q^{\prime})\leq\frac{2^{d}}{2^{d}-1}\frac{CC_{1}}{\delta}|Q|,

and together (1.14) and (1.9) establish the estimate (1.5) and prove Theorem 1.1.

2.
a general case

Let
d
be a positive integer and let
(X,\rho)
be a metric space on which there exists a positive Borel measure
\sigma
that is
d
-Ahlfors regular: there exists
c_{1}>0
such that

(2.1)

\frac{1}{c_{1}}R^{d}\leq\sigma(B(x,R)\leq c_{1}R^{d}

for all
x\in X
and all
0<R\leq\mathop{\rm diam}(X).
We assume for
simplicity that
X
is compact and
\sigma(X)=1.
Following Christ [Ch], there exists a positive integer
N
and a family

{\mathscr{D}}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{\infty}{\mathscr{D}}_{j}

of Borel subsets of
X
satisfying (2.2) - (2.6) below:

(2.2)

\mathop{\rm diam}{Q}\sim 2^{-Nj}~~~{\rm{if}}~~~Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{j};

(2.3)

X=\bigcup_{{{\mathscr{D}}}_{j}}Q,~~~{\rm{for~all}}~~~j;

(2.4)

Q\cap Q^{\prime}=\emptyset~~~{\rm{if}}~~~Q,~Q^{\prime}\in{{\mathscr{D}}}_{j}~~~{\rm{and}}~~~Q^{\prime}\neq Q;

(2.5)

{\rm{if}}~~j<k,~~~Q_{j}\in{\mathscr{D}}_{j}~~{\rm{and}}~Q_{k}\in{\mathscr{D}}_{k},~~~{\rm{then}}~~~Q_{k}\subset Q_{j}~~~{\rm{or}}~~~Q_{k}\cap Q_{j}=\emptyset.

There exists constant
c_{0}
such that for all
Q\in{\mathscr{D}}
there exists
x_{Q}\in Q
with

(2.6)

B(x_{Q},c_{0}\ell(Q))\cap\partial\Omega\subset Q.

Note that by (2.1), (2.2) and (2.6) there is a constant
c_{2}
so that for all
Q\in{\mathscr{D}}
,

(2.7)

\frac{1}{c_{2}}\ell(Q)^{d}\leq\sigma(Q)\leq c_{2}\ell(Q)^{d}.

Now let
\mu
and
\nu
be positive discrete measures on the countable
set
{\mathscr{D}}
, so that there exist
\alpha_{Q}\geq 0
and
\beta_{Q}\geq 0
such that for any
{\mathscr{E}}\subset{\mathscr{D}}

\mu({\mathscr{E}})=\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{E}}}\alpha_{Q}

and

\nu({\mathscr{E}})=\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{E}}}\beta_{Q}.

Analogous to (1.1) and (1.2) we assume
\mu
is a discrete Carleson measure,

(2.8)

C_{1}(\mu)=\sup_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}}\frac{\mu(Q^{*})}{\sigma(Q)}<\infty,

where we write
Q^{*}=\{Q^{\prime}:Q^{\prime}\subseteq Q\}
, and we assume

(2.9)

C_{2}(\nu)\sup_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}}\frac{\beta_{Q}}{\sigma(Q)}<\infty,

which is necessary for
\nu
to be a discrete Carleson measure. Then we have the following abstract version of Theorem 1.1:

Theorem 2.1:
Let
\mu
and
\nu
be measures on
{\mathscr{D}}
satisfying (2.8) and (2.9) respectively. Assume there exist constants
\delta>0
and
C
such that

(2.10)

\nu(Q^{*}\setminus\bigcup_{{\mathscr{F}}}Q^{\prime*})\leq C\sigma(Q)

whenever
Q\in{\mathscr{D}}
and
{\mathscr{F}}\subset{\mathscr{D}}(Q)
is a set of subcubes of
Q
for which

(2.11)

\sup_{Q^{\prime}\subset Q}\frac{\mu(Q^{\prime*}\setminus\bigcup_{{\mathscr{F}}}Q^{\prime\prime*})}{\sigma(Q^{\prime})}\leq\delta.

Then
\nu
is a Carleson measure with constant

C_{1}(\nu)
depending only on
c_{2},C,\delta,C_{1}(\mu)
and
C_{2}(\nu).

But for the choices of constants the proof of Theorem 2.1 is a repetition of the proof of Theorem 1.1 and we omit the details.

Theorem 2.1 can be stated more abstractly as a result about the tree
({\mathscr{D}},\subset)
with transition probabilities
\frac{\sigma(Q^{\prime})}{\sigma(Q)}
for
Q^{\prime}\subset Q.

However for the important applications in [HM], [HMM] and [HMMTZ]

\Omega
is a domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
,
E=\partial\Omega
is uniformly rectifiable,
and
\alpha_{Q}
and
\beta_{Q}
depend critically on the uniform rectifiabity properties of
E
and on the elliptic differential equation whose solutions are being estimated. Thus the real difficulty lies in finding suitable functions

\alpha_{Q}
and
\beta_{Q}.

3.
a third version

The “extrapolation lemma” in [AHLT] assumes
\mu
is a Carleson measure in

R^{d+1}_{+}
, i. e.
\mu
satisfies (1.1) for all cubes
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\nu
is another Borel measure on
R^{d+1}_{+}
satisfying

(3.1)

\nu(T(Q))\leq C_{2}|Q|,

where now
T(Q)=Q\times[\frac{\ell(Q)}{2},\ell(Q)]\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}_{+}.

But instead of a cube family
{\mathscr{F}}
one now works with a nonnegative Lipschitz function
\psi
such that

(3.2)

||\nabla\psi||_{\infty}\leq 1

and the region

\Omega_{\psi}=\{(x,t)\in R^{d}\times(0,\infty):t\geq\psi(x)\}

and instead of (1.4) one tests
\mu(T_{Q}\setminus\Omega_{\psi})
where
T_{Q}
is the tent

T_{Q}=\{(x,t):x\in Q,0\leq t\leq{\rm{dist}}(x,{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus Q)\}.

Theorem 3.1:
Let
\mu
and
\nu
be Borel measures on
R^{d+1}_{+}
satisfying (1.1) and (3.1) respectively. Assume there is are constants
\delta>0
and
C>0
such that whenever
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a cube and
\psi:Q\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a nonnegative Lipschitz function satisfying (3.2)

(3.3)

\nu(Q^{*}\cap\Omega_{\psi})\leq C|Q|

holds provided

(3.4)

\sup_{Q^{\prime}\subset Q}\frac{\mu(T_{Q^{\prime}}\cap\Omega_{\psi})}{|Q^{\prime}|}\leq\delta

where the supremum is taken over the decomposition of
Q
into cubes having side

2^{-n}\ell(Q),n\in{\mathbb{N}}.
. Then
\nu
is a Carleson measure, i.e. for all
Q

(3.5)

\nu(Q^{*})\leq C|Q|

Proof:
First note that (3.1) implies for all
Q

(3.6)

\nu(Q^{*}\setminus T_{Q})\leq(C_{2}+d)|Q|

so that to prove (3.4) for a fixed cube
Q
we may assume

(3.7)

\nu(Q*\setminus T_{Q})=0

and as before we can assume
\nu(\partial T_{Q}^{\prime}\cup T(Q^{\prime}))=0
for all
Q^{\prime}\subset Q
. For each
Q^{\prime}
write
p_{Q^{\prime}}=(c(Q^{\prime}),\frac{\ell(Q^{\prime}}{2})
for the center of
Q^{*}
which is also the vertex of
T_{Q^{\prime}}
and define the discrete measures

\tilde{\nu}=\sum_{Q^{\prime}\subsetneqq Q}\nu(T(Q^{\prime}))\delta_{p_{Q^{\prime}}},~~{\rm{and}}~~\tilde{\mu}=\sum_{Q^{\prime}\subsetneqq Q}\mu(T(Q^{\prime}))\delta_{p_{Q^{\prime}}},

where
\delta_{p}
is the unit point mass at
p
. Then by (3.7)

(3.8)

\tilde{\nu}(Q^{\prime*})=\nu(Q^{\prime*})~~{\rm{and}}~~\tilde{\mu}(Q^{\prime*})=\mu(Q^{\prime*})

for all
Q^{\prime}\subset Q
.

To prove Theorem 3.1 we show that if (3.4)
\Longrightarrow
(3.3) for
\mu
and
\nu
then
(1.4)
\Longrightarrow
(1.3) for
\tilde{\mu}
and
\tilde{\nu}
, so that by (3.8) and Theorem 1.1, (3.5) holds
for
\nu.
To that end let
{\mathscr{F}}=\{Q_{1},....\}
be a pairwise disjoint family of subcubes of
Q
satisfying (1.4) with
\tilde{\nu}.
Set

\psi_{{\mathscr{F}}}(x)=\sum_{{\mathscr{F}}}\frac{2}{\ell(Q_{j})}\chi_{Q_{j}}(x){\rm{dist}}(x,\partial Q_{j}).

Then

Q^{*}\cap\Omega_{\psi}=\bigl(Q^{*}\setminus\bigcup_{{\mathscr{F}}}Q_{j}^{*}\bigr)\cup\bigcup_{{\mathscr{F}}}(Q_{j}^{*}\setminus T_{Q_{j}})

so that by (1.4) and (3.8) we obtain (3.4) for
\mu
and (3.3) for
\nu
and therefore (1.3) for

\tilde{\nu}
, again by (3.8).

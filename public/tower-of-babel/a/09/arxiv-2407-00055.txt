Counterexamples to "Transitive Regret"
Yuan Chang Shuo Li Liu

Abstract
Theorem 1 in Bikhchandani & Segal (2011) suggests that a complete, transitive, monotonic, and continuous preference is regret based if and only if it is expected utility. Their Proposition 1 suggests that transitivity and continuity of a regret-based preference implies an equivalence condition: if random variables X \color[rgb]{0,0,1}X and Y \color[rgb]{0,0,1}Y have the same distribution, then X ∼ Y \color[rgb]{0,0,1}X\sim Y . We give counterexamples to Proposition 1. † † We are in debt to Jingni Yang for comments. All errors are ours.

Notations

Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L
be the set of real finite-valued random variables over
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(S,\Sigma,P)
with
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S=[0,1]
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\Sigma
being the Borel
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\sigma
-algebra,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}P
being the Lebesgue measure, and the set of outcomes being the bounded interval
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}[\underline{x},\bar{x}]
. The decision maker has a preference relation
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\succsim
over
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L
. Denote events by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{i}
.

Definition 1.
The continuous function
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\psi:[\underline{x},\bar{x}]\times[\underline{x},\bar{x}]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
regret function
if for all
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}x
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\psi(x,x)=0
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\psi(x,y)
is strictly increasing in
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}x
and strictly decreasing in
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}y
.

If in some event
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X
yields
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}x
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}Y
yields
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}y
, then
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\psi(x,y)
is a measure of the decision maker’s ex post feelings about the choice of
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X
over
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}Y
. This leads to the next definition.

Definition 2.
Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X,Y\in L
, where
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X=(x_{1},S_{1};\ldots;x_{n},S_{n})
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}Y=(y_{1},S_{1};\ldots;y_{n},S_{n})
. The
regret lottery
evaluating the choice of
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X
over
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}Y
is

\Psi(X,Y)=(\psi(x_{1},y_{1}),p_{1};\ldots;\psi(x_{n},y_{n}),p_{n}),

where
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}p_{i}=P(S_{i})
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}i=1,\ldots,n
.

Refer to
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\psi
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\Psi
as a regret function and a regret lottery. The main model follows.

Definition 3.
The preference relation
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\succsim
is
regret based
if there exists a regret function
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\psi
and a continuous functional
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V
that is defined over regret lotteries such that for any
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X,Y\in L
,

X\succsim Y\text{ if and only if }V(\Psi(X,Y))\geq 0.

2
Counterexamples

Proposition 1

(Bikhchandani & Segal 2011)
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\succsim
be transitive, continuous, and regret based. For all
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}Y
, if they have the same cumulative distribution with respect to
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}P
, then
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X\sim Y
.

One difference between Theorem 1 and Proposition 1 in Bikhchandani& Segal (2011) is that Proposition 1
drops
two axioms: monotonicity and completeness. The following counterexamples imply that the assumptions for Proposition 1 is too weak due to the lack of completeness axiom.

Assume
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\psi(x,y)=x-y
, a continuous regret function.

Counterexample 1:

Under the constant representation
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V(\Psi(X,Y))=-1
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X\not\succsim Y
if
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X,Y
have the same distribution.

It will be proven that
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V
satisfies transitivity, continuity, and regret-based.

Proof.

First, observe
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V(\Psi(X,Y))=-1
for any regret lotteries.
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V
is regret-based because a constant function is continuous and
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\psi(x,y)=x-y
is a continuous regret function.

Transitivity will be proven by contradiction. Assume transitivity does not hold; then, there must exist
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X,Y,Z
such that
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X\succsim Y
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}Y\succsim Z
, and
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X\not\succsim Z
. However,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V(\Psi(X,Y))<0
implies
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X\not\succsim Y
, a contradiction! So transitivity holds.

Since for all
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X,Y:

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V\not\geq 0
, we have
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X\not\succsim Y
for any
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X,Y
. The upper contour sets and lower contour sets of
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\succsim
are all empty sets and thus closed sets, satisfying continuity.
∎

Counterexample 2:
Under the representation
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V(\Psi(X,Y))=-\sum_{i}|\psi(x_{i},y_{i})p_{i}|
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X\not\succsim Y
if
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X,Y
have the same distribution and
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X\neq Y
.

Proof.

First, observe that
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-\sum_{i}|\psi(x_{i},y_{i})p_{i}|
is regret-based because the sum of absolute value functions is continuous and
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\psi(x,y)=x-y
is a continuous regret function.

Assume
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X\succsim Y
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}Y\succsim Z
. Observe that
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-\sum_{i}|\psi(x_{i},y_{i})p_{i}|\leq 0
and the equality holds if
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\psi(x_{i},y_{i})=0
for all
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}i
. Since
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X\succsim Y
implies
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V(\Psi(X,Y))\geq 0
, we have
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V(\Psi(X,Y))=0
. Thus,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}x_{i}=y_{i}
for all
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}i
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X=Y
. Similarly,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}Y=Z
. Therefore,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X\succsim Z
. Transitivity holds.

Observe that the upper contour set
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\{X:X\succsim Y\}=\{X\}
is a singleton and that the lower contour set is also a singleton. Therefore, the upper contour sets and the lower contour sets of
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\succsim
are singleton sets and therefore closed sets, satisfying continuity.
∎

Proposition 1 is the first step in Bikhchandani& Segal (2011)’s proof (page 98) for Theorem 1, and is applied in their Section 3.3, page 100.

Research announcement: A combination theorem for acylindrical complexes of hyperbolic groups and Cannon-Thurston maps
Pranab Sardar, Ravi Tomar

Abstract




2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
20F65, 20F67 (Primary), 30F60(Secondary)

Complexes of groups (see
[
BH99
]
and
[
Hae91
]
) are generalizations of graphs of groups
[
Ser03
]
.
Here we work with developable complexes of groups over finite simplicial complexes. Motivated by the celebrated combination
theorem of Bestvina and Feighn
[
BF92
]
for graphs of hyperbolic groups, one asks if there is an analogous result for complexes of hyperbolic groups. One may start with a simplicial complex of groups
G({\mathcal{Y}})
over a finite simplicial complex
Y

which is developable and such that
(i) each face group is hyperbolic and (ii) for any two faces
\sigma\subset\tau
, the monomorphism
G_{\tau}\rightarrow G_{\sigma}

is a qi embedding. However, under these assumptions it is in general unknown when the fundamental group will be hyperbolic
other than two extreme cases- (1) when all the monomorphisms
G_{\tau}\rightarrow G_{\sigma}
have finite index image in
G_{\sigma}

for any two faces
\sigma\subset\tau
of
Y
(
[
MS12
]
) and (2) in case the action of the fundamental group of
the complex of groups is acylindrical on the universal cover of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
(
[
Mar14
]
). In both these cases additional
hypotheses are needed. For instance, in
[
Mar14
]
, Martin works with simple complexes of groups, i.e. where there are
no twisting elements. However, using the result of
[
Mar15
]
one has the following theorem.

Theorem
(Martin)
Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a developable complex of groups over a finite simplicial complex
Y
such that the following holds:

(1) Local groups are hyperbolic and local maps are quasi-isometric embeddings.

(2) Universal cover of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is a CAT(0) hyperbolic space.

(3) Action of
\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}))
(fundamental group of
G({\mathcal{Y}}))
on universal cover is acylindrical.

Then
\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}))
is hyperbolic and local groups are quasi-convex in
\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}))
.

Remark:
In what follows we shall refer to the above theorem as ‘Martin’s theorem’.

The idea behind the proof of the above theorem came from
[
Dah03
]
. Both Dahmani and Martin for the proofs of their theorems
constructed a candidate compact metric space on which there is a natural action of the
fundamental group of the complex of groups. Then they showed that the actions were uniform
convergence actions. Finally invoking Bowditch’s theorem in
[
Bow98
]
they showed that the
fundamental group of the complex of groups is hyperbolic. In both cases for the proof to work
they needed all the face groups to be infinite. Contrary to this, in the first part of the paper we prove the following
theorem in this connection.

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a developable complex of groups over a finite simplicial complex
Y
such that all the edge groups are finite.
Suppose that the universal cover of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is a hyperbolic space. Then the fundamental group of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is hyperbolic relative to the
infinite vertex groups.

For the proof of the above theorem, we first show that the 1-skeleton of the universal cover is a fine graph; it is also hyperbolic by assumption.
The theorem then follows from Bowditch’s characterization (
[
Bow12
]
) of relatively hyperbolicity using fine hyperbolic graphs.

Corollary 0.2
.

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a developable complex of groups such that the vertex groups are hyperbolic and the edge groups are all finite.
Suppose the universal cover of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is a hyperbolic space. Then the fundamental group of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
, say
G
, is a hyperbolic group
and vertex groups are quasiconvex in
G
.

In the second part of the paper, motivated by the main theorem of
[
KS20
]
, we ask the following question.

Question.
Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a complex of groups satisfying the hypotheses of Martin’s theorem. Let
Y_{1}
be a connected subcomplex of
Y
and let
G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})
be the subcomplex of groups obtained by restricting
G({\mathcal{Y}})
to
Y_{1}
. Assume that the natural homomorphism from
H=\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1}))
to
G=\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}))
is injective. Under what condition(s), is the group
H
hyperbolic and when do we have a Cannon-Thurston map (see
[
Mj19
]
)
for the inclusion
H\rightarrow G
?

The following lemma was very useful for the proof of our next results.

Lemma 0.3
.

(
[
Mit98
, Lemma 2.1]
)

Suppose
X,Y
are hyperbolic geodesic metric spaces and
f:Y\rightarrow X
is an embedding.
Then
f
admits a Cannon-Thurston map if the following holds:

Given
y_{0}\in Y
there exists a non-negative function
M(N)
, such that
M(N)\rightarrow\infty
as
N\rightarrow\infty
and
such that for all geodesic segments
\lambda
lying outside
B(y_{0},N)
in
Y
, any geodesic segment in
X
joining the end points of

f(\lambda)
lies outside
B(f(y_{0}),M(N))
in
X
.

We note that the hypothesis of the above lemma implies that
f
is a proper embedding. Also
Mitra’s lemma gives a sufficient condition for the existence of a Cannon-Thurston map. It is not necessary (see
[
KS20
, Subsection 2.4]
)
unless the spaces involved are proper metric spaces. However, we abstract out the hypothesis of the above lemma as a definition.

Definition.

Suppose
X,Y
are geodesic hyperbolic metric spaces and
f:Y\rightarrow X
is any map.
We say that
f
admits a Cannon-Thurston map in the strong sense if the following holds:

Given
y_{0}\in Y
there exists a non-negative function
M(N)
, such that
M(N)\rightarrow\infty
as
N\rightarrow\infty

and such that for all geodesic segments
\lambda
lying outside
B(y_{0},N)
in
Y
, any geodesic segment in
X
joining end
points of
f(\lambda)
lies outside
B(f(y_{0}),M(N)
in
X
.

However, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 0.4
.

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a complex of groups as in Martin’s theorem and let
G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})
be a subcomplex of groups as in the above question.
Suppose that the natural homomorphism
H\rightarrow G
is injective. Suppose
B_{1}
is the universal cover of
G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})

and
B
is the universal cover of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
. Suppose
B_{1}
is a CAT(0) hyperbolic space, and that there exists Cannon-Thurston
map in the strong sense for the natural inclusion
B_{1}\rightarrow B
. Then the group
H
is hyperbolic and there exists
Cannon-Thurston map for the inclusion
H\rightarrow G
. Moreover,
H\rightarrow G
is a qi embedding if and only if
B_{1}\rightarrow B
admits an injective Cannon-Thurston map.

The idea of the proof of Theorem
0.4
is the following. Hyperbolicity of the group
H
is a direct consequence of Martin’s theorem.
Since both the complexes of groups are satisfying the hypotheses of Martin’s theorem, we can explicitly construct the Gromov boundary for
both
H
and
G
(see
[
Mar14
]
). Also from the construction of Gromov boundaries we have a natural
H
-equivariant map
\partial H\rightarrow\partial G
.
We prove that this map is continuous.

Some Examples.

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a complex of groups over a polygon
Y
satisfying all conditions of Martin’s theorem.
In addition we assume that
Y
has at least 4 edges. Let
Y_{1}
be an edge of
Y
and let
G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})
be
the subcomplex of groups obtained by restricting
G({\mathcal{Y}})
to
Y_{1}
. Let
H
denote the fundamental group of

G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})
and let
G
denote the fundamental group of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
. Then we have:

Proposition 0.5
.

The natural homomorphism
H\rightarrow G
is a qi embedding.

The idea of the proof of the above proposition is the following. Let
T
be the Bass-Serre tree of
G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})
.
We prove that the natural map from
T
to the universal cover of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is an isometric embedding. Then the proposition
follows from the last part of Theorem
0.4
.

The following special case of the proposition is worth mentioning.

Corollary 0.6
.

Let
G({\mathcal{Y}})
be a complex of groups over a polygon
Y
whose number of edges is
n\geq 4
. Assume that all the local groups are hyperbolic and all the local maps are qi embeddings and that the sum of group theoretical angles is less than
(n-2)\pi
. Also assume that the action of
\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}))
on the universal cover of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
is acylindrical. Let
Y_{1}
be an edge in
Y
and let
G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1})
be the restriction of
G({\mathcal{Y}})
to
Y_{1}
. Then
\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}))
is a hyperbolic group and
\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}_{1}))
is a quasi-convex subgroup of
\pi_{1}(G({\mathcal{Y}}))
.

For the definition of group theoretical angle at a corner of a polygon of groups, one is referred to
[
Sta91
]
.

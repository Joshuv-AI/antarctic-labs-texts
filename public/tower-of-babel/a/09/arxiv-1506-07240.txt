Convolution and Product Theorem for the Special Affine Fourier Transform
Ayush Bhandari, Ahmed. I. Zayed

Abstract
The Special Affine Fourier Transform or the SAFT generalizes a number of well known unitary transformations as well as signal processing and optics related mathematical operations. Unlike the Fourier transform, the SAFT does not work well with the standard convolution operation. Recently, Q. Xiang and K. Y. Qin introduced a new convolution operation that is more suitable for the SAFT and by which the SAFT of the convolution of two functions is the product of their SAFTs and a phase factor. However, their convolution structure does not work well with the inverse transform in sofar as the inverse transform of the product of two functions is not equal to the convolution of the transforms. In this article we introduce a new convolution operation that works well with both the SAFT and its inverse leading to an analogue of the convolution and product formulas for the Fourier transform. Furthermore, we introduce a second convolution operation that leads to the elimination of the phase factor in the convolution formula obtained by Q. Xiang and K. Y. Qin.

Introduction

Let
\mathscr{T}:f\to\widehat{f}
be a unitary, integral operator which maps
f

\widehat{f}

\mathscr{T}
is the Fourier operator, that is,

{\mathscr{T}_{\textsf{FT}}\left[f\right]}\left(\omega\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\widehat{f}\left(\omega\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{f\left(t\right){e^{-\jmath\omega t}}dt},

(1)

*
denote the standard convolution operator defined by

(f*g)(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)g(t-x)dx.

It is well known that for some functions

This result is known as the Fourier

I.2
Convolution Theorems for the FrFT and the LCT

In context of signal processing theory, Almeida first studied the fractional Fourier Transform (FrFT) domain representation of convolution and product operators
Almeida1997
. Unfortunately, Almeida’s formulation did not conform with the classical Fourier convolution–multiplication property. That is to say, the convolution of functions in time domain did not result in multiplication of their respective FrFT spectrums. As a follow up, Zayed formulated the convolution operation for the FrFT which resulted in an elegant convolution–multiplication property in FrFT domain
Zayed1998
.

Recently, Xiang and Qin
Xiang2012
introduced a new convolution operation that is more suitable for the SAFT and by which the SAFT of the convolution of two functions is the product of their SAFTs and a phase factor. However, their convolution structure does not work well with the inverse transform in sofar as the inverse transform of the product of two functions is not equal to the convolution of the transforms.

In this article we introduce a new convolution operation that works well with both the SAFT and its inverse leading to an analogue of the convolution and product formulas for the Fourier transform. Furthermore, we introduce a second convolution operation that leads to the elimination of the phase factor in the convolution formula obtained in

I.3
Special Affine Fourier Transform (SAFT)

The SAFT was introduced by Abe and Sheridan
Abe1994
;
Abe1994a
who studied a transformation in phase space that was associated with a general, inhomogeneous, lossless linear mapping. Such a transformation can model a number of optical operations such as rotation and magnification (see Table

Let

denote the complex–conjugate of
f

\left\langle{f,g}\right\rangle=\int{f\left(t\right){g^{*}}\left(t\right)dt}
be the standard

\mathscr{T}_{\textsf{SAFT}}:f\to\widehat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}

{\widehat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}}\left(\omega\right)=\begin{cases}{\left\langle{f,{\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}}\left({\cdot,\omega}\right)}\right\rangle}&{b\neq 0}\\
{\sqrt{d}{e^{{\jmath}\frac{{cd}}{2}{{\left({\omega-p}\right)}^{2}}+{\jmath}\omega q}}x\left({d\left({\omega-p}\right)}\right)}&{b=0}\end{cases},

(4)

where,

•

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{(2\times 3)}
is the augmented SAFT parameter matrix of form,

{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}=\left[{\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}&\vline&{\underline{\lambda}}\end{array}}\right]

(5)

which is in turn parameterized by the LCT matrix
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\textsf{L}}

Moshinsky
(see Table
1

{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}=\bigl[{\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}}\bigr]\mbox{ with }ad-bc=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\underline{\lambda}}=\bigl[{\begin{smallmatrix}p\\
q\end{smallmatrix}}\bigr].

This is the reason the SAFT is sometimes referred to as the
Offset Linear Canonical Transform
or the OLCT.

•

\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(t,\omega\right)
in (
3
) is the SAFT kernel parameterized by SAFT matrix
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}
.

Thanks to the additive property of the SAFT/OLCT

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}
with parameters,

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\left[{\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{+d}&{-b}&\vline&{bq-dp}\\

so that we can obtain a representation of form,

\mathscr{T}_{\textsf{SAFT}}\left[f*_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}g\right]\propto\mathscr{T}_{\textsf{SAFT}}\left[f\right]\mathscr{T}_{\textsf{SAFT}}\left[g\right]

which is consistent with the Fourier convolution theorem (

II
Convolution Theorem for the SAFT

Before we define the convolution operation in the SAFT domain, let us introduce the chirp modulation operation.

Definition 1

(Chirp Modulation)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}=[a_{j,k}]
be a
2\times 2
matrix. We define the modulation function,

m_{\mathbf{A}}\left(t\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\exp\left(\jmath\frac{a_{11}}{2a_{12}}t^{2}\right).

(8)

Furthermore, for a given function
f,
we define its chirp modulated functions associated with the matrix
\mathbf{A}
as,

{\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{f}}}\left({t}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}m_{\mathbf{A}}\left(t\right)f\left(t\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\leftharpoonup}$}}{{f}}}\left({t}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}m^{*}_{\mathbf{A}}\left(t\right)f\left(t\right).

(9)

For example, let
\mathbf{A}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}
, then, we have,
{\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{f}}}\left({t}\right)=m_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}f\left(t\right)=e^{\jmath\frac{at^{2}}{2b}}f\left(t\right)
. For the case when
\mathbf{A}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}
, we get,
{\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{f}}}\left({t}\right)=m_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\left(t\right)f\left(t\right)=e^{-\jmath\frac{dt^{2}}{2b}}f\left(t\right)
.

Next we define the SAFT convolution operator.

Definition 2

(SAFT Convolution/Filtering)
.

Let
f
and
g
be two given functions and
*
denote the usual convolution operation (see (
2
)). The SAFT convolution is defined by,

h\left(t\right)=\left(f*_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}g\right)\left(t\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}K_{b}m^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(t\right)\left({\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{f}}}\left({t}\right)*{\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{g}}}\left({t}\right)\right).

(10)

Figure 1 illustrates the block diagram for SAFT domain convolution defined in Definition 2. Next, we state the convolution and product theorem for the SAFT domain.

Theorem 1

(SAFT Convolution and Product Theorem)
.

Let
f
and
g
be any two given functions for which the convolution
*_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}
exists and set,

h\left(t\right)=\left(f*_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}g\right)\left(t\right).

Furthermore, let
\widehat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right),\widehat{g}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)
and
\widehat{h}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)
be the SAFT of
f,g
and
h
, respectively. Then we have,

h\left(t\right)=\left(f*_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}g\right)\left(t\right)\xrightarrow{{{\mathsf{SAFT}}}}\widehat{h}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)=\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)\widehat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)\widehat{g}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right),

where
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)={e^{\jmath\frac{\omega}{b}\left({dp-bq}\right)}}{e^{-\jmath\frac{{d{\omega^{2}}}}{{2b}}}}
. Moreover, let,

h\left(t\right)=\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\left(t\right)f\left(t\right)g\left(t\right)\mbox{ with }\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\left(t\right)={e^{\jmath\frac{{a{t^{2}}}}{{2b}}}}{e^{-\jmath\frac{t}{b}\left({a{p_{0}}+b{q_{0}}}\right)}},

then, we have,

\widehat{h}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)=C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\left(\widehat{f}*_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\widehat{g}\right)\left(\omega\right)
.

Proof.

We begin with computing the SAFT of
h
,

\displaystyle\widehat{h}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{saft})}}{{=}}\mathscr{T}_{\textsf{SAFT}}\left[h\right]\left(\omega\right)=\left\langle{h\left(t\right),\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left({t,\omega}\right)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{h\left(t\right){\kappa^{*}}\left({t,\omega}\right)dt}\hfill

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{SAFTconv})}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\left({K_{b}m^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(t\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}{{\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{f}}}\left({z}\right){\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{g}}}\left({t-z}\right)dz}}\right){\kappa^{*}}\left({t,\omega}\right)dt}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{{K_{b}^{2}}{e^{\jmath\frac{{d{\omega^{2}}}}{{2b}}}}{e^{-\jmath\frac{\omega}{b}\left({dp-bq}\right)}}}_{C\left(\omega\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{{e^{\jmath\frac{t}{b}\left({p-\omega}\right)}}}\boxed{m_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(t\right)}\left({\boxed{m_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}^{*}\left(t\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{{\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{f}}}\left({z}\right){\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{g}}}\left({t-z}\right)dz}}\right)dt.

In the above development, note that the items in the box cancel one another because
m^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}m_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}=1
(see Definition
9
). Setting
t-z=v
and using (
9
), we obtain an integral of separable form, that is,
\widehat{h}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)={I_{f}}\left(\omega\right){I_{g}}\left(\omega\right)
because,

\displaystyle\underbrace{{K_{b}^{2}}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}^{*}\left(\omega\right)\iint_{\mathbb{R}}{f\left(x\right)m_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(x\right)g\left(v\right)m_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(v\right){e^{\jmath\frac{{v+x}}{b}\left({p-\omega}\right)}}dxdv}}_{\widehat{h}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad={I_{f}}\left(\omega\right){I_{g}}\left(\omega\right)=\widehat{h}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right),

(11)

where, for a given function
f
, we define,

{I_{f}}\left(\omega\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}{K_{b}}\sqrt{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}^{*}\left(\omega\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{f\left(z\right)m_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(z\right){e^{\jmath\frac{z}{b}\left({p-\omega}\right)}}dz}.

(12)

Indeed, using (
12
) and (
4
), it is easy to see that,

I_{f}\left(\omega\right)=\sqrt{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}^{*}\left(\omega\right)}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)\widehat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)

(13)

and this result extends to
I_{g}\left(\omega\right)
by symmetry.

We conclude,

\displaystyle\widehat{h}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{sepint})}}{{=}}I_{f}\left(\omega\right)I_{g}\left(\omega\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{simplifiedIF})}}{{=}}\sqrt{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}^{*}\left(\omega\right)}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)\widehat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)\cdot\sqrt{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}^{*}\left(\omega\right)}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)\widehat{g}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)\widehat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)\widehat{g}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)

which is the statement of part I of Theorem
1

Now we establish the product theorem for the SAFT,

{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}}\left(t\right)f\left(t\right)g\left(t\right)\xrightarrow{{{\mathsf{SAFT}}}}C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\left(\widehat{f}*_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\widehat{g}\right)\left(\omega\right).

Since the inverse–SAFT is the SAFT of a function with
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}
in (
6
), we have,

\displaystyle h\left(t\right)=

\displaystyle C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}K_{b}^{*}\times

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\widehat{h}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right){e^{-\jmath\frac{{\left({a{t^{2}}+d{\omega^{2}}}\right)}}{{2b}}}}{e^{-\jmath\frac{\omega}{b}\left({{p_{0}}-t}\right)}}{e^{\jmath\frac{t}{b}\left({a{p_{0}}+b{q_{0}}}\right)}}d\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}K_{b}^{*}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}^{*}\left(t\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\widehat{h}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right){e^{-\jmath\frac{{d{\omega^{2}}}}{{2b}}}}{e^{-\jmath\frac{{\omega\left({{p_{0}}-t}\right)}}{b}}}d\omega}.

By setting,

\displaystyle\widehat{h}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)

\displaystyle=C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\left(\widehat{f}*_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\widehat{g}\right)\left(\omega\right)

\displaystyle=C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}K_{b}^{*}m_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}^{*}\left(\omega\right)\left({\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{\widehat{f}}}}\left({\omega}\right)*{\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{\widehat{g}}}}\left({\omega}\right)\right),

where, for
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}
(see (
6
)), we have,

m_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\left(\cdot\right)=e^{-\jmath\frac{d\left(\cdot\right)^{2}}{2b}}\mbox{ and }{\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{f}}}\left({\omega}\right)=m_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\left(\omega\right)f\left(\omega\right).

Upon simplification, we obtain the separable integrals,

\displaystyle h\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=\left(C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}K_{b}^{*}\right)^{2}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}^{*}\left(t\right)\iint\widehat{f}\left(\nu\right)m_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\left(\nu\right)\widehat{g}\left(\omega-\nu\right)m_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\left(\omega-\nu\right)d\nu e^{-\jmath\frac{{\omega\left({{p_{0}}-t}\right)}}{b}}d\omega

\displaystyle=\int{\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{\widehat{f}}}}\left({\nu}\right)e^{-\jmath\frac{{\nu\left({{p_{0}}-t}\right)}}{b}}d\nu\int{\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{\widehat{g}}}}\left({\omega}\right)e^{-\jmath\frac{{\omega\left({{p_{0}}-t}\right)}}{b}}d\omega

\displaystyle=I_{f}\left(t\right)I_{g}\left(t\right),

where,

\displaystyle I_{f}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=C_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}K_{b}^{*}\sqrt{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}^{*}\left(t\right)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{f}}}\left({\omega}\right)}e^{-\jmath\frac{{\omega\left({{p_{0}}-t}\right)}}{b}}d\omega.

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}^{*}\left(t\right)}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\left(t\right)f\left(t\right).

As a result, we have,

\displaystyle h\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=I_{f}\left(t\right)I_{g}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}^{*}\left(t\right)}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\left(t\right)f\left(t\right)\cdot\sqrt{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}^{*}\left(t\right)}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\left(t\right)g\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}^{\textrm{inv}}}\left(t\right)f\left(t\right)g\left(t\right)

which is the desired result.
∎

III
Comparison and Alternative Results

In this section we compare our results with those of
Xiang2012
. We show that our approach is not only easier to derive, but also provides more symmetric
formulas to implement. Our convolution formula is the same as the one given in
Xiang2012
, both assert that the SAFT of the convolution of two functions is the product of their SAFT
and a phase factor given by
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)
. But our product formula is different from that in
Xiang2012
which states that the SAFT of the product of two functions
f
and
g
is,

K_{b}^{2}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}^{*}\left(\omega\right)\left({\widehat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)*\widehat{g}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{FT}}}\left({\frac{\omega}{b}}\right)}\right).

(14)

The reason our convolution and product formulas are more symmetric and simpler goes back to our definition of the chirp modulation, see Definition
1
, which uses the adaptive matrix
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}
that accommodates both the forward and backward SAFT.

Furthermore, we will now derive another convolution for SAFT which eliminates the phase factor
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}
from the convolution formula.

Definition 3

(Phase–free SAFT Convolution)
.

Let
f
and
g
be two given functions and
*
denote the usual convolution operation. The second SAFT convolution
\star
is defined by,

h(t)=(f\star g)(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sqrt{2}K_{b}m^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(t\right)\left({\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{f}}}\left({.}\right)*{\overset{\lower 4.09723pt\hbox{$\smash{\scriptscriptstyle\rightharpoonup}$}}{{g}}}\left({.}\right)\right)(\sqrt{2}t).

In view of this SAFT–convolution, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
h(t)=(f\star g)(t).
Then, we have,

\widehat{h}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)=\widehat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{1}}}\left(\omega/\sqrt{2}\right)\widehat{g}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{1}}}\left(\omega/\sqrt{2}\right),

where
\widehat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{1}}
denotes the SAFT of
f
with respect to the matrix
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{1}}=\left[\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\ \ |\ \ {\underline{\lambda}}/\sqrt{2}\right]
(cf. (
5
)).

Proof.

Let
\Omega=bq-dp
. We have,

\displaystyle\widehat{h}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}K^{2}_{b}e^{-\frac{{\jmath}at^{2}}{2b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\frac{{\jmath}}{2b}\left(at^{2}+d\omega^{2}+2tp-2t\omega+2\omega\Omega\right)}dt

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\tau)e^{\frac{{\jmath}at^{2}}{2b}}g(\sqrt{2}t-\tau)e^{\frac{{\jmath}a}{2b}(\sqrt{2}t-\tau)^{2}}d\tau.

Setting
x=\sqrt{2}t-\tau
and simplifying the integrals, we obtain,

\displaystyle\widehat{h}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)

\displaystyle=K^{2}_{b}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}^{*}(\omega)\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\frac{{\jmath}a\tau^{2}}{2b}}f(\tau)d\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)e^{\frac{{\jmath}}{2b}\left(ax^{2}+2(p-\omega)(x+\tau)/\sqrt{2}\right)}dx

\displaystyle=K^{2}_{b}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}^{*}(\omega)\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\frac{{\jmath}}{2b}\left(a\tau^{2}+2(p-\omega)\tau/\sqrt{2}\right)}f(\tau)d\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)e^{\frac{{\jmath}}{2b}\left(ax^{2}+2(p-\omega)x/\sqrt{2}\right)}dx

\displaystyle=K^{2}_{b}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}^{*}(\omega)\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\frac{{\jmath}}{2b}\left(a\tau^{2}+\sqrt{2}p\tau-\sqrt{2}\tau\omega\right)}f(\tau)d\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\frac{{\jmath}}{2b}\left(ax^{2}+\sqrt{2}px-\sqrt{2}x\omega)\right)}g(x)dx.

But since,

\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}^{*}(\omega)={e^{\jmath\frac{1}{{2b}}\left({2d{{\left({\frac{\omega}{{\sqrt{2}}}}\right)}^{2}}+2\sqrt{2}\Omega\frac{\omega}{{\sqrt{2}}}}\right)}},

it follows, that
\widehat{h}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}}\left(\omega\right)=I_{f}(\omega)I_{g}(\omega)
,
where

I_{f}(\omega)=K_{b}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\frac{{\jmath}}{2b}\left(a\tau^{2}+d\left(\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{2}+\sqrt{2}p\tau-\sqrt{2}\tau\omega+\sqrt{2}\Omega(\omega/\sqrt{2})\right]}f(\tau)d\tau

and similar expression for
I_{g}(\omega).
But it is easy to see that

I_{f}(\omega)=\widehat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{1}}}\left(\omega/\sqrt{2}\right),

and this completes the proof.
∎

Relation to Convolution theory of LCTs:
In the special case where
p=q=0\Leftrightarrow\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{S}}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{LCT}}
, the SAFT reduces to the LCT and the last convolution theorem takes the simple form

\mathscr{T}_{\textsf{LCT}}[f\star g](\omega)=\mathscr{T}_{\textsf{LCT}}[f]\left(\omega/\sqrt{2}\right)\mathscr{T}_{\textsf{LCT}}[g]\left(\omega/\sqrt{2}\right).

IV
Conclusion

In this letter, we introduced two definitions of convolution operation that establish the convolution–product theorem for the Special Affine Fourier Transform (SAFT) introduced by Abe and Sheridan
Abe1994
;
Abe1994a
. Our result is quite general in that our convolution–product theorem is applicable to all the listed unitary transformations in Table
1
. Furthermore, we also presented a product theorem for the SAFT which establishes the fact that the product of functions in time amounts to convolution in SAFT domain. We conclude that our construction of the convolution structure for the SAFT domain establishes the SAFT duality principle, that is, convolution in one domain amounts to multiplication in the transform domain and vice–versa. Our results can be used to develop the semi–discrete convolution structure
Bhandari2012
for sampling and approximation theory linked with the Special Affine Fourier Transform, but this will be done in a separate project.

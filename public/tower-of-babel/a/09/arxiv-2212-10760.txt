Critical quantum sensing based on the Jaynes-Cummings model with a squeezing drive
Jia-Hao Lü, Wen Ning, Xin Zhu, Fan Wu, Li-Tuo Shen, Zhen-Biao Yang, Shi-Biao Zheng

Abstract
Quantum sensing improves the accuracy of measurements of relevant parameters by exploiting the unique properties of quantum systems. The divergent susceptibility of physical systems near a critical point for quantum phase transition enables criticality-enhanced quantum sensing. The quantum Rabi model (QRM), composed of a single qubit coupled to a single bosonic field, represents a good candidate for realizing such critical enhancement for its simplicity, but it is experimentally challenging to achieve the ultrastrong qubit-field coupling required to realize the critical phenomena. In this work, we explore an alternative to construct the analog of the QRM for the sensing, exploiting the criticality appearing in the Jaynes-Cummings (JC) model whose bosonic field is parametrically driven, not necessitating the ultrastrong coupling condition thus to some extent relaxing the requirement for the practical implementation.

INTRODUCTION

Quantum sensing makes use of the unique properties, such as quantum coherence or entanglement, in quantum physics, to improve the sensitivity of measurement
Bollinger et al. 1996
;
Giovannetti et al. 2004
;
Giovannetti et al. 2006
;
Giovannetti et al. 2011
;
Degen et al. 2017
. Recently, it was realized that high-precision quantum metrology could be realized by encoding the signal in a quantum system near its critical point, where it exhibits an ultrasensitive response to a tiny change in the relevant parameter
Raghunandan et al. 2018
;
Gammelmark and Mølmer 2011
;
Yang and Jacob 2019
;
Luo et al. 2022
;
Dutta and Cooper 2019
;
Ivanov 2020
;
Garbe et al. 2020
;
Chu et al. 2021
;
Ilias et al. 2022
;
Montenegro et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2021a
. So far, two complementary approaches have been proposed for criticality-enhanced quantum sensing based on equilibrium or steady state response to the signal change. One approach is to exploit the equilibrium properties of critical systems, whose Hamiltonians are slowly varied to ensure the system to remain in the ground states
Tsang 2013
;
Garbe et al. 2020
;
Macieszczak et al. 2016
;
Wald et al. 2020
. For an open system under the interplay between the Hamiltonian dynamics and the dissipative process, its nonequilibrium behavior in a steady state near a dissipative-driven phase transition can also be utilized for critical sensing
Wald et al. 2020
;
Fernández-Lorenzo and Porras 2017
;
Heugel et al. 2019
;
Soriente et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2021b
. Both methods suffer from the relatively long-time evolution required for satisfying the adiabaticity
Rams et al. 2018
, restricting their practical implementations. To overcome this problem, a dynamical method was recently proposed
Chu et al. 2021
, where the signal is encoded in a dynamically-evolved feature, without requirement of adiabatic or slow quench condition. Furthermore, this method is quite general, working even for a mixed state.

The quantum Rabi model (QRM)
Ashhab and Nori 2010
;
Ashhab 2013
;
Ballester et al. 2012
;
Garziano et al. 2014
is an ideal system for realizing these approaches. The QRM, which describes the interaction between a two-level atom and a single-mode bosonic field, has been widely investigated in quantum information and technology. Under the condition that the ratio between the atomic transition frequency and the field frequency tends to infinity, such a model can undergo a quantum phase transition of the normal phase to the superradiant phase, featuring a sudden increase of the photon number at a critical point of the coupling-to-frequency ratio
Hwang et al. 2015
;
Sachdev 2011
;
Kirkova et al. 2022
;
Gietka 2022
;
Puebla et al. 2016
;
Zhang et al. 2021
;
Shen et al. 2021
;
Cai et al. 2021
;
Hwang et al. 2018
;
Wang et al. 2018
. However, the criticality accompanied by the quantum phase transition realized in the QRM requires the ultra-strong coupling between the two-level system and the single-mode bosonic field
Braumüller et al. 2017
;
Lv et al. 2018
, so that the counter-rotating-wave terms play a significant role, which represents an experimental challenge. Another restriction originates from the so-called no-go theorem
Rzażewski et al. 1975
;
Vukics et al. 2014
;
Nataf and Ciuti 2010
, which states the neglected
A^{2}
term would prohibit the superradiant phase transition. The dynamics of the QRM has been simulated on a variety of platforms, such as superconducting circuit systems
Braumüller et al. 2017
;
Langford et al. 2017
and the trapped ions
Lv et al. 2018
. Very recently, the superradiant phase transition was observed in an effective QRM, composed of a superconducting qubit and with a microwave photonic field stored in a resonator
Zheng et al.
, whose interaction was engineered with two deliberately tailored longitudinal modulations and a transverse drive. However, experimental demonstration of criticality-enhanced quantum sensing in such a system remains elusive.

We here explore the criticality-enhanced sensing with a squeezed JC model (SJCM), where the bosonic field is subjected to a parametric squeezing drive. This parametric driving transforms the rotating-wave coupling of the JC model into a combination of the rotating and counter-rotating couplings, realizing an isomorphism of the QRM, where the system frequencies are replaced by their detunings from the driving
Gutiérrez-Jáuregui and Agarwal 2021
. Such an isomorphism offers the possibility to explore light-matter interactions in the ultra-strong coupling regime based on the JCM, to bypass the no-go theorem to access the superradiant phase transition predicted in the QRM
Zhu et al. 2020
, and to harness the associated critical phenomena for quantum technology.

With this quantum critical dynamics, a divergent behavior emerges in the quantum Fisher information (QFI) under certain conditions when it approaches the critical point, allowing for a ultrasensitive measurement of the relevant observable. The results of our study show that such a scheme can achieve the precision close to the quantum Cramér-Rao bound
Braunstein and Caves 1994
;
Escher et al. 2012
;
Fiderer et al. 2019
, which is given by the Cramér-Rao inequality
\langle\Delta^{2}\hat{\theta}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{\nu\mathcal{F}}
, where
\langle\Delta^{2}\hat{\theta}\rangle
is the variance of observable
\hat{\theta}
,
\nu
is the amount of data and
\mathcal{F}
represents the QFI. The QFI gives an absolute lower bound on the measurement of an input state, independent of the measurement method, and is equivalent to the inverse variance of the measurement, which provides great convenience to reflect the achievable measurement precision. We also give the analytical result of the QFI for the correlated quantum dynamics, and propose a scheme for experimental implementation of the model.

The QFI in the critical quantum systems

The QFI about the parameter
\alpha
can be expressed as
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}=4{\rm Var}[h_{\alpha}]_{|\Phi\rangle}
,
where

is the variance of

with respect to the initial state
|\Phi\rangle

Pang and Jordan 2017
.
We consider the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\alpha}=\hat{H}_{0}+\alpha\hat{H}_{1}

\displaystyle[\hat{H}_{\alpha},\hat{\Upsilon}]=\sqrt{\Delta}\hat{\Upsilon},

(1)

where
\hat{\Upsilon}=i\sqrt{\Delta}\hat{M}-\hat{N}
with
\hat{M}=-i[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{H}_{1}]

\hat{N}=-[\hat{H}_{\alpha},[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{H}_{1}]]

\Delta
depends on the parameter
\alpha

\epsilon\sim\sqrt{\Delta}
for
\Delta>0
and becomes imaginary if
\Delta<0

Chu et al. 2021
, which shows that the quantum phase transition behaviors occur at the critical point
\alpha=\alpha_{c}
defined by
\Delta=0
. Besides,

can be expanded as

\displaystyle h_{\alpha}=-i\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(it)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}[\hat{H}_{\alpha},\hat{H}_{1}]_{n},

(2)

where
[\hat{H}_{\alpha},\hat{H}_{1}]_{n}=[\hat{H}_{\alpha},[\hat{H}_{\alpha},\hat{H}_{1}]_{n-1}]

\displaystyle h_{\alpha}=\hat{H}_{1}t+\frac{\cos{(\sqrt{\Delta}t)}-1}{\Delta}\hat{M}-\frac{\sin{(\sqrt{\Delta}t)}-\sqrt{\Delta}t}{\Delta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\hat{N}.

(3)

It shows that as

is dominant. And, the QFI can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(t)\simeq 4\frac{[\sin{(\sqrt{\Delta}t)}-\sqrt{\Delta}t]^{2}}{\Delta^{3}}{\rm Var}[\hat{N}]_{|\Phi\rangle}.

(4)

If

is divergent at
\Delta=0
and scales with

. As was pointed out in Ref.
Chu et al. 2021

VI
conclusion

In summary, we have investigated quantum sensing based on critical phenomena of the SJCM. The two-photon drive enables the JCM to exhibit QRM-like dynamics and associated critical behaviors, without the requirement to reach the ultrastrong coupling regime. The model can be readily realized in different spin-boson systems. In an ion trap, the JC interaction between the internal and external degrees of freedom of a trapped ion can be mediated by a laser tuned to the first red sideband, while the squeezing driving can be realized with a Raman-type driving
Meekhof et al. 1996
. For a circuit quantum electrodynamics architecture, the interaction between a superconducting qubit and a resonator is naturally described by the JCM, and the squeezing driving can be realized by a nonlinear process
Leghtas et al. 2015
;
Grimm et al. 2020
. These experimental advances make it possible to engineer the SJCM, and realize the critical dynamics for enhanced quantum sensing.

\displaystyle\chi_{\omega}(t)=\partial_{\omega}\langle\hat{X}\rangle_{t}=-\frac{1}{2g(\omega-2G)}\chi_{g}(t).

Appendix B
Derivation of local observable of the two-level system of the SJCM

In the main text, we show that one can obtain the information of the parameter by measuring the two-level system directly
ref
. It obtains the observable
\langle\hat{\sigma}_{x}\rangle=Re[\langle\varphi|u^{{\dagger}}_{\uparrow}u_{\downarrow}|\varphi\rangle]
if we set
2c^{*}_{1}c_{2}=1
for the initial state of the two-level system. The inverted variance to estimate the precision of the parameter
g
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{g}=\frac{(\partial_{g}\langle\hat{\sigma}_{x}\rangle)^{2}}{1-\langle\hat{\sigma}_{x}\rangle^{2}}=\frac{Re[\partial_{g}\langle\varphi|u^{{\dagger}}_{\uparrow}u_{\downarrow}|\varphi\rangle]^{2}}{1-Re[\langle\varphi|u^{{\dagger}}_{\uparrow}u_{\downarrow}|\varphi\rangle]^{2}}.

(19)

The initial state
|\varphi\rangle
of the bosonic field in the eigenbasis of
\hat{H}^{\uparrow}_{np}=(\omega+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\Omega})\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a}-G(\hat{a}^{{\dagger}2}+\hat{a}^{2})

\hat{H}^{\downarrow}_{np}=(\omega-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\Omega})\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a}-G(\hat{a}^{{\dagger}2}+\hat{a}^{2})

\displaystyle|\varphi\rangle=\sum_{n}c^{\uparrow}_{n}|\phi^{\uparrow}_{n}\rangle=\sum_{n}c^{\downarrow}_{n}|\phi^{\downarrow}_{n}\rangle,

(20)

where
|\phi^{\sigma}_{n}\rangle=\hat{S}[r_{\sigma}]|n\rangle

\hat{S}[r_{\sigma}]=exp[-(r_{\sigma}/2)(\hat{a}^{{\dagger}2}-\hat{a}^{2})]

r_{\downarrow}=\frac{1}{4}\ln{\frac{\omega-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\Omega}-2G}{\omega-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\Omega}+2G}}

\displaystyle\langle\varphi|u^{{\dagger}}_{\uparrow}u_{\downarrow}|\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle\langle\varphi|e^{i\hat{H}^{\uparrow}_{np}t}e^{-i\hat{H}^{\downarrow}_{np}t}|\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n}c^{\uparrow*}_{m}c^{\downarrow}_{n}e^{i(mE_{\uparrow}-nE_{\downarrow})t}\langle\phi^{\uparrow}_{m}|\phi^{\downarrow}_{n}\rangle,

\tau=4\pi/\sqrt{\Delta}

\displaystyle e^{-i\hat{H}^{\downarrow}_{np}\tau}|\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle\sum_{n}c^{\downarrow}_{n}e^{-in\sqrt{(\omega-{\lambda^{2}}/{\Omega})^{2}-4G^{2}})\tau}|\phi^{\downarrow}_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\sum_{n}c^{\downarrow}_{n}e^{-i2\pi n}|\phi^{\downarrow}_{n}\rangle=|\varphi\rangle.

\displaystyle\langle\varphi|e^{i\hat{H}^{\uparrow}_{np}\tau}|\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle[\langle\varphi|(\partial_{\alpha}u^{\dagger}_{\uparrow})u_{\downarrow}|\varphi\rangle+\langle\varphi|u^{\dagger}_{\uparrow}(\partial_{\alpha}u_{\downarrow})|\varphi\rangle]\frac{\partial\alpha}{\partial g}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-i\frac{\sin(\sqrt{\Delta}t)-\sqrt{\Delta}t}{\Delta^{\frac{3}{2}}}16Gg_{c}(\omega-2G)

Vaughan–Lee’s nilpotent loop of size 12 12 is finitely based Thanks: This material is based upon work supported by the National Science Foundation grant no. DMS 1500254
Peter Mayr

Abstract
. From work of Vaughan–Lee in [ 7 ] it follows that if a finite nilpotent loop splits into a direct product of factors of prime power order, then its equational theory has a finite basis. Whether the condition on the direct decomposition is necessary has remained open since. In the same paper, Vaughan–Lee gives an explicit example of a nilpotent loop of order 12 12 that does not factor into loops of prime power order and asks whether it is finitely based. We give a finite basis for his example by explicitly characterizing its term functions. This also allows us to show that the subpower membership problem for this loop can be solved in polynomial time.

1.
Introduction

In
[
7
]
Vaughan–Lee showed that every finite nilpotent Mal’cev algebra of finite type that has an
equationally defined constant and splits into a direct product of algebras or prime power order
is finitely based. In
[
3
]
Freese and McKenzie generalized this result by removing the assumption
that the algebra has a constant.
Hence a finite nilpotent Mal’cev algebra
{\mathbf{A}}
has a finite basis for its equational theory if it satisfies
one of the following equivalent conditions for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
:

(1)

{\mathbf{A}}
is a direct product of algebras of prime power order;

(2)

all
commutator terms
on
{\mathbf{A}}
, i.e., term functions satisfying
c(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},z)=z
whenever

x_{i}=z
for some
i\leq k
, have essential arity at most
n+1
;

(3)

{\mathbf{A}}
is
n
-
supernilpotent
, i.e.,
[1_{A},\dots,1_{A}]=0_{A}
for the
(n+1)
-ary
higher commutator
.

The equivalence (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) follows from
[
5
, Theorem 3.14]
and (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(3)
from
[
1
, Lemma 7.6]
.

Vaughan–Lee, Freese and McKenzie proved their finite basis results syntactically by developing normal forms
for terms with respect to commutator terms and then making crucial use of the bounded arity of the latter.
Since their work in the 1980s, it remains open whether conditions (1)-(3) above are necessary, that is:

Question 1.1
.

{qu:basis}

Is every finite nilpotent Mal’cev algebra of finite type finitely based?

Unlike for groups, not every finite nilpotent Mal’cev algebra is isomorphic to a direct product of
algebras of prime power order. Already in
[
7
]
Vaughan–Lee gave the following example of an
indecomposable
12
-element nilpotent loop
\mathbf{L}
and asked whether it is finitely based.

For
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{12}
and
C:=4{\mathbb{Z}}_{12}
, let
\bar{x}:=x+C
.
Define binary operations

t\colon{\mathbb{Z}}_{12}^{2}\to C,(x,y)\mapsto\begin{cases}4&\text{if }(x,y)\in\bar{1}\times\bar{3}\cup\bar{3}\times\bar{1},\\
0&\text{else},\end{cases}

and

x\cdot y:=x+y+t(x,y)\quad\text{ for }x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{12}.

Then
\mathbf{L}:=\langle{{\mathbb{Z}}_{12}},{\cdot}\rangle
is a commutative
2
-nilpotent loop with center
C
.
The congruences of
\mathbf{L}
are induced by the normal subloops
0,C,2{\mathbb{Z}}_{12},{\mathbb{Z}}_{12}
.
In particular
\mathbf{L}
is subdirectly irreducible and does not split into a direct product of loops of prime power orders.
Consequently it is not supernilpotent.

To the best of our knowledge there are no published results on the equational bases for any nilpotent,
not supernilpotent Mal’cev algebras.
We present a detailed analysis of Vaughan–Lee’s loop
\mathbf{L}
as a test case and starting point for a future,
hopefully more systematic approach to Question
1.1
.

First we note that
\mathbf{L}
has the group
\langle{{\mathbb{Z}}_{12}},{+}\rangle
as reduct, and
we explicitly characterize its set of
k
-ary term functions
\mathrm{Clo}_{k}(\mathbf{L})
for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

{th:clone}

For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
let

W_{k}:=\{w\colon{\mathbb{Z}}_{12}^{k}\to C\ :\ w(2{\mathbb{Z}}_{12}^{k})=0,w(x)=w(-x)\text{ for all }x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{12}^{k}\}.

Then
\mathrm{Clo}_{k}(\mathbf{L})=\mathrm{Clo}_{k}(\langle{{\mathbb{Z}}_{12}},{+}\rangle)\oplus W_{k}
.

Here
\oplus
denotes the direct sum in the group of all
k
-ary functions
\langle{{\mathbb{Z}}_{12}},{+}\rangle^{{\mathbb{Z}}_{12}^{k}}
.
The proof of Theorem
1.2
makes up all of Section
2
.

From Theorem
1.2
we obtain normal forms for term functions and terms on
\mathbf{L}
and eventually
a finite basis in Section
4
.

Theorem 1.3
.

{th:basis}

\mathbf{L}
is finitely based.

A description of the term clone as above also has computational applications.
The
Subpower Membership Problem

\mathrm{SMP}
for an algebra
{\mathbf{A}}
is the following decision problem:

\mathrm{SMP}({\mathbf{A}})

Input:

a_{1},\ldots,a_{k},b\in A^{n}

Problem:

Is
b
in the subalgebra
\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\rangle
of
{\mathbf{A}}^{n}
generated by
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
?

Willard observed that the Subpower Membership Problem for finite groups and rings can be solved in polynomial time
and asked whether it is in P for any finite algebra with cube term (in particular, for every Mal’cev algebra)

[
4
]
. This question remains open in general but there are some partial results.
Together with Bulatov and Szendrei, the author showed that
\mathrm{SMP}({\mathbf{A}})
is in NP for every finite algebra

{\mathbf{A}}
with a cube term
[
2
]
.
In
[
6
]
we gave a polynomial time algorithm for
\mathrm{SMP}
for finite supernilpotent Mal’cev algebras
using commutator terms and the techniques developed by Vaughan–Lee, Freese and McKenzie for their finite basis results.
Like the answer for the finite basis question, we do not know the computational complexity of subpower membership
for nilpotent, not supernilpotent Mal’cev algebras (even for loops) in general.

However, using the explicit description of term functions in Theorem
1.2
, it is not hard to reduce
\mathrm{SMP}
for
Vaughan–Lee’s loop
\mathbf{L}
to
\mathrm{SMP}
of the group
\langle{{\mathbb{Z}}_{12}},{+}\rangle
. Hence we obtain the following in Section
3
:

Theorem 1.4
.

{th:smp}

\mathrm{SMP}(\mathbf{L})
is in
P
.

We mention without proof that similar but somewhat less involved arguments show that all nilpotent loops of
order
6
or
10
are finitely based and have tractable
\mathrm{SMP}
as well.

Our approach in this paper is tailored to the particular example we consider and has obvious limitations.
In general a full characterization of term functions of arbitrary nilpotent Mal’cev algebras as in
Theorem
1.2
may not be feasible even when restricting the nilpotence degree.
Furthermore, a description of the term clone does not automatically yield a finite basis for the algebra
as we see from the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Still our admittedly limited evidence leads us to
propose the following problems as steps towards a possible positive solution of Question
1.1
.

Question 1.5
.

Does every finite nilpotent Mal’cev algebra have a supernilpotent reduct?

Question 1.6
.

Is every nilpotent expansion of a finite abelian (more generally supernilpotent) Mal’cev algebra finitely based?

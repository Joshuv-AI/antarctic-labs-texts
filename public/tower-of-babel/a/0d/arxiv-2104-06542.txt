Cameron–Storvick theorem associated with Gaussian paths on function space
Jae Gil Choi

Abstract
The purpose of this paper is to provide a more general Cameron–Storvick theorem for the generalized analytic Feynman integral associated with Gaussian process 𝒵 k \mathcal{Z}_{k} on a very general Wiener space C a , b ​ [ 0 , T ] C_{a,b}[0,T] . The general Wiener space C a , b ​ [ 0 , T ] C_{a,b}[0,T] can be considered as the set of all continuous sample paths of the generalized Brownian motion process determined by continuous functions a ⁡ ( t ) a(t) and b ⁡ ( t ) b(t) on [ 0 , T ] [0,T] . As an interesting application, we apply this theorem to evaluate the generalized analytic Feynman integral of certain monomials in terms of Paley–Wiener–Zygmund stochastic integrals.

In

Definition 3.1

{\mathcal{W}}(C_{a,b}[0,T])

(if it exists) is called the first variation of

in the direction

Remark 3.2

Setting

[0,T]

8

\displaystyle E_{x}\big[\delta F(\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)|\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(\theta\odot k_{1},\cdot))\big]

(4.2)

\displaystyle=E_{x}\big[(\theta,\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}F(\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big]-(\theta\odot k_{2},a)_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}E_{x}\big[F(\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big].

Proof 2

Let
G(x)=F(\rho x)
. Then

G(\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)+\alpha\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(w,\cdot))=F(\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)+\rho\alpha\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(w,\cdot))

and

\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}G(\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)+\alpha\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(w,\cdot))\bigg|_{\alpha=0}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}F(\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)+\rho\alpha\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(w,\cdot))\bigg|_{\alpha=0}.

Thus
\delta G(\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)|\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(\theta\odot k_{1},\cdot))=\delta F(\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)|\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(\theta\odot k_{1},\cdot))
.
Hence by equation (
3.4
)
with
F
replaced with
G
, we have

\displaystyle E_{x}\big[\delta F(\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)|\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(\theta\odot k_{1},\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle=E_{x}\big[\delta G(\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)|\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(\theta\odot k_{1},\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle=E_{x}\big[(\theta,\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}G(\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big]-(\theta\odot k_{2},a)_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}E_{x}\big[G(\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle=E_{x}\big[(\theta,\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}F(\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big]-(\theta\odot k_{2},a)_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}E_{x}\big[F(\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big]

which establishes (
4.2
).
∎

Next we provide the Cameron–Storvick theorem for the generalized analytic

\mathcal{Z}_{k}
-Feynman integral on the function space
C_{a,b}[0,T]
.

Theorem 4.3

Let
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
,
\theta
, and
F
be as in Lemma
4.2
.
Then if any two of the three generalized analytic Feynman integrals in the
following equation exist, then the third one also exists, and equality holds:

\displaystyle E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\big[\delta F(\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)|\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(\theta\odot k_{1},\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle=-iqE_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\big[(\theta,\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}F(\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle-(-iq)^{1/2}(\theta\odot k_{2},a)_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\big[F(\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big].

Proof 3

Given
\rho>0
and
\theta\in C_{a,b}^{\prime}[0,T]
, let
\theta_{\rho}=\frac{1}{\rho}\theta
.
Then
\theta_{\rho}
is a function in
C_{a,b}^{\prime}[0,T]
, and

\theta\odot k_{1}=\rho\theta_{\rho}\odot k_{1}
.
By equation (
4.2
) with
\theta
replaced with
\theta_{\rho}
,

\displaystyle E_{x}\big[\delta F(\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)|\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(\theta\odot k_{1},\cdot))\big]

(4.3)

\displaystyle=E_{x}\big[\delta F(\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)|\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(\theta_{\rho}\odot k_{1},\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle=E_{x}\big[(\theta_{\rho},\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}F(\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big]-(\theta_{\rho}\odot k_{2},a)_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}E_{x}\big[F(\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle=\rho^{-2}E_{x}\big[(\theta,\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}F(\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle-\rho^{-1}(\theta\odot k_{2},a)_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}E_{x}\big[F(\rho\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big].

Now let
\rho=\lambda^{-1/2}
. Then equation (
4.3
) becomes

\displaystyle E_{x}\big[\delta F(\lambda^{-1/2}\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)|\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(w,\cdot))\big]

(4.4)

\displaystyle=\lambda E_{x}\big[(\theta,\lambda^{-1/2}\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}F(\lambda^{-1/2}\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle-\lambda^{1/2}(\theta\odot k_{2},a)_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}E_{x}\big[F(\lambda^{-1/2}\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big].

Since
\rho>0
was arbitrary, we have that equation (
4.4
) holds
for all
\lambda>0
. We now use Definition
4.1
to obtain our desired
conclusions.
∎

5
Generalized analytic Feynman integral of
monomials in terms of PWZ stochastic integrals

When we evaluate the following generalized analytic Feynman integral

E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\bigg[\prod_{j=1}^{m}(\theta\odot k_{j},x)^{\sim}\bigg]

(5.1)

we might not be able to use the change of variables theorem of the usual measure theory,
because the set of Gaussian random variables
(\theta\odot k_{j},x)^{\sim}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
,
are generally not independent.
In this case, to apply the change of variables theorem for the calculation
of (
5.1
), we might apply the Gram–Schmidt process for the set of
functions
\{\theta\odot k_{1},\ldots,\theta\odot k_{m}\}
.

Using equation (
4.5
), we indeed see that
the generalized Feynman integral of functionals having the
form (
5.1
) can be calculated very explicitly. In this section
we present interesting examples to which equation (
4.5
)
can be applied.

Example 5.1

Let
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
be functions in
\mathrm{Supp}_{C_{a,b}^{*}}[0,T]
,
and given a function
\theta
in
C_{a,b}^{\prime}[0,T]
, set
F(x)=(\theta,x)^{\sim}

for
x\in C_{a,b}[0,T]
. Then using equations (
3.1
)
and (
2.2
), it follows that for any
w
in
C_{a,b}^{\prime}[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\delta F(\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)|\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(w,\cdot))

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\big\{(\theta,\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}+\alpha(\theta,\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(w,\cdot))^{\sim}\big\}\bigg|_{\alpha=0}

\displaystyle=(\theta,\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(w,\cdot))^{\sim}

\displaystyle=(\theta\odot k_{2},w)^{\sim}

\displaystyle=(\theta\odot k_{2},w)_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}.

From this, we see that

\delta F(\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)|\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(\theta\odot k_{1},\cdot))=(\theta\odot k_{2},\theta\odot k_{1})_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}.

(5.2)

Also using (
4.1
), it follows that

\displaystyle E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\big[F(\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle=E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\big[(\theta,\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}\big]

(5.3)

\displaystyle=E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\big[(\theta\odot k_{1},x)^{\sim}\big]=(-iq)^{-1/2}(\theta\odot k_{1},a)_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}.

Next using equations (
4.5
), (
5.2
), and (
5.3
),
we obtain the formula

\displaystyle E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\big[(\theta\odot k_{2},x)^{\sim}(\theta\odot k_{1},x))^{\sim}\big]

(5.4)

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{C_{a,b}[0,T]}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}(\theta,\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}(\theta,\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\big[(\theta,\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}F(\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{q}E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\big[\delta F(\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot)|\mathcal{Z}_{k_{2}}(\theta\odot k_{1},\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle+(-iq)^{-1/2}(\theta\odot k_{2},a)_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\big[F(\mathcal{Z}_{k_{1}}(x,\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{q}(\theta\odot k_{2},\theta\odot k_{1})_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}+\frac{i}{q}(\theta\odot k_{2},a)_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}(\theta\odot k_{1},a)_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}.

In our next example, for any positive integer
m\in\{3,4,\ldots\}
, we obtain a recurrence
relation for the generalized analytic Feynman integral

E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\bigg[\prod_{j=1}^{m}(\theta\odot k_{j},x)^{\sim}\bigg].

Example 5.2

For a positive integer
m\geq 3
,
let
\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{m-1},k_{m}\}
be a finite set
of functions in
\mathrm{Supp}_{C_{a,b}^{*}}[0,T]
,
and given a function
\theta\in C_{a,b}^{\prime}[0,T]
, set

\displaystyle F(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(\theta\odot k_{j},x)^{\sim}=\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(\theta\odot k_{j},\mathcal{Z}_{b}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}.

First, using equation (
3.1
), it follows that for all
w\in C_{a,b}^{\prime}[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\delta F(

\displaystyle x|\mathcal{Z}_{k_{m}}(w,\cdot))=\delta F(\mathcal{Z}_{b}(x,\cdot)|\mathcal{Z}_{k_{m}}(w,\cdot))

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}\big\{(\theta\odot k_{j},\mathcal{Z}_{b}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}+\alpha(\theta\odot k_{j},\mathcal{Z}_{k_{m}}(w,\cdot))^{\sim}\big\}\bigg|_{\alpha=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}\bigg(\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{1}j=1\\
j\neq l\end{subarray}}^{m-1}(\theta\odot k_{j},\mathcal{Z}_{b}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}\bigg)(\theta\odot k_{l},\mathcal{Z}_{k_{m}}(w,\cdot))^{\sim}.

Then, in particular, it follows that

\displaystyle\delta F(

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{b}(x,\cdot)|\mathcal{Z}_{k_{m}}(\theta\odot b,\cdot))=\delta F(\mathcal{Z}_{b}(x,\cdot)|\mathcal{Z}_{k_{m}}(\theta,\cdot))

(5.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}\bigg(\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{1}j=1\\
j\neq l\end{subarray}}^{m-1}(\theta\odot k_{j},\mathcal{Z}_{b}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}\bigg)(\theta\odot k_{l},\mathcal{Z}_{k_{m}}(\theta,\cdot))^{\sim}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}\bigg(\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{1}j=1\\
j\neq l\end{subarray}}^{m-1}(\theta\odot k_{j},\mathcal{Z}_{b}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}\bigg)(\theta\odot k_{l},\theta\odot k_{m})_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}.

Next, using (
4.5
) with
k_{1}
and
k_{2}

replaced with

and

, respectively, and with

,
(

), and the equation

it follows that

\displaystyle E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\bigg[\prod_{j=1}^{m}(\theta\odot k_{j},x)^{\sim}\bigg]

(5.6)

\displaystyle=E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\bigg[(\theta,\mathcal{Z}_{k_{m}}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(\theta\odot k_{j},\mathcal{Z}_{b}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}\bigg]

\displaystyle=E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\big[(\theta,\mathcal{Z}_{k_{m}}(x,\cdot))^{\sim}F(\mathcal{Z}_{b}(x,\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{q}E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\big[\delta F(\mathcal{Z}_{b}(x,\cdot)|\mathcal{Z}_{k_{m}}(\theta\odot b,\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle+(-iq)^{-1/2}(\theta\odot k_{m},a)_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\big[F(\mathcal{Z}_{b}(x,\cdot))\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{q}\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}(\theta\odot k_{l},\theta\odot k_{m})_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\bigg[\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{1}j=1\\
j\neq l\end{subarray}}^{m-1}(\theta\odot k_{j},x)^{\sim}\bigg]

\displaystyle+(-iq)^{-1/2}(\theta\odot k_{m},a)_{C_{a,b}^{\prime}}E_{x}^{\mathrm{anf}_{q}}\bigg[\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(\theta\odot k_{j},x)^{\sim}\bigg].

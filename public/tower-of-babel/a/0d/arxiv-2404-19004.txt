Topological holography for fermions
Rui Wen, Weicheng Ye, Andrew C. Potter

Abstract
Topological holography is a conjectured correspondence between the symmetry charges and defects of a D D -dimensional system with the anyons in a ( D + 1 ) (D+1) -dimensional topological order: the symmetry topological field theory (SymTFT). Topological holography is conjectured to capture the topological aspects of symmetry in gapped and gapless systems, with different phases corresponding to different gapped boundaries (anyon condensations) of the SymTFT. This correspondence was previously considered primarily for bosonic systems, excluding many phases of condensed matter systems involving fermionic electrons. In this work, we extend the SymTFT framework to establish a topological holography correspondence for fermionic systems. We demonstrate that this fermionic SymTFT framework captures the known properties of 1 + 1 ​ D 1+1D fermion gapped phases and critical points, including the classification, edge-modes, and stacking rules of fermionic symmetry-protected topological phases (SPTs), and computation of partition functions of fermionic conformal field theories (CFTs). Beyond merely reproducing known properties, we show that the SymTFT approach can additionally serve as a practical tool for discovering new physics, and use this framework to construct a new example of a fermionic intrinsically gapless SPT phase characterized by an emergent fermionic anomaly.

Physical boundaries and anyon condensation

In the SymTFT framework different

Namely, for every boundary condition

of a

\mathcal{C}

Or equivalently if it can be dragged out of the boundary without costing any energy. Anyons that are condensed simultaneously need to satisfy a set of consistency conditions. First, all condensed anyons must be bosons. Moreover,
if
a
and
b

a\times b
is necessarily also condensed, These conditions make the condensate have the structure of a separable, connected, commutative algebra. See reference
[
22

For abelian topological orders, a condensable algebra is equivalent to a subgroup of the fusion group, such that all elements of this subgroup have trivial self and mutual statistics.

Anyon condensation in a bulk
2+1D
topological order occurs as a phase transition that results in a new phase. For Abelian topological orders, the new topological order can be viewed as formed by the remaining deconfined anyons, i.e. anyons that braid trivially with the condensate.
In a non-Abelian theory, an anyon of the initial phase may become partially confined/deconfined, and it is less straightforward to identify the new topological order resulting from condensation. In general a composite anyon of the original phase becomes a simple anyon of the new phase. The composite anyons needs to be invariant under fusing with the condensate, since the condensate is the vacuum of the new phase. This gives the anyons in the new phase the structure of a module over the condensable algebra
\mathcal{A}
. That is to say, an excitation of the new phase (possibly confined) is a non-simple anyon of the old phase
\mathcal{M}=\oplus_{i}c_{i}a_{i}
, and there is a product
\nu_{M}:\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
. The product needs to satisfy some natural consistency conditions
[
22
]
. Deconfined excitations are the modules such that braiding with the condensate commute the product, these are formally called local modules.

A useful formula regarding anyon condensation is the dimension formula. If we denote the topological order obtained by condensing
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\dim(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{A})=\dim(\mathcal{C})/\dim(\mathcal{A})

where
\dim(\mathcal{C})=\sqrt{\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}}d_{a}^{2}}
is the total quantum dimension of the topological order, and if
\mathcal{A}=\sum c_{a}a

\dim(\mathcal{A})=\sum_{a}c_{a}d_{a}

If a condensation results in a trivial topological order, the condensable algebra is called Lagrangian. We see from the dimension formula that a condensable algebra is Lagrangian if and only if its dimension equals the total quantum dimension of the topological order:
\dim(\mathcal{A})=\dim(\mathcal{C})

The relation between anyon condensation and boundary condition of a topological order is as follows. A gapped boundary has a Lagrangian condensation while a gapless boundary necessarily has a non-Lagrangian condensation. In the SymTFT setup the reference boundary is always gapped, and different
\mathcal{A}
-phases are realized by choosing different boundary conditions on the physical boundary. Therefore gapped

Physically they are the anyons confined on the boundary. When the boundary is gapless, the condensation on the boundary determines only the gapped excitations, and the gapless excitations on the boundary can not be fully determined by the condensation alone. However it is known that if the gapless boundary is described by some CFT, the SymTFTconstrains the partition function of the CFT via its modular transformation properties

III
SymTFT for fermions: Structure and Examples

We now seek a generalization of the SymTFT framework for fermionic systems.
The key features of the SymTFT that we wish to preserve are that:

1.

It reduces to the original system by dimensional reduction/sandwich construction.

2.

Non-trivial defect lines on the reference boundary have the same structure as symmetry defects of the original system.

3.

Anyons condensed on the reference boundary have the same structure as local charges of the original system.

The sandwich construction of the fermionic SymTFT proceeds similarly to that for bosons with two key differences: first, the gauge group of the SymTFT contains the fermion parity subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{F}_{2}
to account for the conservation of fermion number parity,
(-1)^{F}
, and second, to describe a system with fermion excitations we must explicitly introduce local (ungauged) fermion excitations into the otherwise bosonic SymTFT sandwich.
8
8

8

In our construction, these local fermion excitations will be located on the reference boundary of a bosonic SymTFT. We mention that there are other proposals that introduce local fermions into the bulk of the SymTFT construction
[
34
,
13
,
36
]
. See Section
VIII
for more discussion.

In this section, we illustrate the basic ideas of this approach through a series of examples of how various familiar gapped topological and SPT phases, and gapless critical points arise in the fermion SymTFT framework. In Section
IV
below, we also construct a new fermionic intrinsically gapless SPT phase , characterized by a low-energy emergent symmetry with a fermionic anomaly.
In Section
V
, we formalize this construction, and address various technical details related to spin structures.

III.2.1
Spin structure and partition function of gapped phases

In the continuum a fermionic system is defined on a manifold with a spin structure (or a Pin-structure for un-orientable manifolds), which can loosely be thought of as a “background gauge field of fermion parity” in the sense that the spin structure defines whether a fermion obtains a minus sign when dragged around a non-contractible cycle of the manifold. A spin structure on the torus can specified by periodic or anti-periodic boundary condition along two non-contractible loops (generators of
H_{1}[T^{2}]
). Therefore we will denote a spin structure on the torus by
(P/AP,P/AP)
.

Review: Partition function of the Kitaev chain.

The partition function of a fermionic system depends on the spin structure,
\eta
. For example, the partition function of the Kitaev chain is given by
\text{Arf}(\eta)
in the IR limit, where
\text{Arf}(\eta)=\pm 1
is the Arf invariant.
The values of the Arf invariant on the torus are

\displaystyle\text{Arf}(\eta)=\begin{cases}1&\eta=(P,AP),(AP,P),(AP,AP)\\
-1&\eta=(P,P)\end{cases}

(10)

Physically this comes from that fact that the ground state of the topological phase of the Kitaev chain has opposite fermion parities for periodic and anti-periodic boundary conditions, whereas the trivial phase is adiabatically connected to a product state that is completely insensitive to boundary conditions.
The partition function of the topological phase of the Kitaev chain can then be computed as follows. We denote the ground-states with
P/AP
boundary conditions as
|GS_{P/AP}\rangle
respectively, and define the normalized partition function on a torus (
T^{2}
) with
X^{1},X^{0}=P/AP
boundary conditions in the space and time cycles as
\mathcal{Z}[T^{2},(X^{1},X^{0})]
. Then the ordinary zero-temperature thermal partition function has
AP
boundary conditions in time, from which we identify:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[T^{2},(P,AP)]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\,|GS_{P}\rangle\langle GS_{P}|=1

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[T^{2},(AP,AP)]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\,|GS_{AP}\rangle\langle GS_{AP}|=1

(11)

Partition functions with periodic boundary condition in time can be obtained from these by inserting a
(-1)^{P_{f}}
into the trace which acts like a spacelike defect of fermion parity in a path integral evaluation of the trace:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[T^{2},(X,P)]=\langle GS_{X}|(-1)^{P_{F}}|GS_{X}\rangle.

(12)

The key defining property of the topological superconductor is that the ground-state fermion parity of
P
and
AP
boundary conditions are opposite. The fermion parity of either alone is non-universal, and can be toggled by adding an additional occupied fermion orbital, only the relative fermion parity for
P
vs

III.4
Example of a critical point: The Majorana CFT

Having confirmed that the proposed fermionic SymTFT formulation reproduces various known gapped phases, we now examine phase transitions between these gapped phases, focusing on the critical point separating the Kitaev and trivial gapped phases with no additional symmetries (
G^{F}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
).
It is well known that one possible phase transition between these phases is a massless Majorana CFT with with fixed-point Lagrangian:

\displaystyle L=i\int dtdx\left[\psi_{R}(\partial_{t}-\partial_{x})\psi_{R}+\psi_{L}(\partial_{t}+\partial_{x})\psi_{L}\right]

(15)

where
\psi_{L,R}
are left- and right- moving Majorana (real fermion) fields.
This Majorana CFT is related to the Ising CFT by bosonization (gauging fermion parity by summing over the different boundary conditions for the fermion operators).

In the following, we show that the (ungapped) “nothing condensed” physical boundary where neither
e
nor
m
are condensed is compatible with this CFT, and follow the method developed in
[
29
]
to compute the partition function of the Majorana CFT from that for the bosonic Ising CFT. We note in passing, that, the “nothing condensed” boundary does not fully determine the CFT in the gapless phase, but only topological aspects such as how symmetries are implemented on scaling operators. For example, there are other CFTs (e.g. the tricritical Ising CFT, or non-minimal models) that are also compatible with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
SymTFT, but describe more exotic multi-critical points. We do not consider these more exotic options here.

III.4.1
Review of (bosonic) Ising CFT

To set the stage, we briefly review some important facts about the Ising CFT and its SymTFT description
[
29
]
.
The Ising CFT belongs to the family of rational CFTs (RCFT). An RCFT has finite number of primary operators and the Hilbert space on a circle decomposes into a finite direct sum

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{a,b}M_{ab}\mathcal{H}_{a}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{b}

(16)

where
\mathcal{H}_{a}
(
\overline{H}_{b}
) is an irreducible representation of the left (right) chiral algebra and
M_{ab}
are positive integers. The representations of a chiral algebra form a modular tensor category
\mathcal{C}
, therefore the labels
a,b
can be thought of as taking values in anyons of a 2+1D chiral topological order
\mathcal{C}
and its time reversal
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
. This relation between 1+1D RCFT and 2+1D chiral topological orders can also be understood from a sandwich construction. Consider the non-chiral topological order
\mathcal{Z}[\mathcal{C}]=\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\overline{\mathcal{C}}
and a sandwich construction with physical boundary being the state

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle=\sum_{a,b\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{a}\overline{\chi}_{b}|a,b\rangle.

(17)

Here
\chi_{a},\overline{\chi}_{b}
are the characters of the chiral,anti-chiral algebra representations respectively.
The reference boundary can be expanded in the anyon basis as:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}\rangle=\sum_{a,b\in\mathcal{C}}M_{a,b}|a,b\rangle.

(18)

Then the sandwich has partition function

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}|\Psi\rangle=\sum_{a,b}M_{a,b}\chi_{a}\overline{\chi}_{b},

(19)

which is the partition function of the RCFT. This sandwich construction can be viewed as the SymTFT for the non-invertible categorical symmetry
\mathcal{C}
.

As an example, the Ising CFT is a RCFT with three primaries
1,\sigma,\psi
which form the Ising category. By the above construction it can live on the boundary of the double Ising theory and corresponds to the state

\displaystyle|\Psi_{\sf{Ising}}\rangle=|\chi_{1}|^{2}|1,1\rangle+|\chi_{\sigma}|^{2}|\sigma,\overline{\sigma}\rangle+|\chi_{\psi}|^{2}|\psi,\overline{\psi}\rangle.

(20)

IV
Intrinsically fermionic and gapless SPTs from SymTFT

As we reviewed in Section
II
, SymTFT provides a full characterization for bosonic gapless SPTs, including their classification, edge modes, group extension structures, and emergent anomalies.

Given such success, it is natural to ask whether there is also a correspondence between SymTFT and fermionic gSPT, whether the SymTFT could assist us in constructing new models of fermionic gSPTs and understand topological aspects of them, etc. We provide evidence by studying a SymTFT sandwich construction, which turns out to describe a fermionic gapless SPT with symmetry
\mathbb{Z}_{8}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
and an emergent
k=2

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
fermionic anomaly. We show that this igSPT has the intriguing property of having a half-charged Majorana edge mode.

Previously, models of fermionic igSPTs
[
44
]
have only realized bosonic anomalies: i.e. are essentially bosonic igSPTs realized from the bosonic (spin- or Cooper pair) degrees of freedom in a Mott insulator of fermions.
While this may give a physical mechanism for their experimental realization, the resulting low energy properties (edge states and gapless modes) are indistinguishable from those of a purely bosonic system.
Therefore it is an interesting question whether there exists an “intrinsically-fermionic” igSPTs whose topological features can not arise in a purely bosonic system, but involve gapless fermion degrees of freedom in the IR. Below we construct precisely such an intrinsically fermionic igSPT via the SymTFT methods developed above.
While lattice models and field theories of this state could in principle be constructed without reference to SymTFT, we found that the SymTFT actually serves as a useful algebraic, and graphical tool to quickly prototype these phases. In this way, the SymTFT can also be a practical technique for constructing new physics, as well as re-interpreting known phases.

We consider the gapless sector of the igSPT to have the fermionic symmetry
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
and an emergent anomaly. Anomalies of this symmetry in
1+1D
have an
\mathbb{Z}_{8}

For the case of interests to us here, anomalies of

The symmetry-preserving gapless edge of these 2+1D SPTs are described by the field theory:

\displaystyle+\psi_{L,a}(\partial_{t}+\partial_{x})\psi_{L,a}]

(26)

IV.1
Anomalous fermionic symmetries in SymTFT

The SymTFT of a system with an anomalous bosonic symmetry is generally given by the twisted gauge theory obtained from gauging the corresponding higher-dimensional SPT whose boundary realizes the anomalous symmetry. This can be understood from the fact that any
1+1D
anomalous system can be realized on the boundary of a
2+1D
SPT, and the SymTFT is simply obtained by gauging this
2+1D
SPT. Following this principle we may formulate SymTFT for an anomalous fermionic symmetry(with no gravitational anomaly) as follows. An anomaly of
G^{F}
in
1+1D
corresponds to an SPT of
G^{F}
in
2+1D
, i.e. a (non-chiral)topological superconductor with symmetry
G^{F}
. Then the SymTFT is given by gauging this topological superconductor, which is a non-chiral topological order that we will call a twisted
G^{F}
-gauge theory, although the twist here no longer has a cohomology classification. Independent of the twist, the gauge charges of the twisted
G^{F}
-gauge theory have the same structure as those of an untwisted one, the super-Tannakian category
\text{Rep}(G,P_{f})
. Namely the gauge charges are labelled by representations of the group
G^{F}
, and a gauge charge in the representation
R
is bosonic/fermionic if fermion parity is represented as
+1/-1
. Therefore we can still define the reference boundary of the SymTFT sandwich as the one where all gauge charges are condensed(with the understanding that fermionic charges are condensed together with a local fermion introduced on the reference boundary). This completes our construction for anomalous fermionic symmetry.

Recall that the
2+1D
SPT corresponding to the level
\nu
anomaly of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
is
\nu
stacks of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-neutral
p+ip
superconductor and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-charged
p-ip
superconductor pairs. The theory obtained by gauging
G^{F}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
in the 2+1D bulk of this SPT can be deduced from the stacking structure; this topological order will become the SymTFT for the anomalous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}

Denote the symmetry (
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
) flux by
\Phi
, and fermion-parity (
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
) flux by
\Phi_{F}
. Whereas
\Phi_{F}
effects both the
p\pm ip
layers (all fermions are charged under
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
), the symmetry flux,
\Phi

p-ip

For a single
p\pm ip
superconductor, the flux (superconducting vortex), carries an unpaired Majorana zero mode with topological spin
e^{\pm i\pi/4}
.
Thus, for
\nu=1
, the
\Phi
flux will be such a non-Abelian particle, and the corresponding anomalous edge will also have a non-Abelian character that prevents it from having a tensor-product Hilbert space structure.
We anticipate that this obstacle will prevent the realization of the
\nu=1
edge anomaly as an igSPT – since this anomaly has a “gravitational” character that cannot be alleviated merely by extending the symmetry group.
Hence, we instead focus on even
\nu
.

The
\nu=4
(
n=2
) anomaly is a self-anomaly of the bosonic,
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, part of the symmetry group and does not involve
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
. Equivalently, the 2+1D
\nu=4
SPT is topologically-equivalent to a bosonic SPT, and the fermions are merely spectators.
Therefore, the only interesting candidate for an intrinsically-fermionic igSPT is
\nu=2
(
\nu=6\simeq-2
will be similar), on which we now focus.

We refer to the 2+1D topological order obtained by gauging the symmetry of this
\nu=2
SPT:
D^{F}[\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F},\nu=2]
.
The simple anyons are generated by fluxes
\phi,\phi_{F}
, and their corresponding charges, denoted by
b
and
f
. The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry flux is a
\pi
-flux in two
p+ip
layers. Since a stack of two
p+ip
superconductors is equivalent to an integer quantum Hall state with unit Hall conductance, this
\pi
flux therefore binds
1/2
of a symmetry-charge and acquiring quantum spin
e^{i\pi/4}
.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
-flux is a
\pi
flux in all
p+ip
and
p-ip
layers. It therefore induces equal and opposite contributions to spins from the
+
and
-
layers, and is a boson with fractional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
charge (from the
+
layers).
The
\Phi^{2}
is a
2\pi
-flux in two
p+ip
layers, therefore it binds a charged electron and is identified with
f\times b
.
\phi_{F}^{2}
is a boson, but it binds a charged electron and 7 uncharged electrons, therefore it is charged under
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and is identified with
b

\nu=2
SPT is completely generated by
\Phi
and
\Phi_{F}

\Phi_{F}
and
\Phi

\Phi

\theta_{\phi,\phi_{F}}=e^{i\pi/2}

\nu=2~\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
SPT, which is the SymTFT for the
\nu=2
anomalous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}

IV.2
Fermionic igSPT with
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
symmetry

The topological properties of igSPTs stem from an emergent anomalous symmetry,
G^{F}
. Instead of arising at the boundary of a higher-dimensional gapped
G^{F}
SPT, the anomaly is “cured” by gapped degrees of freedom that transform under a larger group
\Gamma^{F}
, which is an extension of
G^{F}
. In the SymTFT description, this requires that an untwisted
\Gamma^{F}
gauge theory can be reduced to a twisted
G^{F}
gauge theory by partially-confining (non-Lagrangian) anyon condensation,
\mathcal{A}
.

For the intrinsically-fermionic
\nu=2

G^{F}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
anomaly above, one solution is to extend the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry by
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
to
\Gamma^{F}=\mathbb{Z}_{8}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
. In other words, a fermionic system with
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
symmetry can exhibit an intrinisically-fermionic igSPT with the
\nu=2

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
anomaly.

Lifting the anomaly

Denote the anyons of
D(\mathbb{Z}_{8}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F})
as
a_{1,2}
with
a\in{1,e,m}
and composites thereof, and where
1,2
subscripts refer to the
\mathbb{Z}_{8},\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
gauge subgroups respectively. As above, denote the canonical fermion charge as
f_{2}=e_{2}m_{2}
. Then, a suitable partially-confining condensation that yields the desired twisted
D(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F},\nu=2)
topological order is obtained by condensing the anyon
a=e_{1}^{6}m_{1}^{2}e_{2}m_{2}
. The remaining deconfined anyons are generated by
\phi=e_{1}m_{1}
, and
\phi_{F}=e_{1}^{2}m_{2}
, and are topologically identical to those of the anomalous SymTFT
D(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F},\nu=2)

Sandwich construction

A sandwich construction of the igSPT is obtained by choosing a
\Gamma^{F}=\mathbb{Z}_{8}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}

gauge theory for the bulk SymTFT, the canonical reference boundary
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f}=\langle e_{1},e_{2}m_{2}c\rangle
, and the partially-confining (non-Lagrangian) physical boundary,
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{phys}}=\langle e_{1}^{6}m_{1}^{2}e_{2}m_{2}\rangle
.
The physical boundary is necessarily gapless (or symmetry broken). A candidate field theory that matches the anomaly is given by two copies of the Majorana CFT (Eq.
26
with
\nu=2
), or equivalently a massless Dirac fermion with a chiral symmetry anomaly.

The
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
symmetry is generated by inserting a horizontal
m_{1}
line “condiment” in the sandwich. For the igSPT sandwich, a pair of symmetry generators,
m_{1}^{2}
line, can be freely absorbed into the boundaries, by splitting
m_{1}^{2}=a\times(e_{1}^{6}f_{2})
and absorbing the
a
into the physical boundary and the
e_{1}^{6}f_{2}
into the reference boundary. Thus, the
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
symmetry is effectively reduced to an IR
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry. Denoting elements of
\Gamma^{F}=\mathbb{Z}_{8}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
as
(n,m)
, the group extension structure is given by the short exact sequence:

\displaystyle 1\to\mathbb{Z}_{4}\xrightarrow{i}\mathbb{Z}_{8}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\text{IR}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}\to 1.

(28)

Here the embedding map
i
is
\mathbb{Z}_{4}\ni 1\to(2,0)\in\mathbb{Z}_{8}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
.

Edge modes

A defining property of igSPTs is the presence of symmetry-protected topological edge states, that, despite the gapless bulk, are confined to the edge. Moreover, these edge states cannot be realized in any gapped topological phase with the same symmetry.
In the SymTFT sandwich construction, edge modes are revealed by consider a spatial interface between two different gapped physical boundaries : a trivial phase described by condensing symmetry fluxes:
\mathcal{A}^{0}=\langle m_{1},m_{2}\rangle
, and the igSPT boundary,
\mathcal{A}=\langle a=e_{1}^{6}m_{a}^{2}f_{2}\rangle
(Fig
12
).
The short string segment of
e_{1}^{6}f_{2}
localized near the trivial/igSPT interface represents a zero-energy edge state. Decorating the
\sf{B_{ref}}
end of this segment by a local (Majorana) fermion,
\gamma=\frac{1}{2}(c+ic^{\dagger})
, allows it to be absorbed into the fermionic condensate. At
\sf{B_{phys}}
, the segment can split into an
a
anyon and an
m_{1}^{2}
excitation that can respectively be absorbed into the igSPT (
\mathcal{A}
) and trivial
(\mathcal{A}^{0})
condensates. Since the ends of the segment can be absorbed, the resulting operator does not produce an energetic excitation. Moreover, the segment operator has a non-trivial symmetry action on the ground-space of the system. The symmetry generators are horizontal
m_{1}
or
m_{2}
lines. The zero mode operator anticommutes with the
m_{2}
line, as required since the mode is a fermion and is charged under the physical fermion parity. Moreover, the zero mode operator has exchange phase
(-i)=e^{-2\times 2\pi i/8}
with
m_{1}
, indicating that it carries (
-2
) units of the
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
symmetry charge (in terms of the IR
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, the fermionic zero mode appears to carry a “half” symmetry charge).

Ground-state degeneracy with open boundaries

For an igSPT of length
L
, with open boundary conditions (surrounded by trivial phases), the igSPT edge zero-modes span a four-fold ground-space degeneracy. Defining this ground-space degeneracy for a gapless system takes some care. In a system of length for the multi-component Majorana CFT the bulk gap scales
\sim 1/L
, whereas the topological ground-space degeneracy is exponentially small
\sim e^{-L/\xi}
where
\xi
is a microscopic correlation length related to the
a
-condensation energy scale.
To see this, note that the edge mode operator shown in Fig.
12
fails to commute up to an
e^{2i\pi/4}
phase with a horizontal
m_{1}
line (which measures the symmetry charge). This algebra cannot be represented on a unique ground-state, but rather requires a minimum of four degenerate ground-states.
Importantly, unlike for gapped SPTs, this ground-space degeneracy does not factorize into a pair of local Hilbert spaces for each edge. Rather, there is a single, non-local dimension-four ground-space shared by the entire chain.

IV.3
Field theory description

Despite its purely formal appearance, the SymTFT sandwich construction actually directly hints at a potential microscopic mechanism for realizing the igSPT phase.
The insights from SymTFT can also guide the construction of lattice-models and field-theory descriptions, as we now illustrate.

Consider a
1+1D
fermion chain with
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
symmetry. The
e_{1}^{6}m_{1}^{2}e_{2}m_{2}
condensation can then be interpreted via the SymTFT dictionary between anyons and generalized charges. The
m_{1}^{2}
anyon represents a strength-two domain wall of the
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
symmetry. Thus the
e_{1}^{6}m_{2}^{2}
part of the condensate corresponds to decorating strength two domain walls with
6=-2
symmetry charges of the
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
.
This decorated domain wall has fermionic statistics, which can be screened by binding them to a local fermion (recall that in the fermion SymTFT construction, the
e_{2}m_{2}
anyon is the “shadow” of the local fermion excitation on the physical boundary), and then condensing this composite object.

The SymTFT picture suggests a simple bosonized field theory description of the igSPT phase.
We start from a pair of Luttinger liquids, consisting of a
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
-charged boson field
e^{i\varphi_{1}}
, and its dual vortex
e^{i\varphi_{2}}
, and a left and right moving fermion field
\psi_{L,R}=\eta_{L,R}e^{i\phi_{L,R}}
where
\eta
are anticommuting Klein factors. The (Euclidean/imaginary-time) action density for this theory reads:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{4\pi}\sum_{I,J=1,2}\partial_{\tau}\varphi_{I}\sigma^{x}_{IJ}\partial_{x}\varphi_{J}+

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{4\pi}\sum_{I,J=L,R}\partial_{\tau}\phi_{I}\sigma^{z}_{I,J}\partial_{x}\phi_{J}+H_{v},

(29)

where
H_{v}
is the Hamiltonian containing (non-universal) terms that describe the velocity of excitations such as
v_{IJ}\partial_{x}\varphi_{I}\partial_{x}\varphi)_{J}
where
v_{IJ}
is a symmetric, positive matrix (and similarly for the
\Phi
fields), and
\sigma^{x,z}
are Pauli matrices in the
I,J
flavor space(s).
Quantizing the theory yields commutation relations:
e^{i\alpha\partial_{x}\varphi_{I}(x)/2\pi}\varphi_{J}(y)e^{-i\alpha\partial_{x}\varphi_{I}(x)/2\pi}=\varphi_{J}(y)+\alpha\sigma^{x}_{IJ}~\delta_{x,y}
, and (and similarly for
\phi_{I}
with
\sigma^{x}_{IJ}\leftrightarrow\sigma^{z}_{IJ}
), where
\alpha
is a constant.

The
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
symmetry generator,
g
, and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
generator,
(-1)^{F}
acts on the fields as:

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle:\varphi_{1}\rightarrow\varphi_{1}+2\pi/8,

\displaystyle(-1)^{F}

\displaystyle:\phi_{L/R}\rightarrow\phi_{L,R}+\pi

(30)

(other, unlisted fields transform trivially under the symmetry in question).
Crucially this symmetry action is non-anomalous, and
\mathcal{L}_{0}
may emerge as the continuum description of a lattice model with on-site symmetry action.

Domain wall operators

According to the commutation relations above, in the quantized theory, these global symmetry transformations restricted to the interval
I=[x_{1},x_{2}]
are implemented by operators
g|_{I}=e^{i\int_{a}^{b}dx\partial_{x}\varphi_{2}/8}
and
(-1)^{F}|_{I}=e^{i\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}(\phi_{R}-\phi_{L})/2}
. Correspondingly, the operators
m_{1}(x)=e^{i\varphi_{2}(x)/8}
and
m_{2}(x)=e^{i(\phi_{R}(x)-\phi_{L}(x))/2}
, create symmetry domain walls at position
x
.

If the symmetry were gauged, these domain wall operators would correspond to boundary-termination of a a gauge-flux (
m_{1,2}
) line operator.
From this identification, one can immediately recognize a local operator that plays the role of the decorated symmetry domain wall
a=m_{1}^{2}e_{1}^{-2}f_{2}
whose condensation in the SymTFT produces the igSPT boundary with anomalous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
symmetry action as:

a(x)=e^{i\left(2\varphi_{2}(x)/8-2\varphi_{1}(x)+\phi_{R}(x)\right)}.

This operator has trivial self-statistics,
[a(x),a(y)]=0
.
The operator is non-local due to the fractional coefficient in front of the
\varphi_{2}
field, and cannot be directly added to the Hamiltonian, however its fourth power can.
Specifically, the
a
-anyon condensation can be implemented by adding a Hamiltonian term:

\displaystyle H_{\rm igSPT}=-\Delta\int dx\cos\left[\varphi_{2}(x)-8\varphi_{1}(x)+4\phi_{R}(x)\right]

(31)

where
\Delta
is an energy scale (depending on the Luttinger liquid parameters, this term may or may not be perturbatively relevant, however, it can always be made relevant by cranking up the coefficient
\Delta
).

H_{\rm igSPT}
is clearly invariant under the terms in Eq.
30
, and when relevant, gaps out the linear combination of fields appearing in the cosine term, and also fields that are conjugate to this linear combination.

Emergent anomaly

A basis for the resulting low energy fields that commute with
H_{\rm igSPT}
is:

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}_{R}

\displaystyle=\phi_{R}-4\varphi_{1}

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}_{L}

\displaystyle=\phi_{L},

(32)

and an effective low-energy (IR) action-density for the null-space of
H_{\rm igSPT}
takes the form of a (bosonized) massless Dirac fermion:

\mathcal{L}_{\rm IR}=\frac{i}{4\pi}\sum_{IJ}\partial_{\tau}\tilde{\phi}_{I}\sigma^{z}_{IJ}\tilde{\phi}_{J}.

The operators
\eta_{L,R}e^{\pm\tilde{\phi}_{L,R}}
are fermionic fields, and their arguments transform under the symmetry as:

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle:\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\phi}_{R}\\
\tilde{\phi}_{L}\end{pmatrix}\rightarrow\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\phi}_{R}-\pi\\
\tilde{\phi}_{L}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle(-1)^{F}

\displaystyle:\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\phi}_{R}\\
\tilde{\phi}_{L}\end{pmatrix}\rightarrow\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\phi}_{R}+\pi\\
\tilde{\phi}_{L}+\pi\end{pmatrix}

(33)

which is indeed the anomalous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
symmetry action for the edge of the
\nu=2
fermion SPT
[
70
]
.

Edge modes

The igSPT edge modes can be identified by considering an interface with
H_{\rm igSPT}
added for
x>0
, and a trivial mass term for
x<0
:

\displaystyle H_{\rm interface}=-\Delta_{\rm igSPT}\int_{0}^{L}dx\cos\left(\varphi_{2}-8\varphi_{1}+4\phi_{R}\right)-\Delta_{\rm trivial}\int_{x\notin[0,L]}dx\left[\cos\varphi_{2}+\cos(\phi_{L}+\phi_{R})\right]

(34)

To simplify the analysis, we take the extreme limit
\Delta\rightarrow\infty
(or equivalently consider the RG fixed point limit where these terms are relevant).
In this limit, the field configurations are pinned locally to the minima of the cosine. For the igSPT region the ground-space is given by the four distinct minima of the corresponding cosine:

\displaystyle\phi_{R}=2\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}/4+\frac{n\pi}{2}

(35)

The operator
e^{2i\varphi_{1}(x)}
creates a kink between these minima whenever
0<x<L
, which costs a finite energy due to the gradient terms in
H_{v}
, except precisely at the edges:
x=0
or
x=L
.
In the trivial regions, there is a unique local ground-state given by
\phi_{L}=-\phi_{R}
and
\varphi_{2}=0
.
The operators
e^{\varphi_{1}(x)}
and
e^{i\phi_{L,R}(x)}
create local Kink excitations in these ground-state configurations, except when inserted exactly at the interface.

In the SymTFT, the zero-mode operator is implemented by an
e_{1}^{-2}f_{2}
line segment
that splits into an
a=m_{1}^{2}e_{2}^{-2}f_{2}
anyon that terminates on the igSPT side, and an
m_{1}^{-2}
anyon that terminates on the trivial side, and which is decorated by a local fermion on the physical boundary
(Fig.
12
), decorated by a local fermion on the physical boundary.
Translating these operators into the continuum theory gives a zero-mode operator:

\displaystyle\Psi^{\dagger}(0,L)=\eta_{R}\exp\left\{i\left[\phi_{R}(0,L)-2\varphi_{1}(0,L)\right]\right\}

(36)

\Psi^{\dagger}

commutes with the cosine terms in both
H_{\rm igSPT}
and
H_{\rm trivial}
, i.e. it is indeed a zero mode.
Crucially, this operator is only a zero mode when inserted at the igSPT/trivial interface (
x=0
or
L
). Otherwise, as described above, the term creates (massive) kinks in the local ground-state configuration of the fields. Moreover, by inspection,
\Psi^{\dagger}
has odd fermion parity and adds
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
two units of
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
symmetry charge (
g^{\dagger}\Psi^{\dagger}g=e^{i\pi/2}\Psi^{\dagger}
).
As a technical comment, the Klein factor
\eta_{R}
is required to ensure that
\Psi
anticommutes with all local fermion excitations such as
\eta_{L}e^{i\phi_{L}(x)}
(as physically required for any odd-fermion parity excitation).

V
SymTFT for fermions: Systematics and Technical Details

Having illustrated the fermionic SymTFT construction for a variety of specific examples, we now generalize this framework to general finite, unitary fermionic symmetries.

Conceptually, the construction proceeds much as the case without symmetry above, with some technical modifications. The bulk SymTFT is a
G^{F}
gauge theory, whose symmetry charges divide into bosonic and fermionic ones. At the fermionic reference boundary, in the sandwich constructions, one condenses all symmetry gauge charges by binding the fermionic ones to a local fermion
c
to convert them into condensable bosons. This reference boundary is again fermionic and requires choosing a
G^{F}
-spin structure.
The remainder of this section is devoted to making the qualitative description above mathematically explicit.

V.1
General symmetries

Having worked out the topological holography for fermions without a general symmetry, we now consider the SymTFT for a general fermionic symmetry
G^{F}
.
The fermion parity generates a central subgroup
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}\lhd G^{F}
, and the quotient
G^{B}=G^{F}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
is referred to as the “bosonic part” of the symmetry.
Consider a
1+1D
fermionic system
\sf{T}
with symmetry
G^{F}
living on
M
. To start, we assume the symmetry is not anomalous. Following the construction of SymTFT for bosonic symmetries, we may take the
2+1D
SymTFT for
G^{F}
to be the
G^{F}
-gauge theory. To complete the construction we once again consider the sandwich construction. We must choose an appropriate reference boundary condition so that the sandwich is equivalent to the original system
\sf{T}
.

V.1.1
Condensation on the reference boundary

Symmetry “charges” of
G^{F}
corresponded to representations of of
G^{F}
, which are labeled by a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
fermion parity
|R|=0/1
when the representation
R
has even/odd fermion parity respectively.
Fermi-statistics requires that braiding two symmetry charge excitations with representations
R,R^{\prime}
results in the exchange phase
(-1)^{|R|\cdot|R|^{\prime}}
, and similarly charges should have quantum spin (self-statistics)
(-1)^{|R|}
.
Formally, the local charges of
G^{F}
form the super-Tannakian category
\text{Rep}(G^{F},P_{f})

[
71
]
.

Recall anyons in
D(G^{F})
are labeled by pairs
(\sigma,r)
, where
\sigma
is a conjugacy class of
G^{F}
and
r\in\text{Rep}(\mathcal{C}_{G}(\sigma))
is a representation of the centralizer of
\sigma
. The simple anyons correspond to the cases where the conjugacy class is minimal (not a union of two smaller conjugacy classes) and the representation
r
is irreducible. When the conjugacy class is the trivial one
\sigma=\{1\}
, the anyons
(1,R\in\text{Rep}(G))
are called charges and we denote them by
e_{R}
. When the representation
r
is the trivial one, the anyons
(\sigma,1)
are called fluxes and we denote them by
m_{\sigma}
. Anyons that are neither charges nor fluxes are called dyons. For details of quantum double models, see Appendix
A
.

Since all the gauge charges in
D(G^{F})
are bosonic, the set of all gauge charges
e_{R},R\in\text{Rep}(G^{F})
does not have the correct structure of
\text{Rep}(G^{F},P_{f})
.
Then, to transmute the statistics of an odd fermion parity gauge-charge charge into a fermion we can bind a fermion parity flux to it to form a fermionic dyon. Thus the condensation having the structure of
\text{Rep}(G^{F},P_{f})
is given by the algebra

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f}=\left(\bigoplus_{|R|=0}d_{R}e_{R}\right)\bigoplus\left(\bigoplus_{|R|=1}d_{R}e_{R}m^{F}\right).

(37)

where
d_{a}
is the quantum dimension of anyon,
a
.
The
e_{R}m^{F}
anyons are generalization of the
f
-particle of toric code. Similar to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
-SymTFT case, the fermionic anyons
e_{R}m^{F}
must be bound to a local fermion
c
in order to obtain a condensable object with bosonic statistics.

Since the condensation involves fermions, the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f}
is not a usual commutative Frobenius algebra. Instead it has the structure of a super-commutative (i.e. with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
graded commutation relations) Frobenius super-algebra
[
37
,
38
]
. Specifically, we can define even and odd sectors
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f,0},\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f,1}
that contain the bosonic and fermionic charges respectively.
Let us rewrite
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f}
to reveal its super-structure. The even sector is isomorphic to the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[G^{F}]
tensored with the trivial
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
-representation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f,0}=\left(\bigoplus_{R,|R|=0}d_{R}e_{R}\right)=\mathbb{C}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\mathbb{C}_{+}.

(38)

Here
\mathbb{C}_{+}
is the trivial 1d representation of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
:
P_{f}\cdot z=z,\forall z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
.
\mathbb{C}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\mathbb{C}_{+}
is spanned by
|g\rangle\otimes 1
modulo the relation
|P_{f}g\rangle\otimes 1\cong|g\rangle\otimes P_{f}\cdot 1=|g\rangle\otimes 1
. If we decompose the first factor
\mathbb{C}[G]
into direct sum of irreps of
G^{F}
, then the sector
V^{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\mathbb{C}_{+}
is projected out if
R
is odd. Similarly the odd sector
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f,1}
is the tensor product between
\mathbb{C}[G]
and the non-trivial representation of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f,1}=\left(\bigoplus_{R,|R|=1}d_{R}e_{R}m^{F}\right)=\mathbb{C}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\mathbb{C}_{-}.

(39)

\mathbb{C}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\mathbb{C}_{-}
is spanned by
|g\rangle\otimes 1
modulo the relation
|P_{f}g\rangle\otimes 1\cong|g\rangle\otimes P_{f}\cdot 1=-|g\rangle\otimes 1
. The even representations in
\mathbb{C}[G]
are projected out by the
\mathbb{C}_{-}
factor. Put these two sectors together we arrive at a compact expression

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}(\mathbb{C}_{+}\oplus\mathbb{C}_{-})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\mathbb{C}^{1|1}.

(40)

We used
\mathbb{C}^{1|1}
to denote the super vector space with a 1d even sector and a 1d odd sector.
\mathbb{C}^{1|1}
carries a grading-preserving algebra structure, generated by a single Majorana fermion operator
\gamma
in the odd sector. The grading of

is then inherited from that of
\mathbb{C}^{1|1}

\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}
is defined by

\displaystyle(|g\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\gamma^{i})\cdot(|h\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\gamma^{j})=

\displaystyle~~~~\delta_{g,h}(-1)^{j\cdot gh^{-1}}|g\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\gamma^{i+j},

(41)

where
\delta_{g,h}
is 1 if

is in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
and 0 otherwise. It is not hard to verify that this algebra is connected and separable.
In order for
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f}
to define a consistent condensation the algebra also needs to be super-commutative, this means the following diagram commutes,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R(-1)^{|\cdot|\times|\cdot|}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}

Here, the map
\mu
taking the product Eq.(
41
),
and
R(-1)^{|\cdot|\times|\cdot|}
is the “super-braiding”, defined as

\displaystyle R(-1)^{|\cdot|\times|\cdot|}(a\otimes b)=(-1)^{|a|\cdot|b|}R(a\otimes b).

(42)

Indeed the diagram commutes,

\displaystyle\mu\circ R(-1)^{|\cdot|\times|\cdot|}((|g\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\gamma^{i})\otimes(|h\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\gamma^{j}))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{ij}\mu((P_{f}^{i}\cdot|h\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\gamma^{j})\otimes(|g\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\gamma^{i}))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{ij}(-1)^{ij}\mu((\cdot|h\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\gamma^{j})\otimes(|g\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\gamma^{i}))

\displaystyle=\delta_{g,h}(-1)^{hg^{-1}\cdot i}|g\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\gamma^{i+j}

\displaystyle=\delta_{g,h}(-1)^{hg^{-1}\cdot j}|h\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\gamma^{i+j}

\displaystyle=\mu((|g\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\gamma^{i})\otimes(|h\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}}\gamma^{j})).

(43)

Since

V.2
Spin and twisted spin structures of reference boundary

As we have demonstrated in Section
V.2
, fermionic reference boundary with the condensation
1+f
is not unique. In order to fully specify a reference boundary condition, a spin structure on
\sf{B_{ref}}
must be specified in addition to the condensation
1+f
. In Section
V.2
we showed through a diagrammatic arguement that this spin structure is tied to the expectation value of
f
-loops on the reference boundary. For instance, when
\sf{B_{ref}}\cong T^{2}
, the boundary state
|1\rangle+|f\rangle
, for which the
f
-loops in the time- or space-direction both have value
1
, corresponds to the spin structure (AP,AP). This dependence of the reference boundary on spin structure is crucial to the theory of SymTFT for fermionic symmetries, since a fermionic system is defined on a manifold with spin structure. More generally, the symmetry of a fermionic system can be viewed as a group
G^{F}
that contains fermion parity
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
as a subgroup.
G^{B}=G^{F}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
is referred to as the bosonic symmetry of the system. Such a
G^{F}
-symmetric system is defined on a manifold with a twisted version of the usual spin structure, called a
G^{F}
-spin structure. A
G^{F}
-spin structure is a pair
(\eta,a_{b})
, where
\eta
is a usual spin structure, and
a_{b}
is a
G^{B}
-background gauge field satisfying certain constraint. Therefore, in order to describe
G^{F}
-symmetric fermionic systems in the SymTFT framework, we must analyze how the fermionic reference boundary with the condensation Eq. (
37
) depends on
G^{F}
-spin structures.

In this section we discuss this dependence in detail. We take the approach that the reference boundary can be viewed as a boundary state, expanded in field configuration basis as

\displaystyle|\Psi_{\sf{ref}}\rangle=\sum_{A\in H^{1}[M,G^{F}]}\Psi_{\sf{ref}}(A)|A\rangle.

(44)

We will see that there are multiple allowed boundary states with the anyon condensation
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f}
, each labelled by a
G^{F}
-spin structure
(\eta,a_{b})
. We denote these reference states by
|\mathcal{F}(\eta,a_{b})\rangle
, with
\mathcal{F}
standing for “fermionic”. For a given physical boundary state
|\Psi_{phs}\rangle
, the partition function of the sandwich is given by the inner product

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\Psi}(\eta,a_{b}):=\langle\mathcal{F}(\eta,a_{b})|\Psi_{\sf{phys}}\rangle

(45)

which is the partition function of a
1+1D
system in the presence of a
G^{F}
-spin structure
(\eta,a_{b})
.

We will begin by reviewing definition and properties of spin structure. We then discuss the reference boundary state of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
-SymTFT for arbitrary boundary manifold and spin structure. Finally we generalize to
G^{F}
-SymTFT, and derive the boundary states
|\mathcal{F}(\eta,a_{b})\rangle
for arbitrary
G^{F}
-spin structure
(\eta,a_{b})
.

V.2.1
Review of spin structure

Recall that the spin structure on a torus amounts to choosing periodic(P) or anti-periodic(AP) boundary conditions along a basis for the noncontractible loops of the torus. This gives us all four spin structures on the torus.
10
10

10

Mathematically, AP/P boundary condition corresponds to bounding/non-bounding spin structure of the noncontractible loop, and is also known as NS(Neveu-Schwarz)/R(Ramond) sector in the context of string theory.
On a general two dimensional (spacetime) manifold
M
, the spin structure can be similarly defined as a choice of P or AP boundary condition for every loop on the manifold. We can then define an indicator
q(\gamma)
, where
\gamma
is a loop on
M
, and
q(\gamma)=1,-1
when the boundary condition on
\gamma
is AP,P respectively.
The value of this indicator function should be invariant under deformation of the loop, and can be extended to any
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-coefficient formal sum of loops by deforming the formal sum into a single loop. Therefore
q
is a function on the homology group
H_{1}[M,\mathbb{Z}_{2}]
. A fermion loop around the composite cycle
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}
can be deformed into separate loops encircling
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
, where the deformation involves a number of fermion exchanges equal to
\gamma_{1}\cdot\gamma_{2}
: Here
\gamma_{1}\cdot\gamma_{2}
is the number of intersection points of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
, which is homologically invariant
\mod 2
. This requires that
q
satisfy the consistency condition:

\displaystyle q([\gamma_{1}]+[\gamma_{2}])=q([\gamma_{1}])q([\gamma_{2}])(-1)^{\gamma_{1}\cdot\gamma_{2}}.

(46)

Functions satisfying this identity are called quadratic forms on
H_{1}[M,\mathbb{Z}_{2}]
.

Alternatively, the spin structure can be equivalently defined as a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued
1
-cochain,
\eta
that trivializes the 2nd Stiefel-Whitney class:
d\eta=w_{2}
.
There is a one-to-one correspondence between quadratic forms,
q_{\eta}
and the associated cochain,
\eta
.
Any two spin structures,
\eta,\eta^{\prime}
satisfy
d(\eta-\eta^{\prime})=0
, i.e. any two spin structures are related by the addition of a flat,
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued gauge field.
Formally, the set of spin structures is referred to as a torsor of
H^{1}[M,\mathbb{Z}_{2}]
.

The Arf-invariant, which appears in the partition function of the Kitaev chain, is defined by summing over the loops in the quadratic form:

\displaystyle\text{Arf}(\eta):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{H_{1}[M,\mathbb{Z}_{2}]}}\sum_{\gamma\in H_{1}[M,\mathbb{Z}_{2}]}q_{\eta}(\gamma),

(47)

which takes values in
\{1,-1\}
and only depends on the spin structure of the manifold.

V.2.2
Spin structure in the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
-SymTFT

We now generalize our discussion of spin structure dependence in section
III.1.2
for the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
-SymTFT to general boundary manifolds. We can simply apply the argument of moving an
f
-anyon in
Fig.
6
to any loop
\gamma
on the boundary
\sf{B_{ref}}
. Following the same procedure as in Fig.
6
, we can see that the boundary condition along
\gamma
is still related to the expectation value of an
f
-loop on
\gamma
via

\displaystyle P/AP\Leftrightarrow\langle W_{f}(\gamma)\rangle_{\sf{B_{ref}}}=-1/+1.

(48)

Comparing with the definition of the quadratic form
q_{\eta}
, we see that the expectation value of the
f
-loops on
\sf{B_{ref}}
is simply
q_{\eta}
:

\displaystyle\langle W_{f}(\gamma)\rangle_{\sf{B_{ref}}}=q_{\eta}(\gamma).

(49)

This relation tells us that the collection of values of
f
-loops on the reference boundary is in one-to-one correspondence with the spin structure of the reference boundary. Below we will utilize this relation to determine the boundary state with given a spin structure.
11
11

11

We note that this continuum argument can also be explicitly verified in microscopic toric-code-like lattice models with
cf
-condensed boundaries. We provide this lattice construction in Appendix
B
.

We now compute the state of the reference boundary with a given spin structure
\eta
. We write an
f
-condensed boundary state in the field configuration basis as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}\rangle=\sum_{a}\mathcal{F}(a)|a\rangle,

(50)

here
a\in H^{1}[\sf{B_{ref}},\mathbb{Z}_{2}]
is the value of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-gauge field on the boundary,
\mathcal{F}
stands for “fermionic” reference boundary state.
Our goal is now to determine the coefficients
\mathcal{F}(a)
such that the state
|\mathcal{F}\rangle
corresponds to an
f
-condensed boundary with spin structure
\eta
. An
f
-condensed boundary should be able to absorb an
f
-loop on it. We can write an
f
-loop on
\sf{B_{ref}}
as a product of an
e
-loop and an
m
-loop,

\displaystyle W_{f}[\gamma]:=W_{e}[\gamma]W_{m}[\gamma],\gamma\in H_{1}[\sf{B_{ref}},\mathbb{Z}_{2}],

(51)

where we choose an arbitrary framing for
\gamma
so that
W_{f}[\gamma]
is unambiguously defined. The
e
-loop
W_{e}[\gamma]
is the Wilson loop of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-gauge field
a
:

\displaystyle W_{e}[\gamma]=(-1)^{\oint_{\gamma}a}=(-1)^{\int_{\sf{B_{ref}}}a\cup\tilde{\gamma}}

(52)

where we used the Poincare dual of
\gamma
, denoted by
\tilde{\gamma}\in H^{1}[\sf{B_{ref}},\mathbb{Z}_{2}]
, to write the Wilson loop as an integration over the entire boundary
\sf{B_{ref}}
. On the other hand the
m
-loop is the Wilson loop of the dual gauge field
\hat{a}
,

\displaystyle W_{m}[\gamma]=(-1)^{\oint_{\gamma}\hat{a}}=(-1)^{\int_{\sf{B_{ref}}}\hat{a}\cup\tilde{\gamma}}

(53)

The
e/m
-loops act on the basis
|a_{0}\rangle
as follows,

\displaystyle W_{e}[\gamma]|a_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\int_{\sf{B_{ref}}}a_{0}\cup\tilde{\gamma}}|a_{0}\rangle,

(54)

\displaystyle W_{m}[\gamma]|a_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\int_{\sf{B_{ref}}}\hat{a}\cup\tilde{\gamma}}|a_{0}\rangle=|a_{0}+\tilde{\gamma}\rangle.

(55)

Therefore the
f
-loop acts on the boundary state as

\displaystyle W_{f}[\gamma]|\mathcal{F}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}(-1)^{\int_{M}a\cup\tilde{\gamma}}\mathcal{F}(a)|a+\tilde{\gamma}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}(-1)^{\int_{M}a\cup\tilde{\gamma}}\mathcal{F}(a+\tilde{\gamma})|a\rangle.

(56)

|\mathcal{F}\rangle

leads to the condition (we will write
\tilde{\gamma}

\gamma

\displaystyle\sum_{a}(-1)^{\int_{M}a\cup\gamma}\mathcal{F}(a+\gamma)|a\rangle=f(\gamma)\sum_{a}\mathcal{F}(a)|a\rangle,

(57)

for some eigenvalues
f(\gamma)
. By comparing coefficients on both sides we have

\displaystyle(-1)^{\int_{M}a\cup\gamma}\mathcal{F}(a+\gamma)=f(\gamma)\mathcal{F}(a),

(58)

for all
a,\gamma\in H^{1}[\sf{B_{ref}},\mathbb{Z}_{2}]
. Setting
a=0
we see
\mathcal{F}(\gamma)=f(\gamma)\mathcal{F}(0)

\mathcal{F}
satisfies the condition

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{F}(a+\gamma)}{\mathcal{F}(0)}=(-1)^{\int_{M}a\cup\gamma}\frac{\mathcal{F}(\gamma)}{\mathcal{F}(0)}\frac{\mathcal{F}(a)}{\mathcal{F}(0)},

(59)

which states that
\mathcal{F}(a)/\mathcal{F}(0)
is a quadratic form on
H^{1}[\sf{B_{ref}},\mathbb{Z}_{2}]
.
We may then write
\mathcal{F}(a)=\mathcal{F}(0)q_{\eta}(a)
for some spin structure
\eta
. Up to normalization an
f
-condensed boundary is then determined by a spin structure on
\sf{B_{ref}}
, and given by the state

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(\eta)\rangle\propto\sum_{a\in H^{1}[\sf{B_{ref}},\mathbb{Z}_{2}]}q_{\eta}(a)|a\rangle.

(60)

The state
|\mathcal{F}(\eta)\rangle
is an eigenstate of
f
-loops on
\sf{B_{ref}}
with eigenvalue
q_{\eta}(\gamma)
. Therefore according to the relation Eq.(
49
), the state
|\mathcal{F}(\eta)\rangle
is exactly the state corresponding to the spin structure
\eta
on
\sf{B_{ref}}
.

However, there is still a phase ambiguity for every state
|\mathcal{F}(\eta)\rangle
. These phases for different
\eta
s are not independent of each other. To see this, notice that in the SymTFT fermion parity is represented by an
m
-line on
\sf{B_{ref}}
, therefore insertion of an
m
-line should change the spin structure according to
W_{m}[\gamma]|\mathcal{F}(\eta)\rangle=|\mathcal{F}(\eta+\gamma)\rangle
.
Therefore once a state
|\mathcal{F}(\eta)\rangle
with a given spin structure
\eta
is chosen, the states with other spin structures are uniquely determined by the relation
W_{m}[\gamma]|\mathcal{F}(\eta)\rangle=|\mathcal{F}(\eta+\gamma)\rangle
. This will fix the phase ambiguity of the states
|\mathcal{F}(\eta)\rangle
up to an overall common phase, which is inconsequential. To see this, assume the state
|\mathcal{F}(\eta)\rangle
has an arbitrary phase factor
\mathcal{T}(\eta)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(\eta)\rangle=\mathcal{T}(\eta)\sum_{a}q_{\eta}(a)|a\rangle.

(61)

Then the action of an
m
-loop on the state is

\displaystyle W_{m}[\gamma]|\mathcal{F}(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(\eta)\sum_{a}q_{\eta}(a)|a+\gamma\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(\eta)\sum_{a}q_{\eta}(a+\gamma)|a\rangle.

(62)

The state
|\mathcal{F}(\eta+\gamma)\rangle
on the other hand is given by

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(\eta+\gamma)\rangle=\mathcal{T}(\eta+\gamma)\sum_{a}q_{\eta+\gamma}(a)|a\rangle.

(63)

By comparing coefficients we have the relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\eta)q_{\eta}(a+\gamma)=\mathcal{T}(\eta+\gamma)q_{\eta+\gamma}(a).

(64)

Summing over

on both sides, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\eta)\text{Arf}(\eta)=\mathcal{T}(\eta+\gamma)\text{Arf}(\eta+\gamma).

(65)

We see that the solution for

is
\mathcal{T}(\eta)=c\cdot\text{Arf}(\eta)
, where

is arbitrary. Take

, we have the phase-fixed states

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle=\text{Arf}(\eta)\sum_{a\in H^{1}[M,\mathbb{Z}_{2}]}q_{\eta}(a)|a\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in H^{1}[M,\mathbb{Z}_{2}]}\text{Arf}(\eta+a)|a\rangle,

(66)

where we used relation
\text{Arf}(\eta+a)=\text{Arf}(\eta)q_{\eta}(a)
.

Eq.(

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
-SymTFT. The schematic of the SymTFT construction is shown in Fig.

VI
Fermionic SPT phases from SymTFT

We now discuss fermionic SPTs in SymTFT. Let us first review the cohomology and super-cohomology characterizations of
1+1D
fermionic SPTs
[

,
65

The cohomology characterization of a
G^{F}
-SPT is a cocycle
\omega\in Z^{2}[G^{F},U(1)]
and its slant product with fermion parity
\nu(g):=\omega(g,F)-\omega(F,g)
.
13
13

13

We use the convention that
U(1)=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}=[0,1]/(0\simeq 1)
and write multiplication in
U(1)
as addition mod 1.
Physically the phase
\exp(2\pi i\omega)
is the projective phase of edge symmetry actions, and
\nu
keeps track of the fermion parity of edge symmetry operators. Since
\nu
is determined by
\omega
it would seem redundant to include
\nu
in the characterization. But
\nu
participates in the stacking rules of fermionic SPTs in a non-trivial way. More concretely, the stacking rule of
G^{F}
-SPTs is not the same as the group structure of the cohomology group
H^{2}[G^{F},U(1)]
, and
\nu
will encode this difference. Therefore we will still label fermionic SPTs by the pairs
(\omega,\nu)
.

The super-cohomology characterization originates in the decorated domain wall structure of fermionic SPTs. It consists of two functions
\alpha\in C^{2}[G^{B},U(1)],\beta\in C^{1}[G^{B},\mathbb{Z}_{2}]
. Physically
\alpha
is the projective phase of edge
G^{B}
symmetry actions, and
\beta
keeps track of the fermion parity of these edge operators. But since
G^{B}
symmetry actions are not closed under composition if fermion parity extension is non-trivial,
\alpha
is in general not a cocycle.
\alpha,\beta
can be written as pull-backs of
\omega,\nu
via the section map
s
:
\alpha=s^{*}(\omega),\beta=s^{*}(\nu)
. They are subject to relation

\displaystyle\delta\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\beta\cup\rho,~\delta\beta=0

(80)

The cohomology invariants can be recovered by the super-cohomology invariants via the relation

\displaystyle\omega=p^{*}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{2}p^{*}(\beta)\cup t,~\nu=p^{*}(\beta).

(81)

VI.1
Partition function of fermionic SPTs from SymTFT

Now we return to the SymTFT and derive the partition function of fermionic SPTs. The physical boundary corresponding to the
G^{F}
-SPT with cohomology invariants
\omega,\nu
is given by the boundary state

\displaystyle|\omega\rangle:=\sum_{A_{f}}e^{2\pi i\int_{M}A_{f}^{*}(\omega)}|A_{f}\rangle.

(82)

Write
A_{f}=A_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}+A_{b}^{*}(s)

\omega=p^{*}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{2}p^{*}(\beta)\cup t

as

\displaystyle A^{*}_{f}(\omega)

\displaystyle=(A_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}+A_{b}^{*}(s))^{*}(p^{*}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{2}p^{*}(\beta)\cup t)

(83)

\displaystyle=A_{b}^{*}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{2}A_{b}^{*}(\beta)\cup A_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}.

(84)

The reference boundary is given by (

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{F}(\eta,a_{b})|\omega\rangle=\sum_{A_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}},dA_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}=a^{*}_{b}(\rho)}\frac{\text{Arf}(\eta+A_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}+\tau)}{\sqrt{|H^{1}[M,\mathbb{Z}_{2}]|}}\exp\left(2\pi i\int_{M}a_{b}^{*}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{2}a_{b}^{*}(\beta)\cup A_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\right)

This is exactly the partition function of the
(\omega,\nu)

VI.2
Stacking rules of fermionic SPTs from SymTFT

We have shown that the SymTFT is capable of describing fermionic invertible phases. In particular, we have shown how to reveal edge modes of SPT phases via SymTFT, and how to compute the partition function of fermionic SPTs via SymTFT.

Another crucial ingredient to the classification of invertible phases is their group structure under stacking.
Stacking is a physical operation that outputs a new phase from two input phases. Take two
G
-symmetric invertible phases
\mathcal{F}^{1,2}
. Their stacking
\mathcal{F}^{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}^{2}
is obtained by two steps. In the first step we form stack the two phases on top of one another, mathematically: defining a new system as the tensor product
\mathcal{F}^{2}
of
\mathcal{F}^{1}
. This results in a system with an enlarged symmetry
G\times G
. Then in the second step we turn on generic interactions between the two phases that preserve the diagonal symmetry
\{(g,g)|g\in G\}<G\times G
while preserving the gap of the combined system. The resulting system can the be viewed as another
G
-symmetric invertible phase, and this process defines a group structure on the set of invertible phases.

For example, in the
G^{F}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
case we obtained four invertible phases from SymTFT. But there are two possible group structure on this set of invertible phases, either
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
. We want to ask: Can we determine directly from the SymTFT which of the two options is realized? Relatedly, even though the set of
1+1D
fermionic SPTs are the same as the set of
1+1D
bosonic SPTs, the stacking rule (group structure) for
1+1D
fermionic SPTs is not the same as the stacking rule of
1+1D
bosonic SPTs, which is determined by the group structure
H^{2}[G^{F},U(1)]

[
69
,
65
,
74
,
75
]
. In this section, we demonstrate that the stacking rules between fermionic and bosonic SPTs with isomorphic symmetry groups can indeed be differentiated using the techniques of SymTFT.

VI.2.1
Review: Bosonic SPT stacking rules from SymTFT

Let us first focus on bosonic SPT phases with symmetry group
G
.
In SymTFT different
G
SPTs correspond to distinct fully-gapped boundary conditions (Lagrangian condensations) of
D(G)
.
Stacking of gapped boson SPTs then translates to a product structure on the set of Lagrangian condensations of
D(G)
. To derive this product, imagine stacking two phases, each represented by a SymTFT thin-slab. Let
\mathcal{A}^{1,2}
be the two Lagrangian condensations of
D(G^{B})
that produce phases
\mathcal{F}^{1,2}
when put on the physical boundary
\sf{B_{phys}}
. The stacked SymTFT sandwich now has the bulk theory
D(G\times G)
, and the reference boundary has the product condensation algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}\boxtimes\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}
. Similarly the physical boundary now has the product condensation algebra
\mathcal{A}^{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{A}^{2}
. This sandwich now describes a
1+1D
system with symmetry
G\times G
. Following the second step of the stacking operation, We then break the symmetry down to the diagonal subgroup. This can be done by Higgsing the bulk gauge theory to the diagonal subgroup, which is achieved by condensing the following diagonal algebra

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{b}=\bigoplus_{R\in\text{Rep}(G)}e_{R}\otimes e_{R}^{*}.

(87)

in the SymTFT sandwich. After this diagonal condensation, the bulk theory reduces to the theory
D(G^{d})
. The fluxes of the theory
D(G^{d})
are related to the original
D(G\times G)
theory via
m^{d}_{g}\cong m^{1}_{g}m^{2}_{g}
, therefore correspond to the diagonal symmetry when acting on the reference boundary. The boundary conditions on
\sf{B_{ref}}
and
\sf{B_{phys}}
are now boundary conditions of the bulk
G^{d}
-gauge theory. The condensation on the reference boundary will reduce from
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}
to
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}
. This is because the condensation
\mathcal{E}_{b}
identifies
e_{R}
in the first copy of
D(G)
with
e_{R}
in the second copy. In other words the reference boundary condition is invariant under stacking. The new physical boundary is obtained as follows. For simplicity we consider abelian group
G
for now. The physical boundary before condensing
\mathcal{E}_{b}
is given by the Lagrangian algebra
\mathcal{A}^{1}\times\mathcal{A}^{2}
, which is a sum of anyons in
D(G\times G)
. The diagonal condensation will confine certain anyons of
D(G\times G)
, therefore we remove the confined anyons in the product
A^{1}\times A^{2}
and identify the deconfined anyons with anyons in
D(G^{d})
. This gives us a condensation algebra in
D(G^{d})
and defines the new physical boundary condition for
D(G^{d})
. This procedure defines a product between
\mathcal{A}^{1}
and
\mathcal{A}^{2}
, which we denote as
\mathcal{A}^{1}\boxtimes_{b}\mathcal{A}^{2}
.

VI.2.2
Example:
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}

We illustrate this product structure with an example. Consider the SymTFT for
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{A}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{B}
. Condensing
\mathcal{A}^{1}=1+e_{A}m_{B}+e_{B}m_{A}+f_{A}f_{B}
at
\sf{B_{phys}}
gives the non-trivial SPT phase protected by
G
. The reference boundary has the condensation
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}=1+e_{A}+e_{B}+e_{A}e_{B}
. Now let us try to stack two
\mathcal{A}^{1}
s.
The diagonal condensation is

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{b}=1+e_{A}e_{A}^{\prime}+e_{B}e_{B}^{\prime}+e_{A}e_{B}e^{\prime}_{A}e^{\prime}_{B}.

(88)

The deconfined anyons are generated by
e_{A}\mathcal{E}_{b},e_{B}\mathcal{E}_{b},m_{A}m^{\prime}_{A}\mathcal{E}_{b},m_{B}m^{\prime}_{B}\mathcal{E}_{b}
. If we denote anyons of
D(G^{d})
by
a^{*}
, then anyons of
D(G^{d})
are related to those of
D(G\times G)
via
e^{*}_{A}=e_{A}\mathcal{E}_{b},e^{*}_{B}=e_{B}\mathcal{E}_{b},m^{*}_{A}=m_{A}m^{\prime}_{A}\mathcal{E}_{b},m^{*}_{B}=m_{B}m^{\prime}_{B}\mathcal{E}_{b}
. As promised, the new fluxes are diagonal products of the fluxes of the two copies. Using this identification, we see that the reference boundary condition becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}\boxtimes\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}=(1+e_{A}+e_{B}+e_{A}e_{B})(1+e^{\prime}_{A}+e^{\prime}_{B}+e^{\prime}_{A}e^{\prime}_{B})=1^{*}+e^{*}_{A}+e^{*}_{B}+e^{*}_{A}e^{*}_{B}.

(89)

Therefore we have
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}\boxtimes_{b}\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}=\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}
, as claimed. The physical boundary condition is

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{A}^{1}=(1+e_{A}m_{B}+e_{B}m_{A}+f_{A}f_{B})(1^{\prime}+e^{\prime}_{A}m^{\prime}_{B}+e^{\prime}_{B}m^{\prime}_{A}+f^{\prime}_{A}f^{\prime}_{B}).

(90)

Expand this product and collect the deconfined anyons, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{A}^{1}\to 1+e_{A}m_{B}e^{\prime}_{A}m^{\prime}_{B}+e_{B}m_{A}e^{\prime}_{B}m^{\prime}_{A}+f_{A}f_{B}f^{\prime}_{A}f^{\prime}_{B}=1^{*}+m^{*}_{B}+m^{*}_{A}+m^{*}_{A}m^{*}_{B}.

(91)

We therefore have
\mathcal{A}^{1}\boxtimes_{b}\mathcal{A}^{1}=1+m_{A}+m_{B}+m_{A}m_{B}
, which is exactly the Lagrangian condensation that represents the trivial SPT phase. We have recovered the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-stacking rule of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-SPTs.

The stacking of two Lagrangian condensations in other Abelian quantum doubles can be dealt with in a similar fashion, and it can be shown that the stacking of Lagrangian algebras defined in this way agrees with the stacking of bosonic
G
-SPTs. This construction can also be extended naturally to generic non-abelian symmetry
G
, the details of which will appear elsewhere.

VI.2.3
Fermionic SPT stacking rules in SymTFT

Similar to the bosonic case, fermionic SPTs form a group under stacking. By the SymTFT dictionary this means there is another product structure on Lagrangian condensations of
D(G^{F})
, distinct from
\boxtimes_{b}
, that matches with the fermionic SPT stacking rule. To motivate the new product, let us revisit the SymTFT for
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
. The reference boundary has condensation
1+f
. If we stack two
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
-systems, the reference boundary becomes
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}\boxtimes\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}=(1+f)(1+f^{\prime})
. If we continue to use the diagonal condensation
\mathcal{E}_{b}=1+ee^{\prime}
, then, the deconfined anyons in the product
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}\boxtimes\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}=1+f+f^{\prime}+ff^{\prime}
are
1+ff^{\prime}\cong 1+m^{*}
. We see the reference boundary is no longer invariant under the bosonic stacking rule. Consider another diagonal condensation
\mathcal{E}_{f}=1+ff^{\prime}
. The deconfined anyons are generated by
mm^{\prime}\mathcal{E}_{f},f\mathcal{E}_{f}
, which form a single copy of toric code under the identification
m^{*}:=mm^{\prime}\mathcal{E}_{f},f^{*}:=f\mathcal{E}_{f},e^{*}:=em^{\prime}\mathcal{E}_{f}
. Here the identification is chosen so that
mm^{\prime}
is mapped to the new
m^{*}
. This is because
m
-line is identified with the symmetry generator, therefore we wish the diagonal symmetry to be represented by
mm^{\prime}
. The reference boundary now changes to
(1+f)(1+f^{\prime})=(1+ff^{\prime})+(f+f^{\prime})=1^{*}+f^{*}
. The reference boundary is thus invariant under the new diagonal condensation
\mathcal{E}_{f}
. For a general fermionic symmetry
G^{F}
, we take the diagonal condensation that breaks
G^{F}\times G^{F}
to the diagonal
G^{F,d}
to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{f}=

\displaystyle\left(\bigoplus_{R,|R|=0}e_{R}\otimes e_{R}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle\bigoplus\left(\bigoplus_{R,|R|=1}e_{R}m^{F}\otimes e_{R}^{*}m^{F}\right).

(92)

This is the proper modifification of
\mathcal{E}_{b}
, because odd charges of
G^{F}
are really the dyons
e_{R}m^{F}
. For Abelian
G^{F}
, the stacking of two Lagrangian condensations
\mathcal{A}^{1},\mathcal{A}^{2}
at
\sf{B_{phys}}
is defined similar to the bosonic case, namely one first forms the product
\mathcal{A}^{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{A}^{2}
, then deletes the anyons that are confined after condensing
\mathcal{E}_{f}
. The remaining anyons define a Lagrangian condensation of
D(G^{F,d})
. This product is denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{1}\boxtimes_{f}\mathcal{A}^{2}
. Next we use an example to illustrate how the new diagonal condensation
\mathcal{E}_{f}
captures the difference between bosonic and fermionic SPT stacking rules.

VI.2.4
Example: stacking of Kitaev chains

The simplest non-trivial fermionic invertible phase is the Kitaev chain, represented by the condensation
\mathcal{A}=1+e
in the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
-SymTFT. The stacking of two Kitaev chain should be the trivial phase, represented by the condensation
\mathcal{A}_{0}=1+m
in the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
-SymTFT.

Now we apply the general construction illustrated above to derive this. The product algebra is initially

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=1+e+e^{\prime}+ee^{\prime}.

(93)

The anyons in the sum that stay deconfined after the diagonal condensation
1+ff^{\prime}
are
1,ee^{\prime}
.
Using the relation
m^{*}=mm^{\prime}\cong ee^{\prime},f^{*}:=f\cong f,e^{*}:=em^{\prime}\cong e^{\prime}m
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\boxtimes_{f}\mathcal{A}\cong 1+ee^{\prime}\cong 1+m^{*}=\mathcal{A}_{0},

(94)

reproducing the stacking rule of the Kitaev chain.

VI.2.5
Example:
G^{F}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
and
G^{B}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}

The reason for considering this symmetry group is that this is the smallest group for which bosonic and fermionic SPTs have different stacking rules. This means given elements
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in H^{2}[G^{F}\cong G^{B},U(1)]
, and fermionic phases determined by them
\mathcal{F}_{\omega_{1}},\mathcal{F}_{\omega_{2}}
, we have
\mathcal{F}_{\omega_{1}}\boxtimes_{f}\mathcal{F}_{\omega_{2}}\neq\mathcal{F}_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}
, where
\boxtimes_{f}
means stacking of fermionic phases.

There are various ways of deriving the stacking rule, here we provide a method based on symmetry fractionalization of gapped symmetries. For a fermionic phase
\mathcal{F}_{\omega}
in the class
[\omega]\in H^{2}[G^{F},U(1)]
, the symmetry actions
U_{g},~g\in G^{F}
form a projective representation at the edge of the system:
\widetilde{U}_{g}\widetilde{U}_{h}=\omega(g,h)\widetilde{U}_{gh}
. Here
\widetilde{U}
stands for the localized version of the symmetry action that only acts on one of the edges of the system. For the symmetry group
\mathbb{Z}^{A}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{B}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
, since there is no projective representation of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
alone, the projective representations of
\mathbb{Z}^{A}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{B}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
can be labelled by which pairs of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-generators anti-commute. Consider a phase
\mathcal{F}_{1}
with anti-commuting
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{A}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
generators. Denote the localized unitaries by
\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{1}}_{g^{A}}
and
\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{1}}_{P_{f}}
, then we have
\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{1}}_{g^{A}}\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{1}}_{P_{f}}=-\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{1}}_{P_{f}}\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{1}}_{g^{A}}
. Similarly consider another phase
\mathcal{F}^{2}
with anti-commuting
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{B}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
generators, we have
\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{2}}_{g^{B}}\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{2}}_{P_{f}}=-\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{2}}_{P_{f}}\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{2}}_{g^{B}}
. Notice being charged under fermion parity means the operator is fermionic. In the current case this means
\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{1}}_{g^{A}}
and
\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{2}}_{g^{B}}
are both fermionic operators. Next we stack
\mathcal{F}^{1}
with
\mathcal{F}^{2}
, the generator of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{A},\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{B},\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
factors in
\mathcal{F}^{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}^{2}
are
\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{1}}_{g^{A}},\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{2}}_{g^{B}}
and
\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{1}}_{P_{f}}\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{2}}_{P_{f}}
respectively. We see that now the pairs
(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{A},\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F})
and
(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{B},\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F})
anti-commute. This is expected. However, since
\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{1}}_{g^{A}}
and
\widetilde{U}^{\mathcal{F}_{2}}_{g^{B}}
are both fermionic operators, they also anti-commute due to fermion statistics.

If we label a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{A}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{B}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
projective representation by
(i,j,k)
, where
i=0,1
labels whether
\mathbb{Z}^{A}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
generators anti-commute,
j=0,1
labels whether
\mathbb{Z}^{B}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
generators anti-commute, and
k=0,1
labels whether
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{A}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{B}
generators anti-commute. Then the stacking rule we just derived states
(1,0,0)\boxtimes(0,1,0)=(1,1,1)
. However, for a bosonic symmetry with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
replaced by another bosonic
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{C}
factor, we would have
(1,0,0)\boxtimes(0,1,0)=(1,1,0)
: the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{A}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{B}
generators in the stacked system would commute instead of anti-commute. How do we see this difference between bosonic and fermionic stacking rules in SymTFT? Let us first construct the SymTFT for
\mathcal{F}^{1}
and
\mathcal{F}^{2}
.

The SymTFT for
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{A}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{B}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
is the quantum double
D(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{A}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{B}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F})
. The reference boundary condition is
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}=\langle e_{1},e_{2},f_{3}\rangle
. Consider the two choices of gapped physical boundary condition
\mathcal{A}^{1}=\langle e_{1}m_{3},e_{3}m_{1},m_{2}\rangle,\mathcal{A}^{2}=\langle e_{2}m_{3},e_{3}m_{2},m_{1}\rangle
. By studying their edge modes one can verify
\mathcal{A}^{1,2}
describe the fermionic phases
(1,0,0)
and
(0,1,0)
respectively.

The diagonal condensation is
\mathcal{E}_{f}=1+e_{A}e^{\prime}_{A}+e_{B}e^{\prime}_{B}+f_{C}f^{\prime}_{C}
, which leaves
m_{A}^{*}:=m_{A}m_{A}^{\prime},m_{B}^{*}:=m_{B}m_{B}^{\prime},m_{C}^{*}=m_{C}m_{C}^{\prime},e_{A}^{*}:=e_{A},e_{B}^{*}:=e_{B},e_{C}^{*}:=e_{C}m_{C}^{\prime}
deconfined. In the product

\displaystyle e_{1}e_{2}^{\prime}m_{3}m_{3}^{\prime}=e_{1}^{*}e_{2}^{*}m_{3}^{*},~e_{1}e_{3}^{\prime}m_{2}m_{2}^{\prime}m_{3}=e_{1}^{*}e_{3}^{*}m_{2}^{*},~e_{3}e_{2}^{\prime}m_{1}m_{1}^{\prime}m_{3}^{\prime}=e_{3}^{*}e_{2}^{*}m_{1}^{*}.

(95)

Therefore

\mathbb{Z}_{2}

(1,0,0)\boxtimes_{f}(0,1,0)=(1,1,1)

\mathcal{E}_{b}

\mathcal{A}^{1}\boxtimes_{b}\mathcal{A}^{2}=\langle e_{1}e_{2}m_{3},e_{3}m_{2},e_{3}m_{1}\rangle

(1,0,0)\boxtimes_{b}(0,1,0)=(1,1,0)
. Therefore the new diagonal condensation

In general, for two fermionic phases given by

\displaystyle[\omega_{1}]\boxtimes_{f}[\omega_{2}]=[\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\nu_{1}\cup\nu_{2}],

-SPT, and the stacking rule (

VII
Bosonization in SymTFT

The crucial difference between SymTFT for bosonic and fermion symmetries is the choice of reference boundary condition. If we fix the boundary condition at
\sf{B_{phys}}
, choosing different boundary conditions amounts to some topological manipulations acting on the original 1+1D system. A natural question to consider is the following: if we fix the boundary condition at
\sf{B_{phys}}
, is there any relation between the two phases represented by reference boundaries
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{b}
(given by Eq. (
7
))? We show in this section that the relation is exactly bosonization, also known as Jordan-Wigner transformation in
1+1D
. More precisely, if the SymTFT sandwich with
A_{\sf{ref}}^{f}
reference boundary represents a
1+1D
fermionic phase
\sf{T}
with symmetry
G^{F}
, then by changing the reference boundary to
A_{\sf{ref}}^{b}
we obtain exactly the Jordan-Wigner transformation of
\sf{T}
, which may have a different (non-isomorphic) bosonic symmetry
G^{B}
.

Bosonization
[
66
,
76
,
77
]
is a general correspondence between bosonic systems (whose fundamental degrees of freedom are bosonic) and fermionic systems (whose fundamental degrees of freedom
are fermionic). It offers a unique and effective way to deal with complex fermionic problems by transforming them into more manageable bosonic problems. The SymTFT framework we have developed for fermionic symmetries offers a fresh perspective on bosonization within the broader context of managing general topological manipulations, as we now explore.

VII.1
Bosonization of an anomaly-free symmetry

Before we initiate the proof let us review bosonization/Jordan-Wigner transformation in the language of topological field theory. Formally, bosonization is the process of ‘‘summing over spin structures’’ for the partition function of fermionic systems. When no gravitational anomaly is present, bosonization can also be refered to as ‘‘gauging fermion parity’’.
14
14

14

As discussed in
[
35
]
, for
1+1D
fermionic systems, when the gravitational anomaly corresponds to a multiple of 16, there is still a well-defined procedure of “summing over spin structures”, which coincides with a properly defined version of “gauging fermion parity”. When the gravitation anomaly is a multiple of 8 but not 16, this process of “gauging fermion parity” can still be defined but will generate a fermionic theory instead of a bosonic theory. Hence, there is a subtle difference between the two concepts that is fortunately absent in our context.
One can also stack the system with an invertible fermion topological order (e.g. the Kitaev chain in 1+1D) before summing over spin structures. These two options result in phases related by Kramers-Wannier (KW) duality. The standard Jordan-Wigner transformation, where the trivial fermionic phase is mapped to the trivial bosonic phase, corresponds to the second choice.

Following our discussion of spin structure in Section
V.2.3
, the partition function of a

is a subgroup of the bosonic symmetry

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{JW}^{-1}(\sf{T})}[a_{b},a_{p}]=\sum_{\eta}\frac{\text{Arf}(\eta+\tau+a_{p})}{\sqrt{|H^{1}[M,\mathbb{Z}_{2}]|}}\mathcal{Z}_{\sf{T}}[a_{b},\eta].

(98)

Now we derive this result purely within our construction of SymTFT. The fermionic reference boundary state with

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(\eta,a_{b})\rangle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{A_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}},dA_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}=a_{b}^{*}(\rho)}\frac{\text{Arf}(\eta+A_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}+\tau)}{\sqrt{|H^{1}[M,\mathbb{Z}_{2}]|}}|A_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}},A_{b}=a_{b}\rangle.

(99)

Summing over
\eta

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta,d\eta=a_{b}^{*}(\rho)+w_{2}}\frac{\text{Arf}(\eta+a_{p}+\tau)}{\sqrt{|H^{1}[M,\mathbb{Z}_{2}]|}}|\eta,a_{b}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta,A_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}}\frac{\text{Arf}(\eta+a_{p}+\tau)}{\sqrt{|H^{1}[M,\mathbb{Z}_{2}]|}}\frac{\text{Arf}(\eta+A_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}+\tau)}{\sqrt{|H^{1}[M,\mathbb{Z}_{2}]|}}|A_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}},A_{b}=a_{b}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta,A_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}}\frac{q_{\eta+\tau+a_{p}}(a_{p}+A_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}})}{|H^{1}[M,\mathbb{Z}_{2}]|}|A_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}},A_{b}=a_{b}\rangle

Some examples we have studied in previous sections can be understood from the perspective of bosonization. For example, the Majorana CFT is related to the Ising CFT by bosonization, and we have seen that the two phases are indeed related by changing the reference boundary from

VII.2
Bosonization of anomalous symmetry

So far the examples studied have non-anomalous symmetries. In the following we study bosonization of some anomalous symmetries. What is interesting is that in the presence of anomaly the bosonization does not preserve the structure of the symmetry group. The elementary example is when the fermionic symmetry is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
, this symmetry has a
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
-classification of anomaly. The bosonization of the
\nu=2
level anomaly is a
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
symmetry with
k=2
level anomaly. What is even more interesting is the
\nu=1
level anomaly, this is mapped to the non-invertible Ising symmetry under bosonization. These facts once again show that the SymTFT is what is intrinsic in defining a symmetry instead of the group structure. We will show how these dualities can be derived from simple SymTFT considerations.

VII.2.1
The
\nu=2
anomaly of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}

The SymTFT for the
\nu=2
anomalous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
symmetry has been derived in Section
IV.1
. Recall that the theory
\mathcal{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F},\nu=2]
is generated by two order-4 anyons
\phi,\phi_{F}
, which correspond to gauge fluxes of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
respectively. They have quantum spins
\theta_{\phi_{F}}=1,\theta_{\phi}=e^{i\pi/4}
and mutual statistics
\theta_{\phi,\phi_{F}}=e^{i\pi/2}
.

Since
\mathcal{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F},\nu=2]
is a non-chiral Abelian topological order, it must be equivalent to a twisted Abelian quantum double. Indeed it is equivalent to the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
twisted quantum double
D_{k=2}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})
, where
k\in H^{3}[\mathbb{Z}_{4},U(1)]
labels the twists of
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
. The theory
D_{k=2}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})
is generated by a bosonic gauge charge
e
and a flux
m
with quantum spin
\theta_{m}=e^{i\pi/4}
. The mutual statistics is
\theta_{e,m}=e^{i\pi/2}
. We see that an isomorphism to the theory
\mathcal{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F},\nu=2]
can be achieved by the mapping
\phi\simeq m,\phi_{F}\simeq e
.
Consider a bosonic gapped reference boundary of
\mathcal{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F},\nu=2]\cong D_{k=2}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})
obtained by condensing
e
. The non-trivial defect lines on this boundary are generated by
m\simeq\phi
, which has a
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
fusion rule and quantum spin
\theta_{m}=e^{i\pi/4}
. Therefore if we choose to condense
e
on the reference boundary, then the SymTFT describes a bosonic
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
symmetry, with level
k=2
anomaly. This anomaly can also be seen from the
F
-symbols of the
m
-lines. This leads to the conclusion

The fermionic symmetry
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
with anomaly
\nu=2
is dual to the bosonic symmetry
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
with anomaly
k=2
.

This result has been derived by direct bosonization in
[
66
]
, here we see the SymTFT provides a very simple derivation.

Moreover, we may generalize the “equivalent symmetry” principle in
[
7
,
8
]
to include both bosonic and fermionic symmetries. We state that, two symmetries, either bosonic or fermionic, are equivalent, if they provide the same set of constraint on local symmetric operators. Then, the sets of local symmetric operators under two equivalent symmetries have an one-to-one correspondence and the same algebraic relations. The Hamiltonians as sums of those symmetric local operators also have an one-to-one correspondence, and the corresponding Hamiltonians have the same spectrum. Based on the SymTFT framework we developed for fermionic symmetries, we claim that two symmetries, either bosonic or fermionic, are equivalent, if they have isomorphism SymTFT. For instance, the fermionic symmetry
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
, with
\nu=2
anomaly, is equivalent to the bosonic symmetry
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
with anomaly
k=2
.

VII.2.2
The
\nu=1
anomaly of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}

Let us now consider the more exotic case: the root anomaly of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
. This anomaly is realized on the boundary of the root phase of 2+1D
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-symmetric topological superconductor. This 2+1D phase is the stacking
p+ip\boxtimes p-ip
, with the
p+ip
layer charged under
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. The SymTFT for the
\nu=1
anomalous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
is then the gauged
p+ip\boxtimes p-ip
. This theory, denoted by
\mathcal{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F},\nu=1]
, is just
\text{Ising}\boxtimes\overline{\text{Ising}}
. Denote the Ising anyons by
\sigma,\psi
, and the time-reversal by
\overline{\sigma},\overline{\psi}
, with fusion rules
\sigma\times\sigma=1+\psi
. Then the flux of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is the
\pi
-flux in the
p+ip
layer,
\sigma
. The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
flux is the
\pi
-flux in both layers,
\sigma\overline{\sigma}
.
\psi,\overline{\psi}
are charged and uncharged fermions respectively, and
\psi\overline{\psi}
is the charged boson. On the reference boundary we should condense all gauge charges, in this case
\psi
and
\overline{\psi}
. The non-trivial defect lines on the reference boundary is then generated by
\sigma,\overline{\sigma}
. With
\psi,\overline{\psi}
condensed, the flux lines on the reference boundary indeed have the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
fusion rule:
\sigma^{2}\simeq 1,\overline{\sigma}^{2}\simeq 1
.

To obtain the bosonized reference boundary we make the following observation: Recall in the non-anomalous case the fermionic reference boundary condenses the fermionic anyons
e_{R}m^{F}
, which is the bosonic gauge charge
e_{R}
, bound with fermion parity flux
m^{F}
. Reverse this relation, we may say the bosonic gauge charge
e_{R}
, is obtained by binding a fermion parity flux
m^{F}
to
e_{R}m^{F}
. Generalize this observation to the current case, we see that the fermionic charges condensed on the reference boundary are
\psi,\overline{\psi}
. If we bind a fermion parity flux
\sigma\overline{\sigma}
to them, we obtain
\psi\times\sigma\overline{\sigma}=\sigma\overline{\sigma}
and
\overline{\psi}\times\sigma\overline{\sigma}=\sigma\overline{\sigma}
. Therefore, the bosonized reference boundary condenses
\sigma\overline{\sigma}
and
\psi\overline{\psi}

Notice that the theory
\mathcal{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F},\nu=1]
is equivalent to
\text{Ising}\boxtimes\overline{\text{Ising}}
, we may also view it as the SymTFT for the Ising symmetry. The bosonized boundary has
\sigma\overline{\sigma},\psi\overline{\psi}
condensed. This is exactly the Lagrangian condensation that describes the Ising non-invertible symmetry. Namely, this condensation leaves non-trivial defect lines generated by
\sigma\cong\overline{\sigma},\psi\cong\overline{\psi}

\sigma\times\sigma=1+\psi,~\sigma\times\psi=\sigma,~\psi\times\psi=1

The fermionic symmetry
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
with anomaly
\nu=1
is equivalent to the non-invertible Ising symmetry.

We see that the SymTFT provides us a systematic and convenient way to identify equivalent symmetries, including both bosonic and fermionic symmetries.

VIII
Summary and outlook

In this work we developed a framework of topological holograph for fermionic symmetries of fermionic systems. For a group-like symmetry
G^{F}
of
1+1D
systems, the SymTFT is the
2+1D
bosonic topological order
D(G^{F})
together with a fermionic reference boundary, with a fermionic condensation
\mathcal{A}_{\sf{ref}}^{f}
. The SymTFT sandwich describes an effective
1+1D
fermionic system with
G^{F}
symmetry. We showed how various aspects of fermionic systems, such as the non-trivial invertible phase modeled by the Kitaev chain, dependence on spin structure, etc. are described in the SymTFT framework. We analyzed gapped fermionic phases, including the Kitaev chain and fermionic SPTs via the SymTFT. We showed that the SymTFT gives full characterization of fermionic invertible phases, including their partition function, edge modes, and their stacking rules.

We also studied fermionic gapless phases and critical points in SymTFT. We reproduced the partition function of the Majorana CFT via SymTFT. Moreover, we used the SymTFT framework to construct an example of an intrinisically-fermionic, intrinsically gapless SPT with
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
symmetry, uncover its edge-mode structure, and showed how the SymTFT structure can be directly used to construct a field theory description of this phase. This demonstrated that beyond reproducing known properties of phases, the SymTFT also has proved to be a
useful
theoretical tool for designing novel gapless phases.

Finally, we addressed the issue of bosonization in the context of SymTFT. We showed that bosonization in SymTFT amounts to changing the reference boundary from the fermionic condensation
\mathcal{A}^{f}_{\sf{ref}}
to the bosonic condensation
\mathcal{A}^{b}_{\sf{ref}}
. With this understanding, we showed that the
\nu=1
anomalous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
symmetry bosonizes to the non-invertible Ising symmetry and the
\nu=2
anomalous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
symmetry bosonizes to the
k=2
bosonic
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
symmetry.

Jailbreaking Large Language Models Through Alignment Vulnerabilities in Out-of-Distribution Settings
Yue Huang, Jingyu Tang, Dongping Chen, Bingda Tang, Yao Wan, Lichao Sun, Philip S. Yu, Xiangliang Zhang

Abstract
. Recently, Large Language Models (LLMs) have garnered significant attention for their exceptional natural language processing capabilities. However, concerns about their trustworthiness remain unresolved, particularly in addressing “jailbreaking” attacks on aligned LLMs. Previous research predominantly relies on scenarios involving white-box LLMs or specific, fixed prompt templates, which are often impractical and lack broad applicability. In this paper, we introduce a straightforward and novel method called ObscurePrompt for jailbreaking LLMs, inspired by the observed fragile alignments in Out-of-Distribution (OOD) data. Specifically, we first formulate the decision boundary in the jailbreaking process and then explore how obscure text affects LLM’s ethical decision boundary. ObscurePrompt starts with constructing a base prompt that integrates well-known jailbreaking techniques. Powerful LLMs are then utilized to obscure the original prompt through iterative transformations, aiming to bolster the attack’s robustness. Comprehensive experiments show that our approach substantially improves upon previous methods in terms of attack effectiveness, maintaining efficacy against two prevalent defense mechanisms.

This paper makes the following contributions:

\triangleright

Observation about LLMs’ fragile alignment on OOD data.
By visualizing the representations of different queries within the hidden states of LLMs, we observed that OOD queries (

\triangleright

A novel and simple jailbreak method.

ObscurePrompt

ObscurePrompt
is training-free and operates in a black-box setting, meaning it does not require access to the internal architecture of the target LLMs. This approach avoids the reliance on specific prompt templates, enhancing its feasibility and robustness for real-world applications.

Jailbreak Attack & Defense of LLMs

Jailbreak is a burgeoning technology that has emerged alongside the rise in popularity of LLMs. A recent study by
Wei et al. (2023a)
provides an overview of the factors enabling jailbreak attacks to succeed in LLMs, despite their training for safety alignment. Numerous investigations have been conducted to probe the safety vulnerabilities of LLMs, resulting in the proposal of various jailbreak methods

(
Liu et al., 2023c
)

(
Zeng et al., 2024

(
Li et al., 2024c
)

Concurrently, numerous researchers are dedicated to developing effective defenses against jailbreak attacks. In addition to popular alignment technologies like reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF)
(
Christiano et al., 2023
)
, various strategies have been proposed to enhance LLM defense against jailbreaking. For instance,
Phute et al. (2023)
employs a self-reminder mechanism to verify the harmlessness of LLM outputs.
Robey et al. (2023)
and
Ji et al. (2024)
introduces techniques such as random input prompt perturbation or transformation, aggregating results to ensure output harmlessness.
Wei et al. (2023b)

Alon and Kamfonas (2023)
utilizes the perplexity metric to filter out unreadable jailbreak attacks like GCG
(
Zou et al., 2023

Zhou et al. (2024)
propose an optimization method to identify a robust suffix, thereby enhancing jailbreak defense effectively.

3.1.
Preliminary

Consider a pre-trained LLM,
P_{\theta}
, with parameters
\theta
that have not been aligned. To ensure its safety and trustworthiness, developers typically employ alignment strategies such as reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF)
(
Christiano et al., 2023
;
Zhang et al., 2023
)
or Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). We denote
P_{\tilde{\theta}}
as an aligned LLM, and
\mathcal{R}
as the output space. The model response to a user input
x
is denoted as
r\sim P_{\theta}(r|x)
. Building on prior studies
(
Yu et al., 2024
;
Zhang et al., 2023
)
, we present a Bayesian interpretation for prompting LLMs to elucidate the motivation behind our jailbreak method.

Assume
x=\{t_{1},t_{2},...,t_{n}\}
is the prompt composed of
n
tokens. The relationship between two consecutive tokens
t_{i}
and
t_{i+1}
is defined by a function
f
, such that
t_{i+1}=f(t_{i},h_{i},\epsilon_{i})
, where
h_{i}
is the latent variable linking the tokens, and
\epsilon_{i}
represents potential random noise.

The evolution of the latent variable
h_{i}
follows the stochastic process
P_{z}(h_{i}\mid t_{i},\{t_{l},h_{l}\}_{l<i})
, indicating that the distribution of
h_{i}
is influenced by the hidden concept
z
. Under the model
t_{i+1}=f(t_{i},h_{i},\epsilon_{i})
, it holds that
P(r\mid x)=\int_{\mathcal{Z}}\mathrm{d}zP(r\mid x,z)P(z\mid x)
.
h_{i}
reflects only the relationship between two consecutive tokens. To fully capture the semantic content of
x
, we introduce the hidden concept
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, modeled through the evolution of
h_{i}
. The hidden state signifies the nature of the prompt (
e.g.
, harmful or harmless).

For the aligned model
P_{\tilde{\theta}}
, the latent model interpretation of prompting LLMs implies:

\displaystyle r\sim P_{\tilde{\theta}}(r\mid x)

\displaystyle=P_{\tilde{\theta}}\left(r\mid x,z=z_{+}\right)P\left(z=z_{+}\mid x\right)

\displaystyle+P_{\tilde{\theta}}\left(r\mid x,z=z_{-}\right)P\left(z=z_{-}\mid x\right)

where
z_{-}
and
z_{+}
represent harmful and harmless concepts, respectively. This dual-concept model outlines the decision boundary for an “ethical boundary”
(
Zheng et al., 2024
;
Yu et al., 2024
)
:

r\sim P_{\tilde{\theta}}(r\mid x)=\Theta_{{z_{+}}}+\Theta_{{z_{-}}}

\displaystyle\Theta_{z_{+}}=

\displaystyle P_{\tilde{\theta}}\left(r\in\mathcal{R}\backslash\mathcal{R}_{\text{refuse }}\mid z=z_{+}:\Psi(x)=0\right)

\displaystyle\times P\left(z=z_{+}\mid x\right)

\displaystyle\Theta_{z_{-}}=

\displaystyle P_{\tilde{\theta}}\left(r\in\mathcal{R}_{\text{refuse }}\mid z=z_{-}:\Psi(x)=1\right)

\displaystyle\times P\left(z=z_{-}\mid x\right)

Here,
\mathcal{R}_{\text{refuse}}
denotes the responses that refuse to engage with harmful queries. The evaluation function
\Psi(x)
, outputs 1 if
x
is deemed harmless, otherwise 0.
This interpretation suggests that a successful jailbreak attack manipulates the input prompt to align more closely with a harmless concept (
z_{+}
), thereby weakening the effective decision boundary (
i.e.
, the ethical boundary).

4.
ObscurePrompt

This section introduces
ObscurePrompt
, a proposed training-free method to jailbreak black-box LLMs. As illustrated in
Figure 4
, our approach comprises three primary components: (1) Prompt Seed Curation: we begin by constructing a robust base prompt, integrating several established jailbreak techniques, such as "
Avoid Sorry
". (2) Obscure-Guided Transformation: Following a predefined instruction, we refine the base prompt by using
GPT-4
to enhance its obscurity. (3) Attack Integration: By iteratively repeating the aforementioned steps, we generate a series of obscure prompts, which are later used to attack the target LLMs.

4.1.
Prompt Seed Curation

To improve the success rate of the jailbreak attack, it is essential to first develop an effective base prompt template, as demonstrated by the previous study
(
Andriushchenko et al., 2024
)
. The template is based on the current jailbreak tricks which mainly focus on prompt engineering, like prefix injection, role-playing (
e.g.
, DAN
(
Meta, 2024
)
), and so on. The prompt integrates several of these jailbreak methods to enhance the effectiveness when conducting jailbreak attacks. Specifically, many jailbreak attack ways proposed previously and are widely used now such as
Prefix Modification
,
Role Emulation and Scenario Setting
or
Output Styling Constraints
.

Given an initial query
p
that may pose ethical or security concerns (
e.g.
, "
how to make a bomb?
"), the objective is to strategically refine
p
using a subset of jailbreak methods from
S_{m}
to generate a modified prompt
p^{\prime}_{seed}
that mitigates the harmful intentions or guides the LLM toward a safe and constructive response. This process can be formalized as follows:

(1)

p^{\prime}_{seed}=T(p;m_{i:j})

Here,
m_{i:j}
represents the subset of jailbreak methods selected from
S_{m}
, and
T(\cdot)
denotes the transformation function that applies these methods to the initial query
q
to produce the modified prompt
p^{\prime}
. The transformation function
T
encompasses operations like the addition of new strings, modification of the query structure, and the implementation of the specified jailbreak strategies to achieve the desired outcome.

4.2.
Obscure-Guided Transformation

In this phase, we introduce an innovative “obscure-guided transformation” strategy designed to enhance the effectiveness of jailbreaking prompts through strategic obscurity. The core assumption is that the safety protocols in most LLMs are not completely reliable, primarily because the information encoded through these safety protocols is substantially less than the vast knowledge base that LLMs possess. Therefore, by introducing knowledge to the LLMs that is beyond their safety alignment but within their extensive internal knowledge, there is a potential to bypass these safety measures successfully.

Specifically, the obscure-guided transformation can be formulated as:

(2)

p^{\prime}_{obscure}=F(p^{\prime}_{seed})

where
F(\cdot)
is the transformation operation that renders
p^{\prime}_{seed}
more obscure. we employ powerful LLMs specific to
GPT-4
as the obscure transformer. Upon receiving a request or prompt,
GPT-4
is utilized to increase the obscurity of the input text (The prompt for this is "
Please make the following text more obscure
"). Examples of both the original and transformed obscure queries are presented in
Table 1
.

Metrics.
We use Attack Successful Rate (ASR) for our evaluation metric. The ASR is the proportion of successful attacks, calculated as follows:

(3)

\text{ASR}=\frac{\text{\# Successful~Attacks}}{\text{\# All~Attacks}}

To determine whether an attack successfully jailbreaks the LLM, we employ the keyword-matching method used in GCG
(
Zou et al., 2023
)
and AutoDan
(
Liu et al., 2023c
)

This added difficulty could compromise the accuracy of the LLM’s judgment. To assess the reliability of the keyword-matching method in determining the success of an attack, we conduct a human evaluation. Specifically, two undergraduate students and one PhD student are tasked with labeling whether the responses from the LLMs are harmful (i.e., successfully jailbroken) or not. These human-generated labels are then compared with the results obtained from the keyword-matching method to calculate accuracy. Each sample is reviewed by all three evaluators. We randomly select responses from four separate experiments for this evaluation. The evaluation accuracy result is: 96.88% (Batch 1 with 128 samples), 96.67% (Batch 2 with 120 samples), 95.31% (Batch 3 with 128 samples), and 95.83% (Batch 4 with 120 samples).

Baselines.
We use three baselines with two white-box methods (

and

•

GCG

(
Zou et al., 2023
)
: The Greedy Coordinate Gradient (GCG) merges greedy search with gradient optimization to exploit vulnerabilities in LLMs. It starts by setting up an initial suffix and then uses greedy search to identify a group of tokens with adversarial potential.

•

AutoDAN

(
Liu et al., 2023c
)
: AutoDAN employs a meticulously crafted hierarchical genetic algorithm to produce prompts that discreetly facilitate jailbreak autonomously.

•

DeepInception

(
Li et al., 2024c
)
: DeepInception is a black-box jailbreak method, which capitalizes on the LLM’s capability for personification, creating an innovative nested scenario that facilitates an adaptive method for circumventing standard usage controls.

•

PAIR

(
Chao et al., 2024
)
: PAIR is also a black-box jailbreaking technique that employs an attack LLM. In our experiment, we use gpt-4o-mini as the attack LLM. This innovative approach is designed to autonomously generate jailbreaks for a separate target LLM, eliminating the need for human intervention.

Jailbreak Prompt Types.
We have selected four attack ways in the stage of prompt seed curation (§
4.1
), which are widely used based on the previous research
(
Huang et al., 2024c
;
Wei et al., 2023a
)
: “Start With Specified Sentences”, “Forget Restraints”, “Avoid Sorry”, and “Direct Answer”.

•

Start With Specified Sentences
: We instruct LLMs to initiate their responses with predetermined sentences, ensuring a controlled and specific start to their output.

•

Forget Restraints
: This approach falls under the category of
Role Emulation and Scenario Setting
. Here, we direct the LLM to disregard all previously established rules or constraints, simulating a scenario where it operates without its standard ethical or operational guidelines.

•

Avoid Sorry
: Commonly, LLMs express regret, often starting responses to potentially harmful queries with phrases like "I am sorry, …". To prevent such responses, we mandate that LLMs refrain from beginning their replies with "sorry" or any synonymous expressions, aiming to bypass programmed polite refusals.

•

Direct Answer
: This strategy is similar to the
avoid sorry
approach. It involves instructing LLMs to provide a straightforward response to the query, ensuring they directly address the question without evasion or circumlocution.

To optimize the clarity and coherence of the prompt sentences about these attack methods, we pre-experimented various sequencing arrangements of these attack types. Through this process, we determined the most effective order for enhancing understandability: (1) Forget Restraints, (2) Direct Answer, (3) Avoid Sorry, and (4) Start With Specified Sentences. This sequence ensures that the instructions are presented in a logically structured and understandable manner.

5.5.
Defense Against Attacks

To effectively explore the robustness of the proposed method against the defense, we consider two mainstream methods according to the previous study
(
Jain et al., 2023
)
: paraphrasing and perplexity (PPL) filtering.

Paraphrasing.
We use
ChatGPT
and
GPT-4
as our paraphrasing model. We paraphrase the original prompt using the following instruction:

“You are a helpful assistant. Please help me paraphrase the following sentences and return the paraphrased sentences only. The sentences are: [original prompt]”
.

In
Table 4
, the effectiveness of paraphrasing as a defense mechanism against adversarial attacks is quantified by the changes in ASR for various LLMs. Notably, when comparing the original and paraphrased prompts, all models demonstrate a decrease in ASR, with the most significant reduction observed in
Llama2-70b
(from 0.7525 to 0.2442). This suggests that paraphrasing generally reduces the models’ vulnerabilities to our proposed attack. We found that the main reason for this ASR decrease is that the prompt after paraphrasing is not as obscure as the original, making it easier to understand and thus reducing the effectiveness of obscure input. However, despite the reduction, the residual ASR, notably the 17.74% for
GPT-4
’s and 52.54% for
ChatGPT
, indicates a remaining risk. The variance in the impact of paraphrasing on different models suggests that the models’ underlying architectures and training data may influence their resilience to such attacks. These results highlight the importance of developing more robust models that maintain high resistance to adversarial inputs across both original and paraphrased prompts.

CONLUSION

In this paper, we proposed
ObscurePrompt
, a novel jailbreaking method for LLMs inspired by the inefficient alignment observed in OOD data. Our approach leverages the refinement of base prompts through obscure transformations performed by powerful LLMs, significantly improving the effectiveness of jailbreaking attacks beyond existing baselines. Our extensive experimental analysis demonstrates the superior performance of our method, which effectively overcomes the traditional limitations of dependence on white-box settings and specific prompt templates. Moreover, our study evaluates potential defense strategies. The insights gained from these investigations are invaluable for future research aimed at enhancing LLMs’ trustworthiness.

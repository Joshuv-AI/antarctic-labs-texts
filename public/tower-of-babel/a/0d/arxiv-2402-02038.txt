Computing isogenies at singular points of the modular polynomial
William E. Mahaney and Travis Morrison

Abstract
. In this paper we present a method which, given a singular point ( j 1 , j 2 ) (j_{1},j_{2}) on Y 0 ​ ( ℓ ) Y_{0}(\ell) with j 1 , j 2 ≠ 0 , 1728 j_{1},j_{2}\neq 0,1728 and an elliptic curve E E with j j -invariant j 1 {j_{1}} , returns an elliptic curve E ~ \widetilde{E} with j j -invariant j 2 {j_{2}} that admits a normalized ℓ \ell -isogeny ϕ : E → E ~ \phi\colon E\to\widetilde{E} . The isogeny ϕ \phi can then be efficiently computed from E E and E ~ \widetilde{E} using an algorithm of Bostan, Morain, Salvy, and Schost.



1.
Introduction

For an elliptic curve
E
defined over a field
k
, a separable isogeny
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
is determined (up to post-composition with an isomorphism) by its kernel.
This result is effective: for example, Vélu
[
V7́1
]
gives formulas for computing
\phi
(as a rational map) given the points in
\ker\phi
, and Kohel
[
Koh96
, Section 2.4]
gives formulas for computing these rational maps from the
kernel polynomial
of
\phi
, the monic polynomial which vanishes precisely at the
x
-coordinates of the nontrivial points in
\ker\phi
. The methods of Vélu and Kohel also give an equation for
E^{\prime}
, the codomain of
\phi
. The resulting isogeny is always
normalized
, meaning the invariant differential
\omega^{\prime}
of
E^{\prime}
pulls back, under
\phi
, to the invariant differential
\omega
of
E
. The isogeny
\phi
will be defined over
k
if
\ker\phi
is
\Gal(\overline{k})
-stable, or equivalently, if the kernel polynomial of
\phi
has coefficients in
k
.

As mentioned above, a (description of) a cyclic subgroup
K
of
E
determines a unique normalized isogeny
E\to E/K
. Alternatively, given
E^{\prime}
and
d
such that there exists a cyclic, normalized
d
-isogeny
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
, this isogeny can be computed with Stark’s algorithm
[
Sta73
]
and even more efficiently with an algorithm due to Bostan, Morain, Salvy, and Schost
[
BMSS08
]
. Suppose now one only knows there exists a not necessarily normalized isogeny between
E
and
E^{\prime}
of prime degree
\ell
, perhaps because one has found that
\Phi_{\ell}(j(E),j(E^{\prime}))=0
where
\Phi_{\ell}(X,Y)
is the
modular equation
.
Provided that
\Char(k)=0
or is sufficiently large
relative to
\ell
, an algorithm of
Elkies
[
Elk98
, Section 3]
can compute first a
curve
\widetilde{E}\cong E^{\prime}
such that a normalized

\ell
-isogeny
\phi\colon E\to\widetilde{E}
exists,
and then the kernel polynomial for
\phi
,
provided that
(j(E),j(E^{\prime}))
is a non-singular point on

\Phi_{\ell}(X,Y)=0
(see also
[
Sch95
, Section 7]
for further details and
[
Gal12
, Section 25.2.1]
for pseudocode). This algorithm is an
important subroutine in the SEA algorithm for point-counting on elliptic curves over finite fields: for an ordinary curve
E
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, one computes a kernel polynomial for primes
\ell
which split in
\End(E)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
in order to compute the trace of the Frobenius endomorphism of
E
modulo
\ell
.

Elkies’ method for computing an equation for the isogenous curve as described in
[
Sch95
]
works as follows. Suppose
E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
is an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{C}
which is
\ell
-isogenous to the curve
E^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+A^{\prime}x+B^{\prime}
. Then there exists
q_{0}=\exp(2\pi i\tau_{0})\in\mathbb{C}
such that
A=-G_{4}(q_{0})/48
and
B=G_{6}(q_{0})/864
and
j(E)=j(q_{0})
where
G_{4}
and
G_{6}
are the Eisenstein series of weights
4
and
6
respectively and
j(q)
is the
j
-function (in their
q
-expansions). Moreover, if we set
\widetilde{j}(q)\coloneqq j(q^{\ell})
, then
\widetilde{j}(q_{0})=j(E^{\prime})
and setting

\widetilde{A}=\frac{-\ell^{4}}{48}\cdot\frac{\widetilde{j}(q_{0})^{\prime 2}}{\widetilde{j}(q_{0})(\widetilde{j}(q_{0})-1728)},\quad\widetilde{B}=\frac{-\ell^{6}}{864}\cdot\frac{\widetilde{j}(q_{0})^{\prime 3}}{\widetilde{j}(q_{0})^{2}(\widetilde{j}(q_{0})-1728)}

yields coefficients for a short Weierstrass equation for
E^{\prime}
such that the
\ell
-isogeny
\phi\colon E\to\widetilde{E}
is normalized. Therefore one can compute
\widetilde{E}
as soon as one obtains
\widetilde{j}^{\prime}(q_{0})
. Letting

denote the
\ell
-th modular polynomial parametrizing
\ell
-isogenous
j
-invariant pairs, we have that
\Phi_{\ell}(j(q),\widetilde{j}(q))
is identically zero as a power series in
q
. Differentiating with respect to
q

j^{\prime}(q_{0})\Phi_{X}(j(q_{0}),\widetilde{j}(q_{0}))+\ell\widetilde{j}^{\prime}(q_{0})\Phi_{Y}(j(q_{0}),\widetilde{j}(q_{0}))=0

so,

, one can solve for
\widetilde{j}^{\prime}
and hence
\widetilde{E}
in terms of the equation for

and the

-invariant of

.

If

is a singular

, there are (at least) two
\ell
-isogenies

with distinct kernels.

Schoof writes that the approach to computing the isogenous curve
\widetilde{E}
“does not work if
\Phi_{X}(j,\widetilde{j})=\Phi_{Y}(j,\widetilde{j})=0
.” However, the broader idea – compute
\widetilde{j}^{\prime}
in order to find the model

– does. In this paper, we take up the problem of computing an

-isogeny

when

is a singular point on

.

In Section

is a singular point of multiplicity

, we can compute a degree

(which is also assumed in

[

computes

In Section

2.1.
Elliptic Curves

Let
k
be a field. An elliptic curve over
k
is a smooth projective genus
1
curve with a
k
-rational point. We will assume throughout that the characteristic of
k
is not
2
or
3
and that our elliptic curves are given by a
short Weierstrass equation over
k
, that is, an equation of the form
y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
where
x,y
are indeterminates and
A,B\in k
satisfy
4A^{3}+27B^{2}\neq 0
.
Given an elliptic curve
E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
the
invariant differential
of
E
is defined as
[
Sil09
, III.5]

\omega_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{dx}{2y}\in\Omega_{E}

where
\omega_{E}
is regarded as a differential on
E
. The
j
-invariant
of
E
is given by

j(E)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{-1728(4A)^{3}}{-16(4A^{3}+27B^{2})}=\frac{4\cdot 1728A^{3}}{4A^{3}+27B^{2}}

Two elliptic curves
E,E^{\prime}
defined over
k
are
\overline{k}
-isomorphic if and only if
j(E)=j(E^{\prime})
. Defining

E_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+3j(1728-j)x+2j(1728-j)^{2},\quad j\not=0,1728

E_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+1,\quad E_{1728}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+x

produces curves
E_{j}
such that
j(E_{j})=j
for any
j\in k
.

Given a pair of elliptic curves
E,E^{\prime}
an
isogeny

[
Sil09
, III.4]
from
E\to E^{\prime}
is a non-constant morphism
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
of projective varieties such that
\phi(\infty_{E})=\phi(\infty_{E^{\prime}})
.
Any isogeny from
E\to E^{\prime}
induces a group homomorphism
\phi\colon E\left(\overline{k}\right)\to E^{\prime}\left(\overline{k}\right)

[
Sil09
, III.4.8]
. The
degree
of an isogeny is its degree as a rational map. Every isogeny
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
has a
dual isogeny

\widehat{\phi}\colon E^{\prime}\to E
which satisfies
\widehat{\phi}\circ\phi=[\deg\phi]
, the multiplication-by-
\deg\phi
map.
Two elliptic curves
E,E^{\prime}
are
n
-isogenous
, for
n\geq 1
, provided there is an isogeny
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
of degree
n
. We say that two
j
-invariants
j_{1},j_{2}\in K
are
n
-isogenous
provided
E_{j_{1}},E_{j_{2}}
are
n
-isogenous.

An isogeny
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
between elliptic curves is separable
if it is separable as a rational map. If
\phi
is separable, then
\deg\phi=\#\ker\phi
.
We say that a separable isogeny
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
is cyclic provided its kernel is a cyclic subgroup of
E(\overline{k})
. Every isogeny
\phi
factors as
\phi=\phi^{\prime}\circ[n]\circ\pi^{n}
where
\pi
is the
p
-power Frobenius isogeny,
[n]
is the multiplication-by-
n
map, and
\phi^{\prime}
is separable and cyclic.
And every
n
-isogeny
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
can be factored as a composition of prime degree isogenies, so we will focus primarily on separable isogenies of prime degree.

2.2.
Modular polynomials, curves, and functions

Let
\ell
be a prime. The modular curve
X_{0}(\ell)
is an algebraic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
whose non-cuspidal points parametrize elliptic curves with choice of
\ell
-isogeny.
The curve
X_{0}(\ell)
is given by the
modular equation

\Phi_{\ell}(X,Y)=0
which defines a plane curve
Y_{0}(\ell)
; we have that
(j_{1},j_{2})
is a point on
Y_{0}(\ell)
if and only if there are elliptic curves
E_{j_{1}},E_{j_{2}}
and a cyclic
\ell
-isogeny
\phi\colon E_{j_{1}}\to E_{j_{2}}
. The curve
X_{0}(\ell)
is the nonsingular projective model of
Y_{0}(\ell)
. Thus if
E
is an elliptic curve and
K
is a cyclic subgroup of
E
of order
\ell
, then the point
[E,K]
on
X_{0}(\ell)
maps to the point
(j(E),j(E/K))
on
\Phi_{\ell}(x,y)=0
.
The modular polynomial
\Phi_{\ell}(X,Y)
is irreducible, symmetric, and of the form
X^{\ell}Y^{\ell}+X^{\ell+1}+Y^{\ell+1}+\ldots
where
\ldots
denotes terms of lower degree
[
Sch95
, Section 6]
.

There are some particular modular forms we are interested in for this paper, which we will regard in their
q
-expansions. The first are the weights 4 and 6 normalized (constant coefficient one) Eisenstein series
G_{4}(q)
and
G_{6}(q)
which are modular forms of weights
4
and
6
respectively. There is also the
j
-function given by

j(q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1728\frac{G_{4}(q)^{3}}{G_{4}(q)^{3}-G_{6}(q)^{2}}

The
j
-function parametrizes
j
-invariants of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{C}
; in particular for a given
z\in\mathbb{C}
there exists a
q_{0}
with
j(q_{0})=z
and the elliptic curve over
\mathbb{C}
with short Weierstrass equation

E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}-\frac{G_{4}(q_{0})}{48}+\frac{G_{6}(q_{0})}{864}

has
j
-invariant
j(q_{0})
.

2.3.
Singularities of Plane Curves

Two
j
-invariants
(j_{1},j_{2})\in k^{2}
are
\ell
-isogenous over
k
if and only if
\Phi_{\ell}(j_{1},j_{2})=0
, so the
k
-rational points of
Y_{0}(\ell)/k
parameterize pairs of
k
-rational
\ell
-isogenous
j
-invariants. While
X_{0}(\ell)/k
is smooth,
Y_{0}(\ell)/k
is not generally smooth. Before discussing what singularities
Y_{0}(\ell)/k
has let us define singularities and their associated data:

Definition 2.1
.

(Singularities of Plane Curves)

[
Ful89
, Section 3.1]

Let
F(x,y)\in k[x,y]
be a non-constant polynomial. A point
(a,b)\in k^{2}
is a
multiple point
or
singular point
of
F
provided

F(a,b)=\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}(a,b)=\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}(a,b)=0

where the derivatives are formal.
Suppose
(a,b)
is a singular point of
F
. Via change of variables define the polynomial

H(x,y)=F(x+a,y+b).

As
H(0,0)=F(a,b)=0
we can write

H(x,y)=H_{m}(x,y)+\ldots+H_{\operatorname{Deg}(H)}(x,y),

where each
H_{d}
is homogeneous of degree
d
and
H_{m}\neq 0
. The multiplicity of the singularity
(a,b)
of
F
is
m
.
For a curve
C=\mathcal{V}(F)
, a point
(a,b)
on
C
is a singular point provided
(a,b)
is a singular point of
F
and vice versa.

3.
Elkies’ method to compute the isogenous curve

In this section, we review ideas of Elkies
[
Elk98
, Section 3]
and formulas of Schoof
[
Sch95
, Section 7]
for computing, given an elliptic curve
E
and prime
\ell
, an equation for an elliptic curve
\widetilde{E}
such that there exists a normalized
\ell
-isogeny
\phi\colon E\to\widetilde{E}
.

Per
[
Sch95
, Proposition 7.1 (i)]
we have an equality of power series

\displaystyle\frac{j^{\prime}(q)}{j(q)}=-\frac{G_{6}(q)}{G_{4}(q)}

where the above functions are considered as modular forms in their
q
-expansions; this equality gives another

j^{\prime}(q)=-\frac{G_{6}(q)}{G_{4}(q)}j(q)

away from
q_{0}
for which
j(q_{0})\in\{0,\infty\}
.
Per
[
Sch95
, Proposition 7.2]
there exist formal power series
X(\zeta;q),Y(\zeta;q)\in\mathbb{Z}\Big[\Big[q,\zeta,\frac{1}{\zeta-\zeta^{2}}\Big]\Big]
for which

\displaystyle Y(\zeta;q)^{2}=X(\zeta;q)^{3}-\frac{G_{4}(q)}{48}X(\zeta;q)+\frac{G_{6}(q)}{864}

If one took the substitution

\displaystyle A(q)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{-G_{4}(q)}{48}

\displaystyle B(q)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{G_{6}(q)}{864}

Then the prior equality of power series becomes

Y(\zeta;q)^{2}=X(\zeta;q)^{3}+A(q)X(\zeta;q)+B(q)

and each fixed
q_{0}
we have a short Weierstrass equation for an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{C}
whose affine points are the pairs
\left(X(\zeta(z);q_{0}),\pm Y(\zeta(z);q_{0})\right)
where
\zeta(z)=e^{2i\pi z}
and
z
ranges over
\mathbb{C}
.
Fix a particular
q_{0}
. Then the curve
E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y^{2}=X^{3}+A(q_{0})X+B(q_{0})
is
\ell
-isogenous to the curve
\widetilde{E}\colon Y^{2}=X^{3}+A(q_{0}^{\ell})X+B(q_{0}^{\ell})
, and if one replaces
A(q_{0}^{\ell}),B(q_{0}^{\ell})
with
\ell^{4}A(q_{0}^{\ell})
and
\ell^{6}B(q_{0}^{\ell})
then the isogeny connecting the two curves is normalized
[
Elk98
]
. Using the identities presented in
[
Sch95
, Proposition 7.1]
one can compute formulas for
G_{4}(q_{0}),G_{6}(q_{0})
in terms of
j(q_{0})
and
j^{\prime}(q_{0})
, similarly one can compute formulas for
G_{4}(q_{0}^{\ell})
and
G_{6}(q_{0}^{\ell})
in terms of
\widetilde{j}(q_{0})
and
\widetilde{j}^{\prime}(q_{0})
. Therefore if one can compute
\widetilde{j}^{\prime}(q_{0})
one can obtain a model for
\widetilde{E}
which admits an isogeny from
E
, and by scaling this model appropriately compute a normalized model for this isogeny.

Fix a prime
\ell
and the function
\widetilde{j}(q)=j(q^{\ell})
. For every
q_{0}
we have that
j(q_{0}),\widetilde{j}(q_{0})
are
\ell
-isogenous
j
-invariants so as a function of
q
we have
\Phi_{\ell}(j,\widetilde{j})=0
. Moreover if we take the derivative of the function
0=\Phi_{\ell}(j,\widetilde{j})
by applying the multivariate chain rule we get

0=\left(\Phi_{\ell}(j,\widetilde{j})\right)^{\prime}=j^{\prime}\Phi_{X}(j,\widetilde{j})+\ell\widetilde{j}^{\prime}\Phi_{Y}(j,\widetilde{j}).

Solving for
\widetilde{j}^{\prime}
, assuming
\Phi_{y}(j,\widetilde{j})\neq 0
, and then setting

(1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}=\frac{-\ell^{4}}{48}\cdot\frac{\widetilde{j}^{\prime 2}}{\widetilde{j}(\widetilde{j}-1728)}

(2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{B}=\frac{-\ell^{6}}{864}\cdot\frac{\widetilde{j}^{\prime 3}}{\widetilde{j}^{2}(\widetilde{j}-1728)}

yields an equation
Y^{2}=X^{3}+\widetilde{A}X+\widetilde{B}
for the isogenous curve admitting a normalized isogeny from
E
whose points are the pairs
\left(X(\zeta(z);q_{0}^{\ell}),\pm Y(\zeta(z);q_{0}^{\ell})\right)
as
z
ranges over
\mathbb{C}
.

Even though all the above is derived from properties of modular functions over
\mathbb{C}
, the Deuring lifting theorem justifies that these computations can be performed over a finite field as well, provided the characteristic is not
2,3
, or
\ell
.

4.
Singular points on
Y_{0}(\ell)

In this section, we observe that the multiplicity of a point
(j_{1},j_{2})
on
Y_{0}(\ell)
is equal to the number of cyclic
\ell
-isogenies from an elliptic curve with
j
-invariant
j_{1}
to an elliptic curve with
j
-invariant
j_{2}
. We use this in the following section in order to compute normalized models for
\ell
-isogenies at singular points of
Y_{0}(\ell)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
j_{1},j_{2}
are two elements of
k
, neither of which are
0
or
1728
. Let
(j_{1},j_{2})
be a point on
Y_{0}(\ell)
and let
m
be the number of
\ell
-isogenies with distinct kernels between two fixed elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
with
j
-invariants
j_{1}
and
j_{2}
. If
\Char(k)=0
or
\Char(k)>m
, then
(j_{1},j_{2})
is a point of multiplicity
m
on
Y_{0}(\ell)
. In particular, all partial derivatives of
\Phi_{\ell}(X,Y)
of weight less than
m
vanish at
(j_{1},j_{2})
. Moreover, both
\Phi_{X^{m}}(j_{1},j_{2}))
and
\Phi_{Y^{m}}(j_{1},j_{2})
are nonzero.

Proof.

We can identify
X_{0}(\ell)
as the blowup of
Y_{0}(\ell)
at its singular points. The non-cuspidal points on
X_{0}(\ell)
then correspond to tangent directions at points
(j_{1},j_{2})
of
Y_{0}(\ell)
. Suppose there are
m
distinct points on
X_{0}(\ell)
above
(j_{1},j_{2})
. Then the multiplicity of
(j_{1},j_{2})
on
Y_{0}(\ell)
is at least the number of tangent directions at
P
and therefore at least
m
. On the other hand, the number of
\ell
-isogenies between
j_{1}
and
j_{2}
is equal to the multiplicity of
j_{2}
as a root of the degree
\ell+1
polynomial
\phi(Y)\coloneqq\Phi(j_{1},Y)
. If
m<\ell+1
, the assumption on the characteristic of
\Char(k)
implies
\phi^{(m)}(j_{2})\not=0
and therefore the multiplicity is at most
m
. If
m=\ell+1
then
(j_{1},j_{2})
is a point of multiplicity
m
since its multiplicity is at most
\ell+1
.
∎

In the following section, we will need to compute
\widetilde{j}^{\prime}
at singular points by finding it as a root of a polynomial obtained by differentiating the modular equation
m+1
times, where
m
is the multiplicity of the singularity. A straightforward argument via induction gives the following expression for the
n
th derivative of the modular equation:

Proposition 4.2
.

Fix the functions
j(q)
and
\widetilde{j}(q)\coloneqq j(q^{\ell})
in their
q
-expansions as well as the
\ell
th modular polynomial
\Phi=\Phi_{\ell}(X,Y)
.
Then for all integers
n\geq 2
the
n
-fold derivative of
\Phi(j,\widetilde{j})
is given by

\left(\Phi(j,\widetilde{j})\right)^{(n)}=\varepsilon_{n}(q)+F_{n}(q)

where
\varepsilon_{n}(q)
is of the form

\varepsilon_{n}(q)=\sum_{0\leq u+v<n}\varepsilon_{u,v}(q)\Phi_{X^{u}Y^{v}}(j,\widetilde{j})

and
F_{n}(q)
is of the form

F_{n}(q)=\sum_{u=0}^{n}\binom{n}{u}\ell^{n-u}(j^{\prime})^{u}(\widetilde{j}^{\prime})^{n-u}\Phi_{X^{u}Y^{n-u}}(j,\widetilde{j}).

5.
Computing Reductions of
\widetilde{j}^{\prime}

Throughout this section, fix a field
k
, fix
j_{1},j_{2}\in k
with
j_{1},j_{2}\not=0,1728
, let
E_{A,B}
be a curve of
j
-invariant
j_{1}
, and define
j_{1}^{\prime}=\frac{18B}{A}j_{1}
.

Definition 5.1
.

Suppose
P=(j_{1},j_{2})
is a singular point of multiplicity
m
on
Y_{0}(\ell)
with
j_{1},j_{2}\not=0,1728

Assume now that either

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
E,E^{\prime}
be elliptic curves over a field
k
and whose
j
-invariants are not
0
or
1728
. Suppose there exist two normalized, separable
\ell
-isogenies
\phi,\psi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
with distinct kernels. Then
0<\Char(k)<4\ell
.

Proof.

The existence of two distinct subgroups of order
\ell
in
E
implies that
E[\ell]\cong(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{2}
and in particular
\Char(k)\not=\ell
. Consider the (necessarily separable) endomorphism
\alpha=\widehat{\psi}\circ\phi\in\End(E)
. Since
\ker\phi\not=\ker\psi
, the endomorphism
\alpha
is cyclic of degree
\ell^{2}
and therefore
\alpha\not=\pm\ell
. Since both
\phi
and
\psi
are normalized, we have that
\alpha^{*}\omega=\ell\omega
for any invariant differential of
E
. Therefore the endomorphism
\alpha-\ell
is inseparable, so
p=\Char(k)>0
and the degree of
\alpha-\ell
is divisible by
p
. Let
t(\alpha)=\alpha+\widehat{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}
denote the trace of
\alpha
. The degree of
\alpha-\ell
is

\deg(\alpha-\ell)=2\ell^{2}-\ell t(\alpha)\equiv 0\pmod{p},

Since
\alpha
is separable, the prime
p
does not divide
\ell
, so
t(\alpha)-2\ell\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. On the other hand, we have that the discriminant
t(\alpha)^{2}-4\ell^{2}
is negative, so
-2\ell<t(\alpha)<2\ell
. This implies

-4\ell<t(\alpha)-2\ell<0

so
p<4\ell
, as claimed.
∎

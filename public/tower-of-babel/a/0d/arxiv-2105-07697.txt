Abstract
. We study the geometry of exceptional loci of birational contractions of hyper-Kähler fourfolds that are of K3 [2] -type. These loci are conic bundles over K3 surfaces and we determine their classes in the Brauer group. For this we use the results on twisted sheaves on K3 surfaces, on contractions and on the corresponding Heegner divisors. For a general K3 surface of fixed degree there are three (T-equivalence) classes of order two Brauer group elements. The elements in exactly two of these classes are represented by conic bundles on such fourfolds. We also discuss various examples of such conic bundles.



Introduction

A hyper-Kähler variety is a simply-connected smooth projective variety admitting a unique
(up to scalar) non-degenerate holomorphic two-form.
A hyper-Kähler surface is a K3 surface and the divisorial contractions are well-known in this case.
If
E\subset S
is a prime divisor on a K3 surface
S
with
E^{2}<0
,
then
E
is a smooth rational curve and
E^{2}=-2
. Moreover,
E
can be contracted to an ODP point
and any divisorial contraction with prime exceptional divisor is obtained in this way.
Our aim is to discuss an analogous statement for projective hyper-Kähler fourfolds
X

of K3
[2]
type. These fourfolds are obtained by deforming the blow-up along the diagonal
of the symmetric square of a K3 surface. The Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki
quadratic form on the second cohomology group of
X
is denoted by
q
.

Let
X
be a projective fourfold of K3
[2]
type (appropriately general)
containing a prime divisor
E\subset X
with
q(E)<0
. Then there is
a primitive big and nef divisor (class)
H
on
X

such that the map induced by
H
contracts
E
to a K3 surface
K
(possibly after some flops).

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\phi_{H}:\,X&\longrightarrow&Y\\
\cup&&\cup\\
E&\longrightarrow&K~.\end{array}

The description of the nef cone of
X
, due to Hassett-Tschinkel
[
HT2
]
, Markman
[
Ma3
]
, Bayer–Macrì
[
BM
]
, Bayer–Hassett–Tschinkel
[
BHT
]
,
and Mongardi
[
Mo
]
, implies that
there are then two possibilities for
q(E)
,
it is either
-2
or
-8
; the first
case are the BN (Brill-Noether) contractions whereas the latter case are the
HC (Hilbert-Chow) contractions. In both cases the contraction
induces a map
p:E\rightarrow K
which
is a
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration over a K3 surface
K
.
In the HC case this
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration is the projectivisation
of the tangent bundle of
K
,
E={\bf P}({\mathcal{T}}_{K})\rightarrow K
.

In the BN case, the
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration
E\rightarrow K

can have a non-trivial Brauer class in
\Br(K)_{2}
,
the two-torsion subgroup of the Brauer group of
K
.
Then
E\not\cong{\bf P}({\mathcal{V}})
for a rank two vector bundle
{\mathcal{V}}
on
K
,
equivalently, there is no rational section of the map

E\rightarrow K
.
Using the work of Debarre and Macrì
[
DM
]
, which is based on a study of the sublattice
of
H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
generated by
H
and
E
,
we find that there are four types of BN contractions.
These correspond to irreducible components of certain
Heegner divisors in period spaces of polarized hyper-Kähler fourfolds of K3
[2]
type.
In two cases one has
E={\bf P}({\mathcal{V}})
where
{\mathcal{V}}
is the Mukai bundle, a rigid stable vector bundle
of rank two on
K
, see Corollary
4.3
.
These two cases differ only in the degree of the polarization on
K
.
In the other two cases the fibrations
E\rightarrow K
have a non-trivial Brauer class.

To determine the Brauer classes, we first show that
X
is a moduli
space of
\beta
-twisted sheaves on some K3 surface
T
for some Brauer class
\beta\in\Br(T)_{2}
.
From the results of Bayer and Macrì in
[
BM
]

we then deduce that
T=K
and that
\beta
is also
the Brauer class defined by the
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration
E\rightarrow K
, see Theorem
4.2
.

Given a polarized K3 surface
(K,h)
with
\Pic(K)={\bf Z}h
and
h^{2}=2d
,
not all two-torsion elements in
\Br(K)
are obtained as the class of
a
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration
E\rightarrow K
for a BN exceptional divisor
E
.
We classify the non-trivial Brauer classes
\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}
as follows (see Theorem
2.3
).
For any B-field
B\in H^{2}(K,{\bf Q})
which represents
\alpha
,
the intersection number (modulo the integers)
Bh\in\{0,1/2\}
is an invariant of
\alpha
.
In case
4Bh+h^{2}\equiv 0\mod 4
there is a further invariant of
\alpha

which is
B^{2}\in\{0,1/2\}
, again modulo the integers.
In case
4Bh+h^{2}\equiv 2\mod 4
, the value of
B^{2}
is not an invariant of
\alpha

but it is convenient to choose
B
such that
B^{2}=1/2
.
There are thus three types of order two Brauer classes on a generic polarized K3 surface.
The type corresponds to the isometry class
of the index two sublattice of the transcendental lattice of
K
determined by the
Brauer class.
This refines the classification given in
[
vG
]
.

The non-trivial Brauer classes determined by BN exceptional divisors are those with

B^{2}=1/2
.
Using the notation for Heegner divisors which is
explained in Section
3
, Theorem
4.2
and Corollary
4.3
imply:

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
(K,h)
be a polarized K3 surface with
\Pic(K)={\bf Z}h
and
h^{2}=2d

and let
\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}
be a non-trivial two-torsion Brauer class.

Then
\alpha
has a B-field representative with
B^{2}=1/2

if and only if
there is a conic bundle
E\rightarrow K
with Brauer class
\alpha

which is the contraction of an exceptional divisor on a hyper-Kähler fourfold

X
of K3
[2]
type induced by a big and nef divisor class
H
.

The period point of
(X,H)
lies in
{\mathcal{D}}^{(1)}_{2d,8d,\alpha}
if

Bh=1/2
and in
{\mathcal{D}}^{(1)}_{8d,8d,\beta}
if
Bh=0
.

It is easy to show, see Proposition
4.1
,
that the exceptional divisor
E
of a BN contraction has canonical divisor
K_{E}

with self-intersection number
K_{E}^{3}=12
.
Any
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration
p:E\rightarrow K
over a K3 surface
K

is isomorphic to
E={\bf P}({\mathcal{U}})
for an
\alpha
-twisted locally free rank two sheaf
{\mathcal{U}}
over
K
,
where
\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}
is the Brauer class determined by
E
.
Using basic properties of twisted sheaves, we found that there exists a non-trivial conic bundle

p:E\rightarrow K
with

K_{E}^{3}=12
only in case
E
determines a Brauer class
\alpha
having a B-field representative
with
B^{2}=1/2
.

So the basic properties of twisted sheaves already imply that at most two of the three
types of Brauer classes are obtained from BN exceptional divisors on hyper-Kähler fourfolds of

K3^{[2]}
type.

A brief outline of the paper is as follows. Basic results on
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibrations
are recalled in Section
1
.
There we also discuss examples and methods to compute Chern classes associated to such
fibrations.
In Section
2
we give the classification of Brauer classes and the basic
properties of twisted sheaves.
The next section introduces the Heegner divisors and describes the hyper-Kähler fourfolds
admitting contractions. These descriptions are used in Section
4
to prove
the main result Theorem
0.1
. We also discuss relations between the second
cohomology groups of
X
,
E
and
K
. In Section
5

we study examples of conic bundles over K3 surfaces in hyper-Kähler fourfolds and their relations to
classical constructions. We characterise in this way the Brauer classes of known conic bundles.
In the last section we attempt a description of certain conic bundles over quartic K3 surfaces
but we obtain only partial results.
By brute force computations we find
that the Hilbert scheme of two points on the Fermat quartic surface defines
an interesting EPW sextic
Y\subset{\bf P}^{5}
. In fact the
singular locus of
Y
, which is a smooth surface in general,
is now itself singular at
60
points.

1.
Conic bundles

1.1.
The invariants of a conic bundle

Let

p:\,E\,\longrightarrow\,K

be a conic bundle over a K3 surface
K
. We assume that
E
is everywhere non-degenerate,
so all fibres are smooth rational curves. We will also refer to
E

as a
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration over
K
.
We compute the basic invariants associated to
E
,
following
[
Sa
]
,
[
CI
]
.

The relative dualizing sheaf
\omega_{E/K}
, a line bundle on
E
,
is defined by the exact sequence

0\,\longrightarrow\,p^{*}\Omega^{1}_{K}\,\longrightarrow\,\Omega^{1}_{E}\,\longrightarrow\,\omega_{E/K}\,\longrightarrow\,0~.\qquad(\ast)

Taking determinants we find, since
\omega_{K}={\mathcal{O}}_{K}
, that

\omega_{E}\,\cong\,p^{*}\omega_{K}\otimes\omega_{E/K}\,\cong\,\omega_{E/K}~,

so the relative dualizing sheaf is just the canonical bundle.

The Chern classes of
E
can be computed from the exact sequence above:

c_{1}(E)\,=\,-K_{E},\quad c_{2}(E)\,=\,p^{*}c_{2}(K)\,=24f,\quad c_{3}(E)\,=\,-c_{3}(\Omega^{1}_{E})=-p^{*}c_{2}(K)\omega_{E}\,=\,48~,

where
f
be the class of a fiber of
p
and thus
\omega_{E}f=\omega_{E/K}f=-2
where we used
that
(\omega_{E/K})_{|f}=\omega_{f}
(
[
HAG
, Prop. II.8.10]
).

Proposition 1.1
.

For a conic bundle
p:E\rightarrow K
as above let
{\mathcal{W}}:=p_{*}\omega_{E/K}^{-1}
.
Then
{\mathcal{W}}
is a locally free sheaf of rank three on the K3 surface
K
and

c_{1}({\mathcal{W}})\,=\,0,\qquad K_{E}^{2}\,=\,-c_{2}({\mathcal{W}})f,\qquad K^{3}_{E}\,=\,2c_{2}({\mathcal{W}})~.

Proof.
We apply Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch (GRR) to the sheaves
\omega_{E/K}^{\pm 1}
and the map

p:E\rightarrow K
.

As
(\omega_{E/K})_{|f}=\omega_{f}
which has degree
-2
on
f\cong{\bf P}^{1}
,
we have
R^{0}p_{*}\omega_{E/K}=0
and
R^{1}p_{*}\omega_{E/K}
is a line bundle on

K
. Using
[
HM
, Exc. 3.12, p.85]
we find that
R^{1}p_{*}\omega_{E/K}={\mathcal{O}}_{K}
. Thus

p_{!}\omega_{E/K}=-[{\mathcal{O}}_{K}]
and
\mbox{ch}(p_{!}\omega_{E/K})=-1_{K}
.
Since
\mbox{td}({\mathcal{T}}_{K})=1+2P
, where
P\in K
is a point, and
GRR states that
\mbox{ch}(p_{!}\omega_{E/K})\mbox{td}({\mathcal{T}}_{K})=p_{*}(\mbox{ch}(\omega_{E/K})\mbox{td}({\mathcal{T}}_{E}))
, we find

-(1+2P)\,=\,p_{*}(\mbox{ch}(\omega_{E/K})\mbox{td}({\mathcal{T}}_{E}))~.

Using that
\omega_{E/K}=\omega_{E}
is a line bundle with
c_{1}(\omega_{E/K})=K_{E}

and the results from above we find

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mbox{ch}(\omega_{E/K})&=&1+K_{E}+\frac{1}{2}K_{E}^{2}+\frac{1}{6}K_{E}^{3}~,\\
\mbox{td}({\mathcal{T}}_{E})&=&1\,-\,\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}K_{E}\,+\,\mbox{$\frac{1}{12}$}(K_{E}^{2}+24f)\,-\,fK_{E}~.\end{array}

Hence GRR gives

-1\,-\,2P\,=\,p_{*}(1\,+\,\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}K_{E}\,+\,\mbox{$\frac{1}{12}$}(K_{E}^{2}+24f)\,+\,fK_{E})~.

Since
\dim E=3>\dim K=2
we get
p_{*}1=p_{*}1_{E}=0
. Similarly
p_{*}f=0

since the one dimensional fiber is contracted to a point in
K
. Next
p_{*}K_{E}=(-2)\cdot 1_{K}X

since
K_{E}f=\deg(K_{f})=-2
.
Thus the degree zero terms agree. In degree one we must have

0=p_{*}(\mbox{$\frac{1}{12}$}K_{E}^{2})
, hence

K_{E}^{2}\,=\,c_{E}f

for some integer
c_{E}
.

Next we apply GRR to
\omega_{E/K}^{-1}
.
From
(\omega_{E/K})_{|f}=\omega_{f}
we also get
(\omega_{E/K}^{-1})_{|f}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{1}}(2)
,
in particular
h^{0}(f,(\omega_{E/K}^{-1})_{|f})=3
for all fibers of
p
so

{\mathcal{W}}=p_{*}\omega_{E/K}^{-1}
is locally
free of rank three on
K
.
Moreover,

H^{1}(f,(\omega_{E/K}^{-1})_{|f})=0
for all fibers of
p
and thus
R^{i}p_{*}(\omega_{E/K}^{-1})=0

for
i>0
. This implies that
p_{!}\omega_{E/K}^{-1}=p_{*}\omega_{E/K}^{-1}
and since

\omega_{E/K}={\mathcal{O}}_{E}(-K_{E})
, GRR gives

\mbox{ch}({\mathcal{W}})\mbox{td}({\mathcal{T}}_{K})\,=\,p_{*}(\mbox{ch}({\mathcal{O}}_{E}(-K_{E}))\mbox{td}({\mathcal{T}}_{E}))
.
We find:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mbox{ch}({\mathcal{W}})&=&3+c_{1}({\mathcal{W}})+\frac{1}{2}(c_{1}({\mathcal{W}})^{2}-c_{2}({\mathcal{W}}))~,\\
\mbox{td}({\mathcal{T}}_{K})&=&1+\frac{1}{12}c_{2}(K)\;=\;1+2P~,\\
\mbox{ch}({\mathcal{O}}(-K_{E}))&=&1-K_{E}+\frac{1}{2}K_{E}^{2}-\frac{1}{6}K_{E}^{3}~,\\
\mbox{td}({\mathcal{T}}_{E})&=&1-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}K_{E}+\mbox{$\frac{1}{12}$}(K_{E}^{2}+24f)-fK_{E}~.\end{array}

The left hand side of GRR is thus:

\mbox{ch}({\mathcal{W}})\mbox{td}({\mathcal{T}}_{K})\,=\,3+c_{1}({\mathcal{W}})+(6P+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}(c_{1}({\mathcal{W}})^{2}-c_{2}({\mathcal{W}})))~,

whereas the right hand side is:

p_{*}(\mbox{ch}({\mathcal{O}}_{E}(-K_{E}))\mbox{td}({\mathcal{T}}_{E}))\,=\,p_{*}\left(1\,-\,\mbox{$\frac{3}{2}$}K_{E}\,+\,(\mbox{$\frac{13}{12}$}K_{E}^{2}+2f)\,-\,(\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}K_{E}^{3}\,+\,3fK_{E})\right)~.

We already found that
K_{E}^{2}=c_{E}f
for some constant
c_{E}
, hence
p_{*}K^{2}_{E}=p_{*}f=0
.
Thus the degree one term on the right hand side is zero and therefore also
c_{1}({\mathcal{W}})=0
.
Finally we have
K_{E}^{3}=c_{E}fK_{E}=-2c_{E}
and, identifying a zero cycle with its degree,

p_{*}fK_{E}=fK_{E}=-2
,
so the last terms on the right hand side are

p_{*}(\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}K_{E}^{3}\,+\,3fK_{E})\,=\,-c_{E}\,-\,6~.

Comparing with the LHS and using
c_{1}({\mathcal{W}})=0
we get:

6\,-\,c_{2}({\mathcal{W}})\,=\,c_{E}\,+\,6~,\qquad\mbox{hence}\quad c_{2}({\mathcal{W}})\,=\,-c_{E}~.

Substituting this in
K_{E}^{2}=c_{E}f
and
K_{E}^{3}=-2c_{E}
one finds the last two equalities.

\Box

1.2.
The Brauer class of a conic bundle

Let
E\to K
be a
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration over a K3 surface. Since
\Aut({\bf P}^{1})=PGL_{2}({\bf C})
,
the glueing data for
E
provides a Cech cocycle which defines
a class
[E]\in H^{1}(K,PGL_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K}))
.
From the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\ast}\longrightarrow GL_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})\longrightarrow PGL_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})\longrightarrow 0

we obtain a coboundary map
H^{1}(PGL_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K}))\to H^{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\ast})
.
The image of
[E]
through this map is a two-torsion element that we denote by

\alpha_{E}\in H^{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\ast})_{tors}=\Br(K)
, the Brauer group of
K
.
We denote by
\Br(K)_{2}
the subgroup of two-torsion elements in
\Br(K)
.

In case
\alpha_{E}=0
, the
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration
E
is obtained from a class
in
H^{1}(K,GL_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K}))
. Such a class defines a rank two vector bundle
{\mathcal{V}}

and
E\cong{\bf P}({\mathcal{V}})
is then called a trivial
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration or a trivial conic bundle.

Since
E_{K}\cdot f=-2
, one has
h^{0}(E,nK_{E})=0
for all
n>0
. We now consider
h^{0}(E,-K_{E})
.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
p:E\rightarrow K
be a non-trivial
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration over a K3 surface

K
with
\rk\,\Pic(K)=1
. Then
h^{0}(E,-K_{E})=0
.

Proof.
Assume that
s\in H^{0}(-K_{E})
,
s\neq 0
, and let
D\in|-K_{E}|
be the
effective divisor on
E
defined by
s
. Then there is the exact sequence:

0\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(K_{E})\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{O}}_{D}\,\longrightarrow\,0~.

Recall that
h^{0}(K_{E})=0
, moreover

h^{1}(K_{E})=h^{3,1}(E)=h^{2,0}(E)=1
and
h^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{E})=h^{0,1}(E)=0
. Hence
h^{0}(D,{\mathcal{O}}_{D})=2
.

Since
Df=+2
, at least one (and at most two) of the irreducible components of
D
map onto
K
.
If there are two such components or if the unique such component is not reduced,
then
p
has a rational section and hence
E={\bf P}({\mathcal{V}})
for a vector bundle
{\mathcal{V}}
on
K

(cf.
[
Sh
, Lemma 3.5]
) which contradicts the non-triviality
E
.

So we now assume that
D
has a unique reduced, irreducible,
‘horizontal’ component
D_{h}
mapping onto
K
.
The support of any other component of
D
would be the inverse image of a curve in
K
.
Since
\rk\,\Pic(K)=1
the divisor
D
is then linearly equivalent to
D^{\prime}:=D_{h}+p^{*}C
where

C
is a (reduced, irreducible) curve in
K
.
But then
D^{\prime}\in|-K_{E}|
is connected and reduced, contradicting
h^{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{D^{\prime}})=2
.
We conclude that
h^{0}(E,-K_{E})=0
.
∎

1.4.
Chern classes for conic bundles on a double plane

Let
\pi:K\rightarrow{\bf P}^{2}
be a double cover branched along a smooth sextic curve
C

defined by
f_{6}=0
. There are three types of non-trivial conic
bundles on
K
, they are associated to a point of order two, an odd or an even theta characteristic
on
C
(see
[
vG
]
,
[
IOOV
]
and Theorem
2.3
).

We recall well-known conic bundles
E\rightarrow K
which have Brauer classes
of each of these types and we (re)compute the selfintersection
K_{E}^{3}
of their canonical bundle
in two cases, see Proposition
1.4
, Corollary
1.6
respectively.
We will discuss some implications of these results at the end of §
2.4
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
\pi:K\rightarrow{\bf P}^{2}
be a general K3 surface of degree two with branch curve
C
.
A point of order two in
\Pic(C)
defines a conic bundle
p:E\rightarrow K

with
K_{E}^{3}=12
and
c_{2}(p_{*}\omega^{-1}_{E/K})=6
.

Proof.
In view of Proposition
1.1
, it suffices to show that
c_{2}(p_{*}\omega^{-1}_{E/K})=6
.
Let
L\in\Pic(C)
be of order two, so
L^{\vee}=L
, where
L^{\vee}
is the dual line bundle.
Let
j:C\hookrightarrow{\bf P}^{2}
be the inclusion map.
There is a resolution (
[
Ca
]
, the case
e=3
in

[
B
, Proposition 4.6]
)

0\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(-4)^{3}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(-2)^{3}\,\longrightarrow\,j_{*}L\,\longrightarrow\,0

with a symmetric matrix
M
. The coefficients
M_{ij}
of the
3\times 3
-matrix
M
are
elements of
\mbox{Hom}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(-4),{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(-2))=H^{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(2))
.
The determinant of
M
is an equation of the sextic curve
C
.

We will use
[
DK
, Section 2]
to define a conic bundle
E\rightarrow K
as a subvariety of

{\bf P}(p_{*}\omega_{E/K}^{-1})
.
Twisting the sequence by
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(3)
and pulling it back along the finite
(hence affine and flat) map
\pi
to the K3 surface
K

we get the exact sequence, with
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}(d):=\pi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(d)
:

0\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{E}}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{*}M}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}\,\longrightarrow\,\pi^{*}j_{*}L(3)\,\longrightarrow\,0,\qquad{\mathcal{E}}\,:=\,{\mathcal{O}}_{K}(-1)^{3}~.

The determinant of this morphism of vector bundles is
\pi^{*}\det(M)\in H^{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{K}(6))

which has divisor
D=\pi^{*}C=2C_{K}
, where
i:C\hookrightarrow C_{K}\subset K
is the
ramification curve, which is isomorphic to
C
.

The kernel
{\mathcal{K}}
of
\pi^{*}M
is the sheaf on
D
defined as

{\mathcal{K}}\,:=\;\ker(\pi^{*}M_{|D}\,:\,{\mathcal{E}}_{|D}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{E}}_{|D}^{\vee})~,\qquad D=2C_{K}=\pi^{*}C~.

We need to know the restriction of
{\mathcal{K}}
to the curve
C_{K}
. Since

\pi:C_{K}\rightarrow C
is an isomorphism
we have an exact sequence:

0\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{K}}_{|C_{K}}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{E}}_{|C_{K}}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M_{|C_{K}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}_{|C_{K}}\,\longrightarrow\,L(3)\,\longrightarrow\,0~,

where now
L
is viewed as a line bundle on
C_{K}
.
Since
M_{|C_{K}}
is symmetric, after dualizing the surjection of vector bundles on
C
above,
we get
L(-3)\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}_{|C}\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}_{|C}
and thus
{\mathcal{K}}_{|C_{K}}=L(-3)
.

Define a vector bundle
\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
on
K

as the dual of the kernel of the surjection

\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}^{\vee}\,:=\,\ker({\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{E}}_{X|C_{K}}^{\vee}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{K}}_{|C_{K}}^{\vee}=i_{*}L(3)\,\longrightarrow\,0)~,

so that we have the exact sequence (see
[
DK
, (7)]
)

0\,\longrightarrow\,\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}^{\vee}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}\,\longrightarrow\,i_{*}L(3)\,\longrightarrow\,0~.

The quadratic form
\pi^{*}M
on
{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}

defines a nowhere degenerate quadratic form on
\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
.
The conic bundle
E\rightarrow K
is defined by this quadratic form on
\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
.

By Proposition
1.3
we have that
p_{*}\omega_{E/K}^{-1}=\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
.
To compute the Chern classes of
\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
we
recall that, with
h:=c_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{K}(1))
, the total Chern class of
{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}
is

c({\mathcal{E}}^{\vee})=(1+h)^{3}=1+3h+3h^{2}\quad\mbox{so}\quad c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}^{\vee})\,=\,3h,\quad c_{2}({\mathcal{E}}^{\vee})=3h^{2}=6~.

Computing the Chern classes of
i_{*}L(3)
with
[
F
, §2.1, Lemma 1]
we get:

c_{1}({{\mathcal{E}}}^{\vee})=c_{1}(\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}^{\vee})+[C_{K}],\qquad\mbox{hence}\quad c_{1}(\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}^{\vee})\,=\,c_{1}({{\mathcal{E}}}^{\vee})-[C_{K}]\,=\,0~,

as expected since
\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
should be self-dual, and

c_{2}({\mathcal{E}}^{\vee})=c_{2}(\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}^{\vee})+c_{1}(\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}^{\vee})[C_{K}]+([C_{K}]^{2}-i_{*}c_{1}(L(3))),

as
c_{1}(\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}^{\vee})=0
and
[C_{K}]^{2}-i_{*}c_{1}(L(3))=(3h)^{2}-3h\cdot 3h=0
, we find

c_{2}(\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}})=c_{2}(\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}}^{\vee})=6
. Using
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=c_{2}({\mathcal{E}}^{\vee})
we are done.

\Box

1.5.
Computing Chern classes of Azumaya algebras

The following proposition allows us to determine the Chern classes of
some Azumaya algebras on a K3 double plane. The idea is based on
[
IK
]
and
[
Ku1
]
,
but Proposition
1.5
simplifies the computations.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
\pi:K\rightarrow{\bf P}^{2}
be a K3 double cover of
{\bf P}^{2}

and let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a coherent sheaf on
K
of rank
r
with
c_{1}({\mathcal{A}})=0
.
Then we have
c_{1}(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{A}})\,=\,-3rH
, with
H:=c_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(1))
, and:

c_{2}({\mathcal{A}})\,=\,-\mbox{$\frac{9}{2}$}r(r-1)\,+\,c_{2}(\pi_{*}({\mathcal{A}}))~.

Proof.
Recall that the GRR for a map
\pi:T\rightarrow S
and a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{A}}
of rank
r

on
T
states:

{\rm ch(\pi_{!}{\mathcal{A}})\mbox{td}({\mathcal{T}}_{S}})\,=\,\pi_{*}(\rm ch({\mathcal{A}})\mbox{td}({\mathcal{T}}_{T}))~.

In case
T
is a K3 surface (so
K_{T}=-c_{1}({\mathcal{T}})=0
and
c_{2}({\mathcal{T}})=24P
, with
P
a point in
T
)
and
S={\bf P}^{2}
(so
K_{S}=-3H
and
c_{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{S})=3H^{2}
).
For a double cover
\pi:T\rightarrow S

we have
f_{!}{\mathcal{A}}=f_{*}{\mathcal{A}}
since
\pi
is affine and thus
R^{i}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{A}}=0
for
i>0
.
The left hand side of GRR is then

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\mbox{ch}(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{A}})\mbox{td}({\mathcal{T}}_{{\bf P}^{2}})\\
&=&2r\,+\,(3rH\,+\,c_{1}(\pi_{*}({\mathcal{A}})))\,+\,(2r\,+\,\mbox{$\frac{3}{2}$}c_{1}(\pi_{*}({\mathcal{A}}))H\,+\,\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}c_{1}(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{A}})^{2}\,-\,c_{2}(\pi_{*}({\mathcal{A}})))~.\end{array}

The right hand side is

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\pi_{*}(\mbox{ch}({\mathcal{A}})\mbox{td}({\mathcal{T}}_{T}))&=&2r\,+\,\pi_{*}c_{1}({\mathcal{A}})\,+\,(2r\,+\,\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\pi_{*}(c_{1}({\mathcal{A}}))^{2}\,-\,c_{2}({\mathcal{A}}))~,\end{array}

where we view the codimension two cycles as integers.

Since
c_{1}({\mathcal{A}})=0
one finds

c_{1}(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{A}})\,=\,-3rH
and

c_{2}({\mathcal{A}})\,=\,-\left(\mbox{$\frac{3}{2}$}c_{1}(\pi_{*}({\mathcal{A}}))H\,+\,\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}c_{1}(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{A}})^{2}\,-\,c_{2}(\pi_{*}({\mathcal{A}}))\right)~.

Substituting for
c_{1}(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{A}})
and using
H^{2}=1
, one finds the formula for
c_{2}({\mathcal{A}})
.

\Box

The following corollary computes
K_{E}^{3}
for the conic bundle over the double plane
K

defined by an odd theta characteristic on the branch curve. These conic bundles are also
obtained from cubic fourfolds with a plane, see §
5.2
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
\pi:K\rightarrow{\bf P}^{2}
be a general K3 surface of degree two with branch curve
C
.
An odd theta characteristic on
C
defines a conic bundle
p:E\rightarrow K
with

K_{E}^{3}=12
and
c_{2}(p_{*}\omega^{-1}_{E/K})=6
.

Proof.
Let
L
be an odd theta characteristic on
C
, so
L\otimes L\cong\omega_{C}
and
h^{0}(L)
is odd.
Since
K
is general,
h^{0}(L)=1
and
j_{*}L
, where
j:C\hookrightarrow{\bf P}^{2}

has a resolution (see
[
B
, Prop. 4.2(b)]
)

0\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(-2)^{3}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(-3)\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(-1)^{3}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,j_{*}L\,\longrightarrow\,0~,

where
M
is a symmetric matrix with
\det(M)=f_{6}
, a degree six polynomial defining
C
.
Thus
M
also gives a quadratic form

M:\,{\mathcal{E}}:=\,{\mathcal{O}}^{3}\,\oplus\,{\mathcal{O}}(-1)\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{O}}(1),\qquad M\,=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{11}&a_{21}&a_{31}&b_{1}\\
a_{21}&a_{22}&a_{23}&b_{2}\\
a_{31}&a_{32}&a_{33}&b_{3}\\
b_{1}&b_{2}&b_{3}&c\end{array}\right)~,

with
a_{ij},b_{k},c
global sections of
{\mathcal{O}}(1)
,
{\mathcal{O}}(2)
and
{\mathcal{O}}(3)
respectively.
The rulings of the quadric surface defined by
M_{x}
are the fibers of the conic bundle
E

over the points in
\pi^{-1}(x)
for
x\in{\bf P}^{2}
.

Following
[
IK
, p.306-308]
,
[
Ku1
, §3]
,
the (sheaf of) even Clifford algebra(s) of
M
is the sheaf

Cl_{0}(M)\,=\,\left(\oplus_{k=0}^{2}{\mathcal{E}}^{\otimes 2k}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(-k)\right)/I~,

where
I
is generated by the local sections
v\otimes v\otimes\lambda-q(v)\lambda

with
v,\lambda
local sections of
{\mathcal{E}}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(-1)
respectively, so that
q(v)\lambda

is a local section of
{\mathcal{O}}
. The rank of
Cl_{0}(q)
as a sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-modules is
2^{4-1}=8
.
Using the fact that the center of
Cl_{0}(M)
is the rank two sheaf
\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
, one can show that

\pi_{*}{\mathcal{A}}=Cl_{0}(M)
, where
{\mathcal{A}}
is the rank four Azumaya algebra on
K
corresponding to
the conic bundle
E
defined by the odd theta characteristic.
One then finds that:

\pi_{*}{\mathcal{A}}\,=\,{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}\,\oplus\,{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(-1)^{\oplus 3}\,\oplus\,{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(-2)^{\oplus 3}\,\oplus\,{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(-3)~.

The total Chern class is thus, with
H=c_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{2}}(1))
:

c(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{A}})\,=\,1\cdot(1-H)^{3}(1-2H)^{3}(1-3H)\,=\,1\,-\,12H\,+60H^{2}~,

hence
c_{1}(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{A}})=-12H
and
c_{2}(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{A}})=60
.
From Proposition
1.5
we get:

c_{2}({\mathcal{A}})\,=\,-\mbox{$\frac{9}{2}$}r(r-1)\,+\,c_{2}(\pi_{*}({\mathcal{A}}))\,=\,-9\cdot 2\cdot 3\,+\,60=\,6~.

Using the trace map,
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{O}}\oplus{\mathcal{A}}_{0}

and
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\cong p_{*}\omega_{E/K}^{-1}

[
CI
, Corollary 6.5]
, we get

c_{2}(p_{*}\omega_{E/K}^{-1})=6
and thus
K_{E}^{3}=12
by Proposition
1.1
.

\Box

2.
Brauer classes and twisted sheaves

2.1.
Brauer classes and B-fields

We introduced the two-torsion subgroup
\Br(K)_{2}
of the Brauer group
H^{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{*})_{tors}

of a K3 surface
K
in §
1.2
.
The exponential exact sequence gives the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})/\Pic(K)\longrightarrow H^{2}(K,{\mathcal{O}}_{K})\longrightarrow H^{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{*})\longrightarrow 0

where we identify
\Pic(K)=H^{1}(K,{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{*})
with its image in
H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
.
Any
\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}
has a lift
\tilde{\alpha}
to the complex vector space
H^{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
and
since
2\tilde{\alpha}\in H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})/\Pic(K)
there is a

B=B_{\alpha}\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
mapping to

\tilde{\alpha}
. We say that
B
is a B-field representative of
\alpha
.
If
B,B^{\prime}
map to the same
\alpha
then
B=B^{\prime}+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}p+c
for some

p\in\Pic(K)
and some
c\in H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
(
[
Hu2
, §4]
,
[
Ku2
, §6]
).

We now assume that
\Pic(K)={\bf Z}h
and
h^{2}=2d>0
.
Since
h^{2}\in 2{\bf Z}
, for any
a\in{\bf Z}
and
c\in H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})

the intersection number (in
\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}{\bf Z}
)

Bh\,=\,(B^{\prime}+\mbox{$\frac{a}{2}$}h+c)h\,\equiv B^{\prime}h\,\mod\,{\bf Z}

is an invariant of
\alpha
. Since
2B\in H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
we have
Bh\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}{\bf Z}/{\bf Z}=\{0,\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\}
.

Kuznetsov gave a geometrical interpretation of this invariant
in terms of the restriction of a conic bundle representing
\alpha
to curves in
K

in
[
Ku2
, Lemma 6.2]
.

Generalizing
[
Ku2
, Lemma 6.1]
to any
d
, we find another invariant of
\alpha
.
Since
\alpha
is two-torsion,
the B-field
B
can be written as
B=B_{0}/2
with
B_{0}\in H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
.
The lattice
H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
is even, so
B^{2}=B_{0}^{2}/4\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}{\bf Z}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
K
be a K3 surface with
\Pic(K)={\bf Z}h
and
h^{2}=2d
.
Let
\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}
and let
B\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})\subset H^{2}(K,{\bf Q})

be a B-field representing
\alpha
.
If
4Bh+h^{2}\equiv 0\mod 4
, then also
B^{2}\mod{\bf Z}
is an invariant of
\alpha
.

Proof.
With the notation as above,
c^{2},\,2B^{\prime}c,\,2\mbox{$\frac{a}{2}$}ch=\,ach\in{\bf Z}
and thus

B^{2}\,=\,(B^{\prime}+\mbox{$\frac{a}{2}$}h+c)^{2}\,\equiv\,(B^{\prime})^{2}+\mbox{$\frac{a^{2}}{4}$}h^{2}+aB^{\prime}h\mod{\bf Z}~.

Since
Bh=B^{\prime}h\mod{\bf Z}
, we also have
4B^{\prime}h+h^{2}\equiv 0\mod 4
,
and thus
\mbox{$\frac{a^{2}}{4}$}h^{2}+aB^{\prime}h\in{\bf Z}

since
h^{2}
is even and
a^{2}\equiv a\mod 2
.

\Box

For a polarized K3 surface
(K,h)
with
\Pic(K)={\bf Z}h
and
h^{2}=2d
, a Brauer class

\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}
, with B-field representative
B
, thus has the invariant

Bh\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}{\bf Z}/{\bf Z}
and:

if
d
is odd and
Bh\not\in{\bf Z}
there is a further
invariant
B^{2}\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}{\bf Z}/{\bf Z}
,

if
d
is even and
Bh\in{\bf Z}
there is a further invariant

B^{2}\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}{\bf Z}/{\bf Z}
.

For any
d
we have thus distinguished three types of Brauer classes of order two.

The next lemma guarantees that a class in
\Br(K)_{2}

can be represented by a B-field which has good properties.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K
be a K3 surface with
\Pic(K)={\bf Z}h
and
h^{2}=2d
.
Let
\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}
, then there is a B-field

B\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})\subset H^{2}(K,{\bf Q})

representing
\alpha

such that
2B\in H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
is primitive and such that

B^{2},Bh\in\{0,\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\}\in{\bf Q}
.

Proof.
In case
\alpha=0\in\Br(K)_{2}
, we take
B=0
. So we may assume that
\alpha\neq 0
.
Choose a B-field
B\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
representing
\alpha
.

Since
B\not\in H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\Pic(K)
,
the sublattice
M:=<2B,h>
of
H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
has rank two.
If
M
is degenerate, that is
(2B)^{2}h^{2}-(2Bh)^{2}\neq 0
,

Bh
must be an integer since
(2B)^{2}
and
h^{2}
are even integers. Hence
Bh=m
for some
m\in{\bf Z}
.
Since
h\in H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
is primitive, there is a
t\in H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
with
ht=1
and thus

(B-mt)h=0
. Since
B,B-mt
define the same Brauer class, in case

Bh\equiv 0\mod{\bf Z}
we may assume that
Bh=0
.
Then
M
is non-degenerate except if also
B^{2}=0
. In that case, write
B=mB^{\prime}
for a primitive

B^{\prime}\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
and an integer
m
. Since
\alpha\neq 0
,
m
is odd
and
B^{\prime}
is primitive, also defines
\alpha
, and
B^{\prime}h=0
,
(B^{\prime})^{2}=0
so we replace
B
by
B^{\prime}
.

In the other cases,
M
is non-degenerate and
M\subset M_{pr}\subset H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
,
where
M_{pr}
is the primitive closure of
M
, which
are all the
v\in H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
for which an integer multiple lies in
M
.
Then
M_{pr}
is a nondegenerate primitive sublattice of
H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
.
By results of Nikulin
[
Ni
, Theorem 1.14.4]
, the embedding of
M_{pr}
in
H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
is unique up to isometry.
Moreover, there is an isomorphism
H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})\cong U^{3}\oplus E_{8}(-1)^{2}

such that
M_{pr}
maps to
U^{2}
.
Since
h\in M_{pr}
is primitive, we may assume it maps to
(1,d)_{1}
(in the first copy of
U
)
where
h^{2}=2d
. We will write
B=B_{1}+B_{2}\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}(U\oplus U)
.

In case
Bh=0
, one finds that
B_{1}
must be
an integer multiple of
\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}(1,-d)=(0,-d)+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}(1,d)
.
Hence
B_{1}\in H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\Pic(K)
and we
may assume that
B=B_{2}=\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}(a,b)_{2}
for some
a,b\in{\bf Z}
.
Adding a suitable element of
U_{2}\subset H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
we may assume that
a,b\in\{0,1\}
.
Then
2B
is primitive in
H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
,
B^{2}\in\{0,\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\}
and we are done.

In case
Bh\equiv\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\mod{\bf Z}
, write

B=\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}(a,b)_{1}+(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})_{2}
and we may assume that
a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}
.
As
(B-a\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}h)_{1}=\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}(0,c)_{1}
, we may also assume that
a=0
and
then
b=1
(since
B^{\prime}h\not\equiv 0
). For such a
B
we do have
Bh=\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}

and
B^{2}=\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\in\{0,\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\}
, moreover
2B^{\prime}
is primitive
in
H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
.
∎

2.2.
Brauer classes and lattices

There is an isomorphism (cf.
[
vG
]
)

\Br(K)\,:=\,H^{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{*})_{tors}\,=\,\Big(H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})/c_{1}(\Pic(K))\Big)\otimes_{\bf Z}({\bf Q}/{\bf Z})\,=\,\mbox{Hom}_{\bf Z}(T_{K},{\bf Q}/{\bf Z}),

where
T_{K}:=c_{1}(\Pic(K))^{\perp}\subset H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
is the transcendental lattice of
K
.
The kernel of a non-trivial
\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}

is a sublattice of index two in
T_{K}
denoted by
\Gamma_{\alpha}
:

\Gamma_{\alpha}\,=\,\ker(\alpha:\,T_{K}\,\longrightarrow\,{\bf Z}/2{\bf Z})~.

In case
\Pic(K)={\bf Z}h
, with
h^{2}=2d
,
we classify elements of order two in the Brauer group by the isomorphism class of
the lattice
\Gamma_{\alpha}
(this is called T-equivalence in
[
HS1
]
).
We find the same three types as before, see Theorem
2.3
.

To get a more explicit description of the
\Gamma_{\alpha}
, we use the notation from

[
vG
, §9]
.
Let
\Pic(K)={\bf Z}h
with
h^{2}=2d
, then the transcendental lattice
T_{K}
is isomorphic to

T_{K}\cong{\bf Z}v\oplus\Lambda^{\prime}
where
v^{2}=-2d

and
\Lambda^{\prime}\cong U^{2}\oplus E_{8}(-1)^{2}
.
Any
\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}=\mbox{Hom}_{\bf Z}(T_{K},{\bf Z}/2{\bf Z})
can then be written as:

\alpha\colon T_{K}\,\longrightarrow\,{\bf Z}/2{\bf Z},\qquad(nv,\lambda)\,\longmapsto\,a_{\alpha}n+\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda\mod\;2~,

so
\alpha=(a_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha})
for a unique
a_{\alpha}\in\{0,1\}
and
a
\lambda_{\alpha}\in\Lambda^{\prime}
whose class
in
\Lambda^{\prime}/2\Lambda^{\prime}\cong({\bf Z}/2{\bf Z})^{20}
is uniquely determined by
\alpha
.
We will fix an isomorphism
H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})\cong U\oplus\Lambda^{\prime}
such that
h=(1,d)_{1}
,

v=(1,-d)_{1}
, both in the first component
U
. Notice that
(0,1)_{1}\cdot v=1

and also
(0,1)_{1}\cdot h=1
.

A B-field lift
B_{\alpha}\in H^{2}(K,{\bf Q})
of
\alpha=(a_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha})
is determined by

2B_{\alpha}(nv+\lambda)=a_{\alpha}n+\lambda\lambda_{\alpha}\mod 2
.
Thus we can take

B_{\alpha}\,=\,\big((0,\mbox{$\frac{a_{\alpha}}{2}$})_{1},\mbox{$\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}}{2}$}\big)\,\in\,\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}(U\oplus\Lambda^{\prime}),\qquad\mbox{hence}\quad B_{\alpha}h\,=\,\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}a_{\alpha},\quad B_{\alpha}^{2}\,=\,\mbox{$\frac{1}{4}$}\lambda_{\alpha}^{2}~.

The following theorem completes a similar result in
[
vG
, Prop. 9.2]
,
the only difference is for the case
h^{2}=2d
with
d
even;
for these
h
there are also three cases
(the two isomorphism classes of lattices
\Gamma_{\alpha}
with
a_{\alpha}=0

were not distinguished in
[
vG
]
). In case
d=1
these classes are well understood in
terms of points of order two and theta characteristics on the branch curve of
the double cover
\phi_{h}:K\rightarrow{\bf P}^{2}
, see also
[
IOOV
]
.
For a similar classification of Brauer classes of order
p
for
p>2
see

[
MS+
]
.

The dual lattice of
\Gamma_{\alpha}
is

\Gamma_{\alpha}^{*}:=\mbox{Hom}_{\bf Z}(\Gamma_{\alpha},{\bf Z})
and we identify it with

\Gamma_{\alpha}^{*}\,=\,\{x\in\Gamma_{\alpha}\otimes{\bf Q}:\,x\gamma\in{\bf Z},\;\forall\gamma\in\Gamma_{\alpha}\,\},\qquad x:y\longmapsto xy~,

where the intersection form on
\Gamma_{\alpha}\subset H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
is extended
{\bf Q}
-bilinearly.
The discriminant quadratic form on the (finite)
discriminant group
\Gamma^{\ast}_{\alpha}/\Gamma_{\alpha}
is given by

q_{\alpha}:\Gamma^{\ast}_{\alpha}/\Gamma_{\alpha}\,\longrightarrow{\bf Q}/2{\bf Z},\qquad q_{\alpha}(x)\,:=\,x^{2}~.

The isomorphism class of
\Gamma_{\alpha}
is determined
by
q_{\alpha}
(
[
Ni
, Corollary 1.13.3]
).

Theorem 2.3
.

(Refinement of
[
vG
, Prop. 9.2]
)
Let
K
be a K3 surface with
\Pic(K)={\bf Z}h
and
h^{2}=2d
.
Then for each
d\in{\bf Z}_{>0}
, the set of lattices
\Gamma_{\alpha}
,
with
\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}
,
\alpha\neq 0
, is partitioned into three isometry classes.

In the case that
4Bh+h^{2}\equiv 0\mod 4
,
the isomorphism class of
\Gamma_{\alpha}
is determined by

B_{\alpha}h\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}{\bf Z}/{\bf Z}
and
B^{2}\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}{\bf Z}/{\bf Z}
,
otherwise it is determined by
B_{\alpha}h\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}{\bf Z}/{\bf Z}
only.

More explicitly, let
\alpha=(a_{\alpha},\lambda_{\alpha})\in\Br(K)_{2}=\Hom(T_{K},{\bf Z}/2{\bf Z}),\alpha\neq 0
,
have B-field representative
B_{\alpha}
, then:

(1)

if
a_{\alpha}=0
(equivalently
B_{\alpha}h\equiv 0)
, then

\Gamma^{\ast}_{\alpha}/\Gamma_{\alpha}={\bf Z}/2d{\bf Z}\oplus{\bf Z}/2{\bf Z}\oplus{\bf Z}/2{\bf Z}
.
There are

(2)

Proof.
Only part (1) is not contained in

\Gamma^{\ast}_{\alpha}/\Gamma_{\alpha}={\bf Z}/2d{\bf Z}\oplus{\bf Z}/2{\bf Z}\oplus{\bf Z}/2{\bf Z}

(and

else).
The generators of the dual lattice

\lambda_{\alpha}/2

\mu_{\alpha}

2.3.
Moduli spaces of twisted sheaves

We recall the fundamental results on moduli spaces of twisted sheaves
on a K3 surface
K
. The Mukai lattice
{\tilde{H}}(K,{\bf Z})
,
of rank 24, and its bilinear form

<\cdot,\cdot>
are defined by

\tilde{H}(K,{\bf Z})\,:=\,H^{0}(K,{\bf Z})\oplus H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})\oplus H^{4}(K,{\bf Z}),\qquad<(r,v,s),(r^{\prime},v^{\prime},s^{\prime})>\,:=\,-rs^{\prime}-r^{\prime}s+vv^{\prime}.

We consider a B-field
B\in H^{2}(K,{\bf Q})
.
It defines an isometry (where
{\tilde{H}}(K,{\bf Q}):={\tilde{H}}(K,{\bf Z})\otimes{\bf Q}
):

\exp(B):{\tilde{H}}(K,{\bf Q})\,\longrightarrow\,{\tilde{H}}(K,{\bf Q}),\qquad x\longmapsto\exp(B)\wedge x,

where
\exp(B)=(1,B,B\wedge B/2)\in{\tilde{H}}(K,{\bf Q})
,
and
\wedge
indicates the cup product on
{\tilde{H}}(K,{\bf Q})
, so

\exp(B)\wedge(r,v,s)=(r,v+rB,s+B\wedge v+rB\wedge B/2)~.

The B-field
B
defines a (polarized) Hodge structure
{\tilde{H}}(K,B,{\bf Z})
of weight two
on the lattice
\tilde{H}(K,{\bf Z})

as follows (
[
HS1
, Def. 2.3]
):

{\tilde{H}}^{2,0}(K,B):={\bf C}\omega_{K,B},\qquad\mbox{where}\quad\omega_{K,B}:=\exp(B)\omega_{K}=\omega_{K}+B\wedge\omega_{K},

here
\omega_{K}
is a basis of
H^{2,0}(K)
and

B\wedge\omega_{K}\in H^{4}(K,{\bf C})
is the cup product of
B
and
\omega_{K}
, next one defines:

{\tilde{H}}^{0,2}(K,B):=\overline{{\tilde{H}}^{2,0}(K,B)},\qquad{\tilde{H}}^{1,1}(K,B):=\big({\tilde{H}}^{2,0}(K,B)\oplus{\tilde{H}}^{0,2}(K,B)\big)^{\perp}.

The isomorphism class of the Hodge structure

{\tilde{H}}(K,B,{\bf Z})
depends only on the image
\alpha_{B}

of
B
in the
quotient
H^{2}(K,{\bf Q})/(NS(K)_{\bf Q}+H^{2}(K,{\bf Z}))=\Br(K)
.

The trivial Hodge substructure in
{\tilde{H}}(K,B,{\bf Z})
is denoted by

NS(K,B):=\{\,x\in{\tilde{H}}(K,{\bf Z})\,:\;<x,\omega_{K,B}>=0\,\}.

i.e.
NS(K,B)={\tilde{H}}(K,B,{\bf Z})\cap{\tilde{H}}^{1,1}(K,B)
.
Note that if
x=(r,\lambda,s)
then

<x,\omega_{K,B}>\;=\;<(r,\lambda,s),(0,\omega_{K},B\wedge\omega_{K})>\;=\;-rB\wedge\omega_{K}+\lambda\wedge\omega_{K}=(-rB+\lambda)\wedge\omega_{K}.

The kernel of the map
H^{2}(K,{\bf Q})\rightarrow H^{4}(K,{\bf C})
,

\mu\mapsto\mu\wedge\omega_{K}
is
NS(K)_{\bf Q}
.
From this it follows that if

(-rB+\lambda)\wedge\omega_{K}=0

then
-rB+\lambda=D
for some
D\in\Pic(K)_{\bf Q}
,
hence
\lambda=rB+D
and
(r,\lambda,s)=r(1,B,0)+(0,D,0)+(0,0,s)
.
A Brauer class
\alpha
of order
r
in
\Br(K)
has a B-field lift
B\in H^{2}(K,{\bf Q})

such that
rB\in H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})
is primitive and then one finds

NS(K,B)\,=\,<(r,rB,0)>\oplus NS(K)\oplus H^{4}(K,{\bf Z})~.

An element
v\in NS(K,B)
is called a (
B
-twisted) Mukai vector.

In analogy with the untwisted case, one defines the
transcendental lattice of
{\tilde{H}}(K,B,{\bf Z})
as:

T(K,B)\,:=\,\{x\in{\tilde{H}}(K,B,{\bf Z}):\;xy=0\qquad\forall y\in NS(K,B)\,\}.

If
\alpha\in\Br(K)=\Hom(T_{K},{\bf Q}/{\bf Z})
is the Brauer class defined by
B
then
there is an isometry of Hodge structures
(
[
Hu2
, Prop. 4.7]
, where
\Gamma_{\alpha}
,
T(K,B)

are denoted by
T(X,\alpha)
and
T(\phi)
respectively):

\Gamma_{\alpha}\,\cong\,T(K,B)~.

Let
\alpha\in\Br(K)
and let
B
be a B-field representative for
\alpha
.
Then there is a twisted Chern character
ch^{B}
from the K-group of
\alpha
-twisted coherent
sheaves on
K
to
NS(K,B)
and the (twisted) Mukai vector
an
\alpha
-twisted sheaf
E
is defined as
v^{B}(E):=ch^{B}(E)\cdot\sqrt{\mbox{td}(K)}

[
Hu2
, §4]
,
[
HS2
]
.
If
v=(r,\ell,s)
is
a primitive
B
-twisted Mukai vector with
r>0
then
the moduli space
M_{v}(K,B)
of
\alpha
-twisted sheaves
E
with
v^{B}(E)=v

is an irreducible hyper-Kähler manifold of dimension
2+v^{2}

which is deformation equivalent to
K^{[n]}
with
2n=2+v^{2}

(see
[
Y
, Thm. 3.16]
). Moreover, if
v^{2}>0
then there is an isometry of Hodge structures
(see
[
Y
, Thm. 3.19]
)

H^{2}(M_{v}(K,B),{\bf Z})\,\cong\,v^{\perp},\qquad NS(M_{v}(K,B))\,\cong\,v^{\perp}\cap NS(K,B)~.

In particular, the transcendental lattice of
H^{2}(M_{v}(K,B),{\bf Z}))
is
T(K,B)
.

2.4.
Twisted sheaves and Azumaya algebras

Using twisted sheaves, one finds a convenient description for the Azumaya algebra associated
to a
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration.
In the next proposition we do not assume that the Brauer class is non-trivial
so it holds also for
B=0
. In that case, one has
E={\bf P}({\mathcal{V}})
where
{\mathcal{V}}
is a rank two vector
bundle on
K
and the Azumaya algebra is
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{V}}\otimes{\mathcal{V}}^{\vee}
.
The proposition can be generalized to
{\bf P}^{r-1}
-fibrations and locally
free twisted sheaves of rank
r
(cf.
[
Ko
, §9]
).

In Section
4
we show that an exceptional BN divisor
E
is a
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration
over a K3 surface with
K_{E}^{3}=12
. Therefore we compute
K_{E}^{3}
in terms of the Mukai vector
of a twisted sheaf.
Proposition
2.6
then implies that there are Brauer classes on a general
K3 surface that do not arise from such exceptional divisors.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
p:E\rightarrow K
be a
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration over a K3 surface
with Brauer class
\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}
(as in Section
1.2
) and let
B
be a B-field representative of
\alpha
.
Then
E={\bf P}({\mathcal{U}})
for a locally free
\alpha
-twisted sheaf of rank two
{\mathcal{U}}
on
K
,
the Azumaya algebra defined by
E
is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{U}}\otimes{\mathcal{U}}^{\vee}

and one has:

c_{2}({\mathcal{A}})\,=\,v^{B}({\mathcal{U}})^{2}\,+\,8~,\qquad K_{E}^{3}\,=\,2c_{2}({\mathcal{A}})~.

Proof.

The conic bundle
E
is locally trivial (in the complex topology),
so for a suitable open covering
K=\cup_{i}U_{i}
we have
E_{i}:=p^{-1}(U_{i})\cong U_{i}\times{\bf P}^{1}

and the gluing is by isomorphisms
\phi^{\prime}_{ij}\in PGL(2,{\mathcal{O}}_{K}({U_{i}\cap U_{j}}))

which can be lifted to
\phi_{ij}\in GL(2,{\mathcal{O}}_{K}({U_{i}\cap U_{j}}))
.
These
\phi_{ij}
define a cocycle
\alpha_{ijk}\in\Gamma(U_{i}\cap U_{j}\cap U_{k},{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{*})
which
represents the Brauer class
\alpha
of
E
. The
\phi_{ij}
also define a locally free twisted sheaf

{\mathcal{U}}
on
K
with B-field
B
which also determines
\alpha
and
E={\bf P}({\mathcal{U}})

(cf.
[
Y
, §1]
,
[
HS1
, §1]
).

According to
[
C
, Thm. 1.3.5]
and
[
dJ
]
, for any Azumaya algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
over
K

with two torsion Brauer class
\alpha
there
is a locally free
\alpha
-twisted sheaf of rank two
{\mathcal{U}}
such that

{\mathcal{A}}\simeq\mathcal{E}nd({\mathcal{U}})
.
More explicitly, let
{\mathcal{G}}_{i}:=p_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{i}}(1)
, where
{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{i}}(1)
is the pull-back of
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{1}}(1)

along the projection
E_{i}=U_{i}\times{\bf P}^{1}\rightarrow{\bf P}^{1}
.
Then
{\mathcal{G}}_{i}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{i}}\otimes H^{0}({\bf P}^{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf P}^{1}}(1))
, a locally free sheaf of rank two,
and
E_{i}={\bf P}({\mathcal{G}}_{i}^{\vee})
. The
p^{*}{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
are locally free sheaves on
E_{i}
which glue after twisting by a collection of tautological line bundles to
a locally free sheaf of rank two
{\mathcal{G}}
on
E
(
[
Y
, §1.1]
). This sheaf sits in the (non-split)
relative Euler sequence on
E
:

0\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{G}}\,\longrightarrow\,\omega_{E/K}^{-1}\,\longrightarrow\,0

(here
\omega_{E/K}^{-1}
is usually written as
{\mathcal{T}}_{E/K}
). Such extensions are parametrized
by
H^{1}(E,\omega_{E/K})\,(\cong H^{1}(E,\omega_{K})=H^{3,1}(E))
which is one dimensional, hence the exact sequence
characterizes
{\mathcal{G}}
. According to
[
CI
, §6]
, where
{\mathcal{G}}
is denoted by
J
, the Azumaya algebra
on
K
defined by
E
is
{\mathcal{A}}:=p_{*}{\mathcal{E}}nd_{E}({\mathcal{G}})=p_{*}({\mathcal{G}}^{\vee}\otimes{\mathcal{G}})
.

Using the trace map,
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{O}}\oplus{\mathcal{A}}_{0}

and
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\cong p_{*}\omega_{E/K}^{-1}

[
CI
, Corollary 6.5]
.
Hence
K_{E}^{3}=2c_{2}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})=2c_{2}({\mathcal{A}})
by Proposition
1.1
.

Now from
[
Y
, p. 5]
we have
{\mathcal{U}}\otimes{\mathcal{U}}^{\vee}\simeq{\mathcal{A}}
.
Let
ch^{B}({\mathcal{U}})=(r,\lambda,s)
, with
r=2
, be the twisted Chern character of
{\mathcal{U}}
. Then

ch^{-B}({\mathcal{U}}^{\vee})=(r,-\lambda,s)
and thus

ch({\mathcal{A}})\,=\,ch^{B}({\mathcal{U}})\cdot ch^{-B}({\mathcal{U}}^{\vee})\,=\,(r^{2},0,2rs-\lambda^{2}),\qquad\mbox{hence}\quad c_{2}({\mathcal{A}})\,=\,-2rs\,+\,\lambda^{2}~.

On the other hand,
v^{B}({\mathcal{U}})=ch^{B}({\mathcal{U}})\sqrt{\mbox{td}(K)}=(r,\lambda,r+s)
, hence

v^{B}({\mathcal{U}})^{2}=-2r(r+s)+\lambda^{2}
.
∎

As a consequence, if
{\mathcal{U}}
is stable then the moduli space of deformations of
{\mathcal{U}}
has dimension

v^{B}({\mathcal{U}})^{2}+2=c_{2}({\mathcal{A}})-6
, which agrees with
[
HoSt
, Thm 3.6]
.
In the ‘extreme’ case that
v^{B}({\mathcal{U}})^{2}=-2
(equivalently,
c_{2}({\mathcal{U}}\otimes{\mathcal{U}}^{\vee})=6
),
the following proposition shows that the stability of
{\mathcal{U}}
is automatic and thus
{\mathcal{U}}
is rigid.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(K,h)
be a polarized K3 surface of degree
h^{2}=2d
and assume that
\Pic(K)={\bf Z}h
.
Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be a locally free
\alpha
-twisted sheaf of rank two with non-trivial
Brauer class
\alpha\in\Br_{2}(K)
represented by a B-field
B

and assume that
v^{B}({\mathcal{U}})^{2}\leq 0
. Then
{\mathcal{U}}
is stable.

Proof.
First we show that
{\mathcal{U}}
is simple, that is,

\dim\Hom({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{U}})=h^{0}(K,{\mathcal{U}}^{\vee}\otimes{\mathcal{U}})=h^{0}(K,{\mathcal{A}})=1

where
{\mathcal{A}}:={\mathcal{U}}\otimes{\mathcal{U}}^{\vee}
is the Azumaya algebra associated to the
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration

p:E:={\bf P}({\mathcal{U}})\rightarrow K
.
Since
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{O}}\oplus{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
this is equivalent to
h^{0}(K,{\mathcal{A}}_{0})=0
.
As
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}=p_{*}\omega_{E/K}^{-1}
and
\omega_{E/K}=\omega_{K}
,
we get
h^{0}(K,{\mathcal{A}}_{0})=h^{0}(E,-K_{E})=0
, by Proposition
1.2
.
By assumption,
v({\mathcal{U}})^{2}\leq 0
so it now follows from
[
Y
, Prop. 3.12]

that
{\mathcal{U}}
is stable.
∎

We recall that the non-trivial elements
\Br(K)_{2}
come in three types.
For
\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}
,
\alpha\neq 0
, choose a representative B-field
B
.
Then first of all, we have the invariant
2Bh\in{\bf Z}/2{\bf Z}
.
If
4Bh+h^{2}\equiv 0\mod 4
, then
\alpha
has the additional invariant
2B^{2}\in{\bf Z}/2{\bf Z}
.

We now show that in case
4Bh+h^{2}\equiv 0\mod 4
and
B^{2}\in{\bf Z}
there exists no locally free
rank two
\alpha
-twisted sheaf
{\mathcal{U}}
with
v^{B}({\mathcal{U}})^{2}=-2
. Thus only two of the three
classes of elements in
\Br(K)_{2}
have an associated rank four
Azumaya algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
with
c_{2}({\mathcal{A}})=6
. In Proposition
2.7
we show that in case

4Bh+h^{2}\equiv 2\mod 4
such a sheaf
{\mathcal{U}}
is unique up to twisting by line bundles.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(K,h)
be a polarized K3 surface of degree
h^{2}=2d
and assume that
\Pic(K)={\bf Z}h
.
Let
\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}
with B-field representative
B
.
There exists a semistable
\alpha
-twisted sheaf
{\mathcal{U}}
of rank two on
K

with
v^{B}({\mathcal{U}})^{2}=-2
only in one of the following three cases:

i)

\alpha=0
and
h^{2}\equiv 2\mod 4
,

ii)

4Bh+h^{2}\equiv 2\mod 4
(in this case
B^{2}
is not an invariant of
\alpha
)

iii)

4Bh+h^{2}\equiv 0\mod 4
and
B^{2}\notin{\bf Z}
.

Proof.
In case
\alpha=0
, the Mukai vector of
{\mathcal{U}}
should be
v=(2,kh,s)
for some integers
k,s
and thus
v^{2}=-4s+k^{2}h^{2}
. To have
v^{2}=-2

one needs
h^{2}\equiv 2\mod 4
and then, for any odd
k
and
s=(2+k^{2}h^{2})/4
, the moduli
space
M_{v}(K)
consists of one point, this sheaf
{\mathcal{U}}
satisfies all the conditions.

In case
B\neq 0
,
NS(K,B)
is generated by
(2,2B,0),h
and
H^{4}(K,{\bf Z})={\bf Z}
.
The Mukai vector of
{\mathcal{U}}
should thus be
v^{B}=(2,2B+kh,s)
and

(v^{B})^{2}\,=\,(2,2B+kh,s)^{2}\,=\,-4s+4B^{2}+4kBh+k^{2}h^{2}~.

If
4Bh+h^{2}\equiv 2\mod 4
then one can take
k
even so that
4kBh+k^{2}h^{2}\equiv 0\mod 4

and choose
the B-field
B
representative of
\alpha
to have
B^{2}=1/2
and let

s:=1+(4kBh+k^{2}h^{2})/4
. Alternatively, take
k
odd and choose
B
with
B^{2}=0
and

s:=(2+4kBh+k^{2}h^{2})/4
.

In case
4Bh+h^{2}\equiv 0\mod 4
, also
4kBh+k^{2}h^{2}\equiv 0\mod 4
and one needs

B^{2}\in(1/2){\bf Z}
,
B^{2}\not\in{\bf Z}
.
∎

A final result concerns the uniqueness of certain twisted sheaves for one of the three
types of Brauer classes.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(K,h)
be a polarized K3 surface of degree
h^{2}=2d
and assume that
\Pic(K)={\bf Z}h
.
Let
\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}
,
\alpha\neq 0
, have B-field representative
B

and assume that
4Bh+h^{2}\equiv 2\mod 4
.

Let

{\mathcal{U}}
,
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
be locally free rank two
\alpha
-twisted sheaves on
K
with

v^{B}({\mathcal{U}})^{2}=v^{B}({\mathcal{U}}^{\prime})^{2}=-2
.

Then there exists a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
on
K
such that
{\mathcal{U}}\cong{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}
.
Moreover, if
B^{2}=1/2
then there is a line bundle
{\mathcal{M}}
on
K
such that

v^{B}({\mathcal{U}}\otimes{\mathcal{M}})=(2,2B,1)
.

Proof.
Since
\alpha
has order two,
NS(K,B)
is generated by
(2,2B,0),h
and
H^{4}(K,{\bf Z})={\bf Z}
.
As
{\mathcal{U}}
has rank two, we find
ch^{B}({\mathcal{U}})=(2,2B+kh,s)
for some integers
k,s
.
Let
{\mathcal{L}}
be the line bundle on
K
with class
-mh
, for some integer
m
,
then
ch({\mathcal{L}})=1-mh+m^{2}h^{2}/2
. Using
[
HS1
, Prop. 1.2(iii)]

we get

ch^{B}({\mathcal{U}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}})=ch^{B}({\mathcal{U}})ch({\mathcal{L}})=(2,2B+kh,*)(1,-mh,*)=(2,2B+(k-2m)h,*).

Hence choosing
m
suitably and replacing
{\mathcal{U}}
by
{\mathcal{U}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}

we may assume that

ch^{B}({\mathcal{U}})=(2,2B,t)
or
ch^{B}({\mathcal{U}})=(2,2B+h,t)
for some integer
t
.
Then we have
v^{B}({\mathcal{U}})=ch^{B}({\mathcal{U}})\sqrt{\mbox{td}(K)}=(2,2B,t+2)
or

v^{B}({\mathcal{U}})=(2,2B+h,t+2)
. Computing
v^{B}({\mathcal{U}})^{2}
we find:

-2\,=\,-4(t+2)+4B^{2},\qquad\mbox{or}\quad-2\,=\,-4(t+2)+4B^{2}+4Bh+h^{2}~.

Since
4Bh+h^{2}\equiv 2\mod 4
, the value of
B^{2}\mod{\bf Z}
is not an invariant.
If we assume that
B
is chosen such that
B^{2}\not\in{\bf Z}
, then
4B^{2}\equiv 2\mod 4
,
hence the second equation has no solutions. The first equation shows that

{\mathcal{U}}
has Mukai vector
v:=(2,2B,t+2)
with
v^{2}=-2
(so with
t=(4B^{2}-6)/4
).
Assuming
B^{2}=1/2
we find
t=-1
and
v=(2,2B,1)
.

(If instead we assume that
B^{2}\in{\bf Z}
then the first equation has no solution and the second
one shows that
{\mathcal{U}}
has Mukai
vector

2.5.
Brauer classes of degree two K3’s

In the case of a general

]

[

]

-fibration

with Brauer class
\alpha
having

, equivalently, with
K_{E}^{3}=12
.
We exhibited this conic bundle in (the proof of) Proposition
1.4
,
see also §
5.1
.

A Brauer class
\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}
that corresponds to an odd theta characteristic has

Bh\equiv 1/2\mod{\bf Z}
, so
4Bh+h^{2}\equiv 0\mod 4
, and extra invariant
B^{2}\not\in{\bf Z}
.
A
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration
E\rightarrow K
with this Brauer class was given in Corollary
1.6

and it has
K_{E}^{3}=12
, so
E={\bf P}({\mathcal{U}})
with
v^{B}({\mathcal{U}})^{2}=-2
.

Finally, a Brauer class
\alpha\in\Br(K)_{2}
that corresponds to an even theta characteristic has

Bh\equiv 1/2\mod{\bf Z}
, so
4Bh+h^{2}\equiv 0\mod 4
, and extra invariant

.
In this case there does not exist a
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration

with Brauer class

\alpha
which has

where

is the Azumaya algebra
defined by

.
Ingalls and Khalid
[

, Theorem 4.3]
found a two dimensional

This is thus ‘the best possible result’.

3.1.
Contractions and Heegner divisors

In this section we follow Debarre and Macrì
[
DM
]
to classify all divisorial contractions on
a hyper-Kähler fourfold
X
of K3
[2]
-type (with Picard rank two)
in terms of sublattices of
H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
,
or equivalently, in terms of Heegner divisors in certain period spaces.

The following theorem collects the general results on
certain
-2
-classes on
X
cf.
[
HT2
]
. We write
(\cdot,\cdot)
for the bilinear form associated
to the BBF-form on
H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
.
Recall that the base of a general divisorial contraction on a hyper-Kähler fourfold
is a symplectic surface, so it is a K3 surface or an abelian surface (
[
Na
, Prop. 1.6]
).
From
[
BM
]
in the case of
K3^{[2]}
type hyper-Kähler fourfolds it is always a
K3
surface (see Section
4
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be an hyper-Kähler fourfold of K3
[2]
-type,

L\in\Pic(X)
,
e=c_{1}(L)
,
(e,e)=-2
and

(e,h)>0
for some Kähler class
h\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
.
Suppose that
(X,L)
is general in the locus of deformations of

X
which keep
e
of type
(1,1)
.
Let
k
be the divisibility of
e
in
H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
, so

k=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}2&\mbox{if}&(e,H^{2}(X,{\bf Z}))=2{\bf Z},\\
1&\mbox{if}&(e,H^{2}(X,{\bf Z}))={\bf Z}.\end{array}\right.

Then
h^{0}(L^{\otimes k})=1
and the unique effective divisor
E\in|L^{\otimes k}|

is reduced and irreducible (
[
Ma4
]
).

Moreover, let
F\subset X
be an effective, reduced and irreducible divisor
with class
f
such that
(f,f)<0
.
Then by
[
Dr
]
there exists a sequence of flops from
(X,F)
to
(X^{\prime},F^{\prime})

such that
F^{\prime}
is contractible through a projective birational morphism.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
E
and
k
be as above.
Then
E
is called a Brill-Noether (BN)
exceptional divisor if
k=1
(so
E^{2}=-2
) and a Hilbert-Chow (HC) exceptional divisor
if
k=2
(so
E^{2}=-8
).

We now consider exceptional divisors
E
and
the divisor classes on
X
inducing the contraction.
Let
H_{2d}
be a big and nef divisor on
X
,
whose class in
\Pic(X)
is primitive with BBF degree
H_{2d}^{2}=2d

and divisibility
\gamma\in\{1,2\}
,
which contracts a prime divisor
E_{2d}
with
E_{2d}^{2}=-2
.
The Picard group of
X
thus contains the lattice

\Pic(X)\,\supset\,{\bf Z}H_{2d}\,\oplus\,{\bf Z}E_{2d}\;\cong\;<2d>\,\oplus\,<-2>~.

Notice that the same sublattice appears for a HC contraction, where
E_{2d}
is not effective
but
2E_{2d}
is the class of the exceptional divisor.

A general deformation
(X^{\prime},H^{\prime}_{2d})
of
(X,H_{2d})
has
\Pic(X^{\prime})={\bf Z}H_{2d}^{\prime}

and
H^{\prime}_{2d}
is then an ample divisor class with square
2d
with the same divisibility
\gamma
.
Thus
(X^{\prime},H^{\prime}_{2d})
is an element of the (irreducible, quasi-projective, 20 dimensional) moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}^{(\gamma)}_{2d}
of hyper-Kähler fourfolds of K3
[n]
-type with a polarization of BBF-square
2d
and divisibility
\gamma
, where in case
\gamma=2
one has
d\equiv 3\mod 4
.
(see
[
DM
, §3]
).

The lattice
H^{2}(X,{\bf Z})
, with the BBF-form, is isometric to the lattice

\Lambda\,:=\,U^{3}\,\oplus\,E_{8}(-1)^{2}\,\oplus\,<-2>~.

We denote by
\delta
the generator of the last summand of
\Lambda
, so
\delta^{2}=-2

and the divisibility of
\delta
is two since
(\delta,\Lambda)=2{\bf Z}
.

The orthogonal group
O(\Lambda)
acts transitively on the set of primitive elements with fixed
BBF-square
2n
and fixed divisibility
\gamma
, we fix such an element
h^{\gamma}_{2d}\in\Lambda
. It defines a complex variety

\Omega^{\gamma}_{2d}\,:=\,\{\,x\in{\bf P}(\Lambda\otimes{\bf C}):\;q(x,h^{\gamma}_{2d})\,=\,0,\;q(x,x)\,=\,0,\;q(x,\bar{x})\,=\,0\,\}.

Given
(X^{\prime},H^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{M}}^{(\gamma)}_{2d}
, an isometry
H^{2}(X^{\prime},{\bf Z})\cong\Lambda

which maps
H^{\prime}
to
h^{\gamma}_{2d}
will map

H^{2,0}(X)
to an element, its period, in
\Omega^{\gamma}_{2d}
. In this way we obtain the
period map

\wp^{\gamma}_{2d}\,:\,{\mathcal{M}}^{(\gamma)}_{2d}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{P}}_{2d}^{(\gamma)}\,:=\,O(\Lambda,h^{\gamma}_{2d})\backslash\Omega^{\gamma}_{2d},

where
O(\Lambda,h^{\gamma}_{2d}):=\{g\in O(\Lambda):\,g(h^{\gamma}_{2d})=h^{\gamma}_{2d}\}

and the period space
{\mathcal{P}}_{2d}^{(\gamma)}
is a quasi-projective variety.

Since
H_{2d}
contracts a divisor, it is not ample and thus

(X,H_{2d})
does not define a point in
{\mathcal{M}}^{(\gamma)}_{2d}
.
That is, its period point in
{\mathcal{P}}_{2d}^{(\gamma)}
does not lie in the image of the period map.
Notice that the rank of
\Pic(X)
is at least two.

Let
K\subset\Lambda
be a rank two primitive sublattice with signature
(1,1)
, containing
h^{\gamma}_{2d}
. Let

\Omega_{K}\,:=\,\{x\in\Omega^{\gamma}_{2d}\,:\;q(x,k)\,=\,0\quad\forall k\,\in\,K\,\},\qquad{\mathcal{D}}^{(\gamma)}_{2n,K}\,:=\,\mbox{im}(\Omega_{K}\hookrightarrow\Omega^{\gamma}_{2d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{P}}^{(\gamma)}_{2d})~,

maps to a point in

contains a copy of

Since the period map for smooth compact (not necessarily projective) hyper-Kähler
fourfolds is surjective
[

there exists a fourfold of K3
[2]
-type for any given point
in the period domain
{\mathcal{P}}_{2d}^{(\gamma)}

[2]

[

[

It was proven in
[

is contained in a
divisor of the type listed below (notice that we omit

If
\gamma=1

(1)

4.1.

In this section we deduce from the work of Bayer and Macrì

that the exceptional divisor of a
divisorial contraction on a hyper-Kähler fourfold of K3

{\bf P}^{1}

First we show that if

Proposition 4.1
.

The conic bundle

[

, Theorem 7.33]

states that there is an isomorphism, where

is a linear combination of the

In particular, the Betti numbers of

are

for

.
Notice that in general this isomorphism does not respect the Hodge structures
since a general

-fibration over

Using the injectivity of

one can still determine the Hodge numbers of

h^{0,0}(E)=1,\quad h^{1,0}(E)=0,\quad h^{2,0}(E)=1,\quad h^{1,1}(E)=21,\quad h^{3,0}(E)=h^{2,1}(E)=0~.

Using deformations and Hodge theory,
[
O3
, Cor. 3.25.3]
shows that

is injective, notice that both groups have rank

.
Moreover,

s^{\perp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,i^{*}(s^{\perp})\,\subset\,c^{\perp}\,:=\,\{\gamma\in H^{2}(E,{\bf Z}):\;c\cdot\gamma=0\,\}\,=\,p^{*}H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})~,

where

is the class of
E
, and

is the class of a fiber of
p
,

[
O3
, Cor. 3.25.2]

Thus one obtains an injective homomorphism

which is shown to preserve the quadratic forms, so

[

r:\,s^{\perp}\,\longrightarrow\,H^{2}(K,{\bf Z})~.

Finally, using that

5.2.
The Heegner divisor
{\mathcal{D}}^{(1)}_{8,8,\beta}
and Fano’s of cubic fourfolds with a plane

The Fano fourfold
F
of lines in a smooth cubic fourfold

is a hyper-Kähler fourfold
with an ample class
g\in\Pic(F)

defined by the Plücker map which has BB-square
g^{2}=6
.

Let now

be a smooth cubic fourfold with a plane

by the Hassett divisor
{\mathcal{C}}_{8}
.
Then

defines a divisor (class)

\tau=\alpha(T)\in\Pic(F)

\alpha:H^{4}(X,{\bf Z})\rightarrow H^{2}(F,{\bf Z})

Abel-Jacobi map (which only changes the sign on the primitive part).
The Abel-Jacobi map is induced by the incidence correspondence

Z
in
X\times F
, so
Z=\{(x,[l])\in X\times F:\;x\in l\}
(and

is a line in

One has

and

(
[
HT1
, Example 7.5]
).
Thus
\Pic(F)
has the sublattice (which is equal to
\Pic(F)

From the incidence correspondence one finds that
the support
\tau
, denoted by
E
, consists of the classes
[l]\in F
of lines

which meet
T

E=\{[l]\in F:\;l\cap T\,\neq\,\emptyset\},\qquad\tau\,=\,[E]~.

The divisor

is well known to be a conic bundle over a K3 surface
K
of degree two.
Choose a plane
T^{\prime}\subset{\bf P}^{5}
disjoint from
T
.
For

let

be the
{\bf P}^{3}
spanned by

and

. Then

is a quadric in
P_{t}
.
In other words, let

be the blow up of

along

.
Then
\pi
is a quadric bundle over
{\bf P}^{2}=T^{\prime}
,
the fiber of
\pi
over

is isomorphic to
Q_{t}
.

Any line in
Q_{t}
meets
T

and conversely any line

meeting

is contained in a
Q_{t}
.
Hence the (one or two) rulings of

give one or two rational curves in
E\subset F

and these curves can be mapped to

.
This map

factors over
a K3 double cover
K\rightarrow T^{\prime}
branched over sextic curve in
T^{\prime}
, cf.
[
Vo1
]
.

E\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,K\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny 2:1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,T^{\prime}\,\cong\,{\bf P}^{2}~.

The map

is a
{\bf P}^{1}
-fibration.
See Corollary
1.6
(and its proof) for this conic bundle, as well as
[

, §4]
,
and
[

for relations between the associated twisted derived category of

We summarize the discussion above in the following proposition.

5.3.3.
The BOSS bundle as divisor on a Hilbert square.

Let

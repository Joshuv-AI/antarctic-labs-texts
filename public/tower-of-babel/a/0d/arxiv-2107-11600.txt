Noether Currents and Maxwell-type Equations of Motion in Higher Derivative Gravity Theories
Taichiro Kugo Yukawa Institute for Theoretical Physics, Kyoto University, Kyoto 606-8502, Japan

Abstract
In general coordinate invariant gravity theories whose Lagrangians contain arbitrarily high order derivative fields, the Noether currents for the global translation and for the Nakanishi’s O ​ S ​ p ​ ( 8 | 8 ) {O\kern-1.1ptS\kern-0.39993ptp}(8|8) choral symmetry containing the BRS symmetry as its member, are constructed. We generally show that for each of those Noether currents a suitable linear combination of equations of motion can be brought into the form of Maxwell-type field equation possessing the Noether current as its source term.

Introduction

The equation of motion for the Yang-Mills field
A^{a}_{\mu}

in the covariant gauge is
given in the form

\displaystyle{}D^{\nu}F^{a}_{\ \mu\nu}+\partial_{\mu}B^{a}-igf^{a}{}_{bc}\partial_{\mu}\bar{c}^{b}\cdot c^{c}=gj^{a}_{\mu}\,,

(1.1)

where

D^{\nu}F^{a}_{\ \mu\nu}

F^{a}_{\ \mu\nu}

is the color current from the matter field, and

B^{a},c^{a}
and

are Nakanishi-Lautrup (NL), Faddeev-Popov (FP)
ghost and anti-ghost fields, respectively. This equation was first noted by
Ojima

1

\displaystyle{}\partial^{\nu}F^{a}{}_{\mu\nu}+\{Q_{\rm B},D_{\mu}\bar{c}^{a}\}=gJ^{a}_{\mu}\,.

(1.2)

Here,
Q_{\rm B}
is the BRS charge and
J^{a}_{\mu}
in the RHS is the Noether current
for the global gauge transformation
(
=
color rotation) under which all the gauge field
A_{\mu}^{a}
, NL and
FP ghost fields,
B^{a},c^{a},\bar{c}^{a}

given by

J^{a}_{\mu}=(A^{\nu}\times F_{\nu\mu})^{a}+j^{a}_{\mu}+(A_{\mu}\times B)^{a}-i(\bar{c}\times D_{\mu}c)^{a}+i(\partial_{\mu}\bar{c}\times c)^{a}\,,

(1.3)

with

(A\times B)^{a}\equiv f^{a}{}_{bc}A^{b}B^{c}
.
This form of YM field equation (
1.2
) is particular,
firstly in the simple divergence form for the field strength,

\partial_{\nu}F^{a\,\mu\nu}
, and secondly
in the BRS exact form for the NL and FP ghost contribution terms.

Even if we are much less ambitious than to make gravity theory
UV-complete, we still have several motivations to consider higher derivative
gravity theories.

From the low energy effective field theory viewpoint,
it is quite natural to consider the actions
containing higher and higher order derivative fields, successively,
from low to high energies. The
Einstein-Hilbert action is the lowest derivative order, and
the quadratic gravity actions are the next derivative order,
and so on.

Or, alternatively, one may simply wants a
gravity theory with

M

A simple momentum cut-off does not work here since it breaks the GC-invariance.
Pauli-Villars regulators respecting the GC-invariance can be supplied
by considering the covariant higher derivative terms. As noted by
Stelle, the gravity field propagator behaves like
\sim 1/p^{4}

renormalizable in 4D.
As regulators, however, to work sufficiently enough to make all the quantities
finite in 4D, the propagator must drop as fast as
\sim 1/p^{6}

would be supplied, for instance, by the quadratic
term of covariant quantities which contain
third order
derivatives of gravity field.

Those theorems were proved explicitly assuming the renormalizable QED and
Einstein gravity, respectively.
To prove the existence theorem generally, however, it is
necessary to have Maxwell-type gravity equation of
motion in any GC invariant system assuming no particular form of action.

\displaystyle=\overset{\kern-5.7361pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longleftarrow\kern-3.0pt\relbar\kern-3.0pt\longrightarrow}}}{\partial_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-1}}}\cdot\partial_{\alpha_{n}}+\overset{\kern-5.7361pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k=-1}}{{\longleftarrow\kern-3.0pt\relbar\kern-3.0pt\longrightarrow}}}{\partial_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}}}\ .

(2.23)

Then, as a generalization of the

\partial_{\mu}\Big[F^{\mu\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}}\overset{\kern-5.7361pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k}}{{\longleftarrow\kern-3.0pt\relbar\kern-3.0pt\longrightarrow}}}{\partial_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}}}\,G\Big]=F^{\mu\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}}\Bigl({}_{n+k+1}C_{k}\,\partial_{\mu\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}}-\overset{\kern-5.7361pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k-1}}{{\longleftarrow\kern-3.0pt\relbar\kern-3.0pt\longrightarrow}}}{\partial_{\mu\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}}}\Bigr)\,G\ .

and that of a single derivative

\overset{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k-1}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\partial_{\mu}}

\overset{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k-1}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\partial_{\mu}}=\partial_{\mu}+\overset{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k-2}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\partial_{\mu}}=k\partial_{\mu}+\overset{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-1}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}}{\partial_{\mu}}=k\partial_{\mu}-\overset{\leftarrow}{\partial_{\mu}}\,.

The first of this quantity

This can be seen from Eqs. (
2.19
) and (

) which
are rewritten by using the definition
of the
k

k=0
and
-1
, respectively, into

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}{\cal L}_{j}^{\,;\mu\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}}\,\overset{\kern-5.7361pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0}}{{\longleftarrow\kern-3.0pt\relbar\kern-3.0pt\longrightarrow}}}{\partial_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}}}\,G^{j}_{\rho}

\displaystyle=J^{\mu}_{\,\rho}+\delta^{\mu}_{\rho}{\cal L}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}{\cal L}_{j}^{\,;\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}}\,\overset{\kern-5.7361pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-1}}{{\longleftarrow\kern-3.0pt\relbar\kern-3.0pt\longrightarrow}}}{\partial_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}}}\,T^{j\mu}_{\ \,\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta S}{\delta\phi^{j}}T^{j\mu}_{\ \,\rho}\,.

(2.30)

Owing to the general formula (
2.24
), the two quantities,

{\cal J}_{k}
introduced here (
2.28
) and

{\cal K}_{k}
defined previously in Eq. (
2.11
),
satisfy the following recurrence relation:

{{\cal K}_{k}}^{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}\mu}{}_{\!\!\rho}-{{\cal J}_{k}}^{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}\mu}{}_{\!\!\rho}=\partial_{\tau}{{\cal J}_{k+1}}^{\tau\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}\mu}{}_{\!\!\rho}\,.

When applying the formula (
2.24
) to derive this equality,
we should note that the summation
over the set of
n+1
dummy indices
\{\tau,\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}\}
contained in
the RHS quantity

is identified with
the summation over the set
\{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n+1}\}
contained
in the LHS quantities

{{\cal K}_{k}}^{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}\mu}{}_{\!\!\rho}-{{\cal J}_{k}}^{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}\mu}{}_{\!\!\rho}

by identifying

as
\tau

existing in the summations

From this relation (

\displaystyle{{\cal J}_{k}}

\displaystyle={{\cal K}_{k}}-\partial{{\cal J}_{k+1}}={{\cal K}_{k}}-\partial{{\cal K}_{k+1}}+\partial^{2}{{\cal J}_{k+2}}

\displaystyle=\cdots=\sum_{\ell=0}^{K}(-\partial)^{\ell}{{\cal K}_{k+\ell}}+(-\partial)^{K+1}{{\cal J}_{k+K+1}}\ .

Since

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S}{\delta\phi^{j}}T^{j\mu}_{\ \,\rho}

\displaystyle=-J^{\mu}_{\,\rho}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-)^{k}\partial_{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}}{{\cal K}_{k}}^{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}\mu}{}_{\!\!\rho}\ .

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-)^{k}\partial_{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}}{{\cal K}_{k}}^{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}\mu}{}_{\!\!\rho}\equiv-\partial_{\nu}{\cal F}^{\nu\mu}_{\ \ \rho}

\partial_{\nu}{\cal F}^{\nu\mu}_{\ \ \rho}=\partial_{\nu}\widetilde{\cal F}^{\nu\mu}_{\ \ \rho}\,.

As noted before, the tensor

the first
k

\{\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{k}\}

k+1
indices

(2.37)

If we act
k
-ple divergence
\partial_{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}}
on this,

\nu_{j}
indices become dummy, and, since the manifest total symmetry among

k
indices of
{\cal K}_{k}

the
k

(2.39)

This means that
{\cal K}_{k+1}^{\ \,\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}\nu\mu}{}_{\!\!\rho}
can be
replaced by a
\nu\mu
anti-symmetric tensor which we can define as

\widetilde{\cal K}_{k+1}^{\ \,\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}\nu\mu}{}_{\!\!\rho}=\frac{k+1}{k+2}\left({\cal K}_{k+1}^{\ \,\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}\nu\mu}{}_{\!\!\rho}-{\cal K}_{k+1}^{\ \,\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}\mu\nu}{}_{\!\!\rho}\right)\ .

-ple divergence

Thus we find that the ‘field-strength’

in

\widetilde{\cal F}^{\nu\mu}_{\ \ \rho}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(-)^{k}\partial_{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}}{\widetilde{\cal K}_{k+1}}^{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}\nu\mu}{}_{\!\!\rho}\,.

(2.42)

With this antisymmetric field strength,
the gravity equation of motion is finally written in

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta S}{\delta\phi^{j}}T^{j\mu}_{\ \,\rho}

\displaystyle=J^{\mu}_{\,\rho}-\partial_{\nu}\widetilde{\cal F}^{\mu\nu}_{\ \ \rho}\,.

(2.43)

This is an equation for the gauge-unfixed classical system.

-proportional part contained in

-proportional part contributes to

, and obtain

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\cal K}_{k+1}}^{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}\nu\mu}{}_{\!\!\rho}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{k+1}{k+2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-k-1}{}_{n+k+1}C_{k+1}\,\left({\cal L}_{j}^{\,;\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{k}\nu\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}}\,\partial_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}}T^{j\mu}_{\ \ \rho}-(\nu\leftrightarrow\mu)\right)

\displaystyle\text{for}\quad k=0,1,2,\cdots\ .

(2.44)

3
Quantum theory with de Donder gauge

Let us now consider the quantum system.
We add the gauge-fixing and corresponding Faddeev-Popov(FP) term to
the classical GC invariant Lagrangian
{\cal L}_{\rm cl}
. (We call the
Lagrangian
{\cal L}
in the previous section

hereafter.)
We actually adopt Nakanishi’s simpler form of

{\cal L}_{{\rm GF}}+{\cal L}_{{\rm FP}}={\cal L}_{{\rm GF+FP}}

4

5

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{{\rm GF+FP}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{\rm N}(i\kappa^{-1}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\bar{c}_{\nu})=-\kappa^{-1}\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}b_{\nu}-i\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\cdot\partial_{\mu}\bar{c}_{\rho}\cdot\partial_{\nu}c^{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{\rm B}(i\kappa^{-1}\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\bar{c}_{\nu})-\partial_{\mu}\left(i\tilde{g}^{\lambda\nu}\partial_{\lambda}\bar{c}_{\nu}\cdot c^{\mu}\right),

-\kappa c^{\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{\rm B}\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{\rm N}\Phi-{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{\rm N}(x^{\lambda})\partial_{\lambda}\Phi,

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{\rm N}(x^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa c^{\lambda},\qquad{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{\rm N}\Phi=-\kappa\partial_{\mu}c^{\alpha}\cdot\bigl[\Phi\bigr]^{\mu}_{\alpha},

5
Conclusion

In this paper we considered the general GC invariant theory which contains
arbitrarily high order derivative fields. We identified there
the explicit expression for the energy-momentum as the Noether current
corresponding to the rigid case of GC transformation, and have shown that
a linear combination of the equations of motion can be rewritten into
the form of Maxwell-type field equation which has the total
energy-momentum as
its source. This was done both for gauge unfixed classical system and
for the gauge-fixed quantum system in the de Donder-Nakanishi gauge.
The Maxwell-type field equation in the latter has formally an additional
term coming from the gauge-fixing compared with that in the former, which
was shown to take a BRS-exact form just like in the well-known Yang-Mills case.

By using the same technique, we derived similar expressions of Maxwell-type
equations for the Noether currents for the
{I\kern-0.59998ptO\kern-1.1ptS\kern-0.39993ptp}(8|8)
choral symmetries.
It confirmed that the Nakanishi’s original result persists to any GC invariant
system.

These results will be useful for proving the existence
theorem
[
27
]

of graviton
(photon) in any GC (local
U(1)
) gauge invariant system as far as the
translation (global
U(1)
) symmetry is not spontaneously broken. We hope
we can report on this matter near future.

The techniques used for the higher derivative systems in this paper will also
be useful for studying more general problems. For instance, the basic
problems such
as the (non-)equivalence between canonical quantization and path-integral
quantization
may be discussed in a general fashion for the higher derivative systems.
This is so since the present technique can easily be combined with
Ostrogradsky’s canonical formalism for higher derivative
systems
[
30
]
.

A Lefschetz theorem for crystalline representations
Raju Krishnamoorthy, Jinbang Yang, Kang Zuo

Abstract
. As a corollary of nonabelian Hodge theory, Simpson proved a strong Lefschetz theorem for complex polarized variations of Hodge structure. We show an arithmetic analog. Our primary technique is p p -adic nonabelian Hodge theory. Conditional on certain foundational results in logarithmic p p -adic Hodge theory, we also show a logarithmic analog.

An easy corollary of Simpson’s nonabelian Hodge theorem is the following.

[

6.
Local constancy of Chern classes

To explain the local constancy of this section, we first need some preliminaries. Let
S
be a separated scheme and let
f\colon X\rightarrow S
be smooth projective morphism. Let
\ell
be invertible on
S
. Then the
\ell
-adic sheaf
R^{i}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j)
is a constructible, locally constant sheaf, i.e., a lisse
\ell
-adic sheaf, by the smooth base change theorem. Moreover, for any point
s\in S
, the proper base change theorem implies that the natural morphism
R^{i}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j)_{s}\rightarrow R^{i}(f_{s})_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j)
is an isomorphism. In particular, if
\xi\in H^{0}(S,R^{i}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j))
then it is automatically “locally constant”. The following proposition is surely well-known. As we could not find a reference, we document a proof.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
S
be a separated irreducible noetherian scheme. Let
f\colon X\rightarrow S
be a smooth proper morphism. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle on
X
. If
\overline{s}
is a geometric point such that the
\ell
-adic Chern classes of
\mathcal{E}_{\overline{s}}
vanish, then the Chern classes of
\mathcal{E}
vanish at every geometric point.

Proof.

As discussed above, the result directly follows if we show that

•

for every geometric point
\bar{s}
of
S
and non-negative integers
i,j
, the natural map
H^{0}(S,R^{i}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j))\rightarrow H^{i}(X_{\bar{s}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j))
is injective; and

•

the Chern classes live in
H^{0}(S,R^{i}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j))
for appropriate
i,j
.

We first explain how to show the former. As explained in the preamble, by a combination of the smooth and proper base change theorems, the map

R^{i}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j)_{\bar{s}}\rightarrow R^{i}(f_{\bar{s}})_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j)

is an isomorphism of lisse
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-sheaves on

. On the other hand, by the equivalence of local systems and representations of
\pi_{1}
(which, as

is noetherian, by definition reduces to the torsion case

Sta20
, 0DV4]

is precisely the set of
\pi_{1}(S,\bar{s})
-invariants of
R^{i}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j)_{\bar{s}}
. By the explicit construction of the equivalence of categories, the induced map

H^{0}(S,R^{i}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j))\cong H^{i}(X_{\bar{s}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j))^{\pi_{1}(S,\bar{s})}\rightarrow H^{i}(X_{\bar{s}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(j))

is simply the inclusion of the invariants.

The second bullet point amounts to
defining
Chern classes in this level of generality. Again, this is well-known, but we indicate how to do this.
The key is the

; see, e.g.,
[
Sta20
, 02UK]
for a reference (in the context of Chow groups). Let

be the associated full flag scheme; then the following two properties hold:

•

The induced map

is injective.

•

The vector bundle
f^{*}\mathcal{E}
has a filtration whose subquotients are line bundles.

By the splitting principle, it suffices to construct the

for line bundles in the appropriate cohomology group. Let

be a line bundle on

gives a class in

. Consider the Leray spectral sequence:

By the low-degree terms of the Leray spectral sequence, there is a natural exact sequence

(See e.g.

This map is compatible with base change and hence agrees with the notion of

∎

Abstract
. Let C C be a curve and V → C V\to C an orthogonal vector bundle of rank r r . For r ≤ 6 r\leq 6 , the structure of V V can be described using tensor, symmetric and exterior products of bundles of lower rank, essentially due to the existence of exceptional isomorphisms between Spin ⁡ ( r , ℂ ) \mathrm{Spin}(r,{\mathbb{C}}) and other groups for these r r . We analyze these structures in detail, and in particular use them to describe moduli spaces of orthogonal bundles. Furthermore, the locus of isotropic vectors in V V defines a quadric subfibration Q V ⊂ ℙ ​ V Q_{V}\subset{\mathbb{P}}V . Using familiar results on quadrics of low dimension, we exhibit isomorphisms between isotropic Quot schemes of V V and certain ordinary Quot schemes of line subbundles. In particular, for r ≤ 6 r\leq 6 this gives a method for enumerating the isotropic subbundles of maximal degree of a general V V , when there are finitely many.

1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a complex projective smooth curve of genus
g\geq 2
, and
L\to C
a line bundle. A vector bundle
V\to C
is said to be
L
-valued orthogonal
if there is a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\sigma\colon V^{\otimes 2}\to L
. Like symplectic bundles (defined similarly, but with the form being skew-symmetric), orthogonal bundles are interesting examples of decorated vector bundles. Moreover, they are associated bundles of principal
G
-bundles for
G
an orthogonal group
\mathrm{SO}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
,
\mathrm{O}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
or
\mathrm{GO}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
, and provide useful tools and intuition for questions regarding these principal bundles and their moduli. See for example
[
Bea08
]
,
[
BG10
]
,
[
CH14
]
,
[
CH15
]
,
[
Serm12
]
,
[
CCH21b
]
and many other works.

Orthogonal bundles and their moduli enjoy remarkable geometric properties. One reason for this is that the group
\mathrm{SO}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
is not simply connected as
\mathrm{SL}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
and
\mathrm{Sp}(2n,{\mathbb{C}})
are, but has fundamental group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
and universal covering given by the spin group
\mathrm{Spin}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
. The point of departure for the present work is the fact that for
3\leq r\leq 6
, there are exceptional isomorphisms between
\mathrm{Spin}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
and other familiar groups:

\begin{array}[]{c||c|c|c|c}r&3&4&5&6\\
\hline\cr\mathrm{Spin}(r,{\mathbb{C}})&\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})&\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})\times\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})&\mathrm{Sp}(4,{\mathbb{C}})&\mathrm{SL}(4,{\mathbb{C}})\end{array}

Moreover, in each case there are interesting geometric interpretations for the map
\mathrm{Spin}(r,{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathrm{SO}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
; see for example
[
FH91
, Lectures 18–19]
. Taking a cue from the existence of these isomorphisms, we describe the structures of orthogonal bundles of rank
r\leq 6
using sum and tensor operations on bundles of lower rank. This extends the classification in
[
Mum71
, p. 185–187]
of
\mathrm{SO}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
-bundles for
r\leq 4
. Of particular interest is the fact that topologically, orthogonal bundles of rank
r
with trivial determinant are distinguished by another topological invariant, the second Stiefel–Whitney class
w_{2}(V)\in H^{2}(C,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
. This was characterized in
[
Serre90
]
in terms of theta characteristics of
C
, and we discuss it further in §
2.3
. Here is an overview of the structure theorems for the case
L={{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
and
\det(V)\cong{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
and
w_{2}(V)=0
. (The statements for the other cases are given at the relevant points in the paper; compare also with
[
Mum71
, pp. 185-187]
.)

\begin{array}[]{c|cc}\hbox{Rank}&\hbox{Structure}&\\
\hline\cr 2&N\oplus N^{-1}:&N\in\mathrm{Pic}(C)\\
3&\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E\otimes(\det E)^{-1}:&\hbox{$E$ of rank two}\\
4&E_{1}\otimes E_{2}:&\hbox{$E_{1}$, $E_{2}$ of rank two, $\det E_{2}\cong(\det E_{1})^{-1}$}\\
5&\mathrm{Ker}(\wedge^{2}W_{1}\to M):&\hbox{$M$ a square root of ${{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}$, and}\\
&&\hbox{$W_{1}$ a rank four $M$-valued symplectic bundle}\\
6&\wedge^{2}W:&\hbox{$W$ of rank four and trivial determinant}\end{array}

These identifications imply in particular the existence of natural maps between certain moduli spaces of bundles over
C
, which are described in the course of the paper.

An important point is that when
r=2n
is even, our proofs rely on the existence of a rank
n
isotropic subbundle in
V
. In
[
CCH21b
, § 2]
, it was shown that this is equivalent to the condition
\det V\cong L^{n}
(in general, the isomorphism
V\xrightarrow{\sim}V^{*}\otimes L
only gives
(\det V)^{2}\cong L^{2n}
). Moreover, in
[
CH14
]
and
[
CH15
]
it was shown that when
L={{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}=\det V
, the invariant
w_{2}(V)
coincides with the parity of the degrees of the rank
n
isotropic subbundles of
V
. In Lemma
3.2
and Theorem
2.3
we offer more elementary proofs of these results.

Turning our attention to the “quadric fibrations” in the title: The locus of isotropic lines in fibers of an orthogonal bundle
V
is a subfibration
Q_{V}\subset{\mathbb{P}}V
in smooth quadric hypersurfaces, giving
{\mathbb{P}}V
the structure of an “enveloping bundle” (see §
2.5
). For
r\leq 6
, these quadrics are very well understood; see for example
[
GH78
]
:

\begin{array}[]{c||c|c|c|c|c}r&2&3&4&5&6\\
\hline\cr\hbox{Quadric}&\hbox{Two smooth points}&\hbox{Conic }{\mathbb{P}}^{1}&{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}&\mathrm{LG}(2,4)&\mathrm{Gr}(2,4)\end{array}

Here
\mathrm{LG}(2,4)\subset\mathrm{Gr}(2,4)
is the Lagrangian Grassmannian of two-dimensional subspaces of
{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
which are isotropic with respect to a fixed nondegenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form. We use well-known properties of these quadrics to analyze the structures of orthogonal bundles, and in particular their isotropic subbundles. For
3\leq r\leq 6
, it turns out that the isotropic Quot scheme
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{m,e}(V)
, which parameterizes isotropic subbundles of rank
m
and degree
e
in
V
, is isomorphic to a union of Quot schemes of subbundles of certain bundles of lower rank. This allows us in particular to enumerate the maximal isotropic subbundles of a general orthogonal bundle of rank
r\leq 6
, when the number is finite. This gives an analog for low rank orthogonal bundles of the enumeration results in
[
Hol04
]
for maximal degree subbundles of a general vector bundle, and in
[
CCH21a
]
for maximal degree Lagrangian subbundles of a general symplectic bundle.

Some results in the paper are proven in or would follow easily from
[
CCH21b
]
,
[
CH14
]
and
[
CH15
]
. However, our policy is, whenever possible, to give arguments which are elementary and/or use the rich and familiar geometry of the low rank situation.

The paper is organized as follows. We begin by recalling background material on orthogonal bundles and their moduli, isotropic subbundles and isotropic Quot schemes, and quadric subfibrations of projective bundles. Then we give a detailed treatment of the rank two case. This is relatively well known and straightforward, but provides a paradigm for the other cases; moreover, the rank two case has some useful corollaries for higher rank bundles. We then turn to the higher rank cases. It emerges that the results in rank three and five follow naturally from special cases of rank four and six respectively. Therefore, we treat the latter first in each case.

For the present, as mentioned above, we have restricted ourselves in the even rank cases to orthogonal bundles which admit isotropic subbundles of half rank. The structure of bundles not possessing any such subbundle is of interest, and amenable to study by similar methods. This topic will be studied in a future project.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Orthogonal bundles and isotropic subbundles

Here we recall background material on orthogonal bundles. For more information, we refer to
[
Bea08
]
,
[
Mum71
]
,
[
Ram81
]
and
[
CCH21b
]
.

Let
C
be a complex projective smooth curve, and
L\to C
a line bundle. A vector bundle
V\to C
of rank
r
is said to be
L
-valued orthogonal
if there is a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\sigma\colon V^{\otimes 2}\to L
; equivalently, if there is a symmetric isomorphism
V\xrightarrow{\sim}V^{*}\otimes L
.

In particular, for such a
V
we have
(\det V)^{2}\cong L^{r}
. It follows that if
r=2n+1
is odd,

(2.1)

\deg(L)\hbox{ is even, and}\det V\cong M^{2n+1}\hbox{ for a uniquely determined $M$}\\
\hbox{such that }M^{2}\cong L.

On the other hand, if
r=2n
is even, then

(2.2)

\det V\ \cong\ L^{n}\otimes\eta\hbox{ for some $\eta$ satisfying }\eta^{2}\ \cong\ {{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}.

The question of which
\eta
arise in (
2.2
) is linked to the existence of certain isotropic subbundles, which we now discuss.

Let
V
be
L
-valued orthogonal of rank
r
. For any subbundle
E\subset V
, the isomorphism
V\xrightarrow{\sim}V^{*}\otimes L
induces a sequence
0\to E^{\perp}\to V\to E^{*}\otimes L\to 0
, where

E^{\perp}\ :=\ \{v\in V:\sigma(v\otimes e)=0\hbox{ for all }e\in E\}

is the orthogonal complement of
E
with respect to
\sigma
. A subbundle
E\subset V
is
isotropic
if
E\subseteq E^{\perp}
; equivalently, if
\sigma(E\otimes E)=0
.

Suppose
\mathrm{rk}\,(V)=2n
and
V
contains an isotropic subbundle
E
of rank
n
. Then
E=E^{\perp}
and
V
is an extension
0\to E\to V\to E^{*}\otimes L\to 0
. In particular,

(2.3)

if
V
admits a rank
n
isotropic subbundle, then
\det V\cong L^{n}
;

that is,
\eta={{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
in (
2.2
). In fact, by
[
CCH21b
, Lemma 2.5]
the converse of (
2.3
) also holds; in Proposition
3.2
, we shall offer an elementary proof of this fact.

Moreover, in general, not every extension
0\to E\to V\to E^{*}\otimes L\to 0
arises from an orthogonal structure. We shall use the following characterization
[
Hit07
, Criterion 2.1]
.

Criterion 2.1
.

Let
0\to E\to V\to E^{*}\otimes L\to 0
be an extension of vector bundles, with class
\delta(V)\in H^{1}(C,E\otimes E\otimes L^{-1})
. Then
V
admits an orthogonal structure with respect to which
E
is isotropic if and only if, up to the action of
\mathrm{Aut}\,(E)\times\mathrm{Aut}\,(E^{*}\otimes L)
, the extension class
\delta(V)
belongs to
H^{1}(C,\wedge^{2}E\otimes L^{-1})
.

2.2.
Moduli of orthogonal bundles

For any line bundle
N
, let
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(r,N)
be the moduli space of S-equivalence classes of semistable vector bundles of rank
r
and determinant
N
over
C
. As this is noncanonically isomorphic to the moduli space of semistable principal
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-bundles over
C
, by
[
Rth96
, Theorem 5.9]
it is an irreducible projective variety of dimension
(r^{2}-1)(g-1)
. We consider the following closed subloci of
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(r,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
and
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(r,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(nx))
for a fixed
x\in C
.

•

{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(2n,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
, the moduli space of semistable
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
-valued orthogonal bundles of rank
2n
and trivial determinant

•

{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(2n,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x))
, the moduli space of semistable
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
-valued orthogonal bundles of rank
2n
and determinant
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(nx)

•

{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(2n+1,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
, the moduli space of semistable
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
-valued orthogonal bundles of rank
2n+1
and trivial determinant

We shall use the notation
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(r,N)_{\mathrm{st}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(r,L)_{\mathrm{st}}
when we wish to restrict to the stable loci. We write
F\sim F^{\prime}
to indicate that
F
and
F^{\prime}
are S-equivalent semistable vector bundles.

Let us explain why we consider only these three kinds of moduli space. Firstly, when
r=2n+1
, any
L
-valued orthogonal bundle is a twist by a line bundle of one which is
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
-valued with trivial determinant. When
r=2n
, any
L
-valued orthogonal bundle is a twist by a line bundle of either an
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
-valued or an
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
-valued orthogonal bundle. Furthermore, we shall consider only
L
-valued orthogonal bundles which admit rank
n
isotropic subbundles. Hence in view of (
2.3
), we get the restriction on the determinant as above for
r=2n
.

The following seems to be well known, but we include a proof for completeness.

Lemma 2.2
.

Every component of the moduli spaces above is of dimension
\frac{1}{2}r(r-1)(g-1)
, where
r
is the rank.

Proof.

Recall that the
conformal orthogonal group

\mathrm{GO}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
is the image in
\mathrm{GL}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
of the multiplication map
\mathrm{O}(r,{\mathbb{C}})\times{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\to\mathrm{GL}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
. As explained in
[
BG10
, § 2]
, the moduli space of rank
2n
vector bundles admitting an
L
-valued symmetric form for some
L
of degree
\ell
is the image of certain components of the moduli space of semistable
\mathrm{GO}(2n,{\mathbb{C}})
-bundles by the forgetful map to
{\mathcal{U}}\left(r,\frac{r\ell}{2}\right)
. Furthermore, by
[
BS02
, Theorem 8.5 and Remark 8.6]
, this map is finite. Thus by
[
Rth96
, Theorem 5.9]
, this image has dimension

(g-1)\cdot\dim\mathrm{GO}(r,{\mathbb{C}})+\dim Z(\mathrm{GO}(r,{\mathbb{C}}))\ =\ \left(\frac{1}{2}r(r-1)+1\right)(g-1)+1.

Fixing
\ell
and
L
, we obtain a locus of codimension
g
; that is, dimension
\frac{1}{2}r(r-1)(g-1)
as desired. ∎

2.3.
The second Stiefel–Whitney class

As will be discussed later, the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(r,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x))
is irreducible. However,
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(r,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
is in general reducible, due to the presence of another discrete invariant for orthogonal bundles which we shall now discuss.

Firstly, we dispose of a special case. The group
\mathrm{SO}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
via the map

z\ \mapsto\ \begin{pmatrix}\frac{z+1/z}{2}&\frac{-z+1/z}{2\sqrt{-1}}\\
\frac{z-1/z}{2\sqrt{-1}}&\frac{z+1/z}{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Hence it has infinite cyclic fundamental group, unlike
\mathrm{SO}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
for
r\geq 3
. Therefore, during this section we shall assume that
r\geq 3
.

For
r\geq 3
, let
1\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\to\mathrm{Spin}(r,{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathrm{SO}(r,{\mathbb{C}})\to 1
be the exact sequence associated to the universal covering of
\mathrm{SO}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
; see for example
[
FH91
, Lecture 20]
. By for example
[
Gro58
, Chapter 5]
, this gives rise to a sequence of nonabelian cohomology sets

\cdots\ \to\ H^{1}(C,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})\ \to\ H^{1}(C,\mathrm{Spin}(r,{\mathbb{C}}))\ \to\ H^{1}(C,\mathrm{SO}(r,{\mathbb{C}}))\ \to\ H^{2}(C,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}).

Choosing a set of transition functions for
V
defines an element of
H^{1}(C,\mathrm{SO}(r,{\mathbb{C}}))
. Then
w_{2}(V)
is the image of this element in
H^{2}(C,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
. By
[
Gro58
, Proposition 5.7.2]
, the class
w_{2}(V)
is trivial if and only if
V
is obtained from a
\mathrm{Spin}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
-bundle by the extension of structure group given by
\mathrm{Spin}(r,{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathrm{SO}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
. For
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, we denote by
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{i}(r,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
the component of
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(r,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
consisting of bundles with
w_{2}(V)=i\in\{0,1\}
.

The class
w_{2}(V)
has several properties. Firstly, by
[
Serre90
, Theorem 2]
, for every theta characteristic
\kappa
on
C
we have

(2.4)

w_{2}(V)\ \equiv\ h^{0}(V\otimes\kappa)+r\cdot h^{0}(\kappa)\mod 2.

Furthermore, it is shown in
[
CH14
]
and
[
CH15
]
that
w_{2}(V)
coincides with the parity of the degree of any rank
n
isotropic subbundle of
V
, where
n=\left\lfloor\frac{\mathrm{rk}\,(V)}{2}\right\rfloor
. We now offer a more elementary proof of this fact.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
V
be an
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank
2n
or
2n+1
and trivial determinant. If
F\subset V
is a rank
n
isotropic subbundle, then
w_{2}(V)\equiv\deg(F)\mod 2
. In particular, all rank
n
isotropic subbundles of
V
have degree of the same parity.

Caution 2.4
.

Note that we have not proven that
V
has any such subbundle
F
. This will be shown in Proposition
3.2
.

Proof.

Suppose firstly that
r=2n
. By hypothesis and using Criterion
2.1
, there is an exact sequence
0\to F\to V\to F^{*}\to 0
such that the cohomology class
[V]
belongs to
H^{1}(C,\wedge^{2}F)
. For any theta characteristic
\kappa
, this yields a cohomology sequence

(2.5)

0\ \to\ H^{0}(C,F\otimes\kappa)\ \to\ H^{0}(C,V\otimes\kappa)\ \to\ H^{0}(C,F^{*}\otimes\kappa)\ \xrightarrow{\cup[V]}\ H^{1}(C,F\otimes\kappa)\ \to\ \cdots.

Now by Serre duality, there is an identification
H^{1}(C,F\otimes\kappa)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{0}(C,F^{*}\otimes\kappa)^{*}
. We claim that the map
\cup[V]
is antisymmetric. To see this: It is well known that the cup product map
\cup\colon H^{1}(C,F\otimes F)\to\mathrm{Hom}\left(H^{0}(C,F^{*}\otimes\kappa),H^{1}(C,F\otimes\kappa)\right)
is dual to the Petri map

\mu\colon H^{0}(C,F^{*}\otimes\kappa)^{\otimes 2}\ \to\ H^{0}(C,F^{*}\otimes F^{*}\otimes K_{C}).

Thus we must show that

(2.6)

\left\langle[V],\mu\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}H^{0}(C,F^{*}\otimes\kappa)\right)\right\rangle\ =\ 0,

the pairing being defined by Serre duality. But since

\mu\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}H^{0}(C,F^{*}\otimes\kappa)\right)\ \subseteq\ H^{0}(C,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}F^{*}\otimes K_{C})

while
[V]\in H^{1}(C,\wedge^{2}F)\cong H^{0}(C,\wedge^{2}F^{*}\otimes K_{C})^{*}
, we obtain (
2.6
).

By the above claim, the map
\cup[V]
in (
2.5
) has even rank. We write

h\ :=\ h^{0}(C,F^{*}\otimes\kappa)\ =\ h^{1}(C,F\otimes\kappa).

Moreover,
\chi(F\otimes\kappa)=\deg(F)
, so
h^{0}(C,F\otimes\kappa)=\deg(F)+h
. By exactness of (
2.5
), then,

h^{0}(C,V\otimes\kappa)\ =\ h^{0}(C,F\otimes\kappa)+\dim\mathrm{Ker}(\cup[V])\ =\ \left(\deg(F)+h\right)+\left(h-\mathrm{rk}\,(\cup[V])\right).

Since
\mathrm{rk}\,(\cup[V])
is even,
h^{0}(C,V\otimes\kappa)\equiv\deg(F)\mod 2
. The statement now follows from (
2.4
), since
r=2n
.

Suppose now that
\mathrm{rk}\,(V)=2n+1
. We use a technique applied frequently in
[
CH15
]
and
[
CCH21b
, § 8]
, which will appear several times in this paper. The orthogonal direct sum
V\perp{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
is
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
-valued orthogonal of rank
2n+2
and trivial determinant. Let
E
be any rank
n
isotropic subbundle of
V
. Using for example a local trivialization, we can complete
E
to a rank
n+1
isotropic subbundle
\overline{E}
of
V
in two distinct ways, each one an extension
0\to E\to\overline{E}\to{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}\to 0
. Thus
\deg(E)=\deg(\overline{E})
. It follows by the even rank case above that

all rank
n
isotropic subbundles of
V
have parity equal to
w_{2}(V\perp{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
.

On the other hand, an easy computation with (
2.4
) shows that
w_{2}(V)=w_{2}(V\perp{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
.
The statement for
r=2n+1
now follows. ∎

2.4.
Isotropic Quot schemes

For any bundle
W
, we have the Quot scheme

\mathrm{Quot}^{\mathrm{rk}\,(W)-m,\deg(W)-d}(W)

parameterizing coherent quotients of
W
of rank
\mathrm{rk}\,(W)-m
and degree
\deg(W)-d
; equivalently, locally free subsheaves of
W
of rank
m
and degree
d
. We depart from convention and denote this by
\mathrm{Quot}_{m,d}(W)
. We denote the open subset of saturated subsheaves by
\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{m,d}(W)
.

If
V
is an orthogonal bundle of rank
2n
or
2n+1
, then for
1\leq m\leq n
we consider the closed sublocus

\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{m,e}(V)\ :=\ \left\{[j\colon E\hookrightarrow V]\in\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{m,e}(V):\hbox{$j(E)$ isotropic of rank $m$ and degree $e$}\right\}.

Let us say a little more about isotropic subbundles of maximal rank
n
. By
[
CCH21b
, Proposition 3.3]
, the expected dimension of
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{n,e}(V)
is

(2.7)

\begin{cases}-(n-1)e-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}(g-1-\ell)&\hbox{ if }r=2n;\\
-n\left(e+\frac{\ell}{2}\right)-\frac{n(n+1)}{2}(g-1-\ell)&\hbox{ if }r=2n+1.\end{cases}

Note that when
r=2n
and
\ell
is even,
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{n,e}(V)
is in general disconnected, by
[
CCH21b
, Theorem 1.4 (a)]
. In Corollary
4.7
and Corollary
6.10
, we shall see this concretely for
r=4
and
r=6
respectively.

Zero dimensional Quot schemes

Of particular interest for us will be the question of enumerating the points of
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{n,e}(V)
for a general
V
when this scheme is finite. This corresponds to the maximal value of
e
for which
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{n,e}(V)
is nonempty. The analogous question for maximal subbundles of vector bundles was answered in
[
Hol04
, Theorem 4.2]
, and for maximal Lagrangian subbundles of symplectic bundles in
[
CCH21a
]
. We quote a special case
[
Hol04
, Corollary 4.3]
which we shall use later (see also
[
Oxb00
, Lemma 2.2]
).

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
W
be a general stable bundle of rank
r
. If
\deg(W)\equiv 1-g\mod r
then
W
has
r^{g}
maximal line subbundles of degree
\frac{1}{r}\left(\deg(W)-(r-1)(g-1)\right)
.

Using (
2.7
), we now decide which
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{n,e}(V)
have expected dimension zero. The following can be viewed as a special case of
[
CCH21b
, Theorem 1.4 and 1.5]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Write
\ell:=\deg(L)
. We assume that
\ell\in\{0,\frac{r}{2}\}
when
r
is even, and
\ell=0
when
r
is odd. Assume that
V
is
L
-valued orthogonal of rank
r
and general in moduli. In the range
3\leq r\leq 6
, the scheme
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{n,e}(V)
has expected dimension zero
if the following conditions hold:

\begin{array}[]{|r|c|}\hline\cr r=3&e=-g+1\\
\hline\cr r=4&e=\ell-g+1\\
\hline\cr r=5&g\text{ is odd and }e=-\frac{3}{2}(g-1)\\
\hline\cr r=6&\ell-g\text{ is odd and }e=\frac{3}{2}(\ell-g+1)\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

In these cases, each component (that is, point) of
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{n,e}(V)
corresponds to an isotropic subbundle of
V
of rank
\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor
and degree
e
. Later we shall give enumerative results on the number of such isotropic subbundles of rank
m
, for
1\leq m\leq n
.

2.5.
Quadric fibrations

Finally, we discuss a certain quadric subfibration
Q_{V}\subset{\mathbb{P}}V
for an orthogonal bundle
V
. This is closely related to the notion of an enveloping bundle as studied in
[
LM20
]
and
[
LMP15
]
, to which we refer the reader for more information. Note however that our situation is slightly different, as we begin with the vector bundle
V
instead of the quadric fibration
Q_{V}
; and we do not consider
{\mathbb{P}}V
or
Q_{V}
in a projective embedding, but only
Q_{V}
relatively embedded in
{\mathbb{P}}V
over
C
.

Let
V\to C
be a vector bundle of rank
r\geq 2
, and
\pi\colon{\mathbb{P}}V\to C
the associated projective bundle. Let
\sigma
be a nonzero element of
H^{0}(C,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V^{*}\otimes L)\cong H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}V,{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}V}(2)\otimes\pi^{*}L)
. We denote by
Q_{V}
the divisor
(\sigma)\subset{\mathbb{P}}V
. It is a quadric fibration over
C
. If we further assume that
\sigma|_{p}
defines a symmetric isomorphism
V|_{p}\to(V^{*}\otimes L)|_{p}
for each fiber, then
\sigma
defines an
L
-valued orthogonal structure on
V
, and
Q_{V}\to C
is a fibration in
smooth
quadrics. (This was observed for
r=3
and
C={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
in
[
LM20
, Remark 1, p. 9]
.) In this case,
Q_{V}\subset{\mathbb{P}}V
parameterizes lines in fibers of
V
which are isotropic with respect to the quadratic form
\sigma
.

A useful application of the above is:

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank
r\geq 3
. Then
V
admits an isotropic line subbundle.

Proof.

The fibers of
Q_{V}
are quadric hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{r-1}
, so are rationally connected for
r\geq 3
. Thus by
[
GHS03
]
, there is a section
C\to Q_{V}\cong\mathrm{OG}(1,V)
, defining an isotropic line subbundle of
V
. ∎

3.
Rank two

We now consider orthogonal bundles of rank two. These results are straightforward and can be obtained without mentioning quadric fibrations, but we do this as practice for the higher rank cases. Firstly, we give a variation on
[
CCH21b
, Proposition 2.10]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank two and determinant
L
. Then

V\cong N\oplus N^{-1}L

for some line bundle
N
. Moreover,
V
has precisely two isotropic subbundles, which are isomorphic to
N
and
N^{-1}L
respectively.

Proof.

Let
N
be a line subbundle of maximal degree in
V
. Then the orthogonal form on
V
induces the surjective bundle map
V\to N^{-1}L
whose kernel is
N^{\perp}
. Thus we have
N^{\perp}\cong N
.

If
N
is isotropic, Criterion
2.1
shows that
V
is isomorphic to an extension
0\to N\to V\to N^{-1}L\to 0
determined by a class in
H^{1}(C,L^{-1}\otimes\wedge^{2}N)
. As
\wedge^{2}N=0
, we have
V\cong N\oplus N^{-1}L
.

If
N
is not isotropic, then

N\oplus N^{\perp}
is a rank two subsheaf of
V
. Thus in fact
N\oplus N
is a subsheaf of
V
. As
\deg(N)
was assumed to be maximal, this does not drop rank anywhere, so
V\cong N\oplus N
. Since
\det V\cong L
, we have
N\cong N^{-1}L
. In particular,
E\cong N\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{\oplus 2}
, where
N
is an orthogonal line subbundle. Hence by choosing a copy of
N\subset V
whose fiber is isotropic, we can also write
E\cong N\oplus N^{-1}L
, with respect to which the summands are the isotropic subbundles.
∎

We now investigate the structure of
{\mathbb{P}}V
as an enveloping bundle, where
V
is
L
-valued orthogonal of rank two and determinant
L
. Here the locus
Q_{V}
of isotropic lines in
{\mathbb{P}}V
is a fibration in smooth zero-dimensional quadrics; that is, pairs of points. By Theorem
3.1
, we have

Q_{V}\ =\ {\mathbb{P}}N\sqcup{\mathbb{P}}(N^{-1}L)\ \cong\ C\sqcup C.

An isotropic line subbundle corresponds to a section of
{\mathbb{P}}V
belonging to
C\sqcup C
at each point; that is, one of the copies of
C
. Thus
\mathrm{IQ}_{1,e}(V)^{\circ}
is empty unless
e=\deg(N)
or
e=\deg(N^{-1}L)
. (If
e=\deg(N)=\frac{\ell}{2}
then it consists of two points.)

As applications of our results so far, and to make the present paper more self-contained, we offer alternative proofs of some results in
[
CCH21b
]
and
[
CH15
]
which we shall require later.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank
2n
. Then
V
admits an isotropic subbundle of rank
n
if and only if
\det V\cong L^{n}
.

Proof.

(See also
[
CCH21b
, Lemma 2.5]
.) The “only if” implication is (
2.3
). For “if”, we proceed by induction on
n
. The case
n=1
follows from Theorem
3.1
. Suppose
n\geq 2
. By Proposition
2.7
, we can find an isotropic line subbundle
H\subset V
. Then
H^{\perp}/H
is
L
-valued orthogonal of rank
2n-2
. From the exact sequence
0\to H^{\perp}\to V\to H^{-1}L\to 0
, we deduce that

\det\left(H^{\perp}/H\right)\ \cong\ \det(H^{\perp})\otimes H^{-1}\ \cong\ (\det V)\otimes H\otimes L^{-1}\otimes H^{-1}\ \cong\ (\det V)\otimes L^{-1}\ \cong\ L^{n-1}.

By induction,
H^{\perp}/H
has an isotropic subbundle
\overline{E}
of rank
n-1
. The inverse image of
\bar{E}
in
H^{\perp}\subset V
is a rank
n
isotropic subbundle of
V
. ∎

We now give an analog of Theorem
2.3
for the odd degree case (compare with
[
CCH21b
, Theorem 1.4 (b)]
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
\deg(L)
is odd, and let
V
be
L
-valued orthogonal of rank
2n
. Suppose
V
satisfies the equivalent conditions of Proposition
3.2
. Then
V
has isotropic rank
n
subbundles both of even degree and of odd degree.

Proof.

Let
E\subset V
be a rank
n
isotropic subbundle. Let
H\subset E
be any rank
n-1
subbundle, which is necessarily isotropic. Then
H^{\perp}/H
is
L
-valued orthogonal of rank two and determinant
L
, and admits
E/H
as an isotropic line subbundle. By Theorem
3.1
, in fact

\frac{H^{\perp}}{H}\ \cong\ \frac{E}{H}\oplus\left(\left(\frac{E}{H}\right)^{*}\otimes L\right).

Then
(E/H)^{*}\otimes L
is of the form
F/H
for a rank
n
isotropic subbundle
F\subset H^{\perp}\subset V
, and

\deg(F)\ =\ -(\deg(E)-\deg H)+\deg(L)+\deg(H)\ \equiv\ \deg(E)+\deg(L)\mod 2.

As
\deg(L)
is assumed to be odd,
\deg(F)
and
\deg(E)
have opposite parity. ∎

4.
Rank four

We now turn our attention to orthogonal bundles of rank four, as the rank three case turns out to follow from a special case of rank four.

4.1.
Rank four orthogonal bundles with rank two isotropic subbundles

As noted in
[
Mum71
, p. 186]
, given two bundles
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
of rank two, the pairing

(4.1)

(E_{1}\otimes E_{2})\otimes(E_{1}\otimes E_{2})\ \to\ \wedge^{2}E_{1}\otimes\wedge^{2}E_{2}\ =:\ L

defines an
L
-valued orthogonal structure on
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
. Furthermore, for any line subbundles
N_{1}\subset E_{1}
and
N_{2}\subset E_{2}
, the subbundles
N_{1}\otimes E_{2}
and
E_{1}\otimes N_{2}
are isotropic of rank two. We shall now see that this induces finite surjective morphisms of moduli spaces.

Theorem 4.1
.

(a)

The association
(F_{1},F_{2})\mapsto F_{1}\otimes F_{2}
defines finite surjective morphisms

\Theta_{4}^{0}\colon{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})\times{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})\ \to\ {\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{0}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})

and

\Theta_{4}^{1}\colon{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x))\times{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(-x))\ \to\ {\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{1}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}).

In particular,
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
has two irreducible components.

(b)

For
L\cong{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
: The association
(F_{1},F_{2})\mapsto F_{1}\otimes F_{2}
defines a finite surjective morphism

\Phi_{4}\colon{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})\times{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x))\to{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x))

In particular,
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x))
is irreducible.

Proof.

Let us firstly define the morphism
\Theta_{4}^{0}
. As
{\mathbb{C}}
has characteristic zero,
F_{1}\otimes F_{2}
is semistable if
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are so. Therefore, passing to a suitable étale cover, we can define
\Theta_{4}^{0}
on the open subset
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}\times{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}
using a Poincaré bundle. Note that by Theorem
2.3
, the images of
\Theta_{4}^{0}
and
\Theta_{4}^{1}
are contained in
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{0}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
and
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{1}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
, respectively.

Now it is straightforward to check that if
F_{1}\sim F_{1}^{\prime}
and
F_{2}\sim F_{2}^{\prime}
, then
F_{1}\otimes F_{2}\sim F_{1}^{\prime}\otimes F_{2}^{\prime}
. Hence, as any semistable bundle is a limit of stable ones, it follows that
\Theta_{4}^{0}
has a continuous extension to
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})\times{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
. Now since
\Theta_{4}^{0}
is algebraic on
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}\times{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}
, it is bounded on a neighborhood of any point. Therefore, by the Riemann extension theorem, the continuous extension to
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})\times{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
given by the association
(F_{1},F_{2})\mapsto F_{1}\otimes F_{2}
is a morphism.

Now by
[
DN89
, Théorème B (b)]
, the moduli space
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
has Picard number 1. Let
{\mathcal{N}}
be any very ample line bundle on
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{0}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
. Then
\left(\Theta_{4}^{0}\right)^{*}{\mathcal{N}}\cong{\mathcal{L}}^{a}\boxtimes{\mathcal{L}}^{b}
for some integers
a,b
, where
{\mathcal{L}}
is the ample generator of
\mathrm{Pic}\left({\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})\right)
. Since
\Theta_{4}^{0}
is symmetric, we have
a=b>0
. Therefore,
\Theta_{4}^{0}
must be a finite morphism. As
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})\times{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
and
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{0}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
are projective varieties of the same dimension,
\Theta_{4}^{0}
is surjective.

Similar arguments show that
\Theta_{4}^{1}
and
\Phi_{4}
are finite surjective morphisms. (Note that since
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(\pm x))
is a fine moduli space, we can define
\Theta_{4}^{1}
without using the Riemann extension theorem.)
The irreducibility statements follow from the fact that
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,L)
is irreducible. ∎

It is natural to ask if an arbitrary (possibly unstable)
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank four which admits an isotropic subbbundle of rank two can be obtained in this way. The answer is Yes, and the proof of the following theorem shows how to find the expression(s).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
V
be a rank four
L
-valued orthogonal bundle. Then
V
admits a rank two isotropic subbundle if and only if
V
is isomorphic to
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
for some rank two bundle
E_{2}
with
\det E_{2}\cong\det E_{1}^{*}\otimes L
.

Proof.

One implication follows from (
4.1
) and the discussion after it. Conversely, suppose
E_{1}\subset V
is a rank two isotropic subbundle. Then Criterion
2.1
shows that
V
is isomorphic to an extension
0\to E_{1}\to V\to E_{1}^{*}\otimes L\to 0
defined by a class

\delta\ \in\ H^{1}(C,\wedge^{2}E_{1}\otimes L^{*})\ \subset\ H^{1}(C,E_{1}\otimes E_{1}\otimes L^{*}).

Now
\delta
also defines an extension of line bundles
0\to{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}\to E_{2}\to\det E_{1}^{*}\otimes L\to 0
. Tensoring by
E_{1}
, we obtain an extension

0\to E_{1}\to E_{1}\otimes E_{2}\to E_{1}\otimes\det E_{1}^{*}\otimes L\to 0
of class

\mathrm{Id}_{E_{1}}\otimes\delta\ \in\ H^{1}(C,({{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{E_{1}})\otimes\det E_{1}\otimes L^{*})\ \subset\ H^{1}(C,\mathrm{End}(E_{1})\otimes\det E_{1}\otimes L^{*}).

Now since
E_{1}
has rank two, there is an isomorphism
\mathrm{Hom}(E_{1},E_{1}\otimes\det E_{1})\xrightarrow{\sim}E_{1}\otimes E_{1}
inducing an isomorphism of split exact sequences

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{({{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{E_{1}})\otimes\det E_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\wr}
\textstyle{\mathrm{End}(E_{1})\otimes\det E_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\wr}
\textstyle{\mathrm{End}_{0}(E_{1})\otimes\det E_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\wr}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\wedge^{2}E_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{1}\otimes E_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

Under the induced identification
H^{1}(C,\wedge^{2}E_{1}\otimes L^{*})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{1}(C,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{E_{1}}\otimes\det E_{1}\otimes L^{*})
, the extension class
\mathrm{Id}_{E_{1}}\otimes\delta
corresponds simply to
\delta
. This shows that
V
and
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
are isomorphic vector bundles. Also the natural orthogonal structure of
V
induced from the extension
\delta
coincides with that of
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
in view of Criterion
2.1
. ∎

4.2.
Quadric fibration and isotropic subbundles

Let
V=E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle as above. A computation using (
4.1
) shows that the isotropic vectors in
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
are exactly those of the form
e_{1}\otimes e_{2}
for
e_{i}\in E_{i}
.
Therefore:

Proposition 4.3
.

The quadric subfibration
Q_{V}\subset{\mathbb{P}}V
parameterizing isotropic lines is the image of the relative Veronese embedding
{\mathbb{P}}E_{1}\times_{C}{\mathbb{P}}E_{2}\ \hookrightarrow\ {\mathbb{P}}(E_{1}\otimes E_{2})
.

In particular,
{\mathbb{P}}V
is an enveloping bundle for
{\mathbb{P}}E_{1}\times_{C}{\mathbb{P}}E_{2}
. Moreover, we observe:

Remark 4.4
.

(a)

There are natural projections
\rho_{1}\colon Q_{V}\to{\mathbb{P}}E_{1}
and
\rho_{2}\colon Q_{V}\to{\mathbb{P}}E_{2}
, which are morphisms over
C
.

(b)

Via the identification
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Hom}(E_{1}^{*},E_{2})
, the locus
Q_{V}
is the projectivization of the set of maps of rank one.

(c)

The two-dimensional isotropic subspaces of a fiber
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}|_{p}
are exactly those of the form
\Lambda_{1}\otimes E_{2}|_{p}
and
E_{1}|_{p}\otimes\Lambda_{2}
, where
\Lambda_{i}
is a line in
E_{i}|_{p}
.

We now use the enveloping bundle structure to study isotropic subbundles of
V
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose
V=E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
as above. Let
F\subset V
be a rank two isotropic subbundle. Then
F
is either of the form
E_{1}\otimes N_{2}
for a line bundle
N_{2}\subset E_{2}
, or
N_{1}\otimes E_{2}
for a line bundle
N_{1}\subset E_{1}
. In each case,
N_{i}
is uniquely determined as a subbundle of
E_{i}
.

Proof.

The inclusion
F\hookrightarrow V
induces a map
{\mathbb{P}}F\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}V
of ruled varieties over
C
. By isotropy,
{\mathbb{P}}F
is contained in
Q_{V}
, so we have the composed maps
{\mathbb{P}}F\hookrightarrow Q_{V}\xrightarrow{\rho_{i}}{\mathbb{P}}E_{i}
. Clearly at least one of these is dominant, and hence surjective, being a morphism of projective varieties. Moreover, since
F
is isotropic, by Remark
4.4
(c), for all
p\in C
the fiber
F|_{p}
is of the form
\Lambda_{1}\otimes E_{2}|_{p}
or
E_{1}|_{p}\otimes\Lambda_{2}
for some line
\Lambda_{i}\subset E_{i}|_{p}
. Thus
{\mathbb{P}}F
cannot project surjectively to both
{\mathbb{P}}E_{1}
and
{\mathbb{P}}E_{2}
, so exactly one of the projections
{\mathbb{P}}F\to{\mathbb{P}}E_{i}
is surjective.

After reordering if necessary, we may assume that
{\mathbb{P}}F\cong{\mathbb{P}}E_{1}
. As
{\mathbb{P}}F
is not dominant over
{\mathbb{P}}E_{2}
, for general
p\in C
the fiber
{\mathbb{P}}F|_{p}\subset Q_{V}|_{p}
is the line

{\mathbb{P}}E_{1}|_{p}\times\{\mu(p)\}\ \subset\ {\mathbb{P}}E_{1}|_{p}\times{\mathbb{P}}E_{2}|_{p}

for some
\mu(p)\in{\mathbb{P}}E_{2}|_{p}
. We thus obtain a section
\mu\colon C\to{\mathbb{P}}E_{2}
, defining a line subbundle
N_{2}\subset E_{2}
, and
F=E_{1}\otimes N_{2}
. ∎

Remark 4.6
.

If
{\mathbb{P}}E_{1}\cong{\mathbb{P}}E_{2}
then
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
is a twist of
\mathrm{End}\,E_{1}={{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}\oplus\mathrm{End}_{0}\,E_{1}
, and in particular is strictly semistable. Thus if
V=E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
is a stable vector bundle, then
{\mathbb{P}}E_{1}\not\cong{\mathbb{P}}E_{2}
. The situation where
{\mathbb{P}}E_{1}\cong{\mathbb{P}}E_{2}
will arise in the next section in the context of rank three orthogonal bundles.

In what follows, we follow the convention that a Quot scheme
\mathrm{Quot}_{r,d}(W)
is empty if
d
is not an integer.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
V=E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
be a rank four
L
-valued orthogonal bundle as above. Write
d_{i}:=\deg(E_{i})
. For each
e
, there is an isomorphism

\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,\frac{1}{2}(e-d_{2})}(E_{1})\ \sqcup\ \mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,\frac{1}{2}(e-d_{1})}(E_{2})\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ \mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{2,e}(V),

via the map

\begin{cases}N\mapsto N\otimes E_{2}\hbox{ if }N\subset E_{1}\\
N\mapsto E_{1}\otimes N\hbox{ if }N\subset E_{2}.\end{cases}

Remark 4.8
.

Notice that
d_{1}+d_{2}=\ell
. Then a Riemann–Roch computation shows that
\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,\frac{1}{2}(e-d_{2})}(E_{1})
and
\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,\frac{1}{2}(e-d_{1})}(E_{2})
have expected dimension
\ell-e-(g-1)
.
This coincides with the expected dimension of
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{2,e}(V)
given in (
2.7
), agreeing with Corollary
4.7
.

Remark 4.9
.

Note that the ruled surface
{\mathbb{P}}E_{1}
arises both as the projectivization of the isotropic subbundle
E_{1}
and as a component of the isotropic Grassmannian bundle
\mathrm{OG}(V)\subset{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}V)
; that parameterizing isotropic planes of the form
(N\otimes E_{2})|_{p}
for
p\in C
.

As shown by Theorem
2.3
, if
L={{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
then all rank two isotropic subbundles have degree of the same parity (which coincides with the invariant
w_{2}(V)
, by Theorem
2.3
). The above results make this particularly visible for bundles of rank four:

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
V=E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
, where
\det E_{1}\otimes\det E_{2}\cong{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
. Then
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
have degrees of the same parity, which is also the parity of any rank two isotropic subbundle of
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
.

Proof.

We have
\deg(E_{1})\equiv\deg(E_{2})\mod 2
since
\det E_{1}\otimes\det E_{2}\cong{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
. By Proposition
4.5
, any rank two isotropic subbundle
F\subset V
is isomorphic to
E_{i}\otimes N
for some line subbundle
N
and for some
i\in\{1,2\}
. Thus

\deg(F)\ =\ \deg(E_{i}\otimes N)\ =\ \deg(E_{i})+2\deg N\ \equiv\ \deg(E_{i})\mod 2.\qed

We turn now to isotropic line subbundles. By Proposition
4.3
, an isotropic line subbundle
N
of
V=E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
corresponds to a section of
Q_{V}={\mathbb{P}}E_{1}\times_{C}{\mathbb{P}}E_{2}
. Hence
N
is of the form
N_{1}\otimes N_{2}
where
N_{i}
is a line subbundle of
E_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Therefore, we obtain at once:

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
V=E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
be as above. Then
(N_{1},N_{2})\mapsto N_{1}\otimes N_{2}
defines an isomorphism

\bigcup_{d_{1}+d_{2}=d}\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,d_{1}}(E_{1})\times\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,d_{2}}(E_{2})\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ \mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{1,d}(E_{1}\otimes E_{2}).

4.3.
Enumeration of maximal degree isotropic subbundles

Firstly, we discuss the number of isotropic subbundles of rank two with maximal degree. In particular, we obtain another proof of Lemma
2.6
for
r=4
.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
V
be a stable rank four orthogonal bundle which is general in its component of moduli.

(a)

Suppose
V\in{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}
. Then the number of isotropic subbundles of rank two of maximal degree is given as follows.

\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}\hline\cr&{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{0}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})&{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{1}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})\\
\hline\cr g\ \text{even}&\infty\ \text{of degree }-g&2\cdot 2^{g}\ \text{of degree }1-g\\
\hline\cr g\ \text{odd}&2\cdot 2^{g}\ \text{of degree }1-g&\infty\ \text{of degree }-g\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

(b)

Suppose
V\in{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x))_{\mathrm{st}}
. Then
V
admits
2^{g}
isotropic subbundles of rank two and degree
2-g
.

(c)

Suppose
g\geq 3
is odd. Then
V
has
4^{g}
maximal isotropic line subbundles of degree
1-g
.

Proof.

(a) A computation using Corollary
4.7
shows that for each value of
w_{2}(V)
, the scheme of maximal rank two isotropic subbundles of
V=E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
is canonically identified with the union of the Quot schemes of maximal line subbundles of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
. By Theorem
4.1
, we may assume that
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are general in moduli. The statement now follows from Theorem
2.5
. The proof of (b) is similar.

As for (c): It follows from Theorem
4.11
that the degree of an isotropic line subbundle
N_{1}\otimes N_{2}\subset E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
is maximal among the isotropic line subbundles if and only if
N_{i}
is a maximal line subbundle of
E_{i}
for
i=1,2
. By Theorem
2.5
, if
g
is odd, then a general
E_{i}\in{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{\mathcal{O}}_{C})
has
2^{g}
maximal line subbundles of degree
-\frac{1}{2}(g-1)
. Statement (c) follows.
∎

5.
Rank three

Suppose
V
is an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank three. By (
2.1
), the line bundle
L
has even degree and
\det V\cong M^{3}
for some
M
satisfying
M^{2}\cong L
. We now analyse the structure of orthogonal bundles of rank three, expanding upon
[
Mum71
, p. 185]
and
[
CH15
, Lemma 4.1]
.

5.1.
Rank three orthogonal bundles

As noted in the case
L={{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
in
[
Mum71
, § 2]
, for any rank two bundle
E
, the bundle
M\otimes(\det E)^{-1}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E
has an
L
-valued quadratic form induced by

(5.1)

(e_{1}\cdot e_{2})\otimes(f_{1}\cdot f_{2})\ \mapsto\ 2\left(e_{1}\wedge f_{1}\otimes e_{2}\wedge f_{2}+e_{2}\wedge f_{1}\otimes e_{1}\wedge f_{2}\right).

We shall see that this induces finite surjective morphisms of moduli spaces. Noting that any rank three orthogonal bundle is a twist of an
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
-valued orthogonal bundle of determinant
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
, we now set
L=M={{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
and describe the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(3,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
in more detail.

Theorem 5.1
.

(a)

The association
E\mapsto\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E
defines a finite surjective morphism

\Theta_{3}^{0}\colon{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})\ \to\ {\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{0}(3,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}).

(b)

For any
x\in C
, the association
E\mapsto\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(-x)
defines a finite surjective morphism

\Theta_{3}^{1}\colon{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x))\ \to\ {\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{1}(3,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}).

Proof.

Let us firstly define the morphism
\Theta_{3}^{0}
. As
{\mathbb{C}}
has characteristic zero,
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E
is semistable if
E
is. Therefore, passing to a suitable étale cover, we can define
\Theta_{3}^{0}
on the open subset
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}
using a Poincaré bundle. Note that the bundle
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E
and
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(-x)
have isotropic line subbundles of the form
N^{2}
and
N^{2}(-x)
respectively. Hence by Theorem
2.3
, the images of
\Theta_{3}^{0}
and
\Theta_{3}^{1}
land on the components
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{0}(3,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
and
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{1}(3,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
, respectively.

Now, using for example
[
Har77
, Exercise II.5.16]
, it is straightforward to check that if
E\sim E^{\prime}
, then
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E\sim\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E^{\prime}
. Hence, as any semistable bundle is a limit of stable ones, it follows that
\Theta^{3}_{0}
has a continuous extension to
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
. As in the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we can show that
\Theta_{3}^{0}
is a morphism by using the Riemann extension theorem.

As already noted, the moduli space
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
has Picard number 1. Let
{\mathcal{N}}
be any very ample line bundle on
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{0}(3,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
. Then
\left(\Theta_{3}^{0}\right)^{*}{\mathcal{N}}\cong{\mathcal{L}}^{a}
for some positive integer
a
, where
{\mathcal{L}}
is as before the ample generator of
\mathrm{Pic}\left({\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})\right)
. Therefore, as
\Theta_{3}^{0}
is a morphism, it must be finite. Counting dimensions of the projective varieties
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
and
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{0}(3,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
, it must be surjective. This proves (a). The proof of (b) is similar.
∎

As in the rank four case, the following theorem ensures that an arbitrary (possibly non-stable)
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank three has a similar expression, and its proof shows how to find it.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
V
be a rank three
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of determinant
M^{3}
as above. Then
V
is isomorphic as an orthogonal bundle to
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E\otimes(\det E)^{-1}\otimes M
for some rank two bundle
E
.

Proof.

We use an idea from
[
CH15
]
, also applied in
[
CCH21b
]
. The isomorphism
M^{2}\xrightarrow{\sim}L
gives
M
the structure of an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle. The orthogonal direct sum
V\perp M
is then
L
-valued orthogonal of rank four and determinant
M^{4}=L^{2}
. By Proposition
3.2
, we can find a rank two isotropic subbundle in
V\perp M
. By Theorem
4.2
, there exist rank two bundles
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
such that
V\perp M\cong E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
.

Now the subbundle
M\subset E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
is nowhere isotropic, being an orthogonal direct summand. But as noted in Remark
4.4
(b), the locus of isotropic vectors corresponds precisely to the locus of maps
E_{1}^{*}\to E_{2}
of rank at most one. Therefore, the image of
M\to E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
is indecomposable at all points. Hence it defines an isomorphism
E_{1}^{*}\otimes M\xrightarrow{\sim}E_{2}
. Moreover,
E_{1}^{*}\otimes M\cong E_{1}\otimes(\det E_{1})^{-1}\otimes M
since
E_{1}
has rank two. It follows that

V\perp M\ \cong\ \left(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E_{1}\otimes(\det E_{1})^{-1}\otimes M\right)\oplus M.

By uniqueness of direct sum decomposition, we conclude that
V\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E_{1}\otimes(\det E_{1})^{-1}\otimes M
. By
[
Gro57
, Proposition 3.1]
, composing with an automorphism of
V
if necessary, this may be assumed to be an isomorphism of orthogonal bundles. ∎

5.2.
Quadric fibration and isotropic subbundles

We consider an
L
-valued rank three orthogonal bundle
V=\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E\otimes(\det E)^{-1}\otimes M
of determinant
M^{3}
as above. We now describe the locus of isotropic lines
Q_{V}\subset{\mathbb{P}}V
.

Proposition 5.3
.

The fibration
Q_{V}
coincides with the relative Segre embedding

{\mathbb{P}}E\ \hookrightarrow\ {\mathbb{P}}\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ {\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E\otimes(\det E)^{-1}\otimes M).

Proof.

This follows easily from (
5.1
).
For a geometric argument: By Theorem
5.2
, we may assume that the quadratic form is induced from that
V\perp M\cong E\otimes E\otimes(\det E)^{-1}\otimes M
. Thus
Q_{V}=Q_{V\perp M}\cap{\mathbb{P}}V
. By Proposition
4.3
, this is

\left({\mathbb{P}}E\times_{C}{\mathbb{P}}E\right)\cap\left({\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E\otimes(\det E)^{-1}\otimes M)\right),

which is precisely the image of the relative Segre embedding, as desired. ∎

Theorem 5.4

(Isotropic Quot schemes in rank three)
.

Let
V=\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E\otimes(\det E)^{-1}\otimes M
be as above. Write
d:=\deg(E)
and
m:=\deg(M)
.

(a)

For each integer
e
, there is an isomorphism

\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,\frac{1}{2}(e+d-m)}(E)\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ \mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{1,e}(V)

given by
N_{1}\mapsto N_{1}^{2}\otimes(\det E)^{-1}\otimes M
.

(b)

In particular, if
N\subset V
is any isotropic line subbundle, then
\deg(N)\equiv d-m\mod 2
.

Proof.

Let
N\subset V
be an isotropic subbundle of degree
e
. Then
N\otimes(\det E)\otimes M^{-1}
is a line subbundle of
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E
. By Proposition
5.3
, this belongs to the cone over the relative Segre embedding
{\mathbb{P}}E\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E
. Thus it is of the form
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}N_{1}
for some
N_{1}\subset E
of degree
\frac{1}{2}(e+d-m)
.
In particular,
e\equiv d-m\equiv 0\mod 2
. Clearly
N_{1}
is uniquely determined as an element of
\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,\frac{1}{2}(e+d-m)}(E)
. The statement follows. ∎

Remark 5.5
.

A Riemann–Roch computation shows that
\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,\frac{1}{2}(e+d-m)}(E)
has expected dimension
m-e-(g-1)
, which coincides with the expected dimension of
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{1,e}(V)
given in (
2.7
),
in agreement with Corollary
4.7
.

5.3.
Enumeration of isotropic line subbundles

Together with Theorem
2.5
, the above results allow us to count the number of isotropic line subbundles of maximal degree of a general stable
V\in{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{i}(3,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
, when this is finite.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
V
be a stable
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank three and trivial determinant.

(a)

If
g
is even and
V
is general in
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{1}(3,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}
, then
V
has
2^{g}
maximal isotropic line subbundles of degree
1-g
.

(b)

If
g
is odd, and
V
is general in
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{0}(3,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}
, then
V
has
2^{g}
maximal isotropic line subbundles of degree
1-g
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.4
, maximal line subbundles of
E
are in bijection with maximal isotropic line subbundles of
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E\otimes(\det E)^{-1}
. If
g
is even, then by Theorem
2.5
, a general
E\in{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x))_{\mathrm{st}}
has
2^{g}
line subbundles
N_{1}
of degree
1-\frac{g}{2}
. By Theorem
5.4
, these induce
2^{g}
isotropic line subbundles
N_{1}^{2}(-x)\subset\mathrm{Sym}^{2}E\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(-x)
of degree
1-g
, and we obtain (a). The proof of (b) is similar. ∎

6.
Rank six

As was the case for orthogonal bundles of ranks three and four, the rank five case turns out to be closely related to the rank six case. Therefore we now turn to
L
-valued orthogonal bundles of rank six admitting rank three isotropic subbundles; equivalently, in view of Lemma
3.2
, having determinant
L^{3}
.

6.1.
Rank six orthogonal bundles with rank three isotropic subbundles

If
W
is any bundle of rank four and determinant
L
, then there is a natural map

(6.1)

\wedge^{2}W\otimes\wedge^{2}W\ \to\ \wedge^{4}W\ =\ L

given by the determinant. This endows
\wedge^{2}W
with the structure of an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle. Furthermore, every rank three subbundle
F\subset W
defines a rank three subbundle
\wedge^{2}F\subset\wedge^{2}W
, which is isotropic since
\wedge^{4}F=0
. We shall now show that up to a twist, any rank six orthogonal bundle admitting a rank three isotropic subbundle is of the form
\wedge^{2}W
. (In §
6.2
, however, we shall see that not every rank three isotropic subbundle need be of the form
\wedge^{2}F
, even up to twisting.)

As in the previous sections, we shall use this to give a description of the moduli spaces of rank six orthogonal bundles.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
x
be any point of
C
.

(a)

The association
W\mapsto\wedge^{2}W
defines a finite surjective morphism

\Theta_{6}^{0}\colon{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})\ \to\ {\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{0}(6,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}).

(b)

The association
W_{1}\mapsto\wedge^{2}W_{1}\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
defines a finite surjective morphism

\Theta_{6}^{1}\colon{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(-2x))\to{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{1}(6,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}).

(c)

The association
W_{2}\mapsto\wedge^{2}W_{2}
defines a finite surjective morphism

\Phi_{6}\colon{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x))\ \to\ {\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(6,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)).

Proof.

This is similar to the rank three case. As
{\mathbb{C}}
has characteristic zero,
\wedge^{2}W
is semistable whenever
W
is. Using for example
[
Har77
, Exercise II.5.16]
, one checks that if
W
and
W^{\prime}
are S-equivalent semistable bundles of rank four, then
\wedge^{2}W
and
\wedge^{2}W^{\prime}
are S-equivalent. By a similar argument to that in Theorem
5.1
, we obtain the existence, finiteness and surjectivity of the morphisms
\Theta^{i}_{6}
and
\Phi_{6}
. The target component of each
\Theta_{6}^{i}
is determined by Theorem
2.3
and the parity of

\deg(\wedge^{2}F)=2\cdot\deg(F)\ \text{ and }\ \deg(\wedge^{2}F)\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)=2\cdot\deg(F)+3

for rank three subbundles
F\subset W
.
∎

As in the previous cases, we describe a construction required for the next proposition which shows how to get the “inverses” of the morphisms
\Theta_{6}^{i}
and
\Phi_{6}
. Let
V
be a rank six
L
-valued orthogonal bundle admitting a rank three isotropic subbundle
E
. Then by Criterion
2.1
, there is an exact sequence
0\to E\to V\to E^{*}\otimes L\to 0
,
defining an extension class
\delta\in H^{1}(C,\wedge^{2}E\otimes L^{-1})
. Then
\delta
also defines an extension
0\to\wedge^{2}E\to W\to L\to 0
of rank four.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank six. Let
E
,
\delta
and
W
be as above. Then
V
is isomorphic to
(\wedge^{2}W)\otimes(\det E)^{-1}
.

Proof.

To ease notation, write
{\widehat{E}}:=\wedge^{2}E
, a subbundle of
W
. We consider the antisymmetrization map
a\colon W\otimes W\to\wedge^{2}W
. By linear algebra, using the fact that
{\widehat{E}}
has corank one, the restriction of
a
to
W\otimes{\widehat{E}}
is surjective with kernel
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}{\widehat{E}}
. Therefore, we have a diagram

\textstyle{\mathrm{Sym}^{2}{\widehat{E}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{=}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Sym}^{2}{\widehat{E}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\widehat{E}}\otimes{\widehat{E}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a}
\textstyle{W\otimes{\widehat{E}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a}
\textstyle{L\otimes{\widehat{E}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\wedge^{2}{\widehat{E}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\wedge^{2}W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L\otimes{\widehat{E}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

In particular,
\wedge^{2}W
is an extension of class

(6.2)

a_{*}(\delta\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\widehat{E}})\ \in\ H^{1}(C,\mathrm{Hom}(L\otimes{\widehat{E}},\wedge^{2}{\widehat{E}})).

Now write
M:=\wedge^{3}E=\det E
. The association

u\wedge v\ \mapsto\ \left(w\ \mapsto u\wedge v\wedge w\right)

gives a canonical identification

(6.3)

{\widehat{E}}\ =\ \wedge^{2}E\ =\ \mathrm{Hom}(E,\det E)\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ E^{*}\otimes M.

Similarly, we have canonical identifications

(6.4)

\wedge^{2}{\widehat{E}}\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ \wedge^{2}(E^{*}\otimes M)\ \cong\ \wedge^{2}E^{*}\otimes M^{2}\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ E\otimes M.

Thus
\wedge^{2}W
is an extension
0\to E\otimes M\to\wedge^{2}W\to E^{*}\otimes M\otimes L\to 0
, and

\mathrm{Hom}(L\otimes{\widehat{E}},\wedge^{2}{\widehat{E}})\ =\ L^{-1}\otimes E\otimes M^{-1}\otimes E\otimes M\ \cong\ L^{-1}\otimes E\otimes E\otimes\mathrm{End}\;M.

Therefore, to prove the theorem, in view of (
6.2
), (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) it will suffice to prove the following lemma. ∎

Lemma 6.3
.

The element
a_{*}(p\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\widehat{E}})
corresponds to
-p\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{M}
for each
p\in L^{-1}\otimes\wedge^{2}E
.

Proof.

In what follows, we shall assume that
L={{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
, as the general case is only notationally more cumbersome. Let
p
be any element of
L^{-1}\otimes\wedge^{2}E|_{x}=\wedge^{2}E|_{x}
for some
x\in C
. As
E
has rank three, we can write
p=u\wedge v
for some
u,v\in E|_{x}
. If
p
is nonzero then we can choose
w\in E|_{x}
such that
u,v,w
is a basis of
E|_{x}
. Let
u^{*},v^{*},w^{*}
be the dual basis of
E^{*}|_{x}
. Set
m:=u\wedge v\wedge w\in M|_{x}
. Then
u^{*}\wedge v^{*}\wedge w^{*}=m^{*}
where
m^{*}\in M^{*}|_{x}
is dual to
m
(that is,
\langle m^{*},m\rangle=1
). Then via (
6.3
), we have identifications

(6.5)

u\wedge v\ \leftrightarrow\ w^{*}\otimes m,\quad u\wedge w\ \leftrightarrow\ (-v)^{*}\otimes m,\quad\hbox{and}\quad v\wedge w\ \leftrightarrow\ u^{*}\otimes m.

Via these identifications, and using the contraction
m^{*}\otimes m\mapsto 1
, the map
\mathrm{Id}_{\wedge^{2}E}|_{x}
can be identified with

(u\wedge v)\otimes m^{*}\otimes w+(u\wedge w)\otimes m^{*}\otimes(-v)+(v\wedge w)\otimes m^{*}\otimes u.

Thus
p\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\wedge^{2}E}
is expressed as

(u\wedge v)\otimes(u\wedge v)\otimes m^{*}\otimes w+(u\wedge v)\otimes(u\wedge w)\otimes m^{*}\otimes(-v)+(u\wedge v)\otimes(v\wedge w)\otimes m^{*}\otimes u.

Substituting from (
6.5
) (thereby using the first identification in (
6.4
)), we obtain

a_{*}(p\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\wedge^{2}E})=(w^{*}\otimes m)\wedge((-v^{*})\otimes m)\otimes m^{*}\otimes(-v)+(w^{*}\otimes m)\wedge(u^{*}\otimes m)\otimes m^{*}\otimes u.

Applying the contraction
m\otimes m^{*}\mapsto 1
, this becomes

(6.6)

(w^{*}\wedge v^{*})\otimes v\otimes m+(w^{*}\wedge u^{*})\otimes u\otimes m.

Now since
u^{*}\wedge v^{*}\wedge w^{*}=m^{*}
, in particular
w^{*}\wedge v^{*}
is the dual basis element to
(-u)^{*}\otimes m
. Thus via the identification
\wedge^{2}E^{*}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Hom}(E^{*},M^{-1})\cong E\otimes M^{-1}
, we see that
w^{*}\wedge v^{*}
corresponds to
(-u)\otimes m^{*}
. Similarly,
w^{*}\wedge u^{*}
corresponds to
v\otimes m^{*}
. Thus (
6.6
) is identified with

(-u)\otimes m^{*}\otimes v\otimes m+v\otimes m^{*}\otimes u\otimes m,

corresponding to
-(u\wedge v)\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{M}=-p\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{M}
, as desired. ∎

We can now give a structure theorem for orthogonal bundles of rank six.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank six.

(a)

The bundle
V
is isomorphic to
\wedge^{2}W
for some
W
of rank four and determinant
L
if and only if
V
admits a rank three isotropic subbundle of even degree.

(b)

Let
x
be any point of
C
. Then
V
is isomorphic to
\wedge^{2}W_{1}\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
for some
W_{1}
of rank four and determinant
L(-2x)
if and only if
V
admits a rank three isotropic subbundle of odd degree.

Note that (a) and (b) apply simultaneously when
\deg(L)
is odd.

Proof.

As the proofs of (a) and (b) are almost identical, we prove (b) only. Let
E
be a rank three subbundle of
V
which is isotropic and of odd degree. By Proposition
6.2
, we have
V\cong\wedge^{2}W\otimes\det E^{*}
where
0\to\wedge^{2}E\to W\to L\to 0
is constructed from
V
and
E
as previously. By hypothesis, we can find
N
such that
\det E\cong N^{2}(-x)
. Set
W_{1}:=W\otimes N^{-1}
. Then

\det W_{1}\ \cong\ (\det E)^{2}\otimes L\otimes N^{-4}\ \cong\ N^{4}(-2x)\otimes L\otimes N^{-4}\ \cong\ L(-2x),

and
V\cong\wedge^{2}W\otimes N^{-2}\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)=(\wedge^{2}W_{1})\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
.

Conversely, if
V=(\wedge^{2}W_{1})\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
then any rank three subbundle
F\subset W_{1}
yields a subbundle
\wedge^{2}F\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)\subset V
which is isotropic since
\wedge^{4}F=0
, and has odd degree
2\cdot\deg F+3
. ∎

6.2.
Enveloping bundle structure and isotropic subbundles

Let
W
be a bundle of rank four and determinant
L
, and let
V:=\wedge^{2}W
be the associated
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank six. By
[
GH78
, pp. 209–211]
, an element
\omega\in\wedge^{2}W
belongs to the cone over the Plücker image of
\mathrm{Gr}(2,W)
in
{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}W)
if and only if
\omega
is decomposable; equivalently
\omega\wedge\omega=0
in
\wedge^{4}W
. In view of (
6.1
), this is equivalent to
\omega
being isotropic with respect to the orthogonal structure on
\wedge^{2}W
. It follows that:

Proposition 6.5
.

The relative Plücker map embeds
\mathrm{Gr}(2,W)
in
{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}W)
as the projectivization
Q_{\wedge^{2}W}
of the locus of vectors isotropic with respect to the orthogonal structure on
\wedge^{2}W
. In particular,
{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}W)
is an enveloping bundle for
Q_{\wedge^{2}W}=\mathrm{Gr}(2,W)\to C
.

We shall also require the following lemma on isotropic subbundles of orthogonal bundles.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle (of arbitrary rank) and
E\subset V
any subbundle. Then
E
is isotropic if and only if
{\mathbb{P}}E\subset Q_{V}
.

Proof.

One direction is clear from the definition of
Q_{V}
. Conversely, suppose
{\mathbb{P}}E\subset Q_{V}
. Let
v,w
be elements of a fiber
E|_{x}
. By symmetry of
\sigma
, we have

2\cdot\sigma(v\otimes w)\ =\ \sigma\left((v+w)\otimes(v+w)\right)-\sigma(v\otimes v)-\sigma(w\otimes w).

By hypothesis, the right hand side is zero, so
\sigma(v\otimes w)=0
. ∎

Remark 6.7
.

Note that the above proof depends on the symmetry of the bilinear form, and may fail in characteristic
2
.

We shall use these observations to classify isotropic subbundles of
V
, beginning with rank three. By Proposition
6.5
and Lemma
6.6
, a rank three subbundle
E\subset V
is isotropic if and only if the
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
-subbundle
{\mathbb{P}}E\subset{\mathbb{P}}V
is contained in
\mathrm{Gr}(2,W)
. For such an
E
, by
[
GH78
, p. 757]
, one of the following two situations must arise:

(i)

For each
p\in C
, there exists a three-dimensional subspace
F_{p}\subset V|_{p}
such that

{\mathbb{P}}E|_{p}\ =\ \{\Lambda\in\mathrm{Gr}(2,W|_{p}):\Lambda\subset F_{p}\}.

(ii)

For each
p\in C
, there exists a line
N_{p}\subset W|_{p}
such that

{\mathbb{P}}E|_{p}\ =\ \{\Lambda\in\mathrm{Gr}(2,W|_{p}):N_{p}\subset\Lambda\}.

If situation (i) arises, then, using local triviality, there exists a rank three subbundle
F\subset W
such that
{\mathbb{P}}E=\mathrm{Gr}(2,F)
. By definition of the Plücker embedding
\mathrm{Gr}(2,F)\to{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}F)\subset{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}W)
it follows that
{\mathbb{P}}E
is the projectivization of the set of bivectors

\{v\wedge w:v,w\in F\}.

As
F
has rank three, every element of
\wedge^{2}F
is of this form. Thus
E=\wedge^{2}F
. In particular,
\deg E=2\cdot\deg F
is even.

On the other hand, suppose situation (ii) arises. Then there is a line subbundle
N\subset W
such that
{\mathbb{P}}E
is the projectivization of
\{v\wedge w:v\in N,w\in W\}
. We define a map
\tau_{N}\colon N\otimes W\to\wedge^{2}W
by

(6.7)

\tau_{N}(v\otimes w)\ =\ v\wedge w.

Clearly
\tau_{N}
has kernel
N\otimes N
and image exactly
E
. Thus
E\cong N\otimes\frac{W}{N}
, and

\deg E\ =\ 3\cdot\deg N+\deg W-\deg N\ =\ 2\cdot\deg N+\deg L.

In particular,
\deg E\equiv\deg L\mod 2
. Now we can summarize the above discussion as follows.

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
V=\wedge^{2}W
be a rank six orthogonal bundle for a rank four bundle
W
of determinant
L
. Set
\ell:=\deg L
and let
d
be any integer.

(a)

Suppose
\ell
is even. Then there is an isomorphism

\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{3,d}(W)\ \sqcup\ \mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,d-\frac{\ell}{2}}(W)\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ \mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{3,2d}(V).

In particular,
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{3,2d}(V)
is disconnected for
d\ll 0
.

(b)

Suppose
\ell
is odd. Then there are isomorphisms

\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{3,d}(W)\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ \mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{3,2d}(V)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,d-\frac{\ell+1}{2}}(W)\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ \mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{3,2d-1}(V).

In both cases, the isomorphisms are given respectively by

E\ \mapsto\ \wedge^{2}E\quad\hbox{and}\quad N\ \mapsto\ \tau_{N}(N\otimes W)\ \cong\ N\otimes\frac{W}{N}.

Remark 6.9
.

A Riemann–Roch computation shows that
\mathrm{Quot}_{3,d}(W)
and
\mathrm{Quot}_{1,d-\frac{\ell+\varepsilon}{2}}(W)
have the same expected dimensions respectively as those given in (
2.7
) for
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{3,2d}(\wedge^{2}W)
and
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{3,2d-\varepsilon}(\wedge^{2}W)
, in agreement with Theorem
6.8
.

Lastly, we treat the case where all rank three isotropic subbundles have odd degree, so
V
is of the form
\wedge^{2}W\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
.

Corollary 6.10
.

Suppose
\ell
is even. Let
W
be a rank four bundle of determinant
L
, and let
V
be the orthogonal bundle
\wedge^{2}W\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(x)
. Then for any
d
, there are isomorphisms

\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{3,d-1}(W)\ \sqcup\ \mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,d-1-\frac{\ell}{2}}(W)\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ \mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{3,2d+1}(V),

given by

\begin{cases}E\ \mapsto\ \wedge^{2}E\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)\hbox{ if }E\in\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{3,d-1}(W);\\
M\ \mapsto\ \tau_{N}(N\otimes W)\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)\ \cong\ N\otimes\frac{W}{N}\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)\hbox{ if }E\in\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,d-1-\frac{\ell}{2}}(W).\end{cases}

In particular,
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{3,2d+1}(V)
is disconnected for
d\ll 0
.

Proof.

For any rank six
L
-valued orthogonal
V
, tensor product by
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
defines a canonical isomorphism
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{3,2d-2}(V)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{3,2d+1}(V\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x))
. Thus the statement follows from Theorem
6.8
(a). ∎

Remark 6.11
.

The fact that the isotropic Quot schemes of
W
may have two connected components reflects that fact that the isotropic Grassmann bundle
\mathrm{OG}(3,\wedge^{2}W)
has two components. This is studied in more generality in
[
CCH21b
]
. Further detail for rank six is given in Remark
7.6
.

Let us now consider isotropic subbundles of rank two or one. For brevity, we shall suppose throughout that
V
is of the form
\wedge^{2}W
; the case
V=\wedge^{2}W\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
is left to the reader.

Let
F\subset V
be a rank two isotropic subbundle. This corresponds to a
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-subbundle of the Grassmannian bundle
\mathrm{Gr}(2,W)\to C
. By
[
GH78
, pp. 756–757]
, any
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
lying inside
\mathrm{Gr}(2,4)
is of the form

\{\Lambda\in\mathrm{Gr}(2,4)\>:\>p\subset{\mathbb{P}}\Lambda\subset h\}

for some fixed point
p
and hyperplane
h
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
. Using local triviality as above, there exists a flag of subbundles
N\subset H\subset W
with
\mathrm{rk}\,(N)=1
and
\mathrm{rk}\,(H)=3
such that for each
x\in C
we have

{\mathbb{P}}F|_{x}\>=\>\{\Lambda\in\mathrm{Gr}(2,W|_{x})\>:\>N|_{x}\subset\Lambda\subset H|_{x}\}.

It follows that

F\>=\>\{v\wedge w\>:\>v\in N,\ w\in H\}\ =\ \tau_{N}(N\otimes H),

where
\tau_{N}
is the map defined in (
6.7
). Hence
F\cong(N\otimes H)/(N\otimes N)
and
\deg(F)=\deg(H)+\deg(N)
. As a consequence, we obtain:

Theorem 6.12
.

For any rank four bundle
W
, there is an identification

\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{2,d}(\wedge^{2}W)\cong\bigcup_{d_{1}+d_{2}=d}{\mathcal{Q}}uot^{\circ}_{1,d_{1}}({\mathcal{H}}),

where
\pi\colon{\mathcal{Q}}uot_{1,d_{1}}^{\circ}({\mathcal{H}})\to\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{3,d_{2}}(W)
is the relative Quot scheme with
\pi^{-1}(H)=\mathrm{Quot}_{1,d_{1}}^{\circ}(H)
at
H\in\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{3,d_{2}}(W)
, which parameterizes the flags
N\subset H\subset W
given as above.

Remark 6.13
.

Notice that the rank two isotropic subbundle
\tau_{N}(N\otimes H)
is exactly the intersection of the rank three isotropic subbundles
\tau_{N}(N\otimes W)
and
\wedge^{2}H
, which belong to opposite components of
\mathrm{OG}(V)
. Compare with
[
GH78
, Proposition, p. 735]
. We discuss another aspect of this in Remark
7.6
.

Lastly: By Theorem
6.5
, an isotropic line subbundle of
\wedge^{2}W
is simply a section of
\mathrm{Gr}(2,W)\to C
. But this is equivalent to a choice of rank two subbundle
E\subset W
, and then
N=\wedge^{2}E
. Thus we have

Theorem 6.14
.

Let
W
be any rank four bundle and
\wedge^{2}W
the associated orthogonal bundle. Then
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{1,d}(\wedge^{2}W)\cong\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{2,d}(W)
.

6.3.
Enumeration of maximal isotropic subbundles

We now apply the previous results to the problem of enumerating isotropic subbundles of maximal degree of a general rank six
L
-valued orthogonal bundle
V
, when there are finitely many of these. For ranks one and two, for brevity we restrict to the case
V\cong\wedge^{2}W
with
\det(W)\cong{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
.

Theorem 6.15
.

Let
V
be a rank six
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
-valued orthogonal bundle which is general in its component of moduli.

(a)

Suppose
g\equiv 1\mod 4
and
w_{2}(V)=0
. Then
V
has
2\cdot 4^{g}
rank three isotropic subbundles of maximal degree
-\frac{3}{2}(g-1)
.

(b)

Suppose
g\equiv 3\mod 4
and
w_{2}(V)=1
. Then
V
has
2\cdot 4^{g}
rank three isotropic subbundles of maximal degree
-\frac{3}{2}(g-1)
.

(c)

Suppose that
g\equiv 1\mod 12
and
w_{2}(V)=0
. Then
V
has
12^{g}
rank two isotropic subbundles of maximal degree
-\frac{5}{3}(g-1)
.

(d)

Suppose
w_{2}(V)=0
. Then
V
has

2^{3g-1}-(-1)^{g}\cdot 2^{2g-1}

isotropic line subbundles of maximal degree
1-g
.

Proof.

(a) By Theorem
6.1
(a), we have
V\cong\wedge^{2}W
for some
W\in{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
, which may assumed to be general in moduli. Note that
W^{*}
may also be assumed to be general in moduli, and that
\mathrm{Quot}_{3,d}(W)\cong\mathrm{Quot}_{1,d}(W^{*})
. Thus, as
\deg(W)\equiv 1-g\mod 4
, by Theorem
2.5
the bundles
W
and
W^{*}
both have
4^{g}
maximal line subbundles of degree
\frac{3}{4}(g-1)
. By Theorem
6.8
(a), the bundle
V
contains
2\cdot 4^{g}
rank three isotropic subbundles of maximal degree
-\frac{3}{2}(g-1)
.

(b) By Theorem
6.1
(b), we have
V\cong\wedge^{2}W_{1}\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
for some
W_{1}\in{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(-2x))
, which may be assumed to be general in moduli. As
-2\equiv 1-g\mod 4
, by Theorem
2.5
both the bundles
W_{1}
and
W_{1}^{*}
have
4^{g}
maximal line subbundles of degree
d
and
d+1
respectively, for
d=\frac{1}{4}(1-3g)
. By Corollary
6.10
, we have

\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{3,2d+1}(V)\ \cong\ \mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{3,d-1}(W_{1})\sqcup\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,d}(W_{1}).

Since
\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{3,d-1}(W_{1})\cong\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,d+1}(W_{1}^{*})
, we get the desired number.

(c) Here
V=\wedge^{2}W
is as in part (a). By hypothesis, in particular
g\equiv 1\mod 4
, so a general
W\in{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(4,{\mathcal{O}}_{C})
has
4^{g}
maximal rank three subbundles of degree
-\frac{3}{4}(g-1)
. By
[
LN03
, Lemma 2.1]
, these maximal subbundles are general in moduli. As by hypothesis we also have
g\equiv 1\mod 3
, each maximal rank three
H\subset W
has
3^{g}
maximal line subbundles, each of degree
-\frac{11}{12}(g-1)
. Thus there are
4^{g}\cdot 3^{g}=12^{g}
flags
N\subset H\subset W
where both
N\subset H
and
H\subset W
are maximal subbundles. By Theorem
6.12
, then,
V
has
12^{g}
isotropic subbundles of rank two and maximal degree

-\frac{3}{4}(g-1)-\frac{11}{12}(g-1)\ =\ -\frac{5}{3}(g-1).

(d) Using the formulas in
[
Hol04
, § 4]
, one finds that a general bundle of rank four and degree zero has a finite number of rank two subbundles of maximal degree
1-g
, the number being given as follows:

(6.8)

2^{4g-3}\cdot\sum_{z\in\{-1,\pm\sqrt{-1}\}}\left(z(1-z)^{2}\right)^{1-g}\ =\ 2^{3g-1}+(-1)^{g-1}\cdot 2^{2g-1}.

The statement now follows from Theorem
6.14
.
∎

Remark 6.16
.

By the Hirschowitz bound
[
Hir96
]
, every rank six vector bundle of degree zero has a rank two subbundle of degree at least
-\frac{4}{3}(g-1)
. This means that a general

does have rank two subbundles of degree greater than
-\frac{5}{3}(g-1)
, all of which are nonisotropic.

Theorem 6.17
.

Let
V
be a rank six
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
-valued orthogonal bundle of determinant
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(3x)
which is general in moduli. Suppose

is even. Then
V
has
4^{g}
isotropic rank three subbundles of maximal degree
3-\frac{3}{2}g
.

Proof.

By Theorem
6.1
(c), we have
V\cong\wedge^{2}W_{2}
for some
W_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x))
, which may be assumed to be general in moduli. There are two cases.

If
g\equiv 0\mod 4
, put
d:=2-\frac{3}{4}g
, so that
2d-1=3-\frac{3}{2}g
. Then by Theorem
6.8
(b), we have
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{3,2d-1}(V)\cong\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,d-1}(W_{2})
.

If

, put

, so that

. Then by Theorem

(b), we have
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{3,2d}(V)\cong\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{3,d}(W_{2})\cong\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,d-1}(W_{2}^{*})
.

Applying Theorem
2.5
to
\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,d-1}(W_{2})
and
\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,d-1}(W_{2}^{*})
, we get the desired number. ∎

7.
Rank five

Let
L
be a line bundle, and
V
an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank five. By (
2.1
), necessarily
L
has even degree
2m
, and
\det W\cong M^{5}
for some
M
satisfying
M^{2}\cong L
. We shall use the results in §
6
to analyze the structure of
V
, in particular the relation with rank four symplectic bundles.

7.1.
Structure of rank five orthogonal bundles

Let
M
be a line bundle and
W
a rank four bundle with a symplectic form
\alpha\colon\wedge^{2}W\to M
. Then
\deg(W)=2\cdot\deg(M)
, and
S_{W}:=\mathrm{Ker}\left(\alpha\colon\wedge^{2}W\to M\right)
is a rank five subbundle of determinant
M^{5}
. Note that
S_{W}
depends on the choice of
\alpha
, but if for example
W
is stable then
\alpha
is unique up to nonzero scalar multiple.

Proposition 7.1
.

The bundle
S_{W}
is
M^{2}
-valued orthogonal, and there is an orthogonal direct sum decomposition
\wedge^{2}W=S_{W}\perp M
.

Proof.

As
W
is
M
-valued symplectic,
\det W\cong M^{2}
. Thus
S_{W}
inherits the symmetric bilinear form
\sigma\colon(\wedge^{2}W)^{\otimes 2}\to M^{2}
given by the determinant, as discussed in the previous section. Let us show that
\sigma|_{\left(S_{W}\right)^{\otimes 2}}
is nondegenerate. Suppose
\eta\in S_{W}|_{p}
is such that
\sigma(\eta\wedge-)
is the zero functional on
S_{W}|_{p}
. Then in particular
\eta\wedge\eta=0
, so
\eta
is decomposable. Furthermore, as

\eta\ \in\ \mathrm{Ker}\left(\alpha|_{p}\colon\wedge^{2}W|_{p}\ \to\ M|_{p}\right),

if
\eta
is nonzero then
\eta=v\wedge w
where
v
and
w
span a subspace
\Lambda\subset W|_{p}
isotropic with respect to
\alpha
. But then for any
v^{\prime},w^{\prime}\in W|_{p}
spanning an
\alpha
-isotropic subspace complementary to
\Lambda
, we have
v^{\prime}\wedge w^{\prime}\in S_{W}|_{p}
and
v\wedge w\wedge v^{\prime}\wedge w^{\prime}\neq 0
in
M^{2}|_{p}
. Thus
\eta
must be zero, and
\sigma|_{\left(S_{W}\right)^{\otimes 2}}
is nondegenerate. Hence
S_{W}
is
M^{2}
-valued orthogonal.

For the rest: By the nondegeneracy,
S_{W}\cap(S_{W})^{\perp}=0
in
\wedge^{2}W
. Comparing determinants, we see that
(S_{W})^{\perp}\cong M
. Therefore,
\wedge^{2}W=S_{W}\perp M
as desired. ∎

Again, we get morphisms of moduli spaces from this observation. For any line bundle
M
, we denote by
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}_{C}(4,M)
the moduli space of semistable
M
-valued symplectic bundles of rank
4
over
C
. This is a closed irreducible subvariety of
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{U}}_{C}(2n,M^{n})
of dimension
10(g-1)
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
x
be any point of
C
.

(a)

The association
W\mapsto S_{W}
defines a finite surjective morphism

\Theta_{5}^{0}\colon{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})\ \to\ {\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{0}(5,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}).

(b)

The association
W_{1}\mapsto S_{W_{1}}\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
defines a finite surjective morphism

\Theta_{5}^{1}\colon{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(-x))\to\ {\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{1}(5,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}).

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{M}
be an étale cover of
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}
admitting a Poincaré family
{\mathcal{W}}\to\widetilde{M}\times C
together with a symplectic form
\tilde{\alpha}\colon\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{W}}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{M}\times C}
. Then by Proposition
7.1
we see that
S_{{\mathcal{W}}}:=\mathrm{Ker}(\tilde{\alpha})
is a family of rank five orthogonal bundles. Moreover, since
{\mathbb{C}}
has characteristic zero, if a bundle
W
is stable then
\wedge^{2}W
is polystable. Hence the family
S_{{\mathcal{W}}}
defines a morphism
\widetilde{M}\to{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{0}(5,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
which factorizes via a morphism
\Theta_{5}^{0}\colon{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}\to{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{0}(5,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
.

Next, note that if
W
and
W^{\prime}
are S-equivalent semistable
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
-valued symplectic bundles, then

\mathrm{gr}(S_{W})\oplus{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}\ =\ \mathrm{gr}(\wedge^{2}W)\ \cong\ \mathrm{gr}(\wedge^{2}W^{\prime})\ =\ \mathrm{gr}(S_{W^{\prime}})\oplus{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}.

Therefore, if
W\sim W^{\prime}
then
S_{W}\sim S_{W^{\prime}}
. Now by
[
BLS89
]
, the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
has Picard number one. Thus part (a) can be proven similarly as before.
The proof of (b) is similar.

As before, the target component is determined by the parity of degree of rank two isotropic subbundles. The details are described in Theorem
7.3
. ∎

As in previous cases, we now describe how to construct an “inverse” for the operation
W\mapsto S_{W}
, which is valid for an arbitrary
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank five.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
L
be a line bundle of degree
2m
, and
V
a rank five
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of determinant
M^{5}
, where
M^{2}\cong L
.

(a)

The bundle
V
admits an isotropic subbundle
E
of rank two.

(b)

If
\deg(E)\equiv m\mod 2
, then there is an
M
-valued symplectic bundle
(W,\alpha)
of rank four (and determinant
L
) such that
V\cong S_{W}
.

(c)

If
\deg(E)\not\equiv m\mod 2
, then there is an
M(-x)
-valued symplectic bundle
(W_{1},\alpha_{1})
of rank four (and determinant
L(-2x)
) such that
V\cong S_{W_{1}}\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
.

Proof.

As
M^{2}\cong L
, in fact
M
itself is an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle. The orthogonal direct sum
V\perp M
is a rank six orthogonal bundle of determinant
L^{3}
. Then
V\perp M
admits a rank three isotropic subbundle
\overline{E}
by Proposition
3.2
. It is easy to see that

E\ :=\ \overline{E}\cap V\ =\ \mathrm{Ker}\left(\overline{E}\ \to\ (V\perp M)\ \to\ M\right)

is an isotropic subbundle of rank two in
V
. Thus we obtain (a).

Note moreover that
\deg(E)+m=\deg(\overline{E})
. Suppose
\deg(E)\equiv m\mod 2
. Then
\deg(\overline{E})
is even. By Theorem
6.4
(a), there exists a bundle
W
of rank four and determinant
L
such that
V\perp M\cong\wedge^{2}W
.

Now the subbundle
M\subset\wedge^{2}W
is nowhere isotropic with respect to the orthogonal structure, being an orthogonal direct summand. By Proposition
6.5
, vectors in
\wedge^{2}W
isotropic with respect to the orthogonal structure correspond precisely to the decomposable tensors. Therefore, the image of
M\to\wedge^{2}W
is indecomposable at all points. Tensoring by
W^{*}
and contracting, we obtain an antisymmetric isomorphism

M\otimes W^{*}\ \to\ \left(\wedge^{2}W\right)\otimes W^{*}\ \to\ W,

equivalently, an
M
-valued symplectic structure on
W
. By Proposition
7.1
, we have

V\perp M\ =\ \wedge^{2}W\ =\ S_{W}\perp M.

By uniqueness of direct sum decompositions, we have
V\cong S_{W}
as vector bundles; and by
[
Gro57
, Proposition 3.1]
they are isomorphic as orthogonal bundles. This proves (b).

If
\deg(E)\not\equiv m\mod 2
, then
\deg(\overline{E})
is odd. Then
V(-x)\perp M(-x)
is
L(-2x)
-valued orthogonal and admits the isotropic subbundle
\overline{E}(-x)
which is of rank three and even degree. By part (a), there exists a rank four
M(-x)
-valued symplectic bundle
W_{1}
such that
V(-x)\cong S_{W_{1}}
, as desired. ∎

7.2.
Enveloping bundle structure

Let
W
be a rank four
M
-valued symplectic bundle as above, and
V=S_{W}
the associated rank five
M^{2}
-valued orthogonal bundle. We now determine the quadric fibration
Q_{V}\subset{\mathbb{P}}V
.

By Proposition
6.5
, the projective bundle
{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}W)
is an enveloping bundle for
Q_{\wedge^{2}W}=\mathrm{Gr}(2,W)
. As the orthogonal structure on
V
is inherited from
\wedge^{2}W
, we have

Q_{S_{W}}\ =\ Q_{\wedge^{2}W}\cap{\mathbb{P}}V.

Now recall from
[
GH78
, p. 759 ff.]
that a
linear line complex in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
is a three-dimensional family of lines defined by a hyperplane section of the Plücker image of
\mathrm{Gr}(2,{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{5}
. The datum of a linear line complex is equivalent to a choice of bilinear antisymmetric form on
{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
up to scalar, which is nondegenerate if and only if the intersection is smooth; equivalently, the hyperplane is not tangent to
\mathrm{Gr}(2,{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
at any point. By the above discussion, for each
p\in C
, the hyperplane
{\mathbb{P}}V\subset{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{2}W)
determines a smooth linear line complex in
{\mathbb{P}}W|_{p}
. Clearly this is isomorphic to the Lagrangian Grassmannian
\mathrm{LG}(W|_{p})
parameterizing two-dimensional
\alpha
-isotropic subspaces of
W|_{p}
. Summarizing, we have:

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
V=S_{W}
be as above. Then the projectivization of the locus of isotropic vectors in
{\mathbb{P}}V
is the Lagrangian Grassmann bundle
\mathrm{LG}(W)
.

7.3.
Isotropic subbundles of
S_{W}

Let
W
be a rank four
M
-valued symplectic bundle. To classify rank two isotropic subbundles of the rank five
M^{2}
-valued orthogonal bundle
S_{W}
, we could use a strategy similar to that in
[
CCH21b
, § 8]
. However, we offer instead an approach exploiting the geometry of the enveloping bundle, which also gives further insight into the rank six case.

Let
E\subset S_{W}
be a rank two isotropic subbundle. Then
{\mathbb{P}}E
is a
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundle contained in
\mathrm{LG}(W)
. As above, by nondegeneracy of the symplectic form, for each
p\in C
the intersection
{\mathbb{P}}(S_{W}|_{p})\cap\mathrm{Gr}(2,W|_{p})
is smooth. Then by
[
GH78
, p. 759]
, there exist a line
N_{p}
and a hyperplane
H_{p}
in
W|_{p}
such that
{\mathbb{P}}W
parameterizes the pencil of two-dimensional (isotropic) subspaces of
V|_{p}
forming a complete flag

0\ \subset\ \ N_{p}\ \subset\ \Lambda\ \subset\ H_{p}\ \subset\ W|_{p}.

It follows that

(7.1)

E|_{p}\ =\ \{v\wedge w\in\wedge^{2}W:v\in N_{p},w\in H_{p}\}\ \cong\ N_{p}\otimes\frac{H_{p}}{N_{p}}.

We claim moreover that
H_{p}=N_{p}^{\perp}
, the orthogonal complement of
N_{p}
with respect to the symplectic form
\alpha
. To see this:
H_{p}
contains a pencil of two-dimensional subspaces containing
N_{p}
which are moreover isotropic, so in particular annihilate
N_{p}
. As
H_{p}
has dimension three, it is spanned by elements of
N_{p}^{\perp}
. As
\dim(N_{p}^{\perp})=\dim H_{p}
, we have equality. Using local triviality, we see that the union of the subspaces
N_{p}
is a (uniquely determined) line subbundle
N\subset W
.

We now define a map
\lambda_{N}\colon N\otimes N^{\perp}\to\wedge^{2}W
by

\lambda_{N}(v\otimes w)\ =\ v\wedge w.

Clearly
\lambda_{N}
is the restriction to
N\otimes N^{\perp}
of the map
\tau_{N}
defined in (
6.7
), and

\lambda_{N}(N\otimes N^{\perp})\ =\ \tau_{N}(N\otimes V)\cap\left(\wedge^{2}N^{\perp}\right)\ \cong\ N\otimes\frac{N^{\perp}}{N}.

Now as
N^{\perp}/N
is
M
-valued symplectic of rank two, it has determinant
M
and degree
m
. Thus
\deg\mathrm{Im}\,(\lambda_{N})=2\cdot\deg N+m
.

Conversely, given any line subbundle
N\subset W
, the subbundle
\lambda_{N}(N\otimes N^{\perp})
is a rank two subbundle of
\wedge^{2}W
. As
\alpha(v\wedge w)=0
for any
v\in N
and
w\in N^{\perp}
, we have
\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\lambda_{N}\right)\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}\left(\alpha\colon\wedge^{2}W\to M\right)=S_{W}
. As moreover every element of
\mathrm{Im}\,(\lambda_{N})
is decomposable,
{\mathbb{P}}\mathrm{Im}\,(\Lambda_{N})
is contained in
Q_{\wedge^{2}W}\cap{\mathbb{P}}S_{W}=\mathrm{LG}(W)
. Hence
\mathrm{Im}\,(\Lambda_{N})
is isotropic by Lemma
6.6
. Thus we may summarize as follows.

Theorem 7.5
.

The association
N\mapsto\lambda_{N}(N\otimes N^{\perp})
defines isomorphisms
\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,d}(W)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{2,2d+m}(S_{W})
. Note also that the association

N\mapsto\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\lambda_{N}\right)\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
defines an isomorphism

\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{1,d}(W)\cong\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{2,2d+m+2}(S_{W}\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)).

Remark 7.6
.

Let
E
be a rank two isotropic subbundle of
S_{W}
. We observe from the description (
7.1
) that

E\ =\ \tau_{N}(N\otimes V)\cap\left(\wedge^{2}N^{\perp}\right);

that is,
E
is the intersection of two isotropic rank three subbundles
\wedge^{2}N^{\perp}
and
N\otimes\frac{W}{N}
of the rank six orthogonal bundle
\wedge^{2}W
, as described in Theorem
6.8
. Moreover, in view of
[
GH78
, Proposition, p. 735]
, the fact that

\dim\left(\wedge^{2}\left(N^{\perp}|_{p}\right)\cap\lambda_{N}(N\otimes N^{\perp})\right)\ =\ 2\ \not\equiv 3\mod 2

reflects the fact that these two subbundles define elements of opposite components of the orthogonal Grassmannian
\mathrm{OG}(3,\wedge^{2}V|_{p})
at each point.

Next, by Theorem
7.4
, an isotropic line subbundle of
S_{W}
is nothing but a section of the Lagrangian Grassmannian bundle
\mathrm{LG}(W)\to C
. This equivalent to a choice of rank two isotropic subbundle
E\subset W
, and then
\wedge^{2}E
is the isotropic line subbundle of
S_{W}
. Thus we have:

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
W
be an
M
-valued symplectic bundle and
S_{W}
the associated rank five
M^{2}
-valued orthogonal bundle. Then for each
d
, the assignment
E\mapsto\wedge^{2}E
defines an isomorphism

\mathrm{LQ}_{d}^{\circ}(W)\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ \mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{1,d}(S_{W}),

where
\mathrm{LQ}_{d}^{\circ}(W)
is the subscheme of
\mathrm{Quot}^{\circ}_{2,d}(W)
consisting of Lagrangian (maximal rank isotropic) subbundles of the symplectic bundle
W
.

7.3.1.
Enumeration of maximal isotropic subbundles

By Theorem
7.5
, the number of rank two isotropic subbundles of maximal degree of
V
equals the number of maximal line subbundles of the rank four symplectic bundle
W
. However, the counting formula in Theorem
2.5
is valid only for a general vector bundle of rank
four, while the symplectic bundles of rank four are contained in a proper closed subset of the moduli of vector bundles, so we cannot apply Theorem
2.5
directly. Therefore, we leave the following as a conjecture.

Conjecture 7.8
.

Let
V
be a stable rank five
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
-valued orthogonal bundle which is general in its component of moduli.

(a)

Suppose
g\equiv 1\mod 4
. Suppose
w_{2}(V)=0
, so that
V\cong S_{W}
for a
W\in{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
which may be assumed to be general. Then
V
has
4^{g}
maximal rank two isotropic subbundles, each of degree
-\frac{3}{2}(g-1)
.

(b)

Suppose
g\equiv 3\mod 4
. Suppose
w_{2}(V)=1
, so that
V\cong S_{W_{1}}\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
for some
W_{1}\in{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{S}}_{C}(4,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(-x))
which may be assumed to be general. Then
V
has
4^{g}
rank two isotropic subbundles of maximal degree
-\frac{3}{2}(g-1)
.

On the other hand, for isotropic line subbundles we have the following.

Theorem 7.9
.

Let
V
be a rank five

{\mathcal{O}}_{C}
-valued
orthogonal bundle which is general in its component of moduli. Then each isotropic line subbundle has degree
1-g
, and the number of such subbundles is given by

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|}&g\hbox{ even}&g\hbox{ odd}\\
\hline\cr w_{2}(V)=0&2^{g-1}\cdot(3^{g}-1)&2^{g-1}\cdot(3^{g}+1)\\
\hline\cr w_{2}(V)=1&2^{g-1}\cdot(3^{g}+1)&2^{g-1}\cdot(3^{g}-1)\end{array}

Proof.

Suppose
w_{2}(V)=1
. By Theorem
7.2
(b), we may assume that
V=S_{W}\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
for a rank four
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(-x)
-valued symplectic
W
which is general in moduli. By Theorem
7.7
, each maximal isotropic line subbundle is of the form
(\wedge^{2}E)\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x)
for a maximal Lagrangian subbundle
E\subset W
. By
[
CCH21a
, Proposition 3.2]
we have
\deg(E)=-g
, whence
\deg(\wedge^{2}E\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(x))=1-g
; and the number of such
E
is given by
[
CCH21a
, Corollary 6.3
(2) and (4)
]
. The case where
w_{1}(V)=0
is similar. ∎

8.
Higher rank

We shall now conclude by formulating some conjectures on orthogonal bundles of higher rank and their isotropic Quot schemes in general, based on the extra geometric information for low rank gathered in the previous sections. We shall use results from the literature to a greater extent than before.

Let
i\in\{0,1\}
. By
[
CH14
, § 5.4]
and
[
CH15
, Theorem 6.1]
, a general
V\in{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{i}(2n,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
or
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{i}(2n-1,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
has a finite number of isotropic subbundles of rank
n
and maximal degree
-\frac{1}{2}n(g-1)
under the following congruence condition:

n(g-1)\equiv 2i\mod 4

We denote this number by
N^{0}(g,r)
, where
r=2n
or
2n-1
.

Theorems
4.12
,
5.6
and
6.15
and Conjecture
7.8
suggest the following equalities for arbitrary rank:

Conjecture 8.1
.

Let
g,r,n,i
be as above. For all
r\geq 3
, we have

N^{0}(g,2n)\ =\ 2\cdot N^{0}(g,2n-1).

An intuition behind the conjecture is as follows. We have briefly mentioned the orthogonal Grassmannian bundle
\mathrm{OG}(V)
of an orthogonal bundle
V
, which parameterizes isotropic subspaces of maximal dimension in fibers of
V
; equivalently, projective linear subspaces of maximal dimension contained in the quadrics
Q_{V}|_{x}
. If
V
has rank
2n
then by
[
CCH21b
, Proposition 2.12]
, the fibration
\mathrm{OG}(V)
has two connected components
\mathrm{OG}(V)_{0}
and
\mathrm{OG}(V)_{1}
. (This has a particularly clear geometric interpretation in rank four; see Remark
4.9
.) Following
[
CCH21b
, Definition 3.1]
, we define
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{n,e}(V)_{\delta}
to be the component of
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{n,e}(V)
parameterizing subbundles belonging to
\mathrm{OG}(V)_{\delta}
. By
[
CH15
, Proposition 3.4 (3)]
and
[
CCH21b
]
, for all
e
, there are canonical isomorphisms

\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{n,e}\left(V\perp{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}\right)_{\delta}\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ \mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{n-1,e}(V)

for
\delta\in\{0,1\}
. (See Remark
7.6
for related discussion for rank five.) In particular, the number of maximal isotropic subbundles of
V\perp{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
is exactly twice that of
V
. However, since a bundle of the form
V\perp{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
is not general in
{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}^{i}(2n,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
, we need an argument on the conservation of number.

The same argument suggests:

Conjecture 8.2
.

If a general
V\in{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(2n,{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}})
has a finite number
N^{0}(g,2n)
of rank
n
maximal isotropic subbundles, then exactly half of them define sections of
\mathrm{OG}(V)_{0}
and another half those of
\mathrm{OG}(V)_{1}
.

In a forthcoming paper with D. Cheong, we propose to compute the numbers
N^{i}(r,g)
more generally and to investigate the validity of the above conjectures in general.

Metric dimension on sparse graphs and its applications to zero forcing sets An extended abstract of this work has been published in EuroComb 2021 [ 1 ] . The first three authors of this work are supported by ANR project GrR (ANR-18-CE40-0032). The Ignacio M. Pelayo work is supported by project PGC2018-095471-B-100.
Nicolas Bousquet, Quentin Deschamps, Aline Parreau, Ignacio M. Pelayo

Abstract
The metric dimension dim ( G ) \dim(G) of a graph G G is the minimum cardinality of a subset S S of vertices of G G such that each vertex of G G is uniquely determined by its distances to S S . It is well-known that the metric dimension of a graph can be drastically increased by the modification of a single edge. Our main result consists in proving that the increase of the metric dimension of an edge addition can be amortized in the sense that if the graph consists of a spanning tree T T plus c c edges, then the metric dimension of G G is at most the metric dimension of T T plus 6 ​ c 6c . We then use this result to prove a weakening of a conjecture of Eroh et al. The zero forcing number Z ⁡ ( G ) Z(G) of G G is the minimum cardinality of a subset S S of black vertices (whereas the other vertices are colored white) of G G such that all the vertices will turned black after applying finitely many times the following rule: a white vertex is turned black if it is the only white neighbor of a black vertex. Eroh et al. conjectured that, for any graph G G , dim ( G ) ≤ Z ⁡ ( G ) + c ⁡ ( G ) \dim(G)\leq Z(G)+c(G) , where c ⁡ ( G ) c(G) is the number of edges that have to be removed from G G to get a forest. They proved the conjecture is true for trees and unicyclic graphs. We prove a weaker version of the conjecture: dim ( G ) ≤ Z ⁡ ( G ) + 6 ​ c ​ ( G ) \dim(G)\leq Z(G)+6c(G) holds for any graph. We also prove that the conjecture is true for graphs with edge disjoint cycles, widely generalizing the unicyclic result of Eroh et al.

One of the main issues to compute the metric dimension of a graph comes from the fact that it is unstable when the graph is modified. When a vertex is added, the metric dimension can be drastically modified. Indeed, while a path admits a constant size resolving set, a path plus a universal vertex only admits resolving sets of linear size (in the number of vertices). However, in this example even if only one vertex was added, a linear number of edges were also added which had permitted to put all the vertices at distance
2

Metric dimension and cycle rank.

Eroh et al.
[
8

proved that, if

One can then wonder if the metric dimension of a graph which does not contain many cycles is close to the metric dimension of any of its spanning trees. The goal of this paper is to answer this question positively.

One can easily remark that in the star

2.1
Definitions and notations

Unless otherwise stated, all the graphs considered in this paper are undirected, simple, finite and connected. For standard terminology and notations on graphs, we refer the reader to

Let

For

A vertex

The metric dimension

Let

The zero forcing number

The

(or

Resolving sets and zero forcing sets on trees

Chartrand et al.
[

We extend this terminology to general graphs (see Figure

A vertex of degree

A vertex of degree at least 3 is a

A degree-

This section is devoted to some elementary results about metric dimension.

Lemma 10

Let

∎

d(v,\alpha)=d(w,\alpha)\text{ gives }d(v,u_{v})+d(u_{v},u_{\alpha})+d(u_{\alpha},\alpha)=d(w,u_{w})+d(u_{w},u_{\alpha})+d(u_{\alpha},\alpha);

and equalizing we get

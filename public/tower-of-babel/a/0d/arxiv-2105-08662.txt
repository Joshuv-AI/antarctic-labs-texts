Abstract
. In this article we study the well-posedness of the Master Equation of Mean Field Games in a framework of Neumann boundary condition. The definition of solution is closely related to the classical one of the Mean Field Games system, but the boundary condition here leads to two Neumann conditions in the Master Equation formulation, for both space and measure. The global regularity of the linearized system, which is crucial in order to prove the existence of solutions, is obtained with a deep study of the boundary conditions and the global regularity at the boundary of a suitable class of parabolic equations.



1.
Introduction

Mean Field Games theory is devoted to the study of differential games with a large number
N
of small and indistinguishable agents. The theory was initially introduced by J.-M. Lasry and P.-L. Lions in 2006 (
[
27
,
28
,
30
,
29
]
), using tools from mean-field theories, and in the same years by P. Caines, M. Huang and R. Malhamé
[
23
]
.

The macroscopic description used in mean field game theory leads to study coupled systems of PDEs, where the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation satisfied by the single agent’s value function
u
is coupled with the Kolmogorov Fokker-Planck equation satisfied by the distribution law of the population
m
. The simplest form of this system is the following

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}u-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}u)+H(x,Du)=F(x,m)\,,\\
\partial_{t}m-\sum\limits_{i,j}\partial_{ij}^{2}(a_{ij}(x)m)-\mathrm{div}(mH_{p}(x,Du))=0\,,\\
m(0)=m_{0}\,,\hskip 56.9055ptu(T)=G(x,m(T))\,.\end{cases}

Here,
H
is called the
Hamiltonian
of the system, whereas
a
is a uniformly elliptic matrix, representing the square of the diffusion term in the stochastic dynamic of the generic player, and
F
and
G
are the running cost and the final cost related to the generic player.

In his lectures at Collège de France
[
31
]
, P.-L. Lions proved that the solutions
(u,m)
of (
1.1
) are the trajectories of a new infinite dimensional partial differential equation.
This
PDE
is called
Master Equation
and summarizes the informations contained in (
1.1
) in a unique equation.

The definition of the Master Equation is related to its characteristics, which are solution of the MFG system. To be more precise, considering the solution
(u,m)
of the system (
1.1
) with initial condition
m(t_{0})=m_{0}
, one defines the function

(1.2)

U:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}\,,\qquad U(t_{0},x,m_{0})=u(t_{0},x)\,,

where
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
is the set of Borel probability measures on
\Omega
.

In order to give sense at this definition, the equation (
1.1
) must have a unique solution defined in
[0,T]\times\Omega
, for all
(t_{0},m_{0})
. So, we assume that
F
and
G
are
monotone
functions with respect to the measure variable, a structure condition which ensures the existence of a unique solution for large time interval.

If we compute, at least formally, the equation satisfied by
U
, we obtain a Hamilton-Jacobi equation in the space of measures, called Master Equation.

The relevance of the Master Equation was recognized in different papers, and most important topics like existence, uniqueness and regularity results have been developed. For example, in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
Bensoussan, Frehse and Yam reformulated this equation as a PDE set on an
L^{2}
space, and in
[
13
]
Carmona and Delarue interpreted it as a decoupling field of forward-backward stochastic differential equations in infinite dimension. A very general result of well-posedness of the Master Equation was given by Cardaliaguet, Delarue, Lasry and Lions in
[
11
]
.

So far, most of the literature, especially in the Master Equation’s papers, considers the case where the state variable
x
belongs to the flat torus (i.e. periodic solutions,
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
), or, especially in the probabilistic literature, in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

But in many economic and financial applications it is useful to work with a process that remains in a certain domain of existence; thus, some conditions at the boundary need to be prescribed. See for instance the models analyzed by Achdou, Buera et al. in
[
1
]
.

In this paper we want to analyze this situation, by studying the well-posedness of the Master Equation in a framework of Neumann condition at the boundary.

In this case the MFG system (
1.1
) is constrained with the following boundary conditions: for all
x\in\partial\Omega

(1.3)

a(x)D_{x}u(t,x)\cdot\nu(x)=0\,,\qquad{[}a(x)Dm(t,x)+m(H_{p}(x,Du)+\tilde{b}(x)){]}\cdot\nu(x)=0\,,

where
\nu(\cdot)
is the outward normal at
\partial\Omega
and
\tilde{b}
is a vector field defined as follows:

\tilde{b}_{i}(x)={\sum}\limits_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial a_{ji}}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\hskip 2.27626pt,\hskip 56.9055pti=1,\dots,d\hskip 2.27626pt.

.

The Master Equation, in this case, takes the following form

(1.4)

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&-\,\partial_{t}U(t,x,m)-\mathrm{tr}\left(a(x)D_{x}^{2}U(t,x,m)\right)+H\left(x,D_{x}U(t,x,m)\right)\\
&-{\int_{\Omega}}\mathrm{tr}\left(a(y)D_{y}D_{m}U(t,x,m,y)\right)dm(y)\\
&+{\int_{\Omega}}D_{m}U(t,x,m,y)\cdot H_{p}(y,D_{x}U(t,y,m))dm(y)=F(x,m)\\
&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\hskip 2.27626pt,\\
&U(T,x,m)=G(x,m)\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{in }\Omega\times\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\hskip 2.27626pt,\\
&a(x)D_{x}U(t,x,m)\cdot\nu(x)=0\hskip 28.45274pt\quad\,\mbox{for }(t,x,m)\in(0,T)\times\partial\Omega\times\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\,,\\
&a(y)D_{m}U(t,x,m,y)\cdot\nu(y)=0\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{for }(t,x,m,y)\in(0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\times\partial\Omega\,,\end{array}\right.\end{split}

where
D_{m}U
is a derivation of
U
with respect to the measure, whose precise definition will be given later. This definition, anyway, is strictly related to the one given by Ambrosio, Gigli and Savaré in
[
2
]
and by Lions in
[
31
]
.

We stress the fact that the last boundary condition, i.e.

a(y)D_{m}U(t,x,m,y)\cdot\nu(y)=0\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{for }(t,x,m,y)\in(0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\times\partial\Omega\,,

is completely new in the literature. It relies on the fact that we have to pay attention to the space where
U
is defined, i.e.
[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
. Then, together with final data and Neumann condition with respect to
x
, there is another boundary condition caused by the boundary of
\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
.

The importance of this equation arises in the so-called
convergence problem
. The Mean Field Games system approximates the
N
-player differential game, in the sense that the optimal strategies in the Mean Field Games system provide approximated Nash equilibria (called
\varepsilon
-Nash equilibria) in the
N
-player game. See, for instance,
[
12
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
.

Conversely, the convergence of the Nash Equilibria in the
N
-player game towards an optimal strategy in the Mean Field Games presents many difficulties, due to the lack of compactness properties of the problem. Hence, the Master Equation plays an instrumental role in order to study this problem. A convergence result in a framework of Neumann conditions at the boundary will be given in the forthcoming paper
[
34
]
.

There are many papers about the well-posedness of the Master Equation. We point out here that these papers are studied in two different contexts: the first case is the so-called
First order Master Equation
, studied in this article, where the Brownian motions in the dynamic of the agents in the
N
-player differential game are independent each other. The second case is the
Second order Master Equation
, or
Master Equation with common noise
. In this case, the dynamic has also
an additional Brownian term
dW_{t}
, which is common to all players. This leads to a different and more difficult type of Master Equation, with some additional terms depending also on the second derivative
D_{mm}U
. It is relevant to say that Mean Field Games with common noise were already studied by Carmona, Delarue and Lacker in
[
14
]
.

Some preliminary results about the Master Equation were given by Lions in
[
31
]
and a first exhaustive result of existence and uniqueness of solutions was proved, with a probabilistic approach, by Chassagneux, Crisan and Delarue in
[
9
]
, who worked in a framework with diffusion and without common noise. Buckhdan, Li, Peng and Rainer in
[
8
]
proved the existence of a classical solution using probabilistic arguments, when there is no coupling and no common noise. Furthermore, Gangbo and Swiech proved a short time existence for the Master Equation with common noise, see
[
22
]
.

But the most important result in this framework was achieved by Cardaliaguet, Delarue, Lasry and Lions in
[
11
]
, who proved existence and uniqueness of solutions for the Master Equation with and without common noise, including applications to the convergence problem, in a periodic setting (
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
). Other recent results about Master Equation and convergence problem can be found in
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
10
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
20
,
21
,
33
]
.

This article follows the main ideas of
[
11
]
, but many issues appear, connected to the Neumann boundary condition, and more effort has to be done in order to gain the same results.

The function
U
is defined as in (
1.2
) and some estimates like global bounds and global Lipschitz regularity are proved.
The main issue in order to obtain that
U
solves (
1.4
) is to prove the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
character of
U
with respect to
m
. This step requires a careful analysis of the linearized mean field game system (see
[
11
]
) in order to prove strong regularity of U in the space and in the measure variable.

However, these estimates requires strong regularities of
U
, and so of the Mean Field Games system, in the space and in the measure variable.

The space regularity is obtained in
[
11
]
by differentiating the equation with respect to
x
. But in the Neumann case, and in general in any case of boundary conditions, these methods obviously cannot be applied so straightly, and these bounds are obtained using different kind of space-time estimates, which must be handled with care.

Indeed, regularity estimates for Neumann parabolic equation require compatibility conditions between initial and boundary data. Unfortunately, these compatibility conditions will be not always guaranteed in this context. This forces us to generalize the estimates obtained in
[
11
]
, by a deeper study of the regularity of solutions for the Fokker-Planck equation.

The article is divided as follows.

In section
2
we define some useful tools and we state the main assumptions we will need in order to prove the next results.

In the rest of the article (section
3
to
6
), we analyze the well-posedness of the Master Equation (
1.4
).

The idea is quite classical: for each
(t_{0},m_{0})
we consider the
MFG
system (
1.1
) in
[t_{0},T]\times\Omega
, with conditions (
1.3
), and we define

(1.5)

U(t_{0},x,m_{0})=u(t_{0},x)\,.

Then we prove that
U
is a solution of the Master Equation.

Section
3-5
are completely devoted to prove technical results to ensure this kind of differentability.

In section
3
we prove a first estimate of a solution
(u,m)
of the Mean Field Games system, namely

\left\Arrowvert u\right\Arrowvert_{1+\frac{\alpha}{2},2+\alpha}\leq C\,,\qquad\mathbf{d}_{1}(m(t),m(s))\leq C|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,

where
\mathbf{d}_{1}
is a distance between measures called
Wasserstein distance
, whose definition will be given in Section
3.2
.

In section
4
we use the definition of
U
from the Mean Field Games system in order to prove a Lipschitz character of
U
with respect to
m
:

\left\Arrowvert U(t,\cdot,m_{1})-U(t,\cdot,m_{2})\right\Arrowvert_{2+\alpha}\leq C\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{1},m_{2})\,.

In section
5
we prove the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
character of
U
with respect to
m
. This goes through different estimates on linearized MFG systems.

Once proved the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
character of
U
, we can prove that
U
is actually the unique solution of the Master Equation (
1.4
). This will be done in Section
6
.

2.
Notation, Assumptions and Main Result

Throughout this chapter, we fix a time
T>0
.
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
will be the closure of an open bounded set, with boundary of class
\mathcal{C}^{2+\alpha}
, and we define
Q_{T}:=[0,T]\times\Omega
.

For
n\geq 0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
we denote with
\mathcal{C}^{n+\alpha}(\Omega)
, or simply
\mathcal{C}^{n+\alpha}
, the space of functions
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{n}(\Omega)
with, for each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
,
1\leq r\leq n
, the derivative
D^{\ell}\phi
is Hölder continuous with Hölder constant
\alpha
. The norm is defined in the following way:

\displaystyle\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{n+\alpha}:=\sum\limits_{|\ell|\leq n}\left\Arrowvert D^{l}\phi\right\Arrowvert_{\infty}+\sum\limits_{|\ell|=n}\sup\limits_{x\neq y}\frac{|D^{\ell}\phi(x)-D^{\ell}\phi(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\,.

Sometimes, in order to deal with Neumann boundary conditions, we will need to work with a suitable subspace of
\mathcal{C}^{n+\alpha}(\Omega)
.

So we will call
\mathcal{C}^{n+\alpha,N}(\Omega)
, or simply
\mathcal{C}^{n+\alpha,N}
, the set of functions
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{n+\alpha}
such that
aD\phi\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0
, endowed with the same norm
\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{n+\alpha}
.

Then, we define several parabolic spaces we will need to work with during the chapter, starting from
\mathcal{C}^{\frac{n+\alpha}{2},n+\alpha}([0,T]\times\Omega)
.

We say that
\phi:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is in
\mathcal{C}^{\frac{n+\alpha}{2},n+\alpha}([0,T]\times\Omega)
if
\phi
is continuous in both variables, together with all derivatives
D_{t}^{r}D_{x}^{s}\phi
, with
2r+s\leq n
. Moreover,
\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{\frac{n+\alpha}{2},n+\alpha}
is bounded, where

\displaystyle\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{\frac{n+\alpha}{2},n+\alpha}:=\sum\limits_{2r+s\leq n}\left\Arrowvert D_{t}^{r}D^{s}_{x}\phi\right\Arrowvert_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{2r+s=n}\sup\limits_{t}\left\Arrowvert D_{t}^{r}D_{x}^{s}\phi(t,\cdot)\right\Arrowvert_{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{0<n+\alpha-2r-s<2}\sup\limits_{x}\left\Arrowvert D_{t}^{r}D_{x}^{s}\phi(\cdot,x)\right\Arrowvert_{\frac{n+\alpha-2r-s}{2}}\,.

The space of continuous space-time functions which satisfy a Hölder condition in
x
will be denoted by
\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}([0,T]\times\Omega)
. It is endowed with the norm

\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{0,\alpha}=\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left\Arrowvert\phi(t,\cdot)\right\Arrowvert_{\alpha}\,.

The same definition can be given for the space
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha,0}
. Finally, we define the space
\mathcal{C}^{1,2+\alpha}
of functions differentiable in time and twice differentiable in space, with all derivatives in
\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{T}})
. The natural norm for this space is

\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{1,2+\alpha}:=\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{\infty}+\left\Arrowvert\phi_{t}\right\Arrowvert_{0,\alpha}+\left\Arrowvert D_{x}\phi\right\Arrowvert_{\infty}+\left\Arrowvert D^{2}_{x}\phi\right\Arrowvert_{0,\alpha}\,.

We note that, thanks to
Lemma 5.1.1
of
[
32
]
, the first order derivatives of
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{1,2}
satisfy also a Hölder condition in time. Namely

(2.1)

\left\Arrowvert D_{x}\phi\right\Arrowvert_{\frac{1}{2},\alpha}\leq C\left\Arrowvert f\right\Arrowvert_{1,2+\alpha}\,.

In order to study distributional solutions for the Fokker-Planck equation, we also need to define a structure for the dual spaces of regular functions.

We define, for
n\geq 0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the space
\mathcal{C}^{-(n+\alpha)}(\Omega)
, called for simplicity
\mathcal{C}^{-(n+\alpha)}
in this article, as the dual space of
\mathcal{C}^{n+\alpha}
, endowed with the norm

\left\Arrowvert\rho\right\Arrowvert_{-(n+\alpha)}=\sup\limits_{\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{n+\alpha}\leq 1}\langle\rho,\phi\rangle\,.

With the same notations we define the space
\mathcal{C}^{-(n+\alpha),N}
as the dual space of
\mathcal{C}^{n+\alpha,N}
endowed with the same norm:

\left\Arrowvert\rho\right\Arrowvert_{-(n+\alpha),N}=\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{n+\alpha}\leq 1\\
aD\phi\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\end{subarray}}\langle\rho,\phi\rangle\,.

Finally, for
k\geq 1
and
1\leq p\leq+\infty
, we can also define the space
W^{-k,p}(\Omega)
, called for simplicity
W^{-k,p}
, as the dual space of
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
, endowed with the norm

\left\Arrowvert\rho\right\Arrowvert_{W^{-k,p}}=\sup\limits_{\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{W^{k,p}}\leq 1}\langle\rho,\phi\rangle\,.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
two Borel probability measures on
\Omega
.

We call the
Wasserstein distance
between
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
, and we write
\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{1},m_{2})
the quantity

(2.2)

\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{1},m_{2}):=\sup\limits_{Lip(\phi)\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)d(m_{1}-m_{2})(x)\,.

We note that we can also write (
2.2
) as

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{1},m_{2}):=\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq C\\
Lip(\phi)\leq 1\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)d(m_{1}-m_{2})(x)\,,

for a certain
C>0
. Actually, for a fixed
x_{0}\in\Omega
, we can restrict ourselves to the functions
\phi
such that
\phi(x_{0})=0
, since

\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)d(m_{1}-m_{2})(x)=\int_{\Omega}(\phi(x)-\phi(x_{0}))d(m_{1}-m_{2})(x)\,,

and these functions obviously satisfies
\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq C
for a certain
C>0
.

We will always work with (
2.3
), where the restriction in
W^{1,\infty}
allows us to obtain some desired estimates with respect to
\mathbf{d}_{1}
.

In order to give a sense to equation (
1.4
), we need to define a suitable derivation of
U
with respect to the measure
m
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
U:\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
. We say that
U
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
if there exists a continuous map
K:\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that, for all
m_{1}
,
m_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
we have

(2.4)

\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\frac{U(m_{1}+s(m_{2}-m_{1}))-U(m_{1})}{s}=\int_{\Omega}K(m_{1},x)(m_{2}(dx)-m_{1}(dx))\,.

Note that the definition of
K
is up to additive constants. Then, we define the derivative
\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}
as the unique map
K
satisfying (
2.4
) and the normalization convention

\int_{\Omega}K(m,x)dm(x)=0\,.

As an immediate consequence, we obtain the following equality, that we will use very often in the rest of the chapter: for each
m_{1}
,
m_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
we have

U(m_{2})-U(m_{1})=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}((m_{1})+s(m_{2}-m_{1}),x)(m_{2}(dx)-m_{1}(dx))\,.

Finally, we can define the
intrinsic derivative
of
U
with respect to
m
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
U:\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
. If
U
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
and
\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
with respect to the last variable, we define the intrinsic derivative
D_{m}U:\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
as

D_{m}U(m,x):=D_{x}\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}(m,x)\,.

We need the following assumptions:

Hypotheses 2.4
.

Assume that

(i)

(Uniform ellipticity)
\left\Arrowvert a(\cdot)\right\Arrowvert_{1+\alpha}<\infty
and
\exists\,\mu>\lambda>0
s.t.
\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mu|\xi|^{2}\geq\langle a(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\geq\lambda|\xi|^{2}\,;

(ii)

H:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
,
G:\Omega\times\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
and
F:\Omega\times\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
are smooth functions with
H
Lipschitz with respect to the last variable;

(iii)

\exists C>0
s.t.

0<H_{pp}(x,p)\leq CI_{d\times d}\hskip 2.27626pt;

(iv)

F
satisfies, for some
0<\alpha<1
and
C_{F}>0
,

\int_{\Omega}\left(F(x,m)-F(x,m^{\prime})\right)d(m-m^{\prime})(x)\geq 0

and

\sup\limits_{m\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)}\left(\left\Arrowvert F(\cdot,m)\right\Arrowvert_{\alpha}+\left\Arrowvert\frac{\delta F}{\delta m}(\cdot,m,\cdot)\right\Arrowvert_{\alpha,2+\alpha}\right)+\mathrm{Lip}\left(\frac{\delta F}{\delta m}\right)\leq C_{F}\hskip 2.27626pt,

with

\mathrm{Lip}\left(\frac{\delta F}{\delta m}\right):=\sup\limits_{m_{1}\neq m_{2}}\left(\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{1},m_{2})^{-1}\left\Arrowvert\frac{\delta F}{\delta m}(\cdot,m_{1},\cdot)-\frac{\delta F}{\delta m}(\cdot,m_{2},\cdot)\right\Arrowvert_{\alpha,1+\alpha}\right)\hskip 2.27626pt;

(v)

G
satisfies the same estimates as
F
with
\alpha
and
1+\alpha
replaced by
2+\alpha
, i.e.

\sup\limits_{m\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)}\left(\left\Arrowvert G(\cdot,m)\right\Arrowvert_{2+\alpha}+\left\Arrowvert\frac{\delta G}{\delta m}(\cdot,m,\cdot)\right\Arrowvert_{2+\alpha,2+\alpha}\right)+\mathrm{Lip}\left(\frac{\delta G}{\delta m}\right)\leq C_{G}\hskip 2.27626pt,

with

\mathrm{Lip}\left(\frac{\delta G}{\delta m}\right):=\sup\limits_{m_{1}\neq m_{2}}\left(\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{1},m_{2})^{-1}\left\Arrowvert\frac{\delta G}{\delta m}(\cdot,m_{1},\cdot)-\frac{\delta G}{\delta m}(\cdot,m_{2},\cdot)\right\Arrowvert_{2+\alpha,2+\alpha}\right)\hskip 2.27626pt;

(vi)

The following Neumann boundary conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle\left\langle a(y)D_{y}\frac{\delta F}{\delta m}(x,m,y),\nu(y)\right\rangle_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,,\qquad\left\langle a(y)D_{y}\frac{\delta G}{\delta m}(x,m,y),\nu(y)\right\rangle_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,,

\displaystyle\langle a(x)D_{x}G(x,m),\nu(x)\rangle_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,,

for all
m\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
.

Some comments about the previous hypotheses: the first five are standard hypotheses in order to obtain existence and uniqueness of solutions for the Mean Field Games system. The hypotheses about the derivative of
F
and
G
with respect to the measure will be essential in order to obtain some estimates on a linearized MFG system.

As regards hypotheses
(vi)
, the second and the third boundary conditions are natural compatibility conditions, essential to obtain a classical solution for the
MFG
and the linearized
MFG
system. The first boundary condition will be essential in order to prove the Neumann boundary condition of
D_{m}U
, see Corollary
5.13
.

With these hypotheses we are able to prove existence and uniqueness of a classical solution for the Master Equation (
1.4
). The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose hypotheses
2.4
are satisfied. Then there exists a unique classical solution
U
of the Master Equation (
1.4
).

But first, we have to prove some preliminary estimates about the Mean Field Games system and some other estimates on a linearyzed Mean Field Games system, which will be essential in order to ensure the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
character of
U
with respect to
m
.

3.
Preliminary estimates and Mean Field Games system

In this section we start giving some technical results for linear parabolic equations, which will be useful in the rest of the Chapter.

Then we will obtain some preliminary estimates for the Master Equation, obtained by a deep analysis of the Mean Field Games related system.

We start with this technical Lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
a
satisfies
(i)
of Hypotheses
2.4
,
b,f\in L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
. Furthermore, let
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha,N}(\Omega)
, with
0\leq\alpha<1
. Then the unique solution
z
of the problem

\begin{cases}-z_{t}-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}z)+b(t,x)\cdot Dz=f(t,x)\,,\\
z(T)=\psi\,,\\
aDz\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\end{cases}

satisfies

(3.1)

\left\Arrowvert z\right\Arrowvert_{\frac{1+\alpha}{2},1+\alpha}\leq C\left(\left\Arrowvert f\right\Arrowvert_{\infty}+\left\Arrowvert\psi\right\Arrowvert_{1+\alpha}\right)\,.

Proof.

Note that, if
f
and
b
are continuous bounded functions, with
b
depending only on
x
, this result is simply
Theorem 5.1.18
of
[
32
]
. In the general case, we argue as follows.

We can write
z=z_{1}+z_{2}
, where
z_{1}
satisfies

(3.2)

\begin{cases}-{(z_{1})}_{t}-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}z_{1})=0\,,\\
z_{1}(T)=\psi\,,\\
aDz_{1}\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,.\end{cases}

and
z_{2}
satisfies

(3.3)

\begin{cases}-{(z_{2})}_{t}-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}z_{2})+b(t,x)\cdot Dz_{2}=f(t,x)-b(t,x)\cdot Dz_{1}\,,\\
z_{2}(T)=0\,,\\
aDz_{2}\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,,\end{cases}

Since in the equation (
3.2
) of
z_{1}
we do not have a drift term depending on time, we can apply
Theorem 5.1.18
of
[
32
]
and obtain

\left\Arrowvert z_{1}\right\Arrowvert_{\frac{1+\alpha}{2},1+\alpha}\leq C\left\Arrowvert\psi\right\Arrowvert_{1+\alpha}\,.

As regards (
3.3
), obviously
z_{2}(T)\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)

\forall p
, and from the estimate of
z_{1}
we know that
f-bDz_{1}\in L^{\infty}
. So we can apply the Corollary of
Theorem IV.9.1
of
[
26
]
to obtain that,
\forall r\geq\frac{d+2}{2}\,,

\left\Arrowvert z_{2}\right\Arrowvert_{1-\frac{d+2}{2r},2-\frac{d+2}{r}}\leq C\left\Arrowvert f-bDz_{1}\right\Arrowvert_{\infty}\leq C\left(\left\Arrowvert f\right\Arrowvert_{\infty}+\left\Arrowvert\psi\right\Arrowvert_{1+\alpha}\right)\,.

Choosing
r=\frac{d+2}{1-\alpha}
, one has
2-\frac{d+2}{r}=1+\alpha
, and so (
3.1
) is satisfied for
z_{2}
.

Since
z=z_{1}+z_{2}
, estimate (
3.1
) holds also for
z
. This concludes the proof.
∎

If the data
f=0
, we can generalize the result of Lemma
3.1
if
\psi
is only a Lipschitz function.

This result is well-known if
a\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\Omega)
, by applying a classical Bernstein method. In our framework, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
a
and
b
be bounded continuous functions, and
\psi\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
. Then the unique solution
z
of the problem

(3.4)

\begin{cases}-z_{t}-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}z)+b(t,x)\cdot Dz=0\,,\\
z(T)=\psi\,,\\
aDz\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\end{cases}

satisfies a Hölder condition in
t
and a Lipschitz condition in
x
, namely
\exists C
such that

(3.5)

\displaystyle|z(t,x)-z(s,x)|\leq C\left\Arrowvert\psi\right\Arrowvert_{W^{1,\infty}}|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,\qquad|z(t,x)-z(t,y)|\leq C\left\Arrowvert\psi\right\Arrowvert_{W^{1,\infty}}|x-y|\,.

Proof.

If
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{1,N}
, estimates (
3.5
) is guaranteed by (
3.1
) of Lemma
3.1
.

In the general case, we take
\psi^{n}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
such that
\psi^{n}\to\psi
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T]\times\Omega)
and

\left\Arrowvert\psi^{n}\right\Arrowvert_{1}\leq C\left\Arrowvert\psi\right\Arrowvert_{W^{1,\infty}}
, and we want to make a suitable approximation of it in order to obtain a function
\tilde{\psi}^{n}\in\mathcal{C}^{1,N}
, also converging to
\psi
.

In order to do that, we first need some useful tools.

For
\delta>0
,
d(\cdot)
the distance function from
\partial\Omega
,
\Omega_{\delta}=\{x\in\Omega\,|\,d(x)\geq\delta\}
and
x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}
, we consider the following ODE in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\xi^{\prime}(t;x)=-a(\xi(t;x))\nu(\xi(t;x))\,,\\
\xi(0;x)=x\,,\end{cases}

where
\nu
is an extension of the outward unit normal in
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}
. Actually, we know from
[
19
]
that

Dd(x)_{|\partial\Omega}=-\nu(x)\,,

so a suitable extension can be
\nu(x)=-Dd(x)\,
.

Then we consider the corresponding hitting time of
\partial\Omega_{\delta}
:

T(x):=\inf\left\{t\geq 0\,|\,\xi(t;x)\notin\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}\right\}\,.

We have that
T(x)<+\infty
for each
x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}
. To prove that, we consider the auxiliary function

\Phi(t,x)=\delta-d(\xi(t;x)).

So, the function
T(x)
can be rewritten as

T(x)=\inf\left\{t\geq 0\,|\,\Phi(t,x)=0\right\}\,,

and his finiteness is an obvious consequence of the decreasing character of
\Phi
in time:

\partial_{t}\Phi(t,x)=-Dd(\xi(t;x))\cdot\xi^{\prime}(t;x)=-\langle a(\xi(t;x))\nu(\xi(t;x)),\nu(\xi(t;x))\rangle\leq-\lambda<0\,.

Moreover, thanks to Dini’s theorem we obtain that
T(x)
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
function and his gradient is given by

\nabla T(x)=-\frac{\nabla_{x}\Phi(T(x),x)}{\partial_{t}\Phi(T(x),x)}=\frac{\nu(\xi(T(x);x))\mathrm{Jac}_{x}\xi(T(x);x)}{\langle a\nu,\nu\rangle(\xi(T(x);x))}\,.

Actually, thanks to the regularity of
a
and
\Omega
, we can differentiate w.r.t.
x
the ODE (
3.6
) and obtain that
\xi(t;\cdot)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
.

Now we define the approximating functions
\tilde{\psi}^{n}
in the following way:

(3.7)

\tilde{\psi}^{n}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\psi^{n}(x)&\mbox{if }&x\in\Omega_{\delta}\,,\\
\psi^{n}(\xi(T(x);x))&\mbox{if }&x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\delta}\,,\end{array}\right.

eventually considering a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
regularization in
\Omega_{\delta}\setminus{\Omega_{2\delta}}\,
.

From the definition of
\tilde{\psi}^{n}
and the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
regularity of
\xi
and
T
we have
\tilde{\psi}^{n}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
and

\|{\tilde{\psi}^{n}}\|_{1}\leq C\left\Arrowvert\psi^{n}\right\Arrowvert_{1}\leq C\left\Arrowvert\psi\right\Arrowvert_{W^{1,\infty}}\,.

Moreover, since near the boundary
\tilde{\psi}^{n}
is constant along the trajectories
a(\cdot)\nu(\cdot)
, we have that on
\partial\Omega

a(x)D{\tilde{\psi^{n}}}(x)\cdot\nu(x)_{|\partial\Omega}=\frac{\partial\tilde{\psi^{n}}}{\partial(a\nu(x))}(x)_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,,

so
\tilde{\psi}^{n}\in\mathcal{C}^{1,N}
.

Now we consider
z^{n}
as the solution of (
3.4
) with
\psi
replaced by
\tilde{\psi}^{n}
. Then Lemma
3.1
implies that
\tilde{\psi}^{n}
satisfies

\left\Arrowvert z^{n}\right\Arrowvert_{\frac{1}{2},1}\leq C\|{\tilde{\psi}}\|_{1}\leq C\left\Arrowvert\psi\right\Arrowvert_{W^{1,\infty}}\,.

Then, Ascoli-Arzelà’s Theorem tells us that
\exists z
such that
z^{n}\to z
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T]\times\Omega)
. Passing to the limit in the weak formulation of
z^{n}
, we obtain that
z
is the unique solution of (
3.4
).

Finally, since
z^{n}
satisfies (
3.5
), we can pass to the pointwise limit when
n\to+\infty
and obtain the estimate (
3.5
) for
z
. This concludes the Lemma.
∎

Now we start with the first estimates for the Master Equation.

The first result is obtained by the study of some regularity properties of the
MFG
system, uniformly in
m_{0}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

The system (
1.1
) with conditions (
1.3
) has a unique classical solution
(u,m)\in\mathcal{C}^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2},2+\alpha}\times\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{P}(\Omega))
, and this solution satisfies

(3.8)

\sup\limits_{t_{1}\neq t_{2}}\frac{\mathbf{d}_{1}(m(t_{1}),m(t_{2}))}{|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\left\Arrowvert u\right\Arrowvert_{1+\frac{\alpha}{2},2+\alpha}\leq C\hskip 2.27626pt,

where
C
does not depend on
(t_{0},m_{0})
.

Furthermore,
m(t)
has a positive density for each
t>0
and, if
m_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{2+\alpha}
and satisfies the Neumann boundary condition

(3.9)

\left(a(x)Dm_{0}+(\tilde{b}(0,x)+H_{p}(x,Du(0,x)))m_{0}\right)\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,,

then
m\in\mathcal{C}^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2},2+\alpha}
.

Finally, the solution is stable: if
m_{0n}\to m_{0}
in
\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
, then there is convergence of the corresponding solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.3
):
(u_{n},m_{n})\to(u,m)
in
\mathcal{C}^{1,2}\times\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{P}(\Omega))
.

Proof.

We use a Schauder fixed point argument.

Let
X\subset\mathcal{C}([t_{0},T];\mathcal{P}(\Omega))
be the set

X:=\left\{m\in\mathcal{C}([t_{0},T];\mathcal{P}(\Omega))\mbox{ s.t. }\mathbf{d}_{1}(m(t),m(s))\leq L|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\ \forall s,t\in[t_{0},T]\right\}\hskip 2.27626pt,

where
L
is a constant that will be chosen later.

It is easy to prove that
X
is a convex compact set for the uniform distance.

We define a map
\Phi:X\to X
as follows.

Given
\beta\in X
, we consider the solution of the following Hamilton-Jacobi equation

(3.10)

\begin{cases}-u_{t}-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}u)+H(x,Du)=F(x,\beta(t))\,,\\
u(T)=G(x,\beta(T))\,,\\
a(x)Du\cdot\nu(x)_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,.\end{cases}

Thanks to hypothesis
(iv)
of
2.4
, we have
{F(\cdot,\beta(\cdot))}\in\mathcal{C}^{\frac{\alpha}{2},\alpha}
and its norm is bounded by a constant independent of
\beta
. For the same reason
G(\cdot,\beta(T))\in\mathcal{C}^{2+\alpha}
.

It is well known that these hypotheses guarantee the existence and uniqueness of a classical solution. A proof can be found in
[
26
]
,
Theorem V.7.4
.

So, we can expand with Taylor formula the gradient term and obtain a linear equation satisfied by
u
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-u_{t}-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}u)+H(x,0)+V(t,x)\cdot Du=F(x,\beta(t))\,,\\
u(T)=G(x,\alpha(T))\,,\\
a(x)Du\cdot\nu_{\partial\Omega}=0\,.\end{cases}

with

V(t,x):=\int_{0}^{1}H_{p}(x,\lambda Du(t,x))\hskip 2.27626ptd\lambda\hskip 2.27626pt.

Thanks to the Lipschitz hypothesis on
H
,
(ii)
of
2.4
, we know that
V\in L^{\infty}
. So, we can use the Corollary of
Theorem IV.9.1
of
[
26
]
to obtain

Du\in\mathcal{C}^{\frac{\alpha}{2},\alpha}\implies V\in\mathcal{C}^{\frac{\alpha}{2},\alpha}\hskip 2.27626pt.

So, we can apply
Theorem IV.5.3
of
[
26
]
and get

\displaystyle\left\Arrowvert u\right\Arrowvert_{1+\frac{\alpha}{2},2+\alpha}\leq C\left(\left\Arrowvert F\right\Arrowvert_{\frac{\alpha}{2},\alpha}+\left\Arrowvert G\right\Arrowvert_{2+\alpha}\right)\hskip 2.27626pt,

where the constant
C
does not depend on
\beta
,
t_{0}
,
m_{0}
.

Now, we define
\Phi(\beta)=m
, where
m\in\mathcal{C}([t_{0},T];\mathcal{P}(\Omega))
is the solution of the Fokker-Planck equation

(3.11)

\begin{cases}m_{t}-\mathrm{div}(a(x)Dm)-\mathrm{div}(m(\tilde{b}(x)+H_{p}(x,Du)))=0\,,\\
m(t_{0})=m_{0}\,,\\
\left(a(x)Dm+(\tilde{b}+H_{p}(x,Du))m\right)\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,.\end{cases}

It is easy to prove that the above equation has a unique solution in the sense of distribution. A proof in a more general case will be given in the next section, in Proposition
5.3
. We want to check that
m\in X
.

Thanks to the distributional formulation, we have

(3.12)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\phi(t,x)m(t,dx)-\int_{\Omega}\phi(s,x)m(s,dx)\\
\,+&\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(-\phi_{t}-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}\phi)+H_{p}(x,Du)\cdot D\phi)m(r,dx)dr=0\hskip 2.27626pt,\end{split}

for each
\phi\in L^{\infty}
satisfying in the weak sense

\begin{cases}-\phi_{t}-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}\phi)+H_{p}(x,Du)\cdot D\phi\in L^{\infty}(Q_{T})\\
aD\phi\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\end{cases}\hskip 2.27626pt.

Take
\psi(\cdot)
a
1
-Lipschitz function in
\Omega
. So, we choose
\phi
in the weak formulation as the solution in
[t,T]
of the following linear equation

(3.13)

\begin{cases}-\phi_{t}-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}\phi)+H_{p}(x,Du)\cdot D\phi=0\,,\\
\phi(t)=\psi\,,\\
a(x)D\phi\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,.\end{cases}

Thanks to Lemma
3.2
, we know that
\phi(\cdot,x)\in\mathcal{C}^{\frac{1}{2}}([0,T])
and its Hölder norm in time is bounded uniformly if
\psi
is
1
-Lipschitz.

Coming back to (
3.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\psi(x)(m(t,dx)-m(s,dx))=\int_{\Omega}(\phi(t,x)-\phi(s,x))m(s,dx)\leq C|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\hskip 2.27626pt,

and taking the
\sup
over the
\psi

1
-Lipschitz,

\mathbf{d}_{1}(m(t),m(s))\leq C|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\hskip 2.27626pt.

Choosing
L=C
, we have proved that
m\in X
.

Since
X
is convex and compact, to apply Schauder’s theorem we only need to show the continuity of
\Phi
.

Let
\beta_{n}\to\beta
, and let
u_{n}
and
m_{n}
the solutions of (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) related to
\beta_{n}
. Since

is uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{C}^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2},2+\alpha}
, from Ascoli-Arzelà’s Theorem we have
u_{n}\to u
in
\mathcal{C}^{1,2}
.

To prove the convergence of
\{m_{n}\}_{n}
, we take
\phi_{n}
as the solution of (
3.13
) with
Du
replaced by
Du_{n}
. Then, as before,
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{C}^{1}
. Actually, the difference
\phi_{n,m}:=\phi_{n}-\phi_{m}
satisfies

\begin{cases}-(\phi_{n,m})_{t}-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}\phi_{n,m})+H_{p}(x,Du_{n})\cdot D\phi_{n,m}=(H_{p}(x,Du_{m})-H_{p}(x,Du_{n}))\cdot D\phi_{m}\,,\\
\phi_{n,m}(t)=0\,,\\
a(x)D\phi_{n,m}\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,,\end{cases}

and so Lemma
3.1
implies

\left\Arrowvert\phi_{n,m}\right\Arrowvert_{\frac{1+\alpha}{2},1+\alpha}\leq C\left\Arrowvert(H_{p}(x,Du_{m})-H_{p}(x,Du_{n}))\cdot D\phi_{m}\right\Arrowvert_{\infty}\leq C\left\Arrowvert Du_{m}-Du_{n}\right\Arrowvert_{\infty}\leq\omega(n,k)\,,

where

when

, and where we use Lemma

in order to bound

in

, without compatibility conditions.

Using (
3.12
) with
(m_{n},\phi_{n})
and
(m_{k},\phi_{k})
, for

,
s=0
, and subtracting the two equalities, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\psi(x)(m_{n}(t,dx)-m_{k}(t,dx))=\int_{\Omega}(\phi_{n}(0,x)-\phi_{k}(0,x))m_{0}(dx)\leq\omega(n,k)\,.

Taking the sup over the
\psi

1
-Lipschitz and over

, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{n}(t)),m_{k}(t))\leq\omega(n,k)\,,

which proves that
\{m_{n}\}_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in

. Then,
\exists m
such that
m_{n}\to m
in

.

Passing to the limit in (
3.11
), we immediately obtain

, which conclude the proof of continuity.

So we can apply Schauder’s theorem and obtain a classical solution of the problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
). The estimate (

) and (

To prove the uniqueness, let
(u_{1},m_{1})
,

be two solutions of (

)-(

We use inequality (

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{(}H(x,Du_{2})-H(x,Du_{1})-H_{p}(x,Du_{1})(Du_{2}-Du_{1}){)}m_{1}(t,dx)dt\hskip 2.27626pt+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{(}H(x,Du_{1})-H(x,Du_{2})-H_{p}(x,Du_{2})(Du_{1}-Du_{2}){)}m_{2}(t,dx)dt\leq 0

So

Finally, if

\begin{cases}m_{t}-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}m)-m\hskip 2.27626pt\mathrm{div}\left(\tilde{b}(x)+H_{p}(x,Du)\right)-\left(2\tilde{b}(x)+H_{p}(x,Du)\right)Dm=0\\

\left(a(x)Dm+(\tilde{b}+H_{p}(x,Du))m\right)\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\end{cases}\hskip 2.27626pt.

The stability of solutions is obtained in the same way we used for the continuity of

∎

4.
Lipschitz continuity of
U

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(u_{1},m_{1})
and
(u_{2},m_{2})
be two solutions of system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
), with
m_{1}(t_{0})=m_{01}
,
m_{2}(t_{0})=m_{02}
. Then

(4.1)

\begin{split}\left\Arrowvert u_{1}-u_{2}\right\Arrowvert_{1,2+\alpha}&\leq C\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{01},m_{02})\,,\\
\sup\limits_{t\in[t_{0},T]}\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{1}(t),m_{2}(t))&\leq C\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{01},m_{02})\,,\end{split}

where
C
does not depend on
t_{0}
,
m_{01}
,
m_{02}
. In particular

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{m_{1}\neq m_{2}}\left[\left(\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{1},m_{2})\right)^{-1}\left\Arrowvert U(t,\cdot,m_{1})-U(t,\cdot,m_{2})\right\Arrowvert_{2+\alpha}\right]\leq C\,.

So, the solution of the Master Equation is Lipschitz continuous in the measure variable. This will be essential in order to prove the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
character of
U
with respect to
m
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we show the result for
t_{0}=0
.

First step: An initial estimate.
Thanks to the hypotheses on
H
and the Lipschitz bound of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
, (
3.15
) implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|Du_{1}-Du_{2}|^{2}(m_{1}(t,dx)+m_{2}(t,dx))dt\leq

\displaystyle\leq C

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(u_{1}(0,x)-u_{2}(0,x))(m_{01}(dx)-m_{02}(dx))\leq C\left\Arrowvert u_{1}-u_{2}\right\Arrowvert_{\frac{1+\alpha}{2},1+\alpha}\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{01},m_{02}).

Second step: An estimate on
m_{1}-m_{2}
. We call
m:=m_{1}-m_{2}
. We take

and

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\phi(t,x)m(t,dx)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(-\phi_{t}-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}\phi)+H_{p}(x,Du_{1})D\phi\right)m(s,dx)ds+\\

We choose

, with terminal condition

. Using the Lipschitz continuity of

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\psi(x)m(t,dx)\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|Du_{1}-Du_{2}|m_{2}(s,dx)ds+C\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{01},m_{02})\hskip 2.27626pt,

Now we use the Young’s inequality and the first step to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\psi(x)m(t,dx)\leq\hskip 2.27626pt

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|Du_{1}-Du_{2}|^{2}m_{2}(s,dx)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{01},m_{02})\leq

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hskip 2.27626ptC\left(\left\Arrowvert u_{1}-u_{2}\right\Arrowvert_{\frac{1+\alpha}{2},1+\alpha}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{01},m_{02})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{01},m_{02})\right)\hskip 2.27626pt,

and finally, taking the sup over the

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{1}(t),m_{2}(t))\leq C\left(\left\Arrowvert u_{1}-u_{2}\right\Arrowvert_{\frac{1+\alpha}{2},1+\alpha}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{01},m_{02})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{01},m_{02})\right)\hskip 2.27626pt.

and conclusion.
We call

. Then

\begin{cases}-u_{t}-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}u)+V(t,x)Du=f(t,x)\\

where

\displaystyle V(t,x)=\int_{0}^{1}H_{p}(x,\lambda Du_{1}(t,x)+(1-\lambda)Du_{2}(t,x)d\lambda\hskip 2.27626pt;

\displaystyle f(t,x)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta F}{\delta m}(x,\lambda m_{1}(t)+(1-\lambda)m_{2}(t),y)(m_{1}(t,dy)-m_{2}(t,dy))d\lambda\hskip 2.27626pt;

\displaystyle g(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta G}{\delta m}(x,\lambda m_{1}(T)+(1-\lambda)m_{2}(T),y)(m_{1}(T,dy)-m_{2}(T,dy))d\lambda\hskip 2.27626pt.

From the regularity of

bounded in

We want to apply

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left\Arrowvert f(t,\cdot)\right\Arrowvert_{\alpha}

in hypotheses

In the same way

\displaystyle\left\Arrowvert g(\cdot)\right\Arrowvert_{2+\alpha}\leq C\sup\limits_{r\in[0,T]}\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{1}(r),m_{2}(r))\,.

\begin{split}\left\Arrowvert u_{1}-u_{2}\right\Arrowvert_{1,2+\alpha}&\leq C\sup\limits_{r\in[0,T]}\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{1}(r),m_{2}(r))\,.\end{split}

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{1}(t),m_{2}(t))\leq

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left(\sup\limits_{r\in[0,T]}\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{1}(r),m_{2}(r))\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{01},m_{02})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{01},m_{02})\right)\hskip 2.27626pt,

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{1}(t),m_{2}(t))\leq C\mathbf{d}_{1}(m_{01},m_{02})\,.

5.
Linearized system and differentiability of
U
with respect to the measure

The proof of existence and uniqueness of solutions for the Master Equation strongly relies on the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
character of
U
with respect to
m
.

The definition of the derivative
\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}
is strictly related to the solution
(v,\mu)
of the following
linearized system
:

(5.1)

\begin{cases}-v_{t}-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}v)+H_{p}(x,Du)\cdot Dv={\frac{\delta F}{\delta m}}(x,m(t))(\mu(t))\,,\\
\mu_{t}-\mathrm{div}(a(x)D\mu)-\mathrm{div}(\mu(H_{p}(x,Du)+\tilde{b}))-\mathrm{div}(mH_{pp}(x,Du)Dv)=0\,,\\
v(T,x)={\frac{\delta G}{\delta m}}(x,m(T))(\mu(T))\,,\hskip 23.49976pt\mu(t_{0})=\mu_{0}\,,\\
a(x)Dv\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,,\hskip 28.45274pt\left(a(x)D\mu+\mu(H_{p}(x,Du)+\tilde{b})+mH_{pp}(x,Du)Dv\right)\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,,\end{cases}

where we use the notation

{\frac{\delta F}{\delta m}}(x,m(t))(\rho(t)):=\left\langle{\frac{\delta F}{\delta m}}(x,m(t),\cdot),\rho(t)\right\rangle

and the same for
G
.

We want to prove that this system admits a solution and that the following equality holds:

(5.2)

v(t_{0},x)=\left\langle\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}(t_{0},x,m_{0},\cdot),\mu_{0}\right\rangle\,.

First, we have to analyze separately the well-posedness of the Fokker-Planck equation in distribution sense:

(5.3)

\begin{cases}\mu_{t}-\mathrm{div}(a(x)D\mu)-\mathrm{div}(\mu b)=f\,,\\
\mu(0)=\mu_{0}\,,\\
\left(a(x)D\mu+\mu b\right)\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,,\end{cases}

where
f\in L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})
,
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{-(1+\alpha)}
,
b\in L^{\infty}
.

A suitable distributional definition of solution is the following:

Definition 5.1
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})
,
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{-(1+\alpha)}
,
b\in L^{\infty}
. We say that a function
\mu\in\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{-(1+\alpha),N})\cap L^{1}(Q_{T})
is a weak solution of (
5.3
) if, for all
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\xi\in\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha,N}
and
\phi
solution in
[0,t]\times\Omega
of the following linear equation

(5.4)

\begin{cases}-\phi_{t}-\mathrm{div}(aD\phi)+bD\phi=\psi\,,\\
\phi(t)=\xi\,,\\
aD\phi\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,,\end{cases}

the following formulation holds:

(5.5)

\langle\mu(t),\xi\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\mu(s,x)\psi(s,x)\,dxds=\langle\mu_{0},\phi(0,\cdot)\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle f(s),\phi(s,\cdot)\rangle\,ds\,,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality between
\mathcal{C}^{-(1+\alpha),N}
and
\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha,N}
in the first case, between
\mathcal{C}^{-(1+\alpha)}
and
\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}
in the second case and between
W^{-1,\infty}
and
W^{1,\infty}
in the last case.

We note that the definition is well-posed. Actually,
\phi(s,\cdot)
is in
\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}

\forall s
thanks to Lemma
3.1
, so
\langle\mu_{0},\phi(0,\cdot)\rangle
and
\langle f(s),\phi(s,\cdot)\rangle
are well defined. Moreover, we have

\left\Arrowvert\phi(s,\cdot)\right\Arrowvert_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq C\,.

Hence, since
f\in L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})
, the last integral is well defined too.

Remark 5.2
.

We are mainly interested in a particular case of distribution
f
. If there exists an integrable function
c:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\forall\phi\in W^{1,\infty}

\langle f(t),\phi\rangle=\int_{\Omega}c(t,x)\cdot D\phi(x)\,dx\,,

then we can write the problem (
5.3
) in this way:

\begin{cases}\mu_{t}-\mathrm{div}(a(x)D\mu)-\mathrm{div}(\mu b)=\mathrm{div}(c)\,,\\
\mu(0)=\mu_{0}\,,\\
\left(a(x)D\mu+\mu b+c\right)\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,,\end{cases}

writing
f
like a divergence and adjusting the Neumann condition, in order to make sense out of the integration by parts in the regular case.

In this case, in order to ensure the condition
f\in L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})
, we can simply require
c\in L^{1}(Q_{T})
. Actually we have, using Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left\Arrowvert f\right\Arrowvert_{L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})}=\int_{0}^{T}\sup\limits_{\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq 1}\left(\int_{\Omega}c(t,x)\cdot D\phi(x)\,dx\right)dt\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|c(t,x)|\,dxdt=\left\Arrowvert c\right\Arrowvert_{L^{1}}\,,

where
|\cdot|
is any equivalent norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The next Proposition gives us an exhaustive existence and uniqueness result for (
5.3
).

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})
,
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{-(1+\alpha)}
,
b\in L^{\infty}
. Then there exists a unique solution of the Fokker-Planck equation (
5.3
).

This solution satisfies

(5.6)

\sup_{t}\left\Arrowvert\mu(t)\right\Arrowvert_{-(1+\alpha),N}+\left\Arrowvert\mu\right\Arrowvert_{L^{p}}\leq C\left(\left\Arrowvert\mu_{0}\right\Arrowvert_{-(1+\alpha)}+\left\Arrowvert f\right\Arrowvert_{L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})}\right)\,,

where
p=\frac{d+2}{d+1+\alpha}\,
.

Finally, the solution is stable: if
\mu^{n}_{0}\to\mu_{0}
in
\mathcal{C}^{-(1+\alpha)}
,
\{b^{n}\}_{n}
uniformly bounded and
b^{n}\to b
in
L^{p}

\forall\,p
,
f^{n}\to f
in
L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})
, then, calling
\mu^{n}
and
\mu
the solutions related, respectively, to
(\mu^{n}_{0},b^{n},f^{n})
and
(\mu_{0},b,f)
, we have
\mu^{n}\to\mu
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{-(1+\alpha),N})\cap L^{p}(Q_{T})
.

Proof.

For the existence part, we start assuming that
f
,
b
,
\mu_{0}
are smooth functions, and that
\mu_{0}
satisfies

(5.7)

\left(a(x)D\mu_{0}+\mu_{0}b\right)\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,.

In this case, we can split the divergence terms in (
5.3
) and obtain that
\mu
is a solution of a linear equation with smooth coefficients. So the existence of solutions in this case is a straightforward consequence of the classical results in
[
26
]
,
[
32
]
.

We consider the unique solution
\phi
of (
5.4
) with
\psi=0
and
\xi\in\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha,N}
. Multiplying the equation of
\mu
for
\phi
and integrating by parts in
[0,t]\times\Omega
we obtain

(5.8)

\langle\mu(t),\xi\rangle=\langle\mu_{0},\phi(0,\cdot)\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle f(s),\phi(s,\cdot)\rangle\,ds\,.

Thanks to Lemma
3.1
, we know that

(5.9)

\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{\frac{1+\alpha}{2},1+\alpha}\leq C\left\Arrowvert\xi\right\Arrowvert_{1+\alpha}\,.

Then the right hand side term of (
5.8
) is bounded in this way:

\langle\mu_{0},\phi(0,\cdot)\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle f(s),\phi(s,\cdot)\rangle\,ds\leq C\left\Arrowvert\xi\right\Arrowvert_{1+\alpha}\left(\left\Arrowvert\mu_{0}\right\Arrowvert_{-(1+\alpha)}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\Arrowvert f(s)\right\Arrowvert_{W^{1,\infty}}\right)\,.

Coming back to (
5.8
) and passing to the
sup
when
\xi\in\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha,N}
,
\left\Arrowvert\xi\right\Arrowvert_{1+\alpha}\leq 1
, we obtain

(5.10)

\sup\limits_{t}\left\Arrowvert\mu(t)\right\Arrowvert_{-(1+\alpha),N}\leq C\left(\left\Arrowvert\mu_{0}\right\Arrowvert_{-(1+\alpha)}+\left\Arrowvert f\right\Arrowvert_{L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})}\right)\,.

Now we have to prove the
L^{p}
estimate. We consider the solution of (
5.4
) with
t=T
,
\xi=0
and
\psi\in L^{r}
, with
r>d+2
(we recall that in this chapter we call

the dimension of the space.).

Then the Corollary of
Theorem IV.9.1
of
[
26
]
tells us that

(5.11)

\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{1-\frac{d+2}{2r},2-\frac{d+2}{r}}\leq C\left\Arrowvert\psi\right\Arrowvert_{L^{r}}\,.

Choosing
r=\frac{d+2}{1-\alpha}
, one has
2-\frac{d+2}{r}=1+\alpha
. Integrating in
[0,T]\times\Omega
the equation of
\mu
one has

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mu\psi\,dxds=\langle\mu_{0},\phi(0,\cdot)\rangle+\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(s),\phi(s,\cdot)\rangle\,ds\,.

Thanks to (
5.11
) we can estimate the terms on the right-hand side and obtain

(5.12)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mu\psi\,dxds\leq C\left\Arrowvert\psi\right\Arrowvert_{L^{r}}\left(\left\Arrowvert\mu_{0}\right\Arrowvert_{-(1+\alpha)}+\left\Arrowvert f\right\Arrowvert_{L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})}\right)\,.

Passing to the
sup
for
\left\Arrowvert\psi\right\Arrowvert_{L^{r}}\leq 1
, we finally get

\left\Arrowvert\mu\right\Arrowvert_{L^{p}}\leq C\left(\left\Arrowvert\mu_{0}\right\Arrowvert_{-(1+\alpha)}+\left\Arrowvert f\right\Arrowvert_{L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})}\right)\,,

with
p
defined as the conjugate exponent of
r
, i.e.
p=\frac{d+2}{d+1+\alpha}
.

This proves estimates (
5.6
) in the regular case.

In the general case, we consider suitable smooth approximations
\mu_{0}^{k}
,
f^{k}
,
b^{k}
converging to
\mu_{0}
,
f
,
b
respectively in
\mathcal{C}^{-(1+\alpha),N}
,
L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})
and
L^{q}(Q_{T})

\forall q\geq 1
, with

bounded uniformly in

and with
\mu_{0}^{k}
satisying (
5.7
).

We call
\mu^{k}
the related solution of (
5.3
). The above convergences tells us that, for a certain

,

\displaystyle\|{\mu^{k}_{0}}\|_{-(1+\alpha)}\leq C\left\Arrowvert\mu_{0}\right\Arrowvert_{-(1+\alpha)}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\left\Arrowvert b_{k}\right\Arrowvert_{\infty}\leq C\left\Arrowvert b\right\Arrowvert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\|{f^{k}}\|_{L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})}\leq C\left\Arrowvert f\right\Arrowvert_{L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})}\,.

Then we apply (
5.6
), to obtain, uniformly in

,

(5.13)

\sup_{t}\|{\mu^{k}(t)}\|_{-(1+\alpha),N}+\|{\mu^{k}}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\left(\left\Arrowvert\mu_{0}\right\Arrowvert_{-(1+\alpha)}+\left\Arrowvert f\right\Arrowvert_{L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})}\right)\,,

where

actually depends on
b^{k}
, but since

it is bounded uniformly in

.

Moreover, the function
\mu^{k,h}:=\mu^{k}-\mu^{h}
also satisfies (
5.3
) with data

,

,
\mu^{0}=\mu^{k}_{0}-\mu^{h}_{0}
. Then estimates (
5.6
) tell us that

\displaystyle\sup_{t}\|{\mu^{k,h}(t)}\|_{-(1+\alpha),N}\,+\,\|{\mu^{k,h}}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C

\displaystyle\left(\|{\mu^{k}_{0}-\mu^{h}_{0}}\|_{-(1+\alpha)}+\|{f^{k}-f^{h}}\|_{L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})}+\|{\mathrm{div}(\mu^{h}(b^{k}-b^{h}))}\|_{L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})}\right)\,,

The first two terms in the right-hand side easily go to

when

, since
\mu^{k}_{0}
and

are Cauchy sequences. As regards the last term, calling

the conjugate exponent of

, we have

(5.14)

\displaystyle\|{\mathrm{div}(\mu^{h}(b^{k}-b^{h}))}\|_{L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|\mu^{h}(b^{k}-b^{h})\right|\,dxdt\leq C\|{b^{k}-b^{h}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\,,

since
\mu^{k}
is bounded in
L^{p}
by (
5.13
) (here

depends also on
\mu_{0}
and

). So, also the last term goes to
0
since

is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{q}

\forall q\geq 1
.

Hence,
\{\mu^{k}\}_{k}
is a Cauchy sequence, and so there exists
\mu\in\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{-(1+\alpha),N})\cap L^{p}(Q_{T})
such that

\mu^{k}\to\mu\qquad\mbox{strongly in }\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{-(1+\alpha),N})\,,\mbox{ strongly in }L^{p}(Q_{T})\,.

Furthermore,
\mu
satisfies (
5.6
).

To conclude, we have to prove that
\mu
is actually a solution of (
5.3
) in the sense of Definition
5.1
.

We take
\phi
and
\phi^{k}
as the solutions of (
5.4
) related to

and

. The weak formulation for
\mu^{k}
implies that

\langle\mu^{k}(t),\xi\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\mu^{k}(s,x)\psi(s,x)\,dxds=\langle\mu_{0}^{k},\phi^{k}(0,\cdot)\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle f^{k}(s),\phi^{k}(s,\cdot)\rangle\,ds\,,

We can immediately pass to the limit in the left-hand side, using the convergence of
\mu^{k}
previously obtained.

For the right-hand side, we first need to prove the convergence of
\phi^{k}
towards
\phi
. This is immediate: actually, the function
\tilde{\phi}^{k}:=\phi^{k}-\phi
satisfies

\begin{cases}-\tilde{\phi}^{k}_{t}-\mathrm{div}(aD\tilde{\phi}^{k})+b^{k}D\tilde{\phi}^{k}=(b^{k}-b)D\phi\,,\\
\tilde{\phi}^{k}(t)=0\,,\\

Then, the Corollary of

of
[

]
implies, for a certain

and depending on

\|{\tilde{\phi}^{k}}\|_{\frac{1+\alpha}{2},1+\alpha}\leq C\|{(b^{k}-b)D\phi}\|_{L^{q}}\to 0\,,

since

is bounded in

using Lemma

.

Hence,
\phi^{k}\to\phi
in
\mathcal{C}^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2},1+\alpha}
. This allows us to pass to the limit in the right-hand side too and prove that (
5.5
) holds true, and so that
\mu
is a weak solution of (
5.3
). This concludes the existence part.

For the uniqueness part, we consider
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
two weak solutions of the system. Then, by linearity, the function
\mu:=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}
is a weak solution of

\begin{cases}\mu_{t}-\mathrm{div}(a(x)D\mu)-\mathrm{div}(\mu b)=0\,,\\

\left(a(x)D\mu+\mu b\right)\cdot\nu_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,.\end{cases}

Hence, the weak estimation (

\forall\psi\in L^{\infty}
and
\forall\xi\in\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha,N}

\langle\mu(t),\xi\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\mu(s,x)\psi(s,x)\,dxds=0\,,

which implies

\left\Arrowvert\mu\right\Arrowvert_{L^{1}}=\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left\Arrowvert\mu(t)\right\Arrowvert_{-(1+\alpha),N}=0

and concludes the uniquess part.

Finally, the stability part is an easy consequence of the estimates obtained previously. Let
f^{n}\to f
,
\mu^{n}_{0}\to\mu_{0}
and

. Then the function
\tilde{\mu}^{n}:=\mu^{n}-\mu
satisfies (

) with

and

replaced by

,
\mu^{n}_{0}-\mu_{0}
,

. Then we use (

) to obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{t}\|{\tilde{\mu}^{n}}\|_{-(1+\alpha),N}\,+\,\|{\tilde{\mu}^{n}}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C

\displaystyle\left(\|{\mu^{n}_{0}-\mu_{0}}\|_{-(1+\alpha)}+\|{f^{n}-f}\|_{L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})}+\|{\mathrm{div}(\mu(b^{n}-b))}\|_{L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})}\right)\,,

The first two terms in the right-hand side go to

. For the last term, the same computations of (

\|{\mathrm{div}(\mu(b^{n}-b))}\|_{L^{1}(W^{-1,\infty})}\leq C\|{b^{n}-b}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\to 0\,.

Then
\mu^{n}\to\mu
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{-(1+\alpha),N})\cap L^{p}(Q_{T})
, which concludes the Proposition.
∎

The last proposition allows us to get another regularity result of

, when the data

with respect to

Corollary 5.4
.

Let

From now on, we indicate with

We start considering, for

\left\Arrowvert K(t_{0},\cdot,m_{0},y)\right\Arrowvert_{2+\alpha}\leq C\left\Arrowvert\delta_{y}\right\Arrowvert_{-(1+\alpha)}=C

Corollary 5.12
.

Suppose hypotheses
2.4
satisfied. Then the derivative
\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}
is twice differentiable with respect to
y
, together with its first and second derivatives with respect to
x
, and the following estimate hold:

(5.39)

\left\Arrowvert\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}(t,\cdot,m,\cdot)\right\Arrowvert_{2+\alpha,2+\alpha}\leq C\,.

Proof.

We want to prove that,
\forall\,i,j
, the incremental ratio

(5.40)

R^{h}_{i,j}(x,y):=\frac{\partial_{y_{i}}\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}(t_{0},x,m_{0},y+he_{j})-\partial_{y_{i}}\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}(t_{0},x,m_{0},y)}{h}

is a Cauchy sequence for

together with its first and second derivatives with respect to

. Then we have to estimate, for

, the quantity
\left|D^{l}_{x}R^{h}_{i,j}(x,y)-D^{l}_{x}R^{k}_{i,j}(x,y)\right|\,,

We already know that

\partial_{y_{i}}\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}(t_{0},x,m_{0},y)=v(t_{0},x;-\partial_{y_{i}}\delta_{y})\,.

Using the linearity of the system (

\left|D^{l}_{x}R^{h}_{i,j}(x,y)-D^{l}_{x}R^{k}_{i,j}(x,y)\right|=D^{l}_{x}v\left(t_{0},x;\Delta_{h}^{j}(-\partial_{y_{i}}\delta_{y})-\Delta_{k}^{j}(-\partial_{y_{i}}\delta_{y})\right)\,,

Hence, estimate (
5.33
) and Lagrange’s Theorem implies

\displaystyle\left|D^{l}_{x}R^{h}_{i,j}(x,y)-D^{l}_{x}R^{k}_{i,j}(x,y)\right|\leq C\left\Arrowvert\Delta_{h}^{j}(-\partial_{y_{i}}\delta_{y})-\Delta_{k}^{j}(-\partial_{y_{i}}\delta_{y})\right\Arrowvert_{-(2+\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{2+\alpha}\leq 1}\left(\frac{\partial_{y_{i}}\phi(y+he_{j})-\partial_{y_{i}}\phi(y)}{h}-\frac{\partial_{y_{i}}\phi(y+ke_{j})-\partial_{y_{i}}\phi(y)}{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{2+\alpha}\leq 1}\left(\partial^{2}_{y_{i}y_{j}}\phi(y_{\phi,h})-\partial^{2}_{y_{i}y_{j}}\phi(y_{\phi,k})\right)\leq\sup\limits_{\left\Arrowvert\phi\right\Arrowvert_{2+\alpha}\leq 1}|y_{\phi,h}-y_{\phi,k}|^{\alpha}\leq|h|^{\alpha}+|k|^{\alpha}\,,

for a certain

in the line segment between

and

and
y_{\phi,k}
in the line segment between
y
and

when

are Cauchy sequences in

, and so converging when

is twice differentiable with respect to

.

Corollary 5.13

The function

, where
(v,\mu)

6.
Solvability of the first-order Master Equation

The
\mathcal{C}^{1}

U

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

We start from the existence part.

Existence
. We start assuming that
m_{0}
is a smooth and positive function satisfying (
3.9
), and we consider
(u,m)
the solution of
MFG
system starting from
m_{0}
at time
t_{0}
. Then

\partial_{t}U(t_{0},x,m_{0})

can be computed as the sum of the two limits:

\lim\limits_{h\to 0}\frac{U(t_{0}+h,x,m_{0})-U(t_{0}+h,x,m(t_{0}+h))}{h}

and

\lim\limits_{h\to 0}\frac{U(t_{0}+h,x,m(t_{0}+h))-U(t_{0},x,m_{0})}{h}\,.

The second limit, using the very definition of
U
, is equal to

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{h\to 0}\frac{u(t_{0}+h,x)-u(t_{0},x)}{h}=u_{t}(t_{0},x)=-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}u(t_{0},x))+H(x,Du(t_{0},x))

\displaystyle-F(x,m(t_{0}))=-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}_{x}U(t_{0},x,m_{0}))+H(x,D_{x}U(t_{0},x,m_{0}))-F(x,m_{0})\,.

As regards the first limit, defining
m_{s}:=(1-s)m(t_{0})+sm(t_{0}+h)
and using the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
regularity of
U
with respect to
m
, we can write it as

\displaystyle-\lim\limits_{h\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}(t_{0}+h,x,m_{s},y)\frac{(m(t_{0}+h,y)-m(t_{0},y))}{h}\,dyds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}(t_{0},x,m_{0},y)m_{t}(t_{0},y)\,dyds=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}(t_{0},x,m_{0},y)m_{t}(t_{0},y)\,dy

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}(t_{0},x,m_{0},y)\,\mathrm{div}\!\left(a(y)Dm(t_{0},y)+m(t_{0},y)(\tilde{b}+H_{p}(y,Du(t_{0},y)))\right)dy\,.

Taking into account the representation formula (
5.2
) for
\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}
, we integrate by parts and use the boundary condition of
\frac{\delta U}{\delta m}
and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[H_{p}(y,D_{x}U(t_{0},y,m_{0}))D_{m}U(t_{0},x,m_{0},y)-\mathrm{tr}\left(a(y)D_{y}D_{m}U(t_{0},x,m_{0},y)\right)\right]dm_{0}(y)

So with the computation of the two limits we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}U(t,x,m)=-\mathrm{tr}\left(a(x)D_{x}^{2}U(t,x,m)\right)+H\left(x,D_{x}U(t,x,m)\right)

\displaystyle-{\int_{\Omega}}\mathrm{tr}\left(a(y)D_{y}D_{m}U(t,x,m,y)\right)dm(y)+

\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}D_{m}U(t,x,m,y)\cdot H_{p}(y,D_{x}U(t,y,m))dm(y)-F(x,m)\,.

So the equation is satisfied for all
m_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
satisfying (
3.9

The boundary conditions are easily verified thanks to Corollary
5.13
. This concludes the existence part.

Uniqueness
. Let
V
be another solution of the Master Equation (

) with Neumann boundary conditions. We consider, for fixed

and
m_{0}

m_{0}

\begin{cases}\tilde{m}_{t}-\mathrm{div}(a(x)D\tilde{m})-\mathrm{div}\left(\tilde{m}\left(H_{p}(x,D_{x}V(t,x,\tilde{m}))+\tilde{b}\right)\right)=0\,,\\
\tilde{m}(t_{0})=m_{0}\,,\\
\left[a(x)D\tilde{m}+(\tilde{b}+D_{x}V(t,x,\tilde{m}))\right]\cdot\nu(x)_{|\partial\Omega}=0\,.\end{cases}

This solution is well defined since
D_{x}V
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to the measure variable.

Then we define
\tilde{u}(t,x)=V(t,x,\tilde{m}(t))
. Using the equations of
V
and
\tilde{m}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{t}(t,x)=

\displaystyle\,V_{t}(t,x,\tilde{m}(t))+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta V}{\delta m}(t,x,\tilde{m}(t),y)\,\tilde{m}_{t}(t,y)\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,V_{t}(t,x,\tilde{m}(t))+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta V}{\delta m}(t,x,\tilde{m}(t),y)\,\mathrm{div}\!\left(a(y)D\tilde{m}(t,y)\right)\,dy

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta V}{\delta m}(t,x,\tilde{m}(t),y)\,\mathrm{div}\!\left(\tilde{m}\left(H_{p}(x,D_{x}V(t,x,\tilde{m}))+\tilde{b}\right)\right)\,dy\,.

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta V}{\delta m}(t,x,\tilde{m}(t),y)\,\mathrm{div}\!\left(a(y)D\tilde{m}(t,y)\right)\,dy

\displaystyle=-

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(y)D\tilde{m}(t,y)\,D_{m}V(t,x,\tilde{m}(t),y)\,dy+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\delta V}{\delta m}(t,x,\tilde{m}(t),y)\,a(y)D\tilde{m}(t,y)\cdot\nu(t,y)\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}(a(y)D_{y}D_{m}V(t,x,\tilde{m}(t),y))\,\tilde{m}(t,y)\,dy-\int_{\Omega}a(y)D_{m}V(t,x,\tilde{m}(t),y)\cdot\nu(y)\tilde{m}(t,y)dy

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\delta V}{\delta m}(t,x,\tilde{m}(t),y)\,a(y)D\tilde{m}(t,y)\cdot\nu(t,y)\,dy\,,

while for the second

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta V}{\delta m}(t,x,\tilde{m}(t),y)\,\mathrm{div}\!\left(\tilde{m}\left(H_{p}(x,D_{x}V(t,x,\tilde{m}))+\tilde{b}\right)\right)\,dy

\displaystyle=-

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(H_{p}(x,D_{x}V(t,x,\tilde{m}))+\tilde{b}\right)D_{m}V(t,x,\tilde{m},y)\tilde{m}(t,y)dy

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\delta V}{\delta m}(t,x,\tilde{m}(t),y)\left(H_{p}(x,D_{x}V(t,x,\tilde{m}))+\tilde{b}\right)\cdot\nu(y)\,\tilde{m}(t,y)dy\,.

Putting together these estimates and taking into account the boundary conditions on
V
and
m
:

\left[a(x)D\tilde{m}+(\tilde{b}+D_{x}V(t,x,\tilde{m}))\right]\cdot\nu(x)_{|x\in\partial\Omega}=0\,,\qquad a(y)D_{m}V(t,x,m,y)\cdot\nu(y)_{|y\in\partial\Omega}=0\,,

and the relation between the divergence and the trace term

\mathrm{div}(a(x)D\phi(x))=\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}\phi(x))+\tilde{b}(x)D\phi(x)\,,\qquad\forall\phi\in W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\,,

we find

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{t}(t,x)=

\displaystyle\,V_{t}(t,x,\tilde{m}(t))+\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{tr}(a(y)D_{y}D_{m}V(t,x,\tilde{m},y))\,d\tilde{m}(y)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\,\int_{\Omega}-H_{p}(y,D_{x}V(t,y,\tilde{m}))D_{m}V(t,x,\tilde{m},y)\,d\tilde{m}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}_{x}V(t,x,\tilde{m}(t)))+H(x,D_{x}V(t,x,\tilde{m}(t)))-F(x,\tilde{m}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\mathrm{tr}(a(x)D^{2}\tilde{u}(t,x))+H(x,D\tilde{u}(t,x))-F(x,\tilde{m}(t))\,.

This means that
(\tilde{u},\tilde{m})
is a solution of the MFG system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
). Since the solution of the Mean Field Games system is unique, we get
(\tilde{u},\tilde{m})=(u,m)
and so
V(t_{0},x,m_{0})=U(t_{0},x,m_{0})
whenever
m_{0}
is smooth.

Then, using a density argument, the uniqueness is proved.
∎

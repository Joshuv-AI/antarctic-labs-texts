Superspaces for heterotic pure spinor string compactifications
Osvaldo Chandia

Abstract




We study supersymmetry conditions for the heterotic pure superstring preserving and
N=1
supersymmetry in four dimensions directly from the curved superspace defined by the Berkovits-Howe constraints.

Introduction

Understanding string dynamics in curved backgrounds is still one of the most important topics of research in string theory. It is particularly crucial to study dualities and compactifications. Most of the knowledge in string compactifications comes from the Ramond-Neveu-Schwarz (RNS) formalism or the supergravity limit. In the case of heterotic string or Type II strings the RNS formalism allowed significant advances such as non-renormalization theorems
[
1
]
and exact results from topological strings (see,
e.g.
,
[
2
]
for a review). For compactification backgrounds involving Ramond-Ramond (RR) fields most of the work done was restricted to the supergravity limit. The difficulty comes from the RR vertex operators associated with the linearized RR flux.

The pure spinor formalism
[
3
]
introduced by Berkovits has manifest supersymmetry in flat space-time and made possible to study RR backgrounds without the subtleties of the RR vertex operators of the RNS formalism.

1

Another promising way to study RR backgrounds is to use Closed Superstring Field Theory
[
4
]
as in the work
[
5
]
.

In two previous papers
[
6
,
7
]
we started the study of compactifications using the pure spinor formalism
2

2

It was also studied in
[
8
]
using orbifolds
. However, in those works we started with an ansatz for the covariant super derivative algebra without relating it the supergravity background defined by the Berkovits-Howe supergravity constraints
[
9
]
. The assumption was guided by requiring a nilpotent BRST charge in the supergravity limit. In this work we want to study supergravity backgrounds for the heterotic pure spinor string preserving Poincaré symmetry or supersymmetry in four dimensions directly from the curved superspace defined by the Berkovits-Howe constraints. The idea is to construct the Killing supervectors and derive the conditions imposed on the supergeometry such that their lowest components satisfy some general requirements expected for Poincaré or supersymmetry parameters. Knowledge of the
\theta
expansion of Killing supervectors is also useful since they define conserved currents on the worldsheet which can be used to have an explicit form for the supergravity vertex operators. Killing spinors and their algebra were already studied in,
e.g.

[
10
]
but not directly from superspace point of view.

The superspace approach taken here is considerably more involved that the component approach historically used in the literature on superstring compactifications. However if we want to apply covariant formalisms for the superstring to this problem we must understand all steps in the description of curved superspaces for string compactifications. Although the case studied here is one of the oldest compactification models it will serve the base to apply the same ideas to more general compactifications,
e.g.

[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
and also for Type II strings. Curved superspace methods were also used in
[
15

to describe flux compactifications.

3

ten dimensional supergravity with the Berkovits-Howe constraints. In Section
4

2
Heterotic supergravity and the Berkovits-Howe constraints

In this section we review the heterotic string in a curved background in the
description of the pure spinor formalism. The world-sheet action is given by

\displaystyle S=\int d^{2}z~

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\Pi_{\underline{a}}{\overline{\Pi}}^{\underline{a}}+\frac{1}{2}\Pi^{A}{\overline{\Pi}}^{B}B_{BA}+d_{\alpha}{\overline{\Pi}}^{\alpha}+\omega_{\alpha}{\overline{\nabla}}\lambda^{\alpha}+\rho_{\cal A}{\nabla}\rho_{\cal A}\right.

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left.+d_{\alpha}{\overline{J}}^{I}W_{I}{}^{\alpha}+\lambda^{\alpha}\omega_{\beta}{\overline{J}}^{I}U_{I\alpha}{}^{\beta}\right)+S_{FT},

(2.2)

where the superspace coordinates
Z^{M}=(X^{\underline{m}},\theta^{\mu})
(with
{\underline{m}}=0,\dots,9,\mu=1,\dots,16
) defines
\Pi^{A}=\partial Z^{M}E_{M}{}^{A}(Z)=(\Pi^{\underline{a}},\Pi^{\alpha})
, where
E_{M}{}^{A}(Z)
is the vielbein superfield. The world-sheet field
d_{\alpha}
is the generator for superspace translations,
\lambda^{\alpha}
is the pure spinor variable and
\omega_{\alpha}
is its momentum conjugate variable. The world-sheet fields
\rho_{\cal A}
are the heterotic fermions. They are in the fundamental representation of the gauge group
SO(32)
or
E_{8}\times E_{8}
. These fermions define the current
{\overline{J}}^{I}=\frac{1}{2}K^{I}_{\cal{AB}}\rho_{\cal A}\rho_{\cal B}
, where
K_{I}
are the generators of the gauge group Lie algebra in the fundamental representation. The covariant derivatives in (
2.2
) are defined by

\displaystyle{\overline{\nabla}}\lambda^{\alpha}={\overline{\partial}}\lambda^{\alpha}+\lambda^{\beta}\left({\overline{\partial}}Z^{M}\Omega_{M\beta}{}^{\alpha}\right),\quad{\nabla}\rho_{\cal A}=\partial\rho_{\cal A}+K^{I}_{\cal{AB}}\left(\partial Z^{M}A_{IM}\right)\rho_{\cal B},

(2.3)

where
\Omega_{M\beta}{}^{\alpha}
is the background Lorentz connection and
A_{IM}
is the gauge group connection. The background fields are
B,W_{I},U_{I}
and the connections in (
2.3
). Finally,
S_{FT}
is the Fradkin-Tseytlin term and it is given by

\displaystyle S_{FT}=\int d^{2}z~\alpha^{\prime}r^{(2)}\Phi,

(2.4)

where
r^{(2)}
is the world-sheet curvature and
\Phi
is the dilaton superfield. Note that this term breaks classical conformal invariance but it helps to restore it at the quantum level
[
16
]
.

The action (
2.2
) is the most general expression that is invariant under background Lorentz and gauge transformations such that it is also classically conformal invariant. There is another symmetry that constrains the background fields to satisfy the equations of supergravity in ten dimensions and the equations of super Yang-Mills in a curved background
[
9
]
. This symmetry is generated by the pure spinor BRST charge

\displaystyle Q=\oint dz~\lambda^{\alpha}d_{\alpha}.

(2.5)

The nilpotency of
Q
and the holomorphicity of
\lambda^{\alpha}d_{\alpha}
, imply a set of constraints for the background superfields. These constraints are functions of the torsion, curvature, field-strength and
H=dB
components. Let us remind how they are defined. They are given after constructing the super one-forms
E^{A}=dZ^{M}E_{M}{}^{A},\Omega_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=dZ^{M}\Omega_{M\alpha}{}^{\beta},A_{I}=dZ^{M}A_{IM}
and the super two-form
B=\frac{1}{2}dZ^{M}dZ^{N}B_{NM}
. Note that the Lorentz connection has the form

\displaystyle\Omega_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}\Omega^{(s)}+\frac{1}{4}(\gamma^{\underline{ab}})_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\Omega_{\underline{ab}},

(2.6)

where
\Omega^{(s)}
is the connection for scalings and
\Omega_{\underline{ab}}
is the usual Lorentz connection in ten-dimensional superspace. In this way, one can define the connection
\Omega_{A}{}^{B}
with non-zero components
\Omega_{\underline{a}}{}^{\underline{b}}
and
\Omega_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
. The matrix
\gamma^{\underline{ab}}=\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{\underline{a}}\gamma^{\underline{b}}-\gamma^{\underline{b}}\gamma^{\underline{a}})
, where
(\gamma^{\underline{a}})_{\alpha\beta}
and
(\gamma^{\underline{a}})^{\alpha\beta}
are the symmetric
16\times 16

\gamma
-matrices in ten dimensions that satisfy the Dirac algebra

\displaystyle(\gamma^{\underline{a}})_{\alpha\gamma}(\gamma^{\underline{b}})^{\gamma\beta}+(\gamma^{\underline{b}})_{\alpha\gamma} (\gamma^{\underline{a}})^{\gamma\beta}=2\eta^{\underline{ab}}\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}.

(2.7)

The map of a tensor with superspace curved indices to local target space indices is performed with the use of the vielbein and its inverse. For example,

\displaystyle B_{NM}=(-1)^{N(M+A)}E_{M}{}^{A}E_{N}{}^{B}B_{BA},

(2.8)

where
(A,M)
assigns a sign
(+1)
for bosonic directions and
(-1)
for fermionic directions (for more details see
[
17
]
). The torsion, curvature and field-strength super two-forms are given by

\displaystyle T^{A}={\nabla}E^{A}=dE^{A}+E^{B}\Omega_{B}{}^{A},\quad R_{B}{}^{A}=d\Omega_{B}{}^{A}+\Omega_{B}{}^{C}\Omega_{C}{}^{A},\quad F_{I}=dA_{I}+f^{JK}{}_{I}A_{J}A_{K},

(2.9)

\displaystyle T^{A}=\frac{1}{2}E^{B}E^{C}T_{CB}{}^{A},\quad R_{B}{}^{A}=\frac{1}{2}E^{C}E^{D}R_{DCB}{}^{A},\quad F_{I}=\frac{1}{2}E^{B}E^{A}F_{IAB},

(2.10)

where the product between forms is the wedge product and
f^{JK}{}_{I}
are the structure constants of the gauge group Lie algebra. The covariant derivative
\nabla
is

\displaystyle\nabla=E^{A}\nabla_{A}=dZ^{M}(\partial_{M}+\frac{1}{2}\Omega_{M}{}^{\underline{ab}}M_{\underline{ab}}+\Omega^{(s)}_{M}S),

(2.11)

where
M_{ab}
are the Lorentz generators and
S
is the scaling generator.

Together with

\displaystyle H=\frac{1}{6}E^{C}E^{B}E^{A}H_{ABC},

(2.12)

the torsion, the curvature and the field-strength satisfy Bianchi identities. They are given by

\displaystyle{\nabla}_{[A}T_{BC]}{}^{D}+T_{[AB}{}^{E}T_{EC]}{}^{D}-R_{[ABC]}{}^{D}=0,

(2.13)

\displaystyle{\nabla}_{[A}R_{BC]D}{}^{E}+T_{[AB}{}^{F}R_{FC]D}{}^{E}=0,

(2.14)

\displaystyle{\nabla}_{[A}F_{IBC]}+T_{[AB}{}^{D}F_{IDC]}=0,

(2.15)

\displaystyle{\nabla}_{[A}H_{BCD]}+\frac{3}{2}T_{[AB}{}^{E}H_{ECD]}=0,

(2.16)

where the (anti)-symmetrization is on
(ABC)
in the first three equations and it is on
(ABCD)
in the last equation.

The nilpotency of the pure spinor BRST charge and the holomorphicity of the BRST current, together with gauge fixing gauge symmetries of the action, imply that some of the torsion components are

\displaystyle T_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\underline{a}}=-(\gamma^{\underline{a}})_{\alpha\beta},\quad T_{A\alpha}{}^{\beta}=0,

(2.17)

some of the
H
components are

\displaystyle H_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=H_{\underline{ab}\alpha}=0,\quad H_{\alpha\beta\underline{a}}=-(\gamma_{\underline{a}})_{\alpha\beta},

(2.18)

and for the field-strength components

\displaystyle F_{I\alpha\beta}=0.

(2.19)

Using the Bianchi identities of (
2.16
) and the torsion components in (
2.17
), one can prove that the constraints for the curvature are implied. Also, the Bianchi identity involving
{\nabla}_{(\alpha}T_{\beta\gamma)}{}^{\underline{a}}
implies that

\displaystyle T_{\alpha\underline{ab}}=2(\gamma_{\underline{ab}}\Omega^{(s)})_{\alpha}.

(2.20)

It is important to note that since
\Omega^{(s)}_{\alpha}\neq 0
one has to be careful when acting with
\nabla_{\alpha}
on gamma matrices. For example,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}(\gamma^{\underline{a}})_{\beta\delta}=-2\Omega^{(s)}_{\alpha}(\gamma^{\underline{a}})_{\beta\delta}.

(2.21)

Combining the Bianchi identities involving
{\nabla}_{[\alpha}T_{\underline{ab}]}{}^{\underline{c}}
and
{\nabla}_{[\alpha}H_{\beta\underline{ab}]}
one can show that

\displaystyle T_{\underline{abc}}=-H_{\underline{abc}},\quad\Omega^{(s)}_{\underline{a}}=0.

(2.22)

The Bianchi identity involving
{\nabla}_{[\alpha}H_{\underline{abc}]}
together with the equation
(\gamma^{\underline{b}})_{\alpha\beta}T_{\underline{ab}}{}^{\beta}=8{\nabla}_{\underline{a}}\Omega_{\alpha}
(which is derived from the Bianchi identity involving
{\nabla}_{[\underline{a}}T_{\alpha\beta]}{}^{\beta}

[
16
]
) implies that

\displaystyle T_{\underline{ab}}{}^{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{6}(\gamma^{\underline{c}}\nabla)^{\alpha}H_{\underline{abc}}-\frac{4}{3}\nabla_{[\underline{a}}(\gamma_{\underline{b}]}\Omega^{(s)})^{\alpha}.

(2.23)

Finally, the Bianchi involving
R_{(\alpha\beta\gamma)}{}^{\rho}
implies

\displaystyle T_{\underline{abc}}=-(\gamma_{\underline{abc}})^{\alpha\beta}{\nabla}_{\alpha}\Omega^{(s)}_{\beta},

(2.24)

So we have that

\displaystyle H_{\underline{abc}}=(\gamma_{\underline{abc}})^{\alpha\beta}{\nabla}_{\alpha}\Omega^{(s)}_{\beta}.

(2.25)

Note that
\Omega^{(s)}_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{4}{\nabla}_{\alpha}\Phi
as it is required by ghost number anomaly cancellation
[
9
]
(also necessary for vanishing of the beta function at one-loop
[
16
]
). One can also use the Bianchi identities to find the following expressions for the curvatures

\displaystyle R_{{\alpha\beta}}{}^{\underline{ab}}=\nabla_{(\alpha}T_{{\beta})\underline{c}}{}^{\underline{b}}\eta^{\underline{ca}}+T_{({\alpha\underline{d}}}{}^{\underline{c}}T_{\beta)\underline{c}}^{b}\eta^{\underline{da}}+\gamma_{{\alpha\beta}}^{\underline{d}}H_{\underline{d}}{}^{\underline{ab}},

(2.26)

\displaystyle R_{\alpha\underline{b}}{}^{\underline{cd}}=-\nabla_{\underline{b}}T_{\alpha}{}^{\underline{cd}}-T_{\underline{b}}{}^{[{\underline{c}\beta}}\gamma_{{\beta\alpha}}^{\underline{d}]},

(2.27)

\displaystyle R_{\underline{ab}}{}^{\underline{cd}}=-\frac{1}{8}(\gamma^{\underline{cd}})_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\left(\nabla_{\beta}T_{\underline{ab}}{}^{\alpha}-T_{\beta[\underline{a}}{}^{\underline{e}}T_{\underline{b}]\underline{e}}{}^{\alpha} \right),

(2.28)

\displaystyle R^{(s)}_{\alpha\beta}=\nabla_{(\alpha}\Omega_{\beta)}=\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{\underline{a}}_{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\underline{a}}\Phi,

(2.29)

\displaystyle R^{(s)}_{\underline{a}\alpha}=\nabla_{\underline{a}}\Omega_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{4}\nabla_{\underline{a}}\nabla_{\alpha}\Phi,

(2.30)

\displaystyle R^{(s)}_{\underline{ab}}=T_{\underline{ab}}{}^{\alpha}\Omega_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{4}T_{\underline{ab}}{}^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\Phi.

(2.31)

All the gauge covariant background fields depend on the
\Phi
superfield. This is not something new and it is also true with a different set of constraints
[
18
]
. It is interesting to note that for the Type IIB pure spinor string this is not true
[
19
]
. It can be shown that using the Berkovits-Howe constraints the Ramond-Ramond
5
-form, as well as curvature generated from it, does not appear as a higher component of
\Phi

[
20
]
. An explicit example of this is the
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
background
[
3
,
21
]
where all covariant derivatives of
\Phi
vanish.

3
Killing supervectors

As discussed in the previous section all Lorentz covariant tensors can be computed from
\Phi
. Under a general super reparametrization
\xi^{M}(Z)
it transforms as

\displaystyle\delta\Phi=\xi^{M}\nabla_{M}\Phi=\xi^{A}\nabla_{A}\Phi=0,

(3.1)

where we are defining
\xi^{A}=\xi^{M}E_{M}{}^{A}
. However it is not enough to impose that the transformation above vanishes. Generically the covariant tensors are given by

\displaystyle T_{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}\sim\nabla_{A_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{A_{n}}\Phi,

(3.2)

therefore in order to have
\delta T_{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}=0
we must also impose that
\delta\nabla_{A}=0
.
Another way to see it is that particular components of the vielbein cannot be obtained from
\Phi
so to impose that the full background is invariant under some particular super diffeomorphism the covariant derivatives (
2.11
), which depend on
E_{A}{}^{M}
, should also be invariant.

The textbook way, see
e.g.

[
22
]
, to study super diffeomorphisms is to introduce a vector superfield containing
\xi^{A}
, a compensating Lorentz rotation
\Lambda^{\underline{ab}}
and scale transformation
\sigma

\displaystyle K=\xi^{A}\nabla_{A}+{\textstyle{1\over{\raise 0.3014pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{2}$}}}}\Lambda^{\underline{ab}}M_{\underline{ab}}+\sigma S,

(3.3)

Although the pure spinor sigma model has two independent local Lorentz symmetries, acting on vectors and spinors separately, a combination of the two is fixed in the process of solving the Berkovits-Howe constraints. The local scale symmetry is also used to fix the dimension zero torsions, however since we are not eliminating the scale connection from
\nabla_{\alpha}
we still have to include the scale transformation in
K
. As we will see this will effectively reduce the structure group of symmetry transformations from Lorentz times scale to only Lorentz, as expected.

A generic tensor superfield
\mathcal{O}^{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}
transforms under local reparametrizations and local Lorentz transformations as generated by
K
as

\displaystyle\delta{\mathcal{O}}^{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}=K\mathcal{O}^{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}.

(3.4)

Since the covariant derivatives map tensors to tensors we have that the covariant derivatives themselves transform as

\displaystyle\delta\nabla_{A}=[K,\nabla_{A}].

(3.5)

If we apply this idea to
\nabla_{\alpha}
and use the Berkovits-Howe constraints we get

\displaystyle\delta\nabla_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\left(-\nabla_{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}{-\frac{1}{4}}\Lambda^{\underline{ab}}(\gamma_{\underline{ab}})_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}-\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}\sigma\right)\nabla_{\beta}+\left(-\nabla_{\alpha}\xi^{\underline{b}}+\xi^{\beta}\gamma^{\underline{b}}_{{\alpha\beta}}-\xi^{a}T_{{\underline{a}\alpha}}{}^{\underline{b}}\right)\nabla_{\underline{b}}

(3.6)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(-\nabla_{\alpha}\Lambda^{\underline{ab}}+\xi^{C}R_{C\alpha}{}^{\underline{ab}}\right)M_{\underline{ab}}+\left(\xi^{C}R^{(s)}_{C\alpha}-\nabla_{\alpha}\sigma\right)S=0,

(3.7)

where
R^{(s)}_{AB}
is the scaling curvature and
R_{AB}{}^{{ab}}
is the Lorentz curvature. The first term in (
3.6
) defines
\Lambda^{\underline{ab}}
in terms of
\xi^{\alpha}
it also implies that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}=-16\sigma,\quad(\nabla\gamma^{\underline{abcd}}\xi)=0.

(3.8)

Before studying the consequences of these conditions let us first analyze
the second term of (
3.6
), which has the lowest mass dimension. From it we can write an expression for
\xi^{\alpha}
in terms of
\xi^{\underline{a}}
as

\displaystyle\xi^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{10}\gamma_{\underline{a}}^{{\alpha\beta}}\nabla_{\beta}\xi^{\underline{a}}+\frac{9}{5}\xi^{\underline{a}}(\gamma_{\underline{a}}\Omega)^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{10}\nabla_{\beta}\left(\gamma_{\underline{a}}^{{\alpha\beta}}\xi^{\underline{a}}\right)+\frac{8}{5}\xi^{\underline{a}}(\gamma_{\underline{a}}\Omega)^{\alpha}.

(3.9)

We can also write the fundamental equation for
\xi^{\underline{a}}
using the usual ten dimensional gamma matrix identity

\displaystyle\gamma_{\underline{b}({\beta\delta}}\left(-\nabla_{\alpha)}\xi^{\underline{b}}+\xi^{\underline{a}}T_{{\alpha}){\underline{a}}}{}^{\underline{b}}\right)=0,

(3.10)

it depends only on
\xi^{\underline{a}}
and the background. Using the explicit form of

and the fact that the

derivative of

\gamma_{\underline{b}{\beta\delta}}

\displaystyle\nabla_{(\alpha}\left((\gamma_{\underline{b}})_{{\beta\delta})}\xi^{\underline{b}}\right)=0.

(3.11)

A nice way to summarize this is that the isometries of a general supergravity background with the Berkovits-Howe constraints

9
]
are generated by a symmetric bi-spinor satisfying

\displaystyle\lambda^{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}\xi_{{\alpha\beta}}=0,\quad\nabla_{(\alpha}\xi_{{\beta\delta})}=0.

(3.12)

Let us see what consequences are obtained by using the conditions (
3.8
) and the expression for (
3.9
) for
\xi^{\alpha}
. Acting with
{\nabla}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\frac{9}{5}(\xi^{\beta}\gamma^{\underline{a}}_{\beta\alpha}+\xi^{\underline{b}}T_{\alpha\underline{b}}{}^{\underline{a}})(\gamma_{\underline{a}}\Omega)^{\alpha}+\frac{9}{40}\xi^{\underline{a}}\gamma_{\underline{a}}^{\alpha\beta}\{{\nabla}_{\alpha},{\nabla}_{\beta}\}\Phi.

3.26
) implies

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\Lambda_{\underline{bc}}[\gamma^{\underline{bc}},\gamma^{\underline{a}}]_{\alpha\beta}+\xi^{\underline{b}}\left(\nabla_{(\alpha}T_{\beta)\underline{b}}{}^{a}+T_{(\alpha\underline{b}}{}^{\underline{c}}T_{\beta)\underline{c}}{}^{\underline{a}}\right)+\xi^{\gamma}\left(2\gamma^{\underline{a}}_{\gamma(\alpha}\Omega_{\beta)}+\gamma^{\underline{b}}_{\gamma(\alpha}T_{\beta)\underline{b}}{}^{\underline{a}}\right)-2\sigma\gamma^{\underline{a}}_{\alpha\beta}

(3.27)

\displaystyle=-\gamma^{\underline{b}}_{\alpha\beta}\Lambda_{\underline{b}}{}^{\underline{a}}+\xi^{\underline{b}}\left(R_{\alpha\beta\underline{b}}{}^{\underline{a}}-\gamma^{\underline{c}}_{\alpha\beta}T_{\underline{bc}}{}^{\underline{a}}\right)-\xi^{\gamma}\gamma^{\underline{b}}_{\alpha\beta}T_{\gamma\underline{b}}{}^{\underline{a}}.

(3.28)

The terms with
\Lambda
cancel after computing the commutator. The term with
\xi^{\underline{b}}
is also zero because of the Bianchi identity involving
R_{\alpha\beta\underline{b}}{}^{\underline{a}}
. The terms with
\xi^{\gamma}
are equal to

\displaystyle\xi^{\gamma}\left(2\gamma^{\underline{a}}_{\gamma(\alpha}\Omega_{\beta)}+\gamma^{\underline{b}}_{(\alpha\beta}T_{\gamma)\underline{b}}{}^{\underline{a}}\right)=-2\gamma^{\underline{a}}_{\alpha\beta}(\xi^{\gamma}\Omega_{\gamma}),

(3.29)

where we have used the fact that
T_{\beta\underline{b}}{}^{\underline{a}}=2(\gamma_{\underline{b}}{}^{\underline{a}}\Omega)_{\beta}
and the Fierz identity
\gamma^{\underline{b}}_{(\gamma\alpha}(\gamma_{\underline{b}})_{\beta)\rho}=0
. The result of (
3.29
) has to cancel the term with
\sigma
in (
3.28
). Therefore,

\displaystyle\sigma=-\xi^{\gamma}\Omega_{\gamma},

(3.30)

which is a condition already found before (see (
3.16
)).

As we already mentioned before, the scalar superfield
\Phi
should also be invariant under the transformations generated by
K
. Since
\xi^{\gamma}\Omega_{\gamma}=-\sigma
and
\Omega_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{4}{\nabla}_{\alpha}\Phi
, invariance under
K
implies that

\displaystyle\xi^{\underline{a}}\nabla_{\underline{a}}\Phi=4\sigma.

(3.31)

Finally, one can calculate the commutator of two different Killing transformations parametrized by

(\xi^{A}_{1},\Lambda^{\underline{ab}}_{1},\sigma_{1})
and
(\xi^{A}_{2},\Lambda^{\underline{ab}}_{2},\sigma_{2})

\displaystyle K_{1}=\xi_{1}^{A}\nabla_{A}+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{1}^{\underline{ab}}M_{\underline{ab}}+\sigma_{1}S,\quad K_{2}=\xi_{2}^{A}\nabla_{A}+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{2}^{\underline{ab}}M_{\underline{ab}}+\sigma_{2}S.

(3.32)

Both
K
’s leave
\nabla_{A}
invariant, so the parameters
\xi
and
\Lambda
satisfy the equations the come from
[K,\nabla_{A}]=0
. The commutator
K_{3}=[K_{1},K_{2}]
is then given by

\displaystyle K_{3}=\left(\xi_{1}^{B}\nabla_{B}\xi_{2}^{A}-\xi_{2}^{B}\nabla_{B}\xi_{1}^{A}-\xi_{2}^{B}\xi_{1}^{C}T_{CB}{}^{A}+\xi^{\underline{b}}_{[1}\Lambda_{2]\underline{b}}{}^{\underline{a}}\delta_{\underline{a}}^{A}+\frac{1}{4}(\gamma_{\underline{ab}})_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\xi^{\alpha}_{[1}\Lambda_{2]}^{\underline{ab}}\delta_{\beta}^{A}\right)\nabla_{A}

(3.33)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(\xi_{2}^{B}\xi_{1}^{A}R_{AB}{}^{\underline{ab}}+\xi_{[1}^{A}\nabla_{A}\Lambda_{2]}^{\underline{ab}}+\Lambda_{1}^{\underline{[ac}}\Lambda_{2\underline{c}}{}^{\underline{b}]}\right)M_{\underline{ab}}+{\left(\xi^{A}_{[1}{\nabla}_{A}\sigma_{2]}\right)S}.

(3.34)

Using the equations from (
3.6
) and (
3.24
) for the derivatives of
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}
the expression above can be simplified to

\displaystyle K_{3}=\left(-\xi^{\underline{b}}_{[1}\xi^{\alpha}_{2]}T_{\alpha\underline{b}}{}^{\underline{a}}+\xi^{\alpha}_{1}\xi^{\beta}_{2}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\underline{a}}-\xi^{\underline{b}}_{1}\xi^{\underline{c}}_{2}T_{\underline{cb}}{}^{\underline{a}}\right)\nabla_{\underline{a}}-\xi_{1}^{\underline{a}}\xi_{2}^{\underline{b}}T_{\underline{ab}}{}^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}

(3.35)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(\xi_{1}^{A}\xi_{2}^{B}R_{BA}{}^{\underline{ab}}+\Lambda_{1}^{\underline{ac}}\Lambda_{2\underline{c}}{}^{\underline{b}}\right)M_{\underline{ab}}

(3.36)

\displaystyle=-\xi_{1}^{B}\xi_{2}^{C}T_{CB}{}^{A}\nabla_{A}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\xi_{1}^{A}\xi_{2}^{B}R_{BA}{}^{\underline{ab}}+\Lambda_{1}^{\underline{ac}}\Lambda_{2\underline{c}}{}^{\underline{b}}\right)M_{\underline{ab}}+\left(\xi^{A}_{[1}{\nabla}_{A}\sigma_{2]}\right)S

(3.37)

So we finally obtain that

\displaystyle\xi^{A}_{3}=-\xi_{1}^{B}\xi_{2}^{C}T_{CB}{}^{A},\quad\Lambda_{3}^{\underline{ab}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\xi_{1}^{A}\xi_{2}^{B}R_{BA}{}^{\underline{ab}}-\Lambda_{1}^{\underline{c}[\underline{a}}\Lambda_{2\underline{c}}{}^{\underline{b}]}\right),\quad\sigma_{3}=\left(\xi^{A}_{[1}{\nabla}_{A}\sigma_{2]}\right).

(3.38)

For the heterotic string it is also possible to include a gauge field background. The superfields present in the sigma model are
(\mathbf{A}_{B},\mathbf{W}^{\alpha},\mathbf{U},\mathbf{U}^{\underline{ab}})
. They are valued in the Lie algebra of
E_{8}\times E_{8}
or
SO(32)
and
\mathbf{A}_{B}
is the usual gauge superfield. The Berkovits-Howe constraints imply that they can be written in terms of
\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}
. One could include the gauge superfield in the covariant derivatives together with a compensating gauge transformation in
K
and include their contributions to (
3.6
) and (
3.24
). However, a gauge background is better described by its dimension
\frac{3}{2}
covariant field strength
\mathbf{W}^{\alpha}
and its transformation under
K
is

\displaystyle\delta\mathbf{W}^{\alpha}=\xi^{A}\nabla_{A}\mathbf{W}^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{4}\Lambda^{\underline{ab}}\left(\gamma_{\underline{ab}}\mathbf{W}\right)^{\alpha},

(3.39)

where the
\nabla_{A}
above includes the gauge connection. In what follows we will focus purely on the geometry and will not include a gauge background.

3.1
Flat superspace

In flat space
\nabla_{A}\Phi=0
, the solution to (
3.11
) looks like

\displaystyle\xi^{\underline{a}}=\epsilon^{\underline{a}}+\Lambda^{\underline{ab}}x_{\underline{b}}+(\theta\gamma^{\underline{a}}\eta)-\frac{1}{4}(\theta\gamma^{\underline{abc}}\theta)\Lambda_{\underline{bc}}.

(3.40)

where
\epsilon^{\underline{a}}
is the translation parameter,
\Lambda^{\underline{ab}}
is the Lorentz rotation and
\eta^{\alpha}
is the supersymmetry parameter. There are no higher order
\theta
terms in the expansion. One way to see this is to note that the first term in the second line of (
3.6
) in flat space implies that
\Lambda^{\underline{ab}}
is constant. It also implies that
\nabla_{\delta}\nabla_{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}=0
, which means
\xi^{\beta}
is constant in
x
and that
\nabla_{[\alpha_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{\alpha_{n}]}\xi^{\beta}\big|_{\theta=0}=0
for
n\geq 2
so
\xi^{\beta}
is at most linear in
\theta
. This is the complete set of isometries of flat ten dimensional superspace.

We would like to know how the existence of (
3.40
) implies a flat ten dimensional superspace. First we have to define the basic properties of
\xi^{\underline{a}}
in an appropriate way if the superspace is curved. In this case there is no notion of a reparametrization invariant
\theta
expansion. The higher components of
\xi^{\underline{a}}
should be defined as an expansion using the Grassmann odd covariant derivatives. Using the
\Theta
-variable notation of Wess-Bagger
[
17
]
we can represent the
\theta
expansion of
\xi^{\underline{a}}
as

\displaystyle\xi^{\underline{a}}=\xi^{\underline{a}}\big|_{\theta=0}+\Theta^{\alpha}(\nabla_{\alpha}\xi^{\underline{a}})\big|_{\theta=0}+\frac{1}{2}\Theta^{\beta}\Theta^{\alpha}(\nabla_{[\alpha}\nabla_{\beta]}\xi^{\underline{a}})\big|_{\theta=0}+\cdots

(3.41)

where
\cdots
are higher order
\Theta
terms. For flat superspace we could say
\epsilon^{\underline{a}}
,
\Lambda^{\underline{ab}}
and
\eta^{\alpha}
were constants, but that is a frame dependent notion. For a general curved superspace we will impose that the components of
\xi^{\underline{a}}
satisfy

\displaystyle(\xi^{\underline{b}})\big|_{\theta=0}=\epsilon^{\underline{b}},\quad(\nabla_{\underline{a}}\epsilon^{\underline{b}})\big|_{\theta=0}=-\Lambda_{\underline{a}}{}^{\underline{b}},

(3.42)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{\alpha}\xi^{\underline{b}})\big|_{\theta=0}=\gamma^{\underline{b}}_{{\alpha\beta}}\eta^{\alpha},\quad(\nabla_{\underline{a}}\eta^{\alpha})\big|_{\theta=0}=0,

(3.43)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{\underline{a}}(\nabla_{[\alpha}\nabla_{\beta]}\xi^{\underline{a}})\big|_{\theta=0})\big|_{\theta=0}=0,

(3.44)

where
\Lambda_{\underline{ab}}=-\Lambda_{\underline{ba}}
and also that

\displaystyle(\nabla_{[\alpha}\nabla_{\beta]}\xi^{\underline{a}})\big|_{\theta=0}=-\frac{1}{4}\gamma_{{\alpha\beta}}^{\underline{abc}}\Lambda_{\underline{bc}},

(3.45)

is the same
\Lambda
defined by (
3.42
). Because of the nested
\theta=0
projections it is difficult to work with the conditions above. However, it is possible to simplify them in the case where the gravitino (and dilatino) vanishes. The bosonic covariant derivative is defined as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\underline{a}}=E_{\underline{a}}{}^{\underline{m}}(x,\theta)\nabla_{\underline{m}}+E_{\underline{a}}{}^{\mu}(x,\theta)\nabla_{\mu}=(e_{\underline{a}}{}^{\underline{m}}(x)+\cdots)\nabla_{\underline{m}}+(\psi_{\underline{a}}{}^{\mu}(x)+\cdots)\nabla_{\mu}.

(3.46)

where
\cdots
are higher order
\theta
terms. Since
\nabla_{\mu}
is the only operator that has a
\theta
derivative, when
\psi_{\underline{a}}{}^{\mu}=0
we can write

\displaystyle(\nabla_{\underline{a}}({\mathcal{O}}\big|_{\theta=0}))\big|_{\theta=0}=(\nabla_{\underline{a}}{\mathcal{O}})\big|_{\theta=0}.

(3.47)

this allows us to simplify the projections in the conditions for the components of
\xi^{\underline{a}}
and use them in (
3.6
) and (
3.24
). In summary, if we want to impose Poincaré invariance in the full ten dimensional superspace we require that the background is invariant under transformations generated by ten linearly independent vectors defined by
\xi^{\underline{a}}|_{\theta=0}
satisfying
\nabla_{\underline{a}}\xi^{\underline{b}}|_{\theta=0}=-\Lambda_{\underline{a}}{}^{\underline{b}}|_{\theta=0}
, with the further conditions that
\Lambda_{(\underline{ab})}=0
and
\nabla_{\underline{c}}\Lambda_{\underline{ab}}|_{\theta=0}=0
. It is clear that these conditions in (
3.24
) automatically imply the space is flat and without bosonic torsion.

4
Invariance conditions

Before we start let us summarize the results of the previous section. There are only two independent invariance conditions from which all others can be derived, they are

\displaystyle\xi^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\Phi+\xi^{\underline{a}}\nabla_{\underline{a}}\Phi=0,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\xi^{\underline{b}}=\xi^{\beta}\gamma^{\underline{b}}_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{2}\xi_{\underline{a}}(\gamma^{\underline{ab}})_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\nabla_{\beta}\Phi.

(4.2)

We already saw that the second equation can be written purely in terms of
\xi^{\underline{a}}
and
\Phi
, the same can be done with the first and it is simply

\displaystyle\gamma_{\underline{a}}^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\xi^{\underline{a}}\nabla_{\beta}\Phi+10\xi^{\underline{a}}\nabla_{\underline{a}}\Phi=0.

(4.3)

All other equations can be obtained from the ones above and the Bianchi identities. The last equations in both (
3.6
) and (
3.24
) can be obtained from

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\left(\xi^{\underline{a}}\nabla_{\underline{a}}\Phi-4\sigma\right)=0,\quad\nabla_{\underline{a}}\left(\xi^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\Phi+4\sigma\right)=0,

(4.4)

using that
\Omega_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{4}\nabla_{\alpha}\Phi
, the scaling curvatures
R^{(s)}_{AB}
in (
2.26
) and the first lines of (
3.6
) and (
3.24
).

If we can find a set of superfields
(\xi^{A},\Lambda^{\underline{ab}},\sigma)
such that
\delta\nabla_{\alpha}=0
the supergravity background will remain invariant. The idea is to find the superspace conditions for these equations to be satisfied for some specific set of parameters
(\xi^{A},\Lambda^{\underline{ab}},\sigma)
corresponding to translations in four dimensions and one global supersymmetry. From now on we will separate vector indices into four dimensional and six dimensional parts
\underline{a}=(a,i)
. We will decompose the parameters as

\displaystyle\xi^{A}=(\xi^{\alpha},\xi^{a},\xi^{i}),\quad\Lambda^{\underline{ab}}=(\Lambda^{ab},\Lambda^{ai},\Lambda^{ij}).

(4.5)

Later, when imposing four dimensional supersymmetry we will also use complex indices
(I,\bar{I})
for the internal space.

4.1
Four dimensional Poincaré symmetry

Let us first impose only four dimensional Poincaré invariance. Of course the conditions on the background are obvious, however it is still instructive to see how they appear from (
4.1
) and (
4.2
). It is not possible in this case to fix
\xi^{A}=(0,\xi^{a},0)
as superfield conditions. This would constrain the full ten-dimensional superspace to be flat. A less restrictive way to do it is to impose

\displaystyle\xi^{A}\big|_{\theta=0}=(0,\epsilon^{a},0),\quad\Lambda^{{ab}}|_{\theta=0}=(l^{ab},0,0),

(4.6)

where
\epsilon^{a}
is a four dimensional vector and
l^{ab}
a four dimensional Lorentz rotation. All higher components of the superfields
(\xi^{A},\Lambda^{{ab}})
should depend only on
(\epsilon^{a},l^{ab})
and the tensors of the geometry. Since we are dealing with a purely bosonic background we will use that

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{\alpha_{2n+1}}\Phi\right)\Big|_{\theta=0}=0.

(4.7)

Staring from (
4.1
) we see that the first component of
\Phi
should independent of the four dimensional coordinates but it is unconstrained on the internal coordinates.
From using (
3.6
), (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) we fix the next order in
\theta
expansion

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}|_{\theta=0}=-\frac{1}{4}l^{ab}(\gamma_{ab})_{\alpha}{}^{\beta},

(4.8)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\xi^{a}|_{\theta=0}=0,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\xi^{i}|_{\theta=0}=0,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\Lambda^{ab}|_{\theta=0}=0,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\Lambda^{ai}|_{\theta=0}=0,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\Lambda^{ij}|_{\theta=0}=0.

(4.13)

To get the second term in the expansion we first use that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\xi^{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[\nabla_{\beta},\nabla_{\alpha}]\xi^{A}+\frac{1}{2}\{\nabla_{\beta},\nabla_{\alpha}\}\xi^{A}

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[\nabla_{\alpha},\nabla_{\beta}]\xi^{A}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\underline{a}}_{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\underline{a}}\xi^{A}+\frac{1}{2}\xi^{B}R_{\alpha\beta B}{}^{A}

(4.15)

From the second term of (
3.6
) with
A=\underline{a}
we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\nabla_{\beta},\nabla_{\alpha}]\xi^{\underline{a}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\underline{b}}_{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\underline{b}}\xi^{\underline{a}}-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{B}R_{\alpha\beta B}{}^{\underline{a}}+\nabla_{\beta}\xi^{\delta}\gamma_{\alpha\delta}^{\underline{a}}

(4.16)

\displaystyle+2\xi^{\delta}\Omega_{\beta}\gamma_{\alpha\delta}^{\underline{a}}-\nabla_{\beta}\xi^{\underline{b}}T_{\underline{b}\alpha}{}^{\underline{a}}-\xi^{\underline{b}}\nabla_{\beta}T_{\underline{b}\alpha}{}^{\underline{a}}.

(4.17)

Before projecting to
\theta=0
we must find the consequences of Poincaré symmetry conditions
\nabla_{a}\xi^{b}|_{\theta=0}=-l_{a}{}^{b}
,
\nabla_{a}\Lambda^{bc}|_{\theta=0}=0
from (
3.24
). The first term in (
3.24
) gives no information at leading order in
\theta
. Choosing the indices in the second term to be
(a,b)
we find that
T_{abc}|_{\theta=0}=0
. From the choice
(a,i)
and using that
(\nabla_{a}\xi^{i})|_{\theta=0}=0
we have that
T_{cai}|_{\theta=0}=0
. On the other hand, this will imply that
(\nabla_{i}\xi^{b})|_{\theta=0}=0
, as expected. Finally, from the choice
(i,j)
we have that
T_{cij}|_{\theta=0}=0
. From the third term in (
3.24
) we get a vanishing four dimensional curvature
R_{ab}{}^{cd}|_{\theta=0}=0
and also that
R_{ab}{}^{cj}|_{\theta=0}=R_{ab}{}^{ij}|_{\theta=0}=0
. Using Bianchi identities we have that
\nabla_{i}\Lambda^{cd}|_{\theta=0}
and
R_{di}{}^{bc}|_{\theta=0}=0
. Using all this information we get the third term in the covariant theta expansion of
\xi^{a}
and
\xi^{i}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\nabla_{\beta},\nabla_{\alpha}]\xi^{a}|_{\theta=0}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{b}_{\alpha\beta}l_{b}{}^{a}-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{b}R_{\alpha\beta b}{}^{a}-\frac{1}{4}(\gamma^{cd})_{\beta}{}^{\delta}\gamma_{\alpha\delta}^{a}l_{cd}-\epsilon^{b}\nabla_{\beta}T_{{b}\alpha}{}^{a}\right)\Big|_{\theta=0}

(4.18)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{acd}_{\alpha\beta}l_{cd}-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{c}\left(\nabla_{[\alpha}T_{\beta]c}{}^{a}\right)|_{\theta=0},

(4.19)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\nabla_{\beta},\nabla_{\alpha}]\xi^{i}|_{\theta=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{icd}_{\alpha\beta}l_{cd}-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{c}\left(\nabla_{[\alpha}T_{\beta]c}{}^{i}\right)|_{\theta=0}.

(4.20)

The next steps would be to calculate
[\nabla_{\alpha},\nabla_{\beta}]\Lambda^{ab}
and
\nabla_{[\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}\nabla_{\delta]}\xi^{\sigma}
. For the latter we first calculate
[\nabla_{\beta},\nabla_{\alpha}]\xi^{\sigma}
using the same method as above and then applying another covariant derivative and anti symmetrizing in all indices. This will give terms depending on the curvature.
Additional constraints on the background from imposing Poincaré symmetry in four dimensions will come from (
4.1
). At lowest order in
\theta
we only have that

\displaystyle(\xi^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\Phi+\xi^{\underline{a}}\nabla_{\underline{a}}\Phi)|_{\theta=0}=\epsilon^{a}(\nabla_{a}\Phi)|_{\theta=0}=0,

(4.21)

so the first component of
\Phi
, the dilaton, is constant in the four dimensional variables. For the next order we have to compute

\displaystyle\nabla_{[\alpha}\nabla_{\beta]}\left(\xi^{\gamma}\nabla_{\gamma}\Phi+\xi^{\underline{a}}\nabla_{\underline{a}}\Phi\right)\Big|_{\theta=0}=0.

(4.22)

Using the conditions on the symmetry parameters and evaluating at
\theta=0
this expression becomes

\displaystyle\left(2({\nabla}_{\beta}\xi^{\gamma}){\nabla}_{\alpha}{\nabla}_{\gamma}\Phi-2({\nabla}_{\alpha}\xi^{\gamma}){\nabla}_{\beta}{\nabla}_{\gamma}\Phi+([{\nabla}_{\alpha},{\nabla}_{\beta}]\xi^{\underline{a}}){\nabla}_{\underline{a}}\Phi+\xi^{\underline{a}}[{\nabla}_{\alpha},{\nabla}_{\beta}]{\nabla}_{\underline{a}}\Phi\right)\Big|_{\theta=0}=0.

(4.23)

Using (
4.8
), (
4.14
) and (
4.16
) we simplify to

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}l_{ab}(\gamma^{ab})_{[\alpha}{}^{\gamma}({\nabla}_{\beta]}{\nabla}_{\gamma}\Phi)-\frac{1}{2}l_{ab}\gamma^{ab\underline{c}}_{\alpha\beta}{\nabla}_{\underline{c}}\Phi+\epsilon^{b}\left({\nabla}_{[\alpha}T_{\beta]b}{}^{\underline{a}}\right){\nabla}_{\underline{a}}\Phi+\epsilon^{a}[{\nabla}_{\alpha},{\nabla}_{\beta}]{\nabla}_{a}\Phi\right)\Big|_{\theta=0}=0.

(4.24)

Commuting the derivatives in the last term we obtain

\displaystyle[{\nabla}_{\alpha},{\nabla}_{\beta}]{\nabla}_{a}\Phi|_{\theta=0}=-\left({\nabla}_{[\alpha}T_{\beta]a}{}^{\underline{b}}\right){\nabla}_{\underline{b}}\Phi|_{\theta=0}+{\nabla}_{a}[{\nabla}_{\alpha},{\nabla}_{\beta}]\Phi|_{\theta=0},

(4.25)

plugging this into (
4.24
), it becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}l_{ab}(\gamma^{ab})_{[\alpha}{}^{\gamma}({\nabla}_{\beta]}{\nabla}_{\gamma}\Phi)|_{\theta=0}-\frac{1}{2}l_{ab}\gamma^{ab\underline{c}}_{\alpha\beta}{\nabla}_{\underline{c}}\Phi|_{\theta=0}+{\nabla}_{a}[{\nabla}_{\alpha},{\nabla}_{\beta}]\Phi|_{\theta=0}=0.

(4.26)

Now we use the relation

\displaystyle{\nabla}_{\alpha}{\nabla}_{\beta}\Phi=\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\underline{a}}_{\alpha\beta}{\nabla}_{\underline{a}}\Phi-\frac{1}{24}\gamma^{\underline{abc}}_{\alpha\beta}H_{\underline{abc}}=\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\underline{a}}_{\alpha\beta}{\nabla}_{\underline{a}}\Phi-\frac{1}{24}\gamma^{ijk}_{\alpha\beta}H_{ijk},

(4.27)

to finally obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{4}l_{ab}\{\gamma^{ab},\gamma^{\underline{c}}\}_{\alpha\beta}{\nabla}_{\underline{c}}\Phi+\frac{1}{48}l_{ab}H_{ijk}[\gamma^{ab},\gamma^{ijk}]_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{ab\underline{c}})_{\alpha\beta}{\nabla}_{\underline{c}}\Phi-\frac{1}{12}\gamma^{ijk}{\nabla}_{a}H_{ijk}\right)\Big|_{\theta=0}=0.

(4.28)

The first term cancels the third term. The second term is vanishes because
\gamma^{ab}
commutes with
\gamma^{ijk}
. Finally we obtain the equation

\displaystyle({\nabla}_{a}H_{ijk})|_{\theta=0}=0,

(4.29)

which is the expected condition at mass dimension one from (
4.1
). The calculation at fourth order in
\theta
’s is significantly more involved and will not be presented here

4.2
Global four dimensional
N=1
supersymmetry

We now turn to the conditions imposed by four dimensional global supersymmetry and the calculation of the corresponding Killing supervector. First we want to explain the notation we will use. In the breaking of
SO(1,9)
to
SO(1,3)\times SO(6)
the sixteen component spinors will factorize into
SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})\times SU(4)
spinors

\displaystyle\mathbf{16}\to({\mathbf{2}},{\mathbf{4}})+(\bar{\mathbf{2}},\bar{\mathbf{4}}).

(4.30)

The global supersymmetry parameters are the spinors of
SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})
. All the spinors considered in this section will be assumed to be a Grassman odd
SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})
spinor times a Grassmann even
SU(4)
spinor

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}=\varepsilon\otimes\chi+\bar{\varepsilon}\otimes\bar{\chi}.

(4.31)

Will also assume that
\chi
is normalizable such that
\chi\bar{\chi}=1
.
Furthermore, when considering a different spinor
\eta^{\prime}
we will assume that only the
SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})
to be different

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}^{\prime}=\varepsilon^{\prime}\otimes\chi+\bar{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\otimes\bar{\chi}.

(4.32)

This will, for example, imply that

\displaystyle\eta^{\alpha}\gamma^{a}_{\alpha\beta}\eta^{\prime\beta}=(\varepsilon\gamma^{a}\varepsilon^{\prime}),\quad\eta^{\alpha}\gamma^{i}_{\alpha\beta}\eta^{\prime\beta}=0.

(4.33)

When we assumed that
\chi
is normalizable it was already implicit that
\chi
is a nowhere vanishing spinor of the internal manifold which means the internal manifold has
SU(3)
structure.
Locally we can choose a tangent space basis such that an
\mathfrak{su}(3)
subalgebra of
so(1,9)
annihilates
\eta
. To make that explicitly we will choose complex tangent space indices for the internal manifold
(I,\bar{I})
. The antisymmetric product of two gamma matrices can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\gamma^{\underline{ab}}=(\gamma^{ab},\gamma^{aI},\gamma^{a\bar{I}},\gamma^{IJ},\gamma^{\bar{I}\bar{J}},\gamma^{I\bar{J}},\gamma_{\mathbf{1}}).

(4.34)

The spinor
\mathfrak{su}(3)
generators are
\gamma^{I\bar{J}}
and any spinor of the type (
4.31
) will satisfy

\displaystyle(\gamma^{I\bar{J}})_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\tilde{\eta}^{\alpha}=0.

(4.35)

It is clear that we can decompose the real spinor
\tilde{\eta}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}^{\alpha}=\eta^{\alpha}+\bar{\eta}^{\alpha}.

(4.36)

We will choose the normalization of
\gamma_{\mathbf{1}}
such that

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbf{1}}\eta=\eta,\quad\gamma_{\mathbf{1}}\bar{\eta}=-\bar{\eta}.

(4.37)

Finally, the complex pair of spinors
(\eta,\bar{\eta})
also satisfy

\displaystyle\gamma^{\bar{I}}\eta=0,\quad\gamma^{I}\bar{\eta}=0.

(4.38)

With all these set up the Killing supervector and local Lorentz transformation corresponding to
N=1
supersymmetry transformations
will satisfy

\displaystyle\xi^{A}|_{\theta=0}=(\tilde{\eta}^{\alpha},0,0),\quad\Lambda^{{ab}}|_{\theta=0}=(0,0,0),\quad\sigma|_{\theta=0}=0.

(4.39)

Again we can go through the equations imposed by (
3.6
) and (
3.24
) finding the higher components of
(\xi^{A},\Lambda^{{ab}})
and the conditions imposed in the geometry. In this case the only consequences from the
\theta=0
projection coming from (
3.24
) are

\displaystyle(\nabla_{\underline{a}}\tilde{\eta}^{\alpha})|_{\theta=0}=0

(4.40)

It is well known (see,
e.g.
,
[
23
,
24
]
) that this means it is always possible to choose a connection with
SU(3)
holonomy. However it does not yet imply Ricci flatness since the torsion is not constrained. Furthermore, it is possible to have curvature terms like

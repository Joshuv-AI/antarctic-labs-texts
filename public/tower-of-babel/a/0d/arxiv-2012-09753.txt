On some integrable deformations of the Wess-Zumino-Witten model
N. Mohammedi

Abstract
Lie algebra valued equations translating the integrability of a general two-dimensional Wess-Zumino-Witten model are given. We found a simple solution to these equations and identified a new integrable non-linear sigma model. This is a two-parameter deformation of the Wess-Zumino-Witten model.

The two-dimensional coordinates
are

\left(\tau,\sigma\right)
with
\partial_{0}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}
and
\partial_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma}
.
In the rest of the paper, however, we will use the complex coordinates

\left(z=\tau+i\sigma\,,\,\bar{z}=\tau-i\sigma\right)

S=\int{\rm d}z{\rm d}\bar{z}\left[G_{ij}\left(\varphi\right)+B_{ij}\left(\varphi\right)\right]\partial\varphi^{i}\bar{\partial}\varphi^{j}\,\,\,.

(1.1)

The invertible metric
G_{ij}
and the anti-symmetric tensor
B_{ij}
are the backgrounds of the bosonic string theory.
The equations of motion of this theory are

\bar{\partial}\partial\varphi^{l}+\Omega^{l}_{ij}\partial\varphi^{i}\bar{\partial}\varphi^{j}=0\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,\Omega^{k}_{ij}=\Gamma^{k}_{ij}-H^{k}_{ij}\,\,\,\,\,,

(1.2)

where
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}

are, respectively, the Christoffel
symbols and the torsion.

The equations of motion can be cast, for all values of the parameter
\mu
, in the form of a zero curvature relation

\left[\partial+\frac{1}{1+\mu}\left(K_{i}-L_{i}\right)\partial\varphi^{i}\,\,,\,\,\bar{\partial}+\frac{1}{1-\mu}\left(K_{j}+L_{j}\right)\bar{\partial}\varphi^{i}\right]\,=\,0

(1.3)

if the space manifold is equipped with two sets of matrices
K_{i}(\varphi)
and
L_{i}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}K_{j}+\partial_{j}K_{i}-2\Gamma^{l}_{ij}\,K_{l}=0\,\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{i}L_{j}-\partial_{j}L_{i}+2H^{l}_{ij}\,K_{l}=0\,\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{i}L_{j}+\partial_{j}L_{i}-2\Gamma^{l}_{ij}\,L_{l}=\left[L_{i}\,,\,K_{j}\right]+\left[L_{j}\,,\,K_{i}\right]\,\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{i}K_{j}-\partial_{j}K_{i}+2H^{l}_{ij}\,L_{l}=\left[L_{i}\,,\,L_{j}\right]-\left[K_{i}\,,\,K_{j}\right]\,\,\,\,\,.

(1.4)

The last two equations determine the structure of the space manifold of the non-linear sigma model.
On the other hand, the first two relations indicate that the non-linear sigma model is symmetric
under a global isometry transformation

41
]
with

J=\left(K_{i}-L_{i}\right)\partial\varphi^{i}
and
\bar{J}=\left(K_{i}+L_{i}\right)\bar{\partial}\varphi^{i}

two equations
\partial\bar{J}+\bar{\partial}J=0
and
\partial\bar{J}-\bar{\partial}J+\left[J\,,\,\bar{J}\right]=0

Athough the conditions (

[

the most general integrable deformation of the Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) model.

might be a generalisation of the two-parameter integrable
deformations of the WZW model found in
[

The paper is organised as follow: In the next section we give in details the steps

For completeness, we show in section 3 how the Yang-Baxter integrable sigma model is

to the integrability conditions and give, in section 5, the corresponding integrable non-linear sigma models.

2
The general construction

We consider the two-dimensional non-linear sigma model as defined
by the action

\displaystyle S\left(g\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial{\cal{M}}}{\rm d}z{\rm d}\bar{z}\,<g^{-1}\partial g\,\,,\,\,\left(M+N\right)\,g^{-1}\bar{\partial}g>_{{\cal G}}

(2.1)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{6}\int_{{\cal{M}}}{\rm d}^{3}x\,\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\,<g^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g\,\,,\,\,\left[\,g^{-1}\partial_{\nu}g\,\,,\,\,g^{-1}\partial_{\rho}g\right]\,>_{{\cal G}}\,\,\,\,,

where
{\cal M}
is a three-dimensional ball having
x^{\mu}
, with
\mu=1,2,3
, as coordinates
and
\partial{\cal M}
is the boundary of this ball with coordinates
z
and
\bar{z}
.
The bi-linear form
<\,,\,>_{{\cal G}}
is the Killing-Cartan form on the Lie algebra
{\cal G}

and the field
g(z\,,\,\bar{z})
is an element of the Lie group corresponding to
{\cal G}
. The Lie algebra
is of dimension
n
.
The Wess-Zumino-Witten term comes with a parameter
\lambda
.

The Lie algebra
{\cal{G}}
is defined by the commutation
relations
\left[T_{a}\,,\,T_{b}\right]=f^{c}_{ab}T_{c}
.
For a semi-simple Lie algebra the Killing-Cartan form
is
\eta_{ab}=f_{ac}^{d}f_{bd}^{c}
and we have

<T_{a}\,,\,T_{b}>_{{\cal G}}=\eta_{ab}={\rm Tr}\left(T_{a}T_{b}\right)
.
However, for a non semi-simple Lie algebra the bi-linear form is such that

<T_{a}\,,\,T_{b}>_{{\cal G}}=\eta_{ab}
with
\eta_{ab}
an invertible matrix
satisfying
\eta_{ab}f^{b}_{cd}+\eta_{cb}f^{b}_{ad}=0
.

The two quantities
M
and
N
are linear
operator acting on the generators of the Lie algebra
{\cal G}
.
They are required to satisfy the relation

<X\,,\,\left(M+N\right)Y>_{{\cal G}}\,\,=\,\,<\left(M-N\right)X\,,\,Y>_{{\cal G}}\,\,\,\,

(2.2)

for any two elements
X
and
Y
in the Lie algebra
{\cal G}
. In other words,
M
is
symmetric while
N
is anti-symmetric with respect to
<\,,\,>_{{\cal G}}
.

Putting indices, the action of
M
and
N
on the generators
\left\{T_{a}\right\}
of the Lie algebra

{\cal G}
is
M\,T_{a}=M_{a}^{b}\,T_{b}
and
N\,T_{a}=N_{a}^{b}\,T_{b}
and (
2.2
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\eta_{ac}\,M^{c}_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{bc}\,M^{c}_{a}\,\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle\eta_{ac}\,N^{c}_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\eta_{bc}\,N^{c}_{a}\,\,\,\,\,,

(2.3)

where
\eta_{ab}
is the bi-linear form corresponding to the Lie algebra
{\cal G}

as stated above.

It is useful to introduce the two quantities

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{-1}\partial g\,\,\,\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,

\displaystyle\bar{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{-1}\bar{\partial}g\,\,\,\,.

(2.4)

In terms of
A
and
\bar{A}
, the equations of motion of the model take the form

\displaystyle\partial\left[\left(M+N+\lambda I\right)\bar{A}\right]+\bar{\partial}\left[\left(M-N-\lambda I\right)A\right]

\displaystyle+\left[A\,,\,\left(M+N\right)\bar{A}\right]+\left[\bar{A}\,,\,\left(M-N\right)A\right]=0\,\,\,\,.

(2.5)

Multiplying this equation by
g
on the left and
g^{-1}
on the right, we get
the conservation equation

\displaystyle\partial\bar{J}+\bar{\partial}J=0\,\,\,\,\,,

(2.6)

where we have defined the two currents
J
and
\bar{J}
as

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\left(P^{-1}A\right)g^{-1}\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle\bar{J}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\left(Q^{-1}\bar{A}\right)g^{-1}\,\,\,\,.

(2.7)

Here the two linear operators
P^{-1}
and
Q^{-1}
, acting on
A
and
\bar{A}
only, are defined as

\displaystyle P^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M-\left(N-\lambda I\right)\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle Q^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M+\left(N-\lambda I\right)\,\,\,\,,

(2.8)

where
I
is the identity operator on the elements of the Lie algebra
{\cal G}
.

The conservation equation (
2.6
) is a result of to the global symmetry of the action (
2.1
)
under the left multiplication

g\longrightarrow hg\,\,\,\,\,\,,

(2.9)

where
h
is a constant group element.

It is, of course, assumed that the two linear operators
P
and
Q
are invertible. Hence, the inversion of (
2.7
)
gives

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P\left(g^{-1}Jg\right)\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle\bar{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q\left(g^{-1}\bar{J}g\right)\,\,\,\,.

(2.10)

However, the two currents
A
and
\bar{A}
satisfy the Cartan-Maurer identity

\partial\bar{A}-\bar{\partial}A+\left[A\,,\,\bar{A}\right]=0\,\,\,\,.

(2.11)

In terms of the currents
J
and
\bar{J}
, after a use of (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), one finds
the identity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(Q-P\right)\left[g^{-1}\left(\partial\bar{J}+\bar{\partial}J\right)g\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(Q+P\right)\left[g^{-1}\left(\partial\bar{J}-\bar{\partial}J+\varepsilon\left[J\,,\,\bar{J}\right]\right)g\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left(Q+P\right)\left[g^{-1}Jg\,,\,g^{-1}\bar{J}g\right]-Q\left[P\left(g^{-1}Jg\right)\,,\,g^{-1}\bar{J}g\right]

\displaystyle+P\left[Q\left(g^{-1}\bar{J}g\right)\,,\,g^{-1}Jg\right]+\left[P\left(g^{-1}Jg\right)\,,\,Q\left(g^{-1}\bar{J}g\right)\right]=0\,\,\,\,.

(2.12)

We have added and substracted the term proportional to the constant
\varepsilon
.
At this stage
\varepsilon
is just a bookkeeping device but will
later join the constant
\lambda
to form one of the deformation parameters
\lambda\varepsilon
.

In order to have an identity that is suitable for the concept of integrability, we demand that
the linear operators
P
and
Q
are such that the last four terms in (
2.12
)
vanish. That is,

\displaystyle-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left(Q+P\right)\left[g^{-1}Jg\,,\,g^{-1}\bar{J}g\right]-Q\left[P\left(g^{-1}Jg\right)\,,\,g^{-1}\bar{J}g\right]

\displaystyle+P\left[Q\left(g^{-1}\bar{J}g\right)\,,\,g^{-1}Jg\right]+\left[P\left(g^{-1}Jg\right)\,,\,Q\left(g^{-1}\bar{J}g\right)\right]=0\,\,\,\,.

(2.13)

Since the quantities
g^{-1}Jg
and
g^{-1}\bar{J}g
take values in the Lie algebra
{\cal G}
,
this last equation is equivalent to requiring that

\displaystyle\left[PX\,,\,QY\right]-P\left[X\,,\,QY\right]-Q\left[PX\,,\,Y\right]=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left(P+Q\right)\left[X\,,\,Y\right]

(2.14)

for any two Lie algebra elements
X
and
Y
. Notice that the constant
\varepsilon

can be absorbed by a rescaling of the two operators
P
and
Q
(which amounts
to a rescaling of the two currents
J
and
\bar{J}
in (
2.7
)).

When this last relation holds, the currents obey the identity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(Q-P\right)\left[g^{-1}\left(\partial\bar{J}+\bar{\partial}J\right)g\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left(Q+P\right)\left[g^{-1}\left(\partial\bar{J}-\bar{\partial}J+\varepsilon\left[J\,,\,\bar{J}\right]\right)g\right]=0\,\,\,\,\,.

(2.15)

If in addition, the operator
\left(Q+P\right)
is invertible then the two
currents
J
and
\bar{J}
obey the two relations

\displaystyle\partial\bar{J}+\bar{\partial}J

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle\partial\bar{J}-\bar{\partial}J+\varepsilon\left[J\,,\,\bar{J}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,\,\,\,.

(2.16)

Therefore, in addition of being on-shell conserved, the currents
J
and
\bar{J}

have zero curvature.

These last two equations are the consistency conditions of the linear differential system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\partial+\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\mu}\,J\right)\Psi=0\\
\left(\bar{\partial}+\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\mu}\,\bar{J}\right)\Psi=0\end{array}\right.\,\,\,\,.

Here
\Psi\left(z,\bar{z},\mu\right)
is a matrix valued field. The requirement that this linear
differential system
is consistent, for all values of the spectral parameter
\mu
, leads to the equations of motion
of the non-linear sigma model (
2.16
). This is preciseley the statement of the classical integrability
of a two-dimensional non-linear sigma model
[
44
]
.

Finally, in terms of the linear operators
P
and
Q
, the relation (
2.2
)
involving the bi-linear form
<\,\,,\,\,>_{\cal G}
becomes upon using (
2.8
)

<X\,,\,Q^{-1}Y>_{{\cal G}}\,\,=\,\,<P^{-1}X\,,\,Y>_{{\cal G}}-2\lambda<X\,,\,Y>_{{\cal G}}\,\,\,\,.

(2.20)

By writting
X=PZ
and
Y=QW
, where
X
,
Y
,
Z
, and
W
are in the Lie algebra

{\cal G}
, this last relation becomes

<PZ\,,\,W>_{{\cal G}}\,\,=\,\,<Z\,,\,QW>_{{\cal G}}-2\lambda<PZ\,,\,QW>_{{\cal G}}\,\,\,\,.

(2.21)

Summary :

Given two linear operators
P
and
Q
(we assume that
P
,
Q
and
P+Q
are invertible) on a Lie
algebra
{\cal G}
and satisfying, for any two elements
X
and
Y
in
{\cal G}
, the two
relations

\displaystyle<PX\,,\,Y>_{{\cal G}}\,\,=\,\,<X\,,\,QY>_{{\cal G}}-2\lambda<PX\,,\,QY>_{{\cal G}}\,\,\,\,,

(2.22)

\displaystyle\left[PX\,,\,QY\right]-P\left[X\,,\,QY\right]-Q\left[PX\,,\,Y\right]=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left(P+Q\right)\left[X\,,\,Y\right]

(2.23)

then the two-dimensional non-linear sigma model defined by the action

\displaystyle S\left(g\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\partial{\cal{M}}}{\rm d}z{\rm d}\bar{z}\,<g^{-1}\partial g\,\,,\,\,g^{-1}\bar{\partial}g>_{{\cal G}}

(2.24)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{6}\int_{{\cal{M}}}{\rm d}^{3}x\,\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\,<g^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g\,\,,\,\,\left[\,g^{-1}\partial_{\nu}g\,\,,\,\,g^{-1}\partial_{\rho}g\right]\,>_{{\cal G}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\partial{\cal{M}}}{\rm d}z{\rm d}\bar{z}\,<g^{-1}\partial g\,\,,\,\,Q^{-1}\,\left(g^{-1}\bar{\partial}g\right)>_{{\cal G}}\,\,\,\,\,

is classically integrable. We have used (
2.8
) to write
M+N=Q^{-1}+\lambda I
.
The equations of motion stemming from this action are written in (
2.16
) in terms
of two the currents
J
and
\bar{J}

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\left[P^{-1}\left(g^{-1}\partial g\right)\right]g^{-1}\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle\bar{J}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\left[Q^{-1}\left(g^{-1}\bar{\partial}g\right)\right]g^{-1}\,\,\,\,

(2.25)

and are equivalent to the consistency
conditions of the linear system (
2
).

3
The Yang-Baxter sigma model

The so-called Yang-Baxter non-linear sigma model is obtained
as a special case of our construction. Indeed,
let us first assume that the two linear operators are of the form

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa I+\zeta R\,\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa I-\zeta R\,\,\,\,\,,

(3.1)

where
R
is a linear operator
acting on the generators of the Lie algebra
{\cal G}
and

\kappa
and
\zeta^{2}=-\kappa\left(\kappa+\varepsilon\right)>0
are two constants. The parameters

\kappa
and
\varepsilon
are such
\zeta^{2}
is strictly positive.
We also put the Wess-Zumino-Witten term in the action to zero. That is,

\lambda=0\,\,\,\,.

(3.2)

When
\zeta^{2}=-\kappa\left(\kappa+\varepsilon\right)
, the two relations in (
2.22
)
and (
2.23
) become then respectively

\displaystyle<RX\,,\,Y>_{{\cal G}}+<RY\,,\,X>_{{\cal G}}=0\,\,\,\,,

\displaystyle\left[RX\,,\,RY\right]-R\left(\left[RX\,,\,Y\right]+\left[X\,,\,RY\right]\right)=\left[X\,,\,Y\right]\,\,\,\,.

(3.3)

The last relation is known as the modified Yang-Baxter equation while the firt
equation says that the linear operator
R
is anti-symmetric with respect to the bi-linear form.
A solution to these relations is given in
[
6
,
7
]
and is briefly recalled in the next section.

The corresponding action is obtained upon replacing
Q^{-1}
in (
2.24
) and is given by

\displaystyle S\left(g\right)=\int_{\partial{\cal{M}}}{\rm d}z{\rm d}\bar{z}\,<g^{-1}\partial g\,\,,\,\,\left(\kappa I-\zeta R\right)^{-1}\left(g^{-1}\bar{\partial}g\right)>_{{\cal G}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\zeta^{2}=-\kappa\left(\kappa+\varepsilon\right)>0\,\,\,.

(3.4)

This is precisely the action found in
[
7
]
.

Our main concern now is to find solutions to (

\displaystyle 0\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,i,j=1\dots r\,\,\,,

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}H_{i}\,\,\,,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\cal{N}}_{\alpha,\beta}E_{\alpha+\beta}&{\rm{if}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\alpha+\beta\in\Sigma\,\,\,,\\

is the set of roots

7
Conclusions and outlook

We have presented in this work an integrable two-dimensional non-linear sigma model. It is
a two-parameter deformation of the Wess-Zumino-Witten model.
We have found a simple solution to the main integrability equations (
2.22
) and (
2.23
)
of this article. It remains to see if these relations admit other solutions.
The renormalisability of the sigma model studied here and its possible connection
to string theories is another interesting subject to be explored.

There is a strong link between integrability and gauging as shown in
[
8
,
51
]
.
This property is not very neat here. Indeed, the general WZW model (
2.24
)
is related to another theory as follows: The
non-linear sigma model as defined by the action

\displaystyle S\left(g\,,\,h\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{\partial{\cal{M}}}{\rm d}z{\rm d}\bar{z}\,<h^{-1}\partial h\,\,,\,\,Q\,\left(h^{-1}\bar{\partial}h\right)>_{{\cal G}}

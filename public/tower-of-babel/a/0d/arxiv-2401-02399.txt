Numerical Analysis for Dirichlet Optimal Control Problems on Convex Polyhedral Domains
Johannes Pfefferer, Boris Vexler

Abstract
In this paper error analysis for finite element discretizations of Dirichlet boundary control problems is developed. For the first time, optimal discretization error estimates are established in the case of three dimensional polyhedral and convex domains. The convergence rates solely depend on the size of largest interior edge angle. These results are comparable to those for the two dimensional case. However, the approaches from the two dimensional setting are not directly extendable such that new techniques have to be used. The theoretical results are confirmed by numerical experiments.

Introduction

In this paper we consider the following optimal control problem with the control entering the Dirichlet boundary condition of a linear elliptic equation:

\text{Minimize }J(q,u)=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u-u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\lVert q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}

(1a)

over control
q
and state
u
fulfilling the state equation

\displaystyle-\upDelta u

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(1b)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=q

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega

and pointwise control constraints

q_{a}\leq q(s)\leq q_{b}\quad\text{for almost all }s\in\partial\Omega.

(1c)

The precise functional analytic setting is discussed below.

Such problems are often referred as
Dirichlet control problems
. The inherent difficulty in the mathematical treatment of such problems, compared to distributed (control on the right-hand side) or Neumann boundary control, is the fact that the control does not directly enter the standard variational formulation of the state equation.
There are several contributions to the analysis and numerical analysis of such problems. In the majority of the publications on numerical analysis of such problems the computational domain
\Omega
is taken as a two-dimensional polygonal domain, see, e.g.,
Casas and Raymond 2006
;
May et al 2013
, where
\Omega
is in addition assumed to be convex. In
Apel et al 2015
;
Apel et al 2018
also non-convex polygonal domains are treated. In
Deckelnick et al 2009
Dirichlet control problems on smooth two- and three-dimensional domains are considered. The goal of our paper is to provide an a priori error analysis for the finite element discretization of (
1
) in the case of three-dimensional convex polyhedral domains. A direct extension of the approaches from literature treating the two-dimensional case does not seem to be possible and thus, new techniques have to be developed. The main tools we use are estimates in weighted spaces, where the weight is a (smoothed) regularized distance to the boundary. The main contributions of the paper are the following:

•

Normal trace theorem on convex polyhedral domains.
It is well-known, that for a smooth domain
\Omega
the normal trace
\partial_{n}v
of a function
v
in the Sobolev space
W^{2,p}(\Omega)
possesses the regularity
W^{1-\frac{1}{p},p}(\partial\Omega)
for every
1<p<\infty
. For a polyhedral domain this is in general not true, since the normal direction
n
is not continuous. We check, using a general theory from
Maz’ya et al 2010
, that if
v\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, then
\partial_{n}v\in W^{1-\frac{1}{p},p}(\partial\Omega)
for any (convex) polyhedral domain, see
Theorem

4
below. Such a result is available for two dimensional domains, see
(
Casas et al 2009
, Lemma A.2)
. For three dimensional domains we could not locate it in the literature and thus provide a proof. This trace estimates is required in order to obtain the precise regularity of the (very weak) solution of the state equation (
1b
).

•

Weighted
H^{1}
regularity of the discrete harmonic extension.
On the continuous level the very weak solution
u
of the state equation (
1b
) for a given control
q\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
possesses the weighted regularity

\lVert\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

where
\rho(x)
is the distance function to the boundary
\partial\Omega
, see the discussion and references below. We provide a corresponding result on the discrete level, i.e., for the finite element solution
u_{h}
of the corresponding discretized equation we prove

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

(2)

where
\tilde{\rho}
is a regularized distance function to the boundary, see (
21
) below, and
P_{h}^{\partial}
is the
L^{2}
projection on the boundary. This result is given in
Theorem

14
. We use it in our error analysis for the optimal control problem. However, we think it is of an independent interest.

•

Error estimates for the Dirichlet control problem.
We provide a priori error estimates for the error between the optimal solutions
\bar{q}
of (
1
) and
\bar{q}_{h}

of corresponding discretized problems. More precisely, we will establish error estimates for two different types of discretization concepts, the concept of variational discretization and the concept of piecewise linear and continuous discretization, see
Theorem

16
and
Theorem

17
. Under the regularity assumption
u_{d}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
on the desired state we prove the estimate

\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

(3)

for every

s<\min\left(\frac{\pi}{\omega_{\Omega}}-1,\frac{1}{2}\right),

where
\omega_{\Omega}<\pi
is the largest interior edge angle (a precise definition is given below). For
\omega_{\Omega}<\frac{2}{3}\pi
(which is the limiting angle in the above inequality) we even show first order convergence (up to a
\log h
-factor). Please note that the convergence rates only depend on the geometry of the domain. Even more, only edge openings (and not the geometry of vertices) play a role for the convergence rates.
These results are comparable with those for the two-dimensional case from
Apel et al 2018
(under slightly different assumptions on
u_{d}
). For the three-dimensional case the convergence results are absolutely new. We also note that a corresponding result for convex polygonal (i.e. two-dimensional) domains can be shown by the techniques of the present paper as well. In this regard, our new approach seems to be more flexible.

Section

. This paper ends with numerical examples in
Section

6
, which illustrate the theoretical findings of Section 5, and an acknowledgment in
Section

7
.

2
Trace estimates and regularity results for the state equation

Throughout the paper the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is assumed to be polyhedral and convex. The set of faces is denoted by
{\cal F}(\Omega)
, the set of all edges by
{\cal E}(\Omega)
, and the set of all vertices by
{\cal V}(\Omega)
. For every edge
e\in{\cal E}(\Omega)
we denote by
\omega_{e}
the the interior angle at
e
. We define

\omega_{\Omega}=\max_{e\in{\cal E}(\Omega)}w_{e}

(4)

and have
\omega_{\Omega}<\pi
by the convexity of
\Omega
. Moreover, we will use the critical exponent
\lambda_{\Omega}>1
defined as

\lambda_{\Omega}=\frac{\pi}{\omega_{\Omega}}.

(5)

We use the standard notation for the Lebesgue
L^{p}(\Omega)
and Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
as well as the corresponding fractional spaces
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
equipped with Sobolev-Slobodeckij norms, see, e.g.,
(
Grisvard 1985
, Chapter 1.3)
and
(
Adams and Fournier 2003
, Chapter 5)
for details. We will use also weighted Sobolev spaces with a weight being a power of the distance
to the boundary
\partial\Omega
. The function
x\mapsto\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
is Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constant
L=1
, but it is in general not in
W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
, as the boundary
\partial\Omega
is polyhedral. For the definition of the weighted spaces this Lipschitz continuity is sufficient, but for weighted superconvergence estimates (see below) we require a smoothed weight. By
(
Stein 1970
, Theorem 2, page 171)
there exists a function
\rho\colon\bar{\Omega}\to[0,\infty)
with
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
fulfilling

c_{1}\rho(x)\leq\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\leq c_{2}\rho(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in\bar{\Omega},

with some constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
as well as

\lvert D^{\alpha}\rho(x)\rvert\leq c\rho(x)^{1-\lvert\alpha\rvert}\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega,

for every multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3}
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
1<p<\infty
, and
s\in\mathbb{R}
the space
W^{m,p}_{s}(\Omega)
is the space of functions with the finite norm

\lVert v\rVert_{W^{m,p}_{s}(\Omega)}^{p}=\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq m}\int_{\Omega}\lvert D^{\alpha}v(x)\rvert^{p}\rho(x)^{sp}\,dx,

(6)

see
Maz’ya et al 2010
for details. For
s=0
this space obviously coincides with
W^{m,p}(\Omega)
.

We will use the following Hardy type inequalities.

Proposition 1
.

Let
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
. There is a constant
c>0
independent of
v
such that the inequality

\lVert\rho^{-s}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert v\rVert_{H^{s}(\Omega)}

holds for all
v\in H^{s}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We refer to
(
Grisvard 1985
, Theorem 1.4.4.3)
, see also
(
Dyda 2004
, (17))
and
(
Chen and Song 2003
, Corollary 2.4)
.
∎

Proposition 2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<\frac{1}{p}
. There is a constant
c>0
only depending on
\Omega
such that the following inequality holds

\lVert\rho^{-s}v\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{cp}{1-sp}\left(\lVert\rho^{1-s}v\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\lVert\rho^{1-s}\nabla v\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)

for all
v\in W^{1,p}_{1-s}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

The estimate is given in
(
Nečas 2012
, Theorem 2.4, page 293)
. The dependence of the constant on
s
and
p
can be tracked from the proof.
∎

For a face
F\in\mathcal{F}(\partial\Omega)
we will also use standard notion of Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}(F)
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. For
0\leq s\leq 1
the definition of these spaces can be directly extended to the whole boundary leading to the spaces
W^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)
. The space of functions with face-wise regularity is defined as

W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\partial\Omega)=\Set{v\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}{v|_{F}\in W^{s,p}(F)\text{ for all }F\in{\mathcal{F}}(\partial\Omega)}.

(7)

We will use standard trace theorems, which provide continuity for the trace operator
\tau\colon W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to W^{1-\frac{1}{p},p}(\partial\Omega)
, see, e.g.,
(
Grisvard 1985
, Theorem 1.5.1.3)
. The question on regularity of the normal flux
\partial_{n}v
for
v\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
is more involved. For smooth domains the regularity
\partial_{n}v\in W^{1-\frac{1}{p},p}(\partial\Omega)
is well known. On polyhedral domains such a regularity can not be expected in general, since the normal direction
n
is discontinuous. However, we require such regularity only for functions with zero Dirichlet trace, i.e. from
W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Also for
v
from this space the desired regularity can not be expected for a general Lipschitz domain. Below, we provide a corresponding regularity result for polyhedral domains. To this end we first introduce an additional space for functions on the boundary
\partial\Omega
in order to precisely capture the behavior of the normal trace
\partial_{n}v
of a function
v
from a Sobolev space on
\Omega
with a zero trace. For
0<s<1
and
1<p<\infty
we define

N^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)=\Set{g\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}{g\,n_{i}\in W^{s,p}(\partial\Omega),\,1\leq i\leq 3},

(8)

where
n_{i}
are the components of the normal vector
n
. The norm on this space is defined as

\lVert g\rVert_{N^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)}^{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lVert g\,n_{i}\rVert^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)}.

The following proposition describes the behavior of the normal trace on
W^{2,p}_{s}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, see (
6
) for the definition of this weighted space.

Proposition 3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
-\frac{1}{p}<s<1-\frac{1}{p}
. The image of the mapping
\gamma_{1}\colon v\mapsto\partial_{n}v
considered on
W^{2,p}_{s}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is the space
N^{1-s-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}(\partial\Omega)
. The operator
\gamma_{1}\colon W^{2,p}_{s}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\to N^{1-s-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}(\partial\Omega)
is continuous.

Proof.

This statement (which is true on a general Lipschitz domain) follows
from
(
Maz’ya et al 2010
, Theorem 7.8)
with
m=2
using the fact that
all tangential derivatives vanish. For
s=0
and
p=2
this result is explicitly formulated in
(
Gesztesy and Mitrea 2011
, Lemma
6.3)
, where also the corresponding space
N^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
is introduced, cf. also
Geymonat and Krasucki 2000
and
Durán and Muschietti 2001
for a two-dimensional domain.
∎

In the following, we will check
that
N^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)
on convex and polyhedral domains, which allows to prove the desired regularity for the normal trace.

Lemma 1
.

Let
0<s<1
and
1<p<\infty
. Then there holds

N^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{s,p}(\partial\Omega).

Proof.

First we check, that
N^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)\subset W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\partial\Omega)
. Let
g\in N^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)
.
By the definition (
8
) of
N^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)
we have that

gn_{i}\in W^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. Thus, for every vector
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
we obtain

(\gamma\cdot n)g=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\gamma_{i}(gn_{i})\in W^{s,p}(\partial\Omega).

Let
F\in{\mathcal{F}}(\partial\Omega)
be a face with the unit outer normal vector
n_{F}
. Choosing
\gamma=n_{F}
we obtain that
\gamma\cdot n=1
on
F
and therefore
g\in W^{s,p}(F)
with

\lVert g\rVert_{W^{s,p}(F)}\leq c\lVert g\rVert_{N^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)}.

Since
F
was arbitrary we have
g\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\partial\Omega)
.

Let again
g\in N^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)
be arbitrary. By the previous argument
g
is especially in
L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
. In order to prove
g\in W^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)
we have to check, that

I=\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\lvert g(x)-g(y)\rvert^{p}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{2+sp}}\,dx\,dy

is finite. We obtain

I=\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}(\partial\Omega)}\sum_{G\in{\mathcal{F}}(\partial\Omega)}\int_{F}\int_{G}\frac{\lvert g(x)-g(y)\rvert^{p}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{2+sp}}\,dx\,dy.

The contributions for
F=G
are bounded by
\lVert g\rVert^{p}_{W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\partial\Omega)}
. For

F
and
G
with
\bar{F}\cap\bar{G}=\emptyset
, we have

\operatorname{dist}(\bar{F},\bar{G})=\delta_{FG}>0

and therefore

\int_{F}\int_{G}\frac{\lvert g(x)-g(y)\rvert^{p}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{2+sp}}\,dx\,dy\leq c\lVert g\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}.

Thus, it remains to bound the contributions for
F\neq G
with
\bar{F}\cap\bar{G}\neq\emptyset
. Let
n_{F}
and

n_{G}
be the corresponding normal vectors. Since
F
and
G
are neighboring faces of a convex domain, the vectors
n_{F}
and
n_{G}
are linearly independent. This allows us to choose a vector
\gamma=n_{F}+n_{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\gamma\neq 0
with the following properties

\gamma\cdot n_{F}=\gamma\cdot n_{G}=C_{FG}\neq 0.

From the fact that
g\in N^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)
, we know that

g\,n_{i}\in W^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)

for
i=1,2,3
, where
n_{i}
denotes the components of the normal vector
n
. We consider a new function
w
given as

w=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}(g\,n_{i})=(\gamma\cdot n)g.

There holds by construction
w\in W^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)
and therefore especially

\int_{F}\int_{G}\frac{\lvert w(x)-w(y)\rvert^{p}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{2+sp}}\,dx\,dy\leq c\lVert g\rVert_{N^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)}

is finite. Since there holds
w=(\gamma\cdot n)g
with
\gamma\cdot n=C_{FG}\neq 0
on
F\cup G
, the contribution

\int_{F}\int_{G}\frac{\lvert g(x)-g(y)\rvert^{p}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{2+sp}}\,dx\,dy.

is finite and bounded by
\lVert g\rVert_{N^{s,p}(\partial\Omega)}
as well. This completes the proof.
∎

As a corollary we obtain the following trace theorem for the normal trace.

Theorem 4
.

Let
v\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
for some
1<p<\infty
. Then, the normal derivative
\partial_{n}v
on

\partial\Omega
is well defined (in the trace sense) with

\partial_{n}v\in W^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}(\partial\Omega).

There is a constant
c>0
independent of
v
such that

\lVert\partial_{n}v\rVert_{W^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert v\rVert_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
v\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. From
Proposition

3
for
s=0
, we have
\partial_{n}v\in N^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}(\partial\Omega)
and there holds

\lVert\partial_{n}v\rVert_{N^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert v\rVert_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}.

Then by the embedding

N^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}(\partial\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}(\partial\Omega)

from
Lemma

1
we obtain the desired result.
∎

A standard variational formulation of the state equation (
1b
) requires
q\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
. For a functional analytic formulation of the optimal control problem, we require a formulation, which allows for
q\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
. We will use the so called very weak formulation, which is well posed even for
q\in H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
, where
H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
is the dual space of
H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
. For given
q\in H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
we call
u
a very weak solution of (
1b
), if

u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\quad:\quad(u,-\upDelta\varphi)=-\langle q,\partial_{n}\varphi\rangle_{\partial\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(9)

The duality paring between
H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
and its dual is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\partial\Omega}
.

Remark 1
.

It is straightforward to check, that for
q\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
the standard variational solution
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
of (
1b
) fulfills the very weak formulation
Eq.

9
.

The existence, uniqueness, and also additional regularity
u\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Omega)
if
q\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
of a very weak solution in the above sense is well known for smooth domains. Such results for polygonal (and even non-convex) domains can be found in
Apel et al 2016
. The result below holds for three dimensional convex polyhedral domains.

Theorem 5
.

Let
q\in H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
. There exists a unique very weak solution of (
1b
) in the sense of (
9
).

1.

There is a constant
c
depending only on
\Omega
such that the following estimate holds

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert q\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}.

2.

If
q\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
, then
u\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Omega)
and there holds

\lVert\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert u\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

3.

If
q\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
then
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and there holds

\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert q\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}.

Proof.

The existence, uniqueness and the estimate for
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is shown by the Riesz representation theorem. To this end we consider a linear functional
G\colon L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
defined in the following way. Let
\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and let
\varphi
be the weak solution of

\displaystyle-\upDelta\varphi

\displaystyle=\psi

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega.

We set
G(\psi)=-\langle q,\partial_{n}\varphi\rangle_{\partial\Omega}
. By elliptic regularity (due to convexity of the domain, see
(
Grisvard 1985
, Theorem 3.2.1.2)
) we have
\varphi\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and by the trace estimate from
Theorem

4
for
p=2
we get
\partial_{n}\varphi\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
with

\lVert\partial_{n}\varphi\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

There holds

G(\psi)=-\langle q,\partial_{n}\varphi\rangle_{\partial\Omega}\leq\lVert q\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}\lVert\partial_{n}\varphi\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert q\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Thus,
G
is a continuous linear functional, and Riesz representation theorem yields the result.
The estimate for
q\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
is standard and the result for
q\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
is obtained by interpolation between the two previous estimates.
It remains to discuss the weighted estimate. It follows by
(
Jerison and Kenig 1995
, Theorem 4.1)
from the regularity
u\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Omega)
and the fact that
u
is harmonic in
\Omega
.
∎

Theorem 7

Let

the solution

Eq.

10
possesses the regularity
z\in W^{2,2}_{-s}(\Omega)
. There holds the estimate

\lVert z\rVert_{W^{2,2}_{-s}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

with a constant
c>0
only depending on
\Omega
.

Proof.

There holds

\lVert z\rVert_{W^{2,2}_{-s}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert\rho^{-s}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\rho^{-s}\nabla z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\rho^{-s}\nabla^{2}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Since
s<\frac{1}{2}
we obtain by
Proposition

2

\lVert\rho^{-s}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\left(\lVert\rho^{1-s}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\rho^{1-s}\nabla z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\lVert z\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

and

\lVert\rho^{-s}\nabla z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\left(\lVert\rho^{1-s}\nabla z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\rho^{1-s}\nabla^{2}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\lVert z\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Thus, by
H^{2}
regularity we get

\lVert\rho^{-s}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\rho^{-s}\nabla z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\lVert z\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

For the term involving weighted second derivatives we obtain again by
Proposition

2
since
s<\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\lVert\rho^{-s}\nabla^{2}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\left(\lVert\rho^{1-s}\nabla^{2}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\rho^{1-s}\nabla^{3}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\left(\lVert z\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\rho^{1-s}\nabla^{3}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

To estimate the term on the right-hand side involving third derivatives, we will use results from
Maz’ya and Rossmann 2010
. The weighted spaces there are defined using the distance functions to the vertices and edges of the domain. Let
r
be the distance function to the set of edges of
\Omega
. Thus,
\rho(x)\leq r(x)
for all
x\in\Omega
and since
1-s>0
we estimate

\lVert\rho^{1-s}\nabla^{3}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert r^{1-s}\nabla^{3}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Next, we use estimates in weighted spaces form
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann 2010
, Lemma 4.3.1 and Corollary 4.1.10)
. This results in

\lVert r^{1-s}\nabla^{3}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\left(\lVert r^{-s}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert r^{1-s}\nabla f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

under the following conditions on the power of the weight. We choose in
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann 2010
, Lemma 4.3.1)

l=3,\;p=2,\;\beta_{j}=1-s,\;\delta_{k}=1-s\quad\text{for all }j,k.

We use the notation
\Lambda_{v}
for the smallest positive eigenvalue of certain operator
pencils for the vertex
v\in{\cal V}(\Omega)
, see
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann 2010
, Section 4.1.6)
for the precise definition. By the convexity of
\Omega
we have
\Lambda_{v}>1
, see
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann 2010
, Section 4.3.1)
. The conditions of
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann 2010
, Lemma 4.3.1)
which we have to check are

-1-\Lambda_{v}<\frac{1}{2}+s<\Lambda_{v}\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert 1+s\rvert<\lambda_{\Omega}.

The first condition is fulfilled by
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
and
\Lambda_{v}>1
and the second one by
s<s_{\Omega}
and the definition (
11
) of
s_{\Omega}
. Please note, that
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann 2010
, Lemma 4.3.1)
provides regularity in the space
V^{3,2}_{\beta,\delta}
in the notation of
Maz’ya and Rossmann 2010
. Since all
\beta_{j}=\delta_{k}=1-s
the norm of this space gives a bound for the desired term
\lVert r^{1-s}\nabla^{3}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
due to
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann 2010
, (3.12), page 90)
.
Putting terms together we obtain:

\lVert\rho^{-s}\nabla^{2}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\left(\lVert r^{-s}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert r^{1-s}\nabla f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert z\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

The
H^{2}
norm of
z
is estimated as before by the
H^{2}
regularity. The term involving
\nabla f
is obviously bounded by
\lVert\nabla f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. To estimate
\lVert r^{-s}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
we use the Hölder inequality and obtain

\displaystyle\lVert r^{-s}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}r(x)^{-2s}f(x)^{2}\,dx\leq\lVert r^{-2s}\rVert_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\lVert f^{2}\rVert_{L^{3}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\Omega}r(x)^{-3s}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq c\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2},

where we used the embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega)
as well as the fact that
r^{-3s}
is integrable, since
r
is the distance to the set of edges. This completes the proof.
∎

From the regularity
z\in W^{2,2}_{-s}(\Omega)
from the previous theorem we get by
Proposition

3
and
Lemma

1
that
\partial_{n}z\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+s}(\partial\Omega)
. Under an additional assumption on
\Omega
(beyond convexity) we obtain a further regularity result for the normal trace.

Corollary 8
.

Let
\lambda_{\Omega}>\frac{3}{2}
. Then in the setting of
Theorem

7
there holds
\partial_{n}z\in H^{1}(F)
for every face
F\in{\cal F}(\Omega)
and

\lVert\partial_{n}z\rVert_{H^{1}(F)}\leq c\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

with a constant
c>0
independent of
f
.

Proof.

Inspecting the proof of
Theorem

7
in the case of
\lambda_{\Omega}>\frac{3}{2}
, we obtain the existence of some
s
in the range
\frac{1}{2}<s<1
(in contrast to
s<\frac{1}{2}
in
Theorem

7
) with

\lVert r^{1-s}\nabla^{3}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

where
r
is the distance function to the set of edges of
\Omega
.
The corresponding conditions from
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann 2010
, Lemma 4.3.1)
are then still fulfilled for some
s>\frac{1}{2}
. Also for the corresponding terms of lower order there holds

\lVert r^{-s-2}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert r^{-s-1}\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert r^{-s}\nabla^{2}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

The above weighted estimate for
u
and its derivatives imply that
u\in V^{3,2}_{\beta,\delta}(\Omega)
with
\beta_{j}=1-s
and
\delta_{k}=1-s
for all
j,k
in the notation of
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann 2010
, Section 4.1.2)
. Thus, we obtain for every partial derivative
\partial_{x_{i}}u
, that
\partial_{x_{i}}u\in V^{2,2}_{\beta,\delta}(\Omega)
. The trace of
\partial_{x_{i}}u
on every face
F\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega)
fulfills
\partial_{x_{i}}u\in V^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}},2}_{\beta,\delta}(F)
, see
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann 2010
, Section 4.1.5)
. This directly implies
\partial_{n}u\in V^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}},2}_{\beta,\delta}(F)
. Since the weights
\beta_{j}
and
\delta_{k}
are equal, the above spaces correspond to the spaces with the only weight
r
(distance to the edges) and one obtains
\partial_{n}u\in V^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}},2}_{1-s}(F)
in the notation of
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann 2010
, Section 2.1.5)
. By
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann 2010
, Lemma 2.1.10)
, where an equivalent norm of

is presented, we get for every multi-index
\alpha
with
\lvert\alpha\rvert=1

\lVert r^{1-s}D^{\alpha}(\partial_{n}u)\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(F)}\leq\lVert\partial_{n}u\rVert_{V^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}},2}_{1-s}(F)}\leq c\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Since on
F
the distance

describes the distance to the boundary of

Proposition

1
leading to

\lVert D^{\alpha}(\partial_{n}u)\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}=\lVert r^{-(1-s)}r^{1-s}D^{\alpha}(\partial_{n}u)\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}\leq c\lVert r^{1-s}D^{\alpha}(\partial_{n}u)\rVert_{H^{1-s}(F)},

where we used
1-s<\frac{1}{2}
. Thus, we get

\displaystyle\lVert D^{\alpha}(\partial_{n}u)\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert r^{1-s}D^{\alpha}(\partial_{n}u)\rVert_{H^{1-s}(F)}\leq c\lVert r^{1-s}D^{\alpha}(\partial_{n}u)\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(F)}\leq c\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

This results in

∎

3
Optimal control problem

In this section we discuss the optimal control problem (
1
) and provide optimality conditions. Throughout we make the minimal assumption
u_{d}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. We consider the formulation based on the control space
Q=L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
corresponding to the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
regularization term in the cost function (
1a
). Other choices are possible. We refer to
Gunzburger et al 1991
for
H^{1}
type regularization, to
Of et al 2015
;
Chowdhury et al 2017
;
John et al 2018
;
Winkler 2020
for
H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
(energy type) regularization, to
Mateos 2021
for a sparsity promoting regularization, to
Vexler 2007
for a problem with a finite dimensional control space and to
Kunisch and Vexler 2007
for brief overview of possible formulations.

The set of admissible controls is defined by

{Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}=\Set{q\in Q}{q_{a}\leq q(s)\leq q_{b}\text{ for almost all }s\in\partial\Omega}.

For simplicity of presentation and in order to avoid case distinctions we assume throughout
q_{a},q_{b}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

q_{a}<0<q_{b}.

(12)

However, other choices are possible, which eventually lead to more regular solutions, see also the discussion in
Apel et al 2015
;
Apel et al 2018
. The result from
Theorem

5
provides the existence of a (linear and continuous) solution operator
S\colon Q\to H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Omega)
with
S\colon q\mapsto u=u(q)
solving (
9
). Note, that due to
q\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
the duality pairing in (
9
) can be replaced by the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
inner product
(q,\partial_{n}\varphi)_{\partial\Omega}
.

Using this solution operator we define the reduced cost functional

j\colon Q\to R,\quad j(q)=J(q,Sq).

Thus, we formulate the optimal control problem as

\text{minimize }j(q),\quad q\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}.

(13)

It is straightforward to check, that the reduced cost functional
j
is continuous and strictly convex. Thus, the existence and uniqueness of an optimal solution
\bar{q}\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}
follows by standard arguments. We refer to
\bar{u}=S\bar{q}
as the optimal state.

The functional
j
is two times Fréchet differentiable. For a control
q\in Q
and a direction
\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\in Q
the directional derivatives are
given by

j^{\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=(u-u_{d},\delta\mspace{-2mu}u)+\alpha(q,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}

and

j^{\prime\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\alpha\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2},

where
u=Sq
and
\delta\mspace{-2mu}u=S\delta\mspace{-2mu}q
.

The necessary optimality condition for (
13
) is given as a variational inequality:

\bar{q}\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}\quad:\quad j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q-\bar{q})\geq 0\quad\text{for all }\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}.

(14)

By convexity of
j
this condition is also sufficient for the optimality.

To derive the optimality system we require an adjoint based representation of
j^{\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)
. To this end we introduce the corresponding adjoint equation. For a given control
q\in Q
and corresponding state
u=u(q)\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Omega)

we introduce the adjoint state
z=z(q)\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
as the weak solution of the adjoint equation

\displaystyle-\upDelta z

\displaystyle=u-u_{d}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega

with the corresponding weak formulation

z\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\quad:\quad(\nabla\varphi,\nabla z)=(u-u_{d},\varphi)\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(15)

A straightforward calculation provides an expressions for the directional derivative of
j
based on the solution of this adjoint equation, cf., e.g.,
Casas and Raymond 2006
:

j^{\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=(\alpha q-\partial_{n}z,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega},

where
q,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\in Q
,
u=u(q)\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Omega)
and
z=z(q)\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
solves the adjoint equation (
15
).

Remark 2
.

Let
\delta\mspace{-2mu}q
possess additional regularity
\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
and
B\colon H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)\to H^{1}(\Omega)
be an arbitrary extension operator, i.e. fulfilling
\tau B\delta\mspace{-2mu}q=\delta\mspace{-2mu}q
with the trace operator
\tau\colon H^{1}(\Omega)\to H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
. Then the derivative
j^{\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)
can be also expressed as

j^{\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=(u-u_{d},B\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-(\nabla B\delta\mspace{-2mu}q,\nabla z)+\alpha(q,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}.

This expression follows from

\displaystyle(u-u_{d},\delta\mspace{-2mu}u)

\displaystyle=(u-u_{d},\delta\mspace{-2mu}u-B\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)+(u-u_{d},B\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)

\displaystyle=(\nabla z,\nabla(\delta\mspace{-2mu}u-B\delta\mspace{-2mu}q))+(u-u_{d},B\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)

\displaystyle=-(\nabla z,\nabla B\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)+(u-u_{d},B\delta\mspace{-2mu}q),

where we used that for
\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
the solution
\delta\mspace{-2mu}u=S\delta\mspace{-2mu}q
fulfills the weak formulation

\delta\mspace{-2mu}u\in B\delta\mspace{-2mu}q+H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\quad:\quad(\nabla\delta\mspace{-2mu}u,\nabla\varphi)=0\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

On the discrete level we will use a corresponding expression for the derivative of the discrete reduced cost functional.

Due to the structure of the admissible set
{Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}
the optimality condition
Eq.

14
can be equivalently rewritten as

\bar{q}=P_{[q_{a},q_{b}]}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\partial_{n}\bar{z}\right),

(16)

where
\bar{z}=z(\bar{q})
is the optimal adjoint state and
P_{[q_{a},q_{b}]}
is the pointwise projection on the interval
[q_{a},q_{b}]
. Since
\bar{z}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
\partial_{n}\bar{z}\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
by
Theorem

4
, and since this projection is well defined as self-mapping of
H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
, cf.
(
Kunisch and Vexler 2007
, Lemma 3.3)
, we obtain additional regularity for the optimal control
\bar{q}\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
. This allows to use the classical weak formulation of the state equation in the following optimality system.

Theorem 9
.

A control
\bar{q}\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}
is the solution of the optimal control problem
(
13
) if and only if the triple
(\bar{q},\bar{u},\bar{z})

with
\bar{q}\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)\cap{Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}
,
\bar{u}\in B\bar{q}+H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with an arbitrary extension operator
B\colon H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)\to H^{1}(\Omega)
and
\bar{z}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
fulfills the following system:

•

State equation:

(\nabla\bar{u},\nabla\varphi)=0\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

(17a)

•

Adjoint equation:

(\nabla\bar{z},\nabla\varphi)=(\bar{u}-u_{d},\varphi)\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\

(17b)

•

Optimality condition:

(\alpha\bar{q}-\partial_{n}\bar{z},\delta\mspace{-2mu}q-\bar{q})_{\partial\Omega}\geq 0\quad\text{for all }\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}.

(17c)

Moreover, there exists a constant
c>0
only depending on
\alpha
and
\Omega
such that

\lVert\bar{q}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}+\lVert\bar{u}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\lVert\bar{z}\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Remark 3
.

The regularity from th previous theorem can be extended to
\bar{q}\in W^{1-\frac{1}{p},p}(\partial\Omega)
provided
u_{d}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with
2\leq p<p_{\Omega}
and
p_{\Omega}>2
being the critical value for
W^{2,p}
regularity. The analysis in
Casas and Raymond 2006
and
May et al 2013
is based on this regularity setting.

For the two dimensional case the optimal control
\bar{q}
is known to vanish at vertices of
\partial\Omega
. We extend this result to our three-dimensional setting. In particular, we prove that the optimal control is equal to zero on all vertices and edges of
\partial\Omega
. This is especially required when proving the
H^{1}(\partial\Omega)
regularity in
Theorem

12
.

Theorem 10
.

Let
u_{d}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with some
p>3
. Let
\bar{q}
be the optimal control of (
13
),
\bar{u}
be the corresponding state and
\bar{z}
the corresponding adjoint state. Then, we have
\bar{z}\in C^{1,\gamma}(\bar{\Omega})
with some
\gamma>0
, and
\partial_{n}\bar{z},\bar{q}\in C(\partial\Omega)
. Moreover, for every two faces
F,G\in{\mathcal{F}}(\partial\Omega)
,
F\neq G
with non-empty intersection, i.e.,

\bar{F}\cap\bar{G}\neq\emptyset,

there holds

\partial_{n}\bar{z}(s)=\bar{q}(s)=0\quad\text{for all }s\in\bar{F}\cap\bar{G}.

Proof.

From
Theorem

9
we know that
\bar{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and therefore we have for the right-hand side of the adjoint equation (
17b
)
\bar{u}-u_{d}\in L^{r}(\Omega)
with some
r>3
. Thus, by
Proposition

6
, we get
\bar{z}\in C^{1,\gamma}(\bar{\Omega})
with some
\gamma>0
and especially
\nabla\bar{z}
is continuous on
\partial\Omega
. Since
\bar{z}=0
on
F
and on
G
, we have that all tangential derivatives of
\bar{z}
vanish on
F
and on
G
and by continuity on
\bar{F}\cap\bar{G}
. Since the normal vectors
n_{F}
and
n_{G}
are linearly independent, this implies that
\nabla\bar{z}
vanishes on
\bar{F}\cap\bar{G}
. This results in
\partial_{n}\bar{z}=0
on
\bar{F}\cap\bar{G}
and the continuity of
\partial_{n}\bar{z}
on
\partial\Omega
. The same result for the optimal control
\bar{q}
follows from the optimality condition (
16
) taking into account the assumption (
12
), i.e.
q_{a}<0<q_{b}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Based on
Theorem

7
we provide additional regularity for the optimal control, state, and adjoint state assuming
u_{d}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 11
.

Let
u_{d}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
0<s<s_{\Omega}
, where
s_{\Omega}
is defined in (
11
). Let
(\bar{q},\bar{u},\bar{z})
be the solution of the optimality system

Eq.

17
. Then, there holds
\bar{z}\in W^{2,2}_{-s}(\Omega)
,
\bar{q}\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+s}(\partial\Omega)
, and
\bar{u}\in H^{1+s}(\Omega)
. There is a constant
c>0
independent of
u_{d}
such that

\lVert\bar{z}\rVert_{W^{2,2}_{-s}(\Omega)}+\lVert\bar{q}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+s}(\partial\Omega)}+\lVert\partial_{n}\bar{z}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+s}(\partial\Omega)}+\lVert\bar{u}\rVert_{H^{1+s}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

From
Theorem

9
, we obtain
\bar{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega)

and the corresponding estimate. Thus, it holds
\bar{u}-u_{d}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with

\lVert\bar{u}-u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

This allows for the application of the weighted regularity result from
Theorem

7
leading to
\bar{z}\in W^{2,2}_{-s}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and the estimate

\lVert\bar{z}\rVert_{W^{2,2}_{-s}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\bar{u}-u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Application of the trace estimate from
Proposition

3
leads to
\partial_{n}\bar{z}\in N^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+s,2}(\partial\Omega)
and the embedding
N^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+s,2}(\partial\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+s}(\partial\Omega)
from
Lemma

1
results then in
\partial_{n}\bar{z}\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+s}(\partial\Omega)
with

\lVert\bar{q}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+s}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\partial_{n}\bar{z}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+s}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\bar{z}\rVert_{W^{2,2}_{-s}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

where we used, that the projection
P_{[q_{a},a_{b}]}
is well defined as a self-mapping of
H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+s}(\partial\Omega)
, cf.
(
Kunisch and Vexler 2007
, Lemma 3.3)
. The regularity of
\bar{u}
is now obtained by an interpolation argument. For Dirichlet data from
H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
the harmonic extension is in
H^{1}(\Omega)
and for Dirichlet data from
H^{1}(\partial\Omega)
it lies in
H^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(\Omega)
, see
(
Jerison and Kenig 1995
, Theorem 5.15 (b),

)
. Thus, by interpolation we get

\lVert\bar{u}\rVert_{H^{1+s}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\bar{q}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+s}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

(beyond convexity) we obtain a further regularity result.

Theorem 12

Let

and let

be such that
\lambda_{\Omega}>\frac{3}{2}
, where

is the critical exponent defined in (

(\bar{q},\bar{u},\bar{z})
be the solution of the optimality system

17
. Then, for
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
there holds

\lVert\bar{z}\rVert_{W^{2,2}_{-s}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

with a constant
c
independent of
s
and
u_{d}
. Moreover, we have
\bar{q},\partial_{n}\bar{z}\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega)
and the estimate

\lVert\bar{q}\rVert_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}+\lVert\partial_{n}\bar{z}\rVert_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

holds with a constant
c>0
independent of
u_{d}
.

Proof.

The proof of the first result follows the lines of the proof of
Theorem

11
.
Since
\lambda_{\Omega}>\frac{3}{2}
we have
s_{\Omega}=\frac{1}{2}
and the estimate from
Theorem

7
holds for all
s<\frac{1}{2}
leading to

\lVert\bar{z}\rVert_{W^{2,2}_{-s}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

For the second result, we obtain by
Corollary

8

\partial_{n}\bar{z}\in H^{1}(F)
for every
F\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega)
and the corresponding estimate. Since
u_{d}\in H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega)
by Sobolev embedding, we can apply
Theorem

10
leading to
\partial_{n}\bar{z}\in C(\partial\Omega)
. This results in the global regularity
\partial_{n}\bar{z}\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega)
and the corresponding estimate holds. The result for
\bar{q}
follows from the optimality condition
Eq.

17c
. This completes the proof.
∎

4
Discretization estimates for the state equation

For the discretization we consider the space of linear finite elements
\widehat{V}_{h}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
defined on a mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
from a family of shape regular quasi-uniform meshes, see, e.g.,
Brenner and Scott 2008
. The mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}=\{K\}
consists of cells
K
, which are open tetrahedrons.
We also use the subspace
V_{h}=\widehat{V}_{h}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and the space
V_{h}^{\partial}
of traces of functions from
\widehat{V}_{h}
, i.e.

V_{h}^{\partial}=\Set{\tau v_{h}}{v_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}},

where
\tau\colon H^{1}(\Omega)\to H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
is the trace operator. The state equation (
9
) is discretized as follows: For given
q\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
the discrete state
u_{h}=u_{h}(q)\in\widehat{V}_{h}
fulfills

u_{h}\in E_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}q+V_{h}\quad:\quad(\nabla u_{h},\nabla\varphi_{h})=0\quad\text{for all }v_{h}\in V_{h},

(18)

where
P_{h}^{\partial}\colon L^{2}(\partial\Omega)\to V_{h}^{\partial}
is the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
projection and
E_{h}\colon V_{h}^{\partial}\to\widehat{V}_{h}
is the extension-by-zero operator. We call the operator
S_{h}\colon q\mapsto u_{h}(q)
the discrete solution operator. For
q\in V_{h}^{\partial}
it is often called the discrete harmonic extension.

Remark 4
.

The above formulation is independent of the choice of the extension operator and thus, other choices are possible, which lead to equivalent formulations. The choice of the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
projection is due to the consideration of potentially irregular boundary data and allows for optimal convergence of the finite element method under minimal regular assumptions on the data, cf. the discussions in
Berggren 2004
;
Bartels et al 2004
;
Apel et al 2017
.

By straightforward arguments one obtains the unique solvability of (
18
). Moreover, finite element error estimates can be established.

Theorem 13
.

Let
q\in H^{s}(\partial\Omega)
with some
0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Let
u
be the solution of (
9
) and
u_{h}
of (
18
). There holds

\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{s+\frac{1}{2}}\lVert q\rVert_{H^{s}(\partial\Omega)}

with a constant
c>0
independent of
q
and
h
.

Proof.

The proof follows standard arguments. For the convenience of the reader, we repeat the essential steps.
Let
\tilde{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be the harmonic extension of
P_{h}^{\partial}q
, i.e., the weak solution to

-\Delta\tilde{u}=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,\quad\tilde{u}=P_{h}^{\partial}q\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

We split the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-error into two,

\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert u-\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{u}-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(19)

We notice that
\tilde{u}
is also a very weak solution, such that
u-\tilde{u}
satisfies
Eq.

9
with Dirichlet data
q-P_{h}^{\partial}q
. As a consequence, we get from
Theorem

5

\lVert u-\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert q-P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{s+\frac{1}{2}}\lVert q\rVert_{H^{s}(\partial\Omega)},

where we applied a standard estimate for the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
projection in the last step. It remains to estimate the second term in
Eq.

19
. As
\tilde{u}
is also a weak solution with Dirichlet boundary data
P_{h}^{\partial}q
, we can apply a duality argument, i.e., we define
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
as the weak solution to

-\Delta w=\tilde{u}-u_{h}\quad\text{in }\Omega,\quad w=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega

and
w_{h}
its
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
-Ritz-projection.
Moreover, let
\tilde{i}_{h}:H^{1}(\Omega)\to\widehat{V}_{h}
be the Scott-Zhang interpolation operator
Scott and Zhang 1990
. This operator preserves boundary conditions from
V_{h}^{\partial}
and admits the estimate

\lVert\nabla(v-\tilde{i}_{h}v)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{s}\lVert v\rVert_{H^{1+s}(\Omega)}

(20)

for all
v\in H^{1+s}(\Omega)
and
s\in[0,1]
. Then, we obtain

\lVert\tilde{u}-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=(\nabla(\tilde{u}-\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u}),\nabla(w-w_{h}))\leq\lVert\nabla(\tilde{u}-\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\nabla(w-w_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

A standard finite element error estimate employing elliptic regularity in convex domains leads to

\lVert\nabla(w-w_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch\lVert\tilde{u}-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The stability estimate
Eq.

20
for the Scott-Zhang interpolation operator and
Theorem

5
imply

\lVert\nabla(\tilde{u}-\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}.

As a consequence, we get

\lVert\tilde{u}-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}

and by means of an inverse estimate

\lVert\tilde{u}-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

An interpolation argument gives

\lVert\tilde{u}-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{s+\frac{1}{2}}\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{H^{s}(\partial\Omega)}.

The
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
projection is known to be stable in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
. Moreover, it is stable in
H^{1}(\partial\Omega)
by the quasi-uniformity of the mesh, and thus also on
H^{s}(\partial\Omega)
by another interpolation argument. Combining the previous results completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5
.

The above estimate can be extended to the range
0\leq s\leq\frac{3}{2}
(eventually) with a different norm on the right hand side (see e.g.
Bartels et al 2004
for the case
s=\frac{3}{2}
), but this is not required in our analysis.

By means of
Theorem

5
and
Theorem

13
, the discrete solution operator is (uniformly in
h
) bounded as
S_{h}\colon L^{2}(\partial\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
.

We require a discrete version of the weighted stability result from
Theorem

5
. To this end we define a regularized weight
\tilde{\rho}\colon\bar{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
by

\quad\tilde{\rho}(x)=\sqrt{\rho(x)^{2}+\kappa^{2}h^{2}},

(21)

where
\kappa\geq 1
is chosen later large enough independent of
h
.

Theorem 14
.

Let
q\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
and
u_{h}=u_{h}(q)
be the solution of
Eq.

18
. There is a constant
c>0
independent on
h
and
q
such that the following estimate holds

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

To provide a proof of this result we require some weighted interpolation and super-convergence estimates. First, we obtain by direct calculation that

\lvert\nabla\tilde{\rho}\rvert\leq c\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert\nabla^{2}\tilde{\rho}\rvert\leq c\tilde{\rho}^{-1}.

(22)

A direct consequence of the mean value theorem is due to the boundedness of the gradient of
\tilde{\rho}
the existence of a constant
c
independent of
h
such that the following estimate holds

\max_{x\in\bar{K}}\tilde{\rho}(x)\leq c\min_{x\in\bar{K}}\tilde{\rho}(x)\quad\text{for all }K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

(23)

This directly leads to weighted interpolation error estimates for the Lagrange interpolation
i_{h}\colon C(\bar{\Omega})\to\widehat{V}_{h}
, which are summarized in the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
be arbitrary. Then there is a constant
c>0
independent of
\alpha
,
h
and
v
such that

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}(v-i_{h}v)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+h\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}\nabla(v-i_{h}v)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}\nabla^{2}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

holds for all
v\in H^{2}(\Omega)
.

The next lemma provides a weighted inverse estimate.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
be arbitrary. There is a constant
c>0
independent of
\alpha
and
h
such that

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}\nabla v_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}\leq ch^{-1}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}v_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}

is valid for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
v_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}
.

Proof.

The proof follows by standard inverse estimates and
Eq.

23
.
∎

The next result is a superconvergence type estimate. A similar one for a different weight (regularized distance to a point) can be found, e.g., in
(
Leykekhman and Vexler 2016
, Lemma 3)
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
be arbitrary. There is a constant
c>0
independent of
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
h
such that

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}(\operatorname{id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}^{\beta}v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+h\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}\nabla(\operatorname{id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}^{\beta}v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha+\beta-1}v_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

holds for all
v_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}
.

Proof.

For any
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we obtain by a local interpolation estimate and (
22
)

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}(\operatorname{id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}^{\beta}v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}\leq ch^{2}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}\nabla^{2}(\tilde{\rho}^{\beta}v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}.

Using the fact that
v_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}
and thus affine linear on
K
we get

\lvert\nabla^{2}(\tilde{\rho}^{\beta}v_{h})\rvert\leq\lvert\beta(\beta-1)\rvert\tilde{\rho}^{\beta-2}\lvert\nabla\tilde{\rho}\rvert^{2}\lvert v_{h}\rvert+\lvert\beta\rvert\tilde{\rho}^{\beta-1}\lvert\nabla^{2}\tilde{\rho}\rvert v_{h}+2\lvert\beta\rvert\tilde{\rho}^{\beta-1}\lvert\nabla\tilde{\rho}\rvert\lvert\nabla v_{h}\rvert.

By the properties of
\tilde{\rho}
from
Eq.

22
we obtain

\lvert\nabla^{2}(\tilde{\rho}^{\beta}v_{h})\rvert\leq c\tilde{\rho}^{\beta-2}\lvert v_{h}\rvert+c\tilde{\rho}^{\beta-1}\lvert\nabla v_{h}\rvert.

Thus, we have

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}(\operatorname{id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}^{\beta}v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}\leq ch^{2}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha+\beta-2}v_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}+ch^{2}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha+\beta-1}\nabla v_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}.

For the first term we use
\tilde{\rho}\geq\kappa h
from the definition (
21
) of
\tilde{\rho}
and obtain

h^{2}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha+\beta-2}v_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}\leq ch\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha+\beta-1}v_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}.

For the second term we use the weighted inverse estimate from
Lemma

3
. Thus,

h^{2}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha+\beta-1}\nabla v_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}\leq ch\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha+\beta-1}v_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}.

Putting terms together we obtain the desired estimate. The proof for the weighted
H^{1}
seminorm follows the same steps.
∎

Using the above weighted estimates we provide a proof of
Theorem

14
.

Proof.

We define
\tilde{u}
as the harmonic extension of
P_{h}^{\partial}q
, i.e., the solution to

-\upDelta\tilde{u}=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,\quad\tilde{u}=P_{h}^{\partial}q\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

Since
P_{h}^{\partial}q\in V_{h}^{\partial}
and
V_{h}^{\partial}\subset H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
we have
\tilde{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. By
Theorem

5
we get

\lVert\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

The proof now follows three major steps:

1.

In the first step, we show that we can replace
\rho
by
\tilde{\rho}
in the
previous estimate. To this end, we consider the subdomain

\Omega_{h}=\Set{x\in\Omega}{\rho(x)\leq h}.

On this subdomain we get
\tilde{\rho}(x)\leq ch
and therefore

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq ch\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}\leq ch\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq ch\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert^{2}_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2},

where we have used the
H^{1}
estimate for
\tilde{u}
from

Theorem

5
and an inverse estimate on the boundary.
On the compliment
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{h}
we have
h\leq\rho(x)
and therefore
\tilde{\rho}(x)\leq c\rho(x)
. Thus,
Theorem

5
implies

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{h})}^{2}\leq c\lVert\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{h})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq c\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}.

Putting these two estimates together, we obtain

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

(24)

2.

Since
\tilde{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, we are allowed to apply
the Scott-Zhang interpolation operator
\tilde{i}_{h}\colon H^{1}(\Omega)\to\widehat{V}_{h}
, see

Scott and Zhang 1990
. We will next show that the desired estimate holds for
\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u}
. Using the local behavior of the Scott-Zhang
interpolation operator and (
23
), we obtain for every cell
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}\leq c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{K})}+c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{K})},

where
\omega_{K}
is a patch containing
K
. Summing up over all cells in the mesh and using shape regularity, we obtain

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

By boundedness of
\tilde{\rho}
and
Theorem

5
, we get

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

Using this and (
24
), we obtain

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

(25)

3.

For the last step of the proof, we set

w_{h}=\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u}-u_{h}.

It remains to prove an analogous estimate for
\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. By the properties of the Scott-Zhang interpolation we have
w_{h}\in V_{h}
(especially
w_{h}=0
on
\partial\Omega
) and

(\nabla w_{h},\nabla\varphi_{h})=(\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u},\nabla\varphi_{h})\quad\text{for all }\varphi_{h}\in V_{h}

by equation (
18
) for
u_{h}
. There holds

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=(\tilde{\rho}\nabla w_{h},\nabla w_{h})=(\nabla(\tilde{\rho}w_{h}),\nabla w_{h})-(w_{h}\nabla\tilde{\rho},\nabla w_{h})=I_{1}+I_{2}.

Since
\tilde{\rho}w_{h}
vanishes on the boundary
\partial\Omega
(as
w_{h}
), we have
i_{h}(\tilde{\rho}w_{h})\in V_{h}
. Thus,

\begin{split}I_{1}&=(\nabla(\operatorname{id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}w_{h}),\nabla w_{h})+(\nabla i_{h}(\tilde{\rho}w_{h}),\nabla w_{h})\\
&=(\nabla(\operatorname{id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}w_{h}),\nabla w_{h})+(\nabla i_{h}(\tilde{\rho}w_{h}),\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u})\\
&=I_{11}+I_{12}.\end{split}

For
I_{11}
we distribute powers of
\tilde{\rho}
and obtain by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle I_{11}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(\operatorname{id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}w_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(\operatorname{id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}w_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

where in the last step we used the superconvergence estimate from

Lemma

4
. For
I_{12}
, we obtain using
\lvert\nabla\tilde{\rho}\rvert\leq c
from (
22
)

\displaystyle I_{12}

\displaystyle=(\nabla(\tilde{\rho}w_{h}),\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u})+(\nabla(i_{h}-\operatorname{id})(\tilde{\rho}w_{h}),\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u})

\displaystyle=(\tilde{\rho}\nabla w_{h},\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u})+(w_{h}\nabla\tilde{\rho},\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u})+(\nabla(i_{h}-\operatorname{id})(\tilde{\rho}w_{h}),\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u})

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(i_{h}-\operatorname{id})(\tilde{\rho}w_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

where we again used the super convergence estimate from
Lemma

4
.
Similar, we estimate
I_{2}
as

I_{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Putting terms together and absorbing terms involving
\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
into the left-hand side we get

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

The term
\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is estimated in (
25
). It remains to estimate
\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. We use
\tilde{\rho}\geq ch
and obtain

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\tilde{u}-\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\tilde{u}-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

For the interpolation error, we directly get

ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\tilde{u}-\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

where we used again the
H^{1}
estimate for
\tilde{u}
and an
inverse estimate. As
P_{h}^{\partial}(P_{h}^{\partial}q)=P_{h}^{\partial}q
, we obtain from
Theorem

13

ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\tilde{u}-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

Putting terms together yields

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

and therefore

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla w_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

Thus, we have proven

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(u_{h}-\tilde{i}_{h}\tilde{u})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

∎

5
Discretization of the optimal control problem

To discretize the optimal control problem (
13
) we introduce the discrete reduced cost functional

j_{h}\colon Q\to\mathbb{R},\quad j_{h}(q)=J(q,S_{h}q),

where
S_{h}\colon q\mapsto u_{h}(q)
is the discrete solution operator defined by (
18
). We discuss two concepts for control discretization: variational discretization and cellweise linear discretization. For the variational discretization we choose the discrete control space
Q_{h}
as
Q_{h}=Q=L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
, cf.
Hinze 2005
and
Deckelnick et al 2009
in the case of Dirichlet boundary control. For the cellweise linear discretization we choose
Q_{h}=V_{h}^{\partial}
. In both cases we define the discrete admissible set as
{Q_{\mathrm{ad},h}}={Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}\cap Q_{h}
. This leads to the following discretized problem

\text{minimize }j_{h}(q),\quad q\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad},h}}.

(26)

As on the continuous level the functional
j_{h}
is continuous and strictly convex. The existence and uniqueness of a discrete optimal control
\bar{q}_{h}\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad},h}}
follows by standard arguments for both choices of the control discretization. The corresponding discrete state
\bar{u}_{h}=u_{h}(\bar{q}_{h})
is referred as the discrete optimal state. The discrete reduced cost functional is two times continuously differentiable and the directional derivatives at
q\in Q
in the direction
\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\in Q
are given as

j^{\prime}_{h}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=(u_{h}-u_{d},\delta\mspace{-2mu}u_{h})+\alpha(q,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}

(27)

and

j^{\prime\prime}_{h}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\alpha\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2},

(28)

where
u_{h}=S_{h}q
and
\delta\mspace{-2mu}u_{h}=S_{h}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q
. We require also an adjoint representation of
j^{\prime}_{h}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)
. Let
q\in Q
and
u_{h}=S_{h}q
be
the solution of the discrete state equation (
18
). We define
z_{h}
as

z_{h}\in V_{h}\quad:\quad(\nabla\varphi_{h},\nabla z_{h})=(u_{h}-u_{d},\varphi_{h})\quad\text{for all }\varphi_{h}\in V_{h}.

(29)

Similar to the discussion in
Remark

2
we obtain the following representation

j^{\prime}_{h}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=(u_{h}-u_{d},B_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-(\nabla z_{h},\nabla B_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)+\alpha(q,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega},

(30)

where
B_{h}\colon V_{h}^{\partial}\to\widehat{V}_{h}
is an arbitrary discrete extension operator. The above representation suggests the definition of a so called
discrete variational normal trace
of
z_{h}
solving
Eq.

29
. We set

\partial_{n}^{h}z_{h}\in V_{h}^{\partial}\quad:\quad(\partial_{n}^{h}z_{h},\psi_{h})_{\partial\Omega}=(\nabla z_{h},\nabla B_{h}\psi_{h})\\
-(u_{h}-u_{d},B_{h}\psi_{h})\quad\text{for all }\psi_{h}\in V_{h}^{\partial},

(31)

which leads to an equivalent representation

j^{\prime}_{h}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=\alpha(q,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}-(\partial_{n}^{h}z_{h},P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}=(\alpha q-\partial_{n}^{h}z_{h},\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}.

The last step holds due to the definition of the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
projection. The following lemma provides a representation for
j^{\prime}-j^{\prime}_{h}
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
q,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\in Q
, let
u=u(q)
be the solution of the continuous state equation and
u_{h}=u_{h}(q)
. Let
z
be the corresponding continuous adjoint solving (
15
) and
z_{h}
the discrete adjoint (
29
). There holds

j^{\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-j^{\prime}_{h}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=(\partial_{n}z-P_{h}^{\partial}\partial_{n}z,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}\\
+(u-u_{h},S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-(\nabla(z-i_{h}z),\nabla S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q),

where
S_{h}\colon Q\to\widehat{V}_{h}
is the discrete solution operator defined by (
18
) and
i_{h}
is the Lagrange interpolation operator.

Proof.

There holds

j^{\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-j^{\prime}_{h}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=\left(j^{\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q-P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-j^{\prime}_{h}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q-P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)\right)\\
+\left(j^{\prime}(q)(P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-j^{\prime}_{h}(q)(P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)\right)=I_{1}+I_{2}

For
I_{1}
we have by the representations of
j^{\prime}
and
j^{\prime}_{h}

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{n}z-\partial_{n}^{h}z_{h},\delta\mspace{-2mu}q-P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{n}z-P_{h}^{\partial}\partial_{n}z,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q-P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{n}z-P_{h}^{\partial}\partial_{n}z,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega},

where we have used the definition of the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
projection
P_{h}^{\partial}
. For
I_{2}
we use the representation of
j^{\prime}
from
Remark

2
and of
j^{\prime}_{h}
leading to

I_{2}=(u-u_{d},BP_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-(\nabla BP_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q,\nabla z)-(u_{h}-u_{d},B_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)+(\nabla B_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q,\nabla z_{h})

with an arbitrary extension operator
B
and discrete extension operator
B_{h}
. We choose
B_{h}
as discrete harmonic extension, i.e. equal to the discrete solution operator
B_{h}=S_{h}
. Since
B
is applied here only to discrete functions we may choose
B=B_{h}
. This results in

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=(u-u_{h},S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-(\nabla S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q,\nabla(z-z_{h}))

\displaystyle=(u-u_{h},S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-(\nabla S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q,\nabla(z-i_{h}z)),

where in the last step we used the definition of the discrete solution operator
S_{h}
and the fact that
z_{h},i_{h}z\in V_{h}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Next lemma provides an estimate for
j^{\prime}(\bar{q})-j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q})
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
0<s<s_{\Omega}
with
s_{\Omega}
from (
11
). Let
u_{d}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Let
(\bar{q},\bar{u},\bar{z})
be the solution of the optimality system (
17
). Then for all
\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
there holds

\lvert j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q})(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)\rvert\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

with a constant
c>0
independent of
h
,
u_{d}
and
\delta\mspace{-2mu}q
.

Proof.

By the representation from
Lemma

5
we obtain

j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q})(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=(\partial_{n}\bar{z}-P_{h}^{\partial}\partial_{n}\bar{z},\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}\\
+(\bar{u}-\tilde{u}_{h},S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-(\nabla(\bar{z}-i_{h}\bar{z}),\nabla S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q),

where
\tilde{u}_{h}=S_{h}\bar{q}
is the discrete solution to the continuous optimal control.
By
Theorem

11
we have

\bar{z}\in W^{2,2}_{-s}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{n}\bar{z},\bar{q}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+s}(\partial\Omega)

and the corresponding estimates

\lVert\bar{z}\rVert_{W^{2,2}_{-s}(\Omega)}+\lVert\partial_{n}\bar{z}\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+s}(\partial\Omega)}+\lVert\bar{q}\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+s}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

hold. The first term in the above representation of
j^{\prime}(\bar{q})-j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q})
is directly estimated by standard estimates for the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
projection as

\displaystyle\lvert(\partial_{n}\bar{z}-P_{h}^{\partial}\partial_{n}\bar{z},\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\lVert\partial_{n}\bar{z}\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+s}(\partial\Omega)}\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

The term
(\bar{u}-\tilde{u}_{h},S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)
is estimated by
Theorem

13
and by the stability of
S_{h}
as

\displaystyle\lvert(\bar{u}-\tilde{u}_{h},S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\bar{u}-\tilde{u}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch\lVert\bar{q}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

For the term involving
\bar{z}-i_{h}\bar{z}
we use the weight
\tilde{\rho}
, see (
21
), and obtain

\displaystyle\lvert(\nabla(\bar{z}-i_{h}\bar{z}),\nabla S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert(\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(\bar{z}-i_{h}\bar{z}),\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(\bar{z}-i_{h}\bar{z})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The weighted interpolation error is estimated by
Lemma

2

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(\bar{z}-i_{h}\bar{z})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla^{2}\bar{z}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}+s}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-s}\nabla^{2}\bar{z}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch(\kappa h)^{-\frac{1}{2}+s}\lVert\rho^{-s}\nabla^{2}\bar{z}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\lVert\bar{z}\rVert_{W^{2,2}_{-s}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

where we also used
\tilde{\rho}\geq\kappa h
,
s<\frac{1}{2}
and
\tilde{\rho}\geq\rho
.
Using
Theorem

14
and the stability of the
P_{h}^{\partial}
in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
we get

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

resulting in

\lvert(\nabla(\bar{z}-i_{h}\bar{z}),\nabla S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)\rvert\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Under the assumption on
\Omega
that
\lambda_{\Omega}>\frac{3}{2}
we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 15
.

Let the critical exponent
\lambda_{\Omega}
from (
5
) fulfill
\lambda_{\Omega}>\frac{3}{2}
. Then there holds in the setting of
Lemma

6

\lvert j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q})(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)\rvert\leq ch\lvert\ln h\rvert\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

Proof.

We start again by the representation from
Lemma

5

j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q})(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=(\partial_{n}\bar{z}-P_{h}^{\partial}\partial_{n}\bar{z},\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}\\
+(\bar{u}-\tilde{u}_{h},S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)-(\nabla(\bar{z}-i_{h}\bar{z}),\nabla S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q).

From
Theorem

12
we have (since
\lambda_{\Omega}>\frac{3}{2}
)

\lVert\bar{z}\rVert_{W^{2,2}_{-s}}(\Omega)\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

for all
s<\frac{1}{2}
as well as

\lVert\partial_{n}\bar{z}\rVert_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}+\lVert\bar{q}\rVert_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Thus, the first term in the above representation can be estimated as

(\partial_{n}\bar{z}-P_{h}^{\partial}\partial_{n}\bar{z},\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}\leq ch\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\lVert\partial_{n}\bar{z}\rVert_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

The second term is estimated as in the previous theorem leading to

\lvert(\bar{u}-\tilde{u}_{h},S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)\rvert\leq ch\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

For the third term we get following the arguments in the proof of the previous theorem

\lvert(\nabla(\bar{z}-i_{h}\bar{z}),\nabla S_{h}P_{h}^{\partial}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)\rvert\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}h^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

for every
s<\frac{1}{2}
. The choice
s=\frac{1}{2}-\lvert\ln h\rvert^{-1}
provides the desired estimate by observing that

1-2s=\lvert\ln h\rvert^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad h^{-\lvert\ln h\rvert^{-1}}=h^{(\ln h)^{-1}}=\mathrm{e}^{\ln h(\ln h)^{-1}}=\mathrm{e}.

∎

5.1
Variational discretization

In this section we consider the choice
Q_{h}=Q=L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
and correspondingly
{Q_{\mathrm{ad},h}}={Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}
in (
26
). In this case the discrete optimal control
\bar{q}_{h}
is characterized by

\bar{q}_{h}\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}\quad:\quad j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q}_{h})(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q-\bar{q}_{h})\geq 0\quad\text{for all }\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}.

The next theorem provides an error estimate for this choice of discretization concept.

Theorem 16
.

Let
\bar{q}
be the solution of (
13
) and
\bar{q}_{h}
the solution of (
26
) for the choice
Q_{h}=Q
. Let
0<s<s_{\Omega}
with
s_{\Omega}
from (
11
) and
u_{d}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Then, there holds

\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

with a constant
c>0
independent of
h
and
u_{d}
. If
\Omega
fulfills
\lambda_{\Omega}>\frac{3}{2}
then there holds

\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch\lvert\ln h\rvert\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

From the representation (
28
) of
j^{\prime\prime}_{h}
we obtain

\alpha\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}\leq j^{\prime\prime}_{h}(\bar{q})(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h},\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})=j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q})(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})-j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q}_{h})(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})

by affine linearity of
j^{\prime}_{h}(q)
with respect to
q
. By the fact that
\bar{q}_{h}
is admissible for the continuous problem and
\bar{q}
is admissible for the discrete we obtain by both (continuous and discrete) optimality conditions

-j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q}_{h})(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})\leq 0\leq-j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}).

This results in

\alpha\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}\leq j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q})(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})-j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}).

An application of
Lemma

6
and
Corollary

15
leads to the desired estimates.
∎

5.2
Cellwise linear discretization

In this section we consider the choice
Q_{h}=V_{h}^{\partial}
and correspondingly
{Q_{\mathrm{ad},h}}={Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}\cap V_{h}^{\partial}
in (
26
). In this case the discrete optimal control
\bar{q}_{h}
is characterized by

\bar{q}_{h}\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad},h}}\quad:\quad j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q}_{h})(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q_{h}-\bar{q}_{h})\geq 0\quad\text{for all }\delta\mspace{-2mu}q_{h}\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad},h}}.

To proceed we require an interpolation operator on
Q
, which preserves admissibility and have improved approximation property in some negative norms. The
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
projection on
V_{h}^{\partial}
can not be used, since it does not preserve the admissibility of functions from
{Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}
. We consider the quasi-interpolation
\pi_{h}\colon L^{1}(\partial\Omega)\to V_{h}^{\partial}
as introduced in
Carstensen 1999
. In
Carstensen 1999
this operator is defined on the domain and we use it on the boundary, but the definition (and the properties) are very similar. We consider the set
{\cal N}_{h}^{\partial}
of all nodes of the the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
on the boundary
\partial\Omega
. For every node
y\in{\cal N}_{h}^{\partial}
let
\varphi_{y}\in V_{h}^{\partial}
be the local (nodal) basis function (hat-function) on the boundary. The operator
\pi_{h}
is defined as

\pi_{h}q=\sum_{y\in{\cal N}_{h}^{\partial}}\frac{(q,\varphi_{y})_{\partial\Omega}}{(1,\varphi_{y})_{\partial\Omega}}\varphi_{y}.

The definition directly implies that for every
q\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}
we have
\pi_{h}q\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad},h}}
(note that
q_{a}
and
q_{b}
are constants).

Lemma 7
.

Let
q\in H^{t}(\partial\Omega)
for some
0\leq t\leq 1
. Then there holds

\lVert q-\pi_{h}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{t}\lVert q\rVert_{H^{t}(\partial\Omega)}

and

\lVert q-\pi_{h}q\rVert_{H^{-t}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{2t}\lVert q\rVert_{H^{t}(\partial\Omega)}

with a constant
c>0
independent of
h
and
q
.

Proof.

For the case
t=1
the proof of the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
estimate follows as in
(
Carstensen 1999
, Theorem 3.1)
. The negative norm estimate is shown as in
(
de los Reyes et al 2008
, Lemma 4.5)
. The general case of the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
estimate is then obtained by interpolation. See also
(
Apel et al 2016
, Lemma 2.14)
for more details.
∎

The next theorem provides an error estimate for this choice of discretization concept.

Theorem 17
.

Let
\bar{q}
be the solution of (
13
) and
\bar{q}_{h}
the solution of (
26
) for the choice
Q_{h}=V_{h}^{\partial}
. Let
0<s<s_{\Omega}
with
s_{\Omega}
from (
11
) and
u_{d}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Then, there holds

\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

with a constant
c>0
independent of
h
and
u_{d}
. If
\Omega
fulfills
\lambda_{\Omega}>\frac{3}{2}
then there holds

\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch\lvert\ln h\rvert\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

We split the error as

\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\lVert\bar{q}-\pi_{h}\bar{q}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\lVert\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

We start with considering the general case
0<s<s_{\Omega}
and comment on the second case later on. The estimate for
\bar{q}-\pi_{h}\bar{q}
follows from
Lemma

7
using the regularity
\bar{q}\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+s}(\partial\Omega)
from
Theorem

11
leading to

\lVert\bar{q}-\pi_{h}\bar{q}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\lVert\bar{q}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+s}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

As in the proof of
Theorem

16
we use the coercivity of
j^{\prime\prime}_{h}
to get

\alpha\lVert\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}\leq j^{\prime\prime}_{h}(\bar{q})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h},\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})=j^{\prime}_{h}(\pi_{h}\bar{q})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})-j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q}_{h})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}).

Using the fact that
\pi_{h}\bar{q}\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad},h}}
and
\bar{q}_{h}\in{Q_{\mathrm{ad}}}
we can exploit both (continuous and discrete) optimality conditions

-j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q}_{h})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})\leq 0\leq-j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}).

This results in

\displaystyle\alpha\lVert\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq j^{\prime}_{h}(\pi_{h}\bar{q})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})-j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}

with

I_{1}=j^{\prime}_{h}(\pi_{h}\bar{q})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})-j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}),

I_{2}=j^{\prime}_{h}(\bar{q})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})-j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})

and

I_{3}=j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})-j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})=j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}).

For
I_{1}
we get using the primal representation of
j^{\prime}_{h}
from (
27
)

I_{1}=(S_{h}(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}),S_{h}(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}))+\alpha(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q},\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})_{\partial\Omega},

which results by the stability of
S_{h}:L^{2}(\partial\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
and Young’s inequality in

\lvert I_{1}\rvert\leq c\lVert\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\lVert\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\frac{\alpha}{4}\lVert\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}+c\lVert\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}.

The term
I_{2}
is estimated by
Lemma

6
employing the regularity results from
Theorem

11
leading to

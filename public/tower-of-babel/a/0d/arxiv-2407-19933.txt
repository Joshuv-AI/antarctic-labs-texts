The Hadamard Product of Moment Sequences, Diagonal Positivity Preservers, and their Generators
Philipp J. di Dio and Lars-Luca Langer

Abstract
In this work we investigate special aspects of positivity preservers and especially diagonal positivity preservers, i.e., linear maps T : ℝ ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] → ℝ ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}] such that T ​ x α = t α ​ x α Tx^{\alpha}=t_{\alpha}x^{\alpha} holds for all α ∈ ℕ 0 n \alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n} with t α ∈ ℝ t_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R} and T ​ p ≥ 0 Tp\geq 0 on ℝ n \mathds{R}^{n} for all p ∈ ℝ ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}] with p ≥ 0 p\geq 0 on ℝ n \mathds{R}^{n} . We discuss representations of T T , give characterizations of diagonal positivity preservers, and compare these to previous (partial) results in the literature. On the side we get a full characterization of linear maps preserving moment sequences and a new proof of Schur’s product formula. The tool of diagonal positivity preservers simplifies several other existing proofs in the literature. We give a full characterization of generators A A of diagonal positivity preservers, i.e., e t ​ A e^{tA} is a diagonal positivity preserver for all t ≥ 0 t\geq 0 . We give the connection of these generators to infinitely divisible moment sequences.

(understood as a multivariate power) this representation is similar to the one used by Berg
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
and Tyan
[
48
]
to characterize infinitely
\odot
-divisible moment sequences.
This theorem instead characterizes probability measures, meaning the moments of these measures do not need to exist.
For the diagonal operator case one would want to use this Lévy-Khinchine representation and the technique used in the proof of

.
But this requires additional assumptions such that the coefficients of the generator exist.

3
Characterization of all linear Maps
S:\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\to\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
preserving
K
-Moment Sequences

Linear maps preserving moment sequences have been characterized before
[
14
]
.
But these are
\mathds{R}^{n}
-moment sequences.
A characterization of
K
-moment sequence preserving linear maps for general closed
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
with
n\in\mathds{N}
was open so far.
We use the results from
[
17
]
to solve this general characterization.

Recall, that the set of sequences

\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}:=\{s=(s_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\,|\,s_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}\ \text{for all}\ \alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}\}

is a
Fréchet space
when equipped with the family of semi-norms

|s|_{d}:=\max_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}:|\alpha|\leq d}|s_{\alpha}|,

i.e., it is metrizable, complete, and locally convex.
Completeness in
\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
is simply the following.
A sequences
s^{(i)}=(s_{\alpha}^{(i)})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
converges to a sequence
s
, i.e.,
s^{(i)}\xrightarrow{i\to\infty}s
, if and only if
s_{\alpha}^{(i)}\xrightarrow{i\to\infty}s_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
See
[
47
, pp. 91–92, Exm. III]
since
\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\cong\mathds{R}[[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]]
.

The set of sequences
\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
has a topological dual.
It is the set of polynomials
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
equipped with the LF-topology, see
[
47
, Ch. 22]
.
Note, any LF-space is complete
[
47
, Thm. 13.1]
and hence the sequence of polynomials
p^{(i)}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}:|\alpha|\leq d_{i}}c^{(i)}_{\alpha}\cdot x^{\alpha}
,
d_{i}\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, converges to a polynomial
p=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}:|\alpha|\leq d}c_{\alpha}\cdot x^{\alpha}
in the LF-topology, i.e.,
p^{(i)}\xrightarrow{i\to\infty}p
, if and only if
\sup_{i\in\mathds{N}}d_{i}=d<\infty
and
c^{(i)}_{\alpha}\xrightarrow{i\to\infty}c_{\alpha}
.

The duality between
\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
and
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
can be expressed in a natural way by

\langle s,p\rangle:=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}s_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}

and
\langle s,\,\cdot\,\rangle:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}
is the well-known Riesz functional
L_{s}=\langle s,\,\cdot\,\rangle
.
For more on topological vector spaces see e.g.
[
47
,
29
,
30
,
46
]
.

Since every linear operator
T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is given by
Lemma

2.2
with unique
q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
we have that the dual operator

T^{*}:\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\to\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}

defined by

\langle T^{*}s,p\rangle:=\langle s,Tp\rangle

for all
s\in\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
and
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is given by

T^{*}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}(-1)^{|\alpha|}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}M_{q_{\alpha}}

(5)

where
M_{q_{\alpha}}
is the shift operator given by
M_{x^{\beta}}s:=(s_{\alpha+\beta})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
and
M_{af+g}=aM_{f}+M_{g}
for all
a\in\mathds{R}
and
f,g\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
The
\partial^{\alpha}
on
\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
are defined in the distributional sense as in
[
15
]
, see e.g.
[
18
]
for more on distributions.
Hence, every linear operator
S:\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\to\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
is given by

S=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}(-1)^{|\alpha|}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}M_{q_{\alpha}}

(6)

with unique
q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
since
T^{**}=T
we have
S^{*}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
linear.
Additionally,
(Ss)_{\alpha}=\langle Ss,x^{\alpha}\rangle=\langle s,S^{*}p\rangle
with
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
shows that each coordinate of
Ss
is a finite linear combination of the coordinates
s_{\alpha}
of
s
.
We summarize that in the following.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

S:\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\to\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
is linear.

(ii)

S=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}(-1)^{|\alpha|}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}M_{q_{\alpha}}
with unique
q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.

The coordinates
(Ss)_{\alpha}
of
Ss
are a finite linear combination of coordinates of
s
.

With these remarks and
[
17
, Thm. 4.5]
we get the following full characterization of linear operators
S:\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\to\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
which preserve
K
-moment sequences.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
, let
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed, and let
S:\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\to\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
be linear, i.e.,

S=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}(-1)^{|\alpha|}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}M_{q_{\alpha}}

for some
q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The linear operator
S
preserves
K
-moment sequences.

(ii)

For all
y\in K
we have that
(\alpha!\cdot q_{\alpha}(y))_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
is a
(K-y)
-moment sequence.

Proof.

By

\langle Ss,p\rangle=\langle s,S^{*}p\rangle

for all
s\in\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
and
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
we have that
S
preserves
K
-moment sequences if and only if
S^{*}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
preserves
K
-non-negative polynomials.
Linear maps
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
preserving
K
-non-negative polynomials have been fully characterized by
[
17
, Thm. 4.5]
which gives the equivalence to (ii).
∎

From the previous we deduce the following results.
In
Theorem

3.14
we already proved the following.

.

Let

, let

be closed, and let

preservers
K
-moment sequences iff

K
-positive polynomials.

.

Let

, let

By

4
Diagonal Operators
T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

We now turn to our main topic: diagonal operators.
In
Tx^{\alpha}=t_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
the sequences
t=(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
is called the

of

.
The following is known.
We give a shorter proof as in
[
10
]
.

Theorem 4.17

(
[
10
, Cor. 4.3]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and let
T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a diagonal operator with diagonal sequence
t=(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

T\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})
.

(ii)

t
is a moment sequences.

If
\mu
is a representing measure of
t
then
T
fulfills

(Tp)(x)=\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}p(x_{1}y_{1},\dots,x_{n}y_{n})\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})

(7)

for all
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

Proof.

While (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) is clear from (
7
) the reverse follows from
L(p):=(Tp)(1,\dots,1)\geq 0
for all
p\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})
and
L(x^{\alpha})=t_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
with Haviland’s Theorem
[
20
]
.
∎

Remark 4.18
.

Diagonal operators
T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
have three representations:

a)

Tx^{\alpha}=t_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}
,

b)

\displaystyle T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\cdot x^{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}
, and

c)

\displaystyle T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{d_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\cdot(x\partial)^{\alpha}
.

Any of these sequences
(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
,
(c_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
, or
(d_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
determine
T
uniquely.
We switch between these representations, mostly between (a) and (b).
We therefore give here the formulas transforming one into the others:

\displaystyle t_{\alpha}\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{\beta\preceq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}\cdot c_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{\alpha^{\beta}}{\beta!}\cdot d_{\beta},

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{\beta\preceq\alpha}(-1)^{|\alpha-\beta|}\cdot\binom{\alpha}{\beta}\cdot t_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{\beta\succeq\alpha}\frac{\alpha!}{\beta!}\cdot\begin{Bmatrix}\alpha\\
\beta\end{Bmatrix}\cdot d_{\beta}

\displaystyle d_{\alpha}\quad

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{\beta\succeq\alpha}\sum_{\gamma\preceq\beta}(-1)^{|\alpha+\gamma|}\frac{\alpha!}{\beta!}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}\beta\\
\alpha\end{bmatrix}\cdot\binom{\beta}{\gamma}\cdot t_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{\beta\succeq\alpha}(-1)^{|\beta-\alpha|}\frac{\alpha!}{\beta!}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}\beta\\
\alpha\end{bmatrix}\cdot c_{\beta}

These relations follow by direct computations, e.g. with the relations

x^{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\preceq\alpha}(-1)^{|\alpha-\beta|}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}\alpha\\
\beta\end{bmatrix}\cdot(x\partial)^{\beta}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(x\partial)^{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\preceq\alpha}\begin{Bmatrix}\alpha\\
\beta\end{Bmatrix}\cdot x^{\beta}\cdot\partial^{\beta}

where
\begin{bmatrix}\alpha\\
\beta\end{bmatrix}
are the (
unsigned
)
Stirling numbers of the first kind
and
\begin{Bmatrix}\alpha\\
\beta\end{Bmatrix}
are the
Stirling numbers of the second kind
, see e.g.
[
33
]
.

\circ

It is clear that if
(c_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
has only finitely many non-zero entries then only finitely many entries in
(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
are zero.
Also the reverse can hold, i.e., finitely many entries in
(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
are non-zero but only finitely many entries in
(c_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
are zero.

Example 4.19
.

Define the evaluation operator

T_{0}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}],\quad p\mapsto T_{0}p=p(0),

i.e.,
T_{0}x^{\alpha}=\delta_{\alpha,0}x^{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Then
T_{0}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{(-1)^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}\cdot x^{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}
holds.

\circ

Example 4.20
.

For the identity
\mathrm{id}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
defined by
\mathrm{id}\,p:=p
we have
\mathrm{id}=1=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{\delta_{\alpha,0}}{\alpha!}\cdot x^{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}
.

\circ

Every diagonal operator
T
has a representation (a) and (b).
But it does not necessarily have a representation (c) as the next example shows.

Example 4.21

(
Example

4.19
continued)
.

The evaluation operator

T_{0}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{(-1)^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}\cdot x^{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}

diverges for all
\alpha\neq 0
.

\circ

In
Remark

4.18
we have seen that if
t=(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
is represented by
\mu_{t}
then

is represented by
\mu_{c}:=\mu_{t}(\,\cdot\,+\mathds{1})
and hence

, see
[
10
, p. 79]
.
A similar statement holds for the coefficients

in the type (c) representation.

Proposition 4.22
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and let
T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a diagonal operator.
Then the following holds:

(i)

If there exists a representing measure
\mu_{t}
on
(0,\infty)^{n}
of the diagonal sequence
t=(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
with

d_{\alpha}:=\int_{(0,\infty)^{n}}(\ln x)^{\alpha}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)\in(-\infty,\infty)

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
where
\ln x=(\ln x_{1},\dots,\ln x_{n})
then

T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{d_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\cdot(x\partial)^{\alpha}.

(ii)

If there exists a representing measure
\mu_{d}
on
\mathds{R}^{n}
of the
(x\partial)^{\alpha}
-coefficient sequence
d=(d_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
with

t_{\alpha}:=\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}e^{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{d}(x)\in(-\infty,\infty)

for all

then

Tx^{\alpha}=t_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.

Proof.

(i):
Looking at the diagonal values we obtain:

\displaystyle t_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,\infty)^{n}}x^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,\infty)^{n}}e^{\langle\ln x,\beta\rangle}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,\infty)^{n}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{\beta^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\cdot(\ln x)^{\alpha}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)

5
The Product of Diagonal Positivity Preservers

The product of moment sequences has been investigated before.
We already mentioned the work
[
11
]
where the multidimensional case is treated.
Other works treat only the univariate case which is easily solved by the
Schur Product Theorem

2.1
.
In the multidimensional case the
Schur Product Theorem

2.1
is not even applicable but further technical approximations of the moment sequence are required.
We show here that the

2.1
is not necessary.
We use only the diagonal operators and show that with these the problem becomes trivial.
We define the following.

Definition 5.23
.

Let

and let
s=(s_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}

This is the
Hadamard product
of (moment) sequences.

Theorem

4.17
immediately implies the following.

Theorem 5.24
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and let

and

is a moment sequence with representing measure

.

Proof.

Follows immediately from Definitions

and
5.23
and
Theorem

4.17
.
∎

We have the following easy consequence of the previous theorem.

.

Let

and let

If

We have seen in the previous section that the

(i)

The

2.1
has several proofs.
By the classical result that

[

.

2.1

We have that

Example 7.33

8
Diagonal Operators and their Generators

Recall the definitions

\mathfrak{D}:=\left\{T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}\;\middle|\;q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq|\alpha|},\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n},\ \text{and}\ \ker T=\{0\}\right\}

and

\mathfrak{d}:=\left\{\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}\;\middle|\;q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq|\alpha|},\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}\right\}.

from
[
17
, Def. 1.1]
.
For diagonal operators we therefore define the following.

Definition 8.35
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
.
We define subsets
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime},\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathds{R}[[x_{1}\partial_{1},\dots,x_{n}\partial_{n}]]
by

\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}:=\left\{\begin{gathered}T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\\
\text{linear operator}\end{gathered}\ \middle|\ \begin{gathered}Tx^{\alpha}=t_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}\ \text{with}\\
t_{\alpha}>0\ \text{for all}\ \alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}\end{gathered}\right\}

and

\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}:=\left\{\begin{gathered}A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\\
\text{linear operator}\end{gathered}\ \middle|\ \begin{gathered}Ax^{\alpha}=a_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}\ \text{with}\\
a_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}\ \text{for all}\ \alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}\end{gathered}\right\}.

Furthermore, we define the set of
diagonal positivity preservers
with strictly positive eigenvalues as

\mathfrak{D}_{+}^{\prime}:=\left\{T\in\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}\ \middle|\ T\text{ is a positivity preserver}\right\}

and their
generators
as

\mathfrak{d}_{+}^{\prime}:=\left\{A\in\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}\ \middle|\ e^{tA}\in\mathfrak{D}_{+}^{\prime}\text{ for all }t\geq 0\right\}.

Recall that
(\mathfrak{D},\,\cdot\,)
is a regular Fréchet Lie group with Fréchet Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{d},\,\cdot\,,+)
, see
[
17
, Thm. 3.7]
.
For the definition of (regular) Fréchet Lie group see
[
35
, p. 63, Dfn. 1.1]
or e.g.
[
17
, Dfn. 2.10]
.

Theorem 8.36
.

(\mathfrak{D}^{\prime},\,\cdot\,)
is a commutative regular Fréchet Lie sub-group of
(\mathfrak{D},\,\cdot\,)
with the Fréchet Lie sub-algebra
(\mathfrak{d}^{\prime},\,\cdot\,,+)
of
(\mathfrak{d},\,\cdot\,,+)
.
The exponential map

\exp:\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{D}^{\prime},\quad A\mapsto\exp A:=\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\frac{A^{k}}{k!}

is smooth, bijective, and fulfills

(\exp A)x^{\alpha}=e^{a_{\alpha}}x^{\alpha}

for all
A\in\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
, i.e.,
Ax^{\alpha}=a_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Its inverse
\log:\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
is smooth and explicitly given by

(\log T)x^{\alpha}=\ln t_{\alpha}\cdot x^{\alpha}

for all
T\in\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
, i.e.,
Tx^{\alpha}=t_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}\subsetneq\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}\subsetneq\mathfrak{d}
are commutative and closed in the Fréchet topology.
Hence,
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{d}
is closed with respect to the Lie bracket.
Since every diagonal operator
T
resp.
A
is uniquely determined by their diagonal sequence
(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
resp.
(a_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
and

\exp:\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
is smooth with smooth explicit inverse
\log:\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
.
∎

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle c_{e_{i}}

A=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\cdot x^{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{d}_{+}.\qed

Since we characterized positivity preserving diagonal operators and their generators we can now apply these results to moment sequences.
The following was already defined by Tyan

Definition 8.38
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and

be a moment sequence with

for all

if
t^{c}:=(t_{\alpha}^{c})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
is a moment sequence for all

.

Infinitely devisible moment sequences and diagonal operators are connected through the following.

Lemma 8.39
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and let

be a moment sequence with

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Let

be the linear operator defined by

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The moment sequence
t
is infinitely divisible with respect to
\odot
.

(ii)

A\in\mathfrak{d}_{+}^{\prime}
, i.e.

is a positivity preserver for all

.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii):
Using the exponential map from
Theorem

8.36
it follows that

\exp(cA)x^{\alpha}=e^{c\ln t_{\alpha}}x^{\alpha}=t_{\alpha}^{c}x^{\alpha}

holds for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
and
c\geq 0
.
Since
t^{c}
is a moment sequence for all
c\geq 0
and using
Theorem

4.17
it follows that
\exp(cA)
is a positivity preserver for all
c\geq 0
, i.e.,
A\in\mathfrak{d}_{+}^{\prime}
by

.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i):
Similarly, since
\exp(cA)
is a positivity preserver for all

, using
Theorem

4.17
gives a representing measure
\mu_{c}
of

for each

.
Hence,
t^{c}
is a moment sequence for all

and

is infinitely divisible.
∎

We can now use the connection between infinitely divisible moment sequences and diagonal positivity preservers to characterize infinitely divisible moment sequences without the Lévi–Khinchin formula for
\odot
-divisible measures.

Theorem 8.40
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and let
t=(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
be a moment sequence.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The moment sequence
t
is infinitely divisible with respect to
\odot
.

(ii)

There exist a constant
c_{0}\in\mathds{R}
, a vector
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})^{T}\in\mathds{R}^{n}
, a real symmetric positive semi-definite matrix
\Sigma=(\sigma_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{n}\succeq 0
, and a
\sigma
-finite measure
\nu
on
\mathds{R}^{n}
with

\nu(\{0\})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}|\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu(x)<\infty

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
with
|\alpha|\geq 2
such that

t_{\alpha}=e^{C_{\alpha}+I_{\alpha}}

holds with

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=c_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}b_{i}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\sigma_{i,j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(\alpha_{i}-1)\sigma_{i,i}

(10)

and

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}(x+\mathds{1})^{\alpha}-1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\cdot x_{i}\cdot\chi_{\{\|x\|_{2}<1\}}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu(x)

(11)

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.

Proof.

By
Theorem

8.37
and
Lemma

8.39
we have that (i) is equivalent to have an operator
A\in\mathfrak{d}_{+}^{\prime}
with

Ax^{\alpha}=\ln t_{\alpha}\cdot x^{\alpha}

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
, a constant
c_{0}\in\mathds{R}
, a vector
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})^{T}\in\mathds{R}^{n}
, a real symmetric positive semi-definite matrix
\Sigma=(\sigma_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{n}\succeq 0
, and a
\sigma
-finite measure
\nu
on
\mathds{R}^{n}
with

\nu(\{0\})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}|\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu(x)<\infty

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
with
|\alpha|\geq 2
such that

A=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\cdot x^{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}

with

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle\in\mathds{R},

\displaystyle c_{e_{i}}

\displaystyle=b_{i}+\int_{\|x\|_{2}\geq 1}x_{i}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu(x)

\displaystyle\text{for all}\ i=1,\dots,n,

\displaystyle c_{e_{i}+e_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i,j}+\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}x^{e_{i}+e_{j}}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu(x)

\displaystyle\text{for all}\ i,j=1,\dots,n,

and

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu(x)

\displaystyle\text{for all}\ \alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}\ \text{with}\ |\alpha|\geq 3

holds.
By
Remark

4.18
we have

\displaystyle\ln t_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\preceq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}\cdot c_{\beta}

\displaystyle=c_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}b_{i}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\sigma_{i,j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(\alpha_{i}-1)\sigma_{i,i}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\|x\|_{2}\geq 1}\alpha_{i}x_{i}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu(x)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\preceq\alpha\\
|\beta|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}x^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu(x)

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}

|\beta|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}x^{\beta}

\displaystyle\ln t_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}+\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}(x+\mathds{1})^{\alpha}-1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\cdot x_{i}\cdot(\chi_{\{\|x\|_{2}\geq 1\}}(x)-1)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu(x)

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}+\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}(x+\mathds{1})^{\alpha}-1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\cdot x_{i}\cdot\chi_{\{\|x\|_{2}<1\}}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu(x)

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}

9
Summary

In this work we continue our studies about positivity preservers
[
16
,
17
]
and we focus here on diagonal operators since they possess properties the general positivity preservers do not possess.
At first we characterize in
Theorem

3.14
all linear maps
S:\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\to\mathds{R}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
which preserve
K
-moment sequences and hence we generalize
[
14
]
.

In
Section

4
we start our investigation of diagonal operators by looking at different types of representation of diagonal operators, i.e., we have the three representations

(\text{a})\ Tx^{\alpha}=t_{\alpha}x^{\alpha},\quad(\text{b})\ T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\cdot x^{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha},\quad\text{and}\quad(\text{c})\ T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{d_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\cdot(x\partial)^{\alpha}

and the sequences
(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
,
(c_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
, and
(d_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
transform into each other by binomial coefficients and Stirling numbers, see
Remark

4.18
.
Diagonal operators always have a representation (a) and (b).
In
Example

4.21
we give a diagonal positivity preserver which can not be represented by type (c).

In
Section

5
we see that the Hadamard product
s\odot t=(s_{\alpha}\cdot t_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
of two sequences corresponds to the product
ST
of two diagonal operators and the multiplicative convolution
\mu\odot\nu
of their representing measures.
This observation simplifies several previous proofs, e.g. by removing the Schur product arguments in
[
7
]
and
[
11
]
.
In fact, the simplification provides us with another proof of the
Schur Product Theorem

2.1
in
Section

6
.

In
Section

7
we deal with
\odot
-infinitely divisible measures.
These properties are used in
Section

8
to prove
Theorem

8.37
: the full characterization of generators
A
of diagonal positivity preservers, i.e.,
e^{tA}
is a diagonal positivity preserver for all
t\geq 0
.
This characterization is used to give an alternative (and shorter) approach to characterize infinitely divisible moment sequences
[
22
,
23
,
48
]
and reveals the connection between infinitely divisible moment sequences and the generators of diagonal positivity preservers.

Determining a Points Configuration on the Line from a Subset of the Pairwise Distances
Itai Benjamini, Elad Tzalik

Abstract
We investigate rigidity-type problems on the real line and the circle in the non-generic setting. Specifically, we consider the problem of uniquely determining the positions of n n distinct points V = v 1 , … , v n V={v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}} given a set of mutual distances 𝒫 ⊆ ( V 2 ) \mathcal{P}\subseteq{V\choose 2} . We establish an extremal result: if | 𝒫 | = Ω ⁡ ( n 3 / 2 ) |\mathcal{P}|=\Omega(n^{3/2}) , then the positions of a large subset V ′ ⊆ V V^{\prime}\subseteq V , where large means | V ′ | = Ω ⁡ ( | 𝒫 | n ) |V^{\prime}|=\Omega(\frac{|\mathcal{P}|}{n}) , can be uniquely determined up to isometry. As a main ingredient in the proof, which may be of independent interest, we show that dense graphs G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) for which every two non-adjacent vertices have only a few common neighbours must have large cliques. Furthermore, we examine the problem of reconstructing V V from a random distance set 𝒫 \mathcal{P} . We establish that if the distance between each pair of points is known independently with probability p = C ​ ln ⁡ ( n ) n p=\frac{C\ln(n)}{n} for some universal constant C > 0 C>0 , then V V can be reconstructed from the distances with high probability. We provide a randomized algorithm with linear expected running time that returns the correct embedding of V V to the line with high probability. Since we posted a preliminary version of the paper on arxiv, follow-up works have improved upon our results in the random setting. Girão, Illingworth, Michel, Powierski, and Scott proved a hitting time result for the first moment at which an time at which one can reconstruct V V when 𝒫 \mathcal{P} is revealed using the Erdős–Rényi evolution, our extremal result lies in the heart of their argument. Montgomery, Nenadov and Szabó resolved a conjecture we posed in a previous version and proved that w.h.p a graph sampled from the Erdős–Rényi evolution becomes globally rigid in ℝ \mathbb{R} at the moment it’s minimum degree is 2 2 .

In

7

We study rigidity

A graph

To conclude the preceding discussion, we emphasize that although there exists a significant amount of research on globally rigid graphs, it focuses to the generic setting, where the embedding function

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[X_{i+1}>\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{c}{4}\right)X_{i}]\geq\frac{1}{2}

(6)

To show this implies that for suitable
\alpha,\beta
one has:
X_{\beta\cdot\ln(n)}\geq n^{\alpha}
, define:

Y_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}n^{\alpha},&\text{if }i=1\\
\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{c}{4}\right)^{-1}Y_{i-1},&\text{if }X_{i}>\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{c}{4}\right)X_{i-1}\\
Y_{i-1},&\text{ otherwise}\end{array}\right\}

Notice that
\mathbb{P}[X_{i}\leq n^{\alpha}]\leq\mathbb{P}[Y_{i}\geq 1]
since each multiplicative decrease of
Y
is complemented by a multiplicative increase in
X
by at least the same constant. Also, note that
\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]=n^{\alpha}
. By combining
Equation

6
with the law of total expectation this implies that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y_{k}]\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{c}{4}\right)\right)^{-1}\mathbb{E}[Y_{k-1}]

Therefore by setting
k=\beta\cdot ln(n)
and
\gamma=\alpha-\beta\ln\left(\frac{7}{8}+\frac{c}{8}\right)
we get that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y_{\beta\cdot ln(n)}]\leq\left(\frac{7}{8}+\frac{c}{8}\right)^{-\beta\cdot ln(n)}n^{\alpha}\leq exp\left(\gamma\ln(n)\right)=n^{\gamma}

(7)

Notice that for any fixed
c,\beta
for small enough
\alpha
we have
\gamma<0
thus the expectation is polynomially small and by Markov’s inequality:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[Y_{\beta\cdot ln(n)}\geq 1]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[Y_{\beta\cdot ln(n)}]}{1}\leq n^{\gamma}

(8)

Since
\mathbb{P}[X_{i}\leq n^{\alpha}]\leq\mathbb{P}[Y_{i}\geq 1]
we get
\mathbb{P}[X_{i}\leq n^{\alpha}]\leq n^{\gamma}
which is what we aimed to achieve in the first phase.

For the second phase, consider the neighbourhood of some
v\in X_{i}
restricted to
I_{i+1}
, this neighbourhood is distributed according to
\mathcal{D}
. The ball around
v
of size
M
is a random rooted tree with offspring distribution
\mathcal{D}
of height
M
. Let
Z_{v}
denote the random variable of the number of monotonically connected vertices to
v
among this neighbours. By
Corollary

18
we know that
\mathbb{E}[Z_{v}]=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{c^{i}}{i!}>e
for our choice of
M
. Denote
\mathbb{E}[Z_{v}]
by
\mu>e
, and pick
\mu>\mu^{\prime}>e
such that
\mu-\mu^{\prime}<1
.
Denote
X_{i}=t
and the elements of
S_{i}
by
x_{1},...,x_{t}
, notice that
X_{i+1}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{X_{i}}Z_{x_{j}}
, each
Z_{x_{i}}
is bounded by
\sum_{i=0}^{M}T^{i}\leq T^{M+1}
since we use the truncated distribution, and the
Z_{x_{i}}
are independent therefore by Bernstein inequality we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[|\sum_{j=1}^{t}Z_{x_{j}}-\mu t|\geq\left(\mu-\mu^{\prime}\right)t]\leq 2exp\left(-\frac{\left(\mu-\mu^{\prime}\right)^{2}t^{2}/2}{t\left(T^{M+1}\right)^{2}+T^{M+1}\cdot\frac{\left(\mu-\mu^{\prime}\right)t}{3}}\right)

(9)

Since
\mu-\mu^{\prime}<1
we know that
T^{M+1}\cdot\frac{\left(\mu-\mu^{\prime}\right)t}{3}\leq t\left(T^{M+1}\right)^{2}
and thus:

\displaystyle 2exp\left(-\frac{\left(\mu-\mu^{\prime}\right)^{2}t^{2}/2}{t\left(T^{M+1}\right)^{2}+T^{M+1}\cdot\frac{\left(\mu-\mu^{\prime}\right)t}{3}}\right)\leq 2exp\left(-\left(\frac{(\mu-\mu^{\prime})^{2}}{4T^{2M+2}}\right)t\right)=e^{-\Omega(t)}

(10)

If we condition on the event that
X_{\beta\ln(n)}>n^{\alpha}
then this probability is
e^{-\Omega(n^{\alpha})}
which implies that
X_{\frac{\ln(n)}{2}}\geq(\mu^{\prime})^{(\frac{1}{2}-\beta)\ln(n)}X_{\beta\ln(n)}=n^{\ln(\mu^{\prime})(\frac{1}{2}-\beta)}
w.h.p. We now note that we may first choose
T,M,\mu^{\prime}
, then find
\beta
such that
ln(\mu^{\prime})(\frac{1}{2}-\beta)>\frac{1}{2}
, denote this quantity by
\tau>\frac{1}{2}
. Then pick
\alpha
such that
\gamma:=\alpha-\beta\ln\left(\frac{7}{8}+\frac{c}{8}\right)<0
. This implies that
\mathbb{P}[X_{\frac{\ln(n)}{2}}\geq n^{\tau}]>1-n^{-\gamma}-\ln(n)e^{-\Omega(n^{\alpha})}\geq(1-\frac{2}{n^{\gamma}})
.

Therefore the probability that
1,n
are not connected by a monotone path is at most
\frac{2}{n^{\gamma}}+\frac{2}{n^{\gamma}+(1-p)^{n^{2\tau}}}=O(\frac{1}{n^{\gamma}}+(1-p)^{n^{2\tau}})
where the left term corresponds to the event that
1
connects to less than
n^{\tau}
vertices in
\{1,\ldots,\frac{n}{2}\}
, the middle term corresponds to the event that
n
connects to less than
n^{\tau}
vertices in
\{\frac{n}{2}+1,\ldots,n\}
, and the last term corresponds to the probability that both
1
and
n
connects by a monotone path to
n^{\tau}
vertices in their half, and each edge between those sets is not present in the random graph. Since
\tau>\frac{1}{2}
we have
(1-p)^{n^{2\tau}}=O(\frac{1}{n^{\gamma}})
hence the probability of finding a monotone
1
to
n
path is at least
1-O(\frac{1}{n^{\gamma}})
as needed.

∎

4
Concluding Remarks and Open Problems

We remark that the proof of our extremal result is related to a result of
[
6
]
about large cliques in induced
C_{4}
free graphs..

Another direction is to improve the sufficient condition
|\mathcal{P}|=\Omega(n\sqrt{n})
in
Theorem

1
to a weaker one. We conjecture that
Theorem

1
holds for any graph without density assumptions. Explicitly we conjecture that any graph
G=(V,P)
has a globally rigid subgraph in
\mathbb{R}
and
\Omega(\frac{|\mathcal{P}|}{|V|})
vertices.

In a previous version we conjectured that for large enough constant
k
, every
k
connected graph is globally rigid. This was proved to be false in the work of Girão, Illingworth, Michel, Powierski, and Scott
[
5
]
and seperates rigidity in
\mathbb{R}
and
generic
rigidity in higher dimensions by the recent breakthrough of Villányi
[
11
]
which proved that
d(d+1)
connected graphs are globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
assuming the embedding is generic.

In regard to the threshold of monotone path we believe it is interesting to understand what happens at
c=1
? Another problem is to understand the distribution of the number of monotonically connected vertices to a given vertex. In particular consider a Galton-Watson process with off spring distribution
\mathcal{D}
and for which each node
v
gets an auxiliary R.V
X_{v}\sim U[0,1]
, and the root
r
has
X_{r}=0
. What is the distribution of the number of monotonically connected vertices to the root?

We conjectured that random graph
G
sampled via the Erdős-Rényi evolution becomes globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}
exactly at the time its minimum degree is
2
. This was proved by a clever argument of Montgomery, Nenadov and Szabó
[
9
]
and extends to other models of random graphs, e.g.
d
-regular graphs with
d\geq 6
.

We also believe it is interesting to construct sparse globally rigid graphs with average degree as small as possible.

On the Contractivity of Plug-and-Play Operators
C. D. Athalye, K. N. Chaudhury, B. Kumar

Abstract
In plug-and-play (PnP) regularization, the proximal operator in algorithms such as ISTA and ADMM is replaced by a powerful denoiser. This formal substitution works surprisingly well in practice. In fact, PnP has been shown to give state-of-the-art results for various imaging applications. The empirical success of PnP has motivated researchers to understand its theoretical underpinnings and, in particular, its convergence. It was shown in prior work that for kernel denoisers such as the nonlocal means, PnP-ISTA provably converges under some strong assumptions on the forward model. The present work is motivated by the following questions: Can we relax the assumptions on the forward model? Can the convergence analysis be extended to PnP-ADMM? Can we estimate the convergence rate? In this letter, we resolve these questions using the contraction mapping theorem: (i) for symmetric denoisers, we show that (under mild conditions) PnP-ISTA and PnP-ADMM exhibit linear convergence; and (ii) for kernel denoisers, we show that PnP-ISTA and PnP-ADMM converge linearly for image inpainting. We validate our theoretical findings using reconstruction experiments.



1
Introduction

Image reconstruction tasks such as denoising, inpainting, deblurring, and superresolution can be modeled as a linear inverse problem: we wish to recover an image
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
from noisy linear measurements
\boldsymbol{b}=\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\xi}+\boldsymbol{w}
,
where
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is the forward model and
\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is white Gaussian noise.
A standard approach is to solve the optimization problem

\minimize_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,f(\boldsymbol{x})+g(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad f(\boldsymbol{x})=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{b}\|_{2}^{2},

(1)

where the loss function
f
is derived from the forward model and
g
is an image regularizer
[
1
,
2
]
.
The choice of regularizer has evolved from simple Tikhonov and Laplacian regularizers
[
3
]
to wavelet, total-variation, dictionary, etc.
[
4
,
5
,
3
]
, and to more recent learning-based models
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
.

It has been shown that powerful denoisers can also be used for image regularization
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.
One such method is Plug-and-Play (PnP) regularization, where we fix a proximal algorithm for solving (
1
) such as ISTA or ADMM
[
13
]
and formally replace the proximal operator of
g
with a denoiser
[
10
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
.
It is not apparent if this ad-hoc replacement can work in the first place, but PnP has been shown to work surprisingly well for many imaging and signal processing applications
[
10
,
6
]
,
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
.
In fact, it has been shown that simple kernel denoisers such as nonlocal means
[
22
]
can yield state-of-the-art results which are comparable with deep learning methods
[
23
]
.
The empirical success of PnP has sparked interest in theoretical questions stemming from the algorithm. The most basic issue is convergence since the PnP iterations are not strictly derived within an optimization framework. In the absence of a convergence guarantee, the iterations can possibly diverge and result in a poor reconstruction.
Convergence of PnP has been studied in several works; we refer readers to
[
16
,
24
]
and the references therein for a comprehensive survey.
In particular, PnP convergence for linear inverse problems is studied in
[
10
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
,
[
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
.

In this letter, we revisit the convergence question for two popular PnP algorithms, PnP-ISTA and PnP-ADMM
[
10
,
27
,
15
]
.
To keep the analysis tractable, we restrict ourselves to linear inverse problems and linear denoisers
[
10
,
14
,
16
,
23
]
.
Specifically, we show that the convergence results in
[
15
]
can be extended and strengthened in various directions. Similar to
[
15
]
, we exploit the linearity of the denoiser and the fact that PnP-ISTA and PnP-ADMM can be expressed as an iterative map
\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\mathbf{Q}\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{r}
; however, our analysis is fundamentally different from
[
15
]
.
Our main results are summarized below:

(i)

For symmetric denoisers
[
10
,
14
]
, we give necessary and sufficient conditions for PnP-ISTA and PnP-ADMM to be contractive w.r.t. the spectral norm (Theorems
1
and
2
).
Subsequently, using the contraction mapping theorem
[
30
]
, we establish linear convergence of these PnP methods for applications such as inpainting, deblurring, and superresolution (Corollaries
1
and
2
).
To our knowledge, these are the first results on
linear convergence
of PnP, where the assumptions are practically verifiable.

(ii)

For image inpainting using (nonsymmetric) kernel denoisers
[
22
]
, we prove that
\|\mathbf{Q}\|_{\mathbf{D}}<1
for both PnP-ISTA and PnP-ADMM (Theorem
3
), where
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{D}}
is an appropriate matrix norm. It is shown in
[
15
]
that the spectral radius
\varrho(\mathbf{Q})<1
for PnP-ISTA based on kernel denoisers, which is sufficient to guarantee convergence.
However, the condition
\|\mathbf{Q}\|_{\mathbf{D}}<1
allows us to establish linear convergence for PnP-ISTA (Theorem
3
), which is not guaranteed by
\varrho(\mathbf{Q})<1
.

We state and discuss our main results in Section

4

2
Background

In this section, we briefly cover some background material.
In the classical ISTA algorithm for solving (
1
), we start with an initialization
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and perform the updates
\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}=\mathrm{prox}_{\gamma g}\big(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}-\gamma\nabla\!f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\big)
for
k\geqslant 0
,
where
\mathrm{prox}_{g}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the proximal operator of
g
,

\mathrm{prox}_{g}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\argmin_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{z}\|_{2}^{2}+g(\boldsymbol{z})\right),

(2)

and
\gamma>0
is the step size
[
31
]
.
In PnP regularization, instead of going through
g
and its proximal operator, we directly substitute
\mathrm{prox}_{\gamma g}
by a denoiser.
In this work, we consider linear denoisers and a quadratic loss function
f
(see (
1
)), i.e., we perform the updates as

\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}=\mathbf{W}\big(\boldsymbol{x}_{k}-\gamma(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\!\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{x}_{k}-\mathbf{A}^{\top}\boldsymbol{b})\big),

(3)

where
\mathbf{W}
is a linear denoiser. Examples of such denoisers are Yaroslavsky
[
32
]
, bilateral
[
33
]
, nonlocal means
[
22
,
10
]
, LARK
[
34
]
, GLIDE
[
35
]
, and GMM
[
14
]
. We refer to (
3
) as PnP-ISTA.
The above idea can be applied to other iterative algorithms such as PnP-ADMM
[
10
,
17
]
, which is based on the ADMM algorithm
[
13
]
. In PnP-ADMM, starting with
\boldsymbol{y}_{0},\boldsymbol{z}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and some fixed
\rho>0
, the updates are performed as follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{prox}_{\rho f}(\boldsymbol{y}_{k}-\boldsymbol{z}_{k}),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{y}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{W}(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}+\boldsymbol{z}_{k}),

(4)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{z}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{z}_{k}+\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{y}_{k+1}.

In prior work
[
15
]
, it was shown that PnP-ISTA converges if
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{W}
satisfy some assumptions and
\gamma
is small.
More specifically, it was shown that (under some conditions on
\mathbf{W}
) convergence is guaranteed for inpainting if
\gamma\in(0,1)
. The analysis is based on the observation that (
3
) can be written as

\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}=\mathbf{P}\boldsymbol{x}_{k}+\boldsymbol{q},

(5)

where
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{A}^{\top}\!\mathbf{A})
and
\boldsymbol{q}=\gamma\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\boldsymbol{b}
. In particular, it was shown in
[
15
]
that if at least one pixel is sampled from the averaging window (of the denoiser) around each pixel, then
\varrho(\mathbf{P})<1
for inpainting;
subsequently, it follows that the sequence
\{\boldsymbol{x}_{k}\}
in (
5
) converges for an arbitrary initialization
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}

[
36
]
.
However, we show that the above assumption in
[
15
]
can be dropped if we adopt a different analysis.

Similar to PnP-ISTA, we can express (
2
) in terms of a linear dynamical system. More precisely, we have the following observation by adapting the results in
[
27
,
25
]
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\{(\boldsymbol{x}_{k},\boldsymbol{y}_{k},\boldsymbol{z}_{k})\}_{k\geqslant 1}
be the iterates generated by PnP-ADMM starting from
\boldsymbol{y}_{0},\boldsymbol{z}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Define the sequence
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
as follows:
\boldsymbol{u}_{1}=\boldsymbol{y}_{1}+\boldsymbol{z}_{1}
, and

\boldsymbol{u}_{k+1}=\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}(2\mathrm{prox}_{\rho f}-\mathbf{I})(2\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{I})(\boldsymbol{u}_{k})

(6)

for
k\geqslant 1
. Then
\boldsymbol{y}_{k}=\mathbf{W}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}
for
k\geqslant 1
.

The above result can be traced to the observation that ADMM can be interpreted as Douglas-Rachford splitting applied to the dual problem of (
1
), e.g., see
[
13
]
.
For problem (
1
), a direct calculation based on (
2
) gives

\mathrm{prox}_{\rho f}(\boldsymbol{x})=\big(\mathbf{I}+\rho\mathbf{A}^{\top}\!\mathbf{A}\big)^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{h},

(7)

where
\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an irrelevant parameter.
Substituting (
7
) in (
6
), we obtain
\boldsymbol{u}_{k+1}=\mathbf{R}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}+\boldsymbol{s}
, where

\mathbf{R}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{J}),\qquad\mathbf{J}=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{V},

(8a)

\mathbf{F}=2(\mathbf{I}+\rho\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A})^{-1}-\mathbf{I},\qquad\mathbf{V}=(2\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{I}),

(8b)

and
\boldsymbol{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an irrelevant parameter.

Our convergence analysis is built on the contraction mapping theorem
[
30
]
. In particular, we use the following result.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\{\boldsymbol{x}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
be the sequence generated as
\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}=\mathbf{Q}\boldsymbol{x}_{k}+\boldsymbol{r}
. Then
\{\boldsymbol{x}_{k}\}
converges linearly if
\mathbf{Q}
is a contraction, i.e., if there exists a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|\mathbf{Q}\|:=\max\,\{\,\|\mathbf{Q}\boldsymbol{x}\|:\,\|\boldsymbol{x}\|=1\}<1
.

Recall that a sequence
\{\boldsymbol{x}_{k}\}
is said to converge
linearly
to
\boldsymbol{x}^{*}
if there exists
\theta\in(0,1)
such that
\|\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\|\leqslant\theta\,\|\boldsymbol{x}_{k}-\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\|
for all
k

[
13
,
37
]
. More generally, we use the term “linear convergence” if there exist
c>0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
such that
\|\boldsymbol{x}_{k}-\boldsymbol{x}^{*}\|\leqslant c\,\theta^{k}
for all
k

[
38
]
.

3
Results and Discussion

In this section, we motivate and discuss our main results; the proofs are deferred to Appendix.
We first consider linear symmetric denoisers and then nonsymmetric kernel denoisers; the analysis and results are slightly different in each case.

We use
\mathbb{S}^{n}
to denote the space of
(n\times n)
symmetric matrices.
We denote the spectral radius of
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
by
\varrho(\mathbf{Q})
.
A matrix
\mathbf{C}
is said to be nonnegative if all its entries are nonnegative.
We use
\boldsymbol{e}
to denote the vector of all-ones,
\mathbf{I}
for the identity matrix, and
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{C})
for the null space of
\mathbf{C}
.
A matrix
\mathbf{C}
is called stochastic if
\mathbf{C}
is nonnegative and
\mathbf{C}\boldsymbol{e}=\boldsymbol{e}
.

3.1
Symmetric Denoisers

Definition 1
.

A linear denoiser
\mathbf{W}
is said to be symmetric if
\mathbf{W}
is stochastic and symmetric positive semidefinite.

Symmetric denoisers are discussed in
[
39
,
10
,
14
]
.
Note that the eigenvalues of a symmetric denoiser are in the interval
[0,1]
.
The above-cited instances of symmetric denoisers are in fact irreducible; subsequently, it follows from the Perron-Frobenius theorem
[
36
]
that
1
is a simple eigenvalue of
\mathbf{W}
, i.e., its algebraic multiplicity is one.

To motivate the main idea, we consider the PnP-ISTA operator
\mathbf{P}
in (
5
) and write it as

\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{G},\quad\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A}.

(9)

Since
\mathbf{W}
is a symmetric denoiser,
\|\mathbf{W}\|_{2}=\varrho(\mathbf{W})=1
. On the other hand, if
0<\gamma<2/\varrho(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\!\mathbf{A})
, then
\|\mathbf{G}\|_{2}\leqslant 1
.
Since neither
\mathbf{W}
nor
\mathbf{G}
is contractive (w.r.t. to the Euclidean norm), we cannot immediately conclude that
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{G}
is a contraction.
However, we observed in numerical experiments that in the case of symmetric denoisers,
\|\mathbf{P}\|_{2}<1
if
0<\gamma<2/\varrho(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\!\mathbf{A})
.
This raises the following question: when is the composition of two symmetric nonexpansive operators contractive? An equivalent condition for the same is given next, which is a core result of this letter.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
are such that
\|\mathbf{M}\|_{2}\leqslant 1
and
\|\mathbf{N}\|_{2}\leqslant 1
.
Let
\mathbb{U}
and
\mathbb{V}
be the subspaces spanned by the eigenvectors of
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{N}
corresponding to eigenvalues
\pm 1
.
Then
\|\mathbf{M}\mathbf{N}\|_{2}<1
if and only if
\mathbb{U}\cap\mathbb{V}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
.

Using Lemma
1
, we can explain our empirical observation that
\|\mathbf{P}\|_{2}<1
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathbf{W}
be a symmetric denoiser, and let
0<\gamma<2/\varrho(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\!\mathbf{A})
. Then
\|\mathbf{P}\|_{2}<1
if and only if
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W})\cap\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{A})=\{\mathbf{0}\}
.

As explained next, we get the following result from Theorem
1
and Proposition
2
.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\mathbf{W}
be a symmetric denoiser with
1
as a simple eigenvalue and let
\gamma\in(0,2)
. Then PnP-ISTA is contractive and converges linearly for inpainting, deblurring, and superresolution.

Following Theorem
1
, we need to check that
\varrho(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\!\mathbf{A})\leqslant 1
and
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W})\,\cap\,\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{A})=\{\mathbf{0}\}
. This requires us to examine
\mathbf{A}
for the applications under consideration.
In inpainting, we observe a subset of the pixels from the original image
[
1
]
. If we represent the image by a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
n
is the number of pixels, and let
\Omega\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
be the observed pixels, then
\mathbf{A}
for inpainting is the diagonal matrix

\mathbf{A}_{ii}=\begin{cases}1,\ \ &i\in\Omega,\\
0,\ \ &i\notin\Omega.\end{cases}

(10)

We assume that
\Omega\neq\emptyset
, which holds trivially in practice.
Thus,
\varrho(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\!\mathbf{A})=1
for inpainting.
For deblurring and superresolution,
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{B}
and
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{S}\mathbf{B}
, respectively, where
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is a blurring matrix corresponding to a lowpass filter and
\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is a subsampling matrix.
In practice, a blur matrix
\mathbf{B}
is circulant and stochastic
[
1
]
, whereby
\|\mathbf{B}\|_{2}=1
.
Therefore,
\varrho(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\!\mathbf{A})=1
for deblurring. As for superresolution, since
\|\mathbf{S}\|_{2}=\|\mathbf{B}\|_{2}=1
, we have
\varrho(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\!\mathbf{A})=\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2}^{2}\leqslant 1
.
Thus, the range of
\gamma
in Theorem
1
can be
(0,2)
for inpainting, deblurring, and superresolution.

Next, we argue that
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W})\,\cap\,\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{A})=\{\mathbf{0}\}
in Corollary
1
. Since
1
is assumed to be a simple eigenvalue of
\mathbf{W}
, we have
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W})=\mathrm{span}\{\boldsymbol{e}\}
. Note that
\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{e}\neq 0
for inpainting, deblurring, and superresolution.
Therefore, for the applications under consideration in Corollary
1
, using Theorem
1
, we have
\|\mathbf{P}\|_{2}<1
for
\gamma\in(0,2)
. Subsequently, Corollary
1
follows from Proposition
2
.

The contractivity of PnP-ISTA was established for deep denoisers in
[
27
]
, however, under a strong assumption that
f
is strongly convex.
Note that our analysis applies to a broader class of linear inverse problems such as inpainting, deblurring and superresolution for which
f
is not strongly convex.
Also, the present assumption for PnP-ISTA convergence is significantly milder than that in
[
15
]
.
Recall that
1
is indeed a simple eigenvalue of symmetric denoisers discussed in
[
39
,
10
,
14
]
.
Moreover, the convergence interval
\gamma\in(0,2)
in Corollary
1
is similar to that for classical ISTA
[
31
]
.

We next look at PnP-ADMM with a symmetric denoiser. The core result in this regard is the following.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathbf{W}
be a symmetric denoiser and
\rho>0
. Then
\|\mathbf{R}\|_{2}<1
if and only if
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W})\cap\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{A})=\{\mathbf{0}\}
.

As explained earlier, the condition in Theorems
1
and
2
is satisfied for inpainting, deblurring, and superresolution if
1
is a simple eigenvalue of
\mathbf{W}
. Thus, as a consequence of Theorem
2
, we can conclude the following (see Remark
7
in Appendix).

Corollary 2
.

Let
\mathbf{W}
be a symmetric denoiser for which
1
is a simple eigenvalue. Then, for any
\rho>0
, PnP-ADMM is contractive and converges linearly for inpainting, deblurring, and superresolution.

The convergence interval
\rho\in(0,\infty)
in Theorem
2
is similar to standard convergence results for ADMM
[
13
]
.

3.2
Kernel Denoisers

We now turn to kernel denoisers
[
32
,
40
,
33
,
22
,
10
,
34
]
. These are typically nonsymmetric, whereby Theorems
1
and
2
do not apply. First, we make precise what we mean by a kernel denoiser.

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathbf{K}
be nonnegative and symmetric positive semidefinite, and let
\mathbf{D}=\diag(\mathbf{K}\boldsymbol{e})
. We call
\mathbf{K}
a kernel matrix and
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathbf{K}
a kernel denoiser. We assume that
\mathbf{K}\boldsymbol{e}
is a positive vector and
1
is a simple eigenvalue of
\mathbf{W}
.

The last two assumptions are met for most kernel denoisers where
\mathbf{W}
is nonnegative and irreducible
[
41
]
.
In the archetype kernel denoiser, such as nonlocal means
[
22
]
,
\mathbf{K}
is derived from a Gaussian kernel acting on image patches. The precise definition of
\mathbf{K}
is not relevant to our discussion; instead, we refer the reader to
[
41
,
39
]
for precise definitions. We just need the following abstract property of a kernel denoiser (e.g., see
[
16
]
):
\mathbf{W}
is similar to a symmetric positive semidefinite matrix, and its eigenvalues are in
[0,1]
.

It turns out that we generally cannot ensure that
\|\mathbf{P}\|_{2}<1
or
\|\mathbf{R}\|_{2}<1
for kernel denoisers (see Table
1
). However, for the inpainting problem, we can show that
\mathbf{P}
is a contraction with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{D}}
, where
\mathbf{D}
is as in Definition
2
.

Remark 1
.

The inner product and norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
w.r.t. a symmetric positive definite matrix
\mathbf{D}
are defined as follows:

\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle_{\mathbf{D}}:=\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{y},\qquad\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{\mathbf{D}}:=\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}\rangle_{\mathbf{D}}^{1/2},

and
\|\mathbf{Q}\|_{\mathbf{D}}:=\max\,\{\|\mathbf{Q}\boldsymbol{x}\|_{\mathbf{D}}:\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{\mathbf{D}}=1\}
for
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
.

\blacklozenge

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathbf{W}
be a kernel denoiser. Consider the inpainting problem where
\mathbf{A}
is given by (
10
).
If
0<\gamma<2
, then
\|\mathbf{P}\|_{\mathbf{D}}<1
; consequently, PnP-ISTA converges linearly.
On the other hand, if
\mathbf{W}
is nonsingular and
\rho>0
, then
\|\mathbf{R}\|_{\mathbf{D}}<1
; thus, PnP-ADMM converges linearly.

We note that the nonsingularity assumption of
\mathbf{W}
in Theorem
3
holds for nonlocal means
[
23
]
.
Also, note that the use of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{D}}
in Theorem
3
is crucial in guaranteeing contractivity. In fact, as reported in Table
1
, we have observed in numerical experiments that
\|\mathbf{P}\|_{2}
and
\|\mathbf{R}\|_{2}
can indeed exceed
1
.

that

4
Numerical Results

We numerically validate our contractivity and linear convergence results for three restoration applications: inpainting, deblurring, and superresolution.
For all the experiments, we have made some fixed choices. We have used nonlocal means as a kernel denoiser
[
16
]
.
For symmetric denoisers, we have used DSG-NLM
[
10
]
(a form of symmetrized NLM) and the GMM denoiser
[
14
]
.
We have randomly sampled
30\%
pixels for inpainting; the blur matrix
\mathbf{B}
is a uniform
11\times 11
blur for deblurring and superresolution, and
\mathbf{S}
is uniform
2\mbox{x}
-subsampling for superresolution.
The codes of our experiments are available at https://github.com/Bhartendu-Kumar/PnP-Conv.

Therefore, it follows from the contraction mapping theorem that for an arbitrary initialization, the PnP iterates should converge to the unique fixed point of the PnP operator.

5
Conclusion

We provided new insights into the convergence of PnP for linear inverse problems. In particular, using the contraction mapping theorem, we established linear convergence of PnP-ISTA and PnP-ADMM with symmetric denoisers. We numerically validated our results on inpainting, deblurring, and superresolution. Furthermore, we established linear convergence of PnP-ISTA and PnP-ADMM for inpainting with nonsymmetric kernel denoiser.

We can establish contractivity under additional assumptions on
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{W}
for deblurring and superresolution with kernel denoisers. However, these additional assumptions are rarely met in practice, so we did not report these results.
Establishing convergence for deblurring and superresolution with nonsymmetric kernel denoisers thus remains an open problem.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\mathbf{T}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
be such that
\|\mathbf{T}\|_{2}\leqslant 1

\mathbb{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the space spanned by eigenvectors of
\mathbf{T}

\pm 1

\|\mathbf{T}\boldsymbol{z}\|_{2}<\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_{2}

\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{Y}

Proof.

Since
\|\mathbf{T}\|_{2}\leqslant 1

|\lambda|\leqslant 1

\lambda\in\sigma(\mathbf{T})
.
Let
\mathbb{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

\mathbf{T}
corresponding to

|\lambda|<1

Note that
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}

\mathbf{T}

\mathbf{T}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}

\mathbb{X}^{\perp}=\mathbb{Y}

Therefore, for any arbitrary
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{Y}

\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{Y}

\boldsymbol{z}=\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{y}
.
Now, since
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
are orthogonal and
\mathbf{T}
-invariant, we have

\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\mathbf{T}\boldsymbol{z}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\mathbf{T}\boldsymbol{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\mathbf{T}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{2}^{2}.

(11)

From the definition of
\mathbb{Y}
, we have
\|\mathbf{T}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{2}=\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{2}
.
Also, since
\boldsymbol{x}\neq\mathbf{0}

that
\|\mathbf{T}\boldsymbol{x}\|_{2}<\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{2}

Thus, using (

(p, q)-Frame measures on LCA groups: Perturbations and Construction Thanks: This work was partially supported by the Central Tehran Branch, Islamic Azad University.
1 Abdolreza Tahmasebi Birgani, 2 Mohammad Sadegh Asgari

Abstract
. Motivated to generalize the Fourier frame concept to Banach spaces we introduce (p, q)-Bessel/frame measures for a given finite measure on LCA groups. We also present a general way of constructing (p, q)-Bessel/frame measures for a given measure. Moreover, we prove that if a measure has an associated (p, q)-frame measure, then it must have a certain uniformity in the sense that the weight is distributed quite uniformly on its support. Next, we show that if the measures μ \mu and λ \lambda without atoms whose supports form a packing pair, then μ ∗ λ + δ g ∗ μ \mu\ast\lambda+\delta_{g}\ast\mu does not admit any (p, q)-frame measure. Finally, we analyze the stability of (p, q)-frame measures under small perturbations. We prove new theorems concerning the stability of (p, q)-frame measures under perturbation in both Hilbert spaces and Banach spaces.

In this paper, we develop the theory of frame measures to the Banach spaces

We first start by recalling the basic definitions and the necessary notations which will be used throughout the paper.

A LCA group is an arbitrary locally compact Hausdorff abelian group
G
such that the group operation and inversion are
continuous. We will denote the group operation by
+
and neutral element by
0
. For technical reasons we will assume
that
G
is a countable union of compact sets and metrizable. The dual group of
G

continuous characters on
G

\widehat{G}
. The value of the character
\gamma\in\widehat{G}

at the point

is written by
\langle x,\gamma\rangle
. A character is a function
\gamma:G\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
,
where
\mathbb{T}
is the complex numbers whose absolute value is
1
such that for all
x,y\in G

(i)

|\langle x,\gamma\rangle|=1
.

(ii)

\langle x+y,\gamma\rangle=\langle x,\gamma\rangle\langle y,\gamma\rangle
.

As the name would suggest, endowed with the weak
∗
topology and group operation
pointwise multiplication,
\widehat{G}
is a LCA group. The character
\gamma
of the group
G
is called trivial if for every

x\in G

\langle x,\gamma\rangle=1
. So every group
G
have a trivial character. Moreover, every
character of
G
mapping the neutral element of
G
to 1, i.e.
\langle 0,\gamma\rangle=1
. We also have

\displaystyle\overline{\langle x,\gamma\rangle}\langle x,\gamma\rangle=|\langle x,\gamma\rangle|^{2}=1.

Thus

\displaystyle\langle x,\overline{\gamma}\rangle=\overline{\langle x,\gamma\rangle}=\langle x,\gamma\rangle^{-1}=\langle x,-\gamma\rangle=\langle-x,\gamma\rangle.

It follows that if
\gamma\in\widehat{G}
then

\overline{\gamma}\in\widehat{G}
. Moreover, if
G
is a finite order group of
n

element of
G
to the nth unit root. In this case we can traslated range of any character with set of all the nth unit root.
In the other words,

\displaystyle U_{n}=\{\xi^{k}_{n}:\xi_{n}=e^{2\pi i/n},0\leq k\leq n-1\}.

The other fundamental feature is the existence and uniqueness of a Haar measure on an LCA group. A Haar measure on an
LCA group is a non-negative, regular Borel measure, which is not identically zero and is both left and right translation-invariant

\displaystyle L^{p}(G)=\Big\{f:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}:\;f\;\text{is measurable and}\int_{G}|f(x)|^{p}d\lambda_{G}(x)<\infty\Big\}.

If

1.
(p, q)-Frame measures on LCA groups

In this section, we extend the notion of frame measure to (p, q)-frame measure for any
(1<p,q<\infty)
on LCA groups, and
we investigate some general results concerning (p, q)-frame measures.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mu,\nu
be two Borel measures on LCA groups
G
and
\widehat{G}
, respectively. Then

(i)

the Fourier transform of a function
f\in L^{1}(\mu)
with respect to
\mu
is defined by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\widehat{fd\mu}:\widehat{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\hskip 28.45274pt\widehat{fd\mu}(\gamma)=\int_{G}f(x)\overline{\langle x,\gamma\rangle}d\mu(x),\quad\forall\gamma\in\widehat{G}.

By putting
f
to be the characteristic function on the support of
\mu
, we obtain the Fourier-Stieltjes transform of a measure

\mu

(1.2)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}(\gamma)=\int_{G}\overline{\langle x,\gamma\rangle}d\mu(x),\quad\forall\gamma\in\widehat{G}.

(ii)

the inverse Fourier transform of a function
\varphi\in L^{1}(\nu)
with respect
to
\nu
is given by

(1.3)

\displaystyle\widecheck{\varphi d\nu}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\hskip 28.45274pt\widecheck{\varphi d\nu}(x)=\int_{\widehat{G}}\varphi(\gamma)\langle x,\gamma\rangle d\nu(\gamma),\quad\forall x\in G.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that the Fourier-Stieltjes transform of a finite Borel measure
\mu
on LCA group
G
is a bounded and uniformly
continuous function on
\widehat{G}
.

Now, we extend the concepts of (p, q)-Bessel/frame measures as follows.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
. Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on LCA group
G
and
\nu
be a Borel measure on
\widehat{G}
,
respectively. Then the measure
\nu
is called a (p, q)-frame measure for
\mu
if there exist constants
0<A\leq B<\infty

such that

(1.4)

A\|f\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\mu)}\leq\|\widehat{fd\mu}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\nu)}\leq B\|f\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\mu)},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall f\in L^{p}(\mu).

A
and
B
are called frame bounds for
\nu
. If
p=q=2
,
\nu
is
called a frame measure for
\mu
. The measure
\nu
is called a (p, q)-Bessel measure for
\mu
, when it satisfies at least
the upper (p, q)-frame measure inequality. If
A=B=1
,
\nu
is called a (p, q)-Plancherel measure for
\mu
.

Example 1.4
.

Let
x_{k}\in G
,
k=1,\cdots,n
, and let
\Omega\subset\widehat{G}
be a countable subset. Suppose that

\{c_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
is a sequence of positive real numbers such that
0<A\leq\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}c_{\omega}\leq B<\infty
. Then the measure
\nu=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}c_{\omega}\delta_{\omega}
is a (p, q)-frame
measure for
\mu=\delta_{x_{1}}\ast\cdots\ast\delta_{x_{n}}
with frame bounds
A
,
B
, where
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac
measure at
x
.

The following example illustrates the existence of a (p, q)-Bessel measure for an infinite continuous measure on an LCA
group
G
.

Example 1.5
.

Let
\lambda_{G}
,
\lambda_{\widehat{G}}
be the Haar measures on LCA groups
G
and
\widehat{G}
, respectively.
Suppose
1<p\leq 2
and
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
. Then by the Hausdorff-Young Inequality
[
10
]
,
\lambda_{\widehat{G}}

is a (p, q)-Bessel measure for
\lambda_{G}
.

Example 1.6
.

Let
\lambda_{G}
,
\lambda_{\widehat{G}}
be the Haar measures on LCA groups
G
and
\widehat{G}
, respectively.
Suppose that
E\subset G
is a set with positive and finite Haar measure, and also assume that
\varphi\in L^{1}(\widehat{G})

satisfies that
0<A\leq\varphi(\gamma)\leq B<\infty
a.e., on
\widehat{G}
. Define
d\mu=\chi_{E}d\lambda_{G}
and
d\nu=\varphi d\lambda_{\widehat{G}}
. Then
\nu
is a frame measure for
\mu
with frame bounds
A
,
B
.
Indeed, for every
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{G}|f(x)|^{2}d\mu(x)=\int_{G}\chi_{E}(x)|f(x)|^{2}d\lambda_{G}(x)=\int_{G}|(\chi_{E}f)(x)|^{2}d\lambda_{G}(x).

This gives that
\chi_{E}f\in L^{2}(G)
and
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\|\chi_{E}f\|_{L^{2}(G)}
. On the other hand, for every
\gamma\in\widehat{G}
we obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{fd\mu}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(x)\overline{\langle x,\gamma\rangle}d\mu(x)=\int_{G}\chi_{E}(x)f(x)\overline{\langle x,\gamma\rangle}d\lambda_{G}(x)=\widehat{(\chi_{E}f)}(\gamma).

Now by the Plancherel theorem we observe that

\displaystyle A\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle=A\|\chi_{E}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}=A\|\widehat{(\chi_{E}f)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{G})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\widehat{G}}\varphi(\gamma)\big|\widehat{(\chi_{E}f)}(\gamma)\big|^{2}d\lambda_{\widehat{G}}(\gamma)=\|\widehat{fd\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq B\|\widehat{(\chi_{E}f)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{G})}=B\|\chi_{E}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}=B\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

Remark 1.7
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
. Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on LCA group
G
.
Then the family of (p, q)-Bessel measures for
\mu
is never empty. Let
\Omega\subseteq\widehat{G}
be a countable
subset and
\{c_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
is a sequence of positive real numbers such that
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}c_{\omega}\leq B/\mu(G)
. Then the measure
\nu=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}c_{\omega}\delta_{\omega}
is a (p, q)-Bessel
measure for
\mu
with Bessel bound
B
. Indeed, for every
f\in L^{p}(\mu)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{G}}|\widehat{fd\mu}(\gamma)|^{q}d\nu(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}c_{\omega}\int_{\widehat{G}}|\widehat{fd\mu}(\gamma)|^{q}d\delta_{\omega}(\gamma)=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}c_{\omega}|\widehat{fd\mu}(\omega)|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}c_{\omega}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}^{q}\mu(G)\leq B\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}^{q}.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
and let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on LCA group
G
. Then
\mu
is called a (p, q)-frame-spectral
measure if there exists a countable set
\Omega\subset\widehat{G}
such that
\Omega
is a q-frame for
L^{p}(\mu)
, i.e.,
there exist two constants
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that

(1.5)

A\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}^{q}\leq\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}|\widehat{fd\mu}(\omega)|^{q}\leq B\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}^{q},\hskip 28.45274pt\forall f\in L^{p}(\mu).

When
\mu
is a (p, q)-frame-spectral measure, then
\Omega
is called
a (p, q)-frame-spectrum for
\mu
.

Remark 1.9
.

In general, if
\mu
is a (p, q)-frame-spectral measure with (p, q)-frame-spectrum
\Omega
, then
\nu=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\delta_{\omega}

is a (p, q)-frame measure for
\mu
. Indeed, if
A,B
are the frame
bounds for q-frame
\Omega
. Then for every
f\in L^{p}(\mu)
we have

\displaystyle A\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}|\widehat{fd\mu}(\omega)|^{q}=\int_{\widehat{G}}|\widehat{fd\mu}(\gamma)|^{q}d\nu(\gamma)\leq B\|f\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\mu)}.

Conversely, if
\nu
is purely atomic and is a (p, q)-frame
measure for finite Borel measure
\mu
, i.e. there exists a countable subset
\Omega\subseteq\widehat{G}
such that

\nu=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}c_{\omega}\delta_{\omega}
and
c_{\omega}>0
, then the set
\Lambda=\big\{c_{\omega}^{\frac{1}{q}}\omega:\;\omega\in\Omega\big\}
is a q-frame for
L^{p}(\mu)
. Indeed, for each
f\in L^{p}(\mu)

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda}|\widehat{fd\mu}(\gamma)|^{q}=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}|\widehat{fd\mu}(c_{\omega}^{\frac{1}{q}}\omega)|^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{G}}|\widehat{fd\mu}(\gamma)|^{q}d\nu(\gamma)=\|\widehat{fd\mu}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\nu)}.

This shows that
\mu
is a (p, q)-frame-spectral
measure with (p, q)-frame-spectrum

.

2.
Characterization and Construction of (p, q)-frame measures

In this section we present a general way of constructing (p, q)-Bessel/frame measures for a given measure. We prove that
if a measure has an associated (p, q)-frame measure, it must have a certain uniformity in the sense that the weight is distributed
quite uniformly on its support. We also introduce a finite Borel measure on LCA group
G
which does not admit any (p, q)-frame
measure. First, we show that every (p, q)-Bessel measure must be locally finite. So, if
\widehat{G}
is connected, then any
(p, q)-Bessel measure is
\sigma
-finite. Indeed, we can generalize Proposition 2.1 of
[
6
]
to the situation of (p, q)-Bessel
measures.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on LCA group
G
and
\nu
be a Borel measure on
\widehat{G}
, respectively.
Let
\nu
be a (p, q)-Bessel measure for
\mu
. Then for any compact subset
K\subset\widehat{G}
, there exists a
constant
C_{K}
such that
\nu(K)\leq C_{K}
. Consequently, if
\widehat{G}
is connected, then
\nu
is
\sigma
-finite.

Proof.

Let
B
be the Bessel bound for the (p, q)-Bessel measure
\nu
.
Since
\widehat{\mu}(0)=\mu(G)>0
, by the uniformly continuous of
\widehat{\mu}
there is a neighborhood
V

of point zero in
\widehat{G}
and
\delta>0
with
|\widehat{\mu}(\gamma)|\geq\delta
, for all
\gamma\in V
.
Moreover, for every
\xi\in\widehat{G}
we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\xi d\mu}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\langle y,\xi\rangle\overline{\langle y,\gamma\rangle}d\mu(y)=\int_{G}\overline{\langle y,\gamma-\xi\rangle}d\mu(y)=\widehat{\mu}(\gamma-\xi).

This implies that

\displaystyle B\mu(G)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle=B\|\xi\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}^{q}\geq\|\widehat{\xi d\mu}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}^{q}=\int_{\widehat{G}}|\widehat{\xi d\mu}(\gamma)|^{q}d\nu(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{G}}|\widehat{\mu}(\gamma-\xi)|^{q}d\nu(\gamma)\geq\int_{\xi+V}|\widehat{\mu}(\gamma-\xi)|^{q}d\nu(\gamma)\geq\delta^{q}\nu(\xi+V).

Therefore
\nu(\xi+V)\leq{B\mu(G)^{\frac{q}{p}}/\delta^{q}}
for all
\xi\in\widehat{G}
. Now suppose that

K\subseteq\widehat{G}
is compact. Then there exist indices
1\leq i\leq n
such that
K\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_{K}}(\xi_{i}+V)
. So we have

\displaystyle\nu(K)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N_{K}}\nu(\xi_{i}+V)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N_{K}}\frac{B\mu(G)^{\frac{q}{p}}}{\delta^{q}}=\frac{B\mu(G)^{\frac{q}{p}}}{\delta^{q}}N_{K}=C_{K}.

Now, if
\widehat{G}
is connected, then
\nu
is a
\sigma
-compact and so
\nu
is
\sigma
-finite.
∎

The next proposition shows that each finite Borel measure on
\widehat{G}
is a (p, q)-Bessel measure for any finite Borel
measure on
G
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mu
and
\nu
be two finite Borel measures on LCA groups
G
and
\widehat{G}
, respectively. Then
\nu
is a
(p, q)-Bessel measure for
\mu
.

Proof.

Let
p^{\prime}
be the conjugate exponent to
p
. Then for every
f\in L^{p}(\mu)
and
\gamma\in\widehat{G}
we have

\displaystyle\big|\widehat{fd\mu}(\gamma)\big|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}^{q}\|\gamma\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mu)}^{q}=(\mu(G))^{\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}^{q}.

Thus

\displaystyle\big\|\widehat{fd\mu}\big\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\nu)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{G}}\big|\widehat{fd\mu}(\gamma)\big|^{q}d\nu(\gamma)\leq\int_{\widehat{G}}(\mu(G))^{\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}^{q}d\nu(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\nu(\widehat{G})(\mu(G))^{\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}\|f\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\mu)}.

∎

In the following results, we show that the (p, q)-frame measure property is preserved under the convolution of the Dirac
measures. First, we show that the (p, q)-frame measure property is preserved under the convolution of the Dirac measures
on
G
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on
G
and
x\in G
. Then a Borel measure
\nu
on
\widehat{G}
is a (p, q)-frame
measure for
\mu
if and only if
\nu
is a (p, q)-frame measure for
\delta_{x}\ast\mu
with the same frame bounds.

Proof.

Let
\nu
be a (p, q)-frame measure for
\delta_{x}\ast\mu
with frame bounds
A
and
B
. Then for every
f\in L^{p}(\mu)

we have

\displaystyle\int_{G}|f(y-x)|^{p}d(\delta_{x}\ast\mu)(y)=\int_{G}\int_{G}|f(y+z-x)|^{p}d\delta_{x}(y)d\mu(z)=\int_{G}|f(z)|^{p}d\mu(z).

This gives that
f(\cdot-x)\in L^{p}(\delta_{x}\ast\mu)
and
\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}=\|f(\cdot-x)\|_{L^{p}(\delta_{x}\ast\mu)}
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\big|\widehat{f(\cdot-x)d(\delta_{x}\ast\mu)}(\gamma)\big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{G}f(y-x)\overline{\langle y,\gamma\rangle}d(\delta_{x}\ast\mu)(y)\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{G}f(y-x)\overline{\langle y-x,\gamma\rangle}d(\delta_{x}\ast\mu)(y)\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{G}\int_{G}f(y+z-x)\overline{\langle y+z-x,\gamma\rangle}d\delta_{x}(y)d\mu(z)\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{G}f(z)\overline{\langle z,\gamma\rangle}d\mu(z)\Big|=\big|\widehat{fd\mu}(\gamma)\big|.

Putting
f(\cdot-x)
into the definition of (p, q)-frame measure in the hypothesis, we obtain that
\nu
is a (p, q)-frame measure for

\mu
. The converse is similar. ∎

Theorem 2.13

\mu

to a subset of

\displaystyle\int_{G}f(x)\phi(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(x)dT_{a}(\mu|_{F+a})(x)=\int_{G}f(x-a)d\mu|_{F+a}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{F+a}f(x-a)d\mu(x).

Therefore the values of the functions

and
\phi
can be ignored outside

and
so we can assume
f
and
\phi
are supported on

\displaystyle\|g(\cdot-a)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mu)}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}|g(x-a)|^{p}d\mu(x)=\int_{F+a}|g(x-a)|^{p}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}|g(x)|^{p}\phi(x)d\mu(x).

\displaystyle\big|\widehat{g(\cdot-a)d\mu}(\gamma)\big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{G}g(x-a)\overline{\langle x,\gamma\rangle}d\mu(x)\Big|=\Big|\int_{G}g(x-a)\overline{\langle x-a,\gamma\rangle}d\mu(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt[q]{A}}{\sqrt[q]{B}}M^{1-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt[q]{A}M}{\sqrt[q]{B}\sqrt[p]{M}}\leq\frac{\sqrt[q]{A}\|Mg\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}}{\sqrt[q]{B}\|g(\cdot-a)\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}}\leq\frac{\|\widehat{(Mg)d\mu}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}}{\|\widehat{g(\cdot-a)d\mu}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\|\widehat{(Mg)d\mu}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}-\|\widehat{g(\cdot-a)d\mu}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}}{\|\widehat{g(\cdot-a)d\mu}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}}

(iv)

Suppose that
\sigma=\mu\ast\lambda
. Then for any
g\in G
,

\sigma(K_{\mu}+g)=0
.

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
\mu
and
\lambda
be two Borel probability measures without atoms on
G
and let
(\mu,\lambda)
be a
packing pair and
\sigma=\mu\ast\lambda
. Then for any
g\in G
, the measure
\rho_{g}=\sigma+\delta_{g}\ast\mu

does not admit any (p, q)-frame measure.

Proof.

We also can assume that
0\in K_{\lambda}
. In other words, for any open ball
V
centered around
0
, we have

\lambda(V)>0
. We now show that
\rho_{g}
does not admit any (p, q)-frame measure. Suppose on the
contrary that
\rho_{g}
admits a (p, q)-frame measure
\nu
with frame bounds
A,B
. Suppose that

\{U_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an arbitrary countable local base at
0
with
U_{n+1}\subset U_{n}
and

\lambda(U_{1})<\infty
. As
\lambda
has no atoms,
\lambda(U_{n})\to 0
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, take
V_{n}=K_{\mu}+(U_{n}\cap K_{\lambda})
.
Then by
V_{n}\subset K_{\sigma}
we have
\chi_{V_{n}}\in L^{p}(\sigma)
. Furthermore, by Theorem
2.17
we have
\sigma(K_{\mu}+g)=0
.
So, as a consequence of Theorem
2.3
and
2.11
,
\nu
is also a (p, q)-frame measure for
\sigma
and
\mu
with the same
frame bounds
A,B
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle A\sigma(V_{n})^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle=A\Big(\int_{G}|\chi_{V_{n}}(x)|^{p}d\sigma(x)\Big)^{\frac{q}{p}}\leq\int_{\widehat{G}}\big|\widehat{\chi_{V_{n}}d\sigma}(\gamma)\big|^{q}d\nu(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{G}}\big|\widehat{\chi_{K_{\mu}}d\mu}(\gamma)\big|^{q}\big|\widehat{\chi_{U_{n}}d\lambda}(\gamma)\big|^{q}d\nu(\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq\big(\lambda(U_{n})\big)^{q}\int_{\widehat{G}}\big|\widehat{\chi_{K_{\mu}}d\mu}(\gamma)\big|^{q}d\nu(\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq B\big(\lambda(U_{n})\big)^{q}\Big(\int_{G}|\chi_{K_{\mu}}(x)|^{p}d\mu(x)\Big)^{\frac{q}{p}}=B\big(\lambda(U_{n})\big)^{q}.

Now, by Theorem
2.17
we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma(V_{n})=\mu(K_{\mu})\lambda(U_{n}\cap K_{\lambda})=\lambda(U_{n}\cap K_{\lambda})=\lambda(U_{n}).

This implies that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\big(\lambda(U_{n})\big)^{q(1-\frac{1}{p})}}\leq\frac{B}{A}.

Finally, since
\lambda(U_{n})\rightarrow 0
, it follows that the left hand side of (
2.1
) can be made
arbitrarily large, while the right hand side remains bounded. This is a contradiction. ∎

3.
Perturbations of (p, q)-frame measures

The purpose of this section is to study the stability of (p, q)-frame measures. Similar to ordinary frames, (p, q)-frame measures
are stable under small perturbations. The following is an important result of the perturbation of operators that we will use in the
studying of the stability of frame measures.

Proposition 3.1
.

[
01
]

Let
X,Y
be two Banach spaces and let
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a linear operator. Assume that there exist constants

C,D\in[0;1[
such that

\displaystyle\|x-Ux\|\leq C\|x\|+D\|Ux\|,\hskip 19.91684pt\forall x\in X.

Then

U
is invertible and

\displaystyle\frac{1-C}{1+D}\|x\|\leq\|Ux\|\leq\frac{1+C}{1-D}\|x\|,\hskip 8.5359pt\frac{1-D}{1+C}\|x\|\leq\|U^{-1}x\|\leq\frac{1+D}{1-C}\|x\|,\hskip 8.5359pt\forall x\in X.

We start with the definition of a synthesis operator for a (p, q)-Bessel measure. To show that the inverse Fourier transform
appearing in this formula is well-defined, we need the next proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
and let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on LCA group
G
and
\nu
be a Borel measure on

\widehat{G}
. Suppose that
\nu
is a (p, q)-Bessel measure for
\mu
. Then for each
\varphi\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\nu)
, the
mapping
x\mapsto\widecheck{\varphi d\nu}(x)
is a well-defined from
G
to
\mathbb{C}
and
\widecheck{\varphi d\nu}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mu)
, where
p^{\prime},q^{\prime}
are the conjugate exponents for
p,q
, respectively.

Proof.

Assume that
\nu
is a (p, q)-Bessel measure for
\mu
with Bessel bound
B
. Define the operator

\displaystyle U:L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{q}(\nu),\hskip 28.45274ptU(f)=\widehat{fd\mu},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall f\in L^{p}(\mu).

Then
U
is a linear bounded operator with
\|U\|\leq\sqrt[q]{B}
, so the adjoint operator

\displaystyle U^{*}:L^{q^{\prime}}(\nu)\rightarrow L^{p^{\prime}}(\mu),

is also a linear bounded operator with
\|U^{*}\|\leq\sqrt[q]{B}
. In order to find the
expression for
U^{*}
let
\varphi\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\nu)
and
f\in L^{p}(\mu)
. Then by the Fubini’s theorem we have

\displaystyle U^{*}(\varphi)(f)

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{G}}\widehat{fd\mu}(\gamma)\overline{\varphi(\gamma)}d\nu(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{G}}\int_{G}\overline{\varphi(\gamma)}f(x)\overline{\langle x,\gamma\rangle}d\mu(x)d\nu(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(x)\overline{\int_{\widehat{G}}\varphi(\gamma)\langle x,\gamma\rangle d\nu(\gamma)}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(x)\overline{\widecheck{\varphi d\nu}(x)}d\mu(x).

Thus

\displaystyle\|\widecheck{\varphi d\nu}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mu)}=\|U^{*}(\varphi)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mu)}\leq\sqrt[q]{B}\|\varphi\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\nu)}.

∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Assume the conditions of Proposition
3.2

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{fd\mu},\varphi\rangle=\langle f,\widecheck{\varphi d\nu}\rangle,\hskip 14.22636pt\forall f\in L^{p}(\mu),\;\;\;\varphi\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\nu),

where
q^{\prime}
is the
conjugate exponent to
q
.

\displaystyle T_{\mu,\nu}:L^{q^{\prime}}(\nu)\rightarrow L^{p^{\prime}}(\mu),\hskip 28.45274ptT_{\mu,\nu}(\varphi)=\widecheck{\varphi d\nu}\quad\forall\varphi\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\nu),

p^{\prime},q^{\prime}
are the conjugate exponents for
p,q
, respectively.

The following results will provide us with concrete formulas for the (p, q)-Bessel/frame measure operators.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\nu
be a (p, q)-Bessel measure for
\mu
. Then
U^{*}_{\mu,\nu}=T_{\mu,\nu}
.

Corollary 3.7
.

•
Let
\nu
be a frame measure for
\mu
with frame bounds
A
and
B
. Then the operator
T_{\mu,\nu}T^{*}_{\mu,\nu}

is invertible and

\displaystyle A\;\textsl{Id}_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq T_{\mu,\nu}T^{*}_{\mu,\nu}\leq B\;\textsl{Id}_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

First, we derive the following result about the stability of frame measures under small perturbations of the associated Borel
measures on
\widehat{G}
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on LCA group
G
and
\nu,\rho
be two Borel measures on
\widehat{G}
, respectively.
Let
\nu
be a frame measure for
\mu
with frame bounds
A
and
B
and
S:L^{2}(\rho)\rightarrow L^{2}(\nu)
be a bounded
invertible operator. Choose
C,D,M\geq 0
such that
\max\big\{C+\frac{M}{\sqrt{A}\|S^{-1}\|^{-1}},D\big\}<1
. Further, let

(3.1)

\|\widecheck{S(\varphi)d\nu}-\widecheck{\varphi d\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq C\|\widecheck{S(\varphi)d\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+D\|\widecheck{\varphi d\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+M\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\rho)},

for all
\varphi\in L^{2}(\rho)
. Then
\rho
is a frame measure for
\mu
with frame bounds

\displaystyle A\|S^{-1}\|^{-2}\Big(1-\frac{C+D+\frac{M}{\sqrt{A}\|S^{-1}\|^{-1}}}{1+D}\Big)^{2}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046ptB\|S\|^{2}\Big(1+\frac{C+D+\frac{M}{\sqrt{B}\|S\|}}{1-D}\Big)^{2}.

Proof.

We first prove the upper frame bound. For each
\varphi\in L^{2}(\rho)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\widecheck{\varphi d\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widecheck{S(\varphi)d\nu}-\widecheck{\varphi d\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+\|\widecheck{S(\varphi)d\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C)\|\widecheck{S(\varphi)d\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+D\|\widecheck{\varphi d\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+M\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\rho)}.

So

\displaystyle\|\widecheck{\varphi d\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+C}{1-D}\|\widecheck{S(\varphi)d\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+\frac{M}{1-D}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\rho)}.

Since
\nu
is a frame measure for
\mu
, so the synthesis operator
T_{\mu,\nu}
is bounded and
\|T_{\mu,\nu}\|\leq\sqrt{B}
. Let
T_{\mu,\rho}:L^{2}(\rho)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
be the synthesis operator for
\mu
and
\rho
. Then we
have

\displaystyle\|T_{\mu,\rho}(\varphi)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+C}{1-D}\|T_{\mu,\nu}S(\varphi)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+\frac{M}{1-D}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1+C)\sqrt{B}\|S\|+M}{1-D}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\rho)}.

Hence

\displaystyle\|T_{\mu,\rho}(\varphi)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq B\|S\|^{2}\Big(1+\frac{C+D+\frac{M}{\sqrt{B}\|S\|}}{1-D}\Big)^{2}\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\rho)}.

This estimate shows that
\rho
is a Bessel measure for
\mu

with the upper bound

\displaystyle B\|S\|^{2}\Big(1+\frac{C+D+\frac{M}{\sqrt{B}\|S\|}}{1-D}\Big)^{2}.

In order to show the lower frame bound of
\rho
. Since
\nu
is a frame measure for
\mu
, the operator

T_{\mu,\nu}T^{*}_{\mu,\nu}
is invertible and
\|(T_{\mu,\nu}T^{*}_{\mu,\nu})^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{A}
. If we define
the operator
V:L^{2}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{2}(\nu)
by
V(f)=T^{*}_{\mu,\nu}(T_{\mu,\nu}T^{*}_{\mu,\nu})^{-1}(f)
, then

\|V\|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}
. Now take
\varphi=S^{-1}V(f)\in L^{2}(\rho)
. Then by (
3.1
) we have

\displaystyle\|f-T_{\mu,\rho}S^{-1}V(f)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle=\|T_{\mu,\nu}S(\varphi)-T_{\mu,\rho}(\varphi)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+D\|T_{\mu,\rho}S^{-1}V(f)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+M\|S^{-1}V(f)\|_{L^{2}(\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq\big(C+\frac{M}{\sqrt{A}\|S^{-1}\|^{-1}}\big)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+D\|T_{\mu,\rho}S^{-1}V(f)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

By Proposition
3.1
, the operator
T_{\mu,\rho}S^{-1}V
is invertible and

\displaystyle\|(T_{\mu,\rho}S^{-1}V)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1+D}{1-\big(C+\frac{M}{\sqrt{A}\|S^{-1}\|^{-1}}\big)}.

Finally, for each
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
we have

\displaystyle\|f\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle=|\langle f,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}|^{2}=|\langle T_{\mu,\rho}S^{-1}V(T_{\mu,\rho}S^{-1}V)^{-1}(f),f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\langle S^{-1}V(T_{\mu,\rho}S^{-1}V)^{-1}(f),\widehat{fd\mu}\rangle_{L^{2}(\rho)}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|S^{-1}\|^{2}}{A}\|(T_{\mu,\rho}S^{-1}V)^{-1}(f)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\rho)}\|\widehat{fd\mu}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|S^{-1}\|^{2}}{A}\Big(\frac{1+D}{1-\big(C+\frac{M}{\sqrt{A}\|S^{-1}\|^{-1}}\big)}\Big)^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}\|\widehat{fd\mu}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\rho)}.

Dividing both sides of this inequality by
\frac{\|S^{-1}\|^{2}}{A}\Big(\frac{1+D}{1-\big(C+\frac{M}{\sqrt{A}\|S^{-1}\|^{-1}}\big)}\Big)^{2}
completes the proof.
∎

The following theorem is about the stability of measures under small perturbations which have a (p, q)-frame measure.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mu,\lambda
be two finite Borel measures on LCA group
G
and
\nu
be a Borel measure on
\widehat{G}
, respectively.
Let
\nu
be a (p, q)-frame measure for
\mu
with frame bounds
A,B
, and
S:L^{p}(\lambda)\rightarrow L^{p}(\mu)

be a bounded invertible operator. Choose
C,D,M\geq 0
such that
\max\big\{C+\frac{M}{\sqrt{A}\|S^{-1}\|^{-1}},D\big\}<1
.
Further, let

(3.2)

\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}-\widehat{fd\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}\leq C\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}+D\|\widehat{fd\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}+M\|f\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)},

for all
f\in L^{p}(\lambda)
. Then
\nu
is a (p, q)-frame measure for
\lambda
with frame bounds

\displaystyle A\|S^{-1}\|^{-q}\Big(1-\frac{C+D+\frac{M}{\sqrt[q]{A}\|S^{-1}\|^{-1}}}{1+D}\Big)^{q}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046ptB\|S\|^{q}\Big(1+\frac{C+D+\frac{M}{\sqrt[q]{B}\|S\|}}{1-D}\Big)^{q}.

Proof.

We first prove the upper frame bound. For each
f\in L^{p}(\lambda)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\widehat{fd\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}-\widehat{fd\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}+\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C)\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}+D\|\widehat{fd\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}+M\|f\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}.

Hence

\displaystyle\|\widehat{fd\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+C}{1-D}\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}+\frac{M}{1-D}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+C}{1-D}\sqrt[q]{B}\|S\|\|f\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}+\frac{M}{1-D}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+C)\sqrt[q]{B}\|S\|+M}{1-D}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}.

So

\displaystyle\|\widehat{fd\lambda}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\nu)}

\displaystyle\leq B\|S\|^{q}\Big(1+\frac{C+D+\frac{M}{\sqrt[q]{B}\|S\|}}{1-D}\Big)^{q}\|f\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\lambda)}.

To prove the lower frame bound, we compute

\displaystyle\|\widehat{fd\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}

\displaystyle\geq\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}-\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}-\widehat{fd\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}

\displaystyle\geq(1-C)\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}-D\|\widehat{fd\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}-M\|f\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}.

Hence

\displaystyle(1+D)\|\widehat{fd\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}

\displaystyle\geq(1-C)\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}-M\|f\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\geq(1-C)\sqrt[q]{A}\|S(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}-M\|f\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\geq\big((1-C)\sqrt[q]{A}\|S^{-1}\|^{-1}-M\big)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\|\widehat{fd\lambda}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\nu)}

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\frac{(1-C)\sqrt[q]{A}\|S^{-1}\|^{-1}-M}{1+D}\Big)^{q}\|f\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\geq A\|S^{-1}\|^{-q}\Big(\frac{1-C-\frac{M}{\sqrt[q]{A}\|S^{-1}\|^{-1}}}{1+D}\Big)^{q}\|f\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\geq A\|S^{-1}\|^{-q}\Big(1-\frac{C+D+\frac{M}{\sqrt[q]{A}\|S^{-1}\|^{-1}}}{1+D}\Big)^{q}\|f\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\lambda)}.

∎

As a consequence of Theorem
3.9
, we have the following result.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
\mu,\lambda
be two finite Borel measures on
G
and let
\nu
be a (p, q)-frame measure for
\mu
with frame bounds

A,B
. Suppose that
S:L^{p}(\lambda)\rightarrow L^{p}(\mu)
is a bounded invertible operator. Choose
C,D,M\geq 0
such
that
\max\{C,D,M\}<1

\displaystyle\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}-\widehat{fd\lambda}\|^{2}_{L^{q}(\nu)}\leq C\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}\|^{2}_{L^{q}(\nu)}

\displaystyle+2D\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}\|\widehat{fd\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}

\displaystyle A\|S^{-1}\|^{-q}\Big(\frac{1-\sqrt{\eta}}{1+\sqrt{\eta}}\Big)^{q}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046ptB\|S\|^{q}\Big(\frac{1+\sqrt{\eta}}{1-\sqrt{\eta}}\Big)^{q}.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\mu,\lambda
be two finite Borel measures on LCA group
G

\nu

(3.3)

Proof.

\nu

\mu
and
\lambda

A,B
and

\displaystyle\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}-\widehat{fd\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}\leq\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt[q]{D}\|S^{-1}\|}{\sqrt[q]{A}}\Big)\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}.

\displaystyle\|\widehat{S(f)d\mu}-\widehat{fd\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}\leq\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt[q]{B}\|S\|}{\sqrt[q]{C}}\Big)\|\widehat{fd\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}.

\nu

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt[q]{A}\|S^{-1}\|^{-1}}{1+M}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}\leq\|\widehat{fd\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(\nu)}\leq\sqrt[q]{B}(1+M)\|S\|\|f\|_{L^{p}(\lambda)}.

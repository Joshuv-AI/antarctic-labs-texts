The Bootstrap for Dynamical Systems
Kasun Fernando, Nan Zou

Abstract
. Despite their deterministic nature, dynamical systems often exhibit seemingly random behaviour. Consequently, a dynamical system is usually represented by a probabilistic model of which the unknown parameters must be estimated using statistical methods. When measuring the uncertainty of such parameter estimation, the bootstrap stands out as a simple but powerful technique. In this paper, we develop the bootstrap for dynamical systems and establish not only its consistency but also its second-order efficiency via a novel continuous Edgeworth expansion for dynamical systems. This is the first time such continuous Edgeworth expansions have been studied. Moreover, we verify the theoretical results about the bootstrap using computer simulations.

–

–

\displaystyle\sigma_{\theta}

\displaystyle\mu

As before, due to
3.1

\lim_{\theta\to 0}\sigma^{2}_{\theta}=\lim_{\theta\to 0}-\lambda^{(2)}_{\theta}(0)=-\lambda^{(2)}_{0}(0)=\sigma^{2}_{0}.

\displaystyle\theta\mapsto\sigma_{\theta}

\displaystyle\theta=0

\displaystyle 0

Finally, taking the third derivative of (

\displaystyle s=0

i^{3}{\mathbb{E}}_{\mu}(S_{n,\theta}(h_{\theta}))^{3}=n[\lambda_{\theta}^{(3)}(0)+3\lambda^{(2)}_{\theta}(0)m(\Pi^{(1)}_{\theta}\rho_{\mu})]+m(\Pi^{(3)}_{\theta}\rho_{\mu})+m([\Lambda^{n}_{\theta}]^{(3)}\rho_{\mu})

\displaystyle\theta=0

4.
First-order Continuous Edgeworth Expansions

The derivation of the continuous Edgeworth expansion is an extension of the classical proof of the CLT due to Lévy. First, we obtain the asymptotic expansions for the characteristic functions,
\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(e^{isS_{n,\theta}(h_{\theta})})
, by writing their Taylor expansions. Due to (
2.6
) and (
3.14
), this expansion follows from the expansions of
\displaystyle(\lambda_{\theta}(is))^{n}
and
\displaystyle\Pi_{\theta,is}
, and the contribution from
\displaystyle\Lambda^{n}_{\theta,is}
is negligible. This is discussed in
Section

4.1
. Then, in
Section

4.2
, we use the expansion of the characteristic function,
\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(e^{isS_{n,0}(h_{0})})
, to obtain an expansion for the distribution functions. Even though we obtain just the first-order expansion here, it is uniform in the initial distribution
\displaystyle\mu
as well as in
\displaystyle\theta
, i.e., uniform for the family
\displaystyle g_{\theta}
, and hence, they are more general than the first order expansions in the current literature. This is precisely our theoretical contribution to the theory of Edgeworth expansions in dynamical systems.

Throughout the discussion, we let
\displaystyle\Omega\subset\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
be such that all
\displaystyle\mu\in\Omega
are absolutely continuous with density functions
\displaystyle\rho_{\mu}\in\mathcal{C}
and

\sup_{\mu}\|\rho_{\mu}\|_{\mathcal{C}}<\infty

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
is a space in (
3.3
) such that
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{3}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}^{(+)}_{3}
.

4.2.
Edgeworth expansions for distribution functions

5.3.
Markov models

In addition to dynamical systems, our continuous Edgeworth expansion result in
4.2
is also applicable in the Markovian setting. Several ideas in
[
23
,
63
,
33
,
22
]
give us conditions to establish first-order Edgeworth expansions under the optimal moment conditions of the iid case. In the discussion below, using Markov integral operators in place of transfer operators, we show that first-order continuous Edgeworth expansions hold for
\displaystyle V-
geometrically ergodic Markov processes, and hence, the bootstrap has the desired asymptotic accuracy in that setting.

Let
\displaystyle(X,\mathcal{F},m)
be a Borel probability space and
\displaystyle J\subset\mathbb{R}
be a neighbourhood of
\displaystyle 0
. Recall that
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
is the set of Borel probability measures on
\displaystyle X
. For each
\displaystyle\theta\in J
, let
\displaystyle\{x^{\theta}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be a time homogeneous Markov chain on
\displaystyle X
with initial measure
\displaystyle\mu_{\theta}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
and the Markov transition operator

\mathcal{L}_{\theta}(\varphi)(x)=\int_{X}\varphi(y)d\mathbf{P}_{\theta}(x,dy)={\mathbb{E}}(\varphi|x_{0}=x),\,\,\,\varphi\in L^{1}(X).

5.3.1.
\displaystyle V-
geometrically ergodic Markov chains

Let
\displaystyle V:X\to[1,\infty)
be a measurable function with

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{m}(V)<\infty
. Let
\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{V}}=\sup_{x\in X}|f(x)|/V(x)
for
\displaystyle f:X\to\mathbb{C}
, let

L^{\infty}_{V}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{f:X\to\mathbb{C}\Big|\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{V}}<\infty\right\},

and let
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}_{V},L^{\infty}_{V}}
be the operator norm. Now suppose
\displaystyle\{x^{0}_{n}\}
is irreducible and aperiodic. In addition, suppose
\displaystyle\{x^{0}_{n}\}
is
\displaystyle V-
geometrically ergodic, i.e., there exist
\displaystyle\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
,
\displaystyle C>0
and
\displaystyle\kappa\in(0,1)
such that
\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\nu}(V)<\infty
and

(5.7)

\|\mathcal{L}^{n}-{\mathbb{E}}_{\nu}[\,\cdot\,]{\bf 1}_{X}\|_{L^{\infty}_{V},L^{\infty}_{V}}\leq C\kappa^{n}.

As in
[
23
]
, we assume that

(i)

there exist
\displaystyle N,L>0
and
\displaystyle\kappa\in(0,1)
such that
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{N}_{\theta}V\leq\kappa^{N}V+L{\bf 1}_{X},\,\,\theta\in J
,

(ii)

\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to 0}\|\mathcal{L}_{\theta}-\mathcal{L}\|_{L^{\infty},L^{\infty}_{V}}=0.

Let
\displaystyle h_{\theta}\in L^{\infty}_{V},\,\theta\in J

\displaystyle h_{\theta}\to h_{0}
in
\displaystyle L^{\infty}_{V}
as
\displaystyle\theta\to 0
(often, this is vacuous in practice because we take
\displaystyle h_{\theta}=h_{0}
for all
\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle h_{\theta}\in L^{3}

for all
\displaystyle\theta

[

\sup_{a=1,2,3}\sup_{j=0,\dots,a}\|\mathcal{L}_{\theta}(h^{a-j}_{\theta}V^{j/3})\|_{L^{\infty}_{V^{a/3}}}<\infty.

It is not the case that there exist
\displaystyle a\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\nu-

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}-

\displaystyle k=\mathcal{O}(n^{1/6})
, we can obtain the best possible rate of convergence of the standard deviations,

|\sigma^{*}-\sigma|\lesssim|\sigma^{2}_{\theta}-\sigma^{2}_{0}|\lesssim n^{-1/6}.

So, in the case of piecewise expanding maps, we have the following improved asymptotic accuracy of the non-pivoted bootstrap.

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|{\mathbb{P}}_{\mu}(T_{n}\leq z)-{\mathbb{P}}(T^{*}_{n}\leq z\,|\,x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1})\Big|=\mathcal{O}_{\text{a.s.}}(n^{-1/6}).

)
:

As in the previous example, we assume that the mesh-size,
\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}

\displaystyle x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})

9.1.
Data generating processes

We let the choices of sample size be
\displaystyle n=25,50,100
and take the doubling map, the logistic map, and the drill map below as the choices for the transformation function
\displaystyle g
.

9.1.1.
Doubling map

The doubling map also called the dyadic transformation is given by

g(x)=2x\hskip-6.0pt\mod 1,\,\,\,\,\,x\in[0,1].

In general,
\displaystyle r
-adic transformations, including dyadic transformations, allow us to study the statistical properties of the digits of real numbers. Because of its simple yet chaotic nature, von Neumann proposed the doubling map as a random number generator
[
76
]
, and later, Rényi studied its statistical properties
[
1
]
. In fact, it is an expanding (therefore, hyperbolic) map, exhibits sensitive dependence on initial conditions, has a unique exponentially mixing acip – the Lebesgue measure
\displaystyle[0,1]
– and also, is a Bernoulli map. Further, it is a
\displaystyle C^{\infty}-
map of
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}
with constant first derivative
\displaystyle=2>1
, and hence, falls into the class of examples considered in
Section

5.1
.

One could also consider the doubling map as the following map of the binary expansions of data

(9.1)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}w_{j}2^{-j}\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}w_{j+1}2^{-j}.

In other words, the doubling map neglects the first binary digit but shift all the other digit to the left by one unit. Unfortunately, in computer software, the initial state
\displaystyle x_{0}
only has a binary expansion up to a finite order
\displaystyle K
, e.g.,
\displaystyle K=32
when
\displaystyle x_{0}
is a uniform random variable generated in R software; as a result of this round-off error, by (
9.1
), after
\displaystyle K
iterations the orbit of the simulated doubling map will always end up at zero
[
7
]
. To overcome this problem, we let
\displaystyle x_{i}=g(x_{i-1})+2^{-20}{\varepsilon}_{i}
, where
\displaystyle\{{\varepsilon}_{i}\}
are independent
Bernoulli
\displaystyle(1/2)
random variables conditional on
\displaystyle x_{i}\in[0,1]
. By the shadowing lemma, this perturbed orbit of
\displaystyle\{x_{i}\}
stays uniformly close to an unperturbed orbit; see
[
60
, p. 18]
.

9.1.2.
Drill map

The motion of a rotary drill induces a piecewise expanding map of the interval,
\displaystyle g:[0,1]\to[0,1]
, defined as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda}{(\Lambda-1)},\,\,\,q_{[\Lambda]-k}=\max\left\{0,\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\Lambda-1-k}{\Lambda-1}\right\},\ \ k=1,\dots,[\Lambda],

\displaystyle\displaystyle d_{\Lambda}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha\left(k-\sqrt{k^{2}-\frac{k}{\alpha}(k+1-2x)}\right),\ \ q_{[\Lambda]-k}<x\leq q_{[\Lambda]-k+1},\ k=1,2,\dots,[\Lambda],

\displaystyle\displaystyle g^{1/2}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=x+d_{\Lambda}(x)\ \ \text{(mod $\displaystyle 1$)},\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,g(x)=g^{1/2}(g^{1/2}(x)),

where
\displaystyle[\Lambda]
denote the integer part of
\displaystyle\Lambda
and
\displaystyle\Lambda
is a parameter indicating the influence of gravity on fluid motion. We set
\displaystyle\Lambda=3
in our simulation and include the corresponding transformation function
\displaystyle g

9.1.3.
Logistic map

The logistic map is useful as a discrete-time population model of various biological species; see
[
50
]
for an in-depth discussion. The logistic map with parameter
\displaystyle r\in(0,4]
is defined by

g(x)=rx(1-x),\,\,\,\,x\in[0,1].

Here, we focus on the case
\displaystyle r=4
. It is well-known that
\displaystyle g
with
\displaystyle r=4
exhibits sensitive dependency to initial conditions and has a unique acip (despite not being hyperbolic); see p. 34–35 of
[
60

.

Even though
\displaystyle g
does not belong to any of the examples in
Section

5
, its simulations results are includes here because they were comparable to those of the previous two examples. This is an indication that asymptotic accuracy of the bootstrap may hold even in the case of mostly hyperbolic maps considered in
[
79
]
. In fact, we believe that we can establish continuous Edgeworth expansions for such maps based on ideas in
[
22
]
. This will be pursued in the future.

Similar to the doubling map, the logistic map suffers from the round-off error of computer software. As a remedy, we first transform the logistic map to the tent map with
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
defined by
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(x)=2\arcsin(\sqrt{x})/\pi
and then add perturbation to the tent map; see p. 33 of
[
60
]
. Specifically, we let

\displaystyle\displaystyle y_{i-1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(x_{i-1}),

\displaystyle\displaystyle y_{i}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2y_{i-1}+2^{-20}{\varepsilon}_{i},&\text{for}\ x<1/2,\\
2(1-y_{i-1})+2^{-20}(1-{\varepsilon}_{i}),&\text{for}\ x\geq 1/2,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}^{-1}(y_{i})

where
\displaystyle\{{\varepsilon}_{i}\}
are independent
Bernoulli
\displaystyle(1/2)
random variables conditional on
\displaystyle y_{i}\in[0,1]
. By the shadowing lemma, as in the doubling map context, the perturbed orbit
\displaystyle\{y_{i}\}
stays uniformly close to some unperturbed orbit of the tent map, and in turn,
\displaystyle\{x_{i}\}
shadows an orbit of the logistic map.

9.4.1.
Gaussian approximation

To construct the confidence intervals in

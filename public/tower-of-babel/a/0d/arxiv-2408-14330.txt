On the cross-correlation of Golomb Costas permutations
Huaning Liu 1 and Arne Winterhof 2 1 Research Center for Number Theory and Its Applications School of Mathematics, Northwest University Xi’an 710127, China E-mail: hnliu@nwu.edu.cn 2 Johann Radon Institute for Computational and Applied Mathematics Austrian Academy of Sciences Altenbergerstr. 69, 4040 Linz, Austria E-mail: arne.winterhof@oeaw.ac.at

Abstract
In the most interesting case of safe prime powers q q , Gómez and Winterhof showed that a subfamily of the family of Golomb Costas permutations of { 1 , 2 , … , q − 2 } \{1,2,\ldots,q-2\} of size φ ⁡ ( q − 1 ) \varphi(q-1) has maximal cross-correlation of order of magnitude at most q 1 / 2 q^{1/2} . In this paper we study a larger family of Golomb Costas permutations and prove a weaker bound on its maximal cross-correlation. Considering the whole family of Golomb Costas permutations we show that large cross-correlations are very rare. Finally, we collect several conditions for a small cross-correlation of two Costas permutations. Our main tools are the Weil bound and the Szemerédi-Trotter theorem for finite fields. Key words: Costas arrays, cross-correlation, Golomb’s construction, Weil bound, Szemerédi-Trotter theorem, finite fields. MSC 2010: 05B15 (94A05).

Introduction

For a positive integer
n
, let
f
be a permutation of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
satisfying

f(i+k)-f(i)\neq f(j+k)-f(j)

for any integers
1\leq k\leq n-2
and
1\leq i<j\leq n-k
. Such a permutation is called
a
Costas permutation
of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. The binary
n\times n
-matrix
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
defined by
a_{ij}=1
if and only if
f(i)=j
is called a
Costas array
. Costas arrays have many important applications in
radar and sonar, see for example
[
6
,
9
,
10
]
.

The
cross-correlation

C_{f_{1},f_{2}}(u,v)
between two mappings

f_{1},f_{2}:\ \{1,2,\ldots,n\}\longrightarrow\{1,2,\ldots,n\}

at
(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
1-n\leq u,v\leq n-1
, is the number of solutions

x\in\left\{\max\{1,1-u\},\ldots,\min\{n,n-u\}\right\}

of the equation
f_{1}(x)+v=f_{2}(x+u)
. For a family
\mathcal{F}
of Costas permutations of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
with
n\geq 2
, the
maximal cross-correlation

C(\mathcal{F})
is

C(\mathcal{F})=\max_{1-n\leq u,v\leq n-1}\mathop{\max_{f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{F}}}_{f_{1}\neq f_{2}}C_{f_{1},f_{2}}(u,v).

In multi-user systems it is desirable that the signals have low cross-correlation, in order to minimize cross-talk. While Costas arrays guarantee ideal autocorrelation by definition, they typically exhibit poor cross-correlation. Families of Costas arrays with low cross-correlation are, therefore, particularly suitable for such applications, see for example
[
3
]
.

For a prime power
q>2
and primitive elements
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
of the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
let
\pi_{g_{1},g_{2}}
be the permutation of
\{1,\ldots,q-2\}
defined by

\pi_{g_{1},g_{2}}(x)=y\qquad\hbox{if and only if}\qquad g_{1}^{x}+g_{2}^{y}=1.

(1.1)

This construction of Costas permutations is due to Golomb, see

[
2
,
4
,
7
,
10
,

]
.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{q}
denote the set of primitive elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and define

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\pi_{g_{1},g_{2}}:\ g_{1}\in\mathcal{U}_{q}\right\}\quad\mbox{for fixed }g_{2}\in\mathcal{U}_{q}

\displaystyle\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{L}_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\pi_{g_{1},g_{2}}:\ g_{1}\in\mathcal{U}_{q},\ g_{2}\in\mathcal{U}_{q}\right\}.

It is easy to see that for

\pi_{g_{1},g_{2}}=\pi_{g_{3},g_{4}}\quad\mbox{if and only if}\quad(g_{3},g_{4})=(g_{1}^{p^{j}},g_{2}^{p^{j}})\quad\mbox{for some }j\in\{0,1,\ldots,w-1\}.

For the convenience of the reader we give a short proof of
(\ref{equiv})

We call

So we have

|\mathcal{G}_{q}|=\varphi(q-1)\gg\frac{q}{\log\log q}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad|\mathcal{L}_{q}|=\frac{\varphi(q-1)^{2}}{w}\gg\frac{q^{2}}{w(\log\log q)^{2}},

where

denotes Euler’s totient function and
F(N)\gg G(N)
if and only if

for some absolute constant

For a prime power

\frac{q-1}{2}

=\frac{q-1}{t}-1&\hbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

[

is larger than

for some small values of

does not depend on the choice of

in the definition of

Note that

C(\mathcal{L}_{q})=\max_{0\leq u,v\leq q-3}\max_{f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{q}\atop f_{1}\not=f_{2}}C_{f_{1},f_{2}}(u,v)

Now take another primitive element

In particular, if

In this case we have either

•

q
is a

, that is,
\frac{q-1}{2}
is a (

) prime,

•

is a

•

or
q

, that is,

\frac{q-1}{2}
is a prime by

We focus on these three cases
since otherwise the bound on
C({\cal G}_{q})
is larger as well as the size of
{\cal L}_{q}
is smaller (in terms of
q
)
. By a slight abuse of notation we call all such

The maximal cross-correlation
of
\mathcal{L}_{q}

C(\mathcal{L}_{q})\leq\mathop{\max_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}}_{1\leq r,s\leq q-2,\ \gcd(rs,q-1)=1}\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:\ \alpha(1-x)^{r}+\beta x^{s}-1{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}=0}\right\}\right|,

Still finding larger subfamilies of
\mathcal{L}_{q}

\mathcal{G}_{q}
with non-trivial cross-correlation of size at most
q^{1-\varepsilon}

Our first result is a weaker bound than
(\ref{CGq})
but for a larger subfamily of the family of Golomb Costas permutations.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q>7
be a safe prime power. Fix
0<\delta<1/2
.
Then there exists a subfamily
\mathcal{L}_{q,\delta}
of
\mathcal{L}_{q}
of size
|\mathcal{L}_{q,\delta}|\gg\delta q\log q
with cross-correlation
C(\mathcal{L}_{q,\delta})=O\left(q^{1/2+\delta}\right)
.

We prove
(a slightly more precise version of)
Theorem
1.1
in Section
3

in a constructive way
.

Write

g_{3}=g_{1}^{r}\quad\mbox{and}\quad g_{4}=g_{2}^{s}\quad\mbox{with}\quad 1\leq r,s<q-1\mbox{ and }\gcd(rs,q-1)=1.

Theorem
1.1
is based on

C_{\pi_{g_{1},g_{2}},\pi_{g_{3},g_{4}}}(u,v)\leq 1+\min\{s,s^{-1}\}q^{1/2}\quad\mbox{if $(g_{1},g_{2})$ and $(g_{3},g_{4})$ are not conjugates},

(1.4)

where
s^{-1}
denotes the inverse of
s
modulo
q-1
, that is,
1\leq s^{-1}\leq q-2
,
and as the bound of
[
8
]
on the Weil bound, which is optimal for some polynomials.

Our second result states that for any fixed pair of Golomb-Costas permutations the proportion of
(u,v)
with large cross-correlation at
(u,v)
is very small.

For a fixed pair
(u,v)
we also prove a similar result on the number of quadruples
(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4})
with large cross-correlation
C_{\pi_{g_{1},g_{2}},\pi_{g_{3},g_{4}}}(u,v)
.

More precisely, the following theorem suggests that
the number of possibly large cross-correlations is small in both cases, for fixed
(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{3})
and for fixed
(u,v)
, respectively. The proof depends on an analog of the Szemerédi-Trotter theorem for finite fields, that is, a point-line incidence bound. It seems that this technique has not been used for studying cross-correlations of Costas arrays yet.

Theorem 1.2
.

1. Let
\pi_{g_{1},g_{2}},\pi_{g_{3},g_{4}}\in\mathcal{L}_{q}
,

B>0
,
{\cal S}\subset\{0,1,\ldots,q-3\}^{2}
,
and let

N_{q,B,{\cal S}}
be the number of
(u,v)\in{\cal S}
with

C_{\pi_{g_{1},g_{2}},\pi_{g_{3},g_{4}}}(u,v)\geq B.

Then we have for any
q
,

N_{q,B,{\cal S}}\ll\frac{1}{B}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}|{\cal S}|^{1/2}q,&|{\cal S}|\geq q,\\
|{\cal S}|q^{1/2},&q^{1/2}\leq|{\cal S}|<q,\\
q,&|{\cal S}|<q^{1/2}.\end{array}\right.

and for prime
q
the improvement,

N_{q,B,{\cal S}}\ll\frac{(|{\cal S}|q)^{11/15}}{B}\quad\mbox{if }q^{7/8}<|{\cal S}|<q^{8/7},\quad q\mbox{ prime}.

2. Let
u,v\in\{0,1,\ldots,q-3\}
,
B>0
and let
M_{q,B}
be the number of

(\pi_{g_{1},g_{2}},\pi_{g_{3},g_{4}})\in\mathcal{L}_{q}^{2}
such that

C_{\pi_{g_{1},g_{2}},\pi_{g_{3},g_{4}}}(u,v)\geq B.

Then we have

M_{q,B}<\frac{\tau(q-1)\varphi(q-1)^{3}(q-1)}{B}\ll\frac{q^{4+o(1)}}{B},

where
\tau(n)
is the number of positive divisors of

and
f(n)=o(1)
if
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}f(n)=0
.

Although for multi-user systems it would be nice to have small cross-correlation for all
(u,v)
it may be still sufficient to allow some exceptions which appear with rather small probability, which is made explicit in the first part of Theorem
1.2
. Similarly, cross-talk is limited even if, for fixed
(u,v)
, exceptions of pairs of Costas arrays with large cross-correlation occur only with small probability, which motivates the second part.

We prove Theorem
1.2
in Section

\displaystyle\sum_{(u,v)\in{\cal S}}\left|\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:g_{4}^{v}(1-x)^{s}+g_{1}^{ru}x^{r}=1\}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I(P,L)

and the first result follows from
(\ref{Equation:incidence bound})

and
(\ref{Equation:incidence bound2})
.

Moreover, for fixed
(u,v)\in\{0,1,\ldots,q-3\}^{2}
we get from
[
4
, Theorem 2]
that

\displaystyle BM_{q,B}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\sum_{\pi_{g_{1},g_{2}},\pi_{g_{3},g_{4}}\in\mathcal{L}_{q}}}C_{\pi_{g_{1},g_{2}},\pi_{g_{3},g_{4}}}(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathcal{U}_{q}}\mathop{\sum_{s=1}^{q-1}}_{\gcd(s,q-1)=1}\mathop{\sum_{r=1}^{q-1}}_{\gcd(r,q-1)=1}\left|\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\setminus\{1\}:g_{1}^{ru}x^{r}=1-g_{2}^{sv}(1-x)^{s}\}\right|.

Now for a fixed triple
(g_{1},g_{2},s)
we estimate the inner sum over
r
, that is, we have to estimate the number of pairs
(r,x)

with

(g_{1}^{u}x)^{r}=1-(g_{2}^{v}(1-x))^{s}.

Note that
x\mapsto g_{1}^{u}x
is a bijection of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and that for each divisor
d
of
q-1
there are
\varphi\left(\frac{q-1}{d}\right)
different elements
x
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
of the form
x=g^{j}
with
j=0,1,\ldots,q-2
and
\gcd(j,q-1)=d
, where
g
is any fixed primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Now assume that

g_{1}^{u}x=g^{j}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\gcd(j,q-1)=d

and define
k
by

1-(g_{2}^{v}(1-x))^{s}=g^{k},\quad 1\leq k\leq q-2.

We have to estimate the number of
r
with
g^{jr}=g^{k}
, that is,

jr\equiv k\bmod q-1.

There is no solution if
d
does not divide
k
and otherwise we get

\frac{j}{d}r\equiv\frac{k}{d}\bmod\frac{q-1}{d}

which has a unique solution
r
modulo
\frac{q-1}{d}
and thus
d
solutions
r
with
1\leq r\leq q-1
.
Hence, the number of pairs
(r,x)
is at most

\sum_{d|q-1}\varphi\left(\frac{q-1}{d}\right)d<\tau(q-1)(q-1).

Therefore

\displaystyle BM_{q,B}<\tau(q-1)(q-1)\varphi(q-1)^{3}

and the second result follows.

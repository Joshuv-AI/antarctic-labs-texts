Abstract
. We study the main open parts of the Kawaguchi–Silverman Conjecture, asserting that for a birational self-map f f of a smooth projective variety X X defined over ℚ ¯ {\overline{\mathbb{Q}}} , the arithmetic degree α f ​ ( x ) \alpha_{f}(x) exists and coincides with the first dynamical degree δ f \delta_{f} for any ℚ ¯ \overline{{\mathbb{Q}}} -point x x of X X with a Zariski dense orbit. Among other results, we show that this holds when X X has Kodaira dimension zero and irregularity q ⁡ ( X ) ≥ dim X − 1 q(X)\geq\dim X-1 or X X is an irregular threefold (modulo one possible exception). We also study the existence of Zariski dense orbits, with explicit examples.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Kawaguchi–Silverman Conjecture

Let
X
be a smooth complex projective variety of dimension
d
, and let
f:X\dashrightarrow X
be a dominant rational self-map.
The
first dynamical degree
of
f
, defined by the limit

(1.1)

\delta_{f}\mathrel{:=}\lim_{n\to\infty}((f^{n})^{*}H\cdot H^{d-1})^{\frac{1}{n}},

where
H
is an ample divisor,
is a real number
\delta_{f}\geq 1
which measures the complexity of
f
. We refer to §
2
for the definition of the pullback of a divisor under a dominant rational map.
The existence of this limit is proved in the seminal work of Dinh and Sibony
[
DS05
]
,
and is independent of the choice of
H
(see also
[
Tr15
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
Tr20
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Now, assume that the variety
X
and the map
f:X\dashrightarrow X
are both defined over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
We then have another way of measuring the complexity of
f
,
by considering the growth of the Weil heights of the iterations of
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
-points under
f
.
Such an arithmetic approach was
first conceived and studied by Silverman (
[
Si91
]
, see also
[
CS93
]
),
but many fundamental questions,
including the existence of the so-called
arithmetic dynamical degree
,
still remain open.
A series of conjectural statements in this regard were formulated by
Kawaguchi and Silverman
[
KS16b
, Conjecture 6]
, which we now recall.

Throughout the paper,

{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
denotes the semi-group of rational dominant maps
f:X\dashrightarrow X
over its field of definition,
and
{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)\subset{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
the subgroup of birational maps.
For every
f\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
,
let
U(f)
be the largest Zariski open subset of
X
such that

f_{|U(f)}
is a morphism,
and define

X_{f}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}):=\{x\in X({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})\,|\,f^{n}(x)\in U(f)\,\text{ for all }n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\}.

Let
H
be an ample divisor on
X
and let
h_{H}\geq 1

be a height function associated to
H
(see e.g.
[
HS00
, Part B, Theorem B.3.6]
).

Conjecture 1.1

(Kawaguchi–Silverman Conjecture (KSC))
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety defined over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and
let
f\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
.
Then:

(1)

The limit

a_{f}(x):=\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{H}(f^{n}(x))^{\frac{1}{n}}

exists for any
x\in X_{f}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
and the value is an algebraic integer.

(2)

For any
x\in X_{f}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
whose orbit

{{\rm Orb}_{f}(x)}:=\{f^{n}(x)\,|\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\}

under
f
is Zariski dense in
X
, we have

a_{f}(x)=\delta_{f}.

Remark 1.2
.

It is worth noticing that
\delta_{f}
is always an algebraic integer
when
f
is a morphism (see e.g.
[
KS16b
, Remark 35]
),
but there exist examples of transcendental

\delta_{f}
when
f
is not a morphism,
by recent striking results
[
BDJ20
]
and
[
BDJK21
]
.
Therefore Conjecture
1.1
needs to be corrected,
and very likely
a_{f}(x)
could be a transcendental number as well.

When the limit
a_{f}(x)
in KSC (1) exists,
the value
a_{f}(x)
is called the
arithmetic degree
of
f
at
x
.
While KSC (1) is unknown,
we can always define

\overline{a}_{f}(x):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}h_{H}(f^{n}(x))^{\frac{1}{n}}\geq\underline{a}_{f}(x):=\liminf_{n\to\infty}h_{H}(f^{n}(x))^{\frac{1}{n}}\geq 1;

these are called the
upper arithmetic degree
and
lower arithmetic degree
of
f
at

x\in X_{f}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
respectively.
It is known that
\overline{a}_{f}(x)
and
\underline{a}_{f}(x)

do not depend on the choice of an ample divisor
H

[
KS16b
, Proposition 12]
.

KSC (1) is affirmative when
f
is a morphism
[
KS16a
, Theorem 3]
,
but very little is known in general, even when
f
is birational.
KSC (2) is open even when
f
is a morphism.
The only general result we know so far is that

(1.2)

\delta_{f}\geq\overline{a}_{f}(x)

for any
f\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
and
x\in X_{f}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
,
due to Kawaguchi and Silverman
[
KS16a
, Theorem 4]

and Matsuzawa
[
Ma20
, Theorem 1.4]
.
We also know that
a
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
-point
x\in X({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
such that
a_{f}(x)=\delta_{f}
exists
when
f:X\to X
is a surjective morphism,
due to Matsuzawa, Sano and Shibata
[
MSS18
, Corollary 9.3]
.
More remarkably, they prove that
such
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
-points
x
are Zariski dense in
X
.
See e.g.
[
LS21
, Introduction]
for the current status of KSC (2).

1.2.
The existence of Zariski dense orbits

The statement KSC (2) is meaningful only when
there exists
x\in X_{f}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
such that
{{\rm Orb}_{f}(x)}
is Zariski dense in
X
,
otherwise we say that
KSC (2) vacuously holds
.
The existence of such
x\in X_{f}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})

seems to be less studied in the context of KSC.

Question 1.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}

and let
f\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
be a self-map of infinite order.
When does
f
have Zariski dense
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
-orbits?
Namely, when does

\mathcal{Z}(f)\mathrel{:=}\Set{x\in X_{f}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})}{{{\rm Orb}_{f}(x)}\text{ is Zariski dense in }X}\neq\emptyset?

In Question
1.3
, we have an affirmative answer
if we replace the Zariski density of
{{\rm Orb}_{f}(x)}
by the weaker property that

{{\rm Orb}_{f}(x)}
is infinite:
in
[
Am11
]
,
Amerik even showed that the subset

\Set{x\in X_{f}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})}{{|{{\rm Orb}_{f}(x)}|}=\infty}

(and in particular
X_{f}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
)
is Zariski dense in
X
.
When
X
is a smooth projective surface and
f
is birational with
\delta_{f}>1
,
the existence of Zariski dense orbit is proved by Xie
[
Xi15
, Theorem 1.3]
(See also
[
Xi25
]
).

Directly related to Question
1.3

is the so-called Zariski Dense Orbit conjecture,
which would provide
a geometric characterization of
f\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
having Zariski dense
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
-orbits.

Conjecture 1.4

(See e.g.
[
JSXZ21
, Conjecture 1.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and let
f\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
be a self-map of infinite order. Then
\mathcal{Z}(f)\neq\emptyset

as long as there is no non-constant rational function

g
on
X
such that
g\circ f=g
.

Conjecture
1.4
is known to hold for abelian varieties
[
GS17
]
.
We will not say much about Conjecture
1.4
in this work (apart from Remark
4.4
);
for a more general formulation, history and the current status of Conjecture
1.4

see e.g.
[
JSXZ21
, Introduction]
.

In the negative direction,
a result due to Nakayama and Zhang (see Theorem
3.4
) implies that
when
\kappa(X)\geq 1
, a dominant rational self-map
f:X\dashrightarrow X
never has Zariski dense orbits in
X
.

1.3.
Main results

The aim of this paper is to study Conjecture
1.1
(2) and Question
1.3
for

irregular

\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
,

We will prove the following three theorems.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let

2.
Notation

In this paper, we fix an algebraic closure
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
of the field of rational numbers
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Unless stated otherwise, all varieties and rational maps among them are defined over
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
For an abelian group
A
and a field
K
, we denote by
A_{K}
the
K
-vector space
A\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}K
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety defined over
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
For any Cartier divisor

, we denote by

h_{D}:X({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})\to{\mathbb{R}}

the
logarithmic Weil height function
associated to
D
.
The function
h_{D}
is unique up to bounded functions on

.
When
H
is an ample divisor, we may and will choose

so that
h_{H}\geq 1
on
X({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
.
The reader is referred to
[
HS00
, Part B, Theorem B.3.6]
for the basic properties on
h_{D}
and the Weil height machinery.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety over a field
\mathbf{k}
.
Recall the definition of
{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
and
{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
from the introduction.
The subsets of (regular) endomorphisms and automorphisms of
X
are denoted by

{\rm End\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)\subset{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
and
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)\subset{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
respectively.

Let
f:X\dasharrow Y
be a dominant rational map to a projective variety
Y
. We set

I(f):=X\setminus U(f),

where
U(f)
is the largest Zariski open subset of
X
on which
f
is defined as a morphism to
Y
. We call
I(f)
the indeterminacy set of
f
. Since
X
is normal and
Y
is projective,
I(f)
is of codimension at least
2
in
X
.

Assume that
X
is smooth. Let

(2.1)

X\xleftarrow{p}\widetilde{X}\xrightarrow{q}Y

be the resolution of
f
by normalizing the graph of
f
.
For any Cartier divisor
D
on
Y
, define

f^{*}D\mathrel{:=}p_{*}q^{*}D

which is a Cartier divisor.
It is easy to see that if we replace
\widetilde{X}
in (

) by any other normal projective variety birational to

,

The Kodaira dimension of a smooth projective variety

.

3.
Some general lemmas

In this section, we prove some elementary lemmas
which we will use in the rest of this article.

3.1.
First dynamical degree

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varphi:X\dasharrow B
be a dominant rational map from a smooth projective variety
X
to a smooth projective variety
B
such that
\dim B=\dim X
or
\dim B=\dim X-1
. Let
f\in{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
. Assume that there exists
f_{B}\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(B)
such that
f_{B}\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ f
.
Then necessarily
f_{B}\in{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(B)
and
\delta_{f}=\delta_{f_{B}}
.

Proof.

The first assertion is clear as
f\in{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
.
As the first dynamical degree is a birational invariant
[
DS05
, Corollaire 7]
, we may assume that
\varphi
is a surjective morphism by taking a projective resolution of the rational map
f
.
By the product formula for the relative dynamical degree (
[
DNT12
]
,
[
Tr15
, Theorem 1.4]
), we have

\delta_{f}=d_{1}(f)=\max(d_{1}(f|\varphi)d_{0}(f_{B}),d_{0}(f|\varphi)d_{1}(f)).

Here
d_{k}(f|\varphi)
denotes the relative
k
-th dynamical degree of
f
(
[
DNT12
]
,
[
Tr15
, Theorem 1.4]
).
Since
f
is birational and
\dim B=\dim X
or
\dim X-1
, it follows from the definition of
d_{k}(f|\varphi)
that

d_{1}(f|\varphi)=d_{0}(f|\varphi)=1.

Since
d_{1}(f_{B})\geq 1=d_{0}(f_{B})
(for any
f_{B}\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(B)
), it follows that

\delta_{f}=\max(d_{0}(f_{B}),d_{1}(f_{B}))=d_{1}(f_{B})=\delta_{f_{B}}.

∎

3.2.
Zariski density of orbits

Recall that

\mathcal{Z}(f)\mathrel{:=}\Set{x\in X_{f}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})}{{{\rm Orb}_{f}(x)}\text{ is Zariski dense in }X}

for every rational dominant self-map
f:X\dashrightarrow X
of a smooth projective variety

X
over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and let
f\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
. For any
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
,
we have

\mathcal{Z}(f)=\mathcal{Z}(f^{N})\cap X_{f}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})

Proof.

This follows from

\mathrm{Orb}_{f}(x)=\cup_{k=0}^{N-1}f^{k}(\mathrm{Orb}_{f^{N}}(x))

for any
x\in X_{f}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\pi:X\dashrightarrow Y
be a dominant rational map between irreducible varieties.
Let
f\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
and
g\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(Y)
such that

\pi\circ f=g\circ\pi.

Let
x\in\mathcal{Z}(f)
.
Then

\Set{\pi\circ f^{k}(x)\in Y}{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\text{ and }f^{k}(x)\notin I(\pi)}

is dense in
Y
.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that

\Set{\pi\circ f^{k}(x)\in Y}{f^{k}(x)\notin I(\pi)}

is contained
in a proper Zariski closed subset
Z\subsetneq Y
.
Since
\pi\circ f=g\circ\pi
, we would thus have

{{\rm Orb}_{f}(x)}\subset\{\pi^{-1}(Z)\cup I(\pi)\}\subsetneq X,

where
\pi^{-1}(Z)
denotes the closure of

\pi^{-1}(Z\cap\pi(X\backslash I(\pi)))
in
X
, contradicting the assumption that
x\in\mathcal{Z}(f)
.
∎

The following result due to Nakayama–Zhang for
f\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
(see
[
NZ09
, Theorem A]
)
is fundamental,
which is a generalization of the result of Nakamura-Ueno-Deligne for
f\in{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
(see
[
Ue75
, Theorem 14.10]
):

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and
f\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
.
Let

\phi_{mK_{X}}:X\dasharrow B\subset|mK_{X}|^{*}

be the pluricanonical map of
X
.
Then
f
descends to an automorphism of
B
of finite order.
In particular, if the Kodaira dimension of
X
is positive, i.e., if
\kappa(X)\geq 1
, then
{\mathcal{Z}}(f)=\emptyset
.

Remark 3.5
.

Theorem
3.4
and some other results that we will cite are formulated and proved over the complex number field

Lemma 3.6

Let

), dominant rational maps
\varphi_{m}:X_{m}\dasharrow X_{m-1}
(
2\leq m\leq d
) and
f_{m}\in{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(X_{m})
such that
\varphi_{m}\circ f_{m}=f_{m-1}\circ\varphi_{m}
for all
2\leq m\leq d
.
Then
\delta_{f}=1
and KSC (1) and (2) hold for
(X,f)
.

Proof.

Since
\dim X_{m}-\dim X_{m-1}=1
, by Lemma
3.1
,
we have
\delta_{f_{m}}=\delta_{f_{m-1}}

for all

. Moreover
\delta_{f_{1}}=1
as

is a smooth projective curve and
f_{1}\in{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(X_{1})={\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X_{1})
. Hence
\delta_{f_{m}}=1
for all

\delta_{f}=1
and the result follows from Lemma

.
∎

The following lemma is similar to
[

, Theorem 3.4 (i)]
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\nu:X\dashrightarrow Y
be a birational map between smooth projective varieties.
Let
f\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
and
g\in{\rm End\hskip 0.43057pt}(Y)
such that
g\circ\nu=\nu\circ f
.
Let
x\in\mathcal{Z}(f)
.
Then

\overline{a}_{f}(x)\geq{a}_{g}(\nu(x))\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \underline{a}_{f}(x)\leq{a}_{g}(\nu(x)).

If moreover
f^{k}(x)\not\in I(\nu)
for all but finitely many
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}

(e.g. when
\nu
is a morphism),
then

\underline{a}_{f}(x)={a}_{g}(\nu(x)).

Proof.

As
\nu
is a birational map, we may choose a Zariski dense open subset
U
of
X

such that
\nu|_{U}:U\to\nu(U)
is an isomorphism.
Since
{{\rm Orb}_{f}(x)}
is Zariski dense in
X
,
there exists a strictly increasing sequence

\{n_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
of positive integers
such that
\{f^{n_{k}}(x)\}_{k\geq 1}\subset U
.

By
[
MSS18
, Lemma 3.3]
,
for an ample Cartier divisor
H_{X}
(resp.
H_{Y}
) on
X
(resp.
Y
), there are positive constants
M_{1}
,
L_{1}
and constants
M_{2}
and
L_{2}
such that

M_{1}h_{H_{Y}}(\nu(x))+M_{2}\leq h_{H_{X}}(x)\leq L_{1}h_{H_{Y}}(\nu(x))+L_{2}

for all

.
In particular, this implies that

(3.1)

\begin{split}\overline{a}_{f}(x)&\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}h_{H_{X}}(f^{n_{k}}(x))^{\frac{1}{n_{k}}}\\
&=\limsup_{k\to\infty}h_{H_{Y}}(g^{n_{k}}(\nu(x)))^{\frac{1}{n_{k}}}={a}_{g}(\nu(x)).\end{split}

Here for the last equality, we used the fact that KSC (1) holds
for any endomorphism of a
(normal) projective variety
[
KS16a
, Theorem 3]
.
Similarly, we have
\underline{a}_{f}(x)\leq{a}_{g}(\nu(x))
.

Now we prove the second statement.
Since

\Sigma\mathrel{:=}\Set{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}{f^{k}(x)\in I(\nu)}

is finite by assumption, we have

\underline{a}_{f}(x)=\liminf_{k\to\infty}h_{H_{X}}(f^{k}(x))^{\frac{1}{k}}=\liminf_{k\to\infty,k\notin\Sigma}h_{H_{X}}(f^{k}(x))^{\frac{1}{k}},

\underline{a}_{g}(\nu(x))=\liminf_{k\to\infty}h_{H_{Y}}(g^{k}(\nu(x)))^{\frac{1}{k}}=\liminf_{k\to\infty,k\notin\Sigma}h_{H_{Y}}(g^{k}(\nu(x)))^{\frac{1}{k}}.

By
[
MSS18
, Lemma 3.3]
, there exist constants
M
,
M^{\prime}
with
M>0
such that for any
k\notin\Sigma
,

h_{H_{X}}(f^{k}(x))\geq Mh_{H_{Y}}(\nu(f^{k}(x)))+M^{\prime}.

It follows that

\underline{a}_{f}(x)=\liminf_{k\to\infty,k\notin\Sigma}h_{H_{X}}(f^{k}(x))^{\frac{1}{k}}\geq\liminf_{k\to\infty,k\notin\Sigma}h_{H_{Y}}(g^{k}(\nu(x)))^{\frac{1}{k}}=\underline{a}_{g}(\nu(x))={a}_{g}(\nu(x)),

where the last equality follows from
[
KS16a
, Theorem 3]
as
g\in{\rm End\hskip 0.43057pt}(Y)
.
Hence
\underline{a}_{f}(x)={a}_{g}(\nu(x))
.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and
f\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
.
Let
\nu:X\dashrightarrow Y
be a birational map to a smooth projective variety
Y
.
Assume that there is
g\in{\rm End\hskip 0.43057pt}(Y)
such that
g\circ\nu=\nu\circ f
.
Finally, assume that for every

,
we have
f^{k}(x)\not\in I(\nu)
for all but finitely many

.
(Note that the last assumption always holds if
\nu
is a morphism.)

If KSC (2) holds for
(Y,g)
, then the KSC (2) also holds for
(X,f)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{Z}(f)
.
Replacing
x
by some
f^{k}(x)
,
we can assume that
x\notin I(\nu)
. Since
g\circ\nu=\nu\circ f
,
it follows from Lemma
3.3
that
\nu(x)\in{\mathcal{Z}}(g)
.
Suppose that KSC (2)
holds for

, then

.
We thus have the chain of inequalities

\delta_{f}\geq\overline{a}_{f}(x)\geq\underline{a}_{f}(x)=a_{g}(\nu(x))=\delta_{g}=\delta_{f},

where
\delta_{f}\geq\overline{a}_{f}(x)
follows from (
1.2
),

\underline{a}_{f}(x)=a_{g}(\nu(x))
follows from Lemma

, and
\delta_{f}=\delta_{g}

DS05
, Corollaire 7]
.
Thus the limit
a_{f}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{H}(f^{n}(x))^{\frac{1}{n}}

∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\varphi:X\to B
be a surjective morphism between smooth projective varieties
with
\dim X-\dim B\leq 1
. Let
f\in{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
. Assume that there exists
f_{B}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(B)
such that
\varphi\circ f=f_{B}\circ\varphi
.
If KSC (2) holds for
(B,f_{B})
, then the KSC (2) also holds for
(X,f)
.

Proof.

By the assumption
\dim X-\dim B\leq 1
and by Lemma
3.1
, it follows that
\delta_{f}=\delta_{f_{B}}
. If
x\in{\mathcal{Z}}(f)
, then

\varphi(x)\in{\mathcal{Z}}(f_{B})
as well. Then by the assumption, we have
a_{f_{B}}(\varphi(x))=\delta_{f_{B}}
. On the other hand, for an ample divisor
H_{B}
on
B
, by the functoriality of the height function (see e.g.
[
HS00
, Part B, Theorem B.3.6]
), we have

h_{\varphi^{*}H_{B}}(f^{n}(x))=h_{H_{B}}(\varphi(f^{n}(x)))+O(1)=h_{H_{B}}(f_{B}^{n}(\varphi(x)))+O(1).

Combining this with the definition of
\underline{a}_{f}(x)
, we obtain
\underline{a}_{f}(x)\geq\underline{a}_{f_{B}}(\varphi(x))=\delta_{f_{B}}=\delta_{f}
.
By (
1.2
), we get as before

\delta_{f}\geq\overline{a}_{f}(x)\geq\underline{a}_{f}(x)\geq\delta_{f}.

Hence
a_{f}(x)
exists and satisfies

.
∎

4.
irregular varieties with
\kappa=0
and
q=\dim X-1

In this section, we study irregular projective varieties with Kodaira dimension
\kappa=\kappa(X)=0
and
q=\dim X-1
,
in arbitrary dimension. Our main result in this section is the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
{\mathbb{C}}
,
and let
f\in{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
.
Suppose that
\kappa(X)=0
and
q(X)=\dim X-1
.
Then
f
does not have any Zariski dense orbit over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Note that Proposition
4.1
is a result over
{\mathbb{C}}
,
and is therefore applicable in both complex dynamics and algebraic dynamics
(when everything is defined over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
or more generally, over any field of characteristic zero).
Theorem
1.5
(2) then follows immediately,
and we will complete the proof of Theorem
1.5

at the end of this section.

To prove Proposition
4.1
, we start with the following two lemmas.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{C}}
and let
f:E\to E
be an automorphism.
Then
f
has infinite order if and only if
f
is a translation by a non-torsion point.

Proof.

Lemma
4.2
is clear if
f
is a translation.
Assume that
f
is not a translation, then
f-\mathrm{Id}_{E}:E\to E
is not constant.
Fix an origin
o
of
E
.
There exists thus
x_{0}\in E
such that
f(x_{0})-x_{0}=o
,
namely
f(x_{0})=x_{0}
.
If we choose
x_{0}
as the new origin of
E
, then
f
becomes a group automorphism of the elliptic curve
E
,
hence
f
has finite order.
∎

For every projective variety
X
,
let
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}^{0}(X)
denote the identity component of
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
.
When
A
is an abelian variety,
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}^{0}(A)
is the group of translations of
A
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety and
E
an elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
g\mathrel{:=}(g_{A},g_{E})\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}^{0}(A)\times{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(E)
.
Let
f\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(A\times E)
such that
f\circ g=g\circ f
and that

f
descends to
f_{A}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(A)

through the projection
\mathrm{pr}_{1}:A\times E\to A
.
Assume that
g
has finite order and
g_{E}
is not a translation on
E
.
Then
f
has no Zariski dense orbit over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

Since
g_{E}
is not a translation,
it has a fixed point
o\in E
.
Let
o\in E
be the origin of
E
, so that
g_{E}:E\to E
is a group automorphism.

For every
a\in A
, define

f_{a}:E=\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{-1}(a)\xrightarrow{f}\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{-1}(f_{A}(a))=E.

There exist a group automorphism
h\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}_{o}(E)
and a morphism
t:A\to E

such that

f_{a}(x)=h(x)+t(a)

for every
x\in E
.
Here we note that since

{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}_{o}(E)
is finite,
h\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}_{o}(E)
does not depend on
a\in A
.
Since
h\circ g_{E}=g_{E}\circ h
(because
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}_{o}(E)\subset{\rm GL}(1,{\mathbb{C}})={\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
is abelian for a complex elliptic curve
E
; see also
[
Ha77
, Chapter IV, Corollary 4.7]
for a more precise description),
the condition
f\circ g=g\circ f
implies

(4.1)

t(g_{A}(a))=g_{E}(t(a)).

It follows that
t:A\to E
is constant, because otherwise
t
is surjective,
and that
g_{A}
is a translation together with (
4.1
)
implies that
g_{E}
is also a translation, which contradicts the assumption.
Thus
\tau\mathrel{:=}t(a)\in E
and
f_{1}\mathrel{:=}f_{a}:E\to E
are independent of
a\in A
.

Since
g_{E}
is not a translation, it has only finitely many fixed points.
As
g_{E}
is a group automorphism,

g_{E}(\ell\cdot\tau)=\ell\cdot\tau
for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. So
\tau
is torsion,
which implies that
f_{1}
has finite order by Lemma
4.2
.
Thus for every
(x,y)\in A\times E
, we then have

(4.2)

\mathrm{Orb}_{f}(x,y)\subset A\times\Set{f_{1}^{k}(y)}{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset A\times E,

which shows that
\mathrm{Orb}_{f}(x,y)
is not Zariski dense in
Y\times E
because

\Set{f_{1}^{k}(y)}{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is finite.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Let
\alpha:X\to A
be the Albanese map of
X

and let
\gamma:A\to A
be the automorphism induced by
f
,
namely the one which satisfies
\alpha\circ f=\gamma\circ\alpha
.

Assume to the contrary that there exists
x\in X
with Zariski dense
f
-orbit.
Since
\kappa(X)=0
and
q(X)=\dim X-1
,
by
[
Vi81
, Corollary 9.4]
there exists an elliptic fiber bundle
X^{\prime}\to A

such that
X
is birational to
X^{\prime}
over
A
.
Since
X^{\prime}
has no rational curves, we have
a birational morphism
\nu:X\to X^{\prime}
over
A
,
and
f:X\dashrightarrow X
descends to an automorphism
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}
.
By Lemma
3.3
, the
f^{\prime}
-orbit of
y\mathrel{:=}\nu(x)
is Zariski dense in
X^{\prime}
.

Let
E
be a fiber of the elliptic fiber bundle
X^{\prime}\to A
.
By
[
Le82
, Proposition 1]
,
there exists a finite étale Galois cover
\widetilde{A}\to A
of Galois group
G

together with a homomorphism
\psi:G\to{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(E)

such that

(\widetilde{A}\times E)/G\simeq X^{\prime}

over
A
; here the
G
-action on
\widetilde{A}\times E

is diagonal, through the covering action on
\widetilde{A}

and the action induced by

respectively.
It follows from
[
DLOZ
, Lemma 5.2]

that up to replacing

(so
f^{\prime}
) by some finite iteration, which is allowed thanks to Lemma

, the automorphism
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}
lifts to
an automorphism
\tilde{f}:\widetilde{A}\times E\to\widetilde{A}\times E
.
Consider the group homomorphism

(4.3)

\begin{split}{\mathbb{Z}}&\to{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(G)\\
k&\mapsto(g\mapsto\tilde{f}^{k}g\tilde{f}^{-k}).\end{split}

Since
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(G)
is finite, there exists
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\tilde{f}^{N}g\tilde{f}^{-N}=g
for all
g\in G
.
Therefore up to further replacing
f
by
f^{N}
,
we can assume that
\tilde{f}
commutes with the
G
-action.

The
G
-action on

does not purely consist of translations.
Indeed, if
G
acts on

by translations,
then

is an elliptic curve and
we have a dominant map

onto an abelian variety of dimension equal to

,
which contradicts
q(X)=\dim X-1
.
Let
g\in G
such that
\psi(g)\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(E)
is not a translation.
We use the same notation
g
to denote the corresponding automorphism in
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\widetilde{A}\times E)
.
Since
\tilde{f}:\widetilde{A}\times E\to\widetilde{A}\times E
commutes with the
G
-action,
we have
\tilde{f}\circ g=g\circ\tilde{f}
.
Thus
\tilde{f}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\widetilde{A}\times E)
and

satisfy all the assumptions in Lemma

,
which implies that
\tilde{f}
has no Zariski dense orbit.
As
\tilde{f}
is a lifting of
f^{\prime}
and
f^{\prime}
is birational to
f
,
this contradicts the assumption that

\mathrm{Orb}_{f}(x)
is Zariski dense.
∎

.

5.
KSC for irregular threefolds

The following result for endomorphisms was proven in
[
LM21
, Proposition 3.7]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
{\mathbb{C}}
and let

f\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
.
If
f
has a Zariski dense orbit,
then the Albanese map
\alpha:X\to{\rm Alb\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)

is surjective.

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha
is not surjective.
By
[
Ue75
, Theorems 10.3, 10.9]
, the Albanese image

B
is then an étale torus fiber bundle (possibly of relative dimension
0
)
over a projective variety
B^{\prime}
of general type with
\dim B^{\prime}>0
.
The rational map
f
descends to a rational dominant map
f_{B}
on
B
.
Since
B\to B^{\prime}
is the Iitaka fibration of
B
,
the map
f_{B}
further descends to a rational dominant map
f_{B^{\prime}}
on
B^{\prime}
.
As
B^{\prime}
is of general type,
f_{B^{\prime}}
is of finite order by
[
KO75
, Theorem 1]
.
This implies that
f
has no Zariski dense orbit.
∎

We have the following immediate corollary of Proposition
5.1
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let X be a smooth projective variety over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
If the Albanese map
\alpha:X\to{\rm Alb\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)

is not surjective, then KSC (2) holds vacuously for any
f\in{\rm Rat\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
.

In the rest of this section, we prove Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective threefold with
q(X)>0
. By Theorem
3.4
,
we can assume that
\kappa(X)=0
or
-\infty
. Theorem
1.6
(1) and (2) will follow from
Corollary
5.2
, Theorem
1.5
, together with
Propositions
5.4
and
5.6
below.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that
\kappa(X)=0
and
q(X)>0
.
Then the minimal model
Y
of
X
is smooth with

{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(Y)={\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(Y).

Proof.

Since
\dim X=3
and
\kappa(X)=0
, it follows that
X
has a minimal model,
i.e., a normal projective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial variety with only terminal (in particular, isolated) singularities.
Any minimal model
Y
of
X
satisfies
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(mK_{Y})\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}

for some

by the abundance theorem for minimal threefolds (See e.g.
[
Ka92
]
and references therein).
Moreover, the Albanese map
\alpha:Y\to{\rm Alb\hskip 0.43057pt}(Y)

is a surjective étale fiber bundle by
[

, Theorem 8.3]
.
Since

has only isolated singularities and

,
every fiber

of

is smooth. As

is torsion, it follows that

is torsion as well by adjunction.
In particular,
Y
is smooth and contains no isolated rational curve. Indeed, since
\alpha:Y\to{\rm Alb\hskip 0.43057pt}(Y)
is a surjective étale fiber bundle over an elliptic curve

as observed above, every rational curve on

is in a fiber and it also deforms in the family

.
Therefore

has no flop, so the minimal model of

is unique and

{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(Y)={\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(Y)
by
[

, Theorem 1]
.
∎

.

Assume that

.

.
Since

and

and

.
In particular, if

Let

be the birational map to the
a minimal model

is smooth by Lemma
5.3
.

Claim 5.5
.

The Albanese image
\alpha(I(\nu))
of the indeterminacy locus
I(\nu)\subset X
is finite.

We give two proofs of Claim
5.5
,
both of which will be applicable for other problems.

First proof of Claim
5.5
.

By the universal property of the Albanese map,
\alpha
factors through the birational map
\nu:X\dashrightarrow Y
.
Consider the restriction
\nu_{\mathbf{k}(E)}:X_{\mathbf{k}(E)}\dashrightarrow Y_{\mathbf{k}(E)}
of

\nu
where
X_{\mathbf{k}(E)}
and
Y_{\mathbf{k}(E)}
denote the generic fibers of

X
and
Y
over
E
respectively.
Since
Y
is a smooth threefold, it follows that

Y_{\mathbf{k}(E)}
is a smooth surface.
As
K_{Y}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linearly trivial, so is
K_{Y_{\mathbf{k}(E)}}
.
It follows that
\nu_{\mathbf{k}(E)}
is a birational map between smooth
\mathbf{k}(E)
-surfaces
with
Y_{\mathbf{k}(E)}
being minimal,
so
\nu_{\mathbf{k}(E)}
is a morphism
[
IS96
, Corollary 1 in II.7.3]
. Hence
I(\nu)\subset X
does not dominate
E
, which proves Claim
5.5
.
∎

Second proof of Claim
5.5
.

The minimal model program consists of a sequence of divisorial contractions and flips.
By the universal property of the Albanese variety,
these birational modifications are defined over
E
.
Let
X_{i}\dashrightarrow X_{i}^{+}
be a flip in the program and let
C_{i}
be the flipping curve.
Then one has

\alpha(I(\nu))\subset\bigcup_{i}\alpha_{i}(C_{i})

where
\alpha_{i}:X_{i}\to E
is the Albanese map
[
Ka85
, Lemma 8.1]
.
As each
\alpha_{i}(C_{i})\subset E
is finite (because
C_{i}
is a union of finitely many
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
) and there are only finitely many flips in the minimal model program,
\alpha(I(\nu))
is thus finite.
This proves Claim
5.5
.
∎

For every
x\in\mathcal{Z}(f)
,
it follows that
f^{k}(x)\not\in I(\nu)
for all but finitely many
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.
Indeed, if the contrary holds, then, since
\alpha(I(\nu))
is finite,
there exist integers
k_{1}<k_{2}
such that
\alpha(f^{k_{1}}(x))=\alpha(f^{k_{2}}(x))\in\alpha(I(\nu))
.
It would follow that
\alpha(f^{k_{1}+m}(x))=\alpha(f^{k_{2}+m}(x))
for all
m\geq 0
and hence for any
k\geq k_{2}
,
\alpha(f^{k}(x))=\alpha(f^{l}(x))
for some
\ell\leq k_{2}
. Thus

\mathrm{Orb}_{f}(f^{k}(x))\subset\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{k_{2}}\alpha^{-1}(\alpha(f^{\ell}(x))),

which contradicts the Zariski density of
{{\rm Orb}_{f}(x)}
.

Hence we can apply Lemma
3.10
and reduce Proposition
5.4
to
KSC (2) for
(Y,f_{Y})
with
f_{Y}\mathrel{:=}\nu\circ f\circ\nu^{-1}
.
The latter follows from
[
LS21
, Corollary 1.5]
(or rather its proof):
indeed,
f_{Y}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(Y)
by Lemma
5.3

and the Bogomolov decomposition of an étale cover of

has no Calabi-Yau threefold as its irreducible factor so that we may argue as in the proof of
[
LS21
, Corollary 1.5]
.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Assume that
\kappa(X)=-\infty
and
q(X)>0
. Then

(1)

KSC (2) holds for any
f\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
.

(2)

Moreover, KSC (2) holds for any
f\in{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
unless
X
is covered by rational surfaces.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.2
, we can assume that the Albanese map

\alpha:X\to B:={\rm Alb\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)

is surjective.

First we prove (2).
Since
\kappa(X)=-\infty
and
q(X)>0
, we have
\dim B=1
or
2
.
Suppose first that
B
is an abelian surface. Note that any
f\in{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
descends to a
f_{B}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(B)
,
and KSC (2) holds for automorphisms on smooth surface
B={\rm Alb\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
by
[
KS14
, Theorem 2 (c)]
.
Applying Lemma
3.11
to the surjective morphism
\alpha:X\to B
yields KSC (2) for
X
.

Now we treat the case where
B
is an elliptic curve.
Since
\kappa(X)=-\infty
, the variety
X
is covered by rational curves
(as the consequence of the minimal model program and
the abundance theorem for threefolds,
see e.g.
[
Ka92
]
and references therein).
Since
B
is an elliptic curve, a general fiber
X_{t}
of
\alpha:X\to B
is thus uniruled, i.e., covered by rational curves. As poined out by the referee, one can also conclude the uniruledness of
X_{t}
by using the addition formula
[
Ka82
]
.
Note that
q(X_{t})
is constant in
t
for smooth fibers
X_{t}
.

Suppose first that
q(X_{t})=0
for a general fiber
X_{t}
. Then

X_{t}
is a rational surface by the classification of surfaces.
In particular,
X
is covered by rational surfaces,
which is excluded from the assertion (2).

Thus we may assume that
q(X_{t})=q>0
for a general fiber
X_{t}
. Then
q(X_{\eta})=q>0
as well for the generic fiber
X_{\eta}
. Let
S_{\eta}
be the image of the Albanese map of
X_{\eta}
over
\eta={\rm Spec}\,{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}(B)
.
Then by taking a model of
X_{\eta}\to S_{\eta}\to{\rm Spec}\,{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}(B)
over
B
, we have dominant rational maps

X\dasharrow S\dasharrow B

which are
{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
-equivariant with
X
and
S
being smooth.
Thus
\delta_{f}=1
and KSC (2) holds for all
f\in{\rm Bir\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
by Lemma
3.8
.

It remains to show that KSC (2) holds for
f\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
for the case excluded in (2), namely when
X
is covered by rational surfaces.
If
\delta_{f}=1
, then KSC (2) holds by Lemma
3.6
. So we may and will assume that
\delta_{f}>1
.
Moreover, we may and will assume that the induced automorphism
f_{B}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(B)
is of infinite order, otherwise
f_{B}
, and thus
f:X\to X
, have no Zariski dense orbit.
Hence
f_{B}
is a translation automorphism
t_{s}:x\mapsto x+s
of infinite order by Lemma
4.2
.
Since
\alpha:X\to B
has at most finitely many singular fibers and

\alpha\circ f=f_{B}\circ\alpha
, necessarily

\alpha:X\to B
is a smooth morphism.

Now we apply the classification result of
Lesieutre
[
Le18
, Theorem 1.7]
to describe
f\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
.
First of all, if
f\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
is not dynamically minimal (see
[
Le18
, Definition 1.2]
for the definition),
then there exists a divisorial contraction
X\to Y
such that
f
descends to an automorphism
f_{Y}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(Y)
.
Since
\alpha_{Y}\circ f_{Y}=f_{B}\circ\alpha_{Y}
where

\alpha_{Y}:Y\to B
is the Albanese map,
the same argument as above shows that
Y
is smooth.
Repeating the same procedure until it stops (as the Picard number strictly decreases after each divisorial contraction),
we obtain a birational morphism
X\to X^{\prime}
to a smooth projective variety
such that
f\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
descends to
f^{\prime}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X^{\prime})
and that
f^{\prime}
is dynamically minimal.

As
f^{\prime}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X^{\prime})
is dynamically minimal and
\kappa(X)=-\infty
,
by
[
Le18
, Theorem 1.7]

X^{\prime}
, and thus
X
, must have either a conic bundle structure
\pi:X\to V
or a dominant rational map
\pi:X\dasharrow S
to a surface
S
. Moreover, there exists
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
such that
f^{n}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
descends to a birational self-map on
V
in the first case,
and
f^{n}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
descends to a (biregular) automorphism on
S
in the second case.
We may and will assume that
S
is a smooth projective surface by taking an equivariant resolution of singularities.

If
X
has a conic bundle
\pi:X\to V
, then the Albanese map
\alpha:X\to B
factors through
V
, because
B
is an elliptic curve.
Thus
\delta_{f^{n}}=1
by Lemma
3.8
.
Hence KSC (2) holds for
f^{n}
by Lemma
3.6
and thus for
f
by Lemma
3.7
.

Assume that
X
has a dominant rational map
\pi:X\dasharrow S
to a smooth projective surface
S

such that
f^{n}
descends to an automorphism
f_{S}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(S)
.
Let
Z
be the graph of
\pi
in
X\times S
. Then we have an automorphism
f_{Z}
of
Z
induced by
f^{n}\times f_{S}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X\times S)
. Let
W
be an equivariant projective resolution of singularities of
Z
. Then there is
F_{W}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(W)
such that the induced birational morphism
W\to X
is equivariant with respect to
F_{W}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(W)
and
f^{n}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
, and also the induced morphism
W\to S
is equivariant with respect to
F_{W}
and
f_{S}
.

Then KSC (2) holds for
F_{W}
by Lemma
3.11
, as it holds for automorphisms of smooth projective surfaces by
[
KS14
, Theorem 2 (c)]
.
So KSC (2) holds for
f^{n}
by
[
LS21
, Corollary 3.2]
, and thus for
f
by Lemma
3.7
.
This completes the proof of the assertion (2).
∎

(1) and (2) follow from

, Theorem

, together with

and

It remains to show Theorem

Let

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

. Assume that the characteristic polynomial
\Phi(f)
of

satisfies the property that if

x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}

and

.

\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}

Assume to the contrary that

\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
-orbits.
Then by
[
GS17
, Theorem 1.2]
, there exists a dominant rational map

such that
p\circ f=p
.
Such

is defined by a linear system
\Lambda

.
Since

for every

f^{*}:H^{1,1}(E_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})\circlearrowleft

Since

and

We will need the notion of

We call a real algebraic integer

are in the open unit disk

[
Og19

for summary what we need and
[
BDGPS92
]

Let

and

later we may and will even assume that

Let

.

Proposition 6.2
.

We have
\delta_{f_{m}}=a_{m}^{2}>1

Proof.

For the first statement, see
[

. Then since

Now assume that

Let

be the roots of

By construction we have

and

If

∎

Let

be an integer. If

From now on, we assume that

is a Pisot unit in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[m]{2})

Set

By

.

Let

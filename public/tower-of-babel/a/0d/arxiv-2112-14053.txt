From Semantics to Types: the Case of the Imperative λ \lambda -Calculus
Ugo de’Liguoro, Riccardo Treglia

Abstract
We propose an intersection type system for an imperative λ \lambda -calculus based on a state monad and equipped with algebraic operations to read and write to the store. The system is derived by solving a suitable domain equation in the category of ω \omega -algebraic lattices; the solution consists of a filter-model generalizing the well known construction for ordinary λ \lambda -calculus. Then the type system is obtained out of the term interpretations into the filter-model itself. The so obtained type system satisfies the “type-semantics” property, and it is sound and complete by construction.

Introduction

Since Strachey and Scott’s work in the 60’s,
\lambda
-calculus and denotational semantics, together with logic and type theory, have been recognized
as the mathematical foundations of programming languages. Nonetheless, there are aspects of actual programming languages that have shown to be
quite hard to treat, at least with the same elegance as the theory of recursive functions and of algebraic data structures; a prominent case is surely
side-effects.

The introduction of notions of computation as monads by Moggi in
[
23
]
greatly improved the understanding of “impure”, namely non functional
features in the semantics of programming languages, by providing a unified framework for treating computational effects: see
[
27
,
28
]
,
the introductory
[
9
]
, and the large body of bibliography thereafter; a gentle introduction and references can be found e.g. in
[
18
]
.

Starting with
[
15
]
, we have considered an untyped variant of Moggi’s computational
\lambda
-calculus, with two sorts of terms:
values
denoting points of
some domain
D
, and
computations
denoting points of
TD
, where
T
is some generic monad and
D\cong D\xrightarrow{}TD
. The goal was
to show how such a calculus can be equipped with an operational semantics and an intersection type system, such that types are invariant under reduction and expansion
of computation terms, and convergent computations are characterized by having non-trivial types in the system.

Our approach was limited to a pure calculus without constants, where the unit and bind operations are axiomatized by the monadic laws from
[
28
]
.
On the other hand, such operations may
model how morphisms from values to computations compose, but do not tell anything
about how the computational effects are produced.
In the theory of
algebraic effects

[
24
,
25
,
26
]
, Plotkin and Power have shown under which conditions effect operators
live in the category of algebras of a computational monad, which is isomorphic to the category of models of certain equational specifications,
namely varieties in the sense of universal algebra
[
20
]
.

Toward a general theory of intersection type systems for (untyped) computational
\lambda
-calculi with algebraic effects, we have studied in the draft

[
16
]
the case of the imperative
\lambda
-calculus
\lambda_{imp}
, both because the modeling of a functional calculus with (implicitly higher-order)
side-effects is of interest per se, and because it is a case study to test our claim that an intersection types assignment system can be defined and that it is useful to formalize
reasoning about the calculus itself.

Indeed the type system, which we present in the very last section of this work, has not been invented from scratch; on the contrary, it has been constructed moving from the
domain equation
D=D\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{S}D
, where
\mathbb{S}
is (a variant of) the state monad. The method we follow is to solve such equation in the category of
\omega
-algebraic lattices, whose
objects and morphisms can be described via intersection type theories, that are the “logic” of such domains in the sense of
[
2
]
: see also

[
4
]
.
The solution is constructed via the description of compact points of such a
D
, implicitly mirroring the inverse limit construction, but in a conceptually simpler way, consisting
of an inductive definition of types and of their preorders.
Once this has been obtained, subtyping and typing rules are derived in a uniform way, leading to a type assignment system which enjoys the
“type-semantics” property, namely that denotational semantics of terms is fully characterized by their typings in the system.
Soundness and completeness of the type system now follow straightforwardly.

Summary and results
. In section

In section

Section

We conclude establishing the type semantics theorem for our calculus. Finally,

An untyped imperative
\lambda
-calculus:
\lambda_{imp}

Imperative extensions of the
\lambda
-calculus, both typed and type-free, are usually
based on the call-by-value
\lambda

]
, we consider the calculus introduced in
[

]
, to which
we add syntax denoting algebraic effect operations à la Plotkin and Power
[

,
25
,

]

over a suitable state monad.

Let

. Borrowing notation from

\textit{get}_{\ell}

Definition 2.1
.

The syntax of the untyped imperative
\lambda
-calculus
\lambda_{imp}
has two sorts of terms, which are defined by mutual induction as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rrcl@{\hspace{1cm}}l}\textit{Val}:&V,W&::=&x\mid\lambda x.M\hfil\hskip 28.45274pt\\
\textit{Com}:&M,N&::=&[V]\mid M\star V\mid\textit{get}_{\ell}(\lambda x.M)\mid\textit{set}_{\ell}(V,M)\hfil\hskip 28.45274pt&(\ell\in\mathbb{L})\\

Terms are of either sorts

and
Com
, representing values and computations, respectively.
The variable

is bound in
\lambda x.M
and
\textit{get}_{\ell}(\lambda x.M)
;
terms are identified up to renaming of bound variables so that the capture avoiding substitution

is always well defined;

We call
\textit{Val}^{\,0}

\textit{Com}^{0}

Terms of the shape
[V]
and

are written
\texttt{return }V

in Haskell-like syntax, respectively.
With respect to
[
15
]
we write
[V]
instead of
\mbox{\it unit}\;V
.
With respect to the syntax of the “imperative
\lambda
-calculus” in
[
18
]
,
we do not have the

These constructs are indeed definable:
\mbox{{let} $x=M$ {in} $N$}\;\equiv\;M\star(\lambda x.N)

In general, application among computations can be encoded by

In a sugared notation from functional programming languages, we could have written location dereferentiation and assignment, respectively, as follows:

\mbox{{let} $x=\;!\ell$ {in} $M$}~\equiv~\textit{get}_{\ell}(\lambda x.M)\qquad\ell:=V;M~\equiv~\textit{set}_{\ell}(V,M)

Observe that we do not consider locations as
values; consequently they cannot be dynamically created like with the

operator from ML, nor
is it possible to model aliasing. On the other hand, since the calculus is untyped, “strong updates” are allowed (differently than
[
10
]
). By this, we mean that when evaluating
\textit{set}_{\ell}(V,M)
,
the value
V
to which the location
\ell
is updated bears no relation
to the previous value, say
W

\ell
in the store: indeed, in our type assignment system

and
V
may well have completely different types.

Denotational semantics

We illustrate a denotational semantics of the calculus in the category of domains.
The model is based on the solution of the domain equation:

D=[D\xrightarrow{}[S\xrightarrow{}(D\times S)_{\bot}]]

(1)

where
S
is a suitable space of stores over

\mathbb{S}\,D=[S\xrightarrow{}(D\times S)_{\bot}]

[

]

Before embarking on solving the equation (

The partiality and state monad
.
We recall Wadler’s type-theoretic definition of monads
[

28

in Haskell language, a natural interpretation of the calculus is into a cartesian closed category (ccc), such that
two families of combinators, or a pair of polymorphic operators called the “unit” and the “bind”, exist satisfying the

In what follows,
\mathcal{C}

concrete ccc

of Scott domains with continuous functions, and

\omega\text{-}\textrm{\bf ALG}
of algebraic lattices with a countable basis.

Definition 3.1
.

(Computational Monad
[
28
]
§3)

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a concrete ccc. A functional computational monad, henceforth just monad
over

is a triple
(T,\mbox{\it unit}\;\!,\star)
where

is a map over the objects of
\mathcal{C}
, and

and

\begin{array}[]{c@{\hspace{2cm}}c}\mbox{\it unit}_{A}:A\xrightarrow{}TA\hfil\hskip 56.9055pt&\star_{A,B}:TA\times(TB)^{A}\xrightarrow{}TB\end{array}

such that, writing
\star_{A,B}
as an infix operator and omitting subscripts:

\begin{array}[]{l@{\hspace{0.5cm}}rcl@{\hspace{0.5cm}}l}\textit{Left unit}:\hfil\hskip 14.22636pt&\mbox{\it unit}\;a\star f&=&f\,a\hfil\hskip 14.22636pt\\
\textit{Right unit}:\hfil\hskip 14.22636pt&m\star\mbox{\it unit}&=&m\hfil\hskip 14.22636pt\\
\textit{Assoc}:\hfil\hskip 14.22636pt&(m\star f)\star g&=&m\star\mathop{\hbox to0.0pt{$\lambda$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu\raisebox{1.18399pt}{$\lambda$}}d.(f\,d\star g)\hfil\hskip 14.22636pt\end{array}

where

\mathop{\hbox to0.0pt{$\lambda$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu\raisebox{1.18399pt}{$\lambda$}}d.(f\,d\star g)

.

Remark 3.2
.

In case of concrete ccc, Def.
3.1
coincides with the notion of Kleisli triple
(T,\eta,\_^{\dagger})
(see e.g.
[
23
]
, Def. 1.2).
The equivalence is obtained by relating maps

\_^{\dagger}
to

\star

\mbox{\it unit}_{X}=\eta_{X}\qquad\qquad a\star f=f^{\dagger}(a)

The first equality is just a notational variation of the coercion of values into computations. The latter, instead, establishes the connection between the
\star

, also called extension operator: if

.

Now, let us look closer how monads model effectful computations. We focus on
Dom
,
a full subcategory of pointed Dcpo’s with Scott continuous functions as morphisms.
Then

The mapping

Now a function

.

.

(Partiality and state monad)

Given the domain

A function

Algebraic operations over
\mathbb{S}
.
Monads are about composition of morphisms of some special kind.
However, thinking of
f:X\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{S}X
as the meaning of a program
with side effects doesn’t tell anything about side effects themselves, that are produced by reading and writing
values from and to stores in
S
.

To model effects associated to a monad, Plotkin and Power have proposed in
[
24
,
25
,
26
]

a theory of
algebraic operations
. A gentle introduction to the theory can be found in

[
18
]
, chap. 3, from which we borrow the notation.

Suppose that
T
is a monad over a category
\mathcal{C}
with
terminal object
1
and all finite products; then an algebraic operation
op
with arity
n
is a family of
morphisms
\textbf{op}_{X}:(TX)^{n}\xrightarrow{}TX
, where
(TX)^{n}=TX\times\cdots\times TX
is the
n
-times product of
TX
with itself, such that

\textbf{op}_{X}\circ\Pi_{n}f^{\dagger}=f^{\dagger}\circ\textbf{op}_{X}

(3)

where
f:X\xrightarrow{}TX
and
\Pi_{n}f^{\dagger}=f^{\dagger}\times\cdots\times f^{\dagger}:(TX)^{n}\xrightarrow{}(TX)^{n}
.
In case of the concrete category
Dom
,
1
is a singleton and products are sets of tuples.
Then
\textbf{op}_{X}
is an operation of arity
n
of an algebra with carrier
TX
; since
f^{\dagger}:TX\xrightarrow{}TX
,
we have that
(\Pi_{n}f^{\dagger})\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle=\langle f^{\dagger}(x_{1}),\ldots,f^{\dagger}(x_{n})\rangle
, and
Equation

3

reads as:

\textbf{op}_{X}(f^{\dagger}(x_{1}),\ldots,f^{\dagger}(x_{n}))=f^{\dagger}(\textbf{op}_{X}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}))

namely the functions
f^{\dagger}
are homomorphisms w.r.t.
\textbf{op}_{X}
.

Algebraic operations
\textbf{op}_{X}:(TX)^{n}\xrightarrow{}TX
do suffice in case
T
is, say, the nondeterminism or the output monad, but cannot
model side effects operations in case of the store monad. This is because read and write operations implement a bidirectional action
of stores to programs and of programs to stores. What is needed instead is the generalized notion of operation proposed in
[
24
]
(and further studied in
[
19
]
); such construction,
that is carried out in a suitable enriched category, can be instantiated to the case of

as follows:

\textbf{op}:P\times(TX)^{A}\xrightarrow{}TX\cong(TX)^{A}\xrightarrow{}(TX)^{P}

where
P
is the domain of parameters and

of generalized arities, and the rightmost “type” is the interpretation in

of Def. 1 in
[
24
]
.
For such operations
Equation

3
is generalized as follows (see
[
18
]
, Def. 13):

\textbf{op}(p,k)\star f=f^{\dagger}(\textbf{op}(p,k))=\textbf{op}(p,f^{\dagger}\circ k)=\textbf{op}(p,\mathop{\hbox to0.0pt{$\lambda$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu\raisebox{1.18399pt}{$\lambda$}}x.(k(x)\star f))

(4)

where
f:X\xrightarrow{}TX
,
p\in P
and
k:A\xrightarrow{}TX
.

Denotational semantics of terms
.
Given the monad

(\mathbb{S},\eta,\_^{\dagger})

By taking

\llbracket\textit{get}_{\ell}\rrbracket:\textbf{1}\times(\mathbb{S}D)^{D}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{S}D\simeq(\mathbb{S}D)^{D}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{S}D\quad\mbox{ by }\quad\llbracket\textit{get}_{\ell}\rrbracket\,d\,\varsigma=d\,(\varsigma\,(\ell))\,\varsigma

On the other hand, taking

Definition 3.6
.

A
\lambda_{imp}
-model
is a structure
{\cal D}=(D,\mathbb{S},\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket^{D},\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket^{\mathbb{S}D})
such that:

1.

D
is a domain s.t.

via

, where
\mathbb{S}
is the partiality and state monad of stores over

2.

for all

\begin{array}[]{ll}\llbracket x\rrbracket^{D}e=e(x)&\quad\llbracket\lambda x.M\rrbracket^{D}e=\Psi(\mathop{\hbox to0.0pt{$\lambda$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu\raisebox{1.18399pt}{$\lambda$}}d\in D.\,\llbracket M\rrbracket^{\mathbb{S}D}{e[x\mapsto d]})\\[2.84526pt]
\llbracket\,[V]\,\rrbracket^{\mathbb{S}D}e=\mbox{\it unit}\;\,(\llbracket V\rrbracket^{D}e)&\quad\llbracket M\star V\rrbracket^{\mathbb{S}D}e=(\llbracket M\rrbracket^{\mathbb{S}D}e)\star\Phi(\llbracket V\rrbracket^{D}e)\\[2.84526pt]

By unravelling definitions and applying to an arbitrary store

, the last two clauses can be written:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\llbracket\textit{get}_{\ell}(\lambda x.M)\rrbracket^{\mathbb{S}D}e\,\varsigma&=&\llbracket M\rrbracket^{\mathbb{S}D}(e[x\mapsto\varsigma(\ell)])\,\varsigma\\[2.84526pt]
\llbracket\textit{set}_{\ell}(V,M)\rrbracket^{\mathbb{S}D}e\,\varsigma&=&\llbracket M\rrbracket^{\mathbb{S}D}e\,(\varsigma[\ell\mapsto\llbracket V\rrbracket^{D}e])\end{array}

We say that the equation

Proposition 3.7
.

The following equations are true in
{\cal D}

1.

[V]\star(\lambda x.M)=M[V/x]

2.

M\star\lambda x.[x]=M

3.

(L\star\lambda x.M)\star\lambda y.N=L\star\lambda x.(M\star\lambda y.N)

4.

\textit{get}_{\ell}(\lambda x.M)\star W=\textit{get}_{\ell}(\lambda x.(M\star W))

5.

\textit{set}_{\ell}(V,M)\star W=\textit{set}_{\ell}(V,M\star W)

where
x\not\in FV(\lambda y.N)
in (
3
) and
x\not\in FV(W)

Proof.

By definition and straightforward calculations. For example, to see (

\varsigma\in S
be arbitrary
and
e^{\prime}=e[x\mapsto\varsigma(\ell)]
; then, omitting the apices of the interpretation mappings

In this section we switch to the category
\omega\text{-}\textrm{\bf ALG}
of
\omega

\omega\text{-}\textrm{\bf ALG}

Observe that morphisms in

Definition 4.1
.

An intersection type theory, shortly itt, is a pair

\begin{array}[]{c@{\hspace{1.5cm}}c@{\hspace{1.5cm}}c@{\hspace{1.5cm}}c@{\hspace{1.5cm}}c}\alpha\leq_{A}\omega_{A}\hfil\hskip 42.67912pt&\alpha\wedge\beta\leq_{A}\alpha\hfil\hskip 42.67912pt&\alpha\wedge\beta\leq_{A}\beta\hfil\hskip 42.67912pt&{\vbox{\hbox spread0.0pt{\hskip 0.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\penalty\alpha\leq_{A}\alpha^{\prime}\quad\beta\leq_{A}\beta^{\prime}$}\hskip 0.0pt}\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\vrule height=2.75002pt,depth=-2.24998pt,width=73.85652pt\hbox{}}}\hbox{\kern 3.53477pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\alpha\wedge\beta\leq_{A}\alpha^{\prime}\wedge\beta^{\prime}$}}}}\hfil\hskip 42.67912pt\end{array}

In the literature, the operator

, and
\omega_{A}
the
universal type
.
Informally,
\textit{Th}_{A}
is identified with the set of inequalities

Clearly, the quotient
\mathcal{L}_{A}/_{\leq_{A}}

\wedge

\omega_{A}
as top

Definition 4.2

A non empty

\leq_{A}

\textit{Th}_{A}

The following proposition is the fundamental fact about intersection types and
\omega
-algebraic lattices:

Proposition 4.3

(Representation theorem)
.

The partial order
({\cal F}_{A},\subseteq)
of the filters of an intersection type theory
\textit{Th}_{A}
is an
\omega
-algebraic lattice, whose compact elements are the
principal filters. Vice versa, any
\omega
-algebraic lattice
A
is isomorphic the poset
({\cal F}_{A},\subseteq)
of filters of some intersection type theory
\textit{Th}_{A}
.

Proof.

(Sketch).
The first part is established via a direct argument. To the second part, let
\mathcal{L}_{A}=\{\alpha_{d}\mid d\in K(A)\}
(where each
\alpha_{d}
is a new type constant) which is countable by hypothesis, and take

\textit{Th}_{A}=\{\alpha_{d}\leq_{A}\alpha_{e}\mid e\sqsubseteq d\}
. By observing that
\alpha_{d}\wedge_{A}\alpha_{e}=\alpha_{d\sqcup e}
, where

d\sqcup e\in K(A)
if

, and that
\omega_{A}=\alpha_{\top}
, we have that
\textit{Th}_{A}
is an intersection type theory and that

\mathcal{L}_{A}/_{\leq_{A}}
is isomorphic to
K^{\textit{op}}(A)
ordered by the opposite to the ordering of
A
. From this it follows that
{\cal F}_{A}
is isomorphic
to the ideal completion of
K(A)
, which in turn is isomorphic to
A
by algebraicity.
∎

The above proposition substantiates the claim that intersection type theories are the

\omega\text{-}\textrm{\bf ALG}

Lemma 4.5

If

Proof.

We have

and that
\omega_{A}\in Y

\omega_{B}\in X\cdot Y

\varphi_{i}\xrightarrow{}\psi_{i}\in X

\varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi_{2}\xrightarrow{}\psi_{1}\wedge\psi_{2}=(\varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi_{2}\xrightarrow{}\psi_{1})\wedge(\varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi_{2}\xrightarrow{}\psi_{2})\in X

Concerning continuity, we have to show that

where

the sup of a family of filters is the least filter including the union of the family. Inclusion from left to right follows by observing that if

\textit{get}_{\ell}(X)\cdot Y=X\cdot(Y\cdot\{\ell\})\cdot Y

where we assume that
\_\,\cdot\,\_
associates to the left, and we abbreviate
Y\cdot\{\ell\}=\{\delta\in\mathcal{L}_{D}\mid\langle\ell:\delta\rangle\in Y\}
representing the
application of the “store”
Y
to the location
\ell
.
Now, let
Z=_{\textit{def}}\{\tau\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{S}D}\mid\exists\delta\in Y\cdot\{\ell\}.\;\delta\xrightarrow{}\tau\in X\}
then

X\cdot(Y\cdot\{\ell\})\cdot Y=Z\cdot Y

If
\tau=\omega_{\mathbb{S}D}
then
\delta\xrightarrow{}\tau=\omega_{D}
, which trivially belongs to any filter; if instead
\tau\neq\omega_{\mathbb{S}D}
then

\tau=\bigwedge_{I}\sigma_{i}\xrightarrow{}\kappa_{i}
and
\delta\xrightarrow{}\tau\in X
if and only if
\delta\xrightarrow{}\sigma_{i}\xrightarrow{}\kappa_{i}\in X
for all

. From this we conclude that

Z\cdot Y=\{\kappa\in\mathcal{L}_{C}\mid\exists\sigma\in Y\mid\sigma\xrightarrow{}\kappa\in Z\}=\{\kappa\in\mathcal{L}_{C}\mid\exists\langle\ell:\delta\rangle\wedge\sigma\in Y.\;\delta\xrightarrow{}\sigma\xrightarrow{}\kappa\in X\}

and therefore the appropriate definition of
\textit{get}_{\ell}(X)
is

\textit{get}_{\ell}(X)=_{\textit{def}}\;{\tt Filt}\{(\langle\ell:\delta\rangle\wedge\sigma)\xrightarrow{}\kappa\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{S}D}\mid\delta\xrightarrow{}(\sigma\xrightarrow{}\kappa)\in X\}

(8)

Similarly, again by Def.
3.6
, for any
X\in{\cal F}_{D}
,
Y\in{\cal F}_{\mathbb{S}D}
and
Z\in{\cal F}_{S}
we expect:

\textit{set}^{\cal F}_{\ell}(X,Y)\cdot Z=Y\cdot(Z[\ell\mapsto X])

where
Z[\ell\mapsto X]
is supposed to represent the update of
Z
by associating
X
to
\ell
, namely:

Z[\ell\mapsto X]={\tt Filt}\{\{\langle\ell:\delta\rangle\mid\delta\in X\}\cup\{\langle\ell^{\prime}:\delta^{\prime}\rangle\mid\langle\ell^{\prime}:\delta^{\prime}\rangle\in Z\;\And\;\ell^{\prime}\neq\ell\}\}

Then

\begin{array}[]{rcl}Y\cdot(Z[\ell\mapsto X])&=&{\tt Filt}\{\kappa\mid\exists\sigma\xrightarrow{}\kappa\in Z[\ell\mapsto X].\;\sigma\xrightarrow{}\kappa\in Y\}\\[2.84526pt]
&=&{\tt Filt}\{\kappa\mid\exists\sigma^{\prime}\in Z,\delta\in X.\;\langle\ell:\delta\rangle\wedge\sigma^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}\kappa\in Y\;\And\;\ell\not\in\textit{dom}(\sigma^{\prime})\}\end{array}

and therefore we define:

\textit{set}^{\cal F}_{\ell}(X,Y)=_{\textit{def}}{\tt Filt}\{\sigma^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}\kappa\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{S}D}\mid\exists\delta\in X.\;\langle\ell:\delta\rangle\wedge\sigma^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}\kappa\in Y\;\And\;\ell\not\in\textit{dom}(\sigma^{\prime})\}

(9)

Eventually, by construction we have:

Theorem 4.11
.

The structure
{\cal F}=({\cal F}_{D},\mathbb{S},\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket^{{\cal F}_{D}},\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket^{{\cal F}_{\mathbb{S}D}})
is a
\lambda_{imp}
-model where, for

the interpretations

\llbracket V\rrbracket^{{\cal F}_{D}}e\in{\cal F}_{D}

\llbracket M\rrbracket^{{\cal F}_{\mathbb{S}D}}e\in{\cal F}_{\mathbb{S}D}
are defined by:

\begin{array}[]{rcl@{\hspace{2cm}}rcl}\llbracket x\rrbracket^{{\cal F}_{D}}e&=&e(x)\hfil\hskip 56.9055pt&\llbracket\lambda x.M\rrbracket^{{\cal F}_{D}}e&=&\Lambda(\mathop{\hbox to0.0pt{$\lambda$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu\raisebox{1.18399pt}{$\lambda$}}X\in{\cal F}_{D}.\;\llbracket M\rrbracket^{{\cal F}_{\mathbb{S}D}}e[x\mapsto X])\\[2.84526pt]

Proof.

By applying equations (

)-(

) to the clauses of Def.

.

5
Deriving the type assignment system

The definition of a type assignment system can be obtained out of the construction of the filter-model.
The system is nothing else than the description of the denotation of terms in the particular
\lambda_{imp}
-model
{\cal F}
, which is possible because both
\llbracket V\rrbracket^{{\cal F}_{D}}e\in{\cal F}_{D}
and
\llbracket M\rrbracket^{{\cal F}_{\mathbb{S}D}}e\in{\cal F}_{\mathbb{S}D}
, and filters are sets of types.

Types are naturally interpreted as subsets of the domains of term interpretation, namely
{\cal F}_{D}
or
{\cal F}_{\mathbb{S}D}
according to their kind; by applying to the present case the same construction as in
[
7
]
and
[
2
]
, which originates from Stone duality for boolean algebras, we set:

Definition 5.1
.

For
A=D,S,C
and
\mathbb{S}D
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
define:

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}=\{X\in{\cal F}_{A}\mid\varphi\in X\}

Such interpretation is a generalization of the natural interpretation of intersection types over an extended type structure
[
13
]
, which in the present case yields:

Proposition 5.2
.

For
A=D,S,C
and
\mathbb{S}D
and
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
we have:

\llbracket\varphi\wedge\psi\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}=\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}\cap\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{{\cal F}},\qquad\llbracket\omega_{A}\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}={\cal F}_{A}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\varphi\leq\psi\Longrightarrow\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}\subseteq\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}

Moreover:

1.

\llbracket\delta\xrightarrow{}\tau\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}=\{X\in{\cal F}_{D}\mid\forall Y\in\llbracket\delta\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}.\;X\cdot Y\in\llbracket\tau\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}\}

2.

\llbracket\langle\ell:\delta\rangle\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}=\{X\in{\cal F}_{S}\mid X\cdot\{\ell\}\in\llbracket\delta\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}\}

3.

\llbracket\delta\times\sigma\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}=\{X\in{\cal F}_{C}\mid\pi_{1}(X)\in\llbracket\delta\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}\And\pi_{2}(X)\in\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}\}

4.

\llbracket\sigma\xrightarrow{}\kappa\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}=\{X\in{\cal F}_{\mathbb{S}D}\mid\forall Y\in\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}.\;X\cdot Y\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}\}

where, for
X\in{\cal F}_{C}\cong({\cal F}_{D}\times{\cal F}_{S})_{\bot}
,
\pi_{1}(X)=\{\delta\in\mathcal{L}_{D}\mid\exists\sigma\in\mathcal{L}_{S}.\;\delta\times\sigma\in X\}
and similarly for
\pi_{2}(X)
.

Proof.

The first part is immediate from the definition of type interpretation and of filters. Concerning the remaining statements,
we prove just (
1
) as the other ones are similar.
First observe that
\uparrow\varphi\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}
. Hence,
if
X\in\llbracket\delta\xrightarrow{}\tau\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}
then
\delta\xrightarrow{}\tau\in X
, which implies that
X\cdot\uparrow\delta=\;\uparrow\tau\in\llbracket\tau\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}
. Viceversa if

X\cdot Y\in\llbracket\tau\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}
for all
Y\in\llbracket\delta\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}
then in particular
X\cdot\uparrow\delta=\;\uparrow\tau\in\llbracket\tau\rrbracket^{{\cal F}}
, which implies that
for some
\delta^{\prime}\in\;\uparrow\delta
,
\delta^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}\tau\in X
; but then
\delta\leq_{D}\delta^{\prime}
so that
\delta^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}\tau\leq_{D}\delta\xrightarrow{}\tau
and therefore
\delta\xrightarrow{}\tau\in X

as
X
is upward closed.
∎

and similarly,
\Gamma\vdash M:\tau\iff\tau\in\llbracket M\rrbracket^{{\cal F}_{\mathbb{S}D}}e_{\Gamma}

In spite of the complexity of the construction we have been going through in the last sections, the payoff of our work is a system with just one typing rule for
each syntactical construct in the grammar of the calculus, plus the “logical” rules (

[

Moreover, by the obvious interpretation

of types in a model

\Gamma\models V:\delta~\iff~\Gamma\vdash V:\delta\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Gamma\models M:\tau~\iff~\Gamma\vdash M:\tau.

\lambda_{imp}

{\cal D}

and write
\Gamma\models T:\varphi

for all
\cal D

6
Discussion and Related works

This work is a development of
[
15
]
, where we considered a pure untyped computational
\lambda
-calculus, namely without operations nor constants.
Therefore, the monad
T
and the respective unit and bind were generic, so that types cannot convey any specific information about the domain
TD
, nor about effects represented by the monad.

In the unpublished report
[
16
]
we sketched the construction of the type system for the state monad
\mathbb{S}
. This has been motivated on the
ground of an operational interpretation of terms that we illustrated, culminating in the characterization of convergence via the type system.
This is akin to the lazy
\lambda
-calculus
[
3
]
, where convergent terms are characterized by the formula
(t\xrightarrow{}t)_{\bot}
of the endogenous logic of the calculus, in the sense of
[
2
]
.

While deferring the exposition of the convergence characterization to a companion paper, here we have focussed on the construction of the type system itself.
Since
[
7
]
we know that a
\lambda
-model can be constructed by taking the filters of types in an intersection type system with subtyping. The relation
among the filter-model and Scott’s
D_{\infty}
construction has been subject to extensive studies, starting with
[
13
]
and continuing with

[
17
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
. In the meantime Abramsky’s theory of domain logic in
[
2
]

provided a generalization of the same ideas to algebraic domains in the category of 2/3 SFP, of which
\omega\text{-}\textrm{\bf ALG}
is a (full) subcategory, based on Stone duality.

In the present work, we use the mathematical theories just mentioned, but reversing the process from semantics to types. Usually, one starts with a calculus
and a type system, looking for a semantics and studying properties of the model. On the contrary, we move from a domain equation and the definition of the denotational
semantics of the calculus of interest and synthesize a filter-model and an intersection type system.
This synthetic use of domain logic is, in our view, prototypical w.r.t. the construction
of type systems catching properties of any computational
\lambda
-calculus with operators.
We expect that the study of such systems will be of help in understanding the operational semantics of such models, a topic that has been addressed in
[
14
,
21
]
, but with different mathematical tools.

A further research direction is to move from
\omega\text{-}\textrm{\bf ALG}
to other categories such as the category of relations. The latter is deeply related to non-idempotent intersection types
[
12
,
11
]
that have been shown to catch intensional aspects of
\lambda
-calculi involving quantitative reasoning about computational resources.
If the present approach can be rephrased in the category of relations, then our method could produce non-idempotent intersection type systems for effectful
\lambda
-calculi.

In the perspective of considering categories other than
\omega\text{-}\textrm{\bf ALG}
, it is natural to ask whether the synthesis of an “estrinisic” type system in the sense of Reynolds out of the denotational semantics of a calculus, either typed or not, can be described in categorical terms. Intersection type theories and related systems, as we have been using in this work, are a special case of refinement type systems;
a starting point may be the framing of refinement type systems as functors in
[
22
]
.

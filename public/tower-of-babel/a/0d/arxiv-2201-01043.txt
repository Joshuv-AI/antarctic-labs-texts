Spanning trees of K 1 , 4 K_{1,4} -free graphs with a bounded number of leaves and branch vertices
Pham Hoang Ha

Abstract
Let T T be a tree. A vertex of degree one is a leaf of T T and a vertex of degree at least three is a branch vertex of T T . A graph is said to be K 1 , 4 K_{1,4} -free if it does not contain K 1 , 4 K_{1,4} as an induced subgraph. In this paper, we study the spanning trees with a bounded number of leaves and branch vertices of K 1 , 4 K_{1,4} -free graphs. Applying the main results, we also give some improvements of previous results on the spanning tree with few branch vertices for the case of K 1 , 4 K_{1,4} -free graphs.



Keywords:
spanning tree; leaf; branch vertex; independence
number; degree sum

05C05, 05C07, 05C69

Introduction

In this paper, we only consider finite graphs without loops or multiple edges.
Let
G
be a
graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. For any vertex

, we use

and

to denote the set of neighbors of

and the degree of

in

We define

to be the
graph obtained from

by deleting the edge

, and

to be the graph obtained from

by adding an edge
uv

between two non-adjacent vertices

and

of
G
.
For any

, we denote by
|X|
the cardinality of
X
.
Sometime, we use
|G|
to denote
|V(G)|
. We define

N_{G}(X)=\bigcup\limits_{x\in X}N_{G}(x)
and
\deg_{G}(X)=\sum\limits_{x\in X}\deg_{G}(x)
. The subgraph of

induced by

is denoted by

.

A subset

is called an

of

are adjacent in

The maximum size of an independent set in

is denoted by

For each positive integer

\sigma_{p}(G)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}+\infty,&\;\mbox{ if }\alpha(G)<p,\\
\min\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}d_{G}(v_{i})\;|\;\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}\}\,\text{is an independent set in }G\},&\;\mbox{ if }\alpha(G)\geq p.\end{array}\right.

Let

be a tree. A vertex of degree one is a

of

There are several sufficient conditions on the independence

to have a spanning tree with a bounded number of leaves
or branch vertices. Win
[
20
]
obtained the following theorem, which confirms a conjecture of Las Vergnas
[
14
]
. Beside that, recently, the author
[

]

Theorem 1.1
(
[

, Win]
,
[

, Ha]
)

Let

and

be integers and let

be a

, then

has a spanning tree with at most

As a corollary of Theorem
1.1
, we have a sharp result (as a note in
[

]
) for a connected graph to have a bounded number of branch vertices.

Corollary 1.2

Let

and

be two integers and let

be a
m
-connected graph. If

\alpha(G)\leq m+k+1
, then

has a spanning tree with at most
k

branch vertices.

In 1998, Broersma and Tunistra gave the following degree sum condition for a graph to have a spanning tree with at most
l

Theorem 1.3
(
[
1

Let

for a connected graph

having a few leaves or branch vertices. This question is still open. But, in certain graph classes, the answers have been determined.

Theorem 1.14

Let

Let

Applications of the main result

In this section, we introduce some applications of Theorem

.

When

if graphs are

Corollary 2.1

Let

be a possitive interger and let

-free graph of order

. If
\sigma_{2}(G)\geq n-k
, then

When

we state the following result.

Theorem 2.2

Let

be a non-negative interger and let

be a connected

-free graph of order

. If
\sigma_{k+3}(G)\geq n-k
, then

has a spanning tree with at most

We may show that Theorem
1.8
(
i
) and the following theorem as corollaries of Theorem

.

Theorem 2.3
(
[

, Momège]
)

Let

be a connected

-free graph of order

. If
\sigma_{2}(G)\geq\dfrac{2}{3}n,
then

has a Hamiltonian path.

Indeed, it follows from the assumptions of Theorem
2.3
we obtain that
\sigma_{3}(G)\geq\dfrac{3}{2}\sigma_{2}(G)\geq n
(that also satisfies the assumption of Theorem
1.8
(

)). Now, using Theorem

with

and

we conclude that

has a spanning tree

with at most

leaves and branch vertices. If

then

this is a contradiction. Then

this mean that

is a path. Therefore,

has a Hamiltonian path.

Moreover, we note that if the tree

has at most

is an improvement of Theorem

-free graphs with a new approach.

We end this section by constructing an example to show that

is sharp. Let

be positive integers. Let

be a path. Let

For each

For two distinct vertices

of

. If

4
Proof of Theorem
1.15

Suppose that
G
has no spanning tree with at most total
k+m+2
leaves and branch vertices. Choose some spanning
T
of
G
such that:

(C1)
|L(T)|
is as small as possible.

(C2)
|R\_Stem(T)|
is as large as possible, subject to (C1).

By the contrary hypotheses, we note that
|L(T)|+|B(T)|\geq k+m+3.

If
|B(T)|=0,
then
|L(T)|=2.
So
|L(T)|+|B(T)|=2<k+m+3.
This is a contradiction. Hence,
|B(T)|\geq 1
and, in particular,
B(T)\not=\emptyset.

On the other hand, we have

|L(T)|=2+\sum_{b\in B(T)}(\deg_{T}(b)-2)\geq 2+|B(T)|.

So

\displaystyle 2|L(T)|\geq|L(T)|+2+|B(T)|\geq k+m+5\geq m-1+m+5=2m+4

\displaystyle\Rightarrow|L(T)|\geq m+2.

We now have the following claims.

Claim 4.1

L(T)
is independent.

Proof.
Assume that two leaves
s
and
t
are adjacent in
G
. Then
s
has some nearest branch vertex
b
.
Let
T^{\prime}=T-\{bb_{s}\}+\{st\}.
Then
T^{\prime}
is a spanning of
G
satisfying
|L(T^{\prime})|<|L(T)|,
the reason is that either
T^{\prime}
has only one new leaf
b_{s}
and
s,t
are not leaves of
T^{\prime}
or
s
is still a leaf of
T^{\prime}
but
T^{\prime}
has no new leaf and
t
is not a leaf of
T^{\prime}
. This contradicts to the condition (C1). So the claim holds.

Claim 4.2

Let
b\in B(T)
and
x\in N_{T}(b).
For each vertex
s\in L(T),
if
b\in V(P_{T}[s,x])
then
sx\not\in E(G).

Proof.
Assume that
sx\in E(G).
Consider the spanning tree
T^{\prime}=T-\{bx\}+\{sx\}.
Hence,
|L(T^{\prime})|<|L(T)|
(since
s
is not a leaf of
T^{\prime}
), a contradiction with the condition (C1). So the claim is proved.

Claim 4.3

Let
b,r
be two branch vertices of
T
such that
V(P_{T}[b,r])\cap B(T)=\{b,r\}.
Let
s
be a leaf of
T.
If
sx\in E(G)
for some
x\in V(P_{T}[b,r])\setminus\{b\}
then
sx^{-}\not\in E(G).

Proof.
Assume that there exists a vertex
x\in V(P_{T}[b,r])\setminus\{b\}
such that
sx,sx^{-}\in E(G)
(note that possibly
x^{-}=b
). Let
c
be the nearest branch vertex of
s
. Consider the spanning tree
T^{\prime}=T-\{xx^{-},ss_{c}\}+\{sx,sx^{-}\}.
If
s_{c}=c
then
s
is not a leaf of
T^{\prime}.
Hence,
|L(T^{\prime})|<|L(T)|
, a contradiction with the condition (C1). Otherwise,
L(T^{\prime})=L(T)
and
|R\_Stem(T^{\prime})|>|R\_Stem(T)|
(since
s\in V(R\_Stem(T^{\prime}))
), a contradiction with the condition (C2). This completes the proof of claim.

Claim 4.4

Let
b,r
be two branch vertices of
T
such that
V(P_{T}[b,r])\cap B(T)=\{b,r\}.
If
x\in V(P_{T}[b,r])\setminus\{b,r\}
then
|N(L(T))\cap\{x\}|\leq 1.

Proof.
Assume that there exists a vertex
x\in V(P_{T}[b,r])\setminus\{b,r\}
such that
|N(L(T))\cap\{x\}|\geq 2.
Then there are two vertices
s,t\in L(T)
such that
xs,xt\in E(G).
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
b\in V(P_{T}[s,x]).
By Claim
4.2
, we obtain
x^{-}\not=b.
Since Claim
4.1
and Claim
4.3
hold, we have
st,sx^{-},sx^{+},tx^{-},tx^{+}\not\in E(G)
(here
x^{+}
can be
r
). Moreover,
G[x,x^{-},x^{+},s,t]
is not
K_{1,4}
-free. Hence, we obtain
x^{-}x^{+}\in E(G).
Let
c
be the nearest branch vertices of
s.
Consider the spanning tree
T^{\prime}=T-\{xx^{-},xx^{+},cc_{s}\}+\{sx,tx,x^{-}x^{+}\}.
Hence,
|L(T^{\prime})|<|L(T)|,
the reason is that either
T^{\prime}
has only one new leaf
c_{s}
and
s,t
are not leaves of
T^{\prime}
or
s
is still a leaf of
T^{\prime}
but
T^{\prime}
has no new leaf and
t
is not a leaf of
T^{\prime}
. This contradicts to the condition (C1).

Therefore, Claim
4.4
is proved.

Claim 4.5

L(T)
is pseudoindependent with respect to
T
.

Proof.
Suppose two leaves
s
and
t
are pseudoadjacent with respect to
T
. Then there exists some edge
e\in E(T)
such that
sg(e,s),tg(e,t)\in E(G)
. Let
b
and
u
be the nearest branch vertices of
s
and
t
, respectively. Consider two cases as follows:

Case 1. Suppose
g(e,s)\not=g(e,t)
. Then
e_{s}=g(e,t)
and
e_{t}=g(e,s)
, so
se_{t},te_{s}\in E(G)
. Then
T^{\prime}=T-\{e,bb_{s}\}+\{se_{t},te_{s}\}
violates (C1) since
T^{\prime}
has only one new leaf
b_{s}
and
s,t
are not leaves of
T^{\prime}
or
s
is still a leaf of
T^{\prime}
but
T^{\prime}
has no new leaf and
t
is not a leaf of
T^{\prime}.
So the case 1 does not happen.

Case 2: Suppose
g(e,s)=g(e,t)
. Define

. Then
e_{s}=e_{t}
and denoted by vertex
z
. We have

. Since

and

is independent, we have

. Then there exists some vertex

If

then we consider the spanning tree
T^{\prime}=T-\{bb_{s},e\}+\{sz,tx\}.
It follows from Claim
4.2
that

Hence

(since two leaves

and

are lost while
b_{s}
is gained or
s
is still a leaf of
T^{\prime}
but
T^{\prime}
has no new leaf and

is not a leaf of
T^{\prime}
). So

. The same argument gives

.

If

then the spanning tree

violates (C1) (since two leaves
s
and

are lost while
u_{t}
is gained or
t
is still a leaf of
T^{\prime}
but
T^{\prime}
has no new leaf and
s
is not a leaf of
T^{\prime}
). So

. The same argument gives

.

Now, since
G[x,y,z,s,t]
is not
K_{1,4}
-free and
st,sz,sy,tz,ty\notin E(G)
, we obtain

Then the spanning tree
T^{\prime}=T-\{e,xy,bb_{s}\}+\{sx,tx,yz\}
violates (C1), the reason is that either
T^{\prime}
has only one new leaf
b_{s}
and
s,t
are not leaves of
T^{\prime}
or
s
is still a leaf of
T^{\prime}
but
T^{\prime}
has no new leaf and

is not a leaf of
T^{\prime}
.

The claim

has been proven.

Claim 4.6

For each pair branch vertices
b,r\in B(T)
such that
V(P_{T}[b,r])\cap B(T)=\{b,r\}
, there exists some edge
e\in E(P_{T}[b,r])
which has no oblique neighbor in the set
L(T)
.

Proof.
We consider three cases as follows.

Case 1.
V(P_{T}[b,r])=\{b,r\}.
By Claim
4.2
we choose
e=br
.

Case 2.
V(P_{T}[b,r])\not=\{b,r\}.
On
P_{T}[b,r]
we set
x=b^{+}\not=r.
Assume that there doesn’t exist edge in
E(P_{T}[b,r])
which has no oblique neighbor in the set

Hence both of

(note that possibly

) have oblique neighbors in

Then there exist

By Claim

we obtain that

If

Consider the spanning tree

the reason is that either

but

Since

-

Claim 4.7

In the graph
G,

distinct edges of

Proof.
Since
|L(T)|\geq k+3,

For each

be the edge of

Combining with Claim
4.5
we obtain that

. Hence, there are at least

4.6
, the number of edges of

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle\geq|L(T)|-m-2+|E(H)|=|L(T)|-m-2+|V(H)|-1

\displaystyle=|L(T)|-m-2+|B(T)|-1=|L(T)|+|B(T)|-m-3\geq k.

.

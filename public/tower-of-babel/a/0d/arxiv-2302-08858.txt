Analysis of a positivity-preserving splitting scheme for some nonlinear stochastic heat equations
Charles-Edouard Bréhier, David Cohen, Johan Ulander

Abstract
. We construct a positivity-preserving Lie–Trotter splitting scheme with finite difference discretization in space for approximating the solutions to a class of nonlinear stochastic heat equations with multiplicative space-time white noise. We prove that this explicit numerical scheme converges in the mean-square sense, with rate 1 / 4 1/4 in time and rate 1 / 2 1/2 in space, under appropriate CFL conditions. Numerical experiments illustrate the superiority of the proposed numerical scheme compared with standard numerical methods which do not preserve positivity.



AMS Classification.
60H35. 60M15. 65J08.

Keywords.
Stochastic partial differential equations.
Stochastic heat equation. Splitting scheme. Positivity-preserving scheme. Mean-square convergence.

In this work we propose and study a novel positivity-preserving numerical scheme for a fully discrete approximation of the following nonlinear Stochastic Heat Equation (SHE)
with multiplicative space-time white noise

(1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\partial_{xx}^{2}u(t,x)+g(u(t,x))\dot{W}(t,x),\\
&u(t,0)=u(t,1)=0,\\
&u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

for
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times[0,1]

u_{0}\geq 0

g(0)=0

\dot{W}

Taking
g(x)=x

[
18

This equation is used to model (particle) branching processes, hydrodynamics with random forcing, and serves as a model for turbulent diffusions.

To the best of our current knowledge, there are no corresponding results for the case of compact domains with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.

The fully-discrete Lie–Trotter splitting scheme, see equation (
14
), considered in this article
combines a finite difference approximation in space and the explicit recursion

u_{m+1}^{{\rm LT}}=\exp\left(\tau N^{2}D^{N}\right)\hat{u}_{m+1}^{{\rm LT}},

where for
n=1,\ldots,N-1
one has

\hat{u}_{m+1,n}^{{\rm LT}}=\exp\left(\sqrt{N}f(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}})\Delta_{m,n}W-\frac{Nf(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}})^{2}\tau}{2}\right)u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}},

where
\tau=T/M>0
denotes the time step size,
h=1/N
is the mesh size,
\Delta_{m,n}W
denote space-time Wiener increments,

N^{2}D^{N}
the
(N-1)\times(N-1)
matrix of the discrete Laplace operator, and
g(v)=vf(v)
.
Observe that the diffusion part of (
1
) is solved exactly, while
the noise part is solved exactly in the case of the parabolic Anderson model
(where one has
g(v)=v
and
f(v)=1
and thus the subsystem is a geometric Brownian motion).
This shares similarity with the works
[
31
,
77
]
on stochastic differential equations. For a general mapping
g
, we
freeze the factor
f
at the previous time point and obtain a geometric Brownian motion in the spirit
of the exponential scheme proposed in
[
11
]
for finite dimensional problems.

The main results of the paper are the following:

•

We obtain a fully discrete explicit approximation of the stochastic heat equation (
1
) that is positivity-preserving, see Proposition
4
.

•

We show bounds for the second moment of the numerical approximation under a CFL condition
\tau/h={\rm O}(1)
in Proposition
5
.

•

We prove strong convergence, with rate
1/4
, for the temporal discretization under a CFL condition
\tau/h^{2}={\rm O}(1)
,
see Theorem
6

7

We leave the study of weak convergence of the proposed scheme to possible future works.
On top of that, we show positivity of the exact solution to the SPDE (
1

This follows naturally from the numerical analysis of the proposed approximation, see Proposition
2

Let us mention that the CFL conditions above are not due to the discretization of the Laplace operator, since the linear part is solved exactly.
They are due to the discretization of the contribution of the space-time white noise in the temporal evolution. Numerical experiments confirm that the CFL condition is necessary when studying the mean-square convergence of the proposed scheme.

2.
Setting

This section provides the necessary setting for
the description of the considered class of nonlinear stochastic heat equations as well as of its solution.
We recall the notion of a mild solution and a standard well-posedness result for completeness. In addition, we recall
the spatial discretization by finite difference from
[
35
]
.

For any real-valued continuous function
v\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
, let
\|v\|_{\infty}=\underset{x\in[0,1]}{\max}~|v(x)|
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space, equipped with a filtration
\bigl(\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr)_{t\geq 0}
which satisfies the usual conditions. The expectation operator is denoted by
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
. In the sequel,
C
denotes a generic constant that may vary from line to line. We sometimes use subscripts on
C
to indicate dependence on parameters.

2.1.
Description of the SPDE

Let us first introduce the main assumptions needed for the numerical analysis of the proposed time integrator for the stochastic heat equation.

Assumption 1
.

The initial value
u_{0}\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a function of class
\mathcal{C}^{3}
, and satisfies the conditions
u_{0}(0)=u_{0}(1)=0
.

Note that the regularity assumption on the initial value above is for ease of presentation. For weaker conditions, see
[
35
]
or
[
2
]
.

When discussing positivity-preserving properties, a further condition is needed.

Assumption 2
.

The initial value
u_{0}\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies
u_{0}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in[0,1]
.

For the nonlinearity in the considered SPDE, we make use of the following.

Assumption 3
.

The mapping
g\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
, is globally Lipschitz continuous, and satisfies
g(0)=0
.

We denote by
{\rm L_{g}}
the Lipschitz constant of
g
:

{\rm L_{g}}=\underset{v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}},v_{2}\neq v_{1}}{\sup}~\frac{|g(v_{2})-g(v_{1})|}{|v_{2}-v_{1}|}.

The moment bounds and the error estimates presented below depend on the value of the Lipschitz constant
{\rm L_{g}}
.
This is not indicated in order to simplify the notation.

We then introduce the auxiliary mapping
f\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined for all
v\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
by

(2)

f(v)=\frac{g(v)}{v}=\int_{0}^{1}g^{\prime}(rv)\,\text{d}r

and by
f(0)=g^{\prime}(0)
. Since
g^{\prime}
is continuous by Assumption
3
, the mapping
f
is continuous and bounded, and one has the upper bound
\underset{v\in{\mathbb{R}}}{\sup}~|f(v)|\leq{\rm L_{g}}
.

For a fixed time horizon
T>0
, let
W=\left\{W(t,x)\,\colon\,t\in[0,T],x\in[0,1]\right\}
be an
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted Brownian sheet.
We recall that a Brownian sheet is a Gaussian random field with mean zero and covariance
{\mathbb{E}}[W(t,x)W(s,y)]=(t\wedge s)(x\wedge y)
for all
s,t\in[0,T]
and

x,y\in[0,1]
, see for instance
[
79
]
. We consider the stochastic heat equation in the Itô sense

(3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{d}u(t,x)=\partial_{xx}^{2}u(t,x)\,\text{d}t+g(u(t,x))\,\text{d}W(t,x),\\
&u(t,0)=u(t,1)=0,\\
&u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\end{aligned}\right.

for
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in[0,1]
, where
u_{0}
and
g
satisfy Assumption
1
and Assumption
3
, respectively.

In order to define a mild solution of the stochastic heat equation (
3
), we introduce the heat kernel

G(t,x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-j^{2}\pi^{2}t}\sin(j\pi x)\sin(j\pi y),

for
t\geq 0,x,y\in[0,1]
, which is the fundamental solution of the (deterministic) heat equation with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{d}v(t,x)=\partial_{xx}^{2}v(t,x)\,\text{d}t,\\
&v(t,0)=v(t,1)=0,\\
&v(0,x)=\delta(x),\end{aligned}\right.

where the initial value is the Dirac delta function.

A mild solution to the SPDE (
3
) is a random field
\bigl(u(t,x)\bigr)_{t\in[0,T],x\in[0,1]}

satisfying the following integral equation almost surely: for all
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in[0,1]
, one has

(4)

u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{1}G(t,x,y)u_{0}(y)\,\text{d}y+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}G(t-s,x,y)g(u(s,y))\,\text{d}W(s,y).

The stochastic integral in (
4
) is understood in the Itô–Walsh sense, see for instance
[
26
,
46
,
79
]
.

We collect some properties of the mild solution
u(t,x)
to the stochastic heat equation (
3
) in the following statement, see for instance
[
35
, Proposition 3.7]
.

Proposition 1
.

Consider the stochastic heat equation (
3
) under Assumptions
1
and
3
.
There exists a unique mild solution
\left(u(t,x)\right)_{t\in[0,T],x\in[0,1]}
to the SPDE (
3
).
In addition, for all
T\in(0,\infty)
, there exists
C_{T}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{x\in[0,1]}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|u(t,x)|^{2}]\leq C_{T}(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}).

Finally, the solution satisfies the following mean-square regularity property: for all
T\in(0,\infty)
,
there exists
C_{T}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in[0,1]
and all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T]
one has

(5)

\left({\mathbb{E}}[|u(t_{2},x_{2})-u(t_{1},x_{1})|^{2}]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{T}\bigl(|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\frac{1}{4}}+|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigr).

In this article, our objective is to propose and analyze consistent numerical schemes which preserve the following property of the exact solution:
if the initial value
u_{0}
is nonnegative, then the exact solution to the stochastic heat equation,
u(t,\cdot)
, remains nonnegative for all
t>0
.

Proposition 2
.

Consider the stochastic heat equation (
3
) together with Assumptions
1
,
2
and
3
.
Then, for all
t\in(0,\infty)
and all
x\in[0,1]
, almost surely, one has

u(t,x)\geq 0.

The proof of Proposition
2
above is postponed to Section
5.5
.
It is a consequence of the analysis of the fully-discrete numerical scheme and combines two arguments:
on the one hand, the numerical scheme satisfies a variant of Proposition
2
, see Proposition
4
below,
on the other hand, Theorem
6
gives a strong convergence result of the numerical approximation.
Note that similar results are known when considering the stochastic heat equation on the real line, see for instance the works

[
63
,
74
]
and the lecture notes
[
70
]
. We are not aware of positivity-preserving results for SPDEs on bounded domains.

2.2.
Spatial discretization

Let us recall the spatial discretization based on a finite difference approximation on a uniform grid from
[
35
]
. For any integer
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
h=1/N
be the space mesh size,
and let
x_{n}=nh
for
0\leq n\leq N
be the grid points. Let
\kappa^{N}\colon[0,1]\to\{x_{0},\ldots,x_{N}\}
,
be the mapping defined by
\kappa^{N}(x)=x_{n}
for
x\in[x_{n},x_{n+1})
if
n\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
, and
\kappa^{N}(1)=\kappa^{N}(x_{N})=x_{N}=1
.

Throughout this article, we use the convention that for any vector
v=\bigl(v_{n}\bigr)_{1\leq n\leq N-1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}
, we append discrete homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
v_{0}=0
and
v_{N}=0
when needed.

We discretize the initial value
u_{0}
of the stochastic heat equation (
3
) by
u_{0,n}^{N}=u_{n}^{N}(0)=u(0,x_{n})
for
0\leq n\leq N
. Note that discrete homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
u_{0,0}^{N}=u_{0,N}^{N}=0
are satisfied owing to Assumption
1
. Let us then define a piecewise linear extension
u^{N}(0,\cdot)\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying
u^{N}(0,x_{n})=u_{0,n}^{N}
for all
n=0,\ldots,N
, meaning that for
x\in(0,1)
one has

u^{N}(0,x)=N\bigl(\kappa^{N}(x)+h-x\bigr)u(0,\kappa^{N}(x))+N\bigl(x-\kappa^{N}(x)\bigr)u(0,\kappa^{N}(x)+h).

Let
D^{N}=\bigl(D_{ij}^{N}\bigr)_{1\leq i,j\leq N-1}
denote the matrix coming from a standard finite difference discretization of the Laplace operator
at the grid points
x_{n}
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. The matrix
D^{N}
is thus given by

D^{N}=\begin{pmatrix}-2&1&0&\ldots&0&0&0\\
1&-2&1&\ddots&0&0&0\\
0&1&-2&\ddots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\ddots&-2&1&0\\
0&0&0&\ddots&1&-2&1\\
0&0&0&\ldots&0&1&-2\end{pmatrix}.

We then introduce the discrete heat kernel
G^{N}(t)=\bigl(G_{ij}^{N}(t)\bigr)_{1\leq i,j\leq N-1}=e^{tN^{2}D^{N}}
, for
t\geq 0
.
By convention, set
G_{00}^{N}(t)=G_{NN}^{N}(t)=1
,
G_{0N}^{N}(t)=G_{N0}^{N}(t)=0
and
G_{0j}^{N}(t)=G_{Nj}^{N}(t)=0
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
, in order to satisfy homogeneous discrete Dirichlet boundary conditions. Finally, we extend the definition of
G^{N}(t)=\bigl(G_{ij}^{N}(t)\bigr)_{1\leq i,j\leq N-1}
to
\bigl(G^{N}(t,x,y)\bigr)_{t\geq 0,x,y\in[0,1]}
by asking
that
G^{N}(t,x_{i},y_{j})=NG_{ij}^{N}(t)
for
0\leq i,j\leq N
and for
t\geq 0
and
y\in[0,1]

G^{N}(t,x,y)=N\bigl(\kappa^{N}(x)+h-x\bigr)G^{N}(t,\kappa^{N}(x),\kappa^{N}(y))+N\bigl(x-\kappa^{N}(x)\bigr)G^{N}(t,\kappa^{N}(x)+h,\kappa^{N}(y))

for
x\in(0,1)
and
G^{N}(t,0,y)=G^{N}(t,1,y)=0
. As a result, the mapping
(x,y)\mapsto G^{N}(t,x,y)
is piecewise linear in
x
and piecewise constant in
y
at all times
t
.

It is worth recalling the following well-known property of the discrete heat kernel: one has
G_{ij}^{N}(t)\geq 0
for all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
and
t\geq 0
. As a consequence, one has
G^{N}(t,x,y)\geq 0
for
x,y\in[0,1]
and
t\geq 0
. This property follows from the fact that
tN^{2}D^{N}
is a Metzler matrix,
see for instance
[
32
]
for a definition, and the exponential of a Metzler matrix has only non-negative elements.

We are now in position to define the spatial discretization
u^{N}
, for
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, by the following integral equality

(6)

u^{N}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t,x,y)u^{N}(0,\kappa^{N}(y))\,\text{d}y+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t-s,x,y)g(u^{N}(s,\kappa^{N}(y)))\,\text{d}W(s,y)

for
t\geq 0
and
x\in[0,1]
.
Note that the mapping
x\in[0,1]\mapsto u^{N}(t,x)
is linear on
[x_{n},x_{n+1}]
, for every
n\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
, for every
t\geq 0
. In addition, one has
u^{N}(t,0)=u^{N}(t,1)=0
for every
t\geq 0
. For a practical implementation of the scheme, it is sufficient to compute
u_{n}^{N}(t)=u^{N}(t,x_{n})
for all
1\leq n\leq N-1
. This is performed as follows: for all
t\geq 0
and
1\leq n\leq N-1
one has

u_{n}^{N}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}G_{nj}^{N}(t)u_{0,j}^{N}+\sqrt{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\int_{0}^{t}G_{nj}^{N}(t-s)g(u_{j}^{N}(s))\,\text{d}W_{j}^{N}(s),

where

W_{n}^{N}(t)=\sqrt{N}\bigl(W(t,x_{n+1})-W(t,x_{n})\bigr).

By definition of a Wiener sheet, observe that the processes
\bigl(W_{1}^{N}(t)\bigr)_{t\geq 0},\ldots,\bigl(W_{N-1}^{N}(t)\bigr)_{t\geq 0}
are independent standard real-valued Wiener processes, for any
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Introduce the
{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}
-valued process
u^{N}
defined by
u^{N}(t)=\bigl(u_{n}^{N}(t)\bigr)_{1\leq n\leq N-1}
for all
t\geq 0
. This process is solution of the following stochastic differential equation

(7)

\text{d}u^{N}(t)=N^{2}D^{N}u^{N}(t)\,\text{d}t+\sqrt{N}g(u^{N}(t))\,\text{d}W^{N}(t)

with initial value
u^{N}(0)=\bigl(u_{0}^{N}\bigr)_{1\leq n\leq N-1}
, where the notation

\bigl(g(u^{N}(t))\,\text{d}W^{N}(t)\bigr)_{n}=g(u_{n}^{N}(t))\,\text{d}W_{n}^{N}(t)
is used.

Let us recall the following convergence result for the spatial discretization, see
[
35
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Proposition 3
.

Consider the stochastic heat equation (
3
) with a nonlinearity
g
satisfying Assumption
3
.
Denote by
\left(u(t,x)\right)_{t\in[0,T],x\in[0,1]}
its exact solution and by
\left(u^{N}(t,x)\right)_{t\in[0,T],x\in[0,1]}
the numerical approximation by finite differences with mesh size
h=1/N
.
For all
T\in(0,\infty)
and any initial value
u_{0}
satisfying Assumption
1
, there exists
C_{T}(u_{0})\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
h=1/N
with
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
one has

(8)

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{x\in[0,1]}{\sup}~\bigl({\mathbb{E}}[|u^{N}(t,x)-u(t,x)|^{2}]\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{T}(u_{0})h^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In the error analysis below, the following auxiliary result from
[
2
]
(see Proposition
2.4
) on the temporal regularity of
u^{N}
is used:
there exists
C_{T}(u_{0})\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
t,s\in[0,T]
, one has

(9)

\underset{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\sup}~\underset{x\in[0,1]}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|u^{N}(t,x)-u^{N}(s,x)|^{2}]\leq C_{T}(u_{0})|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

3.
The positivity-preserving splitting scheme

In the core part of this paper, we present and study the strong convergence of
an efficient and positivity-preserving time integrator for the stochastic heat equation (
3
).

Let
T\in(0,\infty)
and divide the interval
[0,T]
into
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
subintervals
[t_{m},t_{m+1}]
of length
\tau=T/M
, where
t_{m}=m\tau
for
m\in\{0,\ldots,M\}
.
Introduce the mapping
\ell^{M}\colon[0,T]\to\{t_{0},\ldots,t_{M}\}
, defined by
\ell^{M}(t)=t_{m}
for all
t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1})
, if
m\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}
, and
\ell^{M}(T)=\ell^{M}(t_{M})=t_{M}=T
.

We propose a fully-discrete explicit scheme based on a Lie–Trotter splitting strategy
producing approximations
u_{m}^{{\rm LT}}=\bigl(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}\bigr)_{1\leq n\leq N-1}
of the finite difference approximation

u^{N}(t_{m})=\bigl(u_{n}^{N}(t_{m})\bigr)_{1\leq n\leq N-1}
at the grid times
t_{m}
,
m=0,\ldots,M
. We set the initial value to be
u_{0,n}^{{\rm LT}}=u_{n}^{N}(0)=u_{0,n}^{N}
for all
1\leq n\leq N-1
. As above, one has
u_{m,0}^{{\rm LT}}=0
and
u_{m,N}^{{\rm LT}}=0
for all
m\in\{0,\ldots,M\}
. In this way, homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions are satisfied by the numerical scheme at all times.

We explain the construction of the scheme in Section
3.1
. We then describe the main results of this article: the positivity-preserving property of the splitting scheme (Proposition
4
) and the mean-square convergence in time with order
1/4
(Theorem
6
and Corollary
7
).

3.1.
Description of the time integrator

Let us describe how the splitting scheme is constructed. Given the numerical solution
u_{m}^{{\rm LT}}=\left(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}\right)_{1\leq n\leq N-1}
at grid time
t_{m}=m\tau
for
0\leq m\leq M-1
,
the solution
u_{m+1}^{{\rm LT}}
at the next grid time
t_{m+1}=t_{m}+\tau
is constructed by successively solving two subsystems in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}
:

•

first, the linear Itô SDE system

(10)

\text{d}v_{m,n}^{M,N,1}(t)=\sqrt{N}v_{m,n}^{M,N,1}(t)f(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}})\,\text{d}W_{n}^{N}(t),

for
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
and
t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1}]
, with initial value
v_{m,n}^{M,N,1}(t_{m})=u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}
, where we recall
(see equation (
2
) in Section
2
) that the auxiliary function
f
is such that
g(v)=vf(v)
for all
v\in{\mathbb{R}}
;

•

second, the linear ODE system

(11)

\text{d}v_{m}^{M,N,2}(t)=N^{2}D^{N}v_{m}^{M,N,2}(t)\,\text{d}t,

for
t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1}]
, with initial value
v_{m,n}^{M,N,2}(t_{m})=v_{m,n}^{M,N,1}(t_{m+1})
.

Observe that the solutions of the two subsystems above are known: the solution of the SDE (
10
) is given by

(12)

v_{m,n}^{M,N,1}(t)=\exp\left(\sqrt{N}f(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}})\bigl(W_{n}^{N}(t)-W_{n}^{N}(t_{m})\bigr)-\frac{Nf(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}})^{2}(t-t_{m})}{2}\right)u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}},

for all
t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1}]
, and the solution of the ODE (
11
) is given by

(13)

v_{m}^{M,N,2}(t)=e^{(t-t_{m})N^{2}D^{N}}v_{m}^{M,N,1}(t_{m+1}),

for all
t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1}]
.

Gathering the expressions above gives the following expression for the proposed Lie–Trotter splitting scheme

(14)

u_{m+1}^{{\rm LT}}=e^{\tau N^{2}D^{N}}\left(\exp\Bigl(\sqrt{N}f(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}})\Delta_{m,n}W-\frac{Nf(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}})^{2}\tau}{2}\Bigr)u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}\right)_{1\leq n\leq N-1},

where
\Delta_{m,n}W=W_{n}^{N}(t_{m+1})-W_{n}^{N}(t_{m})
. Observe that the random variables

\bigl(\Delta W_{m,n}\bigr)_{0\leq m\leq M-1,1\leq n\leq N-1}
are independent standard real-valued Gaussian random variables.

The splitting scheme formula (
14
) can also be written as

u_{m+1,n}^{{\rm LT}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}G_{nk}^{N}(\tau)\exp\Bigl(\sqrt{N}f(u_{m,k}^{{\rm LT}})\Delta_{m,n}W-\frac{Nf(u_{m,k}^{{\rm LT}})^{2}\tau}{2}\Bigr)u_{m,k}^{{\rm LT}},

for all
m\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}
and
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
.

One of the key properties of the proposed splitting scheme is the following: if the initial value
u_{0}^{{\rm LT}}=\bigl(u_{0,n}^{{\rm LT}}\bigr)_{1\leq n\leq N-1}
only has nonnegative elements, then for all
m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
the numerical solution
u_{m}^{{\rm LT}}=\bigl(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}\bigr)_{1\leq n\leq N-1}
at time
t_{m}=m\tau
also only has nonnegative elements almost surely. In other words, the proposed scheme is positivity-preserving. This is stated in the next proposition.

Proposition 4
.

Let
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
be arbitrary integers and let
T\in(0,\infty)
.
Let Assumptions
1
,
2
and
3
be satisfied.
Let the sequence
u_{0}^{{\rm LT}},\ldots,u_{M}^{{\rm LT}}
be given by the splitting scheme (
14
), with
h=1/N
and
\tau=T/M
, with initial value
u_{0,n}^{{\rm LT}}=u_{0}(x_{n})\geq 0
for all
n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
. Then, almost surely, one has

u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}\geq 0,

for all
m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
and
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
.

Proof of Proposition
4
.

The proof proceeds by recursion on the time index
m
.

•

Note that
u_{0,n}^{{\rm LT}}=u(0,x_{n})\geq 0
for all
n\in\{0,\ldots,N\}
.

•

Assume that the property
u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}\geq 0
, for all
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
, holds at time
t_{m}=m\tau
. We prove that under this assumption, it also holds at time
t_{m+1}=(m+1)\tau
.

The argument is straightforward: the solutions of the subsystems (
10
) and (
11
) are nonnegative at all times when they have nonnegative initial values. More precisely, first one has

v_{m,n}^{M,N,1}(t_{m+1})=e^{\sqrt{N}f(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}})\Delta_{m,n}W-N\frac{f(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}})^{2}\tau}{2}}u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}\geq 0

for all
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
. Second, using the inequality
G_{nk}^{N}(\tau)\geq 0
(see Section
2.2
), one has

u_{m+1,n}^{{\rm LT}}=v_{m,n}^{M,N,2}(t_{m+1})=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}G_{nk}^{N}(\tau)v_{m,k}^{M,N,1}(t_{m+1})\geq 0.

Thus the positivity property of the numerical solution holds at time
t_{m+1}=(m+1)\tau
.

As a consequence, the property
u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}\geq 0
, for all
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
, holds for any
m\in\{0,\ldots,M\}
. The proof of Proposition
4
is completed.
∎

3.2.
Convergence results

Let us now prove that the proposed numerical scheme provides accurate approximation of the exact solution.
In this article, we show mean-square error estimates and give orders of convergence with respect to
\tau=T/M
and
h=1/N
.

We impose a CFL stability condition in the sequel to ensure stability and convergence of the Lie–Trotter splitting scheme (
14
)
when applied to the stochastic heat equation (
3
); more precisely, we introduce conditions of the type
\tau\leq\gamma h
or
\tau\leq\gamma h^{2}
in the statements below, for some (nonrandom) arbitrary parameter
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
. The conditions on
\tau
and
h
above are equivalent to the conditions
\gamma M\geq TN
and
\gamma M\geq TN^{2}
on
M
and
N
respectively.

Owing to Proposition
3
, it is sufficient to focus on the error
u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}-u_{n}^{N}(t_{m})
to obtain estimates for the total error
u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}-u(t_{m},x_{n})
. Proposition
5
shows moment bounds of the numerical solution and
is used to prove our main result in Theorem
6
. As a corollary we obtain convergence of
u^{{\rm LT}}_{m,n}
to the exact solution
u(t_{m},x_{n})

at the grid points using results from
[
35
]
.

Note that Assumption
2
on the positivity of the initial value is not needed in the statements on the moment bounds and on the convergence of the scheme below.

Proposition 5
.

Assume that Assumptions
1
and
3
are satisfied. Let the sequence
u_{0}^{{\rm LT}},\ldots,u_{M}^{{\rm LT}}
be given by the Lie–Trotter splitting scheme (
14
).

For all
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
and all
T\in(0,\infty)
, there exists
C_{\gamma,T}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
\tau=T/M
and
h=1/N
satisfying the condition
\tau\leq\gamma h
, one has

(15)

\underset{0\leq m\leq M}{\sup}~\underset{1\leq n\leq N-1}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}\left[|u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}|^{2}\right]\leq C_{\gamma,T}\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}\bigr).

The proof of this proposition also provides moment bounds for a space-time continuous version
u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x)
,
defined by equation (
21
), of the Lie–Trotter splitting scheme (
14
).

We are now in position to state the main convergence result of this article. For ease of presentation, we only consider errors at space-time grid points.

Theorem 6
.

Assume that Assumptions
1
and
3
are satisfied. Let the sequence
u_{0}^{{\rm LT}},\ldots,u_{M}^{{\rm LT}}
be given by the Lie–Trotter scheme (
14
), and let
\bigl(u^{N}(t)\bigr)_{t\geq 0,0\leq n\leq N}
be given by the spatial semi-discretization scheme (
7
).

For all
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
and
T\in(0,\infty)
, there exists
C_{\gamma,T}(u_{0})\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
\tau=T/M
and
h=1/N
satisfying the condition
\tau\leq\gamma h
, one has

(16)

\underset{0\leq m\leq M}{\sup}~\underset{0\leq n\leq N}{\sup}~\left({\mathbb{E}}[|u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}-u_{n}^{N}(t_{m})|^{2}]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{\gamma,T}(u_{0})\left(\tau^{\frac{1}{4}}+\left(\frac{\tau}{h}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

In addition, for all
\tau=T/M
and
h=1/N
satisfying the condition
\tau\leq\gamma h^{2}
, one has

(17)

\underset{0\leq m\leq M}{\sup}~\underset{0\leq n\leq N}{\sup}~\left({\mathbb{E}}[|u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}-u_{n}^{N}(t_{m})|^{2}]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{\gamma,T}(u_{0})\tau^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Proving (
17
) from the error estimate (
16
) under the stronger condition
\tau\leq\gamma h^{2}
is straightforward.

Combining Theorem
6
and Proposition
3
, one directly obtains error estimates for the fully-discrete scheme.

Corollary 7
.

Consider the setting and assumptions of Theorem
6
.
For all
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
and
T\in(0,\infty)
, there exists
C_{\gamma,T}(u_{0})\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
\tau=T/M
and
h=1/N
satisfying the condition
\tau\leq\gamma h^{2}
, one has

(18)

\underset{0\leq m\leq M}{\sup}~\underset{0\leq n\leq N}{\sup}~\left({\mathbb{E}}[|u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}-u(t_{m},x_{n})|^{2}]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{\gamma,T}(u_{0})h^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We postpone the proofs of the above results to Section
5
.

Proofs of the main results

The objective of this section is to provide the proof of the results stated in Section

5.1.
Auxiliary process

In this section, for any
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we define an auxiliary stochastic process
\bigl(u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x)\bigr)_{t\in[0,T],x\in[0,1]}
satisfying
u^{{\rm LT}}(t_{m},x_{n})=u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}
for all
m\in\{0,\ldots,M\}
and
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
. The auxiliary process
u^{{\rm LT}}
is piecewise continuous with respect to the spatial variable
x
, while its temporal evolution on each interval
(t_{m},t_{m+1})
follows a stochastic differential equation similar to (
10
).

Recall that the auxiliary mappings
\kappa^{N}\colon[0,1]\to\{x_{0},\ldots,x_{N}\}
and
\ell^{M}\colon[0,T]\to\{t_{0},\ldots,t_{M}\}
are defined in Sections
2.2
and
4
respectively.

Let
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
and
m\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}
, then for all
t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1}]
set

(19)

u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}G_{nk}^{N}(\ell^{M}(t)-t_{m})v_{m,k}^{M,N,1}(t),

where
v_{m,n}^{M,N,1}(t)=\exp\left(\sqrt{N}f(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}})\bigl(W_{n}^{N}(t)-W_{n}^{N}(t_{m})\bigr)-\frac{Nf(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}})^{2}(t-t_{m})}{2}\right)u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}

is the explicit expression (
12
) of the solution at time
t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1}]
of the auxiliary stochastic subsystem (
10
) used in the construction of the splitting integrator. Observe that
u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}(t)=v_{m,n}^{M,N,1}(t)
for all
t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1})
, and, in particular, that
u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}(t_{m})=u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}
. Moreover, by the construction of the splitting scheme, see (
14
), it holds that
u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}(t_{m+1})=u_{m+1,n}^{{\rm LT}}
.

As a result, for any
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
, the mapping
u_{n}^{{\rm LT}}\colon t\in[0,T]\mapsto u_{n}^{{\rm LT}}(t)
defined such that
u_{n}^{{\rm LT}}(t)=u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}(t)
for
t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1}]
is well-defined. It is continuous on each interval
[t_{m},t_{m+1})
, and one has
u_{n}^{{\rm LT}}(t_{m})=u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}
for all
m\in\{0,\ldots,M\}
.

We claim that the following identity holds: for all
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, for all
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
and
t\in[0,T]
, one has

(20)

u_{n}^{{\rm LT}}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}G_{nk}^{N}(\ell^{M}(t))u_{k,0}^{{\rm LT}}+\sqrt{N}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}G_{nk}^{N}(\ell^{M}(t)-\ell^{M}(s))u_{k}^{{\rm LT}}(s)f(u_{k}^{{\rm LT}}(\ell^{M}(s))\,\text{d}W_{k}^{N}(s).

The proof is based on a straightforward recursion argument.

Recall from Section
2.2
that one has the identities
NG_{nk}^{N}(t)=G^{N}(t,x_{n},x_{k})
and
\sqrt{N}\text{d}W_{n}^{N}(t)=N\bigl(W(t,x_{n+1})-W(t,x_{n})\bigr)
.
We are now in position to provide the definition of the auxiliary process
u^{{\rm LT}}
: for
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in[0,1]
, define

(21)

\displaystyle u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t,x,y)u_{0}(\kappa^{N}(y))\,\text{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(\ell^{M}(t)-\ell^{M}(s),x,y)u^{{\rm LT}}(s,\kappa^{N}(y))f(u^{{\rm LT}}(\ell^{M}(s),\kappa^{N}(y))\,\text{d}W(s,y).

In the identity (
21
) above, it is worth recalling that
x\in[0,1]\mapsto G^{N}(t,x,y)
is a piecewise linear mapping, whereas
y\in[0,1]\mapsto G^{N}(t,x,y)
is a piecewise constant mapping, with
G^{N}(t,x_{n},x_{k})=NG_{nk}^{N}(t)
for all
1\leq n,k\leq N-1
and
t\in[0,T]
.

Combining (
20
) and (
21
), one obtains the identity
u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x_{n})=u_{n}^{{\rm LT}}(t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
and
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
, and therefore one obtains the required property
u^{{\rm LT}}(t_{m},x_{n})=u_{n}^{{\rm LT}}(t_{m})=u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}
. Note that, for any
t\in[0,T]
, the mapping
x\in[0,1]\mapsto u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x)
is piecewise linear, more precisely it is linear on each subinterval
[x_{n},x_{n+1}]
.

Auxiliary inequalities

In this subsection we state several inequalities used in the convergence analysis of the splitting scheme.

•

For any continuous function
v\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
, one has (see for instance

\underset{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\sup}~\underset{t\geq 0}{\sup}~\underset{x\in[0,1]}{\sup}~\Big|\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t,x,y)v(\kappa^{N}(y))\,\text{d}y\Big|\leq\underset{x\in[0,1]}{\sup}~|v(x)|.

For all
T\in(0,\infty)
, there exists
C_{T}\in(0,\infty)

[

(23)

For all

, there exists
C_{T}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all

and all
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
one has

(24)

\underset{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\sup}~\underset{x\in[0,1]}{\sup}~\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big|G^{N}(t-s,x,y)-G^{N}(t-\ell^{M}(s),x,y)\big|^{2}\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}s\leq C_{T}\sqrt{\tau}.

A
.
Note that the proof is similar to the proof of
[

.

Let us also recall the following discrete Grönwall inequality, see for instance
[
48
, Lemma A.4]
: assume that a sequence
\bigl(a_{m}\bigr)_{0\leq m\leq M}
of nonnegative numbers satisfies the inequality

a_{m}\leq A+C\tau\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{a_{k}}{\sqrt{t_{m}-t_{k}}},

where we recall that
t_{k}=k\tau=\frac{kT}{M}
, for some
A,C\in(0,\infty)
. Then, there exists
C_{T}\in(0,\infty)
, depending only on
C
and on

, such that one has

(25)

\underset{0\leq m\leq M}{\sup}~a_{m}\leq C_{T}A.

5.3.
Moment bounds

The objective of this section is to prove Proposition
5
. Recall that this requires to impose the condition
\tau\leq\gamma h

where we recall that
\tau=T/M
,
h=1/N
and where
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
is an arbitrary parameter.

Proof of Proposition
5
.

Using the definition (
21
) of the auxiliary process
u^{{\rm LT}}
, for all
m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
and
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
, one has

\displaystyle u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}

\displaystyle=u^{{\rm LT}}(t_{m},x_{n})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m},x_{n},y)u_{0}(\kappa^{N}(y))\,\text{d}y

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m}-\ell^{M}(s),x_{n},y)u^{{\rm LT}}(s,\kappa^{N}(y))f(u^{{\rm LT}}(\ell^{M}(s),\kappa^{N}(y))\,\text{d}W(s,y).

Using Itô’s isometry formula, one obtains

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}|^{2}]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}[\big|\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m},x_{n},y)u_{0}(\kappa^{N}(y))\,\text{d}y\big|^{2}]

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}|G^{N}(t_{m}-\ell^{M}(s),x_{n},y)|^{2}{\mathbb{E}}[|u^{{\rm LT}}(s,\kappa^{N}(y))|^{2}|f(u^{{\rm LT}}(\ell^{M}(s),\kappa^{N}(y))|^{2}]\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}s.

On the one hand, using the auxiliary inequality (
22
) and Assumption
1
, one obtains

{\mathbb{E}}[\big|\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m},x_{n},y)u_{0}(\kappa^{N}(y))\,\text{d}y\big|^{2}]\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}.

On the other hand, recall that Assumption
3
implies that
f
is bounded by
{\rm L_{g}}
. In addition, for all
k\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
and all
s\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})
, one has

{\mathbb{E}}[|u^{{\rm LT}}(s,\kappa^{N}(y))|^{2}]={\mathbb{E}}[|v_{k,n}^{M,N,1}(s)|^{2}]

where
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
is such that
\kappa^{N}(y)=x_{n}
and
\bigl(v_{k,n}^{M,N,1}(s)\bigr)_{s\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}
is the solution of the auxiliary stochastic subsystem (
10
). Using the expression (
12
) for the solution of (
10
) and the tower property of conditional expectation, one obtains the upper bound

{\mathbb{E}}[|v_{k,n}^{M,N,1}(s)|^{2}]={\mathbb{E}}[e^{\frac{Nf(u_{k,n}^{{\rm LT}})^{2}(s-t_{k})}{2}}|u_{k,n}^{{\rm LT}}|^{2}]\leq e^{\frac{N\tau{\rm L_{g}}^{2}}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}[|u_{k,n}^{{\rm LT}}|^{2}]\leq e^{\frac{{\rm L_{g}}^{2}\gamma}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}[|u_{k,n}^{{\rm LT}}|^{2}],

using the boundedness of
f
and the condition
N\tau\leq\gamma
.

Using the auxiliary inequality (
23
), gathering the upper bounds above yields the following inequality: for all
m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
one has

\underset{1\leq n\leq N-1}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}|^{2}]\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}+C_{\gamma,T}\tau\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t_{m}-t_{k}}}\underset{1\leq n\leq N-1}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|u_{k,n}^{{\rm LT}}|^{2}].

Using the discrete Grönwall inequality (
25
) then gives

(26)

\underset{0\leq m\leq M}{\sup}~\underset{1\leq n\leq N-1}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}|^{2}]\leq C_{\gamma,T}\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2},

where
C_{\gamma,T}\in(0,\infty)
is independent of
M
,
N
and
\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}
. This shows moment bounds of the numerical solution at the grid.
It remains to extend this moment bound for
u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x_{n})
when
t
is no longer assumed to be a grid point
t_{m}
.

For all
t\in[0,T)
and
n\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
, let
m\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}
be such that
t_{m}=\ell^{M}(t)
, using the same arguments as above one has

{\mathbb{E}}[|u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x_{n})|^{2}]={\mathbb{E}}[|v_{m,n}^{M,N,1}(t)|^{2}]\leq e^{\frac{{\rm L_{g}}^{2}\gamma}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}[|u_{k,n}^{{\rm LT}}|^{2}]\leq C_{\gamma,T}\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2},

where the inequality (
26
) is used in the last step. As a consequence, one has

(27)

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{1\leq n\leq N-1}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x_{n})|^{2}]\leq C_{\gamma,T}\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Finally, since
x\mapsto u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x)
is linear on each subinterval
[x_{n},x_{n+1}]
, one obtains

(28)

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{x\in[0,1]}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x)|^{2}]\leq\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}~\underset{1\leq n\leq N-1}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x_{n})|^{2}]\leq C_{\gamma,T}\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}.

The proof of Proposition
5
is thus completed.
∎

A straightforward consequence of Proposition
5
is the following result.

Lemma 8
.

Let Assumption
1
and Assumption
3
be satisfied. Let
\left(u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x)\right)_{t\in[0,T],x\in[0,1]}
be given by the mild formula (
21
).

For all
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
and all
T\in(0,\infty)
, there exists
C_{\gamma,T}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
\tau=T/M
and
h=1/N
satisfying the condition
\tau\leq\gamma h
, for all
m\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}
and all
t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1})
, one has

(29)

\underset{1\leq n\leq N-1}{\sup}~\bigl({\mathbb{E}}[|u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x_{n})-u^{{\rm LT}}(t_{m},x_{n})|^{2}]\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{\gamma,T}(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty})\left(\frac{\tau}{h}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof of Lemma
8
.

Let
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
and
m\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}
, then for all
t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1})
one has

\displaystyle u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x_{n})-u^{{\rm LT}}(t_{m},x_{n})

\displaystyle=v_{m,n}^{M,N,1}(t)-v_{m,n}^{M,N,1}(t_{m})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{N}\int_{t_{m}}^{t}v_{m,n}^{M,N,1}(s)f(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}})\,\text{d}W_{n}^{N}(s)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{N}\int_{t_{m}}^{t}u^{{\rm LT}}(s,x_{n})f(u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}})\,\text{d}W_{n}^{N}(s),

where we recall that the auxiliary process
\bigl(v_{m,n}^{M,N,1}(t)\bigr)_{t_{m}\leq t\leq t_{m+1}}
is defined by the auxiliary subsystem (
10
) which gives the first step of the splitting procedure, see Section
3.1
.

Since the mapping
f
is bounded, using Itô’s isometry formula, the condition
\tau N\leq\gamma
and the moment bounds (
15
) from Proposition
5
, one obtains

{\mathbb{E}}[|u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x_{n})-u^{{\rm LT}}(t_{m},x_{n})|^{2}]\leq{\rm L_{g}}^{2}N\tau{\mathbb{E}}[|u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}|^{2}]\leq{\rm L_{g}}^{2}C_{\gamma,T}\bigl(1+\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}\bigr)\tau h^{-1}.

The proof of Lemma
8
is thus completed.
∎

5.4.
Convergence analysis

This section is devoted to the proof of the mean-square convergence of the splitting scheme given in Theorem
6
.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

Recall that
u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}=u^{{\rm LT}}(t_{m},x_{n})
for all
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
and
m\in\{0,\ldots,M\}
, where
\bigl(u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x)\bigr)_{t\in[0,1],x\in[0,1]}
is the process defined by (
19
).

For all
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
and
m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
, let us define

E_{m,n}=u^{N}(t_{m},x_{n})-u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 18.49988ptE_{m}=\underset{1\leq n\leq N-1}{\sup}~{\mathbb{E}}[|E_{m,n}|^{2}].

Using the expression (
6
) for
u^{N}(t,x)
and the expression (
21
) for
u^{{\rm LT}}(t,x)
, one obtains the following decomposition of the error: for all
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
and
m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
, one has

\displaystyle E_{m,n}

\displaystyle=u^{N}(t_{m},x_{n})-u^{{\rm LT}}(t_{m},x_{n})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m}-s,x,y)g(u^{N}(s,\kappa^{N}(y)))\,\text{d}W(s,y)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m}-\ell^{M}(s),x,y)u^{{\rm LT}}(s,\kappa^{N}(y))f(u^{{\rm LT}}(\ell^{M}(s),\kappa^{N}(y))\,\text{d}W(s,y)

\displaystyle=E_{m,n}^{(1)}+E_{m,n}^{(2)},

where we set

\displaystyle E_{m,n}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m}-s,x,y)\bigl[g(u^{N}(s,\kappa^{N}(y)))-u^{{\rm LT}}(s,\kappa^{N}(y))f(u^{{\rm LT}}(\ell^{M}(s),\kappa^{N}(y))\bigr]\,\text{d}W(s,y),

\displaystyle E_{m,n}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}\bigl[G^{N}(t_{m}-s,x,y)-G^{N}(t_{m}-\ell^{M}(s),x,y)\bigr]u^{{\rm LT}}(s,\kappa^{N}(y))f(u^{{\rm LT}}(\ell^{M}(s),\kappa^{N}(y))\,\text{d}W(s,y).

Let us first deal with the error term
E_{m,n}^{(1)}
. Recall that
g(u)=uf(u)
, therefore one has the decomposition
E_{m,n}^{(1)}=E_{m,n}^{(1,1)}+E_{m,n}^{(1,2)}+E_{m,n}^{(1,3)}
, where

\displaystyle E_{m,n}^{(1,1)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m}-s,x,y)\bigl[g(u^{N}(s,\kappa^{N}(y)))-g(u^{N}(\ell^{M}(s),\kappa^{N}(y)))\bigr]\,\text{d}W(s,y)

\displaystyle E_{m,n}^{(1,2)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m}-s,x,y)\bigl[g(u^{N}(\ell^{M}(s),\kappa^{N}(y)))-g(u^{{\rm LT}}(\ell^{M}(s),\kappa^{N}(y)))\bigr]\,\text{d}W(s,y)

\displaystyle E_{m,n}^{(1,3)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m}-s,x,y)\bigl[u^{{\rm LT}}(\ell^{M}(s),\kappa^{N}(y))-u^{{\rm LT}}(s,\kappa^{N}(y))\bigr]f(u^{{\rm LT}}(\ell^{M}(s),\kappa^{N}(y)))\,\text{d}W(s,y).

Using Itô’s isometry formula, the global Lipschitz continuity assumption on
g
, one obtains

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|E_{m,n}^{(1,1)}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq{\rm L_{g}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m}-s,x,y)^{2}{\mathbb{E}}[|u^{N}(s,\kappa^{N}(y))-u^{N}(\ell^{M}(s),\kappa^{N}(y))|^{2}]\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}(u_{0}){\rm L_{g}}^{2}\sqrt{\tau}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m}-s,x,y)^{2}\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}(u_{0})\sqrt{\tau},

where we have used the temporal regularity estimate (
9
) for
u^{N}
and the auxiliary inequality (
23
).

Similarly, using Itô’s isometry formula, the global Lipschitz continuity assumption on
g
, one obtains

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|E_{m,n}^{(1,2)}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq{\rm L_{g}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m}-s,x,y)^{2}{\mathbb{E}}[|u^{N}(\ell^{M}(s),\kappa^{N}(y))-u^{{\rm LT}}(\ell^{M}(s),\kappa^{N}(y))|^{2}]\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}E_{k}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m}-s,x,y)^{2}\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}s.

Using the inequality (
23
), for all
k\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m}-s,x,y)^{2}\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\frac{C_{T}}{\sqrt{t_{m}-s}}\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle=2C_{T}\bigl(\sqrt{t_{m}-t_{k}}-\sqrt{t_{m}-t_{k+1}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=2C_{T}\sqrt{t_{m}-t_{k}}\Bigl(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{\tau}{t_{m}-t_{k}}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C_{T}\tau}{\sqrt{t_{m}-t_{k}}},

where we have used the inequality
1-\sqrt{1-z}\leq z
for all
z\in[0,1]
in the last step. Therefore one has

{\mathbb{E}}[|E_{m,n}^{(1,2)}|^{2}]\leq C_{T}\tau\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{E_{k}}{\sqrt{t_{m}-t_{k}}}.

Finally, for the third term, using Itô’s isometry formula and the boundedness of
f
, one obtains

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|E_{m,n}^{(1,3)}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq L_{g}^{2}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m}-s,x,y)^{2}{\mathbb{E}}[|u^{{\rm LT}}(\ell^{M}(s),\kappa^{N}(y))-u^{{\rm LT}}(s,\kappa^{N}(y))|^{2}]\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma,T}(u_{0})\tau h^{-1}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}G^{N}(t_{m}-s,x,y)^{2}\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma,T}(u_{0})\tau h^{-1}

using the temporal regularity estimate (
29
) from Lemma
8
for
u^{{\rm LT}}
and the auxiliary inequality (
23
).

Let us now deal with the error term
E_{m,n}^{(2)}
. Using Itô’s formula, the boundedness of
f
and the moment bounds (
15
) from Proposition
5
, one obtains

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|E_{m,n}^{(2)}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq{\rm L_{g}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}\big|G^{N}(t-\ell^{M}(s),x,y)-G^{N}(t_{m}-\ell^{M}(s),x,y)\big|^{2}{\mathbb{E}}[|u^{{\rm LT}}(s,\kappa^{N}(y))|^{2}]\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma,T}(u_{0})\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}\big|G^{N}(t-\ell^{M}(s),x,y)-G^{N}(t_{m}-\ell^{M}(s),x,y)\big|^{2}\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma,T}(u_{0})\sqrt{\tau},

owing to the auxiliary inequality (
24
) in the last step.

Gathering the estimates, for all
m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
, one has

E_{m}\leq C_{\gamma,T}(u_{0})\left(\sqrt{\tau}+\tau h^{-1}\right)+C_{T}\tau\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{E_{k}}{\sqrt{t_{m}-t_{k}}}.

Applying the discrete Grönwall inequality (
25
) (see Section
5.2
) then yields

\underset{0\leq m\leq M}{\sup}~E_{m}\leq C_{\gamma,T}(u_{0})\left(\sqrt{\tau}+\frac{\tau}{h}\right).

This gives the error estimate (
16
). When the condition
\tau\leq\gamma h^{2}
is satisfied, one has
\tau h^{-1}\leq\sqrt{\gamma}\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}
and one has the error estimate (
17
). This concludes the proof of Theorem
6
.
∎

Let us also provide the proof of Corollary
7
.

Proof of Corollary
7
.

It suffices to combine the error estimate (
8
) from Proposition
3
for the spatial discretization error, and the error estimate (
16
) from Theorem
6
for the temporal discretization error. One then obtains the error estimate for the splitting scheme

\displaystyle\bigl({\mathbb{E}}[|u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}-u(t_{m},x_{n})|^{2}]\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl({\mathbb{E}}[|u_{m,n}^{{\rm LT}}-u^{N}(t_{m},x_{n})|^{2}]\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\bigl({\mathbb{E}}[|u^{N}(t_{m},x_{n})-u(t_{m},x_{n})|^{2}]\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma,T}(u_{0})\tau^{\frac{1}{4}}+C_{T}(u_{0})h^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma,T}(u_{0})\gamma^{\frac{1}{4}}h^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{T}(u_{0})h^{\frac{1}{2}},

under the condition
\tau\leq\gamma h^{2}
. This gives the error estimate (
18
) and concludes the proof of Corollary
7
.
∎

Proof of Proposition

We conclude this section with the proof of the positivity property of the exact solution to the stochastic heat equation (

Proof of Proposition
2
.

Owing to Corollary
7
and to the temporal regularity estimate (
5
) satisfied by the solution
u
of the SPDE in equation (

), one obtains the following result (recall that
\tau=T/M
and
h=1/N
):
there exists
C_{\gamma,T}(u_{0})\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that

, for all

and

(30)

\bigl({\mathbb{E}}[|u(t,x)-u^{{\rm LT}}(\ell^{M}(t),\kappa^{N}(x))|^{2}]\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{\gamma,T}(u_{0})N^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

6.
Generalization to systems

In this section, we briefly describe how to generalize the construction of the splitting scheme (
14
) and the analysis above to stochastic systems of the type

(31)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{d}u_{1}(t,x)=\partial_{xx}^{2}u_{1}(t,x)\,\text{d}t+g_{1}(u_{1}(t,x),u_{2}(t,x))\,\text{d}W_{1}(t,x),\\
&\text{d}u_{2}(t,x)=\partial_{xx}^{2}u_{2}(t,x)\,\text{d}t+g_{2}(u_{1}(t,x),u_{2}(t,x))\,\text{d}W_{2}(t,x),\\
&u_{1}(t,0)=u_{1}(t,1)=0,\hskip 9.24994ptu_{2}(t,0)=u_{2}(t,1)=0,\\
&u_{1}(0,x)=u_{1,0}(x),\hskip 9.24994ptu_{2}(0,x)=u_{2,0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

for
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times[0,1]
, where
g_{1},g_{2}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
are globally Lipschitz continuous mappings, with initial values
u_{1,0},u_{2,0}
satisfying Assumptions
1
and
2
. The two evolution equations are driven by space-time white noise. The Wiener sheets
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
can either be equal or independent. For ease of presentation we only deal with systems of two equations, while considering systems of arbitrary size would also be possible.

In this setting, to obtain solutions which only have nonnegative values, it is necessary to replace Assumption
3
by the following.

Appendix A
Proof of auxiliary inequalities

Proof of the auxiliary inequality (
24
).

Let us recall some notation. For all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, all
t\geq 0
and
x,y\in[0,1]
, one has

G^{N}(t,x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}e^{-\lambda_{j}^{N}t}\varphi_{j}^{N}(x)\varphi_{j}(\kappa^{N}(y)),

where
\lambda_{j}^{N}=4N^{2}\sin\bigl(\frac{j\pi}{2N}\bigr)^{2}
,
\varphi_{j}(\cdot)=\sqrt{2}\sin(j\pi\cdot)
and
\varphi_{j}^{N}
is the linear interpolation of
\varphi_{j}
at the space grid points
x_{n}=nh
for
n=1,\ldots,N-1
.

Using the orthogonality property

\int_{0}^{1}\varphi_{j}(\kappa^{N}(y))\varphi_{k}(\kappa^{N}(y))\,\text{d}y=\delta_{jk},

one obtains

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big|G^{N}(t-s,x,y)

\displaystyle-G^{N}(t-\ell^{M}(s),x,y)\big|^{2}\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big|\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\bigl(e^{-\lambda_{j}^{N}(t-s)}-e^{-\lambda_{j}^{N}(t-\ell^{M}(s))}\bigr)\varphi_{j}^{N}(x)\varphi_{j}(\kappa^{N}(y))\big|^{2}\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\bigl(e^{-\lambda_{j}^{N}(t-s)}-e^{-\lambda_{j}^{N}(t-\ell^{M}(s))}\bigr)^{2}\varphi_{j}^{N}(x)^{2}\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\bigl(e^{-\lambda_{j}^{N}(t-s)}-e^{-\lambda_{j}^{N}(t-\ell^{M}(s))}\bigr)^{2}\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\lambda_{j}^{N}(t-s)}\bigl(1-e^{-\lambda_{j}^{N}(s-\ell^{M}(s))}\bigr)^{2}\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{\max(1,\lambda_{j}^{N}\tau)^{2}}{\lambda_{j}^{N}}.

One checks that there exists
c\in(1,\infty)
such that for all
N\geq 1
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
one has

c^{-1}\leq\frac{\lambda_{j}^{N}}{j^{2}}\leq c.

Let
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
be an arbitrary positive integer. Owing to the inequalities above, one obtains

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{\max(1,\lambda_{j}^{N}\tau)^{2}}{\lambda_{j}^{N}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\max(1,j^{2}\tau)^{2}}{j^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{L}j^{2}\tau^{2}+C\sum_{j=L+1}^{\infty}j^{-2}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau^{2}L^{3}+CL^{-1},

using standard comparison of series and integrals arguments. Choosing
L=\lfloor\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rfloor\geq 1
(where
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes the integer part), and recalling that
\tau\in(0,1)
, one obtains

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big|G^{N}(t-s,x,y)-G^{N}(t-\ell^{M}(s),x,y)\big|^{2}\,\text{d}y\,\text{d}s\leq C\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The value of
C
is independent of
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
t\in(0,T]
and
x\in[0,1]
. The proof of the auxiliary inequality (
24
) is thus completed.

Nodal higher-order topological superconductivity from C 6 C_{6} -symmetric Dirac semimetals
Guan-Hao Feng

Abstract
Three-dimensional Dirac semimetals (DSMs) have been shown to exhibit one-dimensional hinge modes which are termed the higher-order hinge Fermi-arc (HOFA) states. They are the topological consequences of Dirac points. Superconducting states from Dirac semimetals can inherit the Dirac points to form nodal Dirac superconducting states, raising a question of whether there exists a topological superconducting bulk-hinge correspondence similar to DSMs. In this work, we discuss the nodal superconducting states from half-filled DSMs respecting non-magnetic (Type-II) Shubnikov space group (SSG) P ​ 6 / m ​ m ​ m ​ 1 ′ P6/mmm1^{\prime} . We find that the BdG Dirac points can lead to higher-order topological Dirac superconducting (HOTDSC) states instead of the expected higher-order Majorana-arc (HOMA) states. The HOTDSC states can be regarded as a crossing between the HOFAs in normal states and the BdG shadow states. We demonstrate that HOTDSC states can be indicated by relative topologies of BdG Dirac points by utilizing the theory of magnetic topological quantum chemistry (MTQC).



I
INTRODUCTION

Topological semimetals are bulk-gapless materials with topological
boundary modes
Zhang et al. 2019
. After the discovery
of the 2D DSM graphene which exhibits 1D arc-like boundary
states along zigzag edges
Song et al. 2019
, 3D nodal semimetals
have attracted much attention due to their novel topological surface
states
Wang et al. 2012
;
Xiao and Yan 2021
;
Xu et al. 2020
;
Kruthoff et al. 2017
.
For example, Weyl semimetals host Fermi-arc surface states
Tamai et al. 2016
,
and centrosymmetry nodal-line semimetals host drumhead surface states
Kim et al. 2015
.
From the perspective of the theory of electronic band structure (BS),
despite the presence of nodal points in these semimetals, bands are still
gapped away from nodal points in the Brillouin zone (BZ), such
that the topological invariants are well-defined to allow for the
existence of topological surface states. However, in the presence
of the time-reversal and inversion symmetries, the Fermi-arc surface
states in 3D DSMs are not topologically protected
since the Dirac cones are not the source of Berry curvature. Despite
the absence of topological surface states, it has been recently
shown that for certain DSMs, intrinsic polarization-nontrivial HOFA states are the direct topological consequences of
the Dirac cones
Wieder et al. 2020
;
Lin and Hughes 2018
;
Fang and Cano 2021a
. Regarding one of the momenta as cyclic tuning
parameters, the 2D subsystems can be viewed as topological (crystalline)
insulators when the tunable momentum is away from the Dirac points
Slager et al. 2013
.
Such that the Dirac points are the critical points between two different
topological (crystalline) insulating states. When the 2D subsystems
are either obstructed atomic limit or fragile topological states
with intrinsic
0
D corner modes
Fang and Cano 2021b
;
Gao et al. 2022
;
Ma et al. 2023
;
Schindler et al. 2018
;
Călugăru et al. 2019
,
the nontrivial pumping cycles of these 2nd-order topological states
are equivalent to 3D DSMs with HOFA states
Wieder et al. 2020
.
Thus the HOFA states can be indicated by the 2D filling anomaly of
the subsystems, which can be calculated by the theory of MTQC
Vergniory et al. 2019
;
Cano and Bradlyn 2021
;
Tang et al. 2019a
;
Tang et al. 2019b
;
Bradlyn et al. 2017
;
Tang et al. 2019c
;
Elcoro et al. 2021
.

These Dirac cones can be inherited by the superconducting states which
may lead to HOMA states
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Ahn and Yang 2020
;
Wu and Wang 2022
.
The superconductors with BdG Dirac points are termed the nodal Dirac superconductors

Agterberg et al. 2017
;
Tang et al. 2022
. Similar
to HOFA states, HOMA states are related to the
0
D Majorana corner
modes
Hsu et al. 2020
and can be indicated by
2D BdG filling anomalies. However, not all the nodal Dirac superconductors exhibit
HOMAs. For example, the nodal Dirac superconducting states respecting Type-II
SSG
P4/mmm1^{\prime}
can host HOMA states in odd-parity pairing channels
Wu and Wang 2022
,
whereas the nodal Dirac superconducting states respecting
P6/mmm1^{\prime}
can
only host HOTDSC states
in the time-reversal invariant planes
Zhang et al. 2020
. The different topological properties
of the BdG Dirac points have thus remained an open question as to how the
BdG Dirac points can exhibit higher-order topology inherited from
the Dirac points in normal states.
In Ref.
Zhang et al. 2020
the HOTDSC states are defined as 2D Majorana corner states in the time-reversal invariant planes in nodal Dirac superconductors. We will continue to adhere to this definition and further show that the HOTDSC states are fragile topological states.

In Ref.
Fang and Cano 2021a
, the relative topology is employed to classify the Dirac points in normal states with or without HOFA states. The relative topology concerns if two states
seperated by a bulk nodal point are topologically distinct when all
the symmetries are preserved throughout the deforming process
Po et al. 2018
,
which can be indicated by changes in topological invariants.
In this work, we advance the relative topology to BdG Dirac points and employ the
theory of MTQC to discuss the possible higher-order topological states
in nodal Dirac superconductors respecting Type-II SSG
P6/mmm1^{\prime}
. Given
that the conventional (double) symmetry indicators (SIs)
Song et al. 2018
;
Khalaf et al. 2018
;
Po et al. 2017

used in insulators fall short in distinguishing various topological
superconductors such as the 1D Kitaev chain
Skurativska et al. 2020
;
Ono et al. 2019
;
Ono et al. 2021
,
we introduce the refined SIs in Ref.
Ono et al. 2020
. By
calculating the difference of refined SIs and BdG filling anomalies between
two sides of a BdG Dirac point, we can determine the occurrence of
HOMA or HOTDSC states. We note that although higher-order
topological phases can occur in both crystalline and non-crystalline systems
Yang et al. 2024
;
Manna et al. 2022
;
Manna and Roy 2023
;
Manna et al. 2024
, the relative topology here is only applicable to crystalline systems.

Our results show that the BdG Dirac points can be grouped into two
types:
symmetry-protected
BdG Dirac points and
accidental

BdG Dirac points. A
symmetry-protected
BdG Dirac point arises from
the crossing of two distinct band corepresentations (coreps) and the relative
topology is nontrivial, whereas the
accidental
BdG Dirac
point arises from the crossing of two identical band representations
and the relative topology is trivial. We find that 3D nodal Dirac superconductors
respecting Type-II SSG
P6/mmm1^{\prime}

cannot
host HOMA states
since that all the refined SIs of the 2D planes with
k_{z}\neq 0,\pi

vanish. Instead, for
symmetry-protected
nodal Dirac superconducting states,
there are HOTDSC states at the time-reversal invariant planes only
if the corresponding normal state is HOFA state and the pairing channels
are
B_{1u}
or
B_{2u}
. Interestingly, we demonstrate that the
HOFA states in normal states are obstructed atomic limit states whereas
the HOTDSC states are fragile topological states. The HOTDSC states
arise from the crossing between the HOFAs in the normal states and
those in the corresponding BdG shadow states. Thus the HOTDSC states
can be viewed as a manifestation of a bulk-hinge correspondence for
the 3D nodal Dirac superconductors respecting Type-II SSG
P6/mmm1^{\prime}
.

,

,

VIII
sup
. This is the main result of this work. In Sec.
IV
,
we construct a 3D DSM tight-binding model and introduce the possible

To discuss the possible nodal topological superconducting states in

V
DISCUSSION

In this work, we utilize the theory of MTQC to systematically discuss
the possible HOTDSC phases in DSMs respecting Type-II SSG
P6/mmm1^{\prime}
.
Our results advance the classification of Dirac points in Ref.
Fang and Cano 2021a

to nodal Dirac superconducting systems. We also reveal the relationship between
HOFAs and HOTDSCs at TRIM planes.

The occurrence of Dirac points in DSMs can be determined by compatibility
relations. These Dirac points can be regarded as critical points
between obstructed atomic insulators and trivial phases. Thus
the Dirac points can be classified by the relative topology. We found
that the Dirac points can be inherited by the superconducting states,
which would lead to the BdG Dirac points. We also found that not all
the BdG Dirac points host nontrivial relative topology. Only the
symmetry-protected

BdG Dirac points can be related to HOTDSC states.

For the superconducting states in DSMs respecting Type-II SSG
P6/mmm1^{\prime}
,
our calculation showed that all the refined SIs vanish along the
high-symmetry line
DT
. Thus the BdG Dirac points in these
systems do not exhibit HOMAs as those in Ref.
Wu and Wang 2022
.
Instead, the HOTDSC states would occur at the TRIM planes. For the
symmetry-protected

nodal Dirac superconducting states, the HOTDSC states can be viewed as a
crossing of the HOFAs in normal states and BdG shadow states. We found that
for
symmetry-protected
nodal Dirac superconducting states, the HOTDSC states
can only arise from the HOFA DSMs with
B_{1u}/B_{2u}
pairing representations,
which can be indicated by their relative topologies.

Different from that the HOFA states in DSMs are OAL states, the HOTDSC
states in
symmetry-protected
nodal Dirac superconductors are fragile topological
superconducting states. The HOTDSC states can be viewed as a nontrivial
stacking of two TCIs with opposite mirror Chern numbers. We deduce
that the HOTDSC states in
symmetry-protected
nodal Dirac superconducting
states are the topological bulk-hinge correspondence for the
symmetry-protected

BdG Dirac points.

The tight-binding model for superconducting states with
B_{1u}/B_{2u}
pairings originating from
s-p_{z}
hybridized Dirac semimetals in Sec.
IV.2
is the same as that discussed in Ref.
Zhang et al. 2020
. In fact, the topological invariants introduced in Ref.
Zhang et al. 2020
provide a physical interpretation of the double SIs presented in this paper. The physical interpretation indicates that the double SI formulas and the representative
\bm{B}
vectors here are computed in an arbitrary basis, while the topological invariants in Ref.
Zhang et al. 2020
are expressed in a natural (physical) basis for classifying topological phases. Nevertheless, the SI groups in this paper are consistent with those in Ref.
Zhang et al. 2020
. This paper also extends the discussion to superconducting states in the
A_{1g}/A_{2g}
,
A_{1u}/A_{2u}
,
B_{1g}/B_{2g}
, and
B_{1u}/B_{2u}
pairing channels, as explained in Supplementary Note VIII
sup
.

Specifically, the topological invariants introduced in Ref.
Zhang et al. 2020
are
Q_{j}
,
C_{M_{z}}
, and
\kappa_{2d}
. The topological charge
Q_{j}
is the difference in the rotation eigenvalues between time-reversal-invariant planes at
k_{z}=0
and
k_{z}=\pi
. This is equivalent to the compatibility relation analysis in Sec.
II
, where the occurrence of the (BdG) Dirac points is determined using the theory of band representation. The mirror Chern number
C_{M_{z}}
for the normal states is also introduced here in the form of an SI
z_{6}
for the layer group
p6/mmm1^{\prime}
, as detailed in Eq.
10
of Sec.
II
and Supplementary Note II
sup
. The mirror Chern number for BdG systems,
C_{M_{z}}^{\text{BdG}}
, is given by Eq.
15
. Moreover, the topological invariant
\kappa_{2d}
in Ref.
Zhang et al. 2020
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
inversion symmetry indicator, which is consistent with the refined SI
z_{4}
in this paper (see Eq.
21
in Sec.
III
and Supplementary Note VI D for further details
sup
).

Our results in Supplementary Note VIII indicate that only four cases of DSMs in the
B_{1u}/B_{2u}
superconducting pairing channels can lead to HOTDSC states
sup
. These are the DSMs with small coreps subduced at
\Gamma

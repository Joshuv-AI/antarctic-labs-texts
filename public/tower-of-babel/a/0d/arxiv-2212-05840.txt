Abstract
. Let K = 𝐐 ⁡ ( θ ) K={\mathbf{Q}}(\theta) be an algebraic number field with θ \theta satisfying an irreducible polynomial x 9 − a x^{9}-a over the field 𝐐 {\mathbf{Q}} of rationals and 𝐙 K {\mathbf{Z}}_{K} denote the ring of algebraic integers of K K . 2.1.
Construction of an explicit integral basis from
p
-integral basis

If
L={\mathbf{Q}}(\eta)
is an algebraic number field of degree
n
with
\eta
an algebraic integer, then as is well known that there exists an integral basis
\mathcal{B}:=\{\beta_{0},\cdots,\beta_{n-1}\}
of
L
such that
\beta_{0}=1
,

\beta_{i}=\frac{a_{i,0}+a_{i,1}\eta+\cdots+a_{i,i-1}\eta^{i-1}+\eta^{i}}{d_{i}}

with
a_{i,j},~d_{i}\in{\mathbf{Z}}
and the positive integer
d_{i}
dividing
d_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
; moreover the numbers
d_{i}
are uniquely determined by
\eta
and the index
[A_{L}:{\mathbf{Z}}[\eta]]
to be denoted by
I_{L}(\eta)
equals
\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}d_{i}
(cf.
[
15
, Chapter 2, Theorem 13]
). Fix a prime
p
and let
l_{i}
denote its highest power dividing
d_{i}
, then
v_{p}(I_{L}(\eta))=l_{1}+l_{2}+\cdots+l_{n-1}.
Since
\mathcal{B}
is a
p
-integral basis of
L
, so is
\mathcal{B}^{*}:=\{1,\frac{\beta_{1}d_{1}}{p^{l_{1}}},\cdots,\frac{\beta_{n-1}d_{n-1}}{p^{l_{n-1}}}\}
. It can be easily seen that if
\mathcal{C}=\{1,\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n-1}\}
is another
p
-integral basis of
L
where
\gamma_{i}
’
s
are of the type

\gamma_{i}=\frac{c_{i,0}+c_{i,1}\eta+\cdots+c_{i,i-1}\eta^{i-1}+\eta^{i}}{p^{k_{i}}}

with
c_{i,j},~k_{i}
in
{\mathbf{Z}}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, then on writing each member of
\mathcal{B}^{*}
as a
{\mathbf{Z}}_{(p)}
-linear combination of members of
\mathcal{C}
and vice versa, we see that
l_{i}=k_{i}~\forall~i
and hence

v_{p}(I_{L}(\eta))=l_{1}+l_{2}+\cdots+l_{n-1}=k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n-1}.

(2.1)

In fact such a
p
-integral basis of an algebraic number field

is easier to construct than constructing an integral basis of

and these

-integral bases of

with

as follows.

Let

be all the distinct primes dividing the index

Let
\mathcal{B}_{i}=\{\alpha_{i0},\alpha_{i1},

\cdots,\alpha_{i(n-1)}\}
be a
q_{i}
-integral basis of

,
1\leq i\leq r
with

\alpha_{i0}=1,~\alpha_{ij}=\frac{\eta^{j}+\sum\limits_{s=0}^{j-1}c_{js}^{(i)}\eta^{s}}{q_{i}^{k_{i,j}}},~~1\leq j\leq n-1,

where

and

belonging to

\delta_{ij}=\sum\limits_{s=0}^{j-1}c_{js}^{(i)}\eta^{s}.
For a fixed

for

Theorem 2.2
.

Let

and

be as in the above paragraph. Then
\Bigg\{1,\dfrac{\eta^{j}+\beta_{j}}{\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{r}q_{\ell}^{k_{\ell,j}}}~\bigg|~1\leq j\leq n-1\Bigg\}
is an integral basis of
L
.

The following corollary will be quickly deduced using Theorems

and
2.2

Corollary 2.3
.

with

K
.

The following proposition to be used in the sequel follows immediately from what has been

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
L={\mathbf{Q}}(\eta)
be an algebraic number field of degree
n
with
\eta
an algebraic integer and
p
be a prime number. Let
\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{n-1}\}
be
p
-integral elements of
L
of the type
\alpha_{i}=\frac{c_{i,0}+c_{i,1}\eta+\cdots+c_{i,i-1}\eta^{i-1}+\eta^{i}}{p^{k_{i}}}
where
c_{i,j},~k_{i}
are in
{\mathbf{Z}}
with
0\leq k_{i}\leq k_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Then
\{1,\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n-1}\}
is a
p
-integral basis of
L
if and only if
v_{p}(I_{L}(\eta))=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}k_{i}
, in which case the integers
k_{1},~\cdots,k_{n-1}
are uniquely determined by the prime
p
and the element
\eta
of
L
. Moreover there always exists a
p
-integral basis of
L
of the above type.

Let
K={\mathbf{Q}}(\theta)
with
\theta
a root of an irreducible polynomial
f(x)=x^{9}-a
, where

is a

-th power-free integer. Let

, where

are relatively prime and squarefree integers. With these, we now prove a simple lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

for
1\leq k\leq 8
, then
\frac{\theta^{k}}{C_{k}}
is an algebraic integer.

Proof.

Keeping in mind that
\theta^{9}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{8}a_{i}^{i}

\frac{\theta^{k}}{C_{k}}
satisfies the polynomial
x^{9}-\prod\limits_{i=1}^{8}a_{i}^{ik-9\lfloor\frac{ik}{9}\rfloor}

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
K={\mathbf{Q}}(\theta)
with
\theta
satisfying an irreducible polynomial
f(x)=x^{9}-a
having integer coefficient. Let
q
be any prime divisor of
a
such that
q
does not divide
\gcd(9,v_{q}(a))
. Then

v_{q}(I_{K}(\theta))=\frac{8(v_{q}(a)-1)+\gcd(9,v_{q}(a))-1}{2}.

In this case, the set

S=\bigg\{\frac{\theta^{i}}{q^{\lfloor\frac{iv_{q}(a)}{9}\rfloor}}~|~0\leq i\leq 8\bigg\}

is a
q
-integral basis of
K
.

Proof.

The
q
-Newton polygon of
f(x)
consists of only one edge having slope
\lambda=-\frac{v_{q}(a)}{9}
. The residual polynomial of
f(x)
with respect to
q
is

T(f)(Y)=Y^{\gcd(9,v_{q}(a))}-\overline{a/q^{v_{q}(a)}}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}[Y].

Keeping in mind the hypothesis that
q\nmid\gcd(9,v_{q}(a))
, we see that
T(Y)
has no repeated roots. So applying Theorem
3.6
and Lemma
3.10
, we have

v_{q}(I_{K}(\theta))=\frac{1}{2}[8(v_{q}(a)-1)+\gcd(9,v_{q}(a))-1].

(4.3)

Now, in view of Lemma
3.2
,
\frac{\theta^{i}}{q^{\lfloor\frac{iv_{q}(a)}{9}\rfloor}}
is a
q
-integral element for
1\leq i\leq 8
. Applying Lemma
3.10
, we see that

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{8}\lfloor\frac{iv_{q}(a)}{9}\rfloor=\frac{1}{2}[8(v_{q}(a)-1)+\gcd(9,v_{q}(a))-1]=v_{q}(I_{K}(\theta)).

Therefore, in view of Proposition
3.1
, it follows that the set
S=\{\frac{\theta^{i}}{q^{\lfloor\frac{iv_{q}(a)}{9}\rfloor}}~|~0\leq i\leq 8\}
is a
q
-integral basis of
K
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

with
\theta
satisfying an irreducible polynomial
f(x)=x^{9}-a

3
does not divide
a

(i)

9\nmid(a^{2}-1)
, then
v_{3}(I_{K}(\theta))=0
and the set
\{1,\theta,\cdots,\theta^{8}\}
is a
3
-integral basis of
K
.

(ii)

When
v_{3}(a^{2}-1)=2
, then
v_{3}(I_{K}(\theta))=3
and the set
\{1,\theta,\theta^{2},\cdots,\theta^{5},\frac{\theta^{6}+a\theta^{3}+1}{3},

\frac{(\theta^{6}+a\theta^{3}+1)\theta}{3},\frac{(\theta^{6}+a\theta^{3}+1)\theta^{2}}{3}\}
is a
3
-integral basis of
K
.

(iii)

When
27\mid(a^{2}-1)
, then
v_{3}(I_{K}(\theta))=4
and the set
\{1,\theta,\theta^{2},\cdots,\theta^{5},\frac{\theta^{6}+a\theta^{3}+1}{3},

\frac{(\theta^{6}+a\theta^{3}+1)\theta}{3},\frac{\theta^{8}+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{7}(a\theta)^{j}}{9}\}
is a
3
-integral basis of
K
.

Proof.

Set
\xi=\theta-a

{\mathbf{Q}}(\theta)={\mathbf{Q}}(\xi)
, it is sufficient to find out
v_{3}(I_{K}(\xi))
. Note that
\xi
satisfies the minimal polynomial
g(x)=f(x+a)=x^{9}+9x^{8}a+9x^{7}a^{2}+3x^{6}a^{3}+9x^{5}a^{4}+9x^{4}a^{5}+3x^{3}a^{6}+9x^{2}a^{7}+9xa^{8}+a(a^{2}-1)(a^{2}+1)(a^{4}+1)

(i) When

, then one can see that the

-Newton polygon of

has single egde having vertices
(0,1)
and
(9,0)
. Hence the residual polynomial corresponding to this edge is linear in
\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{3}[Y]
. So by Theorem
3.6
, we see that
v_{3}(I_{K}(\xi))
and hence
v_{3}(I_{K}(\theta))
equals the number of points with positive integer coordinates lying on or below the
3
-Newton polygon of

. Therefore, we have
v_{3}(I_{K}(\theta))=0
. Hence by Proposition
3.1
, it follows that
\{1,\theta,\cdots,\theta^{8}\}
is a
3
-integral basis of
K
.

(ii) When
v_{3}(a^{2}-1)=2

3
-Newton polygon of
g(x)
has two egdes having slopes
\frac{-1}{3}
,
\frac{-1}{6}

3.6

-integral when

Reflections to set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation
Andrea ALBANO, Marzia MAZZOTTA, Paola STEFANELLI

Abstract
The main aim of this paper is to determine reflections to bijective and non-degenerate solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation, by exploring their connections with their derived solutions. This is motivated by a recent description of left non-degenerate solutions in terms of a family of automorphisms of their associated left rack. In some cases, we show that the study of reflections for bijective and non-degenerate solutions can be reduced to those of derived type. Moreover, we extend some results obtained in the literature for reflections of involutive non-degenerate solutions to more arbitrary solutions. Besides, we provide ways for defining reflections for solutions obtained by employing some classical construction techniques of solutions. Finally, we gather some numerical data on reflections for bijective non-degenerate solutions associated with skew braces of small order.

Introduction

The
Yang-Baxter equation
was first introduced by Yang
[
31
]
to study a gas of spin with a
\delta
interaction and, independently, by Baxter
[
1
]
to investigate a two-dimensional solvable statistical model. Much of the attention has been directed to the so-called
quantum
Yang-Baxter equation, which is fundamental in mathematical physics since it occupies a central role in the theory of quantum integrable systems
[
21
]
. The search for solutions to this equation has attracted numerous authors, and it is still an open problem. Due to the complexity of the solutions, Drinfel’d
[
14
]
suggested studying a simplified class. Into the specific, if
X
is a set and

r:X\times X\to X\times X
is a map, the pair
(X,r)
is said to be a
set-theoretic solution of the Yang-Baxter equation
, briefly a
solution
, if the identity

\displaystyle\left(r\times\id_{X}\right)\left(\id_{X}\times r\right)\left(r\times\id_{X}\right)=\left(\id_{X}\times r\right)\left(r\times\id_{X}\right)\left(\id_{X}\times r\right)

(YBE)

holds. Writing
r\left(x,y\right)=\left(\lambda_{x}\left(y\right),\rho_{y}\left(x\right)\right)
, where
\lambda_{x}
and
\rho_{y}
are maps from
X
into itself, for all
x,y\in X
, then

r
is a solution if and only if

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}(z)=\lambda_{\lambda_{x}\left(y\right)}\lambda_{\rho_{y}\left(x\right)}\left(z\right)

(Y1)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda_{y}\left(z\right)}\left(x\right)}\rho_{z}\left(y\right)=\rho_{\lambda_{\rho_{y}\left(x\right)}\left(z\right)}\lambda_{x}\left(y\right)

(Y2)

\displaystyle\rho_{z}\rho_{y}(x)=\rho_{\rho_{z}\left(y\right)}\rho_{\lambda_{y}\left(z\right)}\left(x\right)

(Y3)

for all
x,y,z\in X
. With this notation,
a solution is
bijective
if
r
is a bijective map;
involutive
if
r^{2}=\id_{X\times X}
;
idempotent
if
r^{2}=r
;
left non-degenerate
if
\lambda_{x}\in\Sym_{X}
, for all
x\in X
;
right non-degenerate
if
\rho_{x}\in\Sym_{X}
, for all
x\in X
, and
non-degenerate
if it is both left and right non-degenerate. In the beginning, many authors mainly focused on the class of bijective non-degenerate solutions. In the years that followed, the attention has been shifted to more arbitrary classes of solutions and a wide range of papers has been published in the literature.

Similarly to the YBE, the
reflection equation
is an important tool in the theory of quantum groups and integrable systems. This equation was first studied by Cherednik
[
6
]
to encode the reflection on the boundary of particles in quantum field theory and by Sklyanin
[
28
]
to prove the integrability of quantum models with boundaries. The set-theoretic version of this equation was formulated by Caudrelier and Zhang in
[
5
]
, who also provided the first examples of solutions. It turns out that the set-theoretic reflection equation jointly with the YBE ensures the factorization property of the interactions of
N
-soliton solutions on the half-line. For an explanatory illustration, see Figs. 1-2 in
[
27
]
.

If
(X,r)
is an arbitrary solution, a map
\kappa:X\to X
is a
set-theoretic solution of the reflection equation
, or briefly a
reflection
, for
(X,r)
if it holds

\displaystyle r\left(\id_{X}\times\kappa\right)r\left(\id_{X}\times\kappa\right)=\left(\id_{X}\times\kappa\right)r\left(\id_{X}\times\kappa\right)r.

(RE)

One can easily see that (
RE
) is equivalent to requiring that, for all
x,y\in X
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\kappa\rho_{y}\left(x\right)=\lambda_{\lambda_{x}(\kappa(y))}\kappa\rho_{\kappa(y)}\left(x\right),

(T)

\displaystyle\rho_{\kappa\rho_{\kappa(y)}(x)}\lambda_{x}{\kappa(y)}=\kappa\rho_{\kappa\rho_{y}(x)}\lambda_{x}\left(y\right).

(Q)

The interplay between solutions to the YBE and reflections was deepened by Caudrelier, Crampé, and Zhang in
[
4
]
. In particular, they provided a method for constructing all the reflection maps associated with the quadrirational Yang–Baxter maps.
In the following years, Smoktunowicz, Vendramin, and Weston
[
29
]
proposed a first more systematic approach to the set-theoretic case that makes use of ring-theoretic methods, and more generally methods coming from
the theory of braces, to produce families of reflections. The reflections they provide belong to the class of what we call
\lambda
-centralizing maps
, namely, maps
\kappa
such that
\lambda_{x}\kappa=\kappa\lambda_{x}
, for all
x\in X
. Furthermore, De Commer
[
9
]
introduces a notion of braided action of a group with braiding and shows that it provides reflections.
Besides, Doikou and Smoktunowicz
[
13
]
investigate connections between set-theoretic Yang–Baxter and reflection equations and quantum integrable systems. More recently, Lebed and Vendramin
[
27
]
have constructed a family of solutions
r^{(\kappa)}
indexed by a reflection
\kappa
for a right non-degenerate solution
(X,r)
, including the solution
r
itself and its derived solution. They also show that if
(X,r)
is an involutive left non-degenerate solution, a map
\kappa
is a reflection if and only if (
T
) holds; similarly, if
(X,r)
is right non-degenerate,
\kappa
is a reflection if and only if (
Q
) holds. Thus,
\lambda
-centralizing maps are reflections for involutive left-non degenerate solutions, while maps
\kappa
satisfying
\rho_{\kappa(x)}=\rho_{x}
, for all
x\in X
, that we call
\rho
-invariant
,
are reflections for the involutive right non-degenerate ones. We mention other interesting works on the reflection equation as
[
23
,
8
,
11
,
22
,
24
,
10
]
.

The main aim of this paper is to study reflections for non-degenerate solutions employing the left and the right shelf structures associated with them. We briefly recall that a
left shelf
is a set
X
endowed with a left self-distributive binary operation
\triangleright
, namely, the identity
x\triangleright\left(y\triangleright z\right)=\left(x\triangleright y\right)\triangleright\left(x\triangleright z\right)
is satisfied, for all
x,y,z\in X

x\in X
, the left multiplication
L_{x}:X\to X
defined by
L_{x}\left(y\right)=x\triangleright y
, for all
y\in X
, is a shelf homomorphism. In particular, if
\left(X,r\right)
is a left non-degenerate solution, set
x\triangleright_{r}y:=\lambda_{x}\rho_{\lambda_{y}^{-1}(x)}(y)
, for all
x,y\in X
, one obtain that
\left(X,\triangleright_{r}\right)
is a left shelf that gives rise to a solution
\left(X,r_{\triangleright}\right)
, with
r_{\triangleright}\left(x,y\right)=\left(y,y\triangleright_{r}x\right)
, for all
x,y\in X
, called the
left derived solution of
r
. Analogously, one can define the structure
\left(X,\triangleleft\right)
of
right shelf
, introduce the right multiplications
R_{x}:X\to X
given by
R_{x}(y)=y\triangleleft x
, for all
y\in X
, and the link with right non-degenerate solutions. For our purposes, it will also be useful to introduce classical tools of this theory, such as the
left multiplication group
of a left shelf
(X,\triangleright)
given by
\LMlt(X,\,\triangleright)=\langle L_{x}\,\mid\ x\in X\rangle
. For more details on this topic, see
[
17
,
18
,
25
,
26
]
.

Our study is motivated by a recent description of left non-degenerate solutions in terms of left shelves structures provided in
[
12
, Theorem 2.15]
.
Indeed, any left non-degenerate solution can be written in terms of a family of automorphisms of the left shelf associated with it, i.e., the
Drinfel’d twist
, and of the shelf operation itself.
This naturally suggests splitting the study of reflections into their behaviour with respect to the maps
\lambda_{x}\in\Aut\left(X,\triangleright_{r}\right)
and left multiplications
L_{x}
. Hence, reflections further motivate the investigation of the endomorphism monoid of shelves.

We first focus on reflections for solutions that are only left or right non-degenerate since, in our analysis, they turn out to be different from each other. Following some existing approaches mentioned before, we pay particular attention to
\lambda
-centralizing and
\rho
-invariant reflections and characterize them in the left and right non-degenerate cases in
Theorem

2.1
and in
Theorem

3.1
, respectively. Besides, we reformulate the results achieved in terms of the respective multiplication groups of their associated shelf structure (see
Theorem

2.11
and
Corollary

3.4
). Then, we deal with the non-degenerate bijective case, by combining the observations made separately for the left and right cases, and developing a theory for such solutions. In this context, in Appendix
A
, we gather some numerical data on reflections for bijective non-degenerate solutions associated with skew braces, dividing them by classes of belonging.

The last section is devoted to constructing reflections starting from known ones. More specifically, considering construction techniques for arbitrary solutions, as the
matched product

[
2
]
and the
strong semilattice of solutions

[
3
]
, we define reflections for the new solution obtained, starting from reflections on each solution involved in the construction itself.
Moreover, we focus on the
twisted union
, a method for obtaining involutive non-degenerate solutions provided in
[
16
]
. In particular, we first extend this technique to arbitrary solutions and then we show how to construct a reflection for new solutions determined.

Definition 1.1

[

1.2
Some basics on shelf structures

Let us start this section by recalling some basics on shelves and racks. For further details, we refer to
[
17
,
18
,
25
,
26
]
.

A
left shelf

(X,\triangleright)
is a set
X
equipped with a left self-distributive binary operation
\triangleright
such that

\forall\,x,y,z\in X\quad x\triangleright(y\triangleright z)=(x\triangleright y)\triangleright(x\triangleright z)\,.

(1)

A
shelf homomorphism
between two shelves is defined as any map preserving the shelf operations on each underlying set.
Hereafter, we will denote with
\End(X,\triangleright)
and
\Aut(X,\triangleright)
the endomorphism monoid and the automorphism group of a given shelf
(X,\triangleright)
, respectively.
If we denote with
L_{x}:X\to X,y\mapsto x\triangleright y
the operator of left multiplication by
x
, for all
x\in X
, then equation (
1
) is equivalent to requiring that
L_{x}
is a shelf endomorphism.

A left shelf
(X,\triangleright)
is a
left rack
if all the maps
L_{x}
are bijective. Moreover, a rack
(X,\triangleright)
is a
left quandle
if, in addition,
L_{x}(x)=x
, for all
x\in X
.

In a similar way, it is possible to define a
right shelf

(X,\triangleleft)
, and analogously a
right rack
as a right shelf
(X,\triangleleft)
having all the right multiplications
R_{x}
bijective.

Below, we give some useful examples of left shelves and left racks, some of which are well-known in literature. Clearly, one can derive their right version.

.

X
by setting
i\triangleright j:=2i-j

\left(\text{mod}\,\,n\right)
, for all
i,j\in X
. Then,
(X,\triangleright)
is a left quandle. In particular, the case
n=0
provides the infinite dihedral quandle.

The following results explain the relationship between shelf structures and solutions that will be useful for our purposes.

Proposition 1.17
.

Let
(X,\triangleright)
be a left shelf.
Then, the map
r_{\triangleright}:X\times X\to X\times X
given by

r_{\triangleright}(x,y)=\left(y,y\triangleright x\right)
,
for all
x,y\in X
, is a left non-degenerate solution on
X
, called the
solution associated to the left shelf
(X,\triangleright)
.

Conversely, if
(X,r)
is a left non-degenerate solution, set

x\triangleright_{r}y:=\lambda_{x}\rho_{\lambda_{y}^{-1}(x)}(y),

for all
x,y\in X
, then the structure
(X,\triangleright_{r})
is a left shelf, called the
left shelf associated to the solution
(X,r)
. The solution associated with
\left(X,\triangleright_{r}\right)
is called the
left derived solution
of
(X,r)
.

The correspondence between right non-degenerate solutions and right shelves is analogous and easily obtained by exchanging the roles of the maps
\lambda
and
\rho
.
Specifically, if
(X,r)
is right non-degenerate, then its associated right shelf
(X,\triangleleft_{r})
is defined by setting

\forall\ x,y\in X\qquad x\triangleleft_{r}y:=\rho_{y}\lambda_{\rho^{-1}_{x}(y)}(x).

Proposition 1.18
.

Let
(X,\triangleright)
be a shelf. Then, the solution
\left(X,r_{\triangleright}\right)
is non-degenerate if and only if
(X,\triangleright)
is a rack.

According to
[
12
, Theorem 2.15]
, it is possible to describe any left (or right) non-degenerate solution in terms of its associated shelf. We recall such a result in the direction useful for our purposes.

Theorem 1.19
.

1.

Let
(X,r)
be a left non-degenerate solution and let
(X,\triangleright_{r})
its associated shelf.
Then,
\lambda_{x}\in\Aut\left(X,\triangleright_{r}\right)
and
\rho_{y}\left(x\right)=\lambda^{-1}_{\lambda_{x}\left(y\right)}\left(\lambda_{x}\left(y\right)\triangleright_{r}x\right)
,
for all
x,y\in X
.

2.

Let
(X,r)
be a right non-degenerate solution and let
(X,\triangleleft_{r})
its associated shelf.
Then,
\rho_{x}\in\Aut\left(X,\triangleleft_{r}\right)
and
\lambda_{x}\left(y\right)=\rho^{-1}_{\rho_{y}\left(x\right)}\left(y\triangleleft_{r}\rho_{y}(x)\right)
,
for all
x,y\in X
.

Let us observe that, in particular, any left non-degenerate involutive solution on a set
X
is the solution associated with the trivial rack.

It turns out to be useful to introduce the following group associated with a left rack structure. Similarly, one can define it for a right rack.

Definition 1.20
.

Let
(X,\triangleright)
be a left rack.
The
left multiplication group
of
(X,\triangleright)
is the normal subgroup of
\Aut(X)
defined by

\displaystyle\LMlt(X,\triangleright):=\langle L_{x}\mid x\in X\rangle\,.

This group is an important invariant of a rack and computing it, in general, is a fundamental task in the theory (for example, see
[
18
,
15
]
).

2
Reflections for left non-degenerate solutions

In this section, we focus on reflections for left non-degenerate solutions and, consequently, on left shelf structures. Specifically, we describe
\lambda
-centralizing reflections for this class of solutions, i.e., reflections commuting with all the maps
\lambda_{x}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(X,r)
be a left non-degenerate solution,
(X,\triangleright_{r})
its associated shelf, and
\kappa:X\to X
a
\lambda
-centralizing map.
Then,
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r)
if and only if, for all
x,y\in X
, the following hold:

1.

\kappa L_{x}=\kappa L_{\kappa\left(x\right)}
,

2.

\kappa L_{L_{x}(y)}(x)=L_{\kappa L_{x}(y)}\kappa(x)
.

Proof.

Initially, using
Theorem

1.19
, observe that, by an easy computation, the equation in
1
. is equivalent to (
T
).
Now, notice that

\displaystyle\kappa q\left(x,y\right)=\lambda^{-1}_{t\left(x,y\right)}\kappa L_{t\left(x,y\right)}\lambda_{x}\left(y\right)\quad\text{and}\quad q\left(x,\kappa\left(y\right)\right)=\lambda^{-1}_{t\left(x,\kappa\left(y\right)\right)}L_{t\left(x,\kappa\left(y\right)\right)}\lambda_{x}\kappa\left(y\right),

for all
x,y\in X
.
Thus, if
\kappa
is a reflection for
(X,r)
, we get

\displaystyle\forall\,x,y\in X\quad\kappa q(x,y)=q(x,\kappa(y))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\forall\,x,y\in X\quad\kappa L_{t\left(x,y\right)}\lambda_{x}\left(y\right)=L_{t\left(x,y\right)}\lambda_{x}\kappa\left(y\right)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\forall\,x,y\in X\quad\kappa L_{\kappa L_{\lambda_{x}(y)}(x)}\lambda_{x}\left(y\right)=L_{\kappa L_{\lambda_{x}(y)}(x)}\lambda_{x}\kappa\left(y\right)

\displaystyle\underset{\eqref{eq:1}}{\Leftrightarrow}\forall\,x,y\in X\quad\kappa L_{L_{\lambda_{x}(y)}(x)}\lambda_{x}\left(y\right)=L_{\kappa L_{\lambda_{x}(y)}(x)}\kappa\lambda_{x}\left(y\right),

i.e.,
2
. holds. Conversely, if
1
. and
2
. hold then, by following the previous chain of equivalences we also obtain (
Q
).
∎

We remark that the previous result extends
Proposition

1.9
, since if
(X,r)
is an involutive left non-degenerate solution then
(X,\triangleright_{r})
is the trivial rack.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
(X,r)
be a left non-degenerate solution and let us assume that
\kappa
is a
\lambda
-centralizing map for
(X,r)
. Then,
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r)
if and only if
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r_{\triangleright})
.

Proof.

The claim follows by
Proposition

1.17
and
Theorem

2.1
.
∎

Notice that property
2.
of
Theorem

2.1
might suggest that a
\lambda
-centralizing reflection
\kappa
of a left non-degenerate solution
(X,r)
is an endomorphism of its associated rack
\left(X,\triangleright_{r}\right)
. However, in general, this is not the case, as one can verify in the following example.

Example 2.3
.

If we consider the shelf
\left(X,\triangleright\right)
in
Examples

1.15
-2. on
X=\{1,\,2,\,3\}
with
f:X\to X
given by
f=122
, then the map
\kappa:X\to X
defined by
\kappa=112
is a reflection that is not a shelf endomorphism of
\left(X,\triangleright\right)
.

Therefore, one question that arises is whether left multiplications are reflections for a solution associated with a shelf structure.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(X,\triangleright)
be a left shelf,
(X,r_{\triangleright})
its associated solution, and
a\in X
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

1.

L_{a}\in\mathcal{K}(X,r_{\triangleright})
;

2.

L_{a}L_{x}=L_{a}L_{L_{a}(x)}
, for every
x\in X
;

3.

L_{L_{a}(x)}L_{a}=L_{a}L_{L_{a}(x)}
, for every
x\in X
.

Proof.

It directly follows from
Theorem

2.1
and the self-distributivity property (
1
).
∎

The property
2
. of
Theorem

2.1
further simplifies if we assume that
L_{x}
is bijective, for all
x\in X
, i.e.,
(X,\,\triangleright_{r})
is a rack.
According to
[
12
, Corollary 2.16]
, this is the case of a left non-degenerate solution which is also bijective.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
(X,r)
be a bijective left non-degenerate solution and
\kappa:X\to X
a
\lambda
-centralizing map. Then,
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r)
if and only if
\kappa\in\End\left(X,\triangleright_{r}\right)
such that

\displaystyle\forall\,x\in X\quad\kappa L_{x}=\kappa L_{\kappa\left(x\right)}\,.

(2)

In particular, the following hold:

1.

if
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r)
is involutive, then
\kappa
is an involutive automorphism of
\left(X,\,\triangleright_{r}\right)
.

2.

\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r)
is idempotent, i.e.,
\kappa^{2}=\kappa
, if and only if
\kappa
is an idempotent of
\End\left(X,\,\triangleright_{r}\right)
;

Proof.

The result is an easy consequence of
Theorem

2.1
.
Moreover,
1.
is obvious.
Finally, to prove
2.
, it is sufficient to observe that if we assume that
\kappa:X\to X
is an idempotent endomorphism of
(X,\,\triangleright_{r})
, then the following holds, for all
x\in X
:

\kappa L_{\kappa(x)}=L_{\kappa^{2}(x)}\kappa=L_{\kappa(x)}\kappa=\kappa L_{x}\,,

thus finishing the proof.
∎

Observe that, in general, the converse of
Corollary

2.5
-
1.
is not true (see
Remark

2.15
).

Remark 2.6
.

Reflection maps share an unexpected connection with averaging operators on racks. According to
[
7
]
, if
(X,\,\triangleright)
is a rack then a map
A:X\to X
is a (left)
averaging operator on
(X,\,\triangleright)
if

A\left(x\right)\triangleright A\left(y\right)=A\left(A\left(x\right)\triangleright y\right)
, for all
x,y\in X
. Evidently, if
A\in\End(X,\,\triangleright)
, then
A
is averaging operator on
(X,\,\triangleright)
if and only if
A
satisfies the identity (
2
) in
Corollary

2.5
.

Remark 2.7
.

Let
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
be
\lambda
-centralizing reflections of a bijective left non-degenerate solution
(X,r)
.
If
\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}=\kappa_{2}\kappa_{1}
, then
\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}
is a reflection of
(X,r)
. The converse is not true since in
Example

1.5
we have that
\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}=\kappa_{2}
and
\kappa_{3}\kappa_{2}=\kappa_{4}
.
Moreover, in general, the commutation assumption can not be relaxed since for a given solution there can exist
\lambda
-centralizing reflections
\kappa
and
\omega
whose composition is not again a reflection (see
Examples

1.3
-3.).

In the case of a non-degenerate left derived solution,
Theorem

2.1
allows to characterize all reflections only in terms of the associated rack.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
\left(X,\triangleright\right)
be a left rack and
(X,r_{\triangleright})
its associated solution. Then, a
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r_{\triangleright})
if and only if
\kappa\in\End\left(X,\triangleright_{r}\right)
such that

\forall\,x\in X\quad\kappa L_{x}=\kappa L_{\kappa\left(x\right)}.

The
Corollary

2.8
allows us to easily find examples of reflections of the solutions associated with racks.

Examples 2.9
.

Let
(X,\triangleright)
be a left rack.

1.

Any idempotent endomorphism of
(X,\triangleright)
lies in
\mathcal{K}(X)
.

2.

If
(X,\triangleright)
is a quandle, any constant map with a constant value
c\in X
is a reflection for
\left(X,r_{\triangleright}\right)
.

3.

If
(X,\triangleright)
is the cyclic rack, since one can easily deduce that
\End(X,\triangleright)=\LMlt(X,\triangleright)
, then
\mathcal{K}(X)=\LMlt(X,\triangleright)
.

Another consequence of the previous corollaries is that bijective reflections of a left rack allow us to obtain new solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation, as we explain in the following.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
(X,\triangleright)
be a left rack and let us consider
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}_{\text{bij}}(X,r_{\triangleright})
. Then, the map

r_{\kappa}:X\times X\to X\times X
defined by

\displaystyle r_{\kappa}\left(x,\,y\right)=\left(\kappa\left(y\right),\,\kappa^{-1}\left(y\triangleright x\right)\right),

for all
x,y\in X
, is a bijective and non-degenerate solution on
X
. In addition, it holds that
\omega\in\mathcal{K}(X,r_{\kappa})
if and only if
\kappa\omega=\omega\kappa
.

Proof.

The proof of the fact that
r_{\kappa}
is a bijective and non-degenerate solution is a consequence of
[
12
, Theorem 2.15]
and
Corollary

2.8
.
Moreover, the rest of the claim follows from
Corollary

2.5
.
∎

Notice that the solution
(X,r_{\kappa})
in
Proposition

2.10
is equivalent to
(X,r_{\triangleright})
if and only if
\kappa=\id_{X}
. An easy calculation shows that
\triangleright=\triangleright_{r_{\kappa}}
and therefore
r_{\triangleright}
and
r_{\kappa}
share the same left rack structure.
According to
[
12
, Lemma 2.25]
, it follows that
r_{\triangleright}
and
r_{\kappa}
are Drinfel’d-isomorphic, with the explicit Drinfel’d-isomorphism
\Phi:X\times X\to X\times X
given by

\Phi(x,y)=(x,\kappa(y))
, for all
x,y\in X
.

The
\lambda
-centralizing reflections for bijective left non-degenerate solutions can be directly studied using the
centralizer
of
\LMlt\left(X,\triangleright\right)
, as we will show in the result below.
Hereafter, with
C_{M}(S)
we will denote the centralizer in a monoid
M
of a subset
S
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
(X,r)
be a bijective left non-degenerate solution and
\kappa:X\to X
a
\lambda
-centralizing map.
Then the following hold:

1.

If
\kappa\in C_{\End(X,\triangleright)}\left(\LMlt\left(X,\triangleright\right)\right)
, then
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r)
;

2.

If
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}_{\text{inj}}(X,r)
, then
\kappa\in C_{\End(X,\triangleright)}\left(\LMlt\left(X,\triangleright\right)\right)
;

3.

In particular,
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}_{\text{bij}}(X,r)
if and only if
\kappa\in C_{\Aut(X,\triangleright)}\left(\LMlt\left(X,\triangleright\right)\right)
.

Proof.

The first statement directly follows by
Corollary

2.5
. Now, assume that
\kappa
is an injective reflection.
Again, from property
1.
we can deduce that
L_{x}=L_{\kappa(x)}
, from which it follows that
L_{x}\kappa=L_{\kappa(x)}\kappa=\kappa L_{x}
, for all
x\in X
. Clearly, the last point of the assertion is a consequence of the former two.
∎

Corollary 2.12
.

If
(X,\triangleright)
is a left rack, then

\displaystyle C_{\End(X,\triangleright)}\left(\LMlt\left(X,\triangleright\right)\right)\subseteq\mathcal{K}(X)\,\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{K}_{\text{inj}}(X)\subseteq C_{\End(X,\triangleright)}\left(\LMlt\left(X,\triangleright\right)\right).

In particular,
C_{\Aut(X,\triangleright)}\left(\LMlt\left(X,\triangleright\right)\right)=\mathcal{K}_{\text{bij}}(X)
.

As an easy further consequence, we state the following result.

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
(X,\triangleright)
be a left rack and
a\in X
. Then, the map
L_{a}\in\mathcal{K}(X)

if and only if
L_{a}\in\text{Z}\left(\LMlt\left(X,\triangleright\right)\right)
, the center of
\LMlt(X,\triangleright)
.

Example 2.14
.

Let
X=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and
(X,\triangleright)
be the
dihedral quandle
of order
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
In
[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
the authors prove that
\Aut(X)=\Aff(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
, the group of affine transformations of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.
With an analogous proof one can compute
\End(X)
and show that

\End(X)=\left\{f_{b,a}:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{Z}_{n}\mid f_{b,a}(x)=b+ax,\,x,a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\right\}.

In light of
Corollary

2.8
, a map
\kappa:X\to X
is a reflection for
(X,r_{\triangleright})
if and only if there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
such that
\kappa=f_{b,a}
and it satisfies
\kappa L_{x}=\kappa L_{\kappa(x)}
, for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.
Therefore, a straightforward computation shows that
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r_{\triangleright})
if and only if

2a(a-1)x+2ab=0\text{ (mod $n$)}\,

holds, for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Evidently, this is equivalent to both requiring that

\begin{cases}2ab=0&\text{ (mod $n$)}\,,\\
2a(a-1)=0&\text{ (mod $n$)}\,.\end{cases}

The distinct solutions of such a system will provide all reflections
\kappa
for
(X,r_{\triangleright})
.
For example if
\kappa
is bijective, i.e. if
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{*}
is invertible, then the following cases occur:

-

if
\gcd(n,4)=1
then
(b,a)=(0,1)
;

-

if
\gcd(n,4)=2
then
(b,a)=(0,1),\left(\frac{n}{2},1\right)
;

-

if
\gcd(n,4)=4
then
(b,a)=(0,1),\left(0,\frac{n}{2}+1\right),\left(\frac{n}{2},1\right),\left(\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}+1\right)
.

In particular,
|\mathcal{K}_{\text{bij}}(X,\triangleright)|=\gcd(n,4)
and all bijective reflections are involutive.

An analogous procedure can be carried out to determine that the only reflections for the left derived solution associated to the infinite dihedral quandle are the identity map and the maps of arbitrary constant value.

Remark 2.15
.

Note that the converse of
Corollary

2.5
-
1.
is not true. Indeed, the map

f_{1,-1}:\mathbb{Z}_{3}\to\mathbb{Z}_{3}\,,f_{1,-1}(x)=1-x

is an involutive automorphism of the dihedral quandle on
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
(see
Example

2.14
), but it is not a reflection for the associated left derived solution since, in this case, the unique reflection is the identity map.

Let us conclude this section with some considerations on idempotent left non-degenerate solutions. Observe that the shelf associated with any idempotent solution
(X,r)
is the trivial shelf given by
L_{x}=x
, for every
x\in X
.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
(X,r)
be an idempotent left non-degenerate solution. Then:

1.

a
\lambda
-centralizing map
\kappa:X\to X
is a reflection if and only if
\kappa^{2}=\kappa
;

2.

\kappa:X\to X
is a
\rho
-invariant reflection if and only if
\kappa=\id_{X}
.

Proof.

Let us initially observe that the first statement is a direct consequence of
Theorem

2.1
.
For the second one, note that (
Q
) is satisfied if and only if
\kappa\rho_{y}=\rho_{y}
, for every
y\in X
.
Consequently, (
T
) holds if and only if
\lambda_{x}\kappa=\lambda_{x}
, for every
x\in X
, and, since
r
is left non-degenerate, we get
\kappa=\id_{X}
.
∎

3
Reflections for right-non degenerate solutions

In this section, we investigate reflections for right non-degenerate solutions by shifting the attention to the class of
\rho
-invariant reflections, that is,
\rho_{x}=\rho_{\kappa(x)}
holds, for all
x\in X
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,r)
be a right non-degenerate solution,
(X,\triangleleft_{r})
its associated shelf, and
\kappa:X\to X
a
\rho
-invariant map.
Then,
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r)
if and only if
\kappa R_{x}=R_{x}\kappa
, for all
x\in X
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in X
. Using
Theorem

1.19
, we get
\kappa q(x,y)=\kappa\rho_{\rho_{y}(x)}\lambda_{x}(y)=\kappa R_{\rho_{y}(x)}(y)
and, on the other hand,

\displaystyle q(x,\kappa(y))=\rho_{\kappa\rho_{\kappa(y)}(x)}\lambda_{x}\kappa(y)=R_{\rho_{\kappa(y)}(x)}\kappa(y)=R_{\rho_{y}(x)}\kappa(y).

Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle t(x,y)=\rho^{-1}_{\kappa\rho_{\kappa\rho_{y}(x)}\lambda_{x}(y)}R_{\kappa\rho_{y}(x)\lambda_{x}(y)}\kappa\rho_{y}(x)=\rho^{-1}_{\kappa q(x,y)}R_{\kappa q(x,y)}\kappa\rho_{y}(x)

and, similarly,
t(x,\kappa(y))=\rho^{-1}_{q(x,\kappa(y))}R_{q(x,\kappa(y))}\kappa\rho_{y}(x)
. Thus, (
T
) depends on equation (
Q
).
Therefore, to get the claim it is enough to observe that (
Q
) is equivalent to requiring that
\kappa
commutes with every right multiplication
R_{x}
, for all
x\in X
.
∎

Below, we give a list of consequences of
Theorem

3.1
. First, let us remark that the previous result extends
Proposition

1.10
to the class of arbitrary right non-degenerate solutions which are not necessarily involutive.
This follows from the fact that if
(X,r)
is a right non-degenerate involutive solution then
(X,\triangleleft_{r})
is trivial.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\left(X,r\right)
be a right non-degenerate solution. If
\kappa,\omega:X\to X
are
\rho
-invariant reflections for
\left(X,r\right)
, then
\kappa\omega\in\mathcal{K}\left(X,r\right)
and
\kappa\omega
is
\rho
-invariant.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
(X,r)
be a right non-degenerate solution and let us assume that
\kappa
is a
\rho
-invariant map. Then,
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r)
if and only if
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}\left(X,r_{\triangleleft}\right)
.

Proof.

The claim follows by
Proposition

1.17
and
Theorem

3.1
.
∎

As in the case of left non-degenerate solutions, we can formulate the statement in
Theorem

3.1

by means of the right multiplication group of the right rack structure.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
(X,r)
be a bijective right non-degenerate solution,
(X,\triangleleft_{r})
its associated right rack, and
\kappa:X\to X
a
\rho
-invariant map.
Then, the following hold:

1.

\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r)\iff\kappa\in C_{\Map_{X}}\left(\RMlt\left(X,\triangleleft_{r}\right)\right)
.

2.

\kappa\in\mathcal{K}_{\text{bij}}(X,r)\iff\kappa\in C_{\Sym_{X}}\left(\RMlt\left(X,\triangleleft_{r}\right)\right)
.

Example 3.5
.

Let
X=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and consider
(X,\triangleleft)
the right dihedral quandle of order
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The
Corollary

3.4
implies that a map
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}\left(X,r_{\triangleleft}\right)
if and only if the following holds for all
x,y\in X
:

\kappa(x)+\kappa(2y-x)=2y\,.

(3)

Such a map
\kappa
has to satisfy
\kappa(2y)=2y-\kappa(0)
and
\kappa(2y-1)=2y-\kappa(1)
, for all
y\in X
, therefore it is parametrized by its values in
0
and
1
.
To determine their values, we notice that
2\kappa(0)=0
and
2(\kappa(1)-1)=0
, as a consequence of (
3
).
Therefore, all reflections for
(X,r_{\triangleleft})
can be classified as follows:

-

if
n
is odd then
\kappa=\id_{X}
;

-

if
n
is even then
\kappa(0)\in\{0,\frac{n}{2}\}
,
\kappa(1)\in\{1,\frac{n}{2}+1\}
and
\kappa
is determined as above.

Analogously, one can prove that the right derived solution associated with a right infinite dihedral quandle admits the identity map as its unique reflection.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(X,\triangleleft)
be a right shelf,
(X,r_{\triangleleft})
its associated solution, and
a\in X
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

1.

R_{a}
is a reflection for
(X,r_{\triangleleft})
;

2.

R_{a}R_{x}=R_{x}R_{a}
, for all
x\in X
;

3.

R_{R_{a}(x)}R_{a}=R_{x}R_{a}
, for all
x\in X
.

Proof.

The equivalence of
1.
and
2.
directly follows from
Theorem

3.1
, while the equivalence of
2.
and
3.
easily stems from the definition of right shelf.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

As a direct consequence of
Proposition

3.6
, if
(X,\triangleleft)
is a right rack, then
Z(\RMlt(X,\triangleleft))\subseteq\mathcal{K}(X)
. Moreover, if

is a reflection then

.
In particular, if the right multiplication group is abelian - as in the case of a cyclic rack - then every right multiplication provides a reflection for the associated solution.

Let us conclude with an observation on idempotent right non-degenerate solutions.

Remark 3.8
.

Note that if
(X,r)
is an idempotent right non-degenerate solution, one can check that the unique
\rho
-invariant reflection is the identity
\id_{X}
. On the other hand, the unique bijective
\lambda
-centralizing reflection for
(X,r)
is the identity
\id_{X}
.

4
Reflections for bijective non-degenerate solution

This section is devoted to a global analysis of reflections for bijective non-degenerate solutions in light of results obtained in the previous two sections.

Let us start with some considerations on involutive non-degenerate solutions
(X,r)
. One can easily show that any
\rho
-invariant reflection can be described in terms of the retract solution of
(X,r)
. Recall that the equivalence relation of
retraction
on
X
is defined by setting
x\sim y\iff\lambda_{x}=\lambda_{y}
, for all
x,y\in X
.
Thus, there is a natural induced solution
\Ret(X,r)
on the quotient
\tilde{X}:=X/\sim
, called the
retract
of
(X,r)
, which itself is involutive and non-degenerate. In the sequel,
[x]_{\sim}
denotes the equivalence class of any
x\in X
.
For more information, we refer the reader to the standard literature (see
[
16
, Section 3]
).

Remark 4.1
.

The retraction relation for an involutive non-degenerate solution
(X,r)
can be equivalently defined by requiring that
x\sim y\iff\rho_{x}=\rho_{y}
, for all
x,y\in X
. This follows directly by the identity
\rho_{x}=T\lambda^{-1}_{x}T^{-1}
, for all
x\in X
, where
T:X\to X
is the bijective map defined by
T(x)=\rho_{x}^{-1}(x)
, for all
x\in X
(cf.
[
16
, Proposition 2.2]
).

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(X,r)
be an involutive non-degenerate solution. Then, the following hold:

1.

Any map
\kappa:X\to X
such that
\kappa(x)\in[x]_{\sim}
, for every
x\in X
, is a
\rho
-invariant reflection for
(X,r)
. In particular, all such reflections can be constructed in this manner.

2.

If
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r)
is
\rho
-invariant, then it induces a reflection
\tilde{\kappa}:\tilde{X}\to\tilde{X}
of the retraction
\Ret(X,r)
such that
\tilde{\kappa}([x]_{\sim})=[\kappa(x)]_{\sim}
, for all
x\in X
.

Proof.

The two statements are a direct consequence of
Proposition

1.10
. We added the proof in the file.
∎

Now, we aim to combine the results obtained in the previous sections and analyze what happens in the special case of bijective non-degenerate solutions. To do so, in
[
26
, cf. Proposition 3.9]
, it is shown the existing link between left and right structure racks (see also
[
26
, Remark 3.10]
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(X,r)
be a bijective non-degenerate solution. Then, the left and the right structure racks of
(X,r)
are anti-isomorphic via the bijection
T:X\to X
defined by
T(x)=\rho^{-1}_{x}(x)
, for all
x\in X
. Concretely, the relation

\displaystyle T\left(x\triangleright_{r}y\right)=T(y)\triangleleft_{r}T(x)

holds, for all
x,y\in X
.
Equivalently,
L_{x}=T^{-1}R_{T(x)}T
, where
T^{-1}(x)=\lambda_{x}^{-1}\left(x\triangleright_{r}x\right)
, for all
x\in X
.

Remark 4.4
.

To be thorough, let us observe that if
\left(X,r\right)
is a bijective non-degenerate square-free solution, i.e.,
r\left(x,x\right)=\left(x,x\right)
, for all
x\in X
, then
\triangleright_{r}=\triangleleft_{r}^{op}
.
The converse is not necessarily true (consider for example a non-trivial Lyubashenko’s solution).

However, in general, it is easy to check that two solutions of derived type
\left(X,r_{\triangleright}\right)
and
\left(Y,r_{\triangleleft}\right)
are isomorphic if and only if
L_{a}=\id_{X}
, for every
a\in X
, and
R_{u}=\id_{Y}
, for every
u\in Y
. In particular, this happens only for involutive solutions.

As an easy consequence of
Lemma

4.3
, since any anti-homomorphism between
\left(X,\triangleright_{r}\right)
and
\left(X,\triangleleft_{r}\right)
satisfies
L_{x}=F^{-1}R_{F\left(x\right)}F
, for every
x\in X
, it is a routine computation to check the following.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(X,r)
be a bijective non-degenerate solution,
\kappa:X\to X
a map,
F:X\to X
an anti-homomorphism from
\left(X,\triangleright_{r}\right)
in
\left(X,\triangleleft_{r}\right)
, and

\omega,\bar{\omega}:X\to X
the maps defined by
\omega:=F\kappa F^{-1}
and
\bar{\omega}:=F^{-1}\kappa F
.
Then, the following hold:

1.

if
\kappa
is
\lambda
-centralizing, then,

\displaystyle\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r)\Leftrightarrow\ \begin{cases}\ \forall\,x\in X\quad\kappa L_{x}=\kappa L_{\kappa(x)}&\\
\ \ \kappa\in\End\left(X,\triangleright_{r}\right)\end{cases}\Leftrightarrow\ \begin{cases}\ \forall\,x\in X\quad\omega R_{x}=\omega R_{\omega(x)}&\\
\ \ \omega\in\End\left(X,\triangleleft_{r}\right)\end{cases}

2.

if
\kappa
is
\rho
-invariant, then

\displaystyle\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r)\ \Leftrightarrow\ \forall\,x\in X\quad\kappa R_{x}=R_{x}\kappa\ \Leftrightarrow\ \forall\,x\in X\quad\bar{\omega}L_{x}=L_{x}\bar{\omega}.

If we specialize the
Theorem

4.5
to a solution
(X,r)
of derived type, it does not yield a novel result, as we explain below. One can argue analogously if
(X,r)
is a solution of right derived type.

Remark 4.6
.

If
(X,r)
is a solution whose left derived solution is associated with a left rack
(X,\triangleright)
, then its right derived solution
(X,r_{\triangleleft})
has the form
r_{\triangleleft}(x,y)=(y\triangleleft_{r}x,x)=(x\triangleright y,x)
, for all

, whence
\triangleleft_{r}=\triangleright^{\text{op}}
.
Therefore, the map

from

4.3
actually is an automorphism of
(X,\triangleright)
.
If we notice that
\Aut(X,r)=\Aut(X,\triangleright)
then
Theorem

4.5
simply recovers the observation made in
Remarks

1.2
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
(X,r)
be a bijective non-degenerate solution and let
\kappa:X\to X
be a
\rho
-centralizing and
\rho
-invariant map. Then, the following holds

\displaystyle\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r)\iff\kappa\text{ is $\lambda$-centralizing}\,.

Proof.

First, assume that
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}(X,r)
.
Then, since by assumption
\kappa
is
\rho
-invariant,
Theorem

4.5
-2 ensures that
\kappa
commutes with all
R_{x}
.
As a consequence, for all
x,y\in X
the following holds:

\displaystyle\kappa\lambda_{x}(y)

\displaystyle=\kappa\rho^{-1}_{\rho_{y}(x)}R_{\rho_{y}(x)}(y)=\rho^{-1}_{\rho_{y}(x)}R_{\rho_{y}(x)}\kappa(y)=\lambda_{x}\kappa(y)\,,

proving the first part.
Conversely, the assertion easily follows from
Theorem

4.5
-2. since the hypothesis yields that
\kappa

\kappa R_{x}=R_{x}\kappa
, for every

.
∎

In a general setting and inspired by

Theorem 4.8

Let

be a solution,

Initially, observe that

\displaystyle r\left(\id_{X}\times\omega\right)r\left(\id_{X}\times\omega\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\varphi\times\varphi\right)r\left(\id_{X}\times\kappa\right)r\left(\id_{X}\times\kappa\right)\left(\psi\times\psi\right)

\displaystyle\left(\id_{X}\times\omega\right)r\left(\id_{X}\times\omega\right)r

\displaystyle=\left(\varphi\times\varphi\right)\left(\id_{X}\times\kappa\right)r\left(\id_{X}\times\kappa\right)r\left(\psi\times\psi\right).

Therefore, since

is a reflection, then using (

5.1
The reflection of the matched product of two solutions

Let us begin by recalling the essential notions contained in
[

If
(S,r_{S})
and

5.3
The reflection of the twisted unions

In
[
16
, Section 3.4]
, Etingof, Schedler, and Soloviev provided a technique to construct involutive non-degenerate solutions in the finite case, called the
twisted union
. A computer program generated all solutions, up to isomorphism, on sets of cardinality
|X|\leq 8
, and showed that almost all are twisted union of involutive non-degenerate solutions on smaller sets. We extend such a construction to arbitrary solutions.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
(X,u)
and
(Y,v)
be two solutions. Set
Z=X\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\cup$\cr$\displaystyle\cdot$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\cup$\cr$\textstyle\cdot$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\cup$\cr$\scriptstyle\cdot$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\cup$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$\crcr}}}}Y
and consider the map
r:Z\times Z\to Z\times Z
defined by

r\left(a,\,b\right)=\begin{cases}\ u\left(a,\,b\right)&\ \text{if}\quad a,\,b\in X\\
\ v\left(a,\,b\right)&\ \text{if}\quad a,\,b\in Y\\
\ \left(g\left(b\right),\,f\left(a\right)\right)&\ \text{if}\quad a\in X,b\in Y\\
\ \left(\alpha\left(b\right),\,\beta\left(a\right)\right)&\ \text{if}\quad a\in Y,b\in X\end{cases}

for all
a,b\in Z
, with
f,\alpha:X\to X
and
g,\beta:Y\to Y
maps. Then,
(Z,\,r)
is a solution if and only if for all
x\in X
and
y\in Y
the following hold:

\displaystyle\alpha f=f\alpha

\displaystyle g\beta=\beta g

\displaystyle\left(f\times f\right)u=u\left(f\times f\right)

\displaystyle\left(g\times g\right)v=v\left(g\times g\right)

\displaystyle\left(\alpha\times\alpha\right)u=u\left(\alpha\times\alpha\right)

\displaystyle\left(\beta\times\beta\right)v=v\left(\beta\times\beta\right)

\displaystyle\alpha\lambda_{f\left(x\right)}=\lambda_{x}\alpha

\displaystyle\beta\rho_{g\left(y\right)}=\rho_{y}\beta

\displaystyle f\rho_{\alpha(x)}=\rho_{x}f

\displaystyle g\lambda_{\beta\left(y\right)}=\lambda_{y}g

The pair
(Z,r)
is called the
twisted union of
(X,u)
and
(Y,v)
via
f,\alpha
and
g,\beta
.

Proof.

To get the claim it is enough to verify that

(
Y1
)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\alpha\lambda_{x}=\lambda_{\alpha\left(x\right)}\alpha,\ \ g\lambda_{y}=\lambda_{g\left(y\right)}g,\ \ \alpha\lambda_{f\left(x\right)}=\lambda_{x}\alpha,\ \text{and }\ g\lambda_{\beta\left(y\right)}=\lambda_{y}g,

(
Y2
)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow f\alpha=\alpha f,\ \ g\beta=\beta g,\ \ f\lambda_{x}=\lambda_{f\left(x\right)}f,\ \ \beta\lambda_{y}=\lambda_{\beta\left(y\right)}\beta,\

\displaystyle\,\quad\alpha\rho_{x}=\rho_{\alpha\left(x\right)}\alpha,\ \ \text{and }\ g\rho_{y}=\rho_{g\left(y\right)}g,

(
Y3
)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow f\rho_{x}=\rho_{f\left(x\right)}f,\ \ \beta\rho_{y}=\rho_{\beta\left(y\right)}\beta,\ \ f\rho_{\alpha\left(x\right)}=\rho_{x}f,\ \text{and }\ \beta\rho_{g\left(y\right)}=\rho_{y}\beta,

for all
x\in X
and
y\in Y
, respectively.
∎

Reflections for solutions associated with skew braces

We gather numerical data computing all reflections for some bijective non-degenerate solutions.
To this aim, we focus on the solutions associated with the well-studied algebraic structure of
skew braces
. For more details on these structures, we refer the reader to
[
20
]
. Reflections maps coming from skew braces merit further investigation which will be undertaken in future works.
In particular, we implement the
GAP
software and devise an elementary algorithmic approach to compute all reflections for the solutions associated to the first skew braces in the database furnished by the
YangBaxter
package
[
30
]
.

The algorithm proceeds as explained below:

Input:

A positive integer
n
, a list of positive integers
T
;

1.

For each
m\in T
, consider the skew brace
B
of order
n
and type
m
in the database, compute the associated solution and determine all its reflections with
Lemma

1.6
;

2.

Determine which reflections are
\lambda
-centralizing or
\rho
-invariant;

Output:

A list of the reflections for each type labelled as
\lambda
-centralizing or
\rho
-invariant.

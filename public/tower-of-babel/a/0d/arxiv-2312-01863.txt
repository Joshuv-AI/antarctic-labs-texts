Cauchy problem for singular-degenerate porous medium type equations: well-posedness and Sobolev regularity
Nick Lindemulder, Stefanie Sonner

Abstract
. Motivated by models for biofilm growth, we consider Cauchy problems for quasilinear reaction diffusion equations where the diffusion coefficient has a porous medium type degeneracy as well as a singularity. We prove results on the well-posedness and Sobolev regularity of solutions. The proofs are based on m-accretive operator theory, kinetic formulations and Fourier analytic techniques.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 35K59, 35K65, 35K67; Secondary: 35B65, 46E35, 47H05

1.
Introduction and main results

We prove the well-posedness and investigate Sobolev regularity for the following Cauchy problem for singular-degenerate porous medium type equations,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=\Delta\phi(u)+f(u)

\displaystyle\text{in}\ (0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

\displaystyle u|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=u_{0}

\displaystyle\text{on}\ {\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where the solution
u
takes values in
[0,1)
and
\phi:[0,1)\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a strictly increasing function with
\phi(0)=0
that has a degeneracy
\phi^{\prime}(0)=0
and singularity
\phi(1)=\infty
.
Moreover, the function
f
is Lipschitz continuous,
f(0)=0
and the initial data satisfies
u_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};[0,1))
.

The problem is motivated by models for biofilm growth where a degenerate equation of the form (
1.1
) is coupled to an additional semilinear PDE or ODE for the nutrient concentration
[
EJDW17
,
EPVL01
]
. We refer to these two different settings as PDE-PDE or ODE-PDE model, respectively, below.
In such models, the solution
u
describes the time evolution of the biomass fraction, i.e. the normalized biomass density,
and the actual biofilm is represented by the region
\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:u(t,x)>0\}.

The biomass diffusion coefficient is of the form

(1.2)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(u)=\frac{u^{b}}{(1-u)^{a}},\qquad a\geq 1,b>0,

and the function
f
models biomass production.
The degeneracy
u^{b}
in (
1.2
) is well-known from the porous medium equation. It leads to the formation of free boundaries and enforces a finite speed of propagation, i.e. solutions emanating from initial data with compact support remain compactly supported for all times. The additional singularity
(1-u)^{-a}
in (
1.2
) ensures that solutions remain bounded by 1 despite the growth term
f
in (
1.1
).

The well-posedness for the PDE-PDE biofilm growth model
[
EPVL01
]
in bounded domains with Dirichlet boundary conditions was established in
[
EZE09
]
. The solution theory was extended in
[
HMS22
,
MS23
]
for more general PDE-PDE and ODE-PDE systems involving a degenerate equation of the form (
1.1
) and allowing for mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary conditions which are relevant in applications. The local Hölder regularity of solutions for such PDE-PDE systems was shown in
[
HM22a
]
. For coupled ODE-PDE systems the existence of traveling wave solutions in one spatial dimension was proven in
[
MHS+23
]
.
The Cauchy problem for PDE-PDE systems with the specific diffusion coefficient
\phi^{\prime}(u)=\frac{u^{b}}{(1-u)^{a}}
was considered in
[
HM22b
]
where the existence and uniqueness of weak energy solutions was shown by approximating the equation in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by problems on bounded domains.

The aim of our paper is to develop a solution theory for the more general Cauchy problem (
1.1
) based on mild solutions and the theory of
m
-accretive operators, to show well-posedness and to derive space-time regularity results in the scale of Sobolev spaces. For the latter we use kinetic formulations and velocity averaging inspired by the approach applied to the porous medium equation in
[
GST20
,
TT07
]
. While in models for biofilm growth solutions take non-negative values we formulate our results in a broader framework and allow for sign changing solutions. In the sequel, we assume that the solution
u
takes values in
(-1,1)
, that
\phi:(-1,1)\to{\mathbb{R}}
is strictly increasing and satisfies
\phi(0)=\phi^{\prime}(0)=0
,
\phi(-1)=-\infty
and
\phi(1)=\infty.
The first main result addresses the well-posedness for problem (
1.1
). In
[
GST20
]
and related earlier works on the Sobolev regularity of solutions
[
Ges21
,
CP03
,
TT07
]
the concept of entropy solutions was used while we base our analysis on the notion of mild solutions and the theory of
m
-accretive operators. To avoid technical assumptions the second statement of Theorem
1.1

is formulated for the specific biofilm diffusion coefficient (
1.2
) but we prove it in greater generality in Section
3
and Section
4.6
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\phi\colon(-1,1)\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a maximal monotone function with
\phi(0)=0
and let
f\colon[-1,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a Lipschitz function with
f(0)=0
. Then for every
u_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};[-1,1])
there exists a unique mild solution
u\in C([0,T];L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};[-1,1]))
of the initial value problem

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}u&=\Delta\phi(u)+f(u)\qquad\text{in}\>\>(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\qquad\text{on}\>\>{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.\end{split}

If
d\geq 3
and
\phi^{\prime}(u)=\frac{|u|^{b}}{(1-|u|)^{a}},a\geq 1,b>0,
then
u
is a distributional solution of (
1.3
) and

u(t,x)\in(-1,1)
for all
t\in(0,T)
and almost every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Moreover, if
u_{0}\in[0,1)
then
u(t,x)\in[0,1)
for all
t\in(0,T)
and almost every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Our second main result provides space time regularity in the scale of Sobolev spaces.
In addition to the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
we need to assume that the degeneracy of
\phi
in
0
is of porous medium type. Again, to avoid technicalities we formulate Theorem
1.2
for the specific case (
1.2
) but prove the result under more general assumptions in Section
5
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
hold, let
d\geq 3
and

\phi^{\prime}(u)=\frac{|u|^{b}}{(1-|u|)^{a}},a\geq 1,b\geq 1
.
Let
u\in C(0,T];L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};[-1,1]))
be the unique mild solution of (
1.3
). For
p\in[2,b+1]
we define

\kappa_{t}:=\frac{b+1-p}{pb},\quad\kappa_{x}:=\frac{2(p-1)}{pb}.

Then, for all
\sigma_{t}\in[0,\kappa_{t})\cup\{0\}
and
\sigma_{x}\in[0,\kappa_{x})
, we have

u\in W^{\sigma_{t},p}(0,T;W^{\sigma_{x},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})).

To the authors’ knowledge, earlier well-posedness and regularity results for Cauchy problems for generalized porous medium equations do not cover Problem (
1.3
) where
\phi^{\prime}
has an additional singularity and the equation includes a growth term
f
.
While Theorem
1.1
is based on mild solutions, the proof of Theorem
1.2
requires kinetic formulations in order to employ velocity averaging techniques as done for porous medium type equations in
[
GST20
,
Ges21
,
GS23
]
.
We refer to
[
Vaz07
, Chapter 10]
for the history and an overview of the literature on the semigroup approach and mild solutions to generalized porous medium equations.
A detailed overview of Sobolev regularity results for nonlinear evolution equations based on velocity-averaging techniques
is given in
[
Ges21
]
.
Regularity results in the scale of Sobolev spaces as stated in Theorem
1.2

were shown for non-local porous medium type operators in
[
GS23
]

which in turn are generalizations of earlier works on the porous medium equation
[
GST20
,
Ges21
]
.

The outline of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we introduce notation and function spaces. We also recall existence results for classical solutions of non-degenerate quasilinear problems as well as facts from the theory of
m
-accretive operators. In Section
3
we prove existence and uniqueness for problem (
1.3
) and show that solutions take value in the open interval
(-1,1)
. In Section
4
we establish stability results and show that the concepts of mild and classical solutions coincide for smooth non-degenerate approximations.
We also use the approximations to prove comparison results and show that (
1.3
) preserves the non-negativity of solutions.
Finally, Section
5
is devoted to kinetic formulations and the proof of the Sobolev regularity of solutions.

There are several ways to define
homogeneous Sobolev spaces
. We only consider the spaces
\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

as a consequence of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev theorem on fractional integration (see e.g.

As this embedding is dense, defining

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}_{0}=\widehat{\varphi},\qquad\widehat{\varphi}_{1}(\xi)=\widehat{\varphi}(\xi/2)-\widehat{\varphi}(\xi),\qquad\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(\xi)=\widehat{\varphi}_{1}(2^{-k+1}\xi),\qquad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

satisfies

0\leq\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)\leq 1,\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\qquad\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)=1\ \text{ if }\ |\xi|\leq 1,\qquad\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)=0\ \text{ if }\ |\xi|\geq\frac{3}{2}.

2.2.
Classical solutions of quasilinear parabolic problems

We consider the following Cauchy problem

(2.4)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v=\Delta\psi(v)+g&\text{in}\>\>(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\
v(0)=v_{0}&\text{on}\>\>{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.\end{cases}

Under suitable assumptions on
\psi
,
g
and
v_{0}

the existence and uniqueness of classical solutions follows from
[
LSU68
]

(Theorem 8.1, Chapter V).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\psi\in C^{3}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime\prime}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\psi^{\prime}\geq c>0
and let
\beta\in(0,1)
.
Furthermore, we assume that
g\in BC^{\frac{\beta}{2},\beta}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
v_{0}\in BC^{2+\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Then, there exists a unique classical solution
v
of (
2.4
) and
v\in BC^{1+\frac{\beta}{2},2+\beta}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proof.

Using the notation in
[
LSU68
]
,

\mathcal{L}u=u_{t}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}a_{i}(\cdot,u,\nabla u)+a(\cdot,u,\nabla u),

we have
a_{i}(\cdot,u,\nabla u)=\psi^{\prime}(u)\partial_{x_{i}}u
and
a(\cdot,u,\nabla u)=-g(\cdot).
Moreover, we observe that
a_{ij}(\cdot,u,\nabla u)=\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)\delta_{ij}
which implies that

A(\cdot,u,p)=a(\cdot,u,p)-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{u}a_{i}(\cdot,u,p)p_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}a_{i}(\cdot,u,p)=-g(\cdot)-\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)|p|^{2}.

We verify the assumptions of Theorem 8.1, Chapter 5, in
[
LSU68
]
. Hypothesis (a) holds as the initial data
v_{0}\in C^{2+\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is bounded. Hypothesis (b) holds since
a_{ii}(\cdot,u,\nabla u)=\psi^{\prime}(u)>0
and Young’s inequality implies that

A(\cdot,u,0)u=-g(\cdot)u\geq-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}-c_{1},

for some constant
c_{1}\geq 0
. To verify (c) we observe that the functions
a
and
a_{i}
are continuous and
a_{i}
is continuously differentiable with respect to
x,u
and
p
, and

0<c\leq\psi^{\prime}(u)\leq c_{2}<\infty,\qquad c_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Moreover, using Young’s inequality and that
\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime\prime}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and
g
is bounded we observe that

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(|a_{i}|+\left|\partial_{u}a_{i}\right|\right)(1+|p|)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{x_{j}}a_{i}|+|a|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(|\psi^{\prime}(u)p_{i}|+\left|\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)p_{i}\right|\right)(1+|p|)+|g|\leq c_{3}(1+|p|)^{2},

for some
c_{3}>0
. The functions
a,a_{i},\partial_{p_{j}}a_{i}
and
\partial_{u}a_{i}
are Hölder continuous with respect to
t,x,u
and
p
with exponents
\frac{\beta}{2},\beta,\beta
and
\beta
. Moreover, all constants in the estimates are uniform.
Hence, Theorem 8.1, Chapter V in
[
LSU68
]
implies that there exists a solution that is bounded and satisfies
u\in C^{1+\frac{\beta}{2},2+\beta}([0,T]\times R^{d}).

The uniqueness follows as well since the functions
a_{ij}
and
A
are differentiable with respect to
u
and
p
, and for bounded values of
u
and
p
, we have

\displaystyle\max_{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times[0,T],|(u,p)|\leq R}

\displaystyle\left\{\partial_{u}a_{ij}(t,x,u,p),\partial_{p}a_{ij}(t,x,u,p),\partial_{p}A(t,x,u,p)\right\}\leq C_{R},

\displaystyle\min_{(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times[0,T],|(u,p)|\leq R}

\displaystyle\left\{\partial_{u}A(t,x,u,p)\right\}\geq-C_{R},

for some constant
C_{R}\geq 0
only depending on
R>0
.
∎

2.3.
Abstract nonlinear Cauchy problems governed by m-accretive operators

Concerning
maximal monotone
and
m
-accretive operators

we follow the terminology from

[
Bar10
, Chapters 2 and 3]
(also see
[
Vaz07
, Chapter 10]
), with the difference that we only consider single-valued operators. This is not necessary, but simplifies the presentation and is sufficient for our purposes.

2.3.1.
Maximal monotone functions

We shortly introduce maximal monotone functions and graphs, for further details we refer to
[
Bar10
]
.

Let
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be an open interval and let
\phi:I\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a monotonically increasing function.
A function
\phi
is
maximal monotone
if it satisfies:

•

if
\inf(I)>-\infty
, then
\inf_{I}\phi=-\infty
;

•

if
\sup(I)<\infty
, then
\sup_{I}\phi=\infty
.

A
monotone graph
in
{\mathbb{R}}
is a set
\beta\subset{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}
such that, for all
(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})\in\beta
, it holds true that
(x_{1}-x_{2})(y_{1}-y_{2})\geq 0
.
A monotone graph
\beta
in
{\mathbb{R}}
is called
maximal monotone
if it is not properly contained in any other monotone graph in
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be an open interval,
\phi:I\to{\mathbb{R}}
and consider the graph
\operatorname{graph}(\phi)=\{(r,\phi(r)):r\in I\}
. Then note that the following holds:

•

\phi
is monotonically increasing if and only if
\operatorname{graph}(\phi)
is a monotone graph in
{\mathbb{R}}
.

•

\phi
is a maximal monotone function if and only if
\operatorname{graph}(\phi)
is a maximal monotone graph in
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
I=(-1,1)
,
a\geq 1,b>0
and consider the biofilm diffusion coefficient (
1.2
),

D(z)=\frac{|z|^{b}}{(1-|z|)^{a}},\qquad z\in I,

and let

\phi(\rho)=\int_{0}^{\rho}D(z)\mathrm{d}z,\qquad\rho\in I.

Then
\phi:I\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a maximal monotone function.

Proof.

Since
D>0
on
I\setminus\{0\}
, it follows that
\phi
is strictly monotonically increasing.
Furthermore, as
a\geq 1
we have
-\lim_{\rho\to-1}\phi(\rho)=\lim_{\rho\to 1}\phi=\infty
.
Therefore,
\phi:I\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a maximal monotone function.
∎

2.3.2.
m
-accretive operators

The following theorem corresponds to
[
Bar10
, Theorem 3.7]
. It defines the operator
A_{\phi}
that will allow us to consider (

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be an open interval with
0\in I
and let
\phi:I\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a maximal monotone function with
\phi(0)=0
. Then the operator
A_{\phi}
on
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{D}(A_{\phi})

\displaystyle=\{y\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\phi(y)\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\Delta\phi(y)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\},

\displaystyle A_{\phi}y

\displaystyle=-\Delta\phi(y),\ y\in\operatorname{D}(A_{\phi}),

is m-accretive.

The closure of the domain of
A_{\phi}
has the following explicit characterization, which is taken from
[
BC81
, Proposition 6]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let the notations and assumptions be as in Proposition
2.3
. Then

\overline{\operatorname{D}(A_{\phi})}=L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\overline{I}).

2.3.3.
The Cauchy problem

Let
A
be an
m
-accretive operator on a Banach space
X
and consider the associated Cauchy problem

(2.5)

\begin{cases}y^{\prime}(t)+Ay(t)=f(t),&t\in[0,T],\\
y(0)=y_{0},\end{cases}

for a given
T\in(0,\infty)
, where
y_{0}\in X
and
f\in L^{1}(0,T;X)
.

We recall the notion of mild solutions for (
2.5
)
(see
[
Bar10
, Definition 4.3]
, cf.
[
Vaz07
, Definition 10.6]
).

Definition 2.5
.

A
mild solution
of the Cauchy problem (
2.5
) is a function
y\in C([0,T];X)
with the property that for each
\varepsilon>0
there is an
\varepsilon
-approximate solution
z
of
z^{\prime}+Az=f
on
[0,T]
obtained via implicit time discretizations (in the sense of
[
Bar10
, Definition 4.2]
and
[
Vaz07
, Section 10.2]
) such that
\lVert y(t)-z(t)\rVert\leq\varepsilon
for all
t\in[0,T]
and
y(0)=y_{0}
.

Remark 2.6
.

For our problem we cannot consider the concept of
strong solutions

[
Bar10
, Definition 4.1]
.
Strong solutions are mild solutions but the opposite is generally not true as simple examples in
[
Bar10
, pg. 140,141]
show.
It is a natural question under what conditions mild solutions are strong solutions.
In fact, these solution concepts coincide under additional assumptions on the Banach space plus extra regularity assumptions on
f
and
y_{0}
, see
[
Bar10
, Theorem 4.5]
.
Unfortunately, the extra assumption for the Banach space is reflexivity which is not satisfied by
L^{1}
that is of interest in our case.
Properties of mild solutions can also be shown using the theory of subdifferentials, see Theorem
3.4
.

As
\varepsilon
-approximate solutions take their values in the domain of the operator
A
, it directly follows from the above definition that mild solutions take their values in the closure of the domain of
A
:

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
y\in C([0,T];X)
be a mild solution of the Cauchy problem (
2.5
).
Then
y\in C([0,T];\overline{\operatorname{D}(A)})
.
In particular,
y_{0}=y(0)\in\overline{\operatorname{D}(A)}
.

The following theorem provides existence and uniqueness of mild solutions for problem (
2.5
).

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
A
be an m-accretive operator on a Banach space
X
. Then, for each
y_{0}\in\overline{\operatorname{D}(A)}
and
f\in L^{1}(0,T;X)
, there is a unique mild solution
y
to (
2.5
).
Moreover, if
y
and
\overline{y}
are two mild solutions to (
2.5
) corresponding to
f
,
y_{0}
and
\overline{f}
,
\overline{y}_{0}
, respectively, then

(2.6)

\lVert y-\overline{y}\rVert_{C([0,T];X)}\leq\lVert y_{0}-\overline{y}_{0}\rVert+\lVert f-\overline{f}\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;X)}.

Proof.

The first part concerning the existence of a unique mild solution corresponds to
[
Bar10
, Corollary 4.1]
and the contraction property (
2.6
) follows from a combination of
[
Bar10
, (4.14)]
and
[
Bar10
, Proposition 3.7(iv)]
.
∎

The following lemma is used to obtain compatibility of the mild solutions corresponding to the
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
setting and the
\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
setting.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(X,Y)
be a compatible couple of Banach spaces, let
A
be an m-accretive operator on
X
and let
B
be an m-accretive operator on
Y
.
Assume that
A
and
B
are resolvent compatible in the sense that, for each
\lambda>0
and
z\in X\cap Y
,

(2.7)

(I+\lambda A)^{-1}z=(I+\lambda B)^{-1}z.

Let
z_{0}\in\overline{\operatorname{D}(A)}\cap\overline{\operatorname{D}(B)}
, let
f\in L^{1}(0,T;X\cap Y)
and let
x
and
y
be the unique mild solutions to

(2.8)

\begin{cases}x^{\prime}(t)+Ax(t)=f(t),&t\in[0,T],\\
x(0)=z_{0},\end{cases}

and

(2.9)

\begin{cases}y^{\prime}(t)+By(t)=f(t),&t\in[0,T],\\
y(0)=z_{0},\end{cases}

respectively.
Then
x=y
.

Proof.

A combination of (
2.7
) and the iterative scheme
[
Vaz07
, (10.14)]
yields that we can construct simultaneous
\varepsilon
-approximate solutions for (
2.8
) and (
2.9
).
As both
x
and
y
are obtained by taking the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, we find that
x=y
.
∎

Using the contraction property (
2.6
) in Theorem
2.8
, by means of a standard fixed-point argument we can treat the following abstract problem with a non-linear right hand side.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
A
be an m-accretive operator on a Banach space
X
and let
F:\overline{\operatorname{D}(A)}\to X
be a Lipschitz function.
Then, for each
y_{0}\in\overline{\operatorname{D}(A)}
, there is a unique mild solution
y\in C([0,T];\overline{\operatorname{D}(A)})
to

(2.10)

\begin{cases}y^{\prime}(t)+Ay(t)=F(y(t)),&t\in[0,T],\\
y(0)=y_{0}.\end{cases}

Proof.

By the continuation of mild solutions
[
Vaz07
, Proposition 10.12(ii)]
, it suffices to consider the case
T<L:=[F]_{\mathrm{Lip}}
.
Now fix
y_{0}\in\overline{\operatorname{D}(A)}
and consider the complete metric space

{\mathbb{F}}:=\{y\in C([0,T];\overline{\operatorname{D}(A)}):y(0)=y_{0}\}.

Let
\mathscr{G}:L^{1}(0,T;X)\to{\mathbb{F}}
be the solution operator to (
2.5
) obtained from Theorem
2.8
and consider the mapping
\mathscr{T}:{\mathbb{F}}\to{\mathbb{F}}
defined by
\mathscr{T}(y):=\mathscr{S}(F(y))
.
Then, for
y,z\in{\mathbb{F}}
,

\displaystyle\lVert\mathscr{T}(y)-\mathscr{T}(z)\rVert_{{\mathbb{F}}}

\displaystyle=\lVert\mathscr{G}(F(y))-\mathscr{G}(F(z))\rVert_{{\mathbb{F}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:thm:exist_mild_sol_Cauchy;contr}}}{{\leq}}\lVert F(y)-F(z)\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;X)}

\displaystyle\leq L\lVert y-z\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;X)}\leq LT\lVert y-z\rVert_{C([0,T];X)}=LT\lVert y-z\rVert_{{\mathbb{F}}}.

Since
LT<1
, it follows from Banach’s fixed-point theorem that there is a unique
y\in{\mathbb{F}}
such that
y=\mathscr{T}(y)
.
∎

2.3.4.
Subpotential maximal monotone operators

Let
X
be a Banach space.
An operator
A:X\to X^{*}
is said to be
monotone
if, for all
(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})\in A
,

\langle x_{1}-x_{2},y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle\geq 0.

A monotone operator
A:X\to X^{*}
is said to be
maximal monotone
if it is not properly contained in any other monotone operator
X\to X^{*}
.

For
X=H
a Hilbert space that is identified with its dual
X^{*}
, an operator
A:H\to H
is accretive if and only if is monotone.
As a consequence, in this case, an operator
A:H\to H
is
m
-accretive if and only if is maximal monotone.

We will consider maximal monotone operators that are subdifferentials of a lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) convex function, also referred to as
subpotential maximal monotone operators
.
See Theorem
2.11
below, which can for instance be found in
[
Bar10
, Theorem 2.8]
.

Before we state the theorem, let us provide the definition of subdifferentials.
Let
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}
be a l.s.c. convex proper function.
We write

\operatorname{D}(\varphi):=\{x\in X:\varphi(x)<\infty\}.

The mapping
\partial\varphi:X\to X^{*}
defined by

\partial\varphi(x)=\{x^{*}\in X^{*}:\varphi(x)\leq\varphi(y)+\langle x^{*},x-y\rangle,\forall y\in X\}

is called the
subdifferential
of
\varphi
.
Note that, in general,
\partial\varphi
is multi-valued.
We write

\operatorname{D}(\partial\varphi):=\{x\in X:\partial\varphi(x)\neq\emptyset\}.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
X
be a real Banach space and let
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}
be a l.s.c. proper convex function. Then
\partial\varphi
is a maximal monotone operator.

For us the importance of subpotential maximal monotone operators comes from Brezis’ maximal
L^{2}
-regularity theorem
[
Bre71b
]
.
We will use this result in the form of
[
AH20
, Theorem 2.2]
(cf.
[
Bar10
, Theorem 4.11]
) as an abstract way to use energy methods in Theorem
3.4
.

2.3.5.
Stability

One way to obtain extra information on the regularity of mild solutions is by approximating the equation.
To this end, we recall the notion of convergence of
m
-accretive operators.
Let
(A_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of
m
-accretive operators on a Banach space
X
and let
A
be an
m
-accretive operator on
X
.
Following
[
BC81
, pg. 164]
(cf.
[
Bar10
, Proposition 4.4]
), we say that
A_{n}\to A
as
m
-accretive operators in
X
as
n\to\infty
if

(2.11)

\lim_{n\to\infty}(I+A_{n})^{-1}x=(I+A)^{-1}x,\qquad x\in X.

The following theorem can be considered the nonlinear version of the Trotter–Kato theorem from the theory of
C_{0}
-semigroups.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
(A_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of
m
-accretive operators on a Banach space
X
and let
A
be an m-accretive operator on
X
.
For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
f^{n}\in L^{1}(0,T;X)
,
y^{n}_{0}\in\overline{\operatorname{D}(A_{n})}
and let
y_{n}
be the mild solution to

\begin{cases}y_{n}^{\prime}(t)+A_{n}y_{n}(t)=f^{n}(t),&t\in[0,T],\\
y_{n}(0)=y^{n}_{0}.\end{cases}

If
A_{n}\to A
as
m
-accretive operators in
X
as
n\to\infty
,
f=\lim_{n\to\infty}f^{n}
in
L^{1}(0,T;X)
and
y_{0}=\lim_{n\to\infty}y^{n}_{0}
in
X
, then
y_{n}\to y
in
C([0,T];X)
as
n\to\infty
, where
y
is the mild solution to (
2.5
).

Proof.

Inspection of the proof of
[
Bar10
, Proposition 4.4]
shows that the ’if’-part of the statement only uses
[
Bar10
, (4.105)]
for an arbitrary
\lambda
, where
\lambda
is a resolvent parameter from the condition
[
Bar10
, (4.105)]
.
In particular, we may take
\lambda=1\in(0,\infty)
. Therefore,
[
Bar10
, Theorem 4.14]
is applicable and yields the desired result.
∎

3.
Well-posedness

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
except for the last statement, i.e. we show the well-posedness for Problem
(
1.3
). The non-negativity of solutions is shown in Section
4
.
To show well-posedness we formulate the problem as an abstract Cauchy problem in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and apply the theory of
m
-accretive operators.
To show that solutions take values in the open interval
(-1,1)
requires more work. We need to consider the Cauchy problem in
\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and provide a suitable characterization of the corresponding elliptic operator.

In the following two theorems we consider (
1.3
) as an abstract Cauchy problem of the form (
2.10
) in the Banach space
X=L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for the operator
A=A_{\phi}
in Proposition
2.3
.
The corresponding notion of mild solution was specified in Definition
2.5
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
I
be an open interval with
0\in I
,
\phi\colon I\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a maximal monotone function with
\phi(0)=0
and let
f\colon\overline{I}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a Lipschitz function with
f(0)=0
. Then for every
u_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\overline{I})
there exists a unique mild solution
u\in C([0,T];L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\overline{I}))
of

(3.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=\Delta\phi(u)+f(u)&\text{in}\>\>(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\
u(0)=u_{0}&\text{on}\>\>{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.\end{cases}

In Corollary
3.5
we will even see that if
I
is bounded then for all
t\in(0,T]
, the solution
u(t,x)
takes its values in the open interval
I
for almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Note that

F:L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\overline{I})\to L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\,v\mapsto f(v),

is a well-defined Lipschitz mapping with
[F]_{\mathrm{Lip}}\leq L
.
Indeed, we observe that
f
satisfies
|f(z)|\leq L|z|
for all
z\in\overline{I}
, thanks to
f(0)=0
and
L=[f]_{\mathrm{Lip}}
.
Combining Proposition
2.3
, Proposition
2.4
and Corollary
2.10
we obtain that for every
u_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\overline{I})
there exists a unique mild solution
u\in C([0,T];L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\overline{I}))
of (
1.3
).
∎

The main interest of this paper is the Sobolev regularity of mild solutions given by Theorem
3.1
.
In that context the term

in (

) plays a minor role as the proof of Theorem

shows that

. Hence,

can be considered as a given function and it suffices to consider the following situation.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\phi
satisfy the assumptions in Theorem
3.1
and

.
Then, for every
u_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\overline{I})
there exists a unique mild solution
u\in C([0,T];L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\overline{I}))
of the Cauchy problem

(3.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=\Delta\phi(u)+f&\text{in}\>\>(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\
u(0)=u_{0}&\text{on}\>\>{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.\end{cases}

Proof.

This follows from a combination of Proposition
2.3
, Proposition
2.4
and Theorem
2.8
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

For every
p\in[1,\infty]
, there is the continuous embedding

(3.3)

L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};[-1,1])\hookrightarrow L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

Indeed, for
v\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};[-1,1])
we have

|v|\leq 1
and thus

(3.4)

\lVert v\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|v(x)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|v(x)|\right)^{1/p}=\lVert v\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Remark
3.3
provides us with the flexibility of having the full range of
L^{p}
-spaces at our disposal.
In particular, if the dimension
d\geq 3
, we can choose
p\in(1,2)
such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{d}
.
Then there is the embedding (
2.3
).
This observation will allow us to consider (
1.3
) in
\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Let
\phi
be as in Theorem
3.1
. We denote by
\Phi
the primitive of

with
\Phi(0)=0
, that is

(3.5)

\Phi(r):=\int_{0}^{r}\phi(z)\,\mathrm{d}z,\qquad r\in I.

Note that
\Phi\geq 0
since
\phi(0)=0
and that
\phi
is monotonically increasing.
If
I\neq{\mathbb{R}}
, it will be convenient to view
\phi
and
\Phi
defined on
{\mathbb{R}}
by extending
\phi
as
\pm\infty
on
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus I
and
\Phi
as
\infty
on
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus I
.

For a measurable function

we define the

\varphi(v):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Phi(v)\,\mathrm{d}x=\lVert\Phi(v)\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let the notation and assumptions be as in Theorem

and

.
In addition, assume that

Then the mild solution

.

For the proof of Theorem

we will need the following technical lemma (cf.
[
Bar10
, Lemma 2.6]
).

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose that
d\geq 3
. Let
p\in(1,2)
satisfy
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{d}
and let
q\in(p,\infty)
.
Let
f\in\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
g\in\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
If
fg\geq 0
a.e., then
fg\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

\bigl\langle f,g\bigr\rangle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}fg\geq 0,

where
\bigl\langle\cdot,\cdot\bigr\rangle
denotes the duality pairing between
\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proof.

Pick
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\chi\geq 0
and
\chi\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
0
and such that
\chi
is radially decreasing.
Let
\chi_{n}(x):=\chi(\frac{x}{n})
and

g_{n}:=\chi_{n}g
. We observe that

\nabla g_{n}=(\nabla\chi_{n})g+\chi_{n}\nabla g=\underbrace{\frac{1}{n}\left(\nabla\chi\left(\frac{\cdot}{n}\right)\right)g}_{=:u_{n}}+\underbrace{\chi_{n}\nabla g}_{=:v_{n}}.

By the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we have
v_{n}\to\nabla g
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as
n\to\infty
. Let us next treat
u_{n}
.
As

\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{d},

we have by Hölder’s inequality and a substitution of variables

\displaystyle\lVert u_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\frac{1}{n}\nabla\chi\left(\frac{\cdot}{n}\right)\rVert_{L^{d}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\lVert\nabla\chi\rVert_{L^{d}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

This shows that the sequence
(u_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is bounded in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Therefore, it has a weakly convergent subsequence,
say
u_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as
k\to\infty
.
Testing against
\eta\in\mathscr{D}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we find that
u=0
because

\text{\rm supp\,}(u_{n_{k}})\subset\text{\rm supp\,}\left(\nabla\chi\left(\frac{\cdot}{n}\right)\right)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B(0,n).

Combining the above, we obtain that
\nabla g_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup\nabla g
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as
k\to\infty
.
This means that
g_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup g
in
\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as
k\to\infty
.
Since
g_{n_{k}}\in H^{1}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subset L^{p^{\prime}}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subset L^{q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by (
2.2
) and
p^{\prime}>q^{\prime}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\bigl\langle f,g\bigr\rangle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\bigl\langle f,g_{n_{k}}\bigr\rangle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}fg_{n_{k}}.

By construction,
0\leq fg_{n_{k}}\nearrow fg
a.e. as
k\to\infty
.
Therefore, by the Beppo Levi theorem,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}fg_{n_{k}}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}fg.

The desired result now follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.7
.

First, we show that
\varphi
is a proper convex function.
To see that it is lower semicontinuous, let
\lambda>0
and let
(u_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be such that
u_{n}\to u
in
\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

and
\varphi(u_{n})\leq\lambda
.
Then
(u_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and, thanks to the assumption (
3.7
),

\lVert u_{n}\rVert_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq M(\lambda),\qquad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

As a consequence,
(u_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has a weakly convergent subsequence
(u_{n_{k}})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. As weak convergence in
L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
implies convergence in
\dot{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\hookrightarrow\dot{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
where
\dot{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is the space of tempered distributions modulo polynomials,
we find that
u
is the weak limit of
(u_{n_{k}})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
By
[
Bar10
, Proposition 2.7]
,

\varphi_{q}:L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to[0,\infty],\>v\mapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Phi(v),

is a proper convex, lower semicontinuous function, and thus as a consequence of Mazur’s theorem (see
[
Bar10
, page 5]
) also weakly lower semicontinous.
Therefore,

\varphi(u)=\varphi_{q}(u)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{q}(u_{n})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\varphi(u_{n})\leq\lambda.

This shows that the level set
\{u\in\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\varphi(u)\leq\lambda\}
is closed for each
\lambda>0
, which means that
\varphi
is lower semicontinuous.

Next, we establish the final statement (
3.14
) in the form (
3.15
) following Remark
3.8
.
Denoting the operator on the right-hand side of (
3.15
) by
A
, it suffices to show that
A
is monotone and that
\partial\varphi\subset A
.
Indeed, as
\partial\varphi
is maximal monotone by Theorem
2.11
, this implies that
\partial\varphi=A
.

In order to show that
A
is monotone, let
(u,\phi(u)),(v,\phi(v))\in A
.
Then
(u-v)(\phi(u)-\phi(v))\geq 0
as
\phi
is monotonically increasing.
By Lemma
3.9
we thus obtain that

\bigl\langle u-v,\phi(u)-\phi(v)\bigr\rangle\geq 0,

which proves that
A
is monotone.

Finally, let us show that
\partial\varphi\subset A
.
To this end, fix
(u,w)\in\partial\varphi
, that is,
u\in\operatorname{D}(\partial\varphi)
and
w\in\partial\varphi(u)
.
By
[
Bar10
, Proposition 1.6]
it holds that
\operatorname{D}(\partial\varphi)\subset\operatorname{D}(\varphi)=\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
In particular, we have
u\in\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Furthermore, by definition of
\partial\varphi(u)
, we have

(3.16)

\varphi(u)-\varphi(v)\leq\bigl\langle u-v,w\bigr\rangle

for all
v\in\operatorname{D}(\varphi)=\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Let
B=B(x_{0},r)
for some
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
r>0
and let
u_{0}\in I
.
Then
u_{0}{{\bf 1}}_{B},u{{\bf 1}}_{B}\in L^{q}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subset L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as
q\geq p
, so that
u_{0}{{\bf 1}}_{B},u{{\bf 1}}_{B}\in\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by (
2.3
).
Defining

v:=u_{0}{{\bf 1}}_{B}+u{{\bf 1}}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B}=u+(u_{0}-u){{\bf 1}}_{B}\in\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

we find that

\displaystyle\varphi(u{{\bf 1}}_{B})-\varphi(u_{0}{{\bf 1}}_{B})

\displaystyle=\varphi(u{{\bf 1}}_{B})+\varphi(u{{\bf 1}}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B})-(\varphi(u_{0}{{\bf 1}}_{B})+\varphi(u{{\bf 1}}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B}))

\displaystyle=\varphi(u)-\varphi(v)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:ineq_varphi}}}{{\leq}}\bigl\langle u-v,w\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle(u-u_{0}){{\bf 1}}_{B},w\bigr\rangle.

As
(u-u_{0}){{\bf 1}}_{B}\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
w\in\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subset L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by (
2.2
),
we have
\bigl\langle(u-u_{0}){{\bf 1}}_{B},w\bigr\rangle=\int_{B}(u-u_{0})w
.
The above inequality can thus be rewritten as

\int_{B}(\Phi(u)-\Phi(u_{0}))\leq\int_{B}(u-u_{0})w.

As this holds for arbitrary
B=B(x_{0},r)
with
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
r>0
,
it follows that

\Phi(u)\leq\Phi(u_{0})+(u-u_{0})w

for all
u_{0}\in I
.
The latter means that
w(x)\in\partial\Phi(u(x))
for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
As
\Phi
is a convex function with a.e. derivative
\phi
, we have
\partial\Phi(u(x))=\phi(u(x))
for a.a.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(see e.g.
[
Bar10
, Example 3, page 8]
).
Therefore,
w=\phi(u)
and we conclude that
(u,w)\in A
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let the assumptions and notation be as in Theorem
3.7
.
Moreover, let
A_{\phi}
be the operator on
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
from Proposition
2.3
.
Then
\partial\varphi
and
A_{\phi}
are resolvent compatible in the sense that, for each

and

,

(3.17)

(I+\lambda\partial\varphi)^{-1}f=(I+\lambda A_{\phi})^{-1}f.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda>0
and
f\in\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and set
u:=(I+\lambda\partial\varphi)^{-1}f
.
Then

so that
\phi(u)\in\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subset L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subset L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by Theorem
3.7
and (
2.2
).
Moreover,

-\Delta\phi(u)=\lambda^{-1}(f-u).

It suffices to show that
u\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Indeed, then
u\in\operatorname{D}(A_{\phi})
with

(I+\lambda A_{\phi})u=(I+\lambda\partial\varphi)u=f,

from which it follows that (
3.17
) holds true.

In order to show that
u\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we will formally multiply the equation

(3.18)

u-\lambda\Delta\phi(u)=f\qquad\text{in}\quad\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

by
\operatorname{sign}_{0}(\phi(u))
and integrate over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
In the spirit of
[
Bar10
, page 112]
, to make this rigorous,
let
\gamma_{\varepsilon}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}},\varepsilon>0,
be an approximation of

\operatorname{sign}_{0}
as in
[
Bar10
, (3.26)]
. The explicit form is not important here, we will only need that
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
is a monotonically increasing
BC^{1}
-function with
\gamma_{\varepsilon}(0)=0
and such that
\gamma_{\varepsilon}\to\operatorname{sign}_{0}
on
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Note that
\nabla\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\phi(u))=\gamma^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u)\nabla\phi(u)\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, so
\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\phi(u))\in\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
We can thus test (
3.18
) against
\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\phi(u))
.
This yields

\langle u,\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\phi(u))\rangle+\lambda\langle-\Delta\phi(u),\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\phi(u))\rangle=\langle f,\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\phi(u))\rangle.

Since

\displaystyle\langle-\Delta\phi(u),\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\phi(u))\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla\phi(u),\nabla\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\phi(u))\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla\phi(u),\gamma^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u)\nabla\phi(u)\rangle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\gamma^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u)|\nabla\phi(u)|^{2}\geq 0

it follows that

As

, we have

.
Furthermore, as

and

We can thus invoke Lemma

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\phi(u))=\langle u,\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\phi(u))\rangle\leq\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

∎

\alpha\geq\frac{d-2}{d}
.
If the condition (
4.1
) holds true, then
\phi:I\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies the condition (
4.3
).

Proof.

Let
R>0
. We need to show that the two integrals in (
4.3
) diverge. As the proof for both integrals is similar we will only consider the second one.
To this end, put
s_{R}:=\frac{1}{R^{d-2}}
and
J:=[0,\beta(s_{R})]
, where
\beta=\phi^{-1}.

Then, for each
s\in[0,s_{R}]
we have
r=\beta(s)\in J
, such that

s=\phi(r)\leq C_{J}r^{\alpha}=C_{J}\beta(s)^{\alpha}

and thus,

\beta(s)\geq C_{J}^{-1/\alpha}s^{1/\alpha}.

Since
d-1-\frac{d-2}{\alpha}\geq-1
, it follows that

\int_{R}^{\infty}\rho^{d-1}\beta\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{d-2}}\right)\mathrm{d}\rho\geq C_{J}^{-1/\alpha}\int_{R}^{\infty}\rho^{d-1-\frac{d-2}{\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\rho=\infty.

∎

Example 4.4
.

The maximal monotone function
\phi:(-1,1)\to{\mathbb{R}}
from Example
2.2
satisfies the condition (
4.1
) with
\alpha=b+1
.

Indeed, to see that
\phi
satisfies the condition (
4.1
) with
\alpha=b+1
, let
R\in(0,1)
and note that, for each
\rho\in[-R,R]
,

|\phi(\rho)|\leq\int_{0}^{|\rho|}\frac{z^{b}}{(1-z)^{a}}\mathrm{d}z\leq C_{R}\int_{0}^{|\rho|}z^{b}\mathrm{d}z=\frac{1}{b+1}|\rho|^{b+1},

for some constant
C_{R}>0
depending on
R
.

Combining Propositions
4.2
,
2.4
and Theorem
2.12
immediately yields the following stability result.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let the assumptions in Theorem
4.2
hold.
For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let

,
y^{n}_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
y^{n}_{0}\in\overline{I_{n}}
a.e. and let

(4.4)

as

4.2.
Mild solutions of non-degenerate problems

In this subsection we prove that the notions of mild and classical solutions coincide for smooth, non-degenerate quasilinear problems,
cf. Remark
2.6
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a
C^{2}
-function with
\psi(0)=0
,
\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime\prime}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\psi^{\prime}(r)>0
for all
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
v\in C^{1,2}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be a classical solution of

(4.6)

\partial_{t}v=\Delta\psi(v)+g\quad\text{in}\>\>(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

with
\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}v\in C([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\partial_{t}v\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for a given
g\in W^{1,1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Furthermore, assume that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}|\nabla v(t)|\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}|\partial_{t}\nabla v(t)|\mathrm{d}\sigma=0,

and
\Delta\psi(v(0))+g(0)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
If
v
satisfies the initial condition
v(0)=v_{0}
, then
v
belongs to
W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and is a strong (and thus mild) solution of (
2.4
) in the
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
-setting.

The following lemma provides an
L^{1}
-contraction principle for (
4.6
).
Whereas the final statement (
4.14
) is closest to a classical formulation of an
L^{1}
-contraction principle, the estimates (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) turn out to be important for the proof of Proposition
4.7
.

Lemma 4.8

(
L^{1}
-contraction principle)
.

Let
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a
C^{2}
-function with
\psi(0)=0
,
\psi^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\psi^{\prime}(r)>0
for all
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
v,\widetilde{v}\in C^{1,2}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be classical solutions of

(4.8)

\partial_{t}v=\Delta\psi(v)+g\quad\text{in}\>\>(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

and

\partial_{t}\widetilde{v}=\Delta\psi(\widetilde{v})+\widetilde{g}\quad\text{in}\>\>(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

respectively, where
g,\widetilde{g}\in L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Then, for every
t\in[0,T]
and
R>0
, we have the estimates

\displaystyle\|(v(t,\cdot)-\widetilde{v}(t,\cdot))_{+}\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(0))}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(v(0,\cdot)-\widetilde{v}(0,\cdot))_{+}\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(0))}+\|(g-\widetilde{g})_{+}\operatorname{sign}^{+}_{0}(v-\widetilde{v})\|_{L^{1}((0,t)\times B_{R}(0))}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla(\psi(v)-\psi(\widetilde{v}))\|_{L^{1}(\partial B_{R}(0))}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(v(0,\cdot)-\widetilde{v}(0,\cdot))_{+}\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(0))}+\|(g-\widetilde{g})_{+}\operatorname{sign}^{+}_{0}(v-\widetilde{v})\|_{L^{1}((0,t)\times B_{R}(0))}

\displaystyle+\lVert\psi^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\left(\|\nabla v(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\partial B_{R}(0))}+\|\nabla\widetilde{v}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\partial B_{R}(0))}\right).

and, as consequence, also the estimates

\displaystyle\|v(t,\cdot)-\widetilde{v}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(0))}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|v(0,\cdot)-\widetilde{v}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(0))}+\|g-\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{1}((0,t)\times B_{R}(0))}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla(\psi(v)-\psi(\widetilde{v}))\|_{L^{1}(\partial B_{R}(0))}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|v(0,\cdot)-\widetilde{v}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(0))}+\|g-\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{1}((0,t)\times B_{R}(0))}

\displaystyle+\lVert\psi^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\left(\|\nabla v(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\partial B_{R}(0))}+\|\nabla\widetilde{v}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\partial B_{R}(0))}\right).

In particular, if
\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}|\nabla v(t)|\mathrm{d}\sigma\to 0
and
\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}|\nabla\widetilde{v}(t)|\mathrm{d}\sigma\to 0
for
R\to\infty
, then

(4.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lVert(v(t)-\widetilde{v}(t))_{+}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\\
\leq&\lVert(v(0)-\widetilde{v}(0))_{+}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\lVert(g-\widetilde{g})_{+}\operatorname{sign}^{+}_{0}(v-\widetilde{v})\rVert_{L^{1}([0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.\end{split}

and, as a consequence, also

(4.14)

\lVert v(t)-\widetilde{v}(t)\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\lVert v(0)-\widetilde{v}(0)\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\lVert g-\widetilde{g}\rVert_{L^{1}([0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Proof.

This follows by a modification of the proof of

[
Vaz07
, Proposition 3.5]
for bounded domains
\Omega
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.
Indeed, let
0\leq\eta\leq 1
be a
C^{1}
-approximation of the
\operatorname{sign}^{+}_{0}
function and
w=\psi(v)-\psi(\widetilde{v})
.
Then, integration by parts yields

\int_{\Omega}\Delta w\,\eta(w)\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\eta^{\prime}(w)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla w\cdot\nu\,\eta(w)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,

where
\nu
denotes the outward unit normal vector on
\partial\Omega
.
The boundary term can be estimated by

\displaystyle|\nabla w\cdot\nu\,\eta(w)|

\displaystyle\leq|\nabla w|=|\nabla(\psi(v)-\psi(\widetilde{v}))|=|\psi^{\prime}(v)\nabla v-\psi^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})\nabla\widetilde{v}|

\displaystyle\leq|\psi^{\prime}(v)\nabla v|+|\psi^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})\nabla\widetilde{v}|\leq\lVert\psi^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}(|\nabla v|+|\nabla\widetilde{v}|).

With this modification the argument in the proof of
[
Vaz07
, Proposition 3.5]
yields (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) by taking
\Omega=B_{R}(0)
. The estimate (
4.13
) subsequently follows by monotone convergence.
∎

Next we use the above
L^{1}
-contraction principle to show that
v(t)
and
\partial_{t}v(t)
belong to
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with concrete norm estimates.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let the assumptions of Proposition
4.7
be satisfied.
Then
v(t),\partial_{t}v(t)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with norm estimates

(4.15)

\lVert v(t)\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\lVert v(0)\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\lVert g\rVert_{L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

and

(4.16)

\lVert\partial_{t}v(t)\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\lVert\Delta\psi(v(0))+g(0)\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\lVert\partial_{t}g\rVert_{L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Proof.

The estimate (
4.15
) follows directly from (
4.12
) by taking
\widetilde{v}=0

and
\widetilde{g}=0

in Lemma
4.8
.
Applying Lemma
4.8
with
\widetilde{v}=v(\,\cdot\,+h,\,\cdot\,)
gives through (
4.11
)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left|\frac{v(t+h,x)-v(t,x)}{h}\right|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left|\frac{v(h,x)-v(0,x)}{h}\right|\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left|\frac{g(s+h,x)-g(s,x)}{h}\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}\left|\frac{\nabla(\psi(v(t+h)))-\nabla(\psi(v(t)))}{h}\right|\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Taking the limit
h\to 0
we obtain by dominated convergence

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\partial_{t}v(t)|\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\partial_{t}v(0)|\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\partial_{t}g(s,x)|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}|\partial_{t}\nabla(\psi(v(t)))|\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Observing that

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\nabla(\psi(v))|

\displaystyle=|\partial_{t}(\psi^{\prime}(v)\nabla v)|=|\psi^{\prime\prime}(v)\partial_{t}v\nabla v+\psi^{\prime}(v)\partial_{t}\nabla v|

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\psi^{\prime\prime}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\|\partial_{t}v\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}|\nabla v|+\lVert\psi^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}|\partial_{t}\nabla v|,

and taking the limit
R\to\infty
, we obtain by monotone convergence that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\partial_{t}v(t)|\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\partial_{t}v(0)|\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\partial_{t}g(s,x)|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s.

Using that
v
is a classical solution of (
4.6
) we finally arrive at (
4.16
).
∎

Note that Lemma
4.9
implies that
v(t),\partial_{t}v(t)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. This together with the given norm estimates is already quite close to
v
being a strong solution in the
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
-setting, i.e. the conclusion of Proposition
4.7
.
However, regularity in the form of strong measurablity with respect to
t
as an
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
-valued function is missing.
This technicality is taken care of in the following proof.

Proof of Proposition
4.7
.

By Lemma
4.9
we can view
v
as a function
v:[0,T]\to L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Now note that, for all
h\in L^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

t\mapsto\langle v(t),h\rangle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}v(t,x)h(x)\mathrm{d}x

is a continuous and thus measurable function.
As
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a separable Banach space and
L^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a weak
∗
dense subspace of
[L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})]^{*}=L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
Pettis’ measurability theorem (see
[
HvNVW16
, Theorem 1.1.6]
) yields that
v:[0,T]\to L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is strongly measurable.
In the same way, it follows that
\partial_{t}v:[0,T]\to L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a strongly measurable function.
It is not difficult to see that
\partial_{t}v:[0,T]\to L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is the weak derivative of
v:[0,T]\to L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and subsequently, that
v
belongs to
W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and is a strong solution of
(
2.4
).
∎

4.3.
Decay estimates for non-degenerate problems

In this subsection we prove decay estimates. The main aim is to provide sufficient conditions that imply the assumptions of Proposition
4.7
, particularly, the decay assumption (
4.7
).
Our strategy is to follow an approach based on weighted
L^{p}
-spaces.

For
p\in[1,\infty]
and
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
we consider the weight

(4.17)

w_{\kappa}(x):=\max\{1,|x|^{\kappa}\}=\begin{cases}1,&|x|<1,\\
|x|^{\kappa},&|x|\geq 1,\end{cases}

on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. For an open set
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
the
associated weighted
L^{p}
-space
L^{p}_{\kappa}(U)
is defined by

(4.18)

L^{p}_{\kappa}(U):=\left\{f\in L^{0}(U):w_{\kappa}f\in L^{p}(U)\right\},\quad\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}_{\kappa}(U)}:=\lVert w_{\kappa}f\rVert_{L^{p}(U)},

and, for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the corresponding weighted Sobolev space
W^{k,p}_{\kappa}(U)
by

\displaystyle W^{k,p}_{\kappa}(U)

\displaystyle:=\{f\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(U):\partial^{\alpha}f\in L^{p}_{\kappa}(U),|\alpha|\leq k\},

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{W^{k,p}_{\kappa}(U)}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\lVert\partial^{\alpha}f\rVert_{L^{p}_{\kappa}(U)}.

Furthermore, we define

\displaystyle BC^{k}_{\kappa}(U)

\displaystyle:=W^{k,\infty}_{\kappa}(U)\cap C^{k}(U),

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{BC^{k}_{\kappa}(U)}

\displaystyle:=\lVert f\rVert_{W^{k,\infty}_{\kappa}(U)}.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
p\in(d,\infty)
and
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then we have the continuous embedding

W^{k,p}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\hookrightarrow BC^{k-1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

Proof.

By induction, it suffices to consider the case
k=1
.
Furthermore, by density of
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
in
W^{1,p}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
it suffices to show that

W^{1,p}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

By the classical Sobolev embedding theorem (see e.g.
[
Bre11
, Corollary 9.14]
) and a translation argument we have

W^{1,p}(B_{1}(x_{0}))\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x_{0})),\qquad x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

with a norm estimate independent of
x_{0}
.
Since
w_{\kappa}(x)\eqsim w_{\kappa}(x_{0})

for all
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
x\in B_{1}(x_{0})
, it follows that

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{\kappa}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\eqsim w_{\kappa}(x_{0})\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}\lesssim w_{\kappa}(x_{0})\lVert f\rVert_{W^{1,p}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\eqsim\lVert f\rVert_{W^{1,p}_{\kappa}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{W^{1,p}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

By the nature of the
L^{\infty}
-norm and since
x_{0}
is arbitrary this implies that
\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{W^{1,p}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
.
∎

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\psi\in C^{3}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfy
\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime\prime},\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\psi^{\prime}\geq c
for some
c\in(0,\infty)
. Let
p\in(d,\infty)
,
\kappa\in\big[0,d(1-\frac{1}{p})\big)
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
\beta\in(0,1)
.
Let
g\in C^{\alpha}([0,T];L^{p}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap BC^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
,
v_{0}\in W^{2,p}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap BC^{\beta+2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
v\in C^{1}([0,T];BC^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\cap C([0,T];BC^{\beta+2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
be a solution of the problem

(4.19)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v_{t}=\Delta\psi(v)+g&\text{in}\ (0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\
v(0)=v_{0}&\text{on}\ {\mathbb{R}}^{d}.\end{cases}

Then

(4.20)

v\in C^{1}([0,T];L^{p}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\cap C([0,T];W^{2,p}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})).

Proof.

Note that the equation (
4.19
) can be rewritten as

v_{t}=\psi^{\prime}(v)\Delta v+\psi^{\prime\prime}(v)\nabla v\cdot\nabla v+g.

In particular, we can view
v
as a solution
u=v
of

(4.21)

u_{t}=\psi^{\prime}(v)\Delta u+\psi^{\prime\prime}(v)\nabla v\cdot\nabla u+g,

which is a non-autonomous linear equation in
u
.

By
[
Lun95
, Proposition 1.1.4, Proposition 1.2.13 and Corollary 1.2.18]
there is the mixed-derivative embedding

C^{1}([0,T];BC^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\cap C([0,T];BC^{\beta+2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\hookrightarrow C^{\frac{1}{2}}([0,T];BC^{\beta+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})),

so in particular,

(4.22)

v\in C^{\frac{1}{2}}([0,T];BC^{\beta+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\hookrightarrow C^{\alpha}([0,T];BC^{\beta+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})).

We define

X:=BC^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\qquad D:=BC^{\beta+2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

and, for each
t\in[0,T]
, consider the linear operator

A(t):D\to X,\quad\>u\mapsto\psi^{\prime}(v(t))\Delta u+\psi^{\prime\prime}(v(t))\nabla v(t)\cdot\nabla u.

Note here that, thanks to the embedding (
4.22
) and the assumption
\psi\in C^{3}({\mathbb{R}})
,
\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime\prime},\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we have
\psi^{\prime}(v),\psi^{\prime\prime}(v)\nabla v\in C^{\alpha}([0,T];BC^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
.
By
[
Lun95
, Theorem 3.1.14 and Corollary 3.1.16]
the operator-theoretic conditions of
[
Lun95
, Section 6.1,pg. 212]
are thus satisfied.
As
g\in C^{\alpha}([0,T];X)
and
v_{0}\in D
,
[
Lun95
, Corollary 6.1.6 and Proposition 6.2.2]
yields that there exists a unique strict solution
u
of

(4.23)

u^{\prime}(t)=A(t)u(t)+g(t),\>\>0<t\leq T;\quad u(0)=v_{0}.

A strict solution satisfies
u\in C^{1}([0,T];X)\cap C([0,T];D)
and solves the initial value problem (
4.23
).
As
v
is a strict solution of this equation as well, we conclude that
v=u
.

Next we define

\widetilde{X}:=BC^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{p}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\qquad\widetilde{D}:=BC^{\beta+2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap W^{2,p}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

and, for each
t\in[0,T]
, consider the linear operator

\widetilde{A}(t):\widetilde{D}\to\widetilde{X},\quad u\mapsto\psi^{\prime}(v(t))\Delta u+\psi^{\prime\prime}(v(t))\nabla v(t)\cdot\nabla u,

where we have, as above,
\psi^{\prime}(v),\psi^{\prime\prime}(v)\nabla v\in C^{\alpha}([0,T];BC^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
.
By
[
Lun95
, Theorem 3.1.4 and Corollary 3.1.6]
and
[
HHH03
, Theorem 3.1]
the operator-theoretic conditions of
[
Lun95
, Section 6.1,pg. 212]
are thus satisfied.
As
g\in C^{\alpha}([0,T];\widetilde{X})
and
v_{0}\in\widetilde{D}
,
[
Lun95
, Corollary 6.1.6 and Proposition 6.2.2]
implies that there exists a unique strict solution
\widetilde{u}\in C^{1}([0,T];\widetilde{X})\cap C([0,T];\widetilde{D})
of

(4.24)

\widetilde{u}^{\prime}(t)=\widetilde{A}(t)\widetilde{u}(t)+g(t),\quad 0<t\leq T;\qquad\widetilde{u}(0)=v_{0}.

As
\widetilde{X}\hookrightarrow X
,
\widetilde{D}\hookrightarrow D
and
A(t)|_{\widetilde{D}}=\widetilde{A}(t)
for every
t\in[0,T]
, every strict solution of (
4.24
) is a strict solution of (
4.23
).
Therefore, by uniqueness of strict solutions,
v=u=\widetilde{u}\in C^{1}([0,T];\widetilde{X})\cap C([0,T];\widetilde{D})
.
∎

Next, we generalize the result to obtain higher regularity.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
K:=\max\{k+2,3\}
and
\psi\in C^{K}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfy
\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime\prime},\ldots,\psi^{[K]}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\psi^{\prime}\geq c
for some
c\in(0,\infty)
.
Let
p\in(d,\infty)
,
\kappa\in[0,d(1-\frac{1}{p}))
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
\beta\in(0,1)
.
Let
g\in C^{\alpha}([0,T];W^{k,p}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap BC^{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
,

and

\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}|\nabla u_{k}(t)|\mathrm{d}\sigma\to 0
and
\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{k}(t)|\mathrm{d}\sigma\to 0
for
R\to\infty
.
In view of
1
and
2
, the inequality (
4.13
)
from Lemma

thus yields that, for every

,

\displaystyle\lVert(u_{k}(t)-\widetilde{u}_{k}(t))_{+}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert(u_{k}(0)-\widetilde{u}_{k}(0))_{+}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\quad+\lVert(f_{k}-\widetilde{f}_{k})_{+}\operatorname{sign}^{+}_{0}(u-\widetilde{u})\rVert_{L^{1}([0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Taking the limit for
k\to\infty
we arrive at (
4.30
) thanks to the convergence from
5
.
∎

Corollary 4.18
.

Let
\phi:I\to{\mathbb{R}}
be as in Theorem
4.17
and let
f,\widetilde{f}\colon\overline{I}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be Lipschitz functions with
f(0)=0
and
\widetilde{f}(0)=0
.
Let
u_{0}\,\widetilde{u_{0}}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\overline{I})
and let

be the mild solutions of (
3.1
) and

(4.33)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\widetilde{u}=\Delta\phi(\widetilde{u})+\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{u})&\text{in}\>\>(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\
\widetilde{u}(0)=\widetilde{u}_{0}&\text{on}\>\>{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\end{cases}

respectively.
Then, for all

,

(4.34)

\lVert(u(t)-\widetilde{u}(t))_{+}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq e^{Lt}\left(\lVert(u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0})_{+}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\lVert(f(u)-\widetilde{f}(u))_{+}\rVert_{L^{1}((0,t)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\right),

where
L=[\widetilde{f}]_{\mathrm{Lip}}
.

Proof.

First recall that
f(u),\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{u})\in L^{1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, see the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
So we can apply Theorem
4.17
with
f(u)
and
\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{u})
in place of
f
and
\widetilde{f}
, respectively, which through the estimate (
4.30
) gives that

\lVert(u(t)-\widetilde{u}(t))_{+}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\lVert(u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0})_{+}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\lVert(f(u)-\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{u}))_{+}\operatorname{sign}^{+}_{0}(u-\widetilde{u})\rVert_{L^{1}((0,t)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Since

\displaystyle(f(u)-\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{u}))_{+}\operatorname{sign}^{+}_{0}(u-\widetilde{u})

\displaystyle=(f(u)-\widetilde{f}(u))_{+}\operatorname{sign}^{+}_{0}(u-\widetilde{u})

\displaystyle\quad+(\widetilde{f}(u)-\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{u}))_{+}\operatorname{sign}^{+}_{0}(u-\widetilde{u})

\displaystyle\leq(f(u)-\widetilde{f}(u))_{+}+|\widetilde{f}(u)-\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{u}))|\operatorname{sign}^{+}_{0}(u-\widetilde{u})

\displaystyle\leq(f(u)-\widetilde{f}(u))_{+}+L|u-\widetilde{u}|\operatorname{sign}^{+}_{0}(u-\widetilde{u})

\displaystyle=(f(u)-\widetilde{f}(u))_{+}+L(u-\widetilde{u})_{+},

it follows that

\displaystyle\lVert(u(t)-\widetilde{u}(t))_{+}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert(u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0})_{+}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\lVert(f(u)-\widetilde{f}(u))_{+}\rVert_{L^{1}((0,t)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}L\lVert(u(s)-\widetilde{u}(s))_{+}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\mathrm{d}s.

By the integral form of Gronwall’s inequality we thus obtain (
4.34
).
∎

Corollary 4.19
.

Let the notations and assumptions be as in Theorem

Proof.

Note that
\widetilde{u}=0

Applying Corollary

\displaystyle\lVert(u(t))_{-}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

5.
Regularity

In this section we address the Sobolev regularity of solutions and prove Theorem
1.2
. To this end we first derive the kinetic formulation for (
1.3
) and then apply Fourier analytic techniques to deduce the resularity results.

5.1.
Kinetic formulation

We aim to rigorously derive the kinetic formulation for (
1.3
).
To this end let
u:[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. We introduce the corresponding kinetic function

(5.1)

\displaystyle\chi(t,x,v;u):

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}+1,&0<v<u(t,x),\\
-1,&u(t,x)<v<0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle={{\bf 1}}_{\{0<v<u(t,x)\}}-{{\bf 1}}_{\{u(t,x)<v<0\}},

for
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},v\in{\mathbb{R}}.

This kinetic function satisfies a linear parabolic equation if
u
is the mild solution of (
3.2
).

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be an open interval with
0\in I
and let
\phi:I\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a maximal monotone function with
\phi\in W^{1,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I)
and
\phi(0)=0
.
Assume, in case
d\geq 3
, that there exists
\alpha\geq\frac{d-2}{d}
such that (
4.1
) holds.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\overline{I})
,
f\in L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
u\in C(0,T];L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\overline{I}))
be the unique mild solution of (
3.2
).
Then there exists a positive measure
n\in\Lambda^{\infty}(I;\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
such that

(5.2)

\partial_{t}\chi(t,x,v;u)=\phi^{\prime}(v)\Delta_{x}\chi(t,x,v;u)+\partial_{v}n(t,x,v)+\delta_{v=u(t,x)}f

in the sense of distributions on
(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{x}\times I_{v}
.
Moreover,

(5.3)

\lVert u\rVert_{C([0,T];L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

and

(5.4)

\lVert n\rVert_{\Lambda^{\infty}(I;\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Proposition
5.1
is inspired by
[
CP03
, Section 2]
and
[
GST20
, Lemma A.2]
.
Different from these works we link the mild solution concept to kinetic solutions.
However, besides the kinetic equation itself (
5.2
)/
[
CP03
, (2.16)]
, our kinetic formulation is quite different from
[
CP03
, Definition 2.2]
.
Indeed, motivated by applying it to prove Sobolev regularity, the only property of the measure
n
that is relevant to us is that
n\in\Lambda^{\infty}(I;\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
.
This property is not included in
[
CP03
, Definition 2.2]
, but could be derived from it as shown in
[
GST20
, Lemma A.2]
.

In the proof of Proposition
5.1
we will use Theorem
4.1
to approximate the equation (
3.2
) by smooth, non-degenerate versions.
The approximate solutions are sufficiently regular such that we can carry out the necessary computations leading to the following lemma.
In the proof we use the following observation that also lies at the core of velocity averaging.
Let
H\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
be arbitrary with
H(0)=0
. Then, if
u
takes its values in
\overline{I}
a.e.,
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
an interval, we have

(5.5)

H(u(t,x))=\int_{I}H^{\prime}(v)\chi(t,x,v;u)\mathrm{d}v.

Indeed, this follows from

\displaystyle\int_{I}H^{\prime}(v)\chi(t,x,v;u)\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\int_{I}H^{\prime}(v)({{\bf 1}}_{\{0<v<u\}}-{{\bf 1}}_{\{u<v<0\}})\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&u(t,x)=0\\
\int_{0}^{u(t,x)}H^{\prime}(v)\mathrm{d}v,&u(t,x)>0,\\
-\int_{u(t,x)}^{0}H^{\prime}(v)\mathrm{d}v,&u(t,x)<0,\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0=H(u(t,x)),&u(t,x)=0\\
H(u(t,x)),&u(t,x)>0,\\
--H(u(t,x))=H(u(t,x)),&u(t,x)<0,\end{cases}

\displaystyle=H(u(t,x)).

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a
C^{2}
-function with
\psi(0)=0
,
\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime\prime}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\psi^{\prime}(r)>0
for all
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
g\in L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
w_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
w\in C^{1,2}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be a classical solution of

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w=\Delta\psi(w)+g&\text{in}\>\>(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\
w(0)=w_{0}&\text{on}\>\>{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.\end{cases}

with
\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}|\nabla w|\mathrm{d}\sigma\to 0
for
R\to\infty
.
Then there exists a positive measure
n\in\Lambda^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}};\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
such that

(5.6)

\partial_{t}\chi(t,x,v;w)=\psi^{\prime}(v)\Delta_{x}\chi(t,x,v;w)+\partial_{v}n(t,x,v)+\delta_{v=w(t,x)}g

in the sense of distributions on
(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
Moreover, the measure
n
satisfies

(5.7)

\lVert n\rVert_{\Lambda^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}};\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\lVert g\rVert_{L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

This lemma is to a large extend based on computations in
[
CP03
, Section 2]
.
The main difference is that we omit the entropy formulation and directly use the kinetic formulation.
Moreover, for the kinetic formulation the same differences that were mentioned after Proposition
5.1
apply.

Proof.

We will show that the kinetic equation (
5.6
) is satisfied for the positive measure
n\in M({\mathbb{R}};L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
induced by

C_{c}({\mathbb{R}})\longrightarrow L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\>\eta\mapsto\eta(w)\psi^{\prime}(w)|\nabla w|^{2}

and that this measure satisfies the norm estimate

(5.8)

\lVert n(\eta)\rVert_{L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\lVert\eta\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}(\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\lVert g\rVert_{L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})})

for all
\eta\in C_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Moreover, we will subsequently derive (
5.7
) from (
5.8
).

Step 1:
For every
S\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
the following equation is satisfied:

(5.9)

\partial_{t}S(w)-\nabla\cdot(S^{\prime}(w)\psi^{\prime}(w)\nabla w)=-S^{\prime\prime}(w)\psi^{\prime}(w)|\nabla w|^{2}+S^{\prime}(w)g.

Indeed, as
S^{\prime}(w)\partial_{t}w=\partial_{t}S(w)
and

\displaystyle S^{\prime}(w)\Delta\psi(w)=S^{\prime}(w)\nabla\cdot(\psi^{\prime}(w)\nabla w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{\prime\prime}(w)\psi^{\prime}(w)|\nabla w|^{2}+S^{\prime}(w)\nabla\cdot(\psi^{\prime}(w)\nabla w)-S^{\prime\prime}(w)\psi^{\prime}(w)|\nabla w|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left(S^{\prime\prime}(w)\nabla w\right)\psi^{\prime}(w)\nabla w+S^{\prime}(w)\nabla\cdot(\psi^{\prime}(w)\nabla w)\right)-S^{\prime\prime}(w)\psi^{\prime}(w)|\nabla w|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left(S^{\prime}(w)\psi^{\prime}(w)\nabla w\right)-S^{\prime\prime}(w)\psi^{\prime}(w)|\nabla w|^{2},

multiplying the equation
\partial_{t}w=\Delta\psi(w)+g
with
S^{\prime}(w)
and subsequently rearranging the terms yields (
5.9
).

Step 2:
We now prove the estimate (
5.8
).
Let
\eta\in C_{c}({\mathbb{R}})^{+}
and note that the function

S(v):=\int_{0}^{v}\int_{0}^{\zeta}\eta(s)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}\zeta

satisfies
S\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
,
S^{\prime\prime}=\eta\geq 0
,
S\geq 0
,
S(0)=0

and
\lVert S^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\lVert\eta\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}
.
Using (
5.9
) and invoking Fubini’s theorem, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R}(0)}n(S^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left(-\partial_{t}S(w)+\nabla\cdot(S^{\prime}(w)\psi^{\prime}(w)\nabla w)+S^{\prime}(w)g\right)

for every
R>0
.
As
S\geq 0
and
S(0)=0
, the first term on the right hand side can be estimated by

\int_{B_{R}(0)}\int_{0}^{T}-\partial_{t}S(w)\leq\int_{B_{R}(0)}S(w_{0})\leq|B_{R}(0)|\lVert S^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

and the third term by

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R}(0)}S^{\prime}(w)g\leq\lVert S^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

By the Gauß-Green theorem we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{B_{R}(0)}\nabla\cdot(S^{\prime}(w)\psi^{\prime}(w)\nabla w)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}S^{\prime}(w)\psi^{\prime}(w)\nabla w\cdot\nu\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lVert S^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\lVert\psi^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}|\nabla w|\mathrm{d}\sigma.

As
\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}|\nabla w|\mathrm{d}\sigma\to 0
for
R\to\infty
by assumption and
n(S^{\prime\prime})\geq 0
by positivity of
n
and
S^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
, by monotone convergence it follows that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}n(S^{\prime\prime})\leq\lVert S^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\left(\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\lVert g\rVert_{L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\right).

Recalling that
S^{\prime\prime}=\eta
and
\lVert S^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\lVert\eta\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}
, we arrive at the desired estimate (
5.8
).

Step 3:
Next, we show that the kinetic formulation (
5.6
) holds.
Fix test functions
\zeta\in\mathscr{D}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Let
S\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
be such that
S(0)=0
and
S^{\prime}=\zeta
and let
G\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
be such that
G(0)=0
and
G^{\prime}=S^{\prime}\psi^{\prime}
.
Then, we have

\nabla\cdot(S^{\prime}(w)\psi^{\prime}(w)\nabla w)=\nabla\cdot(\nabla G(w))=\Delta G(w),

and therefore, (
5.9
) can be rewritten as

(5.10)

\partial_{t}S(w)-\Delta G(w)=-n(S^{\prime\prime})+S^{\prime}(w)g.

Using integration by parts, Fubini’s Theorem and (
5.5
) applied to
S
and
G
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\partial_{t}\ S

\displaystyle(w(t,x))\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}(t,x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\ S(w(t,x))\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}(t,x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\zeta(v)\chi(t,x,v;w)\mathrm{d}v\,\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}(t,x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}}\chi(t,x,v;w)\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)\zeta(v)\,\mathrm{d}(t,x,v),

and

\displaystyle\int_{(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Delta

\displaystyle G(w(t,x))(w(t,x))\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}G(w(t,x))\Delta\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\zeta(v)\psi^{\prime}(v)\chi(t,x,v;w)\mathrm{d}v\,\Delta\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}}\psi^{\prime}(v)\chi(t,x,v;w)\Delta\varphi(t,x)\zeta(v)\,\mathrm{d}(t,x,v),

respectively.
Observing that by the definition of the distributional derivative
n(\zeta^{\prime})=-\partial_{v}n(\zeta)
and combining the above two identities with (
5.10
), we conclude that (
5.6
) holds in the sense of distributions on
(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}
.

Step 4:
Finally, we prove that the measure
n
belongs to
\Lambda^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}};\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
with norm estimate (
5.7
).
Recall that
L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subset\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
isometrically and that, by the Riesz representation theorem (see e.g.
[
Con90
, Theorem C.18]
),

\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})=[C_{0}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})]^{*}.

It follows from
[

, Proposition 2.28]
that

(5.11)

\Lambda^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}};\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))=[L^{1}({\mathbb{R}};C_{0}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))]^{*}.

In combination with (
5.8
), this means that we can extend and view
n
in the natural way as an element of
\Lambda^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}};\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
satisfying the norm estimate (
5.7
).
∎

The next lemma provides a temporal-pointwise

-isometry between space-time functions and their corresponding kinetic functions.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
u,w\in C([0,T];L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
. Then, for each
t\in[0,T]
, the corresponding kinetic functions satisfy

(5.12)

\lVert\chi(t,\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,;u)-\chi(t,\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,;w)\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1})}=\lVert u(t)-w(t)\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

In particular, taking

,

(5.13)

\lVert\chi(t,\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,;u)\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1})}=\lVert u(t)\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Proof.

Using the observation that

\displaystyle|\chi(t,x,v;u)

\displaystyle-\chi(t,x,v;w)|={{\bf 1}}_{\{w(t,x)<v<u(t,x)\}}+{{\bf 1}}_{\{u(t,x)<v<w(t,x)\}}\quad\text{a.e.},

we find that

\displaystyle\lVert\chi(t,\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,;u)-\chi(t,\,\cdot\,,

\displaystyle\,\cdot\,;w)\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{{\bf 1}}_{\{w(t,x)<v<u(t,x)\}}+{{\bf 1}}_{\{u(t,x)<v<w(t,x)\}}\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|u(t,x)-w(t,x)|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\lVert u(t)-w(t)\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

∎

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

Pick
\kappa\in(d-1,d)
and let
(\phi_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,

,

and

Now note that the conditions of Lemma

,

,

,

in the sense of distributions on

, and

\lVert n_{k}\rVert_{\Lambda^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}};\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq\lVert u_{0,k}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

To complete the proof, we will show that (
5.2
) can be obtained as the limit of (

) restricted to
I
for

for a suitable positive measure

,

\mathscr{D}^{\prime}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times I)
after restricting to
I
.

To treat the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.14
), let
\eta\in\mathscr{D}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times I)
.
As
\phi_{k}^{\prime}\to\phi^{\prime}
as
k\to\infty
in
(L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I),\sigma(L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I)),L^{1}_{c}(I))))
,
\chi(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot;u_{k})\to\chi(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot;u)
in
C([0,T];L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}))
and
\Delta_{x}\eta\in L^{1}_{c}(I)
, we have

\displaystyle[\phi_{k}^{\prime}(v)\Delta_{x}\chi(t,x,v;u_{k})](\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times I}\phi_{k}^{\prime}(v)\chi(t,x,v;u_{k})\Delta_{x}\eta(t,x,v)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times I}\phi^{\prime}(v)\chi(t,x,v;u)\Delta_{x}\eta(t,x,v)

\displaystyle=[\phi^{\prime}(v)\Delta_{x}\chi(t,x,v;u)](\eta).

This shows that

\phi^{\prime}(v)\Delta_{x}\chi(t,x,v;u)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\phi_{k}^{\prime}(v)\Delta_{x}\chi(t,x,v;u_{k})\quad\text{in}\ \mathscr{D}^{\prime}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times I).

To treat the third term on the right-hand side of (
5.14
), let again
\eta\in\mathscr{D}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times I)
.
As

u=\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}\quad\text{in}\quad C([0,T];L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\hookrightarrow L^{1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

by restricting to a subsequence we may without loss of generality assume that
u=\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}
pointwise almost everywhere.
By the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem we get that

[(t,x)\mapsto\eta(t,x,u(t,x))]=\lim_{k\to\infty}[(t,x)\mapsto\eta(t,x,u_{k}(t,x))]

in
(L^{\infty}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\sigma(L^{\infty}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}),L^{1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
(i.e. the weak
∗
-topology on
L^{\infty}
as a dual to
L^{1}
).
Since
f=\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{k}
in
L^{1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, it follows that

\displaystyle\delta_{v=u_{k}(t,x)}f_{k}\,(\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\eta(t,x,u_{k}(t,x))f_{k}(t,x)d(t,x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\eta(t,x,u(t,x))f(t,x)d(t,x)

\displaystyle=\delta_{v=u(t,x)}f\,(\eta).

So we also have
\delta_{v=u(t,x)}f=\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{v=u_{k}(t,x)}f_{k}
in
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times I)
.

It remains to treat the second term on the right-hand side of (
5.14
).
By
(
5.15
) and restriction from
{\mathbb{R}}
to
I
, we can view
(n_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
as a bounded sequence in
\Lambda^{\infty}(I;\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
.
In exactly the same way as (
5.11
), we have the dual characterization

\Lambda^{\infty}(I;\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))=[L^{1}(I;C_{0}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))]^{*}.

Since
C_{0}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is separable, it follows from the Banach-Alaoglu theorem (see e.g.
[
HvNVW16
, Theorem B.1.7]
) that
(n_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has a convergent subsequence in
\Lambda^{\infty}(I;\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
with respect to the weak
∗
-topology.
Taking this subsequence, we may without loss of generality assume that
(n_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has a weak
∗
-limit in
\Lambda^{\infty}(I;\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
.
Denoting this limit by
n
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert n\rVert_{\Lambda^{\infty}(I;\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\lVert n_{k}\rVert_{\Lambda^{\infty}(I;\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:prop:mild_is_kinetic;est_measures}}}{{\leq}}\liminf_{k\to\infty}(\lVert u_{0,k}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})})

\displaystyle=\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Furthermore,

n=\lim_{k\to\infty}n_{k}\quad\text{in}\quad\mathscr{D}^{\prime}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times I)

and
n
is positive as every
n_{k}
is positive.
∎

Remark 5.5

We comment on the differences between Theorem

In the latter, porous medium type equations are considered, i.e.

Finally, for the classical porous medium equation it is shown in

Remark 5.6

We make some remarks concerning the assumption (

(i)

For a tempered distribution

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
m\in(1,\infty)
and let
\phi\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\cap W^{2,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I)

\lVert(\overline{\chi}_{l,j})_{(l,j)\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\times{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}_{0,\kappa_{x}}({\mathbb{N}}_{0}\times{\mathbb{N}}_{0};L^{m}_{t,x})}^{m}\lesssim\lVert g\rVert_{\mathscr{M}_{t,x,v}}+\lVert n\rVert_{\Lambda^{\infty}_{v}\mathscr{M}_{t,x}}+\lVert\overline{\chi}\rVert^{m}_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}\cap L^{1}_{t,x}}+\lVert\chi\rVert_{L^{1}_{t,x,v}}.

The statement follows from a slight modification of the proof of

\displaystyle\lVert(\overline{\chi}_{l,j})_{(l,j)\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\times{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}_{\kappa_{t},s_{x}\theta}({\mathbb{N}}_{0}\times{\mathbb{N}}_{0};L^{p}_{t,x})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert g\rVert_{\mathscr{M}_{t,x,v}}+\lVert n\rVert_{\Lambda^{\infty}_{v}\mathscr{M}_{t,x}}+\lVert\overline{\chi}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}\cap L^{1}_{t,x}}+\lVert\chi\rVert_{L^{1}_{t,x,v}}

\displaystyle\>\>+\>\>\lVert(-\Delta_{x})^{s}\int\phi^{\prime}\chi\,\mathrm{d}v\rVert_{L^{q}_{t,x}}+1.

In view of
\kappa_{t}>\sigma_{t}
and
s_{x}\theta>\sigma_{x}
, there is the embedding

\displaystyle\ell^{\infty}_{\kappa_{t},s_{x}\theta}({\mathbb{N}}_{0}\times{\mathbb{N}}_{0};L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\ell^{p}_{\sigma_{t},\sigma_{x}}({\mathbb{N}}_{0}\times{\mathbb{N}}_{0};L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))

\displaystyle=\ell^{p}_{\sigma_{t}}({\mathbb{N}}_{0};L^{p}({\mathbb{R}};\ell^{p}_{\sigma_{x}}({\mathbb{N}}_{0};L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})))).

Observing that

\displaystyle\lVert(\overline{\chi}_{l,j})_{(l,j)\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\times{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\rVert_{\ell^{p}_{\sigma_{t}}({\mathbb{N}}_{0};L^{p}({\mathbb{R}};\ell^{p}_{\sigma_{x}}({\mathbb{N}}_{0};L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))))}

\displaystyle=\lVert\overline{\chi}\rVert_{B^{\sigma_{t}}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}};B^{\sigma_{x}}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}

and recalling that

B^{\sigma_{t}}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}};B^{\sigma_{x}}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))=W^{\sigma_{t},p}({\mathbb{R}};W^{\sigma_{x},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})),

the desired result follows.
∎

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
m\in(1,\infty)
and let
\phi
be as in Lemma
5.7
.
Let
\chi\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times I)
with
\lVert\chi\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}\leq 1
be a distributional solution to (
5.19
) in the sense of distributions on
(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times I
, where
g
and
n
are Radon measures satisfying

g\in\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times I),\qquad n\in\Lambda^{\infty}(I;\mathscr{M}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})).

Let
p\in(1,m]
and define

\kappa_{t}:=\frac{m-p}{p}\frac{1}{m-1},\quad\kappa_{x}:=\frac{p-1}{p}\frac{2}{m-1}.

Let
s\in[0,1]
and
q\in[1,p]
and assume that
(-\Delta_{x})^{s}\int\phi^{\prime}\chi\,\mathrm{d}v\in L^{q}_{t,x}
.
Suppose that
\overline{\chi}=\int\chi\mathrm{d}v\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}\cap L^{1}_{t,x}
.
Then, for all
\sigma_{t}\in[0,\kappa_{t})\cup\{0\}

and
\sigma_{x}\in[0,\kappa_{x})
, we have
\overline{\chi}\in W^{\sigma_{t},p}_{t}W^{\sigma_{x},p}_{x}
with corresponding norm estimate

\displaystyle\lVert\overline{\chi}\rVert_{W^{\sigma_{t},p}_{t}W^{\sigma_{x},p}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert g\rVert_{\mathscr{M}_{t,x,v}}+\lVert n\rVert_{\Lambda^{\infty}_{v}\mathscr{M}_{t,x}}+\lVert\overline{\chi}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}\cap L^{1}_{t,x}}

\displaystyle\>\>+\>\>\lVert\chi\rVert_{C_{t}L^{1}_{x,v}}+\lVert(-\Delta_{x})^{s}\int\phi^{\prime}\chi\,\mathrm{d}v\rVert_{L^{q}_{t,x}}+1.

Proof.

Set
\varphi_{k}(t):=\psi(kt)-\psi(kt-T)
, where
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
with
0\leq\psi\leq 1
,
\text{\rm supp\,}(\psi)\subset(0,\infty)
,
\psi(t)=1
for
t>T
and
\lVert\partial_{t}\psi\rVert_{L^{1}}=1
.
Consider
\chi_{k}:=\varphi_{k}\chi
and view it as a function of time on
{\mathbb{R}}
instead of
[0,T]
through extension by zero.
Then

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(\partial_{t},\nabla_{x},v)\chi_{k}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\chi_{k}-D(v)\Delta_{x}\chi_{k}=\partial_{t}(\varphi_{k}\chi)-D(v)\Delta_{x}(\varphi_{k}\chi)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{k}(\partial_{t}\chi-D(v)\Delta_{x}\chi)+\partial_{t}\varphi_{k}\chi

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:lem:GS23_Lem4.2;kinetic_eq}}}{{=}}\varphi_{k}g+\partial_{v}(\varphi_{k}n)+\varphi_{k}^{\prime}\chi.

Thus,
\chi_{k}
satisfies the corresponding equation on
{\mathbb{R}}
with
g
replaced by
g^{k}=\varphi_{k}g+\varphi_{k}^{\prime}\chi
and
n
replaced by
n^{k}=\varphi_{k}n
.
So we can apply Lemma
5.9
to obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\overline{\chi}_{k}\rVert_{W^{\sigma_{t},p}_{t}W^{\sigma_{x},p}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert g^{k}\rVert_{\mathscr{M}}+\lVert n^{k}\rVert_{\Lambda^{\infty}_{v}\mathscr{M}}+\lVert\overline{\chi}_{k}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}\cap L^{1}_{t,x}}+\lVert\chi_{k}\rVert_{L^{1}_{t,x,v}}+\lVert\varphi_{k}^{\prime}\chi\rVert_{\mathscr{M}}

\displaystyle\>\>+\>\>\lVert(-\Delta_{x})^{s}\int\phi^{\prime}\chi_{k}\,\mathrm{d}v\rVert_{L^{q}_{t,x}}+1,

with

\displaystyle\lVert\varphi_{k}^{\prime}\chi\rVert_{\mathscr{M}}

\displaystyle=\lVert\partial_{t}\varphi_{k}\chi\rVert_{L^{1}_{t,x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert k\psi^{\prime}(k\,\cdot\,)\chi\rVert_{L^{1}_{t,x,v}}+\lVert\psi^{\prime}(k\,\cdot\,-T)\chi\rVert_{L^{1}_{t,x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert k|\psi^{\prime}|(k\,\cdot\,)*_{t}|\chi|\rVert_{L^{1}_{x,v}}+\lVert k|\psi^{\prime}|(k\,\cdot\,-T)*_{t}|\chi|\rVert_{L^{1}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\lVert|\chi|(0,\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)\rVert_{L^{1}_{x,v}}+\lVert|\chi|(T,\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,))\rVert_{L^{1}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lVert\chi\rVert_{C([0,T];L^{1}_{x,v})}.

Taking the limit
k\to\infty
we thus get the desired result.
∎

Proof of Theorem

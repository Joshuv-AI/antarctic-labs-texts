Abstract
A graph is α \alpha -excellent if every vertex of the graph is contained in some maximum independent set of the graph. In this paper, we present two characterizations of the α \alpha -excellent 2 2 -trees.

k
-tree.

Preliminary results

A vertex

if every two vertices belonging to

are adjacent in

of a graph

is the unique simplex of

containing

Proposition 2.1
.

No
\alpha
-excellent graph contains a vertex belonging to at least two its simplexes.

Proof.

Assume that a vertex

of a graph

belongs to two simplicies of
G

G[N_{G}[u]]
and
G[N_{G}[w]]
. If
I
is a maximum independent set that contains

(I\setminus\{v\})\cup\{u,w\}
is an independent set of greater cardinality. Thus,
\alpha(G)\geq|(I\setminus\{v\})\cup\{u,w\}|>|I|
, implying that
v
does not belong to any
\alpha
-set of
G
, and proving that

is not an
\alpha
-excellent graph.
∎

For a positive integer

by a finite number of applications of

[

, Rose proved that a graph

the following conditions are fulfilled:
(i)

(ii)

contains

as a subgraph and does not contain

as a subgraph,

(iii)
if

and

are nonadjacent vertices of

induced by the smallest
v\!-\!u
separator is a complete graph on

and

It was proved in
[
4

that a bipartite graph (and, in particular, a tree) is an
\alpha
-excellent graph if and only if it has a perfect matching. In this paper we are interested in possible extensions of that characterization to a characterization of
\alpha
-excellent
k
-trees for every positive integer

. We begin with the following definition.

A set
{\cal P}
of complete subgraphs of a graph

is said to be a

(k+1)
-cover
of

if each subgraph belonging to
\cal P
is of order
k+1
and every vertex of
G
belongs to exactly one subgraph in
{\cal P}
. It is obvious that for

there exists a one-to-one correspondence between perfect 2-covers of a graph and perfect matchings of the graph. Here we are interested in the existence of perfect
(k+1)
-covers in
k
-trees. First of all, one can prove that every

-tree has at most one perfect
(k+1)
-cover. In the following proposition we present the first relationship between

-trees having perfect
(k+1)
-covers and

-excellent graphs.

Proposition 2.2
.

If a
k
-tree

has a perfect
(k+1)
-cover, then

is an
\alpha
-excellent graph.

Proof.

Let
G
be a connected
k
-tree of order

. Then
G
is a

(k+1)
-partite graph, say
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{k+1}
are partite sets of
G
and
assume that
|A_{1}|\geq|A_{2}|\geq\ldots\geq|A_{k+1}|\geq 1
. In addition, since
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{k+1}
are independent sets of vertices and
|A_{1}|+|A_{2}|+\cdots+|A_{k+1}|=n
, it follows that
|A_{1}|\geq n/(k+1)
, and, therefore,
\alpha(G)\geq|A_{1}|\geq n/(k+1)
. Let
I
be an
\alpha
-set of
G
. Assume now that
{\cal P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{\ell}\}
is a perfect
(k+1)
-cover of
G
. Then,
{\ell}=n/(k+1)
,
|I\cap V({P_{i}})|\leq 1
for each

, and

\alpha(G)=|I|=|I\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}V({P_{i}})|=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|I\cap V({P_{i}})|\leq\ell=\frac{n}{k+1}.

Consequently,
|A_{1}|=|A_{2}|=\cdots=|A_{k+1}|=n/(k+1)=\alpha(G)
, and each of the sets

is an
\alpha
-set of

belongs to an
\alpha
-set of

and, therefore,

is an
\alpha
-excellent graph.
∎

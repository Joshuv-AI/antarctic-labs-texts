1.
Introduction

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve. Serre introduced the following representation,

\rho_{E,n}:\text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\text{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(E[n])\cong\text{GL}_{2}\big(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\big),

where
E[n]
is the set of
n
-torsion points in
E(\mathbb{C}).
Serre’s open image theorem says that, if
E
is without complex multiplication, then there exists a constant
c_{E}>0
such that for any prime
\ell>c_{E},
the associated representation
\rho_{E,\ell}
is surjective. It is conjectured that
c_{E}
is uniformly bounded. For the known bounds on
c_{E},
the reader may refer to
[
2
]
and
[
12
]
. When
E
has complex multiplication, the surjectivity is not true for large primes; see page 12 in
[
1
]
. In general, whether the elliptic curve
E
is without complex multiplication or not, Serre showed that for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
(m,30)=1
,
\rho_{E,m}
is surjective if and only if
\rho_{E,\ell}
is surjective for any prime
\ell\mid m
. For reference, the reader may look at
[
2
]
and
[
10
]
. We shall call this phenomenon
local-global property of Galois representations
.

We first consider a similar problem over arbitrary number fields. Let
K
be a number field, and
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
K.
We now have an associated Galois representation defined by

\rho_{E,n}:\text{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)\longrightarrow\text{Aut}_{\mathbb{C}}(E[n])\cong\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}).

The absolute Galois group
\text{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
is contained in
\text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}),
but since
E
is
not necessarily
defined over
\mathbb{Q},
one can not possibly extend
\rho_{E,n}
to
\text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}).
Following Cojocaru’s arguments, and the ramification theory for cyclotomic fields, it is not hard to show that a similar result holds for any
m
with
(m,30d_{K})=1,
or
(\phi(m),D_{k})=1,
where
d_{K}
and
D_{k}
respectively are the discriminant and degree of
K
over
\mathbb{Q}.
We shall have a detailed discussion on this in Theorem
1
.

Let us call a natural number
m,\textit{bad}
if there exists a finite extension
K_{m}
of
K,
and an elliptic curves
E
over
K_{m}
such that
\text{im}(\rho_{E,m})\neq\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})
but
\text{im}(\rho_{E,\ell})=\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
for any prime
\ell\mid m.
Moreover, we call such a number field as
native
to
m
and such an elliptic curve as
exceptional elliptic curve
for the pair
(m,K_{m}).
This is how we measure the failure of local-global property for Galois representations.

For any arbitrary number
K,
we arrange the elliptic curves over
K
with respect to the usual height
h(E)=||(a,b)||,
where we consider the usual norm in
\mathbb{R}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{K}^{2}.
Here
E
is in the Weierstrass form given by
E_{(a,b)}:y^{2}=x^{3}+ax+b
with
a,b\in\mathcal{O}_{K},
the ring of integers of
K.
Denote
S_{K}(x)=\{(a,b)\in\mathcal{O}^{2}_{K}\mid h(E_{(a,b)})\leq x\}.
It can be shown that
|S_{K}(x)|=c_{K}x^{2D_{K}},
for some constant
c_{K}>0.

Let us now state the main result of this article.

Theorem 1
.

Let
K
be a number field with discriminant
d_{K}
and degree
D_{k}
over
\mathbb{Q}.
Consider
E/K
to be an elliptic curve and
m
be any natural number co-prime to
30.

(a)

The induced Galois representation
\rho_{E,m}
is surjective if and only if
\rho_{E,\ell}
is surjective for any prime
\ell|m,
provided that
K
contains no proper abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q},
or if
m
is co-prime to the discriminant
D_{K}.

(b)

Any integer
m
co-prime to
30,
that is not square-free, is bad. Moreover, for any number field,
K_{m}
native to
m,
almost all the elliptic curves over
K_{m}
are exceptional.

Following the same argument, one can show that for any arbitrary number field
K,\text{im}(\rho_{E,m})
contains
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})
, if and only if
\text{im}(\rho_{E,\ell})
contains
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})
for any prime
\ell|m,
assuming that
(m,30)=1.
In particular, it follows from Proposition 5.2 of
[
11
]
that

Corollary 2
.

Let
K
be any arbitrary number field and
m
be any given integer co-prime to
30.
Then for almost all the elliptic curves
E
over
K,~\mathrm{im}(\rho_{E,m})
contains
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}).

Following the notations introduced by Grant in
[
4
]
, we denote
\mathcal{E}_{m}(X)
to be the number of elliptic curves (up-to isomorphism) over
\mathbb{Q}
of height at most
X
, such that the corresponding representation
\rho_{E,m}
is not surjective. Let
m
be a natural number with
(m,210)=1,
then it follows from Cojocaru’s and Grant’s result that,

\mathcal{E}_{m}(X)=\sum_{\ell|m}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(X)\leq\omega(m)X^{1+\varepsilon},

where
\omega(m)
denotes the number of distinct prime factors of
m.

Zywina later considered a related problem over arbitrary number fields in
[
11
]
. The interesting part comes when we want to make Zywina’s bounds effective. For instance, if we look at Proposition 5.7 in
[
11
]
, it is clear that the absolute constant is given by the number of conjugacy classes of
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})
with determinant
1
. Therefore, the absolute constant is about
\frac{m^{3}}{\phi(m)}.
By Theorem
1
, the constant is of order
\sum_{\ell\mid m}\frac{\ell^{3}}{\phi(\ell)},
for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
(m,30d_{K})=1.
Of course, this constant is better when
m
is not square-free, and the prime factors are with sufficiently large valuations.

Serre introduced a representation associated with the pair of elliptic curves (analogously for the arbitrary tuple as well) as,

\rho_{E_{1}\times E_{2},n}(\sigma)=\left({\rho_{E_{1},n}(\sigma)\atop 0}{0\atop\rho_{E_{2},n}(\sigma)}\right)~\mathrm{for}~\mathrm{all}~\sigma\in\text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}).

In this case, Serre showed an analog of his open image theorem. To be more precise, Serre showed that
\text{im}(\rho_{E_{1}\times E_{2},\ell})=\Delta(\ell)

for all but finitely many primes
\ell
, where the diagonal sub-group
\Delta(\ell)
is given by,

\Delta(\ell)=\left\{\left({g_{1}\atop 0}{0\atop g_{2}}\right)\mid\text{det}(g_{1})=\text{det}(g_{2}),\big(g_{1},g_{2}\big)\in\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\times\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\right\}.

Jones in
[
6
]
considered this topic and proved an asymptotic estimate analogous to Grant’s main result in
[
4
]
. Grant’s work was based on counting rational points on certain modular curves. Jones’s approach was by studying the distribution of Frobenius symbols using the multi-dimensional version of Gallagher’s large sieve.

.

Let
K
be a number field with discriminant

and degree

over
\mathbb{Q}.
Consider

The induced Galois representation

\rho_{E,\ell}
has image
\Delta(\ell)
for any prime
\ell|m,
provided that

\mathbb{Q},
or if

is co-prime to the discriminant

In Section

, we shall discuss a modular analog of the local-global phenomenon. We shall see that Theorem

corresponds to newforms, and Theorem

We first generalize the same over number fields. Jones applied his main result to the cases of the pair of elliptic curves and polarized abelian varieties. Taking inspiration from the same, we discuss a further generalization in section

2.
Background on finite groups

This section discusses the main group theoretic tools required to prove the main results of this article. Although there may be many resources available on this, it serves the reader a quick introduction to the subject.

To begin with, let
\mathrm{G}
be a finite group. Denote
\text{Occ}(\mathrm{G})
to be the isomorphism classes of non-abelian simple groups coming as the quotient of composite factors of
\mathrm{G}
. First, we have the following immediate facts.

Lemma 5
.

For any finite group
\mathrm{G}
,

(1)

\mathrm{Occ}(\mathrm{G})=\phi
if and only if
\mathrm{G}
is solvable.

(2)

If
\mathrm{H}
is normal in
G
, then
\mathrm{Occ}(\mathrm{G})=\mathrm{Occ}(\mathrm{H})\bigcup\mathrm{Occ}(\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{H})

As a consequence, we have

Lemma 6
.

For any integer

\mathrm{Occ}(\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}))=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,\mathrm{prime}\\
\ell\mid m\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Occ}(\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})).

2.2.
Generalizations to the Symplectic groups

Let us start by recalling the standard definition of Symplectic groups. Consider the groups,

\mathrm{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})=\Big\{\mathrm{g}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})\mid\mathrm{g}\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\
-I&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{g}^{t}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\lambda\\
-\lambda&0\\
\end{pmatrix},\lambda\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}\Big\}

and

\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})=\Big\{\mathrm{g}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})\mid\mathrm{g}\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\
-I&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{g}^{t}=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\
-I&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\Big\}.

We then have the following fact when we want to compute Occ.

Lemma 10
.

For any integer
m
, we have the following.

\mathrm{Occ}\Big(\mathrm{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})\Big)=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell~\mathrm{prime}\\
\ell\mid m\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Occ}\Big(\mathrm{PSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\Big).

Proof.

Since
\text{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})/\text{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})
is abelian,

\text{Occ}\Big(\mathrm{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})\Big)=\text{Occ}\Big(\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})\Big)=\bigcup_{\ell^{r}||m}\text{Occ}\Big(\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{r}\mathbb{Z})\Big).

The last equality above follows from
(2)
of Lemma
5
. On the other hand,

\mathrm{Occ}\Big(\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{r}\mathbb{Z})\Big)=\mathrm{Occ}\Big(\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\Big)=\mathrm{Occ}\Big(\mathrm{PSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\Big)

since kernel of the projection map
\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{r}\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}),
is a
\ell
-group, hence solvable, and
Occ
of the solvable part is empty.

Lemma 12

For any

\mathrm{comm}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}))=\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}).

Let us first note that
\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})=\prod_{\ell^{r}||m}\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{r}\mathbb{Z}).

\mathrm{comm}(\mathrm{PSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}))=\mathrm{PSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}).

We now need to show if

is a subgroup of
A(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}),
it follows immediately from
(1)
that

\text{pr}_{\ell}(\mathrm{comm}(G^{(\ell)}))=\mathrm{comm}(A(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})),

where
G^{(\ell)}=\text{ker}\circ\pi_{m/\ell^{r}}(G).
It now follows from Lemma
16
that
\mathrm{comm}(G^{(\ell)})=\mathrm{comm}(A(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{r}\mathbb{Z}),
because
\mathrm{comm}(A(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}))
is contained in
\text{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}).
In particular, we have

\mathrm{A(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})}\subseteq\mathrm{G}\subseteq A(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}).

Condition
(1)
and
(2)
together now shows
\mathrm{G}=A(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}).

Note that we are putting an extra local condition at
(2)
, and that is not exactly our question was originally about. From the arguments presented in the previous section, we see that
(2)
is actually true for the cases related to abelian varieties. In general we do not necessarily have that luxury because, once we have
A(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
is a quotient of
\mathrm{G},
we perhaps will not get a simple non-abelian quotient of
A(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
. So to avoid
(2),
we might try to put the condition that

(4)

\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})\subseteq A(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}),

for some
g\leq n/2,
say. This condition implies that
\text{PSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\in\text{Occ}\Big(G^{(\ell)}\Big).
Now arguing similarly as the proof of Theorem 1.3, we have

\text{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})\subseteq\mathrm{G}\subseteq A(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}).

In particular,

|\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{comm}(\mathrm{G})|\leq|\text{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})/|\text{Sp}_{2k}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})|=O(m^{6(g-k)}\phi(m)).

Summarizing the discussion, we see that we can remove
(2)
at the cost of two more conditions. One of them is
(\ref{eqn:cond1}),
and the other one is if we assume that
|\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{comm}(\mathrm{G})|
is sufficiently larger than
m^{6(g-k)}\phi(m).

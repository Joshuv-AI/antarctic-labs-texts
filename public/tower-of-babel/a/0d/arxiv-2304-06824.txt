Even and odd self-similar solutions of the diffusion equation for infinite horizon
L. Mátyás, I.F. Barna

Abstract
. In the description of transport phenomena an important aspect represents the diffusion. In certain cases the diffusion may appear together with convection. In this paper we study the diffusion equation with the self similar Ansatz. With an appropriate change of variables we found original solutions of diffusion equation for infinite horizon. Here we present the even solutions of diffusion equation for the boundary conditions presented. For completeness the odd solutions are also mentioned as well, as part of the previous works. Finally, the diffusion equation with constant source term is discussed, which also has even and odd solutions, too.

Having in mind that diffusion can be a general, three dimensional process beyond Cartesian symmetry here we investigate the one dimensional diffusion equation. The change in time of variable
C(x,t)
is influenced by the presence of it in the neighbors:

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial C(x,t)}{\partial t}=D\frac{\partial^{2}C(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}},

where

C(x,t)
is a sufficiently smooth function together with existing derivatives, regarding both variables.

In this general form one may observe that if

is a solution, then

C(x,t)+C_{0}
is also solution, where
C_{0}
is a constant.

For finite horizon or interval, in case the concentration is fixed at the two ends

and
C(x=L,t)=C_{0}
the solutions are

(2)

C_{k}(x,t)=C_{0}+e^{-D\frac{\pi^{2}k^{2}t}{L^{2}}}\cdot\sin\left(\frac{k\pi}{L}x\right),

k=1,2,3...
, it can be any positive integer number.
In general, beyond
C_{0}

product of the exponent and sine for different

For finite horizon, in the case when the density is fixed to zero on both ends
the solutions are changed to

(3)

C_{k}(x,t)=C_{0}+e^{-D\frac{\pi^{2}n^{2}t}{L^{2}}}\cdot\cos\left(\frac{n\pi}{L}x\right),

where
n=1,2,3...
, can be any positive integer number.
Thanks to the Fourier theorem, with the help of Eq. (
2

3

These are well-known analytic results and can be found in any usual physics textbooks like

3.
The properties of the shape functions and solutions

In the following we study the some properties of the shape functions

f(\eta)
and of the complete solutions
C(x,t)
.
First we consider the
L^{1}
integral norms.

For the case
\alpha=1/2
the form of

(42)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(\eta)d\eta=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{0}e^{-\frac{\eta^{2}}{4D}}d\eta=f_{0}\,2\sqrt{\pi D}.

The constant
f_{0}
is chosen, depending on the problem. If
C
stands for the density which diffuses,

f_{0}
in the above integral is related to the total mass of the system.

Correspondingly

(43)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}C(x,t)dx=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4Dt}}dx=f_{0}\,2\sqrt{\pi D}.

For the case
\alpha=3/2

(44)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(\eta)d\eta=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{0}\cdot e^{-\frac{\eta^{2}}{4D}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2D}\eta^{2}\right)d\eta=0.

It is interesting to see that the integral of first even shape function beyond Gaussian is zero.
An even more remarkable feature is however, that

(45)

\int_{-\infty}^{0}f(\eta)d\eta=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(\eta)d\eta=0.

So the oscillations, the positions of the zero transitions divide the
function in such a way that the integral not only the whole real axis
(-\infty...\infty)
but on the half axis
(0..\infty)
or
(-\infty..0
) gives zero as well.

Evaluating the same type of integrals for the
corresponding solution
C(x,t)
we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}C(x,t)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}C(x,t)dx=\int_{-\infty}^{0}C(x,t)dx=\\
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{0}\cdot\frac{1}{t^{3/2}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4Dt}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2D}\frac{x^{2}}{t}\right)dx=0,

at any time point, (and for any diffusion coefficient D).

The same property is true for all possible higher harmonic solutions if
\alpha
is positive half-integer number

\alpha=(2n+1)/2
when
(n\>\epsilon\>\mathbb{N}
).
This property has far-reaching consequences.
The linearity of the regular diffusion equation and
this additional property of this even series of solutions makes it possible to perturb the usual Gaussian in such a way, that the
total number of particles are conserved during the diffusion process,
however the initial distribution can be changed significantly.
One can see from the final form of the solutions

C(x,t)_{\alpha}\sim\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}
that the decay of these perturbations are however short-lived because they have a quicker decay than the standard Gaussian solutions.
For completeness we present a
C(x,t)
solutions which is a linear combination
of the first two even solutions
\alpha=1/2,3/2
in the form of

(46)

C(x,t)=\frac{60}{t^{\frac{1}{2}}}e^{-\frac{-x^{2}}{4t}}-\frac{0.001}{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{-\frac{-x^{2}}{4t}}\left(1-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}\right),

on Fig. (
7
).
Note, that coefficients with different orders of magnitude had to be applied to reach a visible effect when the sum of two functions have to be visualised with different power-law decay.

As a second property we investigate the cosine Fourier transform of the shape functions:

(47)

C_{\alpha}(k)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}Cos(k\cdot\eta)f_{\alpha}(\eta)d\eta.

In can be shown with direct integration, that the Fourier
transform is

(48)

C_{\alpha=\frac{2N+1}{2}}(k)\propto l\cdot\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\frac{k^{2N}\cdot D^{N}\cdot e^{-k^{2}D}}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{D}}},

for all

positive integer and l is a real constant.
This means, that qualitatively the spectra for all positive half integer
\alpha
are similar.
They start from zero, have a global positive maximum and and a quick decay to zero.
It is generally known from spectral analysis that pulses of finite length have
band spectra which have a minimal a maximal and a central frequency.

4.
The diffusion equation with constant source

At this point we try to find solutions of the
diffusion equation, mainly with the self similar Ansatz, where on the right hand side, there is a constant source term.

(49)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial C(x,t)}{\partial t}=D\frac{\partial^{2}C(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}}+n,

For this equation one also apply the self-similar transformation (
5
), and we get a
modified equation relative to the homogeneous one

(50)

-\alpha t^{-\alpha-1}f(\eta)-\beta t^{-\alpha-1}\eta\frac{df(\eta)}{d\eta}=Dt^{-\alpha-2\beta}\frac{d^{2}f(\eta)}{d\eta^{2}}+n.

The free term on the r.h.s. has no explicit time decay, consequently we expect the same from the other terms, which means

(51)

\displaystyle-\alpha-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

(52)

\displaystyle-\alpha-2\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

The two equations have to be fulfilled simultaneously. Solving these equations, we get the following values for
\alpha
and
\beta
:

(53)

\alpha=-1\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,\beta=\frac{1}{2}

Inserting these values to the equation
(
50
), we get the following ODE

(54)

f(\eta)-\frac{1}{2}\eta\frac{df(\eta)}{d\eta}=D\frac{d^{2}f(\eta)}{d\eta^{2}}+n.

We emphasize, that we arrived to this equation by a
self-similar transformation.
At this point we observe, that if we shift the function
f
by a constant, and introduce the function
h
:

(55)

h(\eta)=f(\eta)-n

we arrive to a slightly modified equation

(56)

h(\eta)-\frac{1}{2}\eta\frac{dh(\eta)}{d\eta}=D\frac{d^{2}h(\eta)}{d\eta^{2}}.

One may observe, that if the transformation

\eta\rightarrow-\eta
and
h(-\eta)=h(\eta)

is applied, the equation still remains the same,
consequently we expect at least one even solution.

If we look for the even solution by polynomial expansion

(57)

h(\eta)=A+B\eta^{2}+...

then we get by direct substitution

(58)

A=2\cdot B\cdot D.

5.
Summary and Outlook

Applying the well-known self-similar Ansatz - together with and additional
change of variables - we derived symmetric solutions for the one dimensional diffusion equations. Using the Fourier series analogy we might say that these solutions may be considered as possible higher harmonics of the fundamental Gaussian solution.
As unusual properties we found that the integral of these solutions give zero on both
the half and the whole real axis as well. Thanks to the linearity of the diffusion equation these kind of functions can be added to the
particle (or energy) conserving fundamental Gaussian solution therefore
new kind of particle diffusion processes can be described. Due to the higher
\alpha

self-similar exponents these kind of solutions give relevant contributions only at smaller time coordinates, because the corresponding solutions decay quicker than the usual Gaussian solution.

These kind of solutions can be also evaluated for two or three dimensional, cylindrical or spherical symmetric systems as well.
Work is in progress to apply this kind of analysis to more sophisticated diffusion systems as well.
We hope that our new solutions have far reaching consequences and they
will be successfully applied in other scientific disciplines like quantum mechanics, quantum field theory in physics, in probability theory or in financial mathematics in the near future.

A polynomial composites
Łukasz Matysiak

Abstract
Polynomial composites were introduced by Anderson, Anderson, and Zafrullah. In this paper we study many different algebraic properties of polynomial composites like ACCP, atomic, SR property. We study relationships between Noetherian polynomial composites certain field extensions.



Keywords: domain, field, irreducible element, polynomial.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
A
,
B
be fields such that
A\subset B
. Let
X
be an indeterminate over

(1)

every nonzero prime ideal of

is maximal;

(2)

every prime ideal
P
different from
XB[X]
is principal;

(3)

T
is atomic.

Proof.

(1) First note that
XB[X]
is maximal since
T/XB[X]\cong K
. Let
P
be a nonzero prime ideal of
T
. Now
X\in P
implies
(XB[X])^{2}\subseteq P
and hence
XB[X]\subseteq P
so
P=XB[X]
. So suppose that
X\notin P
. Then for
N=\{1,X,X^{2},\dots\}
,
P_{N}
is a prime ideal in the PID

. (In fact,
T_{P}\supseteq B[X,X^{-1}]
is a DVR (discrete valuation ring).) So
P
is minimal and is also maximal unless
P\subsetneq XB[X]
. But let

k_{n}\neq 0
, where

for every

X^{n+1}+k_{n}^{-1}k_{n+1}X^{n+2}+\dots+k_{n}^{-1}k_{s}X^{s}\in P
, so

implies that
1+k_{n}^{-1}k_{n+1}X+\dots+k_{n}^{-1}k_{s}X^{s-n}\in P

(2) If
P
is different from
XB[X]
, then it contains an element of the form
1+Xf(X)
, where
f(X)\in B[X]
. Now if

can be factored in

(1+Xg(X))(1+Xh(X))
. Hence

is irreducible in

if and only if it is irreducible in

.

Now let

be irreducible in

and suppose that

in
T
. Then

in

, and so in

we have, say

. Then, in

,

. Now

can be written as

, while if

and

. In either case,

and so

in

. Consequently, in

is prime.

Now since every element of the form

is a product of irreducible elements of the same form and hence is a product of prime elements, it follows that every prime ideal of

different from
XB[X]
contains a principal prime and hence is actually principal.

(3) Thus a general element of

can be written as
aX^{r}(1+Xf(X))
, where

(with

if

) and

is a product of primes.
∎

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

(
\Rightarrow
)

(
\Leftarrow

5
An SR property in polynomial composites

Recall from
[
24
]
a monoid
H
is called
SR
-monoid, if
\Sqf H=\Gpr H
.
We present facts based on monoids. They can be easily transferred to integral domains.

Proposition 5.1
.

If
H
is an
AP
-monoid, then
R
is an
SR
-monoid.

Proof.

Let
s\in\Sqf R
. Then
s=q_{1}\dots q_{n}
, where
q_{1}
,
\dots
,
q_{n}\in\Irr R
,
s_{i}\mbox{\small\rm rpr}s_{j}
. Since
R
is
AP
-monoid, then all
q_{i}\in\Prime R
and since
s=q_{1}\dots q_{n}
and
q_{i}\mbox{\small\rm rpr}q_{j}
then
s\in\Gpr H
.
∎

The opposite fact generally does not hold.

Example 5.2
.

Let
H
be a non-group monoid such that every element of
H
is a square. For example
\mathbb{Q}_{\geqslant 0}
. Then

\Sqf H=\Gpr H=H^{\ast}
, so
H
is an SR-monoid. But since
\Irr H\subset\Sqf H
, then
\Irr H
must be empty. Similarly
\Prime H
is also empty. There is not an
AP
-property. So
H
is
SR
-monoid, non-
AP
-monoid.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
R
be a factorial monoid. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

H
is an
AP
-monoid,

(b)

H
is an
SR
-monoid.

Proof.

From Proposition
5.1
we have (a)
\Rightarrow
(b). So it sufficient to show that every square-free is radical. Let
s\in\Sqf H
and
s\mid x^{m}
for all
x\in H
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{>1}
. Since
H
is factorial, then
s=q_{1}\dots q_{n}
, with
q_{i}\in\Irr H=\Prime H
and
q_{i}\mbox{\small\rm rpr}q_{j}
and
x=p_{1}\dots p_{r}
, with
p_{i}\in\Irr H=\Prime H
. Then
q_{1}\dots q_{n}\mid(p_{1}\dots p_{r})^{m}
.

We see that for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
we have
q_{i}\mid(p_{1}\dots p_{r})^{m}
. Since
q_{i}\in\Prime H\subset\Gpr H
, then
q_{i}\mid p_{1}\dots p_{r}
, i.e.
q_{i}\mid x
. Thus
\lcm(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})\mid x
and
q_{i}\mbox{\small\rm rpr}q_{j}
, then
s\mid x
.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

H
is factorial if and only if

is atomic and

is
SR
-monoid.

The

-property holds in pre-Schreier monoids,

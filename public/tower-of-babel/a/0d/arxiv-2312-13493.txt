Abstract
. For the Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum enveloping algebra U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n + 1 ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}) , we show that the span of Lusztig’s positive root vectors, with respect to Littlemann’s nice reduced decompositions of the longest element of the Weyl group S n + 1 S_{n+1} , form quantum tangent spaces for the full quantum flag manifold 𝒪 q ​ ( F n + 1 ) \mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1}) . The associated differential calculi are direct q q -deformations of the anti-holomorphic Dolbeault complex of the classical full flag manifold F n + 1 \mathrm{F}_{n+1} . As an application we establish a quantum Borel–Weil theorem for 𝒪 q ​ ( F n + 1 ) \mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1}) , giving a noncommutative differential geometric realisation of all the finite-dimensional type- 1 1 irreducible representations of U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n + 1 ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}) . Restricting this differential calculus to the quantum Grassmannians is shown to reproduce the celebrated Heckenberger–Kolb anti-holomorphic Dolbeault complex. Lusztig’s positive root vectors for non-nice decompositions of the longest element of S n + 1 S_{n+1} are examined for low orders, and are exhibited to either not give tangents spaces, or to produce differential calculi of non-classical dimension.



1.
Introduction

The Heckenberger–Kolb differential calculi for the irreducible quantum flag manifolds are among the most important and well-studied structures in the noncommutative geometry of quantum groups. They are an essentially unique covariant
q
-deformation of the de Rham complex of the classical flag manifolds of Hermitian symmetric type, a striking phenomemon given the many difficulties in formulating a comprehensive theory of noncommutative geometry for Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum groups. Moreover, as has become increasingly clear in recent years, these calculi come with an extremely rich
q
-deformed Kähler geometry. They admit direct noncommutative generalisations of Lefschetz decomposition and the Kähler identities
[
33
,
31
]
, of the Borel–Weil theorem
[
6
,
5
]
, and Kodaira vanishing
[
34
]
. This has allowed for the construction of Dolbeault–Dirac Fredholm operators from noncommutative complex geometric arguments
[
10
]
, and for the construction of spectral triples, in the sense of Connes, for special cases such as quantum projective space
[
11
]
and the first odd quantum quadric
[
13
]
.

It is natural to ask is what happens outside the irreducible setting, which is to say, when the classical flag manifold is no longer of Hermitian symmetric type. In this more general setting, quantum flag manifolds exhibit a richer and more challenging structure. Even in the classical setting, the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic tangent spaces of a non-irreducible flag manifold are no longer irreducible as modules over the corresponding Levi subalgebra. For example, the space of one forms of the full flag manifold decomposes into a direct sum of line bundles. Moreover, in the quantum setting, the dual coalgebras of the non-irreducible quantum flag manifolds contain far fewer twisted primitive elements. Even though the Heckenberger–Kolb calculi appeared over twenty years ago, up until now, the question of noncommutative differential structures for non-irreducible examples has received very little attention. There are, to our knowledge, just two studies: First is the quantum BGG sequence of Heckenberger and Kolb
[
22
]
. Second is the work of Yuncken and Voigt
[
48
]
on the noncommutative geometry of the full quantum flag manifold of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{3})
, which resulted in a proof of the quantum Baum–Connes conjecture for the discrete quantum dual of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{3})
.

Here we adopt an approach to this problem based on Lusztig’s celebrated root vectors. Classically the tangent space of the full flag manifold is isomorphic to the nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
. Formulating a quantum analogue of the inclusion
\mathfrak{g}\hookrightarrow U(\mathfrak{g})
is a subtle question, see for example the approach of Majid
[
29
]
. In
[
28
]
, Lusztig showed that the embedding of the nilpotent subalgebra
\mathfrak{n}\hookrightarrow U(\mathfrak{g})
has a direct quantum analogue for each choice of reduced decomposition of the longest element of the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
. These quantum root vectors were originally introduced to prove a quantum generalisation of the classical Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem, and would later find many striking applications, most notably the theory of canonical bases
[
28
]
. Our approach to extending the anti-holomorphic Heckenberger–Kolb construction starts by taking the span of Lusztig’s positive root vectors as a tangent space for the full quantum flag, and then constructing a differential calculus from it. This allows us to make novel connections between the structural theory of Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum groups and their noncommutative differential geometry.

In this paper we focus on the
A
-series Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum groups, and the reduced decomposition
\mathbf{j}
given by

(1)

\displaystyle w_{0}=(s_{n}s_{n-1}\cdots s_{1})(s_{n}s_{n-1}\cdots s_{2})\cdots(s_{n}s_{n-1})s_{n}.

This decomposition is one of the two nice decompositions in the sense of Littlemann
[
26
, §5]
. We show that the associated space of positive Lusztig root vectors forms a quantum tangent space for the full quantum flag manifold. The associated covariant differential calculus is a direct
q
-deformation of the anti-holomorphic subcomplex of the classical
A
-series full quantum flag manifold, in particular it has classical dimension. Its quantum exterior algebra admits a concise generator and relation presentation in terms of the
A
-series root system. The quantum exterior algebra is moreover a Frobenius algebra. Notably, its Nakayama automorphism is
not

q
-deformed. Restricting this differential calculus to the partial quantum flag manifolds then gives a differential calculus for each of them. In particular, for the quantum Grassmannians, we recover the anti-holomorphic subcomplex of the Heckenberger–Kolb differential calculus.

In order to explore and test our new construction, we prove a direct
q
-deformation of the Borel–Weil theorem for the full flag manifold. This gives noncommutative geometric realisations of all the type-
1
finite-dimensional representations of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
, extending previous work for the irreducible quantum flag case
[
6
,
5
]
. The proof requires the introduction of a quantum principal bundle presentation, in the sense of Majid and Brzezinski, of our differential calculus. Moreover, it presents the relative line modules over
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
as noncommutative holomorphic line modules in the sense of Beggs and Majid
[
1
, §7]
.

For low-dimensional cases, we also examine the behaviour of the positive Lusztig roots vectors associated to the other reduced decompositions. We see that for
A_{3}
, all
8
commutation classes of reduced decompositions give tangent spaces. However, apart from the decompositon dual to (
1
) (that is the decomposition given by the action of the opposite automorphism of the
A
-series Dynkin diagram on
\mathbf{j}
) the associated differential calculi have non-classical dimension. Moreover, for the case of
A_{4}
, there are reduced decompositions that are not even tangent spaces. This motivates the conjecture that for the
A
-series, Littlemann’s nice reduced decompositions are the only decompositions with a well-behaved noncommutative geometry.

This work leads naturally to a number of future topics. First is the question of a
q
-deformation
of the entire Dolbeault double complex, along with its classical complex geometry. This will be addressed in subsequent work using Lusztig’s negative root vectors
[
8
]
. Subsequently, the
B,C
, and
D
series will be treated following the same program. Ultimately, it is hoped that this will show the way to an understanding of the noncommutative geometry of Drinfeld–Jimbo coordinate algebras themselves.

1.1.
Summary of the Paper

The paper is organised as follows: In §2 we recall some basic material about covariant differential calculi over Hopf algebras and their associated tangent spaces, as well as the maximal prolongation of covariant first-order differential calculi.

In §3 we treat the space of positive root vectors associated to the reduced decomposition of the longest element of the Weyl group given in (
1
), and show that their span forms a quantum tangent space for
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
. We then determine the module structure of the dual cotangent space, and give a full set of relations for the maximal prolongation of the associated differential calculus
\Omega^{(0,\bullet)}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
. We also present a number of instructive low-dimensional examples. We then examine the algebraic properties of the maximal prolongation, and show that it has classical dimension by examining the associated graded algebra of a canonical filtration on the space of left-coinvariant forms
\Lambda^{(0,\bullet)}_{q}
. We also show that the graded algebra
\Lambda^{(0,\bullet)}_{q}
is a Frobenius algebra and show that the associated Nakayama automorphism is of classical type.

In §4 we begin by recalling some basic results on quantum homogeneous spaces and their tangent spaces. We then examine the restriction of
\Omega^{(0,\bullet)}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
to the quantum Grassmannians, showing that we recover the anti-holomorphic Heckenberger–Kolb differential calculus.

In §5 we examine the restriction of
\Omega^{(0,\bullet)}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
to the full quantum flag manifold
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
. We show that this differential calculus has classical dimension, and present it as a direct sum of line modules over
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
. Notably, we show that its right module structure is more complicated than that of the Heckenberger–Kolb calculus. In particular, we show the differential calculus is not contained the subcategory of relative Hopf modules to which the simple monoidal version of Takeuchi’s categorical equivalence applies.

In §6 we prove a direct
q
-deformation of the Borel–Weil theorem for the classial full flag manifold, giving noncommutative geometric realisations of all the type-
1
finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
.

In §7, for the special cases of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4})
and
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5})
, we examine the situation for reduced decompositions other than Littlemann’s nice decompositions. In all cases examined, we see that the space of root vectors either do not give a tangent space, or the associated differential calculus does not have classical dimension. Motivated by this, we make some conjectures about the general
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
situation.

We finish with two appendices. In the first we present necessary details on Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum groups and Lusztig’s root vectors. In the second we make some comments about filtered algebras and their associated graded algebras.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall some basic material about covariant differential calculi over Hopf algebras and their associated tangent spaces. We use Sweedler notation, denote by
\Delta,\varepsilon
, and
S
the coproduct, counit, and antipode of a Hopf algebra respectively. We write
A^{\circ}
for the dual coalgebra (Hopf algebra) of a (Hopf) algebra
A
, and denote the pairing between
A
and
A^{\circ}
by angular brackets. Throughout the paper, all algebras are over
\mathbb{C}
and assumed to be unital, all unadorned tensor products are over
\mathbb{C}
, and all Hopf algebras are assumed to have bijective antipodes.

2.1.
Covariant Differential Calculi over Hopf algebras

A
differential calculus
, or a
dc
, is a differential graded algebra (dga)

\Big(\Omega^{\bullet}\cong\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\Omega^{k},\mathrm{d}\Big)

which is generated as an algebra by the elements
a,\mathrm{d}b
, for
a,b\in\Omega^{0}
. When no confusion arises, we denote the dc by
\Omega^{\bullet}
, omitting the exterior derivative
\mathrm{d}
. We denote the degree of a homogeneous element
\omega\in\Omega^{\bullet}
by
|\omega|
. For a given algebra
B
, a differential calculus
over

B
is a differential calculus such that
B=\Omega^{0}
. A differential calculus is said to be of
total degree

m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
if
\Omega^{m}\neq 0
, and
\Omega^{k}=0
, for all
k>m
.

A
first-order differential calculus
(fodc) over an algebra
B
is a pair
(\Omega^{1}(B),\mathrm{d})
, where
\Omega^{1}(B)
is a
B
-bimodule and
\mathrm{d}:B\to\Omega^{1}(B)
is a derivation such that
\Omega^{1}
is generated as a left (or equivalently right)
B
-module by those elements of the form
\mathrm{d}b
, for
b\in B
. We say that a differential calculus
(\Gamma^{\bullet},\mathrm{d}_{\Gamma})

extends
a first-order calculus
(\Omega^{1},\mathrm{d}_{\Omega})
if there exists a bimodule isomorphism
\varphi:\Omega^{1}\to\Gamma^{1}
such that
\mathrm{d}_{\Gamma}=\varphi\circ\mathrm{d}_{\Omega}
. It can be shown
[
32
, §2.5]
that any first-order calculus admits an extension
\Omega^{\bullet}
which is
maximal
, in the sense that there exists a unique differential map from
\Omega^{\bullet}
onto any other extension of
\Omega^{1}
. We call this extension the
maximal prolongation
of the first-order calculus.

For
A
a Hopf algebra, a dc
\Omega^{\bullet}
over a left
A
-comodule algebra
(P,\Delta_{L})
is said to be
left covariant
if the coaction
\Delta_{L}:P\to A\otimes P
extends to a (necessarily unique) coaction
\Delta_{L}:\Omega^{\bullet}\to A\otimes\Omega^{\bullet}
, giving
\Omega^{\bullet}
the structure of a left
A
-comodule algebra, such that
\mathrm{d}
is a left
A
-comodule map. A right covariant dc over a right
A
-comodule algebra is defined similarly.

2.2.
Tangent Spaces and Two-Sided Hopf Modules

A
tangent space
for a Hopf algebra
A
is a linear subspace
T
of the dual Hopf algebra
A^{\circ}
such that
X(1)=0
, for all
X\in T
, and
T\oplus\mathbb{C}1
is a right coideal of
A^{\circ}
. For any tangent space
T
, a right
A
-ideal of
A^{+}:=\mathrm{ker}(\varepsilon)
is given by

(2)

\displaystyle I:=\Big\{x\in A^{+}\,|\,X(x)=0,\textrm{ for all }X\in T\Big\},

meaning that the quotient
\Lambda^{1}(A):=A^{+}/I
is naturally an object in the category
\mathrm{Mod}_{A}
of right
A
-modules. We call
\Lambda^{1}(A)
the
cotangent space
of the fodc.

Consider next the category
{}^{A}_{A}\mathrm{Mod}_{A}
: This has as objects left
A
-comodules
{\mathcal{F}}
, endowed with an
A
-bimodule structure, such that the comodule structure map is an
A
-bimodule map with respect to the diagonal
A
-bimodule structure on
A\otimes{\mathcal{F}}
. Its morphisms are left
A
-comodule,
A
-bimodule, maps. We have a functor

\displaystyle A\otimes-:\mathrm{Mod}_{A}\to{}^{A}_{A}\mathrm{Mod}_{A},

\displaystyle V\mapsto A\otimes V,

where the left
A
-module, left
A
-comodule, structure of
A\otimes V
is given by the first tensor factor, and the right
A
-module structure is given by the diagonal action. In the other direction, we have a functor

\displaystyle F:{}^{A}_{A}\mathrm{Mod}_{A}\to\mathrm{Mod}_{A},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}\mapsto{\mathcal{F}}/A^{+}{\mathcal{F}}.

This determines an adjoint equivalence between the two categories, with unit and counit

\displaystyle\mathrm{U}:{\mathcal{M}}\to A\otimes F({\mathcal{M}}),

\displaystyle g\mapsto g_{(1)}\otimes[g_{(0)}],

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}:F(A\otimes V)\to V,

\displaystyle a\otimes v\mapsto\varepsilon(a)v.

This equivalence is known as the
fundamental theorem of two-sided Hopf modules
, see
[
40
, Theorem 5.7]
, or
[
45
, §4.1]
, for more details. The category
{}^{A}_{A}\mathrm{Mod}_{A}
has a monoidal structure given by the usual tensor products of
A
-bimodules and left
A
-comodules. Moreover,
\mathrm{Mod}_{A}
has a monoidal structure given by the tensor product of vector spaces endowed with the diagonal
A
-module structure. With respect to these two monoidal structures, the natural transformation with components

(3)

\displaystyle\mu:F({\mathcal{M}})\otimes F({\mathcal{N}})\to F({\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{A}{\mathcal{N}}),

\displaystyle[m]\otimes[n]\mapsto[mS(n_{(-1)})\otimes n_{(0)}].

gives
F
the structure of a monoidal functor. Note that the inverse of
\mu
is given by

\displaystyle\mu^{-1}:F({\mathcal{M}}\otimes{\mathcal{N}})\to F({\mathcal{M}})\otimes_{A}F({\mathcal{N}}),

\displaystyle[m\otimes n]\mapsto[mn_{(-1)}]\otimes[n_{(0)}]

Consider now the object

\displaystyle{}^{A}_{A}\mathrm{Mod}_{A}\ni\Omega^{1}(A):=A\otimes\Lambda^{1}(A).

If
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a basis for
T
, and
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is the dual basis of
\Lambda^{1}(A)
, then the map

(4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}:A\to\Omega^{1}(A),

\displaystyle a\mapsto[a_{(1)}]\otimes[a_{(2)}^{+}]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}\triangleright a)\otimes e_{i}

is a derivation, and the pair

is a left
A
-covariant fodc over
A
. Moreover, it follows from the theorem of two-sided Hopf modules that all fodc, which are finitely generated as left
A
-modules, arise from a finite-dimensional tangent space. Indeed, this gives a bijective correspondence between isomorphism classes of finite-dimensional tangent spaces and finitely-generated left-covariant fodc
[
49
]
.

Let
W
be a Hopf subalgebra of
A^{\circ}
, and denote by
\pi:(A^{\circ})^{\circ}\to W^{\circ}

as contained in
(A^{\circ})^{\circ}
, we denote

is invariant under the adjoint action
\mathrm{ad}
of

\mathrm{W}T\subseteq T
, then the corresponding ideal

is a subcomodule with respect to the

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad_{R,\pi}}:A^{+}\to A^{+}\otimes K,

\displaystyle a\mapsto a_{(2)}\otimes\pi(S(a_{(1)})a_{(3)}).

This endows
\Lambda^{1}(A)
with a right

-coaction, which in turn gives the associated fodc
\Omega^{1}
the structure of a right
K
-covariant dc, defined by the tensor product

-coaction. Moreover, via the dual pairing

Just as for any covariant fodc, this right

-coaction on

3.
A First-Order Differential Calculus for

Lemma 3.6

3.6.

In this subsection we establish some important additional algebraic properties of the quantum exterior algebra. We use the associated graded algebra
\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}

A
Frobenius algebra
is an associative algebra
A
equipped with a non-degenerate bilinear map

B:A\times A\to\mathbb{C}

satisfying

(12)

\displaystyle B(ab,c)=B(a,bc),

\displaystyle\textrm{ for all }a,b,c\in A.

For any linear isomorphism
\iota:\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}_{2\rho}\to\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle B:\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}\otimes\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}\to\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle v\otimes w\mapsto\iota(v\wedge w)

gives
\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}
the structure of a Frobenius algebra.

Proof.

It follows from the relation set given in (
11
) that, for any
v\in\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}
, there exists a
v^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle v\wedge v^{\prime}=\lambda\bigwedge_{\gamma\in\Delta^{+}}e_{\gamma},

\displaystyle\textrm{ for some }\lambda\in\mathbb{C},

where the basis elements are ordered according to our choice of convex ordering on
\Delta^{+}
. Thus
B
is a non-degenerate bilinear form. Since (
12
) is clearly satisfied, we see that
B
gives
\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}
the structure of a Frobenius algebra.
∎

The corollary below now follows from Bongale’s theorem for filtered algebras, as presented in Appendix

Corollary 3.20

For any linear isomorphism

\alpha_{ij}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=j+1}^{n+1}(\alpha_{ij},\alpha_{ik})+\sum_{f=i+1}^{j-1}(\alpha_{ij},\alpha_{fj})+\sum_{b=j+1}^{n+1}(\alpha_{ij},\alpha_{jb})=j-i-1,

where we have observed that the first and the last summands cancel. Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma<\theta\in\Delta^{+}}(\gamma,\beta)=\sum_{\gamma>\theta\in\Delta^{+}}(\gamma,\beta),

meaning that the identity in (
14
) reduces to

(15)

\displaystyle B\!\left(e_{\gamma},e_{\widehat{\gamma}}\right)=(-1)^{|\Delta^{+}|-1}B\!\left(e_{\widehat{\gamma}},e_{\gamma}\right)\!.

This identifies the Nakayama automorphism on the algebra generators
e_{\gamma}
, and hence on the whole algebra
\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}
.
∎

In the following corollary we use our description of the Nakayama automorphism of
\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}
to produce an analogous description of the Nakayama automorphism of
\Lambda^{(0,\bullet)}_{q}
.

Corollary 3.22
.

The Nakayma automorphism of
\Lambda^{(0,\bullet)}_{q}
is determined by

\displaystyle\sigma(e_{\gamma})=(-1)^{|\Delta^{+}|-1}e_{\gamma},

\displaystyle\textrm{ for any }\gamma\in\Delta^{+}.

Proof.

For any
\gamma\neq\beta\in\Delta^{+}
, we see that the product
e_{\gamma}\wedge e_{\widehat{\beta}}
has weight not equal to
2\rho
, and hence it is zero. For the product
e_{\gamma}\wedge e_{\widehat{\gamma}}
, we see that since the space of
(|\Delta^{+}|-1)
-forms is one-dimensional, there exists a scalar
c
such that

e_{\gamma}\wedge e_{\widehat{\gamma}}=ce_{\widehat{\gamma}}\wedge e_{\gamma}.

Moving to the associated graded algebra, we see that this implies the relation

e_{\gamma}\wedge e_{\widehat{\gamma}}=ce_{\widehat{\gamma}}\wedge e_{\gamma},

where, by abuse of notation, we have not differentiated notationally between the product of
\Lambda^{(0,\bullet)}_{q}
and the product of
\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}
. Now we already know from the presentation of the Nakayama automorphism of
\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}
that

e_{\gamma}\wedge e_{\widehat{\gamma}}=(-1)^{|\Delta^{+}|-1}e_{\widehat{\gamma}}\wedge e_{\gamma}.

Thus we can conclude that
c=(-1)^{|\Delta^{+}|-1}
.
∎

Thus we see that, just as in the classical case, the Frobenius structure of
\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}
and
\Lambda^{(0,\bullet)}_{q}
is
graded symmetric
, which is to say, for any two homogeneous elements
v
and
w
we have

B(v,w)=(-1)^{|v||w|}B(w,v).

Indeed, when
|\Delta^{+}|
is odd, both Frobenius algebras are symmetric.

We finish by observing that
\Lambda^{(0,\bullet)}_{q}
is also a Koszul algebra. Recall that a Koszul algebra is a
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-graded algebra admitting a linear minimal graded free resolution. We refer the reader to the standard text
[
37
]
for more details on Koszul algebras.

Proposition 3.23
.

The algebra
\Lambda^{(0,\bullet)}_{q}
is a Koszul algebra.

Proof.

The algebra
\Lambda^{(0,\bullet)}_{q}
is clearly a PBW-algebra in the sense of Priddy
[
37
, §4.1]
. Thus it follows from Priddy’s theorem
[
37
, Theorem 3.1]
that it is Koszul.
∎

4.
The Heckenberger–Kolb Dolbeault Complex for the Quantum Grassmannians

In this section we examine the restriction of the dc
\Omega_{q}^{(0,\bullet)}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
to the quantum Grassmannians and show that we recover the anti-holomorphic Heckenberger–Kolb dc. The embedding of the higher Heckenberger–Kolb forms into the higher

4.1.
Some Preliminaries on Quantum Homogeneous Spaces

In this preliminary subsection, we recall the definition of a quantum homogeneous space, and the associated generalisations of the fundamental theorem of Hopf modules.

Let
A
be a Hopf algebra. We say that a left coideal subalgebra

is a

-space
if
A
is faithfully flat as a right
B
-module and

. In

it was shown that, for the Hopf algebra surjection
\pi_{B}:A\to A/B^{+}A

\pi_{B}(A)
-coaction

\Delta_{R,\pi_{B}}:=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\pi_{B})\circ\Delta:A\to A\otimes\pi_{B}(A),

the space of coinvariants

is equal to
B
. We call
\pi_{B}(A)
the

of

.

For any quantum homogeneous space

, we define
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{B}
to be the category whose objects are left

-comodules

\Delta_{L}:\mathcal{F}\to A\otimes\mathcal{F}
, endowed with a
B
-bimodule structure such that
\Delta_{L}(bfc)=\Delta(b)\Delta_{L}(f)\Delta(c)
, for all

, and whose morphisms are left
A
-comodule,
B
-bimodule, maps. We call this category the

(A,B)
-relative Hopf modules
.

Let

denote the category whose objects are given by left
\pi_{B}(A)
-comodules
\Delta_{L}:V\to\pi_{B}(A)\otimes V

-module structure satisfying the identity
\Delta_{L}(vb)=v_{(-1)}\pi_{B}(b_{(1)})\otimes v_{(0)}b_{(2)}

\pi_{B}(A)
-comodule maps and right

-module maps.

Consider the functor
\Phi:{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{B}\to{}^{\pi_{B}\,}\mathrm{Mod}_{B}

\Phi(\mathcal{F}):=\mathcal{F}/B^{+}\mathcal{F}
, where the left
\pi_{B}(A)
-comodule structure of

\Delta_{L}[f]:=\pi_{B}(f_{(-1)})\otimes[f_{(0)}],

with square brackets denoting the coset of an element in

\Psi:{}^{\pi_{B}\,}\mathrm{Mod}_{B}\to{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{B}

-comodule structure of

is defined on the first tensor factor, the right

-module structure is the diagonal one, and if

An adjoint equivalence of categories between

and

is given by the functors
\Phi
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{U}:{\mathcal{F}}\to\Psi\circ\Phi({\mathcal{F}}),

\displaystyle f\mapsto f_{(-1)}\otimes[f_{(0)}].

We call this

. The
dimension

of an object

is the vector space dimension of
\Phi({\mathcal{F}})
. As established in
[

, §1]
, for any

, an isomorphism in the category

is given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}:A\otimes_{B}\mathcal{F}\to A\otimes\Phi(\mathcal{F}),

\displaystyle a\otimes f\mapsto af_{(-1)}\otimes[f_{(0)}].

Consider next the full subcategory
{}^{A}_{B}\textrm{Mod}_{0}
of
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{B}
consisting of those objects

which satisfy

of

consists of those objects with the trivial right

-action, and is of course equivalent to the category of left

-comodules. Both sub-categories come equipped with evident monoidal structures. Indeed, Takeuchi’s equivalence restricts to a monoidal equivalence between the subcategories, see
[

4.5.
Some General Lemmas

In this subsection we make some general observations so as to help elucidate the delicate issue of embedding higher forms on a quantum homogeneous space

Lemma 4.5
.

Take an object
\mathcal{F}\in{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}
, and an object
\mathcal{M}\in{}^{A}_{A}\mathrm{Mod}
, which we consider as an object in
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}
with respect to the obvious forgetful functor from
{}^{A}_{A}\mathrm{Mod}
to
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}
. For a morphism
\xi:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{M}
in the category
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}

\displaystyle\widehat{\xi}:\Phi(\mathcal{F})\to F(\mathcal{M}),

\displaystyle[f]\mapsto[\xi(f)].

\widehat{\xi}
is an embedding then
\xi
is also an embedding.

Proof.

Since
\xi
is by assumption a left
B
-module map we have that

\xi(B^{+}\mathcal{F})\subseteq B^{+}\xi(\mathcal{F})\subseteq A^{+}\mathrm{M}.

Thus we see that
\widehat{\xi}
is well defined. Commutativity of the diagram of

\textstyle{\mathcal{F}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{U}_{B}}
\scriptstyle{\xi}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces A\square_{\pi_{B}}\Phi(\mathcal{F})}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{id}\otimes\widehat{\xi}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{U}_{A}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ }
\textstyle{A\otimes F(\mathcal{M}),}

follows directly from the formulae for
\mathrm{U}_{A}
and
\mathrm{U}_{B}
. Thus we see that if
\widehat{\xi}
is injective, then
\xi
is also injective.
∎

We note that the converse to this result is not true. Explicitly, if
\xi
is an embedding then it is not necessarily true that
\widehat{\xi}
is an embedding. An example is given by Example

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

be a quantum homogeneous space,
\Omega^{\bullet}(A)
a dc over

, and
\Omega^{\bullet}(B)
a dc over

be a morphism in
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}
that is also an algebra map, and assume that
Ap(\Omega^{1}(B))
is a right
A
-submodule of
\Omega^{1}(A)
. Then the image of the function

\displaystyle\widehat{p}:\Phi(\Omega^{\bullet}(B))\to F(\Omega^{\bullet}(A)),

is a subalgebra of
F(\Omega^{\bullet}(A))
.

Proof.

We have assumed that
Ap(\Omega^{1}(B))
is a right
A
-submodule of
\Omega^{1}(A)
. Let us also assume that

is a right
A
-submodule of
\Omega^{1}(A)
. Then

\displaystyle Ap(\Omega^{k+1}(B))A=

\displaystyle Ap(\Omega^{k}(B))\wedge p(\Omega^{1}(B))A

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle Ap(\Omega^{k}(B))A\wedge p(\Omega^{1}(B))

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle Ap(\Omega^{k}(B))\wedge p(\Omega^{1}(B))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ap(\Omega^{k+1}(B)).

Thus it follows from an inductive argument that
Ap(\Omega^{\bullet}(B))
is a right
A
-submodule of the dc
\Omega^{\bullet}(A)
.

Let us next note that, for any
\omega\in\Omega^{\bullet}(B)
, and any
a\in A
, it holds that

[ap(\omega)]=\varepsilon(a)[p(\omega)]\in\mathrm{im}(\widehat{p}).

Thus, we see that
F(Ap(\Omega^{\bullet}(B)))
is equal to the image of
\widehat{p}
.

Since
\Omega^{\bullet}(A)
is an algebra object in the the category
{}^{A}_{A}\mathrm{Mod}_{A}
, its image under
F
is an algebra object in
\mathrm{Mod}_{A}
. Explicitly, the multiplication
m
is given by

(18)

\displaystyle m\big([\omega]\otimes[\nu]\big)=[\omega S(\nu_{(-1)})\wedge\nu_{(0)}],

where we have used the monoidal structure map given in (
3
). We claim that the image of
\widehat{p}
is a subalgebra of
F\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(A)\big)
. To show this consider the case where
\omega,\nu\in\Omega^{\bullet}(B)
. Since
Ap(\Omega^{\bullet}(B))
is a right
A
-submodule of
\Omega^{\bullet}(A)
, we have that

p(\omega)S(\nu_{(-1)})\wedge p(\nu_{(0)})\in A\Omega^{\bullet}(B).

Hence the corresponding coset in

is contained in the subspace

, which is to say, it is contained in the image of

∎

4.6.

As we saw in §

Proposition 4.8
.

The unique dga map

5.
The Lusztig DC for the Full Quantum Flag Manifolds

In this section we look at the Lusztig dc for the full quantum flag manifolds, and we see that it is a direct
q
-deformation of the anti-holomorphic subcomplex of the classical
A
-series full quantum flag manifold. We present the dc as a direct sum of line modules over
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
. Notably, we show that the right
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
-module structure is not given by the Hopf algebra counit, as is the case for the Heckenberger–Kolb calculi. Thus it lives outside the subcategory of those relative Hopf modules for which the simple monoidal version of Takeuchi’s equivalence applies.

5.1.
Some Details on Full Quantum Flag Manifolds

We begin by recalling some well-known results about the
A
-series full quantum flag manifolds. This is mainly to fix notation, and we refer the reader to
[
7
, §5]
for a detailed exposition for this material in the notation of this paper.

The quantum flag manifold
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
associated to the choice of simple nodes

is called the
full quantum flag manifold
of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
. In this case the isotropy Hopf algebra is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, which is to say the group Hopf algebra of the weight lattice
\mathcal{P}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Thus the simple objects in
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{0}

-algebra grading

Moreover, the grading is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}\cong{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}={\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda+\mu},

\displaystyle\textrm{ for all }\lambda,\,\mu\in\mathcal{P},

where the first isomorphism is given by multiplication. Thus we see that
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}
is an invertible bimodule with inverse
{\mathcal{E}}^{-1}_{\lambda}={\mathcal{E}}_{-\lambda}

[

and call an invertible bimodule in

, or simple a

.

As shown in
[

, §Theorem 4.1]
, a set of algebra generators for

is given by

(19)

\displaystyle z^{\varpi_{x}}_{i}:=c^{\varpi_{x}}_{f_{i},v_{N_{x}}},

\displaystyle\overline{z}_{i}^{\,\varpi_{x}}:=c^{-w_{0}(\varpi_{x})}_{v_{i},f_{N_{x}}}

\displaystyle\textrm{ for }i=1,\dots,N_{x},\textrm{ and }x=1,\dots,n,

where

\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{x}}

. For any

\displaystyle z^{\lambda}:=(z^{\varpi_{1}}_{N_{1}})^{a_{1}}\cdots\,(z^{\varpi_{n}}_{N_{n}})^{a_{n}}.

Finally, we observe that the
\mathcal{P}
-grading of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})

5.2.
A Higher Order Extension of Majid’s Framing Theorem

As we saw from Example

, the contangent space of a covariant first-order differential calculus over a quantum homogeneous space

does not necessarily embed into the cotangent space of the Hopf algebra

. It was proved by Majid in

that right

Consider the category

As observed in

\pi_{B}(A)

We also have an equivalence between

(25)

\displaystyle\Omega^{(0,k)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\cong

\displaystyle\,\bigoplus_{\gamma_{1}<\cdots<\gamma_{k}}\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})\square_{\pi_{B}}\mathbb{C}e_{\gamma_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{\gamma_{k}}

(26)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\gamma_{1}<\cdots<\gamma_{k}}{\mathcal{E}}_{\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{k}},

where summation is over ordered
k
-tuples of positive roots of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
.
∎

As we will see in the next subsection, the Lusztig dc does not live in subcategory
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{0}
. This means that we do not have access to the convenient monoidal form of Takeuchi’s equivalence, as treated in
[
32
]
. This means that in order to describe forms twisted by line modules, we need some new tools.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
B\subseteq A
be a quantum homogeneous space, and
{\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{G}\in{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{B}
. Then an isomorphism in the category
{}^{\pi_{B}}\mathrm{Mod}
is given by

\displaystyle\psi:\Phi({\mathcal{F}}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{G})\to\Phi({\mathcal{F}})\otimes_{B}\mathcal{G},

\displaystyle[f\otimes g]\mapsto[f]\otimes g.

Proof.

For
f\in{\mathcal{F}},\,g\in\mathcal{G}
, and
b\in B
, we note that

\displaystyle[fb]\otimes g=[f]\otimes bg,

\displaystyle[bf]\otimes g=\varepsilon(b)[f]\otimes g.

From this we can conclude that
\psi
is well-defined. An inverse is given by

\displaystyle\psi^{-1}:\Phi({\mathcal{F}})\otimes_{B}\mathcal{G}\to\Phi({\mathcal{F}}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{G}),

\displaystyle[f]\otimes g\mapsto[f\otimes g],

which is confirmed to be well-defined in an analogous manner.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
A,B,{\mathcal{F}}
, and
\mathcal{G}
be as in the lemma above, and moreover, assume that
\Phi({\mathcal{F}})
has a right
A
-action extending its right
B
-action. Then an isomorphism in the category
{}^{\pi_{B}}\mathrm{Mod}
is given by

\displaystyle\phi:\Phi({\mathcal{F}}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{G})\to\Phi({\mathcal{F}})\otimes\Phi(\mathcal{G}),

\displaystyle[f\otimes g]\mapsto[f]g_{(-1)}\otimes[g_{(0)}].

Proof.

Just as in the previous lemma, it is routine to check that
\phi
is well defined as a morphism in the category
{}^{\pi_{B}}\mathrm{Mod}
. Consider next the map

\displaystyle\phi^{-1}:\Phi({\mathcal{F}})\otimes\Phi(\mathcal{G})\to\Phi({\mathcal{F}})\otimes_{B}\mathcal{G},

\displaystyle[f]\otimes[g]\mapsto[f]S(g_{(-1)})\otimes g_{(0)}.

Using the previous lemma to identify
\Phi({\mathcal{F}}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{G})
and
\Phi({\mathcal{F}})\otimes_{B}\mathcal{G}
, we see that
\phi^{-1}
does indeed define an inverse to
\phi
, and hence we have an isomorphism.
∎

Using the isomorphism given above, together with the right
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
-module structure of
V^{(0,1)}_{\varnothing}
given in Lemma
5.4
, we can now describe the space of forms twisted by a line module as a direct sum of line modules.

Proposition 5.8
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
, we have an isomorphism, in the category
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}
,

(27)

\displaystyle\Omega^{(0,k)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})}{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}\cong\bigoplus_{\gamma_{1}<\cdots<\gamma_{k}}{\mathcal{E}}_{\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{k}+\lambda},

where summation is over ordered
k
-tuples of positive roots of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
5.7
that we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle\Phi\!\left(\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})}{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}\right)\to\Phi(\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1}))\otimes\Phi({\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}).

The claimed isomorphism in (
27
) now follows from Takeuchi’s equivalence, Proposition
5.5
and the additivity of weights with respect to tensor products.
∎

5.4.
The Full Lusztig DC and the Sub-Category
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{0}

It follows from
[
21
, Proposition 3.4]
that all the Heckenberger–Kolb anti-holomorphic fodc are contained in the subcategory
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{0}
. Thus in particular, the Podleś fodc is contained in this subcategory. However, as the following proposition shows, the Podleś sphere is the only full quantum flag manifold for which this happens.

Proposition 5.9
.

For
n\geq 2
, the fodc
\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
is not an object in
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{0}
.

Proof.

Consider the element

b:=u_{11}u_{32}u_{33}u_{44}\cdots u_{n+1,n+1}\in\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1}).

Its weight is given by the sum of the weights occurring in
V_{\varpi_{n}}
, which is to say, it is zero. Thus we see that
b\in\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
.

Acting on
e_{21}\in V^{(0,1)}_{\varnothing}
by
b
, we get the non-zero element

\displaystyle e_{21}b=e_{21}u_{11}u_{32}u_{33}\cdots u_{n+1,n+1}=\,

\displaystyle q^{-1}\nu e_{31},

where the identity
e_{21}u_{32}=\nu e_{31}
makes sense because
n\geq 2
by assumption. Thus we see that
b
does not act on
e_{21}
by
\varepsilon(b)=0
, meaning that
V^{(0,1)}_{\varnothing}
is not contained in the subcategory
{}^{\pi_{B}}\mathrm{Mod}_{0}
. It now follows from the subcategory equivalence between
{}^{\pi_{B}}\mathrm{Mod}_{0}
and
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{0}
that the fodc is not contained in the subcategory
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{0}
, for
n\geq 2
.
∎

5.5.
Some Examples and Observations

In this subsection we treat some special cases of the results of the previous section. Moreover, we make some observations about the fact that the Lusztig dc of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
are not contained in the category
\mathrm{Mod}_{0}
.

Example 5.10
.

In addition to being the simplest example of a quantum Grassmannian, the Podleś sphere
\mathcal{O}_{q}(S^{2})
is also the simplest full quantum-flag manifold. As shown in
[
20
]
, and independently in
[
30
]
, we have an
\mathcal{O}_{q}(S^{2})
-bimodule decomposition

\Omega^{1}_{q}(S^{2})\cong\Gamma^{(1,0)}\oplus\Gamma^{(0,1)},

generalising the decomposition of the complexified
1
-forms of
S^{2}
into holomorphic and anti-holomorphic forms. As a comparison of tangent spaces shows,
\Gamma^{(0,1)}
coincides with
\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(S^{2})
.

Recall that the weight lattice of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
, meaning that we can label the line modules as
{\mathcal{E}}_{k}
, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. The root lattice corresponds to the sublattice
2\mathbb{Z}
, and we have that
\alpha_{1}=2\varpi_{1}
. From this we see that

\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(S^{2})\cong{\mathcal{E}}_{2}

This reproduces Majid’s quantum frame bundle description of the anti-holomorphic one-forms of the Podleś calculus
[
30
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Example 5.11
.

It follows from the description of the module structure of
\Lambda^{(0,1)}_{q}
that the decomposition in Proposition
5.5
is not a decomposition as right
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
-modules. However, the
column
decomposition

\displaystyle\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}M_{i},

\displaystyle\textrm{ where }M_{i}:=\bigoplus_{j=i}^{n}{\mathcal{E}}_{\alpha_{i}+\,\cdots\,+\alpha_{j}},

is a decomposition in the category
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{B}
. We note that the only irreducible summand is
{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
. Finally, it is interesting to note that

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{E}}_{\alpha_{i}\,+\cdots\,+\alpha_{n}}

is a sub-bimodule of the fodc, and that it is an object in the sub-category
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{0}
.

Example 5.12
.

For
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{3})
, the
1
-forms are given explicitly by

\displaystyle\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{3})\cong{\mathcal{E}}_{\alpha_{1}}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{\alpha_{2}}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\cong M_{1}\oplus M_{2}.

For the degree two forms we see that

\displaystyle\Omega^{(0,2)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{3})\cong{\mathcal{E}}_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}}.

Finally, the space of top forms is given by the single line module

\Omega^{(0,3)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{3})\cong{\mathcal{E}}_{2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}={\mathcal{E}}_{2\rho},

where
\rho
is the half-sum of positive roots of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
. It follows from the formula for the
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{3})
-module structure of

that the space of top forms is contained in the subcategory
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{0}
. We note that the space of top forms is non-trivial, which is to say we do not have isomorphism
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}\cong\Omega^{(0,3)}(\mathrm{F}_{3})
, or in other words, the dc is not
orientable
. In the classical case, a Kähler manifold whose space of top anti-holomorphic forms is a trivial line module is called a

. While every flag manifold is a Kähler manifold,
n
o flag manifold is a Calabi–Yau manifold.

Example 5.13
.

For the general
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
case, the space of top anti-holomorphic forms is given by

\Omega^{(0,|\Delta^{+}|)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\cong{\mathcal{E}}_{2\rho}\in{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{0}.

Again we see that the dc is not orientable, generalising the fact that
\mathrm{F}_{n+1}
is not a Calabi–Yau manifold.

6.
A Borel–Weil Theorem for the Full Quantum Flag Manifolds

In this section we construct a direct quantum group generalisation of the classical Borel–Weil theorem for the
A
-series full flag manifold. This gives, for the first time, a noncommutative differential geometric realisation of all the type-
1
finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
. This approach can easily be adapted to treat the quantum Grassmannians
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{Gr}_{n+1,m})
, whereupon it reproduces the quantum Borel–Weil theorem given in
[
6
]
and
[
5
]
. We note that the existence of a tractable fodc
\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
on the Hopf algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
allows for a significant simplification of these earlier proofs.

6.1.
Connections and Quantum Principal Bundles

For
\Omega^{1}
a fodc over an algebra
B
, and
\mathcal{F}
a left
B
-module, a
left connection
for
{\mathcal{F}}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map

\nabla:\mathcal{F}\to\Omega^{1}\otimes_{B}{\mathcal{F}}

satisfying

(28)

\displaystyle\nabla(bf)=\mathrm{d}b\otimes f+b\nabla f,

\displaystyle\textrm{ for all }b\in B,f\in{\mathcal{F}}.

The difference of two connections
\nabla-\nabla^{\prime}
is a left
B
-module map. Any connection can be extended to a map
\nabla:\Omega^{\bullet}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{F}\to\Omega^{\bullet}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{F}
by the formula

\displaystyle\nabla(\omega\otimes f)=\mathrm{d}\omega\otimes f+(-1)^{|\omega|}\,\omega\wedge\nabla f,

where
f\in{\mathcal{F}}
, and
\omega
is a homogeneous element of
\Omega^{\bullet}
of degree
|\omega|
.
The
curvature
of an connection is the left
B
-module map
\nabla^{2}:\mathcal{F}\to\Omega^{2}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{F}
. An connection is said to be
flat
if
\nabla^{2}=0
. Since
\nabla^{2}(\omega\otimes f)=\omega\wedge\nabla^{2}(f)
, an connection is flat if and only if the pair
(\Omega^{\bullet}\otimes_{B}{\mathcal{F}},\nabla)
is a cochain complex.

A
left bimodule connection
on
{\mathcal{F}}
is a pair
(\nabla,\sigma)
where
\nabla
is a left connection and
\sigma:{\mathcal{F}}\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}(B)\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(B)\otimes_{B}{\mathcal{F}}
is a bimodule map satisfying

(29)

\displaystyle\sigma(f\otimes db)=\nabla(fb)-\nabla(f)b.

Note that in the commutative case
\sigma
reduces to the standard flip map.

We now briefly recall Brzezinski and Majid’s theory of quantum principal bundles, a framework for constructing connections. To keep the preliminaries to a minimum, we restrict to the quantum homogeneous space case. For more details, we direct the interested reader to
[
1
, §5]
or to
[
5
, §2]
. A
homogeneous quantum principal bundle
is a pair
(B,\Omega^{1}(A))
consisting of a quantum homogeneous space
B\subseteq A
, and a left
A
-covariant, right
\pi_{B}(A)
-covariant fodc
\Omega^{1}(A)
over
A
. A
principal connection
for
(B,\Omega^{1}(A))
is a left
A
-module, right
\pi_{B}(A)
-comodule, map

\Pi:\Omega^{1}(A)\to\Omega^{1}(A),

satisfying
\Pi^{2}=\Pi
, and
\mathrm{ker}(\Pi)=A\Omega^{1}(B)A
, where
\Omega^{1}(B)
is the restriction of
\Omega^{1}(A)
to a fodc on
B
. We are interested in principal connections because they allow us to construct connections for any relative Hopf module. Again, to keep preliminaries to a minimum, we will restrict our attention to the subcategory
{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{0}
. Any simple object
\mathcal{F}
in this subcategory is isomorphic to a subobject of
A
(see
[
7
, Appendix A.3]
for details). Now if the principal connection is
strong
, that is to say, if
(\mathrm{id}-\Pi)\circ\mathrm{d}B\subseteq\Omega^{1}(B)A
, then an connection for
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq A
is given by

\displaystyle\nabla:{\mathcal{F}}\to\Omega^{1}(B)\otimes_{B}{\mathcal{F}},

\displaystyle f\mapsto j\!\left(\sum_{i}(\mathrm{id}-\Pi)(\mathrm{d}f)\right)\!,

where
j:\Omega^{1}(B){\mathcal{F}}\to\Omega^{1}(B)\otimes_{B}{\mathcal{F}}
is the inverse to the obvious multiplication map from
\Omega^{1}(B)\otimes_{B}{\mathcal{F}}
to
\Omega^{1}(B){\mathcal{F}}
. (Faithful flatness guarantees that the multiplication map is invertible). We call
\nabla
the connection
associated
to
\Pi
. If
\Pi
is additionally assumed to be a left
A
-comodule map, then every associated connection will be a left
A
-comodule map.

6.2.
Holomorphic Structures and Principal Connections

Since the Lusztig fodc on the Hopf algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
is left
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
-covariant we have a homogeneous quantum principal bundle. We start by showing that the zero map is a strong principal connection for the bundle.

Lemma 6.1
.

We have the equality

(30)

\displaystyle\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})=\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})=\,\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1}).

Hence the zero map on
\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
is a principal connection, and it is the unique principal connection for the bundle.

Proof.

It follows from
[
47
, §2.3]
(see also the discussion in
[
32
, §2.3]
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})=

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})\Big(\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})\square_{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}V_{\varnothing}^{(0,1)}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})\otimes V_{\varnothing}^{(0,1)},

which gives us the first identity in (
31
).

To prove the second identity, we follow the approach of
[
5
, Lemma 5.7]
. Note first that the Lusztig fodc is generated as a right
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
-module by those elements of the form
\overline{\partial}b
, for
b\in\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
. Thus we just need to show that, for any
a\in\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
,

(31)

\displaystyle a\overline{\partial}b\in\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1}).

Consider the set of algebra generators
z_{i}^{\varpi_{x}}
of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
presented in (
19
). Since
z_{i}^{\varpi_{x}}
is a highest weight vector with respect to the left action of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
on
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
, it follows from (
4
) that it is contained in the kernel of
\overline{\partial}
, the exterior derivative of our fodc on
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
. Thus, for any
b\in\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
, we see that

(32)

\displaystyle z_{i}^{\varpi_{x}}\overline{\partial}b=\overline{\partial}(z_{i}^{\varpi_{x}}b)-\overline{\partial}(z_{i}^{\varpi_{x}})b=\overline{\partial}(z_{i}^{\varpi_{x}}b).

Consider next the element

\displaystyle(S\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta(z^{\varpi_{x}}_{N_{x}})=\sum_{a=1}^{N_{x}}S(c^{\varpi_{x}}_{f_{N_{x}},v_{a}})\otimes c^{\varpi_{x}}_{f_{a},v_{N_{x}}}=\sum_{a=1}^{N_{x}}S(c^{\varpi_{x}}_{f_{N_{x}},v_{a}})\otimes z^{\varpi_{x}}_{a}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{-\varpi_{x}}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{\varpi_{x}},

where the fact that
S(z^{\varpi_{x}}_{f_{N_{x}},v_{a}})
is an element of
{\mathcal{E}}_{-\varpi_{x}}
follows from the calculation

\displaystyle K_{i}\triangleright S(c^{\varpi_{x}}_{f_{N_{x}},v_{a}})=S(c^{\varpi_{x}}_{K_{i}f_{N_{x}},v_{a}})=q^{-(\alpha_{i},\varpi_{x})}S(c^{\varpi_{x}}_{f_{N_{x}},v_{a}}).

Multiplying both sides of the identity gives us the new identity

(33)

\displaystyle\varepsilon(z^{\varpi_{x}}_{N_{x}})=1=\sum_{a=1}^{N_{x}}S(z^{\varpi_{x}}_{f_{N_{x}},v_{a}})z^{\varpi_{x}}_{a}.

Inserting this into (
32
) gives us the expression

\displaystyle z_{i}^{\varpi_{x}}\overline{\partial}b=\sum_{a=1}^{N_{x}}\overline{\partial}(z_{i}^{\varpi_{x}}bS(z^{\varpi_{x}}_{f_{N_{x}},v_{a}})z^{\varpi_{x}}_{a})=\sum_{a=1}^{N_{x}}\overline{\partial}(z_{i}^{\varpi_{x}}bS(z^{\varpi_{x}}_{f_{N_{x}},v_{a}}))z^{\varpi_{x}}_{a}.

From the fact that our
\mathcal{P}
-grading is an algebra grading, we can conclude that

\displaystyle z_{i}^{\varpi_{x}}bS(z^{\varpi_{x}}_{f_{N_{x}},v_{a}})\in{\mathcal{E}}_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1}).

Thus we see that (
31
) is satisfied for
a=z^{\varpi_{x}}_{i}
. Consider next the dual generator
\overline{z}^{\varpi_{x}}_{i}
, and note that

\displaystyle\overline{z}^{\varpi_{x}}_{i}\overline{\partial}b=\overline{\partial}(\overline{z}^{\varpi_{x}}_{i}b)-\overline{\partial}(\overline{z}^{\varpi_{x}}_{i})b.

An analogous application of the identity in (
33
) shows us that this element is again contained in
\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
, which is to say, (
31
) is again satisfied. Thus the second identity in (
30
) is satisfied. It is now clear that the zero map is a strong principal connection, and moreover, that the zero map is the unique principal connection for the bundle.
∎

The following corollary explicitly describes the connection associated to
\Pi
for a relative Hopf submodule of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
. It is a direct consequence of the various constructions involved, and so, we omit it.

Proposition 6.2
.

For any simple relative Hopf module
{\mathcal{F}}\in{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{0}
, considered as a sub-object of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
, the connection associated to
\Pi
is given explicitly by

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}_{{\mathcal{F}}}:{\mathcal{F}}\to\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})}{\mathcal{F}},

\displaystyle f\mapsto j\!\left(\sum_{\gamma\,\in\,\Delta^{+}}(E_{\gamma}\triangleright f)\otimes e_{\gamma}\right)\!.

Since the zero map is obviously an
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
-module map, the following corollary now follows directly from
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Corollary 6.3
.

For every relative Hopf module
{\mathcal{F}}\in{}^{A}_{B}\mathrm{Mod}_{0}
, the associated connection
\overline{\partial}_{{\mathcal{F}}}:{\mathcal{F}}\to\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})}{\mathcal{F}}
is a bimodule connection.

We will now follow the representation theoretic argument given in
[
12
, §3.2]
and establish uniqueness and flatness for relative line module connections.

Lemma 6.4
.

For each line module
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}
, the connection is the unique left
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
-covariant connection
\overline{\partial}_{{\mathcal{E}}_{\gamma}}
. Moreover, the connection is flat.

Proof.

We know from Proposition
5.8
that

\displaystyle\Phi\!\left(\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})}{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}\right)\cong\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Delta^{+}}\Phi({\mathcal{E}}_{\gamma+\lambda}).

Thus we see that the only morphism

\displaystyle\Phi({\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda})\to\Phi\!\left(\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})}{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}\right)\!.

is the zero morphism. Since the difference of two connections is a left
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
-module map, it now follows from Takeuchi’s equivalence that
\overline{\partial}_{{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}}
is the unique left
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
-covariant connection on
{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}
. (For more details see
[
12
, Proposition 3.4]
.)

Proposition
5.8
also implies that the only morphism

\Phi({\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda})\to\Phi\!\left(\Omega^{(0,2)}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})}{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}\right)

is the zero map. Thus, since curvature is a left
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
-module map, we can similarly conclude that
\overline{\partial}_{{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}}
is flat. (For more details see
[
12
, Proposition 3.3]
.)
∎

This means that we have a complex

\overline{\partial}_{{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}}:\Omega^{(0,\bullet)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}\to\Omega^{(0,\bullet)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda},

which is a direct
q
-deformation of the Dolbeault anti-holomorphic complex of the line modules over the
A
-series full flag manifolds. We denote the cohomology groups of the complex by
H^{(0,\bullet)}_{\overline{\partial}}({\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda})
.

6.3.
A Borel–Weil Theorem for
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})

In this subsection we establish the main result of this section, namely a noncommutative Borel–Weil theorem for the full quantum flag manifold
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
. This gives a noncommutative differential geometric realisation of all the irreducible type-1 finite-dimensional representations of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
.

Theorem 6.5
.

For the full quantum flag manifold
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})
, it holds that:

(1)

H^{0}_{\overline{\partial}}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda})
is an irreducible
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
-module of highest weight
-w_{0}(\lambda)
, for all
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}^{+}
,

(2)

H^{0}_{\overline{\partial}}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda})=0
, for all
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}\,\backslash\,\mathcal{P}^{+}
.

Proof.

It follows from the construction of
\overline{\partial}
, as presented in §
2.2
, that for any
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}
, we have
\overline{\partial}(e)=0
if and only if

(34)

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}\triangleright e=0,

\displaystyle\textrm{ for all }\gamma\in\Delta^{+},

which is to say, if and only if
e
is a highest weight vector, of weight
\lambda
.

If
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{P}^{+}
, then it is clear that no such element exists, meaning that
H^{0}_{\overline{\partial}}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda})=0
. For
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}^{+}
, recalling the Peter–Weyl decomposition of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
, the only highest weight elements of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
of weight

where

is an irreducible

-module of highest weight

.

Restricting to the case of the trivial line module

Corollary 6.6
.

The Lusztig fodc

is

, which is to say

\mathrm{ker}\Big(\overline{\partial}:\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\to\Omega^{(0,1)}_{q}(\mathrm{F}_{n+1})\Big)=\mathbb{C}1.

We note that, by contrast, the fodc on

is not connected. However, this can be rectified by the addition of the anti-holomorphic forms, corresponding to the negative root vectors. This will be addressed in future work.

7.
Outside the Nice Setting

The reduced decompositions
\mathbf{j}
and
\mathbf{j}^{\prime}
(see

\mathbf{j}^{\prime}
) are distinguished, among the reduced decompositions of the longest element of the Weyl group

, as those that are

in the sense of Littlemann. The notion of a nice decomposition is defined for all Weyl groups, and is given in terms of crystal graphs for finite-dimensional

-modules. See
[

for more details.

In this section we consider some low-dimensional examples of Lusztig’s root vectors for non-nice decompositions, or more precisely, for their associated commutation classes. Explicitly, we examine when the span of the root vectors gives a quantum tangent space for the full quantum flag manifold. Since the case of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
is trivial, and for
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3})
both decompositions are nice, we start with the case of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{4})
, treating its
6

U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{5})
, examining those non-nice commutation classes, from a total of
62
such classes, that are one or two braid moves from our choice of nice class.

7.1.
The Opposite Involution, Reduced Decompositions, and DC

In this subsection we look at the action of the opposite involution on the differential calculi associated to reduced decompositions of
w_{0}
the longest element of the Weyl group. In particular, we will show that the dimension of the maximal prolongation of a fodc is invariant under the opposite involution, as defined in Appendix
A.6
.

Lemma 7.1
.

For any reduced decomposition
\mathbf{i}
of
w_{0}
, the space of positive root vectors
\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\mathbf{i}}
is a left, or a right, tangent space if and only if
\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}
is a left, respectively right, tangent space.

Proof.

This follows directly from Proposition
A.4
, and the fact that
\overline{w_{0}}
is a Hopf algebra automorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
.
∎

The following lemma now describes, for the tangent space case, the relationship between the corresponding ideals.

Lemma 7.2
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\mathbf{i}}
is a left, or a right, tangent space, and let us denote by
I\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})^{+}
the corresponding ideal. Then
\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}
is a left, respectively right, tangent space with corresponding ideal
\overline{w_{0}}^{\,*}(I)
.

Proof.

Let us assume that
y\in I
, which is to say, let us assume that

(36)

\displaystyle\big\langle X,y\big\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\textrm{ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \, for all }X\in\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\textbf{i}}.

Equivalently,
y\in I
if and only if

\displaystyle\Big\langle\overline{w_{0}}(X),\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(y)\Big\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\textrm{ for all }X\in\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\textbf{i}}.

Thus, denoting by
I^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})^{+}
the ideal corresponding to the tangent space

\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}=\overline{w_{0}}(\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\mathbf{i}}),

we see that
\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(y)\in I^{\prime}
, and so
\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(I)\subseteq I^{\prime}
. In the other direction, we see that
z\in I^{\prime}
if and only if

\displaystyle 0=\Big\langle\overline{w_{0}}(X),z\Big\rangle=\Big\langle X,\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(z)\Big\rangle,

\displaystyle\textrm{ for all }X\in\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\mathbf{i}}.

Thus, since this is equivalent to having
\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(z)\in I
, we see that we have established the opposite inclusion, and hence equality.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\mathbf{i}
be a reduced decomposition of the longest element of the Weyl group such that
\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\mathbf{i}}
is a right or a left tangent space, and denote by
\Lambda_{q,\mathbf{i}}^{\bullet}
the associated quantum exterior algebra. Then, denoting by
\Lambda_{q,\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}^{\bullet}
the quantum exterior algebra of the quantum tangent space
\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}=\overline{w_{0}}(\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\mathbf{i}})
, an algebra isomorphism
\Lambda^{\bullet}_{q,\mathbf{i}}\to\Lambda^{\bullet}_{q,\mathbf{i}’}
is determined by

\displaystyle[a_{1}]\wedge\cdots\wedge[a_{k}]\penalty\ \mapsto\penalty\ [\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(a_{1})]\wedge\cdots\wedge[\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(a_{k})],

\displaystyle\textrm{ for }k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Proof.

Note first that we have a linear isomorphism

\displaystyle\rho:\Lambda^{1}_{q,\mathbf{i}}\to\Lambda^{1}_{q,\mathbf{i}’},

\displaystyle[a]\mapsto[\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(a)].

We can uniquely extend this to an algebra isomorphism

\rho:\mathcal{T}\big(\Lambda^{1}_{q,\mathbf{i}}\big)\to\mathcal{T}\big(\Lambda^{1}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}\big),

where
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda^{1}_{q,\mathbf{i}})
, and
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda^{1}_{q,\mathbf{i}^{\prime}})
, denote the tensor algebra of
\Lambda^{1}_{q,\mathbf{i}})
, and
\Lambda^{1}_{q,\mathbf{i}^{\prime}})
, respectively. For any element
y\in I
, we now see that

\displaystyle\rho\big([y_{(1)}]\otimes[y_{(2)}]\big)=[\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(y_{(1)})]\otimes[\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(y_{(2)})]=[\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(y)_{(1)}]\otimes[\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(y)_{(2)}]\in(\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(I))^{(2)},

where we have used the fact that
\overline{w_{0}}^{*}
is a Hopf algebra map. An analogous calculation in the opposite direction then confirms that the inverse

(\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(I))^{(2)}
to

. Thus

\Lambda^{\bullet}_{q,\mathbf{i}}
and
\Lambda^{\bullet}_{q,\mathbf{i}’}
as claimed.
∎

The following proposition now follows directly. In particular, it means that one need only check the classical dimension requirement for
\mathbf{i}
or
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}

Proposition 7.4

A reduced decomposition
\mathbf{i}
gives a tangent space whose associated quantum exterior algebra has classical dimension if and only if the same is true for
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}

A.4.

In this subsection we recall Lusztig’s braid group action on

To every

A.5.
The Explicit Calculation

In this subsection we give an explicit description of the root vectors associated to the longest word decomposition given in §3.1. We note first that it follows from the discussions in
[
27
, 1.8(d)]
that

\displaystyle T_{k,k\pm 1}(E_{k})=-E_{k\pm 1}.

We find it convenient to introduce some notation. First we write

\displaystyle E_{ji}:=[\cdots[E_{j-1},E_{j-2}]_{q^{-1}},E_{j-3}]_{q^{-1}},\cdots,E_{i}]_{q^{-1}}.

Then, for any
i\leq j
, we denote

\displaystyle T_{ji}:=T_{j}\circ T_{j-1}\circ\cdots\circ T_{i}.

The following more general formulae can now be derived from this lemma.

Lemma A.2
.

For any
(i,j)\in\Delta^{+}
, it holds that

(1)

T_{n,i+1}(E_{i})=(-1)^{n-i}E_{n+1,i}
,

(2)

T_{n,i-1}(E_{ji})=(-1)^{j-i+1}E_{j-1,i-1}
.

Proof.

1. Assume that, for some
n>b\geq i+1
, we have

\displaystyle T_{b,i+1}(E_{i})=(-1)^{b-i}E_{b+1,i}.

Then it follows that

\displaystyle T_{b+1}\circ T_{b,i+1}(E_{i})=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{b-i}T_{b+1}(E_{b+1,i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{b-i}T_{b+1}\!\left([\cdots[E_{b},E_{b-1}]_{q^{-1}},E_{b-2}]_{q^{-1}},\cdots,E_{i}]_{q^{-1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{b-i}[\cdots[T_{b+1}(E_{b}),E_{b-1}]_{q^{-1}},E_{b-2}]_{q^{-1}},\cdots,E_{i}]_{q^{-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{b-i+1}[\cdots[[E_{b+1},E_{b}]_{q^{-1}},E_{b-1}]_{q^{-1}},E_{b-2}]_{q^{-1}},\cdots,E_{i}]_{q^{-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{(b+1)-i}E_{b+2,i}.

Since the identity clearly holds for
T_{i+1}(E_{i})=-E_{i+2,i}
, the general case now follows by an inductive argument.

2. It follows from (
A.5
) that, for any
k=i,\dots,j-1
, we have

\displaystyle T_{n,i-1}(E_{k})=

\displaystyle\,T_{n,k-1}\circ T_{k-2}\cdots T_{i-1}(E_{ji})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,T_{n,k+2}\circ T_{k}\circ T_{k-1}(E_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-T_{n,k+2}(E_{k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-E_{k-1}.

It now follows that

\displaystyle T_{n,i-1}(E_{ji})=

\displaystyle\,T_{n,i-1}([\cdots[E_{j},E_{j-1}]_{q^{-1}},E_{j-2}]_{q^{-1}},\cdots,E_{i}]_{q^{-1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,([\cdots[T_{n,i-1}(E_{j}),T_{n,i-1}(E_{j-1})]_{q^{-1}},T_{n,i-1}(E_{j-2})]_{q^{-1}},\cdots,T_{n,i-1}(E_{i})]_{q^{-1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{j-i+1}[\cdots[E_{j-1},E_{j-2}]_{q^{-1}},E_{j-3}]_{q^{-1}},\cdots,E_{i-1}]_{q^{-1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{j-i+1}E_{j-1,i-1},

giving the claimed identity.
∎

Proposition A.3
.

For the reduced decomposition
\mathbf{j}
, the associated root vectors are given, up to sign, by the elements

\left\{E_{ji}\,|\,\textrm{ for }\alpha_{ij}\in\Delta^{+}\right\}\!.

Proof.

Any root vector will, by definition, be of the form

\displaystyle T_{n1}\circ T_{n2}\circ\cdots\circ T_{n,k+1}(E_{k}),

\displaystyle\textrm{ for some }1\leq k\leq n.

From the first identity in Lemma
A.2
, we know that

T_{n,k+1}(E_{k})=(-1)^{n-k}E_{n+1,k}.

Moreover, the second identity in Lemma
A.2
tells us that when we operate on

by the operator
T_{n,i-1}
, we get the element
E_{j-1,i-1}
up to sign. Moreover, we see that each

can be realised in this way, giving the claimed presentation of the root vectors.
∎

Finally, we note that each root vector is a weight vector with respect to the adjoint action of

on itself. Explicitly, it holds that

\displaystyle|E_{ji}|=\alpha_{j-1}+\alpha_{j-2}+\cdots+\alpha_{i},

\displaystyle\textrm{ for }\alpha_{ij}\in\Delta^{+}.

A.6.

For the

defined on generators as

\displaystyle\overline{w_{0}}(E_{i}):=E_{i^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\overline{w_{0}}(K_{i})=K_{i^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\overline{w_{0}}(F_{i})=F_{i^{\prime}},

of

. For more details on automorphisms of Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum groups, see
[
23
, §6.1.6]
.

We also have a natural associated inner automorphism of the Weyl group

\displaystyle S_{n+1}\to S_{n+1},

\displaystyle s_{i}\mapsto s_{i^{\prime}},

and we call it the
opposite involution
of
W
. This in turn gives a permutation of the set of commutation classes of reduced decompositions, which we denote by
\mathbf{i}\mapsto\mathbf{i}^{\prime}
, for any commutation class

. We note that different choices of reduced decomposition of the longest element of the Weyl group can give different sets of root vectors. However, two decompositions in the same commutation class will give the same set of root vectors.

Proposition A.4
.

Let
\mathbf{i}
be a reduced decomposition of

. Then it holds that

(42)

\displaystyle\overline{w_{0}}(\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\mathbf{i}})=\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}},

for all reduced decompositions of the longest element of the Weyl group

Proof.

It follows from the (

) that

\displaystyle\overline{w_{0}}\circ T_{i}(E_{k})=T_{i^{\prime}}\circ\overline{w_{0}}(E_{k}),

\displaystyle\textrm{ for all }i,k=1,\dots,n.

It now follows that

\displaystyle\overline{w_{0}}\circ T_{i}(E_{k_{1}}E_{k_{2}}\cdots E_{k_{l}})=

\displaystyle\,\overline{w_{0}}(T_{i}(E_{k_{1}}))\overline{w_{0}}(T_{i}(E_{k_{2}}))\cdots\overline{w_{0}}(T_{i}(E_{k_{l}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,T_{i^{\prime}}(\overline{w_{0}}(E_{k_{1}}))T_{i^{\prime}}(\overline{w_{0}}(E_{k_{2}})\cdots T_{i^{\prime}}(\overline{w_{0}}(E_{k_{l}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,T_{i^{\prime}}\circ\overline{w_{0}}(E_{k_{1}}E_{k_{2}}\cdots E_{k_{l}}).

Thus we see that, for any
X\in U_{q}(\mathfrak{n}_{\mathbf{i}}^{+})
, we have that

\overline{w_{0}}\circ T_{i}(X)=T_{i^{\prime}}\circ\overline{w_{0}}(X).

From this we now see that

\displaystyle\overline{w_{0}}(T_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots T_{{i_{k}}}(E_{j}))=

\displaystyle T_{i^{\prime}_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{i^{\prime}_{k}}\circ\overline{w_{0}}(E_{j})=T_{i^{\prime}_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{i^{\prime}_{k}}(E_{j^{\prime}}).

Thus we see that a root vector corresponding to the
w_{0}

is mapped by
\overline{w_{0}}
to the root vector corresponding to the substring
s_{i^{\prime}_{1}}s_{i^{\prime}_{2}}\cdots s_{i^{\prime}_{k}}
. This means that
\overline{w_{0}}
maps the positive quantum root space
\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\mathbf{i}}
to the positive quantum root space
\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}
as claimed.
∎

In other words, the above proposition says that the following diagram commutes:

Proposition A.5
.

The algebra map

\displaystyle\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(u_{ij})\mapsto-qS(u_{n+2-j,n+2-i}),

\displaystyle\textrm{ for }i,j=1,\dots,n+1,

\displaystyle\langle\overline{w_{0}}(X),a\rangle=\langle X,\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(a)\rangle,

\displaystyle\textrm{ for all }X\in U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}),\,a\in\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1}),

which is to say, it is

to
\overline{w_{0}}
with respect to the bilinear pairing.

A direct check of the defining relations of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
confirms that
\overline{w_{0}}^{*}
gives a well-defined Hopf algebra algebra automorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathrm{SU}_{n+1})
. Now since we have a dual pairing of Hopf algebras, it is sufficient to check (

For the generator

\displaystyle\big\langle\overline{w_{0}}(E_{i}),u_{n-i+2,n-i+1})\big\rangle=\big\langle E_{i^{\prime}},u_{n-i+2,n-i+1})\big\rangle=1,

while on the other hand

\displaystyle\big\langle E_{i},\overline{w_{0}}^{*}(u_{n-i+2,n-i+1})\big\rangle=-q\big\langle E_{i},S(u_{i+1,i})\big\rangle=-q\big\langle S(E_{i}),u_{i+1,i}\big\rangle=q\big\langle E_{i}K_{i},u_{i+1,i}\big\rangle=1.

For all other matrix generators, both sides of (
43
) clearly give zero, which is to say, the identity is trivially satisfied. The case of the generators
F_{i}
and
K_{i}
is similarly confirmed. Thus we see that (
43
) holds.
∎

Appendix B
Some Remarks on Filtered Algebras

We first present a simple system for constructing a filtration from a total order on a set of algebra generators. Our motivation is to provide a convenient framework in which to state the proof of Lemma
3.16
, and we make no claims of originality.

Consider an algebra
A
, together with a set of algebra generators
G
. This gives a surjective algebra map
\mathrm{proj}_{A}:\mathcal{T}(V)\twoheadrightarrow A
, where
V
is the linear span of the elements of
G
, and
\mathcal{T}(V)
is its tensor algebra. A set of
relations
for
A
, with respect to
G
, is a choice of subset
R\subseteq\mathrm{ker}(\mathrm{proj}_{A})
that generates
\mathrm{ker}(\mathrm{proj}_{A})
as a two-sided ideal in
\mathcal{T}(V)
.

Let
X
be a non-empty totally-ordered finite set with
n
elements. Moreover, let
V
be an
n
-dimensional vector space with a basis
\{e_{x}\}_{x\in X}
indexed by the elements of
X
. We identify
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
with the free abelian monoid generated by
X
, and we endow it with the degree lexicographical order
<
induced by the total order of
X
. A
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
-algebra grading on
\mathcal{T}(V)
is uniquely determined by
|e_{x}|=x
. Consider a general element
f\in\mathcal{T}(V)
, written as a sum
f=\sum_{d=1}^{m}f_{d}
, where each summand is homogeneous with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
-grading, and
|f_{i}|<|f_{m}|
, for all
i=1,\dots,m-1
. We call
f_{m}
the
leading term
of
f
and denote

\mathrm{LT}(f):=f_{m}.

An algebra filtration of the tensor algebra

\mathscr{F}^{\mathcal{T}}=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}}\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{T}}\subseteq\mathcal{T}(V)

can then be defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{T}}:=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{e_{x_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{x_{k}}|\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\leq\lambda\right\}.

For any two-sided ideal
J\subseteq\mathcal{T}(V)
, we have the induced filtration
\mathscr{F}
on the quotient
A:=\mathcal{T}(V)/J
. Explicitly

\displaystyle A=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}}\mathrm{proj}_{A}(\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{T}})=:\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}}\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}.

Consider the associated graded algebra

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}}\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}_{\lambda}:=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}}\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}/\mathscr{F}_{\lambda-1}.

For any
a\in\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}
, we find it convenient to denote the coset of
a
in
\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}_{\lambda}
by
[a]_{\lambda}
. By construction of the filtration, the elements
[e_{x}]_{x}
generate
\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}
. We denote by

\mathrm{proj}_{\mathscr{F}}:\mathcal{T}(V)\to\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}

the associated surjective algebra map. Moreover, for
j\in J
, we see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{LT}(j)\in\mathrm{ker}(\mathrm{proj}_{\mathscr{F}}).

We finish with some remarks on Bongale’s theorem
[
3
, Theorem 2]
for filtered Frobenius algebras (see §
3.6
for the definition of Frobenius algebras). This result was originally stated for
\mathbb{Z}
-filtrations, but as observed in
[
24
, Lemma 4.10]
, it holds more generally. We state it for the free abelian monoid
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
endowed with some total order
\geq
. Consider a finite-dimensional algebra
A
, endowed with a filtration

A=\bigcup_{\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}}\mathscr{F}_{\mu}.

Let us assume that the associated graded algebra
\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}\Big(\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}_{0}\Big)=\mathrm{dim}\Big(\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}_{\lambda}\Big)=1,

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}_{\mu}=0,\textrm{ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ for all }\mu>\lambda,

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
.
Bongale’s theorem
states that if
\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}
is a Frobenius algebra, then a Frobenius structure for
A
is given by

\displaystyle B:A\otimes A\to\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle(a,b)\mapsto\iota\big([ab]_{\lambda}\big),

where
\iota:\mathrm{gr}^{\mathscr{F}}_{\lambda}\to\mathbb{C}
is any choice of linear isomorphism. Moreover, all Frobenius structures are of this form.

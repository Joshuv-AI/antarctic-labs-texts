Solvability of invariant systems of differential equations on ℍ 2 \mathbb{H}^{2} and beyond
Martin Olbrich, Guendalina Palmirotta

Abstract
We show how the Fourier transform for distributional sections of vector bundles over symmetric spaces of non-compact type G / K G/K can be used for questions of solvability of systems of invariant differential equations in analogy to Hörmander’s proof of the Ehrenpreis-Malgrange theorem. We get complete solvability for the hyperbolic plane ℍ 2 \mathbb{H}^{2} and partial results for products ℍ 2 × ⋯ × ℍ 2 \mathbb{H}^{2}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{2} and the hyperbolic 3-space ℍ 3 \mathbb{H}^{3} . Mathematics Subject Classification: 58J50, 22E30. Keywords: Linear Differential Operators, Paley-Wiener spaces, Symmetric spaces, Hyperbolic spaces, Homogeneous vector bundles, Representation theory of semi-simple Lie groups, Intertwining operators.

Introduction

In the Euclidean case
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(for some positive integer
n
), it is well-known by Malgrange
[
Mal56
]
and Ehrenpreis
[
Ehr54
]
that all linear differential operators with constant coefficients

D=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}c_{\alpha}D^{\alpha},\;\;\;D^{\alpha}=\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{x_{n}}^{\alpha_{n}}},\;\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\;|\alpha|=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}

are solvable in the space of smooth functions
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Even more, there exists a fundamental solution
E
for
D
, which is a distribution
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
DE=\delta
and consequently, the function
f:=E*g

is a solution of the equation
Df=g
at least for
g
with compact support.
Here,
\delta
denotes the Dirac measure or delta-distribution at the origin on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Constant coefficients operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are exactly invariant differential operators, if we consider
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as a Lie group of translations.
Speaking of Lie groups
G
, one may ask the analog question for invariant differential operators, which are left invariant under
G
:

D(f\circ l_{g})=(Df)\circ l_{g},\;\;\;f\in C^{\infty}(G),\forall g\in G,

(1.1)

where
l_{g}
is the left translation on
G
.
Such differential operators are, in general, not even locally solvable.
On the other hand, the situation is much better for non-zero linear differential operators
D
, which are bi-invariant under
G
, this means that (
1.1
) holds not only for the left but also for the right translation on
G
.
In fact,
D
is locally solvable for simply connected nilpotent Lie groups by Raïs
[
Ra71
]
, for solvable Lie groups by Raïs-Duflo
[
DuRa76
]
and Rouvière
[
Ro76
]
, and for semi-simple Lie groups by Helgason
[
Hel75
]
.
Some interesting results for more general groups have been found in
[
Ce75
]
and
[
Hel75
]
.
Now, if we consider the quotient
X=G/K
with
G
a non-compact connected semi-simple Lie group with finite center and
K
is its maximal compact subgroup, then Helgason
[
Hel89
, Chap. V]
proved that all

G
-invariant differential operators are solvable on Riemannian symmetric spaces
X
of non-compact type.
The key tool of most of these works is the theory of harmonic analysis on the corresponding Lie group or on symmetric spaces, in particular the application of the Fourier transform.

However, what happens, if we genuinely extend this situation and consider systems of linear invariant differential operators, e.g.
D
as a
q\times p
-matrix (
q,p\in\mathbb{N}
) of such linear invariant differential operators? Is it still (locally) solvable?
In case of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the questions have been answered completely by Malgrange
[
Mal61
,
Mal64
]
, Ehrenpreis (
[
Ehr61
]
and
[
Ehr70
, Chap. 6]
) and Palamodov
[
Pal63
,
Pal70
]
.
Already, here, the proof is much more complicated as for a single operator.

In Section
2
, we introduce a setting for the above problem for symmetric spaces
X
of non-compact type in terms of a conjecture, see Conjecture
1
.
Suitably interpreted, it also applies to the above example, with
X=\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
K=\{0\}
. Indeed, we view an invariant system of invariant differential equations as an invariant differential operator between complex homogeneous vector bundles.
The conjecture involves a second invariant differential operator
\widetilde{D}
satisfying
\widetilde{D}\circ D=0
as an integrability condition.
An overview on previously known results covering the conjecture is given at the end of Section
2
.

In Section
3
, we present a possible strategy to solve the conjecture.
The following diagram, which we try to explain in the sequel, pictures the strategy
and also the ‘jungle’ into which the reader is about to adventure.

2
The conjecture

2.1
Invariant differential operators on sections of homogeneous vector bundles

Let
G
be a real connected semi-simple Lie group with finite center of non-compact type.
Let
K\subset G
be a maximal compact subgroup.
The quotient
X=G/K
is a Riemannian symmetric space of non-compact type.
Let
\tau
be a finite dimensional representation of
K
on a complex vector space
E_{\tau}
.
Let
\mathbb{E}_{\tau}:=G\times_{K}E_{\tau}
be the
G
-homogeneous vector bundle over
X
induced by
E_{\tau}.

The space of its smooth sections is denoted by
C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
.
Note that
C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
carries a natural Fréchet topology and it is equipped with a smooth
G
-action.
Moreover, we have the following isomorphism of
G
-modules:

C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})\cong C^{\infty}(G,E_{\tau})^{K}\cong[C^{\infty}(G)\otimes E_{\tau}]^{K}.

Here,
W^{K}
denotes the space of
K
-invariants of a
K
-representation
W.

We also need the
G
-module of distribution sections of
\mathbb{E}_{\tau}

C^{-\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau}):=C^{\infty}_{c}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{\tau}})^{\prime}\cong[C^{-\infty}(G)\otimes E_{\tau}]^{K}\supset C^{\infty}(X,E_{\tau}),

where
(\tilde{\tau},E_{\tilde{\tau}})
is the dual representation of
(\tau,E_{\tau}).

We now consider an additional
K
-representation
(\gamma,E_{\gamma})
and its associated homogeneous vector bundle
\mathbb{E}_{\gamma}
over
X
.

Definition 1
.

A linear non-zero differential operator

D:C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\gamma})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})

between sections of homogeneous vector bundles is said to be
G
-invariant if

D(g\cdot f)=g\cdot(Df),\;\;\;\;\;\forall g\in G,f\in C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\gamma}).

We denote by
\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\gamma},\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
the vector space of all these
G
-invariant differential operators.

Let
D\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\gamma},\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
. As any differential operator, it extends canonically to a differential operator between distribution sections

D:C^{-\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\gamma})\longrightarrow C^{-\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau}).

This extension is again
G
-equivariant.
We look at distribution sections

C^{-\infty}_{\{0\}}(X,\mathbb{E}_{*})\subset C^{-\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{*}),\qquad*=\gamma,\tau,

supported at the origin
o:=eK\in X.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
\mathfrak{k}
) be the Lie algebra of the Lie group of
G
(resp.
K
) and
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
(resp.
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})
) be the universal enveloping algebra of complexification of
\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
\mathfrak{k}
).

C^{-\infty}_{\{0\}}(X,\mathbb{E}_{*})
carries compatible actions of
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
and
K
.
It consists of derivatives of (vector valued) delta distributions.
It follows that
C^{-\infty}_{\{0\}}(X,\mathbb{E}_{*})
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
-module consisting only of
K
-finite elements.
Shortly, it is a finitely generated
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module.
Moreover, it is canonically isomorphic to the
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module

\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{*}
, where the
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
-action is given by left multiplication on the first factor and
K
acts by
\mathrm{Ad}\otimes*
. More details are provided in
[
OlPa24
, Sect. 7]
.
Restriction from
C^{-\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\gamma})
to
C^{-\infty}_{\{0\}}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\gamma})
now induces
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-homomorphisms

D:C^{-\infty}_{\{0\}}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\gamma})\longrightarrow C^{-\infty}_{\{0\}}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})

and hence

D:\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\gamma}\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\tau}.

(2.1)

For any
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module
W
we have a natural isomorphism

\text{Hom}_{(\mathfrak{g},K)}(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{*},W)\cong\text{Hom}_{K}(E_{*},W),\quad\phi\mapsto h_{\phi},\quad h_{\phi}(v):=\phi(1\otimes v).

In particular, we obtain an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\gamma},\mathbb{E}_{\tau})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{K}(E_{\gamma},\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\tau})

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle h_{D}.

(2.2)

The injectivity of the linear map
D\mapsto h_{D}
is obvious, for a discussion of surjectivity we refer again to
[
OlPa24
, Sect. 7]
.
We remark that (
2.1
) gives an algebraic description of invariant differential operators which is closely related to the well-known isomorphism
[
KoRe00
]

\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\gamma},\mathbb{E}_{\tau})\cong[\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}\text{Hom}(E_{\gamma},E_{\tau})]^{K}.

However, it is not trivial to write down directly a well-defined linear map from
[\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}\text{Hom}(E_{\gamma},E_{\tau})]^{K}
to
\text{Hom}_{K}(E_{\gamma},\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\tau})
which gives a correct isomorphism, compare
[
Pa21
, Prop. 1.5.]
.

2.2
Statement of the conjecture

Let
D\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\gamma},\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
.
We wish to find another bundle
\mathbb{E}_{\delta}
and another operator
\widetilde{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\tau},\mathbb{E}_{\delta})
such that the equation
Df=g
is solvable in
C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\gamma})
if and only if
\widetilde{D}g=0
.

We look at (
2.1
) and consider the
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-submodule

\text{Ker}\;D\subset\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\gamma}
.
Since
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
is Noetherian
[
Wal88
, 0.6.1.]
,
\text{Ker}\;D
is again finitely generated as
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
-module. We choose a finite dimensional
K
-invariant generating subspace
E_{0}\subset\text{Ker}\;D
.
It carries a
K
-representation

\tau_{0}:K\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(E_{0}).

Then the embedding

i:\mathbb{E}_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\gamma}
belongs to
\text{Hom}_{K}(E_{0},\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\gamma})
.
By (
2.1
), the map
i
determines an invariant differential operator

D_{0}\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{\gamma})
with
h_{D_{0}}=i.

The image of the corresponding

(\mathfrak{g},K)
-map

D_{0}:\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{0}\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\gamma}

coincides with
\text{Ker}\;D
.
In other words, the sequence

\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle D_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle D}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\tau}

(2.3)

is exact, in particular

D\circ D_{0}=0.

However, since we want the opposite order, we need to dualize.
The dual or ‘adjoint’ of
D
is an invariant differential operator

D^{t}:C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{\tau}})\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{\gamma}}).

We view
D^{t}
as an operator

D^{t}:\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\tilde{\tau}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\tilde{\gamma}}

and
set
\widetilde{D}:=((D^{t})_{0})^{t}
, where
(D^{t})_{0}
is constructed as above with
\gamma,\tau
replaced by
\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{\tau}
.

(D^{t})_{0}
corresponds to a finite-dimensional
K
-invariant generating subspace
F_{0}\subset\text{Ker}(D^{t})\subset\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\tilde{\tau}}
.
Set
\mathbb{F}:=\widetilde{\mathbb{F}_{0}}
.
Then
\widetilde{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\tau},\mathbb{F})
and we have
(\widetilde{D}\circ D)^{t}=D^{t}\circ(D^{t})_{0}=0
.
Thus,

\widetilde{D}\circ D=0
on
C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\gamma})
, i.e.
\text{Im}(D)\subset\text{Ker}(\widetilde{D})
, viewed as a subspace of
C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
D\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\gamma},\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
and
\widetilde{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\tau},\mathbb{F})
be as above.

Then, the differential equation
Df=g
is solvable in
C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\gamma})
for given
g\in C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
if and only if
\widetilde{D}g=0
.
In other words, the sequence

C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\gamma})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle D}}{{\longrightarrow}}\underset{\text{exact}}{C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\widetilde{D}}}{{\longrightarrow}}C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{F})

(2.4)

is exact in the middle, i.e.
\emph{\text{Im}}(D)=\emph{\text{Ker}}(\widetilde{D})
.

Note that one implication is obvious, since
\widetilde{D}\circ D=0
implies that

\widetilde{D}g=\widetilde{D}\circ Df=0.

The following lemma implies in particular that the validity of Conj.
1
is independent of the choice of
\widetilde{D}
, i.e. the choice of the generating subspace
F_{0}.

Moreover, it says that
\widetilde{D}
gives the strongest possible integrability condition for the equation
Df=g
that can be formulated by invariant differential operators.

Lemma 1
.

Let
D\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\gamma},\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
and
\widetilde{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\tau},\mathbb{F})
be as in Conj.
1
.
Let
D_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\tau},\mathbb{E}_{\delta})
for some
K
-representation
(\delta,E_{\delta})
be such that
D_{1}\circ D=0.

Then there exists
P\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{E}_{\delta})
with
D_{1}=P\circ\widetilde{D}.

Proof.

The property
D_{1}\circ D=0
implies
\text{Im}(D_{1}^{t})\subset\text{Ker}(D^{t})
for the operators

D_{1}^{t}:\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\tilde{\delta}}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\tilde{\tau}}

and

D^{t}:\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\tilde{\tau}}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\tilde{\gamma}}
.
Recall that
\text{Ker}(D^{t})
coincides with the image of

\widetilde{D}^{t}:\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}F_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\tilde{\tau}}.

We choose a
K
-invariant complement

W\subset\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}F_{0}
of
\text{Ker}(\widetilde{D}^{t})
.
Sending
v\in E_{\tilde{\delta}}
to the unique
w\in W
with

D_{1}^{t}(1\otimes v)=\widetilde{D}^{t}(w)

defines an element
h\in\text{Hom}_{K}(E_{\tilde{\delta}},\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}F_{0}).

By (
2.1
),
h
is of the form
h_{Q}
for some
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{\delta}},\widetilde{\mathbb{F}}).

By construction
\widetilde{D}^{t}\circ Q=D_{1}^{t}.

Set
P:=Q^{t},
then
P\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{E}_{\delta})
and
D_{1}=P\circ\widetilde{D}.

∎

For many special cases, the conjecture is known to be true (but not in general):

•

Consider
\mathbb{E}_{p}=\wedge^{p}T^{*}X
(
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
) the exterior powers of the cotangent bundle
T^{*}X
, and

D=d_{p}:C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{p})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{p+1})
the exterior differential. Then,
\tilde{D}=d_{p+1}
.
By the Poincaré-Lemma (e.g. see
[
BoT82
, Sect. 4]
) the conjecture in this situation is true, even for any contractible manifold
X
.

•

Notice that if
\mathbb{E}_{\gamma}=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}=\mathbb{C}
are the trivial one-dimensional vector bundles on
X
and
D\neq 0
, then the transposed invariant differential operator
D^{t}
is injective on
C_{c}^{-\infty}(X,\mathbb{C})
.
This implies that
\widetilde{D}=0
, thus we have no ‘integrability’ condition.
Helgason’s result
[
Hel89
, Chap. V]
, mentioned in the introduction, shows that the conjecture is true in this case.

•

Also for elliptic operators between sections of general bundles, we have
\widetilde{D}=0
.
In this case, the conjecture follows from a result by Malgrange
[
Mal56
, p. 341]
, saying in particular that every elliptic operator with analytic coefficients on a non-compact analytic manifold is surjective.

•

Furthermore, the Euclidean analogue of the conjecture is true by the result of Ehrenpreis (
[
Ehr61
]
and
[
Ehr70
, Chap. 6]
),
Malgrange
[
Mal61
,
Mal64
]
and Palamodov
[
Pal63
,
Pal70
]
mentioned in the introduction.
One of the first written proofs appeared in Hörmander
[
Hör73
, Thm. 7.6.13 and Thm. 7.6.14]
.
Note that the first edition of Hörmander’s book appeared already in 1966.
For this proof, Hörmander invented new
L^{2}
-methods.
The result in the Euclidean case is known as a part of the Ehrenpreis Fundamental Principle.

•

An extension of Ehrenpreis’s fundamental principle to symmetric spaces is discussed by Oshima, Saburi and Wakayama
[
OSW91
]
.
In particular, they annonced that the conjecture is true if

\mathbb{E}_{\gamma}=\mathbb{C}^{p},\mathbb{E}_{\tau}=\mathbb{C}^{q}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\delta}=\mathbb{C}^{r}
,
p,q,r\in\mathbb{N}
, with trivial
K
-representations.

•

Another important result is a consequence of the theory of Kashiwara-Schmid
[
KS94
,
Ka08
]
on maximal globalizations of Harish-Chandra modules.
Let
D\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\gamma},\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
.
We look at the operator

D^{t}:\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\tilde{\tau}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{\tilde{\gamma}}.

If
D^{t}
occurs in a projective resolution

\dots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P_{s+1}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{s+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P_{s}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P_{s-1}}}{{\rightarrow}}\dots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P_{0}}}{{\rightarrow}}\;\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})}E_{0}\rightarrow W\rightarrow 0,

(2.5)

i.e.
D^{t}=P_{s}
for some
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, of a Harish-Chandra module
W
(e.g. an irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module W), then the conjecture is true for
D
.
This result, in more abstract language, is announced in
[
KS94
]
.
The full proof appeared in
[
Ka08
]
and is based on the theory of
\mathcal{D}
-modules.
It is one of the motivations of the present paper to find a ‘more elementary’ proof of this result based on the Fourier transform.
Note also that not all invariant differential operators
D
satisfy
D^{t}=P_{s}
for some resolution (
2.5
) as above.

3
On solvability and general strategy

3.1
Topological Paley-Wiener-Schwartz theorems and intertwining conditions

Let us fix a
G
-invariant Riemannian metric on
X
.
Let
(\tau,E_{\tau})
be a finite-dimensional representation of
K
. The corresponding closed ball of radius
r
centered at the origin
o
is defined by
\overline{B}_{r}(o)
.
We consider the space of compactly supported distributional sections

C^{-\infty}_{c}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})=\bigcup_{r\geq 0}C^{-\infty}_{r}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
,
where

C^{-\infty}_{r}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau}):=\{f\in C^{-\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})\;|\;\text{supp}(f)\subset\overline{B}_{r}(o)\}.

In context of the Fourier transform, we refer to this situation as (
Level 2
).
For a second finite-dimensional
K
-representation, we also consider the space of compactly supported
(\gamma,\tau)
-spherical distributions

C^{-\infty}_{c}(G,\gamma,\tau)=\bigcup_{r\geq 0}C^{-\infty}_{r}(G,\gamma,\tau)
.
Here,

\displaystyle C^{-\infty}_{r}(G,\gamma,\tau):=\{S\in C^{-\infty}(G,\text{Hom}(E_{\gamma},E_{\tau})\;|\;l_{k_{1}}r_{k_{2}}S=\tau(k_{2})^{-1}S\gamma(k_{1})\;\forall k_{1},k_{2}\in K,

\displaystyle\text{ and }\text{supp}(S)\subset p^{-1}(\overline{B}_{r}(o))\},

where
l_{k_{1}},r_{k_{2}}
are the left and right regular representations, respectively, and

p:G\rightarrow X
is the natural projection.
Note that
C^{-\infty}_{r}(G,\gamma,\tau)\cong\text{Hom}_{K}(E_{\gamma},C^{-\infty}_{r}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau}))
.
In particular, for irreducible
\gamma
,
C^{-\infty}_{r}(G,\gamma,\tau)
is just the (infinite-dimensional) multiplicity space for the occurrence of
\gamma
in the
K
-representation
C^{-\infty}_{r}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
.
We refer to this situation as (
Level 3
).
The missing (
Level 1
) corresponds to
C^{\pm\infty}_{c}(G)
, where no
K
-representations are involved. It stands in the background of the theory (see
[
OlPa24
,
OlPa23
]
) but will play no role in the present paper.

We fix an Iwasawa decomposition
G=KAN
.
Let
M:=Z_{K}(A)
be the centralizer of
A
in
K
.
Now we come to the Fourier transform.

Definition 2

(Fourier transform for sections of homogeneous vector bundles)
.

Let
g=\kappa(g)a(g)n(g)\in KAN=G
be the Iwasawa decomposition of
g\in G
.
We define the exponential function

e^{\tau}_{\lambda,k}:G\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(E_{\tau}),\quad\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}},k\in K,

by

e^{\tau}_{\lambda,k}(g):=\tau(\kappa(g^{-1}k))^{-1}a(g^{-1}k)^{-(\lambda+\rho)}\in\emph{\text{End}}(E_{\tau}),

where
\rho
is the half sum of the positive roots of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
counted with multiplicity.

•

(
Level 2
)
For
T\in C^{-\infty}_{c}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
, the Fourier transform is given by

\mathcal{F}_{\tau}T(\lambda,k):=\langle T,e^{\tau}_{\lambda,k}\rangle\in E_{\tau},\;\;\;(\lambda,k)\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}\times K/M.

•

(
Level 3
)
For
S\in C^{-\infty}_{c}(G,\gamma,\tau)
, the Fourier transform is defined by

\prescript{}{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau}S(\lambda):=\langle S,e^{\tau}_{\lambda,1}\rangle\in\emph{\text{Hom}}_{M}(\mathbb{E}_{\gamma},\mathbb{E}_{\tau}),\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}.

We fix a basis
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
of
\mathfrak{k}
. Then
Y_{\alpha}=Y_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots Y_{\alpha_{n}},\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})
a multi-index, form basis of
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k})
.
We also introduce

C^{\infty}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}\times K/M,\mathbb{E}_{\tau|_{M}}):=\{f:\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}\times K\stackrel{{\scriptstyle C^{\infty}}}{{\longrightarrow}}E_{\tau}\;|\;f(\lambda,km)=\tau(m)^{-1}f(\lambda,k)\}.

The Riemannian metric on
X
induces Euclidean norms
|\cdot|
on
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Definition 3

(Paley-Wiener-Schwartz space for sections in (
Level 2
) and (
Level 3
),
[
OlPa24
]
, Def. 10)
.

(a)

For
r\geq 0
, let
PWS_{\tau,r}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}\times K/M)
be the space of sections

\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}\times K/M,\mathbb{E}_{\tau|_{M}})
such that:

(2.i)

The section
\psi
is holomorphic in
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

(2.iis)_{r}

(growth condition) For all multi-indices
\alpha
, there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and a positive constant
C_{r,N,\alpha}
such that

\|l_{Y_{\alpha}}\psi(\lambda,k)\|_{E_{\tau}}\leq C_{r,N,\alpha}(1+|\lambda|^{2})^{N+\frac{|\alpha|}{2}}e^{r|\emph{\text{Re}}(\lambda)|},\;\;\;k\in K,

where
\|\cdot\|_{E_{\tau}}
is a
K
-invariant Euclidean norm on the finite-dimensional vector space
E_{\tau}
.

(2.iii)

The intertwining conditions in
[
OlPa24
, Thm. 2 (D.2)]
are satisfied.

We set
PWS_{\tau}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}\times K/M):=\bigcup_{r\geq 0,N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}PWS_{\tau,r,N}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}\times K/M)

equipped with its natural inductive limit topology.

(b)

By considering an additional
K
-representation
\gamma
, let
\prescript{}{\gamma}{PWS}_{\tau,r}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}})
be the space of functions

\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}\ni\lambda\mapsto\varphi(\lambda)\in\emph{\text{Hom}}_{M}(E_{\gamma},E_{\tau})

such that:

(3.i)

The function
\varphi
is holomorphic in
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

(3.iis)_{r}

(growth condition) There exist
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and a positive constant
C_{r,N}
such that

\|\varphi(\lambda)\|_{\text{op}}\leq C_{r,N}(1+|\lambda|^{2})^{N}e^{r|\emph{\text{Re}}(\lambda)|},

where
\|\cdot\|_{\text{op}}
denotes the operator norm on
\emph{\text{Hom}}_{M}(E_{\gamma},E_{\tau})
.

(3.iii)

The intertwining conditions in
[
OlPa24
, Thm. 2 (D.3)]
are satisfied.

We set
\prescript{}{\gamma}{PWS}_{\tau}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}):=\bigcup_{r\geq 0,N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prescript{}{\gamma}{PWS}_{\tau,r,N}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}})

equipped with its natural inductive limit topology.

We do not write the intertwining conditions explicitly here.
We only mention that each intertwining condition comes from a
G
-invariant subspace in a finite sum of (derived) principal series representations of
G
.
Such a subspace is called an intertwining datum.
The most important intertwining data are just proper submodules in a single reducible principal series representation and intertwining operators between two principal series representations.
Each intertwining datum gives a linear relation between the germs of
\psi
(
Level 2
) or
\phi
(
Level 3
) at finitely many points
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s}\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
depending only at the values and possibly finitely many derivatives of
\psi,\varphi
at
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s}
.

By considering the Knapp-Stein and Želobenko intertwining operators, we developed a criterion for real rank one
[
OlPa23
, Thm. 2]
to check when a subset of these intertwining conditions is already sufficient in order to describe the Paley-Wiener-Schwartz space completely.
In particular, for
\mathbf{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})^{d}
(
d\in\mathbb{N}
) and
\mathbf{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
, we obtained explicitly the intertwining conditions in (
Level 2
) and (
Level 3
). These results will be exposed in the upcoming Sections
4
and
5
.

For now, let us present the topological Paley-Wiener-Schwartz theorem, which has already been proved in
[
OlPa24
]
.
We equip the vector spaces
C^{-\infty}_{c}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
as well as
C^{-\infty}_{c}(G,\gamma,\tau)
with the strong dual topology.

Theorem 1

(Topological Paley-Wiener-Schwartz theorem,
[
OlPa24
]
, Thm. 3)
.

(a)

For each
r\geq 0
, the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
is a linear
bijection between the two spaces
C^{-\infty}_{r}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
and the Paley-Wiener-Schwartz space
PWS_{\tau,r}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}\times K/M)
.
Moreover, it is a linear topological isomorphism from
C^{-\infty}_{c}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
onto
PWS_{\tau}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}\times K/M)
.

(b)

Similarly, if we consider an additional finite-dimensional
K
-representation
(\gamma,E_{\gamma})
with associated homogeneous vector bundle
\mathbb{E}_{\gamma}
.
Then, the Fourier transform
\prescript{}{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau}
is a linear
bijection between the two spaces

C^{-\infty}_{r}(G,\gamma,\tau)
and
\prescript{}{\gamma}{PWS}_{\tau,r}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}})
, for each
r\geq 0
, and a linear topological isomorphism from
C_{c}^{-\infty}(G,\gamma,\tau)
onto
\prescript{}{\gamma}{PWS}_{\tau}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

\square

Note that
C^{-\infty}_{r=0}(G,\gamma,\tau)\cong\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\gamma},\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
.
Thus, we can apply the Fourier transform to invariant differential operators. Then, Thm.
1
implies
[
OlPa24
, Prop. 9]
:

\displaystyle\prescript{}{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau}(\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\gamma},\mathbb{E}_{\tau}))

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\prescript{}{\gamma}{PWS}_{\tau,0}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}})

(3.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{P\in\text{Pol}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}},\text{Hom}_{M}(E_{\gamma},E_{\tau}))\;|\;P\text{ satisfies }(3.iii)\text{ of Def.\penalty\ \ref{def:PWSspace}}\}.

In addition, we have the following result.

Proposition 1

(
[
OlPa24
]
, Prop. 10)
.

Let
D\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(\mathbb{E}_{\gamma},\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
be an invariant linear differential operator.
For

we have that

\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(Df)(\lambda,k)=\prescript{}{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau}D(\lambda)\mathcal{F}_{{\gamma}}f(\lambda,k),\;\;\;\;\;\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}},k\in K,

where
\prescript{}{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau}D\in\emph{\text{Pol}}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}},\emph{\text{Hom}}_{M}(E_{\gamma},E_{\tau}))
is a polynomial in
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
with values in
\emph{\text{Hom}}_{M}(E_{\gamma},E_{\tau})
.

\square

Note that
\overline{\chi}_{\mu}(k)=\chi_{\mu}(k^{-1})

\{\tilde{e}_{i},i=1,\dots,d_{\mu}\}

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{\mu\in\widehat{K}}d_{\mu}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\mu}}\int_{K}\langle\mu(k^{-1})e_{i},\tilde{e}_{i}\rangle f(k^{-1}x)\;dk

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\in\widehat{K}}d_{\mu}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\mu}}\int_{K}\langle\mu(kx^{-1})e_{i},\tilde{e}_{i}\rangle f(k)\;dk

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\in\widehat{K}}d_{\mu}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\mu}}\int_{K}f(k)\tilde{\mu}(k^{-1})\tilde{e}_{i}\;dk\;\mu(x^{-1})e_{i}.

\prescript{}{\mu}{f}_{i}:=\int_{K}f(k)\tilde{\mu}(k^{-1})\tilde{e}_{i}\;dk

(3.3)

(3.4)

are the Fourier coefficients.

Lemma 2
.

Fix

and

.
Let
\prescript{}{\mu}{w}_{i}\in\prescript{}{\mu}{PWS}_{\tau}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}),\mu\in\widehat{K},i=1,\dots,d_{\mu}

Proof.

For

\displaystyle\|\prescript{}{\mu}{w}_{i}(\lambda)\|_{\text{op}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Fouriercoeffw}}}{{\leq}}\int_{K}\|w(\lambda,k)\tilde{\mu}(k^{-1})\tilde{e}_{i}\|_{\text{op}}\;dk=\int_{K}|w(\lambda,k)|\;dk\leq\sup_{k\in K}|w(\lambda,k)|=:\|w(\lambda,\cdot)\|_{K,\infty}.

3.3
On closed range and density

We want to show that
\text{Im}(D)=\text{Ker}(\widetilde{D})
, which means that
D
has a closed and dense range in
\text{Ker}(\widetilde{D})\subset C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{E}_{\tau})
.
These properties will follow from analogous properties of the transposed operators
D^{t}
and
\widetilde{D}^{t}
, respectively.
We treat the density and closedness separately.
We start with density.
Let us recall some notions and important results from functional analysis.
We consider a continuous linear operator

A:V_{1}\longrightarrow V_{2}
between locally convex Hausdorff vector spaces and its adjoint
A^{t}:V^{\prime}_{2}\rightarrow V^{\prime}_{1}.

For a linear subspace
W\subset V
, where
V
is a locally convex Hausdorff vector space, we consider its annihilator
W^{\perp}\subset V^{\prime}
given by

W^{\perp}:=\{\tilde{v}\in V^{\prime}\;|\;\langle\tilde{v},w\rangle=0,\;\forall w\in W\}.

Similarly, for
\widetilde{W}\subset V^{\prime}
a linear subspace,

\widetilde{W}^{\perp}:=\{v\in V\;|\;\langle\tilde{w},v\rangle=0,\;\forall\tilde{w}\in\widetilde{W}\}.

Let
V^{\prime}_{\sigma}
be
V^{\prime}
equipped with the weak-* topology.
It is well-known that
(V^{\prime}_{\sigma})^{\prime}=V
(as vector spaces) and hence by the Hahn-Banach theorem

(\widetilde{W}^{\perp})^{\perp}=\overline{\widetilde{W}}\;\;\text{ (weak-* closure)}.

We can now easily derive the following well-known elementary results.

Lemma 3
.

With the notations above, we have

(1)

\emph{\text{Im}}(A)^{\perp}=\emph{\text{Ker}}(A^{t})
.

(2)

\emph{\text{Ker}}(A)^{\perp}=\overline{\emph{\text{Im}}(A^{t})}
(weak-* closure).

\square

Lemma 4
.

We consider two operators between locally convex Hausdorff vector spaces

V_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\longrightarrow}}V_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B}}{{\longrightarrow}}V_{3}

with
B\circ A=0
, i.e.
\emph{\text{Im}}(A)\subset\emph{\text{Ker}}(B)
.

Then,
\emph{\text{Im}}(A)\subset\emph{\text{Ker}}(B)
is dense if and only if
\emph{\text{Im}}(B^{t})\subset\emph{\text{Ker}}(A^{t})
is weak-* dense.

Proof.

By the Hahn-Banach theorem, we have that
\text{Im}(A)\subset\text{Ker}(B)
is dense if and only if
\text{Im}(A)^{\perp}=\text{Ker}(B)^{\perp}
.
Hence by applying Lem.
3
, this is equivalent to
\text{Ker}(A^{t})=\overline{\text{Im}(B^{t})}
, i.e.

\text{Im}(B^{t})\subset\text{Ker}(A^{t})
is weak-* dense.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Assume that
\emph{\text{Im}}(B^{t})\subset\emph{\text{Ker}}(A^{t})
is dense in the strong dual topology.
Then,
\emph{\text{Im}}(A)\subset\emph{\text{Ker}}(B)
is dense.

Proof.

Since strong density implies weak-* density, we directly obtain the result by applying Lem.
4
.
∎

Remark 1

If

is (semi-)reflexive, then we even get an equivalence in Cor.

by the Hahn-Banach theorem.

In the proof of the theorem, we will use the following standard result.

Lemma 7

Let
p(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{k}a_{n}z^{n}
be a polynomial in one variable such that the leading coefficient
a_{k}
is not zero.
For
r>1
, let
f
be a holomorphic function on
B_{r}(0)\subset\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}

Then

|f(0)|\leq{|a_{k}|}^{-1}\sup_{|z|=1}|f(z)p(z)|.

(4.7)

Here,
B_{r}(0)
denotes an open ball of radius
r
centered at
0

\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Note that (
4.7

|f(\lambda)|\leq C_{p}\sup_{|z|=1}|f(\lambda+z)p(\lambda+z)|,\;\;\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}},

(4.8)

where the constant

depends on the polynomial
p
, but not on
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}

Proof of Lem.
7
.

Set
q(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\overline{a}_{k-n}z^{n}
. The product
q\cdot f
is holomorphic on
B_{r}(0)
. By the maximum principle for
B_{1}(0)
, we have

|q(0)f(0)|\leq\sup_{|z|=1}|q(z)f(z)|.

Note that
q(0)=\overline{a}_{k}
.
If
|z|=1
, then we obtain

\overline{p(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\overline{a}_{n}\overline{z}^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\overline{a}_{n}z^{-n}=z^{-k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{a}_{n}z^{k-n}=z^{-k}q(z).

Therefore, for
|z|=1
, we have
|p(z)|=|q(z)|,
and thus the lemma follows.

Before we start to prove Thm.
11
, we need to adapt Lem.
7
for our situation.

Consider a polynomial
p
in two variables
z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
.
It can be decomposed into homogeneous components

p=\sum_{l=0}^{k}p_{k},
where
p_{k}\neq 0.

For all
s\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
, we have

p_{l}(s\cdot z)=s^{l}p_{l}(z),\;\;\forall l=0,\dots,k.

(5.3)

If
p_{k}\neq 0
, then there exists
v\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\}
such that
p_{k}(v)\neq 0
and
|v|=1
.
Fix now
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
and let
p_{\lambda}:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be defined by

p_{\lambda}(z):=p(\lambda+z\cdot v).

Lemma 8
.

The polynomial
p_{\lambda}
is of degree
k
with largest coefficient
a_{k}=a_{k,\lambda}\neq 0
and independent of
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
.
In fact,
a_{k}=p_{k}(v)
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle a_{k}=\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}z^{-k}p_{\lambda}(z)=\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}z^{-k}\sum_{l=0}^{k}p_{l}(\lambda+z\cdot v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}z^{-k}\sum_{l=0}^{k}p_{l}\Big(z\Big(\frac{\lambda}{z}+v\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:rulepsz}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{k}z^{l-k}p_{l}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{z}+v\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{k}(v).\qed

Now, let
f
be an entire function on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
Set
f_{\lambda}(z):=f(\lambda+z\cdot v)
.
By Lem.
7
, we get

|f_{\lambda}(0)|\leq\frac{1}{|a_{k}|}\sup_{|z|=1}|p_{\lambda}(z)f_{\lambda}(z)|
, i.e.

|f(\lambda)|\leq\underbrace{\frac{1}{|a_{k}|}}_{=:C_{p}}\sup_{|z|=1}|p(\lambda+z\cdot v)f(\lambda+z\cdot v)|\leq C_{p}\sup_{\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{2},|\mu|\leq 1}|p(\lambda+\mu)f(\lambda+\mu)|,

(5.4)

where the constant
C_{p}
depends on the highest homogeneous part of
p
.

Proof of Thm.
11
.

We decompose
E_{\tau}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{d_{\tau}}E_{n_{j}}
and
E_{\gamma}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d_{\gamma}}E_{m_{i}}
into irreducible ones.
Note that
n_{j},m_{i}
are now tuples of integers.
Then
\prescript{}{l}{u}
has components

,
\prescript{}{l}{w}:=P\prescript{}{l}{u}
has components
\prescript{}{l}{w}_{i}\in\prescript{}{l}{PWS}_{m_{i}}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}})
, and
P
is given by a matrix

Then

\displaystyle\prescript{}{l}{u}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prescript{}{l}{h_{j}}\cdot q_{l,n_{j}}

\displaystyle P_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{ij}\cdot q_{n_{j},m_{i}}

\displaystyle\prescript{}{l}{w}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prescript{}{l}{b}\cdot q_{l,m_{i}}

for some

\prescript{}{l}{h}_{j},\prescript{}{l}{b}_{i}\in\text{Hol}(\lambda_{1}^{2},\lambda_{2}^{2})
and
a_{ij}\in\text{Pol}(\lambda_{1}^{2},\lambda_{2}^{2})
.
We want to find
\prescript{}{l}{v}_{j}\in\prescript{}{l}{PWS}_{n_{j}}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}})
so that
P\prescript{}{l}{v}=\prescript{}{l}{w},
and to estimate
\prescript{}{l}{v}
in terms of
\prescript{}{l}{w}
.
As in (
4.3
), we define polynomials

by

We set
\prescript{}{l}{\tilde{a}_{ij}}:=a_{ij}r^{l}_{n_{j},m_{i}}
and obtain

\widetilde{A}:=(\prescript{}{l}{\tilde{a}_{ij}})_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\dots,d_{\gamma}\\
j=1,\dots,d_{\tau}\end{subarray}}.

Hörmander’s estimate

tells us that there exist constants

with

for some

,

, one can find

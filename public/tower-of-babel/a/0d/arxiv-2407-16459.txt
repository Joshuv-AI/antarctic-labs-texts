Hasse principle for intersections of two quadrics via Kummer surfaces
Adam Morgan, Alexei Skorobogatov

Abstract
We prove new cases of the Hasse principle for Kummer surfaces constructed from 2-coverings of Jacobians of genus 2 curves, assuming finiteness of relevant Tate–Shafarevich groups. Under the same assumption, we deduce the Hasse principle for quartic del Pezzo surfaces with trivial Brauer group and irreducible or completely split characteristic polynomial, hence the Hasse principle for smooth complete intersections of two quadrics in the projective space of dimension at least 5.



Introduction

The study of the Hasse principle for geometrically rational varieties has a long history.
Fundamental contributions to this subject were made by Manin who introduced the Brauer–Manin obstruction,
and by Colliot-Thélène, Sansuc and Swinnerton-Dyer who pioneered the use of descent and
fibration methods.

By the Castelnuovo–Manin–Iskovskikh classification, smooth and projective
geometrically rational surfaces
that are minimal in the sense of birational geometry (every birational morphism
onto a smooth surface must be an isomorphism) fall into two families:
conic bundles and del Pezzo surfaces
[
Isk79
, Theorem 1]
.
The existence of a conic fibration greatly simplifies the study of rational points,
essentially because universal torsors over conic bundle surfaces have a simple
description in terms of certain complete intersections of quadrics.
As a result, under some assumptions on the degrees of singular closed fibres of the conic fibration,
one can prove that the Brauer–Manin
obstruction is the only obstruction to the Hasse principle and weak approximation
(see the survey
[
CT92
]
,
[
Sko01
, §4.4 and Chapter 7]
,
[
BMS14
]
).
By contrast, no unconditional results of this kind are known for del Pezzo
surfaces without a conic fibration, if we exclude the well understood case of del
Pezzo surfaces of degree larger than
4
.
The most interesting open cases are smooth cubic surfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}_{k}

and quartic del Pezzo surfaces, which are smooth intersections of two quadrics in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}_{k}
.
(Universal torsors over arbitrary del Pezzo surfaces were described in
[
SS07
]
, but
this description looks too difficult to work with directly.)

Attempts were made to prove
the Hasse principle conditionally on the conjecture of Tate and Shafarevich about finiteness
of the Tate–Shafarevich group of abelian varieties.
This is known for many elliptic curves and for some higher-dimensional
abelian varieties. A conceptual reason comes from a theorem of Manin which says
that the Brauer–Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to the Hasse principle
on torsors for an abelian variety provided its Tate–Shafarevich group is finite, see
[
Sko01
, Theorem 6.2.3]
. Thus it seems reasonable
to build on this conjecture when dealing with surfaces that do not have a pencil of rational curves.

For
diagonal
cubic surfaces with coefficients
satisfying certain divisibility properties, Swinnerton-Dyer proved
the Hasse principle conditionally on finiteness of the Tate–Shafarevich group of
elliptic curves
[
SD01
]
. He crucially used the geometric fact that any diagonal cubic surface
is birational to a quotient of a torsor for an abelian surface, namely the product of two diagonal cubic curves.
Extending this approach, Swinnerton-Dyer’s method was developed (and later much simplified)
to prove the Hasse principle for many
Kummer surfaces constructed from 2-coverings of abelian surfaces, assuming finiteness of the relevant
Tate–Shafarevich groups, see
[
SSD05
,
HS16
,
Har19
,
Mor25
]
.

In his thesis, Wittenberg developed an earlier version of this method
applicable to surfaces with a pencil of genus 1 curves
[
CSS98a
]
to
prove the Hasse principle for many classes of quartic del Pezzo surfaces, including the most general case
[
Wit07
, Théorème 3.2]
. In addition to the Tate–Shafarevich conjecture
he had to assume Schinzel’s Hypothesis (H), a notoriously difficult conjecture on
prime values of polynomials, deemed to be inaccessible in the current state of analytic number theory.

In this paper we return to the philosophy of
[
SD01
]
. We study the Hasse principle
on quartic del Pezzo surfaces using their representation as quotients of Kummer surfaces
which are themselves quotients of torsors for the Jacobians of genus 2 curves.

For a quartic del Pezzo surface
X

represented as the zero set of two quadratic forms in 5 variables
\Phi_{1}=\Phi_{2}=0
, one calls

\det(\Phi_{1}-t\Phi_{2})\in k[t]
the
characteristic polynomial
of
X
.
Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem A

Let
k
be a number field. Assume that for every genus
2
curve
over every finite extension of
k
the
2
-primary torsion subgroup of the
Tate–Shafarevich group of its Jacobian is finite.
Then the quartic del Pezzo surfaces
X
over
k
whose characteristic polynomial is irreducible, or whose characteristic polynomial is
completely split and the natural map
{\rm{Br}}(k)\to{\rm{Br}}(X)
is surjective, satisfy the Hasse principle.

To prove Theorem A, we use a new geometric idea that allows us to reduce the study
of rational points on
quartic del Pezzo surfaces to Kummer surfaces attached to 2-coverings of Jacobians of genus 2 curves,
by exploiting a relation between these surfaces spelled out in
[
Sko10
]
.
Namely, we show that the projectivised tangent bundle
of a quartic del Pezzo surface
X
is birationally equivalent to a family
of Kummer surfaces over
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
, each of which is a double cover of
X
.
Although classical in spirit, this construction appears to be new.
Applying the fibration
theorem for zero-cycles of Harpaz and Wittenberg
[
HW16
, Theorem 6.2]
to this family, we
prove the main unconditional result of this paper, Theorem
3.5
.
It says that the Hasse principle with Brauer–Manin obstruction for
X
over
k
follows from
the Hasse principle with algebraic Brauer–Manin obstruction
for Kummer surfaces defined over finite extensions of
k
of odd degree and satisfying
certain local conditions. In the proof, one obtains a closed point of odd degree on
X
, but
since
X
is an intersection of two quadrics, a theorem of Amer–Brumer
implies that
X
has a
k
-point.
The required results on the existence of rational points on Kummer surfaces are proved
conditionally on finiteness of the Tate–Shafarevich groups of Jacobians of genus 2 curves.
In the completely split case we use that the algebraic Brauer–Manin obstruction is
the only obstruction to the Hasse principle on the relevant
Kummer surfaces, as proved by Harpaz in
[
Har19
, Theorem 1.3]
.
In the irreducible case, we establish the Hasse principle for the relevant Kummer surfaces in Theorem
3.8
, which we prove by extending the technique
of
[
HS16
,
Har19
,
Mor25
]
using work of the first named author
[
Mor19
,
Mor23
]
.

Although in this paper we only deal with quartic del Pezzo surfaces with
trivial Brauer group, our method is applicable to arbitrary quartic del Pezzo surfaces.
Instances where the Brauer group is non-trivial will be the subject of a forthcoming work of
Julian Lyczak and the first named author.

The following statement is a consequence of Theorem A, see
[
Wit07
, §3.5.2]
for
this implication.

Theorem B

Let
k
be a number field. Assume that
\textcyr{Sh}({\rm Jac}(C))[2^{\infty}]
is finite
for every genus
2
curve
C
over every finite extension of
k
. Then the
smooth complete intersections of two quadrics in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{k}
,
n\geq 5
, satisfy the Hasse principle.

Previously, this was known under the assumption of finiteness of the Tate–Shafarevich
groups of elliptic curves over number fields and Schinzel’s Hypothesis (H), see

[
Wit07
, Théorème 3.3]
. The best unconditional result in this direction is
due to Heath-Brown
[
HB18
]

who proved that smooth complete intersections of two quadrics in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{k}

satisfy the Hasse principle for
n\geq 7
. See
[
CT24
]
for a different proof.

We briefly outline the structure of the paper. Section
1
contains various preliminary results.
In Section
2
we introduce the main geometric construction of the paper, namely
a stable birational equivalence between a quartic del Pezzo surface
X

and a threefold
Z
fibred into Kummer surfaces over
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
. Using a natural
realisation of
Z/{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
inside a fibration into 3-dimensional Severi–Brauer
varieties, we show the triviality of the vertical Brauer group of
Z/{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
. This result
is not used in the proof of Theorem A, but it is interesting in its own right.
In Section
3
we deduce the Hasse principle with Brauer–Manin obstruction for
X
from
the Hasse principle with algebraic Brauer–Manin obstruction of a special type
for certain closed fibres of
Z/{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
, see Theorem
3.5
for a precise statement.
This reduces Theorem A (=Theorem
3.9
) to
the relevant cases of the Hasse principle for Kummer surfaces,
see Theorem
3.8
. Section
4
contains the proof of Theorem
3.8
.

Preliminaries

1.1
Algebraic notation

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero
with algebraic closure
\bar{k}
and absolute Galois group
\Gamma={\rm{Gal}}(\bar{k}/k)
.

For a variety
X
over
k
we write
{\rm{Br}}(X)
for the cohomological Brauer group

{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
.
We use the standard notation
{\rm{Br}}_{0}(X)
for the image of the natural map
{\rm{Br}}(k)\to{\rm{Br}}(X)

and
{\rm{Br}}_{1}(X)
for the kernel of the natural map
{\rm{Br}}(X)\to{\rm{Br}}(X_{\bar{k}})
.

Let
{\rm S}_{5}
be the group of permutations of
\{1,2,3,4,5\}
.
Define the
{\rm S}_{5}
-module
G
as the zero-sum submodule of the permutation module

\mathbb{F}_{2}[{\rm S}_{5}/{\rm S}_{4}]\simeq({\mathbb{F}}_{2})^{5}
. Since
5
is odd, we have a direct sum decomposition of
{\rm S}_{5}
-modules

\mathbb{F}_{2}[{\rm S}_{5}/{\rm S}_{4}]\cong G\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
.
It is clear that
G
is a faithful
{\rm S}_{5}
-module.

In all of this paper,
P(t)
is a monic separable polynomial in
k[t]
of degree
5
. It gives rise
to an étale
k
-algebra
A=k[t]/(P(t))
and an embedding of the
k
-scheme

\Sigma={\rm{Spec}}(A)
into the affine line
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k}
. We have
A=\oplus_{\sigma\in\Sigma}k_{\sigma}
,
where
k_{\sigma}
is the residue field of a closed point
\sigma\in\Sigma
.

Let
{\rm{Gal}}(P)
be the Galois group of
P(t)
,
and let
\varphi:\Gamma\to{\rm{Gal}}(P)
be the natural surjection.
The action of
{\rm{Gal}}(P)
on the roots of
P(t)
gives rise to an embedding of

{\rm{Gal}}(P)
into
{\rm S}_{5}
(defined up to conjugation).
The Galois group
\Gamma
acts on
G
via
\varphi
, making
G
a finite commutative group

k
-scheme. Namely, we have

G\cong R_{A/k}^{1}(\mu_{2}):={\rm{Ker}}[R_{A/k}(\mu_{2})\to\mu_{2}],

where
R_{A/k}
is the Weil restriction of scalars and the map is induced by the norm map
A\to k
.
Since
{\rm{dim}}_{k}(A)=5
is odd, the exact sequence

1\to G\to R_{A/k}(\mu_{2})\to\mu_{2}\to 1

is split, giving an isomorphism

{\rm H}^{1}(k,G)\cong(A^{\times}/A^{\times 2})_{1}=\big(\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma}k_{\sigma}^{\times}/k_{\sigma}^{\times 2}\big)_{1},

where the subscript 1 denotes the subgroup of elements of norm
1\in k^{\times}/k^{\times 2}
.
Note that there is also an isomorphism
G\cong R_{A/k}(\mu_{2})/\mu_{2}
, which induces
an isomorphism
{\rm H}^{1}(k,G)\cong A^{\times}/(k^{\times}A^{\times 2})
.

The fundamental datum in this paper is a pair
(P(t),\delta)
, where
\delta\in{\rm H}^{1}(k,G)
.
We have
\delta=(\delta_{\sigma})
, where

\delta_{\sigma}\in k_{\sigma}^{\times}/k_{\sigma}^{\times 2}
are such that

\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma}{\rm N}_{k_{\sigma}/k}(\delta_{\sigma})=1\in k^{\times}/k^{\times 2}.

We choose a lifting
\delta^{\prime}\in A^{\times}
of
\delta
such that

{\rm N}_{A/k}(\delta^{\prime})\in k^{\times 2}
and define

\Delta:={\rm{Spec}}(A[x]/(x^{2}-\delta^{\prime})).

It is clear that the isomorphism class of
\Delta

does not depend on the lifting
\delta^{\prime}\in A^{\times}
of
\delta
.
The obvious morphism of
k
-schemes
\Delta\to\Sigma

gives rise to a
\Gamma
-equivariant 2-to-1 map
\Delta(\bar{k})\to\Sigma(\bar{k})
. We choose a set-theoretic section
s:\Sigma(\bar{k})\to\Delta(\bar{k})
to this map. This identifies the group of permutations of the 10-element set
\Delta(\bar{k})
that respect
the map
\Delta(\bar{k})\to\Sigma(\bar{k})
with the semidirect product
({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{5}\rtimes{\rm S}_{5}
. The group
k
-scheme
R_{A/k}(\mu_{2})
acts faithfully on
\Delta
preserving the fibres
of
\Delta\to\Sigma
, thus
R_{A/k}(\mu_{2})(\bar{k})
is naturally identified with
({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{5}
in

({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{5}\rtimes{\rm S}_{5}
. It follows that
G(\bar{k})=R_{A/k}^{1}(\mu_{2})(\bar{k})

is identified with the zero sum subgroup
({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{4}\subset({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{5}
.

For each
r\in\Sigma(\bar{k})
, define a function
\tilde{\delta}_{r}:\Gamma\to\{\pm 1\}
by setting
\tilde{\delta}_{r}(g)=1
if
gs(r)=s(gr)
, and
\tilde{\delta}_{r}(g)=-1
if
gs(r)\neq s(gr)
.

Lemma 1.1

The function
\tilde{\delta}=(\tilde{\delta}_{r}):\Gamma\to({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{5}
is a
1
-cocycle

\Gamma\to G(\bar{k})
with class
\delta
.

Proof.
We have canonical isomorphisms of
\Gamma
-sets

\Sigma(\bar{k})={\rm{Hom}}_{k-{\rm alg}}(A,\bar{k})
and

\Delta(\bar{k})={\rm{Hom}}_{k-{\rm alg}}(A^{\prime},\bar{k})
, where
A^{\prime}=A[x]/(x^{2}-\delta^{\prime})
.
Let
r\in\Sigma(\bar{k})
.
The choice of a section
s:\Sigma(\bar{k})\to\Delta(\bar{k})
is equivalent to the choice of a
lifting of each homomorphism of
k
-algebras
r\colon A\to\bar{k}

to a homomorphism of
k
-algebras
\tilde{r}\colon A^{\prime}\to\bar{k}
, that is, to the choice of a
square root of
r(\delta^{\prime})
in
\bar{k}
. By the definition of
\tilde{\delta}_{r}
we have

\tilde{\delta}_{r}(g)=g(\sqrt{r(\delta^{\prime})})/\sqrt{gr(\delta^{\prime})}.

(1)

Since

{\rm N}_{A/k}(\delta^{\prime})\in k^{\times 2}
, we have
\prod_{r\in\Sigma(\bar{k})}\tilde{\delta}_{r}=1
.
For each closed point
\sigma\in\Sigma
, formula (
1
) with
r\in\sigma
describes
the character
{\rm{Gal}}(\bar{k}/k_{\sigma})\to\{\pm 1\}
of the extension

k_{\sigma}(\sqrt{\delta^{\prime}_{\sigma}})/k_{\sigma}
. Thus

\tilde{\delta}=(\tilde{\delta}_{r})\colon\Gamma\to G(\bar{k})
is a 1-cocycle with class
\delta=(\delta_{\sigma})
.

\Box

We fix a lifting
\delta^{\prime}
, a set-theoretic section
s
and hence the cocycle
\tilde{\delta}
, once and for all.

Let us recall a useful construction from
[
HS16
, §3]
.
Suppose that we are given a finite subset
T=\{\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{t}\}\subset{\rm H}^{1}(k,G)
.
Let us represent each
\alpha_{i}
by a
1
-cocycle
\tilde{\alpha}_{i}:\Gamma\to G(\bar{k})
.
Then the map
\varphi_{T}\colon\Gamma\to G(\bar{k})^{T}\rtimes\textup{Gal}(P)
given by

\varphi_{T}(g)=\big(\tilde{\alpha}_{1}(g),\ldots,\tilde{\alpha}_{t}(g),\varphi(g)\big)

is a homomorphism.
Denote by
k_{T}
the fixed field of
{\rm{Ker}}(\varphi_{T})
, so that
we have an injection
\textup{Gal}(k_{T}/k)\hookrightarrow G(\bar{k})^{T}\rtimes{\rm{Gal}}(P)
.
The extension
k_{T}/k
contains the splitting field of
P(t)
and is the smallest Galois extension of
k
through which each of the cocycles
\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\alpha}_{t}
factors.

From Lemma
1.1
we see that the natural action of
\Gamma
on
\Delta(\bar{k})
is via

\varphi_{\delta}\colon\Gamma\hookrightarrow G(\bar{k})\rtimes{\rm{Gal}}(P)\hookrightarrow({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{5}\rtimes{\rm S}_{5}.

Lemma 1.2

The subgroup
{\rm{Gal}}(k_{\delta}/k)\subset({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{4}\rtimes{\rm S}_{5}
is conjugate to a subgroup of

{\rm S}_{5}
in
({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{5}\rtimes{\rm S}_{5}
if and only if
\delta=0
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\delta=0
if and only if the morphism
\Delta\to\Sigma
has a section.
The stabiliser of a subset of
\Delta(\bar{k})
that maps bijectively onto
\Sigma(\bar{k})
is

h{\rm S}_{5}h^{-1}
for some
h\in({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{5}
.

\Box

Let us denote by
\Lambda_{\delta}
the
k
-torsor for
G
defined by the cocycle
\tilde{\delta}
.
The isomorphism class of
\Lambda_{\delta}
depends only on

\delta=[\Lambda_{\delta}]\in{\rm H}^{1}(k,G)
.
As above, the actions of
\Gamma
on
\Lambda_{\delta}(\bar{k})
and on
\Delta(\bar{k})

determine each other. In both cases,
\Gamma
acts through the faithfully acting finite group

{\rm{Gal}}(k_{\delta}/k)
. The relation between
\Lambda_{\delta}
and
\Delta

can also be described as follows: the image of
\delta
in
{\rm H}^{1}(k,R_{A/k}(\mu_{2}))
induced by

G\hookrightarrow R_{A/k}(\mu_{2})
corresponds to
[\Delta]\in{\rm H}^{1}(A,\mu_{2})

under the isomorphism
{\rm H}^{1}(A,\mu_{2})\cong{\rm H}^{1}(k,R_{A/k}(\mu_{2}))
given by the Weil
restriction.

Let
({\mathbb{F}}_{2})^{A,\delta}
be the vector space over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
with a basis whose vectors bijectively
correspond to the closed points
\sigma\in\Sigma
such that

\delta_{\sigma}\neq 1\in k_{\sigma}^{\times}/k_{\sigma}^{\times 2}
.
Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\subset({\mathbb{F}}_{2})^{A,\delta}
be the subspace spanned by the vector
(1,\ldots,1)
.
Define
{\rm B}_{A,\delta}
as the subgroup of
({\mathbb{F}}_{2})^{A,\delta}/{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
represented by the
vectors
\gamma=(\gamma_{\sigma})\in({\mathbb{F}}_{2})^{A,\delta}
such that

\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma}{\rm N}_{k_{\sigma}/k}(\delta_{\sigma})^{\gamma_{\sigma}}=1\in k^{\times}/k^{\times 2}.

If
\delta=0
, we define
{\rm B}_{A,\delta}=0
.

1.2
Euler identities

Let
B=k[t]/(Q(t))
where
Q(t)\in k[t]
is a monic separable polynomial of degree

n\geq 1
. Let
\theta
be the image of
t
in
B
. Write

Q(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(t-\theta_{i})
,
where
\theta_{i}\in\bar{k}
. A polynomial
R(t)\in k[t]
of degree at most
n-1
is uniquely
determined by its values at
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}
, thus we have the Lagrange interpolation
formula

R(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}R(\theta_{j})\frac{\prod_{i=1,\,i\neq j}(t-\theta_{i})}{\prod_{i=1,\,i\neq j}(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i})}={\rm Tr}_{B/k}\left(R(\theta)\frac{Q(t)}{Q^{\prime}(\theta)(t-\theta)}\right),

where
Q^{\prime}(t)
is the derivative of
Q(t)
. Applying this to
R(t)=1,\ldots,t^{n-1},t^{n}-Q(t)
we obtain

t^{m}={\rm Tr}_{B/k}\left(\theta^{m}\frac{Q(t)}{Q^{\prime}(\theta)(t-\theta)}\right),\ m\leq n-1,\quad t^{n}-Q(t)={\rm Tr}_{B/k}\left(\theta^{n}\frac{Q(t)}{Q^{\prime}(\theta)(t-\theta)}\right).

Subtracting the expression for
t^{m}
from the expression for
t^{m-1}
multiplied by
t
,
we obtain Euler identities

{\rm Tr}_{B/k}\left(\frac{\theta^{m}}{Q^{\prime}(\theta)}\right)=0,\ \ m=0,\ldots,n-2,\quad{\rm Tr}_{B/k}\left(\frac{\theta^{n-1}}{Q^{\prime}(\theta)}\right)=1,

(2)

see also
[
Ser68
, Ch. III, §6, Lemme 2]
.

We shall need to consider quadratic forms
on the
k
-algebra
B
given by

\Phi_{\infty}(u)={\rm Tr}_{B/k}\left(\frac{u^{2}}{Q^{\prime}(\theta)}\right),\quad\Phi_{b}(u)={\rm Tr}_{B/k}\left((b-\theta)\frac{u^{2}}{Q^{\prime}(\theta)}\right),

where
b\in k
.

Lemma 1.3

The determinants of the matrices of
\Phi_{\infty}
and
\Phi_{b}

in the basis
1,\ldots,\theta^{n-1}
of the
k
-vector space
B

are
(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}
and
Q(b)(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}
, respectively.

Proof.
The calculation for
\Phi_{\infty}
follows from (
2
), using that
its matrix has a triangular form, see
[
Ser68
,
loc. cit.]
.
The matrix of
\Phi_{b}
is the product of the matrix of
\Phi_{\infty}
and
the matrix of the linear transformation
given by multiplication by
b-\theta\in B
.
The determinant of this matrix is the norm
{\rm N}_{B/k}(b-\theta)=Q(b)
.
\Box

1.3
Transitive subgroups of
{\rm S}_{5}

Recall that a transitive subgroup of
{\rm S}_{5}
is isomorphic to the cyclic group

\mathbb{Z}/5
, the dihedral group
{\rm D}_{10}
, the Frobenius group
{\rm F}_{20}
, the alternating group
{\rm A}_{5}
, or the full symmetric group
{\rm S}_{5}
. Thus it
contains a
5
-cycle, and contains a double transposition if it is not cyclic.

Lemma 1.4

Let
{\cal G}
be a transitive subgroup of
{\rm S}_{5}
. Then

G
is a simple and faithful
{\cal G}
-module such that

\textup{End}_{\cal G}(G)\cong\mathbb{F}_{2^{r}}
, where
r=4
if
{\cal G}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/5
,

r=2
if
{\cal G}\cong{\rm D}_{10}
, and
r=1
otherwise.
In all cases we have
{\rm H}^{1}({\cal G},G)=0
.

Proof.

The polynomial
t^{5}-1\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[t]
is the product of
irreducible factors
t-1
and
t^{4}+t^{3}+t^{2}+t+1
, thus we have an isomorphism of rings

{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[t]/(t^{5}-1)\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{16}\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
. This is also a direct sum of simple

{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[t]/(t^{5}-1)
-modules.

The ring
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[t]/(t^{5}-1)
is the group ring of the cyclic
group
{\mathbb{Z}}/5
generated by a 5-cycle
\tau\in{\rm S}_{5}
.
As a module over itself, it can be identified with
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[{\rm S}_{5}/{\rm S}_{4}]
,
and then
G
is identified with
{\mathbb{F}}_{16}
. In particular,
G
is a simple
{\mathbb{Z}}/5
-module.

Taking
\tau\in{\cal G}
we conclude that
G
is a simple
{\cal G}
-module.

The
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-algebra
\textup{End}_{\cal G}(G)
is the centraliser of
{\cal G}
in

{\rm{End}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/5}(G)\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{16}
, so
it is the invariant subfield
({\mathbb{F}}_{16})^{\cal G}\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}
where
r
is
1
,
2
, or
4
.
If
{\cal G}\cong\mathbb{Z}/5
, then
r=4
. If
{\cal G}\cong{\rm D}_{10}
, then

({\mathbb{F}}_{16})^{\cal G}

1.4
Severi–Brauer fibrations

We shall need to consider the following situation. Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a smooth, projective,
geometrically integral variety over
k
with a dominant morphism

\pi\colon{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
such that the generic fibre
{\mathcal{S}}_{k(t)}
is a Severi–Brauer variety.
Assume, moreover, that the restriction of
\pi
to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}\setminus\Sigma
is smooth,
and that each closed fibre
\pi^{-1}(\sigma)
,
\sigma\in\Sigma
, consists
of two geometric components of multiplicity 1 individually defined over
k_{\sigma}(\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\sigma}})

for some
\varepsilon_{\sigma}\in k_{\sigma}^{\times}
. Let

\varepsilon\in A^{\times}/A^{\times 2}=\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma}k_{\sigma}^{\times}/k_{\sigma}^{\times 2}

be the image of
(\varepsilon_{\sigma})
.

We write
{\rm{Br}}_{\rm vert}({\mathcal{S}})
for the vertical
Brauer group associated to the morphism
\pi\colon{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
.
The following statement is well-known;
we give the proofs for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition 1.6

Let
\alpha\in{\rm{Br}}(k(t))
be the class of the Severi–Brauer variety
{\mathcal{S}}_{k(t)}
.
Then we have the following statements.

(i)

The residue of
\alpha
at
\sigma\in\Sigma
is the class of
\varepsilon_{\sigma}
in
k_{\sigma}^{\times}/k_{\sigma}^{\times 2}
.
The residue of
\alpha
at any closed point of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}\setminus\Sigma
is zero.
We have
\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma}{\rm N}_{k_{\sigma}/k}(\varepsilon_{\sigma})\in k^{\times 2}
.

(ii)

The natural map
{\rm{Br}}(k)\to{\rm{Br}}({\mathcal{S}})
is injective unless
\varepsilon=1\in A^{\times}/A^{\times 2}
,
in which case its kernel is generated by
\alpha
.

(iii)
The inclusion
{\rm{Br}}_{\rm vert}({\mathcal{S}})\subset{\rm{Br}}({\mathcal{S}})
is an equality.

(iv)
There is a natural isomorphism
{\rm{Br}}({\mathcal{S}})/{\rm{Br}}_{0}({\mathcal{S}})\cong{\rm B}_{A,\varepsilon}
.

(v)
There is a natural isomorphism
{\rm{Br}}({\mathcal{S}})/{\rm{Br}}_{0}({\mathcal{S}})\cong{\rm H}^{1}(k,{\rm{Pic}}({\mathcal{S}}_{\bar{k}}))
.

Proof.
(i)
By Lichtenbaum’s theorem
[
GS17
, Theorem 5.4.11]
, the natural map

{\rm{Br}}(k(t))\to{\rm{Br}}({\mathcal{S}}_{k(t)})
is surjective with kernel generated by
\alpha
.
Thus
\alpha
goes to zero under the restriction map to
{\rm{Br}}(k({\mathcal{S}}))
, hence the residue
of
\alpha
at the generic point of every closed fibre
{\mathcal{S}}_{x}
of
\pi
is trivial.
If
x\notin\Sigma
, then
{\mathcal{S}}_{x}
is geometrically integral, hence the residue field
k(x)
is separably
closed in
k({\mathcal{S}}_{x})
. Using
[
CTS21
, Lemma 11.1.2]
we conclude that the residue of
\alpha

at
x
is zero.
A similar argument gives that
the residue of
\alpha
at a closed point
\sigma\in\Sigma
belongs to the subgroup of

{\rm H}^{1}(k_{\sigma},{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
generated by the class of

\varepsilon_{\sigma}
. The triviality of this residue in the case when

\varepsilon_{\sigma}\notin k_{\sigma}^{\times 2}
implies the existence of a Severi–Brauer
scheme over the local ring of
\sigma
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
with generic fibre
{\mathcal{S}}_{k(t)}
, but this
contradicts the birational invariance of
\varepsilon_{\sigma}
, see
[
Sko15
, Corollary 2.3]
.
The product formula comes from the Faddeev reciprocity law
[
CTS21
, Theorem 1.5.2]
.

(ii) We adapt the argument in the proof of
[
CTS21
, Lemma 11.3.3]
.
Restricting to the generic point
gives an injective map
{\rm{Br}}({\mathcal{S}})\hookrightarrow{\rm{Br}}(k({\mathcal{S}}))
, since
S
is smooth over
k
.
The kernel of
{\rm{Br}}(k)\to{\rm{Br}}({\mathcal{S}})
is thus equal to the kernel of the composition

{\rm{Br}}(k)\hookrightarrow{\rm{Br}}(k(t))\to{\rm{Br}}({\mathcal{S}}_{k(t)})\hookrightarrow{\rm{Br}}(k({\mathcal{S}})),

where the last map is injective since
{\mathcal{S}}_{k(t)}
is smooth over
k(t)
.
We know that the kernel of
{\rm{Br}}(k(t))\to{\rm{Br}}({\mathcal{S}}_{k(t)})
is generated by
\alpha
.
By (i) and the Faddeev reciprocity law
[
CTS21
, Theorem 1.5.2]
,
\alpha

belongs to
{\rm{Br}}(k)
if and only if
\varepsilon=1\in A^{\times}/A^{\times 2}
.

(iii) This follows from the surjectivity of the natural map
{\rm{Br}}(k(t))\to{\rm{Br}}({\mathcal{S}}_{k(t)})
.

(iv) The notation
{\rm B}_{A,\varepsilon}
makes sense by virtue of (i).
In view of Lichtenbaum’s theorem, (iii) follows from (ii) and the standard calculation of
the vertical Brauer group, see
[
CTS21
, Proposition 11.1.8]
.

(v) The generic fibre of
\pi\colon{\mathcal{S}}_{\bar{k}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\bar{k}}
is a Severi–Brauer variety over

\bar{k}(t)
, so it is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}_{\bar{k}(t)}
by Tsen’s theorem.
Thus
{\mathcal{S}}_{\bar{k}}
is rational, hence
{\rm{Br}}({\mathcal{S}}_{\bar{k}})=0
. The claim
follows from
[
CTS21
, Proposition 5.4.2]
and
[
CTS21
, Remark 5.4.3 (2)]
.

\Box

Number fields

When
k
is a number field, we denote by

the ring of integers of
k
.
For a set of places
S
of
k
, let

be the subring of

consisting of the elements
that are integral ouside of
S
. We denote by

k_{v}
the completion of
k
at a place
v
. When
v
is non-archimedean, we denote
the ring of integers of
k_{v}
by
O_{v}
and the residue field of
O_{v}
by
{\mathbb{F}}_{v}
.
Let us denote by

{\rm loc}_{v}\colon{\rm H}^{1}(k,G)\to{\rm H}^{1}(k_{v},G)

the natural restriction map. Define

{\rm H}^{1}_{\rm nr}(k_{v},G):={\rm{Ker}}[{\rm H}^{1}(k_{v},G)\to{\rm H}^{1}(k_{v}^{\rm nr},G)],

where
k_{v}^{\rm nr}
is the maximal unramified extension of

.

For a finite field extension
k^{\prime}/k
we write
{\rm Ram}(k^{\prime}/k)
for the set of primes of
k
ramified in
k^{\prime}
.

In all of this paper we write
S_{0}
for a finite set of places of
k
that contains all the ‘bad’

(P(t),\delta)
:
the archimedean places, the places above the prime 2, the places
v

where
P(t)\not\in O_{v}[t]
, and the places dividing
{\rm discr\,}(P(t))
. We arrange that
\delta

is unramified away from

For a place

{\rm Frob}_{v}\in{\rm{Gal}}(k_{\delta}/k)

defined up to conjugation.

Increasing
S_{0}
by a fixed finite set of primes, we ensure that
any quartic del Pezzo surface over

has more

-points than any genus 1 curve.

Lemma 1.7

Assume that
P(t)
is irreducible and
\textup{End}_{\textup{Gal}(P)}(G)=\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Let
T
be a finite
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent subset of

.
Let

be a finite set of places of

containing

\sigma\in\textup{Gal}(k_{T}/k)

vanishes on the image of

\sigma
. Then there are a prime
\mathfrak{p}\notin S
such that

{\rm{Frob}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\in{\rm{Gal}}(k_{T}/k)
lies in the conjugacy class of
\sigma
, and a quadratic extension
F/k
ramified at
\mathfrak{p}
, split at all places of
S
and unramified at all other primes.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the formal product of
8
and all places in
S
. Let
k_{\mathfrak{m}}
be the ray class field associated to the modulus
\mathfrak{m}
, and let
k_{\mathfrak{m},2}/k
denote its maximal multiquadratic subextension. By Lemma
1.5
, the intersection of

is contained in the splitting field of

. Let
L
be the compositum of
k_{T}
and

Let
\mathfrak{p}

. By construction, the image of
\textup{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}

lies in the conjugacy class of
\sigma
. Since the image of
\textup{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
in

modulo

Fibration theorem for zero-cycles

The following proposition is a particular case of the general fibration theorem
for zero-cycles of Harpaz and Wittenberg

[
HW16
, Theorem 6.2]
. The statement is adapted for our needs in this paper
and does not aim at maximum generality.

Proposition 1.8

Let
k
be a number field.
Let
Z
be a smooth, projective, geometrically integral variety.
Let

be a dominant morphism with geometrically integral generic fibre
such that every geometric fibre of

contains an irreducible component
of multiplicity
1
. Let
U\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
be a non-empty open subset such that
the restriction of
f
to
Z_{U}=f^{-1}(U)
is a smooth morphism
Z_{U}\to U
.
Let

be a finite subgroup and let

be any positive integer.
Assume that there is an adelic point
(x_{v})\in Z({\bf A}_{k})

orthogonal to
(B+f^{*}{\rm{Br}}(k({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k})))\cap{\rm{Br}}(Z)
.
Then
U
contains a closed point
R={\rm{Spec}}(K)
, of degree coprime to
m
, such that
Z_{R}({\bf A}_{K})^{B}\neq\emptyset
.
Moreover, for any non-archimedean places
v_{1},...,v_{n}
of
k
, we can choose
R
such that, for each
i=1,...,n
, there is a place
w_{i}
of
K
over
v_{i}
with
K_{w_{i}}\cong k_{v_{i}}
and

is arbitrarily close to
f(x_{v_{i}})
in
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k}(K_{w_{i}})
.

Proof.
Note that
(B+f^{*}{\rm{Br}}(k({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k})))\cap{\rm{Br}}(Z)
is finite modulo
{\rm{Br}}(k)
,
by
[
HW16
, Lemma 5.3]
,
hence is generated by finitely many elements
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
modulo
{\rm{Br}}(k)
.
Each of
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
has trivial evaluation map at
Z(k_{v})
for almost all places
v
.

Choose an arbitrary closed point
N
in
Z_{U}
.

For each real place
v
, by a version of Bertini’s theorem we can choose a smooth, projective,
geometrically irreducible curve

passing
through
x_{v}
and
N
such that

.
We choose a positive integer

large enough so that if

is a divisor
class of degree
1+mr\,{\rm{deg}}_{k}(N)
, then
the inequality in condition (ii) of
[
HW16
, Theorem 5.1]
holds
and the inequality in condition (iii) of
[
HW16
, Theorem 5.1]

holds for every real place
v
of
k
.

For each place
v
, define
z_{v}
to be an effective zero-cycle in
Z_{k_{v}}

that is a small deformation of
x_{v}+mrN
such that
f(z_{v})
is reduced and is contained in
U_{k_{v}}
.
For each real place
v
we take
z_{v}
to lie on
C_{v}
.
Since
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
have trivial evaluation maps at
Z(k_{v})
for almost all places
v
,
at these places we can take
z_{v}
to be
an
arbitrary
effective zero-cycle of degree

such that

is reduced and contained in

and so arrange that

is integral with respect to some integral model of

.
(An integral

is a faithfully flat

-scheme of finite type
with generic fibre

is integral

such that

Thus, in the notation of
[
HW16

, we have

If

is
a 1-cochain

has Galois group
{\rm D}_{10}

In this section we prove Theorem

The proof proceeds along roughly the same lines as the proof of Theorem

\textup{Gal}(P)
, hence vanishes on
\sigma
. Thus, arguing as in Lemma
1.7
,
we find a place
v\notin S
such that
{\rm{Frob}}_{v}\in\textup{Gal}(K_{T}/k)
is in the conjugacy class of
\sigma
, and
a\in k^{\times}
such that
k(\sqrt{a})/k
is ramified at
v
, split at all places in
S
, and is unramified elsewhere. Write
F=k(\sqrt{a_{0}})
and set
F^{\prime}=k(\sqrt{aa_{0}})
.
The
2
-Selmer conditions for
F
and
F^{\prime}
agree at all places away from
v
. Further, at the place
v
we have
G(\bar{k})/(\textup{Frob}_{v}-1)=0
, hence
{\rm H}^{1}(k_{v},G)=0
. Thus
{\rm Sel}_{2}(J_{b}^{F^{\prime}})={\rm Sel}_{2}(J_{b}^{F})
as subgroups of
{\rm H}^{1}(k,G)
. By Proposition
4.5
we have

\left\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\right\rangle^{\textup{CT}}_{F^{\prime},2}=B(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})

for all
i<j
. Both pairings
B
and
\left\langle~,~\right\rangle^{\textup{CT}}_{F^{\prime},2}
are alternating, so they are equal. Thus
\delta
is the unique non-trivial element in the kernel of
\left\langle~,~\right\rangle^{\textup{CT}}_{F^{\prime},2}
, so
\delta
maps to
0
in
\textcyr{Sh}(J_{b}^{F^{\prime}})
.

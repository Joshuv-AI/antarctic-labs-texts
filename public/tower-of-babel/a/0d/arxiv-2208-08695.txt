I
Introduction

Charge-parity (
C\!P
) violation is one of the conditions necessary to explain the matter-antimatter asymmetry of the universe
Sakharov:1967dj
.
The single complex phase in the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix provides the only source of
C\!P
violation (CPV) in the standard model (SM), but it is not large enough to explain the observed matter-antimatter asymmetry.
Baryogenesis, the process by which the baryon-antibaryon asymmetry of the universe developed, is directly related to baryon CPV
Sakharov:1988vdp
;
Shaposhnikov:1987tw
.
To date, CPV has been observed in the open-flavored meson sector,
but not yet established in the baryon sector.
Since CPV in charm decays is predicted in the SM to be at the level of
10^{-3}
or smaller
Brod:2011re
;
Li:2012cfa
;
Cheng:2019ggx
;
Kagan:2020vri
;
Cheng:2021yrn
,
an observation of CPV in charm decays much greater than
10^{-3}
could indicate new physics beyond the SM
Delepine:2019cpp
;
Dery:2019ysp
;
Chala:2019fdb
;
Saur:2020rgd
.

Singly Cabibbo-suppressed (SCS) decays of charm hadrons
provide an ideal laboratory for studying CPV as they are a unique window on the physics of decay-rate dynamics in the charm sector
Cheng:2021yrn
;
Saur:2020rgd
.
The only observation of CPV in the charm sector was made by the LHCb collaboration
in SCS charmed meson decays,
D^{0}\rightarrow h^{+}h^{-}
(
h\!=\!K,\,\pi
throughout this paper)
LHCb:2019hro
.
Measurements of the direct
C\!P
asymmetry (
A_{C\!P}^{\rm dir}
), induced by the partial widths, in SCS charmed baryon decays are experimentally more challenging than in charmed meson decays and relatively unexplored.
Searches for direct CPV in SCS charmed baryon decays were made in
\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}\rightarrow{}ph^{+}h^{-}

LHCb:2017hwf
and
\mathchar 28932\relax_{c}^{+}\rightarrow{}pK^{-}\pi^{+}

LHCb:2020zkk
.
No direct CPV searches in two-body SCS decays of charmed baryons have been made.

In addition to
A_{C\!P}^{\rm dir}
, the
\alpha
-induced
C\!P
asymmetry (
A_{C\!P}^{\alpha}
) is an essential observable to search for CPV in baryon decays.
Here
\alpha
is the decay asymmetry parameter introduced
to study the parity-violating and parity-conserving amplitudes in weak hyperon decays
Lee:1957qs
.
In a weak decay of
\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}
into a spin
1/2
baryon with positive parity and a pseudoscalar meson,

\alpha\!\equiv\!{2\cdot{\rm Re}(S^{*}P)/(|S|^{2}+|P|^{2})}
,
where

Since

\alpha
-induced

asymmetry for

In the case that

is given by the CPV in

Therefore,

To date, there is only one

Using the precisely measured

II
Detector and data set

This analysis is based on the full data set recorded by the Belle detector
Belle:2000cnh
;
Belle:2012iwr

operating at the KEKB
Kurokawa:2001nw
;
Abe:2013kxa

asymmetric-energy
e^{+}e^{-}

This data sample corresponds to a total integrated luminosity of
980~\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
collected at or near the
\Upsilon(nS)
(
n\!=\!1,\,2,\,3,\,4,\,5
) resonances.
The Belle detector is a large-solid-angle magnetic spectrometer consisting of a silicon vertex detector (SVD), a central drift chamber (CDC), an array of aerogel threshold Cherenkov counters (ACC), a barrel-like arrangement of time-of-flight scintillation counters (TOF), and an electromagnetic calorimeter (ECL) consisting of CsI(Tl) crystals. These components are all located inside a superconducting solenoid coil that provides a 1.5 T magnetic field. The iron flux-return of the magnet is instrumented to detect
K^{0}_{L}
mesons and to identify muons (KLM). The detector is described in detail elsewhere
Belle:2000cnh
;
Belle:2012iwr
.

Monte Carlo (MC) simulated events are generated with
evtgen

Lange:2001uf
and
pythia

Sjostrand:2000wi
,
and are subsequently processed through a full detector simulation based on
geant3

Brun:1987ma
.
Final-state radiation from charged particles is included at event generation using
photos

Barberio:1993qi
.
Signal
\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}
baryons are produced via the inclusive process
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow{}c\bar{c}\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}+{\rm anything}
and
{\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax h^{+}},\,\mathchar 28934\relax^{0}h^{+}
decays, where
\mathchar 28934\relax^{0}\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax\gamma

IV
Event selection and optimization

The
h^{+}
candidates from
\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}
decays are selected as follows.
Charged tracks satisfying
\mathcal{R}(K|\pi)\!=\!{\mathcal{L}_{K}/(\mathcal{L}_{K}+\mathcal{L}_{\pi})}\!>\!0.7
are identified as kaons, while those satisfying
\mathcal{R}(K|\pi)\!<\!0.7
are identified as pions.
Here
\mathcal{L}_{i}
(
i\!=\!\pi,\,K,\,p
) is the particle identification (PID) likelihood for a given particle hypothesis, which is calculated from the photon yield in the ACC, energy-loss measurements in the CDC, and time-of-flight information from the TOF
Nakano:2002jw
.
The highly proton-like tracks with
\mathcal{R}(p|K)\!>\!0.8
and
\mathcal{R}(p|\pi)\!>\!0.8
are rejected as
h^{+}
candidates for signal modes and reference modes, respectively.
To suppress the background from
\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}
semileptonic decays, tracks that are highly electron-like (
{\mathcal{L}_{e}/(\mathcal{L}_{e}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{non-}e})}\!>\!0.95
) or muon-like (
{\mathcal{L}_{\mu}/(\mathcal{L}_{\mu}+\mathcal{L}_{\pi}+\mathcal{L}_{K})}\!>\!0.95
) are rejected.
The electron and muon likelihoods depend primarily on the information from the ECL and KLM, respectively
Hanagaki:2001fz
;
Abashian:2002bd
.
The signal efficiency after applying PID requirements is
83\%
for signal modes and
96\%
for reference modes. About
44\%
and
9\%
of total backgrounds are rejected for signal modes and reference modes, respectively.
We require the
h^{+}
candidates to have at least two hits in the SVD to improve their impact parameter resolution with respect to the interaction point.

The
\mathchar 28931\relax
candidates are reconstructed from one
p
and one
\pi
candidate, which a fit requires to originate from a common vertex.
We require
|M_{\mathchar 28931\relax}-m_{\mathchar 28931\relax}|\!<\!3
MeV/

Summary

In conclusion, based on the 980
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
data set collected with the Belle detector,
we make the first measurement of the direct
C\!P
asymmetry in SCS two-body decays of charmed baryons,

A_{C\!P}^{\rm dir}({\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax K^{+}})\!=\!+0.021\pm 0.026\pm 0.001
and
A_{C\!P}^{\rm dir}({\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28934\relax^{0}K^{+}})\!=\!+0.025\pm 0.054\pm 0.004
.
The relative branching fractions are measured to be,

\mathcal{B}({\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax K^{+}})/\mathcal{B}({\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax\pi^{+}})\!=\!(5.05\pm 0.13\pm 0.09)\%
and

\mathcal{B}({\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28934\relax^{0}K^{+}})/\mathcal{B}({\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28934\relax^{0}\pi^{+}})\!=\!(2.78\pm 0.15\pm 0.05)\%
,
which supersede previous Belle measurements
Belle:2001hyr
.
Using the world average values for the branching fractions for
\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}\rightarrow(\mathchar 28931\relax,\,\mathchar 28934\relax^{0})\pi^{+}
,
we obtain

\mathcal{B}({\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax K^{+}})\!=\!{(6.57\pm 0.17\pm 0.11\pm 0.35)\times 10^{-4}}
and

\mathcal{B}({\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28934\relax^{0}K^{+}})\!=\!{(3.58\pm 0.19\pm 0.06\pm 0.19)\times 10^{-4}}
.
These results are the most precise to date and significantly improve the precision of the world average values
bib:PDG2022
.

We obtain the averaged decay asymmetry parameters

\alpha_{\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}}^{\rm avg}({\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax K^{+}})\!=\!-0.585\pm 0.049\pm 0.018
and

\alpha_{\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}}^{\rm avg}({\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28934\relax^{0}K^{+}})\!=\!-0.54\pm 0.18\pm 0.09
for the first time.
We obtain

\alpha_{\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}}^{\rm avg}({\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax\pi^{+}})\!=\!-0.755\pm 0.005\pm 0.003
and

\alpha_{\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}}^{\rm avg}({\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28934\relax^{0}\pi^{+}})\!=\!-0.463\pm 0.016\pm 0.008
,
which are consistent with previous measurements
bib:PDG2022
but with significantly improved precision.
We also determine the
\alpha
-parameter for
\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}
and
\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997pt\mathchar 28931\relax\kern 0.50003pt}\kern-0.50003pt{}{}^{-}_{c}
individually and search for CPV via the
\alpha
-induced
C\!P
asymmetry, as listed in Table
2
. These results include the first measurements of
A_{C\!P}^{\alpha}
for SCS decays of charmed baryons,

A_{C\!P}^{\alpha}({\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax K^{+}})\!=\!-0.023\pm 0.086\pm 0.071
and

A_{C\!P}^{\alpha}({\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28934\relax^{0}K^{+}})\!=\!+0.08\pm 0.35\pm 0.14
.
We search for
\mathchar 28931\relax
hyperon CPV via the
\alpha
-induced
C\!P
asymmetry in
{\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax\pi^{+}}
and
{\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28934\relax^{0}\pi^{+}}
decays, and determine
A_{C\!P}^{\alpha}({\mathchar 28931\relax\rightarrow p\pi^{-}})\!=\!+0.013\pm 0.007\pm 0.011
by combining the two modes.
No evidence of baryon CPV is found.
The method used in our
A_{C\!P}^{\alpha}({\mathchar 28931\relax\rightarrow p\pi^{-}})
measurement can be applied to other hyperons, such as
A_{C\!P}^{\alpha}({\mathchar 28932\relax^{0,-}\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax\pi^{0,-}})
in
{\mathchar 28931\relax{}_{c}^{+}\rightarrow\mathchar 28932\relax^{0}K^{+}}
and
{\mathchar 28932\relax_{c}^{+,0}\rightarrow\mathchar 28932\relax^{0,-}\pi^{+}}
.
Our measurement is a milestone for hyperon CPV searches in charm CF decays and this method is promising for precise measurements of various hyperon CPV at Belle II and LHCb.

The Belle Experiment

Belle is a first-generation “asymmetric B-factory” experiment at the
KEKB asymmetric-energy
e^{+}e^{-}
collider in Tsukuba, Japan.
Belle was designed to discover
C\!P
violation in
B
meson decays,
as well as to cover a wide range of physics targets.
Belle collected over 1 ab
-1
of collision data between 1999 and 2010,
including 772 million
B\overline{B}
pairs produced at the energy
corresponding to the
\Upsilon(4S)
resonance.
Belle has also collected large samples of data from

\tau
-lepton, charm-, and light-hadron decays,
and data at the energies of the
\Upsilon(1,2,3,5S)
resonances.
In 2001, Belle observed a large
C\!P
asymmetry in
B
meson decays,
providing strong experimental evidence for the theoretical predictions
from Kobayashi and Maskawa.
In 2003, Belle discovered an unexpected new charmonium-like state,

X(3872)
, heralding a new era of hadron physics.
After more than a decade since the end of data-taking,
Belle is still actively analyzing data and reporting new results.

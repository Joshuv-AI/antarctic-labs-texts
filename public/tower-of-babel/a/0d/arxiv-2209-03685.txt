Steenrod operations and algebraic classes
Olivier Benoist

Abstract
. Based on a relative Wu theorem in étale cohomology, we study the compatibility of Steenrod operations on Chow groups and on étale cohomology. Using the resulting obstructions to algebraicity, we construct new examples of non-algebraic cohomology classes over various fields ( ℂ \mathbb{C} , ℝ \mathbb{R} , 𝔽 ¯ p \overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p} , 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} ). We also use Steenrod operations to study the mod 2 2 cohomology classes of a compact 𝒞 ∞ \mathcal{C}^{\infty} manifold M M that are algebraizable, i.e. algebraic on some real algebraic model of M M . We give new examples of algebraizable and non-algebraizable classes, answering questions of Benedetti, Dedò and Kucharz.



1.
Introduction

The theme of this article is the interaction between Steenrod operations and algebraic cohomology classes of smooth projective varieties.

1.1.
Action of Steenrod operations on algebraic classes

Our starting point is a theorem of Kawai
[
Kaw77
]
according to which mod
\ell
Steenrod operations preserve algebraic cohomology classes with
\mathbb{Z}/\ell
coefficients on smooth projective complex varieties (see also
[
Ful98
, Example 19.1.8]
). This implies in particular that mod
\ell
algebraic classes on smooth projective complex varieties are killed by odd degree Steenrod operations, and hence provides topological obstructions to the algebraicity of such classes. One recovers in this way the first counterexamples to the integral Hodge conjecture, constructed by Atiyah and Hirzebruch
[
AH62
]
.

These arguments were adapted by Colliot-Thélène and Szamuely with help from Totaro
[
CTS10
, Theorem 2.1]
to construct counterexamples to the integral Tate conjecture over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
(whose statement is recalled in §
1.4
). We refer to
[
Qui11
,
PY15
,
Kam15
,
Ant16
]
for further examples. In particular, the vanishing of odd degree Steenrod operations on mod
\ell
algebraic classes over algebraically closed fields of characteristic
\neq\ell
, proven in a particular case in
[
CTS10
, Theorem 2.1 (1)]
,
follows in general from Quick’s work
[
Qui11
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Our first goal is to extend this result to arbitrary, not necessarily algebraically closed, fields.

To do so, we compare Brosnan’s Steenrod operations on Chow groups (
[
Bro03
]
, see also §
3.1
) and the Steenrod operations in (twisted) étale cohomology (see
[
GW19
]
and §§
2.1
–
2.2
) by means of the mod
\ell
étale cycle class map
\mathrm{cl}
.

In the next theorem, we write
\mathrm{Sq}
for the total Steenrod square (when
{\ell=2}
) and
P
for the total Steenrod
\ell
-th power (when
\ell
is odd).
When
\ell=2
, the cohomology class
\varpi
is the class of
-1
in
k^{*}/(k^{*})^{2}\simeq H^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(k),\mathbb{Z}/2)
. See also Remark
2.1
(ii).

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.4
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety over a field
k
. Let
\ell
be a prime number invertible in
k
. For
x\in\mathrm{CH}^{c}(X)/\ell
, one has

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sq}(\mathrm{cl}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}(1+\varpi)^{c-i}\cdot\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{Sq}^{2i}(x))

\displaystyle\textrm{ \hskip 5.11108ptif }\ell=2,

\displaystyle P(\mathrm{cl}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}(P(x))

\displaystyle\textrm{ \hskip 5.11108ptif }\ell\textrm{ is odd.}\

It follows from Theorem
1.1
that algebraic cohomology classes are preserved by all Steenrod operations as soon as
k
contains a primitive
\ell^{2}
-th root of unity, and by Steenrod’s
\ell
-th powers when
\ell
is odd and
k
is arbitrary (see Proposition
3.7
). It is however not true that Steenrod squares always preserve algebraic classes (
e.g.
if
\ell=2
and
k=\mathbb{R}
, see Remark
3.8
), and one can view Theorem
1.1
as providing the correct substitute for this incorrect statement.

Theorem
1.1
is proved by comparing two relative Wu theorems (describing the behaviour of Steenrod operations under proper pushforwards): one for Chow groups and another one in étale cohomology. The former is due to Brosnan (
[
Bro03
]
, see Proposition
3.1
). The latter is due to Scavia and Suzuki
[
SS23
, Proposition 3.1]
when
\ell=2
and
k
is algebraically closed, and could be deduced in general from the Riemann–Roch theorems of Panin
[
Pan04
]
and Déglise
[
Dég18
]
. We have found it more convenient to give a self-contained proof in §
2.5
(see Theorem
2.5
).

1.2.
Examples of non-algebraic cohomology classes

Our second goal, following the above-mentioned counterexamples to the integral Hodge and Tate conjectures, is to exploit the obstructions to algebraicity stemming from Theorem
1.1
to give interesting new examples of non-algebraic cohomology classes.

1.2.1.

We first apply classical topological obstructions to algebraicity going back to Atiyah and Hirzebruch
[
AH62
]
(the vanishing of odd degree Steenrod operations) to a variety constructed in
[
BO21
, §5.1]
. This yields a
5
-dimensional counterexample to the integral Tate conjecture over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
(see Remark
4.3
for its Hodge counterpart).

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.2
)
.

Let
p
be an odd prime number. There exists a smooth projective variety of dimension
5
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
on which the
2
-adic integral Tate conjecture for codimension
2
cycles fails.

The counterexamples to the integral Tate conjecture over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
that have appeared so far in the literature all have dimension
\geq 7
.
By
[
Tat68
, (5.10)]
and
[
Sch98
, Theorem 0.5]
, it would follow from the (rational) Tate conjecture over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
that no
3
-dimensional counterexample exists. We do not know any
4
-dimensional counterexample.
In fact, we do not know any counterexample to the integral Tate conjecture for cycles of dimension
2
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

1.2.2.

We then notice that the stability of algebraic classes by Steenrod operations (over, say, algebraically closed fields) yields obstructions to algebraicity that go beyond the vanishing of odd degree Steenrod operations.
For instance, over the complex numbers, it implies that even degree Steenrod operations send mod
\ell
algebraic classes to reductions mod
\ell
of integral Hodge classes (and, over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, to reductions mod
\ell
of integral Tate classes). This really may obstruct the integral Hodge and Tate conjectures, because there are no reasons why reductions mod
\ell
of integral Hodge classes (or of integral Tate classes) should be preserved by even degree Steenrod operations. We illustrate these new Hodge-theoretic (or Galois-theoretic) obstructions to the integral Hodge and Tate conjectures by using them to prove the following theorem (see Remark
4.10
for its Hodge counterpart).

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
4.9
)
.

Choose
\ell\in\{2,3,5\}
and let
p
be a prime number distinct from
\ell
.
There exists a smooth projective variety
X
of dimension
2\ell+2
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with
H^{*}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
torsion-free on which the
\ell
-adic integral Tate conjecture for codimension
2
cycles fails.

Theorem
1.3
gives the first counterexample to the integral Tate conjecture over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with a torsion-free cohomology ring. Indeed, the topological obstructions used in the counterexamples that have previously appeared in the literature force their
\ell
-adic cohomology rings to contain non-zero torsion classes (although the non-algebraic integral Tate classes of
[
PY15
]
live in torsion-free
\ell
-adic cohomology groups).
We also refer to
[
dJP22
, Theorem 1.10]
for more recent examples, valid for all prime numbers
\ell
, and based on entirely different arguments.

Our proof builds upon
[
PY15
]
.
In this article, Pirutka and Yagita use algebraic approximations of classifying spaces of exceptional reductive groups to construct interesting
(2\ell+3)
-dimensional counterexamples to the
\ell
-adic integral Tate conjecture over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
by the Atiyah–Hirzebruch method, for
\ell\in\{2,3,5\}
. We notice that a well-chosen hypersurface in (a blow-up of) their variety has a torsion-free cohomology ring, but the integral Tate conjecture may still fail by the obstruction described above.

In
[
Tot97
]
, Totaro explained how to better understand and generalize the Atiyah–Hirzebruch topological obstructions to the integral Hodge conjecture using complex cobordism. In §
3.4
, we show that our Hodge-theoretic enhancement of these obstructions also has such an interpretation, in terms of the Hodge classes in complex cobordism introduced by Hopkins and Quick
[
HQ15
]
.

1.2.3.

Finally, we give examples of non-algebraic cohomology classes on varieties defined over non-closed fields, that become algebraic (and even vanish) over the algebraic closure of the base field. This is particularly interesting because their non-algebraicity is then a purely arithmetic phenomenon.

Over finite fields, the first examples of such classes were constructed very recently by Scavia and Suzuki
[
SS22
, Theorem 1.3]
. As they relied on obstructions to algebraicity induced by non-vanishing odd degree Steenrod operations, they had to restrict to finite fields containing a primitive
\ell^{2}
-th root of unity. Based on the subtler obstructions to algebraicity stemming from Theorem
1.1
(see Proposition
3.12
), we are able to remove this hypothesis.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
4.12
)
.

Let
p\neq\ell
be prime numbers, and let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite subfield of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
There exist a smooth projective geometrically connected
variety
X
of dimension
2\ell+3
over
\mathbb{F}
and a non-algebraic class

x\in\mathrm{Ker}\big(H^{4}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(2))\to H^{4}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(2))\big).

Scavia and Suzuki’s examples are algebraic approximations of classifying spaces of semisimple algebraic groups. To be able to encompass all finite fields, we instead resort to algebraic approximations of classifying spaces of (not necessarily constant) finite étale group schemes.

Over the field
\mathbb{R}
of real numbers, non-algebraic classes that are geometrically algebraic are harder to construct on real varieties with no real points (as there are then no obstructions to algebraicity induced by the topology of the set of real points). The example of lowest dimension appearing in the literature is the
7
-dimensional anisotropic quadric (
[
BW20a
, Example 2.5]
, see also
[
Yag10
, §5]
). Another application of Proposition
3.12
allows us to produce a
4
-dimensional example. In the next statement, we set
G:=\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})
and we let
H^{*}_{G}(X(\mathbb{C}),-)
denote the
G
-equivariant cohomology of the set of complex points of a smooth variety over
\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem
4.15
)
.

There exists a smooth projective variety
X
of dimension
4
over
\mathbb{R}
such that
X(\mathbb{R})=\varnothing
,
and a non-algebraic class

x\in\mathrm{Ker}\big(H^{4}_{G}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(2))\to H^{4}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(2))\big).

Theorem
1.5
gives a new counterexample to the real integral Hodge conjecture introduced in
[
BW20a
, Definition 2.2]
, which is not explained by a failure of the usual (complex) integral Hodge conjecture
(see Corollary
4.16
). We refer to Remarks
4.17
for a comparison with previously known counterexamples to the real integral Hodge conjecture.

1.3.
Algebraizability of cohomology classes of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifolds

In the last section of this article, we consider questions specific to real algebraic geometry, that are thematically related but logically independent of the above.

Let
X
be a smooth variety over
\mathbb{R}
. Borel and Haefliger (
[
BH61
]
, see also
[
BW20a
, §1.6.2]
) have constructed a cycle class map

\mathrm{cl}_{\mathbb{R}}:\mathrm{CH}^{c}(X)\to H^{c}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2),

associating with a codimension
c
integral subvariety
Z\subset X
the fundamental class of its real locus. Computing or controlling the image
H^{*}_{\mathrm{alg}}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
of
\mathrm{cl}_{\mathbb{R}}
is a central topic in real algebraic geometry (see
e.g.

[
BCR98
, §11.3]
or
[
BK98
]
).

Let
M
be a compact
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifold of dimension
d
. The Nash–Tognoli theorem
[
Tog73
, Corollario p. 176]
asserts that there exists a smooth projective variety
X
of dimension
d
over
\mathbb{R}
and a diffeomorphism
\chi:M{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}X(\mathbb{R})
. We call such a pair
(X,\chi)
an
algebraic model
of
M
. A cohomology class
x\in H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
is said to be
algebraizable
if it belongs to
\chi^{*}H^{*}_{\mathrm{alg}}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
for some algebraic model
(X,\chi)
of
M
.
The problem of determining which cohomology classes are algebraizable, a cohomological strengthening of the Nash–Tognoli theorem, is still largely open.

It is known since Benedetti and Dedò
[
BD84
, Theorem 1]
that there exist non-algebraizable cohomology classes. Teichner
[
Tei95
, Theorem 1]
has found examples in dimension
6
(the smallest dimension possible),
and Kucharz
[
Kuc08
, Theorem 1.13]
has discovered examples of all even degrees
\geq 2
. It remained an open problem, raised by Benedetti and Dedò
[
BD84
, p. 150]
and Kucharz
[
Kuc05
, p. 194]
, to determine the possible degrees of non-algebraizable classes, and in particular to construct non-algebraizable classes of odd degree. We solve this problem entirely.

Theorem 1.6

(Theorem
5.3
)
.

For all
c\geq 2
, there exists a compact
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifold
M
and a class
x\in H^{c}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
that is not algebraizable.

Examples of algebraizable cohomology classes include Stiefel–Whitney classes of topological real vector bundles on
M
and Poincaré-duals of fundamental classes of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
submanifolds of
M
(see
[
BT80
, Theorem 4.2 and Corollary 4.5]

or
[
AK81
, Theorem 2.10]
).
Following Kucharz
[
Kuc05
]
, let us denote by
A(M)\subset H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
the subring generated by these classes. It was asked by Benedetti and Dedò
[
BD84
, Conjecture 2 p. 150]

and again by Kucharz in
[
Kuc05
, Conjecture A]
whether all algebraizable classes belong to
A(M)
. We give a negative answer to this conjecture.

Theorem 1.7

(Theorem
5.6
)
.

There exists a compact
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifold
M
and an algebraizable class
x\in H^{5}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
that does not belong to
A(M)
.

The proofs of Theorems
1.6
and
1.7
rely on the stability of algebraizable classes by mod
2
Steenrod operations, a result due to Akbulut and King (
[
AK85
, Theorem 6.6]
, see also Corollary
5.2
).
To prove Theorem
1.6
, we construct cohomology classes whose images by a Steenrod operation can be shown to be non-algebraizable by the method of Kucharz
[
Kuc08
]
, hence are themselves not algebraizable.
As for the proof of Theorem
1.7
, it relies on the observation that the subring
A(M)
of
H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
is in general not stable under the action of Steenrod operations.

1.4.
Conventions

A variety over a field
k
is a separated
k
-scheme of finite type.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over the algebraic closure
k
of a finitely generated field
k_{0}
. A class in
H^{2c}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(c))
is said to be a Tate class if it belongs to the subgroup

H^{2c}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{1}\times_{k_{1}}k,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(c))^{G_{k_{1}}}
of
H^{2c}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(c))

associated with some model
X_{1}
of
X
over a finite extension
k_{1}
of
k_{0}
with absolute Galois group
G_{k_{1}}
. The classes that lie in the image of the cycle class map
\mathrm{CH}^{c}(X)\to H^{2c}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(c))
are said to be algebraic, and are automatically Tate classes. In this article, the converse statement that all Tate classes are algebraic is called the integral Tate conjecture over
k
(see
[
CTS23
, §1]
for a discussion of variants of this statement).

2.
A relative Wu theorem in étale cohomology

In topology, the behaviour of Steenrod operations with respect to proper pushforwards is controlled by a Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch-type theorem: the relative Wu theorem of Atiyah and Hirzebruch
[
AH61
, Satz 3.2]
.
In this section, we prove an analogue of this theorem in the étale cohomology of schemes.

We first recall, in §
2.1
and §
2.2
, a construction of Steenrod operations on the (twisted) étale cohomology of schemes. The characteristic classes appearing in the statement of the relative Wu theorem are defined and studied in §
2.4
, and the relative Wu theorem is proved in §
2.5
.

In this whole section we fix a prime number
\ell
and we set
S_{\ell}:=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{\ell}])
.

2.1.
Steenrod operations in étale cohomology

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
be the mod
\ell
Steenrod algebra
[
Ste62
, I §3, VI §2]
. It is a graded
\mathbb{Z}/{\ell}
-algebra generated by the Steenrod squares
(\mathrm{Sq}^{i})_{i\geq 1}
which have degree
i
(when
\ell=2
), or by Steenrod’s
\ell
-th powers
(P^{i})_{i\geq 1}
which have degree
2i(\ell-1)
and by the Bockstein
\beta
which has degree
1
(when
\ell
is odd), subject to the Adem relations.
We write
\mathrm{Sq}^{0}=P^{0}=1
. If
\ell=2
, we set
\beta:=\mathrm{Sq}^{1}
.
We use the notation
\mathrm{Sq}:=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathrm{Sq}^{i}
and
P:=\sum_{i\geq 0}P^{i}
.

The algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
acts functorially on the mod
\ell
(relative) cohomology of (pairs of) topological spaces
[
Ste62
, VII, VIII]
.
There are several ways to construct analogous Steenrod operations on the mod
\ell
étale cohomology of schemes
[
Eps66
,
May70
,
Jar89
,
Fen20
]
. We follow Feng
[
Fen20
, §3]
and Scavia and Suzuki
[
SS22
, §2]
in exploiting étale homotopy theory
[
Fri82
]
. This choice will allow us to use Scavia and Suzuki’s analysis of the behaviour of Steenrod operations in Hochschild–Serre spectral sequences in §
4.4
.

Friedlander associates with any Noetherian scheme
X
a pro-simplicial set denoted by
\mathrm{\acute{E}t}(X)
in
[
Fen20
]
: its étale topological type
[
Fri82
, Definition 4.4]
.
Let
f:Y\hookrightarrow X
be a closed immersion of Noetherian schemes with complement open immersion
j:U\to X
.
Let
M(\mathrm{\acute{E}t}(j))
be the mapping cylinder in the sense of
[
Fri82
, p. 140]
of the map
\mathrm{\acute{E}t}(j):\mathrm{\acute{E}t}(U)\to\mathrm{\acute{E}t}(X)
induced by
j
. It is a pro-simplicial set endowed with an inclusion
\mathrm{\acute{E}t}(U)\hookrightarrow M(\mathrm{\acute{E}t}(j))
.
For any abelian group
A
, it follows from
[
Fri82
, Corollary 14.5, Proposition 14.6]
that there are natural isomorphisms

(2.1)

H^{*}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t},Y}(X,A){\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}H^{*}(M(\mathrm{\acute{E}t}(j)),\mathrm{\acute{E}t}(U),A).

When
A=\mathbb{Z}/\ell
, one can use the geometric realization of simplicial sets to let
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
act on the right-hand side of (
2.1
). We may then let
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
act on
H^{*}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t},Y}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell)
functorially in
(X,Y)
, using the isomorphism (
2.1
). By construction, this action has all the properties of the action of
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
on the mod
\ell
cohomology of topological spaces (see
[
Ste62
, §I.1 and §VI.1]
). We will use them freely in the sequel.

If
X
is a complex variety, it follows from
[
Fri82
, Theorem 8.4]
that Artin’s comparison isomorphism
H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell){\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}H^{q}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}/\ell)
is
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
-equivariant.
If
X
is a variety over
\mathbb{R}
, this statement has a
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})
-equivariant analogue: the comparison isomorphism
H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell){\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}H^{q}_{\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}/\ell)
of
[
Sch94
, Corollary 15.3.1]
is
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
-equivariant as a consequence of
[
Cox79
, Theorem 1.1]
.

2.2.
Steenrod operations in twisted étale cohomology

Guillou and Weibel have explained in
[
GW19
, §§1–3]
how to extend the Steenrod operations of §
2.1
to étale cohomology with twisted coefficients. (They work with the definitions of Epstein
[
Eps66
]
and May
[
May70
]
.) We give a self-contained treatment adapted to our needs,
following their arguments closely.

Consider the morphism of schemes
\pi:S^{\prime}_{\ell}\to S_{\ell}
, where
S^{\prime}_{\ell}:=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{\ell},t]/(\frac{t^{\ell}-1}{t-1}))
and
S_{\ell}:=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{\ell}])
. It is a Galois finite étale cover with Galois group
\Gamma:=(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^{*}
, where
n\in\Gamma
sends
t
to
t^{n}
. Decomposing
\mathbb{Z}/\ell[\Gamma]
as a direct sum of one-dimensional
\Gamma
-representations yields an isomorphism

(2.2)

\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}/\ell=\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb{Z}/(\ell-1)}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes r}

of sheaves of algebras on the big étale site of

S_{\ell}
, where
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes r}
is the factor of
\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}/\ell
on which
n\in\Gamma
acts by multiplication by
n^{r}
, and where the algebra structure on the right comes from the canonical isomorphism

(2.3)

\mathbb{Z}/\ell{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes\ell-1}

sending
1
to
\zeta^{\otimes\ell-1}
for any primitive
\ell
-th root of unity
\zeta
.

Let
f:Y\hookrightarrow X
be a closed immersion of
Noetherian
S_{\ell}
-schemes, and let
{f^{\prime}:Y^{\prime}\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}}
denote its base change by
\pi:S^{\prime}_{\ell}\to S_{\ell}
.
As the projection
\pi_{X}:X^{\prime}\to X
is finite, one has
\mathrm{R}^{k}\pi_{X,*}\mathbb{Z}/\ell=0
for
k>0
.
It then follows from (
2.2
)
that one has a
\Gamma
-equivariant decomposition

(2.4)

H_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t},Y^{\prime}}^{*}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}/\ell)=H^{*}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t},Y}(X,\pi_{X,*}\mathbb{Z}/\ell)=\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb{Z}/(\ell-1)}H^{*}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t},Y}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes r}),

where
n\in\Gamma
acts by multiplication by
n^{r}
on the
r
-th factor of the right-hand side.

The action of
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
on the left side of (
2.4
) defined in §
2.1
commutes with the
\Gamma
-action by functoriality, and hence preserves the factors of the right side of (
2.4
). Let
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
act on the
H^{*}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes r})
in this way.
For all
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
,
x\in H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t},Y}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes r})
and

x^{\prime}\in H^{q^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes{r^{\prime}}})
, the standard properties of Steenrod operations imply that
\alpha(x)\in H^{q+\deg(\alpha)}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t},Y}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes r})
,
that the identities

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sq}^{i}(x)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\textrm{ for }

\displaystyle q<i

(In
[

]

\beta

Use a primitive
\ell
-th root of unity

to identify
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}
and
\mathbb{Z}/\ell
on
S_{\ell}^{\prime}
. The short exact sequence (
2.7
) for

then reads

(2.8)

0\to\mathbb{Z}/\ell\to\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell^{2}}\to\mathbb{Z}/\ell\to 0,

and we let
\varpi\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S_{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}/\ell)
be the image of

by its boundary map. The isomorphism class of the extension (
2.8
) is independent of the choice of
\zeta
(if

, multiplication by

on (

) for choices

and

of primitive
\ell
-th roots of unity). It follows that the class
\varpi
is
\Gamma
-invariant. As the degree of
\pi:S_{\ell}^{\prime}\to S_{\ell}
is prime to
\ell
, the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence shows that
\varpi
comes by pull-back from a class that we still denote by
\varpi\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S_{\ell},\mathbb{Z}/\ell)
.

We still call
\varpi\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell)
the pull-back of
\varpi
to any
S_{\ell}
-scheme
X
. When

, the class

is the image of
-1
by the boundary map of the Kummer short exact sequence. (The class

[

]
, and

Lemma 2.2
.

The Bockstein morphism

{\beta:H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t},Y}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes r})\to H^{q+1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t},Y}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes r})}
is equal to
x\mapsto\delta_{r}(x)-r\,\varpi\cdot x
.

Proof.

As the degree of the finite étale cover
\pi_{X}:X^{\prime}\to X
is prime to
\ell
, the pull-back map
\pi_{X}^{*}:H^{*}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t},Y}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes r})\to H^{*}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t},Y^{\prime}}(X^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes r})
is injective. By functoriality, it thus suffices to prove the lemma on
X^{\prime}
. Choose an isomorphism
\mathbb{Z}/\ell{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}
on
X^{\prime}
.
The morphism
\delta_{r}
is then the boundary map of
0\to\mathbb{Z}/\ell\to\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell^{2}}^{\otimes r}\to\mathbb{Z}/\ell\to 0
, while
the morphism
\beta
is the boundary map of

0\to\mathbb{Z}/\ell\to\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{2}\to\mathbb{Z}/\ell\to 0
(this is one of the standard properties of Steenrod operations).
It now follows from
[

that

\delta_{r}(x)=\beta(x)+r\,\varpi\cdot x.

(The reference
[
Gre65
]
deals with the cohomology of topological spaces. By means of the mapping cone construction, the results proven there are still valid for the relative cohomology of pairs of topological spaces.
By making use of the geometric realization functor, they still hold in the relative cohomology of pairs of (pro-)simplicial spaces.
One can then apply them in our setting using (
2.1
).)
∎

2.4.

If
f:Y\hookrightarrow X
is a closed immersion of codimension

of regular

-schemes, the Gysin morphism

defined in
[

, Définition 2.3.1]
is an isomorphism by Gabber’s purity theorem
[
Rio14
, Théorème 3.1.1]
. We may then consider the Thom isomorphisms

\phi_{f}:H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes r}){\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}H^{q+2c}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t},Y}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes r+c})

induced by

, and we let

be the Thom class.

When
X=E
is a vector bundle on
Y
with structural morphism
g:E\to Y
and

is the zero section, one has

(2.10)

\phi_{f}(x)=g^{*}(x)\cdot s_{f}\textrm{\hskip 5.0pt for all }x\in H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes r}).

In this setting, we define the étale Stiefel–Whitney classes
w^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}_{j}(E)\in H^{j}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\mathbb{Z}/\ell)
of

by the formulas

\displaystyle w^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(E)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\phi_{f}^{-1}(\mathrm{Sq}(s_{f}))

\displaystyle\textrm{ \hskip 5.0ptif }\ell=2,

\displaystyle w^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(E)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\phi_{f}^{-1}(P(s_{f}))

\displaystyle\textrm{ \hskip 5.0ptif }\ell\textrm{ is odd.}

In view of (

When

, this definition appears in

, p. 569]
for varieties over algebraically closed fields and in

, Definition 5.2]

Lemma 2.3
.

If

-scheme and if

Let

whose pull-backs by

and

Since the two pull-back morphisms

In this case, let

and

be the inclusions of the factors, let

and

The multiplicativity (

∎

If

-scheme

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle on a regular
S_{\ell}
-scheme

. Write formally

, where the

are the Chern roots of

. Then

(2.14)

\displaystyle w^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i}(1+\varpi+\lambda_{i})

\displaystyle\textrm{ \hskip 5.11108ptif }\ell=2,

(2.15)

\displaystyle w^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i}(1+\lambda_{i}^{\ell-1})

\displaystyle\textrm{ \hskip 5.11108ptif }\ell\textrm{ is odd.}

Proof.

By Grothendieck’s splitting principle, we may assume that
E
is a successive extension of line bundles.
By Lemma
2.3
, we may further assume that

is a line bundle.
In this case, we let
g:E\to Y
be the structural morphism and

be the zero section. The image of the Thom class

by the morphism

As a consequence,

If

by (

), hence

Remark 2.7

In topology, the Wu theorem describes the interaction of Steenrod operations and Poincaré duality. The same holds for Theorem

3.
Steenrod operations and algebraic classes

We now apply the relative Wu theorem proved in Section
2
to study the compatibility of Brosnan’s Steenrod operations on Chow groups (recalled in §
3.1
) and those in étale cohomology (defined in §§
2.1
–
2.2
). Our main result in this direction is Theorem
3.4
. In §

, we explain how to deduce obstructions to the algebraicity of cohomology classes. We clarify their relation with complex cobordism in §

.

Throughout this section, we fix a field

and a prime number

3.1.
Steenrod operations on Chow groups

Let
X
be a smooth
variety over
k
.
Brosnan has defined in
[
Bro03
, Definition 8.11 and §11]
an action of
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
on
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X)/\ell
such that all odd degree elements of
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
act by zero, and such that for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
of even degree and all
x\in\mathrm{CH}^{c}(X)/\ell
, one has
\alpha(x)\in\mathrm{CH}^{c+\deg(\alpha)/2}(X)/\ell
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle on a smooth variety
X
over
k
. Let
c_{j}(E)\in\mathrm{CH}^{j}(X)/\ell
be the mod
\ell
Chern classes of
E
defined as in
[
Gro58
]
(see also
[
Ful98
, p. 325]
),
and let
c(E):=\sum_{j\geq 0}c_{j}(E)
. Write formally
c(E)=\prod_{i}(1+\lambda_{i})
, where the
\lambda_{i}
are the Chern roots of
E
.
Brosnan introduces in
[
Bro03
, p. 1891]
the characteristic classes
w_{2j}(E)\in\mathrm{CH}^{j}(X)/\ell
defined by

(3.1)

w(E)=\sum_{j\geq 0}w_{2j}(E):=\prod_{i}(1+\lambda_{i}^{\ell-1}).

This definition is extended to
K_{0}(X)
by setting
w([E]-[F]):=w(E)\cdot w(F)^{-1}
.

Brosnan uses these classes to describe the behaviour of the Steenrod operations on Chow groups with respect to proper pushforwards.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be a proper morphism between smooth varieties over
k
with virtual normal bundle
N_{f}
. For
x\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(Y)/\ell
, one has

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sq}(f_{*}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{*}(\mathrm{Sq}(x)\cdot w(N_{f}))

\displaystyle\textrm{ \hskip 5.11108ptif }\ell=2,

\displaystyle P(f_{*}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{*}(P(x)\cdot w(N_{f}))

\displaystyle\textrm{ \hskip 5.11108ptif }\ell\textrm{ is odd.}\

Proof.

This follows from
[
Bro03
, Propositions 9.4 (iii) and 10.3]
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

An earlier construction of Steenrod operations on Chow groups was given by Voevodsky in the context of motivic cohomology
[
Voe03
]
. We do not know a published proof of the fact that his operations are compatible with Brosnan’s.

3.2.
Comparing Steenrod operations

Combining Proposition

Let

\displaystyle w^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\kappa)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}(1+\varpi)^{c-j}\cdot\mathrm{cl}(w_{2j}(\kappa))

\displaystyle w^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\kappa)

\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(\lambda)=\varpi\cdot\lambda
. It follows that

\mathrm{Sq}^{2}(\lambda^{2})=\varpi^{2}\cdot\lambda^{2}
is not algebraic.
This shows that Steenrod operations do not preserve algebraic classes in general.

In this example, the algebraic class
\lambda^{2}
is not sent to an algebraic class by
\mathrm{Sq}^{2}
, but it is sent to an algebraic class by
\mathrm{Sq}^{2}+\varpi^{2}
. This is a general phenomenon, as we show in Proposition
3.12
(i) below.

Traditionally, instances of Proposition
3.7
have been used to prove that some cohomology classes are not algebraic, by showing that they are not killed by an odd degree Steenrod operations.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety over
k
.
Assume that
k
contains a primitive
\ell^{2}
-th root of unity. Then
H^{2c}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes c})_{\mathrm{alg}}
is killed by all odd degree Steenrod operations.

Even over algebraically closed fields, Proposition
3.7
yields obstructions to the algebraicity of cohomology classes that go beyond the vanishing of odd degree Steenrod operations.
Over the complex numbers, that algebraic mod
\ell
cohomology classes are reductions mod
\ell
of integral Hodge classes implies the following.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
. Fix
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
and
x\in H^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}/\ell)_{\mathrm{alg}}
.

(i)

If
\deg(\alpha)
is odd, then
\alpha(x)=0
.

(ii)

If
\deg(\alpha)
is even, then
\alpha(x)
is the reduction mod
\ell
of an integral Hodge class.

As algebraic classes are reductions mod
\ell
of Tate classes, we obtain similarly:

Corollary 3.11
.

Assume that
k
is the algebraic closure of a finitely generated field. Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
k
. Fix
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
and
x\in H^{2c}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes c})_{\mathrm{alg}}
.

(i)

If
\deg(\alpha)
is odd, then
\alpha(x)=0
.

(ii)

If
\deg(\alpha)
is even, then
\alpha(x)
is the reduction mod
\ell
of an integral Tate class.

Corollary
3.10
(i) and
3.11
(i) are the well-known topological obstructions to the integral Hodge and Tate conjectures going back to Atiyah and Hirzebruch
[
AH62
]
.
Corollary
3.10
(ii) and
3.11
(ii) yield new Hodge-theoretic (or Galois-theoretic) obstructions to them.

When
k
does not contain a primitive
\ell^{2}
-th root of unity, one can still deduce from Theorem
3.4
and Lemma
3.5
stability properties of algebraic cohomology classes by Steenrod
operations, and hence obstructions to the algebraicity of cohomology classes that go beyond Proposition

3.7
and Corollary
3.9
. We give an example of such an obstruction, to be used in §
4.4
and §
4.5
, in the next proposition.

Proposition 3.12
.

Assume that
\ell=2
and let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety over
k
. Then

(i)

\mathrm{Sq}^{2}+\frac{c(c-1)}{2}\varpi^{2}
sends
H^{2c}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2}^{\otimes c})_{\mathrm{alg}}
to
H^{2c+2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2}^{\otimes c+1})_{\mathrm{alg}}
;

(ii)

\mathrm{Sq}^{3}+(c+1)\,\varpi\cdot\mathrm{Sq}^{2}+\frac{c(c-1)}{2}\,\varpi^{3}
kills
H^{2c}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2}^{\otimes c})_{\mathrm{alg}}
.

Proof.

If
x\in\mathrm{CH}^{c}(X)/2
, it follows from Theorem
3.4
that

\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{Sq}^{2}(x))=\mathrm{Sq}^{2}(\mathrm{cl}(x))+\frac{c(c-1)}{2}\varpi^{2}\cdot\mathrm{cl}(x).

This proves (i).
Applying Lemma
3.5
, we deduce that
H^{2c}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2}^{\otimes c})_{\mathrm{alg}}
is killed by
(\mathrm{Sq}^{1}+(c+1)\,\varpi)(\mathrm{Sq}^{2}+\frac{c(c-1)}{2}\varpi^{2})
. Developing and applying Lemma
3.5
again, we see that
this operator equals
\mathrm{Sq}^{3}+(c+1)\,\varpi\cdot\mathrm{Sq}^{2}+\frac{c(c-1)}{2}\,\varpi^{3}
, proving (ii).
∎

3.4.
Relation with complex cobordism

In
[
Tot97
]
, Totaro reinterpreted the Atiyah–Hirzebruch topological obstructions to the integral Hodge conjecture using complex cobordism. His point of view is that algebraic cohomology classes on a smooth projective complex variety
X
always lie in the image of the natural map
{\mu:\mathrm{MU}^{*}(X)\to H^{*}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})}
. Consequently, an integral Hodge class not in the image of
\mu
cannot be algebraic. This fact also explains the topological obstructions to algebraicity given by Corollary
3.10
(i) (see
[
BO21
, Proposition 3.6]
).
We now explain how to similarly understand the obstruction of Corollary
3.10
(ii) using complex cobordism.

Let
\mathbf{MU}
and
\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{Z})
be spectra representing complex cobordism and ordinary cohomology with integral coefficients. The morphism
\mu:\mathrm{MU}^{*}(X)\to H^{*}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
is induced by a map of spectra
\mu:\mathbf{MU}\to\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{Z})
. Let
\mathbf{H}(\pi_{*}(\mathbf{MU})_{\mathbb{C}})
denote the Eilenberg–MacLane spectrum of the graded ring
\pi_{*}(\mathbf{MU})_{\mathbb{C}}:=\pi_{*}(\mathbf{MU})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
. In
[
HQ15
, §5]
, Hopkins and Quick define a map
\iota:\mathbf{MU}\to\mathbf{H}(\pi_{*}(\mathbf{MU})_{\mathbb{C}})
inducing the complexification
\pi_{*}(\mathbf{MU})\to\pi_{*}(\mathbf{MU})_{\mathbb{C}}
on homotopy groups. If
X
is a smooth projective complex variety of dimension
n
and
c\in\mathbb{Z}
, the map
\iota
induces morphisms

(3.2)

\iota_{*}:\mathrm{MU}^{2c}(X)\to\bigoplus_{e=0}^{n-c}H^{2c+2e}(X(\mathbb{C}),\pi_{2e}(\mathbf{MU})_{\mathbb{C}}).

One recovers the morphism
\mu:\mathrm{MU}^{2c}(X)\to H^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C})
by projecting (
3.2
) onto the
e=0
summand.
In
[
HQ15
, (17)]
, Hopkins and Quick define
\mathrm{Hdg}_{\mathrm{MU}}^{2c}(X)
to be the set of
y\in\mathrm{MU}^{2c}(X)
such that all the components of
\iota_{*}y
are Hodge. They prove that any algebraic class
x\in H^{2c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
belongs to
\mu(\mathrm{Hdg}_{\mathrm{MU}}^{2c}(X))
(see
[
HQ15
, Corollary 7.12]
). We claim that this obstruction to algebraicity combining Hodge theory and complex cobordism is finer than Corollary
3.10
.

, Theorem 1.3 and §1.1]
. Simplifications to the proof were made by Pirutka and Yagita
[
PY15
, §4]
with further additions by Antieau
[
Ant16
, §3]
. We give a proof for the convenience of the reader, and claim no originality.

Recall that one may let any Lie group
H
act freely and properly on a contractible space
EH
. The resulting quotient
BH:=EH/H
is the
classifying space
of
H
.

We also recall that a continuous map
f:Y\to X
between topological spaces is an
N
-
equivalence
if
f_{*}:\pi_{q}(Y,y)\to\pi_{q}(X,f(y))
is an isomorphism for
q<N
and a surjection for
q=N
, for all
y\in Y
.
By the Hurewicz theorem
[
Hat02
, Theorem 4.37]
and the universal coefficient theorem, the morphism
f^{*}:H^{q}(X,A)\to H^{q}(Y,A)
is then an isomorphism for
q<N
and an injection for
q=N
, for all abelian groups
A
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
H
be a connected
reductive complex Lie group.
Let
p
be a prime number, and fix
N\geq 1
.
Then there exists a smooth projective complex variety
Y
of dimension
N
such that:

(i)

There exists an
N
-equivalence
Y(\mathbb{C})\to B(H\times\mathbb{C}^{*})
.

(ii)

The variety
Y
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and has good reduction at
p
.

The following lemma is
[
Eke09
, Lemma 1.1 (ii)-(iv)]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
V
be a
d
-dimensional vector space over an algebraically closed field
K
. For
n\geq d
, let
U_{n}\subset V^{\oplus n}
be the open subset of
n
-tuples that span
V
.

(i)

The complement of
U_{n}
in
V^{\oplus n}
has codimension
\geq n+1-d
.

(ii)

The algebraic group
\mathrm{GL}(V)
acts freely on
U_{n}
.

(iii)

The image of
U_{n}
in
\mathbb{P}(V^{\oplus n})
consists of
\mathrm{SL}(V)
-stable points.

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a reductive group scheme over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
(in the sense of
[
SGA33
, XIX, Définition 2.7]
) with
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C})\simeq H
(see
[
SGA33
, XXV, Corollaire 1.3]
). By
[
SGA31
, VI
B
, Proposition 13.2]
, there exists a closed embedding of group schemes
\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{d-2,\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}
for some
d\geq 2
. Composing it with

M\mapsto\bigg(\begin{smallmatrix}M&0&0\\
0&\det(M)^{-1}&0\\
0&0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigg)

yields embeddings
\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{d,\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}
and
\mathcal{H}\times\mathbf{G}_{m}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{d,\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}
(where
\mathbf{G}_{m}
is embedded by homotheties). Fix
n\gg 0
. Let
\mathcal{H}\times\mathbf{G}_{m}
act linearly on
\mathbb{A}^{nd}_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}=(\mathbb{A}^{d}_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}})^{n}
, in a diagonal way using
n
times the above representation.
By Lemma
4.5
, there exists an open subset
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{A}^{nd}_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}
on which
\mathcal{H}\times\mathbf{G}_{m}
acts freely, whose image
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{P}^{nd-1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}
consists only of
\mathcal{H}
-stable points, and such that the complement of
\mathcal{U}
in
\mathbb{A}^{nd}_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}
has codimension
\geq N+1
in the fibers of
\mathbb{A}^{nd}_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)})
(as
n\gg 0
).

Let
\mathcal{Z}:=\mathbb{P}^{nd-1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}/\!\!/\mathcal{H}
be the GIT quotient (see
[
Ses77
, Theorem 4]
). The complement of the smooth open subset
\mathcal{W}:=\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{Z}
has codimension
\geq N+1
in the fibers of
\mathcal{Z}\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)})
. Choosing a projective embedding of the projective
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-scheme
\mathcal{Z}
, one can use Poonen’s Bertini theorem
[
Poo04
, Theorem 1.1]
to find an
N
-dimensional complete intersection
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
which is smooth and included in
\mathcal{W}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
. Lifting over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
the equations of the projective hypersurfaces defining
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
in
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
yields a complete intersection
\mathcal{Y}
of
\mathcal{Z}
which is smooth of relative dimension
N
over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, and included in
\mathcal{W}
.

Define
Y:=\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{C}}
,
V:=\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{C}}
and
U:=\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
The variety
Y
satisfies (ii) by construction. Applying Hamm’s Lefschetz theorem
[
Ham83
, Theorem 2 and the remark below]
several times shows that the natural map
Y(\mathbb{C})\to V(\mathbb{C})/H=U(\mathbb{C})/(H\times\mathbb{C}^{*})
is an
N
-equivalence. In the diagram of fibrations

U(\mathbb{C})/(H\times\mathbb{C}^{*})\leftarrow(U(\mathbb{C})\times E(H\times\mathbb{C}^{*})/(H\times\mathbb{C}^{*})\to B(H\times\mathbb{C}^{*}),

the left-hand side arrow is a weak equivalence because its fibres are isomorphic to
E(H\times\mathbb{C}^{*})
hence contractible, and the right-hand side arrow is an
N
-equivalence as its fibres are isomorphic to
U(\mathbb{C})
hence are
(N-1)
-connected because the complement of
U(\mathbb{C})
in
\mathbb{C}^{nd}
has high codimension when
n\gg 0
.
Assertion (i) follows.
∎

H^{2\ell+2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\overline{W},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\ell+1))
and
H^{2\ell+2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\ell+1))
have rational coefficients. By hard Lefschetz
[
Del80
, Corollaire 4.3.9]
, so is the characteristic polynomial of
F_{u}
acting on
H^{2\ell+2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\ell+1))_{\mathrm{van}}
. By our choice of
n_{0}
, this characteristic polynomial, which has degree
2d
, cannot annihilate any root of unity of order
\geq n_{0}
. Consequently, none of the eigenvalues of
F_{u}
acting on
H^{2\ell+2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\ell+1))_{\mathrm{van}}
are roots of unity. In other words,
H^{2\ell+2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\ell+1))_{\mathrm{van}}
contains no non-zero Tate classes.

Step 4
.

The cohomology ring
H^{*}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is torsion-free.

By the weak Lefschetz theorem, the restriction map
H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\overline{W},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\to H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is an isomorphism for
q\leq 2\ell+1
and is injective with torsion-free cokernel for
q=2\ell+2
(see
[
Del80
, (4.1.6)]
). In view of (
4.3
) and Lemma
4.6
,
this implies that
H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is torsion-free for
q\leq 2\ell+2
.
Let us show by descending induction on
q
that
H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is also torsion-free for
q\geq 2\ell+3
. The assertion holds for
q\geq 4\ell+5
by cohomological dimension
[
SGA43
, X, Corollaire 4.3]
. Fix
2\ell+3\leq q\leq 4\ell+4
.
Since both
H^{q+1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
and
H^{4\ell+5-q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
are torsion-free, the long exact sequences of cohomology associated with
0\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\xrightarrow{\ell^{m}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m}\to 0

yield isomorphisms

H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})/\ell^{m}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m})\textrm{ \,and }H^{4\ell+4-q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})/\ell^{m}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}H^{4\ell+4-q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m})

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. As
H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m})
and
H^{4\ell+4-q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m})
have the same cardinality by Poincaré duality (see
[
SGA43
, XVIII, (3.2.6.2)]
), and as the cardinality of
H^{4\ell+4-q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})/\ell^{m}
is a linear function of
\ell^{m}
because
H^{4\ell+4-q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is torsion-free, we deduce that the cardinality of
H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})/\ell^{m}
is a linear function of
\ell^{m}
. It follows that
H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is torsion-free. We have thus proven assertion (i).

Step 5
.

The cohomology class
x
.

Let
y\in H^{4}(BE_{8},\mathbb{Z})
be the class given by Lemma
4.6
, let
z\in H^{4}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\overline{W},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
be the class it induces via (
4.3
), and let
x\in H^{4}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(2))
be the class obtained from
z|_{X}\in H^{4}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
by choosing an isomorphism
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(2)
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

By Edidin and Graham
[
EG97
, Theorem 1(c)]
(see also
[
Tot14
, Theorem 2.14]
), the cycle class map
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(B(E_{8}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}\to H^{4}(B(E_{8}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}),\mathbb{Q})
is surjective. Consequently, a multiple of the class
y
, hence also of
x
, is algebraic, proving (ii).

Assume by contradiction that
\alpha(\rho(x))\in H^{2\ell+2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes 2})=H^{2\ell+2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes\ell+1})
is the reduction modulo
\ell
of an integral Tate class
w\in H^{2\ell+2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(\ell+1))
. By the weak Lefschetz theorem (see
[
Del80
, (4.1.6)]
), there is an exact sequence

(4.5)

0\to H^{2\ell+2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\overline{W},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(\ell+1))\to H^{2\ell+2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(\ell+1))\to K\to 0

with

torsion-free. Since

contains no non-zero Tate class by Step

of integral classes.
This is a contradiction because

) and Lemma

. We have finally proved (iii).
∎

Remark 4.10
.

Fix

The proofs of Proposition

and Theorem

may be adapted to show that, over the complex numbers, a

4.4.
Geometrically trivial non-algebraic classes over all finite fields

In this paragraph, we work over a finite field

of characteristic
p
, with algebraic closure

. If

is a variety over

Let

be the absolute Galois group of
\mathbb{F}
, and

be the geometric Frobenius (see
[
Del74

Recall that

has cohomological dimension

and that, if

M/(F-\mathrm{Id}){\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}H^{1}(G,M).

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
p\neq\ell
be prime numbers, and let

Proof.

Consider the étale abelian sheaf

over

and view it as a finite étale group scheme

over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}

be a smooth projective scheme over

obtained by applying Proposition

to

. Define

Proposition

(i), Artin’s comparison theorem
[

, XI, Théorème 4.4]
, the invariance of étale cohomology under extensions of algebraically closed fields

[
SGA43
, XVI, Corollaire 1.6]
and the smooth and proper base change theorems

[

, XVI, Corollaire 2.2]
yield a morphism

which is an isomorphism in degree
\leq 2\ell+2
, and which is

-equivariant. That
\Phi
is an isomorphism in degree

is geometrically connected.

It is well-known that

with

, that

H^{*}(B\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{Z}/2)=\mathbb{Z}/2[x]
with

, and that, when

is odd,

with
x\in H^{1}(B\mathbb{Z}/\ell,\mathbb{Z}/\ell)
and

.
It then follows from the Künneth formula that

H^{*}(B((\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}),\mathbb{Z}/2)=\mathbb{Z}/2[x_{1},x_{2},t],

and that, when
\ell
is odd, one has

H^{*}(B((\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^{2}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}),\mathbb{Z}/\ell)=\mathbb{Z}/\ell[x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2},t]/\langle x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2}\rangle.

We now compute Galois actions. Let
|\mathbb{F}|=p^{a}
be the cardinality of
\mathbb{F}
. By
G
-equivariance of (
4.2
) and our choice of
\mathcal{H}
, one has
F(\Phi(x_{i}))=p^{a}\Phi(x_{i})
in
H^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Z}/\ell)
for
i\in\{1,2\}
.
When
\ell
is odd, one also has
F(\Phi(y_{i}))=p^{a}\Phi(y_{i})
in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Z}/\ell)
for

by
G
-equivariance of the Bockstein. In addition, since
\Phi(t)\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Z}/\ell)
is the first Chern class of a line bundle which is defined over
\mathbb{F}
, by Proposition
4.7
(ii), one has
F(\Phi(t))=p^{a}\Phi(t)
in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Z}/\ell)
.

Define
w:=\Phi(x_{1}x_{2})
. When
\ell=2
, one has

\mathrm{Sq}^{3}\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(w)=\Phi(\mathrm{Sq}^{3}\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(x_{1}x_{2}))=\Phi(\mathrm{Sq}^{3}(x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+x_{1}x_{2}^{2}))=\Phi(x_{1}^{4}x_{2}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{4}).

When
\ell
is odd, one computes similarly that

\displaystyle\beta P^{1}\beta(w)

\displaystyle=\Phi(\beta P^{1}\beta(x_{1}x_{2}))=\Phi(\beta P^{1}(y_{1}x_{2}-x_{1}y_{2}))

\displaystyle=\Phi(\beta(y^{\ell}_{1}x_{2}-x_{1}y^{\ell}_{2}))=\Phi(y_{1}^{\ell}y_{2}-y_{1}y_{2}^{\ell}).

Choose a class
\xi\in H^{0}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\overline{X},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes 2})
and define

.
The action of
F
on the group
H_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{2\ell+2}(\overline{X},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{\otimes 2})
is diagonal in a basis formed by multiples by
\xi
of appropriate monomials in
\Phi(t)
,
\Phi(x_{1})
,
\Phi(x_{2})
(and, when
\ell
is odd,

and
\Phi(y_{2})
).
Paying attention to the Tate twist, we see that the elements
\xi\cdot\Phi(x_{1}^{4}x_{2}^{2})
(when

(when
\ell
is odd) of this group are

-stable.
Taking Remark

(iv) into account, we deduce that
\mathrm{Sq}^{3}\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(z)=\xi\cdot\Phi(x_{1}^{4}x_{2}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{4})

.
∎

5.
Algebraizability of cohomology classes of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifolds

In this section, we study the algebraizability of cohomology classes of compact
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifolds. We refer to §
1.3
for context and for the relevant definitions.

5.1.
Steenrod operations and algebraizable classes

Our main results (Theorems
5.3
and
5.6
below) both rely on the fact that Steenrod operations preserve algebraizable classes.
This statement follows from the next proposition, which is due to Akbulut and King
[
AK85
, Theorem 6.6]
at least when
X
is projective.
For the convenience of the reader, we give a short proof based on the relative Wu theorem of Atiyah and Hirzebruch
[
AH61
, Satz 3.2]
. Our argument is also closely related to
[
BH61
, Proposition 5.19]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety over
\mathbb{R}
. Then
H^{*}_{\mathrm{alg}}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
is stable under mod
2
Steenrod operations.

Proof.

Let
g:Z\hookrightarrow X
be an integral subvariety of codimension
c
of
X
and let
\nu:Y\to Z
be a resolution of singularities of
Z
(see
[
Hir64
]
). Set
f:=g\circ\nu
and denote by
f(\mathbb{R}):Y(\mathbb{R})\to X(\mathbb{R})
the map induced between real point sets. Let
w(N_{f(\mathbb{R})})
be the total Stiefel–Whitney class of the virtual normal bundle
{N_{f(\mathbb{R})}:=f^{*}T_{X(\mathbb{R})}-T_{Y(\mathbb{R})}}
of
f(\mathbb{R})
.
The relative Wu theorem of Atiyah and Hirzebruch
[
AH61
, Satz 3.2]
applied to the map
f(\mathbb{R})
and to the class
1\in H^{0}(Y(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
(exactly as in
[
BW20a
, (1.60) and Remark 1.6 (i)]
) implies that

(5.1)

\mathrm{Sq}(f(\mathbb{R})_{*}1)=f(\mathbb{R})_{*}w(N_{f(\mathbb{R})}).

One has
w(T_{X(\mathbb{R})})\in H^{*}_{\mathrm{alg}}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
and
w(T_{Y(\mathbb{R})})\in H^{*}_{\mathrm{alg}}(Y(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
(see
[
BK98
, Theorem 1.5]
when
X
and
Y
are projective; in general, one may combine
[
Kah87
, Theorem 4]
and
[
BW20a
, Theorem 1.18]
). As algebraic classes are stable under pull-backs, proper pushforwards and cup-product (see
[
BK98
, Theorems 1.3 and 1.4]
under projectivity hypotheses and
[
BW20a
, §1.6.2]
in general), the class (
5.1
) is algebraic. Since
H^{*}_{\mathrm{alg}}(X(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2)
is generated by classes of the form
\mathrm{cl}_{\mathbb{R}}(Z)=f(\mathbb{R})_{*}1
, the proof is now complete.
∎

Proposition
5.1
implies at once the following corollary.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
M
be a compact
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifold. The subset of
H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
consisting of algebraizable classes is stable under mod
2
Steenrod operations.

5.2.
Examples of non-algebraizable classes

Here is our first main result.

Theorem 5.3
.

For all

, there exists a compact
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifold

and a class
x\in H^{c}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
that is not algebraizable.

Proof.

Grant and Szücs have constructed in
[

, Proof of Theorem 1.1 p. 334]
a compact
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifold

and a class
x\in H^{c}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
such that

is not in the image of the reduction modulo

(if

is not in the image of the reduction modulo

(if

Akbulut and King have shown in
[

, Theorem A (b)]
(see also
[

, Remark 4.8]
) that squares of algebraizable classes are reductions modulo

We deduce that

is not algebraizable (if

is not algebraizable (if

is odd). Theorem

now shows that

is not algebraizable even when

∎

Remark 5.4

When

E(n)
, the Thom isomorphism theorem
[
MS74
, Theorem 10.2]
shows that cup-product by
s_{n}
induces an isomorphism
H^{*}(\mathrm{BO}(n),\mathbb{Z}/2){\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\widetilde{H}^{*+n}(\mathrm{MO}(n),\mathbb{Z}/2)
.
By Thom’s definition of Stiefel–Whitney classes (see
[
MS74
, p. 91]
), one has

(5.3)

\mathrm{Sq}^{i}(s_{n})=w_{i}\cdot s_{n}.

In particular, one computes that

(5.4)

s_{n}^{2}=\mathrm{Sq}^{n}(s_{n})=w_{n}\cdot s_{n}.

Moreover, the Wu formula
[
MS74
, Problem 8-B]
shows that

(5.5)

\mathrm{Sq}^{i}(w_{j})=\sum_{t=0}^{i}\binom{j+t-i-1}{t}w_{i-t}w_{j+t},

where it is understood that
w_{j}=0
in
H^{*}(\mathrm{BO}(n),\mathbb{Z}/2)
for
j>n
.
These pieces of information and Cartan’s formula entirely determine
H^{*}(\mathrm{MO}(n),\mathbb{Z}/2)
as an algebra endowed with an action of the mod
2
Steenrod algebra.

Let
M
be a compact
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifold and let
x\in H^{n}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
be a cohomology class. It follows from the Thom–Pontrjagin construction
[
Tho54
, Théorème II.1]
that
x
is Poincaré-dual to the fundamental class of a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
submanifold of codimension
n
in
M
if and only if there exists a continuous map
f:M\to\mathrm{MO}(n)
such that
x=f^{*}s_{n}
.

We also include a proof of the following well-known lemma for lack of a suitable reference.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
K
be a finite
CW
-complex. For all
N\geq 0
, there exists a compact
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifold
M
and an
N
-equivalence
f:M\to K
.

Proof.

By
[
Hat02
, Theorem 2C.5]
, we may assume that
K
is a finite simplicial complex. Viewing
K
as a subcomplex of a simplex of high dimension, we may embed it piecewise linearly in

\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\gg 0
. Let
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a neighbourhood of
K
that is a smooth regular neighbourhood as in
[
Hir62
, Theorem 1 (a) and (a’)]
,
and set
M:=\partial\Theta
. It follows from
[
HZ64
, Corollary 1 p. 725]

that the inclusion
M\hookrightarrow\Theta\setminus K
is a weak homotopy equivalence, the inclusion
\Theta\setminus K\hookrightarrow\Theta
is an
N
-equivalence because
K
has high codimension in
\Theta
when
d\gg 0
,
and the inclusion
K\hookrightarrow\Theta
is a homotopy equivalence by choice of
\Theta
. These facts prove the lemma.
∎

We now reach the goal of this paragraph.

Theorem 5.6
.

There exists a compact
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifold
M
and an algebraizable class
x\in H^{5}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
that does not belong to the subring
A(M)
of
H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
generated by Stiefel–Whitney classes of vector bundles on
M
and Poincaré-duals of fundamental classes of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
submanifolds of
M
.

Proof.

Let
K
be the Thom space of the tautological rank
n
vector bundle on
\mathrm{Grass}(3,\mathbb{R}^{3+m})
.
Comparing
[
MS74
, Theorem 7.1 and Problem 7-B]
and applying the Thom isomorphim theorem
[
MS74
, Theorem 10.2]
shows that the restriction map
H^{*}(\mathrm{MO}(3),\mathbb{Z}/2)\to H^{*}(K,\mathbb{Z}/2)
is an isomorphism in degree
\leq 18
if
m\gg 0
. As
K
is a finite CW-complex, it follows from Lemma
5.5

that there exists a compact
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifold
M
and a continuous map
f:M\to\mathrm{MO}(3)
such that the pull-back map
f^{*}:H^{*}(\mathrm{MO}(3),\mathbb{Z}/2)\to H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
is an isomorphism in degree
\leq 18
.

By the Thom–Pontrjagin construction, the class
f^{*}s_{3}
is Poincaré-dual to the fundamental class of a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
submanifold of
M
, hence is algebraizable by
[
BT80
, Corollary 4.5]
or
[
AK81
, Theorem 1.1]
.
It follows Corollary
5.2
that the class
x:=f^{*}(w_{2}\cdot s_{3})\in H^{5}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
,
which is equal to
\mathrm{Sq}^{2}(f^{*}s_{3})
by (
5.3
), is algebraizable.

Assume for contradiction that
x\in A(M)
.
As
H^{q}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)=0
for
q\in\{1,2\}

and
H^{5}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
is generated by
x=f^{*}(w_{2}\cdot s_{3})
and by
y:=f^{*}((w_{1}^{2}+w_{2})\cdot s_{3})
, we deduce that there exists
z\in\{x,y\}
that is either the fifth Stiefel–Whitney class of a vector bundle
E
on
M
, or Poincaré-dual to the fundamental class of a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
submanifold of codimension
5
in
M
.
In the first case, one has

\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(z)=\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(w_{5}(E))=w_{1}(E)\cdot w_{5}(E)=0

in
H^{6}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
,
where the second equality follows from (
5.5
) and the third equality holds since
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)=0
. One then computes (using the Cartan formula, (
5.3
) and (
5.5
)) that
\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(x)=f^{*}(w_{3}\cdot s_{3})
and
\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(y)=f^{*}((w_{1}^{3}+w_{3})\cdot s_{3})
. This is a contradiction since both classes are non-zero by the computation of
H^{*}(\mathrm{MO}(3),\mathbb{Z}/2)
recalled above and by the injectivity of
f^{*}
in degree
6
.

In the second case, the Thom–Pontrjagin construction shows the existence of a continuous map
g:M\to\mathrm{MO}(5)
with
z=g^{*}s_{5}
. Using the Cartan formula, (
5.3
) and (
5.5
), one computes that

\mathrm{Sq}^{2}\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(s_{5})\cdot s_{5}^{2}=\mathrm{Sq}^{2}(w_{1}\cdot s_{5})\cdot s_{5}^{2}=w_{1}^{3}\cdot s_{5}^{3}+w_{1}w_{2}\cdot s_{5}^{3}=\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(s_{5})^{3}+\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(s_{5})\cdot\mathrm{Sq}^{2}(s_{5})\cdot s_{5}.

Pulling back this equality by the map
g
, we deduce that

(5.6)

\mathrm{Sq}^{2}\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(z)\cdot z^{2}+\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(z)^{3}+\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(z)\cdot\mathrm{Sq}^{2}(z)\cdot z=0

in
H^{18}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)
.
Using the Cartan formula, (
5.3
), (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), one shows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sq}^{2}\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(x)

\displaystyle\cdot x^{2}+\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(x)^{3}+\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(x)\cdot\mathrm{Sq}^{2}(x)\cdot x

\displaystyle=f^{*}(w_{1}^{2}w_{3}s_{3}\cdot(w_{2}s_{3})^{2}+(w_{3}s_{3})^{3}+w_{3}s_{3}\cdot(w_{1}^{2}w_{2}s_{3}+w_{1}w_{3}s_{3})\cdot w_{2}s_{3})

\displaystyle=f^{*}((w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}^{4}+w_{3}^{5})\cdot s_{3}),

and a similar lengthier computation shows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sq}^{2}\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(y)\cdot y^{2}

\displaystyle+\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(y)^{3}+\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(y)\cdot\mathrm{Sq}^{2}(y)\cdot y

\displaystyle=f^{*}((w_{1}^{3}w_{2}^{3}w_{3}^{2}+w_{1}^{2}w_{2}^{2}w_{3}^{3}+w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}^{4}+w_{3}^{5})\cdot s_{3}).

As both these classes are non-zero by the computation of
H^{*}(\mathrm{MO}(3),\mathbb{Z}/2)
recalled above and by the injectivity of
f^{*}
in degree
18
, this contradicts (
5.6
).
∎

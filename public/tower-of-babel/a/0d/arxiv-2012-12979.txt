Abstract
. We classify finite energy harmonic 2-forms on the asymptotically flat gravitational instanton constructed by Chen and Teo. We prove that every U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) -bundle admits a unique anti-self-dual Yang-Mills instanton (up to gauge equivalence) which we describe explicitly in coordinates. As an application, we compute the classical partition function for Maxwell theory with theta term.



1.
Introduction

The classic black hole uniqueness theorem in general relativity states that the set of asymptotically flat (or AF) stationary and axisymmetric black hole solutions of the vacuum Einstein equations is exhausted by the two parameter family of Kerr solutions (for precise hypotheses, see
[
7
, Thm 3.2]
).

In 1980, Lapedes
[
23
]
conjectured that an analogue of this Lorentzian result holds in Riemannian geometry. Consider the homogeneous space
M_{\flat}:=\mathbb{R}^{4}/\psi
, the quotient of flat
\mathbb{R}^{4}=\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
under an isometry
\psi
which acts by a rotation of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and a non-trivial translation of
\mathbb{R}
. An
AF gravitational instanton
is a complete, Ricci-flat Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
that approaches
M_{\flat}
asymptotically at infinity. This terminology is due to an analogy in quantum gravity with Yang-Mills instantons in quantum gauge theory
[
15
]
.

Notice that
M_{\flat}
admits a
T^{2}=U(1)\times U(1)
isometry generated by coaxial rotations
\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}
of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and translations
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}
of
\mathbb{R}
. Lapedes conjectured that the only non-trivial AF gravitational instantons with a 2-torus isometry are the ‘Euclidean Kerr’ manifolds (see (
A.2
)).

A counterexample was constructed thirty one years later by Chen and Teo
[
2
,
3
]
(it necessarily does not admit a Lorentzian section). The Chen-Teo AF instanton
(M,g)
has underlying manifold diffeomorphic to the complex projective plane with circle removed
M\cong\mathbb{C}P^{2}\setminus S^{1}
. Under the
T^{2}
action
(M,g)
has three nuts, two finite bolts, and two infinite bolts within the classification framework of
[
16
]
.

The aim of the present work is to classify Abelian Yang-Mills instantons on
(M,g)
. Since
M
is simply connected, such solutions are in one-to-one correspondence with (anti-)self-dual
L^{2}
harmonic 2-forms that satisfy the ‘Dirac quantization condition’, i.e., its periods over all 2-cycles lie in
2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. We first classify finite energy harmonic 2-forms on
(M,g)
, which we show are spanned by a self-dual form and two anti-self-dual forms which we construct explicitly. Essential to our construction is exploiting the
T^{2}
isometry. In particular, we derive and solve a pair of scalar PDEs whose solutions generate self-dual and anti-self-dual forms respectively.

By integrating these forms over 2-cycles, we identify those harmonic forms satisfying Dirac quantization, and prove that every
U(1)
-bundle
P
over
M
admits a unique Abelian instanton up to gauge equivalence, which we identify explicitly in coordinates. As an application, we calculate the semi-classical partition function for Maxwell Theory with theta term
[
28
,
29
]
.

Harmonic 2-forms on gravitational instantons were intensively studied in the late 90s in relation to Sen’s conjecture
[
27
]
. Gibbons
[
14
]
constructed a class of such harmonic 2-forms on the Taub-NUT geometry, by observing that Killing vector fields naturally give rise to harmonic forms and metrics which admit such Killing fields with bounded norm give rise to finite-energy harmonic forms. Hitchin
[
19
]
subsequently showed this spanned the entire space of
L^{2}
harmonic forms as part of his proof of Sen’s S-duality conjecture in that setting. This approach was also used by Etesi and Hausel to construct Abelian Yang-Mills instantons for the Euclidean Schwarzschild metric
[
10
]
. We also refer the reader to related work of Franchetti on
L^{2}
harmonic forms over asymptotically locally flat gravitational instantons of type
A_{K-1}
and
D_{K}

[
12
]
and on the Taub-bolt geometry
[
13
]
.

2.
L^{2}
cohomology and harmonic forms

Given a complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, the degree
k

harmonic cohomology
, denoted
\mathcal{H}^{k}(M,g)
, is the vector space of
L^{2}
-integrable harmonic
k
-forms. That is

\mathcal{H}^{k}(M,g):=\{\omega\in\Omega^{k}(M;\mathbb{R})|\text{d}\omega=\text{d}\star\omega=0,~\int_{M}\omega\wedge\star\omega<\infty\}.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
(M,g)
is a complete Riemannian 4-manifold of infinite volume with non-negative Ricci curvature, then
\mathcal{H}^{k}(M,g)=0
unless
k=2
.

Proof.

Harmonic
0
-forms must be constant, so since
(M,g)
has infinite volume
\mathcal{H}^{0}(M,g)=0
. Because
\mathrm{Ric}(g)\geq 0
and
g
is complete,
L^{2}
harmonic forms must be parallel (Yau
[
30
]
Thm 6), hence zero since
(M,g)
has infinite volume, so
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)=0
. Hodge duality implies that
\mathcal{H}^{4}(M,g)=\mathcal{H}^{0}(M,g)=0
and
\mathcal{H}^{3}(M,g)=\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)=0
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

For the Chen-Teo instanton
(M,g)
, we have
\mathcal{H}^{2}(M,g)\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.1
it follows that the dimension of
\mathcal{H}^{2}(M,g)
is equal to the
L^{2}
-Euler characteristic

\chi_{(2)}(M,g):=\sum_{i=0}^{4}(-1)^{i}\dim\mathcal{H}^{i}(M,g).

This can be calculated using a result of Carron
[
5
]
. Since
(M,g)
is asymptotically flat, it is quasi-isomorphic to a metric
g^{\prime}
which is flat outside of a compact set. Since Hodge cohomology on complete manifolds is invariant under quasi isomorphism (see for example
[
18
]
§2.1), it follows that

\chi_{(2)}(M,g)=\chi_{(2)}(M,g^{\prime}).

Finally, since the end of
(M,g^{\prime})
, has torsion free fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{R}\times S^{2}\times S^{1})\cong\mathbb{Z}
it follows from Carron (
[
5
]
Thm. 1.7) that
\chi_{(2)}(M,g^{\prime})
agrees with the topological Euler characteristic of
M\cong\mathbb{C}P^{2}\setminus S^{1}
, which is easily shown to equal 3. Alternatively, it equals the integral of the Euler form, which was shown to equal 3 by Chen and Teo
[
2
]
.
∎

The Hodge star operator
\star
determines an order two automorphism of
\mathcal{H}^{2}(M,g)
, so we have a decomposition into self-dual and anti-self-dual forms

\mathcal{H}^{2}(M,g)=\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}(M,g)\oplus\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}(M,g)

where
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\pm}(M,g):=\{\omega\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(M,g)|\star\omega=\pm\omega\}
.

Because the harmonic cohomology
\mathcal{H}^{2}(M,g)
is naturally isomorphic to the
L^{2}
-cohomology, there are natural linear maps

(2.1)

H^{2}_{c}(M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{H}^{2}(M,g)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\rightarrow}}H^{2}(M).

where
H^{2}_{c}(M)
and
H^{2}(M)
are compactly supported and ordinary de Rham cohomology respectively and the composition
j\circ i
is the natural map (see
[
26
]
Lemma 1.3). Since
M\cong\mathbb{C}P^{2}\setminus S^{1}
we have

(2.2)

H^{2}(M)\cong H^{2}_{c}(M)\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}

and by Poincaré duality a non-degenerate pairing

(2.3)

\displaystyle H^{2}(M)\otimes H^{2}_{c}(M)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle(\alpha,\beta):=\int\alpha\wedge\beta.

The compactification
\overline{M}\cong\mathbb{C}P^{2}
satisfies

(2.4)

H^{2}(\overline{M})=H^{2}_{c}(\overline{M})\cong\mathbb{R},

and we have a commuting diagram

(2.5)

\textstyle{H^{*}_{c}(\overline{M})=H^{*}(\overline{M})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{*}_{c}(M)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j\circ i}
\textstyle{H^{*}(M)}

where the diagonal arrows are the natural maps. From (
2.5
) it can be verified that
\mathrm{im}(j\circ i)
coincides with the image of
H^{*}(\overline{M})\rightarrow H^{*}(M)
so in particular,
j\circ i
has rank one. In §
5
we describe
i
and
j
explicitly showing they both have rank two.

The symmetric pairing

(2.6)

\displaystyle H^{2}_{c}(M)\otimes H^{2}_{c}(M)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

restricting (
2.3
) is semi-definite of rank one. We will see in Proposition
4.1
that under our orientation convention,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}(M,g)\cong\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}(M,g)\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}

which implies (
2.6
) is negative definite. This implies for instance that bolts have non-positive self intersection number.

3.
Constructing harmonic forms on a manifold with a local isometry

3.1.
Harmonic forms associated to isometries

On a complete, Ricci-flat Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
admitting an isometry generated by a Killing vector field, it is simple to obtain a harmonic two-form using the following elementary construction (see, e.g.
[
14
]
.) Using the identity

(3.1)

\text{d}\star\text{d}K=2\text{Ric}(K,\sim)\;,

where we denote by
K
both the vector field
K
and its metric dual
g(K,~)
. We see immediately for a Ricci flat space,
\text{d}\star\text{d}K=0
, which implies that
\text{d}K
is co-closed. Since it is trivially closed and
(M,g)
is complete,
\text{d}K
is a harmonic 2-form. Consequently

(3.2)

\omega^{\pm}\equiv\text{d}K\pm\star\text{d}K

are respectively self-dual and anti-self-dual harmonic 2-forms.

The
L^{2}
energy of these forms are given by

(3.3)

\frac{1}{2}||\text{d}K||^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}||\star\text{d}K||^{2}_{L^{2}}\equiv\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\text{d}K\wedge\star\text{d}K.

In the Chen-Teo metric (
4.1
), choose
K=\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}
. It can be checked explicitly (see Proposition
4.1
) that as
r\to\infty
,

(3.4)

\omega^{\pm}\wedge\star\,\omega^{\pm}=2\text{d}K\wedge\star\,\text{d}K\pm 2\text{d}K\wedge\text{d}K=O(r^{-2})\text{d}r\wedge\text{d}\theta\wedge\text{d}\psi\wedge\text{d}\phi

\omega_{+}
and
\omega_{-}
are respectively self-dual and anti-self-dual
L^{2}
harmonic forms and therefore provide two of three basis vectors for
\mathcal{H}^{2}(M,g)
.
A similar construction involving the Killing vector
\partial_{\phi}
of (
4.1
) gives rise to harmonic forms whose
L^{2}
energy density grows like
O(r^{2})\text{d}r\wedge\text{d}\theta\wedge\text{d}\psi\wedge\text{d}\phi
, which clearly diverges.

3.2.
The reduction to three dimensions

The vacuum Einstein equation,
\text{Ric}(g)=0
, for a Riemannian 4-manifold
(M,g)
admitting a Killing vector field
K
can locally be reduced to a system of equations in 3 dimensions as follows. In a local neighbourhood where
K\neq 0
, it is possible to introduce local coordinates
\tau,x^{i}
,
i=1,2,3
so that
K=\partial_{\tau}
and
L_{K}x^{i}=0
and the metric
g
can then be written in the form:

(3.5)

g=\lambda(\text{d}\tau+\Omega)^{2}+\frac{h}{\lambda}

where
\Omega
is 1-form and
h
is a
K
-invariant Riemannian metric in the transverse coordinates
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
, and
\lambda:=g(K,K)=|K|_{g}^{2}
. We will define an orientation on
(M,g)
by choosing
\text{d}\text{Vol}(g)=\lambda^{-1}\text{d}\tau\wedge\text{d}\text{Vol}(h)
to have positive orientation. Introducing the globally defined
twist one-form

(3.6)

T:=\star_{g}(K\wedge\text{d}K)

which is also a form on the orbit space (indeed note that
i_{K}T=0
) , the condition
\text{Ric}(g)=0
is equivalent to the system

(3.7)

\displaystyle R_{ab}(h)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}}\partial_{a}\lambda\partial_{b}\lambda-\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}}T_{a}T_{b}

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{|\text{d}\lambda|^{2}}{\lambda}+\frac{|T|^{2}}{\lambda}\Leftrightarrow\text{d}\star_{h}\text{d}\lambda=\frac{\text{d}\lambda\wedge\star_{h}\text{d}\lambda}{\lambda}+\frac{T\wedge\star_{h}T}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\text{d}T

\displaystyle=0\;,\qquad\text{d}\star_{h}(\lambda^{-2}T)=0\Leftrightarrow\nabla^{a}\left(\frac{T_{a}}{\lambda^{2}}\right)=0.

Here
\star_{h}
,
\Delta_{h}
, and
\nabla
refer to the Hodge dual, Laplacian, and Riemannian connection associated to
h
. We also use
|\alpha|^{2}=h^{ij}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}/p!
to refer to the inner product on a
p-
form
\alpha
induced by
h
.

Since
\text{d}T=0
, we can write
T=\text{d}\zeta
for some locally defined potential
\zeta=\zeta(x^{i})
. In the Chen-Teo case,
M
is simply connected so
\zeta
is globally defined. Finally,
T
is related to
\Omega
locally by

(3.8)

T=-\lambda^{2}\star_{h}\text{d}\Omega,

which is consistent with the
\text{d}\star_{h}(\lambda^{-2}T)=0
equation. Note that
\zeta
must satisfy

(3.9)

\Delta_{h}\zeta=\frac{2}{\lambda}\text{d}\zeta\cdot\text{d}\lambda.

where
\cdot
denotes the inner product on forms induced by
h
.

It is well known that the system
(h,\lambda,\zeta)
is equivalent to a 3d theory of gravity coupled to a ‘non-linear sigma model’. The latter in mathematician’s language is a harmonic map with a target space that is a homogeneous space. This means that
(\lambda,\zeta)
parameterize the 2d manifold
SL(2,R)/SO(1,1)
with a Lorentzian signature target space equipped with the Anti-de Sitter metric, which has constant negative curvature. In the better known setting in which
g
has Lorentzian signature that is relevant to axisymmetric vacuum solutions of general relativity, the target is
SL(2,R)/SO(2)=\mathbb{H}^{2}
equipped with its canonical constant negative curvature metric.

We turn now to the (anti-)self-duality equations. By a result of Hitchin
[
19
]
, if
|K|
grows at most linearly in
|x|
at infinity, then
\omega\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(M,g)
must be invariant under
K
. We have

(3.10)

L_{K}\omega=L_{K}\star\omega=0

which implies,
i_{K}\omega=i_{K}\star\omega=E
for some closed form
E
. In particular, if
M
is simply connected,
E=\text{d}\alpha
for a globally defined function
\alpha
. Restrict to an open set on which
|K|^{2}>0
(in the Chen-Teo metric this could correspond to the Killing field
\partial_{\tau}
in the standard chart). Then (
3.10
) can be inverted to yield

(3.11)

\omega=\frac{1}{\lambda}(K\wedge\text{d}\alpha+\star(K\wedge\text{d}\alpha))=(\text{d}\tau+\Omega)\wedge\text{d}\alpha+\star\left[(\text{d}\tau+\Omega)\wedge\text{d}\alpha\right]

which is manifestly self-dual. We have

\displaystyle\text{d}\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{d}\Omega\wedge\text{d}\alpha+\text{d}\star\left[(\text{d}\tau+\Omega)\wedge\text{d}\alpha\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\text{d}\alpha\wedge\star_{h}T+\text{d}\left[\frac{1}{\lambda}\star_{h}\text{d}\alpha\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\text{d}\alpha\wedge\star_{h}T-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\text{d}\lambda\wedge\star_{h}\text{d}\alpha+\frac{1}{\lambda}\text{d}\star_{h}\text{d}\alpha

so the condition
\text{d}\omega=0
is equivalent to

(3.12)

\text{d}\star_{h}\text{d}\alpha-\frac{1}{\lambda}\text{d}\alpha\wedge\star_{h}(T+\text{d}\lambda)=0.

This is equivalent to the following elliptic second order PDE for
\alpha
:

(3.13)

\Delta_{h}\alpha-\frac{1}{\lambda}\text{d}(\lambda+\zeta)\cdot_{h}\text{d}\alpha=0.

Similarly the anti-self-dual form
(\text{d}\tau+\Omega)\wedge\text{d}\alpha-\star(\text{d}\tau+\Omega)\wedge\text{d}\alpha
is closed if and only if

(3.14)

\Delta_{h}\alpha-\frac{1}{\lambda}\text{d}(\lambda-\zeta)\cdot_{h}\text{d}\alpha=0.

It is natural to associate an
L^{2}
energy density
E[\alpha]
to (anti)-self-dual two forms:

(3.15)

\displaystyle\omega\wedge\star\omega=\frac{2}{\lambda}|\text{d}\alpha|_{g}^{2}\;\text{d}\text{Vol}(g)=2|\text{d}\alpha|^{2}\;\text{d}\text{Vol}(g):=E[\alpha]\,.

Proposition 3.1
.

Consider a four-dimensional Ricci flat metric admitting a local one-parameter family of isometries generated by the Killing vector field
K
. Then
\alpha=\alpha_{+}:=\lambda+\zeta
satisfies (
3.13
) and
\alpha=\alpha_{-}:=\lambda-\zeta
satisfies (
3.14
) where
\lambda=g(K,K)
and
T=\star_{g}(K\wedge\text{d}K)
.

Proof.

We have from (
3.7
) and (
3.9
)

(3.16)

\Delta_{h}(\lambda\pm\zeta)=\frac{|\text{d}\lambda|^{2}}{\lambda}+\frac{|T|^{2}}{\lambda}\pm\frac{2}{\lambda}\text{d}\zeta\cdot\text{d}\lambda=\frac{1}{\lambda}|\text{d}\lambda\pm\text{d}\zeta|^{2}.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

The harmonic forms generated by the smooth functions
\alpha_{\pm}=\lambda\pm\zeta
are precisely the harmonic forms
\omega_{\pm}
(
3.2
). To see this, note the identity, which holds for the one-form
K
associated to a Killing vector field:

(3.17)

\text{d}K=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\left[\star(K\wedge T)+K\wedge\text{d}\lambda\right]

This is derived by a simple computation using the definition of the twist form
T
. We then find that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\text{d}K\pm\star\text{d}K

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\left[\star(K\wedge T)+K\wedge\text{d}\lambda\pm\star(K\wedge\text{d}\lambda)\pm K\wedge T\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\left[K\wedge\text{d}(\lambda\pm\zeta)\pm\star(K\wedge\text{d}(\lambda\pm\zeta))\right].

where we used
T=\text{d}\zeta
. As observed above, if
\text{Ric}(g)=0
, the above (anti-)self-dual form is automatically harmonic. We easily read off this corresponds to the choice
\alpha_{\pm}=\lambda\pm\zeta
.

Remark 3.3
.

The (anti-)self-dual forms generated by solutions of (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) respectively can be expressed as the curvatures of locally defined gauge potentials as follows. We may rewrite the PDEs (
3.13
), (
3.14
) in the form of a conserved current

(3.19)

\text{d}\star_{h}\left[\frac{\text{d}\alpha}{\lambda}\mp\frac{\alpha\text{d}\zeta}{\lambda^{2}}\right]=0

where we used the fact
\text{d}\star_{h}(\lambda^{-2}\text{d}\zeta)=0
which follows from the reduced system (
3.7
). This implies the existence of locally defined one-forms
P_{\pm}
satisfying

(3.20)

\text{d}P_{\pm}=\star_{h}\left[\frac{\text{d}\alpha}{\lambda}\mp\frac{\alpha\text{d}\zeta}{\lambda^{2}}\right].

This allows for the construction of local connection one-forms

(3.21)

\text{d}A^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(K\wedge\text{d}\alpha\pm\star(K\wedge\text{d}\alpha)\right)

where

(3.22)

A^{\pm}:=-\alpha(\text{d}\tau+\Omega)\pm P_{\pm}.

Equation (
3.14
) was considered by
[
4
]
(up to a Moebius transformation) in the context of constructing six-dimensional supergravity solutions fibering over the Chen-Teo metric. They found a second local solution (equation (2.38)
[
4
]
) which corresponds to solution (
4.14
). We will show this local solution extends to a global, finite energy solution and therefore determines the last remaining basis vector for
\mathcal{H}^{2}(M,g)
.

4.
The Chen-Teo metric on
\mathbb{C}P^{2}\setminus S^{1}

Recently Chen-Teo, using the integrability properties of the vacuum Einstein equations restricted to
T^{2}
-invariant solutions, constructed a new two-parameter family of AF gravitational instantons on
\mathbb{C}P^{2}\setminus S^{1}

[
2
]
. The metric is cohomogeneity-two and admits a torus action as isometries, thus producing an explicit counterexample to Lapades’ conjecture
[
23
]
. We review properties of the Chen-Teo instanton in the following section.

4.1.
The local metrics

Consider the following family of Ricci flat metrics
g
given in local coordinates
(\tau,x,y,\phi)
as follows
[
3
]
:
The local form of the metric is:

(4.1)

g=\frac{F(x,y)}{(x-y)H(x,y)}\left(\text{d}\tau+\frac{G(x,y)}{F(x,y)}\text{d}\phi\right)^{2}+\frac{\kappa H(x,y)}{(x-y)^{3}}\left(\frac{\text{d}x^{2}}{X(x)}-\frac{\text{d}y^{2}}{Y(y)}-\frac{X(x)Y(y)}{\kappa F(x,y)}\text{d}\phi^{2}\right)

where
X=P(x),Y=P(y)
are quartic polynomials

(4.2)

P(u)=a_{0}+a_{1}u+a_{2}u^{2}+a_{3}u^{3}+a_{4}u^{4}

and
F(x,y),H(x,y),G(x,y)
are polynomials in
x,y
given by

(4.3)

\displaystyle F(x,y)

\displaystyle=y^{2}X-x^{2}Y

\displaystyle H(x,y)

\displaystyle=(\nu x+y)\left[(\nu x-y)(a_{1}-a_{3}xy)-2(1-\nu)(a_{0}-a_{4}x^{2}y^{2})\right]

\displaystyle G(x,y)

\displaystyle=(\nu^{2}a_{0}+2\nu a_{3}y^{3}+2\nu a_{4}y^{4}-a_{4}y^{4})X+(a_{0}-2\nu a_{0}-2\nu a_{1}x-\nu^{2}a_{4}x^{4})Y

In what follows, we will omit the explicit dependence on the coordinates
x,y
and simply write
F,H,G
, etc. The whole solutions has 7 parameters: the 5
a_{i}
and two other parameters
\nu
and
\kappa
. The local metric (
4.1
) is a solution to the positive-signature vacuum Einstein equations, i.e.

(4.4)

\text{Ric}(g)=0,

for any choice of the
(a_{i},\nu,\kappa)
. Two of the
a_{i}
can be fixed by using scaling symmetries of the metric acting on the
a_{i}
and
(x,y)
, leaving a five-parameter family of local metrics. The remaining constants
a_{i}
are not totally arbitrary, since we will impose that the quartic
P(u)
to have four real roots, corresponding to fixed point sets of the
T^{2}
action generated by the commuting Killing vector fields
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau},\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}
. Note that
\kappa
is just an overall scaling (analogous to the parameter
m
of Schwarzschild) so we should be able to fix this without loss of generality. Assuming the parameters and coordinates are chosen such that
H>0
and
x>y

Asymptotically Flat Gravitational Instantons

Consider the flat Riemannian manifold
(M_{\flat},g_{0})
defined as the quotient space
M_{\flat}:=\mathbb{R}^{4}/\mathbb{Z}
of Euclidean
\mathbb{R}^{4}=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
under the automorphism
(\tau,r,,\theta,\phi)\mapsto(\tau+\beta,r,\theta,\phi+\beta\Omega)
, where
\beta,\Omega\in\mathbb{R}
are certain constants,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
parameterizes the first
\mathbb{R}
factor, and
(r,\theta,\phi)
are spherical coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
r>0,\theta\in(0,\pi),\phi\sim\phi+2\pi
. In this chart we may express the flat metric as

(A.1)

g_{0}=\text{d}\tau^{2}+\text{d}r^{2}+r^{2}(\text{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\text{d}\phi^{2}).

Both
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}
are Killing vector fields on
M_{\flat}
, and
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}
has bounded norm. We can think of
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}
as Euclidean analogues of time translation and rotation respectively.

An
asymptotically flat gravitational instanton
(or AF instanton) is a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
which is geodesically complete, Ricci flat, and approaches
(M_{\flat},g_{0})
asymptotically at infinity for some choice of
\beta
and
\Omega
. We will not require precise decay rates and refer the reader to the discussion given in
[
23
]
. Note that AF gravitational instantons are necessarily non-compact.

In the above definition, there is a preferred vector field
K=\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}
with constant norm in the asymptotic region. We emphasize that the orbits of
K

need not be closed
(that is,
\tau
need not parameterize an
S^{1}
). The classic example of an AF gravitational instanton is the two-parameter family of Ricci flat Euclidean Kerr metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{2}
,

(A.2)

g_{K}=\frac{f}{\rho^{2}}\left(\text{d}\tau+a\sin^{2}\theta\text{d}\phi\right)^{2}+\rho^{2}\left(\frac{\text{d}r^{2}}{f}+\text{d}\theta^{2}\right)+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}\left((r^{2}-a^{2})\text{d}\phi-a\text{d}\tau\right)^{2}

where
f=r^{2}-2mr-a^{2}
, and
\rho^{2}=r^{2}-a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta
. The family is parameterized by constants
m>0,a\geq 0
. The radial coordinate
r\in[r_{+},\infty)
where
r_{+}=m+\sqrt{m^{2}+a^{2}}
is the positive root of
f
and
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. The manifold admits a torus action as isometries generated by
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau},\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}
and the
(\tau,\phi)
plane is identified as
(\tau,\phi)\sim(\tau,\phi+2\pi)
and
(\tau,\phi)\sim(\tau+\beta,\phi+\beta\Omega)
for certain constants
\beta,\Omega
which depend on
m,a
. The metric is indeed geodesically complete and as
r\to\infty
approaches the model metric (
A.1
) and hence is an AF instanton.

Remark A.1
.

Thus this notion of AF instanton overlaps with, but does not contain, the related class of Ricci flat,
asymptotically locally flat
(ALF) manifolds (see, e.g.
[
24
]
).
However, it is not ALF because the vector field
K=\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}
, which satisfies
|K|\to 1
as
r\to\infty
, does not have closed orbits. Rather, the vector field
\hat{K}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}-\frac{a}{r_{+}^{2}-a^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}
which does indeed generate closed orbits and generate an
S^{1}
in the asymptotic region
r\to\infty
, grows linearly in
r
. Note that the ALF Euclidean Schwarzschild instanton is recovered from (
A.2
) upon setting
a=0
.

The above definition of asymptotic flatness was guided by the definition of asymptotic flatness in Lorentzian manifolds that are asymptotic to Minkowski spacetime (see the review
[
6
]
for precise definitions). Indeed the Euclidean Kerr and Schwarzschild gravitational instantons can easily be obtained from their associated Lorentz signature, asymptotically Minkowskian Ricci-flat black hole metrics by ‘analytic continuation’ of the time coordinate
t\to i\tau
of their associated Lorentz-signature, asymptotically flat, Ricci-flat black hole metrics along with suitable continuation of parameters. The fact that the Kerr spacetime exhausts the set of asymptotically flat black hole solutions motivated the conjecture, discussed above, that Euclidean Kerr would exhaust the set of AF gravitational instantons admitting a torus action (assuming that the vector field
K
which has constant norm at infinity generates an isometry)
[
23
]
. In fact one can establish this result in the special case that the Riemannian instanton is ‘static’ (i.e.
K
is orthogonal to a family of hypersurfaces) by modifying the static black hole uniqueness theorem of Israel
[
22
]
. However the standard proof of black hole uniqueness for stationary, axisymmetric solutions does not carry over to the Riemannian setting. Indeed, these proofs make use of the fact that the stationary, axisymmetric Ricci-flat equations with Lorentizan signature reduce to a harmonic map with negatively-curved Riemannian target space; the analogous formulation in the Riemannian case yields a target space with Lorentzian signature.

Appendix B
The reduction to three dimensions

Let us define two scalar potentials, called the Ernst potentials

(B.1)

\mathcal{E}_{+}=\frac{1}{2}(\lambda+\tau)\qquad\mathcal{E}_{-}=\frac{1}{2}(\lambda-\tau)

If one uses the above field equations for
\lambda,\tau
, one finds that

(B.2)

(\mathcal{E}_{+}+\mathcal{E}_{-})\Delta\mathcal{E}_{\pm}=2\nabla\mathcal{E}_{\pm}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{E}_{\pm}

The equations determine the critical points of the functional

(B.3)

I[\mathcal{E}+,\mathcal{E}_{-}]:=\int_{M/K}h^{ab}\frac{\nabla_{a}\mathcal{E}_{+}\nabla_{b}\mathcal{E}_{-}}{(\mathcal{E}_{+}+\mathcal{E_{-}})^{2}}\;\text{d}\text{Vol}(h)

which defines a harmonic map from the transverse space
(M/K,h)\to(N,G)
where the target space metric is

(B.4)

G_{AB}\text{d}X^{A}\text{d}X^{B}=\frac{\text{d}X^{1}\text{d}X^{2}}{(X^{1}+X^{2})^{2}}

This is, curiously enough, the Lorentzian metric on
N=
AdS
2
, the maximally symmetric spacetime with negative curvature. The underlying manifold is
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the Ricci curvature scaled such that
\text{Ric}(G)=-4G
.

Appendix C
Smoothness of the functions
(x,y)

Consider flat space
(\mathbb{R}^{4},\delta)
written as
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. It is obvious one can write the metric as

(C.1)

g=\text{d}r_{1}^{2}+r_{1}^{2}\text{d}\phi_{1}^{2}+\text{d}r_{2}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}\text{d}\phi_{2}^{2}

with

(C.2)

z:=\frac{1}{2}(r_{2}^{2}-r_{1}^{2}),\qquad\rho:=\sqrt{r_{1}^{2}r_{2}^{2}},

which can be inverted:

(C.3)

r_{1}^{2}=\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}-z,\qquad r_{2}^{2}=\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}+z.

Note that the function

(C.4)

\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}\right)\qquad\rho^{2}=r_{1}^{2}r_{2}^{2}

The coordinates

\mathbb{R}^{4}

g=\frac{\text{d}\rho^{2}+\text{d}z^{2}}{2\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}}+(\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}-z)\text{d}\phi_{1}^{2}+(\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}+z)\text{d}\phi_{2}^{2}.

Note that
\rho^{2}=\det g(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})
,
\partial/\partial\phi_{1}
vanishes on the rod
\rho=0,z>0

Consider a general Riemannian manifold with

in the formulas above. The functions

We now consider the Chen-Teo metric (
4.1

are smooth by definition. Now define the functions

(C.6)

R_{1}\equiv\sqrt{\rho^{2}+(z-z_{1})^{2}},\qquad R_{2}\equiv\sqrt{\rho^{2}+(z-z_{2})^{2}},\qquad R_{3}\equiv\sqrt{\rho^{2}+(z-z_{3})^{2}},

(C.7)

(z_{1}-z_{2})R_{3}^{2}+(z_{3}-z_{1})R_{2}^{2}+(z_{2}-z_{3})R_{1}^{2}+(z_{1}-z_{2})(z_{3}-z_{1})(z_{2}-z_{3})=0.

Next define the constants

(C.8)

\displaystyle n_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\xi-1}{(1-\xi)(1-2\xi^{2})^{2}(1-2\xi+2\xi^{2})},\qquad n_{2}=-\frac{1-2\xi+2\xi^{2}}{(1-\xi)(2\xi-1)(1-2\xi^{2})^{2}}

(C.9)

\displaystyle n_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4(1-\xi)\xi^{2}}{(2\xi-1)(2\xi^{2}-1)^{2}(1-2\xi+2\xi^{2})}

(C.10)

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-\xi)(2\xi^{2}-1)^{2}(1-2\xi+2\xi^{2})}\qquad f_{2}=\frac{1}{(1-\xi)(2\xi-1)(2\xi^{2}-1)^{2}}

(C.11)

\displaystyle f_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\xi(2\xi-1)(2\xi^{2}-1)^{2}(1-2\xi+2\xi^{2})}

Note that the

in our parameter range

. The relations (
4.21

4.22

(C.12)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\frac{2(n_{1}R_{3}+n_{2}R_{2}+n_{3}R_{1})+1}{2(f_{1}R_{3}+f_{2}R_{2}+f_{3}R_{1})}

(C.13)

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{2(n_{1}R_{3}+n_{2}R_{2}+n_{3}R_{1})-1}{2(f_{1}R_{3}+f_{2}R_{2}+f_{3}R_{1})}

Appendix D
A homeomorphism from
M
to
\mathbb{C}P^{2}\setminus S^{1}

We can convert between the Chen-Teo coordinates on
M\cong\mathbb{C}P^{2}\setminus S^{1}
, to the action-angle coordinates on
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
familiar in symplectic geometry, and to the homogeneous coordinates of complex geometry. The idea is that since the orbit space
M/T
is a rectangle in the
x
-
y
-coordinates, and is a triangle in action coordinates, we need only find a change of coordinates to transform that rectangle to a triangle.

Consider the linear change of variable
\tilde{x}:=\frac{x_{2}-y}{x_{2}-x_{1}}
and
\tilde{y}:=\frac{x-x_{2}}{x_{3}-x_{2}}
so that
\tilde{x}
and
\tilde{y}
range between
0
and
1
with “infinity” lying at the origin. In coordinates

(D.1)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle:=\frac{(1-\tilde{x}^{2})(1+\tilde{y}^{2})}{2}

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle:=\frac{(1+\tilde{x}^{2})(1-\tilde{y}^{2})}{2}

the orbit space is the triangular region with vertices
(u,v)=(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)
and infinity located at
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
. Then we produce a homeomorphism from
M
to
\mathbb{C}P^{2}\setminus S^{1}
in homogeneous coordinates sending

(u,v,\phi^{2},\phi^{3})\mapsto[\sqrt{1-u-v}:e^{i\phi^{2}}\sqrt{u}:e^{i\phi^{3}}\sqrt{v}].

We note however that this transformation is continuous, but not smooth along the bolts.

Appendix E
L^{2}
energy for
T^{2}
-invariant (anti-)self-dual harmonic forms

In this appendix we present an alternative method for computing the
L^{2}
-energy for the (anti-)self-dual harmonic forms constructed above. The approach applies generally to any setting in which such harmonic forms are invariant under the action of a torus isometry acting on an Ricci-flat, AF Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
. The two-dimensional subspaces of the tangent space orthogonal to the generators of this torus action are assumed to be integrable (a sufficient condition for this to hold is that
\text{Ric}(g)=0
). We will derive a general formula for the energy and then consider in detail the specific case of the Chen-Teo gravitational instanton.

E.1.
General rod strucutre

Recall that for (anti-)self-dual forms invariant under a single local isometry, the computations of §
3
showed that

(E.1)

\omega\wedge\star\omega=\frac{2}{\lambda}|\text{d}\alpha|^{2}_{g}\text{d}\text{Vol}(g)\;.

We can rewrite the PDEs (
3.13
), (
3.14
) in the form of a conserved current:

(E.2)

\text{d}\star_{h}\left[\frac{\text{d}\alpha}{\lambda}\mp\frac{\alpha\text{d}\zeta}{\lambda^{2}}\right]=0

where we used the fact
\text{d}\star_{h}(\lambda^{-2}\text{d}\zeta)=0
which follows from the reduced system (
3.7
). Now suppose that
\omega
is invariant under a second
U(1)
isometry generated in a coordinate chart by
\partial_{\phi}
. We may write the orbit space metric
h
in the form

(E.3)

h=g_{2}+\rho^{2}\text{d}\phi^{2},

where
g_{2}
is the metric induced on the integrable surfaces orthogonal to the
U(1)\times U(1)
action. Let
\beta
be any 1-form satisfying
i_{\partial_{\phi}}\beta=0
. We then have
\star_{h}\beta=\text{d}\phi\wedge(\rho\star_{2}\beta)
where
\star_{2}
is the Hodge dual operation with respect to
g_{2}
. Substituting
\beta=\text{d}\alpha
, (
E.2
) is equivalent to

(E.4)

\text{d}\star_{2}\rho\left[\frac{\text{d}\alpha}{\lambda}\mp\frac{\alpha\text{d}\zeta}{\lambda^{2}}\right]=0

The
L^{2}
energy can be expressed as an integral over
B
:

(E.5)

\frac{1}{2}||\omega||^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\frac{2}{\lambda}|\text{d}\alpha|^{2}_{g}\text{d}\text{Vol}(g)=\int_{B}\frac{|\text{d}\alpha|^{2}_{2}}{\lambda}\rho\text{d}\text{Vol}(g_{2})=-\int_{B}\lambda^{-1}\rho\,\text{d}\alpha\wedge\star_{2}\text{d}\alpha

where
B=M\setminus T^{2}
and
\rho^{2}
is the determinant of
g
restricted to Killing vectors fields
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
generating periodic flow with associated
2\pi-
periodic coordinates
(\phi^{1},\phi^{2})
. The
-
sign arises from our convention for the Hodge dual, i.e.
\star_{2}\text{d}x^{a}=\epsilon_{b}^{~a}\text{d}x^{b}
where
\epsilon_{ab}
represents the volume form associated to
g_{2}
. Then

(E.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}||\omega||^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{B}\text{d}\left[\frac{\rho}{\lambda}\alpha\star_{2}\text{d}\alpha\right]-\alpha\text{d}\left[\frac{\rho}{\lambda}\star_{2}\text{d}\alpha\right]=-\int_{B}\text{d}\left[\frac{\rho}{\lambda}\alpha\star_{2}\text{d}\alpha\right]\mp\alpha\text{d}\left[\frac{\alpha\rho}{\lambda^{2}}\star_{2}\text{d}\zeta\right]

\displaystyle=-\int_{B}\text{d}\left[\frac{\rho}{\lambda}\alpha\star_{2}\text{d}\alpha\mp\frac{\alpha^{2}\rho}{2\lambda^{2}}\star_{2}\text{d}\zeta\right]

where in the first and second lines we used (
E.4
) and the condition
\text{d}(\rho\lambda^{-2}\star_{2}\text{d}\zeta)=0
. Then Stokes’ theorem applied to the two-dimensional manifold
B
with boundary
\partial B
and asymptotic end
\partial B_{\infty}
gives

(E.7)

\frac{1}{2}||\omega||^{2}_{L^{2}}=-\int_{\partial B\cup\partial B_{\infty}}\left[\frac{\rho}{\lambda}\alpha\star_{2}\text{d}\alpha\mp\frac{\alpha^{2}\rho}{2\lambda^{2}}\star_{2}\text{d}\zeta\right].

As discussed in §4,
B
can be given global coordinates
(\rho,z)
with
\rho>0,z\in\mathbb{R}
. Note that
\partial B
, upon which the torus action degenerates, corresponds to
\rho=0
. Hence there should be no contributions to
E
from the interior of rods. However at the corner points,
\lambda
has simple zeroes. To evaluate the boundary integral, we take a semi-circular contour around each corner point and then shrink these contours to zero. Finally at the asymptotic end, note that
\rho\sim r\sin\theta
whereas
\text{d}\alpha=O(1/r)
so these contributions will converge.

In a neighbourhood of a given corner point
z=z_{i}
we may introduce an adapted spherical coordinate system
(R,\theta,\phi_{R},\phi_{L})
centred at
z_{i}

[
21
]
. In this chart, full metric can be expressed as

(E.8)

g=\text{d}R^{2}+R^{2}\left[\text{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\text{d}\phi_{R}^{2}+\cos^{2}\theta\text{d}\phi_{L}^{2}\right]+O(R^{4})

where
R>0
,
\theta\in[0,\pi/2]
,
R=0
corresponds to the centre and
\theta=0
corresponds to the symmetry axis of
\partial_{\phi_{R}}
and similarly for
\theta=\pi/2
and
\partial_{\phi_{L}}
. The Killing fields
\partial_{\phi_{R}},\partial_{\phi_{L}}
vanish to the right (
z>z_{i}
) and left
z<z_{i}
respectively of the corner point. We may express the Killing vector field
K
in terms of this basis:

(E.9)

K=c_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi_{R}}+c_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi_{L}}

for some constants
c_{1},c_{2}
.
Now as
R\to 0
,

(E.10)

\lambda=|K|^{2}=R^{2}\left[c_{1}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta+c_{2}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+O(R^{2})\right],\qquad\rho=R^{2}\sin\theta\cos\theta+O(R^{2})

and we note that the term in the square brackets is non-vanishing. Consider the first term in (
E.7
). We know
\alpha
is a smooth function, so we can write

(E.11)

\text{d}\alpha=\partial_{R}\alpha\text{d}R+\partial_{\theta}\alpha\text{d}\theta,\qquad\star_{2}\text{d}\alpha=-R\partial_{R}\alpha\text{d}\theta+\frac{1}{R}\partial_{\theta}\alpha\text{d}R

Now consider an semi-circular integration contour about the centre with
R=\epsilon
fixed and
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
. Note that

(E.12)

R\partial_{R}\alpha=x^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}\alpha,

where
x^{i}
are Cartesian coordinates and
R^{2}=\sum^{4}_{i=1}(x^{i})^{2}
. This is obviously smooth and the integral is
O(\epsilon)
and hence vanishes as the semicircle shrinks to zero. Next let us examine the term involving the twist potential. In this coordinate chart, we find that

(E.13)

\text{d}\zeta=\star_{g}(K\wedge\text{d}K)=2c_{1}c_{2}R\text{d}R

where we chose the orientation so that
(R,\theta,\phi_{R},\phi_{L})
is positively oriented. It follows that
\star_{2}\text{d}\zeta=-2c_{1}c_{2}R^{2}\text{d}\theta
. Therefore we have, as
R\to 0

(E.14)

\frac{\alpha^{2}\rho}{2\lambda^{2}}\star_{2}\text{d}\zeta=-\frac{\alpha^{2}(0)c_{1}c_{2}\sin\theta\cos\theta}{(c_{1}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta+c_{2}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta)^{2}}\text{d}\theta+O(R)

which we see is
O(1)
as we integrate on a semicircle with
R=\epsilon\to 0
. Using the fact that

(E.15)

\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{c_{1}c_{2}\sin\theta\cos\theta}{(c_{1}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta+c_{2}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta)^{2}}=\frac{1}{2c_{1}c_{2}}

we find, taking into the orientation of
\partial B
, that

(E.16)

-\int_{\partial B}\left[\frac{\rho}{\lambda}\alpha\star_{2}\text{d}\alpha\mp\frac{\alpha^{2}\rho}{2\lambda^{2}}\star_{2}\text{d}\zeta\right]=\pm\frac{1}{2}\sum_{z_{i}}\frac{\alpha(z_{i})^{2}}{c^{i}_{R}c^{i}_{L}}.

where
z_{i}
refer to the corner points with associated frequencies
c^{i}_{R},c^{i}_{L}
and the orientation is chosen so that one integrates in the order of increasing

.

Finally, we consider the contributions to (
E.7
) arising from the asymptotic end. In the asymptotic region, the metric of an AF gravitational instanton approaches

(E.17)

g=A^{2}(\text{d}\phi^{2})^{2}\text{d}r^{2}+r^{2}(\text{d}\vartheta^{2}+\sin^{2}\vartheta\left[\text{d}\phi^{1}+B\text{d}\phi^{2}\right]^{2})

for certain constants

(note that, as in the specific Chen-Teo case discussed at length above, we have chosen

to degenerate on the asymptotic axes of symmetry) and

. In particular we read off, as

. For convenience we will normalize the Killing field

as

We summarize these computations with the following proposition.

Proposition E.1
.

Suppose
(M,g)
is an AF gravitational instanton admitting a torus action as isometries such that in the asymptotic region,

approaches the model metric (
E.17

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}||\omega||^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\omega\wedge\star\omega

\displaystyle=2A\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\pi}r^{2}\sin\vartheta\left(2\alpha\partial_{r}\alpha\mp\alpha^{2}\partial_{r}\zeta\right)\mathrm{d}\vartheta\pm\frac{1}{2}\sum_{z_{i}}\frac{\alpha(z_{i})^{2}}{c^{i}_{1}c^{i}_{2}}.

and

(E.26)

\frac{1}{2}||\omega_{2}||^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{\kappa}{(1-\xi)^{2}(1-2\xi)^{2}(1-4\xi^{4})^{2}(1-2\xi+2\xi^{2})^{2}}

Finally, let us consider the computation of

(E.27)

q_{ij}:=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\omega_{I}\wedge\star\omega_{J}

where
I=1,2
label the anti-self-dual forms. For simplicity we take as our basis
\omega_{-},\omega_{2}
generated by
\alpha_{-},\alpha_{2}
respectively. We have already computed the diagonal components
q_{11},q_{22}
. An easy way to compute
q_{12}
is to use linearity of the PDE satisfied by
\alpha
. Namely, if
\alpha
and
\beta
are functions which give rise to anti-self-dual harmonic forms
\omega_{\alpha},\omega_{\beta}
, then
\alpha+\beta
will give rise to another anti-self-dual harmonic form
\omega_{\alpha+\beta}
. We have the ‘parallelogram identity’

(E.28)

\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\omega_{\alpha}\wedge\star\omega_{\beta}=\frac{1}{2}\left[||\omega_{\alpha+\beta}||^{2}_{L^{2}}-||\omega_{\alpha}||^{2}_{L^{2}}-||\omega_{\beta}||^{2}_{L^{2}}\right].

We find

(E.29)

\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\omega_{-}\wedge\star\omega_{2}=-\frac{16\kappa\xi^{3}}{(1-\xi)(1-2\xi)(1-4\xi^{4})^{2}(1-2\xi+2\xi^{2})^{2}}

This completes the calculation of
q_{ij}
.

Remark E.2
.

Consider the harmonic two-form
\text{d}K
. We may express its energy as

(E.30)

\frac{1}{2}||\text{d}K||^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\text{d}K\wedge\star\text{d}K=\frac{1}{8}(||\omega_{+}||^{2}_{L^{2}}+||\omega_{-}||^{2}_{L^{2}})=\frac{8\kappa\xi^{4}}{(1-2\xi+2\xi^{2})^{2}(1-2\xi^{2})^{2}}

Alternatively, we may compute this integral by using

(E.31)

\int_{M}\text{d}K\wedge\star\text{d}K=\int_{M}\text{d}\left(K\wedge\star\text{d}K\right)=\int_{\partial M}K\wedge\star\text{d}K

and evaluating the integral over the asymptotic boundary of
M
. It is an exercise to verify that this yields (
E.30
).

High Energy String Scattering in AdS
Luis F. Alday, Tobias Hansen, and Maria Nocchi

Abstract
We study the AdS Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude in the limit of fixed-angle high energy scattering. A recent representation as a world-sheet integral allows to compute the amplitude in this regime by saddle point techniques, very much as in flat space. This result is then compared to a classical scattering computation in AdS and agreement is found. As a byproduct of this comparison we show that AdS curvature corrections exponentiate in the high energy limit.

Introduction and main results

Perturbative string theory in flat backgrounds is relatively well understood. In principle world-sheet methods can be used to compute perturbative expansions for a variety of scattering amplitudes
Berkovits:2022ivl
. The situation in curved spacetime is very different. Currently we lack the technology to compute string scattering amplitudes in general curved backgrounds, even at tree level. In this paper we will focus on
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
. Even in this maximally supersymmetric case, the presence of RR backgrounds makes the standard RNS formulation hard to apply

In principle it is possible to use the RNS formulation to compute amplitudes in a curvature expansion around flat space. We expect this computation to become very cumbersome very quickly.
, while other formulations, such as the Green-Schwarz or pure spinor, are not yet at the point where they can yield results for scattering amplitudes. Very recently progress has been made for the simplest case: the four-graviton amplitude at tree-level
A_{4}^{AdS}(S,T)
. Tools from
AdS/CFT

A_{4}^{AdS}(S,T)\sim\int d^{2}z|z|^{-2S}|1-z|^{-2T}W_{0}(z,\overline{z})\left(1+\frac{S^{2}}{R^{2}}W_{3}(z,\overline{z})+\frac{S^{4}}{R^{4}}W_{6}(z,\overline{z})+\cdots\right)\,,

\alpha^{\prime}=1

AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
and how to make the connection to a more direct world-sheet computation. In this paper we study a regime in which such a connection, at least classically, can be made.

String amplitudes in flat space display remarkable properties in the high energy limit

A_{4}^{\text{flat}}(S,T)\sim e^{-2S\log|S|-2T\log|T|-2U\log|U|}\,,

(2)

in clear contrast to field theory amplitudes, which either diverge or fall off as a power. This behaviour can be directly understood from the integral representation for
A_{4}^{\text{flat}}(S,T)

A_{4}^{\text{flat}}(S,T)\sim\int d^{2}z|z|^{-2S}|1-z|^{-2T}W_{0}(z,\overline{z})\,.

(3)

Indeed, at high energies

z=\overline{z}=\frac{S}{S+T}\equiv z_{0}\,,

(4)

A_{4}^{\text{flat}}(S,T)_{\text{HE}}\sim W_{0}(z_{0})e^{-2S\log|S|-2T\log|T|-2U\log|U|}.

(5)

1/R

finite.

A_{4}^{\text{flat}}(S,T)\sim\int{\cal D}g{\cal D}X\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int d\zeta_{1}d\zeta_{2}\sqrt{g}g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}X^{\mu}\partial_{\beta}X_{\mu}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{4}V_{i}(p_{i})\,,

V_{i}(p_{i})\sim\int d^{2}z_{i}\sqrt{g}e^{ip_{i}\cdot X(z_{i})}

2
String scattering on AdS

We consider a classical string scattering problem on
AdS_{d}
. The relevant Lagrangian is

{\cal L}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\partial X^{M}\overline{\partial}X_{M}+\Lambda(X^{M}X_{M}+R^{2})-i\sum_{k=1}^{4}P_{k}^{M}X_{M}\delta^{(2)}(\zeta-z_{k})\,,

(14)

where we parametrise
AdS_{d}
in terms of embedding coordinates
X^{M}
, with
M=0,\cdots,d
. We denote the world-sheet coordinates by
(\zeta,\overline{\zeta})
and the location of the punctures by
z_{k}
, which we assume to be real. The conformal gauge equations of motion arising from the Lagrangian are supplemented by the Virasoro constraints

\partial X^{N}\partial X_{N}=\overline{\partial}X^{N}\overline{\partial}X_{N}=0\,.

(15)

Away from the punctures
\zeta=z_{k}
the equations of motion are

\partial\overline{\partial}X^{M}=\frac{\partial X^{N}\overline{\partial}X_{N}}{R^{2}}X^{M},

(16)

with the following boundary conditions as we approach each of the punctures

X^{M}=-iP^{M}_{k}\log\left|1-\frac{\zeta}{z_{k}}\right|+Q^{M}_{k}+\cdots\,.

(17)

The quadratic constraint
X^{M}X_{M}=-R^{2}
then implies that for each puncture

P_{k}^{M}P_{k,M}=P_{k}^{M}Q_{k,M}=0\,.

(18)

The scattering problem in flat space considered in
Gross:1987kza
;
Gross:1987ar
;
Gross:1988ue
arises as a limit of the
AdS
problem as follows. We split the indices into
M=(0,\mu)
with
\mu=1,\cdots,d
and such that

X^{M}X_{M}=-X^{0}X^{0}+X^{\mu}X_{\mu}\equiv-X^{0}X^{0}+X\cdot X\,,

(19)

where we reserve the notation
X\cdot X=X^{\mu}X_{\mu}
for the
\mu
-indices. We then consider solutions with a large
R
expansion

\displaystyle X^{0}

\displaystyle=R+\frac{1}{R}X^{0}_{1}+\cdots\,,

(20)

\displaystyle X^{\mu}

\displaystyle=X^{\mu}_{0}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}X^{\mu}_{1}+\cdots\,,

where
X^{\mu}_{0}
is the flat space solution. The corresponding momentum at each puncture
k
also admits a
1/R
expansion

\displaystyle P_{k}^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{R}p_{k,1}^{0}+\cdots\,,

(21)

\displaystyle P_{k}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=p^{\mu}_{k,0}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}p^{\mu}_{k,1}+\cdots\,.

As a result of the relations (
18
)
p^{\mu}_{k,0}
is null from the
d-
dimensional flat space point of view, which is precisely the condition for massless scattering in flat space. Furthermore, solving for
X^{0}=\sqrt{R^{2}+X\cdot X}
, the equations of motion away from the punctures and Virasoro constraints for the flat space coordinates
X_{0}^{\mu}
are simply

\partial\overline{\partial}X_{0}^{\mu}=0,~~~~~\partial X_{0}\cdot\partial X_{0}=\overline{\partial}X_{0}\cdot\overline{\partial}X_{0}=0\,.

(22)

Together with the boundary conditions, the equations of motion imply

X^{\mu}_{0}=-i\sum_{k}p_{k,0}^{\mu}\log\left|1-\frac{\zeta}{z_{k}}\right|+q_{0}^{\mu}\,,

(23)

for any constant
q_{0}^{\mu}
. Plugging this into the Virasoro constraints at leading order
we learn the locations of the punctures are related to the Mandelstam variables of the scattering process

\frac{(z_{1}-z_{3})(z_{2}-z_{4})}{(z_{1}-z_{2})(z_{3}-z_{4})}=-\frac{T}{S}\,,

(24)

where
p_{1,0}\cdot p_{2,0}=-2S
,
p_{1,0}\cdot p_{3,0}=-2T
,
p_{1,0}\cdot p_{4,0}=-2U
with
S+T+U=0
. This of course agrees with the solution found in flat space
Gross:1987kza
.

2.1
Higher orders

We would like to solve the equations of motion and Virasoro constraints in a
1/R
expansion

X^{\mu}=X^{\mu}_{0}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}X^{\mu}_{1}+\cdots\,.

(25)

To understand the systematics of this expansion, note that the flat space solution
X^{\mu}_{0}
is single-valued as we move around each puncture
\zeta=z_{k}
in the
\zeta-
plane. Indeed, the solution can be written in terms of single-valued multiple polylogarithms (SVMPLs), whose letters are the locations of the punctures. More specifically

X^{\mu}_{0}=-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{k}p_{k,0}^{\mu}{\cal L}_{z_{k}}(\zeta)\,.

(26)

Consider now the equations of motion at the next order

\partial\overline{\partial}X^{\mu}_{1}=\partial X_{0}\cdot\overline{\partial}X_{0}\,X_{0}^{\mu}=\frac{i}{8}\sum_{i,j,k}\frac{p_{i,0}\cdot p_{j,0}}{(\zeta-z_{i})(\overline{\zeta}-z_{j})}p_{k,0}^{\mu}{\cal L}_{z_{k}}(\zeta)\,.

(27)

There is a systematic procedure to ‘integrate’ the r.h.s in terms of SVMPLs. For the holomorphic derivative this is very easily done

\int d\zeta\frac{{\cal L}_{w}(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z_{i})}\to{\cal L}_{z_{i}w}(\zeta)\,.

(28)

For the anti-holomorphic derivative it is slightly more complicated, but a recursive algorithm has been worked out in
Brown2004a
;
Brown2004b
;
Vanhove:2018elu
, and it is explained in appendix
A
. The result always takes the form

\int d\overline{\zeta}\frac{{\cal L}_{w}(\zeta)}{(\overline{\zeta}-z_{j})}\to{\cal L}_{wz_{j}}(\zeta)+\cdots\,,

(29)

where
\cdots
is given by a sum of terms of uniform weight
|w|+1
. For instance, for
|w|=1
we obtain

\int d\overline{\zeta}\frac{{\cal L}_{z_{k}}(\zeta)}{(\overline{\zeta}-z_{j})}\to{\cal L}_{z_{k}z_{j}}(\zeta)+{\cal L}_{z_{k}}(z_{j}){\cal L}_{z_{j}}(\zeta)-{\cal L}_{z_{j}}(z_{k}){\cal L}_{z_{k}}(\zeta)\,.

(30)

This allows us to write

X^{\mu}_{1}=\frac{i}{8}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{4}p_{i,0}\cdot p_{j,0}\ p_{k,0}^{\mu}\left({\cal L}_{z_{i}z_{k}z_{j}}(\zeta)+{\cal L}_{z_{k}}(z_{j}){\cal L}_{z_{i}z_{j}}(\zeta)-{\cal L}_{z_{j}}(z_{k}){\cal L}_{z_{i}z_{k}}(\zeta)\right)\,,

(31)

so that the equations of motion are satisfied. Note that at each order we have the freedom to add weight one functions
{\cal L}_{z_{i}}(\zeta)
in
\zeta
. At each step we use this freedom to remove all weight one functions from the solution. This procedure can be repeated to higher orders and the solution has the following schematic form

X^{\mu}={\cal L}_{1}(\zeta)+\frac{1}{R^{2}}{\cal L}_{3}(\zeta)+\frac{1}{R^{4}}{\cal L}_{5}(\zeta)+\cdots\,,

(32)

where
{\cal L}_{n}(\zeta)
are linear combinations of pure SVMPLs of weight
n
, with either
\zeta
or
z_{i}
as their arguments
4
4

4

More precisely, by pure we mean that the entire
\zeta
-dependence is through the SVMPLs, and not, for instance, through rational functions multiplying those.
, and letters from the alphabet
\{z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4}\}
. The relation
X^{0}=\sqrt{R^{2}+X\cdot X}
implies a very similar structure for
X^{0}
:

X^{0}=R{\cal L}_{0}(\zeta)+\frac{1}{R}{\cal L}_{2}(\zeta)+\frac{1}{R^{3}}{\cal L}_{4}(\zeta)+\cdots\,,

(33)

with
{\cal L}_{0}(\zeta)=1
. A salient feature of our method, is that once the ‘seed’ solution
X^{\mu}_{0}
is given, the whole tower in
1/R
is fixed by the equations of motion and the integration procedure described above. The solution has two additional features. First, note that SVMPLs of weight higher than zero are defined so that they vanish at the base point
\zeta=0
. This in particular means that the solution we just constructed satisfies

X^{0}(0)=R,~~~X^{\mu}(0)=0\,.

(34)

The second condition is the statement that
\partial X^{M}(0)
and
\overline{\partial}X^{M}(0)
do not receive
1/R
corrections. For
\partial X^{M}(0)
this is easy to see, since at each order weight one SVMPLs with argument
\zeta
are removed, consistently with the equations of motion. For
\overline{\partial}X^{M}(0)
this is also true, but one needs to use the fact that
z_{i}
are real (which was an assumption in constructing our solutions). In the next section we will see that with these conditions it can be shown that our solution satisfies the Virasoro constraints.

2.2
Virasoro constraints

The structure of the solutions in a
1/R
expansion implies the following

\partial X^{M}=\sum_{k=1}^{4}\frac{H_{k}^{M}}{\zeta-z_{k}}\,,~~~\overline{\partial}X^{M}=\sum_{k=1}^{4}\frac{\overline{H}_{k}^{M}}{\overline{\zeta}-z_{k}}\,,

(35)

where
H_{k}^{M}
,
\overline{H}_{k}^{M}
for
k=1,2,3,4
are pure SVMPLs in
\zeta
, of higher and higher weight in a
1/R
expansion. Let us study these objects in more detail. First, they satisfy

\sum_{k=1}^{4}H_{k}^{M}=\sum_{k=1}^{4}\overline{H}_{k}^{M}=0\,,

(36)

which is simply the statement that
\partial X^{M}
and
\overline{\partial}X^{M}
do not have a pole at infinity. Furthermore
X^{M}X_{M}=-R^{2}
implies
X_{M}\partial X^{M}=X_{M}\overline{\partial}X^{M}=0
, so that

X_{M}H^{M}_{k}=X_{M}\overline{H}^{M}_{k}=0\,.

(37)

Let us now write the equations of motion in terms of the quantities
H_{k}^{M}
,
\overline{H}_{k}^{M}
. There are two equivalent ways of writing them, depending on the order in which we take the derivatives. For instance, one can derive

\sum_{k}\frac{\overline{\partial}H_{k}^{M}}{\zeta-z_{k}}=\frac{1}{R^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\frac{H_{i}^{N}\overline{H}_{j\,N}}{(\zeta-z_{i})(\overline{\zeta}-z_{j})}X^{M}\,.

(38)

Focusing on a given pole
\zeta=z_{k}
we obtain

\overline{\partial}H_{k}^{M}=\frac{1}{R^{2}}\sum_{j}\frac{H_{k}^{N}\overline{H}_{j\,N}}{(\overline{\zeta}-z_{j})}X^{M}\,.

(39)

We can now consider the contractions

S_{k,k^{\prime}}=H_{k}^{M}H_{k^{\prime}\,M}\,.

(40)

As a consequence of the equations of motions we find that
S_{k,k^{\prime}}
is actually holomorphic
\overline{\partial}S_{k,k^{\prime}}=0
. On the other hand, our procedure implies
S_{k,k^{\prime}}
is given by SVMPLs in
\zeta
. The only SVMPL which is also holomorphic is the constant function, so that

S_{k,k^{\prime}}\text{ is independent of $\zeta,\overline{\zeta}$}\,.

(41)

This result is valid to all orders in
1/R
. For the solution computed above, we can actually do better and compute these products exactly. In our procedure
H_{k}^{M}
,
\overline{H}_{k}^{M}
are given in terms of SVMPLs, where the point
\zeta=0
has been chosen as the base point. In particular this implies that we can compute
H_{k}^{M}
,
\overline{H}_{k}^{M}
exactly at that point. Indeed

H_{k}^{\mu}(0)=\overline{H}_{k}^{\mu}(0)=-\frac{i}{2}p_{k,0}^{\mu}\,,~~~H_{k}^{0}(0)=\overline{H}_{k}^{0}(0)=0\,.

(42)

Since
S_{k,k^{\prime}}
is constant, we can evaluate it at any point, and in particular at
\zeta=0
, where we obtain
S_{k,k^{\prime}}=-\frac{p_{k,0}\cdot p_{k^{\prime},0}}{4}
. Note in particular
S_{k,k}=0
. Furthermore we also have
S_{1,2}+S_{1,3}+S_{1,4}=0
, which follows from (
36
) together with
S_{k,k}=0
. The same considerations follow for the contractions
\overline{S}_{k,k^{\prime}}=\overline{H}_{k}^{M}\overline{H}_{k^{\prime}\,M}
. With this structure, the Virasoro constraints take exactly the same form as in flat space! In particular we obtain a single constraint on the location of the punctures which is now

\frac{(z_{1}-z_{3})(z_{2}-z_{4})}{(z_{1}-z_{2})(z_{3}-z_{4})}=-\frac{p_{1,0}\cdot p_{3,0}}{p_{1,0}\cdot p_{2,0}}\,,

(43)

exactly as in flat space. This proves that our construction in terms of SVMPLs satisfies both, the equations of motion as well as the Virasoro constraints. In the next section, we consider deformations of our solution, consistent with both, the equations of motion and the Virasoro constraints.

2.3
Higher order momenta and deformations

Given a seed solution
X_{0}^{\mu}
, the procedure described above gives a solution in a
1/R
expansion, to arbitrary order

X_{0}^{\mu}\to X^{\mu}=X_{0}^{\mu}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}X_{1}^{\mu}+\cdots\,.

(44)

Looking at the behaviour around each puncture, this determines the momenta in a
1/R
expansion from the seed/flat space momenta
p_{k,0}^{\mu}
:

p_{k,0}^{\mu}\to P_{k}^{\mu}=p^{\mu}_{k,0}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}p^{\mu}_{k,1}+\cdots.

(45)

In the following we will consider a family of deformations of the solution, consistent with the equations of motion and Virasoro constraints, such that the flat space momenta
p_{k,0}^{\mu}
are invariant. The first deformation is labelled by a constant vector
W^{\mu}
and reduces to translations in the flat space limit. It acts on coordinates as
X^{\mu}\to\hat{X}^{\mu}
and
X^{0}\to\hat{X}^{0}
with

\displaystyle\hat{X}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=X^{\mu}+W^{\mu}\frac{X^{0}}{R}+W^{\mu}W\cdot X\frac{\sqrt{1+W^{2}/R^{2}}-1}{W^{2}}\,,

(46)

\displaystyle\hat{X}^{0}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1+W^{2}/R^{2}}\left(X^{0}+\frac{W\cdot X}{\sqrt{R^{2}+W^{2}}}\right)\,.

(47)

In other words, it is a Lorentz transformation on the embedding coordinates

X^{M}\to\hat{X}^{M}=\Lambda^{M}_{N}X^{N}\,,

(48)

with
\Lambda^{M}_{N}\eta_{MM^{\prime}}\Lambda^{M^{\prime}}_{N^{\prime}}=\eta_{NN^{\prime}}
. As such it preserves the inner products
S_{k,k^{\prime}}
, as well as the equations of motion. As already mentioned, in the flat space limit this transformation reduces to shifts by
w^{\mu}=W^{\mu}(R=\infty)
, and hence the flat space momenta are invariant. On the other hand, it acts on the momenta at higher orders in
1/R
. We will fix the freedom implied by this deformation as follows. While the flat space/seed momenta are conserved
\sum_{k}p_{k,0}^{\mu}=0
, this is not true at higher orders in
1/R
. Since we are considering a scattering problem around flat space, where the coordinates
\mu-
parametrise this space, it seems reasonable to impose momentum conservation on the
\mu-
plane. We can use the
W-
deformation to achieve that, so that the total momentum in the
\mu-
coordinates is conserved
\sum_{k}\hat{P}^{\mu}_{k}=0
. Let us discuss this in detail. Imagine we carry out the procedure described previously. This will lead to
P_{T}^{\mu}=\sum_{k}P^{\mu}_{k}
which is non-zero from order
1/R^{2}
. Perform now a transformation such that

\hat{P}_{T}^{\mu}=P_{T}^{\mu}+W^{\mu}\frac{P_{T}^{0}}{R}+W^{\mu}W\cdot P_{T}\frac{\sqrt{1+W^{2}/R^{2}}-1}{W^{2}}=0

(49)

This can be achieved by choosing (for
P_{T}^{0}>0,(P_{T}^{0})^{2}-P_{T}^{\mu}P_{T}^{\mu}>0
)

W^{\mu}=-\frac{RP_{T}^{\mu}}{\sqrt{(P_{T}^{0})^{2}-P_{T}^{\mu}P_{T}^{\mu}}}\,.

(50)

To leading order this gives

W^{\mu}=\frac{i}{16}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j,k=1\\
i\neq j,i\neq k,j\neq k\end{subarray}}^{4}p_{i,0}^{\mu}\left(\mathcal{L}_{z_{i}}(z_{j})-\mathcal{L}_{z_{j}}(z_{k})\right)+O\left(1/R^{2}\right)\,.

(51)

On the other hand, note that the momentum will not be conserved in the
0

there is an ‘induced’ mass

. It turns out that for the choice in which the momentum along the
\mu-
coordinates is conserved, this induced mass does not depend on the puncture
P_{k}\cdot P_{k}=-m^{2}

In the remainder of the paper we will use the transformed solution (

Let us discuss another type of deformation where we rescale the seed momenta by a factor
p_{0}^{\mu}\to\lambda p_{0}^{\mu}
. This acts in a very simple way at higher orders in
1/R
. More specifically, at each puncture

p^{\mu}_{0}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}p^{\mu}_{1}+\frac{1}{R^{4}}p^{\mu}_{2}+\cdots\to\lambda p^{\mu}_{0}+\frac{\lambda^{3}}{R^{2}}p^{\mu}_{1}+\frac{\lambda^{5}}{R^{4}}p^{\mu}_{2}+\cdots\,.

(52)

At the level of the Mandelstam invariants this rescales

it modifies the Mandelstam invariants as
S\to(1+2\frac{\alpha}{R^{2}}+\cdots)S

as expected.

3
Evaluating the action

Having found the solution in a
1/R
expansion, we need to evaluate the action at this classical solution. In order to make contact with flat space, while still having a finite answer, we need to subtract a constant and the Lagrangian to consider is

{\cal L}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\partial X^{M}\overline{\partial}X_{M}-i\sum_{k=1}^{4}\left(P_{k}^{M}X_{M}+P_{k}^{0}R\right)\delta^{(2)}(\zeta-z_{k})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\partial X^{M}\overline{\partial}X_{M}-iR\sum_{k=1}^{4}P_{k}^{0}\delta^{(2)}(\zeta-z_{k})

(54)

where note that in our signature
X_{0}=-X^{0}=-R+\cdots
. In the second expression we took into account that
P_{k}^{M}X_{M}\to 0
as we approach the puncture, so its contribution vanishes. In other words, the total contribution to the action is given by the bulk action plus the sum over the ‘masses’
Rm=iRP_{k}^{0}
, which, as discussed in the previous section, is independent of the puncture. The contribution from both terms is finite. Indeed,
P_{k}^{0}R
is clearly finite while the bulk contribution is integrable. Indeed, the non-integrable divergence
\partial X^{M}\overline{\partial}X_{M}\sim\frac{P_{k}^{M}P_{k\,M}}{|\zeta-z_{k}|^{2}}
as we approach the puncture vanishes, since
P_{k}^{M}P_{k\,M}=0

and expand in
1/R

{\cal L}=\frac{\partial X\cdot\overline{\partial}X}{2\pi}-i\hskip-1.0pt\sum_{k=1}^{4}P_{k}\cdot X\delta^{(2)}(\zeta-z_{k})-\frac{1}{R^{2}}\hskip-2.0pt\left(\frac{X\cdot\partial XX\cdot\overline{\partial}X}{2\pi}-\frac{i}{2}\hskip-1.0pt\sum_{k=1}^{4}p_{k,1}^{0}X\cdot X\delta^{(2)}(\zeta-z_{k})\right)+\ldots

At leading order we obtain the usual Lagrangian in flat space.

(56)

(57)

\displaystyle-i\sum_{k=1}^{4}\left(p_{k,1}^{0}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}p_{k,2}^{0}+\ldots\right).

3.1
Universality

The AdS Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude in the high energy limit, namely large
R,S,T
with fixed
S/R
and
S/T
, takes the form

A_{4}^{AdS}(S,T)_{\text{HE}}=A_{4}^{\text{flat}}(S,T)_{\text{HE}}\times e^{\frac{S^{2}}{R^{2}}W(S/T)}\,.

(71)

This exponentiation can be understood in terms of a bosonic model describing the scattering of classical strings on
AdS

A_{4,\text{bos}}^{AdS}(S,T)_{\text{HE}}\sim e^{-\mathcal{S}}\,,

(72)

evaluated on the classical solution
-\mathcal{S}=SV_{1}(z_{0})+\frac{S^{2}}{R^{2}}V_{3}(z_{0})+\frac{S^{3}}{R^{4}}V_{5}(z_{0})+\cdots
. The universality of our results can be understood as follows. Note that at the level of the action the contribution we are computing is much smaller than the flat space contribution, indeed
SV_{1}(z_{0})\gg\frac{S^{2}}{R^{2}}V_{3}(z_{0})
, but we keep it because it is in the exponential. Higher order curvature corrections, on the other hand, can be safely ignored. In other words, in the regime we are considering, only the first order curvature corrections around flat space are important.
5
5

5

We thank J. Maldacena for discussions on this point.

Related to the universality of our results, let us discuss two ingredients that were not taken into account in the bosonic model we considered. The first is the specific form of the
AdS
vertex operators. In our computation we assumed that the overall prefactor is that of flat space. As we take into account curvature corrections this will get modified and on dimensional grounds we expect the following schematic form (see appendix C for more details)

\text{pref}_{AdS}=\text{pref}_{\text{flat}}\left(1+\frac{(X^{(0)}_{\text{classical}})^{2}}{R^{2}}+\cdots\right)\,.

(73)

But since
(X^{(0)}_{\text{classical}})^{2}\sim S
, this and higher order corrections can be disregarded in the regime under consideration. Finally, we have also ignored quantum corrections, including contributions from fermionic fields. These corrections can indeed affect our results and are encapsulated in the ‘non-universal’ function
F_{2}(z_{0})
, given above. It would be very interesting to derive
F_{2}(z_{0})
directly from a world-sheet computation. At present this is unavailable.

4
Conclusions

In this paper we have computed the AdS Virasoro Shapiro amplitude, for the scattering of four gravitons in
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
at tree level, in the fixed-angle high energy limit. This limit provides a regime where the amplitude can be computed to all orders in the curvature expansion with the result

A_{4}^{AdS}(S,T)_{\text{HE}}=A_{4}^{\text{flat}}(S,T)_{\text{HE}}\times e^{\frac{S^{2}}{R^{2}}W_{3}(z_{0})}\,,

(74)

with
W_{3}(z_{0})
a transcendental function of weight three. This result can be explicitly checked to order
1/R^{4}
against the results of
Alday:2023mvu
and is reproduced to all orders by a classical scattering computation in
AdS
. This result opens the path to a more direct comparison to a putative world-sheet description of strings on
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
. Perhaps the first step to explore this is to use the RNS formalism to compute the AdS amplitude in a curvature expansion. It would also be interesting to understand whether/how the pure spinor formalism simplifies at high energies, and whether it can be used to compute subleading corrections. There are many other directions that would be worth exploring.

The results of this paper can be fed into the program of
Alday:2023mvu
, and put strong constraints on curvature corrections at higher orders, in particular fixing the answer at the saddle point. It would be interesting to see if this can be combined with results from integrability and the structure of the solution to go to higher orders. It would also be interesting to see if sub-leading corrections to the high energy limit also follow a simple pattern. It is straightforward to extend the computation of the bosonic model to higher point functions. The location of the punctures serve as the letters of the SVMPLs from which the solution will be constructed. Furthermore we expect again that in a
1/R
expansion the location of the saddle point is the same as for flat space. It would also be interesting to study the bosonic model exactly, for finite
R
, perhaps by using a Pohlmeyer-type reduction
Pohlmeyer:1975nb
, as was done for the problem of scattering amplitudes in MSYM at strong coupling
Alday:2009yn
. Finally, a fascinating feature of the world-sheet computation is that one can go to higher genus, and the genus expansion is Borel summable
Mende:1989wt
. It would be very interesting to explore this question in
AdS
.

Appendix A
Single-valued multiple polylogarithms

The classical solutions considered in this paper are built up from single-valued multiple polylogarithms. These are built from multiple polylogarithms (MPLs) which we introduce first. They are functions of one variable
L_{w}(z)
and for the purposes of this paper they are labelled by words in the alphabet
\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3},\sigma_{4}\}
. They are recursively defined as follows

\partial L_{\sigma_{i}w}(z)=\frac{L_{w}(z)}{z-z_{i}},

(75)

together with
\lim_{z\to 0}L_{w}(z)=0
except for
L_{e}(z)=1
where
e
denotes the empty word. Here
z_{i}
denote the location of the punctures, which are taken to be real and non-zero. For instance

\partial L_{\underbrace{\sigma_{i}\cdots\sigma_{i}}_{p\text{ times}}}(z)=\frac{\log^{p}\left(1-\frac{z}{z_{i}}\right)}{p!}\,.

(76)

It is convenient to introduce the generating function

L_{\sigma}(z)=1+\sum_{i=1}^{4}L_{\sigma_{i}}(z)\sigma_{i}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{4}L_{\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}}(z)\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}+\cdots\,.

(77)

In terms of this the recursion relation (
75
) can be written as

\partial L_{\sigma}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\frac{\sigma_{i}}{z-z_{i}}L_{\sigma}(z)\,.

(78)

MPLs are not single-valued as we go around the punctures. In order to construct single-valued multiple polylogarithms (SVMPLs) we follow
Brown2004a
;
Brown2004b
;
Vanhove:2018elu
. Consider the map that reverses the order of letters in a word

w=\sigma_{i_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{r}}\to\widetilde{w}=\sigma_{i_{r}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{1}}\,.

(79)

Then SVMPLs are defined as the coefficients
{\cal L}_{w}(z)
in the following generating series

{\cal L}_{\sigma}(z)=L_{\sigma}(z)\widetilde{\left(\overline{L}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(z)\right)}\,,

(80)

where the letters
\sigma^{\prime}_{i}
are related to the letters
\sigma_{i}
by

\sigma_{i}^{\prime}={\cal L}_{\sigma}(z_{i})^{-1}\sigma_{i}{\cal L}_{\sigma}(z_{i})=\sigma_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{4}{\cal L}_{\sigma_{j}}(z_{i})\left(\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}-\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\right)+\cdots\,.

(81)

With this relation worked out, we can then determine the action of holomorphic and anti-holomorphic derivatives. Indeed

\partial{\cal L}_{\sigma}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\frac{\sigma_{i}}{z-z_{i}}{\cal L}_{\sigma}(z),~~~\overline{\partial}{\cal L}_{\sigma}(z)={\cal L}_{\sigma}(z)\sum_{i=1}^{4}\frac{\sigma^{\prime}_{i}}{\overline{z}-z_{i}}\,.

(82)

For instance, for weight two the second relation implies

\overline{\partial}{\cal L}_{\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}}(z)=\frac{{\cal L}_{\sigma_{i}}(z)}{\overline{z}-z_{j}}+\frac{{\cal L}_{\sigma_{j}}(z_{i})}{\overline{z}-z_{i}}-\frac{{\cal L}_{\sigma_{i}}(z_{j})}{\overline{z}-z_{j}}\,.

(83)

SVMPLs satisfy various relations and in particular the shuffle relations for their product

{\cal L}_{w}(z){\cal L}_{w^{\prime}}(z)=\sum_{W\in w\shuffle w^{\prime}}{\cal L}_{W}(z)\,.

(84)

These relations can be used to isolate the logarithmic singularities of SVMPLs as the argument approaches the first label. For instance

\lim\limits_{z\to z_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}w}(z)=\mathcal{L}_{\sigma_{j}w}(z_{i})\mathcal{L}_{\sigma_{i}}(z)+\,\text{finite}\,,\qquad i\neq j\,.

(85)

We found the tools
Duhr:2019tlz
;
Panzer:2014caa
very useful when working with SVMPLs, in particular for rewriting SVMPLs of
z_{1}
,
z_{2}
,
z_{3}
,
z_{4}
in terms of the cross-ratio
z_{0}
in section
3
. Finally in this work we are interested in integrating expressions containing SVMPLs over the Riemann sphere. All expressions are of the form
Vanhove:2018elu

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}z\frac{{\cal L}_{w}(z)}{(z-z_{i})(\overline{z}-z_{j})}=-{\cal L}_{\sigma_{i}w}(z_{j})+\underset{\overline{z}=\infty}{\text{Res}}\,\frac{{\cal L}_{\sigma_{i}w}(z)}{\overline{z}-z_{j}}\,.

(86)

The contribution from the point at infinity is slightly involved, however, for the integrals considered in this paper (the bulk part of the action), the total contribution at infinity vanishes.

Appendix B
Saddle points

In this paper we compute the fixed-angle high energy limit,
{\it i.e.}

|z|^{-2S}|1-z|^{-2T}=e^{-2S\log|z|-2T\log|1-z|}\,,

(89)

Expanding around this saddle point

(90)

6

6

\int d^{2}\epsilon e^{-\alpha(\epsilon^{2}+\overline{\epsilon}^{2})}=\frac{\pi}{2\alpha\,i}\,,

(91)

(92)

Appendix C
Prefactors

In the body of this paper we have ignored the prefactors in the vertex operators for the bosonic model on
AdS
. The reason is that these prefactors will be evaluated at the classical solution and the difference between the AdS and flat space computations is expected to be of the form

{\cal V}^{AdS}_{k}={\cal V}^{\text{flat}}_{k}\left(1+\frac{cS}{R^{2}}+\cdots\right)\,,

(94)

and hence they are not relevant at leading order in the high energy limit. Regardless, it is interesting to consider simple prefactors for the bosonic model under consideration. Let us consider for instance the prefactor corresponding to massless excitations

{\cal V}_{k}=\partial X^{M}\overline{\partial}X^{N}h_{k,MN},~~~\text{at $\zeta=z_{k}$}\,,

(95)

where the polarisation tensor is perpendicular to the momentum
P_{k}^{M}h_{k,MN}=P_{k}^{N}h_{k,MN}=0
.
h_{MN}
has three components. For instance, its symmetric traceless part describes the graviton, while its trace describes the dilaton. Let us consider this component. For each puncture (and suppressing the index
k
) we can take

h_{MN}^{D}=\eta_{MN}-P_{M}\rho_{N}-\rho_{M}P_{N}\,,

(96)

with
P^{M}\rho_{M}=1
and
\rho^{M}\rho_{M}=0
. The first of these conditions ensures the transversality condition, while the second is chosen for convenience. For each puncture we then need to compute

{\cal V}_{k}=\partial X^{M}\overline{\partial}X_{M}-(P_{k}^{M}\partial X_{M})(\rho_{k}^{M}\overline{\partial}X_{M})-(\rho_{k}^{M}\partial X_{M})(P_{k}^{M}\overline{\partial}X_{M})\,,

(97)

at the location of the puncture. To perform this computation it is convenient to write

\partial X^{M}=\sum_{k=1}^{4}\frac{H_{k}^{M}}{\zeta-z_{k}}\,,~~~\overline{\partial}X^{M}=\sum_{k=1}^{4}\frac{\overline{H}_{k}^{M}}{\overline{\zeta}-z_{k}}\,.

(98)

We furthermore introduce the following notation for contractions of
H_{k}
and
\overline{H}_{k}
:

\Phi_{k,k^{\prime}}(\zeta)\equiv H_{k}^{M}(\zeta)\overline{H}_{k^{\prime}\,M}(\zeta)\,.

(99)

This object is a complicated function, of higher and higher transcendentality in a
1/R
expansion, but one can explicitly check
\Phi_{k,k}(\zeta)=0
and
\sum_{k^{\prime}}\Phi_{k,k^{\prime}}(\zeta)=\sum_{k}\Phi_{k,k^{\prime}}(\zeta)=0
. Furthermore, the matrix with elements
\Phi_{k,k^{\prime}}(\zeta)
is Hermitian. After a short computation one can show

{\cal V}_{k}=\sum_{i,j\neq k}^{4}\frac{\Phi_{i,j}(z_{k})}{(z_{k}-z_{i})(z_{k}-z_{j})}\,.

(100)

This can actually be computed exactly since
\Phi_{k,k^{\prime}}(\zeta)
simplifies at the punctures. Let’s take
k=1
for definiteness and note that

H_{1}(z_{1})=\overline{H}_{1}(z_{1})=-\frac{i}{2}P_{1}\,.

(101)

This then implies

\Phi_{1,2}(z_{1})=H_{1}^{M}(z_{1})\overline{H}_{2\,M}(z_{1})=\overline{H}_{1}^{M}(z_{1})\overline{H}_{2\,M}(z_{1})=S_{1,2}=\frac{S}{2}=\Phi_{2,1}(z_{1})\,,

(102)

and in the same way

\Phi_{3,1}(z_{1})=\Phi_{1,3}(z_{1})=\frac{T}{2},~~~\Phi_{4,1}(z_{1})=\Phi_{1,4}(z_{1})=\frac{U}{2}\,.

(103)

Furthermore as
z_{1}
is real we have
\Phi_{i,j}(z_{1})=\Phi_{j,i}(z_{1})
. We have now enough constraints to fix all of them and we find

\Phi_{2,3}(z_{1})=\Phi_{3,2}(z_{1})=\frac{U}{2}\,,~~~\Phi_{2,4}(z_{1})=\Phi_{4,2}(z_{1})=\frac{T}{2}\,,~~~\Phi_{3,4}(z_{1})=\Phi_{4,3}(z_{1})=\frac{S}{2}\,.

(104)

These relations are valid to all orders in
1/R
. Similar results can be obtained at all punctures. We can then compute

\prod_{k=1}^{4}{\cal V}_{k}=\frac{S^{4}}{2^{12}(z_{1}-z_{2})^{4}(z_{3}-z_{4})^{4}}\,.

(105)

A Selberg zeta function for warped AdS 3 black holes
Victoria L. Martin, Rahul Poddar, and Agla Þórarinsdóttir

Abstract
The Selberg zeta function and trace formula are powerful tools used to calculate kinetic operator spectra and quasinormal modes on hyperbolic quotient spacetimes. In this article, we extend this formalism to non-hyperbolic quotients by constructing a Selberg zeta function for warped AdS 3 black holes. We also consider the so-called self-dual solutions, which are of interest in connection to near-horizon extremal Kerr. We establish a map between the zeta function zeroes and the quasinormal modes on warped AdS 3 black hole backgrounds. In the process, we use a method involving conformal coordinates and the symmetry structure of the scalar Laplacian to construct a warped version of the hyperbolic half-space metric, which to our knowledge is new and may have interesting applications of its own, which we describe. We end by discussing several future directions for this work, such as computing 1-loop determinants (which govern quantum corrections) on the quotient spacetimes we consider, as well as adapting the formalism presented here to more generic orbifolds.

) (the latter of which we do not have). However, we will now see that we can make progress by borrowing a trick from the hidden conformal symmetry program of
Castro:2010fd
and others, by studying instead the symmetry structure of the scalar wave equation. We outline our general approach below before moving on to the specific examples of spacelike warped AdS
3
black holes in Section
3.1
and the self-dual solutions in Section
3.2
.

Consider again the Poincaré patch metric of AdS
3
:

ds^{2}=\frac{L^{2}}{z^{2}}(dw^{+}dw^{-}+dz^{2}).

(33)

The isometry group of (
33
), SL
(2,\mathbb{R})\times
SL
(2,\mathbb{R})
, is generated by six Killing vectors:

\begin{split}H_{1}&=i\partial_{+},\qquad H_{0}=i\left(w^{+}\partial_{+}+\frac{1}{2}z\partial_{z}\right),\qquad H_{-1}=i((w^{+})^{2}\partial_{+}+w^{+}z\partial_{z}-z^{2}\partial_{-}),\\
\bar{H}_{1}&=i\partial_{-},\qquad\bar{H}_{0}=i\left(w^{-}\partial_{-}+\frac{1}{2}z\partial_{z}\right),\qquad\bar{H}_{-1}=i((w^{-})^{2}\partial_{-}+w^{-}z\partial_{z}-z^{2}\partial_{+}).\end{split}

(34)

For the case of warped AdS
3
, the warp parameter
\nu\neq 1
in (
19
) breaks the unbarred SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) symmetry down to a U(1), and thus the Killing vectors of warped AdS
3
are
(H_{0},\bar{H}_{0},\bar{H}_{\pm 1})
.
Our goal in this section is to construct a locally spacelike warped AdS
3
metric in terms of these coordinates, which will be our “warped Poincaré patch.”

Consider for now a massless scalar field
\Phi
on one of our backgrounds of interest (either the spacelike warped AdS
3
black hole or the self-dual solution). In either case, one can show that the solutions of the Klein-Gordon equation are hypergeometric functions (as we will see for the massive scalar case in Section
4
). Since hypergeometric functions transform in representations of SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
), we conclude that the Klein-Gordon operator is related to the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) quadratic Casimir:

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{2}&=-\bar{H}_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\bar{H}_{1}\bar{H}_{-1}+\bar{H}_{-1}\bar{H}_{1})\\
&=\frac{1}{4}(z^{2}\partial_{z}^{2}-z\partial_{z})+z^{2}\partial_{+}\partial_{-}\,.\\
\end{split}

(35)

In particular, we would like for the Klein-Gordon operator to be proportional to the entire SL(2,
\mathbb{R})\times
U(1) Casimir:

(\mathcal{H}^{2}+\lambda H_{0}^{2})\Phi\propto\nabla^{2}\Phi

(36)

where the coefficient
\lambda
of the U(1) generator is yet to be determined.

Our job now is to find a coordinate transformation

\begin{split}w^{+}&=\sqrt{\frac{x-\frac{1}{2}}{x+\frac{1}{2}}}e^{\alpha\phi+\beta t},\\
w^{-}&=\sqrt{\frac{x-\frac{1}{2}}{x+\frac{1}{2}}}e^{\gamma\phi+\delta t},\\
z&=\sqrt{\frac{1}{x+\frac{1}{2}}}e^{1/2((\alpha+\gamma)\phi+(\beta+\delta)t)},\end{split}

(37)

such that (
36
) holds.
In (
37
),
x
is related to the BTZ-like radial coordinate
r
in (
26
) via
x=\frac{r^{2}-1/2(r_{+}^{2}+r_{-}^{2})}{r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2}}
.
For the field ansatz

\Phi=R(x)e^{i(k\phi-\omega t)},

(38)

we find that the quadratic Casimir constructed from the conformal coordinates (
37
) and generators (
34
) is

\begin{split}&(\mathcal{H}^{2}+\lambda H_{0}^{2})\Phi\\
&=\left(\partial_{x}\left(x^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\partial_{x}+\frac{(\omega(\alpha+\gamma)+k(\beta+\delta))^{2}}{4\left(x-\frac{1}{2}\right)(\beta\gamma-\alpha\delta)^{2}}-\frac{(\omega(\alpha-\gamma)+k(\beta-\delta))^{2}}{4\left(x+\frac{1}{2}\right)(\beta\gamma-\alpha\delta)^{2}}+\lambda\frac{(k\delta+\gamma\omega)^{2}}{(\beta\gamma-\alpha\delta)^{2}}\right)\Phi.\end{split}

(39)

Comparing this Casimir to the Klein-Gordon operator will allow us to solve for the conformal coordinate parameters
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)
. With those in hand, we will be able to interpret the identification
\phi\rightarrow\phi+2\pi
group-theoretically, in analogy to
perry2003selberg
, and build a Selberg zeta function for warped black holes. As a nice bonus, we find a warped version of the Poincaré patch metric (
33
), which reduces to (
33
) when
\nu=1
.

Before we move on to specific examples in the next subsections, some comments about (
39
) are in order. Hidden conformal symmetry was studied in warped AdS
3
black holes in
fareghbal2010hidden
and in self-dual warped solutions in
Li:2010zr
. In both cases, the authors throw away a term in the Klein-Gordon operator, making a “near-region limit” or “soft hair” argument, in analogy to
Castro:2010fd
. We show below that the terms they throw away are nothing more than the U(1) piece corresponding to our
\lambda
term in (
39
). We argue that there is no need to take such a limit to discard such terms, as they too are built into the symmetry structure of the Casimir.

3.1
Warped AdS

The Klein-Gordon operator of the warped AdS

In

(42)

As mentioned previously, in

There is no need to throw away this piece, as it is part of the symmetry group and does not cause any obstruction in building the conformal coordinates. In fact, the U(1) piece will help us choose the proper solution branch, as we will see.

Equating the coefficients of

4.1
Conformal weights of the scalar field

Here we compute the conformal weight of highest weight representations of the symmetry algebra of the black hole spacetime.
We use the relationship between the quadratic Casimir and the Laplacian of the massive scalar
Anninos:2009jt

(\mathcal{H}^{2}+\lambda H_{0}^{2})\Phi=\frac{L^{2}}{\nu^{2}+3}m^{2}\Phi.

(59)

We can express the SL(2,
\mathbb{R})
Casimir in terms of the SL(2,
\mathbb{R})
conformal weight

\mathcal{H}^{2}\Phi=h(h-1)\Phi,

(60)

and we know from (

\lambda H_{0}^{2}\Phi=S(k,\omega)\Phi.

(61)

Combining the two we have

h(h-1)=\frac{L^{2}}{\nu^{2}+3}m^{2}-S.

(62)

Solving for
\Delta=2h
and choosing the positive branch, we obtain the conformal weight of the scalar field:

\Delta=1+\sqrt{1+4\left(\frac{m^{2}L^{2}}{\nu^{2}+3}-S\right)}.

(63)

Note that
S
depends on the choice of coordinates.
In the
(\tilde{t},\rho,\tilde{\phi})
coordinates of (
21
), we have

S=\frac{3L^{2}(\nu^{2}-1)}{\nu^{2}+3}\omega^{2}

(64)

which reproduces the result in
Chen:2009hg
.
In the coordinates of
(t,r,\phi)
(
26
), we have

S=\frac{3L^{2}(\nu^{2}-1)}{\nu^{2}+3}\left(\frac{k-L\omega}{r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2}}\right)^{2}

(65)

and in the
(\tilde{\tau},\tilde{r},\tilde{\theta})
coordinates of (
31
) for the self-dual solution, we have

S=\frac{3k^{2}\left(\nu^{2}-1\right)}{4\tilde{\alpha}^{2}\nu^{2}}.

(66)

Equation (

Anninos:2008fx

Notice that for large values of

or

) and (

4.2
Thermal frequencies

A procedure for calculating thermal frequencies in rotating spacetimes was outlined in
Castro:2017mfj
. Due to translation symmetry in both
t
and
\phi
, we can use the following ansatz for
\Phi
:

\Phi(t,r,\phi)=e^{-i\omega t+ik\phi}f(r).

(67)

Using this ansatz in the equation of motion, we obtain a second order differential equation in
r
, which we examine around the outer horizon
r_{+}
:

A(r)f(r)+B(r)(r-r_{+})f^{\prime}(r)+(r-r_{+})^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(r)=0.

(68)

For the spacelike warped AdS
3
black hole, we have

\begin{split}A&=\frac{16r^{2}L^{2}}{((r+r_{+})(r^{2}-r_{-}^{2}))^{2}}\bigg(k^{2}g_{tt}+k\omega g_{t\phi}+\omega^{2}g_{\phi\phi}\bigg)\\
B&=\frac{3r^{4}-r_{+}^{2}r_{-}^{2}-r^{2}(r_{+}^{2}+r_{-}^{2})}{r(r^{2}-r_{-}^{2})(r+r_{+})}.\end{split}

(69)

To construct the indicial equation, we must input a power series solution around
r_{+}
for
A,B
and
f
, like so:

\begin{split}A=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(r-r_{+})^{n},\quad B=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}(r-r_{+})^{n},\quad f=(r-r_{+})^{\alpha}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}(r-r_{+})^{n}.\end{split}

(70)

We only need the leading terms of
A
and
B
for the indicial equation, which after some algebra turn out to be:

\begin{split}a_{0}&=\left(\frac{L(2r_{-}^{2}-2r_{+}^{2}(\nu-1)+r_{+}r_{-}(\sqrt{\nu^{2}+3}-4))k+Lr_{+}\left(2\nu r_{+}-r_{-}\sqrt{\nu^{2}+3}\right)\omega}{(r_{+}-r_{-})^{2}(r_{+}+r_{-})(\nu^{2}+3)}\right)^{2},\\
b_{0}&=1.\end{split}

(71)

If we set
\alpha=in/2
with
n
an integer, we can solve the indicial equation for
\omega
in terms of
n
, obtaining the thermal frequencies:

\omega_{n}=\frac{in(r_{+}-r_{-})^{2}(r_{+}+r_{-})(\nu^{2}+3)-2Lk(2r_{-}^{2}+2r_{+}^{2}(1-\nu)+r_{+}r_{-}(\sqrt{\nu^{2}+3}-4))}{2L^{2}r_{+}\left(2\nu r_{+}-r_{-}\sqrt{\nu^{2}+3}\right)}.

(72)

If we set
\nu
to 1, we recover the thermal frequencies for the BTZ black hole
Castro:2017mfj

\omega_{n}=\frac{Lkr_{-}+in(r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2})}{L^{2}r_{+}},

(73)

which are also known as the Matsubara frequencies. In the
(\tilde{t},\rho,\tilde{\phi})
coordinates of (
21

\omega_{n}=\frac{-4k-in(\rho_{+}-\rho_{-})(\nu^{2}+3)}{4\rho_{+}\nu-2\sqrt{\rho_{+}\rho_{-}(\nu^{2}+3)}}.

(74)

Repeating the analysis in the self-dual solution (

\omega_{n}=-\frac{(\tilde{r}_{-}+\tilde{r}_{+})(k-i\tilde{\alpha}n)}{2\tilde{\alpha}}.

(75)

, as we will see.
Thus we obtain

\omega^{*}_{L}=\frac{k}{L}-i\frac{(\Delta+2j)\left(\nu^{2}+3\right)(r_{+}-r_{-})}{4L^{2}\nu},

(84)

and

\omega^{*}_{R}=-\frac{k}{L}\left(\frac{2\left(r_{+}-r_{-}\right)^{2}}{\nu(r_{+}^{2}+r_{-}^{2})-r_{+}r_{-}\sqrt{\nu^{2}+3}}-1\right)-\frac{i\left(\nu^{2}+3\right)\left(r_{+}+r_{-}\right)\left(r_{+}-r_{-}\right)^{2}(\Delta+2j)}{4L^{2}\left(\nu(r_{+}^{2}+r_{-}^{2})-r_{+}r_{-}\sqrt{\nu^{2}+3}\right)}.

(85)

We recover the BTZ quasinormal modes as calculated in
Birmingham:2001hc
upon setting
\nu
to 1.

4.3.2
Warped self-dual solutions

We compute the quasinormal modes for the self-dual solutions and reproduce previous results
Li:2010zr
;
Li:2010sv
in
(\tilde{\tau},\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\theta})
coordinates. Using the same ansatz:
\Phi(\tilde{\tau},\zeta,\tilde{\theta})=e^{-i\omega\tilde{\tau}+ik\tilde{\theta}}f(\zeta)

and change of coordinates

\zeta=\frac{\tilde{r}^{2}-\tilde{r}_{+}^{2}}{\tilde{r}^{2}-\tilde{r}_{-}^{2}},

(86)

we reach the same radial equation as before
(
78
), where

\begin{split}&A=\left(\frac{k}{2\tilde{\alpha}}+\frac{\omega}{\tilde{r}_{+}+\tilde{r}_{-}}\right)^{2},\\
&B=-\left(\frac{k}{2\tilde{\alpha}}-\frac{\omega}{\tilde{r}_{+}+\tilde{r}_{-}}\right)^{2},\\
&C=\frac{3k^{2}\left(\nu^{2}-1\right)}{4\tilde{\alpha}^{2}\nu^{2}}-\frac{L^{2}m^{2}}{\nu^{2}+3}.\end{split}

(87)

Following the procedure done in Section
4.3.1
, with the corresponding
\Delta
for the self-dual solution, we obtain the quasinormal modes.
The boundary conditions only permit one solution, the right ingoing mode:

\omega^{*}_{R}=-\frac{1}{4}i(\tilde{r}_{+}+\tilde{r}_{-})(\Delta+2j).

(88)

5
Mapping the Selberg zeroes to quasinormal modes

Now we take all the results from the previous sections and show that tuning the zeroes of the Lorentzian Selberg zeta function
s_{*}
to the conformal weights
\Delta
, we recover the condition that the corresponding quasinormal modes must be tuned to the thermal frequencies, in the spirit of
Keeler:2018lza
.

For simplicity, we only consider the ingoing (quasinormal) modes, and so we only need to consider the case with
n\geq 0
in the change of bases listed in (
15
). It should be noted that the change of basis (
15
) did not (to our knowledge) necessarily have to be the same in the warped case. Nevertheless, this change of basis turns out to be consistent in mapping the Selberg zeroes to the warped quasinormal modes. Since the basis change (
15
) is a result from scattering theory on the Euclidean BTZ background
perry2003selberg
, the fact that (
15
) still works in the warped case probably tells us that warping the fiber length does not significantly change the poles of the scattering matrix.

5.1
Warped AdS
3
black holes

The procedure outlined in
Keeler:2018lza
was to express
s_{*}-\Delta
in terms of the corresponding quasinormal mode and BTZ thermal frequencies.
Here we will go in the opposite direction and construct
s_{*}
from the quasinormal modes, the warped black hole’s thermal frequencies and the conformal weight. We will then solve for
a
and
b
used in the quotient, and show that they coincide with (
50
) in the warped black hole case and (
56
) in the self-dual case.
That is, we write

s_{*L}=\Lambda_{L}^{-1}(\omega^{*}_{L}-\omega_{n})+\Delta,\quad s_{*R}=\Lambda_{R}^{-1}(\omega^{*}_{R}-\omega_{n})+\Delta,

(89)

where
\Lambda_{L}
and
\Lambda_{R}
are proportionality constants, which can be determined by eliminating
\Delta
on the right hand side of the equations (since the quasinormal modes depend upon
\Delta
but the zeroes
s_{*}
should not explicitly depend upon
\Delta
).
Plugging everything that we calculated in previous sections into the right hand side of the equations, we get:

\frac{\omega^{*}_{L}-\omega_{n}}{\Lambda_{L}}+\Delta=-2j-\left(\frac{8iL\nu(r_{+}-r_{-})}{r_{+}(\nu^{2}+3)(2r_{+}\nu-\rm\sqrt{\nu^{2}+3})}\right)k-\left(\frac{2\nu(r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2})}{r_{+}(2\nu r_{+}-r_{-}\sqrt{\nu^{2}+3})}\right)n,

(90)

and

\frac{\omega^{*}_{R}-\omega_{n}}{\Lambda_{R}}+\Delta=-2j+\left(\frac{8iL\nu(r_{+}-r_{-})}{r_{+}(\nu^{2}+3)(2r_{+}\nu-\rm\sqrt{\nu^{2}+3})}\right)k-\left(\frac{2((r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2})\nu-r_{+}r_{-}\sqrt{\nu^{2}+3})}{r_{+}(2\nu r_{+}-r_{-}\sqrt{\nu^{2}+3})}\right)n,

(91)

where

\Lambda_{L}=\frac{i(r_{+}-r_{-})(\nu^{2}+3)}{4L^{2}\nu},\quad\Lambda_{R}=\frac{i(r_{+}-r_{-})^{2}(r_{+}+r_{-})(\nu^{2}+3)}{4l^{2}((r_{+}^{2}+r_{-}^{2})\nu-r_{+}r_{-}\sqrt{\nu^{2}+3})}.

(92)

Equating (
90
) and (
91
) to (
16
) (reproduced here for convenience)

s_{*L}=-2j-\left(1+\frac{b}{a}\right)n-\frac{i\pi k}{a},\quad s_{*R}=-2j-\left(1-\frac{b}{a}\right)n+\frac{i\pi k}{a},

(93)

and solving for
a
and
b
, we obtain

\begin{split}a&=\frac{\pi r_{+}(\nu^{2}+3)(2\nu r_{+}-r_{-}\sqrt{\nu^{2}+3})}{8L\nu(r_{+}-r_{-})},\\
b&=\frac{\pi r_{-}(\nu^{2}+3)(-2\nu r_{-}+r_{+}\sqrt{\nu^{2}+3})}{8L\nu(r_{+}-r_{-})},\end{split}

(94)

which reproduces the values obtained from the quotient (
50
).
This shows that the proposed Selberg zeta function zeroes for warped AdS
3
black holes are successfully mapped to the quasinormal modes.

5.2
Warped self-dual solutions

For the self-dual solutions the left quasinormal mode vanishes, and thus we only match one of the zeroes,
s_{*R}
, to the remaining quasinormal mode. Along the lines of (
89
), we write

s_{*R}=\Lambda_{R}^{-1}(\omega^{*}_{R}-\omega_{n})+\Delta,

(95)

and we find that the proportionality constant is

\Lambda_{R}=-\frac{i}{4}(\tilde{r}_{+}+\tilde{r}_{-}).

(96)

Plugging everything into the right hand side of (
95
) (using (
63
), (
66
), (
75
), (
88
) and (
96
)), we find

s_{*R}=-2(j+n)-\frac{2ik}{\tilde{\alpha}}.

(97)

Comparing (
97
) and (
93
) allows us to solve for
a
and
b
:

a=\frac{\pi\tilde{\alpha}}{2},\qquad b=-\frac{\pi\tilde{\alpha}}{2}.

(98)

This matches our previous result from the quotient (
56
). Thus, just as in the warped black hole case in the previous section, the zeroes of our proposed Selberg zeta function for the self-dual solutions are successfully mapped to the quasinormal modes.

6
Discussion

Using the quotient structure of warped AdS
3
black holes
Anninos:2008fx
, we have constructed a Selberg zeta function for this spacetime in the spirit of
perry2003selberg
, providing an example that extends the work of
perry2003selberg
beyond hyperbolic quotients. We have shown that the zeroes of this zeta function are mapped to the scalar quasinormal modes on the warped AdS
3
black hole background
Chen:2009hg
;
Ferreira:2013zta
, in exactly the same way as in the non-warped (BTZ) case
Keeler:2018lza
;
Aros:2009pg
. We repeat this analysis for the warped self-dual solutions reported in
Anninos:2008fx
;
Chen:2010qm
, which are obtained from different quotients than the spacelike warped AdS
3
black holes and are interesting in their own right.

Along the way, we develop a method of constructing a warped version of the AdS
3
Poincaré patch metric, using the symmetry structure of the Klein-Gordon equation and a familiar ansatz for conformal coordinates
Castro:2010fd
;
Perry:2020ndy
. To the best of our knowledge, our metric (
44
) does not appear in the literature.
This metric could be of interest in the context of the warped AdS/warped CFT correspondence
Hofman:2014loa
. The conformal boundary of the metric (
44
) seems to have the correct properties (such as a degenerate metric) required by the geometry on which a boundary warped CFT lives, which is not manifest in other coordinate systems.

Perhaps the most immediate and intriguing direction for future work is to connect the Selberg zeta function to 1-loop determinants on the warped AdS
3
black hole background, in a similar fashion as in
Keeler:2018lza
. With this connection in hand, it might be possible to study quantum corrections on the near-horizon extremal Kerr (NHEK) geometry (which is warped AdS
3
)
Guica:2008mu
, as well as in other contexts where warped AdS
3
appears (see the Introduction for more details).

Furthermore, it is extremely tantalyzing to explore whether this Selberg quotient formalism can be related to the recent and very interesting connection between black hole quasinormal modes and quantum Seiberg-Witten curves
Aminov:2020yma
;
Bianchi:2021mft
. Both this work and
Aminov:2020yma
fix a geometric lens on the problem of computing spectral data, and a connection between them seems both likely and fruitful to persue. Indeed, such a connection was already hinted at in
Tai:2010gd
.

Hyperbolic quotient spacetimes occur in several other interesting physical systems in which quantum corrections are of interest. One notable example is AdS wormholes (see for example
Krasnov:2000zq
). In fact, it was recently shown in
Chandra:2022fwi
that certain
k
-boundary wormholes were constructed by quotienting AdS by the discrete group
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
. Since the quotient group that we consider is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
, these wormholes provide a physically interesting and tractable arena to study more complicated quotienting groups. A second notable example in which hyperbolic quotient geometries arise is in the calculation of holographic entanglement Rényi entropies
Barrella:2013wja
. In that work, one difficulty was calculating the generators of the quotienting Schottky group
\Gamma
, and most of the time the authors relied on an expansion in small cross-ration. Perhaps now that we have more experience in calculating generators of quotient groups from the bulk perspective, this problem can be revisited with more success. The main challenge in that endeavor would be making a meaningful connection to the boundary CFT data.

There is strong evidence that the Selberg trace formula also has a different and interesting physical interpretation: it is likely the formal like between two well-known methods for computing functional determinants, namely the heat kernel method (see for example
giombi2008one
) and the quasinormal mode method
Denef:2009kn
. This can be seen from how the Selberg trace formula is generally derived from the Selberg zeta function. As explained in
perry2003selberg
, one considers two different representations of the Selberg zeta function: the Euler product (
11
) and the Hadamard product

Z_{\Gamma}(s)=e^{Q(s)}\prod_{s_{*}}(1-s/s_{*})e^{s/s_{*}+1/2\left(s/s_{*}\right)^{2}+1/3\left(s/s_{*}\right)^{3}},

(99)

where
Z_{\Gamma}
is meromorphic and
Q(s)
is an entire function. The Selberg trace formula is obtained by taking the logarithmic derivative of (
11
) and (
99
) and equating them.
To extend this to warped AdS
3
quotients, the non-trivial interplay between the asymptotics of
\Delta
,
\omega
and
k
may be important to interpret through the lens of
Denef:2009kn
, since this is used to determine the UV counterterms of the partition function, like for example in
Arnold:2016dbb
. We leave this interesting and potentially insightful direction for future work.

The hyperbolic 3-space can be written in terms of its embedding

ds^{2}=-dU^{2}+dV^{2}+dX^{2}+dY^{2}

(100)

with the constraint

-U^{2}+V^{2}+X^{2}+Y^{2}=-L^{2}.

(101)

x=\frac{Y}{U+X},\qquad y_{E}=\frac{V}{U+X},\qquad z=\frac{L}{U+X},

(102)

J_{AB}=X_{B}\partial_{A}-X_{A}\partial_{B}.

\begin{split}J_{01}=V\partial_{U}+U\partial_{V}&=-xy_{E}\partial_{x}+\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}-y_{E}^{2}+z^{2}+1)\partial_{y_{E}}-y_{E}z\partial_{z},\\

J_{03}=Y\partial_{V}-V\partial_{Y}&=-y_{E}\partial_{x}+x\partial_{y_{E}},\\
J_{12}=X\partial_{U}+U\partial_{X}&=-x\partial_{x}-y_{E}\partial_{y_{E}}-z\partial_{z},\\

J_{23}=Y\partial_{X}-X\partial_{Y}&=\frac{1}{2}(1-x^{2}+y_{E}^{2}+z^{2})\partial_{x}-xy_{E}\partial_{y_{E}}-xz\partial_{z}.\\
\end{split}

(104)

(105)

(107)

Appendix B
BTZ conformal coordinates

In this appendix we illustrate how our method for obtaining conformal coordinates works in the case of the BTZ black hole. We reproduce the Lorentzian version of (
4
), as expected.

The BTZ metric is given by

ds^{2}=-\frac{r^{2}-r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2}}{L^{2}}dt^{2}+\frac{L^{2}r^{2}}{(r^{2}-r_{+}^{2})(r^{2}-r_{-}^{2})}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\phi^{2}-2\frac{r_{+}r_{-}}{L}dtd\phi.

(108)

For the BTZ black hole, we find the Klein-Gordon operator is proportional to the quadratic Casimir as:

\mathcal{H}^{2}=\frac{L^{2}}{4}\nabla^{2}.

(109)

The Klein-Gordon equation with field ansatz (
38
) takes the form

\left(\partial_{x}\left(x^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\partial_{x}+\frac{P_{\text{BTZ}}}{4\left(x-\frac{1}{2}\right)}+\frac{Q_{\text{BTZ}}}{4\left(x+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\right)\Phi=0,

(110)

where
x=\frac{r^{2}-1/2(r_{+}^{2}+r_{-}^{2})}{r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2}}
and

P_{\text{BTZ}}=\frac{L^{2}(kr_{-}-\omega Lr_{+})}{(r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2})^{2}},\qquad Q_{\text{BTZ}}=-\frac{L^{2}(kr_{+}-\omega Lr_{-})}{(r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2})^{2}}.

(111)

When equating the coefficients
k
and
\omega
of (
39
) to those in (
111
), one has 4 branches to chose from.
The branch which yields the correct transformation to the Poincaré patch (
33
) is the following:

\begin{split}\frac{\alpha+\gamma}{\beta\gamma-\alpha\delta}&=\frac{L^{2}r_{+}}{r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2}},\qquad\frac{\beta+\delta}{\beta\gamma-\alpha\delta}=-\frac{Lr_{-}}{r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2}},\\
\frac{\alpha-\gamma}{\beta\gamma-\alpha\delta}&=-\frac{L^{2}r_{-}}{r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2}},\qquad\frac{\beta-\delta}{\beta\gamma-\alpha\delta}=\frac{Lr_{+}}{r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2}}.\end{split}

(112)

On solving these set of equations, we have

\alpha=L\beta=\frac{r_{+}-r_{-}}{L},\qquad\gamma=-L\delta=\frac{r_{+}+r_{-}}{L}.

(113)

Again, we see that these are related to the left and right temperatures
\alpha=2\pi T_{L}
and
\gamma=2\pi T_{R}
. Plugging these values into (
37
), one obtains the coordinate transformation from the Boyer-Lindquist coordinates of the BTZ black hole to the Poincaré patch

\begin{split}w^{+}&=\sqrt{\frac{r^{2}-r_{+}^{2}}{r^{2}-r_{-}^{2}}}e^{\frac{1}{L^{2}}(r_{+}-r_{-})(t+L\phi)},\\
w^{-}&=\sqrt{\frac{r^{2}-r_{+}^{2}}{r^{2}-r_{-}^{2}}}e^{\frac{1}{L^{2}}(r_{+}+r_{-})(L\phi-t)},\\
z&=\sqrt{\frac{r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2}}{r^{2}-r_{-}^{2}}}e^{\frac{1}{L^{2}}(Lr_{+}\phi-r_{-}t)}.\end{split}

(114)

These are nothing more than the Lorentzian version of (
4
).

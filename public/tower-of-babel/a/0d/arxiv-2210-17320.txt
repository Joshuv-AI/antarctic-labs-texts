The Kochawave curve, a variant of the Koch curve
Rémy Sigrist

Abstract
This paper presents the construction and some properties of the Kochawave curve, a simple and asymmetrical variant of the Koch curve.



1
Introduction

The Koch curve
[
9
]
is a well-known fractal described in 1904
by the Swedish mathematician Helge von Koch
[
2
]
.

Since then, many variants of the Koch curve have been described
in the literature, such as the Cesàro fractal
[
8
]

and the quadratic Koch curve
[
1
]
,
just to name two (see Li
[
3
]
, Rani
[
4
]
, Ventrella
[
6
]
and Wikipedia
[
9
]

for other variants).

We present here another variant of the Koch curve:
the Kochawave curve, mentioned and named by Wahl
[
7
]
.
The Kochawave curve has a simple and asymmetrical structure,
and as its name suggests, looks like a wave.

3
Properties

3.1
Length of the Kochawave curve

After
n
iterations of the construction described in section
2.1
,
the length
L_{n}
of the structure is given by:

L_{n}=\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^{n}

Note that
L_{0}=1
as we started with a unit segment.

As
\lim_{n\to\infty}L_{n}=\infty
, the Kochawave curve has infinite length.

3.2
Area of the three entangled Kochawave curves

After
n
iterations of the construction described in section
2.2
,
the area
T_{n}
of the structure is given by:

T_{n}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\cdot\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n}

Note that
T_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}
as we started with a unit sided equilateral triangle.

As
\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{n}=0
, the three entangled Kochawave curves have empty area.
By contrast, the Koch antisnowflake has positive area.

3.3
Area of the Kochawave curve

After
n
iterations of the construction described in section
2.1
,
the area
A_{n}
enclosed between the structure and the initial segment satisfies:

3A_{n}+T_{n}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}

Hence:

A_{n}=\frac{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}-T_{n}}{3}=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{3}}\cdot\left[1-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n}\right]

The area
A
of the Kochawave curve is given by:

A=lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n}=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{3}}

This is one third of the area of a unit sided equilateral triangle,
and indeed, we can divide an equilateral triangle
into three Kochawave curves (see figure
9
).

Figure 9:
An equilateral triangle divided into three Kochawave curves

3.4
Height of the Kochawave curve

After
n
iterations of the construction described in section
2.1
,
the height
H_{n}
of the structure is given by:

H_{n}=\max_{p\in K_{n}}Im(p)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\mathrm{if}\ n=0\\
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}&\mathrm{if}\ n=1\\
\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}&\mathrm{if}\ n>1\end{array}\right.

The height
H
of the Kochawave curve is given by:

H=lim_{n\to\infty}H_{n}=\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}

The leftmost point of height
H
has position
\frac{4+4\omega}{9}
.

The rightmost point of height
H
has position
\frac{5+4\omega}{9}
.

3.5
Centroid of the Kochawave curve

We can dissect a Kochawave curve, say of weight
1
, into five parts:

•

one copy of the curve of weight
\frac{1}{3}
,

•

three copies of the curve of weight
\frac{1}{9}
,

•

one triangle of weight
\frac{1}{3}
.

as depicted in figure
10

(this dissection derives directly from the construction described in section
2.1
).

This leads to the following linear equation for the centroid
m

of the Kochawave curve:

m=\frac{1}{9}\cdot\frac{m}{3}+\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1+(1+\omega)m}{3}+\frac{1}{9}\cdot\frac{2+\omega-\omega m}{3}+\frac{1}{9}\cdot\frac{2+m}{3}+\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{\frac{5}{3}+\frac{\omega}{3}}{3}

Hence the centroid
m
of the Kochawave curve is:

m=\frac{59+17\omega}{111}

3.6
Volume of solid of revolution

The volume
V
of the solid of revolution of the Kochawave curve
around its base unit segment satisfies:

V=2\pi\cdot Im(m)\cdot A=\frac{17}{444}\pi

3.7
Hausdorff dimension of the Kochawave curve

The Kochawave curve satisfies the open set condition;
its Hausdorff dimension
d
is the unique real solution of the following equation:

\frac{1}{3^{d-1}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}^{d}}=1

and has exact value:

d=2\cdot\frac{\log\frac{1+\sqrt{13}}{2}}{\log 3}

which is approximately
1.518
.

3.8
Symmetries

Contrary to the Koch curve, the Kochawave curve has no particular symmetry (see figure
4
).
As for the Koch antisnowflake, the three entangled Kochawave curves have 3-fold rotational symmetry (see figure
7
).

3.9
Cantor set underlying the Kochawave curve

When building the Kochawave curve starting from one unit segment
as described in section
2.1
,
the remainder of this initial segment forms a ternary Cantor set.
This property is shared with the Koch curve.

Remainder of the initial segment after one to four iterations (in blue)

3.10
Connectivity

The Kochawave curve and the three entangled Kochawave curves are simply connected.

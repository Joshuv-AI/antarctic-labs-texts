Quantum correlations on quantum spaces
Arkadiusz Bochniak, Paweł Kasprzak, Piotr M. Sołtan

Abstract
. For given quantum (non-commutative) spaces ℙ \mathbb{P} and 𝕆 \mathbb{O} we study the quantum space of maps 𝕄 ℙ , 𝕆 \mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}} from ℙ \mathbb{P} to 𝕆 \mathbb{O} . In case of finite quantum spaces these objects turn out to be behind a large class of maps which generalize the classical qc \mathrm{qc} -correlations known from quantum information theory to the setting of quantum input and output sets. We prove various operator algebraic properties of the C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebras C ⁡ ( 𝕄 ℙ , 𝕆 ) \C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}) such as the lifting property and residual finite dimensionality. Inside C ⁡ ( 𝕄 ℙ , 𝕆 ) \C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}) we construct a universal operator system 𝕊 ℙ , 𝕆 \mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}} related to ℙ \mathbb{P} and 𝕆 \mathbb{O} and show, among other things, that the embedding 𝕊 ℙ , 𝕆 ⊂ C ⁡ ( 𝕄 ℙ , 𝕆 ) \mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\subset\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}) is hyperrigid and has another interesting property which we call the strong extension property . Furthermore C ⁡ ( 𝕄 ℙ , 𝕆 ) \C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}) is the C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -envelope of 𝕊 ℙ , 𝕆 \mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}} and a large class of non-signalling correlations on the quantum sets ℙ \mathbb{P} and 𝕆 \mathbb{O} arise from states on C ⁡ ( 𝕄 ℙ , 𝕆 ) ⊗ max C ⁡ ( 𝕄 ℙ , 𝕆 ) \C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{max}}}}\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}) as well as states on the commuting tensor product 𝕊 ℙ , 𝕆 ⊗ c 𝕊 ℙ , 𝕆 \mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{c}}}}\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}} . Finally we introduce and study the notion of a synchronous correlation with quantum input and output sets, prove several characterizations of such correlations and their relation to traces on C ⁡ ( 𝕄 ℙ , 𝕆 ) \C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}) .

1.
Introduction

The main objects of this paper are quantum spaces of maps and quantum correlations related to them. In order to properly introduce the reader to the subject we begin with the precise definition of a quantum space as well as the associated notation.

Definition 1.1
.

A
quantum space
is an object
\mathbb{X}
of the category dual to the category of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.

The category of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras is the category whose objects are
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras and whose morphisms are “morphisms of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras” described in
[
34
, Section 1]
,
[
35
, Section 0]
(see also
[
20
, chapter 2]
), i.e. non-degenerate
*
-homomorphisms into multiplier algebras. Given any
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}
the symbol
\Mor(\mathsf{A},\mathsf{B})
will denote the set of all morphisms from
\mathsf{A}
to
\mathsf{B}
. Note that if
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}
are unital then a morphism from
\mathsf{A}
to
\mathsf{B}
is simply a unital
*
-homomorphism from
\mathsf{A}
to
\mathsf{B}
.

According to the conventions adapted e.g. in the theory of quantum groups (cf.
[
2
,
6
,
7
,
26
,
31
]
) the correspondence between quantum spaces and
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras is expressed by denoting
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras by
\C(\mathbb{X})
or
\C_{0}(\mathbb{X})
where
\mathbb{X}
is the corresponding quantum space. The distinction between
\C(\cdot)
and
\C_{0}(\cdot)
is based on whether the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra is unital (in the former case) or not (in the latter case). This notation emphasizes
\mathbb{X}
as the primary focus and this may become cumbersome. Thus in some instances this notation is dropped in favor of more traditional symbols such as
\mathsf{A}
or
\mathsf{B}
denoting
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.

The standard terminology related to quantum spaces includes the following:

•

a quantum space
\mathbb{X}
is called
compact
if the corresponding
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra is unital (and hence denoted by
\C(\mathbb{X})
),

•

a quantum space
\mathbb{X}
is called
finite
if
\C(\mathbb{X})
is finite-dimensional.

Our aim is to generalize and study the notion of a
quantum correlation
on finite sets (see below) to analogous notion for finite quantum spaces. First steps towards such a generalization have been made in e.g.
[
10
,
3
,
32
]
and we propose a more systematic, more general, and “coordinate free” approach thoroughly grounded in non-commutative topology and geometry. As a result we are not only able to reproduce some key results known for particular types of quantum spaces with seemingly simpler and more transparent proofs, but we can also describe and exploit the deep connection between quantum correlations and quantum spaces of maps discussed already in
[
31
]
. Our investigations lead us to study certain universal operator systems (whose particular examples were already explored in
[
3
]
and
[
32
]
) as well as to results about quantum spaces of maps whose generality goes far beyond the setting of finite quantum spaces. Our results seem to be related to forthcoming work of M. Brannan, S. Harris, I. Todorov and L. Turowska
[
4
]
.

Let us briefly discuss the notion of a
quantum correlation
which was the starting point of our work. First let
P
and
O
be finite sets. By a
quantum correlation
or a
quantum strategy
on
P
and
O
we mean a collection of non-negative numbers
\bigl\{p(a,b|x,y)\,\bigr|\bigl.\,a,b\in{O},\>x,y\in{P}\bigr\}
such that for each
(x,y)
the maps

a\longmapsto\sum\limits_{b}p(a,b|x,y)\quad\text{and}\quad b\longmapsto\sum\limits_{a}p(a,b|x,y)

(1.1)

are probability distributions on
P
. A very convenient interpretation of these objects is provided by the theory of
non-local games
where two players – Alice and Bob – are asked questions
x
and
y
from the set
P
and are supposed to provide answers
a
and
b
from the set
O
. The number
p(a,b|x,y)
is interpreted as the conditional probability of Alice answering
a
and Bob answering
b
given questions
x
and
y
.
1
1

1

We are in fact dealing with a simplified version of the theory in which the sets of possible questions and answers are the same for Alice and Bob. The additional generality of allowing different question and answers sets for the two players is not essential, especially since we are aiming to study
synchronous correlations
, cf. Section
6
.
There are various ways to obtain correlations
\bigl\{p(a,b|x,y)\,\bigr|\bigl.\,a,b\in{O},\>x,y\in{P}\bigr\}
and correlations arising from various constructions are classified into “local”, “quantum”, “quantum commuting” etc. One fundamental class of correlations is the so-called non-signalling ones. They are the ones for which the maps (
1.1
) are independent of
y
and
x
respectively (they are the so-called
marginals
of the correlation
\bigl\{p(a,b|x,y)\bigr\}
). For the details of the theory we refer the reader e.g. to
[
10
,
12
,
17
,
23
]
.

The class of correlations we will generalize to the situation when the sets
P
and
O
are replaced by finite quantum spaces
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{O}
is the
quantum commuting
(or simply
\mathrm{qc}
-correlations
), i.e. those of the form

p(a,b|x,y)={\bigl\langle{\xi}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,E_{x,a}F_{y,b}\xi\bigr\rangle},\qquad{x,y}\in{P},\>a,b\in{O}

where
\Psi
is a unit vector in a Hilbert space
\mathsf{H}
and

\bigl\{E_{x,a}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,x\in{P},\>a\in{O}\bigr\}\quad\text{and}\quad\bigl\{F_{y,b}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,y\in{P},\>b\in{O}\bigr\}

are families of projections in
\B(\mathsf{H})
such that

•

for all
(x,y,a,b)\in{P}\times{P}\times{O}\times{O}
we have
E_{x,a}F_{y,b}=F_{y,b}E_{x,a}
,

•

for all
x\in{P}
we have
\sum\limits_{a}E_{x,a}=\mathds{1}_{\mathsf{H}}
and for all
y\in{P}
we have
\sum\limits_{b}F_{y,b}=\mathds{1}_{\mathsf{H}}
.

Note that such correlations are automatically non-signalling. Our generalization of
qc
-correlations is explained in Section
5
where we use the terminology of
realizable correlations
in order to emphasize that such correlations can be
realized
in a certain well understood way.

In recent preprints
[
3
,
32
]
the task to generalize the notion of a quantum correlation to quantum sets of questions and answers has been carried out in certain special cases. Our work is very much inspired by these developments and we feel that our approach provides a more flexible framework for this theory to be developed further. Although many of our results have their origins in
[
3
,
32
]
, our treatment makes it possible to generalize many of them (sometimes quite substantially) and to differentiate between results and necessary techniques which are brought on by the particular examples of quantum spaces and general theorems and arguments independent of the particular quantum sets under investigation.

To conclude the introduction let us briefly summarize the structure and contents of the paper. We begin with the definition of the quantum space
\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
of all maps from a quantum space
\mathbb{P}
to a quantum space
\mathbb{O}
and a short discussion of conditions ensuring its existence. Then we study properties of
\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
as a function of
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{O}
, particularly the behavior under morphisms in either variable. We also prove that the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is residually finite-dimensional whenever
\mathbb{P}
is finite and
\mathbb{O}
is compact and such that
\C(\mathbb{O})
is residually finite dimensional. Next, in Section

related to quantum spaces
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{O}
which later plays an important role in the description of realizable correlations. Among other things we prove that
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-envelope of
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
and that the embedding
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\subset\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is hyperrigid. Moreover we show that any u.c.p. map from
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P}.\mathbb{O}}
to a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra

extends to a u.c.p. map
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\to\mathsf{B}
(a property we call the
strong extension property
, see Definition
3.5
(
2
)). Remarkably both hyperrigidity and the strong extension property provide a conceptual explanation of some other facts concerning
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\subset\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
and they were not emphasized in the case of
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{O}
classical and the case of
\mathbb{P}
matricial (
\C(\mathbb{P})=\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})
) and
\mathbb{O}
classical. Section
4
deals with the lifting properties of
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
and
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
which we show to hold whenever
\C(\mathbb{O})
has the lifting property and is separable (although
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is almost never nuclear). In the following section we specify both
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{O}
to be finite and define realizable correlations with quantum input set
\mathbb{P}
and output set
\mathbb{O}
. Moreover we show that such correlations arise from states on
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{max}}}}\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
as well as states on the commuting tensor product
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{c}}}}\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
. Finally in Section
6
we study the quantum analog of synchronicity of correlations on finite quantum spaces explaining incidentally that the language of finite quantum spaces proves very convenient and effective for the generalization of this concept. We introduce an algebraic definition of synchronicity along the lines of
[
3

and prove their characterization via traces on
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
as well as several other results analogous to those of
[

17

2.
Quantum spaces of maps

By definition mappings between quantum spaces are morphisms of the corresponding
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras understood as going in the opposite direction. Thus given quantum spaces
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
we identify the set
\Mor(\C_{0}(\mathbb{Y}),\C_{0}(\mathbb{X}))
with the set of maps from
\mathbb{X}
to
\mathbb{Y}
. However, the theory of quantum spaces allows “quantum sets” of maps which are more precisely defined as
quantum families of maps
(
[
28
,
34
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
,
\mathbb{O}
and
\mathbb{X}
be quantum spaces. A
quantum family of maps
from
\mathbb{P}
to
\mathbb{O}
indexed by
\mathbb{X}
is a morphism
\Phi\in\Mor(\C_{0}(\mathbb{O}),\C_{0}(\mathbb{P})\otimes\C_{0}(\mathbb{X}))
.
2
2

2

The tensor product
\C_{0}(\mathbb{X})\otimes\C_{0}(\mathbb{X})
is the minimal tensor product of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. We will use other tensor products in Sections
3
,
5
and
6
.

Quantum families of maps are very general objects and hence the interesting ones are those which possess additional properties. Throughout this paper the most important role will be played by the
quantum families of all maps
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{O}
be quantum spaces. We say that

\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\in\Mor\bigl(\C_{0}(\mathbb{O}),\C_{0}(\mathbb{P})\otimes\C_{0}(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\bigr)

is the
quantum family of all maps
from
\mathbb{P}
to
\mathbb{O}
if for any quantum space
\mathbb{X}
and any quantum family
\Psi\in\Mor(\C_{0}(\mathbb{O}),\C_{0}(\mathbb{P})\otimes\C_{0}(\mathbb{X}))
there exists a unique
\Lambda\in\Mor(\C_{0}(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}),\C_{0}(\mathbb{X}))
such that the diagram

\textstyle{\C_{0}(\mathbb{O})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}}
\textstyle{\C_{0}(\mathbb{P})\otimes\C_{0}(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{id}\otimes\Lambda}
\textstyle{\C_{0}(\mathbb{O})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Psi}
\textstyle{\C_{0}(\mathbb{P})\otimes\C_{0}(\mathbb{X})}

is commutative.
3
3

3

This is a diagram in the category of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, so the arrows represent mappings into the corresponding multiplier algebras and their compositions require certain maneuvers, see
[
34
, Section 1]
or
[
20
, Chapter 2]
.

It is easy to see that if
(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}},\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
exists for the given
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{O}
then it is unique up to a natural notion of isomorphism, i.e. if
(\mathbb{M},\boldsymbol{\Phi})
is another pair with the same universal property then there is an isomorphism
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\colon\C_{0}(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\to\C_{0}(\mathbb{M})
such that
\boldsymbol{\Phi}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\boldsymbol{\Lambda})\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
. The quantum space
\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
is called the
quantum space of all maps
from
\mathbb{P}
to
\mathbb{O}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a finite quantum space and
\mathbb{O}
a compact quantum space. Then the quantum space
\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
of all maps from
\mathbb{P}
to
\mathbb{O}
exists and is compact. Moreover the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is generated by the set

\bigl\{(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}(a)\,\bigr|\bigl.\,a\in\C(\mathbb{O}),\>\omega\in\C(\mathbb{P})^{*}\bigr\}.

Theorem
2.3
is proved in
[
26
,
28
]
in the case when
\C(\mathbb{O})
is unital and finitely generated (i.e. a quotient of the full group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra of a finitely generated free group). The latter assumption can be dropped and the proof from
[
26
, Appendix]
can be repeated almost verbatim. The only price one has to pay is to consider the full group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra of the free group with possibly a very large number of generators. An alternative way to prove the existence of
(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}},\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
in the general case is indicated in Remark
2.10
.

Let us note that, the universal map
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
is injective. Indeed, one can consider
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{O}
and

\Psi\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\ni{a}\longmapsto\mathds{1}\otimes{a}\in\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\C(\mathbb{O}).

Then there exists
\Lambda\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\to\C(\mathbb{O})
such that

\mathds{1}\otimes{a}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Lambda)\bigl(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}(a)\bigr),\qquad{a}\in\C(\mathbb{O})

which shows that
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}(a)\neq{0}
if
a\neq{0}
.

Let

2.1.
Disjoint sums of quantum spaces

Throughout the paper the symbol
\boldsymbol{*}
will denote the universal free product of unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras amalgamated over the units.

Consider two compact quantum spaces
\mathbb{P}_{1}
and
\mathbb{P}_{2}
. The quantum space
\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{2}
is defined by setting
\C(\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{2})=\C(\mathbb{P}_{1})\oplus\C(\mathbb{P}_{2})
with the inclusions of
\mathbb{P}_{1}
and
\mathbb{P}_{2}
into
\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{2}
described by the projections
\mathrm{p}_{i}\colon\C(\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{2})\to\C(\mathbb{P}_{i})
for
i=1,2
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{P}_{2}
be finite quantum spaces and
\mathbb{O}
be a compact quantum space. Then the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}})
is isomorphic to the universal free product
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}})\boldsymbol{*}\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}})
, and with this identification the universal quantum family of maps

\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}}\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\longrightarrow\C(\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{2})\otimes\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}})

is given by

\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}}(a)=(\jmath_{1}\otimes\imath_{1})\bigl(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}}(a)\bigr)+(\jmath_{2}\otimes\imath_{2})\bigl(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}}(a)\bigr),\qquad{a}\in\C(\mathbb{O}),

where for
i=1,2
the maps
\jmath_{i}\colon\C(\mathbb{P}_{i})\to\C(\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{2})
are the (non-unital) inclusions of direct summands and
\imath_{i}\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{i},\mathbb{O}})\to\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}})
are the inclusions onto the copies of
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{i},\mathbb{O}})
in the free product.

Proof.

The reasoning is similar to the proof of
[
30
, Theorem 2.1]
: it is enough to show that the pair
(\C(\mathbb{M}),\boldsymbol{\Phi})
with

\C(\mathbb{M})=\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}})\boldsymbol{*}\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}})

and
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
defined by

\boldsymbol{\Phi}(a)=(\jmath_{1}\otimes\imath_{1})\bigl(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}}(a)\bigr)+(\jmath_{2}\otimes\imath_{2})\bigl(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}}(a)\bigr),\qquad{a}\in\C(\mathbb{O})

(2.1)

has the universal property of
(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}},\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}})
. The details are left to the reader.

Remark 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{O},\mathbb{P}_{1}
and
\mathbb{P}_{2}
be as above and
\mathrm{p}_{1}\colon\C(\mathbb{P}_{1})\oplus\C(\mathbb{P}_{2})\to\C(\mathbb{P}_{1})
the canonical projection. By composing
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}}
with

we obtain a unital

for a unique
\Lambda\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}})\to\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}})
. In view of the above proposition (particularly the uniqueness of

.

Lemma 2.7

(

Let
\mathsf{C}

\mathsf{D}=\bigl\{c\in\mathsf{C}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,c\gamma(x)=\gamma(x)c\text{ for all }x\in\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})\bigr\}.

\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathsf{D}

Proof.

The subset
\mathsf{D}

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathsf{C}

x\otimes{d}\longmapsto\gamma(x)d,\qquad{x}\in\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\>d\in\mathsf{D}

i,j\in\{1,\dotsc,n\}
let
E_{i,j}=\gamma(e_{i,j})
, where
\{e_{i,j}\}
are the matrix units in
\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. If

\sum_{i,j}e_{i,j}\otimes{c_{i,j}}

(2.2)

belongs to the kernel of
\Gamma
then for any
k,l,r

0=\Gamma\Biggl((e_{k,l}\otimes\mathds{1})\biggl(\sum_{i,j}e_{i,j}\otimes{c_{i,j}}\biggr)(e_{k,r}\otimes\mathds{1})\Biggr)=E_{k,k}c_{l,r}.

Hence
0=\sum\limits_{k}E_{k,k}c_{l,r}=c_{l,r}
for all
r,l
, so that (
2.2
) is zero.

Surjectivity of
\Gamma
follows from the fact that if
c\in\mathsf{C}
then putting

c_{i,j}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}E_{k,i}cE_{j,k},\qquad{i,j}=1,\dotsc,n

we obtain
c_{i,j}\in\mathsf{D}
for all
i,j
and
\Gamma\biggl(\sum\limits_{i,j}e_{i,j}\otimes{c_{i,j}}\bigg)=\sum\limits_{i,j}E_{i,j}c_{i,j}=c
.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\C(\mathbb{P})=\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and let
\mathbb{O}
be a compact quantum space. Then
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is the relative commutant of
\C(\mathbb{P})
in
\C(\mathbb{P})\boldsymbol{*}\C(\mathbb{O})
and
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
is the composition of the inclusion
\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\boldsymbol{*}\C(\mathbb{O})
with the isomorphism
\C(\mathbb{P})\boldsymbol{*}\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
described in Lemma
2.7
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a quantum space and
\Psi\in\Mor(\C(\mathbb{O}),\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\C_{0}(\mathbb{X}))
a quantum family of maps
\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{O}
. Furthermore let
\mathsf{D}
denote the relative commutant of
\C(\mathbb{P})
in
\C(\mathbb{P})\boldsymbol{*}\C(\mathbb{O})
.

By the universal property of free products there exists a unital
*
-homomorphism
\Pi\colon\C(\mathbb{P})\boldsymbol{*}\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\M(\C_{0}(\mathbb{X}))
such that
\Pi(\imath_{1}(x))=x\otimes\mathds{1}
for all
x\in\C(\mathbb{P})
and
\Pi(\imath_{2}(y))=\Psi(y)
for all
y\in\C(\mathbb{O})
. Note that
\Pi\in\Mor(\C(\mathbb{P})\boldsymbol{*}\C(\mathbb{O}),\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\C_{0}(\mathbb{X}))
.

Now if
d
belongs to
\mathsf{D}
then
\Pi(d)(x\otimes\mathds{1})=(x\otimes\mathds{1})\Pi(d)
for all
d\in\C(\mathbb{P})
because

\Pi(d)(x\otimes\mathds{1})=\Pi(d)\Pi\bigl(\imath_{1}(x)\bigr)=\Pi\bigl(d\imath_{1}(x)\bigr)=\Pi\bigl(\imath_{1}(x)d\bigr)=(x\otimes\mathds{1})\Pi(d).

Therefore the element
\Pi(d)
belongs to the commutant of
\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathds{1}
in
\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\M(\C_{0}(\mathbb{X}))
, i.e. to
\mathds{1}\otimes\M(\C_{0}(\mathbb{X}))
. As a consequence we can define a unital
*
-homomorphism
\Lambda\colon\mathsf{D}\to\M(\C_{0}(\mathbb{X}))
by

\Pi(d)=\mathds{1}\otimes\Lambda(d),\qquad{d}\in\mathsf{D}

and defining
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
as the composition
\Gamma\circ\imath_{2}\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathsf{D}
we immediately get

(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Lambda)\bigl(\boldsymbol{\Phi}(y)\bigr)=\Pi\bigl(\imath_{2}(y)\bigr)=\Psi(y),\qquad{y}\in\C(\mathbb{O}).

(2.3)

Since
\mathsf{D}
is obviously generated by slices of the form
(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})\boldsymbol{\Phi}(y)
(
y\in\C(\mathbb{O})
,
\omega\in\C(\mathbb{P})^{*}
), we immediately see that
\Lambda
is uniquely determined by (
2.3
). It follows that
(\mathsf{D},\boldsymbol{\Phi})
has the universal property of
(\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}),\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
\mathbb{O}
be a compact quantum space and
\mathbb{P}
a finite quantum space with

\C(\mathbb{P})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\Mat_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C}).

Then the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is a free product
\mathsf{D}_{1}\boldsymbol{*}\dotsm\boldsymbol{*}\mathsf{D}_{m}
, where
\mathsf{D}_{i}
is the relative commutant of
\Mat_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C})
in
\Mat_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C})\boldsymbol{*}\C(\mathbb{O})
.

Remark 2.10
.

The explicit description of
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
and
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
in the case
\C(\mathbb{P})=\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})
given in Proposition
2.8
together with Proposition
2.5
provides an alternative way to prove existence of
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
for arbitrary compact
\mathbb{O}
and finite
\mathbb{P}
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\mathbb{O}
be a compact quantum space such that
\C(\mathbb{O})
is residually finite dimensional and let
\mathbb{P}
be a finite quantum space. Then
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is residually finite dimensional.

Proof.

By Corollary
2.9
the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is a free product of algebras which are subalgebras of free products of the form
\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})\boldsymbol{*}\C(\mathbb{O})
. Since residual finite dimensionality passes to free products (
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
) and to subalgebras,
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is residually finite dimensional.
∎

Corollary 2.12
.

For any finite quantum spaces
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{O}
the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
possesses a faithful trace.

Let us note that existence of traces on
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is important in the study of synchronous correlations (see Section
6
).

2.2.
Functorial properties

In this section we will study the properties of the assignment

(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O})\longmapsto\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}},

(2.4)

where
\mathbb{P}
is a finite quantum space and
\mathbb{O}
is a compact quantum space. For brevity let us denote by
\mathfrak{QS}_{\text{\tiny{\rm{fin}}}}
and
\mathfrak{QS}_{\text{\tiny{\rm{cpt}}}}
the full subcategories of the category of quantum spaces consisting of the finite and compact quantum spaces respectively.

The proof the next proposition easily follows from the universal property of
\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
.

Proposition 2.13
.

The mapping (
2.4
) is a bi-functor
\mathfrak{QS}_{\text{\tiny{\rm{fin}}}}\times\mathfrak{QS}_{\text{\tiny{\rm{cpt}}}}\to\mathfrak{QS}_{\text{\tiny{\rm{cpt}}}}
. Given
\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{P}_{2}\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathfrak{QS}_{\text{\tiny{\rm{fin}}}})
,
\mathbb{O}_{1},\mathbb{O}_{2}\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathfrak{QS}_{\text{\tiny{\rm{cpt}}}})
and

\rho\colon\C(\mathbb{P}_{2})\longrightarrow\C(\mathbb{P}_{1}),\quad\pi\colon\C(\mathbb{O}_{1})\longrightarrow\C(\mathbb{O}_{2}),

the associated map
\mathbb{M}_{\rho,\pi}\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}_{1}})\to\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}_{2}})
is the unique
\Lambda
making the diagram

\textstyle{\C(\mathbb{O}_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}_{1}}}
\textstyle{\C(\mathbb{P}_{1})\otimes\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}_{1}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{id}\otimes\Lambda}
\textstyle{\C(\mathbb{O}_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(\rho\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}_{2}}\circ\pi}
\textstyle{\C(\mathbb{P}_{1})\otimes\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}_{2}})}

commutative. In particular the functor
\mathbb{M}_{\text{\tiny$\bullet$},\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}
is contravariant with respect to the first variable and covariant with respect to the second one.

Remark 2.14
.

It is an obvious consequence of the functoriality of
\mathbb{M}_{\text{\tiny$\bullet$},\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}
that if
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
are finite quantum spaces such that
\C(\mathbb{P})\cong\C(\mathbb{P}^{\prime})
then for any compact quantum space
\mathbb{O}
we have
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\cong\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime},\mathbb{O}})
. Similarly if
\C(\mathbb{O})\cong\C(\mathbb{O}^{\prime})
for some compact quantum spaces
\mathbb{O}
and
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}
then
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\cong\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}^{\prime}})
for any finite quantum space
\mathbb{P}
.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
\mathbb{P},\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{P}_{2}\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathfrak{QS}_{\text{\tiny{\rm{fin}}}})
and
\mathbb{O},\mathbb{O}_{1},\mathbb{O}_{2}\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathfrak{QS}_{\text{\tiny{\rm{cpt}}}})
and

\rho\colon\C(\mathbb{P}_{2})\longrightarrow\C(\mathbb{P}_{1}),\quad\pi\colon\C(\mathbb{O}_{1})\longrightarrow\C(\mathbb{O}_{2}).

Then

(1)

if
\pi
is surjective then so is
\mathbb{M}_{\mathrm{id},\pi}\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}_{1}})\to\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}_{2}})
,

(2)

if
\rho
is injective then
\mathbb{M}_{\rho,\mathrm{id}}\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}})\to\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}})
is surjective.

Moreover,

(3)

if
\pi
is injective then so is
\mathbb{M}_{\mathrm{id},\pi}\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}_{1}})\to\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}_{2}})
,

(4)

if
\rho
is surjective then
\mathbb{M}_{\rho,\mathrm{id}}\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}})\to\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}})
is injective.

Proof.

Ad (
1
). Since
\pi
is surjective, we have

\displaystyle\bigl\{(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}_{2}}(y)\,\bigr|\bigl.\,y\in\C(\mathbb{O}_{2}),

\displaystyle\>\omega\in\C(\mathbb{P})^{*}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}_{2}}\bigl(\pi(x)\bigr)\,\bigr|\bigl.\,x\in\C(\mathbb{O}_{1}),\>\omega\in\C(\mathbb{P})^{*}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{M}_{\mathrm{id},\pi}\Bigl(\bigl\{(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}_{1}}(x)\,\bigr|\bigl.\,x\in\C(\mathbb{O}_{1}),\>\omega\in\C(\mathbb{P})^{*}\bigr\}\Bigr).

Furthermore, since
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}_{2}})
is generated by the set on the left-hand side, we get that
\mathbb{M}_{\mathrm{id},\pi}
is surjective.

Ad (
2
). We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{\rho,\mathrm{id}}\Bigl(\bigl\{(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}}(x)\,\bigr|\bigl.\,x\in\C(\mathbb{O}),

\displaystyle\>\omega\in\C(\mathbb{P}_{1})^{*}\bigr\}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\bigl((\omega\circ\rho)\otimes\mathrm{id}\bigr)\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}}(x)\,\bigr|\bigl.\,x\in\C(\mathbb{O}),\>\omega\in\C(\mathbb{P}_{1})^{*}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{(\widetilde{\omega}\otimes\mathrm{id})\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}}(x)\,\bigr|\bigl.\,x\in\C(\mathbb{O}),\>\widetilde{\omega}\in\C(\mathbb{P}_{2})^{*}\bigr\}

due to the injectivity of
\rho
. As the last set generates
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}})
we see that
\mathbb{M}_{\rho,\mathrm{id}}
is a surjection.

Ad (
3
)
Let us first prove the statement (
3
) in the case
\C(\mathbb{P})=\Mat_{k}(\mathbb{C})
. Let us denote
\mathsf{C}_{1}=\Mat_{k}(\mathbb{C})\boldsymbol{*}\C(\mathbb{O}_{1})
and
\mathsf{C}_{2}=\Mat_{k}(\mathbb{C})\boldsymbol{*}\C(\mathbb{O}_{2})
. By
[
24
, Theorem 4.2]
the injective
*
-homomorphism
\pi
defined an injective
*
-homomorphism
\mathrm{id}\boldsymbol{*}\pi\colon\mathsf{C}_{1}\to\mathsf{C}_{2}
. Using the identifications

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}_{1}

\displaystyle=\Mat_{k}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}_{1}})

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}_{2}

\displaystyle=\Mat_{k}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}_{2}})

(cf. Proposition
2.8
) we easily get
(\mathrm{id}\boldsymbol{*}\pi)(x)=\mathbb{M}_{\mathrm{id},\pi}(x)
for all
x\in\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}_{1}})
, and conclude the injectivity of
\mathbb{M}_{\mathrm{id},\pi}
.

In the general case

\C(\mathbb{P})=\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{m}\Mat_{k_{i}}(\mathbb{C})

(i.e.
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{m}
with
\C(\mathbb{P}_{i})=\Mat_{k_{i}}(\mathbb{C})
) and the proof is completed by using Proposition
2.5
and observing that

\mathbb{M}_{\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}\sqcup\dotsm\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{m}},\pi}=\mathbb{M}_{\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}},\pi}\boldsymbol{*}\dotsm\boldsymbol{*}\mathbb{M}_{\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{P}_{m}},\pi}
. Since
\mathbb{M}_{\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{P}_{i}},\pi}
are injective for
i=1,\ldots,m
, the
*
-homomorphism
\M_{\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}},\pi}\boldsymbol{*}\dotsm\boldsymbol{*}\M_{\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{P}_{m}},\pi}
is injective (c.f.
[
24
, Theorem 4.2]
).

Ad (
4
). Since the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
\C(\mathbb{P}_{1})
and
\C(\mathbb{P}_{2})
are finite-dimensional, the fact that
\C(\mathbb{P}_{2})
surjects onto
\C(\mathbb{P}_{1})
means that
\mathbb{P}_{2}=\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{1}
for some finite quantum space
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
. More precisely, there exists an isomorphism
\sigma\colon\C(\mathbb{P}^{\prime})\oplus\C(\mathbb{P}_{1})\to\C(\mathbb{P}_{2})
such that
\rho\circ\sigma\colon\C(\mathbb{P}^{\prime})\oplus\C(\mathbb{P}_{1})\to\C(\mathbb{P}_{1})
is the projection onto
\C(\mathbb{P}_{1})
. In view of Remark
2.6

\bigl((\rho\circ\sigma)\otimes\mathrm{id}\bigr)\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\imath_{1})\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}},

where
\imath_{1}
is the inclusion of
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}})
into
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}})=\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime},\mathbb{O}})\boldsymbol{*}\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}})
. Now the mapping
\mathbb{M}_{\sigma,\mathrm{id}}\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}})\to\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}})
is an isomorphism satisfying

(\sigma\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{\sigma,\mathrm{id}})\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}}.

It follows that

(\rho\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{2},\mathbb{O}}=\bigl((\rho\circ\sigma)\otimes(\mathbb{M}_{\sigma,\mathrm{id}})^{-1}\bigr)\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}}=\Bigl(\mathrm{id}\otimes\bigl((\mathbb{M}_{\sigma,\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{O}}})^{-1}\circ\imath_{1}\bigr)\Bigr)\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}_{1},\mathbb{O}}

and consequently
\mathbb{M}_{\rho,\mathrm{id}}=(\mathbb{M}_{\sigma,\mathrm{id}})^{-1}\circ\imath_{1}
is injective.
∎

2.3.
The opposite algebra

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a quantum space. We will write
\mathbb{X}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
for the quantum space corresponding to the opposite
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra (cf.
[
8
, Section 1.3.3]
) of
\C_{0}(\mathbb{X})
. Thus if, for example,
\mathbb{X}
is compact then by definition
\C(\mathbb{X}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}})=\C(\mathbb{X})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a finite quantum space and
\mathbb{O}
a compact quantum space. Then the pair
(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}},\mathbb{O}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}},\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}},\mathbb{O}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}})
is naturally isomorphic to
({\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}},\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
, where the map of vector spaces
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is regarded as a
*
-homomorphism
\C(\mathbb{O})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}\to\C(\mathbb{P})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}\otimes\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
.

Proof.

It is enough to show that the pair
({\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}},\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
with
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
understood as a map
\C(\mathbb{O})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}\to\C(\mathbb{P})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}\otimes\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
has the universal property of
(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}},\mathbb{O}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}},\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}},\mathbb{O}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}})
. Thus let
\mathbb{X}
be a quantum space and let
\Psi\in\Mor(\C(\mathbb{O})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}},\C(\mathbb{P})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}\otimes\C_{0}(\mathbb{X}))
. Let

\kappa_{\C(\mathbb{O})}\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\longrightarrow\C(\mathbb{O})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}},\quad\kappa_{\C(\mathbb{P})}\colon\C(\mathbb{P})\longrightarrow\C(\mathbb{P})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}},\quad\kappa_{\C_{0}(\mathbb{X})}\colon\C_{0}(\mathbb{X})\longrightarrow\C_{0}(\mathbb{X})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}

and

\kappa_{\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})}\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\longrightarrow\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}

be the identity maps of vector spaces considered as anti-isomorphisms between the respective
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. Then

({\kappa_{\C(\mathbb{P})}}^{-1}\otimes\kappa_{\C_{0}(\mathbb{X})})\circ\Psi\circ\kappa_{\C(\mathbb{O})}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Lambda)\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}

for a unique
\Lambda\in\Mor(\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}),\C_{0}(\mathbb{X})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}})
. In other words

\Psi=\bigl(\mathrm{id}\otimes({\kappa_{\C_{0}(\mathbb{X})}}^{-1}\circ\Lambda\circ{\kappa_{\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})}}^{-1})\bigr)\circ\bigl((\kappa_{\C(\mathbb{P})}\otimes\kappa_{\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})})\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\circ{\kappa_{\C(\mathbb{O})}}^{-1}\bigr).

Note that
(\kappa_{\C(\mathbb{P})}\otimes\kappa_{\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})})\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\circ{\kappa_{\C(\mathbb{O})}}^{-1}
is precisely the map
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
regarded as a
*
-homomorphism
\C(\mathbb{O})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}\to\C(\mathbb{P})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}\otimes\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
. It follows that
\Psi
can be expressed as

\Psi=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Lambda^{\prime})\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}

(with the last map properly understood) for a unique
\Lambda^{\prime}\in\Mor(\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}),\C_{0}(\mathbb{X}))
.
∎

Note that for any finite quantum space
\mathbb{P}
the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\C(\mathbb{P})
is isomorphic to
\C(\mathbb{P})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
, so by Remark
2.14

\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\cong\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}},\mathbb{O}}).

(2.5)

Corollary 2.17
.

Let
\mathbb{O}
be a compact quantum space such that
\C(\mathbb{O})
is isomorphic to
\C(\mathbb{O})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
. Then for any finite quantum space
\mathbb{P}
the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is isomorphic to
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
.

Proof.

By assumption and Remark
2.14
we have
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\cong\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}})
, so in view of (
2.5
) and Proposition
2.16
we get
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\cong\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}},\mathbb{O}^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}})\cong\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
.
∎

Note that if
\mathbb{O}
is a finite quantum space then
\C(\mathbb{O})
is isomorphic to
\C(\mathbb{O})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
, so
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is isomorphic to
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
for all finite quantum spaces
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{O}
. In particular Corollary
2.17
is a generalization of
[
3
, Lemma 1.16]
.

3.
The universal operator system

In this and following sections (in particular in the proof of Theorem
4.1
) we will use the notion of a (
right/left
)
multiplicative domain
of a completely positive map. Our terminology will be the same as in
[
22
, Chapter 3]
.

Also we will freely use the fact that a u.c.p. map
\psi\colon\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{B}
between unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras defines a non-degenerate completely positive map
\mathrm{id}\otimes\psi\colon\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{A}\to\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{B}
, where
\mathcal{K}
denotes the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra of compact operators on
\ell_{2}
. This map, in turn, extends uniquely by strict continuity to a u.c.p. map between the multiplier algebras:
\M(\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{A})\to\M(\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{B})
. Additionally if, for any two
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
\mathsf{C}
and
\mathsf{D}
and any
\omega\in\mathsf{C}^{*}
the slice map
\omega\otimes\mathrm{id}
extends uniquely to a mapping
\M(\mathsf{C}\otimes\mathsf{D})\to\M(\mathsf{D})
. All this is contained in
[
20
, Chapters 5 and 8]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}
be unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. If
\varphi\colon\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{B}
is a unital completely positive map and
a\in\M(\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{A})
belongs to the multiplicative domain of
\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi\colon\M(\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{A})\to\M(\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{B})
then for any
\omega\in\mathcal{K}^{*}
the element
(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(a)
belongs to the multiplicative domain of
\varphi
.

Proof.

Any
\omega\in\mathcal{K}^{*}
is of the form
\omega=\operatorname{Tr}(\cdot\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\cdot)
for some trace-class operator
\rho
. Since any such
\omega
can be approximated in the norm of
\mathcal{K}^{*}
by functionals of the same form with
\rho
finite-dimensional, and the multiplicative domain of a u.c.p. map is closed (
[
22
, Exercise
4.2(\text{iii})
]
), it is enough to prove that
(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(a)
is in the multiplicative domain of
\varphi
for such
\omega
.

Let
r
and
l
be the left and right support of
\rho
, i.e.
l
is the projection onto the range of
\rho
(assumed to be finite-dimensional) and
r
is the projection onto the range of
\rho^{*}
(which is also finite-dimensional). Then for any
y\in\mathcal{K}
we have

\omega(yl)=\operatorname{Tr}(yl\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}(y\rho)=\omega(y)\quad\text{and}\quad\omega(ry)=\operatorname{Tr}(ry\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}(y\rho)

Since
a
is in the multiplicative domain of
\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi
, for any
z\in\mathsf{A}
we have

(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi)\bigl(a(l\otimes{z})\bigr)=\bigl((\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi)(a)\bigr)\bigl((\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi)(l\otimes{z})\bigr)=\bigl((\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi)(a)\bigr)\bigl(l\otimes\varphi(z)\bigr).

Applying
(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})
to both sides we obtain

\varphi\Bigl(\bigl((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(a)\bigr)z\Bigr)=\varphi\bigl((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(a)\bigr)\varphi(z)

Similarly

(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi)\bigl((r\otimes{z})a\bigr)=\bigl((\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi)(r\otimes{z})\bigr)\bigl((\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi)(a)\bigr)=\bigl(r\otimes\varphi(z)\bigr)\bigl((\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi)(a)\bigr).

and application of
(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})
to both sides yields

\varphi\Bigl(z\bigl((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(a)\bigr)\Bigr)=\varphi(z)\varphi\bigl((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(a)\bigr).

Since this is true for all
z
, the element
(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(a)
belongs to the multiplicative domain of
\varphi
.
∎

From this section onward we will make frequent use of Kasparov’s dilation theorem (
[
18
, Theorem 9]
, cf. also
[
20
, Theorem 6.5]
). One of its direct consequences is the following lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a finite quantum space and
\mathbb{O}
a compact quantum space such that
\C(\mathbb{O})
is separable. Let
\mathsf{B}
be a separable unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
\psi\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathsf{B}
be a u.c.p. map. Then there exists
\Psi\in\Mor(\C(\mathbb{O}),\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{B})
such that

\psi(x)=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\omega_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\bigl(\Psi(x)\bigr),\qquad{x}\in\C(\mathbb{O}),

where
\omega_{1,1}
is the functional
{a}\mapsto{\left\langle{e_{1}}\,\vline\,ae_{1}\right\rangle}
on
\mathcal{K}
with
e_{1}
denoting the first vector of the standard basis of
\ell_{2}
.

In what follows, given
\Psi\in\Mor(\C(\mathbb{O}),\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{B})
we will denote mappings of the form

x\longmapsto(\mathrm{id}\otimes\omega_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\bigl(\Psi(x)\bigr),\qquad{x}\in\C(\mathbb{O})

described in Lemma
3.2
by
\Psi_{1,1}
.

Consider the universal quantum family of maps
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\colon\mathbb{C}(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
and let
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
be the closed linear span of the slices of
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
i.e.

\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\bigl\{(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}(x)\,\bigr|\bigl.\,x\in\C(\mathbb{O}),\>\omega\in\C(\mathbb{P})^{*}\bigr\}.

(3.1)

Then
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
is an operator system. Moreover
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
comes equipped with a map

\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\longrightarrow\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}

(3.2)

defined as
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
viewed as a map
\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
. This map is clearly u.c.p. In the next theorem we shall prove that
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
has an interesting universal property.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a finite quantum space and
\mathbb{O}
a compact quantum space such that
\C(\mathbb{O})
is separable. Let
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\subset\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
be the operator system equipped with the u.c.p. map
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
described by (
3.1
) and (
3.2
). For any operator system
\mathsf{S}
and any u.c.p. map
\psi\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathsf{S}
there exists a unique u.c.p. map
\lambda\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\to\mathsf{S}
such that the diagram

\textstyle{\C(\mathbb{O})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}}
\textstyle{\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{id}\otimes\lambda}
\textstyle{\C(\mathbb{O})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathsf{S}}

commutes.

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{B}
be the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra generated by the set

\bigl\{(\phi\otimes\mathrm{id})\bigl(\psi(x)\bigr)\,\bigr|\bigl.\,x\in\C(\mathbb{O}),\>\phi\in\C(\mathbb{P})^{*}\bigr\}.

Then
\mathsf{B}
is separable unital and
\psi
is a u.c.p. map
\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathsf{B}
. By Lemma
3.2

\psi=\Psi_{1,1}
for some
\Psi\in\Mor(\C(\mathbb{O}),\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{B})
.
The universal property of
(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}},\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
provides
\Lambda\in\Mor(\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}),\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{B})
such that
\Psi=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Lambda)\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
. Let
\widetilde{\lambda}=(\omega_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Lambda
. Then
\widetilde{\lambda}
is a u.c.p. map
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\to\mathsf{B}
and defining
\lambda=\bigl.\widetilde{\lambda}\bigr|_{\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}}
we obtain

\psi=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\lambda)\circ\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}.

(3.3)

Slicing both sides of this equation with
\omega\in\C(\mathbb{P})^{*}
we obtain
\lambda\bigl((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}(x)\bigr)=(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})\bigl(\psi(x)\bigr)
for all
x\in\C(\mathbb{O})
. This means, first of all, that the range of
\lambda
is contained in
\mathsf{S}
and secondly, that formula (
3.3
) determines the values of
\lambda
on elements of the form
(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}(x)
. Since such elements span
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
, this shows that
\lambda
is unique.
∎

\mathsf{S}
is an operator system in a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathsf{A}
the symbol
\mathrm{C}^{*}\langle\mathsf{S}\rangle
will denote the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathsf{A}
generated by
\mathsf{S}

-algebra (or

-envelope) of

Remark 3.4
.

Let
\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O},\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
and
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
be as above. Then by construction we have

\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\iota)\circ\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}},

(3.4)

where
\iota

\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\hookrightarrow\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{A}
be an operator system. We say that

(1)

the embedding
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{A}
is
hyperrigid
(cf.
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
) if given a Hilbert space
\mathsf{H}
, a
*
-homomorphism
\pi\colon\mathsf{A}\to\B(\mathsf{H})
and a u.c.p. map
\eta\colon\mathsf{A}\to\B(\mathsf{H})
satisfying
\bigl.\pi\bigr|_{\mathsf{S}}=\bigl.\eta\bigr|_{\mathsf{S}}
we have
\pi=\eta
,

(2)

the embedding
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{A}
has a
strong extension property
if for every
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathsf{B}
and a u.c.p. map
\psi\colon\mathsf{S}\to\mathsf{B}
there exists a u.c.p. extension
\eta\colon\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{B}
of
\psi
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{A}
be hyperrigid and
\mathrm{C}^{*}\langle\mathsf{S}\rangle=\mathsf{A}
. Then
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\text{\tiny{\rm{env}}}}(\mathsf{S})=\mathsf{A}
.

Proof.

The universal property of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\text{\tiny{\rm{env}}}}(\mathsf{S})
(see
[
14
, Corollary 4.2]
) provides a surjective
*
-homomorphism
\rho\colon\mathsf{A}\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\text{\tiny{\rm{env}}}}(\mathsf{S})
such that
\bigl.\rho\bigr|_{\mathsf{S}}
is the identity on
\mathsf{S}
. We will prove that
\rho
is injective. Indeed, embed
\mathsf{A}
into
\B(\mathsf{H})
and denote the embedding by
\iota\colon\mathsf{A}\to\B(\mathsf{H})
. Let
\eta\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathsf{S})\to\B(\mathsf{H})
be the u.c.p. extension of the embedding
\bigl.\iota\bigr|_{\mathsf{S}}\colon\mathsf{S}\to\B(\mathsf{H})
. Then
\eta\circ\rho\colon\mathsf{A}\to\B(\mathsf{H})
is a u.c.p. map such that
\bigl.\eta\circ\rho\bigr|_{\mathsf{S}}=\bigl.\iota\bigr|_{\mathsf{S}}
. By hyperrigidity of
\mathsf{S}
in
\mathsf{A}
we get
\eta\circ\rho=\iota
and hence
\rho
is injective.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Theorem
3.6
remains true even without the assumption that
\mathrm{C}^{*}\langle\mathsf{S}\rangle=\mathsf{A}
: in
[
16
, Theorem 3.10]
it was proven that if
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{A}
is hyperrgid then
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\text{\tiny{\rm{env}}}}(\mathsf{S})=\mathrm{C}^{*}\langle\mathsf{S}\rangle
. This can be deduced from Dritschel-McCullough theory of boundary representations (
[
9
]
). Nevertheless we presented a direct proof of Theorem
3.6
under the assumptions we are interested in.

The hyperrigidity of
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\subset\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
follows from the following more general fact.

Theorem 3.8
.

Suppose that
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}
are unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras,
\mathsf{C}
is a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
\Phi\in\Mor(\mathsf{A},\mathsf{C}\otimes\mathsf{B})
. Define the operator system
\mathsf{S}\subset\mathsf{B}

\mathsf{S}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\bigl\{(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})\bigl(\Phi(x)\bigr)\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\omega\in\mathsf{C}^{*},\>x\in\mathsf{A}\bigr\}.

If
\mathrm{C}^{*}\langle\mathsf{S}\rangle=\mathsf{B}
then
\mathsf{S}
is hyperrigid in
\mathsf{B}
and
\mathsf{B}=\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\text{\tiny{\rm{env}}}}(\mathsf{S})
. In particular for any finite quantum space
\mathbb{P}
and any compact quantum space
\mathbb{O}
the embedding
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\subset\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is hyperrigid and
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\text{\tiny{\rm{env}}}}(\mathsf{S})=\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
.

Proof.

Denoting by
\mathcal{U}(\mathsf{A})
the set of unitary elements in
\mathsf{A}
we easily observe that

\mathsf{S}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\bigl\{(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})\bigl(\Phi(x)\bigr)\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\omega\in\mathsf{C}^{*},\>x\in\mathcal{U}(\mathsf{A})\bigr\}.

Let
\eta\colon\mathsf{B}\to\B(\mathsf{H})
be a u.c.p. map, and
\pi\colon\mathsf{B}\to\B(\mathsf{H})
be a
*
-homomorphism satisfying

\bigl.\eta\bigr|_{\mathsf{S}}=\bigl.\pi\bigr|_{\mathsf{S}}.

(3.5)

In order to prove that
\eta=\pi
it is enough to prove that
\mathsf{S}
is in the multiplicative domain of
\eta
.

Let
\mathsf{L}
be a Hilbert space such that
\mathsf{C}\subset\B(\mathsf{L})
and let us consider the u.c.p. map
(\mathrm{id}\otimes\eta)\colon\M(\mathcal{K}(\mathsf{L})\otimes\mathsf{B})\to\M(\mathcal{K}(\mathsf{L})\otimes\mathcal{K}(\mathsf{H}))
. Equation (
3.5
) is equivalent to

(\mathrm{id}\otimes\eta)\circ\Phi=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\pi)\circ\Phi.

Furthermore, if
u\in\mathcal{U}(\mathsf{A})
then

\displaystyle\Bigl((\mathrm{id}\otimes\eta)\bigl(\Phi(u)\bigr)\Bigr)^{*}\Bigl((\mathrm{id}\otimes\eta)\bigl(\Phi(u)\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\pi)\bigl(\Phi(u^{*})\bigr)(\mathrm{id}\otimes\pi)\bigl(\Phi(u)\bigr)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\pi)\bigl(\Phi(u^{*}u)\bigr)=\mathds{1}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\eta)\bigl(\Phi(\mathds{1})\bigr)=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\eta)\bigl(\Phi(u^{*}u)\bigr)

and similarly

\Bigl((\mathrm{id}\otimes\eta)\bigl(\Phi(u)\bigr)\Bigr)\Bigl((\mathrm{id}\otimes\eta)\bigl(\Phi(u)\bigr)\Bigr)^{*}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\eta)\bigl(\Phi(uu^{*})\bigr).

This shows that
\Phi(u)
is in the multiplicative domain of
(\mathrm{id}\otimes\eta)
. By Lemma
3.1
we conclude that
\mathsf{S}
is in the multiplicative domain of
\eta
which concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a finite quantum space and let
\mathbb{O}
be a compact quantum space such that
\C(\mathbb{O})
is separable. The embedding
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\subset\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
has the strong extension property.

Proof.

Let
\psi\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\to\mathsf{A}
be a u.c.p. map. Applying Lemma
3.2
to the composition

(\mathrm{id}\otimes\psi)\circ\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\longrightarrow\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathsf{A}

we find a
*
-homomorphism
\Psi\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\M(\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{A})
such that
\Psi_{1,1}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\psi)\circ\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
. By the universal property of
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
there exists a
*
-homomorphism
\Lambda\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\to\M(\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{A})
such that

\Psi=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Lambda)\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}.

The proof is completed by noting that
(\omega_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Psi
is a u.c.p. map with range in
\mathsf{A}
which extends
\psi
.
∎

that the

functorial

\mathsf{S}

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a finite quantum space,
\mathbb{O}
a compact quantum space such that
\C(\mathbb{O})
is separable and let
\mathsf{S}
be an arbitrary operator system. Then the canonical map
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{c}}}}\mathsf{S}\to\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{max}}}}\mathsf{S}
is a complete order embedding.

Proof.

We employ a modification of the techniques used in the proofs of
[
15
, Proposition 4.6]
and
[
3
, Lemma 1.11]
. Since
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra, by
[
19
, Theorem 6.6]
we can replace the maximal tensor product
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{max}}}}\mathsf{S}
be the commuting one. Since the embedding
\mathsf{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\hookrightarrow\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is u.c.p., by the functoriality of the commuting tensor product,
[
19
, Theorem 6.3]
, the map
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{c}}}}\mathsf{S}\to\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{c}}}}\mathsf{S}
is also u.c.p.

Let
m
be a natural number and take
y=y^{*}\in\Mat_{m}(\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{c}}}}\mathsf{S})
which is positive when viewed as an element of
\Mat_{m}(\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{c}}}}\mathsf{S})
. We have to show that
y
is positive when viewed as an element of
\Mat_{m}(\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{c}}}}\mathsf{S})
, i.e. given u.c.p. maps
\varphi_{1}\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\to\B(\mathsf{H})
and
\varphi_{2}\colon\mathsf{S}\to\B(\mathsf{H})
with commuting ranges we have to prove the positivity of
\bigl(\mathrm{id}\otimes(\varphi_{1}\cdot\varphi_{2})\bigr)(y)
(cf.
[
19
, Section 6]
, the definition of
\varphi_{1}\cdot\varphi_{2}
is on page 289, see also the proof of Proposition
5.6
).

Denoting by
\mathsf{A}_{\varphi_{1}}
the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra generated by the range of
\varphi_{1}
and using Theorem
3.9
we find a u.c.p. extension
\widetilde{\varphi}_{1}\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\to\mathsf{A}_{\varphi_{1}}
of
\varphi_{1}
. In particular
\widetilde{\varphi}_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
have commuting ranges. This shows that
\widetilde{\varphi}_{1}\cdot\varphi_{2}\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{c}}}}\mathsf{S}\to\B(\mathsf{H})
is a u.c.p. map that extends
\varphi_{1}\cdot\varphi_{2}\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{c}}}}\mathsf{S}\to\B(\mathsf{H})
and we conclude that
\bigl(\mathrm{id}\otimes(\varphi_{1}\cdot\varphi_{2})\bigr)(y)=\bigl(\mathrm{id}\otimes(\widetilde{\varphi}_{1}\cdot\varphi_{2})\bigr)(y)
. Clearly the latter element is positive.
∎

.

With
\mathbb{P}

.

4.
Lifting property of
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
and
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra. Following
[
5

we say that
\mathsf{A}
has the

if given any
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathsf{B}
with an ideal
\mathsf{J}\subset\mathsf{B}
, any contractive completely positive
\varphi\colon\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{B}/\mathsf{J}
admits a contractive completely positive lift

. For unital algebras the lifting property is equivalent to the analogous property with “contractive completely positive” replaced by “unital completely positive” (
[

).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a finite quantum space and let
\mathbb{O}
be a compact quantum space such that
\C(\mathbb{O})
is separable and has the lifting property. Then the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
has the lifting property.

The famous Choi-Effros lifting theorem (
[
5
, Theorem C.3]
) states that any separable nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra has the lifting property. We will see in Remark

that
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is almost never nuclear. We need the assumption of separability of
\C(\mathbb{O})
to be able to use Kasparov’s dilation theorem.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
\mathsf{B}
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra with an ideal
\mathsf{J}\subset\mathsf{B}
and let

be a unital
*
-homomorphism. Consider the homomorphism

\Psi=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\rho)\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\longrightarrow\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes(\mathsf{B}/\mathsf{J})=\bigl(\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathsf{B}\bigr)/\bigl(\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathsf{J}\bigr).

By assumption
\Psi
admits a u.c.p. lift
\widetilde{\Psi}\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathsf{B}
. Now we use Lemma
3.2
to write
\widetilde{\Psi}
in the form
\widetilde{\Psi}=\Theta_{1,1}
for a
\Theta\in\Mor(\C(\mathbb{O}),\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{B})
.

By the universal property of
(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}},\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
there exists a unique
\Lambda\in\Mor(\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}),\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{B})
such that
\Theta=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Lambda)\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
. Now if
q
denotes the quotient map
\mathsf{B}\to\mathsf{B}/\mathsf{J}
then

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}\otimes\rho)\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}=\Psi

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}\otimes{q})\circ\widetilde{\Psi}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}\otimes{q})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\omega_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Theta

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}\otimes{q})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\omega_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Lambda)\circ\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}},

so for any
x\in\C(\mathbb{O})
and
\phi\in\C(\mathbb{P})^{*}

\rho\bigl((\phi\otimes\mathrm{id})\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}(x)\bigr)=\Bigl(q\circ\bigl((\omega_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Lambda\bigr)\Bigr)\bigl((\phi\otimes\mathrm{id})\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}(x)\bigr)

In other words for any
y\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
we have

\rho(y)=\Bigl(q\circ\bigl((\omega_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Lambda\bigr)\Bigr)(y),

and since
\rho\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\to\mathsf{B}/\mathsf{J}
is a
*
-homomorphism, the hyperrigidity of
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\subset\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
, (cf. Theorem

), implies that
q\circ\bigl((\omega_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Lambda\bigr)=\rho
on the whole
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
. In particular
(\omega_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Lambda
is a completely positive lift of
\rho
.

Having established that
*
-homomorphisms
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\to\mathsf{B}/\mathsf{J}
admit u.c.p. lifts we use Kasparov’s theorem again to obtain lifts of u.c.p. maps: let
\varphi\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\to\mathsf{B}/\mathsf{J}
be a u.c.p. map. Then
\varphi=\Phi_{1,1}
, where
\Phi\in\Mor(\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}),\mathcal{K}\otimes(\mathsf{B}/\mathsf{J}))
. Since
\M(\mathcal{K}\otimes(\mathsf{B}/\mathsf{J}))
is the image of
\M(\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{B})
under the canonical extension of
\mathrm{id}\otimes{q}\colon\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathsf{B}\to\mathcal{K}\otimes(\mathsf{B}/\mathsf{J})
(by
[

\mathrm{C}^{*}

\varphi=(\omega_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Phi=(\omega_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes{q})\circ\widetilde{\Phi}=q\circ\bigl((\omega_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\widetilde{\Phi}\bigr),

i.e.
(\omega_{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\widetilde{\Phi}
is a u.c.p. lift of

.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

All separable nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras have the lifting property (by the Choi-Effros theorem). However the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
are usually not nuclear. For example denoting by
\boldsymbol{2}
and
\boldsymbol{3}
the two- and three-point space respectively, by
[
31
, Corollary II.2]
we have

\C(\mathbb{M}_{\boldsymbol{2},\boldsymbol{3}})\cong\mathbb{C}^{2}\boldsymbol{*}\mathbb{C}^{2}\boldsymbol{*}\mathbb{C}^{2}\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\boldsymbol{*}\mathbb{Z}_{2}\boldsymbol{*}\mathbb{Z}_{2})
and
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\boldsymbol{3},\boldsymbol{2}})\cong\mathbb{C}^{3}\boldsymbol{*}\mathbb{C}^{3}\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{3}\boldsymbol{*}\mathbb{Z}_{3})
which are both non-nuclear (
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
).

In view of statements (
1
) and (
2
) of Theorem
2.15
we immediately find that

•

if
\C(\mathbb{O})
has at least two characters and
\dim{\C(\mathbb{P})}\in\{3,5,6.\dotsc\}
then there is a surjective
\pi\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\boldsymbol{2})
and an injective
\rho\colon\C(\boldsymbol{3})\to\C(\mathbb{P})
and hence
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
surjects onto
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\boldsymbol{3},\boldsymbol{2}})
,

•

if
\C(\mathbb{O})
has at least three characters and
\dim{\C(\mathbb{P})}>1
then there is a surjective
\pi\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\boldsymbol{3})
and an injective
\rho\colon\C(\boldsymbol{2})\to\C(\mathbb{P})
and hence
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
surjects onto
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\boldsymbol{2},\boldsymbol{3}})
.

Consequently
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is not nuclear in either case (
[
33
, Corollary 2.5]
).

Similarly if
\C(\mathbb{O})
has
\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with

as a quotient and
\dim{\C(\mathbb{P})}>1
then
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
surjects onto
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\boldsymbol{2},\mathbb{O}})\cong\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})\boldsymbol{*}\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})
which is non-nuclear by
[

. In particular if
\mathbb{O}
is finite,
\dim{\C(\mathbb{O})}>2
and
\dim{\C(\mathbb{P})}>1
then
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
is not nuclear.

Now we can show that the universal operator system
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
has the analogous version of lifting property:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a finite quantum space and
\mathbb{O}
a compact quantum space such that
\C(\mathbb{O})
is separable and has the lifting property. Then the operator system
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}
has the lifting property: given a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathsf{B}
with an ideal
\mathsf{J}\subset\mathsf{B}
and a u.c.p. map
\varphi\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\to\mathsf{B}/\mathsf{J}

\widetilde{\varphi}\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\to\mathsf{B}

.

Proof.

Using the strong extension property of
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\subset\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
(Theorem

) we can find a u.c.p. extension
\eta\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\to\mathsf{B}/\mathsf{J}
. Since

has the lifting property, there is a u.c.p. lift
\widetilde{\eta}\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\to\mathsf{B}
of

\widetilde{\varphi}=\bigl.\widetilde{\eta}\bigr|_{\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}}
.
∎

5.

6.
Synchronous correlations

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{O}
be finite quantum spaces and let us specify the decompositions

\C(\mathbb{P})=\bigoplus_{l=1}^{N_{\mathbb{P}}}\Mat_{m_{l}}(\mathbb{C})\quad\text{and}\quad\C(\mathbb{O})=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{N_{\mathbb{O}}}\Mat_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{C}).

(6.1)

Let
\bigl\{\prescript{l}{}{f_{st}}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,l=1\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{P}},\>s,t=1,\dotsc,m_{l}\bigr\}
and
\bigl\{\prescript{k}{}{e_{ij}}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,k=1\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{O}},\>i,j=1,\dotsc,n_{k}\bigr\}
be the corresponding systems of matrix units in
\C(\mathbb{P})
and
\C(\mathbb{O})
.

Furthermore for each
l
and
k
let
\bigl\{\prescript{l}{}{f_{s}}\bigr\}_{s=1,\dotsc,m_{l}}
and
\bigl\{\prescript{k}{}{e_{i}}\bigr\}_{i=1,\dotsc,n_{k}}
be the standard bases of
\mathbb{C}^{m_{l}}
and
\mathbb{C}^{n_{k}}
respectively. Clearly we have

\prescript{l}{}{f_{st}}={\bigl|\prescript{l}{}{f_{s}}\bigr\rangle}{\bigl\langle\prescript{l}{}{f_{t}}\bigr|}\quad\text{and}\quad\prescript{k}{}{e_{ij}}={\bigl|\prescript{k}{}{e_{i}}\bigr\rangle}{\bigl\langle\prescript{k}{}{e_{j}}\bigr|}

for all
k,l,i,j,s,t
.

Given a
(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O})
-correlation
T\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\otimes\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\C(\mathbb{P})
and natural numbers

\displaystyle k,k^{\prime}

\displaystyle\in\{1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{O}}\}

\displaystyle l,l^{\prime}

\displaystyle\in\{1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{P}}\}

\displaystyle i,j

\displaystyle\in\{1,\dotsc,n_{k}\}

\displaystyle i^{\prime},j^{\prime}

\displaystyle\in\{1,\dotsc,n_{k^{\prime}}\}

\displaystyle s,t

\displaystyle\in\{1,\dotsc,m_{l}\}

\displaystyle s^{\prime},t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\in\{1,\dotsc,m_{l^{\prime}}\}

we define

\prescript{kk^{\prime}}{ll^{\prime}}{X}_{(ij),(i^{\prime}j^{\prime})}^{(st),(s^{\prime}t^{\prime})}\in\mathbb{C}

(6.2)

by

T(\prescript{k}{}{e}_{ij}\otimes\prescript{k^{\prime}}{}{e}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}})=\sum_{s,t,s^{\prime},t^{\prime},l,l^{\prime}}\prescript{kk^{\prime}}{ll^{\prime}}{X}_{(ij),(i^{\prime}j^{\prime})}^{(st),(s^{\prime}t^{\prime})}\bigl(\prescript{l}{}{f}_{st}\otimes\prescript{l^{\prime}}{}{f}_{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\bigr).

(6.3)

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{O}
be finite quantum spaces with decompositions of
\C(\mathbb{P})
and
\C(\mathbb{O})
as in (
6.1
). A
(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O})
-correlation
T\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\otimes\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\C(\mathbb{P})
is called
synchronous
if

\sum_{s,t,i,j,k,l}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}m_{l}}\prescript{kk}{ll}{X}_{(ij),(ij)}^{(st),(st)}=N_{\mathbb{P}},

where the coefficients
\prescript{kk^{\prime}}{ll^{\prime}}{X}_{(ij),(i^{\prime}j^{\prime})}^{(st),(s^{\prime}t^{\prime})}
are defined by (
6.3
).

Remark 6.2
.

The definition of synchronicity proposed above may seem rather technical, but it is in fact strongly related to the classical notion of synchronicity (as in e.g.
[
12
, Section II]
,
[
17
, Section 2]
). In case the sets
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{O}
are classical we have
n_{k}=m_{l}=1
for all
k,l
and there are no “internal indices” inside matrix blocks, so the collection of numbers (
6.2
) related to
T
reduces to
\bigl\{\prescript{kk^{\prime}}{ll^{\prime}}{X}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,k,k^{\prime}=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{O}},\>l,l^{\prime}=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{P}}\bigr\}
and it corresponds to the classical correlation matrix via

\prescript{kk^{\prime}}{ll^{\prime}}{X}=p(k,k^{\prime}|l,l^{\prime}),\qquad{k,k^{\prime}}=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{O}},\>l,l^{\prime}=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{P}}.

Note that in terms of a game with strategy
p(\cdot,\cdot|\cdot,\cdot)
for a fixed
l
the sum of
p(k,k^{\prime}|l,l)
over all
k,k^{\prime}
is the probability of giving any pair of answers to the pair of questions
(l,l)
, so
\sum\limits_{k,k^{\prime}}p(k,k^{\prime}|l,l)=1
. Thus the condition

\sum\limits_{k,l}p(k,k|l,l)=N_{\mathbb{P}}

means that for each
l
we must have
p(k,k^{\prime}|l,l)=0
whenever
k\neq{k^{\prime}}
.

The next proposition shows that synchronicity of a correlation can be checked by evaluating it on one particular element and then taking its expectation on the maximally entangled state.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{O}
be finite quantum spaces with decompositions of
\C(\mathbb{P})
and
\C(\mathbb{O})
as in (
6.1
) and let
T\colon\C(\mathbb{O})\otimes\C(\mathbb{O})\to\C(\mathbb{P})\otimes\C(\mathbb{P})
be a
(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O})
-correlation. Then
T
is synchronous if and only if

{\left\langle{\phi}\,\vline\,T\biggl(\sum_{i,j,k}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}}\prescript{k}{}{e}_{ij}\otimes\prescript{k}{}{e}_{ij}\biggr)\phi\right\rangle}=1,

where
\phi=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\mathbb{P}}}}\sum\limits_{l}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{m_{l}}}\sum\limits_{s}\bigl(\prescript{l}{}{f}_{s}\otimes\prescript{l}{}{f}_{s}\bigr)\in\biggl(\bigoplus\limits_{l=1}^{N_{\mathbb{P}}}\mathbb{C}^{m_{l}}\biggr)\otimes\biggl(\bigoplus\limits_{l=1}^{N_{\mathbb{P}}}\mathbb{C}^{m_{l}}\biggr)
.

Proof.

We have

T\biggl(\sum_{i,j,k}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}}\prescript{k}{}{e}_{ij}\otimes\prescript{k}{}{e}_{ij}\biggr)=\sum_{i,j,k,l,l^{\prime}s,s^{\prime},t,t^{\prime}}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}}\prescript{kk}{ll^{\prime}}{X}_{(ij),(ij)}^{(st),(s^{\prime}t^{\prime})}\bigl(\prescript{l}{}{f}_{st}\otimes\prescript{l^{\prime}}{}{f}_{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\bigr).

Furthermore for any
l,l^{\prime},s,t,s^{\prime},t^{\prime}

\displaystyle{\bigl\langle{\phi}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\bigl(\prescript{l}{}{f}_{st}\otimes\prescript{l^{\prime}}{}{f}_{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\bigr)\phi\bigr\rangle}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{N_{\mathbb{P}}}\sum_{l_{1},l_{2}}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{m_{l_{1}}}}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{m_{l_{2}}}}\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}}{\bigl\langle{\prescript{l_{1}}{}{f}_{s_{1}}\otimes\prescript{l_{1}}{}{f}_{s_{1}}}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\bigl(\prescript{l}{}{f}_{st}\otimes\prescript{l^{\prime}}{}{f}_{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\bigr)\prescript{l_{2}}{}{f}_{s_{2}}\otimes\prescript{l_{2}}{}{f}_{s_{2}}\bigr\rangle}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{N_{\mathbb{P}}}\sum_{l_{1},l_{2}}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{m_{l_{1}}}}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{m_{l_{2}}}}\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}}\delta_{ll_{1}}\delta_{l^{\prime}l_{1}}\delta_{ll_{2}}\delta_{l^{\prime}l_{2}}\delta_{ss_{1}}\delta_{s^{\prime}s_{1}}\delta_{ts_{2}}\delta_{t^{\prime}s_{2}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{N_{\mathbb{P}}}\sum_{l_{1},l_{2}}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{m_{l_{1}}}}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{m_{l_{2}}}}\delta_{ll_{1}}\delta_{l^{\prime}l_{1}}\delta_{ll_{2}}\delta_{l^{\prime}l_{2}}\delta_{ss^{\prime}}\delta_{tt^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{N_{\mathbb{P}}}\tfrac{1}{m_{l}}\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{ss^{\prime}}\delta_{tt^{\prime}}.

Substituting this into the first equality we obtain

{\left\langle{\phi}\,\vline\,T\biggl(\sum_{i,j,k}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}}\prescript{k}{}{e}_{ij}\otimes\prescript{k}{}{e}_{ij}\biggr)\phi\right\rangle}=\tfrac{1}{N_{\mathbb{P}}}\sum_{s,t,i,k,l,j}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}m_{l}}\prescript{kk}{ll}{X}_{(ij),(ij)}^{(st),(st)}.

∎

We now pass to the construction of certain synchronous correlations via the prescription provided by Theorem
5.8
. It says that realizable
(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O})
-correlation is given by a state on
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\otimes\text{\tiny{\rm{max}}}\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
. Let
\sigma
be such a state. The corresponding
T_{\sigma}
is defined by

T_{\sigma}(x\otimes{y})=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\sigma)\bigl(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}(x)_{13}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}(y)_{24}\bigr),\qquad{x,y}\in\C(\mathbb{O}).

We define
\prescript{k}{l}{V}_{ij}^{st}\in\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
by

\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}(\prescript{k}{}{e_{ij}})=\sum_{s,t,l}\prescript{l}{}{f}_{st}\otimes\prescript{k}{l}{V}_{ij}^{st},\qquad\quad\begin{array}[]{r@{\;}l}k&=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{O}},\\
i,j&=1,\dotsc,n_{k}.\end{array}

With this notation we have

T_{\sigma}\bigl(\prescript{k}{}{e_{ij}}\otimes\prescript{k^{\prime}}{}{e_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}}\bigr)=\sum_{s,t,s^{\prime},t^{\prime},l,l^{\prime}}\sigma\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st}_{ij}}\otimes\prescript{k^{\prime}}{l^{\prime}}{V^{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}}\bigr)\bigl(\prescript{l}{}{f_{st}}\otimes\prescript{l^{\prime}}{}{f_{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}}\bigr)

for all
k,k^{\prime},i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\tau
be a trace on
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
. Then there exists a state
\sigma_{\tau}
on
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{max}}}}\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
such that

\sigma_{\tau}\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st}_{ij}}\otimes\prescript{k^{\prime}}{l^{\prime}}{V^{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}}\bigr)=\tau\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st}_{ij}}\prescript{k^{\prime}}{l^{\prime}}{V^{t^{\prime}s^{\prime}}_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}\bigr)

Proof.

Let
(\mathsf{H}_{\tau},\pi_{\tau},\Omega_{\tau})
be the GNS triple for
\tau
. Since
\tau
is a trace, the mapping

\pi_{\tau}(a)\Omega_{\tau}\longmapsto\pi_{\tau}(a^{*})\Omega_{\tau},\qquad{a}\in\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})

(defined on a dense subspace of
\mathsf{H}_{\tau}
) extends to an anti-unitary operator
J_{\tau}\colon\mathsf{H}_{\tau}\to\mathsf{H}_{\tau}
. Therefore
\pi_{\tau}^{\prime}\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\to\B(\mathsf{H}_{\tau})
defined by

\pi_{\tau}^{\prime}(a)=J_{\tau}\pi_{\tau}(a^{*})J_{\tau},\qquad{a}\in\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})

is an anti-representation of
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
, or in other words, a representation of
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
. Thus there exists a representation
\Pi
of
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{max}}}}\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
on
\mathsf{H}
such that

\Pi(a\otimes{b})=\pi_{\tau}(a)\pi_{\tau}^{\prime}(b),\qquad{a,b}\in\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})

and we can define a state
\sigma_{\tau}^{\prime}
on
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{max}}}}\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
by

\sigma_{\tau}^{\prime}(x)={\left\langle{\Omega_{\tau}}\,\vline\,\Pi(x)\Omega_{\tau}\right\rangle},\qquad{x}\in\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\otimes_{\text{\tiny{\rm{max}}}}\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}.

Now recall from Section
2.3
that
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
is isomorphic to
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
. One such isomorphism arises from each pair of isomorphisms
\C(\mathbb{O})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}\cong\C(\mathbb{O})
and
\C(\mathbb{P})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}\cong\C(\mathbb{P})
(cf. the proof of Corollary
2.17
). Taking the transposition on both these algebras yields an isomorphism
\Upsilon\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\to\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})^{\text{\raisebox{1.5pt}{\scalebox{0.7}{\tiny{\rm{op}}}}}}
such that

\Upsilon\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st}_{ij}}\bigr)=\prescript{k}{l}{V^{ts}_{ji}},\qquad\quad\begin{array}[]{r@{\;}l}k&=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{O}},\\
l&=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{P}},\\
i,j&=1,\dotsc,n_{k},\\
s,t&=1,\dotsc,m_{l}.\\
\end{array}

We define
\sigma_{\tau}
by
\sigma_{\tau}^{\prime}\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Upsilon)
.
∎

By a mild abuse of notation let us denote the
(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O})
-correlation arising from
\sigma_{\tau}
(as defined in Lemma
6.4
) by
T_{\tau}
. Clearly

T_{\tau}\bigl(\prescript{k}{}{e_{ij}}\otimes\prescript{k^{\prime}}{}{e_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}}\bigr)=\sum_{s,t,s^{\prime},t^{\prime},l,l^{\prime}}\tau\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st}_{ij}}\prescript{k^{\prime}}{l^{\prime}}{V^{t^{\prime}s^{\prime}}_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}\bigr)\bigl(\prescript{l}{}{f_{st}}\otimes\prescript{l^{\prime}}{}{f_{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}}\bigr)

for all
k,k^{\prime},i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}
, so that

\prescript{kk^{\prime}}{ll^{\prime}}{X}_{(ij),(i^{\prime}j^{\prime})}^{(st),(s^{\prime}t^{\prime})}=\tau\left(\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st}_{ij}}\prescript{k^{\prime}}{l^{\prime}}{V^{t^{\prime}s^{\prime}}_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}\right),\qquad\quad\begin{array}[]{r@{\;}l}k,k^{\prime}&=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{O}},\\
l,l^{\prime}&=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{P}},\\
i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}&=1,\dotsc,n_{k},\\
s,t,s^{\prime},t^{\prime}&=1,\dotsc,m_{l}.\end{array}

(6.4)

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{O}
be finite quantum spaces and
\tau
a trace on
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
. Then the
(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O})
-correlation
T_{\tau}
is synchronous.

Proof.

First we compute
\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\bigl(\prescript{k}{}{e_{ij}}\prescript{k^{\prime}}{}{e_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}\bigr)
in two ways:

\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\bigl(\prescript{k}{}{e_{ij}}\bigr)\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\bigl(\prescript{k^{\prime}}{}{e_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}\bigr)=\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\bigl(\prescript{k}{}{e_{ij}}\prescript{k^{\prime}}{}{e_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}\bigr)=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\bigl(\prescript{k}{}{e_{ii^{\prime}}}\bigr).

The left-hand side is

\displaystyle\biggl(\sum_{s,t,l}\prescript{l}{}{f_{st}}\otimes\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st}_{ij}}\biggr)\biggl(\sum_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime},l^{\prime}}\prescript{l^{\prime}}{}{f_{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}}\otimes\prescript{k^{\prime}}{l^{\prime}}{V^{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,t,l,s^{\prime},t^{\prime},l^{\prime}}\prescript{l}{}{f_{st}}\prescript{l^{\prime}}{}{f_{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}}\otimes\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st}_{ij}}\prescript{k^{\prime}}{l^{\prime}}{V^{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,t,l,t^{\prime}}\prescript{l}{}{f_{st^{\prime}}}\otimes\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st}_{ij}}\prescript{k^{\prime}}{l}{V^{tt^{\prime}}_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,t,l,t^{\prime}}\prescript{l}{}{f_{st}}\otimes\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st^{\prime}}_{ij}}\prescript{k^{\prime}}{l}{V^{t^{\prime}t}_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,t,l}\prescript{l}{}{f_{st}}\otimes\sum_{t^{\prime}}\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st^{\prime}}_{ij}}\prescript{k^{\prime}}{l}{V^{t^{\prime}t}_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}

while the right-hand side is
\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}\sum\limits_{s,t,l}\prescript{l}{}{f_{st}}\otimes\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st}_{ii^{\prime}}}=\sum\limits_{s,t,l}\prescript{l}{}{f_{st}}\otimes\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st}_{ii^{\prime}}}
, so that

\sum_{t^{\prime}}\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st^{\prime}}_{ij}}\prescript{k^{\prime}}{l}{V^{t^{\prime}t}_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st}_{ii^{\prime}}}.

which for
t=s
reads

\sum_{t}\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st}_{ij}}\prescript{k^{\prime}}{l}{V^{ts}_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\prescript{k}{l}{V^{ss}_{ii^{\prime}}}.

(6.5)

Using this information and denoting by
\operatorname{Tr}_{l}
the standard trace on the
l
-th matrix block of
\C(\mathbb{P})
we compute

\displaystyle\sum_{s,t}\prescript{kk^{\prime}}{ll}{X}_{(ij),(i^{\prime}j^{\prime})}^{(st),(st)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,t}\tau\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{V}_{ij}^{st}\prescript{k^{\prime}}{l}{V}_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}^{ts}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\tau\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{V}_{ii^{\prime}}^{ss}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}(\operatorname{Tr}_{l}\otimes\tau)\biggl(\sum_{s,t,l^{\prime}}\prescript{l^{\prime}}{}{f_{st}}\otimes\prescript{k}{l^{\prime}}{V^{st}_{ii^{\prime}}}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}(\operatorname{Tr}_{l}\otimes\tau)\Bigl(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\bigl(\prescript{k}{}{e_{ii^{\prime}}}\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}(\operatorname{Tr}_{l}\otimes\tau)\Bigl(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\bigl(\prescript{k}{}{e_{ip}}\prescript{k}{}{e_{pi^{\prime}}}\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}(\operatorname{Tr}_{l}\otimes\tau)\Bigl(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\bigl(\prescript{k}{}{e_{ip}}\bigr)\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\bigl(\prescript{k}{}{e_{pi^{\prime}}}\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}(\operatorname{Tr}_{l}\otimes\tau)\Bigl(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\bigl(\prescript{k}{}{e_{pi^{\prime}}}\bigr)\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\bigl(\prescript{k}{}{e_{ip}}\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\delta_{ii^{\prime}}(\operatorname{Tr}_{l}\otimes\tau)\Bigl(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\bigl(\prescript{k}{}{e_{pp}}\bigr)\Bigr)

for every
p\in\{1,2,\dotsc,n_{k}\}
.

Putting
k=k^{\prime}
,
i=i^{\prime}
,
j=j^{\prime}
and summing over
i,j\in\{1,\dotsc,n_{k}\}
we get

\sum_{s,t,i,j}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}^{2}}\prescript{kk}{ll}{X}_{(ij),(ij)}^{(st),(st)}=(\operatorname{Tr}_{l}\otimes\tau)\Bigl(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\bigl(\prescript{k}{}{e_{pp}}\bigr)\Bigr).

Next, summing over
p\in\{1,\dotsc,n_{k}\}
we obtain

\sum_{s,t,i,j}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}}\prescript{kk}{ll}{X}_{(ij),(ij)}^{(st),(st)}=(\operatorname{Tr}_{l}\otimes\tau)\biggl(\sum_{p}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}}\bigl(\prescript{k}{}{e_{pp}}\bigr)\biggr)

and summing over
k
we get

Theorem 6.6

(1)

\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ij}^{st}\xi=\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\bigr)^{*}\xi
for all
k,l,i,j,s,t
,

(2)

the restriction of the vector state
\omega_{\xi}
to the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra generated by

\biggl\{\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ij}^{st}\,\biggr|\biggl.\,\begin{array}[]{r@{\;}lr@{\;}l}k&=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{O}},&i,j&=1,\dotsc,n_{k},\\
l&=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{P}},&s,t&=1,\dotsc,m_{l}\end{array}\biggr\}

(6.8)

is a trace,

(3)

the restriction of the vector state
\omega_{\xi}
to the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra generated by

\biggl\{\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\,\biggr|\biggl.\,\begin{array}[]{r@{\;}lr@{\;}l}k&=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{O}},&i,j&=1,\dotsc,n_{k},\\
l&=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{P}},&s,t&=1,\dotsc,m_{l}\end{array}\biggr\}

(6.9)

is a trace.

Moreover the state
\tau
on
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
defined as
\tau=\omega_{\xi}\circ\Lambda_{1}
, where
\Lambda_{1}\colon\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})\to\B(\mathsf{H})
is the unique unital
*
-homomorphism such that

Proof.

Using the Schwarz inequality in
\mathsf{H}
and then in
\mathbb{R}^{2n_{k}+2m_{l}}
we compute

\begin{split}N_{\mathbb{P}}&=\sum_{s,t,i,j,k,l}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}m_{l}}\prescript{kk}{ll}{X}_{(ij),(ij)}^{(st),(st)}=\sum_{s,t,i,j,k,l}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}m_{l}}{\bigl\langle{\xi}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ij}^{st}\xi\bigr\rangle}\\
&\leq\sum_{s,t,i,j,k,l}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}m_{l}}\Bigl|{\bigl\langle{\xi}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ij}^{st}\xi\bigr\rangle}\Bigr|\\
&=\sum_{s,t,i,j,k,l}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}m_{l}}\Bigl|{\bigl\langle{\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\bigr)^{*}\xi}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ij}^{st}\xi\bigr\rangle}\Bigr|\\
&\leq\sum_{s,t,i,j,k,l}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}m_{l}}\bigl\|\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\bigr)^{*}\xi\bigr\|\bigl\|\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ij}^{st}\xi\bigr\|\\
&\leq\sum_{k,l}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}m_{l}}\biggl(\sum_{s,t,i,j}\bigl\|\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\bigr)^{*}\xi\bigr\|^{2}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\biggl(\sum_{s,t,i,j}\bigl\|\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ij}^{st}\xi\bigr\|^{2}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&=\sum_{k,l}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}m_{l}}\biggl(\sum_{s,t,i,j}{\bigl\langle{\xi}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\bigr)^{*}\xi\bigr\rangle}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\biggl(\sum_{s,t,i,j}{\bigl\langle{\xi}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ij}^{st}\bigr)^{*}\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ij}^{st}\xi\bigr\rangle}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&=\sum_{k,l}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}m_{l}}\biggl(\sum_{s,t,i,j}{\bigl\langle{\xi}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ji}^{ts}\xi\bigr\rangle}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\biggl(\sum_{s,t,i,j}{\bigl\langle{\xi}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ji}^{ts}\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ij}^{st}\xi\bigr\rangle}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(6.10)

Next we note that

\displaystyle\sum_{s,t,i,j,k}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}}\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ji}^{ts}=\sum_{i,s,k}\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ii}^{ss}=m_{l}\mathds{1}_{H},

(6.11a)

\displaystyle\sum_{s,t,i,j,k}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}}\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ji}^{ts}\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ij}^{st}=\sum_{j,t,k}\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{jj}^{tt}=m_{l}\mathds{1}_{H}.

(6.11b)

Indeed, the first equality in (
6.11a
) follows from the fact that

\sum_{t}\prescript{k}{l}{U^{st}_{ij}}\prescript{k^{\prime}}{l}{U^{ts}_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\prescript{k}{l}{U^{ss}_{ii^{\prime}}},\qquad\quad\begin{array}[]{r@{\;}l}k,k^{\prime}&=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{O}},\\
l&=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{P}},\\
j,j^{\prime},i,i^{\prime}&=1,\dotsc,n_{k},\\
s&=1,\dotsc,m_{l}.\end{array}

which is obtained by applying
\Lambda_{1}
(described in the statement of the theorem) to both sides of (
6.5
) and noting that

\Lambda_{1}\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{V^{st}_{ij}}\bigr)=\prescript{k}{l}{U^{st}_{ij}},\qquad\quad\begin{array}[]{r@{\;}l}k&=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{O}},\\
l&=1,\dotsc,N_{\mathbb{P}},\\
i,j&=1,\dotsc,n_{k},\\
s,t&=1,\dotsc,m_{l}.\end{array}

(6.12)

The second equality in (
6.11a

). Similarly we derive (

In particular

\sum_{k,l}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}m_{l}}\biggl(\sum_{s,t,i,j}{\bigl\langle{\xi}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ji}^{ts}\xi\bigr\rangle}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\biggl(\sum_{s,t,i,j}{\bigl\langle{\xi}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ji}^{ts}\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ij}^{st}\xi\bigr\rangle}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{k}{\left\langle{\xi}\,\vline\,\xi\right\rangle}=N_{\mathbb{P}}

and the inequalities in (

) are actually equalities. From the fact that the first inequality in (

) is an equality we conclude that

\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ij}^{st}\xi=\prescript{k}{l}{\alpha}_{ij}^{st}\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\bigr)^{*}\xi

for all

&=\prescript{k}{l}{\alpha}_{ij}^{st}{\bigl\langle{\xi}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\bigr)^{*}\xi\bigr\rangle}\end{split}

in both cases we get

\prescript{k}{l}{W}_{ij}^{st}\xi=\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{U}_{ij}^{st}\bigr)^{*}\xi

for all

Remark 6.7
.

Let us give a separate proof that a
(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O})
-correlation
T_{\tau}
defined by a trace
\tau
on
\C(\mathbb{M}_{\mathbb{P},\mathbb{O}})
as explained before Theorem
6.5
is synchronous. We will use Proposition
6.3
.

We compute

\displaystyle{\left\langle{\phi}\,\vline\,T\biggl(\sum_{i,j,k}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}}\prescript{k}{}{e}_{ij}\otimes\prescript{k}{}{e}_{ij}\biggr)\phi\right\rangle}

\displaystyle={\left\langle{\phi}\,\vline\,\biggl(\sum_{i,j,k,l,l^{\prime},s,t,s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\tau\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{V}_{ij}^{st}\prescript{k}{l^{\prime}}{V}_{ji}^{t^{\prime}s^{\prime}}\bigr)\tfrac{1}{n_{k}}\bigl(\prescript{l}{}{f}_{st}\otimes\prescript{l^{\prime}}{}{f}_{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\bigr)\biggr)\phi\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,l,l^{\prime},s,t,s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}}\tau\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{V}_{ij}^{st}\prescript{k}{l^{\prime}}{V}_{ji}^{t^{\prime}s^{\prime}}\bigr){\bigl\langle{\phi}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\bigl(\prescript{l}{}{f}_{st}\otimes\prescript{l^{\prime}}{}{f}_{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\bigr)\phi\bigr\rangle}.

From the identity

\displaystyle{\bigl\langle{\phi}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\bigl(\prescript{l}{}{f}_{st}\otimes\prescript{l^{\prime}}{}{f}_{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\bigr)\phi\bigr\rangle}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{N_{\mathbb{P}}{m_{l}}}\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\sum_{s_{1},t_{1}}{\bigl\langle{\prescript{l}{}{f}_{s_{1}}}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\prescript{l}{}{f}_{s}\bigr\rangle}{\bigl\langle{\prescript{l}{}{f}_{s_{1}}}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\prescript{l}{}{f}_{s^{\prime}}\bigr\rangle}{\bigl\langle{\prescript{l}{}{f}_{t}}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\prescript{l}{}{f}_{t_{1}}\bigr\rangle}{\bigl\langle{\prescript{l}{}{f}_{t^{\prime}}}\,\bigr|\bigl.\,\prescript{l}{}{f}_{t_{1}}\bigr\rangle}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{N_{\mathbb{P}}{m_{l}}}\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{ss^{\prime}}\delta_{tt^{\prime}}

we get

\displaystyle{\left\langle{\phi}\,\vline\,T\biggl(\sum_{i,j,k}\tfrac{1}{n_{k}}\prescript{k}{}{e}_{ij}\otimes\prescript{k}{}{e}_{ij}\biggr)\phi\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,l,l^{\prime},s,t,s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\tfrac{1}{N_{\mathbb{P}}m_{l}n_{k}}\tau\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{V}_{ij}^{st}\prescript{k}{l^{\prime}}{V}_{ji}^{t^{\prime}s^{\prime}}\bigr)\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{ss^{\prime}}\delta_{tt^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,l,s,t}\tfrac{1}{N_{\mathbb{P}}m_{l}n_{k}}\tau\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{V}_{ij}^{st}\prescript{k}{l}{V}_{ji}^{ts}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,k,l,s}\tfrac{1}{N_{\mathbb{P}}m_{l}}\tau\bigl(\prescript{k}{l}{V}_{ii}^{ss}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,s}\tfrac{1}{N_{\mathbb{P}}m_{l}}\tau(\mathds{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,s}\tfrac{1}{N_{\mathbb{P}}m_{l}}=1.

was a trace (although it is needed to define

In this manuscript we study the existence of an optimal configuration for a minimization problem ruled by the nonlinear degenerate and possibly not homogeneous operator
g
-Laplacian
defined as
\Delta_{g}u:=\operatorname{\text{div}}(g(|\nabla u|)\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|})
, where
G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(s)\,ds
is a Young function fulfilling the following growth condition

(L)

1<p^{-}G(t)\leq tg(t)\leq p^{+}G(t)<\infty\qquad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

for fixed constants
p^{+}
and
p^{-}
and

(C)

g(t)\text{ is convex for all }t\geq 0.

These kind of problems appears naturally when studying general optimal design problems, and they are usually formulated as problems of minimization of the energy, stored in the design under a prescribed loading. Solutions of these problems are unstable to perturbations of the loading, and the optimal design problem is formulated as minimization of the stored energy under the most unfavorable loading. This most dangerous loading is one that maximizes the stored energy over the class of admissible functions. Finally, the problem is reduced to minimization involving the behavior on the boundary. See
[
7
,
33
]
for more details.

We describe now our problem. We consider a bounded connected Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\alpha>0
and numbers
c\in[0,|\Omega|]
. For any measurable set
E\subset\Omega
, we consider the quantity
\lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,E)

(
\mathcal{D}
)

\lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,E)=\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega}G(|\nabla u|)\,dx+\alpha\int_{E}G(|u|)\,dx\colon u\in\mathcal{W}(\Omega)\right\},

where
\mathcal{W}(\Omega)
is the set of functions

such that
\int_{\Omega}G(|u|)\,dx=1
. We also consider the quantity

(
\mathcal{N}
)

\lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,E)=\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega}G(|\nabla u|)+G(|u|)\,dx+\alpha\int_{E}G(|u|)\,dx\colon u\in\mathcal{W}(\Omega)\right\},

where in this case
\mathcal{W}(\Omega)
is the set of functions
u\in W^{1,G}(\Omega)
such that
\int_{\Omega}G(|u|)\,dx=1
.
Our minimization problem can be stated as follows: given
\alpha>0
and

we look for

(1.2)

\Lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,c):=\inf\{\lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,E)\colon E\subset\Omega,|E|=c\},

that is, to optimize
\lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,E)
with respect to the class of sets

of fixed volume.
Let

be its characteristic function on

D

) the eigenvalue problem

(
\mathcal{P}_{D}
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta_{g}u+\alpha\chi_{E}g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}=\lambda g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}&\text{ in }\Omega\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

and

(
\mathcal{P}_{N}
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta_{g}u+g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}+\alpha\chi_{E}g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}=\lambda g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}&\text{ in }\Omega\\

In some applications, for certain design purposes, different forms of the cost functional need to be considered. Preliminaries

In this section we introduce some notation and basic results on Orlicz-Sobolev spaces that we will use in this paper.

2.1.
Young functions

An application
G\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is said to be a
Young function
if it admits the integral formulation
G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,d\tau
, where the right continuous function
g
defined on
[0,\infty)
has the following properties:

(
g_{1}
)

\displaystyle g(0)=0,\quad g(t)>0\text{ for }t>0,

(
g_{2}
)

\displaystyle g\text{ is non-decreasing on }(0,\infty),

(
g_{3}
)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)=\infty.

From these properties it is easy to see that a Young function
G
is continuous, non negative, strictly increasing and convex on
[0,\infty)
.

We will assume the following growth condition on Young functions: there exist fixed constants
p^{\pm}
such that

1\leq p^{-}\leq\frac{tg(t)}{G(t)}\leq p^{+}<\infty,\qquad\text{ for all }t>0.

For technical reasons, we will also assume that
g(t)
is convex for all
t\geq 0
.

The following properties on Young functions are well-known. See for instance
[
21
]
for the proof of these results.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a Young function satisfying (
L
) and
a,b\geq 0
. Then

(
L_{1}
)

\displaystyle\min\{a^{p^{-}},a^{p^{+}}\}G(b)\leq G(ab)\leq\max\{a^{p^{-}},a^{p^{+}}\}G(b),

(
L_{2}
)

\displaystyle G(a+b)\leq\mathbf{C}(G(a)+G(b))\quad\text{with }\mathbf{C}:=2^{p^{+}},

(
L_{3}
)

\displaystyle G\text{ is Lipschitz continuous}.

Condition (
L
) is known as the
\Delta_{2}
condition
or
doubling condition
and, as it is showed in
[
21
, Theorem 3.4.4]
, it is equivalent to the right hand side inequality in (
L
).

The
complementary
Young function of a Young function
G
is defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(t):=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{st-G(s)\}.

It is easy to see that the left hand side inequality in (
L
) is equivalent to assume that
\tilde{G}
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition.

The following useful Lemma can be found in
[
19
, Lemma 2.9]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a Young function and let
\tilde{G}
be its complementary function. Then

\tilde{G}(g(t))\leq CG(t).

Finally, we state the following version of the so-called Bathtub principle in our settings. See for instance
[
26
, Theorem 1.14]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Then, the minimization problem

\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega}\eta G(|u|)\,dx\colon 0\leq\eta\leq 1,\int_{\Omega}\eta\,dx=c\right\}

has a solution
\eta=\chi_{E}(x)
where
E
is any set with
|E|=c
and

\{u<t\}\subset E\subset\{u\leq t\},\quad t=\sup\{s\colon|\{u<s\}|<c\}.

2.2.
Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

Given a Young function
G
and a bounded set
\Omega
we consider the spaces

\displaystyle L^{G}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\{u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\text{ measurable such that }\int_{\Omega}G(|u|)\,dx<\infty\},

\displaystyle L^{G}(\partial\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\{u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\text{ measurable such that }\int_{\partial\Omega}G(|u|)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}<\infty\},

\displaystyle W^{1,G}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\{u\in L^{G}(\Omega)\text{ such that }\int_{\Omega}G(|\nabla u|)\,dx<\infty\}.

These spaces are endowed with the so called
Luxemburg norm
defined as follows

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}G\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{G}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\partial\Omega}G\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq 1\right\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{1,G}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)},

and are reflexive and separable Banach spaces if and only if
G
y
\tilde{G}
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition.

We define
W^{1,G}_{0}(\Omega)
as the subset of functions in
W^{1,G}(\Omega)
such that
u=0
in
\partial\Omega
.
We conclude this subsection by recalling that under our assumptions, boundedness of the modular implies boundedness of the norm (see for instance
[
11
, Lemma 2.1.12]
).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a Young function satisfying (
L
). Then, if
\int_{\partial\Omega}G(|u|)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
(resp.
\int_{\Omega}G(|u|)\,dx
) is bounded, then
\|u\|_{L^{G}(\partial\Omega)}
(resp.
\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}
) is bounded.

We also recall the following Poincaré’s inequality: given a bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for any
u\in W^{1,G}(\Omega)
such that
|\{x\in\Omega\colon u(x)=0\}|=\kappa>0
it holds that

\int_{\Omega}G(|u|)\,dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}G(|\nabla u|)\,dx

for some positive constant
C
depending only on
n
,
\kappa
and
p^{\pm}
. The same conclusion holds when
u\in W^{1,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. With this inequality together with Lemma
2.4
, we can ensure that the limit problem (
1.3
) is well possed.

2.3.
Some embedding results

In order to guarantee compact embeddings the following conditions will be assumed

(2.1)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{G^{-1}(s)}{s^{1+\frac{1}{n}}}ds<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{G^{-1}(s)}{s^{1+\frac{1}{n}}}ds=\infty.

For any Young function satisfying (

Combining

with
[

the following embedding holds.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let

be a Young function satisfying (

) and (

be a

The following vectorial inequality will be of use for our purposes. See Lemma 3.1 in

3.
The minimization problems and their related eigenvalue problems

From now on, we will always assume that
G
is a Young function satisfying (
L
) and (
2.1
). Along this section
\alpha>0
and
E\subset\Omega
are fixed.

In this section we state some properties on
\lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,E)
. For that end, we introduce the functionals
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K}\colon W^{1,G}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
are defined as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}G(|\nabla v|)+G(|u|)\,dx+\alpha\int_{E}G(|v|)\,dx

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}G(|v|)\,dx,\qquad\mathcal{K}(v):=\int_{\partial\Omega}G(|v|)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

and whose Frechét derivatives are defined from
W^{1,G}(\Omega)
(in the Neumann or Steklov case, or
W^{1,G}_{0}(\Omega)
in the Dirichlet case) into its dual space, and they are given by

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g(|\nabla u|)\tfrac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\cdot\nabla v+g(|u|)\tfrac{u}{|u|}v\,dx+\int_{E}\alpha g(|u|)\tfrac{u}{|u|}v\,dx

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g(|u|)\tfrac{u}{|u|}v\,dx,\qquad\langle\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=\int_{\partial\Omega}g(|u|)\tfrac{u}{|u|}v\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

We say that
\lambda
is an
eigenvalue
of (

P
D

) (resp. of (

P
N

)) with associated
eigenfunction

u\in W^{1,G}_{0}(\Omega)
(resp.
u\in W^{1,G}(\Omega)
) if

\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=\lambda\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle

for any
v\in W^{1,G}_{0}(\Omega)
(resp.
v\in W^{1,G}(\Omega)
).

In the Dirichlet we can neglect the term
\int_{\Omega}G(|u|)\,dx
in
\mathcal{I}(u)
due to the Poincaré’s inequality.

Similarly,
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of the Steklov equation (

P
S

) with eigenfunction
u\in W^{1,G}(\Omega)
if

\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=\lambda\langle\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle

for any
v\in W^{1,G}(\Omega)
.

We prove now that the quantities defined in (

D

), (

N

) and (

S

) are comparable with eigenvalues of (

P
D

), (

P
N

) and (

P
S

), and eigenfunctions of these eigenproblems minimize (

), (

) and (

Observe that
\lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,E)
defined in (

D

), (

N

) and (

N

) can be written as

(3.1)

\inf\left\{\frac{\mathcal{I}(v)}{\mathcal{J}(v)}\colon v\in W^{1,G}_{0}(\Omega),\int_{\Omega}G(|v|)\,dx=1\right\}\qquad\text{(Dirichlet)}

(3.2)

\inf\left\{\frac{\mathcal{I}(v)}{\mathcal{J}(v)}\colon v\in W^{1,G}(\Omega),\int_{\Omega}G(|v|)\,dx=1\right\}\qquad\text{(Neumann)}

(3.3)

\inf\left\{\frac{\mathcal{I}(v)}{\mathcal{K}(v)}\colon v\in W^{1,G}(\Omega),\int_{\partial\Omega}G(|v|)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=1\right\}\qquad\text{(Steklov)}.

The existence and continuity of such derivatives is guaranteed since
G
and
\tilde{G}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
condition.

We start by proving that
\lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,E)
is attained by a suitable function.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists
u_{D}\in W^{1,G}_{0}(\Omega)
solving (
3.1
) such that
\int_{\Omega}G(|u_{D}|)\,dx=1
. Similarly, there is
u_{N}\in W^{1,G}(\Omega)
solving (
3.2
) such that
\int_{\Omega}G(|u_{N}|)\,dx=1
and
u_{S}\in W^{1,G}(\Omega)
solving (
3.3
) such that
\int_{\Omega}G(|u_{S}|)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=1
.

Proof.

We start with the Dirichlet case. Let
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a minimizing sequence for
\lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,E)
, that is

(3.4)

u_{k}\in W^{1,G}_{0}(\Omega),\quad\mathcal{J}(u_{k})=1\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{I}(u_{k})=\lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,E).

Observe that
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{G}(\Omega)
and for
k
big enough,
\int_{\Omega}G(|\nabla u_{k}|)\,dx\leq\mathcal{I}(u_{k})\leq 1+\lambda(\alpha,E)
. Therefore, from the reflexivity of
W^{1,G}(\Omega)
together with the compact embedding stated in Proposition
2.5
we get that there exists a function
u\in W_{0}^{1,G}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence

\displaystyle u_{k}\rightharpoonup u\;\text{ weakly in }W^{1,G}(\Omega),

\displaystyle u_{k}\to u\;\text{ strongly and a.e. in }L^{G}(\Omega).

The strong convergence in
L^{G}(\Omega)
implies that
\mathcal{J}(u)=1
. Hence, by definition

\lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,E)\leq\mathcal{I}(u).

On the other hand, since modulars are lower semi-continuous with respect to the weak convergence, by Fatou’s Lemma we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(u)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{I}(u_{k})=\lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,E).

From the last two inequalities the lemma follows in the Dirichlet case. The proof for the remaining cases is analogous.

Proof.

We deal with the Dirichlet case, the remaining cases are similar. By Proposition

By Lagrange’s multiplier rule, there exists

We conclude this section by proving Theorem

Given a mensurable set

\Omega^{*}

and replace it by the spherical cap of the same

\Omega^{*}
is the union of these caps.

Now, the spherical symmetrization
u^{*}
of a measurable function

is constructed by symmetrizing the super-level sets so that, for all

\{u^{*}\geq t\}=\{u\geq t\}^{*}.

We refer to
[
22
]
for more details.

Denoting by

the ball of unit radius centered at the origin, the following properties on symmetrization can be found in
[

(see also

]
).

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

and

be its spherical symmetrization. Then,

. Moreover,

(i)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}G(|u^{*}|)\,dx=\int_{B_{1}}G(|u|)\,dx
,

(ii)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{1}}G(|u^{*}|)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial B_{1}}G(|u|)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
,

(iii)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}G(|\nabla u^{*}|)\,dx\leq\int_{B_{1}}G(|\nabla u|)\,dx
,

(iv)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}(\alpha\chi_{D})_{*}G(|u^{*}|)\,dx\leq\int_{B_{1}}\chi_{D}G(|u|)\,dx

where

and

We are now in position to prove our symmetrization result for optimal pairs.

5.

Fixed a subset

and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, in our first result we have focused on the study of the optimization problem

where

is related to the Dirichlet, Neumann or Steklov eigenvalue problem.

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\Omega}(\infty,c):=\inf\left\{\lambda_{\Omega}(\infty,E)\colon E\subset\Omega,|E|=c\right\}

\lambda_{\Omega}(\infty,E)=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{W}(\Omega),v|_{E}\equiv 0}\int_{\Omega}G(|\nabla v|)\,dx.

Now we are in position to prove our second main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We start with the proof in the Dirichlet case. Let
\alpha,c>0
be fixed and let
(u_{\alpha},E_{\alpha})
be an optimal pair related to

\Lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,c)=\inf\{\lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,E)\colon E\subset\Omega,|E|=c\},

\lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,E)

and

.

(5.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Lambda_{\Omega}(\alpha,c)&\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}G(|\nabla u_{0}|)\,dx+\alpha\int_{E_{0}}G(|u_{0}|)\,dx}{\int_{\Omega}G(|u_{0}|)\,dx}\\

with

\alpha

Abstract
In this paper we provide a Stone style duality for monotone semilattices by using the topological duality developed in Celani2020 for semilattices together with a topological description of their canonical extension. As an application of this duality we obtain a characterization of the congruences of monotone semilattices by means of monotone lower-Vietoris-type topologies.

Introduction

In
Birkhoff
Birkhoff proved that every finite distributive lattice is represented by the lower sets of a finite partial order. In
Stone2
Stone generalized the results given by Birkhoff establishing a categorical duality for bounded distributive lattices through spectral spaces with continuous functions. Later, in
Priestley I
Priestley proved another categorical duality for bounded distributive lattices by means of certain ordered topological spaces and continuous order-preserving maps. Along the years both Stone duality and Priestley duality have served as powerful tools not only for the study of bounded distributive lattices, but also as an inspiration for the study of many ordered algebraic structures associated with non-classical logics. Evidence of this is supplied by the several topological representations obtained for diverse classes of algebras with distributive semilattice reducts and semilattice reducts. This is the case of the Stone style duality for distributive semilattices developed in
CelaniTopological
;
CelaniCalomino
, which extends Stone duality and completes the representation given by Grätzer in
Gr78
, the Priestley style duality developed by Bezhanishvili and Jansana in
Guram-Jansana I
;
Guram-Jansana III
for the class of distributive semilattices and implicative semilattices, and more recently the Stone style duality for semilattices and lattices obtained by Celani and González in
Celani2020
.

A particular case of interest raises when noticing that any of these dualities can be combined with some other tools coming from algebra. This is the case of canonical extensions (
Gehrke -Jonsson2000
;
GJ2
;
PalmigianoDunn
;
GPsemilattices
). The studies on this subject started with Jónsson and Tarski’s works for Boolean algebras with operators (
Jonsson y Tarski
;
JT2
). The most remarkable contribution of these papers was that they provided a procedure for transfer the benefits and the working methodology from the duality for Boolean algebras to several classes of algebras with additional operators. The crucial observation is that given a class of algebras with lattice reduct
\bf{L}
and a suitable topological duality for
\bf{L}
, it is possible to build new topological dualities for monotone expansions of
\bf{L}
by using a topological description of the canonical extension of the members of
\bf{L}
in terms of the given duality. This approach was used by Celani and Menchón in
menchon2
to obtain a Stone style duality for monotone distributive semilattices. In
MoshierJipsen
Moshier and Jipsen employed a duality different from the ones of Stone and Priestley to give a topological description of the canonical extension of a (not necessarily distributive) bounded lattice.

The aim of this paper is to establish a Stone style duality for monotone semilattices. To do so, we provide a topological description of the canonical extension of a semilattice by using the duality obtained in
Celani2020
. We conclude by applying our duality to characterize the congruences of semilattices and monotone semilattices in terms of lower-Vietoris-type topologies (
Ivanova-dimova
). These results generalize the results obtained in
CelaniCalomino
for the class of distributive semilattices.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we give several notions and results which are needed for the rest of the paper. In Section
3
we present a topological description of the canonical extension of a semilattice in terms of the Stone style duality given in
Celani2020
and we explore the underlying connection of our results with the description of canonical extensions of semilattices given by Gouveia and Priestley in
GPsemilattices
. Further, we extend our results to a topological description of canonical extensions of monotone maps of semilattices. In Section
4
we establish our main result: a topological duality for the category of monotone semilattices and homomorphisms of monotone semilattices. Finally, in Section
5
as an application of the duality, we prove that the congruences of a semilattice and a monotone semilattice are in correspondence with the lower-Vietoris-type families and monotone lower-Vietoris-type topologies of their respective dual spaces. Moreover, we use such a characterization to obtain a version for semilattices of the results of Fajtlowicz and Schmidt in
Fajtlowicz
concerning the lattice of congruences.

3
Canonical extensions of semilattices

The aim of this section is to exhibit a topological description of the canonical extension of a semilattice in terms of the duality obtained in
Celani2020
. As a result we obtain that the canonical extension of a semilattice is determined by a closure system generated by the set of subasic saturated subsets of its dual space. Our motivations differ from those of
GPsemilattices
because we are interested in producing a duality for monontone semilattices. Further details of the relation between our construction and the Gouveia and Priestley construction will be provided in Section
3.1
.

Definition 6

Let
\langle X,\mathcal{K}\rangle
be an
S
-space. A subset
Z\subseteq X
is a

if there exists a dually directed family
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
such that
Z=\bigcap\{W\colon W\in\mathcal{L}\}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{Z}(X)
the set of all subbasic saturated subsets of an
S
-space
\langle X,\mathcal{K}\rangle
. Note that every subbasic saturated subset is a saturated set.

Lemma 1

Let
\langle X,\mathcal{K}\rangle
be an
S
-space. Then:

1.

If
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
is a dually directed family, then
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}=\{U^{c}\colon U\in\mathcal{L}\}\subseteq S(X)
is a
Y
-family for all
Y\in C_{\mathcal{K}}(X)
.

2.

If
\mathcal{A}\subseteq S(X)
is a
Y
-family for all
Y\in C_{\mathcal{K}}(X)
, then
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}=\{U^{c}\colon U\in\mathcal{A}\}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
is a dually directed family.

Moreover, the assignments
\mathcal{L}\mapsto\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}
and
\mathcal{A}\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}
establish a bijection between the set of dually directed families of
\mathcal{K}
and the families of

which are a

-family for all

.

Proof

1. Let
Y\in C_{\mathcal{K}}(X)
and

. Since

is a dually directed family, there exists

such that

. From

,

and

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}=\{U^{c}\colon U\in\mathcal{L}\}
is a

-family.

2. Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq S(X)
be a
Y
-family for all

. Let

. As
X\in C_{\mathcal{K}}(X)
, there exists

such that

. Therefore,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}=\{U^{c}\colon U\in\mathcal{A}\}\subseteq\mathcal{K}

For the moreover part, observe that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}}=\mathcal{L}
and

If

Proposition 2

Let
\langle X,\mathcal{K}\rangle
be an

Proof

of

It is easy to see that

Proposition 3

Let
\langle X,\mathcal{K}\rangle
be an

and

and

and

and

is dually directed.

Conversely, suppose that the family
\{U\in\mathcal{K}\colon Z\subseteq U\}
is dually directed. Since
Z=\bigcap\{W\colon W\in\mathcal{J}\}
for some family

, then

and

is a subbasic saturated subset.

In the following result we see that order-ideals are in one to one correspondence with the subbasic saturated subsets.

Theorem 3.1

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a semilattice. Then the posets
\langle\mathrm{Id}(\mathbf{A}),\subseteq\rangle
and
\langle\mathcal{Z}(X(\mathbf{A})),\subseteq\rangle

Proof

Let

. We see that

is an order-ideal of
\mathbf{A}
and

. Moreover, if

.

It is clear that

is a downset of
\mathbf{A}
. Let

. So,
\beta(a)\cap Z=\emptyset
and
\beta(b)\cap Z=\emptyset
. Since

for some dually directed family

and

\{\beta(x)\colon\beta(x)^{c}\in\mathcal{L}\}

\beta(b)

The other inclusion is immediate.

. The other inclusion is straightforward.

Then we have a mapping

and

Lemma 2

Let

Theorem 3.2

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a semilattice. Then the pair
\langle E(X(\mathbf{A})),\beta\rangle
is a canonical extension of
\mathbf{A}
.

Proof

From Lemma
3
,
\beta(a)\in E(X(\mathbf{A}))
for all
a\in A
and
\beta
is an embedding.

In order to prove that
E(X(\mathbf{A}))
is dense, let

. Then there exists
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}(X(\mathbf{A}))
such that
V=\bigcap\{U^{c}\colon U\in\mathcal{B}\}
. From Theorem
3.1
, it follows that

U^{c}=\left(\bigcap\{\beta(a)^{c}\colon a\in I_{\mathbf{A}}(U)\}\right)^{c}=\bigcup\beta[I_{\mathbf{A}}(U)]=\bigvee\beta[I_{\mathbf{A}}(U)]

and

V=\bigcap\left\{\bigvee\beta[I_{\mathbf{A}}(U)]\colon U\in\mathcal{B}\right\}.

On the other hand, since

, it follows that
V=\bigcup\{\varphi(P)\colon P\in V\}
. It is easy to check that

V=\bigcup\{\varphi(P)\colon P\in V\}=\bigvee\{\varphi(P)\colon P\in V\}=\bigvee\left\{\bigcap\{\beta(P)\colon P\in V\}\right\}.

Finally, we prove that

is compact. Let

and

be such that

. So,

and
\beta(a)\in\beta[I]

As usual, we write

to denote the canonical extension of a semilattice

3.1
Connection with the construction of Gouveia and Priestley

Canonical extensions of semilattices have been studied in detail by Gouveia and Priestley in
GPsemilattices
. This work is connected with the topological construction of canonical extensions made by Moshier and Jipsen
MoshierJipsen
. Their approach places strong emphasis on the iterated filter completion and the canonical extension: they consider a semilattice
\mathbf{A}
and the lattice of filters of filters of
\mathbf{A}
, denoted by
\Fi^{2}(\mathbf{A})=\Fi(\Fi(\mathbf{A}))
. The family of sets
\Fi^{2}(\mathbf{A})
is an algebraic closure system and forms an algebraic lattice in which arbitrary meet and directed join are given, respectively, by intersection and directed union. The join of an arbitrary family of elements
\{F_{i}\colon i\in I\}
is given by

\displaystyle\bigcap\left\{\mathcal{G}\in\Fi^{2}(\mathbf{A})\colon\bigcup\{F_{i}\colon i\in I\}\subseteq\mathcal{G}\right\}.

In
GPsemilattices
, the authors define an order embedding
e\colon A\rightarrow\Fi^{2}(\mathbf{A})
given by
e(a)=\{F\in\Fi(\mathbf{A})\colon a\in F\}
for all
a\in A
and the pair
\langle\Fi^{2}(\mathbf{A}),e\rangle
is a completion of
\mathbf{A}
. Since
\Fi^{2}(\mathbf{A})
may not be a canonical extension of
\mathbf{A}
, to construct a canonical extension the authors consider the subset of
\Fi^{2}(\mathbf{A})
consisting of those of its elements which are meets of directed joins from
e(A)
, i.e.,

C=\left\{\bigcap\bigcup e(I_{j})\colon\{I_{j}\colon j\in J\}\subseteq\Id(\mathbf{A})\right\}.

Since the canonical extension is unique up to an isomorphism that fixes
\mathbf{A}
, there is an isomorphism between the canonical extension given in
GPsemilattices
and the one we have developed in this work from the topological duality introduced in
Celani2020
. We now proceed to the details. We know by Theorem
3.2
that
\langle E(X(\mathbf{A})),\beta\rangle
is a canonical extension for
\mathbf{A}
, where

E(X(\mathbf{A}))=\left\{\bigcap\{U^{c}\colon U\in\mathcal{B}\}\colon\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}(X(\mathbf{A}))\right\}

and
\beta\colon A\rightarrow E(X(\mathbf{A}))
is given by
\beta(a)=\{P\in X(\mathbf{A})\colon a\in P\}
. So, if we define the mapping
i\colon C\rightarrow E(X(\mathbf{A}))
by

i(X)=\{P\in X(\mathbf{A})\colon P\in X\}=X\cap X(\mathbf{A})

then it is easy to prove that
i(X)=\bigcap\{\alpha(I)^{c}\colon I\in\Id(\mathbf{A})\text{ and }X\subseteq\bigcup e(I)\}
and
i(e(a))=\beta(a)
. We get that
i
is an isomorphism of lattices that fixes
\mathbf{A}
with inverse
j\colon E(X(\mathbf{A}))\rightarrow C
given by

j(Y)=\bigcap\left\{\bigcup e(I)\colon I\in\Id(\mathbf{A})\text{ and }Y\subseteq\alpha(I)^{c}\right\}.

It is also worth noting that
i(\bigcup e(I))=\alpha(I)^{c}
for all
I\in\Id(\mathbf{A})
and
i(\bigcap e(F))=\varphi(F)=Y_{F}
for all
F\in\Fi(\mathbf{A})
.

Extension of monotone maps

In this section we study canonical extensions of monotone maps of semilattices by using Theorem

Let

\displaystyle=\bigvee\left\{\bigwedge\{f(p)\colon x\leq p\in P\}\colon u\geq x\in K(P^{\sigma})\right\},

(4)

\displaystyle f^{\pi}(u)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge\left\{\bigvee\{f(p)\colon y\geq p\in P\}\colon u\leq y\in O(P^{\sigma})\right\}.

(5)

For every order preserving map

\displaystyle=\bigwedge\{f^{\pi}(y)\colon u\leq y\in O(P^{\sigma})\}.

4
Topological duality

As we saw along Section
3
, the topological description of canonical extensions of semilattices can be used to obtain topological descriptions of the canonical extensions of monotone maps of semilattices, namely, the extensions
\pi
and
\sigma
. In this section we take advantage of these results in order to define a suitable category of multirelational topological spaces which will serve as the categorical dual of the category of monotone semilattices. The construction of such multirelations strongly relies in a modeling process raising from the
f^{\pi}
extension of a monotone function
f
between semilattices.

Definition 7

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a semilattice. A map
m\colon A\rightarrow A
is said to be
monotone
if it is an order preserving map, i.e, it satisfies the following condition: if
a\leq b
, then
ma\leq mb
for all
a\in A
. Then a
monotone semilattice
is a pair
\langle\mathbf{A},m\rangle
where
\mathbf{A}
is a semilattice and
m
is a monotone operation on
A
.

Remark 4

It is immediate that for every semilattice
\mathbf{A}
a map
m\colon A\rightarrow A
is monotone if and only if
m(a\wedge b)\leq ma\wedge mb
for all
a,b\in A
.

It is clear from Remark
4
that monotone semilattices form a variety. We write
\mathcal{MS}
for the variety of monotone semilattices. Let
\langle\mathbf{A},m\rangle
and
\langle\mathbf{B},n\rangle
be two monotone semilattices. We say that a homomorphism
h\colon A\rightarrow B
is a
monotone homomorphism
if
h(ma)=nh(a)
for all
a\in A
. We write
\mathsf{mMS}
for the algebraic category of monotone semilattices and monotone homomorphisms.

A
multirelation
on set
X
is a subset of the cartesian product
X\times\mathcal{P}(X)
, that is, a set of ordered pairs

5
Characterization of homomorphic images and congruences by means of lower-Vietoris-type topologies

In
CelaniCalomino
Celani and Calomino proved that homomorphic images of a distributive semilattice are characterized by one-to-one meet-relations and by lower Vietoris families on the dual space. This characterization was motivated by the results given in
Guram-Jansana II
and by the fact that the class of distributive semilattices is not a variety. Of course, this is not the case of semilattices and monotone semilattices. In this section we characterize congruences of semilattices and monotone semilattices by means of lower-Vietoris-type topologies and monotone lower-Vietoris-type topologies of their respective associated dual spaces. To achieve that goal, we start by studying the existing relation between homomorphic images and lower-Vietoris-type topologies in the variety of semilattices and later we extend this study to the variety of monotone semilattices.

Proof

We recall that in Corollary

is precisely the relation claimed in Corollary

Since

for all

if and only if

In this part we study the congruences of monotone semilattices. To achieve this goal, let

Theorem 5.11

Let
\langle\mathbf{A},m\rangle\in\mathcal{MS}

\langle X,\mathcal{K},R\rangle
be an
mS

be a one-to-one monotone meet-relation. Then

is a monotone lower-Vietoris-type family of
\langle X(\mathbf{A}),\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{A}},R_{m}\rangle

Proof

From Theorem 9 we have that
\langle\mathcal{F}_{T},\mathcal{M}\rangle

S

, we will prove that

and suppose that

is a monotone meet-relation we have

Lemma 17

Let
\langle\mathbf{A},m\rangle\in\mathcal{MS}
and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq C_{\mathcal{K}}(X(\mathbf{A}))
be a monotone lower-Vietoris-type family of the dual

-space of
\mathbf{A}

(Y,Z)\in R\Longleftrightarrow Z\in\bigcap\{L_{H_{a}^{c}}\colon a\in A\text{ and }\beta(a)^{c}\notin R_{m}[Y]\}.

is an
mS

Proof

We know that
\langle\mathcal{F},\mathcal{M}\rangle
is an
S
-space. Let
a\in A
. We will see that
m_{R}(H_{a}^{c})\in S(\mathcal{F})
. Since

m_{R}(H_{a}^{c})=\{Y\in\mathcal{F}\colon\forall Z\in R(Y)[Z\cap H_{a}^{c}\neq\emptyset]\},

we have
Y\notin m_{R}(H_{a}^{c})
if and only if there exists
Z\in R(Y)
such that
Z\notin L_{H_{a}^{c}}
. So, by definition of
R
we get
\beta(a)^{c}\in R_{m}[Y]
.

On the other hand, let
\beta(a)^{c}\in R_{m}[Y]
and suppose that
Y\in m_{R}(H_{a}^{c})
. We have
H_{a}\notin R(Y)
. So, there exists
b\in A
such that
\beta(b)^{c}\notin R_{m}[Y]
and
H_{a}\cap H_{b}^{c}=\emptyset
. Thus,
H_{a}\subseteq H_{b}
and since
R_{m}[Y]
is
\mathcal{M}
-increasing we get a contradiction. Therefore,
Y\notin m_{R}(H_{a}^{c})
if and only if
\beta(a)^{c}\in R_{m}[Y]
and it is easy to see that

if and only if

. As
m_{R_{m}}(\beta(a))^{c}=\beta(ma)^{c}
, we finally get that
m_{R}(H_{a}^{c})^{c}=H_{ma}
and then
m_{R}(H_{a}^{c})\in S(\mathcal{F})
. It is immediate to see that
R(Y)=\bigcap\{L_{H_{a}^{c}}\colon a\in A\text{ and }Y\in m_{R}(H_{a}^{c})\}
. So,
\langle\mathcal{F},\mathcal{M},R\rangle
is an
mS
-space.

We will see now that
R_{\mathcal{F}}
is a monotone one-to-one relation. Let
a\in A
. We only need to prove that

\beta(a)^{c}\in R_{m}[Y]\Longleftrightarrow R_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}[\beta(a)^{c}]\in R(Y).

Note that
R_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}[\beta(a)^{c}]=H_{a}

\beta(a)^{c}\in R_{m}[Y]\Longleftrightarrow Y\notin m_{R}(H_{a}^{c})\Longleftrightarrow H_{a}\in R(Y)

and the assertion follows.

5.1
Congruence lattices of semilattices and lattices of algebraic sets

In
Fajtlowicz
, Fajtlowicz and Schmidt developed a duality between congruences of semilattices and the lattices of algebraic sets. Unlike us, they worked with ideals instead of filters. By following the same line and applying our characterization of congruences of semilattices, we conclude this paper by showing that there exists a duality between congruences of semilattices and families of filters.

A subset
X
of a complete lattice
\mathbf{L}
is an
algebraic subset
if it contains
1
, it is closed under arbitrary meets, and it is closed under non-empty directed joins. Following the notation of
Hyndman
, we denote
{\rm{S}}_{p}({\mathbf{L}})
the lattice of all algebraic subsets of
\mathbf{L}
.

Definition 15

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a semilattice and
\theta
be a congruence on
A
. A filter
F
of
\mathbf{A}
is
\theta
-closed
if
(x,y)\in\theta
and
y\in F

.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a semilattice and let
\{F_{i}\colon i\in I\}
be a family of filters of
\mathbf{A}

\Fi(\mathbf{A})
is a complete lattice whose operations are given by

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\bigvee\{F_{i}\colon i\in I\}=F\left(\bigcup\{F_{i}\colon i\in I\}\right)&\text{ and }&\displaystyle\bigwedge\{F_{i}\colon i\in I\}=\bigcap\{F_{i}\colon i\in I\}.\end{array}

Theorem 5.17

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a semilattice. Then the lattice of congruences of
\mathbf{A}
is dually isomorphic to
{\rm{S}}_{p}(\Fi(\mathbf{A}))
.

Proof

We write
{\rm{Con}}(\mathbf{A})
the lattice of all congruences of
\mathbf{A}
. We define the map
\sigma\colon{\rm{Con}}(\mathbf{A})\rightarrow{\rm{S}}_{p}(\Fi(\mathbf{A}))
by setting

\sigma(\theta)
as the set of all
\theta
-closed filters of
\mathbf{A}
. Now, let us consider the map
\rho\colon{\rm{S}}_{p}(\Fi(\mathbf{A}))\rightarrow{\rm{Con}}(\mathbf{A})
defined by

\rho(U)=\{(x,y)\in A^{2}\colon\forall F\in U(x\in F\Longleftrightarrow y\in F)\}.

It is straightforward to check that
\sigma
and
\rho
are order-reversing,
\sigma(\theta)\in{\rm{S}}_{p}(\Fi(\mathbf{A}))
and
\rho(U)\in{\rm{Con}}(\mathbf{A})
. In order to show that
\theta=\rho\sigma(\theta)
, note that if
y<x
and
(x,y)\notin\theta
, then
\{z\in A\colon(x\wedge z,x)\in\theta\}
is a
\theta
-closed filter containing
x
and but not
y
. Hence
(x,y)\notin\rho\sigma(\theta)
. To see that
U=\sigma\rho(U)
, consider a filter
F\notin U
. For each
x\in A
, let
F_{x}=\bigcap\{F\in U\colon x\in F\}
. So,
F_{x}\in U
. Then
\{F_{x}\colon x\in F\}
is directed, whence
\bigcup\{F_{x}\colon x\in F\}\in U
. Thus, the latter union properly contains
F
. Then there exist
y<x
with
x\in F
,
y\in F_{x}
and
y\notin F
, i.e.,
F
is not
\rho(U)
-closed. Therefore,
F\notin U
implies
F\notin\sigma\rho(U)
. This concludes the proof.

Let
\mathbf{A}

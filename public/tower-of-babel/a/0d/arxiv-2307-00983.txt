Stochastic Recursive Optimal Control of McKean-Vlasov Type: A Viscosity Solution Approach
Liangquan Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we study a kind of optimal control problem for forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs for short) of McKean–Vlasov type via the dynamic programming principle (DPP for short) motivated by studying the infinite dimensional Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB for short) equation derived from the decoupling field of the FBSDEs posed by Carmona and Delarue ( Ann Probab , 2015, [ 30 ] ). At the beginning, the value function is defined by the solution to the controlled BSDE alluded to earlier. On one hand, we can prove the value function is deterministic function with respect to the initial random variable; On the other hand, we can show that the value function is law-invariant , i.e., depending on only via its distribution by virtue of BSDE property. Afterward, utilizing the notion of differentiability with respect to probability measures introduced by P.L. Lions [ 53 ] , we are able to establish a DPP for the value function in the Wasserstein space of probability measures based on the application of BSDE approach, particularly, employing the notion of stochastic backward semigroups associated with stochastic optimal control problems and Itô formula along a flow property of the conditional law of the controlled forward state process. We prove that the value function is the unique viscosity solutions of the associated generalized HJB equations in some separable Hilbert space. Finally, as an application, we formulate an optimal control problem for linear stochastic differential equations with quadratic cost functionals of McKean-Vlasov type under nonlinear expectation, g g -expectation introduced by Peng [ 46 ] and derive the optimal feedback control explicitly by means of several groups of Riccati equations.

5
Concluding remark

We analyze the optimal control problem, where the state processes are
governed by FBSDEs of McKean-Vlasov type. The value function
\mathcal{V}

defined by the solution to the backward component of controlled FBSDEs can
be proved a nonlinear extended Feynman-Kac representation in terms of a
class of FBSDEs. We explore the probabilistic representation rigorously to
prove the DPP for value function via BSDE method. As we have well known, the
DPP is playing a crucial part in deriving a characterization of the value
function as a solution of a nonlinear partial differential equation (HJB
equation) on the Wasserstein space of measures basing on the law-invariant
of
\mathcal{V}.
It is necessary to point out that the usual way of solving
these equations is employing the Pontryagin maximum principle, which
requires some smoothness of coefficients and convexity assumptions due to
the requirement of variation equality and adjoint equations. We should point
it out that the classical object functional for forward optimal control of
McKean-Vlasov type is spelled out by the expectation to the running function

f
and terminal cost
\Phi
w.r.t. state and its distribution, which
undoubtedly is deterministic no matter of the selection of initial
condition. Besides, notice that in such a case, from view point of HJB
equation,
f
can be only depend on state variable, badly off for the value
function
\mathcal{V}
and its gradient. In this paper, we prove the
functional is deterministic by virtue of BSDE’s property and the viscosity
property together with a uniqueness result for the value function. The LQ
problem of McKean-Vlasov type under nonlinear expectation (
g
-expectation)
has been attacked as a theoretic result. Nevertheless, there are still some
key points should be stated: 1) We obtain the uniqueness of viscosity
solution via comparison principles in the separable Hilbert space. It is
interesting to consider this problem in the Wasserstein space, or more
generally in metric spaces (see
[
34
]
). 2) The Wiener process
W

accounts for the common random environment in literature of mean field games
in which all the individuals evolve, often called common noise (cf.
[
27
]
). Therefore, it is quite natural to bring the idiosyncratic noises,
independent of
W
, into the systems. Without doubt, it makes the analysis
rather complicated.

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}+\mathcal{X}_{2}-\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)\right]-\varepsilon.

The proof ends from letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

\Box

6.5
Proof of Lemma
2.35

Proof.

The idea is similar to Lemma
2.20
. From Lemma
2.32
, for any
given
\varepsilon>0,
there exists
u^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}^{2}\left[0,T\right]
such that

\mathbb{G}_{t,t+\delta}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}}\left[\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\rho_{t+\delta}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}}\right)\right]\geq\mathcal{V}\left(t,\mu\right)\geq\mathbb{G}_{t,t+\delta}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}}\left[\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\rho_{t+\delta}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}}\right)\right]-\varepsilon.

(6.12)

In the following, we prove the following inequalities with the help of the
above first (second) inequality:

\mathcal{V}\left(t,\mu\right)-\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\mu\right)\geq-\tilde{C}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{ (res., }\leq\tilde{C}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{).}

(6.13)

We prove the first inequality of (
6.13
), the other one is analogous.
It follows from (
6.12
) that, for an arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0,

\mathcal{V}\left(t,\mu\right)-\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\mu\right)\geq\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}-\varepsilon,

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{t,\tau}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}}\left[\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\rho_{t+\delta}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}}\right)\right]-\mathbb{G}_{t,\tau}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}}\left[\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\mu\right)\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{t,\tau}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}}\left[\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\mu\right)\right]-\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\mu\right).

From Lemma
2.31
and
2.13
, we get that, for some constant
C

independent of
u^{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{I}_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\rho_{t+\delta}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}}\right)-\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\mu\right)\right|^{2}\right]\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}\left(\rho_{t+\delta}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}},\mu\right)\right]\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

From
[
4
]
, applying Proposition 1.1 to càdlàg process
X^{t,\xi,u^{\varepsilon}}
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X_{t+\delta}^{t,\xi,u^{\varepsilon}}-\xi\right|^{2}\right]\leq C\delta\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)\right].

Meanwhile, from the definition of
\mathcal{W}_{2},
it yields

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}\left(\rho_{t+\delta}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}},\mu\right)\right]\leq C\delta\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)\right].

Now taking into account the arbitrariness of
\xi\in L^{2}\left(\mathcal{G}_{t};\mathbb{R}^{n}\right),
we get

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}\left(\rho_{t+\delta}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}},\mu\right)\right]\leq C\delta\left(1+\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}\left(\mu,\delta_{0}\right)\right),

from which we immediately derive that

\mathcal{I}_{1}\geq-C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1+\mathcal{W}_{2}\left(\mu,\delta_{0}\right)\right).

(6.14)

We now proceed the second term
\mathcal{I}_{2}.
According to the
definition of
\mathbb{G}_{t,t+\delta}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}}\left[\mathcal{\cdot}\right],
we deduce that
\mathcal{I}_{2}
can be expressed
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{t,t+\delta}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}}\left[\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\mu\right)\right]-\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\mu\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{0}}\Bigg[\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\mu\right)+\int_{t}^{t+\delta}f^{0}\left(\rho_{s}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}},\tilde{\Theta}_{s}^{t,\mu,u^{\varepsilon}},\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{\Theta}_{s}^{t,\mu,u^{\varepsilon}}},u_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\tilde{Z}_{s}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{d}W_{s}\Bigg]-\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\mu\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{0}}\Bigg[\int_{t}^{t+\delta}f^{0}\left(\rho_{s}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}},\tilde{\Theta}_{s}^{t,\mu,u^{\varepsilon}},\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{\Theta}_{s}^{t,\mu,u^{\varepsilon}}},u_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right)\mathrm{d}s\Bigg],

where
\left(\tilde{Y}_{s}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}},\tilde{Z}_{s}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}}\right)_{t\leq s\leq t+\delta}
is the solution of BSDE (
2.21
) with the terminal condition
\eta=\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\mu\right).
Employing the classical method (Schwartz inequality), from
Lemma
2.13
, we have that, for certain constant
C
independent of
t
and
\delta,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{I}_{2}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{0}}\Bigg[\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\left|f^{0}\left(\rho_{s}^{t,\mu;u^{\varepsilon}},\tilde{\Theta}_{s}^{t,\xi,u^{\varepsilon}},\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{\Theta}_{s}^{t,\xi,u^{\varepsilon}}},u_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right)\right|\mathrm{d}s\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Consequently, combining (
6.14
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}\left(t,\mu\right)-\mathcal{V}\left(t+\delta,\mu\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}-\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1+\mathcal{W}_{2}\left(\mu,\delta_{0}\right)\right)-\varepsilon.

The desired result follows from letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.
The
proof is then complete.

6.6
Proof of Lemma
3.7

Proof.

From
[
4
]
, applying Proposition 1.1 to càdlàg process
X^{t,\xi,u}
, we have, for
p\geq 2
the existence of constant
C_{p}>0

such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq s\leq t+\delta}\left|X^{t,\xi,u}-\xi\right|^{p}\right]\leq C_{p}\delta\left(1+\left|\xi\right|^{p}\right),\text{ uniformly in }u\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}^{2}\left[0,T\right].

Therefore, whenever
\delta\downarrow 0,
the random variable
\kappa^{\delta}=\sup_{t\leq s\leq t+\delta}\left|X^{t,\xi,u}-\xi\right|
converges monotone
0.
Denote
\widetilde{\mathscr{Y}}_{s}^{u}=\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{1,u}-\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{2,u}
and
\widetilde{\mathscr{Z}}_{s}^{u}=\mathscr{Z}_{s}^{1,u}-\mathscr{Z}_{s}^{2,u}
for
t\leq s\leq t+\delta.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mathscr{Y}}_{s}^{u}&=&\Big[F\left(s,\mathscr{X}_{s}^{t,\xi;u},\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{1,u},\mathscr{Z}_{s}^{1,u},u_{s}\right)-F\left(s,\mathscr{X}_{s}^{t,\xi;u},\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{2,u},\mathscr{Z}_{s}^{2,u},u_{s}\right)\\
&&-F\left(s,\xi,\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{2,u},\mathscr{Z}_{s}^{2,u},u_{s}\right)+F\left(s,\mathscr{X}_{s}^{t,\xi;u},\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{2,u},\mathscr{Z}_{s}^{2,u},u_{s}\right)\Big]\mathrm{d}s\\
&&-\widetilde{\mathscr{Z}}_{s}^{u}\mathrm{d}W_{s},\text{ }\\
\widetilde{\mathscr{Y}}_{t+\delta}^{u}&=&0.\end{array}\right.

(6.15)

Applying Itô’s formula to
\left|\widetilde{\mathscr{Y}}_{s}^{u}\right|^{2}e^{\beta\left(s-t\right)}
on the interval
\left[t,T\right],
we get the following estimate with same technique used in
Lemma
2.20

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\left(\left|\widetilde{\mathscr{Y}}_{s}^{u}\right|^{2}+\left|\widetilde{\mathscr{Z}}_{s}^{u}\right|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\rho_{0}\left(\left|\mathscr{X}_{s}^{t,\xi;u}-\xi\right|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\delta\rho_{0}\left(\kappa^{\delta}\right)^{2}.

For some
\rho_{0}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\rho_{0}\left(\varepsilon\right)\downarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0.
On the
other hand, from Lemma
2.17
, we know that
\mathscr{Y}_{t}^{1,u}
and
\mathscr{Y}_{t}^{2,u}
are deterministic whenever
u\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}^{2}\left[0,T\right]
is
\mathcal{F}_{s}^{t}
-adapted. It yields

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{\mathscr{Y}}_{t}^{u}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\mathscr{Y}}_{t}^{u}\right]\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg|\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{t}^{t+\delta}F\left(s,\mathscr{X}_{s}^{t,\xi;u},\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{1,u},\mathscr{Z}_{s}^{1,u},u_{s}\right)-F\left(s,\xi,\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{2,u},\mathscr{Z}_{s}^{2,u},u_{s}\right)\mathrm{d}s\Bigg]\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\Big[\rho_{0}\left(\left|\mathscr{X}_{s}^{t,\xi;u}-\xi\right|\right)+\left|\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{1,u}-\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{2,u}\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\mathscr{Z}_{s}^{1,u}-\mathscr{Z}_{s}^{2,u}\right|+\mathcal{W}_{2}\left(\mathbb{P}_{\left(\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{1,u},\mathscr{Z}_{s}^{1,u}\right)},\mathbb{P}_{\left(\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{2,u},\mathscr{Z}_{s}^{2,u}\right)}\right)\Big]\mathrm{d}s\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\delta\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(\kappa^{\delta}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg\{\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\left(\left|\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{1,u}-\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{2,u}\right|^{2}+\left|\mathscr{Z}_{s}^{1,u}-\mathscr{Z}_{s}^{2,u}\right|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}s\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}},

which implies that

\left|\widetilde{\mathscr{Y}}_{t}^{u}\right|\leq C\delta\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(\kappa^{\delta}\right)\right]+\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}^{2}\left(\kappa^{\delta}\right)\right]\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}.

Observe that, for each
\delta>0,

\rho_{0}\left(\kappa^{\delta}\right)
is square integrable. Thus we set
\rho_{1}\left(\delta\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(\kappa^{\delta}\right)\right]+\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}^{2}\left(\kappa^{\delta}\right)\right]\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.
For the general
u\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}^{2}\left[0,T\right]
, since
\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{1,u}
and
\mathscr{Y}_{s}^{2,u}
are continuous
in
u,
the proof is completed via the same technique employed before.

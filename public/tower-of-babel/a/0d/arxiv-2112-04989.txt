The geometry of one-weight codes in the sum-rank metric
Alessandro Neri, Paolo Santonastaso, Ferdinando Zullo

Abstract
. We provide a geometric characterization of k k -dimensional 𝔽 q m {\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}} -linear sum-rank metric codes as tuples of 𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} -subspaces of 𝔽 q m k {\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k} . We then use this characterization to study one-weight codes in the sum-rank metric. This leads us to extend the family of linearized Reed-Solomon codes in order to obtain a doubly-extended version of them. We prove that these codes are still maximum sum-rank distance (MSRD) codes and, when k = 2 k=2 , they are one-weight, as in the Hamming-metric case. We then focus on constant rank-profile codes in the sum-rank metric, which are a special family of one weight-codes, and derive constraints on their parameters with the aid of an associated Hamming-metric code. Furthermore, we introduce the n n -simplex codes in the sum-rank metric, which are obtained as the orbit of a Singer subgroup of GL ⁡ ( k , q m ) \GL(k,q^{m}) . They turn out to be constant rank-profile – and hence one-weight – and generalize the simplex codes in both the Hamming and the rank metric. Finally, we focus on 2 2 -dimensional one-weight codes, deriving constraints on the parameters of those which are also MSRD, and we find a new construction of one-weight MSRD codes when q = 2 q=2 .

1.
Introduction

Codes endowed with the sum-rank metric have been demonstrated to be useful in several contexts. Their notion can be traced back to
[
18
,
24
]
, where they have been linked to space-time coding. However, they became popular when they were used for improving the performance of multishot network coding built on rank-metric codes
[
32
]
. Later, sum-rank-metric codes have also been used in distributed storage,
playing a crucial role in some special constructions of partial MDS codes
[
28
,
27
]
.

In the last few years, a deep mathematical theory of sum-rank-metric codes was developed in a series of papers by Martínez-Peñas
[
29
,
26
,
30
]
. These codes can be seen as a generalization of Hamming-metric codes and rank-metric codes. Vectors are first partitioned in blocks of (possibly) variable lengths, then on each block one considers the rank-metric and sums up the distances of each block. Considering only one block corresponds to the rank metric setting, while choosing all the blocks to have length one is equivalent to equip the ambient space with the Hamming metric.

On the other hand, the theory of error-correcting codes has often been influenced by finite geometry, and the interplay between these two subjects has emerged since the late 50’s. Among their connections, it is well-known that linear codes in the Hamming metric correspond to
projective systems
, that are sets of points in a finite projective space. This correspondence translates metric properties of a linear code into geometric properties of the associated projective system; see
[
42
, Section 1.1]
. This geometric viewpoint is very useful for the classification of one-weight codes shown by Bonisoli in
[
6
]
.
More recently a similar geometric approach was used for codes in the rank metric. A first intuition was given in
[
40
]
, while a full correspondence was provided in
[
41
]
and in
[
38
]
:
k
-dimensional rank-metric codes over an extension field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
correspond to
q
-systems
, which are special
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. This correspondence allowed us to give a complete characterization of nondegenerate
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear one-weight codes in the rank metric; see
[
38
, Theorem 12]
,
[
1
, Proposition 3.16]
.

Our contribution.
Motivated by these results, in this paper we give a complete geometric characterization of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear sum-rank metric codes based on Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.7
, and then we use this geometric point of view to study one-weight codes in the sum-rank metric. In contrast to what happens for the Hamming and the rank metric, in which there is essentially only one nondegenerate one-weight code, we show that there are many different one-weight codes in the sum-rank metric.
We first extend linearized Reed-Solomon codes to
doubly-extended linearized Reed-Solomon codes
, which we prove to be maximum sum-rank distance codes in Theorem
4.6
. Furthermore, in Theorem
4.9
we show that the two-dimensional ones are one-weight, as it happens for the Hamming-metric case.
We then restrict to study constant rank-profile codes, which are sum-rank metric codes such that for every nonzero block-codeword
c=(c_{1}\mid\ldots\mid c_{t})
, the multiset
\{\{\rk_{q}(c_{1}),\ldots,\rk_{q}(c_{t})\}\}
is always the same. We associate a Hamming-metric code to a sum-rank metric code exploiting the geometric correspondence, and use such a code for deriving constraints on constant rank-profile codes; see Corollary
5.8
.
In Theorem
6.2
we provide an orbital construction of constant rank-profile codes, using the action of a transitive subgroup
\mathcal{G}
of
\GL(k,q^{m})
. This leads to the notion of
n
-simplex code
in the sum-rank metric given in Definition
6.6
. We observe then that this code specializes to the simplex code in the Hamming metric when
n=1
, and to the simplex code in the rank metric whenever the action of
\mathcal{G}
is trivial; see Remark
6.11
. Finally, we investigate
2
-dimensional one-weight codes which are also MSRD, introducing the notions of
multi-linear sets
and
scattered multi-linear sets
. First, we prove that these codes must have at least
q+1
blocks. Then, in Theorem
7.4
we show that for
q\geq 3
, whenever
2
-dimensional one-weight MSRD codes with exactly
q+1
blocks exist, they must have the same block lengths of the doubly-extended linearized Reed-Solomon. For
q=2
in Theorem
7.5
we find a special family of one-weight MSRD codes, which we call
2
-fold linearized Reed-Solomon codes
, having different block-lengths. We conclude providing additional constructions of
2
-dimensional one-weight codes, using the
lifting construction
.

Outline.
The paper is structured as follows.
Section
2
contains the basic notions on sum-rank metric codes.
In Section
3
we derive the geometric characterization of sum-rank metric codes in terms of multiple
q
-systems.
Section
4
is dedicated to the study of doubly-extended linearized Reed-Solomon codes. We then associate a sum-rank metric code with a special Hamming-metric code in Section
5
, and derive constraints on the parameters of one-weight codes.
In Section
6
we provide an orbital construction of one-weight sum-rank metric codes using transitive groups, and give the counterpart of simplex codes in the sum-rank metric.
In Section
7
we focus on
2
-dimensional one-weight codes, characterizing the block structure of those that are also MSRD.
Finally, we draw our conclusions and we describe some natural research questions in Section
8
.

Notation.
We fix now the notation that we will use for the whole paper. For us
q
is a prime power and
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is the finite field with
q
elements. We will often consider the degree
m
extension field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
Let
t
be a positive integer. From now on

\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}),\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{t})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{t}
will always be ordered tuples with
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\ldots\geq n_{t}
and
m_{1}\geq m_{2},\geq\ldots\geq m_{t}
, and we set
N\coloneqq n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}
. We use the following compact notations for the direct sum of vector spaces

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\mathbf{n}}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{i}},\qquad{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}},

and for the direct sum of matrix spaces

\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{i}\times m_{i}}.

Furthermore, we follow the notation used in
[
2
, Section 3.3]
. For a vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{r}
, we define

\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{a}}=\mathcal{S}_{a_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{S}_{a_{r}},

where
\mathcal{S}_{i}
is the symmetric group of order
i
. Similarly, we denote by
\GL(\mathbf{a},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
the direct product of the general linear groups of degree
a_{i}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, i.e.

\GL(\mathbf{a},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})=\GL(a_{1},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})\times\ldots\times\GL(a_{r},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}).

Moreover, we denote by
\lambda(\mathbf{n})=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{s})
the vector of positive integers which counts the occurrences of the distinct entries of
\mathbf{n}
. Formally, let
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{n})\coloneqq\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\}=\{n_{i_{1}},\ldots,n_{i_{s}}\}
, with
n_{i_{1}}>\ldots>n_{i_{s}}
and
s\coloneqq|\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{n})|
. Then

\lambda_{j}\coloneqq|\{k\,:\,n_{k}=n_{i_{j}}\}|
for each
j\in[s]\coloneqq\{1,\ldots,s\}
.

Finally, whenever we will talk about duality in this paper, we will consider the standard inner product on the ambient spaces, and denote the
dual
of a space
V
with respect to this bilinear form by
V^{\perp}
.

2.
Sum-rank-metric codes, supports and isometries

2.1.
Matrix codes

In this section we recall the basic notions of sum-rank-metric codes seen as elements in
\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, that will be useful for the rest of the paper. The interested reader is referred to
[
9
,
8
,
10
]
for a more detailed description of this setting.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X\coloneqq(X_{1},\dots,X_{t})\in\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.
The
sum-rank support
of
X
is defined as the space

\supp(X)\coloneqq(\colsp(X_{1}),\colsp(X_{2}),\ldots,\colsp(X_{t}))\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\mathbf{n}},

where
\colsp(X_{i})
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
X_{i}
.
The
rank-list
of
X
is defined as

\rho(X)\coloneqq(\dim(\colsp(X_{1})),\dots,\dim(\colsp(X_{t})))\in\mathbb{N}^{t}.

The
rank-profile
of
X
is the
t
-uple obtained from
\rho(X)
by rearranging its entries in non-increasing order and it is denoted by
\mu(X)
. Finally, the
sum-rank weight
of
X
is the quantity

\ww_{\mathrm{srk}}(X)\coloneqq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\supp(X))=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\rk(X_{i}).

With these definition in mind, we can endow the space
\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
with a distance function, called the
sum-rank distance
,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{srk}}:\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})\times\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}

defined by

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{srk}}(X,Y)\coloneqq\ww_{\mathrm{srk}}(X-Y).

Definition 2.2
.

A
(matrix) sum-rank metric code

\mathcal{C}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear subspace of
\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
endowed with the sum-rank distance.
The
minimum sum-rank distance
of a sum-rank code
\mathcal{C}
is defined as usual via

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{srk}}(\mathcal{C})\coloneqq\min\{\ww_{\mathrm{srk}}(X)\,:\,X\in\mathcal{C},X\neq\mathbf{0}\}.

The
sum-rank support
of the code
\mathcal{C}
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the supports of all the codewords of
\C
, that is

\supp(\C)\coloneqq\sum_{X\in\C}\supp(X)\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\mathbf{n}}.

Finally, we say that
\mathcal{C}
is
sum-rank nondegenerate
if
\supp(\mathcal{C})={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\mathbf{n}}
.

2.2.
Vector codes

From now on, we consider the case in which
m=m_{1}=\cdots=m_{t}
.
We recall a slightly different notion of sum-rank metric code, in which the codewords are vectors with entries from an extension field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
rather than matrices over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. The interested reader is referred to
[
29
,
28
,
26
,
31
,
34
]
for a more detailed description of this setting.

The
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-rank
of a vector
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-dimension of the
vector space generated over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
by its entries, i.e,

\rk_{q}(v)\coloneqq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}.

Let
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, with
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
for any
i
. The
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-sum-rank weight of
x
is defined as the quantity

\ww(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\rk_{q}(x_{i}).

Consequently, the

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-sum-rank distance on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, is

\dd(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\rk_{q}(x_{i}-y_{i}),

for any
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}),y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, with
x_{i},y_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
.

Observe that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
is naturally isomorphic to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{N}
by concatenating the defining vectors. However, in order to define the sum-rank weight and sum-rank distance directly on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{N}
we would need to specify with respect to which partition of
N
we are computing them. For this reason we consider
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
as ambient space, so that the partition is already specified. However, in some situation we will use this natural representation of the elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
as vectors of length
N
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
.
Thus, from now on, we will simply write “sum-rank weight” and “sum-rank distance” when there is no risk of ambiguity on the base field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and on the integer partition of
N
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
k
be a positive integer with
1\leq k\leq N
. A
(vector) sum-rank metric code

\C
is a
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
endowed with the sum-rank metric.
The
minimum sum-rank distance
of
\C
is the integer

\dd(\C)=\min\{\dd(x,y)\,:\,x,y\in\C,x\neq y\}=\min\{\ww(x)\,:\,x\in\C,x\neq 0\}.

For brevity, from now on we will write that
\C
is an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code if
k
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-dimension of
\C
and
d
is its minimum distance, or simply an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code if the minimum distance is not relevant/known.

Since the elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
can also be seen as long vectors in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{N}
, every
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code can be provided with generator and parity-check matrices. Each of them, is naturally partitioned as
G=(G_{1}\mid\ldots\mid G_{t})
, where
G_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n_{i}}
(respectively
H=(H_{1}\mid\ldots\mid H_{t})
, where
H_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{(N-k)\times n_{i}}
).

For vector sum-rank metric codes, a Singleton-like bound holds.

Theorem 2.4

(see
[
29
, Proposition 16]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Then

d\leq N-k+1.

An
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code is called a
Maximum Sum-Rank Distance code
(or shortly
MSRD code
) if
d=N-k+1
.

The next result establishes the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometries of the space
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
endowed with the sum-rank distance, and it has been proved in
[
2
, Theorem 3.7]
. The case
\mathbf{n}=(n,\ldots,n)
was already proved in
[
30
, Theorem 2]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

The group of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometries of the space
({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}},\dd)
is

(({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\ast})^{t}\times\GL(\mathbf{n},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}))\rtimes\mathcal{S}_{\lambda(\mathbf{n})},

which (right)-acts as

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\cdot(\mathbf{a},A_{1},\ldots,A_{t},\pi)\longmapsto(a_{1}x_{\pi(1)}A_{1}\mid\ldots\mid a_{t}x_{\pi(t)}A_{t}).

From now on, when the vector
\mathbf{a}=(1,\ldots,1)
, the action of
(\mathbf{a},A,\pi)
on
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, with
A=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{t})\in\GL(\mathbf{n},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, will be simply denoted by
x\cdot(A,\pi)
, and if in addition
\pi=\mathrm{id}
, we will simply write

x\cdot A
. The same notation is then extended naturally to subsets/subspaces
\mathcal{V}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\mathbf{n}}
, as
\mathcal{V}\cdot(A,\pi)
and
\mathcal{V}\cdot A
.

Since in this setting MacWilliams’s extension theorem does not hold, see e.g.
[
4
]
, we will use the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometries of the whole ambient space in order to define the equivalence of sum-rank metric codes.

Definition 2.6
.

We say that two
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
sum-rank metric codes
{\mathcal{C}}_{1},{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
are
equivalent
if there is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometry
\phi
, such that
\phi({\mathcal{C}}_{1})={\mathcal{C}}_{2}
.

We denote the set of equivalence classes of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
sum-rank metric codes by
\mathfrak{C}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
.

2.3.
Supports and degeneracy of sum-rank metric codes

It is known that to a vector sum-rank metric code we can associate a matrix sum-rank metric code with the same parameters and metric properties in the following way.

For every
r\in[t]
, let
\Gamma_{r}=(\gamma_{1}^{(r)},\ldots,\gamma_{m}^{(r)})
be an ordered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, and let
\Gamma\coloneqq(\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{t})
. Given
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, with
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{i}}
, define the element
\Gamma(x)=(\Gamma_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,\Gamma_{t}(x_{t}))\in\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, where
\mathbf{m}=(m,\ldots,m)
and

x_{r,i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma_{r}(x_{r})_{ij}\gamma_{j}^{(r)},\qquad\mbox{ for all }i\in[n_{r}].

In other words, the
r
-th block of
\Gamma(x)
is the matrix expansion of the vector
x_{r}
with respect to the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis
\Gamma_{r}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
.

The following result is straightforward.

Theorem 2.7
.

For every tuple ordered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{t})
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, the map

\Gamma:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}\longrightarrow\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})

is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear isometry between the metric spaces
({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}},\dd)
and
(\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}),\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{srk}})
.

Definition 2.8
.

The
matrix sum-rank metric code associated
to an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code
\C

with respect to
\Gamma\coloneqq(\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{t})
is

\Gamma(\C)\coloneqq\{\Gamma(x)\,:\,x\in\C\}\subseteq\Mat(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}).

By Theorem
2.7
, all the metric properties of an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code
\C
are preserved via the isometry
\Gamma
, and can be found in the associated matrix code
\Gamma(\C)
. However, one has to be careful, because some of the properties might depend on the choice of the
t
-uple of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-bases
\Gamma
.
It is well-known that for the rank-metric case the notion of support does not depend on the choice of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis
\Lambda
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
; see e.g.
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
. Thus, it is not surprising that the same happens for the sum-rank support of an element
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
. In other words, we have the following result, whose proof is left to the reader.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\C
be an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code, let
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{t}),\Lambda=(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{t})
be two tuples of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-bases of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and let
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
. Then

(1)

\supp(\Gamma(x))=\supp(\Lambda(x))
,

(2)

\rho(\Gamma(x))=\rho(\Lambda(x))
, and

(3)

\mu(\Gamma(x))=\mu(\Lambda(x))
.

Hence, we can give the following definitions of sum-rank support, rank-list and rank-profile for elements in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
. For a deeper understanding of these notions we refer the reader to
[
26
]
.

Definition 2.10
.

The
sum-rank support
of an element
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
is the tuple

\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(x)\coloneqq\supp(\Gamma(x)),

for any (and hence all) choice of
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{t})
, where
\Gamma_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
for each
i\in[t]
.
The
rank-list
of
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})
is the
t
-uple
\rho(x)=(\rk_{q}(x_{1}),\ldots,\rk_{q}(x_{t}))
. The
rank-profile
of
x
is the
t
-uple obtained from
\rho(x)
by rearranging the entries in non-increasing order and it is denoted by
\mu(x)
.

In the same way, we can extend the notions of sum-rank support and degeneracy for an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code.

Definition 2.11
.

We define the
sum-rank support of an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code

\C
as the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the supports of the codewords of
\C
and we denote it by
\supp(\C)
, i.e.

\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(\C)\coloneqq\sum_{c\in\C}\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(c).

Furthermore, we say that
\C
is
sum-rank nondegenerate
if
\supp(\C)={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\mathbf{n}}
. We say that
\C
is
sum-rank degenerate
if it is not sum-rank nondegenerate.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, and let
A=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{t})\in\GL(\mathbf{n},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. Then

\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(x\cdot A)=\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(x)\cdot A.

In particular, if
\C_{1}
and
\C_{2}
are two equivalent
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
codes such that

\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(\C_{1})=(\mathcal{W}_{1},\ldots\mathcal{W}_{t}),\qquad\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(\C_{2})=(\mathcal{Z}_{1},\ldots\mathcal{Z}_{t}),

then there exists
\pi\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda(\mathbf{n})}
and
A\in\GL(\mathbf{n},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
such that

Z_{i}=A_{i}^{\top}\cdot\mathcal{W}_{\pi(i)},
for each
i\in[t]
. In other words, we have

\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(\C_{2})=\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(\C_{1})\cdot(A,\pi)
.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
\C
be a
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\C
is sum-rank nondegenerate.

(2)

For every
A=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{t})\in\GL(\mathbf{n},q)
the code
\C\cdot A
is Hamming-nondegenerate.

(3)

For any generator matrix
G=(G_{1}\,|\,\ldots\,|\,G_{t})
with
G_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n_{i}}
for
i\in[t]
, the columns of each
G_{i}
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear independent.

(4)

\dd(\C^{\perp})>1
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
:
Assume that there exists
A=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{t})\in\GL(\mathbf{n},q)
the code
\C\cdot A
is Hamming-degenerate. This is equivalent to say that one entry (say the last one) of
\C\cdot A
is identically zero. This implies that

\supp({\mathcal{C}}\cdot A)\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{1}}\oplus\ldots\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{t-1}}\oplus{\mathcal{V}},

where
{\mathcal{V}}=\{v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{t}}\,:\,v_{n_{t}}=0\}
.
Hence, by Proposition
2.12
we have

\supp({\mathcal{C}})\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{1}}\oplus\ldots\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{t-1}}\oplus(A_{t}^{\top})^{-1}\cdot{\mathcal{V}}\subsetneq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\mathbf{n}},

meaning that
\C
is sum-rank degenerate.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
:
Suppose that there exists an
i\in[t]
such that the columns of
G_{i}
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly dependent. Then, there exists
A_{i}\in\GL(n_{i},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
such that
G_{i}A_{i}
has a zero column. Thus, for any other choices of
A_{r}\in\GL(n_{r},{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
for
r\neq i
, we have that the code
\C\cdot A
is Hamming-degenerate.

(3)\Rightarrow(4)
:
Suppose that there exists a codeword
c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{t})\in\C^{\perp}
such that
\ww(c)=1
. This means that there exists
i\in[t]
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
and
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n_{i}})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{i}}\setminus\{0\}
such that
c_{j}=0
for all
j\neq i
and
c_{i}=\alpha(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n_{i}})
. Hence,
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n_{i}})
provides a nontrivial
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear dependence among the columns of
G_{i}
, for any
G=(G_{1}\,|\,\ldots\,|\,G_{t})
generator matrix for
\C
.

(4)\Rightarrow(1)
:
Assume by contradiction that
\C
is sum-rank degenerate. Then, up to sum-rank isometry, we can say that there exists
i\in[t]
such that

\supp({\mathcal{C}})\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{1}}\oplus\ldots\oplus{\mathcal{V}}\oplus\ldots\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{t}},

where
{\mathcal{V}}\subseteq\{v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{i}}\,:\,v_{n_{i}}=0\}
. This implies that
(0,\ldots,e_{n_{i}},\ldots 0)\in\C^{\perp}
, where
e_{n_{i}}
denotes the
n_{i}
-th standard basis vector. Hence
\dd(\C^{\perp})=1
.
∎

3.
Geometry of sum-rank metric codes

It is well-known that equivalence classes of Hamming-metric nondegenerate codes are in one-to-one correspondence with equivalence classes of projective systems; see
[
42
, Theorem 1.1.6]
. This connection has been intensively used to get classification results and intriguing constructions in both the areas of coding theory and finite geometry. Recently, in
[
41
,
38
]
it has been shown that equivalence classes of nondegenerate rank-metric codes are in one-to-one correspondence with equivalence classes of
q
-systems, where the latter constitute the
q
-analogue of projective systems; see also
[
1
]
. At this point, it is natural to ask whether it is possible to construct geometric objects able to capture the structure of sum-rank metric codes which generalize both projective systems and
q
-systems. In the following, we focus on this question.

Let
G=(G_{1}\,|\,\ldots\,|\,G_{t})
, where
G_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n_{i}}
. For each
i\in[t]
, define
\mathcal{U}_{i}
to be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G_{i}

and the maps

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\psi_{G_{i}}:&{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{i}}&\longrightarrow&\mathcal{U}_{i}\\
&\lambda&\longmapsto&\lambda G_{i}^{\top},\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\psi_{G}:&{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\mathbf{n}}&\longrightarrow&\mathcal{U}\\
&(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t})&\longmapsto&(\psi_{G_{1}}(\lambda_{1}),\ldots,\psi_{G_{t}}(\lambda_{t})).\end{array}

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code with generator matrix
G=(G_{1}|\ldots|G_{t})
.
Let
\mathcal{U}_{i}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G_{i}
, for
i\in[t]
. Then, for every
v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and
i\in[t]
we have

\psi_{G_{i}}^{-1}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap v^{\perp})=\supp_{n_{i}}(vG_{i})^{\perp}.

In particular,

\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(vG)^{\perp}=\psi_{G}^{-1}((\mathcal{U}_{1}\cap v^{\perp}),\ldots,(\mathcal{U}_{t}\cap v^{\perp}))

and

(1)

\ww(vG)=N-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap v^{\perp}).

Proof.

By assumption,
\C
is non-degenerate and hence by Proposition
2.13
we have
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\mathcal{U}_{i}=n_{i}
for each
i\in[t]
.
Thus,
\psi_{G_{i}}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-isomorphism.

Let us now prove the first part of the statement for
i=1
and let us write
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}_{1},G=G_{1}
and
n=n_{1}
.
Let
r\coloneqq n-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}\cap v^{\perp})
and let
(g_{r+1}^{\prime},\ldots,g_{n}^{\prime})
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
\mathcal{U}\cap v^{\perp}
written as column vectors. We can complete it to an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis
(g_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,g_{n}^{\prime})
of
\mathcal{U}
. Let
B\in\GL(n,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
be such that
GB=(g_{1}^{\prime}\mid\ldots\mid g_{n}^{\prime})
. With this choice, we have that

vGB=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r},0,\ldots,0)

and the
x_{i}
’s must be
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent. Now, we can complete
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
to an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis
\Gamma\coloneqq(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. It is clear that

\Gamma(vGB)=\begin{pmatrix}I_{r}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

and hence
\supp_{n}(vGB)=\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
.
Furthermore, by Proposition
2.12
, we have that
\supp_{n}(vG)=\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\cdot B^{-1}
.

On the other hand, by the choice of
B
, we have that
\psi_{GB}^{-1}(\mathcal{U}\cap v^{\perp})=\langle e_{r+1},\ldots,e_{n}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
. Now, for every
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
we have that

\psi_{GB}(\lambda)=\lambda(GB)^{\top}=(\lambda B^{\top})G^{\top}=\psi_{G}(\lambda B^{\top})
, and hence
\psi_{GB}^{-1}(y)=\psi_{G}^{-1}(y)(B^{\top})^{-1}
for every
y\in\mathcal{U}
. Thus

\psi_{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{U}\cap v^{\perp})=\psi_{GB}^{-1}(\mathcal{U}\cap v^{\perp})\cdot B^{\top}=\langle e_{r+1},\ldots,e_{n}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\cdot B^{\top}
, proving the first part of the statement.
The second part of the statement immediately follows.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Note that when we specialize to the Hamming metric, the result obtained is well-known and can be found for instance in
[
42
]
. In the case of rank-metric codes, the result in Theorem
3.1
is stronger than the ones described in
[
41
,
38
,
1
]
. Indeed, in these papers the authors only shown (
1
), while here we provide a deeper duality connection between the supports of codewords and the associated hyperplanes sections.

Actually, evidence of the existence of a duality can already be found in
[
1
, Theorem 5.6]
. However, the duality was not explicitly derived, and this result immediately follows now from Theorem
3.1
.

Definition 3.3
.

An
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system

\mathcal{U}
is an ordered set
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
, where, for any
i\in[t]
,
\mathcal{U}_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
of dimension
n_{i}
, such that

\langle\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and

d=N-\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\mathcal{H})\,:\,\mathcal{H}\textnormal{ is an ${\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}$-hyperplane of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
be an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system. The
subspace section
of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
H\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
in
{\mathcal{U}}
is the
t
-uple

\chi_{{\mathcal{U}}}(H)\coloneqq(\mathcal{U}_{1}\cap H,\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t}\cap H)

and the
dimension-list
of
H
in
{\mathcal{U}}
is the associated
t
-uple of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-dimensions, that is

\nu_{{\mathcal{U}}}(H)\coloneqq(\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{1}\cap H),\ldots,\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{t}\cap H))
.
The
dimension-profile
of
H
in
{\mathcal{U}}
is the
t
-uple obtained from
\nu_{{\mathcal{U}}}(H)
by rearranging the entries in non-increasing order.

Definition 3.5
.

Two
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
and
(\mathcal{V}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{V}_{t})
are
equivalent
if there exists an isomorphism
\varphi\in\GL(k,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})
, an element
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{t})\in({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*})^{t}
and a permutation
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
, such that for every
i\in[t]

\varphi(\mathcal{U}_{i})=a_{i}\mathcal{V}_{\sigma(i)}.

We denote the set of equivalence classes of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems by
\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
.

Remark 3.6
.

Observe that when
t=1
, the notion of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system coincides with the notion of
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system (or
q
-system) introduced in
[
38
]
; see also
[
1
]
. The case when
n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}=1
is a bit more complicated, but we see now that it coincides with the notion of projective systems only when considering the equivalence classes. Indeed, a
[\mathbf{1},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system is not a projective
[t,k,d]_{q^{m}}
system as defined for instance in
[
42
]
. The difference is that projective systems are sets of points in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
(or equivalently
1
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
), while
[\mathbf{1},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems are
ordered
sets of
1
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. However, when considering the equivalence classes, they coincide, because we identify
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-multiples and we take into account the action of the symmetric group
\mathcal{S}_{t}
.
In other words,
\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{1},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
can be identified with the set of equivalence classes of projective
[t,k,d]_{q^{m}}
systems.

Now we describe the 1-to-1 correspondence between equivalence classes of sum-rank nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code and equivalence classes of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems.

Consider the following maps

\displaystyle\Psi:\mathfrak{C}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}

\displaystyle\to\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}

\displaystyle\to\mathfrak{C}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}.

Let
[\C]\in\mathfrak{C}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
where
\C
is a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Let
G=(G_{1}\,\mid\,\ldots\,\mid\,G_{t})
be a generator matrix for
{\mathcal{C}}
. Define
\Psi([\C])
as the equivalence class of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems
[\mathcal{U}]
, where
\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
and
\mathcal{U}_{i}
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G_{i}
for every
i\in[t]
. Viceversa, given
[(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})]\in\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
, for every
i\in[t]
, fix an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis
\{g_{i,1},\ldots,g_{i,n_{i}}\}
of
\mathcal{U}_{i}
. Define
G_{i}
as the matrix whose columns are
\{g_{i,1},\ldots,g_{i,n_{i}}\}
and let
\Phi([(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})])
be the equivalence class of the sum-rank metric code generated by
G=(G_{1}\,\mid\,\ldots\,\mid\,G_{t}).

We can actually see that these two maps are well-defined. This is due to the two definitions of equivalence for
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
codes (Definition
2.6
) and
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems (Definition
3.5
). First, if we choose two different generator matrices for the same
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code
\C
, then it is clear that the two
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems spanned by the columns of these matrices are equivalent, since one matrix is obtained from the other by left multiplication of an element in
\GL(k,q^{m})
. Moreover, it is also immediate to see that if two
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
codes
\C,\C^{\prime}
are equivalent, then the systems associated to any generator matrices
G
and
G^{\prime}
are equivalent, showing that the map
\Psi
is well-defined. A similar argument can be used to prove that also
\Phi
is well-defined.

Thus we can finally state the corresponding result between
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
codes and
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems. The proof is based on the above discussions and the details are left to the reader.

Theorem 3.7
.

\Psi
and
\Phi
are well-defined and are the inverse of each other.

As a consequence, we can restate Theorem
3.1
in a more compact form, highlighting the duality identified by the sum-rank support of a codeword and the subspace section of an hyperplane as follows.

Corollary 3.8

(Duality supports-sections)
.

Let
\C
be a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code and let
{\mathcal{U}}\in\Psi([\C])
. Then, for every
G=(G_{1}\,\mid\,\ldots\,\mid\,G_{t})
generator matrix of
\C
and every
v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, we have

\chi_{{\mathcal{U}}}(v^{\perp})=\psi_{G}(\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(vG)^{\perp}).

Remark 3.9
.

If
n_{1}=\ldots=n_{t}=1
, up to the identification of
\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{1},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
with the set of equivalence classes of projective
[t,k,d]_{q^{m}}
systems,
\Phi
and
\Psi
define exactly the relation between equivalence classes of projective
[t,k,d]_{q^{m}}
systems and equivalence classes of nondegenerate Hamming-metric codes, see
[
42
, Theorem 1.1.6]
. In this case, we denote by
\Phi^{\HH}
and
\Psi^{\HH}
the maps
\Phi
and
\Psi
, respectively.

Finally, from Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
2.4
, we can rephrase in geometric terms the MSRD property of a code.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
\C
be a sum-rank nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code and let
[(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})]=\Psi([\mathcal{C}])
.
\mathcal{C}
is an MSRD if and only if

\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\mathcal{H})\,:\,\mathcal{H}\mbox{ hyperplane of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}\leq k-1.

The notion of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system is therefore dual to the notion of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Despite these objects are equivalent, the geometric point of view as
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems naturally captures the block structure of the metric space. This can be very convenient, depending on the properties that one aims to study of sum-rank metric codes. We will indeed use this geometric characterization later in the next section with the principal goal of constructing one-weight codes, a problem that seems to us more difficult to deal with in the coding theory setting.
This is not surprising, since there is plenty of literature in which the geometric viewpoint as projective systems allows more naturally to derive results on Hamming-metric codes.

4.
Doubly-extended linearized Reed-Solomon codes

A particular interest among sum-rank metric is the family of MSRD codes. The first construction of MSRD codes was due to Martínez-Peñas in
[
29
]
which is now known as
linearized Reed-Solomon codes
. These codes extend to the sum-rank metric the family of Gabidulin codes in the rank metric and the Reed-Solomon codes in the Hamming metric. They are introduced originally in a vectorial setting while in
[
31
]
they have been revisited in a skew polynomial setting.

To present the family of linearized Reed-Solomon codes we will need the to introduce
skew polynomials, which were originally presented in the seminal paper by Ore
[
33
]
, in a
quite general setting.

Let
\sigma
be a generator of
\Gal({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.
The ring of skew polynomials
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}[x;\sigma]
is a set of formal polynomials

\left\{f=\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}x^{i}\,:\,f_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}},d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\right\}

equipped with the ordinary (component-wise) addition

f+g=\sum_{i\geq 0}(f_{i}+g_{i})x^{i}

and the multiplication rule

x\cdot a=\sigma(a)\cdot x,\mbox{ for any }a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}},

extended to polynomials by associativity and distributivity.

The degree of a skew polynomial is defined as in the commutative case, and for an integer
v
, we denote by
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}[x;\sigma]_{<v}
the set of skew polynomials of degree smaller than
v
.

An element
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
is a root of a skew polynomial
f=\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}x^{i}
, if
f(\beta):=\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}\sigma^{i}(\beta)=0
. The set of roots of
f
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
is denoted by
\ker(f)
and it turns out to be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
having dimension at most
d
, see
[
23
]
and
[
19
, Lemma 3.2]
.
We are interested in studying sum-rank metric codes in the vectorial setting (as in Subsection
2.2
). To this aim, we recall the
generalized operator evaluation
defined in
[
22
]
as follows. Let
f\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}[x;\sigma]
and
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. Define the map

\begin{array}[]{rccl}f(\cdot)_{a}:&{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\\
&\beta&\longmapsto&\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{i}\sigma^{i}(\beta)\mathrm{N}_{i}(a),\end{array}

where
\mathrm{N}_{i}(a)\coloneqq\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\sigma^{j}(a)
.

Let
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{t}
and
\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
. We define the multi-point evaluation map as

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\mathrm{ev}_{a,\beta}:&{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}[x;\sigma]&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}\\
&f&\longmapsto&(f(\beta_{1})_{a_{1}},\ldots,f(\beta_{n})_{a_{1}}\lvert\cdots\lvert f(\beta_{1})_{a_{t}},\ldots,f(\beta_{n})_{a_{t}}).\end{array}

In particular, we will use the multi-point evaluation map under certain assumptions on
a
and
\beta
.

Definition 4.1
.

A pair
(a,\beta)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{t}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
is said to be an
evaluation pair
(with respect to
\sigma
) if the components of
a
have pairwise distinct norm over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, and the elements of
\beta
are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

The properties of the map
\mathrm{ev}_{a,\beta}(\cdot)
have been exploited in
[
29
]
and they lead to the construction of the the following class of MSRD codes.

Definition 4.2

(see
[
29
, Definition 31]
)
.

Let
n\leq m
,
t\leq q-1
and
1\leq k\leq N
. Let
(a,\beta)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{t}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
be an evaluation pair. The corresponding
linearized Reed-Solomon code
is defined by

\mathcal{C}_{k,a,\beta}=\{\mathrm{ev}_{a,\beta}(f)\,:\,f\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}[x;\sigma]_{<k}\}.

In
[
29
, Theorem 4]
, it has been shown that the linearized Reed-Solomon codes
\mathcal{C}_{k,a,\beta}
are MSRD codes.
For a description of the duals of linearized Reed-Solomon codes, see

[
12
,
11
]
.

Hence, using Corollary
3.10
, we can look at the intersections of an
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system associated with a linearized Reed-Solomon code with the hyperplanes.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
k\leq N
,
t<q
and
\mathcal{S}=\langle\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{k,a,\beta}
be a linearized Reed-Solomon code as in Definition
4.2
. Let
[(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})]=\Psi([\mathcal{C}_{k,a,\beta}])
. Then a representative of the class
[(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})]
is

\mathcal{U}_{i}=\{(y,\sigma(y)\mathrm{N}_{1}(a_{i}),\sigma^{2}(y)N_{2}(a_{i}),\ldots,\sigma^{k-1}(y)N_{k-1}(a_{i}))\,:\,y\in\mathcal{S}\}.

In particular,

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\mathcal{H})\leq k-1,

for every hyperplane
\mathcal{H}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
.

Proof.

A generator matrix
G
of
\mathcal{C}_{k,a,\beta}
can be computed as in
[
29
, p. 604]
. Then it is easy to see that the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of each block of
G
gives the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
\mathcal{U}_{i}
.
The last part then follows by Corollary
3.10
.
∎

As it happens for Reed-Solomon codes in the Hamming metric, we now want to extend the evaluations of a linearized Reed-Solomon code also to
0
and to
\infty
. For this reason,
for a skew polynomial
f(x)=f_{0}+f_{1}x+\ldots+f_{k-1}x^{k-1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}[x;\sigma]_{<k}
define
f(\beta)_{\infty}\coloneqq\beta f_{k-1}
for any
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
1\leq k\leq N
,
\gamma,\delta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
and let
(a,\beta)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{t}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
be an evaluation pair. We define the code

\mathcal{C}_{k,a,\beta}^{\gamma,\delta}=\{(\mathrm{ev}_{a,\beta}(f)\lvert f(\gamma)_{0}\lvert f(\delta)_{\infty})\,:\,f\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}[x;\sigma]_{<k}\}.

When
t=q-1
, the code
\mathcal{C}_{k,a,\beta}^{\gamma,\delta}
is called the
doubly-extended linearized Reed-Solomon
code.

Remark 4.5
.

Note that if
m=1
, then we recover the notion of doubly-extended Reed-Solomon codes.

As one might expect, also doubly-extended linearized Reed-Solomon codes are MSRD.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
t\leq q-1
and
2\leq k\leq N
. Let
(a,\beta)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{t}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
be an evaluation pair, let
\gamma,\delta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
and let
\mathcal{S}=\langle\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
. Then

[(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t},\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{U}_{\infty})]=\Psi([\mathcal{C}_{k,a,\beta}^{\gamma,\delta}]).

where

\mathcal{U}_{i}=\{(y,\sigma(y)\mathrm{N}_{1}(a_{i}),\sigma^{2}(y)N_{2}(a_{i}),\ldots,\sigma^{k-1}(y)N_{k-1}(a_{i}))\,:\,y\in\mathcal{S}\},

for any
i\in[t]
,
\mathcal{U}_{0}=\langle(\gamma,0,\ldots,0)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
and
\mathcal{U}_{\infty}=\langle(0,\ldots,0,\delta)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
.
Moreover, the code
\mathcal{C}_{k,a,\beta}^{\gamma,\delta}
is an MSRD code.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.3
we have
[(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})]=\Psi([\mathcal{C}_{k,a,\beta}])
, and hence
[(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t},\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{U}_{\infty})]=\Psi([\mathcal{C}_{k,a,\beta}^{\gamma,\delta}])
.
Now, consider any
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-hyperplane
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and let
h_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+h_{k}x_{k}=0
be an its equation.
Consider

g_{i,\mathcal{H}}=h_{1}+h_{2}\mathrm{N}_{1}(a_{i})x+h_{3}N_{2}(a_{i})x^{2}+\ldots+h_{k}N_{k-1}(a_{i})x^{k-1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}[x;\sigma].

Since
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\mathcal{H})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\ker(g_{i,H})\cap\mathcal{S})
, by Corollary
3.10
one gets

(2)

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\mathcal{H})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\ker(g_{i,\mathcal{H}})\cap\mathcal{S})\leq k-1.

Moreover, by Corollary
3.10
it is enough to prove that

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\mathcal{H})+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}\cap\mathcal{H})+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{\infty}\cap\mathcal{H})\leq k-1,

for every
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-hyperplane
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}
.

Clearly, if
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}\cap\mathcal{H})+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{\infty}\cap\mathcal{H})=0
, then the assertion follow by (
2
).

Suppose now that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}\cap\mathcal{H})=1
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{\infty}\cap\mathcal{H})=0
.
The equation of
\mathcal{H}
is
h_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+h_{k}x_{k}=0
.
By (
2
),

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\mathcal{H})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\ker(g^{\prime}_{i})\cap\mathcal{S})\leq k-2,

with
g^{\prime}_{i}=h^{\prime}_{1}\mathrm{id}+h^{\prime}_{2}\mathrm{N}_{1}(a_{i})\sigma+h_{3}N_{2}(a_{i})\sigma^{2}+\cdots+h^{\prime}_{k-1}N_{k-2}(a_{i})\sigma^{k-2}
, where
h^{\prime}_{i}=\sigma^{m-1}(h_{i+1})
, for any
i\in[k-1]
.

Similarly, the case
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}\cap\mathcal{H})=0
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{\infty}\cap\mathcal{H})=1
can be carried out to get the assertion.

Finally, suppose that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{U}_{0})+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\infty})=2
, then
the equation of
\mathcal{H}
is
h_{2}x_{2}+\ldots+h_{k-1}x_{k-1}=0
. Then, by (
2
), it follows

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\mathcal{H})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\ker(g^{\prime\prime}_{i})\cap\mathcal{S})\leq k-3,

with
g^{\prime\prime}_{i}=h^{\prime\prime}_{1}\mathrm{id}+h^{\prime\prime}_{2}\mathrm{N}_{1}(a_{i})\sigma+h_{3}^{\prime\prime}N_{2}(a_{i})\sigma^{2}+\cdots+h^{\prime\prime}_{k-2}N_{k-3}(a_{i})\sigma^{k-3}
, where
h^{\prime\prime}_{i}=\sigma^{m-1}(h_{i+1})
for any
i\in[k-2]
.
∎

In the following lemma we study some properties of the
[\mathbf{n},2,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system associated with the code
\mathcal{C}_{2,a,\beta}^{\gamma,\delta}.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
m
be a positive integer.
Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{q-1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
such that
\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(a_{i})\neq\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(a_{j})
, if
i\neq j
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{i}=\{(y,a_{i}\sigma(y))\,:\,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\}
, for
i\in[q-1]
. Let
\gamma,\delta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
and define
\mathcal{U}_{0}=\{(a\gamma,0)\,:\,a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\}
and
\mathcal{U}_{\infty}=\{(0,b\delta)\,:\,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\}
. Then for every one-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{2}
there exists
i\in\{0,\ldots,q-1,\infty\}
such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{U}_{i})=1
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{U}_{j})=0
for every
j\neq i
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be any one-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{2}
.
If
\mathcal{S}\in\{\langle(1,0)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}},\langle(0,1)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\}
, then either
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{U}_{0})=1
or
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\infty})=1
. In both the cases, the intersection with remaining
\mathcal{U}_{i}
’s will be trivial.
Suppose now that
\mathcal{S}\notin\{\langle(1,0)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}},\langle(0,1)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\}
. Then
\mathcal{S}=\langle(1,\lambda)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
with
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
and, by the assumptions on the
a_{i}
’s it follows that there exists
i\in[q-1]
such that
\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\lambda)=\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(a_{i})
.
Hence, there exists an element

such that
\lambda=a_{i}\sigma(y)y^{-1}
. This implies that

\langle(1,\lambda)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=\langle(y,a_{i}\sigma(y))\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}},

and hence
\dim(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\langle(1,\lambda)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})>0
.
Moreover, if
y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
and
\rho\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
are such that
\rho(1,\lambda)=(y,a_{i}\sigma(y))
then

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho=y,\\
\rho\lambda=a_{i}\sigma(y),\end{array}\right.

from which we get
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\langle(1,\lambda)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})=1
.
Let
j\in[q-1]
with
j\neq i
.
Since
\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\lambda)\neq\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(a_{j})

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{j}\cap\langle(1,\lambda)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})>0

and
\rho\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
such that
\rho(1,y)=(y,a_{j}\sigma(y))
and so

, which implies that
\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\lambda)=\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(a_{j})

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{U}_{0})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\infty})=0

∎

5.
Associating an Hamming-metric code

The characterization of one-weight codes in a given metric has always been a fascinating problem, due to their interesting combinatorial properties and their strong rigidity structures. One-weight codes in the Hamming metric have been classified in the well-known work of Bonisoli
[
6
]
. An easy way to prove such a result is using the geometric characterization of Hamming-metric codes via projective systems. The geometric approach was the key used very recently to obtain a classification of one-weight (vector) codes in the rank metric in
[
38
]
; see also
[
1
, Proposition 3.16]
. From a more intrinsic point of view, this approach can be also seen as relying on Bonisoli’s result for Hamming-metric codes by using the transformation introduced in
[
1
, Section 4]
to obtain a Hamming-metric code from a rank-metric one. Unfortunately, this idea seems unlikely to work if we try to obtain a similar classification result for one-weight sum-rank metric codes. However, we can use a similar strategy to obtain partial results on sum-rank metric codes with constant rank-profile.

To this aim, we will recall some basics on linear sets.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\Lambda=\mathrm{PG}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k},{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})=\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
.
Let
\mathcal{U}
be an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
system, then the set of points

L_{\mathcal{U}}=\{\langle{u}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\,:\,{u}\in\mathcal{U}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}\}\subseteq\Lambda

is said to be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-
linear set of rank

n
.

For such a linear set we have the following bound on its size

(3)

|L_{\mathcal{U}}|\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}.

Let
P=\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
be a point in
\Lambda
. The
weight of
P
in
L_{\mathcal{U}}
is defined as

\ww_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}(P)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}\cap\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}).

If
L_{\mathcal{U}}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set with the property that
\ww_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}(P)\leq 1
for every point
P\in\Lambda
, we say that
L_{\mathcal{U}}
is
scattered
.
Equivalently,
L_{\mathcal{U}}
is scattered if and only if the size of
L_{\mathcal{U}}
satisfies the equality in (
3
), that is
|L_{\mathcal{U}}|=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
.
Moreover, in
[
5
]
it has been proved that for scattered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear sets of rank
n
in
\Lambda
we have

(4)

n\leq\frac{mk}{2}.

For more details on linear sets see
[
21
,
36
]
.

5.1.
The associated Hamming-metric code

Following the strategy developed in
[
1
, Section 4]
, we associate an equivalence class of Hamming-metric codes to an equivalence class of sum-rank-metric codes. For consistency, we will keep as much as possible the notation introduced in
[
1
]
.

Denote by

\mathfrak{D}(n,k)_{q^{m}/q}
the set of
n
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, and let
\mathfrak{D}(\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1},k)_{q^{m}}
be the set of multisets of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
with cardinality (counted with multiplicity)
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
. Define the map

\begin{array}[]{rccc}\Ext^{1}:&\mathfrak{D}(n,k)_{q^{m}/q}&\longrightarrow&\mathfrak{D}(\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1},k)_{q^{m}}\\
&{\mathcal{U}}&\longmapsto&(L_{\mathcal{U}},\mm_{\mathcal{U}}),\end{array}

where
\mm_{\mathcal{U}}
is the multiplicity function given by

\mm_{\mathcal{U}}(\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\coloneqq\frac{q^{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}({\mathcal{U}}\cap\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})}-1}{q-1}.

For
i\in[t]
, let
({\mathcal{M}}_{i},\mm_{i})\in\mathfrak{D}(n_{i},k)_{q^{m}}
be a collection of multisets of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
. Define their
disjoint union
as

\biguplus_{i=1}^{t}({\mathcal{M}}_{i},\mm_{i})\coloneqq({\mathcal{M}},\mm),

where
{\mathcal{M}}={\mathcal{M}}_{1}\cup\ldots\cup{\mathcal{M}}_{t}
, and
\mm(P)=\mm_{1}(P)+\ldots+\mm_{t}(P)
for every
P\in\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{n},k)_{q^{m}/q}
be the set of
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
systems. Define the map

\begin{array}[]{rccc}\Ext:&\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{n},k)_{q^{m}/q}&\longrightarrow&\mathfrak{D}(\frac{q^{n_{1}}+\ldots+q^{n_{t}}-t}{q-1},k)_{q^{m}}\\
&{\mathcal{U}}\coloneqq({\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})&\longmapsto&\biguplus\limits_{i=1}^{t}\Ext^{1}({\mathcal{U}}_{i}).\end{array}

Proposition 5.2
.

(1)

If
{\mathcal{U}}
is an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
system, then
\Ext({\mathcal{U}})
is a projective
[\frac{q^{n_{1}}+\ldots+q^{n_{t}}-t}{q-1},k]_{q^{m}}
system.

(2)

If
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}}
are two equivalent
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
systems, then
\Ext({\mathcal{U}})
and
\Ext({\mathcal{V}})
are two equivalent projective
[\frac{q^{n_{1}}+\ldots+q^{n_{t}}-t}{q-1},k]_{q^{m}}
systems.

Proof.

(1)

Let
{\mathcal{U}}\coloneqq({\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})
be an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
system. Then, by definition it holds that
\langle\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. Thus, in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
we have

\langle\Ext({\mathcal{U}})\rangle=\langle L_{{\mathcal{U}}_{1}},\ldots,L_{{\mathcal{U}}_{t}}\rangle=L_{\langle{\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{{\mathcal{U}}_{t}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}}=\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m}).

(2)

Let
{\mathcal{U}}\coloneqq({\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})
,
{\mathcal{V}}\coloneqq({\mathcal{V}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{V}}_{t})
be two equivalent
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
systems. Up to permutation, we may assume that there exists
f\in\GL(k,q^{m})
such that
f({\mathcal{U}}_{i})={\mathcal{V}}_{i}
, for every
i\in[t]
. Therefore, we have that

\displaystyle\Ext({\mathcal{V}})

\displaystyle=\biguplus\limits_{i=1}^{t}\Ext^{1}({\mathcal{V}}_{i})=\biguplus\limits_{i=1}^{t}\Ext^{1}(f({\mathcal{U}}_{i}))=\biguplus\limits_{i=1}^{t}(L_{f({\mathcal{U}}_{i})},\mm_{f({\mathcal{U}}_{i})})

\displaystyle=\biguplus\limits_{i=1}^{t}(\overline{f}(L_{{\mathcal{U}}_{i}}),\mm_{f({\mathcal{U}}_{i})})=\overline{\phi}(\Ext({\mathcal{U}})),

where
\overline{f}
is the projection of
f
in
\PGL(k,q^{m})
, proving the claim.

∎

Hence, by Proposition
5.2
, we have a natural induced map

\Ext^{\HH}:\mathfrak{U}[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{U}[M,k]_{q^{m}},

where
M\coloneqq\frac{q^{n_{1}}+\ldots+q^{n_{t}}-t}{q-1}
, and
\mathfrak{U}[M,k]_{q^{m}}
denotes the set of the equivalence classes of projective
[M,k]_{q^{m}}
systems,

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\C
be a sum-rank nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Any code
C\in(\Phi^{\HH}\circ\Ext^{\HH}\circ\Psi)([\C])
is called an
associated Hamming-metric code
with
\C
.

When the choice of the associated Hamming-metric code with an
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code
\C
is not important nor relevant (e.g. when the property we are investigating is invariant under code equivalence), we will simply write
\C^{\HH}
to denote it.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
G=(G_{1}\,|\,\ldots\,|\,G_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times N}
be the generator matrix of a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code, with
G_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n_{i}}
, and let
{\mathcal{U}}=({\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})
be the
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
system where
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G_{i}
. We define

G_{\Ext}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times M}
to be any matrix obtained from the projective
[M,k]_{q^{m}}
system
\Ext({\mathcal{U}})
, where
M\coloneqq\frac{q^{n_{1}}+\ldots+q^{n_{t}}-t}{q-1}
.

Remark 5.5
.

In contrast with what happens for rank-metric codes (see
[
1
, Section 4]
), in this case it is not clear how to determine the minimum distance of
\C^{\HH}
starting from the minimum sum-rank distance of
\C
. However, the following result links the rank list distribution of
\C
with the Hamming weights of
\C^{\HH}
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
G=(G_{1}\,|\,\ldots\,|\,G_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times N}
be the generator matrix of a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code, and let
v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\setminus\{0\}
. Then

\ww_{\HH}(vG_{\Ext})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n_{i}}-q^{n_{i}-\rk(vG_{i})}}{q-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n_{i}}-q^{n_{i}-\rho(vG)_{i}}}{q-1}.

In particular, if
\C
is a nondegenerate
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
code, then the minimum distance of
\C^{\HH}
is given by

\dd(\C^{\HH})=\min_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathrm{S}(\C)}\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n_{i}}-q^{n_{i}-r_{i}}}{q-1},

where
\mathrm{S}(\C)
is the set of rank-lists of
\C
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}=({\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})
be the
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
system choosing as
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}
the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G_{i}
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be the hyperplane defined by
v^{\perp}
. Then Hamming-weight of
vG_{\Ext}
is given by

\mathrm{w}_{\HH}(vG_{\Ext})=M-\sum_{P\in\mathrm{PG}(\mathcal{H},{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})}\mathrm{m}(P),

where
\mathrm{m}
is the multiplicity function of
\Ext(\mathcal{U})
. Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathrm{PG}(\mathcal{H},{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})}\mathrm{m}(P)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\sum_{P\in\mathrm{PG}(\mathcal{H},{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})}\mathrm{m_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}}(P)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{H},\\
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\mathcal{V})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{q^{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\mathcal{V})}-1}{q-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{i=1}^{t}\lvert\mathcal{H}\cap(\mathcal{U}_{i}\setminus\{0\})\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{q^{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{U}_{i})}-1}{q-1}.

Hence

\mathrm{w}_{\HH}(vG_{\Ext})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n_{i}}-1}{q-1}-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{q^{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{U}_{i})}-1}{q-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n_{i}}-q^{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\mathcal{(}H\cap\mathcal{U}_{i})}}{q-1}.

The first part of the assertion follows from Theorem
3.1
, whereas the second one follows by definition of rank-list of
\mathcal{C}
.
∎

The above result extends to the sum-rank metric
[
1
, Theorem 8]
,
[
5
, Section 5]
and
[
43
, Theorem 7.1]
.

5.2.
Parameters of constant rank-profile codes

We have seen how the machinery defined above links sum-rank metric codes to Hamming-metric codes. We now exploit this machinery to derive properties of constant rank-profile codes.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
\C
be a nondegenerate
[(n,\ldots,n),k]_{q^{m}/q}
code which is constant rank-profile. Then
\C^{\HH}
is a constant weight code in the Hamming metric.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{t})
be the constant rank-profile. Let
G
be a generator matrix of
\C
and let us take the code
\C^{\HH}
to be the one generated by
G_{\Ext}
. Then, for every
v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\setminus\{0\}
, we have by Proposition
5.6
that

\ww_{\HH}(vG_{\Ext})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n}-q^{n-r_{i}}}{q-1}

is constant.
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
\C
be a nondegenerate
[(n,\ldots,n),k]_{q^{m}/q}
code which is constant rank-profile, with rank-profile
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{t})
. Then:

(1)

The number

\ell\coloneqq\frac{t(q^{n}-1)(q^{m}-1)}{(q-1)(q^{km}-1)}

is a positive integer.

(2)

It holds that

tq^{m(k-1)}(q^{n}-1)(q^{m}-1)=(q^{km}-1)\Big(tq^{n}-\sum_{i=1}^{t}q^{n-r_{i}}\Big).

Proof.

By Proposition
5.7
, we have that
\C^{\HH}
is a constant weight code in the Hamming metric. However, the only nondegenerate constant weight codes in the Hamming metric are (equivalent to) concatenations of
\ell
copies of the
[\frac{q^{km}-1}{q^{m}-1},k,q^{m(k-1)}]_{q^{m}}
simplex code; see
[
6
]
. Since the length of
\C^{\HH}
is
t\frac{(q^{n}-1)}{(q-1)}
, then we obtain that

\ell=\frac{t(q^{n}-1)(q^{m}-1)}{(q-1)(q^{km}-1)}

is a positive integer.
Moreover, by Proposition
5.6
, the minimum distance of
\C^{\HH}
must be

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n}-q^{n-r_{i}}}{q-1},

leading to the desired equality.
∎

Corollary
5.8
provides constraints on the parameters that a nondegenerate constant profile
[(n,\ldots,n),k]_{q^{m}/q}
code can have, as the following example will illustrate.

Example 5.9
.

Let us assume that we want to construct a nondegenerate
[(n,\ldots,n),k]_{q^{m}/q}
code with constant rank-profile
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{t})
, and let us fix
n=3
,
k=m=2
. Using Corollary
5.8
(1), we obtain that

t\frac{q^{2}+q+1}{q^{2}+1}

is an integer, and thus
t=(q^{2}+1)t^{\prime}
.
Now, using Corollary
5.8
(2), we deduce after some simplifications that

t^{\prime}(q^{3}+q^{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{(q^{2}+1)t^{\prime}}q^{3-r_{i}}.

On the other hand, observe that
3-r_{i}=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}({\mathcal{U}}_{i}\cap H)
for some
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
-hyperplane of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}^{2}
. Since
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}({\mathcal{U}}_{i})=3
, then we can only have
r_{i}\in\{1,2\}
. Let

a_{i}\coloneqq|\{j\,:\,r_{j}=i\}|
, for
i=1,2
. Therefore, we have

\begin{cases}a_{1}+a_{2}=t^{\prime}(q^{2}+1)\\
q^{2}a_{1}+qa_{2}=t^{\prime}(q^{3}+q^{2}),\end{cases}

which leads to the unique solution

a_{1}=t^{\prime},\qquad a_{2}=t^{\prime}q^{2}.

Thus, the constant rank-profile must be

\mathbf{r}=(\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{t^{\prime}q^{2}\text{ {times}}},\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{t^{\prime}\text{ {times}}}).

6.
Orbital construction and simplex codes

In this section we provide a construction of constant rank-profile codes using the orbits of a transitive action on the nonzero elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. When the orbits are obtained by the action of a Singer subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(k,q^{m})
, such codes will be called simplex codes.

6.1.
Orbital construction

Let
\mathcal{G}\leq\GL(k,q^{m})
, and consider the action of
\mathcal{G}
on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\setminus\{0\}
– which we denote by
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}
– induced by the one of
\GL(k,q^{m})
, namely

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\phi_{\mathcal{G}}:&\mathcal{G}\times({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\setminus\{0\})&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\setminus\{0\}\\
&(A,v)&\longmapsto&vA.\end{array}

The same group
\mathcal{G}
can be considered also acting on any Grassmannian. More precisely, for any positive integer
r
dividing
m
, and any positive integer
s
we have that
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}
induces an action on

\mathrm{Gr}_{q^{r}}(s,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k})\coloneqq\{\mathcal{V}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\,:\,{\mathcal{V}\mbox{ is an }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{r}}\mbox{-subspace and }}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{r}}}(\mathcal{V})=s\}.

However, in this case, the action of
\mathcal{G}
is not always faithful, since its kernel is given by
\mathcal{G}\cap{\mathbb{D}}_{q^{r}}
, where
{\mathbb{D}}_{q^{r}}=\{\alpha I_{k}\,:\,\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{r}}^{*}\}
.
However, if for any
r
dividing
m
we restrict to study equivalence classes of the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{q^{r}}(s,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k})
according to Definition
3.5
, then it is clear that also
{\mathcal{G}}\cap{\mathbb{D}}_{q^{m}}
acts trivially. Thus, we have an induced action of the group
\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}\coloneqq\mathcal{G}/(\mathcal{G}\cap{\mathbb{D}}_{q^{m}})
on the set
\mathfrak{U}[s,k,d]_{q^{m}/q^{r}}
, which we denote by

\phi_{\mathcal{G}}^{r,s}
.

Of particular interest for us is the action
\phi_{\mathcal{G}^{\top}}^{m,k-1}
that

\mathcal{G}^{\top}\coloneqq\left\{A^{\top}\,:\,A\in\mathcal{G}\right\}

induces on
\mathrm{Gr}_{q^{m}}(k-1,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k})
, that is the set of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-hyperplanes of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. For brevity, we will denote it by
\hat{\phi}_{\mathcal{G}}
.

From now on we will say that a group
\mathcal{G}\leq\GL(k,q^{m})
is
transitive
whenever the action
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}^{m,1}
is transitive. The following result is straightforward.

Lemma 6.1
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is transitive, then also
\hat{\phi}_{\mathcal{G}}
is transitive.

Proof.

By duality, it is equivalent to show that
\mathcal{G}^{\top}
acts transitively on the one-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, or, in other words, that the action
\phi_{\mathcal{G}^{\top}}^{m,1}
is transitive. Let
X\coloneqq\mathrm{Gr}_{q^{m}}(1,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k})
, and for any matrix
A\in\mathcal{G}
denote by
X^{A}
to be the set of fixed points of
X
under
A
, that is

X^{A}:=\left\{V\in X\,:\,\phi_{\mathcal{G}}^{m,1}(A,V)=V\right\}.

However, any element in
X
is of the form
\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
for some
v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\setminus\{0\}
, and it holds that

\displaystyle X^{A}

\displaystyle=\{\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\,:\,\langle vA\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\}

\displaystyle=\{\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\,:\,vA=\lambda v,\mbox{ for some }\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\in X\,:\,v\in\bigcup_{\lambda\in\sigma_{A}}A_{\lambda}\Big\},

where
\sigma_{A}
denotes the spectrum of
A
, and
A_{\lambda}
is the eigenspace of
A
with respect to the eigenvalue
\lambda
.
By Burnside’s Lemma, one has that the number of orbits of the actions
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}^{m,1}
and
\phi_{\mathcal{G}^{\top}}^{m,1}
are respectively

\displaystyle|X/\mathcal{G}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{A\in\mathcal{G}}|X^{A}|

\displaystyle|X/\mathcal{G}^{\top}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{A\in\mathcal{G}}|X^{A^{\top}}|

Since
A
and
A^{\top}
have the same spectrum and isomorphic eigenspaces, we conclude that

1=|X/\mathcal{G}|=|X/\mathcal{G}^{\top}|,

and therefore
\phi_{\mathcal{G}^{\top}}^{m,1}
is transitive.
∎

At this point we can state the main result of this section, which allows to construct several constant rank-profile codes.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
r
be a divisor of
m
and let
t
be a positive integer such that
n\coloneqq rt\leq km
. Let
\mathcal{G}\leq\GL(k,q^{m})
be a transitive subgroup and let
{\mathcal{O}}=({\mathcal{U}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})
be an orbit of the action
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}^{r,s}
. Then every code
\C\in\Phi([{\mathcal{O}}])
is a constant rank-profile code.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}=v_{1}^{\perp}
,
\mathcal{H}_{2}=v_{2}^{\perp}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
be two
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-hyperplanes. It is enough to show that the dimension-list
\nu_{{\mathcal{O}}}(H_{1})
can be obtained by permuting the dimension-list
\nu_{{\mathcal{O}}}(H_{2})
. Since
\mathcal{G}
is transitive, by Lemma
6.1
also
\widehat{\phi}_{\mathcal{G}}
is transitive, hence there exists

A\in\mathcal{G}
such that
(v_{1}A^{\top})^{\perp}=v_{2}^{\perp}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\nu_{{\mathcal{O}}}(H_{2})

\displaystyle=\nu_{{\mathcal{O}}}((v_{1}A^{\top})^{\perp})=(\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}((v_{1}A^{\top})^{\perp}\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1}),\ldots,\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}((v_{1}A^{\top})^{\perp}\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{t}))

\displaystyle=(\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(v_{1}^{\perp}\cap\phi_{\mathcal{G}}^{r,s}(A,{\mathcal{U}}_{1})),\ldots,\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(v_{1}^{\perp}\cap\phi_{\mathcal{G}}^{r,s}(A,{\mathcal{U}}_{t})))

\displaystyle=\nu_{\phi_{\mathcal{G}}^{r,s}(A,{\mathcal{O}})}(H_{1}).

Since
{\mathcal{O}}
is an orbit of the action
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}^{r,s}
, then
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}^{r,s}(A,{\mathcal{O}})
is just a permutation of
{\mathcal{O}}
, and hence
\nu_{{\mathcal{O}}}(H_{2})
is a permutation of
\nu_{{\mathcal{O}}}(H_{1})
.
∎

In order to use Theorem
6.2
for concrete constructions of constant rank-profile codes, we need to find concrete examples of transitive subgroups
\mathcal{G}\leq\GL(k,q^{m})
.

Remark 6.3
.

It is worth to note that the concatenation of two constant rank-profile codes is a rank-profile code as well.

In the following remark we point out that the construction of one-weight sum-rank metric code in Theorem
4.9
cannot be obtained as a concatenation of orbital constructions.

Remark 6.4
.

Consider the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces associated with the doubly-extended linearized Reed-Solomon code of dimension two:
\mathcal{U}_{i}=\{(y,a_{i}\sigma(y))\,:\,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\}
, for any
i\in[q-1]
where the
a_{i}
’s have pairwise distinct norm over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,
\mathcal{U}_{0}=\langle(1,0)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
and
\mathcal{U}_{\infty}=\langle(0,1)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
.
Suppose that these subspaces have been obtained as the union of orbit under the action of some transitive groups. Since
\mathcal{U}_{0}
and
\mathcal{U}_{\infty}
are the only
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of dimension one, then
\{\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{U}_{\infty}\}
is an orbit under a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(2,q^{m})
, which is not possible.

6.2.
The simplex codes in the sum-rank metric

In this section we define the concept of an
n
-simplex code for the sum-rank metric. This code is an
[(n,\ldots,n),k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
. We will show that it is constant rank-profile and its parameters are fully determined by the weight distribution of a special rank-metric code associated to it. Furthermore, we discuss the equivalence classes of
n
-simplex codes.

Definition 6.5
.

A
Singer subgroup

\mathcal{G}\leq\GL(k,q^{m})
is a cyclic subgroup of order
{q^{km}-1}
.

One may construct a Singer subgroup
\mathcal{G}\leq\GL(k,q^{m})
by taking the multiplicative subgroup of the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{km}}
. More specifically, we can take
{\mathcal{H}}_{p}\coloneqq\{M_{p}^{i}\,:\,0\leq i\leq q^{km}-2\}
, where
M
is the companion matrix of an irreducible polynomial
p
of degree
k
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
.
Furthermore, it is well-known that any Singer subgroup
\mathcal{G}
is conjugate to
{\mathcal{H}}_{p}
, that is

\mathcal{G}=B{\mathcal{H}}_{p}B^{-1}=\left\{BM_{p}^{i}B^{-1}\,:\,0\leq i\leq q^{km}-2\right\},

for some
B\in\GL(k,q^{m})
.

Definition 6.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be an
n
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and let
\mathcal{G}
be a Singer subgroup of
\GL(k,q^{m})
. An
n
-simplex code

\C_{\mathcal{G},{\mathcal{U}}}
is any
[(n,\ldots,n),k]_{q^{m}/q}
code belonging to
\Phi([{\mathcal{O}}])
, where
{\mathcal{O}}=(\phi_{\mathcal{G}}^{1,n}(A,{\mathcal{U}}))_{A\in\overline{\mathcal{G}}}
. If
\mathcal{G}={\mathcal{H}}_{p}
, then we say that the
n
-simplex code is
standard
.

Example 6.7
.

Let
k=m=q=2
and
n=3
. Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{4}={\mathbb{F}}_{2}(\alpha)
, where
\alpha^{2}+\alpha+1=0
and let us fix the
3
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace

{\mathcal{U}}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}(a+b)\alpha+b,&a+c\alpha\end{pmatrix}\,:\,a,b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\right\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{4}^{2}.

Let us consider the field extension
{\mathbb{F}}_{16}={\mathbb{F}}_{2}(\beta)
, where
\beta^{4}+\beta+1=0
. One can compute the minimal polynomial of
\beta
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}(\alpha)
, obtaining
p(x)=x^{2}+x+\alpha^{2}
.
The Singer subgroup
{\mathcal{H}}_{p}
is therefore given by

{\mathcal{H}}_{p}\coloneqq\left\{M_{p}^{i}\,:\,0\leq i\leq 14\right\},

where

M_{p}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&\alpha^{2}\\
1&1\end{pmatrix},

while
\overline{{\mathcal{H}}}_{p}\cong\left\{M_{p}^{i}\,:\,0\leq i\leq 4\right\}.

An
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis for
{\mathcal{U}}
is given by
(\alpha,1),(\alpha^{2},0),(0,\alpha)
. Thus, the
3
-simplex code
\C_{{\mathcal{H}}_{p},{\mathcal{U}}}
is a
[(3,\ldots,3),2]_{4/2}
code with generator matrix

G=(\,A\,\mid\,(M_{p})^{\top}A\,\mid\,\ldots\,\mid\,(M_{p}^{\top})^{4}A\,),

where

A\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\alpha^{2}&0\\
1&0&\alpha\end{pmatrix}.

Thus, we have

G=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc|ccc|ccc|ccc|ccc}\alpha&\alpha^{2}&0&1&0&\alpha&0&\alpha&\alpha&\alpha^{2}&\alpha&\alpha^{2}&\alpha^{2}&\alpha^{2}&\alpha\\
1&0&\alpha&0&\alpha&\alpha&\alpha^{2}&\alpha&\alpha^{2}&\alpha^{2}&\alpha^{2}&\alpha&1&\alpha&0\end{array}\right),

the only sum-rank weight of this code is
9
and the only rank-profile is
(2,2,2,2,1)
. This is an example of a constant rank-profile code whose parameters are those of Example
5.9
with
q=2
and
t=(q^{2}+1)
and
t^{\prime}=1
.

We have just seen in Example
6.7
a construction of a
3
-simplex code, which has constant weight
9
and constant rank-profile
(2,2,2,2,1)
. Thus, it is natural to ask what can be said in general about the only rank-profile and the only sum-rank weight of an
n
-simplex code in the sum-rank metric. The following result provides an answer to this question, linking the rank-profile and the constant weight to the weight distribution and to the total weight of a particular rank-metric code.

In order to do that we introduce some notations. For a given
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
such that
s\coloneqq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, define

\widetilde{{\mathcal{U}}}
to be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear embedding of
{\mathcal{U}}
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{s}
, that is an
[n,s]_{q^{m}/q}
system.

Furthermore, for an element
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{\mathbf{n}}
, we define its
block-Hamming support
, to be the set

\supp_{\mathbf{n}}^{\HH}(v)\coloneqq\{i\in[t]\,:\,v_{i}\neq 0\}.

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be an
n
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, let
\mathcal{G}
be a Singer subgroup of
\GL(k,q^{m})
, and let
t\coloneqq\frac{q^{km}-1}{q^{m}-1}
. Let
\widetilde{{\mathcal{U}}}
be the
[n,s]_{q^{m}/q}
system deriving from
{\mathcal{U}}
, and let
\widetilde{\C}\in\Phi([\widetilde{{\mathcal{U}}}])
be any associated rank-metric code.
Then, for every nonzero

c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{t})\in\C_{\mathcal{G},{\mathcal{U}}}
,
the map

\begin{array}[]{rccc}\iota:&\supp_{\mathbf{n}}^{\HH}(c)&\longrightarrow&\mathrm{PG}(\widetilde{\mathcal{C}},{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})\\
&i&\longmapsto&\langle c_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\end{array}

is
q^{(k-s)m}
-to-
1
.
In particular,

\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(c)
is obtained by rearranging
q^{(k-s)m}
copies of the vector
(\supp(\tilde{c}))_{\tilde{c}\in\mathrm{PG}(\widetilde{\C},{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})}

together with
\binom{k-s}{1}_{q^{m}}
occurrences of the trivial space
\{0\}
.

Thus, the nonzero weight of
\C_{\mathcal{G},{\mathcal{U}}}
is

w=\frac{q^{(k-s)m}}{q^{m}-1}\sum_{c\in\widetilde{\C}}\ww(c).

Proof.

Let
c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{t})\in\C_{\mathcal{G},{\mathcal{U}}}
and let
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
\mathcal{U}
and let
G\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n}
be the matrix whose columns are
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{\overline{G}}=\{A_{1}=id,A_{2},\ldots,A_{t}\}
. It follows that a generator matrix of
\C_{\mathcal{G},{\mathcal{U}}}
is
(G|A_{2}^{\top}G|\ldots|A_{t}^{\top}G)
, since
\{u_{1}A_{i},\ldots,u_{n}A_{i}\}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}^{1,n}(A_{i},\mathcal{U})
. Moreover, note that the span over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
of the rows of
G
and the span over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
of the rows
A_{i}^{\top}G
coincide.
Let
u^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,u^{\prime}_{s}\in\mathcal{U}
and
v^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{s}\in\tilde{\mathcal{U}}
be two
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-basis of
\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
and
\langle\tilde{\mathcal{U}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, respectively.
Let
B\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times s}
with
\rk(B)=s
such that
u^{\prime}_{i}B=v^{\prime}_{i}
for any
i\in[s]
. It follows that
c_{i}\in\mathrm{rowspan}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(A_{i}^{\top}G)=\mathrm{rowspan}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(G)=\mathrm{rowspan}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\tilde{G})
, where the columns of
\tilde{G}
are the
u_{i}B
’s and hence it is a generator matrix of
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
. Then the application
\iota
is well-defined.
Since
\mathcal{G}
is transitive and has order
q^{km}-1
there is a bijection between the element of
\mathcal{G}
and the nonzero vector of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and hence a bijection between
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
and the points of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
.
Since
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\ker_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(G))=k-s
, it follows that
\iota
is
q^{(k-s)m}
-to-
1
. Moreover, a codeword
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{t})=(vG,vA_{2}^{\top}G,\ldots,vA_{t}^{\top}G)
has exactly
\frac{q^{(k-s)m}-1}{q^{m}-1}=\binom{k-s}{1}_{q^{m}}
zero entries since
vA_{i}^{T}
covers the non zero vectors of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, up to a scalar in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
. Since
\iota
is
q^{(k-s)m}
-to-
1
its image has size
(\frac{q^{km}-1}{q^{m}-1}-\frac{q^{(k-s)m}-1}{q^{m}-1})\cdot\frac{1}{q^{(k-s)m}}=\lvert\mathrm{PG}(\widetilde{\C},{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})\rvert
. It follows that
\supp_{\mathbf{n}}(c)
is obtained by rearranging
\binom{k-s}{1}_{q^{m}}
occurrences of the trivial space
\{0\}
together with
(\supp(\tilde{c}))_{\tilde{c}\in\mathrm{PG}(\widetilde{\C},{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})}
counted exactly
q^{(k-s)m}
times.
∎

Back to Example
6.7
, then one can see that
\widetilde{\C}
is the code whose generator matrix is

A\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\alpha^{2}&0\\
1&0&\alpha\end{pmatrix},

and the set of (representatives of) the projective codewords of
\widetilde{\C}
is

\left\{(\alpha,\alpha^{2},0),(1,0,\alpha),(\alpha^{2},\alpha^{2},\alpha),(0,\alpha^{2},\alpha^{2}),(1,\alpha^{2},1)\right\}.

We now observe that we can just restrict to study the
n
-simplex code obtained from the Singer subgroup
{\mathcal{H}}_{p}
. This is due to the following result, showing that all the
n
-simplex codes are equivalent to a standard
n
-simplex code. This is a consequence of the fact that all the Singer subgroups are conjugated.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
B\in\GL(k,q^{m})
and
\mathcal{G}=B{\mathcal{H}}_{p}B^{-1}
. Then
\C_{\mathcal{G},{\mathcal{U}}}
is equivalent to
\C_{{\mathcal{H}}_{p},\phi_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{1,n}(B,{\mathcal{U}})}
.

Proof.

We have that
\C_{\mathcal{G},{\mathcal{U}}}
and
\C_{{\mathcal{H}}_{p},\phi_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{1,n}(B,{\mathcal{U}})}
are equivalent if and only any of their associated
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
systems are equivalent. By definition of
n
-simplex codes we can take
(\phi_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{1,n}(A,{\mathcal{U}}))_{A\in\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}}
as
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
system associated to
\C_{\mathcal{G},{\mathcal{U}}}
. Thus,

\displaystyle(\phi_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{1,n}(A,{\mathcal{U}}))_{A\in\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}}

\displaystyle=(\phi_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{1,n}(A,{\mathcal{U}}))_{A\in B\overline{{\mathcal{H}}}_{p}B^{-1}}=(\phi_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{1,n}(BAB^{-1},{\mathcal{U}}))_{A\in\overline{{\mathcal{H}}}_{p}}

\displaystyle=(\phi_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{1,n}(AB^{-1},\phi_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{1,n}(B,{\mathcal{U}})))_{A\in\overline{{\mathcal{H}}}_{p}}=(\phi_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{1,n}(A,\phi_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{1,n}(B,{\mathcal{U}})))_{A\in\overline{{\mathcal{H}}}_{p}}\cdot B^{-1},

which is clearly equivalent to any
[\mathbf{n},k]_{q^{m}/q}
system associated to
\C_{{\mathcal{H}}_{p},\phi_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{1,n}(B,{\mathcal{U}})}
.
∎

As a consequence we obtain the following result on the number of inequivalent
n
-simplex codes.

Corollary 6.10
.

The number of inequivalent
n
-simplex codes is at most the number of orbits of the action
\phi_{{\mathcal{H}}_{p}}^{1,n}
.

Remark 6.11
.

The definition of
n
-simplex code generalizes those of simplex codes in the Hamming and in the rank metric. For the Hamming metric, we have
n=1
, and any transitive group
\mathcal{G}
clearly acts transitively also on the
1
-dimensional subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
(that is
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
). Thus, the only possibility for an orbital construction proposed in Theorem
6.2
is that the orbit is the full
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
, and therefore the only code obtained in this way is the
[\frac{q^{km}-1}{q^{m}-1},k]_{q^{m}}
simplex code.
If we try to recover a one-weight code in the rank metric from Theorem
6.2
, we need instead that
t=1
, that is the orbit of
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}^{r,s}
consists of only one subspace
{\mathcal{U}}
. Since
\mathcal{G}
is transitive, this is only possible when
{\mathcal{U}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, leading to the simplex code in the rank metric defined in
[
38
,
1
]
.

However, in contrast with the Hamming and the rank metric, in the sum-rank metric one-weight codes are not unique up to equivalence and repetition. As shown in Corollary
6.10
we may have many distinct inequivalent code with the same parameters. The number of such equivalence classes coincides with the number of orbits of the action
\phi_{{\mathcal{H}}_{p}}^{1,n}
. These orbits have been studied in various other contexts; see e.g.
[
16
]
.

Remark 6.12
.

Simplex codes in the sum-rank metric have already been introduced in
[
30
]
, but with a different meaning. Indeed, the definition given there generalizes the property of simplex codes in the Hamming metric of being the dual of the only nontrivial perfect codes with minimum distance
3
. Furthermore, those generalizations of simplex codes are not constant weight and it is not shown any systematic construction nor even their existence. What we are instead proposing here is to generalize simplex codes in the sum-rank metric with the aim of keeping the property of having only one nonzero weight. This leads to codes sharing also the geometric and group theoretical aspects with classical simplex codes. For these reasons, we believe that our generalization of simplex codes may have a deep mathematical meaning and it certainly deserves attention.

Remark 6.13
.

Consider
k
as an odd positive integer and
\mathcal{U}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{k}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}^{k}
and let
\theta
be the Singer cycle induced by a primitive element in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2k}}
. The orbit
\mathcal{O}
of
\mathcal{U}
under the action of the group
\mathcal{G}
generated by

has size
\frac{q^{k}+1}{q+1}
,
[

]
.
From a geometric point of view, the linear sets associated to the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
\mathcal{O}
form a partition of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{2})
in Baer subgeometries, which is known as
classical Baer subgeometry partition
, see
[
3
]
.
In
[
39
]
, it has been proved that a Baer subgeometry partition yields to a one-weight sum-rank metric code.
Even if the group
\mathcal{G}
is not transitive and hence the code associated with

is not an

-simplex, the orbit

still defines a one-weight code.
See
[

]
for other Baer subgeometry partitions which are not classical.

7.
Non-homogeneous case and multi-linear sets

In the above section, we have investigated one-weight sum-rank metric codes in which all the blocks have the same length.
In this section we deal with one-weight codes of dimension two with possibly different length of the blocks; for this reason we call them
non-homogeneous
. By Theorem
3.1
and by the definition of weight of a point with respect to a linear set, the property of being a one-weight code may be rephrased in geometric terms.

Let
\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
, where
\mathcal{U}_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
for every
i
. We define the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-multi-linear set

L_{\mathcal{U}}
as the set of points in
L_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup L_{\mathcal{U}_{t}}
and the
multi-weight

w_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}(P)
of a point
P\in\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
with respect to
L_{\mathcal{U}}
is

w_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}(P)\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{t}w_{L_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}}(P).

We say that an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-multi-linear set
L_{\mathcal{U}}
is
scattered
if

w_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}(P)\leq 1,

for every point
P\in\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})
.
If
L_{\mathcal{U}}
is a scattered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-multi-linear set then
L_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}
’s are pairwise disjoint and they also turn out to be scattered.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-degenerate
[\mathbf{n},2,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Let
\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})\in\Psi([\mathcal{C}])
. Then
\mathcal{C}
is one-weight if and only if

(5)

\ww_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}(P)=N-d,\mbox{ for each }P\in\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m}),

that is the linear sets associated with a non-degenerate one-weight code have to cover all the projective line and each point must have the same multi-weight.

7.1.
Two-dimensional one-weight MSRD codes

In this section we characterize those one-weight codes which are also MSRD codes and we provide bounds on
t
in such a case. The first part is a consequence of (
5
) and Theorem
2.4
.

Corollary 7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
be a
[\mathbf{n},2,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system.
Then
\Phi([\mathcal{U}])
is a class of one-weight MSRD code if and only if
L_{\mathcal{U}}
is a scattered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-multi-linear set and
L_{\mathcal{U}}=\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})
.

The above corollary extends the already known connection between MRD codes and scattered linear sets (see e.g.
[
40
,
37
]
).
Corollary
7.1
yields to a lower and an upper bound on the value
t
when the code considered is MSRD.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathcal{U}}}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t})
be a
[\mathbf{n},2,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system and let
\C\in\Phi([\mathcal{U}])
. Then,
\C
is a one-weight MSRD code if and only if

(6)

q^{m}+1=|L_{\mathcal{U}}|=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n_{i}}-1}{q-1}.

In particular, in this case we have

(7)

q+1\leq t\leq q^{m}+1

and
t\equiv 1\pmod{q}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
7.1
, we have that
w_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}(P)=1
, for each
P\in\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})
, that is
L_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}
is scattered and the
L_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}
’s are pairwise disjoint. Then by (
4
)
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathcal{U}_{i})=n_{i}\leq m
and
\lvert L_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}\rvert=\frac{q^{n_{i}}-1}{q-1}
. Since
\Phi([\mathcal{U}])
is a class of one-weight MSRD code, again by Corollary
7.1
, we have that

q^{m}+1=|L_{\mathcal{U}}|=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\,\,\lvert L_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}\rvert.

Then by (
3
) we have

q^{m}+1=|L_{\mathcal{U}}|=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n_{i}}-1}{q-1}\leq t\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1},

since
L_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}
is scattered for every
i
.
This implies that
t\geq q
. Now, if
t=q
, then we have

q^{m}+1=|L_{\mathcal{U}}|=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n_{i}}-1}{q-1}\equiv 0\pmod{q},

which is a contradiction.
So
t
must be different from
q
and hence
t\geq q+1
. The upper bound follows from the fact that the number of points in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})
is
q^{m}+1
. Finally, one gets
t\equiv 1\pmod{q}
directly from (
6
).
∎

Remark 7.3
.

We point out that the bounds on
t
obtained in the above result are sharp.
Indeed, the value of
t
in the system obtained from the doubly-extended linearized Reed-Solomon code
\mathcal{C}_{2,\mathrm{a},\boldsymbol{\beta}}^{\gamma,\delta}
reaches the lower bound of (
7
). On the other hand, if
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})=\{P_{1}=\langle v_{1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}},\ldots,P_{q^{m}+1}=\langle v_{q^{m}+1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\}
, then
\mathcal{U}_{1}=\langle v_{1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{q^{m}+1}=\langle v_{q^{m}+1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
and hence the value of
t
of such a construction reaches the upper bound in (
7
). This latter construction is the projective system associated to the doubly-extended Reed-Solomon code, and it is well-known to be the unique – up to equivalence –
2
-dimensional one-weight code in the Hamming metric; see
[
6
]
.

Thus, now we focus on one-weight MSRD codes whose number of blocks

is
q+1
, that is extremal codes meeting the lower bound in (
7
) with equality.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
\C
be an
[(n_{1},\ldots,n_{q+1}),2]_{q^{m}/q}
one-weight MSRD code. Then,

(1)

either
n_{1}=\ldots=n_{q-1}=m
and
n_{q}=n_{q+1}=1
,

(2)

or
q=2
,
n_{1}=n_{2}=m-1
and
n_{3}=2
.

Proof.

Let
\C
be an
[(n_{1},\ldots,n_{q+1}),2]_{q^{m}/q}
one-weight MSRD code and assume that
n_{q-1}\leq m-1
. Then by (
6
), we have

q^{m+1}-q^{m}+2q=\sum_{i=1}^{t}{q^{n_{i}}}\leq q^{m+1}-2q^{m}+3q^{m-1},

from which we obtain that
q^{m}+2q\leq 3q^{m-1}
. It is easy to see that this is only possible for
q=2
, and hence for
q\geq 3
the only possibility is the first case.

Now, let us suppose that
q=2
. In this case, (
6
) becomes

2^{n_{1}}+2^{n_{2}}+2^{n_{3}}=2^{m}+4.

If
n_{1}=m
then clearly we must have
n_{2}=n_{3}=1
and we get again the first case. Thus, assume that
n_{1}\leq m-1
. If
n_{2}\leq m-2
, then
2^{n_{1}}+2^{n_{2}}+2^{n_{3}}\leq 2^{m-1}+2^{m-2}+2^{m-2}
, which contradicts (
6
). Then, the only possibility is that
n_{1}=n_{2}=m-1
and
n_{3}=2
.
∎

Because of the existence of
2
-dimensional doubly-extended linearized Reed-Solomon codes, we know that case (1) in Theorem
7.4
is always possible. We now see that also case (2) is possible, providing a construction for every

.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let

be a positive integer with

and let
\mathcal{H}
be an
(m-1)
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-subspace of

For

\displaystyle=\{(x,x^{2})\,:\,x\in\mathcal{H}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}

\displaystyle=\langle(1,0),(0,\delta)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},

[(m-1,m-1,2),2,2m-1]_{2^{m}/2}

Proof.

By Corollary
7.1
, we need to prove that

is a scattered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-multi-linear set and

The
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}
points. Let
z=q^{m}+1-\frac{q-1}{r}\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}
and let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{z}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
such that

\{P_{1}=\langle v_{1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}},\ldots,P_{z}=\langle v_{z}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\}=\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})\setminus\{L_{\mathcal{U}}\}.

Let
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{i}=\langle v_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
for every
i\in[z]
and let
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{z})
.
As a consequence of Lemma
7.11
and of the definition of
\mathcal{U}_{i}
’s, we have that

w_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}(P)+w_{L_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}}}(P)=1,

for every point
P\in\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})
.
Therefore, we have the following result.

Corollary 7.14
.

Let
t=(q-1)/r
, where
r
is the smallest prime that divides
q-1
. Let
\mathcal{G}=\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{t}\}
be the subgroup of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
of order
t
. Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{t}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
be elements with distinct norm such that
\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(a_{i})\in\mathcal{G}
. Let
\eta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
such that
\mathrm{N}_{q^{m}/q}(\eta)\notin\mathcal{G}
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{i}=\{(y+\sigma^{2}(y)\eta N_{2}(a_{i}),a_{i}\sigma(y))\,:\,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}
.
Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{z}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
such that

\{P_{1}=\langle v_{1}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}},\ldots,P_{z}=\langle v_{z}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\}=\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})\setminus\{L_{\mathcal{U}}\}.

Let
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{i}=\langle v_{i}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
for every
i\in[z]
.
Then the class of the
[\mathbf{n},2,mt+z-1]_{q^{m}/q}
system
\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{t},\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{z})
, with
\mathbf{n}=(\underbrace{m,\ldots,m}_{t\mbox{ \scriptsize{times}}},\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{z\mbox{ \scriptsize{times}}})
, defines a class
\Phi([\mathcal{U}])
of non-degenerate one-weight MSRD
[\mathbf{n},2,mt+z-1]_{q^{m}/q}
codes.

7.2.
Lifting construction

We conclude this section by describing a procedure to construct one-weight sum-rank metric codes starting from a set of linear sets in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})
.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{s}
be
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}
, let
\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{s})
and consider
L_{\mathcal{U}}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-multi-linear set associated with
\mathcal{U}
.
Let

M=\max\left\{w_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}(P)\,:\,P\in\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})\right\}.

Denote by
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{U})
a vector of subspaces whose entries (rearranged in decreasing order according to their dimension) are

•

\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{s}
;

•

c
copies of
\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
and a
d
-dimensional subspace of
\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
, for any
P=\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\in\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})
, where
M-w_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}(P)=cm+d
with
c,d\in\mathbb{N}
and
d<m
.

We call
\mathcal{M}({\mathcal{U}})
the
lifting
of
\mathcal{U}
.
Clearly,
w_{L_{\mathcal{M}({\mathcal{U}})}}(P)=M
for every
P\in\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})
.

So, adding more copies of the points with weight less than the maximum value yields to the construction of one-weight sum-rank metric codes in the following way.

Corollary 7.15
.

Let
L_{\mathcal{U}_{1}},\ldots,L_{\mathcal{U}_{s}}
be
s

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear sets in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})
of rank
n_{1},\ldots,n_{s}
, respectively, and let
\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{s})
.
Let

M=\max\left\{w_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}(P)\,:\,P\in\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})\right\}.

Let
\ell=\sum_{P\in L_{\mathcal{U}}}(M-w_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}(P))
and
z=q^{m}+1-|L_{\mathcal{U}}|
.
The elements of an
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{U})
define an
[\mathbf{n},2,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system, with minimum distance
d=\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}+(z-1)M+\ell
.

In particular,
\Phi([\mathcal{M}({\mathcal{U}})])
is a class of non degenerate one-weight
[\mathbf{n},2,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-code.

Any code in
\Phi([\mathcal{M}({\mathcal{U}})])
will be called the
lifted code of

\mathcal{U}
.

Remark 7.16
.

We note that the above procedure can give constructions of one-weight codes which are not of the form in Section
6
.
Suppose that
L_{\mathcal{U}}\neq\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})
and
|L_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}|\geq 2
for every
i
, let
P
be a point in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})
not lying in
L_{\mathcal{U}}
and let
Q=\langle w\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
be such that
w_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}(Q)=M
.
Assume that
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{U})
is the concatenation of orbital constructions.
Then in the subspaces in the orbit of
\mathcal{U}_{P}
under the action of any transitive group
\mathcal{G}
should also cover at least one non-zero vectors in
\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\langle w\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
(counted with multiplicity). This would imply that
\mathcal{M}(L_{\mathcal{U}})(Q)\geq M+1
, a contradiction to the above corollary.

Example 7.17
.

Consider the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set defined by
\mathcal{U}=\{(y,\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(y))\,:\,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\}
, where
\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}
denotes the trace function of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
It is well-known that
L_{\mathcal{U}}
is a club, that is there is only one point of
L_{\mathcal{U}}
having weight
m-1
(which is the point
\langle(1,0)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
in this case) and all the remaining
q^{m-1}
points have weight one; see e.g.
[
15
]
.
So, according to the above described procedure, the entries of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{U})
(up to reordering) are:

•

\mathcal{U}
;

•

an
(m-2)
-dimension
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\langle(1,\eta)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
if
\langle(1,\eta)\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\in L_{\mathcal{U}}
and
\eta\neq 0
;

•

an
(m-1)
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
if
\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\notin L_{\mathcal{U}}
.

By Corollary
7.15
,
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{U})
defines a non degenerate one-weight code
[\mathbf{n},2,d]_{q^{m}/q}
-code, with

\mathbf{n}=(m,\underbrace{m-1,\ldots,m-1}_{q^{m}-q^{m-1}\mbox{ \scriptsize{times}}},\underbrace{m-2,\ldots,m-2}_{q^{m-1}\mbox{ \scriptsize{times}}})\mbox{ and }d=(m-2)q^{m-1}+(m-1)(q^{m}-q^{m-1})+1.

8.
Conclusions and open problems

In this paper we first introduce the notion of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system, extending the ones of
q
-systems and projective systems already known.
Then we have established a one-to-one correspondence between equivalence classes of
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems and equivalence classes of linear
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
sum-rank metric codes, which naturally extends the connections between equivalence classes of projective systems and linear Hamming-metric codes and the one between
q
-systems and linear rank metric codes.
Thank to this connection, we are able to introduce a new class of sum-rank metric codes which we call doubly-extended linearized Reed-Solomon code, since it is obtained by adding two blocks to a linearized Reed-Solomon code, similarly to the well-known class of doubly-extended Reed-Solomon codes in the Hamming metric. As one might expect, this is a family of MSRD codes and when they also have dimension
2
then they are also one-weight codes.
Since every
[\mathbf{n},k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system also defines a projective system, to a linear sum-rank metric code we can associate a linear Hamming-metric code. As a consequence of the well-known result by Bonisoli, we obtain some restrictions on the parameters of a linear sum-rank metric code with constant rank-profile.
Moreover, transitive groups have been used to construct sum-rank metric codes with constant rank-profile, this also yields to a definition of simplex code by making use of Singer subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}(k,q^{m})
. Such a definition of simplex code is different from the one presented by Martínez-Peñas, which was defined through the dual of nontrivial perfect codes with minimum distance
3
.
Finally, we investigate
2
-dimensional linear one-weight sum-rank metric codes by means of the linear sets associated with an
[\mathbf{n},2,d]_{q^{m}/q}
system, which we call
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-multi-linear sets. This enables us to provide bounds on the number of blocks of a one-weight MSRD code and to characterize those that have the smallest number of blocks. We can also enlarge the construction of twisted linearized Reed-Solomon codes to get families of one-weight MSRD codes. We then describe a procedure to construct one-weight sum-rank metric codes starting from any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-multi-linear set contained in the projective line.

We conclude the paper with some questions/problems that we think could be of interest for the reader.

Problem 8.1
.

Is it possible to say more about
extremal
one-weight sum-rank metric codes which are also MSRD codes? We have already shown in Theorem
7.4
that for two-dimensional one-weight MSRD codes with exactly
q+1
blocks, the block-lengths are uniquely determined for
q\geq 3
. Can this result be generalized to
k
-dimensional one-weight MSRD codes for
k\geq 3
?

Problem 8.2
.

The minimum distance of a
\mathbf{n}
-simplex code constructed in Section
6
through a subspace
\mathcal{U}
and a Singer subgroup
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathrm{GL}(k,q^{m})
, depends on the total weight of the rank-metric codes associated with
\mathcal{U}
. It is therefore of interest to study the total weight of rank-metric codes.

Problem 8.3
.

The
2
-dimensional one-weight MSRD codes obtained from Theorem
7.5
, called
2
-fold linearized Reed-Solomon, are special constructions occurring only when
q=2
and which, to the best of our knowledge, cannot be recovered from known constructions.
Can these examples be extended to higher dimensions?

Problem 8.4
.

In Section
7.2
, we describe a geometric construction for linear one-weight sum-rank metric codes of dimension
2
called the lifted code of
\mathcal{U}
. Can be this procedure extended in order to construct linear one-weight sum-rank metric codes of any dimension?

Dense sumsets of Sidon sequences
Sándor Z. Kiss, Csaba Sándor

Abstract
Let k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 be an integer. We say a set A A of positive integers is an asymptotic basis of order k k if every large enough positive integer can be represented as the sum of k k terms from A A . A set of positive integers A A is called Sidon set if all the two terms sums formed by the elements of A A are different. Many years ago P. Erdős, A. Sárközy and V. T. Sós asked whether there exists a Sidon set which is asymptotic basis of order 3 3 . In this paper we prove the existence of a Sidon set A A with positive lower density of the three fold sumset A + A + A A+A+A by using probabilistic methods. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 11B34, 11B75. Keywords and phrases: additive number theory, general sequences, additive representation function, Sidon sets.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of positive integers. Let

. Moreover, for sets

,

Define the lower asymptotic density of a set of natural numbers by

\underline{d}(A)=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{A(n)}{n},

and the upper asymptotic density by

\overline{d}(A)=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{A(n)}{n}.

The density of a set of natural numbers is defined by

d(A)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{A(n)}{n},

whenever the limit is exist.

In this paper we prove the existence of a Sidon set such that the lower asymptotic density of its three fold sumset is positive. Namely, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

There exists a Sidon set
A
with
\underline{d}(A+A+A)>0.064
.

It was proved in
[
8
]
, p.89 that if
A
is a Sidon set, then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{A(n)}{\sqrt{\frac{n}{\log n}}}<\infty,

which implies that
\underline{d}(A+A)=0
.

It is well known
[
8
]
, p.91-95 that there exists a Sidon set
A
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{A(n)}{\sqrt{n}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},

which gives
\overline{d}(A+A)\geq\frac{1}{8}
. On the other hand, if
A
is a Sidon set, then
A(n)\leq(1+o(1))\sqrt{n}
, which implies that

(A+A)(n)\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\right)n.

It follows that
\overline{d}(A+A)\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
Now, we have

\frac{1}{8}\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subset\mathbb{N}\\
A\textnormal{ is Sidon }\end{subarray}}\overline{d}(A+A)\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Problem 2
.

Do there exist constants
\frac{1}{8}<c_{1}<c_{2}<\frac{1}{2}
such that

\displaystyle c_{1}\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subset\mathbb{N}\\
A\textnormal{ is Sidon }\end{subarray}}\overline{d}(A+A)\leq c_{2}?

Furthermore, it is natural to study the upper density of the three fold sumset of a Sidon set. The following problem is weaker than Problem 1, but perhaps it is not hopeless to solve.

Problem 3
.

Does there exist a Sidon set
A
such that
\overline{d}(A+A+A)=1
?

Probabilistic tools

The proof of Theorem 1 is based on the probabilistic method due to Erdős and
Rényi. There is an excellent summary of this method in the Halberstam - Roth
book
[

Let
\Omega

positive integers. In this paper we denote the probability of an event

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
, and the expectation of a random
variable

\mathbb{E}(\zeta)

Lemma 1
.

Let

\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\dots{}

be real numbers satisfying

0\leq\alpha_{n}\leq 1\hskip 11.38109pt(n=1,2,\dots{}).

Then there exists a probability space (
\Omega

,

) with the following two properties:

(i)

For every natural number

\mathcal{E}^{(n)}=\{\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{A}\in\Omega

is measurable, and

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}^{(n)})=\alpha_{n}
.

(ii)

The events
\mathcal{E}^{(1)}
,
\mathcal{E}^{(2)}
, … are independent.

See Theorem 13. in
[
8
]
, p. 142.
We denote the
characteristic function of the event
\mathcal{E}^{(n)}
by
t_{(\mathcal{A},n)}
or we can say the boolean random variable means that:

t_{(\mathcal{A},n)}=t_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 1\textnormal{, if }n\in\mathcal{A}\\
0\textnormal{, if }n\notin\mathcal{A}.\end{aligned}\hskip 8.53581pt\right.

Furthermore, we denote the number of solutions of

a_{i_{1}}+a_{i_{2}}+\dots{}+a_{i_{k}}=n
by
r_{k}(\mathcal{A},n)
,
where

a_{i_{1}}\in\mathcal{A}
,
a_{i_{2}}\in\mathcal{A}
, …,
a_{i_{k}}\in\mathcal{A}
,
1\leq a_{i_{1}}<a_{i_{2}}\dots{}<a_{i_{k}}<n
.
Thus

r_{k}(\mathcal{A},n)=\sum_{\overset{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots{},a_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}{1\leq a_{1}<\dots{}<a_{k}<n}\atop{a_{1}+a_{2}+\dots{}+a_{k}=n}}t_{(\mathcal{A},a_{1})}t_{(\mathcal{A},a_{2})}\dots{}t_{(\mathcal{A},a_{k})}.

It is easy to see that
r_{k}(\mathcal{A},n)
is the sum of random
variables. However for
k>2
these variables are not independent because the
same
t_{(\mathcal{A},a_{i})}
may appear in many terms. To overcome this
problem we need deeper probabilistic tools.

Our proof is based on a method of J. H. Kim and V. H. Vu. In the next section
we give a short survey of this method. Interested reader can find more
details in
[

]
,
[
13
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
.
Assume that
t_{1},t_{2},\dots{},t_{n}
are
independent binary (i.e., all
t_{i}
’s are in
\{0,1\}
) random variables.
Consider a polynomial
Y=Y(t_{1},t_{2},\dots{},t_{n})
in
t_{1},t_{2},\dots{},t_{n}
with degree
k
(where the degree of this polynomial equals
to the maximum of the sum of the exponents of the monomials.) We say a polynomial
Y
is totally positive if it can be
written in the form
Y=\sum_{i}e_{i}\Gamma_{i}
, where the
e_{i}
’s are
positive and
\Gamma_{i}
is a product of some
t_{j}
’s.
Moreover,
Y
is regular if all of its coefficients are between zero and one.
We also say
Y

is simplified, if all of its monomials are square-free
(i.e. do not contain any factor of
t_{i}^{2}
),
and homogeneous if all the monomials have the same
degree. Thus for instance a boolean polynomial is automatically regular and
simplified, though not necessarily homogeneous.
Given any multi-index

\underline{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\dots{},\eta_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we define the partial derivative
\partial^{\underline{\eta}}(Y)
of

Y
by

\partial^{\underline{\eta}}(Y)=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}\right)^{\eta_{1}}\cdots{}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{n}}\right)^{\eta_{n}}Y(t_{1},t_{2},\dots{},t_{n}),

and denote the order of
\underline{\eta}
as
|\underline{\eta}|=\eta_{1}+\dots{}+\eta_{n}
. For any order
d\geq 0
, we denote
\mathbb{E}_{d}(Y)=\max_{\eta:|\eta|=d}\mathbb{E}(\partial^{\underline{\eta}}(Y))
. Then
\mathbb{E}_{0}(Y)=\mathbb{E}(Y)
and
\mathbb{E}_{d}(Y)=0
if
d
exceeds the degree of
Y
.
Define
\mathbb{E}_{\geq d}(Y)=\max_{d^{{}^{\prime}}\geq d}\mathbb{E}_{d^{{}^{\prime}}}(Y)
.
The following result was proved by Kim and Vu.

Lemma 2
.

(J. H. Kim - V. H. Vu)
For every positive integer
k
and
Y=Y(t_{1},t_{2},\dots{},t_{n})
totally
positive polynomial of degree
k
, where
the
t_{i}
’s are independent binary random variables, and for any

\lambda>0
there exists a constant
d_{k}>0
depending only on
k
such
that

\mathbb{P}\left(|Y-\mathbb{E}(Y)|\geq d_{k}\lambda^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{\geq 0}(Y)\mathbb{E}_{\geq 1}(Y)}\right)=O_{k}\left(e^{-\lambda/4+(k-1)\log n}\right).

See
[
15
]
for the proof. Informally, this theorem states that
when the derivatives of
Y
are smaller on average than
Y
itself, and the degree of
Y
is small, then
Y
is concentrated
around its mean. Finally we need the Borel - Cantelli lemma:

Lemma 3
.

(Borel - Cantelli)
Let
\{B_{i}\}
be a sequence of events in a probability space. If

\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\mathbb{P}(B_{j})<\infty,

then with probability 1, at most a finite number of the events

can occur.

This is Theorem 7. in
[
8
]
, p. 135.

Proof of Theorem 4

3.1
Outline of the proof

Let
0<c<1
. Define the random sequence
S
by
\mathbb{P}(n\in S)=\frac{c}{n^{2/3}}
for every positive integer
n
. Let
T
be the set

T=\{s\in S:\exists s^{{}^{\prime}},s{{}^{\prime\prime}},s^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}<s,s^{{}^{\prime}},s{{}^{\prime\prime}},s^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}\in S,s+s^{{}^{\prime}}=s{{}^{\prime\prime}}+s^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}\}.

Then
S\setminus T
is clearly a Sidon set. On the other hand,

(S+S+S)(N)\leq((S\setminus T)+(S\setminus T)+(S\setminus T))(N)+(S+S+T)(N),

thus we have

((S\setminus T)+(S\setminus T)+(S\setminus T))(N)\geq(S+S+S)(N)-(S+S+T)(N).

It was proved by Goguel
[
7

with probability 1, where

\overline{d}(S+S+T)\leq 1.8\Gamma(1/3)^{3}c^{6},

(1)

Then, with probability 1,

\underline{d}((S\setminus T)+(S\setminus T)+(S\setminus T))\geq 1-e^{c^{3}\frac{\Gamma(1/3)^{3}}{6}}-1.8\Gamma(1/3)^{3}c^{6}.

It is easy to check that
\max_{0\leq c\leq 1}1-e^{c^{3}\frac{\Gamma(1/3)^{3}}{6}}-1.8\Gamma(1/3)^{3}c^{6}\geq 0.064
, which proves the statement.

To prove (1), we need an upper estimation to

. To do this, we verify the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Let

be sequences of positive integers with

and

as

. Then

\sum_{n\leq N}R_{A+B}(n)\leq\left(\frac{ab\alpha\beta\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}{(\alpha+\beta)\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}+o(1)\right)N^{\alpha+\beta}\text{ as }N\rightarrow\infty.

Furthermore, it was proved
[
7
]
that with probability 1,

S(N)=(3c+o(1))N^{1/3}

(2)

as
N\rightarrow\infty
.
Next, we show that almost surely

T(N)\leq(10.8c^{4}+o(1))N^{1/3}

(3)

as
N\rightarrow\infty
. If
n\in S+S
and
\frac{n}{2}\notin S
, then

. By Lemma 4 and (2) we have

(S+S)(N)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{n\leq N}R_{S+S}(n)+S\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)\right)\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{(3c)(3c)\cdot\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{3}\Gamma(\frac{1}{3})\Gamma(\frac{1}{3})}{(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{3})\Gamma(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{3})}+o(1)\right)N^{\frac{2}{3}}

as

. By using Lemma 4 and (3), one get that with probability 1,

(S+S+T)(N)\leq\left(\frac{\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{3})^{2}}{\Gamma(\frac{2}{3})}\cdot c^{2}\cdot 10.8c^{4}\cdot\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{2}{3}\Gamma(\frac{1}{3})\Gamma(\frac{2}{3})}{(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2}{3})\Gamma(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2}{3})}+o(1)\right)N

Proof of Lemma 4

Let
\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3}>0
and
A=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots{},\}
,
(1\leq a_{1}<a_{2}<\dots{})

. Then there exists an

\sum_{n\leq N}R_{A+B}(n)=\sum_{a_{n}<N}B(N-a_{n})\leq N_{1}(\varepsilon_{1})B(N)+\sum_{n:\left(\frac{1}{a^{1/\alpha}}-\varepsilon_{1}\right)n^{1/\alpha}<N}B\left(N-\left(\frac{1}{a^{1/\alpha}}-\varepsilon_{1}\right)n^{1/\alpha}\right).

There exists an
N_{2}(\varepsilon_{2})

B(n)<(b+\varepsilon_{2})n^{\beta}
.
This implies that

\sum_{n\leq N}R_{A+B}(n)\leq O(B(N))+(b+\varepsilon_{2})\sum_{n:\left(\frac{1}{a^{1/\alpha}}-\varepsilon_{1}\right)n^{1/\alpha}<N}\left(N-\left(\frac{1}{a^{1/\alpha}}-\varepsilon_{1}\right)n^{1/\alpha}\right)^{\beta}

(4)

as

\mathbb{E}_{1}=O\left(\sum_{x_{1}=1}^{N}\frac{1}{x_{1}^{2/3}}\sum_{1\leq x_{4}\leq x_{1}}\frac{1}{x_{4}^{2/3}}\cdot\frac{1}{x_{1}^{2/3}}\right)=O\left(\sum_{x_{1}=1}^{N}\frac{1}{x_{1}^{4/3}}\sum_{1\leq x_{4}\leq x_{1}}\frac{1}{x_{4}^{2/3}}\right)

Consider the open set
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}

Multi-linear products with odd factors in pseudo-differential calculus with symbols in modulation spaces
Joachim Toft

Abstract
. We give sufficient conditions on the Lebesgue exponents for compositions of odd numbers of pseudo-differential operators with symbols in modulation spaces. As a byproduct, we obtain sufficient conditions for twisted convolutions of odd numbers of factors to be bounded on Wiener amalgam spaces.

We observe that (

to certain regularization techniques for linear operators in

In fact, a convenient way to regularizing operators might be to
enclose them with regularizing operators. For example,
suppose that
T

from the Schwartz space
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to the
set of tempered distributions
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

The operator

\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\begin{gathered}(K_{1},K_{2},K_{3})\mapsto K_{1}\circ K_{2}\circ K_{3},\\[4.30554pt]
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\times\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\times\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\to\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}),\end{gathered}

\displaystyle K_{1}\circ K_{2}\circ K_{3}(x,y)

\displaystyle F(x,y,x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=(K_{1}\otimes K_{3})(x,x_{1},x_{2},y)=K_{1}(x,x_{1})K_{3}(x_{2},y).

The asserted continuity then follows from the facts
that

Recall that the Weyl product
\#

1.
Preliminaries

In this section we introduce notation and discuss the background on
Gelfand–Shilov spaces,
pseudo-differential operators, the Weyl
product, twisted convolution and modulation spaces.
Most proofs can be found in the literature and are therefore omitted.

Let
0<h,s\in\mathbf{R}
be fixed. The space
\mathcal{S}_{s,h}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

consists of all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
such that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{S}_{s,h}}\equiv\sup\frac{|x^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}f(x)|}{h^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}\alpha!^{s}\,\beta!^{s}}

is finite, with supremum taken over all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{N}^{d}
and
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

The space
\mathcal{S}_{s,h}\subseteq\mathscr{S}
(
\mathscr{S}
denotes the Schwartz space) is a Banach space
which increases with
h
and
s
.
Inclusions between topological spaces are understood to be continuous.
If
s>1/2
, or
s=1/2
and
h
is sufficiently large, then
\mathcal{S}_{s,h}
contains all finite linear combinations of Hermite functions.
Since the space of such linear combinations is dense in
\mathscr{S}
, it follows
that the topological dual
(\mathcal{S}_{s,h})^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
of
\mathcal{S}_{s,h}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is
a Banach space which contains
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.

1.1.
Gelfand-Shilov spaces of functions and distributions

The
Gelfand–Shilov spaces

\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and

\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
(cf.
[
13
]
) are the inductive and projective limits, respectively,
of
\mathcal{S}_{s,h}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, with respect to the parameter
h
. Thus

\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\bigcup_{h>0}\mathcal{S}_{s,h}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\quad\text{and}\quad\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\bigcap_{h>0}\mathcal{S}_{s,h}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),

(1.1)

where
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is equipped with the the strongest topology such
that the inclusion map from
\mathcal{S}_{s,h}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
into
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

is continuous, for every choice of
h>0
. The space
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is a
Fréchet space with seminorms

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\mathcal{S}_{s,h}}
,
h>0
. We have
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\neq\{0\}

if and only if
s>1/2
, and
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\neq\{0\}

if and only if
s\geq 1/2
. From now on we assume that
s>1/2
when we
consider
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and
s\geq 1/2
when we consider
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.

The
Gelfand–Shilov distribution spaces

\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

and
\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
are the projective and inductive limit
respectively of
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. This means that

\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\bigcap_{h>0}\mathcal{S}_{s,h}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\quad\text{and}\quad\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\bigcup_{h>0}\mathcal{S}_{s,h}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d}).

(
1.1
)
′

In
[
13
,
19
,
21
]
it is proved that
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

is the topological dual of
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and
\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

is the topological dual of
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.

For each
\varepsilon>0
and
s>1/2
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1/2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\subseteq\Sigma_{s+\varepsilon}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

(1.2)

\displaystyle\quad\text{and}\quad\Sigma_{s+\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{1/2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d}).

The Gelfand–Shilov spaces are invariant under several basic operations,
e. g. translations, dilations, tensor products
and (partial) Fourier transformation.

We normalize the Fourier transform of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
as

(\mathscr{F}f)(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)\equiv(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\,dx,

where
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
denotes the scalar
product on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
. The map
\mathscr{F}
extends
uniquely to homeomorphisms on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

and
\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and restricts to
homeomorphisms on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and

\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and to a unitary operator on
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.

1.2.
Modulation spaces

Next we turn to the basic properties of modulation spaces, and start by
recalling the conditions for the involved weight functions. Let

0<\omega\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Then
\omega
is called

moderate
if there is a function
0<v\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

such that

\omega(x+y)\lesssim\omega(x)v(y),\quad x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(1.3)

Then
\omega
is also called
v
-moderate
.
Here the notation
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
means that there exists
C>0

such that
f(x)\leq Cg(x)
for all arguments
x
in the domain of
f

and
g
. If
f\lesssim g
and
g\lesssim f
we write
f\asymp g
.
The function
v
is called
submultiplicative

if it is even and (
1.3
) holds when
\omega=v
. We note that if
(
1.3
) holds then

v^{-1}\lesssim\omega\lesssim v.

For such
\omega
it follows that (
1.3
) is true when

v(x)=Ce^{c|x|},

for some positive constants
c
and
C
(cf.
[
15
]
).
In particular, if
\omega
is moderate on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
, then

e^{-c|x|}\lesssim\omega(x)\lesssim e^{c|x|},

for some
c>0
.

The set of all moderate functions on
\mathbf{R}^{d}

is denoted by
\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
If
v
in (
1.3
)
can be chosen as
v(x)=\langle x\rangle^{s}=(1+|x|^{2})^{s/2}
for some

s\geq 0
, then
\omega
is
said to be of polynomial type or polynomially moderate. We let

\mathscr{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
be the set
of all polynomially moderate functions on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\setminus 0
be
fixed. The
short-time Fourier transform
(STFT)
V_{\phi}f
of
f\in\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
with respect to the
window function

\phi
is
the Gelfand–Shilov distribution on
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
defined by

V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)\equiv\mathscr{F}(f\,\overline{\phi(\,\cdot\,-x)})(\xi).

For
a\in\mathcal{S}_{1/2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
and

\Phi\in\mathcal{S}_{1/2}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\setminus 0
the

symplectic short-time Fourier transform

\mathcal{V}_{\Phi}a

of
a
with respect to
\Phi
is the defined similarly as

\mathcal{V}_{\Phi}a(X,Y)=\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}\big(a\,\overline{\Phi(\,\cdot\,-X)}\big)(Y),\quad X,Y\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}.

We have

\mathcal{V}_{\Phi}a(X,Y)=2^{d}V_{\Phi}a(x,\xi,-2\eta,2y),\\[4.30554pt]
X=(x,\xi)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d},\ Y=(y,\eta)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d},

(1.4)

which shows the close connection between
V_{\Phi}a

and
\mathcal{V}_{\Phi}a
.
The Wigner distribution
W_{f,\phi}
and
V_{\phi}f
are also closely related.

If
f,\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and
a,\Phi\in\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
then

\displaystyle V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int f(y)\overline{\phi(y-x)}e^{-i\langle y,\xi\rangle}\,dy

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\Phi}a(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\pi^{-d}\int a(Z)\overline{\Phi(Z-X)}e^{2i\sigma(Y,Z)}\,dZ.

Let
\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]

and
\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{1/2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\setminus 0
be fixed. The

modulation space

M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
consists of
all
f\in\mathcal{S}_{1/2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}

\displaystyle\equiv\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)\omega(x,\xi)|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{q/p}\,d\xi\Big)^{1/q}

(1.5)

is finite, and the Wiener amalgam
space
W^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
consists of all
f\in\mathcal{S}_{1/2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}

\displaystyle\equiv\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)\omega(x,\xi)|^{q}\,d\xi\Big)^{p/q}\,dx\Big)^{1/p}

(1.6)

is finite (with obvious modifications in (
1.5
) and
(
1.6
) when
p=\infty
or
q=\infty
).

Remark 1.1
.

As follows from Proposition
1.3
(2) below we have that in fact

contains the superspace
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

of
\mathcal{S}_{1/2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and is contained in the subspace
\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

of
\mathcal{S}_{1/2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, when
\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
. Hence we
could from the beginning have assumed that

in
(
1.5
) and (
1.6
).

On the other hand, in
[
30
]
, certain weight classes containing
\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
and superexponential weights are introduced. For any

,
the corresponding families of modulation spaces are large enough to contain

\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and subspaces of

However, we are not dealing with these large families of modulation spaces
because we need (1) and (2) in Proposition

which are not known to be
true for weights of this generality.

Remark 1.2
.

The literature contains slightly different conventions
concerning modulation and Wiener amalgam spaces. Sometimes our
definition of a Wiener amalgam space is considered as a particular
case of a general class of modulation spaces (cf.

[
5
,
6
,
7

adapted to give the relation (

purpose to transfer continuity for the Weyl product on
modulation spaces to continuity for twisted convolution on Wiener
amalgam spaces.

On the even-dimensional phase space
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
we may define
modulation spaces based on the symplectic STFT.
Thus if
\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{4d})
,

and
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}_{1/2}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\setminus 0
are fixed, then
the
symplectic modulation spaces

\mathcal{M}^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
and Wiener amalgam spaces
\mathcal{W}^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
are obtained by replacing
the STFT
a\mapsto V_{\Phi}a
by the corresponding
symplectic version
a\mapsto\mathcal{V}_{\Phi}a
in (
1.5
) and
(
1.6
).
(Sometimes the word
symplectic
before modulation space is
omitted for brevity.)
By (
1.4
) we have

\mathcal{M}^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=M^{p,q}_{(\omega_{0})}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}),\quad\omega(x,\xi,y,\eta)=\omega_{0}(x,\xi,-2\eta,2y).

It follows that all properties which are valid for
M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}

carry over to
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}_{(\omega)}
.

From

V_{\widehat{\phi}}\widehat{f}(\xi,-x)=e^{i\langle x,\xi\rangle}V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)

(1.7)

it follows that

f\in W^{q,p}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\widehat{f}\in M^{p,q}_{(\omega_{0})}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\qquad\omega_{0}(\xi,-x)=\omega(x,\xi).

In the symplectic situation these formulas read

\mathcal{V}_{\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}\Phi}(\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}a)(X,Y)=e^{2i\sigma(Y,X)}\mathcal{V}_{\Phi}a(Y,X)

(1.8)

and

\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}\mathcal{M}^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})=\mathcal{W}^{q,p}_{(\omega_{0})}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}),\qquad\omega_{0}(X,Y)=\omega(Y,X).

(1.9)

For brevity we denote
\mathcal{M}^{p}_{(\omega)}=\mathcal{M}^{p,p}_{(\omega)}
,
\mathcal{W}^{p}_{(\omega)}=\mathcal{W}^{p,p}_{(\omega)}
, and when

Next we recall some basic facts from pseudo-differential calculus
(cf.
[
18

\mathbf{M}(d,\Omega)
be the set of
all
d\times d
matrices with entries in
\Omega
,

s\geq 1/2
,

, and

be fixed. Then the
pseudo-differential operator
\operatorname{Op}_{A}(a)

\operatorname{Op}_{A}(a)f(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x-A(x-y),\xi)f(y)e^{i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}\,dyd\xi

is a linear and continuous operator on
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

For

the
pseudo-differential operator
\operatorname{Op}_{A}(a)
is defined as the continuous
operator from
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
with
distribution kernel given by

K_{a,A}(x,y)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}(\mathscr{F}_{2}^{-1}a)(x-A(x-y),x-y).

(1.11)

Here
\mathscr{F}_{2}F
is the partial Fourier transform of
F(x,y)\in\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
with respect to the variable
y\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
. This
definition generalizes (
1.10
) and is well defined, since the mappings

\mathscr{F}_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad F(x,y)\mapsto F(x-A(x-y),y-x)

(1.12)

are homeomorphisms on
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.
The map
a\mapsto K_{a,A}
is hence a homeomorphism on

\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.

If
A=0
, then
\operatorname{Op}_{A}(a)
is the standard or Kohn-Nirenberg representation

a(x,D)
. If instead
A=\frac{1}{2}I
, then
\operatorname{Op}_{A}(a)
agrees
with the Weyl operator or Weyl quantization
\operatorname{Op}^{w}(a)
.

For any
K\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})
, let

be the
linear and continuous mapping from
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}})

to
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{2}})

(T_{K}f,g)_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{2}})}=(K,g\otimes\overline{f})_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})},\quad f\in\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}}),\quad g\in\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{2}}).

(1.13)

It is a well known consequence of the Schwartz kernel
theorem that if
t\in\mathbf{R}
, then
K\mapsto T_{K}
and
a\mapsto\operatorname{Op}_{A}(a)
are bijective mappings from
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

to the space of linear and continuous mappings from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to

\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
(cf. e. g.
[
18
]
).

Likewise the maps
K\mapsto T_{K}

and
a\mapsto\operatorname{Op}_{A}(a)
are uniquely extendable to bijective
mappings from
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
to the set of linear and
continuous mappings from
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

the kernel theorem
[

[

The other assertion follows from the fact that

is a homeomorphism on
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.

In particular, for each

and

[

and since the map

\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

\displaystyle|(V_{\Phi}

\displaystyle K_{a,A})(x,y,\xi,-\eta)|

(1.16)

List Packing and Correspondence Packing of Planar Graphs
Daniel W. Cranston, Evelyne Smith-Roberge

Abstract
For a graph G G and a list assignment L L with | L ⁡ ( v ) | = k |L(v)|=k for all v v , an L L -packing consists of L L -colorings φ 1 , ⋯ , φ k \varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{k} such that φ i ​ ( v ) ≠ φ j ​ ( v ) \varphi_{i}(v)\neq\varphi_{j}(v) for all v v and all distinct i , j ∈ { 1 , … , k } i,j\in\{1,\ldots,k\} . Let χ ℓ ⋆ ​ ( G ) \chi^{\star}_{\ell}(G) denote the smallest k k such that G G has an L L -packing for every L L with | L ⁡ ( v ) | = k |L(v)|=k for all v v . Let 𝒫 k \mathcal{P}_{k} denote the set of all planar graphs with girth at least k k . We show that (i) χ ℓ ⋆ ​ ( G ) ⩽ 8 \chi^{\star}_{\ell}(G)\leqslant 8 for all G ∈ 𝒫 3 G\in\mathcal{P}_{3} and (ii) χ ℓ ⋆ ​ ( G ) ⩽ 5 \chi^{\star}_{\ell}(G)\leqslant 5 for all G ∈ 𝒫 4 G\in\mathcal{P}_{4} and (iii) χ ℓ ⋆ ​ ( G ) ⩽ 4 \chi^{\star}_{\ell}(G)\leqslant 4 for all G ∈ 𝒫 5 G\in\mathcal{P}_{5} . Part (i) makes progress on a problem of Cambie, Cames van Batenburg, Davies, and Kang. We also consider the analogue of χ ℓ ⋆ \chi^{\star}_{\ell} for correspondence coloring, χ c ⋆ \chi^{\star}_{c} . In fact, all bounds stated above for χ ℓ ⋆ \chi^{\star}_{\ell} also hold for χ c ⋆ \chi^{\star}_{c} . Keywords: list-coloring, list-packing, correspondence coloring, planar graphs

Lemma 9

Fix a positive integer

and also

.

Proof.

To prove (1), consider an arbitrary edge

Now we prove (2). By Hall’s Theorem, if

, we know

(8,3)
-bigraphs and
(8,4)
-bigraphs. To focus on the proof of our main result, we defer most of these lemmas

Definition 16

In an
(8,3)
-bigraph

•

|N(X)|=3

By Hall’s Theorem, we assume

By Proposition

Lemma 20
.

Let
H
be a bigraph with
|A|\in\{2k,2k+1\}
, for some positive integer
k
.
Denote the vertices in
A
and
B
by, respectively,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{|A|}
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{|A|}
where
d(a_{i})\leqslant d(a_{i+1})
and
d(b_{i})\leqslant d(b_{i+1})
for all
i\in[|A|-1]
. Suppose
d(a_{i})\geqslant\min\{i-1,k\}
and

d(b_{i})\geqslant\min\{i-1,|B|-k\}
for all
i\in[|A|]
.

(1)

Now
H
has a 1-factor unless there exists
i^{\prime}\in[k]
such that
|N(\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i^{\prime}}\})|=i^{\prime}-1
or
there exists
i^{\prime}\in[|B|-k]
such that
|N(\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{i^{\prime}}\})|=i^{\prime}-1
.

(2)

In particular,
H
has a 1-factor if
d(a_{i})\geqslant i
and
d(b_{i})\geqslant i
for all
i\in[k]
.

Proof.

Note that (2) follows immediately from (1), so we prove (1).
By Hall’s Theorem, it suffices to show that
|N(X)|\geqslant|X|
for all
X\subseteq A

unless there exists some exceptional set
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i^{\prime}}\}
or
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{i^{\prime}}\}
as in (1).
Let
i:=|X|
. First suppose that
i\leqslant k
.
If
X\neq\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}\}
, then
X
contains
a_{\ell}
for some
\ell>i
, so
|N(X)|\geqslant d(a_{\ell})\geqslant\min\{\ell-1,k\}\geqslant i=|X|
, and we are done. If instead
X=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}\}
, then either
|N(X)|=i-1
or
|N(X)|\geqslant i
; but in each case we are
done. So suppose instead that
i\geqslant k+1
. For all
j\geqslant|A|-i+2
, by Pigeonhole we have

|N(b_{j})\cap X|\geqslant|X|+((|A|-i+2)-1)-|A|=1
. Thus,
b_{j}\in N(X)
.
If there exists
b_{j^{\prime}}
such that
j^{\prime}\leqslant|A|-i+1
and
b_{j^{\prime}}\in N(X)
,
then
|N(X)|\geqslant|B|-((|B|-i+2)-1)+1=|B|-|B|+i-2+1+1=i=|X|
.
Otherwise,
|N(\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{|A|-i+1}\})|=|A|-i
; and again we are done.

(8,3)
-bigraph with an obstruction X having type

Proof.

.
Now

.

.

.

.

, then

k\geqslant 4
.

|X^{\prime}\cap X|=k

.

6
Open Problems

We suggest the following open problems for further study. For a graph class
\mathcal{G}
,
let
\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}):=\max_{G\in\mathcal{G}}\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(G)
and let
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{G}):=\max_{G\in\mathcal{G}}\chi^{\star}_{c}(G)
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{g}
denote the class of planar graphs with girth at least
g
and, for each positive real
a
,
let
\mathcal{G}_{a}
denote the class of graphs
G
with
\textrm{mad}(G)<a
.

1.

For each integer
g\in\{3,4\}
, determine
\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(\mathcal{P}_{g})
and
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{P}_{g})
. Our main results are that
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{P}_{3})\leqslant 8

and
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{P}_{4})\leqslant 5
and
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{P}_{5})\leqslant 4
. In
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
, the authors present constructions showing
that
\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(\mathcal{P}_{g})=\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{P}_{g})=4
for all
g\geqslant 5
.

2.

For each positive real number
a
, determine
\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}_{a})
and
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{G}_{a})
.
(In fact our proof that
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{P}_{4})\leqslant 5
actually proves that
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{G}_{4})\leqslant 5
.)
Conversely, given a positive integer
k
, determine the maximum values
a(k)
and
b(k)
such that
\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}_{a(k)})\leqslant k

and
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{G}_{b(k)})\leqslant k
.
Note that
a(k)\geqslant b(k)
for all
k
, since every bad
k
-assignment gives rise to a bad
k
-cover.
Here is a perhaps easier question: What are
\limsup_{k\to\infty}a(k)/k
and
\limsup_{k\to\infty}b(k)/k
?
Corollary
6
shows that
b(k)/k\geqslant 1/2
for all
k
. We can improve this somewhat as
follows.

For each integer
k\geqslant 3
, we have
b(2k-1)\geqslant k+k/(k+1)
. Consider a graph
G
and a
(2k-1)
-cover
(\vec{D},\sigma)

such that
G
has no
(\vec{D},\sigma)
-packing, but every proper subgraph does. By Corollary
6
, we
know
\delta(G)\geqslant k
. We use discharging with initial charge
d(v)
for all
v
.
We use a single discharging rule: each
k
-vertex takes
1/(k+1)
from each neighbor.
Suppose
d(v)=k
. By Lemma
11
, we know
d(w)\geqslant k+2
for all
w\in N(v)
. Now
v
finishes with charge

k+k/(k+1)
. If
d(v)=k+1
, then
v
starts and finishes with charge
k+1
. Finally, suppose
d(v)\geqslant k+2
. Now
v

finishes with at least
d(v)-d(v)/(k+1)=d(v)k/(k+1)\geqslant(k+2)k/(k+1)=k+k/(k+1)
. Thus,
\textrm{mad}(G)\geqslant k+k/(k+1)
.

3.

Determine
\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(\mathcal{B})
and
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
, where
\mathcal{B}
denotes the class of all planar bipartite graphs.
We conjecture that
\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(\mathcal{B})\leqslant 4
, and also ask whether
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{B})\leqslant 4
. If the latter is true, then it is best
possible, as shown by Example
7
for (arbitrarily long) even cycles.

4.

For each surface
S
, let
\mathcal{G}_{S}
denote the set of all graphs embeddable in
S
. For every surface
S
, determine

\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}_{S})
and
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{G}_{S})
. Let
h(S)
denote the
Heawood number
of
S
, which is the order of the
largest complete graph
embeddable in
S
. It was famously proved (with the upper bound by Heawood, and the lower bound comprised of work by
various groups, most notably including Ringel and Youngs) that
\chi(\mathcal{G}_{S})=\chi_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}_{S})=h(S)
, for all surfaces except
the plane and the Klein bottle. In fact, it was later proved that if
G
embeds in
S
and
G
has no subgraph
K_{h(S)}
,
then
\chi_{\ell}(G)\leqslant h(S)-1
. In
[
2
]
, it was proved that
\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(K_{t})=t
for all
t
. And Yuster
observed
[
10
]
, based on a construction of Catlin
[
5
]
, that
\chi^{\star}_{c}(K_{t})\geqslant t+1
when
t
is odd
(which is conjectured to be sharp). Regardless of the precise value of
\chi^{\star}_{c}(K_{t})
,
is natural to ask the following.
For which surfaces
S
do we have
\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(\mathcal{G}_{S})=\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(K_{h(S)})
?
And for which do we have
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{G}_{S})=\chi^{\star}_{c}(K_{h(S)})
?
For which surfaces do we have
\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(G)\leqslant h(S)-1
for all
G\in\mathcal{G}_{S}
that
do not contain
K_{h(S)}
as a subgraph? What is true in the analogous situation for
\chi^{\star}_{c}(G)
?

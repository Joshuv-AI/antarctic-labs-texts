On the uniqueness of nondegenerate blowups for the motion by curvature of networks
Alessandra Pluda, Marco Pozzetta

Abstract
In this note we prove uniqueness of nondegenerate compact blowups for the motion by curvature of planar networks. The proof follows ideas introduced in [ 16 ] for the study of stability properties of critical points of the length functional and it is based on the application of a Łojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality.

Introduction

If
G
is an abstract one-dimensional graph, see
2.1
, a network
\Gamma:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is, roughly speaking, an embedding of
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of class
H^{2}
. Topologically, we only consider graphs whose junctions have order three, and we will be mainly interested in regular networks, i.e., networks such that the tangent vectors of the immersions given by
\Gamma
forms equal angles, equal to
\tfrac{2}{3}\pi
, at any junction.

A motion by curvature
\Gamma_{t}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a smooth one-parameter family of networks such that the normal velocity of the evolution is equal to the curvature of the immersions along each edge, see
2.3
. The motion by curvature generalizes the one-dimensional mean curvature flow, called curve shortening flow, to singular one-dimensional objects given by planar networks.
The basic theory concerning short and long time behavior of this geometric flow can be found in
[
2
,
15
,
14
,
9
]
. Just like classical mean curvature flow, the motion by curvature may develop singularities determined by the fact the either the length of some edge goes to zero or the
L^{2}
-norm of the curvature diverges in finite time. A great deal of work has been done to understand the nature of these singularities
and to define the flow past singularities
[
15
,
12
,
14
,
1
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
4
,
3
]
.

A classical procedure employed in order to analyze a singularity for a flow
\Gamma_{t}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is to perform a
blowup
of the motion. Classically, if
\Gamma_{t}
is defined on times
[0,T)
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is fixed, this means to consider sequences of parabolically rescaled motions by curvature
\Gamma^{\mu_{j}}_{t}\vcentcolon=\mu_{j}\big(\Gamma_{-\mu_{j}^{-2}t+T}-x_{0}\big):G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
and to pass to the limit in space-time as
\mu_{j}\to+\infty
. Up to subsequence, one shows that there exists such a limit
\Gamma^{\infty}_{t}
, which is called tangent flow, and it is a homothetically shrinking motion by curvature, see
[
14
, Section 8]
. The shape of
\Gamma^{\infty}_{t}
at any time identifies the motion by curvature
\Gamma_{t}
at infinitesimal scales around
x_{0}
as
t
approaches
T
. However, the construction may clearly depend on the choice of the sequence
\mu_{j}
, and it is today an open problem to prove uniqueness of blowups, i.e., independence of the sequence
\mu_{j}
, for a motion by curvature, or for the mean curvature flow, in full generality. Some of the most influential contributions to the uniqueness of blowups for mean curvature flow are contained in
[
18
]
, for the case of compact blowups,
[
8
]
, for the case of cylindrical blowups,
[
7
]
, for the case of conical blowups.

An equivalent way for proving uniqueness of blowups, employed also in the above mentioned works, is to prove that the so-called rescaled motion by curvature
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}}\vcentcolon=\big(2e^{-\mathfrak{t}}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\Gamma_{T-e^{-\mathfrak{t}}}-x_{0}\big):G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
has a full limit as
\mathfrak{t}\to+\infty
, see
Section

2.2
. As in the case of tangent flows, along sequences
\mathfrak{t}_{n}\to+\infty
, one always finds limits of
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}_{n}}
, but the limit may depend on the diverging sequence of times.

In this note, we prove that if one of such sequential limits
\Gamma_{*}
of
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}_{n}}
is nondegenerate and compact, encoded in the fact that
\Gamma_{*}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a regular network for the same graph
G
which is the domain of the original flow
\Gamma_{t}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then it is unique.
For the precise definitions of the terminology used in the next statement, we refer to
2.1
,
2.3
,
2.4
, and
2.6
below.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma_{t}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a motion by curvature defined on times
t\in[0,T)
with
T<+\infty
, where
G
is a graph without endpoints.

If
\Gamma_{*}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a nonempty nondegenerate blowup of
\Gamma_{t}
at some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and time
T
, then
\Gamma_{*}
is the unique blowup of
\Gamma_{t}
at point
x_{0}
and time
T
.

More precisely, the following two (equivalent) statements hold true.

1.

The rescaled motion by curvature
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{t}
at point
x_{0}
and time
T
smoothly converges to
\Gamma_{*}
as
t\to+\infty
, up to reparametrizations.

2.

For any diverging sequence
\mu_{j}\nearrow+\infty
, the sequence of parabolically rescaled motions by curvature
\Gamma^{\mu_{j}}_{t}
converges locally smoothly in space-time to the homothetically shrinking motion by curvature
\Gamma^{\infty}_{t}
such that
\Gamma^{\infty}_{-\frac{1}{2}}=\Gamma_{*}
.

We remark that the above uniqueness theorem holds true regardless of assumptions on whether
T
is a singularity time for
\Gamma_{t}
of Type I or Type II, see
[
14
, remark 8.21]
. On the other hand, a necessary consequence of the uniqueness result in
1.1
is the fact that if
\Gamma_{t}
develops a singularity at time
T
and a blowup at
T
is a nondegenerate network
\Gamma_{*}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then such singularity is, in fact, a Type I singularity (see
[
14
, Remark 8.24]
).

1.1
is a consequence of
3.9
below, which proves smooth convergence of a rescaled motion
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}}
. The great advantage of working with the flow
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}}
is that such evolution turns out to be the
L^{2}
-gradient flow of the
Gaussian energy
defined by

\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)\vcentcolon=\sum_{i}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-|\gamma^{i}(x)|^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}s,

for any network
\Gamma:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
With the obvious meaning of symbols
\sum_{i}\int_{\gamma^{i}}
and
\int_{\Gamma}
, we shall equivalently write
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)=\sum_{i}\int_{\gamma^{i}}e^{-|p|^{2}/2}=\int_{\Gamma}e^{-|p|^{2}/2}
.

The gradient flow structure permits to apply a general strategy for proving full convergence of a flow out of its sequential convergence along sequences of times, see
[
17
]
. Such strategy is based on the application of a Łojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality
[
19
,
5
,
6
]
derived on the energy functional
\mathcal{G}
. We derive such inequality in
3.8
below.

The main difficulty here is that such an inequality would readily follow by functional analytic results
[
5
]
relying on the possibility of parametrizing networks as normal graphs over fixed critical points of
\mathcal{G}
. However, the nonsmooth structure of networks necessarily implies technical complications, since networks close to a fixed one
\Gamma_{*}
cannot be written as normal graphs over
\Gamma_{*}
. Hence, in order to perform a graph parametrization of networks close to
\Gamma_{*}
we will need to allow for graphs having both a normal and a tangential component with respect to
\Gamma_{*}
. The issue of performing such a graph parametrization and at the same time of achieving the linear functional analytic setup needed for deriving the Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality was investigated in
[
16
]
for the study of stability properties networks that are critical points of the length functional. We shall recall from
[
16
]
the tools needed here at the beginning of
Section

3
, and we refer to
[
16
]
for a more detailed account on the method.

Organization.
In
Section

2
we recall basic definitions on networks, motion by curvature, and blowups. In
Section

3
we first derive the Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality at critical points of
\mathcal{G}
,
3.8
, and then we prove convergence of the rescaled motion by curvature,
3.9
, implying the main result
1.1
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Networks

For a regular curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of class
H^{2}
, we define the tangent vector
\tau_{\gamma}\vcentcolon=\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{|\gamma^{\prime}|}
, and the normal vector
\nu_{\gamma}\vcentcolon={\rm R}(\tau_{\gamma})
, where
{\rm R}
denotes counterclockwise rotation of
\tfrac{\pi}{2}
. We define
\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}\vcentcolon=|\gamma^{\prime}|\,\mathrm{d}x
the arclength element and
\partial_{s}\vcentcolon=|\gamma^{\prime}|^{-1}\partial_{x}
the arclength derivative. The curvature of
\gamma
is the vector

\boldsymbol{k}_{\gamma}\vcentcolon=\partial_{s}^{2}\gamma.

We shall usually drop the subscript
\gamma
when there is no risk of confusion.

Definition 2.1

(Networks)
.

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
and let
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
,
E^{i}:=[0,1]\times\{i\}
,

E:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}E^{i}
and

V:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\{0,1\}\times\{i\}
.

Let
\sim
be an equivalence relation that identifies points of
V
.
Let
G\vcentcolon=E\,/\,\sim
be the graph given by the topological quotient space of
E
induced by
\sim
. Assume that
G
is connected.

Denoting by
\pi:E\to G
the projection onto the quotient, an
endpoint
is a point
p\in G
such that
\pi^{-1}(p)\subset V
and it is a singleton, a
junction
is a point
m\in G
such that
\pi^{-1}(m)\subset V
and it is not a singleton.

For any
(e^{i},i)\in E^{i}
such that
\pi(e^{i},i)
is a junction, we assume that there exist exactly two boundary points
(e^{j},j)\in E^{j},(e^{k},k)\in E^{k}
, for
j\neq k
distinct from
i
, such that
\pi(e^{i},i)=\pi(e^{j},j)=\pi(e^{k},k)
. A graph with such property is said to be
regular
.

We denote by
J_{G}
and
P_{G}
the set of junctions and endpoints, respectively.

A
network
is a continuous map
\Gamma:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\Gamma|_{E^{i}}
is either a
C^{1}
-embedding or a constant map. We say that
\Gamma
is of class
C^{k}
or smooth, if any embedding
\Gamma|_{E^{i}}
is of class
C^{k}
or
C^{\infty}
, respectively.

The
inner tangent vector
of a regular curve
\gamma^{i}\vcentcolon=\Gamma|_{E^{i}}
at
e\in\{0,1\}
is the vector

(-1)^{e}\frac{(\gamma^{i})^{\prime}(e)}{|(\gamma^{i})^{\prime}(e)|}.

A
triple junctions
(or
nondegenerate
)
network
is an embedding
\Gamma:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that any restriction
\Gamma|_{E^{i}}
is nonconstant.

A network is said to be
regular
if it is triple junctions and whenever
\pi(e^{i},i)=\pi(e^{j},j)=\pi(e^{k},k)
is a junction then any two inner tangent vectors of
\gamma^{i},\gamma^{j},\gamma^{k}
at
e^{i},e^{j},e^{k}
, respectively, form an angle equal to
\tfrac{2}{3}\pi
.

Without loss of generality, if
\Gamma:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a network and
p\in G
is an endpoint with
\pi^{-1}(p)=(e,i)
,
we will assume that
e=1
.

In order to treat blowups of the motion by curvature, we introduce the following definition of complete network.

Definition 2.2

(Complete regular network)
.

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
and let
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
. Let
E^{i}\vcentcolon=I^{i}\times\{i\}
, where
I^{i}
is any of the intervals
[0,1]
,
(0,1]
,
[1,0)
, or
(0,1)
. Let
E\vcentcolon=\cup_{i}E^{i}
.

Let
\sim
be an equivalence relation that identifies points on the boundaries of the
E^{i}
’s. Consider a graph
H\vcentcolon=\cup_{i}E^{i}\,/\,\sim
and let
\pi
be the projection onto the quotient. Assume that
H
is connected and that for any
(e^{i},i)\in E^{i}
there exist exactly two boundary points
(e^{j},j)\in E^{j},(e^{k},k)\in E^{k}
, for
j\neq k
distinct from
i
, such that
\pi(e^{i},i)=\pi(e^{j},j)=\pi(e^{k},k)
. Such a graph is said to be
open
.

A continuous map
\Gamma:H\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\Gamma|_{E^{i}}
is either a
C^{1}
-embedding or a constant map is still said to be a
network
.

A
complete regular network
is an embedding
\Gamma:H\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\Gamma|_{E^{i}}
is a complete embedding of class
C^{1}
for any
i
and whenever
\pi(e^{i},i)=\pi(e^{j},j)=\pi(e^{k},k)
is a junction then any two inner tangent vectors of
\gamma^{i},\gamma^{j},\gamma^{k}
at
e^{i},e^{j},e^{k}
, respectively, form an angle equal to
\tfrac{2}{3}\pi
.

Whenever a graph
G
is not explicitly said to be open, then it is assumed to be as in
2.1
; in particular, such a
G
is compact.

Observe that, by definition, a complete regular network has no endpoints. Also, a regular network
\Gamma:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
G
has no endpoints is automatically a complete regular network. This choice is coherent with the fact that we shall restrict ourselves to the study of compact blowups.

2.2
Motion by curvature and blowups

Definition 2.3

(Motion by curvature)
.

A smooth one-parameter family of regular networks
\Gamma_{t}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, for
t\in[0,T)
, is a
motion by curvature
if the parametrizations
\gamma^{i}_{t}
of
\Gamma_{t}
satisfy

\begin{split}\gamma^{i}_{t}(p)=P^{i}&\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,T),\,\,\forall\,p\text{ endpoint of $G$},\\
\left\langle\partial_{t}\gamma^{i}(t,x),\nu^{i}(t,x)\right\rangle\nu^{i}(t,x)=\boldsymbol{k}^{i}(t,x)&\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,T),\,\,\forall\,x\in(0,1),\end{split}

(2.1)

for some points
P^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
The solution is assumed to be maximal, i.e., we ask that it does not exists
another solution defined on
[0,\widetilde{T})
with
\widetilde{T}>T
.

For ease of notation, we shall often write that
\langle\partial_{t}\Gamma_{t},\nu_{t}\rangle\nu_{t}=\boldsymbol{k}_{t}
understanding that the relation holds along any edge
E^{i}
.

Exploiting the analysis carried out in
[
9
]
, we can assume that the parametrizations of a motion by curvature evolve according to the
special flow
, i.e., the evolution is determined by the non-degenerate parabolic second order equation

\partial_{t}\gamma^{i}_{t}=\frac{\partial^{2}_{x}\gamma^{i}_{t}}{|\partial_{x}\gamma^{i}_{t}|^{2}}\,.

(2.2)

Throughout the paper, we will assume that
T<+\infty
is the maximal time of existence for a motion by curvature, which thus develops a singularity at time
T
. In view of
[
14
]
, this means that either the inferior limit of the length of at least one curve of the network is zero as
t\to T^{-}
, or the superior limit of the
L^{2}
-norm of the curvature is
+\infty
as
t\to T^{-}
.

Definition 2.4

(Rescaled motion by curvature)
.

If
\Gamma_{t}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a motion by curvature, the
rescaled motion by curvature at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and time
T
is the flow

\widetilde{\Gamma}_{x_{0}}:[-\tfrac{1}{2}\log T,+\infty)\times G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\qquad\qquad\widetilde{\Gamma}_{x_{0}}(\mathfrak{t},x)\vcentcolon=\frac{\Gamma_{t}(x)-x_{0}}{\sqrt{2(T-t)}},

where
\mathfrak{t}(t)\vcentcolon=-\tfrac{1}{2}\log(T-t)
.
We shall denote
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}}(\cdot)\vcentcolon=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{x_{0}}(\mathfrak{t},\cdot)
, omitting the dependence on
x_{0}
.

Without loss of generality, in the definition of rescaled motion we shall always assume that
x_{0}=0
is the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

A direct computation shows that a rescaled motion by curvature evolves according to

\partial_{\mathfrak{t}}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}}=\sqrt{2(T-t)}\partial_{t}\Gamma_{t}+\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}},

for
t(\mathfrak{t})=T-e^{-2\mathfrak{t}}
, understanding that the relation holds along any edge
E^{i}
. In particular normal and tangent vectors of
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}}
coincide with the ones of
\Gamma_{t}
, that is
\widetilde{\nu}_{\mathfrak{t}}(x)=\nu_{t}(x)
and
\widetilde{\tau}_{\mathfrak{t}}(x)=\tau_{t}(x)
, and the normal velocity is

\langle\partial_{\mathfrak{t}}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}},\widetilde{\nu}_{\mathfrak{t}}\rangle\widetilde{\nu}_{\mathfrak{t}}=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{k}}_{\mathfrak{t}}+\langle\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}},\widetilde{\nu}_{\mathfrak{t}}\rangle\widetilde{\nu}_{\mathfrak{t}}.

In view of
[
14
, Section 7.2]
and
3.5
below, it turns out that a rescaled motion by curvature
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}}
is an
L^{2}
-gradient flow of the Gaussian energy
\mathcal{G}
. Moreover, a complete regular network
\Gamma_{*}:H\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is critical point for
\mathcal{G}
, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{k}_{*}+\langle\Gamma_{*},\nu_{*}\rangle\nu_{*}=0
, if and only if
\Gamma_{*}
is a
shrinker
, that is,
\Gamma_{*}
is complete regular and the motion by curvature starting from
\Gamma_{*}
evolves homothetically contracting.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
14
, Proposition 8.20, Remark 8.21]
)
.

Let
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}}
be a rescaled motion by curvature at the origin
0
and at maximal time
T
of existence of a motion by curvature
\Gamma_{t}
. Assume that
0
is not an endpoint of the motion
\Gamma_{t}
. Then the following hold.

1.

There exists a diverging sequence of times
\mathfrak{t}_{n}\to+\infty
and a (possibly empty) open subgraph
H\subset G
such that: for any
E^{i}\subset G\setminus H
and any
R>0
it holds
\widetilde{\gamma}^{i}_{\mathfrak{t}_{n}}(E^{i})\cap B_{R}(0)=\emptyset
for any
n
large enough, for any
E^{i}\cap H\subset H
the curves
\widetilde{\gamma}^{i}_{\mathfrak{t}_{n}}
converge in
C^{1}\cap H^{2}
locally on
E^{i}\cap H
, up to reparametrization, to either a constant curve or to an embedding of class
H^{2}
.

2.

If also the sequence
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}_{n}}|_{H}
converges to a complete regular network
\Gamma_{*}:H\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
in
C^{1}\cap H^{2}
locally on
H
, up to reparametrizations, then convergence holds in
C^{k}
locally on
H
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

In the notation of the first item in
2.5
, the network
\Gamma_{*}:H\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by the limit immersions
\Gamma_{*}|_{E^{i}}\vcentcolon=\lim_{n}\widetilde{\gamma}^{i}_{\mathfrak{t}_{n}}
is said to be a
blowup of
\Gamma_{t}
(at
0
and time
T
)
.

If the second item in
2.5
occurs, we say that
\Gamma_{*}
is a
nondegenerate blowup of
\Gamma_{t}
(at
0
and time
T
)
.

In the second item of
2.5
, the requirement that
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}_{n}}|_{H}
converges to a complete regular network
\Gamma_{*}:H\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is equivalent to the fact that
\widetilde{\gamma}^{i}_{\mathfrak{t}_{n}}
converges to an embedding
\gamma^{i}_{*}
for any
E^{i}\cap H\subset H
up to reparametrizations, and
\gamma^{i}_{*}(E^{i}\cap H)
intersects
\gamma^{j}_{*}(E^{j}\cap H)
for
i\neq j
only at junctions, i.e., the limit network has multiplicity one.

We remark that nondegenerate blowups of a motion by curvature are automatically shrinkers.

An alternative and equivalent way for defining blowups is given by the already mentioned concept of tangent flow, recalled in the next remark.

Remark 2.6

(Parabolic rescaling and tangent flows)
.

Let
\Gamma_{t}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a motion by curvature defined on the time interval
[0,T)
. For any
\mu>0
, the
parabolically rescaled motion by curvature
\Gamma^{\mu}_{t}
(at point
x_{0}
and time
T
)
is the evolution
\Gamma^{\mu}_{t}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by

\Gamma^{\mu}_{t}|_{E^{i}}(x)\vcentcolon=\mu\big(\Gamma_{\mu^{-2}\,t+T}|_{E^{i}}(x)-x_{0}\big),

for
t\in[-\mu^{2}T,0)
and any
i=1,\ldots,N
and
x\in E^{i}
. It is readily checked that
\Gamma^{\mu}_{t}
is still a motion by curvature.

Suppose without loss of generality that
x_{0}=0
, then
\Gamma^{\mu}_{t}
is related to the rescaled motion by curvature
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}}
at time
T
via the identities

\Gamma^{\mu}_{t}=\sqrt{-2t}\,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\log(\mu/\sqrt{-t})},\qquad\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}}=\frac{e^{\mathfrak{t}}}{\mu\sqrt{2}}\Gamma^{\mu}_{-\mu^{2}e^{-2\mathfrak{t}}}.

In particular
\Gamma^{\mu}_{-\frac{1}{2}}=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\log(\mu\sqrt{2})}
, whenever
-\mu^{2}T\leq-\tfrac{1}{2}
.

Consider now a sequence
\mu_{j}\nearrow+\infty
, together with the sequence of flows
\Gamma^{\mu_{j}}_{t}
. Exploiting the above identities and
2.5
, or (equivalently)
[
14
, Proposition 8.16, Lemma 8.18]
, one can prove that, up to extracting a subsequence, there is an open subgraph
H\subset G
such that: for any
E^{i}\subset G\setminus H
and any
R>0
it holds
\Gamma^{\mu_{j}}_{t}(E^{i})\cap B_{R}(0)=\emptyset
for any
t<0
and
j
large enough, for any
E^{i}\cap H\subset H
the curves
\Gamma^{\mu_{j}}_{t}|_{E^{i}}
converge in
C^{1}\cap H^{2}
locally on
E^{i}\cap H
to curves
\Gamma^{\infty}_{t}|_{E^{i}}
for any
t<0
as
j\to+\infty
, up to reparametrization, which are either constant or embeddings of class
H^{2}
. The flow
\Gamma^{\infty}_{t}
is said to be a
tangent flow
to
\Gamma_{t}
.

In case
\Gamma^{\infty}_{t}:H\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is regular for some (hence, any)
t
, then
\Gamma^{\infty}_{t}
is a homothetically shrinking motion by curvature and
\Gamma^{\infty}_{-\frac{1}{2}}
is the limit of the rescaled motion
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}}
along the sequence of times
\mathfrak{t}_{j}=\log(\mu_{j}\sqrt{2})
.

Conversely, if
\Gamma_{*}
is a sequential limit of
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathfrak{t}}
along some sequence of times
\mathfrak{t}_{n}
as in
2.5
, then there exists the tangent flow
\Gamma^{\infty}_{t}
obtained as the limit of the parabolic rescalings
\Gamma^{\mu_{n}}_{t}
taking
\mu_{n}=e^{\mathfrak{t}_{n}}/\sqrt{2}
.

Hence blowups of a motion by curvature are equivalently identified by tangent flows or by sequential limits of the rescaled motion by curvature, see also
[
14
, Remark 8.27, Remark 7.4]
.

Remark 2.7
.

In the notation and hypotheses of
2.5
, if item 2 occurs, if also
\Gamma_{*}(H)
is compact and nonempty, then
H=G
is a regular graph without endpoints and
\Gamma_{*}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a regular network.

3
Uniqueness of blowups

We need to recall the setup introduced in
[
16
]
in order to canonically parametrize networks close in
H^{2}
to a regular one
\Gamma_{*}
suitably as “graphs” over
\Gamma_{*}
.

For a given regular network
\Gamma_{*}
, for parametrizations of the form
\gamma^{\ell}(x)\vcentcolon=\gamma^{\ell}_{*}(x)+\mathsf{N}^{\ell}(x)\nu^{\ell}_{*}(x)+\mathsf{T}^{\ell}(x)\tau^{\ell}_{*}(x)
, the next lemma contains sufficient conditions to be imposed on the functions
\mathsf{N}^{\ell},\mathsf{T}^{\ell}
so that the resulting parametrizations
\gamma^{\ell}
define a triple junctions network. It readily seen that such conditions are also necessary
[
16
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
16
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Let
\Gamma_{*}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

Stochastic differential switching game in infinite horizon
Brahim EL ASRI, Sehail MAZID

Abstract
We study a zero-sum stochastic differential switching game in infinite horizon. We prove the existence of the value of the game and characterize it as the unique viscosity solution of the associated system of quasi-variational inequalities with bilateral obstacles. We also obtain a verification theorem which provides an optimal strategy of the game. Finally, some numerical examples with two regimes are given.

Introduction

Differential game theory involves multiple persons (also called players or individuals) decision making in the context of dynamic system. The study of the two-person zero-sum differential games could be traced back to the pioneering work by Isaacs
[
21
]
, which inspired further research in this area.

In 1989, Fleming and Souganidis
[
15
]
, for the first time, studied two-person zerosum stochastic differential games. They adopted the definitions of upper and lower value functions introduced by Elliott and Kalton
[
13
]
and proved that the two value functions satisfy the dynamic programming principle and they are the unique viscosity solutions to the associated Hamilton–-Jacobi–-Bellman–-Isaacs partial differential equations (HJBI PDEs). Their work generalized that of Evans and Souganidis
[
14
]
from the deterministic framework to the stochastic one and is now regarded as one of the outstanding results in the field of stochastic differential games. In 1995, Hamadène and Lepeltier
[
17
]
introduced the backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE) theory into the study of stochastic differential games, and there exist also many other works using this BSDEs approach, such as Hamadène, Lepeltier, and Peng
[
18
]
. Subsequently, Buckdahn and Li
[
2
]
extended the findings presented in
[
17
,
18
]
, and generalized the framework introduced in
[
15
]
.

In this paper we consider the state process of the stochastic differential game, defined as the solution of the following stochastic equation:

\begin{array}[]{ll}X_{s}=x+\displaystyle\int_{t}^{s}b(r,X_{r},a_{t},b_{t})dr+\displaystyle\int_{t}^{s}\sigma(r,X_{r},a_{t},b_{t})dW_{r}\qquad s\geq t,\end{array}

with
X_{t^{-}}=x
. Here
W
is a d-dimensional Wiener process, while

a_{t}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\xi_{m}1\!\!1_{(\tau_{m}\leq t<\tau_{m+1}]}(t)\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad b_{t}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\eta_{n}1\!\!1_{(\rho_{n}\leq t<\rho_{n+1}]}(t).

with the cost functional

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rs}f(X_{s},a_{t},b_{t})ds-\sum\limits_{m\geq 1}e^{-r\tau_{m}}C(\xi_{m-1},\xi_{m})+\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}e^{-r\rho_{n}}\chi(\eta_{n-1},\eta_{n})\bigg].\end{array}

(1.1)

The first player chooses the control a from a given finite set
\mathcal{I}
to maximize the payoff (
1.1
), and each actions is related with one cost C, while the second player chooses the control b from
\mathcal{I}
to minimize the payoff (
1.1
), and each of his actions is associated with the other cost
\chi
. The zero-sum stochastic differential games
problems we will investigate is to show the upper and lower value functions coincide and the game admits a value.
The Isaacs’ system of
quasi-variational inequalities for this switching game is the following:
for any
i,j\in\mathcal{I}
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\begin{array}[]{c}max\Big\{min\Big[rV^{ij}(x)-\mathcal{A}V^{ij}(x)-f(x,i,j);\\
\qquad V^{ij}(x)-M^{ij}[V](x)\Big];V^{ij}(x)-N^{ij}[V](x)\Big\}=0,\end{array}

(1.2)

and

\begin{array}[]{c}min\Big\{max\Big[rV^{ij}(x)-\mathcal{A}V^{ij}(x)-f(x,i,j);\\
\qquad V^{ij}(x)-N^{ij}[V](x)\Big];V^{ij}(x)-M^{ij}[V](x)\Big\}=0.\end{array}

(1.3)

where,

M^{ij}[V](x)=\max_{k\neq i}\{V^{kj}(x)-C(i,k)\},\qquad N^{ij}[V](x)=\min_{l\neq j}\{V^{il}(x)+\chi(j,l)\}.

In the finite horizon framework, the switching game have been studied by several authors. The most recent work
discussing this topic includes the papers by Djehiche et al. (2017) (
[
8
]
), Tang and Hou (2007) (
[
32
]
).

The objective of this work is to establish existence and uniqueness of a continuous viscosity solution of (
1.2
) and (
1.3
). The second contribution of our paper is the Verification Theorem which provides an optimal strategy of the game, To the best of our knowledge, this issue have not been addressed in the literature yet.

This paper is organized as follows: in Section 2 we list all the notations, state the full set of assumptions, and define viscosity sub- and supersolutions along with equivalent characterizations. In Section 3, we shall introduce the stochastic differential game problem and give some preliminary results of the lower and the upper value functions of stochastic differential game. In Section 4, by the
dynamic programming principle we prove that the lower and upper value functions of the game satisfy the Isaacs’ system of
quasi-variational inequalities in the viscosity solution sense. In Section 5, we show that the solution of Isaacs’ system of
quasi-variational inequalities is unique. Further, the upper and the lower value functions coincide and the game admits a value. In Section 6, we present a verification theorem which gives an optimal strategy of the switching game, while finally Section 7 will present some numerical examples.

3
The zero-sum switching game

3.1
Setting of the problem

Consider a complete probability space (
\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}
) and a
d
-dimensional standard Wiener process
W=(W_{t})
defined on it. Let
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the natural filtration
generated by the Wiener process, completed with the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets of

Definition 4

Assume we have two players I and II who intervene on a system (e.g. the production of energy from several
sources such as oil, cole, hydro-electric, etc.) with the help of switching strategies. An admissible switching process
for I (resp. II) is a sequence
\delta:=(\tau_{m},\xi_{m})_{m\geq 0}
(resp.
\nu:=(\rho_{n},\eta_{n})_{n\geq 0})
where:

(i)
(\tau_{m})_{m}
(resp.
(\rho_{n})_{n})
, the action times, is a sequence of
\mathcal{F}
-stopping times such that
\tau_{m}<\tau_{m+1}
(resp.
\rho_{n}<\rho_{n+1})
and
\tau_{n}\to\infty
(resp.
\rho_{n}\to\infty
).

(ii)
(\xi_{m})_{m}
(resp.
(\eta_{n})_{n})
, the actions, is a sequence of
\mathcal{I}
-valued random variables, where each
\xi_{m}
(resp.
\eta_{n}

\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{m}}
-measurable (resp.
\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{n}}
-measurable).

Next let
(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{I}
be fixed. We say that the admissible switching strategy
\delta:=(\tau_{m},\xi_{m})_{m\geq 0}
of I (resp.
\nu:=(\rho_{n},\eta_{n})_{n\geq 0})
(resp. II) belongs to
\mathcal{A}^{i}
(resp.
\mathcal{B}^{j}
) if
\tau_{0}=0,\xi_{0}=i
(resp.
\rho_{0}=0,\eta_{0}=j
).

Given an admissible strategy
\delta
(resp.
\nu
) of I (resp. II), one associates a stochastic process
(a_{t})_{t\geq 0}
(resp.

(b_{t})_{t\geq 0})
which indicates along with time the current mode of I (resp. II) and which is defined by:
\forall t\geq 0

a_{t}=\xi_{0}1\!\!1_{\{\tau_{0}\}}(t)+\sum_{m\geq 1}\xi_{m-1}1\!\!1_{(\tau_{m-1},\tau_{m}]}(t)\;(\text{resp.}\;b_{t}=\eta_{0}1\!\!1_{\{\rho_{0}\}}(t)+\sum_{n\geq 1}\eta_{n-1}1\!\!1_{(\rho_{n-1},\rho_{n}]}(t)).

(3.1)

The evolution of
the state of the game is described by the following stochastic equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllllll}dX_{t}=b^{a_{t}b_{t}}(X_{t})dt+\sigma^{a_{t}b_{t}}(X_{t})dW_{t},\qquad\;t\geq 0,\\
X_{0}=x\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

We assume that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
i,j\in\mathcal{I}
, there exists a unique strong solution to (
3.2
). Let now
\delta:=(\tau_{m},\xi_{m})_{m\geq 0}
(resp.
\nu:=(\rho_{n},\eta_{n})_{n\geq 0})
be an admissible strategy for I (resp. II) which belongs
to
\mathcal{A}^{i}

(resp.
\mathcal{B}^{j}
). The interventions of the players are not free and generate a payoff which is a reward
(resp. cost) for I (resp. II) and whose expression is given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}J(x,\delta,\nu)=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rs}f^{a_{s}b_{s}}(X^{x}_{s})ds-\sum\limits_{m\geq 1}e^{-r\tau_{m}}C(\xi_{m-1},\xi_{m})+\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}e^{-r\rho_{n}}\chi(\eta_{n-1},\eta_{n})\bigg].\end{array}

(3.3)

Following Buckdahn and Li
[
2
]
, we define nonanticipative strategy as follows.

Definition 5

A nonanticipative strategy for player I is a mapping

\alpha:\cup_{j\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{B}^{j}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{i}

such that for any stopping time
\tau
and any
b
,
b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}^{j}
, with

on
\llbracket 0,\tau\rrbracket,

\alpha(b)\equiv\alpha(b^{\prime})

\llbracket 0,\tau\rrbracket

(with the notation

A nonanticipative strategy for player

\beta:\cup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{A}^{i}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}^{j}

is defined similarly. The set of all nonanticipative strategies
\alpha
(resp.
\beta
) for player I
(resp. II) is denoted by

\Gamma^{i}
(resp,
\Delta^{j})
.

We define the upper (resp. lower) value of the game by

\overline{V}^{\;ij}(x)=\inf\limits_{\beta\in{\Delta^{j}}}\sup\limits_{\delta\in\mathcal{A}^{i}}J(x,\delta,\beta(\delta))

(3.4)

(
resp.

\underline{V}^{ij}(x)=\sup\limits_{\alpha\in\Gamma^{i}}\inf\limits_{\nu\in\mathcal{B}^{j}}J(x,\alpha(\nu),\nu)\big).

(3.5)

The game has a value if

Lemma 2

There exists some positive constant

Proof.
It is enough to show that the conclusion holds for the gain functional
J
.

For every
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},u\in\mathcal{A}^{i},\nu\in\mathcal{B}^{j}
, we have

\qquad\qquad|J(x,\delta,\nu)-J(x^{\prime},\delta,\nu)|\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rs}|f^{a_{s}b_{s}}(X_{s}^{x})-f^{a_{s}b_{s}}(X_{s}^{x^{\prime}})|ds\bigg].\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

By a standard estimate for the SDE applying Ito’s formula to
|X^{x}-X^{x^{\prime}}|^{2}
and using Gronwall’s lemma, we then obtain from the Lipschitz condition on
b^{ij},\sigma^{ij}
, the
following inequality

E[|X_{t}^{x}-X_{t}^{x^{\prime}}|\,]\leq e^{\rho t}|x-x^{\prime}|\qquad\forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},t\geq 0.

From the Lipschitz condition on
f^{ij}
, we deduce

\qquad\qquad|J(x,\delta,\nu)-J(x^{\prime},\delta,\nu)|\leq C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rs}|X_{s}^{x}-X_{s}^{x^{\prime}}|ds\bigg]\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\\
\leq C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rs}e^{\rho s}|x-x^{\prime}|ds\bigg]\leq C|x-x^{\prime}|,\qquad\qquad

for
r>\rho
. This ends the proof.
\Box

In the sequel, we shall assume that
r
is large enough, which ensures that the value functions are Lipschitz
continuous. We now present the dynamic programming
principle of the zero-sum switching game.

Theorem 1

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\overline{V}^{\;ij}(x)=\inf\limits_{\beta\in{\Delta^{j}}}\sup\limits_{\delta\in\mathcal{A}^{i}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta}e^{-rs}f^{a_{s}b_{s}}(X^{x}_{s})ds-\sum\limits_{\tau_{m}\leq\theta}e^{-r\tau_{m}}C(\xi_{m-1},\xi_{m})\\

(3.16)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\underline{V}^{ij}(x)=\sup\limits_{\alpha\in\Gamma^{i}}\inf\limits_{\nu\in\mathcal{B}^{j}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta}e^{-rs}f^{a_{s}b_{s}}(X^{x}_{s})ds-\sum\limits_{\tau_{m}\leq\theta}e^{-r\tau_{m}}C(\xi_{m-1},\xi_{m})\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum\limits_{\rho_{n}\leq\theta}e^{-r\rho_{n}}\chi(\eta_{n-1},\eta_{n})+e^{-r\theta}\underline{V}^{a_{\theta}b_{\theta}}(X^{x}_{\theta})\bigg],\end{array}

5
Uniqueness of the solution of Isaacs’ system of
quasi-variational inequalities

We prove that the (2.9) admits a unique viscosity solution. We need to make an additional assumption on the switching costs.

[H5]
(The no free loop property)

For any sequence of pairs
\{i_{p},j_{p}\}_{p=1}^{N+1}\subset\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{I}
such that
(i_{N+1},j_{N+1})=(i_{1},j_{1}),
and for
1\leq p\leq N,
either
i_{p+1}=i_{p}
or
j_{p+1}=j_{p}
, we have

\sum_{p=1}^{N}\chi(j_{p},j_{p+1})-\sum_{p=1}^{N}C(i_{p},i_{p+1})\neq 0.

We begin with the technical lemma.

Lemma 4

Let
\vec{U}:=(U^{ij})_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{I}}
and
\vec{V}:=(V^{ij})_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{I}}
be a viscosity subsolution and a viscosity supersolution to the equation (
2.9
), respectively. Let

be fixed and
(\bar{i},\bar{j})\in\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{I}
such that

and

U^{\bar{i}\bar{j}}(x)\leq M^{\bar{i}\bar{j}}[U](x)\quad\text{or}\quad V^{\bar{i}\bar{j}}(x)\geq N^{\bar{i}\bar{j}}[V](x).

(5.2)

Then there exists
(i_{0},j_{0})\in\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{I}
such that

V^{\bar{i}\bar{j}}(x)-U^{\bar{i}\bar{j}}(x)=V^{i_{0}j_{0}}(x)-U^{i_{0}j_{0}}(x)

(5.3)

and

U^{i_{0}j_{0}}(x)>M^{i_{0}j_{0}}[U](x)\quad\text{and}\quad V^{i_{0}j_{0}}(x)<N^{i_{0}j_{0}}[V](x).

(5.4)

Proof
.
Suppose that
U^{\bar{i}\bar{j}}(x)\leq M^{\bar{i}\bar{j}}[U](x_{\epsilon})
, then there exists
k\neq\bar{i}
, such that

U^{\bar{i}\bar{j}}(x)\leq U^{k\bar{j}}(x)-C(\bar{i},k).

(5.5)

Since
\vec{V}
is a subsolution, it satisfies

V^{\bar{i}\bar{j}}(x)\geq V^{k\bar{j}}(x)-C(\bar{i},k)

(5.6)

which implies that

U^{\bar{i}\bar{j}}(x)-V^{\bar{i}\bar{j}}(x)\leq U^{k\bar{j}}(x)-V^{k\bar{j}}(x).

Hence

U^{\bar{i}\bar{j}}(x)-V^{\bar{i}\bar{j}}(x)=U^{k\bar{j}}(x)-V^{k\bar{j}}(x).

(5.7)

Then (
5.5
), (
5.6
) and (
5.7

Similarly if

hold, then there exists

such that

Now if the new index (

or

5.2
) , we repeat this reasoning . If this case continues to occur, finally we find a loop

Hence we obtain a contradiction to the assumption (
H5
). Thus (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) holds for some
(i_{0},j_{0})

\\
+\infty\quad\text{if the above set is empty}\end{array}\right.\\
\\
\text{and}\\
\\
\eta^{*}_{m}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\min\{l\neq\eta^{*}_{m-1},V^{a_{\rho^{*}_{m}}l}(X_{\rho^{*}_{m}})+\chi(\eta^{*}_{m-1},l)\}\quad\text{if}\quad\rho^{*}_{m}<+\infty\\
\\
\eta^{*}_{m-1}\quad\text{if}\quad\rho^{*}_{m}=+\infty\end{array}\right.\bigg)\end{array}\right.

We are now ready to present the verification theorem for our switching game.

Theorem 4

For each
i,j\in\mathcal{I}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and assume that
(\delta^{*},\nu^{*})\in\mathcal{A}^{i}\times\mathcal{B}^{j}
. Then we have

V^{ij}(x)=J(x,\delta^{*},\nu^{*}).

Proof:

First for each
\delta:=(\tau_{m},\xi_{m})_{m\geq 0}\in\mathcal{A}^{i}
and
\nu:=(\rho_{n},\eta_{n})_{n\geq 0}\in\mathcal{B}^{j}
we define
\theta=(\theta_{k})
an increasing sequence of stopping times by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\qquad\qquad\theta_{0}=\tau_{0}=\rho_{0}=0\\
\\
\qquad\qquad\theta_{1}=\min(\tau_{1},\rho_{1})\\
\\
\qquad\qquad\theta_{2}=\min(\tau_{1}1\!\!1_{[\tau_{1}>\theta_{1}]}+\tau_{2}1\!\!1_{[\tau_{1}\leq\theta_{1}]},\rho_{1}1\!\!1_{[\rho_{1}>\theta_{1}]}+\rho_{2}1\!\!1_{[\rho_{1}\leq\theta_{1}]}),\\
\\
\qquad\qquad\theta_{3}=\min(\tau_{1}1\!\!1_{[\tau_{1}>\theta_{2}]}+\tau_{3}1\!\!1_{[\tau_{1}\leq\theta_{2}]},\rho_{1}1\!\!1_{[\rho_{1}>\theta_{2}]}+\rho_{3}1\!\!1_{[\rho_{1}\leq\theta_{2}]}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad,\tau_{2}1\!\!1_{[\tau_{2}>\theta_{2}]}+\tau_{3}1\!\!1_{[\tau_{2}\leq\theta_{2}]},\rho_{2}1\!\!1_{[\rho_{2}>\theta_{2}]}+\rho_{3}1\!\!1_{[\rho_{2}\leq\theta_{2}]}),\\
\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad.....................................................\\
\\
\qquad\qquad\theta_{k}=\min(\tau_{1}1\!\!1_{[\tau_{1}>\theta_{k-1}]}+\tau_{k}1\!\!1_{[\tau_{1}\leq\theta_{k-1}]},\rho_{1}1\!\!1_{[\rho_{1}>\theta_{k-1}]}+\rho_{k}1\!\!1_{[\rho_{1}\leq\theta_{k-1}]},...\par\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad,\tau_{k-1}1\!\!1_{[\tau_{k-1}>\theta_{k-1}]}+\tau_{k}1\!\!1_{[\tau_{k-1}\leq\theta_{k-1}]},\rho_{k-1}1\!\!1_{[\rho_{k-1}>\theta_{k-1}]}+\rho_{k}1\!\!1_{[\rho_{k-1}\leq\theta_{k-1}]}).\end{array}

Then the cost functional is rewritten as:

\begin{array}[]{ll}J(x,\delta,\nu)=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\displaystyle\int_{\theta_{k-1}}^{\theta_{k}}e^{-rs}f^{a_{s}b_{s}}(X_{s})ds-\sum\limits_{m\geq 1}e^{-r\tau_{m}}C(\xi_{m-1},\xi_{m})1\!\!1_{[\tau_{m}=\theta_{k}]}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}e^{-r\rho_{n}}\chi(\eta_{n-1},\eta_{n})1\!\!1_{[\rho_{n}=\theta_{k}]}\bigg].\end{array}

(6.1)

Now, let
(\theta^{*})_{k}
associated with
\delta^{*}
and
\nu^{*}
, then when
\theta^{*}_{k-1}<t<\theta^{*}_{k}
we have

rV^{a_{s}b_{t}}(X_{t})-\mathcal{A}V^{a_{t}b_{t}}(X_{t})=f^{a_{t}b_{t}}

(6.2)

Then by Itô’s formula (see, e.g.,
Sect. IV.45 of
[
31
]
), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\displaystyle\int_{\theta^{*}_{k-1}}^{\theta^{*}_{k}}e^{-rs}f^{a_{s}b_{s}}(X_{s})ds\bigg]=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\displaystyle\int_{\theta^{*}_{k-1}}^{\theta^{*}_{k}}rV^{a_{s}b_{s}}(X_{s})-\mathcal{A}V^{a_{s}b_{s}}(X_{s})ds\bigg]\\
\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\;=\mathbb{E}\big[e^{-r\theta^{*}_{k-1}}V^{a_{\theta^{*}_{k-1}}b_{\theta^{*}_{k-1}}}(X_{\theta^{*}_{k-1}})-e^{-r\theta^{*}_{k}}V^{a_{\theta^{*}_{k-1}}b_{\theta^{*}_{k-1}}}(X_{\theta^{*}_{k}})\big]\end{array}

(6.3)

Substituting this into (
6.1
), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}J(x,\delta^{*},\nu^{*})=\mathbb{E}\bigg[V^{ij}(x)+\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\big[\{-V^{a_{\theta^{*}_{k-1}}b_{\theta^{*}_{k-1}}}(X_{\theta^{*}_{k}})+V^{a_{\theta^{*}_{k}}b_{\theta^{*}_{k}}}(X_{\theta^{*}_{k}})\\
\qquad\qquad-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m\geq 1}C(\xi^{*}_{m-1},\xi^{*}_{m})1\!\!1_{[\tau^{*}_{m}=\theta^{*}_{k}]}+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\chi(\eta^{*}_{n-1},\eta^{*}_{n})1\!\!1_{[\rho^{*}_{n}=\theta^{*}_{k}]}\}e^{-r\theta^{*}_{k}}\big]\bigg].\end{array}

(6.4)

We now estimate the term in the right-hand side of (
6.4
) for each
k\geq 1
.

•

If
\theta^{*}_{k}=\tau^{*}_{m}
for some
m\geq 1
we have

V^{\xi^{*}_{m}b_{\theta^{*}_{k-1}}}(X_{\theta^{*}_{k}})-V^{\xi^{*}_{m-1}b_{\theta^{*}_{k-1}}}(X_{\theta^{*}_{k}})-C(\xi^{*}_{m-1},\xi^{*}_{m})=0

(6.5)

•

If
\theta^{*}_{k}=\rho^{*}_{n}
for some
n\geq 1
we have

V^{a_{\theta^{*}_{k-1}}\eta^{*}_{n}}(X_{\theta^{*}_{k}})-V^{a_{\theta^{*}_{k-1}}\eta^{*}_{n-1}}(X_{\theta^{*}_{k}})+\chi(\eta^{*}_{n-1},\eta^{*}_{n})=0

(6.6)

•

If
\theta^{*}_{k}=\tau^{*}_{m}=\rho^{*}_{n}
for some
m,n\geq 1
we have

V^{\xi^{*}_{m}\eta^{*}_{n}}(X_{\theta^{*}_{k}})-V^{\xi^{*}_{m-1}\eta^{*}_{n-1}}(X_{\theta^{*}_{k}})+\chi(\eta^{*}_{n-1},\eta^{*}_{n})-C(\xi^{*}_{m-1},\xi^{*}_{m})=0.

(6.7)

By (
6.4
) and the estimates in (
6.5
)-(
6.7
) it follows that

J(x,\delta^{*},\nu^{*})=V^{ij}(x)\qquad\qquad\Box

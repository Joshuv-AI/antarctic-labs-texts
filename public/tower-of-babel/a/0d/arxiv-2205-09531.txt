Hydrogen-like ions in plasma environment
Neetik Mukherjee a, Chandra N. Patra b, Amlan K. Roy a

Abstract
The behavior of H-like ions embedded in astrophysical plasmas in the form of dense, strongly and weakly coupled plasmas are investigated. In these, the increase and decrease in temperature is impacted with a change in confinement radius ( r c ) (r_{c}) . Two independent and generalized scaling ideas have been applied to modulate the effect of plasma screening constant ( λ \lambda ) and charge of ion ( Z Z ) on such systems. Several new relations are derived to interconnect the original Hamiltonian and two scaled Hamiltonians. In exponential cosine screened Coulomb potential (ECSCP) (dense) and weakly coupled plasma (WCP) these scaling relations have provided a linear equation connecting the critical screening constant ( λ ( c ) ) (\lambda^{(c)}) and Z Z . Their ratio offers a state-dependent constant, beyond which, a particular state vanishes. Shannon entropy has been employed to understand the plasma effect on the ion. With increase in λ \lambda , the accumulation of opposite charge surrounding the ion increases leading to a reduction in number of bound states. However, with rise in ionic charge Z Z , this effect can be delayed. The competing effect of plasma charge density ( n e n_{e} ) and temperature in WCP and ECSCP is investigated. A recently proposed simple virial-like theorem has been established for these systems. Multipole ( k = 1 − 4 k=1-4 ) oscillator strength (OS) and polarizabilities for these are studied considering 1 ​ s , 2 ​ s 1s,2s states. As a bonus, analytical closed-form expressions are derived for f ( k ) f^{(k)} and α ( k ) ​ ( k = 1 − 4 ) \alpha^{(k)}(k=1-4) involving 1 ​ s 1s and 2 ​ s 2s state, for free H-like ion . PACS: 03.65.-w, 03.65.Ca, 03.65.Ge, 03.65.Db. Keywords: Plasma environment, virial theorem, oscillator strength, polarizabilities

\left[-\frac{1}{2}\ \frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2r^{2}}+V_{c}(r)+V_{0}\ \theta(r-r_{c})\right]\psi_{n,\ell}(r)=\mathcal{E}_{n,\ell}\ \psi_{n,\ell}(r).

\infty

is a Heaviside function that
reaches 1 at
r=r_{c}
, while
zero
otherwise, whereas

represents the various plasma potential discussed later in this section.

II.1
Virial-like Theorem

Recently a virial-like relation has been proposed for free and confined quantum systems, by invoking the time-independent
non-relativistic SE and Hypervirial theorem
mukherjee19
. The generalized form of this equation is expressed as,

\langle\hat{T}^{2}\rangle_{n}-\langle\hat{T}\rangle^{2}_{n}=\langle\hat{V}^{2}\rangle_{n}-\langle\hat{V}\rangle^{2}_{n}

(4)

It can be used as a necessary condition for an exact quantum system to obey. Further, it has been proved that, the following equation,

\displaystyle(\Delta\hat{T}_{n})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\hat{V}\rangle_{n}\langle\hat{T}\rangle_{n}-\langle\hat{T}\hat{V}\rangle_{n}=(\Delta\hat{V}_{n})^{2}=\langle\hat{T}\rangle_{n}\langle\hat{V}\rangle_{n}-\langle\hat{V}\hat{T}\rangle_{n},

(5)

can act as a sufficient condition for a bound, stationary state
mukherjee19
. Moreover, an alteration in boundary condition does not
influence the general form. These are applicable in all coordinate systems, such as ellipsoidal, parabolic, cylindrical, spheroidal, etc.
This also holds good in unconfined and confined systems (including angular confinement). In the present endeavor, this has been extended to
the plasma environment.

II.2
Multipole polarizabilities

The static multipole polarizabilities can be expressed in following form,

\alpha^{(k)}_{i}=\alpha^{(k)}_{i}(\mathrm{bound})+\alpha^{k}_{i}(\mathrm{continuum}).

(6)

It is customary to write

\displaystyle\alpha^{(k)}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\frac{f^{(k)}_{ni}}{(\mathcal{E}_{n}-\mathcal{E}_{i})^{2}}-c\int\frac{|\langle R_{i}|r^{k}Y_{kq}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})|R_{\epsilon n}\rangle|^{2}}{(\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon n}-\mathcal{E}_{i})}\ \mathrm{d}\epsilon,

(7)

\displaystyle\alpha^{(k)}_{i}(\mathrm{bound})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\frac{f^{(k)}_{ni}}{(\Delta\mathcal{E}_{ni}},\ \ \ \ \ \alpha^{k}_{i}(\mathrm{continuum})=c\int\frac{|\langle R_{i}|r^{k}Y_{kq}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})|R_{\epsilon n}\rangle|^{2}}{(\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon n}-\mathcal{E}_{i})}\ \mathrm{d}\epsilon.

In Eq. (
6

k

(8)

Designating the initial and final states as
|n\ell m\rangle
and
|n^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}\rangle

f^{(k)}_{ni}=\frac{8\pi}{(2k+1)}\ \Delta\mathcal{E}_{ni}\ \frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m}\sum_{m^{\prime}}|\langle n^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}|r^{k}Y_{kq}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})|n\ell m\rangle|^{2}.

(9)

The application of Wigner-Eckart theorem and sum rule for
3j
symbol further leads to,

f^{(k)}_{ni}=2\ \frac{(2\ell^{\prime}+1)}{(2k+1)}\ \Delta\mathcal{E}_{ni}\ |\langle r^{k}\rangle^{n^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}}_{n\ell}|^{2}\ \left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\ell^{\prime}\ \ k\ \ \ell\\
0\ \ 0\ \ 0\end{array}\right\}^{2}.

(10)

The transition matrix element is expresses by the radial integral,

\langle r^{k}\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}R_{n^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}}(r)r^{k}R_{n\ell}(r)r^{2}\mathrm{d}r.

(11)

Thus it is clear that

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\ \Delta\mathcal{E}_{np-is}\ |\langle r\rangle^{np}_{is}|^{2}.

(12)

The quadrupole OS (

\displaystyle f^{(2)}_{nd-is}

\displaystyle=2\ \Delta\mathcal{E}_{nd-is}|\langle r^{2}\rangle^{nd}_{is}|^{2}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}2\ \ 1\ \ 0\\

\displaystyle=Z^{2}\ \mathcal{E}_{n,\ell}\ \psi_{n,\ell}(r_{1}).

The eigenfunctions, eigenvalues of initial and modified Hamiltonians are connected as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n,\ell}\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]

\displaystyle=Z^{2}\mathcal{E}_{n,\ell}\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right],

(24)

\displaystyle\psi_{n,\ell}(1;Z;\beta;r_{c};r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z^{\frac{3}{2}}}\psi_{n,\ell}\left(1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c};r_{1}\right).

Then
\langle T^{n}\rangle,\langle V^{n}\rangle,\langle TV\rangle
and
Z
are found to be related as,

\displaystyle\langle V^{n}\rangle\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]

\displaystyle=Z^{2n}\ \langle V^{n}\rangle\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right],\ \langle T^{n}\rangle\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]=Z^{2n}\ \langle T^{n}\rangle\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right]

(25)

\displaystyle\langle TV\rangle\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]

\displaystyle=Z^{4}\ \langle TV\rangle\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right],\ \langle VT\rangle\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]=Z^{4}\ \langle VT\rangle\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right]

The multipole OS now takes the form,

f^{(k)}_{ni}\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]=\frac{f^{(k)}_{ni}\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right]}{Z^{2(k-1)}}.

(26)

This equation suggests that, dipole (
k=1
) OS is independent of this scaling transformation. However, quadrupole (
k=2
), octupole (
k=3
) and
hexadecapole (
k=4
) OSs depend on
Z
. Now, some simple mathematical manipulation provides the modified expression
of
\alpha^{(k)}_{i}(\mathrm{bound})
as follows,

\alpha^{(k)}_{i}(\mathrm{bound})\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]=\frac{\alpha^{(k)}_{i}(\mathrm{bound})\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right]}{Z^{2k+2}}.

(27)

(ii)

Another transformation
(r=\frac{r_{2}}{\beta})
, can be applied to alter the same Hamiltonian as,

H(Z;\beta;r_{c};r)\rightarrow H\left(\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c};r_{2}\right).

(28)

Now the potential is mapped such that, the
\beta
-containing part becomes free of it.

The substitution of
r=\frac{r_{2}}{\beta}
transforms the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
20
) in the form,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}\psi_{n,\ell}(r_{2})+V_{c}\left(\frac{Z}{\beta};1;r_{2}\right)\psi_{n,\ell}(r_{2})+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}V_{0}\theta\left(r_{2}-\beta r_{c}\right)\psi_{n,\ell}(r_{2})

(29)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n,\ell}}{\beta^{2}}\right)\psi_{n,\ell}(r_{2}).

The eigenfunctions, eigenvalues of initial and modified Hamiltonians are related as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n,\ell}\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]

\displaystyle=\beta^{2}\ \ \mathcal{E}_{n,\ell}\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right],

(30)

\displaystyle\psi_{n,\ell}(1;Z;\beta;r_{c};r)

\displaystyle=\beta^{\frac{3}{2}}\ \psi_{n,\ell}\left(1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c};r_{2}\right).

Then
\langle T^{n}\rangle,\langle V^{n}\rangle,\langle TV\rangle
and
\beta
are connected as,

\displaystyle\langle V^{n}\rangle\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]=\beta^{2n}\ \ \langle V^{n}\rangle\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right],\ \ \ \ \langle T^{n}\rangle\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]=\beta^{2n}\ \ \langle T^{2}\rangle\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right]

(31)

\displaystyle\langle TV\rangle\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]=\beta^{4}\ \ \langle TV\rangle\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right],\ \ \ \ \langle VT\rangle\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]=\beta^{4}\ \ \langle VT\rangle\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right]

Now, using Eq. (
25
) into Eq. (
9
), the multipole OS can have the generalized form,

f^{(k)}_{ni}\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]=\left(\frac{f^{(k)}_{ni}\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right]}{\beta^{(2k-2)}}\right).

(32)

This implies that, dipole OS is invariant under this scaling transformation. However, higher order (
k>1
) OS
depend on
\beta
. Again some straightforward mathematical manipulation gives the modified expression of
\alpha^{(k)}_{i}(\mathrm{bound})
as,

\alpha^{(k)}_{i}(\mathrm{bound})\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]=\left(\frac{\alpha^{(k)}_{i}(\mathrm{bound})\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right]}{\beta^{2(k+1)}}\right)

(33)

Thus we have successfully converted the initial Hamiltonian, Eq. (
3
) into two independent scaled Hamiltonians,
viz.
, Eqs. (
23
) and (
29
).
Now, the connecting relations are,

\mathcal{E}_{n,\ell}\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]=Z^{2}\mathcal{E}_{n,\ell}\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right]=\beta^{2}\ \ \mathcal{E}_{n,\ell}\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right].

(34)

Some reorganization leads to the following,

\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n,\ell}\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right]}{\mathcal{E}_{n,\ell}\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right]}=\left(\frac{\beta}{Z}\right)^{2}.

(35)

The expectation values then satisfy the following relations,

\langle V^{n}\rangle\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]=Z^{2n}\langle V^{n}\rangle\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right]=\beta^{2n}\langle V^{n}\rangle\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right].

(36)

A slight rearrangement of the above equation leads to,

\frac{\langle V^{n}\rangle\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right]}{\langle V^{n}\rangle\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right]}=\left(\frac{\beta}{Z}\right)^{2n}.

(37)

In case of kinetic energy, one gets,

\langle T^{n}\rangle\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]=Z^{2n}\langle T^{n}\rangle\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right]=\beta^{2n}\langle T^{n}\rangle\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right].

(38)

which, upon rearrangement, gives,

\frac{\langle T^{n}\rangle\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right]}{\langle T^{n}\rangle\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right]}=\left(\frac{\beta}{Z}\right)^{2n}.

(39)

The multipole OS accordingly becomes,

f^{(k)}_{ni}\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]=\frac{f^{(k)}_{ni}\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right]}{Z^{2(k-1)}}=\frac{f^{(k)}_{ni}\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right]}{\beta^{2(k-1)}}.

(40)

which can be recast to yield,

\frac{f^{(k)}_{ni}\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right]}{f^{(k)}_{ni}\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right]}=\left(\frac{Z}{\beta}\right)^{2(k-1)}.

(41)

Finally, the polarizabilities are connected as,

\alpha^{(k)}_{i}(\mathrm{bound})\left[1;Z;\beta;r_{c}\right]=\frac{\alpha^{(k)}_{i}(\mathrm{bound})\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right]}{Z^{2(k+1)}}=\frac{\alpha^{(k)}_{i}(\mathrm{bound})\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right]}{\beta^{2(k+1)}}.

(42)

This can be written in the following form,

\frac{\alpha^{(k)}_{i}(\mathrm{bound})\left[1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c}\right]}{\alpha^{(k)}_{i}(\mathrm{bound})\left[1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c}\right]}=\left(\frac{Z}{\beta}\right)^{2(k+1)}.

(43)

The foregoing discussion thus shows that, a connection formula, as below, can be derived among three Hamiltonians, corresponding to the
SE in Eqs. (
3
), (
23
) and (
29
),
viz.
,

H\left(1;1;\frac{\beta}{Z};Zr_{c};r_{1}\right)\leftrightarrow H(1;Z;\beta;r_{c};r)\leftrightarrow H\left(1;\frac{Z}{\beta};1;\beta r_{c};r_{2}\right)

(44)

The above equation signifies that, performing the calculation at a particular (
Z,\beta
) pair, one can evaluate the properties of other pair of

(Z,\beta)
(connected by scaling), without solving the SE. These are derived for any two-parameter potentials. These relations are applicable in
all the three potentials used for the plasma characteristics in Sec. II.C. In WCP, ECSCP and SCP,
\beta
becomes
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\sigma=\left(\frac{Z-Ne}{2R^{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}
respectively. Some representative numerical results
(\mathcal{E}_{n,\ell},f^{(1)}_{ns\rightarrow 2p},\alpha^{(1)}_{ns})
for these three Hamiltonians (connecting WCP, ECSCP, SCP) have been provided in Table V of Appendix B.

III.2
Virial-like Theorem

As mentioned in Sec. II.A, the conventional VT is not satisfied in confined condition. Recently
mukherjee19
, a virial-like expression is derived
and successfully applied in H-atom trapped in various confined environment
mukherjee19
. It is found that, at the end, the perturbing potential
does not appear in the final expression. In this subsection, we are probing this theorem in the context of WCP, ECSCP and SCP successively.

In WCP, the necessary expectation values will take the form,

\displaystyle\langle TV\rangle_{n,\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\langle T\left(-\frac{Z}{r}e^{(-\lambda_{1}r)}\right)\right\rangle_{n,\ell},\ \ \ \langle VT\rangle_{n,\ell}=\left\langle\left(-\frac{Z}{r}e^{(-\lambda_{1}r)}\right)T\right\rangle_{n,\ell},

(48)

\displaystyle\langle V^{2}\rangle_{n,\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{Z^{2}}{r^{2}}e^{(-2\lambda_{1}r)}\right\rangle_{n,\ell},\ \ \ \langle V\rangle_{n,\ell}=\left\langle-\frac{Z}{r}e^{(-\lambda_{1}r)}\right\rangle_{n,\ell}.

Now, applying the expression of Eq. (
48
) in Eq. (
5
) we obtain,

\displaystyle\langle T^{2}\rangle_{n,\ell}-\langle T\rangle^{2}_{n,\ell}=(\Delta T_{n,\ell})^{2}=\langle V^{2}\rangle_{n,\ell}-\langle V\rangle^{2}_{n,\ell}=(\Delta V_{n,\ell})^{2}

(49)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{Z^{2}}{r^{2}}e^{(-2\lambda_{1}r)}\right\rangle_{n,\ell}-\left\langle\frac{Z}{r}e^{(-\lambda_{1}r)}\right\rangle^{2}_{n,\ell}

\displaystyle=\langle T\rangle_{n,\ell}\left\langle-\frac{Z}{r}e^{(-\lambda_{1}r)}\right\rangle_{n,\ell}-\left\langle T\left(-\frac{Z}{r}e^{(-\lambda_{1}r)}\right)\right\rangle_{n,\ell}.

The relevant expectation values in ECSCP are expressed as,

\displaystyle\langle TV\rangle_{n,\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\langle T\left(-\frac{Z}{r}e^{(-\lambda_{2}r)}\cos\lambda_{2}r\right)\right\rangle_{n,\ell},\ \ \ \langle VT\rangle_{n,\ell}=\left\langle\left(-\frac{Z}{r}e^{(-\lambda_{1}r)}\cos\lambda_{2}r\right)T\right\rangle_{n,\ell},

(50)

\displaystyle\langle V^{2}\rangle_{n,\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{Z^{2}}{r^{2}}e^{(-2\lambda_{2}r)}\cos^{2}\lambda_{2}r\right\rangle_{n,\ell},\ \ \ \langle V\rangle_{n,\ell}=\left\langle-\frac{Z}{r}e^{(-\lambda_{2}r)}\cos\lambda_{2}r\right\rangle_{n,\ell}.

Now, substituting the results of Eq. (
50
) in Eq. (
5
) we achieve,

\displaystyle\langle T^{2}\rangle_{n,\ell}-\langle T\rangle^{2}_{n,\ell}=(\Delta T_{n,\ell})^{2}=\langle V^{2}\rangle_{n,\ell}-\langle V\rangle^{2}_{n,\ell}=(\Delta V_{n,\ell})^{2}

(51)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{Z^{2}}{r^{2}}e^{(-2\lambda_{2}r)}\cos^{2}\lambda_{2}r\right\rangle_{n,\ell}-\left\langle\frac{Z}{r}e^{(-\lambda_{2}r)}\cos\lambda_{2}r\right\rangle^{2}_{n,\ell}

\displaystyle=\langle T\rangle_{n,\ell}\left\langle-\frac{Z}{r}e^{(-\lambda_{2}r)}\cos\lambda_{2}r\right\rangle_{n,\ell}-\left\langle T\left(-\frac{Z}{r}e^{(-\lambda_{2}r)}\right)\cos\lambda_{2}r\right\rangle_{n,\ell}.

In SCP, the respective expectation values are manifested as,

\displaystyle\langle TV\rangle_{n,\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\langle T\left[-\frac{Z}{r}+\frac{(Z-N_{e})}{R}\left(3-\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2}\right)\right]\right\rangle_{n,\ell},

(52)

\displaystyle\langle VT\rangle_{n,\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left[-\frac{Z}{r}+\frac{(Z-N_{e})}{R}\left(3-\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2}\right)\right]T\right\rangle_{n,\ell},

\displaystyle\langle V^{2}\rangle_{n,\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left[-\frac{Z}{r}+\frac{(Z-N_{e})}{R}\left(3-\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2}\right)\right]^{2}\right\rangle_{n,\ell},

\displaystyle\langle V\rangle_{n,\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left[-\frac{Z}{r}+\frac{(Z-N_{e})}{R}\left(3-\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2}\right)\right]\right\rangle_{n,\ell}.

Finally, engaging the outcome of Eq. (
52
) in Eq. (
5
) becomes,

\displaystyle\langle T^{2}\rangle_{n,\ell}-\langle T\rangle^{2}_{n,\ell}=(\Delta T_{n,\ell})^{2}=\langle V^{2}\rangle_{n,\ell}-\langle V\rangle^{2}_{n,\ell}=(\Delta V_{n,\ell})^{2}

(53)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left[-\frac{Z}{r}+\frac{(Z-N_{e})}{R}\left(3-\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2}\right)\right]^{2}\right\rangle_{n,\ell}-\left\langle\left[-\frac{Z}{r}+\frac{(Z-N_{e})}{R}\left(3-\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2}\right)\right]\right\rangle_{n,\ell}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle T\rangle_{n,\ell}\left\langle\left[-\frac{Z}{r}+\frac{(Z-N_{e})}{R}\left(3-\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2}\right)\right]\right\rangle_{n,\ell}-\left\langle T\left[-\frac{Z}{r}+\frac{(Z-N_{e})}{R}\left(3-\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2}\right)\right]\right\rangle_{n,\ell}.

IV
conclusion

Multipole (up to order 4) OS and polarizabilities are probed for H-like ions in WCP, ECSCP and SCP. In first two cases, investigation
is done in both
free
and
confined
conditions. The connection between
T
and
r_{c}
is proposed and analyzed. It is
found that, the plasma tail effect can be controlled by introducing this confinement. Two generalized
scaling ideas are derived connecting
Z
and
\lambda
separately. The relation between these two independent ideas is also achieved.
Starting from a given Hamiltonian and using these designed relations, one can easily extract results for a series of Hamiltonian.
A new
S_{r}
-driven technique is designed to determine
\lambda^{(c)}_{n,\ell}
for both WCP and ECSCP in free environment accurately,
where it shoots up stiffly. Further, using
S_{r}
-based results, and this scaling idea, a generalized relation between
\lambda^{(c)}_{n,\ell}

and
Z
is proposed, which is applicable to an arbitrary state. The applicability of a recently proposed virial-like theorem has been verified
to the plasma systems studied here. Results are also presented in
free
WCP and ECSCP. A detailed investigation of these
spectroscopic properties for
\ell\neq 0
states would be highly desirable. The influence of plasma
screening effect on two-photon transition amplitude, photoionization cross section also need to be explored in confined condition.
Other information-theoretic quantities like Fisher information, Onicescu energy, complexity, mutual and relative information, etc.,
are required to be examined. Exploration of Hellmann-Feynman theorem in the context of
confined
plasma is necessary. Similar
calculation in Helium plasmas may provide vital insight about the effect of confinement on many-electron plasmas.

Dispersive Effective Model in the Time-Domain for Acoustic Waves Propagating in Bubbly Media
Arpan Mukherjee, Mourad Sini

Abstract
We derive the effective medium theory for the linearized time-domain acoustic waves propagating in a bubbly media. The analysis is done in the time-domain avoiding the need to use Fourier transformation. This allows considering general incident waves avoiding band limited ones as usually used in the literature. Most importantly, the outcome is as follows: 1. As the bubbles are resonating, with the unique subwavelength Minnaert resonance, the derived effective wave model is dispersive. Precisely, the effective acoustic model is an integro-differential one with a time-convolution term highlighting the resonance effect. 2. The periodicity in distributing the cluster of bubbles is not needed, contrary to the case of using traditional two-scale homogenization procedures. Precisely, given any C 1 C^{1} -smooth function K K , we can distribute the bubbles so that locally the number of such bubbles is dictated by K K . In addition to its dispersive character, the effective model is affected by the function K K . Such freedom and generality is appreciable in different applied sciences including materials sciences and mathematical imaging. Key words. Time-Domain Acoustic Scattering, Time-Domain Effective Medium Theory, Contrasting Bubbly Media, Asymptotic Analysis, Retarded Layer and Volume Potentials, Lippmann–Schwinger equation. AMS subject classifications. 35C20, 35L05, 37L50, 35P25, 78M35

In the first part of this section, our aim is to demonstrate the existence and uniqueness of the time-domain Lippmann-Schwinger equation (

3.1.1
Existence and Operator Estimate

Proposition 3.1
.

The Lippmann-Schwinger equation

\hbar\;{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\;\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\bm{\mathrm{Y}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})+\bm{\mathrm{Y}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})+\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\frac{b}{4\pi|x-y|}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}{\mathbf{Y}(x,t-c_{0}^{-1}|x-y|)}dy=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}u^{\textbf{in}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t}),\mbox{ for }\mathrm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathrm{t}\in(0,\mathrm{T}),

(3.3)

has a unique solution in
\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{r}}_{0,\sigma}\big(0,\mathrm{T};\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})\big)
for
u^{\textbf{in}}\in\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{r}+3}_{0,\sigma}\big(0,\mathrm{T};\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})\big)
and it satisfies

\|\bm{\mathrm{Y}}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{r}}_{0,\sigma}\big(0,\mathrm{T};\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})\big)}\lesssim\|u^{\textbf{in}}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{r}+3}_{0,\sigma}\big(0,\mathrm{T};\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})\big)}.

Proof.

First, we define the retarded volume potential
\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathrm{D}}
by

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\big[f\big](\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t}):=\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{c}}}{4\pi|\mathrm{x}-\mathrm{y}|}f(\mathrm{y},\mathrm{t}-\mathrm{c}_{0}^{-1}|\mathrm{x}-\mathrm{y}|)d\mathrm{y},\quad(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})\in\mathbf{\Omega}\times(0,\mathrm{T}).

(3.4)

We, then, analyze the LS equation (
3.3
) with the Laplace-Fourier transform. Let us consider the transform parameter
\mathbf{s}=\sigma+i\omega\in\mathbb{C},
with
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\mathrm{D}\in\mathbb{R}.

We then take the Laplace-Fourier transform of the time domain LS equation, we obtain the following LS equation in the Laplace-Fourier domain:

\displaystyle(\hbar\;\mathbf{s}^{2}+1)\;\hat{\mathbf{Y}}+\mathbf{s}^{2}\;\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\big(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}\big)=\mathbf{s}^{2}\;\widehat{u}^{\textbf{in}},\;\text{in}\;\mathbf{\Omega}

(3.5)

where, we define
\displaystyle\widehat{u}:=\widehat{u}(x,\mathbf{s})=\int_{0}^{\infty}u(x,t)e^{-\mathbf{s}t}dt.

We therefore, consider the following problem of finding
\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})
for a given
\widehat{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})
such that

\displaystyle(\hbar\;\mathbf{s}^{2}+1)\;\hat{\mathbf{Y}}+\mathbf{s}^{2}\;\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\big(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}\big)=\widehat{f}\;\text{in}\;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega}),

(3.6)

where,
\widehat{f}:=\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{b}}}{\mathrm{k}_{\mathrm{b}}}\;\mathbf{s}^{2}\;\widehat{u}^{\textbf{in}}.

We aim to establish the well-posedness of the aforementioned problem by adopting the approach outlined in
[
15
]
. Essentially, we formulate the variational scheme for this problem and employ the Lax-Milgram Lemma to demonstrate its well-posedness.

Let us proceed for that. We multiply the equation (
3.6
) by
\widehat{g}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})
and we integrate over
\mathbf{\Omega}
to obtain the following variational form to find
\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}\big(\hat{\mathbf{Y}},\widehat{g}\big)=\mathbb{B}(\widehat{g})\;\text{in}\;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega}),

(3.7)

where,

\mathbb{A}\big(\hat{\mathbf{Y}},\widehat{g}\big):=\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\Big((\hbar\;\mathbf{s}^{2}+1)\hat{\mathbf{Y}}+\mathbf{s}^{2}\;\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{D}}\big(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}\big)\Big)\;\overline{\widehat{g}}\;dy\quad\text{and}\;\mathbb{B}(\widehat{g}):=\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\widehat{f}\;\overline{\widehat{g}}\;dy.

Now, to prove the coercivity of the above variational form, we choose
\widehat{g}=\mathbf{s}\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}\big(\hat{\mathbf{Y}},\mathbf{s}\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}\big)=\mathbf{s}|\mathbf{s}|^{2}\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}|\hat{\mathbf{Y}}|^{2}\;dy+\overline{\mathbf{s}}\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}|\hat{\mathbf{Y}}|^{2}\;dy+\mathbf{s}|\mathbf{s}|^{2}\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{D}}\big(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}\big)\;\overline{\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}}\;dy.

(3.8)

After Taking Real part of the above equation we derive

\displaystyle\Re\Big(\mathbb{A}\big(\hat{\mathbf{Y}},\mathbf{s}\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}\big)\Big)=\Re\Big(\mathbf{s}|\mathbf{s}|^{2}\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}|\hat{\mathbf{Y}}|^{2}\;dy\Big)+\sigma\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}|\hat{\mathbf{Y}}|^{2}\;dy+\Re\Big(\mathbf{s}\mathbf{s}^{2}\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{D}}\big(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}\big)\;\overline{\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}}\;dy\Big),\;\text{with}\;\sigma>0.

(3.9)

We now denote
\mathrm{z}:=\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\big(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}\big)
and then, we have

\displaystyle-\Delta\mathrm{z}+\bm{\mathrm{s}}^{2}\mathrm{z}=\hat{\mathbf{Y}}\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(3.10)

Therefore, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{D}}\big(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}\big)\;\overline{\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}}\;dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{z}\ \overline{\Big(-\Delta\mathrm{z}+\bm{\mathrm{s}}^{2}\mathrm{z}\Big)}d\mathrm{y}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\mathrm{z}|^{2}+\overline{\bm{\mathrm{s}}}^{2}\;|\mathrm{z}|^{2}d\mathrm{y}.

Then it follows that

\displaystyle\Re\Big(\mathbf{s}\mathbf{s}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathrm{D}}\big(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}\big)\;\overline{\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}}\;dy\Big)=\Re\Big(\bm{\mathrm{s}}|\bm{\mathrm{s}}|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\mathrm{z}|^{2}d\mathrm{y}+\frac{\overline{\bm{\mathrm{s}}}|\bm{\mathrm{s}}|^{4}}{\mathrm{c}^{2}_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mathrm{z}|^{2}d\mathrm{y}\Big)\geq 0.

We also have

\Re\Big(\mathbf{s}|\mathbf{s}|^{2}\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}|\hat{\mathbf{Y}}|^{2}\;dy\Big)\geq 0.

Thus, we have shown that

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}\big(\hat{\mathbf{Y}},\mathbf{s}\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}\big)\geq\sigma\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})}^{2}.

(3.11)

We also have

\displaystyle|\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{s}\widehat{\mathbf{Y}})|:=\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\widehat{f}\;\overline{\mathbf{s}\;\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}}\;dy|

\displaystyle=\Big||\mathbf{\Omega}|\;\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\mathbf{s}\mathbf{s}^{2}\;\widehat{u}^{\textbf{in}}\;\hat{\mathbf{Y}}\;d\mathrm{x}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq|\bm{\mathrm{s}}|^{3}\|\widehat{u}^{\textbf{in}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})}\|\hat{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})}.

(3.12)

Therefore, we deduce that

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\Big((\hbar\;\mathbf{s}^{2}+1)\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{s}^{2}\;\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{V}}}_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\Big)^{-1}\Bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})}\leq\frac{|\bm{\mathrm{s}}|^{3}}{\sigma}.

(3.13)

Let us then define the following operator:

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbf{\Omega}}:=\big(\hbar\;\partial_{\mathrm{t}}^{2}+1\big)\mathbf{I}+\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\partial_{\mathrm{t}}^{2}.

(3.14)

Following Lubich’s notation
[
17
]
, and using the techniques of the Laplace-Fourier transform as discussed in
[
19
,
23
,
15
]
(or see (
2.15
)), we can show that

\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbf{\Omega}}^{-1}:\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{r}+3}_{0,\sigma}\big(0,\mathrm{T};\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})\big)\to\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{r}}_{0,\sigma}\big(0,\mathrm{T};\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})\big)

is bounded.

Thus, we show that the equation (
3.2
) has a unique solution in
\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{r}}_{0,\sigma}\big(0,\mathrm{T};\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})\big)
for
u^{\textbf{in}}\in\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{r}+3}_{0,\sigma}\big(0,\mathrm{T};\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})\big).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.1.2

In this section, we analyze the higher regularity of the solution to the problem (
3.3

Corollary 3.1
.

We consider the Lippmann-Schwinger equation (
3.2

\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial t^{3}}\mathbf{Y}\in\mathrm{C}\big(0,\mathrm{T};L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\Omega})\big)

Proof.

We first rewrite (

\displaystyle\hbar\;\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\bm{\mathrm{Y}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})+\bm{\mathrm{Y}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}u^{\textbf{in}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})-\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\frac{b}{4\pi|x-y|}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}{\mathrm{Y}(x,t-c_{0}^{-1}|x-y|)}dy.

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\mathbf{Y}\in\mathrm{H}^{5}_{0,\sigma}\big(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbf{\Omega})\big)

It is known that the retarded volume potential belongs to

for a density

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\hbar\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{t}^{2}}\mathbf{Y}(\cdot,\mathrm{t})+\mathbf{Y}(\cdot,\mathrm{t})=\mathbf{F}(\cdot,\mathrm{t})\mbox{ in }(0,\mathrm{T}),\\

\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial t^{3}}\mathbf{Y}\in\mathrm{C}\big(0,\mathrm{T};L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\Omega})\big).

So, we obtain that
\mathbf{F}\in\mathrm{H}^{4}_{0,\sigma}\big(0,\mathrm{T};L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\Omega})\big)
. Therefore, we have the following after taking the partial derivative with respect to

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{x_{i}}\mathbf{F}(x,t)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\frac{b}{4\pi|x-y|^{2}}\Big|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}{\mathrm{Y}(y,t-c_{0}^{-1}|x-y|)}\Big|dy+\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\frac{1}{4\pi|x-y|}\Big|\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial t^{3}}{\mathrm{Y}(y,t-c_{0}^{-1}|x-y|)}\Big|dy+\big|\partial_{x}u^{\textbf{in}}(x,t)\big|

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(1),

(3.18)

which implies that
\partial_{x_{i}}\mathbf{F}\in\mathrm{H}^{4}_{0,\sigma}\big(0,\mathrm{T};L^{\infty}(\mathbf{\Omega})\big).
Therefore, if we repeat the above steps, i.e., using the well-posedness of the non-homogeneous differential equation, we have that

Therefore, due to the fact that

and regularity in time of the solution

Let us now consider two representative locations, denoted as

(4.5)

where
\textbf{err}_{(4)}:=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\

is describing the

-block interactions between the inclusions located in

and
\mathrm{D}_{j}
for

and the incident source

. We define the operator
\mathbb{A}:(\mathrm{L}_{r}^{2})^{K+1}\to(\mathrm{L}_{r}^{2})^{K+1}
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}=\big[\mathbcal a_{ij}\big]_{i,j=1}^{K+1}:=\begin{pmatrix}\hbar_{m_{1}}&0&\dots&0\\
0&\hbar_{m_{1}}&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\

(4.6)

with

Using Taylor’s series expansion, the counting lemma (
3.1
) and the regularity of the solution of problem (
4.1
), we deduce the following estimates

\displaystyle\textbf{err}_{(4)}

\displaystyle:=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}^{[\varepsilon^{-1}]}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\end{subarray}}^{K+1}\mathrm{q}_{z_{m_{l}},z_{j_{i}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{t}^{2}}\Big(\widetilde{\mathrm{Y}}_{j_{i}}(\mathrm{t}-\mathrm{c}_{0}^{-1}|\mathrm{z}_{m_{l}}-\mathrm{z}_{j_{i}}|)-\widetilde{\mathrm{Y}}_{j_{i}}(\mathrm{t}-\mathrm{c}_{0}^{-1}|\mathrm{z}_{m}-\mathrm{z}_{j}|)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}^{[\varepsilon^{-1}]}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\end{subarray}}^{K+1}\mathrm{q}_{z_{m_{l}},z_{j_{i}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{t}^{2}}\Big(\widetilde{\mathrm{Y}}_{j_{i}}(\mathrm{t}-\mathrm{c}_{0}^{-1}|\mathrm{z}_{m_{l}}-\mathrm{z}_{j_{i}}|)-\widetilde{\mathrm{Y}}_{j_{i}}(\mathrm{t}-\mathrm{c}_{0}^{-1}|\mathrm{z}_{m_{l}}-\mathrm{z}_{j}|)\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}^{[\varepsilon^{-1}]}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\end{subarray}}^{K+1}\mathrm{q}_{z_{m_{l}},z_{j_{i}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{t}^{2}}\Big(\widetilde{\mathrm{Y}}_{j_{i}}(\mathrm{t}-\mathrm{c}_{0}^{-1}|\mathrm{z}_{m_{l}}-\mathrm{z}_{j}|)-\widetilde{\mathrm{Y}}_{j_{i}}(\mathrm{t}-\mathrm{c}_{0}^{-1}|\mathrm{z}_{m}-\mathrm{z}_{j}|)\Big)

j\neq m\end{subarray}}^{[\varepsilon^{-1}]}\frac{1}{d_{mj}}\Big)=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}).

Therefore, we can rewrite the system (

) as follows

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbb{B}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{t}^{2}}\bm{\mathbb{Y}}(\mathrm{t})+\bm{\mathbb{Y}}(\mathrm{t})=\mathbcal H^{\textbf{in}}+\textbf{err}_{(4)}\mbox{ in }(0,\mathrm{T}),\\
\bm{\mathbb{Y}}(\mathrm{0})=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{t}}\bm{\mathbb{Y}}(\mathrm{0})=0,\end{cases}

(4.7)

where, we define the operator

as

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}=\big[\mathbcal B_{ij}\big]_{i,j=1}^{M}=\mathbb{B}(t):=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{A}_{11}&\dots&\mathbb{C}_{1M}\cdot\mathbb{T}_{1M}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathbb{C}_{M1}\cdot\mathbb{T}_{M1}&\dots&\mathbb{A}_{MM}\end{pmatrix},

(4.8)

with
\mathrm{L}_{r}^{2}:=\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(-r,\mathrm{T}):f=0\;\text{in}\ (-r,0)\}
, the translation operators
\mathbb{T}_{1M}:=\big(\mathcal{T}_{11},\mathcal{T}_{12},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{1M}\big)^{t}
, i.e.
\mathcal{T}_{1M}(f)(t):=f(t-\mathrm{c}_{0}^{-1}|\mathrm{z}_{1}-\mathrm{z}_{M}|)
, and
\ell:=\max_{i\neq j}{\mathrm{c}^{-1}_{0}|z_{1}-z_{M}|}
and
\bm{\mathbb{Y}}=\Big(\mathbb{Y}_{1},\mathbb{Y}_{2},\ldots,\mathbb{Y}_{M}\Big)^{t}.

We observe that
\mathcal{B}_{ii}
is non-singular as
d_{m_{l}}\neq 0\;\text{for}\ m=1,2,...,M.

Additionally, we require the supplementary assumption that

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{\left[\varepsilon^{-1}\right]}\left\|\mathbcal{B}_{ii}^{-1}\mathbcal{B}_{ij}\right\|_{L^{2}_{r}}<1\quad\text{for}\quad i=1,2,\ldots,M,

which is equivalent to the condition

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{\left[\varepsilon^{-1}\right]}\left\|\mathbcal{B}_{ij}\right\|_{L^{2}_{r}}<\left(\left\|\mathbcal{B}_{ii}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\right)^{-1}.

Now, considering
\mathbb{B}=[\mathcal{B}_{ij}]
with blocks
\mathcal{B}_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{K+1\times K+1}
for
i,j=1,2,\ldots,M
, from the previously established result regarding the equivalence of norms, we can infer that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}_{ij}\|_{L^{2}_{r}}\leq\sqrt{K+1}\|\mathcal{B}_{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\sqrt{K+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,l=1\\
i\neq l\end{subarray}}^{K+1}\mathrm{q}_{\mathrm{z}_{m_{l}},\mathrm{z}_{j_{i}}},

(4.9)

where

is finite and
\mathrm{q}_{\mathrm{z}_{m_{l}},\mathrm{z}_{j_{i}}}
is positive. More explicitly, the following condition holds under the assumption

\sqrt{K+1}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}^{M}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,l=1\\
i\neq l\end{subarray}}^{K+1}\mathrm{q}_{\mathrm{z}_{m_{l}},\mathrm{z}_{j_{i}}}<\min\limits_{1\leq l\leq K+1}d_{m_{l}}
. If both of these conditions hold, we refer to
\mathbb{B}
as row block diagonally dominant with respect to the operator norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{r}}
. It is a well-known result in numerical linear algebra that if
\mathbb{B}
is row block strictly diagonally dominant, then it is non-singular. Consequently, after neglecting the error order terms, the system of differential equations (
4.7
) reduces to the following form, and similarly, the well-posedness of the system can be established, as discussed in
[
20
, Section 2.4]
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\bm{\mathbb{Y}}(t)+\mathbb{B}^{-1}\bm{\mathbb{Y}}(t)=\mathbb{B}^{-1}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\mathbcal H^{\textbf{in}}\;\text{in}\;(0,T),\\
\bm{\mathbb{Y}}(0)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bm{\mathbb{Y}}(0)=0.\end{cases}

(4.10)

The aforementioned discussion on the well-posedness of the system is needed in the following two subsections, namely
4.1.2
and
4.2
, to derive an estimate similar to that in (
3.29
).

4.1.1
Reformulation of the Linear System and the Related Lippmann-Schwinger Equation

We start by recalling the algebraic system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbb{A}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{t}^{2}}\bm{\mathbb{Y}}_{m}(\mathrm{t})+\bm{\mathbb{Y}}_{m}(\mathrm{t})+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq m\end{subarray}}^{[\varepsilon^{-1}]}\mathbb{C}_{mj}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{t}^{2}}\mathbb{Y}_{j}(\mathrm{t}-\mathrm{c}_{0}^{-1}|\mathrm{z}_{m}-\mathrm{z}_{j}|)=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{t}^{2}}\mathbcal H_{m}^{\textbf{in}}+\textbf{err}_{(4)}\mbox{ in }(0,\mathrm{T}),\\
\bm{\mathbb{Y}}_{m}(\mathrm{0})=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{t}}\bm{\mathbb{Y}}_{m}(\mathrm{0})=0.\end{cases}

(4.11)

We intend to link the system mentioned above to an integral equation, similar to what we did for the case of the periodic distribution. We consider the following related integral equation by neglecting the error term as follows:

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\;\mathbb{A}\ \cdot\ \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\mathbcal{F}_{m}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})+\mathbcal{F}_{m}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})+\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\mathbcal C(x,y)\ \cdot\ \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}{\mathbcal{F}_{m}(x,t-c_{0}^{-1}|x-y|)}dy=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\mathbcal{F}_{m}^{\textbf{in}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t}),\mbox{ for }\mathrm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathrm{t}\in(0,\mathrm{T}),

(4.12)

where we have
\mathbcal{F}_{m}^{\textbf{in}}:=\big(u^{\textbf{in}},u^{\textbf{in}},\ldots,u^{\textbf{in}}\big)^{t}
. We will also include the initial conditions for
\mathbcal{F}:=\big(\mathcal{F}_{m_{1}},\mathcal{F}_{m_{2}},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{m_{K+1}}\big)^{t}
up to the first order in this equation. Here,
\mathbcal C
is the representation of the corresponding interaction matrix

\displaystyle\mathbcal{C}(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbcal q(x,y)&\ldots&\mathbcal q(x,y)\\
\mathbcal q(x,y)&0&\ldots&\mathbcal q(x,y)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathbcal q(x,y)&\mathbcal q(x,y)&\ldots&0\end{pmatrix},\;\text{with}\;\mathbcal q(x,y)=\frac{b}{|x-y|}\;\text{and}\;x\neq y.

(4.13)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}=\big[\mathbcal a_{ij}\big]_{i,j=1}^{K+1}:=\begin{pmatrix}\hbar&0&\dots&0\\
0&\hbar&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&\hbar\end{pmatrix}.

(4.14)

If we now observe the interaction matrix
\mathbcal C
and the definition of
\mathbcal F_{m},
we rewrite the above system as follows

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\;\mathbb{A}\ \cdot\ \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\mathbcal F_{m}(x,t)+\mathbcal{F}_{m}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})+\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbcal q(x,y)\cdot\sum\limits_{l=1}^{K+1}\mathcal{F}_{m_{l}}(\mathrm{y},\mathrm{t}-c_{0}^{-1}|x-y|)\\
\vdots\\
\mathbcal q(x,y)\cdot\sum\limits_{l=1}^{K+1}\mathcal{F}_{m_{l}}(\mathrm{y},\mathrm{t}-c_{0}^{-1}|x-y|)\end{pmatrix}dy=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\mathbcal{F}_{m}^{\textbf{in}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t}),\mbox{ for }\mathrm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathrm{t}\in(0,\mathrm{T}).

(4.15)

It is evident from the preceding equation that the vector
\mathbcal{F}(\cdot,\cdot)
can be reconstructed as the sum of its components, i.e.,
\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{K+1}\mathcal{F}_{m_{\ell}}(\cdot,\cdot)
. Indeed, summing in (
4.15
), we obtain the following integral equation for
\mathrm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathrm{t}\in(0,\mathrm{T}),
for
\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{K+1}\mathcal{F}_{m_{\ell}}(\cdot,\cdot)

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\;\hbar\ \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{K+1}\mathcal{F}_{m_{l}}(x,t)+\sum\limits_{l=1}^{K+1}\mathcal{F}_{m_{l}}(x,t)+\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}(K+1)\ \mathbcal q(x,y)\ \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{K+1}\mathcal{F}_{m_{l}}(\mathrm{y},\mathrm{t}-c_{0}^{-1}|x-y|)dy=(\mathrm{K}+1)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}u^{\textbf{in}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t}).

(4.16)

Then, we arrive at the following expression after rewriting the unknown as
\sum\limits_{l=1}^{K+1}\mathcal{R}_{m_{l}}(x,t):=\frac{1}{\mathrm{K}+1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{K+1}\mathcal{F}_{m_{l}}(x,t)
,

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\;\hbar\ \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{K+1}\mathcal{R}_{m_{l}}(x,t)+\sum\limits_{l=1}^{K+1}\mathcal{R}_{m_{l}}(x,t)+\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}(K+1)\ \mathbcal q(x,y)\ \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{K+1}\mathcal{R}_{m_{l}}(\mathrm{y},\mathrm{t}-c_{0}^{-1}|x-y|)dy=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}u^{\textbf{in}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t}).

(4.17)

4.1.2
The generated effective medium

Similar to the periodic case, we first define the unknown variable

(4.18)

with zero initial conditions for
\mathbcal G_{m}
up to the first order in this equation. Then, we consider

\displaystyle\mathbcal V(x,t):=\begin{cases}\mathbcal G_{m}(x,t)+\hbar\ \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\mathbcal G_{m}(x,t)&\text{if}\;(x,t)\in\mathbf{\Omega}\times(0,T)\\
\displaystyle u^{\textbf{in}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})-\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}(K+1)\frac{b}{4\pi|x-y|}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}{\mathbcal G_{m}(y,t-c_{0}^{-1}|x-y|)}dy&\text{for}\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\mathbf{\Omega}\times(0,T).\end{cases}

(4.19)

We set
\mathbcal W(x,t):=u^{\textbf{in}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})-\mathbcal V(x,t).
Therefore, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbcal W(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}(K+1)\frac{b}{4\pi|x-y|}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}{\mathbcal G_{m}(y,t-c_{0}^{-1}|x-y|)}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}(K+1)\frac{b}{4\pi|x-y|}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}{\sum\limits_{l=1}^{K+1}\mathcal{G}_{m_{l}}(y,t-c_{0}^{-1}|x-y|)}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{\Omega}}(K+1)\frac{b}{4\pi|x-y|}{\sum\limits_{l=1}^{K+1}\mathcal{R}_{m_{l}}(y,t-c_{0}^{-1}|x-y|)}dy.

(4.20)

Let us begin by recalling the related scattered field approximation (
1.6

\displaystyle u^{\mathrm{s}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\mathrm{M}}\frac{\alpha_{m}\rho_{\mathrm{c}}}{4\pi|\mathrm{x}-\mathrm{z}_{i}|}\;|\mathrm{D}_{m}|\;\widetilde{\mathrm{Y}}_{m}\big(\mathrm{t}-\mathrm{c}_{0}^{-1}|\mathrm{x}-\mathrm{z}_{m}|\big)+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2-l})\;\text{as}\;\delta\to 0

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\end{subarray}}^{[\varepsilon^{-1}]}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\end{subarray}}^{K+1}\frac{\alpha_{m_{l}}\rho_{\mathrm{c}}}{4\pi|\mathrm{x}-\mathrm{z}_{m_{1}}|}\;|\mathrm{D}_{m_{l}}|\;\frac{\rho_{b_{l}}}{\mathrm{k}_{b_{l}}}\widetilde{\mathrm{Y}}_{m_{l}}(\mathrm{t}-\mathrm{c}_{0}^{-1}|x-\mathrm{z}_{m_{l}}|)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\end{subarray}}^{[\varepsilon^{-1}]}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\end{subarray}}^{K+1}\frac{\alpha_{m_{l}}\rho_{\mathrm{c}}}{4\pi}\;|\mathrm{D}_{m_{l}}|\;\frac{\rho_{b_{l}}}{\mathrm{k}_{b_{l}}}\Big(\frac{1}{|\mathrm{x}-\mathrm{z}_{m_{l}}|}-\frac{1}{|\mathrm{x}-\mathrm{z}_{m_{1}}|}\Big)\;\widetilde{\mathrm{Y}}_{m_{l}}(\mathrm{t}-\mathrm{c}_{0}^{-1}|x-\mathrm{z}_{m_{l}}|)+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2-l})

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\end{subarray}}^{[\varepsilon^{-1}]}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\end{subarray}}^{K+1}\frac{b}{4\pi}\Big(\frac{1}{|\mathrm{x}-\mathrm{z}_{m_{l}}|}-\frac{1}{|\mathrm{x}-\mathrm{z}_{m_{1}}|}\Big)\;\widetilde{\mathrm{Y}}_{m_{l}}(\mathrm{t}-\mathrm{c}_{0}^{-1}|x-\mathrm{z}_{m_{l}}|)}_{:=\;\textbf{err}^{(1)}}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2-l}).

(4.21)

Let us estimate

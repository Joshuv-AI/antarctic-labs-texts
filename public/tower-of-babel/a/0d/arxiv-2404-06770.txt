Vibrational ADAPT-VQE: Critical points leads to problematic convergence
Marco Majland, Patrick Ettenhuber, Nikolaj Thomas Zinner, Ove Christiansen

Abstract
Quantum chemistry is one of the most promising applications for which quantum computing is expected to have significant impact. Despite considerable research in the field of electronic structure, calculating the vibrational properties of molecules on quantum computers remain a relatively unexplored field. In this work, we develop a vibrational ADAPT-VQE formalism based on an infinite product representation of anti-Hermitian excitation operators of the Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction wavefunction which allows for preparing eigenstates of vibrational Hamiltonians on quantum computers. In order to establish the vibrational ADAPT-VQE algorithm using the infinite product representation, we study the exactness of disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster theory and show that disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster can formally represent the Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction wavefunction in an infinite expansion. To investigate the performance of the vibrational ADAPT-VQE algorithm, we numerically study whether the vibrational ADAPT-VQE algorithm generates a sequence of operators which may represent the Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction wavefunction. Our numerical results indicate frequent appearance of critical points in the wavefunction preparation using vibrational ADAPT-VQE. These results imply that one may encounter diminishing usefulness when preparing vibrational wavefunctions on quantum computers using vibrational ADAPT-VQE and that additional studies are required to find methods that can circumvent this behavior.

Such methods are referred to as ADAPT-VQE algorithms and rely on gradient-based constructions of the wavefunctions. Such methods, however, are reliant on gradient evaluations which impose difficulties in estimating optimal sequences of operators when the algorithms are subject to noise. However, current studies imply that the ADAPT-VQE algorithms yield more compact wavefunctions compared to UCC wavefunctions in noiseless simulations

The use of ADAPT-VQE for electronic structure calculations relies on the fact that an infinite product of exponentials of low rank excitation operators can formally parametrize the exact electronic full configuration interaction wavefunction

,

.
Evangelista et al. have conducted a comprehensive study on the exactness and associated conditions of such a disentangled Unitary Coupled Cluster theory within electronic structure theory
[

. In addition, numerical investigations suggest that the ordered pools selected by the gradient descent method of ADAPT-VQE may not be optimal and the sequence identified may differ from a finite part of the IPR sequence following from dUCC
[
23

. However, it is an open question whether the disentangled Unitary Coupled Cluster and the vibrational ADAPT-VQE Ansatz can exactly parametrize an exact vibrational wave function.
In this paper, we develop a vibrational ADAPT-VQE algorithm through demonstrating that the Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction wavefunction may be constructed using as an infinite product representation of low rank anti-Hermitian excitation operators. The infinite product representation representation is obtained through studying the exactness of the disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster Ansatz analogous to the methods utilized in Ref.
[
22
]
.
Our work is solely concerned on with the exact representability of the Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction using an infinite product representation as a means for formulating the vibrational ADAPT-VQE algorithm. While optimal orderings of excitation operators in finite product representation of the disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster Ansatz is a very interesting problem, such investigations are outside the scope of the this work. [

25

In contrast to the electronic structure case, our numerical studies of vibrational ADAPT-VQE reveal the emergence of critical points that may impede the convergence of the algorithm. Such critical points have been discussed using the UCC path integral formalism in Ref.
[
22
]
as a theoretical possibility but they have not been observed in practice for the electronic structure case. Furthermore, we numerically investigate the properties of the emerging critical points by varying the parameters of the calculation. We find that the vibrational ADAPT-VQE algorithm appears to converge to distinct subspaces of the vibrational Hamiltonian, depending only on the operator pool but not on the order in which the operators are used, indicating larger critical surfaces rather than isolated critical points.

Theory

II.1
Background

We employ a many-mode second quantization formalism for vibrational structure. In this section, we will introduce the relevant theoretical background along with conventional notation as was used in Ref.
[
26
]
.

Let
\{m_{1},m_{2},...\}
denote distinguishable vibrational modes and let
\{p,q,r,...\}
denote general one-mode functions (modals) for each mode. Let
\{i,j,k,...\}
denote occupied modals and
\{a,b,c,...\}
denote unoccupied modals. The operators
a^{m_{1}\mspace{-0.5mu}\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle\dagger$}}_{\mspace{-0mu}p^{m_{1}}}
and
a^{m_{1}}_{\mspace{-0mu}q^{m_{1}}}
create and annihilate occupation in one-mode basis functions (modals) indexed by
p^{m_{1}}
and
q^{m_{1}}
for a given mode
m_{1}
.
The creation and annihilation operators satisfy commutator relations

[a^{m_{1}}_{\mspace{-0mu}p^{m_{1}}},a^{m_{2}\mspace{-0.5mu}\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle\dagger$}}_{\mspace{-0mu}q^{m_{2}}}]=\delta_{m_{1}m_{2}}\delta_{p^{m_{1}}q^{m_{2}}}

(1)

and

[a^{m_{1}\mspace{-0.5mu}\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle\dagger$}}_{\mspace{-0mu}p^{m_{1}}},a^{m_{2}\mspace{-0.5mu}\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle\dagger$}}_{\mspace{-0mu}q^{m_{2}}}]=[a^{m_{1}}_{\mspace{-0mu}p^{m_{1}}},a^{m_{2}}_{\mspace{-0mu}q^{m_{2}}}]=0

(2)

Note that the creation and annihilation operators are not harmonic oscillator ladder operators and the modals are not assumed to be harmonic oscillator functions. Rather they are formally general one-mode basis functions. In practice a set of modals may, as in this work, be obtained from Vibrational Self-Consistent Field theory. The creation and annihilation operators are encoded onto a quantum computer using the direct encoding. The optimal choice of encoding depends on the structure of the operators to be encoded. However, the direct encoding provides a reasonable trade-off between qubit and gate counts
[
8
,
10
]
.

An anti-Hermitian
N
-body excitation operator is defined as

\kappa_{i^{m_{1}}j^{m_{2}}..}^{a^{m_{1}}b^{m_{2}}...}=\tau_{i^{m_{1}}j^{m_{2}}...}^{a^{m_{1}}b^{m_{2}}...}-\textrm{h.c.},

(3)

with

\tau_{i^{m_{1}}j^{m_{2}}...}^{a^{m_{1}}b^{m_{2}}...}=a^{m_{1}\mspace{-0.5mu}\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle\dagger$}}_{\mspace{-0mu}a^{m_{1}}}a^{m_{1}}_{\mspace{-0mu}i^{m_{1}}}a^{m_{2}\mspace{-0.5mu}\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle\dagger$}}_{\mspace{-0mu}b^{m_{2}}}a^{m_{2}}_{\mspace{-0mu}j^{m_{2}}}a^{m_{3}\mspace{-0.5mu}\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle\dagger$}}_{\mspace{-0mu}c^{m_{3}}}a^{m_{3}}_{\mspace{-0mu}k^{m_{3}}}\dots,

(4)

denoting the vibrational excitation operators.
As in conventional Vibrational Coupled Cluster theory
[
26
]
the cluster operators are defined as

T_{1}=\sum_{m}\sum_{a^{m}}t^{m}_{a^{m}}\tau_{i^{m}}^{a^{m}},

(5)

and

\begin{split}T_{2}&=\sum_{m_{1}<m_{2}}\sum_{a^{m_{1}}b^{m_{2}}}t_{a^{m_{1}}b^{m_{2}}}^{m_{1}m_{2}}\tau^{a^{m_{1}}b^{m_{2}}}_{i^{m_{1}}j^{m_{2}}},\\
\end{split}

(6)

where the cluster amplitudes are real parameters.
Unlike the conventional VCC ansatz of the wave function as
\ket{\Psi_{\textrm{VCC}}}=e^{T}\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}}
we will here follow a unitary formulation as presented in Ref.
[
7
]
for the vibrational case and in accord with the long history of electronic unitary CC theory.
[
27
,
28
]

The Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster ansatz accordingly takes the form

\ket{\Psi_{\textrm{UVCC}}}=e^{T-T^{\dagger}}\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}},

(7)

where
\boldsymbol{i}
denotes the indices of the ground state modals, i.e. the modals that are occupied in the reference state. The cluster operator,
e^{T-T^{\dagger}}
, may not be efficiently decomposed into a set of quantum gates. Thus, it is attractive to turn to the so-called disentangled form
[
22
]
.

II.2
The infinite product representation of the Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction wavefunction

The infinite product representation is defined as

\ket{\Psi_{\textrm{IPR}}}=\prod_{i}^{\infty}e^{t_{\mu_{i}}\kappa_{\mu_{i}}}\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}}

(8)

where
\kappa_{\mu_{i}}
are low rank anti-hermitian excitation operators.
In order to show that an infinite product representation may be used to represent the Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction wavefunction, we present a general formulation of the disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster Ansatz in Section
II.2.1
. In Section
II.2.2
, we show that high-order excitation operators may be decomposed into low rank excitation operators using nested commutator relations. In Section
II.2.3
, we combine these results to show that the infinite product representation may parametrize the Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction wavefunction.

II.2.1
The disentangled unitary vibrational coupled cluster ansatz

The disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster Ansatz is the logical equivalent of the electronic disentangled unitary coupled cluster ansatz
[
22
]
and reads

\ket{\Psi_{\textrm{dUVCC}}}=\prod_{\mu}e^{t_{\mu}\kappa_{\mu}}\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}}.

(9)

The disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster Ansatz may be interpreted as a first-order Trotter expansion of Eq. (
7
). However, since Eq. (
7
) does not decompose efficiently into quantum gates, one may consider Eq. (
9
) as an Ansatz in its own right rather than an approximation to an Ansatz.
Because the excitation and de-excitation operators do not commute, a finite term Trotter expansion of the unitary ansatz would not be exact.

In order for Eq. (
9
) to be a valuable Ansatz, it must be able to represent any state in the Hilbert space.
Let
\ket{\Psi}
denote a general state,

\ket{\Psi}=\sum_{\mu}c_{\mu}\ket{\Phi_{\mu}}.

(10)

where
\ket{\Phi_{\mu}}
denotes Hartree products which in second quantization correspond to a product of creation operators,
a^{m_{1},\dagger}_{p^{m_{1}}}a^{m_{2},\dagger}_{p^{m_{2}}}\cdots
for t. The summation over
\mu
runs over all possible Hartree-Products. In order for the disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster Ansatz to represent any state in the Hilbert space, the following must be shown,

U_{\textrm{dUVCC}}^{-1}\ket{\Psi}=\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}},

(11)

in analogy to the electronic structure proof in Ref.
[
22
]
.
Thus, the disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster operator must be able to invert the general state to the reference state for a given set of parameters. In this section, we prove this for vibrational wave functions closely following the reasoning of Ref.
[
22
]
.

Consider the general expansion in Eq. (
10
). Each single excited state,

\ket{\Phi_{i^{m}}^{a^{m}}}=a^{m\mspace{-0.5mu}\raisebox{0.90417pt}{$\scriptstyle\dagger$}}_{\mspace{-0mu}a^{m}}a^{m}_{\mspace{-0mu}i^{m}}\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}},

(12)

may be eliminated from
\ket{\Psi}
by applying the inverse rotation operator
e^{-t_{i^{m}}^{a^{m}}\kappa_{i^{m}}^{a^{m}}}
.
If
c_{a^{m}}^{{m}}
is the expansion coefficient of
\ket{\Phi_{i^{m}}^{a^{m}}}
and
c_{\boldsymbol{i}}
is the expansion coefficient of
\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}}
we find

\displaystyle e^{-t_{a^{m}}^{{m}}\kappa_{i^{m}}^{a^{m}}}\ket{\Psi}

\displaystyle=(c_{\boldsymbol{i}}\cos(t_{a^{m}}^{{m}})+c_{a^{m}}^{{m}}\sin(t_{a^{m}}^{{m}})\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}}

\displaystyle+(c_{a^{m}}^{{m}}\cos(t_{a^{m}}^{{m}})-c_{\boldsymbol{i}}\sin(t_{a^{m}}^{{m}}))\ket{\Phi_{i^{m}}^{a^{m}}}+\cdots,

(13)

where the second term can be eliminated by the choice

t_{a^{m}}^{{m}}=\arctan\Big(\frac{c_{a^{m}}^{{m}}}{c_{\boldsymbol{i}}}\Big),

(14)

Such a transformation eliminates
\ket{\Phi_{i^{m}}^{a^{m}}}
from the expansion but may introduce higher order excitations. Importantly, the operation does not introduce any further single excitations to the expansion. Repeatedly, one may perform inverse transformations of each single excitation in the expansion. After such an operation, the expansion no longer contains single excitations but may contain different higher order excitations. By then performing the inverse transformation on the double excitations in the expansion, one may analogously eliminate all such terms. In analogy, these inverse transformations do not introduce any new single or double excitations, but may introduce higher order excitations. By performing the inverse transformations for the entire excitation manifold of the expansion, one arrives at
\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}}
.

II.2.2
Decomposition of many-body operators

The disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster Ansatz contains up to
N
-body operators in order to represent any state in the Hilbert space. However, these operators are very costly to implement. In order to obtain operators which are feasible for Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum devices, one may apply rank reduction through recursive commutation relations to decompose the
N
-body operators into one-body and two-body operators
[
24
,
25
]
. Thus, using the recursive relations, one may iteratively reduce the high rank operators to low rank operators such that the disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster Ansatz may be written in terms of only low rank operators. As an example, a triples anti-Hermitian excitation operator may be written as

\kappa_{i^{m_{1}}j^{m_{2}}k^{m_{3}}}^{a^{m_{1}}b^{m_{2}}c^{m_{3}}}=-[\kappa_{{i}^{m_{1}}{j}^{m_{2}}}^{{a}^{m_{1}}{p}^{m_{2}}},\kappa_{{p}^{m_{2}}{k}^{m_{3}}}^{{b}^{m_{2}}{c}^{m_{3}}}]

(15)

where
p
denotes an general modal for which
p\notin\{j,b\}
, or in terms of particle-hole exications

\kappa_{{i}^{m_{1}}{j}^{m_{2}}{k}^{m_{3}}}^{{a}^{m_{1}}{b}^{m_{2}}{c}^{m_{3}}}=-[\kappa_{{i}^{m_{1}}{j}^{m_{2}}}^{{a}^{m_{1}}{p}^{m_{2}}},[\kappa_{{j}^{m_{2}}}^{{p}^{m_{2}}},\kappa_{{j}^{m_{2}}{k}^{m_{3}}}^{{b}^{m_{2}}{c}^{m_{3}}}]].

(16)

Note that these relations may be recursively extended to higher order operators. The proof is presented in the appendix.

II.2.3
Infinite product representation

To show that the infinite product representation may represent the Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction wavefunction, consider the disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster Ansatz

\ket{\Psi_{\textrm{dUVCC}}}=\prod_{\mu}e^{t_{\mu}\kappa_{\mu}}\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}}.

(17)

which may represent the Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction wavefunction according to Section
II.2.1
. Using Eq.
16
and the high-order equivalents along with the commutator expansion
[
22
]
,

e^{[A,B]}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\Big(e^{A/\sqrt{N}}e^{B/\sqrt{N}}e^{-A/\sqrt{N}}e^{-B/\sqrt{N}}\Big)^{N},

(18)

the disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster Ansatz may be decomposed into an infinite product of low rank excitation operators. Thus, the infinite product representation may represent the Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction wavefunction. Note that the above decomposition is equivalent to that in Ref.
[
22
]
.

We are only concerned with the existence of an infinite product representation. We are not entering into a discussion of the optimal ordering of the operators in a finite disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster Ansatz. While the problem of theoretically investigating orderings of the operators is a very interesting problem, we only use the above result as a prerequisite for establishing the infinite product representation of Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction wavefunctions and investigate the sequence of operators generated using the vibrational ADAPT-VQE algorithm. Thus, we utilize the vibrational ADAPT-VQE algorithm to generate the sequence of operators rather than theoretically calculate a sequence of operators for the disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster Ansatz.

II.3
ADAPT-VQE

The ADAPT-VQE algorithm iteratively builds an Ansatz through successively adding parametrized unitary transformations acting on a reference state
\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}}
. At the
k
’th iteration of the ADAPT-VQE algorithm, the wave function may be written as

\ket{\Psi^{(k)}}=\prod_{\mu\in\mathcal{A}^{(k)}}e^{t_{\mu}\kappa_{\mu}}\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}},

(19)

where
\mathcal{A}^{(k)}
is the ordered sequence of excitations entering the wave function up to iteration
k
,

\kappa_{\mu}=\tau_{\mu}-\tau^{\dagger}_{\mu}
, with
\tau_{\mu}
being an excitation operator,
\mu

enumerates the excitation and
\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}}
denotes the reference state. The operators used to construct the Ansatz is chosen from a pool of operators,
\mathcal{A}=\{\kappa_{\mu}\}
, based on
an importance metric,
w(\kappa_{\mu},\ket{\Psi^{(k)}})
. In standard ADAPT-VQE, the importance metric is the gradient of the energy with respect to the parameter of the operator. The energy of the
k+1
st iteration may be written as

E^{(k+1)}=\bra{\Psi^{(k)}}e^{-t_{\mu}\kappa_{\mu}}He^{t_{\mu}\kappa_{\mu}}\ket{\Psi^{(k)}},

(20)

such that

\begin{split}g_{\mu}&=\left.\frac{\partial E^{(k+1)}}{\partial t_{\mu}}\right|_{t_{\mu}=0}\\
&=\langle\Psi^{(k)}|[H,\kappa_{\mu}]|\Psi^{(k)}\rangle.\end{split}

(21)

The product over
\mu
in Eq.(
19
) is to be interpreted as an ordered set of transformations, which may be i) many and formally infinitely, ii) non-commuting, iii) repeating the same basic operator multiple times.

II.4
Path equations

In this section, we present a formalism,
to describe the path of the wave function from a reference state to an arbitrary state. Following
[
22
]
, the path connecting the reference state
\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}}
and a general state
\ket{\Psi}
may be described by a path
s^{k}=[\mathcal{A}^{(k)},\{t^{(k)}_{\mu}\}]
. Thus, the path depends on the operators
\mathcal{A}^{(k)}
of the Ansatz in iteration
k
along with the parameters
\{t^{(k)}_{\mu}\}
optimized in iteration
k
. Consider the ADAPT-VQE Ansatz which reads

\ket{\Psi^{(k)}(s^{k})}=U(s^{k})\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}}

(22)

where
U(s^{k})=\prod_{\mu}e^{t^{(k)}_{\mu}\kappa_{\mu}}
. The path equation reads

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds^{k}}\ket{\Psi^{(k)}(s^{k})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}\frac{dt^{(k)}_{\mu}}{ds^{k}}\frac{dU(s)}{dt^{(k)}_{\mu}}\ket{\Phi_{\boldsymbol{i}}}

(23)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}\frac{dt^{(k)}_{\mu}}{ds^{k}}\ket{\mu}

(24)

where
\{\ket{\mu}\}
denotes an orthogonal basis for the Ansatz derivative. In order for
\ket{\Psi^{(k)}(s^{k})}
to parameterize any state in the Hilbert space,
s^{k}
must be a solution to the above differential equation. In the case for which
\{\ket{\mu}\}
is not a complete basis,
s^{k}
is not a solution to the differential equation and thus the path
s^{k}
is at a critical point. At this point, one may no longer parametrize any state in the Hilbert space and thus
\ket{\Psi^{(k)}(s^{k})}
may not converge to the exact solution. The incompleteness of
\{\ket{\mu}\}
may be investigated by calculating the rank of the Jacobian,

D_{\mu\nu}^{k}=\braket{\Phi_{\mu}|\nu},

(25)

for which the rank must be rank
(D_{\mu\nu}^{k})=\min(|\{\mu\}|,k)
, where
\{\mu\}
is the set of all possible excitations.

III.3
Critical points

In order to investigate the convergence behaviour of vibrational ADAPT-VQE calculations using the SD and SDT pools, the path equation formalism of Ref.
[
22
]
was utilized. The rank of the Jacobian from Eq. (
25
) was calculated for each iteration,
k
, in the vibrational ADAPT-VQE calculations. In the beginning, the rank of the Jacobian increases with each iteration,
\textrm{rank}(D^{k}_{\mu\nu})=k
. However, around the VCISD (VCISDT) energies in the calculations,
\textrm{rank}(D^{k}_{\mu\nu})=k_{\textrm{convergence}}
remains constant throughout additional iterations at the same time the norm of the maximum gradient for additional operators tested with the vibrational ADAPT-VQE selection criterion is small and becomes constant as shown in Figs.
1
and
2
. Thus, all vibrational ADAPT-VQE calculations using an SD (SDT) pool appear to reach critical points at convergence around the VCISD (VCISDT) energies from which it is not able to escape.

In an attempt to avoid the critical points, several modified algorithms, changing the optimization path, were tested. The first modified algorithm utilizes the operator with maximum gradient (standard vibrational ADAPT-VQE) along with a random operator. The second modified algorithm utilizes the two maximum gradient operators. The third modified algorithm utilizes approximate SDT pool containing the standard SD pool along with products of all combinations of one-body and two-body operators, providing decoupled three-body operators, but not including exact three body operators. The fourth modified algorithm uses a pool, denoted SD(
k
), consisting of the standard SD pool with the
k
most important
exact
three-body operators for SDT, where the importance is determined by the order in which they enter into the wave function when using the full SDT pool.

In Fig.
3
, the calculations for the ground state energy of
\textrm{H}_{2}\textrm{O}
are shown for each of the modified algorithms. The first, second and third modified algorithms converge to critical points around the VCISD energy. Thus, utilizing operator selections which differ from the ”one-operator-gradient-selection” appear to converge to the same critical points. When selecting three-body operators in the operator pool, however, it appears that the critical point close to the VCISD energy is avoided. Nonetheless, the modified SD(

IV
Conclusion

In this work, we have presented a formalism for the vibrational ADAPT-VQE algorithm using the exactness of an infinite product representation for the Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction wavefunction. These results were obtained based on investigations on the exactness of the disentangled Unitary Vibrational Coupled Cluster Ansatz. Despite the formal exactness of the infinite product representation of the Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction wavefunction, we numerically demonstrate the appearance of critical points in the convergence of the vibrational ADAPT-VQE algorithm in calculating the ground state for several small molecules. Thus, vibrational ADAPT-VQE appears to be unable to generate a sequence of operators which converge to the exact vibrational wavefunction.

We investigate different operator pools and mode couplings for different potential energy surfaces in an attempt to escape the critical points. Our findings indicate that the critical points appear to be independent of mode couplings but
dependent on the choice of operators pools suggesting critical regions rather than just individual critical points on the accessible manifold. As one includes higher-order excitation operators, the vibrational ADAPT-VQE algorithm converges closer to the Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction energy. However, in contrast to electronic structure, the vibrational ADAPT-VQE algorithm does not converge to the Full Vibrational Configuration Interaction energy unless the operator pool is complete. Given this fact, and the fact that vibrational ADAPT-VQE converges to about the same energy error as the corresponding classical VCI theory with the same number of parameters, the potential for computational advantage on the quantum computer using vibrational ADAPT-VQE appears out of reach. This conclusion is based on the the fact that the gradient contributions for all operators in the pool needs to be measured to evaluate their importance and the size of the operator pool exhibits the same formal scaling as the scaling of classical CI or CC methods.

We therefore conclude that further method development is necessary to chart a path towards quantum computational advantage for vibrational structure using near-term quantum devices.

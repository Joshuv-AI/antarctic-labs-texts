Loop percolation versus link percolation in the random loop model
Volker Betz, Andreas Klippel, Mino Nicola Kraft

Abstract
. In [ Mü21 ] , Peter Mühlbacher showed that in the random loop model without loop weights, a loop phase transition (assuming it exists) cannot occur at the same parameter as the percolation phase transition of the occupied edges. In this work, we give a quantitative version of this result, specifying a minimal gap between the percolation phase transition and a possible loop phase transition. A substantial part of our argument also works for weighted loop models.

Let us re-introduce the notation

From now on, we will work with

We construct elements of

Let

\begin{split}&\frac{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}(\mathrm{Ex}(c))}{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}(\{c\})}\\

&=\,{\rm e}^{\beta^{+}J}\,.\end{split}

The result now follows since

Appendix A
Stochastic domination

This result provides stochastic domination in the case of local domination making it possible to find a coupling between the locally dominating and dominated measure. We will consider measures on
\{0,1\}^{I}
for some set
I
at most countable. For simplification, we identify
I
with
[N]
for some
N\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
with
[\infty]\coloneqq{\mathbb{N}}
. A similar statement with a slightly different assumption to the one of the following proposition can be found in
[
LSS97
]
.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be
\{0,1\}^{I}
-valued a random variables on such that for every finite set
J\subseteq I
, all
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\in J}\subseteq\{0,1\}
and every
i\in I\backslash J
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(X_{i}=1\Bigg|\forall j\in J:~X_{j}=\varepsilon_{j}\right)\;\geqslant\;\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{i}=1\Bigg|\forall j\in J:~Y_{j}=\varepsilon_{j}\right).

Then
Y
is stochastically dominated by
X
.

Proof.

We define functions
(m_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
by

\displaystyle m_{k}((\varepsilon_{j},\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j})_{j\in[k]})\coloneqq\begin{cases}0\text{ if }(\varepsilon_{k},\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k})=(0,1),\\
\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}=1\big|\forall j\;\leqslant\;k-1:~X_{j}=\varepsilon_{j}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{k}=1\big|\forall j\;\leqslant\;k-1:~Y_{j}=\varepsilon_{j}\right)\\
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{ if }(\varepsilon_{k},\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k})=(1,0),\\
\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{k}=1\big|\forall j\;\leqslant\;k-1:~Y_{j}=\varepsilon_{j}\right)\text{ if }(\varepsilon_{k},\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k})=(1,1),\\
1-\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{k}=1\big|\forall j\;\leqslant\;k-1:~Y_{j}=\varepsilon_{j}\right)\text{ if }(\varepsilon_{k},\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k})=(0,0).\end{cases}

Using Kolmogorovs extension theorem, we find a random variable
Z
on
\{0,1\}^{I}\times\{0,1\}^{I}
such that for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
(\varepsilon_{j},\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j})_{j\in[k]}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z=(\varepsilon_{j},\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j})_{j\in[k]})=\prod_{l=1}^{k}m_{l}((\varepsilon_{j},\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j})_{j\in[l]}).

A straightforward calculation shows
Z(\cdot,\{0,1\}^{I})\overset{d}{=}X(\cdot)
and
Z(\{0,1\}^{I},\cdot)\overset{d}{=}Y(\cdot)
. By the definition of
Z
, we have
Z\in\{(a_{i},b_{i})_{i\in I}\in\{0,1\}^{I}:~a_{i}\;\geqslant\;b_{i}\}

\mathbb{P}
-a.s.. This implies that
Y
is stochastically dominated by
X
.
∎

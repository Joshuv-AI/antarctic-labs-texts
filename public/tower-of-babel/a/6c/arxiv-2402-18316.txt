Stability and instability of the quasilinear Gross–Pitaevskii dark solitons
Erwan Le Quiniou

Abstract
We study a quasilinear Schrödinger equation with nonzero conditions at infinity. In previous works, we obtained a continuous branch of traveling waves, given by dark solitons indexed by their speed. Neglecting the quasilinear term, one recovers the Gross–Pitaevskii equation, for which the branch of dark solitons is stable. Moreover, Z. Lin showed that the Vakhitov–Kolokolov (VK) stability criterion (in terms of the momentum of solitons) holds for general semilinear equations with nonvanishing conditions at infinity. In the quasilinear case, we prove that the VK stability criterion still applies, by generalizing Lin’s arguments. Therefore, we deduce that the branch of dark solitons is stable for weak quasilinear interactions. For stronger quasilinear interactions, a cusp appears in the energy-momentum diagram, implying the stability of fast waves and the instability of slow waves.



Keywords:

Quasilinear Schrödinger equation, Gross–Pitaevskii equation, traveling waves, dark solitons, nonzero conditions at infinity, orbital stability.

Introduction

We consider the following quasilinear Gross–Pitaevskii equation in one dimension

i\partial_{t}\Psi+\partial_{xx}\Psi+\Psi(1-|\Psi|^{2})+\kappa\Psi\partial_{xx}(1-|\Psi|^{2})=0,\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},

(1.1)

where
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
is a parameter measuring the strength of the quasilinear term, and
\Psi:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
, satisfies the nonzero conditions at infinity

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|\Psi(x,\cdot)|=1.

(1.2)

Equation (
1.1
) was introduced as a generalization of the case
\kappa=0
also known as the Gross-Pitaevskii equation, which is a standard model in nonlinear optics and quantum hydrodynamics
[
21
,
16
,
20
]
. For instance, in the context of optical fibers in
[
23
]
, they obtained (
1.1
) with
\kappa\geq 0
to model weak nonlocal interactions in the refracting index of the beam of light. We refer to
[
22
]

1.1
) was also obtained with
\kappa\leq 0
in
[
24
]
to describe superfluid films with surface tension.

Equation (
1.1
) is a particular case of the following quasilinear Schrödinger equation in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

i\partial_{t}\Psi+\Delta\Psi+\Psi F(|\Psi|^{2})-\kappa\Psi h^{\prime}(|\Psi|^{2})\Delta h(|\Psi|^{2})=0,\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R},

(1.3)

with
F(y)=1-y
and
h(y)=1-y
,
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
. To our knowledge, (
1.3
) was only considered with vanishing conditions at infinity by the mathematical community.
Studying (
1.3
) with
d=1
, I. D. Iliev and K. P. Kirchev
[
19
]
obtained a continuous branch of solitary-wave solutions of the form

\Psi(x,t)=u_{\omega}(x)e^{i\omega t},
and showed that the wave is stable whenever
\partial_{\omega}||u_{\omega}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}>0
and unstable when
\partial_{\omega}||u_{\omega}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}<0

In arbitrary space dimension, M. Colin, L. Jeanjean, and M. Squassina
[
12
]
obtained the existence and stability or instability by blowup of the ground state solitary-wave for (
1.3
) with power law nonlinearities.
In the semilinear regime (
\kappa=0
in (
1.3
)), we recover

i\partial_{t}\Psi+\Delta\Psi+F(|\Psi|^{2})\Psi=0,\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}.

(1.4)

With vanishing conditions at infinity, M. I. Weinstein showed in
[

the stability of the solitary wave solution to (
1.4
) satisfying a ground state property using Lyapunov functionals.
Assuming
F(\rho_{0})=0

satisfies the nonzero conditions at infinity

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|\Psi(x,t)|^{2}=\rho_{0},\quad\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R},

Z. Lin investigated in
[

\Psi_{c}(x,t)=u_{c}(x-ct).

In particular, he rigorously proves the VK criterion

We also refer to the work of D. Chiron in
[

1.4

Lemma 5
.

Let
\kappa<1/2
,
c\in(0,\sqrt{2})
and
u_{c,\kappa}
be a dark soliton given by Theorem
1.1
. Then there exists
K
and
\varepsilon>0
depending only on
u_{c,\kappa}
such that for every
\Psi\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{R})
, if
\Psi
satisfies
d_{\mathcal{X}}(\Psi,u_{c,\kappa})<\varepsilon
, then we can write
\Psi=\sqrt{1-\eta}e^{i\theta}
with
(\eta,\partial_{x}\theta)\in X
and we have

\frac{1}{K}d_{\mathrm{hy}}(\Psi,u_{c,\kappa})\leq d_{\mathcal{X}}(\Psi,u_{c,\kappa})\leq Kd_{\mathrm{hy}}(\Psi,u_{c,\kappa}).

(4.1)

Proof.

For clarity’s sake, we recall the main arguments of the proof in
[
11
]
.
We remove
\kappa
in the subscript of
u_{c,\kappa}=u_{c}
and write
u_{c}=\sqrt{1-\eta_{c}}e^{i\theta_{c}}
. Since
u_{c}
does not vanish, taking
\varepsilon>0
small enough we deduce that for any
\Psi\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying

, we have
|||\Psi|-|u_{c}|||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|u_{c}(x)|/2
so that

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\Psi(x)|\geq\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|u_{c}(x)|/2.

(4.2)

We can express
\partial_{x}\Psi
in terms of
\eta
and
\theta
,

\partial_{x}\Psi=e^{i\theta}\left(\frac{\eta_{x}}{2\sqrt{1-\eta}}+i\sqrt{1-\eta}\theta_{x}\right),

(4.3)

and similarly for
u_{c}
.
We also need to bound
||\Psi||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
in terms of
\varepsilon
,
||u_{c}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
and
||\partial_{x}u_{c}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
. To do so, notice that for any constants
y<x
, we have

|\Psi(x)|\leq|\Psi(y)|+\int_{y}^{x}|\partial_{x}\Psi|\leq|u_{c}(y)|+||\Psi(y)|-|u_{c}(y)||+\sqrt{x-y}||\partial_{x}\Psi||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

(4.4)

where we used Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to bound the integral. Taking

then integrating (
4.4
) with respect to

from

to
x

|\Psi(x)|\leq|u_{c}|+\int_{\mathbb{R}}||u_{c}|-|\Psi||+||\partial_{x}\Psi||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

so that

||\Psi||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq K_{0}(u_{c},\varepsilon).

(4.5)

We show that
d_{\mathrm{hy}}(\Psi,u_{c})\leq Kd_{\mathcal{X}}(\Psi,u_{c}).

First notice that
|\Psi|^{2}\theta_{x}=\operatorname{Im}(\Psi_{x}\bar{\Psi})
, thus

\partial_{x}\theta-\partial_{x}\theta_{c}=\operatorname{Im}(\partial_{x}u_{c}\bar{u_{c}})\left(\frac{1}{|\Psi|^{2}}-\frac{1}{|u_{c}|^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{|\Psi|^{2}}(\operatorname{Im}((\partial_{x}\Psi-\partial_{x}u_{c})\bar{\Psi})-\operatorname{Im}(\partial_{x}u_{c}(\bar{u_{c}}-\bar{\Psi}))),

\displaystyle\text{hence, }\hskip 36.98866pt||\partial_{x}\theta-\partial_{x}\theta_{c}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{Im}(\partial_{x}u_{c}\bar{u_{c}})(||\Psi||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+||u_{c}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})})}{\inf_{\mathbb{R}}|\Psi|^{2}\inf_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{c}|^{2}}|||\Psi|-|u_{c}|||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\inf_{\mathbb{R}}|\Psi|^{2}}\left(||\Psi||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}||\partial_{x}\Psi-\partial_{x}u||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+||\partial_{x}u_{c}(\bar{u_{c}}-\bar{\Psi})||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right).

(4.6)

Since
\Psi(x)-u_{c}(x)=\Psi(0)-u_{c}(0)+\int_{0}^{x}\partial_{x}\Psi-\partial_{x}u_{c}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we get

|\partial_{x}u_{c}(x)||\Psi(x)-u_{c}(x)|\leq|\partial_{x}u(x)||\Psi(0)-u_{c}(0)|+|\partial_{x}u_{c}(x)\sqrt{|x|}|||\partial_{x}\Psi-\partial_{x}u_{c}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.

From the decay estimate (

4

(4.8)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle||\partial_{x}\eta-\partial_{x}\eta_{c}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq 2||\Psi||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}||\partial_{x}\Psi-\partial_{x}u_{c}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+2||\partial_{x}{u_{c}}(\bar{u_{c}}-\bar{\Psi})||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

Appendix A
Extensions of the stability result

Finally, we briefly describe the Lyapunov functional approach to the stability of the dark solitons (see
[
10
]
).
For the sake of conciseness, we chose not to use this method in this work to avoid tedious computations.
As pointed out by C. A. Stuart in Section 4–5 in
[
26
]
, in the Hamiltonian system framework of
[
17
]
, the stability criterion (
1.17
) together with
Assumptions 1–3
allow us to construct a Lyapunov function invariant by translation by taking
K
large enough in

V(\eta,v)=E(\eta,v)-cP(\eta,v)-E(u_{c})+cP(u_{c})+K(P(\eta,v)-P(u_{c}))^{2},

(A.1)

giving a quantitative measure of stability. The heuristic is the following, since
V
is constant on the flow of solutions, to ensure the stability, we want
V
to satisfy, for some
\alpha>0

V(\eta,v)\geq\alpha||(\eta,v)-(\eta_{c},v_{c})||^{2}_{X},

for some
\varepsilon>0
and all
\eta
,
v
satisfying
\inf_{s\in\mathbb{R}}||(\eta,v)-(\eta_{c},v_{c})(\cdot-s)||_{X}<\varepsilon
. This problem reduces to the positive definiteness of
V^{\prime\prime}(\eta_{c},v_{c})
which writes

\langle V^{\prime\prime}(\eta_{c},v_{c})(\delta\eta,\delta v),(\delta\eta,\delta v)\rangle=\langle H_{c}(\delta\eta,\delta v),(\delta\eta,\delta v)\rangle+2K(\langle P^{\prime}(u_{c}),(\delta\eta,\delta v)\rangle)^{2}\geq C||(\delta\eta,\delta v)||^{2}_{X},

for some
C>0
.
We readily compute
-P^{\prime}(u_{c})=\partial_{c}(E^{\prime}-cP^{\prime})(u_{c})
and since
E^{\prime}(u_{c})-cP^{\prime}(u_{c})=0
for every
c\in(0,\sqrt{2})
, we get
\partial_{c}(E^{\prime}(u_{c})-cP^{\prime}(u_{c}))=H_{c}\partial_{c}u_{c}-P^{\prime}(u_{c})=0
by the chain rule. Testing this linear form against
\partial_{c}(\eta_{c},v_{c})\in X
, if (
1.17
) holds, we deduce that,

\langle H_{c}\partial_{c}(\eta_{c},v_{c}),\partial_{c}(\eta_{c},v_{c})\rangle=\frac{d}{dc}P(\eta_{c},v_{c})<0.

(A.2)

By Lemma 5.3 in
[
26
]
, considering
Assumption 3
, we can assume the decomposition to be the orthogonal decomposition

u=a\partial_{c}(\eta_{c},v_{c})+b\tau^{\prime}(0)(\eta_{c},v_{c})+p,\quad\text{ for all }u\in X,

(A.3)

where
P=\{\partial_{c}(\eta_{c},v_{c}),\tau^{\prime}(0)(\eta_{c},v_{c})\}^{\perp}
. Then we can show that taking
K
big enough in (
A.1
) yield the coercivity of the quadratic form
V^{\prime\prime}
on
\mathrm{span}\{\partial_{c}(\eta_{c},v_{c})\}
which upon further computations leads to (see Lemma 4.3 in
[
26
]
)

\langle V^{\prime\prime}(\eta_{c},v_{c})(\delta\eta\delta v),(\delta\eta,\delta v)\rangle\geq C||\Pi(\delta\eta,\delta v)||_{X}^{2},

(A.4)

for some
C>0
and where
\Pi
is the orthogonal projection on
\{\tau^{\prime}(0)(\eta_{c},v_{c})\}^{\perp}
.
Finally if
(\delta\eta,\delta v)=(\eta-\eta_{c},v-v_{c})
then we can show that (see Lemma 2.1 in
[
26
]
)

||\Pi(\delta\eta,\delta v)||=\inf_{s\in\mathbb{R}}||(\eta,v)-(\eta_{c},v_{c})(\cdot-s)||_{X}.

(A.5)

For any solution
(\eta,v)
to (
1.7

for all

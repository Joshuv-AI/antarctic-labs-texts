Sharpness in Bohr’s Inequality
Mario Guillén, Pablo Sevilla-Peris

Abstract
We make a careful analysis of Bohr’s inequality, in the line started by Kayumov and Ponnusamy, where some extra summand (depending on the function) is added in the right-hand side of the inequality. We analyse the inequality when smaller radius are taken, giving sharp constants. As a result of this point of view, some previous results are improved.

Introduction

Harald Bohr proved in
[
1
]
the following celebrated result for holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
(the open unit disc of the complex plane), now commonly known as
Bohr’s inequality

Theorem 1.1
.

If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is holomorphic and
|f(z)|\leq 1
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1,\,\text{ for every }r\leq\frac{1}{3}

(1)

and the radius
1/3
is sharp.

This inequality has been carefully analysed and extended under different points of view. One way to look at this is to wonder if there is some room left in the left-hand side of the inequality. To put it in slightly more precise terms, if some extra term can be added, so that the inequality still holds true. A quick thought with the constant function
1
immediately shows that there is no hope to find a term that is valid
for every function
. However, thinking again in constant functions (this time with modulus strictly smaller than
1
), shows that for each particular function there is room for an extra factor. Then, the (for the moment vague) question is: for each particular function
f
, what can be added in the left-hand side of (
1
) (which necessarily has to depend on
f
) so that the inequality still holds. The first steps in this direction were given in
[
4
]
and
[
3
]
, where, for each
f
and
0<r<1
, the following quantity is considered

S_{r}=S_{r}(f)\coloneqq\int_{r\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(w)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}A(w)=\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert^{2}r^{2n}\,,

(2)

(note that if
f
is a univalent function, then this coincides with the area of
f(r\mathbb{D})
). Then in
[
3
, Theorem 10]
the following result is given.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is holomorphic and
|f(z)|\leq 1
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}\right)\leq 1\,,

for every
r\leq 1/3
. Moreover the constants
1/3
and
16/9
are sharp.

Here the fact that the constant
16/9
is sharp means that it is the biggest constant so that the inequality holds for every holomorphic function on
\mathbb{D}
such that
|f(z)|\leq 1
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
(the class of which we denote by
\mathcal{B}
) and every
0<r\leq 1/3
. Our first aim is to give a closer look at this inequality, looking, for each fixed radius
0<R<1/3
, for the best constant
\Lambda(R)
so that, for each
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}
we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\Lambda(R)\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}\right)\leq 1,\,\text{ for every }r\leq R\,.

(3)

Clearly
\Lambda(R)\geq 16/9
.

To tackle this question we take a general point of view, considering arbitrary functions depending on
f
and
r
. Given
\varphi\colon\mathcal{B}\times[0,1/3]\to[0,\infty)
, we look for constants
\lambda_{R}
so that for each
f\in\mathcal{B}
we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\lambda_{R}\varphi(f,r)\leq 1,

(4)

for every
0\leq r\leq R
. With this in mind, the
sharp constant
of
\varphi
for
0\leq R\leq 1/3
is defined as

\Lambda_{\varphi}(R)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\lambda_{R}\geq 0:\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\lambda_{R}\varphi(f,r)\leq 1,\,\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{B}\text{ and }0\leq r\leq R\right\}\,.

Whenever there is no risk of confusion we will simply write
\Lambda(R)
. So, our main aim from now on is to give, for a given function
\varphi
, a reasonable description of
\Lambda_{\varphi}(R)
.

In the definition (and other characterisations, see Proposition
2.1
below), all functions in
\mathcal{B}
are taken into account. We will see that, under certain mild conditions on
\varphi
(see Theorem
2.2
) it is enough to consider functions of the form

\phi_{a}(z)=\frac{z-a}{1-az}

(5)

for
0\leq a\leq 1
(note that, for
0\leq a<1
these are Möbius transformations and
\phi_{1}
is the constant function
-1
). This makes the problem far easier to handle, and in certain cases will allow us to give an explicit formula for
\Lambda_{\varphi}(R)
.

Before we proceed, let us note that, defining
\Upsilon\colon\mathcal{B}\times[0,1/3]\to[0,\infty)

\Upsilon(f,r)\coloneqq 1-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}

(6)

the sharp constant of a function
\varphi
can be rewritten as

\Lambda(R)=\sup\left\{\lambda_{R}\geq 0:\lambda_{R}\leq\frac{\Upsilon(f,r)}{\varphi(f,r)},\,\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{B}\text{ and }0\leq r\leq R\right\}\,.

(7)

2
Sharp constants

As a straightforward consequence of the reformulation of
\Lambda(R)
given in (
7
) we have the following result, which combined with Proposition
2.5
gives a way to check if a given function is feasible.

Proposition 2.1
.

The sharp constant of a function
\varphi
is given by

\Lambda(R)=\inf_{\mathcal{B}\times[0,R]}\frac{\Upsilon(f,r)}{\varphi(f,r)}\,.

Note that the supremum in the definition of
\Lambda(R)
is actually a maximum. In other words,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert r^{n}+\Lambda(R)\varphi(f,r)\leq 1\,,

for every holomorphic function
f
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and every
0\leq r\leq R
.

This expression is difficult to compute because it involves an infimum over
\mathcal{B}
(which in the end is the unit ball of the Banach space
H_{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
of bounded holomorphic functions on the disk). Our next step is to show that, under certain conditions on
\varphi
, it is enough to consider the functions
\phi_{a}
from (
5
). This gives an expression that will be much easier to deal with.

Let us note that simple computations yield

\phi_{a}(z)=-a+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-a^{2})a^{n-1}z^{n}

(8)

for each
0\leq a\leq 1
. This allows us to give a precise formula for
\Upsilon(\phi_{a},r)
: if
0\leq a\leq 1
and
0\leq r\leq 1/3
, then

\begin{split}\Upsilon(\phi_{a},r)=1-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert r^{n}=(1-c)-&\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}r^{n}=(1-a)-(\varphi_{a}(r)+c)\\
&=1-2a-\frac{r-a}{1-ar}=1-a-r\frac{1-a^{2}}{1-ar}\,.\end{split}

(9)

On the other hand, from
[
3
, Lemma 3]
we know that if
f\in\mathcal{B}
, then

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert r^{n}\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} r\dfrac{1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert^{2}}{1-r\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert}&\text{ if }\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert\geq r,\\
r\dfrac{\sqrt{1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-r}}&\text{ if }\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert<r.\end{aligned}\right.

(10)

(just take
p=1
and
m=0
in the statement).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\varphi
be a function such that the following conditions hold

1.

\varphi(f,r)\leq\varphi(\phi_{|a_{0}|},r)
for each
0\leq r\leq 1/3
and for every
f\in\mathcal{B}

2.

\displaystyle\inf_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0 \leq a \leq 1}{0 \leq s \leq R}}\frac{\Upsilon(\phi_{a},s)}{\varphi(\phi_{a},s)}\leq\inf_{0\leq s\leq R}\Bigg(\inf_{0\leq a\leq s}\bigg(1-a-s\frac{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}}\bigg)\frac{1}{\varphi(\phi_{a},s)}\Bigg)
.

Then, its sharp constant is given by

\Lambda(R)=\inf_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0 \leq a \leq 1}{0 \leq r \leq R}}\frac{\Upsilon(\phi_{a},r)}{\varphi(\phi_{a},r)}.

Proof.

Fix
0\leq R\leq 1/3
. From Theorem
2.1
we have

\Lambda(R)=\inf_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{f \in\mathcal{B}}{0 \leq r \leq R}}\frac{\Upsilon(f,r)}{\varphi(f,r)}\leq\inf_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0 \leq a \leq 1}{0 \leq r \leq R}}\frac{\Upsilon(\phi_{a},r)}{\varphi(\phi_{a},r)}\,.

Therefore it is only left to prove that the converse inequality holds, and to do that is suffices to show that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert r^{n}+\bigg(\inf_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0 \leq a \leq 1}{0 \leq s\leq R}}\frac{\Upsilon(\phi_{a},s)}{\varphi(\phi_{a},s)}\bigg)\varphi(f,r)\leq 1\,,

(11)

for every
f\in\mathcal{B}
and
0\leq r\leq R
. We fix, then, some
f
and
r
, and distinguish two cases. First of all, if
r\leq\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert\leq 1
we know from (
10
) that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert r^{n}\leq r\dfrac{1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert^{2}}{1-r\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert}\,.

On the other hand, (
9
) and condition
1
give

\inf_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0 \leq a \leq 1}{0 \leq s\leq R}}\frac{\Upsilon(\phi_{a},s)}{\varphi(\phi_{a},s)}\leq\frac{\Upsilon(\phi_{\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert},s)}{\varphi(\phi_{\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert},s)}\\
=\bigg(1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert-r\frac{1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert^{2}}{1-r\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert}\bigg)\frac{1}{\varphi(\phi_{|a_{0}|},r)}\leq\bigg(1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert-r\frac{1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert^{2}}{1-r\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert}\bigg)\frac{1}{\varphi(f,r)}\,.

These two facts immediately yield (
11
).

Suppose now that
0\leq|a_{0}|\leq r
. Condition
2
and (
9
) give

\inf_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0 \leq a \leq 1}{0 \leq s\leq R}}\frac{\Upsilon(\phi_{a},s)}{\varphi(\phi_{a},s)}\leq\inf_{0\leq s\leq R}\Bigg(\inf_{0\leq a\leq s}\bigg(1-a-s\frac{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}}\bigg)\frac{1}{\varphi(\phi_{a},s)}\Bigg)\\
\leq\inf_{0\leq a\leq r}\bigg(1-a-r\frac{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\bigg)\frac{1}{\varphi(\phi_{a},s)}\leq\bigg(1-|a_{0}|-r\frac{\sqrt{1-|a_{0}|^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\bigg)\frac{1}{\varphi(f,r)}\,.

This, combined with condition
1
and (
10
) show that (
11
) holds also in this case.
∎

The factor in Theorem
2.2
–
2
is going to play some rôle in what follows. To handle it, we define the function
J:[0,1]\times[0,1/3]\to\mathbb{R}
given by

J(a,r)\coloneqq 1-a-r\frac{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\,.

Remark 2.3
.

If, for each fixed
0\leq r\leq 1/3
the function given by
a\rightsquigarrow\frac{J(a,r)}{\varphi(\phi_{a},r)}
is decreasing in
[0,r]
, then condition
2
in Theorem
2.2
is trivially satisfied.

Let us note that the function
J_{r}(a)\coloneqq J(a,r)
is positive and decreasing in
[0,r]
for each
r
. Indeed, we have

J_{r}^{\prime}(a)=-1+\sqrt{\frac{r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}}\frac{a}{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}\,,

Since
a\in[0,r]
and
r\in[0,1/3]
, we have

J^{\prime}(a,r)\leq-1+\frac{r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\leq-1+\frac{1}{3^{2}}<0\,

and
J_{r}
is decreasing. Therefore
J(a,r)\geq J(r,r)=1-2r>0
.

Remark 2.4
.

If, on the other hand,
\varphi
is an increasing function with respect to
r
(for each fixed function
f\in\mathcal{B}
), then things get easier, and its sharp constant is given by

\displaystyle\Lambda(R)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\lambda_{R}\geq 0:\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|R^{n}+\lambda_{R}\varphi(f,R)\leq 1,\,\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{B}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\lambda_{R}\geq 0:\lambda_{R}\leq\frac{\Upsilon(f,R)}{\varphi(f,R)},\,\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{B}\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{f\in\mathcal{B}}\frac{\Upsilon(f,R)}{\varphi(f,R)}\,,

(recall Proposition
2.1
for the last formulation). If
\varphi
moreover satisfies conditions
1
and
2
in Theorem
2.2
, then

\Lambda(R)=\inf_{0\leq a\leq 1}\frac{\Upsilon(\phi_{a},R)}{\varphi(\phi_{a},R)}.

We say that a function
\varphi:\mathcal{B}\times[0,1/3]\to[0,\infty)
is
feasible
if there exists some constant
\lambda>0
such that for every holomorphic
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
we have,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\lambda\varphi(f,r)\leq 1,\,\text{ for every }r\leq\frac{1}{3}.

(12)

With this notation, Theorem
1.2
gives that the function
\varphi(f,r)=\frac{S_{r}(f)}{\pi-S_{r}(f)}
is feasible. On the other hand, the function
\Upsilon
defined in (
6
) is clearly feasible. Observe also that a function
\varphi
is feasible if and only if there is
\lambda>0
such that

\lambda\varphi(f,r)\leq\Upsilon(f,r)

(13)

for all
f
and
r
. This and (
7
) give the following characterisation of feasible functions.

Proposition 2.5
.

A function
\varphi
is feasible if and only if
\Lambda(1/3)>0
.

We give now some necessary conditions for feasible functions.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\varphi
be a feasible function and fix
0\leq r\leq 1/3
. Then,

1.

\varphi(e^{i\theta},r)=0
for every
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

2.

\lim_{\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert\to 1}\varphi(f,r)=0
.

3.

\lim_{a\to 1}\varphi(\phi_{a},r)=0
.

Proof.

If
e^{i\theta}
denotes the constant function, we clearly have
\Upsilon(e^{i\theta},r)=0
, and (
13
) gives
1
.

Fix now
\varepsilon>0
and take
f\in\mathcal{B}
so that
1-|a_{0}|<\varepsilon
. We know from
[
1
]
(see also
[
2
, Lemma 8.4]
) that
0\leq\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert\leq 1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert^{2}
for every
n\geq 1
. A straightforward computation shows that
0\leq\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert\leq 2\varepsilon
and, then

\Upsilon(f,r)=1-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert r^{n}=(1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert)-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert r^{n}<\varepsilon\Big(1-\frac{r}{1-r}\Big)\,.

This shows that
\lim_{\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert\to 1}\Upsilon(f,r)=0
and again (
13
) gives
2
. Finally,
3
is a particular case of
2
.
∎

Note that this implies that no feasible function can be increasing in
[0,1]
(recall Remark
2.3
).

3
Analysing Bohr’s inequality

We can now address our first goal (stated in (
3
)) of making a detailed analysis of Theorem
1.2
. In this case we have the feasible function

\varphi_{0}(f,r)=\frac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{S_{r}/\pi}-1}\,,

where
S_{r}
was defined in (
2
). Note in first place that, for each
0\leq a\leq 1
and
0\leq r\leq 1/3
, (
8
) gives

\frac{S_{r}(\phi_{a})}{\pi}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert^{2}r^{2n}=(1-a^{2})^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n(a^{n-1})^{2}r^{2n}=\frac{r^{2}(1-a^{2})^{2}}{(1-a^{2}r^{2})^{2}}.

Then,

\frac{1}{S_{r}/\pi}-1=\frac{(1-r^{2})(1-r^{2}a^{4})}{r^{2}(1-a^{2})^{2}}\,,

and

\varphi_{0}(\phi_{a},r)=\frac{r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\cdot\frac{(1-a^{2})^{2}}{1-a^{4}r^{2}}\,.

(14)

Also, from (
9
) we have

\Upsilon(\phi_{a},r)=\frac{1-a}{1-ar}\left(1-r(1+2a)\right)\,,

for every
a
and
r
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For every fixed
0\leq R\leq 1/3
, and every holomorphic function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
such that
|f(z)|\leq 1
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert r^{n}+\Lambda(R)\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}\right)\leq 1,\,\text{ for every }0\leq r\leq R\,,

where the sharp constant is given by

\Lambda(R)=\inf_{a\in[0,1]}\frac{1-R^{2}}{R^{2}}\cdot\frac{1-a^{4}R^{2}}{(1-a)(1+a)^{2}}\cdot\frac{1-R(1+2a)}{1-aR}.

(15)

Proof.

The result follows directly from Theorem
2.2
(actually from Remark
2.4
, since
\varphi_{0}(f,\cdot)
is clearly increasing for each fixed
f
), once we have checked that the function
\varphi_{0}
satisfies the two conditions in the statement. To begin with, fix some
f\in\mathcal{B}
and note that
[
4
, Lemma 1]
yields

\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\leq\frac{r^{2}(1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert^{2})^{2}}{(1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert^{2}r^{2})^{2}}\,,

for every
0<r\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
, which gives

1-\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\geq\frac{(1-r^{2})(1-r^{2}\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert^{4})^{2}}{(1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert^{2}r^{2})^{2}}\,.

Hence (recall (
14
))

\varphi_{0}(f,r)\leq\frac{r^{2}(1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert^{2})^{2}}{(1-r^{2})(1-r^{2}\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert^{4})}=\varphi_{0}(\phi_{|a_{0}|},r)

(16)

for every
f\in\mathcal{B}
and
1\leq r\leq 1/3
, showing that
1
in Theorem
2.2
holds.

We show that condition
2
is satisfied by using Remark
2.3
. So, it is enough to see that, for each fixed
0\leq r\leq 1/3
, the function

a\rightsquigarrow\frac{J(a,r)}{\varphi_{0}(\phi_{a},r)}=\frac{1-r^{2}}{r^{2}}\left(1-a-r\sqrt{\frac{1-a^{2}}{1-r^{2}}}\right)\frac{1-a^{4}r^{2}}{(1-a^{2})^{2}}

(17)

is decreasing and positive in
[0,r]
. We start by fixing some such
r
and rewriting the quotient as

\frac{J(a,r)}{\varphi_{0}(\phi_{a},r)}=\frac{1-r^{2}}{r^{2}}\overbrace{(1-a^{4}r^{2})}^{P_{1}(a)}\overbrace{\frac{1}{(1-a)(1+a)^{2}}}^{P_{2}(a)}\overbrace{\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}}\sqrt{\frac{1+a}{1-a}}\right)}^{P_{3}(a)}\,.

(18)

Since
\frac{1-r^{2}}{r^{2}}>0
, we only have to check that each one of the factors
P_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
is decreasing (as functions of
a
) in
[0,r]
and, to see that they are positive, that evaluated at
a=r
are positive. Both conditions are clearly satisfied for
P_{1}
. To see that
P_{2}
is decreasing it is enough to check that the polynomial
(1-a)(1+a)^{2}
is increasing in
[0,r]
. An easy exercise with the first and second derivatives gives that it has a local minimum at
a=-1
, and a local maximum at
a=1/3
; hence it is increasing in
[0,r]
, proving our claim. Again, it is clear that
P_{2}(r)>0
. Finally,
P_{3}
is clearly decreasing in
[0,r]
. As to the evaluation at
a=r
, note that

\sqrt{\frac{r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}}\sqrt{\frac{1+r}{1-r}}=\sqrt{\frac{r^{2}}{(1-r)^{2}}}=\sqrt{\frac{r^{2}}{r^{2}-2r+1}}<1\,,

since
2r<1
. This gives
P_{3}(r)>0
and completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Let us study now the behaviour of the sharp constant in Theorem
3.1
. To do that we consider the function

M(a,r)\coloneqq\frac{\Upsilon(\phi_{a},r)}{\varphi_{0}(\phi_{a},r)}=\frac{1-r^{2}}{r^{2}}\cdot\frac{1-a^{4}r^{2}}{(1-a)(1+a)^{2}}\cdot\frac{1-r(1+2a)}{1-ar}\,,

and we have that
\Lambda(R)=\inf_{0\leq a\leq 1}M(a,R)
for each
0\leq R\leq 1/3
. We note in first place that, taking
R=1/3
we get

\Lambda(1/3)=\inf_{0\leq a<1}M(a,1/3)=\inf_{0\leq a<1}\frac{16}{9}\frac{9-a^{4}}{(1+a)^{2}(3-a)}=\frac{16}{9}\,,

recovering in this way
[
3
, Theorem 10]
.

We see first that the infimum in (
15
) is actually a minimum. Note that, for each fixed
0\leq r\leq 1/3
, the function
f_{r}(a)\coloneqq M(a,r)
is clearly continuous in
[0,1)
and satisfies
\lim_{a\to 1^{-}}f_{r}(a)=+\infty
. Thus there is
a^{*}(r)\in[0,1)
so that

\inf_{0\leq a<1}M(a,r)=M(a^{*}(r),r)\,.

Elementary computations show that
f^{\prime}_{r}(0)=\frac{r^{4}-1}{r^{2}}<0
. Hence the function is decreasing at
0
, which gives that, in fact
a^{*}(r)\in(0,1)
and it is a local minimum (so, a critical point of
f_{r}
). Again, elementary (but tedious) computations show that the critical points of
f_{r}
in
(0,1)
are the roots of the polynomial

p_{r}(x)\coloneqq 2r^{4}x^{7}+2(r-2)r^{3}x^{6}+r^{2}\left(-7r^{2}-4r+1\right)x^{5}+r^{2}\left(-3r^{2}+12r+1\right)x^{4}\\
+2r^{2}(2r+1)x^{3}+2r(r-4)x^{2}-\left(r^{2}-3\right)x-r^{2}-1\,.

Note that, since
p_{0}(x)=3x-1
, we have
\lim_{r\to 0}a^{*}(r)=\frac{1}{3}
.

Let us give now lower and upper bounds for these sharp constants´. For
0\leq a\leq 1
,
0\leq r\leq 1/3
and
\lambda\geq 0
consider the functions

\displaystyle C_{1}(a,r,\lambda)

\displaystyle\coloneqq a+r\frac{1-a^{2}}{1-ar}+\lambda\frac{r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\frac{(1-a^{2})^{2}}{1-a^{4}r^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle C_{2}(a,r,\lambda)

\displaystyle\coloneqq a+r\sqrt{\frac{1-a^{2}}{1-r^{2}}}+\lambda\frac{r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\frac{(1-a^{2})^{2}}{1-a^{4}r^{2}}\,.

Then , as a consequence of (
10
) we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert r^{n}+\lambda\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}\right)\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} C_{1}(|a_{0}|,r,\lambda),\text{ if }0\leq|a_{0}|\leq r,\\
C_{2}(|a_{0}|,r,\lambda),\text{ if }r\leq|a_{0}|\leq 1\,.\end{aligned}\right.

Each one of these is increasing on
r
(for fixed
a

\lambda

) that
C_{i}(a,1/3,16/9)\leq 1
for every

0\leq r\leq 1/3
(and
i=1,2

\lambda_{0}(r)=\frac{2(1-r^{2})}{9r^{2}}
and note that, for each

C_{i}(a,r,\lambda_{0}(r))\leq C_{i}(a,1/3,\lambda_{0}(r))=C_{i}(a,1/3,16/9)\leq 1\,.

This shows that (

\lambda_{0}(r)
and then, by the definition of sharp constant

\frac{2}{9}\left(\frac{1-R^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)\leq\Lambda(R)\,.

For an upper bound let us simply observe that

\inf_{0\leq a<1}M(a,R)\leq M(1/3,R)=\frac{27}{32}\left(\frac{1-R^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)\frac{(1-\frac{R^{2}}{81})(1-\frac{5R}{3})}{1-\frac{R}{3}}\\
=\frac{1}{96}\left(\frac{1-R^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{(81-R^{2})(3-5R)}{(3-R)}\right)

Taking all these into account we have

\frac{2}{9}\left(\frac{1-R^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)\leq\Lambda(R)\leq\frac{1}{96}\left(\frac{(81-R^{2})(3-5R)}{(3-R)}\right)\left(\frac{1-R^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)\,.

Remark 3.3
.

If
g
is a bounded, positive function on
[0,1]
we may consider the function

\varphi(f,r)=g\big(|f(0)|\big)\varphi_{0}(f,r)=g\big(|a_{0}|\big)\frac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}

for
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathcal{B}
and
0<r<1/3
. Since
g
is positive, (
16
) yields

\varphi(f,r)\leq\varphi(\phi_{|a_{0}|},r)

for every
f
and
r
. Now, if
g
is such that, for each fixed
0\leq r\leq 1/3
the function given by

a\rightsquigarrow\frac{J(a,r)}{g(a)\varphi_{0}(\phi_{a},r)}=\frac{1-r^{2}}{r^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{g(a)}\cdot\frac{1-a^{4}r^{2}}{(1-a^{2})^{2}}\cdot\Big(1-a-r\frac{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\Big)

(19)

is decreasing in
[0,r]
, then we can apply Theorem
2.2
to have that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert r^{n}+\Lambda(R)\left(g(\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert)\frac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}\right)\leq 1\,,

for every
f\in\mathcal{B}
all
0\leq R\leq 1/3
and
0<r\leq R
. Here the sharp constant is given by

\Lambda(R)=\bigg(\frac{1-R^{2}}{R^{2}}\bigg)\inf_{0\leq a\leq 1}\bigg(\frac{1}{g(a)}\cdot\frac{1-a^{4}R^{2}}{(1-a)(1+a)^{2}}\cdot\frac{1-R(1+2a)}{1-aR}\bigg)\,.

(20)

Condition in (
19
) might not be easy to check. However, recall that we already saw in the proof of Theorem
3.1
that
a\rightsquigarrow\frac{J(a,r)}{\varphi_{0}(\phi_{a},r)}
is decreasing as a function of

The challenge now is to find functions satisfying (
19
) that improves Theorem
1.2
, in the sense that, keeping the factor
\frac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}
we can add a term which for each function is bigger than
\frac{16}{9}

Example 3.4
.

We take the function
g\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
given by

g(a)=\frac{(9+a^{4})}{(2+a^{2})(3-a)}\,,

which is clearly positive. We need to see that (
19
) is satisfied. To do that let us note that
(3-a)
is decreasing as a function of
a
, and so also is
\Big(1-a-r\frac{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\Big)\Big(\frac{2+a^{2}}{9-a^{2}}\Big)
. Taking this and (
18
) into account, we have that (
19
) is indeed satisfied. Then by (
20
) we have

\Lambda(1/3)=\frac{16}{9}\inf_{0<a<1}\frac{\left(a^{2}+2\right)\left(a^{4}-9\right)}{(a+1)^{2}\left(a^{2}-9\right)}=\frac{4}{3}\,,

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert r^{n}+\frac{4}{3}\cdot\frac{(9+|a_{0}|^{4})}{(2+|a_{0}|^{2})(3-|a_{0}|)}\cdot\frac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}\leq 1\,,

for every
f\in\mathcal{B}
and all
0\leq r\leq 1/3
. Note finally that
g(a)\geq g(1)=\frac{4}{3}
for every
0\leq a\leq 1
. Hence, the factor multiplying
\frac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}
is bigger than or equal to
\frac{16}{9}
for every function, improving in this way Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem
2.2
provides a way to find functions that produce an improved version of Bohr’s inequality. As we already noted, condition
2
in Theorem
2.2
is somewhat difficult to handle, so in Remark
2.3
we gave an easier condition. On the other hand, we look for functions that are as big as possible, so that the extra factor that we are adding in (
4
) is as big as possible. We give now a step in this direction. Consider the function
\Psi:[0,1]\times[0,1/3]\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\Psi(a,r)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1-a-r\dfrac{1-a^{2}}{1-ar}\,,&\text{ if }r\leq a\leq 1\\
\\
1-a-r\dfrac{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\,,&\text{ if }0\leq a\leq r\end{array}\right.

and define
\psi\colon\mathcal{B}\times[0,1/3]\to\mathbb{R}
by

\psi(f,r)\coloneqq\Psi(|f(0)|,r)\,.

This function obviously satisfies both conditions in Theorem
2.2
; hence its sharp constant is
1
and we have the following strengthening of (
10
).

Theorem 3.5
.

For every holomorphic function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
such that
|f(z)|\leq 1
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and
0\leq r\leq 1/3
we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert r^{n}+\Big(1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert-r\dfrac{1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert^{2}}{1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert r}\Big)\leq 1\,,\text{ if }r\leq|a_{0}|\leq 1

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert r^{n}+\Big(1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert-r\dfrac{\sqrt{1-\left\lvert a_{0}\right\rvert^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\Big)\leq 1\,,\text{ if }0\leq|a_{0}|<r\,.

Moreover, the inequalities are optimal, in the sense that there is no
\lambda_{r}>1
so that
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert r^{n}+\lambda_{r}\psi(f,r)\leq 1
for every
f\in\mathcal{B}
.

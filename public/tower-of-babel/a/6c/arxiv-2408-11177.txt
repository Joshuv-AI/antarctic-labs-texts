Nonlinear excitations in multi-dimensional nonlocal lattices
Brian Choi

Abstract
We study the formation of breathers in multi-dimensional lattices with long-range interactions. By variational methods, the exact relationship between various parameters (dimension, nonlinearity, nonlocal parameter α \alpha ) that defines positive excitation thresholds is characterized. We establish a sharp mass-threshold dichotomy: no positive threshold in the mass-subcritical regime, and a strictly positive threshold at and above the critical regime. In the anti-continuum regime, a family of unique ground states characterizes the excitation thresholds, enabling explicit computations. Analytic formulas of the excitation thresholds, determined by the ground states, are derived and corroborated with numerical simulations. We not only characterize the sharp spatial decay of ground states, which varies continuously in α \alpha , but also identify the time decay of dispersive waves, which undergoes a discontinuous transition in α \alpha .

1.1
Formulation

The behavior of breathers depends crucially on the nonlocal coupling

Definition 1.1

Let

for all

be an envelope such that (i) if

and

such that

for all

; (ii) if
\alpha=\infty

\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}k^{d+\beta}\eta(k)=0
for any

(I)

Stable-like in the far-field:
there exist

such that for any

with

J_{n}\leq c_{+}\eta(|n|),\hskip 20.00003pt\overline{J}_{k}:=\sum_{m\in S_{k}}J_{m}\geq c_{-}k^{d-1}\eta(k).

(II)

Nearest-neighbor nondegeneracy:

\min_{m\in S_{1}}J_{m}>0.

Define

Lu_{n}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus{\{n\}}}J_{n-m}(u_{n}-u_{m}),

from which our main model is given by

(1.3)

where the time-independent model is obtained by taking

for
\omega>0
; unless the anti-continuum limit is discussed, assume
\kappa=1
. The two conserved quantities

is bounded on

with an upper bound in operator norm

. Define the discrete Fourier transform

denote the Euclidean norm on the Fourier domain. Then, the symbol of

\sigma(\xi)=2\sum\limits_{m\neq 0}J_{m}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{m\cdot\xi}{2}\right)

Examples of

with

include the graph Laplacian, when

Q_{*}\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
is a ground state satisfying (
2.12
) with
\omega>0
.

Proof.

In general, dichotomy cannot happen for

or
Q^{(n)}
; our argument below for

generalizes to

. Consequently if (

) is not attained, then those sequences cannot be tight; otherwise, a strongly convergent subsequence can be extracted whose strong limit is a minimizer, which contradicts
Lemma

2.3
. For
q^{(n)}
, that the strong limit is a minimizer at
\nu_{0}
satisfying the Euler-Lagrange equation follows from the Lagrange multiplier method as
Proposition

2.1
. On the other hand,
Q^{(n)}
is a ground state at
\nu_{n}
, and the standard limit argument yields a ground state at
\nu_{0}
, if
\{Q^{(n)}\}
were tight. Therefore for any

and without relabeling the subsequence, we obtain

\sup_{m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{m\in B(m^{\prime},R)}|q^{(n)}_{m}|^{2}\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{}0,

(2.14)

and similarly for
Q^{(n)}
.

If the total mass splits into
\nu_{1}\in(0,\nu_{0})
and
\nu_{0}-\nu_{1}
, then we reason as (

) and decompose
q^{(n)}=a^{(n)}+b^{(n)}+o(1)
as

where

. Then,
H[q^{(n)}]=H[a^{(n)}]+H[b^{(n)}]+o(1)
. We claim that
H[a^{(n)}]+H[b^{(n)}]
is bounded away from the origin for all sufficiently large

, which forces a contradiction since
H[q^{(n)}]\rightarrow 0
.

For any
q\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, (

) implies

H[q]\geq\left(1-\left(\frac{M[q]}{\nu_{0}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\right)\frac{\langle Lq,q\rangle}{2}.

(2.15)

By symmetry, let
q=a^{(n)}
. Since

by hypothesis, the first factor of the RHS of (

) is bounded below from zero uniformly for all

. To bound the second factor, there exist a sequence of centers
\{m_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and some

such that

for all

\langle La^{(n)},a^{(n)}\rangle\gtrsim_{R}\sum\limits_{m\in B(m_{n},R)}|a^{(n)}_{m}|^{2}\geq\delta,

for a fixed

. The same argument shows that dichotomy is impossible for
\{Q^{(n)}\}
.

Suppose (
2.10
) is attained at
\tilde{Q}\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
where

by scaling. We claim vanishing cannot happen for

and hence tightness. Then, a usual subsequence extraction yields a strong limit

, modulo gauge and translation symmetries, that is a minimizer of the action at
\nu_{0}
.

Let

where the strict inequality may be assumed by taking a subsequence. Then,
M[r_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{Q}]=\nu_{n}
and

I_{\nu_{n}}\leq H[r_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{Q}]=-r_{n}(r_{n}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}-1)\frac{\langle L\tilde{Q},\tilde{Q}\rangle}{2}.

(2.16)

Arguing as (
2.15
), we have

I_{\nu_{n}}=H[Q^{(n)}]\geq-(r_{n}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}-1)\frac{\langle LQ^{(n)},Q^{(n)}\rangle}{2},

(2.17)

Combining (
2.16
), (
2.17
),

\langle LQ^{(n)},LQ^{(n)}\rangle\geq r_{n}\langle L\tilde{Q},\tilde{Q}\rangle>\langle L\tilde{Q},\tilde{Q}\rangle>0,

(2.18)

for all

by the discrete analogue of

2.18
).

∎

Remark 2.3
.

If (

. For

be the Shannon interpolation of

, and let

for all

. That

2.2
Decay of ground states

It was claimed in
[
7
, Section 3]
that when
d=1
and
J_{k}=k^{-(1+\alpha)}
, the tail behavior of the localized states is exponential if
\alpha>2
and algebraic if

. Soon after in
[
13
]
, an analysis on a similar model (Klein-Gordan type with cubic potential and LRI) was reported with numerical evidence that the decay near the origin undergoes an exponential-to-algebraic crossover for
|n|>n_{c}
for some transitional lattice site
n_{c}\in\mathbb{N}
. Here we provide a rigorous error analysis with the sharp order of decay. If
q\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
is a ground state satisfying (
2.12
), then there exists
\omega>0
such that

q=(\omega+L)^{-1}q^{p}=K\ast q^{p}

(2.19)

where the resolvent kernel is given by

K_{n}=[\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\omega+\sigma(\xi))^{-1}]_{n}=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{e^{in\cdot\xi}}{\omega+\sigma(\xi)}d\xi.

(2.20)

For
d=1,J_{k}=k^{-(1+\alpha)}
, (
2.20
) was approximated in
[
7
]
for
|n|\gg 1
by

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(n\xi)}{\omega+a\xi^{2}}d\xi=\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\frac{\omega}{a}}|n|}}{2\sqrt{a\omega}}

when
\alpha>2
, for some
a>0
, using the properties of the polylogarithm, which yields an exponential decay of
q
. Note that
\sigma(\xi)\simeq|\xi|^{2}
is valid for
|\xi|\ll 1
by
Proposition

3.1
, but not for
|\xi|\gtrsim 1
, and therefore an extension of the integral from
\mathbb{T}^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
should be dealt with care. The non-analytic branch term at
\xi=0
governs the asymptotic decay.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
q\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
satisfy (
2.12
) with
M[q]=\nu
. For any
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
, the decay estimate

|q_{n}|\leq C(d,\alpha,p,\omega)\nu^{\frac{p}{2}}\langle n\rangle^{-(d+\alpha)},\ \forall n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

(2.21)

holds, and for
\alpha=\infty
,
q_{n}=O_{\beta}(|n|^{-\beta})
for any
\beta>0
. Furthermore, if
\epsilon>0,\ \alpha\in(0,\infty)
, then

q_{n}\notin O(|n|^{-(d+\alpha+\epsilon)}).

(2.22)

Proof.

By an explicit computation of
\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\sin^{2}\left(\frac{m\cdot\xi}{2}\right)]_{n}
, we have

s_{n}:=[\mathcal{F}^{-1}\sigma]_{n}=\sum_{m\neq 0}J_{m}\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\sin^{2}\left(\frac{m\cdot\xi}{2}\right)]_{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{m\neq 0}J_{m}&,\text{ if }n=0\\
-\frac{1}{2}J_{n}&,\text{ if }n\neq 0.\end{cases}

(2.23)

Assume
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. To obtain a decay estimate of
q
from (
2.19
), an argument similar to the Picard iteration is implemented. Note that the decay of
K
corresponds to the regularity of
(\omega+\sigma(\xi))^{-1}
, and therefore of
\sigma(\xi)
, which in turn corresponds to the decay of
s_{n}
; the symbols
(\omega+\sigma(\xi))^{-1}
and
\sigma(\xi)
are in the same Hölder space since
\sigma\geq 0
and
\omega>0
. In short,
K=O(|n|^{-(d+\alpha)})
.

By
l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\hookrightarrow l^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
R>0
such that
|q_{n}|\leq\epsilon
whenever
|n|>R
. For
|n|>2R
, the convolution in (
2.19
) is split into
|m|\leq R
and
|m|>R
to obtain

\begin{split}q_{n}&\leq\|q\|_{l^{2}}^{p}\sum_{|m|\leq R}K_{n-m}+\epsilon^{p-1}\sum_{|m|>R}K_{n-m}q_{m}\\
&\lesssim\|q\|_{l^{2}}^{p}\sum_{|m|\leq R}\langle n-m\rangle^{-(d+\alpha)}+\epsilon^{p-1}\sum_{|m|>R}K_{n-m}q_{m}=:S_{1}+S_{2}.\end{split}

(2.24)

Since
|n-m|\geq\frac{|n|}{2}
, we have

S_{1}\leq C\langle n\rangle^{-(d+\alpha)}\simeq\|q\|_{l^{2}}^{p}R^{d}\langle n\rangle^{-(d+\alpha)}.

For
\delta>0
, let
C_{0}(\delta)>0
be the best constant that satisfies

q_{n}\leq C_{0}(\delta+\langle n\rangle^{-(d+\alpha)}),\ \forall n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

(2.25)

Indeed
C_{0}
exists since
\frac{q_{n}}{\delta+\langle n\rangle^{-(d+\alpha)}}\leq\frac{\nu^{1/2}}{\delta}
. Define
C_{1}>0
by

\sum\limits_{m}K_{n-m}\langle m\rangle^{-(d+\alpha)}\leq C_{1}\langle n\rangle^{-(d+\alpha)},

which can be shown by comparing the series with the corresponding integral. By (
2.24
), (
2.25
),

\begin{split}q_{n}&\leq C\langle n\rangle^{-(d+\alpha)}+C_{0}\epsilon^{p-1}\sum_{|m|>R}K_{n-m}(\delta+\langle m\rangle^{-(d+\alpha)})\\
&\leq(\epsilon^{p-1}\sum_{m}|K_{m}|)C_{0}\delta+(C+C_{0}(\epsilon^{p-1}C_{1}))\langle n\rangle^{-(d+\alpha)}\\
&\leq(C+\frac{C_{0}}{2})(\delta+\langle n\rangle^{-(d+\alpha)}),\end{split}

where
0<\epsilon\ll 1
was chosen at the last inequality, implying
C_{0}\leq C+\frac{C_{0}}{2}
, and therefore
C_{0}\leq 2C
. By (
2.25
) and
C>0
, the estimate (
2.21
) follows by taking
\delta\rightarrow 0
in (
2.25
). If
\alpha=\infty
, a similar analysis as above shows

decay for any
\beta>0
.

Suppose there exists
\epsilon>0
satisfying
d+\alpha+\epsilon\notin\mathbb{Z}
and
\epsilon<(p-1)(d+\alpha)
such that
q_{n}=O(|n|^{-(d+\alpha+\epsilon)})
. By (
2.19
), this implies
\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\sigma\widehat{q}]_{n}=(Lq)_{n}=(s\ast q)_{n}=O(|n|^{-(d+\alpha+\epsilon)})
. Let
R\gg 1
such that
R^{-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\sum\limits_{m}|s_{m}|\ll 1
and
|q_{m_{0}}|>\frac{\|q\|_{l^{\infty}}}{2}
for some
|m_{0}|<R

2R\leq|n|\leq 2^{-\frac{1}{d+\alpha}}R^{\frac{d+\alpha+\epsilon/2}{d+\alpha}}
such that

where the first inequality holds by the averaged lower bound in
Definition

1.1
and
|n-m_{0}|\geq R\gg 1
, and the second inequality holds by the limit property of

and
|n-m_{0}|\leq\frac{3|n|}{2}
. Then,

\begin{split}|n|^{-(d+\alpha+\epsilon)}\gtrsim|(s\ast q)_{n}|&\geq\left|\sum_{|m|\leq R}s_{n-m}q_{m}\right|-\left|\sum_{|m|>R}s_{n-m}q_{m}\right|\\
&\gtrsim\sum_{|m|\leq R}J_{n-m}q_{m}-\sum_{|m|>R}\frac{|s_{n-m}|}{|m|^{d+\alpha+\epsilon}}\\

(2.26)

where an explicit computation (
2.23
) and

were used in the third inequality.
∎

3
Excitation thresholds for LRI

3.1
Radial algebraic decay

The existence of excitation thresholds hinges upon the following lemma. Let
\langle z\rangle=(|z|^{2}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. We introduce an effective interaction exponent
\alpha^{\prime}
to describe the asymptotic behavior of the lattice Laplacian.

Proposition 3.1
.

Define

\alpha^{\prime}=\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha}{2}&,\text{ if }\alpha\in(0,2)\\
1&,\text{ if }\alpha\in[2,\infty].\end{cases}

(3.1)

Then,

\sigma(\xi)\simeq_{d,\alpha}\delta(|\xi|):=\begin{cases}|\xi|^{2\alpha^{\prime}}&,\text{ if }\alpha\in(0,\infty]\setminus\{2\}\\
|\xi|^{2}\langle\log|\xi|\rangle&,\text{ if }\alpha=2.\end{cases}

(3.2)

For the following counting lemma, let
\#
denote the cardinality of lattice points of interest.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a\in(0,d^{-\frac{1}{2}})
. There exists
k_{1}(a,d)\in\mathbb{N}
and
C(d)>0
such that

\sup_{\tilde{\omega}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\#\left\{m\in S_{k}:\frac{|m\cdot\tilde{\omega}|}{k}\leq a\right\}\leq Cak^{d-1},\ \forall k\geq k_{1},

Proof.

Since the statement is trivial for
d=1
, let
d\geq 2
. Fix
\tilde{\omega}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and let
E=\{m\in S_{k}:\frac{|m\cdot\tilde{\omega}|}{k}\leq a\}
. Consider
\tilde{Q}=\bigcup\limits_{m\in E}(m+[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d})
. Define
T:\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
given by
x\mapsto\frac{|x|_{1}}{|x|_{2}}x
. Then,
T
maps
S_{k}
into
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|_{2}=k\}
. In the polar coordinate
(r,\omega^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, we have
T(x)=|\omega^{\prime}|_{1}r\omega^{\prime}=|\omega^{\prime}|_{1}x
since
|\omega^{\prime}|_{2}=1
. Then,
J_{T}(x)=|\omega^{\prime}|_{1}^{d}\geq|\omega^{\prime}|_{2}^{d}=1
, and

\#E=\text{Vol}(\tilde{Q})\leq\int_{\tilde{Q}}J_{T}(x)dx=\text{Vol}(T(\tilde{Q})).

Let
x\in\tilde{Q}
. Then, there exists
m\in E
such that
|x-m|_{2}\leq\frac{\sqrt{d}}{2}
. Since
||x|_{1}-|m|_{1}|\leq\sqrt{d}|x-m|_{2}
and
|T(x)|_{2}=|x|_{1}
, we have a radial bound:
|T(x)|_{2}\in[k-\frac{d}{2},k+\frac{d}{2}]
. On the other hand, let
\omega^{\prime}_{x}=\frac{x}{|x|_{2}},\ \omega^{\prime}_{m}=\frac{m}{|m|_{2}}
, and
\angle(\omega^{\prime}_{x},\omega^{\prime}_{m})\in[0,\pi]
be the angle between two vectors. Since

|m|_{2}\geq\frac{|m|_{1}}{\sqrt{d}}=\frac{k}{\sqrt{d}},\ |x|_{2}\geq|m|_{2}-|x-m|_{2}\geq\frac{k}{\sqrt{d}}-\frac{\sqrt{d}}{2},

we have

\sin\angle(\omega^{\prime}_{x},\omega^{\prime}_{m})\leq\frac{|x-m|_{2}}{\min(|x|_{2},|m|_{2})}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{k},

for all
k\geq d
, and hence an angular bound:
\angle(\omega^{\prime}_{x},\omega^{\prime}_{m})\lesssim_{d}\frac{1}{k}
for all
k
sufficiently large.

Since
m\in E
, we have
|\cos\angle(\omega^{\prime}_{m},\tilde{\omega})|\leq a\sqrt{d}<1
. Estimating
\cos^{-1}(a\sqrt{d})
, by the Taylor expansion for example, an angular bound
\angle(\omega^{\prime}_{m},\tilde{\omega})\in[\frac{\pi}{2}-2a\sqrt{d},\frac{\pi}{2}+2a\sqrt{d}]
follows. Then,

T(\tilde{Q})\subseteq\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|_{2}\in[k-\frac{d}{2},k+\frac{d}{2}]\text{ and }\angle(\omega^{\prime}_{x},\tilde{\omega})\in[\frac{\pi}{2}-(2a\sqrt{d}+\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{k}),\frac{\pi}{2}+(2a\sqrt{d}+\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{k})]\right\},

and the volume of RHS (sector) is bounded above by
C_{1}ak^{d-1}+C_{2}k^{d-2}
for some
C_{1},C_{2}>0
. If
k\geq k_{1}(a,d)
, then the lower order term
k^{d-2}
is bounded above
ak^{d-1}
independent of
\tilde{\omega}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

3.1
.

Assume
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
; the case
\alpha=\infty
follows similarly. Fix
R\gg 1
in
Definition

1.1
and let
|\xi|<R^{-1}
. If
|\xi|\geq R^{-1}
, then
\sigma(\xi)\simeq 1
where the lower bound is by the nearest-neighbor degeneracy and the upper bound is by the compactness of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, and hence
\sigma(\xi)\simeq\delta(|\xi|)
away from the origin.

To show
\sigma(\xi)\lesssim\delta(|\xi|)
on
|\xi|<R^{-1}
, note that

\begin{split}\sigma(\xi)&=\sum_{|m|\leq R}J_{m}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{m\cdot\xi}{2}\right)+\sum_{R<|m|\leq|\xi|^{-1}}J_{m}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{m\cdot\xi}{2}\right)+\sum_{|m|>|\xi|^{-1}}J_{m}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{m\cdot\xi}{2}\right)\\
&=:\mathscr{I}_{1}+\mathscr{I}_{2}+\mathscr{I}_{3}.\end{split}

By direct estimation using the upper bound
J_{m}\lesssim\eta(|m|)
,

\mathscr{I}_{1}+\mathscr{I}_{2}+\mathscr{I}_{3}\lesssim|\xi|^{2}\sum_{|m|\leq R}J_{m}|m|^{2}+|\xi|^{2}\sum_{R<k\leq|\xi|^{-1}}k^{1-\alpha}+\sum_{k>|\xi|^{-1}}k^{-(1+\alpha)}\lesssim\delta(|\xi|).

To show the lower bound, we claim

\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{k}}[(m\cdot\xi)^{2}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{k}}\left[\left(m\cdot\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\right)^{2}\right]|\xi|^{2}\gtrsim k^{2}|\xi|^{2}.

(3.3)

Let
\tilde{\omega}=\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}
and
0<\delta\ll d^{-1}
. Define
S_{\delta}=\{m\in S_{k}:\left(\frac{m\cdot\tilde{\omega}}{k}\right)^{2}>\delta\}
. By
Lemma

3.1
,

\mu_{k}(S_{\delta}^{c})\leq(\#S_{\delta}^{c})\sup_{m\in S_{k}}\mu_{k}\{m\}\lesssim\sqrt{\delta},

where the last inequality follows from
\mu_{k}\{m\}\lesssim\frac{J_{m}}{k^{d-1}\eta(k)}\lesssim k^{-(d-1)}
. Then,

\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{k}}\left[\left(m\cdot\tilde{\omega}\right)^{2}\right]\geq k^{2}\delta\mu_{k}(S_{\delta})\geq C_{1}k^{2}\delta(1-C_{2}\sqrt{\delta}),

for some dimensional constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
, which justifies the claim. Then for
R\leq k<|\xi|^{-1}
,

\sigma(\xi)\geq\sum_{R<|m|\leq|\xi|^{-1}}J_{m}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{m\cdot\xi}{2}\right)=\sum_{R<k\leq|\xi|^{-1}}\overline{J}_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{k}}\left[\sin^{2}\left(\frac{m\cdot\xi}{2}\right)\right]\gtrsim|\xi|^{2}\sum_{R<k\leq|\xi|^{-1}}\overline{J}_{k}k^{2},

where the last inequality is by (
3.3
). By definition,
\overline{J}_{k}\gtrsim k^{d-1}\eta(k)
, and therefore,

|\xi|^{2}\sum_{R<k\leq|\xi|^{-1}}\overline{J}_{k}k^{2}\gtrsim|\xi|^{2}\sum_{R<k\leq|\xi|^{-1}}k^{1-\alpha}\gtrsim\delta(|\xi|).

∎

We now clarify how the excitation threshold depends on the nonlinearity. The next two propositions show a dichotomy: in the mass-subcritical regime there is no positive threshold, while at and above the mass-critical regime a strictly positive threshold appears. Weak nonlinear effects let energy disperse, whereas stronger interactions counteract dispersion and sustain localized states. On the other hand, when
\alpha=0
, we give a partial result in
d=1
where a logarithmic refinement yields positive thresholds for any power nonlinearity, thereby extending
Proposition

3.3
.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
p<1+\frac{4\alpha^{\prime}}{d}
, then
K=0
. Consequently
\nu_{0}=0
, and
I_{\nu}
has a minimizer for any
\nu>0
.

The proposition is shown by an explicit construction of test functions similar to
[
15

. An extension of this idea to LRI requires more technicality.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u_{n}=e^{-\rho|n|^{\gamma}}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z},\ \rho>0,\ \gamma\in(0,2)
. Then as
\rho\rightarrow 0
,
\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|u_{n}|^{q}\simeq(q\rho)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}
holds for any
q\in(0,\infty)
. For
\rho\ll 1
,

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\xi|^{\beta}|\widehat{u}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\simeq\begin{cases}\rho^{\frac{\beta-1}{\gamma}}&,\text{ if }\beta\in(-1,1+2\gamma)\\
\rho^{2}|\log\rho|&,\text{ if }\beta=1+2\gamma\\
\rho^{2}&,\text{ if }\beta>1+2\gamma.\end{cases}

(3.4)

Proof.

Observe that
u_{n}
is the fundamental solution of the fractional heat equation

\partial_{\rho}u=-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}u,\ (\xi,\rho)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}

(3.5)

on the Fourier side where
\rho
is the time variable. Let
p_{\rho}(x)
be the fundamental solution of (
3.5
) posed on
\mathbb{R}
.
From the semigroup theory of
\exp(-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}})
, we have (see
[
32
, Section 2.6]
, for example)

p_{\rho}(x)\simeq\min(\rho^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}},\frac{\rho}{|x|^{1+\gamma}}),\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.6)

On
\mathbb{T}
, the fundamental solution
\tilde{p}_{\rho}
is periodized from
p_{\rho}
, i.e,

\tilde{p}_{\rho}(\xi)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}p_{\rho}(\xi+2m\pi)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\rho|n|^{\gamma}}\cos n\xi,

where the last equality is by the Poisson summation formula. By the relationship above,
\tilde{p}_{\rho}
obeys (
3.6
) for
|\xi|\leq\pi
, and therefore

To show linear dispersive estimates, the basics of the polylogarithm functions are revised. The linear flow of (

u(t)=U(t)\phi=K_{t}\ast\phi;\hskip 20.00003pt(K_{t})_{n}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{i(n\xi-\sigma(\xi)t)}d\xi.

(4.1)

By scaling, let

\sigma(\xi)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^{2}\left(\frac{k\xi}{2}\right)}{k^{1+\alpha}}=\frac{1}{2}\zeta(1+\alpha)-\frac{1}{4}(\mathrm{Li}_{1+\alpha}(e^{i\xi})+\mathrm{Li}_{1+\alpha}(e^{-i\xi})),

where the polylogarithm

is a holomorphic function in
s,z\in\mathbb{C}
; see
[

]
and references therein for more details regarding this special function. The radius of convergence (in
z
) is 1, inside which

\mathrm{Li}_{s}(z):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k^{s}}.

Outside the radius of convergence, there exists an analytic continuation in
\mathbb{C}\setminus[1,\infty)
where for
\Re s>0
, the polylogarithm can be identified with the Bose-Einstein integral

\mathrm{Li}_{s}(z)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{s-1}}{e^{t}/z-1}dt.

(4.2)

An important property of polylogarithms pertinent to our interest is

\frac{d}{d\xi}\mathrm{Li}_{s}(e^{\pm i\xi})=\pm i\mathrm{Li}_{s-1}(e^{\pm i\xi}),\ s\in\mathbb{C},\ \xi\notin 2\pi\mathbb{Z}.

(4.3)

Although
\sigma\in C(\mathbb{T})
by the summability of

\sigma
are locally identified with those of the polylogarithms, by uniqueness of analytic continuation, which furnishes the following dispersive estimates.

Proposition 4.1

(Linear dispersive decay)

For any

where

if

and
\rho=\rho_{-}:=\frac{1}{2}
if

.

Our proof, based on the Van der Corput Lemma, which was also used in

Assuming small data, the linear dispersive estimates extend to the nonlinear regime under certain technical hypotheses. The conditions below are most likely not sharp.

Proposition 4.2

(Nonlinear dispersive decay)
.

Suppose that

p>\begin{cases}3&,\ \alpha\in(0,1]\\
\max(4,1+4\alpha^{\prime})&,\ \alpha\in(1,\infty)\end{cases};\hskip 20.00003ptq\in\begin{cases}(\frac{2(p+1)}{p-1},p+1]&,\ \alpha\in(0,1]\\
(\frac{2(p+1)}{p-2},p+1]&,\ \alpha\in(1,\infty).\end{cases}

Then,

\|u(t)\|_{l^{q}}\leq C\langle t\rangle^{-(\rho_{\pm})\frac{(q-2)}{q}}\|\phi\|_{l^{q^{\prime}}},

(4.6)

whenever
\|\phi\|_{l^{q^{\prime}}}\ll_{\alpha,p,q}1
where
C(\alpha,p,q)>0
depends on the constants of the Strichartz estimates (
4.5
).

Proof.

The proof is in the spirit of
[
35
, Theorem 7]
where an a priori bound is obtained to run a fixed point argument. We only give a sketch of proof for
\alpha\in(0,1]
to illustrate the source of our technical hypotheses.

Let
X=\{(u_{n}(t)):\|u(t)\|_{l^{q}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-(\rho_{-})\frac{(q-2)}{q}}\|\phi\|_{l^{q^{\prime}}}\}
and

\Gamma u:=U(t)\phi+i\int_{0}^{t}U(t-t^{\prime})\left(|u(t^{\prime})|^{p-1}u(t^{\prime})\right)dt^{\prime}.

(4.7)

To show the existence of a fixed point in
X
, and therefore
u=\Gamma u
, observe that the interpolation of
U(t)
for
l^{1}\rightarrow l^{\infty}
(
4.4
) and
l^{2}\rightarrow l^{2}
(unitary action) yields (
4.6
) with
u(t)
replaced by
U(t)\phi
for all
q\in[2,\infty]
. The condition
q\leq p+1
ensures
\|u(t^{\prime})\|_{l^{pq^{\prime}}}\leq\|u(t^{\prime})\|_{l^{q}}
, and the lower bound on
q
yields the estimate
\int_{0}^{t}\langle t-t^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{q-2}{2q}}\langle t^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{p(q-2)}{2q}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{q-2}{2q}}
. Taking
\|\phi\|_{l^{q^{\prime}}}\ll_{\alpha,p,q}1
, an a priori estimate

\begin{split}\|\Gamma u\|_{l^{q}}&\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-(\rho_{-})\frac{(q-2)}{q}}\|\phi\|_{l^{q^{\prime}}}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle t-t^{\prime}\rangle^{-(\rho_{-})\frac{(q-2)}{q}}\|u(t^{\prime})\|_{l^{pq^{\prime}}}^{p}dt^{\prime}\\
&\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-(\rho_{-})\frac{(q-2)}{q}}(\|\phi\|_{l^{q^{\prime}}}+\|\phi\|_{l^{q^{\prime}}}^{p})\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-(\rho_{-})\frac{(q-2)}{q}}\|\phi\|_{l^{q^{\prime}}},\end{split}

holds and the standard contraction argument yields the desired nonlinear decay.
∎

A stronger assumption on the power of nonlinearity yields a well-defined wave operator and global scattering for small data.

Proposition 4.3

(Scattering)
.

Suppose
p\geq 5
if
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
p\geq 7
if
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
. There exists
\delta>0
such that whenever
\|\phi\|_{l^{2}}<\delta
, there exists a unique asymptotic state
u_{\pm}\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with

\|u_{\pm}\|_{l^{2}}=\|\phi\|_{l^{2}}
such that

u(t)-U(t)u_{\pm}\xrightarrow[t\rightarrow\pm\infty]{}0\text{ in }l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

(4.8)

Furthermore the map
\phi\mapsto u_{\pm}
defines homeomorphism on the
\delta
-neighborhood of the origin.

Proof.

The proof is standard in the literature, having developed the linear dispersive estimates. However we illustrate the main ideas for
\alpha\in(0,1]
for the sake of completeness. Assume
t\in[0,\infty)
without loss of generality.

A fixed point argument using
\Gamma:X\rightarrow X
where
\Gamma
is by (
4.7
) and
X=C(\mathbb{R};l^{2})\cap L_{t}^{\frac{4p}{3}}l^{\frac{2p}{p-3}}
yields a global solution
u\in X
with
\|\phi\|_{l^{2}}\ll 1
where the implicit constant depends on
\alpha,p
and the Strichartz constants. Since

\left|\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}U(-t^{\prime})\left(|u(t^{\prime})|^{p-1}u(t^{\prime})\right)dt^{\prime}\right|\right|_{l^{2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]}^{\frac{4p}{3}}l^{p}}^{p}\leq\|u\|_{L_{t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]}^{\frac{4p}{3}}l^{\frac{2p}{p-3}}}^{p}\xrightarrow[t_{1},t_{2}\rightarrow\infty]{}0,

there exists
u_{+}=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}U(-t)u(t)\in l^{2}
such that (
4.8
) holds by reasoning as above. Injectivity follows from the uniqueness of limit.

To show surjectivity, let
u_{+}\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and define

\tilde{\Gamma}u(t):=U(t)u_{+}-i\int_{t}^{\infty}U(t-t^{\prime})\left(|u(t^{\prime})|^{p-1}u(t^{\prime})\right)dt^{\prime},

and let
T\gg 1
such that
\|U(t)u_{+}\|_{L_{t\in[T,\infty)}^{\frac{4p}{3}}l^{\frac{2p}{p-3}}}\ll 1
. Another fixed point argument using
\tilde{\Gamma}
yields
u\in C([T,\infty);l^{2})\cap L_{t}^{\frac{4p}{3}}l^{\frac{2p}{p-3}}
such that its asymptotic state is
u_{+}
. Then flow
u(T)
backwards in time to obtain
\phi=u(0)
. Continuity follows from the contractivity of
\Gamma,\tilde{\Gamma}
and the Strichartz estimates.
∎

5
Conclusion

We analyzed excitation thresholds in multi-dimensional DNLS lattices with nonlocal interactions, deriving explicit conditions on how dimension
d
, nonlinearity
p
, and the nonlocal exponent
\alpha
determine breather formation. Our results reveal that long-range interactions fundamentally alter excitation thresholds and dispersive decay, with a sharp transition at
\alpha=1
. For
\alpha=0
, the standard framework breaks down due to the lack of summability, requiring a novel logarithmic correction. This result highlights the robustness of excitation thresholds even in the extreme long-range regime. These findings have implications for nonlinear wave systems such as optical waveguides, Bose-Einstein condensates, and photonic lattices, where tuning
\alpha
could enable precise control over localization and energy transport. Future work includes analyzing the spectral and dynamical stability of breather solutions, extending results to anisotropic and disordered lattices, and refining numerical methods for higher-dimensional settings.

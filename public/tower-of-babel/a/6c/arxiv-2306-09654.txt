Orlicz functions that do not satisfy the Δ 2 \Delta_{2} -condition and high order Gateaux smoothness in h M ​ ( Γ ) h_{M}(\Gamma)
Milen Ivanov, Stanimir Troyanski, Nadia Zlateva

Abstract
We study Orlicz functions that do not satisfy the Δ 2 \Delta_{2} -condition at zero. We prove that for every Orlicz function M M such that lim sup t → 0 M ⁡ ( t ) / t p > 0 \limsup_{t\to 0}M(t)/t^{p}>0 for some p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 , there exists a positive sequence T = ( t k ) k = 1 ∞ T=(t_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty} tending to zero and such that sup k ∈ ℕ M ⁡ ( c ​ t k ) M ⁡ ( t k ) ​ < ∞ , for all ​ c > ​ 1 , \sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{M(ct_{k})}{M(t_{k})}<\infty,\text{ for all }c>1, that is, M M satisfies the Δ 2 \Delta_{2} condition with respect to T T . Consequently, we show that for each Orlicz function with lower Boyd index α M < ∞ \alpha_{M}<\infty there exists an Orlicz function N N such that: (a) there exists a positive sequence T = ( t k ) k = 1 ∞ T=(t_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty} tending to zero such that N N satisfies the Δ 2 \Delta_{2} condition with respect to T T , and (b) the space h N h_{N} is isomorphic to a subspace of h M h_{M} generated by one vector. We apply this result to find the maximal possible order of Gâteaux differentiability of a continuous bump function on the Orlicz space h M ​ ( Γ ) h_{M}(\Gamma) for Γ \Gamma uncountable.

Introduction

Since the seminal work of Bonic and Frampton
[
1
]
the exact smoothness of particular classes of Banach spaces has been studied, see e.g.
[
4
, Chapter 5]
,
[
3
, Chapter III.27]
.
The exact order of Gâteaux smoothness of Orlicz spaces
\ell_{M}(\Gamma)
over an uncountable index set
\Gamma
when
M
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition at zero, was completely characterised by Maleev, Nedev and Zlatanov in
[
10
]
in terms of the lower Boyd index, e.g.
[
9
]
,

\alpha_{M}:=\sup\left\{p:\ \sup\left\{\frac{M(uv)}{u^{p}M(v)}:\ u,v\in(0,1]\right\}<\infty\right\}.

(1.1)

From the convexity of the Orlicz function
M
it follows that
1\leq\alpha_{M}\leq\infty
.

Here we show that the characterisation from
[
10
]
is still valid in
h_{M}(\Gamma)
even without the
\Delta_{2}
-condition.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be an Orlicz function, let
\Gamma
be an uncountable set and let
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that there exists continuous
p
-times Gâteaux differentiable bump function on
h_{M}(\Gamma)
. Then at least one of the following conditions holds:

\mathrm{(a)}

p<\alpha_{M}
;

\mathrm{(b)}

p=\alpha_{M}
and
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}M(t)/t^{p}=0
;

\mathrm{(c)}

p
is even and
M
is equivalent to
t^{p}
at zero. (Obviously in this case also
p=\alpha_{M}
.)

Theorem
1.1
is sharp. In case (a) there even exists equivalent
p
-times Ftéchet differentiable norm on
h_{M}(\Gamma)
, see
[
11
, Corollary 12]
. Case (b) is a modification of
[
11
, Lemma 2 and Lemma 4]
, see also
[
10
, Remark 1]
.
Of course, in the (c) case
h_{M}(\Gamma)
is just
\ell_{p}(\Gamma)
which for an even
p
is infinitely smooth.

The proof of the exact order of Gâteaux smoothness of Orlicz spaces
\ell_{M}(\Gamma)
over an uncountable index set
\Gamma
when
M
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition at zero due to
[
10
, Corollary 1]
, relies on Stegall Variational Principle
[
14
,
15
]
. The Stegall Variational Principle applies when the domain of the minimized function is a dentable set. When
M
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition at zero the authors of
[
10
]
use that the unit vectors form a boundedly complete basis of
\ell_{M}
and, therefore,
\ell_{M}
has the Radon-Nikodým property. If, however,
M
fails the
\Delta_{2}
-condition at zero then
h_{M}
contains a subspace isomorphic to
c_{0}
and, therefore,
h_{M}
fails also the Radon-Nikodým property and Stegall Variational Principle cannot be applied directly.

We will briefly explain the new technique we use to circumvent the lack of
\Delta_{2}
-condition in Theorem
1.1
.

Elaborating on an idea of Leung
[
8
]
we show that there is a positive sequence
(t_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
tending to zero such that
M
satisfies a kind of
\Delta_{2}
-condition relative to this sequence. More precisely:

Definition 1.2
.

Let
M
be a non-degenerate Orlicz function and let
T=(t_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a positive sequence tending to
0
. We say that
M
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition
with respect to

T
if

\sup_{t\in T}\frac{M(ct)}{M(t)}<\infty

(1.2)

for all
c>1
.

The merit of the concept defined above is that if
M
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition with respect to a sequence
T
then the unit vector basis
(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
in
h_{M}(\Gamma)
is boundedly complete with respect to this
T
. More precisely, let
T=(t_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a positive sequence tending to
0
and let

\left(\{\pm T\}\cup\{0\}\right)^{\Gamma}
be the set of all functions
x=x(\gamma)
defined on
\Gamma
with values in
\{\pm T\}\cup\{0\}
. In other words,

x\in\left(\{\pm T\}\cup\{0\}\right)^{\Gamma}\iff|x(\gamma)|\in T\cup\{0\},\ \forall\gamma\in\Gamma.

(1.3)

Fact 1.3
.

Let
T=(t_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a positive sequence tending to
0
and let
M
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition with respect to
T
. Let
(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be the unit vector basis of
h_{M}(\Gamma)
. Let
x\in\left(\{\pm T\}\cup\{0\}\right)^{\Gamma}
.
Assume that for any finite subset
A
of
\Gamma

\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in A}x(\gamma)e_{\gamma}\right\|\leq 1.

(1.4)

Then
x\in h_{M}(\Gamma)
.

Indeed, (
1.4
) is equivalent to
\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x)\leq 1
.
Let

, where

. From (

) it follows that
s_{i}<\infty
. So,

and, therefore,

\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(ix)\leq s_{i}\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x)\leq s_{i}<\infty,

for all

. This means that

.

Our new insight is recorded in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let the Orlicz function
M
be such that

\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{M(t)}{t^{p}}>0

for some

.
Then there are constants

and a positive sequence

tending to

satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition with respect to

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{M(t_{k})}{t_{k}^{p}}\geq a\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{M(t)}{t^{p}}.

In Section

then there is a subspace of

generated by one vector and isometric to

, where

is an Orlicz function such that

. Thus, (

) holds for

. So,

satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition with respect to a suitable

. Leung
[
8
]
has provided an example of an Orlicz function

with
\alpha_{M}=\infty
for which there is a sequence

such that

satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition with respect to
T
. Of course, if

as

, such

does not exist.

The problem of finding spaces
h_{N}
isomorphic to subspaces of a given space
h_{M}
generated by
one vector, and the problem of finding spaces
h_{N}

just isomorphic to subspaces of
h_{M}
, are quite different.
For a given Orlicz function
M
, those Orlicz functions

for which
h_{N}
is a

are characterized in
[

, Theorem 4.a.8]
. This condition is

which are isomorphic to a subspace of

[

, Theorem 4.a.9]
. The

[

, Proposition 5]
of those Orlicz functions

for which

is isomorphic to a subspace of

generated by one vector, is harder to

[

and
[

such that

has no subspaces generated by one vector isomorphic to

for any

. For instance, if

for some suitable

and extended in a convex fashion for

, then

has no subspaces generated by one vector isomorphic to

.

The way the sequence

provided by Theorem

can be used is as follows. If
\alpha_{M}>1
then the unit vector basis

of

is shrinking, i.e. the conjugate system

generates the dual

. Thus the intersection of the unit ball of

with the set

, see (

), is weekly compact, cf.

, Section 3, Examples]
. Therefore, the latter intersection is dentable and Stegall Variational Principle can be used on it as in
[

,
10
]
. However, we prefer to use direct induction of the type originating from
[

]
and elaborated in
[
11
]
. Note that another way for completing this step is the Ekeland Variational Principle with respect to an appropriate metric as in
[

]
and restricted to

, because without

that metric is not complete on the whole of

. The special perturbation method from
[

]
should also work if restricted in the same way.

It is worth mentioning here that the exact order of Gâteaux smoothness of

for

is not known and it is an interesting and apparently tough open problem. Therefore, it is not possible at present to derive Theorem

from the countable case.

The paper is organised as follows. After Section

a necessary condition for smoothness which, when applied to a suitable subspace, provided by Section

, yields Theorem

.

2
Preliminaries

Here we recall some definitions and known facts that we use.

On a Banach space
X
the function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is
p
-times Gâteaux differentiable,
p\in\mathbb{N}
, if

f(x+ty)=f(x)+P(ty)+o_{y}(t^{p}),

where
P
is a bounded polinomial of degree at most
p
, such that
P(0)=0
, and
o_{y}(t^{p})
means any function
\omega:X\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{t\to 0}{\omega(y,t)\over t^{p}}=0,\quad\forall y\in X,

for details see
[
4
, Section 1.4]
.

The function
b:X\to\mathbb{R}
is called
bump
if its support set

\mathrm{supp}\,b:=\{x:\ b(x)\neq 0\}

is bounded. Here we assume non-trivial bumps, that is, such with non-empty support.

A non-degenerate Orlicz function
M
, that is, an even convex function on
\mathbb{R}
such that
M(0)=0
and
M(t)>0
for
t\neq 0
, is always assumed throughout the paper. Recall, e.g.
[
9
]
, that
M
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition at zero if

\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{M(2t)}{M(t)}<\infty,

which is equivalent to

\sup_{t\in(0,1]}\frac{M(ct)}{M(t)}<\infty,\text{ for all }c>1.

The Orlicz space
\ell_{M}(\Gamma)
is the set of all bounded real-valued functions on a set
\Gamma
such that
\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x/\lambda)<\infty
for some
\lambda>0
, where

\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x):=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}M(x(\gamma)).

The summation is in the sense of the supremum over the sums on all finite subsets of
\Gamma
.

We denote for
x\in\ell_{M}(\Gamma)

\mathrm{supp}\,x:=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\ x(\gamma)\neq 0\}.

It is clear that
\mathrm{supp}\,x
is countable for each
x\in\ell_{M}(\Gamma)
.

The Luxembourg norm of the Orlicz space
\ell_{M}(\Gamma)
is given by the Minkowski functional of the set
\{x\in\ell_{M}(\Gamma):\ \sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x)\leq 1\}
, that is,

\|x\|:=\inf\{\lambda>0:\ \sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x/\lambda)\leq 1\}.

Then
\ell_{M}(\Gamma)
is a Banach space. We are interested in its closed subspace

h_{M}(\Gamma):=\{x\in\ell_{M}(\Gamma):\ \sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x/\lambda)<\infty,\ \forall\lambda>0\}.

It is easy to see that
h_{M}(\Gamma)
is the subspace of
\ell_{M}(\Gamma)
generated by the unit vector basis
(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
. Clearly,
(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is a symmetric basis in
h_{M}(\Gamma)
. With
(e_{\gamma}^{*})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
we denote the conjugate system to the basis
(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
, that is,
e_{\gamma}^{*}(x)=x(\gamma)
.

It follows directly from the definition of the canonical norm in
\ell_{M}
that for a sequence
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\ell_{M}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}\|=0\iff\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(ix_{n})=0,\quad\forall i\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.1)

Indeed,
\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(ix)\leq 1\iff\|ix\|\leq 1\iff\|x\|\leq 1/i
. On the other hand,
\|x\|\leq\varepsilon/i\iff\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(ix/\varepsilon)\leq 1
. From convexity
\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(\varepsilon y)\leq\varepsilon\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(y)
for
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, so
\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(ix)\leq\varepsilon\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(ix/\varepsilon)\leq\varepsilon
.

Note that if
M
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition at zero, then
x_{n}\to 0\iff\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x_{n})\to 0
. This is the main technical obstacle we here overcome.

An important part of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is a reduction to a subspace. In our case due to lack of
\Delta_{2}
-condition this reduction is a bit more technical than in
[
10
]
. We do it Section
4
, but here we will introduce the needed concepts. The following definition is well-known, see e.g.
[
9
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with symmetric basis
(u_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
. Let
A
be an index set and let for each
\alpha\in A
be given a sequence
(\alpha_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of elements of
\Gamma
. Let also these sequences be disjoint, i.e.
\{\alpha_{n}:\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}\cap\{\beta_{n}:\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}=\emptyset
if
\alpha\neq\beta
.

Then for any
z\in X\setminus\{0\}
represented as

z=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}u_{\gamma_{n}},

where
\gamma_{n}\neq\gamma_{m}
for
n\neq m
, the basic set
(v_{\alpha}^{(z)})_{\alpha\in A}
defined by

v_{\alpha}^{(z)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}u_{\alpha_{n}}

is called a
block basis generated by the vector

z
and the subspace spanned by this block basis is called
subspace generated by

z
.

The work
[
7
]
characterised the Orlicz subspaces of
h_{M}(\Gamma)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
7
, Proposition 5]

Let
M
and

be Orlicz functions and let
\Gamma
be an uncountable set. Then
h_{M}(\Gamma)
contains a subspace

isomorphic to

if and only if there exists a vector
\displaystyle z=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e_{\gamma_{n}}\in h_{M}(\Gamma)
such that the corresponding block basis is a basis of

and
N=N(t)
is equivalent at zero to

Q(t):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M(a_{n}t).

Here for the sake of completeness of the presentation we will prove one basic and easy part of the above characterisation.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
z\in h_{M}(\Gamma)
,
z\neq 0
, and let

N(t):=\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(tz).

Then
h_{N}(\Gamma)
is isometric to a subspace of

.

Let

\mathrm{supp}\,z\subset\{\gamma_{n}:\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

We identify

with

and consider

Then

\displaystyle\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(Ux)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}M(x(\gamma)z(\gamma_{n}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

We also recall the variant of Bonic-Frampton Lemma
[
1
]
(see also
[

) we need.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let

\liminf_{t\to 0}\frac{M(t)}{t^{n-1}}=0.

(2.2)

Then for any bounded polynomial
P
on
h_{M}
of degree

\mathrm{deg}\,P<n,

such that
P(0)=0
there is at most countable
A\subset\Gamma
such that if
\gamma\not\in A
then
P(te_{\gamma})=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Since a polynomial is a finite sum of forms, it is actually enough to prove that
P(e_{\gamma})=0
for
\gamma\not\in A
, because then we will simply unite the respective
A
’s for each of the forms comprising
P
and use that the latter are homogeneous.

We do induction on
k:=\mathrm{deg}\,P
. If
k=0
the statement is trivial. Let it be true for all polynomials of degree less than
k-1
. Let
P
be a
k
-form,
k<n
. Then

P(x+y)=P(x)+P_{1}(x,y)+P(y),\quad\forall x,y\in h_{M},

where
P_{1}(x,0)=0
and the degree of
P_{1}
with respect to
y
is less than

. So, by induction hypothesis, for each
x\in h_{M}
there is countable
A_{x}\subset\Gamma
such that
P_{1}(x,e_{\gamma})=0
for each
\gamma\not\in A_{x}
, so
P_{1}(x,te_{\gamma})=0
for all
t
. Since
P
is
k
-homogeneous
P(te_{\gamma})=t^{k}P(e_{\gamma})
and we get

P(x+te_{\gamma})=P(x)+t^{k}P(e_{\gamma}),\quad\forall\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus A_{x}.

(2.3)

Assume now that
P(e_{\gamma})\neq 0
for uncountably many
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Then there is
r>0
such that
|P(e_{\gamma})|\geq r
for uncountably many
\gamma
’s. By multiplying
P
by a non-zero constant we may assume that

P(e_{\gamma})\geq 1,\quad\forall\gamma\in B,

(2.4)

where
B\subset\Gamma
is uncountable.

We will show that
\sup_{\|x\|\leq 1}P(x)=\infty
and get a contradiction.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be arbitrary. From (
2.2
) there is
t\in(0,\varepsilon)
such that

M(t)<\varepsilon t^{n-1}\leq\varepsilon t^{k}.

Let

m:=\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon t^{k}}\right].

We iterate on (

) and (

) to get

as follows. Set
x_{0}=0
. If
x_{j}
is constructed, take
\gamma_{j}\in B\setminus(A_{x_{j}}\cup\mathrm{supp}\,x_{j})
and let

. So,

P(x_{j+1})\geq P(x_{j})+t^{k},

and, therefore,

P(x_{m})\geq mt^{k}\geq\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon t^{k}}-1\right)t^{k}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-t^{k}>\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{k}\to\infty,

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
On the other hand, since

x_{m}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}te_{\gamma_{j}},

and
\gamma_{j}
’s do not repeat, we have

\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x_{m})=mM(t)<m\varepsilon t^{k}\leq\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon t^{k}}\right)\varepsilon t^{k}=1,

so
\|x_{m}\|\leq 1
.
∎

3
Necessary Condition for Smoothness

In this section we prove the following necessary condition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
.
If there is continuous
p
-times Gâteaux differentiable bump function on
h_{M}(\Gamma)
, then either

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{M(t)}{t^{p}}=0,

(3.1)

or
p
is even and
M
is equivalent to
t^{p}
at zero, that is,
h_{M}(\Gamma)
and
\ell_{p}(\Gamma)
are isomorphic.

We start with:

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let

\varphi(t):=\frac{M(t)}{t^{p}},

so that
\varphi
is continuous on
(0,1]
and

\limsup_{t\to 0}\varphi(t)>0,

because of (
1.5
). We can choose a sequence
t_{k}\to 0
which satisfies (
1.6
) and

\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(t_{k})}\leq s,\quad\forall t\in[t_{k},1],\ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}

(3.2)

for some
s>0
.

Indeed, if
\varphi(t)
is bounded on
(0,1]
, say,
\varphi(t)\leq b
for all
t\in(0,1]
, take some sequence
t_{k}\to 0
such that
\varphi(t_{k})\geq r>0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, meaning that (
1.6
) is fulfilled, but also
\varphi(t)/\varphi(t_{k})\leq b/r
for all
t\in(0,1]
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, so (
3.2
) holds.

If, on the other hand,
\varphi(t)
is unbounded, take
t_{k}\in[k^{-1},1]
such that
\varphi(t_{k})=\max\{\varphi(t):\ t\in[k^{-1},1]\}
. Obviously,
\varphi(t_{k})\to\infty
, so
t_{k}\to 0
, and (
1.6
) is true, but also
\varphi(t)/\varphi(t_{k})\leq 1
for all
t\in[t_{k},1]
, and (
3.2
) is true.

Now, from (
3.2
) it easily follows that
M
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition with respect to
T=(t_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
. Indeed, fix
c>1
and let
T_{c}=\{t\in T:\ tc\leq 1\}
. Since the set
T\setminus T_{c}
is finite, it is enough to prove (
1.2
) for
T_{c}
. But for
t\in T_{c}
the estimate (
3.2
) gives
\varphi(ct)\leq s\varphi(t)
, that is,
M(ct)\leq sc^{p}M(t)
, so

\sup_{t\in T_{c}}\frac{M(ct)}{M(t)}\leq sc^{p}.

∎

We will first prove Proposition
3.1
under the additional assumption (
3.3
), because after that the general case easily follows.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
M
be such that

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{M(t)}{t^{p-1}}=0.

(3.3)

If there is a continuous
p
-times Gâteaux differentiable bump function on
h_{M}(\Gamma)
, then either (
3.1
) is fulfilled or
p
is even and
M
is equivalent to
t^{p}
at zero.

Proof.

Assume the contrary, that is, (
3.1
) fails and, if
p
is even,
M
is not equivalent to
t^{p}
at zero; yet there is continuous
p
-times Gâteaux differentiable bump function
b
on
h_{M}(\Gamma)
.

By considering instead of
b
the bump
b(r(x-x_{0}))
, where
x_{0}
is such that
b(x_{0})\neq 0
and
r>0
is large enough, we can assume without loss of generality that
0\in\mathrm{supp}\,b\subset B_{h_{M}(\Gamma)}
, the unit ball of
h_{M}(\Gamma)
. For technical reasons we introduce the function

f(x):=\begin{cases}b^{-2}(x)-b^{-2}(0),&\mbox{if }x\in\mathrm{supp}\,b,\\
+\infty,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Clearly,
f
is a continuous function from
h_{M}(\Gamma)
to
[0,\infty]
which is
p
-times Gâteaux differentiable at each point of its domain

\mathrm{dom}\,f:=\{x\in h_{M}(\Gamma):\ f(x)<\infty\}=\mathrm{supp}\,b,

and
f(0)=0
.

Because (
3.1
) fails, (
1.5
) holds and we can apply Theorem
1.4
to get a sequence
T=(t_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
tending to
0
such that
M
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition with respect to
T
and (
1.6
) holds.

Claim.
For each
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
there is uncountable
B(x)\subset\Gamma\setminus\mathrm{supp}\,x
such that for each
\gamma\in B(x)
there is
s=s(x,\gamma)\in\{-1,1\}
such that

f(x+st_{k}e_{\gamma})-f(x)<\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x+st_{k}e_{\gamma})-\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x)

(3.4)

for all
k
large enough.

Indeed, because
f
is
p
-times Gâteaux differentiable at
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
,

f(x+ty)=f(x)+P(ty)+t^{p}Q(y)+o_{y}(t^{p}),

where
P
is a polinomial of degree at most
(p-1)
such that
P(0)=0
and
Q
is a
p
-form.

From (
3.3
) and Lemma
2.4
there is a countable
A\subset\Gamma
such that
P(te_{\gamma})=0
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus A
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We can assume
\mathrm{supp}\,x\subset A
, because the former is countable. So, for any
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus A

f(x+te_{\gamma})-f(x)=t^{p}Q(e_{\gamma})+o_{\gamma}(t^{p}),\quad\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x+te_{\gamma})-\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x)=M(t).

(3.5)

We can immediately conclude if the set
\{\gamma:\ Q(e_{\gamma})\neq 0\}
is countable. Indeed, we may assume that
A
contains that set as well. Then for any
\gamma\in B(x):=\Gamma\setminus A

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{f(x+te_{\gamma})-f(x)}{t^{p}}=0,

while
\liminf_{k\to\infty}M(t_{k})/t_{k}^{p}>0
by (
1.6
) and the assumption that (
3.1
) fails. So, (
3.4
) is fulfilled for
s=1
and all
k
large enough.

Let now
\{\gamma:\ Q(e_{\gamma})\neq 0\}
be uncountable set. Then there is uncountable
B(x)\subset\Gamma\setminus A
such that all
Q(e_{\gamma})
’s have the same sign on
B(x)
. Set
s=-\mathrm{sign}\,Q(e_{\gamma})
. So, (
3.5
) can be rewritten as:

f(x+st_{k}e_{\gamma})-f(x)=s^{p}t_{k}^{p}Q(e_{\gamma})+o_{\gamma}(t_{k}^{p}),\quad\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x+st_{k}e_{\gamma})-\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x)=M(t_{k}).

We have three distinct cases to consider:

(i)
p
odd,

(ii)
p
even and
\limsup_{t\to 0}M(t)/t^{p}=\infty
, and

(iii)
p
even and
\limsup_{t\to 0}M(t)/t^{p}<\infty
.

In the case (i) we have
s^{p}=s
, so
r_{\gamma}:=s^{p}Q(e_{\gamma})<0
for
\gamma\in B(x)
and, therefore,
f(x+st_{k}e_{\gamma})-f(x)<0
for any fixed
\gamma\in B(x)
and all
k\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, because
\lim_{k\to\infty}(f(x+st_{k}e_{\gamma})-f(x))/t_{k}^{p}=r_{\gamma}
. On the other hand
M(t_{k})>0
. Hence, (
3.4
) is fulfilled.

In the case (ii) because of (
1.6
) we have that
M(t_{k})/t_{k}^{p}\to\infty
, while
|f(x+st_{k}e_{\gamma})-f(x)|/t_{k}^{p}
is bounded for fixed
\gamma\in B(x)
, so (
3.4
) again is easily fulfilled.

The case (iii) is actually impossible under our present assumptions. Indeed, by assumption
M
is not equivalent to
t^{p}
at zero, so
\liminf_{t\to 0}M(t)/t^{p}=0
, but then Lemma
2.4
implies
Q(e_{\gamma})=0
for all but countably many
\gamma
’s, contradiction.

For convenience we assume that the sequence
T=(t_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
is strictly decreasing.
For
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
and
\gamma\in B(x)
we denote by
N(x,\gamma)
the set of all positive integers
k
for which (
3.4
) holds for some
s=s(x,\gamma)
. From the
Claim
we get that
N(x,\gamma)\neq\emptyset
. We denote by
m(x)
the smallest positive integer in the set
\displaystyle\bigcup_{\gamma\in B(x)}N(x,\gamma)
. We choose a
\mu(x)\in B(x)
in such a way that

m(x)\in N(x,\mu(x)).

Set
x_{0}=0
. Since
f(x_{0})=0
, of course
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
. Using the
Claim
inductively, we choose sequences
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathrm{dom}\,f
,
(\gamma_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\Gamma
,
(s_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\{-1,1\}
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\gamma_{n}=\mu(x_{n-1}),

(3.6)

\nu_{n}=t_{m(x_{n-1})},

(3.7)

x_{n}:=x_{n-1}+s_{n}\nu_{n}e_{\gamma_{n}},

f(x_{n})-f(x_{n-1})\leq\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x_{n})-\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x_{n-1}).

(3.8)

Since all
x_{n}
’s are in the domain of
f
which is within the unit ball of
h_{M}(\Gamma)
, we get
\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_{j}\nu_{j}e_{\gamma_{j}}\|=\|x_{n}\|\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. From Fact
1.3
it follows that the series
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}s_{j}\nu_{j}e_{\gamma_{j}}
converges in norm to some
\bar{x}\in h_{M}(\Gamma)
. Iterating on (
3.8
) we get
f(x_{n})-f(x_{0})\leq\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x_{n})-\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x_{0})
, that is,
f(x_{n})\leq\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x_{n})
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. From the continuity of
f
and
\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x_{n})\leq 1
we get
f(\bar{x})\leq 1
, so
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
.

Pick
\beta\in B(\bar{x})\setminus\mathrm{supp}\,\bar{x}
. Let
s=s(\bar{x},\beta)
and
l\in N(\bar{x},\beta)
. By definition this means
f(\bar{x}+st_{l}e_{\beta})-f(\bar{x})<\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(\bar{x}+st_{l}e_{\beta})-\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(\bar{x})
. But the latter is equal to
M(t_{l})
, so

f(\bar{x}+st_{l}e_{\beta})-f(\bar{x})<M(t_{l}).

(3.9)

On the other hand,
f(x_{n}+st_{l}e_{\beta})-f(x_{n})\to f(\bar{x}+st_{l}e_{\beta})-f(\bar{x})
, as
n\to\infty
. This and (
3.9
) imply
f(x_{n}+st_{l}e_{\beta})-f(x_{n})<M(t_{l})
for all
n
large enough. Note that
\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x_{n}+st_{l}e_{\beta})-\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(x_{n})=M(t_{l})
, because
\beta\not\in\mathrm{supp}\,\bar{x}\supset\mathrm{supp}\,x_{n}
. So,
l\in N(x_{n},\beta)
for all large
n
’s. This implies
l\geq m(x_{n})
and, therefore,
m(x_{n})\geq t_{l}>0
. From (
3.7
) we deduce that
\nu_{n}\geq t_{l}>0
for all
n
large enough, which contradicts the convergence of the series
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}s_{j}\nu_{j}e_{\gamma_{j}}
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

We do induction on
p
.

If
p=1
then (
3.3
) is just
M(t)\to 0
as
t\to 0
, which is so, and Proposition
3.2
shows that the statement is true in this case.

Let the statement be true for
p-1
.

If (
3.3
) is fulfilled then by Proposition
3.2
the statement is true also for
p
.

Assume that (
3.3
) is not fulfilled and there is a continuous
p
-times Gâteaux differentiable bump function
b
on
h_{M}(\Gamma)
. Since
b
is also
(p-1)
-times Gâteaux differentiable, from the induction hypothesis
M
must be equivalent to
t^{p-1}
at zero, but this implies (
3.1
) and the induction is complete.
∎

4
Reduction to a subspace

In this final section we show how to derive Theorem
1.1
from Proposition
3.1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If

\sup\left\{\frac{M(uv)}{u^{p}M(v)}:\ u,v\in(0,1]\right\}=\infty,

(4.1)

then there is a
z\in h_{M}
such that

\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(tz)}{t^{p}}=\infty.

(4.2)

Proof.

If

\limsup_{t\searrow 0}\frac{M(t)}{t^{p}}=\infty

any unit vector will do.

Assume that

\limsup_{t\searrow 0}\frac{M(t)}{t^{p}}<\infty.

(4.3)

Since the function
M(uv)/u^{p}M(v)
is continuous on the square
(u,v)\in(0,1]\times(0,1]
, we get from (
4.3
) that for any
a\in(0,1]

\sup\left\{\frac{M(uv)}{u^{p}M(v)}:\ u\in(0,1],\ v\in(a,1]\right\}<\infty.

From (
4.1
) it follows that

\sup\left\{\frac{M(uv)}{u^{p}M(v)}:\ u\in(0,1],\ v\in(0,a]\right\}=\infty.

Thus there are
u_{k},v_{k}\in(0,1]
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

M(v_{k})<\frac{1}{k^{3}},

(4.4)

and

\frac{M(u_{k}v_{k})}{u_{k}^{p}M(v_{k})}>k^{3}.

(4.5)

Note that from (
4.5
) and the convexity of
M
it follows that

k^{3}<\frac{M(u_{k}v_{k})}{u_{k}^{p}M(v_{k})}\leq\frac{u_{k}M(v_{k})}{u_{k}^{p}M(v_{k})}=\frac{1}{u_{k}^{p-1}},

so

u_{k}<\frac{1}{k^{3/(p-1)}}.

(4.6)

Let
A_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, be disjoint finite subsets of
\Gamma
such that
A_{k}
has
[1/k^{2}M(v_{k})]
elements, or, in other words,

\sum_{\gamma\in A_{k}}1=\left[\frac{1}{k^{2}M(v_{k})}\right].

(4.7)

Note that from (
4.4
) all
A_{k}
’s are non-empty.

Define the vector
z
with support in
\cup A_{k}
by

z(\gamma)=\frac{v_{k}}{n},\quad\gamma\in A_{k},\ k\in[2^{n-1}+1,2^{n}].

To check that
z\in h_{M}
, fix
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

\displaystyle\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(iz)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\sum_{\gamma\in A_{k}}M(iz(\gamma))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2^{n-1}+1}^{2^{n}}\sum_{\gamma\in A_{k}}M\left(\frac{iv_{k}}{n}\right)\quad\mbox{from \eqref{eq:An-card} }\Rightarrow

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2^{n-1}+1}^{2^{n}}\left[\frac{1}{k^{2}M(v_{k})}\right]M\left(\frac{iv_{k}}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2^{n-1}+1}^{2^{n}}\frac{M((i/n)v_{k})}{k^{2}M(v_{k})}.

In order to see that
\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(iz)<\infty
we estimate the tail
\sum_{n=i}^{\infty}
of the above sum. For
n\geq i
we have
M((i/n)v_{k})\leq M(v_{k})
, so

\sum_{n=i}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2^{n-1}+1}^{2^{n}}\frac{M((i/n)v_{k})}{k^{2}M(v_{k})}\leq\sum_{n=i}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2^{n-1}+1}^{2^{n}}\frac{1}{k^{2}}=\sum_{k=2^{i-1}+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}}<\infty.

Therefore,

\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(iz)<\infty,\ \forall i\in\mathbb{N}\Rightarrow z\in h_{M}.

Now,

\displaystyle\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(nu_{2^{n}}z)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in A_{2^{n}}}M(nu_{2^{n}}z(\gamma))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in A_{2^{n}}}M(u_{2^{n}}v_{2^{n}})\quad\mbox{from \eqref{eq:An-card} }\Rightarrow

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{2^{2n}M(v_{2^{n}})}\right]M(u_{2^{n}}v_{2^{n}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2^{2n}M(v_{2^{n}})}\right)M(u_{2^{n}}v_{2^{n}})\omega_{n},

where because of (
4.4
)

\omega_{n}=\left[\frac{1}{2^{2n}M(v_{2^{n}})}\right]\left(\frac{1}{2^{2n}M(v_{2^{n}})}\right)^{-1}\to 1,\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

From (
4.5
) it follows that

\frac{M(u_{2^{n}}v_{2^{n}})}{M(v_{2^{n}})}\geq 2^{3n}u^{p}_{2^{n}}

and so

\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(nu_{2^{n}}z)\geq 2^{n}u^{p}_{2^{n}}\omega_{n}.

Therefore,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sigma\hskip-2.84526pt_{{}_{M}}(nu_{2^{n}}z)}{(nu_{2^{n}})^{p}}\geq\lim\frac{2^{n}}{n^{p}}=\infty.

Since from (
4.6
) it follows that

nu_{2^{n}}<\frac{n}{2^{3n/(p-1)}}\to 0,

(
4.2
) holds.
∎

Now we are ready to conclude.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

If
p>\alpha_{M}
then by (
1.1
)

\sup\left\{\frac{M(uv)}{u^{p}M(v)}:\ u,v\in(0,1]\right\}=\infty,

and Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
2.3
show that
h_{M}(\Gamma)
has a subspace
h_{N}(\Gamma)
generated by one vector with

\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{N(t)}{t^{p}}=\infty.

By Proposition
3.1
there is no continuous
p
-times Gâteaux differentiable bump function on
h_{N}(\Gamma)
. But if
b
is a continuous
p
-times Gâteaux differentiable bump on
h_{M}(\Gamma)
, such that
b(x_{0})\neq 0
then the reduction of
x\to b(x-x_{0})
to
h_{N}(\Gamma)
is a continuous
p
-times Gâteaux differentiable bump. This contradiction shows that necessarily

\alpha_{M}\leq p.

If
\alpha_{M}<p
then we have (a) and if
\alpha_{M}=p
the two cases of Proposition
3.1
cover (b) and (c).
∎

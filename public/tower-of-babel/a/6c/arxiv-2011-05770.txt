Abstract
. We consider matrices on infinite trees which are universal covers of Jacobi matrices on finite graphs. We are interested in the question of the existence of sequences of finite covers whose normalized eigenvalue counting measures converge to the density of states of the operator on the infinite tree. We first of all construct a simple example where this convergence fails and then discuss two ways of constructing the required sequences: with random boundary conditions and through normal subgroups.

Introduction

This paper is a contribution to the growing literature on periodic Jacobi matrices on trees
[
3
,
5
,
7
,
10
]
. In particular, we refer the reader to
[
3
]
whose notation and ideas we will follow; see that paper for the definitions from graph theory that we will use. We will always associate a graph with the obvious topological space.

One starts out with a finite leafless graph,
{\mathcal{G}}
, and a Jacobi matrix on that graph. By this we mean a matrix with indices labelled by the vertices in the graph, whose diagonal elements are a function,
b
, on the vertices and off diagonal elements, which are only non-zero for pairs of vertices which are the two ends of some edge, with matrix elements determined by a strictly positive function,
a
, on the edges. The universal cover,
{\mathcal{T}}
, of
{\mathcal{G}}
is always a leafless tree. There is a unique and natural lift of any Jacobi matrix,
J
, on
{\mathcal{T}}
to an operator,
H
, (still called a Jacobi matrix) on
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{T}})
. Because
H
is invariant under a group of deck transformations on
{\mathcal{T}}
(see
[
12
]
or
[
18
, Section 1.7]
for the covering space language we exploit), we call
H
a
periodic Jacobi matrix
.

There are three big general theorems in the subject: (1) a result of Sunada
[
19
]
(see also
[
3
]
) on labelling of gaps in the spectrum that implies the spectrum of
H
is at most
p
bands where
p
is the number of vertices in the underlying finite graph
{\mathcal{G}}
, (2) a result of Aomoto
[
2
]
stating that if
G
has a fixed degree, then
H
has no point spectrum (see also
[
3
,
5
]
), and (3) a result of Avni–Breuer–Simon
[
3
]
that there is no singular spectrum because matrix elements of the resolvent are algebraic functions. Besides a very few additional theorems, the subject at this point is mainly some interesting examples and lots of conjectures and questions.

A basic object is the
density of states
(DOS),
dk
(and the weight this measure assigns to
(-\infty,E)
, the
integrated density of states
(IDS),
k(E)
). We fix a finite graph,
{\mathcal{G}}
, with
p
vertices and
q
edges. For each vertex,
j\in{\mathcal{G}}
, the spectral measure for
H
at vertex
r\in{\mathcal{T}}
,
d\mu_{r}
is the same for all
r\in{\mathcal{T}}
with
\Xi(r)=j
, where
\Xi:{\mathcal{T}}\to{\mathcal{G}}
is the covering map. The DOS is defined by picking one
d\mu_{r}
for each
j\in{\mathcal{G}}
, summing over
j
and dividing by
p
, the number of vertices in
{\mathcal{G}}
, that is

dk(\lambda)=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{G}};r\text{ so that }\Xi(r)=j}d\mu_{r}(\lambda)

(1.1)

In the one dimensional case, a fundamental fact
[
4
]
is that the DOS can be computed as a limit of normalized eigenvalue counting of operators restricted to boxes with either periodic or free boundary conditions (we say one dimensional because we are discussing trees but the result in
[
4
]
holds also on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\nu}
). In
[
3
]
, it is proven that for any tree but the line, this result fails for the free boundary condition case and our goal in this paper is to explore the case of periodic boundary conditions.

Of course, it isn’t quite clear what one should mean by periodic BC which is illuminated by the lego block picture. Before discussing that, we want to remind the reader about what
[
3
]
calls the lego block picture of periodic Jacobi matrices on trees, an unpublished realization of Christiansen, Simon and Zinchenko. In this view, the fundamental trees are
{\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell}
, the homogenous degree
2\ell
tree. Any leafless finite graph,
{\mathcal{G}}
has as its universal cover, a tree which can be realized as some
{\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell}
with each vertex replaced by a finite tree! (see also
[
6
,
13
]
for discussions of universal covers of non-regular graphs). Indeed, if
{\mathcal{G}}
, has
p
vertices and
q
edges, there is a maximal connected subtree,
{\mathcal{F}}
, obtained by removing
\ell
edges and it is then easy to see that the homotopy group of
{\mathcal{G}}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
, the free non-abelian group on
\ell
generators, whose natural Cayley graph is
{\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell}
. In general we think of the
2\ell
edges coming out of each vertex as labelled
e_{1}^{+},\dots,e_{\ell}^{+},e_{1}^{-},\dots,e_{\ell}^{-}
with the rule that each
e_{j}^{\pm}
has to be connected to an
e_{j}^{\mp}
edge of a neighboring graph.

Now view
{\mathcal{G}}
as the finite tree,
{\mathcal{F}}
, with
\ell
edges added but rather than removing these edges, one imagines cutting them and leaving
2\ell
half edges
e_{1}^{+},\dots,e_{\ell}^{+},e_{1}^{-},\dots,e_{\ell}^{-}
dangling. These are the lego blocks which we place one at each vertex of
{\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell}
and connect the dangling edges by the above rules. Thus the Hilbert space for
{\mathcal{H}}=\ell^{2}({\mathcal{T}})
on which
H
acts is replaced by vector valued functions on
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell};{\mathbb{C}}^{p})
.
H
is now a block Jacobi matrix where the diagonal elements are
p\times p
block Jacobi matrices obtained by restricting the original Jacobi matrix on
{\mathcal{G}}
to the subgraph
{\mathcal{F}}
, i.e. dropping the
a
’s from the cut edges. The off diagonal piece linking two neighboring vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
is a rank one
p\times p
matrix, which associated to the edge
e_{j}
linking
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell}
has a single non-zero matrix element (namely the
{\mathcal{G}}
Jacobi parameter associated to
e_{j}
) at the vertices inside the copies of
{\mathcal{F}}
corresponding to the vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
.

Because of the lego representation, if we prove convergence of eigenvalue counting measure for some class of periodic BC operators for the case of scalar
{\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell}
but where these periodic operators respect
e_{1}^{+},\dots,e_{\ell}^{+},e_{1}^{-},\dots,e_{\ell}^{-}
labelling (see below after (
1.6
)), then automatically we have results for infinite trees built over general finite leafless graphs
{\mathcal{G}}
. Thus, henceforth, we will only discuss and state results for the scalar
{\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell}
case bearing in mind that these automatically imply the more general results.

Because all the measures we consider (i.e., normalized eigenvalue counting and DOS) have supports in some fixed bounded set (once we fix all the Jacobi parameters), to prove convergence of measures, it suffices to prove convergence of moments. In this regard, we will need a graphical representation of the moments of the density of states. If
d\mu_{j}
is the spectral measure of a point
j\in{\mathcal{T}}
, then

\int\lambda^{k}d\mu_{j}(\lambda)=\langle\delta_{j},H^{k}\delta_{j}\rangle

(1.2)

Expanding
H^{k}
, one sees that

\langle\delta_{j},H^{k}\delta_{j}\rangle=\sum_{\omega\in W_{j,k}}\rho(\omega)

(1.3)

where
W_{j,k}
is the set of all “walks” of length
k
starting and ending at site
j
, i.e.
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{k+1}\in{\mathcal{T}}
where
\omega_{1}=\omega_{k+1}=j
and for
m=1,\dots,k
, one has that either
\omega_{m+1}=\omega_{m}
or
\omega_{m}
and
\omega_{m+1}
are neighbors in
{\mathcal{T}}
(i.e. two ends of a single edge). Moreover

\rho(\omega)=\rho_{1}(\omega)\dots\rho_{k}(\omega)

(1.4)

\rho_{m}(\omega)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}b_{\omega_{m}},&\hbox{ if }\omega_{m}=\omega_{m+1}\\
a_{(\omega_{m},\omega_{m+1})}&\hbox{ if }\omega_{m}\neq\omega_{m+1}\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

On the other hand, suppose we have a finite cover,
{\mathcal{G}}_{r}
of
{\mathcal{G}}
(of covering order
r
) and we let
H_{r}
be the lift of
J
to
{\mathcal{G}}_{r}
,
n_{r}=\#({\mathcal{G}}_{r})
and
N_{r}
the normalized eigenvalue counting measure for
H_{r}
(later we’ll sometimes use
N^{(r)}
), then

\displaystyle\int\lambda^{k}dN_{r}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=n_{r}^{-1}\text{\rm{Tr}}(H_{r}^{k})

\displaystyle=n_{r}^{-1}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{G}}_{r}}\langle\delta_{j},H_{r}^{k}\delta_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=n_{r}^{-1}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{G}}_{r}}\sum_{\omega\in W_{j,k,r}}\rho(\omega)

(1.6)

where
W_{j,k,r}
is defined like
W_{j,k}
except that we require
\omega_{m}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{r}
instead of
\omega_{m}\in{\mathcal{T}}
.

In the tree, the only paths that start and end at
j
retrace where they have been so a little thought shows that difference between the average of (
1.3
) over a cell and (
1.6
) is due to the existence of simple closed loops in
{\mathcal{G}}_{r}
. Thus if there are no short closed loops in
{\mathcal{G}}_{r}
as
r\to\infty
, we expect that
dN_{r}
will converge to
dk
. Indeed, it suffices that there be few such closed path compared to
n_{r}
.

If the operator is acting on
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell};{\mathbb{C}}^{p})
with matrix Jacobi parameters, there is still a random walk representation. In (
1.5
) the
a
’s and
b
’s are replaced by matrices, the order in (
1.4
) matters and the right side of (
1.4
) is
\text{\rm{Tr}}(\rho_{1}(\omega)\dots\rho_{k}(\omega))
. All the arguments in later sections where we prove results for general period
1
operators on
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell})
easily extend to matrix valued period
1
Jacobi operators on
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell};{\mathbb{C}}^{p})
. Then via the Lego representation we get results for general scalar periodic Jacobi matrices on trees.

One way to construct natural periodic boundary condition objects is to start with a fixed vertex in
{\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell}
, most naturally, the identity after
{\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell}
is identified with
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
, and look at
\Lambda_{r}
, the set of all vertices a distance at most
r
from the centered point. The boundary of this set (i.e. all points a distance equal to
r
from the center),
\partial\Lambda_{r}
has
(2\ell)(2\ell-1)^{r-1}
points. Each of them can be viewed as having
2\ell-1
dangling half edges sticking out which have natural labels with one of
e_{1}^{+},\dots,e_{\ell}^{+},e_{1}^{-},\dots,e_{\ell}^{-}
missing. We can form a natural cover of the basic
{\mathcal{G}}
(which for this case is a graph,
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}_{\ell}
, with one vertex and
\ell
self loops) by pairing each dangling
e_{j}^{+}
with some dangling
e_{j}^{-}
.

For a natural way to do this, view
{\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell}
as the Cayley graph of
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
, take the center of
\Lambda_{r}
to be the identity in
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
so that
\Lambda_{r}
is all words in
e_{1}^{+},\dots,e_{\ell}^{+},e_{1}^{-},\dots,e_{\ell}^{-}
(with the only relation being that
e_{j}^{-}
is the inverse of
e_{j}^{+}
) of length at most
r
. If
b=w_{1}\dots w_{r}\in\partial\Lambda_{r}
, then
\tilde{b}=w_{1}^{-1}\dots w_{r}^{-1}
are distinct points in
\partial\Lambda_{r}
whose dangling edges match (i.e. for each
e_{j}^{\pm}
coming out of
b
where is an
e_{j}^{\mp}
coming out of
\tilde{b}
) and we can form a cover,
{\mathcal{G}}_{r}
, by joining together these dangling edges. This is in some way the most natural kind of periodic BC object and in Section
2
we will see this has too many short closed loops and its normalized eigenvalue counting measures do
not
converge to
dk
. Our main result in Section
2
will be that if the dangling edges in
\Lambda_{r}
are connected in a random manner, then the eigenvalue counting measures do converge to
dk
. This is because we’ll show such random graphs have very few small loops. This result is related, of course, to the result of McKay
[
14
]
that the eigenvalue measures for Laplacians of large random graphs of fixed degree converge to the DOS of the Laplacian of a tree of the same degree.

There is, of course, another way of thinking about collections of larger and larger finite covers of a fixed graph,
{\mathcal{G}}
. By the theory of covering spaces
[
12
]
the covers,
{\mathcal{C}}
, of
{\mathcal{G}}
are associated to subgroups of the fundamental group of
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}
is a finite cover if and only the subgroup is of finite index. We’ll sketch the ideas here with details in Section
3
. Thus a sequence of periodic BC objects is a sequence of finite index subgroups
H_{1},H_{2},\dots
of
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{G}})
. The naive notion of their going to infinity is that for any finite subset,
F
, of
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{G}})
not containing the identity, eventually, one has that
H_{j}\cap F
is empty. One might expect that is the same as saying there are no small closed loops but that is, in general, wrong (as can be seen by looking at the example at the start of Section
2
): the point is that the condition

\text{(condition K)}\quad\forall_{\text{finite }F\text{ not containing the identity}}H_{j}\cap F=\emptyset,\text{ eventually}

only implies the absence of small closed loops containing the base point of the cover. For other points we need
hH_{j}h^{-1}\cap F=\emptyset
for conjugates of
H
. Thus a special role is played by covers, which we’ll call
homogenous covers
, associated to normal subgroups of
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{G}})
(we note that the subgroups associated to the covers of Section
2
are (once
r
is large) never normal). If all the
H_{j}
are normal, we will see easily that condition K is enough to imply that eigenvalue counting measures converge to the DOS. So the issue becomes the existence of sequences of normal subgroups of
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
that obey condition K. This is a well known folk theorem that we’ll discuss in Section
3
providing a second class of periodic BC objects for which the desired convergence result holds.

In summary, we construct two classes of periodic BC Hamiltonians for which the normalized eigenvalue counting measures converge to the tree DOS. In Section
2
, we look at
\Lambda_{r}
with random pairings of the dangling ends. In Section
3
we construct homogeneous covers with this property.

Remark
.

In general (unless the tree is of degree two), the spectrum of the Jacobi matrix on the finite graph is not contained in the spectrum of its lift to the tree
[
20
]
. The convergence of the eigenvalue counting measures of random covers to the density of states says in particular that these covers have few eigenvalues outside the spectrum of the tree. Related to this, using Bordenave-Collins
[
8
]
, it is possible to show that for the sequence of random covers (chosen uniformly of the set of all degree k covers) of growing degree, the Hausdorff distance between the added eigenvalues and the spectrum of the tree converges in probability to zero, a direct analog of McKay’s result
[
14
]
. That is there is a third collection of covers for which we can prove convergence of the normalized eigenvalue counting measure to the DOS of the tree.

2.
Random Centered BC

In this section, we will fix
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}};\ell\geq 2
and take
{\mathcal{G}}
to be the graph with one vertex and
\ell
self loops so that the covering tree,
{\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell}
, is the homogeneous tree of degree
2\ell
, which we can identify with the Cayley graph of
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
, the free group on
\ell
generators. We also fix a Jacobi matrix,
J
, on
{\mathcal{G}}
. We’ll discuss covers of
{\mathcal{G}}
where we can label the edges coming out from a vertex as
e_{1}^{+},\dots,e_{\ell}^{+},e_{1}^{-},\dots,e_{\ell}^{-}
with the rule that each
e_{j}^{\pm}
has to be connected to an
e_{j}^{\mp}
edge of a neighboring vertex of the cover. As we explained in the introduction, if we prove results about a class of finite covers whose normalized eigenvalue counting measure converges to the tree DOS, then using lego blocks, there are automatically results about general graphs which we will not state explicitly (essentially by extending the results to vector valued functions and block Jacobi matrices).

We will first describe our general framework, then give the example mentioned in the introduction with too many short loops, next state our general result and finally describe its proof.

For the time being, we fix
r
although eventually, we will use

as a label on sequences where we take

. In
{\mathcal{T}}_{2\ell}
, let
\Lambda_{r}
be the set of all vertices a distance at most

from the origin of

thought of as
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
so that
\Lambda_{r}
is the all words in
e_{1}^{+},\dots,e_{\ell}^{+},e_{1}^{-},\dots,e_{\ell}^{-}
of length at most
r
(with the relations that
e_{j}^{-}=(e_{j}^{+})^{-1}
). Let

so that

\#(\partial\Lambda_{r})=\tfrac{2\ell}{2\ell-1}M_{r};\qquad\#(\Lambda_{r})=\tfrac{\ell M_{r}-1}{\ell-1}

(2.1)

are comparable to
M_{r}
. For each vertex in
\partial\Lambda_{r}
we imagine extra half edges labelled by all the labels among
e_{1}^{+},\dots,e_{\ell}^{+},e_{1}^{-},\dots,e_{\ell}^{-}
except for the label of the edge that connects that vertex to the interior of
\Lambda_{r}
. It is easy to see that for each

and
\pm
there are exactly

dangling half vertices. By a

half-edge (at the same or a different vertex in

is

. For each

, we get a graph,

by adding to

. We let

be the associated Jacobi matrix on

and let

be the associated normalized eigenvalue counting measure.

In Section
1
, we defined a map

of
\Lambda_{r}
which also maps
\partial\Lambda_{r}
to itself (the square of the map is
{\boldsymbol{1}}
and it leaves no point of

Proposition 2.1
.

For any choice of Jacobi parameters

on
{\mathcal{G}}
, the limit
\lim_{r\to\infty}\int\lambda^{2}dN^{(r)}_{q_{0}}(\lambda)
exists and is strictly large than the second moment of the associated infinite tree DOS.

Proof.

Let
b
be the Jacobi parameter for the vertex in
{\mathcal{G}}
and

for the
\ell
edges. Since the full tree is fully translation invariant, the DOS is the spectral measure associated to any vertex so, by (

) and (

), we see that

\int\lambda^{2}dk(\lambda)=b^{2}+2\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}a_{m}^{2}

(2.2)

since paths of length two that start and end at a vertex on the tree either stay at that vertex for two steps (giving

) or go out and back in one of the
2\ell
edges

giving each

twice.

For all vertices in
{\mathcal{G}}_{q_{0}}
other than those on the boundary, these are the only paths of length

so the contribution to (
1.6
) from sites

is as given on the right of (

). But there are additional paths of length two that start at

by going to

\tilde{j}

Thus, because of small closed loops, we can’t expect the periodic BC eigenvalue counting measure to converge to the DOS for all possible pairs and we turn to random pairings. We put normalized counting measure,

For almost every choice

Step 1.

B_{m,\alpha}=\{q\,\mid\,q\text{ is }m,\alpha\text{-bad }\}

We will prove that there is a constant

{\mathbb{P}}_{r}(B_{m,\alpha})\leq T_{m}/M_{r}

Step 2.
If

Step 3.
Prove that for each positive integer,

Prove that for almost every choice

Proof of Step 1.

This is the most involved argument in the paper and is critical. We use the word “finitely” to indicate that a quantity only depends on
m
but can be chosen independently of

3.
Homogeneous BC

In this section, we will identify finite covers of the basic graph
{\mathcal{G}}
with
1

\ell
self loops with subgroups of
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
and use this to find sequences of finite covers whose normalized eigenvalue counting measures converge to the DOS (distinct from the examples in Section
2
). By using the lego block representation one could then construct such sequences of finite covers for the Jacobi matrix on any finite leafless graph.

We quickly recall some basics of covering spaces for this situation. We can view the universal cover as the Cayley graph
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
. If
{\mathcal{C}}
is any cover of
{\mathcal{G}}
, then there is a covering map
\pi:{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\to{\mathcal{C}}
and if
{\boldsymbol{1}}
is the identity in
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
and we let
v_{0}=\pi({\boldsymbol{1}})
, then
H\equiv\pi^{-1}[v_{0}]
is precisely those
h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
so that the simple path from
{\boldsymbol{1}}
to
h
is pushed by
\pi
to a closed curve. This easily implies that
H
is a group and that
\pi
from
H
to the just mentioned closed path is an isomorphism of
H
to the fundamental group,
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{C}},v_{0})
. Thus there is a 1-1 correspondence between subgroups of
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
and equivalence classes of covers of
{\mathcal{G}}
. The finite covers correspond precisely to subgroups,
H
, of finite index.

We are interested in sequences of finite covers,
\{{\mathcal{C}}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, which in some sense converge to
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
. One notion of this that the corresponding subgroups
H_{n}
have the property that any given
h\neq{\boldsymbol{1}}
lies in only finitely many
H_{n}
. We’ll call the corresponding covering Jacobi matrices
a sequence of periodic BC objects converging to infinity
. We are especially interested in the case where each
{\mathcal{C}}_{n+1}
is a finite cover of
{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
for all
n
, which we call
a tower of periodic BC objects converging to infinity
(if it indeed converges to infinity). We have a tower if and only if the sequence
H_{n}
is nested, i.e.
H_{n}\subset H_{n+1}
in which case convergence to infinity is equivalent to
\cap H_{n}=\{{\boldsymbol{1}}\}
.

As we’ve seen, to get convergence of the normalized eigenvalue counting measures to the DOS we need very few small loops. At first sight, one might think that is automatic when the covers converge to infinity since
H
is connected to closed loops through
v_{0}
so a condition like
\cap H_{n}=\{{\boldsymbol{1}}\}
. Indeed, by the random walk representation, this implies that
\langle\delta_{v_{0}},J_{{\mathcal{C}}_{n}}^{m}\delta_{v_{0}}\rangle
converges to the
m
th moment of the DOS. But, of course, it can’t always be true for the normalized eigenvalue counting measures since all the periodic BC operators we discussed in Section
2
converge to infinity and by Proposition
2.1
we do not have convergence of the normalized counting measures for the sequence of
q_{0}
’s!

A little thought shows that if
v_{1}=\pi(g)
for some
g\in{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
and
{\mathcal{C}}
corresponds to the subgroup,
H
, then the paths through
v_{1}
moved to
{\boldsymbol{1}}
are given by
gHg^{-1}
. In terms of measuring how big the closed paths are, that might seem harmless, but we note that if, for example,
\ell=2
and

are generators of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
, then

is distance

from

, while if

, then

is close to

. The moral is that the absence of small loops through
{\boldsymbol{1}}
does not imply the same for all points. But, if

, then no small loops through

implies no small loops through

! This suggests that finite covers associated to normal subgroups should be especially interesting. We will call the cover associated to such a normal subgroup a

for reasons that will become clear in a moment.

If

is a homogeneous cover associated to a normal subgroup

is the Cayley graph of the quotient group

Theorem 3.1
.

If
J_{n}
a sequence of periodic BC objects converging to infinity which are all operators on homogenous covers, then its normalized eigenvalue counting measures converge to the DOS.

Of course, for this to be interesting, there have to exist such sequences so the following is interesting

Theorem 3.2

For any
\ell

and so towers of homogenous covers converging to infinity. In particular, for these towers, we get associated Jacobi matrices whose normalized eigenvalue counting measures converge to the DOS.

There is of course a huge literature and knowledge about the structure of

and we believe many experts would regard this theorem as folk wisdom, but we feel it is useful to sketch one explicit construction. We note that if
{\mathbb{H}}_{n}\subset{\mathbb{G}}

Proposition 3.3

Proof.

It is remarkable that this algebraic fact about discrete groups will be proven using covering space theory, a subject that arose in complex analysis! For

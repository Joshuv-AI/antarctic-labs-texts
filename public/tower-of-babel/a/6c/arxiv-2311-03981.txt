Non-singular word maps for linear groups
Henry Bradford, Jakob Schneider, Andreas Thom

Abstract
. We study the word image of words with constants in GL ⁡ ( V ) \GL(V) and show that it is large provided the word satisfies some natural conditions on its length and its critical constants. There are various consequences: We prove that for every l ≥ 1 l\geq 1 , there are only finitely many pairs ( n , q ) (n,q) such that the length of the shortest non-singular mixed identity PSL n ⁡ ( q ) \PSL_{n}(q) is bounded by l l . We generalize the Hull–Osin dichotomy for highly transitive permutation groups to linear groups over finite fields. Finally, we show that the rank limit of GL n ⁡ ( q ) \GL_{n}(q) for q q fixed and n → ∞ n\to\infty is mixed identity free.



Introduction

Denote by
\mathbf{F}_{r}=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\rangle
the free group on
r
generators. Let
G
be a group and let
w\in G\ast\mathbf{F}_{r}
be a word with
r
variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
and constants in
G
. We say that
w
is a
mixed identity
for
G
if
w
is non-trivial and
w(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})=1_{G}
for all
h_{1},\dots,h_{r}\in G.

Here we write
w(h_{1},\ldots,h_{r})
for the image
\varphi(w)
of

under the unique homomorphism
\varphi\colon G\ast\mathbf{F}_{r}\to G
which maps
x_{i}\mapsto h_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
and fixes
G
elementwise. On
G\ast\mathbf{F}_{r}
we consider the word length, which assigns length one to the generators
x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{r}^{\pm 1}
and length zero to elements of
G
. In this setting, the
augmentation map

\varepsilon\colon G\ast\mathbf{F}_{r}\to\mathbf{F}_{r}
denotes the unique homomorphism which maps all
G\ni g\mapsto 1_{\mathbf{F}_{r}}
to the identity and fixes each element of
\mathbf{F}_{r}
. The elements in the kernel of
\varepsilon
are called
singular
and
\varepsilon(w)
is called the
content
of

.

In this article we continue the study of non-singular word maps on finite groups, which we started in
[
17
]
, where the second and third named authors treated the case of symmetric and alternating groups.
One of the main results in
[
17
]
was that any non-singular mixed identity of
S_{n}
is of length at least
\Omega(\log(n)/\log(\log(n))).
This is in contrast to the length of singular mixed identities, which can be of bounded length. See
[
3
]
for more information on mixed identities in this context. Non-singular word maps for compact Lie groups where studied in
[
11
]
and for linear algebraic groups in
[
7
]
.

A
law
or an
identity
of a group
G
is a non-trivial word
w\in\mathbf{F}_{r}
such that
w(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})=1_{G}
for all
h_{1},\dots,h_{r}\in G
. We say that
w\in\mathbf{F}_{r}
is a
coset identity
if there exists a subgroup of finite index
H
and cosets
g_{1}H,\dots,g_{r}H
, such that
w(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})=1_{G}
for all
h_{i}\in g_{i}H,i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
. It is a major open problem to decide if every finitely generated pro-finite group that admits a coset identity also admits an identity, see for example
[
15
]
*Problem 12.95 and the work of Larsen–Shalev
[
12
]
*Problem 3.1. Note that every coset identity yields a non-singular mixed identity

w^{\prime}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})\coloneqq w(g_{1}x_{1}^{d},\dots,g_{r}x_{r}^{d})

for
d=[G:H]!
.
Hence, a potential strategy to answer Larsen and Shalev’s question is to study the length of the shortest non-singular mixed identity of a finite group
G
and compare it to the length of the shortest identity. It can be shown that the answer to Larsen and Shalev’s question is positive if these quantities can be bounded in terms of each other.

Question 1
.

Does the existence of a non-singular mixed identity

for a finite group
G

We are able to prove that the length of the shortest non-singular mixed identity for

tends to infinity as

\PSL_{n}(q)
has been studied in
[
4

. Hence, this contributes another class of cases where this question has a positive answer, see Corollary

In view of the corresponding result of Jones
[
10
]
on laws for non-abelian finite simple groups, we put forward the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1
.

Let

. The number of isomorphism classes of non-abelian finite simple groups satisfying a non-singular mixed identity of length
\leq l
is finite.

Similarly to our results for symmetric groups, our analysis of word maps allows also for detailed information on the image of word maps, singular or non-singular, as long as the lengths of the critical constants (see Section
1

It was shown by Gordeev–Kunyavskii–Plotkin in

In Section

We prove a similar result for highly linearly transitive groups acting on a countably infinite dimensional vector space over a finite field, see Theorem

*Proposition A.1, see Theorem

It is a consequence of the original Hull–Osin dichotomy that full groups of ergodic, probability measure preserving, measurable equivalence relations on a non-atomic probability space are mixed identity free, see the discussion in Section

Finally, this paper might be the starting point of a more systematic study of mixed identities for oligomophic permutation groups such as

of

1.
Basic notions

Let’s recall some basics where we follow the notation introduced in
[
17
]
. Let
G
be a group. Throughout the entire article, let

w=c_{0}x_{\iota(1)}^{\varepsilon(1)}c_{1}\cdots c_{l-1}x_{\iota(l)}^{\varepsilon(l)}c_{l}\in G\ast\mathbf{F}_{r}=G\ast\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\rangle

be a fixed word with constants
c_{j}\in G
(for
j\in\{0,\ldots,l\}
) with
\varepsilon(j)\in\{\pm 1\}
and
\iota(j)\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
(for
j\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
). Subsequently,
H\leq G
will be the group whose elements we plug into
w
. Set
J_{0}(w)\coloneqq\{j\in\{1,\ldots,l-1\}\,|\,\iota(j)\neq\iota(j+1)\}
,
J_{+}(w)\coloneqq\{j\in\{1,\ldots,l-1\}\,|\,\iota(j)=\iota(j+1)\text{ and }\varepsilon(j)=\varepsilon(j+1)\}
, and
J_{-}(w)\coloneqq\{j\in\{1,\ldots,l-1\}\,|\,\iota(j)=\iota(j+1)\text{ and }\varepsilon(j)=-\varepsilon(j+1)\}
. We call the elements of
J_{-}(w)
the
critical indices
and the group elements
c_{j}
for
j\in J_{-}(w)
the
critical constants
of
w
. Furthermore, we assume that the above expression for
w
is
reduced
, i.e.
c_{j}\notin\mathbf{C}_{G}(H)\setminus\mathbf{1}
for
j\in J_{0}(w)\cup J_{+}(w)
and
c_{j}\notin\mathbf{C}_{G}(H)
for all
j\in J_{-}(w)
. Here
\mathbf{C}_{G}(H)
denotes the
centralizer
of
H
in
G
.
We call the word
w

strong
if it has no critical constants, i.e.
J_{-}(w)=\emptyset
. And we call it
cyclically strong
if
w
is strong and, in addition, it does not hold that
\iota(1)=\iota(l)
and
\varepsilon(1)=-\varepsilon(l)
. Finally, we write
\lvert w\rvert\coloneqq l
for the
length
of the word
w
.

Throughout, we will need some measure of distance on our groups. That is why we define the notion of a
seminorm
on a group. For a group
G
, this is a mapping
\lvert\bullet\rvert\colon G\to I
, where
I
is either the unit interval
[0,1]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
or the set
\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, such that
\lvert g\rvert\geq 0
(
non-negativity
),
\lvert gh\rvert\leq\lvert g\rvert+\lvert h\rvert
(
subadditivity
), and
\lvert g\rvert=\lvert g^{-1}\rvert
(
homogeneity
) for all
g,h\in G
. Each seminorm induces a left invariant
pseudometric

\dist\colon G\times G\to I
by
\dist(g,h)\coloneqq\lvert g^{-1}h\rvert.

A seminorm becomes a norm if
\lvert g\rvert=0
implies
g=1_{G}
for all
g\in G
(
positivity
). In this case, it induces a metric. For each seminorm
\lvert\bullet\rvert
, the set
G_{0}\coloneqq\lvert\bullet\rvert^{-1}(0)\leq G
is a subgroup. The seminorm is called
invariant
if it is invariant under conjugation, i.e.
\lvert g^{h}\rvert=\lvert h^{-1}gh\rvert=\lvert g\rvert
for all
g,h\in G
. In this case,
G_{0}\trianglelefteq G
is a normal subgroup; then
\lvert\bullet\rvert
descends to a norm on
G/G_{0}
. Then, the corresponding pseudometric
\dist\colon G\times G\to I
will be
bi-invariant
, i.e.
\dist(fg,fh)=\dist(g,h)=\dist(gf,hf)
for all
f,g,h\in G
. In this article, all seminorms will be invariant, whence all pseudometrics will be bi-invariant. For every seminorm
\lvert\bullet\rvert\colon G\to I
and a normal subgroup
N\trianglelefteq G
, we define the
quotient seminorm

\lvert\bullet\rvert\colon G/N\to I
by
\lvert\overline{g}\rvert\coloneqq\dist(g,N)=\inf\{\dist(g,n)\,|\,n\in N\}
for all
g\in G
.

For
w\in G\ast\mathbf{F}_{r}
and a fixed seminorm on
G
, we define the
critical length

\lvert w\rvert_{\rm crit}\coloneqq\min\{\diam(G),\lvert c_{j}\rvert\,|\,j\in J_{-}(w)\},

where the diameter is measured with respect to the fixed seminorm. Besides the word length of a word, the critical length turns out to be the most important quantity controlling lower bounds on the size of the word image.

2.
Multiply linearly transitive groups

Let
V
be a vector space over the field
K
and of finite or countably infinite dimension. We only consider the cases when
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a finite field, or
K=\mathbb{C}
are the complex numbers. Write
\GL(V)
for the general linear group of
V
.
Denote by
\lvert\bullet\rvert\colon\GL(V)\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
the
projective rank seminorm
, which is defined by

\lvert g\rvert\coloneqq\min_{\lambda\in K^{\times}}\rk(g-\lambda 1_{V})\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}

for
g\in\GL(V)
.
It descends to a
norm
, which we also denote by

\lvert\bullet\rvert\colon\PGL(V)\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}.

This induces the
projective rank pseudometric
on
\GL(V)
and a
projective rank metric
on
\PGL(V)
, both denoted by
\dist
.

Definition 1
.

Let
V
be a vector space of finite or countably infinite dimension and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
A linear group
H\leq\GL(V)
is called
linearly
k
-transitive
if for all
k
-element sets of linearly independent vectors
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\}
and
\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\}
there is a map
h\in H
such that
u_{i}.h=w_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
.

Here is our main technical result:

Theorem 1
.

Let
V
be some vector space of finite or countably infinite dimension over the field
K
. If
K
is finite, we allow
\dim(V)
to be infinite, whereas for
K=\mathbb{C}
, we require it to be finite. Let

and let the word

w=x_{\iota(1)}^{\varepsilon(1)}c_{1}\cdots c_{l-1}x_{\iota(l)}^{\varepsilon(l)}\in\GL(V)\ast\mathbf{F}_{r}

be reduced of length

.
Let

be a linearly

\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}\}

\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{d}\}

\iota(1)=\iota(l)

\varepsilon(1)=-\varepsilon(l)

\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{d}\}

d\leq\frac{1}{l}\cdot\min\{\lvert w\rvert_{\rm crit}-1,D\},

u_{i}.w(h_{1},\ldots,h_{r})=w_{i}

Proof.

We set
U=\langle u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}\rangle
and
W\coloneqq\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{d}\rangle.

In order to prove the theorem, we will define vectors
v_{i,j}^{-\varepsilon(j)}\in V

for
(i,j)\in\{1,\dots,d\}\times\{1,\dots,l\}
and elements
h_{1},\ldots,h_{r}\in H
such that

\displaystyle u_{1}=v_{1,1}^{\varepsilon(1)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h^{\varepsilon(1)}_{\iota(1)}}}{{\mapsto}}v_{1,1}^{-\varepsilon(1)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c_{1}}}{{\mapsto}}v_{1,2}^{\varepsilon(2)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h^{\varepsilon(2)}_{\iota(2)}}}{{\mapsto}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h^{\varepsilon(l-1)}_{\iota(l-1)}}}{{\mapsto}}v_{1,l-1}^{-\varepsilon(l-1)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c_{l-1}}}{{\mapsto}}v_{1,l}^{\varepsilon(l)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h^{\varepsilon(l)}_{\iota(l)}}}{{\mapsto}}v_{1,l}^{-\varepsilon(l)}=w_{1}

\displaystyle u_{d}=v_{d,1}^{\varepsilon(1)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h^{\varepsilon(1)}_{\iota(1)}}}{{\mapsto}}v_{d,1}^{-\varepsilon(1)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c_{1}}}{{\mapsto}}v_{d,2}^{\varepsilon(2)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h^{\varepsilon(2)}_{\iota(2)}}}{{\mapsto}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h^{\varepsilon(l-1)}_{\iota(l-1)}}}{{\mapsto}}v_{d,l-1}^{-\varepsilon(l-1)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c_{l-1}}}{{\mapsto}}v_{d,l}^{\varepsilon(l)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h^{\varepsilon(l)}_{\iota(l)}}}{{\mapsto}}v_{d,l}^{-\varepsilon(l)}=w_{d}.

This requirement will determine vectors
v^{\varepsilon(j)}_{i,j}\in V
by the equation
v_{i,j}^{\varepsilon(j)}\coloneqq v_{i,j-1}^{-\varepsilon(j-1)}.c_{j-1}
for
2\leq j\leq l
and
v^{\varepsilon(1)}_{i,1}\coloneqq u_{i}.

The choice of the vectors
v^{-\varepsilon(j)}_{i,j}\in V
proceeds by induction on the lexicographic order
\leq
on the set
\{1,\ldots,d\}\times\{1,\ldots,l\}
. During the procedure, we want to ensure that the sets

\{v_{i,j}^{\varepsilon}\,|\,i\in\{1,\ldots,d\},j\in\{1,\ldots,l\},\iota(j)=k\}
are linearly independent for all
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
and
k\in\{1,\dots,r\}
and we can put
v_{i,l}^{-\varepsilon(l)}\coloneqq w_{i}
at the end of the procedure.

Once this is done, we can choose
h_{k}\in H
such that

v^{+}_{i,j}.h_{k}=v^{-}_{i,j}
whenever
\iota(j)=k
and the picture is complete. For this, it is enough that

is linearly

-transitive which we assumed to be the case. Thus, in order to finish the proof, it only remains to carry out the inductive process and keep track of the linear independence.

Before we start with the induction, let’s first describe some auxiliary objects which are needed in the inductive procedure. Suppose that all
v^{\varepsilon}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
with
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\leq(i,j)
are already defined. We define subspaces for

,

,

, and
\varepsilon\in\{\pm\}
.

\displaystyle V_{i,j,k}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\langle v_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{\varepsilon}\,|\,(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\leq(i,j),\iota(j^{\prime})=k\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{r}{V_{i,j,k}^{\varepsilon}}=\langle v_{1,1}^{\varepsilon},\ldots,v_{1,l}^{\varepsilon},\ldots,v_{i,1}^{\varepsilon},\ldots,v_{i,j}^{\varepsilon}\rangle\eqqcolon V_{i,j}^{\varepsilon}\leq V,

so that
\dim(V_{i,j}^{\varepsilon})\leq(i-1)l+j
. We also set
V_{i,0,k}^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq V_{i-1,l,k}^{\varepsilon}
for

,
k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
,
\varepsilon\in\{\pm\}
.
For
\varepsilon\in\{\pm\}
and
\iota\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, we need to introduce the subspaces:

\displaystyle V_{\iota}^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\begin{cases}U&\text{if }(\iota,\varepsilon)=(\iota(1),\varepsilon(1))\text{ and }(\iota,\varepsilon)\neq(\iota(l),-\varepsilon(l))\\
W&\text{if }(\iota,\varepsilon)\neq(\iota(1),\varepsilon(1))\text{ and }(\iota,\varepsilon)=(\iota(l),-\varepsilon(l))\\

We will now describe how to choose the vectors

.
In the

Case 1.

\displaystyle v_{i,j}^{-\varepsilon(j)}.c_{j}\notin V_{i,j,\iota(j+1)}^{\varepsilon(j+1)}+V_{\iota(j+1)}^{\varepsilon(j+1)}

V_{\lambda}\coloneqq\left\{v\in V\mid v.(c_{j}-\lambda)\in V_{i,j-1,\iota(j)}^{-\varepsilon(j)}+V_{\iota(j+1)}^{\varepsilon(j+1)}\right\}

n-d_{k}\geq|c_{j}|\geq|w|_{\rm crit}>dl
is still valid and provides a bound of
n-2
on the dimension of each

for
\lambda_{k}
an eigenvalue of
c_{j}
. In order to deal with the other choices of
\lambda

\lambda\coloneqq D=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}\},
note that

is a

is of codimension at least

Case 2.3:
If

and

to consider and the inverse image of

Thus, this procedure can be carried out until we arrive at

3.
Diameter and dimension of the word image

3.1.
Diameter estimates

From Theorem
1
we get the following corollary on the diameter bound of the word image.

Corollary 1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional vector space over
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}
or
\mathbb{C}
and
w\in\GL(V)\ast\mathbf{F}_{r}
be a reduced word of length
l\geq 2
. Then the word image
w(\SL(V)^{r})
has diameter at least

\diam(w(\SL(V)^{r}))\geq\frac{\lvert w\rvert_{\rm crit}}{l}-1

in the projective rank seminorm.

Equivalently,

(1)

\lvert w\rvert(\diam(w(\SL(V)^{r}))+1)\geq\lvert w\rvert_{\rm crit}.

Let’s now assume that
w
is non-singular and try to get a lower bound on the diameter of the word image only depending on the word length and not the critical length. The argument follows closely the corresponding argument for permutation groups in
[
17
]
. If we remove the smallest critical constant
c_{j}
in
w
and perform the corresponding cancellation, we get a word
w^{\prime}
such that

\dist(w(h_{1},\ldots,h_{r}),w^{\prime}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{r}))\leq\dist(c_{j},1)=\lvert w\rvert_{\rm crit}

for all
h_{1},\ldots,h_{r}\in H
by bi-invariance of the rank metric.
Hence

\lvert\diam(w(\SL(V)^{r}))-\diam(w^{\prime}(\SL(V)^{r}))\rvert\leq 2\lvert w\rvert_{\rm crit}.

so by (
1
) we get

\displaystyle\diam(w^{\prime}(\SL(V)^{r}))

\displaystyle\leq\diam(w(\SL(V)^{r}))+2\lvert w\rvert_{\rm crit}

\displaystyle\leq\diam(w(\SL(V)^{r}))+2\lvert w\rvert(\diam(w(\SL(V)^{r}))+1).

Therefore, we obtain

\diam(w^{\prime}(\SL(V)^{r}))+1\leq(1+2\lvert w\rvert)(\diam(w(\SL(V)^{r}))+1)

Hence, if we iterate these reductions and
w=w_{0},\ldots,w_{m}
is a chain of words such that
w_{j}
is an reduction of
w_{j-1}
(
1\leq j\leq m
) and
w_{m}
is
strong
, then we get

\diam(w_{j}(\SL(V)^{r}))+1\leq(1+2\lvert w_{j-1}\rvert)(\diam(w_{j-1}(\SL(V)^{r}))+1)

for all
j=1,\ldots,m
, and by iterating we obtain

\displaystyle\diam(w_{m}(\SL(V)^{r}))+1

\displaystyle\leq(\diam(w_{0}(\SL(V)^{r}))+1)\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(1+2\lvert w_{j}\rvert)

\displaystyle\leq(\diam(w_{0}(\SL(V)^{r}))+1)(1+2\lvert w_{0}\rvert)^{m}

\displaystyle\leq(\diam(w(\SL(V)^{r}))+1)(1+2\lvert w\rvert)^{\left\lfloor\lvert w\rvert/2\right\rfloor}.

This is since
\lvert w_{j-1}\rvert\geq\lvert w_{j}\rvert-2
so that
m\leq\left\lfloor\lvert w\rvert/2\right\rfloor
. Hence by Inequality (
1
) we get

\displaystyle n=\lvert w_{m}\rvert_{\rm crit}

\displaystyle\leq(\diam(w_{m}(\SL(V)^{r}))+1)\lvert w_{m}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq(\diam(w(\SL(V)^{r}))+1)(1+2\lvert w\rvert)^{\left\lfloor\lvert w\rvert/2\right\rfloor}\lvert w\rvert.

This gives

\frac{1}{(1+2\lvert w\rvert)^{\left\lfloor\lvert w\rvert/2\right\rfloor}\lvert w\rvert}\leq\frac{\diam(w(\SL(V)^{r}))+1}{n}.

In particular, this implies that when
\lvert w\rvert
is bounded and
n\to\infty
, then also
\diam(w(\SL(V)^{r}))\to\infty
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
q
be a prime power.
Let
w
be a non-singular mixed identity for
{\rm PSL}_{n}(q)
. Then, we have

|w|=\Omega\left(\frac{\log(n)}{\log\log(n)}\right).

Corollary 3
.

For every
l\geq 1
, there are only finitely many pairs
(n,q)
such that the length of the shortest non-singular mixed identity
\PSL_{n}(q)
is bounded by
l
.

Proof.

It is known from
[
3
]
that any mixed identity for
\PSL_{n}(q)
is of length
\Omega(q)
, where the implied constant is independent of

. This and the previous corollary imply the claim.
∎

By the proof of

3.2.
Esimates of the dimension

In this section, we follow the notation from the work of Gordeev–Kunyavskii–Plotkin
[
7
]
where we stick to the special case

. Their main result is
[
7
]
*Theorem 1.1, which states that for a fixed non-singular equation

with

Theorem 2
.

Let

Proof.

Note that
d=\left\lfloor l^{-1}\lvert w\rvert_{\rm crit}-1\right\rfloor
is a valid choice for

in Theorem
1
. Hence, by linear

-transitivity of the image of

-matrices of rank

.
∎

4.
A Hull–Osin type result for linear groups

The following dichotomy theorem is due to Hull and Osin
[
9
]
*Theorem 1.6. Let’s recall it quickly before we explain our generalization to the linear case.

Theorem 3

(Hull–Osin)
.

Let
\Omega
be a countably infinite set and let
H\leq\Sym(\Omega)
be a highly transitive permutation group. Then either

\Alt(\Omega)\trianglelefteq H\quad\mbox{or}\quad\mbox{$H$ has no mixed identities.}

There is also a quantitative version of this theorem due to Le Boudec–Matte Bon
[
13
]
*Proposition A.1:

Theorem 4

(Le Boudec–Matte Bon)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
H\leq\Sym(\Omega)
be a
k
-transitive permutation group which satisfies a mixed identity of length
l
. Then either

\Alt(\Omega)\trianglelefteq H\quad\mbox{or}\quad k<l.

Remark 3
.

Now we come back to the discussion of mixed identities for oligomorphic groups at the end of the introduction. In view of
[
13
]
*Proposition A.1, an interesting case to study is the automorphism group of the random
k
-uniform hypergraph for
k\geq 3
, which acts
(k-1)
-transitively but not
k
-transitively in its natural action. It is an open problem if a dense subgroup could have a highly transitive action and maybe this is obstructed by the existence of a mixed identity. This provides an interesting test case for
[
9
]
*Question 6.1.

For the statement of the Hull–Osin-type result in the linear case, we need the following definition: For a countably infinite dimensional vector space
V
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we define the normal subgroup
\GL_{\rm fin}(V)\coloneqq\{g\in\GL(V)\,|\,\lvert g\rvert<\infty\}
of
\GL(V)
. This group consists of all the elements
g\in\GL(V)
such that there is a subspace
W\leq V
of finite codimension with
w.g=\lambda w
for all
w\in W
and a fixed scalar
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.

Before we state the linear version of Theorem
3
, we need the following preparatory lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
g\in\GL_{\rm fin}(V)
act as the scalar
\lambda
on the subspace
W\leq V
of finite codimension
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
U\leq V
be a complement of
W
, i.e.
U\oplus W=V
. Then
U^{\prime}\coloneqq U+U.g
is a
g
-invariant subspace of dimension
\leq 2n
. There is a complement
W^{\prime}\leq W
of
U^{\prime}
in
V
.

Proof.

Take
u^{\prime}=u+w\in U^{\prime}
arbitrarily for
u\in U
,
w\in W
. Then
w=u^{\prime}-u\in U^{\prime}+U=U^{\prime}
, hence
u^{\prime}.g=u.g+w.g=u.g+\lambda w\in U.g+U^{\prime}=U^{\prime}
, showing that
U^{\prime}
is
g
-invariant. Define
W^{\prime}
to be a complement to
U^{\prime}\cap W
in
W
. Then
U^{\prime}\cap W^{\prime}=U^{\prime}\cap W\cap W^{\prime}=\mathbf{0}
by definition and
U^{\prime}+W^{\prime}=U^{\prime}+(U^{\prime}\cap W)+W^{\prime}=U^{\prime}+W=V
, so
U^{\prime}\oplus W^{\prime}=V
. This completes the proof.
∎

Here comes the analogue of Theorem
3
for linear groups over finite field.

Theorem 5
.

Let
V
be a countably infinite dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
H\leq\GL(V)
be a highly linearly transitive linear group. Then either

(i)

H\cap\GL_{\rm fin}(V)
is a highly transitive, locally finite, normal subgroup, or

(ii)

H
has no mixed identities.

We will first prove a quantitative form, generalizing the result of Le Boudec–Matte Bon to linear groups.

Theorem 6
.

Let
V
be a countably infinite dimensional vector space over a finite field. Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and let
H\leq\GL(V)
be a subgroup which is linearly
k
-transitive and admits a mixed identity of length
l
. Then, at least one of the following cases hold:

(i)

H\cap\GL_{\rm fin}(V)
is a locally finite, linearly
(\left\lceil k/2\right\rceil-1)
-transitive, normal subgroup of
H
, or

(ii)

k\leq 2l.

Proof.

The inequality in Case (ii) can be proven using Theorem
1
provided all non-trivial elements of
H
, and hence critical constants in a potential mixed identity
w\in H\ast\mathbf{F}_{r}
, are of length at least
k/2
. Indeed,
k>2l
implies
\lvert w\rvert_{\rm crit}-1\geq\left\lceil k/2\right\rceil-1\geq l
and we can apply Theorem
1
with

and
D\coloneqq k
. Hence, any such
w
of length at most
l
is not a mixed identity, contradicting an assumption in the statement of the theorem. We conclude that
k\leq 2l
must hold.

Hence, in order to show Case (i), we may suppose that there exists a critical constant

of non-zero length less than
k/2
. We may assume
k\geq 3.

Using Lemma
1
, we get for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
that there exists a vector space
V_{1}
of codimension
k
with complement
V_{0}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
, such that
c
has a matrix of the form

c=\begin{pmatrix}c^{\prime}&0\\
0&\lambda 1_{V_{1}}\end{pmatrix},

with respect to the decomposition
V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}
, where
c^{\prime}\in\GL_{k}(q)
is a non-scalar matrix. Now, since
H
is linearly
k
-transitive, it contains elements of the form

g=\begin{pmatrix}g^{\prime}&*\\
0&g^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

for arbitrary
g^{\prime}\in\GL_{k}(q).

Thus, since
\SL_{k}(q)
is quasi-simple and
c^{\prime}
is non-scalar, the normal subgroup of
H
generated by
c
will contain elements of the form as above with
g^{\prime}\in\SL_{k}(q)
arbitrary. Together with the
k
-linear transitivity of
H
, this shows that the normal subgroup generated by
c
is
k^{\prime}
-linearly transitive for
k^{\prime}\coloneqq\left\lceil k/2\right\rceil-1
. Indeed, any two linearly independent sets
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k^{\prime}}\}
and
\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k^{\prime}}\}
can be moved into
V_{0}
by some element
h\in H
. Now, since
2k^{\prime}<k
, there exists some element
g^{\prime}\in\SL(V_{0})\cong\SL_{k}(q)
exchanging the two sets
\{\alpha_{1}.h,\dots,\alpha_{k^{\prime}}.h\}
and
\{\beta_{1}.h,\dots,\beta_{k^{\prime}}.h\}
. Let
g\in\langle\!\langle c\rangle\!\rangle
, which contains
g^{\prime}
in its upper left corner. Then, this shows that

\alpha_{i}.hgh^{-1}=\beta_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,k^{\prime}\}.
It is clear that the normal subgroup generated by

is contained in
H\cap\GL_{\rm fin}(V)
and that this group is locally finite. This finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5
.

Since
H
is highly linearly transitive, we can apply Theorem
6
for any

. In particular, the existence of a mixed identity implies Case (i) in Theorem

is a highly transitive, locally finite, normal subgroup of

.

However, the statement of Theorem

is a bit stronger since it says that Cases (i) and (ii) are mutually exclusive. We will now show that Case (i) arises whenever

contains an element
h
such that

, i.e. whenever

is non-trivial. Let
W\leq V
be a subspace of codimension

such that
h

\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
on

. For any

5.
No mixed identities for
A(q)

It was proved in
[
2
]
*Proof of Theorem 1.10 that the topological full group of a faithful and minimal, continuous action of a group on a Cantor set is mixed identity free. This is essentially a direct application of the Hull–Osin theorem to the action of the full group on an infinite orbit. A similar reasoning can be applied to the study of full groups of an ergodic, probability measure preserving equivalence relation on a non-atomic standard probability space.
In this section, we want to prove a linear version of this, where a certain group
A(q)
, constructed in
[
5
]
, replaces the full group.

Let’s briefly recall the construction of
A(q)
, which resembles a construction of the full group of the hyperfinite equivalence relation. Let
q
be a prime power as usual.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider
\GL_{2^{n}}(q)
and the diagonal embeddings

\iota_{n}\colon\GL_{2^{n}}(q)\to\GL_{2^{n+1}}(q);h\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&h\end{pmatrix}.

Write
\GL_{2^{\omega}}(q)=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\GL_{2^{n}}(q)
for the inductive limit of the countable chain of inclusions
(\GL_{2^{n}}(q),\iota_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Clearly, this is a countable, locally finite group. Note that the normalized rank metric is well-defined on
\GL_{2^{\omega}}(q)
. We denote its Cauchy completion by
A(q)
. Note that we could have started with
\SL_{2^{n}}(q)
and arrived at the same completion. Furthermore,
A(q)/Z
, where
Z
denotes its center
Z\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, is the completion of the inductive limit of the groups
\PGL_{2^{n}}(q)
with respect to the normalized projective rank metric. See
[
5
]
for more details on the group
A(q).
Throughout this section, we work with the normalized projective rank metric and a suitable version of Theorem
1
and its corollaries.

Now we prove the main result of this section:

Theorem 7
.

The group
A(q)/Z
does not admit a mixed identity.

Proof.

Let

w=c_{0}x^{\varepsilon(1)}_{\iota(1)}c_{1}\cdots c_{l-1}x^{\varepsilon(l)}_{\iota(l)}c_{l}\in A(q)\ast\mathbf{F}_{r}

be some reduced word with constants, where
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
. We prove that
w
has a non-trivial word image
w(A(q)^{r})
.
At first, note that, by construction, we can find a large number
n=n(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
, such that we can choose constants
c^{\prime}_{0},\dots,c^{\prime}_{l}\in\SL_{2^{n}}(q)
so that
d(c^{\prime}_{j},c_{j})\leq\varepsilon
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,l\}
.
Define the word

w^{\prime}\coloneqq c^{\prime}_{0}x^{\varepsilon(1)}_{\iota(1)}c^{\prime}_{1}\cdots c^{\prime}_{l-1}x^{\varepsilon(l)}_{\iota(l)}c^{\prime}_{l}\in\SL_{2^{n}}(q)\ast\mathbf{F}_{r}<A(q)\ast\mathbf{F}_{r}.

Note that then for all
h_{1},\ldots,h_{r}\in A(q)
, when have

\displaystyle d(w^{\prime}(h_{1},\dots,h_{r}),

\displaystyle w(h_{1},\dots,h_{r}))

\displaystyle=d(c^{\prime}_{0}h^{\varepsilon(1)}_{\iota(1)}c^{\prime}_{1}\cdots c^{\prime}_{l-1}h^{\varepsilon(l)}_{\iota(l)}c^{\prime}_{l},c_{0}h^{\varepsilon(1)}_{\iota(1)}c_{1}\cdots c_{l-1}h^{\varepsilon(l)}_{\iota(l)}c_{l})

\displaystyle\leq d(c^{\prime}_{0}h^{\varepsilon(1)}_{\iota(1)}c^{\prime}_{1}\cdots c^{\prime}_{l-1}h^{\varepsilon(l)}_{\iota(l)}c^{\prime}_{l},c^{\prime}_{0}h^{\varepsilon(1)}_{\iota(1)}c^{\prime}_{1}\cdots c^{\prime}_{l-1}h^{\varepsilon(l)}_{\iota(l)}c_{l})

\displaystyle+\cdots+d(c^{\prime}_{0}h^{\varepsilon(1)}_{\iota(1)}c_{1}\cdots c_{l-1}h^{\varepsilon(l)}_{\iota(l)}c_{l},c_{0}h^{\varepsilon(1)}_{\iota(1)}c_{1}\cdots c_{l-1}h^{\varepsilon(l)}_{\iota(l)}c_{l})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{l}{d(c^{\prime}_{j},c_{j})}\leq(l+1)\varepsilon,

Hence, we obtain

\lvert\diam(w^{\prime}(A(q)^{r}))-\diam(w(A(q)^{r}))\rvert\leq 2(l+1)\varepsilon.

From the definition
\lvert w^{\prime}\rvert_{\rm crit}\geq\lvert w\rvert_{\rm crit}-\varepsilon

and for
m\geq n
, Corollary
1
yields

\diam(w^{\prime}(\SL_{2^{m}}(q)^{r}))\geq\frac{\lvert w^{\prime}\rvert_{\rm crit}}{l}-\frac{1}{2^{m}}\geq\frac{\lvert w\rvert_{\rm crit}-\varepsilon}{l}-\frac{1}{2^{m}}

and hence

\diam(w(\SL_{2^{m}}(q)^{r}))\geq\frac{\lvert w\rvert_{\rm crit}-\varepsilon}{l}-\frac{1}{2^{m}}-2(l+1)\varepsilon.

Thus, taking
\varepsilon>0
small enough and
m\geq n(\varepsilon)
large enough, we see that the word image of
w
is non-trivial. This finishes the proof.
∎

On characterizations of a some classes of Schauder frames in Banach spaces
Rafik Karkri, Samir Kabbaj and Hamad Sidi Lafdal

Abstract
In this paper, we prove the following results. There exists a Banach space without basis which has a Schauder frame. There exists an universal Banach space B B (resp. B ~ \tilde{B} ) with a basis (resp. an unconditional basis) such that, a Banach X X has a Schauder frame (resp. an unconditional Schauder frame ) if and only if X X is isomorphic to a complemented subspace of B B (resp. B ~ \tilde{B} ). For a weakly sequentially complete Banach space, a Schauder frame is unconditional if and only if it is besselian. A separable Banach space X X has a Schauder frame if and only if it has the bounded approximation property. Consequenty, The Banach space ℒ ⁡ ( ℋ , ℋ ) \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}) of all bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space ℋ \mathcal{H} has no Schauder frame. Also, if X X and Y Y are Banach spaces with Schauder frames then, the Banach space X ​ ⊗ ^ π ​ Y X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y (the projective tensor product of X X and Y Y ) has a Schauder frame. From the Faber − - Schauder system we construct a Schauder frame for the Banach space C ⁡ [ 0 , 1 ] C[0,1] (the Banach space of continuous functions on the closed interval [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] ) which is not a Schauder basis of C ⁡ [ 0 , 1 ] C[0,1] . Finally, we give a positive answer to some open problems related to the Schauder bases (In the Schauder frames setting).

In the following, we give a brief history of frame theory.

In

A frame for a Hilbert space

(1)

A Riesz basis for

is a family of the form

where

A sequence

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\right\|

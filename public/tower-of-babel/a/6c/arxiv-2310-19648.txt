Joe Boninger and Joshua Evan Greene

Abstract
. We prove that a special alternating knot does not decompose as a non-trivial band sum. This restricts concordances from special alternating knots, and we conjecture that special alternating knots are ribbon concordance minimal. We verify our conjecture in many cases. This work is motivated by another conjecture of Owens and the second author, which posits that the set of alternating knots is downward closed under ribbon concordance.



1.
Introduction

A diagram
D\subset S^{2}
of a link
K\subset S^{3}
is
alternating
if a traveller along
D
alternates between passing over and under crossing points. It is
special
if Seifert’s algorithm returns one of the chessboard surfaces of
D
or, equivalently, if one of the chessboard surfaces of
D
is orientable.

A link
K
is
alternating
if it admits an alternating diagram, and it is
special alternating
if it admits a diagram which is both special and alternating.
Special alternating links and their canonical Seifert surfaces are building blocks for the set of all alternating links under the operation of Murasugi sum.
They include
(2,n)
-torus links, and they can be obtained more generally from any bipartite planar graph by inverting the Tait graph construction.

Suppose knots
K_{0},K_{1}\subset S^{3}
are separated from each other by a 2-sphere
S^{2}
, and let
b:I\times I\to S^{3}
be an embedded band such that
b^{-1}(K_{i})=I\times\{i\}
,
i\in\{0,1\}
. The
band sum

K=K_{0}\#_{b}K_{1}\subset S^{3}
is the knot
(K_{0}\cup K_{1}-b(I\times\partial I))\cup b(\partial I\times I)
. This band sum is
trivial
if
b^{-1}(S^{2})=I\times\{1/2\}
, so that
K_{0}\#_{b}K_{1}
is equivalent to the connected sum
K_{0}\#K_{1}
. A knot
K
is
band prime
if it cannot be written as a non-trivial band sum.

The main result of this note is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Special alternating knots are band prime.

Our motivation for studying band sums is their relationship to ribbon concordance, which was introduced by Gordon in an influential paper
[
9
]
. Two knots
K_{0}
,
K_{1}
are (smoothly)
concordant
if there exists a smooth, properly embedded annulus
C\subset S^{3}\times I
such that
\partial C\cap(S^{3}\times\{i\})=K_{i}\times\{i\}
. The annulus
C
is called a
ribbon concordance from
K_{1}
to
K_{0}
, and we write
K_{0}\leq K_{1}
, if the restriction
h|_{C}
of the height function
h:S^{3}\times I\to I
is Morse and has no critical points of index two. Ribbon concordance generalizes the notion of a ribbon knot, since a knot is ribbon if and only if it is ribbon concordant to the unknot.

Theorem
1.1
implies:

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose that
K_{1}
is a special alternating knot, and
K_{0}\leq K_{1}
by a ribbon concordance
C\subset S^{3}\times I
such that
h|_{C}
has two critical points. Then
K_{0}\simeq K_{1}
, and
C
is isotopic to the trivial concordance
K_{1}\times I
.

Proof.

Morse theory implies
h|_{C}
has one critical point of index
0
—a birth—and one of index
1
—a saddle. Equivalently, the knot
K_{1}
is a band sum of
K_{0}
and an unknot, and the result follows from Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Agol
[
1
]
proved ribbon concordance forms a partial ordering on the set of all knots in
S^{3}
, resolving a long open conjecture of Gordon
[
9
, Conjecture 1.1]
. This ordering has important properties: for instance, if
K_{0}\leq K_{1}
, then the Seifert genus of
K_{0}
is less than or equal to that of
K_{1}

[
27
]
; the Alexander polynomial of
K_{0}
divides that of
K_{1}

[
8
,
6
]
; and if
K_{1}
is fibered, then so is
K_{0}

[
20
,
27
]
. In this vein, the second author and Owens conjectured that if
K_{0}\leq K_{1}
and
K_{1}
is alternating, then so is
K_{0}

[
12
, Conjecture 9]
.

Motivated by this conjecture and by
1.2
, we propose:

Conjecture 1.3
.

Special alternating knots are ribbon concordance minimal.

That is, if
K_{1}
is special alternating, and
K_{0}\leq K_{1}
, then
K_{0}\simeq K_{1}
.
Note that a positive answer to
[
12
, Conjecture 9]
does not directly imply one to
1.3
.
On the other hand,
1.3
implies
1.1
, because Miyazaki proved that ribbon concordance minimal knots are band prime: for a prime knot, this is
[
19
, Theorem 3]
, and for a composite knot, this additionally requires
[
21
, Theorem 1]
.

We report on some more evidence in support of
1.3
. One piece of evidence follows quickly from work of Zemke
[
27
]
:

Proposition 1.4
.

Suppose
K
is a special alternating knot, and
K^{\prime}\leq K
. Then

\widehat{HFK}(K^{\prime})\cong\widehat{HFK}(K).

In particular, the two knots have the same genus, Alexander polynomial, and determinant.

Here
\widehat{HFK}(K)
denotes the hat flavor of the knot Floer homology of
K
with
\mathbb{F}_{2}
coefficients.

More evidence draws on the notions of
\mathbb{Q}
-anisotropy and transfinite nilpotence, which we recall in Section 3.
Gordon proved that any knot which is
\mathbb{Q}
-anisotropic and transfinitely nilpotent is ribbon concordance minimal, and he asked whether alternating knots are transfinitely nilpotent
[
9
, Theorem 1.3 and Question 5.2]
.
An elementary argument shows that a special alternating knot is
\mathbb{Q}
-anisotropic (
Lemma

3.1
below).
Thus, a positive answer to Gordon’s question would imply a positive answer to
1.3
.
Transfinite nilpotence is known in certain circumstances, which leads to the following result:

Proposition 1.5
.

Suppose
K
is a special alternating knot such that

(1)

K
is fibered,

(2)

the leading coefficient of its Alexander polynomial is a prime power, or

(3)

K
is a two-bridge knot.

Then
K
is ribbon concordance minimal.

We close the introduction with a conjecture strengthening
1.1
and
1.3
and a piece of evidence for it.
A knot
K\subset S^{3}
is
positive
if it admits an oriented diagram in which every crossing is positive.
If we orient a special alternating diagram, then every crossing has the same sign, positive or negative.
Thus, up to mirroring, every special alternating knot is a positive knot.
Nakamura proved that special alternating knots are the knots which are both alternating and positive up to mirroring
[
22
, Theorem 1]
.
Extending
1.3
, we posit:

Conjecture 1.6
.

Positive knots are ribbon concordance minimal.

Other examples of positive knots include torus knots, which Gordon showed are ribbon concordance minimal
[
9
, Remark before Theorem 1.3]
. More broadly:

Proposition 1.7

(cf.
[
4
]
)
.

Fibered positive knots are ribbon concordance minimal.

Proof.

Suppose that
K_{0}\leq K_{1}
and
K_{1}
is fibered and positive.
As noted above,
K_{0}
is fibered and
g(K_{0})\leq g(K_{1})
, where
g
denotes the Seifert genus.
Since
K_{1}
is positive, work of Rudolph implies that
g(K_{1})=g_{4}(K_{1})
, where
g_{4}
denotes the smooth
4
-genus
[
23
,
24
]
.
Hence

g(K_{1})=g_{4}(K_{1})=g_{4}(K_{0})\leq g(K_{0})\leq g(K_{1}),

implying that
g(K_{0})=g(K_{1})
.
Miyazaki observed that a genus-preserving ribbon concordance between fibered knots is trivial
[
20
, Remark before Proposition 5]
.
Hence
K_{0}\simeq K_{1}
.
∎

1.7
implies item (1) of
1.5
; below, we give an alternate proof of the latter using classical methods. Concordance of positive knots has also been studied by Baker
[
3
]
and Stoimenow
[
26
]
. In particular, a positive solution to both
[
9
, Question 6.1]
, which strengthens the slice-ribbon conjecture, and to
1.3
would answer both
[
3
, Question 7]
and
[
26
, Question 4.2]
.

Outline

2
, we prove Theorem
1.1
. In Section
3
, we prove Propositions
1.4

2.
Proof of the Main Theorem

We begin by reducing the proof of
1.1
to the case of prime special alternating knots; this is possible thanks to results of Eudave-Muñoz
[
5
]
and Menasco
[
17
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If a composite knot is not band prime, then one of its factors is not band prime.

Proof.

Suppose a composite knot
K
can be written as a non-trivial band sum
K_{0}\#_{b}K_{1}
. Eudave-Muñoz showed that there exists a decomposing sphere
F\subset S^{3}
for
K
(as a composite knot) which is disjoint from the band
b

[
5
, Theorem 2]
.
This implies

2=|F\cap K|=|F\cap(K_{0}\sqcup K_{1})|,

and therefore
F
is a (possibly trivial) decomposing sphere for either
K_{0}
or
K_{1}
. We assume the latter without loss of generality, and let
K_{1}=K_{A}\#K_{B}
be the decomposition of
K_{1}
determined by
F
. Since
F
is disjoint from
b
,
b
is contained on one side of
F
, which we assume is the side containing
K_{A}
. Equivalently, we have

K=(K_{0}\#_{b}K_{A})\#K_{B}.

If the band sum
K_{0}\#_{b}K_{A}
is trivial, then

K=(K_{0}\#K_{A})\#K_{B}=K_{0}\#(K_{A}\#K_{B})=K_{0}\#K_{1}.

By an observation of Miyazaki following Eudave-Muñoz’s work, this implies the band sum
K_{0}\#_{b}K_{1}
is trivial
[
21
, Theorem 1]
. This contradiction shows the band sum
K_{0}\#_{b}K_{A}
is nontrivial, so
K
admits a factor which is not band prime.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

If a special alternating knot
K
decomposes as a connected sum
K_{0}\#K_{1}
, then each of
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
are special alternating.

Proof.

Let
D
be an alternating diagram of
K
. A theorem of Menasco implies that
K_{i}
has an alternating diagram
D_{i}
for
i=0,1
such that
D=D_{0}\#D_{1}

[
17
, Theorem 1]
. It is straightforward to check that each of the
D_{i}
is special if
D
is.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

If there exists a special alternating knot which is not band prime, then there exists a prime special alternating knot which is not band prime. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
K\subset S^{3}
be a special alternating knot, and suppose toward a contradiction that
K
decomposes as a non-trivial band sum
K_{0}\#_{b}K_{1}
. By
2.3
, we may assume
K
is prime.

Gabai showed that there exists a minimal genus Seifert surface
S
for
K
and a properly embedded arc
\alpha\subset S
, coinciding with a co-core of
b
, such that
S^{\prime}=S-\eta(\alpha)
is a Seifert surface for the split link
K_{0}\sqcup K_{1}

[
7
, Proof of Theorem 1]
. Here
\eta
denotes a regular open neighborhood.

Hirasawa-Sakuma proved any minimal genus surface of a special alternating knot is a chessboard surface of an alternating diagram of that knot
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Accordingly, we choose an alternating diagram
D\subset S^{2}
of
K
admitting
S
as a chessboard surface.
Let
G\subset S^{2}
be the corresponding Tait graph, and let
\lambda_{S}
denote the symmetrized Seifert pairing on
H_{1}(S)
.
Since
S
is a chessboard surface and
D
is alternating, Gordon and Litherland proved in this case that
(H_{1}(S),\lambda_{S})
is isometric, up to an overall sign, to the lattice
\mathcal{F}(G)
of integer-valued flows on
G

[
10
, Theorem 1]
.
In particular,
\lambda_{S}
is definite.

A Mayer-Vietoris argument shows the inclusion-induced map
H_{1}(S^{\prime})\to H_{1}(S)
injects, so it follows that
\lambda_{S^{\prime}}
is definite as well.
Hence both
S
and
S^{\prime}
are incompressible, by
[
11
, Corollary 3.2]
.

Let
F\subset S^{3}
be a 2-sphere separating
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
, chosen so
F
and
S^{\prime}
are in general position and
F\cap S^{\prime}
has the minimal number of components. A standard innermost curve argument, using the incompressibility of
S^{\prime}
, shows
F\cap S^{\prime}=\varnothing
.
Thus
S^{\prime}
has two components which are separated by the sphere
F
; we denote these by
S_{0}
and
S_{1}
, so that
\partial S_{i}=K_{i}
. Viewing
S
as
S_{0}\cup_{\alpha}S_{1}
, we conclude that the inclusion map
S_{0}\sqcup S_{1}\hookrightarrow S
induces a lattice isomorphism
(H_{1}(S_{0}),\lambda_{S_{0}})\oplus(H_{1}(S_{1}),\lambda_{S_{1}})\cong(H_{1}(S),\lambda_{S})
.

We claim each of
H_{1}(S_{0})
and
H_{1}(S_{1})
are non-zero. Suppose not; then
H_{1}(S_{1})
, say, has rank
0
, so
S_{1}
is a disk. In this case the boundary of a regular neighborhood of
S_{1}
in
S^{3}-K_{0}
is a sphere which separates
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
and meets
S
in a single arc parallel to
\alpha
, implying
b
is trivial. This contradiction proves the claim, and it follows that
(H_{1}(S),\lambda_{S})
admits a non-trivial decomposition as an orthogonal direct sum.

We have thereby shown that
\mathcal{F}(G)\cong(H_{1}(S),\pm\lambda_{S})
admits a non-trivial decomposition as an orthogonal direct sum.
By a result of Bacher, de la Harpe, and Nagnibeda, it follows that
G
is
separable
: there exist subgraphs
G_{0},G_{1}\subset G
with
\mathcal{F}(G_{i})\cong(H_{1}(S_{i}),\lambda_{S_{i}})
, such that
G_{0}\cup G_{1}=G
and
G_{0}\cap G_{1}
consists of a single vertex
[
2
, Proposition 4]
.
Equivalently,
D
admits a connected sum decomposition
D=D_{0}\#D_{1}
with
G_{i}
a Tait graph of
D_{i}
. Let
K_{i}\subset S^{3}
be the knot represented by
D_{i}
,
i=0,1
.
Each form
\lambda_{S_{i}}
is non-zero and definite, so the signature satisfies
\sigma(K_{i})=\sigma(\lambda_{S_{i}})\neq 0
, implying each
K_{i}
is non-trivial.
We conclude that
K
is not prime, a contradiction which proves the theorem.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

The proof of
1.1
shows, in fact, that all non-split special alternating links are band prime. While the cited results of Eudave-Muñoz
[
5
]
, Gabai
[
7
]
, and Miyazaki
[
21
]
are stated only for knots, the remark in
[
5
]
following the proof of Theorem 2 can be used in place of
[
5
, Theorem 2]
, and
[
25
, Proposition 8.9]
can be used in place of Gabai’s work. Similarly, the proof of Miyazaki’s result extends to non-split links.
On the other hand, it would require more effort to extend the result to split special alternating links: one would like to know that the split union of band prime links is again band prime.
We decided not to pursue this issue, besides to sense that it is non-trivial.

Remark 2.5
.

We had originally attempted to prove
[
12
, Conjecture 9]
using the characterization of alternating knots in terms of definite spanning surfaces
[
11
]
.
However, this approach proved more challenging to implement than expected.
The use of a definite chessboard surface in the proof of
1.1
is the surviving remnant of this idea.

3.
Ribbon Concordance

In light of Corollary
1.2
, Theorem
1.1
provides evidence that special alternating knots are ribbon concordance minimal. In this section we prove Propositions
1.4
and
1.5
, which provide more support for this conjecture.

Proof of
1.4
.

The relation
K^{\prime}\leq K
implies
\sigma(K^{\prime})=\sigma(K)
.
Zemke showed that for any ribbon concordance from
K
to
K^{\prime}
, the induced map
\widehat{HFK}(K^{\prime})\to\widehat{HFK}(K)
is injective
[
27
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Since knot Floer homology detects genus, it follows that
g(K^{\prime})\leq g(K)

[
27
, Theorem 1.3]
.
As in the proof of
1.1
, since
K
is special alternating, it satisfies
|\sigma(K)|=2g(K)
. Thus

|\sigma(K)|=|\sigma(K^{\prime})|\leq 2g(K^{\prime})\leq 2g(K)=|\sigma(K)|,

and
g(K)=g(K^{\prime})
. Additionally, because
K
is alternating,
K
—and therefore
K^{\prime}
—are homologically thin.
Letting
\Delta
denote the Alexander polynomial, we obtain

\text{span}(\Delta_{K^{\prime}})=2g(K^{\prime})=2g(K)=\text{span}(\Delta_{K}).

Next, we use the fact that
K^{\prime}\leq K
implies
\Delta_{K^{\prime}}
divides
\Delta_{K}

[
8
,
6
]
. Since
\text{span}(\Delta_{K^{\prime}})=\text{span}(\Delta_{K})
, we have
m\Delta_{K^{\prime}}=\Delta_{K}
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\Delta_{K}(1)=\Delta_{K^{\prime}}(1)=1
, we have
m=1
and
\Delta_{K^{\prime}}=\Delta_{K}
. Finally, because
K^{\prime}
and
K
are homologically thin and supported in the same
\delta
-grading, their knot Floer homology groups are determined by their Alexander polynomials. Thus,

\widehat{HFK}(K^{\prime})\cong\widehat{HFK}(K).\qed

Before proving
1.5
, we review some terminology.
Given a knot
K
, we let
C
denote the commutator subgroup of the knot group
\pi_{1}(S^{3}-K)
. The lower central series
\{\gamma_{i}(C)\}_{i\geq 1}
of
C
is defined recursively by

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(C)

\displaystyle=C,

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}(C)

\displaystyle=[G,\gamma_{n-1}(G)],

and for a limit ordinal
\omega
we define
\gamma_{\omega}(G):=\bigcap_{\alpha<\omega}\gamma_{\alpha}(G)
. We say
C
is
transfinitely nilpotent
if its lower central series is eventually trivial, and we say
K
is transfinitely nilpotent if
C
is. Free groups give the simplest examples of groups which are transfinitely nilpotent but not nilpotent
[
15
, Chapter 5]
.

If
X
denotes the universal abelian cover of
K
, then Milnor defined a quadratic form
\mu
on
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Q})
with rank rk
(\mu)=\text{span}(\Delta_{K})
and signature
\sigma(\mu)=\sigma(K)

[
18
]
.
Following Gordon, we say
K
is
\mathbb{Q}
-anisotropic
if
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Q})
contains no non-trivial subspace which is annihilated by
\mu
and invariant under deck transformations
[
9
, Proposition 4.5]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Special alternating knots are
\mathbb{Q}
-anisotropic.

Proof.

If
K
is a special alternating knot, then we have

|\sigma(\mu)|=|\sigma(K)|=\text{span}(\Delta_{K})=\text{rk}(\mu),

so
\mu
is a definite form. It follows that there is no non-trivial subspace of
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Q})
annhilated by
\mu
, let alone one which is invariant under deck transformations.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

If
K
is a special alternating knot and
K
is transfinitely nilpotent, then
K
is ribbon concordance minimal.

Proof.

Gordon proved that any knot which is
\mathbb{Q}
-anisotropic and transfinitely nilpotent is ribbon concordance minimal
[
9
, Theorem 1.3]
.
Hence the result follows from
Lemma

3.1
.

Alternatively, Gordon proved that if
K^{\prime}\leq K
,
K
is transfinitely nilpotent, and span
(\Delta_{K})=\text{span}(\Delta_{K^{\prime}})
, then
K^{\prime}\simeq K

[
9
, Lemma 3.4]
.
Hence the result follows from
1.4
.
∎

Proof of
1.5
.

If
K
satisfies any of the listed hypotheses, then
K
is transfinitely nilpotent:

(1)

follows because commutator subgroups of fibered knots are free,

(2)

is a classical result of Mayland and Murasugi
[
16
, Theorem A]
, and

(3)

was proven more recently by Johnson
[
14
, Corollary 1.3 and Remark 1.20]
.

Hence the result follows from
3.2
.
∎

Variational formulation of the Melan equation
Filippo GAZZOLA – Yongda WANG – Raffaella PAVANI

Abstract
The Melan beam equation modeling suspension bridges is considered. A slightly modified equation is derived by applying variational principles and by minimising the total energy of the bridge. The equation is nonlinear and nonlocal, while the beam is hinged at the endpoints. We show that the problem always admits at least one solution whereas the uniqueness remains open although some numerical results suggest that it should hold. We also emphasize the qualitative difference with some simplified models. Keywords: Melan equation, suspension bridges, nonlinear nonlocal terms. Mathematics Subject Classification: 34B15, 74B20.



1
Introduction

At the end of the 19th century, Josef Melan
[
8
]
suggested the following fourth order ordinary differential equation
to describe the behavior of suspension bridges:

EIw^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(x)-(H+h(w))w^{\prime\prime}(x)-h(w)y^{\prime\prime}(x)=p\qquad\forall x\in(0,L),

(1)

where
L
is the distance between the two towers,
w=w(x)
denotes the vertical displacement of the beam representing the deck,
y=y(x)
is the position
of the sustaining cable at rest,
E
and
I
are, respectively, the elastic modulus of the material composing the deck and the moment of inertia of the
cross section so that
EI
is the flexural rigidity,
H
is the horizontal tension of the cable when subject to the dead load
q=q(x)
,
h(w)
represents the
additional tension in the cable produced by the live load
p=p(x)
. The dead load
q
includes the weights of the cable, of the hangers, and of the deck.
In the book by von Kármán-Biot
[
7
, (5.5)]
, (
1
) is called the
fundamental equation of the theory of the suspension bridge
. In Figure
1
we sketch a picture of a suspension bridge.

From a mathematical point of view, the additional tension
h(w)
in (
1
) deserves a particular attention since it is nonlocal and it introduces
a nonlinearity into the equation. As we shall see, the computation of
h(w)
is delicate and, in literature, there are several different ways to approximate
it, see
[
4
,
7
,
12
]
. For both the original term
h(w)
and these approximate forms of it, one can show that there exists at least one solution
of the Melan equation (
1
) with hinged boundary conditions, see
[
4
, Section 5]
.
The Melan equation (
1
) is also quite challenging for numerical analysts, see

[
2
,
5
,
6
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
13
]
where several approximating procedures for the solution of (
1
) have been discussed for different forms of the term
h(w)
.

The purpose of the present paper is to derive the Melan equation from a variational principle and to study its behavior. We prove that the Euler-Lagrange
equation, see (
8
), admits at least one solution and we prove uniqueness for certain values of the parameters. We also discuss uniqueness
for the remaining values of the parameters and we give some numerical results which show how delicate and unstable the equation is. Finally,
we emphasize the role of the nonlinearity and a qualitative difference between the solution of the variational problem with the solution of a simplified problem.

2
How to derive the Melan equation from a variational principle

Following von Kármán-Biot
[
7
, Section VII.5]
, we view the main span of a suspension bridge as a combined system of a perfectly flexible string (the
sustaining cable) and a beam (the deck). The beam and the string are connected by a large number of inextensible hangers, see Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
Beam sustained by a cable through parallel hangers.

The point
O
is the origin of the orthogonal coordinate system and positive displacements are oriented downwards. The point
M
has coordinates

(0,L)
with
L
being the length of the deck between the two towers.

If no live loads act on the beam, there is no bending moment in the beam and the cable is in the position
y(x)
, while the unloaded beam is in the horizontal
position of the segment connecting
O
and
M
. In this situation, the horizontal component
H>0
of the tension of the cable is constant. Hence, there is an
equilibrium position in the system and the configuration of the cable is obtained by solving the equation (see
[
7
, (1.3), Section VII]
)

Hy^{\prime\prime}(x)=-q\qquad\forall x\in(0,L).

(2)

Since the endpoints of the cable are at the same level
\ell
(the height of the towers) and since the dead load
q
is constant, the solution of (
2
) is

y(x)=\ell+\frac{q}{2H}x(L-x)\qquad\forall x\in(0,L):

hence, the cable has the shape of a parabola and

y^{\prime}(x)=\frac{q}{H}\left(\frac{L}{2}-x\right),\quad y^{\prime\prime}(x)=-\frac{q}{H}\qquad\forall x\in(0,L).

(3)

Therefore, the length of the cable at rest is

L_{c}=\int_{0}^{L}{\sqrt{1+y^{\prime}(x)^{2}}}dx=\frac{L}{2}\sqrt{1+\frac{q^{2}L^{2}}{4H^{2}}}+\frac{H}{q}\log\left(\frac{qL}{2H}+\sqrt{1+\frac{q^{2}L^{2}}{4H^{2}}}\right).

(4)

When a live load
p
acts on the deck of the bridge, the beam may leave the horizontal position and produce a displacement
w

(positive displacements are oriented downwards). By assuming that
w
,
w^{\prime}
,
w^{\prime\prime}
are small, let us compute the energies involved when the
system is in this new position.

The potential energy produced by the live and dead loads reads

\mathcal{E}_{L}=\int_{0}^{L}{(p+q)w}dx.

The elastic energy
\mathcal{E}_{B}
necessary to bend the beam is the squared curvature times half the flexural rigidity, that is,

\mathcal{E}_{B}=\frac{EI}{2}\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(w^{\prime\prime})^{2}}{(1+(w^{\prime})^{2})^{3}}\sqrt{1+(w^{\prime})^{2}}}dx=\frac{EI}{2}\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(w^{\prime\prime})^{2}}{(1+(w^{\prime})^{2})^{5/2}}}dx\approx\frac{EI}{2}\int_{0}^{L}{(w^{\prime\prime})^{2}}dx,

where we used the fact that
w^{\prime}
and
w^{\prime\prime}
are both small and we neglect the terms
o(w^{\prime})^{2}
of superquadratic order.

Since the cable is assumed to be perfectly flexible, it has no resistance to bending. Then the only internal force is its tension which consists of two
parts: the tension at rest
H(x)
and the additional tension
h(w)
due to the variation of length of the cable. The former is

H(x)=H\sqrt{1+y^{\prime}(x)^{2}}
and the amount of energy needed to deform the cable at rest under the tension
H(x)
in the infinitesimal interval
[x,x+dx]

from the original position
y(x)
to the new position
y(x)+w(x)
is the variation of length times the tension, that is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{C_{1}}dx=H\sqrt{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}\left(\sqrt{1+(w^{\prime}+y^{\prime})^{2}}-\sqrt{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}\right)dx.

Then, the energy necessary to deform the whole cable at rest under the tension
H(x)
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{C_{1}}

\displaystyle=H\int_{0}^{L}{\sqrt{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}\left(\sqrt{1+(w^{\prime}+y^{\prime})^{2}}-\sqrt{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}\right)}dx

\displaystyle=H\int_{0}^{L}{\left[1+(y^{\prime})^{2}\right]\left(\sqrt{1+\frac{2w^{\prime}y^{\prime}}{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}+\frac{(w^{\prime})^{2}}{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}}-1\right)}dx.

In order to maintain only the at most quadratic terms, we use the asymptotic expansion
\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}\approx 1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
; with an integration by parts we then get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{C_{1}}

\displaystyle\approx H\int_{0}^{L}{\left[1+(y^{\prime})^{2}\right]\left(\frac{w^{\prime}y^{\prime}}{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}+\frac{(w^{\prime})^{2}}{2(1+(y^{\prime})^{2})^{2}}\right)}dx=\frac{H}{2}\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(w^{\prime})^{2}}{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}}dx+H\int_{0}^{L}{w^{\prime}y^{\prime}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{H}{2}\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(w^{\prime})^{2}}{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}}dx+q\int_{0}^{L}{w}dx.

(5)

For the additional tension
h(w)
, we note that the hangers connecting the cable and the beam are inextensible. Therefore, the deflection of the cable follows the displacement
w
of the deck, that is, the cable reaches the new position
y+w
and the variation of the cable length due to the deformation
w
is given by (see (
3
)-(
4
))

\Gamma(w)=\int_{0}^{L}{\sqrt{1+\left(w^{\prime}(x)+y^{\prime}(x)\right)^{2}}}dx-L_{c}.

(6)

If
A
denotes the cross-sectional area of the cable and
E_{c}
is the modulus of elasticity, then the additional tension
h(w)
in the cable produced by the live load
p
and the corresponding energy
\mathcal{E}_{C_{2}}(w)
are given by

h(w)=\frac{E_{c}A}{L_{c}}\Gamma(w),\qquad\mathcal{E}_{C_{2}}(w)=\frac{E_{c}A}{2L_{c}}\Gamma(w)^{2}.

(7)

Therefore, the total energy necessary to deform the cable is

\mathcal{E}_{C}=\mathcal{E}_{C_{1}}+\mathcal{E}_{C_{2}}=\frac{H}{2}\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(w^{\prime})^{2}}{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}}dx+q\int_{0}^{L}{w}dx+\frac{E_{c}A}{2L_{c}}\Gamma(w)^{2}.

Remark 1
.

When computing the energy
\mathcal{E}_{C_{1}}
, Timoshenko-Young obtain

\mathcal{E}_{C_{1}}=\frac{H}{2}\int_{0}^{L}{(w^{\prime})^{2}}dx+q\int_{0}^{L}{w}dx,

see
[
12
, Section 11.16]
. This formula should be compared with (
2
): it is obtained by approximating
y^{\prime}\approx 0
. As explained in
[
4
]
this may generate some significant errors in the solutions. It was the civil and structural German engineer Franz Dischinger who discovered around 1950 the dramatic consequences of
this approximation on the structures.

Summarizing, the total energy in the system after the deformation
w
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{B}+\mathcal{E}_{C}-\mathcal{E}_{L}=\frac{EI}{2}\int_{0}^{L}{(w^{\prime\prime})^{2}}dx+\frac{H}{2}\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(w^{\prime})^{2}}{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}}dx+\frac{E_{c}A}{2L_{c}}\Gamma(w)^{2}-\int_{0}^{L}{pw}dx.

The Euler-Lagrange equation of the system is obtained by taking the critical points of the energy
\mathcal{E}
. Then by recalling (
3
) and that the beam is hinged at its endpoints, we obtain the following boundary value problem

\begin{cases}EIw^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(x)-H\left(\frac{w^{\prime}(x)}{1+(y^{\prime}(x))^{2}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{E_{c}A}{L_{c}}\frac{w^{\prime\prime}(x)-q/H}{(1+(w^{\prime}(x)+y^{\prime}(x))^{2})^{3/2}}\Gamma(w)=p\quad&x\in(0,L)\\
w(0)=w(L)=w^{\prime\prime}(0)=w^{\prime\prime}(L)=0.\quad&\end{cases}

(8)

One should compare (
8
) with the classical Melan equation (
1
). For the history and the details on the derivation of the Melan equation we also refer to the recent monograph
[
3
]
.

3
Main result

For simplicity, we put
a=EI
,
b=H
, and
c=\frac{E_{c}A}{L_{c}}
. Then the problem (
8
) reads

\begin{cases}aw^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(x)-b\left(\frac{w^{\prime}(x)}{1+(y^{\prime}(x))^{2}}\right)^{\prime}-c\frac{w^{\prime\prime}(x)-q/H}{(1+(w^{\prime}(x)+y^{\prime}(x))^{2})^{3/2}}\Gamma(w)=p\quad&x\in(0,L)\\
w(0)=w(L)=w^{\prime\prime}(0)=w^{\prime\prime}(L)=0,\quad&\end{cases}

(9)

where
a,b,c>0
and the functional
\Gamma(w)
is as in (
6
), it is nonlinear nonlocal and of indefinite sign. Define
\alpha,\beta>0
by

\alpha^{2}:=\left[1+\frac{q^{2}L^{2}}{12H^{2}}\right]\frac{L}{H},\qquad\beta^{2}:=\left[1+\frac{q^{2}L^{2}}{4H^{2}}\right]\frac{1}{H}.

(10)

Given
k\in[1,\infty]
, we denote the
L^{k}
-norm by
\|u\|_{k}
for any
u\in L^{k}(0,L)
.
We also introduce the following scalar product on the second order Sobolev space
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
:

(u,v)_{y}:=a\int_{0}^{L}{u^{\prime\prime}v^{\prime\prime}}dx+b\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{u^{\prime}v^{\prime}}{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}}dx\quad\mbox{ for any }u,v\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L),

(11)

where the function
y^{\prime}
is as in (
3
). Let
\mathcal{H}
be the dual space of
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
; we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
the
\mathcal{H}
-norm and by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the corresponding duality between
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
and
\mathcal{H}
.

If
p\in\mathcal{H}
, we say that
w\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
is a weak solution of (
9
) if

(w,v)_{y}+c\Gamma(w)\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(w^{\prime}+y^{\prime})v^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1+(w^{\prime}+y^{\prime})^{2}}}}dx=\langle p,v\rangle\qquad\mbox{for all }v\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L).

(12)

Then we prove

Theorem 2
.

For any
p\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists at least one weak solution of the problem (
9
). Moreover, assume that

0<c<\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}.

(13)

Then for all
p\in\mathcal{H}
satisfying

\|p\|_{\mathcal{H}}<\frac{(1-c\alpha^{2})^{2}}{c\alpha\beta^{2}},

(14)

the problem (
9
) admits a unique weak solution
w\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
.

The uniqueness statement holds if both
c>0
and
\|p\|_{\mathcal{H}}
are sufficiently small. However, the assumption
c<\alpha^{-2}
does not hold in
general for actual bridges, see
[
13
]
. Therefore, we now discuss the case
c\geq\alpha^{-2}
.

First, we study what happens in the “limit case” where
c\to+\infty
: the problem (
9
) degenerates to

\frac{w^{\prime\prime}-q/H}{(1+(w^{\prime}+y^{\prime})^{2})^{3/2}}\Gamma(w)=0\quad x\in(0,L)\ ,\qquad w(0)=w(L)=0.

(15)

Clearly,
w=0
and
w=-\frac{q}{2H}(L-x)x
are two solutions of (
15
). But let us also analyze the functional
\Gamma(w)
. We shift it by

Y(x):=\frac{q}{2H}x(L-x)
(so that
Y\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
and
Y^{\prime}=y^{\prime}
) and,
for all
0\not\equiv w\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
, we define the real function

\gamma_{w}(t):=\Gamma(tw-Y)=\int_{0}^{L}{\left[\sqrt{1+(tw^{\prime})^{2}}-\sqrt{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}\right]}dx\qquad\mbox{ for any }t\in\mathbb{R}.

Clearly,
\gamma_{w}(\pm\infty)=+\infty
and
\gamma_{w}
is strictly convex in
\mathbb{R}
. Since
\gamma_{w}(0)<0
, there exist
T^{-}_{w}<0<T^{+}_{w}

such that
\gamma_{w}(T^{\pm}_{w})=0
. Hence, for any
w\neq 0
we have
\Gamma(T^{\pm}_{w}w\!-\!Y)=0
, that is,
T^{\pm}_{w}w\!-\!Y
solves (
15
);
therefore, (
15
) admits infinitely many solutions.
The qualitative graph of the functional
\frac{c}{2}\Gamma^{2}
is depicted in Figure
2
; since
J_{p}-\frac{c}{2}\Gamma^{2}

is convex (see again Figure
2
), if
c
is large then the behavior of the functional
J_{p}
is not clear; in this situation, the uniqueness and/or
multiplicity for (
9
) is an open problem.

Figure 2:
Qualitative shape of the graphs of the functionals
w\mapsto\frac{c}{2}\Gamma(w)^{2}
and
w\mapsto J_{p}(w)-\frac{c}{2}\Gamma(w)^{2}
.

Second, we point out that the numerical results reported in the next section suggest that uniqueness holds also for
c\geq\alpha^{-2}
.

Numerical results

5
Proof of Theorem
2

Since
y^{\prime}
is bounded and
a,b>0
, the scalar product (
11
) induces a norm on
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
denoted by

\|u\|_{y}:=\left(a\int_{0}^{L}{(u^{\prime\prime})^{2}}dx+b\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(u^{\prime})^{2}}{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}}dx\right)^{1/2}\quad\mbox{ for any }u\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L),

which is equivalent to
\|u^{\prime\prime}\|_{2}
, that is, the standard
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}
-norm. For all
u\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|u^{\prime}\|_{1}^{2}&\leq&\int_{0}^{L}{(1+(y^{\prime})^{2})}dx\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(u^{\prime})^{2}}{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}}dx=\left[1+\frac{q^{2}L^{2}}{12H^{2}}\right]L\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(u^{\prime})^{2}}{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}}dx\leq\alpha^{2}\|u\|_{y}^{2}\\
\|u^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}&\leq&\|1+(y^{\prime})^{2}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(u^{\prime})^{2}}{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}}dx=\left[1+\frac{q^{2}L^{2}}{4H^{2}}\right]\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(u^{\prime})^{2}}{1+(y^{\prime})^{2}}}dx\leq\beta^{2}\|u\|_{y}^{2}\end{array}

with
\alpha,\beta>0
as in (
10
). In addition, the simple inequality

\left|\sqrt{1+(\lambda+\mu)^{2}}-\sqrt{1+\mu^{2}}\right|\leq|\lambda|\qquad\forall\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}

(19)

implies that for any
u,v\in H_{0}^{1}\cap H^{2}(0,L)

|\Gamma(u)|\leq\|u^{\prime}\|_{1},\qquad|\Gamma(u)-\Gamma(v)|\leq\|u^{\prime}-v^{\prime}\|_{1}.

(20)

We first state the continuity and differentiability of the functional
\Gamma(w)^{2}
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\Gamma(w)
be as in (
6
). Then
\Gamma(w)^{2}
is weakly continuous and differentiable in
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
.

The proof of Lemma
3
is standard and we omit it. The energy functional corresponding to (
9
) is

J_{p}=J_{p}(w)=\frac{1}{2}\|w\|_{y}^{2}+\frac{c}{2}\Gamma(w)^{2}-\langle p,w\rangle\qquad\mbox{ for any }w\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L).

According to Lemma
3
, weak solutions of (
9
) are the critical points of the functional
J_{p}
. The next step is to prove the geometrical properties (coercivity) and compactness properties (Palais-Smale (PS) condition) of
J_{p}
.

Lemma 4
.

For any
p\in\mathcal{H}
, the functional
J_{p}
is coercive and bounded below in
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
. Moreover, it satisfies the (PS) condition.

Proof.
Since
p\in\mathcal{H}
and
c>0
, we have for any
w\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)

J_{p}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|w\|_{y}^{2}-\langle p,w\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|w\|_{y}^{2}-\|p\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|w\|_{y}\geq-\frac{\|p\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}}{2},

which implies that the functional
J_{p}
is coercive and bounded below.

Consider now a sequence
\{w_{n}\}
such that
J_{p}(w_{n})
is bounded and
J^{\prime}_{p}(w_{n})\to 0
in
\mathcal{H}
. Then

\displaystyle\exists M>0,\qquad M\geq\frac{1}{2}\|w_{n}\|_{y}^{2}+\frac{c}{2}\Gamma(w_{n})^{2}-\langle p,w_{n}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|w_{n}\|_{y}^{2}-\|p\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|w_{n}\|_{y}.

Hence,
\|w_{n}\|_{y}
is bounded and there exists some
\overline{w}\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
such that
w_{n}
weakly converges to
\overline{w}
in
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
, up to a subsequence. Therefore, one has
\langle J^{\prime}_{p}(w_{n}),v\rangle\to\langle J^{\prime}_{p}(\overline{w}),v\rangle
for all
v\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
, which proves that
J^{\prime}_{p}(\overline{w})=0
. It follows that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\langle J^{\prime}_{p}(w_{n}),w_{n}\rangle&=&\|w_{n}\|_{y}^{2}+c\Gamma(w_{n})\langle\Gamma^{\prime}(w_{n}),w_{n}\rangle-\langle p,w_{n}\rangle\\
&\to&0=\langle J^{\prime}_{p}(\overline{w}),\overline{w}\rangle=\|\overline{w}\|_{y}^{2}+c\Gamma(\overline{w})\langle\Gamma^{\prime}(\overline{w}),\overline{w}\rangle-\langle p,\overline{w}\rangle.\end{array}

By (
20
) and compact embedding we know that
\Gamma(w_{n})\langle\Gamma^{\prime}(w_{n}),w_{n}\rangle\to\Gamma(\overline{w})\langle\Gamma^{\prime}(\overline{w}),\overline{w}\rangle
. Since
\langle p,w_{n}\rangle\to\langle p,\overline{w}\rangle
, by (
5
) we deduce that
\|w_{n}\|_{y}\to\|\overline{w}\|_{y}
. Together with the weak convergence
w_{n}\rightharpoonup\overline{w}
, this shows that

w_{n}\to\overline{w}
in
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
.
This proves (PS) condition.

\Box

By Lemma
4
, the functional
J_{p}
admits a global minimum in
H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
for any
p\in\mathcal{H}
. This
minimum point is a critical point for
J_{p}
and hence a weak solution of (
9
). This proves the first
part of Theorem
2
.

We now discuss uniqueness. We first remark that if
w
is a weak solution of (
9
), then by (
12
) we have

\|w\|_{y}^{2}+c\Gamma(w)\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(w^{\prime}+y^{\prime})w^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1+(w^{\prime}+y^{\prime})^{2}}}}dx=\langle p,w\rangle\leq\|p\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|w\|_{y}.

(23)

By (
5
) and (
20
) we deduce that

\left|\Gamma(w)\right|\left|\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(w^{\prime}+y^{\prime})w^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1+(w^{\prime}+y^{\prime})^{2}}}}dx\right|\leq\|w^{\prime}\|_{1}^{2}\leq\alpha^{2}\|w\|_{y}^{2}

so that, assuming (
13
), from (
23
) we infer the following a priori bound for solutions of (
9
):

\|w\|_{y}\leq(1-c\alpha^{2})^{-1}\|p\|_{\mathcal{H}}:=R_{p}.

(24)

Next we fix
v\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
and consider the linear problem

\begin{cases}aw^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(x)-b\left(\frac{w^{\prime}(x)}{1+(y^{\prime}(x))^{2}}\right)^{\prime}=c\frac{v^{\prime\prime}(x)-q/H}{(1+(v^{\prime}(x)+y^{\prime}(x))^{2})^{3/2}}\Gamma(v)+p\quad&x\in(0,L)\\
w(0)=w(L)=w^{\prime\prime}(0)=w^{\prime\prime}(L)=0,\quad&\end{cases}

(25)

where
0<c<\alpha^{-2}
. Since
\frac{v^{\prime\prime}(x)-q/H}{(1+(v^{\prime}(x)+y^{\prime}(x))^{2})^{3/2}}\Gamma(v)\in\mathcal{H}
and
p\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists a unique solution
w\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)
of (
25
) due to the Lax-Milgram theorem. We define the closed ball
B_{p}
and the map
\Phi
by

B_{p}:=\{w\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L);\|w\|_{y}\leq R_{p}\},\qquad\Phi:B_{p}\to H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L);\quad\Phi(v)=w,

with
w
being the unique solution of (
25
).

Lemma 5
.

If (
13
) and (
14
) hold, then the map
\Phi
satisfies
\Phi(B_{p})\subseteq B_{p}
and it is contractive in
B_{p}
.

Proof.
For any fixed
v\in B_{p}
, by testing (
25
) with its solution
w=\Phi(v)
, we get

\displaystyle\|w\|_{y}^{2}

\displaystyle=c\Gamma(v)\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(v^{\prime\prime}-q/H)w}{(1+(v^{\prime}+y^{\prime})^{2})^{3/2}}}dx+\langle p,w\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-c\Gamma(v)\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(v^{\prime}+y^{\prime})w^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1+(v^{\prime}+y^{\prime})^{2}}}}dx+\|p\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|w\|_{y}

by (
5
)-(
20
)

\displaystyle\leq c\|v^{\prime}\|_{1}\|w^{\prime}\|_{1}+\|p\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|w\|_{y}\leq\left(c\alpha^{2}\|v\|_{y}+\|p\|_{\mathcal{H}}\right)\|w\|_{y}

\displaystyle\leq\left(c\alpha^{2}R_{p}+\|p\|_{\mathcal{H}}\right)\|w\|_{y}=R_{p}\|w\|_{y}.

Hence,
\|w\|_{y}\leq R
which shows that
\Phi(B_{p})\subseteq B_{p}
.

Note that the function
s\mapsto s/\sqrt{1+s^{2}}
is globally Lipschitzian with constant 1, that is,

\left|\frac{s_{1}}{\sqrt{1+s_{1}^{2}}}-\frac{s_{2}}{\sqrt{1+s_{2}^{2}}}\right|\leq|s_{1}-s_{2}|\qquad\forall s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

(26)

Take
v_{1},v_{2}\in B_{p}
and let
w_{1}=\Phi(v_{1})
,
w_{2}=\Phi(v_{2})
, then we have for all
u\in H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,L)

(w_{i},u)_{y}=c\Gamma(v_{i})\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(v^{\prime\prime}_{i}-q/H)u}{(1+(v^{\prime}_{i}+y^{\prime})^{2})^{3/2}}}dx+\langle p,u\rangle\qquad i=1,2.

Put
u=w=w_{1}-w_{2}
, subtract these two equations and recall (
3
). Then, after integration by parts we get

\displaystyle\|w\|_{y}^{2}=

\displaystyle c\Gamma(v_{2})\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(v^{\prime}_{2}+y^{\prime})w^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1+(v^{\prime}_{2}+y^{\prime})^{2}}}}dx-c\Gamma(v_{1})\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(v^{\prime}_{1}+y^{\prime})w^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1+(v^{\prime}_{1}+y^{\prime})^{2}}}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\Gamma(v_{2})\left[\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(v^{\prime}_{2}+y^{\prime})w^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1+(v^{\prime}_{2}+y^{\prime})^{2}}}}dx-\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(v^{\prime}_{1}+y^{\prime})w^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1+(v^{\prime}_{1}+y^{\prime})^{2}}}}dx\right]

\displaystyle+c\left[\Gamma(v_{2})-\Gamma(v_{1})\right]\int_{0}^{L}{\frac{(v^{\prime}_{1}+y^{\prime})w^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1+(v^{\prime}_{1}+y^{\prime})^{2}}}}dx

by (
20
)-(
26
)

\displaystyle\leq c\|v^{\prime}_{2}\|_{1}\int_{0}^{L}{|v^{\prime}_{1}-v^{\prime}_{2}||w^{\prime}|}dx+c\|v^{\prime}_{1}-v^{\prime}_{2}\|_{1}\|w^{\prime}\|_{1}

by (
5
)-(
19
)

\displaystyle\leq c\alpha\beta^{2}R_{p}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{y}\|w\|_{y}+c\alpha^{2}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{y}\|w\|_{y}.

Hence, by the definition of
B_{p}
in (
24
), we infer that

\|\Phi(v_{1})-\Phi(v_{2})\|_{y}=\|w\|_{y}\leq c\alpha\left(\alpha+\beta^{2}R_{p}\right)\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{y}=c\alpha\left(\alpha+\frac{\beta^{2}\|p\|_{\mathcal{H}}}{1-c\alpha^{2}}\right)\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{y}:=\rho\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{y}.

Since the condition (
14
) yields that
0<\rho<1
, this proves that
\Phi
is contractive in
B_{p}
.

\Box

Assume (
13
) and (
14
). From (
24
) we know that any solution of (
9
) belongs to
B_{p}
. By Lemma
5
and the Banach Contraction principle,
\Phi
admits a unique fixed point in
B_{p}
, which solves (
9
). This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.

6
Conclusion

We considered a variational form of the Melan equation, see (
8
). The novelty consists in taking into account the
shape of the cable and not replacing
y^{\prime}(x)
with 0, as erroneously done in
[
7
]
. Indeed, von Kármán-Biot
[
7
, p.277]
warn the reader
by writing that
whereas the deflection of the beam may be considered small, the deflection of the string, i.e., the deviation of its shape from a straight
line, has to be considered as of finite magnitude
; then, they
neglect
y^{\prime}(x)^{2}
in comparison with unity
, see
[
7
, (5.14)]
.
We also maintained the nonlinearity given by the additional tension in the sustaining cable. This gives some difficulties in proving uniqueness of the
solution, see Theorem
2
and the comments that follow. Our numerical results suggest that one may have uniqueness for any
c>0
but Figure
2

leaves some doubts. The numerical procedure turns out to be extremely unstable, see the plots in Figure
3
. The numerically found solutions exhibit a clear
nonlinear behavior of the equations and a strong dependence on the curvature of the cable. Hence, one cannot drop the nonlinearity nor approximate

1+(y^{\prime})^{2}\approx 1
. We are confident that this paper might be the starting point for refined theoretical and numerical researches on the Melan equation.

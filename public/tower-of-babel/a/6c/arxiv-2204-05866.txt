Generators for the cohomology ring of the moduli of one-dimensional sheaves on ℙ 2 {\mathbb{P}}^{2}
Weite Pi, Junliang Shen

Abstract
. We explore the structure of the cohomology ring of the moduli space of stable 1-dimensional sheaves on ℙ 2 {\mathbb{P}}^{2} of any degree. We obtain a minimal set of tautological generators, which implies an optimal generation result for both the cohomology and the Chow ring of the moduli space. Our approach is through a geometric study of tautological relations.

1.1.
Normalizations

Under the assumption
\mathrm{gcd}(d,\chi)=1
, there is a universal family (see
[
22
, Theorem 4.6.5]
) over
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times M_{d,\chi}
, which we denote by
{\mathbb{F}}
. Let

\pi_{P}:{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times M_{d,\chi}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2},\quad\pi_{M}:{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times M_{d,\chi}\to M_{d,\chi}

be the projections. The choice of
{\mathbb{F}}
is not unique; for another universal family
{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}
there exists a line bundle
L\in\mathrm{Pic}(M_{d,\chi})
with

{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}\otimes\pi_{M}^{*}L.

Nevertheless, as a universal sheaf
{\mathbb{F}}
is a torsion sheaf on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times M_{d,\chi}
supported on a divisor, its first Chern character, which records its support, is uniquely determined.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a universal family. We have

\mathrm{ch}_{1}({\mathbb{F}})=\widetilde{h}^{*}c_{1}({\mathcal{O}}(d,1))\in A^{1}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times M_{d,\chi}).

Here
\widetilde{h}:{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times M_{d,\chi}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{2},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}(d))
is induced by (
1
). In particular
\mathrm{ch}_{1}({\mathbb{F}})
does not depend on the choice of
{\mathbb{F}}
.

Proof.

A universal sheaf
{\mathbb{F}}
is a torsion sheaf on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times M_{d,\chi}
supported on a divisor. Hence its first Chern character recovers its support, which is the pullback via
\widetilde{h}
of the incidence variety whose class is given by

c_{1}({\mathcal{O}}(d,1))\in A^{1}\left({\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{2},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}(d))\right).\qed

The class
\mathrm{ch}_{2}({\mathbb{F}})
is dependent on the choice of
{\mathbb{F}}
, which we use to conduct the normalization. For a universal family
{\mathbb{F}}
and a class

\alpha=\pi_{P}^{*}\alpha_{P}+\pi_{M}^{*}\alpha_{M}\in A^{1}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times M_{d,\chi}),\quad\textup{with}~~\alpha_{P}\in A^{1}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}),~~~\alpha_{M}\in A^{1}(M_{d,\chi}),

we consider the twisted Chern character

\mathrm{ch}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{F}}):=\mathrm{ch}({\mathbb{F}})\cdot\mathrm{exp}(\alpha),

and we denote
\mathrm{ch}_{k}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{F}})
its degree
k
-part. For any
\gamma\in A^{*}({\mathbb{P}}^{2})
, we set

\int_{\gamma}\mathrm{ch}_{k}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{F}}):=\pi_{M*}\left(\pi_{P}^{*}\gamma\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{k}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{F}})\right)\in A^{*}(M_{d,\chi}).

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a universal family. There exists a unique class
\alpha
as above such that

\int_{H}\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{F}})=0,\quad\int_{\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}}\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{F}})=0.

Proof.

We have

\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{\alpha}({\mathbb{F}})=\mathrm{ch}_{2}({\mathbb{F}})+\alpha\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{1}({\mathbb{F}}).

Hence the conditions read

(3)

\int_{H}\mathrm{ch}_{2}({\mathbb{F}})=-\int_{H}\alpha\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{1}({\mathbb{F}}),\quad\int_{\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}}\mathrm{ch}_{2}({\mathbb{F}})=-\int_{\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}}\alpha\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{1}({\mathbb{F}}).

By

26

35
]
, the Picard group of

is spanned by 2 classes, which we denote by

and

. Without loss of generality we set

to be the pullback of the hyperplane class via (

to be a relative ample class. Therefore Lemma

implies that

\mathrm{ch}_{1}({\mathbb{F}})=d\cdot\pi_{P}^{*}H+\pi_{M}^{*}D_{0}.

We may assume that

\alpha=\lambda_{1}\cdot\pi_{P}^{*}H+\lambda_{2}\cdot\pi_{M}^{*}D_{0}+\lambda_{3}\cdot\pi_{M}^{*}D_{1}.

Then by a direct calculation,

is determined by the second equation of (

We define the tautological class

{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, and
\chi_{0}\equiv\chi\mod d
with
-2d-1\leq\chi_{0}\leq-d+1
.

This theorem allows us to compute the Betti numbers for
M_{d,\chi}
by the (known) Betti numbers of Hilbert scheme of points. Note that by
[
32
]
, to apply Theorem
3.1
we can take
\chi=1
, so that
\chi_{0}=-d+1
and
\frac{d(d-3)}{2}-\chi_{0}=\frac{(d+1)(d-2)}{2}
. In particular, for fixed
d\geq 4
we have

(8)

b_{2d-4}(M_{d,\chi})=b_{2d-4}({{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}^{[\frac{(d+1)(d-2)}{2}]}).

Our goal is to show that the invariant (
8
) matches exactly the number of monomials formed by
c_{k}(j)
of cohomological degree
2d-4
, where there are two generators of degree
2
and three generators for each degree
4,6,\ldots,2d-4
. Combined with Proposition
1.5
, this will imply the freeness in
H^{\leq 2d-4}(M_{d,\chi},\mathbb{Q})
.

To this end, we invoke Göttsche’s formula on Betti numbers of Hilbert schemes of points. Denote by
P(-,z):=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}b_{i}(-)z^{i}
the Poincaré polynomial.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
20
]
)
.

Let
S
be a nonsingular projective surface. We have an identity between two series in variables
z
and
t
:

\sum_{n\geq 0}P(S^{[n]},z)t^{n}=\prod_{k\geq 1}\prod_{i=0}^{4}(1-z^{2k-2+i}t^{k})^{(-1)^{i+1}b_{i}(S)}.

Specializing to
S=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, Theorem
3.2
yields

\sum_{n\geq 0}\Big(\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}b_{i}(X^{[n]})z^{i}\Big)t^{n}=\prod_{k\geq 1}(1-z^{2k-2}t^{k})^{-1}(1-z^{2k}t^{k})^{-1}(1-z^{2k+2}t^{k})^{-1}.

In order to look at the coefficient of
z^{2d-4}t^{\frac{(d+1)(d-2)}{2}}
in the right-hand side, we expand it into a power series:

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHS}=\;

\displaystyle(1+t+t^{2}+\cdots)(1+z^{2}t+z^{4}t^{2}+\cdots)(1+z^{4}t+z^{8}t^{2}+\cdots)

\displaystyle(1+z^{2}t^{2}+z^{4}t^{4}+\cdots)(1+z^{4}t^{2}+z^{8}t^{4}+\cdots)(1+z^{6}t^{2}+z^{12}t^{4}+\cdots)\cdots.

Note that
2d-4<\frac{(d+1)(d-2)}{2}
whenever
d\geq 4
. Taking into account the first term
(1+t+t^{2}+\cdots)
, we see that it suffices to count the number of monomials with degree
2d-4
in the expansion

(1+z^{2}+z^{4}+\cdots)(1+z^{4}+z^{8}+\cdots)(1+z^{2}+z^{4}+\cdots)(1+z^{4}+z^{8}+\cdots)(1+z^{6}+z^{12}+\cdots)\cdots.

The result then follows from the combinatorial operation to ‘replace’
z
by the tautological generators
c_{k}(j)
. More precisely, we compare the above expansion with the generating series

\big[\sum_{i\geq 0}c_{0}(2)^{i}\big]\big[\sum_{i\geq 0}c_{1}(2)^{i}\big]\big[\sum_{i\geq 0}c_{2}(0)^{i}\big]\big[\sum_{i\geq 0}c_{2}(1)^{i}\big]\big[\sum_{i\geq 0}c_{2}(2)^{i}\big]\cdots

where we have two generators in cohomological degree 2 and three in each degree among
4,6,\ldots,2d-4
. We conclude that
b_{2d-4}(M_{d,\chi})
matches with the
maximal possible
dimension, which implies that no relations can occur among the tautological generators. This completes the proof of Theorem
0.2
(b). ∎

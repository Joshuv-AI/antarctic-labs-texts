Abstract

We consider, for n ⩾ 3 n\geqslant 3 , K K -quasiregular vol N × \mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times} -curves M → N M\to N of small distortion K ⩾ 1 K\geqslant 1 from oriented Riemannian n n -manifolds into Riemannian product manifolds N = N 1 × ⋯ × N k N=N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{k} , where each N i N_{i} is an oriented Riemannian n n -manifold and the calibration vol N × ∈ Ω n ​ ( N ) \mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times}\in\Omega^{n}(N) is the sum of the Riemannian volume forms vol N i \mathrm{vol}_{N_{i}} of the factors N i N_{i} of N N . We show that, in this setting, K K -quasiregular curves of small distortion are carried by quasiregular maps. More precisely, there exists K 0 = K 0 ​ ( n , k ) > 1 K_{0}=K_{0}(n,k)>1 having the property that, for 1 ⩽ K ⩽ K 0 1\leqslant K\leqslant K_{0} and a K K -quasiregular vol N × \mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times} -curve F = ( f 1 , … , f k ) : M → N 1 × ⋯ × N k F=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})\colon M\to N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{k} there exists an index i 0 ∈ { 1 , … , k } i_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,k\} for which the coordinate map f i 0 : M → N i 0 f_{i_{0}}\colon M\to N_{i_{0}} is a quasiregular map. 1.
Introduction

In this article, we consider the quasiconformal geometry of mappings
M\to N
between Riemannian manifolds, where
\dim M\leqslant\dim N
. Our main focus is in the stability of Liouville’s theorem and in Reshetnyak’s theorem for quasiregular curves of small distortion. To set the stage, we discuss first Liouville’s theorem for conformal curves associated to calibrations and then discuss results related to curves of small distortion. Here we call a mapping, associated to a calibration of manifold, a
curve
. We begin by introducing this terminology in more detail.

Let
M
and
N
be Riemannian manifolds of dimensions
n=\dim M\leqslant\dim N
. An
n
-form
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
is a
calibration on
N
if
\omega
is a closed non-vanishing
n
-form having point-wise comass norm
\lVert\omega\rVert
equal to one.
Recall that the comass norm of an
n
-form
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
at
p\in N
is

\lVert\omega_{p}\rVert=\max\{\omega_{p}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\colon|v_{1}|\leqslant 1,\ldots,|v_{n}|\leqslant 1\}.

For example, the Riemannian volume form of a Riemannian manifold and the symplectic form of a Kähler manifold are calibrations. We refer to the seminal article of Harvey and Lawson
[
15
]
for calibrations associated to exceptional Riemannian geometries and for the role of calibrations in the theory of minimal surfaces.

We say that a continuous mapping
F\colon M\to N
in
W^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}(M,N)
, where
n=\dim M
, is an
\omega
-calibrated curve for a calibration
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
on
N
if

(1.1)

\lVert DF\rVert^{n}=\star F^{*}\omega

almost everywhere in
M
, where
\lVert DF\rVert
is the operator norm of the differential and
\star
is the Hodge star operator. For any calibration, a mapping satisfying (
1.1
) is weakly conformal; see Lemma
4.1
. Here, and in what follows, a continuous mapping
F\colon M\to N
between Riemannian manifolds is
weakly conformal
if
F
belongs to the Sobolev space
W^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}(M,N)
for
n=\dim M
and the differential
DF
of
F
is a conformal linear map almost everywhere in
M
.

Conformal mappings and holomorphic curves are well-understood examples of calibrated curves.

Example 1.1
.

A continuous map
M\to N
between Riemannian manifolds of the same dimension is a calibrated curve if and only if it is a conformal map, that is, a
C^{\infty}
-smooth local homeomorphism having conformal non-vanishing differential. Here the smoothness follows from Ferrand’s theorem
[
26
]
on smoothness of conformal mappings between smooth manifolds.

In particular, by the classical Liouville’s theorem, a continuous map
\Omega\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
, where
\Omega
is a domain in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, is a calibrated curve if and only if it is a restriction of a Möbius transformations by the classical Liouville’s theorem. We refer to Iwaniec and Martin
[
20
]
and
[
21
, Section 5]
, Shachar
[
38
]
, and Liu
[
27
]
for the discussion of Liouville’s theorem under low regularity assumptions.

Example 1.2
.

By elementary linear algebra (see Remark
5.3
), curves
\Omega\to\mathbb{C}^{k}
calibrated by the standard symplectic form
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}=dx_{1}\wedge dy_{1}+\cdots+dx_{k}\wedge dy_{k}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{k})
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
is a domain, have weakly conformal coordinate functions.
Thus, by Weyl’s lemma, a map
\Omega\to\mathbb{C}^{k}
is an
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}
-calibrated curve if and only if it is a holomorphic curve.

More generally, a continuous map
\Sigma\to N
from a Riemann surface to a Kähler manifold
(N,J,\omega)
is an
\omega
-calibrated curve if and only if it is a
J
-holomorphic curve. The smoothness of the curve follows from higher integrability and elliptic regularity; see e.g. McDuff and Salamon
[
30
, Theorem B.4.1]
and the discussion below. We refer to Gromov
[
10
]
for more details on
J
-holomorphic curves.

Example 1.3
.

An almost complex structure
J
on a Kähler manifold
N
is a special case of a vector cross product
Q\colon\wedge^{k-1}TN\to TN
. To each vector cross product
Q
, we may associate a calibration
\omega_{Q}\in\Omega^{k}(N)
by

\omega_{Q}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})=\langle Q(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k-1}),v_{k}\rangle,

where
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in T_{p}N
for
p\in N
.

A continuous map
F\colon M\to N
in
W^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}(M,N)
, where
n=\dim M
, is a
Smith map with respect to an
(n-1)
-vector cross product
Q
on
N
if

Q\circ\wedge^{n-1}DF=\lVert DF\rVert^{n-2}DF\circ\star

almost everywhere on
M
. By Cheng, Karigiannis, and Madnick
[
6
, Propositions 2.32 and 3.2]
, a continuous Sobolev map
F\colon M\to N
in
W^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}(M,N)
is a Smith map with respect to a vector cross product
Q
if and only if
F
is a
\omega_{Q}
-calibrated curve with respect to the calibration
\omega_{Q}
associated to
Q
.

We refer to
[
6
]
for further discussion on the Brown–Gray classification
[
4
]
of vector cross products and properties of Smith maps.

Example 1.4
.

In the previous examples, curves stem from the point of view of classical mapping theory. To the other direction, we may interpret
\omega
-calibrated curves as holonomic sections of partial differential relations associated to the calibration
\omega
; we refer to Gromov’s book
[
11
]
for the terminology. Indeed, given a calibration
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
, let
G(\omega,p)\subset\widetilde{\mathrm{Gr}}(n,T_{p}N)
be the maximal
n
-planes of the calibration
\omega_{p}
of
T_{p}N
in the oriented Grassmannian
\widetilde{\mathrm{Gr}}(n,T_{p}N)
. Then
F\colon M\to N
is an
\omega
-calibrated curve if and only if
F
is a continuous Sobolev map in
W^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}(M,N)
satisfying

(1.2)

(DF)_{x}\in\{L\in\mathrm{CO}(T_{x}M,T_{F(x)}N)\colon L(T_{x}M)\in G(\omega,F(x))\}

for almost every
x\in M
, where
\mathrm{CO}(T_{x}M,T_{F(x)}N)
is the space of conformal linear maps from
T_{x}M
to
T_{F(x)}N
and
T_{x}M
is oriented by
\mathrm{vol}_{M}
.

For the volume form
\mathrm{vol}_{N}
or the symplectic form
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}
, all solutions to the partial differential relation (
1.2
) are smooth. This follows from the the standard elliptic regularity theory for the Laplacian. By the results of Cheng, Karigiannis, and Madnick in
[
6
]
, Smith maps associated to vector cross products are
C^{1,\alpha}
-regular and
C^{\infty}
-smooth away from the critical set. This follows from the regularity theory of
n
-harmonic mappings. In fact, all calibrated curves are energy minimizers for the
n
-energy; see Section
5
.

To our knowledge, it is an open question whether all solutions
M\to N
of the partial differential relation (
1.2
) are
C^{\infty}
-smooth.
Also, to our knowledge, it is an open question whether the critical set of a non-constant
\omega
-calibrated curve
M\to N
is empty. Weakly conformal maps in general are too flexible to have such additional regularity. Consider, for example, a folding map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto(|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{n}|)
, which is a Lipschitz regular weakly conformal map.

1.1.
Liouville theorems for curves into product manifolds

Our first main theorem is a Liouville theorem for calibrated curves
M\to N
into product manifolds
N=N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{k}
, where the calibration is a canonical multisymplectic form on
N
. More precisely, let
N=N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{k}
be a Riemannian product manifold of dimension
nk
, where each factor
N_{i}
is
n
-dimensional. The manifold
N
carries a canonical
n
-form

\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\pi_{i}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N_{i}}\in\Omega^{n}(N)

associated to the product structure
N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{k}
of
N
, where each
\mathrm{vol}_{N_{i}}
is the Riemannian volume form of the manifold
N_{i}
and each
\pi_{i}\colon N\to N_{i}
is a projection
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})\mapsto p_{i}
. Note that
\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{C}^{k}}^{\times}
is the classical symplectic form
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}
on
\mathbb{C}^{k}
. For detailed discussion on multisymplectic forms see e.g. Cantrijn, Ibort, and de León
[
5
]
.

In contrast to holomorphic curves,
\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times}
-calibrated curves in dimensions
n\geqslant 3
are extremely rigid. Heuristically, we may say that these curves are carried by conformal maps in the sense of the following theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
M
be an oriented and connected Riemannian
n
-manifold for
n\geqslant 3
and let
N=N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{k}
be a Riemannian product of oriented Riemannian
n
-manifolds
N_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Then for each non-constant
\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times}
-calibrated curve
F=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})\colon M\to N
there exists an index
i_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
for which

(1)

the coordinate map
f_{i_{0}}\colon M\to N_{i_{0}}
is a conformal map and

(2)

for
i\neq i_{0}
the coordinate map
f_{i}\colon M\to N_{i}
is constant.

Note that, by Ferrand’s theorem,
f_{i_{0}}
and hence also
F
are
C^{\infty}
-smooth. In the particular case that
M
is a domain in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
N=(\mathbb{S}^{n})^{k}
, we further have by Liouville’s theorem that
f_{i_{0}}
is a restriction of a Möbius transformation.

We may interpret Theorem
1.5
also in terms of minimal surfaces. Since the bundle
G(\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times})\to N
, where
G(\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times})=\bigcup_{p\in N}G(\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times},p)
, is a product bundle with discrete fibers, it is easy to see that a
C^{1}
-smooth minimal surface is contained in a submanifold, which is an isometric copy of a factor
N_{i}
in
N
. Theorem
1.5
now shows that image of an
\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times}
-calibrated curve is always contained in such a minimal surface.

1.2.
From conformal to quasiconformal geometry

Theorem
1.5
shows that, for
n\geqslant 3
, calibrated
\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times}
-curves
M\to N
exhibit the same rigidity as the conformal mappings between manifolds of the same dimension. To prove Theorem
1.5
we consider curves of small distortion.

A continuous mapping
F\colon M\to N
between Riemannian manifolds
M
and
N
of dimensions
n=\dim M\leqslant\dim N
is a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve
, for
K\geqslant 1
and a non-vanishing closed form
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
, if
F
belongs to the Sobolev space
W^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}(M,N)
and satisfies the distortion inequality

(1.3)

(\lVert\omega\rVert\circ F)\lVert DF\rVert^{n}\leqslant K(\star F^{*}\omega)

almost everywhere in
M
. If
\omega
is a calibration and
K=1
, we recover Equation (
1.1
).

The term quasiregular curve stems from the notion of quasiregular mapping. A continuous mapping
F\colon M\to N
between oriented Riemannian
n
-manifolds is
K
-quasiregular for
K\geqslant 1
if
F\in W^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}(M,N)
and

\lVert DF\rVert^{n}\leqslant KJ_{F}

almost everywhere in
M
, where
J_{F}
is the Jacobian determinant of
F
. Since
J_{F}=\star F^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}
, we readily observe that a continuous mapping
M\to N
in the Sobolev space
W^{1,n}_{\operatorname{loc}}(M,N)
is a
K
-quasiregular mapping if and only if it is a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve for a non-vanishing form
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
.

We refer to monographs of Iwaniec and Martin
[
21
]
, Reshetnyak
[
35
]
, and Rickman
[
36
]
for the theory of quasiregular mappings and to
[
16
]
,
[
31
]
, and
[
32
]
for discussions on properties of quasiregular curves. We merely note that, similarly as for quasiregular mappings, the defining condition for quasiregular curves is a
C^{0}
-closed partial differential relation by
[
32
, Theorem 1.9]
; see Gromov
[
11
, Section 1.2.3]
for the terminology.

As our first result on quasiregular curves, we show that quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times}
-curves
M\to N
of small distortion are local quasiconformal embeddings.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,
k\geqslant 1
, and
H>1
. Then there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,k,H)>0
having the property that each non-constant
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times}
-curve
M\to N
from an oriented and connected Riemannian
n
-manifold
M
to a Riemannian product
N=N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{k}
of oriented Riemannian
n
-manifolds is a local
H
-quasiconformal embedding.

Since the analytic definition of quasiconformal mappings is not at our disposal in this context, Theorem
1.6
is stated in terms of the metric definition of quasiconformal mappings. A local embedding
F\colon M\to N
between Riemannian manifolds is
H
-quasiconformal for
H\geqslant 1
, if

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\sup_{d_{M}(y,x)=r}d_{N}(F(y),F(x))}{\inf_{d_{M}(y,x)=r}d_{N}(F(y),F(x))}\leqslant H

for every
x\in M
. We refer to Heinonen and Koskela
[
17
]
for the metric theory of quasiconformal mappings.

Our interest to Theorem
1.6
stems from the classical Reshetnyak’s theorem for quasiregular mappings
[
34
]
:
a non-constant quasiregular mapping between oriented Riemannian
n
-manifolds is discrete and open
. Although quasiregular curves into higher dimensional targets are never open, Theorem
1.6
shows that
\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times}
-quasiregular curves
M\to N
of small distortion are discrete in the case
\dim M\geqslant 3
. It should be noted that this discreteness property fails for quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves of large distortion and even for quasiregular
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}
-curves of small distortion.

For curves of large distortion, the seminal example is by Iwaniec, Verchota, and Vogel
[
22
]
:
There exists a Lipschitz mapping
F=(f_{1},f_{2})\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
which satisfies
J_{f_{1}}+J_{f_{2}}=1
in the upper half-plane and is constant in the lower half-plane.
To our knowledge, this map constructed by Iwaniec, Verchota, and Vogel is the first example of a non-constant quasiregular
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}
-curve, which is not discrete. We show in Section
2
that, for each
n\geqslant 3
, there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
and a non-constant quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
which is constant in the lower half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{-}
. In particular, these non-constant curves are not discrete.

The failure of discreteness for quasiregular
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}
-curves of small distortion follows from an example of Rosay
[
37
]
:
For every
K>1
, there exist a non-constant map
F\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
satisfying

(1.4)

\left|\partial_{\bar{z}}F\right|\leqslant\frac{K-1}{K+1}\left|\partial_{z}F\right|,

which is not discrete.
In particular, Rosay’s example shows that Beltrami systems
\partial_{z}F=\mu\partial_{z}F
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
carry non-discrete solutions independently of the size of the Beltrami coefficient
\mu
. Rosay’s map is also a
K
-quasiregular
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}
-curve. We discuss this example in more detail in Section
3
.

1.3.
Stability of Liouville theorems

We return now to an analog of Theorem
1.5
for quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times}
-curves
M\to N
of small distortion.
We show that, heuristically, these curves do not change their direction in the Grassmannian
\widetilde{\mathrm{Gr}}(n,TN)
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
and
k\geqslant 1
. Then there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,k)>0
for the following. Let
M
be an oriented and connected Riemannian
n
-manifold and let
N=N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{k}
be a Riemannian product of oriented Riemannian
n
-manifolds. Then, for a non-constant
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times}
-curve
F=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})\colon M\to N
, there exists a unique index
i_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
for which the coordinate map
f_{i_{0}}\colon M\to N_{i_{0}}
is a quasiregular local homeomorphism.

Theorem
1.7
resembles a theorem of Ball and James
[
2
]
on the convergence of differentials of Sobolev maps. Indeed, by Theorem
1.5
,
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-calibrated curves
\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
have rigid derivatives. Thus we may view Theorem
1.7
as a convergence theorem of differentials as the distortion of the quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves tends to one. See also Kirchheim and Székelyhidi
[
23
]
on related results on the rank one convex hull of differentials of Lipschitz maps.

Remark 1.8
.

The smallness of the distortion is crucial in Theorem
1.7
. The curve constructed in Section
2
gives also an example of a quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
without quasiregular component mappings.

We record two corollaries of Theorem
1.7
. First we observe that these curves have the same local properties as quasiregular mappings; see e.g. Rickman
[
36
, Chapter I]
.

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
and
k\geqslant 1
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(n,k)>0
for the following. Let
F\colon M\to N
be a non-constant
(1+\varepsilon_{0})
-quasiregular map from an oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold
M
to a product
N=N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{k}
of oriented Riemannian
n
-manifolds. Then

\star F^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times}>0

almost everywhere in
M
and the set

B_{F}=\{x\in M\colon F\text{ is not a local embedding at }x\}

has topological dimension at most
n-2
and Hausdorff
n
-measure zero.

Second, we observe that, if
M
is conformally equivalent to a complete manifold with finite volume and
N
is simply connected, we have, by the Gromov–Zorich global homeomorphism theorem for quasiregular mappings, that the coordinate mapping
f_{i_{0}}\colon M\to N_{i_{0}}
carrying the curve
F\colon M\to N
is an embedding with a dense image in
N_{i_{0}}
; see Zorich
[
39
]
for the Euclidean global homeomorphism theorem and Gromov
[
12
, Remark 6.30]
and Zorich
[
40
]
for the Riemannian case, or
[
18
]
for the Gromov–Zorich theorem for mappings of finite distortion between Riemannian manifolds. In particular, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
and
k\geqslant 1
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(n,k)>0
having the property that, if there exists a non-constant
(1+\varepsilon_{0})
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times}
-curve
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
for
N=N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{k}
, then one of the factors
N_{i}
of
N
is either covered by
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

To our knowledge, it is an open question whether quasiregular curves
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
of arbitrary distortion have a similar property, that is, whether one of the factors
N_{i}
of
N=N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{k}
admits a non-constant quasiregular map from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if there exists a non-constant quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times}
-curve
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
. Such manifolds
N_{i}
are called quasiregularly elliptic. We refer to Bonk and Heinonen
[
3
]
and
[
33
]
for discussion on quasiregularly elliptic manifolds. Note that the problem is not local in the sense that it is easy to find quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves
F=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
for which none of the coordinate mappings
f_{i}
are quasiregular; see the example in Section
2
.

Outline of the proofs

Since the methods used in the proofs of the main theorems may have independent interest, we give an outline leading to the proofs of the main theorems.

The main issue in the proofs of Theorems
1.5
and
11.1
is that the differential
DF
of the curve
F\colon M\to N
is merely an
L^{n}
-function. Therefore, although the maximal planes
G(\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times},{F(x)})
of the calibration
\mathrm{vol}_{N}^{\times}
form a discrete set in the oriented Grassmannian
\widetilde{\mathrm{Gr}}(n,T_{F(x)}N)
for each
x\in M
, we do not have a priori control on the oscillation of the differential between the maximal planes. The example in Section
2
shows that for curves of large distortion this oscillation is, in fact, possible.

To obtain additional a priori regularity, we begin by showing that calibrated curves
M\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
are
C^{1,\alpha}
-regular; see Theorem
5.1
. This follows essentially from the local quasiminimality of quasiregular curves (
[
32
, Theorem 1.6]
). After this a priori regularity result, we work with quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}
-curves
\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
of small distortion, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a domain, and prove first Euclidean versions of the results stated in the introduction.

Using the Hölder continuity of the derivative, we prove in Section
5
a Euclidean version (Theorem
5.2
) of the Liouville theorem (Theorem
1.5
). Having this Euclidean version at our disposal, we show, using a method of Iwaniec
[
19
]
for quasiregular mappings, that
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves
\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
of small distortion are uniformly close to
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-calibrated curves; see Proposition
7.4
for a precise statement. In the course of the proof, we show that bounded

4.
Preliminaries on calibrations
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}

In this section, we recall elementary facts on the comass of
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
and linear maps
L\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
satisfying

\lVert L\rVert^{n}=\star L^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}.

Recall that the comass norm of
\omega\in\wedge^{n}V^{*}
is given by

\lVert\omega\rVert=\max\{\omega(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\colon|v_{i}|\leqslant 1\}=\max_{L}\star L^{*}\omega,

where the second maximum is taken over all linear maps
L\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to V
satisfying
|L(e_{i})|\leqslant 1
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Here, the optimal linear maps are conformal. We rephrase this fact as follows.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\omega\in\wedge^{n}V
be a calibration. Then each linear map
L\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to V
satisfying

\lVert L\rVert^{n}=\star L^{*}\omega

is conformal.

Proof.

By the singular value decomposition, we have that
L=QDP
, where
Q\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to V
and
P\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
are isometries, and
D\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a diagonal map whose diagonal elements are singular values of
L
. Then

L^{*}\omega=(QDP)^{*}\omega=P^{*}D^{*}Q^{*}\omega=(\det P)(\det D)Q^{*}\omega

and

\lVert L\rVert=\lVert QDP\rVert=\lVert D\rVert.

Thus

\lVert D\rVert^{n}=\lVert L\rVert^{n}=\star L^{*}\omega=(\det P)(\det D)\star Q^{*}\omega\leqslant(\det D)\lVert Q\rVert^{n}=\det D.

We conclude that
D=\lVert D\rVert{\operatorname{id}}
and hence
L
is conformal.
∎

The classification of the maximal planes of
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
is well-known. For the reader’s convenience we give, in the following lemma, a simple proof which simultaneously shows that the forms
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
are calibrations.

Lemma 4.2
.

For each
n\geqslant 2
and
k\geqslant 1
, the form
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
is a calibration. Moreover, if a linear map
L=(L_{1},\ldots,L_{k})\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
satisfies

\lVert L\rVert^{n}=\star L^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}},

then each linear map
L_{i}
is conformal and, if

, there exists
i_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
for which
L_{i}=0
for
i\neq i_{0}

As a preliminary step, for each
n\geqslant 2
, let

be the function
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto\sqrt{1-x_{1}^{2}}\cdots\sqrt{1-x_{n}^{2}}+x_{1}\cdots x_{n}

For

and

\psi_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})<\psi_{n-1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})<\psi_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\leqslant\psi_{2}(x_{1},x_{1})=1,

\max_{x}\psi_{n}(x)=1
for each
n\geqslant 3
and we have that

E_{n}=\{x\in[0,1]^{n}\colon\psi_{n}(x)=1\}=\{(0,\ldots,0),(1,\ldots,1)\}.

We prove now both claims in the lemma simultaneously by induction in
k\in\mathbb{N}
. For
k=1
, we have that
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}=\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. Thus clearly
\lVert\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}\rVert=1
and a linear mapping
L\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
\lVert L\rVert^{n}=\star L^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is conformal by Lemma
4.1
.

Suppose now that the claim holds for
k-1
. Let
L=(L_{1},\ldots,L_{k})\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a linear map satisfying
|L(e_{i})|\leqslant 1
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Let also
L^{\prime}=(L_{1},\ldots,L_{k-1})\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k-1}
. For each
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, let also
x_{j}=|L_{k}(e_{j})|\in[0,1]
. Then

(4.1)

\begin{split}\star L^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}&=\star(L^{\prime})^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k-1}}^{\times}+\star L_{k}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\\
&=\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k-1}}^{\times}(L^{\prime}(e_{1}),\ldots,L^{\prime}(e_{n}))+\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(L_{k}(e_{1}),\ldots.L_{k}(e_{n}))\\
&\leqslant\sqrt{1-x_{1}^{2}}\cdots\sqrt{1-x_{n}^{2}}\lVert\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k-1}}^{\times}\rVert+x_{1}\cdots x_{n}\lVert\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rVert\\
&\leqslant\sqrt{1-x_{1}^{2}}\cdots\sqrt{1-x_{n}^{2}}+x_{1}\cdots x_{n}=\psi_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\leqslant 1.\end{split}

Hence

\lVert\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}\rVert=\max_{L}\star L^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}=1.

Let now
L=(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a linear map for which
\lVert L\rVert^{n}=\star L^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
. Then, by Lemma
4.1
, the map
L
is conformal and we may further assume that
\lVert L(e_{i})\rVert=1
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Let
L^{\prime}=(L_{1},\ldots,L_{k-1})\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k-1}
and let
x_{j}=|L_{k}(e_{j})|
for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
as above. Since
\star L^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}=1
, we have that all inequalities in (
4.1
) are equalities. In particular, we have that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in E_{n}
.

For
n\geqslant 3
, we have that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is either
(0,\ldots,0)
or
(1,\ldots,1)
. In the first case, we have that
L_{k}=0
and
\star(L^{\prime})^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k-1}}^{\times}=1
. Then, by the induction assumption, there exists
i_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
for which
L_{i}=0
for
i\neq i_{0}
. In the second case, we have that
L^{\prime}=0
and we may take
i_{0}=k
.

For
n=2
, we have that
x_{1}=x_{2}
. Since we have
\star(L^{\prime})^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k-1}}^{\times}\leqslant\sqrt{1-x_{1}^{2}}\sqrt{1-x_{2}^{2}}
and we have an equality in (
4.1
), we obtain that

\det L_{k}=\star L_{k}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=x_{1}x_{2}=|L_{k}(e_{1})||L_{k}(e_{2})|.

We have by the Hadamard inequality that
L_{k}
is conformal. Since
L
and
L_{k}
are conformal, we conclude that

L^{\prime}(e_{1})\cdot L^{\prime}(e_{2})=L(e_{1})\cdot L(e_{2})-L_{k}(e_{1})\cdot L_{k}(e_{2})=0.

Since
|L^{\prime}(e_{1})|=\sqrt{1-x_{1}^{2}}=\sqrt{1-x_{2}^{2}}=|L^{\prime}(e_{2})|
, we have that
L^{\prime}
is conformal and
\lVert L^{\prime}\rVert^{2}=\star(L^{\prime})^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{k-1}}^{\times}
. Thus the linear maps
L_{1},\ldots,L_{k-1}
are conformal by the induction assumption.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

For
n=1
and
k\geqslant 1
, the comass norm depends on
k
and we have that

\lVert\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{1})^{k}}^{\times}\rVert=\sqrt{k}.

Indeed, for a linear map
L=(L_{1},\ldots,L_{k})\colon\mathbb{R}\to(\mathbb{R}^{1})^{k}
,
t\mapsto(ta_{1},\ldots,ta_{k})
, satisfying
|L(1)|=|(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})|\leqslant 1
, we have that

\star L^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{1})^{k}}^{\times}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}}(L_{i}(1))=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\leqslant\sqrt{k}\sqrt{a_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{k}^{2}}=\sqrt{k}.

Since the upper bound
\sqrt{k}
is reached, we conclude that
\lVert\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{1})^{k}}^{\times}\rVert=\sqrt{k}
.

5.
Calibrated curves are
n
-energy minimizing

In this section we show that calibrated curves with Euclidean targets are
n
-harmonic and hence
C^{1,\alpha}
-Hölder continuous. The
n
-harmonicity follows along the same lines as quasiminimality of quasiregular curves; see
[
32
, Section 2]
. The main difference is that in
[
32
]
the energy of the map is understood in terms of the operator norm instead of the Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Recall that the Hilbert–Schmidt norm of a linear map
L\colon V\to W
is

\lVert L\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|L(e_{i})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

where
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
is an orthonormal basis of
V
. Note that

\lVert L\rVert\leqslant\frac{1}{n^{1/2}}\lVert L\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}},

where the inequality is an equality if
L
is conformal.

We say that a mapping
u\colon M\to N
between Riemannian manifolds in
W_{{\operatorname{loc}}}^{1,n}(M,N)
is a
local
n
-minimizer
if for each compact submanifold
G\subset M
with boundary and each
W^{1,n}(G,N)
-mapping
v\colon G\to N
having the same trace at
\partial G
as
u
, we have that

(5.1)

\int_{G}\lVert Du\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^{n}\leqslant\int_{G}\lVert Dv\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^{n};

see Hardt and Lin
[
14
]
for the terminology.

It is easy to show that calibrated curves
M\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
are local
n
-minimizers and hence
C^{1,\alpha}
-regular by a result of Hardt and Lin
[
14
, Corollary 3.2]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian
n
-manifold,
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
a calibration, and
F\colon M\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
an
\omega
-calibrated curve. Then
F
is a local
n
-minimizer. In particular,
F
is
C^{1,\alpha}
-regular for
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Let
G\subset M
be a compact submanifold with boundary. Suppose first that
v\colon G\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a continuous map in
W^{1,n}(G,\mathbb{R}^{m})
satisfying
v|_{\partial G}=F|_{\partial G}
. Since
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is contractible, the map
v
is homotopic to
F|_{G}\colon G\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
(rel
\partial G
). Thus

\int_{G}F^{*}\omega=\int_{G}v^{*}\omega.

Since
F
is
\omega
-calibrated, we have that
\frac{1}{n^{n/2}}\lVert DF\rVert^{n}_{\mathrm{HS}}=\star F^{*}\omega
almost everywhere in
M
. Thus

\frac{1}{n^{n/2}}\int_{G}\lVert DF\rVert^{n}_{\mathrm{HS}}=\int_{G}F^{*}\omega=\int_{G}v^{*}\omega\leqslant\int_{G}\lVert Dv\rVert^{n}\leqslant\frac{1}{n^{n/2}}\int_{G}\lVert Dv\rVert^{n}_{\mathrm{HS}}.

The general case of a test mapping
v\in W^{1,n}(G,N)
, having the same trace at
\partial G
as
F
, follows now by a standard convolution argument.
∎

The Euclidean version of Theorem
1.5
is now an immediate consequence of Theorem
5.1
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
and
k\geqslant 1
. Let
F=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\colon\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-calibrated curve, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a domain. Then there exists an index
i_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
for which the coordinate mapping
f_{i_{0}}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a restriction of a Möbius transformation
\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
and each
f_{i}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
i\neq i_{0}
is a constant map.

Proof.

We may assume that
F
is non-constant. By Theorem
5.1
, the differential
DF
is continuous. Let

\widetilde{\Omega}=\{x\in\Omega\colon DF(x)\neq 0\}.

Since
F
is non-constant, we have that
\widetilde{\Omega}
is a non-empty open set.

For each
x\in\widetilde{\Omega}
, there exists, by continuity of
DF
and Lemma
4.2
, a connected neighborhood
U_{x}\subset\widetilde{\Omega}
of
x
and an index
i_{x}\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
for which
Df_{i_{x}}
is non-vanishing in
U_{x}
and
Df_{i}|_{U_{x}}=0
for
i\neq i_{0}
. Since
f_{i_{x}}
is conformal in
U_{x}
, we have that it is Möbius by the classical Liouville’s theorem.

Let now
G\subset\widetilde{\Omega}
be a component of
\widetilde{\Omega}
. Then
\{U_{x}\}_{x\in G}
is a covering of
G
. Since
G
is connected, we conclude that there exist a unique index
i_{G}\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
for which
f_{i_{G}}|_{G}
is a non-constant Möbius transformation and that each
f_{i}
is constant in
G
for
i\neq i_{G}
. Let
f\colon\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
be the unique Möbius transformation extending
f_{i_{G}}|_{G}
.

Since the zero set of
DF
is contained in the zero set of
Df
, which is empty, we conclude that
\widetilde{\Omega}=\Omega
. Thus
G=\Omega
. The claim is proven.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Similarly
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{k}}^{\times}
-calibrated curves are holomorphic curves. Indeed, let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and let
F\colon(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})\colon\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{k}
be a
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{k}}^{\times}
-calibrated curve. Then, by Lemma
4.2
we have that each
f_{i}
has conformal differential. Hence each
f_{i}
is a weakly conformal map in the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Since each
f_{i}
is a weak solution to the Cauchy–Riemann equations, we have that
f_{i}
is holomorphic by Weyl’s lemma; see e.g. Astala–Iwaniec–Martin
[
1
, Lemma A.6.10]
.

6.
Bounded sequences of quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves are normal

In this section, we show that a bounded sequence of
K
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves is normal. Since locally uniform limits of
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curves are
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curves by
[
32
, Theorem 1.9]
, it suffices to show that a bounded sequence of
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curves
\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a domain, has a converging subsequence when
\omega
is a calibration with constant coefficients.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
and let
\omega\in\wedge^{n}\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a calibration. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain, let
K\geqslant 1
and let
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence of
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curves
F_{k}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then the sequence
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
has a locally uniformly converging subsequence and the limiting map is a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve.

The proof of Theorem
6.1
is based on a quantitative version of local Hölder continuity for quasiregular curves. In
[
31
]
it is shown, using the now standard Morrey’s argument, that, for a calibration
\omega\in\wedge^{n}\mathbb{R}^{m}
, a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve is locally
\alpha
-Hölder continuous with

(6.1)

\alpha=\frac{1}{K}\frac{\lVert\omega\rVert}{|\omega|_{\ell_{1}}}=\frac{1}{K}\frac{1}{|\omega|_{\ell_{1}}},

where
|\omega|_{\ell_{1}}
is the
\ell_{1}
-norm of
\omega
. It is not known whether the exponent
\alpha
is sharp.

In what follows, we verify that the same argument yields also uniform estimates for the multiplicative Hölder constant for sequences of bounded curves. This immediately yields Theorem
6.1
. For the initial step in the proof we quote the following Caccioppoli type estimate which states that, locally, the diameter of the image controls the energy of the curve; see
[
31
, Lemma 6.1]
for details.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
and let
\omega\in\wedge^{n}\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a calibration. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain, let
K\geqslant 1
and let
F\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve.
Then

\left(\int_{\frac{1}{2}B}\lVert DF\rVert^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\leqslant C(n)K\operatorname{diam}(FB)

for every ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
\overline{B}\subset\Omega
.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

By the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem, it suffices to show that the sequence
(F_{k})
is locally uniformly
\alpha
-Hölder. That is, there exists a constant
0<\alpha\leqslant 1
for which for every
x\in\Omega
there exists a constant
C>0
and a neighborhood
U
satisfying

|F_{k}(x)-F_{k}(y)|\leqslant C|x-y|^{\alpha}

for every
y\in U
and every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
x\in\Omega
and let
R>0
be such that
\overline{B^{n}(x,8R)}\subset\Omega
. Let also
a\in B^{n}(x,R)
and
r>0
be such that
B_{r}=B^{n}(a,r)\subset B^{n}(x,2R)
.

Unraveling the statement of
[
31
, Lemma 5.1]
, we obtain that, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
in (
6.1
),
we have that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\lVert DF_{k}\rVert^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{|B(0,1)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}\frac{r^{\alpha-1}}{R^{\alpha}}\left(\int_{B^{n}(x,3R)}\lVert DF_{k}\rVert^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let now

L=\sup_{k}\operatorname{diam}(F_{k}\Omega)<\infty.

Then, by Lemma
6.2
, we have that

\left(\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\lVert DF_{k}\rVert^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\leqslant C(n,R,\alpha)KLr^{\alpha-1}

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

We may now apply standard chain argument (see Hajłasz–Koskela
[
13
]
for the general result or
[
31
, Lemma 5.2]
for the special case), to obtain

\displaystyle|F_{k}(x)-F_{k}(y)|\leqslant C(n,R,\alpha,K,L)|x-y|^{\alpha}\left(\int_{B^{n}(x,4R)}\lVert DF_{k}\rVert^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

for every
y\in B^{n}(x,R)
and every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By applying Lemma
6.2
again, we have that

|F_{k}(x)-F_{k}(y)|\leqslant C(n,R,K,L,\omega)|x-y|^{\alpha}

for every
y\in B^{n}(x,R)
and every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

7.
Local injectivity of
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves of small distortion

In this section, we prove that quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves of small distortion are local quasiconformal embeddings.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,
k\geqslant 1
and
H>1
. Then there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,k,H)>0
for the following. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain and let
F\colon\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a non-constant
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve. Then
F
is a local
H
-quasiconformal embedding.

Our proof follows the proof of Iwaniec
[
19
]
for the Martio–Rickman–Väisälä theorem on the local injectivity of the quasiregular mappings of small distortion; see Martio–Rickman–Väisälä
[
28
, Theorem 4.6]
or Rickman
[
36
, Theorem VI.8.14]
. See also Kopylov
[
25
]
for a functional approach to the stability of Liouville’s theorem.

We divide the discussion into three parts. For the proof, we introduce first sheaves of quasiregular curves and consider an associated gauge function measuring the distance of quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves to
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-calibrated curves. In the second step, we develop a Harnack estimate for quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves of small distortion. Finally, in the third step, we combine the distance estimate in terms of the gauge function and the Harnack estimate to obtain the local injectivity and metric quasiconformality of curves of small distortion.

7.1.
Sheaves
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{n,k}
of quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves

We define the following auxiliary sheaves
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{n,k}
of quasiregular curves. For a general discussion of sheaves on manifolds; see
[
8
, Chapter 1.6]
. Let
n\geqslant 3
and
k\geqslant 1
. For each
t\geqslant 0
, let
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{n,k}
be the sheaf of
(1+t)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, that is, for each open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{n,k}(\Omega)
be the family of all
(1+t)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves
\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
. Note that
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{n,k}
is a sheaf, since a restriction of a
K
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve into a subdomain is a
K
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve and two
K
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves
\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
and
\Omega^{\prime}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
, which agree on
\Omega\cap\Omega^{\prime}
, define a
K
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve
\Omega\cup\Omega^{\prime}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
.

Let also
\mathscr{F}^{n,k}
be the sheaf of
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-calibrated curves. Then
\mathscr{F}_{0}^{n,k}=\mathscr{F}^{n,k}
. Note that, by Theorem
5.2
, we have that
\mathscr{F}^{n,k}
consists of constant mappings
\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
and mappings
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k})\colon\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
for which there exists
i_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
so that
\varphi_{i_{0}}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a restriction of a Möbius transformation
\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
and each
\varphi_{i}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a constant map for
i\neq i_{0}
. Note that, in particular,
\mathscr{F}^{n,1}
is the family of constant mappings
\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and restrictions of Möbius transformations
\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

The sheaves
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{n,k}
are also closed under translation and dilation and, by Theorem
6.1
, they are also closed under locally uniform limits. We record these properties as lemmas. The proof follows from basic properties of quasiregular curves and we omit the details.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
and
k\geqslant 1
. Let
F\in\mathscr{F}_{t}^{n,k}(\Omega)
, where
t\geqslant 0
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a domain. Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
y_{0}\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
,
\lambda>0
and
\mu>0
. Then the map
x\mapsto\lambda F(\mu x+x_{0})+y_{0}
belongs to
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{n,k}(\widetilde{\Omega})
, where
\widetilde{\Omega}=\{\frac{1}{\mu}(x-x_{0})\colon x\in\Omega\}
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,
k\geqslant 1
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain. Then, for a sequence
t_{i}\searrow 0
and a bounded sequence
(F_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of mappings
F_{i}\in\mathscr{F}_{t_{i}}^{n,k}(\Omega)
,
there exists a subsequence
(F_{i_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and a mapping
F\in\mathscr{F}^{n,k}(\Omega)
for which
F_{i_{j}}\to F
locally uniformly in
\Omega
.

Having sheaves
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{n,k}
at our disposal, we may now define, as in
[
19
]
, an auxiliary gauge function
\tau^{n,k}\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
by setting

\tau^{n,k}(t)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant s\leqslant t\\
F\in\mathscr{F}_{s}^{n,k}(B^{n})\\
\sup_{B}|F|\leqslant 1\end{subarray}}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in\mathscr{F}^{n,k}(B^{n})\\
\varphi(0)=F(0)\end{subarray}}d(F,\varphi)

for
t\geqslant 0
, where

d(F,\varphi)=\sup_{x\in B^{n}}\,(1-|x|)|F(x)-\varphi(x)|.

Note that the minimum in the definition of
\tau^{n,k}
is well-defined by Lemma
7.3
. Also
\lim_{t\to 0}\tau^{n,k}(t)=0
; see
[
19
]
for details.

The gauge function
\tau^{n,k}
serves several purposes in the proof of Theorem
7.1
. An immediate application is to approximate curves in
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{n,k}
by calibrated curves. Since the proof of the following proposition is verbatim to the proof of
[
19
, Lemma 1]
, we merely recall the statement here.

Proposition 7.4

(
[
19
, Lemma 1]
)
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
and
k\geqslant 1
. Let
B=B^{n}(a,r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a ball. Let also
t\geqslant 0
and
F\in\mathscr{F}_{t}^{n,k}(B)
. Then there exists
\varphi\in\mathscr{F}^{n,k}(B)
for which
\varphi(a)=F(a)
and

\sup_{\sigma B}|F-\varphi|\leqslant\frac{\tau^{n,k}(t)}{1-\sigma}\sup_{B}|F|

for every
\sigma\in[0,1)
.

7.2.
Harnack estimate

The following Harnack estimate for curves of small distortion, in terms the gauge function
\tau^{n,k}
, is the basis of the forthcoming distortion estimates.

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
and
k\geqslant 1
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain. Let also
t\geqslant 0
and
F\in\mathscr{F}_{t}^{n,k}(\Omega)
. Suppose that
\tau^{n,k}(t)\leqslant 10^{-3}
. Then

\sup_{\frac{1}{2}B}|F|\leqslant 2^{\frac{4-4\rho}{\rho}}\sup_{\rho B}|F|

for every ball
B\subset\Omega
and every
0<\rho\leqslant\frac{1}{2}
.

Since the proof of Proposition
7.5
is based on the analysis of the norm of the mapping
F
, that is, the function
x\mapsto|F(x)|
, the proof is essentially the same as the proof of
[
19
, Lemma 3]
. For this reason, we merely discuss briefly the proof of
[
19
, Proposition 2]
, where the particular structure of
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-calibrated curves have a role, and omit other details; see also
[
19
, Lemmas 2 and 3]
. The following proposition is a restatement of
[
19
, Proposition 2]
.

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,
k\geqslant 1
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain. Let also
t\geqslant 0
and
F\in\mathscr{F}_{t}^{n,k}(\Omega)
. Then

\sup_{\frac{4}{5}B}|F|\leqslant 8\sup_{\frac{2}{5}B}|F|+19\tau^{n,k}(t)\sup_{B}|F|

for every ball
B\subset\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
B\subset\Omega
be a ball. By Proposition
7.4
, there exists
\varphi\in\mathscr{F}^{n,k}(B)
for which

\sup_{\sigma B}|F-\varphi|\leqslant\frac{\tau^{n,k}(t)}{1-\sigma}\sup_{B}|F|

for every
\sigma\in[0,1)
. Then, by the triangle inequality, we have the estimate

\sup_{\frac{4}{5}B}|F|\leqslant\sup_{\frac{4}{5}B}|F-\varphi|+\sup_{\frac{4}{5}B}|\varphi|\leqslant 5\tau^{n,k}(t)\sup_{B}|F|+\sup_{\frac{4}{5}B}|\varphi|.

Since
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k})\colon\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
, where either
\varphi
is constant or exactly one coordinate map
\varphi_{i}
is a restriction of a Möbius transformation
\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
and the other coordinate maps
\varphi_{j}
are constant, we have that

\sup_{\frac{4}{5}B}|\varphi|\leqslant 8\sup_{\frac{2}{5}B}|\varphi|;

see the proof of
[
19
, Proposition 2]
for details. Then

8\sup_{\frac{2}{5}B}|\varphi|\leqslant 8\sup_{\frac{2}{5}B}|F-\varphi|+8\sup_{\frac{2}{5}B}|F|\leqslant 14\tau^{n,k}(t)\sup_{B}|F|+8\sup_{\frac{2}{5}B}|F|

and the claim follows.
∎

7.3.
Local injectivity and metric quasiconformality

Theorem
7.1
follows almost immediately from the following local distortion estimate.

Proposition 7.7
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,
k\geqslant 1
, and
\delta>0
. There exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,k,\delta)>0
for the following. Let
B=B^{n}(a,r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a ball and let
F\colon B\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve. Then

\sup_{x\in\rho B}|F(x)-F(a)|\leqslant\left(\frac{1+2\rho}{1-2\rho}+2^{\frac{4}{\rho}}\delta\right)\min_{|x-a|=\rho r}|F(x)-F(a)|

for every
0<\rho\leqslant\frac{1}{4}
.

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

First, let
\rho=\rho(H)\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
be such that

\frac{1+2\rho}{1-2\rho}\leqslant\frac{H+1}{2}.

Second, fix
\delta=\delta(\rho,H)\in(0,2^{-16})
for which

2^{\frac{4}{\rho}}\delta\leqslant\frac{H-1}{2}.

Let now
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,k,\delta)>0
be as in Proposition
7.7
. Since
\delta=\delta(\rho,H)
and
\rho=\rho(H)
, we have that
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,k,\delta)=\varepsilon(n,k,H)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain and let
F\colon\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a non-constant
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve. To obtain that
F
is a local embedding, it suffices to show that
F
is locally injective.

Let
B=B^{n}(x,R)
be a ball for which
B^{n}(x,9R)\subset\Omega
. Suppose that there exists distinct points
a,b\in B
for which
F(a)=F(b)
. Then
B^{n}(a,4|a-b|)\subset\Omega
. Applying Proposition
7.7
with
\rho=1/4
yields

\max_{|x-a|\leqslant|b-a|}|F(x)-F(a)|\leqslant 4\min_{|x-a|=|b-a|}|F(x)-F(a)|=0.

Thus,
F
is constant in
B^{n}(a,|b-a|)
. By repeating the same argument, it follows that
F
is constant in the whole domain
\Omega
. Hence,
F
must be injective in
B
. It remains to show that
F
is
H
-quasiconformal.

Let
a\in\Omega
. Let
r>0
for which
B^{n}(a,9\frac{r}{\rho})\subset\Omega
. Then
F
is injective in

and

. Thus, we obtain the estimate

\frac{\max_{|x-a|=r}|F(x)-F(a)|}{\min_{|x-a|=r}|F(x)-F(a)|}\leqslant\frac{1+2\rho}{1-2\rho}+2^{\frac{4}{\rho}}\delta\leqslant H

by applying Proposition
7.7
in the ball
B^{n}(a,\frac{r}{\rho})
. The claim follows.
∎

Lemma 7.8
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,

, and

. Let also

be a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, for each
F\in\mathscr{F}_{t}^{n,k}(B)
, we have that

(7.1)

\left(1-2^{\frac{4-\rho}{\rho}}\tau^{n,k}(t)\right)\sup_{x\in\rho B}|F(x)-F(a)|\leqslant\frac{1+2\rho}{1-2\rho}\min_{|x-a|=\rho r}|F(x)-F(a)|

for every
0<\rho\leqslant\frac{1}{4}
. In particular, if
\tau^{n,k}(t)\leqslant 2^{-17}
, then

(7.2)

\sup_{x\in\frac{1}{4}B}|F(x)-F(a)|\leqslant 4\min_{|x-a|=\frac{1}{4}r}|F(x)-F(a)|.

Proof.

Since (
7.2
) follows immediately from (
7.1
), it suffices to prove (
7.1
).

We may assume that
a=0
and
F(a)=0
. By Proposition
7.4
, since
F|_{\frac{1}{2}B}\in\mathscr{F}_{t}^{n,k}(\frac{1}{2}B)
, there exists
\varphi\in\mathscr{F}^{n,k}(\frac{1}{2}B)
for which
\varphi(0)=0
and

\sup_{\frac{\sigma}{2}B}|F-\varphi|\leqslant\frac{\tau^{n,k}(t)}{1-\sigma}\sup_{\frac{1}{2}B}|F|

for every
\sigma\in[0,1)
. In particular,

\sup_{\rho B}|F-\varphi|\leqslant\frac{\tau^{n,k}(t)}{1-2\rho}\sup_{\frac{1}{2}B}|F|\leqslant 2\tau^{n,k}(t)\sup_{\frac{1}{2}B}|F|.

Since
\varphi(0)=0
, we have either
\varphi\equiv 0
or
\varphi=(0,\ldots,0,\varphi_{i_{0}},0\ldots,0)
, where
\varphi_{i_{0}}
is a restriction of a Möbius transformation
\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Thus
\varphi
satisfies the estimate

\sup_{\rho B}|\varphi|\leqslant\frac{1+2\rho}{1-2\rho}\min_{|x|=\rho r}|\varphi(x)|\leqslant\frac{1+2\rho}{1-2\rho}\left(\sup_{\rho B}|F-\varphi|+\min_{|x|=\rho r}|F(x)|\right).

Then, by the triangle inequality and Proposition
7.5
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\rho B}|F|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{\rho B}|F-\varphi|+\frac{1+2\rho}{1-2\rho}\left(\sup_{\rho B}|F-\varphi|+\min_{|x|=\rho r}|F(x)|\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1+2\rho}{1-2\rho}\min_{|x|=\rho r}|F(x)|+4\sup_{\rho B}|F-\varphi|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1+2\rho}{1-2\rho}\min_{|x|=\rho r}|F(x)|+8\tau^{n,k}(t)\sup_{\frac{1}{2}B}|F|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1+2\rho}{1-2\rho}\min_{|x|=\rho r}|F(x)|+2^{\frac{4-\rho}{\rho}}\tau^{n,k}(t)\sup_{\rho B}|F|.

The claim follows.
∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
7.7

Proof of Proposition
7.7
.

Since
\tau^{n,k}(t)\to 0
as
t\to 0
, we may fix
\varepsilon>0
for which
\tau^{n,k}(\varepsilon)\leqslant\min\{\delta/2,2^{-17}\}
. Let now
B=B^{n}(a,r)
be a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
F\colon B\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
a
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiregular

Thus, by (

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\rho B}|F(x)-F(a)|\leqslant

8.
Almost maximal planes of
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}

In the following proposition we characterize planes, in terms of linear maps, which almost maximize the comass norm of
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
. This proposition can be viewed as a linear algebraic version of Theorem
1.5
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,
k\geqslant 1
, and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/(100k))
. Then, for a linear map
L=(L_{1},\ldots,L_{k})\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
satisfying

(8.1)

\lVert L\rVert^{n}\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\left(\star L^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}\right),

there exists an index
i_{0}\in\{1,\ldots k\}
for which

(8.2)

\lVert L_{i_{0}}\rVert^{n}\leqslant(1+7k\sqrt{\varepsilon})\det L_{i_{0}}.

Moreover,

(1)

\lVert L_{i_{0}}\rVert\geqslant\lVert L\rVert/(1+\varepsilon)
, and

(2)

\lVert L_{i}\rVert\leqslant 5\sqrt{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\lVert L\rVert
for every
i\neq i_{0}
.

Proof.

We may assume that
L
is non-constant and that
\det L_{i}\geqslant 0
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Indeed, the claim holds trivially for constant maps and, by post-composing each
L_{i}
with an orientation reversing isometry, if necessary, we obtain a linear map
\widetilde{L}=(\widetilde{L}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{L}_{k})\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
satisfying (
8.1
) with
\lVert\widetilde{L}\rVert=\lVert L\rVert
,
\star L^{*}\omega\leqslant\star\widetilde{L}^{*}\omega
, and
\lVert\widetilde{L}_{i}\rVert=\lVert L_{i}\rVert
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. We prove first (
1
). Then we prove (
8.2
) and finally (
2
).

For the proof of (
1
), let
\lambda_{i}=\lVert L_{i}\rVert
for each
i=1,\ldots,k
. Since (
8.1
) is invariant under permutation of the factors of
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
, we may assume that
\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{k}\geqslant 0
. For (
1
) it suffices to show that
\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lVert L\rVert/(1+\varepsilon)
.

For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, let
Q_{i}\in\mathrm{SO}(n)
be such an orientation preserving isometry that
Q_{i}(L_{i}(e_{n}))=c_{i}e_{n}
, where
c_{i}\geqslant 0
, and denote
R_{i}=Q_{i}L_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let also
R=(R_{1},\ldots,R_{k})\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
. Then

(8.3)

\lVert R\rVert^{n}\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\left(\star R^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}\right).

Indeed, for each
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have that

|R(v)|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|R_{i}(v)|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|(Q_{i}L_{i})(v)|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|L_{i}(v)|^{2}=|L(v)|^{2}.

Thus
\lVert R\rVert=\lVert L\rVert
. Since we also have that

\displaystyle\star R^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\star R_{i}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\star L_{i}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=\star L^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times},

we obtain (
8.3
).

For each
i=1,\ldots,k
, let
\widehat{R}_{i}=\pi\circ R_{i}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, where
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is the projection
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
. Then, for each
i
, we have that
\det R_{i}=(\det\widehat{R}_{i})c_{i}
. Let also
\widehat{R}=(\widehat{R}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{R}_{k})\colon\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})^{k}
.

Since
\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{i}\geqslant c_{i}
and
\det R_{i}\geqslant 0
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, we have that

\displaystyle\lVert R\rVert^{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\left(\star R^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}\right)=(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{i=1}^{k}\det R_{i}=(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\det\widehat{R}_{i}

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\lambda_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\det\widehat{R}_{i}=(1+\varepsilon)\lambda_{1}(\star\widehat{R}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})^{k}}^{\times})\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\lambda_{1}\lVert\widehat{R}\rVert^{n-1}.

Since
\lVert\widehat{R}\rVert\leqslant\lVert R\rVert
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\geqslant\frac{\lVert R\rVert^{n}}{(1+\varepsilon)\lVert\widehat{R}\rVert^{n-1}}\geqslant\frac{\lVert R\rVert}{(1+\varepsilon)}=\frac{\lVert L\rVert}{1+\varepsilon}.

This concludes the proof of (
1
).

To prove (
8.2
), by precomposing
L
with an isometry, we may assume that
\lVert L_{1}\rVert=|L_{1}(e_{1})|
. Let now
L^{\prime}=(L_{2},\ldots,L_{k})\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k-1}
. Then, by (
1
),

\displaystyle|L^{\prime}(e_{1})|^{2}

\displaystyle=|L(e_{1})|^{2}-|L_{1}(e_{1})|^{2}\leqslant\lVert L\rVert^{2}-\lVert L_{1}\rVert^{2}\leqslant\left((1+\varepsilon)^{2}-1\right)\lVert L_{1}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(2+\varepsilon)\lVert L_{1}\rVert^{2}\leqslant 3\varepsilon\lVert L_{1}\rVert^{2}.

By Hadamard’s inequality,

\displaystyle\star(L^{\prime})^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k-1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{k}\star L_{j}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leqslant\sum_{j=2}^{k}|L_{j}(e_{1})|\cdots|L_{j}(e_{n})|

\displaystyle\leqslant(k-1)\sqrt{3\varepsilon}\lVert L_{1}\rVert\lVert L^{\prime}\rVert^{n-1}\leqslant(k-1)\sqrt{3\varepsilon}\lVert L\rVert^{n}.

Since

\star L^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}=\star L_{1}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}+\star(L^{\prime})^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k-1}}^{\times}\leqslant\star L_{1}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}+(k-1)\sqrt{3\varepsilon}\lVert L\rVert^{n}

and
(1+\varepsilon)(k-1)\leqslant k
, we have that

\lVert L_{1}\rVert^{n}\leqslant\lVert L\rVert^{n}\leqslant\frac{1+\varepsilon}{1-k\sqrt{3\varepsilon}}(\star L_{1}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})\leqslant(1+7k\sqrt{\varepsilon})\det L_{1}.

This proves (
8.2
).

Finally, to prove (
2
), let
i\in\{2,\ldots,k\}
and let
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a unit vector for which
\lVert L_{i}\rVert=|L_{i}(v)|
. Since
v
is a unit vector, we have that
|L_{1}(v)|
is at least the smallest singular value of
L_{1}
.
By (
8.2
),

|L_{1}(v)|\geqslant\frac{\det L_{1}}{\lVert L_{1}\rVert^{n-1}}=\frac{\det L_{1}}{\lVert L_{1}\rVert^{n}}\lVert L_{1}\rVert\geqslant\frac{1}{1+7k\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\lVert L_{1}\rVert\geqslant(1-7k\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lVert L_{1}\rVert,

and, using (
1
),

\displaystyle\lVert L_{i}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=|L_{i}(v)|^{2}\leqslant|L(v)|^{2}-|L_{1}(v)|^{2}\leqslant\lVert L\rVert^{2}-(1-7k\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{2}\lVert L_{1}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left((1+\varepsilon)^{2}-(1-7k\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{2}\right)\lVert L_{1}\rVert^{2}\leqslant 18k\sqrt{\varepsilon}\lVert L_{1}\rVert^{2}<25k\sqrt{\varepsilon}\lVert L\rVert^{2}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

In the following lemma, we record a quantitative estimate for the fact that, linear maps
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
close to conformal linear maps
L\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
satisfying
\lVert L\rVert^{n}=\star L^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
have small distortion.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
,
k\geqslant 1
, and
0<\nu<2^{-n}
. Let
L\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a linear map for which
\star L^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}=\lVert L\rVert^{n}
. Let
R\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a linear map satisfying

|R(e_{i})-L(e_{i})|\leqslant\frac{\nu}{\sqrt{n}}\lVert L\rVert

for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then
\lVert R\rVert\leqslant(1+\nu)\lVert L\rVert
and

\lVert R\rVert^{n}\leqslant\frac{(1+\nu)^{n}}{1-2^{n}\nu}\left(\star R^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}\right).

Proof.

On one hand, let
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which
|v|=1
. Then

\displaystyle|R(v)|

\displaystyle\leqslant|L(v)|+|R(v)-L(v)|\leqslant\lVert L\rVert+\sum_{i=1}^{n}|v_{i}||R(e_{i})-L(e_{i})|

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert L\rVert+\frac{\nu}{\sqrt{n}}\lVert L\rVert\sum_{i=1}^{n}|v_{i}|\leqslant\lVert L\rVert+\frac{\nu}{\sqrt{n}}\lVert L\rVert\sqrt{n}|v|

\displaystyle=(1+\nu)\lVert L\rVert.

Thus
\lVert R\rVert\leqslant(1+\nu)\lVert L\rVert
.

On the other hand, let
x_{i}=R(e_{i})-L(e_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then

\displaystyle\star R^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}(L(e_{1})+x_{1},\ldots,L(e_{n})+x_{n})

\displaystyle=\star L^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}+\sum_{j=1}^{2^{n}-1}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}(y_{j_{1}},\ldots,y_{j_{n}}),

where each
y_{j_{i}}
is either
y_{j_{i}}=L(e_{i})
or
y_{j_{i}}=x_{i}
and each
n
-tuple
(y_{j_{1}},\ldots,y_{j_{n}})
contains at least one
y_{j_{i}}=x_{i}
. Thus, for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,2^{n}-1\}
, we have by Hadamard’s inequality that

\left|\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}(y_{j_{1}},\ldots,y_{j_{n}})\right|\leqslant\prod_{i=1}^{n}|y_{j_{i}}|\leqslant\left(\frac{\nu}{\sqrt{n}}\lVert L\rVert\right)(\lVert L\rVert^{n-1})\leqslant\nu\lVert L\rVert^{n}.

We obtain that

\star R^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}\geqslant(1-2^{n}\nu)\lVert L\rVert^{n}

and the claim follows.
∎

9.
Approximation of quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves of small distortion with piecewise linear curves

In this section we prove a version of Kopylov’s approximation theorem for quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curves. Theorem
1.11
, stated in the introduction, is a direct consequence of Theorem
9.1
below. Since the piecewise linear approximation is given almost canonically, we begin by restating the result.

Let
n\geqslant 3
and let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{Q}_{j}(E)
be the collection of dyadic cubes
Q
with side length
2^{-j}
for which the intersection
Q\cap E
is non-empty. Here
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a dyadic cube with side length
2^{-j}
if
Q=2^{-j}(v+[0,1]^{n})
for some
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Further, let
\Delta_{j}(E)
be the collection of
n
-simplicies obtained by taking barycentric subdivisions of cubes in
\mathcal{Q}_{j}(E)
and let

D_{j}(E)=\bigcup_{\Delta\in\Delta_{j}(E)}\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Given a map
F\colon\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
, we denote
\widehat{F}_{j}^{E}
the piecewise affine map
\widehat{F}_{j}^{E}\colon D_{j}(E)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
satisfying

\widehat{F}_{j}^{E}(v_{i})=F(v_{i})

for
i=0,\ldots,n
whenever the
n
-simplex
[v_{0},\ldots,v_{n}]
belongs to
\Delta_{j}(E)
. We say that
\widehat{F}_{j}^{E}
is the
piecewise affine map associated to
F
on
\Delta_{j}(E)
.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,
k\geqslant 1
, and
\varepsilon>0
. There exists
\delta=\delta(n,k,\varepsilon)>0
for the following. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain, let
U\Subset\Omega
be a compactly contained subdomain, and let
F\colon\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a
(1+\delta)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve. Then there exists a sequence
(j_{m})
so that for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
j\geqslant j_{m}
, the piecewise affine map
(\widehat{F}_{j}^{\overline{U}})|_{U}\colon U\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
is a
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve satisfying

\sup_{U}|F-\widehat{F}_{j}^{\overline{U}}|<\frac{1}{m}.

We divide the proof of Theorem
9.1
into three parts. First, we recall classical estimates for Möbius transformations on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to their derivatives. Second, we prove results regarding affine maps agreeing with a quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve of small distortion at the vertices of a simplex. Finally, we prove Theorem
9.1
using these results.

9.1.
Möbius estimates

We begin by recalling two classical estimates for Möbius transformations. As they have a crucial role in the proof of Theorem
9.1
, we give short proofs. The first lemma is a distortion estimate in terms of the derivative.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
and let
\varphi\colon B^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a restriction of a Möbius transformation
{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\to{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
. Then

(9.1)

\frac{1}{3}\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert\leqslant\sup_{x\in B^{n}(\frac{1}{2})}|\varphi(x)-\varphi(0)|\leqslant\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case that
\varphi
is a restriction of a Möbius transformation, also denoted
\varphi
, satisfying
\varphi^{-1}(\infty)\neq\infty
.

Let
(u_{m})
be a sequence in
B^{n}
tending to the origin. Then

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{|\varphi(u_{m})-\varphi(0)|}{|u_{m}|}=\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert.

Let
x\in B^{n}(\frac{1}{2})
. Then, for each
m
, we have

|x-p|\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(0)|}{|x|}=|u_{m}-p|\frac{|\varphi(u_{m})-\varphi(0)|}{|u_{m}|},

where
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B^{n}
is the pole of
\varphi
; see e.g.
[
19
, (7)]
.

To obtain the second inequality in (
9.1
), it suffices to observe that

|\varphi(x)-\varphi(0)|=\frac{|x||p|}{|x-p|}\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert\leqslant\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert.

To obtain the first inequality in (
9.1
), it suffices to observe that

|\varphi(p/(2|p|))-\varphi(0)|=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|p|}{|p/(2|p|)-p|}\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert\geqslant\frac{1}{3}\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert.

∎

In the following lemma, we record the fact that, for a Möbius transformation
\varphi\colon B^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the second order error term
x\mapsto|\varphi(x)-\varphi(0)-D\varphi(0)x|
is controlled by
x\mapsto\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert|x|^{2}
near the origin.

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
and let
\varphi\colon B^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a restriction of a Möbius transformation
{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\to{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
. Then there exists
C=C(n)>0
for which

|\varphi(x)-\varphi(0)-D\varphi(0)x|\leqslant C\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert|x|^{2}

for
x\in B^{n}(\frac{1}{2})
.

Proof.

Let
x\in B^{n}(\frac{1}{2})
. Then there exists
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in B^{n}(\frac{1}{2})
satisfying

\varphi(x)=\varphi(0)+D\varphi(0)x+\frac{1}{2}(x^{t}H_{1}x,\ldots,x^{t}H_{n}x),

where
H_{i}=(h_{\ell j}^{i})=D^{2}\varphi_{i}(\xi_{i})
for each
i
. We obtain

|\varphi(x)-\varphi(0)-D\varphi(0)x|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x^{t}H_{i}x|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert H_{i}\rVert

by the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality. For each
i
, we have that
\lVert H_{i}\rVert\leqslant C(n)\max_{\ell,j}|h_{\ell j}^{i}|
, so it suffices to show that

\max_{\ell,j}|h_{\ell j}^{i}|\leqslant C(n)\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert.

Since
\varphi
is a Möbius transformation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the pole of
\varphi
is outside the unit ball
B^{n}
, there exists an orthogonal matrix
A=(a_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
p=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B
,
c>0
, and
\zeta\in\{0,2\}
satisfying

\varphi(y)=b+\frac{cA(y-p)}{|y-p|^{\zeta}},

for
y\in B^{n}
; see e.g.
[
9
]
and
[
21
]
. If
\zeta=0
, the claim is immediate, so suppose that
\zeta=2
. Then we also have that
\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert=\frac{c}{|p|^{2}}
.

Now, for
i,j=1,\ldots,n
and for
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in B^{n}
, we have

\frac{\partial\varphi_{i}}{\partial y_{j}}(y)=\frac{ca_{ij}}{|y-p|^{2}}-\frac{2c(y_{j}-p_{j})}{|y-p|^{4}}s_{i}(y),

where
s_{i}(y)=\sum_{m=1}^{n}a_{im}(y_{m}-p_{m})
. Thus, for
i,j,\ell=1,\ldots,n
and
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in B^{n}
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi_{i}}{\partial y_{\ell}\partial y_{j}}(y)=

\displaystyle ca_{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{\ell}}\left(\frac{1}{|y-p|^{2}}\right)-2c\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{\ell}}\left(\frac{(y_{j}-p_{j})}{|y-p|^{4}}s_{i}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2ca_{ij}\frac{y_{\ell}-p_{\ell}}{|y-p|^{4}}-\frac{2c}{|y-p|^{4}}\left(\delta_{\ell j}s_{i}(y)+a_{i\ell}(y_{j}-p_{j})\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2c}{|y-p|^{4}}\frac{4(y_{j}-p_{j})(y_{\ell}-p_{\ell})}{|y-p|^{2}}s_{i}(y),

where
\delta_{\ell j}
is the Kronecker delta.

Since
D^{2}\varphi_{i}(\xi_{i})=(h_{\ell j}^{i})
and
\xi_{i}\in B^{n}(\frac{1}{2})
for each
i
, it follows that we have

|h_{\ell j}^{i}|\leqslant C(n)\frac{c}{|\xi_{i}-p|^{3}}

for each
i
,
j
, and
\ell
. On the other hand, since
\frac{1}{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}|p|\leqslant|\xi_{i}-p|
for each
i
, we have that

\frac{c}{|\xi_{i}-p|^{3}}=\frac{1}{|\xi_{i}-p|}\frac{c}{|\xi_{i}-p|^{2}}\leqslant 2\cdot 4\frac{c}{|p|^{2}}=8\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert.

This concludes the proof.
∎

9.2.
Affine approximation of a quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve in a simplex

The following lemma is similar to
[
24
, Lemma 6]
. It states that for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
for which a
(1+\delta)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve
F\colon B^{n}(2)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
, normalized to satisfy
F(0)=0
and
\sup_{B^{n}}|F|=1
, can be approximated near the origin by a linear map
P\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
, which is also a
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve. In this case, we also obtain a uniform bound for the operator norm of
P
, which will be useful later.

Lemma 9.4
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,
k\geqslant 1
, and
\varepsilon>0
. There exists
t_{0}=t_{0}(n,\varepsilon)\in(0,1/2)
and
\delta=\delta(n,k,\varepsilon)>0
for the following. Let
F\colon B^{n}(2)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a
(1+\delta)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve satisfying
F(0)=0
and
\sup_{B^{n}}|F|=1
. Let
P\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be the linear map defined by
P(e_{i})=\frac{1}{t_{0}}F(t_{0}e_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then
P
is a
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve and
\lVert P\rVert\leqslant 12
.

Proof.

Let
C_{0}=C_{0}(n)>0
be as in Lemma
9.3
. Let
0<\nu<2^{-n}
for which

\frac{(1+\nu)^{n}}{1-2^{n}\nu}<1+\varepsilon

and let
t_{0}\in(0,1/2)
satisfy
2^{13}\cdot 7C_{0}t_{0}\leqslant\frac{\nu}{\sqrt{n}}
. Let then
\delta>0
for which
\tau^{n,k}(\delta)\leqslant\min(2^{-14},C_{0}t_{0}^{2}/2)
, where
\tau^{n,k}
is the gauge function defined in Section
7
.

Let
F\colon B^{n}(2)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a
(1+\delta)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve satisfying
F(0)=0
and
\sup_{B^{n}}|F|=1
. Let
P\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be the linear map which is defined by
P(e_{i})=\frac{1}{t_{0}}F(t_{0}e_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

By Proposition
7.4
, there exists a map
\varphi\in\mathscr{F}^{n,k}(B^{n})
for which
\varphi(0)=0
and

\sup_{B^{n}(\frac{1}{2})}|F-\varphi|\leqslant 2\tau^{n,k}(\delta).

Now
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k})\colon B^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
is a map for which either
\varphi\equiv 0
or
\varphi=(0,\ldots,0,\varphi_{i_{0}},0,\ldots,0)
, where
\varphi_{i_{0}}\colon B^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a restriction of a Möbius transformation
{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\to{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
. It follows that we have

\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert^{n}=\star(D\varphi(0))^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}.

Then, by Lemma
8.2
, it suffices to show that

|P(e_{i})-(D\varphi(0))(e_{i})|\leqslant\frac{\nu}{\sqrt{n}}\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert

for
i=1,\ldots,n
to obtain that
P
is a
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve and

\lVert P\rVert\leqslant(1+\nu)\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert\leqslant 2\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert.

Since
\tau^{n,k}(\delta)\leqslant 10^{-3}
, we obtain that

\sup_{B^{n}(\frac{1}{2})}|F|\geqslant 2^{-12}

by applying Proposition
7.5
in the ball
B^{n}(2)
with
\rho=\frac{1}{4}
. Further, the estimate
\tau^{n,k}(\delta)\leqslant 2^{-14}
yields that

\sup_{B^{n}(\frac{1}{2})}|\varphi|\leqslant 2\tau^{n,k}(\delta)+1\leqslant 2

and

\sup_{B^{n}(\frac{1}{2})}|\varphi|\geqslant 2^{-12}-2\tau^{n,k}(\delta)\geqslant 2^{-13}.

We obtain that there exists a unique Möbius transformation coordinate map
\varphi_{i_{0}}\colon B^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

2^{-13}\leqslant\sup_{B^{n}(\frac{1}{2})}|\varphi_{i_{0}}|\leqslant 2.

Then (
9.1
) yields the estimate

2^{-13}\leqslant\lVert D\varphi_{i_{0}}(0)\rVert\leqslant 6.

Now, for each
i
, we have

\displaystyle|P(e_{i})-(D\varphi(0))(e_{i})|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t_{0}}|F(t_{0}e_{i})-(D\varphi(0))(t_{0}e_{i})|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{t_{0}}\left(|F(t_{0}e_{i})-\varphi(t_{0}e_{i})|+|\varphi(t_{0}e_{i})-(D\varphi(0))(t_{0}e_{i})|\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{t_{0}}\left(2\tau^{n,k}(\delta)+|\varphi_{i_{0}}(t_{0}e_{i})-(D\varphi_{i_{0}}(0))(t_{0}e_{i})|\right).

Further, by Lemma
9.3
, we have that

|\varphi_{i_{0}}(t_{0}e_{i})-(D\varphi_{i_{0}}(0))(t_{0}e_{i})|\leqslant C_{0}\lVert D\varphi_{i_{0}}(0)\rVert t_{0}^{2}\leqslant 6C_{0}t_{0}^{2}.

Combining these estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle|P(e_{i})-(D\varphi(0))(e_{i})|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{13}\frac{2\tau^{n,k}(\delta)+6C_{0}t_{0}^{2}}{t_{0}}\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{13}\frac{C_{0}t_{0}^{2}+6C_{0}t_{0}^{2}}{t_{0}}\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert=2^{13}\cdot 7C_{0}t_{0}\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\nu}{\sqrt{n}}\lVert D\varphi(0)\rVert,

since
\tau^{n,k}(\delta)\leqslant C_{0}t_{0}^{2}/2
and
2^{13}\cdot 7C_{0}t_{0}\leqslant\frac{\nu}{\sqrt{n}}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

We obtain the following proposition from Lemma
9.4
by scaling.

Proposition 9.5
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,
k\geqslant 1
, and
\varepsilon>0
. Let
t_{0}=t_{0}(n,\varepsilon)\in(0,1/2)
and
\delta=\delta(n,k,\varepsilon)>0
be as in Lemma
9.4
. Let
F\colon B^{n}(2/t_{0})\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a
(1+\delta)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve and let
P\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be the affine map defined by
P(0)=F(0)
and
P(e_{i})=F(e_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then
P
is a
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve and

\sup_{x\in\Delta}|P(0)-P(x)|\leqslant 12t_{0}\sup_{x\in B^{n}(1/t_{0})}|F(0)-F(x)|,

where
\Delta=[0,e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}]\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the standard
n
-simplex.

Proof.

We may assume that

m=\sup_{x\in B^{n}(1/t_{0})}|F(0)-F(x)|>0.

Let
\Psi\colon B^{n}(2)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be the map
x\mapsto\frac{1}{m}(F(x/t_{0})-F(0))
. Then, the map
\Psi
is a
(1+\delta)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve for which
\Psi(0)=0
and
\sup_{B^{n}}|\Psi|=1
. Let
\tilde{P}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be the linear map defined by
\tilde{P}(e_{i})=\frac{1}{t_{0}}\Psi(t_{0}e_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Now, by Lemma
9.4
,
\tilde{P}
is a
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve and
\lVert\tilde{P}\rVert\leqslant 12
. Since
P=F(0)+mt_{0}\tilde{P}
, we obtain that
P
is a
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve and

\sup_{x\in\Delta}|P(0)-P(x)|=mt_{0}\sup_{x\in\Delta}|\tilde{P}(x)|\leqslant mt_{0}\lVert\tilde{P}\rVert\sup_{x\in\Delta}|x|\leqslant 12mt_{0}.

∎

Having Proposition
9.5
at our disposal, we are ready to prove Theorem
9.1
.

Proof of Theorem
9.1
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,
k\geqslant 1
, and
\varepsilon>0
. Let
t_{0}=t_{0}(n,\varepsilon)\in(0,1/2)
and
\delta=\delta(n,k,\varepsilon)>0
be as in Lemma
9.4
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain, let
U\Subset\Omega
be a compactly contained subdomain, and let
F\colon\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a
(1+\delta)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve. Let also
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
V\Subset\Omega
be a compactly contained subdomain satisfying
\overline{U}\subset V
and let
\eta_{m}>0
for which
\eta_{m}(1+12t_{0})<\frac{1}{m}
. Since
F
is uniformly continuous on
\overline{V}
, there exists
\theta_{m}>0
for which
|F(x)-F(y)|<\eta_{m}
for
x,y\in V
satisfying
|x-y|<\theta_{m}
. Fix
j_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
having the property that
\frac{1}{t_{0}}2^{-(j_{m}+1)}<\theta_{m}
and, for every
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{j_{m}}(\overline{U})
, we have
\operatorname{diam}Q<\theta_{m}
and
{\operatorname{dist}\,}(Q,\partial V)>\frac{1}{t_{0}}2^{-j_{m}}
. Let now
j\geqslant j_{m}
and let
\widehat{F}_{j}^{\overline{U}}\colon D_{j}(\overline{U})\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be the piecewise affine map associated to
F
on
\Delta_{j}(\overline{U})
.

Let
\Delta\in\Delta_{j}(\overline{U})
. Since
\Delta
is an
n
-simplex, there exists
v_{0},\ldots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which
\Delta=[v_{0},\ldots,v_{n}]
. Let
P\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be the affine map defined by
P(v_{i})=F(v_{i})
for
i=0,\ldots,n
. Then
P|_{\Delta}=(\widehat{F}_{j}^{\overline{U}})|_{\Delta}
and it suffices to show that
P
is a
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve which is uniformly close to
F
in
\Delta
.

Since the
n
-simplex
[v_{0},\ldots,v_{n}]
is obtained from the barycentric subdivision of some cube
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{j}(\overline{U})
, there exists an index
i_{0}\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
and a permutation
\sigma\colon(\{0,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{i_{0}\})\to\{1,\ldots,n\}
with the property that
v_{i}=v_{i_{0}}\pm 2^{-(j+1)}e_{\sigma(i)}
for
i\neq i_{0}
. Let
B=B^{n}(v_{i_{0}},2^{-(j+1)}/t_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a ball. Since
v_{i_{0}}\in Q
and
{\operatorname{dist}\,}(Q,\partial V)>\frac{1}{t_{0}}2^{-j}
, we have that
2B\subset V
.

Let
A\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the affine map defined by
A(0)=v_{i_{0}}
and
A(e_{\sigma(i)})=v_{i}
for
i\neq i_{0}
. Then applying Proposition
9.5
to the map
F\circ A
yields that
P
is a
(1+\varepsilon)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve for which

\sup_{x\in\Delta}|P(v_{i_{0}})-P(x)|\leqslant 12t_{0}\sup_{x\in B}|F(v_{i_{0}})-F(x)|.

Now, since
\Delta\cup B\subset V
and
|x-v_{i_{0}}|\leqslant\max(\operatorname{diam}Q,2^{-(j+1)}/t_{0})<\theta_{m}
for
x\in\Delta\cup B
, we have that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\Delta}|F(x)-P(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{x\in\Delta}|F(x)-F(v_{i_{0}})|+\sup_{x\in\Delta}|P(v_{i_{0}})-P(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{x\in\Delta}|F(x)-F(v_{i_{0}})|+12t_{0}\sup_{x\in B}|F(v_{i_{0}})-F(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\eta_{m}(1+12t_{0})<\frac{1}{m}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

10.
Quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{n}}^{\times}
-curves of small distortion have a quasiregular dominating coordinate map

In this section, we prove the following theorem, which is the Euclidean and quantitative counterpart of Theorem
1.7
.

Theorem 10.1
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
,
k\geqslant 1
and let
\varepsilon>0
. There exists
\delta=\delta(n,k,\varepsilon)>0
for the following. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain and let
F=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})\colon\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a non-constant
(1+\delta)
-quasiregular
\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}
-curve. Then there exists a unique index
i_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
for which the coordinate map
f_{i_{0}}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is non-constant and
(1+7k\sqrt{\varepsilon})
-quasiregular. Moreover, for almost every
x\in\Omega
, we have that

\lVert DF(x)\rVert\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)(1+7k\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{n}}\lVert Df_{i_{0}}(x)\rVert

and

\lVert Df_{i}(x)\rVert\leqslant 5\sqrt{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}(1+\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{n}}\lVert DF(x)\rVert

for
i\neq i_{0}
.

First, we prove a result similar to Proposition
8.1
for piecewise affine maps.

Proposition 10.2
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
and
k\geqslant 1
. There exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,k)>0
for the following. Let
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
be
n
-simplicies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which the intersection
\Delta=\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}
is an
(n-1)
-simplex. Let
P=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})\colon(\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2})\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a piecewise affine map for which
P_{|\Delta_{j}}
is non-constant for
j=1,2
and

\lVert DP\rVert^{n}\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\left(\star P^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}\right)

in
(\mathrm{int}(\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}))\setminus\Delta
. Then there exists
i_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
for which
P_{i_{0}}
is
(1+7k\sqrt{\varepsilon})
-quasiregular in
\mathrm{int}(\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2})
. Furthermore, for
i\neq i_{0}
, we have
\lVert DP_{i_{0}}\rVert\geqslant\lVert DP\rVert/(1+\varepsilon)
and
\lVert DP_{i}\rVert\leqslant 5\sqrt{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\lVert DP\rVert
in
(\mathrm{int}(\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}))\setminus\Delta
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1/(100k))
satisfy
(1+\varepsilon)(1+7k\sqrt{\varepsilon})<3/2
and
5\sqrt{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}<1/2
.

Let
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
be
n
-simplicies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which the intersection
\Delta=\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}
is an
(n-1)
-simplex and let
P=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})\colon(\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2})\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
be a piecewise affine map for which
P_{|\Delta_{j}}
is non-constant for
j=1,2
and

\lVert DP\rVert^{n}\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\left(\star P^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}}^{\times}\right)

in
(\mathrm{int}(\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}))\setminus\Delta
. By translation, we may assume that

and that

.

Since

is linear and non-constant in

, there exists a non-trivial linear map

for which

for

. Similarly, there exists a non-trivial linear map

satisfying
P(x)=R(x)
for
x\in\Delta_{2}
. Since
\varepsilon<1/(100k)
, we obtain indices
i_{L},i_{R}\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
as in in Proposition
8.1
for

and
R
, respectively. It suffices to show that
i_{L}=i_{R}
.

Suppose towards contradiction that
i_{L}\neq i_{R}
. Let
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the linear subspace spanned by
\Delta
and let
\sigma_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant\sigma_{n}
be the singular values of

. Then,
\lVert L_{i_{L}}\rVert=\sigma_{n}
and
\lVert(L_{i_{L}})|_{V}\rVert\geqslant\sigma_{1}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\lVert L_{i_{L}}\rVert^{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+7k\sqrt{\varepsilon})\det L_{i_{L}}\leqslant(1+7k\sqrt{\varepsilon})\prod_{j=1}^{n}\sigma_{j}\leqslant(1+7k\sqrt{\varepsilon})\sigma_{1}\sigma_{n}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+7k\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lVert(L_{i_{L}})|_{V}\rVert\lVert L_{i_{L}}\rVert^{n-1}.

Thus,

\lVert L\rVert\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\lVert L_{i_{L}}\rVert\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)(1+7k\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lVert(L_{i_{L}})|_{V}\rVert\leqslant\frac{3}{2}\lVert(L_{i_{L}})|_{V}\rVert.

On the other hand, since
i_{L}\neq i_{R}
, we have

\lVert(R_{i_{L}})|_{V}\rVert\leqslant\lVert R_{i_{L}}\rVert\leqslant 5\sqrt{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\lVert R\rVert\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\lVert R\rVert.

Since
L|_{\Delta}=R|_{\Delta}
and both maps
L
and
R
are linear, we have that
L|_{V}=R|_{V}
. Then, combining the obtained estimates yields

\lVert L\rVert\leqslant\frac{3}{2}\lVert(L_{i_{L}})|_{V}\rVert=\frac{3}{2}\lVert(R_{i_{L}})|_{V}\rVert\leqslant\frac{3}{4}\lVert R\rVert.

Since
\lVert R\rVert>0
, this implies that
\lVert L\rVert<\lVert R\rVert
. Similarly, we obtain that
\lVert R\rVert<\lVert L\rVert
. This contradiction yields the claim.
∎

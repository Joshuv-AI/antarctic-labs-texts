An algebraic approach to asymptotics of the number of unlabelled bicolored graphs
A. Salch

Abstract
. We define and study two structures associated to permutation groups: Dirichlet characters on permutation groups, and the “cycle form,” a bilinear form on the group algebras of permutation groups. We use Dirichlet characters and the cycle form to find a new upper bound on the number of unlabelled bicolored graphs with p p red vertices and q q blue vertices. We use this bound to calculate the asymptotic growth rate of the number of such graphs as p , q → ∞ p,q\rightarrow\infty , answering a 1973 question of Harrison in the case where q − p q-p is fixed. As an application, we show that, in an asymptotic sense, “most” elements of the power set P ⁡ ( { 1 , … , p } × { 1 , … , q } ) P(\{1,\dots,p\}\times\{1,\dots,q\}) are in free Σ p × Σ q \Sigma_{p}\times\Sigma_{q} -orbits.

We develop a few basic properties of the cycle form. Whenever convenient, we will restrict to the case where the permutation groups are symmetric groups.

We begin by showing that the cycle form is degenerate. Usually one wants bilinear forms to be nondegenerate, but the degenerateness of the cycle form is actually very useful, and in Lemma

is in the radical of the cycle form. That is, for any
\beta
in any permutation group
H
, we have

(9)

\displaystyle\langle(1-\alpha)(1-\alpha^{\prime}),\beta\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

Proof.

Since
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
are disjoint, the number of fixed points of
\alpha\alpha^{\prime}
is equal to the number of fixed points of
\alpha
minus the number of
non
-fixed points of
\alpha^{\prime}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle c_{1}(\alpha\alpha^{\prime})

\displaystyle=c_{1}(\alpha)-(p-c_{1}(\alpha^{\prime})).

Hence we have

\displaystyle\langle\alpha\alpha^{\prime},\beta\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,s}\gcd(r,s)c_{r}(\alpha\alpha^{\prime})c_{s}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r>1}\sum_{s}\gcd(r,s)(c_{r}(\alpha)+c_{r}(\alpha^{\prime}))c_{s}(\beta)+\sum_{s}(c_{1}(\alpha)+c_{1}(\alpha^{\prime})-p)c_{s}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle+\langle\alpha^{\prime},\beta\rangle-\langle 1,\beta\rangle,

and (
9
) follows.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
p,q
be positive integers, and let
\alpha,\beta
be elements of the symmetric groups
\Sigma_{p}
and
\Sigma_{q}
, respectively.
Then we have an equality

(10)

\displaystyle\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}(\alpha)\langle\gamma_{j},\beta\rangle+(1-c(\alpha))\cdot p\cdot c(\beta),

where
\gamma_{j}
is any
j
-cycle in
\Sigma_{p}
.

Proof.

Write
\alpha
as a product of disjoint cycles,
\alpha=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\dots\alpha_{c(\alpha)}
, including singleton cycles. Apply Lemma
3.4
repeatedly:

\displaystyle\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha_{1},\beta\rangle+\langle\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\dots\alpha_{c(\alpha)},\beta\rangle-\langle 1,\beta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha_{1},\beta\rangle+\langle\alpha_{2},\beta\rangle+\langle\alpha_{3}\dots\alpha_{c(\alpha)},\beta\rangle-2\langle 1,\beta\rangle

\displaystyle=\dots

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{c(\alpha)}\langle\alpha_{k},\beta\rangle-(c(\alpha)-1)\langle 1,\beta\rangle,

which is equal to the right-hand side of (
10
).
∎

Our next task is to bound the value of
\langle 1-\alpha,\beta\rangle
.
The bound
\langle 1-\alpha,\beta\rangle\geq 0
is straightforward to see, and when
\ell
is prime, it is a trivial consequence of (
8
).
A more interesting bound is obtained as follows. Suppose that
\ell
is a positive integer. A quantity of basic interest is the difference
c(\beta)-\frac{q}{\ell}
, since it is positive if and only if the average length of a cycle in
\beta
, including length
1
cycles, is greater than
\ell
. One of the fundamental properties of the cycle form is that it is bounded by the quantity
c(\beta)-\frac{q}{\ell}
, in the following sense:

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\ell
be a positive integer, and let
\gamma_{\ell}\in\Sigma_{p}
be an
\ell
-cycle. Then, for all
\beta\in\Sigma_{q}
, we have

(11)

\displaystyle\langle 1-\gamma_{\ell},\beta\rangle\geq(\ell-1)\left(c(\beta)-\frac{q}{\ell}\right).

Proof.

Unpacking definitions, we have equalities

\displaystyle\left((\ell-1)\left(c(\beta)-\frac{q}{\ell}\right)\right)-\langle 1-\gamma_{\ell},\beta\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{s}(p-\ell+\gcd(s,\ell)-p)c_{s}(\beta)\right)+\left(\sum_{s}(\ell-1)c_{s}(\beta)\right)-\frac{q(\ell-1)}{\ell}

(12)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{s}\left(\gcd(s,\ell)-1\right)c_{s}(\beta)\right)-\frac{q(\ell-1)}{\ell}.

We claim that the sum
\sum_{s}\left(\gcd(s,\ell)-1\right)c_{s}(\beta)
is less than or equal to
\frac{q(\ell-1)}{\ell}
. The way to see this is to regard it as an optimization question: the numbers
c_{1}(\beta),c_{2}(\beta),\dots,c_{q}(\beta)
must be nonnegative
integers
with the property that

\displaystyle\sum_{j}j\cdot c_{j}(\beta)

\displaystyle=q.

Whichever choice of such integers
c_{1}(\beta),c_{2}(\beta),\dots,c_{q}(\beta)
maximizes the sum
\sum_{s}\left(\gcd(s,\ell)-1\right)c_{s}(\beta)
, it can be no greater than the greatest possible value of the sum
\sum_{s}\left(\gcd(s,\ell)-1\right)x_{s}
where
x_{1},\dots,x_{q}
are required only to be nonnegative
real numbers
satisfying
\sum_{j}j\cdot x_{j}=q
. It is elementary to verify that the choice of real numbers
x_{1},\dots,x_{q}
satisfying those constraints, and maximizing the value of
\sum_{s}\left(\gcd(s,\ell)-1\right)x_{s}
, is the choice given by letting
x_{\ell}=q/\ell
and letting
x_{s}=0
for all
s\neq\ell
. In that case, the sum
\sum_{s}\left(\gcd(s,\ell)-1\right)x_{s}
is equal to
\frac{q(\ell-1)}{\ell}
.

Hence
\sum_{s}\left(\gcd(s,\ell)-1\right)c_{s}(\beta)
can be no greater than
\frac{q(\ell-1)}{\ell}
, i.e., (
12
) is negative. The bound (
11
) follows.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
p
be a positive integer, and let
\alpha\in\Sigma_{p}
. Then we have an inequality

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{c_{j}(\alpha)}{j}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c(\alpha)-p}{2}.

Proof.

By elementary algebra, (
13
) is equivalent to the inequality
\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}(\alpha)(1-\frac{2}{j})\leq p
, which is satisfied since
p\geq c(\alpha)=\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}(\alpha)
.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
p,q
be positive integers.
Let
B_{u}(p,q)
denote the set of unlabelled bicolored graphs with
p
red vertices and
q
blue vertices.
Then we have the inequality

(14)

\displaystyle\left|B_{u}(p,q)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{pq/2}((\chi_{1/2},\chi_{2^{q/2}})).

Proof.

(15)

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{p}}2^{\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{p}}2^{(1-c(\alpha))\langle 1,\beta\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}(\alpha)\langle\gamma_{j},\beta\rangle}

\displaystyle=2^{p\cdot c(\beta)}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{p}}2^{-p\cdot c(\alpha)\cdot c(\beta)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}(\alpha)\langle\gamma_{j},\beta\rangle}

(16)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p\cdot c(\beta)}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{p}}2^{-p\cdot c(\alpha)\cdot c(\beta)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}(\alpha)\left(\langle 1,\beta\rangle-(j-1)(c(\beta)-q/j)\right)}

\displaystyle=2^{p\cdot c(\beta)}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{p}}2^{(c(\beta)+q)\cdot c(\alpha)}2^{-c(\beta)\sum_{j=1}^{p}j\cdot c_{j}(\alpha)}2^{-q\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}(\alpha)/j}

(17)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p\cdot c(\beta)}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{p}}2^{c(\alpha)\cdot(c(\beta)-q/2)}2^{p(q/2-c(\beta))}

\displaystyle=2^{p\cdot c(\beta)}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{p}}2^{(c(\alpha)-p)(c(\beta)-q/2)},

with (
15
),(
16
), and (
17
) due to Lemmas
3.5
,
3.6
, and
3.7
, respectively.
Summing over
\beta
, we get

(18)

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{p}}\sum_{\beta\in\Sigma_{q}}2^{\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{pq/2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{p}}\sum_{\beta\in\Sigma_{q}}2^{c(\alpha)\cdot c(\beta)}(2^{-q/2})^{c(\alpha)}

(19)

\displaystyle=2^{pq/2}\cdot p!\cdot q!\cdot((\chi_{1/2},\chi_{2^{q/2}})),

and (
14
) follows, using (
1
).
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
k
be a nonnegative integer. Then
the number of bicolored graphs with
p
red vertices and
p+k
blue vertices grows asymptotically no faster than
\frac{2^{p(p+k)}}{p!(p+k)!}
.
That is,

(20)

\displaystyle\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left|B_{u}(p,p+k)\right|}{2^{p(p+k)}/(p!(p+k)!)}

\displaystyle\leq 1.

Proof.

Immediate from Theorem
3.8
and Proposition
2.7
.
∎

We point out that the word “nonnegative” can safely be dropped from the statement of Corollary
3.9
, since
\left|B_{u}(p+k,p)\right|=\left|B_{u}(p,p+k)\right|

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
f(p,q)
be the proportion of elements in the power set
P(\{1,\dots,p\}\times\{1,\dots q\})
which are members of free
\Sigma_{p}\times\Sigma_{q}
-orbits.
Let
k
be an integer. Then the limit
\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}f(p,p+k)
is equal to
1
.

Proof.

Consider the sizes of the orbits of the action of
\Sigma_{p}\times\Sigma_{q}
on
P(\{1,\dots,p\}\times\{1,\dots,q\})
. The size of each orbit is a divisor of
p!q!
. There are
\left|B_{u}(p,q)\right|
orbits, and the sum of their sizes is
2^{pq}
. If there are no free orbits (i.e., no orbits of size
p!q!
), then we must have
\frac{p!q!}{2}\cdot\left|B_{u}(p,q)\right|\geq 2^{pq}
. By a similar argument, if

\displaystyle\left(\left|B_{u}(p,q)\right|-r\right)\frac{p!q!}{2}

\displaystyle<2^{pq}-p!q!r,

then
P(\{1,\dots,p\}\times\{1,\dots,q\})
must have more than
r
free orbits.

Hence, in the case
q=p+k
, we have the inequality

\displaystyle f(p,p+k)

\displaystyle\geq 2-\frac{p!(p+k)!\left|B_{u}(p,p+k)\right|}{2^{p(p+k)}}.

In the limit, due to Corollary
3.9
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}f(p,p+k)

\displaystyle\geq 1.

Since
f(p,p+k)
is a ratio, it cannot be less than
1
. Hence the limit must be
1
, as claimed.
∎

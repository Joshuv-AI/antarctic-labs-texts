Existence of a weak solution to a nonlinear fluid-structure interaction problem with heat exchange
Václav Mácha, Boris Muha, Šárka Nečasová, Arnab Roy & \& Srđan Trifunović

Abstract
In this paper, we study a nonlinear interaction problem between a thermoelastic shell and a heat-conducting fluid. The shell is governed by linear thermoelasticity equations and encompasses a time-dependent domain which is filled with a fluid governed by the full Navier-Stokes-Fourier system. The fluid and the shell are fully coupled, giving rise to a novel nonlinear moving boundary fluid-structure interaction problem involving heat exchange. The existence of a weak solution is obtained by combining three approximation techniques – decoupling, penalization and domain extension. In particular, the penalization and the domain extension allow us to use the methods already developed for compressible fluids on moving domains. In such a way, the proof is more elegant and the analysis is drastically simplified. Let us stress that this is the first time the heat exchange in the context of fluid-structure interaction problems is considered.

1.2
Problem description

We consider the flow of heat conducting compressible fluid in a

The fluid domain is determined by elastic displacement which is in turn obtained by solving the linearized Koiter shell equation with the forcing coming from the fluid, i.e. the fluid and the structure are fully coupled and we consider a moving boundary problem.

The main novelties of the present work are:

1.

We consider a model where both the fluid and the structure conduct heat and there is heat coupling given through temperature continuity and entropy flux given in
(
1.10
)
and
(
1.12
)
. While, in the context of fluid-structure interaction, heat-conducting fluids have been studied in

]
and thermoelastic structures have been studied in

]
, to the best of our knowledge this is the first work that takes into account heat conduction of both components, and heat exchange between the components. One nice consequence of this approach is that we were able to prove that the plate temperature is positive which does not hold if one considers just a linear plate model without coupling it to the fluid with heat exchange.

2.

The fluid model we consider is from
[
15
]
, and the same result also holds for the model given in
(
1.37
)
below. This way, a wide range of physical cases are covered. In particular, we allow pressure laws which are not uniform and can change depending on the region of the

From the methodological point of view, we introduced a new approach to construct the approximate solutions. More precisely, we introduced a construction scheme that combines three approximation methods - decoupling, penalization and domain extension. This allows to decouple the problem and directly use sophisticated results and methods that are already developed to study compressible fluid on the moving domains

14

Remark 1.5
.

It seems that most thermoelastic plate models in literature (including our own) are based upon the assumptions that the temperature is small with respect to the reference temperature, and that the entropy depends linearly on temperature
[
34
, Chapter 1.5]
. While this makes sense for the stability analysis of plates, it is in contrast with the thermodynamical properties of our fluid, which has strictly positive and arbitrarly large temperature and has a component of the entropy which depends logarithmically on the temperature. A possible solution to this problem might be to derive a new nonlinear thermoelastic plate model specially for our interaction problem, under the assumption that the thermodynamical properties of the plate are similar to the ones of the fluid, and then study its interaction with a heat-conducting fluid. This is a topic for future research.

1.4
Outline of the proof and organization of the paper

The proof is split into three parts that correspond to various level of approximation in the construction of approximate solutions. Each step includes limiting procedure which uses standard tool for analysis of the compressible fluids equations.

Step 1

Existence of a weak solution to the extended problem.
In the first step we define the extended problem on a large domain
B
. The extension involves approximation parameters
\eta,\omega,\nu,\lambda
and follows approach from
[
31
,
13
]
. We also add pressure regularization with approximation parameter
\delta
. This regularization improves integrability of the pressure and by now standard in the analysis of compressible Navier-Stokes equations.
In this step we prove existence of a weak solution to the extended problem (see Definition
3.1

The solution defined in the above definition depends on the parameters
\omega,\eta,\nu,\lambda,\delta

Throughout the rest of the paper, we adapt the convention that we do write this explicit dependence on parameter only in the limiting procedure related to that parameter. When there is no possibility of confusion we will omit the parameters at all.

The advantage of this formulation is that the fluid equations are given on a time-independent domain
B
and therefore, following ideas from
[
31

[

[
47

\frac{1}{\Delta t}
. Moreover, the sub-problems “communicate” with each other via the penalization terms. Physically, in the approximate problems we make the interface transparent so the fluid can pass through it (see figure

\Gamma^{w}(t)
becomes impermeable, and therefore we obtain a weak solution to the extended problem from Definition

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

\displaystyle\rho_{|B\setminus\Omega^{w}(t)}(t,\cdot)\equiv 0,\quad\text{for a.a. }t\in(0,T).

Let

\displaystyle g_{0}=\begin{cases}0,\quad&\text{on }\Gamma\cup\partial B,\\
>0,\quad&\text{in }B\setminus\Omega,\\

and

\displaystyle\nabla g_{0}(X)=h(d(X))\mathbf{n}(\pi(X)),\quad h(x)\geq c>0

)
.
We introduce the function

Before we proceed, let us calculate on

\displaystyle\nabla g(t,X)=\nabla\big(g_{0}(\Phi^{-1}(t,X))\big)=\nabla g_{0}\big(\Phi^{-1}(t,X)\big)\cdot\nabla\Phi^{-1}(t,X)

(3.16)

Denoting

Solving the sub-problems

Here, we give a brief explanation how the each of the sub-problems is solved and how the estimates they satisfy are obtained.

First, in order to solve

Next, to solve

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\displaystyle\tau_{\Delta t}-T_{\Delta t}\tau_{\Delta t}}{\displaystyle\Delta t}\tilde{\psi}\to-\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\theta\partial_{t}\tilde{\psi}-\int_{\Gamma}\theta_{0}\tilde{\psi}(0,\cdot),\quad\text{as }\Delta t\to 0,\quad\forall\tilde{\psi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\Gamma).

Therefore we have:

\displaystyle-\delta\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\frac{\displaystyle\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}-T_{\Delta t}\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}}{\displaystyle\Delta t}\psi+(1-\delta)\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\partial_{t}w\partial_{t}\psi-(1-\delta)\int_{0}^{T}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}w\psi\to\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}w\partial_{t}\psi+\int_{\Gamma}w_{0}\psi(0,\cdot),

\displaystyle-\delta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\displaystyle\tau-T_{\Delta t}\tau}{\displaystyle\Delta t}\tilde{\psi}-(1-\delta)\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\theta\partial_{t}\tilde{\psi}+(1-\delta)\int_{\Gamma}\theta\tilde{\psi}\Big|_{0}^{T}\to\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\theta\partial_{t}\tilde{\psi}+\int_{\Gamma}\theta_{0}\tilde{\psi}(0,\cdot).

Now, from
(
3.37
)
,
w_{\Delta t}\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Gamma))\hookrightarrow C^{0,\alpha}(0,T;C^{0,1-2\alpha}(\Gamma))
,
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
, so

\displaystyle w_{\Delta t}\to w,\quad\text{in }C^{\frac{1}{4}}([0,T]\times\Gamma),

(3.50)

which implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\big((\Omega^{w_{\Delta t}}\setminus\Omega^{w})\cup(\Omega^{w}\setminus\Omega^{w_{\Delta t}})\big)\to 0,\quad\text{in }C^{\frac{1}{4}}([0,T]),

(3.51)

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &f_{\omega}^{w_{\Delta t}}\to f_{\omega}^{w},\quad\text{in }C^{\frac{1}{4}}([0,T]\times B),\\
&\chi_{\nu}^{w_{\Delta t}}\to\chi_{\nu}^{w},\quad\chi_{\eta}^{w_{\Delta t}}\to\chi_{\eta}^{w},\quad\text{in }L^{\infty}([0,T]\times B).\end{aligned}

(3.52)

The uniform bounds
(
3.41
)
and
(
3.42
)
directly give us

\displaystyle\rho_{\Delta t}\rightharpoonup\rho,\quad\text{weakly}^{*}\text{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{5}{3}}(B)),

\displaystyle\vartheta_{\Delta t}\rightharpoonup\vartheta,\quad\text{weakly}^{*}\text{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{4}(B)),

\displaystyle\vartheta_{\Delta t}\rightharpoonup\vartheta,\quad\text{weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(B)).

Now, the continuity equation
(
3.11
)
together with
(
3.39
)
yield

\displaystyle\rho_{\Delta t}\to\rho,\quad\text{ in }C_{w}([0,T];L^{\frac{5}{3}}(B)),

while
(
3.44
)
implies

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{u},\text{weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(B)),

(3.53)

which together give rise to

\displaystyle\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\rightharpoonup\rho\mathbf{u},\text{weakly}^{*}\text{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{5}{4}}(B))

by the compact imbedding of
L^{\frac{5}{3}}(B)
into
H^{-1}(B)
and the uniform bounds
\eqref{linftybound},\eqref{nablau}
. Thus, we infer that

\displaystyle\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\rightharpoonup\rho\mathbf{u},\text{weakly}^{*}\text{ in }C_{w}([0,T];L^{\frac{5}{4}}(B)).

Finally, by the momentum equation
(
3.12
)
,
(
3.45
)
and the compact imbedding of
L^{\frac{5}{4}}(B)
into
H^{-1}(B)
, one obtains

\displaystyle\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\to\rho\mathbf{u},\quad\text{in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(B)),

so by
(
3.53
)
and
(
3.46
)
one has

\displaystyle\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\rightharpoonup\rho\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u},\quad\text{in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{\frac{30}{29}}(B)).

3.8
Weak convergence of the pressure

Using the estimates
(
3.48
)
,
(
3.51
)
and
(
3.52
)
, one has

\displaystyle p_{\eta,\delta}^{w_{\Delta t}}(\rho_{\Delta t},\vartheta_{\Delta t})=p_{M}(\rho_{\Delta t},\vartheta_{\Delta t})+\frac{a_{\eta}}{3}\vartheta_{\Delta t}^{4}+\delta\rho_{\Delta t}^{\beta}\rightharpoonup\overline{p_{M}(\rho,\vartheta)}+\frac{a_{\eta}}{3}\overline{\vartheta^{4}}+\delta\overline{\rho^{\beta}},\quad\text{weakly in }L^{1}(O)

for any
O\Subset[0,T]\times\overline{B}
such that
O\cap\big([0,T]\times\Gamma^{w}(t))=\emptyset
, where bar notation from now on denotes the weak limit. The idea is to take a sequence of compact sets
\{O_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle O_{i}\cap\big([0,T]\times\Gamma^{w}(t)\big)=\emptyset,\quad O_{i}\subset O_{i+1},\quad O_{i}\to[0,T]\times B\text{ as }i\to\infty,

in order to obtain a weak limit of
p_{\eta,\delta}^{w_{\Delta t}}(\rho_{\Delta t},\vartheta_{\Delta t})
on the entire set
[0,T]\times B
. The key issue is that this convergence doesn’t exclude a possible concentration of
L^{1}
norm of
p_{\eta,\delta}^{w_{\Delta t}}(\rho_{\Delta t},\vartheta_{\Delta t})
as
\Delta t\to 0
that may result in a measure appearing at the moving boundary
\Gamma^{w}
, which is then felt by test functions that do not vanish at the moving boundary, unlike the case in
[
31
]
(comprehensible description of the idea may be found in
[
15
, Section 2.2.6]
). To deal with this issue, we can use the approach developed in
[
33
]
which was later adapted to the fluid-structure interaction framework in
[
5
]
:

Lemma 3.4
.

For any given
\kappa>0
, there exists a
(\Delta t)_{0}>0
and
A_{\kappa}\Subset(0,T)\times B
such that for all
\Delta t<(\Delta t)_{0}

\displaystyle A_{\kappa}\cap\big([0,T]\times\Gamma^{w_{\Delta t}}\big)=\emptyset,\quad\int_{((0,T)\times B)\setminus A_{\kappa}}p_{\eta,\delta}^{w_{\Delta t}}(\rho_{\Delta t},\vartheta_{\Delta t})\leq\kappa.

Proof.

The proof can be carried out in the same way as in
[
5
, Lemma 7.4]
, since all the fluid terms in the coupled momentum equation (except the pressure) are the same and the corresponding integrants are bounded in at least
L^{1}(0,T;L^{p}(B))
for some
p>1
. For reader’s convenience, we present the proof here.

The key idea is to construct a test function which has an arbitrarily large and positive divergence near the boundary
\Gamma^{w_{\Delta t}}
. First, let
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a function satisfying

\displaystyle 0\leq f\leq 1,\quad\text{supp}f\Subset(a_{\partial\Omega},b_{\partial\Omega}),\quad f=1\text{ in a neighbourhood of }[m,M],

where
m,M
are defined in
(
3.49
)
, and fix
K>0
small enough such that that

f\equiv 1\mbox{ on }\ [m-\frac{1}{K},M+\frac{1}{K}].

(3.54)

We define

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}(t,X):=f(d(X))\times\begin{cases}\min\big\{K(d(X)-w_{\Delta t}(t,\pi(X))),1\big\}\mathbf{n}(\pi(X)),\quad&\text{ on }[0,T]\times N_{a}^{b},\\
0,\quad&\text{ on }[0,T]\times(B\setminus N_{a}^{b}),\end{cases}

where
d,\pi
and
N_{a}^{b}
are defined in
\eqref{d},\eqref{p},\eqref{nab}
, and

\displaystyle S_{K}(t):=\{X:|d(X)-w(t,\pi(X))|<1/K\}.

Note that using the property (
3.54
) and the definition of the space
S_{K}(t)
, we have
f(d(X))=1
on
[0,T]\times S_{K}(t)
. Moreover, we have
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}=0
on
[0,T]\times\Gamma^{w}(t)
.

Now, denote by
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1}(X)
and
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{2}(X)
the orthogonal tangential vectors at point
X\in\Gamma
. We have on
[0,T]\times S_{K}(t)
:

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}(t,X)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mathbf{n}(\pi(X))}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}(t,X)\cdot\mathbf{n}(\pi(X))+\partial_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1}(\pi(X))}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}(t,X)\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1}(\pi(X))+\partial_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{2}(\pi(X))}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}(t,X)\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{2}(\pi(X))

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

The first term can be estimated in the following way

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\Big[\partial_{\mathbf{n}(\pi(X))}f(d(X))\Big]}_{=0,~\text{ on }[0,T]\times S_{K}(t)}\min\big\{K(d(X)-w_{\Delta t}(t,\pi(X))),1\big\}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{f(d(X))}_{=1,~\text{ on }[0,T]\times S_{K}(t)}\underbrace{\Big[\partial_{\mathbf{n}(\pi(X))}\min\big\{K(d(X)-w_{\Delta t}(t,\pi(X))),1\big\}}_{=K,~\text{ on }[0,T]\times S_{K}(t)}\Big]

\displaystyle=K,

where we have used the relations

\nabla d(X)=\mathbf{n}(\pi(X)),\quad\partial_{\mathbf{n}(\pi(X))}d(X)=1,\quad\partial_{\mathbf{n}(\pi(X))}\pi(X)=0.

We estimate the second and third terms by

\displaystyle|I_{i+1}|

\displaystyle\leq\big|\partial_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\pi(X))}\big[f(d(X))\min\big\{K(d(X)-w_{\Delta t}(t,\pi(X))),1\big\}\big]\underbrace{\mathbf{n}(\pi(X))\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\pi(X))}_{=0}\big|

\displaystyle\quad+\big|f(d(X))\min\big\{K(d(X)-w_{\Delta t}(t,\pi(X))),1\big\}\partial_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\pi(X))}\mathbf{n}(\pi(X))\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\pi(X))\big|

\displaystyle\leq c

for
i=1,2
. Thus,

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}\geq K-c,\quad\text{ on }[0,T]\times S_{K}(t).

Next, one has the following for all
p<\infty

\displaystyle||\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}||_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(S_{K}(t)))}\leq C\big(||\nabla w_{\Delta t}||_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\Gamma)}+1\big)\leq C(K+1),

(3.55)

\displaystyle||\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}||_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(B\setminus S_{K}(t)))}\leq C,

(3.56)

\displaystyle||\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}||_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times B)}\leq C,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}=0,\quad\text{on }[0,T]\times(B\setminus S_{K}(t)),

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}\leq C,\quad\text{ on }[0,T]\times(B\setminus S_{K}(t)),

\displaystyle||\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}||_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{p}(S_{K}(t)))}\leq C(K+1)||\partial_{t}w_{\Delta t}||_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\Gamma))}\leq C(K+1),

(3.57)

where
C
only depend on
f,p,\Gamma
and the initial energy. Now, choosing
(\boldsymbol{\varphi},\psi)=(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K},0)
in
(
3.12
)
gives us

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{S_{K}(t)}p_{\eta,\delta}^{w_{\Delta t}}(\rho_{\Delta t},\vartheta_{\Delta t})(K-c)\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{S_{K}(t)}p_{\eta,\delta}^{w_{\Delta t}}(\rho_{\Delta t},\vartheta_{\Delta t})(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K})

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B\setminus S_{K}(t)}p_{\eta,\delta}^{w_{\Delta t}}(\rho_{\Delta t},\vartheta_{\Delta t})(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K})

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle\int_{B}\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}-\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle\int_{B}(\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}):\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle\int_{B}\nabla\mathbb{S}_{\omega}^{w}(\vartheta_{\Delta t},\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}):\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}+\int_{0}^{T}\frac{d}{dt}\displaystyle\int_{B}\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}

The critical terms are the second and the third term on the right-hand side. We can bound the second term as:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B}\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{S_{K}(t)}\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}

\displaystyle\leq C||\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}||_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{5}{4}}(B))}||1||_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{6}(S_{K}(t)))}(K+1)\leq CK^{-\frac{1}{6}}(K+1),

(3.58)

where we have used the estimates (
3.45
),
(
3.57
)
and the fact that
\mathcal{M}(S_{K}(t))\leq CK^{-1}
. Next, from
\eqref{varphidtk1},\eqref{varphidtk2},\eqref{rhouu}
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle\int_{B}(\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}):\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle\int_{S_{K}(t)}(\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}):\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}+\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle\int_{B\setminus S_{K}(t)}(\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}):\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}

\displaystyle\leq C||\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}||_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{\frac{30}{29}}(B))}\Big[(K+1)||1||_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{30}(S_{K}(t)))}+1\Big]\leq C(K^{-\frac{1}{30}}(K+1)+1).

Noticing that the remaining terms can be bounded in a similar fashion, we conclude

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{S_{K}(t)}p_{\eta,\delta}^{w_{\Delta t}}(\rho_{\Delta t},\vartheta_{\Delta t})\leq C\frac{K^{-j}(K+1)+1}{K-c},

for a
j>0
, so we can always choose
K>0
large enough such that

\displaystyle C\frac{K^{-j}(K+1)+1}{K-c}\leq\kappa,

for any
\kappa>0
. Now, the proof follows for
A_{\kappa}=(0,T)\times(B\setminus S_{K}(t))
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

The above lemma also holds with assumptions
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=0
and
p(\rho,\vartheta)=\rho^{\gamma}+\rho\vartheta+\frac{a}{3}\vartheta^{4}
, for some
\gamma>12/7
.

Proof.

In this case, the only difference is in closing the estimate in
(
3.58
)
. With the information we have here, we can conclude (see
[
37
, Corollary 2.9]
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}||\partial_{t}w_{\Delta t}||_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}^{2}\leq C(s,||w_{\Delta t}||_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Gamma))})\int_{0}^{T}||\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}||_{H^{1}(\Omega^{w_{\Delta t}}(t))}^{2}\leq C,

for any
s<\frac{1}{2}
, so

\displaystyle||\partial_{t}w_{\Delta t}||_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{r}(\Gamma))}\leq C(r),

for any
r<4
, by the imbedding of Sobolev spaces. This finally gives us

\displaystyle||\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}||_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{r}(S_{K}(t)))}\leq C(K+1)||\partial_{t}w_{\Delta t}||_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{r}(\Gamma))}\leq C(K+1)

so we can bound

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B}\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{S_{K}(t)}\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}

\displaystyle\leq C||\rho_{\Delta t}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}||_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(B))}||1||_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{q}(S_{K}(t)))}||\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\Delta t}^{K}||_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{r}(S_{K}(t)))}

\displaystyle\leq CK^{-\frac{1}{q}}(K+1).

for some
q<\infty
and
r<4
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma}+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=1.

Note that such
q
always exists due to condition
\gamma>12/7
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

(1) The above lemma and remark reflect the only difference in the analysis for these two fluid models. The only reason we need
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}>0
is to close the estimate
(
3.58
)
, since in our case we only have
\rho\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, so
\partial_{t}w_{\Delta t}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{r}(\Gamma))
,
r<4
, is not enough (in 2D case,
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=0
is enough, due to stronger imbedding results).

(2) A careful reader would have noticed that we also need
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}>0
in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, since the regularity
\partial_{t}\nabla w\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{T})
is also required there. However, once we prove that
\rho_{|B\setminus\Omega^{w}(t)}(t,\cdot)=0
for a.a.
t\in(0,T)
, it will hold throughout the later convergences. This means that in the case
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=0
and
p(\rho,\vartheta)=\rho^{\gamma}+\rho\vartheta+\frac{a}{3}\vartheta^{4}
, for some
\gamma>12/7
, we can deal with this issue by adding, say, a term
-\delta\partial_{t}\nabla w\cdot\nabla\psi
to the coupled momentum equation
(
3.12
)
.

3.9
Pointwise convergence of the fluid density and the fluid temperature

In order to identify the limits of the remaining terms in
\eqref{RCE1},\eqref{coupledmomeq1}
and
(
3.13
)
,
it is enough the prove a.e. convergence of
\vartheta_{\Delta t}
and
\rho_{\Delta t}
.

First, let

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{\Delta t}:=\Bigg[\rho_{\Delta t}s_{\eta}^{w_{\Delta t}}(\rho_{\Delta t},\vartheta_{\Delta t}),~\rho_{\Delta t}s_{\eta}^{w_{\Delta t}}(\rho_{\Delta t},\vartheta_{\Delta t})\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}-\frac{\kappa_{\nu}^{w_{\Delta t}}(\vartheta_{\Delta t})\nabla\vartheta_{\Delta t}}{\vartheta_{\Delta t}}\Bigg],

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{\Delta t}:=[G(\vartheta_{\Delta t}),0,0,0],

where
G
is a bounded and Lipschitz function on
[0,\infty)
. In order to apply div-curl lemma, one should note that we cannot deal with the boundary terms in the coupled entropy balance
(
3.13
)
, so we need to use this lemma locally. Fix
O\Subset(0,T)\times B
such that
O\cap\big((0,T)\times\Gamma^{w_{\Delta t}}(t)\big)=\emptyset
, for all
\Delta t\leq(\Delta t)_{O}
. From
(
3.47
)
,
\mathbf{U}_{\Delta t}\in L^{p}(O)
for some
p>1
, while one easily has that
\mathbf{W}_{\Delta t}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(O)
and that
\text{Curl}_{t,x}\mathbf{W}_{\Delta t}
is precompact in
W^{-1,s}(O)
, for some
s>1
. Now, in order to prove the precompactness of
\text{Div}_{t,x}\mathbf{U}_{\Delta t}
, let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(O)
. One has from
(
3.13
)

\displaystyle\int_{O}\text{Div}_{t,x}\mathbf{U}_{\Delta t}\varphi=-\langle\sigma_{\omega,\nu};\varphi\rangle_{[\mathcal{M},C]([0,T]\times O)}+\int_{O}\lambda\vartheta^{4}\varphi\leq C||\varphi||_{C(O)}\leq C||\varphi||_{W^{m,p}(O)},\quad

for some
m<1
and
p>4
.
Thus,
\text{Div}_{t,x}\mathbf{U}_{\Delta t}
is bounded in
W^{-m,p^{*}}(O)
and therefore precompact in
W^{-1,s}(O)
, for
s\in[1,4/3)
. Since any
O^{\prime}\Subset(0,T)\times B
such that
O^{\prime}\cap\big((0,T)\times\Gamma^{w}(t)\big)=\emptyset
is a subset of some
O
defined above (existence of the corresponding
(\Delta t)_{O}
follows by
(
3.50
)
), we can conclude by the div-curl lemma

\displaystyle\overline{\rho s_{\eta}^{w}(\rho,\vartheta)G(\vartheta)}=\overline{\rho s_{\eta}^{w}(\rho,\vartheta)}~\overline{G(\vartheta)},\quad\text{a.e. in }(0,T)\times B.

(3.59)

The next step is to show

\displaystyle\overline{\rho s_{M}(\rho,\vartheta)G(\vartheta)}\geq\overline{\rho s_{M}(\rho,\vartheta)}~\overline{G(\vartheta)},\quad\overline{\vartheta^{3}G(\vartheta)}\geq\overline{\vartheta^{3}}~\overline{G(\vartheta)}

(3.60)

for any continuous and increasing function
G
. This part can be done by application of theory of parametrized Young measures (see
[
15
, Section 3.6.2]
). Combining
(
3.59
)
and
(
3.60
)

\displaystyle\overline{\vartheta^{4}}=\overline{\vartheta^{3}}\vartheta

which then by monotonicity of
x\mapsto x^{4}
gives us

\displaystyle\vartheta_{\Delta t}\to\vartheta,\quad\text{a.e. in }(0,T)\times B.

(3.61)

In order to prove the pointwise convergence of the density, we use the convergence of the effective viscous flux, in particular:

Lemma 3.6
.

The equality

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\int_{Q^{w}_{T}}\varphi\left(p_{M}(\rho_{\Delta t},\vartheta_{\Delta t})-\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\vartheta_{\Delta t})+\eta(\vartheta_{\Delta t})\right){\rm div}\mathbf{u}_{\Delta t}\right)\rho_{\Delta t}{\rm d}x{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{T}^{w}}\varphi\left(\overline{p_{M}(\rho,\vartheta)}-\overline{\left(\frac{4}{3}\mu(\vartheta)+\eta(\vartheta)\right){\rm div}{\bf u}}\right)\rho{\rm d}x{\rm d}t

holds for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(Q_{T}^{w})
.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma does not differ from the one presented in
[
15
, Section 3.6.5]
and therefore we do not provide it here. Note that for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(Q_{T}^{w})
, there is a small enough
(\Delta_{t})_{0}
such that for all
\Delta t\leq(\Delta t)_{0}
(at least for a subsequence) one has
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(Q_{T}^{w_{\Delta t}})
, which is a direct consequence of
(
3.51
)
.
∎

Now it is sufficient to take
b(\rho)=\rho\log\rho
in the renormalized continuity equation

\partial_{t}b(\rho)+{\rm div}(b(\rho){\bf u})+(b^{\prime}(\rho)\rho-b(\rho)){\rm div}{\bf u}=0

(note that this equation holds true for both
\rho,{\bf u}
and
\rho_{\Delta t},{\bf u}_{\Delta t}
)
and we infer that

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{B}\rho_{\Delta t}{\rm div}{\bf u}_{\Delta t}\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}t=\int_{B}\rho_{0}\log\rho_{0,\delta}{\rm d}x-\int_{B}\rho_{\Delta t}(\tau,\cdot)\log\rho_{\Delta t}(\tau,\cdot)\ {\rm d}x

(3.62)

and

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{B}\rho{\rm div}{\bf u}\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}t=\int_{B}\rho_{0,\delta}\log\rho_{0,\delta}\ {\rm d}x-\int_{B}\rho(\tau,\cdot)\log\rho(\tau,\cdot)\ {\rm d}x

(3.63)

for every
\tau\in[0,T]
. Lemma
3.6
and the monotonicity of
p_{M}
yield
\overline{\rho{\rm div}{\bf u}}\geq\rho{\rm div}{\bf u}
and therefore, with help of
(
3.62
)
and
(
3.63
)

\overline{\rho\log(\rho)}=\rho\log(\rho)

Due to the convexity of
\rho\mapsto\rho\log\rho
we have just deduced that

\displaystyle\rho_{\Delta t}\to\rho\quad\text{a.e. in }(0,T)\times B,

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B\setminus\Omega^{w_{k}}(t)}\vartheta_{k}^{4}\varphi_{k}\to 0.

Proof.

First, one has

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B\setminus\Omega^{w_{k}}(t)}a\eta\vartheta_{k}^{4}\text{div }\varphi_{k}\Big|\leq a\eta||\vartheta_{k}^{4}||_{L^{1}((0,T)\times B)}||\text{div}\varphi_{k}||_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times B)}\leq Ca\eta||\vartheta_{k}||^{4}_{L^{5}((0,T)\times B)}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\eta}{\lambda^{\frac{4}{5}}}=Ck^{\frac{36}{5}}\to 0,

by
(
3.40
)
, and

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B\setminus\Omega^{w_{k}}(t)}a\eta\vartheta_{k}^{3}\mathbf{u}_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{k}\Big|\leq a\eta||\vartheta_{k}^{3}||_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}((0.T)\times B)}||\mathbf{u}_{k}||_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(B))}||\nabla\varphi_{k}||_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times B)}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\eta}{\omega}||\vartheta_{k}^{3}||_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{\frac{40}{27}}(B\setminus\Omega^{w}(t)))}\leq C\frac{\sqrt{\eta}}{\omega}||\vartheta^{3}||_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}((0,T)\times B)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\eta||\vartheta^{3}||_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{4}{3}}(B\setminus\Omega^{w}(t)))}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\sqrt{\eta}}{\omega\sqrt{\lambda}}=Ck^{\frac{3}{2}}\to 0,

by
\eqref{vartheta5},\eqref{varthetarho},\eqref{nablau}
and the interpolation of Lebesgue spaces. Next

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{(B\setminus\Omega^{w_{k}}(t))}\mathbb{S}_{k}^{w}(\vartheta_{k},\nabla\mathbf{u}_{k}):\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\Big|\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{(B\setminus\Omega^{w_{k}}(t))}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\vartheta_{k}}}|\mathbb{S}_{k}^{w}(\vartheta_{k},\nabla\mathbf{u}_{k}):\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}|\sqrt{\vartheta_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{(B\setminus\Omega^{w_{k}}(t))}\Big|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\vartheta_{k}}}\mathbb{S}_{k}^{w}(\vartheta_{k},\nabla\mathbf{u}_{k})\Big|^{\frac{10}{9}}\Big)^{\frac{9}{10}}||\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}||_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times B)}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{(B\setminus\Omega^{w_{k}}(t))}\vartheta_{k}^{5}\Big)^{\frac{1}{10}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{10}}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{(B\setminus\Omega^{w_{k}}(t))}\Big|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\vartheta_{k}}}\sqrt{\big(\mathbb{S}_{k}^{w}(\vartheta,\nabla\mathbf{u}_{k}):\nabla\mathbf{u}_{k}\big)\big(\mu_{k}^{w}(\vartheta_{k})+\zeta_{k}^{w}(\vartheta_{k})}\big)\Big|^{\frac{10}{9}}\Big)^{\frac{9}{10}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{10}}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{(B\setminus\Omega^{w}(t))}\frac{1}{\vartheta_{k}}\mathbb{S}_{k}^{w}(\vartheta_{k},\nabla\mathbf{u}_{k}):\nabla\mathbf{u}_{k}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{(B\setminus\Omega^{w_{k}}(t))}(\mu_{k}^{w}(\vartheta_{k})+\zeta_{k}^{w}(\vartheta_{k}))^{\frac{5}{4}}\Big)^{\frac{2}{5}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{10}}}\Big(||\mu(\vartheta_{k})||^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{5}((0,T)\times(B\setminus\Omega^{w}(t)))}+||\zeta(\vartheta_{k})||^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{5}((0,T)\times(B\setminus\Omega^{w}(t)))}\Big)||f_{k}^{w_{k}}||^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}((0,T)\times(B\setminus\Omega^{w}(t)))}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{5}}}=Ck^{\frac{3}{10}}\to 0,

where we used
\eqref{mu_1},\eqref{eta_1},\eqref{fomega},\eqref{vartheta5},\eqref{nablau}
and

\displaystyle|\mathbb{S}_{k}^{w}(\vartheta,\nabla\mathbf{u})|^{2}\leq\big(\mathbb{S}_{k}^{w}(\vartheta,\nabla\mathbf{u}):\nabla\mathbf{u}\big)\big(\mu_{k}^{w}(\vartheta)+\zeta_{k}^{w}(\vartheta)\big).

Now,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B\setminus\Omega^{w_{k}}(t)}\frac{\kappa_{k}^{w_{k}}(\vartheta_{k})\nabla\vartheta_{k}}{\vartheta_{k}}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\nu}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B\setminus\Omega^{w_{k}}(t)}\frac{\kappa_{k}^{w_{k}}(\vartheta_{k})|\nabla\vartheta_{k}|^{2}}{\vartheta_{k}^{2}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}||\nabla\varphi_{k}||_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times B)}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B\setminus\Omega^{w_{k}}(t)}\kappa(\vartheta_{k})\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

by

and finally,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B\setminus\Omega^{w_{k}}(t)}\lambda\vartheta_{k}^{4}\varphi_{k}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{1}{5}}\Big(\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B\setminus\Omega^{w_{k}}(t)}\vartheta_{k}^{5}\Big)^{\frac{4}{5}}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{1}{5}}=Ck^{\frac{1}{5}}\to 0,

so the proof is finished.

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}^{w}}\big[\partial_{t}(\rho s)\varphi+\nabla\cdot(\rho s\mathbf{u})\varphi\big]=

\displaystyle-\int_{Q_{T}^{w}}\big[\rho s\partial_{t}\varphi+(\rho s\mathbf{u})\cdot\nabla\varphi\big]-\int_{\Omega^{w_{0}}}\rho_{0}s(\vartheta_{0},\rho_{0})\varphi(0,\cdot)

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}(\rho s)\circ\Phi_{w}\partial_{t}w\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{w}d\Gamma+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma^{w}}\rho s\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{w}d\Gamma^{w}}_{=0,\hskip 8.19447pt\text{by }\eqref{kinc}},

where we have used the Raynolds transport theorem and integration by parts. Next,

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}^{w}}\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\varphi=-\int_{Q_{T}^{w}}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\nabla\varphi}{\vartheta}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma^{w}(t)}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\mathbf{n}^{w}}{\vartheta}\varphi d\Gamma^{w}

so we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}^{w}}\rho s(\partial_{t}\varphi+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\varphi)-\int_{Q_{T}^{w}}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\nabla\varphi}{\vartheta}+\int_{Q_{T}^{w}}\frac{\varphi}{\vartheta}\Big(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla\mathbf{u}):\nabla\mathbf{u}+\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta}\Big)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma^{w}(t)}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\mathbf{n}^{w}}{\vartheta}\varphi d\Gamma^{w}=-\int_{\Omega^{w_{0}}}\rho_{0}s(\vartheta_{0},\rho_{0})\varphi(0,\cdot).

(5.16)

Next, multiply
(
1.3
)
with
\tilde{\psi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\Gamma)
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\theta\partial_{t}\tilde{\psi}-\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\tilde{\psi}+\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}\tilde{\psi}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Gamma}\theta_{0}\tilde{\psi}(0,\cdot)-\int_{\Gamma}\nabla w_{0}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\psi}(0,\cdot)+\int_{\Gamma_{T}}q\tilde{\psi}.

(5.17)

Now, we sum up
(
5.16
)
and
(
5.17
)
for non-negative test functions
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega^{w}(t)})
and
\tilde{\psi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\Gamma)
such that
\gamma_{|\Gamma^{w}}\varphi=\tilde{\psi}
on
\Gamma_{T}
, which then by the coupling condition
(
1.12
)
finally give us
(
2.3
)
.

Note that here we have actually derived
(
2.3
)
in a form of equality. However, in the definition of our weak solution
2.1
, both entropy and energy identities are replaced with inequalities, which owes to the lower semicontinuity of norms. We argue that this is enough, i.e. if the weak solution is regular enough, then
(
2.3
)
and
(
6
)
are satisfied as an identity. More precisely, let smooth functions
(\vartheta,\rho,\mathbf{u},w,\theta)
be a weak solution in the sense of definition. Following the ideas from
[
54
]
, we first assume that there is a smooth non-negative test function
\tilde{\varphi}
such that w.l.o.g.
\tilde{\varphi}\leq\vartheta
and
(
2.3
)
holds as a strict inequality

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}^{w}}\rho s(\partial_{t}\tilde{\varphi}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\varphi})-\int_{Q_{T}^{w}}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\nabla\tilde{\varphi}}{\vartheta}+\int_{Q_{T}^{w}}\frac{\tilde{\varphi}}{\vartheta}\Big(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla\mathbf{u}):\nabla\mathbf{u}+\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta}\Big)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\theta\partial_{t}\tilde{\psi}-\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\tilde{\psi}+\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}\tilde{\psi}

\displaystyle<-\int_{\Omega^{w_{0}}}\rho_{0}s(\vartheta_{0},\rho_{0})\varphi(0,\cdot)-\int_{\Gamma}\theta_{0}\tilde{\psi}(0,\cdot)-\int_{\Gamma}\nabla w_{0}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\psi}(0,\cdot).

(5.18)

Now, by choosing
(\boldsymbol{\varphi},\psi)=(\mathbf{u},\partial_{t}w)
in
(
2.2
)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega^{w}(t)}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\Big|_{0}^{T}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}|\partial_{t}w|^{2}\Big|_{0}^{T}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}|\Delta w|^{2}\Big|_{0}^{T}-\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Delta\theta\partial_{t}w+\alpha_{1}\int_{\Gamma_{T}}|\partial_{t}\nabla w|^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\int_{\Gamma}|\partial_{t}\nabla w|^{2}\Big|_{0}^{T}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega^{w}(t)}p(\rho,\vartheta)(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u})-\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla\mathbf{u}):\nabla\mathbf{u}.

while the energy inequality gives us

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega^{w}(t)}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\Big|_{0}^{T}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}|\partial_{t}w|^{2}\Big|_{0}^{T}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}|\Delta w|^{2}\Big|_{0}^{T}+\alpha_{1}\int_{\Gamma_{T}}|\partial_{t}\nabla w|^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\int_{\Gamma}|\partial_{t}\nabla w|^{2}\Big|_{0}^{T}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega^{w}(t)}\rho e(\rho,\vartheta)\Big|_{0}^{T}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}|\theta|^{2}\Big|_{0}^{T}-\int_{\Gamma_{T}}|\nabla\theta|^{2}.

which together imply

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega^{w}(t)}p(\rho,\vartheta)(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u})-\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla\mathbf{u}):\nabla\mathbf{u}\leq-\int_{\Omega^{w}(t)}\rho e(\rho,\vartheta)\Big|_{0}^{T}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}|\theta|^{2}\Big|_{0}^{T}-\int_{\Gamma_{T}}|\nabla\theta|^{2}-\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Delta\theta\partial_{t}w.

Now, we sum up
(
2.3
)
with
\varphi=\vartheta-\tilde{\varphi}
with
(
5.18
)
and obtain that
(
2.3
)
holds as a strict inequality for
\varphi=\vartheta
. Then, one has by the Gibbs identity
(
1.26
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega^{w}(t)}p(\rho,\vartheta)(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u})-\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla\mathbf{u}):\nabla\mathbf{u}>-\int_{\Omega^{w}(t)}\rho e(\rho,\vartheta)\Big|_{0}^{T}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}|\theta|^{2}\Big|_{0}^{T}-\int_{\Gamma_{T}}|\nabla\theta|^{2}-\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Delta\theta\partial_{t}w,

which is obviously a contradiction. Therefore,
(
2.3
)
must hold as an identity. Using the ideas from the above calculation, we easily obtain that now the energy inequality
(
6
)
has to hold as an identity as well.

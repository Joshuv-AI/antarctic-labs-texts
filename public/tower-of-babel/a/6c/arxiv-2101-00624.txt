1.
Introduction and main result

The kinetic Langevin diffusion
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which describes the motion of a particle with position
X_{t}
and velocity
V_{t}
in the statistical physics, is given by

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=V_{t}\,\text{\rm{d}}t,\\
\text{\rm{d}}V_{t}=-V_{t}\,\text{\rm{d}}t-\nabla U(X_{t})\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\text{\rm{d}}B_{t},\end{cases}

where
U\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion. In particular,
-\nabla U(x)
means the force subject to damping and random collisions. Since the driven noise appears only in the second component, (
1.1
) is a degenerate stochastic differential equation (SDE). This type degenerate SDE is named as a stochastic damping Hamiltonian system with the Hamiltonian function

H(x,v):=U(x)+\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}

in the probability community; see
[
22
]
.
When
U
is smooth, the celebrated Hörmander’s hypoellipticity theorem tells us that the distribution density
p(t,x,v)
of the process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
given by (
1.1
) is also smooth and it solves the following kinetic Fokker-Planck equation

(1.2)

\partial_{t}p+v\cdot\nabla_{x}p-\nabla U(x)\cdot\nabla_{v}p=\frac{1}{2}\triangle_{v}p-{\rm div}_{v}(vp).

For background on stochastic Hamiltonian systems and related kinetic Fokker-Planck equations as well as their applications, the readers are referred to
[
19
]
. Furthermore, one can verify that the measure

\mu_{*}(\text{\rm{d}}x,\text{\rm{d}}v):=\text{\rm{e}}^{-H(x,v)}\,\text{\rm{d}}x\,\text{\rm{d}}v

is invariant for the process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Equivalently, the equilibrium of the kinetic Fokker-Planck equation (
1.2
) is given by

\rho_{\infty}(x,v)=\text{\rm{e}}^{-H(x,v)}.

Nowadays, the rate of convergence to the equilibrium
of (
1.1
) has been investigated considerably; see e.g.
[
21
]
.

In this work, we will consider (
1.1
) with the Brownian motion
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
therein replaced by a pure jump Lévy process
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. More generally, we will consider the
following stochastic Hamiltonian system driven by Lévy noises:

(1.3)

\begin{cases}\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=(aX_{t}+bV_{t})\,\text{\rm{d}}t,\\
\text{\rm{d}}V_{t}=U(X_{t},V_{t})\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\text{\rm{d}}L_{t},\end{cases}

where
a\geq 0
,
b>0
,
U:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued pure jump Lévy process associated with the Lévy measure
\nu
on
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
such that
\nu(\{{\bf 0}\})=0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1\wedge|u|^{2})\,\nu(\text{\rm{d}}u)<\infty
; that is, the infinitesimal generator
\mathscr{L}_{0}
for the process
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is given by

(1.4)

(\mathscr{L}_{0}g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(g(x+u)-g(x)-\langle\nabla g(x),u\rangle\mathds{1}_{\{|u|\leq 1\}}\big)\,\nu(\text{\rm{d}}u),\quad g\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Analogous to (
1.1
), (
1.3
) can be used to describe the motion of particles perturbed by a discontinuous stochastic force, and (
1.3
) has a close connection with the non-local kinetic Fokker-Planck equation. Recently the study of stochastic Hamiltonian systems driven by Lévy noises and non-local kinetic Fokker-Planck equations has been developed greatly. For example, see
[
6
,
23
]
on the existence of smooth fundamental solutions for (
1.3
) when
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a subordinated Brownian motion, and see
[
3
,
5
,
10
,
11
]
on Schauder estimates and
L^{p}
-estimates for non-local kinetic Fokker-Planck equations, and so on. However, the research on the ergodicity of stochastic Hamiltonian systems driven by Lévy noises (equivalently, the convergence to equilibrium for non-local kinetic Fokker-Planck equations) is still widely open. The purpose of this paper is to study exponential ergodicity under a proper Wasserstein-type distance for the stochastic Hamiltonian system with Lévy noises (
1.3
).

To present our main result, we need to state assumptions on
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
given by (
1.3
). First, we assume that
U:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies the condition as follows:

({\bf A_{0}})

The function
(x,v)\mapsto U(x,v)
is locally Lipschitz continuous, i.e., for any
R>0
, there is a constant
\lambda(R)>0
such that for all
x,x^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\in B(0,R):=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|z|\leq R\},

\big|U(x,v)-U(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})|\leq\lambda(R)(|x-x^{\prime}|+|v-v^{\prime}|).

Then, it is well known that the SDE (
1.3
) has a unique strong solution
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
up to the explosive time

(1.5)

\xi:=\inf\{t>0:|X_{t}|+|V_{t}|=\infty\};

see
[
1
, Theorem 6.2.3, p. 367]
for more details.
Moreover, the process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has the strong Markov property, and the associated generator, acting on
f\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, is given by

(1.6)

\begin{split}(\mathscr{L}f)(x,v)&=\big\langle ax+bv,\nabla_{x}f(x,v)\big\rangle+\big\langle U(x,v),\nabla_{v}f(x,v)\big\rangle\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(f(x,v+u)-f(x,v)-\langle\nabla_{v}f(x,v),u\rangle\mathds{1}_{\{|u|\leq 1\}}\big)\,\nu(\text{\rm{d}}u).\end{split}

Next, we further impose the following two assumptions.

({\bf A_{1}})

There exist a
C^{1,2}
-function
\mathcal{W}:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\rightarrow[1,\infty)
with
\mathcal{W}(x,v)\rightarrow\infty
as
|x|+|v|\rightarrow\infty
and constants
c_{0},C_{0}>0
such that for all
x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.7)

(\mathscr{L}\mathcal{W})(x,v)\leq-c_{0}\mathcal{W}(x,v)+C_{0},

where
\mathscr{L}
is given by (
1.6
) and

C^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is the set of all real-valued functions
f(x,v)
defined on

\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
which are continuously once differentiable in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and twice differentiable in
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

({\bf A_{2}})

There is
a non-negative measure
\nu^{*}\leq\nu
on
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
such that

(i)

there exist a constant
r_{0}>0
and a non-decreasing and concave function
\sigma_{r_{0}}(\cdot)\in C([0,r_{0}])\cap C^{2}((0,r_{0}])
such that
\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\sigma_{r_{0}}(l)\,\text{\rm{d}}l<\infty
and

\sigma_{r_{0}}(r)\leq rJ(r)
for all
r\in(0,r_{0}]
, where

(1.8)

J(s):=\inf_{|x|\leq s}\big(\nu^{*}\wedge(\delta_{x}*\nu^{*})\big)(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad s>0.

(ii)

there exist constants
\eta\in(0,1)
and
c_{*}>0
such that for all
x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|\mathcal{W}(x,v+u)-\mathcal{W}(x,v)\big|\,\nu^{*}(\text{\rm{d}}u)\leq c_{*}\mathcal{W}(x,v)^{\eta},

where
\mathcal{W}
is given in
{\bf(A_{1})}
.

{\bf(A_{1})}
indicates that
\mathcal{W}(x,v)
is a Lyapunov function for the process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. In particular, according to (
1.7
) and
[
18
, Theorem 1.2]
, the process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is conservative; that is, the explosive time defined by (
1.5
) satisfies that
\xi=\infty
a.s. Roughly speaking,
{\bf(A_{2})}
(i) means that there are many active small jumps of the Lévy process
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, which can be regarded as a non-degenerate condition for the Lévy measure near zero; see
[
13
,
15
]
for more details.
{\bf(A_{2})}
(ii) is a technical condition to handle the exponential ergodicity in terms of multiplicative Wasserstein-type distance as shown in Theorem
1.1
.

In the following, let
P_{t}((x,v),\cdot)
be the transition kernel of the process
(X_{t},V_{t})
with the starting point
(x,v)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Under Assumptions
{\bf(A_{0})}
,
{\bf(A_{1})}
and
{\bf(A_{2})}
, the process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
determined by (
1.3
) is exponentially ergodic in the sense that there exist a unique invariant probability measure
\mu
and a constant
\lambda_{*}>0
such that for all
x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
,

(1.9)

W_{\Psi}\big(P_{t}((x,v),\cdot),\mu\big)\leq C(x,v)\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda_{*}t},

where

(1.10)

\Psi\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big):=\big((|x-x^{\prime}|+|v-v^{\prime}|)\wedge 1\big)\big(\mathcal{W}(x,v)+\mathcal{W}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big)

and
C(x,v)
is a positive measurable function dependent on
x,v
.

For the definition of the Wasserstein-type distance
W_{\Psi}
, one can refer to Subsection
5.1
in the Appendix section.

1.1.
Example: kinetic Langevin process with Lévy noises

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we are going to treat exponential ergodicity under the Wasserstein-type distance for
the following kinetic Langevin process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}

(1.11)

\begin{cases}\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=V_{t}\,\text{\rm{d}}t,\\
\text{\rm{d}}V_{t}=-\alpha V_{t}\,\text{\rm{d}}t-\beta\nabla U_{0}(X_{t})\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\text{\rm{d}}L_{t},\end{cases}

where
\alpha,\beta>0
,
U_{0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued pure jump Lévy process with the Lévy measure
\nu
satisfying that for some
\theta\in(0,1]
,

(1.12)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(|u|^{2}\wedge|u|^{\theta}\big)\,\nu(\text{\rm{d}}u)<\infty.

That is, (
1.11
) is a special case of the SDE (
1.3
) with
a=0
,
b=1
and
U(x,v)=-\alpha v-\beta\nabla U_{0}(x).

Note that (
1.11
) is exactly the same form as (
1.1
) with
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
replaced by
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

Below we will assume that the potential term
U_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
fulfills the following conditions:

({\bf B_{0}})

x\mapsto\nabla U_{0}(x)
is locally Lipschitz continuous, i.e., for any
R>0,
there exists a constant
\lambda_{U_{0}}(R)>0
such that

|\nabla U_{0}(x)-\nabla U_{0}(y)|\leq\lambda_{U_{0}}(R)|x-y|,\quad x,y\in B(0,R).

({\bf B_{1}})

There exist constants
\lambda_{1}>0
and
\lambda_{i}\geq 0

(i=2,3,4,5)
with

(1.13)

\lambda_{2}\lambda_{4}<\lambda_{1},\quad 2\beta\lambda_{4}\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}+\sqrt{\beta(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\lambda_{4})}\,\alpha

such that

(1.14)

\big\langle x,\nabla U_{0}(x)\big\rangle\geq\lambda_{1}|x|^{2}+\lambda_{2}U_{0}(x)-\lambda_{3},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

and

(1.15)

U_{0}(x)\geq-\lambda_{4}|x|^{2}-\lambda_{5},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

{\bf(B_{2})}

There are constants
c>0
and
\theta_{0}\in(0,\theta/2)
such that

(1.16)

\nu(\text{\rm{d}}z)\geq\frac{c}{|z|^{d+\theta_{0}}}\mathds{1}_{\{0<z_{1}\leq 1\}}\,\text{\rm{d}}z,

where
z_{1}
is the first component of the vector
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Remark 1.2
.

We make some comments on Assumption
({\bf B_{1}})
.

(i)

The condition
\lambda_{2}\lambda_{4}<\lambda_{1}
is reasonable, since it, together with (
1.14
) and (
1.15
), gives us that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(1.17)

\langle x,\nabla U_{0}(x)\rangle\geq(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\lambda_{4})|x|^{2}-\lambda_{2}\lambda_{5}-\lambda_{3}

with
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}\lambda_{4}
. (
1.17
) is a common condition in the literature to yield the exponential ergodicity of Langevin diffusions and SDEs with additive Lévy noise; see
[
7
,
13
]
.

(ii)

When
U_{0}
is bounded from below by a negative constant, we can take
\lambda_{4}=0
and so (
1.13
) and (
1.15
) hold trivially. On the other hand, it is easy to see that (
1.14
) holds for
U_{0}(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{l}
with
l\geq 1
or
U_{0}(x)=\text{\rm{e}}^{(1+|x|^{2})^{l}}
with
l>0
, which is different from assumptions of the potential
U_{0}(x)
for kinetic Langevin diffusions in
[
8
,
21
]
. More precisely,
in
[
21
, Theorem A. 8]
it was required that
|\nabla^{2}U_{0}|\leq c(1+|U_{0}|)
for some
c>0
; in
[
8
, Assumption 2.1]
it was assumed that
\nabla U_{0}(x)
is globally Lipschitz continuous.

(iii)

We can further check that
{\bf(B_{1})}
indeed holds for a large class of double well potentials which are super-linear growth at infinity, including
U_{0}(x)=c_{1}(1+|x|^{2})^{l}-c_{2}|x|^{2}
with
l>1
or
U_{0}(x)=c_{1}\text{\rm{e}}^{(1+|x|^{2})^{l}}-c_{2}|x|^{2}
with
l>0
for any
c_{1},c_{2}>0
. See Subsection
5.3
in the Appendix section of this paper for the simple proof.

As we shall see in Section
4
,
for the framework (
1.11
),

{\bf(B_{1})}
and (
1.12
) are imposed herein to guarantee that the Lyapunov condition
{\bf(A_{1})}
is valid, while
{\bf(B_{2})}
is put to ensure that
{\bf(A_{2})}
is satisfied under (
1.12
). It is clear that, for the SDE (
1.11
), Assumption
{\bf(A_{0})}
holds true under Assumption
{\bf(B_{0})}
. Therefore, we have

Theorem 1.3
.

Under Assumptions
{\bf(B_{0})}
,
{\bf(B_{1})}
and
{\bf(B_{2})}
, the assertion of Theorem
1.1
holds true for the process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
defined by (
1.11
), where

\Psi((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime}))
is given by (
1.10
) with

W(x,v)=\big(1+W_{0}(x)+|x|^{2}+|v|^{2}\big)^{\theta/2},\quad x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\theta\in(0,1]
is given in (
1.12
) and

W_{0}(x):=U_{0}(x)+\lambda_{4}|x|^{2}+\lambda_{5}
with constants
\lambda_{4},\lambda_{5}
given in (
1.15
).

Note that for any (irrationally invariant) symmetric
\alpha_{0}
-stable Lévy process with
\alpha_{0}\in(0,2)
, (
1.12
) holds with
\theta\in(0,\alpha_{0}-\varepsilon)
for any
\varepsilon>0
and (
1.16
) is satisfied with
\theta_{0}\in(0,\alpha_{0}]
. In particular, for any symmetric
(1+\varepsilon)
-stable Lévy process with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, both (
1.12
) and (
1.16
) hold with
\theta=1
. Hence, according to Theorem
1.3
and the fact that

|x-x^{\prime}|+|v-v^{\prime}|\leq(1\wedge(|x-x^{\prime}|+|v-v^{\prime}|))\big(1+|x|+|v|+|x^{\prime}|+|v^{\prime}|\big),\quad x,x^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

we can immediately deduce the following statement from Theorem
1.3
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the process defined by (
1.11
) such that Assumptions
{\bf(B_{0})}
and
{\bf(B_{1})}
are satisfied, and

is a symmetric
\alpha_{0}
-stable Lévy process with

.
Then, the assertion of Theorem

holds with the standard

-Wasserstein distance with the metric

((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime}))\mapsto(|x-x^{\prime}|+|v-v^{\prime}|)^{1\wedge(\alpha_{0}-\varepsilon)},\quad x,x^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

for any

1.2.
Approach

We shall make some comments on the approach adopted in this paper.

First, under the Lyapunov drift condition in Assumption
{\bf(A_{1})}
, a standard way to yield the exponential ergodicity of the process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is to verify that it has the strong Feller property and the irreducible property; see
[
18
]
. But so far it is unclear whether either of those two properties holds true for the process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
under Assumptions
{\bf(A_{0})}
,
{\bf(A_{1})}
and
{\bf(A_{2})}
, even for the special process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
given by (
1.11
) under
{\bf(B_{0})}
,
{\bf(B_{1})}
and
{\bf(B_{2})}
. See
[
17
,
20
,
22
]
for the ergodicity of kinetic Langevin diffusions via the method based on a Lyapunov drift condition.

The argument based on functional inequalities is one of powerful tools in the study of long time behavior of kinetic Langevin diffusions; see
[
21
]
and references therein for more details. This methodology relies heavily on the explicit formulation of the associated invariant probability measure, which now is unavailable for the process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
defined by (
1.3
) or (
1.11
) (even when
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a symmetric stable-Lévy process).

The approach for Theorem
1.1
is based on the probabilistic coupling method. Markov couplings have been successfully used to establish the exponential ergodicity of non-degenerate SDEs with Lévy noises or non-degenerate McKean-Vlasov SDEs with additive Lévy noises; see
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
. However, the result for degenerate
SDEs with jumps in this direction is still open. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first paper to investigate via Markov coupling the exponential ergodicity for the stochastic Hamiltonian systems with Lévy noises.

Coupling argument has been exploited to derive
the ergodicity of kinetic Langevin diffusions; see
[
2
,
8
]
.
The remarkable work
[
8
]
shows that the coupling method can not only provide qualitative convergence rate (rather than the quantitative one) to the equilibrium but also a good probabilistic understanding of the dynamics involved. The coupling idea of our paper is partly inspired by that in
[
8
]
; that is, instead of considering directly the coupling for the process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, we will study the coupling for the transformed process
(X_{t},X_{t}+\alpha^{-1}V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with some proper
\alpha>0
. One of the key observations is that with this transformation the first component can be contractive for the process
(X_{t},X_{t}+\alpha^{-1}V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
when the distance between two marginal processes of the associated coupling processes is bounded and the impact of the Lévy noise is small. Despite the similar spirit in the approach, there are still a few of essential differences between kinetic Langevin diffusions and the stochastic Hamiltonian systems with Lévy noises, which require some new ideas as indicated in the present framework. For example,

(i)

The rate of convergence to equilibrium for kinetic Langevin diffusions was investigated in
[
8
]
via a combination of a reflection coupling and a synchronous coupling, which depends on some nice properties of Brownian motion (e.g. the Lévy characterization). Since the Lévy process
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in (
1.3
) may be non-symmetric or has a degenerate Lévy measure, we will adopt the refined basic coupling for pure jump Lévy processes, which was initiated in the work
[
15
]
. We emphasize that the refined basic coupling is more powerful in considering degenerate SDEs with jumps in the sense that we do not need the approximation technique as taken in
[
8
]
for kinetic Langevin diffusions.

(ii)

The other crucial ingredient to prove the exponential ergodicity of the process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
under the Wasserstein-type distance is to construct a proper cost function by using some distance-like function. Similar to
[
8
]
, we will employ the multiplicative distance
W_{\Psi}
(which is first introduced in
[
9
]
to establish the weak Harris’ theorem). In the present setting, we are concerned with Lévy noises, then the infinitesimal generator (i.e., coupling operator) of the Markov coupling process is a non-local operator, which makes related estimates for the coupling operator acting on the cost function much more involved. In particular, we need to handle the folded terms and make sure that such terms can be ignored (which is indeed guaranteed by Assumption
{\bf(A_{2})}
(ii)). On the other hand, because the Lévy measure may have infinite second (even first) moment, one shall apply merely the basic refined coupling to the component of Lévy process
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
instead of the original Lévy process
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
(see Section
2
for more details). Moreover, the construction of a Lyapunov condition herein is much more delicate (see the proof of Theorem
1.3
). Besides, our proof essentially makes full use of the coupling operator only, rather than by means of Itô formula. Note that the latter tool (for example, the formula of the Itô product rule for stochastic integral with jumps) would look tedious in our setting. Last but not least, with contrast to the existing literature, we can deal with a large class of double well potentials
U_{0}
with super-linear growth at infinity in (
1.11
).

The rest of this paper is arranged as follows. In Section
2
, we construct a new Markov coupling for the stochastic Hamiltonian system with Lévy noises
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
defined by (
1.3
), which is partly motivated by the refined basic coupling for pure jump Lévy processes introduced in
[
15
]
. In Section
3
, we present some estimates for the coupling operator associated with the Markov coupling process given in Section
2
, which are crucial to yield the exponential ergodicity for the process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. In Section
4
, we present the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
. In particular, some explicit sufficient conditions put
directly on the coefficients of (
1.1
) and the Lévy measure
\nu
are also given here to show that Assumptions
{\bf(A_{1})}
and
{\bf(A_{2})}
are satisfied.

2.
A new Markov coupling for stochastic Hamiltonian system with Lévy noises

In this section, we will construct a Markov coupling process for the stochastic Hamiltonian system with Lévy noises
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
solved by (
1.3
). For this, we will consider a coupling operator for the operator
\mathscr{L}
defined by (
1.6
), where the refined basic coupling for pure jump Lévy processes is fully used. The refined basic coupling for pure jump Lévy processes was initiated in the work
[
15
]
, and was further developed to investigate gradient estimates for SDEs driven by multiplicative Lévy noises in
[
14
]
and exponential ergodicity for McKean-Vlasov SDEs with additive Lévy noises in
[
13
]
.

Let us first introduce some necessary notation. Given the threshold
\kappa>0,
define

(x)_{\kappa}=\left(1\wedge\frac{\kappa}{|x|}\right)x\penalty\ \mbox{ for }\penalty\ {\bf 0}\neq x\in\mathbb{R}^{d};\penalty\ \penalty\ (x)_{\kappa}={\bf 0}\penalty\ \mbox{ for }\penalty\ x={\bf 0}.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\delta_{x}
be the Dirac measure or the unit mass at the point
x
. Let
\nu^{*}
be a non-negative measure on
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
such that
\nu^{*}\leq\nu
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
let

(2.1)

\nu^{*}_{x}(\text{\rm{d}}u)=\big(\nu^{*}\wedge(\delta_{x}*\nu^{*})\big)\,(\text{\rm{d}}u),

which indeed is a finite measure on
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
when
x\neq{\bf 0}
. In fact, for
x\neq{\bf 0}
,
a direct calculation shows

\displaystyle\nu^{*}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|u|\geq{|x|}/{2}\}}\,\nu^{*}(\text{\rm{d}}u)+\int_{\{|u|\leq{|x|}/{2}\}}\,\nu^{*}(\text{\rm{d}}(u-x))

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\{|u|\geq{|x|}/{2}\}}\,\nu^{*}(\text{\rm{d}}u)\leq\frac{8}{|x|^{2}\wedge 4}\int_{\{1\wedge|u|\geq 1\wedge{|x|}/{2}\}}(1\wedge|u|^{2})\,\nu^{*}(\text{\rm{d}}u)

\displaystyle\leq 8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1\wedge|u|^{2})\,\nu^{*}(\text{\rm{d}}u)(1\wedge|x|)^{-2}\leq 8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1\wedge|u|^{2})\,\nu(\text{\rm{d}}u)(1\wedge|x|)^{-2}<\infty,

where in the second inequality we used the fact that
|u-x|\geq|x|/2
if
|u|\leq|x|/2
. See
[
15
, Appendix]
for detailed properties of the measure
\nu^{*}_{x}
. In particular, it is obvious that
\nu^{*}_{x}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\nu
.

As we will see later, instead of considering the process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
directly, we will study the coupling for
(X_{t},X_{t}+\alpha^{-1}V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, where
\alpha>0
is to be determined later.
Then, according to the strategy of
[
15
]
(in particular, see
[
15
, (2.7)]
therein for more details), the refined basic coupling of the infinitesimal operator
\mathscr{L}_{0}
of the pure jump Lévy process
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is constructed via the following relationship, for
x,x^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
q:=x-x^{\prime}+\alpha^{-1}(v-v^{\prime})

\displaystyle(v,v^{\prime})\rightarrow\begin{cases}(v+u,v^{\prime}+u+\alpha(q)_{\kappa}),&\quad\frac{1}{2}\nu^{*}_{-\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}(\text{\rm{d}}u),\\
(v+u,v^{\prime}+u-\alpha(q)_{\kappa}),&\quad\frac{1}{2}\nu^{*}_{\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}(\text{\rm{d}}u),\\
(v+u,v^{\prime}+u),&\quad\big(\nu-\frac{1}{2}\nu^{*}_{-\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}-\frac{1}{2}\nu^{*}_{\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}\big)(\text{\rm{d}}u),\end{cases}

where
\nu^{*}_{\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}
(resp.
\nu^{*}_{-\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}
) is defined by (
2.1
) with
x=\alpha(q)_{\kappa}
(resp.
x=-\alpha(q)_{\kappa}
).
For
x,x^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
f\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4d})
, define

(2.2)

\big(\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}f\big)\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big)=\big(\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{1}f\big)\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big)+\big(\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{x,x^{\prime}}\big)f\big((x,\cdot),(x^{\prime},\cdot)\big)(v,v^{\prime}),

where

(2.3)

\begin{split}&\big(\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{1}f\big)\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big)\\
&:=\big\langle ax+bv,\nabla_{x}f\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big)\big\rangle+\big\langle ax^{\prime}+bv^{\prime},\nabla_{x^{\prime}}f\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big)\big\rangle\\
&\quad\,+\big\langle U(x,v),\nabla_{v}f\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big)\big\rangle+\big\langle U(x^{\prime},v^{\prime}),\nabla_{v^{\prime}}f\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big)\big\rangle\end{split}

and, for
g\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
,

(2.4)

\begin{split}(\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{x,x^{\prime}}g)(v,v^{\prime})&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(g(v+u,v^{\prime}+u+\alpha(q)_{\kappa})-g(v,v^{\prime})-\langle\nabla_{v}g(v,v^{\prime}),u\rangle\mathds{1}_{\{|u|\leq 1\}}\\
&\quad\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -\langle\nabla_{v^{\prime}}g(v,v^{\prime}),u+\alpha(q)_{\kappa}\rangle\mathds{1}_{\{|u+\alpha(q)_{\kappa}|\leq 1\}}\big)\,\nu^{*}_{-\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}(\text{\rm{d}}u)\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(g(v+u,v^{\prime}+u-\alpha(q)_{\kappa})-g(v,v^{\prime})-\langle\nabla_{v}g(v,v^{\prime}),u\rangle\mathds{1}_{\{|u|\leq 1\}}\\
&\quad\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \,\,-\langle\nabla_{v^{\prime}}g(v,v^{\prime}),u-\alpha(q)_{\kappa}\rangle\mathds{1}_{\{|u-\alpha(q)_{\kappa}|\leq 1\}}\big)\,\nu^{*}_{\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}(\text{\rm{d}}u)\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(g(v+u,v^{\prime}+u)-g(v,v^{\prime})-\langle\nabla_{v}g(v,v^{\prime}),u\rangle\mathds{1}_{\{|u|\leq 1\}}\\
&\quad\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \,\,-\langle\nabla_{v^{\prime}}g(v,v^{\prime}),u\rangle\mathds{1}_{\{|u|\leq 1\}}\big)\,\Big(\nu-\frac{1}{2}\nu^{*}_{-\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}-\frac{1}{2}\nu^{*}_{\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}\Big)(\text{\rm{d}}u).\end{split}

Lemma 2.1
.

The operator
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}
defined by (
2.2
) is a coupling operator of
\mathscr{L}
given by (
1.6
).

Proof.

We only need to verify that, for any
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{x,x^{\prime}}
is a coupling operator of
\mathscr{L}_{0}
given by (
1.4
).
Recall that
q=x-x^{\prime}+\alpha^{-1}(v-v^{\prime})
for
x,x^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus, it is sufficient to check that for
g,h\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(2.5)

(\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{x,x^{\prime}}f)(v,v^{\prime})=(\mathscr{L}_{0}g)(v)+(\mathscr{L}_{0}h)(v^{\prime}),

where
f(v,v^{\prime}):=g(v)+h(v^{\prime})
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{x,x^{\prime}}f)(v,v^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathscr{L}_{0}g(v)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(h(v^{\prime}+u+\alpha(q)_{\kappa})-h(v^{\prime})-\langle\nabla h(v^{\prime}),u+\alpha(q)_{\kappa}\rangle\mathds{1}_{\{|u+\alpha(q)_{\kappa}|\leq 1\}}\big)\,\nu^{*}_{-\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}(\text{\rm{d}}u)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(h(v^{\prime}+u-\alpha(q)_{\kappa})-h(v^{\prime})-\langle\nabla h(v^{\prime}),u-\alpha(q)_{\kappa}\rangle\mathds{1}_{\{|u-\alpha(q)_{\kappa}|\leq 1\}}\big)\,\nu^{*}_{\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}(\text{\rm{d}}u)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(h(v^{\prime}+u)-h(v^{\prime})-\langle\nabla h(v^{\prime}),u\rangle\mathds{1}_{\{|u|\leq 1\}}\big)\,\Big(\nu-\frac{1}{2}\nu^{*}_{-\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}-\frac{1}{2}\nu^{*}_{\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}\Big)(\text{\rm{d}}u).

Then, by changing the variables
u+\alpha(q)_{\kappa}\rightarrow u
and
u-\alpha(q)_{\kappa}\rightarrow u
, respectively, and by using the facts that (see
[
15
, (2.3) or Corollary (A2) in the Appendix]
for more details)

(2.6)

\nu^{*}_{-\alpha(q)_{k}}(\text{\rm{d}}(u-\alpha(q)_{k}))=\nu^{*}_{\alpha(q)_{k}}(\text{\rm{d}}u),\quad\nu^{*}_{\alpha(q)_{k}}(\text{\rm{d}}(u+\alpha(q)_{k}))=\nu^{*}_{-\alpha(q)_{k}}(\text{\rm{d}}u),

the desired assertion (
2.5
) is available.
∎

In the sequel, we shall construct explicitly the coupling process associated with the coupling operator
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}
defined by (
2.2
). In terms of the Lévy-Itô decomposition (see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 2.4.16, p. 126]
),
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
can be represented as below

L_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\{|u|\leq 1\}}u\,\overline{N}(\text{\rm{d}}s,\text{\rm{d}}u)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\{|u|>1\}}u\,N(\text{\rm{d}}s,\text{\rm{d}}u),\quad t\geq 0,

where
N(\text{\rm{d}}t,\text{\rm{d}}u)
is the Poisson random measure with the intensity measure
\text{\rm{d}}t\,\nu(\text{\rm{d}}u)
and
\overline{N}(\text{\rm{d}}t,\text{\rm{d}}u)
is its compensated Poisson random measure, i.e.,

\overline{N}(\text{\rm{d}}t,\text{\rm{d}}u)=N(\text{\rm{d}}t,\text{\rm{d}}u)-\text{\rm{d}}t\,\nu(\text{\rm{d}}u).

To characterize the coupling process, inspired by
[
16
, Section 2.2]
(see also
[
14
,
15
]
for more details), we need to extend the Poisson measure
N
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
to the counterpart on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]
. Let
(p_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the Poisson point process related to
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, i.e.,

p_{t}=L_{t}-L_{t-},\quad t\in\mathcal{D}_{p}:=\big\{s>0:L_{s}\neq L_{s-}\big\}.

It holds that

N((0,t],U)=\#\big\{s\in\mathcal{D}_{p}:s\leq t,p_{s}\in U\big\},\quad t>0,U\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\#\{\cdot\}
denotes the counting measure.
Let
(p_{t}^{e})_{t\geq 0}
be the extension of the Poisson point process
(p_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, and
N^{e}
the Poisson random measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]
corresponding to
(p_{t}^{e})_{t\geq 0}
, i.e.,

N^{e}((0,t]\times U)=\#\big\{s\in\mathcal{D}_{p}:s\leq t,p_{s}^{e}\in U\big\},\quad t>0,U\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]).

In accordance with
[
12
, Chapetr II, Lemma 7.2]
, we infer

\overline{N}^{e}(\text{\rm{d}}t,\text{\rm{d}}u,\text{\rm{d}}l)=N^{e}(\text{\rm{d}}t,\text{\rm{d}}u,\text{\rm{d}}l)-\text{\rm{d}}t\,\nu(\text{\rm{d}}u)\,\mathds{1}_{[0,1]}(l)\,\text{\rm{d}}l.

In particular,
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
can be reformulated as

\displaystyle L_{t}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\{|u|\leq 1\}\times[0,1]}u\,\overline{N}^{e}(\text{\rm{d}}s,\text{\rm{d}}u,\text{\rm{d}}l)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\{|u|>1\}\times[0,1]}u\,N^{e}(\text{\rm{d}}s,\text{\rm{d}}u,\text{\rm{d}}l)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]}u\,\widetilde{N}^{e}(\text{\rm{d}}s,\text{\rm{d}}u,\text{\rm{d}}l),\quad t\geq 0.

Now we consider the following SDE

(2.7)

\begin{cases}\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=(aX_{t}+bV_{t})\,\text{\rm{d}}t,\\
\text{\rm{d}}V_{t}=U(X_{t},V_{t})\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\text{\rm{d}}L_{t},\\
\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{\prime}=(aX_{t}^{\prime}+bV_{t}^{\prime})\,\text{\rm{d}}t,\\
\text{\rm{d}}V_{t}^{\prime}=U(X_{t}^{\prime},V_{t}^{\prime})\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\text{\rm{d}}L_{t}^{*}.\end{cases}

Herein,

\displaystyle L_{t}^{*}:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]}\Big\{

\displaystyle((u+\alpha(Q_{s-})_{\kappa})\mathds{1}_{\{l\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho(-\alpha(Q_{s-})_{\kappa},u)\}}

\displaystyle+(u-\alpha(Q_{s-})_{\kappa})\mathds{1}_{\{\frac{1}{2}\rho(-\alpha(Q_{s-})_{\kappa},u)<l\leq\frac{1}{2}(\rho(-\alpha(Q_{s-})_{\kappa},u)+\rho(\alpha(Q_{s-})_{\kappa},u))\}}

\displaystyle+u\mathds{1}_{\{\frac{1}{2}(\rho(-\alpha(Q_{s-})_{\kappa},u)+\rho(\alpha(Q_{s-})_{\kappa},u))<l\leq 1\}}\Big\}\,\widetilde{N}^{e}(\text{\rm{d}}t,\text{\rm{d}}u,\text{\rm{d}}l),

where

(2.8)

Q_{t}:=Z_{t}+\alpha^{-1}W_{t}\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{ with }\penalty\ \penalty\ Z_{t}:=X_{t}-X_{t}^{\prime}\penalty\ \mbox{ and }\penalty\ W_{t}:=V_{t}-V_{t}^{\prime}

and

(2.9)

\rho(x,u):=\frac{\nu_{x}^{*}(\text{\rm{d}}u)}{\nu(\text{\rm{d}}u)},\quad x,u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

A straightforward calculation shows

(2.10)

\text{\rm{d}}L_{t}^{*}=\text{\rm{d}}L_{t}+\alpha(Q_{t-})_{\kappa}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]}\Lambda(\alpha(Q_{t-})_{\kappa},u,l)\,\widetilde{N}^{e}(\text{\rm{d}}t,\text{\rm{d}}u,\text{\rm{d}}l),

where, for
x,u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
l\in[0,1]
,

\Lambda(x,u,l):=\mathds{1}_{\{l\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho(-x,u)\}}-\mathds{1}_{\{\frac{1}{2}\rho(-x,u)<l\leq\frac{1}{2}(\rho(-x,u)+\rho(x,u))\}}.

By invoking (
2.10
), (
2.7
) can be rewritten as

(2.11)

\begin{cases}\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=(aX_{t}+bV_{t})\,\text{\rm{d}}t,\\
\text{\rm{d}}V_{t}\,=U(X_{t},V_{t})\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\text{\rm{d}}L_{t},\\
\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{\prime}=(aX^{\prime}_{t}+bV^{\prime}_{t})\,\text{\rm{d}}t,\\
\text{\rm{d}}V_{t}^{\prime}=U(X_{t}^{\prime},V_{t}^{\prime})\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\text{\rm{d}}L_{t}\\
\qquad\quad+\alpha(Q_{t-})_{\kappa}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]}\Lambda(\alpha(Q_{t-})_{\kappa},u,l)\,\widetilde{N}^{e}(\text{\rm{d}}t,\text{\rm{d}}u,\text{\rm{d}}l).\end{cases}

Note that, under Assumptions
{\bf(A_{0})}

and
{\bf(A_{1})}
the SDE (
1.3
) has a unique strong solution
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Then, by following the proof of
[
15
, Proposition 2.2]
, (
2.7
) also has a unique strong solution
((X_{t},V_{t}),(X_{t}^{\prime},V_{t}^{\prime}))_{t\geq 0}
. Furthermore,
the following statement indicates that
((X_{t},V_{t}),(X_{t}^{\prime},V_{t}^{\prime}))_{t\geq 0}
solving (

) (i.e. (

satisfying (

).

Proposition 2.2
.

The infinitesimal generator of the process
((X_{t},V_{t}),(X_{t}^{\prime},V_{t}^{\prime}))_{t\geq 0}
is just the operator
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}
given by (

). Consequently,

Proof.

Let
\widehat{\mathscr{L}}
be the infinitesimal generator of

. Recall that

for any

Then, according to the structure of (

), we derive from (

) that for any

\displaystyle(\widehat{\mathscr{L}}f)((x,v),(x^{\prime}v^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]}\Big(f\big((x,v+u),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime}+(u+\alpha(q)_{\kappa})\mathds{1}_{\{l\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho(-\alpha(q)_{\kappa},u)\}}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad+(u-\alpha(q)_{\kappa})\mathds{1}_{\{\frac{1}{2}\rho(-\alpha(q)_{\kappa},u)<l\leq\frac{1}{2}(\rho(-\alpha(q)_{\kappa},u)+\rho(\alpha(q)_{\kappa},u))\}}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad+u\mathds{1}_{\{\frac{1}{2}(\rho(-\alpha(q)_{\kappa},u)+\rho(\alpha(q)_{\kappa},u))<l\leq 1\}})\big)

∎

3.
Construction of cost function and related estimates

This section is devoted to the construction of the cost function
\widetilde{\Psi}
in the Wasserstein distance (which is comparable with
\Psi
in Theorem
1.1
) and to presenting some related estimates, both of which are crucial to obtain exponential ergodicity of the process
(X_{t},V_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

3.1.
Rough estimates

For any
x,x^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\alpha_{0},\varepsilon>0
, we set

(3.1)

\begin{split}&z:=x-x^{\prime},\quad w:=v-v^{\prime},\quad q:=z+\alpha^{-1}w,\quad r:=\alpha_{0}|z|+|q|,\\
&H\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big):=f(r),\quad G\big((x,v),(x,v^{\prime})\big):=1+\varepsilon(\mathcal{W}(x,v)+\mathcal{W}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})).\end{split}

Here,
f
is chosen to satisfy that
f(0)=0
,
f^{\prime}>0
and
f^{\prime\prime}<0
on
(0,\infty)
whose explicit expression will be given in the next part, and
\mathcal{W}
is the Lyapunov function given in Assumption
\bf(A_{1})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For the coupling operator
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}
given in (
2.2
), it holds that

(3.2)

\big(\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}(HG)\big)\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big)=\big(H(\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}G)+G(\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}H)+\Pi\big)\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big),

where

(3.3)

\begin{split}\Pi&\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big)\\
&:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(H\big((x,v+u),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime}+u+\alpha(q)_{\kappa})\big)-H\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big)\Big)\\
&\qquad\times\Big(G\big((x,v+u),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime}+u+\alpha(q)_{\kappa})\big)-G\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big)\Big)\,\nu^{*}_{-\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}(\text{\rm{d}}u)\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(H\big((x,v+u),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime}+u-\alpha(q)_{\kappa})\big)-H\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big)\Big)\\
&\qquad\times\Big(G\big((x,v+u),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime}+u-\alpha(q)_{\kappa})\big)-G\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big)\Big)\,\nu^{*}_{\alpha(q)_{\kappa}}(\text{\rm{d}}u).\end{split}

Proof.

Recall that
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}=\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{1}+\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{x,x^{\prime}}
, where
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{1}
and
\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{x,x^{\prime}}
are defined by (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), respectively.
By the chain rule, it is easy to see that

(3.4)

\big(\widetilde{\mathscr{L}_{1}}(HG)\big)\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big)=\big(G\big(\widetilde{\mathscr{L}_{1}}H\big)+H\big(\widetilde{\mathscr{L}_{1}}G\big)\big)\big((x,v),(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big).

On the other hand,

) with
\mathcal{W}
given by (
4.4
).

Finally, we will present the proof of Theorem
1.3
. Recall that (
1.11
) is a special case of the SDE (
1.1
) with
a=0
,
b=1
,
U(x,v)=-\alpha v-\beta\nabla U_{0}(x)
. Obviously, Assumption
{\bf(B_{0})}
implies that
{\bf(A_{0})}
holds. Assumption
{\bf(B_{2})}
is just (
4.6
) in Lemma
4.2
. Therefore, by Proposition
4.3
, in order to prove Theorem
1.3
, we only need to verify the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose that (
1.12
) and Assumption
{\bf(B_{1})}
hold. Then, (
4.2
) holds with
a=0
,
b=1
,
U(x,v)=-\alpha v-\beta\nabla U_{0}(x)
and
\mathcal{V}_{0}(x)=\beta(U_{0}(x)+\lambda_{4}|x|^{2}+\lambda_{5}),
where
\lambda_{4},\lambda_{5}\geq 0
are given in (
1.15
).

Proof.

In view of
\beta>0
and (
1.15
),
\mathcal{V}_{0}(x)=\beta(U_{0}(x)+\lambda_{4}|x|^{2}+\lambda_{5})\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then, for any
r>r_{0}>0
and
\varepsilon>0
, it follows from (
1.14
) and the Young inequality that

\displaystyle\Gamma(x,v):

\displaystyle=\langle r^{2}x+r_{0}v+\nabla\mathcal{V}_{0}(x),ax+bv\rangle+\langle v+r_{0}x,U(x,v)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(r^{2}+2\beta\lambda_{4})x+r_{0}v+\beta\nabla U_{0}(x),v\rangle+\langle v+r_{0}x,-\alpha v-\beta\nabla U_{0}(x)\rangle

\displaystyle=-(\alpha-r_{0})|v|^{2}-\beta r_{0}\langle x,\nabla U_{0}(x)\rangle+(r^{2}+2\beta\lambda_{4}-\alpha r_{0})\langle x,v\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-(\alpha-r_{0})|v|^{2}-\beta r_{0}\lambda_{1}|x|^{2}-\beta r_{0}\lambda_{2}U_{0}(x)+(r^{2}+2\beta\lambda_{4}-\alpha r_{0})\langle x,v\rangle+\lambda_{3}\beta r_{0}

\displaystyle=-(\alpha-r_{0})|v|^{2}-\beta r_{0}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\lambda_{4})|x|^{2}+(r^{2}+2\beta\lambda_{4}-\alpha r_{0})\langle x,v\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-r_{0}\lambda_{2}\mathcal{V}_{0}(x)+\beta r_{0}(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{2}\lambda_{5})

\displaystyle\leq-\Big(\alpha-r_{0}-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}(r^{2}+2\beta\lambda_{4}-\alpha r_{0})^{2}\Big)|v|^{2}-\Big(\beta r_{0}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\lambda_{4})-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)|x|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-r_{0}\lambda_{2}\mathcal{V}_{0}(x)+\beta r_{0}(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{2}\lambda_{5}).

Below, we take
r_{0}=\alpha/2
and

\alpha/2<r<(r_{0}^{2}/2+\alpha_{0}\sqrt{\beta(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\lambda_{4})}-2\beta\lambda_{4})^{1/2},

which is well defined by (
1.13
).
With the
choices of
r_{0}
and
r
above, we have

(r^{2}+2\beta\lambda_{4}-\alpha r_{0})^{2}<4\beta(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\lambda_{4})(\alpha-r_{0})r_{0}.

In particular, we can find a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that

\alpha-r_{0}-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}(r^{2}+2\beta\lambda_{4}-\alpha r_{0})^{2}>0,\quad\beta r_{0}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\lambda_{4})-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}>0.

Therefore, (

5.1.
Wasserstein distance

Let
\Phi
be a function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

such that
\Phi({\bf 0},{\bf 0})=0
and
\Phi
is strictly positive elsewhere. Given two probability measures
\mu_{1}
and

\mu_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the following quantity (which can be called
a Wasserstein-type distance or a Kantorovich distance)

W_{\Phi}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\inf_{\Pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(x,y)\,\text{\rm{d}}\Pi(x,y),

where
\mathscr{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is the collection of all measures on

\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
having
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
as marginals. In
particular, when
\Phi(x,y)=|x-y|^{\theta}
with
\theta\in(0,1]
,
W_{\Phi}
is just the
standard
L^{1}
-Wasserstein distance with the metric
(x,y)\mapsto|x-y|^{\theta}
, which is simply denoted by

W_{1}
when
\theta=1
; on the other hand, when
\Phi(x,y)=\mathds{1}_{\{x\neq y\}}
,
W_{\Phi}

reduces to the total variation distance

W_{\Phi}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|_{\operatorname{Var}}.

Note that in applications it is not necessarily to require that
\Phi(x,y)
is a distance function. Sometime the following type of distance-like function

\Phi(x,y)=\Phi_{1}(x,y)W(x,y),\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

is more applicable, where
\Phi_{1}(x,y)
is a distance function and
W(x,y)
is a strictly positive weighted function fulfilling some growth condition. For example,
\Phi(x,y)=(1\wedge|x-y|)(1+|x|+|y|)^{\theta}
for some
\theta\in(0,1]
as used in Theorem
1.3
. Then, the associated Wasserstein-type distance
W_{\Phi}
is of the multiplicative form.
The use of
the multiplicative distance
W_{\Phi}
is inspired by
[
9
]
, where
the weak Harris’ theorem was
initiated. As mentioned in
[
9
]
, the distance
of multiplicative form is more practical for SDEs with degenerate
noises or infinite dimensional SDEs, where convergence in terms of
the total variation norm no longer holds. We note that the multiplicative distance

W_{\Phi}
is merely a multiplicative semi-metric, since the
triangle inequality may be violated; see
[
9
, Section 4]
for
more related details. See also
[
7
,
13
]
for the study of exponential ergodicity for diffusions and SDEs with jumps (including McKean-Vlasov type SDEs) in terms of the multiplicative distance
W_{\Phi}
, respectively.

5.2.
A technical lemma

For the sake of convenience, let us recall a technical lemma due to
[
13
, Lemma 2.8]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
l_{0}>0
, and let
g\in C([0,2l_{0}])\cap C^{3}((0,2l_{0}])
be such that
g^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
,
g^{\prime\prime}(s)\leq 0,
and
g^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)\geq 0
for
s\in(0,2l_{0}].
Then, for any
c>0,
the function

(5.1)

f(s):=\begin{cases}c\,s+\int_{0}^{s}\text{\rm{e}}^{-g(u)}\text{\rm{d}}u,&\quad s\in[0,2l_{0}],\\
f(2l_{0})+f^{\prime}(2l_{0})\frac{s-2l_{0}}{1+s-2l_{0}},&\quad s>2l_{0},\end{cases}

satisfies

(i)

f\in C^{1}((0,\infty))
and
cs\leq f(s)\leq(c+1)s
for all
s\in[0,2l_{0}];

(ii)

for any
s\in(0,2l_{0}]
,

f^{\prime}(s)\geq 0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ f^{\prime\prime}(s)\leq 0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ f^{(3)}(s)\geq 0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ f^{(4)}(s)\leq 0;

(iii)

for any
0\leq\delta\leq s,

f(s+\delta)+f(s-\delta)-2f(r)\leq 0;

(iv)

for any
0\leq\delta\leq s\leq l_{0},

f(s+\delta)+f(s-\delta)-2f(r)\leq f^{\prime\prime}(s)\delta^{2}.

A typical choice of the function
g
that satisfies the assumption of Lemma
5.1
is

g(r)=\int_{0}^{r}1/\sigma(l)\,\text{\rm{d}}l,

where
\sigma\in C([0,2l_{0}])\cap C^{2}((0,2l_{0}])
is a non-decreasing and concave function such that
\int_{0+}1/\sigma(l)\,\text{\rm{d}}l<\infty.

For example,
\sigma(r)=cr^{\theta}
with some
c>0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
.

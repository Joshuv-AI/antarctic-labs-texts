Existence and uniqueness of solutions to some anisotropic elliptic equations with a singular convection term
G. di Blasio – F. Feo – G. Zecca

Abstract
We prove the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions to a class of anisotropic elliptic equations with coefficients of convection term belonging to some suitable Marcinkiewicz spaces. Some useful a priori estimates and regularity results are also derived.

Introduction

In this paper we obtain existence and uniqueness results for the weak solutions of the following class of Dirichlet problems

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial_{x_{i}}\left[\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\nabla u)+\mathcal{B}_{i}(x,u)\right]+\mathcal{G}(x,u)=\mathcal{F}&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
&\\
u=0&\hbox{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
with Lipschitz boundary,
N>2
,
p_{i}>1
for every
i=1,...,N
with

\bar{p}<N
, denoting by
\overline{p}
the harmonic mean of
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{N})
,
i.e.

\frac{1}{\overline{p}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{p_{i}}.

(1.2)

Throughout this paper, we make the following assumptions for any
i=1,...,N,

(\mathcal{H}1)\quad\mathcal{A}_{i}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory function
that satisfies

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\xi)|\leqslant\beta_{i}|\xi_{i}|^{p_{i}-1}

(1.3)

\displaystyle\alpha|\xi_{i}|^{p_{i}}\leqslant\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\xi)\,\xi_{i}

(1.4)

\displaystyle 0<(\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\xi)-\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\eta))\,(\xi_{i}-\eta_{i})\quad\,\,\xi\neq\eta

(1.5)

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and for any vector
\xi,\eta
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
0<\alpha\leqslant\beta_{i}
are constants.

(\mathcal{H}2)\quad\mathcal{B}_{i}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory function such that

|\mathcal{B}_{i}(x,s)|\leqslant b_{i}(x)|s|^{\frac{\bar{p}}{p_{i}^{\prime}}},

(1.6)

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
, with
b_{i}:\,\Omega\to[0,+\infty)
measurable function such that

b_{i}\in L^{\frac{N\,p^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega).

(1.7)

(\mathcal{H}3)

\mathcal{G}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory function such that

|\mathcal{G}(x,s)|\leqslant\tilde{\mu}|s|^{\gamma}

(1.8)

with
1\leq\gamma<p_{\infty}-1
and

\mathcal{G}(x,s)\,s\geq 0

(1.9)

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\tilde{\mu}
is a non negative constant and
p_{\infty}=\max\{\overline{p}^{*},p_{\max}\}
, with
p_{\max}=\max_{i}p_{i}
.

(\mathcal{H}4)

\mathcal{F}
belongs to the dual space
(W_{0}^{1,\vec{p}}(\Omega))^{*}
, where
W_{0}^{1,\vec{p}}(\Omega)
is the anisotropic Sobolev space defined in Section
2
.

We observe that in our assumptions, the following definition of weak solution is well posed.

Definition 1.1

For any
\mathcal{F}\in(W_{0}^{1,\vec{p}}(\Omega))^{*}
we say that

u\in W_{0}^{1,\vec{p}}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to (
1.1
) provided

\int_{\Omega}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\nabla u)+\mathcal{B}_{i}(x,u)\right]\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi+\mathcal{G}(x,u)\varphi\right]\,dx=\langle\mathcal{F}\,,\varphi\rangle

(1.10)

\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality product of
(W_{0}^{1,\vec{p}}(\Omega))^{*}
and
W_{0}^{1,\vec{p}}(\Omega)
.

In the anisotropic framework the natural space where looking for weak solutions of Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) is the anisotropic Sobolev space
W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
(see
\lx@sectionsign

2
for definition). When the harmonic mean
\bar{p}
, defined in (
1.2
), is less then the dimension
N
, it is well-known (see
\lx@sectionsign

2
) that
W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in the Lorentz space
L^{\bar{p}^{*},\bar{p}}(\Omega)
. On the other hand, by Poincaré inequality (see (
2.7
) below) the space
W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
is embedded in the Lebesgue space
L^{p_{\max}}(\Omega)
. This suggests us to link, as in our assumption (
1.8
), the growths of zero order term with
p_{\infty}
, which takes over how the
p_{i}
are spread out.

The prototype equation of our class of problems (
1.1
) is

-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial_{x_{i}}\left[|\partial_{x_{i}}u|^{p_{i}-2}\partial_{x_{i}}u+\beta_{i}(x)|u|^{\frac{\bar{p}}{p_{i}^{\prime}}-1}u\right]+\tilde{\mu}|u|^{\gamma-1}u=\mathcal{F}\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(1.11)

where
p_{i}\geq 1,\bar{p}<N,\tilde{\mu}\geq 0,1\leq\gamma<p_{\infty}-1,\beta_{i}\in L^{\frac{N\,p^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{F}
belongs to the dual space. We stress that when
\bar{p}^{*}\geq p_{\max}
using the Sobolev embedding in the Lorentz space
L^{\bar{p}^{*},\bar{p}}(\Omega)
the summability assumption on
\beta_{i}
is optimal to assure
|\beta_{i}|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}|u|^{\bar{p}}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Moreover we observe that when
p_{i}=p
,
\beta_{i}=0
and
\widetilde{\mu}=0
, the principal part in equation (
1.11
) becomes the so-called pseudo-Laplacian operator (see
[
27
]
pp. 106 and 155) or orthotropic
p
-Laplacian operator (see
[
11
]
), extensively studied in the literature.

A goal of this paper is to analyze the existence of weak solutions for this class of problems (see
[
21
]
for the isotropic case). Since under our assumptions the coercivity for the involved operator in problem (
1.1
) is not guaranteed, we will proceed as usual by approximations. The existence of weak solutions can be expected when the datum and the coefficients are smooth enough. Then we first consider problem with
b_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and then we reduce to the general case
b_{i}\in L^{\frac{N\,p^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)
assuming a control on suitable distance of
b_{i}
from

(see assumption (

) in Theorem

). This strategy allows us to overcome that the norms in the Marcinkiewicz

are not absolutely continuous and that
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is not dense in Marcinkiewicz spaces.
Finally when we pass to the limit in the approximated problems we have to deal with an extra difficulty due to the fact that in our assumption
the operator
u\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)\rightarrow{b_{i}}(x)|u|^{\frac{\bar{p}}{p^{\prime}_{i}}-1}u\in L^{p^{\prime}_{i}}(\Omega)
is not compact in general. We emphasize that our assumption on the distance (
3.2
), firstly considered in
[

]
in the isotropic case (see also
[

]
,
[

]
), is weaker than asking the smallness of the norms of

which is the standard approach to treat the presence of the first order term.

We also analyze the regularity for weak solutions of problem (
1.1
) and we obtain Stampacchia type regularity, extending
[
10
,
31
,
20
]
where the case
\mathcal{B}\equiv 0
is studied. Regularity results for local solutions of problem (

) has been recently obtained in
[

]
.

For what concerns the uniqueness (see Theorems
7.1
,
7.3
and

) we emphasize that our proofs strongly uses the presence of the zero order term and his monotonicity assumption (
7.1
) below. In order to describe our result, let us reduce
ourselves to the model case (
1.11
) for simplicity. The main difficulty is due to the presence of the lower order terms that are only Hölder continuous, but not Lipschitz continuous with respect to solution
u
when
p_{i}<2
for all
i
.
We recall
[
7
]
for equations with a Hölder continuous dependence on the solution of the coefficient in the main part of the operator.

If one wants to neglect the term
\mathcal{G}
, Corollary
6.2
gives a partial uniqueness result when the datum is zero (see
[

]
for isotropic case).
Otherwise also in the isotropic case the uniqueness is proved in
[

]
requiring a control of the partial derivative of first order term
B(x,u)
with respect to

, which is not satisfied in the simple case
B(x,u)=\textbf{b}(x)|u|^{p-2}u
with
\textbf{b}\in\left(L^{\frac{N}{p-1}}(\Omega)\right)^{N}
.

2
Preliminaries

In the present section we recall some known function spaces, useful in the sequel.

We start by recalling definitions of Lorentz spaces and their properties (see
[
29
]
for more details).

Here we assume that
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
N>2
is an open set. Given
1<p,q<+\infty
, the Lorentz space
L^{p,q}(\Omega)
consists of all measurable functions
f
defined on
\Omega
for which the quantity

\|f\|_{p,q}^{q}=p\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\Omega_{t}|^{\frac{q}{p}}t^{q-1}dt

(2.1)

is finite, where
\Omega_{t}=\left\{x\in\Omega:|f(x)|>t\right\}
and
|\Omega_{t}|
is the Lebesgue measure of
\Omega_{t}
, that is,
\mu_{f}(t)=|\Omega_{t}|
is the distribution function of
f
. Note that
\|\cdot\|_{p,q}
is equivalent to a norm and
L^{p,q}(\Omega)
becomes a Banach space when endowed with it. For
p=q
, the Lorentz space

L^{p,p}(\Omega)
reduces to the Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\Omega)
. For
q=\infty
, the class
L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)
consists of all measurable functions
f
defined on
\Omega
such that

\|f\|^{p}_{p,\infty}=\sup_{t>0}t^{p}\mu_{f}(t)<+\infty

(2.2)

and it coincides with the Marcinkiewicz class or the so-called weak-
L^{p}(\Omega)
.

It well-known that if
\Omega
is bounded, the following inclusions hold

L^{r}(\Omega)\subset L^{p,q}(\Omega)\subset L^{p,r}(\Omega)\subset L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)\subset L^{q}(\Omega),

(2.3)

whenever
1\leqslant q<p<r\leqslant\infty.
Moreover, for
1<p<\infty
,
1\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
,
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
, if
f\in L^{p,q}(\Omega)
,
g\in L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)
we have the Hölder–type inequality

\int_{\Omega}|f(x)g(x)|dx\leqslant\|f\|_{p,q}\|g\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}.

We remark that
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is not dense in
L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)
for
p\in{}]1,+\infty[
. Therefore it is possible to define the distance of a given function
f\in L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)
to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
as

{\rm{dist}}_{L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)}(f,L^{\infty}(\Omega))=\inf_{g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|f-g\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)}.

(2.4)

Note that, since
\|~\|_{p,\infty}
is not a norm,
{\rm{dist}}_{L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)}
is just equivalent to a metric. In
[
15
]
is proved that

{\rm{dist}}_{L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)}(f,L^{\infty}(\Omega))=\lim_{M\to+\infty}\|f-T_{M}f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)},

(2.5)

where the truncation at level
M>0
is defined as

T_{M}\,y=\frac{y}{|y|}\min\{|y|,M\}.

(2.6)

Now,
let
\vec{p}=(p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{N})
with
p_{i}>1
for
i=1,...,N
, and let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. As usual the anisotropic Sobolev space is the Banach space defined as

W^{1,\vec{p}}(\Omega)=\{u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega):\partial_{x_{i}}u\in L^{p_{i}}(\Omega),i=1,...,N\}

equipped with

\|u\|_{W^{1,\vec{p}}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\partial_{x_{i}}u\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\Omega)}.

It is well-known that in the anisotropic setting a Poincarè type
inequality holds true (see
[
23
]
). Indeed for every
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
\Omega
a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary we have

\|u\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\Omega)}\leq C_{P}\|\partial_{x_{i}}u\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\Omega)},\qquad\qquad i=1,...,N

(2.7)

for a constant
C_{P}
proportional to the width of
\Omega
in the direction of
e_{i}
and then to the diameter of
\Omega
. Moreover, for
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R^{N}})
the following anisotropic Sobolev inequality holds true (see
[
32
]
)

\|u\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R^{N}})}\leq S_{N}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\|\partial_{x_{i}}u\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R^{N}})}^{\frac{1}{N}},

(2.8)

where
S_{N}
is an universal constant and
p=\bar{p}^{*}

and
q=\bar{p}
whenever
\bar{p}<N
, where
\bar{p}
is defined in (
1.2
) and

\bar{p}^{*}=\frac{N\bar{p}}{N-\bar{p}}.

(2.9)

Using the inequality between geometric and arithmetic mean we can replace the right-hand-side of (
2.8
) with
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\partial_{x_{i}}u\|_{L^{p_{i}}}
.
In
[
18
]
is proved a generalization of (
2.8
) to product of function. We recall an its simpler form that we will need in the following

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\bar{p}^{*},\bar{p}}(\Omega)}\leq\widetilde{S}_{N}\left\|\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left|\partial_{x_{i}}u\right|\right)^{1/N}\right\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\Omega)},

(2.10)

for a suitable universal constant
\widetilde{S}_{N}
.

When
\overline{p}<N
and
\Omega
is a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary, the space
W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)=\overline{C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\partial_{x_{i}}u\|_{L^{p_{i}}}}
is continued embedding into
L^{q}(\Omega)
for
q\in[1,p_{\infty}]
, with

p_{\infty}:=\max\{\overline{p}^{\ast},\max_{i}p_{i}\}
as a consequence of (
2.8
) and (
2.7
).

We recall the following useful lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(
see
[
28
, page 43]
)
Let
X
be a rearrangement invariant space and let
0\leq\theta_{i}\leq 1
for
i=1,...,M,
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{M}\theta_{i}=1
, then

\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{M}|f_{i}|^{\theta_{i}}\right\|_{X}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{M}\|f_{i}\|_{X}^{\theta_{i}}\quad\forall f_{i}\in X.

3
An useful a priori estimates

In this section we suppose that a weak solution
u
of the problem (
1.1
) exists and we prove the following a priori estimate under suitable assumptions on
{\rm{dist}}_{L^{\frac{Np^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)}(b_{i},L^{\infty}(\Omega))
, defined in (
2.4
), for
i=1,\cdots,N
.

Since
\mathcal{F}
belongs to
(W_{0}^{1,\vec{p}}(\Omega))^{*}
, in what follows we can write it as

\mathcal{F}=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}(f_{i})_{x_{i}},\mbox{ with }f_{i}\in L^{p_{i}^{\prime}}(\Omega)\,\,\forall i=1,...,N.

(3.1)

Lemma 3.1

Let us assume that
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain,
p_{i}>1
for
i=1,..,N
,
\bar{p}<N
,
(\mathcal{H}1)-(\mathcal{H}4)
are in force and let
u\in W_{0}^{1,\vec{p}}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of (
1.1
). Then there exists a positive constant
d=d(N,\alpha,\vec{p})
such that whenever

\max_{i}\left\{{\emph{dist}}_{L^{\frac{Np^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)}(b_{i},L^{\infty}(\Omega))\right\}<d,

(3.2)

the following uniform estimate holds

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}|u_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}dx\leq C,

(3.3)

where
C=C(\alpha,N,\overrightarrow{p},d,\|\mathcal{F}\|_{(W_{0}^{1,\vec{p}}(\Omega))^{*}}).

Proof.
Using as test function
T_{k}u
in (
1.10
), by (
1.4
) and (
1.6
) we get

\displaystyle\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|u_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}dx+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}(x,u)T_{k}u\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|b_{i}(x)||u|^{\frac{\bar{p}}{p_{i}^{\prime}}}|u_{x_{i}}|dx+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|f_{i}||u_{x_{i}}|dx,

where
\Omega_{k}=\{x\in\Omega:|u(x)|<k\}
. For all
M>0
, using (
1.9
), Young and Hölder inequality we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|u_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}dx\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|u|^{\bar{p}}dx+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|b_{i}(x)-T_{M}b_{i}\|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}_{L^{\frac{Np^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)}\|T_{k}u\|_{L^{\bar{p}^{*},\bar{p}}(\Omega)}^{\bar{p}}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|f_{i}|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}dx\right),

where
C
is a suitable positive constant which can vary from line to line.

Denoting
B=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\|(T_{k}u)_{x_{i}}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\Omega)}
, by Sobolev inequality (
2.8
) it follows

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|u_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}dx\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}|u|^{\bar{p}}dx+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|b_{i}(x)-T_{M}b_{i}\|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}_{L^{\frac{Np^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)}B^{\frac{\bar{p}}{N}}\right.

(3.4)

\displaystyle+\left.\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|f_{i}|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}dx\right).

Previous inequality gives us an estimate of the
j^{th}
addendum of the sum at the left-hand side of (
3.4
) as well. Then, elevating to the power
\frac{1}{Np_{j}}
, making the product on the left and right sides of (
3.4
) we get

\displaystyle B^{\frac{1}{N}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}|u|^{\bar{p}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\bar{p}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|b_{i}(x)-T_{M}b_{i}\|^{\frac{p^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}}}_{L^{\frac{Np^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)}B^{\frac{1}{N}}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|f_{i}|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\bar{p}}}\right].

At this point, assuming
d<\left(\frac{1}{CN}\right)^{\frac{\bar{p}}{p^{\prime}_{i}}}
in (
3.2
) and using (
2.5
), we can fix now
M
large enough in order to have

\displaystyle B^{\frac{1}{N}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}|u|^{\bar{p}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\bar{p}}}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|f_{i}|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\bar{p}}}\right].

(3.5)

Combining (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{k}}

\displaystyle|u_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}dx\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}|u|^{\bar{p}}dx+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|f_{i}|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}dx\right),

(3.6)

for every
k>0
and with
C
positive costant independent of
u
and
k.

We prove now that previous estimate gives (
3.3
). To this aim we follow the idea of
[
21
, Lemma 2]
. We argue by contradiction and assume that there exists a sequence of functions
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subseteq W^{1,\vec{p}}_{0}(\Omega)

satisfying (
3.6
) and such that

\|u_{n}\|:=\|u_{n}\|_{W^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}_{0}(\Omega)}\rightarrow\infty

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
For every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\varepsilon>0
, we set
k_{n}=\varepsilon\|u_{n}\|
so that by (
3.6
), for
i=1,...,N,

\begin{split}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{x_{i}}(T_{k_{n}}u_{n})|^{p_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}&\leqslant C^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\left(1+\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}|^{\bar{p}}\chi_{\{|u_{n}|<k_{n}\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}},\\
\end{split}

where
T_{k_{n}}
is defined in (
2.6
). Since
\bar{p}<\bar{p}^{*}
, using Lemma
2.1
with
X=L^{\bar{p}}(\Omega)
and
\theta_{i}=\frac{\bar{p}}{p_{i}N}
, we get from the previous inequality

\begin{split}\left(\int_{\Omega}\prod^{N}_{i=1}|\partial_{x_{i}}(T_{k_{n}}u_{n})|^{\frac{\bar{p}}{N}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{\bar{p}}}&\leqslant\prod^{N}_{i=1}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{x_{i}}(T_{k_{n}}u_{n})|^{p_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{Np_{i}}}\\
&\leqslant C^{\frac{1}{\bar{p}}}\left(1+\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}|^{\bar{p}}\chi_{\{|u_{n}|<k_{n}\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{\bar{p}}}.\\
\end{split}

(3.7)

We set

w_{n}=\frac{u_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|}\,.

so that, up to subsequence not relabeled, there exists
\bar{w}\in W^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

w_{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{w}
weakly in
W^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
,
w_{n}\to\bar{w}
strongly in
L^{\bar{p}}(\Omega)
and
w_{n}\to\bar{w}
for a.e. in
\Omega
.
Dividing both sides of
(
3.7
)
by
\|u_{n}\|^{\bar{p}}
we have

\int_{\Omega}\prod^{N}_{i=1}|\partial_{x_{i}}(T_{\varepsilon}w_{n})|^{\frac{\bar{p}}{N}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\prod^{N}_{i=1}|\partial_{x_{i}}(T_{k_{n}}u_{n})|^{\frac{\bar{p}}{N}}}{\|u_{n}\|^{\bar{p}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leqslant C\left(\frac{1}{\|u_{n}\|^{\bar{p}}}+\int_{\Omega}|w_{n}|^{\bar{p}}\chi_{\{|w_{n}|<\varepsilon\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right).

(3.8)

Assume now that

|\{x\in\Omega:|\bar{w}(x)|=\varepsilon\}|=0\,.

(3.9)

In this case we have
\chi_{\{|w_{n}|<\varepsilon\}}\to\chi_{\{|\bar{w}|<\varepsilon\}}
a.e. in
\Omega
and hence
w_{n}\,\chi_{\{|w_{n}|<\varepsilon\}}\to\bar{w}\,\chi_{\{|\bar{w}|<\varepsilon\}}

strongly in
L^{\bar{p}}(\Omega)
. So, since
T_{\varepsilon}w_{n}\rightharpoonup T_{\varepsilon}\bar{w}
weakly in
W^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
and
T_{\varepsilon}w_{n}\to T_{\varepsilon}\bar{w}
strongly in
L^{\bar{p}}(\Omega)
, letting
n\to+\infty
in (
3.8
), using the semicontinuity of the norm with respect to weak convergence in
W^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
, we arrive to the following estimate

\int_{\Omega}{\prod^{N}_{i=1}|\partial_{x_{i}}(T_{\varepsilon}\bar{w})|^{\frac{\bar{p}}{N}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leqslant C\int_{\Omega}|\bar{w}|^{\bar{p}}\chi_{\{|\bar{w}|<\varepsilon\}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(3.10)

Using Sobolev inequality (
2.10
) and Hölder inequality by (
3.10
) we get

\varepsilon^{\bar{p}}\,|\{x\in\Omega:|\bar{w}|\geqslant\varepsilon\}|^{\frac{\bar{p}}{\bar{p}*}}\leqslant C\,\varepsilon^{\bar{p}}\,|\{x\in\Omega:0<|\bar{w}|<\varepsilon\}|\,.

Passing to the limit as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, we deduce

|\{x\in\Omega:|\bar{w}|>0\}|=0\,,

that is,
\bar{w}(x)=0
a.e. Note that previous equality has been obtained assuming (
3.9
). Nevertheless, the set of values
\varepsilon>0
for which (
3.9
) fails is at most countable.
This means
w_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
W^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
. On the other hand, at this point we can use again the same argument above to obtain that
w_{n}\to 0
strongly in
W^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
, and this gives the contradiction, since by definition
\|w_{n}\|=1
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

4
Regularity results

In this section we study the regularity of weak solutions of problem (
1.1
). Here and in the following we shall assume notation (
3.1
) being in force. When the datum
f_{i}\in L^{p_{i}}(\Omega)
for
i=1,..,N
with
\bar{p}<N
, as follows by the anisotropic Sobolev embedding, a solution
u
belongs to
L^{\overline{p}^{*}}(\Omega)
, where
\bar{p}^{*}
is defined in (
2.9
). Otherwise if
{f}_{i}\in L^{s_{i}}(\Omega)
with
s_{i}>p_{i}
for
i=1,..,N
, the summability of
u
improves. In order to study the higher summability of
u
, the following minimum

\mu=\min_{i}\left\{\frac{s_{i}}{p_{i}^{\prime}}\right\}

(4.1)

first introduced in
[
10
]
, plays a crucial role. In
[
18
, Theorem 2.1 and Remark 5.3]
the following Stampacchia type regularity result is proved (see
[
30
]
as classical reference).

Theorem 4.1

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
p_{i}>1
for
i=1,..,N
,
\bar{p}<N
and let
s_{1},\cdots,s_{N}
be such that

1<\mu<\frac{N}{\bar{p}},

where
\mu
is defined in (
4.1
). Assume that
(\mathcal{H}1)-(\mathcal{H}3)
are fulfilled and
f_{i}\in L^{s_{i}}(\Omega)
for
i=1,..,N
. There exists a positive constant
d=d(\vec{r},N,\alpha,\vec{p})
such that if

\max_{i}\left\{{\emph{dist}}_{L^{\frac{Np^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)}(b_{i},L^{\infty}(\Omega))\right\}<d

and
u\in W^{1,\vec{p}}_{0}(\Omega)

is a weak solution to (
1.1
), then

u\in L^{s}(\Omega)\quad\text{with }s=\max\{(\mu\bar{p})^{*},\mu p_{\max}\}.

where

(\mu\bar{p})^{*}=\frac{N\mu\bar{p}}{N-\mu\bar{p}}.

Without lower order terms in
[
10
]
the authors have proved that the boundedness of a weak solution of Dirichlet problems is guaranteed under the assumption

\mu>\frac{N}{\bar{p}},

where
\mu
is defined in (
4.1
). However if
\mathcal{B}_{i}\not\equiv 0
for
i=1,\cdots,N
the boundedness is not assured assuming that (
3.2
) is in force as showed in Example 4.8 of
[
24
]
(when
p_{i}=2
for
i=1,\cdots,N
). The smallness of
\|b_{i}\|_{L^{\frac{Np_{i}^{\prime}}{\bar{p}},\infty}}
for
i=1,\cdots,N
neither is sufficient to get boundedness, as showed in Example 2.3 of
[
18
]
.

In order to get the boundedness of solutions, we need to improve the summability of data and of the coefficients
b_{i}
.

Lemma 4.2

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
p_{i}>1
for
i=1,..,N
,
\bar{p}<N
,
and let
s_{1},\cdots,s_{N}
be such that

\mu>\frac{N}{\bar{p}}

(4.2)

where
\mu
is defined in (
4.1
). Assume that
(\mathcal{H}1)-(\mathcal{H}3)
are fulfilled and
b_{i},f_{i}\in L^{s_{i}}(\Omega)
for
i=1,..,N
. Then every weak solution
u
of problem (
1.1
) is bounded and there exists a positive constant
C=C(\alpha,\Omega,N,\overrightarrow{p},\|{b}_{i}\|_{L^{s_{i}}(\Omega)},\|{f}_{i}\|_{L^{s_{i}}(\Omega)})
such that

\|u\|_{\infty}\leq C.

Proof.
The proof is quite standard, and for the convenience of the reader we give some details. So, let
u
be a weak solution of problem (
1.1
). For
k>0
, we use as a test function
G_{k}u:=u-T_{k}u
in (
1.10
) and by (
1.3
), (
1.6
) we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}|(G_{k}u)_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}dx+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}(x,u)G_{k}udx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}|b_{i}(x)||u|^{\frac{\bar{p}}{p_{i}^{\prime}}}|(G_{k}u)_{x_{i}}|dx+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}|f_{i}||(G_{k}u)_{x_{i}}|dx.

Denoting
A_{k}=\{x\in\Omega:|u(x)|>k\}
, we note that by Lemma
3.1
for every
k\geq 0
we have:

|A_{k}|\leq\frac{\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}}{k}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{k},

(4.3)

where
C_{0}
is a positive constant indepedent on
u
. We stress that (
3.2
) is obviously satisfied when
b_{i}\in L^{s_{i}}(\Omega)
.

Using (
1.9
) and Young inequality we have

\displaystyle\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}|(G_{k}u)_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}dx\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{A_{k}}|b_{i}(x)|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}|G_{k}u|^{\bar{p}}dx+C\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{A_{k}}|b_{i}(x)|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}k^{\bar{p}}dx

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}|(G_{k}u)_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}dx+C\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{A_{k}}|f_{i}|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}dx+\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{A_{k}}|(G_{k}u)_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}dx,

for every
\varepsilon>0
and a suitable
C>0
. Choosing
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\alpha,\vec{p})
small enough we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}|(G_{k}u)_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}dx\leq C\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{A_{k}}|b_{i}|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}|G_{k}u|^{\bar{p}}dx+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{A_{k}}|b_{i}(x)|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}k^{\bar{p}}dx+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{A_{k}}|f_{i}|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|b_{i}\|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}_{L^{\frac{Np^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}}}(A_{k})}\|G_{k}u\|_{L^{\bar{p}^{*}}(\Omega)}^{\bar{p}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{A_{k}}\left(|b_{i}(x)|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}k^{\bar{p}}+|f_{i}|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}\right)dx\right],

where, here and below,
C
is a suitable positive constant which can vary from line to line.

As before, denoting
B=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\|(G_{k}u)_{x_{i}}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(A_{k})}
, by Sobolev inequality (
2.8
) it follows

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}|(G_{k}u)_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}dx\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|b_{i}\|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}_{L^{\frac{Np^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}}}(A_{k})}B^{\frac{\bar{p}}{N}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{A_{k}}\left(|b_{i}(x)|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}k^{\bar{p}}+|f_{i}|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}\right)dx\right].

(4.4)

Previous inequality gives us an estimate of the
j^{th}
addendum of the sum at the left-hand side of (
4.4
) as well. Then, elevating to the power
\frac{1}{Np_{j}}
, making the product on the left and right sides of (
4.4
) we get

\displaystyle B^{\frac{1}{N}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|b_{i}(x)\|^{\frac{p^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}}}_{L^{\frac{Np^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}}}(A_{k})}B^{\frac{1}{N}}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{A_{k}}(|b_{i}(x)|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}k^{\bar{p}}+|f_{i}|^{p_{i}^{\prime}})dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\bar{p}}}\right].

At this point, we can choose
k=k(N,\vec{p},b_{i},\alpha,C)>1
large enough in order to have
|A_{k}|
small and so by absolutely continuity of the Lebesgue norm we have

\displaystyle B^{\frac{1}{N}}\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{A_{k}}(|b_{i}(x)|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}k^{\bar{p}}+|f_{i}|^{p_{i}^{\prime}})dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\bar{p}}}\right]

(4.5)

for suitable
C_{1}>0
. Combining (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}|(G_{k}u)_{x_{i}}|^{p_{i}}dx\leq C_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{A_{k}}(|b_{i}(x)|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}k^{\bar{p}}+|f_{i}|^{p_{i}^{\prime}})dx\right).

(4.6)

As before, we note that previous inequality gives us an estimate of the
j^{th}
addendum of the sum at the left-hand side of (
4.6
) as well. Then, elevating to the power
\frac{1}{Np_{j}}
, making the product on the left and right sides of (
4.6
) and using Sobolev inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|G_{k}u\|_{L^{\bar{p}^{*}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{A_{k}}(|b_{i}(x)|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}k^{\bar{p}}+|f_{i}|^{p_{i}^{\prime}})dx\right)^{\frac{1}{Np_{j}}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{A_{k}}(|b_{i}(x)|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}k^{\bar{p}}+|f_{i}|^{p_{i}^{\prime}})dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\bar{p}}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}k\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\|b_{i}\|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}_{s_{i}}+\|f_{i}\|_{s_{i}}^{p_{i}^{\prime}})\,\,|A_{k}|^{1-\frac{p^{\prime}_{i}}{s_{i}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\bar{p}}}.

We fix now
k_{0}>1
such that
|A_{k_{0}}|<1
. Note that in view of (
4.3
) we can fix such
k_{0}
independent on
u
.

Applying Hölder inequality at the left hand side of previous inequality, for every
k\geq k_{0}
we get

\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}u|\,dx\leq C\,k|A_{k}|^{\frac{\eta}{\bar{p}}+1-\frac{1}{\bar{p}^{*}}}\qquad\mbox{ where }\quad\eta=\min_{i}\{1-\frac{p^{\prime}_{i}}{s_{i}}\}.

(4.7)

Observe now that the function

g(k):=\int_{\Omega}|G_{k}u|\,dx

is a non-negative and decreasing function such that
g^{\prime}(k)=-|A_{k}|
. Hence we can rewrite (
4.7
) as

\left(\frac{g(k)}{k}\right)^{a}\leq-Cg^{\prime}(k)\qquad\mbox{ where }\quad a:=\frac{\bar{p}\bar{p}^{*}}{\eta\bar{p}^{*}+\bar{p}\bar{p}^{*}-\bar{p}}

and by (
4.2
), it holds
(1-a)>0
.
Let us consider now every value of
k>k_{0}
such that
g(k)\not=0.
By previous inequality we get

{k}^{-a}\leq-Cg^{\prime}(k)g(k)^{-a}=-C(g(k)^{(1-a)})^{\prime}.

Integrating previous inequality with respect to
k
from
k_{0}
to
k
we get

{k}^{1-a}-{k_{0}}^{1-a}\leq-C(g(k)^{1-a}-g(k_{0})^{(1-a)}).

This implies

g(k)^{1-a}\leq Cg(k_{0})^{(1-a)}-{k}^{1-a}+{k_{0}}^{1-a}.

It is obvious at this point that, since
g(k_{0})\leq\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
, there exists a value
\bar{k}
(independent in
u
) such that
g(\bar{k})=0
and the thesis follows.

5
Existence result

In this section we analyze the existence of weak solutions of problem (
1.1
).
It is well known that, if the operator in problem (
1.1
) is coercive, then a solution exists in
W_{0}^{1,\vec{p}}(\Omega)
. Unfortunately, under our assumptions the coercivity for the involved operator in problem (
1.1
) is not guaranteed.
Another difficulty is due to the
singularity of coefficients
b_{i}
in the lower order term. Indeed in the Marcinkiewicz space
L^{\frac{N\,p^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)
, which is slightly larger than Lebesgue space
L^{\frac{N\,p^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}}}(\Omega)
, the bounded functions are not dense and the norm is not absolutely continuous (i.e. a function can have large norm even if restricted to a set with small measure).

Theorem 5.1

Let us assume that
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain,
p_{i}>1
for
i=1,..,N
,
\bar{p}<N
and (
\mathcal{H}1
)-(
\mathcal{H}4
) are in force. There exists a positive constant
d=d(N,\alpha,\vec{p})
such that if (
3.2
) holds,
then there exists
at least a weak solution to problem (
1.1
).

For example in our prototype (
1.11
) we can consider as coefficient of the lower order term the function

\beta_{i}(x)=\frac{\kappa_{i}}{|x|^{\gamma_{i}}}+\beta_{i}^{0}(x)

with
\kappa_{i}
suitable small constant,
\beta_{i}^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and

\gamma_{i}=\frac{\bar{p}}{p_{i}^{\prime}}
for
i=1,\cdots,N
. Indeed, in this case, an easy computation shows that

{\rm{dist}}_{L^{\frac{Np^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)}(\beta_{i},L^{\infty}(\Omega))=|\kappa_{i}|\omega_{N}^{\frac{\gamma_{i}}{N}}
.

Our proof is detailed in the next subsection.

5.1
Proof of Theorem
5.1

We split the proof in three steps.

Step 1: Existence when
b_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
f_{i}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
for
i=1,\cdots,N
.

We stress that also in this case the operator
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[-\partial_{x_{i}}(\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\nabla u)+\mathcal{B}_{i}(x,u))\right]+\mathcal{G}(x,u)
can be not coercive. To overcome this difficulty we use the boundedness result proved in Lemma
4.2
and so we suppose that a solution
u
there exists. Using Lemma
4.2
, it follows that

\|u\|_{\infty}\leq M,

(5.1)

where
M
is a constant depending only on the data of the equation.

Now we define

\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}(x,s)=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}\mathcal{B}(x,s)&\hbox{ if }|s|\leq M,\\
\mathcal{B}(x,M)&\hbox{ if }|s|>M.\end{array}\right.

Problem (
1.1
) with
\mathcal{B}(x,s)=\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}(x,s)
,
b_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and smooth data has a solution
u
, since the operator is coercive. Moreover this solution
u
verifies estimate (
5.1
), because
\mathcal{B}(x,s)
verifies (
1.6
). It follows that
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}(x,s)=\mathcal{B}(x,s)
and we have proved the existence of a solution to problem (
1.1
) when
b_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and data are smooth.

Step 2: Existence when
b_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{F}\in(W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega))^{*}
.

We consider the following sequence of approximate problems

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial_{x_{i}}\left[\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\nabla u_{n})+\mathcal{B}_{i}(x,u_{n})\right]+\mathcal{G}(x,u_{n})=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}(f^{n}_{i})_{x_{i}}&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
&\\
u_{n}=0&\hbox{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(5.2)

where
f^{n}_{i}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

f^{n}_{i}\rightarrow f_{i}\quad\text{ in }L^{p_{i}^{\prime}}(\Omega),\quad\|f^{n}_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i}^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq\|f_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i}^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\text{ for }i=1,\cdots,N.

(5.3)

Step 1 assure the existence of a solution
u_{n}
of problem (
5.2
). We stress that estimate (
3.3
) holds for problem (
5.2
) when
b_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
for
i=1,\cdots,N
.

We get that the sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded in
W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
by a constant independent of
n
. Then there exists a function
u\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
such that (up to a subsequence denoted again by
u_{n}
)

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\text{ weakly in }W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega),

(5.4)

u_{n}\to u\text{ in }L^{q}(\Omega)\text{ for }q<p_{\infty}

(5.5)

and

u_{n}\to u\text{ a.e. }\Omega.

(5.6)

To take into account the terms
\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\nabla u_{n})
we need the following lemma, which is the anisotropic version of Lemma 2.2 of
[
27
]
. Note that if
A_{i}
is strongly monotone, the claim is obvious.

Lemma 5.2

Assume
(\mathcal{H}1)
be in force. Let us suppose
u_{n},u\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
,
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
and

\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\nabla u_{n})-\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\nabla u)\right)\left[\partial_{x_{i}}u_{n}-\partial_{x_{i}}u\right]\,dx\rightarrow 0

(5.7)

for
i=1,\cdots,N
. Then for
i=1,\cdots,N

\partial_{x_{i}}u_{n}\rightarrow\partial_{x_{i}}u\quad\text{ a.e. in }\Omega.

(5.8)

Proof.
The proof is standard and follows
as in Lemma 2.2 of
[
27
]
. For the convenience of the reader we give some details. Let us denote for
i=1,\cdots,N

Q_{n}^{i}(x):=\left(\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\nabla u_{n}(x))-\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\nabla u(x))\right)\left[\partial_{x_{i}}u_{n}(x)-\partial_{x_{i}}u(x)\right].

Since (
1.5
) and (
5.7
) we get (up to a subsequence) that

Q_{n}^{i}(x)\rightarrow 0\text{ for every }x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0}

with
|\Omega_{0}|=0
. Moreover by our assumptions we get (up to a subsequence)
u_{n}(x)\rightarrow u(x)
for
x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0}
(using that
W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{q}(\Omega)
for
q<p_{\infty}
). Let us suppose
x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0}
and let us denote
\partial_{x_{i}}u_{n}(x)=\xi_{n}^{i}
and
\partial_{x_{i}}u(x)=\xi^{i}
and by
\bar{\xi}^{i}
one of the limit of
\xi_{n}^{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,N
. By (
1.4
) and (
1.3
) we have

Q_{n}^{i}(x)\geq\alpha|\xi_{n}^{i}|^{p_{i}}-\beta_{i}|\xi_{n}^{i}|^{p_{i}-1}|\xi^{i}|-\beta_{i}|\xi_{n}^{i}||\xi^{i}|^{p_{i}-1}.

(5.9)

If we assume
|\bar{\xi}^{i}|=+\infty
then by (
5.9
) we obtain
Q_{n}^{i}(x)\rightarrow+\infty
, which is a contradiction (see (
5.7
)). Then
|\bar{\xi}^{i}|<+\infty\quad\forall i=1,\cdots,N
. Using the continuity of
\mathcal{A}_{i}
with respect to
\xi
we conclude that

\left(\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\bar{\xi})-\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\xi)\right)\left[\bar{\xi}^{i}-\xi^{i}\right]=0\quad\forall i=1,\cdots,N.

So assumption (
1.5
) yields then
\bar{\xi}=\xi
, i.e.
\partial_{x_{i}}u_{n}(x)\rightarrow\partial_{x_{i}}u(x)\quad\forall i=1,\cdots,N
for every
x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0}
.

Now we prove that (
5.7
) is in force. We use
\varphi=u_{n}-u
as test function in the variational formulation of the approximating problems (
5.2
):

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left[\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\nabla u_{n})+\mathcal{B}_{i}(x,u_{n})\right]\partial_{x_{i}}(u_{n}-u)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}(x,u_{n})(u_{n}-u)\,dx=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}f_{i}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}(u_{n}-u)\,dx.

Adding and subtracting in the previous equality the term

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\nabla u)\partial_{x_{i}}(u_{n}-u)
, by (
1.3
), (
5.4
) and (
5.3
) we easily obtain that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\nabla u)\partial_{x_{i}}(u_{n}-u)\rightarrow 0\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}f_{i}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}(u_{n}-u)\,dx\rightarrow 0.

Then recalling again (
5.4
) in order to prove (
5.7
) it is enough to show that

\mathcal{B}_{i}(x,u_{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{i}(x,u)\text{ in }L^{p^{\prime}_{i}}(\Omega)\quad\forall i=1,\cdots,N,

(5.10)

\mathcal{G}(x,u_{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{G}(x,u)\text{ in }L^{p^{\prime}_{\infty}}(\Omega).

(5.11)

To this aim we observe that by continuity of
\mathcal{B}_{i}
with respect to
s
and by (
5.6
) we get
\mathcal{B}_{i}(x,u_{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{i}(x,u)
a.e. in
\Omega
. Now (
1.6
), Hölder inequality, Sobolev inequality (
2.8
) and (
3.3
) yields that for every measurable set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|\mathcal{B}_{i}(x,u_{n})|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}\,dx&\leq\|b_{i}\|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}_{\infty}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|u_{n}|^{\bar{p}}\,dx\leq\|b_{i}\|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}_{\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|u_{n}|^{\bar{p}^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{\bar{p}}{\bar{p}^{*}}}|\Omega^{\prime}|^{1-\frac{\bar{p}}{\bar{p}^{*}}}\\
&\leq C\|b_{i}\|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}_{\infty}|\Omega^{\prime}|^{1-\frac{\bar{p}}{\bar{p}^{*}}},\end{split}

where
C
is independent of
n
. By Vitali convergence Theorem we conclude that (
5.10
) holds.

On the other hand by continuity of
\mathcal{G}
with respect to
s
we get
\mathcal{G}(x,u_{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{G}(x,u)
a.e. in
\Omega
, by (
5.6
). Now (
1.8
), Hölder inequality, Sobolev inequality (
2.8
) and (
3.3
) yields that for every measurable set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega

Then (

(5.12)

Taking

Step 3 : Existence dropping assumptions that

&\\

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left[\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\nabla u_{n})+\mathcal{\vartheta}^{i}_{n}(x)\mathcal{B}_{i}(x,u_{n})\right]\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi_{n}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}(x,u_{n})\varphi_{n}\,dx=\langle\mathcal{F}\,,\varphi_{n}\rangle

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left[\frac{\mathcal{\vartheta}^{i}_{n}(x)\mathcal{B}_{i}(x,u_{n})}{1+|u_{n}-u|^{2}}\right]^{p^{\prime}_{i}}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left[b_{i}(x)^{p^{\prime}_{i}}|u|^{\bar{p}}+\frac{b_{i}(x)^{p^{\prime}_{i}}|u_{n}-u|^{\bar{p}}}{1+|u_{n}-u|^{2}}\right]dx,

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\frac{b_{i}(x)^{p^{\prime}_{i}}|u_{n}-u|^{\bar{p}}}{1+|u_{n}-u|^{2}}\leq\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}b_{i}(x)^{p^{\prime}_{i}}|u_{n}-u|^{(\bar{p}-2)}dx\leq\|b_{i}\|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}_{L^{s^{\prime}\,p^{\prime}_{i}}(\Omega^{\prime})}\|u_{n}-u\|^{(\bar{p}-2)}_{s(\bar{p}-2)}\leq C\|b_{i}\|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}_{L^{s^{\prime}\,p^{\prime}_{i}}(\Omega^{\prime})},

\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\nabla u_{n})-\mathcal{A}_{i}(x,\nabla u)\right)\left[\partial_{x_{i}}\eta(u_{n}-u)\right]\,dx\rightarrow 0.

Now, since

The end of proof runs as in the previous step, obtaining that

6
Positivity of solutions

In the isotropic case it is well known (see
[
8
]

b(x)\in L^{N/p-1}(\Omega)
, then the solution is non-negative. In this section we prove a similar result for the anisotropic problem (
1.1
).
In order to give a precise statement we introduce the following notation:
\mathcal{F}\geq 0
means
<\mathcal{F},\phi>\geq 0
for every
\phi\geq 0
when
\mathcal{F}\in(W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega))^{*}
. We emphasize that the following result holds assuming
\mathcal{G}\equiv 0
as well.

Proposition 6.1

Let
u\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
be a weak solution to problem (
1.1
) under assumptions (
\mathcal{H}1
)-(
\mathcal{H}4
). If
\mathcal{F}\geq 0
, then we have
u\geq 0
.

Proof.
Let us denote
u^{-}(x):=\min\{0,u(x)\}
. Taking
T_{h}(u^{-})
as test function with
h>0
in (
1.10
), by (
1.4
), (
1.6
), (
1.9
) and assumption on the data we get

\displaystyle\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\{-h<u<0\}}|\partial_{x_{i}}T_{h}(u^{-})|^{p_{i}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\{-h<u<0\}}|b_{i}(x)||u|^{\frac{\bar{p}}{p^{\prime}_{i}}}|\partial_{x_{i}}T_{h}(u^{-})|dx.

By Young inequality it follows that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\{-h<u<0\}}|\partial_{x_{i}}T_{h}(u^{-})|^{p_{i}}dx\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\{-h<u<0\}}|b_{i}(x)|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}|u|^{\bar{p}}dx,

(6.1)

where
C
is a suitable positive constant. Using the definition (
2.2
) in (
6.1
) we get for every

\displaystyle\int_{\{-h<u<0\}}|\partial_{x_{j}}T_{h}(u^{-})|^{p_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\{-h<u<0\}}|\partial_{x_{i}}T_{h}(u^{-})|^{p_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\bar{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|b_{i}\|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}_{L^{\frac{Np_{i}^{\prime}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{0}^{|\{-h<u<0\}|}s^{-\bar{p}/N}\,ds.

By Sobolev inequality (
2.8
), the previous inequality becomes

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|T_{h}(u^{-})|^{\bar{p}^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\bar{p}^{*}}}\leq C\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(\int_{\{-h<u<0\}}|\partial_{x_{j}}T_{h}(u^{-})|^{p_{j}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{Np_{j}}}

(6.2)

\displaystyle\leq h\left(\int_{0}^{|\{-h<u<0\}|}s^{-\bar{p}/N}\,ds\right)^{1/\bar{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|b_{i}\|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}_{L^{\frac{Np_{i}^{\prime}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{1}{Np_{j}}}.

Now we observe that for
\delta>h
,

h|\{u<-\delta\}|^{1/\bar{p}^{*}}=\left(\int_{\{u<-\delta\}}|T_{h}(u^{-})|^{\bar{p}^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\bar{p}^{*}}}.

(6.3)

Combining (
6.2
) and (
6.3
), it follows that

|\{u<-\delta\}|^{1/\bar{p}^{*}}\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{|\{-h<u<0\}|}s^{-\bar{p}/N}\,ds\right)^{1/\bar{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|b_{i}\|^{p_{i}^{\prime}}_{L^{\frac{Np_{i}^{\prime}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{1}{Np_{j}}}.

Observing that

\int_{0}^{|\{-h<u<0\}|}s^{-\bar{p}/N}\,ds\rightarrow 0\text{ as }h\rightarrow 0^{+},

we conclude that the measure of the set
\{u<-\delta\}
is zero for every
\delta>0
.

As a consequence of Proposition
6.1
we get the following partial uniqueness result when
\mathcal{F}\equiv 0
.

Corollary 6.2

be a weak solution to problem (
1.1
) under assumptions (
\mathcal{H}1
)-(
\mathcal{H}4
). If
\mathcal{F}\equiv 0
, then we have
u\equiv 0
.

Proof.
The thesis follows arguing as in the previous proposition taking
T_{h}(u^{-})
and
T_{h}(u^{+})
as test function.

We stress that a similar argument can be used to prove the uniqueness when

for all

and the coefficient

8

7
Uniqueness results

First of all we observe that prototype (
1.11
) verifies a strongly monotone condition, but not
the assumption of Lipschitz continuity in
u
when

are not all equals and
p_{i}\leq 2
for

as required in the classical uniqueness result.
Then the novelty of our result consists in the possibility to
deal with cases when
\mathcal{B}(x,u)
is only Hölder continuous with respect to

(see (
7.3
) below). In general the presence of a zero order term could help in order to obtain uniqueness result, even in the case
p_{i}\geq 2
for

. In what follows we assume that

\mathcal{G}\left(x,s\right)\text{ is strictly monotone increasing function in $s$.}

(7.1)

Note that whenever
\mathcal{G}
satisfies (
7.1
) and
\mathcal{G}(x,0)\equiv 0
for all

then (
1.9
) obviously holds. For example
\mathcal{G}\left(x,s\right)=\widetilde{\mu}|s|^{\gamma-1}s
with
\widetilde{\mu}>0,0<\gamma<p_{\infty}-1
verifies (
7.1
) and (
1.8
) and (
1.9
).
We observe that we can relax condition (
7.1
) assuming that

\left(\mathcal{G}\left(x,s\right)-\mathcal{G}\left(x,s^{\prime}\right)\right)(s-s^{\prime})>0\text{ for }s>s^{\prime}.

As usual when we deal with uniqueness results for equation in which we have the dependence on the unknown
u
we consider two cases: first when
p_{i}\leq 2
for every
i=1,\cdots,N
, then when
p_{i}\geq 2
for every

7.1
First case:

for every
i=1,\cdots,N

In this subsection we prove the uniqueness of weak solutions of problem (

) when all
p_{i}\leq 2
.
For example in model cases (

with
\beta_{i}\in L^{\frac{Np^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)

We assume that

\left(\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}_{i}\left(x,\xi\right)-\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\xi^{\prime}\right)\right)\left(\xi_{i}-\xi_{i}^{\prime}\right)\geq\widetilde{\alpha}\left(\left|\xi_{i}\right|+\left|\xi_{i}^{\prime}\right|\right)^{p_{i}-2}\left|\xi_{i}-\xi_{i}^{\prime}\right|^{2}

(7.4)

for

We stress that
\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\xi\right)=\alpha|\xi_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\xi_{i}

\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,s\right)=\beta_{i}(x)|s|^{\frac{\bar{p}}{p_{i}^{\prime}}-1}s

7.3

\frac{\bar{p}}{p_{i}^{\prime}}\leq 1

\bar{p}\leq 2\leq p_{i}^{\prime}

Theorem 7.1

Let
u\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)

) under assumptions (

)-(

\mathcal{G}\not\equiv 0

Proof.
We generalize the ideas contained in
[
14

Let
u
and
v
be two weak solutions to problem (

). Let us denote

w=\left(u-v\right)^{+}

\varepsilon>0

\int_{\delta(\varepsilon)}^{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{s^{2r_{\min}}}ds=1.

\Psi^{\sigma}_{\varepsilon}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}0&\hbox{ se }t\leq\delta(\varepsilon),\\
\int_{\delta(\varepsilon)}^{t}\frac{1}{s^{\sigma}}ds&\hbox{ se }\delta(\varepsilon)<t<\varepsilon\\

(7.5)

We take
\Psi_{\varepsilon}(t)=\Psi^{\sigma}_{\varepsilon}

\sigma=2r_{\min}

\Psi_{\varepsilon}(t)

\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t)\geq 0

\varphi\Psi_{\varepsilon}(w)\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)

\varphi\geq 0

\varphi\in W^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)

Supposing that
D
has positive measure, we use
\varphi\Psi_{\varepsilon}(w)
as test function in the difference of the equations. It follows

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}:=

\displaystyle\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\int_{\Omega}\left\{\left[\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\nabla u\right)-\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\nabla v\right)\right]+\left[\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,u\right)-\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,v\right)\right]\right\}\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi\Psi_{\varepsilon}(w)\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left[\mathcal{G}(x,u)-\mathcal{G}(x,v)\right]\varphi\Psi_{\varepsilon}(w)\,dx=

\displaystyle-\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\int_{\Omega}\left\{\left[\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\nabla u\right)-\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\nabla v\right)\right]+\left[\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,u\right)-\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,v\right)\right]\right\}\partial_{x_{i}}w\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w)\varphi\,dx.

By (
7.2
) and (
7.3
) we get

\displaystyle-\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\left\{\left[\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\nabla u\right)-\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\nabla v\right)\right]\partial_{x_{i}}w\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w)\varphi+\left[\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,u\right)-\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,v\right)\right]\right\}\partial_{x_{i}}w\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w)\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle\leq-\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{\alpha}\frac{|\partial_{x_{i}}w|^{2}}{(|\partial_{x_{i}}u|+|\partial_{x_{i}}v|)^{2-p_{i}}}\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w)\varphi\,dx+\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{b}_{i}(x)w^{r_{i}}|\partial_{x_{i}}w|\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w)\varphi\,dx,

where
r_{i}=\frac{\bar{p}}{p^{\prime}_{i}}
. Using Young inequality with
\theta<\widetilde{\alpha}
we obtain

\displaystyle-\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\left\{\left[\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\nabla u\right)-\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\nabla v\right)\right]+\left[\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,u\right)-\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,v\right)\right]\right\}\partial_{x_{i}}w\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w)\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\int_{\Omega}(\widetilde{\alpha}-\theta)\frac{|\partial_{x_{i}}w|^{2}}{(|\partial_{x_{i}}u|+|\partial_{x_{i}}v|)^{2-p_{i}}}\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w)\varphi\,dx+

\displaystyle+C(\theta)\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{b}^{2}_{i}(x)w^{2r_{i}}(|\partial_{x_{i}}u|+|\partial_{x_{i}}v|)^{2-p_{i}}\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w)\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\theta)\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\varepsilon^{2(r_{i}-r_{\min})}\int_{\{\delta(\varepsilon)<w<\varepsilon\}}\widetilde{b}^{2}_{i}(x)(|\partial_{x_{i}}u|+|\partial_{x_{i}}v|)^{2-p_{i}}\,dx=J_{\varepsilon}.

We underline that
\widetilde{b}^{2}_{i}(x)(|\partial_{x_{i}}u|+|\partial_{x_{i}}v|)^{2-p_{i}}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Indeed we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{b}^{2}_{i}(x)(|\partial_{x_{i}}u|+|\partial_{x_{i}}v|)^{2-p_{i}}\,dx&\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{b}_{i}^{p^{\prime}_{i}}(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p^{\prime}_{i}}}\left(\int_{\Omega}(|\partial_{x_{i}}u|+|\partial_{x_{i}}v|)^{p_{i}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}\\
&\leq C(\Omega,p_{i})\|\widetilde{b}_{i}\|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}_{L^{\frac{N\,p^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{\Omega}(|\partial_{x_{i}}u|+|\partial_{x_{i}}v|)^{p_{i}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}.\end{split}

Since
(r_{i}-r_{\min})\geq 0
, letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
we get
J_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0
. Moreover
\Psi_{\varepsilon}(w)\rightarrow\chi_{\{u-v>0\}}
, where
\chi_{\{u-v>0\}}
denotes the characteristic function of the set
\{u-v>0\}
. By the Lebesgue dominated convergence Theorem we obtain

\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\int_{\{u-v>0\}}\left\{\left[\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\nabla u\right)-\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\nabla v\right)\right]+\left[\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,u\right)-\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,v\right)\right]\right\}\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi+\left[\mathcal{G}(x,u)-\mathcal{G}(x,v)\right]\varphi\,dx\leq 0.

(7.6)

Taking
\varphi=1
in (
7.6
) it follows

\int_{\{u-v>0\}}\left[\mathcal{G}(x,u)-\mathcal{G}(x,v)\right]\,dx\leq 0.

Assumption (
7.1
) allows us to conclude that
{\{u-v>0\}}
ha zero measure. Exchanging
u
for
v
we conclude.

7.2
Second case:
p_{i}\geq 2
for every
i=1,\cdots,N

In this subsection we prove the uniqueness of weak solutions of problem (
1.1
) when all
p_{i}\geq 2
.
In the model cases (
1.11
) the term
B_{i}(x,u)=\beta_{i}(x)|u|^{\frac{\bar{p}}{p^{\prime}_{i}}-1}u
with
|\beta_{i}|\in L^{\frac{Np^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)
are locally Lipschitz continuous with respect to solution
u
, but it is well-known that even in the isotropic case when
p>2
the uniqueness can fail (see for example in
[
2
]
at the end of Section 2). Again to prove uniqueness we will strongly use the presence of zero order term.
We assume

\left(\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}_{i}\left(x,\xi\right)-\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\xi^{\prime}\right)\right)\left(\xi_{i}-\xi_{i}^{\prime}\right)\geq\widehat{\alpha}\left|\xi_{i}-\xi_{i}^{\prime}\right|^{p_{i}}

(7.7)

with
\widehat{\alpha}>0
and

\left|\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,s\right)-\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,s^{\prime}\right)\right|\leq\widehat{b}_{i}(x)\left|s-s^{\prime}\right|\left(|s|+|s^{\prime}|+\zeta\right)^{\frac{\bar{p}}{p^{\prime}_{i}}-1}

(7.8)

with
\widehat{b}_{i}(x)
defined as in (
7.4
) and
\zeta\geq 0
.
We stress that
\frac{\bar{p}}{p^{\prime}_{i}}\geq 1
under our assumptions. Moreover
\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\xi\right)=\alpha|\xi_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\xi_{i}
verifies (
7.7
) and
\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,s\right)=\beta_{i}(x)|s|^{\frac{\bar{p}}{p_{i}^{\prime}}-1}s
verifies (
7.3
), recalling that
p_{i}^{\prime}\leq 2\leq\bar{p}
.

Theorem 7.3

Let
u\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
be a weak solution to problem (
1.1
) under assumptions (
\mathcal{H}1
)-(
\mathcal{H}4
) with
p_{i}\geq 2
for
i=1,..,N
,
\bar{p}<N
and
\mathcal{G}\not\equiv 0
. If (
7.1
), (
7.7
) and (
7.8
) are in force, then
u
is the unique weak solution to problem (
1.1
).

Proof.
The idea of the proof follows the previous theorem. Let
u
and
v
be two weak solutions to problem (
1.1
). Let us denote

w=\left(u-v\right)^{+}
and
D=\left\{x\in\Omega:w>0\right\}
.

Let
\Psi_{\varepsilon}(t)=\Psi^{\sigma}_{\varepsilon}(t)
with
\sigma=\min_{i}{p^{\prime}_{i}}
defined in (
7.5
).
Supposing that
D
has positive measure, we use
\varphi\Psi_{\varepsilon}(w)
as test function in the difference of the equations. By
(
7.7
) and (
7.8
) we get

\displaystyle-\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\left\{\left[\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\nabla u\right)-\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\nabla v\right)\right]\partial_{x_{i}}w\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w)\varphi+\left[\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,u\right)-\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,v\right)\right]\right\}\partial_{x_{i}}w\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w)\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle\leq-\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\alpha}|\partial_{x_{i}}w|^{p_{i}}\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w)\varphi\,dx+\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\int_{\Omega}\widehat{b}_{i}w\left(|u|+|v|+\zeta\right)^{r_{i}-1}|\partial_{x_{i}}w|\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w)\varphi\,dx,

where we put
r_{i}=\frac{\bar{p}}{p^{\prime}_{i}}
.
Using Young inequality with
\theta<\widehat{\alpha}
we obtain

\displaystyle-\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\left\{\left[\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\nabla u\right)-\mathcal{A}_{i}\left(x,\nabla v\right)\right]+\left[\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,u\right)-\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(x,v\right)\right]\right\}\partial_{x_{i}}w\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w)\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\int_{\Omega}(\widehat{\alpha}-\theta)|\partial_{x_{i}}w|^{p_{i}}\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w)\varphi\,dx+

\displaystyle+C(\theta)\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\int_{\Omega}\widehat{b}_{i}^{p^{\prime}_{i}}w^{p^{\prime}_{i}}\left(|u|+|v|+\zeta\right)^{(r_{i}-1)p^{\prime}_{i}}\Psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w)\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\theta)\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\overset{N}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\varepsilon^{(p^{\prime}_{i}-\min_{i}p^{\prime}_{i})}\int_{\{\delta(\varepsilon)<w<\varepsilon\}}\widehat{b}_{i}^{p^{\prime}_{i}}\left(|u|+|v|+\zeta\right)^{(\bar{p}-p^{\prime}_{i})}\,dx=J_{\varepsilon}.

We underline that
\widehat{b}^{p^{\prime}_{i}}_{i}\left(|u|+|v|+\zeta\right)^{(\bar{p}-p^{\prime}_{i})}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Indeed we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\widehat{b}_{i}^{p^{\prime}_{i}}\left(|u|+|v|+\zeta\right)^{(r_{i}-1)p^{\prime}_{i}}\,dx&\leq C\left(\|\widehat{b}_{i}\|^{{}^{p^{\prime}_{i}}}_{L^{\frac{N\,p^{\prime}_{i}}{\bar{p}},\infty}(\Omega)}\||u|+|v|\|^{\bar{p}-p^{\prime}_{i}}_{L^{\frac{N(\bar{p}-p^{\prime}_{i})}{N-\bar{p}},\bar{p}-p^{\prime}_{i}}(\Omega)}+\|\widehat{b}_{i}\|^{p^{\prime}_{i}}_{L^{p^{\prime}_{i}}(\Omega)}\right).\end{split}

The right-hand side is finite, since
u,v\in L^{\frac{N(\bar{p}-p^{\prime}_{i})}{N-\bar{p}},\bar{p}-p^{\prime}_{i}}(\Omega)
and
L^{\bar{p}^{*},\bar{p}}(\Omega)\subset L^{\frac{N(\bar{p}-p^{\prime}_{i})}{N-\bar{p}},\bar{p}-p^{\prime}_{i}}(\Omega)
. We conclude as in Theorem
7.1
letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Quantitative Runge type approximation theorems for zero solutions of certain partial differential operators
A. Debrouwere 1, T. Kalmes 2

Abstract
. We prove quantitative Runge type approximation results for spaces of smooth zero solutions of several classes of linear partial differential operators with constant coefficients. Among others, we establish such results for arbitrary operators on convex sets, elliptic operators, parabolic operators, and the wave operator in one spatial variable. Our methods are inspired by the study of linear topological invariants for kernels of partial differential operators. As a part of our work, we also show a qualitative Runge type approximation theorem for subspace elliptic operators, which seems to be new and of independent interest. Keywords: Quantitative Runge type approximation theorems; Quantitative Lax-Malgrange theorem; Partial differential operators; Linear topological invariants for kernels of differential operators MSC 2020: 35A35, 35E20, 46A63



1.
Introduction and statement of the main results

A well-known consequence of Runge’s classical theorem on approximation by rational functions is that for open subsets
Y\subseteq X
of the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, every holomorphic function on
Y
can be approximated uniformly on compact subsets by holomorphic functions on
X
if and only if
X
does not contain a compact connected component of
\mathbb{C}\backslash Y
. This result has been generalized independently by Lax
[
18
]
and Malgrange
[
19
]
to kernels of elliptic constant coefficient differential operators. Browder
[
2
,
3
]
thoroughly investigated Runge type approximation theorems for elliptic differential operators with variable coefficients in various function and distribution spaces.

For non-elliptic differential operators much less is known. Given any non-zero constant coefficient partial differential operator
P(D)
, Malgrange
[
19
, Chapitre 1.2, Théorème 2]
(see also
[
11
, Theorem 10.5.1]
) showed that the linear span of the exponential solutions of
P(D)
is dense in the space of smooth zero solutions of
P(D)
on a convex set. Hörmander
[
11
, Theorem 10.5.2]
and Trèves
[
26
, Theorem 26.1]
proved implicit approximation results for general constant coefficient partial differential operators that are applicable to arbitrary open sets
Y,X
and to
P
-convex open sets
Y,X
, respectively. However, geometrical conditions on
Y,X
(
Y
non-convex) ensuring the validity of a Runge type approximation theorem are only known for special types of operators. In
[
5
,
9
,
13
]
approximation theorems for the heat operator have been shown. The second author
[
17
]
recently extended these results to constant coefficient differential operators with a single characteristic direction, including the Schrödinger operator and parabolic operators like the heat operator. Runge type approximation theorems for variable coefficient parabolic differential operators of second order have been lately obtained in
[
6
]
.

In the past few years, there has been a considerable interest in
quantitative
approximation results. Roughly speaking, the term quantitative here means that given an approximation error
\varepsilon>0
and a solution
f
to a differential equation on a certain set, the size of the approximant
h_{\varepsilon}
, which is a solution to the same differential equation on a larger set, can be estimated in terms of
\varepsilon
and the size of
f
(where the size of a function is measured in terms of a scale of seminorms). The goal is thus to determine the cost of approximation in the context of Runge type theorems. In their seminal work
[
24
]
, Rüland and Salo proved quantitative Runge type approximation theorems for fractional Schrödinger operators. Shortly after this work, quantitative approximation results were shown for various other types of operators, see
[
23
]
for elliptic variable coefficient operators of second order,
[
25
]
for the fractional heat and wave operator, and
[
7
]
for the Schrödinger operator. We remark that quantitative approximation results may be used to obtain stability results for various ill-posed inverse problems
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
.

Inspired by the above results, we establish in the present paper quantitative Runge type approximation results for spaces of zero solutions of several classes of constant coefficient partial differential operators. Most notably, we consider arbitrary operators on convex sets, elliptic operators, parabolic operators, and the wave operator in one spatial variable.

Let
P\in\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}]
be a polynomial and consider the corresponding constant coefficient differential operator
P(D)=P(-i\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,-i\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{d}})
. Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open. We write
\mathcal{E}(X)
for the space of smooth functions on
X
endowed with its natural Fréchet space topology, i.e., the one induced by the family of seminorms

(1.1)

\|f\|_{K,r}=\sup_{x\in K,|\alpha|\leq r}|\partial^{\alpha}f(x)|,\qquad K\subseteq X\mbox{ compact},r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

We set

\mathscr{E}_{P}(X)=\{f\in\mathscr{E}(X)\,;\,P(D)f=0\}

and endow it with the subspace topology from
\mathscr{E}(X)
. A pair of open sets
Y\subseteq X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies the (qualitative) Runge approximation property with respect to the operator
P(D)
if the restriction map

r^{P}_{\mathscr{E}}:\mathscr{E}_{P}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}_{P}(Y),f\mapsto f_{|Y}

has dense range. Our goal is to give quantified versions of this property in terms of the seminorms defined in (
1.1
). If
P(D)
is hypoelliptic, the topology of the space
\mathscr{E}_{P}(X)
is generated by the sup-seminorms
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{K}=\|\,\cdot\,\|_{K,0}
,
K\subseteq X
compact. Hence, in this case, it seems more natural to look for quantitative approximation results with respect to the seminorms
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{K}
.

We now state a sample of our main results. We start with a quantitative approximation result for arbitrary operators on convex sets.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}]\backslash\{0\}
. Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact and convex. Let
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open with
K\subseteq Y
. Then, for all
L\subseteq Y
compact with
K\subseteq\operatorname{int}L
,
M\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
compact, and
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there are
s,C>0
such that

\displaystyle\forall f\in\mathscr{E}_{P}(Y),\varepsilon\in(0,1)\,\exists h_{\varepsilon}\in\mathscr{E}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,:

\displaystyle\|f-h_{\varepsilon}\|_{K,r_{1}}\leq\varepsilon\|f\|_{L,r_{1}+1}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{M,r_{2}}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{s}}\|f\|_{L}.

If
P(D)
is hypoelliptic, we have that for all
L\subseteq Y
compact with
K\subseteq\operatorname{int}L
and
M\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
compact there are
s,C>0
such that

\displaystyle\forall f\in\mathscr{E}_{P}(Y),\varepsilon\in(0,1)\,\exists h_{\varepsilon}\in\mathscr{E}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,:

\displaystyle\|f-h_{\varepsilon}\|_{K}\leq\varepsilon\|f\|_{L}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{M}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{s}}\|f\|_{L}.

Next, we consider elliptic operators. An open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to have
continuous boundary
if for each
\xi\in\partial U
there exists a homeomorphism
h
from the closed unit ball
\overline{B}(0,1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
onto a compact neighborhood
K
of
\xi
such that
h(0)=\xi

and
\overline{U}\cap K=h\left(\{x\in\overline{B}(0,1)\,;\,x_{d}\leq 0\}\right)
. Obviously, every Lipschitz domain has continuous boundary. The next result may be considered as a quantified version of the Lax-Malgrange theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}]
be elliptic and let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open. Let
K
be a compact subset of
X
that is the closure of an open set with continuous boundary. Suppose that
X
does not contain a bounded connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K
. Let
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open with
K\subseteq Y\subseteq X
.
Then, for all
L\subseteq Y
compact with
K\subseteq\operatorname{int}L
and
M\subseteq X
compact there are
s,C>0
such that

\displaystyle\forall f\in\mathscr{E}_{P}(Y),\varepsilon\in(0,1)\,\exists h_{\varepsilon}\in\mathscr{E}_{P}(X)\,:

\displaystyle\|f-h_{\varepsilon}\|_{K}\leq\varepsilon\|f\|_{L}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{M}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{s}}\|f\|_{L}.

Theorem
1.2
for the Cauchy-Riemann operator is due to Petzsche
[
21
, Theorem 4.2 (a)]
(he did not need to assume that
U
has continuous boundary). To the best of our knowledge, Petzsche’s result seems to be the first quantitative Runge type approximation result. Furthermore, Theorem
1.2
complements
[
23
, Theorem 3]
, where a similar result is proven for elliptic variable coefficient operators of second order with respect to Sobolev type norms.

In
[
17
, Corollary 4]
an approximation result for certain
k
-parabolic operators on tubular domains is shown. We now give a quantified version of it.
We denote the elements of
\mathbb{R}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by
(t,x)
with
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
Q\in\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}]
be an elliptic polynomial of degree
m
with principal part
Q_{m}
, let
r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r<m
, and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
.
Suppose that
\{Q_{m}(x)\,;\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}\}\cap\{\alpha t^{r}\,;\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}=\emptyset
. Consider the (hypoelliptic) differential operator

P(D)=\alpha D^{r}_{t}-Q(D_{x}).

Let
G\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open. Let
K
be a compact subset of
G
that is the closure of an open set with continuous boundary.
Suppose that
G
does not contain a bounded connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash K
, and let
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an interval. Let
H\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open with
K\subseteq H\subseteq G
and let
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an open interval with
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subseteq J
. Then, for all
L\subseteq H
compact with
K\subseteq\operatorname{int}L
,
\delta>0
with
[t_{1}-\delta,t_{2}+\delta]\subseteq J
, and
M\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times G
compact there are
s,C>0
such that

\displaystyle\forall f\in\mathscr{E}_{P}(J\times H),\varepsilon\in(0,1)\,\exists h_{\varepsilon}\in\mathscr{E}_{P}(\mathbb{R}\times G)\,:

\displaystyle\|f-h_{\varepsilon}\|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]\times K}\leq\varepsilon\|f\|_{[t_{1}-\delta,t_{2}+\delta]\times L}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{M}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{s}}\|f\|_{[t_{1}-\delta,t_{2}+\delta]\times L}.

Theorem
1.3
is particularly applicable to parabolic operators like the heat operator.

We also consider the wave operator in one spatial variable.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
P(D)=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial_{t}^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial_{x}^{2}}
and let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be open such that
X
is
P
-convex for supports. Let
K\subseteq X
be compact and let
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be open with
K\subseteq Y\subseteq X
such that there is no
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
X
contains a compact connected component of one of the sets
(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash Y)\cap\{(x_{1}+s,x_{2}+s)\,;\,s\in\mathbb{R}\}
or
(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash Y)\cap\{(x_{1}-s,x_{2}+s)\,;\,s\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Then, for all
L\subseteq Y
compact with
L=\overline{\mbox{int}(L)}
and
K\subseteq\operatorname{int}L
such that there is no
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
Y
contains a bounded connected component of one of the sets
(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash L)\cap\{(x_{1}+s,x_{2}+s)\,;\,s\in\mathbb{R}\}
, or
(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash L)\cap\{(x_{1}-s,x_{2}+s)\,;\,s\in\mathbb{R}\}
,
M\subseteq X
compact and
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there are
s,C>0
such that

\displaystyle\forall f\in\mathscr{E}_{P}(Y),\varepsilon\in(0,1)\,\exists h_{\varepsilon}\in\mathscr{E}_{P}(X)\,:

\displaystyle\|f-h_{\varepsilon}\|_{K,r_{1}}\leq\varepsilon\|f\|_{L,r_{1}+1}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{M,r_{2}}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{s}}\|f\|_{L}.

Theorems
1.1
-
1.3
are more natural than Theorem
1.4
in the sense that in the former results we impose
geometric conditions on the compact set on which we approximate the given function
f
, while in the latter we need to impose such conditions on its supersets
Y
and
L
. We believe that an analogue of Theorem
1.3
for the wave operator in one spatial variable is true but are unable to show this.

To the best of our knowledge, approximation results for the wave operator have not yet been studied. The proof of Theorem
1.4
depends heavily on the fact that the wave operator in one spatial variable can be factored into two first order operators. Therefore, our proof cannot be extended to the multidimensional setting. It would be very interesting to find Runge type approximation results for the wave operator in several spatial variables.

As already stated above, Malgrange
[
19
, Chapitre 1.2, Théorème 2]
(see also
[
11
, Theorem 10.5.1]
) showed that the linear span of the exponential solutions of
P(D)
is always dense in
\mathscr{E}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, by
[
19
, Chapitre 1.2, Théorème
2^{\prime}
]
, polynomial solutions of
P(D)
are dense in
\mathscr{E}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if every non-constant factor of
P
vanishes at the origin. Hence, in any of the above global approximation results, i.e., when
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the approximating function
h_{\varepsilon}
can always be chosen to be a linear combination of exponential solutions of
P(D)
. If every non-constant factor of
P
vanishes at the origin,
h_{\varepsilon}
can even be chosen to be a polynomial solution of
P(D)
.

Finally, we would like to point out that the way we deduce quantitative approximation results from qualitative ones is different from the one used by Rüland and Salo in
[
23
,
24
]
. Their method relies on a quantitative unique continuation result and techniques from spectral theory for Hilbert spaces (singular value decompositions) combined with a duality argument. Instead, we use here an
internal
quantitative approximation result for the space
\mathscr{E}_{P}(X)
(Proposition
4.7
) to achieve this goal. The latter is shown in two steps: First, we prove an analogous result for the space of distributional zero solutions (Proposition
4.6
) by combining a result from
[
28
, Section 3.4.5]
with abstract functional analytic arguments (Grothendieck’s factorization theorem and the Schwartz kernel theorem). Hereafter, we deduce the desired result for the space of smooth zero solutions from it by a regularization procedure developed in our recent article
[
4
]
. This method is heavily inspired by the study of linear topological invariants for kernels of partial differential operators
[
1
,
4
,
16
,
27
]
, we explain this relation at the end of Section
2
.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
we discuss some preliminary notions and results. We also give an outline of our strategy to deduce quantitative approximation results from qualitative ones. Section
3
is devoted to qualitative Runge type approximation theorems. We show an approximation result for subspace elliptic differential operators and apply it to obtain such a result for differential operators that factor into first order operators, including the wave operator in one spatial variable. The main technical core of our work is given in Section
4
, where we prove a crucial auxiliary quantitative approximation result for the space
\mathscr{E}_{P}(X)
. Finally, in Section
5
, we establish quantitative Runge type approximation theorems for various types of operators. In particular, we show Theorems
1.1
-
1.4
.

2.
Preliminaries and outline of the proof strategy

In this section we fix the notation and recall various notions and results which will be used throughout. We also sketch our strategy to obtain quantitative Runge type approximation results. We use standard terminology from functional analysis
[
12
,
20
]
, distribution theory
[
10
]
, and linear partial differential operators
[
11
]
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open. We denote by
\mathscr{E}(X)
the Fréchet space of smooth functions on
X
, its topology is generated by the seminorms (
1.1
). Given
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
compact, we write
\mathscr{D}(K)
for the space of smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with support in
K
and endow it with the subspace topology from
\mathscr{E}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The space of test functions on
X
is given by

\mathscr{D}(X)=\varinjlim_{\tiny{K\subseteq X\mbox{ compact}}}\mathscr{D}(K).

The space of distributions
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)
is defined as the dual of
\mathscr{D}(X)
, we endow it with its strong dual topology.

Let
P\in\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}]
and consider the corresponding differential operator
P(D)=P(-i\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,-i\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{d}})
. We set
\check{P}(\xi)=P(-\xi)
. Let
P(\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}
, with
m
the degree of
P
. We denote by
P_{m}
the principal part of
P
, i.e.,
P_{m}(\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}
.
A hyperplane
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,;\,\langle N,x\rangle=c\}
,
N\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
,
c\in\mathbb{R}
, is called a
characteristic hyperplane
for
P(D)
if
P_{m}(N)=0
. The differential operator
P(D)
defines a continuous linear self map on both
\mathscr{E}(X)
and
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)
. We set

\mathscr{E}_{P}(X)=\{f\in\mathscr{E}(X)\,;\,P(D)f=0\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{P}(X)=\{f\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)\,;\,P(D)f=0\},

and endow these spaces with the subspace topology from
\mathscr{E}(X)
and
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)
, respectively.

Malgrange
[
19
]
(see also
[
11
, Section 10.6]
) showed that
P(D):\mathscr{E}(X)\to\mathscr{E}(X)
is surjective if and only if
X
is
P
-convex for supports
, i.e., if for every
K\subseteq X
compact there is
L\subseteq X
compact such that for all
f\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(X)

\operatorname{supp}\,\check{P}(D)f\subseteq K\Longrightarrow\operatorname{supp}\,f\subseteq L.

Hörmander
[
11
, Section 10.7]
showed that
P(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)
is surjective if and only if
X
is both
P
-convex for supports as well as
P
-convex for singular supports
, the latter meaning that for every
K\subseteq X
compact there is
L\subseteq X
compact such that for all
f\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(X)

\operatorname{sing\,supp}\,\check{P}(D)f\subseteq K\Longrightarrow\operatorname{sing\,supp}\,f\subseteq L.

By the theorems of support and singular support
[
10
, Theorem 7.3.2 and Theorem 7.3.9]
, convex open sets
X
are
P
-convex for supports and singular supports for any
P\neq 0
. If
P
is elliptic, every
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
open is
P
-convex for supports and singular supports
[
11
, Corollary 10.8.2 and Theorem 11.1.1]
.

We define
P^{+}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d+1})=P(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d})
and call the corresponding differential operator
P^{+}(D)
the
augmented operator
of
P(D)
. As explained at the end of this section, the surjectivity of
P^{+}(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})
will be one of our basic assumptions for establishing quantitative Runge type approximation results. Note that
P(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)
is always surjective if
P^{+}(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})
is so. The question whether
P^{+}(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})
is surjective if
P(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)
is so, was posed by Bonet and Domański
[
1
, Proposition 8.1]
in connection with the problem of parameter dependence of surjective differential operators. In
[
15
]
the second author showed that for
d=2
the augmented operator
P^{+}(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})
of a surjective differential operator
P(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)
is always surjective, while, for
d\geq 3
, he constructed in
[
14
]
a (hypoelliptic) differential operator and an open set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
P(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)
is surjective but
P^{+}(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})
is not.

Next, we give some positive results concerning the surjectivity of the augmented operator for certain classes of differential operators
[
15
,
16
]
. To this end, recall that a function
f:X\rightarrow[0,\infty]
,
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
open, is said to satisfy the
minimum principle
in a closed set
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
if for every compact subset
K
of
X\cap F
it holds that

\inf_{x\in K}f(x)=\inf_{x\in\partial_{F}K}f(x),

where
\partial_{F}K
denotes the boundary of
K
in
F
. The
boundary distance
of
X
is given by the function

d_{X}:X\rightarrow[0,\infty],\quad d_{X}(x)=\inf\{|x-y|\,;\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash X\}.

A polynomial
P\in\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}]
is said to
act along a subspace

W\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
if
P(\xi)=P(\pi_{W}\xi)
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\pi_{W}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
W
. A polynomial
P
which acts along a subspace
W
is said to be
elliptic on

W
if its principal part
P_{m}
satisfies
P_{m}(\xi)\neq 0
for every
\xi\in W\backslash\{0\}
. In such a case,
P(D)
is called
subspace elliptic
. For example, the augmented operator of an elliptic operator is subspace elliptic. We also mention that for a semi-elliptic polynomial
P
with principal part
P_{m}
the set
\{P_{m}=0\}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,;\,P_{m}(\xi)=0\}
is a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see e.g.
[
8
, Proposition 2(iii)]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}]
and let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open.

(a)

[
16
, Theorem 9 and Theorem 16]
Suppose that
P
acts along the subspace
W
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and is elliptic there. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

P(D):\mathscr{E}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(X)
is surjective.

(ii)

P(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)
is surjective.

(iii)

P^{+}(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})
is surjective.

(iv)

The boundary distance
d_{X}
satisfies the minimum principle in the affine subspace
x+W
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(b)

[
16
, Corollary 5 and Theorem 16]
Suppose that
P
is semi-elliptic with principal part
P_{m}
such that
\{P_{m}=0\}
is one-dimensional. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

P(D):\mathscr{E}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(X)
is surjective.

(ii)

P(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)
is surjective.

(iii)

P^{+}(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})
is surjective.

(iv)

The boundary distance
d_{X}
satisfies the minimum principle in every characteristic hyperplane for
P(D)
.

(c)

[
15
, Theorem 21]
Assume that
d=2
. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

P(D):\mathscr{E}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(X)
is surjective.

(ii)

P(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)
is surjective.

(iii)

P^{+}(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})
is surjective.

(iv)

The boundary distance
d_{X}
satisfies the minimum principle in every characteristic line for
P(D)
.

Remark 2.2
.

Theorem
2.1
(a) with
W=\mathbb{R}^{d}
impies that
P^{+}(D):\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})
is always surjective if
P(D)
is elliptic. This result is essentially due to Vogt
[
27
]
.

We now outline our strategy to obtain quantitative Runge type approximation results. In particular, we explain how our method is inspired by and connected to the linear topological invariants
(\Omega)
for

\displaystyle\forall g\in\mathscr{E}_{P}(X),\varepsilon\in(0,1)\,\exists h_{\varepsilon}\in\mathscr{E}_{P}(X):

3.
Qualitative Runge type approximation results

The purpose of this section is to show a qualitative Runge type approximation theorem for subspace elliptic differential operators. As a consequence, we will give an approximation result for differential operators that factor into first order operators, including the wave operator in one spatial variable. Furthermore, we recall a Runge type approximation theorem for semi-elliptic operators with a single characteristic direction from

Proposition 3.1

Let

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
Y\subseteq X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open. Let
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a non-empty closed set such that
d_{X}
satisfies the minimum principle in
x+F
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose that there is no
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
X
contains a compact connected component of
(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Y)\cap(x+F)
. Then,
d_{Y}
satisfies the minimum principle in
x+F
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

We may assume
Y\neq X
. We argue by contradiction, so assume that there is
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
K\subseteq Y\cap(x+F)
compact such that

\min_{z\in K}d_{Y}(z)<\min_{z\in\partial_{x+F}K}d_{Y}(z).

Choose
z_{0}\in K
and
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Y
with
|z_{0}-y_{0}|=\min_{z\in K}d_{Y}(x)
.
Hence,

(3.1)

|z_{0}-y_{0}|<\inf\{|y-z|\,;\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Y,z\in\partial_{x+F}K\}.

This implies that
\partial_{y_{0}-z_{0}+x+F}(y_{0}-z_{0}+K)=y_{0}-z_{0}+\partial_{x+F}K\subseteq Y
. Since
y_{0}\in y_{0}-z_{0}+x+F
, we obtain that the connected component
C_{y_{0}}
of
(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Y)\cap(y_{0}-z_{0}+x+F)
containing
y_{0}
satisfies
C_{y_{0}}\subseteq y_{0}-z_{0}+K
. In particular,
C_{y_{0}}
is compact. If
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, this gives the desired contradiction. If
X\neq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we conclude that
\emptyset\neq\partial X\cap C_{y_{0}}
. Set

t_{0}=\inf\{t\in[0,\infty)\,;\,t(y_{0}-z_{0})+K\cap\partial X\neq\emptyset\}.

We have that
t_{0}>0
because
K\subseteq X
is compact, while
C_{y_{0}}\subseteq y_{0}-z_{0}+K
and
\emptyset\neq\partial X\cap C_{y_{0}}
imply that
t_{0}\leq 1
. Note that
t(y_{0}-z_{0})+K\subseteq X
for all
t\in(0,t_{0})
. Since
t_{0}(y_{0}-z_{0})+K\cap\partial X\neq\emptyset
, it holds that

(3.2)

d\left(t(y_{0}-z_{0})+K,\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash X\right)\leq(t_{0}-t)|y_{0}-z_{0}|,\qquad\forall\,t\in(0,t_{0}).

Set

\delta=\inf\{|y-z|\,;\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Y,z\in\partial_{x+F}K\}-|z_{0}-y_{0}|,

so that
\delta>0
by (
3.1
). For all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Y
,
z\in\partial_{x+F}K
and
t\in(0,t_{0})
we have that

|y-\left(t(y_{0}-z_{0})+z\right)|\geq|y-z|-t|z_{0}-y_{0}|\geq|y-z|-|z_{0}-y_{0}|\geq\delta.

Hence, for
t\in(0,t_{0})
it follows that

\min_{z^{\prime}\in\partial_{t(y_{0}-z_{0})+x+F}(t(y_{0}-z_{0})+K)}d_{X}(z^{\prime})=\inf\{|y-\left(t(y_{0}-z_{0})+z\right)\,;\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash X,z\in\partial_{x+F}K\}\geq\delta.

By evaluating (
3.2
) for
t\in(0,t_{0})
with
(t_{0}-t)|y_{0}-z_{0}|<\delta/2
, we obtain that

\min_{z^{\prime}\in t(y_{0}-z_{0})+K}d_{X}(z^{\prime})<\min_{z^{\prime}\in\partial_{t(y_{0}-z_{0})+x+F}(t(y_{0}-z_{0})+K)}d_{X}(z^{\prime}).

Since
t(y_{0}-z_{0})+K\subseteq X\cap(t(y_{0}-z_{0})+x+F)
,
d_{X}
does not satisfy the minimum principle in
t(y_{0}-z_{0})+x+F
, a contradiction.
∎

, we denote by

the boundary of

in the one-point compactification of

. Thus,

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}]
be a polynomial which acts along a subspace
W
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and is elliptic on
W
. Let
X,Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open with
Y\subseteq X
such that
X
is
P
-convex for supports. Suppose that there is no
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
X
contains a compact connected component of
(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Y)\cap(x+W)
. Then,
Y
is
P
-convex for supports and both restriction maps

r_{\mathscr{E}}^{P}:\mathscr{E}_{P}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}_{P}(Y)
and
r_{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{P}:\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{P}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{P}(Y)

have dense range.

Proof.

Since
X
is
P
-convex for supports, Theorem
2.1
(a) implies that

satisfies the minimum principle in

for every

. Hence, by the hypothesis on the geometry of

satisfies the minimum principle in
x+W

is
P
-convex for supports.

By Proposition
3.1
, it suffices to show that for every
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(X)
with
\operatorname{supp}\,\check{P}(D)\varphi\subseteq Y

\operatorname{supp}\,\varphi\subseteq Y
.
Set
K=\operatorname{supp}\,\check{P}(D)\varphi\subseteq Y
. We claim that

\left\{y\in Y\,;\,\,d_{Y}(y)<d(K,\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Y)\right\}\cap\operatorname{supp}\,\varphi=\emptyset.

Once we have proved this,
ch(\operatorname{supp}\,\varphi)=ch(K)
(theorem of supports) implies that

\operatorname{supp}\,\varphi\subseteq\left[\{y\in Y\,;\,\,d_{Y}(y)\geq d(K,\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Y)\}\cap ch(K)\right]\cup\left[(X\backslash Y)\cap\operatorname{supp}\,\varphi\right].

Set
K_{1}=\{y\in Y\,;\,\,d_{Y}(y)\geq d(K,\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Y)\}\cap ch(K)
and

. Then,

is contained in the union of the disjoint compact sets

. Hence,
\varphi=\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}
with
\varphi_{j}\in\mathscr{D}(K_{j}),j=1,2
, and thus
\check{P}(D)\varphi_{2}=0
. The injectivity of
\check{P}(D)
on

, whence

.

We now show the claim. Let

. We need to show that
x\notin\operatorname{supp}\,\varphi
. We claim that there exists a continuous, piecewise affine curve
\gamma:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\left(X\backslash K\right)\cap(x+W)
with
\gamma(0)=x
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}d(\gamma(t),\partial_{\infty}X)=0
. Let us assume, for the moment, that we have shown that such a curve
\gamma
exists. Since
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}d(\gamma(t),\partial_{\infty}X)=0
, there exists
T>0
such that
\gamma(T)\notin\operatorname{supp}\,\varphi
. Let

be a partition of
[0,T]
such that
\gamma
is affine in
[t_{j},t_{j+1}]
for every

. Let
\varepsilon>0
be such that
B(\gamma(T),\varepsilon)\cap\operatorname{supp}\,\varphi=\emptyset
and
\left(\gamma([0,T])+B(0,\varepsilon)\right)\cap K=\emptyset
. Consider the open convex sets
X_{2}=\gamma([t_{n-1},T])+B(0,\varepsilon)
and
X_{1}=B(\gamma(T),\varepsilon)\subseteq X_{2}
(
X_{2}
is convex because
\gamma
is affine on

). Then,
\varphi
vanishes in
X_{1}
and
\check{P}(D)\varphi
vanishes in
X_{2}
. Note that every characteristic hyperplane for

is of the form

and

. Hence, each characteristic hyperplane that intersects

already intersects

. It follows from

that
\varphi
vanishes in

. Iteration of this argument yields that
\varphi
vanishes in
\gamma([t_{j},t_{j+1}])+B(0,\varepsilon)
for every

. In particular,
\varphi
vanishes on

and thus

.

We now show the existence of a curve
\gamma
with the above properties. Let
C
be the connected component of
(X\backslash K)\cap(x+W)
that contains
x
. Then,
C
is pathwise connected. If
C
is unbounded, there is a continuous, piecewise affine curve
\gamma:[0,\infty)\rightarrow C
with
\gamma(0)=x
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}|\gamma(t)|=\infty
. In particular,
\gamma
has the desired properties. If

is bounded, as

is
P
-convex for supports,
[

and
[

with
\alpha(0)=x
and
\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}d(\alpha(t),\partial_{\infty}Y)=0
. Note that
\alpha([0,\infty))\subseteq C
. Since

is bounded, we obtain that
\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}d(\alpha(t),\partial Y)=0
. Hence, there are
\xi\in\partial Y\cap(x+W)

\varepsilon>0

\alpha(T)\in B(\xi,\varepsilon)
. If

, we consider the line

\gamma
with the desired properties. If

\beta_{1}:[T,T+1]\rightarrow B(\xi,\varepsilon)\cap(x+W)

\beta_{1}(T+1)=\xi

,

contains some

X
contains a compact connected component of

\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash Y\right)\cap\left(x+\operatorname{span}\{e_{2}\}\right).

∎

Note that Theorem
3.6
is applicable to homogeneous polynomials in two variables, in particular to the wave operator in one spatial variable. In the next result we further study the Runge approximation property for zero solutions of this operator.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
P(D)=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{2}^{2}}
be the wave operator in one spatial variable and let
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be open and
P
-convex for supports. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

r_{\mathscr{E}}^{P}:\mathscr{E}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow\mathscr{E}_{P}(Y)
has dense range.

(ii)

There is an open connected set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
Y\subseteq X
such that
X
is
P
-convex for supports and the restriction map
r_{\mathscr{E}}^{P}:\mathscr{E}_{P}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}_{P}(Y)
has dense range.

(iii)

For all
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
the following extension property holds: For each
f\in C^{r}(Y)\cap\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{P}(Y)
and
K\subseteq Y
compact there is
g\in C^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with compact support such that
g=f
on a neighborhood of
K
.

Proof.

The implications
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
and
(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
are obvious. We now show
(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
. By making the change of variables
(y_{1},y_{2})=(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1},\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})
, it suffices to show the following statement for the operator
Q(D_{y})=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y_{1}\partial y_{2}}
and an open set
\tilde{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
that is
Q
-convex for supports:

Let
\tilde{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be open and connected with
\tilde{Y}\subseteq\tilde{X}
such that
\tilde{X}
is
Q
-convex for supports and the restriction map
r_{\mathscr{E}}^{Q}:\mathscr{E}_{Q}(\tilde{X})\rightarrow\mathscr{E}_{Q}(\tilde{Y})
has dense range. Then, for all
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
,
f\in C^{r}(\tilde{Y})\cap\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Q}(\tilde{Y})
, and
K\subseteq\tilde{Y}
compact there is
g\in C^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with compact support such that
g=f
on a neighborhood of
K
.

By Theorem
3.6
, there is no
y\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\tilde{X}
contains a compact connected component of one of the sets
(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\tilde{Y})\cap\{(s,y)\,;\,s\in\mathbb{R}\}
or
(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\tilde{Y})\cap\{(y,s)\,;\,s\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Let
\pi_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},(y_{1},y_{2})\mapsto y_{k}
,
k=1,2
. For a connected component
C
of
\tilde{Y}
we set
R(C)=\pi_{1}(C)\times\pi_{2}(C)
.

Auxiliary Claim 1:

If
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are disjoint connected components of
\tilde{Y}
, then
R(C_{1})
and
R(C_{2})
are disjoint as well.

Proof of auxiliary claim 1
. Suppose that
(y_{1},y_{2})\in R(C_{1})\cap R(C_{2})
. We find

such that
(z_{k},y_{2})\in C_{k}
,
k=1,2
. Since
C_{1}
and

are disjoint connected components of
\tilde{Y}
, there is
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that

. The geometric relation of
\tilde{X}
and
\tilde{Y}
implies that there is
\mu\in(0,1)
such that
(\mu z_{1}+(1-\mu)z_{2},y_{2})\notin\tilde{X}
. Hence, the intersection of
\tilde{X}
with the line

has at least two non-empty connected components (as it contains

,

). As this line is characteristic for

and

is connected and

-convex for supports, the desired contradiction follows from

, Theorem 10.8.3]
.

Let

,
f\in C^{r}(\tilde{Y})\cap\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Q}(\tilde{Y})
, and
K\subseteq\tilde{Y}
be arbitrary.

Auxiliary Claim 2:

For each connected component
C
of
\tilde{Y}
there are
g_{k,C}\in C^{r}(\pi_{k}(C))
,
k=1,2
, with

,
(y_{1},y_{2})\in C
.

Proof of auxiliary claim 2
. It is clear that for any open rectangle
R\subseteq C
there are

,
k=1,2
, with

,
(y_{1},y_{2})\in R
. For two such rectangles
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
that are overlapping, there is
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
such that

g_{1,R_{1}}(y_{1})=g_{1,R_{2}}(y_{1})+\alpha,\qquad g_{2,R_{1}}(y_{2})=g_{2,R_{2}}(y_{2})-\alpha,

for all

,
y_{2}\in\pi_{2}(R_{1}\cap R_{2})
. This implies that
g^{\prime}_{k,R_{1}}=g^{\prime}_{k,R_{2}}
on
\pi_{k}(R_{1}\cap R_{2})
,

. It follows that there are

,

, that satisfy

on every open rectangle
R\subseteq C
. Since
\pi_{j}(C)
is an interval, there are

,

\tilde{g}:\pi_{1}(C)\times\pi_{2}(C)\rightarrow\mathbb{C},(y_{1},y_{2})\mapsto\tilde{g}_{1}(y_{1})+\tilde{g}_{2}(y_{2})

satisfies

,

on

. Now, if

is an arbitrary

-curve with

in

(for

, we obtain that

We are ready to show the result. Set
\mathscr{C}=\{C\,;\,C\mbox{ connected component of }\tilde{Y}\}
. For each

we choose

with
\operatorname{supp}\,\varphi_{k,C}\subseteq\pi_{k}(C)
and
\varphi_{k,C}=1
on a neighborhood of

,

. Define

g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\,g(y_{1},y_{2})=\begin{cases}\varphi_{1,C}(y_{1})g_{1,C}(y_{1})+\varphi_{2,C}(y_{2})g_{2,C}(y_{2}),&\mbox{if }(y_{1},y_{2})\in R(C)\\
0,&\mbox{if }(y_{1},y_{2})\notin\bigcup_{C\in\mathscr{C}}R(C),\end{cases}

where
g_{k,C}
,
k=1,2
, are the functions from the auxiliary claim 2. The function

is well-defined by the auxiliary claim 1. Furthermore, it is clear that it satisfies all requirements.
∎

4.
An auxiliary quantitative approximation result

In this section, we show an internal quantitative approximation result for
\mathscr{E}_{P}(X)
. Namely, we show condition
(\Omega)
for
\mathscr{E}_{P}(X)
(see (
2.1
)) with control on the compact
L=L(K,k)
and the number
l=l(K,k)
occurring in it. We will combine this result with the qualitative Runge type approximation theorems from Section
3
to obtain quantitative approximation theorems in the next section (cf. the explanation at the end of Section
2
).

We start with a definition that will play a central role in the rest of our work.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}]
, let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and let
K\subseteq L\subseteq X
be compact sets. We say that the pair
(K,L)
is
augmentedly
P
-locating for
X
if for every interval
[a,b]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
it holds that

(4.1)

\operatorname{supp}\,\check{P}^{+}(D)f\subseteq K\times[a,b]\,\Rightarrow\,\operatorname{supp}\,f\subseteq L\times[a,b],\qquad\forall\,f\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R}),

and

(4.2)

\operatorname{sing\,supp}\,\check{P}^{+}(D)f\subseteq K\times[a,b]\,\Rightarrow\,\operatorname{sing\,supp}\,f\subseteq L\times[a,b],\qquad\forall\,f\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R}).

A compact set
K\subseteq X
is said to be augmentedly
P
-locating for
X
if
the pair
(K,K)
is so.

In Proposition
4.7
below we show how this notion may be used to formulate a general internal quantitative approximation result for
\mathscr{E}_{P}(X)
. First, we give sufficient geometric conditions ensuring that a pair of compact sets
(K,L)
is augmentedly
P
-locating for an open set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The next result is nothing else but a reformulation of the theorems of supports and singular supports.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}]\backslash\{0\}
. Every convex set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is augmentedly
P
-locating for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Although we will not explicitly use it, we would like to point out the following generalization of Lemma
4.2
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}]
and let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open such that
P(D)^{+}:\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})
is surjective. Let
K\subseteq X
be compact and define

\hat{K}_{X}=ch(K)\cap\{x\in X\,;\,d_{X}(x)\geq d(K,\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash X)\}.

Then,
(K,\hat{K}_{X})
is augmentedly
P
-locating for
X
.

Proof.

Set
\delta=d(K,\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash X)
.
Let
[a,b]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an arbitrary interval and let
f\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(X\times\mathbb{R})
be such that
\operatorname{(sing)\,supp}\check{P}^{+}(D)f\subseteq K\times[a,b]
. Since
X\times\mathbb{R}
is
P^{+}
-convex for supports and singular supports,
[
11
, Theorem 10.6.3 and Theorem 10.7.3]
imply that

\displaystyle\delta=d(K\times[a,b],\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash X\times\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(\operatorname{(sing)\,supp}\check{P}(D)f,\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash X\times\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(\operatorname{(sing)\,supp}f,\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash X\times\mathbb{R}).

By the theorems of supports and singular supports, we have that

ch(\operatorname{(sing)\,supp}f)=ch(\operatorname{(sing)\,supp}\check{P}^{+}(D)f)\subseteq ch(K)\times[a,b].

Hence,

\displaystyle\operatorname{(sing)\,supp}\operatorname{supp}\,f

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\{(x,t)\in ch(K)\times[a,b]\,;\,\delta\leq d\left((x,t),\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash X\times\mathbb{R}\right)=d_{X}(x)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{X}\times[a,b].

∎

Next, we consider subspace elliptic operators and semi-elliptic operators with a single characteristic direction.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}]
be a polynomial which acts along a subspace

and is elliptic on

. Let

be open and let

be compact. Suppose that there is no

is augmentedly

-locating for

.

Proof.

Let
[a,b]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an arbitrary interval.
The proofs of (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) (with

) are very similar. Therefore, we first show (
4.2
) in detail, and sketch the proof of (
4.1
) afterwards. Let

\operatorname{sing\,supp}\,\check{P}^{+}(D)f\subseteq K\times[a,b]
. We need to show that
\operatorname{sing\,supp}\,f\subseteq K\times[a,b]
. By the theorem of singular supports,
\operatorname{sing\,supp}\,f\subseteq ch(K)\times[a,b]
. Hence, it suffices to show that for every

. To this end, we use the same technique as in the proof of Theorem
3.3
. Let

be the connected component of

in the one-point compactification of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that contains

. Then,

is pathwise connected. Our hypothesis on the geometry of

and

implies that

intersects
\partial_{\infty}X
. Hence, there exists a continuous, piecewise affine curve
\gamma:[0,\infty)\rightarrow C\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K)\cap(x^{\prime}+W)
with

and

Let

. Every hyperplane of the form

The proof of (

), one only has to refer to

instead of
[

and

, and note that a hyperplane

The following result is the condition

[

Proposition 4.6
.

Let

and let

,

and denote by

the unit ball in

. Note that

. As shown in
[

Note that we did not distinguish notationally between distributions defined on

Finally, we deduce Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
from Theorem
3.3
and Corollary
5.8
, respectively. To this end, we need a geometrical lemma. Recall that an open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to have continuous boundary if for each
\xi\in\partial U
there exists a homeomorphism
h
from the closed unit ball
\overline{B}(0,1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
onto a compact neighborhood
K
of
\xi
such that
h(0)=\xi
and
\overline{U}\cap h\left(\overline{B}(0,1)\right)=h\left(\{x\in\overline{B}(0,1)\,;\,x_{d}\leq 0\}\right)
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and let
U\Subset X
have continuous boundary. Set
K=\overline{U}
. Suppose that
X
contains no bounded connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K
. Then, every open subset
Y
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
K\subseteq Y\subseteq X
contains an open set
Z
with
K\subseteq Z
such that
X
contains no bounded connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Z
.

Proof.

We set
\varepsilon_{0}=d(K,\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Y)/2
if
Y\neq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\varepsilon_{0}=1/2
otherwise. For
\rho>0
we abbreviate
K_{\rho}=K+B(0,\rho)
. Then,
K_{\varepsilon_{0}}\Subset Y
and
\partial X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\overline{K_{\varepsilon_{0}}}
.

Before we proceed, we give a short outline of the proof. The idea is to construct
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Z
by considering the unbounded connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K_{\varepsilon_{0}}
together with its connected components that intersect
\partial X
and to successively enlarge these components. We will show that only a finite number of enlargement steps are needed to come close enough to the boundary of
K
. Here, ”close enough” means that it is possible to take the union of this enlargement with a suitable open superset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K
without losing the property that bounded connected components intersect
\partial X
.

Let
R>0
be such that
K_{\varepsilon_{0}}\subseteq\overline{B}(0,R)
. Then, the single unbounded connected component
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K_{\varepsilon_{0}}
contains
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\overline{B}(0,R)
. Let
\mathscr{C}_{0}
be the set consisting of all connected components
C
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K_{\varepsilon_{0}}
that are unbounded or intersect
\partial X
. Set
B_{1}=\cup_{C\in\mathscr{C}_{0}}C
.

Auxiliary Claim 1:

(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\overline{B}(0,R))\cup\partial X\subseteq B_{1}
and
B_{1}
is a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof of auxiliary claim 1
. The first assertion is clear. To show that
B_{1}
is closed, let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
B_{1}
that converges to
x
. We write
C_{\infty}
for the unbounded connectecd component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K_{\varepsilon_{0}}
. We distinguish two cases. Suppose that infinitely many
x_{n}
belong to
C_{\infty}
. Since
C_{\infty}
is closed in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we obtain that
x\in C_{\infty}\subseteq B_{1}
. If only finitely many
x_{n}
belong to
C_{\infty}
, we may assume that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the connected component
C_{n}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K_{\varepsilon_{0}}
that contains
x_{n}
is bounded. Since
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\overline{B}(0,R)\subseteq C_{\infty}
we have that
C_{n}\subseteq\overline{B}(0,R)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By the definition of
\mathscr{C}_{0}
, there is
z_{n}\in\partial X\cap C_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We may assume that
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to some

z\in\partial X\cap\overline{B}(0,R)\subseteq\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\overline{K_{\varepsilon_{0}}}\right)
. Let
C
be the connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\overline{K_{\varepsilon_{0}}}
that contains
z
. As
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\overline{K_{\varepsilon_{0}}}
is open, there is
\delta>0
such that
B(z,\delta)\subseteq C
. Since
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
z
, we may assume that
z_{n}\in B(z,\delta)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,
B(z,\delta)\subseteq C_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and thus
C_{1}=C_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
C_{1}
is closed in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it follows from
x_{n}\in C_{n}=C_{1},n\in\mathbb{N}
, that
x\in C_{1}\subseteq B_{1}
.

We set

\delta(x)=\min\left\{1,\frac{d(x,K)}{2}\right\},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we recursively define
B_{n+1}=\bigcup_{x\in B_{n}}\overline{B}(x,\delta(x))
.

Auxiliary Claim 2:

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that
B_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K_{\varepsilon_{0}/2^{n-1}}
, every bounded connected component of
B_{n}
intersects
\partial X
, and
B_{n}
is a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof of auxiliary claim 2.
For
n=1
the first two assertions are clear, while the third one has been shown in auxiliary claim 1. The assertions for general
n
can be shown by induction, the details are left to the reader.

Auxiliary Claim 3:

There are
m\in\mathbb{N}
and finitely many continuous homeomorphism
h_{j}:\overline{B}(0,1)\rightarrow h_{j}(\overline{B}(0,1))\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
1\leq j\leq k
, such that

(a1)

\forall\,1\leq j\leq k:\,B_{m}\cap h_{j}\left(B(0,1)\right)\neq\emptyset
,

(a2)

\partial K\subseteq\cup_{j=1}^{k}h_{j}\left(B(0,1)\right)\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}h_{j}\left(\overline{B}(0,1)\right)\subseteq K_{\varepsilon_{0}}
,

(a3)

\forall\,1\leq j\leq k:\,K\cap h_{j}\left(\overline{B}(0,1)\right)=h_{j}\left(\{x\in\overline{B}(0,1)\,;\,x_{d}\leq 0\}\right)
.

Proof of auxiliary claim 3.
Fix an arbitrary
\xi_{0}\in\partial K
. Since
K
has continuous boundary, there is a homeomorphism
h:\overline{B}(0,1)\rightarrow h_{j}(\overline{B}(0,1))\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
h(\xi)=0

and
K\cap h\left(\overline{B}(0,1)\right)=h\left(\{x\in\overline{B}(0,1)\,;\,x_{d}\leq 0\}\right)
. By possibly shrinking the ball
\overline{B}(0,1)
and rescaling the function
h
, we may assume that
h\left(\overline{B}(0,1)\right)\subseteq K_{\varepsilon_{0}}
. Set
V=h\left(B(0,1)\right)
. We now prove that there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
B_{m}\cap V\neq\emptyset
.
Note that
V\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K\right)\neq\emptyset
.
Let
y_{0}\in V\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K\right)
be arbitrary. We are going to show that
y_{0}\in B_{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K
is open, its connected components are pathwise connected. We distinguish two cases. Suppose that
y_{0}
belongs to the unbounded connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K
. Fix
z_{0}
in the unbounded connected component of
B_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K
. Then there is a continuous curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K
with
\gamma(0)=z_{0}\in B_{1}
and
\gamma(1)=y_{0}
. If
y_{0}
does not belong to the unbounded connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K
, the hypothesis that
X
does not contain any bounded connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K
implies that there is a continuous curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K
with
\gamma(0)=z_{0}\in\partial X\subseteq B_{1}
and
\gamma(1)=y_{0}
.
Hence, in either case, there is a continuous curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K
with
\gamma(0)\in B_{1}
and
\gamma(1)=y_{0}
. Set

\delta=\min\left\{1,\frac{d(\gamma([0,1]),K)}{2}\right\}>0.

Since
\gamma
is uniformly continuous, there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|\gamma(r)-\gamma(t)|<\delta
for all
r,t\in[0,1]
with
|r-t|\leq 1/N
. As
\gamma(0)\in B_{1}
and
\delta<\delta(\gamma(0))
, it holds that
\overline{B}(\gamma(0),\delta)\subseteq B_{2}
. By the choice of
N
, we have that
\gamma(1/N)\in\overline{B}(\gamma(0),\delta)\subseteq B_{2}
. Likewise, as
\delta\leq\delta(\gamma(1/N))
, it holds that
\gamma(2/N)\in\overline{B}(\gamma(1/N),\delta)\subseteq B_{3}
. Continuing this process, we find that
y_{0}=\gamma(1)\in B_{N+1}
. Since
\xi_{0}\in\partial K
was chosen arbitrarily, the claim now follows from the compactness of
\partial K
and the fact that
B_{n}\subseteq B_{n+1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We can now prove the assertion of the lemma. To this end, let
m
and
h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}
be as in the auxiliary claim 3. Choose
\rho\in(0,1)
such that

(5.3)

\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}h_{j}\left(\{x\in\overline{B}(0,1)\,;\,x_{d}\leq\rho\}\right)\subseteq K_{\varepsilon_{0}/2^{m-1}}.

Set
Z^{\prime}:=K\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}h_{j}\left(\{x\in B(0,1)\,;\,x_{d}<\rho\}\right)
. Then,
Z^{\prime}
is open and
K\subseteq Z^{\prime}\subseteq K_{\varepsilon_{0}/2^{m-1}}\subseteq Y
. Let
C
be an arbitrary connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Z^{\prime}
. Then,

\emptyset\neq C\cap\partial Z^{\prime}\subseteq C\cap\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}h_{j}\left(\{x\in\overline{B}(0,1)\,;\,x_{d}\leq\rho\}\right)\right),

This implies that there is
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
such that

\emptyset\neq C\cap h_{j}\left(\{x\in\overline{B}(0,1)\,;\,x_{d}=\rho\}\right)\subseteq C\cap h_{j}\left(\{x\in\overline{B}(0,1)\,;\,x_{d}\geq\rho\}\right).

As the union of two connected sets with non-empty intersection is again connected, we obtain that
C\cup h_{j}\left(\{x\in\overline{B}(0,1)\,;\,x_{d}\geq\rho\}\right)
is connected. Additionally, by condition (a1) and the inclusion
B_{m}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K_{\varepsilon_{0}/2^{m-1}}
, (
5.3
) implies that there is a connected component
D
of
B_{m}
such that
D\cap h_{j}\left(\{x\in\overline{B}(0,1)\,;\,x_{d}\geq\rho\}\right)\neq\emptyset
. Hence,
C\cup h_{j}\left(\{x\in\overline{B}(0,1)\,;\,x_{d}\geq\rho\}\right)\cup D
is connected. This implies that each of the connected components of the set

A=\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Z^{\prime}\right)\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}h_{j}\left(\{x\in\overline{B}(0,1)\,;\,x_{d}\geq\rho\}\right)\cup B_{m}

contains a connected component of
B_{m}
and therefore is unbounded or intersects
\partial X
(auxiliary claim 2). Furthermore, this set is closed in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
B_{m}
is closed by the auxiliary claim 2).
Finally, we define
Z=\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash A
. Then,
Z
is open,
Z\subseteq Z^{\prime}\subseteq Y
and each bounded connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash Z=A
intersects
\partial X
. We also have that
K\subseteq Z
because
K\subseteq Z^{\prime}
,
K\subseteq K_{\varepsilon_{0}/2^{m-1}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash B_{m}
and the fact that, by (a3),
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash h_{j}\left(\{x\in\overline{B}(0,1)\,;\,x_{d}\geq\rho\}\right)
for all
j=1,\ldots,k
. ∎

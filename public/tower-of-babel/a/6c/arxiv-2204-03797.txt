Abstract
. Equivariant complex K K -theory and the equivariant sphere spectrum are two of the most fundamental equivariant spectra. For an odd p p -group, we calculate the zeroth homotopy Green functor of the localization of the equivariant sphere spectrum with respect to equivariant complex K K -theory. Further, we calculate the zeroth homotopy Tambara functor structure in the case of odd cyclic p p -groups.

To prove

2.1.
Algebra

We will make use of several commutative rings associated to

Let

Let

For

This is the ring of

•

Let

•

Let

There are canonical ring maps

(2.1)

none of which are necessarily isomorphisms. The first map is induced by the operation that sends a finite

to

to

and

Recall that a Green functor is a Mackey functor that takes values in commutative rings and for which the restriction maps are ring maps and the transfer maps satisfy Frobenius reciprocity.
Equivalently, a Green functor is a commutative monoid in the symmetric monoidal category of Mackey functors

[
20
]

Each of the constructions above extends to a Green functor. We will denote the associated Green functors with an underline. For example,
\underline{A}
is the
G

\underline{A}(G/H)=A(H)
. As all of the maps in

(2.2)

\underline{A}\to\underline{R\mathbb{Q}}\to\underline{RO}\to\underline{RU}\to\underline{\Cl}.

In fact, each of these constructions, as well as

\underline{\pi}_{0}\left(L_{KU_{G}/q}S_{G}\right)\cong\ker(\psi^{\ell}-1)\cong(\underline{A}/\underline{J})^{\wedge}_{q}

by Proposition
6.7
.

Now

\underline{\pi}_{1}\left(L_{KU_{G}/q}S_{G}\right)\cong\coker\left(\psi^{\ell}-1\colon\underline{\pi}_{2}(KU_{G})^{\wedge}_{q}\to\underline{\pi}_{2}(KU_{G})^{\wedge}_{q}\right).

To see that
\underline{\pi}_{1}L_{KU_{G}/q}S_{G}
is finite, it suffices to show that
\psi^{\ell}-1
is injective on
\underline{\pi}_{2}(KU_{G})^{\wedge}_{q}\cong\underline{RU}^{\wedge}_{q}\{\beta\}
, where
\beta
is the ordinary Bott class. Since
\psi^{\ell}-1
is base changed along the flat extension
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}_{q}
from the action of
\psi^{\ell}-1
on
\underline{RU}\{\beta\}
, it suffices to show that the action on
\underline{RU}\{\beta\}
is injective. Since this action is levelwise, we may show that

\psi^{\ell}-1\colon RU(G)\{\beta\}\to RU(G)\{\beta\}

is injective. Since
RU(G)
is a finitely generated free
\mathbb{Z}
-module, we may base change to
\mathbb{C}
and work with class functions, giving us

\psi^{\ell}-1\colon\Cl(G)\{\beta\}\to\Cl(G)\{\beta\}.

We wish to show that this map is an isomorphism. We will consider the basis consisting of indicator functions.
The indicator functions are permuted by the action of
\psi^{\ell}
. If the associated permutation matrix is
S
, then
\psi^{\ell}
acts on
\Cl(G)\{\beta\}
by
\ell S
. Then we are interested in the determinant of the integer matrix
\ell S-\Id
in which
\ell\geq 2
. Since this matrix is invertible mod
\ell
, the determinant is nonzero.
∎

7.
The splitting of
G
-spectra, away from the order of the group

For the duration of this section, we fix a prime
p
not dividing the order of the finite group
G
. We review the fact that the
p
-local
G
-equivariant stable homotopy category splits as a product of Borel-equivariant homotopy categories.
This essentially appeared first in
[
13
]
*Appendix A, and also more recently in
[
9
,
21
]
, and explicitly in
[
32
]
;
as we will need an explicit description of this splitting, we prove the result in full.

This splitting arises from a corresponding splitting of the
p
-local Burnside ring of
G

[
18
]
. The
p
-local splitting of
A(G)
arises from the existence of certain idempotents
e_{H}^{G}\in A(G)_{(p)}
, one for each conjugacy class of subgroups. The idempotent
e^{G}_{H}
is of the form

(7.1)

e^{G}_{H}=\frac{1}{|W_{G}(H)|}G/H+\sum_{(K)}c^{H}_{K}G/K,

where
K
runs over conjugacy classes of
G
that are properly subconjugate to
H
and
c^{H}_{K}\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
.

Given the isomorphism
\pi_{0}(S_{G})\cong A(G)
, this allows us to define, for any
p
-local
G
-spectrum
X
, the
G
-spectrum

e^{G}_{H}X
as the telescope

e^{G}_{H}X=\hocolim(X\xrightarrow{e^{G}_{H}}X\xrightarrow{e^{G}_{H}}X\xrightarrow{e^{G}_{H}}\dots).

When
H=\{1\}
is the trivial subgroup, this idempotent is smashing with the free
G
-space
EG_{+}
.
To see this consider the cofiber sequence

EG_{+}\longrightarrow(G/G)_{+}\longrightarrow\widetilde{EG}
of based
G
-spaces, which gives rise to the cofiber sequence of
G
-spectra
EG_{+}\longrightarrow S_{G}\longrightarrow\widetilde{EG}
.
The map
e_{1}^{G}
is the composition of maps of
G
-spectra

(7.2)

S_{G}\xrightarrow{\frac{\mathrm{tr}}{|G|}}G_{+}\longrightarrow S_{G}.

Since the underlying spectrum of
\widetilde{EG}
is contractible, it follows that
e^{G}_{1}\widetilde{EG}\simeq\ast
and
e^{G}_{1}S_{G}\simeq e^{G}_{1}EG_{+}
.
However, on
EG_{+}
, the composition
Equation

7.2
is the identity since
EG
has only free cells, and we conclude that
e^{G}_{1}S_{G}\simeq EG_{+}
. We prove a generalization of this equivalence to
H\subseteq G
in
7.4
.

Theorem 7.3

(
[
2
,
13
,
9
,
21
,
32
]
)
.

Let
p
be a prime not dividing the order of the group
G
.
Then the collection of geometric fixed point functors, as
(H)
runs over conjugacy classes of subgroups, yields a symmetric monoidal equivalence of categories

\Ho\Sp^{G}_{(p)}\xrightarrow{(\Phi^{H})}\bigoplus_{(H)}\Ho\Sp^{hW_{G}(H)}_{(p)}.

Proof.

The fact that the collection of geometric fixed point functors is fully faithful is stated as
[
13
, Theorem A.16]
, for the case of rationalization. However, the argument is based on
[
2
]
, which provides the needed results at the level of
p
-localization, as we now recall.

We have the chain of isomorphisms

\begin{split}[X,Y]^{G}_{p}&\cong\bigoplus_{(H)}[e_{H}^{G}X,e_{H}^{G}Y]^{G}_{p}\cong\bigoplus_{(H)}[e^{NH}_{H}X,e^{NH}_{H}Y]^{NH}_{p}\\
&\cong\bigoplus_{(H)}[e_{1}^{WH}\Phi^{H}X,e_{1}^{WH}\Phi^{H}Y]^{WH}_{p}\\
&\cong\bigoplus_{(H)}[EWH_{+}\wedge\Phi^{H}X,EWH_{+}\wedge\Phi^{H}Y]^{WH}_{p}\\
&\cong\bigoplus_{(H)}[\Phi^{H}X,\Phi^{H}Y]^{hWH}_{p}.\\
\end{split}

Here, the second isomorphism is given by
[
2
, Theorem 3.5]
and the third by
[
2
, Theorem 4.7]

To see that the collection of geometric fixed point functors is essentially surjective, we provide, for each
Y\in\Sp^{hWH}_{p}
, a
G
-spectrum whose
K
-geometric fixed points vanish unless
K=H
, up to conjugacy, and whose
H
-geometric fixed points is
Y
.

For a subgroup
H\leq G
, denote by
\mathcal{F}[H]
the family of subgroups of
NH
which do not contain
H
. Since
H
is normal in
NH
, this is indeed a family, meaning that it is closed under subgroups and conjugation.
We then claim that the
G
-spectrum

X=\uparrow_{NH}^{G}\left(\widetilde{E\mathcal{F}[H]}\wedge EWH_{+}\wedge Y\right)

has the desired fixed point properties.

First note that the
NH
-space
\widetilde{E\mathcal{F}[H]}\wedge EWH_{+}
satisfies

\Big(\widetilde{E\mathcal{F}[H]}\wedge EWH_{+}\Big)^{K}\simeq\begin{cases}S^{0}&K=H\\
\ast&\text{else.}\end{cases}

We will write
E\langle H\rangle=\widetilde{E\mathcal{F}[H]}\wedge EWH_{+}
.

Now for any
NH
-spectrum
Z
, the double coset formula gives

\begin{split}\downarrow^{G}_{NK}\uparrow_{NH}^{G}Z&\simeq\bigvee\uparrow^{NK}_{NK\cap NH^{g}}c_{g}\downarrow^{NH}_{NK^{g^{-1}}\cap NH}Z\\
&\simeq\bigvee\uparrow^{NK}_{NK\cap NH^{g}}\downarrow^{NH^{g}}_{NK\cap NH^{g}}c_{g^{-1}}Z.\end{split}

Then

\begin{split}\Phi^{K}\Big(\downarrow^{G}_{NK}\uparrow_{NH}^{G}E\langle H\rangle\wedge Y\Big)&\simeq\bigvee\Phi^{K}\Big(\uparrow^{NK}_{NK\cap NH^{g}}\downarrow^{NH^{g}}_{NK\cap NH^{g}}c_{g^{-1}}E\langle H\rangle\wedge Y\Big)\\
&\simeq\bigvee\uparrow^{WK}_{\frac{NK\cap NH^{g}}{K}}\Phi^{K}\Big(\downarrow^{NH^{g}}_{NK\cap NH^{g}}c_{g^{-1}}E\langle H\rangle\wedge Y\Big)\\
&\simeq\begin{cases}Y&K=H^{g}\\
\ast&\text{else.}\end{cases}\end{split}

This verifies that the collection
(\Phi^{H})
of geometric fixed point functors is essentially surjective.
Finally, the equivalence is symmetric monoidal simply because each geometric fixed point functor is symmetric monoidal.
∎

In the proof of

, we employed the

-local idempotents

. We will use the following description of the interaction of the idempotents with fixed points.

Proposition 7.4
.

For

,

Proof.

Since fixed points with respect to

Recall that
\Phi^{H}(X)=(\widetilde{E}\mathcal{F}[H]\wedge X)^{H}

We will show that

E(G/H)_{+}\wedge e^{G}_{H}X\simeq E(G/H)_{+}\wedge\widetilde{E}\mathcal{F}[H]\wedge X

in
\Sp^{G}_{(p)}
.
The result then follows by passage to
H
-fixed points, since

Note that, as a

, where

is the family of subgroups of

Consider the cofiber sequence

Again, the left term is

are induced from proper subgroups of

Since the restriction of

On the other hand, on

Proposition 7.10

\Phi^{H}L_{(KU_{G})/p}S_{G}\simeq L_{\Phi^{H}(KU_{G})/p}\Phi^{H}S_{G}

as non-equivariant spectra.
Since
\Phi^{H}
preserves cofiber sequences,
Propositions

3.5
and
3.10
imply that

\Phi^{H}((KU_{G})/p)\simeq\Phi^{H}(KU_{G})/p
is a free
KU/p
-module for
H
cyclic, and contractible otherwise.
Thus, as non-equivariant spectra,

(8.8)

\Phi^{H}L_{(KU_{G})/p}S_{G}\simeq\begin{cases}L_{\bigvee KU/p}S\simeq L_{KU/p}S\qquad&\mbox{$H$ cyclic}\\
\**&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Corollary

7.12
then implies
Equation

8.6
.

Towards
Equation

8.7
, we note that

\Phi^{H}(ECyc_{+}\wedge\mathrm{inf}^{G}_{G/G}L_{KU/p}S)\simeq(ECyc^{H})_{+}\wedge\Phi^{H}(\mathrm{inf}^{G}_{G/G}L_{KU/p}S)\simeq\begin{cases}L_{KU/p}S\qquad&\mbox{$H$ cyclic}\\
\**&\mbox{else},\end{cases}

so that the geometric fixed points of
L_{KU_{G}/p}S_{G}
agree with those of
ECyc_{+}\wedge\mathrm{inf}^{G}_{G/G}L_{KU/p}S
.
It remains only to produce a map of
E_{\infty}
-rings of
G
-spectra

\mathrm{inf}_{G/G}^{G}L_{KU/p}S\longrightarrow L_{KU_{G}/p}S_{G},

or equivalently a map of
E_{\infty}
-rings

L_{KU/p}S\longrightarrow(L_{KU_{G}/p}S_{G})^{G}.

In other words, it suffices to show that
(L_{KU_{G}/p}S_{G})^{G}
is
KU/p
-local.

Thus let
X
be a
KU/p
-acyclic. We wish to show that
[X,(L_{KU_{G}/p}S_{G})^{G}]=0
. The assumption is equivalent to the statement that
X/p
is
KU
-acyclic. The vanishing is equivalent to the vanishing of

[\mathrm{inf}_{G/G}^{G}X,L_{KU_{G}/p}S_{G}]^{G}.

Thus it suffices to show that
\mathrm{inf}_{G/G}^{G}X
is
KU_{G}/p
-acyclic, or equivalently that
\mathrm{inf}_{G/G}^{G}X/p
is
KU_{G}
-acyclic. But

\mathrm{inf}_{G/G}^{G}X/p\wedge\mathrm{inf}_{G/G}^{G}KU\simeq\mathrm{inf}_{G/G}^{G}(X/p\wedge KU)\simeq\ast,

so the result follows by base change along the
E_{\infty}
-ring map
\mathrm{inf}_{G/G}^{G}KU\longrightarrow KU_{G}
.
∎

9.
Computing using the arithmetic fracture square

We need one final lemma.

Lemma 9.1
.

For all finite groups
G
there is an equivalence of rational equivariant spectra

\mathbb{Q}\otimes L_{KU_{G}}S_{G}\simeq H(\mathbb{Q}\otimes\underline{A}/\underline{J}),

where
H(\mathbb{Q}\otimes\underline{A}/\underline{J})
is the equivariant Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum.

Proof.

It follows from
[
11
, Proposition 2.11]
, that
\mathbb{Q}\otimes L_{KU_{G}}S_{G}\simeq L_{\mathbb{Q}\otimes KU_{G}}S_{G}
. The functor
(\Phi^{H})
is an equivalence from the category of rational
G
-equivariant spectra to the product of the rational Borel-equivariant categories.

Now, by
Lemma

8.1
, we have equivalences

\Phi^{H}(L_{\mathbb{Q}\otimes KU_{G}}S_{G})\simeq L_{\Phi^{H}(\mathbb{Q}\otimes KU_{G})}\Phi^{H}S_{G}\simeq L_{\Phi^{H}(\mathbb{Q}\otimes KU_{G})}S

of non-equivariant spectra.
Moreover, we have

L_{\Phi^{H}(\mathbb{Q}\otimes KU_{G})}S\simeq\begin{cases}L_{H\mathbb{Q}}S\simeq H\mathbb{Q}\qquad&\mbox{$H$ cyclic}\\
\**\qquad&\mbox{else},\end{cases}

since
\mathbb{Q}\otimes\Phi^{H}KU_{G}\simeq*
when
H
is not cyclic,
and

\mathbb{Q}\otimes\Phi^{H}KU_{G}
is a nontrivial
H\mathbb{Q}
-module when
H
is cyclic.
The rational analogue of
Corollary

7.12
then implies that

\mathbb{Q}\otimes L_{KU_{G}}S_{G}\simeq H(\mathbb{Q}\otimes\underline{A}/\underline{J})
.
∎

We can now prove our main result.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Adapting
Equation

2.3
with
X=L_{KU_{G}}S_{G}
yields the following homotopy pullback square of

E_{\infty}
-rings in
G
-spectra

(9.2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{KU_{G}}S_{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{p}L_{KU_{G}/p}S_{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}\otimes L_{KU_{G}}S_{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}\otimes\displaystyle\prod_{p}L_{KU_{G}/p}S_{G}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

This yields a long-exact sequence in Mackey functor-valued homotopy. Since
\underline{\pi}_{1}L_{KU_{G}/p}S_{G}
is trivial except when
p=2
or
p=q
, when it is torsion by
[
11
]
*Corollary 4.5 with
Proposition

8.5
or
6.8
, we have the long-exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow\underline{\pi}_{0}L_{KU_{G}}S_{G}\longrightarrow\left(\mathbb{Q}\otimes\underline{\pi}_{0}L_{KU_{G}}S_{G}\right)\oplus\left(\prod\underline{\pi}_{0}L_{KU_{G}/p}S_{G}\right)\overset{f-g}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{Q}\otimes\prod\underline{\pi}_{0}L_{KU_{G}/p}S_{G}.

Applying
Lemmas

9.1
,
6.8
and
8.5
, this is the long exact sequence of Mackey functors

0\longrightarrow\underline{\pi}_{0}L_{KU_{G}}S_{G}\longrightarrow(\mathbb{Q}\otimes\underline{A}/\underline{J})\oplus(\prod_{p}(\underline{A}/\underline{J})^{\wedge}_{p})\times(\underline{A}/\underline{J}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2})\xrightarrow{f-g}\mathbb{Q}\otimes\left(\prod_{p}(\underline{A}/\underline{J})^{\wedge}_{p}\times(\underline{A}/\underline{J}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2})\right).

The factor containing
\mathbb{F}_{2}
arises from the fact that
\pi_{0}L_{KU/2}S\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Note that there is an isomorphism of Mackey functors

\mathbb{Q}\otimes\left(\prod_{p}(\underline{A}/\underline{J})^{\wedge}_{p}\times(\underline{A}/\underline{J}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2})\right)\cong\mathbb{Q}\otimes\left(\prod_{p}(\underline{A}/\underline{J})^{\wedge}_{p}\right).

It follows that
\underline{A}/\underline{J}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}
is in the kernel of
f-g
.
The remaining part of the exact sequence is the arithmetic fracture square for
\underline{A}/\underline{J}
. As
A(H)/J(H)
is a finitely generated free abelian group for each

, we have
\underline{A}/\underline{J}\oplus(\underline{A}/\underline{J}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2})=\ker(f-g)
.
∎

Given
Theorem

1.1
, it is natural to wonder if there is an isomorphism

\underline{\pi}_{i}L_{KU_{G}}S_{G}\cong\underline{A}/\underline{J}\otimes\pi_{i}L_{KU}S

or even if
(L_{KU_{G}}S_{G})^{H}\simeq L_{KU}S\otimes A(H)/J(H)
. This is already false for
i=1
.

Proposition 9.3
.

For
G=C_{3}
, we have

\underline{\pi}_{1}L_{KU_{G}}S_{G}\cong\big(\underline{A}\otimes\pi_{1}L_{KU}S\big)\oplus\underline{T},

where
\underline{T}
is the unique
C_{3}
-Mackey functor with
\underline{T}(G/e)=0
and
\underline{T}(G/G)=\mathbb{Z}/3
.

Proof.

We first recall
[
11
]
*Corollary 4.5, Corollary 4.6 that
\pi_{1}L_{KU}S\cong\pi_{1}L_{KU/2}S\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
and that
\pi_{1}L_{KU/p}S\cong 0
if
p\neq 2
.
The pullback square
Equation

9.2
implies that

\underline{\pi}_{1}\left(L_{KU_{G}}S_{G}\right)\cong\underline{\pi}_{1}\Big(\displaystyle\prod_{p}L_{KU_{G}/p}S_{G}\Big)\cong\prod_{p}\underline{\pi}_{1}\left(L_{KU_{G}/p}S_{G}\right).

Proposition

8.5
gives that, for
p\neq 3
,

\underline{\pi}_{1}L_{KU_{G}/p}S_{G}\cong\underline{A}\otimes\pi_{1}L_{KU/p}S,

since the ideal
\underline{J}
vanishes if
G
is cyclic.
Thus it follows that

\underline{\pi}_{1}L_{KU_{G}}S_{G}\cong\underline{\pi}_{1}\left(L_{KU_{G}/3}S_{G}\right)\oplus\left(\underline{A}\otimes\pi_{1}L_{KU/2}S\right).

It remains to determine
\underline{\pi}_{1}\left(L_{KU_{G}/3}S_{G}\right)
.

We may compute
\underline{\pi}_{1}\left(L_{KU_{G}/3}S_{G}\right)

by use of the fiber sequence
Equation

6.4
.
As in
6.8
, this Mackey functor can be computed as

\underline{\pi}_{1}\left(L_{KU_{G}/3}S_{G}\right)\cong\coker\left(\psi^{\ell}-1\colon(\underline{RU}_{G})^{\wedge}_{3}\{\beta\}\to(\underline{RU}_{G})^{\wedge}_{3}\{\beta\}\right).

Here, we may take
\ell=2
.
At the underlying level, this is the classical
\pi_{1}(L_{KU/3}S)
, which vanishes. At the fixed point level, let us again write
RU(C_{3})=\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^{3}-1)
. Then
\psi^{2}(x\cdot\beta)=2x^{2}\cdot\beta
and similarly
\psi^{2}(x^{2}\cdot\beta)=2x\cdot\beta
. The homomorphism

\psi^{2}-1\colon RU(C_{3})^{\wedge}_{3}\{\beta\}\longrightarrow RU(C_{3})^{\wedge}_{3}\{\beta\}

may therefore be represented by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}2-1&0&0\\
0&-1&2\\

0&-1&2\\
0&0&3\end{pmatrix}.

We conclude that the cokernel is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/3
.
∎

-ring structure on

In this final section, we apply the results of

to show that

We will make use of the norm construction

(see

*Section 2.2.3).

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H\mathrm{-}E_{\infty}\mathrm{-ring}(Sp^{H})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\downarrow^{G}_{H}}

We will also follow
[

]

for the composite functor

on

-spectra. More generally, by decomposing a finite

Proposition 10.2
.

For
G
an odd
q
-group,

admits the structure of a

-ring.

Proof.

By
[
16
, Theorem 3.9]
, it suffices to show that for

and each

-set

, the norm

preserves
KU_{G}
-acyclics. After decomposing

into transitive
L

\coprod_{i}L/H_{i}
, we have

N^{G\times_{L}T}(X)\cong\bigwedge_{i}N^{G/H_{i}}(X).

Thus it suffices to show that each norm

preserves
KU_{G}
-acyclics. Therefore we may assume that
G\times_{L}T\cong G/H
.

Let
X
be a
KU_{G}
-acyclic

\Phi^{K}KU_{G}\simeq\ast
for
K\subseteq G

\Phi^{K}X
is
\Phi^{K}KU_{G}
-acyclic for

cyclic. Further, since
\Phi^{K}KU_{G}
is a free

-module, we have that
\Phi^{K}X\wedge KU[\frac{1}{q}]\simeq\ast
for

\Phi^{e}X\wedge KU\simeq\ast
.

Thus, to show that

is
KU_{G}
-acyclic, it suffices by
[
16
]
*Proposition 3.2 to show that

\Phi^{K}(N^{G/H}X)
is

-acyclic for

nontrivial cyclic and that
\Phi^{e}(N^{G/H}X)
is
KU
-acyclic.
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
provides us with an equivalence

\Phi^{K}(N^{G/H}X)\simeq\bigwedge_{KgH\in K\\
G/H}\Phi^{K^{g}\cap H}X,

where

. For

a nontrivial cyclic subgroup of

,

is cyclic and may be trivial. Either way, it follows that
\Phi^{K^{g}\cap H}X\wedge KU[\frac{1}{q}]\simeq\ast
. If

is trivial, then all of the factors are

and these are

-acyclic. Thus
\Phi^{K}(N^{G/H}X)
is
\Phi^{K}KU_{G}
-acyclic for each cyclic subgroup

and

is
KU_{G}
-acyclic.
∎

Recall from
[
15
]
*Section 2.3.3 that if

is a

-

-ring, then

where

).

In the case where

is a cyclic

-group, we will simplify our notation and write

N_{i}^{j}=N_{C_{q^{i}}}^{C_{q^{j}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad R_{i}^{j}=R_{C_{q^{i}}}^{C_{q^{j}}}

for the norm and restriction maps of a

-Tambara functor.

The following lemma was suggested to us by Balderrama.

Lemma 10.3

(Balderrama)
.

Let

be a cyclic odd

-group. For

,

As

Since

has no nilpotents, we conclude that

divides

.

By the discussion above, we see that

We recall that

is a free abelian group with generators

for

. Then

Proposition 10.6
.

Let

be a cyclic odd
q
-group.
With notation as in

and above,
N_{i}^{i+1}(x_{i})=x_{i+1}(1+y_{i})

Since

and

has no nilpotents, we know that

Here, the coefficients

can be taken to be 0 or 1, as
2x_{i+1}=0
.
We then have

\displaystyle R^{i+1}_{i}N_{i}^{i+1}(x_{i})

\displaystyle=x_{i}\left(a_{i+1}+a_{i}q+a_{i-1}qy_{i-1}+\dots+a_{0}qy_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=x_{i}\left(a_{i+1}+a_{i}+a_{i-1}y_{i-1}+\dots+a_{0}y_{0}\right),

where the last equality follows from the fact that

is odd.
Since
R^{i+1}_{i}N_{i}^{i+1}(x_{i})=x_{i}^{q}=0
,
it follows that
a_{i-1}=a_{i-2}=\dots=a_{0}=0
and
a_{i+1}+a_{i}\in 2\mathbb{Z}
.
But by

,
N_{i}^{i+1}(x_{i})\neq 0
, and so we must have

.
Thus

N_{i}^{i+1}(x_{i})=x_{i+1}(1+y_{i})
.
∎

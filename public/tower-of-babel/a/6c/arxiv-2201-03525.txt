The isoperimetric problem via direct method in noncompact metric measure spaces with lower Ricci bounds
Gioacchino Antonelli, Stefano Nardulli, Marco Pozzetta

Abstract
. We establish a structure theorem for minimizing sequences for the isoperimetric problem on noncompact 𝖱𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( K , N ) \mathsf{RCD}(K,N) spaces ( X , 𝖽 , ℋ N ) (X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N}) . Under the sole (necessary) assumption that the measure of unit balls is uniformly bounded away from zero, we prove that the limit of such a sequence is identified by a finite collection of isoperimetric regions possibly contained in pointed Gromov–Hausdorff limits of the ambient space X X along diverging sequences of points. The number of such regions is bounded linearly in terms of the measure of the minimizing sequence. The result follows from a new generalized compactness theorem, which identifies the limit of a sequence of sets E i ⊂ X i E_{i}\subset X_{i} with uniformly bounded measure and perimeter, where ( X i , 𝖽 i , ℋ N ) (X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathcal{H}^{N}) is an arbitrary sequence of 𝖱𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( K , N ) \mathsf{RCD}(K,N) spaces. An abstract criterion for a minimizing sequence to converge without losing mass at infinity to an isoperimetric set is also discussed. The latter criterion is new also for smooth Riemannian spaces.

1.
Introduction

The
isoperimetric problem
can be formulated on every ambient space possessing notions of
volume measure

\mathfrak{m}
and
perimeter

P
on (some subclass of) its subsets. Among sets having assigned positive volume, the problem deals with finding those having least perimeter. Among the most basic questions in the context of the isoperimetric problem, one would naturally ask whether there exist minimizers, called
isoperimetric regions
(or
isoperimetric sets
), but also what goes wrong in the minimization process in case such minimizers do not exist. The value of the infimum of the perimeter among sets of a given volume
V
is called
isoperimetric profile
at
V
.

A natural class of spaces where to set the isoperimetric problem is given by metric measure spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
(see
Section

2
). Indeed, the nonnegative Borel measure
\mathfrak{m}
plays the role of a volume functional, and, together with a distance
\mathsf{d}
, it is possible to give a definition of perimeter
P
(see
Definition

2.3
). The smooth and more classical counterpart of these spaces is given by Riemannian manifolds. If
(M,g)
is a Riemannian manifold of dimension
N
, the natural Riemannian distance and the
N
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{N}
yield the structure of metric measure space, and the corresponding definition of perimeter recovers the classical well-known notion à la Caccioppoli–De Giorgi. In fact, the theory of
BV
functions and of the perimeter functional on metric measure spaces has been blossoming in the last decades
[
47
,
4
,
5
,
48
,
7
]
.

We are here interested in the problem of the existence of isoperimetric regions, assuming least possible hypotheses on the ambient space. Hence the most natural way to approach the problem is to argue
by direct method
, that is, by studying the behavior of a minimizing sequence of sets
E_{i}
of fixed volume
V
whose perimeter is converging to the isoperimetric profile at volume
V
. It is therefore understood that, by usual precompactness and lower semicontinuity, the problem of existence is nontrivial only in case the ambient is
noncompact
(actually, with infinite measure), which we will always assume.

Already in the smooth ambient, the development of an effective theory of a direct method for the isoperimetric problem is a difficult task. Studying the problem in Euclidean solid cones, in
[
61
]
the authors identified a general mass splitting phenomenon of a minimizing sequence for the problem, where the sequence decomposes into two components, one converging in the space and the other diverging at infinity. Such result is here generalized in
Theorem

3.3
. Combining this approach with a concentration-compactness argument, in
[
57
]
the author performed a better description of the possible mass lost at infinity for the problem on Riemannian manifolds satisfying some mild asymptotic hypotheses on their ends. The theory has then been successfully applied to get existence theorems in
[
50
]
and further generalized in
[
54
]
. Finally, in
[
15
]
the above mentioned hypotheses on the ends of the manifold have been removed, further generalizing the method on Riemannian manifolds.

After the results in
[
15
]
, it is today understood that, already in smooth Riemannian manifolds, the isoperimetric problem becomes trivial, i.e., the isoperimetric profile vanishes, unless it is assumed a lower bound on the Ricci curvature and a positive lower bound on the volume of unit balls. In fact, as one of the two hypotheses is not satisfied, one can find examples where a description of the behavior of minimizing sequences is actually compromised, see
[
15
]
. Therefore, it becomes natural to consider the isoperimetric problem on
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
metric measure spaces, which are spaces encoding synthetic notions of Ricci curvature bounded below by
K\in\mathbb{R}
and dimension bounded above by
N\in(0,+\infty]
, see
Section

2
. Moreover, we shall address in this work the case of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces of the form
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N})
, i.e., endowed with the
N
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. We will call such spaces
N
-dimensional
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces. The case of arbitrary volume measures
\mathfrak{m}
appears to be more involved and related to a better understanding of the properties of the density of
\mathfrak{m}
with respect to the Hausdorff measure of the essential dimension of the space. We stress that the class of
N
-dimensional
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces, that has been recently introduced and studied in the works
[
44
,
35
,
14
,
25
]
, is the non-smooth generalization of the class of non collapsed Ricci limit spaces
[
30
]
, in which a volume convergence theorem holds. The Riemannian assumption is necessary here to exploit the convergence and stability results of
[
6
]
.

It is remarkable to notice that the development of a theory on such nonsmooth spaces already is a
necessary consequence
also of the approach by direct method of the problem on perfectly smooth Riemannian manifolds
[
15
]
. Indeed, nonsmooth
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N})
arise as limits in pointed Gromov–Hausdorff sense (
Definition

2.12
) of smooth manifolds
M
with Ricci and volume of unit balls bounded below along sequences of points diverging on
M
.

Capitalizing on the methods developed in
[
57
,
15
,
16
]
, we are able to give a description of the behavior of perimeter minimizing sequences for the isoperimetric problem on
\mathsf{RCD}
spaces as follows, generalizing and improving our previous results. For the notation and the notions of convergence appearing in
Theorem

1.1
, see
Section

2
,
Definition

2.12
, and
Definition

2.15
below.

Theorem 1.1

(Asymptotic mass decomposition)
.

Let
K\leq 0
and
N\geq 2
.
Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N})
be a noncompact
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space. Assume there exists
v_{0}>0
such that
\mathcal{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x))\geq v_{0}
for every
x\in X
. Let
V>0
. For every minimizing
(
for the perimeter
)
sequence of bounded sets
\Omega_{i}\subset X
of volume
V
, up to passing to a subsequence, there exist a nondecreasing bounded sequence
\{N_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, disjoint finite perimeter sets
\Omega_{i}^{c},\Omega_{i,j}^{d}\subset\Omega_{i}
, and points
p_{i,j}
, with
1\leq j\leq N_{i}
for any
i
, such that the following claims hold

•

\lim_{i}\mathsf{d}(p_{i,j},p_{i,\ell})=\lim_{i}\mathsf{d}(p_{i,j},o)=+\infty
, for any
j\neq\ell\leq\overline{N}
and any
o\in X
, where
\overline{N}:=\lim_{i}N_{i}<+\infty
;

•

\Omega_{i}^{c}
converges to
\Omega\subset X
in the sense of finite perimeter sets,
\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega_{i}^{c})\to_{i}\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega)
, and
P(\Omega_{i}^{c})\to_{i}P(\Omega)
. Moreover
\Omega
is an isoperimetric region in
X
;

•

for every
0<j\leq\overline{N}
,
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N},p_{i,j})
converges in the pmGH sense to a pointed
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space
(X_{j},\mathsf{d}_{j},\mathcal{H}^{N},p_{j})
. Moreover there are isoperimetric regions
Z_{j}\subset X_{j}
such that
\Omega^{d}_{i,j}\to_{i}Z_{j}
in
L^{1}
-strong and
P(\Omega^{d}_{i,j})\to_{i}P(Z_{j})
;

•

it holds that

(1.1)

I_{X}(V)=P(\Omega)+\sum_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}P(Z_{j}),\qquad\qquad V=\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega)+\sum_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}\mathcal{H}^{N}(Z_{j}).

Theorem

1.1
states a general behavior for minimizing sequences of the isoperimetric problem. Roughly speaking, the mass of a sequence splits into at most finitely many pieces and it is totally recovered by finitely many isoperimetric regions sitting in spaces “located at infinity” with respect the original ambient space. Notice that
Theorem

1.1
is not an existence theorem, nor it is a nonexistence result, instead it is a general tool for treating the problem by direct method. With such theorem it is then possible to recover the main existence and nonexistence results previously proved in
[
50
,
15
,
13
]
, and, actually, to suitably extend those to the nonsmooth
\mathsf{RCD}
setting. We also mention that, taking into account
Proposition

4.1
below, (
1.1
) can be thought as a generalized existence result of isoperimetric regions.

The asymptotic mass decomposition result above actually follows from the following new result of generalized compactness of sequences of sets with uniformly bounded volume and perimeter. Notice that a generalized compactness result like the following one has been proved in
[
54
]
in the Riemannian setting.

Theorem 1.2

(Generalized compactness)
.

Let
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\geq 2
.
Let
(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathcal{H}^{N})
be a sequence of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces, and let
E_{i}\subset X_{i}
be bounded sets of finite perimeter such that
\sup_{i}P(E_{i})+\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{i})<+\infty
. Assume there exists
v_{0}>0
such that
\mathcal{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x))\geq v_{0}
for every
x\in X_{i}
, and for every
i
.

Then, up to subsequence, there exist a nondecreasing, possibly unboundend, sequence
\{N_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, points
p_{i,j}\in X_{i}
, with
1\leq j\leq N_{i}
for any
i
, and pairwise disjoint subsets
E_{i,j}\subset E_{i}
such that

•

\lim_{i}\mathsf{d}_{i}(p_{i,j},p_{i,\ell})=+\infty
, for any
j\neq\ell<\overline{N}+1
, where
\overline{N}:=\lim_{i}N_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}
;

•

For every
1\leq j<\overline{N}+1
, the sequence
(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathcal{H}^{N},p_{i,j})
converges in the pmGH sense to a pointed
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space
(Y_{j},\mathsf{d}_{Y_{j}},\mathcal{H}^{N},p_{j})
as
i\to+\infty
;

•

there exist sets
F_{j}\subset Y_{j}
such that
E_{i,j}\to_{i}F_{j}
in
L^{1}
-strong and there holds

(1.2)

\lim_{i}\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}\mathcal{H}^{N}(F_{j}),

(1.3)

\sum_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}P(F_{j})\leq\liminf_{i}P(E_{i}).

Moreover, if
E_{i}
is an isoperimetric set in
X_{i}
for any
i
, then
F_{j}
is an isoperimetric set in
Y_{j}
for any
j<\overline{N}+1
and

(1.4)

P(F_{j})=\lim_{i}P(E_{i,j}),

for any
j<\overline{N}+1
.

We notice that, in the first part of
Theorem

1.2
, no a priori minimality property is required on the sequence of sets
E_{i}
.
Theorem

1.2
is clearly mostly useful in case the spaces
X_{i}
in the statement are noncompact. Nevertheless, the statement applies also in case the diameter of the
X_{i}
’s is uniformly bounded, in which case one trivially finds
N_{i}=\overline{N}=1
for any
i
.

In
[
17
]
, the above results are crucially exploited to show useful properties of the isoperimetric profile of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces, without assumptions on the existence of isoperimetric sets. Building on such properties we shall prove a more explicit upper bound on the number
\overline{N}
in
Theorem

1.1
, which turns out to be bounded from above linearly in terms of the volume
V
. More precisely, we obtain the next corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
K\leq 0
,
N\geq 2
, and
v_{0}>0
. Then there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(K,N,v_{0})>0
such that the following holds.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N})
be a noncompact
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space such that
\mathcal{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x))\geq v_{0}
for every
x\in X
. Let
V>0
and let
\Omega_{i}\subset X
be a minimizing (for the perimeter) sequence of bounded sets of volume
V
. Letting
\overline{N}
be given by
Theorem

1.1
, then

\overline{N}\leq 1+\frac{V}{\varepsilon}.

We further present the last result of this work. Employing
Theorem

3.3
, we shall give an equivalent condition for a minimizing sequence for the isoperimetric problem to converge to an isoperimetric set without losing mass at infinity, see
Theorem

1.4
. This abstract condition involves a new function
I^{\infty}_{X}
, which can be interpreted as the isoperimetric profile at infinity (see
Definition

5.1
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
K\leq 0
, and let
N\geq 2
. Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N})
be a noncompact
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space. Assume that the
isoperimetric profile
I_{X}
is continuous.

Let
V\in(0,\mathcal{H}^{N}(X))
. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

I_{X}(V)<I_{X}(V_{1})+I^{\infty}_{X}(V_{2})
for all
V_{1}+V_{2}=V
with
V_{2}\in(0,V]
.

(2)

For any sequence
\Omega_{k}\subset X
such that
\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega_{k})\to V
and
P(\Omega_{k})\to I_{X}(V)
, there exists a subsequence converging in
L^{1}(X)
.

Let us briefly comment on the statement above.
If
K\in\mathbb{R}
,
N\geq 2
, and
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N})
is an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, we recall that the assumption that
I_{X}
is continuous is equivalent to require that it is lower semicontinuous. Indeed, by
[
15
, Lemma 3.1]
, the isoperimetric profile of these spaces is always upper semicontinuous.

Notice also that the item (1) in
Theorem

1.4
implies that for any sequence
\Omega_{k}\subset X
such that
\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega_{k})=V
and
P(\Omega_{k})\to I_{X}(V)
there exists a subsequence converging in
L^{1}(X)
, even without assuming the continuity of the isoperimetric profile. The proof is done verbatim as in the proof of
Theorem

1.4
, by using
Theorem

3.3
.

Moreover, the latter implication holds on open subsets of complete Riemannian manifolds because its proof only relies on Ritoré–Rosales Theorem, cf.
[
61
, Theorem 2.1]
(see also the discussion in
Remark

3.4
).

We also observe that Item (2) (or, equivalently, (1)) in
Theorem

1.4
is a much stronger property than just existence of isoperimetric regions. Such property clearly does not hold on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, or in general on noncompact spaces endowed with some strong homogeneity structure, like simply connected models of constant sectional curvature
K\leq 0
. However, in presence of a homogeneity structure on the space, one expects to be able to apply
Theorem

1.1
much more directly and that
Theorem

1.4
is not needed (see also
[
39
,
58
]
).

On the other hand, one expects that
Theorem

1.4
is applicable on spaces with no homogeneity invariance satisfying some asymptotic assumptions. For instance, it follows from
Lemma

5.3
that if
I_{X}
is strictly subadditive on
(0,V]
, then item (1) is satisfied, and thus by
Theorem

1.4
existence of isoperimetric regions of volume
V
holds. After
[
17
]
, this reasoning applies, for example, to every noncompact
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)
space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N})
(different from
\mathbb{R}^{N}
) that is Gromov–Hausdorff asymptotic to
\mathbb{R}^{N}
at infinity. This also extends to the nonsmooth setting some existence theorems in
[
15
]
.

We conclude this introduction by mentioning some applications in relation with the existing literature.

Understanding existence or nonexistence of isoperimetric regions in relation with the geometric properties of an ambient space is certainly an intriguing problem in its own.
As mentioned above, existence issues in Euclidean solid cones have been investigated in
[
61
]
. In more general convex bodies, it is treated in
[
45
]
.
The existence of isoperimetric sets on Riemannian manifolds
(M^{n},g)
with compact quotient
M/\mathrm{Iso}(M^{n})
has been pointed out by Morgan
[
51
, Chapter 3]
, building also on
[
2
]
.
On nonnegatively curved surfaces, a complete positive answer to the existence of isoperimetric sets has been given in
[
60
]
. The existence of isoperimetric sets in
3
-manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature and asymptotically flat asymptotics has been established in
[
27
]
. Existence results for isoperimetric sets of large volumes in asymptotically flat manifolds were also obtained in
[
36
,
37
,
56
]
, in asymptotically hyperbolic spaces in
[
33
]
, and in the asymptotically conical case in
[
34
]
. When the ambient space is a nonnegatively Ricci curved cone, isoperimetric regions exist for any given volume and are actually characterized
[
53
]
. Recently, the isoperimetric problem for large volumes in nonnegatively Ricci curved manifolds has been studied in
[
13
]
.
As discussed more diffusely in
[
15
]
, it is possible to recover several of the above mentioned existence result by employing a tool like
Theorem

1.1
.

On the other hand, existence issues for the isoperimetric problem are also related to the other important questions on the geometry of the ambient where the problem is set. For instance, the problem is related to the existence of foliations by constant mean curvature hypersurfaces in the end of a manifold
[
33
,
34
]
. Moreover, the existence of isoperimetric regions has been crucial for the derivation of differential properties of the isoperimetric profile
[
19
,
52
,
21
,
20
,
22
]
. As shown in
[
17
]
,
Theorem

1.1
is a crucial ingredient for proving such properties without assuming existence of isoperimetric regions, as well as, for deriving isoperimetric inequalities and other geometric functional inequalities like the ones contained in
[
1
,
18
,
24
]
.

2.
Preliminaries and auxiliary results

We recall that a
metric measure space,
\mathrm{m.m.s.}
for short,

(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is a triple where
(X,\mathsf{d})
is a locally compact separable metric space and
\mathfrak{m}
is a nonnegative Borel measure bounded on bounded sets. A
pointed metric measure space
is a quadruple
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},x)
where
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is a metric measure space and
x\in X
is a point. For simplicity, and since it will always be our case, we will always assume that given
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
a m.m.s.​ the support
{\rm spt}\,\mathfrak{m}
of the measure
\mathfrak{m}
is the whole
X
. We denote with
B_{r}(x)
the open ball of radius
r
and center
x\in X
.

2.1.
Concentration-compactness on sequences of metric measure spaces

The following version of the concentration-compactness theorem is a slight improvement of
[
15
, Lemma 4.3]
(cf. also
[
57
, Lemma 2.1]
) but it is stated in the broad generality of metric measure spaces.

Lemma 2.1

(Concentration-compactness)
.

Let
(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i})
be a sequence of metric measure spaces. Let
E_{i}\subset X_{i}
be a sequence of bounded measurable sets such that
\lim_{i}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i})=W\in(0,+\infty)
. Then, up to passing to a subsequence, exactly one of the following alternatives occur.

(1)

For any
R>0
it holds

\lim_{i}\sup_{p\in X_{i}}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R}(p))=0.

(2)

There exists a sequence of points
p_{i}\in X_{i}
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,W/2)
there exist
R\geq 1
,
i_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{i}))-W|\leq\varepsilon
for any
i\geq i_{\varepsilon}
, and any
R^{\prime}\geq R
. Moreover, there is
I\in\mathbb{N},r\geq 1
such that
\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{r}(p_{i}))\geq\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{r}(q))
for any
q\in X
and
\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{r}(p_{i}))>W/2
for any
i\geq I
.

(3)

There exist
w\in(0,W)
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,w/2)
there exist a sequence of points
p_{i}\in X_{i}
and
R\geq 1
,
i_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
, and a sequence of open sets

U_{i}=X_{i}\setminus\overline{B}_{R_{i}}(p_{i})\quad\text{for some $R_{i}\to+\infty$},

such that

(2.1)

\begin{split}|\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R}(p_{i}))-w|<\varepsilon,&\\
|\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap U_{i})-(W-w)|<\varepsilon,&\\
\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R}(p_{i}))\geq\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R}(q))&\qquad\forall\,q\in X_{i},\end{split}

for every
i\geq i_{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

Define
Q_{i}(\rho)\vcentcolon=\sup_{p\in X_{i}}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{\rho}(p))
. The functions
Q_{i}:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
are nondecreasing and uniformly bounded, since
\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i})\to W
. Hence the sequence
Q_{i}
is uniformly bounded in
BV_{\rm loc}(0,+\infty)
and then, up to subsequence, there exists a nondecreasing function
Q\in BV_{\rm loc}(0,+\infty)
such that
Q_{i}\to Q
in
BV_{\rm loc}
and pointwise almost everywhere. Also, let us pointwise define
Q(\rho)\vcentcolon=\lim_{\eta\to 0^{+}}{\rm ess}\inf_{(\rho-\eta,\rho)}Q
, so that
Q
is defined at every
\rho\in(0,+\infty)
. Moreover, observe that
Q(\rho)\leq W
for any
\rho>0
. Now three disjoint cases can occur, distinguishing the cases enumerated in the statement.

(1)

We have that
\lim_{\rho\to+\infty}Q(\rho)=0
, and hence
Q\equiv 0
since it is nondecreasing. Then item 1 of the statement clearly holds.

(2)

We have that
\lim_{\rho\to+\infty}Q(\rho)=W
. Then there is
r\geq 1
such that
\exists\lim_{i}\sup_{p}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{r}(p))=Q(r)\geq\tfrac{3}{4}W
. Let
p_{i}\in X_{i}
such that
\sup_{p}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{r}(p))=\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{r}(p_{i}))
for any
i
. We claim that the sequence
p_{i}
satisfies the property in item 2. Indeed, let
\varepsilon\in(0,W/2)
be given. Arguing as above, since
\lim_{\rho\to+\infty}Q(\rho)=W
, there is a radius
r^{\prime}>0
and a sequence
p_{i}^{\prime}\in X
such that
\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{r^{\prime}}(p_{i}^{\prime}))\geq W-\varepsilon
, and
|\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i})-W|\leq\varepsilon
for any
i\geq i_{\varepsilon}
. Then
\mathsf{d}_{i}(p_{i},p_{i}^{\prime})<r+r^{\prime}
, for otherwise

W\xleftarrow{}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i})\geq\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{r}(p_{i}))+\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{r^{\prime}}(p_{i}^{\prime})),

and the right hand side is
>W
for
i
large enough. Hence taking
R=r+2r^{\prime}
we conclude that
\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{i}))\geq\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R}(p_{i}))\geq W-\varepsilon
for
i\geq i_{\varepsilon}
. Moreover
\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{i}))\leq\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i})\leq W+\varepsilon
, and thus we get the sought claim.

(3)

We have that
\lim_{\rho\to+\infty}Q(\rho)=w\in(0,W)
. Then for given
\varepsilon\in(0,w/2)
there is
R\geq 1
such that

w-\frac{\varepsilon}{8}\leq Q(R)=\lim_{i}\sup_{p}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R}(p))=\lim_{i}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R}(p_{i})),

for some
p_{i}\in X_{i}
, where in the last equality we used that

\sup_{p}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R}(p))=\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R}(p_{i})),

for some
p_{i}
since
E_{i}
is bounded. This implies that
\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R}(p_{i}))\geq\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R}(q))
for any
i
and any
q\in X_{i}
, and there is
i_{\varepsilon}
such that
|\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R}(p_{i}))-w|<\varepsilon/4
for
i\geq i_{\varepsilon}
.

For
i\geq i_{\varepsilon}
, there is an increasing sequence
\rho_{j}\to+\infty
such that
Q(\rho_{j})=\lim_{i}Q_{i}(\rho_{j})
and we have

\begin{split}w&=\lim_{j\to+\infty}Q(\rho_{j})=\lim_{j}\lim_{i}\sup_{p}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{\rho_{j}}(p))\geq\limsup_{j}\limsup_{i}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{\rho_{j}}(p_{i}))\\
&=\limsup_{j}\limsup_{i}\left(\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R}(p_{i}))+\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{\rho_{j}}(p_{i})\setminus B_{R}(p_{i}))\right)\\
&\geq w-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}+\limsup_{j}\limsup_{i}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{\rho_{j}}(p_{i})\setminus B_{R}(p_{i})).\end{split}

Then there is
j_{0}
such that for any
j\geq j_{0}
we have that
\rho_{j}>R
and there is
i_{j}
, with
i_{j}
increasing to
+\infty
as
j\to+\infty
, that satisfies

(2.2)

\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{\rho_{j}}(p_{i})\setminus B_{R}(p_{i}))<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\qquad\forall\,i\geq\max\{i_{\varepsilon},i_{j}\}.

Hence define

R_{i}\vcentcolon=\rho_{\max\{j\ :\ i\geq i_{j}\}}.

In this way
\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R_{i}}(p_{i})\setminus B_{R}(p_{i}))<\varepsilon/2
for any
i\geq\max\{i_{\varepsilon},i_{j_{0}}\}
by (
2.2
). Defining
U_{i}\vcentcolon=X_{i}\setminus\overline{B}_{R_{i}}(p_{i})
we finally get

\begin{split}W\xleftarrow{}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i})&=\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R}(p_{i}))+\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R_{i}}(p_{i})\setminus B_{R}(p_{i}))+\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap U_{i})\\
&\leq w+\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon+\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap U_{i}),\end{split}

for
i\geq\max\{i_{\varepsilon},i_{j_{0}}\}
. By the first line in the above identity, recalling that
|\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap B_{R}(p_{i}))-w|<\varepsilon/4
, we also see that
\limsup_{i}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(E_{i}\cap U_{i})\leq W-w+\varepsilon/4
. Hence the proof of item 3 is completed renaming
\max\{i_{\varepsilon},i_{j_{0}}\}
into
i_{\varepsilon}
and by eventually taking a slightly bigger

in order to ensure the validity of the second inequality of item 3.

2.2.
Perimeter and isoperimetry on metric measure spaces and

We now discuss the basic definitions and properties of the objects involved in the following.

Definition 2.3

(Perimeter and isoperimetric profile)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space. A function
f\in L^{1}(X,\mathfrak{m})
is said to belong to the space of
bounded variation functions

BV(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
if there is a sequence
f_{i}\in{\rm Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X)
such that
f_{i}\to f
in
L^{1}(X,\mathfrak{m})
and
\limsup_{i}\int_{X}{\rm lip\,}f_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}<+\infty
, where
{\rm lip\,}u(x)\vcentcolon=\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}
is the
slope
of
u
at
x
, for any accumulation point
x\in X
, and
{\rm lip\,}u(x):=0
if
x\in X
is isolated. In such a case we define

|Df|(A)\vcentcolon=\inf\left\{\liminf_{i}\int_{A}{\rm lip\,}f_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\ :\ \text{$f_{i}\in{\rm Lip}_{\rm loc}(A),f_{i}\to f$ in $L^{1}(A,\mathfrak{m})$}\right\},

for any open set
A\subset X
.

If
E\subset X
is a Borel set and
A\subset X
is open, we define the
perimeter
P(E,A)
of
E
in
A
by

P(E,A)\vcentcolon=\inf\left\{\liminf_{i}\int_{A}{\rm lip\,}u_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\ :\ \text{$u_{i}\in{\rm Lip}_{\rm loc}(A),u_{i}\to\chi_{E}$ in $L^{1}_{\rm loc}(A,\mathfrak{m})$}\right\}.

We say that
E
has
finite perimeter
if
P(E,X)<+\infty
, and we denote by
P(E)\vcentcolon=P(E,X)
. Let us remark that the set functions
|Df|,P(E,\cdot)
above are restrictions to open sets of Borel measures that we denote by
|Df|,|D\chi_{E}|
respectively, see
[
7
]
, and
[
47
]
.

The
isoperimetric profile of
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is

I_{X}(V)\vcentcolon=\inf\left\{P(E)\ :\ \text{$E\subset X$ Borel, $\mathfrak{m}(E)=V$}\right\},

for any
V\in[0,\mathfrak{m}(X))
. If
E\subset X
is Borel with
\mathfrak{m}(E)=V
and
P(E)=I_{X}(V)
, then we say that
E
is an
isoperimetric region
.

Definition 2.4

(PI space)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space.
We say that
\mathfrak{m}
is
uniformly locally doubling
if for every
R>0
there exists
C_{D}>0
such that the following holds

\mathfrak{m}(B_{2r}(x))\leq C_{D}\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x)),\qquad\forall x\in X\;\forall r\leq R.

We say that a
weak local
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality
holds on
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
if there exists
\lambda\geq 1
such that for every
R>0
there exists
C_{P}
such that for every pair of functions
(f,g)
where
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X,\mathfrak{m})
, and
g
is an upper gradient (cf.
[
43
, Section 10.2]
) of

f
,
the following inequality holds

\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|f-\overline{f}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\leq C_{P}r\fint_{B_{\lambda r}(x)}g\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m},

for every
x\in X
and
r\leq R
, where
\overline{f}(x):=\fint_{B_{r}(x)}f\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}
.

We say that
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is a
PI space
when
\mathfrak{m}
is uniformly locally doubling and a weak local
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality holds on
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
.

Remark 2.6

If

is a metric measure space where

In the following remark we record the famous Bishop–Gromov comparison results.

Remark 2.7

(Models of constant sectional curvature and Bishop–Gromov Comparison Theorem)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
with
N\geq 2
and
K\in\mathbb{R}
.

We denote by
v(N,K,r)
the volume of the ball of radius
r
in the (unique up to isometry) simply connected Riemannian manifold of sectional curvature
K
and dimension
N
, and by
s(N,K,r)
the volume of the boundary of such a ball.

For an arbitrary
\mathsf{CD}((N-1)K,N)
space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
the classical Bishop–Gromov volume comparison holds. More precisely, for a fixed
x\in X
, the function
\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))/v(N,K,r)
is nonincreasing in
r
and the function
P(B_{r}(x))/s(N,K,r)
is essentially nonincreasing in
r
, i.e., the inequality

P(B_{R}(x))/s(N,K,R)\leq P(B_{r}(x))/s(N,K,r),

holds for almost every radii
R\geq r
, see
[
65
, Theorem 18.8, Equation (18.8), Proof of Theorem 30.11]
. Moreover, it holds that

(2.3)

P(B_{r}(x))/s(N,K,r)\leq\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))/v(N,K,r),

for any
r>0
, indeed the last inequality follows from the monotonicity of the volume and perimeter ratios together with the coarea formula on balls.

In addition, if
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N})
is an
\mathsf{RCD}((N-1)K,N)
space, one can conclude that
\mathcal{H}^{N}
-almost every point has a unique measured Gromov–Hausdorff tangent isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
[
35
, Theorem 1.12]
), and thus, from the volume convergence in
[
35
]
, we get

(2.4)

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))}{v(N,K,r)}=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}=1,\qquad\text{for $\mathcal{H}^{N}$-almost every $x$},

where
\omega_{N}
is the Euclidean volume of the Euclidean unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Moreover, since the density function
x\mapsto\lim_{r\to 0}\mathcal{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))/\omega_{N}r^{N}
is lower semicontinuous (
[
35
, Lemma 2.2]
), it is bounded above by the constant
1
. Hence, from the monotonicity at the beginning of the remark we deduce that, if
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N})
is an
\mathsf{RCD}((N-1)K,N)
space, then for every
x\in X
we have

(2.5)

\mathcal{H}^{N}(B_{r}(x))\leq v(N,K,r),

for every
r>0
. Furthermore, combining (
2.5
) with (
2.3
), we also have that
P(B_{r}(x))\leq s(N,K,r)
for any
x\in X
and
r>0
. If also
\mathcal{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x))\geq v_{0}>0
for any
x\in X
, then we have that if
\mathcal{H}^{N}(B_{r_{i}}(x_{i}))\to 0
then
r_{i}\to 0
and
P(B_{r_{i}}(x_{i}))\to 0
.

Lemma 2.8

(Covering Lemma,
[
16
, Lemma 3.7]
)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space where
\mathfrak{m}
is uniformly locally doubling, let
\overline{R}>0
, and let
\rho\leq\overline{R}
.

(1)

Let
C_{1},s
be the constants associated to the radius
\overline{R}
as in
Remark

2.6
. Hence, for any
\alpha>0
,
z\in X
,
\alpha\rho\leq R\leq\overline{R}
, it holds

\sharp\mathscr{F}\leq C_{1}\left(\frac{2R}{\alpha\rho}\right)^{s},

for any family
\mathscr{F}
of disjoint balls of radius
\alpha\rho
contained in

.

(2)

If
\Omega\subset X
is open and
D\subset\Omega
is dense in
\Omega
, there exist countably many points
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset D
such that

(2.6)

\begin{split}B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{i})\cap B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{j})=\emptyset&\qquad\forall\,i\neq j,\\
\bigcup_{i}B_{\rho}(x_{i})\supset D&,\\
\bigcup_{i}B_{\lambda\rho}(x_{i})\supset\Omega&\qquad\forall\,\lambda>1.\end{split}

Moreover, for any
\beta\leq\overline{R}
and

, it holds

(2.7)

\sharp\left\{\text{balls $B_{\beta\rho}(x_{i})\ :\ z\in B_{\beta\rho}(x_{i})$}\right\}\leq\max\left\{1,C_{1}(8\beta)^{s}\right\},

where
C_{1},s
are the constants as in
Remark

2.6
associated to
2\overline{R}^{2}
.

If
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
, then the same holds with

.

Remark 2.9

(Relative isoperimetric inequalities)
.

Relative isoperimetric inequalities are today well understood in the following settings of metric measure spaces.

•

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a PI space. Let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded set, let

, and let

\min\left\{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x)\cap E)^{1-\frac{1}{s}},\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x)\setminus E)^{1-\frac{1}{s}}\right\}\leq C_{\rm RI}^{\prime}P(E,B_{5\lambda r}(x)),

and

The next lemma essentially states that, in the suitable setting, it is always possible to find a ball where the mass of a set of finite perimeter concentrates. Such result will be properly used to rule out the occurrence of the first alternative in

.

Lemma 2.10

(Local mass lower bound)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
space. Assume that
\mathfrak{m}(B_{1}(x))\geq v_{0}>0
for any
x\in X
. Then there exists a constant
C_{M}=C_{M}(N,K,v_{0})>0
such that for any finite perimeter set
E
with
\mathfrak{m}(E)\in(0,+\infty)
there exists
x_{0}\in X
such that

\mathfrak{m}(E\cap B_{1}(x_{0}))\geq\min\left\{C_{M}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(E)^{N}}{P(E)^{N}},\frac{v_{0}}{2}\right\}.

Proof.

If there is
x_{0}\in X
such that
\mathfrak{m}(E\cap B_{1}(x_{0}))\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\mathfrak{m}(B_{1}(x_{0}))
, then
\mathfrak{m}(E\cap B_{1}(x_{0}))\geq v_{0}/2
. So we can assume instead that

(2.10)

\mathfrak{m}(E\cap B_{1}(x))<\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{m}(B_{1}(x))\qquad\forall\,x\in X.

We apply item (2) in
Lemma

2.8
with
\Omega=D=X
and
\beta=\rho=1
, which yields a covering
\{B_{1}(x_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X
. Let
i_{0}
be such that

L\vcentcolon=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{m}(E\cap B_{1}(x_{i}))^{\frac{1}{N}}\leq 2\mathfrak{m}(E\cap B_{1}(x_{i_{0}}))^{\frac{1}{N}}.

By (
2.10
) and (
2.9
) we get

(2.11)

\mathfrak{m}(E\cap B_{1}(x))^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\leq C_{\rm RI}P(E,B_{1}(x))\qquad\forall\,x\in X,

where
C_{\rm RI}
is the constant in (
2.9
) associated to
R=1
.
Therefore, using (
2.11
), (
2.7
), and the choice of
i_{0}
, we estimate

(2.12)

\begin{split}\mathfrak{m}(E)&\leq\sum_{i}\mathfrak{m}(E\cap B_{1}(x_{i}))=\sum_{i}\mathfrak{m}(E\cap B_{1}(x_{i}))^{\frac{1}{n}}\mathfrak{m}(E\cap B_{1}(x_{i}))^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\\
&\leq L\sum_{i}C_{\rm RI}P(E,B_{1}(x_{i}))\leq 2C_{\rm RI}\max\{1,C_{1}8^{N}\}P(E)\mathfrak{m}(E\cap B_{1}(x_{i_{0}}))^{\frac{1}{N}},\end{split}

where
C_{1}
is as in
Remark

2.6
with
R=1
and depends on
K,N
only. Hence (
2.12
) completes the proof.
∎

Definition 2.12

(pGH and pmGH convergence)
.

A sequence
\{(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},x_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of pointed metric spaces is said to converge in the
pointed Gromov–Hausdorff topology, in the
\mathrm{pGH}
sense for short,
to a pointed metric space
(Y,\mathsf{d}_{Y},y)
if there exist a complete separable metric space
(Z,\mathsf{d}_{Z})
and isometric embeddings

\begin{split}&\Psi_{i}:(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i})\to(Z,\mathsf{d}_{Z}),\qquad\forall\,i\in\mathbb{N},\\
&\Psi:(Y,\mathsf{d}_{Y})\to(Z,\mathsf{d}_{Z}),\end{split}

Let

and

are m.m.s.​ If in addition to the previous requirements we also have

with respect to duality with continuous bounded functions on

pointed measure Gromov–Hausdorff topology, or in the

sense for short

Remark 2.13

(pmGH limit of
\mathsf{RCD}
spaces)

We recall that, whenever it exists, a pmGH limit of a sequence

Lemma 2.23
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N})

I_{X}(V)\leq P(E\sqcup B_{r_{V}}(x_{0}))=P(E)+P(B_{r_{V}}(x_{0}))\leq P(E)+Cr_{V}^{N-1}\leq I_{X}(V_{0})+Cr_{V}^{N-1}+\varepsilon,

2.3

\frac{\mathcal{H}^{N}(B_{r_{V}}(x_{0}))}{v(N,K,r_{v})}\geq\frac{\mathcal{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}{v(N,K,1)}=:\eta_{0}(N,K,v_{0}),

spaces. Such property is classical, and we just quickly outline the arguments that apply to our setting, since we were not able to find a precise reference to quote.

Remark 3.1

(Compactness of
W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
space with
K\leq 0
and
N<+\infty
. Let us fix
R>0
and
x\in X
, and denote
B:=B_{R}(x)
. We want to show that if
f_{i}\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X)
are such that

\sup_{i}\left(\|f_{i}\|_{L^{1}(B)}+\||Df_{i}|\|_{L^{1}(B)}\right)<+\infty,

hence there exists
f\in L^{1}(B)
such that
f_{i}\to f
in
L^{1}(B)
.

Indeed, let us first notice that, as a consequence of Bishop–Gromov comparison, the measure
\mathfrak{m}
is locally doubling. Moreover, as a consequence of
[
59
, Theorem 1.1]
and Bishop–Gromov comparison, we have that, for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the pair
(f_{i},|Df_{i}|)
satisfies a
1
-Poincare inequality in
B
according to the definition in
[
43
, Equation (5)]
.

Moreover, an application of
[
43
, Theorem 9.7]
where
\Omega,s
there become
B,n
here, gives us that

(3.1)

\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(B)}\int_{B}|f_{i}-c|^{n/(n-1)}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\right)^{(n-1)/n}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\mathfrak{m}(B)}\int_{B}|Df_{i}|\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m},

for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, where
C_{1}
is some constant depending on
B
. Notice that
[
43
, Theorem 9.7]
can be applied since the hypotheses are met due to the fact that Bishop–Gromov comparison theorem holds, balls are John domains in
X
(cf.
[
43
, Corollary 9.5]
) and
(f_{i},|Df_{i}|)
has the truncation property (cf.
[
43
, Theorem 10.3]
). As a consequence of (
3.1
) we have that, calling
\overline{f}_{i}:=(\int_{B}f_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m})/\mathfrak{m}(B)
, we get,

\|f_{i}-\overline{f_{i}}\|_{L^{n/(n-1)}(B)}\leq C_{2}\||Df_{i}|\|_{L^{1}(B)},

for every
i>0
, and for some constant
C_{2}
depending on
B
. From the previous one easily gets, by using the triangular inequality, that

\|f_{i}\|_{L^{n/(n-1)}(B)}\leq C_{3}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{1}(B)}+C_{2}\||Df_{i}|\|_{L^{1}(B)}.

Now, since
[
43
, Equation (46)]
is met, we can apply
[
43
, Theorem 8.1]
and conclude that, up to subsequences,
f_{i}\to f
in
L^{1}(B)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
space with
K\leq 0
and
N<+\infty
. Let
g_{i}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X,\mathfrak{m})
such that, for every open bounded set
\Omega\subseteq X
, we have

\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\|g_{i}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega,\mathfrak{m})}+|Dg_{i}|(\Omega)\right)<+\infty.

Hence there exists
g\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
such that
g_{i}\to g
up to subsequences in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X,\mathfrak{m})
.

Proof.

Let us fix
x\in X
and
B:=B_{R}(x)
,
2B:=B_{2R}(x)
for some
R>0
. Choosing
\Omega=2B
,
from the hypothesis and from the definition of the total variation
|Dg_{i}|
we have the existence of
f_{i}\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}(2B)
such that

\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\|f_{i}\|_{L^{1}(B,\mathfrak{m})}+\||Df_{i}|\|_{L^{1}(2B,\mathfrak{m})}\right)<+\infty,\quad\text{and for all $i$ we have}\|f_{i}-g_{i}\|_{L^{1}(B,\mathfrak{m})}\leq 1/i.

Hence
Remark

3.1
implies that there is a subsequence, still denoted by
f_{i}
, and
g\in L^{1}(B,\mathfrak{m})
such that we have
f_{i}\to g
in
L^{1}(B,\mathfrak{m})
. Finally, the choice of
f_{i}
, we also have
g_{i}\to g
in
L^{1}(B,\mathfrak{m})
.

By taking
R_{i}\to+\infty
and using a diagonal argument, we get the existence of
g\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X,\mathfrak{m})
as in the statement.
∎

We can now prove the main result of this section on the mass splitting of perimeter minimizing sequences.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
K\leq 0
, and let
N\geq 2
. Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N},x)
be a pointed noncompact
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space. Let us fix a minimizing (for the perimeter) sequence
\{\Omega_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of Borel sets of volume
V
. Hence, up to passing to a subsequence, there exists a finite perimeter set
\Omega\subseteq X
and sequence of finite perimeter sets
\{\Omega_{k}^{c}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\Omega_{k}^{d}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that the following holds.

(i)

There exists a diverging sequence of radii
\{r_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
such that

\Omega_{k}^{c}:=\Omega_{k}\cap B_{r_{k}}(x),\qquad\Omega_{k}^{d}:=\Omega_{k}\setminus B_{r_{k}}(x).

(ii)

We have that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left(P(\Omega_{k}^{c})+P(\Omega_{k}^{d})\right)=I_{X}(V).

(iii)

\Omega
is an isoperimetric region and

(3.2)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega_{k}^{c})=\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega),\qquad\text{and},\qquad\lim_{k\to+\infty}P(\Omega_{k}^{c})=P(\Omega).

Proof.

Let
f_{k}:=\chi_{\Omega_{k}}
. From
Lemma

3.2
we get that there exists
g\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X,\mathcal{H}^{N})
such that, up to passing to subsequences,
f_{k}\to g
in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X,\mathcal{H}^{N})
. Hence
g=\chi_{\Omega}
for some Borel set
\Omega\subseteq X
, and
\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega)\leq V
by Fatou’s Lemma. Moreover, by lower semicontinuity, we get that
P(\Omega)\leq I_{X}(V)
. Hence if
\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega)=V
we get the conclusion with
\Omega_{k}^{c}:=\Omega_{k}
and
\Omega_{k}^{d}:=\emptyset
.
Suppose from now on that
\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega)<V
.

Let us set
r_{0}:=0
. We want to define
r_{k}
inductively in such a way that, up to eventually passing to a subsequence, we have

(a)

\{r_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is divergent and
r_{k}-r_{k-1}\geq k
for every
k\geq 1
;

(b)

P(\Omega_{k}\cap B_{r_{k}}(x))=P(\Omega_{k},B_{r_{k}}(x))+P(B_{r_{k}}(x),\Omega_{k}^{(1)})
for every
k\geq 1
;

(c)

P(\Omega_{k}\setminus B_{r_{k}}(x))=P(\Omega_{k},X\setminus B_{r_{k}}(x))+P(B_{r_{k}}(x),\Omega_{k}^{(1)})
for every
k\geq 1
;

(d)

P(B_{r_{k}}(x),\Omega_{k}^{(1)})\leq V/k
for every
k\geq 1
;

(e)

there holds

\int_{B_{r_{k}}(x)}|\chi_{\Omega_{k}}-\chi_{\Omega}|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{N}\leq 1/k,

for every
k\geq 1
.

First fix
r^{\prime}_{k}\geq r_{k-1}+2k
. Hence, up to passing to a subsequence in
k
, we can assume (e) holds with
r^{\prime}_{k}
in place of
r_{k}
. This can be done since
\chi_{\Omega_{k}}\to\chi_{\Omega}
in
L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(X,\mathcal{H}^{N})
. Moreover notice that, from the coarea formula we have that

\int_{I}P(B_{s}(x),\Omega_{k}^{(1)})\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega_{k}^{(1)})=V,

on every interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
.
Hence, by the previous estimate, we can find an
r_{k}\in[r^{\prime}_{k}-k,r^{\prime}_{k}]
such that (d) holds. Moreover, such an
r_{k}
can be taken in such a way that (b) and (c) hold, see
[
16
, Corollary 2.6]
. Moreover, since
r_{k}\leq r^{\prime}_{k}
, (e) still holds. Finally, since
r_{k}\geq r^{\prime}_{k}-k\geq r_{k-1}+k
, we get that the sequence diverges and (a) holds as well.

Let us prove item (ii). We have, for every
k\geq 1
, that

I_{X}(V)\leq P(\Omega_{k})\leq P(\Omega_{k}^{c})+P(\Omega_{k}^{d})\leq P(\Omega_{k})+2P(B_{r_{k}}(x),\Omega_{k}^{(1)}),

where we are using item (b), (c), and (d) proved above. Taking
k\to+\infty
we thus get item (ii).

Let us now prove the first convergence in (
3.2
). By the triangle inequality we have

|\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega_{k}^{c})-\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega)|\leq\int_{B_{r_{k}}(x)}|\chi_{\Omega_{k}}-\chi_{\Omega}|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{N}+\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega\setminus B_{r_{k}}(x)).

Taking
k\to+\infty
, and taking into account item (a), and (e) above, we get the sought convergence.

Let us now prove that
P(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}P(\Omega_{k}^{c})
. Indeed, this is a consequence of the fact that, by construction, for every
k\geq 1
we have

\int_{B_{r_{k}}(x)}|\chi_{\Omega_{k}}-\chi_{\Omega}|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{N}=\int_{B_{r_{k}}(x)}|\chi_{\Omega_{k}^{c}}-\chi_{\Omega}|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{N}.

Hence the previous equality, together with item (a) above, shows that
\chi_{\Omega_{k}^{c}}\to\chi_{\Omega}
in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X,\mathcal{H}^{N})
. Hence, by lower semicontinuity of the perimeter, we get the sought claim.

Let us call
\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega):=V_{0}
. Let us now prove that
\Omega
is an isoperimetric region. If not, there exists a Borel set
E
such that
\mathcal{H}^{N}(E)=V_{0}
and
P(E)<P(\Omega)
. Again by the coarea formula, and since item (a) holds, there exists, for every
k\geq 1
, a
t_{k}\in[r_{k-1},r_{k}]
such that

P(B_{t_{k}}(x),E^{(1)})\leq V_{0}/k.

Moreover,
t_{k}
can be chosen in such a way that
P(E\cap B_{t_{k}}(x))=P(E,B_{t_{k}}(x))+P(B_{t_{k}}(x),E^{(1)})
, see
[
16
, Corollary 2.6]
.
Notice that if
E_{k}:=E\cap B_{t_{k}}(x)
we have
\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{k})\to V_{0}
as
k\to+\infty
.

Let us now fix
p\in E^{(1)}
and
q\in E^{(0)}
. Recall by
Remark

2.7
that
P(B_{r}(p))\leq s(N,K/(N-1),r)
and
P(B_{r}(q))\leq s(N,K/(N-1),r)
hold for every
r>0
.

Since
\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega_{k}^{d})\to V-V_{0}
as
k\to+\infty
, we have that for
k
large enough there exist
\rho_{p,k},\rho_{q,k}<1
such that

\mathcal{H}^{N}((E_{k}\cup B_{\rho_{q,k}}(q))\setminus B_{\rho_{p,k}}(p))=V-\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega_{k}^{d}),

and moreover
\rho_{q,k},\rho_{p,k}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
. This is done by arguing verbatim as in the proof of
[
15
, Lemma 3.1]
.
Let us call
E^{\prime}_{k}:=(E_{k}\cup B_{\rho_{q,k}}(q))\setminus B_{\rho_{p,k}}(p)
and
T_{k}:=E^{\prime}_{k}\cup\Omega_{k}^{d}
. Hence

(3.3)

\begin{split}P(T_{k})&\leq P(E_{k})+P(B_{\rho_{q,k}}(q))+P(B_{\rho_{p,k}}(p))+P(\Omega_{k}^{d})\leq P(E,B_{t_{k}}(x))\\
&+P(B_{t_{k}}(x),E^{(1)})+s(N,K/(N-1),\rho_{q,k})+s(N,K/(N-1),\rho_{p,k})+P(\Omega_{k}^{d})\\
&\leq P(E)+V_{0}/k+s(N,K/(N-1),\rho_{q,k})+s(N,K/(N-1),\rho_{p,k})+P(\Omega_{k}^{d}).\end{split}

Hence, taking
k\to+\infty
in the previous inequality, we get

(3.4)

\begin{split}I_{X}(V)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}P(T_{k})&\leq P(E)+\liminf_{k\to+\infty}P(\Omega_{k}^{d})<P(\Omega)+\liminf_{k\to+\infty}P(\Omega_{k}^{d})\\
&\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}P(\Omega_{k}^{c})+\liminf_{k\to+\infty}P(\Omega_{k}^{d})\leq I_{X}(V),\end{split}

where in the previous inequality we are using
P(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}P(\Omega_{k}^{c})
, that we proved above, and the item (ii). This is a contradiction.

In order to conclude the proof we should just prove the second of (
3.2
). Moreover, since we already proved
P(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}P(\Omega_{k}^{c})
, we just need to prove that
P(\Omega)\geq\limsup_{k\to+\infty}P(\Omega_{k}^{c})
. If not, up to subsequence

P(\Omega)<\lim_{k\to+\infty}P(\Omega_{k}^{c}).

In order to reach a contradiction from the previous inequality, and thus conclude the proof, it suffices the run verbatim the argument we used above to prove that
\Omega
is an isoperimetric region, but with
\Omega
in place of
E
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

As one can notice by a careful inspection of the previous reasoning, the proof of
Theorem

3.3
is likely to hold in a metric measure setting more general than the one of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N})
. A key property is the vanishing, as
r\to 0
, of the perimeter of the balls of radius
r
around points of the metric measure space. Since this goes beyond the scope of this note, we will not discuss it further, but it will be subject of further investigations.

4.
The isoperimetric problem via direct method

We shall need the following useful result stating the equality between the isoperimetric profile of a space
X
and the one of an arbitrary disjoint union of
X
with some of its pmGH limits at infinity.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
K\leq 0
and let
N\geq 2
. Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N})
be a noncompact
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space. Assume there exists
v_{0}>0
such that
\mathcal{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x))\geq v_{0}
for every
x\in X
. Let
\{p_{i,j}\ :\ i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of points on
X
, for
j=1,\ldots,\overline{N}
where
\overline{N}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}
. Suppose that each sequence
\{p_{i,j}\ :\ i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is diverging along
X
and that
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N},p_{i,j})
converges in the pmGH sense to a pointed
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space
(X_{j},\mathsf{d}_{j},\mathcal{H}^{N},p_{j})
. Defining

(4.1)

I_{X\sqcup_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}X_{j}}(v):=\inf\left\{P(E)+\sum_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}P(E_{j}):E\subseteq X,E_{j}\subseteq X_{j},\mathcal{H}^{N}(E)+\sum_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{j})=v\right\},

it holds
I_{X\sqcup_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}X_{j}}(v)=I_{X}(v)
for any
v>0
.

Proof.

The inequality
I_{X\sqcup_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}X_{j}}\leq I_{X}
trivially follows from the definition of isoperimetric profile, so we need to prove the opposite inequality. By a minor variant of
Lemma

2.18
, cf.
[
15
, Lemma 2.19]
, one can prove that

\begin{split}I_{X\sqcup_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}X_{j}}(v):=\inf\bigg\{P(E)+\sum_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}P(E_{j})\ :\ &E\subset X,E_{j}\subset X_{j},\mathcal{H}^{N}(E)+\sum_{j=1}^{\overline{N}}\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{j})=v,\\
&E,E_{j}\text{ bounded for any }j\bigg\}.\end{split}

Now let
v>0
. Let us prove the result in the case
\overline{N}=+\infty
, as it will be clear that the case of
\overline{N}\in\mathbb{N}
follows by the analogous simplified argument.
For any
\varepsilon>0
there are bounded sets
E\subset X,E_{j}\subset X_{j}
such that

\mathcal{H}^{N}(E)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{j})=v,\qquad P(E)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}P(E_{j})\leq I_{X\sqcup_{j=1}^{\infty}X_{j}}(v)+\varepsilon.

For any
\delta>0
there is
j_{\delta}
such that
v(j_{\delta})\vcentcolon=\sum_{j_{\delta}+1}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{j})<\delta
and
\sum_{j_{\delta}+1}^{\infty}P(E_{j})<\delta
. Taking
\delta<v_{0}
, since
\{p_{i,j}\}_{i}
is diverging, repeatedly applying
Lemma

2.17
we can find disjoint sets
B_{r_{\delta}}(o),F_{1},\ldots,F_{j_{\delta}}\subset X
contained in balls mutually located at positive distance such that

\mathcal{H}^{N}(B_{r_{\delta}}(o))=v(j_{\delta}),\qquad\mathcal{H}^{N}(F_{j})=\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{j}),\qquad P(F_{j})\leq P(E_{j})+\varepsilon/j_{\delta},

for any
j=1,\ldots,j_{\delta}
, and
E
is located at positive distance from
B_{r_{\delta}}(o)
. Hence
E\cup B_{r_{\delta}}(o)\cup F_{1}\cup\ldots\cup F_{j_{\delta}}
has measure equal to
v
and

\begin{split}I_{X}(v)&\leq P(E)+P(B_{r_{\delta}}(o))+\sum_{j=1}^{j_{\delta}}P(F_{j})\leq P(E)+P(B_{r_{\delta}}(o))+\sum_{j=1}^{j_{\delta}}P(E_{j})+\varepsilon\\
&\leq s(N,K/(N-1),r_{\delta})+I_{X\sqcup_{j=1}^{\infty}X_{j}}(v)+2\varepsilon-\delta.\end{split}

Since
\varepsilon,\delta
are arbitrary, and
r_{\delta}\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
, we can let
\delta\to 0
and then
\varepsilon\to 0
to get the desired inequality.
∎

4.1.
Proof of the main results

We are ready for proving our main results leading to the description of the behavior of perimeter minimizing sequences on
\mathsf{RCD}
spaces. We start from the generalized compactness result in
Theorem

1.2
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

Up to subsequence, we may assume
P(E_{i})\to A
,
\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{i})\to W
. From the isoperimetric inequality for small volumes
Proposition

2.22
, we get that if
A=0
then
W=0
, and then the statement trivializes. Hence we may assume
A>0
. Moreover we may assume
W>0
as well without loss of generality. Furthermore, we shall identify
E_{i}
with the set of density
1
points
E_{i}^{(1)}
in this proof for ease of notation.

The proof follows the same strategy of
[
15
, Theorem 4.6]
, except that here we deal with a sequence of different ambient spaces
X_{i}
and the sequence
E_{i}
has no a priori minimality properties. However, several arguments do not make use of such properties, and then an analogous proof can be carried out also in this setting. We refer the reader to
[
15
, Theorem 4.6]
for detailed proofs of the first three steps below, which are based on an iterative application of
Lemma

2.1
together with
Lemma

2.10
.

Step 1.

Up to passing to a subsequence in
i
, we claim that for any
i
there exist an increasing sequence of natural numbers
N_{i}\geq 1
with limit
\overline{N}\vcentcolon=\lim_{i}N_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}
, points
p_{i,1},\ldots,p_{i,N_{i}}\in X_{i}
for any
i
, radii
R_{j}\geq 1
and numbers
\eta_{j}\in(0,1]
defined for
j<\overline{N}
, and, if
\overline{N}<+\infty
, also a sequence of radii
R_{i,\overline{N}}\geq 1
, such that

(4.2)

\begin{split}\lim_{i}\mathsf{d}_{i}(p_{i,j},p_{i,k})=+\infty&\qquad\forall\,j\neq k<\overline{N}+1,\\
\mathsf{d}_{i}(p_{i,j},p_{i,k})\geq R_{j}+R_{k}+2&\qquad\forall\,i\in\mathbb{N},\forall\,j\neq k\leq N_{i},\\
&\qquad j\neq\overline{N},k\neq\overline{N},\\
\exists\,\lim_{i}\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{i}\cap B_{R_{j}}(p_{i,j}))=w_{j}^{\prime}>0&\qquad\forall\,j<\overline{N},\\
P(E_{i},\partial B_{R_{j}}(p_{i,j}))=0&\qquad\forall\,i,\forall\,j\leq N_{i},j\neq\overline{N},\\
P(B_{R_{j}}(p_{i,j}),E_{i})\leq\frac{\eta_{j}}{2^{j}}&\qquad\forall\,i,\forall\,j\leq N_{i},j\neq\overline{N},\\
\text{if also $\overline{N}=+\infty$, then }W\geq\lim_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{i}}\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{i}\cap B_{R_{j}}(p_{i,j})),&\\
\text{if instead $\overline{N}<+\infty$, then $\lim_{i}R_{i,\overline{N}}=+\infty$,}&\\
P(E_{i},\partial B_{R_{i,\overline{N}}}(p_{i,\overline{N}}))=0&\qquad\forall\,i\ :\ N_{i}=\overline{N},\\
P(B_{R_{i,\overline{N}}}(p_{i,\overline{N}}),E_{i})\leq\frac{1}{2^{\overline{N}}}&\qquad\forall\,i\ :\ N_{i}=\overline{N},\\
\mathsf{d}_{i}\left(\partial B_{R_{i,\overline{N}}}(p_{i,\overline{N}}),\partial B_{R_{j}}(p_{i,j})\right)>2&\qquad\forall\,i\ :\ N_{i}=\overline{N},\forall\,j\neq\overline{N},\\
W=\lim_{i}\mathcal{H}^{N}\left(B_{R_{i,\overline{N}}}(p_{i,\overline{N}})\cap\left(E_{i}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{\overline{N}-1}B_{R_{j}}(p_{i,j})\right)\right)&+\sum_{j=1}^{\overline{N}-1}\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{i}\cap B_{R_{j}}(p_{i,j})).\\
\end{split}

Step 2.

We claim that if
\overline{N}=+\infty
then

(4.3)

W=\lim_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{i}}\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{i}\cap B_{R_{j}}(p_{i,j})).

Moreover, we claim that, up to passing to a subsequence in
i
, there exist sequences of radii
\{T_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
T_{i,j}\in(R_{j},R_{j}+1)
for any
j<\overline{N}
, and
T_{i,\overline{N}}\in(R_{i,\overline{N}},R_{i,\overline{N}}+1)
if
\overline{N}<+\infty
, such that the following hold

(4.4)

\begin{split}B_{T_{i,j}}(p_{i,j})\cap B_{T_{i,k}}(p_{i,k})=\emptyset&\qquad\forall\,i\in\mathbb{N},\forall\,j\neq k\leq N_{i},j\neq\overline{N},k\neq\overline{N},\\
\lim_{i}T_{i,j}=T_{j}<+\infty&\qquad\forall\,j<\overline{N},\\
P(E_{i},\partial B_{T_{i,j}}(p_{i,j}))=0&\qquad\forall i\in\mathbb{N},\,\,\forall 1\leq j\leq N_{i},\,j\neq\overline{N}\\
\text{if also $\overline{N}<+\infty$, then $\lim_{i}T_{i,\overline{N}}=+\infty$ and }&\\
\partial B_{T_{i,\overline{N}}}(p_{i,\overline{N}})\cap\partial B_{T_{i,j}}(p_{i,j})=\emptyset&\qquad\forall\,i\ :\ N_{i}=\overline{N},\forall\,j<\overline{N},\\
P(E_{i},\partial B_{T_{i,\overline{N}}}(p_{i,\overline{N}}))=0&\qquad\forall\,i\in\mathbb{N}.\end{split}

Moreover

(4.5)

\lim_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{i}}P(B_{T_{i,j}}(p_{i,j}),E_{i})=0.

Step 3.

We claim that letting
E_{i}^{v}\vcentcolon=E_{i}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{N_{i}}\left(E_{i}\cap B_{T_{i,j}}(p_{i,j})\right)
, then

(4.6)

\lim_{i}\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{i}^{v})=0,

and that, if

5.
An equivalent condition for compactness of minimizing sequences

In this final chapter we present an equivalent condition that ensures compactness of the minimizing sequences for the isoperimetric problem. This equivalent condition involves a kind of isoperimetric profile at infinity that we are going to define.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a noncompact metric measure space. For any
V\in(0,\mathfrak{m}(X))
we define

I^{\infty}_{X}(V)\vcentcolon=\inf\left\{\liminf_{i}P(E_{i})\ :\ \mathfrak{m}(E_{i})\to V,\,\quad\text{$\forall$ ball B},\,\,E_{i}\cap B=\emptyset\quad\text{for $i$ large enough}\right\},

\widetilde{I}^{\infty}_{X}(V)\vcentcolon=\inf\left\{\liminf_{i}P(E_{i})\ :\ \mathfrak{m}(E_{i})\to V,\,\lim_{i}\mathfrak{m}(E_{i}\cap B)=0\quad\text{$\forall$ ball B}\right\}.

We call
I_{X}^{\infty}

the isoperimetric profile at infinity of
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
and
\tilde{I}_{X}^{\infty}

the isoperimetric profile at infinity volumewise of
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
.

In the previous definition, we obviously have that
\widetilde{I}^{\infty}_{X}\leq I^{\infty}_{X}
. Under the following regularity assumptions on the space
X
, we can prove that the converse holds. The notion of isotropicity was introduced in

[
11
, Definition 6.1]
. We remark that all

{\sf RCD}(K,N)
spaces with
N<\infty
, are isotropic PI spaces;
cf.
[
23
, Example 1.31(iii)]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a noncompact metric measure space. Assume that
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is PI and isotropic, then
\widetilde{I}^{\infty}_{X}=I^{\infty}_{X}
.

Proof.

Let
V\in(0,\mathfrak{m}(X))
. We need to prove that
\widetilde{I}^{\infty}_{X}(V)\geq I^{\infty}_{X}(V)
. Let
E_{i}\subset X
be such that
\mathfrak{m}(E_{i})\to V
,
\lim_{i}\mathfrak{m}(E_{i}\cap B)=0
for any ball
B
, and
\lim_{i}P(E_{i})=\widetilde{I}^{\infty}_{X}(V)
. Fix
o\in X
. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, since
\int_{0}^{k}P(B_{r}(o),E_{i}^{(1)})\,\mathrm{d}r=\mathfrak{m}(E_{i}\cap B_{k}(o))\to_{i}0
, using also
[
16
, Corollary 2.6]
, we find
i_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
and
r_{k}\in(\tfrac{k}{2},k)
such that

\mathfrak{m}(E_{i_{k}}\cap B_{r_{k}}(o))<\frac{1}{k},\qquad P(B_{r_{k}}(o),E^{(1)}_{i_{k}})<\frac{1}{k},

P(E_{i_{k}}\setminus B_{r_{k}}(o))=P(E_{i_{k}},X\setminus B_{r_{k}}(o))+P(B_{r_{k}}(o),E^{(1)}_{i_{k}}).

Setting
F_{k}\vcentcolon=E_{i_{k}}\setminus B_{r_{k}}(o)
we get that

I^{\infty}_{X}(V)\leq\liminf_{k}P(F_{k})\leq\liminf_{k}P(E_{i_{k}})=\widetilde{I}^{\infty}_{X}(V).

∎

Let us now show a couple of inequalities involving
I_{X}
and
I_{X}^{\infty}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a noncompact isotropic PI space. Assume that the isoperimetric profile
I_{X}
is lower semicontinuous, then
I_{X}\leq\widetilde{I}^{\infty}_{X}=I^{\infty}_{X}
and

I_{X}(V)\leq I_{X}(V_{1})+I^{\infty}_{X}(V_{2}),\qquad\forall\,V_{1}+V_{2}=V,

for any
V\in(0,\mathfrak{m}(X))
.

Proof.

Assuming that
I_{X}
is lower semicontinuous, then

I_{X}(V)=\inf\left\{\liminf_{i}P(E_{i})\ :\ \lim_{i}\mathfrak{m}(E_{i})=V\right\},

for any
V\in(0,\mathfrak{m}(X))
. Hence, recalling
Lemma

5.2
, then
I_{X}\leq\widetilde{I}^{\infty}_{X}=I^{\infty}_{X}
.

Now let
V_{1},V_{2},V
be fixed as in the statement. Fix also
\varepsilon>0
. Let
E\subset X
such that
\mathfrak{m}(E)=V_{1}
and
P(E)\leq I_{X}(V_{1})+\varepsilon
. Let also
E_{i}\subset X
such that
\mathfrak{m}(E_{i})\to V_{2}
,
E_{i}\cap B=\emptyset
for any ball
B
for large
i
, and
P(E_{i})\leq I^{\infty}_{X}(V_{2})+\varepsilon
. Up to subsequence, there is a diverging sequence
r_{i}>0
such that
E_{i}\cap B_{2r_{i}}(o)=\emptyset
for any
i
, for some
o\in X
, and

P(E\cap B_{r_{i}}(o))=P(E,B_{r_{i}}(o))+P(B_{r_{i}}(o),E^{(1)}),\qquad P(B_{r_{i}}(o),E^{(1)})\leq\frac{1}{i},

where we used
[
16
, Corollary 2.6]
and the coarea formula. Letting
F_{i}\vcentcolon=(E\cap B_{r_{i}}(o))\cup E_{i}
, then
\mathfrak{m}(F_{i})\to V
and then

I_{X}(V)\leq\liminf_{i}P(F_{i})\leq\liminf_{i}\left(P(E)+P(E_{i})\right)\leq I_{X}(V_{1})+I^{\infty}_{X}(V_{2})+2\varepsilon.

By arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
, the claim follows.
∎

The following theorem is a slight empowered version of
Theorem

3.3
, where we allow that the volumes of the minimizing sequence are not fixed, but just tend to a fixed value
V
. The proof is obtained arguing verbatim as in the proof of

3.3

Theorem 5.4
.

Let

, and let

Hence, up to passing to a subsequence, there exists a finite perimeter set
\Omega\subseteq X
and a sequence of finite perimeter sets
\{\Omega_{k}^{c}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\Omega_{k}^{d}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that the following holds.

(i)

There exists a diverging sequence of radii

\Omega_{k}^{c}:=\Omega_{k}\cap B_{r_{k}}(x),\qquad\Omega_{k}^{d}:=\Omega_{k}\setminus B_{r_{k}}(x).

(ii)

We have that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left(P(\Omega_{k}^{c})+P(\Omega_{k}^{d})\right)=I_{X}(V).

(iii)

\Omega
is an isoperimetric region and

(5.1)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega_{k}^{c})=\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega),\qquad\lim_{k\to+\infty}P(\Omega_{k}^{c})=P(\Omega).

We now conclude the section by proving the equivalent criterion for all minimizing sequences to precompactly converge to a solution of the isoperimetric problem without loss of mass.

Proof of
Theorem

1.4
.

We start from the implication
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. Let
\Omega_{k}\subset X
such that
\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega_{k})\to V
and
P(\Omega_{k})\to I_{X}(V)
. Let
r_{k},\Omega_{k}^{c},\Omega_{k}^{d},\Omega
be given by applying
Theorem

5.4
. If by contradiction
V_{2}\vcentcolon=\lim_{k}\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega_{k}^{d})>0
, then

I_{X}(V)=P(\Omega)+\lim_{k}P(\Omega^{d}_{k})\geq I_{X}(\mathcal{H}^{N}(\Omega))+I^{\infty}_{X}(V_{2}),

contradicting
(1)
.

It remains to prove that
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
. Let
V_{1}+V_{2}=V
with
V_{2}\in(0,V]
. We consider the case
V_{2}<V
, the case
V_{2}=V
being analogous and simpler. Suppose by contradiction that
I_{X}(V)\geq I_{X}(V_{1})+I^{\infty}_{X}(V_{2})
.

Let
E_{j}\subset X
be such that
\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{j})=V_{1}
and
P(E_{j})\leq I_{X}(V_{1})+\tfrac{1}{j}
, and
\tilde{E}_{i}\subset X
such that
\mathcal{H}^{N}(\tilde{E}_{i})\to V_{2}
,
\tilde{E}_{i}\cap B=\emptyset
for any ball
B
for large
i
, and
P(\tilde{E}_{i})\to I^{\infty}_{X}(V_{2})
. Arguing similarly as in the proof of
Lemma

5.3
, for any
j
we can find
i_{j}\geq j
and a diverging sequence
r_{j}>0
such that

P(E_{j}\cap B_{r_{j}}(o))=P(E_{j},B_{r_{j}}(o))+P(B_{r_{j}}(o),E_{j}^{(1)}),\quad\mathcal{H}^{N}(E_{j}\setminus B_{r_{j}}(o))\leq\frac{1}{j},\quad P(B_{r_{j}}(o),E_{j}^{(1)})\leq\frac{1}{j},

and
\tilde{E}_{i_{j}}\cap B_{2r_{j}}(o)=\emptyset
for any
j
, for some
o\in X
.
Letting
F_{j}\vcentcolon=(E_{j}\cap B_{r_{j}}(o))\cup\tilde{E}_{i_{j}}
, then
\mathcal{H}^{N}(F_{j})\to V
and then

I_{X}(V)\leq\liminf_{j}P(F_{j})\leq I_{X}(V_{1})+I^{\infty}_{X}(V_{2})\leq I_{X}(V),

where the last inequality is the absurd hypothesis. Hence we can apply
(2)
on the sequence
\{F_{j}\}
, which then admits a subsequence converging in
L^{1}(X)
. However, the sets
\tilde{E}_{i_{j}}
’s are diverging and their measure converges to
V_{2}>0
, hence no subsequences of
\{F_{j}\}
can converge strongly in
L^{1}(X)
, contradicting
(2)
.
∎

I^{\infty}_{X}
.

Remark 5.5
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{N})
be a noncompact
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space. Assume there exists
v_{0}>0

\mathcal{H}^{N}(B_{1}(x))\geq v_{0}

. Let

for

I^{\infty}_{X}=I_{X^{\infty}}
. Indeed, the equality follows arguing similarly as in the proof of
Proposition

4.1
.

X

[

,
36
,
62
,
15
]
), then one clearly can identify

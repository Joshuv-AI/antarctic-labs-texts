1.
Introduction and main results

In this paper we are interested in the study of the critical behaviour of global in time solutions of the initial value problem

\begin{cases}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{d}u=|x|^{\alpha}|u|^{p}+\zeta(t){\mathbf{w}}(x)\ \quad\mbox{for }(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
N\geqslant 1
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
p>1
and
\zeta,{\mathbf{w}}
are given functions. The operator
(-\Delta)^{d}
for
d\geqslant 1
integer is the
d
-fold composition of the Laplacian with itself while for
d\in(0,1)
,
(-\Delta)^{d}
should be interpreted as the fractional Laplacian defined by

(-\Delta)^{d}\,u:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(|\xi|^{2d}\mathcal{F}(u)\right)
where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
are the Fourier transform and inverse Fourier transform, respectively
[
13
]
. We will consider the situation
\alpha\geqslant 0
in which case
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
0<-\alpha<\min(2d,N)
with
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
.

Reaction-diffusion equations with forcing term arises in many physical phenomena and biological species theories, such as the concentration of diffusion of some fluid, the density of some biological species, and heat conduction phenomena, see
[
8
,
6
,
21
,
17
,
18
]
and references therein.

Our main goal in this paper is to find the critical exponent which separates the existence and nonexistence of global solutions of (
1.1
).
It goes back to the work of Fujita
[
4
]
that there is an exponent
p_{F}
(critical Fujita exponent) for which the behavior of globally defined solutions of the reaction-diffusion model

u_{t}-\Delta u=|u|^{p}\ \hskip 2.84544pt\mbox{in}\hskip 2.84544pt\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty)

are classified according to whether one is in the subcritical case (
1<p<p_{F}
), critical (
p=p_{F}
) or supercritical (
p_{F}<p<\infty)
. A very well-known argument allowing one to predict the correct value of
p_{F}
, whenever it is finite, for several semilinear parabolic equations consists of the study of the (linear) stability of the zero solution, see for instance
[
12
]
. A more direct and simple approach pertaining to problems posed in the whole space and intrinsically connected to the scaling of the underlying equation has been proposed by the authors in
[
3
]
. More specifically, a scaling analysis on (
1.1
) without the forcing term, that is,

u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{d}u=|x|^{\alpha}|u|^{p}\ \hskip 2.84544pt\mbox{in}\hskip 2.84544pt\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\hskip 5.69046ptu(0)=u_{0}

(1.2)

shows that the only Lebesgue space which leaves invariant the norm of the appropriately rescaled initial data is
L^{p_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
p_{c}=\frac{N(p-1)}{2d+\alpha}
. Thus one may anticipate the critical Fujita exponent for (
1.2
) to be given by
1+\frac{2d+\alpha}{N}
which corresponds to the value of
p
for which
p_{c}=1
. The recent work
[
28
]
asserts in turn that this observation is actually correct. Indeed, the global well-posedness theory for (
1.2
) among other questions has been investigated in the latter reference – in particular, small data (in weak-Lebesgue space
L^{p_{c},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
global existence was obtained in
C_{b}([0,\infty);L^{p_{c},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
,
p_{c}=\frac{N(p-1)}{2d+\alpha}
provided we require
\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2d}<p<\infty
and
0<-\alpha<2d<N
while under the conditions
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(x)dx>0
and
1<p<1+\frac{2d+\alpha}{N}
, non-existence of weak solutions was established, see
[
28
]
. Note that the previous well-posedness statement does not cover the range
(1+\frac{2d+\alpha}{N},\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2d})
, a restriction which essentially emanates from the choice of the functional setting together with the hypotheses needed for the applicability of certain key estimates heavily utilized in the analysis. This gap, however, could be closed by using the two-norms technique as illustrated in
[
28
]
. Moving on, it is easy to see that the previous procedure fails to predict the critical exponent
p^{\star}
for (
1.1
) due to the presence of a forcing term. Nevertheless, as we shall see, granted the above knowledge on (
1.2
), the behavior of the inhomogeneous term plays a crucial role in determining
p^{\star}
. Throughout this manuscript, unless otherwise specified we will assume that
\zeta:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is a continuous function satisfying either

\zeta(t)\,\underset{t\to 0}{\sim}\,t^{\sigma},\quad\zeta(t)\underset{t\to\infty}{\sim}\,t^{m},\;\;\;(\sigma>-1,\,m\in\mathbb{R}),

(1.3)

or

\zeta(t)=t^{\sigma},\;\;\sigma>-1.

(1.4)

The study of the reaction-diffusion equation involving a source term has been considered by a number of authors. When the forcing term is purely space-dependent (i.e.
\zeta\equiv 1
) with
d=1
and
\alpha=0
, it is known that the Fujita critical exponent reads

p_{F}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\infty\hskip 5.69046pt\mbox{if}\hskip 5.69046ptN=1,2\\
\frac{N}{N-2}\hskip 5.69046pt\mbox{if}\hskip 5.69046ptN>2\end{array}\right.

in the sense that for
p
below
p_{F}
and
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\textbf{w}(x)dx>0
, Problem (
1.1
) does not admit any global solution – a conclusion which persists in the critical case
p=p_{F}
under the additional assumptions on
w
whilst for
p>p_{F}
global-in-time solutions exist provided
w
and
u_{0}
have strong decay property at infinity. These statements can be found in
[
2
]
where various extensions and results related to other geometries were obtained, see also
[
30
]
for the geometrical setting given by a manifold. When the source term contains a polynomial in time just as considered in (
1.4
) and for
d=1
,
\alpha=0
it was proved in
[
10
]
that the critical exponent is a function of
\sigma
which for
N\geqslant 3
has a discontinuity at zero,

p_{F}(\sigma)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{N-2\sigma}{N-2\sigma-2}\hskip 5.69046pt\mbox{if}\hskip 5.69046pt-1<\sigma<0\\
\infty\hskip 5.69046pt\mbox{if}\hskip 5.69046pt\sigma>0.\end{array}\right.

The situation
d=1
and
-2<\alpha<0
has been investigated in
[
15
]
. It was proved that for
N\geqslant 3
, solutions blow up in each of the following cases:
m\leqslant 0
,
p<\frac{N-2m+\alpha}{N-2m-2}
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\textbf{w}dx>0
and
p>1
. Furthermore, these results do not depend on the behavior of
\zeta
at small times. However, the study of global-in-time solutions has not been taken into account and we wish to cover that in this work.

Our results generalize earlier existing works and reveal new aspects of the solution. Prescribing initial data
u_{0}
and
w
small in Marcinkiewicz space gives rise to a global solution which lives in a small ball in the solution space and may as well be singular. As a byproduct, global existence holds in Lebesgue analogues of the previous functional setting whenever
u_{0}
and
w
are chosen small in the corresponding topologies. In particular, there are solutions which are initially singular and if additionally data are assumed to be radially symmetric (resp. radially monotone), then the solution enjoys the same properties. Moreover, when the principal operator in (
1.1
) is the nonlocal fractional Laplacian (
d\in(0,1)
) or the Laplace operator
\Delta
, (
d=1
) then the solution is positive along with
u_{0}
and
w
. In the process, we use an approach which in nature differs from those which have been employed in the aforementioned papers – for instance, the use of Kato’s two-norms technique which disallow unconditional uniqueness criterion and excludes a priori the existence of solutions which at time
t=0
are singular – our global wellposedness statement claims the converse. We essentially rely on smoothing effect type bounds in weak-Lebesgue spaces
[
28
]
for the singular polyharmonic heat semigroup and its integral kernel combined with Yamazaki’s type estimates
[
27
]
which have a wide range of application – they allow us among others to derive unconditional uniqueness of globally defined solutions for prescribed data in
L^{p_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Remarkably, we allow
u_{0}
and
\mathbf{w}
to be singular functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Concerning blow-up results, our Theorem
1.10
reinforces the fact that blow up whenever it occurs does not depend on the behavior of the forcing term at small times. To prove this theorem, we proceed via the test function method introduced by the authors in
[
19
]
.

We adopt the following notion of solution.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
u
is a global weak solution of (
1.1
) if it satisfies the conditions

u_{0}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad|x|^{\alpha}|u|^{p}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty))

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty)}u(-\partial_{t}\psi+(-\Delta)^{d}\psi)dxdt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(x)\psi(x,0)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty)}|x|^{\alpha}|u|^{p}\psi dxdt+

\displaystyle\qquad{}\hskip 85.35826pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty)}\zeta(t)\textbf{w}\psi\,dxdt

for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,\infty))
.

As is a standard practice, (
1.1
) is equivalent in an appropriate framework to the Duhamel formulation

u(x,t)=e^{-t(-\Delta)^{d}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)(-\Delta)^{d}}\left(|\cdot|^{\alpha}|u(s)|^{p}\right)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\,\zeta(s)\,e^{-(t-s)(-\Delta)^{d}}{\mathbf{w}}\,ds,

(1.5)

where
e^{-t(-\Delta)^{d}}
is the linear semi-group generated by
(-\Delta)^{d}
. A solution to the integral equation (
1.5
) is often called mild solution of (
1.1
). In our setting, one can see that a mild solution is a weak solution. See
[
24
,
25
,
22
,
14
]
for a discussion on the equivalence between the differential and integral formulation for the nonlinear heat equation.

In parallel to the questions discussed above, we also study local existence of solutions. Our first result in this direction deals with the case of initial data in the space of bounded continuous functions in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose we have (
1.3
),
{\mathbf{w}}\in BC(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
0<-\alpha<\min(2d,N)
and
N\geqslant 2
. Given
u_{0}\in BC(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exists a time
T:=T(u_{0})>0
and a unique mild solution
u\in C([0,T];BC(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
of Eq. (
1.1
) such that
u(0)=u_{0}.

Our next goal is to investigate the local well-posedness in Lebesgue spaces. The first result of this flavor was obtained in
[
1
]
for (
1.1
) with
d=1
and without a forcing term.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p_{c}=\frac{N(p-1)}{2d+\alpha}
. Assume
0<-\alpha<\min(2d,N)
and
q>\max\big\{p_{c},\frac{Np}{N+\alpha}\big\}
with
q>1
. Suppose that (
1.3
) holds,
u_{0},{\mathbf{w}}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
N\geqslant 2
.Then, there exists
T:=T(u_{0})>0
and a unique function
u\in C([0,T];L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
mild solution of Eq. (
1.1
). Let
T_{\max}
be the maximal time of existence of this solution. If
T_{\max}<\infty
, then one has

\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{\max}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\infty.

Remark 1.4
.

Observe that although the solution obtained in Theorem
1.2
has the regularity of the initial data, it is not true in general that this solution enjoys a better regularity, see
[
31
]
for this evidence in some special situation. A similar result can be stated for data in
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the set of continuous function vanishing at infinity in which case, the solution naturally belongs to
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In theorem
1.3
, the fact that one can compare
p_{c}
and
\frac{Np}{N+\alpha}
leads to the question whether or not local solutions can be constructed if the condition imposed on
q
fails to hold true. This involves three different cases namely
p_{c}>\frac{Np}{N+\alpha}
,
p_{c}=\frac{Np}{N+\alpha}
and
p_{c}<\frac{Np}{N+\alpha}
which are worth investigating. We do not pursue in this direction here. We point out, however, that local wellposedness in the critical case
p_{c}>\frac{Np}{N+\alpha}
appears as a byproduct of Theorem
1.6
below.

Next, motivated by the study of local-in-time solutions which are smooth
C^{\infty}
instantly, we consider
\alpha>0
and introduce the space
C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
which collects continuous functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfying the condition

\|\Lambda u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\infty
where
\Lambda(x)=(1+|x|)^{\frac{\alpha}{p-1}}
and

. We endow this space with the norm given by

The following result establishes local existence for initial data in

.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
\zeta
satisfying (
1.4
). Then, Eq. (
1.5
) is locally wellposed. More precisely, given
u_{0},{\mathbf{w}}\in C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exist
T\in(0,\infty)
and a unique solution
u
of (
1.5
) in
C([0,T];C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
. This solution in turn is classical and can be extended to a maximal interval

where

and
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{\max}}\|u(t)\|_{\Lambda}=\infty

2.
Background materials

In this section we collect auxiliary results which will later find their usefulness and applicability in the proofs of our main results. Recall
the space
BC(\mathbb{R}^{N})
which collects bounded and continuous functions in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denoting the space of continuous functions in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
vanishing at infinity.

We will need some basic properties of Lorentz spaces. For
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, denote by
f^{\ast}(\lambda)=\inf\{\tau>0:|\{|f|>\tau\}|\leqslant\lambda\}
its decreasing function and let
f^{\ast\ast}(\tau)=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}f^{\ast}(s)ds
. The Lorentz space
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, (weak-Lebesgue or Marcinkiewicz space in case
q=\infty
) collects all measurable functions
f
such that
\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}
is finite,

\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bigg(\dfrac{q}{p}\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}[t^{1/p}f^{\ast\ast}(t)]^{q}\dfrac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{1/q},&\mbox{if}\hskip 5.69046ptp\in(1,\infty),\hskip 5.69046ptq\in[1,\infty)\\
\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}t^{1/p}f^{\ast\ast}(t),&\mbox{if}\hskip 5.69046ptp\in(1,\infty],\hskip 5.69046ptq=\infty.\end{array}\right.

These spaces increase with the second exponent and contain Lebesgue spaces as subspace. In fact,
L^{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{p,q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{p,q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
1<p<\infty
,
1\leqslant q_{1}\leqslant p\leqslant q_{2}\leqslant\infty
with continuous injection. Moreover,
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
may alternatively be realized as the real interpolation space between the Lebesgue
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=[L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})]_{1-1/p,q},\hskip 5.69046ptp\in(1,\infty),q\in[1,\infty]

and more generally, for
1<p_{1}<p_{2}<\infty
,
\dfrac{1}{p}=\dfrac{1-\theta}{p_{1}}+\dfrac{\theta}{p_{2}}
,
\theta\in(0,1)
and
1\leqslant q,q_{1},q_{2}\leqslant\infty
we have the interpolation identity

3.
Proofs of the main results

Starting with the proofs of theorems pertaining to the local theory, recall that
\mathbf{S}_{d,\alpha}(t)
is a
C_{0}
semigroup on Lebesgue spaces and on
BC(\mathbb{R}^{N})
so that we can systematically appeal to the abstract result in
[
26
]
. For this end, we define for
0<-\alpha<N
the map

\mathscr{K}_{t}u=\mathbf{S}_{d,-\alpha}(t)|u|^{p}+\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t)\zeta(\cdot)\mathbf{w},\hskip 5.69046ptt>0.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Given
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we can find a constant
\eta:=\eta(p)>0
such that

\big||a|^{p}-|b|^{p}\big|\leqslant\eta|a-b|(|a|^{p-1}+|b|^{p-1}),\hskip 5.69046ptp>1.

(3.1)

By invoking Proposition
2.2
with
p_{2}=p_{1}=\infty
, it is easy to verify that
\mathscr{K}_{t}
is bounded on the space
BC(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and the following bound holds, namely
\|\mathbf{S}_{d,0}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant c\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Next, call
\overline{B}_{L}(0)\subset BC(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the ball with center at zero and radius
L>0
, we prove that
\mathscr{K}_{t}
is Lipschitz continuous on
\overline{B}_{L}(0)
for each
t>0
. Take
u,v\in\overline{B}_{L}(0)
, by (
3.1
) and making use of Proposition
2.2
once again we arrive at

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{K}_{t}u-\mathscr{K}_{t}v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant c_{L}(t)\|u-v\|_{L^{\infty}}

with the Lipschitz constant given by
c_{L}(t)=CL^{p-1}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2d}}\in L^{1}(0,\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon>0
and for each
L>0
since
-\alpha<2d
. Moreover, the kernel
E_{d}
has the property

E_{d}(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}E_{d}(x-y,t-s)E_{d}(y,s)dy

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
s,t>0
; which obviously implies
\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t)\phi=\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t-s)[\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(s)\phi]
,
0<s<t
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(s)\mathcal{K}_{t}u

\displaystyle=\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(s)\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t)|\cdot|^{\alpha}|u|^{p}+\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(s)\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t)\zeta(\cdot)\mathbf{w}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t+s)|\cdot|^{\alpha}|u|^{p}+\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t+s)\zeta(\cdot)\mathbf{w}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{S}_{d,-\alpha}(t+s)|u|^{p}+\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t+s)\zeta(\cdot)\mathbf{w}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}_{t+s}u.

On the other hand, we have
\mathcal{K}_{t}(0)=\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t)\zeta(\cdot)\mathbf{w}
, so that for some
\varepsilon>0
and by using (
1.3
), there holds

\displaystyle\big\|\|\mathscr{K}_{t}(0)\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|_{L^{1}(0,\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=\big\|\|\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t)\zeta(\cdot)\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|_{L^{1}(0,\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\zeta\|_{L^{1}(0,\varepsilon)}\leqslant C.

At this point, a simple application of Theorem 1 in
[
26
]
yields the desired conclusion.

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Here, the reasoning goes in the same spirit as in the above proof. Let
u_{0},\mathbf{w}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then granted (
1.3
), under the condition
q>\frac{Np}{2d+\alpha}
, we can apply Proposition
2.2
with
(p_{1},p_{2})=(q/p,q)
to obtain that
\mathscr{K}_{t}
is bounded on
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for each
t>0
and there holds
\|\mathbf{S}_{d,0}u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}\leqslant C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}
by Proposition
2.1
. We eventually want to verify that the following conditions are fulfilled:

(1)

\mathscr{K}_{t}:L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a locally Lipschitz map with constant
c_{L}(t)\in L^{1}(0,\varepsilon)
for each
L>0
and for some
\varepsilon>0
.

(2)

The map
t\mapsto\|\mathscr{K}_{t}(0)\|_{L^{q}}
belongs to
L^{1}(0,\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon>0.

(3)

\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(s)\mathscr{K}_{t}=\mathscr{K}_{t+s}
for
s,t>0.

The third property has already been established in the previous lines. As regards the second, for an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, we have that

\displaystyle\big\|\|\mathscr{K}_{t}(0)\|_{L^{q}}\big\|_{L^{1}(0,\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=\big\|\|\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t)\zeta(\cdot)\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{q}}\big\|_{L^{1}(0,\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{q}}\|\zeta\|_{L^{1}(0,\varepsilon)}\leqslant C.

Let us now show that the first condition is equally satisfied. Fix
L>0
and denote by
\overline{B}_{L}(0)
the closed ball in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
centered at the origin and with radius
L
. We compute

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{K}_{t}u-\mathscr{K}_{t}v\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{S}_{d,-\alpha}(t)(|u|^{p}-|v|^{p})\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{L}(t)\|u-v\|_{L^{q}}

with
c_{L}(t)=CL^{p-1}t^{-\frac{N(p-1)}{2qd}+\frac{\alpha}{2d}}
. Note that in order to get this bound, we use the pointwise estimate (
3.1
) and invoked Proposition
2.2
with
(p_{1},p_{2})=(\frac{q}{p},q)
. Since
q>\frac{N(p-1)}{2d+\alpha}=p_{c}
, it follows that
c_{L}(t)\in L^{1}(0,\varepsilon)
for all
L>0
and for all
\varepsilon>0
. We are now in position to apply Theorem 1 in
[
26
]
which yields the statement of Theorem
1.3
.

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

We turn to the proof of the local well-posedness result subject to data in
C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Recall
\Lambda(x)=(1+|x|)^{\frac{\alpha}{p-1}}
. In this case, we proceed in a slightly different fashion with the aim of reaching the hypotheses that will allow an application of a Banach fixed point theorem.

Let
0<T<1
be a time to be chosen later and
\delta>0
. Consider the space

X_{T,\Lambda}=\bigg\{u\in C([0,T];C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})):\|\Lambda u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leqslant\delta,t\in(0,T)\bigg\}

on which we put the metric
d(u,v)=\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\Lambda(u(t)-v(t))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
. We will prove in the sequel that the operator
\mathcal{Q}
defined as

\mathcal{Q}u=\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}_{d,-\alpha}(t-s)|u(s)|^{p}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t-s)\zeta(s)\mathbf{w}ds

(3.2)

possesses a fixed point in
C([0,T];C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
. We first show that
\mathcal{Q}
is a self-mapping from
X_{T,\Lambda}
onto itself. Using Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t)u_{0}|

\displaystyle=|e^{-t(-\Delta)^{d}}u_{0}|=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,E_{d}(x-y,t)u_{0}(y)\,dy\bigg|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|E_{d}(x-y,t)||u_{0}(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,t^{\frac{-N}{2d}}|g(t^{-\frac{1}{2d}}(x-y))||u_{0}(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leqslant\|u_{0}\|_{C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,t^{\frac{-N}{2d}}|g(t^{-\frac{1}{2d}}(x-y))|\Lambda^{-1}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leqslant\|u_{0}\|_{C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|g(y)|\left(1+|x-t^{1/2d}\,y|\right)^{-\frac{\alpha}{p-1}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leqslant C(\alpha,p,N)\|u_{0}\|_{C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,(1+|x|)^{-\frac{\alpha}{p-1}}.

Similarly, setting
Bu(t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)(-\Delta)^{d}}|\cdot|^{\alpha}|u(s)|^{p}\,ds
,
u\in X_{T,\Lambda}
, an application of Lemma
2.4
once again permits us to write

\displaystyle|B(t)u|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,(t-s)^{-\frac{N}{2d}}g\big((t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2d}}(x-y)\big)|y|^{\alpha}|u(s)|^{p}\,dyds\bigg|

\displaystyle\leqslant C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,(t-s)^{-\frac{N}{2d}}|g\big((t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2d}}(x-y)\big)||y|^{\alpha}|\Lambda(y)u(y,s)|^{p}\Lambda^{-p}(y)\,dyds

\displaystyle\leqslant C\delta^{p}\int_{0}^{t}\,(t-s)^{-\frac{N}{2d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|g\big((t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2d}}(x-y)\big)|y|^{\alpha}(1+|y|)^{-\frac{\alpha\,p}{p-1}}\,dyds

\displaystyle\leqslant C\delta^{p}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|g(z)|(1+|x-(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2d}}z|)^{-\frac{\alpha}{p-1}}\,dzds

\displaystyle\leqslant C\delta^{p}\int_{0}^{T}\,(1+|x|)^{-\frac{\alpha}{p-1}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leqslant C\delta^{p}(1+|x|)^{-\frac{\alpha}{p-1}}T.

Finally, setting
Du(t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\,e^{-(t-s)(-\Delta)^{d}}{\mathbf{w}}\zeta(s)\,ds
, we invoke Lemma
2.4
to arrive at

\displaystyle|D(t)u|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,(t-s)^{-\frac{N}{2d}}g\big((t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2d}}(x-y)\big)|{\mathbf{w}}(y)|\zeta(s)\,dyds\bigg|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,(t-s)^{-\frac{N}{2d}}\big|g\big((t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2d}}(x-y)\big)\big||\Lambda(y)|^{-1}\zeta(s)\,dyds

\displaystyle\leqslant\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|g(z)|\zeta(s)(1+|x-(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2d}}z|^{-\frac{\alpha}{p-1}}\,dzds

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\frac{T^{\sigma+1}}{\sigma+1}\Lambda^{-1}(x)

so that
\|\Lambda Du(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant C\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\dfrac{T^{\sigma+1}}{\sigma+1}
. Gluing together all the above estimates, we find that

\|\Lambda\mathcal{Q}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant C\big(\|u_{0}\|_{C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\delta^{p}T+\frac{T^{\sigma+1}}{\sigma+1}\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\big),\;\;0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

(3.3)

where
C
is a constant depending on
N,\alpha
and
p
. Let
\delta>0
such that
C\|u_{0}\|_{C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\delta
. Pick
T<1
with the property that
C\big(\|u_{0}\|_{C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\delta^{p}T+\frac{T^{\sigma+1}}{\sigma+1}\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\big)\leqslant\delta
to draw the conclusion that
\mathcal{Q}
maps
X_{T,\Lambda}
into itself. Next, let
u,v\in X_{T,\Lambda}
and let’s additionally impose the condition
C\delta^{p-1}T<1
where
C
is that constant appearing in (
3.3
). Then
\mathcal{Q}
is a contraction. To see this write

\displaystyle|\mathcal{Q}u(t)-\mathcal{Q}v(t)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}E_{d}(x-y,t-s)|y|^{\alpha}\big||u(s)|^{p}-|v(s)|^{p}\big|\,dyds\bigg|

\displaystyle\leqslant C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|E_{d}(x-y,t-s)||y|^{\alpha}|u-v|(|u|^{p-1}+|v|^{p-1})\,dyds

\displaystyle\leqslant C\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|E_{d}(x-y,t-s)||y|^{\alpha}|u-v|\Lambda(y)|\Lambda u|^{p-1}\Lambda^{-p}(y)dyds+

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|E_{d}(x-y,t-s)||y|^{\alpha}|u-v|\Lambda(y)|\Lambda v|^{p-1}\Lambda^{-p}(y)\,dyds\bigg)

\displaystyle\leqslant Cd(u,v)\bigg(\|\Lambda u(t)\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|g(z)||x-(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2d}}z|^{-1}dzds+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\|\Lambda v(t)\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|g(z)||x-(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2d}}z|^{-1}\,dzds\bigg).

This yields, utilizing Lemma
2.4
the bound

d\left(\mathcal{Q}u,\mathcal{Q}v\right)\leqslant C\delta^{p-1}Td(u,v).

Existence of a solution as claimed in Theorem
1.5
is now a simple consequence of the Banach fixed point theorem. The blow up alternative is verified using standard arguments. Since
C_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have that
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T];C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
and by parabolic regularity theory,
u
satisfies (
1.1
) in the classical sense. The proof of Theorem
1.5
is now complete.

Our next focus is on the proofs of global existence and non-existence results. Recall the definition of the beta function

\mathcal{B}(a,b)=\int_{0}^{1}s^{a-1}(1-s)^{b-1}ds=\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)},\quad a,b>0.

where

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

We will distinguish between two cases assuming first that
p>\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2d}
with
-\alpha<2d<N
. The second case

will be handle subsequently. Let
u_{0}\in L^{p_{c},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\mathbf{w}\in L^{\ell,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{c},\infty}}+\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\ell,\infty}}<\varepsilon_{0}
for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. We wish to show that the equation

u=w+\mathcal{F}(u)\hskip 5.69046pt\mbox{in}\hskip 5.69046pt\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty);\quad w=\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t-s)\zeta(s)\mathbf{w}ds

(3.4)

has a unique fixed point in a closed ball
\overline{B}_{2\varepsilon}(0)
of
C_{b}((0,\infty);L^{p_{c},\infty})
,
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\varepsilon_{0})
where for
t>0
,

\mathcal{F}(u)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}_{d,-\alpha}(t-s)|u(s)|^{p}ds.

Apply Lemma
2.5
with
k=p_{c}
(i.e

) in (
2.8
) and

|\phi(s)|^{p}=\begin{cases}|u(t-s)|^{p}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{if}\hskip 5.69046pts\in(0,t)\\
0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{if}\hskip 5.69046pts\geqslant t\end{cases}
to obtain the estimate

\|\mathcal{F}u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{p_{c},\infty})}\leqslant C\sup_{t>0}\|u(t)\|^{p}_{L^{p_{c},\infty}}.

(3.5)

Arguing in a similar fashion, we have that for
u
and
v
in
L^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{p_{c},\infty})
,

\|\mathcal{F}(u)-\mathcal{F}(v)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{p_{c},\infty})}\leqslant C\|u-v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{p_{c},\infty})}(\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{p_{c},\infty})}+\|v\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{p_{c},\infty})}).

(3.6)

Observe that
\ell=\frac{Np_{c}}{N+2(\sigma+1)dp_{c}}>1
in view of the condition imposed on

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{p_{c},\infty}}\leqslant C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{c},\infty}}+\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t-s)\zeta(s)\mathbf{w}ds\bigg\|_{L^{p_{c},\infty}}

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t-s)\zeta(s)\mathbf{w}ds\bigg\|_{L^{p_{c},\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in L^{p_{c}^{\prime},1}\\
\|\psi\|_{L^{p_{c}^{\prime},1}}=1\end{subarray}}\bigg|\langle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t-s)\zeta(s)\mathbf{w}ds,\psi\rangle\bigg|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in L^{p_{c}^{\prime},1}\\
\|\psi\|_{L^{p_{c}^{\prime},1}}=1\end{subarray}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t-s)\zeta(s)\mathbf{w}\psi(x)dxds\bigg|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in L^{p_{c}^{\prime},1}\\
\|\psi\|_{L^{p_{c}^{\prime},1}}=1\end{subarray}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t-s)\psi\|_{L^{\ell^{\prime},1}}\|\zeta(s)\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\ell,\infty}}ds\bigg)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in L^{p_{c}^{\prime},1}\\
\|\psi\|_{L^{p_{c}^{\prime},1}}=1\end{subarray}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{N}{2d}(\frac{1}{p_{c}^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{\ell^{\prime}})}\|\psi\|_{L^{p_{c}^{\prime},1}}\|\zeta(s)\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\ell,\infty}}ds\bigg)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in L^{p_{c}^{\prime},1}\\
\|\psi\|_{L^{p_{c}^{\prime},1}}=1\end{subarray}}\|\psi\|_{L^{p_{c}^{\prime},1}}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\ell,\infty}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{N}{2d}(\frac{1}{\ell}-\frac{1}{p_{c}})}\zeta(s)ds

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in L^{p_{c}^{\prime},1}\\
\|\psi\|_{L^{p_{c}^{\prime},1}}=1\end{subarray}}\|\psi\|_{L^{p_{c}^{\prime},1}}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\ell,\infty}}\int_{0}^{1}\tau^{\sigma}(1-\tau)^{-(\sigma+1)}d\tau

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\ell,\infty}}\mathcal{B}(\sigma+1,-\sigma)

where we have systematically used Fubini’s Theorem, smoothing effect in Lorentz space (
2.4
), the generalized Hölder’s inequality and in the estimate before the last we performed the change of variables
\tau=s/t
together with the fact that
\frac{1}{p_{c}^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{\ell^{\prime}}=\frac{2(\sigma+1)d}{N}
,
-1<\sigma<0
and
\ell^{\prime}
being the conjugate exponent of
\ell
. Hence, we have that

\|w\|_{L^{p_{c},\infty}}\leqslant C_{1}(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{c},\infty}}+\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\ell,\infty}})\leqslant C_{1}\varepsilon_{0}.

Put
\varepsilon=C_{1}\varepsilon_{0}
and
R=(2^{p}\,C)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
. Assuming that
\varepsilon<R
, we deduce from (

) and (

) has a unique fixed point in

which indeed is the solution of (
1.5
) we were looking for.
Now assume that
p_{F}\leqslant p\leqslant\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2d}
. We shall argue differently in this case since the strategy employed before will clearly fail to work well here. This being said, pick a number

such that the inequality below is satisfied,

\max\bigg\{\frac{\alpha p+2d}{Np(p-1)},\frac{1}{p_{c}}+\frac{2d\sigma}{N}\bigg\}<\frac{1}{r}<\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{p_{c}},\frac{N+\alpha}{Np}\bigg\},\quad r>p.

(3.7)

This choice is possible. In fact, in view of the condition

, one can easily check that all inequalities in (

because

Moving on, observe that from (

and if we put

then

Introduce the function space
\mathbf{X}
defined by

\mathbf{X}=\bigg\{u\in C_{b}\big((0,\infty);L^{p_{c},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big):t^{\mu}u\in C_{b}\big((0,\infty);L^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\bigg\}

which carries out the structure of a complete metric space when equipped with the distance

\mathbf{X}
which is unique in

a closed ball centered at the origin and with radius

\|\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{r,\infty}}\leqslant Ct^{-\frac{N}{2d}\big(\frac{1}{p_{c}}-\frac{1}{r}\big)}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{c},\infty}}\leqslant Ct^{-\mu}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{c},\infty}}.

Since

that

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}_{d,-\alpha}(t-s)|u(s)|^{p}ds\bigg\|_{L^{r,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{N}{2d}\big(\frac{p}{r}-\frac{1}{r}\big)+\frac{\alpha}{2d}}\||u|^{p}\|_{L^{r/p,\infty}}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant C\int_{0}^{t}\,(t-s)^{-\frac{N}{2d}\big(\frac{p}{r}-\frac{1}{r}\big)+\frac{\alpha}{2d}}s^{-p\mu}(s^{\mu}\|u\|_{L^{r,\infty}})^{p}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant C(\sup_{t>0}t^{\mu}\|u\|_{L^{r,\infty}})^{p}\int_{0}^{t}\,(t-s)^{-\frac{N(p-1)}{2rd}+\frac{\alpha}{2d}}s^{-p\mu}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant Ct^{-\frac{N(p-1)}{2rd}+\frac{\alpha}{2d}-p\mu+1}\|u\|^{p}_{\mathbf{X}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{-\frac{N(p-1)}{2rd}+\frac{\alpha}{2d}}s^{-p\mu}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant Ct^{-\mu}\|u\|^{p}_{\mathbf{X}}\mathcal{B}\bigg(1-p\mu,1-\frac{N(p-1)}{2rd}+\frac{\alpha}{2d}\bigg)

To estimate the last term, we utilize (

) and proceed as follows

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t-s)\mathbf{w}\zeta(s)\bigg\|_{L^{r,\infty}}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{N}{2d}\big(\frac{1}{\ell}-\frac{1}{r}\big)}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\ell,\infty}}\zeta(s)ds

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\ell,\infty}}t^{-\frac{N}{2d}\big(\frac{1}{\ell}-\frac{1}{r}\big)+\sigma+1}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{-\frac{N}{2d}\big(\frac{1}{\ell}-\frac{1}{r}\big)}s^{\sigma}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\ell,\infty}}t^{-\frac{N}{2d}\big(\frac{1}{\ell}-\frac{1}{r}\big)+\sigma+1}\mathcal{B}\bigg(\sigma+1,1-\frac{N}{2d}\big(\frac{1}{\ell}-\frac{1}{r}\big)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\ell,\infty}}t^{-\mu}.

Summarizing, if
\mathcal{Q}u=w+\mathcal{F}(u),\hskip 2.84544ptw=\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}_{d,0}(t-s)\zeta(s)\mathbf{w}
, then

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}t^{\mu}\|\mathcal{Q}u(t)\|_{L^{r,\infty}}\leqslant C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{c},\infty}}+K^{p}+\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\ell,\infty}}\right)\leqslant C\varepsilon_{0}.

Upon taking
\varepsilon_{0}
and

C(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{c}}}+\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\ell,\infty}})\leqslant K
and thus
\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}t^{\mu}\|\mathcal{Q}(t)\|_{L^{r,\infty}}\leqslant K
so that

\overline{B}_{K}(0)
into itself. Arguing as above, we can show without ambiguity that
\mathcal{Q}
is a contraction map on
\overline{B}_{K}(0)
for appropriately chosen

(small). Applying the Banach fixed point theorem, we thus obtain the existence of a solution

to (

) in
\mathbf{X}
which is unique in
\overline{B}_{K}(0)

Also,

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

) has a global weak solution in the sense of Definition

. Consider the cut-off functions
\psi_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{0}([0,\infty))

\psi_{1}(s)=\begin{cases}1\hskip 3.41418pt\text{if}\hskip 3.41418pt1/2\leqslant s\leqslant 3/4\\
0\hskip 3.41418pt\text{if}\hskip 3.41418pts\in[0,1/4]\cup[4/5,\infty)\end{cases},\quad\psi_{2}(s)=\begin{cases}1\hskip 3.41418pt\text{if}\hskip 3.41418pts\in[0,1]\\
0\hskip 3.41418pt\text{if}\hskip 3.41418pts\geqslant 2.\end{cases}

Next, pick
T>0

\psi_{T}(x,t)=\psi_{1}\bigg(\frac{t}{T}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\psi_{2}\bigg(\frac{|x|^{2d}}{T}\bigg)^{\frac{2dp}{p-1}}.

Since
u

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{T}u\partial_{t}\psi_{T}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{T}(x,0)u_{0}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt\qquad{}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{T}\textbf{w}(x)\zeta(t)\psi_{T}.

Remark that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{T}(x,0)u_{0}(x)dx=0

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{T}|x|^{\alpha}|u|^{p}\psi_{T}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{T}\textbf{w}(x)\zeta(t)\psi_{T}

\displaystyle\hskip 65.44142pt\qquad{}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]}|u||\partial_{t}\psi_{T}|.

|(-\Delta)^{d}\psi_{T}|\leqslant CT^{-1}\psi_{2}\bigg(\frac{|x|^{2d}}{T}\bigg)^{\frac{2d}{p-1}}\psi_{1}\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}.

\displaystyle\leqslant cT^{-\frac{1}{p-1}-\frac{\alpha}{2d(p-1)}+\frac{N}{2d}}

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T]}|u|^{p}|x|^{\alpha}\psi_{T}dxdt+cT^{-\frac{1}{p-1}-\frac{\alpha}{2d(p-1)}+\frac{N}{2d}}.

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]}|u|^{p}|x|^{\alpha}\psi_{T}+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]}\bigg|\partial_{t}\psi_{1}\bigg(\frac{t}{T}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\bigg|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\psi_{1}(t/T)^{-\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}}\psi_{2}^{\frac{2dp}{p-1}}(|x|^{2d}/T)|x|^{-\frac{\alpha}{p-1}}

(3.11)

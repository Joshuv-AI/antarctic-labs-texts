Twistor Constructions for Higher-spin Extensions of (Self-dual) Yang-Mills


Abstract
We present the inverse Penrose transform (the map from spacetime to twistor space) for self-dual Yang-Mills (SDYM) and its higher-spin extensions on a flat background. The twistor action for the higher-spin extension of SDYM (HS-SDYM) is of ℬ ​ ℱ \mathcal{B}\mathcal{F} -type. By considering a deformation away from the self-dual sector of HS-SDYM, we discover a new action that describes a higher-spin extension of Yang-Mills theory (HS-YM). The twistor action for HS-YM is a straightforward generalization of the Yang-Mills one.

Introduction

It is well known that many interesting integrable systems, such as self-dual Yang-Mills (SDYM)
[
1
]
or self-dual gravity (SDGRA)
[
2
]

One of the advantages of formulating physics in terms of twistor variables is that we can sometimes have a more natural and geometrical interpretation than studying the same physics on spacetime. For instance, supersymmetric SDYM and SDGRA can be formulated as Chern-Simons theories on super twistor space
[
3
,
4
,
5

[
2
,
6
]
. Conventional theories such as (super) Yang-Mills theory can also be constructed from twistor space
[
7
,
4
]
. In addition, the long-sought twistor action for general relativity formulated in terms of Plebanski action has been discovered recently
[
8
]
.

The connection between twistor space and spacetime is known as twistor correspondence. Let us demonstrate it by the following double fibration:

In this paper, we consider the inverse map, i.e. the map that lifts a system from spacetime to twistor space (through

One of the advantages of this approach is that we can start with a covariant action (if available) that has all the desired properties of a field theory in spacetime. Hence, the outcome from the opposite direction, i.e. from twistor space to spacetime, is guaranteed to be covariant. To study the inverse map, we first revisit the cases of self-dual Yang-Mills

A common feature of these theories is that they have only cubic interactions in flat spacetime with two fields of the same-sign helicities, and another with opposite helicity plays the role of a Lagrangian multiplier.

The Penrose transform.

To understanding the inverse Penrose transform, it is helpful to review the concept of the Penrose transform first. One of the goals of twistor theory is to establish the correspondence between field equations in
\mathbb{M}^{4}
and holomorphic structures on
\mathbb{PT}
. For massless fields of any spin in 4d Minkowski, we can represent them as cohomology classes on twistor space via the Penrose transform
[
40
,
41
,
36
]
. In the case of the spin-1 field, the field strength reads

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}\mapsto F_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}\beta\beta^{\prime}}=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}F_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}+\epsilon_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}F_{\alpha\beta}

(7)

where
F_{\alpha\beta}
and
F_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}
are referred to as the self-dual and anti-self-dual parts of the field strength. The Maxwell equations are then reduced to a single (conformally invariant) equation

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}F^{\alpha\beta}=0\,

(8)

under this decomposition of the field strength. The above equation (
8

A^{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}

[
40
,
41
,
36
]

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}_{\ \alpha^{\prime}}A^{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}=0\,.

(9)

The above equation is invariant under the gauge transformation
\delta A^{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}=\partial^{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\xi
. Here, we use the convention that indices that are symmetrized are denoted by the same Greek letters, e.g. the term
\lambda_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}
denotes
\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda_{\alpha_{1}}\xi_{\alpha_{2}}+\lambda_{\alpha_{2}}\xi_{\alpha_{1}}\big)

T_{\alpha(m)}=T_{\alpha_{1}...\alpha_{m}}

\displaystyle S=\int d^{4}x\,\psi_{\alpha\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}_{\ \alpha^{\prime}}A^{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\,,

(10)

F_{\alpha\alpha}
to
\psi_{\alpha\alpha}
to differ from the notation of the usual field strength. Now, let us turn our focus to twistor space and consider the following action

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\int_{\mathbb{PT}}D^{3}Z\,\mathcal{B}\wedge\bar{\partial}\mathcal{A}\,,

\displaystyle D^{3}Z=\epsilon_{ACBC}Z^{A}dZ^{B}\wedge dZ^{C}\wedge dZ^{D}=\langle\lambda d\lambda\rangle\wedge[d\mu\wedge d\mu]\,.

Here,

-form connection and

\displaystyle S=\int d^{4}x\,\text{Tr}\Big[\psi_{\alpha\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}_{\ \alpha^{\prime}}A^{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\Big]+\int d^{4}x\,\text{Tr}\Big(\psi_{\alpha\alpha}[A^{\alpha,}_{\ \ \alpha^{\prime}},A^{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}]\Big)\,.

\displaystyle\delta A^{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}=\partial^{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\xi+[A^{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}},\xi]\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\delta\psi_{\alpha\alpha}=[\psi_{\alpha\alpha},\xi]\,.

Twistor actions from the inverse Penrose transform

After reviewing all the required elements to construct the map between the twistor space and spacetime, we can now present the inverse Penrose transform. The input is a covariant action on spacetime that can be written as self-dual part plus non-self-dual part with (

2.2
Twistor construction for Yang-Mills

To get the full Yang-Mills theory, one can add a deformation to the SDYM action as a perturbation around the self-dual sector

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{2g}\int d^{4}x\,\text{Tr}[F_{\alpha\alpha}F^{\alpha\alpha}]\,.

The above action depends only on the self-dual field strength of the gauge field and is equivalent to the Yang-Mills action. Next, recall that the pullback of
\psi

(0,1)

such that

to maintain locality in spacetime.

7

7

This is a special feature of the twistor space. Since it plays the role of an auxiliary space, it is harmless to have non-local interactions on twistor space. One just needs to make sure that the interactions in spacetime are local after the Penrose transform.
In terms of components, these coordinates read

\displaystyle Z_{1}=(\lambda_{1\alpha},\mu_{1}^{\alpha^{\prime}}=x^{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\lambda_{1\alpha})\,,\hskip 20.00003ptZ_{2}=(\lambda_{2\alpha},\mu_{2}^{\alpha^{\prime}}=x^{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\lambda_{2\alpha})\,.

Let us consider a pair of twistors

The twistor dual of

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{i}=3\langle\hat{\lambda}_{i}d\hat{\lambda}_{i}\rangle\frac{\psi_{\alpha\alpha}\hat{\lambda}_{i}^{\alpha}\hat{\lambda}_{i}^{\alpha}}{\langle\hat{\lambda}_{i}\lambda_{i}\rangle^{4}}\,.

(48)

Observe that (

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\int D^{3}Z\,\text{Tr}[\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}]-\frac{g}{2}\int D^{3}Z_{1}\wedge D^{3}Z_{2}\,\text{Tr}[\mathcal{B}_{1}\mathcal{B}_{2}]\,.

(49)

Up to this point, we have restricted ourselves in the Woodhouse gauge because it is convenient to perform the (inverse) Penrose transform. However, one can switch from Woodhouse gauge to axial gauge

3
Twistor actions of free scalar field and free higher-spin fields

In this section, we first show how one can construct a twistor action for the scalar field. Then, we show how to generalize the twistor action of a free spin-1 field to higher-spin fields by considering
generalized connections
and
generalized Lagrangian multiplier twistor fields
on twistor space.

3.1
The scalar field

Having the scalar field in the spectrum is essential for generic higher spin theories to be consistent in spacetime. For that reason, let us start this subsection by constructing the twistor action of a free scalar field. Recall that the free action for a scalar field is
-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}
. Let us introduce an auxiliary field
\vartheta
, and write the free action for a scalar field as

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\Big(\phi\partial^{\alpha}_{\ \alpha^{\prime}}\vartheta_{\alpha}^{\ \alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{\vartheta^{\alpha}_{\ \alpha^{\prime}}\vartheta_{\alpha}^{\ \alpha^{\prime}}}{2}\Big)\,.

(50)

It is easy to check that this action is equivalent to the free scalar action after integrating out the auxiliary field
\vartheta
. Observes that (
50
) is a one-derivative action, and hence feasible to be lifted to twistor space by the inverse Penrose transform. The dual twistor action of (
50
) reads

\displaystyle-\int D^{3}Z\wedge D^{3}\bar{Z}\Big(\phi\bar{\partial}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\tilde{\vartheta}^{\alpha^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\vartheta}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\lambda_{\alpha}\vartheta^{\alpha}_{\ \alpha^{\prime}}\Big)\,,

(51)

where
\tilde{\vartheta}^{\alpha^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\langle\hat{\lambda}\lambda\rangle}\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}\vartheta_{\alpha}^{\ \alpha^{\prime}}
. Using the basis (
18
), we can write the above as

\displaystyle-\int D^{3}Z\wedge D^{3}\bar{Z}\Big(\phi\bar{\partial}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\tilde{\vartheta}^{\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\vartheta}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\bar{\partial}_{0}\tilde{\vartheta}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\Big)\,.

(52)

The action (
52
) can be further reduced to

\displaystyle\int D^{3}Z\,\varphi\bar{\partial}\varphi\,,

(53)

where

\displaystyle\varphi=\phi\bar{e}^{0}+\tilde{\vartheta}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\,.

(54)

Therefore,
\varphi\in H^{0,1}(\mathbb{PT},\mathcal{O}(-2))
, which follows from (
14
). We note that the above twistor construction of the scalar field does not require supersymmetry. A nice feature of (
53
) is that it contains only one derivative instead of two like in spacetime.

3.2
Free higher spin fields

Moving to free higher spin fields, there is a simple action that satisfies both (
14
) and (
17
), and is a straightforward generalization of (
10
). It reads

\displaystyle S=\int d^{4}x\,\psi_{\alpha(2s)}\partial^{\alpha}_{\ \alpha^{\prime}}\Phi^{\alpha(2s-1),\alpha^{\prime}}\,.

(55)

The above action is invariant under
\delta\Phi^{\alpha(2s-1),\alpha^{\prime}}=\partial^{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\xi^{\alpha(2s-2)}
. One can go back to the usual second order action by considering

\displaystyle S=\int d^{4}x\,\psi_{\alpha(2s)}\partial^{\alpha}_{\ \alpha^{\prime}}\Phi^{\alpha(2s-1),\alpha^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\,\psi_{\alpha(2s)}\psi^{\alpha(2s)}\sim\int d^{4}x\,\Big(\partial^{\alpha}_{\ \alpha^{\prime}}\Phi^{\alpha(2s-1),\alpha^{\prime}}\Big)^{2}\,.

(56)

The EOM resulting from the above action is gauge invariant and describes both helicities. Note that (
55
) is valid for any spin, including half-integer ones. It is the advantage of using spinorial indices instead of Lorentz ones. The above formulation is in contrast to the usual expression of free higher spin fields in terms of Fronsdal fields which is quadratic in derivatives for free bosonic fields and linear in derivatives for free fermionic fields. We will employ the first order action (
55
) because it is easier to work with when we consider interactions between higher-spin fields.

In addition, as discussed in
[
9
]
, fields that are written in terms of pure unprimed indices or have only one primed indices are said to live in
maximally unbalanced representations
of the Lorentz group
S(m,n)
in 4d. It turns out that this description of higher-spin fields is more useful than the usual formulation using Fronsdal fields.
8
8

8

One of the main motivations to use the above expression is that higher-spin theories written in terms of Fronsdal fields contain non-local vertices starting from the quartic
[
48
]
. This results in a severe No-Go obstruction for the existence of higher-spin theories with interactions, see e.g.
[
12
,
49
,
50
,
51
,
52
,
48
,
53
]
.
Indeed, one can write down Lorentz covariant actions that are invariant off-shell for HS-SDYM, self-dual gravity, and its higher spin extensions
[
10
,
9
]
using the above representation. Moreover, higher spin fields described by maximally unbalanced representations can live on not only maximally symmetric backgrounds such as (A)dS but also on more general backgrounds that are self-dual. Here, by self-dual backgrounds, we mean all backgrounds whose half of the Weyl tensor vanishes. The twistor action for a free spin-
s
field reads

\displaystyle\int D^{3}Z\,\mathcal{B}\bar{\partial}\omega\,,

(57)

where

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}+\omega_{0}\bar{e}^{0}=\underbrace{\lambda^{\alpha}\cdots\lambda^{\alpha}}_{2s-1\ \text{times}}\Phi_{\alpha(2s-1),\alpha^{\prime}}\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}+\omega_{0}^{s}\bar{e}^{0}\,,

(58a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}_{0}\bar{e}^{0}+\mathcal{B}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}=(2s+1)\underbrace{\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}\cdots\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}}_{2s\ \text{times}}\psi_{\alpha(2s)}\frac{\langle\hat{\lambda}d\hat{\lambda}\rangle}{\langle\hat{\lambda}\lambda\rangle^{2s+2}}+\mathcal{B}_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{s}\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\,.

(58b)

We will refer
\omega
as
generalized connection
and
\mathcal{B}
as
generalized Lagrangian multiplier twistor field
. The free twistor action for a free spin-
s
field (
57
) is invariant under

\displaystyle\delta\omega=\bar{\partial}\xi\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\delta\mathcal{B}=\bar{\partial}\chi\,,

(59)

for smooth sections
\xi\in\mathcal{O}(2s-2)
, and
\chi\in\mathcal{O}(-2s-2)
. We once again note that to go to spacetime action (
55
) from (
57
), it is most convenient to use Woodhouse gauge.

4
Twistor constructions for HS-SDYM and HS-YM

In this section, we construct the twistor action of HS-SDYM
[
9
]
via the inverse Penrose transform. By adding a small perturbation to HS-SDYM, we obtain a higher-spin extension action for Yang-Mills which we denote HS-YM for short. The dual twistor action of HS-SDYM has a similar form to
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}
-action. Therefore, from the twistor point of view, HS-SDYM should be integrable and quasi-topological in spacetime.

4.1
Twistor action of HS-SDYM

The action for HS-SDYM
[
9
]
(or one-derivative chiral higher-spin theory) reads

\displaystyle S=\sum_{s}\int\text{Tr}[\psi_{\alpha(2s)}\partial^{\alpha}_{\ \alpha^{\prime}}\Phi^{\alpha(2s-1),\alpha^{\prime}}]+\sum_{m+n=2s}\text{Tr}\Big(\psi_{\alpha(m+n)}[\Phi^{\alpha(m),}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\hskip 8.19447pt\alpha^{\prime}},\Phi^{\alpha(n),\alpha^{\prime}}]\Big)\,.

(60)

This action is invariant under the higher-spin extension of Yang-Mills gauge transformation

\displaystyle\delta\Phi^{\alpha(2s-1),\alpha^{\prime}}=\partial^{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\xi^{\alpha(2s-2)}+[\Phi,\xi]^{\alpha(2s-1),\alpha^{\prime}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\delta\psi_{\alpha(2s)}=[\psi,\xi]_{\alpha(2s)}\,.

(61)

The light-cone action for HS-SDYM is closely related to the Chalmers-Siegel action

\displaystyle S=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s\geq 1}\int d^{4}\boldsymbol{p}\,\text{Tr}[\Phi_{-s}\Phi_{+s}]\boldsymbol{p}^{2}+\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}}\int d^{4}\boldsymbol{p}\,a_{s_{1},s_{2}}\overline{\mathbb{P}}\,\text{Tr}\big[\Phi_{-(s_{1}+s_{2}-1)}\Phi_{+s_{1}}\Phi_{+s_{2}}\big]\,.

(62)

Twistor action.

For the case of HS-SDYM, higher-spin symmetry, i.e. the interactions between higher-spin fields, is encoded by the sum over the unprimed indices in (
60
). This information can be realized by considering the following twistor fields

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle\equiv\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}+\bar{e}^{0}\omega_{0}=\sum_{s}\big(\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{s}\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}+\omega_{0}^{s}\bar{e}^{0}\big)=\sum_{s}\underbrace{\lambda^{\alpha}\cdots\lambda^{\alpha}}_{2s-1\ \text{times}}\Phi_{\alpha(2s-1),\alpha^{\prime}}\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}+\sum_{s}\omega_{0}^{s}\bar{e}^{0}\,,

(63)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle\equiv\bar{e}^{0}\mathcal{B}_{0}+\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\mathcal{B}_{\alpha^{\prime}}=\sum_{s}\big(\mathcal{B}_{0}^{s}\bar{e}^{0}+\mathcal{B}_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{s}\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\big)=\sum_{s}(2s+1)\,\underbrace{\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}\cdots\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}}_{2s\ \text{times}}\psi_{\alpha(2s)}\frac{\langle\hat{\lambda}d\hat{\lambda}\rangle}{\langle\hat{\lambda}\lambda\rangle^{2s+2}}+\sum_{s}\mathcal{B}_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{s}\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\,.

(64)

The dual twistor action of (
60
) is therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\int D^{3}Z\,\text{Tr}[\mathcal{B}(\bar{\partial}\omega+\omega\wedge\omega)]\,.

(65)

One can easily check that the action (
65
) reduces to (
60
) after the Penrose transform in the Woodhouse gauge. On twistor space, when interactions between higher-spin fields are taken into account, the generalized connection (
63
) becomes cohomology class of

\displaystyle\omega\in\bigoplus_{s}H^{0,1}(\mathbb{PT},\mathcal{O}(2s-2))=H^{0,1}(\mathbb{PT},\bigoplus_{s}\mathcal{O}(2s-2))\,.

(66)

Similarly, the generalized Lagrangian multiplier field
\mathcal{B}
are now cohomology class of

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\in\bigoplus_{s}H^{0,1}(\mathbb{PT},\mathcal{O}(-2s-2))=H^{0,1}(\mathbb{PT},\bigoplus_{s}\mathcal{O}(-2s-2))\,.

(67)

Uniqueness.

Now, let us prove that the above action is unique. Similar to the case of the twistor action for SDYM, consider the following deformation of the complex structure
\bar{\partial}
on twistor space:

\displaystyle\bar{D}=\bar{\partial}+\omega\,.

(68)

where
\omega=\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}+\bar{e}^{0}\omega_{0}=\sum_{s}\big(\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{s}\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}+\omega_{0}^{s}\bar{e}^{0}\big)
on
\mathbb{PT}\cong\mathbb{PS}
. Using the basis (
18
) and (
19
), one can decompose
\bar{D}
into

\displaystyle\bar{D}\equiv\bar{e}^{0}\bar{D}_{0}+\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\bar{D}_{\alpha^{\prime}}=\bar{e}^{0}\big(\bar{\partial}_{0}+\omega_{0}\big)+\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\big(\bar{\partial}_{\alpha^{\prime}}+\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}\big)\,.

(69)

The integrability condition
\mathcal{F}=\bar{D}^{2}=0
reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\bar{D}\omega=\bar{\partial}\omega+\omega\wedge\omega=\Big(\bar{\partial}_{0}\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}-\bar{D}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\omega_{0}\Big)\bar{e}^{0}\wedge\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}+\bar{D}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\omega_{\beta^{\prime}}\,\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\wedge\bar{e}^{\beta^{\prime}}=0\,,

(70)

where
\bar{D}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\bullet=\bar{\partial}_{\alpha^{\prime}}+[\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}},\bullet]
. Note that
\bar{D}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\omega_{0}\,\bar{e}^{0}\wedge\bar{e}^{\alpha^{\prime}}=\bar{D}(\omega_{0}\bar{e}^{0})
and therefore could be removed by the
generalized
gauge transformation
\omega\rightarrow\omega+\bar{D}\xi
for some smooth sections
\xi
(see below). Therefore, we can always restrict ourselves to the Woodhouse gauge, where we set
\omega_{0}
to zero, starting from (
70
). In terms of components, the integrability condition (
70
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\bar{\partial}_{0}\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{s}\,,

(71a)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\bar{\partial}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\omega_{\beta^{\prime}}^{s}+\sum_{m+n=s}[\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{m},\omega_{\beta^{\prime}}^{n}]\,,

(71b)

The first equation in (
71
) tells us that
\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{s}
should be holomorphic in
\lambda
. In addition, treating (
17
) as the constraint on positive-helicity fields, then

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{s}=\underbrace{\lambda^{\alpha}\cdots\lambda^{\alpha}}_{2s-1\ \text{times}}\Phi_{\alpha(2s-1),\alpha^{\prime}}\,,

(72)

as expected. Therefore, we interpret that
\omega\in H^{0,1}(\mathbb{PT},\bigoplus_{s}\mathcal{O}(2s-2))
. From this, the second equation in (
71

\begin{split}0&=\epsilon_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}\sum_{s}\underbrace{\lambda^{\alpha}\cdots\lambda^{\alpha}}_{2s\ \text{times}}\Big(\partial_{\alpha\gamma^{\prime}}\Phi_{\alpha(2s-1),}^{\hskip 16.38895pt\hskip 8.19447pt\gamma^{\prime}}+\sum_{m+n=2s}[\Phi_{\alpha(m),\gamma^{\prime}},\Phi_{\alpha(n),}^{\hskip 8.19447pt\ \ \gamma^{\prime}}]\Big)\,\\
&=\epsilon_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}\sum_{s}\underbrace{\lambda^{\alpha}\cdots\lambda^{\alpha}}_{2s\ \text{times}}F_{\alpha(2s)}\,.\end{split}

(73)

The above equation can only be satisfied if
F_{\alpha(2s)}=0
. Notice, that
F_{\alpha(2s)}
is the same with the equation of motion for
\Phi
that can be derived from the spacetime action (
60
). Hence, we have shown that the generalized covariant almost complex structure
\bar{D}
defined in (
68

\mathcal{F}=0

The action of HS-SDYM has a form similar to the

5
Discussion

In this paper, we presented the inverse Penrose transform on flat spacetime for various known examples and some new examples of HS-(SD)YM. The most important takeaway from this paper is that the inverse Penrose transform can help us gain insight into the twistor construction of theories in spacetime. Due to the twistor correspondence, most (if not all) properties of the twistor actions will descend to spacetime via the Penrose transform. We have shown that all self-dual theories considered in this paper are dual to (generalized)
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}
theory on twistor space. Therefore, they are integrable from the twistor perspective.

The results of the inverse mapping can be summarized as follows. Consider a spacetime action of the one-derivative type in the main text that lives on flat spacetime. Then, the inverse map exists if we can decompose the spacetime action into a self-dual sector plus a sector that contains non-positive-helicity fields. It would be interesting to test this idea for other theories. One of the purposes of studying this inverse mapping is to guess (or construct) the mechanism on twistor space that can generate higher derivative interactions in spacetime.

As a remark, in this paper, we write down fields in the maximally unbalanced representations of the Lorentz group. This approach turns out to be more natural, compared to the formulations that employ Lorentz indices, towards the goal of classifying all possible vertices
[
54
,
55
]
and writing down a consistent off-shell action with higher-spin interactions, see for examples
[
15
,
9
]
. In addition, theories formulated in the maximally unbalanced representations are closely related to what is known as the spinor-helicity formalism that is used extensively in computing scattering amplitudes due to its efficiency. Therefore, having a covariant action written in terms of spinorial indices to start with maybe beneficial since one can employ amplitudes techniques to investigate the theory. It is indeed the case for HS-SDYM and HS-SDGRA
[
9
]
. Since we can write down Lorentz covariant descriptions for these two examples, some of the puzzles in higher spin have been resolved.

It would be compelling to construct (or at least guess) the twistor action for the SDGRA and its higher-spin extension
[
9
]
using the inverse Penrose transform. Originally, the SDGRA action in
[
10
]
was derived from what is called the
b\tau
-action on twistor space
[
5
]
:

\displaystyle S[b,\tau]=\int_{\mathbb{PS}}b\wedge\tau\wedge d\tau\wedge d\tau\,.

(79)

Even though the SDGRA in flat space contains only cubic interactions, its twistor origin is quartic in the fields. Hence, it is hard to immediately predict whether SDGRA is integrable from the twistor perspective. The higher-spin extension of SDGRA also admits a very similar form of the SDGRA action in spacetime. Therefore, with the help of the inverse map, we should be able to obtain the dual twistor actions that contain only cubic interactions for these two theories. It would naturally give a geometrical explanation of why SDGRA and its higher-spin extension are integrable.

The full Yang-Mills is obtained by perturbing around its self-dual sector. The strategy of adding extra terms to the self-dual sector is used in gravity as well. Indeed, by adding an infinite number of terms to the Lagrangian of SDGRA, one recovers GR
[
10
]
. We applied this technique to obtain HS-YM from HS-SDYM. It would be interesting to see whether we can discover a large class of parity-invariant higher spin theories with propagating d.o.f in this way. It is then compelling to check whether parity-invariant higher spin theories obtained by deforming the self-dual ones make sense by performing the analysis in the light-cone gauge similar to
[
15
]
.

There are several proposals for twistor deformations that result in higher-derivative interactions, e.g. deformation of the connection
[
34
,
35
]
, deformation using the Poisson bracket or the star product
[
5
]
, etc. We hope that with the help of twistor theory, one can gain enough intuition to finally write down a covariant action for the chiral HSGRA. Moreover, it would be interesting to find a general approach for deformations on twistor space such that we can have a covariant action in spacetime with higher-derivative interactions.

Crash course on Twistor Geometry

More on twistor space.

When twistor geometry was introduced in 1967 by Penrose
[
56
]
, it provided an alternative way to work with physical processes in an auxiliary space called the twistor space. Consider a 3-dimensional complex projective space, denoted
\mathbb{CP}^{3}
, with homogeneous coordinates

\displaystyle Z^{A}=(Z^{1},Z^{2},Z^{3},Z^{4})\,,\hskip 10.00002ptZ^{i}\neq 0\,,\hskip 10.00002ptt\,Z^{A}\sim Z^{A}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\,.

(85)

The projective twistor space
\mathbb{PT}
is defined to be an open subset of
\mathbb{CP}^{3}
with coordinates

\displaystyle Z^{A}=(\lambda_{\alpha},\mu^{\alpha^{\prime}})\,.

It is well-known that
\mathbb{CP}^{3}
can be covered by two patches
\mathcal{U}_{\pm}

[
37
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{+}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\lambda_{\alpha}^{+},\mu^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{+}=\frac{\mu^{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\lambda_{0}}\right)\Bigg|\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda_{0}\neq 0\,,\,\lambda_{\alpha}^{+}\in U_{+}\right\}\,,

(86)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{-}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\lambda_{\alpha}^{-},\mu^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{-}=\frac{\mu^{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\lambda_{1}}\right)\Bigg|\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda_{1}\neq 0\,,\,\lambda_{\alpha}^{-}\in U_{-}\right\}\,,

(87)

where
\lambda_{\alpha}=(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1})
are homogeneous coordinates of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. Bases on the
hat
operator in (
3
), the canonical choice for the coordinates on the fibres are

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha}^{+}=\binom{\lambda_{+}}{-1}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda_{\alpha}^{-}=\binom{1}{-\lambda_{-}}\,.

(88)

Here,
\lambda^{\pm}
are coordinates of
U_{\pm}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{1}=U_{+}\cup U_{-}
. In these patches the incident relations (
1
) become

\displaystyle\mu^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\pm}=x^{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\lambda_{\alpha}^{\pm}\,.

(89)

By introducing a dual twistor
\hat{Z}^{A}=(\hat{\lambda}_{\alpha},\hat{\mu}^{\alpha^{\prime}})
in the same patches, namely
\hat{Z}^{A}_{\pm}=(\hat{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{\pm},\hat{\mu}^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\pm})
, one can show that

\displaystyle x^{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}=\frac{\mu^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\pm}\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}_{\pm}-\hat{\mu}^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\pm}\lambda^{\alpha}_{\pm}}{\langle\hat{\lambda}_{\pm}\lambda_{\pm}\rangle}\,,

(90)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{+}^{\alpha}=\binom{1}{\bar{\lambda}_{+}}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\hat{\lambda}_{-}^{\alpha}=\binom{\bar{\lambda}_{-}}{1}

(91)

The top-form
K
in these patches becomes

\displaystyle K=\frac{d\lambda_{\pm}d\bar{\lambda}_{\pm}}{(1+\lambda_{\pm}\bar{\lambda}_{\pm})^{2}}\,.

(92)

To demonstrate how the integral over the fibers works, let us consider the patch
U_{+}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. One can make the following change of variables
\lambda_{+}=re^{i\theta}
and
\bar{\lambda}_{+}=re^{-i\theta}
where

. Then,

\displaystyle K=-2i\frac{rdrd\theta}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}

(93)

which is in accordance with the fact that
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
is diffeomorphic to
S^{2}
. An important observation to prove (

\theta
cancels all components that are
\theta

\displaystyle-4\pi i\int\frac{rdr}{(1+r^{2})^{m+2}}S_{\alpha(m)}T^{\alpha(n)}\delta_{m,n}=-\frac{2\pi i}{m+1}S_{\alpha(m)}T^{\alpha(m)}\,.

(94)

Complex Structure.

On
\mathbb{PT}
, an almost complex structure
J
is defined to be a linear map
J:T(\mathbb{PT})\rightarrow T(\mathbb{PT})
on the tangent bundle
T(\mathbb{PT})
of
\mathbb{PT}
where
J^{2}=-\text{id}
. Therefore,

can be decomposed (as in usual complex geometry) into eigenspaces of

Then, a
(1,0)

+\text{i}
, while a
(0,1)
-vector is a vector that has eigenvalue
-\text{i}
. Therefore, the tangent bundle
T(\mathbb{PT})
can be decomposed into

\displaystyle T(\mathbb{PT})=T_{1,0}(\mathbb{PT})\oplus T_{0,1}(\mathbb{PT})\,.

(95)

We can define

\displaystyle\partial=dZ^{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z^{A}}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\bar{\partial}=d\bar{Z}^{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{Z}^{A}}=d\hat{Z}^{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{Z}^{A}}\,

(96)

to split
T(\mathbb{PT})
into
T_{1,0}(\mathbb{PT})
and
T_{0,1}(\mathbb{PT})
. Note that we take
\bar{\partial}
as our definition of complex structure on
\mathbb{PT}
. A complex structure is said to be integrable if
\bar{\partial}^{2}=0
.

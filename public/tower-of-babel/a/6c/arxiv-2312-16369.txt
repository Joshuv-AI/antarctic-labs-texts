Let

Let

Recently, some relations between Jordan algebras and Lie algebra of

2
The Allison-Benkart-Gao functor and Berman-Moody functor

2.1
The Allison-Benkart-Gao construction

Let
A
be an alternative algebra over
k
which is not necessarily unital. Denote

L_{a}
and
R_{a}
are respectively the left and right multiplication on
A
by
a\in A
. A
k
-linear morphism
D\in\text{End}_{k}(A)
is called a
derivation

on
A
if for all
a,b\in A
,

D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b)
, which is equivalent to
[D,L_{a}]=L_{D(a)}
or
[D,R_{b}]=R_{D(b)}
in
\text{End}_{k}(A)
.

For
a,b\in A
, define

D_{a,b}=[L_{a},L_{b}]+[R_{a},R_{b}]+[L_{a},R_{b}]\in\text{End}_{k}(A).

The following results are well-known (can be found in
[
3
]
or
[
17
]
)

Proposition 2.1

For
a,b,c\in A
,
D\in\text{Der}(A)
,

(1)
D_{a,b}=L_{[a,b]}-R_{[a,b]}-3[L_{a},R_{b}]
,
D_{a,b}(c)=[[a,b],c]+3(a,c,b)
;

(2)
D_{a,b}\in\text{Der}(A)
.

(3)
D_{a,b}+D_{b,a}=0
;

(4)
D_{ab,c}+D_{bc,a}+D_{ca,b}=0
;

(5)
[D,D_{a,b}]=D_{D(a),b}+D_{a,D(b)}
.

A linear span of
D_{a,b}
is called an
inner derivation
of
A
, and we denote the subspace of all inner derivations by
\text{Inner}(A)
, which is an ideal of
\text{Der}(A)
by (5) of the previous proposition.

Let
A
be an alternative algebra. Set
I(A)
is the subspace of
\Lambda^{2}A
spanned by

ab\wedge c+bc\wedge a+ca\wedge b,

where
a,b,c
run over the elements in
A
. Set the quotient space

\mathcal{B}(A)=\Lambda^{2}A/I(A),

and denote the canonical image of
a\wedge b
in
\mathcal{B}(A)
by
\langle a,b\rangle
.

Let

sl_{3}(k)=\{x\in M_{3\times 3}(k)\ |\ \text{Tr}(x)=0\}

be the split simple Lie algebra over
k
of type
A_{2}
. The Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
is the set of all the traceless diagonal matrices in
sl_{3}(k)
.

Allison-Benkart-Gao Construction

In
[
1
]
, from the appropriate unital coordinate algebras according to the type of root systems, a unified explicit construction of centrally closed Lie algebras graded by finite root systems is expressed systematically by B. Allison, G. Benkart and Y. Gao, which is functorial on the coordinate algebras. We apply this construction for non-unital alternative algebras to construct Lie algbras of
A_{2}
-type and call it Allison-Benkart-Gao functor in this paper.

For an alternative algebra
A
,
the Allison-Benkart-Gao algebra
{\mathcal{ABG}}(A)
is defined as the vector space

{\mathcal{ABG}}(A)=\mathcal{B}(A)\oplus(sl_{3}(k)\otimes A).

The bracket product is defined by bilinear
extension of the formulae

\displaystyle 1.\ \ \ \ [x\otimes a,y\otimes b]=

\displaystyle\frac{\text{Tr}(xy)\langle a,b\rangle}{3}+[x,y]\otimes\frac{ab+ba}{2}

\displaystyle+\left(xy+yx-\frac{2}{3}\text{Tr}(xy)I_{3}\right)\otimes\frac{ab-ba}{2},

\displaystyle 2.\ \ \ \ [\langle a,b\rangle,x\otimes c]=

\displaystyle-[x\otimes c,\langle a,b\rangle]=x\otimes D_{a,b}(c),

\displaystyle 3.\ \ \ \ [\langle a,b\rangle,{\langle c,d\rangle}]=

\displaystyle\langle D_{a,b}(c),d\rangle+\langle c,D_{a,b}(d)\rangle,

where
Tr
is the trace form on
sl_{3}(k)
and
a,b,c,d\in A,x,y\in sl_{3}(k)
.

In the Theorem 4.13 in
[
1
]
or Proposition 2.2 in
[
3
]
, one has seen that

Theorem 2.1

{\mathcal{ABG}}(A)
is a Lie algebra over
k
for any alternative algebra
A
.
\Box

Remark.
When
A
is a unital alternative algebra, it is proved that
{\mathcal{ABG}}(A)
is isormorphic to the Steinberg Lie algebra
st_{3}(A)
in
[
3
]
. We will see that is not true when
A
is non-unital in the observation after Lemma
3.1
.

2.2
The Berman-Moody functor

We introduce the category
{\text{\bf Lie}_{\mathfrak{R}}}
in this subsection.

Let
R
be the subcategory of Lie algebras over
k
, whose objects are also
sl_{3}(k)
-modules and the elements of
sl_{3}(k)
act as derivations on them. The homomorphisms in this category are morphisms of both Lie algebras and
sl_{3}(k)
-modules simultaneously. Note that any Lie algebra containing a subalgebra isomorphic to
sl_{3}(k)
is an object in
R
.

Let

\Delta=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},-\alpha_{1},-\alpha_{2},-(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\}

be the root system of
sl_{3}(k)
. Let
\mathfrak{R}
be the subcategory of
sl_{3}(k)
-modules whose objects are weight
sl_{3}(k)
-modules with weights only in

\Delta\cup\{0\}
. If
M
is an object in
\mathfrak{R}
, then
M=M^{sl_{3}}\oplus M^{\text{ad}}
, where
M^{sl_{3}}
is the invariant submodule of
M
and
M^{\text{ad}}
is the isotypic component of
M
of adjoint type.

Definition 2.1

Let
{\text{\bf Lie}_{\mathfrak{R}}}
be the full subcategory of
R
such that all objects in
{\text{\bf Lie}_{\mathfrak{R}}}

are also objects in the category
\mathfrak{R}
, namely that any object
\mathfrak{g}
in
{\text{\bf Lie}_{\mathfrak{R}}}
has a decomposition

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}^{sl_{3}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}^{\text{ad}},

as an
sl_{3}(k)
-module,

Moreover, the weight space decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
is given by

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta}{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}.

In
[
4
]
, the Lie algebras graded by finite root systems are introduced. All
A_{2}
-graded Lie algebras are objects in
{\text{\bf Lie}_{\mathfrak{R}}}
, which contain split simple subalgebras isomorphic to
sl_{3}(k)
.

Lemma 2.1

If
A
is an alternative algebra over
k
, then the Allison-Benkart-Gao Lie algebra
{\mathcal{ABG}}(A)
is an object in
{\text{\bf Lie}_{\mathfrak{R}}}
. And,
{\mathcal{ABG}}:{\text{\bf Alt}_{k}}\rightarrow{\text{\bf Lie}_{\mathfrak{R}}},A\mapsto{\mathcal{ABG}}(A)
is a functor from the category
{\text{\bf Alt}_{k}}
of alternative algebras over
k
to the category
{\text{\bf Lie}_{\mathfrak{R}}}
. Indeed, one has that

{\mathcal{ABG}}(A)^{sl_{3}}=\mathcal{B}(A),\ \ \ {\mathcal{ABG}}(A)^{\text{ad}}=sl_{3}(k)\otimes A.

Proof.
The actions of
sl_{3}(k)
are given by

x.(y\otimes a)=[x,y]\otimes a,\ \ \ x.\langle a,b\rangle=0,

for
x,y\in sl_{3}(k)
and
a,b\in A
.

It is straightforward to show that the actions are derivations for the brackets given in the definition of
{\mathcal{ABG}}(A)
.

The fact that

A\mapsto\mathcal{B}(A)
is functorial implies that
{\mathcal{ABG}}(-)={\mathcal{B}}(-)\oplus(sl_{3}(k)\otimes-)
is also a functor.

\Box

Note that if
A
is not unital,
{\mathcal{ABG}}(A)
is not an
A_{2}
-graded Lie algebra since the split simple subalgebra does not always exist. Hence, the category
{\text{\bf Lie}_{\mathfrak{R}}}
contains more objects besides
A_{2}
-graded Lie algebras. At least, some Lie algebras in
{\text{\bf Lie}_{\mathfrak{R}}}
are not perfect.

For a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
in
{\text{\bf Lie}_{\mathfrak{R}}}
, denote

{\mathcal{BM}}({\mathfrak{g}})={\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}},

as the highest weight space in
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Then,

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}^{sl_{3}}\oplus(sl_{3}(k)\otimes{\mathcal{BM}}({\mathfrak{g}})).

When
\mathfrak{g}
is an
A_{2}
-graded Lie algebras, the Recognition Theorem for types

in Section 3 of
[
4
]
shows that
{\mathcal{BM}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is an alternative algebra with identity over

[e_{ij}\otimes a,e_{kl}\otimes b]=\delta_{jk}e_{il}\otimes ab-\delta_{li}e_{kj}\otimes ba

for

.

Respectively, in category
{\text{\bf Lie}_{\mathfrak{R}}}
, one has

Theorem 2.2

For
\mathfrak{g}\in\text{Obj}({\text{\bf Lie}_{\mathfrak{R}}})
,

is an alternative algebra over

(2.1)

Furthermore,

is a functor from the category

to the category

Proof.

Next, we will show the product is alternative.
The proof of Recognition Theorem in

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[e_{13}\otimes a,e_{21}\otimes cb]=e_{23}\otimes(cb)a.

and

3.1

Let

For

-modules to the category of

(resp.

for the trivial

-module

.

The (co)homology of
{\mathfrak{g}}
can be computed using the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex
(\wedge^{*}{\mathfrak{g}},d_{*})
, where the differentials
d_{n}:\wedge^{r+1}{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\wedge^{r}{\mathfrak{g}}
given by

d_{r}(x_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{r})=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{i}x_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge[x_{i},x_{i+1}]\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{r}.

(3.1)

More details can be found in Chapter 7 of
[
23
]
.

We introduce the suitable module category for Lie algebras in
{\text{\bf Lie}_{\mathfrak{R}}}
.

Definition 3.1

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a Lie algebra in the category
R
. A
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module

is called admissible, if it is also an
sl_{3}(k)
-module and these actions are compatible, i.e., for any

and

, one has

(x.a).m=x.(a.m)-a.(x.m).

(3.2)

holds. All admissible
{\mathfrak{g}}
-modules make up a category
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathfrak{g}})
of modules, whose homomorphisms are the
k
-linear morphisms commuting with both
\mathfrak{g}
-actions and

-actions simultaneously. Moreover, the category of admissible
\mathfrak{g}
-modules in
\mathfrak{R}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathfrak{R}}({\mathfrak{g}})

There are examples of admissible
\mathfrak{g}
-modules,

(1) The direct sums, the tensor products and the quotient of admissible modules are admissible;

(2) The adjoint module
{\mathfrak{g}}
is admissible. Then, the tensor product
T({\mathfrak{g}})=\oplus_{n\geq 0}T^{n}({\mathfrak{g}})
and the universal enveloping algebra

are admissible;

(3) If
M
is an
sl_{3}(k)
-module, we define the trivial actions of
\mathfrak{g}
on

. Then,
M
is admissible. In particular, the trivial module

is admissible.

Using above constructions, a free
\mathfrak{g}
-module generated by

-modules is admissible. Furthermore, any
\mathfrak{g}
-module generated by
sl_{3}(k)
-modules subject to relations stable under the

-actions is always admissible.

Consider the (co)homology of Lie algebras in
{\text{\bf Lie}_{\mathfrak{R}}}
with the coefficients of admissible
\mathfrak{g}
-modules,

Proposition 3.1

(1) For

, all

and

are functors from the category

to the category of

-modules.

(2) If

is a weight

-module, so are

and

-modules are also

-modules.
Hence, so are

Next, since the actions of

Since the trivial module

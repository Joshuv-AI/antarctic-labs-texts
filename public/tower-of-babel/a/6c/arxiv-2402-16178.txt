Symmetries of one-loop deformed q-map spaces
Vicente Cortés, Alejandro Gil-García, and Danu Thung

Abstract
Q-map spaces form an important class of quaternionic Kähler manifolds of negative scalar curvature. Their one-loop deformations are always inhomogeneous and have been used to construct cohomogeneity one quaternionic Kähler manifolds as deformations of homogeneous spaces. Here we study the group of isometries in the deformed case. Our main result is the statement that it always contains a semidirect product of a group of affine transformations of ℝ n − 1 \mathbb{R}^{n-1} with a Heisenberg group of dimension 2 ​ n + 1 2n+1 for a q-map space of dimension 4 ​ n 4n . The affine group and its action on the normal Heisenberg factor in the semidirect product depend on the cubic affine hypersurface which encodes the q-map space. Keywords: quaternionic Kähler manifolds, special geometry, isometry groups MSC classification: 53C26

Introduction

Quaternionic Kähler manifolds are Riemannian
4n
-manifolds
(n>1)
whose holonomy group is contained in
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
, hence they are Einstein. In this paper we are interested in a class of quaternionic Kähler manifolds of negative scalar curvature arising from physics, namely
supergravity c-map spaces
. The construction of these spaces have its origin in supergravity. It was shown in
[
FS90
]
(see also
[
Hit09
]
) that to any projective special Kähler manifold
\bar{M}
of (real) dimension
2n-2
one can associate a quaternionic Kähler manifold
\bar{N}
of dimension
4n
. This corresponds to dimensional reduction of
\mathcal{N}=2
supergravity coupled to vector multiplets from 4 to 3 space-time dimensions. It was moreover shown in
[
RSV06
]
that the supergravity c-map metric (sometimes referred to as the Ferrara-Sabharwal metric) admits a one-parameter deformation by quaternionic Kähler metrics of negative scalar curvature. This metric is known as the
one-loop deformed supergravity c-map metric
(this deformation can be interpreted as a perturbative quantum correction in the string coupling in type IIB string theory).

A mathematical proof that the (one-loop) deformed supergravity c-map metric is indeed quaternionic Kähler of negative scalar curvature can be described as the following two-step process. Given a projective special Kähler manifold
\bar{M}
, one has a conical affine special Kähler manifold
M
on a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-bundle over
\bar{M}
. The cotangent bundle
N=T^{*}M
of
M
is a pseudo-hyper-Kähler manifold
[
CFG89
,
ACD02
]
equipped with a rotating circle symmetry
[
ACM13
]
, so one can apply the HK/QK correspondence
[
Hay08
,
ACM13
]
to obtain a quaternionic Kähler manifold
\bar{N}
. Finally, one checks that the metric obtained by this method is the deformed supergravity c-map metric
[
ACDM15
]
. Summarizing we have the following diagram:

Therefore the deformed supergravity c-map produces a one-parameter family of quaternionic Kähler metrics (depending on a real parameter
c\in\mathbb{R}
) from a projective special Kähler manifold. Note that the case
c=0
is the Ferrara-Sabharwal metric. For a fixed projective special Kähler manifold, the metrics in the image of the deformed supergravity c-map are locally isometric for different values of
c>0

[
CDS17
]
. Moreover, any deformed supergravity c-map space is non-locally homogeneous
[
CGS23
]
.

There is a special class of supergravity c-map spaces known as
supergravity q-map spaces
. These spaces arise as the composition of the supergravity r-map and the supergravity c-map. The
supergravity r-map
produces a projective special Kähler manifold
\bar{M}
of (real) dimension
2n-2
from a projective special real manifold
\mathcal{H}
of dimension
n-2
. This construction was introduced in
[
dWVP92
]
and it is induced by dimensional reduction of
\mathcal{N}=2
supergravity theories from 5 to 4 space-time dimensions. Therefore, applying the supergravity q-map to a projective special real manifold
\mathcal{H}
of dimension
n-2
, we obtain a quaternionic Kähler manifold of dimension
4n
. Since the supergravity c-map admits a one-parameter deformation, so does the supergravity q-map. It is known that for any
c\geq 0
, a supergravity q-map space is complete provided that the projective special real manifold is complete
[
CDS17
]
. From the resolution of the Alekseevsky conjecture
[
BL23
]
we know that all homogeneous quaternionic Kähler manifolds of negative scalar curvature are Alekseevsky spaces
[
Ale75
,
Cor96
]
, that is admit a simply transitive solvable Lie group of isometries. Moreover, except
\HH^{n}
and
\mathrm{SU}(n,2)/\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(n)\times\mathrm{U}(2))
, all of these spaces are in the image of the supergravity q-map
[
dWVP92
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to determine explicitly a universal Lie group acting effectively and isometrically on any deformed supergravity q-map space
(\bar{N},g_{\bar{N}}^{c})
for
c\geq 0
, see Theorem
3.6
. For the undeformed case
c=0
the group was described in
[
CDJL21
, Appendix A]
, compare with
[
dWVVP93
,
dWVP95
]
.

The only space that is in the image of the supergravity c-map but is not obtained as a supergravity q-map space is
(\bar{N}=\mathrm{SU}(n,2)/\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(n)\times\mathrm{U}(2)),g_{\bar{N}}^{c})
. For this particular case, a Lie group playing a similar role for supergravity c-map spaces (rather than supergravity q-map spaces) acting effectively and isometrically was obtained in
[
CRT21
]
by integrating the corresponding Killing vector fields.

In this paper we directly work on the level of group actions rather than Lie algebra actions. First we recall how the group
\Aut(M)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,

, acts globally on the rigid c-map space

and

7

\big((\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\Aut(\mathcal{H}))\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\big)\ltimes(\Heis_{2n+1}/\mathcal{F}),

is an infinite cyclic subgroup of the Heisenberg center, acts isometrically and effectively on the quaternionic Kähler manifold
\bar{N}\subset P
viewed as a hypersurface of the circle bundle

denotes the automorphism group of the projective special real manifold
\mathcal{H}
. The cyclic group
\mathcal{F}

\mathbb{R}
-bundle.

We will use the Einstein summation convention throughout the text for the computations in coordinates.

2
Symmetries of rigid c-map spaces

In this section we recall some relevant background material. In particular, we recall the definition and some properties of conical affine special Kähler manifolds, as well as the rigid c-map construction, which assigns a pseudo-hyper-Kähler manifold with a rotating Killing vector field to any conical affine special Kähler manifold. Moreover, we review how to construct Hamiltonian automorphisms of the rigid c-map structure.

2.1
Special Kähler manifolds

Definition 2.1
.

An
affine special Kähler (ASK) manifold

(M,g,J,\nabla)
is a (pseudo-) Kähler manifold
(M,g,J)
endowed with a flat torsion-free connection
\nabla
such that
\nabla\omega=0
and
\mathrm{d}^{\nabla}J=0
, where
\omega=gJ:=g(J\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the Kähler form.

Definition 2.2
.

A
conical affine special Kähler (CASK) manifold

(M,g,J,\nabla,\xi)
is an ASK manifold
(M,g,J,\nabla)
endowed with a complete vector field
\xi
, called the
Euler vector field
, such that

\bullet

g
is negative-definite on
\spn\{\xi,J\xi\}
and positive-definite on its orthogonal complement,

\bullet

D\xi=\nabla\xi=\Id
, where
D
is the Levi-Civita connection of
g
.

Moreover,
M
is endowed with a principal
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action generated by
\xi
and
J\xi
.

We collect in the following proposition some well-known facts about the interaction between the vector fields
\xi,J\xi
and the affine special Kähler structure (see e.g.
[
CM09
, Proposition 3]
and
[
CHM12
, page 652]
for a proof).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(M,g,J,\nabla,\xi)
be a CASK manifold. Then

(a)

The vector field
\xi
is holomorphic and homothetic:
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g=2g
.

(b)

The vector field
J\xi
is holomorphic and Killing.

(c)

The function
f=\frac{1}{2}g(\xi,\xi)
is a Kähler potential for
g
and a Hamiltonian function for
J\xi
, where our convention is
\mathrm{d}f=-\omega(J\xi,\cdot)
.

Given a CASK manifold
(M,g,J,\nabla,\xi)
, the quotient space
\bar{M}:=M/\mathbb{C}^{*}
inherits a (positive-definite) Kähler metric
\bar{g}
and
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
is called a
projective special Kähler (PSK) manifold
. The metric is obtained by Kähler reduction exploiting the fact that
J\xi
is a Hamiltonian Killing vector field. Indeed,

\bar{M}=M/\mkern-5.0mu/S^{1}=f^{-1}(-\tfrac{1}{2})/S^{1}

is the Kähler quotient of the CASK manifold
M
by the
S^{1}
-action generated by
J\xi
.

Following
[
CST21
]
we introduce the natural notion of symmetry in this setting.

Definition 2.4
.

\bullet

An
automorphism
of a CASK manifold
(M,g,J,\nabla,\xi)
is a diffeomorphism of
M
which preserves
g
,
J
,
\nabla
and
\xi
.

\bullet

An
automorphism
of a PSK manifold
\bar{M}=M/\mkern-5.0mu/S^{1}
is a diffeomorphism of
\bar{M}
induced by an automorphism of the CASK manifold
M
.

The corresponding groups of automorphisms are denoted by
\Aut(M)
and
\Aut(\bar{M})
, respectively. At the infinitesimal level we have the following notion.

Definition 2.5
.

\bullet

An
infinitesimal automorphism
of a CASK manifold
(M,g,J,\nabla,\xi)
is a vector field
X\in\Gamma(TM)
such that its local flow preserves the CASK data on
M
. The Lie algebra of such vector fields is denoted by
\mathfrak{aut}(M)
.

\bullet

An
infinitesimal automorphism
of a PSK manifold is a vector field
\bar{X}
induced by an infinitesimal automorphism of the corresponding CASK manifold (which always projects since it commutes with
\xi
and
J\xi
). The corresponding Lie algebra is denoted by
\mathfrak{aut}(\bar{M})
.

It was shown in
[
CST21
, Proposition 2.18]
that
\mathfrak{aut}(M)
and
\mathfrak{aut}(\bar{M})
are isomorphic when the Levi-Civita connection
D
and the special connection
\nabla
are not equal.

2.2
The rigid c-map

Let us consider a CASK manifold
(M,g,J,\nabla,\xi)
. We will discuss the natural geometric structure that exists on its cotangent bundle
N=T^{*}M
. For this, it will be useful to recall some basic facts about vector bundles (see e.g.
[
MS22
]
).

Let
\pi:E\longrightarrow M
be a vector bundle over a manifold
M
. We can pull
E
back to a bundle over its total space:
\pi^{*}E\longrightarrow E
. This bundle always admits a tautological section
\Phi\in\Gamma(\pi^{*}E)
, which assigns to the point
e\in E
the value
e
. In the case where
E=T^{*}M
, this is precisely (one interpretation of) the tautological one-form
\lambda
. The tangent vectors to the fibers of
E
determine a canonical vertical distribution
T^{\mathrm{V}}E\subset TE
, which is moreover canonically isomorphic to
\pi^{*}E
. The corresponding isomorphism is denoted by

\mathcal{V}:\pi^{*}E\longrightarrow T^{\mathrm{V}}E.

In the case where
E=T^{*}M
, local coordinates
\{q^{j}\}
on
M
induce canonical coordinates
\{q^{j},p_{j}\}
on
T^{*}M
and the isomorphism
\mathcal{V}:\pi^{*}(T^{*}M)\longrightarrow T^{\mathrm{V}}(T^{*}M)
is implemented by mapping
\pi^{*}(\mathrm{d}q^{j})
to
\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}}
.

Now assume that
E
comes equipped with some connection
\nabla
. Then we may use the pullback connection on
\pi^{*}E
to compute
(\pi^{*}\nabla)\Phi
. The assignment
X\longmapsto(\pi^{*}\nabla)_{X}\Phi
, where
X\in\Gamma(TE)
, then provides a left inverse to
\mathcal{V}
. Thus, a vector field
X
is determined by
(\pi^{*}\nabla)_{X}\Phi\in\Gamma(\pi^{*}E)
and
\pi_{*}(X)\in\Gamma(\pi^{*}(TM))
. This is just another way of phrasing the fact that a connection on
E
induces a splitting
TE\cong\pi^{*}(TM)\oplus\pi^{*}E
. In particular, for
E=T^{*}M
we obtain the splitting
T(T^{*}M)\cong\pi^{*}(TM)\oplus\pi^{*}(T^{*}M)
. With respect to this splitting, we may define the following tensor fields:

g_{N}=\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\
0&g^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad I_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}J&0\\
0&J^{*}\end{pmatrix},\qquad I_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\omega^{-1}\\
\omega&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad I_{3}=I_{1}I_{2}.

(1)

It is well-known that these tensor fields define a pseudo-hyper-Kähler structure on
N=T^{*}M
if and only if
(M,g,J,\nabla)
is an ASK manifold
[
CFG89
,
ACD02
]
. Furthermore, the conical structure gives rise to the vector field
Z:=-\widetilde{J\xi}
, where the tilde denotes the horizontal lifting with respect to
\nabla
(i.e.
(\pi^{*}\nabla)_{Z}\lambda=0
where
\lambda
is the tautological one-form). This vector field is Killing and
\omega_{1}
-Hamiltonian, but satisfies
\mathcal{L}_{Z}\omega_{2}=\omega_{3}
and
\mathcal{L}_{Z}\omega_{3}=-\omega_{2}

[
ACM13
, Proposition 2]
. It is therefore called a
rotating
Killing field, and the circle action it generates is called rotating as well. The Hamiltonian function for
Z
with respect to
\omega_{1}
is denoted by
f_{Z}^{c}
, and is given by
f_{Z}^{c}=-\frac{1}{2}g_{N}(Z,Z)-\frac{1}{2}c
,
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

Taken together, the above hyper-Kähler structure with rotating circle action is referred to as the
rigid c-map structure
on
N=T^{*}M
and
(N,g_{N},I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
as the
rigid c-map space
.

Definition 2.6
.

A diffeomorphism
\varphi:N\longrightarrow N
is called an
automorphism of the rigid c-map structure
, or equivalently of the hyper-Kähler structure with rotating circle action, if it preserves
g_{N}
,
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
,
I_{3}
and
f_{Z}^{c}
.

Note that an automorphism in the above sense automatically commutes with the rotating circle action. The group of all automorphisms of the rigid c-map structure is denoted by
\Aut_{S^{1}}(N)
. The subgroup of
\omega_{1}
-Hamiltonian automorphisms is denoted by
\Ham_{S^{1}}(N)
.

Given a hyper-Kähler structure with rotating circle action, there is a canonical, closed two-form
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}:=\omega_{1}+\mathrm{d}\iota_{Z}g_{N}
of type (1,1) with respect to each
I_{k}

[
CST22
, Lemma 2.7]
that plays a central role in the following. Note that any vector field which is
\omega_{1}
-Hamiltonian and preserves
Z
and
g_{N}
is automatically
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
-Hamiltonian as well. This applies, in particular, to the rotating Killing field
Z
, whose Hamiltonian with respect to
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
we denote by
f_{\mathrm{H}}^{c}
, and is given by
f_{\mathrm{H}}^{c}=f_{Z}^{c}+g_{N}(Z,Z)=\frac{1}{2}g_{N}(Z,Z)-\frac{1}{2}c
. In the following, we will focus on Hamiltonian functions with respect to
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
rather than
\omega_{1}
.

There are two important sources of Hamiltonian automorphisms of the rigid c-map structure: canonical lifts of CASK automorphisms and translations in the fibers.

For completeness of the exposition we mention that in the case of the rigid c-map the pre-symplectic form
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
is in fact symplectic.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(N,g_{N},I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
be a rigid c-map space. Then the canonical two-form
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}=\omega_{1}+\mathrm{d}\iota_{Z}g_{N}
associated with the rotating Killing field
Z
is a symplectic structure.

Proof.

Since the one-form
\iota_{Z}g_{N}
is the pull-back of
\alpha=-\iota_{J\xi}g
we can calculate its differential as
\pi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha
, where
\mathrm{d}\alpha
can be expressed as twice the skew-symmetric part of the Levi-Civita covariant derivative

D\alpha=-g(D(J\xi),\cdot)=-g(J\cdot,\cdot)=-\omega.

Since this is skew-symmetric, we see that
\mathrm{d}\iota_{Z}g_{N}=\pi^{*}\mathrm{d}\alpha=-2\pi^{*}\omega
. This shows that

\omega_{\mathrm{H}}=\begin{pmatrix}-\omega&0\\
0&\omega^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

(2)

∎

Incidentally, the above expression for
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
shows that the endomorphism

I_{\mathrm{H}}:=g_{N}^{-1}\omega_{\mathrm{H}}=\begin{pmatrix}-J&0\\
0&J^{*}\end{pmatrix}

is a
g_{N}
-skew-symmetric almost complex structure, which obviously commutes with
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
. In this way we recover the statement that
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
is of type
(1,1)
for the three complex structures.

Corollary 2.8
.

Any rigid c-map space
(N,g_{N},I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
carries a canonical almost Kähler structure
(g_{N},I_{\mathrm{H}})
.

2.3
Canonical lifts

Let
\Aut(M)
be the group of CASK automorphisms of the CASK manifold
M
. Canonically lifting to
N=T^{*}M
, we obtain a group of
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
-Hamiltonian automorphisms of the rigid c-map structure, as proven in
[
CST21
]
. It was remarked in
[
CRT21
, Proposition 2.10]
that this group even admits an equivariant moment map
\mu:N\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with respect to
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
. We recall the result in Proposition
2.9
below adding details and fixing notation.

Let
X
denote a vector field on
M
generating a one-parameter family of automorphisms of the CASK structure, and
Y
its canonical lift to
N
. Then, with respect to the splitting
T(T^{*}M)=T^{\mathrm{H}}N\oplus T^{\mathrm{V}}N\cong\pi^{*}(TM)\oplus\pi^{*}(T^{*}M)
, we may write

Y=\pi^{*}X-\lambda\circ(\pi^{*}\nabla)(\pi^{*}X).

Here
\lambda:\xi\longmapsto\lambda_{\xi}
is the tautological section of the vector bundle
\pi^{*}(T^{*}M)
, defined as

\lambda_{\xi}(v)=\xi(v),\qquad\xi\in N_{p}=T_{p}^{*}M,\,v\in(\pi^{*}(TM))_{\xi}=T_{p}M,\,\pi(\xi)=p,

where
\pi^{*}X=X\circ\pi\in\Gamma(\pi^{*}(TM))
and
\lambda\circ(\pi^{*}\nabla)(\pi^{*}X)\in\Gamma(\pi^{*}(T^{*}M))\subset\Gamma(T^{*}N)
is the one-form sending a vector field
A\in\Gamma(TN)
to the smooth function
\lambda((\pi^{*}\nabla)_{A}(\pi^{*}X))
, and where we have identified
\pi^{*}(T^{*}M)=(\pi^{*}(TM))^{*}=(T^{\mathrm{H}}N)^{*}=(T^{\mathrm{V}}N)^{0}\subset T^{*}N
.

Note that

(\pi^{*}\nabla)_{Y}\lambda=-\lambda\circ(\pi^{*}\nabla)(\pi^{*}X).

(3)

Indeed, for a vector field
Y\in\Gamma(TN)
and
\lambda\in\Gamma(\pi^{*}(T^{*}M))
the tautological section,
(\pi^{*}\nabla)_{Y}\lambda
gives us the vertical component of
Y
, which is precisely
-\lambda\circ(\pi^{*}\nabla)(\pi^{*}X)
.

In local coordinates
\{q^{i},p_{i}\}
on
N
induced by local
\nabla
-affine coordinates
\{q^{i}\}
on
M
, the tautological section
\lambda
on
N
is given by
\lambda(\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}})=p_{i}
and the vector field
Y\in\Gamma(TN)
by

Y=X^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{j}}-p_{i}\frac{\partial X^{i}}{\partial q^{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}}

in terms of the components
\{X^{i}\}
of the vector field
X
in the local coordinates
\{q^{i}\}
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
X
be an infinitesimal CASK automorphism and
Y
its canonical lift as explained above. Then

\mu_{Y}=\frac{1}{2}\left(g_{N}(Z,Y)+\pi^{*}\omega^{-1}(\lambda\circ(\pi^{*}\nabla)(\pi^{*}X),\lambda)\right)

is a
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
-Hamiltonian function for
Y
, where
Z=-\widetilde{J\xi}
. This assignment determines an equivariant (co)moment map
\mu:\mathfrak{aut}(M)\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(N)
,
X\longmapsto\mu_{X}:=\mu_{Y}
, for the action of
\mathfrak{aut}(M)
on
N=T^{*}M
.

Proof.

We have to show that
\iota_{Y}\omega_{\mathrm{H}}=-\mathrm{d}\mu_{Y}
. Recall that
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
is given by (
2
) with respect to the splitting
T(T^{*}M)\cong\pi^{*}(TM)\oplus\pi^{*}(T^{*}M)
. Thus we have

\iota_{Y}\omega_{\mathrm{H}}=-\pi^{*}(\iota_{X}\omega)+\iota_{(\pi^{*}\nabla)_{Y}\lambda}\pi^{*}\omega^{-1}.

Let us compute these two terms. First notice that, by Proposition
2.3
(a) we have that

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\omega=2\omega
and, hence

2\iota_{X}\omega=\iota_{X}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\omega=\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}(\iota_{\xi}\omega)=\mathcal{L}_{X}(\iota_{\xi}\omega)-\mathrm{d}(\iota_{X}\iota_{\xi}\omega)=\mathrm{d}(g(-J\xi,X)),

since
\mathcal{L}_{X}(\iota_{\xi}\omega)=0
(recall that
X
is an infinitesimal CASK automorphism). So we see that

\pi^{*}(\iota_{X}\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}(g_{N}(Z,Y)).

(4)

To compute the second term, let
A
be an arbitrary section of
\pi^{*}(T^{*}M)\cong T^{\mathrm{V}}(T^{*}M)
. Let
\{q^{j}\}
be special real coordinates on
M
and
\{q^{j},p_{j}\}
the corresponding canonical coordinates on
N
. With respect to these, we can write
A=A_{i}\mathrm{d}q^{i}
and, using
(\pi^{*}\nabla)_{Y}\lambda=-\lambda\circ(\pi^{*}\nabla)(\pi^{*}X)=-p_{i}\frac{\partial X^{i}}{\partial q^{j}}\mathrm{d}q^{j}
, we get

\pi^{*}\omega^{-1}((\pi^{*}\nabla)_{Y}\lambda,A)=-\omega^{jk}p_{i}\frac{\partial X^{i}}{\partial q^{j}}A_{k}.

Thus,
\iota_{(\pi^{*}\nabla)_{Y}\lambda}\pi^{*}\omega^{-1}
is the one-form on
T^{*}M
which vanishes when applied to a horizontal vector field and evaluates on a vertical vector field
A=A_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}
(corresponding to the
A
consider earlier by the canonical isomorphism
\pi^{*}(T^{*}M)\cong T^{\mathrm{V}}(T^{*}M)
) as above. This means that we can write it, in local coordinates, as

\iota_{(\pi^{*}\nabla)_{Y}\lambda}\pi^{*}\omega^{-1}=-\omega^{jk}p_{i}\frac{\partial X^{i}}{\partial q^{j}}\mathrm{d}p_{k}.

(5)

Now, we compute the differential of our proposed moment map:

\mathrm{d}\mu_{Y}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathrm{d}(g_{N}(Z,Y))+\mathrm{d}(\pi^{*}\omega^{-1}(\lambda\circ(\pi^{*}\nabla)(\pi^{*}X),\lambda))\right),

the second term of which can be computed (using (
3
) and (
5
)) in local coordinates as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}(\pi^{*}\omega^{-1}(\lambda\circ(\pi^{*}\nabla)(\pi^{*}X),\lambda))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\left(\omega^{jk}p_{i}p_{k}\frac{\partial X^{i}}{\partial q^{j}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\omega^{jk}\frac{\partial X^{i}}{\partial q^{j}}(p_{i}\mathrm{d}p_{k}+p_{k}\mathrm{d}p_{i})

\displaystyle=\omega^{jk}p_{i}\frac{\partial X^{i}}{\partial q^{j}}\mathrm{d}p_{k}=-\iota_{(\pi^{*}\nabla)_{Y}\lambda}\pi^{*}\omega^{-1},

where, in passing to the second line, we used that both
\omega^{jk}
and
\frac{\partial X^{i}}{\partial q^{j}}
are anti-symmetric in their indices; the latter fact is just the equation
\mathcal{L}_{X}\omega=0
in (
\nabla
-affine) coordinates.

This computation, together with (
4
), concludes the proof that
\mu_{Y}
is indeed a Hamiltonian function for
Y
.

2.4
Translations in the fibers

One of the crucial features of the rigid c-map metric on
N
is that it is semi-flat. This means that it is foliated by half-dimensional flat submanifolds. These are just the fibers of
\pi:N=T^{*}M\longrightarrow M
, and the reason they are flat is that the metric in each fiber is constant with respect to the affine structure induced by the vector space structure of the fiber, as can be seen directly from (
1
).

An important consequence is the following result.

Proposition 2.11
.

The cotangent bundle
N=T^{*}M
carries locally an
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
-Hamiltonian action of the group
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
by automorphisms of the rigid c-map structure, which preserves the fibers and acts on them by translations. If the holonomy group of the special connection
\nabla
on
M
is trivial (in particular, if
M
is simply connected), then the action is global.

Proof.

We first give a description of the local action. Choosing special real coordinates
\{q^{j}\}
on
M
and using the associated canonical coordinates
\{q^{j},p_{j}\}
on
N
, the action of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
is generated by the locally defined vector fields
\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}}
. A quick look at (
1
) reveals that the full hyper-Kähler structure is preserved by these vector fields. Moreover, they commute with the rotating circle action as well, since
Z=-\widetilde{J\xi}
only depends on the coordinates
\{q^{j}\}
on the base
M
. This implies that they also preserve the
\omega_{1}
-Hamiltonian function
f_{Z}^{c}=-\frac{1}{2}g_{N}(Z,Z)-\frac{1}{2}c
. Finally, let us give a local Hamiltonian with respect to
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
. Writing (
2
) in the above coordinates, we have

\omega_{\mathrm{H}}=\frac{1}{2}(-\omega_{ij}\mathrm{d}q^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}q^{j}+\omega^{ij}\mathrm{d}p_{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}p_{j}),

where each
\omega_{ij}
is constant (and hence so are the components
\omega^{ij}
of the inverse matrix), and we are omitting pullbacks when no confusion can arise.

Hence, we find

\iota_{\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{k}}}\omega_{\mathrm{H}}=\omega^{kj}\mathrm{d}p_{j}=-\mathrm{d}(-\omega^{kj}p_{j}).

We can thus assign the local Hamiltonian function

\mu_{\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{k}}}=-\omega^{kj}p_{j}

(6)

to
\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{k}}
. More generally,
\mu
assigns to every vertical vector field
v=v_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{k}}
with constant coefficients the function
\mu_{v}=-\omega^{kj}v_{k}p_{j}
. The vector fields
\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{k}}
generate an action of the group
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
with the claimed properties. In the local coordinates
\{q^{j},p_{j}\}
a vector
v=(v_{j})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
acts by
(q,p)\longmapsto(q,p+v)
.

To define a global moment map
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{2n}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(N)
and a global group action of

on

it suffices to have a global frame of the vertical bundle

parallel with respect to the connection
\pi^{*}\nabla
. Such a frame exists if and only if the holonomy of the (flat) special connection is trivial.
∎

As a comment on the last step of the proof of Proposition

we note that locally (over the preimage

of a suitable open set

) a parallel frame of

can be chosen of the form

, where

are local

2.5
The semidirect product

Under the assumption that the holonomy group of
\nabla
is trivial, we have constructed two subgroups of
\Ham_{S^{1}}(N)
and the next thing to do is to study how they interact. In this section, we check that the generators of the two groups combine into a semidirect product. Let us start by emphasizing that the group
\Gamma_{\nabla}(T^{*}M)\cong\mathbb{R}^{2n}
of
\nabla
-parallel sections acts by addition on
N=T^{*}M
, that is

\alpha\cdot\beta=\alpha(p)+\beta,

for all
\alpha\in\Gamma_{\nabla}(T^{*}M)
,
\beta\in T_{p}^{*}M
,
p\in M
. The group
\Aut(M)
acts naturally on
N=T^{*}M
:

h\cdot\beta=h_{*}\beta=(h^{-1})^{*}\beta,

for all
h\in\Aut(M)
,
\beta\in T_{p}^{*}M
,
p\in M
.

Proposition 2.12
.

The subgroups
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
and
\Aut(M)
of
\Ham_{S^{1}}(N)
generate a group
G\subset\Ham_{S^{1}}(N)
which is a semidirect product
\Aut(M)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

Proof.

It is clear that the two subgroups have trivial intersection, as
\Aut(M)
lifts a subgroup of diffeomorphisms of
M
whereas
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
preserves each fiber of
N=T^{*}M
. The action of
\Aut(M)
is linear on the fibers and preserves the space of
\nabla
-parallel one-forms. We check explicitly that the elements
h\in\Aut(M)
normalize the group of translations:

(h\cdot\alpha\cdot h^{-1})\cdot\beta=h_{*}(\alpha(h^{-1}(p))+h^{*}\beta)=(h_{*}\alpha)(p)+\beta=(h_{*}\alpha)\cdot\beta,

for all
\alpha\in\Gamma_{\nabla}(T^{*}M)
,
\beta\in T_{p}^{*}M
,
p\in M
. This proves that
h\cdot\alpha\cdot h^{-1}=h_{*}\alpha\in\Gamma_{\nabla}(T^{*}M)
for all
\alpha\in\Gamma_{\nabla}(T^{*}M)
.
∎

Infinitesimally, we can describe the semidirect product structure in terms of structure constants if we choose a basis. Thus, let
\{Y_{\alpha}\}
be infinitesimal generators of the action of
\Aut(M)
on
N
, obtained by canonically lifting generators
\{X_{\alpha}\}\subset\mathfrak{aut}(M)
. With respect to canonical coordinates induced by (conical) special real coordinates on
M
, we can express
Y_{\alpha}
as

Y_{\alpha}=X_{\alpha}^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{j}}-p_{i}\frac{\partial X_{\alpha}^{i}}{\partial q^{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}},

where the component functions
X_{\alpha}^{j}
of
X_{\alpha}
are linear functions. The generators of the
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
-action are the vectors
\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{k}}
. The structure constants are now easily computed:

\left[Y_{\alpha},\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{k}}\right]=\frac{\partial X_{\alpha}^{k}}{\partial q^{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}}.

Note that the coefficients multiplying
\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}}
on the right-hand side are indeed constants, since any
X\in\mathfrak{aut}(M)
is
\nabla
-affine (i.e.
\mathcal{L}_{X}\nabla=0
). Together with the structure constants of
\Aut(M)
this determines the structure of
\mathfrak{aut}(M)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

3
Symmetries under the HK/QK correspondence

Now that we have a completely explicit description of the group
\Aut(M)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n}\subset\Ham_{S^{1}}(N)
, the next step is to transfer the group action to the quaternionic Kähler manifold
\bar{N}
. Up to a so-called elementary modification explained below, this is essentially done by first lifting to

and subsequently studying the induced action on
\bar{N}
, which we realize as a submanifold of
P
. We will continue to assume that the holonomy group of the flat special connection
\nabla
is trivial to ensure the global

-action.

3.1
The HK/QK correspondence as a twist

The HK/QK correspondence

ACM13

produces a quaternionic Kähler manifold from a (pseudo-)hyper-Kähler manifold equipped with a rotating circle action. This correspondence was studied in
[

using the twist formalism introduced by Swann in
[

[

Let

Z_{P}=\tilde{Z}+\pi_{N}^{*}f_{Z}^{c}X_{P},

3.2
Infinitesimal description

The infinitesimal description of the transfer of symmetries under the HK/QK correspondence appears explained in
[
CST21
]
: one performs an (elementary) modification and then twists. More precisely, let
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{ham}_{S^{1}}(N)
be a subalgebra with moment map
\mu:N\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with respect to
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
. Denote the Hamiltonian function corresponding to
V\in\mathfrak{g}
by
\mu_{V}
. Then, the first step consists in modifying
V
to

V_{\mathrm{H}}:=V-\frac{\mu_{V}}{f_{\mathrm{H}}^{c}}Z\in\Gamma(TN).

We will sometimes refer to
V_{\mathrm{H}}
as the elementary deformation of

. The second step is twisting
V_{\mathrm{H}}
to a vector field
\operatorname{tw}(V_{H})\in\Gamma(T\bar{N})

V_{\mathrm{Q}}
. Twisting is done by lifting
V_{\mathrm{H}}
horizontally (with respect to a given connection
\eta
whose curvature is
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
) to the trivial circle bundle
P=N\times S^{1}
and then projecting down to
\bar{N}
. In other words

\operatorname{tw}(V_{\mathrm{H}})=V_{\mathrm{Q}}=\mathrm{d}\pi_{\bar{N}}(\tilde{V}_{\mathrm{H}})=\mathrm{d}\pi_{\bar{N}}\left(\tilde{V}-\frac{\mu_{V}}{f_{\mathrm{H}}^{c}}\tilde{Z}\right),

where we have denoted the
\eta
-horizontal lift to
P
by a tilde.

This procedure gives rise to an injective, linear map
\varphi_{\mu}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{aut}_{S^{1}}(\bar{N})

\mu
. Here,
\mathfrak{aut}_{S^{1}}(\bar{N})
denotes the space of Killing fields of the quaternionic Kähler manifold

\bar{N}
generated by
Z_{\bar{N}}=\operatorname{tw}(-\frac{1}{f_{\mathrm{H}}^{c}}Z)
.

It is shown in
[

]
that this linear map is a homomorphism of Lie algebras if and only if the moment map
\mu
is equivariant. Indeed, choosing a basis
\{V_{i}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
with corresponding structure constants
\{c_{ij}^{k}\}
, the equivariance condition can be expressed as

\omega_{\mathrm{H}}(V_{i},V_{j})-c_{ij}^{k}\mu_{V_{k}}=0.

The left-hand side is precisely what measures the failure of
\varphi_{\mu}
to be a homomorphism, according to
[
CST21
, Theorem 3.8]
.

In the case at hand, we are considering
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{aut}(M)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, and we have a canonical moment map. Since this canonical moment map is equivariant for the action of the subgroup
\Aut(M)
by Proposition
2.9
, we obtain a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{aut}_{S^{1}}(\bar{N})
isomorphic to
\mathfrak{aut}(M)
. However, the canonical choice of moment map we gave for
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
in (
6
) is not equivariant. Indeed

\omega_{\mathrm{H}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}}\right)=\omega^{ij}\neq 0,

while
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
is of course abelian and therefore has vanishing structure constants. Following
[
CST21
, Theorem 3.8]
, this implies that
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
gives rise to a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{aut}_{S^{1}}(\bar{N})
which is a one-dimensional central extension of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
by
Z_{\bar{N}}
, and whose non-trivial brackets are given by the coefficients of
\omega^{-1}
. In other words,

\left[\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\right)^{\mathrm{Q}},\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}}\right)^{\mathrm{Q}}\right]=\omega^{ij}Z_{\bar{N}}.

Since
\omega^{-1}
is the natural symplectic form on the fibers of

\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}

We thus obtain two algebras of Killing fields, isomorphic to
\mathfrak{aut}(M)

[\mathfrak{aut}(M),\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}]\subset\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}

Y=X^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}}-\frac{\partial X^{i}}{\partial q^{j}}p_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}}\in\mathfrak{ham}_{S^{1}}(N)

with canonical Hamiltonian function (see Proposition
2.9
)

\mu_{Y}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-\omega_{ij}q^{i}X^{j}+\omega^{jk}p_{i}p_{k}\frac{\partial X^{i}}{\partial q^{j}}\right).

Using these expressions, we find
\eta(Y)=\frac{1}{2}\iota_{Y}\iota_{\Xi}\omega_{\mathrm{H}}=\mu_{Y}
. The upshot is that
\hat{Y}=Y
(see (
8
)), i.e. the lifted action of
\Aut(M)
to
P=N\times S^{1}
is trivial on the
S^{1}
-factor and it corresponds to the action of
\Aut(M)
described in the first equation of (
7
).

Finally, we consider the lift of the group
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. For an element of its Lie algebra
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
we have the local expression
v=v_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{k}}
and corresponding moment map
\mu_{v}=-\omega^{kj}v_{k}p_{j}
. This time, we find
\eta(v)=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{v}
, and consequently
\hat{v}=v+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{v}X_{P}
. These vector fields do not induce an action of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
since they no longer commute. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle[\hat{v},\hat{w}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(v(\mu_{w})-w(\mu_{v}))X_{P}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}(v)-\mathrm{d}\mu_{v}(w))X_{P}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(-(\iota_{w}\omega_{\mathrm{H}})(v)+(\iota_{v}\omega_{\mathrm{H}})(w))=\omega_{\mathrm{H}}(v,w)X_{P},

or, in local coordinates,
[\hat{v},\hat{w}]=\omega^{ij}v_{i}w_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}
. Since
\omega^{ij}
is constant and
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}
is central, the conclusion is that we are now dealing with an infinitesimal action of a one-dimensional central extension of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
whose non-trivial commutators are given by a symplectic form, i.e. a Heisenberg algebra
\mathfrak{heis}_{2n+1}
. Integrating, we obtain the action of
\Heis_{2n+1}
described in the second line of (
7
).
∎

To state Theorem
3.6
, we have to focus on a particular class of CASK manifolds, namely those coming from projective special real manifolds
\mathcal{H}
. We now briefly describe them. We follow the notation and conventions of
[
CT22
]
. For more details, see for instance
[
CHM12
,
CDJL21
]
.

Definition 3.3
.

A
projective special real (PSR) manifold
is a Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{H},g_{\mathcal{H}})
such that
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is a hypersurface and there is a homogeneous cubic polynomial
h:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying:

\bullet

\mathcal{H}\subset\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\mid h(t)=1\}
.

\bullet

g_{\mathcal{H}}=-\partial^{2}h|_{T\mathcal{H}\times T\mathcal{H}}
.

We denote the coordinates of
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
by
t^{a}
. In particular we write the cubic polynomial
h
as

h(t^{a})=\frac{1}{6}k_{abc}t^{a}t^{b}t^{c},

where the coefficients
k_{acb}\in\mathbb{R}
are symmetric in the indices.

Let
(\mathcal{H},g_{\mathcal{H}})
be a PSR manifold defined by the real cubic polynomial
h
, and let
U:=\mathbb{R}_{>0}\cdot\mathcal{H}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\setminus\{0\}
. We define
\bar{M}:=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}+iU\subset\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
with the canonical holomorphic structure, where the global holomorphic coordinates are given by
z^{a}:=b^{a}+it^{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}+iU
. On
\bar{M}
we consider the metric

\bar{g}=\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{K}}{\partial z^{a}\partial\bar{z}^{b}}\mathrm{d}z^{a}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{b},

where
\mathcal{K}:=-\log K(t)
and
K(t):=8h(t)=\frac{4}{3}k_{abc}t^{a}t^{b}t^{c}
. Then it can be shown that
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
is a PSK manifold
[
CHM12
]
. In fact, one can find the following explicit expression for a PSK metric coming from a PSR manifold:

\bar{g}=-\frac{1}{4}\frac{\partial^{2}\log h(t)}{\partial t^{a}\partial t^{b}}(\mathrm{d}b^{a}\mathrm{d}b^{b}+\mathrm{d}t^{a}\mathrm{d}t^{b})=\left(-\frac{k_{abc}t^{c}}{4h(t)}+\frac{k_{acd}k_{bef}t^{c}t^{d}t^{e}t^{f}}{(4h(t))^{2}}\right)(\mathrm{d}b^{a}\mathrm{d}b^{b}+\mathrm{d}t^{a}\mathrm{d}t^{b}).

Definition 3.4
.

The construction of the PSK manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
from the PSR manifold
(\mathcal{H},g_{\mathcal{H}})
as explained above is known as the
supergravity r-map
.

Given a PSK manifold
\bar{M}
in the image of the supergravity r-map, its corresponding CASK manifold is defined via the CASK domain
(M,\mathfrak{F})
, where
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is given by

M:=\{(X^{0},\ldots,X^{n-1})=X^{0}\cdot(1,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid X^{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{*},z\in\bar{M}\}

and

\mathfrak{F}(X)=-\frac{h(X^{1},\ldots,X^{n-1})}{X^{0}}=-\frac{1}{6}k_{abc}\frac{X^{a}X^{b}X^{c}}{X^{0}}.

We know recall what is a CASK domain and how to obtain a CASK manifold from it.

Definition 3.5
.

A
CASK domain
is a pair
(M,\mathfrak{F})
such that:

\bullet

M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariant domain (with respect to the usual
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
by multiplication).

\bullet

\mathfrak{F}:M\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphic function, homogeneous of degree 2 with respect to the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action, called the
holomorphic prepotential
.

\bullet

With respect to the natural holomorphic coordinates
(X^{0},\ldots,X^{n-1})
of
M
, the matrix

(\tau_{ij}):=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\mathfrak{F}}{\partial X^{i}\partial X^{j}}\right)

satisfies that
\operatorname{Im}(\tau_{ij})
has signature
(n-1,1)
and
\operatorname{Im}(\tau_{ij})X^{i}\bar{X}^{j}<0
for
X\in M
.

From this data, we obtain a CASK manifold
(M,g,\omega,\nabla,\xi)
by

g=\operatorname{Im}(\tau_{ij})\mathrm{d}X^{i}\mathrm{d}\bar{X}^{j},\quad\omega=\frac{i}{2}\operatorname{Im}(\tau_{ij})\mathrm{d}X^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{X}^{j},\quad\xi=X^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial X^{i}}+\bar{X}^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{X}^{i}}.

The flat connection
\nabla
is defined such that

\mathrm{d}x^{i}:=\operatorname{Re}(\mathrm{d}X^{i})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{d}y_{i}:=-\operatorname{Re}(\mathrm{d}(\frac{\partial\mathfrak{F}}{\partial X^{i}}))

(10)

is a flat frame of
T^{*}M
.

Note that for a CASK manifold
M
determined by
\mathcal{H}
as explained above, the functions
x^{i}:=\operatorname{Re}(X^{i})
and
y_{i}:=-\operatorname{Re}(\frac{\partial\mathfrak{F}}{\partial X^{i}})
are globally defined and their differentials (
10
) give rise to a parallel frame of
T^{*}M
, hence
\mathrm{Hol}(\nabla)
is trivial and therefore the action of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
given by Proposition
2.11
is global.

In this situation, where the CASK manifold
M
is determined by a PSR manifold
\mathcal{H}
, a group of isometries preserving the CASK structure was described in
[
CDJL21
, Appendix A]
. More precisely, the group we are considering is

\mathrm{Aff}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}):=(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\Aut(\mathcal{H}))\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\hookrightarrow\Aut(M)\subset\mathrm{Sp}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}),

where

\Aut(\mathcal{H}):=\{A\in\mathrm{GL}(n-1,\mathbb{R})\mid A\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}\}

and the arrow is a certain embedding
[
CDJL21
, Proposition 23]
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
M
be a CASK manifold determined by the PSR manifold
\mathcal{H}
as explained above. Then the group
\mathrm{Aff}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\ltimes(\Heis_{2n+1}/\mathcal{F})
, where
\mathcal{F}
is an infinite cyclic subgroup of the Heisenberg center, acts effectively and isometrically on
(\bar{N},g_{\bar{N}}^{c})
for
c\geq 0
.

Proof.

From the explicit description of the action of
\mathrm{Aff}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
on
M
given in
[
CDJL21
, Appendix A]
we see that the function
X^{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(P)
changes under this action only by a real positive factor. It follows that the submanifold
\bar{N}\subset P
given by (
9
) is invariant under the action of
\mathrm{Aff}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\ltimes\Heis_{2n+1}
, since the action of
\Heis_{2n+1}
preserves the fibers of
\pi\circ\pi_{N}:P\longrightarrow M
, where
\pi:N=T^{*}M\longrightarrow M
and
\pi_{N}:P\longrightarrow N
. Hence the group action on
P
described in (
7
) restricts to
\bar{N}
. From the explicit description, it is also easy to check that the action of
\mathrm{Aff}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
is effective on
\bar{N}\subset P
.

Finally, the action of
\Heis_{2n+1}
is not quite effective, since its center acts by translations along the
S^{1}
-factor of
P
. To obtain an effective action we need to divide out the infinite cyclic subgroup of the center, whose elements correspond to shifting
s\in S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
by
2\pi k
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The group
\mathrm{Aff}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\ltimes(\Heis_{2n+1}/\mathcal{F})
acts by isometries on
\bar{N}
by construction.
∎

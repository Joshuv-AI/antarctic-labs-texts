Tensor categories
Alexei Davydov

Abstract
We present here definitions and constructions basic for the theory of monoidal and tensor categories. We provide references to the original sources, whenever possible. Group-theoretical categories are used as examples.



Department of Mathematics, Ohio University, Athens, OH 45701, USA

davydov@ohio.edu

Keywords/Objectives:
monoidal categories and functors, braided and symmetric monoidal categories, rigid and spherical monoidal categories, dimension, algebras and modules in monoidal categories, linear and abelian monoidal categories, tensor and fusion categories.

1
Introduction

Monoidal categories were defined in
[
Bénabou (1963)
]
and further studied in
[
1
]
and
[
Kelly (1964)
]
.
The notion turned out to be a fundamental one.
From philosophical point of view it is the result of applying the so-called
categorification
(in the sense of e.g.
[
Crane and Frenkel (1994)
,
Baez and Dolan (1998)
]
) to the notion of a monoid, i.e. the process of replacing sets with categories, functions with functors, and equations between functions by natural isomorphisms between functors, which in turn should satisfy certain equations of their own, called “coherence laws”. The pseudo-monoid nature of monoidal categories explains their appearance in low dimensional topology, through (extended) topological field theories
[
Turaev (1994)
]
.
Other (somewhat related) applications of monoidal categories include mathematical logic and theoretical computer science (see
[
Baez and Stay (2010)
]
).

A special kind of monoidal categories is formed by linear (hom-sets are vector spaces, composition and tensor product of morphisms are bilinear maps) and abelian categories. The natural source of such categories is the representation theory of groups and other related structures (Lie algebras, Hopf algebras, etc.).
An abelian monoidal (or
tensor
) category can thus be seen as the category of all possible linear realisations of a certain symmetry. This point of view is beneficial in such areas as the theory of motives or Langlands correspondence and was driving the development of (symmetric) tensor categories
[
Deligne and Milne (1982)
,
Deligne (2002)
]
.

The concept of
fusion
(finite semi-simple tensor) categories originated in high energy physics
[
Moore and Seiberg (1990)
]
.
Braided fusion categories appear as representation categories of chiral algebras in 2d conformal field theory or as values on the circle of corresponding 3d topological field theories. More general fusion categories are formed by boundary data of such theories.
Currently the largest consumer of fusion categories is condensed matter physics (topological states of matter)
[
Kitaev (2006)
]
.

For definitions and basic facts of category theory, such as adjoint functors, limits, ends see
[
Mac Lane (1971)
]
.
We follow Mac Lane’s notation denoting the set of morphisms between objects
X
and
Y
of a category
{\cal A}
by
{\cal A}(X,Y)
.
For a functor
F:{\cal C}\to{\cal D}
we denote by
{\cal I}m_{f}(F)
the full subcategory of
{\cal D}
with objects of the form
F(X)
for
X\in{\cal C}
(the
full image
of
F
) and
by
{\cal I}m(F)
is the subcategory of
{\cal D}
with objects of the form
F(X)
for
X\in{\cal C}
and morphisms of the form
F(f)
for
f
a morphism in
{\cal C}
(the
proper image
of
F
).
Thus
F
factors as
{\cal C}\to{\cal I}m(F)\to{\cal I}m_{f}(F)\to{\cal D}
, where the first functor is full, the second is faithful, and the last is fully faithful (an
embedding
).

2
Monoidal categories

A category
{\cal C}
is
semi-groupal
if it comes equipped with a functor (the
tensor product
)

{\cal C}\times{\cal C}\to{\cal C}\qquad(X,Y)\mapsto X{\otimes}Y

together with a collection of isomorphisms (the
associativity constraint
)
a_{X,Y,Z}:X{\otimes}(Y{\otimes}Z)\to(X{\otimes}Y){\otimes}Z
natural in
X,Y,Z\in{\cal C}
,
such that the diagram

\textstyle{(X{\otimes}Y){\otimes}(Z{\otimes}W)}
\textstyle{X{\otimes}(Y{\otimes}(Z{\otimes}W))}
\textstyle{((X{\otimes}Y){\otimes}Z){\otimes}W}
\textstyle{X{\otimes}((Y{\otimes}Z){\otimes}W)}
\textstyle{(X{\otimes}(Y{\otimes}Z)){\otimes}W}
\scriptstyle{a_{X,Y,Z{\otimes}W}}
\scriptstyle{a_{X{\otimes}Y,Z,W}}
\scriptstyle{1{\otimes}a_{Y,Z,W}}
\scriptstyle{a_{X,Y{\otimes}Z,W}}
\scriptstyle{a_{X,Y,Z}{\otimes}1}

(1)

commutes for any
X,Y,Z,W\in{\cal C}
.

A semi-groupal category is
strict
if
a_{X,Y,Z}=1
for all
X,Y,Z\in{\cal C}
.

Example 2.1
.

For a category
{\cal M}
denote by
{\cal E}nd({\cal M})
the category of endofunctors
{\cal M}\to{\cal M}
. This is a strict semi-groupal category with composition of functors as the tensor product.

A functor
F:{\cal C}\to{\cal D}
between semi-groupal categories is
semi-groupal
if it comes equipped with a collection of isomorphisms (the
semi-groupal constraint
)
F_{X,Y}:F(X){\otimes}F(Y)\to F(X{\otimes}Y)
natural in
X,Y\in{\cal C}
,
such that the diagram

\textstyle{F(X){\otimes}(F(Y){\otimes}F(Z))}
\textstyle{F(X){\otimes}F(Y{\otimes}Z)}
\textstyle{F(X{\otimes}(Y{\otimes}Z))}
\textstyle{(F(X){\otimes}F(Y)){\otimes}F(Z)}
\textstyle{F(X{\otimes}Y){\otimes}F(Z)}
\textstyle{F((X{\otimes}Y){\otimes}Z)}
\scriptstyle{a_{F(X),F(Y),F(Z)}}
\scriptstyle{1{\otimes}F_{Y,Z}}
\scriptstyle{F_{X,Y{\otimes}Z}}
\scriptstyle{F_{X,Y}{\otimes}1}
\scriptstyle{F_{X{\otimes}Y,Z}}
\scriptstyle{F(a_{X,Y,Z})}

(2)

commutes for any
X,Y,Z\in{\cal C}
.

A natural transformation
c:F\to G
between semi-groupal functors
F,G:{\cal C}\to{\cal D}
is
semi-groupal
if the diagram

\textstyle{F(X){\otimes}F(Y)}
\textstyle{F(X{\otimes}Y)}
\textstyle{G(X){\otimes}G(Y)}
\textstyle{G(X{\otimes}Y)}
\scriptstyle{c_{X}{\otimes}c_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{F_{X,Y}}
\scriptstyle{G_{X,Y}}
\scriptstyle{c_{X{\otimes}Y}}

(3)

commutes for any
X,Y\in{\cal C}
.

Clearly composites of semi-groupal functors and natural transformations are semi-groupal.

A semi-groupal category
{\cal C}
is
monoidal
if it has an object
I\in{\cal C}
(the
monoidal unit
) together with collections of isomorphisms
l_{X}:I{\otimes}X\to X
,
r_{X}:X{\otimes}I\to X
natural in
X\in{\cal C}
such that the diagram

\textstyle{X{\otimes}(I{\otimes}Y)}
\textstyle{(X{\otimes}I){\otimes}Y}
\textstyle{X{\otimes}Y}
\scriptstyle{1{\otimes}l_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{a_{X,I,Y}}
\scriptstyle{r_{X}{\otimes}1}

(4)

commutes for any
X,Y\in{\cal C}
.

Remark 2.2
.

The monoidal unit object is unique up to an isomorphism, i.e. being monoidal is a property of a semi-groupal category.

In a strict monoidal category one also has
l=r=1
.

A semi-groupal functor
F:{\cal C}\to{\cal D}
is
monoidal
if it comes equipped with an isomorphism
\iota:I\to F(I)
such that the diagrams

\textstyle{F(I){\otimes}F(X)}
\textstyle{F(I{\otimes}X)}
\textstyle{I{\otimes}F(X)}
\textstyle{F(X)}
\scriptstyle{\iota{\otimes}1}
\scriptstyle{F_{I,X}}
\scriptstyle{F(l_{X})}
\scriptstyle{l_{F(X)}}

\textstyle{F(X){\otimes}F(I)}
\textstyle{F(X{\otimes}I)}
\textstyle{F(X){\otimes}I}
\textstyle{F(X)}
\scriptstyle{1{\otimes}\iota}
\scriptstyle{F_{X,I}}
\scriptstyle{F(r_{X})}
\scriptstyle{r_{F(X)}}

(5)

commute for any
X\in{\cal C}
.

The
scalars
of a monoidal
{\cal C}
form the submonoid
{\cal C}_{s}(I,I)=\{c\in{\cal C}(I,I)|\ l_{X}(c{\otimes}1_{X})=r_{X}(1_{X}{\otimes}c)\ \forall X\in{\cal C}\}
of the monoid of endomorphisms
{\cal C}(I,I)
. By the Eckman-Hilton argument the monoid
{\cal C}(I,I)
is commutative.

For a monoidal
{\cal C}
the
opposite category

{\cal C}^{op}
(the category with morphism sets
{\cal C}^{op}(X,Y)={\cal C}(Y,X)
) is monoidal with
a^{op}_{X,Y,Z}=a_{X,Y,Z}^{-1},\ l^{op}_{X}=l_{X}^{-1},\ r^{op}_{X}=r_{X}^{-1}
.
The
reverse

{\cal C}^{rev}
of a monoidal
{\cal C}
is the category
{\cal C}
with a new tensor product
X{\otimes}^{rev}Y=Y{\otimes}X
and
a^{rev}_{X,Y,Z}=a_{Z,Y,X}^{-1},\ l^{rev}_{X}=r_{X},\ r^{rev}_{X}=l_{X}
.
We also write:
{\cal C}^{oprev}=(C^{rev})^{op}
.

3
Braided and symmetric monoidal categories

A monoidal category
{\cal C}
is
braided
if it comes equipped with a collection of isomorphisms

c_{X,Y}:X{\otimes}Y\to Y{\otimes}X
(
braidings
) natural in
X,Y\in{\cal C}
such that the diagrams

\textstyle{X{\otimes}(Y{\otimes}Z)}
\textstyle{(X{\otimes}Y){\otimes}Z}
\scriptstyle{a_{X,Y,Z}}
\textstyle{Z{\otimes}(X{\otimes}Y)}
\scriptstyle{c_{X{\otimes}Y,Z}}
\textstyle{(Z{\otimes}X){\otimes}Y}
\scriptstyle{a_{Z,X,Y}}
\textstyle{X{\otimes}(Z{\otimes}Y)}
\scriptstyle{1c_{Y,Z}}
\textstyle{(X{\otimes}Z){\otimes}Y}
\scriptstyle{a_{X,Z,Y}^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{c_{X,Z}1}

\textstyle{(X{\otimes}Y){\otimes}Z}
\textstyle{X{\otimes}(Y{\otimes}Z)}
\scriptstyle{a_{X,Y,Z}^{-1}}
\textstyle{(Y{\otimes}Z){\otimes}X}
\scriptstyle{c_{X,Y{\otimes}Z}}
\textstyle{Y{\otimes}(Z{\otimes}X)}
\scriptstyle{a_{Y,Z,X}^{-1}}
\textstyle{(Y{\otimes}X){\otimes}Z}
\scriptstyle{c_{X,Y}1}
\textstyle{Y{\otimes}(X{\otimes}Z)}
\scriptstyle{a_{Y,X,Z}^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{1c_{X,Z}}

(6)

commute for all
X,Y,Z\in{\cal C}
. It is also assumed that
c_{I,Y}=l_{Y}^{-1}r_{Y}
and
c_{X,I}=r_{X}^{-1}l_{X}
.

A monoidal functor
F:{\cal C}\to{\cal D}
between braided monoidal categories is
braided
if the diagram

\textstyle{F(X){\otimes}F(Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F_{X,Y}}
\scriptstyle{c_{F(X),F(Y)}}
\textstyle{F(X{\otimes}Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F(c_{X,Y})}
\textstyle{G(X){\otimes}G(Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{G_{X,Y}}
\textstyle{G(X{\otimes}Y)}

(7)

commutes for all
X,Y\in{\cal C}

[
Joyal and Street (1993)
]
.

The
conjugate

\overline{{\cal C}}
of a braided monoidal category
{\cal C}
is the monoidal category
{\cal C}
with the braiding

\overline{c}_{X,Y}=c_{Y,X}^{-1}
.

Example 3.1
.

For a monoidal category
{\cal C}
define
{\cal Z}({\cal C})
as the category of pairs
(Z,z)
, where
Z\in{\cal C}
together with a collection of isomorphisms
z_{X}:X{\otimes}Z\to Z{\otimes}X
natural in
X\in{\cal C}
(
half-braiding
) and such that the diagram obtained by replacing
c
with
z
in the first diagram of (
6
) commutes.
The category
{\cal Z}({\cal C})
is monoidal with the tensor product
(Z,z){\otimes}(W,w)=(Z{\otimes}W,z|w)
, where
(z|w)_{X}:X{\otimes}(Z{\otimes}W)\to(Z{\otimes}W){\otimes}X
is defined by a diagram similar to the second diagram of (
6
).
The monoidal category
{\cal Z}({\cal C})
is braided (with the braiding
c_{(Z,z),(W,w)}=z_{W}
)
and is known as the
monoidal
(or
Drinfeld
)
centre
of
{\cal C}

[
Joyal and Street (1991b)
,
Majid (1991)
]
.

A braided monoidal category
{\cal C}
is
symmetric
if
c_{Y,X}c_{X,Y}=1
for all
X,Y\in{\cal C}
.
The full subcategory
{\cal Z}_{sym}({\cal C})=\{X\in{\cal C}|\ c_{Y,X}c_{X,Y}=1\ \forall Y\in{\cal C}\}
is symmetric (the
symmetric or Mueger’s
centre of
{\cal C}
).

A braiding of
{\cal C}
can be thought of as the structure of a monoidal functor
{\cal C}\to{\cal C}^{rev}
on the identity functor
Id_{\cal C}
.
For a symmetric
{\cal C}
the composite
{\cal C}\to{\cal C}^{rev}\to{\cal C}
coincides with
Id_{\cal C}
as a monoidal functor.

More generally, a
balancing
of a braided monoidal
{\cal C}
is a monoidal isomorphism between the composite
{\cal C}\to{\cal C}^{rev}\to{\cal C}
and
Id_{\cal C}

[
Joyal and Street (1993)
]
. The group
Aut_{\otimes}(Id_{\cal C})
of monoidal automorphisms of the identity functor acts freely on the set of possible balancings of a balanced category.

Not all structures of a monoidal functor
{\cal C}\to{\cal C}^{rev}
on the identity functor
Id_{\cal C}
come from braidings of
{\cal C}
.
A monoidal
{\cal C}
together with a structure of monoidal functor
{\cal C}\to{\cal C}^{rev}
on the identity functor
Id_{\cal C}
such that the composite
{\cal C}\to{\cal C}^{rev}\to{\cal C}
coincides with
Id_{\cal C}
as a monoidal functor was called
coboundary
in
[
Drinfeld (1989)
]
.

4
Rigid monoidal categories

The
(left) internal hom

[X,Y]
of objects
X,Y
of a semi-groupal category
{\cal C}
is the terminal object
([X,Y],ev)
in the category of pairs
(T,\tau)
where
T
is an object and
\tau:T{\otimes}X\to Y
is a morphism in
{\cal C}
correspondingly.
Equivalently,
[X,-]
is the right adjoint to the functor
-{\otimes}X
, i.e. there is a collection of isomorphisms

{\cal C}(T,[X,Y])\simeq{\cal C}(T{\otimes}X,Y)
natural in
X,Y,T\in{\cal C}
.
The universal property of the internal hom gives rise to morphisms

[Y,Z]{\otimes}[X,Y]\longrightarrow[X,Z],\qquad X{\otimes}[Y,Z]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}[Y,X{\otimes}Z],\qquad[X,[Y,Z]]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}[X{\otimes}Y,Z]

natural in
X,Y,Z\in{\cal C}
.
A semi-groupal category
{\cal C}
is
(left) closed
if
[X,Y]
exists for any
X,Y\in{\cal C}

[
Eilenberg and Kelly (1966)
]
.

Remark 4.1
.

The existence of
[X,-]
, as a right adjoint, can e.g. be deduced from the adjoint functor theorems (see
[
1
]
).
If
[X,-]
exists the functor
-{\otimes}X
is a left adjoint and hence preserves colimits. Thus the tensor product of a closed category preserves colimits (in both arguments).

In a monoidal category
{\cal C}
one has
[I,X]\simeq X
for any
X\in{\cal C}
.
A monoidal category
{\cal C}
is
(left) rigid
of
(left) autonomous
if it is (left) closed and
[I,Y]{\otimes}[X,I]\longrightarrow[X,Y]
is an isomorphism for any
X,Y\in{\cal C}
. In this case
X^{*}=[X,I]
is called the
(left) dual
of
X
.
The (left) dual
X^{*}
of
X
is characterised by the existence of morphisms
ev:X^{*}{\otimes}X\to I,coev:I\to X{\otimes}X^{*}
such that
(coev{\otimes}1)(1{\otimes}ev)=1_{X}
and
(1{\otimes}coev)(ev{\otimes}1)=1_{X^{*}}
(here we suppress associators). Such morphisms are unique up to an automorphism of
I
(see
[
Saavedra Rivano (1972)
]
).

Example 4.2
.

The (left) dual to an endofunctor
F
as an object of the monoidal category
{\cal E}nd({\cal M})
is its left adjoint.
Thus a left rigid
F\in{\cal E}nd({\cal M})
preserves limits and a right rigid endofunctor preserves colimits in
{\cal M}
.

Remark 4.3
.

A monoidal functor
F:{\cal C}\to{\cal D}
preserves duals
F(X^{*})=F(X)^{*}
.
Thus for a rigid
{\cal C}
the proper and full images
{\cal I}m(F)
and
{\cal I}m_{f}(F)
of a monoidal functor are rigid.

Remark 4.4
.

The monoidal functor
{\cal C}\to{\cal E}nd({\cal C})
sending
X
to the endofunctor
Y\mapsto X{\otimes}Y
sends rigid object to functors preserving limits and colimits. Thus the tensor product of a rigid category preserves limits and colimits (in both arguments).

For rigid
{\cal C}
the assignment
X\mapsto X^{*}
extends to morphisms and defines a monoidal equivalence
{\cal C}\to{\cal C}^{oprev}
.
A
pivotal structure
on a rigid monoidal
{\cal C}
is a monoidal isomorphism
\tau
between
Id_{\cal C}
and the composite
(-)^{**}:{\cal C}\to{\cal C}^{oprev}\to{\cal C}

[
Freyd and Yetter(1989)
]
.
The group
Aut_{\otimes}(Id_{\cal C})
acts freely on the set of pivotal structures of a pivotal category.

For an endomorphism
f:X\to X
of a pivotal category
{\cal C}
the composite
tr(f)=ev_{X^{*}}(\tau_{X}f{\otimes}1)coev_{X}
is an endomorphism of
I
called the
trace
of
f
(here we suppress associators).
A pivotal category
{\cal C}
is
spherical
if
tr(f^{*})=tr(f)
for any endomorphism
f
in
{\cal C}

[
Barrett and Westbury (1999)
]
.

5
Module categories

A category
{\cal M}
is a
(left) module
category over a monoidal category
{\cal C}
if it comes equipped with a monoidal functor
{\cal C}\to{\cal E}nd({\cal M})
. Equivalently, a
{\cal C}
-module structure of
{\cal M}
is encoded in a functor

{\cal C}\times{\cal M}\to{\cal M},\ (X,M)\mapsto X*M
.
The monoidal constraint of
{\cal C}\to{\cal E}nd({\cal M})
corresponds to a collection of isomorphisms (the
action associativity constraint
)
a_{X,Y,M}:X*(Y*M)\to(X{\otimes}Y)*M
natural in
X,Y\in{\cal C}
,
M\in{\cal M}
,
such that the diagrams similar to (
1
),(
4
) commute.
A functor
F:{\cal M}\to{\cal N}
between
{\cal C}
-module categories is
{\cal C}
-module functor
if it comes equipped with a collection of isomorphisms (the
module constraint
)
F_{X,M}:F(X)*F(M)\to F(X*M)
natural in
X\in{\cal C}
,
M\in{\cal M}
,
such that the diagrams similar to (
2
),(
5
) commute.
A natural transformation
c:F\to G
between
{\cal C}
-module functors
F,G:{\cal M}\to{\cal N}
is
{\cal C}
-module
if the diagram similar to (
3
) commutes.

Clearly composites of
{\cal C}
-module functors and natural transformations are
{\cal C}
-module.
In particular,
{\cal C}
-module endofunctors of a
{\cal C}
-module category
{\cal M}
together with
{\cal C}
-module natural transformations form a strict monoidal category
{\cal E}nd_{{\cal C}{\text{-}}}({\cal M})
.

Example 5.1
.

The tensor product can be seen as an action of a monoidal category
{\cal C}
on itself (the
regular
left
{\cal C}
-module category).
The assignment
F\mapsto F(I)
is a monoidal equivalence
{\cal E}nd_{{\cal C}{\text{-}}}({\cal C})\to{\cal C}
. Thus any monoidal category is equivalent to a strict one (Mac Lane’s coherence theorem).

Similarly one defines right
{\cal C}
-module and
{\cal C}
-bimodule categories.
For a
{\cal C}
-bimodule category
{\cal M}
denote by
{\cal E}nd_{{\cal C}{\text{-}}{\cal C}}({\cal M})
the strict monoidal category of
{\cal C}
-bimodule endofunctors.

Example 5.2
.

The tensor product turns a monoidal category
{\cal C}
into a bimodule over itself.
The assignment
F\mapsto F(I)
extends to a monoidal equivalence
{\cal E}nd_{{\cal C}{\text{-}}{\cal C}}({\cal C})\to{\cal Z}({\cal C})
with the
{\cal C}
-bimodule structure of
F
corresponding to the half braiding on
F(I)
.

The notion of internal hom extends to module categories.
The
internal action hom

[M,N]_{\cal M}
of objects
M,N
of a (left)
{\cal C}
-module category
{\cal M}
is the terminal object
([M,N]_{\cal M},ev)
in the category of pairs
(T,\tau)
where
T\in{\cal C}
and
\tau:T*M\to N
is a morphism in
{\cal M}
.
Equivalently,
[M,-]_{\cal M}
is the right adjoint to the functor
-*M
, i.e. there is a collection of isomorphisms

{\cal C}(T,[M,N]_{\cal M})\simeq{\cal M}(T*M,N)
natural in
M,N\in{\cal M},T\in{\cal C}

[
Ostrik (2003)
]
.
The universal property of the internal hom gives rise to morphisms

[M,N]_{\cal M}{\otimes}[L,M]_{\cal M}\longrightarrow[L,N]_{\cal M},\qquad X{\otimes}[M,N]_{\cal M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}[M,X*N]_{\cal M},\qquad[X,[M,N]_{\cal M}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}[X*M,N]_{\cal M}

natural in
L,M,N\in{\cal M},X\in{\cal C}
.
For rigid
{\cal C}
the second morphism is an isomorphism.

6
Internal algebra

The language of algebras and their modules internal to a monoidal category is a useful tool for defining and computing with module categories and for constructing braided monoidal categories.

An
associative unital algebra
in a monoidal category
{\cal C}
is a triple
(A,\mu,\iota)
consisting of an object
A\in{\cal C}
together with a
multiplication

\mu:A\otimes A\to A
and a
unit
map
\iota:I\to A
, satisfying the
associativity

\mu\left(\mu\otimes 1_{A}\right)=\mu\left(1_{A}\otimes\mu\right),
and the
unit

\mu\left(\iota\otimes 1_{A}\right)=1_{A}=\mu\left(1_{A}\otimes\iota\right)
axioms.

A
right module
over an algebra
A
is a pair
(M,\nu)
, where
M
is an object of
{\cal C}
and
\nu:M\otimes A\to M
is a morphism (
action map
), such that
\nu\left(\nu\otimes 1_{A}\right)=\nu\left(1_{M}\otimes\mu\right).
A
homomorphism
of right
A
-modules
M\to N
is a morphism
f:M\to N
in
{\cal C}
such that
\nu_{N}\left(f\otimes 1_{A}\right)=f\nu_{M}.

Right modules over an algebra
A\in{\cal C}
together with module homomorphisms form a category
{\cal C}_{A}
. The forgetful functor
{\cal C}_{A}\to{\cal C}
has a left adjoint, which sends an object
X\in{\cal C}
into the
free
A
-module

X\otimes A
, with
1_{X}{\otimes}\mu
as the
A
-module structure. More generally, for
X\in{\cal C}
and
M\in{\cal C}_{A}
the tensor product
X{\otimes}M
is a right
A
-module under
1_{X}{\otimes}\nu_{M}
.
This categorical pairing is a left
C
-module action on
{\cal C}_{A}
. The free
A
-module functor
-{\otimes}A:{\cal C}\to{\cal C}_{A}
and the forgetful functor
{\cal C}_{A}\to{\cal C}
are
C
-module functors.

Conversely, let
M
be an object of a
{\cal C}
-module category
{\cal M}
such that internal action homs
[M,-]_{\cal M}
exist,
Then
[M,M]_{\cal M}
is an algebra and
[M,N]_{\cal M}
is a right
[M,M]_{\cal M}
-module in
{\cal C}
.
For rigid
{\cal C}
the functor
[M,-]_{\cal M}:{\cal M}\to{\cal C}_{[M,M]_{\cal M}}
is
{\cal C}
-module and the free
[M,M]_{\cal M}
-module functor factorises

\textstyle{{\cal C}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{-*M}
\textstyle{{\cal C}_{[M,M]_{\cal M}}}
\textstyle{{\cal M}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{[M,-]_{\cal M}}

An algebra
A
in a braided monoidal category is
commutative
if
\mu c_{A,A}=\mu
.
For
M,N\in{\cal C}_{A}
denote by
M{\otimes}_{A}N
the coequaliser of
\nu_{M}1,(1\nu_{N})(1c_{A,N}):M{\otimes}A{\otimes}N\to M{\otimes}N
.
When these coequalisers exist for all
M,N\in{\cal C}_{A}
the category
{\cal C}_{A}
becomes monoidal with the tensor product
{\otimes}_{A}

[
Pareigis (1995)
]
. The free-module functor
{\cal C}\to{\cal C}_{A}
is monoidal and
central
, i.e. lifts to a braided monoidal functor
{\cal C}\to{\cal Z}({\cal C}_{A})
.

Conversely for a central functor
F:{\cal C}\to{\cal D}
with the right adjoint
R:{\cal D}\to{\cal C}
the object
R(I)\in{\cal C}
has a structure of commutative algebra. The functor
F
induces a monoidal functor
\tilde{F}:{\cal C}_{R(I)}\to{\cal D}
and
F=\tilde{F}\circ(-{\otimes}R(I))

[
Davydov et al (2013)
]
. Note that the algebra
R(I)
coincides with the internal action hom
[I,I]
for the
{\cal C}
-action on
{\cal D}
defined by the functor
F
.

Example 6.1
.

For a
{\cal C}
-module category
{\cal M}
there is defined a central monoidal functor
{\cal Z}(C)\to{\cal E}nd_{\cal C}({\cal M})
(called
\alpha
-induction
). The corresponding internal action hom
[Id_{\cal M},Id_{\cal M}]
is a commutative algebra
Z({\cal M})
in
{\cal Z}(C)
called the

of
{\cal M}
.
For
{\cal M}={\cal C}_{A}
the full centre
Z(A)=Z({\cal C}_{A})
is the terminal object of the category of the pairs
(Z,\zeta)
, where
Z\in{\cal Z}({\cal C})
and
\zeta:Z\to A
is a morphism in
{\cal C}
such that
\mu(\zeta{\otimes}1_{A})=\mu(1_{A}{\otimes}\zeta)z_{A}
(where
z_{A}
is the half-braiding of

)
[
Fröhlich et al (2006)
,
Davydov (2010)
]
.

More generally for a commutative algebra

the coequaliser

(with

]
.

For closed
{\cal C}
the category of

is also closed with the internal hom

being by the equaliser of
[1,\widetilde{\nu}_{N}],[\nu_{M},1]:[M,N]\to[M,[A,N]]

\widetilde{\nu}_{N}:N\to[A,N]

.
For a rigid
{\cal C}

is also rigid if the algebra
A

]
.

For a commutative algebra
A

.

7
Linear and abelian monoidal categories

It is convenient when dealing with additive and linear categories to use the language of categories
enriched
in a symmetric monoidal category
{\cal V}
, or
{\cal V}
-category
.
In a
{\cal V}
-category
{\cal A}
homs
{\cal A}(X,Y)
are not sets but rather objects of
{\cal V}
and composition is a morphism
{\cal A}(Y,Z){\otimes}_{\cal V}{\cal A}(X,Y)\to{\cal A}(X,Z)
(see
[
Kelly (1982)
]
for all details).
For
{\cal V}
being the category of sets
{\cal S}et
,
{\cal V}
-category is just a category.
When
{\cal V}
is the category of abelian groups,
{\cal V}
-category is an additive category (
{\cal A}(X,Y)
is an abelian group and composition is bilinear).
When
{\cal V}
is the category
{\cal V}{\it ect}
of vector spaces over a fixed field
k
,
{\cal V}
-category is a linear category (
{\cal A}(X,Y)
is a vector space and composition is bilinear).

A
{\cal V}
-category
{\cal C}
is
monoidal
if it comes equipped with a
{\cal V}
-functor
{\cal C}\boxtimes_{\cal V}{\cal C}\to{\cal C}
together with an associativity constraint and the unit object. Here
{\cal A}\boxtimes_{\cal V}{\cal B}
is the
{\cal V}
-category with objects
Ob({\cal A}\boxtimes_{\cal V}{\cal B})=Ob({\cal A})\times Ob({\cal B})
and homs
({\cal A}\boxtimes_{\cal V}{\cal B})((X,Z),(Y,W))={\cal A}(X,Y){\otimes}_{\cal V}{\cal B}(Z,W)
.
For example in a monoidal linear category the tensor product on morphisms is given by bilinear maps
{\cal C}(X,Y){\otimes}_{k}{\cal C}(Z,W)\to{\cal C}(X{\otimes}Z,Y{\otimes}W)
.
The addition operation on morphisms can be quite useful.
For an additive functor
F:{\cal C}\to{\cal D}
the proper image
{\cal I}m(F)
can be described as the quotient
{\cal C}/Ker(F)
by the
ideal

Ker(F)
of all morphisms mapped by
F
to zero. When the functor
F
is monoidal the ideal
Ker(F)
is also
monoidal
, i.e.
Ker(F)
is closed not just under compositions but also under tensor products with arbitrary morphisms.

Example 7.1
.

In a spherical linear category negligible morphisms form a monoidal ideal
N
. A morphism
f:X\to Y
is
negligible
if

tr(fg)=0
for any
g:Y\to X

[
Turaev (1994)
,
Barrett and Westbury (1999)
]
.

A
pre-abelian
category is an additive category with finite limits and colimits (
finitely bicomplete
).
A pre-abelian category is
abelian
if any monomorphism is a kernel and any epimorphism is a cokernel.

For (co)complete
{\cal V}
(with (co)continuous tensor product) the category
\widehat{\cal A}={\cal F}unct_{\cal V}({\cal A}^{op},{\cal V})
of
{\cal V}
-functors (the
{\cal V}
-presheaf category
) is also (co)complete.
The
Yoneda embedding

Y:{\cal A}\to\widehat{\cal A}
sending
X
to the functor
{\cal A}(X,-)
is fully faithful.

For a monoidal
{\cal V}
-category
{\cal C}
the presheaf category
\widehat{\cal C}
is monoidal with respect to the

Day convolution
defined as the coend
(U{\otimes}V)(X)=\int^{Y,Z\in{\cal C}}{\cal C}(X,Y{\otimes}Z){\otimes}_{\cal V}U(Y){\otimes}_{\cal V}V(Z)

[
Day (1970)
]
.
If
{\cal V}
is closed the monoidal presheaf category
\widehat{\cal C}
is also closed with the internal hom
given by the end
[U,V](X)=\int_{Y\in{\cal C}}[U(Y),V(X{\otimes}Y)]_{\cal V}
.

Example 7.2
.

For a linear category
{\cal A}
the presheaf category
\widehat{\cal A}
is abelian.

An abelian linear monoidal category
{\cal C}
is
tensor
if it is rigid and the identity object
I
is simple and
{\cal C}(I,I)=k
.
Here an object is
simple
if all morphisms out of it are monomorphisms and morphism in it are epimorphisms.

8
Finite tensor and fusion categories

A tensor category is
finite
if all hom-spaces are finite dimensional and any object has a finite length (i.e. has a finite filtration with simple factors). As an abelian category a finite tensor category is equivalent to the category of finite dimensional modules over a finite dimensional algebra.
As the result a finite tensor category is finitely complete and cocomplete, and a tensor functor between finite tensor categories has left and right adjoints.
In particular, internal action homs for a finite module category exist.

The
categorical dimension

dim({\cal C})
of a finite tensor category is the dimension
d(Z(I))
of the full centre of the trivial algebra
I\in{\cal C}
.
The categorical dimension has the following properties:

dim(Z({\cal C}))=dim({\cal C})^{2}
,
dim({\cal C}_{A})d(A)=dim({\cal C})
and
dim({\cal C}_{A}^{loc})d(A)^{2}=dim({\cal C})
for a separable commutative algebra
A\in{\cal Z}({\cal C})
such that
{\cal C}(I,A)=k

[
Fröhlich et al (2006)
,
Etingof et al (2015)
]
.

A finite tensor category
{\cal C}
is
incompressible
if any tensor functor
{\cal C}\to{\cal D}
into a tensor category is fully faithful (see
[
Coulembier et al (2023)
]
and references therein).
For an incompressible finite tensor category
{\cal C}
there should be no non-trivial separable commutative algebras
A
in
{\cal Z}({\cal C})
with
{\cal C}(I,A)=k
.

Example 8.1
.

An incompressible symmetric tensor category in zero characteristic is either the category of vector spaces
{\cal V}{\it ect}
or its super-analog
s{\cal V}{\it ect}

[
Deligne (2002)
]
.
A symmetric tensor category
{\cal C}
is
Tannakian
if there is a faithful symmetric tensor functor
{\cal C}\to{\cal V}ect
and
super-Tannakian
if there is a faithful symmetric tensor functor
{\cal C}\to s{\cal V}ect
.

The structure of incompressible symmetric tensor categories in finite characteristic is much richer (see
[
Coulembier et al (2023)
]
and references therein).

For an incompressible braided finite tensor category
{\cal C}
there should be no non-trivial separable commutative algebras
A
in
{\cal C}
with
{\cal C}(I,A)=k
(
complete anisotropy
of
{\cal C}
, see
[
Davydov et al (2013)
]
).

Example 8.2
.

A braided tensor category
{\cal C}
is
non-degenerate
if
{\cal Z}_{sym}({\cal C})={\cal V}{\it ect}
and is
slightly-degenerate
if
{\cal Z}_{sym}({\cal C})=s{\cal V}{\it ect}
.

Completely anisotropic braided finite tensor categories are either non-degenerate or slightly-degenerate.

A finite tensor category is
fusion
if it is semi-simple, i.e. any object is a direct sum of simple objects.

Example 8.3
.

Equivalence classes of completely anisotropic non-degenerate braided fusion categories form a group, the so-called
Witt group

[
Davydov et al (2013)
]
.
The same is true for slightly-degenerate braided fusion categories.

9
Example: pointed categories

Let
G
be a semi-group.
Denote by
{\cal V}(G)
the category of vector spaces (over a field
k
) graded by
G
with grading preserving linear maps as morphisms.
Note that it is a semi-simple category (any object is a direct product of simple objects) with simple objects being one dimensional vector space
I(g)=k
concentrated in the a single degree
g\in G
.
It is also a monoidal category with the tensor product of vector spaces graded by
(U{\otimes}_{k}V)_{f}={\oplus}_{gh=f}U_{g}{\otimes}_{k}V_{h}
.
The unit object
I
is the one dimensional vector space concentrated in the trivial degree
I=I(e)
.
It follows from the naturality that the general form of an associativity constraint on homogeneous
u\in U_{f},v\in V_{g},w\in W_{h}
is
a_{U,V,W}(u{\otimes}(v{\otimes}w))=\alpha(f,g,h)(u{\otimes}v){\otimes}w
, where
\alpha(f,g,h)\in k^{\times}
are non-zero scalars.
The coherence (
1
) is equivalent to the function
\alpha:G^{\times 3}\to k^{\times}
being a 3-cocycle of
G
with coefficients in the trivial
G
-module
k^{\times}
.
Denote by
{\cal V}(G,\alpha)
the category of
G
-vector spaces with the associator corresponding to a 3-cocycle
\alpha\in Z^{3}(G,k^{\times})
.

The category
{\cal V}(G,\alpha)
is closed with the left internal hom
[U,V]=Hom_{k}(U,V)
being the space of linear maps with the grading
[U,V]_{f}=\{l|\ l(U_{g})\subset V_{fg}\}
(the right internal hom
\{U,V\}=Hom_{k}(U,V)
is graded by
\{U,V\}_{f}=\{l|\ l(U_{g})\subset V_{gf}\}
). A graded vector space
U
is dualisable if and only if
U
is finite dimensional and supported on invertible elements of
G
.
For a (finite) group
G
the subcategory
{\cal V}_{fd}(G,\alpha)
of finite dimensional graded vector spaces is rigid and fusion (a so-called
pointed fusion category
).

A monoidal functor
F:{\cal V}(G,\alpha)\to{\cal V}(Q,\beta)
corresponds to a group homomorphism
\varphi:G\to Q
(the effect of
F
on simple objects) and a 2-cochain
\phi\in C^{2}(G,k^{\times})
(defining the monoidal constraint). Explicitly,
F(U)_{q}={\oplus}_{\varphi(g)=q}U_{g}
with the monoidal constraint
F_{U,V}(u{\otimes}v)=\phi(f,g)u{\otimes}v
on
u\in U_{f},v\in V_{g}
.
The coherence (
2
) is equivalent to the coboundary condition
d(\phi)=\alpha^{-1}\varphi^{*}(\beta)
in
C^{*}(G,k^{\times})
.

Let
X
be a
G
-set with the action
G\times X\to X,\ (g,x)\mapsto g.x
.
The category
{\cal M}(X)
of
X
-graded vector spaces is a module category over
{\cal V}(G)
with the categorical action
U*M=U{\otimes}_{k}M
graded by
(U*M)_{x}={\oplus}_{g.y=x}U_{g}{\otimes}_{k}M_{y}
and the identity associativity constraint.
To make the category of
X
-graded vector spaces a module category over
{\cal V}(G,\alpha)
one needs a coboundary
\xi\in C^{2}(G,Map(X,k^{\times}))
for
\alpha
considered an element of
Z^{3}(G,k^{\times})\subset Z^{3}(G,Map(X,k^{\times}))
. Here
Map(X,k^{\times})
is the multiplicative group of maps
X\to k^{\times}
with the
G
-action coming from the
G
-set structure. Such
\xi
defines an action associativity constraint
U*(V*M)\to(U{\otimes}V)*M,\ \ u{\otimes}(v{\otimes}m)\mapsto\xi(f,g,x)(u{\otimes}v){\otimes}m
with
u\in U_{f},v\in V_{g},m\in M_{x}
. The action coherence is equivalent to the equation
d(\xi)=\alpha
.
The
{\cal V}(G,\alpha)
-module category
{\cal M}(X,\xi)
is action closed with the action internal hom
[M,N]={\oplus}_{g}[M,N]_{g}
where
[M,N]_{g}=\{l\in Hom_{k}(M,N)|\ l(U_{x})\subset V_{g.x}\}
.
Note that any semi-simple module category over
{\cal V}(G,\alpha)
is (equivalent to) such
{\cal M}(X,\xi)
for some
(X,\xi)
(with
X
being the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects).
Indecomposable semi-simple
{\cal V}(G,\alpha)
-module categories correspond to transitive
G
-sets and have the form
{\cal M}(G/H,\xi)
for a subgroup
H\subset G
. Shapiro’s lemma allows one to replace
\xi\in C^{2}(G,Map(G/H,k^{\times}))
by
\tilde{\xi}\in C^{2}(H,k^{\times})
with
d(\tilde{\xi})=\alpha|_{H}
.
Categories of module endofunctors
{\cal E}nd_{{\cal V}(G,\alpha)}({\cal M}(G/H,\xi))
are called
group-theoretical
(see
[
Etingof et al (2015)
]
and references therein). For example,
{\cal E}nd_{{\cal V}(G,1)}({\cal M}(G/G,1))
is the category
{\cal R}ep(G)
of representations of
G
.

An associative algebra in
{\cal V}(G,\alpha)
is a
G
-graded vector space
A={\oplus}_{g}A_{g}
with a multiplication, which is
graded
(or
\alpha
-)
associative
, i.e.
a(bc)=\alpha(f,g,h)(ab)c
for any
a\in A_{f},b\in A_{g},c\in A_{h}
. The subalgebra
A_{e}={\cal V}(G,\alpha)(I,A)
is an associative algebra in
{\cal V}ect
.
For example, the action internal end
[I(x),I(x)]
for a simple object
I(x)\in{\cal M}(X,\xi)
is supported on the stabiliser
H=St_{G}(x)
and coincides with the twisted group algebra
k[H,\tilde{\xi}]
. The category of modules
{\cal V}(G,\alpha)_{k[H,\tilde{\xi}]}
is equivalent to
{\cal M}(G/H,\xi)
as a
{\cal V}(G,\alpha)
-module category.

The monoidal centre
{\cal Z}({\cal V}(G,1))
is equivalent to the category with objects,
G
-graded vector spaces
Z={\oplus}_{g}Z_{g}
with a linear
G
-action compatible via
f(Z_{g})=Z_{fgf^{-1}}
. The tensor product is the standard one.
The half-braiding of
Z
with a
G
-graded vector space
V
is given by
z_{U}(u{\otimes}z)=f.z{\otimes}u
for
u\in U_{f}
.
The monoidal centre
{\cal Z}({\cal V}(G,\alpha))
has a similar model (see e.g.
[
Davydov and Simmons (2013)
]
for details).

The category
{\cal V}(G,\alpha)
affords a braiding only if the group
G
is abelian.
The naturality restricts the general form of a braiding to
c_{U,V}(u{\otimes}v)=\gamma(f,g)v{\otimes}u
for
u\in U_{f},v\in V_{g}
, with some
\gamma(f,g)\in k^{\times}
.
The coherence (
6
) is equivalent to
(\alpha,\gamma)
being an
abelian
3-cocycle (a 3-cocycle of the Eilenberg-Mac Lane cohomology complex
C^{*}(G,2,k^{\times})
).
The coherence (
7
) for the monoidal constraint of a braded monoidal functor
F:{\cal V}(G,\alpha,\gamma)\to{\cal V}(Q,\beta,\xi)
is equivalent to the coboundary condition
d(\phi)=(\alpha,\gamma)^{-1}\varphi^{*}(\beta,\xi)
in
C^{*}(G,2,k^{\times})
.
In particular, up to a braided equivalence
{\cal V}(G,\alpha,\gamma)
depends only on the cohomology class
[(\alpha,\gamma)]\in H^{3}(G,2,k^{\times})
. Eilenberg and Mac Lane proved that the assignment
(\alpha,\gamma)\mapsto q(g)=\gamma(g,g)
gives an isomorphism between
H^{3}(G,2,k^{\times})
and the group
Q(G,k^{\times})
of
quadratic
functions, i.e. functions
q:G\to k^{\times}
such that
q(g^{-1})=q(g)
and
q(f)q(g)q(h)q(fgh)=q(fg)q(fh)q(gh)
(see e.g.
[
Joyal and Street (1991b)
,
Davydov et al (2013)
]
for details and references). It is convenient to denote (the braided equivalence class of)
{\cal V}(G,\alpha,\gamma)
by
{\cal C}(G,q)
. For example, the conjugate
\overline{{\cal C}(G,q)}
is
{\cal C}(G,q^{-1})
and the symmetric centre
{\cal Z}_{sym}({\cal C}(G,q))={\cal C}(Ker(q),1)
, where
Ker(q)=\{g\in G|\ q(fg)=q(f)\ \forall f\in G\}
is the
kernel
of the quadratic function
q
. The braided linear category
{\cal C}(G,q)
is non-degenerate whenever the quadratic function
q
is.

The twisted group algebra
k[H,\tilde{\xi}]
in
{\cal C}(G,q)
is commutative if and only if
H
is an
isotropic
subgroup, i.e.
q|_{H}=1
. Since
\tilde{\xi}
is uniquely determined by
H
we denote the commutative algebra
k[H,\tilde{\xi}]
by
A(H)
.
The category
{\cal C}(G,q)_{A(H)}^{loc}
of local
A(H)
-modules is equivalent to
{\cal C}(H^{\perp}/H,q)
, where
H^{\perp}=\{g\in G|\ q(fg)=q(f)\ \forall f\in H\}
is the
orthogonal complement
of
H
.
The braided linear category
{\cal C}(G,q)
is completely anisotropic whenever the pair
G,q)
is, i.e. whenever
G
does not have proper isotropic subgroups.
The subgroup of the Witt group generated by braided pointed fusion categories is the classical Witt group of quadratic functions studied by C.T.C. Wall) see
[
Davydov et al (2013)
]
for details).

10
Conclusions

The development of the theory of tensor categories was driven by the applications in
representation theory (resulting in a good understanding of Tannakian categories
[
Deligne and Milne (1982)
]
),
high energy and condensed matter physics (bringing fusion categories to the centre of attension).
Concepts crucial for the emergent theory of tensor categories came from or play an important role in these applications:
non-degenerate braided fusion categories (representation categories of chiral algebras), module categories (categories of boundary data), full centres (full state spaces of conformal field theories, modular invariants), Witt equivalence (boson condensation, defects in 2d conformal field theories).

Interest to non-rational conformal field theories in two dimensions stimulates the study of more general (finite) tensor categories.

Higher categorical analogues of tensor categories (fusion 2-categories) play an important role in 4d topological field theories.
Progress in their study is impossible without better understanding of their partial decategorifications.
Thus it is natural to expect further developments in the area of (finite set-theoretical) monoidal categories.

Due to the space limitation many important references are not listed here.

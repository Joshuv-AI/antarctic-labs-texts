A decomposition theorem for the affine Springer fibers
Zongbin Chen

Abstract
According to Laumon, an affine Springer fiber is homeomorphic to the universal abelian covering of the compactified Jacobian of a spectral curve. We construct equivariant deformations f n : 𝒫 ¯ n → ℬ n f_{n}:\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{n}\to\mathcal{B}_{n} of the finite abelian coverings of this compactified Jacobian, and decompose the complex R ​ f n , ∗ ​ 𝐐 ℓ Rf_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell} as direct sum of intersection complexes. Pass to the limit, we obtain a similar expression for the homology of the affine Springer fibers. A quite surprising consequence is that we can reduce the homology to its Λ 0 \Lambda^{0} -invariant subspace. As an application, we get a sheaf-theoretic reformulation of the purity hypothesis of Goresky, Kottwitz and MacPherson. In an attempt to solve it, we propose a conjecture about the punctural weight of the intermediate extension of a smooth ℓ \ell -adic sheaf of pure weight.

Corollary 3
.

The homology of the affine Springer fiber is pure in the sense of Grothendieck-Deligne if and only if the group

\bigoplus_{i^{\prime}=0}^{i}{\rm Im}\Big\{\mathcal{H}^{2\tau_{\gamma}-(i-i^{\prime})}_{\{0\}}\big(j_{!*}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{E}^{\perp})^{\vee}\big)\to\mathcal{H}^{2\tau_{\gamma}-(i-i^{\prime})}_{\{0\}}\big(j_{!*}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\big)\Big\}

is pure of weight
-i
for any
i\in\mathbf{N}_{0}
.

Let

be an irreducible smooth quasi-projective algebraic variety over a finite field

be the inclusion of an open subvariety, let

Let

be the strict Henselization of
X
at

. Let

be a geometric generic point of

Conjecture 4

Under the above setting, suppose that

is puncturally pure of weight

is semisimple, then the fiber

2.1
Laumon’s work

In an attempt to deform the affine Springer fibers, Laumon
[
L
]
discovered a remarkable relationship between the affine Springer fibers and the compactified Jacobians of certain algebraic curves.
By definition, the affine Springer fiber

parametrizes the lattices

such that

-modules of finite type in

-modules can be globalized.
Let
C_{\gamma}
be an irreducible projective algebraic curve over
\mathbf{C}

(i)

has unique singularity at
c

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{\gamma},\,c}\cong\mathcal{O}[\gamma]

to
\infty\in C_{\gamma}
.

The curve

will be called the

of

.
Let

\mathcal{O}[\gamma]
-modules of finite type in

.
Similar to the obvious fact that
\mathcal{O}_{C_{\gamma}}
can be obtained by gluing

-module

of generic rank

2.2
Ngô’s support theorem

Let
\pi:(\mathcal{C},C_{\gamma})\to({\mathcal{B}},0)
be an algebraization of a miniversal deformation of
C_{\gamma}
, i.e.
\pi:\mathcal{C}\to{\mathcal{B}}
is a proper flat family of curves with geometrically integral fibers such that the restriction of
\pi
to the formal neighborhood
\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}_{0}
of
0\in{\mathcal{B}}
is a miniversal deformation of
C_{\gamma}
.
The generic fiber of
\pi
is then an irreducible smooth projective algebraic curve of genus
\delta_{\gamma}=\delta(C_{\gamma})
.
Let

f:\overline{\mathcal{P}}=\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}{}^{\,0}_{\mathcal{C}/{\mathcal{B}}}\to{\mathcal{B}}

be the relative compactified Jacobian of the family
\pi:\mathcal{C}\to{\mathcal{B}}
. The morphism
f
is proper flat with geometrically integral fibers, and it is smooth whenever the fiber of
\pi
is. As
f^{-1}(0)=\overline{P}_{C_{\gamma}}
, we get a natural deformation of
\overline{P}_{C_{\gamma}}
.

According to Fantechi-Göttsche-van Straten
[
FGS
]
, the total space
\overline{\mathcal{P}}
is smooth over
k
.
As the morphism
f:\overline{\mathcal{P}}\to{\mathcal{B}}

is proper, the complex
Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
is pure by Deligne’s Weil-II
[
D2
]
. The decomposition theorem of Beilinson, Bernstein, Deligne and Gabber
[
BBDG
]
then implies that

Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{2\delta_{\gamma}}{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{n}(Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})[-n].

Theorem 2.4

(Ngô
[
N
]
)
.

We have decomposition

Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{2\delta_{\gamma}}j_{!*}(R^{n}f^{\mathrm{sm}}_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})[-n],

where
j:{\mathcal{B}}^{\rm sm}\to{\mathcal{B}}
is the natural inclusion. In particular,

H^{n}\big(\overline{P}_{C_{\gamma}},\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}\big)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\mathcal{H}^{n-i}\big(j_{!*}{\textstyle{\bigwedge^{i}}}R^{1}f^{\mathrm{sm}}_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}\big)_{0},\hskip 10.00002ptn=0,\cdots,2\delta_{\gamma}.

We make a brief recall of Ngô’s proof. Let
X
be a scheme of finite type over
k
, then any simple perverse sheaf on
X
is of the form

i_{x,*}\mathrm{IC}_{\overline{\{x\}}}(\mathcal{L}_{x})[{\rm d}_{x}]

for some point (not necessarily closed)
x\in X
, and some simple
\ell
-adic local system
\mathcal{L}_{x}
on
\{x\}
, here

i_{x}:\overline{\{x\}}\hookrightarrow X
is the natural inclusion and
{\rm d}_{x}=\dim(\overline{\{x\}})
.
A complex
K\in\mathcal{D}_{c}^{b}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
is said to be
semisimple
if

K=\bigoplus_{i}{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{i}(K)[-i].

Then

K=\bigoplus_{i}\bigoplus_{x\in X}i_{x,*}\mathrm{IC}_{\overline{\{x\}}}(\mathcal{L}_{x}^{i})[{\rm d}_{x}-i]

for some points
x\in X
and some semisimple
\ell
-adic local system
\mathcal{L}_{x}^{i}
on
\{x\}
. The set

\operatorname{supp}(K):=\{x\in X\mid\mathcal{L}_{x}^{i}\neq 0\text{ for some }i\}

is called the
support
of
K
. Let

{\rm occ}_{x}(K)=\{i\mid\mathcal{L}_{x}^{i}\neq 0\},

and let

n_{x}^{+}(K)=\max\{i\mid\mathcal{L}_{x}^{i}\neq 0\},\hskip 10.00002ptn_{x}^{-}(K)=\min\{i\mid\mathcal{L}_{x}^{i}\neq 0\}.

The quantity

\mathrm{Amp}_{x}(K):=n_{x}^{+}(K)-n_{x}^{-}(K)

is called the
amplitude
of
K
at
x
. If
K
is auto-dual, then
n_{x}^{-}=-n_{x}^{+}
and
\mathrm{Amp}_{x}(K)=2n_{x}^{+}(K)
.

The relative Jacobian
\mathcal{P}=\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathcal{C}/{\mathcal{B}}}^{0}
acts naturally on
\overline{\mathcal{P}}
.
They form a
weak abelian fibration
in the sense of Ngô
[
N
]
.
Recall that such a fibration over a
k
-scheme
S
consists of a proper morphism
f:M\to S
and a smooth commutative group scheme
g:P\to S
, with an action

\mathrm{act}:P\times_{S}M\to M

satisfying the properties:

(1)

The morphism
f
and
g
have the same relative dimension
d
.

(2)

The action of
P
on
M
has only
affine
stabilizers.

(3)

Let
P^{0}
be the open sub group scheme of the neutral connected components of the fibers of
P
and let
g^{0}:P^{0}\to S
be the restriction of
g
to
P^{0}
, let

\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}(P^{0})=H^{-1}\big(g^{0}_{!}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}[2d](d)\big)

be the sheaf of the Tate modules of
P^{0}
, then
\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}(P^{0})
is
polarizable
, i.e. there exists an alternating bilinear form

\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}(P^{0})\times\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}(P^{0})\to\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}(1)

such that over any geometric point
\bar{s}
of
S
, the bilinear form has kernel
\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}(R_{\bar{s}})
and induces a perfect pairing for
\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}(A_{\bar{s}})
, where
R_{\bar{s}}
and
A_{\bar{s}}
are factors of the dévissage of Chevalley

1\to R_{\bar{s}}\to P^{0}_{\bar{s}}\to A_{\bar{s}}\to 1.

(2.9)

The key observation is that the abelian part
A_{\bar{s}}
of
P^{0}_{\bar{s}}
doesn’t contribute to the degeneration of the nearby fibers of
M_{\bar{s}}
to
M_{\bar{s}}
. In other words, it behaves as
A_{\bar{s}}
can be extended to an étale neighborhood of
\bar{s}
. More precisely, with the action
\mathrm{act}:P\times_{S}M\to M,

Ngô constructs a canonical morphism

\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}(P^{0})\otimes{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{n}(Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})\to{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}).

(2.10)

Let
K_{s}^{n}
be the direct sum of the simple perverse factors of
{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{n}(Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
with support
s\in S
, its restriction to an open subscheme
V_{s}
of
\overline{\{s\}}
is a
pure
local system denoted
\mathcal{K}^{n}_{s}[-n]
.
Let

\mathcal{K}_{s}=\bigoplus_{n}\mathcal{K}_{s}^{n}[-n].

Moreover, after further shrinking
V_{s}
, we can find a finite radical base change
V_{s}^{\prime}\to V_{s}
, such that the dévissage of Chevalley (
2.9
) at a geometric point
\bar{s}
over
s
descends to
V_{s}^{\prime}
as

1\to R_{s}\to P^{0}|_{V_{s}^{\prime}}\to A_{s}\to 1,

where
R_{s}
is a fiberwise connected smooth affine group scheme on
V_{s}^{\prime}
and
A_{s}
is an Abelian scheme on
V_{s}^{\prime}
. This implies an exact sequence of Tate modules

0\to\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}(R_{s})\to\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}(P^{0}|_{V_{s}^{\prime}})\to\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}(A_{s})\to 0.

As
V_{s}^{\prime}
is finite radical over
V_{s}
, they have equivalent étale sites, and we can consider the above exact sequence as objects over
V_{s}
.
Notice that the Tate module
\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}(A_{s})
is pure of weight
-1
, while the Tate module
\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}(R_{s})
relies only on the toric part of
R_{s}
, and is therefore pure of weight
-2
.
As
\mathcal{K}_{s}^{n}[-n]
is pure of weight
n
, comparing the weights, we find that the action (
2.10
) actually factorizes as

\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}(A_{s})\otimes\mathcal{K}_{s}^{n}[-n]\to\mathcal{K}_{s}^{n-1}[-n+1].

Let
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{s}^{\vee,\bullet}=\bigoplus_{i}\bigwedge^{i}\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}(A_{s})[i]
. We obtain then a morphism

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{s}^{\vee,\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{s}\to\mathcal{K}_{s}.

Proposition 2.5

(Ngô
[
N
]
, prop. 7.4.10)
.

The local system
\mathcal{K}_{s}
is a free graded module over the graded algebra
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{s}^{\vee,\bullet}
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{s}^{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{i}H^{i}(A_{s},\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})[-i]
be the ring of cohomology of
A_{s}
. The freeness property implies that the local system
\mathcal{K}_{s}
is of the form
\Lambda_{s}^{\bullet}[\dim(A_{s})]\otimes\mathcal{L}_{s}
for some local system
\mathcal{L}_{s}
, whence the inequality

\mathrm{Amp}_{s}(Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})\geqslant 2\dim(A_{s}).

Let
\delta_{s}^{\mathrm{aff}}=\dim(R_{s})
, then the above inequality can be rewritten as

{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{Amp}_{s}(Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}[d_{M}])\geqslant d-\delta_{s}^{\mathrm{aff}}.

(2.11)

On the other hand, assume that
M
is smooth over
k
, we have the inequality of Goresky and MacPherson (
[
N
]
, théorème 7.3.1),

{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{Amp}_{s}(Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}[d_{M}])\leqslant d-\mathrm{Codim}_{S}(\{s\}).

(2.12)

The inequality as stated is different from that of the theorem, but the proof is the same.
Let
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
be an extra local system over
\{s\}
with degree
n\in{\rm occ}_{s}\big(Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}[d_{M}]\big),n\geqslant 0,
i.e.

appears as a direct summand of

.
As

is non-trivial only in cohomological degrees

, this implies

n-{\rm d}_{s}\leqslant 2d-d_{M}.

With the equality
d_{M}=\dim(S)+d
, this simplifies to

n\leqslant d-\mathrm{Codim}_{S}(\{s\}).

As
M
is smooth and
f
is proper, the complex
Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}[d_{M}]
is autodual, and we have

{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{Amp}_{s}(Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}[d_{M}])=\max\Big\{n\,\big|\,n\in{\rm occ}_{s}\big(Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}[d_{M}]\big)\Big\},

whence the inequality (
2.12
).
We combine (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) as

d-\delta_{s}^{\mathrm{aff}}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Amp}_{s}(Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}[{\rm d}_{M}])\leqslant d-\mathrm{Codim}_{S}(\{s\}).

In case that

, both equalities hold in the above inequality and we have

\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Amp}_{s}(Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}[{\rm d}_{M}])=d-\delta_{s}^{\mathrm{aff}}=d-\mathrm{Codim}_{S}(\{s\}).

(2.13)

On the other hand, by proposition
2.5
, the extra local systems over
s
always appear in group of the form
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{s}^{\bullet}[\dim(A_{s})]\otimes\mathcal{L}
.
As the complex

is autodual, we can assume that
\mathcal{L}
is of degree
\geqslant 0
.
Hence the top degree piece

is of degree

n\geqslant\dim(A_{s})=d-\delta_{s}^{\mathrm{aff}}.

(2.14)

The equations (
2.13
) and (
2.14
), together with the trivial
n\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Amp}_{s}(Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}[{\rm d}_{M}])
, implies that

n=\dim(A_{s})=d-\delta_{s}^{\mathrm{aff}}.

Then
\mathcal{L}
will be of degree
0
, and
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{s}^{\rm top}\otimes\mathcal{L}
appears as a direct summand of
(R^{2d}f_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})_{s}
.
We summarize the above discussions as:

Theorem 2.6

(Ngô
[
N
]
, proposition 7.3.2)
.

Let
S
be a separated finite type
k
-scheme. Let

be a projective morphism of pure relative dimension

, equipped with the action of a smooth group scheme

. Suppose that

is smooth over

and that

acts on

with affine stabilizers, and that the Tate module of

of

satisfies the inequality

In case that both equalities hold at some support
\{s\}
, the extra local systems appears in the form
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{s}^{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{L}
of degree
0
such that
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{s}^{\rm top}\otimes\mathcal{L}
appears as direct summands of
({R^{2d}f_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}})_{s}
.

We then apply the theorem to the family

.
Let

be a support of

.
According to Diaz and Harris
[

]
, we have the inequality of Severi

(2.15)

On the other hand, we have the equality

hence the inequality of theorem

must be equalities, and the extra local systems

]

Let
C_{0}=\mathrm{Spf}\big(\mathbf{C}[\![x,y]\!]/f(x,y)\big)

Let
\varphi:(C,C_{0})\to(S,0)

Let

be the Tjurina number of

. Then

{\mathbf{A}}^{\tau}

Let

be the Zariski closure of

.

Let

be a line in general position and sufficiently close to

.
As

is smooth at

[

]
or
[
GM2
]

As the singularities of
C
are planar, according to Altman-Iarrobino-Kleiman
[
AIK
]
and Rego
[
Re
]
,
\overline{P}_{C}
is geometrically integral and
J_{C}
is dense open in
\overline{P}_{C}
.
Hence by restriction we get a bijection between the set of irreducible components of
\overline{P}^{\prime}
and that of
J^{\prime}
.
By construction, the eigen-sheaves of
\Phi^{\prime}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{J^{\prime}}
under the action of
A
are just the restriction of
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{P}^{\prime}_{C},\,\chi}
to
J_{C}
,
\chi\in\widehat{A}
.
Let
\iota:J_{C}\to\overline{P}_{C}
be the natural inclusion, it induces the morphism

\iota^{*}:\mathrm{Pic}_{\overline{P}_{C}/k}\to\mathrm{Pic}_{J_{C}/k},

then

\mathcal{T}_{J^{\prime}/J_{C}}=\iota^{*}\mathcal{T}_{\overline{P}^{\prime}/\overline{P}_{C}}.

(4.5)

To understand
\mathrm{Pic}_{J_{C}/k}
, recall that
J_{C}
admits the dévissage of Chevalley

1\to R\to J_{C}\xrightarrow{\phi^{*}}J_{\widetilde{C}}\to 1,

where
R
is the maximal connected affine sub group scheme of
J_{C}
. A precise form of the dévissage can be found in (
2.3
).
In particular, it is shown there that
R
is commutative, being the product of a split multiplicative subgroup and a commutative unipotent subgroup.
Hence
\phi^{*}:J_{C}\to J_{\widetilde{C}}
induces an isomorphism

\phi_{\star}:\mathrm{Pic}_{J_{\widetilde{C}}/k}\cong\mathrm{Pic}_{J_{C}/k}.

We denote by
\iota^{\star}
the composite

\iota^{\star}:\mathrm{Pic}_{\overline{P}_{C}/k}\xrightarrow{\iota^{*}}\mathrm{Pic}_{J_{C}/k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{\star}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Pic}_{J_{\widetilde{C}}/k}.

By construction, we have the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm Pic}^{0}_{\overline{P}_{C}/k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm Pic}^{0}_{J_{\widetilde{C}}/k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J_{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J_{\widetilde{C}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{\star}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{L}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{\phi^{*}L}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{*}}

Combining equation (
4.5
), we obtain that

\displaystyle A_{\phi^{*}L}^{*}\circ\phi_{\star}^{-1}(\mathcal{T}_{J^{\prime}/J_{C}})

\displaystyle=A_{\phi^{*}L}^{*}\circ\iota^{\star}(\mathcal{T}_{\overline{P}^{\prime}/\overline{P}_{C}})=\phi^{*}\circ A_{L}^{*}(\mathcal{T}_{\overline{P}^{\prime}/\overline{P}_{C}})=\phi^{*}(\mathcal{T}_{C^{\prime}/C})

\displaystyle=\big|\mathrm{Ker\,}(\phi^{*}|_{\mathcal{T}_{C^{\prime}/C}})\big|\cdot\phi^{*}(\mathcal{T}_{C^{\prime}/C})^{\mathrm{red}}.

As
A_{\phi^{*}L}^{*}
and
\phi_{\star}
are isomorphisms, this means that
\Phi^{\prime}:J^{\prime}\to J_{C}
is a trivial
\mathrm{Ker\,}(\phi^{*}|_{\mathcal{T}_{C^{\prime}/C}})
-covering of a finite abelian covering of
J_{C}
, which restricts via the morphism
J_{C}\to J_{\widetilde{C}}
to the finite abelian covering of
J_{\widetilde{C}}
associated to the torsion subgroup
\phi^{*}(\mathcal{T}_{C^{\prime}/C})^{\mathrm{red}}
. As
\widetilde{C}
is projective smooth, this last finite abelian covering is irreducible. Hence the irreducible components of
J^{\prime}
are naturally parametrized by
\mathrm{Ker\,}(\phi^{*}|_{\mathcal{T}_{C^{\prime}/C}})
, and so is
\overline{P}^{\prime}
.

∎

4.2
Applications to the spectral curve and its compactified Jacobian

Coming back to our spectral curve
C_{\gamma}
.
We apply the theory of the previous section to the deformation
\pi:\mathcal{C}\to{\mathcal{B}}
of
C_{\gamma}
and its relative compactified Jacobian
f:\overline{\mathcal{P}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
.

(1) Finite abelian coverings of deformations of the spectral curves

Let
\gamma=\mathrm{diag}(\gamma_{i})_{i=1}^{r}
with
\gamma_{i}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{d_{i}}(\mathcal{O})
such that the characteristic polynomial of
\gamma_{i}
is irreducible over
F
for all
i
.
Let
A=\mathcal{O}[\gamma]
and
\widetilde{A}
the normalization of
A
in
E=\mathrm{Frac}(A)
.
By theorem
4.2
and its proof, a finite abelian covering
C^{\prime}\to C_{\gamma}
is uniquely determined by a finite formal sum
\mathcal{T}_{C^{\prime}/C_{\gamma}}
of torsion subgroups of
J_{C_{\gamma}}
. By the equation (
2.2
), we have

J_{C_{\gamma}}\cong\widetilde{A}^{\times}/A^{\times}
. It is product of a unipotent subgroup and a split maximal torus
\mathrm{S}_{\gamma}=\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
Hence the torsion subgroup of
J_{C_{\gamma}}
coincides with that of
\mathrm{S}_{\gamma}
.
For
n\in\mathbf{N},(n,p)=1
, we denote by
\psi_{n}:C_{n}\to C_{\gamma}
the finite abelian covering corresponding to the torsion subgroup
\mathrm{S}_{\gamma}[n]=\mu_{n}^{r}/\mu_{n}
.
The Galois group of the covering is naturally identified with the dual

{\rm Hom}\big(\mathrm{S}_{\gamma}[n],{\overline{\mathbf{Q}}}_{\ell}^{\times}\big)={\rm Hom}\big(\,{\textstyle\frac{1}{n}}X_{*}(\mathrm{S}_{\gamma})/X_{*}(\mathrm{S}_{\gamma}),\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}\big)\cong\Lambda^{0}/n.

Moreover, as
C_{\gamma}
is rational, by theorem
4.3
, the irreducible components of
C_{n}
are naturally parametrized by
\mathrm{S}_{\gamma}[n]
.

Proposition 4.4
.

H^{1}(C_{\gamma},\mathbf{Z}/n)
is killed under the pull-back
\psi_{n}^{*}:H^{1}(C_{\gamma},\mathbf{Z}/n)\to H^{1}(C_{n},\mathbf{Z}/n)
.

Proof.

We have canonical isomorphism

H^{1}(C_{\gamma},\mathbf{Z}/n)=J_{C_{\gamma}}[n]\otimes\mu_{n}^{-1}=\mathrm{S}_{\gamma}[n]\otimes\mu_{n}^{-1}.

In terms of invertible sheaves, the torsion points in
\mathrm{S}_{\gamma}[n]
correspond bijectively to the eigen-sheaves in the decomposition

\psi_{n,*}\mathcal{O}_{C_{n}}=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\widehat{\Lambda^{0}/n}}\mathcal{O}_{C_{n},\,\chi}.

We need to show that their pull-back under
\psi_{n}
are all trivial.
As
\psi_{n}
is étale Galois, we have

\psi_{n}^{*}\psi_{n,*}\mathcal{O}_{C_{n}}=\mathcal{O}_{C_{n}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C_{\gamma}}}\mathcal{O}_{C_{n}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{C_{n}}^{\oplus\deg(\psi_{n})},

whence

\psi_{n}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{C_{n},\,\chi}=\mathcal{O}_{C_{n}},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,\chi\in\widehat{\Lambda^{0}/n}.

∎

To extend the finite abelian covering
\psi_{n}:C_{n}\to C_{\gamma}
across the family
\pi:\mathcal{C}\to{\mathcal{B}}
, we need to extend the finite subgroup scheme
\mathcal{T}_{C_{n}/C_{\gamma}}=J_{C_{\gamma}}[n]
over
{\mathcal{B}}
.
As
n
is coprime to
p
, the morphism
\mathrm{Pic}_{{\mathcal{C}}/{{\mathcal{B}}}}[n]\to{{\mathcal{B}}}
is quasi-finite and étale (cf.
[
BLR
]
, §7.3, lemma 2).
We have canonical isomorphism

R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbf{Z}/n=\mathrm{Pic}_{{\mathcal{C}}/{{\mathcal{B}}}}[n]\otimes\mu_{n}^{-1},

(4.6)

hence the obstruction for the quasi-finite group scheme
\mathrm{Pic}_{{\mathcal{C}}/{{\mathcal{B}}}}[n]
over
{{\mathcal{B}}}
to be finite étale lies in the monodromy action

\rho^{(n)}:\pi_{1}({\mathcal{B}}-\Delta_{\pi},\,\bar{\xi})\to{\rm Aut}\big(H^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{\bar{\xi}},\mathbf{Z}/n)\big),

where
\bar{\xi}
is a geometric generic point of
{\mathcal{B}}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For the family
\pi:\mathcal{C}\to{\mathcal{B}}
, we have

R\pi_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2}j_{!*}(R^{i}\pi^{\rm sm}_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})[-i].

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is smooth over
k
and
\pi
is proper,
R\pi_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
is pure by Weil-II
[
D2
]
, and so

R\pi_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2}{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{i}(R\pi_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})[-i]

by the decomposition theorem.
To determine the support of each
{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{i}(R\pi_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
, we apply the inequality of Goresky-MacPherson (
[
N
]
, théorème 7.3.1).
According to the theorem, as
\pi
is proper of pure relative dimension
1
, any extra support of
{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{i}(R\pi_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
will be of codimension
1
, and over its generic point the sheaf
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
will admit a non-trivial local system as a direct summand. But the fibers of
\pi
are all geometrically irreducible, hence there can not be any extra support.

∎

Let
V=(R^{1}\pi^{\rm sm}_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})_{\bar{\xi}}
. For any geometric point
x
of
{\mathcal{B}}
, let
\bar{\xi}_{x}
be a geometric generic point of
{\mathcal{B}}_{\{x\}}
.
We fix an embedding
\bar{\xi}\hookrightarrow\bar{\xi}_{x}
, which allow us to identify
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{B}}_{\{x\}}-\Delta,\,\bar{\xi}_{x})
as a subgroup of
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{B}}-\Delta,\,\bar{\xi})
and to identify
(R^{1}\pi^{\rm sm}_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})_{\bar{\xi}_{x}}
with
V
. Geometrically, this can be done by fixing a smooth path connecting
\bar{\xi}
and
\bar{\xi}_{x}
. We have then

H^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{x},\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})=(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})_{x}=(j_{!*}R^{1}\pi_{*}^{\mathrm{sm}}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})_{x}=V^{\pi_{1}({\mathcal{B}}_{\{x\}}-\Delta,\,\bar{\xi}_{x})}.

The equation actually holds for
\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}
-coefficient, as both
H^{1}(C_{\gamma},\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})
and
H^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{\bar{\xi}},\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})
are free
\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}
-modules. Let
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}=H^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{\bar{\xi}},\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})
, then

H^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{x},\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})=\mathbb{V}_{\ell}^{\pi_{1}({\mathcal{B}}_{\{x\}}-\Delta,\,\bar{\xi}_{x})}.

(4.7)

Let
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{(p)}=\prod_{l\neq p\text{ prime}}\mathbf{Z}_{l}
and
{\mathbb{V}}=\prod_{l\neq p\text{ prime}}\mathbb{V}_{l}
, then

H^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{x},\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{(p)})=\mathbb{V}^{\pi_{1}({\mathcal{B}}_{\{x\}}-\Delta,\,\bar{\xi}_{x})},

(4.8)

and so

H^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{x},\mathbf{Z}/n)=({\mathbb{V}}/n)^{\pi_{1}({\mathcal{B}}_{\{x\}}-\Delta,\,\bar{\xi}_{x})}=H^{1}\big(\mathcal{C}_{\bar{\xi}},\mathbf{Z}/n\big)^{\pi_{1}({\mathcal{B}}_{\{x\}}-\Delta,\,\bar{\xi}_{x})}.

(4.9)

Consider the monodromy action
\rho^{(n)}
and its local analogue

\rho^{(n)}_{x}:\pi_{1}({\mathcal{B}}_{\{x\}}-\Delta,\,\bar{\xi}_{x})\to{\rm Aut}\big(H^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{\bar{\xi}_{x}},\mathbf{Z}/n)\big).

As
{\rm char}(k)
is assumed to be large enough, both actions of the fundamental groups factor through their tame quotients.
Let
G_{\rho}^{(n)}
and
G_{\rho_{x}}^{(n)}
be their images.
With the embedding
\bar{\xi}\hookrightarrow\bar{\xi}_{x}
,
G_{\rho_{x}}^{(n)}
can be naturally identified as a subgroup of
G_{\rho}^{(n)}
.
Let
\Delta_{0}
be the irreducible component of
\Delta
containing
0
, and let
\Delta^{(0)}
be the union of the other irreducible components of
\Delta
. Let
{\mathcal{B}}^{\circ}={\mathcal{B}}-\Delta^{(0)}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

For any geometric point
x
of
\Delta-\Delta^{(0)}
, the monodromy group
G_{\rho_{x}}^{(n)}
is conjugate to a subgroup of
G_{\rho_{0}}^{(n)}
by an element of
G_{\rho}^{(n)}
.

Proof.

By assumption,
{\rm char}(k)
is large enough, hence the local fundamental groups
\pi_{1}^{\rm tm}({\mathcal{B}}_{\{x\}}-\Delta,\,\bar{\xi}_{x})
are isomorphic to their complex analytic counter part. For this case, as
{\mathcal{B}}
is smooth, by the local Lefschetz theorem for the fundamental groups, we have surjections

\pi_{1}(L_{x}-\Delta,\,\bar{\xi}_{x})\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}({\mathcal{B}}_{\{x\}}-\Delta,\,\bar{\xi}_{x}),

where
L_{x}
is a generic line sufficiently close to
x
in a sufficiently small open ball
B_{x}(r_{x})
with center
x
.
The monodromy group
G_{\rho_{x}}^{(n)}
is then generated by the image under
\rho_{x}^{(n)}
of a set of generators for
\pi_{1}(L_{x}-\Delta,\,\bar{\xi}_{x})
.

Recall that the equisingular stratum containing
x
is smooth (Teissier,
[
Z2
]
appendix), and that the union of the strict
\delta
-strata is Whitney regular along the equisingular strata lying at their frontier
[
C2
]
.
This implies that as we move
x
along the equisingular stratum containing it,
G_{\rho_{x}}^{(n)}
is changed to one of its conjugates under the action of
G_{\rho}^{(n)}
.

Note that we can move
x
along its equisingular stratum to a point
x^{\prime}
which is as close as we want to the equisingular stratum containing
0
. Indeed, the curve
\mathcal{C}_{x}
can be degenerated until it has a unique singularity with Milnor number
\mu_{\gamma}
, the degenerated curve corresponds then to a point
0^{\prime}
on the equisingular stratum containing
0
, because plane curve singularities having the same Milnor number are equisingular
[
T
]
.
It is then clear that the set of generators for
\pi_{1}(L_{x^{\prime}}-\Delta,\,\bar{\xi}_{x^{\prime}})
becomes part of a set of generators for
\pi_{1}(L_{0^{\prime}}-\Delta,\,\bar{\xi}_{0^{\prime}})
.
This implies that
G_{\rho_{x}}^{(n)}
is conjugate to a subgroup of
G_{\rho_{0}}^{(n)}
by an element of
G_{\rho}^{(n)}
by what we have explained above.

∎

Proposition 4.7
.

There exists a quasi-finite étale covering of
{\mathcal{B}}^{\circ}
over which we can extend
J_{C_{\gamma}}[n]
to a finite étale sub group scheme of
\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathcal{C}/{\mathcal{B}}}[n]
.

Proof.

From the isomorphism (
4.6
) and (
4.9
), we obtain that any element of

can be extended to a section of
\mathrm{Pic}_{{\mathcal{C}}/{{\mathcal{B}}}}[n]
over an étale neighborhood of

.
The above lemma implies that in the process of extending

to a finite étale sub group scheme of
\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathcal{C}/{\mathcal{B}}}[n]
the ramification appears only at the divisors
\Delta^{(0)}
. So we can go to a quasi-finite étale covering of
{\mathcal{B}}^{\circ}
which kills the ramifications to get the extension.

∎

By theorem

, the morphism

, which is formally smooth. The morphism
q_{1}

∎

Remark 4.33
.

Let
A\hookrightarrow A^{\prime}
be a morphism of algebras with
A^{\prime}\subset\widetilde{A}
. Then the above results about the deformation functor
\mathrm{Def}^{\rm top}_{A\hookrightarrow A_{I_{\bullet}}}
continue to hold if we replace
A_{I_{\bullet}}
with
A^{\prime}
.

We can draw conclusions about
\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet}}
from the above local analysis. By proposition
4.28
,
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
is the schematic image of the morphism
\mathrm{Def}^{\rm top}_{A\hookrightarrow A_{I_{\bullet}}}\to\mathrm{Def}^{\rm top}_{A}
.
As the morphism is finite and generically an isomorphism onto
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
, and that the functor
\mathrm{Def}^{\rm top}_{A\hookrightarrow A_{I_{\bullet}}}
is formally smooth, we deduce that the functor
\mathrm{Def}^{\rm top}_{A\hookrightarrow A_{I_{\bullet}}}
is actually representable by the normalization of
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
.
Let
\varphi_{I_{\bullet}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}\to{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
be the normalization, then there exists a unique point
\tilde{0}
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
lying over
0\in\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet}}
, it corresponds to
(A\hookrightarrow A_{I_{\bullet}})\in\mathrm{Def}^{\rm top}_{A\hookrightarrow A_{I_{\bullet}}}(k)
.
Let
\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}}_{I_{\bullet}}
be the formal neighbourhood of
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
at
\tilde{0}
, it can be identified with
\mathrm{Def}^{\rm top}_{A\hookrightarrow A_{I_{\bullet}}}
.
The morphism
\mathrm{Def}^{\rm top}_{A\hookrightarrow A_{I_{\bullet}}}\to\mathrm{Def}^{\rm top}_{A_{I_{\bullet}}}
then induces a morphism
\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}}_{I_{\bullet}}\to\prod_{j=1}^{l}\mathrm{Def}^{\rm top}_{\widehat{C}_{I_{j}}}
.
By corollary
4.32
, the morphism is formally smooth.
We summarize the above discussion as:

Theorem 4.34
.

The normalization
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
of
{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
is smooth over
k
, and there exists a natural morphism
\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}}_{I_{\bullet}}\to\prod_{j=1}^{l}\mathrm{Def}^{\rm top}_{\widehat{C}_{I_{j}}}
which is formally smooth.

The identification of
\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}}_{I_{\bullet}}
with
\mathrm{Def}^{\rm top}_{A\hookrightarrow A_{I_{\bullet}}}
extends to
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
, let
\tilde{\phi}_{I_{\bullet}}^{+}:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}:={\mathcal{C}}_{I_{\bullet}}\times_{\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet}}}\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
be the universal family over it.
Then
\tilde{\phi}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}
is a finite flat morphism, it can be thought of as a simultaneous partial resolution.
Geometrically, what it does is to detach the intersections between the local branches defined by
P_{I_{j}}(x,y,\mathbf{t}),j=1,\cdots,l
.
Over
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}\times_{{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}}\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}
, the geometric fibers of the family
\mathcal{C}_{I_{\bullet}}^{+}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
are projective irreducible curves with
h_{I_{\bullet}}
ordinary double points, and the morphism
\tilde{\phi}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}
is just the simultaneous normalization of the restriction of
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}
to
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}
.
Over
\tilde{0}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
, we get the partial resolution
\phi_{I_{\bullet}}:\widetilde{C}_{I_{\bullet}}\to C_{\gamma}
, which is an isomorphism over
C_{\gamma}\backslash\{c\}
, and such that
\phi_{I_{\bullet}}^{-1}(c)=\{c_{1},\cdots,c_{l}\}
and that the singularity of
\widetilde{C}_{I_{\bullet}}
at
c_{j}
is isomorphic to
\widehat{C}_{I_{j}}
for all
j
.
Let
\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
be the structural morphism, it is a projective flat family of curves with generic geometric fibers being smooth projective of genus
\delta_{\gamma}-h_{I_{\bullet}}
.
By theorem
4.34
, the restriction of
\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}
to the formal neighbourhood
\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}}_{I_{\bullet}}
is the product of versal deformations of the singularities of
\widetilde{C}_{I_{\bullet}}
.
In other words, we have:

Theorem 4.35
.

The family
\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
is an algebraization of a versal deformation of
\widetilde{C}_{I_{\bullet}}
.

Remark 4.36
.

Following the same lines of reasoning as Teissier
[
T
]
, I-1.3.2, and with the fact that
\tilde{\phi}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}
is a finite flat morphism, one can show that
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}
coincides with the normalization of
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}
.

5
The decomposition theorems

Consider the families

f_{n}:\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{n}\to{\mathcal{B}}_{n},(n,p)=1.

In this section, we will work out a decomposition theorem for the complex
Rf_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
. Pass to the projective limit, we get a similar decomposition for the affine Springer fibers.

5.1
The decomposition theorem for the finite abelian coverings

By proposition
4.12
, the total space
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
is smooth over
k
. As
f_{n}
is projective, the complex
Rf_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
is pure by Deligne’s Weil-II
[
D2
]
. By the decomposition theorem of Beilinson, Bernstein, Deligne and Gabber
[
BBDG
]
, we have

Rf_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2\delta_{\gamma}}{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{i}(Rf_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})[-i].

We will make explicit the perverse sheaves
{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{i}(Rf_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
.

By theorem
4.27
, a geometric fiber of
f_{n}:\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{n}\to{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
can have multiple irreducible components only when it lies over the union
\bigcup_{I_{\bullet}\vdash\{1,\cdots,r\}}\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}
.
Let
\pi_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}:\mathcal{C}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}:=\mathcal{C}\times_{{\mathcal{B}}}\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}
be the restriction of the family
\pi:\mathcal{C}\to{\mathcal{B}}
to
\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}
, it is a flat family of projective geometrically irreducible curves with
h_{I_{\bullet}}
ordinary double points.
Let
\tilde{\phi}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{C}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}
be the normalization, then the family
\tilde{\pi}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}
is a simultaneous resolution of singularities for the family
\pi_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}
.
The pull-back of invertible sheaves along
\tilde{\phi}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}
defines a morphism

(\tilde{\phi}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ})^{*}:\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathcal{C}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}/\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}}\to\mathrm{Pic}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}/\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}},

and this identifies
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}/\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}}
as the abelian factor of
\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathcal{C}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}/\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}}
.
Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\circ}_{n,I_{\bullet}}\subset\mathrm{Pic}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}/\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}}[n]\times_{\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}}\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet},n}
be the image under
(\tilde{\phi}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ})^{*}
of the base change to
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet},n}
of
\mathcal{T}_{n}\subset\mathrm{Pic}_{{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}}[n]\times_{{\mathcal{B}}}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
. The finite group scheme
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\circ}_{n,I_{\bullet}}
defines a finite abelian covering

\widetilde{\Phi}^{\circ}_{n,I_{\bullet}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet},n}\to\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}/\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}}\times_{\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}}\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet},n}.

Compose it with the structural morphism to
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet},n}
, we get an Abelian scheme

\tilde{f}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet},n}:\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet},n}\to\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet},n}.

Recall that we have defined a fiberwise dense open subfamily
f_{n}^{\circ}:\mathcal{P}_{n}\to{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
of
f_{n}
before proposition
4.12
, which is the
\Lambda^{0}/n
-covering of the base change to
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
of the family
\mathcal{P}\to{\mathcal{B}}
determined by
\mathcal{T}_{n}
. As a corollary of theorem
4.27
and the proof of theorem
4.3
, we have:

Proposition 5.1
.

The irreducible components of the geometric fibers of
f_{n}:\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{n}\to{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
over
\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{\circ}
are parametrized by
\mathbb{V}_{I_{\bullet}}/n
. Moreover, the abelian factor of
\mathcal{P}_{n}|_{\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{\circ}}
can be identified with
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet},n}
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{i}=R^{i}(\tilde{f}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet},n})_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
and we simplify
\mathcal{F}_{I_{\bullet}}^{i}=\mathcal{F}_{I_{\bullet},1}^{i}
. As
\widetilde{\Phi}^{\circ}_{n,I_{\bullet}}
is an isogeny of abelian schemes, we have actually
\mathcal{F}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{i}=\varpi_{n}^{*}\mathcal{F}_{I_{\bullet}}^{i}
. Moreover, we have
\mathcal{F}_{I_{\bullet}}^{i}=\bigwedge^{i}\mathcal{F}_{I_{\bullet}}^{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{I_{\bullet}}^{1}\cong R^{1}\tilde{\pi}_{I_{\bullet},*}^{\circ}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

For the family
f_{n}:\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{n}\to{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
, we have

Rf_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2\delta_{\gamma}}j_{n,!*}(R^{\,i}f^{\mathrm{sm}}_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})[-i]\oplus\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{\bullet}\text{ partition of}\\
\{1,\cdots,r\}\end{subarray}}\bigoplus_{i^{\prime}=0}^{2(\delta_{\gamma}-h_{I_{\bullet}})}\bigg\{(j_{I_{\bullet},n})_{!*}\mathcal{F}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{i^{\prime}}(-h_{I_{\bullet}})[-i^{\prime}-2h_{I_{\bullet}}]\bigg\}^{\oplus\,|(\mathbb{V}_{I\bullet}/n)^{\circ}|},

where
j_{n}:{\mathcal{B}}_{n}^{\mathrm{sm}}\to{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
and
j_{I_{\bullet},n}:\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}
are the natural inclusions.

Proof.

We follow the strategy of Ngô, as briefly recalled in §
2.2
.
It is clear that the action
\mathcal{P}_{n}\times_{{\mathcal{B}}_{n}}\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{n}\to\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
forms a weak abelian fibration.
For each point
s_{n}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
, let
\delta_{s_{n}}^{\mathrm{aff}}
be the dimension of the maximal connected affine sub group scheme of
\mathcal{P}_{n,\,\bar{s}_{n}}
. By theorem
2.6
, any support
s_{n}
of
Rf_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
satisfies the inequality

\delta_{\gamma}-\delta_{s_{n}}^{\mathrm{aff}}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Amp}_{s_{n}}(Rf_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})\leqslant\delta_{\gamma}-\mathrm{Codim}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{n}}(\{s_{n}\}).

(5.1)

Let
s=\varpi_{n}(s_{n})
. As
\mathcal{P}_{n}\to\mathcal{P}\times_{{\mathcal{B}}}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
is finite étale, we have

\delta_{s_{n}}^{\mathrm{aff}}=\delta(\mathcal{C}_{s}).

On the other hand, we have the inequality of Severi

\mathrm{Codim}_{{\mathcal{B}}}(\{s\})\geqslant\delta(\mathcal{C}_{s}).

(5.2)

It is clear that
\mathrm{Codim}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{n}}(\{s_{n}\})=\mathrm{Codim}_{{\mathcal{B}}}(\{s\})
.
The inequalities (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) imply that both must be equality. Hence the extra support
\{s_{n}\}
of
Rf_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
must be among the preimages in
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
of the strict
\delta
-strata in
{\mathcal{B}}
.
Moreover, any extra local system over
s_{n}
appears in the form
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{s_{n}}^{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{L}
of degree
0
, such that the top degree piece
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{s_{n}}^{\rm top}\otimes\mathcal{L}
appears as direct summands of
(R^{2\delta_{\gamma}}f_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})_{s_{n}}
, where
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{s_{n}}^{\bullet}
is the cohomological ring of the abelian part of
\mathcal{P}_{n,\bar{s}_{n}}
.

By theorem
4.27
, for the geometric fibers of
f_{n}
to have multiple irreducible components over
\overline{\{s_{n}\}}
, we need
s_{n}
to lie in the union
\bigcup_{I_{\bullet}}\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}
. The extra constraint
\delta(\mathcal{C}_{s})=\mathrm{Codim}_{{\mathcal{B}}}(\{s\})
implies that
s_{n}
must be
\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{\circ}
for some partition
I_{\bullet}
. Moreover, we have
(R^{2\delta_{\gamma}}f_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})_{\bar{s}_{n}}=\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}(-\delta_{\gamma})^{\mathbb{V}_{I_{\bullet}}/n}
by the same theorem.
We claim that
(R^{2\delta_{\gamma}}f_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})|_{\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{\circ}}
is constant.
By theorem
4.3
, the irreducible components of
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{n,s_{n}}
are naturally parametrized by
\mathrm{Ker\,}(\phi_{\bar{s}_{n}}^{*}|_{\mathcal{T}_{n,s_{n}}})
, where
\phi_{\bar{s}_{n}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\bar{s}_{n}}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\bar{s}_{n}}
is the normalization of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\bar{s}_{n}}
and
\phi_{\bar{s}_{n}}^{*}
is the induced morphism between their Jacobians.
Compare with the parametrization of irreducible components of
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{n,0}
, as
\mathcal{T}_{n}
is constant over
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
, we get an injection from the set of irreducible components of
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{n,s_{n}}
into that of
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{n,0}
. Hence the sheaf
(R^{2\delta_{\gamma}}f_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})|_{\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{0}}
can not have non-trivial monodromy and has to be constant, so

(R^{2\delta_{\gamma}}f_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})|_{\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{\circ}}=\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}(-\delta_{\gamma})^{\mathbb{V}_{I_{\bullet}}/n}.

By proposition
5.1
, the abelian factor of
\mathcal{P}_{n}|_{\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{\circ}}
can be identified with
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet},n}
. Hence we can identify
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{s_{n}}^{\bullet}
with the fiber
\big(\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{F}^{i}_{I_{\bullet},n}\big)_{s_{n}}
.
Now that
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{s_{n}}^{\rm top}\otimes\mathcal{L}
appears as direct summands of
(R^{2\delta_{\gamma}}f_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})_{s_{n}}
, and that
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{s_{n}}^{\rm top}=\mathcal{F}^{2(\delta_{\gamma}-h_{I_{\bullet}})}_{I_{\bullet},n}=\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}(-\delta_{\gamma}+h_{I_{\bullet}})
, the local system
\mathcal{L}
has to be
\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}(-h_{I_{\bullet}})^{\oplus\,m}
for some multiplicity
m
.
The multiplicity can be calculated by subtracting from
\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}(-\delta_{\gamma})^{\mathbb{V}_{I_{\bullet}}/n}|_{\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet},n}}
the contributions from the intermediate extensions of similar local systems over the stratum
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}^{\prime}}
having
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet}}
in the closure.
The stratum closure relation has been explained in remark
4.24
, and the multiplicity
m
can be easily calculated to be
|(\mathbb{V}_{I\bullet}/n)^{\circ}|
.

∎

We call the first summand in the theorem the
main term
for the decomposition of
Rf_{n,*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
.
We will show that the remaining terms are of the same nature as that of the main term.

We use the notations of §
4.4
.
Let
I_{\bullet}
be a non-trivial partition of
\{1,\cdots,r\}
of length
l
.
Recall that for the normalization
\varphi_{I_{\bullet}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}\to\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet}}
we have constructed a simultaneous partial resolution
\tilde{\phi}_{I_{\bullet}}^{+}:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}
and get a flat family of projective curves
\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
, which is an algebraization of a versal deformation of the partial resolution
\widetilde{C}_{I_{\bullet}}
by theorem
4.35
.
The restriction of
\varphi_{I_{\bullet}}
to
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}
is an isomorphism, and with it we can identify the restriction of the family
\tilde{\pi}_{I_{\bullet}}^{+}
to
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}
with the family
\tilde{\pi}_{I_{\bullet}}^{\circ}
.

Let
\tilde{f}_{I_{\bullet}}^{+}:\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{I_{\bullet}}^{+}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
be the relative compactified Jacobian of the family
\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
. The partial resolution
\tilde{\phi}_{I_{\bullet}}^{+}:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}
induces a morphism

(\tilde{\phi}_{I_{\bullet}}^{+})^{*}:\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathcal{C}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}/\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}}\to\mathrm{Pic}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}/\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}}.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}=\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}\times_{\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet}}}\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}
.
Note that
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{\circ}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}\times_{\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}}\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{\circ}
coincides with
\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{\circ}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}
is the normalization of
{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}
as
\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}
is étatle over
\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet}}
.
Let

\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{+}_{n,I_{\bullet}}\subset\mathrm{Pic}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}/\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}}[n]\times_{\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}}\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}

be the image under
(\tilde{\phi}_{I_{\bullet}}^{+})^{*}
of the base change to
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}
of
\mathcal{T}_{n}\subset\mathrm{Pic}_{{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}}[n]\times_{{\mathcal{B}}}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
.
Then
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{+}_{n,I_{\bullet}}
defines a finite abelian covering

\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}_{n,I_{\bullet}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet},n}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}\times_{\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}}\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}.

Compose it with the structural morphism to
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}
, we get a family

\tilde{f}^{+}_{I_{\bullet},n}:\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet},n}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}.

By construction, the restriction of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{+}_{n,I_{\bullet}}
to
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{\circ}
coincides with
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\circ}_{n,I_{\bullet}}
, and the restriction of
\tilde{f}^{+}_{I_{\bullet},n}
to
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{\circ}
coincides with
\tilde{f}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet},n}
.

By theorem
4.35
,
\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{+}_{I_{\bullet}}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet}}
is an algebraization of a versal deformation of
\widetilde{C}_{I_{\bullet}}
.
With the same lines of reasoning as theorem
5.2
, we get a decomposition theorem for the family
\tilde{f}^{+}_{I_{\bullet},n}
in the form

R\big(\tilde{f}^{+}_{I_{\bullet},n}\big)_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2(\delta_{\gamma}-h_{I_{\bullet}})}\Big\{\big(\tilde{j}^{+}_{I_{\bullet},n}\big)_{!*}R^{\,i}\big(\tilde{f}^{+,\mathrm{sm}}_{I_{\bullet},n}\big)_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}\Big\}[-i]\oplus\bigoplus\text{\it Remaining terms},

(5.3)

where
\tilde{j}^{+}_{I_{\bullet},n}:\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{\circ}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}
is the inclusion. As the restriction of
\tilde{f}^{+}_{I_{\bullet},n}
to
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{\circ}
coincides with
\tilde{f}^{\circ}_{I_{\bullet},n}
, we can identify

R^{\,i}\big(\tilde{f}^{+,\mathrm{sm}}_{I_{\bullet},n}\big)_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}=\mathcal{F}_{I_{\bullet},n}^{i}.

Let
{\varphi}_{I_{\bullet},n}:\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{I_{\bullet},n}\to\mathcal{S}_{I_{\bullet},n}

5.2
Consequence for the affine Springer fibers

In this section, we restrict to the case
k=\mathbf{C}
as we will use homology instead of cohomology. The reasoning can be easily extended to the finite field case by defining the
\ell
-adic homology formally as the dual of
\ell
-adic cohomology.

For
m|n
, we have finite abelian covering
n\Lambda^{0}\backslash\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0}\to m\Lambda^{0}\backslash\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0}

H_{*}\big(n\Lambda^{0}\backslash\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0},\mathbf{Z}\big)\to H_{*}\big(m\Lambda^{0}\backslash\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0},\mathbf{Z}\big).

(5.8)

As

varies, we get a projective system.

Proposition 5.5
.

The homology of
\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0}
can be recovered from that of the quotients
n\Lambda^{0}\backslash\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0}
by

H_{*}\big({\mathcal{X}}^{0}_{\gamma},\mathbf{Z}\big)=\varprojlim_{n}H_{*}\big(n\Lambda^{0}\backslash\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0},\mathbf{Z}\big).

Proof.

By construction, the singular chains defining the homology of a topological space are finite sums of singular simplices. In particular, they are compactly supported. Hence the natural projection
\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0}\to n\Lambda^{0}\backslash\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0}
induces morphism of homology

H_{*}\big(\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0},\mathbf{Z}\big)\to H_{*}\big(\Lambda^{0}\backslash\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0},\mathbf{Z}\big).

It is clearly compatible with the transition map (
5.8
), hence we get a morphism

H_{*}\big(\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0},\mathbf{Z}\big)\to\varprojlim_{n}H_{*}\big(n\Lambda^{0}\backslash\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0},\mathbf{Z}\big).

(5.9)

As the singular chains are compactly supported, any chain for
\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0}
together with its boundary will be mapped identically into
n\Lambda^{0}\backslash\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0}
for
n
sufficiently large. Hence the morphism (
5.9
) must be an injection. On the other hand, for
(c_{n})_{n}\in\varprojlim_{n}H_{*}\big(n\Lambda^{0}\backslash\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0},\mathbf{Z}\big)
, as we are working with
\mathbf{Z}
-coefficient (in particular the coefficients are not infinitely divisible), for
n
sufficiently large the chains representing
c_{n}
should be stabilized, i.e. for all
n|n^{\prime}
a chain representing
c_{n^{\prime}}
should be a lift of a chain for
c_{n}
. This implies that the chains actually come from a chain in
\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0}
, hence the morphism (
5.9
) is surjective and so is an isomorphism.

The formula in proposition

To begin with, we examine the local monodromies at

The cup product on the cohomology of curves induces a non-degenerate alternating pairing

\langle\,,\rangle:\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}\to\mathbf{Q}(-1).

(5.11)

It induces a non-degenerate alternating bilinear form

\langle\,,\rangle:\mathrm{F}\times\mathrm{F}\to\mathbf{Q}(-1).

Let
B_{0}(\epsilon)
an open ball of sufficiently small radius centered at

(5.13)

3.1

Proposition 5.10
.

For all

, we have

\Big(\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\{0_{n}\}}\big(j_{n,!*}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\vee}\big)_{\rm fr}\Big)\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{Q}={\rm Im}\Big\{\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\{0\}}\big(j_{!*}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{E}^{\perp})^{\vee}\big)\to\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\{0\}}\big(j_{!*}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\big)\Big\}.

Proof.

Let
U_{n}
be the connected component of
B_{0}(\epsilon)\times_{{\mathcal{B}}}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
containing
0_{n}
. As
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}\to{\mathcal{B}}
is étale and
B_{0}(\epsilon)
is sufficiently small, we can identify
B_{0}(\epsilon)
and
U_{n}
. In this way, all the sheaves introduced above can be seen as sheaves on

Note that the restriction of
\Phi_{n}:\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{n}\to\overline{\mathcal{P}}\times_{{\mathcal{B}}}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
to
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}^{\mathrm{sm}}
is an isogeny of abelian schemes. Hence the natural morphism
\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\vee}\to\mathbb{F}_{1}^{\vee}
is just an inclusion of sheaves.
This holds also for the morphism
\mathbb{F}_{n^{\prime}}^{\vee}\to\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\vee}
with
n|n^{\prime}
.
In particular, we have

\varprojlim_{n}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\vee}=\bigcap_{n}\big(\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\vee}\big)=\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\big(\textstyle{\bigcap}_{n}\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\vee}\big).

(5.15)

Note that
\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\vee}\otimes\mathbf{Q}=\varpi_{n}^{*}\mathcal{F}^{\vee}
, hence we have inclusion

\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\{0_{n}\}}\big(j_{n,!*}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\vee}\big)_{\rm fr}\subset\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\{0\}}\big(j_{!*}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\big).

(5.16)

Let
c=(c_{n})_{n}
be an element of
\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\{0_{n}\}}\big(j_{n,!*}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\vee}\big)_{\rm fr}
.
By the chain construction of the intermediate extension of Goresky and MacPherson
[
GM1
]
,
c_{n}
is represented by a singular chain with coefficients in
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\vee}
satisfying certain intersection properties. The compatibility between

then implies that the coefficients can be taken in
\bigcap_{n}\big(\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\vee}\big)
. Hence

actually lies in the image of

\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\{0\}}\big(j_{!*}\big(\textstyle{\bigcap}_{n}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\vee}\big)\big)_{\rm fr}\to\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\{0_{n}\}}\big(j_{n,!*}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\vee}\big)_{\rm fr}.

With the inclusion (
5.16
), this implies that

\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\{0_{n}\}}\big(j_{n,!*}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\vee}\big)_{\rm fr}={\rm Im}\Big\{\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\{0\}}\big(j_{!*}\big(\textstyle{\bigcap}_{n}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\vee}\big)\big)\to\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\{0\}}\big(j_{!*}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\big)\Big\}.

(5.17)

To make

(5.19)

The morphism

Remark 5.14

Typical height of the (2+1)-D Solid-on-Solid surface with pinning above a wall in the delocalized phase
Naomi Feldheim, Shangjie Yang

Abstract
. We study the typical height of the (2+1)-dimensional solid-on-solid surface with pinning interacting with an impenetrable wall in the delocalization phase. More precisely, let Λ N \Lambda_{N} be a N × N N\times N box of ℤ 2 {\mathbb{Z}}^{2} , and we consider a nonnegative integer-valued field ( ϕ ⁡ ( x ) ) x ∈ Λ N (\phi(x))_{x\in\Lambda_{N}} with zero boundary conditions ( i.e. ϕ | Λ N ∁ = 0 \phi|_{\Lambda_{N}^{\complement}}=0 ) associated with the energy functional 𝒱 ( ϕ ) = β ∑ x ∼ y | ϕ ( x ) − ϕ ( y ) | − ∑ x h 𝟏 { ϕ ( x ) = 0 } , {\mathcal{V}}(\phi)=\beta\sum_{x\sim y}|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|-\sum_{x}h\mathbf{1}_{\{\phi(x)=0\}}, where β > 0 \beta>0 is the inverse temperature and h ≥ 0 h\geq 0 is the pinning parameter. Lacoin has shown that for sufficiently large β \beta , there is a phase transition between delocalization and localization at the critical point h w ​ ( β ) = log ⁡ ( e 4 ​ β e 4 ​ β − 1 ) . h_{w}(\beta)=\log\left(\frac{e^{4\beta}}{e^{4\beta}-1}\right). In this paper we show that for β ≥ 1 \beta\geq 1 and h ∈ ( 0 , h w ) h\in(0,h_{w}) , the values of ϕ \phi concentrate at the height H = ⌊ ( 4 ​ β ) − 1 ​ log ⁡ N ⌋ H=\lfloor(4\beta)^{-1}\log N\rfloor with constant order fluctuations. 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

The solid-on-solid (SOS) model, introduced in
[
5
,
21
]
, is
a crystal surface model, which acts as a qualitative approximation of the Ising model in low temperature (see
[
6
]
for more details).

Now we formally describe the
(d+1)
-dimensional solid-on-solid model on the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
.
Let
\Lambda_{N}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket^{d}
denote a box of size
N
in the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

and we define its external boundary to be

\partial\Lambda_{N}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\setminus\Lambda_{N}:\ \exists y\in\Lambda_{N},x\sim y\right\}

where
x\sim y
denotes that
x
and
y
are nearest neighbors in the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Given
\phi\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{\Lambda_{N}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathbb{Z}}^{\Lambda_{N}}
, we define the Hamiltonian for the solid-on-solid model with zero boundary condition as

(1.1)

{\mathcal{H}}_{N}(\phi)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{x,y\}\subset\Lambda_{N}\\
x\sim y\end{subarray}}|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Lambda_{N},\;y\in\partial\Lambda_{N}\\
x\sim y\end{subarray}}|\phi(x)|.

Then for
\beta>0
(inverse temperature), we define a probability measure on
\widetilde{\Omega}_{N}={\mathbb{Z}}^{\Lambda_{N}}
as follows

(1.2)

\forall\phi\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{N},\quad\quad{\mathbf{P}}_{N}^{\beta}(\phi)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}^{\beta}}e^{-\beta{\mathcal{H}}_{N}(\phi)}

where

\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}^{\beta}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{\psi\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{N}}e^{-\beta{\mathcal{H}}_{N}(\psi)}\leq\left(\frac{1+e^{-d\beta}}{1-e^{-d\beta}}\right)^{|\Lambda_{N}|}

and we refer to
[
19
, Equations (3.8)-(3.10)]
for a proof of the last inequality.
It is known (see
[
9
,
21
,
22
]
)
that for any
\beta>0
, the
(1+1)
-dimensional SOS surface is rough (delocalized), which means the expectation of the absolute value of the height at the center diverges in the thermodynamic limit. However, for
d\geq 3
, it is shown in
[
4
]
by Peierls argument that for any
\beta>0
, the
(d+1)
-dimensional SOS surface is rigid (localized), that is,
the expectation of the absolute value of the height at the center is uniformly bounded. The interesting case is
d=2
which exhibits a phase transition between rough (for small
\beta
, c.f.
[
10
,
12
,
11
]
) and rigid (for large
\beta
, c.f.
[
2
,
13
]
). Moreover, numerical simulations suggest that
\beta_{c}\approx 0.806
is where the delocalization/localization transition occurs
[
6
]
.

1.2.
The
(2+1)
-dimensional SOS surface above a wall

The probability distribution of the
(2+1)
-dimensional SOS interface above an impenetrable wall (taking non-negative integer values) is the conditional distribution

(1.3)

\forall\phi\in\Omega_{N}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{\phi\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{N}:\phi\geq 0\right\},\quad\quad{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta}\left(\phi\right)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathbf{P}}_{N}^{\beta}\left(\phi\right)/{\mathbf{P}}_{N}^{\beta}\left(\Omega_{N}\right).

In
[
3
]
, Bricmont, Mellouki, and Fröhlich
showed that for large
\beta
, the average height
H
of the surface
satisfies

\frac{1}{C\beta}\log N\leq H\leq\frac{C}{\beta}\log N.

Later in
[
6
]
, Caputo, Lubetzky, Martinelli, Sly and Toninelli showed that for
\beta\geq 1
,
the typical height of the surface concentrates at

H=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{4\beta}\log N\right\rfloor

with fluctuations of order
O(1)
, where
\lfloor x\rfloor\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sup\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\ n\leq x\}
, as follows.

Theorem A

(
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

There exist two universal constants
C,K>0
such that for all
\beta\geq 1
and all integer
k\geq K
, we have for all
N
,

{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta}\left(|\{x\in\Lambda_{N}:\ \phi(x)\geq H+k\}|>e^{-2\beta k}N^{2}\right)\leq e^{-Ce^{-2\beta k}N\left(1\wedge e^{-2\beta k}N\log^{-8}N\right)},

and

{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta}\left(\left|\{x\in\Lambda_{N}:\ \phi(x)\leq H-k\}\right|>e^{-2\beta k}N^{2}\right)\leq e^{-e^{\beta k}N}.

This result describes the effect of the impenetrable wall in the large
\beta
regime, as the surface is pushed up to the height of order
\frac{1}{4\beta}\log N
, instead of remaining uniformly bounded when no wall is present. This effect is often called
entropic repulsion
. Furthermore, in
[
7
]
these authors provided
a full description of the macroscopic shape of the SOS surface, including the scaling limit and fluctuations of the rescaled macroscopic level
lines.
In particular, they show in
[
7
, Theorem 1]
that the surface concentrates on two values:
H
and
H-1
.
Moreover, Gheissari and Lubetzky
[
14
]
proved a phase transition in the occurrence of entropic repulsion when the hard wall is at a negative level.

1.3.
The
(2+1)
-dimensional SOS surface with pinning above a wall

In this paper, we are interested in the case where the
(2+1)
-dimensional SOS surface above a wall interacts with a pinning (or wetting) attraction to the wall.

More precisely, we model this surface in the box
\Lambda_{N}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
by an element of
\Omega_{N}={\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{\Lambda_{N}}
, where
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[0,\infty)
.
Given
\beta>0
and
h\geq 0
, we define the probability measure for the
(2+1)
-dimensional SOS surface above a wall with zero boundary conditions and pinning reward
h
, namely
{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}
on
\Omega_{N}
, by

(1.4)

{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}(\phi)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\tfrac{1}{{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}^{\beta,h}}e^{-\beta{\mathcal{H}}_{N}(\phi)+h\left|\{x\in\Lambda_{N}:\ \phi(x)=0\}\right|},

where

(1.5)

{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}^{\beta,h}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{\phi\in\Omega_{N}}e^{-\beta{\mathcal{H}}_{N}(\phi)+h|\{x\in\Lambda:\ \phi(x)=0\}|}\leq e^{h|\Lambda_{N}|}\left(\frac{1+e^{-2\beta}}{1-e^{-2\beta}}\right)^{|\Lambda_{N}|}.

By
[
19
, Equation (2.9)]
, we know the existence of the following limit

\textsc{f}(\beta,h)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2}}\log{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}^{\beta,h}

which is called the free energy.
By Hölder’s inequality, for
\theta\in[0,1]
we have

{\mathcal{Z}}_{N}^{\beta,\theta h_{1}+(1-\theta)h_{2}}\leq\left({\mathcal{Z}}_{N}^{\beta,h_{1}}\right)^{\theta}\cdot\left({\mathcal{Z}}_{N}^{\beta,h_{2}}\right)^{1-\theta},

and then
\textsc{f}(\beta,h)
is increasing and convex in
h
since
\textsc{f}(\beta,h)
is the limit of a sequence of increasing and convex functions in
h
.
Therefore, at points where
\textsc{f}(\beta,h)
is differentiable in
h
, the convexity (c.f.
[
15
, Appendix A.1.1]
) allows us to exchange the order of limit and derivative to obtain the asymptotic contact fraction

\partial_{h}\textsc{f}(\beta,h)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2}}{\mathbb{E}}_{N}^{\beta,h}\left[|\phi^{-1}(0)|\right],

where we have used the notation
\phi^{-1}(A)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in\Lambda_{N}:\ \phi(x)\in A\}
for
A\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
\phi^{-1}(k)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\phi^{-1}(\{k\})
for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
In
[
8
]
, Chalker showed that there exists a critical value

(1.6)

h_{w}(\beta)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sup\left\{h\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}:\textsc{f}(\beta,h)=\textsc{f}(\beta,0)\right\}

which is positive for all
\beta>0
, thus separating the delocalized phase
(\partial_{h}\textsc{f}(\beta,h)=0)
from the localized phase
(\partial_{h}\textsc{f}(\beta,h)>0)
.
We refer to the surveys
[
17
,
23
]
for a comprehensive bibliography on the subject of localization/delocalization of surface models.
Chalker further showed that for all
\beta>0
,

(1.7)

\log\left(\frac{e^{4\beta}}{e^{4\beta}-1}\right)\leq h_{w}(\beta)\leq\log\left(\frac{16(e^{4\beta}+1)}{e^{4\beta}-1}\right).

Later, Alexander, Dunlop and Miracle-Solé
[
1
]
showed that the lower bound in (
1.7
) is asymptotically sharp, and when
h
decreases to
h_{w}
the system undergoes a sequence of layering transitions (
i.e.
the typical height of the surface varies as
h
decreases to
h_{w}
).
More recently, Lacoin proved in
[
19
, Proposition 5.1]
that for
\beta>\beta_{1}
(where
\beta_{1}\in(\log 2,\log 3)
is given by
[
19
, (2.20)]
), we have

(1.8)

h_{w}(\beta)=\log\left(\frac{e^{4\beta}}{e^{4\beta}-1}\right),

and there exists a constant
C_{\beta}
such that

\forall u\in(0,1],\quad C_{\beta}^{-1}u^{3}\leq\textsc{f}(\beta,u+h_{w}(\beta))-\textsc{f}(\beta,h_{w}(\beta))\leq C_{\beta}u^{3}.

In fact, this constant
C_{\beta}
can be determined more precisely under additional conditions on
\beta
, for which we refer to
[
19
, Theorem 2.1]
. Furthermore, when
h>h_{w}
,
a complete picture of the typical height, the Gibbs states and regularity of the free energy is provided in
[
20
]
.

1.4.
Subcritical regime

In this paper, our goal is to describe the typical height of the
(2+1)
-dimensional SOS surface above a wall with pinning parameter
h\in(0,h_{w})
.
Our main result is a generalization of Theorem
A
to the subcritical pinning regime. We note that
for
h\in(0,h_{w})
we have
e^{-h}+e^{-4\beta}>1
, and then define for
\delta>0
,

(1.9)

\kappa(\beta,h,\delta)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{4\beta+\delta}{\log\left(e^{-h}+e^{-4\beta}\right)}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
\beta\geq 1
,
h\in(0,h_{w})
and
N\geq 1
. Let
H=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{4\beta}\log N\right\rfloor
.

(i)

There exist two universal constants
C,K>0
such that for all integer
m\geq K
,

{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}\left(\left|\phi^{-1}([H+m,\infty))\right|>e^{-2\beta m}N^{2}\right)\leq e^{-Ce^{-2\beta m}N\left(1\wedge e^{-2\beta m}N\log^{-8}N\right)}.

(ii)

For
\delta>0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have

{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}\left(\left|\phi^{-1}([0,H-m])\right|>2e^{-2\beta m}N^{2}\right)\leq 3e^{-\min\left(\frac{1}{2}e^{2\beta m}-4\beta(1+\kappa),\;\delta\right)N},

where
\kappa
is defined in (
1.9
).

We expect that similarly, for
h\in(0,h_{w})
, the surface concentrates on two values
H-c(h)
and
H-c(h)-1
where
c(h)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
tends to infinity when
h
tends to
h_{w}
.

1.5.
Behavior at criticality

Next we consider the behavior at critical
h=h_{w}
,
as defined in (
1.6
). Our main result is that
the amount of non-isolated zeros is at most of order
N
with high probability.
For
\phi\in\Omega_{N}
, we define its isolated and non-isolated zeros to be respectively

(1.10)

\begin{split}q_{1}(\phi)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{x\in\Lambda_{N}:\ \phi(x)=0,\forall y\in\Lambda_{N},y\sim x,\phi(y)\geq 1\right\},\\
q_{2+}(\phi)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{x\in\Lambda_{N}:\ \phi(x)=0,\exists y\in\Lambda_{N},y\sim x,\phi(y)=0\right\}.\end{split}

We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
\beta\geq 1
and
h=h_{w}
, we have for all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
C>0
,

{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h_{w}}\left(\phi\in\Omega_{N}:\ |q_{2+}(\phi)|\geq CN\right)\leq e^{-N\left(\frac{C}{20}e^{-6\beta}-4\beta\right)}.

When
h=h_{w}
, it is conjectured that the surface height concentrates around the value

(1.11)

H_{w}=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{6\beta}\log N\right\rfloor,

with fluctuations similar to Theorem
1.1
(
[
18
]
).
The intuition for this different typical height is a balance at criticality between entropic cost of lifting the surface up and the reward for isolated zeros.
Theorem
1.2
indicates that non-isolated zeros should not contribute to this balance.

Our last result gives further evidence for the conjecture. We show that the probability of downwards fluctuations from the conjectured typical height
H_{w}
is very small, if the amount of zeros is at most of order
N^{4/3}
.

Proposition 1.3
.

For all
\beta\geq 1
,
C>0
,
h=h_{w}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, letting
H_{w}=\lfloor\frac{1}{6\beta}\log N\rfloor
we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h_{w}}

\displaystyle\left(\left\{|\phi^{-1}(0)|\leq CN^{\frac{4}{3}}\right\}\bigcap\left\{\left|\phi^{-1}([1,H_{w}-m])\right|\geq 2e^{-2\beta m}N^{2}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(4\beta N+4\beta CN^{\frac{4}{3}}-\tfrac{1}{2}e^{2\beta m}N^{\frac{4}{3}}\right).

As a consequence of Theorem
1.2
and Proposition
1.3
, it is enough to prove that for large enough
C>0
, we have

(1.12)

{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h_{w}}\left(|q_{1}(\phi)|>CN^{4/3}\right)=o(1),\quad N\to\infty

in order to obtain a lower bound on the typical height of the surface at criticality, matching the conjectured height in (
1.11
).

1.6.
Open problems and heuristic arguments

Subcritical regime.
Theorem
1.1
opens the door to more advanced questions about the structure of the surface when
h\in(0,h_{w})
. Shape results similar to
[
7
]
are expected to hold.
In particular, we expect that for
h\in(0,h_{w})
, the surface concentrates on two values
H-c(h)
and
H-c(h)-1
, where
c(h)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
tends to infinity when
h
tends to
h_{w}
.

At criticality.

The behavior of the surface at
h=h_{w}
remains to be analysed. In particular, proving an analogous result to Theorem
1.1
with typical height as in (
1.11
) is of interest, as are more advanced shape results (similar to
[
7
, Theorems 1 and 2]
). As mentioned earlier, our results would imply a lower bound matching this typical height, provided that (
1.12
) holds. We turn to offer heuristic arguments for the conjectured estimates (
1.11
) regarding the typical height and (
1.12
) regarding the number of isolated zeros.

For large
\beta
(low temperature), neighboring vertices tend to have close values. Thus the surface is expected to be nearly flat - most of the vertices will take a certain value
H_{w}
, up to the inner boundary. Nonetheless, some vertices will take the value zero (even without pinning, as proved in
[
3
]
).
Due to a spatial mixing property
[
19
, Equation (3.13)]
, these zeros have nearly i.i.d. behavior, with a probability of
e^{-4\beta H_{w}}
for each vertex to be a zero (stemming from having
4
neighbors at height
H_{w}
, see (
1.4
)). At criticality, the pinning reward is not influenced at all by isolated zeroes (see (
4.3
)), and the main contribution is therefore from pairs of neighboring zeros, whose amount is typically
N^{2}e^{-6\beta H_{w}}
. The main penalty to the Hamiltonian (
1.1
) is from lifting the inner boundary up to
H_{w}
while the outer boundary remains at
0
, thus contributing
e^{-4\beta NH_{w}}
to the computation of probability in (
1.4
). The value of
H_{w}
is one which balances these two factors, that is,

\exp\left(h_{w}N^{2}e^{-6\beta H_{w}}\right)e^{-4\beta NH_{w}}\asymp 1,

which implies
H_{w}=\lfloor\frac{1}{6\beta}\log N\rfloor
as in (
1.11
). Now, by the i.i.d. model for zeros,
the mean number of isolated zeros is

N^{2}e^{-4\beta H_{w}}=N^{4/3},

which implies (
1.12
).

2.
Theorem
1.1
-(1): upward fluctuations for
h\in(0,h_{w})

Intuitively, the height of the
(2+1)
-dimensional SOS surface above a wall with pinning (
i.e.

h\geq 0
) is stochastically dominated by that without pinning (
i.e.

h=0
). We use this comparison between
{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,0}
to prove part (
i
), where
{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,0}={\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta}
is defined in Subsection
1.2
.

2.1.
Partial order and stochastic domination

We define a partial order ”
\leq
” on
\Omega_{N}\times\Omega_{N}
as follows

\phi\leq\psi\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\forall x\in\Lambda_{N},\text{ }\phi(x)\leq\psi(x).

Moreover, a function
f:\Omega_{N}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
is increasing if

\phi\leq\psi\quad\Rightarrow\quad f(\phi)\leq f(\psi).

Similarly, an event
{\mathcal{A}}\subset\Omega_{N}
is increasing if its indicator function
\mathbf{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
is increasing.
For two probability measures
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
on
\Omega_{N}
, we say that
\mu_{2}
dominates
\mu_{1}
, denoted by
\mu_{1}\preceq\mu_{2}
, if for any bounded increasing function
f:\Omega_{N}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

\mu_{1}(f)\leq\mu_{2}(f).

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
\beta>0
and
0\leq h_{1}\leq h_{2}
, we have

(2.1)

{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h_{2}}\preceq{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h_{1}}.

Proof.

Since
[
16
, Theorem 6]
is applied for finite distributive lattice, we set

{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{\phi\in\Omega_{N}:\max_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\phi(x)\leq n\right\}.

It is fundamental to verify Holley’s condition
[
16
, Equation (7)]
to obtain

{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h_{2}}\left(\cdot\ |\ {\mathcal{A}}_{n}\right)\preceq{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h_{1}}\left(\cdot\ |\ {\mathcal{A}}_{n}\right),

and then for any bounded increasing function
f:\Omega_{N}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
we have

(2.2)

\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{N}^{\beta,h_{2}}[f\mathbf{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{n}}]}{{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h_{2}}({\mathcal{A}}_{n})}\leq\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{N}^{\beta,h_{1}}[f\mathbf{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{n}}]}{{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h_{1}}({\mathcal{A}}_{n})}.

Moreover, by the dominate convergence theorem, for all
h\geq 0
we have

(2.3)

{\mathbb{E}}_{N}^{\beta,h}\left[f\right]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{N}^{\beta,h}[f\mathbf{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{n}}]}{{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}({\mathcal{A}}_{n})}.

Combining (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we conclude the proof.
∎

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
-(
i
).

Note that for any integer
m
,
the event

\left\{\phi\in\Omega_{N}:\ |\{x\in\Lambda_{N}:\ \phi(x)\geq H+m\}|>e^{-2\beta m}N^{2}\right\}

is increasing. We combine Lemma
2.1
and Theorem
A
to conclude the proof.
∎

3.
Theorem
1.1
-(ii): downward fluctuations for
h\in(0,h_{w})

To prove part (
ii
) of Theorem
1.1
,
we first show that
|\phi^{-1}(0)|
is at most of order

, with high probability, adopting the strategy in

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
\beta\geq 1
,
h\in[0,h_{w})
,
\delta>0
and
N\geq 1
, we have

{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}\left(|\phi^{-1}(0)|\geq\kappa N\right)\leq e^{-\delta N},

where
\kappa=\kappa(\beta,h,\delta)
is defined in (

).

Proof.

For
\phi\in\Omega_{N}
and each
A\subseteq\phi^{-1}(0)
, we define
U_{A}\phi:\Lambda_{N}\mapsto{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
as follows

(U_{A}\phi)(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{cases}\phi(x)+1,&\text{ if }x\not\in A,\\
0,&\text{ if }x\in A.\end{cases}

Since the action
U_{A}
increases the height of each site in
\Lambda_{N}\setminus A
by one, we have

(3.1)

\begin{gathered}{\mathcal{H}}_{N}(U_{A}\phi)\leq{\mathcal{H}}_{N}(\phi)+4|A|+4N,\\
|\phi^{-1}(0)|-|(U_{A}\phi)^{-1}(0)|=|\phi^{-1}(0)\setminus A|.\end{gathered}

Therefore,

{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}\left(U_{A}\phi\right)\geq{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}\left(\phi\right)\cdot\exp\left(-h|\phi^{-1}(0)\setminus A|-4\beta|A|-4\beta N\right),

and then

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{A\subseteq\phi^{-1}(0)}{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}\left(U_{A}\phi\right)

\displaystyle\geq e^{-4\beta N}\cdot{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}\left(\phi\right)\sum_{A\subseteq\phi^{-1}(0)}\exp\left(-h|\phi^{-1}(0)\setminus A|-4\beta|A|\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-4\beta N-h|\phi^{-1}(0)|}\cdot{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}\left(\phi\right)\sum_{n=0}^{|\phi^{-1}(0)|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq\phi^{-1}(0)\\
|A|=n\end{subarray}}\exp\left(-n(4\beta-h)\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-4\beta N-h|\phi^{-1}(0)|}\left(1+e^{-(4\beta-h)}\right)^{|\phi^{-1}(0)|}{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}\left(\phi\right).

Observe that for
A,A^{\prime}\subseteq\phi^{-1}(0)
with
A\neq A^{\prime}
, we have

U_{A}\phi\neq U_{A^{\prime}}\phi.

Furthermore, for
\phi\neq\psi
, if
A\subseteq\phi^{-1}(0)
and
B\subseteq\psi^{-1}(0)
, we have

U_{A}\phi\neq U_{B}\psi,

because we can recover
A
from
U_{A}\phi
by zero-value sites and then proceed to recover
\phi
. Therefore

\sum_{\phi\in\Omega_{N}}\ \sum_{A\subset\phi^{-1}(0)}{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}(U_{A}\phi)\leq 1
.
In particular, using (
3.2
) we obtain

(3.3)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\phi:\ |\phi^{-1}(0)|\geq\kappa N}\ \sum_{A\subset\phi^{-1}(0)}{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}(U_{A}\phi)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\phi:\ |\phi^{-1}(0)|\geq\kappa N}e^{-4\beta N-h|\phi^{-1}(0)|}\left(1+e^{-(4\beta-h)}\right)^{|\phi^{-1}(0)|}{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}\left(\phi\right)

\displaystyle\geq e^{-4\beta N}\left(e^{-h}+e^{-4\beta}\right)^{\kappa N}{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{\beta,h}\left(|\phi^{-1}(0)|\geq\kappa N\right)

where in the last inequality we have used that
e^{-h}+e^{-4\beta}>1
for
h\in[0,h_{w})
.
By the definition of
\kappa
in (
1.9
) we have

\left(e^{-h}+e^{-4\beta}\right)^{\kappa}e^{-4\beta}=e^{\delta}.

Plugging this into (
3.3
), we conclude the proof of Lemma

.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

,

and

where in the last inequality we have used that for
j\geq 0
,

\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}e^{2\beta(j+1)}N^{\frac{4}{3}}\right)}{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}e^{2\beta j}N^{\frac{4}{3}}\right)}\leq\exp\left(-\beta e^{2\beta j}N^{\frac{4}{3}}\right)\leq e^{-\beta N^{\frac{4}{3}}}.

∎

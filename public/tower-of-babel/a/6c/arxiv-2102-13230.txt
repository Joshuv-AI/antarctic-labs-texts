RECONSTRUCTING TRIANGULATIONS OF 3 3 -MANIFOLDS FROM THEIR INTERSECTION MATRIX
Jorge L. Arocha, Jorge Fernández-Hidalgo

Abstract
The intersection matrix of a simplicial complex has entries equal to the rank of the intersection of its facets. In [ 1 ] the authors prove the intersection matrix is enough to determine a triangulation of a surface up to isomorphism. In this work we show the intersection matrix is enough to determine the triangulation of a 3 3 -manifold up to isomorphism.

Introduction

Definition 1.1

(simplicial complex)
.

An abstract simplicial complex is a pair
(V,\Delta)
such that,
V
is a finite set,
\Delta\subseteq 2^{V}
and the following condition is satisfied, if
X\in\Delta
and
Y\subseteq X
then
Y\in\Delta
. We call an element of
\Delta
a simplex. If a simplex has
i
elements we shall say it has rank
i
, dimension
i-1
and call it an
i-1
-simplex. We denote the set of all
i
-simplices with
\Delta_{i}
. The maximal elements of
\Delta
are called facets.

Definition 1.2

(isomorphism of simplicial complices)
.

Given two simplicial complices
(V,\Delta)
and
(V^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
we say a map
\varphi:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
is an isomorphism if
\varphi
is bijective and
X\in\Delta
if and only if
\varphi(X)\in\Delta^{\prime}
.

In this work we focus on simplicial complices with associated geometric realizations where the underlying space is a
3
-dimensional manifold (we call such objects triangulations of
3
-dimensional manifolds). For our purposes it is convenient to work with the underlying abstract simplicial complex and only use the geometric realization to give conditions the abstract simplicial complex must satisfy. One such condition is the following, given in terms of the neighbourhood of vertices.

Definition 1.3

(neighbourhood of a vertex)
.

Let
(V,\Delta)
be a simplicial complex of dimension
3
and let
v\in V
. We define the neighbourhood of
v
as the simplicial complex of dimension
2
spanned by the set of facets
\Delta^{\prime}_{2}=\{X-\{v\}|v\in X\in\Delta_{3}\}
.

Observation 1.4
.

If
(V,\Delta)
is a triangulation of
3
-manifold the neighbourhood of
v
must span a space homeomorphic to a sphere of dimension
2
.

The intersection matrix of a simplicial complex is the matrix such that entry
i,j
is equal to the rank of the intersection of the
i
’th facet and the
j
’th facet.

We use the following definition of an intersection preserving map which captures the notion of two simplicial complices having the same intersection matrix.

Definition 1.5

(intersection preserving map)
.

Given two simplicial complices
(V,\Delta)
and
(V^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
of dimension
3
with corresponding sets of facets
\Delta_{3}
and
\Delta^{\prime}_{3}
we say a map
f:\Delta_{3}\rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}_{3}
is an intersection preserving map if
f
is bijective and
|X\cap Y|=|f(X)\cap f(Y)|
for all
X,Y\in\Delta_{3}
.

We say
f
extends to an isomorphism if there exists an isomorphism
\varphi:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
such that
\varphi(X)=f(X)
for all
X\in\Delta_{3}
.

The main result we shall prove is the following,

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(V,\Delta)
and
(V^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
be two triangulations of
3
-dimensional manifolds and
f:\Delta_{3}\rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}_{3}
an intersection preserving map, then
f
extends to an isomorphism.

Section
2
proves some results about extensions of intersection preserving maps, and provides sufficient conditions for such an extension to exist. Sections
3
and
4
show these conditions hold in our case. Section
3
is dedicated to introducing and classifyng
3
-cyclic shells, and in section
4
we use this classification to obtain the desired result.

2
Extending intersection preserving maps

Let us first introduce a ”reasonable” way of extending a map between facets to all simplices.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(V,\Delta)
and
(V^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
be simplicial complices of dimension
3
and let
f:\Delta_{3}\rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}_{3}
be any map. We define
F:\Delta\rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}
via

F(X)=\bigcap\limits_{Y\in\Delta_{3}|X\subseteq Y}f(Y).

Observation 2.2
.

Let
(V,\Delta)
be a triangulation of a
3
-manifold, then for all
v\in V
the intersection of all facets containing
v
is
\{v\}
.

Proof.

Let
v\in V
, one has

\bigcap\limits_{X\in\Delta_{3}|v\in X}X=\bigcap\limits_{X\in\Delta_{2}|v\in X}X

This is because every triangle is contained in exactly two tetrahedra(since the simplicial complex is a triangulation), so every intersacand of the RHS is the intersection of two intersecands of the LHS.

We can see

\bigcap\limits_{X\in\Delta_{2}|v\in X}X=\{v\}

by only considering triangles contained in one tetrahedra
X
.

∎

The following lemma shows
F
can be the only extension of an intersection preserving map between
3
-manifolds.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(V,\Delta)
and
(V^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
be triangulations of
3
-manifolds and let
f:\Delta_{3}\rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}_{3}
be a bijection between facets which is extended by the isomorphism
\varphi:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
. Then for each
v\in V
the set
F(\{v\})
is equal to
\{\varphi(v)\}
.

Proof.

Let
v\in V
. Since
f(X)=\varphi(X)
for all
X\in\Delta_{3}
we have,

\bigcap\limits_{X\in\Delta_{3}|v\in X}f(X)=\bigcap\limits_{X\in\Delta_{3}|v\in X}\varphi(X).

Because
\varphi
is bijective and
\bigcap\limits_{X\in\Delta_{3}|v\in X}X=\{v\}
we obtain,

\bigcap\limits_{X\in\Delta_{3}|v\in X}\varphi(X)=\{\varphi(v)\}

∎

We now show whenever this map and the inverse map can be defined, the resulting maps are isomorphisms.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(V,\Delta)
and
(V^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
be triangulations of
3
-manifolds and
f:\Delta_{3}\rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}_{3}
be a bijection between facets such that for all
v\in V
and
v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
the sets
F(\{v\})
and
F^{-1}(\{v^{\prime}\})
are singletons. Then the map
\varphi:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
defined by
\{\varphi(v)\}=F(v)
is an isomorphism and the map
\sigma:V^{\prime}\rightarrow V
defined by
\{\sigma(v^{\prime})\}=F^{-1}(\{v^{\prime}\})
is its inverse.

Proof.

We first show if
v\in X\in\Delta_{3}
then
\varphi(v)\in f(X)
. One can see this by noticing
\{\varphi\{v\}\}=\bigcap\limits_{Y\in\Delta_{3}|v\in Y}f(Y)\subseteq f(X)

One can apply the previous argument once more (but in the opposite direction) using the element
\varphi(v)\in f(X)\in\Delta^{\prime}_{3}
to obtain
\sigma(\varphi(v))\in X
for all
v\in X\in\Delta_{3}
.

This implies
\sigma(\varphi(v))\in\bigcap\limits_{X\in\Delta_{3}|v\in X}X=\{v\}
. Therefore
\varphi
is bijective and
\sigma
is its inverse.

It only remains to be proved that
\varphi(X)=f(X)
for all
X\in\Delta_{3}
. We already have
\varphi(X)\subseteq f(X)
and the equality is derived from the fact that
\varphi
is injective and
X,f(X)
are two finite sets of the same size.

∎

The following lemma will be used to prove theorem
1.6
. In section
4
we show the conditions of the lemma are met.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(V,\Delta)
and
(V^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})

3

This proof will be slightly long and is composed of various lemmas.

Lemma 2.6
.

For all

\bigcap\limits_{X\in\Delta_{3}|v\in X}f(X)=\bigcap\limits_{X\in\Delta_{2}|v\in X}F(X)
.

Proof.

For the

containment we notice if

contains

containing

,such that

, it follows

.

For the
\subseteq
containment we use
2.5
to see every intersecand in the RHS is the intersection of two elements of the LHS.

∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
v\in V
and
X\in\Delta_{3}
with
v\in X
. The set
\bigcap\limits_{Y\in\Delta_{2}|v\in Y\subseteq X}F(Y)
contains exactly one element.

Proof.

This is the intersection of
3
triangles contained in the tetrahedra
F(X)
. To see this is the case we note if
Y
contains
X
then
F(Y)
is contained in
F(X)
, and because
F
is a bijection between triangles we can be sure there are
3
distinct intersecands.

∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
v\in V
and
X\in\Delta_{2}
with
v\in X
. The set
\bigcap\limits_{Y\in\Delta_{1}|v\in Y\subseteq X}F(Y)
contains exactly one element.

Proof.

This is the intersection of
2
edges contained in the triangle
F(X)
. To see this is the case we note if
Y
contains
X
then
F(Y)
is contained in
F(X)
, and because
F
is a bijection between edges we can be sure there are
2
distinct intersecands.

∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
v\in V
,
X\in\Delta_{3}
and
Y\in\Delta_{2}
with
v\in Y\subseteq X
, then we have

\bigcap\limits_{Z\in\Delta_{2}|v\in Z\subseteq X}F(Z)=\bigcap\limits_{Z\in\Delta_{1}|v\in Z\subseteq Y}F(Z)

(2.1)

Proof.

We first show if
Z\in\Delta_{1}
and
v\in Z\subseteq Y
then,

F(Z)=\bigcap\limits_{W\in\Delta_{2}|Z\subseteq W\subseteq X}F(W)

To see this we first notice the
\subseteq
relation holds, because
F(Z)\subseteq F(W)
for both triangles
W
in the intersection. To see equality holds we notice there are two distinct intersecands in the right hand side, so the intersection is an edge.

We have thus shown every intersecand in the RHS of

is the intersection of two intersecands of the LHS. It follows we have the
\subseteq
relation in
2.1
. Because both sets have exactly one element (by

and
2.8
) it follows they are equal.

∎

Corollary 2.10
.

If
X
and
Y
are two tetrahedra and
|X\cap Y|=3
then

\bigcap\limits_{Z\in\Delta_{2}|v\in Z\subseteq X}F(Z)=\bigcap\limits_{Z\in\Delta_{2}|v\in Z\subseteq Y}F(Z)

Proof.

Use
2.9
two times with the triangle
X\cap Y
.
∎

Because of
1.4
we have the following observation.

Observation 2.11
.

Let
X,Y
be two tetrahedra containing
v
. Then there is a finite sequence of tetrahedra

such that

.

Corollary 2.12
.

If
X
and

are two tetrahedra containing

then

\bigcap\limits_{Z\in\Delta_{2}|v\in Z\subseteq X}F(Z)=\bigcap\limits_{Z\in\Delta_{2}|v\in Z\subseteq Y}F(Z)

We are now finally able to prove

F(\{v\})=\bigcap\limits_{X\in\Delta_{2}|v\in X}F(X)=\bigcap\limits_{X\in\Delta_{3}|v\in X}(\bigcap\limits_{Y\in\Delta_{2}|v\in Y\subseteq X}F(Y)).

A note on trigonometric approximations of Bessel functions of the first kind, and trigonometric power sums
Luca Guido Molinari

Abstract
. I reconsider the approximation of Bessel functions with finite sums of trigonometric functions, in the light of recent evaluations of Neumann-Bessel series with trigonometric coefficients. A proper choice of the angle allows for an efficient choice of the trigonometric sum. Based on these series, I also obtain straightforward non-standard evaluations of new parametric sums with powers of cosine and sine functions.

Primary 33C10, Secondary 42A10, 33B10

1.
Introduction

Bessel functions are among the most useful and studied special functions. Analytic expansions exist for different regimes
[
18
]
, and numerical algorithms for their
precise evaluation
[
15
]
[
21
]
[
7
]
[
13
]
.
Their simplest approximations are polynomials
[
1
]
[
12
]
[
14
]

and finite trigonometric sums, that can be advantageous in applications
[
5
]
.

Let’s consider
J_{0}
. Several trigonometric sums appeared in the decades, sometimes being rediscovered. These very simple ones

\displaystyle J_{0}(x)\simeq\tfrac{1}{4}[1+\cos x+2\cos(\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}x)]

(1)

\displaystyle J_{0}(x)\simeq\tfrac{1}{6}\left[1+\cos x+2\cos(\tfrac{1}{2}x)+2\cos(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}x)\right]

(2)

have errors
\epsilon=J_{0}-J_{0}^{\rm approx}
with power series (the marvel of Mathematica)

\epsilon(x)=-\tfrac{x^{8}}{2^{8}\cdot 20560}(1-\tfrac{x^{2}}{36}+...),\qquad\epsilon(x)=-\tfrac{x^{12}}{2^{12}\cdot 239500800}(1-\tfrac{x^{2}}{52}+...)

In practice, an error less than
0.001
is achieved for
x\leq 3
or
x\leq 5.9
.
These approximations were obtained by Fettis with the Poisson formula
[
11
]
. Rehwald
[
20
]
and later
Waldron
[
24
]
, Blachman and Mousavinezhad
[
6
]
and
[
3
]
used the strategy of truncating to the first term Neumann-Bessel series like

J_{0}(x)+2J_{8}(x)+2J_{16}(x)+...=\tfrac{1}{4}\cos[1+\cos x+2\cos(\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}x)]

that can be obtained from the Bessel generating function. The examples correspond to
n=4,6
of eq.19 in
[
2
]
:

\displaystyle J_{0}(x)+2{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}(-)^{kn}J_{2kn}(x)=\frac{1}{n}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\cos(x\cos\tfrac{\pi}{n}\ell)

(3)

and the errors reflect the behaviour
J_{2n}(x)\approx(x/2)^{2n}
of the first neglected term, but with much larger denominators.

The truncation yields
J_{0}
as a sum of cosines that corresponds to the evaluation of the Bessel integral
J_{0}(x)=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{\pi}\cos(x\cos\theta)
with the trapezoidal rule with
n
nodes

[
22
]
[
4
]
[
23
]
. Increasing
n
increases accuracy:
n=15
is a formula by Fettis
[
11
]
with 8 cosines (instead of 15, symmetries of the roots of unity reduce the number of terms):

\displaystyle J_{0}(x)\simeq\tfrac{1}{15}\cos x+\tfrac{2}{15}{\sum}_{k=1}^{7}\cos(x\cos\tfrac{k\pi}{15})

(4)

The error now is order
x^{30}\times 10^{-42}
and less than
10^{-6}
for
x<15
.

In this note I reconsider the approximations for
J_{0}
in the light of new Neumann-Bessel trigonometric
series in ref.
[
17
]
. They extend the series (
3
) by including an angular parameter, that is chosen to kill the
term with
J_{2n}
, so that the truncation involves the next-to-next term
J_{4n}
of the series.

The same strategy on appropriate series is then used for Bessel functions
J_{n}
of low order, that are discussed in section 3.

In section 4, I show that the series give in very simple way some parametric sums of powers of cosines and sines. Some are
in the recent literature (Jelitto
[
10
]
, 2022) while the following ones, to my knowledge, are new:

\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\sin^{p}(\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{n})\begin{array}[]{c}\sin\\
\cos\\
\end{array}(q\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{n})\qquad(p,q=0,1,...).

2.
The Bessel function
J_{0}

Consider the Neumann trigonometric series eq.11 in
[
17
]
:

\displaystyle J_{0}(x)+2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-)^{kn}J_{2kn}(x)\cos(2kn\theta)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\cos[x\cos(\theta+\tfrac{\pi}{n}\ell)]

(5)

The approximations (
1
), (
2
) and (
4
) are obtained with
\theta=0
,
n=4
,
6
,
15
, and neglecting functions
J_{8}
,
J_{12}
,
J_{30}
and higher orders. However they are not optimal. The advantage of eq.(
5
)
is the possibility to choose the angle
\theta=\pi/{4n}
to kill all terms
J_{2n}
,
J_{6n}
, etc. Then:

\displaystyle J_{0}(x)-2J_{4n}(x)+2J_{8n}(x)-...=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\cos(z\cos\tfrac{1+4\ell}{4n}\pi)

(6)

An expansion for
J_{0}
results, again, by neglecting the other terms.

Some examples:

\bullet

n=2
. It is

J_{0}(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}[\cos(x\cos\tfrac{\pi}{8})+\cos(x\sin\tfrac{\pi}{8})]+\epsilon_{2}(x)
. If we neglect the error,
the first zero occurs at
\pi\sqrt{2-\sqrt{2}}=2.4045
(
j_{0,1}=2.4048
).

\bullet

n=3.
The approximation has three cosines:

\displaystyle J_{0}(x)=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{3}[\cos(x\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})+\cos(x\tfrac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2\sqrt{2}})+\cos(x\tfrac{\sqrt{3}+1}{2\sqrt{2}})]+\epsilon_{3}(x)

(7)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{3}(x)=

\displaystyle\tfrac{x^{12}}{2^{12}\cdot 239500800}\left[1-\tfrac{x^{2}}{52}+\tfrac{x^{4}}{52\cdot 112}-\tfrac{x^{6}}{52\cdot 112\cdot 180}+\ldots\right].

Remarkably, the first powers of the error are opposite of those for the expansion eq.(
2
), that would involve 6 terms if
not for the degeneracy of the roots of unity.
The half-sum of (
2
) and (
7
),

\displaystyle J_{0}(x)\simeq\tfrac{1}{12}\big[1+\cos x+2\cos(\tfrac{1}{2}x)+2\cos(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}x)+2\cos(x\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})

\displaystyle+2\cos(x\tfrac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2\sqrt{2}})+2\cos(x\tfrac{\sqrt{3}+1}{2\sqrt{2}})\big]

(8)

has error

\epsilon(x)=-\tfrac{x^{24}}{5.2047}\times 10^{-30}[1-\tfrac{x^{2}}{100}+\tfrac{x^{4}}{20800}-\dots]
.

\bullet

n=6
gives a precision similar to the sum (
8
):

\displaystyle J_{0}(z)=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{6}\big[\cos(x\cos\tfrac{\pi}{24})+\cos(x\cos\tfrac{3\pi}{24})+\cos(x\cos\tfrac{5\pi}{24})

\displaystyle+\cos(x\sin\tfrac{\pi}{24})+\cos(x\sin\tfrac{3\pi}{24})+\cos(x\sin\tfrac{5\pi}{24})\big]+\epsilon_{6}(x).

(9)

The error has power expansion
\epsilon_{6}(x)=\frac{x^{24}}{5.2047}\times 10^{-30}[1-\frac{x^{2}}{100}+\ldots]
.

\bullet

n=8
is a sum of 8 cosines and compares with the formula (
4
) by Fettis.
The two approximations are different but with the same number of terms (because
\theta=0
produces degenerate terms) and similar
precision.

3.
Bessel functions
J_{n}
.

\bullet

\mathbf{J_{1}}
is evaluated via
J_{1}=-J_{0}^{\prime}
. Eq. (
8
) gives:

\displaystyle J_{1}(x)\simeq\tfrac{1}{12}\big[\sin x+\sin(\tfrac{1}{2}x)+\sqrt{3}\sin(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}x)+\sqrt{2}\sin(x\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})

\displaystyle+(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}-1}{\sqrt{2}})\sin(x\tfrac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2\sqrt{2}})+(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}+1}{\sqrt{2}})\sin(x\tfrac{\sqrt{3}+1}{2\sqrt{2}})\big]

(10)

with error
\epsilon(x)\simeq(x/20)^{23}\times 3.87\times[1-\tfrac{13}{1200}x^{2}+\dots].

\bullet

\mathbf{J_{2},J_{4}}
can be evaluated with the following identity
(the real part of eq.(5) in
[
17
]
):

\displaystyle J_{p}(x)+{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}[J_{kn+p}(x)+(-1)^{kn+p}J_{kn-p}(x)]\cos(kn\theta)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{n}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\cos[x\sin(\theta+\tfrac{2\pi\ell}{n})+p(\theta+\tfrac{2\pi\ell}{n})]

(11)

Because of the term
J_{n-p}
, we take
2p<n
. With
y=\frac{\pi}{2n}
:

\displaystyle J_{p}(x)-(-1)^{p}J_{2n-p}(x)+\ldots=\tfrac{1}{n}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\cos[x\sin(\tfrac{1+4\ell}{2n}\pi)+p\tfrac{1+4\ell}{2n}\pi]

If only
J_{p}
is kept, the approximation depends on the parity of
p
:

\displaystyle J_{p}(x)\simeq\begin{cases}\quad\cos(p\tfrac{1+4\ell}{2n}\pi)\times\tfrac{1}{n}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\cos[x\sin(\tfrac{1+4\ell}{2n}\pi)]&\text{$p$ even}\\
\,-\sin(p\tfrac{1+4\ell}{2n}\pi)\times\tfrac{1}{n}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\sin[x\sin(\tfrac{1+4\ell}{2n}\pi)]&\text{$p$ odd}\end{cases}

(12)

p=2
,
n=6
, give the short formula

\displaystyle J_{2}(x)\simeq\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}\big[\cos(x\sin\tfrac{\pi}{12})-\cos(x\cos\tfrac{\pi}{12})\big]

(13)

with error
\epsilon(x)=2.69114\times(x/10)^{-10}[1-\frac{x^{2}}{44}+...]
.
The first zero is evaluated
\frac{2}{3}\pi\sqrt{6}\simeq 5.1302

(
j_{2,1}=5.13562
). A better approximation is
n=8
,
y=\frac{\pi}{16}
:

\displaystyle J_{2}(x)\simeq

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{4}\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{8})[\cos(x\sin\tfrac{\pi}{16})-\cos(x\cos\tfrac{\pi}{16})]

(14)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{4}\sin(\tfrac{\pi}{8})[\cos(x\cos\tfrac{5\pi}{16})-\cos(x\sin\tfrac{5\pi}{16})]

with error
\epsilon(x)=7.00119\times 10^{-16}x^{14}[1-\frac{x^{2}}{60}+...]
;
\epsilon(5)=3\times 10^{-6}
,
\epsilon(8)=0.0010
.

For
J_{4}
we select
p=4
,
n=8
,
\theta=\frac{\pi}{16}
. Now the lowest neglected term is
J_{12}
:

\displaystyle J_{4}(x)\simeq\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{8}\big[

\displaystyle\cos(x\sin\tfrac{\pi}{16})+\cos(x\cos\tfrac{\pi}{16})-\cos(x\sin\tfrac{5\pi}{16})-\cos(x\cos\tfrac{5\pi}{16})\big]

(15)

The error is less that
10^{-3}
at
x<6.3
.

\bullet

\mathbf{J_{3},J_{5}}
. A useful sum for odd-order Bessel functions is eq.(17) in
[

]
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-)^{n+k}J_{(2n+1)(2k+1)}(x)\cos[(2k+1)\theta]=\sum_{\ell=0}^{2n}\frac{\sin[x\cos(\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{2n+1})]}{2(2n+1)}

(16)

The angle
\theta=\frac{\pi}{6}
cancels
J_{6n+3}
,

etc. and gives the approximation

\displaystyle J_{2n+1}(x)\simeq\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\sqrt{3}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2n}\frac{\sin[x\cos\tfrac{1+12\ell}{12n+6}\pi]}{2n+1}

(17)

that neglects
J_{10n+5}
etc.
With
n=1
and
n=2
we obtain:

\displaystyle J_{3}(x)\simeq

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{3\sqrt{3}}[\sin(x\cos\tfrac{\pi}{18})-\sin(x\sin\tfrac{2\pi}{9})-\sin(x\sin\tfrac{\pi}{9})]

(18)

\displaystyle J_{5}(x)\simeq

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{5\sqrt{3}}\big[\sin(x\cos\tfrac{\pi}{30})+\sin(x\sin\tfrac{\pi}{15})-\sin(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}x)

\displaystyle\qquad-\sin(x\sin\tfrac{4\pi}{15})+\sin(x\cos\tfrac{2\pi}{15})\big].

(19)

The expansion for
J_{3}
has error
\epsilon=2.33373\times 10^{-17}x^{15}[1-\tfrac{x^{2}}{64}+...]
.
The second one has error

.

4.
Trigonometric identities

The Neumann-Bessel series here used provide sums of powers of sines and cosines.
They arise by expanding in powers of
x
the Bessel functions in the series,

J_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{(x/2)^{n+2k}}{k!(k+n)!}

and the trigonometric functions in the sum of the series.

\bullet
Consider the series eq.(
5
).
At threshold powers
x^{2n}
,
x^{4n}
etc. new Bessel functions
(-)^{n}2J_{2n}\cos(2n\theta)
,
2J_{4n}\cos(4n\theta)
etc. enter
a term in the sum of cosines.

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{n}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\left[\cos\tfrac{\theta+\ell\pi}{n}\right]^{2k}=

(20)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4^{k}}\binom{2k}{k}&0\leq k<n\\
\frac{1}{4^{k}}\left[\binom{2k}{k}+2\binom{2k}{k-n}\cos(2\theta)\right]&n\leq k<2n\\
\frac{1}{4^{k}}\left[\binom{2k}{k}+2\binom{2k}{k-n}\cos(2\theta)+2\binom{2k}{k-2n}\cos(4\theta)\right]&2n\leq k<3n\\
...&...\end{cases}

By replacing
\theta
with
\theta+n\frac{\pi}{n}
we obtain:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{n}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\left[\sin\tfrac{\theta+\ell\pi}{n}\right]^{2k}=

(21)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4^{k}}\binom{2k}{k}&0\leq k<n\\
\frac{1}{4^{k}}\left[\binom{2k}{k}+(-)^{n}2\binom{2k}{k-n}\cos(2\theta)\right]&n\leq k<2n\\
\frac{1}{4^{k}}\left[\binom{2k}{k}+(-)^{n}2\binom{2k}{k-n}\cos(2\theta)+2\binom{2k}{k-2n}\cos(4\theta)\right]&2n\leq k<3n\\
...&...\end{cases}

Examples:

\frac{1}{9}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{8}\left[\sin\frac{\theta+\ell\pi}{9}\right]^{20}=\frac{1}{4^{10}}\left[\binom{20}{10}-2\binom{20}{1}\cos(2\theta)\right].

\frac{1}{n}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\left[\cos(\frac{1+6\ell}{6n}\pi)\right]^{2n}=\frac{1}{4^{n}}\left[\binom{2n}{n}+1\right],

\frac{1}{n}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\left[\cos(\frac{1+4\ell}{4n}\pi)\right]^{4n}=\frac{1}{16^{n}}\left[\binom{4n}{2n}-2\right].

For
\theta=0
and
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
these identities are eqs. 4.4.2 in
[
19
]
, 2.1 and 2.2 (together with several other non-parametric sums) in
[
9
]
. The series had also been studied in
[
16
]
.
Parametric averages on the full circle were recently evaluated by Jelitto
[
10
]
, with a different method.

\bullet
With the Neumann series (
16
) we obtain:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2n+1}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{2n}\left[\cos\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{2n+1}\right]^{2k+1}=

(22)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\qquad\qquad 1\leq 2k+1<2n+1\\
\frac{1}{4^{k}}\binom{2k+1}{k-n}\cos\theta&\quad 2n+1\leq 2k+1<3(2n+1)\\
\frac{1}{4^{k}}\left[\binom{2k+1}{k-n}\cos\theta+\binom{2k+1}{k-3n-1}\cos(3\theta)\right]&3(2n+1)\leq 2k+1<5(2n+1)\\
...&...\end{cases}

The sums of even powers of cosines are obtained from the series eq.16 in
[
17
]
:

\displaystyle J_{0}(x)+2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-)^{k}J_{(4n+2)k}(x)\cos(2k\theta)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{2n}\frac{\cos[x\cos\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{2n+1}]}{2n+1}

(23)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2n+1}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{2n}\left[\cos\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{2n+1}\right]^{2k}=

(24)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4^{k}}\binom{2k}{k}&\qquad\;0\leq k<2n+1\\
\frac{1}{4^{k}}\left[\binom{2k}{k}+2\binom{2k}{k-2n-1}\cos(2\theta)\right]&2n+1\leq k<4n+2\\
\frac{1}{4^{k}}\left[\binom{2k}{k}+2\binom{2k}{k-2n-1}\cos(2\theta)+2\binom{2k}{k-4n-2}\cos(4\theta)\right]&4n+2\leq k<6n+3\\
...&...\end{cases}

Example:

(\cos\tfrac{\theta}{3})^{12}+(\cos\tfrac{\theta+\pi}{3})^{12}+(\cos\tfrac{\theta+2\pi}{3})^{12}=\tfrac{3}{4^{6}}[\binom{12}{6}+2\binom{12}{3}\cos(2\theta)+2\cos(4\theta)].

The formulae with sines are obtained by shifting the parameter
\theta
.

\bullet
Now let’s consider the sum eq.(
11
) with
p<n-p
.
The equations are new and are easier to state if we distinguish the parity of
n
and of
p
.

Case
\mathbf{n=2m}
and
\mathbf{p=2q}
. Eq.(
11
) now is:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2m}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{2m-1}\cos[x\sin(\tfrac{\theta+\pi\ell}{m})]\cos[2q\tfrac{\theta+\pi\ell}{m}]=J_{2q}(x)+

\displaystyle+[J_{2m-2q}(x)+J_{2m+2q}(x)]\cos(2\theta)+[J_{4m-2q}(x)+J_{4m+2q}(x)]\cos(4\theta)+...

Separation of even and odd parts in
x
, and expansion in
x
give:

\tfrac{1}{2m}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{2m-1}[\sin\tfrac{\theta+\pi\ell}{m}]^{2k+1}\sin(2q\tfrac{\theta+\pi\ell}{m})=0,\qquad\forall k

This result is obvious as the sum from
0
to
m-1
is opposite of the rest of the sum. The symmetry is used also
in the other result:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{m}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{m-1}[\sin\tfrac{\theta+\pi\ell}{m}]^{2k}\cos(2q\tfrac{\theta+\pi\ell}{m})=

(25)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{(-)^{q}}{4^{k}}\begin{cases}0&k<q\\
\binom{2k}{k-q}&q\leq k<m-q\\
\binom{2k}{k-q}+(-1)^{m}\binom{2k}{k-m+q}\cos(2\theta)&m-q\leq k<m+q\\
\binom{2k}{k-q}+(-1)^{m}\left[\binom{2k}{k-m+q}+\binom{2k}{k-m-q}\right]\cos(2\theta)&m+q\leq k<2m-q\\
...&...\end{cases}

Case
\mathbf{n=2m}
,
\mathbf{p=2q+1}
. Eq.(
11
) becomes:

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2m}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{2m-1}\sin[x\sin(\tfrac{\theta+\pi\ell}{m})]\sin[(2q+1)\tfrac{\theta+\pi\ell}{m}]=J_{2q+1}(x)+

\displaystyle+[-J_{2m-2q-1}(x)+J_{2m+2q+1}(x)]\cos(2\theta)+[J_{4m-2q-1}(x)+J_{4m+2q+1}(x)]\cos(4\theta)+...

The non trivial result is:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{m}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{m-1}[\sin\tfrac{\theta+\pi\ell}{m}]^{2k+1}\sin[(2q+1)\tfrac{\theta+\pi\ell}{m}]=

(26)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{(-)^{q}}{2^{2k+1}}\begin{cases}0&k<q\\
\binom{2k+1}{k-q}&q\leq k<m-q-1\\
\binom{2k+1}{k-q}+(-1)^{m}\binom{2k+1}{k+m-q}\cos(2\theta)&m-q-1\leq k<m+q\\
\binom{2k+1}{k-q}+(-1)^{m}\left[\binom{2k+1}{k+m-q}+\binom{2k+1}{k-m-q}\right]\cos(2\theta)&m+q\leq k<2m-q-1\\
...&...\end{cases}

Example:
\frac{1}{5}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{4}\sin^{13}(\tfrac{\pi\ell}{5})\sin(\tfrac{3\pi\ell}{5})=-\frac{1}{2^{13}}\left[\binom{13}{5}-\binom{13}{10}-\binom{13}{0}\right]=-\frac{125}{1024}
.

Case
\mathbf{n=2m+1}
and
\mathbf{p=2q}
:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2m+1}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{2m}\cos[x\sin(\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{2m+1})]\cos(2q\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{2m+1})

\displaystyle=J_{2q}(x)+[J_{4m+2-2q}(x)+J_{4m+2+2q}(x)]\cos(2\theta)+...

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2m+1}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{2m}\sin[x\sin(\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{2m+1})]\sin(2q\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{2m+1})

\displaystyle=[J_{2m+1-2q}(x)-J_{2m+1+2q}(x)]\cos\theta+...

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2m+1}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{2m}[\sin\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{2m+1}]^{2k}\cos(2q\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{2m+1})=

(27)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{(-)^{q}}{4^{k}}\begin{cases}0&k<q\\
\binom{2k}{k-q}&q\leq k<2m+1-q\\
\binom{2k}{k-q}-\binom{2k}{k+q-1-2m}\cos(2\theta)&2m+1-q\leq k<2m+1+q\\
\binom{2k}{k-q}-\left[\binom{2k}{k+q-1-2m}+\binom{2k}{k-q-1-2m}\right]\cos(2\theta)&2m+1+q\leq k<4m+2-q\\
...&...\end{cases}

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2m+1}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{2m}[\sin\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{2m+1}]^{2k+1}\sin(2q\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{2m+1})=

(28)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{(-)^{q+m+1}}{2^{2k+1}}\begin{cases}0&k<m-q\\
\binom{2k+1}{k-m+q}\cos\theta&m-q\leq k<m+q\\
\left[\binom{2k+1}{k-m+q}-\binom{2k+1}{k-m-q}\right]\cos\theta&m+q\leq k<3m+1-q\\
...&...\end{cases}

Case
\mathbf{n=2m+1}
and
\mathbf{p=2q+1}
:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2m+1}{\sum}_{\ell=0}^{2m}

\displaystyle\cos[x\sin\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{2m+1}]\cos[(2q+1)\tfrac{\theta+2\pi\ell}{2m+1}]=[J_{2m-2q}(x)+J_{2m+2q+2}(x)]\cos\theta+...

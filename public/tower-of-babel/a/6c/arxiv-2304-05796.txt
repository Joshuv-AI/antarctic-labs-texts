On diagrams of algebras
Vladimir Hinich

Abstract
. We present a proof of the formula [ L.HA ] , 2.4.3.18 for the operad governing K K -diagrams of 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -algebras.



1.
Introduction

This is a note about
\infty
-operads. In this note we will use
the word “category” to denote
\infty
-categories and “operad” to
denote an
\infty
-operad as defined by Lurie in
[
L.HA
]
, Section 2.

To work in a well-defined context, we accept quasicategories
as a model for
\infty
-categories; but
all our constructions are presented in a
\infty
-categorical language,
as it is described in
[
H.EY
]
, Section 2, so that they make sense in
any model.

The term “conventional categories” stands for those categories whose
spaces of morphisms are equivalent to sets.

In this note
\mathtt{Cat}
denotes the category of small categories,
\mathit{Fin}_{*}

is the category of finite pointed sets and an operad is a functor

p:\mathcal{O}\to\mathit{Fin}_{*}
satisfying the standard properties of Definition 2.1.1.10 of
[
L.HA
]
. In particular,
\mathtt{Com}=\mathit{Fin}_{*}
is the operad for commutative
algebras.

The category of operads
\mathtt{Op}
is defined as the subcategory of
\mathtt{Cat}_{/\mathit{Fin}_{*}}
spanned by the operads, with the arrows preserving cocartesian liftings of the inerts. It can also be defined as a
Bousfield localization as follows.
Let
\mathtt{Cat}^{+}_{/\mathit{Fin}_{*}^{\natural}}
the category of marked categories
over
\mathit{Fin}_{*}
endowed with the standard marking (inert arrows are marked).
Then
\mathtt{Op}
indentifies with the full subcategory of

\mathtt{Cat}^{+}_{/\mathit{Fin}_{*}^{\natural}}
spanned by the operads with the inerts
as the marked arrows. The full embedding

R:\mathtt{Op}\to\mathtt{Cat}^{+}_{/\mathit{Fin}_{*}^{\natural}}
admits a left adjoint

L:\mathtt{Cat}^{+}_{/\mathit{Fin}_{*}^{\natural}}\to\mathtt{Op},

so that
\mathtt{Op}
becomes the Bousfield localization of

\mathtt{Cat}^{+}_{/\mathit{Fin}_{*}^{\natural}}
with respect to the equivalence
determined by
L
(called the operadic equivalence).

2.
An operad for diagrams of algebras

Let
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{C}
be operads and
K
be a category. In this note we
discuss the functor
assigning to
\mathcal{O},\mathcal{C}
and
K
the category
\operatorname{Fun}(K,\mathtt{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}))
.
This functor is representable in different ways.

•

As a functor of
\mathcal{C}
, it is(co)represented by an operad that we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, see
[
H.EY
]
, 2.10.5(3). This is the operad governing
K
-diagrams of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras. By definition,
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is an operad endowed with an operadic equivalence

\gamma:K\times\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}_{K},

where
K\times\mathcal{O}
is considered as marked category over

\mathit{Fin}_{*}^{\natural}
, where an arrow
(\alpha,\beta)
in
K\times\mathcal{O}
is marked iff
\alpha
is an equivalence and
\beta
is inert.

•

As a functor of
\mathcal{O}
, it is represented by the operad
\mathcal{C}^{K}

so that, in the case when
\mathcal{C}
is a symmetric monoidal category,

\mathcal{C}^{K}
is the symmetric monoidal category of functors
K\to\mathcal{C}
, see
Section
3
.

Furthermore, both
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
\mathcal{C}^{K}
have an explicit expression in
terms of the operad
K^{\sqcup}
defined in
[
L.HA
]
, 2.4.3.

Here are the main results of this work.

1.

The operad
K^{\sqcup}
is flat, see Lemma
2.2.1
.

2.

There is an equivalence
\mathcal{C}^{K}=\mathtt{Funop}(K^{\sqcup},\mathcal{C})
,
see
3.2.1
.

3.

There is an equivalence
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathcal{O}\times_{\mathtt{Com}}K^{\sqcup}
, see
Theorem
2.3.1
proven in
3.2
.

The most interesting equivalence is the Claim 3. It was first mentioned in
[
L.HA
]
, 2.4.3.18, but the reasoning there was based on an incorrect Remark 2.4.3.6.

2.1.

Recall the definition of
K^{\sqcup}
,
[
L.HA
]
, 2.4.3.1.

Define
\Gamma^{*}
as the (conventional) category of pairs

(I_{*},i)
with
I_{*}\in\mathit{Fin}_{*}
and
i\in I
, with the arrows

(I_{*},i)\to(J_{*},j)
given by arrows
I_{*}\to J_{*}
carrying
i
to
j
.
The functor
\pi:\Gamma^{*}\to\mathit{Fin}_{*}
carries

(I_{*},i)
to
I_{*}
.

For
K\in\mathtt{Cat}
, we define
K^{\sqcup}
as a category over

\mathtt{Com}=\mathit{Fin}_{*}
representing the functor

B\mapsto\operatorname{Map}(B\times_{\mathit{Fin}_{*}}\Gamma^{*},K).

The fiber of
K^{\sqcup}
at
I_{*}\in\mathtt{Com}
is
K^{I}
; an arrow
in
K^{\sqcup}
over
\alpha:I_{*}\to J_{*}
from
x:I\to K
to
y:J\to K

is given by a collection of arrows
x(i)\to y(j)
for all pairs
(i,j)\in I\times J
with
\alpha(i)=j
.

2.1.1.

In the case when
K
is a conventional category,
K^{\sqcup}
is a conventional operad. Its colors are the objects of
K
and
an operation from
\{x_{i}\}
to
y
is given by a collection of arrows

x_{i}\to y
. The composition of operations is defined in an obvious
way.

2.1.2.

In the special case when
K\in\mathtt{Cat}
has finite coproducts,
K^{\sqcup}

is the operadic presentation of the cocartesian SM category
K
,
see
[
L.HA
]
, 2.4.3.12.

2.2.
Flatness of
K^{\sqcup}

Recall
[
H.EY
]
, 2.8.2, that an operad
\mathcal{O}
is called
flat

if for any pair of composable active arrows
s:x_{0}\to x_{1}\to x_{2}
in
\mathit{Fin}_{*}
the base change
\mathcal{O}\times_{\mathit{Fin}_{*}}[2]\to[2]
is flat
in the sense of
[
L.HA
]
, B.3. If an operad
\mathcal{O}
is flat, one can
define a functor
\mathcal{P}\mapsto\mathtt{Funop}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{P})
so that

\mathtt{Alg}_{\mathcal{Q}}(\mathtt{Funop}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{P}))=\mathtt{Alg}_{\mathcal{Q}\times\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{P}),

where
\mathcal{Q}\times\mathcal{O}
is the product in
\mathtt{Op}
.

2.2.1 Lemma
.

The operad
K^{\sqcup}
is flat.

Proof.

By
[
H.EY
]
, 2.8.2, we have to verify that for any pair of composable active arrows
s:x_{0}\to x_{1}\to x_{2}
in
\mathit{Fin}_{*}
the base change
K^{\sqcup}\times_{\mathit{Fin}_{*}}[2]\to[2]
is flat.

Since the restriction of

K^{\sqcup}
to the active part of
\mathit{Fin}_{*}
is a cartesian fibration,
the flatness is immediate.
∎

The following result slightly generalizes
[
H.EY
]
, 2.8.10.

2.2.2 Proposition
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a symmetric monoidal category and
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}

be its operadic presentation. Then for any
K\in\mathtt{Cat}
the operad

\mathtt{Funop}(K^{\sqcup},\mathcal{M}^{\otimes})
is the operadic presentation of the
symmetric monoidal category
\operatorname{Fun}(K,\mathcal{M})
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{M}
is cartesian,
\mathtt{Funop}(K^{\sqcup},\mathcal{M}^{\otimes})
is also cartesian.

Proof.

We denote
\mathcal{F}=\mathtt{Funop}(K^{\sqcup},\mathcal{M}^{\otimes})\in\mathtt{Op}
. Let us first of all describe the underlying category
\mathcal{F}_{1}
. One has

\mathcal{F}_{1}=\mathtt{Alg}_{\mathtt{Triv}}(\mathcal{F})=\mathtt{Alg}_{\mathtt{Triv}\times_{\mathit{Fin}_{*}}K^{\sqcup}}(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes})=\operatorname{Fun}(K,\mathcal{M}).

Here
\mathtt{Triv}
, the trivial operad, is the subcategory of
\mathit{Fin}_{*}
spanned by the inert arrows.

Let
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
and
g
be functors
K\to\mathcal{M}
. Let us describe

\operatorname{Map}^{p}(f,g)
, the space of arrows in
\mathcal{F}
over the active arrow

p:\langle n\rangle\to\langle 1\rangle
. The calculation is very similar to (but considerably easier)
[
H.EY
]
, 4.2.2 and 4.2.3.
We denote by
C_{n}
the operad generated by one
n
-ary operation
and by
C_{n}^{\circ}
the subcategory of inert arrows in
C_{n}
. The
pair
(f,g)=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n},g)
defines a
C_{n}^{\circ}
-algebra in
\mathcal{F}

and the space
\operatorname{Map}^{p}_{\mathcal{F}}(f,g)
is the fiber of
the restriction map

\mathtt{Alg}_{C_{n}}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathtt{Alg}_{C_{n}^{\circ}}(\mathcal{F})

at
(f,g)
.
Note that
\mathtt{Alg}_{C_{n}}(\mathcal{F})=\mathtt{Alg}_{C_{n}\times_{Fin_{*}}K^{\sqcup}}(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes})
.

Denote by
K^{\sqcup}_{p}
,
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}_{p}
the categories over
[1]
obtained
from
K^{\sqcup}
,
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}
by the base change
[1]\to\mathit{Fin}_{*}
defined
by the active arrow
p:\langle n\rangle\to\langle 1\rangle
. One has

\mathtt{Alg}_{C_{n}\times_{Fin_{*}}K^{\sqcup}}(\mathcal{M}^{\otimes})=\operatorname{Fun}_{[1]}(K^{\sqcup}_{p},\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}_{p}).

Now,
K^{\sqcup}_{p}
is the cartesian fibration classified by the
diagonal map
K\to K^{n}
, whereas
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}_{p}
is the cocartesian
fibration classified by the (multiple) tensor product
\mathcal{M}^{n}\to\mathcal{M}
.
This allows one to identify
\operatorname{Map}^{p}(f,g)
with the space

\mathrm{Hom}_{\operatorname{Fun}(K,\mathcal{M})}(f_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{n},g)

where
f_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{n}
is defined as the composition

K\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{diag}\,}}{{\to}}K^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\prod f_{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{M}^{n}\to\mathcal{M},

where the last map is the (multiple) tensor product in
\mathcal{M}
.
This proves that
\mathtt{Funop}(K^{\sqcup},\mathcal{M}^{\otimes})
is a symmetric monoidal category.

Let now
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes}
be cartesian. Let
1\in\mathcal{M}

and
\mathbbold{1}\in\operatorname{Fun}(K,\mathcal{M})
be final objects of
\mathcal{M}
and of
\operatorname{Fun}(K,\mathcal{M})

respectively.
Given
f_{1},f_{2}:K\to\mathcal{M}
, we have to verify that the diagram

f\otimes\mathbbold{1}\longleftarrow f\otimes g\longrightarrow\mathbbold{1}\otimes g

is cartesian that is equivalent to saying that its evaluation at any
x\in K
is cartesian in
\mathcal{M}
. This follows from the fact that
\mathcal{M}
is cartesian.

∎

2.3.

The natural map
\gamma:K\times\mathtt{Com}\to K^{\sqcup}
is given by the
projection

(K\times\mathtt{Com})\times_{\mathtt{Com}}\Gamma^{*}=K\times\Gamma^{*}\to K.

Given an operad
\mathcal{O}
, we obtain, by the base change, the map

(1)

\gamma_{\mathcal{O}}:K\times\mathcal{O}\to K^{\sqcup}\times_{\mathtt{Com}}\mathcal{O}.

We see
K\times\mathcal{O}
as an object of the category

\mathtt{Cat}^{+}_{/\mathit{Fin}^{\natural}_{*}}
.

The following result is central for our discussion.

2.3.1 Theorem
.

\gamma_{\mathcal{O}}
is an operadic equivalence.

The theorem is proven in
3.2
.

3.
Path space of an operad

3.1.

Given an operad
\mathcal{P}
and a category
K
, we define a category

\mathcal{P}^{K}
over
\mathit{Fin}_{*}
by the formula

\mathcal{P}^{K}=\operatorname{Fun}(K,\mathcal{P})\times_{\operatorname{Fun}(K,\mathit{Fin}_{*})}\mathit{Fin}_{*},

where
\mathit{Fin}_{*}\to\operatorname{Fun}(K,\mathit{Fin}_{*})
assigns to
I_{*}
the constant functor

K\to\mathit{Fin}_{*}
with the value
I_{*}
.

3.1.1 Lemma
.

\mathcal{P}^{K}
is an operad.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\mathtt{Env}(\mathcal{P})
be the symmetric monoidal envelope of
\mathcal{P}

and let
\mathcal{M}_{1}
be the underlying category. In this case
\mathcal{M}^{K}
is
a cocartesian fibration over
\mathit{Fin}_{*}
representing the standard symmetric
monoidal structure on
\operatorname{Fun}(K,\mathcal{M}_{1})
. Obviously
\mathcal{P}^{K}
is the full suboperad of
\mathcal{M}^{K}
spanned by
\mathcal{P}_{1}\subset\mathcal{M}_{1}
.
∎

The following result is almost immediate.

3.1.2 Proposition
.

There is a canonical equivalence

\operatorname{Map}_{\mathtt{Op}}(\mathcal{O}_{K},\mathcal{P})=\operatorname{Map}_{\mathtt{Op}}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{P}^{K}).

Proof.

One has

\displaystyle\operatorname{Map}_{\mathtt{Op}}(\mathcal{O}_{K},\mathcal{P})=\operatorname{Map}_{\mathtt{Cat}^{+}_{/\mathit{Fin}_{*}^{\natural}}}(\mathcal{O}\times K^{\flat},\mathcal{P})=\hskip 144.54pt

\displaystyle\operatorname{Map}_{\mathtt{Cat}^{+}_{/\mathit{Fin}_{*}^{\natural}}}(\mathcal{O},\operatorname{Fun}(K,\mathcal{P})\times_{\operatorname{Fun}(K,\mathit{Fin}_{*})}\mathit{Fin}_{*})=\operatorname{Map}_{\mathtt{Op}}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{P}^{K}).

∎

3.2.
Proof of (
2.3.1
)

The map
\gamma_{\mathcal{O}}
induces a map of operads

\bar{\gamma}_{\mathcal{O}}:\mathcal{O}_{K}\to\mathcal{O}\times_{\mathtt{Com}}K^{\sqcup}.

We will prove it is an equivalence of operads using the reconstruction
theorem
[
HM
]
, 4.4.4.

The map
\bar{\gamma}_{\mathcal{O}}
is equivalence on colors, so it is enough to verify
that it induces the equivalence of the categories of algebras in
\mathcal{S}
.
By Proposition
2.2.2

\mathtt{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathtt{Funop}(K^{\sqcup},\mathcal{S}))=\mathtt{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\operatorname{Fun}(K,\mathcal{S}))=\operatorname{Fun}(K,\mathtt{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{S})).

The map
\gamma_{\mathcal{O}}
identifies this category with

\mathtt{Alg}_{K^{\flat}\times\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{S})
. This proves the result.

Finally, one has

3.2.1 Corollary
.

There is a natural equivalence
\mathcal{C}^{K}=\mathtt{Funop}(K^{\sqcup},\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.3.1
the operadic equivalence
\gamma_{\mathcal{O}}
induces
an equivalence

\mathtt{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}\times_{\mathtt{Com}}K^{\sqcup}}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathtt{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}(\mathcal{C})=\mathtt{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}^{K}),

or, in other words, an equivalence

\mathtt{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathtt{Funop}(K^{\sqcup},\mathcal{C}))\to\mathtt{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C}^{K}).

This implies the claim.
∎

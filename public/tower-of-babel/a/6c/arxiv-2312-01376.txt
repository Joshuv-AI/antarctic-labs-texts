An application of functional analysis to the Riemann zeta function
Kevin Smith

Abstract
. Lindelöf conjectured that the Riemann zeta function ζ ⁡ ( σ + i ​ t ) \zeta(\sigma+it) grows more slowly than any fixed positive power of t t as t → ∞ t\rightarrow\infty when σ ≥ 1 / 2 \sigma\geq 1/2 . Hardy and Littlewood showed that this is equivalent to the existence of the 2 ​ k 2k th moments for all fixed k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} and σ > 1 / 2 \sigma>1/2 . In this paper we show that the completeness of Hilbert space implies that if the 2 ​ k 2k th moment exists for σ > σ k > 1 / 2 \sigma>\sigma_{k}>1/2 then it also exists on the line σ = σ k \sigma=\sigma_{k} .



1.
Introduction

For fixed

\displaystyle M_{k}(\sigma,T)=\int_{1}^{T}|\zeta(\sigma+it)|^{2k}dt\hskip 28.45274pt(T>1)

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{M_{k}(\sigma,T)}{T}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d^{2}_{k}(n)}{n^{2\sigma}}\hskip 28.45274pt(\sigma>1/2,k\in\mathbb{N}).

The cases

[

]
and Ingham
[

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{M_{k}(\sigma,T)}{T}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d^{2}_{k}(n)}{n^{2\sigma}}\hskip 28.45274pt(\sigma>\sigma_{k})

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{M_{k}(\sigma,T)}{T}=\infty\hskip 28.45274pt(1/2\leq\sigma<\sigma_{k}\textrm{ if }\sigma_{k}>1/2)

and

(1.4)

\displaystyle M_{k}(\sigma_{k},T)\ll T^{1+\epsilon}\hskip 28.45274pt(\epsilon>0)

in general (Titchmarsh
[
8
]
, Section 7.9). Upper bounds for

and estimates for the rate of convergence in (

[
6
]

, the strongest known estimates are

. Then

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{S\cap[1,T]}|f|^{2}dt}{\int_{1}^{T}|f|^{2}dt}=\limsup_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left|\int_{1}^{T}f^{2}gdt\right|}{\int_{1}^{T}|f|^{2}dt}\leq\sup\{x:\Pr(|g|\geq x)>0\}=0

which gives (
2.1
).

Now suppose that
\int_{1}^{T}|f|^{2}dt\gg T
. We have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\int_{S\cap[1,T]}|f|^{2}dt}{\int_{1}^{T}|f|^{2}dt}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\left|\left(\frac{\int_{S\cap[1,T]}|f|^{2}dt}{\int_{1}^{T}|f|^{2}dt}\right)^{1/2}-\left(\frac{\int_{S\cap[1,T]}|f_{N,S}|^{2}dt}{\int_{1}^{T}|f|^{2}dt}\right)^{1/2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\int_{S\cap[1,T]}|f-f_{N,S}|^{2}dt}{\int_{1}^{T}|f|^{2}dt}\right)^{1/2}

so (
2.3
) implies (
2.1
). Conversely, (
2.1
) implies (
2.3
) by taking
f_{N,S}=0
.

To complete the proof, we note that if
\int_{1}^{T}|f|^{2}dt\ll T
then
f
is bounded except possibly on a null set
S=S(f)
, so there is already a bounded function
f_{N,S^{c}}
such that
|f-f_{N,S^{c}}|^{2}<\epsilon
on
S^{c}
. Thus, taking

\displaystyle f_{N}=f_{N,S}\chi_{S}+f_{N,S^{c}}\chi_{S^{c}},

we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{1}^{T}|f-f_{N}|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{S\cap[1,T]}|f-f_{N,S}|^{2}dt+\frac{1}{T}\int_{S^{c}\cap[1,T]}|f-f_{N,S^{c}}|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{S\cap[1,T]}|f-f_{N,S}|^{2}dt}{\int_{1}^{T}|f|^{2}dt}+\frac{\epsilon}{T}\textrm{meas}\left(S^{c}\cap[1,T]\right)

and using (
2.3
) gives (
2.4
). Conversely, (
2.4
) implies (
2.3
) if
\int_{1}^{T}|f|^{2}dt\gg T
.

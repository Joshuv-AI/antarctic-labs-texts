Three-dimensional ferromagnetism and magnetotransport in van der Waals Mn-intercalated tantalum disufide
Yu Liu, 1,∗ Zhixiang Hu, 1,2 Eli Stavitski, 3 , Klaus Attenkofer, 3,† and C. Petrovic 1,2

Abstract
Van der Waals (vdW) ferromagnets are an important class of materials for spintronics applications. The recent discovery of atomically vdW magnets CrI 3 and Cr 2 Ge 2 Te 6 has triggered a renaissance in the area of two-dimensional (2D) magnetism. Herein we systematically studied 2H-Mn 0.28 TaS 2 single crystal, a 2D vdW ferromagnet with T c T_{c} ∼ \sim 82.3 K and a large in-plane magnetic anisotropy. Mn K K -edge x-ray absorption spectroscopy was measured to provide information on its electronic state and local atomic environment. The detailed magnetic isotherms measured in the vicinity of T c T_{c} indicates that the spin coupling inside 2H-Mn 0.28 TaS 2 is of a three-dimensional (3D) Heisenberg-type coupled with the attractive long-range interaction between spins that decay as J ⁡ ( r ) ≈ r − 4.85 J(r)\approx r^{-4.85} . Both resistivity ρ ⁡ ( T ) \rho(T) and thermopower S ⁡ ( T ) S(T) exhibit anomalies near T c T_{c} , confirming that the hole-type transport carriers strongly interact with local moments. An unusual angle-dependent magnetoresistance is further observed, suggesting a possible field-induced novel magnetic structure.

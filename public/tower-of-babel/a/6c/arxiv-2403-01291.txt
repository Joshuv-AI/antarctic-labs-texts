A fractional-order trace-dev-div inequality
C. Carstensen, N. Heuer

Abstract




Abstract.

The trace-dev-div inequality in
H^{s}
controls the trace in the norm of
H^{s}
by that of the deviatoric part plus the
H^{s-1}
norm of
the divergence of a quadratic tensor field different from the constant unit matrix.
This is well known for
s=0
and established for orders
0\leq s\leq 1
and arbitrary space dimension in this note.
For mixed and least-squares finite element error analysis in linear elasticity, this inequality allows to establish robustness with respect to the Lamé parameter
\lambda
.

Key words.

regularity, linear elasticity,
\lambda
-robustness, trace-deviatoric-diveregence inequality, fractional Sobolev spaces

AMS subject classifications.

35Q74, 35A23, 46E35, 35Q74, 74B05

1
Main Result and Comments

This short note establishes the estimate

C_{\mathrm{tdd}}^{-1}\|\textrm{tr}\,\tau\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}\leq\|\textrm{dev}\,\tau\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}+\|\textrm{div}\,\tau\|_{H^{s-1}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all }\tau\in\Sigma.

(1.1)

This trace-deviator-divergence inequality (tr-dev-div for short) holds for any closed linear subspace
\Sigma
of the Sobolev space
H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})

for
0\leq s\leq 1
and a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
arbitrary dimension
2\leq n\in\mathbb{N}

provided
\Sigma\not\ni\textrm{id}
. It is clear that the constant unit matrix
id
violates (
1.1
) and has to be excluded;
but
\Sigma
is closed and so an entire neighbourhood
(with respect to the norm in

H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
)
of
id
lies outside
\Sigma
.

Theorem 1
(tr-dev-div).

If the closed linear subspace
\Sigma
of

H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
for some
0\leq s\leq 1

does not include
\mathrm{id}
, then (
1.1
) holds for
a positive constant
C_{\mathrm{tdd}}
.

Some comments are in order before
the proof of Theorem
1

follows below with Bogovskiĭ’s right-inverse of the
divergence operator
[
6
,
7
]
.

Remark 2
(s=0).

The tr-dev-div inequality (
1.1
) is well established in the least-squares
and mixed finite element community for
s=0
(when
H^{0}(\Omega)=L^{2}(\Omega)
)

[
2
,
4
]
.
It has been utilised for Stokes equations or linear elasticity
to provide
\lambda
-robust error control.

Remark 3
(symmetric variant).

Linear elasticity involves symmetric tensors

\mathbb{S}=\sym\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

and
\Sigma
may well be a
closed linear subspace of
H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{S})

with
\mathrm{id}\notin\Sigma

(because
H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{S})
is a closed subspace of

H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
).

Remark 4
(s=1, n=2).

The tr-dev-div inequality (
1.1
) leads to the
\lambda
-robust
regularity estimate
[
3
, Eq (11.2.33)]
from
[
13
, Thm A.1]
of

\lambda\|\ddiv u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}
for
pure homogeneous Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions
on a convex polygon,
n=2
,
s=1
, and a constant Lamé parameter
\lambda>0
.

Remark 5
(
\lambda
-robust regularity).

The paper
[
10
]
computes the singular functions for rather general (mixed) boundary
conditions in
n=2
dimensions and provides
\varepsilon(u)
in
H^{s}(\Omega)
for
explicit
s>0
. Given positive material constants
\lambda
and
\mu
, called the
Lamé constants, the elastic stress reads

\sigma=2\mu\varepsilon{u}+\lambda(\ddiv u)\mathrm{id}
and satisfies

\ddiv\sigma=-f\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
for the given source term in

L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
. Therefore,
the tr-dev-div inequality (
1.1
)
provides
\lambda\,\ddiv u\in H^{s}(\Omega)
and
controls its norm
\lambda\|\ddiv u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}

with
\lambda
-independent constants and the
H^{s-1}
norm of the source term.

Example 6
(convex domain and pure boundary condition for
n=2,3
).

Given a source term
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{n}
in a convex bounded domain

\Omega
and suppose either pure Dirichlet conditions
(resp. pure Neumann conditions) are prescribed with data

u_{D}\in H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)^{n}
(resp. compatible data

g\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)^{n}

with
\int_{\Omega}f\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}g\,ds=0
).
Then the weak solution
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{n}
and the associated
stress
\sigma=2\mu\varepsilon{u}+\lambda(\ddiv u)\mathrm{id}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S})

satisfy
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)^{n}
and
\sigma\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{S})

and

\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\lambda\|\ddiv u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u_{D}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)})

for the pure Dirichlet problem
(resp.
\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega)})
for the pure
compatible Neumann problem).
The point is that the constant
C_{\mathrm{reg}}
exclusively depends on
\Omega
and
\mu

but not on
\lambda
.
(The proof follows from well known higher regularity for convex domains and
Remark
5
.)

2
Sobolev spaces by interpolation

Let
\widetilde{H}^{m}(\Omega):=H^{m}_{0}(\Omega):=\overline{\mathcal{D}(\Omega)}
be the closed subspace of
H^{m}(\Omega)

for all non-negative integers
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The Sobolev spaces for the
negative integers follow by duality (with pivot space
L^{2}
and pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
)

H^{-m}(\Omega):=\widetilde{H}^{m}(\Omega)^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{H}^{-m}(\Omega):=H^{m}(\Omega)^{*}\quad\text{for }m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Given the Sobolev spaces
\widetilde{H}^{m}(\Omega)
and
H^{m}(\Omega)
for all integers
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, define

H^{r}(\Omega)=[H^{m}(\Omega),H^{m+1}(\Omega)]_{s}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{H}^{r}(\Omega)=[\widetilde{H}^{m}(\Omega),\widetilde{H}^{m+1}(\Omega)]_{s}\quad\text{for }r=m+s

with
0<s<1
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
by complex interpolation

[
1
,
5
,
9
,
11
,
12
]
.
A real interpolation method could also be utilised and may lead to additional equivalence constants. The complex interpolation, after
Calderón
[
5
]
and Lions
[
9
]
,
allows for equal norms on the dual of an interpolation
space compared to the interpolation of the duals.
We frequently utilise the symmetry
[X,Y]_{s}=[Y,X]_{1-s}
and
the duality theorem

([X,Y]_{s})^{*}=[X^{*},Y^{*}]_{s}

with equal norms for reflexive spaces
X,Y
,
cf.
[
1
, Theorems 4.2.1(a), 4.3.1]

and
[
1
, Corollary 4.5.2]
, respectively.

We underline that
H^{s}(\Omega)=\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
for

0\leq s<1/2
while, for
1/2\leq s\leq 1
,
\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
is a strict subspace of
H^{s}(\Omega)
(with vanishing traces for
1/2<s\leq 1
).
The definition of
\widetilde{H}^{1/2}(\Omega)
by interpolation
results in the Lions–Magenes space
H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)
, a strict subset of
H_{0}^{1/2}(\Omega)=\overline{\mathcal{D}(\Omega)}

[
8
]
.
For instance,
H^{-1/2}(\Omega)
is dual to
\widetilde{H}^{1/2}(\Omega)\equiv H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)
.
The norm in
H^{-1/2}(\Omega)
arises in (
1.1
) for
s=1/2
as the dual norm to

\widetilde{H}^{1/2}(\Omega)\equiv H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)

which has a stronger than
H^{1/2}(\Omega)
. Hence
(
1.1
) holds in particular when
\|\textrm{div}\,\tau\|_{H^{-1/2}(\Omega)}
is confused with

\|\textrm{div}\,\tau\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-1/2}(\Omega)}=\|\textrm{div}\,\tau\|_{H^{1/2}_{0}(\Omega)^{*}}\geq\|\textrm{div}\,\tau\|_{H^{-1/2}(\Omega)}
.

3
Gradient and divergence in
\widetilde{H}^{r}(\Omega)

The gradient
\nabla:H^{m}(\Omega)\to H^{m-1}(\Omega)^{n}
and its restriction

\widetilde{\nabla}:=\nabla|_{\widetilde{H}^{m}(\Omega)}:\widetilde{H}^{m}(\Omega)\to\widetilde{H}^{m-1}(\Omega)^{n}
as well as
the divergence
\textrm{div}:H^{m}(\Omega)^{n}\to H^{m-1}(\Omega)
and its restriction

\widetilde{\textrm{div}}:=\textrm{div}|_{\widetilde{H}^{m}(\Omega)^{n}}:\widetilde{H}^{m}(\Omega)^{n}\to\widetilde{H}^{m-1}(\Omega)

are well defined pointwise a.e. for any positive integer
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, those definitions provide linear and bounded operators

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}\in

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526ptL(\widetilde{H}^{m}(\Omega);\widetilde{H}^{m-1}(\Omega)^{n})

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\textrm{div}}\in

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526ptL(\widetilde{H}^{m}(\Omega)^{n};\widetilde{H}^{m-1}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\nabla\in

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526ptL(H^{m}(\Omega);H^{m-1}(\Omega)^{n})

with dual

\displaystyle\nabla^{*}\in

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526ptL(\widetilde{H}^{1-m}(\Omega)^{n};\widetilde{H}^{-m}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\textrm{div}\in

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526ptL(H^{m}(\Omega)^{n};H^{m-1}(\Omega))

with dual

\displaystyle\textrm{div}^{*}\in

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526ptL(\widetilde{H}^{1-m}(\Omega);\widetilde{H}^{-m}(\Omega)^{n}).

Integration by parts for

(\varphi,\Psi)\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)\times C^{\infty}_{\textrm{c}}(\overline{\Omega})^{n}
,

\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,\textrm{div}\,\Psi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\Psi\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx=0,

and the density of
C^{\infty}_{\textrm{c}}(\overline{\Omega})
in
H^{r}(\Omega)

and
\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
in
\widetilde{H}^{r}(\Omega)
justify the definition of

\widetilde{\nabla}:=-\textrm{div}^{*}\in L(\widetilde{H}^{1-m}(\Omega);\widetilde{H}^{-m}(\Omega)^{n})

for
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Integration by parts for
(\varphi,\Psi)\in C^{\infty}_{\textrm{c}}(\overline{\Omega})\times\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{n}
justifies the
definition of
\widetilde{\textrm{div}}:=-\nabla^{*}\in L(\widetilde{H}^{1-m}(\Omega)^{n};\widetilde{H}^{-m}(\Omega))
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
In summary this defines

\widetilde{\nabla}\in L(\widetilde{H}^{r}(\Omega);\widetilde{H}^{r-1}(\Omega)^{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{\textrm{div}}\in L(\widetilde{H}^{r}(\Omega)^{n};\widetilde{H}^{r-1}(\Omega))

(3.1)

for any integer
r\in\mathbb{Z}
and thereafter complex interpolation defines the operators (
3.1
)
for any real
r
. The norms of the resulting operators are controlled by interpolation: The operator norm of

\widetilde{\nabla}
and
\widetilde{\textrm{div}}
from (
3.1
) is at most

\sqrt{n}
and
1
, respectively, for
r=0
; while
1
and
\sqrt{n}
, respectively, for
r=1
.
For the operator norms and
0\leq s\leq 1
,

\|\widetilde{\nabla}\|_{L(\widetilde{H}^{1-s}(\Omega);\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)^{n})}\leq n^{s/2}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\widetilde{\textrm{div}}\|_{L(\widetilde{H}^{1-s}(\Omega)^{n};\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega))}\leq n^{(1-s)/2}

(3.2)

follows by interpolation.

Remark 7
(alternative Sobolev spaces).

The extension of

\nabla:H^{s}(\Omega)\to H^{s-1}(\Omega)^{n}
for a general real
s
is linear and bounded except for
s=1/2

in the standard textbook
[
8
, Thm 1.4.4.6]
. This is not a contradiction to (
3.2
) for all real
s

because
[
8
]

utilises

H^{-1/2}(\Omega)
as the topological dual of

H^{1/2}_{0}(\Omega)=H^{1/2}(\Omega)
which gives
\widetilde{H}^{-1/2}(\Omega)
.

4
Poincaré inequality in
H^{s}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
for
0\leq s\leq 1

This subsection investigates the constant
\|B\|_{s}
in the estimate

\|B\|_{s}^{-1}\,\|g\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}\leq\sup_{v\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}g\,\textrm{div}\,v\,dx}{\|v\|_{\widetilde{H}^{1-s}(\Omega)}}\leq n^{(1-s)/2}\,\|g\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}

(4.1)

for all
g\in H^{s}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
. Here and throughout this paper
L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)=L^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}

abbreviates the Lebesgue functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with integral zero over the domain.
Since the supremum defines the norm
\|\nabla g\|_{H^{s-1}(\Omega)}
,
(
4.1
) is a Poincaré inequality (sometimes called Poincaré–Friedrichs inequality) for
s=0
and an important duality estimate
for
s=1
usually attributed to Nečas with applications to a proof of Korn’s inequality.

The remaining parts of this subsection are devoted to the proof of
(
4.1
) based on
Bogovskiĭ’s right-inverse of the divergence operator.
The proof departs from duality

\|g\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}=\sup_{f\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}g\,f\,dx}{\|f\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)}}\quad\textrm{for all }g\in H^{s}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

(4.2)

Since
1\in H^{s}(\Omega)
, the integral mean
\overline{f}=\langle f,1\rangle/|\Omega|
is well defined for
f\in\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)

and the continuous linear operator

f\mapsto f-\overline{f}
has a norm at most one in
L(L^{2}(\Omega))
and in
L(\widetilde{H}^{-1}(\Omega))
and so also in

L(\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega))
, i.e.,

\|f-\overline{f}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)}
for
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
This provides the inequality in the dual norm

\|g\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}=\sup_{f\in\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle f,g\rangle}{\|f\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)}}\leq\sup_{f\in\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle f-\overline{f},g\rangle}{\|f-\overline{f}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)}}

with
\langle\overline{f},g\rangle=0
in the last step. The upper bound allows the substitution of
f-\overline{f}
for
f\in\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)
by

f\in\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and therefore provides “
\leq
” in (
4.2
); its converse “
\geq
” is obvious.

Bogovskiĭ’s right-inverse of the divergence operator
[
6
]
serves as a standard tool in the analysis of the Stokes equations
and
[
7
, Thm 2.5]
provides the existence of
B\in L(\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega);\widetilde{H}^{1-s}(\Omega)^{n}
with operator norm
\|B\|_{s}

and the right-inverse property

v:=Bf\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{n}\quad\textrm{satisfies }\textrm{div}\,v=f\quad\textrm{for all }f\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

This enables the substitution of
f\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
in the supremum of (
4.2
) by
\textrm{div}\,v=f
for

v:=Bf\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{n}
. This and
\|v\|_{\widetilde{H}^{1-s}(\Omega)}\leq\|B\|_{s}\,\|f\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)}
reveals the
first inequality in (
4.1
). The second follows from

\int_{\Omega}g\,\textrm{div}\,v\,dx\leq\|\widetilde{\textrm{div}}\,v\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)}\|g\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}
and

\|\widetilde{\textrm{div}}\,v\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)}\leq n^{(1-s)/2}\|v\|_{\widetilde{H}^{1-s}(\Omega)}
by (
3.2
).

5
Proof of tr-dev-div (
1.1
) for
a particular
\Sigma

We select
\tau\in H^{s}_{\mathrm{tr0}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}):=

\{\sigma\in H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}):\;\mathrm{tr}\,\sigma\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\}
,
a closed subspace of
H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
. Then

g:=\textrm{tr}\,\tau\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and
(
4.1
) motivates the analysis of
\int_{\Omega}g\,\textrm{div}\,v\,dx

for some
v\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{n}
. Since
n^{-1}\textrm{tr}\,\tau\,\mathrm{id}=\tau-\textrm{dev}\,\tau
a.e. in
\Omega
, some algebra
reveals

n^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\textrm{tr}\,\tau\,\textrm{div}\,v\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\tau:Dv\,dx-\int_{\Omega}(\textrm{dev}\,\tau):Dv\,dx

with the functional matrix
Dv\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
that consists of the row-wise action of the gradient
\widetilde{\nabla}
.
Recall

v\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{n}
and infer

\int_{\Omega}\tau:Dv\,dx=-\langle\textrm{div}\,\tau,v\rangle\;\leq\;\|\textrm{div}\,\tau\|_{H^{s-1}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{\widetilde{H}^{1-s}(\Omega)}.

Since
Dv
is the row-wise application of
\widetilde{\nabla}
, (
3.2
)
provides
\|Dv\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)}\leq n^{s/2}\,\|v\|_{\widetilde{H}^{1-s}(\Omega)}
. Thus

-\int_{\Omega}(\textrm{dev}\,\tau):Dv\,dx\leq\|\textrm{dev}\,\tau\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}\|Dv\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-s}(\Omega)}\leq n^{s/2}\,\|\textrm{dev}\,\tau\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{\widetilde{H}^{1-s}(\Omega)}.

Altogether, for any
v\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{n}
with
\|v\|_{\widetilde{H}^{1-s}(\Omega)}=1
,

n^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\textrm{tr}\,\tau\,\textrm{div}\,v\,dx\leq n^{s/2}\,\|\textrm{dev}\,\tau\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}+\|\textrm{div}\,\tau\|_{H^{s-1}(\Omega)}.

This and the first estimate in (
4.1
) conclude the proof of
(
1.1
) for

\Sigma=H^{s}_{\mathrm{tr0}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})

with the constant
C_{\mathrm{tdd}}=n^{1+s/2}\|B\|_{s}
.

6
Proof of tr-dev-div (
1.1
) for general
\Sigma

Recall
\Sigma
is a closed linear subspace of

H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})

that does not include the identity
\mathrm{id}
. Suppose the trace-dev-div estimate (
1.1
) fails and so we find some sequence

\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\dots
in

\Sigma\subset H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})

with (for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
)

\|\textrm{tr}\,\tau_{j}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{j\to\infty}\|\textrm{dev}\,\tau_{j}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}=0=\lim_{j\to\infty}\|\textrm{div}\,\tau_{j}\|_{H^{s-1}(\Omega)}.

Without loss of generality suppose that the integral mean
\mu_{j}:=|\Omega|^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\textrm{tr}\,\tau_{j}\,dx\geq 0

is non-negative. Owing to the previous subsection,

\dot{\tau}_{j}:=\tau_{j}-\mu_{j}n^{-1}\mathrm{id}\in

H^{s}_{\mathrm{tr0}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})

satisfies (
1.1
).
Since the divergence and the deviator part of
\tau_{j}
and
\dot{\tau}_{j}
are identical, we infer

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|\textrm{tr}\,\dot{\tau}_{j}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}=0<1=\lim_{j\to\infty}\|\textrm{tr}\,\tau_{j}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)},

whence
\lim_{j\to\infty}\mu_{j}\|1\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}=1
. In other words,

\lim_{j\to\infty}\tau_{j}=\|1\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}^{-1}n^{-1}\mathrm{id}
. Since

\Sigma\ni\tau_{j}
is closed, it
contains the limit

\|1\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}^{-1}n^{-1}\mathrm{id}\in\Sigma

and the vector space
\Sigma
contains
\mathrm{id}
.

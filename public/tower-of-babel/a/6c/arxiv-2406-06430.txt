Decomposition of higher Deligne-Lusztig representations
Sian Nie

Abstract
. Higher Deligne-Lusztig representations are virtual smooth representations of parahoric subgroups in a p p -adic group. They are natural analogs of classical Deligne-Lusztig representations of reductive groups over finite fields. The most interesting higher Deligne-Lusztig representations are those attached to elliptic maximal tori, whose compact inductions are expected to realize supercuspidal representations of p p -adic groups. Under a mild condition on p p , in this paper we establish an explicit decomposition of these higher Deligne-Lusztig representations into irreducible summands. Surprisingly, all the irreducible summands are built in the same way as those in Yu’s construction of irreducible supercuspidal representations, the only difference being that the Weil-Heisenberg representations in Yu’s construction are replaced by their geometric analogs. As an application, we show that each irreducible supercuspidal representation of a p p -adic group, attached to an unramified cuspidal datum, is a direct summand of the compact induction of a suitable higher Deligne-Lusztig representation, whenever the cardinality of the residue field of the p p -adic field is not too small.

1.1.
Background and motivations

In the seminal work
[
12

, Deligne and Lusztig introduced a geometric way to construct representations of finite reductive groups, using the cohomology of so-called Deligne-Lusztig varieties. The (virtual) representations arisen this way are called Deligne-Lusztig representations, which play a central role in building up a powerful and elegant representation theory for finite reductive groups, known as Deligne-Lusztig theory.

Given the great success in the finite setting, in
[

p

The motivations are twofold. On one hand, higher Deligne-Lusztig varieties are of independent interest which admit very nice cohomological and arithmetic properties. On the other hand, the associated higher Deligne-Lusztig representations can be used to realize irreducible supercuspidal representations of
p

1.2.
Irreducible decomposition

The main purpose of this paper is to give an explicit irreducible decomposition for elliptic higher Deligne-Lusztig representations, which presents a striking resemblance between their irreducible summands and Yu’s representations. To this end, we employ a strategy inspired from the work of Chen-Stasinski
[
11
]
. The key is to introduce a new
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-variety of Deligne-Lusztig type

Z_{\phi,U,r},

which also admits a natural action by
G_{\textsl{x}}^{F}\times T_{\textsl{x}}^{F}
. Similarly, using Deligne-Lusztig induction above one obtains another (virtual)
G_{\textsl{x}}^{F}
-module

\mathcal{R}_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi):=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}H_{c}^{i}(Z_{\phi,U,r},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})[\phi|_{T_{\textsl{x}}^{F}}].

The first step is to show that

Proposition 1.1
.

If
p
is not a bad prime for
G
and
p\nmid|\pi_{1}(G_{\rm der})|
, then
R_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi)=\mathcal{R}_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi)
as virtual
G_{\textsl{x}}^{F}
-modules.

The proof of Proposition
1.1
is to show the equalities

\langle R_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi),R_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi)\rangle_{G_{r}^{F}}=\langle\mathcal{R}_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi),R_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi)\rangle_{G_{r}^{F}}=\langle\mathcal{R}_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi),\mathcal{R}_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi)\rangle_{G_{r}^{F}}.

The first inner product is computed by Chan
[
4
]
. By extending methods of Lusztig
[
27
]
, Chen-Stasinski
[
10
]
and Yu
[
30
]
, we compute the last two and it turns out that all the three inner products coincide. This concludes the desired equality
R_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi)=\mathcal{R}_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi)
.

Remark 1.2
.

In a follow-up work
[
23
]
, we will give a cohomology-theoretic proof of the equality
R_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi)=\mathcal{R}_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi)
, without computing inner products.

Given Proposition
1.1
, the problem is reduced to the study of
\mathcal{R}_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi)
. Compared with
X_{T,U,r}
, the variety
Z_{\phi,U,r}
has much simpler structure. As a result we are able to give an explicit decomposition of
\mathcal{R}_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi)
. To describe it, we invoke a result by Howe
[
18
]
and Kaletha
[
25
]
on Howe factorizations of smooth characters
\phi:T^{F}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}
. It says that if
p
is as in Proposition
1.1
then there is a generic datum (see §
3.2
)

(G^{i},\phi_{i},r_{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant d},

where
T\subseteq G^{0}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq G^{d}=G
are
k
-rational Levi subgroups of
G
and
\phi_{i}:(G^{i})^{F}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}
are characters satisfying certain genericity conditions relative to the integer sequence
0\leqslant r_{0}<r_{1}<\cdots<r_{d-1}\leqslant r_{d}
such that

\phi_{-1}:=\phi^{-1}\prod_{i=0}^{d}\phi|_{T^{F}}

is a character (of
T^{F}
) of depth
0
. Following Yu’s construction, we consider the following subgroups

\displaystyle K_{\phi}=K_{\phi,{\textsl{x}}}

\displaystyle=(G^{0})_{\textsl{x}}(G^{1})_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{0}/2}\cdots(G^{d})_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{d}/2}\subseteq G_{\textsl{x}}

\displaystyle H_{\phi}=H_{\phi,{\textsl{x}}}

\displaystyle=(G^{0})_{\textsl{x}}^{0+}(G^{1})_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{0}/2}\cdots(G^{d})_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{d}/2}\subseteq K_{\phi},

where
(G^{i+1})_{\textsl{x}}^{s}\subseteq(G^{i+1})_{\textsl{x}}
denotes the
s
th Moy-Prasad subgroup attached to
x
for
s\in\tilde{}\mathbb{R}
. We show that each isotropic space

H_{c}^{i}(Z_{\phi,U,r}\cap H_{\phi,r},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})[\phi|_{(T_{\textsl{x}}^{0+})^{F}}]

is a natural
K_{\phi}^{F}
-module, where
H_{\phi,r}\subseteq G_{r}
is the natural image of
H_{\phi}
and
T_{\textsl{x}}^{0+}
is the pro-unipotent radical of
T_{\textsl{x}}
. Define

\kappa_{\phi}=\kappa_{\phi,U}:=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}H_{c}^{i}(Z_{\phi,U,r}\cap H_{\phi,r},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})[\phi|_{(T_{\textsl{x}}^{0+})^{F}}].

The next step is the following result.

Proposition 1.3
.

The virtual
K_{\phi}^{F}
-module
\kappa_{\phi}
is irreducible.

Note that the restriction
\kappa_{\phi}|_{H_{\phi,r}^{F}}
is still obtained from the cohomological Deligne-Lusztig induction. Hence we can use the Künneth formula (as in
[
12
, §6.6]
) to show that

\langle\kappa_{\phi}|_{H_{\phi,r}^{F}},\kappa_{\phi}|_{H_{\phi,r}^{F}}\rangle_{H_{\phi,r}^{F}}=1,

that is,

is irreducible. However, the virtual
K_{\phi}^{F}
-module
\kappa_{\phi}
is not constructed by Deligne-Lusztig induction. So it is much more challenging to show the irreducibility of

Proposition 1.4

(Theorem
6.2
)
.

There is a unique non-negative integer

such that
H_{c}^{i}(Z_{\phi,U,r}\cap H_{\phi,r},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})[\phi|_{(T_{\textsl{x}}^{0+})^{F}}]\neq\{0\}
if and only if

.

Remark 1.5
.

When the pair
(T,U)
is of Coxeter type, an analogous concentration property also holds for certain closed subsets of higher Deligne-Lusztig varieties

, see
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
,
[

]
and
[

]
.

Proposition

[

]
and
[

]

Having the above preparations, we now state the main theorem.

Theorem 1.6

(Theorem
7.5
)
.

Let

be as in Proposition
1.1
and let notation be as above. Suppose

R_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi)=\mathcal{R}_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi)={\rm ind}_{K_{\phi}^{F}}^{G_{\textsl{x}}^{F}}\kappa_{\phi}\otimes R_{T,U,0}^{G^{0}}(\phi_{-1})=\sum_{\rho}m_{\rho}{\rm ind}_{K_{\phi}^{F}}^{G_{\textsl{x}}^{F}}\kappa_{\phi}\otimes\rho,

where

is a classical Deligne-Lusztig representation for the reductive quotient of
(G_{\textsl{x}}^{0})^{F}
(viewed as a

-module by inflation), and

ranges over its irreducible summands with multiplicitiy

.

Moreover, the summands

are pairwise non-isomorphic irreducible representations of

.

Remark 1.7
.

Pioneering results were obtained by Chan-Oi
[
9
]
when

is toral and by Chen-Stasinski
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
when

is regular and

is hyperspecial, using different methods. In both results,

is an irreducible

-module formulated in terms of Yu’s representations

]
and Gérardin’s representations
[

]
, respectively.

Remark 1.8
.

Note that in Theorem

the irreducible

-modules

\pm\kappa_{\phi}\otimes\rho

are constructed in the same spirit of Yu’s construction. The only different is that the Weil-Heisenberg representation

used in Yu’s construction is replaced by the representation

arising from geometry. In fact, the group

has a finite quotient isomorphic to a Heisenberg

1.3.
Application

Now we discuss an application of Theorem
1.6
on supercuspidal representations of
p
-adic groups. In
[
30
]
, Yu introduced the notion of cuspidal
G

\pi_{\Xi}
of
G^{F}
. Thanks to work by Kim
[

]
and Fintzen
[

, it is known that when

does not divide the order of the absolute Weyl group of

G^{F}
are exhausted by Yu’s representations
\pi_{\Xi}
.

Recall that a cuspidal

contains a sequence of tamely ramified Levi subgroups

and a point

in the Bruhat-Tits building of

. We say
\Xi
is unramified if
G^{0}
(and hence all the Levi subgroups

) splits over
{\breve{k}}

Our second main result is the following.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
p
be as in Theorem
1.6
and assume that
q
be sufficiently large. Let
\Xi
be an unramified cuspidal
G
-datum as above. Then
\pi_{\Xi}
is a direct summand of the compact induction

\text{c-}{\rm ind}_{Z^{F}G_{\textsl{x}}^{F}}^{G^{F}}R_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi),

where
Z
is the center of
G
and
R_{T,U,r}^{G}(\phi)
is some higher Deligne-Lusztig representation as in Theorem
1.6
, extended to a
Z^{F}G_{\textsl{x}}^{F}
-module on which
Z^{F}
acts via
\phi
.

We refer to Theorem
8.8
for the precise largeness condition on
q
. The proof is based on combining Theorem
1.6
with the methods of Kim
[
29
]
and Fintzen
[
14
]
.

Remark 1.10
.

If
\Xi=(S,\theta)
is a toral cuspidal

-datum, with

an unramified elliptic maximal torus and
\theta:S^{F}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{\times}
a character of depth
\geqslant r
, it is proved by Chan and Oi

]
that

\pi_{\Xi}\cong\text{c-}{\rm ind}_{Z^{F}G_{\textsl{x}}^{F}}^{G^{F}}R_{S,V,r}^{G}(\theta\varepsilon[\theta]),

where

is some/any

-fixed point in the apartment of

over
{\breve{k}}
, and

is certain quadratic character of

. For general

, there is a lack of information on the construction of

in Corollary
1.9
. The reason is that the relation between the Weil-Heisenberg representations

and their geometric analogs

is unclear. Guided by the work
[

]
and

]
, we expect that

.

3.1.
Generic datum

A generic datum of

is a tuple

such that

•

•

for
0\leqslant i\leqslant d
;

•

0=:r_{-1}<r_{0}<\cdots<r_{d-1}\leqslant r_{d}
if

and
0\leqslant r_{0}
if

;

•

\phi_{i}
is of depth

and is

-generic in the sense of
[
30
, §9]
for

;

•

\phi_{d}
is of depth

if

and is trivial otherwise.

Moreover, we say
\Lambda
is normalized if (the pull-back of)

is trivial over
(G_{\rm sc}^{i})^{F}
for

.

Let
\Lambda=(G^{i},\phi_{i},r_{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant d}
be a normalized generic datum such that
{\textsl{x}}\in\mathcal{B}(G^{0},k)
. We define
the following

-stable subgroups

\displaystyle K_{\Lambda}=K_{G,\Lambda}

\displaystyle=(G^{0})_{\textsl{x}}(G^{1})_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{0}/2}\cdots(G^{d})_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{d-1}/2};

\displaystyle H_{\Lambda}=H_{G,\Lambda}

\displaystyle=(G^{0})_{\textsl{x}}^{0+}(G^{1})_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{0}/2}\cdots(G^{d})_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{d-1}/2};

\displaystyle K_{\Lambda}^{+}=K_{G,\Lambda}^{+}

\displaystyle=(G^{0})_{\textsl{x}}^{0+}(G^{1})_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{0}/2+}\cdots(G^{d})_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{d-1}/2+};

\displaystyle E_{\Lambda}=E_{G,\Lambda}

\displaystyle=(G^{0}_{\rm der})_{\textsl{x}}^{0+,0+}(G^{1}_{\rm der})_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{0}+,r_{0}/2+}\cdots(G^{d}_{\rm der})_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{d-1}+,r_{d-1}/2+}.

Here
(G^{i}_{\rm der})_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{i-1}+,r_{i-1}/2+}\subseteq G_{\textsl{x}}
is the subgroup generated by
(G^{i}_{\rm der})_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{i-1}+}
and
(G^{\alpha})_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{i-1}/2+}
for

, where

is any
{\breve{k}}
-splitting maximal torus of
G^{0}
, and
G^{\alpha}\subseteq G
is the root subgroup corresponding to
\alpha
.

For
0\leqslant i\leqslant d
let
\hat{\phi}_{i}
be the character of
(G^{i})_{\textsl{x}}^{F}(G_{\textsl{x}}^{r_{i}/2+})^{F}
defined in
[
30

, which extends
\phi_{i}
. We define a character of
(K_{\Lambda}^{+})^{F}
by

\chi_{\Lambda}=\prod_{i=0}^{d}\hat{\phi}_{i}|_{(K_{\Lambda}^{+})^{F}}.

By definition

is trivial on
(E_{\Lambda})^{F}
.

Let

be
{\breve{k}}
-rational Borel subgroup of

with unipotent radical

. Note that the set
\Phi(B,T)
of roots in

forms a positive system of
\Phi(G,T)
. We say a
{\breve{k}}
-rational Levi subgroup

is standard with respect to

or

if
\Phi(M,T)
is standard with respect to

, that is, simple roots of

are also simple roots of
\Phi(B,T)
. In this case,
M
and
U
generates a parabolic subgroup

of

, where

is the unipotent radical of

.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a
{\breve{k}}
-rational Borel subgroup
B
containing
T
such that
(G^{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant d}
is standard with respect to
B
.

Proof.

We fix a Borel subgroup
B
containing
T
. Let
W
be the Weyl group of the root system
\Phi(G,T)
. It suffices to show there exists
w\in W
such that
\Phi(G^{i},T)
is standard with respect to
w(\Phi(B,T))
for
0\leqslant i\leqslant d
.

We argue by induction on

. If
d=1
, the statement is a well-known result on root systems. Now assume that

and the statement holds for

. We show it also holds for

. Indeed, by induction hypothesis there exists

such that
\Phi(G^{d},T)
is standard with respect to
x(\Phi(B,T))
. Note that
\Phi(G^{d},T)\cap x(\Phi(B,T))
is a positive system of
\Phi(G^{d},T)
. By induction hypothesis there exists

such that
\Phi(G^{i},T)
(
0\leqslant i\leqslant d-1
) is standard with respect to

u(\Phi(G^{d},T)\cap x(\Phi(B,T)))=\Phi(G^{d},T)\cap ux(\Phi(B,T)).

Here
W(G^{d})
denotes the Weyl group of
\Phi(G^{d},T)
. Let

. By construction, it follows that
\Phi(G^{i},T)
is standard with respect to

for

. The proof is finished.
∎

Optical manipulation of matter waves
Kamran Akbari, Valerio Di Giulio, F. Javier García de Abajo

Abstract
Light is extensively used to steer the motion of atoms in free space, enabling cooling and trapping of matter waves through ponderomotive forces and Doppler-mediated photon scattering. Likewise, light interaction with free electrons has recently emerged as a versatile approach to modulate the electron wave function for applications in ultrafast electron microscopy. Here, we combine these two worlds by theoretically demonstrating that matter waves can be optically manipulated via inelastic interactions with optical fields, allowing us to modulate the translational wave function and produce temporally and spatially compressed atomic beam pulses. Specifically, we realize such modulation through stimulated photon absorption and emission by atoms traversing phase-matching evanescent optical fields generated upon light scattering by a nanostructure, but also via stimulated Compton scattering in free space without any assistance from material media. Our results support optical manipulation of matter waves as a powerful tool for microscopy, spectroscopy, and the exploration of novel fundamental phenomena associated with light-atom interactions.

II
Inelastic interaction between free atoms and optical fields

We based our results on a rigorous quantum description of free atoms, which leads to an effective Hamiltonian for the CM motion, encapsulating the internal atomic degrees of freedom and accounting for both elastic and inelastic atom-photon processes. The object of our study is a neutral atom moving with nonrelativistic velocity
{\bf v}
in the presence of external illumination, which is introduced through its associated classical vector potential
{\bf A}({\bf r},t)
within the minimal coupling scheme in the Coulomb gauge. We assume moderate light intensities and optical frequencies that are far from any electronic resonance compared with the width of the latter, so that the atom is just slightly perturbed from its initial internal ground state during the interaction period. Near-resonant stimulated Compton scattering

A disadvantage of PINAM is that it requires a material structure to mediate the atom-light interaction, and this imposes severe restrictions on the optical modes and light intensities that can be employed, as well as a minimum atom energy required to produce phase matching. i=1,2

v\ll c

Concluding remarks

The atom-light inelastic interactions here explored open a viable approach to shape the translational wave function of free-space atoms boldly beyond currently existing capabilities
Gould et al. 1986
;
Bucksbaum et al. 1988
;
Berkhout et al. 1989

;

;

. For a coupling strength

, maximum compression is observed in the CM wave function of Eq. (
4
) after a propagation distance

;

d\approx 0.0733\,z_{\rm T}/|\beta|
. For example, for Rb atoms (mass

Control over the transverse wave function is also possible through the phase of both
\varphi
and
\beta

\beta
for SCS is directly given by the differences between the phases of the two externally applied light waves [see Eq. (
7

;
Cai et al. 2018

, as well as focused atomic beams

. This approach could be used to implement beam splitters and mixers (Fig.

, quantum erasers, and delayed-choice setups

.

The present results can be readily extended to molecular beams, thus suggesting applications in precise epitaxial growth, as well as in the control of nanoscale chemical reactions. A direct extension of the PINEM formalism to ions shows that the coupling coefficient should be linear in the charge. However, free-space optical manipulation of ions through ponderomotive forces is limited by the inverse-mass dependence of the
A^{2}
coupling Hamiltonian. In contrast, the coupling coefficient is independent of the atomic mass in the SCS configuration here investigated for neutral atoms.

By incorporating resonant components in the external illumination, additional means of control of atoms and molecules are enabled by engaging their internal excitations, which could be activated, for example, through
\pi
pulses introduced at designated positions along the particle path, thus reconfiguring the set of available optical transitions to engineer the SCS interaction, and also providing an alternative source of CM momentum. In molecules with rotational and vibrational degrees of freedom, low-energy excitations provide an extra range of resonance frequencies, over two orders of magnitude below electronic excitations, thus suggesting the use of external illumination extending from the infrared to the microwaves for near-resonant inelastic SCS manipulation.

Appendix B
Light-atom interaction: effective Hamiltonian

The time-dependent state of the atom can be expanded in the complete set of free-atom states as

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi(t)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bf p}j}c_{{\bf p}j}(t)\,{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}(\varepsilon_{\bf p}+\omega_{j})t}\,\ket{{\bf p}j}.

From the Schrödinger equation
\hat{\mathcal{H}}(t)\ket{\Psi(t)}={\rm i}\hbar\ket{\dot{\Psi}(t)}
, the expansion coefficients
c_{{\bf p}j}(t)
are found to follow the equation of motion

\displaystyle{\rm i}\hbar\,\dot{c}_{{\bf p}j}(t)=\sum_{{\bf p}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}

\displaystyle c_{{\bf p}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t)\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}(\varepsilon_{{\bf p}{\bf p}^{\prime}}+\omega_{jj^{\prime}})t}

(19)

\displaystyle\times\big[H^{\prime}_{{\bf p}j,{\bf p}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t)+H^{\prime\prime}_{{\bf p}j,{\bf p}^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(t)\big],

where
\varepsilon_{{\bf p}{\bf p}^{\prime}}=\varepsilon_{\bf p}-\varepsilon_{{\bf p}^{\prime}}
, whereas the matrix elements of
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}(t)
and
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime\prime}(t)
are given by Eqs. (
16

We assume the system to be initially prepared in the ground state
j=0
[i.e.,
c_{{\bf p}j}(-\infty)=0
for
j\neq 0
], and further adopt the following two approximations: (
i
) the population of any excited state
\ket{j\neq 0}
is small compared to that of
\ket{0}
, so that we only consider transitions to
\ket{j\neq 0}
coming from
\ket{0}
; and (
ii
) the process is Markovian
Louisell 1973
, implying that the probability of each event only depends on the state reached in the immediately previous event. We then follow a standard procedure consisting in solving
c_{{\bf p}j}(t)
for
j\neq 0
and introducing the result back in Eq. (
19
) to obtain an effective Schrödinger equation for the
j=0
component. In doing so, we find that
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime\prime}
produces a contribution
\propto\,A^{2}
in
c_{{\bf p},j\neq 0}(t)
, and thus terms
\propto\,A^{4}
in the effective Hamiltonian, which are negligible for the field strengths considered in this work. Consequently, the solution for the expansion coefficients with
j\neq 0
can be approximated by integrating Eq. (
19
) without including
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime\prime}
. This leads to

\displaystyle c_{{\bf p}j}(t)\approx-\frac{{\rm i}}{\hbar}\sum_{{\bf p}^{\prime}}\int_{-\infty}^{t}dt^{\prime}\;c_{{\bf p}^{\prime}0}(t^{\prime})\,H^{\prime}_{{\bf p}j,{\bf p}^{\prime}0}(t^{\prime})\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}(\varepsilon_{{\bf p}{\bf p}^{\prime}}+\omega_{j0})t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\approx\!-\frac{1}{\hbar}\!\sum_{{\bf p}^{\prime}}c_{{\bf p}^{\prime}0}(t)\!\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\,H^{\prime}_{{\bf p}j,{\bf p}^{\prime}0,\omega}\;\frac{{\rm e}^{{\rm i}(\varepsilon_{{\bf p}{\bf p}^{\prime}}+\omega_{j0}-\omega)t}}{\varepsilon_{{\bf p}{\bf p}^{\prime}}+\omega_{j0}-\omega-{\rm i}\kappa_{j}/2},

(20)

j=0

i

18

\kappa_{j}
of the excited atomic state
\ket{j}
. Also, we move
c_{{\bf p}0}(t)
outside the integral under the assumption that these coefficients vary slowly compared with the relatively fast oscillations of the exponential factor
Louisell 1973
[approximation (
ii
)]. Then, we insert Eq. (
20

19
) and specify the left-hand side for
j=0
to write

\displaystyle{\rm i}\hbar\,\dot{c}_{{\bf p}0}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bf p}^{\prime}}c_{{\bf p}^{\prime}0}(t)\,H^{\rm eff}_{{\bf p}{\bf p}^{\prime}}(t)\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\varepsilon_{{\bf p}{\bf p}^{\prime}}t},

(21)

where

\displaystyle H^{\rm eff}_{{\bf p}{\bf p}^{\prime}}(t)=\frac{e^{2}Z}{2m_{\rm e}c^{2}V}\frac{M}{m_{\rm n}}\int\!d^{3}{\bf r}\;{\rm e}^{{\rm i}({\bf p}^{\prime}-{\bf p})\cdot{\bf r}}\,A^{2}({\bf r},t)

(22)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\hbar}\sum_{{\bf p}^{\prime\prime}j}\iint\frac{d\omega d\omega^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})t}\,\frac{H^{\prime}_{{\bf p}0,{\bf p}^{\prime\prime}j,\omega^{\prime}}\,H^{\prime}_{{\bf p}^{\prime\prime}j,{\bf p}^{\prime}0,\omega}}{\varepsilon_{{\bf p}^{\prime\prime}{\bf p}^{\prime}}+\omega_{j0}-\omega-{\rm i}\kappa_{j}/2}

is the effective interaction Hamiltonian that describes the evolution of the CM. We note that the first term in Eq. (
22

\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime\prime}(t)
at order
\propto\,A^{2}
in the external field.

At this point, we adopt the nonrecoil approximation, so that the atom velocity
{\bf v}
is taken to be constant during the interaction with the optical field
García de Abajo and Di
Giulio 2020

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{{\bf p}{\bf p}^{\prime}}\approx({\bf p}-{\bf p}^{\prime})\cdot{\bf v}.

In addition, we approximate
{\bf p}^{\prime}\approx M{\bf v}/\hbar
in the rightmost part of Eq. (
16a
), which becomes

\displaystyle H^{\prime}_{{\bf p}j,{\bf p}^{\prime}j^{\prime},\omega}

(23)

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{{\rm i}e}{cV}\;{\bf d}_{jj^{\prime}}\cdot\bigg\{\omega_{jj^{\prime}}\,{\bf A}_{{\bf p}-{\bf p}^{\prime},\omega}+({\bf p}-{\bf p}^{\prime})\,\big[{\bf v}\cdot{\bf A}_{{\bf p}-{\bf p}^{\prime},\omega}\big]\bigg\}

\displaystyle H^{\rm eff}({\bf r},t)=\sum_{\bf p}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}{\bf p}\cdot{\bf r}}\,H^{\rm eff}_{{\bf p}0}(t)

\displaystyle\psi({\bf r},t)=V^{-1/2}\sum_{\bf p}c_{{\bf p}0}(t)\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}{\bf p}\cdot{\bf r}-{\rm i}\varepsilon_{\bf p}t}

Substituting Eq. (

\displaystyle H^{\rm eff}({\bf r},t)=\frac{e^{2}Z}{2m_{\rm e}c^{2}}\frac{M}{m_{\rm n}}\,A^{2}({\bf r},t)

\displaystyle H^{\rm eff}({\bf r},t)=\frac{e^{2}Z}{2m_{\rm e}c^{2}}\frac{M}{m_{\rm n}}\,A^{2}({\bf r},t)

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar c^{2}}\sum_{j\neq 0}\int\frac{d^{3}{\bf q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}-{\rm i}\omega t}\,\frac{|\hat{\bf x}\cdot{\bf d}_{j0}|^{2}}{(\omega_{j0}+{\bf q}\cdot{\bf v}-\omega-{\rm i}\kappa_{j}/2)}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\bigg\{\omega_{j0}\,{\bf A}({\bf r},t)+{\rm i}\nabla_{\bf r}\,\big[{\bf v}\cdot{\bf A}({\bf r},t)\big]\bigg\}\cdot\bigg\{\omega_{j0}\,{\bf A}_{{\bf q},\omega}+{\bf q}\,\big[{\bf v}\cdot{\bf A}_{{\bf q},\omega}\big]\bigg\},

As a relevant configuration, we note that the effective Hamiltonian in Eq. (

{\bf v}
. Then, using the Thomas-Reiche-Kuhn (TRK) sum rule

;

;
Kuhn 1925

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq 0}\omega_{j0}\,|\hat{\bf x}\cdot{\bf d}_{0j}|^{2}=\frac{\hbar Z}{2m_{\rm e}}\frac{M}{m_{\rm n}}

\displaystyle H^{\rm eff}({\bf r},t)=\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar c^{2}}\sum_{j\neq 0}\omega_{j0}\,|\hat{\bf x}\cdot{\bf d}_{j0}|^{2}\int\frac{d^{3}{\bf q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}-{\rm i}\omega t}\,\frac{{\bf q}\cdot{\bf v}-\omega}{\omega_{j0}+{\bf q}\cdot{\bf v}-\omega-{\rm i}\kappa_{j}/2}\,{\bf A}({\bf r},t)\cdot{\bf A}_{{\bf q},\omega}.

Appendix C
PINAM interaction with monochromatic evanescent fields

We consider an atom moving with velocity
{\bf v}
along the
z

{\bf E}({\bf r},t)={\bf E}({\bf R})\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}q_{z}z-{\rm i}\omega t}+{\rm c.c.}

\omega

q_{z}

{\bf A}({\bf r},t)=-({\rm i}c/\omega)\,{\bf E}({\bf R})\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}q_{z}z-{\rm i}\omega t}+{\rm c.c.}
with
{\bf R}=(x,y)
. Inserting this expression in Eq. (
26

3

and two more terms proportional to
\exp\{\pm 2{\rm i}(q_{z}z-\omega)t^{\prime}\}

t^{\prime}

L

\omega=q_{z}v
[i.e., Eq. (
5

t^{\prime}
simply yields a factor
L/v
(the interaction time) and the post-interaction CM wave function reduces to

\displaystyle\psi({\bf r},t)=\psi^{\rm inc}({\bf r},t)\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\varphi({\bf R})}\,\exp\big\{-\beta({\bf R})\,{\rm e}^{2{\rm i}\omega(z-vt)/v}+\beta^{*}({\bf R})\,{\rm e}^{-2{\rm i}\omega(z-vt)/v}\big\},

(29)

where

\displaystyle\varphi({\bf R})

\displaystyle=-\frac{e^{2}ZL}{\hbar m_{\rm e}v\omega^{2}}\frac{M}{m_{\rm n}}\,\big|{\bf E}({\bf R})\big|^{2}+\frac{2e^{2}L}{\hbar^{2}v\omega^{2}}\sum_{j\neq 0}\frac{|\hat{\bf x}\cdot{\bf d}_{0j}|^{2}}{\omega_{j0}}\bigg(\omega^{2}_{j0}\,\big|{\bf E}({\bf R})\big|^{2}+v^{2}\big|(q_{z}\hat{\bf z}-{\rm i}\nabla_{\bf R})\,E_{z}({\bf R})\big|^{2}\bigg),

(30a)

\displaystyle\beta({\bf R})

\displaystyle=-\frac{{\rm i}e^{2}ZL}{2\hbar m_{\rm e}v\omega^{2}}\frac{M}{m_{\rm n}}\,E^{2}({\bf R})+\frac{{\rm i}e^{2}L}{\hbar^{2}v\omega^{2}}\sum_{j\neq 0}\frac{|\hat{\bf x}\cdot{\bf d}_{0j}|^{2}}{\omega_{j0}}\bigg(\omega^{2}_{j0}\,E^{2}({\bf R})-v^{2}\big[(q_{z}\hat{\bf z}-{\rm i}\nabla_{\bf R})\,E_{z}({\bf R})\big]^{2}\bigg)

(30b)

are an elastic phase and an inelastic atom-light coupling coefficient, respectively, and we have neglected
\kappa_{j}
in front of
\omega_{j0}
. This result can be simplified by using the TRK sum rule

;

;
Kuhn 1925
given in Eq. (

sum exactly cancels the first term in the right-hand side of Eqs. (

\displaystyle\varphi({\bf R})=\frac{L}{\hbar v}\,\alpha_{0}\,\big|(\hat{\bf z}-{\rm i}(v/\omega)\nabla_{\bf R})\,E_{z}({\bf R})\big|^{2},

(31a)

\displaystyle\beta({\bf R})=-\frac{{\rm i}L}{2\hbar v}\,\alpha_{0}\,\big[(\hat{\bf z}-{\rm i}(v/\omega)\nabla_{\bf R})\,E_{z}({\bf R})\big]^{2},

(31b)

where
\alpha_{0}=(2e^{2}/\hbar)\sum_{j\neq 0}|\hat{\bf x}\cdot{\bf d}_{0j}|^{2}/\omega_{j0}

The coefficient
\beta({\bf R})
plays a role similar to the coupling coefficient in photon-induced near-field electron microscopy
Barwick et al. 2009
(PINEM), but in contrast to a linear dependence on the field, we find a quadratic scaling. Following similar methods as in PINEM
Park et al. 2010
;
García de Abajo and Di
Giulio 2020

29

4

\ell

and in energy by

{\bf R}
.

As a possible realization of phase-matching monochromatic illumination, we consider an atom reflected from a polariton-supporting planar surface under grazing incidence conditions. To analyze the coupling strength, we then assume parallel motion of the atom relative to the surface, as well as a polariton characterized by an in-plane wave vector
q_{z}
and frequency
\omega
satisfying the phase-matching condition in Eq. (
5

\mathcal{E}_{0}(\hat{\bf z}+{\rm i}\gamma\,\hat{\bf x})\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}q_{z}z-\kappa_{p}x-{\rm i}\omega t}+{\rm c.c.}

and
x

\displaystyle\varphi({\bf R})=\frac{L}{\hbar v}\,\alpha_{0}|\mathcal{E}_{0}|^{2}\,{\rm e}^{-2\kappa_{p}x}(2-v^{2}/c^{2}),

\displaystyle\beta({\bf R})=-\frac{{\rm i}vL}{2\hbar c^{2}}\,\alpha_{0}\mathcal{E}_{0}^{2}\,{\rm e}^{-2\kappa_{p}x}.

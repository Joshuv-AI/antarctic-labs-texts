Convergence of Fourier truncations for compact quantum groups and finitely generated groups
Marc A. Rieffel

Abstract
. We generalize the Fejér-Riesz operator systems defined for the circle group by Connes and van Suijlekom to the setting of compact matrix quantum groups and their ergodic actions on C*-algebras. These truncations form filtrations of the containing C*-algebra. We show that when they and the containing C*-algebra are equipped with suitable quantum metrics, then under suitable conditions they converge to the containing C*-algebra for quantum Gromov-Hausdorff distance. Among other examples, our results are applicable to the quantum groups S ​ U q ​ ( 2 ) SU_{q}(2) and their homogeneous spaces S q 2 S^{2}_{q} .

For a spectral triple

It is extremely interesting to ask

In
[
13
,
57

the operator systems

play an important role. While for the circle the spectral truncations

of functions whose Fourier coefficients

The purpose of the present paper is to give an answer to this latter
question in a quite broad setting, namely that of compact matrix
quantum groups, which includes the case
of finitely generated discrete groups. Our setting
also includes ergodic actions of compact
matrix quantum groups on unital C*-algebras. It seems to me
that the feature underlying the proof in

that

is a compact group.

2.
Preliminaries about compact quantum groups

In this section we gather well-known facts about compact quantum
groups that we will need. We give few proofs,
and instead refer the reader to
[
59
,
17
,
56
,
18
]

and the references they include.

A compact quantum group is a pair
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
, where
{\mathcal{A}}
is a
unital C*-algebra and
\Delta:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}
is a
unital
*
-homomorphism, called the comultiplication, such that

(1)

(\Delta\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}})\Delta=(I^{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\Delta)\Delta
as
homomorphisms
{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}
(coassociativity)

(2)

The spaces
({\mathcal{A}}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{A}})\Delta({\mathcal{A}}):=\mathrm{span}\{(a\otimes 1_{\mathcal{A}})\Delta(b):a,b\in{\mathcal{A}}\}

and
(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}})\Delta({\mathcal{A}})
are
dense in
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}
(the cancellation property).

Here
I^{\mathcal{A}}
is the identity operator on
{\mathcal{A}}
, and the tensor
product is the minimal tensor product for C*-algebras.

A fundamental fact about compact quantum groups is that one
can prove that they have a generalization of the normalized
Haar measure on ordinary compact groups. That is, there is
a unique state, the “Haar state”
{\mathsf{h}}:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
that
satisfies the left and right invariance conditions

(I^{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathsf{h}})\circ\Delta={\mathsf{h}}1_{\mathcal{A}}=({\mathsf{h}}\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}})\circ\Delta.

In general the Haar state may not be a trace, and it may not be
faithful on
{\mathcal{A}}
.

The following class of examples is of importance for this paper.

Example 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group. Denote by
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{u}(\Gamma)
the
full group C*-algebra of
\Gamma
. Because
\Gamma
is discrete,
this is a unital C*-algebra. Every
x\in\Gamma
determines
a unitary element,
\delta_{x}
, of
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{u}(\Gamma)
, and the linear span,

C_{f}(\Gamma)
, of these
unitary elements forms a dense
*
-subalgebra of
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{u}(\Gamma)
.
This subalgebra
can be viewed as consisting of the finitely supported functions on
\Gamma
.
The mapping
\delta_{x}\mapsto\delta_{x}\otimes\delta_{x}
determines a unital homomorphism,

\Delta
, from
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{u}(\Gamma)
into
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{u}(\Gamma)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{u}(\Gamma)

that makes
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{u}(\Gamma)
into a compact quantum group.
(Note that in this situation one might expect to use
the maximal C*-tensor product, but for quantum groups
it is usual to use the minimal C*-tensor product,
for which the construction of this example remains valid.)
For this class of examples the Haar state is just the usual tracial state
coming from evaluating functions in
C_{f}(\Gamma)

at the identity element of
\Gamma
.
(For the details of this construction see
[
7
]
; for even locally compact
\Gamma

see the earlier
[
26
]
.) In the case that
\Gamma
is not amenable,
the Haar state on
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{u}(G)
is not faithful,
almost by one definition of amenablilty.
(But the Haar state is always faithful on
C_{f}(\Gamma)

The reduced C*-algebra of
\Gamma
,
defined by the GNS
construction for the Haar state,
is a compact quantum group in essentially the same way.
If
\Gamma=F_{2}
, the free group on two generators, then the
Haar state is faithful on its reduced C*-algebra, but
F_{2}

is not amenable.

For a compact quantum group
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
the
C*-algebra

gives the “space”,
and has its own
*

of finite-dimensional unitary representations of groups

called finite-dimensional unitary “corepresentations”.
Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a finite dimensional Hilbert space
(with inner product linear in the second variable).
Consider the right Hilbert

-module
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}

{\mathcal{A}}

\langle\xi\otimes a,\eta\otimes b\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}=\langle\xi,\eta\rangle a^{*}b

\xi,\eta\in{\mathcal{H}}

A unitary corepresentation of
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
on

is a linear map

u:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}
that satisfies

(1)

(u\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}})\circ u=(I^{\mathcal{H}}\otimes\Delta)\circ u

(2)

\langle u(\xi),u(\eta)\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}=\langle\xi,\eta\rangle{\mathbb{C}}1_{\mathcal{A}}
for
\xi,\eta\in{\mathcal{H}}

(3)

[

Given

u

\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}

\Delta(u_{jk})=\sum^{n}_{\ell=1}u_{j\ell}\otimes u_{\ell k}.

\widehat{\Delta}

All of the unital Hopf

Instead, let

Essentially by definition, a compact Lie group (not necessarily connected)
has a faithful finite-dimensional unitary representation.
By taking the direct sum of this representation with its
conjugate if necessary,
we can assume that this representation is self-conjugate.
By then taking the direct sum with the trivial one-dimensional representation if necessary, we can assume that this representation
is self-conjugate and contains the trivial representation.
There are many choices of such a faithful representation.
Let
G
be a compact Lie group, and let
({\mathcal{H}},U)
be a faithful
finite-dimensional unitary representation that
is self-conjugate and contains the trivial representation.
Let
C(G;U)
be the linear span of the coordinate functions of
U
.
Then
C(G;U)
is a finite-dimensional subspace of
C(G)
that is closed under complex conjugation and contains the constant functions.
Thus the set of
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued functions that it contains
is an order-unit space, and when
C(G)
is viewed as a
commutative C*-algebra,
we see that
C(G;U)
is an operator system.
Because the representation
({\mathcal{H}},U)
is faithful, the functions
in
C(G;U)
separate the points of
G
. Thus the
(algebraic) subalgebra of
C(G)
generated by
C(G;U)
is dense
in
C(G)
, according to the Stone-Weierstrass theorem.
(Because
C(G;U)
is finite-dimensional, we say that
C(G)

is “finitely generated”, with
C(G;U)
as generating set.)
Let
{\mathcal{A}}=C(G)
, and for

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
let
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}

be the linear span of products of
n
elements of
C(G;U)

(with
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}={\mathbb{C}}1_{\mathcal{A}}
).
Then each
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
is a finite-dimensional operator system
in
{\mathcal{A}}
, and for any
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

{\mathcal{A}}_{m}{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{m+n}
. Thus

\{{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\}
is a filtration of the C*-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
.
We view the
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
’s as Fourier truncations of
{\mathcal{A}}
.
We now generalize this structure to the setting of compact
quantum groups.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
be a compact quantum group and let

({\mathcal{H}},u)
be a finite-dimensional corepresentation of

({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
. Much as above, we can arrange that this
corepresentation is self-conjugate and contains the
trivial corepresentation (which
is
u(z)=z\otimes 1_{\mathcal{A}}
for
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
).
Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
be the linear span of the coordinate
elements of
u
, as defined above. It is a finite-dimensional
operator system in
{\mathcal{A}}
. For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

let
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}

be the linear span of products of
n
elements of
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}

(with
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}={\mathbb{C}}1_{\mathcal{A}}
).
Then each
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
is a finite-dimensional operator system
in
{\mathcal{A}}
, and for any
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

{\mathcal{A}}_{m}{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{m+n}
. But in general
the union of the
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
’s is not dense in
{\mathcal{A}}
.

Definition 2.3
.

With notation as just above, we say that a finite-dimensional corepresentation

({\mathcal{H}},u)
is
faithful
if the union of all the
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
’s is
dense in
{\mathcal{A}}
(so
{\mathcal{A}}
is finitely generated and
the
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
’s form a filtration of
{\mathcal{A}}
). A faithful
corepresentation is often called a “fundamental”
corepresentation. In the context of

[
13
,
57
]
we view the
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
’s as Fourier truncations
of
{\mathcal{A}}
.

A compact quantum group that has a faithful finite-dimensional
corepresentation is called a “compact matrix quantum group”.
These are exactly the quantum generalization of compact Lie
groups.

Example 2.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group. We can choose a finite
generating set

that is closed under taking inverses
and contains the identity element of
\Gamma
. The irreducible
corepresentations of
{\mathcal{A}}=C^{*}(\Gamma)
(all versions)
are all one-dimensional,
and correspond to the elements of
\Gamma
. Given
x\in\Gamma
,
for the corresponding corepresentation
u^{x}

we can choose
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathbb{C}}
, and define
u^{x}
by

u^{x}(z)=z\delta_{x}\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}
for
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
u^{S}
be the direct sum of the
u^{x}
’s as
x

ranges over
S
. The range of
u^{S}
can be naturally
identified with the subspace of
functions supported on
S
. It forms a finite dimensional
operator system in
{\mathcal{A}}
that generates
{\mathcal{A}}
. Thus it
determines a familiar filtration of
{\mathcal{A}}=C^{*}(\Gamma)

(used e.g. in
[
45
,
40
,
10
]
), whose elements
we view as Fourier truncations of
C^{*}(\Gamma)
.
In the case that
\Gamma
is
{\mathbb{Z}}
the elements of
this filtration are exactly the
Fejér-Riesz operator systems of
[
13
,
57
]
.

3.
Preliminaries about actions

Just as actions of compact groups on compact spaces are
of much importance, so too, actions of compact quantum
groups on unital C*-algebras are of much importance.
We recall here the facts that we will need. For details, see
for example

[
9
,
42
,
7
,
17
,
54
]
.
Let
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
be a compact quantum group, and let
{\mathcal{B}}

be a unital C*-algebra. By a (left) action of
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
on

{\mathcal{B}}
we mean a unital injective

*
-homomorphism,
\alpha
, from
{\mathcal{B}}

into
{\mathcal{B}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}
such that

(3.1)

(\alpha\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}})\circ\alpha=(I^{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\Delta)\circ\alpha

and the linear span of
\alpha({\mathcal{B}})(1_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}})

is dense in
{\mathcal{B}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}
. (Often
\alpha
is called
a “coaction”. Also, some authors call this a “right” action.
But Li in
[
36
]
calls it a “left” action, and since we make
important use of his results there we will follow his usage.)
Notice that
\Delta
can be viewed as
giving an action of
{\mathcal{A}}
on itself that is appropriately viewed
as the “left regular action” of this quantum group.

For each
\gamma\in\widehat{\Delta}
let

U^{\gamma}=\{u^{\gamma}_{jk}\}
be a unitary matrix
in
M_{n}({\mathcal{A}})
that represents
\gamma
, and thus satisfies

\Delta(u^{\gamma}_{jk})=\sum^{n}_{\ell=1}u^{\gamma}_{j\ell}\otimes u^{\gamma}_{\ell k}.

Then, as described in section 2 of
[
54
]
(and a number
of other places beginning with
[
42
]
),
there are elements
\phi^{\gamma}_{ij}
of
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
such that

\phi^{\beta}_{ij}(u^{\gamma}_{kl})=\delta_{\beta\gamma}\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}

for all
\beta,\gamma
and all appropriate i, j, k, l.
For each
\gamma\in\widehat{\Delta}
define an operator,
E^{\gamma}_{\alpha}
,
from
{\mathcal{B}}
to
{\mathcal{B}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}={\mathcal{B}}
by

E_{\alpha}^{\gamma}=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{\gamma}}(I^{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\phi^{\gamma}_{kk})\circ\alpha,

where
n_{\gamma}
is the dimension of the corepresentation
\gamma
.
(Notice the hint of a trace in this formula.)
Then
E^{\gamma}_{\alpha}
is the projection onto the
\gamma
-isotypic
component,
{\mathcal{B}}^{\gamma}
, of

{\mathcal{B}}
for the action
\alpha
. The product of any two of these projections
for different
\gamma
’s is 0. Also,

\alpha({\mathcal{B}}^{\gamma})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}^{\gamma}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}^{\gamma}
.

We denote the isotypic component of the trivial corepresentation
by
{\mathcal{B}}^{\alpha}
. It consists
exactly of the elements
b
of
{\mathcal{B}}
that are
\alpha
-invariant,
that is,
{\mathcal{B}}^{\alpha}=\{b\in{\mathcal{B}}:\alpha(b)=b\otimes 1_{\mathcal{A}}\}
. In
lemma 4 of

[
9
]
Boca shows that there is a canonical conditional
expectation,
E
, from
{\mathcal{B}}
onto
{\mathcal{B}}^{\alpha}
, given by

E(b)=(I^{\mathcal{B}}\otimes{\mathsf{h}})\alpha(b)
.

For our purposes we want the action
\alpha
to be ergodic, that is,
the isotypic component of the trivial representation,
{\mathcal{B}}^{\alpha}

should be
exactly
{\mathbb{C}}1_{\mathcal{B}}
. In this case, the conditional expectation

E
is of the form
E(b)=\omega(b)1_{\mathcal{B}}
where
\omega
is the
unique
\alpha
-invariant state on
{\mathcal{B}}
, where
\phi\in S({\mathcal{B}})

is said to be
\alpha
-invariant if

(\phi\otimes\mu)\alpha(b)=\mu(1_{\mathcal{A}})\phi(b)
for all

\mu\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
and
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
. (Ergodic actions are sometimes
called “quantum homogeneous spaces”, e.g.
[
25
]
.)

Boca proved
[
9
]

that if
\alpha
is ergodic then all of the isotypic components
{\mathcal{B}}^{\gamma}

are finite dimensional. Let
{\mathcal{B}}_{c}
be the algebraic direct sum
of all the isotypic components. It is a dense
*
-subalgebra
of
{\mathcal{B}}
that is an analog of the coordinate subalgebra
of
{\mathcal{A}}
, and it is sometimes called the subalgebra
of “regular” elements of
{\mathcal{B}}
.

For a finite subset
S
of
\widehat{\Delta}

let us set
{\mathcal{B}}^{S}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in S}{\mathcal{B}}^{\gamma}
.
Suppose that
S
is closed under taking conjugate
corepresentations
and contains the trivial corepresentation.
Then
{\mathcal{B}}^{S}
is an operator system in
{\mathcal{B}}
.
For each

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
let
S^{n}
denote the collection of all irreducible
corepresentations that are contained in the
n
-fold
tensor products of elements of
S
. Let
S^{0}
consist of just
the trivial corepresentation, Then the
S^{n}
’s form an increasing
sequence of finite subsets of
\widehat{\Delta}
, each of which is closed under
taking conjugate corepresentations,
and contains the trivial corepresentation. Thus the
{\mathcal{B}}^{S^{n}}
’s
form an increasing sequence of operator systems in
{\mathcal{B}}
.
Suppose further that
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
is a compact matrix quantum group,
and that the direct sum of the irreducible corepresentations in

S
is a faithful corepresentation of
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
.
Then the
{\mathcal{B}}^{S^{n}}
’s form a filtration of
{\mathcal{B}}
. These are
the filtrations in which we are interested. In the context of

[
13
,
57
]
we can view the
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
’s as
Fourier truncations of
{\mathcal{B}}
.

We summarize the above discussion with:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
be a compact matrix quantum group,
and let
\alpha
be an ergodic action of
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)

on a unital C*-algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
. Let
S
be a finite subset
of
\widehat{\Delta}
such that the direct sum of the elements of
S

is a faithful unitary corepresentation of
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
, and that

S
is closed under taking conjugate corepresentations,
and contains the trivial corepresentation. Then, with notation
as above, the Fourier truncations
{\mathcal{B}}^{S^{n}}
’s
form a filtration of
{\mathcal{B}}
.

4.
Quantum metrics

The definition of quantum metrics was given in
[
44
]
in the setting of
order-unit spaces. Here we only need the definition for the case of
operator systems (notably those of the filtrations
discussed above), so we now recall the definition for that
case
[
30
,
31
,
36
,
20
]
.
We will need the fact that an operator system
{\mathcal{C}}
has
a well-defined state space, which we will denote by
S({\mathcal{C}})
.
It is compact for the weak-
*
topology.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an operator system. A quantum metric on
{\mathcal{C}}
is
a seminorm,
L
, on
{\mathcal{C}}
that plays the role of assigning the Lipschitz
constant to functions on a compact metric space.
As such, it can take the value

+\infty
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an operator system, and let
L
be a seminorm on
{\mathcal{C}}

that may take the value
+\infty
. We say that

L
is a
Lip-norm
if it satisfies the following properties:

(1)

For any
c\in{\mathcal{C}}
we have
L(c^{*})=L(c)
, and

L(c)=0
if and only if
c\in{\mathbb{C}}1_{\mathcal{C}}
.

(2)

\mathrm{Dom}(L):=\{c:L(c)<+\infty\}
is dense in
{\mathcal{C}}

(and so is a dense subspace).

(3)

Define a metric,
d^{L}
, on
S({\mathcal{C}})
by

d^{L}(\mu,\nu)=\sup\{|\mu(c)-\nu(c)|:L(c)\leq 1\}.

(A priori this can take the value
+\infty
. Also,
an argument given just before definition 2.1 of
[
46
]

shows that it suffices to take the supremum
only over self-adjoint
c
’s.) We require that the topology
on
S({\mathcal{C}})
determined by this metric agrees with the
weak-
*
topology.

(4)

L
is lower semi-continuous with respect to the
operator norm.

We will call a pair
({\mathcal{C}},L)
with
{\mathcal{C}}
an operator
system and
L
a Lip-norm on
{\mathcal{C}}
a “metrized
operator system”.

The third condition is the one that is often difficult to
verify for specific examples, but it is crucial for our purposes.
If the fourth condition is not satisfied, then the closure of
L
as defined in
[
44
]
will satisfy it,
with no change in the metric
d^{L}
. Since a compact space that is metrizable is separable (i.e. has a countable dense subset), any operator system on which a Lip-norm is defined must be separable.

In proposition 1.1 of
[
45
]
it is shown that if
E
is a countable subset of a separable order-unit space
C
then there are many Lip-norms on
C
that are finite on
E
. Let
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
be a compact quantum group such that
{\mathcal{A}}
is separable, and let

{\mathcal{A}}_{c}
be the corresponding dense coordinate subalgebra.
It follows that there are many Lip-norms on
{\mathcal{A}}
that are
finite on
{\mathcal{A}}_{c}
. In
[
36
]
Hanfeng Li calls such Lip-norms “regular”.

It will be important for us that the Lip-norms that we use are
suitably invariant. Here are the definitions given by Li in
[
36
]
:

Definition 4.2
.

A regular Lip-norm
L
on a compact
quantum group
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
is said to be
“left-invariant” (or “right-invariant”) if for
all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
with
a^{*}=a
and all
\mu\in S({\mathcal{A}})
we have

L(a*\mu)\leq L(a)\hskip 11.74988pt(\mathrm{or}\ \ L(\mu*a)\leq L(a))

where
a*\mu=(\mu\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}})\Delta(a)
and similarly
for
\mu*a
.
Then
L
is said to be “bi-invariant” if it is both left and right invariant.

Li shows in proposition 8.9 of
[
36
]
that if

L
is any regular Lip-norm on
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
, and if
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)

is coamenable, then one obtains a left-invariant regular Lip-norm

L^{\prime}
by setting

L^{\prime}(a)=\sup_{\mu\in S({\mathcal{A}})}L(a*\mu).

There is a similar result producing right-invariant regular
Lip-norms. Using these constructions, Li shows that one
can obtain bi-invariant regular Lip-norms.

We summarize the above results of Li that we need with:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
be a separable coamenable compact
quantum group. Then there exist (probably many)
bi-invariant regular Lip-norms on
{\mathcal{A}}
.

We emphasize that the Lip-norms on the algebras
discussed above
may well not satisfy the Leibniz inequality

(4.1)

L(aa^{\prime})\leq L(a)\|a^{\prime}\|+\|a\|L(a^{\prime}),

which they would satisfy if they came from a first-order differential
calculus or a spectral triple.
Thus they may not relate particularly well to the
products on the algebras. (Of course, for operator systems that
are not algebras this Leibniz condition has no meaning.)

Question 4.4
.

How does one characterize the separable compact
quantum groups that admit a bi-invariant regular
Lip-norm that satisfies the
Leibniz inequality (or, even better, the strong Leibniz condition
defined in
[
49
]
and studied in
[
1
]
,
or better yet, comes from a spectral triple)?

Examples of ones which do have Lip-norms coming from
spectral triples can be found in
[
45
,
40
,
10
,
29
]
. The compact
quantum groups implicit in the first three of
these papers are those corresponding
to certain classes of finitely generated groups. But many
related questions remain for other classes
of finitely generated groups.

In the next section
we will need the results we give below, which are mostly due to Li.
Much as in section 2 of
[
44
]
,
we define the radius of a metrized operator
system
({\mathcal{C}},L)
to be half of the
diameter of the compact metric space
(S({\mathcal{C}}),d^{L})
.
(For a thorough discussion of the nuances concerning Lip-norms
on operator systems as opposed to order-unit spaces see
section 2 of
[
29
]
.)
Equivalently, according to proposition 2.2 of
[
44
]
,

r_{\mathcal{C}}
is the smallest constant,
r
, such that for any
c\in{\mathcal{C}}

with
c^{*}=c

we have
\|c\|^{\prime}\leq rL(c)
, where
\|\cdot\|^{\prime}
is the quotient
norm on
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathbb{C}}1_{\mathcal{C}}
(so that there is some
t\in{\mathbb{R}}

such that
\|c-t1_{\mathcal{C}}\|\leq rL(c)
).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
({\mathcal{C}},L)
be a metrized operator system.
For any
c\in{\mathcal{C}}
with
c^{*}=c
and any
\mu\in S({\mathcal{C}})

we have
\|c-\mu(c)1_{\mathcal{C}}\|\leq 2r_{\mathcal{C}}L(c)
.

Proof.

Given
c\in{\mathcal{C}}
with
c^{*}=c
, choose

t\in{\mathbb{R}}

such that
\|c-t1_{\mathcal{C}}\|\leq r_{\mathcal{C}}L(c)
).
Then for any
\mu\in S({\mathcal{C}})
we have

\displaystyle\|c-\mu(c)1_{\mathcal{C}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|c-t1_{\mathcal{C}}\|+\|t1_{\mathcal{C}}-\mu(c)1_{\mathcal{C}}\|

\displaystyle\leq r_{\mathcal{C}}L(c)+\|\mu(t1_{\mathcal{C}}-c)1_{\mathcal{C}}\|\leq 2r_{\mathcal{C}}L(c).

.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

(lemma 8.4 of
[
36
]
)
Let
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
be a compact quantum group with Haar
state
{\mathsf{h}}
,
and let
L
be a regular Lip-norm on

({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
.
Then for any
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
with
a^{*}=a
we
have

\|a-{\mathsf{h}}(a)1_{\mathcal{A}}\|\leq 2r_{\mathcal{A}}L(a).

The following key proposition is a general version of much
of lemma 8.6 of
[
36
]
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a unital C*-algebra, and let
L
be a
Lip-norm on
{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
({\mathcal{H}},\pi)
be a
faithful
*
-representation of

{\mathcal{A}}
, and let
{\mathcal{K}}
be a dense subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}
.
Let
S_{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{A}})
be the set of finite convex combinations
of vector states determined by unit vectors in
{\mathcal{K}}
.
Then for every
\mu\in S({\mathcal{A}})
and every
\epsilon>0

there exists a
\nu\in S_{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{A}})
such that

|\mu(a)-\nu(a)|\leq\epsilon L(a)

for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

Proof.

Let
r_{\mathcal{A}}
be the radius of
(S({\mathcal{A}}),d^{L})
,
as defined before Lemma
4.5
, and let

B_{L}=\{a\in{\mathcal{A}}:\ a^{*}=a,\ L(a)\leq 1,\ \|a\|\leq r_{\mathcal{A}}\}.

Then if
a^{*}=a
and
L(a)\leq 1
, there is a
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

a-t1_{\mathcal{A}}
is in
B_{L}
. By the basic criterion for
verifying property 3 in the definition of a
Lip-norm (see theorem 4.5 of

[
46
]
)
B_{L}
is a totally bounded subset of
{\mathcal{A}}
.

Let
\mu
and
\epsilon>0
be given. Let
a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}
be
elements of
B_{L}
such that
the balls around them of radius
\epsilon/4
cover
B_{L}
.
It is easily seen that
S_{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{A}})
is dense in
S({\mathcal{A}})

for the weak-
*
topology. (Argue as for corollary T.5.10 of
[
58
]
.)
Accordingly, we can find a
\nu\in S_{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{A}})
such that

\|\mu(a_{j})-\nu(a_{j})\|\leq\epsilon/2
for
j=1,\cdots,m
.

Let
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
. We only need to consider

a
’s for which
L(a)<\infty
. By scaling we can assume that

L(a)=1
. For such an
a
with
a^{*}=a
,
there is a
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

a-t1_{\mathcal{A}}
is in
B_{L}
. Thus there is a
j_{0}
such that

\|a-t1_{\mathcal{A}}-a_{j_{0}}\|\leq\epsilon/4
. Then

\displaystyle|\mu(a)-\nu(a)|

\displaystyle=|(\mu-\nu)(a-t1_{\mathcal{A}})|

\displaystyle\leq|(\mu-\nu)(a-t1_{\mathcal{A}}-a_{j_{0}})|+|(\mu-\nu)(a_{j_{0}})|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon/2+\epsilon/2=\epsilon=\epsilon L(a).

For general
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
we can replace
\epsilon
with
\epsilon/2
in the
above argument, and apply the result to the real and imaginary
parts of
a
.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

(Lemma 8.6 of
[
36
]
)

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
be a compact quantum group and let
L
be a
regular Lip-norm on
{\mathcal{A}}
. Assume that
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
is in reduced form,
that is, its Haar state is faithful. Then for any
\mu\in S({\mathcal{A}})
and
any
\epsilon>0
there is a
\nu\in S({\mathcal{A}})
and a finite subset

such that

|\mu(a)-\nu(a)|\leq\epsilon L(a)

for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
, but
\nu({\mathcal{A}}^{\gamma})=0
whenever
\gamma\in\widehat{\Delta}
but

\gamma
is
not
in

.

Proof.

Let
({\mathcal{H}},\pi)
be the GNS representation of
{\mathcal{A}}
for the
Haar state.
Since
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
is in reduced form, this representation is faithful.
Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be the image in
{\mathcal{H}}
of the dense subalgebra

{\mathcal{A}}_{c}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
. Then
{\mathcal{K}}
is a dense subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}
.
Let
\mu\in S({\mathcal{A}})
and

\epsilon>0
be given.
Then according to Proposition
4.7
there is a finite convex
combination,
\nu
, of vector states using vectors from

{\mathcal{K}}
, such that

for all

. Let
a_{1},...,a_{m}
be the

{\mathcal{A}}_{c}
of length-one in

determining the vector

,
and for each

be defined as in Proposition

.
Set

. From Proposition

5.
Induced Lip-norms for actions

Suppose now that
\alpha
is an action of a compact quantum
group
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
on a unital C*-algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
. If
{\mathcal{A}}
is
separable then
\widehat{\Delta}
is countable. If also
\alpha
is ergodic,
then
{\mathcal{B}}
is separable. In theorem 1.4 and section 8
of
[
36
]
Li
shows that if
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
also is coamenable (and

\alpha
is ergodic),
then any regular Lip-norm
L^{\mathcal{A}}
on
{\mathcal{A}}
induces a
corresponding Lip-norm

L^{\mathcal{B}}
on
{\mathcal{B}}
, defined by

(5.1)

L^{\mathcal{B}}(b)=\sup_{\phi\in S({\mathcal{B}})}L^{\mathcal{A}}(b*\phi)

where
b*\phi=(\phi\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}})\alpha(b)
,
and that
L^{\mathcal{B}}
is regular on
{\mathcal{B}}
in the sense that
it is finite on
{\mathcal{B}}_{c}
. As Li remarks, this is a generalization
to the setting of compact quantum groups of the
corresponding fact for actions of ordinary compact groups
given in theorem 2.3 of
[
43
]
.

Let
L^{\mathcal{B}}
be any regular Lip-norm on
{\mathcal{B}}
. Then Li
calls
L^{\mathcal{B}}
“
\alpha
-invariant” if

(5.2)

L^{\mathcal{B}}(\mu*b)\leq L^{\mathcal{B}}(b)

for all
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
with
b^{*}=b
and all
\mu\in S({\mathcal{A}})
,
where
\mu*b=(I^{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\mu)\alpha(b)
. Li shows that
if
L^{\mathcal{B}}
is induced as above from a right-invariant
regular Lip-norm on
{\mathcal{A}}
then
L^{\mathcal{B}}
is
\alpha
-invariant.
(Note that
(\mu,b)\mapsto\mu*b
gives in action of
the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
, with its convolution product, on
{\mathcal{B}}
.)

We will now present an important
step in Li’s proof of these results, since this step is
crucial for our purposes.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
be a compact quantum group, and let

\alpha
be an action of
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
on a unital C*-algebra

{\mathcal{B}}
. Assume that
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
is coamenable, and let

{\varepsilon}
be the coidentity element (which is a

*
-homomorphism from
{\mathcal{A}}
into
{\mathbb{C}}
,
so an element of
S({\mathcal{A}})
). Then

{\varepsilon}*b=b
for all
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
, that is,

(I^{\mathcal{B}}\otimes{\varepsilon})\circ\alpha=I^{\mathcal{B}}.

Proof.

(a fragment from the proof of lemma 2.2 of
[
53
]
)
Set
\alpha_{o}=(I^{\mathcal{B}}\otimes{\varepsilon})\circ\alpha
. On multiplying
equation (
3.1
) on the left by

I^{\mathcal{B}}\otimes{\varepsilon}\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}}
and simplifying, we obtain

(\alpha_{o}\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}})\circ\alpha=\alpha.

Then for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
we have

(\alpha_{o}\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}})((\alpha(b))(1_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes a))=((\alpha_{o}\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}})\alpha(b))(1_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes a)=\alpha(b)(1_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes a).

Thus
\alpha_{o}\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}}
coincides with

I^{\mathcal{B}}\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}}
on
\alpha({\mathcal{B}})(1_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}})
.
But the latter spans a dense subspace of

{\mathcal{B}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}
according to the non-degeneracy hypothesis
in the definition of an action. It follows that

\alpha_{o}\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}}=I^{\mathcal{B}}\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}}
, and so

\alpha_{o}=I^{\mathcal{B}}
as desired.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

(Related to lemma 8.7 of
[
36
]
)

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
be a compact quantum group, and let

\alpha
be an ergodic action of
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
on a unital C*-algebra

{\mathcal{B}}
.
Let
L^{\mathcal{A}}
be a regular right-invariant Lip-norm on
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Assume that
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
is coamenable, and
let
L^{\mathcal{B}}
be defined by equation (
5.1
),
so that
L^{\mathcal{B}}
is an
\alpha
-invariant regular
Lip-norm on
{\mathcal{B}}
.
Let
{\varepsilon}
be the coidentity of
{\mathcal{A}}
,
viewed as an element of
S({\mathcal{A}})
.
Let
\epsilon>0
be given, and let
{\varepsilon}
be used as
\mu

in Proposition
4.8
to produce the state
\nu

and the finite set
F
with the properties described
in that proposition. Let
P_{\nu}
be the operator on

{\mathcal{B}}
defined by

P_{\nu}(b)=\nu*b=(I^{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\nu)\alpha(b)

for
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
.
Then the range of
P_{\nu}
is contained in
{\mathcal{B}}^{F}

(the direct sum of the isotypic components
{\mathcal{B}}^{\gamma}
for

\gamma\in F
), and

L^{\mathcal{B}}(P_{\nu}(b))\leq L^{\mathcal{B}}(b)\hskip 11.74988pt\hskip 11.74988ptand\hskip 11.74988pt\hskip 11.74988pt\|b-P_{\nu}(b)\|\leq\epsilon L^{\mathcal{B}}(b).

for all
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
with
b^{*}=b
.

Proof.

The fact that
L^{\mathcal{B}}(P_{\nu}(b))\leq L^{\mathcal{B}}(b)
follows
directly from the definition of
\alpha
-invariance (
5.2
)
and the definition of
P_{\nu}(b)
.
Because
\alpha({\mathcal{B}}^{\gamma})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}^{\gamma}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}^{\gamma}

for each
\gamma\in\widehat{\Delta}
, we see from the properties of
\nu

that the range of

P_{\nu}
is contained in
{\mathcal{B}}^{F}
.
Finally, let
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
be given with
b^{*}=b
.
Notice that for any
\phi\in S({\mathcal{B}})
, any
\mu\in S({\mathcal{A}})
,
and any
c\in{\mathcal{B}}
we have

\phi(\mu*c)=(\phi\otimes\mu)\alpha(c)=\mu(c*\phi)
.
Then

\displaystyle\|b-P_{\nu}(b)\|

\displaystyle=\|{\varepsilon}*(b-P_{\nu}(b))\|=\sup_{\phi\in S({\mathcal{B}})}|\phi({\varepsilon}*(b-\nu*b))|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi\in S({\mathcal{B}})}|{\varepsilon}(b*\phi)-\nu(b*\phi)|\leq\sup_{\phi\in S({\mathcal{B}})}\epsilon L^{\mathcal{A}}(b*\phi)

\displaystyle=\epsilon L^{\mathcal{B}}(b),

where we have used Lemma
5.1
for the first equality,
the self-adjointness of
b
for the second equality, Proposition

4.8
and the choice of
\nu
for the inequality, and
equation (
5.1
) for the final equality.
∎

We remark that the
P_{\nu}
above can be viewed as a
generalization of the
P_{n}
used in the proof of theorem
8.2 of
[
46
]
, and that the above Proposition

can be viewed as a generalization of lemma
8.3 in the proof of that theorem. Also,

and

can be viewed as analogues of the classical
Fejer kernels of harmonic analysis.

6.
The main theorem: convergence of truncations

In discussing the convergence of truncations we will use
the quantum
Gromov-Hausdorff distance that was first introduced in

[
46
]
. Its setting is order-unit spaces. But the
spaces of operators which we will use below are
operator systems, which are order-unit spaces
with important extra structure. Quite soon after
the appearance of
[
46
]
David Kerr introduced
a stronger version of quantum Gromov-Hausdorff
distance
[
30
]
that was especially tailored to the setting
of operator systems. It uses spaces of unital
completely positive
maps into matrix algebras (as generalizations
of the state space),
and Kerr has referred
to it as “complete Gromov-Hausdorff distance”.
At about the same time Hanfeng Li developed
a fairly different strategy for defining quantum
Gromov-Hausdorff-type distances, and provided
a version tailored for C*-algebras
[
34
]
,
and a version tailored for order-unit spaces
[
35
]
.
Subsequently Kerr and Li wrote a paper
[
31
]
in
which they showed that when Li’s strategy is applied
to operator systems, it leads to exactly the same
quantum Gromov-Hausdorff-type distance as
Kerr’s complete Gromov-Hausdorff distance.
They then call this “operator Gromov-Hausdorff
distance”. (For C*-algebras the best current quantum
Gromov-Hausdorff-type distance is Latrémolière’s
dual propinquity
[
32
]
, but since it explicitly
uses the Leibniz inequality, it can not be
applied to operator systems.)

Since most of the spaces of operators used below are
operator systems, it would be appropriate to
use here Kerr and Li’s operator Gromov-Hausdorff distance.
But I have chosen not to do this since it would
considerably complicate the notation, and so
somewhat obscure the ideas. But I fully
expect that with small adjustments the
arguments given below would work well for
operator Gromov-Hausdorff distance, though
I have not checked thoroughly that this is the
case.

For the readers’ convenience we begin by
recalling definition 4.2 of
[
46
]
, which is
the definition of quantum
Gromov-Hausdorff distance, adapted here for operator systems
much as in
[
29
]
.
Let
({\mathcal{C}},L^{\mathcal{C}})
and
({\mathcal{D}},L^{\mathcal{D}})
be metrized operator systems.
Let
{\mathcal{M}}(L^{\mathcal{C}},L^{\mathcal{D}})

be the set of all Lip-norms
L
on
{\mathcal{C}}\oplus{\mathcal{D}}
such
that
\mathrm{Dom}(L)=\mathrm{Dom}(L^{\mathcal{C}})\oplus\mathrm{Dom}(L^{\mathcal{D}})

and such that the quotient of
L
on
{\mathcal{C}}
coincides with
L^{\mathcal{C}}

and similarly for
{\mathcal{D}}
. For this condition the inclusion of

S({\mathcal{C}})
into
S({\mathcal{C}}\oplus{\mathcal{D}})
is an isometry
for the metric
d^{L^{\mathcal{C}}}
on
S({\mathcal{C}})
and the metric

d^{L}
on
S({\mathcal{C}}\oplus{\mathcal{D}})
, and similarly for
{\mathcal{D}}
,
so we can view
S({\mathcal{C}})
and
S({\mathcal{D}})
as subspaces
of
S({\mathcal{C}}\oplus{\mathcal{D}})
with the induced metric from
d^{L}
.
Then the quantum Gromov-Hausdorff distance,

\mathrm{dist}_{q}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}})
, between
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{D}}

is defined by

\mathrm{dist}_{q}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}})=\inf\{\mathrm{dist}_{H}^{d^{L}}(S({\mathcal{C}}),S({\mathcal{D}})):L\in{\mathcal{M}}(L^{\mathcal{C}},L^{\mathcal{D}})\},

where
\mathrm{dist}_{H}
denotes ordinary
Hausdorff distance.

Thus for any particular example, the challenge is to construct
elements
L
of
{\mathcal{M}}(L^{\mathcal{C}},L^{\mathcal{D}})
that bring

S({\mathcal{C}})
and
S({\mathcal{D}})
appropriately close together.
A convenient way to approach this (section 5 of
[
46
]
)
is to look for
L
’s of the form

L(c,d)=L^{\mathcal{C}}(c)\vee L^{\mathcal{D}}(d)\vee N(c,d)

for
c\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
d\in{\mathcal{D}}
(and
\vee
means “max”).
Here
N
should be a
norm-continuous seminorm on
{\mathcal{C}}\oplus{\mathcal{D}}
such that

N(1_{\mathcal{C}},1_{\mathcal{D}})=0
but
N(1_{\mathcal{C}},0_{\mathcal{D}})\neq 0
,
and such that for any
c\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
\epsilon>0
there is a
d\in{\mathcal{D}}

such that

L^{\mathcal{D}}(d)\vee N(c,d)\leq L^{\mathcal{C}}(c)+\epsilon,

and similarly for
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{D}}
interchanged.
In
[
46
]

the term
N
is called a “bridge”.

We now assume that
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
is a compact matrix quantum group,
and we let
({\mathcal{H}},u)
be a
fundamental unitary corepresentation
of
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
, as defined in Definition
2.3
. As discussed
just before that definition, arrange that
({\mathcal{H}},u)
is
self-conjugate and contains the
trivial corepresentation. Let
S
be the set of irreducible
unitary corepresentations that appear in the decomposition of

({\mathcal{H}},u)
. For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we define

S^{n}
as done just before Proposition
3.1
, so the

S^{n}
’s form a “filtration” of
\widehat{\Delta}
.

We also assume that
\alpha
is an ergodic action of

({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
on a unital C*-algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
, and we let the

{\mathcal{B}}^{S^{n}}
’s be defined just as before Proposition
3.1
,
so that they form a filtration of
{\mathcal{B}}
.

The following theorem,
which is the main theorem of this paper, can be viewed
as a generalization of theorem 8.2 of
[
46
]
(which is the
case in which our compact matrix quantum group
is an ordinary compact Lie group).

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
be a coamenable compact matrix quantum group, and
let
\alpha
be an ergodic action of

({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
on a unital C*-algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
.
Let notation be as above. Let
L^{\mathcal{A}}
be a regular Lip-norm on

{\mathcal{A}}
which is right invariant, and let

L^{\mathcal{B}}
be the seminorm on
{\mathcal{B}}

defined by Equation
5.1
(which is a regular
\alpha
-invariant
Lip-norm). For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
let

L^{n}
be the restriction of
L^{\mathcal{B}}
to the operator system

{\mathcal{B}}^{S^{n}}
, so that
({\mathcal{B}}^{S^{n}},L^{n})
is a metrized
operator system. Then

\mathrm{dist}_{q}({\mathcal{B}}^{S^{n}},{\mathcal{B}})\ \to\ 0\hskip 11.74988pt\mathrm{as}\hskip 11.74988ptn\ \to\ \infty.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
be given. According to Proposition
5.2

we can find a state
\nu
of
{\mathcal{A}}
and a finite subset

F
of
\widehat{\Delta}
such that the range of
P_{\nu}
is
contained in
{\mathcal{B}}^{F}
and

L^{\mathcal{B}}(P_{\nu}(b))\leq L^{\mathcal{B}}(b)\hskip 11.74988pt\hskip 11.74988ptand\hskip 11.74988pt\hskip 11.74988pt\|b-P_{\nu}(b)\|\leq\epsilon L^{\mathcal{B}}(b)

for all
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
with
b^{*}=b
.
Since
F
is finite, we can find an
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

F\subseteq S^{N}
. Then for all
n\geq N
we have

F\subseteq S^{n}
so that the range of
P_{\nu}
is
contained in
{\mathcal{B}}^{S^{n}}
.
We can then immediately
apply proposition 8.5 of
[
46
]
to conclude that

\mathrm{dist}_{q}({\mathcal{B}}^{S^{n}},{\mathcal{B}})<\epsilon
for all
n\geq N
.
The bridge for this situation is simply
\epsilon^{-1}\|b-a\|
.
∎

For the convenience of the reader, we now state
proposition 8.5 of
[
46
]
, for the case of
metrized operator systems. We let
{\mathcal{A}}^{sa}

denote the set of self-adjoint elements of
{\mathcal{A}}
,
and similarly for
{\mathcal{B}}
.

Proposition 6.2
.

(proposition 8.5 of
[
46
]
)
Let
({\mathcal{A}},L^{\mathcal{A}})
be a metrized operator system,
and let
{\mathcal{B}}
be an operator
subsystem of
{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
L^{\mathcal{B}}
denote the restriction of

L^{\mathcal{A}}
to
{\mathcal{B}}
, so that
({\mathcal{B}},L^{\mathcal{B}})
is a metrized
operator system. Let
P
be a function (not
necessarily even linear or continuous) from
{\mathcal{A}}^{sa}
to

{\mathcal{B}}^{sa}
for which there is an
\epsilon>0

such that

L^{\mathcal{B}}(P(a))\leq L^{\mathcal{A}}(a)\hskip 11.74988pt\hskip 11.74988ptand\hskip 11.74988pt\hskip 11.74988pt\|a-P(a)\|\leq\epsilon L^{\mathcal{A}}(a)

for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}^{sa}
.
Then
\mathrm{dist}_{q}({\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{A}})<\epsilon
.

7.
Examples

We will now give a number of examples to which our results above apply.

Example 7.1
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group, and let
{\mathcal{A}}=C(G)
.
Choose an
Ad
-invariant inner product on the
Lie algebra of
G
, and let
D
be the corresponding Dirac operator
on the Hilbert
space
{\mathcal{S}}
of spinor fields, as described in many places, for
example in
[
51
,
48
]
.
Then

({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{S}},D)
is a spectral triple, and so one
can define a seminorm,
L^{D}
, (with value
+\infty
allowed) on
{\mathcal{A}}
by

L^{D}(a)=\|[D,a]\|

for any
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Then
L^{D}
is a C*-metric, and in particular

L^{D}
is a Lip-norm that satisfies the
Leibniz inequality
4.1
. See proposition 6.5 of
[
51
]
,
as well as its anticedent theorem 4.2 of
[
43
]
.
Our results in the preceding sections apply to this class of examples,
including to the many ergodic actions of compact Lie groups
on unital C*-algebras
[
51
]
.

The case in which
G
is the
circle group is the example treated in section 3.2 of
[
57
]
concerning
Fejér-Riesz operator systems.

If we only have a continuous length function on
G
, it too can be used
to define a Lip-norm satisfying the Leibniz inequality on any unital
C*-algebra on which
G
has an ergodic action. When our results
of previous sections are applied, one obtains theorem 8.2
of
[
46
]
.

Example 7.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group, as in Example
2.1
.
Both its full and its reduced C*-algebras are compact quantum
groups. We can view them as (co)acting on themselves on the
left using
\Delta
.
They both acquire a filtration consisting of operator systems from any
given finite set
S
of generators of
\Gamma
closed under
taking inverses and containing the identity element of
G
.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}=C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
, the reduced C*-algebra,
with its faithful representation on
{\mathcal{H}}=\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
, and
let
\{{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\}
be the
corresponding filtration by operator systems using
S
.
For each
n
let
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}
be the image
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
in
{\mathcal{H}}
, so that the
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}
’s form an increasing
family of finite dimensional subspaces whose union is dense, with
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
the
span of
1_{\mathcal{A}}
. Set
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}={\mathcal{H}}_{0}
, and
for each integer
n\geq 1
set
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}={\mathcal{H}}_{n}\ominus{\mathcal{H}}_{n-1}
.
Let
D
be the unbounded operator on

{\mathcal{H}}
whose domain is the algebraic sum
\oplus{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
,
and which multiplies all elements of
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
by
n
, for
all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then

({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)
is a spectral triple. This is the main class of
examples discussed in Connes’ first paper
[
12
]
on the
metric aspects of non-commutative geometry. One can
again define a seminorm,
L
, on
{\mathcal{A}}
by

L(a)=\|[D,a]\|

for any
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
. It is easily seen that
L
satisfies properties 1,
2 and 4 of the definition
4.1
of a Lip-norm, as well as the
Leibniz inequality
4.1
. But property
3 of definition
4.1
is only known to hold
in the case of finitely generated
groups of polynomial growth
[
45
,
10
]
(so virtually
nilpotent), and the case of hyperbolic groups
and some related free product groups
[
40
]
. No counterexamples
are known for other groups. It is a very interesting open question
to determine for which other groups property 3 is satisfied. (The
corresponding spectral triples for group algebras twisted
by a 2-cocycle are studied in
[
38
]
, but that is
somewhat outside the the scope of the present paper.)

Of course one can always use Li’s results discussed above
to choose a regular Lip-norm
L
on
{\mathcal{A}}
that is invariant for the
left (co)action of
{\mathcal{A}}
on itself (but which may well not satisfy the
Leibniz inequality). But it is only if
{\mathcal{A}}
is coamenable, that
is, if
\Gamma
is amenable, that we can apply the results in the earlier sections
to conclude that the operator systems
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
, equipped with the
restrictions of
L
to them, converge to
({\mathcal{A}},L)
for quantum
Gromov-Hausdorff distance.

Example 7.3
.

Let
G
be a group of rapid decay. (See
[
5
]
for the definition.)
In

In

Example 7.5
.

Let

Example 7.6
.

In
[

Example 7.7
.

Let

be the compact quantum group

.

]

Even better, in

Example 7.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be the standard Podleś sphere,

There have been many proposals for Dirac operators on

Let us denote one of these proposals, that of

.
It is shown in
[

,

where here the action

For the Dirac operators

to

is

. To put this in the
context of Li’s framework, we use the following simple result.

Proposition 7.9
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta)
be a coamenable compact quantum group,
and let
{\mathcal{B}}

be an embedded homogeneous space in
{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
L^{\mathcal{A}}

be a regular Lip-norm on
{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
L^{\mathcal{B}}
be Li’s
corresponding induced Lip-norm on
{\mathcal{B}}
as defined in

Let
\mu\in S({\mathcal{A}})

{\mathcal{B}}

.
Since for any

we see that

b*\phi=(\phi\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}})\Delta(b)=(\mu\otimes I^{\mathcal{A}})\Delta(b)=b*\mu.

But any

can be extended (perhaps in many ways)
to be an element

of

. It follows easily that

.

Consequently, the filtration of

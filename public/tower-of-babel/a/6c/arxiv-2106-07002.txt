On CR maps from the sphere into the tube over the future light cone Thanks: The authors were supported by the Austrian Science Fund (FWF): Projekt I3472 (Internationale Projekte-RSF/Russland 2017).
Michael Reiter, Duong Ngoc Son

Abstract
. We determine all local smooth or formal CR maps from theunit sphere 𝕊 3 ⊂ ℂ 2 \mathbb{S}^{3}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2} into the tube 𝒯 := 𝒞 × i ​ ℝ 3 ⊂ ℂ 3 \mathcal{T}:=\mathcal{C}\times i\mathbb{R}^{3}\subset\mathbb{C}^{3} over the future light cone 𝒞 := { x ∈ ℝ 3 : x 1 2 + x 2 2 = x 3 2 , x 3 > 0 } \mathcal{C}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\colon x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}=x_{3}^{2},\ x_{3}>0\right\} . This result leads to a complete classification of proper holomorphic maps from the unit ball in ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} into Cartan’s classical domain of type IV in ℂ 3 \mathbb{C}^{3} that extend smoothly to some boundary point. Up to composing with CR automorphisms of the source and target, the classification consists of four algebraic maps. Two maps among them were known earlier in the literature, which were shown to be “rigid” in the higher dimensional case in a recent paper by Xiao and Yuan. Two newly discovered quadratic polynomial maps provide counterexamples to a conjecture appeared in the same paper for the case of dimension two.

1.
Introduction

The study of proper holomorphic maps between balls goes back to Poincaré
[
35
]
and Alexander
[
2
]
who showed that the proper holomorphic self-maps of a ball in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
,
N\geqslant 2
, are precisely the automorphisms which have explicit parametrizations. When the target is a ball of higher dimension, the discovery of inner functions implies that there are proper holomorphic maps between balls that do not extend to any boundary point
[
1
,
15
,
29
,
30
]
. On the other hand, by a well-known result of Forstnerič
[
13
, Theorem 1.4]
, proper holomorphic maps between balls, which extend sufficiently smooth to a boundary point, extend to rational maps with no poles on the boundary and induce global CR maps between spheres.

There is an interesting phenomenon for CR maps of spheres (i.e.
sphere maps
) depending on the size of the
codimension
(i.e. the difference of the CR dimensions of the source and target sphere.) When the codimension is “low,” the maps are “rigid,” which means that there is only the linear map up to composition with automorphisms, see
[
39
,
11
,
18
]
. When the codimension is not too large, the sphere maps may be completely classified
[
10
,
11
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
. In case there is no restriction on the codimension, the collection of sphere maps is known to be huge
[
7
,
16
]
. A particular interesting case is when the source is
\mathbb{S}^{3}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
. In this case, the smooth CR maps into
\mathbb{S}^{5}\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}
have been classified by Faran
[
10
]
as four “spherical equivalence” classes of maps. See also
[
6
,
24
,
36
]
for different proofs. However, a classification of the maps from
\mathbb{S}^{3}
into
\mathbb{S}^{7}
is only available for monomial maps
[
7
]
.

Besides the study of proper holomorphic maps of balls, there is a large literature devoted to the study of proper holomorphic maps between various models such as the complex ellipsoids, Reinhardt domains, and the classical domains. In particular, there are many works devoted to the study of proper holomorphic maps as well as isometric holomorphic embeddings from one bounded symmetric domain into another by Mok, Ng, Xiao, Yuan and others, see, e.g.
[
34
,
41
]
. In
[
41
]
, Xiao–Yuan give complete classifications of proper maps from
\mathbb{B}^{n}
into
{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{m}}\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
(the Cartan’s classical domain of type IV of
m
dimension) in the “low” codimensional case (i.e.,
4\leqslant n\leqslant m-1\leqslant 2n-4
) and for the holomorphic isometries from
\mathbb{B}^{n}
into
{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{n+1}}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
for all
n\geqslant 2
. When the codimension is higher, there are many more explicit examples of proper holomorphic maps given in
[
41
]
. However, in the case
n=2
and
m=3
, only two equivalence classes of proper holomorphic maps were known (and they are all isometries), yet the classification problem was left open in these particular dimensions. We refer the reader to, e.g.,
[
32
,
33
,
34
,
41
]
and their references for more detailed information.

In this paper, we study local CR maps from
\mathbb{S}^{3}
into the well-known
tube over the future light cone

\mathcal{T}
of real dimension
5
which has been of interest in many papers, e.g.,
[
9
,
25
]
. This is an everywhere Levi-degenerate 2-nondegenerate CR manifold (cf.
[
9
, Example 4.2.1]
) which is homogeneous and has a “large” stability group; see, e.g.,
[
25
,
12
]
. Moreover,
\mathcal{T}
is locally CR equivalent to the smooth boundary part
\mathcal{R}
of
{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}}
and our study provides a complete classification of proper holomorphic maps from
\mathbb{B}^{2}
into
{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}}
which extend smoothly to a boundary point.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
M
and
M^{\prime}
be CR manifolds and let
H\colon(M,p)\to M^{\prime}
,
\tilde{H}\colon(M,\tilde{p})\to M^{\prime}
be germs of smooth CR maps at
p
and
\tilde{p}
, respectively. We say that
H
and
\tilde{H}
are
equivalent
if there exist germs of local CR diffeomorphisms
\gamma\colon(M,p)\to(M,\tilde{p})
and
\psi\colon(M^{\prime},\tilde{H}(\tilde{p}))\to(M^{\prime},H(p))
, such that

H=\psi\circ\tilde{H}\circ\gamma^{-1}.

We say that CR maps are
equivalent
if they represent two equivalent germs at some interior points in their domains of definition.

In our main theorem below, we classify the germs of CR maps from
\mathbb{S}^{3}
into
\mathcal{T}
as five equivalence classes of map germs. Their representing maps have simple formulas when being expressed in the Heisenberg model
\mathbb{H}^{3}:=\left\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\colon\operatorname{Im}w=|z|^{2}\right\}
for
\mathbb{S}^{3}
and the rational model
\mathcal{X}
for the tube given explicitly as follows.

\mathcal{X}:=\left\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\colon v=\frac{|z|^{2}+\operatorname{Re}(z^{2}\bar{\zeta})}{1-|\zeta|^{2}},\ |\zeta|^{2}<1\right\},\quad w=u+iv.

An explicit local equivalence of
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{T}
is given by Fels and Kaup in
[
12
, Proposition 4.16]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
U
be an open subset of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
and
H\colon U\to\mathcal{X}
a smooth CR map. Then the following hold:

(a)

If
H
is CR transversal at some point
p\in U
, then
H
is CR transversal on
U
, the germs
(H,q)
,
q\in U
, are mutually equivalent and are equivalent to exactly one of the following four pairwise inequivalent germs at the origin:

(i)

\ell(z,w)=(z,0,w)
,

(ii)

r_{1}(z,w)=\left(\dfrac{z(1+iw)}{1-w^{2}},\dfrac{2z^{2}}{1-w^{2}},\dfrac{w}{1-w^{2}}\right)
,

(iii)

r_{-1}(z,w)=\left(\dfrac{z(1-iw)}{1-w^{2}},\dfrac{-2z^{2}}{1-w^{2}},\dfrac{w}{1-w^{2}}\right)
,

(iv)

\iota(z,w)=\left(2z,\ 2w,\ 2w\right)\bigl/\left(1+\sqrt{1-4w^{2}-4iz^{2}}\right)
.

Consequently,
H
extends as an algebraic map from
\mathbb{C}^{2}
to
\mathbb{C}^{3}
sending
\mathbb{H}^{3}
into
\mathcal{X}
.

(b)

If
H
is nowhere CR transversal, then for each
q
, the germ
(H,q)
is equivalent to the germ at the origin of a map
t_{q}\colon(z,w)\mapsto(0,\phi_{q}(z,w),0)
for a smooth CR function
\phi_{q}
defined in a neighbourhood of the origin.

Remark 1
.

The smoothness assumption in
1.2
can be weakened to
C^{2}
-smoothness by a result of Kossovskiy–Lamel–Xiao
[
27
]
. Regularity of CR maps into the tube over the future light cone has been studied in
[
31
,
40
,
14
]
. Similar statements hold for germs of formal holomorphic maps sending
\mathbb{H}^{3}
into
\mathcal{X}
; we omit the details.

As briefly mentioned above, this theorem implies a complete classification of proper holomorphic maps from
\mathbb{B}^{2}
to the classical domain
{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}}
that extend smoothly to some smooth boundary point. Recall
[
4
, §V]
that the Cartan’s classical domain of type IV in dimension
m
, denoted by
{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{m}}
, is the domain of
m
-dimensional vectors

Z=(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}

satisfying the inequalities

1-2Z\overline{Z}^{t}+\left|ZZ^{t}\right|^{2}>0,\quad\left|ZZ^{t}\right|<1,

where
Z^{t}
is the
m\times 1
transpose of
Z
. This domain is also called the
Lie ball
and the
complex sphere
in the literature. This domain is homogeneous: the global autormophism group
\Aut({D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}})
acts transitively on it. We say that two proper holomorphic maps
H,\widetilde{H}\colon\mathbb{B}^{2}\to{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}}
are
equivalent
if
there exist
\psi\in\Aut({D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}})
and
\gamma\in\Aut(\mathbb{B}^{2})
such that
\widetilde{H}:=\psi\circ H\circ\gamma^{-1}
. Then we obtain a classification of proper holomorphic maps from
\mathbb{B}^{2}
to
{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}}
as follows.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
H\colon\mathbb{B}^{2}\to{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}}
be a proper holomorphic map which extends smoothly to some boundary point
p\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{2}
. Then
H
is equivalent to exactly one of the following four pairwise inequivalent maps:

(i)

R_{0}(z,w)=\left(\dfrac{z}{\sqrt{2}},\dfrac{2w^{2}+2w-z^{2}}{4(w+1)},\dfrac{i\left(2w^{2}+2w+z^{2}\right)}{4(w+1)}\right)
,

(ii)

P_{1}(z,w)=\left(zw,\dfrac{z^{2}-w^{2}}{2},\dfrac{i(z^{2}+w^{2})}{2}\right)
,

(iii)

P_{-1}(z,w)=\left(z,\dfrac{w^{2}}{2},\dfrac{iw^{2}}{2}\right)
,

(iv)

I(z,w)=\left(z,w,1-\sqrt{1-z^{2}-w^{2}}\,\right)\bigl/\sqrt{2}
.

Remark 2
.

The maps
R_{0}
and
I
were known earlier in the classification of isometric embeddings, studied in, e.g.,
[
5
,
38
]
, by methods that are very different from ours. They also appeared in
[
41
]
which studies the rigidity of transversal holomorphic maps sending a piece of the sphere into the smooth boundary part of a type IV domain in higher dimension. They are essentially the only isometric embeddings (up to a normalizing constant) of the respective Bergman metrics on
\mathbb{B}^{2}
and
{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}}
. The quadratic polynomial maps
P_{\pm 1}
are not isometric and provide counterexamples to Conjecture 2.9 in
[
41
]
of Xiao and Yuan in the case the source has dimension
n=2
(the case
n=3
and the case of
C^{1}
-extendability are still open). In fact, there is an 1-parameter smooth family of rational proper maps
R_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, such that
R_{\alpha}\sim P_{1}
for
\alpha>0
and
R_{\alpha}\sim P_{-1}
for
\alpha<0
, yet
R_{0}
is not equivalent to
R_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\neq 0
. In essence, the map
R_{0}
is not “locally rigid” (see
[
8
]
for a recent discussion on the local rigidity of CR maps). This exhibits an important difference of our setting from the case
n\geqslant 4
and the case of isometries treated in
[
41
,
38
,
5
]
in which the rigidity holds.

It is immediate to see that these four maps are pairwise inequivalent. Indeed,
R_{0}
and
I
(which is irrational) have singularities on the sphere, the image of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
via
P_{1}
lies entirely in the smooth part
{\mathcal{R}}\subset\partial{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}}
, while
P_{-1}
sends the circle
\{(e^{it},0)\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{3}
into the singular part of
\partial{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}}
and sends
\mathbb{S}^{3}\setminus\{(e^{it},0)\}
transversally into
{\mathcal{R}}
. Hence, the inequivalences are evident.

Our proof of Corollary
1.3
is independent from that of the classification of proper holomorphic maps and isometric embeddings in
[
5
,
38
,
41
]
.

Remark 3
.

Corollary
1.3
is analogous to Faran’s well-known classification of maps from
\mathbb{B}^{2}
to
\mathbb{B}^{3}
as a list of four equivalence classes of maps
[
10
]
(see also
[
36
,
24
]
). We point out, however, that there is a difference when considering
local
equivalences of map germs. For each map
H\in\{R_{0},P_{1},P_{-1},I\}
and each
p,p^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
such that
H(p),H(p^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{R}}
, the germs
(H,p)
and
(H,p^{\prime})
are equivalent. In contrast, the sphere map
(z,w)\mapsto(z,zw,w^{2})
from
\mathbb{B}^{2}
to
\mathbb{B}^{3}
represents inequivalent germs when the base point
p
varies on and off the circle of “umbilical points”
\{(0,e^{i\theta})\in\mathbb{S}^{3}\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
. The sphere map
(z,w)\mapsto(z^{3},\sqrt{3}zw,w^{3})
also exhibits a similar phenomenon, see
[
36
]
and
[
28
, Table 1]
.

Remark 4
.

There is a convenient way to construct proper holomorphic maps from a ball into a type IV domain of certain dimension as first noticed in
[
41
]
. In essence, it relies on an equation of the form

(1.1)

2\sum_{j=1}^{m}|h_{j}|^{2}-\left|\sum_{j=1}^{m}h_{j}^{2}\right|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}|f_{j}|^{2},

where
H=(h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{m})
and
F=(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{m^{\prime}})
are two holomorphic maps defined on some relevant open set of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. If
H
maps
\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}
into
\partial{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{m}}
, then
F
maps
\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}
into
\mathbb{S}^{2m^{\prime}+1}
and vice versa, if
F
maps
\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}
into
\mathbb{S}^{2m^{\prime}+1}
, then
H
maps
\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}
into the real variety
\{\varrho_{{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{m}}}=0\}
which contains
\partial{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{m}}
. Equation (
1.1
) implies that
H|_{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
and
F|_{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
have the same Ahlfors tensor (defined in Section 2). If we take
H=P_{1}
, then (
1.1
) holds for
F(z,w)=(z^{2},\sqrt{2}zw,w^{2})
, which maps
\mathbb{S}^{3}
into
\mathbb{S}^{5}
. Both maps have Ahlfors tensor equal to
1/2
(when evaluated on an appropriate frame). If
H=P_{-1}
, then there is no map
F\colon\mathbb{S}^{3}\to\mathbb{S}^{5}
such that (
1.1
) holds, since
P_{-1}
has a negative Ahlfors tensor, cf.
[
41
, Example 2.8]
, which can never be satisfied by any sphere map, see
[
28
]
.

Each of the isometric embeddings
R_{0}
and
I
restricts to a CR map on an open dense subset of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
having the property that a certain coefficient in its partial normal form (given below in (
3.1
)) vanishes. In this case we say that the map has vanishing
geometric rank
at the centered point. The notion of geometric rank in our present setting is similar to that of sphere maps, first used by Huang
[
18
]
, see also
[
19
]
. It also appears recently in
[
23
]
as a boundary characterization for isometric embeddings into indefinite hyperbolic space. We define this invariant notion for CR transversal maps into the tube over the future light cone precisely in Definition
3.2
. By inspecting the special case of vanishing geometric rank in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we can observe a phenomenon that is similar to the main result in
[
23
]
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
H\colon\mathbb{B}^{2}\to{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}}
be a proper holomorphic map. If
H
extends smoothly to a neighbourhood of a point
p\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
and has vanishing geometric rank at
p
, then
H
is a holomorphic isometry. Conversely, each isometric embedding from
\mathbb{B}^{2}
into
{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}}
extends to a real-analytic CR map from an open dense proper subset of the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{3}
to the boundary of
\partial{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}}
with vanishing geometric rank at every point and is everywhere CR transversal in its domain.

We conclude this introduction by describing our approach in the proof of Theorem
1.2
and the main difficulties we need to overcome. Similarly to, e.g.
[
36
,
41
]
, we analyze the “mapping equation” for the components of a map
H
which expresses the fact that
H
sends
\mathbb{H}^{3}
into
\mathcal{X}
. But there are differences to the positive but “low” codimension case, as treated in
[
41
]
, when “rigidity” holds, i.e., when there are only “few” equivalence classes of maps: An essential ingredient for the rigidity is Huang’s lemma
[
18
]
and the fact that the maps are all of vanishing “geometric rank”. In our present setting (as well as in the case of sphere maps from
\mathbb{S}^{3}
to
\mathbb{S}^{5}
, cf.
[
10
,
36
]
) Huang’s lemma is not available and rigidity
fails
. Thus, the solution set in the present setting is expected to be rather “large” and we need to solve the mapping equation for an
a priori
unknown number of (discrete or continuous families of) solutions.

To solve the mapping equation, we first normalize the map (the “unknown”) to a specific form, given in (
3.1
), using explicit formulas for the local CR automorphisms of the source and target (
2.2
). In this partial normal form, the mapping equation can be viewed as either a system of infinitely many (linear and nonlinear) equations for the coefficients in the Taylor series expansions of the components the map (the jets of the map at the origin) or a system of infinitely many equations arising from differentiation and evaluation along the first Segre variety
\Sigma:=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\colon w=0\}
of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
at the origin. By the
2
-nondegeneracy of
\mathcal{X}
, the jets of the map of all orders are determined once the
4
-jet is determined. Such a behavior is also observed in the sphere case, see
[
36
]
.

A bulk of the present paper is to identify the
4
-jets at the origin that arise from maps sending
\mathbb{H}^{3}
into
\mathcal{X}
. This process involves analyzing the mapping equation and various derived ones. It turns out that the
4
-jets of the maps can only be fully identified by analyzing all equations arising from the Taylor series expansions upto weighted order
16
. There are examples of maps which satisfy all but three of weight
14
(
example

5.3
). This shows that considering such high orders is in some sense necessary. Once we determine the
4
-jets of the maps at the origin, and at the same time, the
2
-jets along the first Segre variety, we can construct a system of three holomorphic functional equations depending on two real parameters whose unique and explicit solutions, as presented in Theorem
1.2
, are genuine maps sending
\mathbb{H}^{3}
into
\mathcal{X}
.

The computational complexity also poses some difficulties. To overcome this issue, we use the computer algebra system Mathematica when doing some formal differentiations, collecting terms in polynomial expressions, and computing determinants of matrices of some (relatively small) size. But we want to point out, that the operations and expressions involved in the proofs are in principle simple and elementary enough to be checked by hand.

The paper is organized as follows: In
section

2
we provide the local representations of the tube over the future light cone, its automorphisms and an Ahlfors-type tensor, which allows us to distinguish equivalence classes of maps. In
section

3
we give a partial normal form for the maps and introduce the geometric rank in this setting. In
section

4
we give the proof of our main result
1.2
and in
section

5
we discuss additional examples of maps and provide explicit biholomorphisms to show equivalence to the maps listed in
1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Models for the tube over the future light cone

Following
[
12
]
we would like to describe representations of the tube over the future light cone. A model for the three-dimensional space time is the space
V
of real symmetric
2\times 2
-matrices where the time coordinate is the normalized trace of the matrices. In this model, the
future cone
is

\Omega:=\left\{v=\begin{pmatrix}t+x_{1}&x_{2}\\
x_{2}&t-x_{1}\end{pmatrix}\colon v\text{ positive definite }\right\}.

The smooth boundary part of
\Omega
is the
future light cone

\mathcal{C}
is given explicitly by

t^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2},\quad t>0.

To construct the tube over
\mathcal{C}
, one identifies
V\oplus iV
with the space of complex symmetric
2\times 2
-matrices. The
tube over the future light cone
is given by

\mathcal{T}:=\mathcal{C}\oplus iV=\left\{z\in V\oplus iV\colon\det(z+\bar{z})=0,\ \operatorname{Re}\trace(z+\bar{z})>0\right\}.

The tube
\mathcal{T}
is the smooth boundary part of a tube domain with everywhere degenerate Levi-form. In fact, it is everywhere
2
-nondegenerate as a CR manifold; see
[
9
, Example 4.2.1]
. It has been studied in many papers
[
9
,
25
,
12
]
. Our interest in this tube is that it is
holomorphically homogeneous
, i.e., its CR automorphism group acts transitively on it, and has quite “large” stability group at each point. Moreover, from Fels–Kaup
[
12
]
,
\mathcal{T}
has a local rational model similar as in the case of the sphere. Namely, at every point it is locally CR equivalent to the real hypersurface
\mathcal{X}
near the origin in
\mathbb{C}_{z,\zeta,w}
given by

v=\frac{|z|^{2}+\operatorname{Re}(z^{2}\bar{\zeta})}{1-|\zeta|^{2}},\quad w=u+iv.

In fact, the rational map

\Phi:(z,\zeta,w)\mapsto\frac{1}{1+\zeta}\begin{pmatrix}z^{2}-i\zeta w-iw&\sqrt{2}w\\
\sqrt{2}w&1-\zeta\\
\end{pmatrix}

sends
\mathcal{X}
into
\mathcal{T}

[
12
, Proposition 4.16]
and is locally biholomorphic on a dense set of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.
In this model, the automorphism group and the stability group have quite simple representations. In this paper, we shall work extensively with this model.

For the tube model, the maps are given as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}
be a connected open set and
H\colon U\to\mathcal{T}
be a smooth CR map. Then

(a)

If
H
is CR transversal at some point
p\in U
, then for all
q\in U
, the germs
(H,q)
are mutually equivalent and is equivalent to one of the following germs at the origin:

(i)

\mathcal{R}_{0}(z,w)=\begin{pmatrix}z^{2}-iw&\sqrt{2}z\\
\sqrt{2}z&1\\
\end{pmatrix}
,

(ii)

\mathcal{R}_{1}(z,w)=\dfrac{1}{1-w^{2}+2z^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}z^{2}-iw&\sqrt{2}z(1+iw)\\
\sqrt{2}z(1+iw)&1-w^{2}-2z^{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}
,

(iii)

\mathcal{R}_{-1}(z,w)=\dfrac{1}{1-w^{2}-2z^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}z^{2}-iw&\sqrt{2}z(1-iw)\\
\sqrt{2}z(1-iw)&1-w^{2}+2z^{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}
,

(iv)

\mathcal{I}(z,w)=\dfrac{1}{1+2w+\varepsilon}\begin{pmatrix}-i(1+2w-\varepsilon)&2\sqrt{2}z\\
2\sqrt{2}z&1-2w+\varepsilon\\
\end{pmatrix}
,
\varepsilon:=\sqrt{1-4w^{2}-4iz^{2}}
.

(b)

If
H
is nowhere CR transversal, then for each
p\in U
,
(H,p)
is equivalent to a germ at the origin of the form
\phi(z,w)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\varphi\\
\end{pmatrix}

for some smooth CR function
\varphi
.

It is well-known that
\mathcal{T}
is locally CR equivalent to the smooth boundary part of Cartan’s classical domain of type IV. Recall from
[
4
, Section V]
that the symmetric domain of type IV of
m
dimension, denoted by
{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{m}}
, is the domain of
m
-dimensional vectors

Z=(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}

satisfying the conditions

or the

in the literature. The complex 3-dimensional case (i.e.

{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}}
is also equivalent to the classical domain of type II, denoted by

, consisting of

The biholomorphisms of

2.2.
Automorphisms of the tube over the future light cone

The local CR automorphisms of the tube over the future light cone are well-studied. They are the restrictions of birational but not necessarily biholomorphic transformations of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
; a local CR equivalence of
\mathcal{T}
does not necessarily extend to a global CR equivalence
[
25
]
. By homogeneity, the local CR equivalences of
\mathcal{T}
are determined once the automorphism group
\Aut(\mathcal{T},p)
of the germs at an arbitrary point
p
is determined. In fact,
\Aut(\mathcal{T},p)
is a solvable real Lie group of dimension
5
(isomorphic to the stability of a point on a sphere in
\mathbb{C}^{2}

[
25
]
). For our purpose, we shall describe this group explicitly in the local rational model of Fels–Kaup
[
12
]
. The result of Fels–Kaup is presented in
[
26
]
by writing the Lie algebra

as

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{-2}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}.

The stability group
G_{0}^{c}
generated by the component

\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{c}:=\mathrm{span}\left\{z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+2w\frac{\partial}{\partial w},\ iz\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+2i\zeta\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}\right\}

is parametrized by
\lambda>0
and
\varphi\in\mathbb{R}
as follows

(z,\zeta,w)\mapsto\left(\lambda e^{i\varphi}z,e^{2i\varphi}\zeta,\lambda^{2}w\right).

The component
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is

\mathfrak{g}_{2}=\mathrm{span}\left\{zw\frac{\partial}{\partial z}-iz^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}+w^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\right\}.

Integrating yields 1-parameter subgroup, we get

(z,\zeta,w)\mapsto\bigl(z,\zeta-sw\zeta-isz^{2},w\bigr)/(1-sw),\quad s\in\mathbb{R}.

The component
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is given by

\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mathrm{span}\biggl\{(z^{2}+iw(\zeta+1))\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+2z(\zeta+1)\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}+2zw\frac{\partial}{\partial w},\\
(iz^{2}-w(\zeta-1))\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+2iz(\zeta-1)\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}+2izw\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\biggr\}.

Integrating the first vector field, we obtain

(z,\zeta,w)\mapsto\bigl(z(1-tz)+it(1+\zeta)w,-\delta_{1}+\zeta+1,w\bigr)/\delta_{1},\ \delta_{1}=(1-tz)^{2}-i(\zeta+1)t^{2}w.

Integrating the second vector field, we have

(z,\zeta,w)\mapsto\bigl(z(1-irz)-r(\zeta-1)w,\delta_{2}+\zeta-1,w\bigr)\bigl/\delta_{2},\quad\delta_{2}=(1-irz)^{2}+i(\zeta-1)r^{2}w.

Composing the automorphisms obtained above, we obtain

Lemma 2.2

2.3.
An invariant for CR maps into the tube over the future light cone

In this section, we analyze a tensor attached to each transversal CR map into the tube over the future light cone or into
{\mathcal{R}}
that is similar to the CR Ahlfors derivative of CR immersions studied recently by Lamel and the second author. We refer the reader to
[
28
]
for some details about the origin, motivation, and a general differential geometric construction of the CR Ahlfors tensor for CR immersions in the strictly pseudoconvex setting. In our current setting, the construction is rather ad-hoc, yet the resulting tensor is still useful for our purpose. Namely, it has an invariant property and gives an easy way to distinguish inequivalent maps into the tube over the future light cone. Indeed, we shall use it in the proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Let
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
be a real hypersurface defined by
\varrho_{M}=0
and
H\colon M\to{\mathcal{R}}
a CR transversal CR map extending holomorphically to a neighbourhood of
M
. By the CR transversality,
there is a real-valued smooth
v
such that

\varrho_{{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{m}}}\circ H=\pm e^{v}\varrho_{M},\quad\varrho_{{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{m}}}:=1-2Z\overline{Z}^{t}+|ZZ^{t}|^{2}.

Without lost of generality, we assume that the positive sign occurs. If
M
is a sphere we take
\varrho_{M}:=1-|z|^{2}
and fix the defining function of the target
{\mathcal{R}}
to be
\varrho_{{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{m}}}
. Then we define the
Ahlfors-type tensor

\mathcal{A}(H)
by

(2.8)

\mathcal{A}(H)=v_{Z\overline{Z}}\bigl|_{T^{(1,0)}M\times T^{(0,1)}M},

where
v_{Z\overline{Z}}
is the
(1,1)
-Hessian of
v
. For any
\psi\in\Aut({D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{m}})
, we have

\varrho_{{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{m}}}\circ\psi=|q|^{2}\varrho_{{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{m}}}

for some rational function
q
having no pole and zero on
{\mathcal{R}}
. Thus, arguing as in
[
28
]
, we see that
\mathcal{A}(H)
is invariant with respect to composing with automorphisms of
{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{m}}
, i.e., on the smooth boundary part
{\mathcal{R}}
,

\mathcal{A}(H)=\mathcal{A}(\psi\circ H),\quad\forall\ \psi\in\Aut({D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{m}}).

Similarly, on the sphere

\mathcal{A}(H\circ\gamma^{-1})=|\tilde{q}|^{2}\mathcal{A}(H)

holds for every automorphism
\gamma
of the ball with a nonvanishing CR function
\tilde{q}
. These invariant properties do not hold for general defining functions of the source and target.

Let
\{Z_{\alpha}\}
be a local frame for the
T^{(1,0)}M
, then we say that
H
has
Ahlfors rank

k
at
p
if the Hermitian form in (

) has rank

\mathrm{Rk}_{\mathcal{A}}(H)
the Ahlfors rank of

at
p

\mathrm{Rk}_{\mathcal{A}}(H)|_{p}=\mathrm{Rk}_{\mathcal{A}}(\psi\circ H\circ\gamma^{-1})\bigl|_{\gamma(p)}.

If
M
is the Heisenberg hypersurface and

is a smooth CR map, then we can define the Ahlfors tensor of

which has the desired invariant property by using the defining function

for the source and the defining function

the target. In fact, with this choice of the defining function for

In the partial normal form (

) of a map germ at a center point

is an invariant property of the map germ. Thus, in analogy with the case of sphere maps
[
18
,
19
]
, we make the following definition.

Definition 3.2
.

Let

be a smooth transversal CR map and

. We say that

has
geometric rank
zero at

if

can be brought into the partial (formal) normal form (
3.1
) with
\alpha=0
. Otherwise we say that
H
has
geometric rank

1
at

.

The geometric rank is precisely the rank of the Ahlfors tensor defined in Section
2.3
but the latter can be computed easily (without the use of explicit formulas for the CR automorphisms). This relation was first noticed by Lamel and the second author in the case of sphere maps in
[
28
]
. Thus the invariant property of the geometric rank also follows from that of the Ahlfors tensor. Indeed, if
H
is given in (
3.1
) and if

\widetilde{\varrho}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ H=\widetilde{Q}\cdot\varrho_{\mathbb{H}^{3}},

then at the origin,

\widetilde{Q}=2,\quad\widetilde{Q}_{z}=0,\quad\widetilde{Q}_{z\bar{z}}=2\alpha.

Hence, for the defining functions as above and for
L:=\partial_{z}-2i\bar{z}\partial_{w}
a section of

, we have

\mathcal{A}(H)(L,\overline{L})\bigl|_{0}=\left(\log\widetilde{Q}\right)_{Z\overline{Z}}(L,\overline{L})\biggl|_{0}=\widetilde{Q}^{-2}\widetilde{Q}_{z\bar{z}}\bigl|_{0}=\frac{\alpha}{2}.

The relation also holds at an arbitrary point

by the invariant property of the left hand side.

Then we obtain the map

as in (i) via

Moreover,

\varrho_{{D^{\mathrm{IV}}_{3}}}\circ R_{0}=\varrho_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}

The family

obtained by composing

Remark 6

Although

and

which has a singularity at the origin. On
V
, we have
\operatorname{Re}(z^{2}\bar{\zeta})=-|\zeta|^{2}v
and thus on
\mathcal{X}\cap V
,

v=\frac{|z|^{2}-v|\zeta|^{2}}{1-|\zeta|^{2}},

implying
v=|z|^{2}
. The real subvariety
V\cap\mathcal{X}
contains a complex variety
(0,\zeta,0)
, and a graph over the Heisenberg hypersurface
v=|z|^{2}
given by
(z,-iz^{2}/w,w)
,
w\neq 0
. All germs of nontranversal CR maps at the origin send an open set in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
into this complex variety, see Lemma
4.24
. From Lemma
2.2
, we find that
V\cap\mathcal{X}
is “invariant” in the following sense: For
(\tilde{z},\tilde{\zeta},\tilde{w})
in the stability group, we have

\tilde{w}\tilde{\zeta}+i\tilde{z}^{2}=-\lambda^{2}u^{2}(w\zeta+iz^{2})/\delta.

That is
\gamma(\mathcal{X}\cap V)\subset\mathcal{X}\cap V
for all
\gamma\in\Aut(\mathcal{X},0)
. From the observation above, we can construct a examples of maps from
\mathbb{H}^{3}
into
\mathcal{X}
as follows.

Example 5.1
.

Each map in the family of rational maps
H_{t}(z,w)=(z,-iz^{2}/(w+t),w+t)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, sends
\mathbb{H}^{3}\setminus\{(0,-t)\}
into
\mathcal{X}\cap V
transversally, but does not extend holomorphically to the point
(0,-t)
. It turns out that at any point
p\in\mathbb{H}^{3}\setminus\{(0,-t)\}
,
H_{t}
represents a germ at
p
that is equivalent to the germ of
\ell
at the origin. In fact,

\widetilde{\varrho}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ H_{t}=|w+t+2i|^{-2}\varrho_{\mathbb{H}^{3}},

and hence outside the pole of
H_{t}
, the Ahlfors tensor
\mathcal{A}(H_{t})
vanishes identically. Thus,
H_{t}
must be equivalent to either
\ell
or
\iota
. But the latter possibility is ruled out by the rationality and
H_{t}
is equivalent to
\ell
. Transferring this map into a map from the unit ball into the type IV model, we obtain a map that is equivalent to
R_{0}
. Explicitly, with

\Phi\circ H_{0}\circ\mathcal{C}\circ\gamma_{1}=-R_{0},

for

is an automorphism of
\mathbb{B}^{2}
exchanging the origin and
(0,-1/3)
.

Example 5.3
.

The mapping equation (
4.2
) can be regarded as a system of infinitely many linear and nonlinear equations for the Taylor coefficients of the components of the map under consideration. Each solution to this system gives rise to holomorphic or formal CR map sending the germ
(\mathbb{H}^{3},0)
into
(\mathcal{X},0)
as a subset of formally. We give an example of a holomorphic map whose Taylor coefficients solve all
but
three equations of weight 14 as well as three holomorphic functional equations in Lemma
4.19
, yet the map does
not
send
\mathbb{H}^{3}
into
\mathcal{X}
. Indeed, consider the following family of holomorphic maps

H:=(f,\phi,g)=\left(z+2tzw^{3},-4itz^{2}w^{2},w+tw^{4}\right),\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

which satisfies the functional equations in Lemma
4.19
.
If
\Sigma_{1}\colon(z,\bar{z},\bar{w})\mapsto(z,\bar{w}+2iz\bar{z})
is a parametrization of the (complexification) of the Heisenberg hypersurface, then

Hence,

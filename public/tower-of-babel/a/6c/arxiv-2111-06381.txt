Hyperbolic geometry and real moduli of five points on the line
Olivier de Gaay Fortman

Abstract
We show that each connected component of the moduli space of smooth real binary quintics is isomorphic to an open subset of an arithmetic quotient of the real hyperbolic plane. Moreover, our main result says that the induced metric on this moduli space extends to a complete real hyperbolic orbifold structure on the space of stable real binary quintics. This turns the moduli space of stable real binary quintics into the quotient of the real hyperbolic plane by an explicit non-arithmetic triangle group.

14J15, 14P99 (primary), 22E40 (secondary).

Introduction

For interesting classes of complex varieties, there is a period map that identifies the moduli space with an open subset of an arithmetic quotient of a hermitian symmetric domain. Classical examples include abelian varieties, K3-surfaces, and configurations of points on the line. To study moduli of real algebraic varieties, several authors analyzed the equivariance of the complex period map with respect to the action of complex conjugation on cohomology
[
Kha76
,
Nik79
,
Kha84
,
SS89
,
Thu98
,
Yos98
,
AY98
,
DIK00
]
. An important difference between the complex and real case is that moduli spaces of smooth real varieties are often not connected. This implies that a real period map has to be defined on each connected component of the moduli space separately; in favourable cases, this defines an isomorphism between any such a component and the quotient of a Riemannian manifold by a discrete group of isometries, see e.g.
[
GH81
,
ACT10
,
Chu11
,
HR18
]
.

To salvage the non-connectedness of the real moduli space, one can sometimes define a slightly larger moduli space by allowing mild singularities. The idea is that in such a larger space, the smooth varieties of one topological type now do deform into smooth varieties of another topological type, making the moduli space
connected
.
In their beautiful paper
[
ACT10
]
, Allcock, Carlson and Toledo showed that for cubic surfaces, the real period maps defined on the various connected components of the moduli space of smooth surfaces extend to a global period map, defined on the moduli space of stable real cubic surfaces. In this way, they identified the latter with a single non-arithmetic real hyperbolic quotient. They proved analogous results for moduli of stable binary sextics, and stable binary sextics with a double root at infinity
[
ACT06
,
ACT07
]
.

It turns out that binary quintics provide a new example of this phenomenon. Let
X\cong{\mathbb{A}}^{6}_{\mathbb{R}}
be the real algebraic variety that parametrizes homogeneous polynomials
F\in\mathbb{R}[x,y]
of degree five. Let
X_{0}\subset X
parametrize polynomials with distinct roots, and
X_{s}\subset X
polynomials with roots of multiplicity at most two (i.e. stable in the sense of geometric invariant theory). The principal goal of this paper is to study the geometry of the moduli space of stable
real binary quintics

{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})\coloneqq\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus X_{s}(\mathbb{R})\supset\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus X_{0}(\mathbb{R})\eqqcolon{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(\mathbb{R}).

Let
P_{s}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})^{5}
be the set of five-tuples
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{5})
such that no three
x_{i}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
coincide (cf.
[
MS72
]
), and let
P_{0}\subset P_{s}
be the subset of five-tuples whose coordinates are distinct. These spaces are naturally acted upon by
{\mathfrak{S}}_{5}
, the symmetric group on five letters. Moreover, complex conjugation
\sigma\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})^{5}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})^{5}
induces an anti-holomorphic involution
\sigma\colon P_{s}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{5}\to P_{s}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{5}
that preserves
P_{0}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{5}
. Let
(P_{0}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{5})(\mathbb{R})
and
(P_{s}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{5})(\mathbb{R})
denote the respective fixed loci. Then

{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\cong\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus(P_{0}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{5})(\mathbb{R})\;\;\;\;\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\;\;\;\;{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})\cong\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus(P_{s}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{5})(\mathbb{R}).

In other words,
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of subsets
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
of cardinality
|S|=5
stable under complex conjugation modulo real projective transformations; in
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
one or two pairs of points are allowed to collapse.

By Deligne–Mostow theory, the coarse moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{C})=\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\setminus X_{s}(\mathbb{C})
of stable complex binary quintics has a complex hyperbolic orbifold structure.
Indeed, for five distinct points
u_{1},\dotsc,u_{5}\in{\mathbb{A}}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
, the projective model of the normalization of the affine curve
z^{5}=(x-u_{1})^{2}\cdots(x-u_{5})^{2}
is a smooth curve
C
of genus six; this curve
C
carries an automorphism of order five that induces an automorphism on the space
{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(C,\Omega^{1}_{C})
of holomorphic one-forms on
C
whose
e^{2\pi i/5}
-eigenspace defines a line in the corresponding eigenspace in
{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(C(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C})
. This line is negative for a natural hermitian form, and hence one can associate to
\left\{u_{1},\dotsc,u_{5}\right\}
a point in a certain two-dimensional complex ball quotient
P\Gamma\setminus{\mathbb{C}}H^{2}
. This construction was already known to Shimura, see
[
Shi63
,
Shi64
]
. By varying the subset
\left\{u_{1},\dotsc,u_{5}\right\}
of points on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
, or rather the associated complex binary quintic, one obtains a period map
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(\mathbb{C})=\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\setminus X_{0}(\mathbb{C})\to P\Gamma\setminus{\mathbb{C}}H^{2}
(see Section
4.1
for details). Results of Deligne and Mostow
[
DM86
]
imply that this period map extends to an isomorphism of complex analytic spaces
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{C})=\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\setminus X_{s}(\mathbb{C})\xrightarrow{\sim}P\Gamma\setminus\mathbb{C}H^{2}
, see Theorem
4.1
. Since strictly stable quintics correspond to points in a hyperplane arrangement
{\mathscr{H}}\subset\mathbb{C}H^{2}
, see Proposition
4.3
, one thus obtains an isomorphism

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(\mathbb{C})=\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\setminus X_{0}(\mathbb{C})\xrightarrow{\sim}P\Gamma\setminus\left(\mathbb{C}H^{2}-{\mathscr{H}}\right).

(1)

By investigating the equivariance of the period map with respect to suitable anti-holomorphic involutions
\alpha_{j}\colon\mathbb{C}H^{2}\to\mathbb{C}H^{2},
we obtain the following real analogue.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
j\in\{0,1,2\}
, let
{\mathscr{M}}_{j}
be the connected component of
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
parametrizing
\textnormal{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})
-stable subsets
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
with
2j
complex and
5-2j
real points. The period map induces an isomorphism of real analytic orbifolds

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}_{j}\xrightarrow{\sim}P\Gamma_{j}\setminus\left(\mathbb{R}H^{2}-{\mathscr{H}}_{j}\right).

(2)

Here
\mathbb{R}H^{2}
is the real hyperbolic plane,
{\mathscr{H}}_{j}
a union of geodesic subspaces in
\mathbb{R}H^{2}
, and
P\Gamma_{j}
an arithmetic lattice in
{\mathrm{PO}}(2,1)
. Moreover, the lattices

are projective orthogonal groups attached to explicit quadratic forms over
\mathbb{Z}[\lambda]
,

In particular,

1.1
endows each connected component

with a hyperbolic metric. Since one can deform the topological type of a
\textnormal{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})
-stable five-element subset of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
by allowing two points to collide, the compactification
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})\supset{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
is connected. One may wonder whether the metrics on the components
{\mathscr{M}}_{j}
extend to a metric on the whole of
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
. If so, what does the resulting space look like at the boundary? Our main result answers these questions in the following way.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a complete hyperbolic metric on
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
that restricts to the metrics on
{\mathscr{M}}_{j}
induced by (
2
). Let
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{\mathbb{R}}
denote the resulting metric space, and define
\Gamma_{3,5,10}
as the group

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3,5,10}=\langle a,b,c\mid a^{2}=b^{2}=c^{2}=(ab)^{3}=(ac)^{5}=(bc)^{10}=1\rangle.

(3)

There exists an open embedding
\coprod_{j}P\Gamma_{j}\setminus\left(\mathbb{R}H^{2}-{\mathscr{H}}_{j}\right)\hookrightarrow\Gamma_{3,5,10}\setminus\mathbb{R}H^{2}
and an isometry

(4)

that extend the real analytic orbifold isomorphisms (

is isometric to the hyperbolic triangle

3
Preliminaries on quintic covers of the projective line branched along five points

In this section, we recollect some known results on quintic covers
C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
ramified along five points with local monodromy
\exp(4\pi i/5)
around each point. Some of these results are well-known but hard to find in the literature; we state and prove those for convenience of the reader. Other results of this section are available in the literature, but we formulate them in a different manner.

Recall from the introduction that
X\cong{\mathbb{A}}^{6}_{\mathbb{R}}
is the real algebraic variety that parametrizes homogeneous polynomials of degree five,
X_{0}
the subvariety of polynomials with distinct roots, and
X_{s}\subset X
the subvariety of polynomials with roots of multiplicity at most two, i.e. non-zero polynomials whose class in the associated projective space is stable in the sense of geometric invariant theory
[
MFK94
]
for the action of
\textnormal{SL}_{2,\mathbb{R}}
on it.

3.1
Refined Hodge numbers of a quintic cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
branched along five points

Let
F\in X_{0}(\mathbb{C})
be a smooth binary quintic; thus
F=F(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree five whose zeros in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
all have multiplicity one. Let
t_{1},\dotsc,t_{5}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
be the zeros of
F
, with
t_{j}=[u_{j}\colon v_{j}]
in homogeneous coordinates of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
, for
j=1,\dotsc,5
. Let

\displaystyle C_{F}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}

(5)

be the cyclic quintic cover with branch points
t_{1},\dotsc,t_{5}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
and local monodromy

\displaystyle\exp(2\pi i\cdot 2/5)\in\mu_{5}

(6)

around
t_{j}
for each
j\in\left\{1,\dotsc,5\right\}
. If the points
t_{j}=[u_{j}\colon 1]
are all in
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
, the cover (
5
) is in affine coordinates given by the normalization of the curve defined by the equation

\displaystyle z^{5}=(x-u_{1})^{2}\cdot(x-u_{2})^{2}\cdots(x-u_{5})^{2},

with
\zeta\in\mu_{5}\subset\mathbb{C}
acting by
(x,z)\mapsto(x,\zeta\cdot z)
. We have
g=6
for the genus
g=g(C_{F})
of the curve
C_{F}
. Let
JC_{F}
be the Jacobian of the curve
C_{F}
, so that

JC_{F}(\mathbb{C})={\mathrm{H}}^{1}(C_{F}(\mathbb{C}),\mathcal{O}_{C_{F}})/{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(C_{F}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})(1),

with weight
-1
Hodge decomposition

\displaystyle{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(JC_{F}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C})={\mathrm{H}}^{1}(C_{F}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})(1)\otimes\mathbb{C}={\mathrm{H}}^{-1,0}(JC_{F})\oplus{\mathrm{H}}^{0,-1}(JC_{F}).

(7)

Note that
{\mathrm{H}}^{0,-1}(JC_{F})
is naturally isomorphic to the space
{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(C_{F},\Omega^{1})
of global holomorphic differentials on the curve
C_{F}
. The order five automorphism

\zeta\colon C_{F}\xrightarrow{\sim}C_{F}

defined above induces an embedding of rings

\varphi\colon\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]\to\textnormal{End}(JC_{F}),\quad\varphi(\zeta)=(\zeta^{-1})^{\ast},

which is compatible with the Hodge decomposition (

). For
k\in\left\{1,2,3,4\right\}
, define

{\mathrm{H}}^{0,-1}(JC_{F})_{\zeta^{k}}=\left\{x\in{\mathrm{H}}^{0,-1}(JC_{F})\mid\varphi(\zeta)=\zeta^{k}\right\}\subset{\mathrm{H}}^{0,-1}(JC_{F})={\mathrm{H}}^{1,0}(C_{F})={\mathrm{H}}^{0}(C_{F},\Omega^{1}),

It is well-known how to calculate the refined Hodge numbers

and

; the result is as follows.

Let

3.2
The hermitian lattice

We fix a smooth binary quintic

. Let

Consider the different ideal

3.3
The signature

Define a CM type
\Phi\subset\textnormal{Hom}(K,\mathbb{C})
as

\displaystyle\Phi=\left\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\colon K\to\mathbb{C}\right\},\quad\sigma_{k}(\zeta)=\zeta^{k}\quad\text{for}\quad k=1,2.

(9)

Observe that, for
k=1,2
, we have

\Lambda\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K},\sigma_{k}}\mathbb{C}=\left(\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}\right)_{\zeta^{k}}={\mathrm{H}}^{1}(C(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C})_{\zeta^{k}}.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
k\in\left\{1,2\right\}
, consider the hermitian form

{\mathfrak{h}}^{\sigma_{k}}\colon{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(C(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C})_{\zeta^{k}}\times{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(C(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C})_{\zeta^{k}}\to\mathbb{C}.

Then
{\textnormal{sign}}({\mathfrak{h}}^{\sigma_{1}})=(2,1)
and
{\textnormal{sign}}({\mathfrak{h}}^{\sigma_{2}})=(3,0)
, where
{\textnormal{sign}}({\mathfrak{h}}^{\sigma_{k}})
is the signature of
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\sigma_{k}}
.

Proof.

Write
\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}=\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}={\mathrm{H}}^{1}(C(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C})
.
For each embedding
\phi\colon K\to\mathbb{C}
, the restriction of the hermitian form

\phi\left(5/\eta\right)\cdot E_{\mathbb{C}}(x,\bar{y})\colon\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}\times\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{C}

to
(\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}})_{\phi}\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
coincides with

{\mathfrak{h}}^{\phi}
by
[
GF23
, Lemma 11.3]
. Moreover, the hermitian form

i\cdot E_{\mathbb{C}}(x,\bar{y})\colon{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(C(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C})\times{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(C(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{C}

is positive definite on

{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(C,\Omega^{1})={\mathrm{H}}^{1,0}(C)={\mathrm{H}}^{0,-1}(JC)

and negative definite on
{\mathrm{H}}^{-1,0}(JC)={\mathrm{H}}^{0,1}(C)
, see
[
Voi02
, Théorème 6.32]
. As
\Im(\sigma_{1}(\eta))>0
and
\Im(\sigma_{2}(\eta))<0
, we have
\Im(\sigma_{1}(5/\eta))<0
and
\Im(\sigma_{2}(5/\eta))>0
. Consequently, the hermitian form

\sigma_{1}(5/\eta)\cdot E_{\mathbb{C}}(x,\bar{y})={\mathfrak{h}}^{\sigma_{1}}(x,y)\colon{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(C(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C})_{\zeta}\times{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(C(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C})_{\zeta}\to\mathbb{C}

is negative definite on
{\mathrm{H}}^{0,-1}(JC)_{\zeta}
and positive definite on
{\mathrm{H}}^{-1,0}(JC)_{\zeta}
, so that
{\textnormal{sign}}({\mathfrak{h}}^{\sigma_{1}})=(2,1)
by Lemma
3.1
. Similarly, the hermitian form

\sigma_{2}(5/\eta)\cdot E_{\mathbb{C}}(x,\bar{y})={\mathfrak{h}}^{\sigma_{2}}(x,y)\colon{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(C(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C})_{\zeta^{2}}\times{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(C(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C})_{\zeta^{2}}\to\mathbb{C}

is positive definite on
{\mathrm{H}}^{0,-1}(JC)_{\zeta^{2}}
and negative definite on
{\mathrm{H}}^{-1,0}(JC)_{\zeta^{2}}
. Hence, using Lemma
3.1
again, we conclude that
{\textnormal{sign}}({\mathfrak{h}}^{\sigma_{2}})=(3,0)
.
∎

3.4
The monodromy representation

Consider the real algebraic variety
X_{0}
introduced in Section
1
. Let
D\subset\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be the subgroup
D=\left\{\zeta^{j}\cdot\textnormal{Id}\right\}\subset\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
of scalar matrices of the form
\zeta^{j}\cdot\textnormal{Id}
with
j\in\left\{0,1,2,3,4\right\}
and
\textnormal{Id}\in\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
the identity two by two matrix. Define

\displaystyle G(\mathbb{C})=\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})/D.

(10)

The group
G(\mathbb{C})
acts from the left on
X_{0}(\mathbb{C})
in the following way: if

F=F(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]

is a binary quintic, we may view
F
as a function
\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
, and define
g\cdot F=F(g^{-1})
for
g\in G(\mathbb{C})
. This gives a canonical isomorphism of complex analytic orbifolds

{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(\mathbb{C})=G(\mathbb{C})\setminus X_{0}(\mathbb{C}),

where
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
is the moduli stack of smooth binary quintics.
Define

\displaystyle g\colon{\mathscr{C}}\to X_{0}

(11)

as the universal family of cyclic quintic covers
C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
ramified along a smooth binary quintic
\{F=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
with local monodromy given by (
6
), and let

J=J({\mathscr{C}})\to X_{0}

be the relative Jacobian of
{\mathscr{C}}/X_{0}
. By Lemma
3.2
,
J
is a polarized abelian scheme of relative dimension six over
X_{0}
, equipped with
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-action of signature
\{(2,1),(3,0)\}
with respect to
\Phi=\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\}
. Let
{\mathbb{V}}=R^{1}g_{\ast}\mathbb{Z}
be the local system of hermitian
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-modules underlying the abelian scheme
J/X_{0}
. Attached to
{\mathbb{V}}
and the base point
F_{0}\in X_{0}(\mathbb{C})
, we have a representation

\displaystyle\rho_{\Gamma}\colon\pi_{1}(X_{0}(\mathbb{C}),F_{0})\to\Gamma\coloneqq\textnormal{Aut}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}(\Lambda,{\mathfrak{h}})

(12)

whose composition with the quotient map
\Gamma\to P\Gamma=\Gamma/\mu_{K}
defines a homomorphism

P(\rho_{\Gamma})\colon\pi_{1}(X_{0}(\mathbb{C}),F_{0})\to P\Gamma.

(13)

3.5
Marked binary quintics and points on the projective line

Let
F\in X_{0}(\mathbb{C})
and consider the hypersurface

Z_{F}\coloneqq\{F=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
. A
marking
of
F
is an ordering
m\colon Z_{F}(\mathbb{C})\xrightarrow{\sim}\left\{1,2,3,4,5\right\}
of the five-element set
Z_{F}(\mathbb{C})=\left\{x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\mid F(x)=0\right\}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
F\in X_{0}(\mathbb{C})
. To give a marking of
F
is to give an isomorphism of rings
{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(Z_{F}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{Z}^{5}
. Moreover, sending a ring automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{5}
to the induced permutation of the canonical basis of
\left\{e_{1},\dotsc,e_{5}\right\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{5}
defines a group isomorphism
\textnormal{Aut}_{{\mathrm{ring}}}(\mathbb{Z}^{5})\cong{\mathfrak{S}}_{5}
.

Define
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
as the set of marked smooth complex binary quintics
(F,m)
, and consider the map that forgets the marking:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{0}\to X_{0}(\mathbb{C}),\quad\quad(F,m)\mapsto F.

(14)

To provide
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
with a complex manifold structure such that (
14
) is a finite covering map, we define
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
in a slightly different but equivalent way. Define algebraic subgroup

T\coloneqq\left\{(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{5})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{5}\mid\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{5}=1\right\}\subset(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{5}.

The group
T
acts freely on
(\mathbb{C}^{2}-\left\{0\right\})^{5}
in the usual way, i.e. by letting
(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{5})\in T
act as

\left((a_{1},b_{1}),\dotsc,(a_{5},b_{5})\right)\mapsto\left(\lambda_{1}\cdot(a_{1},b_{1}),\dotsc,\lambda_{5}\cdot(a_{5},b_{5})\right).

As
\dim(T)=4
, the quotient variety

{\mathcal{N}}\coloneqq(\mathbb{C}^{2}-\left\{0\right\})^{5}/T

is a complex manifold of dimension six, equipped with a holomorphic principal
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-bundle map

\displaystyle f\colon{\mathcal{N}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})^{5},\quad\quad\left(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{5}\right)\mapsto\left([v_{1}],\dotsc,[v_{5}]\right),

(15)

where
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
acts on
{\mathcal{N}}
by
\lambda\cdot(v_{1},v_{2},\dotsc,v_{5})=(\lambda v_{1},v_{2},\dotsc,v_{5})=(v_{1},\lambda v_{2},\dotsc,v_{5})=\cdots=(v_{1},v_{2},\dotsc,\lambda v_{5})\in{\mathcal{N}}
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
and
(v_{1},v_{2},\dotsc,v_{5})\in{\mathcal{N}}
. For
i,j\in\left\{1,\dotsc,5\right\}
with
i<j

\overline{\Delta}_{ij}=\left\{(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{5})\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})^{5}\mid x_{i}=x_{j}\right\}
, and let

\Delta_{ij}\coloneqq f^{-1}(\overline{\Delta}_{ij}),

where

is the principal bundle map (
15

\overline{\Delta}_{ij}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})^{5}
is Zariski closed in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})^{5}

\Delta_{ij}
is Zariski closed in
{\mathcal{N}}
. Define a Zariski open subset

as follows:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{0}\coloneqq{\mathcal{N}}-\bigcup_{i<j}\Delta_{ij}.

Lemma 3.4
.

The complex manifold
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
is connected and there is a canonical bijection between
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
and the set of marked smooth complex binary quintics
(F,m)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{0}\to X_{0}(\mathbb{C}),\quad\left((a_{1},b_{1}),(a_{2},b_{2}),\dotsc,(a_{5},b_{5})\right)\mapsto\prod_{j=1}^{5}\left(b_{j}\cdot x-a_{j}\cdot y\right)

{\mathfrak{S}}_{5}

Proof.

Clear.
∎

In a similar way, we define, for

with
i<j<k

3.7
Framed binary quintics, nodal binary quintics and local monodromy

By a
framing
of a point
F\in X_{0}(\mathbb{C})
we mean a projective equivalence class
[f]
, where

f\colon\mathbb{V}_{F}={\mathrm{H}}^{1}(C_{F}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Lambda

is an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-linear isometry: two such isometries are in the same class if and only if they differ by an element in
\mu_{K}=(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\ast})_{{\mathrm{tors}}}
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
be the collection of all framings of all points
F\in X_{0}(\mathbb{C})
. The set
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
can naturally be given the structure of a complex manifold, in a way similar to the procedure described in
[
ACT02a
, (3.9)]
. In the sequel, we consider
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
as a complex manifold.

Lemma 3.7
.

1.

The complex manifold
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
is connected and the map

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\to X_{0}(\mathbb{C}),\quad\quad(F,[f])\mapsto F

(20)

that forgets the framing is a Galois covering map, with Galois group
P\Gamma
.

2.

The spaces
P\Gamma_{\theta}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
are isomorphic as covering spaces of
X_{0}(\mathbb{C})
. In particular, there is a covering map
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\to{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
with Galois group
P\Gamma_{\theta}
.

Proof.

Indeed,
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
is connected by Corollary
3.6
. The isomorphism
P\Gamma_{\theta}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\cong{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
of covering spaces of
X_{0}(\mathbb{C})
follows from the isomorphism
P\Gamma/P\Gamma_{\theta}\cong{\mathfrak{S}}_{5}
as quotients of
\pi_{1}(X_{0}(\mathbb{C}),F_{0})
, which was shown in Proposition
3.5
. The lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

The subvariety
\Delta\coloneqq X_{s}-X_{0}
is an irreducible normal crossings divisor in
X_{s}
.

Proof.

The irreducibility of

[

.
Let
(p,F)
be a point on the incidence variety
{\mathcal{I}}=\left\{(p,F)\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X_{s}\mid p\in{\textnormal{Sing}}(F)\right\}
, choose a hyperplane away from
p
, and view
p\in\mathbb{C}
and
F

x=\left\{1,i,-1,-i,0\right\},\quad\phi(z)=i\cdot z\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C}).

As a consequence, we have

∎

We proceed to prove the real analogue of Proposition

Proposition 6.4
.

Let

such that the stabilizer group

We have an injection

which is equivariant for the embedding

\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})_{x}\subset\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})_{x}

have subgroups isomorphic to

or

. Consider the involution
\nu=(z\mapsto 1/z)\in\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
We will prove the proposition by using the following steps.

Step 1:

Let
\tau\in\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Consider a subset
S=\{x,y,z\} \subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})

\tau(x)=x

\tau(y)=z
and
\tau(z)=y

that maps

to either

, and that satisfies

\tau^{2}=\textnormal{id}
.

Proof.

Indeed, two transformations

∎

Step 2:

By

Step 3:

Define

.
Let
x=(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{5})\in(P_{s}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{5})(\mathbb{R})
. Let
\phi\in\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

. There is a transformation

mapping

to

a primitive third root of unity. The stabilizer of

to the subgroup of
\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
generated by
\rho
and
\nu
. In particular, we have

Proof.

By Step 1, there are elements

which form an orbit under

. Since complex conjugation preserves this orbit, one element in it is real; since

\mathbb{R}

\infty

\infty

is equivalent to an element of the form

∎

Recall that
\lambda=\zeta_{5}+\zeta_{5}^{-1}\in\mathbb{R}

Step 4:

Let
x=(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{5})\in(P_{s}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{5})(\mathbb{R})
. Suppose

of order five. There is a transformation

to

and identifying the stabilizer of

with the subgroup of
\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

and

Proof.

Let

are pairwise distinct, and we may assume that

for

and

Let

6.2
Comparing the orbifold structures

There are two orbifold structures on the space
\left|{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})\right|
: on the one hand, one has the natural orbifold structure on
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
by considering it as the real locus of a smooth separated Deligne–Mumford stack over
\mathbb{R}
(see
[
GF22
, Proposition 2.12]
), this is the orbifold structure of the quotient

; on the other hand, one has the orbifold structure
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{\mathbb{R}}
introduced in Definition

. The goal of Section

is to calculate the difference between these orbifold structures.

We first show that there are no cone points in the orbifold
\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus(P_{s}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{5})(\mathbb{R})
. These are orbifold points whose stabilizer group is
\mathbb{Z}/n
(

) acting on the orbifold chart by rotations. By Proposition
6.4
, the fact that
\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus(P_{s}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{5})(\mathbb{R})
has no cone points follows from:

Lemma 6.5
.

Let

such that
\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})_{x}=\langle\tau\rangle

\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})_{x}
-stable open neighborhood

for

an open ball, such that
\phi
identifies
\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})_{x}
with
\mathbb{Z}/2
acting on
B
by reflections in a line through

.

Proof.

Using Step 1 in the proof of Proposition
6.4
, one checks that the only possibilities for the element

are
(-1,0,\infty,\beta,\beta^{-1})

(-1,i,-i,\beta,\beta^{-1})

∎

To analyze the difference between the two orbifolds
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
and

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
X
be a set and let
\Gamma
and
G
be groups with commuting actions on

x\in X
with images
\bar{x}\in\Gamma\setminus X
and

\Gamma_{[x]}
be the stabilizer of

in
\Gamma

be the stabilizer of

in

\displaystyle\Gamma_{[x]}/\Gamma_{x}\to G_{\bar{x}}/G_{x},\quad\quad\gamma\mapsto\phi(\gamma)

(37)

Proof.

The map (

) is well-defined because if

Recall, see

Proposition 6.7

Consider the orbifold structures

6.3
The real moduli space as a hyperbolic triangle

The goal of Section
6.3
is to prove Theorem
6.2
.

The results in the above Sections
6.1
and
6.2
give the orbifold singularities of
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{\mathbb{R}}
together with their stabilizer groups. In order to determine the hyperbolic orbifold structure of
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{\mathbb{R}}
, we also need to know the underlying topological space
\left|{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})\right|
of
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{\mathbb{R}}
. The first observation is that
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
is compact. Indeed, it is classical that the topological space
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{C})=G(\mathbb{C})\setminus X_{s}(\mathbb{C})
, parametrizing complex stable binary quintics, is compact. This follows from the proper surjective map
\overline{M}_{0,5}(\mathbb{C})/{\mathfrak{S}}_{5}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{C})
and the properness of the stack of stable five-pointed curves
\overline{M}_{0,5}

[
Knu83
]
, or from the fact that
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{C})
is homeomorphic to a compact ball quotient
[
Shi64
]
. Moreover, the map
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})\to{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{C})
is proper, which proves the compactness of
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
. The second observation is that
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
is connected, since
X_{s}(\mathbb{R})
is obtained from the euclidean space
X(\mathbb{R})=\{F\in\mathbb{R}[x,y]\colon F\text{ homogeneous and}\deg(F)=5\}
by removing a subspace of codimension two. In the following lemma we generalize both of these observations.

Lemma 6.8
.

The moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
is homeomorphic to a closed disc
\overline{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Proof.

The idea is to show that the following holds:

1.

For each
j\in\{0,1,2\}
, the embedding
{\mathscr{M}}_{j}\hookrightarrow\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{j}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{s}({\mathbb{R}})
of the connected component
{\mathscr{M}}_{j}
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
into its closure in
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}({\mathbb{R}})
is homeomorphic to the embedding
D\hookrightarrow\overline{D}
of the open unit disc into the closed unit disc in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

2.

We have
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})=\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{0}\cup\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{1}\cup\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{2}
, and this gluing corresponds up to homeomorphism to the gluing of three closed discs
\overline{D}_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
as in Figure
1
.

To prove this, one considers the moduli spaces of real smooth (resp. stable) genus zero curves with five real marked points, as well as twists of this space. Define two anti-holomorphic involutions
\sigma_{i}\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})^{5}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})^{5}
by

\sigma_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})=(\bar{x}_{1},\bar{x}_{2},\bar{x}_{3},\bar{x}_{5},\bar{x}_{4}),

and
\sigma(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})=(\bar{x}_{1},\bar{x}_{3},\bar{x}_{2},\bar{x}_{5},\bar{x}_{4}).

Then define

P_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{R})=P_{0}^{\sigma_{1}},\;\;\;P_{s}^{1}(\mathbb{R})=P_{1}(\mathbb{C})^{\sigma_{1}},\;\;\;P_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{R})=P_{0}^{\sigma_{2}},\;\;\;P_{s}^{2}(\mathbb{R})=P_{1}(\mathbb{C})^{\sigma_{2}}.

It is clear that
{\mathscr{M}}_{0}=\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus P_{0}(\mathbb{R})/{\mathfrak{S}}_{5}
. Similarly, we have:

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}_{1}=\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus P_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{R})/{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}\quad{\textnormal{ and }}\quad{\mathscr{M}}_{2}=\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus P_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{R})/{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}.

Moreover, we have
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{0}=\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus P_{s}(\mathbb{R})/{\mathfrak{S}}_{5}
. We define

\displaystyle\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{1}=\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus P_{s}^{1}(\mathbb{R})/{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{2},\quad{\textnormal{ and }}\quad\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{2}=\textnormal{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus P_{s}^{2}(\mathbb{R})/{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}.

Each
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{j}
is homeomorphic to a closed disc in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Moreover, the natural maps
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{j}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
are closed embeddings of topological spaces, and one can check that the images glue to form
{\mathcal{M}}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
in the prescribed way. We leave the details to the reader.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

To any closed two-dimensional orbifold
O
one can associate a set of natural numbers
S_{O}=\{n_{1},\dotsc,n_{k};m_{1},\dotsc,m_{l}\}
by letting
k
be the number of cone points of
X_{O}
,
l
the number of corner reflectors,
n_{i}
the order of the
i
-th cone point and
m_{j}
the order of the
j
-th corner reflector (see
[
Thu80
, Proposition 13.3.1]
). A closed two-dimensional orbifold
O
is determined, up to orbifold-structure preserving homeomorphism, by its underlying space
X_{O}
and the set
S_{O}

[
Thu80
]
. By Lemma
6.8
,
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{\mathbb{R}}
is homeomorphic to a closed disc in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. By Proposition
6.7
,
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{\mathbb{R}}
has no cone points and three corner reflectors whose angles are
\pi/3,\pi/5
and
\pi/10
. This implies that
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\Delta_{3,5,10}
are isomorphic as topological orbifolds. Consequently, the orbifold fundamental group of
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{\mathbb{R}}
is abstractly isomorphic to the group
\Gamma_{3,5,10}
defined in equation (
3
).

Let
\phi\colon\Gamma_{3,5,10}\hookrightarrow\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
be
any
embedding such that
X\coloneqq\phi\left(\Gamma_{3,5,10}\right)\setminus\mathbb{R}H^{2}
is a hyperbolic orbifold; we claim that there is a fundamental domain
\Delta
for
X
isometric to
\Delta_{3,5,10}
. To see this, consider the generator
a\in\Gamma_{3,5,10}
. Since
\phi(a)^{2}=1
, there exists a geodesic
L_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}H^{2}
such that
\phi(a)\in\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})=\text{Isom}(\mathbb{R}H^{2})
is the reflection across
L_{1}
. Next, consider the generator
b\in\Gamma_{3,5,10}
. There exists a geodesic
L_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}H^{2}
such that
\phi(b)
is the reflection across
L_{2}
, and we have
L_{2}\cap L_{1}\neq\emptyset
.
Let
x\in L_{1}\cap L_{2}
. Then
\phi(a)\phi(b)
is an element of order three that fixes
x
, hence is a rotation around
x
. Therefore, one of the angles between
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
must be
\pi/3
. Finally, we know that
\phi(c)
is an element of order two in
\textnormal{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, hence a reflection across a line
L_{3}
. As
L_{3}\cap L_{2}\neq\emptyset
and
L_{3}\cap L_{1}\neq\emptyset
, the three geodesics
L_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}H^{2}
enclose a hyperbolic triangle. As the orders of the three elements
\phi(a)\phi(b)
,
\phi(a)\phi(c)
and
\phi(b)\phi(c)
are respectively three, five and ten,
the three interior angles of the triangle are
\pi/3
,
\pi/5
and
\pi/10
. Thus,
X
is isometric to
\Delta_{3,5,10}
.

Consequently,
P\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}H^{2}
is isometric to
\Gamma_{3,5,10}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}H^{2}
. It follows that the lattices
P\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\Gamma_{3,5,10}
are conjugate in
{\mathrm{PO}}(2,1)
, see e.g.
[
Rat99
, Lemma 1]
.
∎

In this section, we describe the monodromy group
P\Gamma

X_{0}(\mathbb{C})
, as well as the groups
P\Gamma_{\alpha}
appearing in Proposition
5.4
. As for the lattice
(\Lambda,{\mathfrak{h}})
(see (
8

Theorem 7.1

(Shimura)
.

There is an isomorphism of hermitian
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-lattices

\left(\Lambda,{\mathfrak{h}}\right)\cong\left(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{3},\textnormal{diag}\left(-\lambda,1,1\right)\right),\quad\lambda=\zeta_{5}+\zeta_{5}^{-1}=\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}.

Proof.

See

as well as item (5) in the table on page 1 of
loc. cit.

Write
\Lambda=\mathcal{O}_{K}^{3}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}=\textnormal{diag}(-\lambda,1,1)
. Consider the
{\mathbb{F}}_{5}
-vector space
W=\Lambda/(1-\zeta_{5})\Lambda
equipped with the quadratic form
q={\mathfrak{h}}\bmod\theta
.
Define three anti-isometric involutions as follows:

\begin{split}\alpha_{0}\colon&(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto(\bar{x}_{0},\;\;\;\bar{x}_{1},\;\;\bar{x}_{2})\\
\alpha_{1}\colon&(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto(\bar{x}_{0},-\bar{x}_{1},\;\;\bar{x}_{2})\\
\alpha_{2}\colon&(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto(\bar{x}_{0},-\bar{x}_{1},-\bar{x}_{2}).\end{split}

(39)

Lemma 7.2
.

An anti-unitary involution of
\Lambda
is
\Gamma
-conjugate to exactly one of the
\pm\alpha_{j}
. In particular,
\left|P\Gamma\setminus P{\mathscr{A}}\right|=3
and the
\alpha_{i}
defined in (

) form a set of representatives for

.

Proof.

For isometries

(W^{\alpha},q|_{W^{\alpha}})
are conjugacy-invariant. Using this, one can show that the elements
\pm\alpha_{i}
are pairwise non
\Gamma
-conjugate. Moreover,
\left|P\Gamma\setminus P{\mathscr{A}}\right|=\left|\pi_{0}({\mathcal{M}}_{0}(\mathbb{R}))\right|=3
by Proposition
5.4
and Theorem
7.1
.
∎

Define

\theta=\zeta_{5}-\zeta_{5}^{-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}.

Note that
|\theta|^{2}=\frac{\sqrt{5}+5}{2}
. The fixed lattices of the anti-unitary involutions

defined in (
39
) are

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Lambda^{\alpha_{0}}&=\mathbb{Z}[\lambda]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[\lambda]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[\lambda],\\

(40)

where

\displaystyle\lambda=\zeta_{5}+\zeta_{5}^{-1}=\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}.

(41)

Restricting
{\mathfrak{h}}
to the
\Lambda^{\alpha_{j}}

q_{0}
,
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
on

defined as follows:

\begin{split}q_{0}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})&=-\lambda x_{0}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2},\\
q_{1}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})&=-\lambda x_{0}^{2}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{5}+5}{2}\right)\cdot x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2},\\
q_{2}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})&=-\lambda x_{0}^{2}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{5}+5}{2}\right)\cdot x_{1}^{2}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{5}+5}{2}\right)\cdot x_{2}^{2},\end{split}

We consider
\mathbb{Z}[\lambda]
(with

as in (

via the embedding that sends

Theorem 7.3
.

Consider the quadratic forms
q_{j}
defined in (
42
), with
\lambda
as in (

). There is a union of geodesic subspaces
{\mathscr{H}}_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}H^{2}

\left(j\in\{0,1,2\}\right)
and an isomorphism of hyperbolic orbifolds

{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\cong\coprod_{j=0}^{2}\textnormal{PO}(q_{j},\mathbb{Z}[\lambda])\setminus\left(\mathbb{R}H^{2}-{\mathscr{H}}_{j}\right).

By Proposition
5.4
and Lemma

, we obtain an isomorphism

{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\cong\coprod_{j=0}^{2}P\Gamma_{\alpha_{j}}\setminus(\mathbb{R}H^{2}_{\alpha_{j}}-{\mathscr{H}}).

Remark that
P\Gamma_{\alpha_{j}}=N_{P\Gamma}(\alpha_{j})
for the normalizer
N_{P\Gamma}(\alpha_{j})
of
\alpha_{j}
in

. If
h_{j}
denotes the restriction of

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

In
[

]
, Apéry and Yoshida proved that
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{0}
is the hyperbolic triangle of angles
\pi/2,\pi/4
and
\pi/5
.
As the two hyperplanes in Figure
1
intersect orthogonally, this implies that the bottom angle of the triangle
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{0}
in Figure
1
(i.e. its angle at
(0,-1,\infty,\infty,1)
) equals
\pi/2
, and that the angle of
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{0}
at
(0,-1,-1,\infty,\infty)
equals
\pi/4
. One deduces that the left angle of
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{1}
is
\pi/2
, and that the angle of
\overline{{\mathscr{M}}}_{2}
at
(0,-1,-1,\infty,\infty)
equals

.

\alpha,\beta,\gamma
and sides

a
is the side opposite to
\alpha
,
b
the side opposite to
\beta
and
\gamma

\displaystyle\cosh(c)=\frac{\cos(\alpha)\cos(\beta)+\cos(\gamma)}{\sin(\alpha)\sin(\beta)}.

(45)

Applying (

8
Non-arithmetic lattices in the projective orthogonal group

In a previous paper we proved a result, see
[
GF23
, Theorem 1.8]
, that has the following consequence. For
n\geq 2
, define

{\mathscr{L}}_{\zeta_{5}}^{n}(\lambda)\coloneqq\left(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5}),\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{5}]^{n,1}_{\lambda}\right),\quad\quad\lambda=\zeta_{5}+\zeta_{5}^{-1}=(\sqrt{5}-1)/2.

Here,
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{5}]^{n,1}_{\lambda}
is the free
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{5}]
-module of rank
n+1
equipped with the hermitian
form
h
defined as
h(x,y)=-\lambda\cdot x_{0}\bar{y}_{0}+\cdots+x_{n}\bar{y}_{n}
. Then
{\mathscr{L}}_{\zeta_{5}}^{n}(\lambda)
is a hermitian lattice of rank
n+1
in the sense of
[
GF23
, Definition 2.2]
(indeed, this follows from
[
GF23
, Example 2.12]
).
For each
n\geq 2
, perform the gluing construction of
[
GF23
, Definition 1.1]
to associate to the hermitian lattice
{\mathscr{L}}_{\zeta_{5}}^{n}(\lambda)
a topological space

M({\mathscr{L}}_{\zeta_{5}}^{n}(\lambda)).

By
[
GF23
, Theorem 1.2]
, there exists a canonical real hyperbolic orbifold structure on
M({\mathscr{L}}_{\zeta_{5}}^{n}(\lambda))
such that each connected component of
M({\mathscr{L}}_{\zeta_{5}}^{n}(\lambda))
is isomorphic to the quotient of real hyperbolic
n
-space
{\mathbb{R}}H^{n}
by a lattice in
{\mathrm{PO}}(n,1)
. Define an anti-unitary involution
\alpha_{0}\colon\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{5}]^{n,1}_{\lambda}\to\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{5}]^{n,1}_{\lambda}
by
\alpha_{0}(x)=\bar{x}
, let

M\left({\mathscr{L}}_{\zeta_{5}}^{n}(\lambda),\alpha_{0}\right)\subset M\left({\mathscr{L}}_{\zeta_{5}}^{n}(\lambda)\right)

be the connected component that contains the image of the natural map
{\mathbb{R}}H^{n}_{\alpha_{0}}\to M({\mathscr{L}}_{\zeta_{5}}^{n}(\lambda))
, and let

\Gamma_{\zeta_{5}}^{n}(\lambda)\subset{\mathrm{PO}}(n,1)

be a lattice such that
M({\mathscr{L}}_{\zeta_{5}}^{n}(\lambda),\alpha_{0})\cong\Gamma_{\zeta_{5}}^{n}(\lambda)\setminus{\mathbb{R}}H^{n}
(compare
[
GF23
, page 7]
).

By combining
[
GF23
, Theorem 1.8]
with the main results of this paper, one can prove the following result.

Theorem 8.1
.

For each
n\geq 2
, the lattice
\Gamma_{\zeta_{5}}^{n}(\lambda)\subset{\mathrm{PO}}(n,1)
is non-arithmetic.

Proof.

Write
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{5}]^{2,1}_{\lambda}=\Lambda
; this abuse of notation is harmless in view of Theorem
7.1
. Let
\Gamma=\textnormal{Aut}(\Lambda)
.
By Lemma
7.2
,
an anti-unitary involution of
\Lambda
is
\Gamma
-conjugate to exactly one of the involutions
\pm\alpha_{j}
defined in equation (
39
). We can therefore apply
[
GF23
, Theorem 1.8]
, which implies that
\Gamma_{\zeta_{5}}^{n}(\lambda)\subset{\mathrm{PO}}(n,1)
is non-arithmetic for each
n\geq 2
provided that
\Gamma_{\zeta_{5}}^{2}(\lambda)\subset{\mathrm{PO}}(2,1)
is non-arithmetic. In other words, we are reduced to the case
n=2
. By Theorem
7.1
, the lattice
\Gamma_{\zeta_{5}}^{2}(\lambda)\subset{\mathrm{PO}}(2,1)
is conjugate to the lattice
P\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}\subset{\mathrm{PO}}(2,1)
defined in Corollary
5.9
. Moreover, by Theorem
6.2
, the lattice
P\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}
is conjugate to the lattice
\Gamma_{3,5,10}\subset{\mathrm{PO}}(2,1)
defined in equation (
3
). Finally, by Takeuchi’s classification of arithmetic triangle groups, see
[
Tak77
]
, the subgroup
\Gamma_{3,5,10}\subset{\mathrm{PO}}(2,1)
is non-arithmetic. Thus,
\Gamma_{\zeta_{5}}^{2}(\lambda)
is non-arithmetic, and the theorem follows.
∎

Smoothed Q-learning University College London Centre for AI Research Note
David Barber

Abstract
In Reinforcement Learning the Q-learning algorithm provably converges to the optimal solution. However, Q-learning can also overestimate the values and thereby spend too long exploring unhelpful states. Double Q-learning is a provably convergent alternative that mitigates some of the overestimation issues, though sometimes at the expense of slower convergence. We introduce an alternative algorithm that replaces the max operation with an average, resulting also in a provably convergent off-policy algorithm which can mitigate overestimation yet retain similar convergence as standard Q-learning.



1
Q-learning and Overestimation

We consider Reinforcement Learning
[
1
]
based on a Markov Decision Process, with states
s
, actions
a
, state transition distribution
p(s^{\prime}|s,a)
and (stochastic) reward
r(s^{\prime}|s,a)
and discounting
\gamma<1
. The optimal value
Q_{*}(s,a)
of taking action
a
from state
s
is the solution to the equation

Q_{*}(s,a)=\sum_{s^{\prime}}p(s^{\prime}|s,a)\left({r(s^{\prime}|s,a)+\gamma\max_{a}Q_{*}(s^{\prime},a)}\right)

(1)

Q-learning is an off-policy approach to finding
Q_{*}
based on the update

Q_{t+1}(s_{t},a_{t})=Q_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})+\alpha_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})\left({r_{t}+\gamma\max_{a}Q_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-Q_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})}\right)

(2)

Here off-policy means that, provided the learning rate satisfies certain conditions and every state-action pair is explored
[
1
]
then, no matter how the states and actions are visited, the update equation(
2
) converges to the optimal policy
Q_{*}
.

In
[
2
]
Hasselt points out that the maximisation operation in the update equation(
2
) means that, for stochastic rewards, the
Q_{t}
values can be over-estimated, resulting in the learning process spending too long in states which are not profitable. Ideally we would replace these
Q_{t}
values by their average over many episodes (since equation(
1
) is an optimality condition for the expected
Q_{*}
over infinitely many episodes). Double Q-learning
[
2
]
addresses this overestimation by estimating two values
Q_{A}(s,a)
,
Q_{B}(s,a)
and deciding on actions for system A based on the values for B and vice-versa.

Whilst double Q-learning can reduce the time spend in unprofitable states, it can also converge more slowly (though there are analyses for the linear case that suggest that this can be overcome by doubling the learning rate and taking the final
(Q_{A}(t)+Q_{B}(t))/2
as the estimate of the Q-value
[
3
]
). Other approaches similarly attempt to reduce variance in the estimation of the
Q_{t}
for example by calculating a running average, see
[
4
]
. However, as far as we are aware, all previous works do not replace the max operation, which can also be considered to be part of the reason for the over-estimation in Q-learning. Our modest contribution is an approach that replaces the max operation with a smoother average operation.

2
Smoothed Q-learning

We define Smoothed Q-learning as

\tilde{Q}_{t+1}(s_{t},a_{t})=\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})+\alpha_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})\left({r_{t}+\gamma\sum_{a}q_{t}(a|s_{t+1})\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})}\right)

(3)

where
q_{t}(a|s_{t+1})
is a distribution. When
q_{t}(a|s_{t+1})
places all its mass in the state
a
that maximises
\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)
, then this update is equivalent to standard Q-learning; otherwise it ‘smooths’ the delta distribution and places mass in more than the most likely state. Based on the simple fact that the average is less than the maximum, we have

\sum_{a}q_{t}(a|s_{t+1})\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)\leq\max_{a}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)

(4)

This means that the single step update of smoothed Q-learning will give a lower update value to the maximal state than standard Q-learning, potentially therefore avoiding overconfident estimates of the Q-value.

The update equation(
3
) is reminiscent of the SARSA algorithm
[
5
,
1
]
– an on-policy algorithm to estimate the value of a policy (the actions taken by the system follow a defined policy
\pi(a|s)
)

Q^{\pi}_{t+1}(s_{t},a_{t})=Q^{\pi}_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})+\alpha_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})\left({r_{t}+\gamma Q^{\pi}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-Q^{\pi}_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})}\right)

(5)

Whilst SARSA is an on-policy approach that converges to the value of the policy
\pi
, smoothed Q-learning is an off-policy approach that converges to the optimal value (provided we choose a
q_{t}
that increasingly concentrates on the most likely action – see below).

2.1
Smoothing Choices

We want to define
q_{t}(a|s^{\prime})
that, as
t
increases,
q_{t}
places increasing mass on the maximum value state
a^{*}=\arg\max_{a}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s^{\prime},a)
. That is, we define distributions
q_{t}(a|s^{\prime})
that place mass
1-\delta_{t}
in the maximal state
a^{*}
with
\delta_{t}
decreasing to zero as
t
increases. Provided we choose a smoothing distribution that increasingly concentrates its mass on the maximal state, then smoothed Q-learning converges to the optimal value (see section(
A
) for a proof sketch). There are many smoothing choices and we describe here some natural ones:

Softmax

The distribution

q_{t}(a|s_{t+1})\propto e^{\beta_{t}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)}

(6)

sharpens around the maximal state as
\beta_{t}
increases with time.

Clipped Max

Another simple choice is to place
1-\delta_{t}
mass in the most likely state and spread the remaining
\delta_{t}
mass uniformly in the non-maximal states, decreasing
\delta_{t}
towards zero as
t
increases (assuming there are
A
actions in state
s_{t+1}
):

q_{t}(a^{*}|s_{t+1})=1-\delta_{t},\hskip 28.45274ptq_{t}(b|s_{t+1})=\delta_{t}/(A-1)

(7)

where
a^{*}=\underset{a}{\mathrm{argmax}\ }{\tilde{Q}(s_{t+1},a)}
, and
b
is any state other than
a^{*}
.

Other choices which take into account the number of times a state-action pair has been visited would also be worthy of future investigation.

Demonstration

The experiment comes from the maximisation bias example in
[

]
, see figure(

). The starting state is
A
, with actions
Left
(goes to state
B
) and
Right
(terminates). Both actions have zero reward. From
B
there are 8 actions, each of which takes the model to the terminal state, each action having reward sampled from a Gaussian distribution with mean -0.1 and variance 1. This example is challenging for standard Q-learning since, because of the high variance in the reward, going
Left
from
A
will likely result in some positive rewards (whereas the optimal action is always to go
Right

A

\epsilon=0.1

\gamma=0.99

\beta_{t}=0.1+0.1(t-1)

\delta_{t}=\exp(-0.02t)
.

The example shows that double Q-learning avoids some of the issues with Q-learning and pays less attention to initial over-estimates of the Q value (due to unhelpful random rewards that confuse the model to thinking that going left is a good strategy).

4
Conclusion

The overestimation issue of standard Q-learning is potentially addressable by replacing the max operation in the standard Q-learning by an average. We showed that this converges to the optimal Q-value and that the method has some empirical support based on a standard toy problem from the literature.

Appendix A
Convergence

We here sketch a proof that makes use of the following result (see
[
6
]
for a more formal statement of the result) and follows the line of thought in
[
7
]
:

Theorem 1

The random process
\left\{{\Delta_{t}}\right\}
taking value in
R
and defined as

\Delta_{t+1}(x)=(1-\alpha_{t}(x))\Delta_{t}(x)+\alpha_{t}(x)F_{t}(x)

(8)

converges to 0 with probability 1 under the following assumptions:

•

0\leq\alpha_{t}\leq 1,\hskip 7.11317pt\sum_{t}\alpha_{t}(x)=\infty,\hskip 7.11317pt\sum_{t}\alpha_{t}^{2}(x)<\infty
;

•

\left|\!\left|{\mathbb{E}\left[{F_{t}(x)}\right]}\right|\!\right|_{W}\leq\kappa\left|\!\left|{\Delta_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{W}+c_{t}
,
\kappa\in[0,1)
and
c_{t}
convergences to 0 with probability 1;

•

var(F_{t}(x))\leq C(1+\left|\!\left|{\Delta_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{W})^{2}
,
C>0

where
\left|\!\left|{\Delta}\right|\!\right|_{W}
denotes a weighted max norm.

We are interested convergence of
\tilde{Q}_{t}
towards the optimal value
Q_{*}
and therefore define

\Delta_{t}=\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})-Q_{*}(s_{t},a_{t})

(9)

It is convenient to write the smoothed update as

\tilde{Q}_{t+1}(s_{t},a_{t})=\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})+\alpha_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})\left({r_{t}+\gamma\left\langle{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{a}-\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})}\right)

(10)

where
\left\langle{f(x)}\right\rangle_{x}
means expectation of the function
f(x)
with respect to the distribution of
x
. Using the smoothed update, we can write

\displaystyle\Delta_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\tilde{Q}_{t+1}(s_{t},a_{t})-Q_{*}(s_{t},a_{t})

(11)

\displaystyle=\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})+\alpha_{t}\left({r_{t}+\gamma\left\langle{\tilde{Q}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{a}-\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})}\right)-Q_{*}(s_{t},a_{t})

(12)

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{t})\Delta_{t}+\alpha_{t}\left({r_{t}+\gamma\left\langle{\tilde{Q}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{a}-Q_{*}(s_{t},a_{t})}\right)

(13)

In terms of theorem(
1
), we therefore define

F_{t}\equiv r_{t}+\gamma\sum_{a}q_{t}(a|s_{t+1})\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-Q_{*}(s_{t},a_{t})

(14)

For convergence, we need to bound the norm of the expected value of
F_{t}
. Using the fixed-point of the optimal value
Q_{*}
we can write

\frac{1}{\gamma}\mathbb{E}\left[{F_{t}}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{p(s_{t+1}|s_{t},a_{t})}\left[{\sum_{a}q_{t}(a|s_{t+1})\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-\max_{a}Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right]

(15)

Defining

D_{t}=\sum_{a}q_{t}(a|s_{t+1})\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-\max_{a}Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)

(16)

we can form the bound

\frac{1}{\gamma}\left|\!\left|{\mathbb{E}\left[{F_{t}}\right]}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}=\left|\!\left|{\mathbb{E}\left[{D_{t}}\right]}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{\left|\!\left|{D_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}}\right]=\left|\!\left|{D_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}

(17)

which means that if we can bound
\left|\!\left|{D_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}
appropriately, the mean criterion will be satisfied.

Assuming that
q_{t}
places
(1-\delta_{t})
mass in the maximal state of
\tilde{Q}
we can write

\displaystyle D_{t}

\displaystyle=\max_{a}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-\max_{a}Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)-\delta_{t}\max_{a}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)+\sum_{b\neq a}q_{t}(b|s_{t+1})\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},b)

(18)

and

\displaystyle\left|\!\left|{D_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\!\left|{\max_{a}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-\max_{a}Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+\left|\!\left|{\delta_{t}\max_{a}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-\sum_{b\neq a}q_{t}(b|s_{t+1})\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},b)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}{}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{a}\left|\!\left|{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+\left|\!\left|{\delta_{t}\max_{a}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-\sum_{b\neq a}q_{t}(b|s_{t+1})\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},b)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}{}

\displaystyle=\left|\!\left|{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+\left|\!\left|{\delta_{t}\max_{a}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-\sum_{b\neq a}q_{t}(b|s_{t+1})\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},b)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}{}

\displaystyle=\left|\!\left|{\Delta_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+\left|\!\left|{\delta_{t}\max_{a}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-\sum_{b\neq a}q_{t}(b|s_{t+1})\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},b)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}{}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\!\left|{\Delta_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+\left|\!\left|{\delta_{t}\max_{a}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-\delta_{t}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},b{{}_{-}})}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}{}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\!\left|{\Delta_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+\delta_{t}\left({\left|\!\left|{\max_{a}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+\left|\!\left|{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},b_{-})}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}}\right)

(19)

where
b_{-}=\arg\min_{b\neq a}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},b)
and the penultimate line follows from the fact that only a maximum of
\delta_{t}
mass can be placed in the minimal state
b_{-}
(since
(1-\delta_{t})
mass is placed in state
a^{*}
). Putting this together we have

\left|\!\left|{\mathbb{E}\left[{F_{t}}\right]}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}\leq\gamma\left|\!\left|{\Delta_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+\gamma\delta_{t}\left({\left|\!\left|{\max_{a}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+\left|\!\left|{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},b)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}}\right)

(20)

where

c_{t}\equiv{\gamma}\delta_{t}\left({\left|\!\left|{\max_{a}\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+\left|\!\left|{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},b)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}}\right)

(21)

Since the
\tilde{Q}_{t}
are bounded (which follows directly from iterating the
\tilde{Q}
update), then the term
c_{t}
converges to zero with probability 1, provided
\delta_{t}
converges to 0 with probability 1. The mean criterion is therefore satisfied.

For the variance criterion, since the rewards are bounded,
\tilde{Q}_{t}
and
\Delta_{t}
are also bounded. This means that the variance is bounded and that the criterion must therefore be satisfied for sufficiently large
C
. A more detailed argument is to first write

F_{t}-\mathbb{E}\left[{F_{t}}\right]=r_{t}-\bar{r}(s_{t},a_{t})+\gamma\sum_{a}q_{t}(a|s_{t+1})\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-\gamma\sum_{s_{t+1}}p(s_{t+1}|s_{t},a_{t})\sum_{a}q_{t}(a|s_{t+1})\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)

(22)

where
\bar{r}(s_{t},a_{t})=\sum_{s^{\prime}}p(s^{\prime}|s_{t},a_{t})r(s^{\prime}|s_{t},a_{t})
.

We can write equation(
22
) as

\displaystyle\Delta F_{t}

\displaystyle=r_{t}-\left\langle{r_{t}}\right\rangle_{s_{t+1}}+\gamma\left({\left\langle{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{a}-\left\langle{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{s_{t+1},a}}\right)

(23)

\displaystyle=\Delta r_{t}+\gamma\left({\left\langle{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{a}-\left\langle{Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{a}+\left\langle{Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{a}-\left\langle{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{s_{t+1},a}}\right)

(24)

\displaystyle=\Delta r_{t}+\gamma\left\langle{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{a}-\gamma\left\langle{\left\langle{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{s_{t+1}}-Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{a}

(25)

We can bound the variance using

var(F)=\left\langle{\Delta F_{t}^{2}}\right\rangle=\left|\!\left|{\left\langle{\Delta F_{t}^{2}}\right\rangle}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}\leq\left\langle{\left|\!\left|{\Delta F_{t}^{2}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}}\right\rangle=\left|\!\left|{\Delta F_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}^{2}

(26)

and use the triangle inequality,

\left|\!\left|{\Delta F_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}\leq\left|\!\left|{\Delta r_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+\gamma\left|\!\left|{\left\langle{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)-Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{a}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+\gamma\left|\!\left|{\left\langle{\left\langle{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{s_{t+1}}-Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{a}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}

(27)

and using
\left|\!\left|{\left\langle{x}\right\rangle}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}\leq\left|\!\left|{x}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\left|\!\left|{\Delta F_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\!\left|{\Delta r_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+\gamma\left|\!\left|{\Delta_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+\gamma\left|\!\left|{\left\langle{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{s_{t+1}}-Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}

(28)

We now write

\displaystyle\left|\!\left|{\left\langle{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{s_{t+1}}-Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}

(29)

\displaystyle=\left|\!\left|{\left\langle{\tilde{Q}_{t}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{s_{t+1}}-\left\langle{Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{s_{t+1}}+\left\langle{Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{s_{t+1}}-Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}

(30)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\!\left|{\Delta_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+\left|\!\left|{\left\langle{Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right\rangle_{s_{t+1}}-Q_{*}(s_{t+1},a)}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}

(31)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\!\left|{\Delta_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}+B

(32)

for some constant
B_{1}
since the optimal
Q_{*}
is bounded (for
\gamma<1
and bounded rewards). Hence, since the rewards are bounded, there exists
B
such that

\left|\!\left|{\Delta F_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}\leq 2\gamma B+2\gamma\left|\!\left|{\Delta_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{\infty}=2\gamma B(1+\left|\!\left|{\Delta_{t}}\right|\!\right|_{W})

(33)

for suitably defined weighted max norm. This shows that the variance condition is satisfied.

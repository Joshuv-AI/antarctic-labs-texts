Real toric manifolds associated with chordal nestohedra Thanks: This work was supported by the National Research Foundation of Korea Grant funded by the Korean Government (NRF-2021R1A6A1A10044950).
Suyoung Choi, Younghan Yoon

Abstract
. This paper investigates the rational Betti numbers of real toric manifolds associated with chordal nestohedra. We consider the poset topology of a specific poset induced from a chordal building set, and show its EL-shellability. Based on this, we present an explicit description using alternating ℬ \mathcal{B} -permutations for a chordal building set ℬ \mathcal{B} , transforming the computing Betti numbers into a counting problem. This approach allows us to compute the a a -number of a finite simple graph through permutation counting when the graph is chordal. In addition, we provide detailed computations for specific cases such as real Hochschild varieties corresponding to Hochschild polytopes.

1.
Introduction

A
toric variety

X
is a normal complex algebraic variety containing the algebraic torus
(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{O\})^{n}
on itself extends to an action on
X
.
A smooth compact toric variety
X
is called a
toric manifold
, and the real locus
X^{\mathbb{R}}
of
X
, that is, the fixed point set of
X
by the canonical involution induced from a complex conjugation, is called a
real toric manifold
.
By the fundamental theorem of toric geometry, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the class of
n
-dimensional toric manifolds and the class of non-singular complete fans in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
It is well known
[
16
]
that a toric variety is projective if and only if its associated fan is the normal fan of some simple polytope.
An
n
-dimensional simple polytope is said to be
Delzant
if for each vertex
v
the outward normal vectors of the facets containing
v
can be chosen so that they form an integral basis for
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Since the normal fan of a Delzant polytope
P
is a non-singular complete fan,
P
induces the projective toric manifold and the corresponding real toric manifold.

Our investigation centers on some family of Delzant polytopes known as
nestohedra
, which has been well studied in various literature, such as
[
35
]
,
[
36
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
20
]
, and
[
8
]
.
For a finite set
S
of natural numbers, a
building set
on
S
is a collection of nonempty subsets of
S
which includes every singleton of
S
and satisfies the following condition:

I,J\in\mathcal{B}\text{ and }I\cap J\neq\emptyset\Rightarrow I\cup J\in\mathcal{B}.

The notion of building sets is defined in
[
18
]
with respect to a hyperplane arrangement of vector spaces.
An inclusion-maximal subset in
\mathcal{B}
is called a
connected component
of
\mathcal{B}
.
If there is a unique connected component of
\mathcal{B}
, a building set
\mathcal{B}
is said to be
connected
.

A
nestohedron

P_{\mathcal{B}}
associated with
\mathcal{B}
is defined as the Minkowski sum of simplices

P_{\mathcal{B}}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{B}}{\conv(I)},

where
\conv(I)
is the convex hull of the
i
th coordinate vectors for all
i\in I
.

Since
P_{\mathcal{B}}
is Delzant as shown in
[
35
]
, it induces a projective toric manifold.
We refer to this toric manifold as the
toric manifold associated with a building set
\mathcal{B}
and denoted it by
X_{\mathcal{B}}
, and its corresponding real toric manifold by
X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}
.
For a connected building set
\mathcal{B}
on
S
of cardinality
n+1
, the associated toric manifold
X_{\mathcal{B}}
is of dimension
n
.
By
[
36
, Remark 6.7]
, if
\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{\ell}
are the connected components of a building set
\mathcal{B}
, then
P_{\mathcal{B}}
is isomorphic to the direct product

P_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}\times\cdots\times P_{\mathcal{B}_{\ell}}.

Moreover, by
[
16
, Theorem 2.4.7]
, the associated toric manifold
X_{\mathcal{B}}
is isomorphic to the product
X_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}\times\cdots\times X_{\mathcal{B}_{\ell}}
as projective toric manifolds.
Hence, to study toric manifolds or real toric manifolds associated with building sets, it is sufficient to consider the case when the building set is connected.

As established in
[
17
]
, for a building set
\mathcal{B}
, the rational Betti numbers of
X_{\mathcal{B}}
and the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-Betti numbers of
X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}
are completely determined from the numbers of faces of
P_{\mathcal{B}}
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is the field of order
2
.
Particularly, in the case where
\mathcal{B}
is a
chordal building set
on
S
, that is,

I=\{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\}\in\mathcal{B}\Rightarrow\{i_{s},\ldots,i_{r}\}\in\mathcal{B}\text{ for each $1<s<r$},

there is an explicit description, established in
[
36
]
, of the face numbers of
P_{\mathcal{B}}
in terms of permutations on
S
.
Note that this description confirms that the Gal’s conjecture
[
22
]
is true for chordal nestohedra.
To discuss more details, we briefly introduce some notions for a building set
\mathcal{B}
on
S
:

•

For any subset
I
of
S
,
\mathcal{B}|_{I}=\{J\in\mathcal{B}\colon J\subset I\}
is a
restricted building set
of
\mathcal{B}
to
I
.

•

A (one-line notation) permutation
x=(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{k})
on
S
is a
\mathcal{B}
-permutation
if, for each
1\leq i\leq k
, there is
J\in\mathcal{B}|_{\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i}\}}
such that
J
contains both

x_{i}
and
\max{\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i}\}}
.

From now on in this paper, for a topological space
M
,
\beta_{k}(M)
will simply be referred to as the
k
th rational Betti number of
M
.
From
[
17
]
and
[
36
]
, for a connected chordal building set
\mathcal{B}
on
[n+1]=\{1,2,\ldots,n+1\}
, the
k
th Betti numbers of
X_{\mathcal{B}}
is

(1.1)

\beta_{k}(X_{\mathcal{B}})=\begin{cases}\#\text{$\mathcal{B}$-permutations with $\frac{k}{2}$ descents},&\mbox{if $0\leq k\leq 2n$ is even,}\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

where a
descent
of a permutation
(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n})
on
[n+1]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
is
1\leq i<n
where
x_{i}>x_{i+1}
.

It is also natural to consider Betti number formulas for real toric manifold
X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}
associated with
\mathcal{B}
.
Although the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-Betti numbers of
X_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathbb{R}}
can also be computed in the same manner as in (
1.1
),
the computation of its rational Betti numbers is significantly more complicated in general.

One typical and important example of a chordal building set is the maximal building set
\mathcal{B}
on
[n+1]
, that is,
\mathcal{B}
is the set of nonempty subsets of
[n+1]
.
The corresponding polytope is known as a
permutohedron
and has dimension
n
.
Likewise, the associated toric variety, having a complex dimension of
n
, is referred to as the
Coxeter variety
of type
A_{n}
, denoted by
X_{A_{n}}
.
In many literature, it is also known as a
permutohedral variety
.
Furthermore, its real counterpart
X_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathbb{R}}
of dimension
n
is called a
real Coxeter variety

X_{A_{n}}^{\mathbb{R}}
of type
A_{n}
or a
real permutohedral variety
.
In his paper
[
25
]
, Henderson employed a geometric approach to compute the
k
th Betti numbers of
X_{A_{n}}^{\mathbb{R}}
, which can be expressed as follows:

\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{A_{n}})=\binom{n+1}{2k}a_{2k},

where
a_{2k}
denotes the
2k
th Euler zigzag number (A000111 in
[
37
]
), signifying the number of alternating permutations of length
2k
.
Later, this formula is revisited by several studies including
[
42
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
7
]
, which explored poset topologies based on the formula established in
[
43
]
and
[
12
]
.
Additionally, the multiplicative structure of the cohomology ring of
X_{A_{n}}^{\mathbb{R}}
has been completely described in terms of alternating permutations in our previous work
[
13
]
.

One observes that a
\mathcal{B}
-permutation for the
maximal building set
\mathcal{B}
on
[n+1]
is an ordinary permutation.
In other words, in this case, the Betti number of
X_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathbb{R}}=X_{A_{n}}^{\mathbb{R}}
can be described using alternating (
\mathcal{B}
-)permutations, whereas that of
X_{\mathcal{B}}=X_{A_{n}}
is describable using permutations as in (
1.1
).
This observation motivates us to investigate whether the Betti numbers of
X_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathbb{R}}
for a general chordal building set
\mathcal{B}
can similarly be described by alternating
\mathcal{B}
-permutations, where an
alternating
\mathcal{B}
-permutation
is a
\mathcal{B}
-permutation
x=(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n})
such that
x_{1}>x_{2}<x_{3}>\cdots
.

Now, we introduce the main result of this paper, and the proof will be given in Section
5
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a connected chordal building set on
[n+1]=\{1,2,\ldots,n+1\}
.
The
k
th Betti number of
X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is

\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}})=\sum_{I\in{[n+1]\choose 2k}}{\#\text{alternating $\mathcal{B}|_{I}$-permutations}}.

It should be noted that the condition of the chordality of a building set is necessary.
An example introduced in Section
6.5
demonstrates that Theorem
1.1
does not hold for an arbitrary building set even for small dimensions
n
.

To prove the main theorem, for a chordal building set
\mathcal{B}
on
[n+1]
, we need to consider the specific poset
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
consisting of all subsets
I
of
[n+1]
such that
\mathcal{B}|_{I}
does not admit odd components.
The key part is showing that the poset
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is EL-shellable in Section
3
.
EL-shellability has been extensively studied in the literature, such as in
[
29
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
26
]
, and
[
27
]
.
The authors wish to emphasize that this provides a non-trivial example of naturally occurring EL-shellable complexes.
Due to EL-shellability of
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
, in Section
4
, we deal with the poset topology of
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
in terms of alternating
\mathcal{B}
-permutations.
The full proof of Theorem
1.1
will be given in Section
5
.

A graph is said to be

if every cycle containing four or more vertices has a chord, which is an edge that connects two nonadjacent vertices of the cycle.
According to
[
36
]
, a finite simple graph

(refer Section

\mathcal{B}(G)

For a chordal graph

1.1
provides a combinatorial interpretation of the

\mathcal{B}(G)
-permutations.
It is indeed beneficial because the computation of

In particular, the results of Corollary

are equal to the

-numbers of

,

, and

Corollary 1.2
.

(1)

For a complete graph
K_{n+1}
having
n+1
vertices,

\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}(K_{n+1})})=\sum_{I\in{[n+1]\choose 2k}}\#\text{alternating permutations on $I$}.

(2)

For a path graph
G=P_{n+1}
having
n+1
vertices labeled consecutively as
1,\ldots,n+1
,

\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}(P_{n+1})})=\sum_{I\in{[n+1]\choose 2k}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq r_{I}}\#\text{$312$-avoiding alternating permutations on $I_{i}$},

where the induced subgraph
G|_{I}
consists of the even order components
G|_{I_{1}},\ldots,G|_{I_{r_{I}}}
.

(3)

For a star graph
K_{1,n}
having
n+1
vertices with the central vertex labeled as
n+1
,

\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}(P_{n+1})})=\sum_{I\in{[n]\choose 2k-1}}\#\{\text{alternating permutations on $I\cup\{n+1\}$, whose $1$st entry is $n+1$}\}.

In particular, when
n+1=2k
,
\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}(P_{2k})})
, which is known to be the
k
th Catalan number
[
11
]
, represents the number of
312
-avoiding alternating permutations on
[n+1]=[2k]
.
This provides an alternative proof that the number of
312
-avoiding alternating permutations of length
2k
is equal to the
k
th Catalan number
[
40
]
.

Hochschild polytopes, including Stanley-Pitman polytopes, are chordal nestohedra, but most of them are not graph associahedra, as will be shown in Propositions
2.5
and
2.7
.
For each pair
(m,n)
of non-negative integers, the
(m,n)
-Hochschild polytope
\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(m,n)
is defined as the nestohedron associated with the building set
\mathcal{B}
on a set
[m+n]=\{1,2,\ldots,m+n\}
, where the building set
\mathcal{B}
consists of
I\subset[m+n]
that satisfy the following condition:

\left|I\right|\geq 2\Rightarrow I\cap[m+1,m+n]\text{ is either }\emptyset\text{ or }[m+r,m+n]\text{ for }1\leq r\leq n,

where
[i,j]\coloneqq\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\colon i\leq\ell\leq j\}
for integers
i\leq j
.
See Section
2.3
for more details.

Although the complex and real toric manifolds corresponding to a Hochschild polytope appear interesting, the authors are not aware of any existing topological and algebraic geometrical studies on them as of the writing of this paper.
It would be appropriate to refer to such complex and real toric manifolds as the
Hochschild
and
real Hochschild variety
, respectively.
Due to our main theorem, we are able to compute the Betti numbers of real Hochschild varieties.

For non-negative integers
s
and
r
, consider the set of alternating permutations
(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{s+r})
on
[s+r]
such that

s+r\geq x_{i}>x_{j}\geq s+1\Rightarrow i<j,

which is denoted by
\mathrm{Alt}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(s,r)}
.

Corollary 1.3
.

For each non-negative integers
m
and
n
, the
k
th Betti numbers of a real Hochschild variety
X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(m,n)}
are

\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(m,n)})=\sum_{s+r=2k}{m\choose s}\left|\mathrm{Alt}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(s,r)}\right|,

where
s
and
r
are non-negative integers.

Moreover, if
n\geq m+2
,

\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(m,n)})=\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(m,m+2)}),

for all
k\geq 0
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Real toric manifolds associated with nestohedra

Let us consider a complex
n
-dimensional toric manifold, denoted by
X
, and its associated real toric manifold, denoted by
X^{\mathbb{R}}
.
By the fundamental theorem of toric geometry,
X
corresponds to a non-singular complete fan
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Since
\Sigma
is a simplicial fan, one can replace it with the pair
(K,\lambda)
obtained by

(1)

a simplicial complex
K
on the set
V
of rays in
\Sigma
such that a
(k-1)
-simplex of
K
corresponds to a
k
-dimensional cone of
\Sigma
, and

(2)

a linear map
\lambda
from
V
to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, called a
characteristic map
of
X
, such that the image
\lambda(v)
is the primitive vector in the direction of
v
for each
v\in V
.

Then
X
is completely determined by
(K,\lambda)
.
In a similarly way,
X^{\mathbb{R}}
is completely determined by a pair
(K,\lambda^{\mathbb{R}})
, where
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}
is the map, called a
mod
2
characteristic map
of
X^{\mathbb{R}}
, defined by the composition
V\xrightarrow{\lambda}\mathbb{Z}^{n}\xrightarrow{\text{mod}2}\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
.
One may regard
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}
by an
n\times m

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-matrix, where each column is
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}(v)
for
v\in V
, and the row space of this matrix is denoted by
\row\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}
.
For each element
\omega\in\row\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}
,
\omega
can be regarded as a subset of
V
via the standard correlation between the power set of
V
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
.
Hence,
\omega
is associated with the induced subcomplex
K_{\omega}
of
K
with respect to
\omega
.

Remark that the Betti numbers of
X^{\mathbb{R}}
is determined by
(K,\lambda^{\mathbb{R}})
in accordance with the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
43
]
,
[
12
]
)
.

Let
X^{\mathbb{R}}
be the real toric manifold associated with
(K,\lambda^{\mathbb{R}})
.
Then the
k
th rational Betti number is given by

\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}})=\sum_{\omega\in\row\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}}\widetilde{\beta}_{k-1}(K_{\omega}),

where
K_{\omega}
is the induced subcomplex of
K
with respect to
\omega
and
\widetilde{\beta}_{k-1}
denotes the
(k-1)
th reduced Betti number.

For a finite set
S
of natural numbers, a finite collection
\mathcal{B}
of nonempty subsets of
S
is a
building set
on
S
if

(1)

\{i\}\in\mathcal{B}
for
i\in S
, and

(2)

if
I,J\in\mathcal{B}
and
I\cap J\neq\emptyset
, then
I\cup J\in\mathcal{B}
.

An inclusion-maximal subset in
\mathcal{B}
is called a
connected component
of
\mathcal{B}
, and we call
\mathcal{B}
a
connected building set
if
S\in\mathcal{B}
.
For a building set
\mathcal{B}
on
S
with
I\subset S
, the
restricted building set

\mathcal{B}|_{I}
of
\mathcal{B}
to
I
is
\{J\in\mathcal{B}\colon J\subset I\}
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a connected building set on
[n+1]=\{1,\ldots,n+1\}
.
The
nestohedron

P_{\mathcal{B}}
with respect to
\mathcal{B}
is defined as the Minkowski sum of simplices

P_{\mathcal{B}}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{B}}{\conv(I)},

where
\conv(I)
is the convex hull of the
i
th coordinate vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
for all
i\in I
.
Denote by
K_{\mathcal{B}}
the face structure of
P_{\mathcal{B}}
with reverse inclusion.
Then, since each facet of
P_{\mathcal{B}}
can be identified with an element of
\mathcal{B}\setminus\{[n+1]\}
,
K_{\mathcal{B}}
can be regarded as a simplicial complex on
\mathcal{B}\setminus\{[n+1]\}
.
A subset
N\subseteq\mathcal{B}\setminus\{[n+1]\}
is called a
nested set
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

For each
I,J\in N
one has either
I\subseteq J
,
J\subseteq I
, or
I\cap J=\emptyset
.

(2)

For any collection of
k\geq 2
disjoint subsets
I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\in N
, the union
I_{1}\cup\cdots\cup I_{k}\notin\mathcal{B}
.

According to
[
35
, Theorem 7.4]
,
K_{\mathcal{B}}
is isomorphic to the simplicial complex on
\mathcal{B}\setminus\{[n+1]\}
which is defined by the set of all nested sets of
\mathcal{B}
, known as a
nested set complex
, as abstract simplicial complexes.

In addition, since the dimension of
P_{\mathcal{B}}
is
n
, one can define maps
\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}\colon\mathcal{B}\setminus\{[n+1]\}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathbb{R}}\colon\mathcal{B}\setminus\{[n+1]\}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
by
\lambda(I)
is the primitive vector in the direction of the outward normal vector of the facet corresponding to
I
, and
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}(I)\equiv\lambda(I)\pmod{2}
for all
I\in\mathcal{B}\setminus\{[n+1]\}
.
More precisely,
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is given by

\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}(I)=\sum_{i\in I}e_{i},

where
e_{i}
is the
i
th standard vector of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{2}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
e_{n+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}
.

By
[
35
, Proposition 7.10]
, the pair
(K_{\mathcal{B}},\lambda_{\mathcal{B}})
determines the toric manifold, denoted by
X_{\mathcal{B}}
, and the pair
(K_{\mathcal{B}},\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}})
determines the real toric manifold, denoted by
X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}
.
The main purpose of this paper is to compute the rational Betti numbers of
X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}
using Theorem
2.1
.

Let
\omega_{i}
be the element of
\row\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathbb{R}}
corresponding to the
i
th row of
\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathbb{R}}
as a matrix for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Then, each element
\omega=\omega_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\omega_{i_{k}}
of
\row\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathbb{R}}
can be identified with a subset
I_{\omega}\subset[n+1]
that has an even cardinality as follows:

(2.1)

I_{\omega}=\begin{cases}\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\},&\mbox{if }k\mbox{ is even}\\
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k},n+1\},&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For an even cardinality subset
I\subset[n+1]
, denote by
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
the full subcomplex of
K_{\mathcal{B}}
induced by all vertices

Let

\max\mathcal{C}_{1}>\max\mathcal{C}_{2}>\cdots>\max\mathcal{C}_{r}

\mathcal{B}|_{I}

\mathcal{B}|_{J}

\mathcal{C}_{i}\setminus I

For each

where
k_{0}=0
and
\alpha_{2k_{0}+\cdots+2k_{i-1}+s}>\alpha_{2k_{0}+\cdots+2k_{i-1}+t}

Let
1\leq\ell\leq N
be given.
Define
I_{\ell}=I\cup\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{2\ell}\}
and the index

\mathsf{C}(I_{\ell};I_{\ell-1})\subset\mathcal{C}_{s_{\ell}}
.
Then one can express

(3.1)

where
\mathcal{C}_{0}=\emptyset
.
By the chordality of the building set
\mathcal{B}
, the subset
\{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s_{\ell}}\colon\alpha\geq\alpha_{2\ell}\}
of
\mathcal{C}_{s_{\ell}}
is also an element of
\mathcal{B}
,
and hence,
I_{\ell}
is an element of
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
.
Moreover, combining the fact that
\max\mathcal{C}_{s_{\ell}}\in\{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s_{\ell}}\colon\alpha\geq\alpha_{2\ell}\}
with (
3.1
), we have that
\max\mathcal{C}_{s_{\ell}}
is contained in
I_{\ell}
.
In conclusion,

\mu_{\mathcal{B}}(I_{\ell-1},I_{\ell})=(\max\mathsf{C}(I_{\ell};I_{\ell-1}),(\alpha_{2\ell-1},\alpha_{2\ell}))=(\max\mathcal{C}_{s_{\ell}},(\alpha_{2\ell-1},\alpha_{2\ell})),

where
I_{0}=I
.
Since
s_{\ell}\leq s_{\ell+1}
, we have that
\max\mathcal{C}_{s_{\ell}}\geq\max\mathcal{C}_{s_{\ell+1}}
, and, hence,
\mu_{\mathcal{B}}(I_{\ell-1},I_{\ell})\succ\mu_{\mathcal{B}}(I_{\ell},I_{\ell+1})
.
We define the maximal chain

of the interval

Consider another maximal chain

of

Let

, and

.

.

\max\mathcal{C}_{s_{i}}=\max\mathsf{C}(I_{i};I_{i-1})\leq\max\mathsf{C}(I^{\prime}_{j};I^{\prime}_{j-1})\leq\max\mathcal{C}_{s_{i}},

and, hence,
\max\mathsf{C}(I_{i};I_{i-1})=\max\mathsf{C}(I^{\prime}_{j};I^{\prime}_{j-1})=\max\mathcal{C}_{s_{i}}
.

Now, let us consider two cases where

or
\mathsf{C}(I^{\prime}_{i};I^{\prime}_{i-1})\not\subset\mathcal{C}_{s_{i}}
.

CASE 1.
\mathsf{C}(I^{\prime}_{i};I^{\prime}_{i-1})\subset\mathcal{C}_{s_{i}}
:

In this case,

\max\mathsf{C}(I^{\prime}_{i};I^{\prime}_{i-1})\leq\max\mathcal{C}_{s_{i}}=\max\mathsf{C}(I^{\prime}_{j};I^{\prime}_{j-1}).

If
\mathsf{C}(I^{\prime}_{i};I^{\prime}_{i-1})<\max\mathcal{C}_{s_{i}}

Otherwise,
i
.

\mu_{\mathcal{B}}(c)

A simplicial complex is
pure
if all its maximal simplices has the same dimension.
A finite pure
d
-dimensional simplicial complex is said to be
shellable
if there exists an ordering
F_{1},F_{2},\ldots
of its maximal simplices, say a
shelling
, such that
(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k-1}F_{i})\cap F_{k}
is pure and of dimension
d-1
for each
k\geq 2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}}=\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}\setminus\{\emptyset,I\}
be a partially ordered set whose order is given by inclusion.
Given that
F
is a facet of the order complex
\Delta(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}})
if and only if
F\cup\{\emptyset,I\}
is a facet of
\Delta(\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}})
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a chordal building set on a finite set of cardinality
2k
.
Then,
\Delta(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}})
is shellable, and hence, its geometric realization is homotopy equivalent to a bouquet of
(k-2)
-dimensional spheres; that is,

\Delta(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}})\simeq\bigvee^{\mathsf{a}(\mathcal{B})}S^{k-2},

where
\mathsf{a}(\mathcal{B})
denotes the number of spheres.

Proof.

From
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
, if a bounded graded poset
\mathcal{P}
is EL-shellable, the order complex
\Delta(\mathcal{P})
of
\mathcal{P}
is shellable.
By Lemma
3.4
, the order complex
\Delta(\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}})
is shellable, and so is
\Delta(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}})
.
In addition, it is well known that every shellable complex is Cohen-Macaulay
[
39
]
, and then, its geometric realization is homotopy equivalent to a bouquet of spheres of the same dimension.
Since the length of maximal chains of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}}
is

, the dimension of each sphere is

, as desired.

, which confirms that
\Psi(c)
is an alternating
\mathcal{B}
-permutation.

Now, we consider the case where
\max\mathsf{C}(I_{N-1};I_{N-2})\geq\max\mathsf{C}(I_{N};I_{N-1})
.
Note that
c
does not have a decreasing position at
(N-1)
, that is,

(4.2)

\mu_{\mathcal{B}}(I_{N-2},I_{N-1})\not\succ\mu_{\mathcal{B}}(I_{N-1},I_{N}).

It can be observed that
\mathsf{C}(I_{N-1};I_{N-2})=\mathsf{C}(I_{N};I_{N-1})
and
x^{c}_{\ell}>x^{c}_{2N-1}>x^{c}_{2N}
.
By the chordality of the building set
\mathcal{B}
, we have that

\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathsf{C}(I_{N};I_{N-1})\colon x>x^{c}_{2N}\}

is also an element of
\mathcal{B}
.
Thus,

\{x^{c}_{\ell},x^{c}_{2N-1}\}\subset\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{B}|_{\{x^{c}_{1},\ldots,x^{c}_{2N-1}\}}.

Since
x^{c}_{\ell}
is also
\max\{x^{c}_{1},\ldots,x^{c}_{2N-1}\}
, the condition (C2) holds for
c
.
By (
4.2
),

((x^{c}_{2N-3},x^{c}_{2N-2}),(x^{c}_{2N-1},x^{c}_{2N}))\notin\mathcal{R}_{1},

and hence, the condition (C1) holds for
c
.

By mathematical induction,
\Psi(c)
is an alternating
\mathcal{B}
-permutation for all
c\in\mathcal{G}
.
Since
\Phi(\Psi(c))=c
for
c\in\mathcal{G}
,
\Phi
is surjective, as desired.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a chordal building set.
Then,
\mathsf{a}(\mathcal{B})
is the number of alternating
\mathcal{B}
-permutations.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a chordal building set on
S
of size
2k
.
There is an one-to-one correspondence between
(\ell-1)
-simplices in
\Delta(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}})
and chains of length
\ell
in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}}
for
1\leq\ell\leq k-1
.
Then each
(\ell-1)
-simplex can be regarded as an
\ell
-length chain of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}}
.

Let an
\ell
-length chain
c=(S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{\ell})
of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}}
be given, where
1\leq\ell\leq k
.
For convenience, let
S_{0}\coloneqq\emptyset
and
S_{\ell+1}\coloneqq S
.
For each
1\leq i\leq\ell+1
, according to Lemma
3.4
, there is the unique maximal chain

c^{i}=(S_{i-1}=I^{c}_{k_{0}+\cdots+k_{i-1}},I^{c}_{k_{0}+\cdots+k_{i-1}+1},\ldots,I^{c}_{k_{0}+\cdots+k_{i-1}+k_{i}}=S_{i}),

of the interval
[S_{i-1},S_{i}]
of
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
such that
\mu_{\mathcal{B}}(c^{i})
is decreasing, where
k_{0}=0
, and
2k_{i}=\left|S_{i}\setminus S_{i-1}\right|
.
In conclusion, for each
\ell
-length chain
c
of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}}
, there is the induced maximal chain

\bar{c}\coloneqq(I^{c}_{0},I^{c}_{1},\ldots,I^{c}_{k})

of
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
.

Let us consider a maximal chain
d=(I_{0},I_{1},\ldots,I_{k})
of
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
with
N
decreasing positions, where
1\leq N\leq k-1
.
Define the set

\mathcal{I}_{d}=\{1\leq i\leq k-1\colon\text{ $d$ does not have a decreasing position at $i$.}\}

of indices.
Let
c=(I_{i_{1}},\ldots,I_{i_{\ell}})
be an
\ell
-length chain of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}}
.
The induced maximal chain
\bar{c}
of
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is equal to
d
if and only if

\mathcal{I}_{d}\subset\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{\ell}\}.

Consequently, the number of
\ell
-length chains of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}}
that induces
d
is given by

{N\choose\ell-\left|\mathcal{I}_{d}\right|}={N\choose\ell-(k-1-N)}={N\choose k-1-\ell},

if
k-1-N\leq\ell\leq k-1
, and
0
otherwise.

Let
\mathsf{a}_{N}
denote the number of maximal chains of
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
with
N
decreasing positions for each
0\leq N\leq k-1
.
The total number of
\ell
-length chains of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}}
is

\sum_{N=k-1-\ell}^{k-1}{N\choose k-1-\ell}\mathsf{a}_{N}.

Now, the Euler characteristic
\chi(\Delta(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}}))
of
\Delta(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}})
is

\displaystyle\chi(\Delta(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}{(-1)^{\ell-1}\#\ell\text{-length chains of }}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\sum_{N=k-1-\ell}^{k-1}(-1)^{\ell-1}{N\choose k-1-\ell}\mathsf{a}_{N}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{a}_{k-1}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\sum_{N=k-1-\ell}^{k-1}(-1)^{\ell-1}{N\choose k-1-\ell}\mathsf{a}_{N}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{a}_{k-1}+\sum_{N=0}^{k-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=k-1-N}^{k-1}(-1)^{\ell-1}{N\choose k-1-\ell}\right)\mathsf{a}_{N}.

By the binomial theorem, we have that

\sum_{\ell=k-1-N}^{k-1}(-1)^{\ell-1}{N\choose k-1-\ell}=0

for all
1\leq N\leq k-1
, and, hence,
\chi(\Delta(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}}))=\mathsf{a}_{k-1}+(-1)^{k-2}\mathsf{a}_{0}
.

We note that
\mathsf{a}_{k-1}=1
by Lemma
3.4
.
In addition, by Theorem
3.5
,
\chi(\Delta(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}}))=1+(-1)^{k-2}\mathsf{a}(\mathcal{B})
.
Therefore,
\mathsf{a}(\mathcal{B})=\mathsf{a}_{0}
, that is the number of alternating
\mathcal{B}
-permutations by Lemma
4.3
.

∎

5.
Proof of the main theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
.
For a connected building set
\mathcal{B}
on
[n+1]=\{1,\ldots,n+1\}
, the rational Betti number of
X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is determined by the reduced Betti numbers of
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
for even cardinality subset
I
of
[n+1]
by (
2.2
), where
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
the full subcomplex of
K_{\mathcal{B}}
induced by all vertices
J
, where
\left|J\cap I\right|
is odd.

Let us consider the case when
n+1
is even, and
I=[n+1]
.
We denote by
K_{\mathcal{B}}^{\odd}
(respectively,
K_{\mathcal{B}}^{\even}
) the induced subcomplex of a nested set complex
K_{\mathcal{B}}
whose vertices have odd (respectively, even) cardinality.
In other words,
K_{\mathcal{B}}^{\odd}=(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{[n+1]}
and
K_{\mathcal{B}}^{\even}
is the complement of the simplicial complex of
K_{\mathcal{B}}^{\odd}
in
K_{\mathcal{B}}
.
Since the geometric realization of
K_{\mathcal{B}}
is topologically homeomorphic to the
(2k-2)
-dimensional sphere
S^{2k-2}
, by the Alexander duality,
H_{\ast}(K_{\mathcal{B}}^{\odd})
is completely determined by
H^{\ast}(K_{\mathcal{B}}^{\even})
.
In addition, with the coefficients of (co)homology being in a field
\mathbb{Q}
, by the Universal Coefficient Theorem, we have

(5.1)

\tilde{H}_{i}(K_{\mathcal{B}}^{\odd})\cong\tilde{H}_{2k-3-i}(K_{\mathcal{B}}^{\even})\text{ for all $i\geq 0$}.

Refer
[
31
, Theorem 71.1 and 53.5]
for the Alexander duality and the Universal Coefficient Theorem, respectively.

Lemma 5.1
.

The geometric realizations of
\Delta(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}})
and
K_{\mathcal{B}}^{\even}
are homeomorphic.
In addition, if
\mathcal{B}
is chordal, the
i
th reduced rational Betti numbers
\tilde{\beta}_{i}
of
K_{\mathcal{B}}^{\odd}
are

\tilde{\beta}_{i}(K_{\mathcal{B}}^{\odd})=\begin{cases}\mathsf{a}(\mathcal{B}),&\mbox{if }i=k-1,\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

A proof of the former statement of this lemma, limited to graphical building sets, is provided in
[
11
, Lemma 4.7]
.
However, upon closer inspection, the proof does not actually rely on the condition of being a graphical building set, confirming that the lemma holds for any building sets.
The latter statement follows from the preceding statement, Theorem
3.5
and (
5.1
).
∎

Now, let us consider more general cases.
For any simplicial complex
K
, the
link

\Lk_{K}(v)
of a vertex
v
in
K
is the set of all faces
\sigma\in K
such that
v\notin\sigma
and
\sigma\cup\{v\}\in K
.
It is well known that
\Lk_{K}(v)
is a full subcomplex of
K
.
If
\Lk_{K}(v)
is a topological cone, then
K
is homotopy equivalent to the simplicial complex
K-v\coloneqq\{\sigma\setminus\{v\}\colon\sigma\in K\}
.

A simplicial complex
K
is said to be
flag
if it contains every clique of its
1
-skeleton.
It can be immediately observed that an induced subcomplex of a flag complex is also a flag complex.
For each subset
I
of
[n+1]
with even cardinality, we shall discuss an induced subcomplex
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
in order to compute the Betti numbers of
X_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathbb{R}}
.
If a nested set complex
K_{\mathcal{B}}
is flag, then all induced subcomplexes
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
are also a flag complex.
For each vertex
X
in
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
, a vertex
Y
in
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
is also a vertex in
\Lk_{(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}}(X)
if and only if the pair
(X,Y)
holds the following conditions:

(5.2)

X\subsetneq Y,Y\subsetneq X,\text{ or }X\cup Y\notin\mathcal{B}.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that a nested set complex
K_{\mathcal{B}}
is flag.
For any
I\subset[n+1]
with even cardinality, an induced subcomplex
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
is homotopy equivalent to
K_{\mathcal{B}|_{I}}^{\text{odd}}
.

Proof.

Define the partial order
\trianglelefteq
of the set of vertices of
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
as
X_{1}\trianglelefteq X_{2}
if
X_{1}\cap I\subseteq X_{2}\cap I
for each pair
(X_{1},X_{2})
of vertices in
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
.
Let
\mathcal{V}
be the set of vertices in
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
that are not in
I
, with the understanding that
\mathcal{V}
is nonempty, since the case where
\mathcal{V}=\emptyset
is straightforward.
For each
1\leq i\leq N
, we define
\mathcal{V}_{i}
as the set of minimal elements of

\mathcal{U}_{i}=\mathcal{V}\setminus(\bigcup_{0\leq j\leq i-1}\mathcal{V}_{j})

with respect to the order
\trianglelefteq
, where
\mathcal{V}_{0}=\emptyset
and
N
is a sufficiently large positive integer.

Now, for each
1\leq i\leq N
, let
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}^{i}
denote the simplicial complex obtained by removing from
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
belonging to
\bigcup_{0\leq j\leq i-1}\mathcal{V}_{j}
.
Let
X\in\mathcal{V}_{i}
be a given.
Considering that
\left|X\cap I\right|
is odd, one can choose a connected component
X_{0}
of
\mathcal{B}|_{X\cap I}
with odd cardinality.
For each vertex
Y
of
\Lk_{(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}}(X)
, the pair
(X,Y)
satisfies (
5.2
).
To show that either
Y=X_{0}
or
Y\in\Lk_{(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}}(X_{0})
, we consider (
5.2
) by dividing it into three cases.

(1)

If
X\subsetneq Y
, then
X_{0}\subseteq X\subsetneq Y
which confirms that
Y\in\Lk_{(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}}(X)
.

(2)

Let
Y\subsetneq X
.
If
Y\subseteq I
, since
Y\in\mathcal{B}|_{X\cap I}
and
X_{0}
is a connected component of
\mathcal{B}|_{X\cap I}
, either
Y\subseteq X_{0}
or
Y\cup X_{0}\notin\mathcal{B}
.
If
Y\not\subset I
, then
Y\in\mathcal{V}_{j}
for
j\geq i
.
Since
Y\cap I\subseteq X\cap I
and
X\subset\mathcal{V}_{i}
, we have
Y\cap I=X\cap I
, and then

X_{0}\subseteq X\cap I=Y\cap I\subseteq Y.

Thus,
Y=X_{0}
or
Y\in\Lk_{(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}}(X_{0})
.

(3)

Let
X\cup Y\notin\mathcal{B}
.
If
X_{0}\cup Y\in\mathcal{B}
, since
X_{0}\subset X
, then

X\cup Y=X\cup(X_{0}\cup Y)\in\mathcal{B},

which contradicts that
X\cup Y\notin\mathcal{B}
.
Hence,
X_{0}\cup Y
is not an element of
\mathcal{B}
.
It follows that
Y\in\Lk_{(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}}(X)
.

In conclusion, the link
\Lk_{(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}}(X)
of
X
in
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}^{i}
is a topological cone whose apex is
X_{0}
.
Hence, one can remove each elements of
\mathcal{V}_{i}
from
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}^{i}
, ensuring that its homotopy type remains unchanged.

Therefore, from
\mathcal{V}_{1}
to
\mathcal{V}_{N}
, one can sequentially remove all elements in
\mathcal{V}
from
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
without changing its homotopy type.
Since
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
with all elements of
\mathcal{V}
removed is isomorphic to
K_{\mathcal{B}|_{I}}^{\text{odd}}
, we finish the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
K_{\mathcal{B}}
be a flag nested set complex.
For each subset
I\subset[n+1]
with even cardinality, if
\mathcal{B}|_{I}
has a connected component of odd order, then
K_{\mathcal{B}|_{I}}^{\text{odd}}
is contractible.

Proof.

Choose a connected component
I_{0}
of
\mathcal{B}|_{I}
with odd cardinality.
Let
Y
be a vertex of
K_{\mathcal{B}|_{I}}^{\text{odd}}
.
If
Y\subseteq I_{0}
, then either
Y=I_{0}
or
(I_{0},Y)
satisfies (
5.2
).

Thus, we assume that
Y\not\subset I_{0}
.
Since
I_{0}
is a component of
\mathcal{B}|_{I}
, we have
Y\cup I_{0}\notin\mathcal{B}|_{I}
.
Hence,
(I_{0},Y)
holds (
5.2
), which confirms that
K_{\mathcal{B}|_{I}}^{\text{odd}}
is a topological cone with its apex is
I_{0}
, as desired.
∎

Now, we introduce the main result, restating Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 5.4
.

For a connected chordal building set
\mathcal{B}
on
[n+1]=\{1,2,\ldots,n+1\}
, the
k
th Betti number of
X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is

\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}})=\sum_{I\in{[n+1]\choose 2k}}\mathsf{a}(\mathcal{B}|_{I}),

where
\mathsf{a}(\mathcal{B}|_{I})
is the number of alternating
\mathcal{B}|_{I}
-permutations.

Proof.

From
[
36
, Proposition 9.7]
, if a building set
\mathcal{B}
is chordal, then a nested set complex
K_{\mathcal{B}}
is a flag complex.
Combining (
2.2
) with Lemma
5.2
, we have

\beta_{k}(X_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathbb{R}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n+1]\\
\left|I\right|\text{ is even}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\beta}_{k-1}(K^{\odd}_{\mathcal{B}|_{I}}).

Since every restricted building set of a chordal building set is chordal, for
a subset
I
of
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
with
\left|I\right|=2k
, we have
\tilde{\beta}_{i}(K_{\mathcal{B}|_{I}}^{\text{odd}})=\mathsf{a}(\mathcal{B}|_{I})\cdot\delta_{i,k-1}
for all integers
i
, where
\delta_{i,j}
is the Kronecker delta by Lemma
5.1
.
We have

\sum_{I\in\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}}\widetilde{\beta}_{k-1}(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}\\
\left|I\right|=2k\end{subarray}}\mathsf{a}(\mathcal{B}|_{I}).

Let
I
be a subset of
[n+1]
such that
I\notin\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}
.
By Proposition
4.2
and Lemma
5.3
,
\mathsf{a}(\mathcal{B}|_{I})
and
\tilde{\beta}_{i}(K^{\odd}_{\mathcal{B}|_{I}})
both become equal to
0
for all
i
, respectively.
Consequently, one can write that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\notin\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}\\
\left|I\right|\text{ is even}\end{subarray}}(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}=0=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\notin\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{B}}\\
\left|I\right|=2k\end{subarray}}\mathsf{a}(\mathcal{B}|_{I}).

Therefore,

\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n+1]\\
\left|I\right|\text{ is even}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\beta}_{k-1}(K^{\odd}_{\mathcal{B}|_{I}})=\sum_{\left|I\right|=2k}\mathsf{a}(\mathcal{B}|_{I})

for each integers
k
, where
\mathsf{a}(\mathcal{B}|_{I})
is the number of alternating
\mathcal{B}|_{I}
-permutations by Theorem
4.4
.
∎

1.2
.

6.4.
Hochschild polytopes

In this section, we assume that
(m,n)
is a pair of non-negative integers such that
m+n
is even.
Denote by
\mathrm{Alt}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(m,n)}
the set of alternating permutations
(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{m+n})
on
[m+n]=\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
such that for each
i,j\in[m+n]
;

m+n\geq x_{i}>x_{j}\geq m+1

implies that
i<j
.

Consider a building set
\mathcal{B}_{m,n}
defined in Section
2.3
.

Proposition 6.1
.

For a permutation
x
on
[m+n]
,
x
is an alternating
\mathcal{B}_{m,n}
-permutation if and only if
x\in\mathrm{Alt}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(m,n)}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{m,n}
.
If
x
is an element of
\mathrm{Alt}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(m,n)}
, it can be easily verified that
x
is an alternating
\mathcal{B}
-permutation.
Then we only focus on the part of ‘only if’.

Let
x=(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{m+n})
be an alternating
\mathcal{B}
-permutation.
Assume that
x
is not an element of
\mathrm{Alt}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(m,n)}
.
Then one can choose a pair
(i,j)
of elements of
[m+n]
such that

i<j\text{ and }m+1\leq x_{i}<x_{j}\leq m+n.

Note that the singleton
\{x_{i}\}
is a connected component of the restricted building set
\mathcal{B}|_{\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}\}}
.
Since
x
is a
\mathcal{B}
-permutation, we have that
x_{i}=\max\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}\}
.
If there exists
1\leq i^{\prime}<i
such that
x_{i^{\prime}}>x_{i}
, it follows that

\max\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}\}\geq x_{i^{\prime}}>x_{i}.

which contradicts that
x_{i}=\max\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}\}
.
Thus, we assume that
\max\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}\}=x_{i}
.
Then either
i=1
or
x_{i-1}<x_{i}
.
Considering that
x
is alternating, we have that
x_{i}>x_{i+1}
.
It follows that the singleton
\{x_{i}\}
forms also a connected component of
\mathcal{B}|_{\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i+1}\}}
.
We conclude that
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
are not contained in the same connected component of
\mathcal{B}|_{\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i+1}\}}
, which contradicts
x
is a
\mathcal{B}
-permutation.
∎

For non-negative integers
m
and
n
, by Proposition
6.1
, the set
\mathrm{Alt}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(m,n)}
can be regarded as the set of alternating
\mathcal{B}_{m,n}
-permutations.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
m
and
n
be non-negative integers such that
m+n
is even.
If
n>m+2
, there is no alternating
\mathcal{B}_{m,n}
-permutation.

Proof.

Let us consider an alternating
\mathcal{B}_{m,n}
-permutation
x=(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{m+n})
.
Note that, for each
1\leq i\leq\frac{m+n}{2}
,
x_{2i}
is the maximal element of
\{x_{2i},\ldots,x_{m+n}\}
.
Since
m+n
is even, we have
n\geq m+4
.
By the pigeonhole principle, there exist
1\leq\ell<\frac{m+n}{2}
such that

\{x_{2\ell-1},x_{2\ell}\}\subset[m+1,m+n].

Since
x_{2\ell}>x_{2\ell+1}
, it contradicts the fact that
x
is alternating.
In conclusion, if
n\geq m+2
, there is no alternating
\mathcal{B}_{m,n}
-permutation.
∎

Now, we prove Corollary
1.3
.

Proof for Corollary
1.3
.

Let an integer
0\leq k\leq\frac{m+n}{2}
be given.
For each
1\leq r\leq\min\{2k,n\}
, let
\mathcal{I}_{r}
be the set defined as

\mathcal{I}_{r}=\left\{I\in{[m+n]\choose 2k}\colon I\cap[m+1,m+n]=[m+n-(r-1),m+n]\right\},

and
\mathcal{I}_{0}\coloneqq{[m]\choose 2k}
.
We note that
\left|\mathcal{I}_{r}\right|={m\choose 2k-r}
, and for each
I\in\mathcal{I}_{r}
, the number of alternating
\mathcal{B}_{m,n}|_{I}
-permutations is exactly
\left|\mathrm{Alt}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(s,r)}\right|
, where
s=2k-r
.

For any given
I\in{[m+n]\choose 2k}
, by (
2.3
), the restricted building set
\mathcal{B}_{m,n}|_{I}
has no connected component of odd order if and only if
I\cap[m+1,m+n]
is either the empty set or
[m+r,m+n]
for some
1\leq r\leq n
.
Combining Theorem
1.1
with Proposition
4.2
, we conclude that

\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(m,n)})=\sum_{s+r=2k}{m\choose s}\left|\mathrm{Alt}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(s,r)}\right|,

where
s
and
r
are non-negative integers.

Fix a positive integer
m
, and assume that
n\geq m+2
.
From Lemma
6.2
, the cardinality of the set

\{(s,r)\colon s+r=2k,0\leq s\leq m,0\leq r\leq\min\{n,s+2\}\}

is constant.
For each
n\geq m+2
, we conclude that

\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(m,n)})=\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathds{H}\mathrm{och}(m,m+2)})

for all
k\geq 0
.
∎

6.5.
Cyclohedra

For
n\geq 5
, let us consider a cycle graph
C_{n}
with the vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
.
Let any vertex labeling of
C_{n}
be given.
There are two neighbors
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
of the vertex
1
.
Without loss of generality, assume that
x_{1}>x_{2}
.
Choose a neighbor
x_{3}\neq 1
of the vertex
x_{2}
.

When
I=\{1,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}
, the induced subgraph
C_{n}|_{I}
of
C_{n}
is given by
x_{1}
1
x_{2}
x_{3}

.

Since
1<x_{2}<x_{1}
, by similarly way of Example
4.1
, we find that there are exactly three alternating
\mathcal{B}(C_{n})|_{I}
-permutations.
In contrast, the
a
-number
a(C_{n}|_{I})
of
C_{n}|_{I}
is given by

(\#\text{edges of }C_{n}|_{I})-1

from
[
11
]
, and then
\beta_{1}((K_{\mathcal{B}(C_{n})})_{I})=2
which is different from the number of alternating
\mathcal{B}(C_{n})|_{I}
-permutations.
Note that

is not chordal which implies that
\mathcal{B}(C_{n})
is not a chordal building set for any vertex labeling of

.

7.
Open problems on the nested set complex

In this section, we aim to propose several interesting problems based on our study of the nested set complex.

7.1.
Venustus building sets

Let us briefly summarize all the steps for the main proof.
For a connected chordal building set
\mathcal{B}
on
[n+1]=\{1,\ldots,n+1\}
, we have that

\displaystyle\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n+1]\\
\left|I\right|\text{ is even}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\beta}_{k-1}((K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I})\quad(\text{by~\eqref{Betti_comp} as a variant of Theorem~\ref{Choi-Park2017_torsion}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n+1]\\
\left|I\right|\text{ is even}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\beta}_{k-1}(K_{\mathcal{B}|_{I}}^{\odd})\quad(\text{required the flagness of $K_{\mathcal{B}}$, and by Lemma~\ref{remove}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n+1]\\
\left|I\right|\text{ is even}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\beta}_{\left|I\right|-k-2}(K_{\mathcal{B}|_{I}}^{\even})\quad(\text{by~\eqref{Alex_dual} as the Alexander duality})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n+1]\\
\left|I\right|\text{ is even}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\beta}_{\left|I\right|-k-2}(\Delta(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}|_{I}}))\quad(\text{by Lemma~\ref{geo_real}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in{[n+1]\choose 2k}}a(\mathcal{B}|_{I}))\quad(\text{required the chordality of $\mathcal{B}$, and by Theorem~\ref{thm:shellability_of_K_P_for_chordal}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in{[n+1]\choose 2k}}{\#\text{alternating $\mathcal{B}|_{I}$-permutations}}\quad(\text{by Theorem~\ref{thm:a(G)}}).

The one of the important steps in the above proof is Theorem
3.5
, which concerns the shellability of
\Delta(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{B}|_{I}})
.
Consequently, the reduced homology of each
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
is concentrated in a single degree, with the degree depending only on the size of
I
.
More precisely, if
|I|=2k
, then only
\widetilde{\beta}_{k-1}((K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I})
can be positive, and hence, each
I
contributes
\beta_{k}(X_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathbb{R}})
of degree
k
, which is half the cardinality of
I
.
This phenomenon is advantageous as it simplifies the calculation of Betti numbers for real toric manifolds associated with any chordal building set and allows for a highly structured homology decomposition.
Since this phenomenon does not occur for all building sets, we would like to designate a specific term to those building sets that exhibit this phenomenon.

Definition 7.1
.

A connected building set
\mathcal{B}
on
[n+1]
is said to be
venustus
if, for each
I\in{[n+1]\choose 2k}
, the reduced homology of
(K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I}
is concentrated in the degree
k-1
; that is,

\widetilde{\beta}_{i-1}((K_{\mathcal{B}})_{I})=0

for all
i\neq k
.

As mentioned in Section
2.2
, the class of venustus building sets contains not only chordal building sets but also graphical ones, as illustrated in Figure
6
.

Problem 7.2
.

Characterize venustus building sets.

It would be natural to extend this concept to the class of all real toric manifolds.
Let
X^{\mathbb{R}}
be an
n
-dimensional real toric manifold.
Recall that
X^{\mathbb{R}}
is completely determined by a pair
(K,\lambda^{\mathbb{R}})
, where
K
is an
(n-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex and
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}
is a mod
2
characteristic map over
K
.
Let
\omega_{i}
be the element of
\row\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathbb{R}}
corresponding to the
i
th row of
\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathbb{R}}
as a matrix for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Each nonzero element
\omega=\omega_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\omega_{i_{k}}
of
\row\lambda_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathbb{R}}
can be regarded as a subset
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
of
2^{[n]}
.
If there is a map
\sigma\colon 2^{[n]}\to[0,n]
such that

\widetilde{\beta}_{i-1}(K_{\omega})=0\quad\text{for all $i\neq\sigma(\omega)$},

then we say that the (rational) homology group
H^{\ast}(X^{\mathbb{R}})
of
X^{\mathbb{R}}
is
concentrated in
\sigma
.
In particular, a building set
\mathcal{B}
on
[n+1]
is venustus if and only if the homology of
X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is concentrated in
\sigma_{n}
defined by

\sigma_{n}(\omega)=\begin{cases}\frac{\left|\omega\right|}{2},&\mbox{if $\left|\omega\right|$ is even},\\
\frac{\left|\omega\right|+1}{2},&\mbox{if $\left|\omega\right|$ is odd},\end{cases}

for each
\omega\in 2^{[n]}
.
Motivated by this, one can define for a pair
(K,\lambda^{\mathbb{R}})
to be
venustus
if the corresponding real toric manifold has the homology that is concentrated in
\sigma_{n}
.

The characterization of venustus pairs seems quite challenging.
Most pairs
(K,\lambda^{\mathbb{R}})
are not venustus, and finding a venustus one is likely to be difficult.
For a pair to be venustus, it undoubtedly needs to have good combinatorial and topological structures for both
K
and
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}
.

One nontrivial example of venustus pairs is the
real Coxeter variety

X_{B_{n}}^{\mathbb{R}}
of type
B_{n}
.
According to
[
10
]
, the corresponding complexes in the case of
X_{B_{n}}^{\mathbb{R}}
are flag and shellable, which makes the variety venustus.
In contrast, for other types, none are venustus as shown in
[
9
]
and
[
14
]
.

Just as a real permutohedral variety
X_{A_{n}}^{\mathbb{R}}
is generalized to a real toric manifold derived from a building set,
X_{B_{n}}^{\mathbb{R}}
can also be extended to ones associated with building sets, as studied in
[
19
]
,
[
32
]
, and
[
44
]
.
It would also be interesting to ask the characterization of
type
B
venustus
building sets.
More generally, we propose the following problem.

Problem 7.3
.

Characterize venustus pairs
(K,\lambda^{\mathbb{R}})
; that is, the characterize real toric manifolds whose homology is concentrated in
\sigma_{n}
.

7.2.
Unimodality and multiplicative structures

As shown in Section
6
, for certain chordal building sets calculated by the authors so far, it has been observed that the sequence of the Betti numbers of
X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}
is unimodal.
In particular, we can rigorously prove that the unimodality holds for real permutohedral varieties.
Denote by
a_{2k}
the
2k
th Euler zigzag number (A000111 in
[
37
]
).

Lemma 7.4

(Proposition 3.2 in
[
38
]
)
.

The sequences
\left\{\frac{a_{2k}}{(2k)!}\right\}_{r\geq 0}
is strictly log-concave.

Theorem 7.5
.

For a fixed positive integer
n
, the sequence
\left\{\binom{n+1}{2k}a_{2k}\right\}_{r\geq 0}
is log-concave.

Proof.

Set
\alpha_{k}=\frac{a_{2k}}{(2k)!}
and
\beta_{k}=\binom{n+1}{2k}a_{2k}
.
Then we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{k}^{2}}{\beta_{k-1}\beta_{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1-2k+2)(n+1-2k+1)\alpha_{k}^{2}}{(n+1-2k)(n+1-2k-1)\alpha_{k-1}\alpha_{k+1}}

\displaystyle>\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{\alpha_{k-1}\alpha_{k+1}}

\displaystyle>1\quad\text{(by Lemma~\ref{prop:a/nfac}).}

∎

Even if we consider graphical building sets, we have failed to find any example whose Betti number sequence is not unimodal.
Based on these findings, we propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 7.6
.

For a venustus building set
\mathcal{B}
, the sequence
\{\beta_{k}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}})\}_{k\geq 0}
representing the rational Betti numbers of the associated real toric manifold is unimodal.

On the other hand, for a chordal building set
\mathcal{B}
, one remarkable advantage of our result is that the generator of the cohomology ring of
X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}
can also be expressed by alternating
\mathcal{B}
-permutations.
This allows for a nice combinatorial description of multiplicative structures in terms of permutations, similar to a real permutohedral variety
[
13
]
.
However, there is a key difference.
In the case of real permutohedral varieties,
K_{\mathcal{B}|_{I}}^{\odd}
is a pure simplicial complex, whereas
K_{\mathcal{B}|_{I}}^{\odd}
is not pure in general.
This difference makes it difficult to directly apply the methods from
[
13
]
to the case of a chordal building set
\mathcal{B}
.
Therefore, we leave the following problem.

Problem 7.7
.

For a connected chordal building set
\mathcal{B}
, give an explicit description of a multiplicative structure of rational cohomology ring
H^{\ast}(X^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}})
in terms of alternating
\mathcal{B}
-permutations.
